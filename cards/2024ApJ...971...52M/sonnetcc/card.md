## Analysis recipe

1. **Restore ALMA calibration and self-calibrate**: Restore pipeline-calibrated data with scriptForPI.py in CASA, then phase self-calibrate Sgr A* and J1700 as point sources  
  > "We use the `scriptForPI.py` code to restore the calibrated data from the automatic ALMA pipeline in `CASA` version 5.4.0-70 (Pipeline-CASA54-P1-B, r42254)." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA); "Like the VLA data, we phase self-calibrated Sgr A\* and J1700, assuming each was a point source to mitigate residual baseline-based phase errors." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: CASA 5.4.0-70, Pipeline-CASA54-P1-B r42254  
  > "We use the `scriptForPI.py` code to restore the calibrated data from the automatic ALMA pipeline in `CASA` version 5.4.0-70 (Pipeline-CASA54-P1-B, r42254)." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - assumption: Sgr A* and J1700 each treated as a point source  
  > "Like the VLA data, we phase self-calibrated Sgr A\* and J1700, assuming each was a point source to mitigate residual baseline-based phase errors." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract ALMA light curves**: Import into AIPS and use DFTPL (mean of the real visibilities at the phase center) with 1 minute bins on baselines >= 20 klambda  
  > "Finally, we import the data into `AIPS` and use `DFTPL` to extract the light curves with a $1$ minute binning time on baselines $\geq20~\text{k}\lambda$." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA); "`DFTPL` averages the real portion of the visibilities at the phase center to determine light curves without the need for other (e.g., imaging-based) techniques." (2.1 Radio: Very Large Array)
   - software: AIPS, DFTPL
   - parameter: 1 minute binning; baselines >= 20 klambda  
  > "Finally, we import the data into `AIPS` and use `DFTPL` to extract the light curves with a $1$ minute binning time on baselines $\geq20~\text{k}\lambda$." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - assumption: Emission below 20 klambda is dominated by the extended Circumnuclear Disk  
  > "finding that sizes below this threshold are dominated by the extended emission from the Circumnuclear Disk." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Baseline cut chosen after inspecting visibility amplitude versus projected baseline  
  > "We chose this limit after viewing the visibility amplitude as a function of projected baseline distance" (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
3. **Post-process ALMA light curves**: Discard the last execution block on 21 July; scale blocks so the phase-calibrator flux matches the first block, applying the factors to the phase calibrator and Sgr A*  
  > "We discarded the last execution block on 21 July 2019 due to amplitude-decorrelation on several antennas caused by the low altitude of our calibrators." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA); "We follow to correct this by using the first block as our reference." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA); "These scale factors are applied to the phase calibrator and Sgr A\*." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - parameter: Reference: first execution block; flux changes of order a few percent between blocks  
  > "The calculated flux of the phase calibrator changed (on the order of a few percent) between execution blocks." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - assumption: Phase calibrator is constant in flux, so its changes between blocks are calibration artifacts  
  > "The phase calibrator’s average flux in subsequent execution blocks was used to calculate multiplicative correction factors." (2.2 Submillimeter: ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure time delays between bands**: pyCCF cross-correlation with Monte Carlo realizations; mean delay and 95% interval reported; ALMA curve inverted for the submm-X-ray dip  
  > "We complete cross-correlation analyses to check for time-delayed emission using `pyCCF` (cite: Peterson1998; PYCCF)." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations); "We invert the ALMA light curve before completing the cross-correlation between the submm and X-ray light curves" (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - software: pyCCF
   - parameter: 5000 Monte Carlo realizations, removing a random number of light curve points each time  
  > "we use 5000 Monte Carlo realizations that remove a random number of light curve points each time and build a time delay histogram between two light curves." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - parameter: Delay significant if zero is outside the 95% interval  
  > "We consider that the light curves are delayed if zero is not contained within the 95% interval." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - assumption: Cross-correlation responds to peak-peak, not peak-trough, matching  
  > "Cross correlations are sensitive to peaks in emission" (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Expected delay across the ALMA band estimated from Iwata2020 (~30 s, below the 60 s binning)  
  > "Since we average the light curve in 60-second increments, any time delays shorter than this will be washed out." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
5. **Test simultaneity of submm and IR peaks**: Gaussian process regression of ALMA and Spitzer data, then pyCCF on the interpolated curves  
  > "we follow in using a Gaussian process regression to interpolate the ALMA data between execution blocks." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations); "We again use `pyCCF` to search for time lags between the interpolated ALMA and *Spitzer*  data" (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - software: sklearn, pyCCF
   - parameter: Compound constant + radial-basis kernel  
  > "We use the Gaussian process toolkit, part of the `sklearn` Python package, and choose a compound constant + radial-basis kernel." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - parameter: 68% interval used as interpolated data errors in pyCCF  
  > "as the interpolated data error inputs to `pyCCF` instead of the 95% interval for consistency with Figure" (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - fitting: Gaussian process regression with constant + RBF kernel; optimizer and priors not stated  
  > "choose a compound constant + radial-basis kernel." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Periodogram and significance of the 35 min feature**: numpy FFT of 1 minute binned Spitzer data; Monte Carlo resampling within flux errors for error weighting; significance against 35,000 simulated red-noise light curves  
  > "so we use `numpy` fast Fourier transforms (FFT) to detect evidence for transient periodicity." (3.1.2 Periodograms); "we randomly resample each of the observed points within their (normally distributed) flux uncertainties and redo the FFT on the" (3.1.2 Periodograms); "We generate 35,000 red-noise light curves (using the `stochastic`[^4] Python package) with a spectral density exponent $\alpha=2.75$ (cite: Weldon2023)." (3.1.2 Periodograms)
   - software: numpy, stochastic
   - parameter: 35,000 resamplings and 35,000 red-noise light curves; alpha = 2.75  
  > "We generate 35,000 red-noise light curves (using the `stochastic`[^4] Python package) with a spectral density exponent $\alpha=2.75$ (cite: Weldon2023)." (3.1.2 Periodograms)
   - assumption: Red-noise light curves scaled to the same mean and standard deviation as the data  
  > "We scale each of the generated light curves to have the same mean and standard deviation as the Sgr A\* light curve." (3.1.2 Periodograms)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Argued from single-day occurrence, same feature in ALMA, absence in a larger Spitzer catalogue, and the pointing-wobble state  
  > "There are several key reasons why we trust the validity of this transient periodicity." (3.1.2 Periodograms)
7. **Constrain the 21 July occultation plasma with a parameter grid**: Grid of electron index, field and radius; compute IR flux and maximum 334 GHz flux change; keep models consistent with the observed submm drop and IR limits  
  > "We make grid of $500^3$ realizations linearly sampling the electron energy index ($p\in\left[0.34, 5\right]$), the magnetic field strength ($B\in\left[0.1, 150\right]$ Gauss), and the initial radius ($R\in\left[0.01, 10\right]~\rm{R}_{\rm{S}}$)." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling); "We limit the possible models using several constraints." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
   - parameter: 500^3 grid; p in [0.34, 5], B in [0.1, 150] G, R in [0.01, 10] R_S  
  > "We make grid of $500^3$ realizations linearly sampling the electron energy index ($p\in\left[0.34, 5\right]$), the magnetic field strength ($B\in\left[0.1, 150\right]$ Gauss), and the initial radius ($R\in\left[0.01, 10\right]~\rm{R}_{\rm{S}}$)." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
   - parameter: Accepted submm drop within 20% of 610 mJy (488-732 mJy)  
  > "but consider models where the flux difference is within $20\%$ of this value ($488-732$ mJy) to account for scatter within the ALMA light curve." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
   - parameter: IR flux bounds: upper 5 x RMSE (RMSE ~3.2 mJy); lower 5 x 0.65 mJy  
  > "As an upper limit, we calculate $5\times$RMSE (RMSE $\sim3.2$ mJy) of the *Spitzer* data during the low SNR period (about 1-2.1 hr UTC)." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
   - assumption: Quiescent flux S_q = 4.48 Jy (average before the occultation)  
  > "For this calculation, we assume $S_q = 4.48$ Jy, which is the average flux before the occultation" (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
   - assumption: Electron energy bounds E_0 = E_1 = 1 MeV and E_2 = 1 GeV  
  > "For this analysis, we assume $E_0 = E_1 = 1$ MeV and $E_2 = 1$ GeV." (4.1.1 Hotspot Equations)
   - assumption: Homogeneous spherical hotspot with uniform field; equipartition-type electron density; pitch angle 90 degrees  
  > "We further assume the hotspot is a homogeneous sphere with an initial radius $R$ and contains an initial magnetic field strength $B$." (4.1.1 Hotspot Equations); "We have set the pitch angles to $90^\circ$" (4.1.1 Hotspot Equations)
   - fitting: No true fit; statistics of the grid realizations consistent with the data (pseudo-posterior)  
  > "While we cannot directly fit the light curves, we use the realizations consistent with the data to determine the statistical properties of the flare." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
   - validation: Magnetic-field distribution stays flat even with a 500 G maximum  
  > "Even at these strengths, the distribution of values consistent with the data is still flat (not shown)." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
8. **Jointly fit the 26 July radio and submm flare**: Joint fit of VLA Ka-band and ALMA light curves with one adiabatic hotspot (S_0, t_0, p, v_exp) plus separate linear quiescent models for radio and submm  
  > "we jointly fit the radio and submm light curves shown in Figure" (5.2 26 July 2019); "we use two different sets of quiescent parameters to independently model the radio and submm emission" (5.2 26 July 2019); "The variable model is characterized by four parameters: $S_0$, $t_0$, $p$, and $v_{\rm{exp}}$." (4.2.1 Flaring Emission Model)
   - parameter: Hotspot parameters S_0, t_0, p, v_exp shared across bands; quiescent I_0, slope and spectral index per band  
  > "The flaring emission is fit using a single set of hotspot parameters." (5.2 26 July 2019)
   - assumption: Synchrotron cooling ignored in the adiabatic hotspot model; IR light curve not fit  
  > "we have ignored any contribution of synchrotron cooling to the light curves." (5.3 Effects of Synchrotron Cooling); "Therefore, we do not model the IR light curve, as the modeled parameters will have little physical meaning." (5.2 26 July 2019)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Independent fits of radio and submm separately give p ~1.3, used to explain the low joint p  
  > "To confirm this, we independently fit the radio and submm data with a single adiabatically-cooling hotspot (not shown)." (5.3 Effects of Synchrotron Cooling)
9. **Derive equipartition field, radius and expansion speed of the 26 July hotspot**: Equipartition calculation from fitted p and peak flux over an assumed electron energy range  
  > "Given these values, we can calculate an equipartition magnetic field strength and radius for the observed flare." (5.2.2 Hotspot Properties)
   - parameter: Electron spectrum between 1 and 1000 MeV; alternative upper bound 175 MeV gives B = 94 G  
  > "we assume the $E^{-0.59}$ electron energy spectrum is valid from energies between 1 to 1000 MeV" (5.2.2 Hotspot Properties)
   - assumption: Linear expansion of the hotspot  
  > "Assuming linear expansion of the hotspot yields an expansion velocity of $0.029c$." (5.2.2 Hotspot Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with GRMHD conditions and earlier hotspot sizes and speeds; field about a factor 2 above earlier values  
  > "the magnetic field strength here is much stronger than previously observed by a factor $\sim2$." (5.2.2 Hotspot Properties)

## Qualitative findings

- No statistically significant time delay across the ALMA bandwidth (~14 GHz) on 21 July  
  > "We report no statistically significant time delay across the ALMA bandwidth ($\sim14$ GHz)." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
- On 26 July the flare appears successively in IR, submm and radio, with delays between bands  
  > "In Figure [9](#fig:july26_xcorr)e, we present the stacked mid-IR, submm, and radio light curves, which clearly show the time delay evolution of the flare." (3.2.1 Cross Correlations)
- Fitted quiescent radio and submm emission in 2019 is consistent with earlier analyses  
  > "Similarly, our fitted submm spectral index and flux are well within the range of those observed" (5.2.1 Quiescent Properties)
- Low fitted p is attributed to ignoring synchrotron cooling  
  > "the low value of $p$ measured in the joint radio-submm plasmon fit is caused by ignoring synchrotron cooling in the evolution of the submm flare." (5.3 Effects of Synchrotron Cooling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier work covered only the first campaign day and left out the HST and VLA data; this paper presents the remaining two days with all observatories  
  > "(cite: Boyce2022) focused only on modeling multiwavelength features on the first day of observation and did not include contemporaneous HST (IR) and VLA (radio) observations during this campaign." (Introduction); "Here, we present the remaining two days of monitoring with all participating observatories." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Occultation model chosen among several interpretations of the submm and IR/X-ray anti-correlation  
  > "While several interpretations aim to describe the anti-correlation between submm and IR/X-ray light curves, we will focus on the occultation model." (4 Models of Observed Events in the Light Curves)
- Alternative: Simultaneity of submm and IR peaks across the ALMA gap tested with Gaussian process interpolation  
  > "Due to the lack of ALMA data near 3.5 hr UTC, we cannot rule out the possibility of simultaneity." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
- Alternative: Spitzer pointing wobble considered and disfavoured as the origin of the 35 minute periodicity  
  > "A wobble with a sustained period would have been detected in their analysis at higher significance since they used *Spitzer* data beginning in late 2013." (3.1.2 Periodograms)
- Alternative: IR light curve not modeled because synchrotron cooling dominates there  
  > "Therefore, we do not model the IR light curve, as the modeled parameters will have little physical meaning." (5.2 26 July 2019)
- Alternative: Plasmoid depleted-magnetization dips versus flare occultation as alternative origins of submm dips  
  > "However, plasmoids cause dips in the submm flux from depleted magnetization in the accretion flow rather than a flare occulting it" (5.1.1 Transient Periodicity)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): VLA Q-band data on 18 and 21 July unusable; only the 26 July Ka-band data kept  
  > "Despite our best efforts, these data were not salvageable to extract light curves." (2.1 Radio: Very Large Array)
- Limitation (authors): Field strengths uncertain by a factor of 2 or more because of unconstrained electron energy bounds  
  > "In both cases, the uncertainty in the appropriate lower and upper electron energy bounds may inflate the derived equipartition field strengths by a factor of 2 or more." (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): Synchrotron cooling neglected, biasing p low and B high  
  > "Overall, ignoring synchrotron cooling causes a biased value of $p$ and tends to overpredict the calculated equipartition magnetic field strength compared to previous observational and theoretical values." (5.3 Effects of Synchrotron Cooling)
- Limitation (authors): Occultation field strength degenerate because only one submm frequency was used  
  > "Additionally, the flat distribution of magnetic field strengths is an artifact of degeneracy since we only used one frequency to model the occultation." (5.1.2 Occultation Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): 60 second averaging hides short delays  
  > "Since we average the light curve in 60-second increments, any time delays shorter than this will be washed out." (3.1.1 Cross Correlations)
- Limitation (authors): No general relativistic effects such as lensing included  
  > "For example, the lack of general relativistic effects, such as lensing, may also affect our results." (5.3 Effects of Synchrotron Cooling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fits have no proper posterior or likelihood: occultation uses a grid with flat acceptance windows, the joint fit gives 1 sigma errors with no stated method and p = 0.59 lies outside the physical range
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one ALMA band and one VLA band; model degeneracies (B, p, R) are unbroken
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 4 ALMA execution blocks over two days with gaps; Gaussian-process interpolation drives the IR-submm lag result
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Periodogram significance depends on the assumed red-noise slope and on excluding a Spitzer wobble origin
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA ACA baselines >= 20 klambda cut and self-cal assumptions affect the light-curve amplitudes without a stated flux-scale uncertainty
