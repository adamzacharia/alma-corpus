## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image Stokes I, Q, U, V maps**: Run ALMA calibration pipeline and image Stokes I, Q, U, V parameters for each epoch using CASA tclean with Briggs weighting (robust=0.5)  
  > "Data processing was carried out using `CASA` version 6.5.4.9 and ALMA Pipeline version 2023.1.0.124 (cite: alma_pipeline). The clean images were generated using CASA task `tclean` with weighting = briggs (robust=0.5)." (Sample, observations and data reduction); "For each epoch, we created maps of the Stokes parameters $I$, $Q$, $U$, and $V$." (Sample, observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, ALMA Pipeline, tclean
   - parameter: weighting = briggs (robust=0.5)  
  > "The clean images were generated using CASA task `tclean` with weighting = briggs (robust=0.5)." (Sample, observations and data reduction)
   - assumption: Systematic Band 3 flux error is 5%  
  > "According to the ALMA Proposer’s Guide, the systematic flux error for Band 3 observations is 5%." (Sample, observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked consistency of flux, polarization degree, and polarization angle of calibrators and check sources against the AMAPOLA monitoring database  
  > "Flux measurements from the polarization calibrators, as well as the check sources, yielded consistent results, justifying the application of a 5% error margin in our analysis." (Sample, observations and data reduction); "Furthermore, the estimated degree and angle of polarization for the calibrators were consistent with the measurements from the AMAPOLA calibrator monitoring" (Sample, observations and data reduction)
2. **Measure polarized intensity, polarization degree, and upper limits**: Compute polarized intensity P = sqrt(Q^2 + U^2), debiased polarization degree PD = P/I * 100%, and 3-sigma upper limits PD_lim at peak total intensity I when undetected  
  > "The polarized intensity was thus calculated as $$\begin{equation*}
    P = \sqrt{Q^2 + U^2},
\end{equation*}$$ where $Q$ and $U$ are the Stokes parameters in units of flux." (Sample, observations and data reduction); "The polarization degree (PD) was then calculated as PD = $P/I \times 100\%$, where $I$ is the total intensity, and debiased following the method outlined in (cite: montier1; montier2)." (Sample, observations and data reduction); "$$\begin{equation*}
    {\rm PD}_{\rm lim} = \frac{3 \times \sqrt{\sigma_Q^2 + \sigma_U^2 }}{I} \times 100\%, 
\end{equation*}$$" (Sample, observations and data reduction)
   - assumption: Circular polarization V is zero  
  > "Circular polarization was not detected in the maps and was therefore assumed to be zero, as it is typically weak at millimeter wavelengths." (Sample, observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Calculate in-band spectral index and spectral index maps**: Fit a power law F_nu proportional to nu^alpha across the four spectral windows for the AGN core and pixel-by-pixel for pixels exceeding 2 sigma in all four spectral windows  
  > "The in-band spectral index of the AGN emission, calculated across four spectral windows assuming $F_\nu \propto \nu^{\alpha}$, was found to be $\alpha = -0.92 \pm 0.40$." (NGC 3783); "The maps are created for only pixels with a value exceeding 2$\sigma$ at all four frequencies." (Extended mm structure and polarization)
   - parameter: 2 sigma threshold across all four spectral windows for spectral index mapping  
  > "The maps are created for only pixels with a value exceeding 2$\sigma$ at all four frequencies." (Extended mm structure and polarization)
   - assumption: Power-law spectrum F_nu proportional to nu^alpha  
  > "The in-band spectral index of the AGN emission, calculated across four spectral windows assuming $F_\nu \propto \nu^{\alpha}$, was found to be $\alpha = -0.92 \pm 0.40$." (NGC 3783)
   - fitting: Power-law fit across four spectral windows  
  > "The in-band spectral index of the AGN emission, calculated across four spectral windows assuming $F_\nu \propto \nu^{\alpha}$, was found to be $\alpha = -0.92 \pm 0.40$." (NGC 3783)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Analyze intra-observation and multi-epoch mm flux variability**: Re-image observations in time intervals of tens of minutes to test intra-observation variability, and compare multi-epoch Band 3 fluxes and spectral indices using archival ALMA data with resolution < 0.3''  
  > "First, we checked for intra-observational variability by re-imaging the observations and dividing them into time intervals of tens of minutes." (Variability in mm); "To examine variability on longer timescales, we compiled ALMA Band 3 data for all three sources. We selected observations with comparable angular resolution (less than 0.3$''$) to minimize contamination from nearby structures." (Variability in mm)
   - parameter: Angular resolution < 0.3'' for archival Band 3 data  
  > "We selected observations with comparable angular resolution (less than 0.3$''$) to minimize contamination from nearby structures." (Variability in mm)
   - assumption: Flux uncertainty of 5%  
  > "No source exhibited significant flux changes beyond the measurement uncertainty (assumed to be 5%)." (Variability in mm)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate synchrotron emitting region sizes (R_min, R_max) and equipartition magnetic fields**: Use Laor & Behar (2008) equipartition relations with 100 GHz luminosities to derive R_min and B_eq, and fit power laws to archival low-frequency radio data (<40 GHz) to estimate R_max from the highest frequency following the radio power law  
  > "To derive rough constraints on the emitting region size, we follow the approach of (cite: laor08), assuming that the mm emission originates from a homogeneous synchrotron source with a uniform magnetic field $B$ and a power-law energy distribution of relativistic electrons." (The size of the emitting region); "Based on the radio luminosity detected at the highest frequency that follows the radio power-law, we estimated upper limits for $R_{\rm max}$." (The size of the emitting region)
   - parameter: 14–150 keV bolometric correction of 8.48  
  > "considering a uniform 14–150 keV bolometric correction of 8.48" (5.4 The size of the emitting region)
   - assumption: Homogeneous synchrotron source with uniform magnetic field in equipartition with photon energy density (B = B_eq)  
  > "To derive rough constraints on the emitting region size, we follow the approach of (cite: laor08), assuming that the mm emission originates from a homogeneous synchrotron source with a uniform magnetic field $B$ and a power-law energy distribution of relativistic electrons. Additionally, we assume that the magnetic energy density is in equipartition with the photon energy density." (The size of the emitting region)
   - fitting: Power-law fit to low-frequency radio data (<= 40 GHz)  
  > "In all cases, the low-frequency radio data ($\lesssim$40 GHz) are well described by a power-law fit." (The size of the emitting region)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare RQ AGN polarization with archival ALMA observations of RL AGN**: Examine archival high-resolution (<0.5'') ALMA polarization and multi-band continuum data for non-blazar RL AGN (NGC 1052, 3C 120, Cen A, M87) and FSRQ 3C 273  
  > "To investigate RL AGN with extended radio jets and without strong Doppler beaming, we selected non-blazar RL sources having high-resolution archival ALMA data (<0.5$''$). This dataset included three RL sources: NGC 1052, Cen A, and 3C 120." (Comparison with radio-loud sources)
   - parameter: Angular resolution < 0.5''  
  > "we selected non-blazar RL sources having high-resolution archival ALMA data (<0.5$''$)." (Comparison with radio-loud sources)
   - assumption: Calibration uncertainty of 0.1% for archival polarization limits  
  > "Despite this, no polarized signal was detected above the 0.1% calibration uncertainty in the November 2018 Band 4 and 6 data, confirming the absence of detectable mm polarization." (Comparison with radio-loud sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- No millimeter polarization was detected at the AGN positions of NGC 3783, MCG 5-23-16, or NGC 4945, with 3-sigma upper limits between 0.5% and 1.5%, consistent with extreme Faraday depolarization in an X-ray corona.  
  > "No polarized signal was detected in any of the AGN, with an upper limit of 0.5–1.5%, supporting the X-ray corona scenario." (abstract)
- Deep Band 3 imaging revealed faint extended continuum emission (0.1–0.2 mJy/beam, 2–3 sigma) extending over tens of parsecs around the compact cores of NGC 3783 and MCG 5-23-16.  
  > "surrounding NGC 3783 and MCG 5–23–16, extending over at least a few tens of parsecs, which had not been previously detected" (Conclusions)
- An unresolved source with 17% +/- 2% polarization was detected ~20.2 pc north of the AGN in NGC 3783, co-spatial with the extended mm structure and narrow-line outflow.  
  > "However, we detected a compact source with 17% polarization in NGC 3783, 20 pc away from the AGN, co-spatial with the mm and narrow-line outflow, likely linked to a shock propagating through the outflowing material." (abstract)
- No intra-observational flux variability was detected over tens of minutes to hours, while multi-epoch archival Band 3 data show typical flux variations by a factor of ~2 across all three RQ AGN.  
  > "The studied sources show no mm flux variability during the observational sessions, suggesting that the mm source size exceeds$\sim$1 light hour. A compilation of archival ALMA Band 3 data for the RQ AGN in our sample revealed that the mm flux varies in all sources by a typical factor of 2 (Sec. 5.3)." (Conclusions)
- High-resolution archival ALMA observations of non-blazar radio-loud AGN cores also show either undetected polarization (<0.1%) or low polarization (0.5%–2.8%), comparable to the RQ AGN upper limits.  
  > "Notably, archival high-resolution ALMA data of central emission in non-blazar RL AGN also show either no mm polarization or one comparable with the upper limits for our RQ sample (Sec. 5.5)." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected the three brightest RQ AGN at 100 GHz from a volume-limited (<50 Mpc) sample with prior <100 mas ALMA detections, spanning AGN types 1, 1.9, and 2 and a wide range of column densities and inclination angles, observed near the ~100 GHz synchrotron turnover frequency.  
  > "From the sample from (cite: ricci23), we chose the three brightest sources at 100 GHz, selecting objects of different AGN types and covering the widest possible range of column densities." (Sample, observations and data reduction); "This selection was designed to ensure a variety of inclination angles, allowing us to investigate both potential absorption effects in external AGN structures and to observe different magnetic field projections." (Sample, observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Millimeter polarimetry near or above the synchrotron self-absorption turnover (~100 GHz) probes optically thin synchrotron radiation where coronal Faraday depths (RM ~ 10^11 rad m^-2) cause complete depolarization while jets and outflows retain a few percent polarization.  
  > "Observing mm emission near the turnover frequency or at higher frequencies may allow us to detect synchrotron radiation in the optically thin regime." (Expected levels of mm polarization); "Thus, if there is a jet or outflow with a compact source of synchrotron emission passing through its medium, we are likely to detect polarization at a fairly low level, on the order of a few percent. In the case of a corona, however, the observed polarization will be strictly zero." (Faraday rotation in mm emitting sources)
- Alternative: Depolarization solely by a disordered magnetic field with N_c > 2000 random cells was rejected because such a large number of independent zones would smooth out the rapid day-scale flux variability observed in RQ AGN.  
  > "To reach even the highest measured upper limit of PD (1.5%), the emission region would require $N_{\rm c} > 2000$ uncorrelated cells. Such a large number of randomly oriented emission zones would also smooth out any variability." (What causes depolarization of AGN mm emission?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The first session of NGC 4945 had formally insufficient parallactic angle coverage (51.12 deg instead of 60.00 deg) to reliably constrain instrumental polarization leakage.  
  > "Despite formally insufficient parallactic angle coverage during the first session (51.12$^\circ$ instead of the desired 60.00$^\circ$) to reliably constrain instrumental polarization leakage and accurately recover the intrinsic polarization angle (see details in, e.g., (cite: 3c286; MV16)), it was deemed close enough to allow flux and polarization estimates." (NGC 4945)
- Limitation (authors): Without kinematic information on the faint extended mm components in NGC 3783 and MCG 5-23-16, their origin in AGN activity cannot be confirmed.  
  > "Although the spatially compact central emission and the extended mm emission overlap, without kinematic information on this faint extended component, we cannot confirm whether these structures originate from AGN activity." (Extended mm structure and polarization)
- Limitation (authors): The total intensity at the offset polarized source in NGC 3783 (~0.2 mJy) may be contaminated by the nearby brighter AGN (~2 mJy), potentially underestimating the true intrinsic polarization degree.  
  > "The polarized source, with a total flux of $\sim$0.2 mJy, is located near the brighter AGN ($\sim$2 mJy), meaning that the observed total intensity could be contaminated by the neighboring AGN emission." (Extended mm structure and polarization)
- Limitation (authors): The in-band spectral index of the polarized region in NGC 3783 (-3.6 +/- 2.4) has a large uncertainty due to the narrow frequency coverage, limiting a definitive interpretation.  
  > "The in-band spectral index of total intensity in the region exhibiting polarized emission is extremely steep, $-3.6 \pm 2.4$, though the large uncertainty significantly limits the robustness of this measurement." (NGC 3783)
- Limitation (authors): A polarization non-detection in a narrow frequency window around 100 GHz with 2 GHz spectral windows is subject to bandwidth depolarization above RM ~ 9 x 10^6 rad m^-2 and cannot alone distinguish the depolarization mechanism without higher-frequency or narrower-channel observations.  
  > "However, a non-detection in a narrow frequency window around 100 GHz is insufficient to constrain the origin of the depolarization." (What causes depolarization of AGN mm emission?); "However, to strengthen this argument, highly sensitive mm polarimetric observations with higher spectral resolution, on the order of at least a few tens of MHz, are necessary to avoid bandwidth depolarization caused by the large rotation measures discussed in Sec. 2." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Sparsity of archival ALMA observations and lack of simultaneous X-ray monitoring prevent determining the physical mechanism driving the mm flux variability.  
  > "Unfortunately, no simultaneous X-ray observations were available, preventing a direct comparison between X-ray and mm variability." (Variability in mm); "Given the sparsity of the data, it is impossible to establish a definitive variability pattern." (Variability in mm)
- Limitation (authors): Low-frequency radio fluxes in NGC 4945 are heavily contaminated by surrounding star-forming structures and can only be treated as upper limits on the intrinsic AGN radio emission.  
  > "In contrast, for NGC 4945, the measured radio fluxes are significantly contaminated by surrounding structures. Therefore, we treat all available radio data as upper limits on the intrinsic radio emission of AGN." (The size of the emitting region)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The offset polarized feature in NGC 3783 sits only ~1 beam width (0.11'') from a 10x brighter unpolarized point source (~2 mJy), where off-axis instrumental polarization leakage or beam squint artefacts can mimic localized polarization if not modeled in visibility space.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In-band spectral indices measured across only ~14 GHz total lever arm (90.5 to 104.5 GHz) carry large systematic uncertainties (+/- 0.40) and in Appendix A show smaller statistical-only errors than in Table 3.
