## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST and ALMA data and extract spectra and continuum photometry for D1**: Reduce JWST NIRSpec and MIRI/MRS cubes with standard pipeline plus custom hot/cold pixel steps; extract D1 spectrum via simultaneous 2D Gaussian deblending of D1 and D0 with PSF star aperture correction; calibrate and clean ALMA Band 7 and 9 data (with phase self-calibration on CO(6-5) for Band 9) and extract central point-source continuum fluxes.  
  > "We extracted D1 spectrum by simultaneously applying 2D Gaussian models to both D1 and D0 to correctly deblend them at longer wavelengths." (Appendix A.1: JWST); "We used the standard ALMA pipeline (CASA v6.2.1; McMullin et al. 2007) to calibrate and clean the data." (Appendix A.2: ALMA); "We measured the central fluxes in the ALMA data using a point-source extraction, and compiled the PACS and SPIRE point-source extracted fluxes from Chu et al. (2017)." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties)
   - software: CASA, MRS pipeline
   - parameter: Band 7 beam FWHM 0''.19 x 0''.16; Band 9 beam FWHM 0''.28 x 0''.24  
  > "The synthesised beam full-width half-maximum (FWHM) is 0''.19×0''.16 and the maximum recoverable scale is 5''.7." (Appendix A.2: ALMA); "The synthesised beam FWHM is 0''.28×0''.24 and the maximum recoverable scale is 2''.5." (Appendix A.2: ALMA)
   - fitting: Simultaneous 2D Gaussian models fitted to D1 and D0 per spectral channel for JWST extraction; point-source extraction for ALMA continuum.  
  > "We extracted D1 spectrum by simultaneously applying 2D Gaussian models to both D1 and D0 to correctly deblend them at longer wavelengths." (Appendix A.1: JWST)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Subtract baseline continuum and PAH emission from JWST spectra**: Fit PAH profile using modified PAHFIT, subtract PAH features, then fit and subtract a cubic polynomial baseline while masking all remaining spectral features.  
  > "Using the PAH-subtracted spectrum, we then fit a cubic polynomial function masking all the remaining features present in the spectrum" (Appendix B: Baseline); "Using the PAH-subtracted spectrum, we then fit a cubic polynomial function masking all the remaining features present in the spectrum (see Fig. B.1)."    - software: PAHFIT
   - parameter: cubic polynomial baseline

   - fitting: Modified PAHFIT profile fit followed by masked cubic polynomial fit.  
  > "Using the PAH-subtracted spectrum, we then fit a cubic polynomial function masking all the remaining features present in the spectrum (see Fig. B.1)." (Appendix B: Baseline)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Model gas-phase rovibrational H2O and CO bands (~4.5-7.3 µm)**: Fit continuum-subtracted 12CO, 13CO, C18O (v=1-0) and H2O (v2=1-0) bands using a library of 4-component (torus, outflow, SF-extended, cold-extended) spherically symmetric non-local, non-LTE radiative transfer models, masking or simultaneously fitting contaminating atomic/ionic/H2/PAH lines.  
  > "We analysed the rovibrational CO and H2O bands (including the 12CO ν=1-0, 13CO ν=1-0, and C18O ν=1-0 bands) to characterise the properties of the various components present in the spectrum of II Zw 096-D1." (3. H2O and CO modelling); "The statistical equilibrium populations of H2O and CO were calculated using non-local and non-LTE radiative transfer computationsincluding a careful treatment of blending among lines of the same or different species." (3.1. Library of tailor-made models and fitting strategy); "For fitting this source, we used four components (as identified in Section 2.1), with different physical properties (Tdust, Tgas, column density, gas density, and velocity dispersion)." (3.1. Library of tailor-made models and fitting strategy)
   - parameter: Grid sizes: 396 torus models, 243 outflow models, 243 SF extended models, 81 cold extended models (~2x10^9 total combinations)  
  > "Given the large number of combinations ∼2×109 and points to be evaluated, the fitting process was performed in three steps." (3.1. Library of tailor-made models and fitting strategy); "The torus models (396) have NCO=1-4×1018 cm−2, Tdust=300-400 K and 12CO/13CO=50-70. The outflow models (243) have CO density of 2-8×106 cm−3, Tdust=300-400 K, 12CO/13CO=50-70 and velocity dispersion ranging from 200 to 240 km/s. The SF extended component models (243) have NCO=1-4×1019 cm−2, Tdust=300-400 K, 12CO/13CO=50-70 and a range of covering factors to produce the P-R asymmetry. The cold extended component models (81) is represented by a cold component with Tgas=20-40 K, illuminated by an IR source with Tdust=300-400 K and 12CO/13CO=50-70." (Appendix D: Molecular grid of models and fitting of emission lines)
   - assumption: Standard CO/H2 abundance of ~3.2x10^-4 for estimating H2 column densities  
  > "Hydrogen column densities are estimated by using the standard value of the CO/H2 abundance of ∼3.2×10−4 (e.g. Bolatto et al. 2013)." (Table 1)
   - assumption: Spherically symmetric gas and dust distributions  
  > "The model components encompass diverse spherically symmetric gas and dust distributions." (3.1. Library of tailor-made models and fitting strategy)
   - fitting: Iterative 3-step chi-squared minimization across the 4-component model grids.  
  > "To obtain the best-fit model, we minimised the χ2 in the fit to the entire CO and H2O bands. Given the large number of combinations ∼2×109 and points to be evaluated, the fitting process was performed in three steps." (3.1. Library of tailor-made models and fitting strategy)
   - validation: Inspection of fit residuals across the CO and H2O bands (Appendix F) and comparison of spatially resolved 12CO v=1-0 P(13) emission map with calibration PSF star (Appendix E).  
  > "Although the intensity map is centrally concentrated (top panel of Fig. E.1), the size fitted with a Gaussian profile is larger than that measured in a calibration point source (NIRSpec TYC 4433-1800-1, Program ID 1128, PI: N. Luetzgendorf)." (Appendix E: Spatially resolved mid-IR CO); "Figure F.1 shows the residuals of the best-fit model." (Appendix F: Residuals of the best-fit model of the molecular bands)

  > "To build the 1-850 µm SED of D1, we used the entire NIRSpec and MRS spectra (resampled to ∼100 points), high angular resolution ALMA archival data (Appendix A), and lower angular resolution far-IR fluxes from Herschel." (4. (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties); "Following the same method as in Efstathiou et al. (1995), we obtain multi-wavelength images of the best-fit torus model (see Fig. G.1). Finally, by fitting a Gaussian, we measured sizes of ∼3 and 11 pc (diameter) at 5 µm and 1 mm, respectively." (Appendix G: Best-fit torus model images)

   - parameter: Cold grey body T = 57+-4 K and beta = 1.8; Herschel fluxes set as upper limits  
  > "However, given the large physical area traced by the Herschel data (ranging from ∼5.3 to 34.7''; ∼3.8 to 25 kpc), we considered to set them as upper limits in the fit." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties); "We include an additional grey body of 57±4 K and β=1.8 (following the same method as in Kovács et al. 2010)." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties)
   - assumption: Standard Galactic dust mix of 53% silicates and 47% graphite in torus models

## Qualitative findings

- Rovibrational lines of H2O v2=1-0 (~5.3-7.2 µm) with P-branch in emission and R-branch in absorption, and 12CO v=1-0 (~4.45-4.95 µm) with both R- and P-branches in emission, are detected in II Zw 096-D1.  
  > "We report the detection of rovibrational lines of H2O ν2=1-0 (∼5.3-7.2 µm) and 12CO ν=1-0 (∼4.45-4.95 µm) in D1. The CO band shows the R- and P-branches in emission and the spectrum of the H2O band shows the P-branch in emission and the R-branch in absorption." (abstract)
- Weak Q-branch C2H2 (13.7 µm) and HCN (14.02 µm) mid-IR molecular absorption bands and ALMA submm HCN-vibrational (4-3) 356.256 GHz emission are detected in D1, confirming a compact deeply embedded nucleus.  
  > "We also detect relatively weak Q-branch C2H2 (13.7µm) and HCN (14.02µm) molecular absorption in D1 (Appendix C)." (2.1. Molecular bands of II Zw 096-D1); "Indeed, the bottom panel of Fig. C.1 shows that the HCN-vibrational (4–3) 356.256 GHz is clearly detected in D1 (see Appendix A for details on the ALMA data reduction)." (Appendix C: C2H2, HCN and submm HCN-vib (4-3) detection)
- High-ionization coronal lines indicative of an AGN (such as [Si VI], [Ar V], [Ar VI], [Ne V], [Ne VI]) are not clearly detected in D1 due to the exceptionally high covering factor of the dusty torus.  
  > "Although AGN emission is not clearly detected, there is evidence for the presence of an accreting SMBH in D1." (5. Alternative ways of looking for AGN in D1); "The exceptionally high covering factor of the dusty structure inhibits the direct detection of AGN emission." (6. Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST NIRSpec and MIRI/MRS provide unprecedented high angular and spectral resolution (R~1500-3500) across 1.0-28.1 µm needed to detect and resolve gas-phase rovibrational H2O and CO lines in extragalactic buried nuclei, complemented by high-resolution ALMA archival submm data to sample the cold/submm SED and HCN-vib (4-3) emission.  
  > "The unprecedented combination of high angular and spectral resolution (R∼1500-3500) in the 1.0-28.1 µm range afforded by the JWST Near-IR Spectrograph (NIRSpec; Jakobsen et al. 2022; Böker et al. 2022) and MIRI ( Rieke et al. 2015; Wells et al. 2015; Wright et al. 2015) are key to investigating deeply embedded sources such as II Zw 096-D1." (1. Introduction); "To build the 1-850 µm SED of D1, we used the entire NIRSpec and MRS spectra (resampled to ∼100 points), high angular resolution ALMA archival data (Appendix A), and lower angular resolution far-IR fluxes from Herschel." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties)
- Why this method: Combining radiative transfer torus SED models with the 4.5-7.0 µm continuum constraints inferred from non-LTE H2O and CO rovibrational band modelling breaks multi-component AGN+SF SED fitting degeneracies in distant luminous IR systems.  
  > "Their central region consists of a combination of AGN and an intense SF activity component (e.g. Martínez-Paredes et al. 2015; Herrero-Illana et al. 2017; Efstathiou et al. 2022). Thus, best-fit solutions might be subject to multi-component fitting degeneracies. Here, we use the radiative transfer CYprus models for Galaxies and their NUclear Spectra (CYGNUS) project (Efstathiou et al. 2022)." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties)
- Alternative: SED fit without prior continuum constraints from the H2O and CO molecular bands (rejected because it yields F_7µm^torus ~ 70% which would produce very deep molecular absorption bands that are not observed).  
  > "The resulting fit has a high fractional contribution of the torus to the SED at 7 µm (Ftorus 7µm ∼70%). We can discard this scenario given that this strong central component would produce very deep absorption bands that are not observed in D1." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties)
- Alternative: Compact maximal starburst or extreme top-heavy IMF starburst powering D1 (rejected because the IR surface brightness of ~3.6x10^8 L_sun/pc^2 exceeds the theoretical maximal starburst limit of ~10^7 L_sun/pc^2, and top-heavy IMF systems are unstable on short timescales of a few Myr).  
  > "In addition, from the theoretical point of view, there is a limit of ∼107 L⊙/pc2 for a maximal SB (e.g. Thompson et al. 2005; Pereira-Santaella et al. 2021). Although we cannot rule out the possibility of an extreme top-heavy initial mass function (IMF) SB, this is unlikely since these systems are not stable on short timescales of few Myr (see e.g. Pereira-Santaella et al. 2021)." (5. Alternative ways of looking for AGN in D1)
- Alternative: Identification of the 1.43 µm emission line as [Si X] (rejected because [Si VI], which is typically 1.8 times brighter than [Si X] in AGN, is not detected).  
  > "An emission line at 1.43 µm coincides with the expected position of [Si X] (IP: 351.1 eV), but [Si VI] is not detected, which is typically brighter than [Si X] in AGN (1.8 times on average; Rodríguez-Ardila et al. 2011). This indicates that the emission line observed at 1.43µm is not likely to be related to [Si X]." (5. Alternative ways of looking for AGN in D1)

- Limitation (authors): The molecular band model assumes a single-temperature blackbody for the dust, whereas the CYGNUS torus models account for temperature gradients across the dust, resulting in a factor of ~2 difference in the derived 5 µm size (1.4 pc vs 3 pc).  
  > "The size of the dusty structure at 5 µm derived from the torus model is roughly twice as large as that determined by the molecular bands fit. This difference arises from the torus models accounting for temperature gradients across the dust, whereas the model of molecular bands includes a single temperature blackbody." (4. Inner dusty structure modelling: Molecular bands as a tool for constraining the torus properties)
- Limitation (authors): The best-fit SED model underestimates the continuum emission at ~12-15 µm, likely due to standard Galactic dust grain composition assumptions in a sub-solar metallicity source.  
  > "The resulting fit in Fig. 4 (left) underestimates the continuum emission at ∼12-15µm." (4.   > "The latter are detected in the submm (e.g. Neufeld et al. 2000, 2002; Plume et al. 2004), and are not included in this model. (Appendix F: Residuals of the best-fit model of the molecular bands)
