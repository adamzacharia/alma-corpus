## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare complementary visibility combinations**: Subtract continuum separately for each resolution, then concatenate HR+MR and HR+MR+LR visibilities for each source.  
  > "The line emission was conservatively estimated and the continuum subtracted using the CASA task `uvcontsub` to produce the continuum subtracted visibility file for each resolution." (Observations and Data reduction); "First, a concatenation of the High resolution and Medium Resolution was made using `concat` for each source." (Observations and Data reduction); "Secondly, a High resolution + Medium resolution + Low resolution concatenation was made using the CASA task `concat` for each source." (Observations and Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, concat
   - parameter: HR+MR and HR+MR+LR  
  > "High+Medium+Low resolution (HR+MR+LR) - for all 3 resolutions combined and HR+MR - for the high and medium resolution concatenation." (Observations and Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pipeline calibration manually checked; no issues found.  
  > "The pipeline calibration was manually checked and no issues were found with the calibration." (Observations and Data reduction)
2. **Image the line cubes**: Create a dirty image, estimate peak-channel RMS in an aperture outside the galaxy, and clean with tclean.  
  > "In order to produce the final data cubes, cleaning was undertaken using the CASA task `tclean`" (Observations and Data reduction); "A dirty image was produced, and the RMS per pixel, of the peak channel, estimated using an aperture located outside of the galaxy (to avoid emission from the galaxy)." (Observations and Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs weighting; robust = 0.5.  
  > "a Briggs weighting (with robust = 0.5) was chosen to best compromise between resolution and sensitivity." (Observations and Data reduction)
   - parameter: Cleaning threshold: 2.5×RMS.  
  > "All galaxies were cleaned to a threshold of $2.5\times {\rm RMS}$." (Observations and Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Resultant moment-0 RMS and synthesised beams tabulated.  
  > "The resultant RMS per pixel and synthesised beam for each moment 0 map are shown in Table" (Observations and Data reduction)
3. **Select line channels and construct moment maps**: Evaluate all channel ranges, select the range maximising aperture S/N, and make moment 0 and 1 maps with immoments.  
  > "This was calculated for all possible channel ranges in the data-cube, the velocity range which maximised the S/N is considered to be the channel range containing line emission." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps); "Moment 0 and 1 maps were produced using the CASA task `immoments` using the channels previously selected to contain line emission." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
   - software: CASA, immoments
   - parameter: S/N search aperture diameter: 3″ for HR+MR+LR; 1.5″ for HR+MR.  
  > "The total signal to noise (S/N) was measured within an aperture of diameter $3\ensuremath{''}$ (for the HR+MR+LR resolution concatenation) or $1.5\ensuremath{''}$ for the HR+MR cubes." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
   - parameter: Moment-1 sigma cut: 3–5σ depending on galaxy morphology.  
  > "For the HR+MR and HR+MR+LR moment 1 maps,a $\sigma$-cut was made (between 3-5$\sigma$ depending on the galaxies morphology)" (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pixels below the chosen moment-1 S/N cut set to null.  
  > "any pixel with a S/N less than defined above is set to null." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
4. **Image the continuum and compare stellar morphology**: Use non-continuum-subtracted visibilities and the same concatenations; exclude line channels, image continuum, and compare with [CII], HST and VISTA emission.  
  > "These were produced directly from non-continuum subtracted visibility files." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps); "All spectral windows in the band were selected, however the channels found to contain line emission - described above - were excluded." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps); "in order to compare the stellar distribution and obscured star formation to the \[CII\] distribution." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
   - software: CASA, concat, tclean
   - parameter: multifrequency mode; Briggs Robust = 0.5; clean to 2.5×RMS.  
  > "Using `tclean`, with a `multifrequency` mode and a Briggs weighting (Robust = 0.5), a dirty continuum image was produced for each observation." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps); "Using an aperture of an empty background region, the RMS was estimated and cleaning was repeated to 2.5$\times {\rm RMS}$ in order to produce clean continuum maps." (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: RMS measured in an empty background aperture.  
  > "Using an aperture of an empty background region, the RMS was estimated" (Production of moment 0 and 1 maps)
5. **Assess component reliability**: Compute Fidelity(S/N) = 1 − Nneg(S/N)/Npos(S/N), comparing positive and negative sources with comparable or larger connected-pixel counts.  
  > "Fidelity(S/N) = 1 - \frac{N_{neg} (S/N)}{N_{pos} (S/N)}" (Fidelity of Galaxy Components); "we count the number of positive sources of comparable or larger number of connected pixels as the component at a given S/N, and the same is conducted for negative sources" (Fidelity of Galaxy Components)
   - parameter: Minimum detection threshold: S/N where fidelity reaches 60%, for a given connected-pixel count.  
  > "The S/N where this fidelity is equal to 60% is the value that is considered the minimum for a detection to be considered real, for a given number of connected pixels." (Fidelity of Galaxy Components)
   - assumption: Non-Gaussian noise; equal positive and negative peak counts in the absence of detections.  
  > "assuming that the noise is non-Gaussian, the number of positive and negative peaks, above a given flux limit, should be the same, if no detection is present." (Fidelity of Galaxy Components)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: VC8326 main components: 100% fidelity at 4σ; DC8187 [CII] 60% fidelity threshold: S/N 3.6.  
  > "The two components are clearly real components as they correspond to a fidelity of 100% (at the 4 $\sigma$ level)." ([CII] flux analysis — VC8326); "For this observation, the S/N at which the fidelity reaches 60% is 3.6" ([CII] flux analysis — DC8187)
6. **Measure integrated and component line fluxes**: Aperture photometry on moment-0 maps; maximise S/N over aperture radius; manually adjust component apertures and compare fluxes between concatenations.  
  > "Therefore, aperture photometry was conducted on the moment 0 maps" ([CII] flux analysis); "The aperture radius is selected by finding the radius where the S/N is maximised." ([CII] flux analysis); "For flux measurements of features within galaxies (clumps or other components) the apertures were manually adjusted, as they are poorly fit by a 2d Gaussian." ([CII] flux analysis)
   - parameter: HR+MR / HR+MR+LR aperture radii (kpc): DC8737 2.5 / 4.5; VC8326 4.0 / 4.0; DC8187(E) 2.9 / 4; DC8187(C) 2.25 / 4.4; DC8187(W) 1.0 / 1.4.  
  > "| DC8737 | $4.32 \pm 0.27$ | 2.5 | $6.8 \pm 0.26$ | 4.5 | $5.84\pm 0.27$ |
| VC8326 | $2.89\pm 0.36$ | 4.0 | $3.31 \pm 0.29$ | 4.0 | $2.92\pm 0.23$ |" (Table 2); "| DC8187(E) | $3.05 \pm 0.19$ | 2.9 | $3.54\pm 0.23$ | 4 | $2.6\pm 0.20$ |
| DC8187(C) | $4.37 \pm 0.19$ | 2.25 | $5.3 \pm 0.23$ | 4.4 | $4.9\pm 0.30$ |" (Table 2); "| DC8187(W) | $0.74 \pm 0.15$ | 1.0 | $0.95\pm 0.19$ | 1.4 | $0.8\pm 0.20$ |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: DC8187 galaxy apertures do not overlap; component apertures larger than the synthesised beam.  
  > "For DC8187, photometric measurements were conducted individually for each constituent of the merging system (whilst ensuring no overlap between the apertures)." ([CII] flux analysis); "we made sure to ensure that the aperture size was larger than the synthesised beam to ensure an accurate measurement" ([CII] flux analysis)
   - assumption: Noise scaled from single-beam error using the number of synthesised beams per aperture; flux calibration errors excluded.  
  > "The noise was estimated with a consistent method to that used in the ALPINE survey (cite: 2020A&A...643A...2B) by scaling the error associated to a single beam with the number of synthesised beams per aperture." ([CII] flux analysis); "We note that we did not include flux calibration errors - which can amount to 10% of the total flux." ([CII] flux analysis)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian photometry rejected for clumpy or irregular morphologies.  
  > "DC8737, DC8187 and VC8326 have clumpy or irregular morphologies which are poorly fit by a two dimensional Gaussian." ([CII] flux analysis)
   - validation: Using the HR+MR+LR aperture on HR+MR data recovers the HR+MR+LR flux within large errors.  
  > "We emphasise that when the same aperture is used as in the HR+MR+LR concatenation the HR+MR+LR flux is recovered (within the large errors)." ([CII] flux analysis)
7. **Compare gas and obscured-star-formation radial profiles**: Integrate [CII] and continuum in growing circular apertures; convert to molecular-gas and obscured-SFR surface densities and plot against physical aperture scale.  
  > "The radial profiles are obtained integrating the signal (\[CII\] or continuum) within circular apertures of increasing size (step 1 pixel), starting with a circle that has the same radius as the major axis of the beam." (Continuum analysis and radial profiles); "we convert the \[CII\] fluxes, for each aperture, to the molecular gas mass surface density" (Continuum analysis and radial profiles); "The surface density of molecular gas mass and obscured star formation (taken for each aperture) are plotted against the physical scale of the corresponding aperture." (Continuum analysis and radial profiles)
   - parameter: Aperture step: 1 pixel; initial radius equals beam major axis; centred on [CII] barycentre.  
  > "within circular apertures of increasing size (step 1 pixel), starting with a circle that has the same radius as the major axis of the beam." (Continuum analysis and radial profiles); "The profiles are centered on the barycenter of the \[CII\] emission" (Continuum analysis and radial profiles)
   - assumption: Λ-CDM: h0=0.7, ΩΛ=0.3 and Ωm=0.3, as printed.  
  > "This paper assumes a $\Lambda$-CDM cosmology, with $h_0$=0.7, $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.3$ and $\Omega_m=0.3$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: α[CII] = 31 M⊙/L⊙.  
  > "where $\alpha_{[\mathrm{CII}]}$ is the conversion factor between \[CII\] and molecular gas mass (31 $M_\odot$/$L_\odot$, (cite: zanella18))" (Continuum analysis and radial profiles)
   - assumption: 158 μm continuum represents obscured star formation.

## Qualitative findings

- DC8737 contains compact central [CII] emission, an extended halo and fainter clumps; its continuum is aligned but more compact.  
  > "DC8737 shows a very bright and concentrated \[CII\] emission in the center, surrounded by a \[CII\] halo and a few \[CII\] fainter clumps." (Conclusions and summary); "The continuum emission is also aligned with \[CII\] (although much less extended)." (Conclusions and summary)
- VC8326 resolves into two [CII] regions within a broader component; merger versus in-situ star-forming clumps remains unresolved.  
  > "VC8326, on the other hand, is resolved by the high-resolution data presented in this paper into two \[CII\] regions, embedded into an underlying broader component (not as diffuse as the one around DC8737)." (Conclusions and summary); "Understanding whether VC8326 is composed of 2 merging galaxies or rather a single galaxy with 2 in-situ clumps is not trivial." (Discussion — VC8326)
- VC8326 continuum has three components and differs from both its [CII] morphology and the single central UV/optical component.  
  > "the continuum emission is divided into 3 components, one of which is located at the center of the galaxy, near the barycenter of the \[CII\] emission." (Continuum analysis and radial profiles — VC8326); "Both emissions significantly differ from the appearance of the \[CII\] or continuum emission, as they show only a single component well centered on the barycenter of the \[CII\] emission, with no evidence for substructure." (Continuum analysis and radial profiles — VC8326)
- DC8187 has newly resolved [CII] and dust clumps that may be induced by the interaction; the two types of clumps are spatially distinct.  
  > "We revealed \[CII\] and dust bright clumps that were not identified by the low resolution ALPINE data, and that could be induced by the merger activity, as suggested by recent simulations." (Conclusions and summary); "they are not coincident with the \[CII\] clumps" (Discussion — DC8187)
- DC8187(W) is compact and possibly rotating; confirmation is pending.  
  > "Remarkably, we also revealed that the third non-interacting galaxy in DC8187 is a compact object, very likely rotating." (Conclusions and summary); "If confirmed by upcoming JWST NIRSpec-IFU observations, this could be one of the most distant settled disk ever identified." (Conclusions and summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Select bright [CII] systems with >7σ continuum, aligned [CII]/continuum and no HST evidence of late-stage mergers; use high resolution to resolve previously hidden morphology.  
  > "i) the 3 brightest galaxies observed in \[CII\] in the ALPINE survey, ii) they had a strongly detected continuum (\>7-$\sigma$)" (The Sample studied in this paper); "iii) the \[CII\] and continuum appeared aligned in ALPINE observation, and iv) finally the HST images of these galaxies showed no evidence of late stage mergers." (The Sample studied in this paper); "The low resolution severely impacted the ability of ALPINE to trace the properties (i.e. mergers, clumps and components in velocity) in (some of) these galaxies." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Concatenation balances S/N and compact/extended sensitivity; Briggs robust 0.5 balances resolution and sensitivity; irregular morphology motivates aperture photometry.  
  > "This concatenation allows for a better coverage of the uv-plane, allowing the tracing of both extended and compact components of emission." (Observations and Data reduction); "a Briggs weighting (with robust = 0.5) was chosen to best compromise between resolution and sensitivity." (Observations and Data reduction); "DC8737, DC8187 and VC8326 have clumpy or irregular morphologies which are poorly fit by a two dimensional Gaussian." ([CII] flux analysis)
- Alternative: Two-dimensional Gaussian photometry rejected because the sources are clumpy or irregular.  
  > "DC8737, DC8187 and VC8326 have clumpy or irregular morphologies which are poorly fit by a two dimensional Gaussian." ([CII] flux analysis)
- Alternative: PV contours use fractions of peak flux rather than RMS because channel RMS varies.  
  > "The contours were drawn with this method instead of using the RMS as the RMS is variable within each channel." (Position-Velocity Diagrams)
- Alternative: DC8737 broad component may be an outflow or infalling merger; star formation and an AGN are possible outflow drivers.  
  > "a merging object falling onto the central one at that velocity would be observed an additional emission component, offset with respect to the main one." (Outflows and Velocity Groups - DC8737); "The source of this outflow could be the intense star formation identified in the center of DC8737, we cannot rule out the presence of an Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) which could also produce such outflows." (Outflows and Velocity Groups - DC8737)
- Alternative: VC8326 may be two merging galaxies or one galaxy with two in-situ clumps.  
  > "Understanding whether VC8326 is composed of 2 merging galaxies or rather a single galaxy with 2 in-situ clumps is not trivial." (Discussion — VC8326)
- Alternative: VC8326 continuum-only component may be an old gas-exhausted clump or a less massive dust-rich merging component.  
  > "this could after-all be an old clump that exhausted all the gas within it, or a less massive dust-rich merging component." (Discussion — VC8326)
- How they decided it was enough: Clean to 2.5×RMS; choose line ranges and aperture radii by maximum S/N; require at least 60% fidelity for component detections.  
  > "All galaxies were cleaned to a threshold of $2.5\times {\rm RMS}$." (Observations and Data reduction); "The aperture radius is selected by finding the radius where the S/N is maximised." ([CII] flux analysis); "The S/N where this fidelity is equal to 60% is the value that is considered the minimum for a detection to be considered real, for a given number of connected pixels." (Fidelity of Galaxy Components)
- Limitation (authors): Only a fraction of the planned observing time was obtained; requested sensitivity was not reached.  
  > "Objects were observed for 937 s and 1975 s in the C43-5 and C43-3, respectively, only a fraction of the 30 h originally planned for the observations." (Observations and Data reduction); "The new high resolution data, although not reaching the sensitivity that was originally requested, reveal many more details than the original ALPINE data." (Conclusions and summary)

  > "We note that we did not include flux calibration errors - which can amount to 10% of the total flux." ([CII] flux analysis)

  > "these higher resolution concatenations lack the integration time needed to detect the extended components above noise." (Discussion — DC8737)
