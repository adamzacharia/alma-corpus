## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate, continuum-subtract and image archival data**: Calibrate with CASA and the ALMA Pipeline, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, image line cubes and continuum with tclean (Briggs robust 2)  
  > "The data were calibrated using the appropriate version of the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA) software" (3 Data Reduction); "We reconstructed the line cubes and the underlying continuum maps using the CASA task" (3 Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: uvcontsub fitorder=1  
  > "(fitorder=1) to study the line emissions" (3 Data Reduction)
   - parameter: tclean threshold 3 sigma  
  > "with a threshold of $3\:\sigma$" (3 Data Reduction)
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 2  
  > "We used Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 2 (natural weighting), following previous studies" (3 Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **make integrated-intensity images and identify detections**: immoments integrated-intensity images for the 13 galaxies; CO detected in 6  
  > "we generate images of the integrated intensity for the AGN neighborhoods in the 13 galaxies that cover each CO line" (4 Results); "From 6 of them (IC 4296, NGC 315, NGC 1316, NGC 4261, NGC 4374, and NGC 4636) we detected CO emission around their centers." (4 Results)
   - software: CASA immoments
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **measure CO line flux or 3-sigma upper limit**: Extract spectra of the emission regions; derive line flux density from the brightness of the integrated-intensity images (not Gaussian fits); 3-sigma upper limits for non-detections  
  > "Instead, we derived the line flux densities from the brightness of the integrated intensity images" (4 Results); "We extract spectra from the emission regions for the 6 line-detected galaxies" (4 Results)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: IC 4296 channels with prominent absorption ignored  
  > "For IC 4296 we ignore the channels corresponding to the prominent absorption" (4 Results)
   - parameter: Upper-limit line assumed within +-500 km/s of the galaxy velocity  
  > "by assuming that a possible line is within $\pm 500\:\rm km\: s^{-1}$ of the galaxy" (4 Results)
   - fitting: No Gaussian fits adopted because not all spectra are Gaussian  
  > "we did not fit the spectra of the 6 galaxies with one or two Gaussian components, because not all of them are represented by Gaussians" (4 Results)
   - validation: Gaussian-fit and intensity-image methods agree where spectra are Gaussian  
  > "We have confirmed that the two methods (Gaussian fit and intensity image) give consistent results when the spectrum can be well fitted by Gaussians." (4 Results)
4. **convert CO flux to molecular gas mass within 500 pc**: M_mol from S_CO Delta v with the Bolatto et al. 2013 relation, with F_ul excitation correction, X_CO and luminosity distance; 3-sigma upper bounds for non-detections  
  > "we estimate the masses of the molecular gas from the CO intensities" (4.1 Molecular gas mass); "we estimate the upper bounds of the mass from the $3\:\sigma$ upper bounds of $S_{\rm CO}\Delta v$" (4.1 Molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: F_21 = 3.2 (CO(2-1)/CO(1-0)), F_32 = 7.2 (CO(3-2)/CO(1-0))  
  > "$F_{21}$ gives a CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) line ratio on the flux density (Jy) scale of 3.2, and $F_{32}$ gives a CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) ratio of 7.2" (4.1 Molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: Region: central 500 pc  
  > "Following Paper I, we focus on the central 500 pc region because it is well resolved with ALMA" (4 Results)
   - assumption: X_CO = 2e20 cm^-2 (K km/s)^-1 (Milky Way default)  
  > "we adopt a default value of $X_{\rm CO}=2\times 10^{20}\rm\: cm^{-2}(K\: km\: s^{-1})^{-1}$ measured in the Milky Way" (4.1 Molecular gas mass)
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "We assume $H_0=70\rm\: km\: s^{-1}\: Mpc^{-1}$, $\Omega_m=0.3$, and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.7$ in this study." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **derive continuum luminosities L_con and L_1.4**: L_con from ALMA line-free-channel continuum flux, K-corrected to the CO(1-0) frequency with a power law; L_1.4 from literature 1.4 GHz fluxes  
  > "The luminosity is calculated from the continuum flux in the line-free ALMA channels." (4.3 Circumnuclear gas and continuum emission); "We also calculate the continuum luminosity at 1.4 GHz ($L_{\rm 1.4}$) from the observed flux at 1.4 GHz taken from the literature" (4.3 Circumnuclear gas and continuum emission)
   - parameter: reference frequency nu_10 = 115.3 GHz  
  > "rest-frame CO(1-0) line frequency ($\nu_{10}=115.3$ GHz)" (4.3 Circumnuclear gas and continuum emission)
   - assumption: Power-law continuum S_nu proportional to (nu/nu_10)^gamma, gamma = -0.75  
  > "assuming that the continuum emission is represented by a power law of $S_\nu \propto (\nu/\nu_{10})^\gamma$, where $\nu$ is the frequency" (4.3 Circumnuclear gas and continuum emission); "A typical spectral index of $\gamma=-0.75$ is assumed, following Paper I." (4.3 Circumnuclear gas and continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Results said not to depend much on gamma because dust contribution is not significant  
  > "the contribution of dust emission is not significant" (4.3 Circumnuclear gas and continuum emission)
6. **test and fit correlations of M_mol with P_cav, L_con, L_1.4**: Kendall rank correlation on log scales and power-law regression including upper limits, using R package NADA  
  > "The Kendall rank correlation coefficient on the logarithmic scales is $\tau=0.472$ with a $p$-value of $1.9\times 10^{-3}$" (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power); "are calculated for all galaxies including 7 galaxies for which only the upper limit of $M_{\rm col}$ was obtained using the R/CRAN package NADA" (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power)
   - software: R/CRAN NADA
   - parameter: Significance threshold p < 0.01  
  > "which is below the standard threshold of 0.01" (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power)
   - fitting: Power-law regression log(P_cav) = A1 log(M_mol) + B1 (same form for L_con, L_1.4) with censored upper limits; no sampler or priors  
  > "we get $A_1 = 1.3$ and $B_1 = -8.6$" (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power)
   - validation: Checked within 250 pc radius; NCEGs alone tested separately  
  > "This is because no correlation can be found for the 12 NCEGs alone." (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power)
7. **repeat the P_cav-M_mol test within 250 pc**: Recompute M_mol within 250 pc for all NCEGs and 5 BCGs and repeat the correlation test  
  > "We also checked the molecular gas mass within the radius of 250 pc." (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power); "we include all NCEGs and 5 BCGs (NGC 5044, Abell 262, Abell 3581, Abell 2052, and M 87)" (4.2 Circumnuclear gas and jet power)
   - software: R/CRAN NADA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **test whether black hole mass drives jet power**: Correlate literature M_BH with P_cav for all 22 galaxies  
  > "For all 22 galaxies, the correlation coefficient is $\tau = 0.234$, and the $p$-value is 0.13" (5.2 Black hole mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **assess the origin of the mm/submm continuum**: Compare F_con/F_1.4 ratios and overplot a power-law plus modified blackbody SED normalised to NGC 5044; t-test of NCEG vs BCG ratios  
  > "With the exception of NGC 5044, the ratio is less than one, indicating that most of the continuum emission is synchrotron emission from relativistic electrons." (5.3 The origin of the mm/submm continuum emission); "A t-test shows that the ratios of $F_{\rm con}/F_{\rm 1.4}$ for the NCEGs are statistically indistinguishable from those for the BCGs." (5.3 The origin of the mm/submm continuum emission)
   - parameter: gamma = -0.75, nu_0 = 1.5 THz, b = 1.5  
  > "where $\gamma=-0.75$, $\nu_0=1.5$ THz, $b=1.5$" (5.3 The origin of the mm/submm continuum emission)
   - assumption: Dust temperature T = 10 K  
  > "The dust temperature is assumed to be $T=10$ K" (5.3 The origin of the mm/submm continuum emission)
   - fitting: N_AGN and N_BB adjusted by hand to match NGC 5044  
  > "The values for $N_{\rm AGN}$ and $N_{\rm BB}$ are adjusted to match the observations of NGC 5044." (5.3 The origin of the mm/submm continuum emission)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO emission in all 6 detected NCEGs is concentrated within 500 pc, unlike the more extended BCGs of Paper I  
  > "For all 6 galaxies in Figure [2](#fig:image), the emission is concentrated on a scale smaller than $500$ pc." (4 Results)
- In NGC 1316 and NGC 4636 the CO peak is offset from the AGN  
  > "In the case of NGC 1316 and NGC 4636, the peak of the CO is offset from the location of the AGN" (4 Results)
- No CO detected in 7 of 13 galaxies (6 NCEGs + M 87); only upper limits  
  > "we could not detect any cold gas, and only the upper limit of the mass is discussed" (4 Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Archival ALMA CO data for ellipticals with known X-ray-cavity jet powers; not a complete sample  
  > "Since we have simply selected the targets that have been observed with ALMA and for which $P_{\rm cav}$ has been estimated, they are not a complete sample." (Sample Selection)
- Why this method: A fixed 500 pc physical radius avoids a distance-dependent beam bias; Kendall tau used as in Paper I footnote  
  > "Since the physical scale of the region is fixed, the above bias should be less likely to occur." (Introduction); "the Kendall rank correlation coefficient also rejects correlations" (Footnote 4)
- Alternative: Gaussian-component spectral fitting (used in Paper I) was not used because not all spectra are Gaussian  
  > "we did not fit the spectra of the 6 galaxies with one or two Gaussian components, because not all of them are represented by Gaussians" (4 Results)
- Alternative: Mass accretion rate via turbulent viscosity was not discussed  
  > "we do not discuss the mass accretion rate due to turbulent viscosity" (4 Results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Sample is not complete  
  > "they are not a complete sample" (Sample Selection)
- Limitation (authors): X_CO is poorly known for ellipticals; a Milky Way value is adopted  
  > "Since the conversion factor $X_{\rm CO}$ is not well understood for elliptical galaxies" (4.1 Molecular gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): More samples needed for the NCEG correlation  
  > "We need more samples to infer the correlation among the NCEGs." (5.1 Correlations and Scatter)
- Limitation (authors): No M_BH-P_cav correlation may be due to large uncertainties in the data  
  > "This may be due to the large uncertainties in the data" (Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Kendall tau on a sample dominated by BCGs with 7 upper limits; correlation could be driven by distance/luminosity common-factor effects despite the fixed radius
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single Milky Way X_CO and fixed CO line ratios for all galaxies, with a possible systematic offset for ellipticals
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heterogeneous archival beams (0.15-2.7 arcsec) mean the 500 pc aperture is poorly resolved for the most distant targets
- Caveat (inferred, ours): P_cav, M_BH and 1.4 GHz fluxes come from inhomogeneous literature sources with large errors
