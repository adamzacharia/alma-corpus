# A multiwavelength study of multiple spectral component jets in AGN: testing the IC/CMB model for the large-scale-jet X-ray emission

`2023MNRAS.518.3222B` | Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 2023 | first author: Breiding, Peter | arXiv: 2210.13104 | DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stac3081

## Science

- Fields: agn_and_quasars
- Question: Whether the inverse-Compton scattering of the cosmic microwave background (IC/CMB) model can explain the anomalous large-scale X-ray emission in 45 extragalactic Multiple Spectral Component (MSC) AGN jets when tested against Fermi/LAT gamma-ray observations and multi-wavelength SEDs.  
  > "In this work, we test the IC/CMB model in 45 extragalactic X-ray jets using observations from the Fermi Large Area Telescope to look for the expected high level of gamma-ray emission" (abstract); "The primary goal of this study is to assess the viability of the IC/CMB model accounting for the anomalous X-ray emission in resolved (kpc-scale), MSC X-ray jets hosted by AGN." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image radio/sub-mm interferometric data (VLA, ALMA, ATCA) and measure jet knot flux densities**: Calibrate visibilities via CASA pipeline (scriptForPI.py for ALMA) or manual calibration, image with tclean/clean using Briggs robust=0.5 and MTMFS (nterms=2), apply phase and amplitude self-calibration, and extract flux densities or 95% upper limits.  
  > "Typically, the images were made with Briggs weighting and robust parameter of 0.5 (although sometimes a natural weighting was utilized)." (2.1 Very Large Array)
   - software: CASA, tclean, clean, scriptForPI.py, MIRIAD
   - parameter: Briggs weighting robust = 0.5, MTMFS nterms = 2  
  > "Similar to the VLA data, we then imaged the visibility data via the CASA task tclean (with Briggs weighting and robust parameter of 0.5). MTMFS imaging was employed with nterms=2" (2.2 Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA))
   - assumption: 35% uncertainty on measured jet component flux densities (~20% absolute flux calibration, ~15% cross-frequency comparison)  
  > "For any flux densities measured for jet components, we assume very conservative 35% errors on these measured flux densities." (2 DATA ANALYSIS)
   - fitting: Taylor polynomial expansion (nterms=2) during MTMFS deconvolution  
  > "This type of analysis relies on modeling the sky intensity distribution as a Taylor polynomial, expanded about some reference frequency for the subsequent deconvolution and image reconstruction (Rau & Cornwell 2011)." (2.1 Very Large Array)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Reduce HST archival optical/IR/UV imaging and measure jet knot photometry**: Download flat-fielded images processed by calwf3 from MAST, fit and subtract core PSF and host galaxy Sersic models in GALFIT, combine dithered frames with Astrodrizzle, and measure jet flux densities or RMS upper limits.  
  > "As all observations show a bright point source at the jet ‘core’, we performed a point spread function (PSF) subtraction to obtain a measurement of the extended jet. For this purpose, we used the publicly available GALFIT" (2.4 Hubble Space Telescope (HST)); "Finally, we subtracted the best-fit PSF and Sérsic models from the images and combined the dithered PSF-subtracted image files together with Astrodrizzle (Gonzaga et al. 2012)" (2.4 Hubble Space Telescope (HST))
   - software: calwf3, GALFIT, TinyTim, Astrodrizzle
   - parameter: Single Sersic component for host galaxy; nearby stars, TinyTim, or dedicated PSF star for PSF model  
  > "The host galaxies were each fit with single Sérsic components and we used nearby stars, TinyTim, or a dedicated PSF star observation for the PSF models" (2.4 Hubble Space Telescope (HST))
   - assumption: Extinction corrections from Schlafly & Finkbeiner (2011) and aperture corrections from Bohlin (2016)  
  > "Appropriate extinction corrections were applied (Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011), along with the relevant aperture corrections (Bohlin 2016)." (2.4 Hubble Space Telescope (HST))
   - fitting: 2D PSF and single Sersic galaxy model fitting in GALFIT  
  > "The host galaxies were each fit with single Sérsic components and we used nearby stars, TinyTim, or a dedicated PSF star observation for the PSF models" (2.4 Hubble Space Telescope (HST))
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Reprocess Chandra X-ray observations and fit X-ray knot spectra**: Reprocess events with chandra_repro (0.4-8 keV), filter flares >2 sigma above mean, align and merge multi-epoch observations with merge_obs, sub-pixel bin by 0.2, extract spectra with spectract, and fit absorbed power-law in SHERPA.  
  > "Briefly, we performed the initial reprocessing with the chandra_repro task in CIAO using calibration data from CALDB v4.9.4 and discarded events outside the range of 0.4-8 keV." (2.5 Chandra); "We extracted the spectra for each source using the spectract task in CIAO and fit an absorbed power-law model in SHERPA (Freeman et al. 2001) for events falling in the 0.4-8 keV range" (2.5 Chandra)
   - software: CIAO, chandra_repro, merge_obs, spectract, SHERPA, WebPIMMS, SAOImageDS9
   - parameter: Energy range 0.4-8 keV, flare cut > 2 sigma, image binning 0.2 ACIS-S pixel, spectral binning 1 ct/bin  
  > "Finally, we binned the final events file by a factor of 0.2 (1/5th native ACIS-S pixel) to produce a sub-pixel image for each source." (2.5 Chandra); "We binned the spectra to 1 ct/bin and used Nelder-Mead optimization method with WStat statistic to perform the fitting." (2.5 Chandra)
   - assumption: Galactic hydrogen absorption column density fixed to reference sky location value from WebPIMMS  
  > "the hydrogen absorption column density was fixed to the reference value based on sky location 3 ." (2.5 Chandra)
   - fitting: Absorbed power-law fit using Nelder-Mead optimization with WStat statistic  
  > "We binned the spectra to 1 ct/bin and used Nelder-Mead optimization method with WStat statistic to perform the fitting." (2.5 Chandra)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Extract Fermi/LAT gamma-ray flux upper limits or minimum steady fluxes using recombined light curves**: Perform binned likelihood analysis in 5 energy bands over 100 MeV-100 GeV. For non-4FGL sources, compute 95% upper limits over the full dataset. For 4FGL sources, bin into 3-week GTI light curves, sort bins from lowest to highest TS, and contiguously recombine bins to find minimum flux/upper limits.  
  > "After obtaining converged fits with the maximum likelihood optimizations, we determined upper limits (or lowest flux states) for the large-scale-jet gamma-ray fluxes from our sources in the five energy bands: 100 MeV−300 MeV, 300 MeV−1 GeV, 1 GeV−3 GeV, 3 GeV−10 GeV, and 10 GeV−100 GeV." (2.6 Fermi); "Subsequently, the Test Statistic 5 (TS, roughly equivalent to 𝜎 2) of our sources were computed for each time bin, and we recombined the data from lowest to highest TS (one time bin at a time)." (2.6 Fermi)
   - software: Fermi science tools, make4FGLxml.py, gtobssim
   - parameter: ROI = 10 deg, energy = 100 MeV-100 GeV, zenith cut = 90 deg, event class = 128, event type = 3, 30 log energy bins, 0.2 deg spatial bins, 3-week GTI time bins, detection threshold TS = 10  
  > "Fermi/LAT event and spacecraft data were extracted using a 10 ◦ region of interest (ROI), an energy cut of 100 MeV−100 GeV, a zenith angle cut of 90 ◦, and the recommended event class and type for point source analysis (128 and 3, respectively)." (2.6 Fermi); "a binned counts map was made with 30 logarithmically-spaced energy bins and 0.2 degree spatial bins." (2.6 Fermi)
   - assumption: Galactic diffuse model gll_iem_v07.fits, isotropic template iso_P8R3_SOURCE_V3_v1.txt, photon index fixed at 2 for non-4FGL point sources  
  > "Additionally, the galactic diffuse emission model, gll_iem_v07.fits, and recommended isotropic diffuse emission model for point sources, iso_P8R3_SOURCE_V3_v1.txt, were used for the analyses." (2.6 Fermi)
   - fitting: Binned maximum likelihood optimization  
  > "After obtaining converged fits with the maximum likelihood optimizations, we determined upper limits (or lowest flux states)" (2.6 Fermi)
   - validation: Simulated light curves with gtobssim (based on PKS 1150+497) verified that TS-ordered bin recombination does not violate the simulated steady IC/CMB flux level.  
  > "The results from our light curve simulation shown in Figure A1 clearly show that, in general, the combined bin flux density/upper limit minima do not violate the steady state IC/CMB flux simulation level." (A1 Robustness of Recombined Light Curve Constraints)
5. **Construct broadband SEDs, fit synchrotron and IC/CMB models, and derive Doppler and Lorentz factor upper limits**: Fit phenomenological power-law with exponential cutoff models by eye to radio-to-optical synchrotron SEDs, shift in frequency and luminosity via IC/CMB equations to match X-ray flux, compare predicted GeV flux against Fermi limits, and calculate equipartition magnetic fields B_eq, Doppler factor limits delta, and kpc-scale Lorentz factor limits Gamma_max,kpc.  
  > "Phenomenological synchrotron spectral models are fit by eye to the observed radio-to-optical flux densities using single power laws" (3 RESULTS); "These 𝛿 limits are determined by the requirement that the IC/CMB spectra not violate the Fermi flux density limits, and assume equipartition magnetic fields for the emission regions" (3 RESULTS)
   - parameter: gamma_min = 10 for EED in equipartition calculation  
  > "7 For these measurements we assume the EED 𝛾 min = 10 and estimate radio jet volumes and flux densities from our images." (3 RESULTS)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with H0 = 67.74 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_lambda = 0.69, Omega_m = 0.31; equipartition magnetic field; kpc-scale jet viewing angle theta < theta_max from pc-scale VLBI  
  > "Throughout this paper we adopt a ΛCDM cosmology, with H 0 = 67.74 km s −1 Mpc −1, Ω 𝜆 = 0.69, and Ω 𝑚 = 0.31 (Planck Collaboration et al. 2016)." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Furthermore, we assume the jet angle to the line of sight for the kpc-scale jet is less than 𝜃 max." (4.5 Evidence For Jet Deceleration)
   - fitting: By-eye phenomenological fitting of Eq. A1 and analytical IC/CMB shifting via Eqs. A2 and A3  
  > "Phenomenological synchrotron spectral models are fit by eye to the observed radio-to-optical flux densities using single power laws" (3 RESULTS)
   - validation: Evaluated sensitivity of Gamma_max,kpc limits to magnetic field strengths a factor of two out of equipartition.  
  > "We additionally give error bars on Γ max, kpc representing the Γ max, kpc limits corresponding to a magnetic field strength a factor of two out of equipartition." (4.5 Evidence For Jet Deceleration)
6. **Decompose radio spectra, compute orientation indicators, and perform sample correlation and beaming analyses**: Decompose NED radio spectra into core and lobe components to compute R_CE, L_ext, L_kin, and orientation indicator OI; perform 10,000-trial bootstrap Spearman rank correlation tests and OLS linear regression on (alpha_r, alpha_x) and (log R_x, log R_CE); compare with single-zone and two-zone spine-sheath beaming models.  
  > "Following the methodology outlined in Meyer et al. (2011) and Keenan et al. (2021) and using data obtained from the NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database (NED), we also decomposed the radio spectra into the extended (isotropic) “lobe” components and beamed “core” components" (3 RESULTS); "Following the methodology outlined in Curran (2014), we determine empirical distributions of 𝜌 s by performing 10,000 trials of bootsrapping with perturbation on the (𝛼 r, 𝛼 x) data pairs." (4.3 X-ray & Radio Spectral Indexes)
   - parameter: 10,000 bootstrap trials, significance threshold p-value < 0.05, well-measured spectral index error threshold < 0.4  
  > "In gray we plot the indexes with errors > 0.4, where the colored indexes have both radio and X-ray spectral index errors < 0.4 and we consider these the well-measured indexes." (4.3 X-ray & Radio Spectral Indexes); "In this case, failing the significance test is defined as having a p-value > 0.05 for a one-tailed Student’s t-test" (4.3 X-ray & Radio Spectral Indexes)
   - assumption: Core spectral index = 0, large-scale jet radio spectral index = 0.7, initial jet alignment = 5 degrees for misalignment curves  
  > "This fiducial jet is assumed to be at an initial alignment of 5 degrees to the line of sight and connected points are spaced by 5 degrees." (4.4 X-ray & Radio Beaming Patterns); "These misalignment curves assume a flat radio core spectral index of 0, a radio spectral index for the observed large-scale-jet radio emission of 0.7" (4.4 X-ray & Radio Beaming Patterns)
   - fitting: Non-parametric Spearman's rank-order correlation with Student's t-test and Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) linear regression  
  > "we fit ordinary least squares (OLS) linear regression lines to both the ruled-out and unconfirmed subsamples in the 10,000 bootstrapped trials." (4.4 X-ray & Radio Beaming Patterns)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- No evidence of a steady IC/CMB gamma-ray plateau signature was found in any of the 23 Fermi-detected 4FGL MSC jets analyzed.  
  > "Following the methodology presented in section 2.6, we searched for the plateau signature in all 23 of the Fermi-detected MSC jets which are members of the 4FGL catalog. We did not find evidence for such a gamma-ray signature in any of the Fermi/LAT observations of the AGN presented here." (3 RESULTS)
- There is no significant difference in kinetic jet power (L_kin) between the IC/CMB ruled-out and unconfirmed jet subsamples, whereas the unconfirmed subsample shows a skew toward higher orientation indicator (OI) alignment.  
  > "We discern no significant difference in the ruled-out versus unconfirmed jets in terms of L kin." (4.2 Jet Alignment & Kinetic Powers); "Interestingly, we do find support for the unconfirmed sources showing some skew towards more aligned jets than the ruled-out jets when comparing the OI histograms." (4.2 Jet Alignment & Kinetic Powers)
- Radio and X-ray spectral indexes of the large-scale jet knots show no significant positive correlation, suggesting distinct particle populations produce the radio and X-ray emission.  
  > "Interestingly, the correlation tests between 𝛼 r and 𝛼 x for all subsets considered overwhelmingly fail to find a significant correlation. This result suggests that distinct particle populations are responsible for the observed radio and X-ray emission in these jets" (4.3 X-ray & Radio Spectral Indexes)
- Log(R_x) and log(R_CE) show a positive correlation with a much shallower slope than predicted by the single-zone IC/CMB model, consistent instead with two-zone spine-sheath models where X-rays originate in a faster spine and radio in a slower sheath.  
  > "It is clear that the observed correlation between log (R x) and log (R CE) is much less steep than the IC/CMB model predictions" (4.4 X-ray & Radio Beaming Patterns); "As is evident from Figure 5, both the 2nd synchrotron and “two-zone” IC/CMB models of panels III and IV can account for the more mildly beamed X-rays in a manner which is consistent with the observed correlation." (4.4 X-ray & Radio Beaming Patterns)
- Comparison of pc-scale VLBI lower limits on bulk-flow Lorentz factors with kpc-scale upper limits from Fermi constraints indicates that FR II quasar jets decelerate significantly from pc to kpc scales and are at most mildly relativistic on kpc scales.  
  > "The Γ limits plotted all fall well below this line, indicating that the jets are at most mildly relativistic on the kpc scale and are experiencing significant deceleration from pc to kpc scales." (4.5 Evidence For Jet Deceleration)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA and HST observations are used to constrain the high-frequency turnover of the radio-to-optical synchrotron spectrum, which directly determines the predicted MeV-to-GeV IC/CMB gamma-ray flux tested against Fermi/LAT observations.  
  > "In this work, we test the IC/CMB model in 45 extragalactic X-ray jets using observations from the Fermi Large Area Telescope to look for the expected high level of gamma-ray emission, utilizing observations from the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) and the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) when possible to best constrain the predicted gamma-ray flux." (abstract)
- Why this method: Recombining Fermi/LAT light curve time bins from lowest to highest TS isolates periods when the bright, variable sub-pc quasar core is quiescent, providing the deepest possible integration on any steady gamma-ray emission from the kpc-scale jet.  
  > "The general principle behind this method is to utilize data from periods when the bright and variable component is in its most quiescent states, allowing for the longest integration times when the core is not producing a high level of gamma-ray emission and one has a better chance of detecting the steady and low level of IC/CMB gamma-ray flux." (2.6 Fermi)
- Alternative: Synchrotron radiation from a second, higher-energy (multi-TeV) electron energy distribution accelerated in situ  
  > "These electrons would be much more energetic than the ones producing the observed radio-to-optical synchrotron spectrum (multi-TeV versus MeV-to-GeV energies)." (4.1 Alternative Models for the X-ray Emission)
- Alternative: Hadronic emission models (direct relativistic proton synchrotron or synchrotron from secondary electrons)  
  > "Alternatively, it is possible the anomalous X-rays originate via hadronic emission processes, where the X-rays result either from direct (relativistic) proton synchrotron radiation, or from the synchrotron radiation produced by secondary electrons" (4.1 Alternative Models for the X-ray Emission)
- Alternative: Two-zone spine-sheath IC/CMB and second-synchrotron models  
  > "In panels II-IV of Figure 5, we consider two-zone models for the large-scale jet in which there is a fast-flowing jet spine surrounded by a slower-moving jet sheath (e.g., Sol et al. 1989; Celotti et al. 2001)." (4.4 X-ray & Radio Beaming Patterns)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 'unconfirmed' sample may still contain non-IC/CMB X-ray jets where high-frequency radio/sub-mm/optical data or Fermi integration time are currently insufficient to rule out the IC/CMB model, potentially washing out sample differences.  
  > "When comparing the “unconfirmed” sample to the “ruled out” sample, it is important to stress that the “unconfirmed” sample may still contain X-ray jets which are not truly IC/CMB X-ray jets but for which we do not have sufficient data to rule out the IC/CMB hypothesis." (3 RESULTS)
- Limitation (authors): Nine of the 45 jets do not have sufficient multi-wavelength constraints to definitively rule out a single synchrotron spectrum extending from radio to X-rays.  
  > "The remaining nine jets do not have enough constraints to rule out a single-synchrotron spectrum from radio to X-rays but have been modeled as IC/CMB in the literature previously." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Limitation (authors): Upper limits on kpc-scale Doppler and Lorentz factors rely on assuming equipartition magnetic fields and that the jet does not bend significantly between pc and kpc scales.  
  > "These sources could only remain highly relativistic if they are very far from equipartition, which would greatly increase the energy content in these jets." (4.5 Evidence For Jet Deceleration); "Furthermore, we assume the jet angle to the line of sight for the kpc-scale jet is less than 𝜃 max. This assumption is reasonable allowing that the jet does not significantly bend in the plane of observation." (4.5 Evidence For Jet Deceleration)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Phenomenological synchrotron SED fits are performed 'by eye' rather than through formal statistical optimization, introducing subjective choices in turnover frequency (nu_1) and cutoff steepness (beta) where sub-mm/optical detections are missing.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a flat 35% flux uncertainty across all radio/sub-mm interferometric measurements is a coarse approximation for missing flux due to uv-coverage differences across arrays and bands.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating the GHz radio spectral index down to tens-to-hundreds of MHz (which corresponds to the electrons upscattering the CMB into X-rays) ignores potential low-frequency spectral curvature.
