## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate visibilities and extract normalized absorption spectra**: CASA and ALMA pipeline calibration; dirty map without CLEAN; brightest QSO peak divided by the continuum to obtain normalized spectra; archival calibrator data re-flagged and recalibrated  
  > "and ALMA data reduction pipeline were used to carry through the first part of data reduction, including corrections for baseline, system temperature, and water vapor fluctuations." (2.1 CO absorption data from ALMA); "no deconvolution using the CLEAN method was performed" (2.1 CO absorption data from ALMA); "The brightest peak of the QSO was extracted and divided by the continuum intensity to obtain a normalized profile." (2.1 CO absorption data from ALMA)
   - software: CASA, ALMA data reduction pipeline
   - parameter: Natural weighting  
  > "Natural weighting was selected to maximize the continuum sensitivity." (2.1 CO absorption data from ALMA)
   - parameter: Flags on absorption and atmospheric channels removed in archival calibrator data by a script  
  > "We thus generated a script to remove the flags in those parts to include them in our analysis." (2.2 Absorption data from ALMA calibration data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify detected and searched species**: Line identification against CDMS, JPL and SLAIM catalogs with attention to hyperfine structure and contamination; partition functions from CDMS or direct sums  
  > "The line identification was carried out with the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS)" (3.1.1 Detected species); "Careful removal of the effects of the contamination by other species and other hyperfine components was also done." (2.2 Absorption data from ALMA calibration data)
   - software: CDMS, JPL catalog, SLAIM
   - parameter: Q_CMB from CDMS or by summing states, with T_CMB = 2.73 K  
  > "we either referenced value from CDMS or summed up the states with" (3.1.1 Detected species); "is the cosmic microwave background temperature (2.73 K)" (3.1.1 Detected species)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Atmospheric and instrumental interference lines tabulated and excluded (Table 5)  
  > "Instrumental and atmospheric artifacts are also visible at some frequencies." (3.1.3 Possible interference)
3. **Define velocity components and classify host clouds**: Components identified from CO, 13CO and HCO+ absorption plus NRO CO emission (24 intervals, Table 6); cloud types from column density and FUGIN channel maps; R_G from velocity; arm association from l-v diagram  
  > "We identified velocity components along this line of sight based on the absorption profiles in the CO," (3.2.1 Overall characteristics of velocity components and species); "We classified the molecular clouds based on the following criteria:" (3.2.2 Characteristics of the parent clouds)
   - parameter: R0 = 8.5 kpc and flat rotation curve at 220 km/s for Galactocentric distance  
  > "to be 8.5 kpc and the flat rotation curve at 220" (3.2.2 Characteristics of the parent clouds)
   - assumption: Spiral arm positions from Reid et al. 2016  
  > "based on the identification of spiral arms by (cite: 2016ApJ...823...77R)" (3.2.2 Characteristics of the parent clouds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive column densities and abundances from integrated opacity**: Column density from integral of tau, assuming optically thin lines and LTE; Tex set to T_CMB for most species; abundances relative to N(H2) = 2e21 cm^-2 for Components 7 and 16  
  > "by assuming optically thin lines and the LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium) conditions" (3.1.2 Marginal and non-detection); "the column density was obtained by assuming LTE for optically thin lines and that the excitation temperature is the CMB temperature." (3.6 Chemical compositions); "by assuming $N$(H$_2$) to be $2 \times 10^{21}$ cm$^{-2}$ for Components 7 and 16." (3.1.2 Marginal and non-detection)
   - parameter: 1 sigma upper limits for undetected species; narrower velocity ranges used for CN, HCN and HNC to limit hyperfine blending  
  > "narrower velocity ranges were set to minimize the blending by the other hyperfine components of CN and HCN" (3.6.1 CN-bearing molecules)
   - assumption: Optically thin LTE; Tex = T_CMB for most species  
  > "the column density was obtained by assuming LTE for optically thin lines and that the excitation temperature is the CMB temperature." (3.6 Chemical compositions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive CO and 13CO excitation temperatures and check emission-based Tex**: Tex from the ratio of integrated tau(2-1) to tau(1-0) via an analytic LTE solution without the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation; compared with CO brightness temperatures from NRO 45 m; NRO 45 m HCO+, HCN, HNC, C2H emission searched to constrain Tex of dense-gas tracers  
  > "we finally get the analytical solution of" (4.1 Excitation temperature through multi-transition study of CO absorption); "To check the validity of this method toward general Galactic disk clouds, the CO excitation temperatures obtained through the excitation analysis of the absorption lines were compared with the CO brightness temperatures." (4.2 Validity of CO excitation temperature estimated from CO main beam antenna temperature)
   - parameter: E_J=1/kB = 5.53 K for CO and 5.29 K for 13CO; nu(2-1) ~ 2 nu(1-0); S(2,1) ~ 2 S(1,0)  
  > "Here, $E_{J=0}/k_{\rm B}$ = 0 K" (4.1 Excitation temperature through multi-transition study of CO absorption)
   - assumption: LTE with constant Tex for all transitions  
  > "remains constant for all transitions" (4.1 Excitation temperature through multi-transition study of CO absorption)
   - assumption: Rayleigh-Jeans approximation not valid at these low Tex  
  > "However, the excitation temperature is quite small for the current absorption study and the assumption for the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation" (4.1 Excitation temperature through multi-transition study of CO absorption)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Absorption Tex compared with CO brightness temperature: absorption values tend to be higher, explained by opacity  
  > "The opacity explains this discrepancy because the excitation analysis of the CO absorption lines can only be done when the lines are not saturated" (4.2 Validity of CO excitation temperature estimated from CO main beam antenna temperature)
6. **Estimate total H2 column density along the sightline**: Six estimates compared: Planck A_V with gas-to-extinction ratio, CO J=1-0 integrated intensity with X_CO, optically thin LTE 13CO emission, CO absorption, HCO+ absorption, c-C3H2 absorption  
  > "We estimated the total molecular gas column density toward this line of sight using several different means." (4.4 Total amount of molecular gas along the line of sight)
   - parameter: X(13CO) = 2e-6 and standard Galactic CO-to-H2 factor  
  > "as a canonical value for Galactic disk clouds (cite: 2008ApJ...679..481P)" (3.2.2 Characteristics of the parent clouds)
   - assumption: Universal HCO+ and c-C3H2 abundances relative to H2 taken from the literature  
  > "can be converted to $N$(H$_2$) by assuming the universal HCO$^+$ abundance" (4.4 Total amount of molecular gas along the line of sight)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Estimates agree within about (0.6-2)e22 cm^-2 except A_V and CO-absorption based values  
  > "All these estimations of total molecular column density are in reasonable agreement with" (4.4 Total amount of molecular gas along the line of sight)
7. **Resolve velocity substructure of CO absorption**: Simultaneous multiple-Gaussian fit of CO J=1-0 and J=2-1 (13CO for saturated components) with shared centre velocity and width for both transitions  
  > "simultaneous multiple-Gaussian fitting was done with the CO $J$=1–0 and $J$=2–1 absorption profiles by assuming the same center velocity" (4.5 Multiple-Gaussian line fit of CO absorption)
   - parameter: Saturated CO absorbers not fitted in CO; 13CO used instead  
  > "Gaussian fitting was not possible for these saturated absorption features" (4.2 Physical conditions and CO abundance of substructures)
   - fitting: Multi-Gaussian least-squares style fit reported with reduced chi-square; optimizer, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "The overall fitting was quite satisfactory, and the corresponding reduced $\chi^2$ ranged from 0.54 to 1.17." (4.5 Multiple-Gaussian line fit of CO absorption)
   - validation: Reduced chi-square 0.54-1.17; fitted profiles compared with CO emission profiles  
  > "The overall fitting was quite satisfactory, and the corresponding reduced $\chi^2$ ranged from 0.54 to 1.17." (4.5 Multiple-Gaussian line fit of CO absorption)
8. **Infer density and CO abundance of subcomponents with non-LTE models**: ndradex/RADEX grids of Tex and tau versus n(H2) and X(CO)/(dv/dr) at fixed Tk; solution at the crossing of constant-Tex and constant-tau lines; only subcomponents with tau(1-0) <~ 1  
  > "Here we deduce the physical conditions and CO abundance of some of the substructures identified by absorption measurements with the aid of" (4.2 Physical conditions and CO abundance of substructures)
   - software: ndradex, RADEX
   - parameter: Tk = 50 K  
  > "Instead, we assume $T_{\rm k}$ = 50 K suggested from the large HCN/HNC ratio toward these velocity components throughout this subsection." (4.2 Physical conditions and CO abundance of substructures)
   - assumption: Canonical X(CO) not assumed; velocity gradient dv/dr degenerate with X(CO), reported as X(CO)/(dv/dr)  
  > "We thus do not assume the canonical $X$(CO) value in the present analysis." (4.2 Physical conditions and CO abundance of substructures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Estimate sizes of substructures from filling factors**: Compare observed NRO 45 m main-beam temperature with the synthesized temperature f(1-exp(-tau))(Tex-T_CMB) computed from absorption; deficits imply f < 1 and compact size  
  > "the sizes of these subcomponents can be estimated as the filling factor of the individual subcomponent within the single-dish beam through a comparison of the actual main beam antenna temperature from single-dish emission-line observations with the" (4.3 Sizes of substructures)
   - parameter: NRO 45 m beam of 15 arcsec and distance 5 kpc  
  > "or $<$0.1 pc at 5 kpc" (4.3 Sizes of substructures)
   - assumption: f = 1 for subcomponents consistent with the synthesized temperature  
  > "by assuming $f$ = 1" (4.3 Sizes of substructures)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO and 13CO absorption lines are much sparser than emission and show discrete velocity components  
  > "Both the CO and $^{13}$CO absorption lines are much more sparse than their corresponding emission lines and show discrete velocity components." (3.2.1 Overall characteristics of velocity components and species)
- HCN/HNC ratio differs strongly between diffuse absorbing clouds (high) and dark cloud cores (about 1)  
  > "On the other hand, the HCN/HNC ratio for dark cloud cores is about 1, indicating that the ratios are very different." (3.6.1 CN-bearing molecules)
- HNC abundance over HCO+ decreases at low N(HCO+), attributed to selective dissociation of HNC  
  > "This may be attributed to the selective dissociation of HNC in diffuse molecular gas." (3.6.1 CN-bearing molecules)
- C2H and c-C3H2 are tightly and linearly correlated at higher column densities  
  > "A tight linear relationship was observed in this study, at least for higher column density components" (3.6.3 Hydrocarbons)
- Some components show CO emission and HCO+ absorption without CO absorption, most prominently near 68 km/s  
  > "The most prominent case is found near 68 km s" (3.2.1 Overall characteristics of velocity components and species)
- One velocity component shows absorption of SO, SiO and CS (shock tracers)  
  > "In this study, one of the velocity components showed absorption of SO, SiO, and CS." (3.6.6 CS, SO, and SiO)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Bright QSOs are used as background sources for absorption studies; this sightline has one of the largest visual extinctions among such sources and high-velocity-resolution CO data  
  > "is one of such sources with the largest visual extinction" (Introduction); "Bright QSOs can be used as background sources for such studies with radio interferometers" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Absorption sampling is not biased to dense gas the way emission is  
  > "In contrast, absorption line studies equally sample dense and diffuse gas through a narrow pencil beam toward background sources." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Rayleigh-Jeans approximation for the rotation diagram rejected because Tex is too low; analytic non-approximate Tex used instead  
  > "However, the excitation temperature is quite small for the current absorption study and the assumption for the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation" (4.1 Excitation temperature through multi-transition study of CO absorption)
- Alternative: Canonical X(CO) rejected for the RADEX analysis because it cannot reproduce the low tau  
  > "We thus do not assume the canonical $X$(CO) value in the present analysis." (4.2 Physical conditions and CO abundance of substructures)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Archival calibrator data have poorer angular resolution, frequency resolution and sensitivity than the dedicated CO data  
  > "the angular resolution, frequency resolution, and sensitivity were not as good as those of the CO data." (2.2 Absorption data from ALMA calibration data)
- Limitation (authors): Some components heavily contaminated by other lines were excluded for CN, HCN and C2H  
  > "Nevertheless, some velocity components were heavily contaminated by the other lines and were not included in the study for CN, HCN, and C" (2.2 Absorption data from ALMA calibration data)
- Limitation (authors): Narrower line components may exist but SNR prevents identification  
  > "the limited signal-to-noise ratio prevented us from further identification." (4.5 Multiple-Gaussian line fit of CO absorption)
- Limitation (authors): H2CO only marginally detected because of limited sensitivity  
  > "Although the sensitivity is limited, we see marginal detection of H$_2$CO in Components 7 and 16." (3.6.8 H2CO and C)
- Limitation (authors): Atomic carbon results limited by archive SNR  
  > "though the poor signal-to-noise ratio of the archived data prevents further study." (3.6.8 H2CO and C)
- Limitation (authors): N(H2) from 13CO emission in diffuse gas is highly uncertain and often severely underestimated  
  > "is highly doubtful and is often severely underestimated" (Appendix Reliability of H2 column density)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heavy reliance on optically thin LTE with Tex = T_CMB for non-CO species; no per-species uncertainty on this assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): N(HCO+) used as the H2 proxy with a single abundance; CO-poor conclusions depend on it and on circular use of N(HCO+) on both axes of CO/HCO+
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extraction of the normalized spectrum from a dirty-map peak is not described in enough detail to check systematics (continuum level, beam dilution, resolved QSO structure)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Narrow/broad component split is admitted to be unclear (line width histogram not bimodal) yet used as a headline result
- Caveat (inferred, ours): RADEX results depend on fixed Tk = 50 K and degenerate X(CO)/(dv/dr); inconsistent 4e-6 in text versus 2.6e-6 in the table for Subcomponent 5-1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Many correlation conclusions rest on a mix of this work and heterogeneous literature data
