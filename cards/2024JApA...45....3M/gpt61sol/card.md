## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure continuum emission**: Create continuum maps with TCLEAN/HOGBOM, correct the primary beam with IMPBCOR, and fit 2D Gaussians with IMFIT.  
  > "We created a continuum emission map of G10 and G31 using the CASA task `TCLEAN` with `HOGBOM` deconvolver." (Observations and data reductions); "Finally, we used the CASA task `IMPBCOR` to correct the primary beam pattern for both the continuum and spectral data cubes." (Observations and data reductions); "After creating the continuum emission map of G10 and G31, we fitted the 2D Gaussian over the continuum emission images of G10 and G31 using the CASA task `IMFIT`." (Continuum emission towards the G10 and G31)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN, IMPBCOR, IMFIT
   - parameter: Continuum frequencies: G10 140.92 GHz; G31 140.90 GHz.  
  > "We have shown the millimeter-wavelength continuum emission images of hot core G10 and G31 at frequencies of 140.92 GHz and 140.90 GHz" (Continuum emission towards the G10 and G31)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting with IMFIT  
  > "After creating the continuum emission map of G10 and G31, we fitted the 2D Gaussian over the continuum emission images of G10 and G31 using the CASA task `IMFIT`." (Continuum emission towards the G10 and G31)
   - validation: Compare continuum emission-region sizes with synthesized beams; both are unresolved.  
  > "We find that the continuum emission regions of G10 and G31 are smaller than the synthesized beam size" (Continuum emission towards the G10 and G31); "It indicates that the continuum emission maps of G10 and G31 are not resolved at frequencies of 140.92 GHz and 140.90 GHz, respectively." (Continuum emission towards the G10 and G31)
2. **Extract continuum-subtracted spectra**: Separate targets with MSTRANSFORM, subtract continuum with UVCONTSUB, create TCLEAN spectral cubes, apply IMPBCOR, and extract spectra from circular regions.  
  > "We used task `MSTRANSFORM` with all available rest frequencies to separate the target data of G10 and G31 after the initial data reduction." (Observations and data reductions); "We used the task `UVCONTSUB` in the UV plane of the separated calibrated data of G10 and G31 for continuum subtraction." (Observations and data reductions); "At first, we extract the molecular spectra of G10 and G31 from the spectral data cubes to create 23.55$^{\prime\prime}$ and 34.02$^{\prime\prime}$ diameter circular regions over G10 and G31." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - software: CASA, MSTRANSFORM, UVCONTSUB, TCLEAN, IMPBCOR
   - parameter: Circular extraction diameters: G10 23.55″; G31 34.02″.  
  > "At first, we extract the molecular spectra of G10 and G31 from the spectral data cubes to create 23.55$^{\prime\prime}$ and 34.02$^{\prime\prime}$ diameter circular regions over G10 and G31." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - parameter: VLSR: G10 68.50 km s−1; G31 97.0 km s−1.  
  > "The systematic velocity ($V_{LSR}$) of the G10 and G31 is 68.50 km s$^{-1}$ and 97.0 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: rof11; suz16)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify PN and fit its column density**: Fit LTE PN model spectra from CDMS using MCMC in CASSIS.  
  > "To detect the rotational emission line of PN in the spectra of G10 and G31, we used the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) model spectra with the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS) (cite: mu05)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31); "For fitting the LTE model over the observed spectra of PN, we use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) in CASSIS." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - software: CASSIS, CDMS
   - parameter: PN J = 3–2 rest frequency without hyperfine structure: 140967.684 MHz.  
  > "Here, we use the rest frequency of the PN (J = 3–2) line without the hyperfine structure at a frequency of 140967.684 MHz taken from the CDMS and (cite: caz06)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - parameter: G10 source size: 10.78″.  
  > "The best-fit column density of PN towards G10, using the LTE modelled spectra, is (3.60$\pm$0.2)$\times$10$^{13}$ cm$^{-2}$ with an excitation temperature of 150$\pm$25 K and a source size of 10.78$^{\prime\prime}$." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - parameter: Excitation temperature, column density, source size, FWHM, and VLSR can be varied.  
  > "All spectral parameters, such as excitation temperature, column density, source size, FWHM, and V$_{LSR}$, can be varied." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - assumption: Excitation temperature assumed to be 150±25 K for the single PN transition in both hot cores.  
  > "During the estimation of the column density, we assume an excitation temperature of 150$\pm$25 K to estimate the column density of the single transition line of PN." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31); "We use the same excitation temperature for the two hot cores because the gas densities of G10 and G31 are nearly similar (cite: suz16)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - assumption: LTE justified by warm inner-region gas densities of ∼1.2×10^7 cm−3.  
  > "The LTE assumptions are valid in the inner regions of G10 and G31 because the gas density of the warm inner regions of G10 and G31 is $\sim$1.2$\times$10$^{^7}$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: rof11; suz16; min20)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - fitting: MCMC random walk with χ² minimization; several initial random states.  
  > "The MCMC technique uses a random walk to iteratively go over all of the parameters and move into the solution space, making it the ultimate outcome by $\chi^{2}$ minimization." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31); "This algorithm runs with several initial random states" (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - validation: CDMS check finds no blending with nearby molecular lines; hyperfine components remain unresolved.  
  > "we also check for the possible blending effect in the PN emission spectra with nearby molecular transitions using CDMS databases, but we find that the identified emission spectra of PN do not blend with nearby molecular lines." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31); "After the LTE analysis, we find that the hyperfine transitions of PN towards G10 and G31 do not resolve due to insufficient spectral resolution to resolve the hyperfine lines of PN." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
4. **Derive PN fractional abundances**: Estimate PN fractional abundances with respect to H2 using the literature H2 column densities.  
  > "The estimated fractional abundances of PN towards G10 and G31 with respect to H$_{2}$ are 2.76$\times$10$^{-10}$ and 5.68$\times$10$^{-11}$ respectively" (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31); "whereas the column densities of H$_{2}$ towards G10 and G31 are 1.3$\times$ 10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 1.6$\times$ 10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$, respectively, (cite: ik01)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - parameter: H2 column densities: G10 1.3×10^23 cm−2; G31 1.6×10^23 cm−2.  
  > "whereas the column densities of H$_{2}$ towards G10 and G31 are 1.3$\times$ 10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 1.6$\times$ 10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$, respectively, (cite: ik01)." (Identification of PN towards the G10 and G31)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Map PN and estimate emitting sizes**: Create integrated PN maps with IMMOMENTS, overlay continuum maps, and fit 2D Gaussians with IMFIT.  
  > "After detecting the rotational emission line of PN, we produce an integrated emission map of PN with transition J = 3–2 using the CASA task `IMMOMENTS` towards G10 and G31." (Spatial distribution of PN towards the G10 and G31); "The resultant integrated emission maps of PN towards G10 and G31 are overlaid with the 140.92 GHz and 140.90 GHz continuum emission maps of G10 and G31." (Spatial distribution of PN towards the G10 and G31); "we estimate the emitting regions of PN towards G10 and G31 by fitting the 2D Gaussian over the integrated emission maps of PN using the CASA task `IMFIT`." (Spatial distribution of PN towards the G10 and G31)

- PN J = 3–2 is identified in both hot cores; its hyperfine components are unresolved.

  > "We observe that the integrated emission maps of PN towards G10 and G31 had a peak at the continuum position." (Spatial distribution of PN towards the G10 and G31)

  > "This indicates that the J = 3–2 transition line of PN is not spatially resolved or, at best, marginally resolved." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: Select G10 and G31 from the survey because their luminosities are higher than those of the other observed sources.  
  > "We chose only G10 and G31 from that survey data because the luminosities of G10 and G31 are higher than those of other observed sources." (Observations and data reductions)
- Why this method: LTE is justified by high inner-region gas densities; new chemical modelling is motivated by densities higher than those in previous models.  
  > "The LTE assumptions are valid in the inner regions of G10 and G31 because the gas density of the warm inner regions of G10 and G31 is $\sim$1.2$\times$10$^{^7}$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: rof11; suz16; min20)."
