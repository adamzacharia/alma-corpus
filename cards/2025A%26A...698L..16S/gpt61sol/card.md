## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct matched continuum maps**: Clean with mtmfs, nterms = 2, natural weighting and a common uv-range; restore to 0.5″, PB-correct and smooth both maps to 1.0″.  
  > "The final continuum maps were cleaned using `deconvolver` =‘mtmfs’ (`nterms` = 2) and natural weighting" (Observations and data reduction); "The primary beam (PB) correction was applied to all the images." (Observations and data reduction); "To improve the signal-to-noise ratio, both maps have been smoothed to 1.0$''$ angular resolution." (Results)
   - parameter: Common uv-range with inner cutoff >10 kλ; restoring beam 0.5″  
  > "a common uv-range in both bands (inner uv-cutoff: `uvrange` =‘${\rm >10klambda}$’) and a restoring beam of 0.5$''$." (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-calibration improves map dynamic range by ∼5.  
  > "The data were self-calibrated using line-free channels, improving the dynamic range of the maps by a factor $\sim$5." (Observations and data reduction)
2. **Identify dusty outflow cavity walls**: Compare extended continuum emission with red- and blue-shifted CO (2–1) emission.  
  > "Comparison with red- and blue-shifted CO (2–1) emission (2016.1.00209.S; (cite: Cruz-SaenzDeMiera19)) indicates that these continuum structures trace the edges of the cavities" (Results)
   - parameter: Southern continuum structure detected above 6σ to ∼1300 au in B3 and ∼1800 au in B6  
  > "This structure is detected above the 6$\sigma$ level up to $\sim$1300 au (in B3) and $\sim$1800 au (in B6) from L1551 IRS5." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CO comparison associates the continuum structures with the southwest blue and northeast red outflow cavities.  
  > "these continuum structures trace the edges of the cavities opened by the blue- (southwest) and red-shifted (northeast) lobes of the outflow driven by L1551 IRS5" (Results)
3. **Estimate dust temperature and optical depth**: Compute the radial dust-temperature map, then τ = −ln[1 − Iν/Bν(Tdust)] in both bands.  
  > "where $T_{\rm dust}(r) = {\rm 38~K}\times(L_{\rm bol}/\rm L_{\odot})^{0.2}\times(\it r/ \rm 100~{\rm au})^{-0.4}$." (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls); "The continuum opacity can then be computed as $\tau = - {\rm ln}[1 - I_{\nu}/B_\nu$($T_{\rm dust})]$" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - parameter: Lbol ∼27 L⊙  
  > "a total bolometric luminosity $L_{\rm bol}$$\sim$27 L$_\odot$ (for a distance of 147 pc; (cite: Green13))." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Envelope thermal balance dominated by central heating from the accreting protostars  
  > "we assume that the thermal balance of the L1551 IRS5 envelope is dominated by central heating from the accreting protostars." (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - assumption: Distance 147±5 pc  
  > "the source distance of 147$\pm$5 pc ((cite: Connelley18))" (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Both bands give τ ≲0.20, interpreted as optically thin emission.  
  > "The $\tau$ derived at 1.3 and 3.0 mm are always $\lesssim$0.20, implying optically thin emission" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
4. **Derive cavity dust mass and gas density**: Use southern-wall integrated continuum intensity for dust mass and column density; divide by an assumed line-of-sight width for a volume-density lower limit.  
  > "From an integrated intensity of 55 mJy along the southern cavity wall, we infer a mass of dust in the cavity of $M_{\rm dust}$ $\sim$ 58 M$_{\Earth}$." (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls); "Assuming that the length of the cavity wall along the line of sight is equal to its width in B3, $\sim$250 au" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - parameter: Tdust = 30 K, the median along the cavity walls  
  > "we assumed $T_{\rm dust} = 30$ K, corresponding to the median value along the cavity walls" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - parameter: κ1.3mm = 0.9 cm² g−1  
  > "a dust opacity at 1.3 mm, $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm}$= 0.9 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - assumption: Icy-mantled grains at ∼10^6 cm−3  
  > "appropriate for icy-mantled dust grains at a density of $\sim$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: Ossenkopf94)" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - assumption: Dust-to-gas ratio of 100, as written  
  > "Assuming a dust-to-gas ratio of 100" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - assumption: Line-of-sight length equals the B3 wall width, ∼250 au  
  > "Assuming that the length of the cavity wall along the line of sight is equal to its width in B3, $\sim$250 au" (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Assess additional heating and gas-to-dust ratio uncertainty; masses and densities could decrease ∼25-40%.  
  > "the resulting masses and densities may be reduced by $\sim$25-40% compared to those estimated assuming 30 K." (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
5. **Map the millimeter spectral index**: Compute αmm from the logarithmic B3/B6 PB-corrected flux ratio; propagate flux noise and calibration uncertainties.  
  > "\alpha_{\rm mm} = \frac{{\rm log_{10}}F(\nu_{\rm B3}) - {\rm log_{10}}F(\nu_{\rm B6})}{{\rm log_{10}}(\nu_{\rm B3}) - {\rm log_{10}}(\nu_{\rm B6})}" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "$F(\nu_{\rm B3})$ and $F(\nu_{\rm B6})$ are the PB-corrected fluxes in B3 and B6, respectively" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "This error map is generated via the standard propagation of the system calibration uncertainties (5% in Band 3 and 10% in Band 6; (cite: Cortes22)) and the uncertainties in the observed ALMA fluxes" (Additional results)
   - parameter: Representative frequencies: B3 100.6 GHz; B6 225.4 GHz  
  > "$\nu_{\rm B3} = 100.6$ GHz and $\nu_{\rm B6} = 225.4$ GHz are the representative frequencies of the two ALMA Bands." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
   - parameter: Signal-to-noise ratio >3 in both bands  
  > "where emission at both bands is detected with a signal-to-noise ratio $>$3" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
   - parameter: Calibration uncertainties: 5% in B3 and 10% in B6  
  > "the system calibration uncertainties (5% in B3 and 10% in B6; (cite: Cortes22))" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
   - assumption: Thermal free-free contamination not expected to be relevant at mm wavelengths  
  > "thermal free-free contamination is not expected to be relevant at mm wavelengths." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect the propagated αmm error map and spatial consistency; direct cm-wavelength contamination constraints remain future work.  
  > "These values are consistent across the entire structure, and no clear variation is observed for $\alpha_{\rm mm}$ at different positions along the cavity wall." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "future analysis based on cm-wavelengths will provide direct constrains on the fraction of free-free contamination in the L1551 IRS5 cavity walls." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
6. **Infer emissivity indices and radial profiles**: Use βmm = αmm − 2; average temperature, brightness temperature, opacity and indices in circular regions along both cavity walls.  
  > "can be derived as $\beta_{\rm mm} = \alpha_{\rm mm} -2$" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "These profiles were derived by averaging values within circular regions, each with an area equivalent to three resolution beams" (Radial distributions along dusty cavities)
   - parameter: Circular apertures with area equal to three resolution beams  
  > "circular regions, each with an area equivalent to three resolution beams" (Radial distributions along dusty cavities)
   - parameter: Use a 3σ upper limit at 3.0 mm to estimate lower limits on αmm and βmm  
  > "Lower limits on $\alpha_{\rm mm}$ and $\beta_{\rm mm}$ were estimated assuming a 3$\sigma$ upper limit at 3.0 mm where the emission was not detected above this threshold." (Radial distributions along dusty cavities)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and validity of the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation for Tdust = 25–50 K  
  > "dust emission is optically thin at both wavelengths and that the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation ($h\nu/k_{\rm B}T\ll 1$) is always valid for the derived $T_{\rm dust}$, which is in the range 25–50 K." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)

## Qualitative findings

- Extended continuum emission traces the edges of the blue and red outflow cavities.  
  > "these continuum structures trace the edges of the cavities opened by the blue- (southwest) and red-shifted (northeast) lobes of the outflow driven by L1551 IRS5" (Results)
- The northern counterpart is fainter, less extended and detected only at 1.3 mm.  
  > "A second, fainter and less extended counterpart is detected toward the North, visible only at 1.3 mm." (Results)
- No clear spectral-index variation is observed along the cavity wall.  
  > "These values are consistent across the entire structure, and no clear variation is observed for $\alpha_{\rm mm}$ at different positions along the cavity wall." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
- Low βmm strongly suggests mm-sized dust grains along the outflow cavities.  
  > "The low inferred value ($\beta_{\rm mm} < 1$) strongly suggests, for the first time, the presence of mm-sized dust grains along the protostellar outflow cavities." (Conclusions)
- Wind transport from the disc is proposed; subsequent fallback is speculative.  
  > "Our results suggest that these grains have been transported from the inner disc to the envelope by protostellar winds" (abstract); "we can speculate that large grains along the cavity walls may subsequently fall back onto the protostellar disc due to gravity as predicted by (cite: Tsukamoto21)" (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-scale, multi-wavelength, high-sensitivity observations of a well-studied source enable investigation of grain growth from small to envelope scales.  
  > "This makes L1551 IRS5 an excellent candidate to investigate the dust growth process from small to envelope scales, exploiting multi-scale and multi-wavelengths high-sensitivity observations of ALMA-FAUST." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Two-band continuum gives the SED slope; optically thin emission and the assumed Rayleigh-Jeans validity permit βmm = αmm − 2.  
  > "To constrain the maximum size of the grains emitting along the cavity walls, we use the maps of continuum emission in ALMA Band 3 and 6" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "dust emission is optically thin at both wavelengths and that the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation ($h\nu/k_{\rm B}T\ll 1$) is always valid" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "can be derived as $\beta_{\rm mm} = \alpha_{\rm mm} -2$" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
- Alternative: In-situ growth/coagulation in cavity walls versus disc growth followed by outflow/wind entrainment  
  > "the dust growth/coagulation occurs in-situ, within the outflow cavity walls" (Discussion); "large grains grow in the protostellar disc and are entrained into the envelope by outflows/winds." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Additional UV irradiation or slow-shock heating could raise temperatures and reduce inferred masses and densities.  
  > "the temperature in the outflow cavities may be a factor $1.2-1.5$ higher than what estimated in this section, if there is a additional heating due to UV irradiation and/or slow shocks (cite: Visser12; Lee15)." (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls); "the resulting masses and densities may be reduced by $\sim$25-40% compared to those estimated assuming 30 K." (Physical properties of the dusty cavity walls)
- Alternative: Thermal free-free contamination is considered unlikely to matter at mm wavelengths, but is not directly constrained in these walls.  
  > "thermal free-free contamination is not expected to be relevant at mm wavelengths." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "future analysis based on cm-wavelengths will provide direct constrains on the fraction of free-free contamination in the L1551 IRS5 cavity walls." (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls)
- How they decided it was enough: Use >3 signal-to-noise in both bands for the spectral-index map; use 3σ upper limits at 3.0 mm for index lower limits.  
  > "where emission at both bands is detected with a signal-to-noise ratio $>$3" (Dust spectral index in the dusty cavity walls); "Lower limits on $\alpha_{\rm mm}$ and $\beta_{\rm mm}$ were estimated assuming a 3$\sigma$ upper limit at 3.0 mm where the emission was not detected above this threshold." (Radial distributions along dusty cavities)
