- Question: What is the spatial morphology, kinematics, and chemical/physical origin of the sulphur monoxide (SO) emission in the HD 100546 protoplanetary disk, and is it connected to embedded forming giant planets?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, combine, and image continuum and molecular lines (SO, SiO, SiS)**: Combine self-calibrated short-baseline (43C-2) data with pipeline-calibrated long-baseline (43C-5) data, perform phase and amplitude self-calibration, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, concatenate the two SO spectral windows using concat, image continuum and lines across a Briggs robust grid (+1.0, +0.5, 0.0, -0.5) using tCLEAN with multi-scale deconvolver and Keplerian masking, and apply the JvM correction.  
  > "In this work we take these self-calibrated data and combine it with the more recent pipeline calibrated long baseline observations." (ALMA Observations); "The lines were imaged with `tCLEAN` using Briggs robust weighting, the "multi-scale" deconvolver, and a keplerian mask [^1]." (ALMA Observations); "To increase the signal-to-noise in the SO image the two SO spectral window measurement sets were concatenated using CASA task `concat` and imaged together." (ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA, tCLEAN, uvcontsub, concat, keplerian_mask
   - parameter: Briggs robust = +1.0, +0.5, 0.0, -0.5  
  > "A range of robust parameters (+1.0, +0.5, 0.0, -0.5) were explored in the imaging." (ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: Velocity binning: 0.12 km s^-1 for SO, 0.25 km s^-1 (or 0.24 km s^-1) for SiO and SiS  
  > "The velocity resolution of the SO spectral windows is 0.06 km s$^{-1}$ but the data were rebinned to 0.12 km s$^{-1}$ to improve the channel map signal-to-noise ratio." (ALMA Observations); "Similarly, SiO and SiS were observed at 0.12 km s$^{-1}$ and 0.24 km s$^{-1}$ respectively and both imaged at a spectral resolution of 0.25 km s$^{-1}$." (ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: JvM epsilon ~ 0.50 for robust +0.5 line images (0.56 for robust 0.5 continuum)  
  > "The typical epsilon value for the JvM correction was $\approx$0.50 for the Briggs robust +0.5 images." (ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Imaged across a grid of Briggs robust parameters (+1.0, +0.5, 0.0, -0.5) and checked stacked SO spectrum both before and after continuum subtraction to confirm the azimuthal asymmetry and central cavity.  
  > "The asymmetry in the north west and south east of the disk is present across all of the images and the cavity becomes more pronounced with lower robust values." (Line emission maps); "As an additional check we imaged the stacked SO before and after continuum subtraction and extracted the spectrum (as described in Section 3.3)." (Line emission maps)
2. **Extract disk-integrated spectra, measure integrated line fluxes/upper limits, and constrain inner emitting radius from kinematics**: Extract spectra from an elliptical aperture of semi-major axis 0.6'' matching the disk inclination and PA; integrate SO line profiles between +/-7.5 km s^-1 around the source velocity; compute 3-sigma upper limits for SiO and SiS; determine the inner ring radius of SO from the maximum velocity drop-off assuming Keplerian rotation.  
  > "The spectra for each transition are extracted from an ellipse with the same position and inclination angle as the disk and a semi-major axis of $0.6\ensuremath{''}$, and are shown in Figure 2." (Kinematics); "We calculate the integrated lines fluxes (listed in Table 1) for the two SO transitions by integrating the line profiles between $\pm7.5$ km s$^{-1}$." (Kinematics); "Assuming Keplerian rotation, an inclination angle of 32$^{\circ}$ (as derived by (cite: 2019ApJ...871...48P) for the inner 2.0$''$ of the disk), and stellar dynamical mass of 2.2 M$_{\odot}$ results in a inner ring radius of $\approx$9.8 $\pm0.3$au." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: Elliptical aperture semi-major axis = 0.6''  
  > "The spectra for each transition are extracted from an ellipse with the same position and inclination angle as the disk and a semi-major axis of $0.6\ensuremath{''}$, and are shown in Figure 2." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: Velocity integration range = +/- 7.5 km s^-1  
  > "We calculate the integrated lines fluxes (listed in Table 1) for the two SO transitions by integrating the line profiles between $\pm7.5$ km s$^{-1}$." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation around a stellar dynamical mass of 2.2 M_sun, source velocity of 5.7 km s^-1, and disk inclination angle of 32 deg (or 40 deg)  
  > "This shows that the line is symmetric in width about the source velocity (5.7 km s$^{-1}$, (cite: 2017A&A...607A.114W))." (Kinematics); "Assuming Keplerian rotation, an inclination angle of 32$^{\circ}$ (as derived by (cite: 2019ApJ...871...48P) for the inner 2.0$''$ of the disk), and stellar dynamical mass of 2.2 M$_{\odot}$ results in a inner ring radius of $\approx$9.8 $\pm0.3$au." (Kinematics); "If the inclination in this region of the disk is higher (40$^{\circ}$; (cite: 2021arXiv211200123B)) then this results in a larger inner radius of $\approx$14.5 $\pm0.3~$au." (Kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mirrored the stacked line profile about the source velocity to verify velocity symmetry; also imaged Cycle 7 data at 1.0 km s^-1 channel width to directly compare with Cycle 0 spectra and computed residual spectrum.  
  > "The line profile from the stacked image is shown in Figure 2 alongside a mirrored version." (Kinematics); "In this Figure, the Cycle 7 data have been imaged at 1.0 km s$^{-1}$ in order to make a direct comparisons to the Cycle 0 data." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)
3. **Extract radial emission profiles via spectral shifting and stacking**: Shift and stack spectra from each pixel in the channel maps within concentric annuli of width 0.05'' using GoFish, calculating errors per radial bin from the rms in the shifted spectra.  
  > "Using the tool GoFish[^2] (cite: GoFish) we shift and stack the spectra from each pixel in the channel maps." (Radial emission profile); "The spectra are extracted from annuli with width 0.05$''$." (Radial emission profile); "The errors in this profile are calculated per radial bin from the rms in the shifted spectra." (Radial emission profile)
   - software: GoFish
   - parameter: Annulus width = 0.05''  
  > "The spectra are extracted from annuli with width 0.05$''$." (Radial emission profile)
   - assumption: Flat emitting surface (z/r = 0)  
  > "These profiles are extracted assuming a flat emitting surface." (Radial emission profile)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared GoFish shifted-and-stacked radial profiles against azimuthally averaged radial profiles extracted directly from the integrated intensity maps.  
  > "This outer ring is also tentatively present in the azimuthally averaged radial profiles from the intensity maps (shown in the Appendix in Figure B.3) and the spectral stacking confirms this feature." (Radial emission profile)
4. **Derive radial, azimuthal, and disk-averaged column densities and abundance ratios**: Convert radial emission profiles, azimuthal integrated intensity profiles at r = 20 au, and 0.6'' integrated fluxes/upper limits into column densities of SO, SiO, and SiS assuming optically thin LTE emission at fixed rotational temperatures, averaging the two detected SO transitions.  
  > "Following a similar analysis as, e.g., (cite: 2018ApJ...859..131L) and (cite: 2021ApJ...906..111T), we assume that the lines are optically thin, in local-thermodynamic equilibrium and, since we do not have enough transitions detected over a range of upper energy levels in order to perform a meaningful rotational diagram analysis, we fix the rotational temperature ($T_{\mathrm{rot}}$)." (Column density of SO); "Azimuthal profiles are extracted from the integrated intensity maps at 20 au (the radius at which the SO peaks) and converted to column density." (Column density of SO); "This was done using the integrated fluxes as listed in Table 1 and an assumed emitting area of the 0.6$''$ ellipse ($1.8~\times~10^{-11}$ steradians) from which the spectra were extracted." (Column density of SO)
   - parameter: Azimuthal extraction radius = 20 au  
  > "Azimuthal profiles are extracted from the integrated intensity maps at 20 au (the radius at which the SO peaks) and converted to column density." (Column density of SO)
   - parameter: Inner disk emitting area = 1.8 x 10^-11 steradians (0.6'' ellipse)  
  > "This was done using the integrated fluxes as listed in Table 1 and an assumed emitting area of the 0.6$''$ ellipse ($1.8~\times~10^{-11}$ steradians) from which the spectra were extracted." (Column density of SO)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Following a similar analysis as, e.g., (cite: 2018ApJ...859..131L) and (cite: 2021ApJ...906..111T), we assume that the lines are optically thin, in local-thermodynamic equilibrium and, since we do not have enough transitions detected over a range of upper energy levels in order to perform a meaningful rotational diagram analysis, we fix the rotational temperature ($T_{\mathrm{rot}}$)." (Column density of SO)
   - assumption: Rotational temperature T_rot = 75, 100, 200 K for inner disk (<100 au) and 30, 40, 50 K for outer disk (>100 au)  
  > "For the inner disk ($<$100 au) $T_{\mathrm{rot}}$ is chosen as 75, 100, and 200 K and in the outer disk ($>$100 au) 30, 40 and 50 K are chosen." (Column density of SO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified that optical depth remains thin across the entire disk for all assumed T_rot values.  
  > "For all values of $T_{\mathrm{rot}}$ the line is optically thin across the entirety of the disk." (Column density of SO)

## Qualitative findings

- SO emission in HD 100546 is distributed in two rings closely following the millimeter dust rings: an inner ring peaking at 20 au extending into the dust cavity down to ~10-15 au, and a fainter outer ring at 160-240 au peaking just beyond the outer dust ring.  
  > "The SO emission detected in the HD 100546 disk is constrained to two rings." (Morphology of the SO emission); "From the kinematics, the SO emission is detected down to $\approx 10-15$ au and is therefore tracing gas within the millimeter dust cavity (cite: 2019ApJ...871...48P)." (Morphology of the SO emission); "The outer SO ring is located from 160 to 240 au, peaking just beyond the second dust ring, and is a factor of 100 times lower in peak flux than the inner ring." (Morphology of the SO emission)
- The inner SO ring exhibits a factor of 2 azimuthal brightness and column density asymmetry between the north-west and south-east sides of the disk, peaking near the inferred location of the giant planet candidate HD 100546 c.  
  > "The asymmetry that is seen in the integrated intensity map is much more pronounced in the peak intensity map with the peak emission differing in strength by approximately a factor of 2 between the north-west and south-east sides of the disk." (Line emission maps); "The peak of the SO emission is co-spatial with the location of HD 100546 c according to the scattered light observations of (cite: 2015ApJ...814L..27C) and the excess CO signal in 2017 from (cite: 2019ApJ...883...37B)." (Connections to ongoing giant planet formation in the disk)
- The SO line profile in Cycle 7 (June 2021) is symmetric in velocity width about the source velocity, showing a significant change over 8.5 years compared to Cycle 0 (November 2012) data which peaked at the source velocity with a blue-shifted excess.  
  > "The Cycle 0 data were taken in November 2012 and the Cycle 7 data in June 2021, i.e., over a $\approx$8.5 year baseline." (Origin of the SO asymmetry); "The overall line profile in the Cycle 7 data is symmetric in velocity about the source velocity compared to the Cycle 0 spectra." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)
- Shock tracers SiO and SiS are undetected (SiO/SO < 1.4%, SiS/SO < 5%), indicating that any shock present is weak and insufficient to sputter silicate grain cores, only liberating ice mantles.  
  > "In our data we also have a non-detection of another shock tracer SiO with an average SiO/SO ratio of $<$1.4%." (Origin of the SO asymmetry); "This is an indication that if SO is enhanced in the inner disk due to a shock, then the strength of the shock is not sufficient for the sputtering of the grain cores, and is limited to the removal of ices." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous Cycle 0 ALMA observations at 1.0'' resolution left the SO emission spatially unresolved, motivating follow-up high angular resolution (~0.2'', ~20 au) Band 7 observations combining short and long baselines to resolve the spatial morphology and kinematics of SO and test shock tracers SiO and SiS.  
  > "In the 1.0$''$ data presented by (cite: 2018A&A...611A..16B) the SO emission was spatially unresolved but there was an indication the emission was spatially asymmetric and the kinematics were clearly non-Keplerian when compared to the molecular disk traced in ." (The HD 100546 disk and its planets); "In this paper we aim to determine the origin of the SO in the HD 100546 disk with follow up high-angular resolution ALMA observations." (The HD 100546 disk and its planets)
- Why this method: Applied the JvM correction to account for non-Gaussian dirty beams when combining multi-configuration data; concatenated the two SO transitions to boost S/N; used GoFish spectral shifting and stacking to uncover faint outer-disk ring emission; fixed T_rot under LTE because two transitions with similar upper state energies are insufficient for a rotational diagram analysis.  
  > "Following (cite: 1995AJ....110.2037J) and (cite: 2021ApJS..257....2C) the JvM correction was then applied to the data to account for the non-gaussianity of the dirty beam - especially important when combining data from multiple configurations." (ALMA Observations); "Using the tool GoFish[^2] (cite: GoFish) we shift and stack the spectra from each pixel in the channel maps." (Radial emission profile); "Following a similar analysis as, e.g., (cite: 2018ApJ...859..131L) and (cite: 2021ApJ...906..111T), we assume that the lines are optically thin, in local-thermodynamic equilibrium and, since we do not have enough transitions detected over a range of upper energy levels in order to perform a meaningful rotational diagram analysis, we fix the rotational temperature ($T_{\mathrm{rot}}$)." (Column density of SO)
- Alternative: Disk wind origin for the SO emission: rejected/disfavoured because the new Cycle 7 line profile is symmetric in velocity about the source velocity and lacks an extra wind velocity component.  
  > "The Cycle 7 data therefore do not strongly support the disk wind hypothesis." (Origin of the SO asymmetry); "Whilst we still see a brightness asymmetry we do not see an additional component of emission that could be attributed to a disk wind." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)
- Alternative: Warped inner disk driving azimuthal X-ray illumination/temperature variations: considered as a potential origin for the SO asymmetry, though VLTI/GRAVITY shows only a ~4 deg misalignment and existing warp chemical models lack large mm-dust cavities.  
  > "A potential origin for the SO brightness asymmetry is an azimuthal temperature variation which could be caused by a warp." (Origin of the SO asymmetry); "On sub-au scales the inner disk has been traced by (cite: 2021arXiv211200123B) via VLTI/GRAVITY observations but the position and inclination angles vary only by $\approx$4$^{\circ}$ compared to the outer disk traced in CO." (Origin of the SO asymmetry); "It is unclear if a warp of this degree could create the temperature/chemical gradient we observe." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)
- Alternative: Viewing angle effect across near/far sides of the disk (as seen for CS in MAPS disks): rejected because the SO asymmetry in HD 100546 is not oriented across the near/far axis.  
  > "In these other disks the asymmetry is across the near and far sides of the disks, so could just be due to our viewing angles of the systems; however, in HD 100546 the asymmetry is not across this axis." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)
- Alternative: Strong accretion shock sputtering silicate dust grain cores: rejected because SiO and SiS are undetected (SiO/SO < 1.4%), limiting any shock to weak ice-mantle removal.  
  > "This is an indication that if SO is enhanced in the inner disk due to a shock, then the strength of the shock is not sufficient for the sputtering of the grain cores, and is limited to the removal of ices." (Origin of the SO asymmetry)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Spatial resolution is not high enough to constrain the vertical emission surface of the SO emission.  
  > "We do not have high enough spatial resolution to constrain an emission surface for the SO emission; however, as discussed in (cite: 2021ApJS..257....3L), for more extreme emission heights of $z/r \approx 0.1-0.5$ the shape and structure of the radial emission profiles are not significantly altered." (Radial emission profile)
- Limitation (authors): Not enough SO transitions over a range of upper energy levels are detected to perform a rotational diagram analysis, requiring fixed rotational temperatures to be assumed.  
  > "Following a similar analysis as, e.g., (cite: 2018ApJ...859..131L) and (cite: 2021ApJ...906..111T), we assume that the lines are optically thin, in local-thermodynamic equilibrium and, since we do not have enough transitions detected over a range of upper energy levels in order to perform a meaningful rotational diagram analysis, we fix the rotational temperature ($T_{\mathrm{rot}}$)." (Column density of SO)
- Limitation (authors): If the true radial width of the inner SO emitting ring is much narrower than the ~20 au beam, the optical depth and derived column density may be underestimated.  
  > "If the width of the emitting area of the SO in the inner ring were much narrower than we observe then the optical depth and therefore column density we derive may be underestimated." (Column density of SO)
- Limitation (authors): Disk-averaged column densities over the 0.6'' ellipse underestimate the true local column density compared to radially/azimuthally resolved profiles.  
  > "We note that these average values will underestimate the column density when compared to the radially/azimuthally resolved analysis." (Column density of SO)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot distinguish between a chemical abundance asymmetry and a radiative transfer temperature asymmetry (factor of ~2 in temperature) from two SO transitions of similar upper state energy alone.  
  > "This could either be due to a chemical asymmetry, i.e., there are more SO molecules on the southern side of the disk than the northern, or a radiative transfer effect due to a temperature asymmetry." (Column density of SO)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison between Cycle 0 (1.0'' resolution, single compact configuration) and Cycle 7 (~0.2'' resolution, combined configurations with self-calibration and JvM correction) spectra is made in the image plane rather than matching uv-coverage or tapering Cycle 7 to the Cycle 0 beam, which could affect recovered flux or line profile shape.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Application of the JvM correction rescales the residuals by epsilon (~0.5), which lowers the apparent image rms noise and boosts quoted peak S/N values compared to standard unscaled tCLEAN images.
