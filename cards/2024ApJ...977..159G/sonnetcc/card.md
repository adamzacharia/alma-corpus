## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce KCWI data and build mosaics**: KDERP pipeline in IDL for bias, gain, cosmic-ray and sky corrections, then resample and mosaic with IFSRED  
  > "We reduced the data using the KCWI Data Extraction and Reduction Pipeline (KDERP) in IDL." (Data reduction); "and create a mosaic for each target, we used the IFSR_KCWIRESAMPLE and IFSR_MOSAIC routines from the IFSRED library (cite: Rupke2014a)" (Data reduction)
   - software: KDERP, IFSRED
   - parameter: Blue low-dispersion grating, medium slicer, R=1800, 2x2 detector binning  
  > "We used a detector binning of $2 \times 2$ for all targets." (Observations); "and a spectral resolution of $\rm R=1800$" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure systemic velocity from the central stellar spectrum**: Sum spaxels around the stellar peak, mask emission lines, fit stellar continuum with pPXF; mean stellar velocity taken as systemic velocity  
  > "Spaxels surrounding the peak of the stellar emission are spatially-integrated, yielding a high quality central spectrum." (Systemic Velocity Measurements); "The mean stellar velocity in the core of the galaxy is obtained from this fit and used as the systemic velocity." (Systemic Velocity Measurements)
   - software: IFSFIT, pPXF
   - parameter: Legendre polynomials of order 15; SPS models vary in age, metallicity fixed to solar  
  > "We used Legendre polynomials of order 15." (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting); "The SPS models are allowed to vary in age, but their metallicity is fixed to that of the sun." (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting)
   - assumption: Central stellar velocity reflects the velocity of the central galaxy  
  > "operating under the assumption that these stars are gravitationally bound to the central galaxy" (Systemic Velocity Measurements)
   - fitting: pPXF with linear combination of SPS models and Legendre polynomials  
  > "The fit is a linear combination of SPS models from (cite: SSP2005) and Legendre polynomials." (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting)
   - validation: Ca H & K fit checked against intermediate-age population bias; shifted stellar models fit worse than unshifted  
  > "In all instances, the shifted stellar models do not fit the observed spectrum as well as the non-shifted one, thus confirming the reality of the peculiar nebular velocities." (Systemic Velocity Measurements)
3. **Fit stellar continuum and [OII] emission lines per spaxel with 1 or 2 Gaussians**: pPXF for the starlight, MPFIT for the emission lines; 1 or 2 Gaussian components chosen by BIC and S/N criteria with spatial clustering  
  > "Each emission line is fit with 1 or 2 Gaussian profiles, as needed." (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting); "A second component is used when the BIC of the two components fit is smaller than the BIC of the one component fit." (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting)
   - software: IFSFIT, pPXF, MPFIT
   - parameter: Two-component criteria include flux ratio of components below 10 and neighbouring spaxels also two-component  
  > "the flux ratio of the components is less than 10" (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting); "Two component fits are used when neighbouring spaxels also use two component fits." (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting)
   - assumption: [OII]3729/3726 fixed to 1.2 (electron density 400 cm-3)  
  > "which is fixed to 1.2, assuming an electron density of 400 cm$^{-3}$ (cite: Pradhan2006)" (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting)
   - fitting: MPFIT least-squares Gaussian fits; BIC = chi^2 + k ln(n) model selection (k = 3 or 6)  
  > "This last criterion is evaluated using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC):" (Stellar continuum and emission line fitting)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Build kinematic maps (v50, sigma, v02, v98) and flux-weighted regional means**: Maps of median velocity, dispersion and 2%/98% extreme velocities from the fitted profiles; flux-weighted means over regions; S/N >= 5 cut  
  > "The maximum redshifted (blueshifted) velocities are obtained by determining the velocity at 2% (98%) of the cumulative velocity distribution; these values probe the most extreme velocities of the ionized gas." (Abell 262: Kinematics); "a $\textrm{S/N} \geq 5$ flux is applied for every spaxel." (Results)
   - software: IFSFIT
   - parameter: Two-component maps use softer cut S/N >= 3 per component  
  > "we only require that each component of the resulting fit has a $\text{S/N} \geq 3$." (Abell 1835: Kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainties of a few km/s in bright regions, 20-50 km/s at the edges  
  > "reaching values of $20-50$ km s$^{-1}$" (Notes on uncertainties)
5. **Integrate CO emission from ALMA maps and compare with the [OII] area**: CO flux maps integrated over the targeted CO line; CO area defined by a 10% of peak flux cut; compared with [OII] area  
  > "Maps of the CO fluxes were measured by integrating over the targeted CO emission lines." (Archival observations); "Total area with detected CO emission, using a flux cut of 10% of the peak CO flux." (Table notes (Results))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CO contours reported consistent with earlier papers  
  > "The resulting CO contours are consistent with results from previous papers (cite: McNamara2014; Russell2016; Vantyghem2017; Russell2019; Olivares2019; Vantyghem2019)." (Archival observations)
6. **Estimate ionized gas mass from [OII] luminosity**: M_ion = mu m_H L_[OII] / (gamma_[OII] n_e) with emissivity from PyNeb  
  > "is the mean mass per hydrogen atom which we set to $\mu=1.4$" (Abell 262: Morphology); "Assuming an electron temperature of $\sim 10 000$ K and an electron density of $\sim 400$ cm$^{-3}$, we obtain an effective line emissivity" (Abell 262: Morphology)
   - software: PyNeb
   - assumption: Te ~ 10000 K, ne ~ 400 cm-3, mu = 1.4  
  > "Assuming an electron temperature of $\sim 10 000$ K and an electron density of $\sim 400$ cm$^{-3}$, we obtain an effective line emissivity" (Abell 262: Morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Scale molecular gas mass using the [OII]-to-CO ratio**: Take [OII] luminosity to M_mol ratio inside the CO area (CO >= 10% of peak) and apply it to the total [OII] luminosity in the FOV  
  > "we scale the observed molecular gas mass to estimate the total M$_{\textrm{mol}}$ that may be located in the extended nebula." (Molecular Gas Masses)
   - assumption: [OII] luminosity to molecular gas mass ratio is constant within an object; [OII] used as a sensitive proxy for CO  
  > "is a more sensitive indicator of molecular and atomic gasses" (Molecular Gas Masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Infer cavity speeds from velocity profiles of churned gas**: Extract v50 of the second component along the cavity axis, interpret gradients as gas lifted in the bubble wake, compare to sound speeds from literature temperatures  
  > "we infer cavity velocities of about $185-195$ km s$^{-1}$" (Abell 1835); "we find sound speeds of $840-910$ km s$^{-1}$" (Abell 1835)
   - assumption: Empty bubbles rise at about half the local sound speed (based on M87 study)  
  > "A detailed study of M87’s bubble dynamics inferred speeds of roughly half the local sound speed (cite: Churazov2001)." (Abell 1835)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Perpendicular axis shows no v50 correlation with distance, as expected  
  > "As expected, the v$_{50}$ of the churned nebular gas show no correlation with distance along the axis orthogonal to the X-ray cavities." (Abell 1835)

## Qualitative findings

- [OII] emission is much more extended than CO, though the brightest CO and [OII] regions coincide  
  > "However, the detected \[OII\] gas is much more extended by tens of kpc." (Summary)
- Molecular and nebular gas velocity structures are broadly similar but do not match in detail  
  > "The velocity structures of the molecular and nebular gas are broadly similar but do not match in detail." (Summary)
- Abell 262 shows high velocity dispersion nebular gas along the radio jets  
  > "is observed along the radio jets in the central galaxy of Abell 262, indicating a strong interaction between the jets and the surrounding gas." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA archival CO maps used to compare cold molecular gas with the warm ionized gas  
  > "Complimentary archival observations are used to compare the properties of the ionized gas in the ICM with the other gas phases, as well as to study the interactions between AGN feedback and gas dynamics." (Archival observations)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Cavities launched at different epochs and bubbles launched in different directions due to jet precession were considered for PKS 0745-191 but none explained all aspects of the data  
  > "They include cavities launched at different epochs, and bubbles launched in different directions due to jet precession." (PKS 0745-191); "None of these scenarios explain all aspects of the data." (PKS 0745-191)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ALMA early science data for Abell 1835 are too crude to interpret the high-velocity scatter region  
  > "The ALMA early science data are too crude to clearly interpret this apparent interaction, which must await deeper ALMA imaging." (Abell 1835)
- Limitation (authors): Nebular and molecular velocity comparison is limited by resolution and velocity reference frame differences  
  > "Our attempt to compare the nebular and molecular gas velocities is complicated by differences in spatial resolution and velocity reference frame." (Molecular & Nebular Gas Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Second kinematic component in Abell 262 cannot be robustly detected with current data  
  > "Additional observations are needed to further study the possibility of multiple kinematic components in this nebula." (Abell 262: Kinematics)
