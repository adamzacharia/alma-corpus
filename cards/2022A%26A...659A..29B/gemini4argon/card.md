## Qualitative findings

- First detection of dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3) in a protoplanetary disk, the largest COM detected in a protoplanetary disk to date.  
  > "In this paper, we report the analysis of ALMA data of IRS 48 including the first detection of dimethyl ether () in a protoplanetary disk and a tentative detection of methyl formate ()." (Introduction); "is the largest complex organic molecule that has been detected in a protoplanetary disk to date." (Introduction)
- Tentative detection of methyl formate (CH3OCHO) and first detection of nitric oxide (NO) in a protoplanetary disk, plus an additional transition of SO2 at 363.16 GHz.  
  > "We report the first detections of dimethy ether (), nitric oxide (NO), and a tentative detection of methyl formate () in a protoplanetary disk." (Conclusions); "We report an additional detection of a transition in the disk with an upper energy level of $\approx$250 K." (Conclusions)
- Emissions of the detected COMs are directly linked with the southern asymmetric dust trap, supporting thermal ice sublimation as their origin.  
  > "The emissions of the detected species show a direct link with the asymmetric dust trap in the southern region of the disk, further suggesting that molecular complexity in this disk is due to ice sublimation." (Conclusions)
- Stronger CH3OH lines are likely optically thick and beam diluted, as two weaker CH3OH lines require a factor of 4 higher column density (2 x 10^15 cm^-2).  
  > "We also detect two weaker lines in the stacked spectra that are better fit at a higher column density of 2 $\times$ cm$^{-2}$ and still at a temperature of 100 K (see Figure 2 for a comparison of the two models)." (Revising the column density); "These weak lines are reproduced at a different column density, which likely indicates that the emission traced by the stronger lines is optically thick." (Revising the column density)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: IRS 48 is a warm Herbig transition disk with a highly asymmetric dust trap of large grains where icy COMs sublimate into the gas phase and become observable with ALMA.  
  > "Also, another Herbig source was revealed to have a rich observable chemistry: the IRS 48 transition disk (cite: van2014warm; van2021major; booth2021major)." (Introduction); "The bulk of the ice reservoir of the IRS48 disk is constrained to the larger dust grains, and because of UV irradiation from the central star, the dust temperature increases enough to liberate the from the ices." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Stacking cleaned images with GoFish over the whole azimuth increases S/N to identify weak and blended lines because the observations are not well spatially resolved.  
  > "The cleaned images were subsequently stacked using GoFish version 1.3.6 (cite: teague2019gofish) in order to increase the signal-to-noise ratio." (Observations); "This method makes it possible to identify potential weak lines and also distinguish between lines that are blended (very close in frequency)." (Observations); "We extract spectra over the whole azimuth of the disk although the lines are co-spatial with the dust trap. This is done because the observations are not well spatially resolved." (Observations)
- Alternative: Other candidate molecules were considered for the 363.48-363.49 GHz emission feature before selecting methyl formate as the closest fit.  
  > "While the modelled spectrum of does not provide an exact fit for the emission feature, it is the closest fit found after considering other possible candidates." (Detection of dimethyl ether and methyl formate)
- Alternative: Single blended feature at 350.68 GHz was rejected as sufficient proof of NO detection until the double feature at 351.04 and 351.05 GHz was also fit.  
  > "NO has two transitions at this frequency, but a single line was not enough to confirm a definitive detection of the molecule because this line is also blended with ." (Other line detections and upper limits)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Observations are not well spatially resolved, so the true COM emitting area is uncertain and strong CH3OH lines may be optically thick and beam diluted.  
  > "We extract spectra over the whole azimuth of the disk although the lines are co-spatial with the dust trap. This is done because the observations are not well spatially resolved." (Observations); "The latter situation could be due to the lines being optically thick but beam diluted." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Only a few transitions of dimethyl ether are detected, so its excitation temperature cannot be independently constrained and column densities are calculated over 70-250 K (typically a factor of two uncertainty).  
  > "We also calculated the best-fit column density at both 70 K and 250 K to have an estimate of the column density error for the detected species, which is typically a factor of two." (Spectral analysis); "The excitation temperature of may be lower than that of (cite: jorgensen2018alma) but given that only a few transitions are detected, we calculate the column densities over a range of excitation temperatures from 80 to 250 K (listed in Table 1)." (Detection of dimethyl ether and methyl formate)
- Limitation (authors): Methyl formate detection is only tentative because the model does not fit the main emission feature very well and only one significant feature is covered.  
  > "Confirmation of the detection is needed because our models do not fit the emission feature very well and we only have one significant feature to fit given the frequency coverage of the observations." (Prospects for further complexity in the IRS 48 ice trap)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extracting the spectrum over the full 360-degree azimuth with GoFish while assuming the 5-sigma dust continuum crescent area for column density conversion could introduce a mismatch between the flux extraction aperture and the assumed emitting solid angle if not scaled consistently.
