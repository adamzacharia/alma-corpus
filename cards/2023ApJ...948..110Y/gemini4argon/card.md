## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract azimuthal profiles and assess clump significance in Band 6 and Band 3 continuum images**: Extract azimuthal intensity profiles for the inner ring (0.14''-0.28'') and outer ring (0.34''-0.48'') by averaging over 10 degree azimuthal bins, and measure brightness contrast between clump peaks and adjacent valleys.  
  > "To have a detailed comparison of the asymmetric structures in the two different bands, we draw the azimuthal profiles of the inner ring and the outer ring in Band 3 and Band 6 (Figure 2). The profile is drawn by averaging over bins of 10$^\circ$ widths." (Comparison with ALMA Band 3 image); "To assess whether or not a clump may be spurious, we measured the brightness contrast between its peak and the "valleys" with the lowest intensity in the inter-clump regions." (Comparison with ALMA Band 3 image)
   - parameter: Inner ring radial range: 0.14''-0.28''; Outer ring radial range: 0.34''-0.48''; Bin width: 10 deg  
  > "The profile is drawn by averaging over bins of 10$^\circ$ widths. Figure 2(a) compares the azimuthal profile of the inner ring (0.14$\ensuremath{''}$-0.28$\ensuremath{''}$)." (Comparison with ALMA Band 3 image); "The azimuthal profiles of the outer ring (0.$\ensuremath{''}$34-0.$\ensuremath{''}$48) are shown in Figure 2(b)." (Comparison with ALMA Band 3 image)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare peak-to-valley brightness contrasts in units of sigma and check whether structures appear in both Band 3 and Band 6.  
  > "So despite the North Arc and the South Arc (III) as well as the Inner Arc (B), which are indeed localized clumps since they are observed in both two Bands, we suggest that other clumps, I, IV, V, A, C, D, as well as the two sub-peaks of B, may be spurious" (Comparison with ALMA Band 3 image)
2. **Create moment 0 maps and fit CO isotopologue spectra for emission and foreground absorption**: Generate moment 0 maps using immoments over selected velocity channels where >4 sigma emission within 0.5'' appears, and fit the spatially integrated spectra within 0.5'' using a sum of emission and absorption Gaussians.  
  > "We made the moment 0 (intensity) maps of them using the command `immoments`, and they are shown in Figure 4." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "To determine the strengths, velocity, and width of the emission and absorption lines, we fit the spectra with the following equation (cite: Kim2020-qi):" (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "The fitting is done by using SciPy *curve_fit* command, with Trust Region Reflective algorithm. Since the disk is nearly face-on, we used intensities within $0.\ensuremath{''}5$ for the fitting." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - software: CASA, SciPy
   - parameter: Channel ranges: -1.96 to 8.09 km/s (12CO), -1.96 to 6.75 km/s (13CO), -0.62 to 6.75 km/s (C18O); spectral extraction radius: < 0.5''  
  > "In detail, for the $^{12}$CO line, the channels between -1.96km/s and 8.09 km/s are chosen, for $^{13}$CO line, the channels between -1.96km/s and 6.75 km/s are chosen, and for C$^{18}$O line the channels between -0.62km/s and 6.75 km/s are chosen." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "Since the disk is nearly face-on, we used intensities within $0.\ensuremath{''}5$ for the fitting." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - assumption: Emission and foreground absorption components follow Gaussian profiles since the disk is nearly face-on.  
  > "The emission component can be approximated by a Gaussian curve since SR 21 seems close in face-on." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - fitting: SciPy curve_fit with Trust Region Reflective algorithm  
  > "The fitting is done by using SciPy *curve_fit* command, with Trust Region Reflective algorithm." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - validation: Compare fitted emission systemic velocity (-7.7 km/s barycentric) with Gaia radial velocity (-7.3±1.64 km/s).  
  > "The fitting results show that the emission lines center at Kinematic Local Standard of Rest (LSRK) velocity about 2.8 km/s, corresponding to barycentric velocity about -7.7 km/s, which is consistent with the radial velocity -7.3$\pm$1.64 km/s calibrated by GAIA (cite: GAIA2018)." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
3. **Derive gas surface density and Toomre Q parameter in the inner disk**: Smooth CO isotopologue cubes to 0.105''x0.105'' using imsmooth, create moment 8 maps of 13CO (with continuum) to derive gas temperature via Planck function, compute C18O peak optical depth from C18O moment 8 map, convert to C18O column density and H2 gas surface density, and calculate Toomre Q.  
  > "For calculation, we unite the beam sizes of CO isotopologue images to $0.\ensuremath{''}105\times0.\ensuremath{''}105$ using the `imsmooth` command. Firstly, we make the moment 8 map of $C^{18}O$ (Figure 7(a)), which shows the peak intensities of every pixel." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "Here we use the moment 8 map of $^{13}$CO with continuum (Figure 7(b)) to calculate the brightness temperature $T_{^{13}CO}$ using the Planck function $I_{^{13}CO,peak}=B(\nu_{^{13}CO},T_{^{13}CO})$. Then the peak optical depth of C$^{18}$O emission $\tau_{C^{18}O, peak}$ can be derived via the radiative transfer equation:" (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Common beam: 0.105'' x 0.105''  
  > "For calculation, we unite the beam sizes of CO isotopologue images to $0.\ensuremath{''}105\times0.\ensuremath{''}105$ using the `imsmooth` command." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - assumption: 13CO is optically thick and its brightness temperature equals the gas temperature and C18O excitation temperature; C18O/H2 ratio = 1.79e-7; Gaussian optical depth profile; epicyclic frequency equals Keplerian angular velocity with M_star = 1.67 M_sun.  
  > "Therefore, the $^{12}CO$ and $^{13}CO$ lines are optically thick, and we can use their brightness temperatures as gas temperatures." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "here $\chi$ is the ratio of $C^{18}O$ to $H_2$, and we use the interstellar abundance 1.79$\times10^{-7}$ (cite: 1999RPPh...62..143W)." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "and $\Omega_k$ is the epicyclic frequency, which we assume that it equals the angular velocity, i.e., $\Omega_k=\sqrt{GM_{star}/r^3}$." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Compute spectral index map and fit dust surface density and maximum grain size**: Re-image Band 6 continuum to match Band 3 uv-range (50-5000 kλ) and beam (0.10''x0.09'', PA=90 deg), register images using Gaia proper motion, compute spectral index map for >4 sigma pixels, and fit Band 3 and Band 6 azimuthal ring profiles using a radiative transfer slab model with dust scattering.  
  > "In this step, we re-image the Band 6 data to make sure it has the same beam size (0.$\ensuremath{''}$10$\times$0.$\ensuremath{''}$09, and PA=90$^\circ$) and UV-distance ($50-5000$ k$\lambda$) as the Band 3 data, and register the two images based on the proper motion data got by (cite: GAIA2018)." (Dust Size); "Therefore, we used the azimuthal profiles of the inner ring and the outer ring in Band 3 and 6 to perform fittings, hoping to understand the dust distributions of the rings. We use the method developed by (cite: Carrasco-Gonzalez2019-mq) and (cite: Sierra2020-zs)." (Dust Size)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: nu_3 = 110.2 GHz, nu_6 = 217.0 GHz; threshold > 4 sigma; grid: 10 μm <= a_max <= 1 cm and 0.001 g/cm^2 <= Sigma_d <= 10 g/cm^2, 1000 logarithmic bins each; r_inner = 0.21'', r_outer = 0.41''  
  > "$\nu_{3}$=110.2 GHz and $\nu_{6}$=217.0 GHz are the rest frequencies of Band 3 and 6 observations, respectively. Only the pixels brighter than 4$\sigma$ are used to calculate the index." (Dust Size); "And since we only have two bands, we fixed the dust temperature at the inner ring (at 0.$\ensuremath{''}$21) and the outer ring (at 0.$\ensuremath{''}$41) so that there are only two free parameters." (Dust Size); "The explored parameter space is a logarithmically spaced grid between $10\mu m\leqslant a_{max} \leqslant 1 cm$ and $0.001 g/cm^2 \leqslant \Sigma_d \leqslant 10 g/cm^2$, each divided into 1000 bins." (Dust Size)
   - assumption: Power-law dust size distribution index q = 3.5; DSHARP dust optical constants; inclination i = 15 deg; dust temperature fixed to passively heated flared disk mid-plane temperature profile of Huang et al. (2018).  
  > "We assumed q=3.5 in our fitting" (Dust Size); "By assuming that the temperature of grown dust is equal to the disk mid-plane temperature, we calculated the dust temperature using Equation (3) in (cite: Huang2018-sa)" (Dust Size); "The DSHARP dust optical constants ((cite: Birnstiel2018-jb)) are employed in our fitting." ## Qualitative findings

- The Band 6 continuum image of SR 21 reveals two dust rings (0.14''-0.28'' and 0.34''-0.48'') and three crescent-like asymmetric structures: the Inner Arc in the inner ring, and the North Arc and South Arc in the outer ring.  
  > "1. Based on the ALMA Band 3 and Band 6 dust continuum images, the protoplanetary disk around SR 21 under sub-millimeter bands consists of two rings with a radius of about 0.$\ensuremath{''}$14-0.$\ensuremath{''}$28 and 0.$\ensuremath{''}$34-0.$\ensuremath{''}$48. Three crescent-like asymmetric structures, one in the inner ring and two in the outer ring, are detected" (Conclusions)
- CO isotopologue emissions are compact and mostly confined within the inner ring (<0.28''), likely affected by foreground absorption at LSRK velocity ~3.1 km/s, while the inner disk is gravitationally stable (Toomre Q >> 1).  
  > "The absorption features center at about 3.1 km/s, corresponding to a barycentric velocity of about -7.4 km/s resulting from foreground absorption." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions); "The result is shown in Figure 8(c), considering that Q$\gg$1, the inner disk is gravitational stable." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
- Most small clumps seen in the Band 3 image (I, IV, V, A, C, D, and sub-peaks of B) have low peak-to-valley contrasts (~1-2 sigma) and may be spurious, except clump II which is 4 sigma brighter than adjacent valleys.  
  > "we suggest that other clumps, I, IV, V, A, C, D, as well as the two sub-peaks of B, may be spurious, while II could represent the actual structure since it is 4$\sigma$ brighter than its nearby valleys." (Comparison with ALMA Band 3 image)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous sub-millimeter observations of SR 21 had spatial resolutions of only 0.2''-0.3'', which was insufficient to resolve the large-dust counterparts to the complex rings and tight spirals seen at 0.05'' resolution in near-infrared VLT/SPHERE observations.  
  > "While previous observations of SR 21 in sub-millimeter bands have spatial resolutions of only about 0.2$\ensuremath{''}$-0.3$\ensuremath{''}$, it is not enough to understand the corresponding structures of large dust in the disk. Therefore, higher-resolution observations in the sub-millimeter band are pretty necessary." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Estimating maximum grain size directly from spectral indices is inaccurate when the disk is optically thick and/or when dust scattering cannot be neglected, motivating radiative transfer slab fitting that includes dust scattering.  
  > "Estimating $a_{max}$ from spectral indices is not accurate when the disk is optically thick and/or dust scattering cannot be neglected ((cite: Liu2019ApJ...877L..22L)). Therefore, we used the azimuthal profiles of the inner ring and the outer ring in Band 3 and 6 to perform fittings" (Dust Size)
- Alternative: Dead zone-induced vortices as the cause of the outer ring elongated arcs (rejected in favor of planet-induced vortices because the azimuthal profiles have off-center peaks).  
  > "Therefore, we suggested that the two asymmetric structures in the outer ring are induced by a growing planet between the rings rather than a dead zone." (Disk Morphology)
- Alternative: Gas depletion outside 0.28'' as the cause of compact CO emission (rejected in favor of foreground absorption due to high extinction A_V=6.2 and observed absorption features).  
  > "The compact features in the CO isotopologue emissions seem to indicate the depletion of gas outside of $0.\ensuremath{''}28$. However, considering the large extinctions of SR 21 ($A_V$=6.2, (cite: Herczeg2014-dm)), one more reasonable explanation is that they are affected by the foreground adsorptions." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
- Alternative: Gravitational instability or gap-opening planet density waves as the origin of the NIR spiral arms (rejected because Q >> 1 in the inner disk and the 18-58 M_earth planet is too low-mass to trigger visible spirals in scattered light).  
  > "Thus, the predicted gap-opening planet cannot explain the spiral arm-like features seen in (cite: Muro-Arena2020). While the gravitational instability may also produce spiral arms in scattered light (cite: dong15giarm), it is unlikely the cause of the spirals in this case (Section 3.2)." (Potential Planet)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 12CO, 13CO, and C18O emissions likely originate from different vertical layers in the disk, so using 13CO brightness temperature to compute C18O optical depth assumes they trace the same region, which is not necessarily true.  
  > "Firstly, emission from these three tracers CO, $^{13}$CO, C$^{18}$O is likely coming from different vertical heights in the disc, thus using $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O together to calculate $I_{C^{18}O, peak}$ assumes the two are tracing similar parts of the disc, which is not necessarily true." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming the interstellar C18O/H2 abundance ignores CO freeze-out, isotope-selective photodissociation, and CO depletion, which could mean the actual disk mass is higher and Toomre Q is smaller.  
  > "Secondly, we assume that the ratio of $C^{18}O$ to $H_2$ is the same as the interstellar abundance, which is also not necessarily true: the CO molecules suffer from several effects such as CO freeze-out (cite: Qi2011-fb), isotope-selective photodissociation (cite: Miotello2016-gw; Miotello2018-lt) and CO depletion (cite: Krijt2020-oe), these effects will lower the observed abundance of CO molecules; thus the actual disk mass could be higher, and Q parameter could be smaller than our calculation." (ALMA Band 6 CO Isotopologue Emissions)
- Limitation (authors): With only two ALMA bands (Band 3 and Band 6), the dust temperature had to be fixed and the SED fitting cannot break the degeneracy between the small-dust and large-dust solutions.  
  > "And since we only have two bands, we fixed the dust temperature at the inner ring (at 0.$\ensuremath{''}$21) and the outer ring (at 0.$\ensuremath{''}$41) so that there are only two free parameters." (Dust Size); "Theoretical studies favor the large dust solutions at the peaks of asymmetric structures and clumps; however, from the observational view, it is hard to judge which solution is proper." (Dust Size)
- Limitation (authors): Because SR 21 is nearly face-on, it is difficult to determine which side is nearer or the rotation direction, preventing a test of whether wavelength-dependent peak offsets match RWI vortex predictions.  
  > "The SR 21 disk is nearly face-on, so it is hard to judge which side is nearer to us, then it is hard to know whether the gas in the disk rotates clockwise or counterclockwise. Therefore it is hard to judge if the observed offsets are consistent with the theoretical prediction." (Disk Morphology)
- Limitation (authors): The gap and vortices could instead be triggered by a single planet inside the central cavity with an unconstrained orbit, and the planet mass calculation depends on the assumed mid-plane temperature profile.  
  > "Firstly, the gap may be triggered by just one planet inside the central cavity." (Potential Planet); "Secondly, our calculation is aspect-ratio dependent, and the aspect-ratio is dependent on the temperature of the disk middle plane. The result will also change if the actual temperature deviates from Equation (3) in (cite: Huang2018-sa)." (Potential Planet)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Foreground cloud absorption severely affects the CO isotopologue line peaks, so using moment 8 maps of 13CO and C18O without correcting pixel-by-pixel for foreground absorption may bias both the derived excitation temperature and C18O optical depth.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Equation (9), the residual term (I_nu - I_nu,model) is printed without a square exponent, likely a typographical error in the paper text for a standard chi-squared Gaussian likelihood.
