## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: Quantify azimuthal offsets between molecular gas (CO), star formation (Halpha), and stellar mass peaks in spiral arms of nearby galaxies to test and differentiate between spiral arm formation theories.  
  > "The main goal of this paper is to quantify CO-H$\alpha$ offsets in the PHANGS[^1] sample of nearby galaxies." ## Qualitative findings

- Individual azimuthal offsets within spiral arms exhibit large scatter (median standard deviation ~1 kpc), showing that star formation is not uniquely initiated at a coherent spiral shock.  
  > "The median of the standard deviation in the offsets is ${\sim}1$ kpc." (Offsets between stellar mass and molecular gas); "In this context, the scatter in our measurements implies that star formation is not uniquely initiated at a coherent spiral shock." (Star formation is not uniquely initiated at a spiral shock)
- Most galaxies (58%) show net positive CO-Halpha offsets of a few hundred parsecs, indicating that gas and stars cross and overtake the spiral perturbation inside co-rotation.  
  > "Despite significant scatter, in 58% of the galaxies we find net positive offsets between CO and H$\alpha$, suggesting that the gas rotates faster than the spiral perturbation (i.e. gas ‘overtakes’ the spiral)." (Do stars and gas cross spiral arms?)
- CO-NIR offsets are also predominantly positive and decrease from inner to outer radii, consistent with gas shocking upstream of the stellar potential minimum.  
  > "Figure [4](#fig:histo_CO-Ha) shows that CO-NIR offsets also tend to be predominantly positive, in agreement with the classical picture from (cite: 1969ApJ...158..123R)." (Offsets between stellar mass and molecular gas); "We also find a net decrease in the mean offset when shifting from inner to outer radii." (Offsets between stellar mass and molecular gas)
- Different mechanisms sustain spiral structure across nearby galaxies: ~17% match a single-pattern-speed density wave, ~42% suggest multiple overlapping modes, and ~42% are compatible with co-rotating material or transient dynamical arms.  
  > "In four galaxies (17%), we find positive mean offsets and a clear radial trend (Fig. [5](#fig:classification))." (Summary and conclusions); "For ten galaxies (42%), the mean offset is positive, but there is not such a clear radial trend, which could suggest the superposition of several modes (such that a spiral mode might not develop beyond its own co-rotation)." (Summary and conclusions); "In the remaining ten galaxies (42%), we did not find significant positive offsets (Fig. [5](#fig:classification))." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO and Halpha are established as the optimal tracers for gas-star formation offsets in spiral arms, and PHANGS-ALMA provides the largest and highest-resolution (~100 pc, ~5x sharper than Egusa et al. 2009) sample of nearby spiral galaxies with high CO flux completeness.  
  > "The conclusion of (cite: 2013ApJ...763...94L) was that CO and H$\alpha$ are the optimal tracers when it comes to establishing offsets between gas and star formation in spiral arms." (Introduction); "PHANGS provides the largest and highest resolution sample of nearby spiral galaxies with CO data to carry out this experiment (cite: 2021ApJS..257...43L), with an approximately five times higher physical resolution than (cite: 2009ApJ...697.1870E)." (Introduction); "This provides high CO flux completeness, which makes it ideal for our purpose of determining offsets between peaks." (CO as molecular gas tracer)
- Why this method: Measuring azimuthal peak offsets in elliptical radial bins within spiral masks and fitting against angular frequency Omega follows Egusa et al. (2009) to distinguish single density waves, multi-mode spirals, and material/transient arms; running means are used instead of running medians to capture bulk average offsets from zero.  
  > "There are three basic scenarios for spiral structure that we would like to differentiate, and where the offset measurements can be informative, as illustrated by Fig. [2](#fig:method)." (Methodology and expectations); "We opt for running means instead of running medians because we are interested in the bulk average offset with respect to zero, and not the typical offset at a given radius." (Offsets between molecular gas and star formation)
- Alternative: Running medians instead of running means (rejected because the goal is to measure the bulk average offset relative to zero rather than the typical offset at a given radius).  
  > "We opt for running means instead of running medians because we are interested in the bulk average offset with respect to zero, and not the typical offset at a given radius." (Offsets between molecular gas and star formation)

(Do stars and gas cross spiral arms?)
- Limitation (authors): Where spiral arms have very low pitch angles (nearly tangent to circular radial bins), measured azimuthal offsets can become exceedingly large (e.g. NGC 1300).  
  > "For some galaxies, the offsets at certain radii are exceedingly large because at that point the spiral is nearly tangent to the circular bins where we perform the measurements (very low pitch angle); this is the case for NGC 1300, for example." (Radial variation in the offsets)
- Limitation (authors): Circular radial bins assume circular orbits, whereas true orbits in an m=2 spiral potential are elliptical, which can underestimate orbital displacements and affect fitted star formation timescales.  
  > "Our offset measurements rely on galactocentric radial bins, which implicitly assume circular trajectories for gas and stars." (Robustness of results and caveats); "This means that if there is a systematic displacement between gas and star formation along the orbit, that distance will be underestimated by our circular bins, especially for more open spirals (large pitch angle) with strong stellar arm/interarm contrasts." (Robustness of results and caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in disc inclination and position angle affect in-plane offsets and correlation coefficients by up to a few multiples of 10%.  
  > "We find that perturbing the orientation parameters within their error bars has a typical impact on the offsets and correlation coefficients of at most a few multiples of 10%, and importantly, it does not significantly wash out any radial trends." (Robustness of results and caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Halpha emission is subject to dust extinction, which may be stronger upstream and at smaller radii, potentially affecting the slope of radial trends and inferred star formation timescales.  
  > "Another methodological detail that might be questionable is the choice of H$\alpha$ to trace recent star formation, as this optical tracer is known to suffer from severe extinction." (Robustness of results and caveats); "However, dust extinction could be stronger in ‘upstream’ locations compared to ‘downstream’ regions (due to higher gas column densities), and it could also be stronger near the centres of the galaxies." (Robustness of results and caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Star formation timescales are assumed constant in the fit but can vary locally with gas density and environment (between ~5 and ~10 Myr across PHANGS galaxies).  
  > "Another related problem could be associated with changing star formation timescales (cite: 2024ARA&A..62..369S), which could make the radial behaviour more erratic." (Robustness of results and caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Limited PHANGS-ALMA field of view (typically out to ~0.5 R25) biases mean offsets toward larger inner-disc values and limits the ability to detect sign flips beyond co-rotation.  
  > "It is worth noting that even in the framework of quasi-stationary density wave theory, the mean measured offset for a given galaxy will depend on the field of view." (Robustness of results and caveats); "The limited field of view also affects our ability to test whether offsets indeed become negative after a certain radius, as we commented on in Sect. [5.3](#Sec:single_OmegaP)." (Robustness of results and caveats)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The PHANGS Halpha maps are cited as 'Razza et al. (in prep.)', which may complicate independent access if not all 23 maps are included in existing PHANGS public releases.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rotation curves v_rot(R) are cited from Lang et al. (2020), but it is not specified how radial bins not covered by or noisy in the CO kinematic rotation curves were handled, or which rotation curve was used for M51.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No software packages or scripts used for the elliptical binning, unsharp masking, or linear fitting are named or released.
