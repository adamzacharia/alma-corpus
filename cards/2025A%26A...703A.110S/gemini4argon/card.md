(Observations and data reduction); "To accurately estimate the polarization degree, we created the PD maps masking areas with a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $<$ 5 to avoid bias and unreliable polarization measurements in low-S/N regions." ## Qualitative findings

- Nearly all compact millimeter-emitting sources in the central region of NGC 4945 show no detectable polarization (much less than 1.5%), except for two persistent regions.  
  > "Our findings indicate that nearly all mm-emitting sources in the galaxy show no detectable polarization (much less than 1.5%), with the exception of a peculiar unidentified source located above the galactic plane, which exhibits extremely high polarization." (Introduction); "Ultimately, we confirmed only two polarized regions that were consistently observed during both S1 and S2." (Results)
- Punctum exhibits no molecular emission lines at the 1sigma level and shows no statistically significant flux variability across a four-year period (2019 to 2023).  
  > "Due to the faintness of the source, this spectral analysis mostly revealed noise, with no evidence of emission lines typically found in the molecular gas of NGC 4945, not even at the 1$\sigma$ level." (Results); "This indicates that the observed flux variations of *Punctum* over the four-year period remain within 3$\sigma$ uncertainties and are therefore not statistically significant." (Search for the counterparts)
- Punctum has no identified counterparts in existing radio, infrared, optical, or X-ray observations, and its combined mm luminosity and ~50% polarization do not match any known class of synchrotron-emitting astrophysical object.  
  > "A comparison of the radio, mm (including polarization), and X-ray properties with known astrophysical sources emitting synchrotron radiation, such as accreting neutron stars, supernova remnants, and non-thermal galactic filaments, revealed no clear match in any of these scenarios." (abstract); "To sum up, none of the currently known sources exhibit the combination of properties – most notably the mm luminosity and polarization – seen in *Punctum*, highlighting its distinct nature." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 4945 was observed as part of the first dedicated ALMA polarimetric campaign to investigate synchrotron mm emission from compact cores in radio-quiet AGNs and other mm sources in the galaxy; a second session (S2) was taken because the first session (S1) had insufficient parallactic angle coverage (51.12 deg instead of 60.00 deg) for the polarization calibrator.  
  > "To investigate the properties of synchrotron mm emission from compact cores in RQ AGNs, we initiated the first dedicated ALMA polarimetric campaign, with results detailed in (cite: shab25)." (Introduction); "During the first session (hereafter referred to as S1), an insufficient parallactic angle coverage (51.12$^\circ$ instead of the desired 60.00$^\circ$) for the polarization calibrator J1256–0547 prompted the need for a second session (hereafter S2)." (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: PD maps were masked at S/N < 5 and debiased following Montier et al. (2015a,b) to avoid bias and unreliable polarization measurements in low-S/N regions; candidate polarized sources were required to appear in both epochs S1 and S2 to confirm them as real.  
  > "The polarization degree was then calculated as PD = $P/I$, where $I$ is the total intensity, and debiased following the method outlined in (cite: montier1; montier2)." (Observations and data reduction); "To accurately estimate the polarization degree, we created the PD maps masking areas with a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $<$ 5 to avoid bias and unreliable polarization measurements in low-S/N regions." (Results)
- Alternative: Galactic foreground source: rejected due to the absence of an optical counterpart, suggesting it is embedded in NGC 4945 and obscured by dust.  
  > "The absence of an optical counterpart suggests that the source is not Galactic and is most likely embedded within the central region of the galaxy NGC 4945, obscured by dust." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Background blazar: rejected because blazars typically exhibit at most ~15% mm polarization in AMAPOLA and the extrapolated probability from number counts of finding a ~0.1 mJy blazar within 3.4'' of the nucleus is <0.4%.  
  > "Furthermore, the source is unlikely to be a background object, such as a blazar, which are known to exhibit much lower degrees of polarization in the mm band, typically $\sim$15% at maximum, according to the AMAPOLA database[^4]. Moreover, while the number density of blazars at such low flux levels is not commonly reported, we extrapolated the number counts from (cite: tucci11) and estimated that the probability of finding a blazar with a flux of $\sim$0.1 mJy within 3.4$''$ of the nucleus of NGC 4945 is less than 0.4%." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Gamma-ray burst (GRB) or transient event: rejected because Punctum shows no significant flux variations between 2019 and 2023 and GRBs have <1% mm polarization.  
  > "showing no significant flux variations beyond the 3$\sigma$ level between 2019 and 2023. This effectively rules out a transient event such as a gamma-ray burst (GRB)" (Discussion)
- Alternative: Dust scattering or magnetized thermal dust emission: rejected because dust polarization is much lower (~1% to 3-4%) and thermal dust has a steep positive spectral index (~3.5).  
  > "Similarly, polarization from magnetized thermal dust is much lower, ranging from $\lesssim$1% (cite: 9io9) to $\sim$3–4% (cite: bonavera17; Clements25). Moreover, the expected spectral index of dust emission in the mm regime is $\sim$3.5 (cite: mullaney11), which definitively rules out a dust-related origin." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Maser emission: rejected because only continuum emission was detected with no emission lines present.  
  > "As only continuum emission has been detected from this source, with no emission lines present, a maser origin is ruled out." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Pulsars and magnetars: rejected or disfavored because known mm-emitting pulsars and magnetars are 10^4 to 10^8 times fainter in mm luminosity than Punctum.  
  > "Assuming its distance $\sim$300 pc (cite: caraveo01), the Vela pulsar luminosity is $\sim$3 $\times$ 10$^{27}$ erg s$^{-1}$, which is $10^8$ times fainter than our source." (Discussion); "Despite the high polarization supporting a magnetar interpretation, the mm luminosities of known magnetars are too faint to match the observed source." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Supernova remnants (SNRs) and microquasars: disfavored because although SNRs can match the mm and X-ray luminosity, both SNRs (e.g., Crab Nebula at ~7-8%) and microquasars (e.g., SS 433 at <1%) exhibit much lower mm polarization.  
  > "However, the mm polarization of M1 is significantly lower ($\sim$7–8%; (cite: crab_pol)), and thus does not fully align with the high polarization observed in *Punctum*." (Discussion); "However, (cite: ss433) reported very low mm polarization (<1%) in the brightest microquasar SS 433, which contradicts our observations." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Non-thermal galactic filaments: disfavored because despite reaching up to 40-50% polarization, their 100 GHz luminosities (~few x 10^32 erg s^-1) are orders of magnitude fainter than Punctum.  
  > "Given their steep spectra, the luminosity at 100 GHz would decrease to only a few times $10^{32}$ erg s$^{-1}$, which remains insufficient to account for our mm source." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: Consistency of calibrator polarization degrees and angles with the AMAPOLA monitoring database at both S1 and S2 confirmed S1 reliability, and persistent detection across S1, S2, and the 2019 archival epoch (S/N ~ 6.1, 8.9, and 11) confirmed the source.  
  > "Furthermore, the estimated degree and angle of polarization for the calibrators were consistent with the measurements from the AMAPOLA calibrator monitoring[^2] at both the S1 and S2 epochs, indicating that the polarization measurements at S1 also remain reliable." (Observations and data reduction); "We measured its flux in each epoch, finding values of 0.104 $\pm$ 0.018 mJy for S1 and 0.125 $\pm$ 0.016 mJy for S2 averaged over all spectral windows with S/N $\approx$ 6.1 and 8.9, respectively." (Results); "In this case, the flux averaged over the spectral windows was measured to be 0.150 $\pm$ 0.015 mJy, assuming a 5% error and background rms $\sigma = 0.014$ mJy/beam, with S/N $\approx$ 11." (Search for the counterparts)
- Limitation (authors): During the first session (S1), the polarization calibrator J1256-0547 had insufficient parallactic angle coverage (51.12 deg instead of the desired 60.00 deg).  
  > "an insufficient parallactic angle coverage (51.12$^\circ$ instead of the desired 60.00$^\circ$) for the polarization calibrator J1256–0547" (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): The first (extended) polarized region is located at the edge of the bright unpolarized molecular disk, making it difficult to isolate and potentially reducing the measured polarization due to contamination.  
  > "Due to its proximity to a much brighter unpolarized mm emitting extended region, it is difficult to isolate the source of polarized emission. Contamination from the unpolarized component could significantly reduce the measured polarization." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Punctum is too faint to reliably measure the polarization degree per frequency or per spectral window, or to detect spectral lines.  
  > "Unfortunately, the source is too faint to reliably estimate the PD per frequency or per spectral window due to increased noise, preventing us from tracing polarization changes across the spectral windows." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainties in the Band 3 flux density and spectral index prevent reliable extrapolation to radio frequencies, identification of a spectral turnover, or constraining the source size via synchrotron self-absorption modeling.  
  > "In our ALMA Band 3 dataset, the flux density and spectral index uncertainties are so large that a reliable radio flux extrapolation is impossible." (Search for the counterparts); "Additionally, the large uncertainties in the spectral index across all epochs in both 2019 and 2023 prevent us from reliably identifying a spectral turnover, making it difficult to constrain the source size via synchrotron self-absorption modeling." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): When angular resolution is worse than 0.3'', Punctum cannot be separated from the millimeter emission of nearby structures.  
  > "When the resolution is worse than 0.3$''$, *Punctum* cannot be separated from the mm emission of other nearby structures." (Search for the counterparts)
- Limitation (authors): Spitzer observations are saturated, JWST data do not cover the region of interest, optical data (HST, MUSE) are heavily obscured by dust in the edge-on disk, and Chandra X-ray upper limits include unmodeled contributions from surrounding star-forming material.  
  > "For NGC 4945, data from the *James Webb Space Telescope* are available for other regions of the galaxy, though not for the zone of interest. *Spitzer* observations of the central region of NGC 4945 have saturated flux, making them unsuitable for further analysis." (Search for the counterparts); "It is important to note that this represents an upper limit, as contributions from surrounding star-forming material were not accounted for." (Search for the counterparts)
- Limitation (authors): The isotropic mm luminosity estimate does not account for potential relativistic beaming, which could imply a lower intrinsic luminosity.  
  > "[^5]: This estimate assumes isotropic mm emission. While the nature of the source remains uncertain, relativistic beaming cannot be excluded and may imply a lower intrinsic luminosity." (Footnote 5)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): At S/N ~ 6.1 and 8.9 in total intensity, the polarized intensity detection is only ~3-4.5 sigma before debiasing, making the exact value of PD sensitive to the choice of debiasing estimator and noise distribution in Q and U.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The in-band spectral index is unconstrained (uncertainties of +/- 2.4 to 2.5 over a narrow ~14 GHz lever arm), so a flat or inverted spectrum cannot be ruled out by the ALMA data alone.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The methods for extracting the flux density of Punctum (e.g., peak pixel vs. Gaussian fitting vs. aperture photometry) are not specified.
