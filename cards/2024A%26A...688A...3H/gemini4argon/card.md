## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify compact continuum cores and extract average spectra**: Identify 65 compact cores using the 50% peak-intensity contour around intensity peaks (>5 sigma_rms) on 1.2 mm continuum maps (in Paper II), and extract average spectra from primary beam corrected cubes over enclosed pixels.  
  > "A total of 65 compact sources were identified in the continuum images of the 20 fields in the LMC, defined by the 50% intensity contour level around well-defined intensity peaks (see Paper II)." (3. Results and analysis); "50% peak-intensity contour of the 1.2 mm continuum emission maps." (3.1 Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - software: spectral-cube
   - parameter: 50% peak-intensity contour aperture; peak continuum flux > 5 sigma_rms  
  > "A total of 65 compact sources were identified in the continuum images of the 20 fields in the LMC, defined by the 50% intensity contour level around well-defined intensity peaks (see Paper II)." (3. Results and analysis); "These objects have peak continuum fluxes above five times the image rms noise and constitute clear detections of compact sources." (3. Results and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Estimate spectral noise and identify molecular transitions**: Perform sigma-clipping analysis on each extracted spectrum to determine noise (sigma_rms), classify lines as definite (>5 sigma_rms) or tentative (3-5 sigma_rms), and identify transitions using the CDMS catalog.  
  > "we identified the molecular lines toward the most chemically rich sources (i.e., spectra with the largest number of line detections; see Fig. 3 for an example) using the CDMS catalog (Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy; Müller et al. 2005)." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "For this, we estimated the noise level (σ_rms) in each spectrum (i.e., for each source and spectral window) by performing a sigma clipping analysis." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "We considered the lines as a definite detection if their peak value exceeded 5σ_rms and as tentative features if the line strength is between 3 and 5σ_rms." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - parameter: Definite detection threshold > 5 sigma_rms; tentative detection threshold 3 to 5 sigma_rms; upper limit threshold S/N < 3  
  > "We considered the lines as a definite detection if their peak value exceeded 5σ_rms and as tentative features if the line strength is between 3 and 5σ_rms." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Model molecular spectra and derive physical parameters (T, N, FWHM, v_LSR)**: Solve 1D radiative transfer equation with XCLASS. Fit CH3OH in non-LTE (and LTE for comparison), SO2 and CH3CN in LTE when >2 transitions detected, and fix T for remaining molecules to the CH3OH temperature (or median 27 K). Use a two-layer model (sub-beam hot core + beam-filling cold envelope) for hot cores 4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A.  
  > "Based on these inaccuracies, we switched to non-LTE for fitting CH 3 OH, which resulted in better fits." (3.1 Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "Based on these inaccuracies, we switched to non-LTE for fitting CH3OH, which resulted in better fits." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "For four sources (4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A), a one-component model for CH3OH and SO2 did not give satisfactory results. A two-layered morphology with a hot central component and a colder envelope as the foreground provided the most convincing results." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - software: XCLASS, MAGIX
   - parameter: Beam-filling factor = 1 (for single-component fits and foreground envelope); A/E CH3OH ratio = 1; fixed n_col = 2.0e6 cm^-3 when <=2 CH3OH lines; fixed median T = 27 K when CH3OH undetected; fixed T_e = 10^4 K for H36beta fit  
  > "Therefore, we fixed the source size for all the molecules to a very large value to impose a beam-filling factor of one." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "We fixed the A to E CH3OH ratio to one and fit the collision partner density in the cores with more than two CH3OH lines." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "For the sources with one or two CH3OH lines, we fixed the density of collision partners to the median value of the rest of the cores: 2.0 × 10^6 cm^-3." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - assumption: All molecular species are located within a core layer at the position of the source (except the 4 two-component hot cores); isotopologues share the same temperature and velocity profile as main species; LAMDA collision rates apply for non-LTE CH3OH.  
  > "Using XCLASS, we analyzed the spectra assuming that all the considered molecular species are located within a core layer at the position of the source (i.e., we did not consider foreground clouds located between the source and the observer)." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "We used the flexibility of XCLASS to link the isotopologues to the main species, giving them the same temperature and velocity profile to fit the isotopologue ratio from the ratio of column densities." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - fitting: Chi-squared minimization in MAGIX using a genetic algorithm (global minimizer) followed by Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm (local minimizer), plus MCMC on a few spectra for error estimation.  
  > "The fitting procedure in XCLASS uses an algorithm chain from the MAGIX interface, including the genetic algorithm as a global minimizer and the Levenberg-Marquart algorithm as a local minimizer." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "We also ran XCLASS with a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm on a few spectra to estimate typical errors on the different fitting parameters." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
   - validation: Compared LTE and non-LTE fits for CH3OH and SO2; ran MCMC on a subset of spectra; compared derived temperatures and abundances for 4A, 6A, and 6B against literature values (ST16, N105-2 B, N105-2 A).  
  > "For these sources, we compared the LTE and non-LTE fits, finding no major differences in the output temperatures and fit quality." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "Comparisons of the derived temperatures (left) and abundances (right) from the current study and the literature." (Figure 12 caption)
4. **Calculate core masses, H2 column densities, and fractional molecular abundances**: Calculate dust and gas masses and H2 column densities from 1.2 mm continuum using XCLASS temperatures (in Paper II), then divide molecular column densities N(X) by N(H2) to obtain fractional abundances.  
  > "We used the temperatures estimated in the current paper to calculate the dust and gas mass of each core, assuming that the 1.2 mm continuum emission originates in optically thin dust" (3 Results and analysis); "The abundances were derived by dividing the column density of every molecule by the molecular hydrogen column density (N(H2), derived in Paper II)." (3.2.3. Chemical contents of the cores)
   - parameter: Dust opacity = 1.04 cm^2 g^-1; gas-to-dust mass ratio = 316 (or 320); metallicity scaling factors: 5 (SMC), 2.6 (LMC), 4 (outer Galaxy)

## Qualitative findings

- Four hot molecular cores (4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A, with 14A being a new discovery) and one hot core candidate (3A) are identified among the 65 continuum cores, raising the total number of known LMC hot cores to seven.  
  > "According to the chemical richness of the cores and high temperatures from the XCLASS fitting, we report the detection of four hot cores and one hot core candidate. With one new hot core detection in this study, the number of detected hot cores in the LMC increases to seven." (abstract)
- S-bearing molecules are on average more present and abundant than N- and O-bearing species across the 65 LMC cores, and the four hot cores exhibit the largest SO2 abundances.  
  > "we observed that S-bearing species are, on average, more abundant than N- and O-bearing species." (3.2.3 Chemical contents of the cores); "Interestingly, these four sources are those with the largest SO2 abundances, thus supporting the interpretation by Shimonishi et al. (2023) that bright SO2 lines are a good tracer of hot molecular cores in the low-metallicity environment of the LMC and SMC." (3.2.3. Chemical contents of the cores)
- Six out of the seven known hot cores in the LMC are located in the LMC stellar bar region and show COM emission, whereas the only known COM-devoid hot core (ST11) lies outside the bar.  
  > "Six out of seven hot cores detected in the LMC to date are located in the stellar bar region of this galaxy. These six hot cores show emission from complex organic molecules (COMs), such as CH3OH, CH3CN, CH3OCHO, and CH3OCH3. The only known hot core in the LMC with no detection of COMs is located outside the bar region." (abstract)
- Comparisons with LMC chemical models indicate that initial dust temperatures of 10 to 20 K reproduce the observed hot core abundances, whereas warm initial dust (25 K) underpredicts them.  
  > "the models where the initial dust temperature is assumed to be between 10 and 20 K reproduce the observed molecular abundances." (4 Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 20 massive Spitzer YSOs without ice features or strong fine-structure lines, located away from strong H-alpha emission, and bright at Herschel 160 um to target the hot core evolutionary stage with cold initial conditions and high dust column densities; ALMA Band 6 at ~0.4" resolves ~0.09 pc core scales at the LMC distance.  
  > "We selected YSOs without ice features or strong fine-structure lines in their Spitzer IRS spectra (Seale et al. 2009) and with Herschel counterparts at submillimeter wavelengths (Seale et al. 2014) to maximize the chance of selecting the evolutionary stage of hot cores and not earlier or later evolutionary stages." (2.1. Sample selection); "The selected sources are all bright at 160 µm to ensure the presence of large amounts of dust and large column densities, favoring the detection of molecular lines." (2.1. Sample selection); "which resulted in an angular resolution of ≈ 0''.4 (corresponding to ∼ 0.09 pc at the distance of the LMC)" (2.2. ALMA observations)
- Why this method: Used XCLASS non-LTE fitting for CH3OH because LTE fits yielded unrealistically low temperatures (5-17 K) and poorly fit line intensities; used a two-component (hot core + cold foreground envelope) model for 4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A because a single-component model could not fit both low- and high-excitation CH3OH and SO2 lines.  
  > "In the first iteration, we fit CH3OH using an LTE approximation, which resulted in low temperatures (5–17 K; see Table B.1) and some line features to be underfit or overfit (see Fig. B.1). Based on these inaccuracies, we switched to non-LTE for fitting CH3OH, which resulted in better fits." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores); "For four sources (4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A), a one-component model for CH3OH and SO2 did not give satisfactory results. A two-layered morphology with a hot central component and a colder envelope as the foreground provided the most convincing results." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
- Alternative: 1D LTE radiative transfer fitting for CH3OH (rejected in favor of non-LTE because LTE yielded 5–17 K temperatures and underfit/overfit line features)  
  > "In the first iteration, we fit CH3OH using an LTE approximation, which resulted in low temperatures (5–17 K; see Table B.1) and some line features to be underfit or overfit (see Fig. B.1). Based on these inaccuracies, we switched to non-LTE for fitting CH3OH, which resulted in better fits." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
- Alternative: Non-LTE fitting for SO2 across the sample (tested on 5 sources, found no major differences from LTE, so LTE was adopted for all fields)  
  > "However, only in five sources were there enough SO2 lines to fit the spectra using non-LTE. For these sources, we compared the LTE and non-LTE fits, finding no major differences in the output temperatures and fit quality. Therefore, we fit SO2 for all the fields using an LTE approach." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
- Alternative: Single-component model for CH3OH and SO2 in sources 4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A (rejected in favor of a two-layered hot core + cold foreground envelope model)  
  > "For four sources (4A, 6A, 6B, and 14A), a one-component model for CH3OH and SO2 did not give satisfactory results. A two-layered morphology with a hot central component and a colder envelope as the foreground provided the most convincing results." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Possible thermal free-free emission contamination in the 1.2 mm continuum flux for at least the most massive core (3A, where H36beta is tentatively detected), causing core mass, H2 column density, and H2 volume density to be overestimated.  
  > "1.2 mm continuum fluxes for at least the most massive core. Therefore, the higher-end value of the masses, H 2 column, and volume densities may be overestimated." (3 Results and analysis (footnote 2)); "The mass derived for this source is likely overestimated in the current analysis." (3.2.3. Chemical contents of the cores)
- Limitation (authors): Degeneracy between column density and source size in the absence of optically thick transitions (requiring a fixed beam-filling factor of 1 for single-component models).  
  > "In the absence of optically thick transitions, column density and source size are degenerate and cannot be determined separately. Therefore, we fixed the source size for all the molecules to a very large value to impose a beam-filling factor of one." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
- Limitation (authors): Degeneracy between temperature, column density, and collision partner density in the non-LTE XCLASS solution, resulting in large parameter uncertainties (40%-60% on temperature, 20%-40% on column density, 10%-30% on line width and velocity shift).  
  > "The error values range from 40%-60% for the temperatures, 20%-40% for the column densities, and 10%-30% for the line widths and velocity shifts. In the non-LTE solution, there is a degeneracy in defining the temperature, column density, and collision partner density that makes it hard to agree on an error value." (3.1. Spectral analysis of the continuum cores)
- Limitation (authors): Maximum recoverable scale of ~5" (~1.2 pc) is insufficient to map large extended structures.  
  > "This maximum recoverable scale, although not enough to map large extended structures, allowed us to detect dense cores and clumps with typical sizes ≤ 0.5 pc." (2.2. ALMA observations)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity of the dataset is insufficient to detect fainter N- and O-bearing COMs (such as HDCO, CH2CO, and NH2CHO previously seen in deeper observations of N105).  
  > "Finally, we searched for detections of other species, such as HDCO, CH2CO, and NH2CHO, that had been previously detected by Sewiło et al. (2022). We could not confirm their detection because the expected brightness of the species is less than the sensitivity of the current dataset." (4.1.2. Comparison of 4A, 6A, and 6B with previous studies); "Therefore, the detection of N- and O-bearing species, especially the COMs, needs higher sensitivity in future observations." (5. Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): The Acharyya & Herbst (2018) chemical models used for comparison do not include shock chemistry.  
  > "This model does not include the effects of shocks in the chemical evolution." (4.2.2. Comparison with chemical models); "The higher observed abundances could imply the presence of shocks in the hot cores of the LMC, which is not included in the modelings." (Appendix C: Comparisons of molecular abundances in hot cores with chemical models)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing the sub-beam source size for the hot component in the 4 two-layer hot core models directly controls the derived hot-component column densities, yet the exact adopted sub-beam sizes are not reported in this paper.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For molecules with only 1-2 transitions, fixing the excitation temperature to the CH3OH kinetic temperature assumes co-spatial emission and identical excitation conditions, which may not hold for shock tracers like SiO or envelope tracers like H13CO+.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sample selection deliberately avoided strong H-alpha emission (prominent HII regions), which could introduce selection biases when drawing galaxy-wide conclusions about where hot cores form in the LMC.
