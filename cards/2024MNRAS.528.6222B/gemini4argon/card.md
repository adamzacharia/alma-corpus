## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce HST WFC3/F110W snapshot observations and apply astrometric correction**: Flatfield correction, background subtraction, cosmic-ray cleaning, distortion correction, rotation, drizzling with 4-point sub-pixel dithering, and matching Gaia eDR3 sources to HST counterparts.  
  > "Each set of observations is flatfield corrected, background subtracted, cosmic-ray cleaned, distortion corrected, rotated and drizzled." (2.2 HST snapshot observations and data reduction); "Additionally, we perform a first-order astrometric correction by matching the Gaia early Data Release 3 sources (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2021) with their nearest HST counterparts." (2.2 HST snapshot observations and data reduction)
   - software: AstroDrizzle
   - parameter: output pixel size = 0.064 arcsec; FOV = 136 arcsec x 123 arcsec  
  > "For drizzling, we use a 4-point sub-pixel dithering pattern that results in an output pixel size of 0.064 arcsec, which is roughly half of the original pixel size. The size of the field of view (FOV) is 136 arcsec × 123 arcsec." (2.2 HST snapshot observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Classify lens candidates via visual inspection and multiwavelength archival data**: Visual inspection of HST images, cross-matching with high-resolution ALMA, SMA, and NOEMA continuum images to identify multiple images/arcs or unlensed DSFGs, and updating classification after lens subtraction.  
  > "In order to allocate the 281 objects to the four classes, we proceed by first carrying out a visual inspection of the HST data alone, without performing any galaxy subtraction and without relying on any multiwavelength ancillary data." (3 LENS CLASSIFICATION); "As a second step in the classification, we exploit the available high spatial resolution sub-mm/mm data to look for evidence of multiple images and arcs." (3 LENS CLASSIFICATION)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Model and subtract foreground lens surface brightness in HST images**: Residual sky subtraction using source masks and biweight location; PSF construction from stacked smoothed point sources; 2D parametric surface brightness decomposition (Sersic, de Vaucouleurs, Gaussian, exponential disk, PSF) or non-parametric isophotal fitting for edge-on disks; error estimation via Monte Carlo mock galaxy simulations.  
  > "For the parametric modelling, we adopt the Galaxy Surface Photometry 2-Dimensional Decomposition algorithm (GASP2D; Méndez-Abreu et al. 2008, 2017) to model the type 1 and type 3 lensing systems and the Galaxy Fitting algorithm (GALFIT; Peng et al. 2002, 2010) for the type 2 foreground lensing systems." (4 SURFACE BRIGHTNESS MODELLING); "We adopt the non-parametric Isophote Fitting algorithm (ISOFIT; Ciambur 2015) to model highly-inclined disk lenses for which GASP2D and GALFIT fail." (4 SURFACE BRIGHTNESS MODELLING); "Therefore, we estimate the errors on the fitted parameters by analysing a sample of mock galaxies we build through a series of Monte Carlo (MC) simulations following Dalla Bontà et al. (2018)." (4.1.5 Error estimate)
   - software: GASP2D, GALFIT, ISOFIT, CMODEL, IRAF, IDL, photutils, astropy, sgolay2
   - parameter: Sersic index range [0.3, 10]; effective radius range [0.5 FWHM_PSF, +inf)  
  > "which are [0.3, 10] for the Sérsic index and [0.5 FWHM PSF, +∞) for the effective radius" (4.1.4 Parametric modelling)
   - assumption: Planck18 cosmology: H0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_0,m = 0.31, Omega_0,Lambda = 0.69  
  > "In this paper we adopt the values of 𝐻0 = 67.7 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ω0,m = 0.31, and Ω0,Λ = 0.69 from Planck18 cosmology" (1 INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: Iterative non-linear least-squares chi^2 minimization  
  > "They derive the best-fitting values of the structural parameters by comparing the model of the surface brightness distribution with the observed photon counts of the galaxy in each image pixel with an iterative procedure of non-linear least-squares minimization." (4.1 Parametric approaches)
   - validation: Visual inspection of residual images and Monte Carlo simulations of >7800 mock galaxies confirming biweight location of relative/absolute error distributions is consistent with zero.  
  > "Then, we visually inspect the residual image to look for spurious sources and/or lensing features." (4.1.4 Parametric modelling); "We do not identify any systematic error, as all biweight location values are consistent with zero." (4.1.5 Error estimate)
4. **Derive global lens photometric properties and K-corrections**: Compute curve of growth from 10^4 MC realizations of component parameters to derive R50, mu50, m_F110W, and C31 = R75/R25; measure aperture photometry of lensed background sources at 1.1 um; compute K-corrections using Brown Atlas elliptical templates.  
  > "For all the combinations, we estimate 𝑅50 by computing the curve of growth (COG) and retrieving the radius within which half of the total flux is encircled." (4.4 Properties of the lenses); "Lastly, we measure the flux densities at 1.1 𝜇m 𝑆1.1 of the background sources by taking the aperture photometry in the regions dominated by the lensing features on the lens subtracted residuals." (4.4 Properties of the lenses); "As a template for the ETG SED, we use the 17 local elliptical galaxies available in the Brown Atlas (Brown et al. 2014)." (6 Discussion)
   - software: ISOFIT, PySynphot
   - parameter: 10^4 realizations per galaxy  
  > "For each galaxy, we sampled 10^4 different combinations of its components to take into account the uncertainties of the best-fitting model of the surface brightness distribution." (4.4 Properties of the lenses)
   - assumption: Cosmological surface brightness dimming factor (1 + z)^4; 17 local elliptical galaxy SED templates from Brown Atlas  
  > "We also correct the total effective surface brightness for cosmological dimming by multiplying the F110W fluxes by a factor of (1 + 𝑧)^4." (4.4 Properties of the lenses); "As a template for the ETG SED, we use the 17 local elliptical galaxies available in the Brown Atlas (Brown et al. 2014)." (6 DISCUSSION)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of median C31 with Watkins et al. (2022) and lens properties with SLACS survey (Bolton et al. 2008).  
  > "We measure a median ⟨𝐶31⟩ = 6.8 ± 0.4, which is slightly higher than the median value ⟨𝐶31⟩ = 5.2 ± 0.1 measured by Watkins et al. (2022)" (4.4 Properties of the lenses); "We compare the properties of our lenses (i.e., total effective radii, Einstein radii, and total effective surface brightnesses) to those of the SLACS sample of confirmed lenses (Bolton et al. 2008)." (6 DISCUSSION)
5. **Perform gravitational lens modelling and background source reconstruction**: Fit singular isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) mass model (plus external shear for S_3, S_19, S_40) and reconstruct background source using a preliminary Sersic parametric fit followed by a Voronoi pixelization with constant regularization.  
  > "we adopt a singular isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) for the mass model" (5 Lens modelling); "In order to reduce the complexity of the lens model, we adopt a singular isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) for the mass model (see Nightingale et al. 2018 for details)." (5 LENS MODELLING); "We then use the posteriors of the mass model obtained in this parametric fit and the priors on the position of the background sources on the image plane to initialize a Voronoi pixelization with a constant regularization coefficient 𝜆" (5 LENS MODELLING)
   - software: PyAutoLens, dynesty
   - parameter: Voronoi pixelization shape prior [20, 45] pixels; regularization coefficient lambda prior [10^-6, 10^6]; SNR > 3 threshold on source plane for magnification and circularised radius  
  > "For the pixelization, we use as priors a uniform distribution between 20 and 45 pixels for the shape of the pixelization and a log-uniform distribution between 10−6 and 106 for 𝜆." (5 LENS MODELLING); "For each modelled lens, we extract 500 fits from the lens modelling parameters posteriors, and, for each fit, we compute the regions of the source plane with SNR > 3." (5 LENS MODELLING)
   - assumption: Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid (SIE) mass distribution  
  > "In order to reduce the complexity of the lens model, we adopt a singular isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) for the mass model (see Nightingale et al. 2018 for details)." (5 LENS MODELLING)
   - fitting: Nested sampling non-linear search via dynesty with Gaussian, uniform, and log-uniform priors  
  > "The lens modelling is performed with PyAutoLens [11] (Nightingale et al. 2018, 2021), by using a non-linear search trough dynesty (Speagle 2020) [12]" (5 Lens modelling)
   - validation: Comparison of derived Einstein radii with literature measurements from near-IR and sub-mm/mm observations (Table 8, Fig. 12).  
  > "A comparison between our Einstein radii and those available in the literature (Bussmann et al. 2013, 2015; Calanog et al. 2014; Geach et al. 2015; Enia et al. 2018; Dye et al. 2022; Maresca et al. 2022; Kamieneski et al. 2023a) is shown in Table 8 and in Fig. 12." (5 LENS MODELLING)

## Qualitative findings

- The overall surface brightness distribution and concentration of the foreground lenses follow expectations for early-type galaxies (ETGs), though ~47% require multiple surface brightness components.  
  > "A high fraction of these galaxies ( 47%) show complex surface brightness profiles that often need more than one component (e.g., two or three Sérsic profiles or a combination of Sérsic profiles and PSF)." (4.3 Surface brightness modelling results); "the overall surface brightness distribution of the lenses and their morphology follows the relations expected for ETGs; however, the foreground lensing galaxies often needed multiple surface brightness components" (7 CONCLUSIONS)
- Most confirmed lensing systems are single-galaxy lenses (51 out of 65), with only ~7% (4 systems) spectroscopically confirmed as group or cluster lenses.  
  > "our 65 confirmed lenses mostly comprise systems where the lens is a single galaxy (51). Only 4 are made of spectroscopically confirmed groups or clusters (S_36, S_48, S_60, and S_61)" (6 DISCUSSION); "most of the lensing systems are consistent with single galaxy lenses, with only ∼ 7% of them being confirmed as a group or cluster lenses;" (7 CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HST WFC3/F110W snapshot observations maximize SNR in short exposures to cover stellar emission of foreground ETG lenses and resolve arcsecond-scale lensing features, while archival ALMA/SMA/NOEMA data detect dust-obscured lensed background sources.  
  > "This observing band was chosen to maximise the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for short exposures and efficiently cover the stellar emission from the foreground galaxy in the gravitational lensing system, usually an ETG (e.g., Auger et al. 2009)." (2.2 HST snapshot observations and data reduction); "Because lensed DSFGs emit mostly in the far-IR/sub-mm/mm (Negrello et al. 2014), the best way to detect and characterise the multiple images is via high-angular resolution observations at sub-mm/mm wavelengths obtained with interferometers" (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: GASP2D automatically sets initial parameter guesses for single/well-separated lenses, GALFIT enables simultaneous fitting of blended multi-galaxy lenses, and ISOFIT handles highly inclined disk lenses where parametric fits fail.  
  > "GASP2D has the advantage of automatically setting the initial guess values needed to fit the galaxy image, whereas GALFIT allows us to perform the simultaneous fit of multiple systems." (4.1 Parametric approaches)
- Alternative: Global sky subtraction from standard HST pipeline reduction was rejected as unreliable for faint lensing features in favor of custom source-masked biweight sky subtraction.  
  > "The sky level provided by the image reduction consists of a global estimate across the FOV after automatically masking the luminous sources. This subtraction proves to be unreliable in the analysis of the faintest lensing features since a residual sky level could still be present in the image." (4.1.1 Residual sky subtraction)
- Alternative: Formal chi^2 minimization errors on surface brightness parameters were rejected because they underestimate true uncertainties, motivating Monte Carlo mock galaxy simulations.  
  > "The formal errors on the fitted parameters obtained from the 𝜒2 minimisation procedure usually underestimate the real errors of the structural parameters (e.g., Méndez-Abreu et al. 2008)." (4.1.5 Error estimate)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Because the HST snapshot program only observed 290 of 398 candidates with priority given to brighter/known sources, statistical conclusions on the parent population cannot be drawn.  
  > "Given that this sub-sample represents a random collection of the entire candidate population, we limit our focus on presenting the properties of this sub-sample without drawing any statistical conclusion on the parent sample" (2.1 Sample selection)
- Limitation (authors): Missing spectroscopic redshifts for secondary galaxies in type 2 and 3 systems prevent confirming whether all foreground galaxies contribute to the lensing.  
  > "It is worth noticing that for type 2 and type 3 systems, we can not be sure whether all the galaxies are contributing to the lensing. In most cases, we do not know the redshift of all the galaxies, so we can not rule out the possibility that they are unrelated field objects." (4 SURFACE BRIGHTNESS MODELLING)
- Limitation (authors): Shallow HST snapshot depth may miss faint, high-redshift, or heavily dust-obscured lensed background sources or disk-wide star-forming knots.  
  > "Moreover, the depth of our observations does not allow us to exclude that other, more obscured and undetected, star-forming knots are present throughout the disk, implying that mass buildup could happen disk-wide." (6 DISCUSSION); "To conclude, we point out that the HST snapshots alone, even after carefully identifying and subtracting the candidate lenses, might miss some of the fainter lensed background sources." (7 CONCLUSIONS)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison between HST near-IR source sizes and SMA sub-mm sizes is not performed on an identical sample (only partially overlapping) and uses different size definitions (SNR > 3 pixelized area vs parametric Sersic effective radius).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Many lens and source redshifts used to convert angular quantities to physical sizes and absolute magnitudes are photometric redshifts with large uncertainties.
