## Analysis recipe

1. **Combine archival multi-array ALMA data and extract multi-aperture spectra from ALMA line cubes**: Combine 12m, ACA, and total power data for archival IRC+10216 SiS J=5-4; convolve ALMA Band 3 spectral line cubes to progressively larger circular Gaussian beams until line flux reaches a plateau, and extract spectra at each beam size  
  > "The line cubes were convolved to progressively larger circular Gaussian beams, with full width at half maximum (FWHM) beginning at the major-axis size of the original synthesised beam and gradually increasing until the resulting line flux reached a plateau" (Spectral line surveys); "We combined these datasets using the same process as employed in , and extracted multi-aperture spectra from the combined cube." (Supplementary observations)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS, Astropy
   - parameter: Circular Gaussian beam FWHMs starting from original synthesised beam major-axis up to total emission extent  
  > "with full width at half maximum (FWHM) beginning at the major-axis size of the original synthesised beam and gradually increasing until the resulting line flux reached a plateau, implying that all detected emission has been recovered." (Spectral line surveys)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with archival single-dish observations to verify no flux was resolved out; compared APEX SiS spectra with literature to verify calibration consistency  
  > "Comparing with archival SD observations (cite: Woods_et_al_2003), we find no evidence of resolved out flux in the lines of these species in our ALMA observations." (Spectral line surveys); "For IRAS 15194$-$5115 and IRAS 07454$-$7112, we compared our APEX SiS line spectra to those from (cite: Danilovich_et_al_2018), and found them to be identical within the calibration uncertainties." (Supplementary observations)
2. **Model non-LTE radiative transfer of SiO, SiS, and isotopologues to derive abundance profiles**: Run 1D accelerated lambda iteration (ALI) non-LTE radiative transfer models across 2D grids of peak abundance (f0) and e-folding radius (Re), and find best-fit models by chi-squared minimisation against multi-aperture ALMA and single-dish integrated line intensities and intersection of isotopologue contours  
  > "We use the latest version of the 1D accelerated lambda iteration (ALI) non-LTE spectral line RT code (cite: Maercker_et_al_2008; Danilovich_et_al_2018; Danilovich_et_al_2019) to model the line emission in this work." (Modelling procedure); "To find the best-fit models, we employ $\chi^2$ minimisation between the modelled and observed integrated line intensities, taking into account the spatial constraints from the multi-aperture ALMA spectra, and covering a large range of excitation conditions traced by multiple SD lines." (Modelling procedure); "we model all the above isotopologues with both $f_0$ and $R_\mathrm{e}$ as free parameters, and use the intersection of the respective $1\sigma$ $\chi^2$ contours of all modelled isotopologues per molecule, to determine the overall range of plausible models for each species." (Modelling procedure)
   - software: ALI
   - parameter: SiO, 29SiO, 30SiO: J = 0-40 in v = 0, 1 (82 levels, 160 radiative transitions)  
  > "in our $^{28}$SiO, $^{29}$SiO, $^{30}$SiO models, we include energy levels $J$ = 0 $-$ 40, from both the $v = 0$ and $v = 1$ vibrational levels, leading to a total of 82 levels and 160 radiative transitions." (Molecular data)
   - parameter: 28SiS: J = 0-99 in v = 0, 1, 2 (300 levels, 891 radiative transitions); 29SiS, 30SiS, Si34S: J = 0-40 in v = 0, 1 (82 levels, 160 transitions)  
  > "For $^{28}$SiS, we include energy levels from $J$ = 0 to 99 from each of the vibrational levels $v = 0,~1,$ and 2, amounting to a total of 300 levels and 891 radiative transitions, including ro-vibrational transitions." (Molecular data); "For the other SiS isotopologues studied in this work, we included energy levels from $J$ = 0 to 40 in the vibrational levels $v = 0$ and $v = 1$, totalling 82 levels, with 160 radiative transitions." (Molecular data)
   - parameter: 820 collisional rate coefficients for v=0 up to J=40 with H2  
  > "the $v=0$ SiO-H$_2$ and SiS-H$_2$ collisional rates, scaled and extrapolated from the SiO-H$_2$ rates by (cite: Dayou_and_Balanca_2006), covering levels up to $J = 40$ and giving a total of 820 collisional rate coefficients, were used for all studied isotopologues." (Molecular data)
   - assumption: Spherically symmetric, smooth CSE with isotropic, constant mass-loss rate  
  > "We adopt a spherically symmetric, smooth CSE model, with an isotropic, constant MLR as in ." (Modelling procedure)
   - assumption: Gaussian fractional abundance profile f(r) = f0 * exp(-(r/Re)^2) relative to H2  
  > "All fractional abundances used in this work are defined as relative abundances with respect to molecular hydrogen (H$_2$), as in . We use Gaussian abundance profiles, given by $$\begin{equation}
f(r) = f_0 \exp\left(-\left(\frac{r}{R_e}\right)^2\right),
\label{eq:gaussian_abundance_profile}
\end{equation}$$" (Modelling procedure)
   - assumption: Only H2 considered as collision partner (He neglected); amorphous carbon dust opacities from Suh (2000)  
  > "Our RT code can only take into account one collision partner and hence we have not taken into consideration collisions with other partners, such as He." (Molecular data); "We tested changing the input dust opacities for amorphous carbon grains from those adopted from (cite: Suh_2000) to those of (cite: Preibisch_et_al_1993)" (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch)
   - assumption: Blended 30SiO J=5-4 and Si34S J=12-11 lines and anomalous SiS J=6-5 line excluded from fitting; IRC+10216 SiO constrained using ALMA spectra only  
  > "We note that for $^{30}$SiO, for all five sources, we only use the $J = 4 - 3$ and $J = 6 - 5$ lines, as the $J = 5 - 4$ line is blended with the Si$^{34}$S $J = 12 - 11$ line." (SiO); "We therefore choose to constrain our models using the SiS $J = 5 - 4$ ALMA line and all available SD lines, and exclude the $J = 6 - 5$ lines from this analysis, for all sources." (SiS); "Overall, for IRC +10 216, given the incompatibility between the ALMA and SD spectra, we choose to constrain our SiO and isotopologue models using only our ALMA spectra" (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch)
   - fitting: 2D grid search over (f0, Re) using chi-squared minimisation between modelled and observed integrated line intensities, taking the intersection of 1-sigma (or 2-sigma for IRAS 07454-7112 SiS) contours across isotopologues  
  > "To find the best-fit models, we employ $\chi^2$ minimisation between the modelled and observed integrated line intensities, taking into account the spatial constraints from the multi-aperture ALMA spectra, and covering a large range of excitation conditions traced by multiple SD lines." (Modelling procedure); "For this source, we use the overlapping region of the 2$\sigma$ contours of $^{28}$SiS and $^{29}$SiS to define the best-fit region (see Fig. 9), as the 1$\sigma$ contours covered only unrealistically narrow ranges." (IRAS 07454-7112)
   - validation: Tested extended vibrational levels (v=0-6 for SiO, v=0-5 for SiS), stellar luminosity variations (5000-16000 L_sun), alternative dust opacities (Preibisch et al. 1993), turning off IR pumping, setting SiS v=1-0 J=6-5 Einstein A-coefficient to zero, and step-function/non-Gaussian abundance profiles  
  > "we tested an SiO molecular description which included levels ranging from $v = 0 - 6$. The resulting modelled line profiles match those modelled using only the $v$ = 0,1 levels within the observational uncertainties." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch); "we tested an extended SiS dataset including the levels $v = 0 - 5$ along with the vibration-rotation transitions with $\Delta v = 1, 2$ for the $v=0, 1$, and 2 levels, and found that the resulting spectra matched with those modelled using only the $v = 0,~1, 2$ levels." (SiS); "We tested whether radiative excitation via IR pumping (cite: Agundez_and_Cernicharo_2006; Agundez_et_al_2012; Velilla-Prieto_et_al_2019) contributes to the line excitation of SiO and SiS." (Radiative excitation)
3. **Calculate silicon and sulfur isotopic ratios**: Take the ratio of the best-fit peak abundance (f0) values of the respective isotopologues derived from the RT models  
  > "Table notes: Isotopic ratios are calculated as the ratio of the $f_0$ values of the best-fit models for the relevant species (Table [\[tab:RT_modelling_results\]](#tab:RT_modelling_results))." (Discussion)
   - assumption: No differential self-shielding between isotopologues because photodissociation occurs via broadband continuum  
  > "These species are photodissociated by radiation across the broadband continuum, and hence do not exhibit differential self-shielding between their isotopologues." (Modelling procedure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived isotopic ratios with solar isotopic ratios and previous single-dish literature values  
  > "The calculated ratios are very close to the solar values for all five sources, as in the case of the $^{32}$S/$^{34}$S ratios derived from CS abundances in ." (Isotopic ratios)
4. **Run 1D circumstellar chemical models and compare with RT-derived abundance profiles**: Compute radial chemical abundance profiles for each source using a 1D CSE chemical code with the RATE22 UMIST network, using RT-derived initial abundances for SiO and SiS, and compare resulting radial profiles and e-folding radii with RT results  
  > "We modelled the chemistry in the CSEs of our sources using a circumstellar chemical model based on the `RATE22` update of the UMIST Database for Astrochemistry (cite: Millar_et_al_2024)." (Chemical modelling); "We ran individual models for each of our sources, with the input physical parameters customised to match their circumstellar properties as modelled in , and set the initial abundances of SiO and SiS to those derived from our RT models for each source." ## Qualitative findings

- SiS peak abundances are very similar across the carbon star sample (2.0e-6 to 4.7e-6), whereas SiO peak abundances in IRAS 15194-5115, IRAS 15082-4808, and IRAS 07454-7112 are ~3-5 times larger than in IRC +10 216.  
  > "While the SiS peak abundances are very similar across the sample (2.0$\times$10$^{-6}-4.7\times$10$^{-6}$), we find that the SiO peak abundances of the rest of the stars are a factor of $\sim$5 larger than that of IRC +10 216." (abstract); "Our results show that the SiO abundances for IRAS 15194$-$5115, IRAS 15082$-$4808, and IRAS 07454$-$7112 are consistently larger than that of IRC +10 216, by a factor of $\sim$3–5, which is significant beyond the 1$\sigma$ uncertainties of the corresponding models" (Discussion)
- The e-folding radii (Re) of both SiO and SiS increase with circumstellar mass-loss gas density, and generally follow R_out,SiS < R_out,SiO < R_out,CS.  
  > "The $R_\mathrm{e}$ increases with gas density for both SiO and SiS." (abstract); "The extents of the molecular emitting regions derived from the azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles of CS, SiO, and SiS line emission from also follow the same order, i.e. $R_\mathrm{out, SiS}$ < $R_\mathrm{out, SiO}$ < $R_\mathrm{out, CS}$" (Discussion)
- 1D RT models cannot simultaneously fit the ALMA low-J (J=2-1) and single-dish higher-J SiO lines for IRC +10 216, nor can they simultaneously fit the ALMA SiS J=5-4 and J=6-5 lines across the sample.  
  > "Our RT models cannot simultaneously fit the low- and high-$J$ SiO lines of IRC+10216." (abstract); "We note that for all sources, except AFGL 3068 where we do not have ALMA data, the ALMA spectra for the SiS $J = 5 - 4$ and $6 - 5$ lines cannot be simultaneously fit, as all models that fit the SiS $J = 5 - 4$ line significantly over-predict the SiS $J = 6 - 5$ line." (SiS)
- Infrared pumping via dust emission significantly contributes to the excitation of both SiO and SiS, reducing modelled line intensities by 30-60% for SiO and 30-50% for SiS when turned off.  
  > "The modelled line intensities drop by $\sim$30-60% for SiO, and $\sim$30-50% for SiS, when IR pumping is turned off." (Radiative excitation)
- Derived silicon (28Si/29Si, 28Si/30Si, 29Si/30Si) and sulfur (32S/34S) isotopic ratios are close to solar values across all five sources.  
  > "We estimated the $^{28}$Si/$^{29}$Si, $^{28}$Si/$^{30}$Si, $^{29}$Si/$^{30}$Si, and $^{32}$S/$^{34}$S isotopic ratios from the derived abundances of SiO, SiS and their isotopologues (see Table [\[tab:isotopic_ratios\]](#tab:isotopic_ratios)). The calculated ratios are very close to the solar values for all five sources" (Isotopic ratios)
- Chemical models reproduce the SiO radial abundance profiles well, but consistently over-predict the SiS e-folding radii, implying the assumed SiS photodissociation rate is underestimated.  
  > "Chemical models reproduce the derived SiO abundance profiles well, while over-predicting the SiS $R_\mathrm{e}$ values." (abstract); "We find that the currently assumed SiS photodissociation rate in chemical models is underestimated." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining spatially resolved ALMA multi-aperture spectra with single-dish transitions across a wide range of J levels simultaneously constrains both the radial morphological extent and the excitation conditions throughout the CSE.  
  > "Such integrated modelling, simultaneously employing both excitation and morphological constraints, represents a necessary advancement in accurately determining molecular abundances in CSEs." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Extracting spectra from ALMA cubes convolved to progressively larger Gaussian beams mimics single-dish observations of increasing beam size, allowing 1D non-LTE ALI radiative transfer models to directly fit radial intensity variations alongside single-dish data.  
  > "Extracting spectra from these convolved cubes allows tracing the line fluxes across different spatial scales and map the radial variations in line intensities. The spectral line profiles extracted from these cubes represent those that would result from observations using an SD telescope with the corresponding Gaussian beam size." (Spectral line surveys)
- Alternative: Varying input stellar luminosity between 5000 and 16000 L_sun to fix the ALMA-SD SiO fit mismatch in IRC +10 216 (rejected: did not affect modelled line profiles)  
  > "We also tried varying the input stellar luminosity within the range 5000-16000 $L_\odot$, but found that this did not affect the modelled line profiles." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch)
- Alternative: Changing amorphous carbon dust opacities from Suh (2000) to Preibisch et al. (1993) or turning off dust radiation field to fix the SiO fit mismatch (rejected: affected all lines similarly without differential effect between low-J and high-J)  
  > "We tested changing the input dust opacities for amorphous carbon grains from those adopted from (cite: Suh_2000) to those of (cite: Preibisch_et_al_1993), and found that this did not change the modelled line intensities." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch)
- Alternative: Including higher vibrational levels (v = 0-6 for SiO, v = 0-5 for SiS) in the RT molecular models (rejected: results matched v = 0-1 / v = 0-2 models within uncertainties)  
  > "we tested an SiO molecular description which included levels ranging from $v = 0 - 6$. The resulting modelled line profiles match those modelled using only the $v$ = 0,1 levels within the observational uncertainties." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch); "we tested an extended SiS dataset including the levels $v = 0 - 5$ along with the vibration-rotation transitions with $\Delta v = 1, 2$ for the $v=0, 1$, and 2 levels, and found that the resulting spectra matched with those modelled using only the $v = 0,~1, 2$ levels." (SiS)
- Alternative: Step-function inner abundance profile + outer Gaussian profile, or varying the Gaussian exponent in the abundance profile, to reconcile ALMA and SD SiO lines in IRC +10 216 (rejected: over-predicted small-aperture ALMA lines or scaled all lines together)  
  > "We also attempted using modified abundance profiles, which had a central step function with a constant abundance $f_\mathrm{c}$ out to a radius $R_\mathrm{c}$, followed by the Gaussian profile at larger radii." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch); "We also varied the exponent in our input abundance profile description (see Eq. [\[eq:gaussian_abundance_profile\]](#eq:gaussian_abundance_profile)), originally set to 2 for Gaussian profiles." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch)
- Alternative: Including dust-gas interaction / depletion chemistry in the 1D chemical models (found no significant effect due to warm dust temperatures Tc ~ 1500-1800 K)  
  > "Including dust-gas chemistry in these models, to allow for possible changes in the radial gas-phase abundance distributions of our species due to dust-gas interactions, including depletion onto the dust, did not lead to significantly different abundance profiles." (Chemical modelling)
- How they decided it was enough: Convolved ALMA cubes to larger beams until the integrated line flux reached a plateau (indicating all emission was recovered), and manually refined the (f0, Re) parameter grids until the 1-sigma (or 2-sigma) chi-squared contours were adequately spanned and sampled.  
  > "beginning at the major-axis size of the original synthesised beam and gradually increasing until the resulting line flux reached a plateau, implying that all detected emission has been recovered." (Spectral line surveys); "The grid coverage was manually refined where needed to ensure that the total parameter space explored was adequately spanned and sampled." (Modelling procedure)
- Limitation (authors): The 1D RT code is static, assumes a smooth spherically symmetric envelope and a blackbody central star, and cannot model 3D substructures (arcs, spirals), stellar phase time-delay effects, or stellar atmospheric absorption/emission bands.  
  > "the time delay in receiving the stellar light can be different for different $J$ lines, especially if their excitation regions peak at different radii (see e.g. Fig. 16), an effect that cannot be incorporated into our RT models, which are static." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch); "Using a detailed stellar atmosphere model instead of a blackbody is currently not possible using our RT code." (Addressing the ALMA - SD fit mismatch); "Addressing these demands RT modelling, possibly in 3D, that properly takes into account not only the observed complex circumstellar density structures such as arcs and possible spirals and related variations in the gas and dust temperature profiles" (Modified SiO abundance profiles)
- Limitation (authors): The RT code can only include one collision partner (H2, neglecting He) and cannot account for infrared line overlaps with other molecular species (such as C2H2 and HCN overlapping with SiS v=1-0 ro-vibrational transitions).  
  > "Our RT code can only take into account one collision partner and hence we have not taken into consideration collisions with other partners, such as He." (Molecular data); "Such potential issues of overlaps with the transitions of other molecules in the infrared (IR) ro-vibrational levels (see Sect. 5.2) cannot be directly taken into account in our RT models, which can only include transition information from the species being modelled." (A note on SiS J = 6 - 5)
- Limitation (authors): AFGL 3068 lacks spatially resolved observations, leaving its SiO and SiS abundance profiles and uncertainties unconstrained and biasing the high-density end of the Re vs. gas density trend.  
  > "For AFGL 3068, we only have 4 SD SiO lines available (Fig. [\[fig:AFGL-3068-SiO\]](#fig:AFGL-3068-SiO)), and no spatially resolved observations, leading to neither $f_0$ nor $R_\mathrm{e}$ being properly constrained" (SiO); "We note, however, that these observed trends in this work are influenced by the values for AFGL 3068, which are highly uncertain." (Trends with mass-loss density)
- Limitation (authors): Reported abundance uncertainties do not include uncertainties in the fixed input mass-loss rates and stellar distances, and the heterogeneous multi-telescope dataset introduces calibration and multi-epoch uncertainties.  
  > "We note that the reported uncertainties do not take into account possible uncertainties in the input MLR and distance, which are taken as fixed parameters in the modelling." (SiS); "Though the heterogeneous origin of the datasets used to constrain the models introduces some uncertainty" (Summary and conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For IRC +10 216, the SiO abundance profile is ultimately derived by discarding all single-dish lines and fitting only the ALMA J=2-1 multi-aperture data, whereas the other three resolved stars are fit using both ALMA and SD lines; this methodological difference could affect the direct comparison of IRC +10 216's SiO abundance to the rest of the sample.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1D ALI radiative transfer code is not publicly linked, making exact numerical reproduction of the non-LTE excitation grids dependent on access to the private code or re-implementation in another 1D non-LTE code (e.g. LIME or RADEX/RATRAN).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 3 (for 30SiO) prints the rest frequency of the J=2-1 line as 169.486877 GHz (identical to the J=4-3 line below it), which is a typographical error in the table for the ~84.74 GHz J=2-1 transition.
