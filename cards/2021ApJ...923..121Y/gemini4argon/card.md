  > "The initial calibration was performed using the ALMA pipeline on $\tt CASA$." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN); "The positional offset between the phase (map) center and the emission peak of the T Tau N disk was adjusted using the $\tt CASA$ task $\tt fixvis$." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN); "Next, to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the image by correcting a systematic gain error (e.g., antenna-based and baseline-based errors), we performed two rounds of phase (longer at the 1st (98 s) and down to the integration time at the 2nd (49 s) with $\tt calmode = p$) and one round of amplitude and phase (integration time of 98 s with $\tt calmode = ap$) self-calibrations." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN)
   - software: CASA, tclean, fixvis
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "The data were firstly imaged with the $\tt tclean$ task (hereafter CLEAN) by adopting Briggs weighting ($\tt robust = 0.5$)." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN)
   - parameter: self-cal solution intervals: 98 s (1st phase), 49 s (2nd phase), 98 s (amp+phase)  
  > "Next, to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the image by correcting a systematic gain error (e.g., antenna-based and baseline-based errors), we performed two rounds of phase (longer at the 1st (98 s) and down to the integration time at the 2nd (49 s) with $\tt calmode = p$) and one round of amplitude and phase (integration time of 98 s with $\tt calmode = ap$) self-calibrations." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SNR improved by a factor of 3.8; peak intensity and RMS noise compared and found in good agreement with Long et al. (2019).  
  > "We obtained the final CLEAN image (= CLEAN model convolved with CLEAN beam $+$ residual map) after self-calibration with the signal-to-noise ratio improved by a factor of 3.8, compared with the initial one." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN); "These values are in relatively good agreement with those reported previously in (cite: Long2019) (i.e., peak intensity = $64.56~\rm mJy~beam^{-1}$, RMS noise = $52~ \rm \mu Jy~beam^{-1}$)." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN)
2. **Super-resolution image reconstruction with Sparse Modeling (SpM) and evaluation of effective resolution and noise**: Reconstruct image from self-calibrated visibilities using PRIISM ver.0.3.0 by minimizing cost function with chi-squared, l1-norm, and TSV regularizers; select optimal (Lambda_l, Lambda_tsv) via 10-fold cross-validation over 5x5 grid; evaluate effective resolution and detection threshold by injecting artificial point sources at 0''.4 north and measuring off-source noise statistics and DIFMAP residual map.  
  > "The self-calibrated visibility data were adopted for the image reconstruction with the latest SpM imaging task, $\tt PRIISM$, ver.0.3.0 (cite: nakazato2020) working with $\tt CASA$." (Imaging with Sparse Modeling); "In this study, we used the 10-fold CV implemented in $\tt PRIISM$ and searched for the optimal parameter set with $5\times5$ sets of regularization parameters, which are $\Lambda_{l} = (10^{3},10^{4}, ...,10^{7}$) and $\Lambda_{tsv} =(10^{7}, 10^{8}, ..., 10^{11}$)." (Imaging with Sparse Modeling); "We performed two kinds of SpM imaging simulations. One is for estimating effective spatial resolution in the SpM imaging, and another is for evaluating the detection threshold. For both, we injected an artificial point source at $0''.4$ north in the observed data." (Effective Spatial Resolution and Detection Threshold of SpM image)
   - software: PRIISM, CASA, DIFMAP

## Qualitative findings

- 13CO (J=2-1) and C18O (J=2-1) line emissions associated with T Tau S and N were not identified in the ALMA data.  
  > "The $\rm ^{13}CO$ and $\rm C^{18}O$ data were analyzed, but emissions associated with T Tau S and N were not identified in the two lines." (Data Reduction and Imaging with CLEAN)
- The T Tau N disk is optically thick at 1.3 mm (high brightness temperature of 257 K at peak and low spectral index of 1.9), more massive locally at the outer ring than the MMSN, and gravitationally stable (Toomre Q > 3).  
  > "The disk then shows the high brightness temperature of $257\pm1$ K at the peak and the low spectral index of $1.9\pm0.1$, suggesting the optically thick at 1.3 mm." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: T Tau N is a bright (~180 mJy at 1.3 mm) and compact (~20 au) disk where previous ALMA CLEAN imaging had insufficient resolution (0''.12 or 17 au) to resolve substructures, but Manara et al. (2019) found ~3sigma residuals after subtracting axisymmetric models, suggesting tentative substructures.  
  > "Intriguingly, (cite: Manara2019) pointed out that significance residuals ($\sim3\sigma$) were found both at the T Tau N and T Tau S after subtracting axisymmetric models of the two sources from the ALMA continuum image." (Introduction); "Such bright and compact disks around T Tau system would be most suitable for exploring several au-scale structures using SpM imaging." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Sparse modeling (SpM) directly solves for incomplete uv sampling, avoids the resolution loss caused by convolving with an idealized CLEAN beam, achieves ~30%-40% of the CLEAN beam size, and produces a smooth brightness distribution rather than a patchy sum of point sources like the CLEAN model.  
  > "SpM imaging can directly solve for the incomplete sampling of visibilities in the spatial frequency and potentially improve the fidelity and effective spatial resolution of ALMA images." (Abstract); "The SpM image shows more smooth structures than the CLEAN model and therefore seems more reasonable." (Comparison between CLEAN and SpM Images)
- Alternative: Beam-convolved CLEAN image and CLEAN point-source model (rejected for main disk analysis because CLEAN beam convolution causes visibility amplitude deviation at 0.2-1.1 Mlambda and 1.5-2.2 Mlambda, and the CLEAN model produces an unphysical patchy pattern of point sources).  
  > "The visibility profile obtained from the CLEAN image significantly deviates from the observed visibility at $\rm 0.2-1.1~M\lambda$ and at $\rm 1.5-2.2~M\lambda$." (Comparison between CLEAN and SpM Images); "As a result, we see a patchy pattern in the CLEAN model image, which we consider irrelevant for disk structures." (Comparison between CLEAN and SpM Images)
- Alternative: Hyper-resolution SpM image with (Lambda_l = 10^5, Lambda_tsv = 10^7) achieving ~19% of CLEAN beam (rejected because it is smaller than 20% of the CLEAN beam and its feasibility is difficult to evaluate).  
  > "The SpM image with such a “hyper” spatial resolution (i.e., beam smaller than $20\%$ of the CLEAN beam) may reflect the presence of a small substructure in the inner disk, but it appears difficult to evaluate the feasibility." (Selection of Optimum Image in SpM Imaging of T Tau N Disk)
- Alternative: Snow line (ice sublimation front) and secular gravitational instability as origins of the T Tau N gap (could not be confirmed or ruled out because the disk is optically thick at 1.3 mm, making midplane temperature modeling challenging, and physical parameters for secular GI remain uncertain).  
  > "That being said, the required physical parameters remain highly uncertain at this stage, and it cannot conclude the possibility of secular gravitational instability as the origin of the rings yet." (Physical Properties of T Tau N Disk); "However, the brightness temperature of the T Tau N disk is much higher than that of the regular disk, and this disk appears optically thick at 1.3 mm. It can be thus challenging to find a reasonable disk midplane temperature model that matches observations." (Origins of Gap in the T Tau N Disk)
- How they decided it was enough: Used 10-fold cross-validation across a 5x5 grid of regularization parameters to select the image with minimal cross-validation error, verified that the gap and binary components appear robustly across low-CVE parameter sets, and checked that emission exceeds the off-source detection threshold I_DT.  
  > "An image with the minimum CVE can be regarded as the optimal image (cite: akiyama2017a; akiyama2017b; Kuramochi2018; yamaguchi2020)." (Imaging with Sparse Modeling); "Based on the above evaluations, emission features above $I_{\rm DT}$ are considered significant in the SpM image." (SpM image and evaluation of its noise levels); "It should be noted that the annular gap structure of the T Tau N disk and the two separated emissions around T Tau Sa/Sb are commonly seen in images with a CVE of approximately $100.0\%$, for example, $\left(\Lambda_{l}=10^{4},~\Lambda_{tsv}=10^{9}\right)$ and $\left(\Lambda_{l}=10^{5},~\Lambda_{tsv}=10^{8}\right)$." (Selection of Optimum Image in SpM Imaging of T Tau N Disk)
- Limitation (authors): SpM image suffers from artificial positive emissions in off-source areas due to non-negative constraints and thermal/systematic noise.  
  > "As described in (cite: yamaguchi2020), owing to both thermal and systematic noise, an SpM image suffers from (unexpected) positive emissions in its off-source area (i.e., outside the target source area)." (SpM image and evaluation of its noise levels)
- Limitation (authors): T Tau Sa and Sb disk sizes (6x4 au and 7x3 au) are only slightly larger than the SpM effective resolution (5x4 au), so they are not sufficiently resolved and represent conservative upper limits.  
  > "These disk sizes are slightly larger than the effective spatial resolution of the SpM image ($\theta_{\rm eff} = 5\times 4$ au) and not resolved sufficiently. Hence, these sizes should be considered to be the conservative upper limits." (Dust Emissions from T Tau Sa and Sb)
- Limitation (authors): The T Tau N disk is optically thick at 1.3 mm, so the brightness temperature traces the disk atmosphere rather than the midplane, making derived dust surface densities and dust masses lower limits.  
  > "It should be noted that a disk midplane temperature will generally be lower than $T_{\mathrm{br}}(r)$ at optically thick regions. Thus, dust surface densities (and dust masses) estimated in what follows would be lower limits in such a case." (Physical Properties of T Tau N Disk)
- Limitation (authors): There is no quantitative metric to distinguish the SpM image from the CLEAN model in reduced chi-squared, and potential bias in the SpM image remains an open question, requiring future ALMA observations at <=30 mas resolution to confirm the gap.  
  > "Ultimately, ALMA observations with a higher spatial resolution comparable to the effective resolution, at least 30 mas, can assess the consistency of the super-resolution imaging and confirm the existence of the substructure." (Conclusions); "We do not yet have more quantitative measurements that can distinguish the SpM image from the CLEAN model image , and the bias that the SpM image may have is still an open question." (Comparison between CLEAN and SpM Images)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The gap at r = 12 au is shallow (delta_I = 1.22 +/- 0.06) and relies on super-resolution deconvolution at ~30% of the synthesized beam rather than direct baseline coverage at the gap scale.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying Kanagawa et al. (2015, 2016) gas-gap relations directly to an optically thick 1.3 mm continuum surface density profile requires strong assumptions about optical depth, constant dust opacity, and perfect gas-dust coupling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The beam-smearing correction assuming Delta * delta = const (from Nomura et al. 2016) substantially alters the inferred gap depth (from 2.1 to 5.4 in Sigma_d, and 1.22 to 1.78 in I_nu) and directly drives the derived planet mass.
