## Analysis recipe

1. **Obtain continuum images**: Use published Band 6 images for AS 209, HD 163296, GM Aur, LkCa 15; for V1094 Sco and AA Tau, pipeline-calibrate archival data, self-calibrate in CASA 6.4.1 (short baselines first, then combined) and image with Briggs weighting 1 and 2, without primary-beam or JvM correction  
  > "After the initial data calibration following the provided pipeline, self-calibration was performed with CASA 6.4.1 starting from the short-baseline data and then for the combined dataset." (Data); "The imaging used in this study does not apply primary beam or JvM corrections." (Data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs weighting 1 (V1094 Sco) and 2 (AA Tau)  
  > "The final images for V1094 Sco and AA Tau were produced with Briggs weighting parameters of 1 and 2" (Data)
   - assumption: Missing primary-beam correction is negligible because disk emission is much smaller than the primary beam  
  > "This choice is justified because the disk continuum emission is much smaller than the primary beam, so the lack of correction has a negligible impact within the ring regions where our analysis is concentrated." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: JvM-corrected versus uncorrected LkCa 15 image gave nearly identical fitted parameters  
  > "All fitted parameters are nearly identical before and after JvM correction." (The influence of JvM correction)
2. **Measure image rms noise**: Compute rms in concentric annuli, adopt the median beyond the emission where the radial rms profile is flat  
  > "We measure the rms noise level in the emission-free regions of each image." (Data); "We adopt the rms noise level as the median value measured in annuli beyond the emission region, where the radial profile of the rms deviation becomes flat with radius." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Set disk geometry, extract radial profiles and define ring masks**: Find the image center by rotating the image by 180 degrees and minimising the squared residuals of the difference; adopt PA and inclination from literature; extract radial intensity profile with gofish; fit only where the azimuthally averaged intensity exceeds half the ring peak  
  > "To determine the center of each disk image, we apply a 180-degree rotation to the image and subtract the rotated version from the original one." (Data); "Using these geometric parameters, we extract the radial intensity profile for all targets with the" (Data); "Our fitting procedure focuses on regions where the azimuthally averaged intensity exceeds half of the ring’s peak value" (Data)
   - software: gofish
   - parameter: Ring selection: radially resolved, fitted Gaussian width greater than the beam size, and sufficiently sharp; exactly two rings per disk  
  > "We focus on rings that are radially resolved, have a fitted Gaussian width greater than the beam size, and are sufficiently sharp for our analysis." (Data); "This criterion selects exactly two rings per disk for analysis" (Data)
   - assumption: PA and inclination taken from previous studies  
  > "we adopt values from previous studies, as summarized in Table" (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit the ring model to the image with MCMC**: Fit each ring with an empirical model: Gaussian vertical optical depth profile, two half-Gaussian radial widths, single ring temperature, I_los = (1-exp(-tau_los)) B_nu(T_ring), convolved with the beam; five free parameters per ring (T, r0, w_i, w_o, H_d)  
  > "we adopt an empirical approach by representing the optical depth distribution with Gaussian profiles in the vertical direction" (Model); "These formulations allow us to model a single ring with 5 free parameters" (Model); "is differentials between the observation and the beam-convolved model" (Data)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: tau_0 fixed at 0.5  
  > "We pragmatically assume that the optical depth at the top of the ring always satisfies $\tau_0 = 0.5$." (Model)
   - parameter: 32 walkers, run until the autocorrelation time converges  
  > "initializing 32 walkers and running the chains until the autocorrelation time converges" (Data)
   - parameter: Burn-in of twice the maximum autocorrelation time  
  > "we discard a number of steps equal to twice the maximum autocorrelation time as burn-in" (Data)
   - assumption: Single temperature per ring  
  > "Given the relatively narrow width of the rings, we assume that each ring has a single temperature, $T_{\rm ring}$." (Model)
   - assumption: Scattering neglected  
  > "we also neglect the contribution of scattering, which becomes more significant in the optically thick regime" (Model)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC; Gaussian log-likelihood summed over masked pixels with sigma_sample = sqrt(A_beam/A_pixel) x sigma (rms); uniform priors on all parameters, all strictly positive  
  > "Uniform priors are adopted for all parameters, with the additional constraint that all parameters are strictly positive." (Data); "with $\sigma_{\rm sample} = \sqrt{A_{\rm beam}/A_{\rm pixel}} \times \sigma$" (Data)
   - validation: Test case on GM Aur with tau and T free gives the same other parameters; maximum likelihood estimator compared with posterior; residual maps examined  
  > "In a test case, we allow both $\tau_0$ and $T$ to be free parameters, and the conclusions of this work remain unchanged" (Model); "In addition to the posterior estimates, we compute the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE), which corresponds to the parameter set that maximizes the likelihood function." (Data)
5. **Summarise posteriors and set upper limits on scale height**: Report the 50th percentile with 16th and 84th percentiles as 1 sigma; where the MLE of H_d is below the 16th percentile and near zero, quote the 84th percentile as an upper bound  
  > "The estimated parameter values are taken as the 50th percentile of the marginalized posterior distributions, with the 16th and 84th percentiles defining the 1 $\sigma$ uncertainty range" (Data); "In such cases, we take the 84th percentile of the posterior distribution as the upper bound." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residuals in AS 209 and V1094 Sco below 3 sigma; azimuthal intensity profiles shown for reference; residual asymmetries discussed  
  > "For AS 209 and V1094 Sco, the residuals are overall less than 3 $\sigma$" (Results)
6. **Derive gas scale height and dust settling coefficient alpha_z/St**: H_g = c_s/Omega_K with isothermal hydrostatic gas; three temperatures (fitted ring T, flaring-disk T with phi = 0.02, CO-isotopologue mid-plane T); alpha_z/St = (H_d/H_g)^2 for partially settled dust  
  > "For gas scale height, assuming that the gas is isothermal and in hydrostatic equilibrium in the vertical direction" (Results); "we offer three different temperatures for reference" (Results); "If the dust is partially settled, i.e., the Stokes number St is larger than the diffusivity coefficient $\alpha_z$, the settling coefficient can be expressed as" (Results)
   - parameter: mean molecular weight mu = 2.3  
  > "where $\mu = 2.3$ is the mean molecular weight" (Results)
   - parameter: flaring angle phi = 0.02 for the flaring-disk temperature  
  > "$\phi=0.02$ is the so-called flaring angle" (Results)
   - assumption: Dust vertical diffusion and settling in equilibrium  
  > "assuming that an equilibrium between dust vertical diffusion and settling has been reached" (Results)
   - assumption: Gas temperature uncertain; H_g in the main table uses the fitted ring temperature equal to the gas temperature  
  > "We firstly estimate the gas scale height $H_g$ using the temperature from our fitting by assuming same temperature for dust and gas." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: The three gas-temperature choices give H_g within 30% of each other for all rings  
  > "the differences among $H_g$, $H_{g, \rm flaring}$, and $H_{g, \rm CO}$ are within $30\%$ for all of the rings" (Results)
7. **Test whether grain-size (Stokes number) differences can explain the inner/outer contrast**: Assume fragmentation-limited St with constant v_frag and delta_frag = alpha_z, giving alpha_z,1/alpha_z,2 = sqrt(C T2/T1) with C = (alpha_z,1/alpha_z,2)(St2/St1); compare lower limit on C with the observed temperature ratio  
  > "we consider a fragmentation limited regime of dust growth" (Discussions); "Our measurement constrain the lower limit of $C\gtrsim10-100$" (Discussions)
   - assumption: Constant fragmentation velocity between rings; vertical diffusion dominates the collision velocity so delta_frag = alpha_z  
  > "Assuming constant $v_{\rm frag}$ between rings, and vertical diffusion dominate the mutual collision velocity among pebbles, $\delta_{\rm frag} = \alpha_z$" (Discussions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- In 4 of 6 disks (V1094 Sco, LkCa 15, GM Aur, HD 163296) the inner ring is geometrically thicker than the outer ring, unlike a simple flaring-disk expectation  
  > "In summary, 4 out of 6 disks in our sample show a geometrically thicker inner ring than the outer ring" (Results)
- AS 209 and AA Tau, with the lowest stellar masses in the sample, give only upper limits for both rings  
  > "For AS 209 and AA Tau, both the inner and outer rings only have upper limit constraints." (Results)
- The AA Tau residual asymmetries are off the major axis and likely intrinsic (e.g. shadowing by a misaligned inner disk), beyond the scope of this work  
  > "Finally, the residuals of AA Tau show a pair of nearly symmetrical blobs on either side of the disk, but not on the major axis" (Results)
- Scattered-light imaging shows rings at the inner continuum ring radii in LkCa 15 and HD 163296 and no outer-ring polarized light in the four puffed-up disks, consistent with shadowing by puffed-up inner rings  
  > "For all four disks, no polarized light from the outer ring is detected." (Comparison between ALMA continuum and scattered light images)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: These six disks are the only known sample with multiple well-resolved rings, suitable inclination and angular resolution  
  > "The 6 disks are the only sample (to our knowledge) with multiple rings, and the required inclination and angular resolution to conduct our analysis." (Data)
- Why this method: Ring-based azimuthal modelling probes dust scale height variation across a large radial range  
  > "This ring-based approach provides a powerful means of probing dust scale height variations across a large radial distance." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Optically thick ring hypothesis rejected as incompatible with line-of-sight optical depth variation  
  > "However, the optically thick ring hypothesis is incompatible with line-of-sight optical depth variation because one never sees through the optically thick ring." (Model)
- Alternative: JvM correction not used; tested for LkCa 15 and gave nearly identical parameters  
  > "Therefore, we opted out of using it." (Data); "All fitted parameters are nearly identical before and after JvM correction." (The influence of JvM correction)
- Alternative: tau_0 and T both free tested on GM Aur; degenerate, conclusions unchanged  
  > "In a test case, we allow both $\tau_0$ and $T$ to be free parameters, and the conclusions of this work remain unchanged" (Model)
- How they decided it was enough: MCMC chains run until autocorrelation time converges  
  > "initializing 32 walkers and running the chains until the autocorrelation time converges" (Data)
- Limitation (authors): Gas temperature is very uncertain, hence three temperature choices for H_g  
  > "A caveat is the gas temperature estimation is very uncertain, therefore, we offer three different temperatures for reference." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Gaussian radial and vertical dust distributions may not hold  
  > "it should also be noted the assumption that dust follows Gaussian distributions, in both radial and vertical direction, may not always hold" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): tau and T are degenerate in the model, so tau_0 is fixed  
  > "given our model assumptions, it is impossible to distinguish between the line of sight optical depth and the temperature of the ring" (The degeneracy of ring optical depth and temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Method needs very high-quality, well-resolved data and lacks realistic radiative transfer  
  > "Our method requires very high-quality data to fully resolve the ring structures in order to achieve the accuracy needed for our MCMC-based framework, and lack of realistic radiative transfer modeling." (Comparison with other works)
- Limitation (authors): Oscillatory azimuthal patterns in settled rings arise from beam smearing  
  > "We note that an oscillatory pattern appears in some of the blue rings, but this is primarily due to non-negligible beam smearing" (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing tau_0 = 0.5 with a single ring temperature makes the retrieved H_d depend on the model assumptions, and the paper tests this only on GM Aur
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits for most outer rings come from beam-smearing-limited resolution, so the 'razor-thin' claim is an upper bound rather than a measurement
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Text gives LkCa 15 outer-ring H_d < 0.4 au while the main tables give < 0.6 au (< 0.4 appears in the JvM-corrected fit); an internal inconsistency
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar masses, luminosities, distances and geometries come from unnamed numbered references, which affects H_g and alpha_z/St
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only six disks and two rings each, so the 4-of-6 occurrence rate has large small-number uncertainty; two disks (AS 209, AA Tau) give no constraint
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rings were selected as radially resolved and 'sharp', a selection that may favour particular ring types
