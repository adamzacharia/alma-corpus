## Analysis recipe

1. **Fit disk geometry**: Fit MCFOST models simultaneously to Stokes Qphi, Q and U.  
  > "Models were fit to Stokes $\mathcal{Q}_\phi$, $\mathcal{Q}$ and $\mathcal{U}$ data simultaneously, as $\mathcal{Q}_\phi$ is the only available data with the quadrupole noise signal removed." (Disk Morphology)
   - software: MCFOST
   - parameter: Free: minimum grain size, porosity, dust mass, PA, inclination, inner radius, outer radius and critical radius.  
  > "the minimum grain size ($a_\text{min}$), grain porosity, dust mass (within the grain size distribution used) $M_\text{d}$, disk position angle (PA), inclination (*i*), dust inner radius $R_\text{in}$, dust outer radius $R_\text{out}$, and the critical radius $R_\text{c}$" (Disk Morphology)
   - assumption: Spherical grains; Mie scattering; azimuthally symmetric, optically thin disk; 1.65 microns.  
  > "Following the procedure in (cite: esposito2018), our models assume Mie scattering from spherical grains in an azimuthally-symmetric, optically-thin disk centered on the star, observed at 1.65 microns (the *H* band peak wavelength)." (Disk Morphology)
   - assumption: Scale height/radius = 0.055.  
  > "The disk has a vertical structure where the scale height is a constant fraction of the radius, set to 0.055," (Disk Morphology)
   - fitting: Parallel-tempered MCMC: three temperatures, 120 walkers each, 3500 iterations; discard 2900, retain final 600 zeroth-temperature iterations.  
  > "We used a parallel-tempered MCMC with three temperatures, each with 120 walkers." (Disk Morphology); "Walkers were initialized randomly from a uniform distribution and then simulated for 3500 iterations." (Disk Morphology); "We discarded the first 2900 iterations as “burn-in” where the ensemble of walkers had not yet converged to their peak in posterior space, and used the final 600 iterations of the zeroth temperature walkers" (Disk Morphology)
   - validation: Inspect posterior corner plot; use maximum likelihood or 99.7% bounds for strongly non-Gaussian posteriors.  
  > "A corner plot of posteriors from the MCMC is shown in Figure [2](#mcfost-corner)." (Disk Morphology); "parameters with strongly non-Gaussian posteriors are reported as either maximum likelihood values or 99.7% confidence upper/lower limits." (Disk Morphology)
2. **Measure radial profiles**: Deproject with diskmap; average radial wedges; estimate visual outer radius from profiles consistent with zero by 2σ; least-squares fit a smoothly broken power law.  
  > "we first deproject the disk based on our determined value of inclination, such that each radial cut traverses the same distance from the center of the disk, using the Python package `diskmap` (cite: 2016A&A...596A..70S)." (Radial Brightness Profiles); "we rebin the brightness profiles to 5 pixel wide annuli, and determine where the signal becomes consistent with zero by 2$\sigma$ in our images at each angle." (Radial Brightness Profiles); "we fit a smoothly broken power law (as defined via MCFOST and below in Equation [\[label:surfdens\]](#label:surfdens)) to the average measured surface density profile using least-squares," (Radial Brightness Profiles)
   - software: diskmap
   - parameter: Wedges: 5 pixels radially, 45° wide; mask within 15 AU.  
  > "Radial brightness profiles are the average of a wedge at the given angle range that is **5** pixels long in the radial direction and 45$^\circ$" (Radial Brightness Profiles); "Pixels within 15 AU of the center are masked, as this region is within the influence of the FPM (FPM) and therefore contains large errors." (Radial Brightness Profiles)
   - assumption: Constant z; β = 0, H0/rH = 0.055, γ = 1.  
  > "We set the $z$ coordinate to a constant for simplicity," (Radial Brightness Profiles); "where $\beta$=0 and $H_0/r_H$=0.055, set the exponent $\gamma$=1," (Radial Brightness Profiles)
   - fitting: Free rc, αin, αout and normalization N; least-squares.  
  > "leave $r_c$, $\alpha_\text{in}$, and $\alpha_\text{out}$ as free parameters, along with an arbitrary scaling/normalization factor $N$." (Radial Brightness Profiles); "to the average measured surface density profile using least-squares," (Radial Brightness Profiles)
   - validation: Independent morphology check against scattered-light modeling; profile errors from wedge variance.  
  > "with its error as the variance of that slice of the wedge." (Radial Brightness Profiles); "As an independent check on the disk morphology," (Radial Brightness Profiles)
3. **Measure scattering phase functions**: Measure brightness at multiple radii, correct for surface density and r², average radii, and split East/West halves.  
  > "SPFs were instead determined by taking measurements of $B_\text{obs}(r,\theta)$ at multiple radii around the disk, then averaging over the varying radii." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions); "f(\theta) \sim \frac{B_\text{obs}(r,\theta)}{\rho(r)} \cdot r^2" (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions); "SPFs were measured separately for the East and West halves of the disk (divided approximately along the minor axis)," (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions)
   - parameter: 15–78 AU; surface-density proxy from average best-fit radial brightness profile, peak normalized to 1.  
  > "this range is 15 AU (at the FPM limit) to 78 AU." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions); "The disk surface density, $\rho(r)$, was approximated using the average best fit radial brightness profile where the peak surface brightness was normalized to 1." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions)
   - assumption: Azimuthal symmetry, optical thinness and MCFOST morphology; scattering independent of azimuthal angle.  
  > "we take the disk to be azimuthally symmetric (but radially varying, related to the observed surface brightness) and optically thin, with the previously-described morphological parameters inferred via `MCFOST`." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions); "We also assume that scattering is independent of azimuthal angle" (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions)
   - fitting: Fit Henyey-Greenstein functions after dividing polarized SPFs by a Rayleigh polarization curve.  
  > "We additionally fit a Henyey-Greenstein SPF (cite: henyey1941diffuse) to each of our observed scattering SPFs divided by a Rayleigh polarization curve (approximating the total intensity SPF)," (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions)
   - validation: Propagate annular pixel variance; assess East/West HG fits.  
  > "Errors were derived by the variance of pixels in each annulus, similar to the procedure for the radial brightness profiles, and propagated appropriately through the calculation." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions); "For the Eastern half of the disk, the HG function is a poor fit, even with the best fit value $g$ = 0.33;" (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions); "however, the HG function with $g$ = 0.45 is a reasonable approximation for the Western half." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions)
4. **Assemble SED constraints**: Collect photometry, convert magnitudes to Jy, and synthesize W3/W4 and S9W photometry through filter transmission profiles.  
  > "Most measurements were obtained in magnitudes from the IRSA or Vizier databases, and then converted to Janskys using the zero points provided by each mission/project’s documentation." (Thermal Emission); "Given the wide bandpasses of the *WISE* W3/W4 (cite: wright2010wide) and *AKARI* S9W (cite: fujiwaraakari) filters, we compute synthetic photometry" (Thermal Emission)
   - software: IRSA, VizieR, Spanish Virtual Observatory
   - parameter: ALMA 1240 μm flux: 0.00072 ± 0.00011 Jy; tabulated photometry before color corrections.  
  > "|  | ALMA | 1240 | 0.00072$\pm$0.00011 | (cite: lieman2016debris) |" (Thermal Emission); "all data listed here are before color corrections," (Thermal Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Reject IRAS photometry for poor quality flags; include WISE and AKARI absolute calibration uncertainties.  
  > "Although photometric data from *IRAS* were available for this object, they were not used due to poor data quality flags present in the catalog." (Thermal Emission); "Additional absolute calibration uncertainties were incorporated for the WISE data points (cite: cutri2012vizier) and AKARI data points (cite: tanabe2008absolute)." (Thermal Emission)
5. **Fit blackbody temperatures**: Two single-temperature blackbody curves for stellar and disk emission.  
  > "We initially fit a simple model consisting of two single-temperature blackbody curves for the stellar emission plus the disk’s thermal emission" (Thermal Emission)
   - software: SciPy
   - parameter: Free disk temperature Td and emitting-area parameter A.  
  > "The free parameters included are disk temperature ($T_d$) and a parameter proportional to the disk emitting area ($A$)." (Thermal Emission)
   - assumption: Fixed stellar radius and temperature from Table 1; equilibrium blackbody grain for radius conversion.  
  > "The stellar radius ($R_*$) and temperature ($T_*$) are set using the values in Table [1](#tab:star) from (cite: mellon2019discovery)." (Thermal Emission); "if treated as an equilibrium temperature for a grain radiating as a blackbody." (Thermal Emission)
   - fitting: SciPy Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares.  
  > "using a Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares fit implemented in `scipy`." (Thermal Emission)
   - validation: Reduced χ² = 1.088; poor long-wavelength fit motivates modified emissivity.  
  > "with a reduced chi squared $\chi_\nu^2$ value of 1.088," (Thermal Emission); "Given the poor fit at long wavelengths while using a single-temperature blackbody to represent thermal emission from dust," (Thermal Emission)
6. **Fit modified blackbody**: Single-temperature blackbody with wavelength-dependent emissivity.  
  > "using a single-temperature blackbody plus an additional modified wavelength-dependent emissivity, following the formalism in (cite: backman1992infrared)." (Thermal Emission)
   - software: SciPy
   - parameter: Fit temperature and λ0.  
  > "This model was again fit using the `scipy` implementation of a Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares fit, now for both temperature and $\lambda_0$." (Thermal Emission)
   - assumption: No spatial distribution or grain size distribution assumed.  
  > "Again, this model does not assume a spatial distribution or grain size distribution." (Thermal Emission)
   - fitting: SciPy Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares.  
  > "This model was again fit using the `scipy` implementation of a Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares fit, now for both temperature and $\lambda_0$." (Thermal Emission)
   - validation: Reduced χ² = 1.21; neither simple model captures AKARI 9 μm.  
  > "with a $\chi_\nu^2$ value of 1.21," (Thermal Emission); "Neither model is able to capture the 9$\mu$m *AKARI* point, and a modified emissivity is necessary to reproduce the flux observed by ALMA." (Thermal Emission)
7. **Constrain inner radius with radiative transfer**: Fit spatially resolved, azimuthally symmetric MCFOST SED models.  
  > "To constrain the inner radius, we then compute the SED for a spatially resolved, azimuthally symmetric disk using `MCFOST`." (Thermal Emission)
   - software: MCFOST, emcee
   - parameter: Free rin, amin and Md; uniform priors, 0 < rin < 15 and -2 < log amin < 1.  
  > "leaving only $r_\text{in}$, $a_\text{min}$, and $M_\text{d}$ as free parameters." (Thermal Emission); "with bounds on the priors for $r_{\rm in}$ (0$< r_{\rm in} <$15) and log$a_{\rm min}$ (-2$< {\rm log}a_{\rm min} <$1)." (Thermal Emission)
   - assumption: Kurucz 9000 K atmosphere; same grain and disk assumptions as scattered-light model.  
  > "We set up this resolved model using a Kurucz 9000 K model atmosphere (cite: castelli2004new) and the same parameters and assumptions (e.g. Mie scattering, dust grain size and composition) as the scattered-light model" (Thermal Emission)
   - fitting: emcee affine invariant ensemble: 16 walkers, uniform initialization and priors, 1500 iterations, discard 100 and retain 1400.  
  > "We explore the posterior for those parameters again with a (cite: goodman2010ensemble) affine invariant MCMC ensemble sampler using the `emcee` package with 16 walkers and uniform priors," (Thermal Emission); "Walkers were initialized randomly from a uniform distribution and then simulated for 1500 iterations." (Thermal Emission); "We discarded the first 100 iterations as “burn-in” where the ensemble of walkers had not yet converged to their peak in posterior space, and used the remaining 1400 iterations" (Thermal Emission)
   - validation: Compare model with photometry and posterior corner plot; reduced χ² = 1.11, with AKARI and ALMA residuals.  
  > "The maximum likelihood model SED is shown compared to available photometry in Figure [9](#fig:mcfost-sed), and a corner plot of the approximate posteriors is shown in Figure [10](#fig:mcfost-sed-corner)." (Thermal Emission); "This best-fit MCFOST SED model has $\chi_\nu^2$ = 1.11, similar to the simpler models above." ## Qualitative findings

- No recovery of the disk in total intensity after PSF subtraction.  
  > "Even after applying PSF-subtraction, however, we were unable to recover the disk in total intensity light," (Data Reduction and Polarized Differential Imaging)
- No tested model satisfactorily explains all photometric points.  
  > "no single model we have tested is able to satisfactorily explain the entire set of photometric data points available." (Discussion)
- Close-in warm dust with sub-blowout grains is favored under the adopted assumptions.  
  > "the thermal signature of the disk is best explained by a population of close-in warm dust with sub-blowout size grains, and a modified emissivity trend at longer wavelengths." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use higher-quality 2019 GPI data and visible-to-millimeter photometry to constrain the unseen inner edge.  
  > "The 2019 data was higher quality and is what will be used in our analysis." (Gemini Planet Imager Observations); "The main goal of our investigation of HD 156623’s SED is to constrain the location of the disk’s unseen inner edge." (Thermal Emission); "Photometric measurements of HD 156623 were acquired from a variety of sources, ranging from visible to mid-infrared to millimeter ALMA observations." (Thermal Emission)
- Why this method: Spatially resolved SED models explicitly constrain inner radius; single-temperature models do not.  
  > "However, these simple models do not explicitly fit for the inner radius we are interested in, as they assume a single temperature across the entire disk." (Thermal Emission); "To constrain the inner radius, we then compute the SED for a spatially resolved, azimuthally symmetric disk using `MCFOST`." (Thermal Emission)
- Alternative: IRAS photometry rejected for poor catalog quality flags.  
  > "Although photometric data from *IRAS* were available for this object, they were not used due to poor data quality flags present in the catalog." (Thermal Emission)
- Alternative: Additional total-intensity reductions such as NMF not pursued.  
  > "Although other recent algorithms have focused on improving this issue of self-subtraction (e.g. non-negative matrix factorization (NMF), (cite: ren2018non)), we do not pursue additional reductions in this work;" (Data Reduction and Polarized Differential Imaging)
- Alternative: Physical dust fits to phase functions not pursued because of limited scattering-angle coverage.  
  > "we do not pursue physical dust model fits to our phase function given the limited range of scattering angles for the available data." (Observed Polarized Scattering Phase Functions)
- Alternative: Two-component cold outer disk and warm inner belt proposed for future work.  
  > "One could additionally imagine that the debris disk has two components: a cold outer disk imaged by GPI and ALMA, and a separate warm inner belt" (Discussion); "Future work may include exploring two-component models," (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: Limit SED model complexity because four constraining photometric points already support three free parameters.  
  > "given the limited number of photometric data points currently available (four data points–AKARI 9$\mu$m, W3, W4, and ALMA–and currently three free parameters), we choose not to add further free parameters to our model" (Thermal Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Image-derived inner radius limited by focal-plane mask.  
  > "Scattered-light image modeling is limited by the focal-plane mask in its determination of $r_\text{in}$," (Thermal Emission)
- Limitation (authors): SED inner radius and minimum grain size reach prior boundaries.  
  > "SED modeling, on the other hand, returns a maximum likelihood value of 2.37 AU and is limited by the prior bound at zero," (Thermal Emission); "This $a_\text{min}$ value from SED modeling reached a bound on the prior at 0.01 $\mu$m," (Thermal Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass functions mainly as a brightness scaling factor.  
  > "Additionally, we do not expect the dust mass $M_\text{d}$ to be physically meaningful; instead, it essentially functions as a scaling factor for the global surface brightness of the disk." (Disk Morphology)
- Limitation (authors): Radial surface-density profile may be biased by low back-scattering SNR or assumed scattering phase function.  
  > "this average profile may be biased due to a lower SNR detection of the back-scattering half of the disk, where the outer edge is much less well-defined," (Radial Brightness Profiles); "or a different scattering phase function than that assumed by `MCFOST`, which may change the ratio of surface brightness to surface density." (Radial Brightness Profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Scattered-light geometry and grain properties are degenerate.  
  > "the observed scattered-light distribution is a complicated and difficult-to-disentangle combination of disk geometry, grain size distribution, grain properties (e.g. porosity, scattering efficiency), and more (cite: stark2014revealing)." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Surface-density assumptions can significantly change the inferred inner radius.  
  > "this modeling assumes a certain power law for surface density; different assumptions in that value may lead to significant changes in the value of $r_\text{in}$," (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA contribution is an imported flux measurement; this paper does not provide an independent ALMA reduction recipe.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inner/outer power-law labels are reversed between the morphology prose and table.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The morphology chain’s stated model count is inconsistent with retaining only 600 iterations from 120 zeroth-temperature walkers.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Prior-bound posteriors and only four constraining SED points weaken the robustness of the grain-size and inner-radius limits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas drag is a proposed interpretation rather than a fitted gas–dust dynamical result.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A near-unity reduced chi squared does not resolve the acknowledged AKARI and ALMA residuals.
