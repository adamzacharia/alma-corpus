## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum images**: Image visibilities with natural weighting, then apply a 0.5″ uv-plane taper; use the ≈0.9″ images.  
  > "We imaged the data using natural weighting of the visibilities, yielding a synthesized beam of $\approx$0.6$''$." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "We also applied a 0.5$''$ uv-plane taper to produce images with $\approx$0.9$''$ resolution, which we use in the remainder of this work." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: Natural weighting; 0.5″ uv-plane taper  
  > "We imaged the data using natural weighting of the visibilities, yielding a synthesized beam of $\approx$0.6$''$." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "We also applied a 0.5$''$ uv-plane taper to produce images with $\approx$0.9$''$ resolution, which we use in the remainder of this work." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: More aggressive tapers up to 1.5″, giving ≈2.1″ beams, produced consistent photometry.  
  > "We tested both more aggressive uv tapers (up to 1.5$''$, yielding a $\approx$2.1$''$ synthesized beam) and larger aperture sizes, and find consistent photometric measurements." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
2. **Measure individual continuum fluxes**: 2.0″ diameter aperture photometry at emission peaks for detections and phase centers for nondetections; uncertainties from random apertures of the same size.  
  > "We measure flux densities from 2.0$''$ diameter apertures placed at the peak of the detected emission (C130284, C132776) or the phase center for undetected galaxies (C022260, C074512, C110509)," (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "and measure uncertainties using randomly-placed apertures of the same size." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: 2.0″ aperture diameter; reported limits are 2σ  
  > "We measure flux densities from 2.0$''$ diameter apertures" (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "All limits are 2$\sigma$." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Aperture centers coincide with CO peaks; larger apertures give consistent measurements.  
  > "In all cases, the aperture centers coincide with the peak of the CO(2–1) emission." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "We tested both more aggressive uv tapers (up to 1.5$''$, yielding a $\approx$2.1$''$ synthesized beam) and larger aperture sizes, and find consistent photometric measurements." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
3. **Stack continuum nondetections**: Redshift-rescale and median-stack the three individually undetected images.  
  > "For the three individually-undetected sources, we stacked the images to reach modestly deeper limits." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "We opt for a simple image-based median stack because the depth and resolution of the input images are essentially the same." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "Before stacking, we rescale each image by a factor that accounts for the different redshifts of the galaxies (cite: spilker14; scoville16)." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: Redshift-rescaling factor <15%; weighted-average physical properties  
  > "This factor is $<$15%," (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "The stack of individually undetected sources (‘stack’) uses the weighted average for the physical properties." (Table 1)
   - assumption: Input images have essentially the same depth and resolution.  
  > "We opt for a simple image-based median stack because the depth and resolution of the input images are essentially the same." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Harmonize comparison samples**: Assemble literature gas/dust measurements; adopt UV+IR SFRs; exclude post-starburst selections and rest-frame continuum wavelengths below 200 μm; retain sSFR more than 3× below the star-forming sequence.  
  > "For ease and consistency, we adopt the SFRs based on the UV$+$IR luminosity for both our own targets and literature comparison galaxies described below." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "We do not include sources selected to be post-starburst, recently-quenched, etc.," (Literature Comparison Galaxies); "we retained only galaxies with sSFR more than 3$\times$ below the star-forming sequence at the redshift of each galaxy." (Literature Comparison Galaxies)
   - parameter: 0.5<z<4 comparison sample; continuum rest wavelength ≥200 μm  
  > "We assembled a sample of $0.5<z<4$ quiescent galaxies with gas and/or dust measurements from the literature." (Literature Comparison Galaxies); "We also excluded any galaxies for which the continuum observation probed rest-frame wavelength $<$200 $\mu\rm{m}$," (Literature Comparison Galaxies)
   - assumption: Chabrier initial mass function  
  > "and a (cite: chabrier03) initial mass function." (Introduction)
   - assumption: IR contribution to UV+IR SFRs comes solely from 24 μm imaging.  
  > "In practice the “IR” portion of the UV$+$IR SFRs comes solely from 24 $\mu\rm{m}$ imaging; neither far-IR nor ALMA observations are used." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Calculate dust masses**: Calculate Mdust from continuum flux, luminosity distance, frequency-dependent dust absorption, and the Planck-function difference Bν(Tdust)−Bν(TCMB).  
  > "$\ensuremath{M_{\rm{dust}}}= \frac{S_{\nu_{\rm{obs}}} D_L^2}{\kappa_{\nu_{\rm{rest}}} (1+z)} (B_{\nu_{\rm{rest}}}(\ensuremath{\rm{T}_{\rm{dust}}}) - 
B_{\nu_{\rm{rest}}}(T_{\mathrm{CMB}}))^{-1}$" (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations); "where $B_\nu(T)$ is the Planck function evaluated at temperature $T$ and $\kappa_\nu \equiv \kappa_{\nu_0} (\nu/\nu_0)^\beta$ is the dust absorption coefficient." (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations)
   - assumption: κν0 = 2.64 m²/kg at ν0 = 2.4 THz; β = 2.0  
  > "We adopt the emissivity of (cite: dunne03), with $\kappa_{\nu_0} = 2.64$ m$^2$/kg at $\nu_0 = 2.4$ THz varying with frequency with $\beta = 2.0$." (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations)
   - assumption: Mass-weighted Tdust = 25 K  
  > "We assume a mass-weighted $\ensuremath{\rm{T}_{\rm{dust}}}= 25$ K" (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations)
   - assumption: Flat ΛCDM; Ωm = 0.3; H0 = 68 km/s/Mpc  
  > "We use a flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $\Omega_m=0.3$ and $H_0=68$ km/s Mpc$^{-1}$, as in SIMBA," (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Test alternative temperatures and emissivities.  
  > "Lowering $\rm{T}_{\rm{dust}}$ from 25 to 20 K (cite: magdis21) only increases $M_{\rm{dust}}$ by a factor of $\approx$40%," (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses); "We explored a number of different dust emissivity parameterizations from the literature to understand its impact (cite: hildebrand83; james02; dunne03; draine07; guillet18; hensley23)." (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses)
6. **Calculate molecular gas masses**: Recompute molecular masses from CO transitions or [CI] using uniform excitation and conversion assumptions.  
  > "We recompute dust and gas masses for our sample and the literature comparison galaxies and stacks." (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations); "All of our target galaxies used CO(2–1) detections to constrain the molecular gas masses, but literature studies have used other CO transitions or \[CI\] as alternate tracers." ## Qualitative findings

- The target galaxies have unusually weak dust emission for their CO luminosities.  
  > "we conclude that our sample of $z\sim1$ quiescent galaxies show dust emission significantly weaker than expected." (A Deficit of Dust Emission)
- Observed gas and dust properties are broadly compatible with SIMBA, but real quiescent galaxies show more gas-property scatter.  
  > "This exercise is primarily intended to illustrate that the landscape of current observations is broadly compatible with the expectations of modern cosmological simulations" (Gas and Dust Fractions); "evidence that there is more scatter in the cold gas properties of real quiescent galaxies than in the simulation." (Are Quiescent Galaxies Gas-Rich or Dust-Poor?)
- Dust continuum cannot reliably trace cold gas in high-redshift quiescent galaxies with current understanding.  
  > "While acknowledging our small sample size, we are faced with the sobering conclusion that observations of the dust continuum cannot reliably be used to trace the cold gas contents of high-redshift quiescent galaxies." (Discussion and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Continuum follow-up of CO-detected quiescent galaxies directly constrains gas-to-dust ratios and tests SIMBA.  
  > "In this paper, we present new ALMA 870 $\mu\rm{m}$ follow-up observations of five $z\sim1$ quiescent galaxies that had previous detections of CO(2–1) in order to constrain $\delta_{\rm{GDR}}$ and test the predictions from SIMBA." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Median stacking uses images of essentially equal depth and resolution; rest-frame wavelength cuts keep dust estimates on the Rayleigh-Jeans tail.  
  > "We opt for a simple image-based median stack because the depth and resolution of the input images are essentially the same." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations); "We also excluded any galaxies for which the continuum observation probed rest-frame wavelength $<$200 $\mu\rm{m}$, intended to ensure that the dust mass is estimated from the Rayleigh-Jeans tail of the dust SED." (Literature Comparison Galaxies)
- Alternative: r21=1.0 rather than 0.8: reported 25% reduction in CO(1–0) luminosity is insufficient.  
  > "Increasing $r_{21}$ from 0.8 to 1.0 (i.e. thermalized CO emission) would only decrease the CO(1–0) luminosity by 25%, far lower than the factors of 2.5 to $>$6 needed" (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses)
- Alternative: Tdust=20 K rather than 25 K: ≈40% larger dust masses are insufficient.  
  > "Lowering $\rm{T}_{\rm{dust}}$ from 25 to 20 K (cite: magdis21) only increases $M_{\rm{dust}}$ by a factor of $\approx$40%, which is likewise too small to bring $\delta_{\rm{GDR}}$ down to 100-200." (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses)
- Alternative: Including HI does not remove high gas-to-dust ratios in SIMBA.  
  > "We verify that, at least in SIMBA, galaxies with abnormally high molecular $\delta_{\rm{GDR}}$ also have abnormally high total (HI$+$H$_2$) $\delta_{\rm{GDR}}$," (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses)
- Alternative: Lower αCO could restore typical ratios for most targets, but is considered unlikely and cannot be ruled out.  
  > "Lowering $\alpha_{\rm{CO}}$ by a factor of 4.4 would bring most of our measurements into the typical range of $\delta_{\rm{GDR}}$." (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses); "While we cannot rule out this scenario, it seems unlikely that our low-SFR galaxies predominantly have molecular gas in the warm, excited, low-opacity phase typical of local IR-luminous mergers." (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses)
- Alternative: Alternative emissivities: only (cite: draine07) increases dust masses, by ≈50%, insufficient for the observed deficit.  
  > "Of these, only the emissivity of (cite: draine07) increased the dust masses, by $\approx$50% compared to our fiducial assumption, again smaller than the deficit of dust emission we observe." (Impact of Assumptions on Gas and Dust Masses)
- Alternative: Exclude the dust-selected z∼0.3 comparison sample.  
  > "We explicitly choose not to include the $z\sim0.3$ sample of (cite: donevski23), because those quiescent galaxies were selected to have far-IR detections, i.e. were intentionally selected to be dust-rich." (Literature Comparison Galaxies)
- How they decided it was enough: Photometry remains consistent with larger apertures and more aggressive uv tapers.  
  > "We tested both more aggressive uv tapers (up to 1.5$''$, yielding a $\approx$2.1$''$ synthesized beam) and larger aperture sizes, and find consistent photometric measurements." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Small CO-selected sample with uncertain representativeness  
  > "It is not clear whether our target galaxies, preselected to have CO detections, are typical in their gas and/or dust properties." (Discussion and Conclusions); "While acknowledging our small sample size," (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): No metallicity estimates; super-solar metallicity is expected from a mass–metallicity relation.  
  > "No metallicity estimates are available for these galaxies, but they are expected to be super-solar based on the mass–metallicity relation (cite: mannucci10)." (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): UV+IR SFRs may overestimate true SFRs because old stars heat dust.  
  > "We expect these to overestimate the true SFR because old stars can also contribute to the dust heating at low SFR" (Quiescent Galaxies and ALMA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Comparison-sample selection criteria vary and are not scrutinized in detail.  
  > "The selection criteria can vary among different studies, but typically include either sSFR or UVJ color-color cuts (or both)." (Literature Comparison Galaxies); "It is beyond the scope of this work to scrutinize these differences in detail." (Literature Comparison Galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): αCO dominates CO excitation uncertainty; carbon abundance dominates reasonable [CI] excitation uncertainty.  
  > "For our CO(2–1) observations, the uncertainty in CO excitation is far subdominant to that in $\alpha_{\rm{CO}}$." (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations); "with the carbon abundance as the main uncertainty for any reasonable value of $T_\mathrm{ex}$." (Dust and Gas Mass Calculations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two continuum detections and a stack of three nondetections provide limited leverage on the population distribution.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Agreement with SIMBA does not uniquely identify thermal sputtering or inefficient grain growth as the cause.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas-to-dust ratios remain conditional on αCO, dust emissivity, and temperature assumptions.
