## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare line maps and noise estimates**: Reduce with CASA, then GILDAS and Python/Astropy; convert maps to kelvin; estimate rms in an emission-free channel near the galaxy centre.  
  > "Initial reduction of the data was carried out using the ALMA reduction package CASA" (ALMA data); "and then exported to GILDAS" (ALMA data); "by calculating the rms of the signal over an area towards the centre of the galaxy in a channel with no line detection" (ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS, Python, Astropy
   - parameter: CS(7-6): eleven-field mosaic; 17″ per field; final FOV ~50″; 138 minutes on source  
  > "A mosaic of eleven fields from band 7 was used to cover both the CND and SB ring, each with a field of view of 17$''$." (Cycle 0); "with on source times of 24 - 39 minutes each, for a total of 138 minutes." (Cycle 0); "The field of view of the final mosaic is $\sim50''$" (Cycle 0)
   - parameter: CS(7-6) windows: LSB 341.94, 343.75 GHz; USB 353.81, 355.68 GHz; 1.88 GHz bandwidth; 488 kHz (~13 km s^-1)  
  > "Two of the spectral windows were in the lower sideband (LSB) centred on 341.94 GHz and 343.75 GHz and two in the upper sideband (USB) centred on 353.81 GHz and 355.68 GHz." (Cycle 0); "Each of these windows had a bandwidth of 1.88 GHz and a resolution of 488 kHz ($\sim$ 13 km s$^{-1}$)." (Cycle 0)
   - parameter: CS(2-1) windows: LSB 86.3, 88.1 GHz; USB 98.2, 100.1 GHz; 1.87 GHz bandwidth; 977 kHz (~9 km s^-1); FOV 70″  
  > "Each spectral window had a bandwidth of 1.87 GHz and a resolution of 977 kHz ($\sim$ 9 km s$^{-1}$)." (Cycle 2); "The two LSB windows were centred at 86.3 and 88.1 GHz, and in the USB at 98.2 and 100.1 GHz." (Cycle 2); "The resulting 70$''$ FOV was sufficient to cover both the CND and SB ring." (Cycle 2)
   - parameter: CS(3-2): 44 minutes; baselines 15 - 1100 m; beam 0.66″ × 0.44″ (PA 85°)  
  > "were taken with an exposure time of 44 minutes and baselines of 15 - 1100 m, using a $0.66'' \times 0.44'' (\text{PA }85^\circ)$ beam" (Cycle 3)
   - parameter: CS(3-2) windows: LSB 144.6, 146.4 GHz; USB 156.6, 158.5 GHz; 1.87 GHz bandwidth; 1953 kHz (~4 km s^-1); FOV 42″  
  > "centred on 144.6 and 146.4 GHz in the LSB and 156.6 and 158.5 GHz in the USB." (Cycle 3); "Each of these windows has a resolution of 1953 kHz ($\sim$ 4 km s$^{-1}$) and bandwidth of 1.87 GHz." (Cycle 3); "The resulting map has an FOV of 42$''$" (Cycle 3)
   - parameter: CS(6-5): 61 minute exposure; 21″ FOV  
  > "with a 61 minute exposure and baselines of 15 - 772 m to produce a map with a 21$''$ FOV." (Cycle 3)
   - parameter: CS(6-5) windows: LSB 291.87, 293.74 GHz; USB 303.79, 305.66 GHz; 3.74 GHz bandwidth; 3906 kHz (~4 km s^-1)  
  > "The spectral windows in the LSB were centred on 291.87 and 293.74 GHz, and on 303.79 and 305.66 GHz in the USB" (Cycle 3); "with bandwidths of 3.74 GHz and resolutions of 3906 kHz ($\sim$ 4 km s$^{-1}$) in each." (Cycle 3)
   - parameter: Jy beam^-1 to K factors: CS(7-6) 36; CS(2-1) 340; CS(3-2) 194 K Jy^-1 beam  
  > "a conversion factor of 36 K Jy$^{-1}$ beam was used" (Cycle 0); "Conversion of the map to K was done with a conversion factor of 340 K Jy$^{-1}$ beam" (Cycle 2); "A conversion factor of 194 K Jy$^{-1}$ beam and an assumed absolute flux error of 15% were used." (Cycle 3)
   - parameter: CS(6-5) conversion factor: 63 K Jy^-1 beam; CS(3-2) rms: 0.05 K  
  > "The assumed absolute error on flux values was 15%, and the conversion factor 63 K Jy$^{-1}$ beam." (Cycle 3); "The $1\sigma$-rms noise level was 0.05 K." (Cycle 3)
   - parameter: Noise-channel centres: CS(2-1) 497; CS(3-2) 247; CS(6-5) 805; CS(7-6) -450 km/s  
  > "These velocity channels were centred on $497$ km/s for CS(2-1), $247$ km/s for CS(3-2), $805$ km/s for CS(6-5) and $-450$ km/s for CS(7-6)." (ALMA data)
   - assumption: Distance ~14 Mpc; 1 arcsecond = 70 pc; systemic velocity 1127 km s^-1 (HEL)  
  > "we take the distance to NGC 1068 to be $\sim$14 Mpc, such that 1 arcsecond corresponds to 70 pc" (ALMA data); "take the systemic velocity of the galaxy as $v_{sys}\text{(HEL)} = 1127$ km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Garcia)." (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: For CS(3-2), explicitly check that the rms measurement area contains no channel emission.  
  > "extra care was taken to ensure the area over which the rms was calculated contained no emission in the channel." (ALMA data)
2. **Measure sub-region line intensities**: Fit Gaussians to detected spectra at the selected sub-regions; use Gaussian areas for integrated line intensities.  
  > "with Gaussians fitted to the emission in the cases where there is a detection." (Molecular line emission); "In this analysis we take $W$ to be equal to the area of the fitted Gaussian, $1.06\times T_{peak}\Delta\nu$" (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Spectra between ±250 km/s; 3σ-rms detection threshold  
  > "between $\pm 250$ km/s" (Emission maps and spectra); "the red the $3\sigma$-rms detection threshold." (Emission maps and spectra)
   - parameter: FWHM in velocity units; Gaussian geometry factor 1.06  
  > "$\Delta\nu$ the full width at half-maximum (FWHM) in units of velocity and the factor $1.06$ accounts for the geometry." (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Exclude CS(6-5) from SB ring analysis  
  > "we exclude the CS(6-5) transition from the analysis of sub-regions within the SB ring." (Cycle 3)
   - fitting: Gaussian emission-line fits  
  > "with Gaussians fitted to the emission in the cases where there is a detection." (Molecular line emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compare spatial line ratios**: Gaussian-convolve maps to the CS(2-1) resolution and divide corresponding pixels of un-clipped maps.  
  > "degrade them all to the lowest common spatial resolution through convolution with an appropriate Gaussian for each transition." (Molecular line ratios); "Three independent CS line ratio maps were thus produced by dividing the flux in corresponding pixels of the two un-clipped emission maps" (Molecular line ratios)
   - parameter: 0.75″ × 0.51″ (75°)  
  > "In this case a resolution of $0.75'' \times 0.51'' (75^\circ)$ was chosen, corresponding to the CS(2-1) map." (Molecular line ratios)
   - parameter: R32/21, R65/21, R76/21  
  > "CS(3-2)/CS(2-1), CS(6-5)/CS(2-1) and CS(7-6)/CS(2-1), hereafter referred to as R32/21, R65/21 and R76/21 respectively." (Molecular line ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Calculate LTE CS column densities**: Calculate N_u from integrated line intensity, Einstein A-coefficient, filling factor and optical-depth correction; convert each transition to total CS column density with the Boltzmann relation.  
  > "N_u = \frac{8\pi k\nu^2W}{hc^3A_{ul}}\left(\frac{\Delta\Omega_a}{\Delta\Omega_s}\right)C_\tau" (LTE analysis); "N = \frac{N_uZ(T)}{g_ue^{\frac{-E_u}{kT}}}" (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Temperatures: 15, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250 K  
  > "|   EKnot    |  15   |   11    |   8.5   |   8.8   |   19    |" (Table 2); "|            |  50   |   31    |   17    |   3.4   |   3.4   |
|            |  100  |   59    |   29    |   4.1   |   3.5   |" (Table 2); "|            |  150  |   110   |   56    |   7.0   |   5.6   |
|            |  200  |   170   |   82    |   9.6   |   7.5   |
|            |  250  |   160   |   81    |   9.2   |   7.1   |" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Source fills beam; filling factor unity; all sub-region emission traces the same gas  
  > "We also initially assume that the source completely fills the beam such that the beam filling factor is unity and that all emission within a sub-region traces the same gas." (LTE analysis)
   - assumption: Optically thin; optical-depth correction unity  
  > "We have assumed here that the gas is optically thin, setting the optical depth correction factor" (LTE analysis); "to unity." (LTE analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare column densities inferred from different transitions; disagreement indicates at least one failed assumption.  
  > "For a given temperature all of the transitions should theoretically give the same value for the column density in a sub-region; however, this is not the case here." (LTE analysis); "This suggests that at least one of the assumptions made is incorrect." (LTE analysis)
5. **Fit rotational diagrams and test opacity**: Fit ln(N_u/g_u) versus upper-level energy; infer rotational temperature from negative inverse slope; iterate opacity corrections; also fit low-J and high-J pairs separately.  
  > "A rotational diagram is a plot of the natural logarithm of column density over statistical weight of each transition against the energy in kelvin of the upper energy level" (Rotation diagram); "The negative inverse slope of this line gives the rotational temperature" (Rotation diagram); "line fits were also obtained for the CS(2-1) and CS(3-2) transitions only, and similarly for CS(6-5) and CS(7-6) only." (Rotation diagram)
   - parameter: Column-density errors: band 3 10%; bands 4 and 7 15%  
  > "The error bars were obtained by assuming an error of 10% on the transition column densities for transitions observed in band 3, and 15% for bands 4 and 7 observations." (Rotation diagram)
   - parameter: Cτ = τ/(1 - e^-τ); iterate until stable  
  > "C_\tau = \frac{\tau}{1 - e^{-\tau}}" (Rotation diagram); "This process is repeated iteratively until a stable value is found for $C_\tau$." (Rotation diagram)
   - assumption: Initially optically thin; rotational temperature equals kinetic temperature only for thermalised, optically thin levels  
  > "Using the values of $N_u$ calculated under the optically thin assumption" (Rotation diagram); "The rotational and kinetic temperature are only equivalent if all levels are thermalised and optically thin." (Rotation diagram)
   - fitting: Straight-line single-phase and separate low-J/high-J fits  
  > "If the assumption of LTE is correct the data can be fit by a straight line." (Rotation diagram); "line fits were also obtained for the CS(2-1) and CS(3-2) transitions only, and similarly for CS(6-5) and CS(7-6) only." (Rotation diagram)
   - validation: Cτ ≈ 1 immediately; diagrams unchanged; compare inferred columns with Boltzmann columns at the nearest kinetic temperature.  
  > "We immediately find $C_\tau \approx 1$ for each sub-region, such that the values calculated from our new rotational diagrams remain unchanged, and so we cease iterating." (Rotation diagram); "calculated for that sub-region at the kinetic temperature closest to the obtained rotational temperature." (Rotation diagram)
6. **Fit non-LTE excitation with RADEX**: Grid H2 density, CS column density and kinetic temperature; compare predicted main-beam temperature to observed peak temperature using reduced χ².  
  > "By varying the H$_2$ density, CS column density and kinetic temperature in the model, a grid of $\sim 85~000$ points was produced" (RADEX); "comparing the main beam temperature predicted ($T_{mb}$) to the peak temperature ($T_{peak}$) observed using the reduced $\chi^2$ test." (RADEX)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: H2 density 10^3 - 10^7 cm^-3; CS column density 10^12 - 10^18 cm^-2; kinetic temperature 10-200 K  
  > "H$_2$ density: $10^3 - 10^7$ cm$^{-3}$" (RADEX); "CS column density: $10^{12} - 10^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$" (RADEX); "Kinetic temperature: $10-200$ K" (RADEX)
   - parameter: Line widths: CND 150 km s^-1; SB ring 50 km s^-1  
  > "This was taken to be 150 km s$^{-1}$ within the CND and 50 km s$^{-1}$ within the SB ring, where the transitions appear narrower." (RADEX)
   - parameter: Constraint contours: 3χ²_min; NSB-c 1.5χ²_min; AGN χ² = 3  
  > "For each sub-region, the contour corresponding to a value of $3\chi_{min}^2$ is plotted" (RADEX); "The NSB-c and AGN sub-regions are exceptions to this, with the contour instead placed at $1.5\chi_{min}^2$ and $3$" (RADEX)
   - assumption: Uniform sphere escape probability  
  > "Testing found that the geometry used had negligible impact on the results, and so the uniform sphere geometry was arbitrarily chosen." (RADEX)
   - assumption: LAMDA molecular data; H2 collisions; background 2.73 K  
  > "The molecular data files used in the simulation were obtained from the LAMDA database (cite: LAMDA), using H$_2$ as the collision partner." (RADEX); "The background temperature in each sub-region was set equal to the cosmic background, 2.73 K" (RADEX)
   - assumption: Impose 50 K CO rotational temperature as upper bound when constraining other parameters  
  > "Thus we choose to use the CO rotational temperature of 50 K derived by (cite: Viti) as an upper bound for the temperatures when constraining the other parameters." (RADEX)
   - fitting: Reduced-χ² grid search; fixed-variable slices use the minimum-χ² value  
  > "using the reduced $\chi^2$ test." (RADEX); "In each case, the value of the fixed variable is chosen by taking the value that gives the lowest $\chi^2$ in each sub-region." (RADEX models)
   - validation: Test sphere, LVG and slab geometries; extend temperature grid to 1,000 K; upper temperatures remain unconstrained.  
  > "Three different options are available for the applied escape probability method, that of a uniform sphere, an expanding sphere (LVG) or a plane parallel slab." (RADEX); "Testing found that the geometry used had negligible impact on the results" (RADEX); "Testing a larger grid of temperatures with values up to $1,000$ K also resulted in unconstrained upper bounds for the temperatures." (RADEX)
7. **Estimate RADEX beam filling factors**: Vary filling factor after selecting the best RADEX model; compare corrected observations with that model using χ².  
  > "This was done by varying the beam filling factor assumed, and then comparing the corrected observations to the best fitting model previously selected for the sub-region, again using the $\chi^2$ test to evaluate the fits." (RADEX)
   - assumption: Do not fit a J-dependent filling factor jointly with physical parameters  
  > "The addition of this additional parameter would worsen the constraints we have for the other parameters, and so we forgo the inclusion in favour of our current fits." (RADEX)
   - fitting: Choose filling factor giving the best χ² fit to the previously selected model  
  > "The value which gives the best fit is then taken as the beam filling factor in that sub-region." (RADEX)
   - validation: Near-unity factors judged implausible; motivates chemical-feasibility modelling  
  > "As it is unlikely that the gas completely fills the beam in all sub-regions these values suggest the conditions found to be incorrect, likely as a result of the models not considering the chemical feasibility of the parameters." (RADEX)
8. **Generate chemically constrained SLEDs**: Free-fall collapse followed by fixed-density heating and evolution; convert chemical abundances and extinction to CS column density; pass physical conditions and column density to RADEX.  
  > "The first stage allows the cloud to collapse by free-fall until it reaches a specified density, in order to create a high density cloud with self consistent abundances." (UCLCHEM); "In the second stage the collapse is halted such that the density remains constant and a burst of star formation is simulated by heating the gas up to a specified temperature." (UCLCHEM); "From these the CS column density is computed and input into RADEX alongside the temperature and density of the model to produce theoretical SLEDs for each of the sub-regions." (UCLCHEM)
   - software: UCLCHEM, RADEX
   - parameter: Initial density 10 cm^-3; collapse at 10 K; evolution for 10^7 years  
  > "an initial density of 10 cm$^{-3}$." (UCLCHEM); "This collapse is carried out at a fixed temperature of 10 K" (UCLCHEM); "The cloud is then allowed to evolve for $10^7$ years." (UCLCHEM)
   - parameter: Final densities 10^4, 10^5, 10^6 cm^-3; corresponding A_v 10, 20, 50 mag; temperatures 50, 100, 300 K  
  > "Final density: $10^4, 10^5, 10^6$ cm$^{-3}$" (UCLCHEM); "A$_v$: $10, 20, 50$ mag" (UCLCHEM); "Temperature: $50, 100, 300$ K" (UCLCHEM)
   - parameter: ζ = 1, 10, 10^2, 10^3 ζ0; 36 models stated  
  > "$\zeta$: $1, 10, 10^2, 10^3~\zeta_0$" (UCLCHEM); "A total of 36 models were run over the following parameter grid, chosen to cover a range of conditions:" (UCLCHEM)
   - assumption: Diffuse atomic initial cloud; standard solar metallicity ISM abundances  
  > "starting with a cloud of diffuse atomic gas, at standard solar metallicity ISM abundances (cite: abundances)" (UCLCHEM)
   - assumption: Collapse-stage ζ0 = 5 × 10^-17 s^-1  
  > "with the standard cosmic ray ionisation rate for molecular hydrogen of $\zeta_0 = 5\times 10^{-17} s^{-1}$ (cite: Williams)." (UCLCHEM)
   - assumption: Cloud size varied with density to obtain density-dependent extinction  
  > "The initial size of the cloud was also varied in tandem with the density, so as to ensure the resulting cloud had a visual extinction typical of its density" (UCLCHEM)
   - assumption: Enhanced cosmic ray ionisation intended to replicate enhanced X-ray flux  
  > "the large range of cosmic ray ionisation rates is intended to replicate the effect of an enhanced X-ray flux." (UCLCHEM)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Select chemical models and compare regions**: Group predicted SLEDs by ladder shape; select each sub-region's best χ² model within its matching group; estimate filling factors a posteriori and compare with RADEX-only fits.  
  > "first splitting the former into 5 groups based on the shapes of their predicted ladders, independently of the physical parameters, labelled A through E." (UCLCHEM); "Using a standard $\chi^2$ test, the best fitting model from amongst those in the same group as the observations in each sub-region were selected." (UCLCHEM); "using the results of our UCLCHEM & RADEX modelling to obtain a value a posteriori" (UCLCHEM)
   - software: UCLCHEM, RADEX
   - parameter: A, B, C: peaks at CS(2-1), CS(3-2), CS(6-5), respectively  
  > "SLEDs in the first three groups are characterised by the transition for which their ladders peak at, CS(2-1), CS(3-2) and CS(6-5) for groups A, B and C respectively." (UCLCHEM)
   - parameter: D: CS(3-2) ≈ CS(6-5); E: CS(6-5) ≈ CS(7-6)  
  > "In the remaining groups the two brightest transitions have approximately equal emission" (UCLCHEM); "These transitions are CS(3-2) and CS(6-5) in the case of group D, and CS(6-5) and CS(7-6) in the case of group E." (UCLCHEM)
   - assumption: Fit each sub-region independently  
  > "no single model is able to match all of the sub-regions either, once again necessitating that each sub-region be fit independently." (UCLCHEM)
   - fitting: Shape-group restriction followed by standard χ² selection  
  > "Using a standard $\chi^2$ test, the best fitting model from amongst those in the same group as the observations in each sub-region were selected." (UCLCHEM)
   - validation: Compare observed SLEDs with coupled-model and RADEX-only SLEDs; RADEX-only often fits better.  
  > "In many cases, it appears that the results from using RADEX alone give a better fit to the observations than the coupled models (e.g. for the AGN and CND-S sub-regions)." (UCLCHEM)

## Qualitative findings

- Physical conditions vary between sub-regions.  
  > "A common result across all of the modelling methods used is the requirement of different conditions to fit the emission observed in the various sub-regions, showing that the conditions in the galaxy are non-uniform." (Conclusions)
- Lower-J CS dominates around the AGN in the ratio maps.  
  > "These drops in ratio signify that the lower J CS emission is dominant over the higher J in these central areas surrounding the AGN." (Molecular line ratios)
- Rotational diagrams point to gas outside LTE; two phases and sub-thermal excitation remain degenerate.  
  > "Nor can we differentiate between the gas being two phased or sub-thermally excited." (Rotation diagram); "Instead, these results point to the gas not being in LTE." (Rotation diagram)
- RADEX-only fits often outperform chemically coupled fits, especially for AGN and CND-S.  
  > "In many cases, it appears that the results from using RADEX alone give a better fit to the observations than the coupled models (e.g. for the AGN and CND-S sub-regions)." (UCLCHEM)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Four CS transitions resolve sub-regions and compare inner and outer gas affected by AGN and starburst activity.  
  > "In this paper we focus on the chemical analysis of CS observations within NGC 1068, using four CS line transitions with a common spatial resolution of 35 pc." (Introduction); "we aim to disentangle the effects of the AGN and SB on the gas near the centre of the galaxy." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Couple UCLCHEM to RADEX to enforce chemical feasibility absent from excitation-only modelling.  
  > "A limitation of the RADEX modelling is that it does not consider the chemical feasibility of the conditions used, such that the resulting best fit model may not be physical." (UCLCHEM); "the output of the chemical code UCLCHEM (cite: UCLCHEM) was coupled with RADEX in order to produce realistic parameters" (UCLCHEM)
- Alternative: Uniform sphere, expanding sphere/LVG and plane parallel slab give negligible geometry dependence.  
  > "Three different options are available for the applied escape probability method, that of a uniform sphere, an expanding sphere (LVG) or a plane parallel slab." (RADEX); "Testing found that the geometry used had negligible impact on the results" (RADEX)
- Alternative: Extending the RADEX temperature grid to 1,000 K does not constrain upper temperatures.  
  > "Testing a larger grid of temperatures with values up to $1,000$ K also resulted in unconstrained upper bounds for the temperatures." (RADEX)
- Alternative: Jointly fitting a J-dependent beam filling factor rejected because it would weaken other parameter constraints.  
  > "The addition of this additional parameter would worsen the constraints we have for the other parameters, and so we forgo the inclusion in favour of our current fits." (RADEX)
- Alternative: Two-phase gas, varying UV strength and shocks proposed as model improvements.  
  > "One way in which these fits could be improved is to once again adopt a two phase model for the gas" (UCLCHEM); "Accounting for the change in UV strength between the CND and the SB ring" (UCLCHEM); "It may also be necessary to include shocks in the modelling of the CND regions in order to accurately reproduce the observations." (UCLCHEM)
- Alternative: Bar region excluded because CS is not detected.  
  > "Due to this lack of CS detection in the bar region, we do not look at it in this analysis." (Molecular line emission)
- How they decided it was enough: Stop opacity iterations when Cτ ≈ 1 leaves rotational diagrams unchanged.  
  > "We immediately find $C_\tau \approx 1$ for each sub-region, such that the values calculated from our new rotational diagrams remain unchanged, and so we cease iterating." (Rotation diagram)
- Limitation (authors): Only 3-4 transitions per sub-region limit modelling reliability.  
  > "currently only 3-4 transitions are available for each which limits the reliability of our modelling efforts." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Rotational diagrams cannot reliably constrain conditions or distinguish two phases from sub-thermal excitation.  
  > "Due to the low number of transitions in the rotational diagrams, we are unable to use these results to reliably constrain the conditions in each of the sub-regions." (Rotation diagram); "Nor can we differentiate between the gas being two phased or sub-thermally excited." (Rotation diagram)
- Limitation (authors): RADEX temperatures have unconstrained upper bounds.  
  > "We found the upper bound of the temperature in each sub-region to be unconstrained within this temperature range." (RADEX)
- Limitation (authors): 2.73 K background may be inappropriate at the AGN.  
  > "The background temperature in each sub-region was set equal to the cosmic background, 2.73 K, though this may not be completely valid for the AGN sub-region." (RADEX)
- Limitation (authors): Coarse chemical grid makes tabulated values approximate.  
  > "Note that, due to the coarseness of the grid used, the actual values likely differ from those in the table." (UCLCHEM)
- Limitation (authors): Sulphur depletion not investigated.  
  > "We are aware that sulphur is often considered to be depleted in chemical star formation models, however such considerations are beyond the scope of this work" (UCLCHEM)
- Limitation (authors): Cause of Boltzmann/rotational column-density discrepancy remains uncertain.  
  > "we are unsure as to the cause of the discrepancy between the two methods." (Rotation diagram)
- Limitation (authors): CS(6-5) cannot cover the SB ring; CS(3-2) SNR drops outside the ring.  
  > "we exclude the CS(6-5) transition from the analysis of sub-regions within the SB ring." (Cycle 3); "the SNR begins to drop off immediately outside the SB ring." (Cycle 3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The listed chemical-grid dimensions imply 108 combinations, whereas the paper states 36 models; the actual run selection is unresolved.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abstract reports hydrogen volume densities in cm^-2, while the modelling sections and conclusions use cm^-3.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The introductory common-resolution description needs reconciliation with the explicit statement that only ratio maps use convolved images.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ad hoc χ² contour multipliers are not stated confidence intervals.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A posteriori filling factors do not propagate their covariance with excitation and chemical parameters.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The externally imposed 50 K RADEX bound should not be treated as a measured CS temperature.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Coarse-grid temperatures, ionisation rates and proposed shock chemistry are weaker answer keys than directly measured line intensities.
