## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the 1.3 mm continuum**: Standard ALMA pipeline calibration in CASA, 10 s time averaging, tclean continuum imaging with natural weighting  
  > "The data were calibrated using the standard ALMA pipeline in" (2.1 Observational information); "We carried out continuum imaging and deconvolution using the "tclean" task in CASA." (2.1 Observational information)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: visibilities time-averaged in 10 s intervals  
  > "To reduce the data volume, the calibrated visibilities were time-averaged in intervals of 10s." (2.1 Observational information)
   - parameter: natural weighting  
  > "To obtain the greatest signal-to-noise ratio, we reconstructed the image using natural weighing." (2.1 Observational information)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure surface brightness profiles and ansa asymmetry**: Radial profiles along major and minor axes with radial averaging in 10 deg-wide swaths; compare peak fluxes of the two ansae  
  > "we analysed the surface brightness profiles along both the major and minor axes with radial averaging using $10\deg$-wide swaths." (2.2 Results)
   - parameter: SW ansa peak 158 +/- 16 uJy/beam, about 2 sigma above the opposite ansa  
  > "the peak flux of SW ($158 \pm 16$ $\mu$Jy $\rm beam^{-1}$)" (2.2 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Image the two observation blocks separately and measure central flux**: Individual OB1 and OB2 images; OB2 smoothed to OB1 beam with UV tapering; peak pixel near the stellar position taken as central emission  
  > "We first created a version of the OB2 image with the same beam size as OB1 by smoothing the OB2 image with UV tapering." (2.2 Results); "We also find the pixel with peak flux near the stellar position and report this flux as a measure of the central emission in the three images shown in Fig." (2.2 Results)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Two epochs used to confirm the authenticity of each signal  
  > "The existence of two epochs helps in identifying any time evolution in the HD 105211 system whilst confirming the authenticity of each signal." (2.1 Observational information)
4. **Fit parametric disc models to the ALMA visibilities**: Axisymmetric Gaussian ring and eccentric ring models, Fourier transformed and sampled at the observed uv points, chi2 against the visibilities; stellar-position point-source flux F_c added  
  > "The image generated was then Fourier transformed to the visibility plane and sampled at the same UV points as our continuum observations" (3.2.1 Parametric model); "so we added another parameter: the flux density at stellar position" (3.2.1 Parametric model)
   - software: emcee, zodipic-like geometric model, CASA statwt
   - parameter: data averaged to 10 s and four channels per spectral window, weights recomputed with statwt  
  > "We averaged the data into 10-second long chunks and spectrally averaged the four spectral windows down to four channels per spectral window." (3.2.1 Parametric model); "Then we used the CASA "statwt" task to recompute the visibility weighting following the averaging process." (3.2.1 Parametric model)
   - parameter: 100 walkers, 1000 steps, first 900 discarded as burn-in  
  > "We used 100 walkers and chains with 1000 steps and discard the first 900 steps as a" (3.2.1 Parametric model)
   - assumption: optically thin torus; uniform posterior distribution functions for all parameters  
  > "We assumed posterior distribution functions are uniform for all parameters." (3.2.1 Parametric model); "to place more constraints on the disc as an optically thin torus" (3.2.1 Parametric model)
   - fitting: Ensemble MCMC (emcee), chi2 goodness of fit in visibility space; model initialised near the optimal solution from prior testing runs  
  > "The model was initialised near the optimal solution based on prior testing runs." (3.2.1 Parametric model); "computing the $\chi^{2}$ between the model and the data to quantify the goodness-of-fit" (3.2.1 Parametric model)
   - validation: Residual maps of the Gaussian model; posterior distributions show little degeneracy  
  > "we only computed the residual maps of the Gaussian model at the same observational resolution" (3.2.2 Results); "The distributions show that the parameters are well constrained and show little degeneracy." (6 Posterior probability distributions of disc parameters)
5. **Fit OB1 and OB2 separately to test variability and asymmetry**: Same Gaussian and eccentric models fitted to each observation block  
  > "we perform parameter model fitting on the data of the two observations (OB1 and OB2) separately to quantify the impacts of noise and variability on the results" (3.3 Variability)
   - software: emcee
   - fitting: Same as step 4  
  > "We used both the Gaussian and eccentric models and show the results in Table" (3.3 Variability)
   - validation: Fitted stellar fluxes compared with the SED-expected 100 uJy (about 4 sigma and 5 sigma excess) to rule out noise or variability  
  > "confirming that the excess flux obtained in Sect." (3.3 Variability)
6. **Fit the SED with a two-temperature model**: PHOENIX stellar photosphere plus modified blackbody disc components; ALMA 1.3 mm flux taken from the visibility fit; warm component forced to near pure blackbody to match the ALMA central flux  
  > "Hence, we adopted a two-temperature model to characterise the infrared excesses." (3.1 Spectral energy distribution); "the ALMA Band 6 flux was obtained from our best-fit parameters including the disc flux" (3.1 Spectral energy distribution)
   - software: MultiNest, PHOENIX model grid
   - parameter: modified blackbody: lambda0 = 380 um, beta = 2.3 (cold); lambda0 = 160 um, beta ~ 0 (warm)  
  > "$\lambda_{0} = 380\mu$m, $\beta = 2.3$" (3.1 Spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: blackbody multiplied by (lambda0/lambda)^beta for lambda >= lambda0  
  > "the blackbody function was multiplied by a factor $(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)^{\beta}$ for wavelengths $\lambda \geq \lambda_{0}$" (3.1 Spectral energy distribution)
   - fitting: MultiNest MCMC fit across all photometric and spectral points  
  > "Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm (cite: 2009MNRAS.398.1601F)" (3.1 Spectral energy distribution); "minimisation was done across all photometric and spectral points" (3.1 Spectral energy distribution)
   - validation: A single-temperature model could not fit the mid-IR shape, motivating the second component  
  > "We found that a single temperature model cannot fit the shape of the mid-IR emission in our SED, so an additional warm component was needed to fit the IRS spectrum." (3.1 Spectral energy distribution)
7. **Derive disc mass loss, dust mass and radius ratio**: Mass-loss rate from steady-state collisional cascade equation; mm dust mass from Wyatt 2008 eq. 5 with kappa = 2.3 cm^2/g; Gamma = actual radius / blackbody radius  
  > "By assuming a steady-state collisional cascade model, we could estimate the mass-loss rate of the smallest grains" (4.2 Collisional status of the disc); "the mass of millimetre-sized dust can be calculated using equation (5) of (cite: 2008ARA&A..46..339W) and the best-fitting flux" (4.2 Collisional status of the disc)
   - assumption: millimetre dust opacity 2.3 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "Assuming a millimetre dust opacity of $\kappa=2.3$ $cm^{2}$ $g^{-1}$" (4.2 Collisional status of the disc)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Dust mass compared with Hengst et al. (2017) value  
  > "The dust mass obtained in the model results by (cite: 2017MNRAS.468.4725H) is $2.45\times10^{-2} M_{\oplus}$" (4.2 Collisional status of the disc)
8. **Constrain the warm inner dust component**: Compare the observed warm fractional luminosity with the maximum steady-state collisional value at the blackbody radius, and compare the blackbody-like spectrum with cascade expectations  
  > "Using equation (20) in (cite: 2007ApJ...658..569W) and assuming the warm component blackbody radius $R_{bb}$ = $7\pm1$ au" (4.3 Warm inner dust component)
   - assumption: two origin scenarios considered: in situ planetesimal belt or comet delivery  
  > "we considered two potential processes for this dust component." (4.3 Warm inner dust component)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Compare ALMA model with Herschel 70 um profile**: Best-fit ALMA model radial profile convolved with the ALMA and Herschel 70 um beams, compared with the Herschel 70 um image profile  
  > "We also compared the radial profiles between ALMA and the *Herschel* 70 $\mu$m original image in Fig." (4.1 Comparison with Herschel)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The ALMA disc is a narrow ring, unlike the broad disc inferred from Herschel; the Herschel breadth is attributed to small grains modified by radiation forces  
  > "The structure distribution in the ALMA images displays a narrow ring, different to the broad structure of Herschel images" (5 Conclusions)
- Both observation blocks show excess emission at the stellar position above the stellar flux, suggesting a warm millimetre-sized dust component  
  > "The excess emission from the stellar position suggests that there should be a warm millimetre-sized dust component close to the star" (abstract)
- Brightness asymmetries between OB1 and OB2 are not explained by eccentricity; possible transient collision or noise  
  > "The different brightness distributions between the two observation blocks suggest the presence of potential transient collision events" (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Two epochs allow checking time evolution and confirming the authenticity of signals  
  > "The existence of two epochs helps in identifying any time evolution in the HD 105211 system whilst confirming the authenticity of each signal." (2.1 Observational information)
- Why this method: Parametric optically thin torus fit to visibilities is common for mm-resolved debris discs; natural weighting for best S/N  
  > "We fit a parametric disc model to place more constraints on the disc as an optically thin torus using observed visibilities, which is commonly used for describing millimetre-resolved debris discs." (3.2.1 Parametric model); "To obtain the greatest signal-to-noise ratio, we reconstructed the image using natural weighing." (2.1 Observational information)
- Alternative: Eccentric ring model tested because the profile suggested eccentricity; rejected in favour of the Gaussian ring  
  > "Since the surface brightness profile indicates that the disc might be eccentric, we also considered an eccentric model" (3.2.1 Parametric model); "Therefore, we have chosen the Gaussian model parameters as our result." (3.3 Variability)
- Alternative: Single-temperature SED model rejected; two-temperature model adopted  
  > "We found that a single temperature model cannot fit the shape of the mid-IR emission in our SED" (3.1 Spectral energy distribution)
- Alternative: Warm dust origin: in situ asteroid belt vs comet delivery, not decisively separated  
  > "we considered two potential processes for this dust component." (4.3 Warm inner dust component)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Vertical structure may be unresolved  
  > "Considering the shape of the disc and the size of the beam, the vertical direction may not be resolved." (3.2.2 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Origin of central-source extension (inner component or noise) unresolved  
  > "Whether these emissions come from an inner component or just noise needs to be investigated further." (2.2 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass strongly depends on assumed opacity  
  > "However, the estimation of dust mass is strongly dependent on the assumption about the opacity" (4.2 Collisional status of the disc)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The beam (~1.6 arcsec) is comparable to the ring width, so the 23.6 au width is poorly resolved and depends on the model shape.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Central excess of ~5 sigma relies on the SED-predicted stellar flux; the paper reports an unresolved spectroscopic binary and residuals around the star below 2 sigma.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Eccentricity upper limit is quoted as e <= 0.03 in the abstract but e <= 0.02 in Sect. 3.2.2, while the combined fit gives 0.014 +/- 0.007; the text is inconsistent.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Width quoted as 24.6 au in Sect. 4.1 but 23.6 au elsewhere.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absolute pointing offset of ~0.15 arcsec in OB1 is attributed to ALMA pointing precision without verification.
