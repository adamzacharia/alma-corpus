## Analysis recipe

1. **Orient maps and extract limb points**: Rotate solar North upwards, correct coordinates for Earth's orbital eccentricity, and identify scan-derivative maxima and minima as solar limb coordinates.  
  > "The first step is to extract the solar limb coordinates from each map which are defined as the maximum and minimum points of the numerical differentiation" (Radius Determination); "All solar maps are rotated so that the position of the solar North points upwards, and the coordinates are corrected according to the eccentricity of the Earth’s orbit" (Radius Determination)
   - parameter: Accept center-to-limb distances between 815 arcsec (0.85 Rθ) and 1100 arcsec (1.15 Rθ)  
  > "With this radius determination filter, only points with a center–to–limb distance between ${815}^{\prime\prime}$ ($0.85 R_{\theta}$) and ${1100}^{\prime\prime}$ ($1.15 R_{\theta}$) are considered." (Radius Determination)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Limb-point filtering intended to reject active-region, instrumental-error, and high-opacity contamination  
  > "During the limb points extraction, some criteria are adopted to avoid extracting limb points associated with active regions, instrumental errors or high atmospheric opacity" (Radius Determination)
2. **Fit equatorial and polar radii for each map**: Fit circles to latitude-selected limb coordinates; calculate radius as the average center-to-limb distance; apply the same method to SST and ALMA.  
  > "The solar limb coordinates are fit by a circle and the radius is calculated as the average of the center-to-limb distances." (Radius Determination); "The same method is applied to both telescopes, so that a comparison between the 212 GHz radius of SST and the 230 GHz radius of ALMA could be made." (Radius Determination)
   - parameter: Equatorial: 30° N to 30° S; polar: above 60° N and below 60° S  
  > "First, the radius of each map is determined using only the equatorial latitudes of the solar disk – points between ${30}^{\circ}$ N and ${30}^{\circ}$ S." (Radius Determination); "Then, the polar radius is determined considering only points above ${60}^{\circ}$ N and below ${60}^{\circ}$ S." (Radius Determination)
   - parameter: Retain at least 35% of limb points, usually 6 out of 16; otherwise discard the map  
  > "This process is repeated until there are at least 35% of the points, usually 6 out of 16 depending on the map and the latitude region – polar or equatorial." (Radius Determination); "If there are fewer points remaining, the entire map is discarded; otherwise, the radius is calculated." (Radius Determination)
   - parameter: Store radii between 800 and 1300 arcsec with standard deviation below 20 arcsec  
  > "If the radius value is between ${800}^{\prime\prime}$ and ${1300}^{\prime\prime}$ and the standard deviation is below ${20}^{\prime\prime}$, then the calculated radius is stored and the next map is submitted to this process." (Radius Determination)
   - assumption: Circle representation of the solar limb within each selected latitude region  
  > "The solar limb coordinates are fit by a circle" (Radius Determination); "First, the radius of each map is determined using only the equatorial latitudes of the solar disk" (Radius Determination)
   - fitting: Successive circle fits, discarding points outside R̄ ±10 arcsec after each fit  
  > "For each fit, the points with center-to-limb distance (obtained from the fitting) outside the interval ($\bar{R}-$${10}^{\prime\prime}$, $\bar{R}+$${10}^{\prime\prime}$) are discarded, and then a new circle fit is performed with the remaining points." (Radius Determination)
   - validation: Compare SST 212-GHz and ALMA 230-GHz radius determinations  
  > "The same method is applied to both telescopes, so that a comparison between the 212 GHz radius of SST and the 230 GHz radius of ALMA could be made." (Radius Determination)
3. **Reject SST outliers and calculate average radii**: Sigma-clip the SST radius distribution after subtracting a running mean; obtain average radii from the remaining values.  
  > "Thus, we applied a sigma clipping on the distribution, subtracting from the distribution a running mean of 300 points, and then discarding values that are outside the $\pm 2.5\sigma$ range." (Radius Determination); "From the remaining values, we obtain the average radii." (Radius Determination)
   - parameter: SST running mean: 300 points; clipping: ±2.5σ  
  > "Thus, we applied a sigma clipping on the distribution, subtracting from the distribution a running mean of 300 points, and then discarding values that are outside the $\pm 2.5\sigma$ range." (Radius Determination)
   - parameter: Used 29 088 SST maps: 24 186 at 212 GHz and 4902 at 405 GHz  
  > "We used 29 088 SST maps (from a total of 36 034 maps) to calculate the radii at 212 GHz (24 186 maps) and 405 GHz (4902 maps)" (RESULTS)
   - parameter: Used 196 ALMA maps: 125 at 100 GHz and 71 at 230 GHz  
  > "196 ALMA maps to calculate the radii at 100 GHz (125 maps) and 230 GHz (71) maps" (RESULTS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive emission altitudes**: Subtract the visible solar radius from mean subterahertz radii to determine emission altitudes above the photosphere.  
  > "Finally, the visible solar radius is subtracted from the the mean subterahertz radii to determine the altitudes in the atmosphere at which the 100, 212, 230, and 405 GHz emissions are predominantly produced." (Radius Determination)
   - assumption: Optical reference radius Rθ = 959.63 arcsec, corresponding to R⊙ = 6.9599×10^8 m  
  > "At optical wavelengths the canonical value of the mean apparent solar radius is $R_\theta=$${959.63}^{\prime\prime}$ corresponding to $R_\odot=6.9599\times 10^8$ m." (INTRODUCTION); "This value has been widely used in the literature, hence we adopt it as the reference value." (INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Assess beam-induced radius uncertainty**: Convolve 2-D antenna beam matrices with a uniform-temperature solar disk.  
  > "To assess the quality of the radius determinations, here we carry out a series of simulations convolving 2-D antenna beam matrix representations with a solar disk with uniform temperature." (Radius Determination)
   - assumption: Uniform solar-disk temperature  
  > "a solar disk with uniform temperature." (Radius Determination)
   - assumption: Different observing times distribute the SST scan-direction uncertainty uniformly across the data set  
  > "Since SST has an altazimuthal mount and maps are obtained at different times of the day, the uncertainty is uniformly distributed in the data set." (Radius Determination)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Simulations show increased radius uncertainty perpendicular to azimuth-elevation scans  
  > "The results show that azimuth-elevation maps increase the uncertainty of radius determination in the direction perpendicular to scans." (Radius Determination)
6. **Compare radii with the literature frequency trend**: Plot measured radii alongside Table 1 literature values and overplot a least-square exponential trend.  
  > "Our results are plotted with those from other authors" (Subterahertz Radii); "To guide the eye, an exponential curve (dashed line) is over plotted to show the trend of the radius as a function of the observing frequency or wavelength" (Subterahertz Radii)
   - fitting: Least-square exponential fit; not a physical model  
  > "Note that the trend curve is just a least-square exponential fit, not a physical model." (Subterahertz Radii)
   - validation: SST and ALMA radii appear consistent within uncertainties with the radius-frequency trend  
  > "SST and ALMA radii seem to agree within uncertainties with the trend" (Subterahertz Radii)
7. **Compare measured radii with the SSC model**: Generate SSC brightness-temperature profiles at the four observing frequencies and compare their radii with measurements.  
  > "Next, we used a 2-D solar atmospheric model developed by (cite: selhorst05) (hereafter referred to as the SSC model) to generate profiles of temperature brightness, $T_B$, at 100, 212, 230, and 405 GHz" (Subterahertz Radii)
   - parameter: 100, 212, 230, and 405 GHz  
  > "to generate profiles of temperature brightness, $T_B$, at 100, 212, 230, and 405 GHz" (Subterahertz Radii)
   - assumption: 2-D solar atmospheric SSC model developed by (cite: selhorst05)  
  > "a 2-D solar atmospheric model developed by (cite: selhorst05) (hereafter referred to as the SSC model)" (Subterahertz Radii)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 212-, 230-, and 405-GHz radii are close to model radii; 100-GHz radii are about 4 arcsec larger  
  > "Our results at 212, 230 and 405 GHz are very close to the radii derived from the model, whereas the 100-GHz radii are about ${4}^{\prime\prime}$ bigger, not agreeing with the model." (Subterahertz Radii)
8. **Construct radius and solar-proxy time series**: Use daily radius averages smoothed over 100 days, solar proxies smoothed over 396 days, and six-month averages.  
  > "To analyze the radius variation over the solar activity cycle, we use the average taken every 6 months from the solar radii and solar proxies." (Observational Time Series); "We use radius daily averages, smoothed over a 100-day period to build time series at radio frequencies." (Observational Time Series); "both smoothed by a 396-day running mean (13 months) to avoid the influence of annual modulations." (Observational Time Series)
   - parameter: Radius smoothing: 100 days; proxy running mean: 396 days (13 months); averages every 6 months  
  > "we use the average taken every 6 months from the solar radii and solar proxies." (Observational Time Series); "We use radius daily averages, smoothed over a 100-day period to build time series at radio frequencies." (Observational Time Series); "both smoothed by a 396-day running mean (13 months)" (Observational Time Series)
   - parameter: Solar activity proxies: 10.7-centimeter solar flux F10.7 and mean photospheric magnetic field intensity |B|  
  > "As solar activity proxies, we used the 10.7-centimeter solar flux, $F_{10.7}$, and the intensity of the mean photospheric magnetic field, $|B|$" (Observational Time Series)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare six-month SST 212-GHz and ALMA 230-GHz equatorial and polar radius series  
  > "We compared the equatorial and polar radius time series at 212 GHz (SST) with the time series of the equatorial and polar radii at 230 GHz (ALMA), with 6-month averages." (Correlation with Solar Activity)
9. **Assess relationships with solar activity**: Calculate linear correlation coefficients between SST radii and solar proxies; qualitatively analyze ALMA radius variations.  
  > "Linear correlation coefficients, $\rho$, between solar radii and solar proxies." (Table 3); "We analyze the equatorial and polar radius behavior over time, with qualitative analysis for 100 and 230 GHz (ALMA) from 2015 to 2018." (INTRODUCTION); "Also, we determine the correlation coefficient between the solar activity proxies and equatorial and polar radii time series at 212 and 405 GHz (SST), from 2007 to 2019." (INTRODUCTION)
   - parameter: SST correlations: 212 and 405 GHz, 2007–2019; ALMA qualitative comparison: 100 and 230 GHz, 2015–2018  
  > "We analyze the equatorial and polar radius behavior over time, with qualitative analysis for 100 and 230 GHz (ALMA) from 2015 to 2018." (INTRODUCTION); "Also, we determine the correlation coefficient between the solar activity proxies and equatorial and polar radii time series at 212 and 405 GHz (SST), from 2007 to 2019." (INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare behavior across frequencies, latitude regions, and both solar proxies  
  > "the calculated correlation coefficients, $\rho$, are organized by frequency, solar latitude regions and solar proxies." (Correlation with Solar Activity); "The radii derived from SST’s maps are found to have both average values and behavior over time very close to ALMA’s" (Correlation with Solar Activity)

## Qualitative findings

- SST and ALMA radii agree within uncertainties with the literature radius-frequency trend.  
  > "SST and ALMA radii seem to agree within uncertainties with the trend" (Subterahertz Radii)
- 212-, 230-, and 405-GHz radii are close to SSC model predictions; 100-GHz radii are about 4 arcsec larger.  
  > "Our results at 212, 230 and 405 GHz are very close to the radii derived from the model, whereas the 100-GHz radii are about ${4}^{\prime\prime}$ bigger, not agreeing with the model." (Subterahertz Radii)
- SST equatorial radii correlate positively and polar radii negatively with |B|, without strong correlations overall.  
  > "The radii time series are not strongly correlated with the solar proxies" (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS); "with the polar radius being anticorrelated and the equatorial radius being correlated with $|B|$." (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)
- SST 212-GHz and ALMA 230-GHz radius averages and temporal behavior are very close.  
  > "We compared the equatorial and polar radius time series at 212 GHz (SST) with the time series of the equatorial and polar radii at 230 GHz (ALMA), with 6-month averages." (Correlation with Solar Activity); "The radii derived from SST’s maps are found to have both average values and behavior over time very close to ALMA’s" (Correlation with Solar Activity)
- At both ALMA frequencies, equatorial radii decrease during 2015–2017 and increase during 2017–2018; polar radii increase during 2015–2018, opposite to |B|.  
  > "At both ALMA frequencies, the equatorial radii decrease from 2015 to 2017 and increase from 2017 to 2018 – roughly close to $|B|$." (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS); "The polar radii time series just increases from 2015 to 2018, which is the opposite of $|B|$." (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Fill the gap in subterahertz solar-radius measurements; use SST data from 2007 onward following receiver upgrades and reflector repair.  
  > "At subterahertz frequencies there is a gap of measurements of the radius and other parameters of the solar atmosphere" (INTRODUCTION); "The SST radiometers were upgraded in 2006 to improve bandwidth, noise and performance, and in 2007 the SST reflector was repaired to provide better antenna efficiency, according to (cite: kaufmann08)." (OBSERVATIONS AND DATA); "Therefore, we used data from 2007 onward." (OBSERVATIONS AND DATA)
- Why this method: Inflection points are less susceptible to beam irregularities and solar brightness-profile variations; 396-day proxy smoothing suppresses annual modulation.  
  > "(cite: menezes21b) showed that the inflection point method is less susceptible to the irregularities of the telescope beams and to the variations of the brightness temperature profiles of the Sun" (Radius Determination); "both smoothed by a 396-day running mean (13 months) to avoid the influence of annual modulations." (Observational Time Series)
- Alternative: Half-power radius measurement considered; inflection point method selected for lower susceptibility to beam and brightness-profile effects.  
  > "Two widely used methods for measuring the radio solar radius are the inflection point method (cite: alissan17) and the the half-power method (cite: costa99; selhorst11; menezes17)." (Radius Determination); "Thus, the inflection point method brings a low bias to the calculation of the solar radius and, therefore, we use this method to determine the solar radius." (Radius Determination)
- How they decided it was enough: Map acceptance requires at least 35% of limb points, radius between 800 and 1300 arcsec, and standard deviation below 20 arcsec.  
  > "This process is repeated until there are at least 35% of the points, usually 6 out of 16 depending on the map and the latitude region – polar or equatorial." (Radius Determination); "If the radius value is between ${800}^{\prime\prime}$ and ${1300}^{\prime\prime}$ and the standard deviation is below ${20}^{\prime\prime}$, then the calculated radius is stored and the next map is submitted to this process." (Radius Determination)
- Limitation (authors): Large scatter remains in SST radius values despite strict selection.  
  > "Even with the strict criteria adopted in the mean radius determination, there is still a large scattering in the distribution of SST radius values." (Radius Determination)
- Limitation (authors): Azimuth-elevation scanning increases radius uncertainty perpendicular to the scan direction.  
  > "The results show that azimuth-elevation maps increase the uncertainty of radius determination in the direction perpendicular to scans." (Radius Determination)
- Limitation (authors): SST radius time series are not strongly correlated with solar proxies.  
  > "The radii time series are not strongly correlated with the solar proxies" (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA observations cover a shorter period; longer observations are needed to establish trends.  
  > "Moreover, we could also observe analogous behavior between SST and ALMA time series, even with the shorter period of ALMA solar observations." (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS); "Longer periods of future observations at these frequencies will reveal the polar and equatorial trends of the solar atmosphere." (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Limitation (authors): Different radius definitions, methods, and instrumental bandwidths complicate comparisons between studies.  
  > "We note, however, that the different works use different definitions and methods for determining the solar radius." (INTRODUCTION); "Measuring the solar radius at subterahertz frequencies is not an easy task as well as to compare the values from different studies, since different instruments observe at different band widths." (DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Limitation (authors): The exponential radius-frequency trend is descriptive rather than a physical model.  
  > "Note that the trend curve is just a least-square exponential fit, not a physical model." (Subterahertz Radii)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The statement that all four radii correlate more strongly with |B| than with F10.7 conflicts with Table 3: only the 212-GHz equatorial coefficient is larger in absolute value for |B|.
