## Analysis recipe

1. **Select, concatenate, continuum-subtract, and image deep ALMACAL calibration cubes**: Select observations in identical frequency setups with total integration > 40 min, inspect individual images to remove artefacts, concatenate per field, run UVCONTSUB, image with 0.5 arcsec taper, remove cubes with bad residuals or r.m.s. > 5 mJy beam^-1, and flag noisy channels.  
  > "The chosen observations were concatenated for each field into a single file and converted to a data cube with standard CASA routines." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection); "To account for that, we ran a continuum fitting routine (UVCONTSUB) on the concatenated files and removed large-scale noise structures." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection); "Therefore, we removed data cubes from the sample for which the r.m.s. was higher than 5 mJy beam$^{-1}$. Additionally, we flagged the channels with noise levels averaged over the field-of-view exceeding 1$\sigma$ of the noise variation in the cube (less than 20 per cent of all data)." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection)
   - software: CASA, UVCONTSUB, CASA ANALYSIS UTILITIES
   - parameter: integration time > 40 min (up to 4 hours)  
  > "For each ALMACAL calibrator field, we chose observations undertaken in the same frequency setup accumulating a total observation time longer than 40 minutes." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection)
   - parameter: spatial resolution = 0.5 arcsec, minimal pixel size = 0.17 arcsec, spectral window width >= 0.5 GHz, channel separation = 15.6 MHz  
  > "we taper all visibility data to the exact spatial resolution of 0.5 arcsec, limiting the minimal pixel size to 0.17 arcsec. In combining the data, we exclude spectral windows narrower than 0.5 GHz." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection)
   - parameter: cube r.m.s. <= 5 mJy beam^-1  
  > "Therefore, we removed data cubes from the sample for which the r.m.s. was higher than 5 mJy beam$^{-1}$." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection)
   - fitting: UVCONTSUB continuum fitting on concatenated visibility files  
  > "To account for that, we ran a continuum fitting routine (UVCONTSUB) on the concatenated files and removed large-scale noise structures." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection)
   - validation: Visual inspection of individual observations before concatenation and removal of 10 per cent of sample affected by imperfect calibrator subtraction  
  > "Before concatenation, we imaged each observation and visually checked the quality to remove any data suffering from artefacts related to imperfect calibration or dynamic range limitations." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection); "Nevertheless, 10 per cent of the sample was still affected by imperfect calibrator subtraction and these affected cubes were removed from the analysis." (ALMACAL-CO sample selection)
2. **Perform untargeted 3D emission-line search in non-primary-beam-corrected cubes**: Run SoFiA source finder using sigma clipping and multi-scale spatial and spectral smoothing kernels, then filter detections by peak S/N, reliability, primary beam attenuation, line width, and visual inspection.  
  > "We searched for the emission lines in the cubes before applying the primary-beam correction (which is required to perform flux measurements). The source finding was performed using the sigma clipping algorithm and spectral smoothing over a set of kernels increasing spatially and spectrally." (Source detection)
   - software: SoFiA
   - parameter: detection threshold = 3 sigma, minimum spatial size = 2 pixels (0.3 arcsec), minimum spectral width = 3 channels  
  > "We limited the search to a 3$\sigma$ threshold and required the minimal size of the detection to be two pixels (0.3 arcsec) and three channels (corresponding to 140 km s$^{-1}$ in Band 3, 43 km s$^{-1}$ in Band 6 and 30 km s$^{-1}$ in Band 8)." (Source detection)
   - parameter: candidate cuts: peak S/N > 4, reliability R > 0.3, primary beam gain >= 0.3, line width <= 1/4 of cube spectral extent  
  > "They should have a peak flux signal-to-noise \> 4 and reliability $R > 0.3$ (Sec. 3.3). Additionally, as we searched the non-primary-beam corrected cube, we removed candidates detected in regions where the primary beam gain is less than 0.3." (Source detection); "We also reject the emission lines of which the width is larger than a quarter of the spectral extent of the cube." (Source detection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of detections and channel maps; K-S test (p = 0.007) comparing radial distribution of retrieved vs injected mock sources; archival VLT/MUSE and HST counterpart confirmation for two sources  
  > "A K-S test resulted in a $p$-value of 0.007, confirming that the spatial distribution of the retrieved sources is consistent with that of the randomly injected ones." (Source detection); "Therefore, we identify the optical counterparts for these two CO detections in field J0334-4008 and confirm the redshifts (Fig. 4)." (Source detection)
3. **Measure completeness and reliability of line detections**: Inject 20 unresolved Gaussian mock sources per cube (repeated 20 times per cube) and recover with SoFiA to measure completeness as a function of S/N and line width; search inverted cubes for negative detections and fit an error function to R(S/N) = 1 - N_neg/N_pos.  
  > "We inject 20 mock sources per cube, and repeat the random mock source injection 20 times in each cube of the survey to populate the mock sources parameter space." (Completeness); "The negative detections are searched for in the inverted cube." (Reliability); "To make the reliability calculation more general, we fit the model error function to the distribution of such calculated reliability with $S/N$:" (Reliability)
   - software: SoFiA
   - parameter: Mock peak flux = 1 to 8 times cube r.m.s. (step 0.1), line width = uniform 10 to 500 km s^-1  
  > "The peak flux was defined to have a range of 1 to 8 times the r.m.s. (in the steps of 0.1) of the noise in the given cube. The detection width was then assigned randomly from a uniform distribution between 10 and 500 km s$^{\rm-1}$ ." (Completeness)
   - parameter: Reliability fit parameters: C = 6.24, sigma = 2.56, a = 0.65  
  > "The model fit to the data (Fig. 8) results in the model parameters: $C = 6.24$, $\sigma = 2.56$ and $a = 0.65$." (Reliability)
   - assumption: Mock sources are spatially unresolved Gaussian line profiles; negative detections represent pure noise peaks  
  > "For each source we assume a gaussian line profile with a random central frequency within the spectral range of a single cube." (Completeness); "All mock sources are spatially unresolved." (Completeness)
   - fitting: Error function model fit R(S/N) = 0.5 * erf((S/N - C)/sigma) + a  
  > "To make the reliability calculation more general, we fit the model error function to the distribution of such calculated reliability with $S/N$:" (Reliability)
   - validation: Repeated completeness injection for marginally resolved mock sources (2, 3, or 4 times the beam size), confirming completeness coefficients agree within errors  
  > "To verify if the same completeness coefficient applies to marginally resolved sources, we repeated the procedure described above for mock sources with spatial sizes 2, 3, or 4 times the beam size. We find that regardless of the size of the source, the completeness coefficients are within the error." (Completeness)
4. **Estimate redshift probability distributions and CO(1-0) luminosities for candidates**: Build 2D histograms of CO flux and redshift (plus corresponding CO(1-0) flux) from ~10 million SHARK SAM galaxies to assign redshift and transition probabilities to each unconfirmed detection; also compute a conservative lowest-J limit using Daddi et al. (2015) SLEDs; adopt MUSE redshifts with 100% probability for the two J0334-4008 sources.  
  > "We generated a catalogue of SHARK simulated galaxies, requiring the brightest CO transition to be brighter than 0.05 mJy km s$^{-1}$. The selection resulted in a catalogue of about 10 million sources covering redshifts from $z = 0 - 6$. For each CO transition, we created a 2D histogram binning the data of CO transition flux and redshift." (Redshift probability function based on SHARK SAMs); "Additionally, to constrain limits on our simulation-based transition classification, we explore a conservative *lowest-$J$* assumption. In this approach, we assign the detection with the lowest possible $J_{\rm CO}$ transition observable at the observed frequency." (Lowest possible J-transition limit); "Those systems have confirmed redshifts, and for further analysis, we adopt that redshift solution with 100 per cent probability." ## Qualitative findings

- The cosmic molecular gas mass density measurements from the ALMACAL-CO pilot survey at 0 < z < 1.5 are consistent with previous untargeted CO surveys and theoretical predictions, and cannot currently rule out a non-evolving molecular gas mass density.  
  > "The results of our pilot survey are consistent with the findings of other untargeted emission-line surveys and the theoretical model predictions and currently cannot rule out a non-evolving molecular gas mass density." (Abstract)
- Two of the six CO emission-line detections (in field J0334-4008) are confirmed by archival VLT/MUSE and HST observations as CO(1-0) emitters at z = 0.133 and z = 0.185.  
  > "archival MUSE data are available for one of our fields named J0334-4008, in which the two ALMACAL detections coincide spatially with two bright galaxies at redshifts $z_1 = 0.133$ and $z_2 = 0.185$, securely placing the line candidates as CO(1$-$0) transition at these redshifts." (Search for optical counterparts)
- None of the candidate redshift classifications coincide with the redshift of the corresponding central ALMA calibrator, indicating the detections are not associated with clustering around the calibrators.  
  > "We found that none of the potential redshift classifications is close to one of the corresponding calibrators (see Fig. 5)." (Redshift probability function based on SHARK SAMs)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMACAL provides deep, untargeted ALMA observations widely distributed over many pointings across the southern sky, making a CO line survey largely free from cosmic variance and complementary in frequency coverage to single-field surveys.  
  > "With this pilot survey, we probe a cosmologically significant volume of $\sim 10^5$ cMpc$^3$, widely distributed over many pointings in the southern sky, making the survey largely insusceptible to the effects of cosmic variance." (Abstract)
- Why this method: Because ALMA calibrator fields lack deep multi-wavelength ancillary data to identify optical counterparts for single CO lines, a statistical redshift probability distribution approach using the SHARK Semi-Analytical Model is used.  
  > "We are also introducing a novel statistical approach, addressing the emission-line classification challenges of a sky-wide survey without corresponding deep optical follow-up observations. We construct the redshift probability functions of each emission-line detection based on the CO flux predictions from the SHARK Semi-Analytical Model of galaxy formation (cite: lagos18; lagos20)." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Using an empirical database of observed CO emission lines in redshifted galaxies to construct redshift probability functions (rejected because current multi-CO line observations are not extensive enough).  
  > "For this probabilistic approach, one could ideally consider using a database of observed CO emission lines in red-shifted galaxies. However, currently available observations of CO emission (especially multi-CO line observations from the same object) are not extensive enough to realize such an approach." (CO SLEDs of SHARK galaxies)
- Alternative: Including line width in the reliability calculation as done in later ASPECS works (rejected due to small sample size).  
  > "In later works, ASPECS updated their method, by including the line width for the reliability calculations of the candidates. However we do not have a large enough sample to adopt this approach, and settled on a simpler method described below." (Reliability)
- Alternative: Identifying candidate emission lines as z ~ 4.5 [CII] emission (rejected because semi-analytical [CII] luminosity functions predict less than one source in the probed volume).  
  > "Regardless of the model used, the predicted number of [CII] sources in ALMACAL-CO pilot is less than one." (Alternative identification of sources as [CII]); "As the [CII] solution is deemed unlikely, we only include the associations with CO lines in our analysis." (Alternative identification of sources as [CII])
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Results are preliminary and exploratory due to the small sample size (6 candidates), preventing reconstruction of full CO(1-0) luminosity functions or constraining rho(M_H2) at z > 1.5.  
  > "We stress that our survey results at the current stage are preliminary, and we cannot reconstruct the full CO(1$-$0) luminosity functions (LFs) with the existing data." (The molecular gas mass density); "Due to the low number of candidates which line classification places them at the redshift $z > 1.5$ we cannot provide constraining measurements of $\rho(M_{\rm H2})$ at this redshift range." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Four of the six detections lack optical/IR counterparts or secondary lines, and because the survey probes the limits of interferometric detection reliability (R >= 0.3), a fraction of the detections may be bright noise peaks.  
  > "We are probing the limits of the detection reliability for sub-mm interferometric data and a fraction of the presented detections are likely bright noise peaks." (Discussion and Conclusions); "At this stage of the project, we are unable to assess the real reliability of these detections further, due to the lack of secondary lines and optical counterparts." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Calibrator data have inhomogeneous noise properties and uneven frequency coverage, and observations at z < 1 probe small cosmological volumes.  
  > "As calibrator data are not as homogeneous as the science program observations, the noise properties and uneven frequency coverage make the survey more challenging in some aspects. Nevertheless, in the subset of the deepest observations, we detected six candidates making a case for an ALMACAL-wide search, however, the untargeted observations at redshift $z<1$ suffer because only small cosmological volumes are probed." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Calibrator fields could be minimally biased towards over-densities around the central blazar, especially for calibrators with unknown redshifts.  
  > "Nevertheless, every galaxy survey conducted on the ALMACAL data may be minimally biased towards over-densities, especially for sources/calibrators with unknown redshift." (The ALMACAL survey)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reliability threshold R > 0.3 is substantially lower than the 60% threshold typically adopted in untargeted surveys like ASPECS, and two of the four unconfirmed candidates have reliabilities of only 0.32 and 0.38.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual inspection was used to discard roughly half of the initial detections based on channel map appearance, which introduces a subjective human step that is not accounted for in the automated mock-source completeness or inverted-cube reliability metrics.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Field J0334-4008, the two confirmed CO(1-0) detections at z = 0.133 and z = 0.185 are separated by ~52 Mpc along the line of sight, and the text does not compare this to the calibrator redshift (or if one is associated with a foreground group), which could affect the low-z volume density bin given the very small CO(1-0) volume (958 cMpc^3).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SHARK redshift probability calculator operates on single objects without incorporating the survey's frequency coverage or volume sensitivity per transition as a prior.
