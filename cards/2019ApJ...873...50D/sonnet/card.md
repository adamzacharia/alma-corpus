## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and line data and extract spectra**: CASA CLEAN natural weighting; continuum subtraction; spectra from 1''-2.5'' apertures; Gaussian fits for line flux/FWHM  
  > "We extract spectra using $1''$ to $2.5''$ radius apertures and 38 MHz channel width for all sources except SPT0529-54." (2.1 ALMA Cycle 2)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 38 MHz channels (76 MHz for SPT0529-54)  
  > "For this source, we use 76 MHz channels because the data have a much lower signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)." (2.1 ALMA Cycle 2)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits; for SPT2134-50 channel-by-channel integration  
  > "To estimate the line properties, we fit Gaussian functions to the spectra, except for SPT2134-50" (2.1 ALMA Cycle 2)
   - validation: Uncertainties from 1000 noise realizations  
  > "repeat the procedure for getting the flux and FWHM 1000 times" (2.1 ALMA Cycle 2)
2. **Visibility-based lens modeling of continuum and CO channels**: SIE lens(es) plus Sérsic sources fit to visibilities; continuum and channels fit simultaneously with shared lens parameters; also single-channel models  
  > "we fit the continuum and the CO line channels simultaneously, i.e. fitting multiple source components with one shared set of lens parameters." (3 Lens modeling)
   - software: visilens, emcee
   - parameter: time averaging up to 10 min with uv separation <15 m; velocity bins 75-250 km/s  
  > "For line data we divide the data into 75–250 km s$^{-1}$ velocity bins, depending on the flux distribution and SNR of the line." (3 Lens modeling)
   - parameter: 1000 chains, 1000 burn-in, 1000 extra steps  
  > "Generally, we run 1000 chains with 1000 burn-in steps and 1000 extra chain steps." (3 Lens modeling)
   - assumption: Flat Planck LCDM: h=0.677, Om=0.308, OL=0.691  
  > "we assume a flat Planck $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, where $h = 0.677$, $\Omega_m = 0.308$, and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.691$" (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC; median and 1 sigma intervals  
  > "We quote the median value of each parameter of the sample as the estimated value and the $1\sigma$ interval as the uncertainty." (3 Lens modeling)
   - validation: Chain convergence, residual images, DIC model selection  
  > "we use the Deviance Information Criterion (cite: Spiegelhalter02) for model selection" (3 Lens modeling)
3. **Compare CO and dust sizes and magnifications**: Circularized half-light size sqrt(a_S b_S) from MCMC chains for CO single channels (500-800 km/s) vs continuum; magnification comparisons across channels, continuum and transitions  
  > "We calculate the circularized size and its uncertainty from the MCMC chains as $\sqrt{a_S b_S}$" (4.1 Source Sizes)
   - software: visilens
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare mu*L'_intrinsic to apparent L' (Figure 3, Figure 8)  
  > "We quantify the ability of the lens models to recover the line fluxes by comparing the apparent CO line luminosities ($L^\prime_\mathrm{apparent}$) to the product of the magnification and intrinsic line luminosities" (4 Analysis)
4. **LVG radiative transfer modeling with apparent vs intrinsic line ratios**: RADEX with MCMC over N_CO/dv, T_kin, n_H2; three scenarios (all lines apparent, two lines intrinsic, two lines apparent)  
  > "We model under three scenarios." (4.3 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - software: RADEX, corner
   - parameter: flat priors: N_CO/dv 1e14-1e19 cm^-2 (km/s)^-1; T_kin T_CMB-300 K; n_H2 10-1e6 cm^-3; tau<100  
  > "we apply a prior that the line optical depth $\tau$ be less than 100" (4.3 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - assumption: background temperature T_CMB = 2.73(1+z) K  
  > "set the background radiation temperature to the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature $T_\mathrm{CMB}=2.73(1+z)$ K" (4.3 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - fitting: MCMC; likelihood of observed line ratios (sampler not named)  
  > "With the corresponding line ratios from the observations, we can compute the likelihood that the given set of parameters reproduce the data." (4.3 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO emitting region is larger than dust continuum in three of four modeled galaxies even for high-J CO  
  > "We find, for three of the four galaxies, a trend that the size of the CO-emitting region appears larger than the dust continuum" (4.1 Source Sizes)
- SPT0532-50 and SPT2147-50 show gradients consistent with ordered rotation; SPT0346-52 likely a merger  
  > "Two of them (SPT0532-50 and SPT2147-50) show gradients consistent with ordered rotation." (5 Conclusions)
- No acceptable lens model for SPT2134-50 with a simple SIE  
  > "For SPT2134-50, we were unable to find an acceptable fit to the data." (4 Analysis)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Visibility-plane modeling avoids correlated image noise and accounts for residual calibration errors  
  > "With this technique we can better understand the measurement and its noise, avoiding the correlated noise inherent to interferometric images" (3 Lens modeling)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Parameterized Sérsic modeling cannot independently constrain faint emission at large radii  
  > "the faint emission at large radii is not independently constrained because of our parameterized modeling" (4.1 Source Sizes)
- Limitation (authors): Simple Sérsic profiles unlikely to fully characterize source structure  
  > "While simple Sérsic profiles are unlikely to fully characterise the source structure" (3 Lens modeling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only five sources, LVG study on two
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO(8-7) flux mismatch with another dataset handled with ad hoc 25% error
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Size comparisons depend on Sérsic parametrization and differ in S/N between dust and CO
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Key comparison datasets (CO(2-1) lens models) are from unpublished work
