## Qualitative findings

- 7 out of 12 SMGs in the complete WHDF sample are best identified as obscured quasars, 3/12 show no preference between AGN and star-forming templates, 1/12 is a z=0.046 spiral galaxy, and 1/12 has an unidentified counterpart.  
  > "This optical/NIR comparison combined with Spitzer MIR colours and faint Chandra X-ray detections shows that 7/12 SMGs are best fitted with an obscured quasar model, a further 3/12 show no preference between AGN and star-forming templates, leaving only a $z=0.046$ spiral galaxy and one unidentified source." (Abstract)
- Two previously known X-ray absorbed quasars (LAB-05 and LAB-11) are confirmed by SED fitting to be dust reddened, but at an A_V level an order of magnitude lower than implied by their X-ray neutral hydrogen column assuming Galactic gas-to-dust ratios.  
  > "By comparing their spectral energy distributions (SEDs) with unabsorbed quasars in the same field, we confirm that they are dust reddened although at a level significantly lower than implied by their X-ray absorption." (Abstract); "We note that the dust absorption implied for the two quasars with well-measured X-ray absorption is an order of magnitude higher based on their HI column, assuming Galactic gas-dust ratios than indicated by SED fitting." (Conclusions)
- 10 out of 12 sub-mm sources satisfy the Spitzer [3.6]-[4.5] > 0.4 mag quasar colour selection criterion, and their 3.6-4.5 um MIR brightness is on average ~10x fainter than X-ray selected broad-line quasars in the same field.  
  > "10 out of 12 sub-mm sources show MIR colours that satisfy the $[3.6]-[4.5]>0.4$ quasar selection criteria of (cite: Stern12), confirming the result of (cite: Dud20)." (Conclusions); "We find that the MIR brightness of sub-mm sources are typically a factor of $\approx10$ below those of X-ray quasars although the bolometric luminosities of both populations are similar with the increased sub-mm emission compensating for decreased X-ray and optical emission." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The WHDF has high-resolution (0.095 arcsec), high-S/N ALMA 870 um imaging for accurate counterpart identification and 75 ksec Chandra X-ray data with low background (ideal for single-photon AGN detections above 10^42 erg/s), along with deep UBRIZHK, HST i, Spitzer SPIES, Herschel HeLMS, and VLA data.  
  > "Nevertheless, for our current purpose, the availability of high resolution $0.''09$, high S/N FIR data from ALMA is ideal for checking for counterparts and looking for low surface brightness features such as bars and spiral arms." (Data); "Nevertheless, in searching for AGN X-ray signatures from typical bright SMGs we shall see that our Chandra combination of exposure time, resolution and low background are ideal." (Data)
- Why this method: Restricted the comparison to two simple AGN templates and two simple star-forming galaxy templates with the same dust reddening model to keep the number of free parameters low and make a fair comparison, using Sawicki (2012) maximum likelihood fitting to properly incorporate non-detection upper limits.  
  > "Since our intent here is to test if two, simple, reddened AGN templates can fit our optical/MIR data, we shall also restrict ourselves to using two basic star-forming galaxy templates with the same, simple, dust reddening model to act as reference points." (SED Fitting Results); "Here we broadly follow the method set out by (cite: Sawicki12) which deals with upper limits as well as detected fluxes, coded in Fortran." (SED fitting method)
- Alternative: Multi-component star-forming galaxy SED models (such as MAGPHYS used by Dudzeviciute et al. 2020 / da Cunha et al. 2015) were not used because with enough model parameters they can fit almost any SMG SED, whereas the goal here was a fair, low-parameter comparison against simple AGN templates.  
  > "In making a comparison between AGN and star-forming galaxy SMG fits, we first acknowledge that (cite: Dud20) has shown that, with enough assumed model components, it is certainly possible to get good $\chi^2$ fits to their $\approx700$ SMGs with their star-forming galaxy templates." (SED Fitting Results); "However, our only aim is to see how close the AGN models can get to the quality of fit of the star-forming models and this justifies our approach of minimising the number of model parameters for both AGN and SFG templates." (SED Fitting Results)
- Alternative: Increasing empirical flux errors to reduce high reduced chi^2 values (such as on LAB-05 u-band) was rejected.  
  > "We decided against any increase of the empirical error to provide a smaller $\chi^2$ so we shall bear in mind that the AGN models are not perfect fits to this known QSO." (Sub-mm source SED fitting)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing newly fitted AGN templates against a fixed median star-forming SMG template (AS2UDS, which already has intrinsic dust attenuation baked in) with only positive additional A_V allowed is asymmetric and naturally penalizes the star-forming fits for bluer or lower-z sources.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several new Chandra X-ray detections (LAB-01, LAB-03, LAB-04) rely on 1-2 photon counts at a formal 90% confidence threshold (S/N > 1.43), which could include star-forming X-ray binaries or Poisson background fluctuations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spitzer [3.6]-[4.5] > 0.4 colour selection is known to be contaminated by z > 2 star-forming galaxies and cannot uniquely separate AGN from starbursts without mid-IR spectroscopy or 8-24 um photometry.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Composite AGN + star-forming galaxy SED models were not fitted; an optical/MIR AGN SED does not prove that the 870 um sub-mm emission is powered by the AGN rather than host galaxy star formation.
