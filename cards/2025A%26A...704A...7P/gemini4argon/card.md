## Qualitative findings

- G2/DSO, D9, and X7 are rediscovered at their predicted astrometric positions on Keplerian orbits with <2% average deviation from 2005-2019 orbital elements.  
  > "All the sources inspected in this work are rediscovered on their predicted astrometric positions, underlying the robustness of our monitoring efforts of the Galactic center." (abstract); "In comparison to the Keplerian elements that covered a data baseline until 2019, our recent measurements show accuracy of less than 2\% with respect to the original orbital solutions;" (Conclusion)
- G2/DSO shows a constant Br-gamma luminosity and no signatures of an inspiralling trajectory or destruction.  
  > "Furthermore, we find no signatures of a Br\gamma luminosity variability for G2/DSO." (abstract); "For G2/DSO, we do not find any signs of an inspiralling trajectory leading to the destruction of the source;" (Conclusion)
- D9 remains intact as an unmerged binary system and continues to exhibit its periodic radial velocity modulation; D2 is also rediscovered ~25 mas away and will superpose with D9 between 2025 and 2027.  
  > "Based on the Keplerian approximations presented in (cite: Peissker2020b) and (cite: Peissker2024a), we successfully rediscover D2 on its orbit. It is expected that both sources will be superposed between 2025 and 2027." (D9); "The D9 binary system is still intact and has not merged;" (Conclusion)
- X3 exhibits the same 207 km/s separated double-peaked Br-gamma outflow signature in 2023 as in 2014, while its system centroid velocity accelerated by ~100 km/s.  
  > "X3, located in the mini cavity close to the evaporating cluster IRS 13, does show the same double peak Br\gamma line in 2023 as observed in 2014;" (Conclusion); "The centroid of the Br\gamma doublet associated with X3 underwent an acceleration by about 100 km/s;" (Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ERIS-SPIFFIER upgrades the decommissioned SINFONI instrument with improved optics, enhanced spectral resolution, and a 12.5 mas spatial scale, closing the 2019-2024 monitoring gap with much shorter integration times.  
  > "The successor to the two instruments is called ERIS and offers upgraded optics and improved properties, including an enhanced spectral resolution." (abstract); "Using the recently obtained ERIS data, we can close the gap in the data baseline between 2019 and 2024 to study peculiar objects in the S cluster." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Direct 5-pixel aperture extraction without local or distant background subtraction paired with Asymmetric Least Squares continuum slope removal avoids artifacts from extreme stellar crowding, and Lucy-Richardson deconvolution is omitted.  
  > "In contrast to previous studies (cite: Peissker2019; Peissker2020b; peissker2021c), we are not applying the Lucy-Richardson algorithm to the data." (Data); "For the S/N ratio indicated in Table 3, we used neither of the methods but selected directly the dusty objects without a local or distant background subtraction." (Spectra of the dusty objects)
- Alternative: Coreless gas cloud / tidally stretched gas streamer with drag-induced inspiral for G2/DSO and X7  
  > "Although the scenario of a gas cloud that might partially be accreted by Sgr A* is not supported by observations" (G2/DSO); "Considering the constant continuum magnitude, Br\gamma luminosity, and astrometric position, we can not confirm signatures of an in-spiral motion of G2/DSO as suggested by (cite: Gillessen2019; Gillessen2025)." (The origin of G2/DSO); "This suggests that X7 is still following our proposed orbital trajectory and does not show any tidally stretched emission along its orbit" (X7 – a tidally stretched gas streamer?)
- Alternative: Lucy-Richardson deconvolution  
  > "In contrast to previous studies (cite: Peissker2019; Peissker2020b; peissker2021c), we are not applying the Lucy-Richardson algorithm to the data." (Data)
- Alternative: Local annular or distant background subtraction for spectral extraction  
  > "For the S/N ratio indicated in Table 3, we used neither of the methods but selected directly the dusty objects without a local or distant background subtraction." (Spectra of the dusty objects)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Limited IFU field of view covers only about 33% of X7's elongated structure, introducing a systematic offset of the fitted tip position toward Sgr A*.  
  > "Considering a projected length of the dust emission of X7 of about 0.3 arcsec in 2021 as displayed in (cite: Ciurlo2023), our measurements of the Doppler-shifted Br\gamma line emission only covers about 33\% due to the limited FOV" (X7); "because the FOV does not cover the complete source, our projected data points suffer from a systematic offset towards Sgr A*." (X7 – a tidally stretched gas streamer?)
- Limitation (authors): Low orbital coverage (5-15% for G2/DSO and D9) limits improvement in absolute orbital uncertainties and creates a ~0.6 sigma difference relative to observable-biased priors.  
  > "However, the absolute uncertainties of the Keplerian elements are only marginally affected by the longer data baseline which is due to the low orbital coverage of the observations (cite: ONeil2019; Ali2020)." (Keplerian orbits); "The dusty sources G2/DSO and D9 are in the last group with an orbital coverage of 5-15\%, resulting in a difference of \approx 0.6\sigma between the observable-biased prior and the uniform priors used in this work" (Incomplete orbits)
- Limitation (authors): OH sky line emission (e.g., at 2.158 um) and high stellar crowding in the S cluster impact extracted spectra and Gaussian fit uncertainties.  
  > "For the spectrum of G2/DSO shown in Fig. 13, the Gaussian may be impacted by the OH line emission at 2.158\mu m (cite: Rousselot2000), resulting in a fit uncertainty of 8.9 km/s." (Spectra of the dusty objects); "We want to stress that close-by stellar sources in the crowded S cluster do have a significant impact on the quality of the extracted spectrum." (Spectra of the dusty objects)
- Limitation (authors): The putative inverse P Cygni absorption feature in X3 at 2.1667 um is weak and close to noisy rest-wavelength Br-gamma, so an artificial origin cannot be excluded.  
  > "Due to the noise, especially close to the Br\gamma rest wavelength at 2.1661\mu m and the weakness of this putative spectral feature, an artificial origin cannot be excluded." (X3)
- Limitation (authors): For X3, no 12.5 mas plate-scale ERIS observations were available, restricting the analysis to the 100 mas setting.  
  > "Due to the lack of observations with the highest plate-scale, we use the 100 mas setting of the ERIS observation executed in 2023." (X3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data (2016.1.00870.S) are only used as a background layer in the Figure 1 finding chart with no processing details or quantitative analysis.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Keplerian orbit updates rely on combining 2023-2024 ERIS points with 2005-2019 SINFONI/NACO astrometric and radial velocity series from earlier papers that are not tabulated in full in this article.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 5 mas offset between G2/DSO's measured position and its Keplerian prediction is comparable to the 5-10 mas gas-drag deviation predicted by competing models, though sub-pixel at the 12.5 mas scale.
