## Qualitative findings

- At similar density, low-MLR S-star envelope sizes lie between larger C-star and smaller M-star envelopes.  
  > "The new ACA observations presented in this paper show that, for a similar density, the sizes of the CO CSEs of low-MLR S-type stars fall between the sizes of C-CSEs, which are larger, and M-CSEs which are smaller." (Discussion and summary)
- Low-MLR sizes show no density dependence; high-MLR sizes correlate with density, more steeply for S-stars.  
  > "As for the C- and M-stars in (cite: deathstar), the CSE size distribution of the low-MLR stars presented in this paper shows no density dependence, irrespective of their variability types." (Discussion and summary); "A correlation between size and density is nevertheless observed for high-MLR stars." (Discussion and summary); "The density dependence of the size is steeper for the S-stars compared to the other chemical types at high MLR." (Discussion and summary)
- Low-density S-CSEs are smaller than expected; high-density S-CSEs appear much larger than predicted CO(2–1) sizes.  
  > "The measured sizes of the low-density S-CSEs are smaller than expected based on the photodissociation results of (cite: Saberi2019), whereas the high-density CSEs of S-stars appear to be much larger than the predicted CO(2–1) sizes." (Discussion and summary)
- Most southern S-star envelopes are consistent with a spherical, smooth outflow.  
  > "Our results show that the CO CSEs of most of the southern S-stars in the DEATHSTAR sample are consistent with a spherically symmetric and smooth outflow." (Discussion and summary)
- RZ Sgr and FU Mon show extended spiral structures in both CO transitions.  
  > "Thanks to the high sensitivity offered by the ACA, unusual extended spiral structures are observed in the images of RZ$\,$Sgr and FU$\,$Mon, observable in both the 2–1 and 3–2 lines" (Features indicative of anisotropy)
- AM Cen and TT Cen likely have intrinsically large CSEs, conditional on the adopted distances being lower limits.  
  > "Assuming that the Gaia distances validate that the distances adopted in our calculations are lower limits, we can conclude that the large measured sizes of both stars are likely to be lower limits" (AM$\,$Cen and TT$\,$Cen); "which would imply that AM Cen and TT Cen have intrinsically large CSEs." (AM$\,$Cen and TT$\,$Cen)
- Detected 13CO, CS, SiO, and 29SiO in S-stars; no further molecular-emission analysis.  
  > "We report the detection of several molecules other than $^{12}$CO in our sample of S-type stars, namely $^{13}$CO, CS, SiO, and $^{29}$SiO." (Discussion and summary); "No further analysis of the emission from molecules other than $^{12}$CO has been attempted." (Discussion and summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO(2–1) probes cooler outer gas and approximates the full CO-emitting extent; TP recovers extended flux.

  > "This method takes away any bias and artefacts introduced by processes such as gridding and weighting in deconvolved images, and permits the estimation of the size of small sources." (Fitting the emission distribution); "We noticed that cleaning using the *Högbom* algorithm led to negative bowls around the sources." (Observation with total power and data combination); "Therefore, we run $tclean$ with the $multiscale$ algorithm and $100\,000$ iterations for all four sources." (Observation with total power and data combination)
