## Qualitative findings

- Continuum emission has an elongated east-west centre and asymmetric northeast/southwest extensions forming an S-shaped morphology.  
  > "It displays a bright elongated central structure (east-west) with two nearly perpendicular and opposite asymmetric extensions (towards the northeast and the southwest) forming an S-shaped morphology, consistent with previous studies." (Continuum emission)
- Polarised emission is marginally detected and barely resolved.  
  > "The polarised emission is marginally detected (with a peak polarisation fraction of 1.4%) and is barely resolved in the object, resulting in an imprecise estimate of the polarisation vector distribution." (Conclusions)
- Electric vectors may align with the equatorial plane and the magnetic field may align with the bipolar outflows.  
  > "Nevertheless, one possibility is that the electric vectors are indeed aligned along the equatorial plane; therefore, the magnetic field might be concordant with the bipolar outflows." (Conclusions)
- Best-fit SED models include silicates and amorphous carbon.  
  > "It is to be noted that our best-fit models include a mixture of silicates and amorphous carbon (cite: Rouleau1991) for an H$_2$O/OH maser emission object." (DUSTY modelling)
- The additional blackbody may represent a very cool binary companion or hot dust.  
  > "which suggests the possible presence of either a (very) cool binary companion or hot dust." (DUSTY modelling)

- Why this data: ALMA dust polarisation can test whether the field is organised and toroidal or polar.

- Why this method: Use DUSTY radiative transfer modelling to test whether optical depth causes low polarisation.  
  > "In order to examine if the optical depth is indeed the cause of low polarisation in K 3-35, we computed a radiative transfer model using the 1-D radiative transfer code DUSTY (cite: dusty)" (DUSTY modelling)
- Alternative: High optical depth is unlikely to cause the low polarisation at the observed wavelength.  
  > "Thus, the low detected polarisation level is unlikely to be due to the high optical depth of the source." (DUSTY modelling)
- Alternative: Self-scattering is an alternative to magnetic-field-linked dust polarisation; the interpretation assumes the latter.  
  > "In addition, it should also be pointed out that this interpretation assumes that dust polarisation is principally linked to the presence of magnetic fields and not to self-scattering (cite: Kataoka2015)." (The lack of polarisation in K3-35)
- Alternative: Depolarisation through collisions could impede polarisation detection.  
  > "Finally, the intricate physical conditions within the source, such as depolarisation processes due to collisions for example (which ultimately affects the grain size and the alignment) could impede polarisation detection." (The lack of polarisation in K3-35)
- Alternative: The 1800 K blackbody may be a very cool binary companion or hot dust.
