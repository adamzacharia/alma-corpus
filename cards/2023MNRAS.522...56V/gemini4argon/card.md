## Analysis recipe

1. **Time-sliced and concatenated continuum imaging with spatial filtering**: Image pipeline-calibrated visibilities in Stokes I (and Stokes Q for ORBS) using multi-frequency synthesis (mfs) in CASA TCLEAN with a uv-range cut (>138 klambda) to filter extended emission, creating a concatenated image, 8 epoch images (1.16 h), 24 sub-epoch images (20 min), 17 images (4 min), and 265 high-cadence images (8 s).  
  > "The calibrated dataset was imaged with the TCLEAN task in CASA. We used the Stokes plane $I$ and spectral definition mode ‘mfs’ (Multi-Frequency Synthesis)." (Observations and Data Reduction); "we applied spatial filtering of the visibility data using baselines of the ($u$, $\nu$) range longer than 138 k$\lambda$ ($\sim$414 km)" (Observations and Data Reduction); "All these images were used to generate light curves (LCs) at 1-h and 20-min time resolution, leading to 8 and 24 individual images, respectively." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN
   - parameter: deconvolver = Hogbom, weighting = Briggs, robust = 0.5  
  > "The Hogbom deconvolution algorithm and a Briggs weighting method with a robustness parameter of 0.5 were used." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: image size = 2048 x 2048 pixels, pixel size = 0.05 arcsec, pblim = 0.2  
  > "The image size for all the observations is $2048\times2048$ pixels with a pixel size of 0.05 arcseconds" (Observations and Data Reduction); "Photometry was extracted from images that were corrected for the primary beam (PB) response following a PB gain level cut-off of 20 per cent (pblim$=0.2$)" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: uvrange > 138 klambda (~414 km)  
  > "we applied spatial filtering of the visibility data using baselines of the ($u$, $\nu$) range longer than 138 k$\lambda$ ($\sim$414 km)" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Extended structures larger than ~1.5 arcsec are thermal dust/nebular emission that do not vary on timescales of minutes to days.  
  > "while at the same time we are not expecting variability from extended structure (associated with thermal dust emission) on the short timescales studied here of minutes to days." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spatial filtering (>138 klambda) increased the number of detected compact sources by 39 per cent and enabled Gaussian fitting of sources embedded in complex emission (such as ORBS).  
  > "We noted a significant improvement for source detection by using the additional spatial filtering of the visibility data allowing a 39 per cent increase in detected sources" (Source Detection)
2. **Simulate ALMA observations to quantify systematic artificial variability**: Generate 100 2D Gaussian constant artificial source models combined with 3 real background cutouts from the concatenated image (300 sky models), simulate visibilities across 7 hour angles (1 h each) using Simobserve, image with Simanalyze, extract fluxes with iterative IMFIT, and compute the variability factor (VF = max/min peak flux density).  
  > "We made use of the Simobserve task in CASA to first simulate the visibilities consisting of 7 observations of 1 h integration time each all of them at different hour angles ranging from $-5$ h to $+1$ h" (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "This resulted in 300 input sky models whose visibilities were simulated for 7 different hour angles (HAs) on the sky and subsequently imaged with Simanalyze with a pixel size of 0.05 arcsec and an image size of 100 pixels per side." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "The flux measurements were then used to analyze the LCs of each source in order to assess the maximum change in peak flux density throughout the 7 observations, hereafter variability factor (VF), and defined as the ratio between the maximum and minimum peak flux density in the LCs." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
   - software: CASA, Simobserve, Simanalyze, IMFIT
   - parameter: Source FWHM = 0.1 to 0.9 arcsec; peak flux density = 5 to 100 mJy beam^-1  
  > "The artificial sources were 2D Gaussian models with a range of sizes for both major and minor axis equivalent to FWHM between 0.1 and 0.9 arcsec" (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "The amplitude of these model sources were set to cover a range of peak flux density between 5 and 100 mJy beam$^{-1}$." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
   - parameter: Hour angles = -5 h to +1 h (7 observations of 1 h each); pixel size = 0.05 arcsec; image size = 100 x 100 pixels  
  > "consisting of 7 observations of 1 h integration time each all of them at different hour angles ranging from $-5$ h to $+1$ h" (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "subsequently imaged with Simanalyze with a pixel size of 0.05 arcsec and an image size of 100 pixels per side." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
   - assumption: Artificial sources have constant intrinsic flux density; measured flux variations arise from the changing synthesized beam interacting with source structure and complex background.  
  > "we performed an analysis of simulated ALMA observations for a set of artificial, constant sources." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
   - fitting: Iterative 2D Gaussian fitting with CASA IMFIT varying fitting area and position offsets  
  > "we obtained flux information using a Gaussian fitting algorithm based on the IMFIT task in CASA that iterates over each input source using different values for fitting area around the source and different offsets from the input position to avoid nearby contamination." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
   - validation: Restricted HA range matching actual observations (-3h20m < HA < +1h) yields maximum systematic VF ~ 1.6; adopted conservative systematic threshold of VF = 2.  
  > "If we limit these simulations to $-$3h20m$<$HA$<+1$h, we then obtain a maximum systematic variability of VF$\sim$1.6." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "a reasonable cut-off for systematic variability that can affect at least a sample of sources given certain conditions (e.g., source size in combination with a complex background) is VF$=$2." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
3. **Source detection, photometry, and multiwavelength catalog cross-matching**: Visually inspect the concatenated continuum map and automatically search at positions of known X-ray (COUP), cm (VLA), and mm (CARMA, ALMA) sources; extract peak flux densities and deconvolved sizes using iterative Gaussian fitting (IMFIT) with a 5-sigma detection threshold; cross-match against X-ray, cm, mm, and optical/IR disk catalogs.  
  > "We thus searched for compact sources by visual inspection of the concatenated image followed by an automatic search only on the position of known X-ray sources in the Chandra Orion Ultra-deep Project (COUP; (cite: get05b)), known sources detected at cm-wavelengths with the VLA reported by (cite: for16) and (cite: vargas2021), and millimeter sources reported in (cite: friedel2011), (cite: eisner2016), and (cite: otter2021)." (Source Detection); "Due to the presence of noise peaks with S/N levels in the range of $\sim3-4$, we have enforced a detection threshold of 5$\sigma$, leading to a total of 133 sources." (Source Detection)
   - software: CASA, IMFIT
   - parameter: Detection threshold = 5 sigma; catalog cross-match search radius = 0.5 arcsec (extended to 1.0 arcsec for additional counterparts/disks)  
  > "we have enforced a detection threshold of 5$\sigma$, leading to a total of 133 sources." (Source Detection); "Only 4 additional nearby VLA sources (angular separations $\leq$0.8 arcsec) can be included if extending the search radius from 0.5 arcsec to 1 arcsec" (Source Detection)
   - assumption: Distance to the ONC is ~400 pc.  
  > "equivalent to physical lengths of 96$-$144 au at the distance of the ONC (assumed to be $\sim400$ pc; (cite: grossschedl2018; kuhn2019))." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting using CASA IMFIT  
  > "We obtained flux information following the source extraction method described in section [3](#sec:alma_sim) based on a Gaussian fitting algorithm using the IMFIT task in CASA." (Source Detection)
   - validation: Compared 3-mm fluxes with Otter et al. (2021) for 47 common sources within the HPBW primary beam (57% compatible within 3 sigma).  
  > "we can compare their flux measurements for those within the HPBW primary beam (47 sources). 57 per cent of them have flux measurements compatible within 3$\sigma$ uncertainty" (Variability distribution in Orion-KL)
4. **Light curve extraction, variability factor (VF) analysis, and physical flare characterization**: Measure peak flux densities of the 133 catalog sources across the 1-h, 20-min, 4-min, and 8-s time-sliced images using IMFIT (adopting 3x local rms noise as upper limit when S/N < 5); compute VF = S_max / S_min; derive emitting region size upper limit from light travel time of the flare rise and estimate brightness temperature and radio luminosity.

## Qualitative findings

- A strong millimeter flare was discovered from the known radio-flaring YSO ORBS (Source 74), showing an order of magnitude flux increase within ~4 minutes, a total duration of ~10 minutes, and a double-peaked light curve at 8-s time resolution.  
  > "Further analysis of this flare at high-time resolution of 8-s cadence allowed us to constrain the development of this strong event that had a duration of $\sim$10 min with more than an order of magnitude change in peak flux density in $\sim$4 min." (Summary and Conclusions); "At this high-time resolution we are also able to resolve a lightcurve substructure at the peak of the event where a double peak is seen at $\sim$4.9 and $\sim$5.8 mJy beam$^{-1}$ separated by 3 min." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Time-sliced imaging of a complex region with a changing synthesized beam introduces artificial variability of up to VF ~ 2 for constant sources.  
  > "Using simulated observations, we conclude that these systematic effects could produce artificial variability of up to a factor of VF$\sim$2." (Summary and Conclusions)
- At least 3 out of 133 sources (ORBS, Source 86, and Source 87) exhibit extreme millimeter variability (VF >= 10) within ~1276 cumulative YSO observing hours, which can bias millimeter dust mass estimates by up to an order of magnitude.  
  > "our observations alone can already demonstrate that within a cumulative observing time of $\sim1276$ h (8 epochs of $\sim1.2$ h each with 133 sources) we find evidence of at least three out of 133 sources whose millimeter variability could lead to incorrect disk mass estimates with up to an order of magnitude difference in the estimated dust mass." (Strong Flaring source ORBS)
- Sources with averaged peak flux densities above ~2.0 mJy beam^-1 (such as Source BN and Source I) are essentially constant on short timescales and dominated by thermal dust or free-free emission.  
  > "Sources with averaged peak flux density above $\sim$2.0 mJy beam$^{-1}$ are essentially constant and are most likely dominated by dust millimeter emission." (Variability distribution in Orion-KL)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 3 provides high sensitivity even on very short timescales and C43-5 long baselines mitigate extended nebular emission, while the ONC BN/KL core provides a large sample of YSOs in a single pointing.  
  > "Given the lack of a statistical sample of strong and short-lived millimeter flares we started a first systematic search for such events in YSOs using ALMA, targeting the BN/KL region close to the core of the ONC for a large sample of sources, and observing on short timescales of minutes to days." (Introduction); "where the longest baselines are particularly relevant to mitigate the extended emission in the Orion Nebula." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Why this method: Time-sliced imaging with a >138 klambda uv cut followed by iterative Gaussian fitting (IMFIT) was required because the crowded field and complex multi-scale extended emission prevent direct visibility fitting, and visual plus catalog-guided detection avoids spurious noise artifacts.  
  > "In order to further reduce the impact of extended emission on the point-like source extraction process, we applied spatial filtering of the visibility data using baselines of the ($u$, $\nu$) range longer than 138 k$\lambda$" (Observations and Data Reduction); "An assessment of lower variability levels in this observations involves dealing with technical difficulties due to the ubiquitous complex multi angular scale emission in the ONC on top of its source density which necessarily requires time-sliced imaging of the whole field" (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "automated source extraction methods typically require significant manual intervention to deal with artifacts. We thus searched for compact sources by visual inspection of the concatenated image followed by an automatic search only on the position of known X-ray sources" (Source Detection)
- Alternative: Direct fitting of a point source model to the visibilities (rejected because the ONC field contains multiple resolved and unresolved sources on top of a complex, variable background).  
  > "The opposite scenario would be an isolated and unresolved source on top of a flat background where instead of time-sliced imaging a direct fit of a point source model to the visibilities would be a suitable method for flux measurements." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region)
- Alternative: Imaging without the >138 klambda spatial filtering uv cut (rejected for photometry because extended emission lowers source detection by 39% and prevents Gaussian fitting of sources like ORBS).  
  > "We noted a significant improvement for source detection by using the additional spatial filtering of the visibility data allowing a 39 per cent increase in detected sources, amongst these the flaring source discussed in section [4.2.1](#sec:orbs)" (Source Detection)
- Alternative: Fully automated blind source extraction across the map (rejected because spatially filtered extended emission leaves uneven noise and spurious point-like artifacts).  
  > "Even after applying additional spatial filtering, this often remains as uneven noise with occasional spurious point-like emission, and therefore automated source extraction methods typically require significant manual intervention to deal with artifacts." (Source Detection)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dual-polarization mode (XX, YY) prevents recovery of Stokes V (circular polarization) and is insufficient to obtain full linear polarization without additional calibration.  
  > "the observations were carried out in dual-polarization mode recording the XX and YY correlations that allow us to recover the Stokes I intensity maps (Stokes Q is also accessible but insufficient to obtain overall linear polarization without additional calibration)." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Since the observation were carried out in dual-polarization mode it is not possible to recover Stokes V information." (Strong Flaring source ORBS)
- Limitation (authors): Poor weather conditions on 2017 December 29 and during epoch 4 degraded the sensitivity to 0.18-0.31 mJy beam^-1, worse than early theoretical predictions (~10-100 uJy).  
  > "However, such sensitivity was not achieved in the ALMA band 3 observations that we are presenting here, which ranges between $\sim$100 and $\sim$300 $\mu$Jy" (Introduction); "These increased rms noise levels were due to poor weather conditions over the course of the last day of observations and also for epoch 4." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic effects from a changing synthesized beam in time-sliced imaging limit the study of minor variability (VF <= 2) and noise widens the VF distribution envelope up to VF ~ 3-4 (1-h) and VF ~ 5-7 (20-min) for faint sources.  
  > "While sources in our actual ALMA data with VF$\leq$2 may still show real variability, the main focus in our study is to find the strongest events and how often those occur rather than a detailed study of minor variability." (Simulated observations to assess systematic artificial variability in a complex region); "the envelope of the VF distribution at the two different time resolutions shown in Figure [7](#fig:VF_dist) also appears to display a systematic effect where a large dispersion is still seen above VF$=2$ , which is described by a noise distribution that increases towards lower peak flux densities and peaks at around $S_{\nu}\sim0.3-0.4$ mJy beam$^{-1}$, reaching VF$\sim3-4$ at 1-h time resolution and VF$\sim5-7$ at 20-min time resolution." (Variability distribution in Orion-KL)
- Limitation (authors): Visual inspection for faint sources introduces a selection bias toward regions with lower background noise.  
  > "This envelope then decreases towards the faintest sources likely due to completeness issues and a selection bias where the faintest sources were mostly selected by visual inspection of the concatenated image" (Variability distribution in Orion-KL)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The variability factor (VF = S_max / S_min) is positively biased by noise for low-S/N sources and sensitive to outlier measurements or upper limits, unlike chi-squared or normalized excess variance metrics.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Peak flux density measurements from 2D Gaussian fits (IMFIT) on un-smoothed time-sliced images can still be affected by beam-shape variations for marginally resolved sources if the beam is not convolved to a common resolution across epochs.
