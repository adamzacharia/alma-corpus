## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and lines**: Calibrate in CASA, Fourier transform and CLEAN with Briggs robust=+0.5 (lines) and natural/robust/uniform(uv>80 klambda) weighting (continuum)  
  > "The same procedure was adopted for the continuum image, except that we adopted the Natural weighting, robust=+0.5 weighting, and the Uniform weighting" (2 ALMA Observation and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust=+0.5; uv>80 klambda for highest-resolution continuum  
  > "Uniform weighting using the data with projected $uv$ distances larger than 80 $k\lambda$" (2 ALMA Observation and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Synthesized beam checked to be clean, primary sidelobe ~25%  
  > "we confirmed that the synthesized beam is still clean with the primary sidelobe level of $\sim$25$\%$" (2 ALMA Observation and Data Reduction)
2. **Fit protostar components and derive disk masses**: Gaussian fits to the two compact continuum components, subtract them to isolate the circumbinary disk, then compute masses from M=S d^2/(kappa B(T))  
  > "we subtracted two Gaussian components with parameters derived from the fit to the two compact dust components" (3.1 0.9-mm Continuum)
   - parameter: Td = 10-42 K, beta=0.5, kappa0.9mm=0.053 cm2/g, d=140 pc  
  > "With $T_{d}$ = 10 – 42 K and $\kappa_{0.9mm}$ = 0.053 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$" (3.1 0.9-mm Continuum)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100, dust opacity from Hildebrand 1983 with beta=0.5  
  > "on the assumption of a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100" (3.1 0.9-mm Continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Ossenkopf & Henning opacity (factor 3 higher masses)  
  > "Thus, adopting the dust mass opacity by Ossenkopf & Henning (1994) provides a factor 3 higher masses of the circumstellar and circumbinary disks." (3.1 0.9-mm Continuum)
3. **Map velocity structure and non-Keplerian residuals**: Moment and channel maps, P-V diagrams; subtract SMA best-fit Keplerian model from line-of-sight velocities pixel by pixel (Appendix B)  
  > "We computed the line-of-sight non-Keplerian velocity component for each pixel in the channel maps using expression A7" (B Subtraction of the Keplerian Rotation Motion)
   - parameter: M=0.8 Msun, i=62 deg, theta=167 deg  
  > "$i$ the inclination angle (= 62$\ensuremath{^\circ}$), and $\theta$ the position angle (= 167$\ensuremath{^\circ}$)" (B Subtraction of the Keplerian Rotation Motion)
   - assumption: Geometrically thin disk, circular Keplerian bulk motion  
  > "Assuming that the circumbinary disk is geometrically thin" (B Subtraction of the Keplerian Rotation Motion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Hydrodynamic simulation of binary + circumbinary disk**: 3D AMR isothermal simulation in a rotating frame with sink particles, gas injected at boundary with specific angular momentum for 300 AU centrifugal radius; adopt state after ~20 orbits  
  > "We adopted the simulation result after the $\sim$20 orbital periods of the binary ($\sim$4 $\times$ 10$^{4}$ $yr$)." (4.1 Nature of the Circumbinary Disk)
   - software: SFUMATO
   - parameter: Box 1740 AU, finest cell 0.85 AU, sink radius 3.4 AU, n_inj=1.5e5 cm-3, T=13.5 K  
  > "Gas Number Density at the Boundary | 1.5 $\times$ 10$^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Circular binary orbit, isothermal gas, self-gravity ignored, disk coplanar with orbit  
  > "The self-gravity of the gas can be safely ignored" (C.1 Model of the Hydrodynamic Simulation)
   - fitting: No fit; parameters chosen from SMA results and to reproduce inner depression and accretion rate  
  > "The choice of specific angular momentum is guided also by the need to reproduce the inner depression in dust emission in the circumbinary disk." (4.1 Nature of the Circumbinary Disk)
   - validation: Tested T=25 K with no significant difference in gas distributions  
  > "we confirmed that using a temperature of 25 K does not produce any significant differences in the gas distributions in our hydrodynamic simulations" (4.1 Nature of the Circumbinary Disk)
5. **Radiative transfer and mock ALMA observation**: LTE ray integration through the model with power-law temperature, then simobserve and the same imaging as real data  
  > "the theoretical images are passed through the CASA task “simobserve” to create simulated visibility data" (C.4 Radiative Transfer Calculation)
   - software: CASA simobserve
   - parameter: T(r)=max[23 K (rA/300AU)^-0.2, 19 K (rB/300AU)^-0.2]; kappa0.9mm=0.053; C18O abundance 1.7e-7  
  > "Dust Opacity$\ensuremath{^{c}}$ | $\kappa_{0.9~mm} = 0.053~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$" (Table 2)
   - assumption: LTE, Tex = Tk  
  > "assuming the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) condition ($i.e.$, $T_{ex}$ = $T_{k}$)" (C.4 Radiative Transfer Calculation)
   - fitting: Temperature normalisation tuned by eye to match peaks and compact flux densities  
  > "Then, the power-law index of the temperature profile was determined to closely reproduce the observed flux density of the compact continuum component" (4.1 Nature of the Circumbinary Disk)
   - validation: Qualitative comparison of simulated and observed continuum, channel maps, P-V diagrams and mean velocity maps  
  > "These channel maps closely capture all the important features seen in the ALMA image cube as described above." (4.1 Nature of the Circumbinary Disk)

## Qualitative findings

- The 0.9-mm continuum shows two U-shaped brightenings in the circumbinary disk and a depression between it and the primary's circumstellar disk.  
  > "The 0.9-mm continuum shows two opposing $U$-shaped brightenings in the circumbinary disk" (abstract)
- C18O shows no depression, implying it is more optically thick than the dust.  
  > "The observed behavior suggests that C$^{18}$O is more optically thick than dust" (3.2.1 Integrated Intensity Map)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Simulation tailored to L1551 NE needed to interpret the new features  
  > "To aid in interpreting the results, we performed a 3-dimensional hydrodynamic simulation of the distribution and motion of matter around a binary protostellar system tailored to the inferred properties of L1551 NE." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Rotation plus infall alone cannot explain the differing signs of velocity gradients along and parallel to the minor axis.  
  > "Thus, the different signs of the velocity gradients along and parallel to the minor axis cannot be simply explained by combination of rotation and infall." (3.2.2 Velocity Structure)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Missing flux and outflow complicate other lines near systemic velocity; temperatures in hydrodynamics (isothermal 13.5 K) and radiative transfer (power law) are inconsistent.  
  > "Closer to the systemic velocity, the spatial-kinematic structure in the other lines is complicated by the effects of missing flux as well as an outflow (see Appendix A)." (2 ALMA Observation and Data Reduction)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The model-observation comparison is qualitative, with no quantitative fit or goodness-of-fit metric
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model parameters were tuned by hand, so agreement is not independent evidence
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Magnetic fields were not simulated, so exclusion is by absence of need, not by test
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disk masses depend heavily on an assumed Td range and opacity
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing flux near systemic velocity limits the kinematic analysis
