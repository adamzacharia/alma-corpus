## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum**: tclean on pipeline-calibrated data with per-image weighting chosen to maximize inner-disk S/N  
  > "for each individual disk and observing wavelength the images were produced with different weighting scheme." (2.2 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust between 0.5 and -1.0, or natural/uniform/superuniform  
  > "natural, uniform, and Briggs (with robust parameter between 0.5 and -1.0) weighting" (2.2 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure inner and outer disk flux densities**: 2D Gaussian imfit for inner flux; aperture photometry above 3 rms for total; outer = total minus inner; 5 rms upper limits  
  > "the flux density of the detected central emission $F_\text{inner}$ is estimated through a 2D Gaussian fitting with the CASA `imfit` task." (4.1 Central emission modeling); "The outer disk flux density $F_\text{outer}$ is then estimated subtracting $F_\text{inner}$ from $F_\text{tot}$." (4.1 Central emission modeling)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: upper limit of 5 times the rms if no detection at 3 rms and cavity resolved  
  > "an upper limit of 5 times the rms is adopted" (4.1 Central emission modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive spectral indices**: Linear fit of log F vs log nu; curve_fit if >2 points, else two-point; for GG Tau, HD100546, HD142527 extrapolate 1 mm dust to 3 mm and give interval [alpha_extrap, alpha_3mm-cm]  
  > "the best-fit parameters – $\log A_\text{outer}$ and $\alpha_\text{outer}$ – are estimated using the `curve_fit` function of the `scipy` module" (4.2 Millimeter spectral index); "In this way, the interval $[\alpha_\text{extrap},\alpha_\text{3mm-cm}]$ is the best estimate for the spectral index of the inner central emission." (4.2 Millimeter spectral index)
   - software: scipy curve_fit
   - assumption: 1 mm emission dominated by thermal dust for extrapolation  
  > "Assuming that the emission at 1 mm is dominated by thermal dust with only a small contribution from the free-free emission" (4.2 Millimeter spectral index)
   - fitting: least-squares linear fit in log-log  
  > "The spectral index of the emission has been estimated for both the central emission and the outer emission, with a linear fit, given an assumed correlation between the flux density and frequency" (Millimeter spectral index)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate inner-disk dust masses**: Optically thin blackbody dust mass formula using Band 6/7 flux, R_id upper limit = beam size  
  > "assuming as an upper limit on $R_\text{id}$ the corresponding beam size" (5.1 Inner-disk dust masses)
   - parameter: kappa = 10 cm2/g at 1000 GHz, beta = 1.0, flaring angle 0.02, R_subl = 0.07 sqrt(L*)  
  > "the dust opacity $\kappa_\nu$ is 10 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at 1000 GHz, with an opacity power-law index of $\beta=1.0$" (5.1 Inner-disk dust masses)
   - assumption: optically thin emission, no scattering  
  > "In the assumption of optically thin emission and in the blackbody approximation" (5.1 Inner-disk dust masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Test free-free luminosity vs L_acc and L_X**: Pearson correlation between free-free luminosity (from mm flux) and accretion/X-ray luminosity  
  > "no strong correlation between the free-free luminosity, as inferred from the millimeter flux, and the accretion luminosity is found" (5.2 Photoevaporative wind or jet-and-MHD wind)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compute ionized mass loss rate and correlate with accretion rate**: Reynolds jet model equation; linmix regression of Mdot_i vs Mdot  
  > "we estimated the ionized mass loss rate $\dot M_\text{i}$ following the modeling of the free-free emission from a jet by (cite: 1986Reynolds)" (5.3 Properties of the jet emission); "The liner regression was performed with `linmix`" (Footnote)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: T = 1e4 K, nu_m = 40 GHz, theta_min/theta_maj = 0.5, v_jet = 140 (M*/0.5 Msun)^0.5 km/s, jet inclination = outer disk inclination  
  > "A value of $T=10^4$ K is adopted for the ionized gas" (5.3 Properties of the jet emission); "We assumed that the jet is perpendicular to the plane of the disk" (5.3 Properties of the jet emission)
   - assumption: central emission is jet free-free emission  
  > "Under the assumption that the central emission in those ten systems is associated with the gas from an ionized jet" (5.3 Properties of the jet emission)
   - fitting: linmix linear regression  
  > "The liner regression was performed with `linmix`" (Footnote)
   - validation: Compared with Mdot_i/Mdot from [OI] HVC (Nisini 2018)  
  > "These results are consistent with ionized mass ejection rates measured from HVC \[O I\]6300" (5.3 Properties of the jet emission)

## Qualitative findings

- Outer disk emission is consistent with dust; central emission in 10/11 disks is consistent with free-free emission.  
  > "10 out of 11 of the spectral indices estimated for the central emission close to the star suggest that this emission is free-free emission" (abstract)

## Claim tuples

- H: Central emission is thermal dust or free-free | E: Spectral index of inner and outer emission from ALMA plus cm data | R: alpha_outer >2 in all, alpha_inner <2 in most | F: Inner emission in 10/11 disks is free-free from jet or wind | **supported**
- H: Free-free emission arises from a photoevaporative wind | E: Correlate free-free luminosity with L_acc and L_X | R: no significant correlations | F: photoevaporative wind unlikely | **supported**
- H: Ionized outflow is connected to accretion | E: Jet model Mdot_i vs Mdot regression | R: r=0.86, slope 0.62 | F: sub-linear correlation, accretion driven by jet/MHD wind | **supported**
- H: Inner disks contain abundant mm dust | E: Dust mass from inner flux | R: low masses 0.004-1.37 Mearth | F: lack of mm dust in inner disks | **partially**

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Transition disk cavities let central emission be disentangled from the outer disk  
  > "the central emission can be fully disentangled from the outer disk even at moderate angular resolution of $0''.1$" (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Photoevaporative wind origin rejected because of no correlation with L_acc or L_X and flat/positive spectral indices  
  > "flat or positive spectral indices, as the one estimated for our sample, are expected to be associated with partially optically thick free-free emission due to other mechanisms" (5.2)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dust masses highly uncertain; radiative transfer needed  
  > "A full radiative transfer modeling approach is thus required to obtain a better estimate of the inner disk dust masses." (5.1)
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneous accretion and free-free measurements; variability  
  > "since a correlation is observed despite the accretion rates and the free-free emission having not been estimated at the same time" (5.3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small sample (n=11, 10 for regression) and the correlation may be driven by distance/luminosity scaling common to both axes
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral indices contaminated by dust and non-simultaneous cm data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Jet model assumptions (velocity, opening angle, turnover) fixed rather than fitted
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Marginally resolved/unresolved inner emission measured with imfit may suffer from sidelobe contamination
