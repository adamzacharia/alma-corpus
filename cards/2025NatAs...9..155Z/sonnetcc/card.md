## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce MIRI LRS data to a 1D spectrum**: JWST pipeline stage 1 unchanged, spec2pipeline per exposure, separate per-dither Background2D subtraction, spec3pipeline combination, manual boxcar 1D extraction  
  > "Then, we run the `spec2pipeline` stage, which performs flux calibration and various instrument corrections, separately on the individual count-rate exposures, yielding six individual `s2d` images." (JWST/MIRI observations and data reduction); "Finally, we perform 1D extraction manually using a boxcar filter with a width that scales with the MIRI PSF FWHM" (JWST/MIRI observations and data reduction)
   - software: JWST pipeline, photutils
   - parameter: Background2D median background with 2x2 pixel box, masking r=3.5 pixel region around lines  
  > "We estimate the median background using a box size of $2\times 2$ pixels." (JWST/MIRI observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative standard-pipeline reduction gives a similar spectrum; [OIII] luminosity about 20% brighter but lower S/N  
  > "An alternative reduction following the standard steps in the pipeline, results in a similar spectrum but with a continuum baseline slightly offset towards negative values." (JWST/MIRI observations and data reduction)
2. **Fit Halpha and [OIII] lines for redshift and fluxes**: Single Gaussian for Halpha; simultaneous two-Gaussian fit to [OIII] doublet with shared width  
  > "A single Gaussian function was used to fit the H$\alpha$ line assuming negligible contribution from the \[NII\] doublet." (Spectroscopic redshift and line measurements)
   - software: lmfit
   - parameter: [OIII] line width free but common; 5007/4959 peak ratio fixed to 3  
  > "During this procedure we assume the same line-width for the two lines (leaving it as a free parameter) and fix the 5007/4959 peak line ratio to 3." (Spectroscopic redshift and line measurements)
   - assumption: Negligible [NII] contribution to Halpha  
  > "A single Gaussian function was used to fit the H$\alpha$ line assuming negligible contribution from the \[NII\] doublet." (Spectroscopic redshift and line measurements)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least squares  
  > "We use a Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm to perform non-linear least-square minimization (using the lmfit function)." (Spectroscopic redshift and line measurements)
   - validation: Line widths compared with MIRI instrumental resolution; redshift compared with NIRSpec  
  > "Similar redshift constraints were derived from the NIRSpec observations(cite: Castellano2024) with a weighted redshift average between four different emission lines" (Spectroscopic redshift and line measurements)
3. **Re-examine ALMA spectrum at the updated redshift**: Visual inspection of the 254.35 GHz region and a single-Gaussian fit to the extracted spectrum centred on the peak channel; result treated as an upper limit  
  > "With the updated redshift information, we visually inspect the 254.35 GHz region and fit the extracted spectrum using a single Gaussian function, centered at the peak channel." (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density); "to be conservative, we treat it here only as an upper limit." (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)
   - parameter: Best-fit line flux about 2.5e-19 erg/s/cm2 with FWHM about 200 km/s  
  > "The best-fit implies a line flux of $\sim2.5\times10^{-19}\rm\,erg/s/cm^{-2}$ with $\rm FWHM\approx200\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$" (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian; fitting code not stated  
  > "fit the extracted spectrum using a single Gaussian function, centered at the peak channel" (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)
   - validation: Consistent with the earlier upper limit once the different line widths are taken into account  
  > "which is consistent with the previously reported(cite: Bakx2023; Popping2023) upper limit" (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)
4. **Constrain electron density from [OIII]5007/[OIII]88um ratio**: Compare observed line-ratio limit with PyNeb emissivity ratio grid over electron density and temperature  
  > "were generated using the PyNeb getEmissivity package(cite: Luridiana2015), with the default atomic data." (Photoionization and line emissivity models)
   - software: PyNeb
   - parameter: Grid log(ne)=0-10 cm^-3, log(Te)=3.7-4.5 K  
  > "The ratio was calculated for different values of electron density and temperature ranging from $\log(n_e\rm[cm^{-3}])=0-10$ and $\log(T_e\rm[K])=3.7-4.5$." (Photoionization and line emissivity models)
   - assumption: Typical Te=10,000 K for the quoted density limit  
  > "Assuming the typical value of $T_e=10,000\,$K, the current constraints imply an extreme $n_e$ value above $1,000\,\rm cm^{-3}$." (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA and JWST line constraints together imply a high electron density, consistent with an evolution toward higher densities at high redshift  
  > "This is consistent with the picture of higher electron densities at high redshifts, as shown in Extended Data Fig. [5](#fig:ne_redshift)." (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)
- The earlier tentative ALMA line at 258.7 GHz is inconsistent with the MIRI and NIRSpec redshift  
  > "This is inconsistent with the MIRI and NIRSpec spectroscopic redshift, implying that the line was not real or, at least, not associated with GHZ2." (Revisiting the ALMA data and constraining the electron density)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MIRI is the only instrument with wavelength coverage to detect Halpha and [OIII] at these redshifts  
  > "The MIRI instrument on board JWST is thus the only astronomical instrument with the required wavelength coverage to detect these spectral lines at higher redshifts" (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Standard pipeline reduction rejected in favour of modified reduction with better rms noise  
  > "Given that our modified reduction provides better r.m.s. noise across the whole spectrum, we adopt it for our analysis" (JWST/MIRI observations and data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Redshift uncertainty dominated by MIRI/LRS wavelength calibration  
  > "The dominant uncertainty on the spectroscopic redshift comes from the current MIRI/LRS wavelength calibration." (Spectroscopic redshift and line measurements)
- Limitation (authors): Dust attenuation cannot be constrained with current data  
  > "Deeper observations will be necessary to put any constraints on the presence (or absence) of dust in this galaxy via the Balmer decrement." (Inferred parameters from the emission lines.)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA line is only about 3 sigma and treated as an upper limit, so the density limit depends on an assumed 200 km/s width and a visually chosen channel
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The density limit relies on adopting Hbeta/[OIII] and [OIII]88um without lensing or aperture correction between JWST and ALMA
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Hbeta flux is inferred from Halpha assuming no dust, which feeds the metallicity and ionization results
