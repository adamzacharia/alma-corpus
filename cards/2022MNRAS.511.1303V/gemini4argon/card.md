## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract and measure spectroscopic [O II] line flux and redshift for DC_881725**: Reduce Keck/MOSFIRE K-band data, calibrate wavelength with sky lines, perform absolute flux calibration using a 2MASS standard star matched to a Pickles G5 V model normalized to UltraVISTA K-band, extract 1D spectrum in a 6-pixel spatial window, and fit a single Gaussian to the [O II] line.  
  > "The data was reduced and wavelength calibrated using the current MOSFIRE data reduction pipeline." (DC_881725: Spectroscopic [O ii] Measurement at z=4.58); "The one-dimensional spectrum of *DC_881725* is extracted in a spatial window of $6$ pixels (corresponding to $1.08\ensuremath{''}$ at a pixel scale of $0.18\ensuremath{''}/{\rm px}$) around the centre of the identified [O ii] emission line." (DC_881725: Spectroscopic [O ii] Measurement at z=4.58); "standard star from the 2MASS star catalogue was included in the mask to derive a wavelength-dependent absolute flux calibration of the extracted one-dimensional spectrum." (A Unique Sample of z~4.5 Galaxies with [O ii] Measurements, DC_881725)
   - software: MOSFIRE data reduction pipeline
   - parameter: spatial extraction window = 6 pixels (1.08 arcsec at 0.18 arcsec/px)  
  > "The one-dimensional spectrum of *DC_881725* is extracted in a spatial window of $6$ pixels (corresponding to $1.08\ensuremath{''}$ at a pixel scale of $0.18\ensuremath{''}/{\rm px}$) around the centre of the identified [O ii] emission line." (DC_881725: Spectroscopic [O ii] Measurement at z=4.58)
   - parameter: 500 Monte Carlo samples for flux uncertainty  
  > "The uncertainties of the total flux are determined using $500$ Monte Carlo samples for each of which the fluxes are changed according to their uncertainties (assumed Gaussian) obtained from the variance of the spectrum." (DC_881725: Spectroscopic [O ii] Measurement at z=4.58)
   - assumption: Equal flux of the two blended [O II] doublet lines for velocity shift calculation; G5 V stellar model for standard star  
  > "The velocity shifts quoted assume equal flux of the two blended [O ii] lines." (Footnote 4); "We therefore use the G5 V normalisation through out the paper." (Absolute Flux Calibration of the MOSFIRE Spectrum)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian fit with variable mean, sigma, and total flux; uncertainties from 500 Monte Carlo realisations  
  > "line flux is derived by fitting a single Gaussian with variable mean," (A Unique Sample of z~4.5 Galaxies with [O ii] Measurements, DC_881725)
   - validation: Compared derived [O II] luminosity to EL-COSMOS catalogue predictions  
  > "*EL-COSMOS* agrees very well with our narrow-band and spectroscopic [O ii] measurements." (Comparison of Optical Emission Line Measurements to EL-COSMOS)
2. **Measure [O II] line fluxes and luminosities from narrow-band imaging for 9 galaxies**: Reduce Subaru/MOIRCS NB2071 and NB2083 images, calibrate astrometry and stack, degrade PSFs to match UltraVISTA DR4 Ks-band, run dual-image source extraction using Ks as detection image, solve system of two equations for continuum and line flux, and combine the two narrow-band filters using inverse-variance weighting.  
  > "For the source detection and extraction on each narrow-band image, SEXTRACTOR (cite: Bertin1996) was used in dual-image mode using the much deeper ($24.8\,{\rm mag}$ at $5\sigma$) *Ks*$-$band image as the detection image." ([O ii] Measurements from Narrow-Band Imaging); "The following system of two equations involving the narrow-band and the underlying *Ks*-band photometry was solved for the continuum ($f^{\lambda}_{\rm cont}$) and [O ii] line flux ($f^{\lambda}_{\rm line}$) at wavelength $\lambda$ of the [O ii] line (cite: Hu2019):" ([O ii] Measurements from Narrow-Band Imaging); "Finally, an inverse-variance weighting scheme was adopted to combine fluxes obtained from the two narrow-band filters, with weights defined as $W_i = T_i^2 / \sigma_i^2$, where $T_i$ is the narrow-band transmission curve at the [C ii] redshift and $\sigma_i$ is the narrow-band flux uncertainty." ([O ii] Measurements from Narrow-Band Imaging)
   - software: IRAF, MCSRED2, SCAMP, SWARP, SEXTRACTOR, PSFEX, PHOTUTIL
   - parameter: DETECT_MINAREA = 5, DETECT_THRESH = 1.5, ANALYSIS_THRESH = 1.5  
  > "We set detection parameters of `DETECT_MINAREA` $=5$, `DETECT_THRESH` $=1.5$, and `ANALYSIS_THRESH` $=1.5$." (Footnote 5)
   - assumption: Delta-function [O II] line profile in each narrow-band filter and flat continuum profile  
  > "In the calculation, we assumed an [O ii] line profile resembling a $\delta$-function in each narrow-band filter and a flat continuum profile." ([O ii] Measurements from Narrow-Band Imaging)
   - fitting: Monte-Carlo iterations drawing narrow-band and Ks-band fluxes from Gaussian distributions; 16th and 84th quantiles define uncertainties  
  > "The uncertainties of the [O ii] fluxes were obtained using Monte-Carlo iterations for each of which the narrow-band and *Ks*-band fluxes were drawn from a Gaussian distribution with a width corresponding to their associated uncertainties. The [O ii] flux uncertainty is then defined as the 16$^{\rm th}$ and 84$^{\rm th}$ quantiles of the resulting [O ii] flux distribution." ([O ii] Measurements from Narrow-Band Imaging)
   - validation: Compared narrow-band [O II] luminosities to EL-COSMOS catalogue predictions  
  > "*EL-COSMOS* agrees very well with our narrow-band and spectroscopic [O ii] measurements." (Comparison of Optical Emission Line Measurements to EL-COSMOS)
3. **Estimate average gas-phase metallicity from rest-frame UV absorption lines**: Median stack the 10 rest-UV Keck/DEIMOS spectra, compute equivalent widths (EWs) of Si III (~1300 A), C II (~1335 A), Si IV (~1400 A), and C IV (~1550 A) absorption complexes, and apply empirical EW-metallicity calibrations from Faisst et al. (2016b).  
  > "We therefore perform median stacking the $10$ spectra before we compute the EWs of several absorption complexes around Si iii ($\sim 1300\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$), C ii ($\sim 1335\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$), Si iv ($\sim 1400\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$), and C iv ($\sim 1550\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$)." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines); "We then use the relations between absorption line EW and metallicity as calibrated via galaxies in the local universe as well as $z\sim2$ and $z\sim3$ in (cite: FAISST16b)." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines)
   - parameter: Absorption complexes at ~1300 A (Si III), ~1335 A (C II), ~1400 A (Si IV), ~1550 A (C IV)  
  > "We therefore perform median stacking the $10$ spectra before we compute the EWs of several absorption complexes around Si iii ($\sim 1300\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$), C ii ($\sim 1335\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$), Si iv ($\sim 1400\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$), and C iv ($\sim 1550\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$)." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines)
   - assumption: Solar oxygen abundance 12 + log(O/H) = 8.76  
  > "We assume a solar oxygen abundance of ${\rm \log(O/H)+12} = 8.76$ according to the “galactic concordance abundances” (cite: NIEVA12; NICHOLLS17)" (Footnote 6)
   - fitting: Combine probability distribution functions of all absorption complexes with Monte-Carlo iterations including spectral variances  
  > "The uncertainties are computed via Monte-Carlo iterations including the spectral variances." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines); "Combining the probability distribution functions of all absorption complexes, we find an average metallicity of $12+\log{(\rm O/H}) = 8.41^{+0.31}_{-0.54}$." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines)
   - validation: Consistent with expected metallicity based on stellar masses at these redshifts  
  > "Such values are consistent with the expected metallicity based on the stellar masses of our galaxies at these redshifts (cite: FAISST16b; ANDO07)." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines)
4. **Derive total SFRs, dust-correct [O II] and Halpha luminosities, and evaluate the [O II]-SFR relation**: Compute total SFR from UV + FIR continuum (for 5 FIR-detected galaxies) or from the [C II]-SFR relation of Schaerer et al. (2020) (for 5 FIR-undetected galaxies); compute dust attenuation correction for [O II] and Halpha using IRX (for FIR-detected) or SED/UV-slope beta (for FIR-undetected); compare dust-corrected [O II] luminosity and total SFR against Kennicutt (1998) and Kewley et al. (2004) relations.  
  > "For galaxies detected in far-infrared continuum ($5$ out of $10$), we compute the total SFR as the sum of UV and dust obscured star formation." (Total SFRs Derived from UV+Far-Infrared Continuum and [C ii] Emission); "For the remaining five galaxies without far-infrared continuum detection, we use the relation between [C ii] line emission and total SFR as presented by (cite: ALPINE_SCHAERER20)" (Total SFRs Derived from UV+Far-Infrared Continuum and [C ii] Emission); "We compute [O ii] dust correction factors using the stellar continuum dust attenuation $E_{s}{(B-V)}$ values via $$\begin{equation} f_{\rm corr} = 10^{0.4 E_{s}{(B-V)} k_{\lambda}/f}, \end{equation}$$ where $k_{\rm \lambda}$ is the reddening curve with $\lambda=3727\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$ and we assume a differential dust attenuation factor between stellar continuum and nebular emission of $f=0.44$ (cite: CALZETTI00)" (The [O ii]-SFR relation at z~4.5)
   - software: LePhare
   - parameter: f = 0.44 (differential dust attenuation factor, also tested f = 0.7); lambda = 3727 A  
  > "where $k_{\rm \lambda}$ is the reddening curve with $\lambda=3727\,{\rm \text{\normalfont\AA}}$ and we assume a differential dust attenuation factor between stellar continuum and nebular emission of $f=0.44$ (cite: CALZETTI00)" (The [O ii]-SFR relation at z~4.5)
   - assumption: Chabrier (2003) IMF (conversion factor 1.77 from Salpeter); stacked FIR SED dust temperature 43 +/- 5 K; Calzetti (2000) and Reddy et al. (2015) reddening curves  
  > "These SFR values have already been converted from a (cite: SALPETER55) to a (cite: CHABRIER03) IMF by division of a factor of $1.77$ (in linear scale)." (Total SFRs Derived from UV+Far-Infrared Continuum and [C ii] Emission); "The dust temperature of this stack is $43\pm5\,{\rm K}$, which is consistent with the individual dust temperatures measured for $4$ *ALPINE* galaxies with sufficient far-infrared coverage from ALMA (cite: FAISST20b)." (Total SFRs Derived from UV+Far-Infrared Continuum and [C ii] Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared 4 methods of deriving E_s(B-V) (SED fitting, beta with Calzetti 2000, beta with Reddy 2015, IRX-beta from Fudamoto 2020), finding agreement within 0.05 mag (<30% difference in dust factors); compared Halpha to EL-COSMOS catalogue  
  > "We find that all four methods agree within $0.05\,{\rm mag}$, which results in $<30\%$ differences in the dust factors for [O ii]." (The [O ii]-SFR relation at z~4.5)

## Qualitative findings

- The [O II]-SFR relation at z ~ 4.5 cannot be described using standard local descriptions (Kennicutt 1998 overestimates SFR by factors of 3-5), but is consistent with the Kewley et al. (2004) metal-dependent relation assuming metallicities around 50% solar.  
  > "The [O ii]$-$SFR relation at $z\sim4.5$ cannot be described using standard local descriptions, but is consistent with a metal-dependent relation assuming metallicities around $50\%$ solar." (Abstract); "We find that the original (cite: KENNICUTT98) relation would significantly overestimate [O ii]-derived SFRs by factors of $3-5$ if applied to *dust-corrected* [O ii] luminosities." (The [O ii]-SFR relation at z~4.5)
- Typical log(M/M_sun) > 9 main-sequence galaxies at z ~ 4.5 have broadly similar ISM properties (log(U) < -2 and log(n_e) ~ 2.5-3) as their descendants at z ~ 2-3 when matched by specific SFR, and suggest a rapid evolution of ISM properties since the Epoch of Reionisation at z > 6.  
  > "The analysis of this pilot sample suggests that typical $\log(M/{\rm M_{\odot}}) > 9$ galaxies at $z\sim4.5$ to have broadly similar ISM properties as their descendants at $z\sim2$ and suggest a strong evolution of ISM properties since the Epoch of Reionisation at $z>6$." (Abstract); "All in all, we find that the ISM properties of this representative sample of $z\sim4.5$ galaxies are similar to their descendants at $z\sim2-3$ when matched by their specific SFRs." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: At z = 4-6, current ground-based facilities only allow observation of the [O II] doublet among strong optical lines; combining rest-UV absorption lines, optical [O II] and Spitzer Halpha, and ALMA [C II] and dust continuum mitigates the lack of multiple optical lines to constrain ISM properties and total SFR.  
  > "Unfortunately, current facilities only allow the observation of one strong optical line (the [O ii] doublet at rest-frame $3726\text{\normalfont\AA}$ and $3729\text{\normalfont\AA}$) at these redshifts, thus studies with multiple optical lines as the ones mentioned above are not possible." (Introduction); "We mitigate the lack of optical emission lines by entering the far-infrared and in addition use spectral information from the rest-frame UV." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Median stacking of the 10 rest-UV spectra is used because the S/N of individual rest-UV spectra is too low to estimate metal content; CLOUDY models are used to interpret the [O II]/Halpha and [O II]/[C II] line ratios in terms of U and n_e.  
  > "The S/N of individual rest-UV spectra of our galaxies is too low to estimate their metal content. We therefore perform median stacking the $10$ spectra before we compute the EWs of several absorption complexes" (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines)
- Alternative: Double Gaussian fit to the [O II] doublet in DC_881725 (rejected due to low signal-to-noise and resolution)  
  > "We also tried to fit a double Gaussian to account for the doublet nature of the line. However, given the low signal-to-noise and resolution, no robust fit was obtained." (DC_881725: Spectroscopic [O ii] Measurement at z=4.58)
- Alternative: Multi-sector CLOUDY models (rejected because photometric uncertainties in [O II] and Halpha are too large for more complicated models to improve robustness)  
  > "More complicated “multi-sector” models could be investigated, however, we do not think that given the uncertainties in our (photometric) measurements of [O ii] and H$\alpha$ this would improve the robustness of our results." (Caveats)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N even in the stacked rest-UV spectrum causes apparent differences between individual absorption complexes.  
  > "We note that even for the stack the S/N is relatively low, which is likely the cause for the apparent differences between the individual absorption complexes." (Estimate of Metal-Enrichment from Rest-Frame UV Absorption Lines)
- Limitation (authors): Differential dust attenuation factor (f-factor) between nebular emission and stellar continuum is unknown at z ~ 4.5 and is the largest contribution to the total uncertainty in dust-corrected [O II] and Halpha luminosities.  
  > "The differential dust attenuation between nebular emission and stellar continuum is by far the largest contribution to the total uncertainty." (The [O ii]-SFR relation at z~4.5)
- Limitation (authors): Large measurement and systematic uncertainties in dust-corrected [O II] and total SFR prevent distinguishing between different metallicities in the Kewley et al. (2004) [O II]-SFR relation at high significance.  
  > "The large uncertainties, mainly due to the unknown differential dust attenuation factors but also the uncertain total SFR, do not allow us to distinguish relations for different metallicities at significance." (The [O ii]-SFR relation at z~4.5)
- Limitation (authors): Nine of the 10 galaxies reside in the footprint of the PCl J1001+0220 proto-cluster at z ~ 4.57, so environmental effects cannot be completely ruled out.  
  > "We note that nine of the galaxies lie in a greater structure associated with a proto-cluster at $z\sim4.57$ (cite: Lemaux2018). It is possible that the proto-cluster environment has altered their properties in some way that could be relevant for this study." (The log(L_[OII]/L_[CII]) luminosity ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Three out of 10 galaxies have Spitzer photometry contaminated by nearby sources and are excluded from the [O II]/Halpha analysis.  
  > "Note that we are only using $7$ out of the $10$ galaxies whose *Spitzer* photometry is not contaminated by nearby sources." (The log(L_[OII]/L_Halpha) luminosity ratio)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): [C II] 158 um emission can arise from neutral photodissociation regions (PDRs) and diffuse cold/warm neutral gas in addition to H II regions, which can complicate simple single-cloud photoionisation comparisons if differential spatial extent or PDR fraction varies.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Half of the sample lacks direct FIR continuum detections and relies on the [C II]-SFR relation to estimate total SFR, introducing circularity when comparing to [C II]-based quantities.
