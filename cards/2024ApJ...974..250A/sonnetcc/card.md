## Analysis recipe

1. **image line cubes and continuum**: Hoogbom CLEAN with tclean, Briggs weighting of 0, 4 mas pixels, line cubes at native and coarser channel widths  
  > "In both bands we used the Hoogbom algorithm in the *tclean* task of CASA" (2 Observations and data analysis); "Briggs weighting of 0" (2 Observations and data analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: pixel size 4 milliarcsec  
  > "and a **pixel size** of 4 milliarcsec" (2 Observations and data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **measure continuum flux density and spectral index**: Integrated flux densities at 93 and 224 GHz give a spectral index; source unresolved  
  > "The integrated flux density is $0.12 \pm 0.01$ Jy at 93 GHz and $0.17 \pm 0.03$ Jy at 224 GHz" (3 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with ATCA 8.64 GHz flux and spectral index; combined index 8.6 to 93 GHz of 0.45  
  > "we obtain a spectral index between 8.6 and 93 GHz of 0.45" (4 Discussion)
3. **model the H50beta profile**: Fit three Gaussians to the noisy H50beta line profile to build a smooth model profile  
  > "Since the H50$\beta$ line is too noisy, we fitted three gaussians to better describe its profile." (3 Results)
   - assumption: H50beta is formed in LTE when H30alpha is masing  
  > "the H50$\beta$ line is formed in LTE (cite: 1996ApJ...470.1118S)." (3 Results)
   - fitting: Sum of three Gaussians; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "we fitted three gaussians to better describe its profile." (3 Results)
   - validation: Residuals of the fit shown in Figure 3  
  > "the gaussians, the model profile (sum of the gaussians) and the residuals of the fitting." (3 Results)
4. **predict LTE profiles and find maser and dasar residuals**: Scale the H50beta model profile by C(n alpha, 50 beta) (oscillator strengths, quantum numbers, frequencies) to get LTE Hn alpha profiles, then subtract from observed profiles  
  > "we can use the observed H50$\beta$ line to estimate the profile that each of the H$n\alpha$ lines would have if they were emitted in LTE." (3 Results); "The values of $C( n\alpha, 50\beta)$ are 23.8, 5.3, and 4.6 for the H30$\alpha$, H40$\alpha$ and H42$\alpha$ lines, respectively (cite: gol68)." (3 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Strong narrow H30alpha residuals taken to confirm maser nature; negative H40alpha and H42alpha residuals at -25 km/s taken as dasar  
  > "The strong narrow lines in the H30$\alpha$ residuals confirm the maser nature of the emission." (3 Results); "the negative residuals seen in the H40$\alpha$ and H42$\alpha$ lines, centered at LSR velocity $-25$ km s$^{-1}$, were unexpected" (3 Results)
5. **locate emission centroids per velocity bin**: Resample cubes into coarse bins and fit a two-dimensional Gaussian in the image plane of each bin with the CASA fit routine  
  > "we fitted a two-dimensional gaussian in the image plane of each bin, and obtained the position of its centroid" (4.1 The structure of the emitting region); "We used the CASA fitting routine *fit*" (Footnote 3)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 10 channels (12.6 km/s) for H30alpha and 9 channels (13.3 km/s) for H40alpha  
  > "we resampled our cube in bins of 10 channels for the H30$\alpha$ line and 9 channels for H40$\alpha$, resulting in a velocity resolution of 12.6 and 13.3 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (4.1 The structure of the emitting region)
   - assumption: Signal to noise about 100 per channel for both lines  
  > "$\Re_{\rm sn} \simeq 100$ for each channel of both lines" (4.1 The structure of the emitting region)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit per bin; priors and sampler not stated  
  > "we fitted a two-dimensional gaussian in the image plane of each bin" (4.1 The structure of the emitting region)
   - validation: Fit errors compared with thermal-noise expression; absolute position uncertainty from the ALMA prescription  
  > "they are compatible with the thermal noise (cite: tho17)" (4.1 The structure of the emitting region)
6. **estimate source size and density from continuum**: From the 93 GHz flux density with optical depth 1 and Te = 10^4 K, derive solid angle, radius and electron density  
  > "From the flux density at 93 GHz and assuming the optical depth of the continuum as $\tau_{\rm C} = 1$ and the electron temperature $T_{\rm e}=10^4$ K, we found the solid angle of the source as $\Omega = 7.5 \times 10^{-14}$ sr," (4 Discussion)
   - assumption: Optical depth of continuum 1, electron temperature 10^4 K, distance 2.7 kpc, line-of-sight length equal to the radius  
  > "Assuming this value for the length of the source in the direction of the observer, we find an electron density $n_{\rm e} = 10^7$ cm$^{-3}$," (4 Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **model line and continuum optical depth for the dasar cloud**: Compute tau_C + tau_L for H30alpha, H40alpha, H42alpha and H50beta at several densities using b_n coefficients and Gaunt factors; compare with LTE values  
  > "for the electron temperature $7500$ K, $\Delta v = 20$ km s$^{-1}$ and electron densities $10^2$, $10^3$ and $10^4$ cm$^{-3}$." (4.3 The coexistence of masers and dasars)
   - parameter: Te = 7500 K, delta v = 20 km/s, ne = 10^2, 10^3, 10^4 cm^-3  
  > "for the electron temperature $7500$ K, $\Delta v = 20$ km s$^{-1}$ and electron densities $10^2$, $10^3$ and $10^4$ cm$^{-3}$." (4.3 The coexistence of masers and dasars)
   - assumption: Gaussian line profile; optical depths approximate since b_n exclude continuum and background fields  
  > "Notice that the values of $b_n$ do not include either the continuum field produced by the emitting plasma or the background photon field, and therefore the values of the optical depths are only approximations" (4.3 The coexistence of masers and dasars)
   - fitting: Dashed lines are quadratic fits to the data points (Figure 6 caption)  
  > "Dashed lines are quadratic fittings to the data." (Figure 6 caption)
   - validation: Optical depths differ from LTE more for H40alpha and H42alpha than for H30alpha and H50beta, matching the observed dasar  
  > "being higher for the H40$\alpha$ and H42$\alpha$ lines than that for H30$\alpha$ and H50$\beta$, explaining the dasar effect." (4.3 The coexistence of masers and dasars)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA HRL observations to understand the properties of the central ionized region  
  > "We have carried out ALMA observations of HRLs in Mz3 in order to understand the properties of its central ionized region." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: H50beta used as an LTE reference because it is formed in LTE when H30alpha masers occur  
  > "the H50$\beta$ line is formed in LTE (cite: 1996ApJ...470.1118S)." (3 Results)
- Alternative: The maser source may be a dense rotating disk or the combination of several compact sources; a third source for the H50beta emission profile cannot be excluded  
  > "The maser source can be either a dense rotating disk or the combination of several compact sources" (4.3 The coexistence of masers and dasars); "but it is not possible to know if there is still a third source" (4.1 The structure of the emitting region)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Source not spatially resolved in continuum or lines  
  > "Mz3 was not resolved by ALMA, either in the continuum of 93 and 224 GHz, or in the HRLs H30$\alpha$, H40$\alpha$, H42$\alpha$ or H50$\beta$." (4 Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Centroid positions only valid for relative comparison  
  > "The calculated positions can be used only to compare the relative positions of the different velocity channel images." (4.1 The structure of the emitting region)
- Limitation (authors): Optical depth calculations are approximate  
  > "the values of the optical depths are only approximations" (4.3 The coexistence of masers and dasars)
- Limitation (authors): Higher spatial resolution is needed to determine the real structure  
  > "The determination of the real structure of the recombination line region needs observation with higher spatial resolution" (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Three-Gaussian H50beta model is noisy and unconstrained by stated priors; dasar residuals depend directly on it
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dasar interpretation relies on one model (Strelnitski et al. 1996) and on b_n values that omit continuum and background fields
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Band 3 and band 6 were observed 9 months apart, so variability between epochs could affect LTE scaling and the 93-224 GHz spectral index
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux scale uncertainty and calibration details are not given, yet the spectral index and LTE-based residual amplitudes depend on them
