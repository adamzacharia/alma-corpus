## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, agn_and_quasars, astrochemistry, star_formation
- Question: Investigate the properties of dense molecular gas tracers (HCN, HCO+, and HNC J=2-1) and 183 GHz H2O 3(1,3)-2(2,0) lines in the nuclei of ten nearby (ultra)luminous infrared galaxies with different levels of AGN energetic contributions.  
  > "We conducted ALMA band 4–5 ($\sim$2 mm) observations of nine ultraluminous infrared galaxies (ULIRGs; L$_{\rm IR}$ $>$ 10$^{12}$L$_{\odot}$) at $z =$ 0.04–0.14, and one starburst-dominated luminous infrared galaxy, NGC 1614 (LIRG; L$_{\rm IR}$ $=$ 10$^{11.7}$L$_{\odot}$) at $z =$ 0.016, with the aim of investigating the properties of dense molecular gas tracers (HCN, HCO$^{+}$, and HNC J=2–1) and 183 GHz H$_{2}$O 3$_{1,3}$–2$_{2,0}$ lines." (Summary); "In short, we selected nearby (U)LIRGs that (1) had different levels of AGN’s energetic contributions to the bolometric luminosity, based on optical/infrared/(sub)millimeter spectroscopic energy diagnostic methods and (2) were expected to show bright molecular emission lines, to investigate their flux ratios with small statistical uncertainty." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum subtraction and imaging of continuum and spectral line cubes**: Determine continuum level using line-free channels, subtract continuum in uv-plane with uvcontsub, and image line cubes (binned by 20 channels for ULIRGs and 10 channels for NGC 1614) and line-free continuum with CASA clean.  
  > "We determined the continuum level using channels that did not contain discernible emission lines, and subtracted it using the CASA task “uvcontsub”." (Observations and Data Analysis); "We then applied the CASA task “clean” (Briggs weighting; robust $=$ 0.5 and gain $=$ 0.1) to create maps of the continuum-subtracted molecular line data, by binning 20 channels for all ULIRGs (velocity resolution $\sim$ 35 km s$^{-1}$)." (Observations and Data Analysis); "In addition, we created cleaned continuum maps by integrating the line-free-channels’ data." (Observations and Data Analysis)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, clean
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5, gain = 0.1; 20-channel binning (~35 km/s) for ULIRGs, 10-channel binning (~20 km/s) for NGC 1614; pixel scale 0.025''-0.1'' pixel^-1  
  > "We then applied the CASA task “clean” (Briggs weighting; robust $=$ 0.5 and gain $=$ 0.1) to create maps of the continuum-subtracted molecular line data, by binning 20 channels for all ULIRGs (velocity resolution $\sim$ 35 km s$^{-1}$)." (Observations and Data Analysis); "The pixel scales were set as 0.025$''$–0.1$''$ pixel$^{-1}$, which were less than one-fifth of the synthesized beam size of each data." (Observations and Data Analysis)
   - assumption: <=10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty  
  > "We adopted $\lesssim$10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty for our band 4–5 data, based on the ALMA Cycle 5 Proposer’s Guide" (Observations and Data Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Minimum baseline of 15 m gives MRS > 10'' (>8 kpc for ULIRGs, >3 kpc for NGC 1614), fully recovering compact nuclear emission  
  > "the maximum recoverable scale (MRS) is $>$10$''$ at $\sim$2 mm for the minimum baseline of 15 m (Table 2), which corresponds to $>$8 kpc for all the ULIRGs and $>$3 kpc for the LIRG NGC 1614." (Results)
2. **Create moment 0, 1, and 2 maps and measure deconvolved emission sizes**: Integrate signals in channels with significant line detection to create moment 0, moment 1, and moment 2 maps at original beam resolution, and measure deconvolved intrinsic emission sizes on moment 0 and continuum maps using CASA task imfit.  
  > "Integrated intensity (moment 0) maps of the HCN J=2–1, HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1, HNC J=2–1 and 183 GHz H$_{2}$O lines are created by integrating signals in channels with significant line detection, and the peak emission flux as well as detection significance is tabulated in Table 4." (Results); "Figure 4 shows the intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment 1) maps of the HCN J=2–1, HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1, HNC J=2–1, and 183 GHz H$_{2}$O emission lines whenever they are detected with significantly high S/N ratios to obtain meaningful velocity information (Table 4)." (Results); "We also (3) compare the measured emission size of the H$_{2}$O with those of other dense molecular gas tracers, by applying the CASA task “imfit” to the original beam moment 0 maps (Figure 1)." (Compactness)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian deconvolution via CASA imfit  
  > "Cols.(2)–(7): Deconvolved size in milli-acrsecond (mas) in the original beam moment 0 map (Figure 1) derived from the CASA task “imfit”." (Table 8)
   - validation: Confirm spatial coincidence of molecular line peak with continuum peak within beam-size/(S/N)  
  > "These moment 0 maps of the original beam are primarily used for (a) verification of significant detection at the peak position of the molecular line and (b) confirming its spatial coincidence with continuum peak within peak determination uncertainty (= beam-size/signal-to-noise \[S/N\] ratio)." (Table 4)
3. **Smooth cubes to matched physical scales and extract nuclear spectra**: Convolve data cubes to a common physical beam size (~1 kpc: 1.0, 1.2, or 1.6 kpc for ULIRGs; 200 pc and 1 kpc for NGC 1614; and 2 kpc for ULIRGs) using CASA task imsmooth, and extract spectra at continuum peak positions.  
  > "We modify the beam to 1 kpc for all molecular lines in ULIRGs if their beam sizes are smaller than 1 kpc, using the CASA task “imsmooth”, and then extract 1 kpc beam-sized spectra, which are overplotted as red dotted lines in Figure 3." (Results); "For the LIRG NGC 1614, we modify the beam size of all molecular lines to 200 pc and extracted 200pc-beam spectra at four bright continuum peak positions, which are shown as red dotted lines in Figure 2a–h." (Results)
   - software: CASA, imsmooth
   - parameter: Target beam sizes: 1 kpc (or 1.2 kpc for IRAS 12112+0305 and IRAS 15250+3609; 1.6 kpc for IRAS 06035-7102), 200 pc and 1 kpc for NGC 1614, and 2 kpc for ULIRGs  
  > "This is the case for all ULIRGs except IRAS 06035$-$7102, IRAS 12112$+$0305, and IRAS 15250$+$3609, for which we set the beam size to 1.6 kpc, 1.2 kpc, and 1.2 kpc, respectively, because the beam size of some lines is larger than 1 kpc." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare ~1 kpc and 2 kpc beam fluxes to verify that the bulk of nuclear emission is recovered within ~1 kpc without adding excessive noise  
  > "We find that the flux increase from $\sim$1 kpc to 2kpc-beam data, is as high as $\sim$80% in the starburst-dominated ULIRG IRAS 13509$+$0442, but significantly smaller in the remaining ULIRGs (Appendix B)." (Results)
4. **Fit Gaussian profiles to spectral lines and compute line flux ratios**: Fit single or double Gaussian profiles to extracted spectra at original, ~1 kpc, 2 kpc, and 200 pc beams to measure velocity-integrated fluxes, and calculate flux ratios among HCN, HCO+, HNC J=2-1, 183 GHz H2O, HC3N, and CS lines.  
  > "Gaussian fits are applied to estimate the molecular emission line flux from nuclear $\sim$1 kpc regions in individual (U)LIRGs (and 200 pc regions for NGC 1614)." (Results); "However, some molecular emission lines in certain ULIRGs display double-peaked profiles with central dips and clearly deviate from a single Gaussian profile (e.g., HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 line of IRAS 12112$+$0305 NE in Figure 3e). For these, we apply two Gaussian fits (two emission components)." (Results)
   - fitting: Single or double Gaussian profile fitting (fixing peak or FWHM to best-fit value when needed for faint/blended lines)  
  > "We apply a single Gaussian fit as long as the observed emission line profile is approximated by a single peaked profile with small deviation." (Results); "$^{A}$ Fixed to the best fit value." (Table 6)
   - validation: For faint lines where Gaussian fit is <3sigma, check detection significance (>3sigma) in ~1kpc-beam moment 0 maps  
  > "Detection significance of this H$_{2}$O line in these two sources is $<$3$\sigma$ in the Gaussian fit in the 1kpc-beam spectra (Table 6), but $>$3$\sigma$ in the moment 0 maps." (Moment 0 Maps of the 183 GHz H$_{2}$O Emission Line for IRAS 12127$-$1412 and IRAS 13509$+$0442.)
5. **Derive line luminosities, nuclear dense molecular gas masses, and gas depletion times**: Convert ~1kpc-beam velocity-integrated line fluxes to L_line (L_sun) and L'_line (K km s^-1 pc^2) using Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005) equations, derive dense gas mass M_dense from HCO+ J=2-1 luminosity, and compute lower limits on gas depletion time t_dep = M_dense / SFR.  
  > "We use the following formulas to convert molecular emission line flux to luminosity (cite: sol05)," (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass); "Table 9 (column 6) summarizes the derived dense molecular gas mass in the observed (U)LIRGs’ nuclei, where we adopt the conversion factor of 2–5 (M$_{\odot}$ \[K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$\]$^{-1}$) from optically thick HCO$^{+}$ J=1–0 line luminosity to dense H$_{2}$ mass (cite: ler17), and the above validated assumption that HCO$^{+}$ emission is thermalized and optically thick at J=2–1 and J=1–0." (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass); "The estimated lower limit of the depletion time (t$_{\rm dep}$ $\equiv$ M$_{\rm dense}$/SFR) is $\sim$1–3 $\times$ 10$^{6}$ yr, which is shorter than that derived in dense molecular clouds in our Galaxy and nearby star-forming galaxies, $\sim$10$^{7-8}$ yr (cite: lad10; zha14; liu16; tan18; jia20)," (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass)
   - parameter: H0 = 71 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.27, Omega_Lambda = 0.73  
  > "We adopt the cosmological parameters, H$_{0}$ $=$ 71 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm M}$ = 0.27, and $\Omega_{\rm \Lambda}$ = 0.73, throughout this paper." (Introduction)
   - assumption: HCO+ emission is thermalized and optically thick at J=2-1 and J=1-0; conversion factor alpha_HCO+ = 2-5 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1; SFR (M_sun yr^-1) = 4.5 x 10^-44 L_IR (erg s^-1)  
  > "Table 9 (column 6) summarizes the derived dense molecular gas mass in the observed (U)LIRGs’ nuclei, where we adopt the conversion factor of 2–5 (M$_{\odot}$ \[K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$\]$^{-1}$) from optically thick HCO$^{+}$ J=1–0 line luminosity to dense H$_{2}$ mass (cite: ler17), and the above validated assumption that HCO$^{+}$ emission is thermalized and optically thick at J=2–1 and J=1–0." (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass); "We can estimate a lower limit of the depletion time (t$_{\rm dep}$) of the nuclear dense molecular gas by star-formation, by assuming (1) lower side of the derived mass in Table 9 (column 6) and (2) the nuclear star formation rate (SFR) of SFR (M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$) = 4.5 $\times$ 10$^{-44}$ L$_{\rm IR}$ (ergs s$^{-1}$) (cite: ken98), where infrared (8–1000 $\mu$m) and far-infrared (40–500 $\mu$m) luminosities are considered comparable for (U)LIRGs." (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of J=2-1 L_IR-L'_line relation with published J=4-3 relation confirms HCN and HCO+ are nearly thermally excited and optically thick  
  > "Our J=2–1 data roughly follow the updated J=4–3 relation by (cite: tan18). If the HCN and HCO$^{+}$ are almost thermally excited at up to J=4 and optically thick, the ratio of molecular line luminosity (in units of K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$) to infrared luminosity (in units of L$_{\odot}$) is expected to be comparable for J=4–3 and J=2–1." (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass)

## Qualitative findings

- Higher HCN to HCO+ J=2-1 flux ratios are observed in several, but not all, AGN-important ULIRGs compared to starburst-classified nuclei.  
  > "Higher HCN to HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 flux ratios are observed in some, but not all, AGN-important ULIRGs than in starburst-classified sources." (Abstract)
- 183 GHz H2O emission is detected in almost all AGN-important ULIRGs, but except for the Superantennae, it largely originates from the entire nuclear region (~1 kpc) rather than a compact (<<1 kpc) AGN megamaser.  
  > "We detect 183 GHz H$_{2}$O emission in almost all AGN-important ULIRGs, and elevated H$_{2}$O emission is found in two sources with elevated HCN J=2–1 emission, relative to HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1. Except one ULIRG (the Superantennae), the H$_{2}$O emission largely comes from the entire nuclear regions ($\sim$1 kpc), rather than AGN-origin megamaser at the very center ($<<$1 kpc)." (Abstract)
- Vibrationally excited HCN and HNC J=2-1 lines are detected in a few (U)LIRGs while HCO+-VIB J=2-1 is undetected in all, suggesting HCO+ and HNC are substantially less abundant than HCN in the innermost mid-infrared-irradiated regions.  
  > "Vibrationally excited J=2–1 emission lines of HCN and HNC are detected in a few (U)LIRGs, but those of HCO$^{+}$ are not. It is suggested that in mid-infrared-radiation-exposed innermost regions around energy sources, HCO$^{+}$ and HNC are substantially less abundant than HCN." (Abstract)
- Two serendipitous ~2 mm continuum sources are detected within ~10'' of the target ULIRGs, interpreted as an infrared luminous dusty galaxy at z > 1 (near IRAS 13509+0442) and a blazar (near the Superantennae).  
  > "In our ALMA $\sim$2 mm data of ten (U)LIRGs, two continuum sources are serendipitously detected within $\sim$10$''$, which are likely to be an infrared luminous dusty galaxy at $z >$ 1 and a blazar." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Submillimeter/millimeter wavelengths suffer <=1/20 the dust extinction of hard X-rays and mid-IR; ALMA Band 4/5 covers HCN, HCO+, HNC J=2-1 (lower excitation than J=3-2/4-3) and 183 GHz H2O simultaneously at sub-arcsecond (<~1 kpc) resolution for z > 0.02 (U)LIRGs where J=1-0 is redshifted below Band 3.  
  > "(Sub)millimeter at 0.8–3.5 mm is one such wavelengths, with $\lesssim$1/20 extinction effects than hard X-rays at $\sim$10 keV and infrared $\sim$20 $\mu$m (cite: hil83)." (Introduction); "Because the excitation energy level is lower at J=2 than at J=3 or J=4, we can (1) discuss dense molecular line flux ratios at J=2–1 less affected by uncertainty of excitation conditions than at J=3–2 and J=4–3, and (2) J=2–1 lines of HCN, HCO$^{+}$, and HNC may be more reliable dense molecular gas mass tracers," (Introduction); "Although sensitive observations of the 183 GHz H$_{2}$O lines are difficult for very nearby sources at $z \sim$ 0 owing to the poor atmospheric transmission of Earth at $\sim$183 GHz, we can observe this line without severe Earth’s atmospheric H$_{2}$O absorption for (U)LIRGs at $z >$ 0.02, rendering this H$_{2}$O line as another potentially good AGN indicator." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Smoothing cubes to a matched ~1 kpc physical beam enables direct comparison across galaxies at different redshifts while isolating energetically dominant nuclei from extended (>a few kpc) host-galaxy starbursts; HCO+ J=2-1 is used for dense gas mass because its L_IR slope is closest to unity and it is less biased by AGN chemistry.  
  > "Since probed physical scale in the original beam spectra is largely different among different (U)LIRGs (Table 3), we compare molecular emission line fluxes with the same physical scale for further discussion. We choose 1 kpc, because we can investigate dense molecular emission line properties at energetically dominant ULIRGs’ nuclei (cite: soi00; dia10; ima11; per21), with minimum contaminations from spatially extended ($\gtrsim$a few kpc) star-formation in the host galaxies." (Results); "We derive nuclear dense molecular gas mass from HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 luminosity, because (1) the slope of infrared and HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 luminosity relation is the closest to unity among the four lines (Figure 9), and (2) HCO$^{+}$ can be a less biased dense molecular gas mass tracer than HCN and HNC, particularly in the vicinity of a luminous AGN (cite: ima20)." (Emission Line Luminosity and Nuclear Dense Molecular Gas Mass)
- Alternative: Using a single Gaussian fit vs. two Gaussian fits for double-peaked emission lines with central dips: adopted two Gaussian fits when they better trace the profile.  
  > "However, some molecular emission lines in certain ULIRGs display double-peaked profiles with central dips and clearly deviate from a single Gaussian profile (e.g., HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 line of IRAS 12112$+$0305 NE in Figure 3e). For these, we apply two Gaussian fits (two emission components)." (Results)
- Alternative: Explaining the smaller flux increase of H2O vs HNC J=2-1 from original to ~1 kpc beam solely by compact H2O emission vs. central suppression of HNC abundance: tested by comparing H2O with HCN and HCO+ J=2-1 from ~1 kpc to 2 kpc beam.  
  > "However, as HNC abundance can be low in AGN-illuminated warm molecular gas at the center of a galaxy nucleus (cite: ima20), it may be possible that the observed higher HNC J=2–1 flux increase is simply due to the suppression of HNC J=2–1 emission at the innermost region, rather than the presence of compact H$_{2}$O emission. To overcome this ambiguity, we combine the H$_{2}$O data with the HCN and HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 line data as well (different synthesized beams from H$_{2}$O)" (Compactness)
- Alternative: Identifying the 156.34 GHz line in the serendipitous northern source of IRAS 13509+0442 as CO J=2-1 at z ~ 0.47: rejected in favor of higher-J CO (J=3-2 to 6-5 at z = 1-4) due to non-detection of CO J=3-2 and 4-3 in Band 6/7 and lack of optical/NIR counterparts.  
  > "If we assume that this emission line signature originates from a bright CO J=2–1 ($\nu_{\rm rest}$ = 230.538 GHz) line, then the redshift of this source becomes $z \sim$ 0.47." (Serendipitously Detected Continuum Sources); "The non-detection of optical and near-infrared counterparts at the position of this northern continuum source (cite: kim02; ima18b) also makes it unlikely that this source is only at $z \sim$ 0.47." (Serendipitously Detected Continuum Sources)
- How they decided it was enough: Comparing ~1 kpc and 2 kpc beam spectra showed that most ULIRGs have small flux increases beyond ~1 kpc, whereas increasing the beam size further adds substantial noise and hampers faint line detection.  
  > "Namely, we recover the bulk of ULIRGs’ nuclear dense molecular and 183 GHz H$_{2}$O line emission in the $\sim$1kpc-beam data. A further increase of the beam size does not significantly increase dense molecular emission line signals, but increases noise a lot, making the detection of faint emission lines difficult." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): The sample of ten (U)LIRGs is neither statistically unbiased nor complete.  
  > "Although our sample is not statistically unbiased, nor complete, it can provide useful information on the possible variation of molecular line flux ratios, depending on different AGN’s energetic contributions." (Targets)
- Limitation (authors): One sideband in some Band 5 observations fell into the 182-184.5 GHz terrestrial H2O atmospheric absorption band and was completely flagged by the pipeline.  
  > "Although the targeted molecular lines of our sources were confirmed to be not strongly affected by the Earth’s atmospheric absorption, the other sideband in some sources fell into the frequency of low Earth’s atmospheric transmission. These data were completely flagged by ALMA pipeline processes and not used for our analysis." (Observations and Data Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Deconvolved intrinsic sizes from imfit cannot be reliably constrained when emission is a factor of >>2-3 smaller than the synthesized beam, or when S/N is limited.  
  > "Because it is very difficult to constrain the deconvolved intrinsic size to be considerably (a factor of $>>$2–3) smaller than the beam size (Table 3, column 6), in the case of extremely compact emission, the derived value can still be larger than the actual value." (Table 8)
- Limitation (authors): Outer regions of 183 GHz H2O emission are not sufficiently probed in moment 1 maps due to fainter H2O flux compared to HCN, HCO+, and HNC.  
  > "However, since H$_{2}$O emission is fainter than the other dense molecular lines and outer regions are not sufficiently probed in the moment 1 maps (Figure 4), further discussion of the possible dynamical difference is difficult." (Dynamics)
- Limitation (authors): HCN-VIB J=2-1 fluxes in IRAS 12112+0305 NE and IRAS 22491-1808 are only approximate due to blending with the nearby bright HCO+ J=2-1 line.  
  > "These values are only approximate for the HCN-VIB J=2–1 emission line flux, because we choose the integrated frequency range to minimize the contaminations from the nearby bright HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 emission line" (Table 7 notes)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-transition (J=2-1) line ratios cannot uniquely separate chemical abundance variations from excitation/density effects without multi-transition non-LTE radiative transfer modeling (deferred to a companion paper).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Infrared luminosities used to compute star-formation rates and gas depletion times are measured from large (>30'') IRAS apertures and include unknown fractional AGN contributions, making SFR and t_dep comparison with 1 kpc ALMA beams systematically uncertain.
