## Analysis recipe

1. **Combine 12 m and 7 m visibilities and image 1.3 mm continuum**: Combine 12 m and 7 m datasets with CASA task CONCAT, regrid single polarization (XX) continuum to 9 MHz channels, and clean line-free channels with TCLEAN (both 12 m + 7 m combined and 7 m alone); estimate recovered flux fraction relative to ATLASGAL 870 um.  
  > "The 12 m main array and 7 m ACA data are combined by CASA task CONCAT in order to create the 12 m + 7 m array combined continuum images (referred to as combined images or combined continuum, hereafter)." (3.2. ALMA data reduction); "The CASA task TCLEAN is then performed on the line-free channels to finally obtain the cleaned images." (3.2. ALMA data reduction); "Assuming a dust emissivity spectral index β ≃ 1.5, we estimate the missed 1.3 mm flux in 7 m + 12 m array observations by comparing with single-dish ATLASGAL 870 µm flux in the ALMA imaged field." (3.2. ALMA data reduction)
   - software: CASA, CONCAT, TCLEAN, CASA Analysis Utilities
   - parameter: cell = 0.3" (combined) and 1" (7 m); robust = 0.5; clean threshold = 0.4 mJy Beam^-1 (combined) and 4 mJy Beam^-1 (7 m); field down to 20% primary beam power point  
  > "The pixel size “cell” in the cleaned image is set to be 0.3 ′′, which is about one fifth of the synthesized beam ≃ 1.3 ′′ ." (3.2. ALMA data reduction); "The Briggs weighting with a robust of 0.5 is chosen. The threshold for stopping clean process is set to be 0.4 mJy Beam −1, about 2 to 3 rms noise." (3.2. ALMA data reduction); "The pixel size and threshold for stopping clean process in task TCLEAN are set to be 1 ′′ and 4 mJy Beam −1, respectively." (3.2. ALMA data reduction)
   - assumption: Dust emissivity spectral index beta ~ 1.5 for extrapolating ATLASGAL 870 um flux to 1.3 mm  
  > "Assuming a dust emissivity spectral index β ≃ 1.5, we estimate the missed 1.3 mm flux in 7 m + 12 m array observations by comparing with single-dish ATLASGAL 870 µm flux in the ALMA imaged field." (3.2. ALMA data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: For AS1 (which had higher combined rms), imaged 12 m-only dataset to verify that the number of detected cores did not change at lower rms; extended AS2 field of view to 10% primary beam power point to check for missed outer cores.  
  > "We image AS1 continuum with only 12 m array dataset, resulting in an image rms of 0.2–0.3 mJy Beam −1, which is better than combined image of AS1 (0.55 mJy Beam −1 ) and close to combined images of other candidate HMSCs." (4.5. Diversity of fragmentation); "Using the same Astrodendro parameter, the number of identified cores in AS1 12 m image is the same as the one in the combined image." (4.5. ## Qualitative findings

- Within the sample of eight clumps, there is no clear difference in the ~0.025 pc-scale core mass and separation distributions between H II-region-impacted (AS) and non-impacted (NA) candidate HMSCs.  
  > "We did not find a clear difference in the fragmented ALMA core mass and separations between AS HMSCs and NA HMSCs in our limited sample, although H ii regions seem to affect the morphology of the cores’ spatial distribution." (7. Conclusion)
- Fragmentation in both AS and NA candidate HMSCs is dominated by two-level hierarchical thermal Jeans fragmentation, where clumps fragment into ensembles at ~lambda_J,clump^th and ensembles sub-fragment into cores at ~0.3 lambda_J,clump^th (~1 to 1.5 lambda_J,ens^th).  
  > "Both types of candidate HMSCs present a thermal fragmentation with two-level hierarchical features at the clump thermal Jeans length λ th J,clump and 0.3λ th J,clump ." (abstract); "As a result, the early-stage fragmentation in the high-mass clumps probably takes place in a hierarchical fashion: clumps fragment into ensembles with separations around clump thermal Jeans length" (Sect. 4.3 Hierarchical fragmentation shown by 7 m data)
- No high-mass prestellar cores are found in the sample; the most massive cores (> 8 M_sun) show star-forming signposts such as outflows, shocked/warm gas tracers, or IR point sources.  
  > "The most massive cores ( M core 8 M ⊙ ) in our candidate HMSCs commonly present star-forming signposts, implying that there is no high-mass prestellar core in our sample." (4.5. Diversity of fragmentation)
- H II regions shape the spatial distribution of dense gas and dust at ~0.025 pc scale in deeply impacted clumps (cometary globule morphology in AS1 and wall-like core alignment along the ionization front in AS2) and likely assist or induce star formation.  
  > "ALMA continuum reveals that AS1 presents a cometary globular morphology meanwhile the fragmented cores in AS2 show a spatial distribution aligned with the ionization front." (7. Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Public ALMA Cycle 4 Band 6 (12 m + 7 m) continuum observations from project 2016.1.01346.S provide ~1.3" (~0.025 pc) resolution for four H II-impacted candidate HMSCs from Paper I and allow pairing with four non-impacted HMSCs of matched mass, column density, size, ellipticity, and distance.  
  > "We cross-matched candidate HMSCs in Paper I and sources for which their ALMA data are public. A total of four AS candidate HMSCs impacted by H ii regions were extracted." (2.1. Sample selection); "To limit the bias and independent variables, these NA are required to have the most similar single-dish derived clump mass ( M clump ), column density ( N H 2 ), size (FWHM), ellipticity ( e ), and distance ( D ) compared to AS counterparts in the same ALMA project." (2.1. Sample selection)
- Why this method: Astrodendrograms capture hierarchical structures across scales, Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) robustly detects characteristic fragmentation scales, and combining core mass and separation comparisons mitigates individual uncertainties from projection effects and core dust temperatures.  
  > "tree diagram to describe the hierarchical structures over a range of scales." (Sect. 3.3 Core extraction and physical properties); "Both core mass and separation estimations have pros and cons and therefore we analyze the fragmentation by combining both M J th and λ th J to attain a more consistent conclusion." (4.1. Calculation); "In particular, Clarke et al. (2019) have demonstrated that MST is able to robustly detect the characteristic fragmentation scales." (4.1. Calculation)
- Alternative: Turbulent Jeans fragmentation (both total support and supersonic compressible flow models) was tested and rejected for most clumps because observed ensemble/core masses and separations are much smaller than turbulent Jeans predictions, and compressible flow Jeans lengths are too small.  
  > "The turbulent fragmentation model is inappropriate for most HMSCs because both ensemble mass and separations are significantly smaller than M J tot,clump and λ tot J,clump ." (4.3. Hierarchical fragmentation shown by 7 m data); "fragmentation model is inappropriate for most HMSCs because both ensemble mass and separations are significantly smaller than" (Sect. 4.3 Hierarchical fragmentation shown by 7 m data)
- Alternative: Collect-and-collapse (C&C) formation of clump AS1 was tested and rejected because the theoretical shell fragmentation timescale (~0.9 Myr) exceeds the estimated age of bubble N1 (~0.4 Myr).  
  > "The t frag is larger than bubble age ≃ 0.4 Myr, revealing that AS1 is a pre-existing clump rather than a C&C triggered clump." (5.2. Shaping structures in candidate HMSCs at ~ 0.025 pc scale)
- Alternative: Unimodal distribution of scaled core separations across 31 candidate HMSCs was tested and rejected using Hartigan's Dip test and ACR multimodal testing in favor of a bimodal distribution.  
  > "by applying Hartigan’s Dip test (Hartigan & Hartigan 1985). Then we test multimodal distributions by ACR methods" (Sect. 6 Discussion)
