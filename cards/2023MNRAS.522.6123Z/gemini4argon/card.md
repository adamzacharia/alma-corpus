## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image VLA 44 GHz and ALMA 237 GHz continuum data and align images**: Calibrate raw data using CASA pipelines, flag CO(6-5) line and atmospheric absorption channels in ALMA data, compute dirty image RMS, clean down to 3 sigma using hogbom and Briggs robust +0.5 in tclean, and overlay contours in CARTA (shifting VLA 8.2 GHz WCS to match the VLA 44 GHz SE peak pixel).  
  > "Prior to imaging the continuum, we flagged the channels with CO(6-5) line emission, as well as pseudo-lines due to the severe atmospheric absorption. Next, we created a dirty image without any clean to calculate the root-mean-square noise $(\sigma)$ under the ’briggs’ weighting with a robustness parameter +0.5. Then, we cleaned the image non-interactively by setting $3\sigma$ as the stop threshold of the cleaning." (ALMA Band 6); "Then the contours from different observations are overlaid on false-color images based on their WCS using Cube Analysis and Rendering Tool for Astronomy (cite: angus_comrie_2021_4905459)." (Alignment); "We, therefore, take the peak pixel of the SE hot spot observed in VLA A-configuration at 44 GHz as the reference to shift the WCS of VLA 8.2 GHz such that they match." (Alignment)
   - software: CASA, tclean, CARTA
   - parameter: robustness parameter +0.5; clean stop threshold 3 sigma; hogbom deconvolver  
  > "For all the observations, using the ’hogbom’ deconvolution algorithm, we imaged the data choosing the ’briggs’ weighting with a robustness parameter +0.5, and put a mask on the strongest signal to avoid artefacts." (VLA Band Q); "Then, we cleaned the image non-interactively by setting $3\sigma$ as the stop threshold of the cleaning." (ALMA Band 6)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked RA and DEC positional offsets of the SE peak pixel between ALMA Cycle 6 and VLA 44 GHz BnA- and A-configurations.  
  > "Taking the SE peak pixel as the reference, the ALMA Cycle 6 has an offset of $0.025\ensuremath{''}$ between VLA 44 GHz in BnA-configuration along RA, and an offset of $0.011\ensuremath{''}$ between VLA 44 GHz in A-configuration along RA." (Alignment)
2. **Measure component flux densities, deconvolved hotspot size, spectral indices, and radio luminosities/powers**: Integrate flux densities over specified apertures for NW, SE, and Core components, fit a single Gaussian component to the image plane to measure the deconvolved size of the SE hotspot, and calculate spectral indices, spectral luminosities, and radio powers.  
  > "Column (3): Integrated flux densities over a certain region." (Table 2); "Column (5): the spectral luminosity $L_\nu=S_\mathrm{obs}4\pi D^2_L/(1+z)^{1+\alpha}$, where $S_\mathrm{obs}$ is the observed integrated flux densities and $D_L$ is the luminosity distance. Column (6): the radio power $P_{\nu_0}=4\pi D^2_L(1+z)^{-\alpha-1}S_{\nu_0}\nu_0$ (cite: 2010ApJ...720.1066C)." (Table 2); "By fitting a single Gaussian component to the image plane, the size of the SE hotspot deconvolved from the beam is $(0.054\ensuremath{''}\pm0.015\ensuremath{''})\times(0.033\ensuremath{''}\pm0.01\ensuremath{''})$" (Compact-Steep-Source)
   - parameter: Apertures: 0.4'' x 0.2'' (NW at 44 GHz), 0.5'' x 0.4'' (SE at 44 GHz), 0.07'' x 0.12'' (SE at 237 GHz), 0.4'' x 0.3'' (Core at 44 GHz)  
  > "| $44\times10^3$ | NW | $0.29\pm0.07$ | $-1.35\pm0.16$ | $(1.2\pm0.3)\times10^{25}$ | $(5.1\pm1.5)\times10^{42}$ | $0.4\ensuremath{''}\times0.2\ensuremath{''}$ | 4 |" (Table 2); "| $44\times10^3$ | SE | $1.88\pm0.09$ | $-1.87\pm0.07$ | $(1.3\pm0.1)\times10^{26}$ | $(5.9\pm0.3)\times10^{43}$ | $0.5\ensuremath{''}\times0.4\ensuremath{''}$ | 4 |" (Table 2); "| $237\times10^3$ | SE | $0.12\pm0.01$ | $-1.64\pm0.06$ | $(6.6\pm0.7)\times10^{24}$ | $(1.6\pm0.2)\times10^{43}$ | $0.07\ensuremath{''}\times0.12\ensuremath{''}$ | 4 |" (Table 2)
   - assumption: At 4.7 and 8.2 GHz, peak flux densities equal integrated flux densities (S_peak = S_total); at 237 GHz, SE sub-component is dominated by AGN synchrotron radiation; 3 sigma upper limit assumed for NW at 237 GHz.  
  > "At 4.7 and 8.2 GHz, we treated the peak flux densities of NW and SE hotspots as the integrated flux densities, i.e., $S_\mathrm{peak}=S_\mathrm{total}$, since both NW and SE hotspots are marginally resolved" (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED)); "we treat this sub-component as dominated by synchrotron radiation related to AGN activities throughout this work." (Spatial Distribution); "We assumed an upper limit of the NW component based on $3\sigma$ level to estimate the flux ratio at 237 GHz, which gives $S\mathrm{_{NW,237GHz}=0.0194\ mJy}$." (Bi-modal Flux Ratios)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian component fitted to the image plane for SE hotspot size  
  > "By fitting a single Gaussian component to the image plane, the size of the SE hotspot deconvolved from the beam is $(0.054\ensuremath{''}\pm0.015\ensuremath{''})\times(0.033\ensuremath{''}\pm0.01\ensuremath{''})$" (Compact-Steep-Source)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit low-frequency curved SED and classify as Compact-Steep-Spectrum (CSS) source**: Fit observed total flux densities at <= 8.2 GHz with a curved power-law model S_nu = a * nu^alpha * exp(q * (ln nu)^2) and compare the derived turnover frequency with the empirical size-turnover frequency relation for CSS sources.  
  > "To better investigate the spectrum as a curve, we fit the observed flux densities at frequencies $\leq8.2$ GHz by a curved power-law model given by (cite: 2017ApJ...836..174C) $$\begin{equation}
    S_\nu=a\nu^\alpha e^{q(\ln\nu)^2},
\end{equation}$$" (Compact-Steep-Source); "Using an empirical relation that correlates the projected angular size ($l$ in kpc) and $\nu_\mathrm{peak}$ (in GHz) for CSS sources (cite: 1997AJ....113..148O), $$\begin{equation}
    \log \nu_\mathrm{peak}=-0.21(\pm0.05)-0.65(\pm0.05)\times\log l,
\end{equation}$$ the estimated turnover frequency is $116\pm20$ MHz for $l\sim13$ kpc." (Compact-Steep-Source)
   - parameter: Frequencies <= 8.2 GHz; projected linear size l ~ 13 kpc  
  > "To better investigate the spectrum as a curve, we fit the observed flux densities at frequencies $\leq8.2$ GHz by a curved power-law model" (Compact-Steep-Source); "Since the projected linear separation of the two hotspots is only $\sim1.5\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim13$ kpc at $z=1.92$), the Dragonfly galaxy can be classified as a CSS source" (Compact-Steep-Source)
   - assumption: Observations at <= 1.4 GHz represent the sum of all three components (core, SE, NW)  
  > "All observations at $\leq1.4$ GHz have no resolved components, thus the integrated flux densities of these observations are simply treated as the sum of all three components." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared curved power-law turnover frequency (~50-60 MHz) with empirical CSS correlation estimate (116 +/- 20 MHz).  
  > "The corresponding peaked frequency is $\nu_\mathrm{peak}=e^{-\alpha/2q}\sim50-60$ MHz." (Compact-Steep-Source); "the estimated turnover frequency is $116\pm20$ MHz for $l\sim13$ kpc. This estimation is close to the value given by the curved power-law" (Compact-Steep-Source)
4. **Fit synchrotron ageing models and derive equipartition magnetic fields and synchrotron ages**: Fit the radio SEDs of NW and SE hotspots (>= 147 MHz) using SYNCHROFIT with continuous injection (CI), JP, and KP models to obtain break frequency nu_br, injection index s (alpha_inj = (1-s)/2), and remnant fraction T; compute equipartition magnetic field B_eq and synchrotron age t_s.  
  > "We fit the radio SED of NW and SE hotspots using SYNCHROFIT to estimate the break frequency $\nu_\mathrm{br}$, above which the synchrotron radiation ages, and injection index $s$ that reflects the initial power-law of the electron energy distribution (cite: 2018MNRAS.474.3361T; 2018MNRAS.476.2522T)." (Synchrotron Fitting); "The synchrotron age, i.e., the time-scale during which the continuous acceleration lasts, is calculated by (cite: 2018MNRAS.474.3361T; 2020A&A...634A...9M): $$\begin{equation}
    {t}_\mathrm{s}=1610\frac{B_\mathrm{eq}^{1/2}}{B_\mathrm{eq}^2+B_\mathrm{CMB}^2}\frac{1}{\nu_\mathrm{br}(1+z)^{1/2}},
\end{equation}$$" (Fitting Results); "The estimation of $B_\text{eq}$ in an energy equipartition condition can be calculated using (cite: 1980ARA&A..18..165M; 2020A&A...634A...9M): $$\begin{equation}
    B_\mathrm{eq}=9.6\times10^{-12}\left(\frac{P_\text{1.4GHz}(1+k)}{V^3}\right)^{2/7},
\end{equation}$$" (Fitting Results)
   - software: SYNCHROFIT
   - parameter: Frequencies >= 147 MHz; proton-to-electron energy ratio k = 1; Doppler boosting factor delta = 2-4; B_CMB = 3.25(1+z)^2 uG  
  > "We only present the fitting results using observational data at $\geq147$ MHz here" (Synchrotron Fitting); "*k* is the protons to electrons energy ratio in the hotspot and set as 1, and *V* is the hotspot volume in kpc$^3$." (Fitting Results); "The estimated $B_\text{eq}$, taking a Doppler boosting factor $\delta=2-4$ (see the discussion in §[\[jet-ism\]](#jet-ism)), is $175-213\ \mu$G for the SE and $214-261\ \mu$G for the NW hotspot, respectively." (Fitting Results)
   - assumption: Unresolved flux densities at <= 1.4 GHz emanate only from NW and SE hotspots with flux ratio S_SE : S_NW = 15 : 1; hotspot occupies an elliptical cylinder volume.

## Qualitative findings

- A previously undetected radio core is identified in VLA 44 GHz BnA-configuration observations, coinciding with the NW galaxy and lying on the line connecting the SE and NW hotspots.  
  > "The new observations confirm the South-East (SE) and North-West (NW) hotspots identified by previous low-resolution VLA observations at 4.7 and 8.2 GHz and identify a radio core undetected in all previous observations." (Abstract); "This source also lies on the dashed line that links the double hotspots, suggesting that it can be the radio core – the launch point of the bipolar radio jets – of the RLAGN." (Spatial Distribution)
- ALMA Band 6 (237 GHz) continuum imaging detects a sub-component offset by ~1 kpc from the SE galaxy that coincides with the SE radio hotspot.  
  > "One serendipitous and interesting finding is a sub-component adjacent to the SE galaxy through the ALMA Band 6 observation (see panel (SE) in Fig. [1](#fig:imaging)). This sub-component has its location perfectly coincided with the SE hotspot" (Spatial Distribution); "ALMA Band 6 observation catches a sub-component offset from the SE galaxy by $\sim1$ kpc and coincided perfectly with the location of the SE hotspot." (Conclusion)
- The Dragonfly galaxy is classified as a young Compact-Steep-Spectrum (CSS) source experiencing transient or intermittent AGN activity.  
  > "Based on the CI model, the synchrotron ages of NW and SE hotspots have an order of $10^5$ yr, consistent with the order of magnitude $10^3 - 10^5$ yr observed in CSS sources associated with radio AGNs at an early evolution stage. The CI model also favors the scenario in which the double hotspots have experienced a quiescent phase, suggesting that this RLAGN may have transient or intermittent activities." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution and high-frequency observations are needed to resolve the radio core and hotspots, measure the high-frequency synchrotron spectral behavior above the break frequency, and determine the synchrotron age and jet geometry of high-redshift CSS sources.  
  > "Lacking high-resolution and high-frequency observations, their structure and evolution stages are not well understood at high redshifts." (Abstract); "The high-frequency behavior of the synchrotron radiation is crucial to investigate the evolution of the radio AGN, especially when the radio jets launched by the AGN have interacted with their ambient medium." (Introduction)
- Why this method: The continuous injection (CI) model in SYNCHROFIT is chosen because radio jets continuously accelerate electrons in situ in hotspots, and the CI model yields a reduced-chi^2 at least three times smaller than the JP and KP models.  
  > "Previous studies have shown that the injection spectrum of a radio hotspot is better described by the CI model, which is natural considering the radio jet continuously accelerates the electrons in situ (cite: 1991ApJ...383..554C; 2011A&A...526A.148M; 2020A&A...634A...9M). Additionally, the reduced-$\chi^2$ of the JP and KP models is larger than that of the CI model by at least a factor of three." (Synchrotron Fitting)
- Alternative: Inverse-Compton (IC) losses off the CMB as the dominant high-frequency energy loss mechanism: rejected because the equipartition magnetic field B_eq (>= 160 uG) is much higher than B_CMB.  
  > "However, in the SE hotspot, adopting a lower limit of the magnetic field in energy equipartition $B\mathrm{_{eq}}$, (cite: 2000AAS..145..121P) gives $B\mathrm{_{eq}}=160\ \mu$G, which is much higher than the expected $B_\mathrm{CMB}$ (see §[\[sec:fitting\]](#sec:fitting)). Therefore, for the Dragonfly galaxy, the dominated energy loss at high frequency should be explained by synchrotron ageing" (Synchrotron Ageing)
- Alternative: Jaffe-Perola (JP) and Kardashev-Pacholczyk (KP) single-burst synchrotron ageing models, as well as the continuous injection (CI) model with zero remnant fraction (T = 0): rejected because JP/KP have reduced-chi^2 at least 3 times larger than CI, and T = 0 cannot reproduce the observed SED curvature.  
  > "Additionally, the reduced-$\chi^2$ of the JP and KP models is larger than that of the CI model by at least a factor of three. Furthermore, we have performed fitting by fixing $T=0$, assuming that there is no quiescent phase during the jet-medium interaction. However, as clearly shown in Fig. [\[fig:synchrofit\]](#fig:synchrofit), with or without ALMA 237 GHz data, the observed curvature of the SED of the SE hotspot cannot be reproduced by the algorithm in $T=0$ cases, and this scenario can be rejected." (Synchrotron Fitting)
- Alternative: Confinement of the radio jets by the dense ISM of the host (NW) galaxy to explain the compact CSS size: rejected because the jets have already left the NW host galaxy.  
  > "In the Dragonfly, since the jet has obviously left its host galaxy, i.e., the NW galaxy, and interacted with the ambient medium to create the hotspots, confinement by the host galaxy is not favored." (Compact-Steep-Source)
- Alternative: Star formation (supernova remnants and HII regions) or the radio core contaminating the 4.7 and 8.2 GHz flux densities to cause the bi-modal flux ratio (15:1 vs 6:1): rejected because star formation contributes <= 0.11 mJy and the core contributes <= 0.7 mJy, orders of magnitude too small.  
  > "The resulting $S_{\nu,\text{non-thermal}}\sim 0.07$ and $S_{\nu,\text{thermal}}\sim 0.11$ mJy are smaller than the observed flux density by more than two orders of magnitude. Hence this scenario can be undoubtedly rejected." (Bi-modal Flux Ratios); "Radio core is the second possibility, but can be immediately rejected because the core component in general shows flatter spectral indices relative to radio hotspots and lobes" (Bi-modal Flux Ratios)
- Alternative: The massive molecular gas Companion being an outflow driven by the current jet-ISM interaction: rejected because the travel time (~7 Myr) far exceeds the hotspot lifetime (~10^5 yr).  
  > "Assuming this Companion in the form of molecular gas that may reach an outflow velocity of $\sim1000$ km s$^{-1}$ (cite: 2012ApJ...757..136W), the Companion takes $\sim7$ Myr to travel such a distance. However, the upper limit of the lifetime of the SE hotspot has an order of $10^5$ yr, suggesting that the bulk of the massive molecular gas cannot be the outflow driven by the jet-ISM interaction." (The Possible Jet-ISM Interaction)

- Limitation (authors): At 237 GHz (~700 GHz rest-frame), thermal dust emission and free-free emission cannot be decomposed from synchrotron radiation in the SE sub-component with current data.  
  > "However, since a jet-induced star formation cannot be confirmed and its associated dust thermal emission cannot be evaluated in the current phase, we treat this sub-component as dominated by synchrotron radiation related to AGN activities throughout this work." (Spatial Distribution); "Based on current observations, we cannot decompose the 237 GHz data into different emission mechanisms which may contaminate the observed flux density of pure synchrotron radiation." (Fitting Results)
- Limitation (authors): Low-frequency observations (<= 1.4 GHz) do not resolve the individual components, and diffuse radio lobes may contaminate the 4.7 and 8.2 GHz hotspot flux densities, making the derived synchrotron ages order-of-magnitude estimates.  
  > "Although the Dragonfly galaxy has been observed at low frequencies over decades, the low angular resolutions leave the radio emissions unresolved. Hence, there is no available structural information on radio emissions in observations at ${\nu}_\mathrm{obs}\leq1.4$ GHz." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED)); "Additionally, the contribution of the diffuse radio lobes to the low-resolution flux densities cannot be measured with the current observations. Furthermore, the exact flux ratio between the two hotspots cannot be determined unambiguously because of the limited resolution and sensitivity throughout all observations." (Caveats); "Hence, the obtained synchrotron age is just an order of magnitude estimation." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA 237 GHz sub-component adjacent to the SE galaxy could be a chance alignment.  
  > "The sub-component adjacent to the SE galaxy may be a chance alignment." (Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux densities across 74 MHz to 237 GHz are measured with vastly different beam sizes and uv-coverages without uv-tapering or matching spatial scales, so high-frequency interferometric filtering of diffuse lobe emission can artificially steepen the spectrum and mimic synchrotron ageing or a quiescent remnant phase.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Partitioning unresolved low-frequency (<= 1.4 GHz) total flux densities between the SE and NW hotspots using a fixed ratio (15:1) ignores the steep-spectrum diffuse radio lobes that usually dominate low-frequency emission.
