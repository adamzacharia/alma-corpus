## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure ALMA stellar positions and uncertainties**: Use pipeline-processed 2023 data; split each 50-min experiment into three parts and measure mean positions, scatter and A–B separation.  
  > "were derived from pipeline-processed data using additional analysis to determine the internal position uncertainties." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB); "These were estimated by splitting each 50-min experiment into three independent parts and then determining the mean stellar position and the error from the scatter among the three parts." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB); "The separation of $\alpha$ Cen A and $\alpha$ Cen B was also obtained for these observations, again by splitting up each 50-min experiment into three parts." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB)
   - parameter: Phase calibrator J1408-5712; absolute position uncertainty about 1.5 mas; separation 5.4°  
  > "The absolute star positions in the DDT observations are tied to the phase calibrator (J1408-5712) whose absolute position is measured by global VLBI observations." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB); "Its absolute position has an uncertainty of about 1.5 mas" (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB); "The calibrator is located only 5.4$^\circ$ away from $\alpha$ Cen" (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB)
   - parameter: June 1 tracked α Cen A; June 17 and 26 phase center near α Cen A without time tracking  
  > "For the 2023 June 1 observations, the $\alpha$ Cen A position was tracked with time" (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB); "for the 2023 June 17 and 26 observations, the phase center was located near $\alpha$ Cen A, but was not tracked with time." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB)
   - assumption: Stellar motion during an hour, 0.4 mas, is much less than atmospheric variations.

5. **Find allowed S1+C1 orbits**: Generate astrometric OFTI orbits; retain dynamically stable orbits; reject detectable follow-up positions; compare predicted RV amplitudes with limits.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Millimeter observations provide high-SNR stellar astrometry on the ICRF frame for JWST positioning.  
  > "At these wavelengths, the two stars are bright enough to yield high SNR data with milli-arcsec precision in $\sim$ an hour of observing time with absolute positions on the ICRF reference frame." (Astrometry of the $\alpha$ Cen System)
- Why this method: Time splitting estimates internal position errors; differential A–B positions cancel shared absolute-position errors.  
  > "The main improvement from the previous ALMA observations (cite: Akeson2021) is the measurement of internal position errors." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB); "much of the error that affect the absolute position of A and B cancels when calculating the stellar position difference." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB)
- Alternative: Background and foreground Solar System interpretations of S1 rejected.  
  > "We definitively show that $S1$ is neither a foreground nor a background object." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Detector and PSF-subtraction artifacts considered unlikely, based on Paper II tests.  
  > "Detailed analysis, including various tests, presented in Paper II (cite: Aniket2025) show that the source is unlikely to be a detector or speckle artifact." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Several atmosphere grids rejected because they require Teff ≥250 K for radius <1.2 RJup.  
  > "we did not find suitable solutions with these grids, as they all required a $T_{\rm eff} \geq 250$ K to fit the photometry for a radius $<1.2$ $R_{\rm Jup}$." (Planet Atmospheric Models)
- Alternative: 200 K atmosphere fits fail for radii <1.2 RJup.  
  > "We were unable to fit the photometry with 200 K models for planet radii $<$ 1.2 $R_{\rm Jup}$." (Planet Atmospheric Models)
- Alternative: Exozodi-knot explanation not pursued because no exozodi was detected.  
  > "Given the lack of exozodi detection reported" (Planet Ring System Models); "we do not consider the exozodi knot interpretation further" (Planet Ring System Models)
- Alternative: Optically thin circumplanetary dust considered, but small grains complicate simultaneous S1/C1 fitting.  
  > "Small grains with realistic optical properties, in models in which the dust is optically thin, are heated above blackbody temperature." (Planet Ring System Models); "This increases the flux in the VLT/NEAR bandpass and makes it more challenging to simultaneously fit the $S1$ and $C1$ photometry" (Planet Ring System Models)
- Alternative: STPSF models inadequate for removing α Cen B speckles at the required level.  
  > "Analysis with the test observations obtained in July 2024 showed that the use of `STPSF` models was inadequate to remove speckles from $\alpha$ Cen B at the required level." (Mitigating the Effects of $\alpha$ Cen B)
- Alternative: Small a <1.5 au orbit fraction excluded for disagreement with S1 astrometry within 1σ.  
  > "We do not consider the small fraction ($\sim$0.2% of the total number) of $a < 1.5$ au orbits" (Incorporating Constraints From Non-Detections); "as they do not agree with $S1$’s relative astrometry within 1$\sigma$ uncertainties." (Incorporating Constraints From Non-Detections)
- How they decided it was enough: ALMA ephemeris precision ∼2 mas compared with the ∼10 mas coronagraph-centering requirement.  
  > "yields a precision of $\sim$2 mas in the predicted position of $\alpha$ Cen A" (Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen and $\epsilon$ Mus); "the centering accuracy requirement ($\sim$10 mas) for best performance behind the MIRI coronagraphic mask" (Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen and $\epsilon$ Mus)
- Limitation (authors): Only one JWST sighting; planet status cannot be confirmed.  
  > "However, with only a single sighting by JWST, the candidate cannot be unambiguously confirmed as a bona fide planet." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): S1 orbit cannot be uniquely constrained; modeling links S1 and C1.  
  > "With only a single JWST/MIRI sighting (and non-detections at two other epochs), it is challenging to uniquely constrain the orbit of $S1$." (Orbital Modeling of $S1$ + $C1$)
- Limitation (authors): Two photometric points leave substantial atmospheric degeneracies.  
  > "Given the numerous atmospheric parameter degeneracies involved in fitting two photometric data points" (Planet Atmospheric Models); "we aim only to provide example scenarios that can explain the observed flux measurements." (Planet Atmospheric Models)
- Limitation (authors): Ring geometry, shadowing and emitting area are highly unconstrained.  
  > "We stress that the ring model discussed above is highly simplified." (Planet Ring System Models); "In the absence of strong constraints on the planet candidate’s orbit and with only two photometric points, the problem is highly unconstrained." (Planet Ring System Models)
- Limitation (authors): Exozodi sensitivity assumes binary-coplanar, near-edge-on disks and a tailored recovery method.  
  > "the model disks’ near-edge-on orientation, which our recovery method is tailored to (cite: Aniket2025)." (Comparison with Previous Exozodi Searches)
- Limitation (authors): JWST observations are insensitive to a possible exozodi interior to ∼0.4 au.  
  > "While a planetesimal belt could survive interior to the planet, the current JWST/MIRI observations are not sensitive to any possible exozodi so close to the star." (Can an Exozodi Disk and an $S1+C1$-like Planet Coexist Around $\alpha$ Cen A?)
- Limitation (authors): June 1 ALMA phase tracking leaves the absolute image frame uncertain.  
  > "the location of the absolute frame of the images is uncertain, resulting in a larger absolute position error estimate" (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB)
- Limitation (authors): Phase-calibrator absolute-position uncertainty limits absolute astrometry.  
  > "Its absolute position has an uncertainty of about 1.5 mas which is, unfortunately, relatively large for an ICRF source since it has not been observed very often." (New ALMA Astrometry of $\alpha$ Cen AB)
- Limitation (authors): February Roll 2 and April Roll 1 are lower quality because of centering offsets.
