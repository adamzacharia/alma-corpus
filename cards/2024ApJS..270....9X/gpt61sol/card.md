## Analysis recipe

1. **Form continuum images**: Select line-free channels, image with TCLEAN, and self-calibrate  
  > "The imaging was conducted through the `TCLEAN` task in CASA 5.3." (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging); "To aggregate the continuum emission, line-free channels were meticulously selected by visual inspection" (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging); "A total of three rounds of phase self-calibration and one round of amplitude self-calibration were run" (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN
   - parameter: hogbom; briggs; robust=0.5  
  > "During imaging, the deconvolution was set as “hogbom” while the weighting parameter was set as “briggs” with a robust value of 0.5" (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging)
   - parameter: Reference antenna DA47; pblimit=0.2  
  > "For self-calibration, antenna DA47 was designated as the reference antenna." (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging); "The primary beam correction is conducted with `pblimit=0.2`." (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Improved sensitivity and dynamic range after self-calibration  
  > "Following self-calibration, the sensitivity and dynamic range of the final continuum image were significantly improved" (ALMA Data Calibration and Imaging)
2. **Extract cores and measure continuum properties**: Circularize unpbcor maps; getsf separates sources, filaments, and background; correct measured fluxes by primary beam response  
  > "we use the `getsf` extraction algorithm that spatially decomposes the observed images to separate relatively round sources from elongated filaments as well as their background emission" (Core Extraction and Catalog); "The `unpbcor` map is firstly smoothed into one with a circular beam whose size is equal to the major axis of the original beam." (Core Extraction and Catalog); "two flux-related parameters (integrated flux and peak intensity) are corrected by the primary beam response" (Core Extraction and Catalog)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: Source size larger than beam and smaller than MRS  
  > "The `getsf` is set to extract sources whose sizes should be larger than the beam size but smaller than the MRS." (Core Extraction and Catalog)
   - parameter: S/N >1; peak intensity >5× local intensity noise; total flux >5× local flux noise  
  > "signal-to-noise ratio larger than unity" (Core Extraction and Catalog); "peak intensity at least five times larger than the local intensity noise" (Core Extraction and Catalog); "total flux density at least five times larger than the local flux noise" (Core Extraction and Catalog)
   - parameter: Ellipticity ≤2; footprint-to-major-axis ratio >1.15  
  > "ellipticity not larger than 2 to ensure a core-like structure" (Core Extraction and Catalog); "footprint-to-major-axis ratio larger than 1.15 to rule out cores with abrupt boundary emission." (Core Extraction and Catalog)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian-like ellipses characterized by major/minor FWHM and position angle  
  > "Cores are characterized by 2D Gaussian-like ellipses with the FWHM of the major and minor axes" (Core Physical Properties)
   - validation: Compare recovered flux with ATLASGAL; test core counts against mass sensitivity and physical resolution  
  > "we integrate the ATLASGAL 870 $\mu$m flux over the field of view of the ASSEMBLE clumps." (Core Extraction and Catalog); "no overall correlation between the number of detected cores and the mass sensitivities with a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.08." (Core Extraction and Catalog); "no overall correlation between the number of detected cores and the physical resolution with a Pearson correlation coefficient of -0.04." (Core Extraction and Catalog)
3. **Classify core evolutionary stages**: Associate outflows channel by channel; classify using outflows, H2CS, and CH3OCHO multiple-transition detections  
  > "The classification of the evolutionary stages of the 248 cores is based on the identification of star-formation indicators, including molecular outflows, H$_2$CS multiple transition lines, and CH$_3$OCHO multiple transition lines." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages); "we updated the outflow catalogs by a channel-by-channel analysis of the outflow lobes to determine their association with the extracted cores" (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
   - parameter: Hot cores: >3σ detections of both CH3OCHO and H2CS multiple transitions  
  > "we first classify those cores with robust ($>3\sigma$) detections of both CH$_3$OCHO and H$_2$CS multiple transition lines as “hot cores”" (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
   - parameter: Warm cores: robust H2CS detection without CH3OCHO; no indicators: prestellar candidates  
  > "we define cores with only robust detection of H$_2$CS but without detection of CH$_3$OCHO lines as warm cores." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages); "142 core without the star-forming indicators mentioned above (outflows, H$_2$CS, or CH$_3$OCHO lines) are then classified as a prestellar core candidate" (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
   - assumption: Outflow-associated cores are protostellar  
  > "If a core is assigned outflows, then it is classified as protostellar (cite: Nony2023IMF-V)." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Band-3 cross-match for absent hot cores in I14382-6017 and I17204-3636  
  > "two massive star-forming clumps, namely I14382-6017 and I17204-3636, do not exhibit any detection of hot cores." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages); "This absence of hot cores has been confirmed by cross-matching with the ALMA Band-3 dataset, ensuring their non-existence (cite: Qin2022ATOMS-VIII)." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
4. **Assign core dust temperatures**: Use CH3OCHO rotational temperatures for hot cores, H2CS temperatures, or clump-averaged SED temperatures  
  > "Temperature estimation utilizes three hybrid methods (clump-averaged temperature, H$_2$CS line, and CH$_3$OCHO line) based on the core properties." (Core Physical Properties); "To trace the dust temperature of hot cores, CH$_3$OCHO molecule with upper energy up to $\sim589$ K is employed instead." (Core Physical Properties); "To estimate the temperature, we used the rotational temperature derived from the multi-transition lines of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$OCHO." (Conclusion)
   - parameter: H2CS J=10-9 upper energies 90–420 K; CH3OCHO upper energy up to ~589 K  
  > "The ASSEMBLE spectral window encompasses multiple hyperfine components of the $J=10-9$ transitions, with upper energy levels from 90 to 420 K (cite: Xu2023SDC335)." (Core Physical Properties); "CH$_3$OCHO molecule with upper energy up to $\sim589$ K is employed instead." (Core Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Without either molecular tracer, core shares the clump SED temperature and has no developed temperature gradient  
  > "In cases where neither H$_2$CS nor CH$_3$OCHO lines are detected" (Core Physical Properties); "the core has not developed its own temperature gradient and thus is assumed to share the same temperature as the clump from the SED fitting." (Core Physical Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare temperatures from CH3OCHO and H2CS  
  > "Temperatures obtained from CH$_3$OCHO (mean value of 110 K) are consistently higher than those derived from H$_2$CS (mean value of 95 K)" (Core Physical Properties)
5. **Derive core masses, radii, and densities**: Dust-flux mass conversion; deconvolved geometric-mean radius; spherical volume density, surface density, and peak column density  
  > "M_{\mathrm{core}} = \mathcal{R}\frac{F^\mathrm{int}_\nu D^2}{\kappa_\nu B_\nu (T_\mathrm{dust})}" (Core Physical Properties); "the angular radius can be calculated as the geometric mean of the deconvolved major and minor axes" (Core Physical Properties); "The core-averaged surface density can be calculated by $\Sigma=M_{\rm core}/(\pi R^2_{\rm core})$." (Core Physical Properties)
   - parameter: η=2.4; R_core=θ_core×D  
  > "We have elected to use a value of $\eta=2.4$" (Core Physical Properties); "the core physical radius can be directly calculated by $R_{\rm core} = \theta_{\rm core}\times D$" (Core Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Single dust temperature; all emission from optically thin dust  
  > "Assuming that all the emission comes from dust in a single $T_{\rm dust}$ and that the dust emission is optically thin" (Core Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio 100; κν=1.89 cm2 g−1 at ~350 GHz  
  > "$\mathcal{R}$ is the gas-to-dust mass ratio (assumed to be 100)" (Core Physical Properties); "$\kappa_{\nu}$ is assumed to be 1.89 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at $\nu\sim350$ GHz (cite: Xu2023SDC335)" (Core Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Thin ice mantles; MRN distribution; gas density 10^6 cm−3  
  > "assuming grains with thin ice mantles and the MRN (cite: MRN1997) size distribution and a gas density of $10^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (Core Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Spherical cores; μH2=2.81  
  > "The number density, $n$, is then calculated by assuming a spherical core" (Core Physical Properties); "we adopt the molecular weight per hydrogen molecule $\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm H_2} = 2.81$ (cite: Evans2022SlowSF)" (Core Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Uncertainties: gas-to-dust ratio 28%, opacity 23%, flux ~14%, temperature ~20%, distance 10%  
  > "We adopt the uncertainties derived by (cite: Sanhueza2017G28) of 28% for the gas-to-dust ratio and of 23% for the dust opacity" (Core Physical Properties); "The uncertainties of the core flux ($\sim$14%), temperature ($\sim$20%), and distance (assumed to be 10%) are included." (Core Physical Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Monte Carlo uncertainty estimation; 1σ confidence intervals  
  > "Monte Carlo methods are adopted for uncertainty estimation and $1\sigma$ confidence intervals are given for core mass, volume density, surface density and peak column density" (Core Physical Properties)
6. **Build a comparison sample and test core growth**: Compare ASSEMBLE core statistics with ASHES; re-extract ASHES cores from mock 0.87 mm images for spatial comparisons  
  > "the statistics of the core parameters are summarized" (Evolution of Massive Protoclusters); "which can be directly compared to Table 5 in (cite: Sanhueza2019ASHES)." (Evolution of Massive Protoclusters); "we simulate the Band-7 (0.87 mm) observations following the ASSEMBLE project with the ASHES Band-6 (1.3 mm) continuum data as input" (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: Scale flux by opacity ratio and Planck-function ratio; κ1.3mm=0.9 and κ0.87mm=1.89 cm2 g−1  
  > "\Gamma(T_{\rm dust}) \equiv \frac{F_{\rm 1.3\,mm}}{F_{\rm 0.87\,mm}} = \frac{\kappa_{\rm 1.3\,mm}}{\kappa_{\rm 0.87\,mm}} \frac{B_{\rm 1.3\,mm}(T_{\rm dust})}{B_{\rm 0.87\,mm}(T_{\rm dust})}" (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps); "$\kappa_{\rm 1.3\,mm}$ = 0.9 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Sanhueza2019ASHES) and $\kappa_{\rm 0.87\,mm}=1.89$ cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Xu2023SDC335)." (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps)
   - parameter: Crop to ASSEMBLE field shape and size; align axes with ASHES field and center on densest part  
  > "the flux-converted images are then cropped into fields with the same shape and size as ASSEMBLE data" (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps); "The mock field is placed with both the major and minor axes aligned with those of the ASHES field with its center towards the densest part of the cluster." (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps)
   - assumption: Negligible free-free contamination in ASHES; uniform clump dust temperature; one flux-scaling factor per field  
  > "the free-free contamination especially at high-frequency bands (0.87 and 1.3 mm) is negligible." (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps); "we assume that dust the temperature of the entire clump is uniform" (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps); "we simply adopt the same $T_{\rm dust}$ and then the same $\Gamma(T_{\rm dust})$ in one field" (Mock Band-7 continuum images for ASHES clumps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check mass-sensitivity differences and getsf extraction on ASHES 12m-alone data  
  > "The slightly worse sensitivity ($\bar\sigma=0.089$ $M_\odot$) of the ASSEMBLE compared to the ASHES ($\bar\sigma=0.078$ $M_\odot$)" (Core Growth and Mass Concentration); "we also perform the source extraction using `getsf` in the 12m-alone data of ASHES" (Core Growth and Mass Concentration); "only obtaining a much lower core number of 66" (Core Growth and Mass Concentration)
7. **Measure the clump–most-massive-core relation**: Compare clump mass with maximum core mass; calculate power-law slope and Pearson/Spearman correlations  
  > "a positive sublinear correlation is observed between $M_{\rm ASSEMBLE, max}$ and $M_{\rm ASSEMBLE, clump}$, with a power law index of 0.75(0.08)." (Coevolution of Clump and Most Massive Core); "The Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients are calculated to be 0.67 and 0.73, respectively." (Coevolution of Clump and Most Massive Core)
   - fitting: Power-law relation; fitting implementation not stated  
  > "with a power law index of 0.75(0.08)." (Coevolution of Clump and Most Massive Core)
   - validation: Both correlation p-values <0.05; compare with ASHES pilot relation  
  > "both correlation coefficients exhibit p-values below 0.05" (Coevolution of Clump and Most Massive Core); "The Spearman rank correlation coefficient is calculated to be 0.04, with a corresponding p-value of 0.90." (“Nurture” but not “Nature”: A Dynamic View of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm clump}$ Relation)
8. **Measure the core-cluster–most-massive-core relation**: Sum core masses per protocluster; fit maximum core mass versus total core-cluster mass; compare with stellar-cluster relations  
  > "we refer $M_{\rm cluster}$ as the sum of all the core masses in a protocluster." (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation); "both the ASHES and the ASSEMBLE protoclusters have positive correlation between $M_{\rm max}$ and $M_{\rm cluster}$." (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation)
   - fitting: Second-order polynomial y = -0.27 x^2 + 1.96 x - 1.51; mean-derivative first-order approximation; direct linear regression  
  > "A second-order polynomial model “$y = -0.27 x^2 + 1.96 x - 1.51$” fits the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ relation best" (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation); "the first-order power-law index can be estimated by considering the average derivatives within the given value range." (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation); "we directly perform the linear regression to derive a power-law index of 0.9, validating our first-order approximation of 0.88." (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation)
   - validation: Polynomial correlation coefficient 0.68; direct regression slope 0.9 agrees with first-order approximation 0.88  
  > "with a correlation coefficient of 0.68" (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation); "we directly perform the linear regression to derive a power-law index of 0.9, validating our first-order approximation of 0.88." (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation)
9. **Measure core separations**: Prim's MST with distance edge weights; compare MST edge-length distributions with ASHES  
  > "In this paper, we use Prim’s algorithm" (Minimum Spanning Tree Methods); "The edge weight is set to be the length between two vertices (cite: Prim1957Algorithm)." (Minimum Spanning Tree Methods); "we take the “edge” of MST as the separation between the cores." (Core Separation)
   - parameter: N−1 edges per clump; 1000 distance-uncertainty Monte Carlo runs  
  > "A total of $N-1$ separation lengths are defined in each clump where $N$ is the core number." (Core Separation); "1000 Monte Carlo runs are adopted to simulate the 1-$\sigma$ distribution dispersion" (Core Separation)

## Qualitative findings

- Core mass and surface density increase from IRDCs to IRBCs  
  > "These mass or surface density increments have also been observed in another comparative work between IRDCs and IRBCs with hub-filament structures" (Core Growth and Mass Concentration); "the more evolved ASSEMBLE protoclusters show systematic increases in the average and maximum mass as well as in the surface density of both protostellar and prestellar cores." (Conclusion)
- More massive clumps contain more massive cores  
  > "This positive correlation indicates a coevolution between the clump and MMC, i.e., a more massive clump contains a more massive core" (Coevolution of Clump and Most Massive Core)
- Core-cluster mass relation is steeper than the stellar-cluster relation; multiplicity is a proposed explanation  
  > "the slope of the $\log M_{\rm max}$ versus $\log M_{\rm cluster}$ relation is notably steeper compared to that of the $\log M_{\rm \star max}$ versus $\log M_{\rm \star cluster}$ relation." (Implications of the $M_{\rm max}$ versus $M_{\rm cluster}$ Relation); "which can be reconciled by an increasing trend of stellar multiplicity with mass." (Conclusion)
- ASSEMBLE cores have systematically smaller separations than ASHES cores  
  > "the core separations in the ASSEMBLE protoclusters are systematically smaller than those in the ASHES protoclusters" (Core Separation)
- Later evolutionary stages tentatively have more centrally condensed core distributions  
  > "Statistically, it’s tentatively evident that the $\mathcal{Q}$ parameter of the protostellar clusters should increase in later evolutionary stages" (Evolution of the $\mathcal{Q}$ Parameter)
- Mass segregation is primordial relative to stellar-cluster dynamical relaxation  
  > "the mass segregation is unlikely to be caused by dynamical relaxation" (Origin of Mass Segregation); "In the context of a stellar cluster, such mass segregation should be considered “primordial”, although it has already evolved from its initial stage." (Origin of Mass Segregation)
- Gravitational concentration and gas accretion are proposed origins of segregation  
  > "We propose that this could be naturally due to the gravitational concentration of the entire clump or gas accretion toward the center." (Origin of Mass Segregation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Massive, luminous clumps with blue profiles provide infall-selected, later-stage counterparts to early-stage IRDCs  
  > "all the 11 massive clumps are chosen from pilot single-dish surveys with evident blue profiles indicating global infall motions and rapid mass assembly." (Introduction); "the sample in the survey provides more evolved protoclusters compared to early-stage IRDCs." (Introduction)
- Why this method: getsf handles uneven background/noise and separates blended cores, filaments, and extended emission  
  > "deal with uneven background and rms noise" (Core Extraction and Catalog); "can separate the blended sources/filaments" (Core Extraction and Catalog); "extract extended emission features." (Core Extraction and Catalog)
- Alternative: astrodendro considered less suitable than getsf for SDC335-like source extraction  
  > "the `getsf` algorithm is a better choice than `astrodendro` in the case study of SDC335" (Core Extraction and Catalog)
- Alternative: CH3OCHO preferred over potentially optically thick H2CS for hot-core dust temperatures  
  > "H$_2$CS lines could be optically thick towards massive hot cores, therefore only tracing the core envelope." (Core Physical Properties); "To trace the dust temperature of hot cores, CH$_3$OCHO molecule with upper energy up to $\sim589$ K is employed instead." (Core Physical Properties)
- Alternative: Hierarchical fragmentation remains an alternative to contraction for close core separations  
  > "we are still unable to rule out another possibility that the closer separation is due to hierarchical fragmentation to produce a series of condensations inside a massive core." (Gravitational Contraction: Protoclusters Evolve to Greater Compactness)
- Alternative: Two-body dynamical relaxation disfavored as the mass-segregation origin  
  > "Considering the short formation timescale of massive stars, the mass segregation is unlikely to be caused by dynamical relaxation" (Origin of Mass Segregation)
- How they decided it was enough: ASHES pilots adequately represent total-sample separations and have a comparable sample size to ASSEMBLE  
  > "the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test between the distributions of the ASHES Totals and Pilots give a p-value of 0.57" (Core Separation); "the ASHES Pilots are good enough to represent the ASHES Totals in the case of studying core separation." (Core Separation); "Since the sample size of the ASHES Pilots is comparable to that of the ASSEMBLE, we only compare core separations from the ASSEMBLE with those from the ASHES Pilots" (Core Separation)
- Limitation (authors): Weak outflows from low-mass objects can be missed, especially in more distant regions  
  > "the method will miss those weak outflows associated with the lowest mass objects, especially for the more distant regions, naturally yielding a lower limit in the number of protostellar objects." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
- Limitation (authors): External heating can cause starless cores to meet warm-core criteria  
  > "external heating by hot cores in the vicinity can also excite H$_2$CS lines in some prestellar cores, so some warm cores can have no stars form inside" (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
- Limitation (authors): Prestellar candidates include both bound and unbound cores  
  > "prestellar core candidates may include both pre-protostellar cores that are gravitational bound, and cores that are not bound and unable to form star." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
- Limitation (authors): 22 warm cores have insufficient H2CS transitions for temperature calculation  
  > "Among these warm cores, 22 have insufficient H$_2$CS transitions available for the calculation of temperature." (Core Classification and Evolutionary Stages)
- Limitation (authors): 12-m-only spatial filtering can underestimate core flux  
  > "the ASSEMBLE data only have the ALMA 12-m array configuration, so the core flux can be underestimated with extended flux filtered out" (High-mass Prestellar Cores in Protoclusters?)
- Limitation (authors): Clump-averaged temperatures may overestimate prestellar-core temperatures and underestimate masses  
  > "we adopt the clump-averaged temperature as the temperature of the prestellar core, which can be overestimated, resulting in an underestimated core mass." (High-mass Prestellar Cores in Protoclusters?)
- Limitation (authors): MSI loses spatial-profile information and lacks a fully established interpretation  
  > "the MSI collapses the $\Lambda_{\rm MSR}$ profile into a scalar value, thus disregarding the potentially various spatial distributions that can produce the same MSI value." ($\mathcal{I}^{\rm MSR}_{\Lambda}$: Mass Segregation Integral (MSI)); "the physical and mathematical interpretations of the MSI are not yet fully understood" ($\mathcal{I}^{\rm MSR}_{\Lambda}$: Mass Segregation Integral (MSI))
- Limitation (authors): Statistical significance is mainly limited by sample size  
  > "In this paper, our analyses and their statistical significance are mainly limited by sample size." (Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cross-sectional ASHES–ASSEMBLE differences do not uniquely establish time evolution or accretion as the cause.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The MSI formula averages Λ, whose no-segregation baseline is 1, but the simulated no-segregation reference is centered on 0; the required transformation is unexplained.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Conclusion's 18.6 M_sun average for the most massive prestellar cores conflicts with the discussion's maximum of 18.3 M_sun.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Conclusion gives 15–37 cores per clump, while the core-statistics table includes a clump with 13.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The imaging text gives an rms upper limit of 1.7 mJy beam−1, while the observing table lists 2.5 for I16060-5146.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample table notes attribute effective radius to Source Extractor, while the main text specifies 2D Gaussian fitting.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The mock Band-7 recipe describes flux conversion and cropping, but does not fully specify how the interferometric response and noise are matched.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust-only continuum masses in radio-bright evolved clumps need a quantified free-free-contamination assessment.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The most massive reported prestellar candidates remain below the paper's adopted >30 M_sun high-mass threshold.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The relaxation-time argument uses representative initial conditions rather than measured parameters for every cluster.
