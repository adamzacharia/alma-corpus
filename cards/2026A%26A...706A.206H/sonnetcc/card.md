## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble multi-frequency light curves**: ACT daily point-source flux densities from matched-filter extraction on 2x2 deg maps, outliers excluded and same-frequency arrays combined; OVRO 15 GHz, ALMA calibrator-catalogue, CRTS and ZTF light curves added  
  > "Light curves are generated by creating $2\times2\,\si{deg\squared}$ maps centred on the source for each day the source was observed." (2.1 The ACT observations); "we exclude all points flagged as outliers and we combine all light curves from different polarised arrays that are observed at the same frequency" (2.1 The ACT observations)
   - software: pixell
   - parameter: CRTS and ZTF rebinned to daily cadence with a weighted mean  
  > "Data from CRTS and ZTF were rebinned to a daily cadence using a weighted mean scheme." (2.2 Key Data at Other Wavelengths)
   - assumption: ACT band centres computed for a synchrotron spectral index of -0.7  
  > "whose band centres for a synchrotron spectral index of $-0.7$, averaged across the PAs, were 95.0, 147.1 and 225.0 GHz, respectively." (2.1 The ACT observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-check of ACT 95 GHz against ALMA (step 3)  
  > "providing a useful cross-check of the calibration of the ACT data." (3.2 The light curves of PKS 2131$-$021)
2. **Fix the sinusoid period from OVRO**: Fit OVRO 15 GHz data alone over the time range where ACT data exist, then hold that period fixed in the joint fit  
  > "We determine the period $P$ by fitting the OVRO data alone in the range where there are ACT data." (3.1 Method)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: OVRO-only fit ranges 57500 < MJD < 59800 (PKS 2131-021) and 57634 < MJD < 59343 (PKS J0805-0111)  
  > "The period, $P$, was determined from an initial fit including only the OVRO data in the range $57500 < \mathrm{MJD} < 59800$." (Table notes (PKS 2131-021 fits)); "The period, $P$, was determined from an initial fit including only the OVRO data in the range $57634 < \mathrm{MJD} < 59343$." (Table notes (PKS J0805-0111 fits))
   - assumption: Restricting to the ACT time range limits bias from superimposed non-sinusoidal jet variations and is best for phase shifts  
  > "Restricting the fit to the time range when both OVRO and ACT data exist is the best approach for measuring the phase shifts as a function of frequency" (3.1 Method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Joint sine-wave fit of OVRO and ACT light curves**: Maximise a Gaussian likelihood with per-light-curve offset, amplitude, phase and a correlated-noise term xi added in quadrature to the errors, period held fixed; phase shift defined relative to OVRO  
  > "We fitted a sine wave function to the OVRO and ACT data by maximizing the following likelihood function:" (3.1 Method); "there are four free parameters per light curve: the sine-wave offset $\bar{S}_j$, amplitude $A_j$, and phase $\phi_j$, plus a term quantifying the correlated noise in the data, $\xi_j$." (3.1 Method)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: reference time t0 = MJD 59000 (2020 May 31)  
  > "the reference time for the sinusoid phase is set at $t_0 =$ MJD 59 000 (2020 May 31)" (3.1 Method)
   - assumption: No noise covariance between different light curves; effectively independent fits sharing one period  
  > "does not assign any noise covariance between different light curves; they are essentially independent fits, all using the same period that is measured in the OVRO data." (9.1 Background)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; uncertainties are the 68% range of marginalised posteriors; priors not stated  
  > "calculated with a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) using the EMCEE code" (3.1 Method); "The uncertainties represent the 68% confidence range of the marginalized posterior distributions." (3.1 Method)
   - validation: Compared with ALMA light curves (step 4) and empirical injection tests (step 7); authors note MCMC errors may be underestimated  
  > "are possibly underestimated. However, as we argue in Appendix [9](#app:empirical_estimate), light curves of more SMBHB candidates are needed to confirm such an assessment." (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts)
4. **Cross-check ACT 95 GHz against ALMA calibrator light curves**: Colour-correct ALMA 91.5 and 103.5 GHz to the ACT band centre with a spectral index measured from the ALMA band ratio, then compare same-day flux ratios and sinusoid offsets  
  > "after colour-correcting the ALMA data to the ACT band centre using a spectral index of $-0.38\pm0.12$, which we obtained from the ratio of the ALMA 91.5 GHz and 103.5 GHz data for the 219 instances where both bands had simultaneous measurements." (3.2 The light curves of PKS 2131$-$021)
   - parameter: 30 same-day pairs (ACT 95 vs ALMA 91.5 GHz); 28 pairs (ACT 95 vs ALMA 103.5 GHz)  
  > "using the 30 pairs of data points measured on the same day in both experiments" (3.2 The light curves of PKS 2131$-$021); "using the 28 available pairs of data" (3.2 The light curves of PKS 2131$-$021)
   - assumption: ACT band centre not itself colour-corrected (difference subdominant to ~3% array-to-array band-centre scatter)  
  > "the difference is subdominant to other uncertainties, particularly the ${\approx}3\%$ scatter in band centre from detector array to detector array, and we ignore it in this analysis." (Footnote 7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual scatter after removing the sinusoid is similar in ALMA and ACT, and Pearson correlation is significant on timescales > 1 month  
  > "On timescales $> 1$ month the level of correlation is significant; on shorter time scales the correlation is noise dominated." (3.2 The light curves of PKS 2131$-$021)
5. **Fit the phase-frequency relation**: Fit a power law dphi = a (nu^b - nu0^b) with nu0 = 15 GHz to Haystack, ALMA and ACT phase shifts (infrared and optical omitted); compare with the K25 quadratic relation  
  > "$\nu_0$ is the reference frequency for the phase shifts, in our case 15 GHz." (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts); "Including the infrared and optical frequencies result in a poor fit, so we omit them." (Footnote 9)
   - parameter: Flat priors b = (0.05, 4) and a = (-2, -0.05), cut off before zero to avoid piling up at unphysical a = 0  
  > "Fits were performed with a Metropolis–Hastings Monte Carlo (MC) with flat priors in the ranges $b = (0.05, 4)$ and $a = (-2, -0.05)$." (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts)
   - assumption: Emission-zone distance goes as r ~ nu^-1 when the jet is optically thick; phase shift proportional to r via light-travel time; data points treated as independent  
  > "the distance along the jet of the emission zone goes as $r \propto \nu^{-1}$ when the jet is optically thick" (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts); "Note that we do not know the level of covariance between all of the data points, which are treated independently in our fits" (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts)
   - fitting: Metropolis-Hastings Monte Carlo, flat priors as above; likelihood form not stated; 68% credible intervals  
  > "Uncertainties are 68% credible intervals." (Table 4 notes)
   - validation: Accuracy of the MC checked with Marquardt-Levenberg fits giving consistent best-fit values; fits repeated over different frequency ranges  
  > "To check the accuracy of the MC, we also performed fits with the Marquardt–Levenberg algorithm and found consistent best-fitting values." (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts)
6. **Test achromaticity of the sinusoid amplitude**: Compare fractional amplitude A/S-bar across 15, 95, 147 and 225 GHz; quoted uncertainty is the standard deviation of the four ratios  
  > "The fractional change in intensity, expressed as $A/\bar{S}$, is similar for all frequencies" (4.2 Achromatic variability)
   - assumption: KO model predicts achromatic fractional amplitude if the spectral index alpha is constant  
  > "The model also predicts that the fractional amplitude of the sinusoidal variations should be achromatic, assuming the spectral index $\alpha$ is constant" (4.2 Achromatic variability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Empirical phase-shift uncertainties by sine injection**: Inject sinusoids with the SMBHB-candidate SNR_sin and period into other real ACT AGN light curves, refit with curve_fit, and take MAD/0.6745 of the recovered phase (absolute) and phase differences between frequencies (relative)  
  > "We estimate the width of the distribution using the median absolute deviation (MAD) statistic, since it is robust to outliers" (9.3 Results and discussion)
   - software: scipy
   - parameter: Ensemble cuts: span >= 95% of the candidate's span and SNR_var < 5; 10 to 18% of the original 204 light curves remain  
  > "After both cuts, 10 to 18% of the original 204 light curves remain, depending on the frequency and the AGN being studied" (9.2 Method)
   - assumption: Type-A and Type-B variations affect non-SMBHB blazars the same way as the SMBHB candidates (stated as untested)  
  > "Under the assumption that the Type-A and Type-B variations affect blazars that are not SMBHB candidates in the same way that they affect the two SMBHB candidates discovered thus far" (9.2 Method)
   - fitting: Non-linear least squares (scipy curve_fit) of A sin[2 pi (t - t0)/P0] + b  
  > "optimization package of SCIPY to do a non-linear least squares fit" (9.2 Method)
   - validation: Varied injected SNR_sin, SNR_var cut and injected phase shifts; MCMC errors about 2 times smaller than empirical ones, judged in broad agreement  
  > "The MCMC uncertainties are ${\sim}$2 times smaller than our empirical estimates." (9.3 Results and discussion)
8. **Test periodicity in optical light curves**: Generalized Lomb-Scargle periodogram of joint CRTS+ZTF light curve; significance from 20 000 simulated red-noise light curves (global p-value) and four tests  
  > "We analyzed the joint CRTS V-band and ZTF g-band light curve of PKS 2131$-$021 with the generalized Lomb-Scargle (GLS) periodogram" (10 Optical light curve periodicity)
   - software: lcsim
   - parameter: Null hypothesis: pure red-noise stochastic process  
  > "Our null hypothesis is that the detected periodicity is a spurious result of a pure red-noise stochastic process." (10 Optical light curve periodicity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Global p-value 0.937 for the optical periodicity alone; 0.047 with prior knowledge of the radio period  
  > "We find a global p-value of 0.937." (10 Optical light curve periodicity)

## Qualitative findings

- ACT mm light curves of both blazars show the same sinusoid as OVRO 15 GHz, including the sinusoid switching off in mid-2020 in PKS J0805-0111  
  > "the sinusoid appears to turn off in the ACT light curves in the same manner as in the OVRO data in mid-2020" (7 Summary and discussion)
- Higher-frequency sinusoids lead the 15 GHz sinusoid in time, monotonically with frequency, in both sources  
  > "In both cases the mm phase leads the radio phase in time." (7 Summary and discussion)
- Sinusoid is more dominant over non-sinusoidal variation in mm than in optical data for PKS 2131-021, motivating mm surveys for SMBHB searches

## Reasoning

- Why this data: PKS 2131-021 is an ALMA calibrator observed during the ACT period, giving a cross-check of ACT calibration  
  > "is an ALMA calibrator that was observed during the same time period as the ACT data, as it affords us the opportunity to compare the ACT 95 GHz light curve with ALMA at 91.5 and 103.5 GHz" (3.2 The light curves of PKS 2131$-$021)
- Why this method: Period measured only over the ACT time range so the phase shifts are best measured  
  > "Restricting the fit to the time range when both OVRO and ACT data exist is the best approach for measuring the phase shifts as a function of frequency" (3.1 Method)
- Alternative: Infrared and optical phase shifts excluded from the power-law fit because they give a poor fit (emission likely more complicated near the central engine)  
  > "Including the infrared and optical frequencies result in a poor fit, so we omit them." (Footnote 9)
- Alternative: Quadratic phase-frequency relation of K25 is phenomenological; a power law considered more physically realistic  
  > "A power law is potentially more physically realistic" (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Fit uncertainties omit systematics from superimposed shorter-term variations; MCMC errors may be underestimated  
  > "The fit uncertainties do not account for systematics due to superimposed, shorter term variations" (Table notes (PKS 2131-021 fits))
- Limitation (authors): Covariance between data points in the phase-frequency fits is unknown, so points are treated independently  
  > "Note that we do not know the level of covariance between all of the data points, which are treated independently in our fits" (4.1 Frequency-dependent phase shifts)
- Limitation (authors): ACT-only data cannot constrain the power-law index  
  > "ACT data alone cannot well-constrain the power-law index for either PKS J0805$-$0111 or PKS 2131$-$021 given current uncertainties." (7 Summary and discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Preliminary ACT calibration uncertainty only; full analysis deferred to Ma et al.  
  > "Ma et al. will provide a detailed uncertainty analysis of the calibration" (2.1 The ACT observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two SMBHB candidates; phase-frequency and achromaticity conclusions rest on very few sources and few cycles
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA enters only as a cross-check of ACT calibration for one source, so ALMA-specific reproduction is minimal
- Caveat (inferred, ours): MCMC and empirical phase errors differ by about a factor 2, and the fit ignores cross-frequency covariance
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The injection ensemble (204 non-SMBHB AGN, 10-18% retained) assumes SMBHB and non-SMBHB variability behave alike
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reported power-law prior range for b is positive while best-fit b is negative, which is unclear without the code
