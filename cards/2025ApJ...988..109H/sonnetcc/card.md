## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate the ALMA visibilities**: Flagging and calibration with the ALMA CASA 6.2 pipeline  
  > "with flagging and calibration performed using the ALMA CASAv6.2 pipeline." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA))
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: J1924-2914 flux/bandpass; J1733-1304 phase calibrator  
  > "J1924$-$2914 was used as a flux/bandpass calibrator, J1733$-$1304 was used as a phase calibrator" (Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: J1742-1517 observed as a check source in all bands  
  > "J1742$-$1517 was used as a check source in all bands." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA))
2. **Self-calibrate and image ALMA data**: Phase-only self-calibration, then imaging with standard procedures and natural weighting  
  > "Phase-only self-calibration was performed, and imaging followed standard procedures with a natural weighting scheme to maximize sensitivity." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA))
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: natural weighting; no uv-tapering  
  > "we do not apply any further manipulation of the visibilities during imaging (e.g., no $uv$-tapering)." (Results and Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure ALMA flux density**: Point-source fit in the image plane with IMFIT  
  > "Flux densities of the source were then measured by fitting a point source in the image plane (with the IMFIT task)." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA))
   - software: CASA IMFIT
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Build integrated light curves**: Sum flux densities of multiple components per epoch, adding errors in quadrature  
  > "we sum the flux densities from the individual components, adding their errors in quadrature as an estimate of the integrated flux density errors." (Results and Discussion)
   - assumption: Summing core and ejecta mimics low angular resolution monitoring  
  > "it mimics what would be measured with only low angular resolution monitoring and still captures much of the critical astrophysics." (Results and Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compute inter-band radio spectral indices**: Inter-band indices only for strictly simultaneous multi-frequency observations from a single observatory (ATCA, VLA or ATA)  
  > "As the source is highly variable, we only calculate these inter-band spectral indices for strictly simultaneous multi-frequency observations." (Results and Discussion); "each spectral index is computed using flux densities from a single observatory (ATCA, VLA, or ATA)" (Results and Discussion)
   - assumption: Intra-band indices avoided because of known biases  
  > "we exclusively calculate inter-band values given that intra-band spectral indices have known biases (cite: Heywood2016)." (Results and Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Make monochromatic 10 GHz light curve**: Scale each observation to 10 GHz using the nearest spectral index, with Gaussian error propagation; exclude frequencies <1 GHz or >100 GHz  
  > "each observation is scaled to 10 GHz using the nearest spectral index measurement." (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s)); "We fold the error in the spectral indexes into our monochromatic flux densities, adopting Gaussian error propagation." (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
   - parameter: Exclude central frequencies more than an order of magnitude from 10 GHz  
  > "we exclude observations with central frequencies that have more than an order of magnitude separation from 10 GHz (i.e., $<$ 1 GHz or $>$ 100 GHz)" (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
   - assumption: Spectral-index variability is an unaccounted-for systematic; qualitative use only  
  > "spectral index variability is an unaccounted-for systematic effect on the scaled flux density of single-frequency observations" (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Estimate minimum flare energy**: Minimum internal energy prescription of Fender et al., equipartition, distances 3.7-5.5 kpc  
  > "Following the prescription from (cite: fender2019), assuming equipartition of energy between particles and magnetic fields" (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
   - parameter: Distance 3.7-5.5 kpc  
  > "adopting distances of 3.7–5.5 kpc (cite: Mata2025distance1727; Burridge2025distance1727)" (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
   - assumption: Energy equipartition between particles and magnetic fields  
  > "assuming equipartition of energy between particles and magnetic fields" (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with energies from other sources; alternative methods give orders of magnitude higher values  
  > "consistent with the range of energies inferred from other sources" (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))

## Qualitative findings

- Soft-state radio emission is optically thin and attributed to long-lived jet ejecta, with the compact jet quenched  
  > "consistent with the radio emission originating from jet ejecta" (The Soft State)
- Radio emission plateaued after X-ray quiescence due to long-lasting jet ejecta  
  > "Despite returning to X-ray quiescence, the radio emission plateaued due to long-lasting jet ejecta, presumably interacting with the ISM." (The Final Hard State and the Return to Quiescence)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Provide the community with comprehensive radio light curves to aid other (non-radio) campaigns  
  > "The primary purpose of this work is to provide the broader astronomical community with these light curves to assist with the interpretation of other observing campaigns" (abstract)
- Why this method: Only inter-band spectral indices, from simultaneous data of one observatory, to avoid intra-band bias and angular-scale differences  
  > "we exclusively calculate inter-band values given that intra-band spectral indices have known biases (cite: Heywood2016)." (Results and Discussion); "thereby avoiding complications arising from differences in angular scale sensitivity across telescopes" (Results and Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only statistical errors reported; absolute flux-scale systematics not included  
  > "We only report statistical errors, but we note that telescopes have (frequency-dependent) systematic errors due to uncertainty in the absolute flux scale." (Results and Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Combining telescopes with different angular-scale sensitivity may resolve out some flux density  
  > "Care should be taken when combining data from different telescopes as they will probe different angular scales and, as a result, may resolve some flux density" (Results and Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): 10 GHz monochromatic light curves are qualitative only because spectral index variability is not accounted for  
  > "we include the monochromatic light curves as a qualitative description of the flaring progression, and we caution readers to view it as such." (The Initial Hard State and State Transition(s))
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only four ALMA epochs (7 flux measurements) among ~197 epochs; ALMA processing is described in a few sentences with no tclean, solint or flux-model details
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Internal inconsistencies: 197 epochs in abstract vs 198 in conclusion; 0.3-230 GHz vs 0.3-225 GHz; some dates written as 2024 where MJD implies 2023
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Statistical-only errors; absolute flux-scale uncertainty (several percent at mm bands) not propagated into spectral indices
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral indices at mm/cm frequencies mix instruments with different calibration and resolution; ALMA not used for inter-band indices
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Scientific interpretation (jet ejecta, steep spectra) is qualitative and not tested with models
