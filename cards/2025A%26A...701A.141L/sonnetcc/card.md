## Analysis recipe

1. **Image SO and SO2 cubes and make moment maps**: Combine C6 and C3 12-m data and image with CASA tclean (Briggs robust 0.5; natural weighting added for L1527), multiscale deconvolver with auto-multithresh  
  > "we combined the 12-meter array data using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5" (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up); "we additionally applied *natural* weighting, which resulted in a lower noise level but poorer resolution." (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; robust = 2 (natural) for L1527 outflow-scale SO  
  > "In CASA’s `tclean`, *Briggs* weighting with a *robust* parameter of 2 is effectively equivalent to natural weighting." (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up)
   - parameter: auto-multithresh sidelobethreshold 2.0, noisethreshold 4.25  
  > "using `sidelobethreshold` of 2.0 and `noisethreshold` of 4.25" (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up)
   - assumption: 5% flux calibration uncertainty  
  > "A flux calibration uncertainty of 5% was assumed (cite: 2020AJ....160..270F)." (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit an elliptical ring to the Oph IRS 63 SO moment 0 map**: 2D elliptical Gaussian ring model fitted with non-linear least squares; inclination from arccos(b/a)  
  > "was determined by fitting the model to the observed 2D emission map using a non-linear least squares optimization method" (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring); "The inclination angle is estimated as $\theta_{\rm view} = \arccos(b/a) \approx 40.5^\circ \pm 1^\circ$." (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring)
   - software: Python, scipy curve_fit
   - parameter: Free parameters p = (x0, y0, a, b, phi_view, sigma, H, C); initial guesses from visual inspection  
  > "Initial guesses were based on visual inspection of the data." (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring)
   - assumption: Ring emission is an elliptical Gaussian ring on a constant background  
  > "We modeled the ring-like emission as an elliptical Gaussian ring characterized by the intensity profile" (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring)
   - fitting: scipy curve_fit least squares; 1-sigma errors from the covariance matrix  
  > "Fitting was performed using the `curve_fit` routine from the `scipy` Python package, and uncertainties on the fitted parameters were estimated from the covariance matrix, with errors reported as one standard deviation (1$\sigma$)." (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring)
   - validation: Fitted model reproduces the elliptical morphology; systematic errors from streamers and non-elliptical shape acknowledged; fitted inclination compared with 45 deg from the literature  
  > "successfully reproduces the elliptical morphology of the ring emission" (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring); "The fitted value of $\theta_{\rm view}$ is close to that reported by (cite: 2020Natur.586..228S) ($45^\circ$)." (3.1.1 SO ring)
3. **Fit ring rotation and estimate central mass of Oph IRS 63**: Fit the velocity along the elliptical ring assuming pure rotation, then Keplerian mass at 65 au  
  > "The rotational velocity ($V_{\rm rot}$) and systemic velocity ($V_{\rm sys}$) are fitted to be approximately 2.2 km s$^{-1}$ and 3 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (3.1.1 SO ring); "Assuming Keplerian rotation at a radius of 65 au, this corresponds to a central mass of $\sim 0.35\ M_\ensuremath{\odot}$" (3.1.1 SO ring)
   - parameter: Radius of centrifugal barrier 65 au (semi-major axis of SO ring)  
  > "The radius of centrifugal barrier ($R_{\rm C}$) of the globally infalling envelope is adopted as $30\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim$65 au), the length of the semi-major axis of the SO ring" (2.3 Target protostars)
   - assumption: Pure rotation along the ring; Keplerian rotation at 65 au  
  > "Assuming Keplerian rotation at a radius of 65 au" (3.1.1 SO ring)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with 0.5 Msun in the literature, found slightly smaller  
  > "slightly smaller than the $\sim 0.5\ M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ reported in the literature" (3.1.1 SO ring)
4. **Model SO spiral features as free-fall streamers**: Ballistic parabolic free-fall trajectories projected onto the sky, parameters theta0, phi0, R_C adjusted by hand to match the morphology  
  > "we adopt a simplified, hand-drawn, illustrative approach aimed at qualitatively matching the observed morphology" (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types); "we model them as gas streamers undergoing ballistic free-fall toward the disk" (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
   - parameter: theta_view and phi_view fixed to the SO ring values (theta_view = 45 deg during follow-up fitting)  
  > "During the follow-up fitting, we fixed $\theta_{\rm view}$ to 45$^\circ$." (3.1.1 SO ring); "we fix the viewing angles $\theta_{\rm view}$ and $\phi_{\rm view}$ to match the inclination and azimuth of the SO ring" (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
   - parameter: Free-fall velocities scaled down by ~1.5  
  > "we manually scale down the free-fall velocities by a factor of approximately 1.5." (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
   - assumption: Central mass 0.35 Msun  
  > "The above fitting assumes a central stellar mass of 0.35 $M_\ensuremath{\odot}$" (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
   - assumption: Zero mechanical energy, non-zero specific angular momentum (parabolic orbit)  
  > "The free-falling gas, characterized by zero mechanical energy and non-zero specific angular momentum, follows a parabolic trajectory in 3D space." (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
   - fitting: Manual adjustment, no objective function stated; theta0 uncertainty ~10 deg  
  > "The uncertainty of $\theta_0$ is approximately $10^\circ$." (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
   - validation: Midplane-only infall tested via the maximum azimuthal span (<110 deg) and rejected for S1  
  > "indicating that their trajectories cannot be explained by midplane infall alone." (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
5. **Model inner-envelope rotation and infall kinematics (Oph IRS 63)**: 2D projected velocity model for a rotating and infalling disk-like inner envelope compared with SO moment 1  
  > "we modeled the 2D distribution of the projected velocity for a rotating and infalling inner envelope" (3.1.4 Inner envelope motion)
   - parameter: Rotation power-law index beta = -1; infall velocity fixed; velocity ratio adjusted to match data  
  > "The rotation follows a power-law with an index ($\beta$) of $-1$, while the infall velocity is fixed." (3.1.4 Inner envelope motion); "We adjusted the ratio of these velocities to match the observations." (3.1.4 Inner envelope motion)
   - assumption: Negligible envelope flare so infall does not project onto the major axis  
  > "(assuming negligible envelope flare)" (3.1.4 Inner envelope motion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Reproduces the moment 1 offset and S-shaped pattern; beta = -1 and -1/2 cannot be distinguished at current sensitivity  
  > "we cannot conclusively distinguish between $\beta = -1$ and $\beta = -1/2$" (3.1.4 Inner envelope motion)
6. **Derive Tex and N(SO2) at the Oph IRS 63 warm spot**: Rotational diagram of SO2 lines from spectra extracted at the warm spot; optically thin column density with opacity correction  
  > "we analyze the SO$_2$ line profiles and construct a rotational diagram of the warm spot" (3.1.3 SO2 ring and warm spot); "Linear fitting of the data yields the values of $N$ and $T_{\rm ex}$." (Appendix: Calculation of column density)
   - parameter: Optical depth correction factor f_tau = tau/(1-exp(-tau)) with tau from peak Tb/Tex  
  > "If $T_{r}$ is not small in comparison to the excitation temperature ($T_{\rm ex}$), a correction factor ($f_{\tau}$) should be applied" (Appendix: Calculation of column density)
   - assumption: Optically thin limit; partition function Q(Tex) from CDMS tables  
  > "in the optically thin limit" (Appendix: Calculation of column density); "which can be interpolated from the tabulated values available online (cite: 2001A&A...370L..49M)" (Appendix: Calculation of column density)
   - fitting: Linear fit in (Eu, ln(Nu/gu)) space  
  > "Linear fitting of the data yields the values of $N$ and $T_{\rm ex}$." (Appendix: Calculation of column density)
   - validation: Tex uncertainty estimated to be within 20%  
  > "The main uncertainty in the derived column densities stems from the excitation temperature, which is estimated to be accurate within 20%" (3.1.3 SO2 ring and warm spot)

## Qualitative findings

- SO emission is concentrated in rings at the centrifugal barriers of the infalling envelopes  
  > "SO emission is found to be concentrated in rings at the centrifugal barriers of the infalling envelopes." (abstract)
- Common spiral-like SO streamers with warm (Tex > 50 K) and possibly hot (Tex >~ 100 K) SO2 spots or segments near the centrifugal barriers  
  > "Spiral-like streamers with SO emission are also common, with warm ($T_{\rm ex} > 50$ K) and even hot ($T_{\rm ex} \gtrsim 100$ K) spots or segments of SO$_2$ observed near the centrifugal barriers." (abstract)
- Streamers split into in-midplane and off-midplane types, with different shock patterns  
  > "We classify the streamers into two types—those in the midplane and those off the midplane." (abstract)
- The inner SO ring of L1527 may trace the SO snowline  
  > "By analogy, the inner SO ring may represent the snowline of SO." (3.3.2 Two rings of SO at the disk scale)
- A mildly shocked conical SO layer infalls along the L1527 outflow cavity wall  
  > "we propose the presence of a mildly shocked, conical SO-emitting layer undergoing downward-spiraling infall, which warps the outflow cavity." (3.3.3 Infalling layer of SO along the outflow boundary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets chosen from the two programs because observations were complete, SO/SO2 emission was resolved, and disk inclinations differ  
  > "selected based on the following criteria: (1) the observations for these sources have been completed; (2) they show resolved signatures of SO and SO$_2$ emission; and (3) their disks have different inclination angles ($\theta_{\rm view}$)" (2.3 Target protostars)
- Why this method: Briggs robust 0.5 chosen to resolve disk-scale structure while keeping sensitivity; natural weighting for L1527 to raise S/N of extended SO  
  > "To resolve disk-scale structures while maintaining sensitivity, we combined the 12-meter array data using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5" (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up); "we additionally applied *natural* weighting, which resulted in a lower noise level but poorer resolution." (2.1.1 ALMA observational set up)
- Alternative: Midplane-only free-fall streamers rejected for features such as S1 (azimuthal span >110 deg)  
  > "indicating that their trajectories cannot be explained by midplane infall alone." (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
- Alternative: Weak 2-sigma feature near SO2 12_{3,9}-12_{2,10} attributed to vibrationally excited SO2 not adopted; judged a random spike  
  > "we consider it more likely that this line feature is a random observational spike, given its low S/N in Oph IRS 63" (3.1.5 Tentative features in the SO2 line profiles)
- Alternative: Classical CMU model disfavoured because it sends half the envelope mass onto the disk, which conflicts with the lack of SO and SO2 in the central disk region  
  > "However, this conflicts with observations showing that shock tracers such as SO and SO$_2$ are lacking in the central disk region" (4.1 CMU model)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): L1527 SO2 temperature unconstrained because only one transition was observed  
  > "Due to the limited data (only one transition), the temperature of the SO$_2$-emitting region cannot be well constrained." (3.3.1 Parallel slabs of SO)
- Limitation (authors): DK Cha disk rotation direction uncertain  
  > "Despite the wealth of spatial and spectral information, the exact rotational direction of DK Cha’s disk remains uncertain." (3.2.5 Discussion of the direction of rotation)
- Limitation (authors): Ring-fit errors are formal only; streamers and non-elliptical shape add systematics  
  > "We note, however, that the presence of streamers and deviations from a perfectly elliptical ring may introduce systematic uncertainties not captured in the formal error estimates." (Appendix: Fitting an elliptical ring)
- Limitation (authors): Magnetic fields and shock types not modeled  
  > "this study does not include the effects of magnetic fields on streamer and envelope dynamics" (5.2 Beyond the observations and models)
- Limitation (authors): Streamer S4 only plausible, S/N of the connecting emission <~3  
  > "we consider the presence of S4 to be plausible" (3.1.2 SO spiral-like streamers of different types)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Streamer parameters (Table 1) are hand-tuned with an extra 1.5 factor on free-fall velocity; no goodness-of-fit, so streamer-type classification is qualitative.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Warm-spot Tex of Oph IRS 63 rests on low-S/N SO2 lines (some S/N ~2.5-3 in moment maps) and the quoted 20% uncertainty is not a fit error; N(SO) uses the SO2 Tex by assumption.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Centrifugal barrier radius is inconsistent: 30'' (~65 au) in Sect. 2.3 is likely a typo for 0.3-0.5'', and the summary quotes ~80 au.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SW-boundary SO-CO offset is given as 0.7'' +/- 0.01'' in the text but 0.75 +/- 0.05'' in Table 2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DK Cha inclination from a marginally resolved continuum (minor axis 0.38 +/- 0.11'') is weak; the rotation direction is unresolved, so streamer geometry there is speculative.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The inner-envelope model is not fitted to data with a likelihood; the claim of its necessity vs. a pure CMU model with disk surface shocks is not tested quantitatively.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample of three sources chosen from incomplete observations and selected by SO/SO2 detection, which may bias conclusions about how common the features are.
