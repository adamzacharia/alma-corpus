## Analysis recipe

1. **Coadd visibilities and fit two-component models (host + point source) in the uv-plane**: Coadd high and low resolution Band 5 visibilities using CASA 'concat', export to GILDAS via 'exportuvfits' and 'fits.to.uvt', and perform two-component (elliptical Spergel profile + central point source) fitting in the uv-plane using 'uv_fit' for CO(5-4) and continuum (Bands 5, 7, 9).  
  > "For each quasar, the high and low resolution visibilities are coadded using the CASA task ‘concat’." (Method / Data); "First the coadded visibilities are exported from CASA and imported to GILDAS (cite: Guilloteau2000) using exportuvfits and fits.to.uvt tasks. Two components are included in the model to account for an extended (disk-like) and a central unresolved (quasar) component." (Method / Model fitting in the uv-plane); "We model the source in the $uv$-plane (Section [2.3](#sec:method)) using the task $'uv\_fit'$ in GILDAS to quantify the emission strength and its spatial distribution." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500: a luminous quasar in a starbursting host)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: CO velocity integration ranges (FWHM): 455 km s^-1 (J1236+0500), 220 km s^-1 (J2317-1033), 480 km s^-1 (J2345-1104)  
  > "$\Delta$v (FWHM) | 455 | 220 | 480 | km s$^{-1}$" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Continuum Spergel index tested for 3 cases: fixed to CO fit value, fixed to -0.3, and free  
  > "we run model fits for three different cases: (a) the Spergel index fixed to the output value from the CO fit, (b) index set to -0.3, the mean value for galaxies in the sample of (cite: Tan2024b), and (3) a free index." (Results / Luminous quasars in typical star-forming hosts / Continuum emission)
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "Throughout this work, we use a Hubble constant of $H_0 = 70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ and cosmological density parameters $\Omega_\mathrm{m} = 0.3$ and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Point-source component traces unresolved quasar/torus emission while extended Spergel component traces the host galaxy  
  > "Two components are included in the model to account for an extended (disk-like) and a central unresolved (quasar) component." (Method / Model fitting in the uv-plane)
   - fitting: GILDAS uv_fit in the uv-plane  
  > "We model the source in the $uv$-plane (Section [2.3](#sec:method)) using the task $'uv\_fit'$ in GILDAS to quantify the emission strength and its spatial distribution." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500: a luminous quasar in a starbursting host)
   - validation: Inspection of 2D residual images in the image plane and comparison of visibility amplitude versus uv-distance profiles  
  > "In the top row, the middle and right panels show the best-fit model and residual image." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500: a luminous quasar in a starbursting host); "We further demonstrate that an unresolved component is subdominant by displaying the amplitude as a function of uv distance for the data and best-fit model (Fig. [1](#fig:model_fit_J1236))." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Continuum)
2. **Model 3D gas kinematics of SDSS J1236+0500**: Fit the CO(5-4) data cube with a 3D tilted-ring thin disk model using 3D-Barolo and fit the 1D spectrum with a double Gaussian profile.  
  > "The line is symmetric and well fit with a double Gaussian profile shown in red. For a proper assessment, we fit the cube with a thin disk model using 3D-Barolo (cite: DiTeodoro2015)." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Gas kinematics)
   - software: 3D-Barolo
   - assumption: Thin rotating disk geometry  
  > "For a proper assessment, we fit the cube with a thin disk model using 3D-Barolo (cite: DiTeodoro2015)." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Gas kinematics)
   - fitting: 3D-Barolo tilted-ring fitting  
  > "For a proper assessment, we fit the cube with a thin disk model using 3D-Barolo (cite: DiTeodoro2015)." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Gas kinematics)
   - validation: Comparison of velocity field and velocity dispersion data maps with best-fit model and residual maps  
  > "The individual lower panels show the velocity field, best-fit model, and residual image with the equivalent for the dispersion shown in the three right-most panels." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Gas kinematics)
3. **Construct FIR SEDs, derive IR luminosities and SFRs, and evaluate the CO-FIR relation**: Compare decomposed extended and nuclear fluxes across Bands 5, 7, and 9 with modified blackbody models and Lyu et al. (2017) quasar templates; compute L'_CO(5-4), convert to L_TIR via Daddi et al. (2015) and to SFR via Kennicutt & Evans (2012); fit empirical AGN contribution to CO(5-4) following Valentino et al. (2021).  
  > "the flux, along with bands 5 and 7, is consistent with a thermal spectrum (i.e., modified black body with $\beta=1.6$ and T=47 K), well above the standard quasar template (cite: Lyu2017b)" (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Continuum); "Inferring the SFRs using the CO (J=5–4) luminosities of the extended components for the three quasars and the CO (J=5–4) – L$_{IR}$ relation (cite: Daddi2015) with its uncertainty, the total infrared luminosities of 7.3, 1.5 and 1.35 (units of 10$^{12}$ L$_{\odot}$) are found and correspond to SFRs of 975$\pm200$, 232$\pm55$ and 181$\pm53$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ using the relation in (cite: Kennicutt2012)." (Results / Presence of star formation in luminous quasar hosts); "Following the simplified empirical approach in (cite: Valentino2021), we attempt to quantify the effect of the AGN on the CO (J=5-4) emission in our quasars by modeling the CO gas luminosities as a function simultaneously of the extended FIR emission" (Results / CO - FIR relation / Quasar influence on CO (J=5-4))
   - parameter: Modified blackbody parameters: beta = 1.6, T_dust = 47 K  
  > "the flux, along with bands 5 and 7, is consistent with a thermal spectrum (i.e., modified black body with $\beta=1.6$ and T=47 K)" (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Continuum)
   - assumption: Chabrier initial mass function  
  > "We assume a Chabrier initial mass function for estimates of mass and star formation rate." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Point-source emission originates from the unresolved quasar component while extended emission is heated solely by star formation  
  > "In both panels, we assumed that the point source emission originates from the unresolved component of the quasar, while the extended component is assigned to the heating solely due to star formation." (Results / CO - FIR relation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of SFRs derived independently from resolved Band 5 continuum, unresolved Band 7 continuum, and extended CO(5-4) emission  
  > "Then we compare the SFRs based on the FIR continuum luminosity with the CO (J=5–4) luminosity in Figure [2](#fig:sfr_comp)." (Results / Presence of star formation in luminous quasar hosts)
4. **Estimate host galaxy dynamical masses and CO Tully-Fisher stellar masses**: Extract 1D CO(5-4) spectra within a 1 arcsec aperture for pixels with S/N > 2, fit double-horn (J1236+0500, J2345-1104) or single Gaussian (J2317-1033) line profiles, and compute M_dyn(R) using MCMC and M_* using the local CO Tully-Fisher relation.  
  > "From the reduced ALMA data cubes, one-dimensional spectra are extracted using pixels within a 1$^{\prime\prime}$ diameter aperture, centered on each target, and having S/N $>$ 2." (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses); "For J1236+0500 and J2345-1104, a double-horn profile is an appropriate choice for the model" (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses); "Lastly, we use the local CO Tully-Fisher relation (cite: Tiley2016a) to infer the stellar mass based on the COLD-GASS survey:" (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses)
   - parameter: Aperture diameter = 1 arcsec; pixel selection threshold S/N > 2  
  > "From the reduced ALMA data cubes, one-dimensional spectra are extracted using pixels within a 1$^{\prime\prime}$ diameter aperture, centered on each target, and having S/N $>$ 2." (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses)
   - assumption: Rotation-dominated thin disk with inclination cos(i) = b/a and circular velocity v_circ = 0.5 * W_20 / sin(i)  
  > "Using these best-fit models, we assume that the gas distribution is described by a rotation-dominated, thin disk (cite: Neeleman2021)" (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses); "$v_{\rm circ} = 0.5\times W_{\rm 20}/\sin i$" (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses); "The inclination ($i$) of the galaxy is estimated from minor-to-major axis ratio." (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses)

## Qualitative findings

- The FIR continuum and CO(5-4) emission in all three z=2 luminous quasars are dominated by an extended host galaxy component (r_eff ~ 1 kpc), with central unresolved components being subdominant.  
  > "The continuum and line emission are primarily dominated by an extended component, characteristic of the ISM in massive star-forming galaxies." (Summary); "The FIR continuum is well modeled with an extended, smooth, and compact ($r_{eff}\sim1$ kpc) profile resembling a disk in both Band 5 and 9." (Summary)
- There is no evidence for quasar-heated dust enhancing the extended galaxy-scale FIR continuum above the level expected from star formation.  
  > "As a consequence, there is no evidence for additional continuum emission on extended scales which could be attributed to quasar-heated dust." (Abstract); "The high level of CO emission does not support a scenario where the quasar is responsible for heating the extended dust above that from star formation." (Summary)
- The starbursting quasar host SDSS J1236+0500 exhibits smooth, undisturbed rotation-dominated gas kinematics with no evidence of mergers or galaxy-scale outflows.  
  > "The disk is rotation dominated with $v/\sigma=6.5$ ($v_{rot}=260$ km s$^{-1}$ and $\sigma=40$ km s$^{-1}$)." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Gas kinematics); "Therefore, there are no signs of ongoing merging or other disturbances on the gas." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Gas kinematics)
- The host galaxies of these luminous z=2 quasars are actively forming stars on or above the star-forming main sequence, showing no evidence of galaxy-wide quenching by quasar-mode feedback.  
  > "Therefore, our results do not provide evidence for a quasar-mode feedback, even for the most luminous cases at $z=2$." (Abstract); "The hosts for our three quasars are forming stars either at rates consistent with the MS or higher, if the stellar masses are lower than $10^{11}$ M$_\odot$." (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution (~0.06-0.3 arcsec) ALMA Band 5 and Band 9 observations of the most luminous (L_bol ~ 10^47 erg s^-1) z=2 quasars are needed to spatially separate the central nuclear torus from the extended host galaxy and to observe CO(5-4) simultaneously as an independent SFR tracer.  
  > "It is at these high luminosities where we would expect radiative-mode feedback to impact the ISM of their host galaxies, if not at lower luminosities." (Introduction); "Specifically, we use the extended component in CO (J=5–4) to infer the contribution of star formation to the infrared continuum luminosity thus placing constraints on the dust being heated by the quasar, i.e., breaking the degeneracy between quasar and stellar heating." (Method); "Band 9 observations (0.06 and 0.3$^{\prime\prime}$) are available for two of the three quasars to further differentiate the extended continuum from the presence of an unresolved nuclear component to the FIR emission as further detailed below." (Method)
- Why this method: Fitting a two-component model (elliptical Spergel profile + point source) directly in the uv-plane avoids systematic biases from image-plane fitting and CLEAN deconvolution.  
  > "We perform model fitting in the $uv$-plane to determine the flux and half-light radii of the CO (J=5–4) and continuum emission thus mitigating inherent systematic biases when fitting sources in the image plane." (Method / Model fitting in the uv-plane); "The extended source is described by an elliptical Spergel profile (cite: Spergel2010) which is an analytic alternative to a Sersic profile with forms in both real and Fourier space thus able to be implemented in $uv$-space which avoids issues with working with images based on a clean algorithm." (Method / Model fitting in the uv-plane)
- Alternative: Fitting the J1236+0500 CO(5-4) point-source component fixed to a common center with the extended Spergel profile was tested, which resulted in a negative flux and led to rejecting a physical nuclear CO component in that source.  
  > "However, the reality of this component is questionable since a fit with the point-source component, fixed to a common center with the Spergel profile, results in a negative flux." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500: a luminous quasar in a starbursting host)
- Alternative: Alternative quasar torus templates lacking hot or warm dust (shallower FIR slope) were considered to explain the unresolved Band 5 flux in J1236+0500, though they overpredict the WISE fluxes.  
  > "It is also possible that the torus may lack hot or warm dust as shown by the additional quasar templates which have a shallower slope than the standard quasar and provide a better match to the ALMA constraints at the expense of over predicting the fluxes in the WISE bands." (Results / SDSS J1236+0500 / Continuum)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single-epoch MgII virial black hole masses may be overestimated in light of recent GRAVITY results.  
  > "We recognize that these may be overestimates given the recent results from GRAVITY (cite: Abuter2024) and discuss the implications which do not impact our analysis of the ISM properties of their hosts." (Method / Targets)
- Limitation (authors): The S/N of the host galaxy emission in J2317-1033 and J2345-1104 is insufficient to accurately constrain the Spergel index or point-source continuum fraction in Band 5 without fixing the index.  
  > "Based on (cite: Tan2024a), the signal-to-noise of the host is insufficient to accurately determine the Spergel or effective Sersic indices for these two sources." (Results / Luminous quasars in typical star-forming hosts); "Due to the lower S/N detections of the continuum relative to CO, the point-source contributions are not well constrained." (Results / Luminous quasars in typical star-forming hosts / Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Compact nuclear star formation may contribute to the unresolved central component, making the inferred AGN contribution an upper limit.  
  > "We understand that there may be compact components due to star formation which may not have been accounted for in the model of the extended component. Thus, our results are likely to be an upper limit on the quasar contribution." (Results / CO - FIR relation / Quasar influence on CO (J=5-4))
- Limitation (authors): Direct stellar mass measurements are unavailable due to the bright optical quasar glare, and applying the local CO Tully-Fisher relation at z~2 is subject to zero-point evolution (-0.41 dex offset).  
  > "direct detection of the stellar emission is not currently available and is likely to be challenging given the brightness of our quasars, possibly even with JWST." (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses); "However, (cite: Tiley2016b) indicates that the zero-point of Tully-Fisher relation can have a $-$0.41 dex offset over the last 8 billion years ($z\sim1$). Since our targets are $z\sim2$ quasars, the stellar mass can be smaller than that based on the local relation for which we use in any case." (Discussion / Dynamical and inferred stellar masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sample size is very small (3 quasars, with Band 9 data for only 2), limiting statistical generalizations about the overall z=2 luminous quasar population.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single dust temperature (T_dust = 47 K) and emissivity index (beta = 1.6) are assumed for the host galaxies rather than fitting spatially resolved dust temperatures across Bands 5, 7, and 9.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The resolved Band 5 continuum SFRs are systematically lower by 27-61% compared to the unresolved Band 7 continuum SFRs even when point-source contributions are small (e.g., 14% in J1236+0500), suggesting either missing flux in the higher-resolution Band 5 data or deviations from the assumed single-temperature modified blackbody SED.
