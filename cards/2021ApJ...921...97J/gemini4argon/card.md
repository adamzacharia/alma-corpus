## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and spectral lines and extract 1D spectra**: Image continuum by combining LSB and USB excluding line channels; subtract continuum in uv-plane using uvcontsub (order 1); image spectral cubes at 100 km/s resolution and single-channel moment 0 maps; extract 1D spectra using apertures of 2.5'' (W) and 1.2'' (E).  
  > "The continuum images are produced by combining the lower and upper side bands (LSB, USB) and excluding the channels containing the line emission." (Data Reduction and Imaging); "To generate the spectral cubes, we use the same weighting as with the continuum maps and a velocity averaging of 100 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$, after subtracting the continuum using `CASA` task `uvcontsub` with a polynomial fit of order 1." (Data Reduction and Imaging); "The spectra in W and E are extracted from the 100 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ spectral cubes using an aperture of 2.5$\ensuremath{''}$ and 1.2$\ensuremath{''}$, respectively." (Spectral Line Decomposition)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 0.5 are used, which provide a good compromise between resolution and noise." (Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - parameter: Spectral cube velocity averaging = 100 km s^-1; Moment 0 single channel width = 1000 km s^-1 (W) and 500 km s^-1 (E)  
  > "To generate the velocity integrated intensity maps (moment 0), we produce single channel cubes with a width of 1000 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ in W and 500 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ in E, which is $\sim$2 $\times$ full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the lines." (Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - parameter: Spectral extraction apertures: 2.5'' (W) and 1.2'' (E)  
  > "The spectra in W and E are extracted from the 100 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ spectral cubes using an aperture of 2.5$\ensuremath{''}$ and 1.2$\ensuremath{''}$, respectively." (Spectral Line Decomposition)
   - assumption: Velocity separation of ~750 km s^-1 between W and E based on [CII] observations  
  > "We consider a velocity separation of $\sim$750 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ between W and E based on the high resolution \[CII\] observations (cite: marrone18)." (Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - fitting: uvcontsub polynomial fit of order 1  
  > "To generate the spectral cubes, we use the same weighting as with the continuum maps and a velocity averaging of 100 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$, after subtracting the continuum using `CASA` task `uvcontsub` with a polynomial fit of order 1." (Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Decompose blended and non-Gaussian spectral lines and calculate line luminosities**: Re-image ALMA 240 GHz [CII] data with the same weighting and velocity averaging to use as a high-SNR template; scale the [CII] template to observed line spectra (jointly for blended lines) via least squares; integrate flux from line center - 3*sigma_v to + 3*sigma_v; repeat 3000 times with random Gaussian noise to get median and standard deviation; convert I_obs to L_line and L'_line using Solomon et al. (1997) equations.  
  > "Since the spectral lines are non-Gaussian and blended in the case of $\rm CO(7-6)$ and \[CI\]$\rm (2-1)$, and $\rm CO(10-9)$ and p-$\rm H_{2} O (3_{1,2}-2_{2,1})$, we use \[CII\] from the ALMA 240 GHz observations (cite: marrone18) as a template to derive the spectral properties." (Spectral Line Decomposition); "The velocity integrated observed line flux, I$_{\rm obs}$ (Jy $\rm km\ s^{-1}$), is obtained by adding the flux density under the scaled \[CII\] spectrum from line center $-$ 3$\sigma_{\nu}$ to line center + 3$\sigma_{\nu}$ $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ where $\sigma_{\nu}$ is FWHM/2.35." (Spectral Line Decomposition); "We estimate the line properties by repeating the scaling 3000 times with random Gaussian noise added to the flux density and taking the median value of all the fits." (Spectral Line Decomposition)
   - parameter: [CII] template FWHM = 779 +/- 25 km s^-1 (W) and 371 +/- 12 km s^-1 (E)  
  > "The \[CII\] spectrum obtained has a FWHM of 779 $\pm$ 25 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ in W and 371 $\pm$ 12 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ in E." (Spectral Line Decomposition)
   - parameter: Integration range: line center - 3*sigma_v to line center + 3*sigma_v; 3000 Monte Carlo iterations  
  > "The velocity integrated observed line flux, I$_{\rm obs}$ (Jy $\rm km\ s^{-1}$), is obtained by adding the flux density under the scaled \[CII\] spectrum from line center $-$ 3$\sigma_{\nu}$ to line center + 3$\sigma_{\nu}$ $\rm km\ s^{-1}$ where $\sigma_{\nu}$ is FWHM/2.35." (Spectral Line Decomposition); "We estimate the line properties by repeating the scaling 3000 times with random Gaussian noise added to the flux density and taking the median value of all the fits." (Spectral Line Decomposition)
   - assumption: Molecular lines share the same kinematic profile as the [CII] template; unresolved ATCA CO(3-2) flux distributed between W and E by scaling to CO(6-5); flat LambdaCDM cosmology (h = 0.677, Omega_m = 0.307, Omega_Lambda = 0.693)  
  > "We use flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology where $h$ = 0.677, $\Omega_{m}$ = 0.307, and $\Omega_{\Lambda}$ = 0.693 (cite: planck16cosmo)." (Introduction); "Since $\rm CO(3-2)$ is spatially unresolved ($\sim$5 $-$ 6$''$) into W and E, we distribute the flux density of $\rm CO(3-2)$ by scaling to $\rm CO(6-5)$ in the two galaxies." (Ancillary Data)
   - fitting: Least squares fit of scaled [CII] template with 3000 Monte Carlo noise realizations  
  > "This \[CII\] spectrum is scaled to the observed spectrum of the lines using a least squares fit." (Spectral Line Decomposition); "We estimate the line properties by repeating the scaling 3000 times with random Gaussian noise added to the flux density and taking the median value of all the fits." (Spectral Line Decomposition)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Perform gravitational lens modeling in the visibility plane**: Fit parametric lens models to visibility data of 140 GHz and 101 GHz continuum and single-channel (1000 km/s) CO(6-5), CO(7-6), and CO(10-9) lines in W using visilens with SIE lens profile and Sersic source profile(s).  
  > "In this analysis, we perform lens modeling on the 140 and 101 GHz ($\sim$272 and 375 $\mu$m rest-frame, respectively) continuum, and $\rm CO(6-5)$, $\rm CO(7-6)$, and $\rm CO(10-9)$ molecular line transitions in W." (Lens Modeling); "Since the signal-to-noise is not sufficiently high for pixellated modeling, we use a parametric lens modeling code, `visilens` (cite: spilker16)." (Lens Modeling); "For the lines, we fit single channel models with the width of 1000 $\rm km\ s^{-1}$, which is equivalent to the $\sim$2 $\times$ FWHM to estimate the overall magnification." (Lens Modeling)
   - software: visilens, emcee
   - parameter: 1000 MCMC chains; lens parameters constrained within best-fit values of 140 GHz single-lens model  
  > "As the best-fit parameters, we take the median value of the 1000 MCMC chains with 1$\sigma$ uncertainty." (Lens Modeling); "For all the lensing reconstruction in this paper, we vary the lens parameters only within the best-fit values obtained from the high resolution 140 GHz measurement set using a single lens." (Lens Modeling)
   - assumption: SIE lens mass profile, Sersic source profile; fixed magnification mu = 1.3 for E and mu = 2.2 for p-H2O in W from Marrone et al. (2018)  
  > "In this code, the background source is parameterized by one or more S$\rm \acute{e}$rsic (cite: sersic68) profiles with seven free parameters: source position relative to the lens ($\rm x_{S}, y_{S}$), flux density (S), half light radius major axis ($\rm a_{S}$), S$\rm \acute{e}$rsic index ($\rm n_{S}$), axis ratio ($\rm b_{S}/a_{S}$), and position angle ($\rm \phi_{S}$)." (Lens Modeling); "The lensing source is represented by one or more Singular Isothermal Ellipsoids (SIEs), which has five free parameters: the center of the lens relative to phase center ($\rm x_{L}$, $\rm y_{L}$), the lens mass ($\rm M_{L}$) which determines the strength of lensing, the ellipticity of the lens ($\rm e_{L}$), and the position angle of the lens major axis ($\rm \phi_{L}$) in degrees east of north." (Lens Modeling); "Since we do not have a good signal-to-noise for lensing reconstruction in E, we adopt a magnification of 1.3 for all the molecular lines. For the same reason, we adopt $\mu = 2.2$ from (cite: marrone18) in p-$\rm H_{2} O (2_{1,1}-2_{0,2})$ in W." (Lens Modeling)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling (emcee) in the visibility plane; Deviance Information Criterion (DIC) used to select number of sources  
  > "This model uses a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm (`emcee` package; (cite: foremanmackey13)) to sample the parameter space." (Lens Modeling); "The performance of different models is measured by Deviance Information Criterion (DIC; (cite: spiegelhalter02)) to determine the number of sources to include in the model." (Lens Modeling)
   - validation: 140 GHz lens parameters compared and found to agree with Marrone et al. (2018) pixellated lens model  
  > "The lens parameters obtained from the 140 GHz data agree with (cite: marrone18) model." (Lens Modeling)
4. **Fit modified blackbody dust SED to derive dust temperature, dust mass, L_FIR, and L_IR**: Fit a uniform-temperature modified blackbody function (including CMB effect) to magnification-corrected rest-frame >50 um photometry using MCMC to sample M_dust and T_dust, then integrate rest-frame 8-1000 um for L_IR and 42.5-122.5 um for L_FIR.  
  > "We estimate the $\rm L_{IR}$ as flux integrated from 8$-$1000 $\rm \mu m$ and total far-infrared luminosity ($\rm L_{FIR}$) from 42.5$-$122.5 $\rm \mu m$ in the rest frame (cite: helou85)." (Introduction); "In this section, we assume a uniform dust temperature ($\rm T_{dust}$) in each source and fit a modified blackbody function to the photometry greater than 50 $\mu m$ rest frame and the equations are adopted from (cite: weiss07):" (SED Fitting and Dust Mass); "We use MCMC algorithm to sample the dust mass and the dust temperature by giving an upper limit on the effective radius as 5000 pc in W and 1300 pc in E." (SED Fitting and Dust Mass)
   - parameter: 15% calibration/lensing error added in quadrature; upper limit on effective radius = 5000 pc (W) and 1300 pc (E)  
  > "A 15% absolute calibration error is added in quadrature to the statistical error to account for the uncertainty from the absolute flux calibration and lens modeling." (SED Fitting and Dust Mass); "We use MCMC algorithm to sample the dust mass and the dust temperature by giving an upper limit on the effective radius as 5000 pc in W and 1300 pc in E." (SED Fitting and Dust Mass)
   - assumption: Uniform dust temperature; kappa_nu = 0.038 * (nu / 372.7 GHz)^beta_Td [m^2 kg^-1] from Draine (2003); beta_Td fixed to 2.0  
  > "The frequency dependent dust absorption coefficient is adopted from (cite: draine03) $$\begin{equation}\rm \kappa_{\nu} = 0.038(\nu/372.7 \ GHz)^{\beta_{T_{d}}} \ [m^{2} \ kg^{-1}] \end{equation}$$" (SED Fitting and Dust Mass); "We fix $\beta_{T_{d}}$ to 2.0 (cite: greve12)." (SED Fitting and Dust Mass)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling of M_dust and T_dust

## Qualitative findings

- p-H2O(2_1,1-2_0,2) is detected in SPT0311-58 W at z = 6.9, representing the most distant detection of water in a galaxy without any evidence for an AGN.  
  > "This is the most detailed study of molecular gas content of a galaxy in the EoR to-date, with the most distant detection of $\rm H_{2}O$ in a galaxy without any evidence for active galactic nuclei in the literature." (Abstract); "We detect p-$\rm H_{2} O (2_{1,1}-2_{0,2})$ in W with a signal-to-noise $\sim$4 above the continuum." ($L_{H_{2}O} - L_{FIR}$ Correlation)
- There is no significant differential magnification between the CO transitions and the dust continuum in SPT0311-58 W, and the CO emission region marginally decreases in size with increasing J level.  
  > "From the top panel in Figure 11, we see that the magnification is consistent between the CO transitions and the dust continuum, i.e., we do not observe differential magnification between these components in SPT0311-58 W." (Magnification and Intrinsic Size); "From the bottom panel of the figure, we see that the CO emission area is marginally decreasing with increasing excitation level." (Magnification and Intrinsic Size)
- The CO SLEDs of SPT0311-58 W and E peak at J_up ~ 6, typical of high-redshift starburst SMGs, while high L_CO(10-9)/L_CO(6-5) and T_kin/T_dust > 1 suggest mechanical heating in addition to photoelectric heating.  
  > "Both the galaxies in SPT0311-58 peak at J$_{\rm up}$ $\sim$ 6, similar to other high-redshift starburst galaxies." (CO SLED and AGN Fraction); "The ratio of $\rm T_{kin}/T_{dust}$ is $>$ 1 in both galaxies, which indicates that there are additional heating mechanisms such as X-rays, stellar outflows, and supernovae in addition to photoelectric heating in dense molecular gas." (Summary and Conclusion)
- There is a statistically significant negative correlation between alpha_CO and T_dust in main sequence galaxies and SPT-SMGs, and no evidence for evolution of gas depletion time with redshift in SMGs at z > 3.  
  > "We fit a linear function to the main sequence galaxies and the SPT-SMGs using MCMC and find a statistically significant correlation between $\rm \alpha_{CO}$ and $\rm T_{dust}$." (Gas Mass Conversion Factor); "We find no evidence of evolution of depletion time with redshift in SMGs at z $>$ 3." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA Band 3 and 4 observations spatially resolve SPT0311-58 into West and East and sample mid-J CO, high-J CO(10-9), [CI](2-1), and p-H2O(2_1,1-2_0,2) to constrain the ISM density, temperature, gas mass, and heating mechanisms at z = 6.9.  
  > "In this paper, we combine the previous observations with high resolution molecular line imaging with ALMA, including the high-J CO transition, $\rm CO(10-9)$ and p-$\rm H_{2} O (2_{1,1}-2_{0,2})$." (Introduction); "For the continuum flux density in B3, we use the current observations which are at a higher resolution of $\sim$0.5$''$ compared to previous observations at $\sim$3.5$''$." (Ancillary Data)
- Why this method: Used [CII] template fitting because lines are non-Gaussian and blended; used parametric visibility-plane lens modeling (visilens) because SNR is insufficient for pixellated reconstruction and to account for correlated noise; used Turbulence non-LTE LVG modeling to realistically represent a continuous log-normal density distribution in turbulent gas with fewer parameters than a 2-component model.  
  > "Since the spectral lines are non-Gaussian and blended in the case of $\rm CO(7-6)$ and \[CI\]$\rm (2-1)$, and $\rm CO(10-9)$ and p-$\rm H_{2} O (3_{1,2}-2_{2,1})$, we use \[CII\] from the ALMA 240 GHz observations (cite: marrone18) as a template to derive the spectral properties." (Spectral Line Decomposition); "Since the signal-to-noise is not sufficiently high for pixellated modeling, we use a parametric lens modeling code, `visilens` (cite: spilker16)." (Lens Modeling); "The analysis is performed in the visibility plane instead of the image plane to account for the correlated noise in the interferometric images." (Lens Modeling)
- Alternative: Pixellated lensing reconstruction was not used for the new continuum and CO datasets because the signal-to-noise ratio is not sufficiently high.  
  > "Since the signal-to-noise is not sufficiently high for pixellated modeling, we use a parametric lens modeling code, `visilens` (cite: spilker16)." (Lens Modeling)
- Alternative: 1-component and 2-component LVG models were run alongside the Turbulence model, and N > 2 components were avoided due to increased degeneracies from additional free parameters.  
  > "However, due to the increased degeneracies with increased number of free parameters, we use 1 and 2 components in this analysis." (N-component and Turbulence Models)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): CO(3-2) from ATCA is spatially unresolved (5-6'') between W and E and had to be distributed by scaling to CO(6-5).  
  > "Since $\rm CO(3-2)$ is spatially unresolved ($\sim$5 $-$ 6$''$) into W and E, we distribute the flux density of $\rm CO(3-2)$ by scaling to $\rm CO(6-5)$ in the two galaxies." (Ancillary Data)
- Limitation (authors): Signal-to-noise is insufficient for lensing reconstruction in E and for p-H2O(2_1,1-2_0,2) in W, requiring adoption of fixed magnification factors from Marrone et al. (2018).  
  > "Since we do not have a good signal-to-noise for lensing reconstruction in E, we adopt a magnification of 1.3 for all the molecular lines. For the same reason, we adopt $\mu = 2.2$ from (cite: marrone18) in p-$\rm H_{2} O (2_{1,1}-2_{0,2})$ in W." (Lens Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): [CI](2-1) and p-H2O(2_1,1-2_0,2) are not significantly detected in E, and the lack of [CI](1-0) observations prevents direct measurement of the [CI] excitation temperature and confirmation of the [CI] abundance-density relation.  
  > "However, it has to be noted that, due to the lack of \[CI\]$\rm (1-0)$ observations, we cannot reliably confirm the relationship between \[CI\] abundance and gas density." (Model Outputs); "The non-detection of \[CI\]$\rm (2-1)$ and p-$\rm H_{2} O (2_{1,1}-2_{0,2})$ in E gives large uncertainty on the depletion time estimate." (Depletion Time)
- Limitation (authors): In the LVG models, T_kin/T_dust is assumed constant across all visual extinctions/densities rather than varying with extinction or density.  
  > "However, it has to be noted that, while T$_{\rm K}$/T$_{\rm dust}$ is a function of visual extinction (cite: tielens85), we consider a constant value at all extinctions." (Model Outputs); "One caveat is that we do not consider the change in $\rm T_{kin}$/$\rm T_{dust}$ with density in the LVG model." (Energy Budget)
- Limitation (authors): Observations of important neutral gas coolants [OI] (63 um) and [SiII] (35 um), which contribute >50% of total cooling, are unavailable.  
  > "However, since we do not have observations of the two important coolants \[OI\] and \[SiII\], which are found to contribute to more than 50% of the total cooling budget (cite: rosenberg15), we cannot provide the complete picture of the neutral gas." (Energy Budget)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distributing unresolved ATCA CO(3-2) flux between W and E using the CO(6-5) ratio assumes identical CO(6-5)/CO(3-2) excitation in both galaxies, which directly affects the low-J CO SLED and LVG-derived L'_CO(1-0).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deblending CO(7-6)+[CI](2-1) and CO(10-9)+p-H2O(3_1,2-2_2,1) by fixing the line profiles to the [CII] 158 um template assumes that high-J CO, [CI], and H2O share the exact same spatial/kinematic distribution as [CII].
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two different dust mass absorption coefficient normalizations are used in the paper: kappa_nu = 0.038 * (nu/372.7 GHz)^beta in Section 3.3 vs kappa_nu = 0.04 * (nu/250 GHz)^beta in Section 4.4, which introduces a systematic offset between MBB and LVG dust masses.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The empirical correlation between alpha_CO and T_dust is derived using M_dust and T_dust fitted from the same FIR photometry assuming a fixed GDMR = 100, creating correlated errors between the two axes.
