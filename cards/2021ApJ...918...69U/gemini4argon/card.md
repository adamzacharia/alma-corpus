## Reasoning

- Why this data: Submm/mm observations are required to uncover dust-enshrouded star formation and characterize the ISM kinematics and conditions via [C II] and CO(4-3); compact array configurations (e.g. C40-1) and multi-resolution tapering were chosen to recover spatially extended emission.  
  > "Massive star-forming galaxies are easily enshrouded by dust in an intensely star-forming phase, and often undetectable in the optical to NIR (e.g., (cite: 2020A&A...640L...8U) and references therein). Furthermore, the molecular/fine-structure lines at these wavelengths provide powerful tools to characterize the nature and conditions of the interstellar medium (ISMs) in galaxies." (Introduction); "To extract spatially extended emission and also perform an angular-resolution-matched comparison with CO(4–3) and 860 $\mu$m data, we also produce $1.0^{\prime\prime}$ and $1.4^{\prime\prime}$ cubes in the same way but applying varying $uv$-tapering and 80 km s$^{-1}$ velocity bins." (ALMA Band 8); "The C40–1 array configuration utilized was the most compact configuration at the time (baseline lengths of 15 to 279 m), which was suitable to detect extended components." (ALMA Band 7)
- Why this method: Connected-voxel 2 sigma masking before primary beam correction was used to effectively extract extended [C II] emission while suppressing noise; sequential image-plane fitting with imfit was used to deblend crowded continuum sources; 1.4" apertures were used for group line properties because deblending line cubes in the crowded core is not straightforward.  
  > "To isolate the emission from each component, bright sources were sequentially modeled and subtracted using casa/imfit." (Dust continuum in LAB1); "This process allows us to effectively extract extended emission from the data cube, suppressing the influence of noise." (Overview); "Since it is not straightforward to isolate line fluxes from the cube in this crowded region, we derive line properties for groups on the basis of 1.4$^{\prime\prime}$ resolution data." (Measurements of properties)
- Alternative: AGN activity as the source of [C II] or Ly-alpha emission (disfavored due to lack of X-ray/radio detection and higher observed L_[CII]/L_IR than typical AGNs, though a heavily obscured AGN cannot be completely excluded)  
  > "We note that there is currently no evidence to support AGN activity in LAB1 ((cite: 2009ApJ...700....1G)), although we cannot exclude the possibility that there is a heavily obscured AGN present. Nevertheless, the observed range of $L_{\rm [CII]}/L_{\rm IR}$ is higher than the value reported for $z=1-2$ AGNs (log($L_{\rm [CII]}/L_{\rm IR}$)$\sim-3.5$ for AGNs with $10^{13}-10^{14}L_{\rm IR}$, (cite: 2010ApJ...724..957S)), which is also consistent with a lack of AGN activity." (The origin of the [CII] emission)
- Alternative: Single virialized halo of M_halo <= 10^12 M_sun hosting the LAB1 galaxies (rejected as highly unlikely because the stellar mass of the four massive members implies a ~10^13 M_sun halo)  
  > "In LAB1, the galaxies are distributed within a region expected if they are bounded in a halo with $\lesssim10^{12}$ $M_\odot$. However, this scenario is highly unlikely." (Galaxy Assembly via multiple-merging); "Hence the stellar-mass-halo mass relation, which is derived from clustering analysis, suggests a halo with $\sim10^{13}$ $M_\odot$ (e.g., (cite: 2015A&A...576L...7D))." (Galaxy Assembly via multiple-merging)
- Alternative: Previously reported Ks-band galaxy K1 (K15b) as a z=3.1007 member of LAB1 (rejected due to non-detection of [C II] and dust continuum, supporting a lower redshift)  
  > "We cannot detect any \[C II\] (and dust continuum emission) from K1 and so our observations also do not support the idea that K1 is a member of LAB1. We do not include this source in discussion in this paper." (A galaxy previously reported to be at z=3.1)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Fluxes of fainter continuum sources in crowded regions may be underestimated due to difficulty of source deblending, and Band 3 beam size is too large to isolate upper limits on fainter sources.  
  > "$\dagger$ Some fraction of fluxes of fainter sources may be underestimated due to difficulty of source deblending. $\ddagger$ Upper limits are shown only for the brightest three sources since the beam size is too large to isolate limits on fainter sources." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): It remains unclear whether ALMA completely recovers all of the 850 um flux measured by single-dish SCUBA2 (remaining difference 0.7 +- 1.2 mJy).  
  > "We note that it remains unclear whether or not all of the emission observed by the single-dish telescope is completely recovered by ALMA or not, as the remaining uncertainty ($0.7\pm1.2$ mJy) indicates." (Dust continuum in LAB1)
- Limitation (authors): L_IR and SFR_IR estimates rely on single-point 860 um photometry scaled with SMG SED templates, which may overestimate the dust temperature in extended/intergalactic regions and thus underestimate L_[CII]/L_IR.  
  > "These estimates, however, are based on single point photometry and thus remain uncertain." (Measurements of properties); "Although, the current estimate has relied on SED templates of bright dusty star-forming galaxies due to the shortage of photometry; this may overestimate the dust temperature for an intergalactic region (or a region in the outskirts of a galaxy) and thus underestimate the $L_{\rm [CII]}/L_{\rm IR}$." (The origin of the [CII] emission)
- Limitation (authors): Line-of-sight projection effects (e.g., viewing a filament nearly face-on) could explain the small apparent velocity dispersion of the galaxies.  
  > "We note that a caveat is the influence of line of sight projection on the measured velocity dispersion. While a simple, spherical geometry is assumed in deriving the track of escape velocity in Figure 7, this may not the case." (Galaxy Assembly via multiple-merging)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Image-plane sequential Gaussian subtraction (imfit) in a complex, interacting system with extended bridge emission can introduce systematic residuals compared to simultaneous uv-plane modeling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Molecular gas mass estimates from CO(4-3) depend heavily on both the assumed excitation ratio (r_41 = 0.61) and conversion factor (alpha_CO = 3.6); if the central merging starburst has higher excitation or a ULIRG-like alpha_CO (~0.8), the molecular gas mass would be several times lower.
