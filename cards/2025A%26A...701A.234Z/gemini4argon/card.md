## Analysis recipe

1. **Select protocluster member galaxies within one virial radius**: Select galaxies from COSMOS2020 catalog using photometric/spectroscopic redshifts within 1 R_vir as defined in Sillassen et al. (2024)  
  > "within a virial projected radius based on the COSMOS2020 catalog (Weaver et al. 2022)" (2.1 NICE-COSMOS protoclusters); "For the following analysis, we focus on galaxies within one virial radius, 1 R vir, as defined in (Sillassen et al. 2024), as this area is also well covered by the NOEMA and ALMA observations." (2.1. NICE-COSMOS protoclusters)
   - parameter: |z_phot - z_spec,group| < 0.1(1 + z_spec,group)  
  > "< 0.1(1 + z spec , group ) within a virial projected radius based on the COSMOS2020 catalog" (2.1 NICE-COSMOS protoclusters)
   - parameter: r <= 1 R_vir (141 pkpc to 429 pkpc)  
  > "The virial radii range from 141 pkpc to 429 pkpc, and the number of member galaxies per structure varies between 13 and 31." (2.1. NICE-COSMOS protoclusters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive stellar masses and star formation rates for member galaxies**: Take stellar masses and SFRs from COSMOS2020 LePhare fits; for FIR-detected galaxies, cross-match with super-deblended catalog and compute SFR from 3 GHz radio emission (if S/N > 3) or 24 µm flux (if S/N_3GHz < 3 or radio-loud AGN)  
  > "We used stellar masses and star formation rates (SFRs) derived with LePhare (Arnouts et al. 2002) in COSMOS2020 (Weaver et al. 2022)." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates); "For galaxies detected at 3 GHz (Smolčić et al. 2017) with a signal-tonoise ratio (S/N) greater than 3, we calculated the SFR from L IR (Kennicutt 1998), as derived from the 3 GHz radio emission." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates); "For sources with S/N 3GHz < 3, we computed L IR from 24 µm fluxes using the conversion from Wuyts et al. (2008)." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates)
   - software: LePhare
   - parameter: cross-match separation < 1''  
  > "We first cross-matched the members in the superdeblended catalog (Jin et al. 2018) with a separation smaller than 1 ′′ ." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates)
   - parameter: S/N_3GHz > 3 threshold  
  > "For galaxies detected at 3 GHz (Smolčić et al. 2017) with a signal-tonoise ratio (S/N) greater than 3, we calculated the SFR from L IR (Kennicutt 1998), as derived from the 3 GHz radio emission." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates)
   - assumption: Chabrier (2003) initial mass function and ΛCDM cosmology (H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7)  
  > "We adopt a standard ΛCDM cosmology with H 0 = 70 km s −1 Mpc −1, Ω m = 0.3, and Ω Λ = 0.7, and a Chabrier (2003) initial mass function." (1. Introduction)
   - assumption: Infrared-radio correlation q_IR(z, M_*) from Delvecchio et al. (2021) and radio spectral index α = -0.8  
  > "This was done using the infrared radio correlation (IRRC, q IR ( z, M ⋆ ), Delvecchio et al. 2021) and a spectral index of α = -0.8." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared SFR_radio against SFR_24µm, deblended FIR SFR, LePhare SFR, and dust-continuum-scaled SFR (Fig. 1)  
  > "Comparison of SFRs derived from radio emission with those derived using other methods." (Figure 1 caption); "As we show in Fig. 1, SFR radio and SFR 24 µ m are consistent." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates)
3. **Measure dust continuum and CO fluxes and derive molecular gas masses**: Fit visibilities of NOEMA and ALMA 3 mm and 2 mm observations, then convert Rayleigh-Jeans dust continuum at rest-frame 850 µm (Hughes et al. 2017 prescription) and CO(3-2)/CO(4-3) line emission to molecular gas masses  
  > "The molecular gas mass is converted from the RayleighJeans dust continuum at 850 µm, M gas RJ, as calibrated with the prescription in Hughes et al. (2017)." (2.3. Gas masses); "For spatially resolved sources, we used a Gaussian model to fit the visibilities. Unresolved sources were modeled as point sources." (2.3. Gas masses); "We also estimated the gas masses from CO(3-2) or CO(4-3) line emission, M gas CO, by converting them to the ground-state CO(1-0) luminosity" (2.3. Gas masses)
   - assumption: CO-SLED of star-forming galaxies at z = 2.0 - 2.7 from ASPECS (Boogaard et al. 2020)  
  > "assuming the CO spectral line energy distribution (CO-SLED) of star-forming galaxies at z = 2.0 - 2.7 from the ASPECS survey (Boogaard et al. 2020)." (2.3. Gas masses)
   - assumption: α_CO derived using mass-SFR-metallicity relation from Genzel et al. (2015) and metallicity dependence of α_CO from Tacconi et al. (2018)  
  > "The conversion factor α CO was derived using the mass–SFR–metallicity relation from Genzel et al. (2015) and the metallicity dependence of α CO from Tacconi et al. (2018) for our sample." (2.3. Gas masses)
   - fitting: Gaussian model fit to visibilities for spatially resolved sources; point source model for unresolved sources  
  > "For spatially resolved sources, we used a Gaussian model to fit the visibilities. Unresolved sources were modeled as point sources." (2.3. Gas masses)
   - validation: Compared M_gas^RJ from dust continuum with M_gas^CO from CO lines, finding consistency within 0.3 dex (median ratio -0.18 dex, M_gas^RJ ~ 1.5x M_gas^CO)  
  > "The two methods yield consistent results within 0.3 dex, with M gas RJ approximately 1.5 times higher than M gas CO." (2.3. Gas masses)
4. **Analyze cluster-wide and galaxy-level star formation and gas scaling relations**: Evaluate ΣSFR/M_halo evolution, scale member SFRs to z = 2.5 to compare with the SFMS, examine radial distribution of stellar mass and starburstiness, and normalize µ_gas and τ_gas to A3COSMOS field relations  
  > "We adopted the integrated SFR and best estimates of halo masses of the eight protoclusters from (Sillassen et al. 2024) to obtain the total SFR per unit halo mass (Σ SFR /M halo )." (3.1. Enhanced Star Formation in Massive Halos in the Early Universe); "Galaxies at different redshifts are scaled to z = 2.5, following the SFMS normalization and redshift evolution from Schreiber et al. (2015)." (3.2. Star-forming main sequence in dense environments); "To assess the environmental impact on the gas content, we normalized µ gas and τ gas to the corresponding field levels, accounting for their dependence on stellar mass, starburstiness, and redshift." (3.4. Gas content)
   - parameter: z = 2.5 reference redshift for SFMS scaling  
  > "Galaxies at different redshifts are scaled to z = 2.5, following the SFMS normalization and redshift evolution from Schreiber et al. (2015)." (3.2. Star-forming main sequence in dense environments)
   - assumption: SFMS normalization and redshift evolution from Schreiber et al. (2015)  
  > "Galaxies at different redshifts are scaled to z = 2.5, following the SFMS normalization and redshift evolution from Schreiber et al. (2015)." (3.2. Star-forming main sequence in dense environments)
   - assumption: Field galaxy scaling relations µ_gas(z, M_*, R_SB) and τ_gas(z, M_*, R_SB) from Liu et al. (2019)  
  > "For the less massive galaxies, CO or dust continuum detections are limited to the most starbursting ones, which follow the scaling relation ( µ gas ( z, M ⋆, R SB ), τ gas ( z, M ⋆, R SB ), Liu et al. 2019) observed in the field galaxies." (4. Summary and discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- ΣSFR/M_halo increases steeply with redshift as (1+z)^5.4, reaching one to two orders of magnitude higher than the field at z > 2  
  > "Our analysis reveals a steep increase in the star formation rates per halo mass (Σ SFR /M halo ) with redshifts in these intensively star-forming protoclusters, reaching values one to two orders of magnitude higher than those observed in the field at z > 2." (abstract); "We find that the total SFR per unit halo mass (Σ SFR /M halo ) in protoclusters at z > 2 increases rapidly with redshift, scaling as (1+ z ) 5 . 4 (Fig. 3)." (4. Summary and discussion)
- Member galaxies generally follow the field star-forming main sequence, with a moderate enhancement at the low-mass end  
  > "The member galaxies still generally follow the same star-forming main sequence as in the field, with a moderate enhancement at the low-mass end." (abstract)
- Massive galaxies are strongly concentrated in the protocluster cores and are almost all detected in CO or dust continuum  
  > "which are concentrated in the core regions, are nearly all detected via CO or dust continuum emission." (3.4 Gas content)
- The most massive protocluster galaxies (M_* > 8 x 10^10 M_⊙) exhibit higher gas fractions (~2x) and longer gas depletion times than field counterparts while remaining on the main sequence  
  > "Notably, the most massive protocluster galaxies ( M ⋆ > 8 × 10 10 M ⊙ ) exhibit higher µ gas and τ gas than their field counterparts, while remaining on the star-forming main sequence." (abstract); "These massive galaxies generally exhibit higher gas content ( ∼ 2 × ) compared to their counterparts in the field (Fig. 6 a, b)." (3.4. Gas content)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The COSMOS field has extensive multi-wavelength ancillary data, and the FoV of NOEMA (~30'') and ALMA (~1') observations covers the 1 R_vir central region of the protoclusters.  
  > "The COSMOS field provides extensive ancillary data across optical, near-infrared (NIR), far-infrared (FIR), submillimeter, and radio wavelengths (Weaver et al. 2022; Jin et al. 2018; Smolčić et al. 2017)" (1. Introduction); "The field of view (FoV) of the NOEMA and ALMA observations, with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of ∼ 30 ′′ and ∼ 1 ′, respectively, is sufficiently large to encompass the area within the virial radius of the protoclusters." (3.3. Build-up of massive galaxies in the dense cores)
- Why this method: VLA 3 GHz observations have high spatial resolution (0.75'') to mitigate blending in crowded cores, and RJ dust continuum at rest-frame 850 µm traces the entire cold gas reservoir with higher sensitivity than high-J CO lines.  
  > "The high spatial resolution (0.75 ′′ ) of the VLA 3 GHz observations helps mitigate blending issues in crowded regions, which can affect FIR flux measurements." (2.2. Stellar masses and star formation rates); "This method leverages dust continuum emission, which is intrinsically associated with gas in the entire cold molecular gas reservoir (Genzel et al. 2015; Scoville et al. 2016), and offers a much more sensitive measure than high- J CO emission" (2.3. Gas masses)
- Alternative: Evaluated four alternative SFR tracers (24 µm, deblended FIR, LePhare SED, and protocluster FIR SED scaled by dust continuum) alongside 3 GHz radio SFR; used 24 µm instead of 3 GHz for radio-loud AGN 839791.  
  > "The comparison includes SFRs estimated from Wuyts et al. (2008, green) , deblended FIR fluxes from Jin et al. (2018, red), SFRs derived with LePhare as in COSMOS2020 (Weaver et al. 2022, blue), and SFRs scaled from overall average SEDs of the protoclusters to dust continuum (black)." (Figure 1 caption); "radio emission is dominated by AGN, we used SFR 24 µ m to minimize the contamination from AGN-related radio" (2.2 Stellar masses and star formation rates)
- Alternative: Estimated gas masses from both CO(3-2)/CO(4-3) line emission and Rayleigh-Jeans dust continuum, adopting dust continuum gas masses for consistency.  
  > "Table 1 lists the α CO values and gas masses calculated with different tracers. For consistency, in the following we adopt gas masses estimated from the dust continuum." (2.3. Gas masses)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cluster members selected via photometric redshifts may include interlopers, particularly affecting the low-mass end of the SFMS.  
  > "It is important to note that the cluster members were primarily selected based on photometric redshifts, which may include interlopers. This may affect the results shown in Fig. 4, especially at the low-mass end, where the contamination is most significant." (3.2. Star-forming main sequence in dense environments)
- Limitation (authors): The 3σ detection limit of NICE corresponds to M_gas ~ 10^11 M_⊙ at z ~ 2.5, restricting detections at low stellar mass to strong starbursts and leaving the gas content of non-detected low-mass members uncertain.  
  > "The 3 σ detection limit of the NICE observations corresponds to a gas mass of M gas ∼ 10 11 M ⊙ for galaxies at z ∼ 2.5, meaning that our sample predominantly includes the most massive members and the less massive galaxies with high starburstiness (Fig. 6c)." (3.4. Gas content); "It remains unclear whether the CO- or dustnondetected cluster members have enhanced or deficient gas content." (3.4. Gas content)
- Limitation (authors): The NICE survey selection is biased toward intensively star-forming protoclusters.  
  > "However, we caution that the NICE survey is biased toward protoclusters in active star-forming phases. The influence of such environments on member galaxies may differ from that observed in protoclusters selected through other methods." (4. Summary and discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Higher ΣSFR/M_halo at higher redshifts could be partly driven by lower halo masses, and declining gas content trends at z ~ 4 may partly reflect field sample selection bias.  
  > "We also caution that the higher Σ SFR /M halo values at higher redshifts could also be partly driven by lower halo masses." (4. Summary and discussion); "We speculate that our observed trend may partly reflect a selection bias in constructing the field sample, which tends to favor the brightest sources at high redshifts, typically located in dense environments." (3.4. Gas content)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High-J CO transitions (CO 3-2 and 4-3) require assumed excitation ladders (CO-SLED) to convert to CO(1-0), and rest-frame 850 µm continuum gas masses assume a fixed dust temperature / RJ calibration, which may vary across protocluster environments.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): One of the most gas-rich sources (HPC1001 central source) is noted as a potential blend of several galaxies, which could artificially inflate its single-galaxy stellar and gas mass.
