## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA Band 3 continuum and measure source properties**: Calibrate using the ALMA pipeline in CASA, image continuum with interactive tclean using manual mask and primary beam correction with both natural and Briggs robust=0 weighting, and fit 2D Gaussian functions to the robust=0 image to obtain positions, peak intensities, integrated fluxes, and deconvolved sizes. Image lower and upper sidebands separately to compute in-band spectral index.  
  > "Data reduction was carried out following standard procedures using the ALMA pipeline within CASA (v. 6.1.1-15)" (ALMA observations (Night 2)); "Continuum images were produced using `tclean` interactively and applying a manually selected mask. These images were corrected for primary beam attenuation and were produced using both natural weighting and a Briggs `robust=0` weighting (cite: briggs95)." (ALMA observations (Night 2)); "Table [\[tab:alma_res\]](#tab:alma_res) reports the peak coordinates, the peak intensity, the integrated flux, and the deconvolved size obtained by fitting a 2D Gaussian function to the high-resolution image (`robust=0`)." (Millimetre emission)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting and Briggs robust=0  
  > "These images were corrected for primary beam attenuation and were produced using both natural weighting and a Briggs `robust=0` weighting (cite: briggs95)." (ALMA observations (Night 2))
   - parameter: LSB central frequency 91.47886 GHz, USB central frequency 103.5115 GHz  
  > "To determine the spectral index of J1023 within the ALMA bandwidth, we imaged the continuum emission from the lower and upper sidebands, which have central frequencies of 91.47886 GHz and 103.5115 GHz, respectively." (Millimetre emission)
   - assumption: Distance of 1.37 kpc  
  > "The deconvolved size obtained from the 2D Gaussian fit in the `robust=0` image is $0.305''\times0.205''$, which corresponds to approximately 418AU $\times$ 281AU ($\sim$$6.3\times10^{15}$ cm $\times$ $4.2\times10^{15}$ cm) at a distance of 1.37 kpc" (Millimetre emission)

   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract ALMA millimetre light curves, mode-resolved fluxes, and short flare properties**: Produce natural-weighted images scan by scan (~4–5 min), during X-ray high and low mode time intervals (selected from NICER light curves with 2.5 counts s^-1 threshold), and over short time intervals (15 s) to search for flares; measure peak intensities, flux densities above 3sigma, and fit 2D Gaussians to flares.  
  > "To probe variability in the millimetre emission, we obtained images scan by scan (typically with a duration of $\sim$4–5 minutes), using a natural weighting. We then computed the peak intensities and flux densities considering the emission above $3\sigma$ level." (Millimetre emission); "Figure [3](#fig:timeseries2) also shows the ALMA time series extracted using time intervals of variable length corresponding to the durations of the X-ray-mode episodes." (Millimetre emission); "Therefore, we performed a dedicated search for short flaring episodes by extracting images of different durations using the entire dataset." (Millimetre emission)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: NICER threshold 2.5 counts s^-1 for high/low mode selection  
  > "We applied intensity thresholds to the light curve to select the time intervals corresponding to the high ($>$2.5 counts s$^{-1}$) and low ($<$2.5 counts s$^{-1}$) X-ray modes." (X-ray and UV emissions)
   - parameter: 3sigma detection threshold; 15 s intervals for peak flare measurements  
  > "We then computed the peak intensities and flux densities considering the emission above $3\sigma$ level." (Millimetre emission); "These values are measured at the peak of the flares over time intervals of length 15 s" (Millimetre emission)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting to flare images  
  > "The 2D Gaussian fitting (see Table [\[tab:flares\]](#tab:flares)) indicates that the emission is elongated along the north-east to south-west direction during Flare 1" (Millimetre emission)
   - validation: Estimated nominal rms absolute positional accuracy as beam_FWHM * (0.9 * S/N)^-1  
  > "The nominal rms absolute positional accuracy for standard ALMA observations can be estimated as $beam_{\rm FWHM} \times (0.9 \times {\rm S/N})^{-1}$" (Millimetre emission)
3. **Process multi-wavelength X-ray, UV, optical, NIR, polarimetric, and radio observations**: Reduce and extract light curves, spectra, photometry, polarimetry, and pulsar searches from XMM-Newton, NuSTAR, HST, NTT/SOFI, VLA, FAST, NICER, Swift, REM, and VLT/FORS2 following standard and literature prescriptions.  
  > "Table [\[tab:log\]](#tab:log) lists all the observations of J1023 presented in this paper. In the following, we describe these observations and the procedures adopted to process and analyse these data." (Observations and data analysis)
   - software: SAS, NUSTARDAS, stis_photons, DAOPHOT, CASA, invgain, PRESTO, NICERDAS, HEASOFT, IRAF, XSPEC, CIAO
   - parameter: VLA 60 s image intervals with niters=10, cycleniter=10; 3sigma detection threshold  
  > "To extract the light curve, we imaged the field using a 60 s time interval and a mask around the target with the CASA `tclean` task, setting `niters=10` and `cycleniter=10`." (VLA (Night 1))
   - parameter: VLT/FORS2 80 s combined bins, 2sigma detection threshold for LP  
  > "we combined the measurements so that each LP data point covers 80 s (four HWP angles $\times$ 10 s of integration $\times$ two sets). We define an LP measurement as a detection when it reaches a confidence level of greater than 2$\sigma$." (Optical polarimetric properties)
   - assumption: FAST pulse duty cycle between 0.05–0.3, DM range 3–100 pc cm^-3  
  > "we created de-dispersed time series for each pseudo-pointing over a dispersion measure (DM) range between 3 pc cm$^{-3}$ and 100 pc cm$^{-3}$." ## Qualitative findings

- First detection of millimetre continuum emission from PSR J1023+0038 and from a transitional millisecond pulsar in general, making J1023 the faintest X-ray binary firmly detected in the millimetre band.  
  > "We have reported the detection of millimetre continuum emission for the first time from J1023 (and from a tMSP in general) using ALMA observations at $\approx$100 GHz." (The millimetre continuum emission of J1023 and other X-ray binary systems); "J1023 currently holds the record as the faintest XRB firmly detected in the millimetre band." (The millimetre continuum emission of J1023 and other X-ray binary systems)
- Discovery of a new compact millimetre source ALMA J102349.1+003824 located ~22'' south-east of J1023 with no detected X-ray, UV, optical, NIR, or cm radio counterparts.  
  > "The other source, which we designate ALMA J102349.1$+$003824 following the naming convention of new sources discovered by ALMA, is located $\sim$22$^{\prime\prime}$ south-east of J1023 and was detected at a significance level of $\sim$9$\sigma$." (Millimetre emission)
- Two short (~15 s), bright (~0.7–1.0 mJy) millimetre flares were detected with ALMA, with the first occurring right at a switch from the X-ray high mode to the low mode.  
  > "The first episode (hereafter ‘Flare 1’) occurred on 2021 June 26 within the time range 22:19:29 – 22:19:44 (UTC) at the switch from high mode to low mode and reached a peak flux density of 1.01$\pm$0.08 mJy." (Millimetre emission)
- X-ray low-mode egress times are systematically longer than ingress times (~80% of episodes), consistent with rapid dynamical ejection of the inner accretion flow followed by slower thermal/viscous refilling.  
  > "As shown in Fig. [8](#fig:ingress_egress), the ingress time is shorter than the egress time from the low mode ($\sim 80\%$)." (The low-mode ingress and egress timescales)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Simultaneous multi-wavelength coverage from radio and millimetre through NIR, optical, UV, and X-rays is required to connect X-ray mode switching to jet/ejection phenomena and model the broadband SEDs in the high and low modes.  
  > "This paper presents the results of the largest multi-wavelength campaign ever performed on J1023. Our aim is to ultimately understand the root cause of the mode changes in the active sub-luminous X-ray state and its implication for our understanding of the accretion-ejection coupling in XRB systems that harbour rapidly spinning NSs." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Simultaneous MCMC fitting of both high- and low-mode SEDs allows sharing common physical parameters across modes while using wide uniform priors to avoid excluding true source parameters; time-resolved ALMA imaging on scan, mode, and 15 s timescales probes both minute-scale variability and rapid flares.  
  > "The integration times adopted above are effective for studying the variability of the millimetre emission on minute timescales, but may average out large enhancements of the millimetre flux on much shorter timescales (of the order of seconds). Therefore, we performed a dedicated search for short flaring episodes by extracting images of different durations using the entire dataset." (Millimetre emission); "Employing both datasets (high and low mode), we performed an MCMC sampling of the posterior probability density function for the parameter space of the two models simultaneously (since some of the free parameters are shared by both models)." (Model fit)
- Alternative: Intrinsic magnetospheric phenomena (intermittency or nulling) as the cause of the non-detection of radio pulsations, rejected because such phenomena were never observed when J1023 was in the radio pulsar state.  
  > "The non-detection of radio pulsations could be due to ‘intrinsic’ phenomena within the pulsar magnetosphere (i.e. irrespective of the presence of external drivers such as accreting material) such as intermittency and/or nulling (see e.g. (cite: weng22) for recent similar considerations for the system LS I $+$61 303). However, these phenomena were not observed during extensive radio timing campaigns when the system was in the radio pulsar state (e.g. (cite: archibald13; stappers14)), so they are unlikely to be the cause of the non-detection." (The non-detection of radio pulsations)
- Alternative: Scattering of radiation on the accretion disc surface as the origin of the optical linear polarisation, rejected due to the lack of modulation of the Stokes parameters with the binary orbital period.  
  > "Given the lack of modulation of the Stokes parameters with the orbital period, it is unlikely that the source of the polarisation is scattering of radiation in any region of the system (such as the surface of the accretion disc), in contrast to previous suggestions (cite: baglio16)." (Optical polarisation measurements and predictions of X-ray polarisation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): First half of the VLA observation was unusable due to bad weather conditions and was completely flagged out.  
  > "Unfortunately, the first half of the observation turned out to be unusable, possibly due to the bad weather conditions that occurred during the observation, and was completely flagged out." (VLA (Night 1))
- Limitation (authors): Most REM K-band images were of bad quality with an empty FOV, preventing K-band coverage during the low mode.  
  > "Unfortunately, most of the $K$-band images are of bad quality, resulting in an empty FOV." (Optical/NIR photometric properties)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of continuous ALMA coverage before and during the high-to-low mode switch for Flare 2 prevents determining the exact epoch of the flare onset.  
  > "The lack of continuous coverage in the millimetre band before and during the switch from the high to the low mode does not allow us to determine the exact epoch of the flare onset." (Millimetre emission)
- Limitation (authors): Offsets between ALMA flare peak positions and the radio position (~0.3–0.4'') are comparable to ALMA's absolute positional accuracy, requiring caution.  
  > "However, caution must be exercised, as this offset is comparable to the positional accuracy of ALMA." (Millimetre emission)
- Limitation (authors): No flare was observed simultaneously at two different frequencies (ALMA and VLA were on different nights), preventing proper constraint of the van der Laan expanding blob model parameters.  
  > "Since no flare in this study was observed simultaneously at two different frequencies, we are unable to properly constrain the model parameters." (Millimetre flares as the fingerprint of discrete ejecta)
- Limitation (authors): Scarce low-frequency data points and overlapping IR/optical components cause strong parameter correlations in the SED fit (between nu_sync and F_sync, and between F_0 and alpha_1).  
  > "This is however not surprising considering the scarce number of points at the lowest frequencies and the impossibility to constrain quantities such as $\nu_{\rm break}$ and $\nu_{\rm sync}$, due to numerous overlapping components at infrared and optical frequencies." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Significance of optical linear polarisation in high and low modes separately is not high enough to distinguish between a jet origin and a shock origin (compatible within 1sigma).  
  > "Unfortunately, the significance of our polarisation results obtained in the high and low modes separately is not high enough to verify this, as the results are compatible with each other within $1\sigma$ ($0.36^{+0.27}_{-0.35}\%$ in the low mode and 0.65$\pm$0.43% in the high mode)." (Optical polarisation measurements and predictions of X-ray polarisation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): VLA (Night 1) and ALMA (Night 2) observations were not simultaneous, so combining radio and millimetre flux densities in the mode-resolved SEDs relies on the assumption that the average radio flux in each mode is stationary across weeks.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two out of seven high-to-low mode switches covered by ALMA showed detectable millimetre flares, meaning the link between every mode switch and a discrete millimetre flare relies on invoking variable ejecta properties or absorption.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 4 (Tab:fit_res) in the paper text is missing the printed posterior median values for the shared parameters (T_*, Log r_in,opt/UV, Log nu_break, alpha_1, alpha_2, Log F_0), although some are quoted in the text and shown in Figure 11.
