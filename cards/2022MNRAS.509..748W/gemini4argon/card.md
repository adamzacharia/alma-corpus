## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract continuum source structures and properties**: Dendrogram extraction on the ALMA 1.05 mm continuum image out to the 30% primary beam response level, and 2D Gaussian fitting on ALMA and VLA continuum images  
  > "We use the ASTRODENDRO Python package (cite: rosolowsky08dendro) to extract structures across the mosaic area, out to the 30 per cent response level (shown in Figure [1](#fig:g19))." (Continuum emission); "Observed properties of the centimetre emission are estimated from 2D Gaussian fitting, and are presented in Table [\[tab:leaves\]](#tab:leaves)." (Continuum emission); "For the purposes of the SED, we measure the integrated flux density at each wavelength by fitting a single 2D Gaussian to the emission, consistent with the approach of (cite: cyganowski11a) for the SMA and CARMA datapoints: the resulting flux densities are presented in Table [1](#tab:sed_fluxes)." (SED modelling of MM1)
   - software: astrodendro, CASA (IMFIT)
   - parameter: I_min = 5 sigma_rms = 1.25 mJy beam^-1, Delta I_min = 1 sigma_rms = 0.25 mJy beam^-1, n_pix = 25 pixels  
  > "We set these parameters to $I_{\mathrm{min}}= 5\sigma_{\mathrm{rms}} = 1.25$ mJy beam$^{-1}$, $\Delta I_{\mathrm{min}}=1\sigma_{\mathrm{rms}} = 0.25$ mJy beam$^{-1}$, and $n_{\mathrm{pix}}=25$ pixels $\approx n_{\mathrm{pix,beam}}/2$ (where $n_{\mathrm{pix,beam}}$ is the number of pixels in the ALMA synthesised beam) respectively." (Continuum emission)
   - fitting: Intensity-weighted second moment (HWHM converted to FWHM) in astrodendro for ALMA Table 2; 2D Gaussian fitting (IMFIT) for VLA and SED flux densities  
  > "ALMA: sizes are calculated from the intensity-weighted second moment (HWHM, using ASTRODENDRO) converted to FWHM (and multiplied by $\sqrt{8\ln2}$; shown in Figure [3](#fig:mom1)). VLA: from two-dimensional Gaussian fitting." (Table 2)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify molecular line transitions and map velocity gradients**: Cross-match observed lines with JPL and CDMS catalogues, generate LTE synthetic spectra with CLASS/Weeds to confirm detections, compute moment 1 maps, and measure velocity gradient position angles from the slope connecting most blue-shifted and red-shifted positions  
  > "We use the JPL (cite: jpl) and CDMS (cite: cdms) catalogues to identify the observed molecular lines." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1); "we produce LTE synthetic spectra for each species (cite: herbst09) using the Weeds extension (cite: maret11) of the CLASS software, accounting for beam dilution." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1); "We measure the position angle of the velocity gradient in a similar way to that outlined by (cite: hunter14), by measuring the slope of the line that connects the most blue-shifted and most red-shifted positions for each line." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1)
   - software: GILDAS / CLASS (Weeds)
   - parameter: 19 lines in broad spws + 1 narrow-band CH3OH line; 3 lines with extended emission excluded from disc PA and inclination averages  
  > "For the 16 lines in Figure [3](#fig:mom1) shown to have compact emission (excluding the 3 with more extended emission) and the narrow-band CH$_{3}$OH line in Figure [4](#fig:masers)a, we find a mean position angle of $78.0 \pm 8.0 ^{\circ}$ (with standard error), in agreement with the position angle of MM1 from the 1.05 mm continuum of $78.7^{\circ}$." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1)
   - assumption: LTE conditions with hot core rotational temperatures > 100 K  
  > "A line is considered a firm detection if the LTE synthetic spectrum reasonably reproduces the observed brightness temperatures for typical model parameters expected of a hot core, such as line rotational temperatures of $>100$ K." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of velocity gradient PA with 1.05 mm continuum PA and perpendicularity to the SMA 12CO(2-1) bipolar outflow PA  
  > "The position angle of the candidate disc is $96.5\pm9.4^{\circ}$ from that of the outflow, consistent with a perpendicular orientation and the observation of a disc-outflow system." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1)
3. **Fit 6.7 GHz Class II CH3OH maser positions and ring geometry**: Fit 2D Gaussian point sources to each channel with >5 sigma maser detection using CASA IMFIT, then fit an ellipse to the maser positions  
  > "To characterise the morphology and kinematics of the maser emission on scales smaller than the beam, we fit the observed emission in each channel with a $>$5$\sigma$ maser detection with a 2D Gaussian using the CASA IMFIT task." (6.7 GHz methanol masers)
   - software: CASA (IMFIT)
   - parameter: >5 sigma detection criterion per channel; point source fit with axes and PA fixed to synthesised beam  
  > "The masers were fit as point sources, i.e. with the major and minor axes and position angle of the emission fixed to those of the synthesised beam (see Section [2.2](#sec:vla_obs)), as we expect the emission to be unresolved (cite: hunter18; towner21)." (6.7 GHz methanol masers)
   - assumption: Maser emission is unresolved in each channel; circular symmetry assumed when estimating inclination of the maser ring  
  > "The masers were fit as point sources, i.e. with the major and minor axes and position angle of the emission fixed to those of the synthesised beam (see Section [2.2](#sec:vla_obs)), as we expect the emission to be unresolved (cite: hunter18; towner21)." (6.7 GHz methanol masers); "we note that the inclination angle of the maser ring, calculated from the fitted size of the ellipse (§[\[sec:masers\]](#sec:masers)) assuming circular symmetry, is $i=61^{\circ}$, within the range found for the thermal lines."    - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting with CASA IMFIT; ellipse fitting to maser centroids

## Reasoning

- Why this data: EGO G19.01–0.03 MM1 drives a highly collimated bipolar outflow and is associated with 6.7 GHz Class II CH3OH masers, making it an excellent target to search for an accreting MYSO disc at sub-arcsecond resolution.  
  > "In all, these signatures indicate that MM1 is a young millimetre source at an early stage of evolution that is undergoing active accretion, with the highly collimated outflow suggesting that MM1 is an excellent target in the search for discs around MYSOs." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Dendrograms (astrodendro) extract hierarchical structures at regular isocontours to account for non-Gaussian low-lying extended 1.05 mm emission; LTE synthetic spectra in CLASS/Weeds distinguish blended lines in the coarse (~1 km s^-1) wideband spectra.  
  > "Whilst a single 2D Gaussian would reasonably reproduce the MYSO’s bright compact emission, it could not account for the non-Gaussian low-lying extended emission. Dendrograms, in contrast, extract structures at regular isocontours of a map, and are typically used in the understanding of hierarchical structure" (Continuum emission); "As a number of candidate molecular lines may have indistinguishable rest frequencies within the $\sim$1 km s$^{-1}$ spectral resolution of the data, we produce LTE synthetic spectra for each species (cite: herbst09) using the Weeds extension (cite: maret11) of the CLASS software, accounting for beam dilution." (Compact molecular line emission towards MM1)
- Alternative: Edge-on (i ~ 90 deg) and face-on (i ~ 0 deg) disc inclinations were considered and ruled out based on the high velocity (>100 km s^-1) and extended morphology of the bipolar outflow.  
  > "We note that while higher angular resolution observations, which better resolve the candidate disc, are required to better measure $i$ directly, we can rule out both very low and very high values of $i$ based on the observed properties of the outflow." (Kinematic Modelling of MM1)
- Alternative: A single-component dust-only SED model was rejected because dust emission alone cannot explain the centimetre-wavelength emission.  
  > "As Figure [4](#fig:sed) illustrates, the millimetre-wavelength emission of MM1 is dominated by dust, but dust emission alone cannot explain the observed shape of the SED." (SED modelling of MM1)
- Alternative: Swollen, non-ZAMS accreting protostar configuration considered as a contributor to low ionising flux, though accretion models still predict L > 10^5 L_sun for M_* > 30 M_sun.  
  > "Theoretical models of accreting protostars also predict L$>$10$^5$ L$_{\odot}$ for M$_*>$30 M$_{\odot}$ (cite: kuiper13). This, together with the positional offset noted in §[\[sec:continuum\]](#sec:continuum), motivates the consideration of another possibility: that the central stellar mass is instead distributed in a high-mass binary system." (The Nature of MM1)
- Alternative: An asymmetric HII region or ionised jet from a single MYSO was considered to explain the 5.01 cm positional offset, but rejected because the offset is not aligned with the bipolar outflow axis.  
  > "In a single MYSO scenario, however, the centimetre emission from an H ii region expanding into the cavities of the bipolar outflow (cite: sartorio19; kuiper18) would be expected to be aligned with the outflow axis, which does not appear to be the case in G19.01 (Figure [\[fig:leaves\]](#fig:leaves)c)." (The Nature of MM1)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 3D motions of the 6.7 GHz CH3OH masers (relative contributions of rotation vs expansion/infall) cannot be established without multi-epoch VLBI observations.  
  > "Multi-epoch high-resolution observations of the 6.7 GHz masers in G19.01 MM1 would be required to establish the relative contributions of rotation and expansion or infall to the 3D maser motions to help distinguish among these scenarios." (6.7 GHz methanol masers)
- Limitation (authors): Strong degeneracy between enclosed mass M_enc and inclination angle i in PV modelling; inclination estimates from different lines may be affected by abundance, chemistry, optical depth, and temperature.  
  > "As seen in equation [\[eq:PV\]](#eq:PV), there is a well-known degeneracy between the enclosed mass and disc inclination angle." (Kinematic Modelling of MM1); "We caution, however, that the broad range of inclinations estimated from different molecular species may be influenced by molecular abundance, chemistry, optical depth, and/or temperature effects." (Kinematic Modelling of MM1)
- Limitation (authors): Inferred M_enc may be an overestimate because modelling is limited to the outer disc (1700-2000 AU) where rotation is expected to be super-Keplerian.  
  > "We note that since we are limited to a narrow range of radii in the outer disc, the inferred M$_{\mathrm{enc}}$ may be an overestimate since rotation in the outer disc is expected to be super-Keplerian (cite: kuiper11; kuiper18)." (Kinematic Modelling of MM1)
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution (~0.4'' ~ 1600 AU) is insufficient to resolve the inner disc or disentangle abundance, excitation, and optical depth effects in PV diagrams.  
  > "Disentangling the effects of abundance, excitation, and optical depth would require well-resolved images of a range of molecular species and transitions, and hence higher-resolution (sub)millimetre observations." (Kinematic Modelling of MM1)
- Limitation (authors): The 7-parameter SED model fit to 6 datapoints is not unique due to parameter degeneracies, and the dust temperature T_d is effectively unconstrained.  
  > "We note that this model is not unique, as there are degeneracies between model parameters that cannot be broken without additional high-resolution data: in particular, the dust temperature T$_d$ is effectively unconstrained." (SED modelling of MM1)
- Limitation (authors): Isothermal dust mass estimates do not capture spatial variations in temperature, opacity, and optical depth within the disc, and the adopted 100-130 K temperature range may be an underestimate on ALMA scales.  
  > "We note, however, that these estimates do not capture expected variations in temperature and opacity within a disc (cite: carrasco19; jankovic19), and that our adopted temperature range may underestimate the temperature on the smaller sizescales probed by our ALMA observations." (Disc properties and stability)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The kinematic modelling explores only a very narrow annulus (R_in = 1700 AU to R_out = 2000 AU) because the ~0.4'' beam (~1600 AU) barely resolves the candidate disc, and fits are performed by eye rather than via forward radiative transfer / visibility modelling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Super-Keplerian rotation in the outer disc / infalling envelope is explicitly noted (referencing Rolf Kuiper's work, who is also thanked as the referee in the acknowledgements) as a reason M_enc may be overestimated.
