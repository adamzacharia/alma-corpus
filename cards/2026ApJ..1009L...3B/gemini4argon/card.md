## Analysis recipe

1. **Pre-process raw Keck/NIRC2 vortex coronagraph frames and subtract stellar PSF via PCA-ADI and RDI**: Apply dark-field and flat-field corrections, bad pixel interpolation in a 5x5 box, geometrical distortion correction, PCA-based sky subtraction, image registration via speckle cross-correlation, and stellar PSF removal using annular and full-frame PCA-ADI as well as PCA-RDI and median-filtered de-rotation.  
  > "The raw data were processed with a custom pipeline based on the Vortex Image Processing package (`VIP`; (cite: GomezGonzalez2017; Christiaens2023))." (Data reduction); "We then removed the stellar point spread function (PSF) using a principal PCA-ADI approach implemented in `VIP`, exploring different numbers of principal components (PCs) together with both annular and full-frame PCA methods." (Data reduction); "To investigate the detectability of circumstellar disks in our observations, we applied a reference differential imaging (RDI) approach." (Disks retrieval)
   - software: VIP
   - parameter: Bad pixel replacement box: 5x5 neighboring box  
  > "Using `VIP`, bad, hot, and dead pixels were replaced with the median value within a $5\times5$ neighboring box." (Data reduction)
   - parameter: Annular PCA parameters: 0.2-FWHM parallactic angle threshold, 1-FWHM inner mask, 15-pixel (0.15 arcsec) wide annuli  
  > "For the annular PCA, we adopted a 0.2-Full Width at Half Maximum (FWHM) parallactic angle threshold (to partially mitigate the effects of self-subtraction), except for RX J1633.9-2442, where the limited field rotation prevented its use, together with a 1-FWHM inner mask and 15-pixel (0.15$^{\prime\prime}$) wide annuli." (Data reduction)
   - parameter: Pixel scale: 9.971 mas pixel^-1  
  > "For comparison, the pixel scale of the NIRC2 vortex coronagraph is 9.971 mas pixel$^{-1}$ (cite: Service2016)." (Observing strategy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Evaluated significance across a wide range of subtracted PCs (1 to >27 PCs) and injected fake planets to optimize RDI library size.  
  > "The optimal number of reference frames was determined by injecting a fake planet into the science cubes and measuring its signal-to-noise ratio after PCA-RDI while iterating over the library size." (Disks retrieval); "First, we applied different PCA approaches (as described in Section [2](#sec:obs_red)) over a wide range of subtracted PCs, and estimated the detection significance in each case." (Elias 2-24)
2. **Derive astrometry and photometry of detected point sources using NEGFC**: Negative Fake Companion (NEGFC) technique using unocculted stellar PSF models to measure separation, position angle, and flux ratio relative to the host star.  
  > "Astrometry and photometry for all detected point sources were derived using the NEGFC technique (cite: Marois2010; Lagrange2010) implemented in `VIP`, yielding the companion separation, position angle, and flux ratio relative to the host star." (Data reduction)
   - software: VIP
   - fitting: Initial position and flux estimates from firstguess() refined with MCMC sampling via mcmc_negfc_sampling().  
  > "Initial estimates of the position and flux were obtained with the `firstguess()` function and refined using `mcmc_negfc_sampling()`." (Data reduction)
   - validation: Injected negative companion with MCMC parameters to verify residual noise is consistent with radial background, and injected positive synthetic companion at a different PA to confirm recovery.  
  > "We then removed the companion from the reduced data cube by injecting a negative companion with the parameters derived from the MCMC analysis, and re-processed the data using the different PCA approaches." (Elias 2-24); "Finally, we injected a synthetic positive companion with the MCMC-derived parameters at a different position angle and verified whether it could be recovered, finding that the injected companion is robustly recovered." (Elias 2-24)
3. **Compute 5-sigma contrast curves and planetary mass detection limits**: Compute 5-sigma contrast curves across multiple numbers of PCs taking the deepest contrast at each separation, accounting for small-sample statistics with a t-distribution, and convert contrasts to planet mass limits using evolutionary models.  
  > "We computed the $5\sigma$ contrast curves for each target (Figure [3](#contrasts), left, and Table [\[contrast_table\]](#contrast_table) in Appendix [7](#app:contrasts)) using the `AppleFy` package (cite: Bonse2023), based on the ADI-PCA procedure implemented in `VIP`." (Detection limits); "The corresponding mass detection limits are shown in Figure [3](#contrasts) (right), obtained by converting the contrast curves using the ages and distances listed in Table [1](#obs), together with the ATMO-NEQ-STRONG 2023 evolutionary models (cite: Chabrier2023)." (Detection limits)
   - software: AppleFy, VIP
   - parameter: Detection threshold: 5 sigma  
  > "We computed the $5\sigma$ contrast curves for each target (Figure [3](#contrasts), left, and Table [\[contrast_table\]](#contrast_table) in Appendix [7](#app:contrasts)) using the `AppleFy` package (cite: Bonse2023), based on the ADI-PCA procedure implemented in `VIP`." (Detection limits)
   - assumption: Gaussian noise with t-distribution for small-sample statistics; system ages and distances from Table 1 (1 Myr upper limit for Elias 2-24); ATMO-NEQ-STRONG 2023 models.  
  > "This approach accounts for small-sample statistics (cite: Mawet2014) by adopting a t-distribution for the test statistic under the assumption of Gaussian noise." (Detection limits); "For this target, no uncertainty on the detection limits is reported, since we adopted an upper-limit age of 1 Myr." (Detection limits)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Test common proper motion, background false alarm probability, and orbital motion of Elias 2-24 b**: Compare 2018 and 2020 Keck astrometry against static background hypothesis, compute proper-motion False Alarm Probability (FAP) using a synthetic star sample from the Besancon Galaxy Model, and estimate expected Keplerian orbital displacement by 2026.  
  > "To verify whether the candidate’s astrometry is compatible with that of a bound companion, we first compare its position in the 2020 epoch with the expected position of a static background object (Figure [2](#propermotiontest))." (Elias 2-24); "We first generated a synthetic sample of background stars using the Besançon Galaxy Model interface[^2] (cite: Czekaj2014), selecting stars at distances between 0 and 50 kpc within a 5 arcmin radius of the position of Elias 2-24." (Elias 2-24); "Finally, we estimated the angular motion of Elias 2-24 b between our 2018 Keck epoch and 1 June 2026, assuming a circular orbit coplanar with the disk." (Elias 2-24)
   - software: Besancon Galaxy Model
   - parameter: Besancon query: distances 0-50 kpc, radius 5 arcmin around Elias 2-24, apparent L' = 15-17 mag, FoV = 2.6 arcsec  
  > "We first generated a synthetic sample of background stars using the Besançon Galaxy Model interface[^2] (cite: Czekaj2014), selecting stars at distances between 0 and 50 kpc within a 5 arcmin radius of the position of Elias 2-24. We further selected stars with apparent $L'$ magnitudes comparable to that of the candidate (15–17 mag), assuming an interstellar extinction of $A_V=8.7$ mag (more details in Section [4](#sec:characterization))." (Elias 2-24); "Considering a field of view of $2.6^{\prime\prime}$, we derive a FAP of $1.50\cdot10^{-4}$, corresponding to a $3.6\sigma$ significance." (Elias 2-24)
   - assumption: Circular orbit coplanar with the disk (inclination = 23.6 deg, PA = 43.4 deg) and stellar mass = 0.78 +0.35/-0.13 M_sun; interstellar extinction A_V = 8.7 mag.  
  > "Adopting a disk inclination of $23.6^\circ$ and a position angle of $43.4^\circ$ (cite: Cieza2017), together with a stellar mass of $0.78^{+0.35}_{-0.13}$ M~$\odot$~ (cite: Andrews2018), we infer a predicted motion of $6.2^{+2.2}_{-1.1}$$^\circ$." (Elias 2-24)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Characterize the evolutionary mass, effective temperature, and mass accretion rate of Elias 2-24 b**: Convert L' contrast to absolute L' magnitude correcting for distance and extinction, compare with 1 Myr isochrones of ATMO-NEQ-STRONG 2023, AMES-COND, AMES-DUSTY, and BEX (coldest/warm, hot, hottest start) evolutionary models, and interpolate circumplanetary disk accretion models of Zhu (2015).  
  > "In order to estimate the absolute magnitude of the planet, we combine our derived $L'$ contrast and the distance of $139.3$ pc reported in Table [1](#obs), while accounting for interstellar extinction." (Characterization of Elias 2-24 b); "To investigate the dependence of the evolutionary mass and effective temperature (T~eff~) of Elias 2-24 b on the initial conditions (e.g., the initial entropy) and atmospheric properties, we estimated these quantities using different evolutionary models." (Characterization of Elias 2-24 b); "Comparing our estimate for the absolute magnitude of Elias 2-24 b (in Section [4](#sec:characterization)) with the models of (cite: Zhu2015), and assuming an inner circumplanetary disk radius of 2 R~Jup~, we derive $M_p\dot{M}=2.78\cdot10^{-6}$ M~Jup~$^2$ yr$^{-1}$ through logarithmic interpolation." (Discussion)
   - software: completo21
   - parameter: Inner circumplanetary disk radius: 2 R_Jup; planet masses tested for accretion rate: 0.41, 1.72, and 3.0 M_Jup  
  > "Comparing our estimate for the absolute magnitude of Elias 2-24 b (in Section [4](#sec:characterization)) with the models of (cite: Zhu2015), and assuming an inner circumplanetary disk radius of 2 R~Jup~, we derive $M_p\dot{M}=2.78\cdot10^{-6}$ M~Jup~$^2$ yr$^{-1}$ through logarithmic interpolation. Adopting the mass predictions from the hydrodynamical models of (cite: Zhang2018), we then estimate accretion rates for two masses: 0.41 and 1.72 M~Jup~." (Discussion); "We also use the (cite: Zhu2015) models to estimate the accretion rate for a mass of 3.0 M~Jup~, corresponding to our central evolutionary mass estimate, deriving a value of $\dot{M}=9.26\cdot10^{-7}$ M~Jup~ yr$^{-1}$." (Discussion)
   - assumption: Age = 1 Myr, distance = 139.3 pc, A_V = 8.7 mag (A_L' = 0.5 mag, equal for star and planet), evolutionary models neglect accretion luminosity.  
  > "As reported in (cite: Cieza2021), Elias 2-24 has an extinction of $A_V=8.7$ mag, corresponding to $0.5$ mag in the $L'$ band. Assuming that the star and the planet experience the same extinction, we derive an absolute $L'$ magnitude of $10.08^{+0.53}_{-0.87}$ mag for Elias 2-24 b." (Characterization of Elias 2-24 b); "We then estimated the evolutionary mass and effective temperature of Elias 2-24 b by assuming an age of 1 Myr, while noting that the system may be even younger." (Characterization of Elias 2-24 b); "All these models neglect accretion effects, assuming that the observed luminosity is entirely due to the internal luminosity of the planet." (Characterization of Elias 2-24 b)
   - fitting: Logarithmic interpolation for accretion rate

## Qualitative findings

- No clear circumstellar disk emission is detected in any of the seven targets using either ADI or RDI techniques.  
  > "We report no clear disk emission detections using either the ADI or RDI techniques, with only Elias 2-24 and PDS 70 (more details in Appendix [8](#app:pds70)) exhibiting point-like sources in our reductions." (Confirmation of Elias 2-24 b)
- Elias 2-24 b is confirmed as a physically bound Jovian protoplanet inside the narrow 1.3 mm continuum gap of the Elias 2-24 disk, making it the youngest directly imaged exoplanet discovered to date (age <= 1 Myr).  
  > "Since Elias 2-24 b (age $\lesssim$ 1 Myr) is the youngest exoplanet discovered to date, its confirmation has important implications for planet formation timescales and the origin of the gaps observed in very young disks." (Abstract); "The consistency among the multiple observations, together with the repeated detection of the source at a compatible location across different instruments and observatories and the common proper motion analysis, allows us to confirm the planetary nature of the candidate, hereafter referred to as Elias 2-24 b." (Elias 2-24)
- Elias 2-24 b demonstrates that giant planets can form via core accretion at ~50 au within <= 1 Myr and its luminosity is better described by hot-start, dusty/non-equilibrium models due to ongoing accretion.  
  > "Independently of the exact mass of Elias 2-24 b, its confirmation has strong implications for planet formation because it demonstrates that a Jovian planet can be formed by core accretion at $\sim$50 au in just $\lesssim$1 Myr, much faster than what can be inferred from the PDS 70 and WISPIT 2 systems, which are both $\sim$5 Myr old (cite: Muller2018; Close2025)." (Discussion); "We therefore conclude that the upper mass and effective temperature limits of Elias 2-24 b are better described by hot-start, dusty evolutionary models." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected young (<= 10 Myr) systems from the ODISEA survey and backup targets hosting protoplanetary disks with clear millimeter/submillimeter substructures (gaps, rings, cavities) potentially carved by embedded protoplanets, and observed PDS 70 in Ms band to estimate the L-M color of its protoplanets.  
  > "Our sample was initially drawn from the targets included in the ODISEA survey (cite: Cieza2019). More specifically, we focused on young ($\lesssim$10 Myr) systems hosting disks with clear substructures in millimeter/submillimeter, such as gaps, rings, and cavities, potentially carved by embedded protoplanets." (Sample presentation); "PDS 70 was observed in the $M_s$ band (central wavelength $4.670\,\mu$m) to estimate the $L$–$M$ color of its protoplanets in the event of a detection (more details in Appendix [8](#app:pds70))." (Observing strategy)
- Why this method: Used AppleFy because it accounts for small-sample statistics via a t-distribution and evaluates multiple numbers of principal components simultaneously to select the deepest contrast at each separation; used ATMO-NEQ-STRONG 2023 models because they describe out-of-equilibrium atmospheres expected in extremely young targets; also tested median-filtered de-rotation because it is more effective than ADI at recovering azimuthally symmetric disks.  
  > "Tests on datasets with known disks showed that this approach is more effective than ADI at recovering azimuthally symmetric disks (cite: Canovas2017), which are strongly affected by ADI self-subtraction, and performs better at larger angular separations (cite: 2018MNRAS.480..236Z)." (Disks retrieval); "A key advantage of `AppleFy` is the ability to evaluate multiple numbers of principal components simultaneously and derive an optimal curve by selecting the deepest contrast at each separation." (Detection limits); "These models are well suited to describe out-of-equilibrium atmospheres, which are expected in our targets given the extreme youth of the sample." (Detection limits)
- Alternative: Static or moving background star hypothesis for Elias 2-24 b (rejected at 3.2 sigma for static and 3.6 sigma / FAP = 1.50e-4 for moving background star).  
  > "The comparison between the expected position of a background source, shown as a blue square, and the position of the candidate, shown as a purple circle, shows that the candidate is incompatible with the static background hypothesis at the 3.2$\sigma$ confidence level." (Elias 2-24); "Considering a field of view of $2.6^{\prime\prime}$, we derive a FAP of $1.50\cdot10^{-4}$, corresponding to a $3.6\sigma$ significance. We therefore conclude that the candidate is physically bound to Elias 2-24 at a high level of confidence." (Elias 2-24)
- Alternative: Gravitational instability formation followed by inward migration for Elias 2-24 b (considered unlikely due to lack of spiral fragmentation and lack of evidence for gap or disk size migration in Ophiuchus).  
  > "Strictly speaking, it is possible that a giant planet could form by gravitational instability in a fragmenting disk, very early on and at large distances, and then migrate inward (together with the gap) to a region of the disk showing no evidence of fragmentation. However, this scenario seems unlikely since there is no evidence of gap migration either." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Coldest-start and warm-start evolutionary mass solutions (~13-14 M_Jup) for Elias 2-24 b (ruled out because hydrodynamical models show a planet > 1.72 M_Jup would produce disk structures inconsistent with the observed gap).  
  > "Indeed, hydrodynamical models by (cite: Zhang2018), based on the observed properties of the disk gap (e.g., its width, edge ellipticity, and asymmetry), infer a planetary mass between 0.41 and 1.72 M~Jup~ to reproduce the observed disk structure. This range rules out the upper mass estimates obtained with the coldest-start and warm-start models (Table [3](#masses)), while remaining compatible, at least within the lower end of the uncertainties, with the values inferred from the ATMO-NEQ-STRONG 2023 and the BEX-DUSTY hot-start and hottest-start models." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The single-epoch detection significances of Elias 2-24 b (3.85 sigma in 2018, 2.82 sigma in 2020) do not reach the standard 5-sigma confidence threshold.  
  > "Although the detections do not reach the standard $5\sigma$ confidence threshold, the combination of these results with the previous tests strengthens the interpretation of the observed signals as genuine detections rather than residual speckle noise." (Elias 2-24)
- Limitation (authors): Keplerian orbital motion of Elias 2-24 b between the 2018 and 2020 epochs (~1.5 deg expected) is smaller than the astrometric uncertainties and cannot yet be probed.  
  > "As of now, we are not yet able to probe the Keplerian motion of Elias 2-24 b, despite its re-detection in the 2020 Keck epoch. Assuming a circular orbit, the expected angular motion between the two epochs is only $\sim1.5^\circ$, which is smaller than our uncertainties." (Elias 2-24)
- Limitation (authors): Evolutionary models neglect accretion luminosity and disk extinction uncertainties, so inferred evolutionary masses and effective temperatures are upper limits.  
  > "All these models neglect accretion effects, assuming that the observed luminosity is entirely due to the internal luminosity of the planet. However, as pointed out by several studies (e.g., (cite: Mordasini2017)), at such young ages the accretion luminosity can dominate the intrinsic luminosity of the planet. This results in an overestimation of the internal luminosity and, consequently, of the inferred evolutionary mass and effective temperature. Therefore, the values reported in Table [3](#masses) should be interpreted as upper limits rather than as definitive estimates." (Characterization of Elias 2-24 b)
- Limitation (authors): Inner regions of the images (within ~1-4 FWHM) remain dominated by speckle noise and imperfect stellar PSF subtraction, and limited field rotation degraded contrast for RX J1633.9-2442 and PDS 70.  
  > "For the annular PCA, we adopted a 0.2-Full Width at Half Maximum (FWHM) parallactic angle threshold (to partially mitigate the effects of self-subtraction), except for RX J1633.9-2442, where the limited field rotation prevented its use, together with a 1-FWHM inner mask and 15-pixel (0.15$^{\prime\prime}$) wide annuli." (Data reduction); "The inner region (within $\sim1$–$4$ FWHM), however, remains dominated by speckle noise and imperfect stellar PSF subtraction." (Disks retrieval); "We do not detect any of the PDS 70 planets, nor any emission from the disk, due to the limited field rotation (Table [1](#obs)), which resulted in poor contrast (Figure [3](#contrasts) and Table [\[contrast_table\]](#contrast_table)) at all separations." (PDS 70)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Both Keck detections of Elias 2-24 b have moderate formal significance (3.85 sigma in 2018 and 2.82 sigma in 2020), so the confirmation relies heavily on spatial coincidence with previous VLT/NACO and ALMA CO detections.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extinction toward the embedded planet is assumed equal to the stellar extinction (A_V = 8.7 mag), whereas local circumplanetary or disk dust could increase extinction significantly and alter both non-accreting and accreting mass estimates.
