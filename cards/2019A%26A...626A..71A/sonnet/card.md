## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure continuum positions, sizes and fluxes**: 2D Gaussian fits in the image plane  
  > "sub-millimetre coordinates, deconvolved sizes, and fluxes calculated by fitting two-dimensional (2D) Gaussians in the image plane" (3.1 Continuum)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive disc masses from continuum**: Optically thin dust emission with single temperature Planck function  
  > "The disc masses (*M$_\mathrm{disc}$*) were calculated from the continuum fluxes (*F$_\mathrm{0.87mm}$*)" (4.1 disc mass)
   - parameter: kappa 0.87mm = 0.0175 cm2/g; d = 139 pc  
  > "A distance of 139 $\pm$ 6 pc (cite: Mamajek2008) and $\kappa$$_\mathrm{0.87mm}$ of 0.0175 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Ossenkopf1994)" (4.1 disc mass)
   - assumption: Tdust = 15 K; gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "The calculated disc masses (gas + dust, assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100)" (4.1 disc mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with literature masses; masses drop by ~3 for Tdust = 30 K  
  > "the disc masses will decrease by a factor of $\sim$3 when a dust temperature of 30 K is assumed instead" (4.1 disc mass)
3. **Estimate stellar masses from PV diagrams**: Peak emission per channel via imfit, projected on disc PA; Keplerian v∝r^-0.5 fit to |v|>2 km/s; infall profile for IRAS 16253-2429  
  > "The peak emission for each channel was obtained through the CASA task `imfit,` and the offset position was calculated by projecting the peak emission onto the disc position angle" (4.2 Stellar mass)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: Velocities above ±2 km/s used to avoid envelope  
  > "a Keplerian profile (*$v$* $\propto$ *r$^{-0.5}$*) was employed to fit the points with velocities above $\pm$2 km s$^{-1}$" (4.2 Stellar mass)
   - assumption: Inclination 70 deg from plane of sky  
  > "adopting an inclination (*i*) of 70$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ from the plane of the sky" (4.2 Stellar mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mass changes <14% for ±10 deg inclination; compared with literature  
  > "The stellar mass changes by less than 14$\%$ when the inclination varies with $\pm$10$\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (4.2 Stellar mass)
4. **Fit Lbol-M* relation and derive accretion rate**: Power-law fit to Class I sources; Macc = Lbol R*/(G M*)  
  > "the mass accretion rate (*$\dot{M}$$_\mathrm{acc}$*) can be estimated from" (4.3 Mass evolution)
   - assumption: R* = 3 Rsun; M* and Lbol uncertainties 20% and 15%; binary values halved  
  > "Assuming *R$_{\star}$* = 3R$_{\odot}$ (cite: Stahler1980)" (4.3 Mass evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Yen et al. 2017 accretion rates  
  > "The mean value is consistent with the mass accretion rates from (cite: Yen2017)" (4.3 Mass evolution)
5. **Extract line intensities at continuum peak and compare with Lbol, Tbol**: Spectrum from peak continuum pixel fitted with 1-3 Gaussians; intensities summed  
  > "the spectrum was extracted from the pixel that corresponds to the peak of the continuum emission" (5.1)
   - parameter: 3 sigma upper limit 0.013 Jy/beam km/s per 1 km/s for non-detections  
  > "the 3$\sigma$ upper limit per 1 km s$^{-1}$, that is, 0.013 Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$, is used in the comparison" (5.1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Constrain CH3OH column density and abundance**: Synthetic LTE spectrum compared with 3 sigma rms  
  > "The upper limit to the column density is estimated using the predictions for a synthetic spectrum for methanol calculated under the assumption of LTE" (5.2)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Tkin = 100 K, line width 5 km/s  
  > "adopting a kinetic temperature of 100 K and a typical line width of 5 km s$^{-1}$" (5.2)
   - assumption: Emission fills beam uniformly  
  > "(assuming that the emission fills the beam uniformly)" (5.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- C17O traces the least evolved/luminous sources; SO2 (Eu~197 K) is compact, broad and seen towards more luminous sources, possibly accretion shocks  
  > "C$^{17}$O traces the densest and colder regions of the disc-envelope system, while SO$_{2}$ may be associated with regions of higher temperature, such as accretion shocks." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Representative sample covering wide Lbol and Menv with Tbol<400 K  
  > "With this approach, the sample is a representative set for exploring the physical and chemical structures of Class I sources." (2 Observations)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Keplerian disc or disc wind rejected as explanation for SO2, though neither fully ruled out  
  > "A Keplerian disc or disc winds are less plausible scenarios to explain the SO$_{2}$ emission." (5.3)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Caveats on envelope density profiles and disc temperature structure; chemical suppression not excluded  
  > "Open questions are for instance the density profiles of the envelopes and the temperature structures of the embedded discs." (5.2)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single dust temperature of 15 K and a single opacity make disc masses uncertain by factor ~3
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Lbol-M* fit uses few Class I points with large uncertainty on slope
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar masses from noisy PV diagrams with a common assumed inclination
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line intensities are only at the continuum peak, not integrated
