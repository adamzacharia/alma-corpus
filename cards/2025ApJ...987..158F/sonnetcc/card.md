## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce ALMA Band 3/4 data to line cubes**: Adopt QA2 calibration, subtract continuum with UVCONTSUB after masking the expected line, clean with tclean (natural weighting), bin to 60 km/s  
  > "We adopt calibrations performed in the second level of quality assurance (QA2)." (ALMA Data and Reduction); "imaged and cleaned using the TCLEAN algorithm in CASA" (ALMA Data and Reduction); "We binned the data cubes spectrally to channels of 60 km s$^{-1}$ to increase the SNR." (ALMA Data and Reduction)
   - software: CASA 6.2.1.7, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: line mask = +/-2 FWHM of mid-J CO line around expected [CI] frequency  
  > "of the expected line, centered on the expected frequency of the" (ALMA Data and Reduction)
   - parameter: clean threshold 2 sigma  
  > "with a cleaning threshold of 2$\sigma$" (ALMA Data and Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: line-free channels of the continuum-subtracted cubes were imaged to check that all emission was removed  
  > "We then imaged the line–free channels in the continuum–subtracted image cubes to verify that all emission had been fully removed." (ALMA Data and Reduction)
2. **Extract [CI] spectra and FWHM**: Spectra extracted at the peak-emission position with a circular aperture; single-Gaussian fit gives FWHM  
  > "We extract the line spectra from the position of the peak emission using an aperture of" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections); "the FWHM was derived from a single–Gaussian fitting to the spectra shown in Fig." ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 5'' diameter aperture  
  > "in diameter, to maximise the SNR" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - fitting: single-Gaussian fit to spectrum; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "the FWHM was derived from a single–Gaussian fitting to the spectra shown in Fig." ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - validation: median CO(4-3 or 3-2)/[CI](1-0) line-width ratio is 1.0 +/- 0.1  
  > "The median line-width ratio of CO(4–3) or CO(3–2) to" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
3. **Measure [CI] line fluxes, upper limits and 3 mm continuum**: Build 0th-moment maps over twice the mid-J CO line width; choose aperture by curve of growth (1.5'' to 40''); extract fluxes in an aperture of 2x beam FWHM; 3 sigma upper limits from RMS of moment-0 map away from the target  
  > "collapsed over a velocity range twice the corresponding mid" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections); "recovers most of the line flux while maximising the SNR, so we adopt that aperture size to extract final fluxes" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections); "upper limits on the emission by calculating the RMS over an aperture covering a region of the" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - software: CASA, IMFIT
   - parameter: continuum fluxes from a circular aperture of 2x synthesised beam size on the continuum images  
  > "We extract continuum fluxes in a similar manner by using a circular aperture of size" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: consistent with fluxes from CASA IMFIT 2D Gaussian fits on the moment-0 maps  
  > "the fluxes obtained using the CASA task IMFIT, which conducts 2D Gaussian fitting on the" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
4. **Convert [CI] fluxes to line luminosities**: L'[CI] = 3.25e7 I_[CI] nu_obs^-2 D_L^2 (1+z)^-3 K km/s pc^2 following Solomon  
  > "line intensities into line luminosities following (cite: solomon2005)" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - parameter: D_L in Mpc, nu_obs in GHz  
  > "is the luminosity distance in Mpc" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
   - assumption: LCDM with H0 = 67.8, Omega_M = 0.310, Omega_Lambda = 0.690  
  > "$H_0$ = 67.8 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_\mathrm{M}$ = 0.310 and $\Omega_\Lambda$ = 0.690 (cite: planck2016)" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare L'[CI] with L_IR and with L'CO(1-0)**: Plot L'[CI] vs L_IR and L'[CI]/L'CO(1-0) vs z against literature; chi-squared test against the Valentino relation  
  > "–value = 0.7, consistent with being drawn from the same distribution" ([C I] Line Luminosities)
   - software: MAGPHYS
   - parameter: L_IR from MAGPHYS SED fitting over 8-1000 um  
  > "was estimated through SED fitting with MAGPHYS (cite: highz_magphys), integrating over 8–1000" ([C I] Line Luminosities)
   - parameter: literature mid-J CO converted to CO(1-0) with r21 = 0.9  
  > "= 0.9 (cite: birkin2020)" ([C I] Line Luminosities)
   - assumption: AGN excluded and only CO(1-0)/CO(2-1) literature sources kept to limit excitation corrections  
  > "we restrict our compilation to only sources with either CO(1–0) or CO(2–1) observations to minimise the uncertainty introduced by excitation corrections" ([C I] Line Luminosities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: p-value = 0.7 for being drawn from the Valentino et al. relation  
  > "–value = 0.7, consistent with being drawn from the same distribution" ([C I] Line Luminosities)
6. **Constrain ISM density and UV field with PDR models**: Compare [CI](1-0), CO(1-0), mid-J CO and L_FIR ratios to PDRTOOLBOX model grid to get n and G_UV  
  > "We thus use a combination of these ratios to determine the PDR properties for this sample of SMGs." (PDR Modelling)
   - software: PDRTOOLBOX
   - parameter: 3 sigma upper/lower limits used where appropriate  
  > "upper/lower limits" (PDR Modelling)
   - assumption: default solar-metallicity models; FIR continuum and [CI] model fluxes multiplied by 2 for two-sided emission  
  > "We compare the data to default, solar-metallicity PDRTOOLBOX models" (PDR Modelling); "we multiply the predicted fluxes by a factor of 2." (PDR Modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: re-run without CO(1-0): results unchanged except three sources  
  > "We repeat the process excluding the CO(1–0) line: the results do not change appreciably except for" (PDR Modelling)
7. **Cross-calibrate [CI] and CO gas-mass conversion factors**: Combine M(H2)[CI] = 9.51e-5/(X_[CI] Q10) L'[CI] with M(H2)CO = alpha_CO L'CO to constrain X_[CI] x alpha_CO = 2.07e-4 L'[CI]/L'CO; test trends with z and L_IR  
  > "we can constrain the product of the two main unknowns for each tracer" ([C I](1–0) Line Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - software: LINMIX
   - parameter: T_ex = 30 K, Q10 = 0.46 (LTE)  
  > "= 30 K (cite: weiss2003; walter2011; harrington2021), which results in" ([C I](1–0) Line Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 1 M_sun (K km/s pc^2)^-1 for SMGs  
  > "is the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor, which we take to be 1 M$_\odot$" ([C I](1–0) Line Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - assumption: LTE with T_ex = 30 K gives about 4% higher mass than non-LTE  
  > "only implies a 4% higher mass compared more complex non–LTE approaches" ([C I](1–0) Line Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - fitting: LINMIX Bayesian linear regression including errors and upper limits; priors not stated  
  > "We look for any trends in the sample with LINMIX (cite: kelly2007), a Bayesian linear regression fitting model which can fit data with errors along with the upper limits." ([C I](1–0) Line Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - validation: slopes consistent with zero; z<3.5 and z>3.5 bins give consistent means  
  > "This is consistent with no dependence on either parameter, and we can thus compute a constant conversion factor." ([C I](1–0) Line Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
8. **Cross-calibrate dust continuum conversion factor**: Compute rest-frame 850 um luminosity from the 3 mm flux, then derive alpha_CO x alpha_850 = L850/L'CO and X_[CI]/alpha_850  
  > "can be calculated following (cite: scoville2016; scoville2017):" (Dust Continuum Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - software: LINMIX
   - parameter: T_dust = 30 K and beta = 2.1  
  > "we set $T_\mathrm{dust}$ = 30 K and $\beta$ = 2.1 and calculate" (Dust Continuum Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - assumption: 3 mm continuum approximately on the Rayleigh-Jeans tail, optically thin  
  > "Because the Rayleigh-Jeans (RJ) tail of the dust emission is almost always optically thin" (Dust Continuum Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - fitting: LINMIX fits versus redshift; slopes consistent with no trend  
  > "We fit the distributions again using LINMIX." (Dust Continuum Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
   - validation: T_dust = 25 K and beta = 1.8 would give 15% higher luminosities  
  > "the luminosities would have been 15% higher" (Dust Continuum Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
9. **Model CMB effect on CO and [CI] emission**: RADEX non-LTE models of CO(1-0) and [CI](1-0) intensities and their ratio versus T_CMB for grids of density and kinetic temperature  
  > "We use RADEX (cite: radex2007) for the models, a non-LTE radiative transfer code which uses the escape probability method" (CMB Effect on CO and [C I] Emission at High Redshift)
   - software: RADEX, LAMBDA database
   - parameter: ortho-to-para ratio 3:1; log(N/dv) = 18 for CO and C  
  > "We fix the H$_2$ ortho–to–para ratio to 3:1 and set log($N_\mathrm{CO}$/$d$v) = log($N_\mathrm{C}$/$d$v) = 18." (CMB Effect on CO and [C I] Emission at High Redshift)
   - assumption: lower bound on T_kin from PDR CO(1-0) excitation temperature, 18-20 K  
  > "this is 18–20 K, though the real" (CMB Effect on CO and [C I] Emission at High Redshift)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: effect on the line ratio compared with the scatter of the observed ratio  
  > "Given the current scatter of the CO(1–0) and" (CMB Effect on CO and [C I] Emission at High Redshift)

## Qualitative findings

- [CI](1-0) and CO(1-0) appear correlated on global scales regardless of galaxy type, L_IR and redshift  
  > "appear to be correlated on global scales regardless of galaxy type, $L_\mathrm{IR}$ and redshift." ([C I] Line Luminosities)
- Conversion factors show no redshift or L_IR dependence; alpha_CO=1 implies X_[CI] higher than the canonical 3e-5, consistent with solar or super-solar metallicity  
  > "would imply solar or super–solar metallicities" (Discussion)
- The CMB effect on the ratio is small relative to current scatter, but can be large on individual lines  
  > "The effect on the ratio is too small to be differentiated from the current scatter of the data" (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Luminous unlensed SMGs are the ideal targets to detect CO(1-0) without gravitational lensing, and ground-state transitions of both species avoid excitation corrections  
  > "Such luminous, massive galaxies are therefore the ideal targets to detect the faint CO(1–0) line with current facilities without the need for gravitational lensing." (Introduction); "obviate the need for excitation corrections" (Discussion)
- Why this method: Natural weighting chosen to maximise SNR; aperture of 2x beam chosen from a curve-of-growth as best SNR/flux compromise  
  > "We adopted natural weighting to maximise the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the detections" (ALMA Data and Reduction); "recovers most of the line flux while maximising the SNR, so we adopt that aperture size to extract final fluxes" ([C I] Line and Continuum Detections)
- Alternative: Computing PDR properties without CO(1-0) was tried and gave similar results  
  > "We repeat the process excluding the CO(1–0) line: the results do not change appreciably except for" (PDR Modelling)
- Alternative: Dust factors recomputed with T_dust = 25 K and beta = 1.8 would raise luminosities by 15%  
  > "the luminosities would have been 15% higher" (Dust Continuum Emission as a Gas Mass Tracer)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Source companions are unresolved in AS2COS0001.1 and AS2COS0008.1, so [CI] fluxes are likely overestimated  
  > "the \[C I\] line flux densities are likely overestimated" (Footnote 1)
- Limitation (authors): Conversion factor constraints are limited to very active sources and need samples across masses and metallicities  
  > "We caveat that current constraints on the conversion factors at high redshift are limited to typically very active sources." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Absolute CMB effect on individual lines cannot be determined without well-sampled SLEDs and non-LTE modelling  
  > "it will be necessary to obtain more complete SLEDs to perform complex, non–LTE modelling of the gas excitation conditions" (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only the product X_[CI] x alpha_CO is constrained; alpha_CO = 1 is assumed and the metallicity argument is circular with it
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Eight of the 20 sources have [CI]/CO values from other papers or unpublished sources with possibly different reduction, and the ~5'' beams cannot separate companions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Eleven sources lack CO(1-0) detections so the L'[CI]/L'CO ratio relies on upper limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LTE with fixed T_ex = 30 K and fixed T_dust/beta adopted for all sources
- Caveat (inferred, ours): PDR n and G_UV values are from single-slab models with an ad hoc factor of 2
