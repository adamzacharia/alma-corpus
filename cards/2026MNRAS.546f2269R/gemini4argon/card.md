## Analysis recipe

1. **Create CO moment maps and position-velocity diagrams**: Masked moment technique: smooth a copy of the cleaned cube spatially with a Gaussian (FWHM = 1.5x synthesized beam) and in velocity with Hanning smoothing, apply a 0.9 sigma flux-density threshold to create a 3D mask, and compute moments 0, 1, and 2 from the un-smoothed cubes within the mask; extract PVDs along slits A, B, and C  
  > "In short, in this technique, a copy of the cleaned data cube is first Gaussian-smoothed spatially (with a FWHM that is $1.5x$ that of the synthesised beam) and then Hanning-smoothed in velocity." (Moment maps); "Given the significance of our CO detections (see Table 2), a threshold of 0.9$\sigma$ (where $\sigma$ is the rms noise level measured in the un-smoothed data cube) has been used in all the cases." (Moment maps); "Further support to the presence of disturbed/non-circular kinematic components comes from the analysis of the CO position-velocity diagrams (PVDs) extracted along different PAs and illustrated in Figures 8 and 9." (Moment maps)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: spatial smoothing FWHM = 1.5x synthesized beam, velocity smoothing = Hanning, mask threshold = 0.9 sigma  
  > "a copy of the cleaned data cube is first Gaussian-smoothed spatially (with a FWHM that is $1.5x$ that of the synthesised beam) and then Hanning-smoothed in velocity." (Moment maps); "a threshold of 0.9$\sigma$ (where $\sigma$ is the rms noise level measured in the un-smoothed data cube) has been used in all the cases." (Moment maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked dynamical mass M_dyn = v_rot^2 r / G (~10^11 M_sun) to rule out regular circular rotation  
  > "However, if we assume that the observed velocity curves arise from gas in circular motions, the dynamical mass ($M_{\rm dyn} = \dfrac{v_{\rm rot}^{2}r}{G}$) in the “flat" part of the rotation curve would be $\approx10^{11}$ $M_{\odot}$, which is unrealistically large." (Moment maps)
2. **Extract integrated CO spectra, fit line profiles, and measure integrated flux densities**: Extract spectra from un-smoothed cubes within polygonal areas drawn by-eye around line structures; fit single and double Gaussian components using lmfit; integrate flux density over FWZI velocity range where channel intensities >= 3 sigma

(Line widths and profiles); "To rigorously demonstrate the presence of such secondary spectral components and determine the overall properties of the line profiles, we performed Gaussian fits on the integrated spectra using the least-squares minimization Python routine `lmfit` (cite: Newville14)." (Line widths and profiles); "We note that line widths were also measured as full-width at zero-intensity (FWZI), which was defined as the full velocity range covered by channels (identified interactively in the channel maps) with clear line detections with intensities $\geq3\sigma$." (Line widths and profiles)
   - software: lmfit
   - parameter: FWZI threshold >= 3 sigma; 1-Gaussian vs 2-Gaussian models (mean, sigma, normalisation free)

  > "we performed Gaussian fits on the integrated spectra using the least-squares minimization Python routine `lmfit` (cite: Newville14)." (Line widths and profiles)

\end{eqnarray}$$" (Molecular gas mass); "From this luminosity we derived the H$_{\rm 2}$ mass using the conversion equation, expressed as in (cite: Bolatto13), $$M(H_{\rm 2})=\alpha~L'_{\rm CO}~M_{\odot},$$" (Molecular gas mass)

## Qualitative findings

- I00183 likely formed through a major prograde merger between two gas-rich spiral galaxies no older than ~2 Gyr, evidenced by prominent tidal tails extending up to 40 kpc.  
  > "We claim that the most likely explanation for such structures is that they are tidal tails, whose characteristics lead us to conclude that I00183 probably formed through a major merger between two gas-rich spirals, characterised by a prograde encounter and no older than 2 Gyr." (Summary and conclusions)
- Extreme molecular gas excitation (R_31 up to >=60, T_ex >> 50 K, optically thin) is spatially correlated with the radio lobes, indicating an ongoing jet-ISM interaction.  
  > "The analysis of the $R_{\rm 31} \equiv S_{\rm CO(3-2)}/S_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ flux density ratio shows the presence of a high-excitation gas component with $R_{\rm 31}$ reaching values up to $60$ (see Section 3.4)." (Summary and conclusions); "The spatial correlation between the regions where the largest line ratios are observed and the location of the radio lobes suggests that also in I00183 the expanding radio plasma is likely responsible for the extreme gas conditions in the circumnuclear regions." (Summary and conclusions)
- The CO kinematics shows no ordered circular rotation, but contains a high-velocity non-rotational component associated with a kpc-scale molecular outflow that cannot be powered by supernovae or current AGN radiation pressure alone, pointing to frustrated radio jets or past higher AGN activity.  
  > "By comparing the energetics and geometrical properties of the outflow with that of possible driving mechanisms (i.e. SNe, AGN radiation pressure, jet-induced shocks), we conclude that this may be triggered by either radiation from the AGN in the past or the kinetic energy input from a frustrated radio jet." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (~1 kpc) ALMA CO(1-0) and CO(3-2) observations combined with deep VST i-band imaging and VLBI data allow exploring the link between merger history, AGN/jet activity, and kpc-scale molecular outflows in a transitional ULIRG at z > 0.3, complementing 10x lower-resolution Cycle 0 ALMA data.  
  > "Such multi-wavelength analysis allows us to explore the link between galaxy merger, AGN activity, radio jet expansion and galactic-scale (i.e. $\sim$kpc-scale) molecular outflows, for the first time with a high level of detail in an ULIRG at $z>0.3$." (Introduction); "The analysis of the multiple CO transitions presented in this paper allows us to complement the results obtained from the study at ten times lower spatial resolution (i.e. beam FWHM $= 3.10\ensuremath{''} \times 2.20\ensuremath{''}$, equivalent to $\approx 14.7 \times 10.4$ kpc) of the sole CO(1-0) transition presented in (cite: Ruffa18)." (CO kinematics)
- Why this method: Briggs robust=0.5 gives the best trade-off between sensitivity and resolution; masked moment technique recovers flux while minimising noise; matching uv-range and beam is required for reliable line ratio maps; BIC test rigorously verifies the need for a broad second Gaussian component.  
  > "In all the cases, Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5 was adopted, thus obtaining the best trade-off between sensitivity and resolution." (Continuum and line imaging); "We also performed a BIC (Bayesian Information Criterion) test (cite: Liddle07) to verify the statistical significance of one model with respect to the other." (Line widths and profiles); "To study the relative strength of two gas transitions in the most reliable way possible, the data cubes used to make the ratio maps need to be identical." (Line ratio)
- Alternative: Regular circular rotation for the CO velocity gradient: rejected because the implied dynamical mass in the flat part of the PVD (~10^11 M_sun) is unrealistically large.  
  > "However, if we assume that the observed velocity curves arise from gas in circular motions, the dynamical mass ($M_{\rm dyn} = \dfrac{v_{\rm rot}^{2}r}{G}$) in the “flat" part of the rotation curve would be $\approx10^{11}$ $M_{\odot}$, which is unrealistically large. We therefore conclude that the observed features do not trace regular circular rotation" (Moment maps)
- Alternative: Single Gaussian spectral profile for CO(1-0) and CO(3-2): rejected via BIC test in favour of a two-Gaussian model (Delta BIC = 21.8 and 52.2).  
  > "In both cases, the model with two Gaussians (overlaid as labelled in Figure 12) resulted as preferred and the evidence for the need of the second component as very strong, with $\Delta$BIC$= 21.8$ for the CO(1-0) and $\Delta$BIC$= 52.2$ for the CO(3-2)." (Line widths and profiles)
- Alternative: Close binary nuclei as the origin of the two central optical compact residuals: rejected due to lack of spatial correlation with the optical peak, radio core, CO peaks, and ALMA continuum.  
  > "We therefore conclude that it is unlikely that the two residual point-like structures in Figure 15 trace the presence of close binary nuclei." (Photometric analysis)
- Alternative: Cavagnolo et al. (2010) scaling relation for estimating radio jet power: rejected because it yields an unrealistically high jet power (two orders of magnitude higher) and is unsuited to compact rapidly evolving radio sources.  
  > "We note that using the well-known scaling relations of (cite: Cavagnolo10) results in an unrealistic jet power which is two orders of magnitude higher, and age correspondingly shorter – because this relation is not well-suited to compact radio sources which evolve rapidly through the size-luminosity space" (What powers the outflow?)
- Alternative: Supernovae or present-day AGN radiation pressure as the sole driver of the molecular outflow: rejected because estimated SNe mass outflow rate (68 M_sun/yr) and AGN radiation force (2.06e35 g cm s^-2) are too low.  
  > "This excludes the power from supernovae as the main driver of the molecular outflow." (What powers the outflow?); "We thus conclude that the AGN radiation pressure is unlikely to be the only driver of the molecular outflow, at least at present times." (What powers the outflow?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
