## Analysis recipe

1. **Re-image archival and ACES data**: Re-ran CASA imaging with the ALMA pipeline parameters, fixing diverged cleans and imaging windows left un-imaged or at full spectral resolution; archival 2012 and 2017 data re-imaged with the ACES pipeline  
  > "adopting the same parameters as used in the original ALMA-delivered pipeline products, but with modifications as needed to fix bad cleans (specifically, iterative clean runs that diverged and produced spurious signals)" (2 Observations); "We re-imaged the archival data from both programs using the ACES pipeline." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA, ALMA pipeline
   - parameter: CASA 6.4.3-2 for imaging  
  > "The data were imaged using CASA 6.4.3-2" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Overlapping lines in the 2012 archival data used to check the ACES detections are not spurious  
  > "the consistent detections of overlapping lines gives us high confidence that the ACES detections are not spurious" (2 Observations)
2. **Fit line profiles at the peak position**: Extract spectra at the peak emission position and fit Gaussian profiles, with foreground absorption components for CS 2-1 and SO 2(3)-1(2)  
  > "We fitted Gaussian line profiles to the detected lines, and for CS 2-1 and SO 2(3)-1(2), we included foreground absorption components in our models" (3.1 Spectral); "These spectra are extracted from the peak emission location, i.e., ICRS equatorial coordinates 17h45m57.75s" (3.1 Spectral)
   - fitting: Gaussian line profile fits (optimizer, priors, likelihood not stated)  
  > "All values are from Gaussian fits to the line profile, so the widths are the Gaussian width parameter, not the FWHM." (Table 2 notes)
   - validation: Lines of CS, SO, 34SO, SO2 detected in ACES and several in 2012 data  
  > "We detect lines of CS, SO, and SO$_2$ listed in Table" (3.1 Spectral)
3. **Measure source position and size**: 2D Gaussian fits to continuum and integrated-intensity images in all three data sets; beam-deconvolved sizes reported  
  > "We fitted 2D Gaussian profiles to the continuum and integrated intensity images in all three data sets." (3.2 Spatial); "the alternating rows with no errorbars give the beam-deconvolved sizes." (Table 3 notes)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fits (statistical errors only quoted)  
  > "The uncertainties in this table give only the statistical errors" (3.2 Spatial)
   - validation: Systematic uncertainty from background separation is expected to exceed the statistical offsets  
  > "we expect the dominant systematic uncertainty to be from separation between the structured background brightness and the compact Gaussian" (3.2 Spatial)
4. **Look for a velocity gradient**: Collapse the red and blue sides of the SO 2(3)-1(2) cube into moment-0 images, excluding the extended cloud near 45 km/s, and fit their positions  
  > "We therefore collapsed the red (64 to 152 $\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$) and blue ($-68$ to 34 $\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$) sides of the cube into moment-0 images" (3.3 Spatio-Spectral)
   - parameter: Red 64 to 152 km/s, blue -68 to 34 km/s  
  > "We therefore collapsed the red (64 to 152 $\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$) and blue ($-68$ to 34 $\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$) sides of the cube into moment-0 images" (3.3 Spatio-Spectral)
   - assumption: d = 8 kpc  
  > "The separation is 2000$\pm200$ au assuming $d=8$ kpc" (3.3 Spatio-Spectral)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Per-channel 2D Gaussian fits were too unreliable at the modest S/N; systematics expected to cancel between red and blue  
  > "We fitted 2D Gaussian profiles to each channel in the SO 2(3)-1(2) cube, but found that the fits were too unreliable at the modest S/N in each channel." (3.3 Spatio-Spectral)
5. **Continuum spectral index and dust mass**: Assume a single point source, derive spectral index between Band 3 and Band 7, and estimate mass from both frequencies with an optically thin dust formula  
  > "Assuming the continuum comes from a single point source, which is consistent with the measurements in Table" (3.4 Continuum); "we estimate the mass from both frequencies" (3.4 Continuum)
   - parameter: T = 20 K, kappa = 0.002 cm^2/g at 102 GHz and 0.018 cm^2/g at 350 GHz  
  > "\left(\frac{\kappa}{0.002\mathrm{~cm^{2}~ g^{-1}}}\right)^{-1}" (3.4 Continuum)
   - assumption: Ossenkopf (1994) dust opacity, T=20 K, gas-to-dust mass ratio 100, D = 8 kpc  
  > "assuming T=20 K and gas-to-dust mass ratio 100 (so the mass in $\kappa$ is the total gas+dust mass)" (3.4 Continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative opacities and gas-to-dust ratios explored in Section 4.4 give masses from ~0.5 to 5x the nominal value  
  > "If the dust is comprised primarily of carbon, the mass may be as much as $15\times$ smaller than we reported" (4.4 Weird dust?)
6. **Constrain dust temperature from the SED**: Use Spitzer and Herschel surface brightness as upper limits and overlay modified blackbodies with beta = 1.5  
  > "The modified blackbodies are labeled with the adopted temperature in K and column density in $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ assuming $\beta=1.5$." (3.4 Continuum)
   - assumption: Power-law opacity kappa proportional to nu^beta with beta = 1.5  
  > "For a modified blackbody with a power law opacity function, $\kappa\propto\nu^\beta$" (3.4 Continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **LTE rotational diagram of SO and column densities**: Rotational diagram from two detected SO transitions and an upper limit on a third; model spectra overlaid with CS and SO2 columns scaled at the fitted temperature  
  > "allowing us to make a rotational diagram and fit the temperature and column density assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions" (4.1 LTE Modeling); "the CS and SO$_2$ column densities are scaled to fit the data while assuming the fitted SO rotational temperature" (4.1 LTE Modeling)
   - assumption: LTE, justified by the dust-derived density; same temperature for other molecules  
  > "Assuming the same temperature for the other molecules, we obtain column densities" (4.1 LTE Modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Peak optical depth ~0.1 so lines optically thin; non-LTE RADEX check in Section 4.2  
  > "At these column densities, the peak line optical depth is $\tau \sim 0.1$, so the lines are still well-approximated as optically thin." (4.1 LTE Modeling)
8. **Test non-LTE excitation**: Grid of RADEX one-zone models, using the SO 2(3)-1(2)/2(2)-1(1) ratio and a lower limit on the SO 2(3)-1(2) intensity from filling factor <= 1  
  > "We ran a grid of RADEX (cite: vanderTak2007) models to test whether non-LTE conditions can match the data." (4.2 Non-LTE conditions?); "RADEX one-zone models, adopting $dv=70$ $\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$, give a wide range of solutions for different temperatures." (4.2 Non-LTE conditions?)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: dv = 70 km/s, LVG approximation  
  > "The models use the large velocity gradient (LVG) approximation with $dv=70$ $\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$." (10 RADEX models)
   - assumption: Dust-based H2 number density combined with the models to rule out low-density solutions  
  > "if we incorporate our estimate of the H$_2$ number density based on the dust, the non-LTE models are ruled out" (4.2 Non-LTE conditions?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Non-LTE models confirm the LTE column density  
  > "Non-LTE models confirm the LTE column density estimate." (4.2 Non-LTE conditions?)
9. **Chemical modelling of SO/CS and SO/SiO ratios**: Meudon PDR constant-density slabs for line and column density ratios; UCLCHEM time-dependent collapse models for SO/SiO  
  > "we ran models of a collapsing cloud that varied in final densities, UV irradiation, cosmic ray ionization rate, and temperatures" (4.3 Chemical Modeling); "We ran Meudon PDR[^2] (cite: LePetit2006) models spanning a range of extinction, cosmic ray ionization rate, density, and UV field" (4.3 Chemical Modeling)
   - software: Meudon PDR, UCLCHEM
   - parameter: Meudon grid: n_H 2e5 and 2e7 cm^-3, G0 = 1e3, zeta_CR 2e-17 to 2e-14 s^-1, A_V,max 20/200/800  
  > "| FUV radiation field, $G_0$ | – | 10$^3$ | \(3\) |" (Table 4)
   - parameter: UCLCHEM grid: final n_H 1e5, 1e6, 1e7 cm^-3; T 15 and 50 K  
  > "| $n_\mathrm{H,\,final}$ | cm$^{-3}$ | $10^5, 10^6, 10^7$ |" (Table 5)
   - assumption: Sulfur and silicon depletion included  
  > "evaluating both a model with solar initial abundances and a model with a depletion of a factor 10 for S and 100 for Si" (9.2 UCLCHEM models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Models compared with observed SO/CS intensity ratio and LTE-derived column density ratio  
  > "The model predictions result in a narrow range of parameters consistent with the observed SO/CS intensity ratio and estimated SO/CS column density ratio" (9.1 FUV and CR irradiation models)
10. **Compare dust SED with protostar model grid**: Search the Robitaille/Richardson grid for models within 25% of the ALMA flux densities in 5000 au apertures and below the 24 and 70 micron upper limits  
  > "We search for models that have flux density within $5000$ au apertures matching the ALMA measurements to within 25% and falling below the 24 and 70 $\mathrm{\mu m}$ upper limits." (6.1.1 Protostar)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The line profile appears Gaussian and no SiO emission is detected, suggesting no strong shocks  
  > "Despite the high velocity dispersion, no emission is observed from SiO, suggesting that there are no strong ($\gtrsim10~\ensuremath{\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}}$) shocks in the molecular gas." (abstract)
- No counterpart is detected at other wavelengths (X-ray, infrared, radio)  
  > "There are no detections at other wavelengths, including X-ray, infrared, and radio." (abstract)
- Absorption by Galactic Center clouds in front of the broad lines places the source in the Galactic Center  
  > "It is likely in the Galactic Center, since the broad lines show absorption from Galactic Center clouds." (Conclusion)
- Line width, molecular and dust continuum intensity stayed roughly constant from 2012 to 2022  
  > "The line width and the molecular and dust continuum intensity have all stayed roughly constant from 2012 to 2022" (6.6 Supernova)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: An archive search for overlapping observations to verify the feature was not an imaging artifact  
  > "To verify that this feature was not an image artifact (though there was no particular reason to suspect it was), we searched the ALMA archive for overlap with this object." (2 Observations)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Non-LTE low-density excitation of SO was disfavoured given the dust-derived density and Meudon models  
  > "We therefore disfavor the non-LTE, low-density model" (4.2 Non-LTE conditions?)
- Alternative: Protostellar outflow considered and judged very unlikely  
  > "A protostellar source is not strictly ruled out, but is very unlikely." (6.1.2 Protostellar outflow)
- Alternative: Evolved star, (pre-)planetary nebula, HVCC, supernova each judged not to account for all features; IMBH and stellar merger not ruled out  
  > "We regard it as a possible explanation, but do not favor it above other models." (6.4 Intermediate-Mass Black Hole (IMBH)); "In summary, we cannot rule out a supernova remnant as an explanation for the MUBLO, but such a model does not account for all of its features." (6.6 Supernova)
- How they decided it was enough: Consistent detections of overlapping lines in the 2012 data gave confidence the ACES detections are real  
  > "the consistent detections of overlapping lines gives us high confidence that the ACES detections are not spurious" (2 Observations)
- Limitation (authors): 2012 data have artifacts severe enough that no images are shown and results should not be trusted on statistical errors alone  
  > "The problems with the 2012 data set are severe enough that we choose not to show any of the images and strongly caution against interpreting the measurements of these data based only on their statistical errors." (2 Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial measurement uncertainties are statistical only; systematics are larger  
  > "all of the spatial measurements are likely affected by systematic errors that are larger" (3.2 Spatial)
- Limitation (authors): Column densities are dominated by systematic uncertainty in excitation temperature  
  > "These estimates are dominated by systematic uncertainty in the excitation temperature, which may be more than an order of magnitude." (4.1 LTE Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): ACES data are still being processed, so there is no complete census of the broader context  
  > "The full ACES data are still being processed, so as of this publication, we do not yet have a complete census of the broader context." (2 Observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass depends strongly on assumed dust opacity, temperature (20 K vs LTE 13-14 K) and gas-to-dust ratio; the paper itself shows range of ~0.5 to ~5x
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral index mixes ALMA 12m Band 3 with a noisy 7m Band 7 detection at the field edge, so flux calibration and resolution differences matter
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The LTE temperature rests on two SO lines (one possibly with absorption); non-LTE is rejected partly using the same dust-derived density
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gaussian fits and absorption components were done without a stated fitting package or priors
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No SiO detection upper limit is quantified from the data in a formal fit; model spectra were overplotted instead
