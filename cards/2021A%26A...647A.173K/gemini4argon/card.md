## Science

- Fields: stellar_physics, transients
- Question: Study the jet and accretion disk properties of GRS 1915+105 during its 2018-2019 outburst decay to determine if it has descended into a low-luminosity hard X-ray state.  
  > "We conducted two full polarization Atacama Large Millimeter Array observations to study the jet properties during the outburst decay by analyzing the spectral, polarization, and intra-epoch variability for both observation epochs." (abstract); "We show that the X-ray and millimeter (mm) spectral, timing, and polarization properties are consistent with those of a typical decaying X-ray binary outburst and that GRS 1915+105 has descended into the low-luminosity hard X-ray state." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, flag, and extract flux densities and polarization from ALMA Band 3 data**: Run provided ALMA calibration script in CASA, apply manual channel and antenna flags for bad D-terms and gain/phase outliers, image the data, and fit a delta function to the uv-plane using uvmultifit to measure Stokes I, Q, U fluxes (full band, per spectral window, and per scan).  
  > "by running the calibration script provided with the data. We flagged the first ten channels in the second spectral window due to bad D-terms for both epochs." (2.1. ALMA); "Flux densities were compared with both imaging methods and a delta function was fitted to the uv-plane using uvmultifit 3.0.0 (Martí-Vidal et al. 2014); we find that they agree with each other." (2.1. ALMA); "We estimated the Stokes Q and U polarized fluxes averaging over all spectral windows and all scans." (2.1. ALMA)
   - software: CASA 5.4.0-70, uvmultifit 3.0.0
   - parameter: Flagged first 10 channels in spw 2 (both epochs); flagged DA63 (Epoch 1) and DA41 (Epoch 2) all spws; flagged DA42 and DA48 spw 1 (Epoch 2)  
  > "We flagged the first ten channels in the second spectral window due to bad D-terms for both epochs. In addition, antennas DA63 and DA41 for the first and second epochs, respectively, were flagged for all spectral windows due to outlier values in gain amplitude." (2.1. ALMA)
   - parameter: Scan length ~10 min for intra-epoch timing  
  > "Both epochs lasted approximately two hours, consisting of several alternating scans of the science source (∼ 10 min), polarization calibrator (∼2 min), and phase calibrator sources (∼0.5 min)." (2.1. ALMA)
   - assumption: Point source model (delta function in uv-plane)  
  > "Flux densities were compared with both imaging methods and a delta function was fitted to the uv-plane using uvmultifit 3.0.0 (Martí-Vidal et al. 2014); we find that they agree with each other." (2.1. ALMA)
   - fitting: uv-plane delta function fitting in uvmultifit 3.0.0 and power-law spectral index fitting (S_nu propto nu^alpha)  
  > "Flux densities were compared with both imaging methods and a delta function was fitted to the uv-plane using uvmultifit 3.0.0 (Martí-Vidal et al. 2014); we find that they agree with each other." (2.1. ALMA); "Flux densities were compared with both imaging methods and a delta function was fitted to the uv-plane using uvmultifit 3.0.0" (2.1. ALMA)
   - validation: Compared uvmultifit and imaging fluxes; checked phase calibrator gain amplitudes, WVR data, and split array into DV and DA sub-arrays to test intra-epoch variability.  
  > "We examined the gain amplitudes from the phase calibration to verify that any changes in the amplitudes are not correlated with the flux density variations." (2.1. ALMA); "This is indeed seen in our third check where we divided the array into two individual sub-arrays (DV and DA antennas separately) and repeated the flux density estimation." (2.1. ALMA)
2. **Reduce NICER X-ray observations and model Power Spectral Densities (PSDs) and QPO lags**: Filter NICER events using nicerdas, calculate rms-normalized PSDs in 1-10 keV, fit PSDs in ISIS with up to three Lorentzians plus power-law and constant Poisson noise, and compute time lags between 2-4 keV and 5-10 keV bands.  
  > "For the timing analysis, we calculated the power spectral densities (PSDs) directly from the cleaned event files using a binning of 2^−7 s (or 2^−4 s in case of the data in the obscured phase), an energy band 1–10 keV, and a segment length of 16 s (or 256 s in case of the data in the obscured phase)." (2.2. NICER); "We fit all the PSDs with up to three Lorentzians: a Lorentzian centered at zero frequency for the band-limited noise and two Lorentzians for the QPO and its upper harmonic." (3.2.2. Description of the PSD model); "We also checked whether the QPO frequencies presented any time lags between a soft (2–4 keV) and a hard X-ray (5–10 keV) band (see Fig. 4)." (3.2.3. The exponential and linear decay phases)
   - software: nicerdas 6a, xselect v2.4g, isis
   - parameter: nicercal_filtexpr="EVENT_FLAGS=bx1x000", cor_range="4-", PI channels 30-1200 (0.3-12 keV), exposure > 500 s  
  > "We reduced the observations using nicerdas version 6a with parameters nicercal_filtexpr="EVENT_FLAGS=bx1x000", which removes all undershoot, overshoot, forced trigger, and fast-only events, and cor_range="4-" to remove high particle radiation intervals associated with the Earth’s auroral zones." (2.2. NICER)
   - parameter: PSD binning 2^-7 s (or 2^-4 s in obscured phase), segment length 16 s (or 256 s in obscured phase), geometric rebinning factor 1.05  
  > "For the timing analysis, we calculated the power spectral densities (PSDs) directly from the cleaned event files using a binning of 2^−7 s (or 2^−4 s in case of the data in the obscured phase), an energy band 1–10 keV, and a segment length of 16 s (or 256 s in case of the data in the obscured phase)." (2.2. NICER); "All the segments were further averaged over the whole pointing and binned geometrically by a factor of 1.05 before importing them to the Interactive Spectral Interpretation System (isis; Houck & Denicola 2000) for model fitting." (2.2. NICER)
   - assumption: Poisson noise is constant power component  
  > "We also model the Poisson noise present in the PSD as a constant power component." (3.2.2. Description of the PSD model)
   - fitting: Multi-Lorentzian + power-law + constant fitting in ISIS  
  > "We fit all the PSDs with up to three Lorentzians: a Lorentzian centered at zero frequency for the band-limited noise and two Lorentzians for the QPO and its upper harmonic." (3.2.2. Description of the PSD model)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit NICER X-ray energy spectra and iron line evolution across outburst decay phases**: Adaptively bin NICER spectra (0.3-12 keV) by S/N and fit in ISIS with vphabs * (relxill + lines) + powerlaw (adding xillver in the obscured phase) to extract continuum parameters, fluxes, and iron absorption/emission line properties.  
  > "Thus, the total model can be described as: vphabs × (relxill + lines) + powerlaw." (3.2.1. Description of the spectral model); "Thus, the model can be now described as: vphabs × (relxill + xillver + lines) + powerlaw." (3.2.5. The obscured phase)
   - software: xselect v2.4g, isis, relxill, xillver, vphabs
   - parameter: Adaptive binning: 0.3-1.5 keV to S/N=5-10, 1.5-5.0 keV to S/N=20-70, 5.0-8.0 keV to S/N=15-50, 8.0-12.0 keV to S/N=10-30  
  > "For the spectral analysis, we binned the data adaptively in the ranges 0.3-1.5 keV to S/N=5-10, 1.5-5.0 keV to S/N=20-70, 5.0-8.0 keV to S/N=15-50, and 8.0-12.0 keV to S/N=10-30 depending on the flux and exposure time of the pointing to ensure similar statistics for each spectrum." (2.2. NICER)
   - parameter: Obscured phase: R_in = 1, R_f = -2, inclination free  
  > "We further set the ionization parameter to 1, fixed the reflection factor to −2 corresponding to an obscured reflection scenario, and allowed the inclination to vary freely ( likely corresponding to some mean scattering angle of the obscuring cloud)." (3.2.5. The obscured phase)
   - assumption: Black hole spin a = 0, inclination i = 70 deg (except obscured phase), solar iron abundance  
  > "Therefore, we decided to fix the spin to zero. We further fixed the inclination to 70 degrees (Reid et al. 2014; Mirabel & Rodríguez 1994), and iron abundance to the solar value." (3.2.1. Description of the spectral model)
   - fitting: Spectral fitting in ISIS with Monte Carlo error estimation  
  > "We performed spectral fitting using isis. We estimated the errors on the parameter values and fluxes through Monte Carlo analysis." (2.2. NICER)
   - validation: Reduced chi-squared (chi^2_red) reported for every spectrum in Table A2  
  > "the photon power law index (Γ), ionization parameter (log ξ), the corresponding fit quality (χ^2/d.o.f), and the absorbed 1-10 keV flux density" (Figure 5 caption)
4. **Evaluate the millimeter/X-ray luminosity correlation and fit the X-ray light curve decay profile**: Combine ALMA Band 3 luminosities and NICER 3-10 keV luminosities with archival JCMT/NMA and RXTE data to fit the mm/X-ray correlation slope, and fit the NICER 1-10 keV light curve with an exponential-to-linear decay model.  
  > "To investigate the radio/X-ray correlation of GRS 1915+105, we searched for millimeter and X-ray observations during radio-quiet/plateau states to compare them with our ALMA data." (3.3.2. Millimeter/X-ray correlation); "Powell et al. (2007), Heinke et al. (2015), and Tetarenko et al. (2018) corresponding to an exponential decay" (4.2. Viscous outburst decay?)
   - assumption: Distance d = 8.6 kpc, black hole mass M = 12 M_sun, Eddington luminosity L_Edd = 1.5 x 10^39 erg s^-1, flat radio/mm spectrum  
  > "Assuming a black hole mass of 12 M_⊙ (Reid et al. 2014) the Eddington luminosity is L_Edd = 1.5 × 10^39 erg s^−1." (3.1. X-ray monitoring); "Assuming a black hole mass of 12 M ⊙ (Reid et al. 2014) the Eddington luminosity is" (3.1. X-ray monitoring)
   - fitting: Power-law correlation fit (L_radio propto L_X^beta) and piecewise exponential-linear decay fit (Eq. 1)  
  > "The resulting mm/X-ray correlation can be seen in Fig. 11 with a correlation coefficient of 0.58±0.05." (3.3.2. Millimeter/X-ray correlation); "The fit resulted in the following parameters: τ_e = 58 ± 4 days, τ_l = 461 ± 6 days, t_b = 58328±1 days (MJD), f_t = 0.195 ± 0.001× 10^−8 erg s^−1 cm^−2, and f_2 = 0.07 ± 0.02× 10^−8 erg s^−1 cm^−2." (4.2. Viscous outburst decay?)
   - validation: Compared correlation slope with GX 339-4 hard-state radio/X-ray correlation  
  > "For comparison, we plotted the GX 339-4 hard-state radio/X-ray correlation (gray triangles; same X-ray band, but radio luminosity is from 8.4 GHz; from Koljonen & Russell 2019)." (3.3.2. Millimeter/X-ray correlation)

## Software

- **CASA** 5.4.0-70: ALMA data reduction, calibration, flagging, and imaging  
  > "by running the calibration script provided with the data. We flagged the first ten channels in the second spectral window due to bad D-terms for both epochs." (2.1. ALMA)
- **uvmultifit** 3.0.0: Fitting a delta function in the uv-plane to measure ALMA Stokes I, Q, and U flux densities  
  > "Flux densities were compared with both imaging methods and a delta function was fitted to the uv-plane using uvmultifit 3.0.0 (Martí-Vidal et al. 2014); we find that they agree with each other." (2.1. ALMA)
- **nicerdas** 6a: NICER X-ray observation data reduction and event filtering  
  > "We reduced the observations using nicerdas version 6a with parameters nicercal_filtexpr="EVENT_FLAGS=bx1x000", which removes all undershoot, overshoot, forced trigger, and fast-only events, and cor_range="4-" to remove high particle radiation intervals associated with the Earth’s auroral zones." (2.2. NICER)
- **xselect** v2.4g: Extracting NICER X-ray spectra  
  > "We extracted the X-ray spectra using xselect v2.4g." (2.2. NICER)
- **isis** : Model fitting of NICER X-ray power spectral densities and energy spectra  
  > "All the segments were further averaged over the whole pointing and binned geometrically by a factor of 1.05 before importing them to the Interactive Spectral Interpretation System (isis; Houck & Denicola 2000) for model fitting." (2.2. NICER); "We performed spectral fitting using isis." (2.2. NICER)
- **relxill** : Relativistic reflection and power-law continuum modeling of NICER X-ray spectra  
  > "We fit the individual pointing spectra from the NICER dataset with a model consisting of an absorbed power law and reflection model components (relxill; García et al. 2014; Dauser et al. 2014) modified by Gaussian emission and/or absorption line components when necessary." (3.2.1. Description of the spectral model)
- **xillver** : Neutral lower ionization reflection modeling of NICER X-ray spectra during the obscured phase  
  > "Instead, we used the modeling results of Koljonen & Tomsick (2020), and added a neutral lower ionization reflection component to the model (xillver) with all parameters tied to the relxill component except normalization." (3.2.5. The obscured phase)
- **vphabs** : Variable-abundance photoelectric absorption component in X-ray spectral fitting  
  > "For the photoelectric absorption component, we chose vphabs, which allow the abundances of the elements of the neutral absorber to be changed." (3.2.1. Description of the spectral model)

## Qualitative findings

- GRS 1915+105 is unresolved as a point source within the ALMA beam in both epochs.  
  > "The resulting images show that the source is a point source within the beam (∼0.6′′ and ∼2.5′′ in the first and second epochs, respectively) without any observed structure." (2.1. ALMA)
- The polarization position angles in the ALMA observations are aligned parallel to the known jet position angle, implying the magnetic field is parallel to the jet axis and Faraday rotation is small.  
  > "Thus, the polarization position angles of ALMA observations are consistent with being parallel to the jet." (3.3. ALMA results); "Due to absorption effects, the intrinsic electric vector position angle of the steady jet is expected to align parallel to the magnetic field, which is expected to be parallel to the jet axis." (3.3. ALMA results)
- Intrinsic minute-timescale millimeter flux density variability is detected in GRS 1915+105 during the second ALMA epoch.  
  > "However, during the second epoch, the phase calibrator is very stable, and we conclude that the variability seen on timescales of minutes is intrinsic to the source, demonstrating usefulness of ALMA in studying fast variability in XRBs." (3.3.1. Intra-epoch variability)
- In the obscured phase, highly ionized iron absorption lines transition to emission lines, QPOs vanish leaving pure power-law red noise, and up to ~80% of the intrinsic 1-10 keV flux is absorbed or scattered.  
  > "When the source transits to the obscured phase, there is a rapid drop in Γ, log ξ, and flux, and a change from the highly ionized iron absorption lines to emission lines." (3.2.5. The obscured phase); "meaning that at most approximately 80% of the intrinsic flux density is absorbed and/or scattered in the 1–10 keV band." (3.2.5. The obscured phase)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Full polarization ALMA observations probe the compact jet spectrum, polarization, magnetic field alignment, and fast variability, while nearly daily NICER pointings track the accretion flow geometry, timing, and ionization during the outburst decay.  
  > "We conducted two full polarization Atacama Large Millimeter Array observations to study the jet properties during the outburst decay by analyzing the spectral, polarization, and intra-epoch variability for both observation epochs." (abstract); "In addition, we analyzed almost daily Neutron Star Interior Composition Explorer pointing observations, modeling X-ray power spectral densities, spectral energy distributions, and light curves with a physically motivated model to follow the changing accretion disk properties throughout the outburst decay and relating them to the jet emission." (abstract)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Leaving black hole spin free in relxill X-ray spectral fits was rejected because spin was unconstrained and inner disk radius was large; spin was fixed to zero.  
  > "However, we found that the spin is not constrained, and the inner radius presents large values indicating that the X-ray spectra are not sensitive to relativistic effects. Therefore, we decided to fix the spin to zero." (3.2.1. Description of the spectral model)
- Alternative: Leaving iron abundance free in X-ray spectral fits was rejected because it tended towards unphysically low values; it was fixed to solar.  
  > "In the fitting process, we found that the iron abundance tended towards unphysically low values, and therefore we decided to fix it to the solar value." (3.2.1. Description of the spectral model)
