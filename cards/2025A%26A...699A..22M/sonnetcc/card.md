## Analysis recipe

1. **measure the continuum positions and fluxes of A and C**: 2D Gaussian fit to the 25 mas Band 6 continuum map; flux integrated over a 3 sigma area  
  > "We derived their characteristics using a 2D Gaussian fit." (3.1 Position of the companion); "The integrated flux density over a 3$\sigma$ area of the primary was $28.3 \pm 2.8$ mJy, and $3.3 \pm 0.3$ mJy for the secondary." (3.1 Position of the companion)
   - parameter: noise sigma = 1.31e-5 Jy/beam; peaks of 736 sigma and 112 sigma  
  > "Two sources were spatially resolved and detected at peak values of 736$\sigma$ and 112$\sigma$, respectively." (3.1 Position of the companion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: brightest component confirmed as the AGB star by earlier work (citation not shown)  
  > "confirmed that the brightest component is the AGB star and the other is its suspected inner companion" (3.1 Position of the companion)
2. **constrain inclination and ascending node from the gas kinematics**: moment-1 maps and SiO P-V diagram; null line-of-sight velocity direction from HCN (about 55 deg) and SiO (about 50 deg) averaged to PA 52.5 deg, giving Omega  
  > "For HCN and SiO, found the direction of minimum line-of-sight velocity of the gas of $\sim 55^\circ{}$ and $\sim 50^\circ{}$ (position angle from north to east), respectively." (3.2.1 Constraints from the gas distribution); "We adopt the mean value of $52.5 \pm 5^\circ{}$ for the null radial velocity direction of the companion, and thus a longitude of the ascending node $\Omega = 322 \pm 5^\circ{}$." (3.2.1 Constraints from the gas distribution)
   - parameter: adopted orbital inclination 38 +/- 5 deg (broader than the literature 38 +/- 3 deg)  
  > "we conservatively adopt an orbital inclination of $38 \pm 5 ^\circ{}$ with respect to the plane of the sky (with the southern part of the orbit toward Earth)" (3.2.1 Constraints from the gas distribution)
   - assumption: inner gas follows the orbital motion; radial outflow negligible; gas plane co-planar with the orbit  
  > "We assume that, near the inner companion, the bulk gas motion mainly follows the orbital motion due to the changing location of the primary star caused by its orbital motion (i.e., the radial outflow is negligible)." (3.2.1 Constraints from the gas distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: P-V diagram maximum projected velocity follows a Keplerian field  
  > "It demonstrates that the maximum projected velocity of the inner gas distribution follows a Keplerian velocity field, consistent with our hypothesis." (3.2.1 Constraints from the gas distribution)
3. **use the HIPPARCOS-Gaia proper motion anomaly as a constraint**: PMa of the primary attributed entirely to C; sensitivity function shows the G0V companion B is negligible  
  > "The PMa is defined as the difference between the *Gaia* short-term and HIPPARCOS-*Gaia* long-term proper motion." (3.2.2 HIPPARCOS and Gaia proper motion anomaly); "We therefore attribute the PMa entirely to the new C companion." (3.2.2 HIPPARCOS and Gaia proper motion anomaly)
   - parameter: tangential velocity anomaly 6.07 +/- 0.33 km/s, PA 358.00 +/- 1.38 deg (July 2014 to May 2017)  
  > "The tangential velocity anomaly of the primary, measured between July 2014 and May 2017, is $6.07 \pm 0.33$ km s$^{-1}$, with a position angle of $358.00 \pm 1.38^\circ{}$." (3.2.2 HIPPARCOS and Gaia proper motion anomaly)
   - assumption: photocenter tracks only the primary  
  > "In this analysis, we assumed that the photocenter only tracks the primary star, as confirmed by the non-detection of the companion in the visible with VLT/SPHERE" (3.2.2 HIPPARCOS and Gaia proper motion anomaly)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **fit the orbit of C with Bayesian nested sampling**: posterior and evidence from MLFriends nested sampling in UltraNest, constrained by ALMA 2019 astrometry, the 0 km/s direction at PA 52 +/- 5 deg and the PMa; best fit is the maximum likelihood  
  > "we derived posterior probability distributions and Bayesian evidence using the nested sampling Monte Carlo algorithm" (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters); "The fit was constrained by several observations, namely the astrometry from the ALMA continuum 2019 observation" (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
   - software: UltraNest, kepler.py
   - parameter: flat priors: M_A 1.1-1.9 Msun; q = M_C/M_A 0.2-0.8; a 5-10 au  
  > "$q= M_\mathrm{C}/M_\mathrm{A}$ between 0.2 and 0.8," (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters); "$a$, the semi-major axis of the orbit, between 5 and 10 au" (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
   - parameter: flat priors: e 0-1; Omega and omega 0-360 deg; i 33-43 deg; tau_0 2009-2024  
  > "$i$, the inclination, between 33 and 43$^\circ{}$" (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters); "$\tau_0$, the epoch of the periastron between 2009 and 2024" (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
   - assumption: reference frame of the primary; momentum conservation relates the C tangential velocity to the PMa of A  
  > "We adopted the reference frame of the primary." (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
   - fitting: UltraNest step sampler, 1000 live points, number of steps equal to twice the number of parameters; likelihood not stated  
  > "We used the step sampler with 1000 live points and a number of steps equal to twice the number of parameters." (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
   - validation: model predicts C at the head of the polarized tail in ZIMPOL epochs not used as constraints; runs with fixed or Gaussian priors on M_A and i shift other parameters only marginally  
  > "At each epoch where contemporaneous ALMA continuum detection is not available, the orbital solution predicts a C companion at the head of the polarized tail." (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters); "Runs with fixed values or Gaussian priors centered on literature values and their uncertainties for these parameters resulted in only marginal shifts of the other parameters within their error bars." (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
5. **search the AAVSO light curve for the orbital period**: Lomb-Scargle periodogram of visual observations  
  > "We analyzed the data with the `LombScargle` function implemented in `Astropy` ." (3.4 Variability of the pi1 Gru system in the visible); "We selected only the visual observations because of their better time sampling and range." (3.4 Variability of the pi1 Gru system in the visible)
   - software: Astropy
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **build the SED and visible detection limits for A, B and C**: MARCS model for A, PHOENIX spectra for B and C, WD model; Lucy-Richardson deconvolution of ZIMPOL intensity images to get upper limits at the ALMA position of C  
  > "using the `Pyraf` implementation of the Lucy-Richardson algorithm , with 10 iterations." (3.5 Photometry from the ultraviolet to the millimeter); "we estimated the detection limit by summing the brightness over a PSF surface area." (3.5 Photometry from the ultraviolet to the millimeter)
   - software: PyRAF, PySSED, MARCS, PHOENIX
   - parameter: angular diameter of A set to 14.7 mas; SEDs smoothed with a 4 A rectangular window; Rayleigh-Jeans extrapolation beyond 5 micron; WD radius 0.009 Rsun and Teff 15 000 K  
  > "we adopted an angular diameter of 14.7 mas in place of the inferometric H band measurement of $18.37 \pm 0.18$ mas" (3.5 Photometry from the ultraviolet to the millimeter); "We selected an effective temperature of 15 000 K matching the far-ultraviolet photometry from GALEX." (3.5 Photometry from the ultraviolet to the millimeter)
   - assumption: models have no circumstellar dust, are static and in chemical equilibrium; no circumstellar extinction included  
  > "We note that both MARCS and PHOENIX models do not include circumstellar dust, are static (i.e., do not consider pulsations), and assume chemical equilibrium." (3.5 Photometry from the ultraviolet to the millimeter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **test whether the UV excess is chromospheric or from accretion**: compare FUV/NUV flux ratio with the 0.06 chromospheric threshold of earlier GALEX AGB work  
  > "They showed that if the FUV/NUV flux ratio, $R_{FUV/NUV}$, is less than 0.06, the UV emission can be explained by chromospheric and photospheric emission from an AGB star." (4.2 The apparently inconsistent photometry)
   - parameter: R_FUV/NUV = 0.1 for the A+B+C system  
  > "$\pi^1$ Gru system (A+B+C), $R_{FUV/NUV}=0.1$." (4.2 The apparently inconsistent photometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **model the 1.3 mm emission of C as a thermal accretion disk or free-free emission**: blackbody steady optically thick disk (Pringle 1981) over a grid of inner radii and accretion rates; dust heated by A; free-free from a constant-density model  
  > "We can compute the radial temperature distribution if the disk is optically thick and in thermal equilibrium, using the equation below (cite: Pringle1981):" (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk); "Free-free emission may also contribute to the observed 1.3 mm continuum emission toward $\pi^1$ Gru C" (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk)
   - parameter: inner radius 0.001-0.01 au; outer radius <= 2 au  
  > "We computed a large grid of models with $0.001 <r_i ({\rm au}) < 0.01$." (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk); "The outer disk radius was conservatively set to $r_o ({\rm au}) \le 2$ because the continuum source is unresolved with a beam of $0.025''$." (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk)
   - assumption: disk emits as a blackbody; gas-to-dust ratio 200; primary mass-loss rate 7.7e-7 Msun/yr  
  > "Assuming the disk emits as a blackbody, we compute the emission for various values of $\mbox{$\dot{M}$}_{a}$." (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk); "For a gas-to-dust ratio of 200, this corresponds to a gas mass of $3.4\times10^{-5}$ M$_\odot$." (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- A polarized dust tail trails the companion and the orbital solution places C at its head  
  > "We propose that this tail, which extends behind the companion and forms a spiral-like structure, results from the interaction with the stellar wind" (3.1 Position of the companion)
- C is not detected in the visible even after Lucy-Richardson deconvolution

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SPHERE observations were scheduled within 15 days of ALMA extended-configuration data so that both trace the same circumstellar environment  
  > "The ZIMPOL and ALMA extended-configuration observations are separated by at most 15 days, as was intended with the ToO request on VLT/SPHERE." (2.2 ALMA observations)
- Why this method: inclination fixed from the gas kinematics assuming the gas is co-planar with the orbit, with a broader interval to explore more parameter space  
  > "In the following modeling, assuming co-planarity between the inner gaseous features and the orbital motion of the inner companion, we conservatively adopt an orbital inclination of $38 \pm 5 ^\circ{}$ with respect to the plane of the sky" (3.2.1 Constraints from the gas distribution)
- Alternative: chromospheric emission as the origin of the UV excess, rejected for the FUV  
  > "This implies that the FUV emission seen toward $\pi^1$ Gru cannot be produced by chromospheric emission and instead results from accretion activity related to A and C." (4.2 The apparently inconsistent photometry)
- Alternative: stellar photosphere as the origin of the mm emission (would need an M3III star), rejected  
  > "For instance, to match the ALMA detection, we would need an M3III type star." (4.2 The apparently inconsistent photometry)
- Alternative: fixed values or Gaussian priors for M_A and i, tested and found to change other parameters only marginally  
  > "Runs with fixed values or Gaussian priors centered on literature values and their uncertainties for these parameters resulted in only marginal shifts of the other parameters within their error bars." (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): primary mass and inclination poorly constrained by the fit  
  > "As indicated by their relatively flat posterior distributions, the primary mass and inclination are poorly constrained." (3.2.3 Derivation of orbital parameters)
- Limitation (authors): circular orbit cannot be fully excluded  
  > "although a circular orbit cannot be fully excluded." (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): visible upper limits ignore circumstellar material around C  
  > "providing such an estimate is not feasible due to the complex geometry of the material around $\pi^1$ Gru A and C." (3.5 Photometry from the ultraviolet to the millimeter)
- Limitation (authors): dust versus free-free origin of the mm emission cannot be discriminated  
  > "This prevents a clear discrimination between thermal dust emission and free-free emission scenarios." (4.3 Considerations on the accretion disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination and Omega are set from gas kinematics assuming co-planarity with the orbit, so the orbit is partly circular reasoning with the ALMA data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The PMa is attributed entirely to C with a single 2016 epoch, and the likelihood is not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA position comes from one epoch, so the orbit rests mostly on a single astrometric point plus indirect constraints
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flat priors on M_A and i make the quoted M_C depend on poorly constrained parameters
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Best-fit values are the maximum likelihood with posterior standard deviations, which are asymmetric and not directly comparable to medians
