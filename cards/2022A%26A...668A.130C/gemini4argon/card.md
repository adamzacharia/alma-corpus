## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies
- Question: Characterize spatially resolved scaling relations between ISM components (dust, gas, metallicity) and galaxy properties (stellar mass, total baryonic content, SFR) and their ratios in 18 nearby face-on spiral DustPedia galaxies across physical scales from 0.3 to 3.4 kpc.  
  > "We perform a homogeneous analysis of an unprecedented set of spatially resolved scaling relations (SRs) between interstellar medium (ISM) components, that is to say dust, gas, and gas-phase metallicity, and other galaxy properties, such as stellar mass (M$_{\rm star}$), total baryonic content, and star-formation rate (SFR), in a range of physical scales between 0.3 and 3.4 kpc." (abstract); "The aim of this paper is therefore to give a view that is as complete as possible of the resolved SRs in spiral galaxies of the local Universe." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble and compute surface density maps of galaxy components, metallicity, and SFR**: Collect dust, stellar, SFR, HI, and CO maps from C17 and add new CO maps (including ALMA maps for NGC 3621 and NGC 1365). Compute H2, total gas, total baryonic content, and azimuthally averaged metallicity maps, correcting all surface density maps for galaxy inclination.  
  > "We use mass surface density maps of various galactic components (dust, molecular, atomic and total gas, stars, and total baryonic content) and of the SFR. All maps are corrected for inclination $i$" (3 The dataset); "Combining available data on molecular and atomic gas, we have produced total gas-mass surface-density maps summing atomic and molecular gas masses and correcting this sum for the helium contribution by multiplying by a factor of 1.36 (cite: schruba11), $\Sigma_{\rm{tot\,gas}}$ = 1.36 $\times$ ($\Sigma\rm_{HI}$ + $\Sigma\rm_{H2}$)." (The gas mass maps); "We produce total baryonic content maps summing stellar, total gas, and dust masses, $\Sigma_{\rm bar} = \Sigma_{\rm star} + \Sigma_{\rm tot\,gas} + \Sigma_{\rm dust}$." (The total baryonic content maps)
   - parameter: Region restricted to within optical disk r <= r_25  
  > "We focus on the region within the optical disk ($r \leq r_{25}$) for all maps of all galaxies." (The dataset)
   - assumption: Constant X_CO = 2.0 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 (alpha_CO = 3.2 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1, without helium) or metallicity-dependent X_CO from Amorin et al. (2016): log(alpha_CO) = 0.68 - 1.45[12 + log(O/H - 8.7)] corrected for helium  
  > "We adopt the constant value of $X_{\rm CO} = 2.0 \times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ with $\pm$30$\%$ uncertainty (cite: bolatto13), corresponding to a CO-to-H$_{2}$ conversion factor expressed in terms of $\alpha_{CO}$ ($\alpha_{CO} = {\rm M(H_{2})}/{\rm L_{CO}}$, where ${\rm M(H_{2})}$ is the H$_{2}$ mass in M$_\odot$ and ${\rm L_{CO}}$ the CO line luminosity in K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$) of $\alpha_{CO} = 3.2\,{\rm M}_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: narayanan12)." (The gas mass maps); "We assume the calibration for the metallicity-dependent $X_{\rm CO}$ by (cite: amorin16): ${\rm log}(\alpha_{\rm CO}) = 0.68 - 1.45[12 + {\rm log(O/H} - 8.7)]$, corresponding to $X_{\rm CO} \propto (Z/Z_\odot)^{-1.5}$." (The gas mass maps)
   - assumption: 12CO line ratio R_21 = I_CO(2-1)/I_CO(1-0) = 0.7  
  > "In the case of the use of $^{12}$CO(2–1) observations, we assumed a $^{12}$CO line ratio $R_{21} = I_{\rm CO(2-1)}/I_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ = 0.7, a typical value for nearby spiral galaxies (cite: schruba11; leroy13; casasola15; yajima21)." (The gas mass maps)
   - assumption: Helium contribution factor of 1.36 for total gas  
  > "Combining available data on molecular and atomic gas, we have produced total gas-mass surface-density maps summing atomic and molecular gas masses and correcting this sum for the helium contribution by multiplying by a factor of 1.36 (cite: schruba11), $\Sigma_{\rm{tot\,gas}}$ = 1.36 $\times$ ($\Sigma\rm_{HI}$ + $\Sigma\rm_{H2}$)." (The gas mass maps)
   - assumption: Solar oxygen abundance 12 + log(O/H) = 8.69 +/- 0.05 and N2 metallicity calibration  
  > "We adopt a solar oxygen abundance of 12 + log(O/H) = $8.69 \pm 0.05$ from (cite: asplund09)." (The gas-phase metallicity maps); "We adopt a solar oxygen abundance of 12 + log(O/H) = $8.69 \pm 0.05$" (3.4 The gas-phase metallicity maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested two alternative X_CO calibrations from Bolatto et al. (2013) and Madden et al. (2020) in Appendix C  
  > "We have tested two other prescriptions on $X_{\rm CO}$, one from (cite: bolatto13) and the other one from (cite: madden20)." (Results for individual galaxies)

  > "All images are convolved at the resolution of 36$''$, the same of the $\Sigma_{\rm{dust}}$ maps." (Image convolution and resizing); "All maps are resampled to a pixel size equal to the adopted dust resolution, 36$''$, that is one pixel per beam (cite: viaene14; vutisalchavakul14; casasola15; saikia20)."   > "The galaxies NGC 3621 and NGC 4736 are excluded from the analysis at 3.4 kpc because the convolution at this resolution produces maps of only 4 pixels size." (Image convolution and resizing)

  > "After eliminating possible corrupted pixels and pixels with negative values, we use those with $\geq$3$\sigma$ significance within $r_{25}$ of all galaxy maps in order to study the pixel-by-pixel SRs."    - parameter: Significance threshold >= 3 sigma within r_25  
  > "After eliminating possible corrupted pixels and pixels with negative values, we use those with $\geq$3$\sigma$ significance within $r_{25}$ of all galaxy maps in order to study the pixel-by-pixel SRs." (Fitting method)

(Dust-to-metal ratio)
   - fitting: Linear fit in logarithmic space log(Sigma_y) = q + m * log(Sigma_x), evaluating slope m, intercept q, Pearson correlation coefficient R, and residual dispersion sigma  
  > "For each linear fit we also provide the Pearson correlation coefficient $R$, the standard deviation of residuals (dispersion) $\sigma$, and the number of datapoints taken into account." (Fitting method)
   - validation: Verified that >= 3 sigma pixels represent ~97-100% of pixels within r_25 so excluding non-detections has no systematic impact on slopes.  
  > "We stress that the $\geq$3$\sigma$ pixels represent $\sim$97–100$\%$ of the pixels within the optical radius of the explored galaxies. (Fitting method)

## Qualitative findings

- All explored scaling relations are moderate or strong correlations except the Sigma_dust-Sigma_HI relation, which is weak or absent for most galaxies at subkpc/kpc scales.  
  > "All the SRs are moderate or strong correlations except the dust-Hi SR that does not exist or is weak for most galaxies." (abstract)
- Resolved scaling relations between 0.3 and 3.4 kpc do not have a universal form; each galaxy displays distinct correlations shaped by local processes and peculiarities, and slopes are approximately independent of physical scale in this range.  
  > "The SRs do not have a universal form but each galaxy is characterized by distinct correlations, affected by local processes and galaxy peculiarities." (abstract); "For a given galaxy, the slopes and correlation coefficients of the SRs are approximately independent on scale, in the range 0.3–3.4 kpc." (Conclusions)
- When all galaxies are evaluated together at the common scale of 3.4 kpc, individual peculiarities cancel out and the scaling relations match those of whole galaxies, supporting self-regulation of star formation driven by local processes.  
  > "By evaluating all galaxies together at the common scale of 3.4 kpc, differences due to peculiarities of individual galaxies are cancelled out and the corresponding SRs are consistent with those of whole galaxies." (abstract); "In this scenario, our results are consistent with the hypothesis of self-regulation of the star-formation process." (abstract)
- Subgalactic dust-to-stellar, dust-to-gas (DGR), and dust-to-metal (DTM) ratios are consistent with global galaxy values from z = 0 to 5, and DGR scales superlinearly with metallicity as (O/H)^(1.2-1.3).  
  > "The mean values and the evolution of specific dust mass, DGR, and DTM for nearby galaxies at subgalactic scales are consistent with those derived for whole galaxies from $z = 0$ to 5, suggesting that also these ratios are mainly set by local processes. We find that DGR depends on metallicity as DGR $\propto$ (O/H)$^{1.2-1.3}$ and this is consistent with theoretical expectations including dust grain growth." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 18 nearby face-on spiral galaxies from DustPedia that are resolved across at least 15 Herschel-SPIRE 500 um resolution elements, and replaced/added CO(1-0) and ALMA CO maps to improve optical disk coverage and avoid R_21 line ratio uncertainties where possible.  
  > "They have been imaged over their whole extent with both PACS and SPIRE in *Herschel* and they have a submillimeter diameter $D_{\rm{submm}} \geq 9^{\prime}$, corresponding at least to 15 spatial resolution elements in the SPIRE 500 $\mu$m maps (FWHM = 36$''$). For this latter reason, we define these galaxies as resolved in dust emission." (The galaxy sample); "This substitution is dictated by the fact that the use of the $^{12}$CO(1–0) emission line eliminates the uncertainty on the $R_{21}$ line ratio in the H$_2$ mass derivation and the COMING map, differently by that used in C17, covers the entire optical radius of NGC 3521." (The gas mass maps)
- Why this method: Resampled pixel size to equal the 36'' spatial resolution (one pixel per beam) so that pixels are roughly statistically independent datapoints, and adopted the same X_CO calibrations as C20 to allow consistent comparison with global DustPedia scaling relations.  
  > "Studying pixel-by-pixel SRs, we prefer to assume the pixel size equivalent to the spatial resolution so that the pixels can be considered as roughly statistically independent, and there should be little correlation among them." (Image convolution and resizing); "The adopted calibrations of $X_{\rm CO}$ by (cite: bolatto13) and (cite: amorin16) are the same ones used in the study of the global ISM SRs for DustPedia late-type galaxies of (cite: casasola20), allowing therefore a consistent comparison of the SRs between small and global scales." (The gas mass maps)
- Alternative: Discarded N2 metallicity gradients for NGC 5194 and NGC 1097 as unreliable; adopted the direct-method O/H gradient from Croxall et al. (2015) for NGC 5194 and the mean metallicity for NGC 1097.  
  > "We decide to discard the values of metallicity gradients derived from the N2 calibration of NGC 5194 and NGC 1097 because they are unreliable and quite unusual for typical nearby spiral galaxies (cite: magrini11; magrini16; sanchez14; belfiore17; maiolino19; yates21)." (The gas-phase metallicity maps); "We decide to discard the values of metallicity gradients derived from the N2 calibration of NGC 5194 and NGC 1097 because they are unreliable and quite unusual for typical nearby spiral galaxies" (3.4 The gas-phase metallicity maps)
- Alternative: Tested two alternative X_CO calibrations from Bolatto et al. (2013) (metallicity and surface density dependent) and Madden et al. (2020) (Cloudy model based), but did not adopt them for the full sample because they did not yield extremely different results.  
  > "We have tested two other prescriptions on $X_{\rm CO}$, one from (cite: bolatto13) and the other one from (cite: madden20). Since these tests do no give extremely different results from those obtained under the adopted prescriptions, we do not show them for the entire sample." (Results for individual galaxies)
- Alternative: Replaced the IRAM-30m 12CO(2-1) map of NGC 3521 used in C17 with the Nobeyama 45-m COMING 12CO(1-0) map.  
  > "In particular, we replace the IRAM–30m $^{12}$CO(2–1) map of NGC 3521 used in C17 with $^{12}$CO(1–0) observations mentioned above." (The gas mass maps)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Metallicity maps are azimuthally averaged from radial metallicity gradients (or mean metallicity for NGC 1097) rather than complete 2D maps, neglecting azimuthal variations and local chemical inhomogeneities.  
  > "We use metallicity maps derived from metallicity gradients instead of “real” and complete metallicity maps. In this way we have neglected the azimuthal variations that may be present at each galactocentric radius or local chemical inhomogeneities as present, for example, in Hii regions (cite: rosolowsky08; sanders12; clark19)." (The gas-phase metallicity maps)
- Limitation (authors): NGC 3621 and NGC 4736 are excluded from the 3.4 kpc common-scale analysis because convolution to 3.4 kpc yields maps of only 4 pixels.  
  > "The galaxies NGC 3621 and NGC 4736 are excluded from the analysis at 3.4 kpc because the convolution at this resolution produces maps of only 4 pixels size." (Image convolution and resizing)
- Limitation (authors): Not all maps are available for all 18 galaxies (H2 for 15/18, HI for 15/18, total gas and baryonic content for 13/18, metallicity for 17/18), and the dataset does not reach Sigma_H2/Sigma_HI >= 10 to fully trace the high molecular ratio regime.  
  > "Combining all $^{12}$CO data, $\Sigma_{\rm H2}$ maps are available for 15/18 sample galaxies" (3.2 The gas mass maps); "The $\Sigma_{\rm{tot\,gas}}$ maps are available for 13/18 sample galaxies." (The gas mass maps); "With our dataset, we are not able to draw this trend up to $\Sigma_{\rm H2}/\Sigma_{\rm HI} \sim 10$ and higher, but the behavior of DGR at $\Sigma_{\rm H2}/\Sigma_{\rm HI} \approx 1$ seems to be consistent with the hill-like shape published in C20." (Dust-to-total gas mass ratio)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlating ratio quantities against their own denominators or components (e.g., log(Sigma_dust/Sigma_star) vs log(Sigma_star), or log(DGR) vs f_gas) can introduce artificial correlation from shared variables.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Combining 12CO(1-0) and 12CO(2-1) maps across the sample with a fixed R_21 = 0.7 ignores radial variations in CO excitation within individual disks.
