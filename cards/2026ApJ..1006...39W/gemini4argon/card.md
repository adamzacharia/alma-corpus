## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST NIRCam imaging and WFSS data and extract 2D and 1D spectra**: Calibrate NIRCam imaging and WFSS data using CALWEBB and unfold_jwst, align to GAIA DR3, detect sources in F356W with SourcExtractor++, and extract 2D and 1D spectra for all F356W-detected sources  
  > "The NIRcam images were reduced using version 1.10.2 of the JWST Calibration Pipeline (CALWEBB)." (NIRCam imaging data reduction); "After matching PSF, we used the SourcExtractor++ dual-image mode with the respective weight maps for each filter with F356W as the detection band to extract photometry." (NIRCam imaging data reduction); "We extracted both 2D and 1D spectra for all sources detected in the F356W imaging." (NIRCam WFSS data reduction)
   - software: CALWEBB, unfold_jwst, tweakwcs, photutils, SourcExtractor++, PypeIt
   - parameter: jwst_1080.pmap (CRDS v11.16.21); pixel scale 0.031'' (SW), 0.0315'' (LW), pixfrac=0.8  
  > "We used the reference files (jwst_1080.pmap) from version 11.16.21 of the standard Calibration Reference Data System (CRDS) to calibrate our data." (NIRCam imaging data reduction); "During the resampling step, we used a fixed pixel scale of 0.031$''$ for SW images and 0.0315$''$ for LW images with pixfrac=0.8." (NIRCam imaging data reduction)
   - parameter: DETECT_THRESH=3.0, Kron k, Rmin = 1.2, 1.7, DETECT_MINAREA=10 pixels, PHOT_APERTURES=12 pixels  
  > "We set DETECT_THRESH=3.0, Kron parameters k, Rmin = 1.2, 1.7, DETECT_MINAREA=10 pixels, and PHOT_APERTURES= 12 pixels." (NIRCam imaging data reduction)
   - parameter: 2D wavelength grid 9.8 Angstrom pixel^-1; boxcar extraction aperture diameter 7 pixels (0.411'')  
  > "The 2D spectrum of each source is extracted from each individual exposure, and the individual 2D spectra are then coadded into a 2D spectrum after resampling them to common wavelength (9.8 $\rm \AA~pixel^{-1}$) and spatial grids following the histogram2D technique in the PypeIt software (cite: Pypeit; Pypeit_ascl)." (NIRCam WFSS data reduction); "We used an aperture diameter of 7 pixels ($0.411\ensuremath{''}$) for the boxcar spectral extraction." (NIRCam WFSS data reduction)
   - assumption: Galactic extinction corrected using Schlegel et al. (1998) map and Schlafly & Finkbeiner (2011) law  
  > "The Galactic extinctions in each filter were corrected on the basis of the dust reddening map from (cite: schlegel98) and the dust extinction law derived from (cite: Schlafly11)." (NIRCam imaging data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Astrometry compared against Gaia DR3 showing ~50 mas offsets; photometric uncertainties from 1000 empty apertures  
  > "The aligned mosaic images have a precise astrometry and the comparison of NIRCam positions with Gaia catalog typically reveals small offsets of $\sim$50 mas after considering proper motions." (NIRCam imaging data reduction); "To determine the photometric uncertainties and limiting magnitudes in each ASPIRE fields, we placed 1000 empty apertures across the images after masking out real sources using the segmentation map, and measured the median absolute deviation of flux in the fixed aperture size." (NIRCam imaging data reduction)
2. **Identify [O III] line-emitting galaxies from NIRCam WFSS 1D and 2D spectra**: Semiautomatic emission-line search on continuum-subtracted 1D and 2D spectra requiring detection in both 1D and 2D algorithms, followed by multi-inspector visual grading  
  > "Based on the emission-line searching algorithm developed by (cite: Wang23), we further improved the efficiency and purity of the emission-line detections and performed a semiautomatic search using the continuum-subtracted 2D and 1D spectra." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm); "To ensure the robustness of the [O III] emitter candidate identification, we only kept objects that were identified as [O III] emitter candidates from both the 1D and 2D algorithms." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm); "To reduce the bias of visual inspection, we required that each object be inspected by at least three different team members (E.B., J.B.C., X.J., K.K., R.A.M., F.W., Y.W., J.Y.)." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm)
   - software: unfold_jwst, Photutils
   - parameter: 1D median filter window = 51 pixels (~0.05 um); 1D peak S/N > 1.2 in smoothed spectrum and integrated S/N > 2 within +-2 pixels; [O III] 5008 S/N > 5 and [O III] 4960 or Hbeta S/N > 2  
  > "For the 1D case, we generated a median-filtered continuum model with a window size of 51 pixels (corresponding to $\sim0.05~\mu$m), which was then subtracted from the optimally extracted spectrum." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm); "To remove such artifacts, we only treat peaks with a total S/N of $\rm S/N>2$ as emission line candidates, where the total S/N was measured by integrating $\pm2$ original pixels (i.e., the S/N spectra without convolving with the spectral resolution) from the center of the peak." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm); "To identify if a certain object is a [O III] emitter candidate, we first assume that all identified lines with $\rm S/N>5$ (if exists) are the [O III] $\lambda5008$ line and then ask if a corresponding [O III] $\lambda4960$ or H$\beta$ line also exists (i.e., with $\rm S/N>2$)." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm)
   - parameter: 2D cutout diameter = 5 pixels; >= 3 connected pixels with S/N > 0.8 and integrated flux > 2 sigma  
  > "We treat all blobs that have at least three connected pixels with $\rm S/N>0.8$ and the integrated flux with $>2\sigma$ significance as emission line candidates." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested recovery against Wang et al. (2023) sample in J0305-3150, recovering all but the faintest emitter and finding 10 new emitters; visual inspection by >=3 team members  
  > "We found that this strategy can recover all [O III] emitters except for the faintest one (ASPIRE-J0305M31-O3-023) in (cite: Wang23) and identified 10 additional new [O III] emitters in this field." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm)
3. **Search for [C II] line-emitting galaxies in ALMA Band 6 mosaic cubes**: Run INTERFEROPY (Python implementation of FINDCLUMPs) with SExtractor on averaged channels of continuum-subtracted ALMA cubes across a range of kernel widths  
  > "Briefly, the [C II] line emitters were identified using the INTERFEROPY software (cite: Interferopy), which is a Python implementation of FINDCLUMPs (cite: Walter16)." (The discovery of 17 [C II] emitters from ALMA data); "INTERFEROPY runs SExtractor (cite: SExtractor) on averaged channels of continuum subtracted cubes with a range of kernel widths and identifies objects with S/N$>$3." (The discovery of 17 [C II] emitters from ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, INTERFEROPY, SExtractor
   - parameter: Cube channel width = 30 km s^-1, robust = 2.0; candidate detection S/N > 3, final selection threshold S/N > 6  
  > "The data cubes were created with a channel width of 30 km s$^{-1}$ using robust=2.0, clark deconvolver, $2\sigma$ threshold and a mask with a radius of 1.0$''$ centered on the central quasars." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "In this work, we consider only line emitter candidates with S/N$>6$, a threshold at which the fidelity reaches $\sim$100%, ensuring a highly pure galaxy sample suitable for clustering analysis." (The discovery of 17 [C II] emitters from ALMA data)
   - assumption: Line emitter candidates in the LSB with S/N > 6 are [C II] emitters at the quasar redshifts  
  > "In total, we identify 17 positive line emitter candidates with S/N$>6$, 16 of which lie within the line search bandwidth (LSB) corresponding to the expected [C II] wavelength at the quasar redshifts." (The discovery of 17 [C II] emitters from ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Fidelity calculated by comparing positive and negative candidate S/N distributions; S/N > 6 corresponds to ~100% fidelity  
  > "Each line emitter candidate was assigned with a fidelity by comparing the candidate’s S/N with the S/N distribution of positive and negative candidates for a given kernel width." (The discovery of 17 [C II] emitters from ALMA data)
4. **Measure and fit the quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function (CCF)**: Count quasar-galaxy pairs <QG> and expected random pairs <QR> in radial cylindrical shells for both [O III] and [C II] emitters, and fit a power-law real-space CCF using MCMC  
  > "To determine the clustering of galaxies around ASPIRE quasars, we follow (cite: hennawi06b) and measure a volume-averaged projected cross-correlation function between quasars and line emitters (both [O III] and [C II] emitters)" (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function); "We perform an MCMC fit to the measured cross-correlation function using emcee (cite: emcee)." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
   - software: unfold_jwst, Corrfunc, emcee
   - parameter: Cylinder height Z = 7 cMpc h^-1 (Delta v_los <= 1000 km s^-1) for [O III]; Z ~ 14 cMpc h^-1 (Delta v_los ~ +-2100 km s^-1, full LSB) for [C II]; 5x10^6 mock sources injected per field for [O III] selection function  
  > "Given the limited number of [C II] emitter candidates, we use all galaxies in LSB to measure $\langle QG \rangle$, corresponding to a cylinder height of $Z\sim 14~{\rm cMpc}\,h^{-1}$ or line-of-sight velocity $\Delta v_{\rm los} \sim \pm2100 \, \text{km s}^{-1}$." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function); "Using these line flux limit maps, we then constructed an unfold_jwst detected random source catalog and a corresponding selection function by injecting five million ($5\times10^{6}$) mock sources into the full F356W imaging footprint of each quasar field and identifying which mock sources met the detection threshold of the emitter search." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function); "We then used the Corrfunc python package (cite: Sinha20) to count quasar-galaxy pairs, $\langle QG \rangle$, and quasar-mock galaxy pairs, $\langle QR \rangle$, within cylindrical bins defined by $(R_{\rm min}, R_{\rm max})$ and a cylinder height of $Z = 7~{\rm cMpc}\,h^{-1}$, corresponding to a line-of-sight velocity of $\Delta v_{\rm los} \lesssim 1000~{\rm km~s^{-1}}$ at the ASPIRE quasar redshifts." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
   - parameter: Fixed slope gamma_QG = 2.0 (also tested free gamma_QG)  
  > "When fixing the slope at $\gamma_{\rm{QG}} = 2.0$ to enable a comparison to other studies (cite: Shen10; Eilers24), we derive $r_0^{\rm{QG}} = 8.68^{+0.51}_{-0.55}~h^{-1}~{\rm cMpc}$ for ASPIRE quasars with an average redshift of $\langle z\rangle=6.6645$." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "Throughout the paper, we adopt a $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $H_0=70~{\rm km~s^{-1}~Mpc^{-1}}$, $\rm \Omega_M=0.3$, and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.7$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: [O III] luminosity function from Matthee et al. (2022) with no redshift evolution for mock injection; [C II] background density estimated from USB  
  > "Since the ALMA mosaic observations provide uniform survey depth in both the lower and upper sidebands (LSB and USB), corresponding to galaxies at the quasar redshifts and at slightly lower redshifts, respectively, we can directly estimate the field galaxy number density, $n_{\rm G}(z)$, from the USB data." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function); "The flux distribution of the injected sources was matched to the [O III] luminosity function from (cite: Matthee22), assuming no redshift evolution." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
   - fitting: MCMC fit with emcee to power law xi_QG = (r / r_0^QG)^(-gamma_QG) with Poisson uncertainties  
  > "Note that the uncertainties of all $\chi(R_{\rm min}, R_{\rm max})$ measurements in Table 4 include only Poisson noise." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function); "We perform an MCMC fit to the measured cross-correlation function using emcee (cite: emcee)." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
   - validation: Consistent CCF between [O III] and [C II] emitters; compared with TRINITY + Uchuu simulation predictions  
  > "Since the quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function measured from [O III] emitters is consistent with that of [C II] emitters, we fit all data together." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function); "The TRINITY predicted quasar-[O III] emitter cross-correlation function is shown in Figure 8." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)
5. **Infer quasar auto-correlation length, host dark matter halo mass, duty cycle, and lifetime**: Calculate quasar auto-correlation from xi_QG = sqrt(xi_QQ * xi_GG), link r_0^QQ to minimum halo mass using halomod, and derive duty cycle and lifetime from halo abundance matching  
  > "Assuming that quasars and [O III] emitters trace the same underlying dark matter density field (cite: Garcia17), the quasar auto-correlation $\xi_{\rm{QQ}}$ can be inferred from the galaxy-galaxy auto-correlation $\xi_{\rm{GG}}$ and the quasar-galaxy cross-correlation $\xi_{\rm{QG}}$" (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles); "Using the halomod (cite: Murray13; Murray21) tool with an assumption of a step-function Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD), we estimate that $\rm log(M_{\rm{halo, min}}/M_\odot)= 12.27^{+0.21}_{-0.26}$ with a cumulative abundance of dark matter halos $n_{\rm{halo,min}}= {\rm 54.6^{+510.0}_{-48.5}\, cGpc^{-3}}$ with $M>M_{\rm{halo, min}}$." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles); "Together with $n_{\rm{halo,min}}$, we can then infer quasars’ duty cycle ($f_{\rm duty}$) and UV-luminous life time ($t_Q$)" (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)
   - software: halomod
   - parameter: r_0^GG = 4.78 +0.50/-0.55 h^-1 cMpc; n_Q = 0.76 cGpc^-3  
  > "After applying the $r_0^{\rm{GG}}$ of [O III] emitter ($r_0^{\rm{GG}}=4.78^{+0.50}_{-0.55}\,\rm{h}^{-1}\,\rm{cMpc}$) measured directly from ASPIRE galaxies (Huang et al. in prep), we derive an quasar auto-correlation length of $r_0^{\rm{QQ}}=15.76^{+2.48}_{-2.70}~h^{-1}~{\rm cMpc}$." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles); "The spatial density of ASPIRE quasars (i.e., $6.5<z<7$ and $M_{1450}>-25$) are estimated to be $n_{\rm Q}=\rm 0.76~cGpc^{-3}$ based on the luminosity function from (cite: Wang19b) and (cite: Matsuoka18c)." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)
   - assumption: Quasars and [O III] emitters trace the same dark matter density field; step-function Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD)  
  > "Assuming that quasars and [O III] emitters trace the same underlying dark matter density field (cite: Garcia17), the quasar auto-correlation $\xi_{\rm{QQ}}$ can be inferred from the galaxy-galaxy auto-correlation $\xi_{\rm{GG}}$ and the quasar-galaxy cross-correlation $\xi_{\rm{QG}}$" (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles); "Using the halomod (cite: Murray13; Murray21) tool with an assumption of a step-function Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD), we estimate that $\rm log(M_{\rm{halo, min}}/M_\odot)= 12.27^{+0.21}_{-0.26}$" (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with TRINITY model prediction (log(M_halo/M_sun) = 12.04+-0.22) and lower-z quasar clustering measurements  
  > "The corresponding median halo mass in TRINITY is $\rm log(M_{\rm{halo}}/M_\odot)= 12.04{\pm0.22}$, also similar to our measurements." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)

  > "The galaxy overdensity $\delta_{\rm gal, [OIII]}$ is measured using galaxies within NIRCam module A (with $r_{\rm eff} \lesssim 3~{\rm cMpc}$, or $V \sim 500~{\rm cMpc}^3$ assuming a projected length of $\rm \Delta |v_{\rm los}|\le1000~km~s^{-1}$), where we have uniform depth and continuous coverage." ## Qualitative findings

- On average, luminous z ~ 7 quasars are strong tracers of galaxy overdensities and reside in massive (~10^12.27 M_sun) dark matter halos, with no significant evolution in characteristic quasar halo mass across cosmic time.  
  > "Our results suggest that the characteristic dark matter halo mass of quasars does not evolve significantly with redshift up to $z \sim 7$, remaining at a few times $10^{12}~M_\odot$." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles); "On average, luminous $z \sim 7$ quasars are effective tracers of galaxy overdensities, with 18 out of 25 quasars hosting more than one companion [O III]-emitting galaxy and exhibiting overdensities relative to the expected cosmic average." (Summary)
- Both ASPIRE measurements and TRINITY predictions show a small-scale excess at R <= 0.2 h^-1 cMpc in the CCF relative to a single power law, indicating the presence of a one-halo term.  
  > "As shown in Figure 8, both the ASPIRE measurements and the TRINITY predictions exhibit an excess in the quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function, $\xi_{\rm{QG}}$, relative to the power-law fit at $R \lesssim 0.2~h^{-1}~\mathrm{cMpc}$. This excess indicates the presence of the so-called one-halo term in HOD models" (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)
- Quasar environments at z > 6.5 are intrinsically diverse, with companion [O III] galaxy counts ranging from 0 to 20 per field (overdensities 0 to 22), challenging models that require all early luminous quasars to reside in highly overdense environments.  
  > "Figure 11 demonstrates that the number of quasar companion galaxies ranges from zero to 20, corresponding to overdensities from zero to $\delta_{\rm gal, [OIII]} \simeq 22$." (Diverse quasar environments); "Our study reveals that a substantial fraction of ASPIRE quasars do not reside in overdense regions, implying a far more diverse range of environments and host halo masses for the first quasars than traditionally assumed." (Implications for the early growth of SMBHs)
- [C II]-emitting galaxies preferentially reside in fields with strong [O III] overdensities, showing that dusty and unobscured star-forming galaxies jointly trace the same large-scale structures.  
  > "Specifically, four of the seven fields with significant [O III] overdensities ($\delta_{\rm gal,[OIII]} > 5$) contain [C II] companions, compared to only two of the seven fields with low overdensities ($\delta_{\rm gal,[OIII]} \le 1$)." (Diverse quasar environments); "This finding indicates that [O III]- and [C II]-emitting galaxies jointly map the large-scale structure surrounding high-redshift quasars, providing complementary perspectives on the physical conditions within early overdense regions." (Diverse quasar environments)
- Companion galaxies in protocluster fields appear distributed in unvirialized filamentary structures without coherent velocity gradients, but show a rapid increase in |v_los| within <= 100 kpc of the quasars, consistent with massive (> 5x10^12 M_sun) halos in simulations.  
  > "We find no clear evidence of velocity gradients or coherent motions within individual structures. This is similar to what we have seen in cosmological simulations (cite: Costa24) and suggesting that we are observing unvirialized filamentary structures in the early Universe." (Distribution and kinematics of companion galaxies); "We find that, on average, companion galaxies within a projected distance of $\lesssim 100$ kpc from the quasars exhibit a steep rise in $v_{\rm los}$ in these fields, albeit with small number statistics." (Distribution and kinematics of companion galaxies)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 25 luminous quasars (M_1450 < -25) at 6.5 < z <= 6.8 (sweet spot for NIRCam/WFSS F356W Hbeta+[O III] detection) and Dec < +30 deg to allow 23-pointing ALMA Band 6 mosaics probing [C II] emitters beyond 1 cMpc while minimizing cosmic variance.  
  > "This motivated us observing quasars at $6.5<z\lesssim6.8$, putting them at the sweet-spot of NIRCam/WFSS sensitivity (with the F356W filter) to the H$\beta$+[O III] of physically associated galaxies." (Overview); "Additionally, we only targeted quasars with declinations less than $+30^\circ$, allowing us to utilize ALMA to identify dusty galaxies that could be missed by JWST observations." (Overview); "To discover dusty galaxies in quasar environments beyond the one-megaparsec scale, we designed an ALMA mosaic program (2022.1.01077.L, PI: F. Wang), which consists of 23 Nyquist-sampled pointings in Band 6 (Figure 3)." (ALMA observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Measuring the quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function overcomes the impossibly low space density of z ~ 7 quasars for direct auto-correlation; C-2/3 ALMA configuration balances sensitivity and resolution to avoid confusion with JWST; smoothed 1D + 2D spectral search avoids hot pixels.  
  > "However, the extremely low density of $z\simeq7$ quasars precludes the possibility of measuring their auto-correlation function. A complementary approach for determining quasar host halo masses is to measure the clustering of galaxies around them." (Overview); "To balance maximizing the sensitivity and avoiding confusion when matching ALMA data with JWST, we chose to use C-2/3 configuration which delivers a resolution of 0.5$''$–1.0$''$." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "To address this issue, we instead searched for $\mathrm{S/N} > 1.2$ peaks in a smoothed S/N spectrum rather than in the raw 1D spectrum." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm)
- Alternative: NIRCam pre-imaging followed by NIRSpec/MSA spectroscopy was rejected in favor of NIRCam WFSS for unbiased emission-line selection.  
  > "We utilize the NIRCam WFSS observation mode instead of NIRCam pre-imaging followed by NIRSpec/MSA observations for the ASPIRE program to select objects with strong emission lines." (JWST observations)
- Alternative: Searching for peaks directly in unsmoothed 1D WFSS spectra (as in Wang et al. 2023) was replaced with a smoothed S/N spectrum search combined with 2D blob detection to avoid hot pixels.  
  > "(cite: Wang23) searched for peaks directly in the continuum-subtracted 1D spectra, which introduced a large number of hot pixels. To address this issue, we instead searched for $\mathrm{S/N} > 1.2$ peaks in a smoothed S/N spectrum rather than in the raw 1D spectrum." ([O III] emitter finding algorithm)
- Alternative: Fitting the CCF with a free slope gamma_QG was tested, but fixed gamma_QG = 2.0 was adopted for the main analysis due to strong degeneracy between gamma_QG and r_0^QG.  
  > "Nevertheless, there is a strong degeneracy between $\gamma_{\rm{QG}}$ and $r_0^{\rm{QG}}$ as shown in Figure 9. Therefore, we use the value from the fixed $\gamma_{\rm{QG}}$ in the following analyses, similar to previous studies (cite: Eilers24)." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Conclusions apply exclusively to extremely luminous quasars with M_1450 < -25.  
  > "Since this work considers only quasars with $M_{1450} < -25$, the conclusions drawn here are applicable exclusively to these extremely luminous objects." (Overview)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties on the CCF measurements in Table 4 include only Poisson noise and do not yet incorporate cosmic variance.  
  > "Note that the uncertainties of all $\chi(R_{\rm min}, R_{\rm max})$ measurements in Table 4 include only Poisson noise." (Quasar-galaxy cross-correlation function)
- Limitation (authors): Quasar duty cycle and lifetime estimates have large uncertainties because the halo mass function is extremely steep at the high-mass end.  
  > "Because the halo mass function is extremely steep in this regime, the corresponding number density, $n_{\rm halo, min}$, is highly sensitive to even small changes in $M_{\rm halo, min}$. As a result, estimates of the quasar duty cycle and lifetime are subject to substantial uncertainties." (Quasar host dark matter halos and duty cycles)
- Limitation (authors): The correspondence between [O III] overdensities and [C II] companions and the kinematic analysis of |v_los| within 100 kpc are limited by small number statistics, and it is uncertain whether the protoclusters will virialize into clusters by z = 0.  
  > "While the small number of fields limits the statistical significance, the relative fractions (57% versus 29%) point to a consistent trend in which [C II]-emitting galaxies preferentially reside in fields that exhibit enhanced [O III] emitter populations." (Diverse quasar environments); "However, our current measurements of $|v_{\rm los}|$ rely on a small number of galaxies, and we do not yet have evidence that these structures will be virialized. Therefore, no firm conclusion can yet be drawn about whether they will eventually evolve into massive galaxy clusters by $z=0$." (Distribution and kinematics of companion galaxies)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Slight inconsistency in the text regarding the exact count of ALMA [C II] emitters at the quasar redshifts (Abstract and Section 3.3 state 16 in LSB out of 17 total, Introduction states 14, and the end of Section 3.3 says 15, while Table 4 sums to 16 pairs).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The background number density of [C II] emitters n_G(z) is derived from only 1 emitter in the USB, leading to large Poisson uncertainty (+0.76/-0.52 dex) on the ALMA CCF normalization.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inferences of r_0^QQ and M_halo,min rely on a simplified deterministic bias relation xi_QG = sqrt(xi_QQ * xi_GG) and a step-function HOD without separately modeling the 1-halo excess seen at R < 0.2 h^-1 cMpc.
