## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce IRDIS polarimetric data to Q_phi images**: IRDAP alignment, bad pixel correction, sky background subtraction, then polarimetric differential imaging; median-combined Q_phi with stellar polarization removed, default parameters  
  > "`IRDAP` performs alignment, bad pixel correction, and sky background subtraction." (Observation and Data Reduction); "By adopting the default `IRDAP` reduction parameters, we use median-combined output $\mathcal{Q}_\phi$ images with stellar polarization removed for spiral motion analysis" (Observation and Data Reduction)
   - software: IRDAP
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Rescale images by r^2 to correct for stellar illumination**: Multiply Q_phi images by an r^2 map normalized at 0.5 arcsec  
  > "we rescaled the brightnesses of the $\mathcal{Q}_\phi$ images by multiplying them with a $r^2$ map" (Observation and Data Reduction); "to obtain the $r^2$-scaled maps (normalized at $0\hbox{$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}5$)" (Observation and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: inclination 16.7 deg from face-on, position angle 61.9 deg  
  > "assuming a $16\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}7$ inclination from face-on and a $61\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}9$ position angle for the major axis of the disk (cite: bohn22)" (Observation and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Infinitely thin disk because of the low inclination  
  > "Given the low disk inclination, we assumed an infinitely thin disk for subsequent analysis" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure spiral peak radii at each azimuth and epoch**: Transform r^2-scaled maps to polar coordinates; at each angle fit a normal distribution to the radial profile to get the peak and its error  
  > "For each angular location, which is defined as a clockwise separation from the major axis of the disk, we fit a normal distribution to obtain the peak and its associated error on the spiral radial profile." (Results)
   - parameter: 1 deg angular step  
  > "With a $1^\circ$ angular step, we present the peaks and their errors" (Results)
   - fitting: Normal (Gaussian) distribution fit per angular location; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "we fit a normal distribution to obtain the peak and its associated error on the spiral radial profile" (Results)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit spiral morphology and pattern motion across epochs**: Fit an identical polynomial to each arm at the three epochs with an angular offset per epoch via dummy variables; best-fit offset is the angular rotation between epochs; fit uncertainty propagated with true north and astrometric uncertainties  
  > "we followed Ref. (cite: ren20) to fit an identical but angularly offset polynomial" (Results); "the best-fit offset will be the angular rotation among the epochs" (Results); "the polynomial fitting uncertainty will be propagated with three epochs of $0\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}08$ SPHERE true north pointing uncertainty in Ref. (cite: maire16) and three epochs of SPHERE astrometric uncertainty of $0\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}16$." (Results)
   - parameter: p-degree polynomial in angle theta; degrees used 6, 7 and 8 (arm and fit dependent); astrometric uncertainty 0.16 deg in polar coordinates  
  > "The astrometric accuracy of SPHERE/IRDIS is $\sim$2 mas ($\sim$0.16 pixels; (cite: Zurlo2014)), thus translating into $0\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}16$ in polar coordinates." (Results)
   - fitting: Polynomial with dummy-variable epoch offsets; reduced chi-squared reported; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "we followed Ref. (cite: ren20) to fit an identical but angularly offset polynomial" (Results)
   - validation: Motion measured separately for S1, S2 and both arms combined and found consistent  
  > "motion measurements using different parts of the disk (i.e., S1, S2, and two arms combined) are consistent with each other" (Results)
5. **Derive spiral motion rates under single-driver and two-driver scenarios**: Fit the two spirals independently and simultaneously; compare rates (positive = clockwise)  
  > "On the one hand, we fit the two spirals independently." (Results); "On the other hand, we fit the two spirals simultaneously" (Results)
   - parameter: S1: 6-degree polynomial; S2: 8-degree polynomial; simultaneous fit: two 7-degree polynomials  
  > "for two $7$-degree polynomials with $\chi_{v}^{2}$ of 2.67" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: S1 and S2 rates agree within 1 sigma, taken to indicate a single driver  
  > "The spiral motions of S1 and S2 are consistent within $1\sigma$, suggesting that they are driven by a single driver." (Results)
6. **Convert rotation rate to orbital period and radius of the driver**: Circular orbit around a 1.6 Msun star at 135.0 pc  
  > "For a $1.6\pm0.1M_\odot$ star and assuming a circular orbit, the obtained $\omega = -0\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}85\pm0\hbox{$.\!\!^\circ$}05~{\rm yr}^{-1}$ rotation rate corresponds to a hidden planet at $66\pm3$ au" (Conclusions)
   - assumption: Circular orbit; stellar mass 1.6 +/- 0.1 Msun; distance 135.0 +/- 0.4 pc  
  > "The corresponding stellocentric radius is $66\pm3$ au (i.e, $0\hbox{$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}49\pm0\hbox{$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}02$) for a $1.6\pm0.1M_\odot$ star" (Implications)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compare predicted driver orbit with ALMA gap, filament and JWST candidate**: Overplot the predicted orbit on ALMA dust continuum and compare with the gap, the filament and the JWST/NIRCam candidate  
  > "The predicted driver orbit coincided with the disk gap observed in the thermal emission and a filament structure that connects two rings" (Implications); "our data suggest that the *James Webb Space Telescope* (*JWST*)/NIRCam candidate in Ref. (cite: cugno24) is not driving the two spirals at ${>}5\sigma$." (Implications)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Test the impact of disk flaring on the motion measurement**: Deproject the surface map with a flared disk model h = 0.048 d^1.15 and repeat the same measurements  
  > "we adopted a disk model of $h=0.048\times d^{1.15}$ from Ref. (cite: Andrews2011)" (Disk Flaring); "Then we deprojected the disk surface map and performed the same measurements" (Disk Flaring)
   - assumption: Scale height h = 0.048 d^1.15 with d the cylindrical distance  
  > "where $h$ represents the disk scale height and $d$ is the distance in cylindrical coordinates" (Disk Flaring)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: S1 and S2 rates remain consistent within 1.3 sigma after considering flaring  
  > "After considering the disk flaring, the spiral motions of S1 and S2 are still consistent within $1.3\sigma$." (Disk Flaring)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Same band (J) at three epochs to get the longest high-quality baseline and avoid wavelength-dependent spurious motion  
  > "To establish the longest temporal separation of high-quality data for analysis, and reduce the possibility that wavelength-dependent imaging manifests spurious spiral motion" (Observation and Data Reduction)
- Why this method: Spiral motion tracking dynamically tests spiral formation mechanisms  
  > "tracking the motion of the spirals can dynamically test leading spiral formation mechanisms" (Introduction)
- Alternative: Gravitational instability not tested again because the earlier 1-yr study ruled it out  
  > "since it has been confidently ruled out with the study on $1$-yr separation for SAO 206462 (cite: xie21)" (Results)
- Alternative: Two independent planets would sit at 69 au and 62 au, close together but not in low-order resonance, which is taken as consistent with the single-driver scenario  
  > "While the two planets are mathematically close to each other, they are not under low order mean motion resonance" (Disk Flaring)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Fits assume independent azimuthal angles; correlation among adjacent pixels is not accounted for  
  > "thus it is necessary to take into account the correlation among adjacent pixels." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): The predicted protoplanet is not detected  
  > "While this protoplanet is currently not detected" (Implications)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reduced chi-squared of 1.8 to 3.5 indicates the polynomial error model is not fully adequate, so quoted 0.05 deg/yr uncertainties may be underestimated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA data play only a qualitative comparison role; no ALMA processing, imaging or measurements are reported
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Circular-orbit and thin-disk assumptions drive the 66 au radius; eccentric orbits are not explored
