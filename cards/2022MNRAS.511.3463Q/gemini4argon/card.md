## Analysis recipe

1. **Inspect cores, extract spectra, and select hot core candidates**: Inspect line emission of the 453 compact dense cores one by one, extract spectra at the line-rich positions, and select line-rich cores with >3 transitions of COMs.  
  > "We have inspected the line emission of the 453 cores one by one and extracted spectra at the line-rich positions." (Line and hot core identifications); "In total 60 line-rich cores are considered as hot core candidates." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - parameter: More than 3 transitions of C2H5CN, CH3OCHO, or CH3OH detected  
  > "More than 3 transitions of C$_2$H$_5$CN and CH$_3$OCHO are detected in the 59 line-rich cores." (Line and hot core identifications); "We identify a dense core as a hot core if more than 3 transitions of the COMs are identified and have rotation temperature above 100 K." (Conclusions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with statistical detection of >=20 COM transitions (>3 sigma) in 54 cores by Liu et al. (2021)  
  > "The results are in good agreement with those by Liu et al. (2021) where 54 cores were found in a statistic manner to have significant detection of at least 20 COM transitions with line intensities larger than 3 $\sigma$ level." (Line and hot core identifications)
2. **Identify molecular lines and derive rotation temperatures, column densities, and relative abundances**: Solve the radiative transfer equation and generate synthetic spectra for C2H5CN, CH3OCHO, and CH3OH using XCLASS and optimize parameters with MAGIX.  
  > "Then we use the eXtended CASA Line Analysis Software Suite (XCLASS, Möller et al. 2017) for further line identification and parameter calculation." (Line and hot core identifications); "Assuming the molecular gas satisfies local thermodynamical equilibrium condition (LTE), the XCLASS solves radiative transfer equation and produces synthetic spectra for specific molecular transitions by taking source size, beam filling factor, line profile, line blending, excitation, and opacity into account." (Line and hot core identifications); "To obtain optimized rotation temperature and column density parameters, we employ Modeling and Analysis Generic Interface for eXternal numerical codes (MAGIX; Möller et al. 2013) for further calculation." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - software: XCLASS, MAGIX, CDMS, JPL
   - parameter: Source size fixed to deconvolved angular size of the continuum source (theta_source = sqrt(a*b))  
  > "In our case, we take deconvolved angular sizes of the continuum sources as source sizes which are listed in Table 1." (Line and hot core identifications); "$\theta_{\rm source}$ is the source size derived from the deconvolved continuum size on assuming $\pi\theta^{2}$=$\pi$ab, where a and b are major and minor axes of the continuum source from Gaussian fitting." (Table 1)
   - parameter: Velocity offset fixed with reference to CH3OH line at 100.6389 GHz  
  > "The velocity offsets are determined when referred to commonly detected CH$_3$OH line at 100.6389 GHz." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - parameter: Hot core criteria: T_rot > 100 K and source size < 0.1 pc  
  > "The XCLASS calculations suggested that all the 59 line-rich cores have gas temperature derived from C$_2$H$_5$CN and CH$_3$OCHO higher than 100 K." (Line and hot core identifications); "Considering the distances (Liu et al. 2021a) and source sizes, the 60 cores have source size smaller than 0.1 pc." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamical equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Assuming the molecular gas satisfies local thermodynamical equilibrium condition (LTE), the XCLASS solves radiative transfer equation and produces synthetic spectra for specific molecular transitions by taking source size, beam filling factor, line profile, line blending, excitation, and opacity into account." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - assumption: For cores with <3 CH3OH transitions detected, CH3OH gas temperature is assumed equal to T_rot of CH3OCHO (or C2H5CN if CH3OCHO is undetected)  
  > "For CH$_3$OH with less than 3 transitions detected, column densities are estimated by assuming gas temperature equal to the rotation temperatures of oxygen-bearing molecule CH$_3$OCHO, or equal to the rotation temperatures of C$_2$H$_5$CN in case that CH$_3$OCHO is not detected." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - fitting: Free parameters: rotation temperatures, column densities, and velocity widths optimized via MAGIX  
  > "Therefore we set rotation temperatures, column densities and velocity widths as free parameters to simulate the observed spectra." (Line and hot core identifications); "To obtain optimized rotation temperature and column density parameters, we employ Modeling and Analysis Generic Interface for eXternal numerical codes (MAGIX; Möller et al. 2013) for further calculation." (Line and hot core identifications)
   - validation: Visual overlay of modeled molecular spectra on observed spectra  
  > "Figure 1 presents sample spectra toward a few hot cores with modeled molecular spectra (C$_2$H$_5$CN, CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$OH) overlaid on the observed ones." (Line and hot core identifications)
3. **Map spatial distributions and identify N/O chemical differentiation and heating mechanisms**: Create line images of C2H5CN (98523 MHz), CH3OCHO (98792 MHz), CH3OH (100639 MHz), H40alpha (99023 MHz), and SiO (86847 MHz), and compare their emission peak positions with continuum and H40alpha peaks.  
  > "We choose three molecular line transitions of C$_2$H$_5$CN, CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$OH at 98523, 98792 and 100639 MHz for the images, respectively." (Line images); "Considering position accuracy of 0.3 arcsec in line images, 29 cores clearly show nitrogen and oxygen separation, i.e., the emissions of C$_2$H$_5$CN and CH$_3$OCHO peak at different positions." (Line images); "In this section, we want to differentiate internal and external heating mechanisms by comparing the relative position of line images of COMs and UC Hii regions." (Heating mechanism)
   - parameter: Position accuracy threshold of 0.3 arcsec (from 1.9 arcsec resolution and 3 sigma S/N)  
  > "Taking angular resolution of 1.9 arcsec and 3 $\sigma$ level , the position accuracy of line images due to the noise is estimated to be better than 0.3 arcsec by using formula $\Delta \theta$=0.45 $\frac{\theta_{\rm FWHM}}{\rm {S/N}}$ (Reid et al. 1988)." (Observations)
   - assumption: Hot cores adjacent to H40alpha-traced UC HII regions with CH3OH peaks offset from the continuum peak are externally heated  
  > "For the sources having H40$\alpha$ line emission, peaks of CH$_3$OH are offset from the continuum images except for IRAS 18056–1952." (Heating mechanism); "Twenty four hot cores are thought to be heated by adjacent UC Hii regions and the other sources are classified as internally heated hot cores, suggesting that some hot cores are not simply linked to HMPOs and precursors to UC H ii regions in evolutionary sequence during massive star formation (Law et al. 2021), but they only trace physical and chemical environments of hot, dense and line-rich regions." (Heating mechanism)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Statistical analysis of rotation temperatures and column densities**: Perform linear fittings between rotation temperatures and log column densities and between log column densities of different species, and apply Kolmogorov-Smirnov (K-S) tests comparing externally and internally heated hot cores.  
  > "A linear fitting to the data gives log(N(CH$_3$OH))=0.022T$_{\rm rot}$+14 with correlation coefficient of 0.86." (Rotation temperatures, column densities and abundances relative to CH$_3$OH); "The linear fittings give log(N(CH$_3$OCHO))=1.1log(N(CH$_3$OH))–2.5 and log(N(C$_2$H$_5$CN))=1.2log(N(CH$_3$OH))–5.1 with correlation coefficients of 0.95 and 0.82 for CH$_3$OCHO and C$_2$H$_5$CN, respectively." (Rotation temperatures, column densities and abundances relative to CH$_3$OH); "The rotational temperatures of CH$_3$OCHO tend to be higher in the externally heated hot cores than in the internally heated hot cores, although this difference is not very much significant with a p-value of 0.14 given by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test." (Heating mechanism)
   - fitting: Linear regression and two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test  
  > "A linear fitting to the data gives log(N(CH$_3$OH))=0.022T$_{\rm rot}$+14 with correlation coefficient of 0.86." (Rotation temperatures, column densities and abundances relative to CH$_3$OH); "The rotational temperatures of CH$_3$OCHO tend to be higher in the externally heated hot cores than in the internally heated hot cores, although this difference is not very much significant with a p-value of 0.14 given by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test."    - validation: Correlation coefficients and K-S test p-values

## Qualitative findings

- Line peak intensities of C2H5CN are larger than those of CH3OCHO in 19 cores, the opposite in 5 cores, C2H5CN is absent in 4 cores, and CH3OCHO is undetected in 9 cores.  
  > "From Figure 1 and the figures in the supplementary material, one can see that line peak intensities of C$_2$H$_5$CN are larger than those of CH$_3$OCHO in 19 cores while the opposite is seen in 5 cores." (Line and hot core identifications); "It is worth mentioning that C$_2$H$_5$CN line emission is absent in 4 cores while CH$_3$OCHO is not detected in 9 cores." (Line and hot core identifications)
- Column densities of CH3OH and CH3OCHO increase with rotation temperature and correlate tightly with each other, supporting grain surface formation.  
  > "Our large sample shows that column densities of CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$OH correlate well with their rotation temperatures, and column densities of the two molecules have a positive correlation." (Conclusions); "The results support that CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$OH originate from the grain surface chemistry." (Conclusions)
- 29 hot cores show spatial separation between C2H5CN and CH3OCHO emission peaks, but no clear differences in rotation temperature are observed due to the low upper-level energies of the 3 mm lines.  
  > "Nitrogen and oxygen differentiation is observed in 29 hot cores with nitrogen-bearing and oxygen-bearing molecules peaking at different positions, but no clear differences in temperature and column density are seen from our data." (Conclusions); "The targeted lines of ATOMS have upper level energies of C$_2$H$_5$CN and CH$_3$OCHO less than 139 K, hence the hottest components are not observed." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 3 mm ALMA observations suffer much less from line blending than submillimeter bands, and the 12-m array alone is sufficient because hot cores are compact and COM lines suffer less from missing flux.  
  > "Comparing with the submillimeter waveband, the lines at 3 mm have much less blending problem." (Introduction); "Since hot cores have smaller source size and COM lines suffer from less missing flux problem, we only use the 12 m array data for identifications of COM lines and hot cores in this work." (Observations)
- Why this method: XCLASS solves the radiative transfer equation under LTE to produce synthetic spectra taking source size, beam filling factor, line profile, line blending, excitation, and opacity into account simultaneously.  
  > "Assuming the molecular gas satisfies local thermodynamical equilibrium condition (LTE), the XCLASS solves radiative transfer equation and produces synthetic spectra for specific molecular transitions by taking source size, beam filling factor, line profile, line blending, excitation, and opacity into account." (Line and hot core identifications)
- Alternative: Gas-phase molecule-ion reactions as the dominant formation pathway for the observed CH3OCHO (rejected in favor of grain-surface synthesis because gas-phase efficiency is too low and observed T_rot < 200 K).  
  > "However, gas phase mechanisms by molecule-ion reactions have very low efficiency in producing CH$_3$OCHO (Horn et al. 2004; Geppert et al. 2006)." (Chemistry); "We then favor CH$_3$OCHO observed by us at lower frequency band is synthesized on grain surface." (Chemistry)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Targeted C2H5CN and CH3OCHO transitions at 3 mm have low upper-level energies (30-139 K and 22-58 K), so the innermost, hottest gas components of the hot cores are not sampled.  
  > "The observed C$_2$H$_5$CN and CH$_3$OCHO transitions span upper level energies of 30–139 K and 22–58 K, respectively." (Line and hot core identifications); "Therefore we do not expect very hot gas components to be detected." (Line and hot core identifications); "Probably our targeted lines of the C$_2$H$_5$CN and CH$_3$OCHO have low upper level energies which only sample the freshly evaporated species, and then can not probe the innermost and hottest structure of the hot cores." (Nitrogen and oxygen differentiation)
- Limitation (authors): A 48 MHz frequency setup difference between C43-2 and C43-3 configurations caused two CH3OH transitions not to be covered in C43-3 observations.  
  > "Note that this is a lower limit since the frequency setup of C43-2 and C43-3 configurations has a 48 MHz difference which leads to two CH$_3$OH transitions not covered in C43-3 observations." (Line and hot core identifications)
- Limitation (authors): The spatial resolution (~1.2-1.9 arcsec beam) may cover parts of both nitrogen and oxygen cores simultaneously.  
  > "Another reason is that in most cases our beam may cover part of both nitrogen and nitrogen cores." (Chemistry)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing the COM emitting source size to the 3 mm continuum deconvolved size may introduce systematic bias in column densities, especially for externally heated cores where continuum includes free-free emission or is spatially offset from the COM peak.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlation between T_rot and N for CH3OH and CH3OCHO could be partly affected by parameter degeneracy in LTE fitting when only a small number of transitions or a narrow E_u range (22-58 K for CH3OCHO) is available.
