  > "To better see the disk structure, we rotate the disk to align its major axis with the vertical (Fig. 1b)." (Main text); "The aspect ratio of the disk structure is ∼ 0.3, indicating that the disk axis is inclined by ∼ 18 [◦] to the plane of the sky" (Main text); "In order to better see the spirals, we first made an annularly averaged map of the continuum (see Figure 2b) then subtracted it from the deprojected map [9] ."    - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Note that barlike structures near the central source are likely deprojection artifacts of the unresolved central core  
  > "Near the central source, two barlike structures connect to the inner ends of the spiral structures. Since they are not clearly seen in the original map, these could be artifacts of the deprojection of the central region, which is spatially unresolved in the observation." (Main text)
3. **Extract spiral arm peak positions and fit spiral morphology and pitch angles**: Locate emission peak positions of the NE and SW arms by fitting Gaussians by eye to radial intensity cuts every 10 deg in position angle on the difference map, then fit peak positions in polar coordinates (R, theta) with logarithmic and Archimedean spiral models  
  > "We locate the emission peak positions of the spirals by fitting by eye a Gaussian to their emission intensity in the difference map cut along lines of constant position angle every 10 [◦] ." (Methods: Spiral Structures); "We fitted the points with the two spiral shapes, one logarithmic with R = R 0 exp [aθ] and the other Archimedean with R = R 0 + bθ, where b is the increase rate of the spiral radius with the angle, using the nonlinear least-squares Marquardt-Levenberg algorithm." (Main text)
   - parameter: Radial cuts every 10 deg in position angle  
  > "We locate the emission peak positions of the spirals by fitting by eye a Gaussian to their emission intensity in the difference map cut along lines of constant position angle every 10 [◦] ." (Methods: Spiral Structures)
   - assumption: Radial error bars taken as width at half maximum of the fitted Gaussians  
  > "The uncertainties in the peak positions are shown by radial error bars, marking the width at half maximum." (Methods: Spiral Structures)
   - fitting: Nonlinear least-squares Marquardt-Levenberg algorithm comparing chi^2 of logarithmic (R = R_0 exp(a*theta)) and Archimedean (R = R_0 + b*theta) models  
  > "We fitted the points with the two spiral shapes, one logarithmic with R = R 0 exp [aθ] and the other Archimedean with R = R 0 + bθ, where b is the increase rate of the spiral radius with the angle, using the nonlinear least-squares Marquardt-Levenberg algorithm." (Main text)
   - validation: Comparison of chi^2 values between logarithmic and Archimedean fits for SW and NE arms  
  > "Based on the χ [2] values, it appears that the SW arm is slightly better fitted by a logarithmic shape, while the NE arm is slightly better fitted by an Archimedean shape." (Main text)
4. **Model 343 GHz dust continuum of VLA 1 disk to derive disk mass and Toomre Q stability parameter**: Construct axisymmetric parametrized flared disk model with outer thinning, compute LTE radiative transfer integrated along lines of sight tilted by ~18 deg, convolve with telescope beam to fit observed continuum map, and integrate surface density to obtain disk mass and Toomre Q profile  
  > "We adopt a parametrized flared disk model, the same as that used to reproduce the disk emission in the well-resolved Class 0 disk in HH 212 [36] ." (Methods: Disk Model); "We then compute the dust emission assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium, and integrate along a grid of lines of sight the local emission that is attenuated by the optical depth, as done before for the HH 212 disk [36] . We then convolve the resulting map with the telescope’s beam, producing a model map to be compared with the observed map at the same resolution." (Methods: Disk Model); "We then compute the dust emission assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium, and integrate along a grid of lines of sight the local emission that is attenuated by the optical depth" (Methods, Disk Model)
   - parameter: 6 free parameters: R_t, R_o, q, T_t, p, and n_t  
  > "The model has 6 free parameters: R t, R o, q, T t, p, and n t ." (Methods: Disk Model)
   - parameter: Disk axis tilted ~18 deg from plane of sky  
  > "To compute the dust emission, we tilt the model disk so its axis is ∼ 18 [◦] from the plane of the sky, as estimated from the observed aspect ratio." (Methods: Disk Model)
   - assumption: Vertical hydrostatic equilibrium with Keplerian rotation around M_* ~ 1.5 M_sun, and outer geometric thinning between R_t and R_o  
  > "The model disk is in vertical hydrostatic equilibrium, with the number density of molecular hydrogen decreasing as a Gaussian in height above the midplane" (Methods: Disk Model); "The model disk is in vertical hydrostatic equilibrium, with the number density of molecular hydrogen decreasing as a Gaussian in height above the midplane" (Methods, Disk Model)
   - assumption: Mass density rho = 1.4 n m_H2 (helium at solar abundance) and gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100  
  > "The mass density is then ρ = 1.4nm H 2, accounting for helium at solar abundance relative to hydrogen." (Methods: Disk Model); "Here β is the dust opacity spectral index and κ ν the opacity per gram of gas and dust for a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100." (Methods: Disk Model)

  > "Supplementary Figure 2 shows our best-fit continuum map. The model has 6 free parameters: R t, R o, q, T t, p, and n t ." (Methods: Disk Model)
   - validation: Visual comparison of model and observed contours and inspection of residual map and optical depth profile along the major axis  
  > "However, since the residuals are reasonably small (Supplementary Figure 2b), the model adequately describes the radial structure." (Methods: Disk Model)

## Qualitative findings

- A pair of symmetric, trailing m=2 spiral arms (NE and SW) is detected in the 343 GHz dust continuum of the embedded Class I protostellar disk HH 111 VLA 1, extending from ~40 au to ~160 au.  
  > "Figure 2c shows the difference map, which clearly shows a pair of symmetric spiral structures labeled “NE” and “SW” extending from within ∼ 0 [′′] . 1 (40 au) of the central source to the outer edge of the disk." (Main text); "The spiral structures trail the rotation of the disk measured in molecular lines [5] ." (Main text)
- The companion source HH 111 VLA 2 is spatially resolved for the first time into a ~25.6 au disklike structure nearly orthogonal to the VLA 1 disk and roughly perpendicular to the HH 121 jet.  
  > "The emission around the VLA 2 source is spatially resolved for the first time, appearing as a disklike structure, with a Gaussian deconvolved size (diameter) of 0 [′′] . 064 (25.6 au) along the major axis at position angle 118.3 [◦] ." (Main text); "Note that the VLA 2 disklike structure is nearly orthogonal to the VLA 1 disk, indicating the two components of this wide protobinary system are strongly misaligned." (Main text)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combined high-resolution Cycle 5 C43-8 observations (0''.04 resolution) with shorter-baseline Cycle 3 observations to resolve spiral substructure while recovering scales up to ~1''.4 without missing flux.  
  > "In order to restore a larger size scale, we combined these observations with our previous Cycle 3 observations [23], which had the same correlator setup but a larger MRS of ∼ 1 [′′] . 4. Thus, there is no noticeable missing flux in our maps of the disks around the two VLA sources, which have a size ∼< 1 [′′] ." (Methods: Observations)
- Why this method: Subtracted an annularly averaged map from the deprojected continuum map to reveal faint (5-20% contrast) spiral arms on top of the bright axisymmetric disk profile, and added outer geometric thinning to the hydrostatic disk model to match the observed thinning at the outer disk edge.  
  > "In order to better see the spirals, we first made an annularly averaged map of the continuum (see Figure 2b) then subtracted it from the deprojected map [9] ." (Main text); "With this scale height, increasing monotonically in radius, we failed to fit the observed thinning of the disk near its outer edge. Thus, as in HH 212, we assume the disk to be geometrically thinner near its outer edge." (Methods: Disk Model)
- Alternative: Spiral arms driven by an internal planet/companion at ~40 au, an external companion (VLA 2 at ~1200 au projected separation), or a putative ~1 M_sun companion at ~186 au inferred from jet wiggling.  
  > "The spirals in the VLA 1 disk could instead be raised by a planet or companion. Our fit of the Archimedean form to the spirals indicates the pitch angle increases inwards to ∼ 40 au (0 [′′] . 1), where we would thus expect the planet or companion to be located and to clear a gap in the disk." (Main text); "One possibility is VLA 2, but we believe this is not likely to be the driver because the disk of VLA 1 is highly inclined with respect to the plane of the sky, being nearly edge-on." (Main text); "Another possibility is a putative stellar companion of ∼ 1M ⊙, inferred to lie at a distance of ∼ 186 au from VLA 1 based on the wiggling of the HH 111 jet and counterjet [35] . However, such a massive young star should have a disk detectable with our sensitive ALMA continuum observation if it is at an evolutionary stage similar to that of VLA 1, yet nothing is seen." (Main text)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Inner barlike structures connecting to the spiral arms may be artifacts from deprojecting the spatially unresolved central core.  
  > "Near the central source, two barlike structures connect to the inner ends of the spiral structures. Since they are not clearly seen in the original map, these could be artifacts of the deprojection of the central region, which is spatially unresolved in the observation." (Main text)
- Limitation (authors): Current angular resolution and sensitivity cannot definitively distinguish between logarithmic and Archimedean spiral shapes or detect a narrow planet-carved gap in the massive disk.  
  > "Since neither shape fits both arms best, higher-resolution and deeper observations are needed to better distinguish these two possibilities." (Main text); "However, since the disk seems to be too massive for a planet to open a gap detectable at the current resolution [34], observations at higher resolution are needed to search for a gap and check for this scenario." (Main text)
- Limitation (authors): The dust opacity spectral index beta is uncertain, which affects the derived disk mass and Toomre Q parameter, and the radiative transfer model is axisymmetric and neglects self-gravity in the vertical scale height.  
  > "Since the dust opacity is uncertain, further observations are needed to confirm the instability and check whether the inner part is also gravitationally unstable." (Methods: Disk Model); "However, in our model, the Q parameter is of order unity or larger, and the effect of self-gravity on the scale height is moderate (at most a factor of 2)." (Methods: Disk Model)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deprojecting a highly inclined (axis inclined ~18 deg to plane of sky, i.e., ~72 deg inclination) flared disk with finite vertical thickness (h/R ~ 0.17) using a simple 2D planar stretch can introduce azimuthal projection distortions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spiral peak positions were measured by fitting Gaussians 'by eye' along radial cuts, which introduces subjective uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The disk is partly optically thick at 343 GHz along the major axis, making surface density and Toomre Q estimates sensitive to the assumed temperature structure and dust opacity.
