## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure multi-band ALMA dust continuum flux and noise**: Convolve all four continuum images with a fixed resolution element of 3.4" x 2.3", integrate total flux within one FWHM of the resolution element at the image center, and estimate RMS by averaging RMS measurements within one beam at multiple locations near the center.  
  > "To make a consistent measurement of the continuum flux over the four bands, we convolved all the continuum images with a fixed resolution element of $3.4^{\prime\prime}\times 2.3^{\prime\prime}$." (Methods); "We integrated total flux within one FWHM of the resolution element in the re-convolved images." (Methods); "To estimate the noise level, we measured the root mean square (RMS) within one beam at multiple locations that are close to the center for each band, using the CASA viewer $statistic$ task." (Methods)
   - software: CASA, CASA viewer statistic
   - parameter: Convolved beam FWHM = 3.4" x 2.3"  
  > "we convolved all the continuum images with a fixed resolution element of $3.4^{\prime\prime}\times 2.3^{\prime\prime}$." (Methods)
   - assumption: Nucleus thermal contribution is negligible (at most 3.73, 1.65, 0.64, and 0.36 uJy in Bands 7, 6, 4, 3 for r_n <= 0.4 km and Bond albedo 0.04)  
  > "The contributions of the nucleus to the fluxes at Bands 7, 6, 4 and 3 are at most ${\sim\,}$3.73, 1.65, 0.64 and 0.36 $\,\mu{\rm Jy}$, respectively, for a nucleus upper-limit radius of ${\sim\,}$0.4$\,{\rm km}$ (ref.(cite: Hui2020)) and a Bond albedo of 0.04" (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Model dust thermal SED and derive dust particle size, porosity, mass, and mass loss rate**: Use Mie theory combined with Bruggeman effective medium theory to compute absorption cross sections C_abs(a, lambda), solve radiative equilibrium for grain temperature T(a, r_h), compute model flux density for single-sized grains and Divine-type size distributions, and derive mass loss rate from beam crossing time.  
  > "We use Mie theory combined with the Bruggman effective medium theory (cite: Bohren:1983) to calculate $C_{\rm abs}(a,\lambda)$, the absorption cross sections of spherical porous aggregates of radii $a$ at wavelength $\lambda$." (Methods); "We have also modeled the SED in terms of the Divine-type cometary size distribution (cite: Divine1986) $dn/da\propto \left(1-a_{\rm min}/a\right)^{\gamma}\times\left(a_{\rm min}/a\right)^{\alpha}$ in the size range of $a_{\rm min}< a <a_{\rm max}$." (Methods); "Adopting a dust velocity of 3$\,{\rm m}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ for mm-sized particles around perihelion (ref.(cite: Cremonese2020)) and an effective beam size of 2.8$^{\prime\prime}$ (projected distance of $4.12\times10^3\,{\rm km}$), we obtained a beam crossing time $\tau$ ${\sim\,}1.36\times10^{6}\,{\rm s}$, in turn, a dust mass loss rate $Q({\rm dust})\sim200\,{\rm kg}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
   - parameter: Divine-type distribution: alpha = 3.5, a_min = 1 um, a_max = 1 cm, peak a_p = 1 mm (area-weighted peak a_p' = 2.3 mm)  
  > "As illustrated in Fig. [2](#fig:sedmod)b, with $\alpha=3.5$ (see ref.(cite: Lasue2009)), $a_{\rm min}=1\,{\rm \mu m}$ and $a_{\rm max}=1\,{\rm cm}$, compact, spherical dust particles with a Divine-type size distribution peaking at $a_p=1\,{\rm mm}$ and a total dust mass of $M_{\rm dust}\approx2.57\times10^{8}\,{\rm kg}$ could reproduce the ALMA photometry fairly well." (Methods)
   - parameter: Single effective dust size ~ 5 mm  
  > "Assuming an effective dust size of ${\sim\,}$5$\,{\rm mm}$, we obtained a total dust mass loss of $\approx2.81\times10^{8}\,{\rm kg}$ within the ALMA beam (see Fig. [2](#fig:sedmod))." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
   - assumption: Spherical grains; mass mixing ratio m_carb/m_sil = 0.5; compact dust density rho_CD = 2650 kg m^-3 (or porous P = 0.80 with rho_CD = 530 kg m^-3); dust velocity v_d = 3 m s^-1; nucleus bulk density = 500 kg m^-3 and radius r_n = 0.4 km.  
  > "We adopt $m_{\rm carb}/m_{\rm sil}$ = 0.5, as implied from the mass spectrometry of the dust particles from 67P" (Methods); "(for compact dust, $\rho_{\rm CD}\approx2,650 \,{\rm kg}\,{\rm m}^{-3}$; for porous dust of porosity $P=0.80$, $\rho_{\rm CD}\approx530\,{\rm kg}\,{\rm m}^{-3}$; see ref.(cite: Li2003))." (Methods); "Adopting a typical bulk density of 500$\,{\rm kg}\,{\rm m}^{-3}$ for the nucleus (cite: Patzold2019) and assuming uniform erosion of the nucleus surface (with $r_n=0.4\,{\rm km}$ for the nucleus radius; see ref.(cite: Hui2020))" (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
   - fitting: Varying cutoff sizes (a_min, a_max), power exponents (alpha, gamma), grain size a, porosity P, and total dust mass M_dust to reproduce ALMA photometry  
  > "By varying the lower and upper cutoff sizes ($a_{\rm min}$, $a_{\rm max}$) as well as the power exponents ($\alpha$, $\gamma$), close fits to the observed SED are achieved if the dust particles are predominantly mm-sized (or larger)." (Methods)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to composition (pure silicate vs pure amorphous carbon) and compared single-size vs Divine-type size distributions  
  > "Indeed, our calculations show that both pure silicate particles and pure amorphous carbon particles can reproduce the ALMA observations, provided that they are mm-sized (or larger) pebbles." (Methods)
3. **Extract and model CO J=3-2 and J=2-1 emission to derive CO production rate, temperature, and CO/H2O ratio**: Extract CO spectra from emission peak positions, model simultaneously with a spherically symmetric LTE coma radiative transfer model subject to solar photolysis, account for interferometric spatial filtering via CASA simobserve, and combine with literature H2O production rate.  
  > "The CO observations were analyzed using a spherically symmetric coma radiative transfer model(cite: Cordiner2020), expanding with a constant velocity, and subject to photolysis by solar radiation." (Methods); "Spatial filtering of the model coma images by the interferometer was accounted for using the CASA `simobserve` task, with the same hour angle, observation duration and array configuration as the science observations." (Methods); "Spectra of the two CO lines (extracted from their respective emission peak positions) were modelled simultaneously, allowing the temperature ($T$) and production rate ($Q$) to vary as free parameters until the best fit to the observed spectra was obtained (as determined using the reduced chi-squared statistic)." (Methods)
   - software: CASA, simobserve
   - parameter: Same hour angle, observation duration, and C43-2 array configuration in simobserve  
  > "Spatial filtering of the model coma images by the interferometer was accounted for using the CASA `simobserve` task, with the same hour angle, observation duration and array configuration as the science observations." (Methods)
   - assumption: Spherically symmetric coma in LTE expanding at constant outflow velocity of 0.47 km s^-1; H2O production rate Q(H2O) = (10.7 +/- 1.2) x 10^26 s^-1  
  > "so we adopted an outflow velocity of 0.47$\,{\rm km}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$, derived from spectrally resolved CO observations by Cordiner et al.(cite: Cordiner2020) on 15–16 December 2019." (Methods); "Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) was found to be applicable due to the relatively small ALMA beam size combined with the long lifetime of CO with respect to radiative transitions (cite: Cordiner2020)." (Methods)
   - fitting: Reduced chi-squared minimization with free parameters temperature T and production rate Q(CO)  
  > "allowing the temperature ($T$) and production rate ($Q$) to vary as free parameters until the best fit to the observed spectra was obtained (as determined using the reduced chi-squared statistic)." (Methods)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure optical coma reflectivity slope, Af rho, and optical dust mass loss rates from VLT/FORS2**: Process FORS2 images in ESO MIDAS, calibrate photometry against Pan-STARRS and SDSS stars, measure magnitudes in a 5" radius circular aperture, convert R magnitudes to Af rho, and compute order-of-magnitude Q(dust).  
  > "The data were processed in the standard way using ESO MIDAS software, as described in ref. (cite: Hainaut2019)." (Methods); "The magnitudes measured using a $5^{\prime\prime}$ radius aperture are reported in Supplementary Table 2. The $R$ magnitudes are converted into $Af\rho$" (Methods); "An order-of-magnitude dust mass loss rate $Q({\rm dust})$ could be derived from $Af\rho$ (see ref.(cite: Cremonese2020))" (Methods)
   - software: ESO MIDAS
   - parameter: Aperture radius = 5"  
  > "The magnitudes measured using a $5^{\prime\prime}$ radius aperture are reported in Supplementary Table 2." (Methods)
   - assumption: rho_d = 2650 kg m^-3, v_d = 3 m s^-1, mean dust size <a> = 1 mm, geometrical albedo p_V = 0.04  
  > "By taking $\rho_d=\rho_{\rm CD}\approx2,650\,{\rm kg}\,{\rm m}^{-3}$, $v_d=3\,{\rm m}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$, $\langle a\rangle=1\,{\rm mm}$, and $p_V=0.04$ (ref. (cite: Cremonese2020; Kim2020)), we derived the dust mass loss rates and listed them in Supplementary Table 2." (Methods)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The coma of 2I/Borisov is dominated by compact, pebble-sized dust particles (a >= 1 mm) that emit like a blackbody at ALMA wavelengths, rather than small (< 1 mm) or porous particles.  
  > "We found that very large, “pebble”-like *compact* particles of radii $a\lower.5ex\hbox{$\buildrel > \over \sim$}1\,{\rm mm}$ are required to reproduce the ALMA photometry." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov); "smaller particles with $a<1\,{\rm mm}$ or larger *porous* particles with $a>1\,{\rm mm}$ would emit too little at $\lambda\lower.5ex\hbox{$\buildrel > \over \sim$}1300 \,{\rm \mu m}$ to account for the ALMA photometry." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- The high dust-to-gas ratio (>= 3) is consistent with 2I/Borisov forming via gravitational collapse of a pebble cloud.  
  > "The relatively high dust-to-gas ratio of 2I/Borisov is consistent with the scenario of its formation in a collapsing pebble cloud." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- The optical color and coma profile of 2I/Borisov showed no temporal or spatial evolution, indicating no significant dust fragmentation or ice sublimation from grains in the coma.  
  > "We found that the dust color and the dust coma profile showed no sign of evolution with time." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov); "The absence of temporal and spatial color variations indicates that no significant dust fragmentation and/or sublimation from grains occurred in the coma of 2I/Borisov" (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- CO sublimation is decoupled from H2O production, with CO/H2O increasing from ~30% pre-perihelion to ~160% post-perihelion, reflecting nucleus chemical heterogeneity and radial mixing in its native disk rather than exposure of fresh subsurface layers.  
  > "The abundance of CO with respect to H$_2$O increased from about 30% (pre-perihelion) to nearly 160% (post-perihelion), making 2I/Borisov the most CO-rich comet observed within 3$\,{\rm au}$ of the Sun" (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov); "Instead, the large variation in the CO/H$_2$O ratio reflects a heterogeneity of the cometary nucleus, most likely with components formed at different locations beyond the volatile snowline, with differing chemical abundances." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-band ALMA submm/mm continuum observations directly sample thermal emission from large mm-sized coma dust particles that carry the bulk of the dust mass, complemented by CO line observations and VLT/FORS2 optical monitoring.  
  > "It is well recognized that the bulk of the particulate coma mass is typically carried by large dust particles and optical observations preferentially sample the smaller particles" (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- Why this method: Convolved all four ALMA bands to a common 3.4" x 2.3" beam and measured flux only within one beam at the center because extended structures had insufficient SNR and native beam sizes varied widely across frequency.  
  > "However, due to the limited signal-to-noise ratio of these structures, we excluded them from further analysis and only measured the flux at the center of the image within one ALMA beam, where the strongest signal was detected." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov); "To make a consistent measurement of the continuum flux over the four bands, we convolved all the continuum images with a fixed resolution element of $3.4^{\prime\prime}\times 2.3^{\prime\prime}$." (Methods)
- Alternative: Small particles (a < 1 mm) or large porous particles (P = 0.80, a > 1 mm) were tested and rejected because they emit too little at lambda >= 1300 um to fit the ALMA SED.  
  > "As shown in Fig. [2](#fig:sedmod), smaller particles with $a<1\,{\rm mm}$ or larger *porous* particles with $a>1\,{\rm mm}$ would emit too little at $\lambda\lower.5ex\hbox{$\buildrel > \over \sim$}1300 \,{\rm \mu m}$ to account for the ALMA photometry." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- Alternative: Pure silicate and pure amorphous carbon compositions were considered as alternatives to the mixed composition and found to also reproduce the observations if grains are mm-sized pebbles.  
  > "Indeed, our calculations show that both pure silicate particles and pure amorphous carbon particles can reproduce the ALMA observations, provided that they are mm-sized (or larger) pebbles." (Methods)
- Alternative: Discrete dipole approximation (DDA) for irregular grains was rejected because it is limited to grain sizes not much larger than the wavelength.  
  > "The discrete dipole approximation, a powerful method for modeling the interaction between light and irregular particles, is limited to dust of sizes not much larger than the incident wavelength (cite: Draine1994)." (Methods)
- Alternative: Subsurface fresh material exposure via erosion was considered to explain the post-perihelion CO increase, but rejected because water and other volatiles dropped steeply post-perihelion.  
  > "However, unveiling sub-surface fresh materials is not compatible with the steep decline in the post-perihelion water production rate." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Extended dust structures near the center in Bands 4 and 6 had too low SNR to be confirmed or included in the analysis.  
  > "However, due to the low signal-to-noise ratio of these features, we can not confirm the extended features are real features." (Methods)
- Limitation (authors): Larger pebbles (a >= 1 cm or dm-sized) emit less effectively at ALMA wavelengths and are not well constrained, making the dust mass loss rate and dust-to-gas ratio lower limits.  
  > "Larger pebbles (e.g., $a\lower.5ex\hbox{$\buildrel > \over \sim$}1\,{\rm cm}$) emit less effectively at the ALMA bands and cannot be well constrained, therefore, our dust mass loss estimate is considered as a lower limit (see discussion in Methods)." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov)
- Limitation (authors): CO rotational temperature is poorly constrained by the two transitions (1-sigma range 13-181 K), and spectral resolution was insufficient to measure the CO outflow velocity directly.  
  > "However, the temperature is not well constrained by the observations, with a ($1\sigma$) error range of ${\sim\,}$13–181$\,{\rm K}$." (Pebbles and volatile evolution in the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov); "Our data were of insufficient spectral resolution to determine the gas radial velocity based on the observed emission line profiles" (Methods)
- Limitation (authors): Dust particles were assumed to be spherical because no accurate light-scattering solution exists for mm-sized irregular particles.  
  > "Unfortunately, there is no accurate solution to the absorption and scattering of light by mm-sized, irregular particles." (Methods)
- Limitation (authors): Optical Af rho-based dust mass loss rates are only order-of-magnitude estimates because optical data preferentially probe micron-sized dust.  
  > "We note that these are just order-of-magnitude estimates since the optical observations typically probe small, micron-sized dust more effectively than mm-sized dust" (Methods)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA continuum detections have modest significance (3.8 sigma in Band 7, 2.9 sigma in Band 6, 3.0 sigma in Band 4, and 1.3 sigma non-detection in Band 3), so spectral index constraints rely on low-SNR photometry.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The native Band 3 beam (4.05" x 2.83") is larger than the stated common resolution element (3.4" x 2.3"), creating ambiguity about whether the 3.4" x 2.3" kernel was convolved onto the native images (yielding a larger final beam) or if target-resolution smoothing was attempted.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The dust mass loss rate depends directly on the assumed dust outflow velocity (3 m/s) and effective particle size, neither of which is directly measured by ALMA.
