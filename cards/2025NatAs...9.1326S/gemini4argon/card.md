- Question: What is the chemical composition and gas-phase C/O ratio in the inner and outer regions of a protoplanetary disk (d203-504) exposed to strong external UV radiation from nearby massive stars?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive disk size, thickness, and inclination from VLT/MUSE [C I] emission**: Fit a 2D Gaussian to the [C I] (8727 A) emission line map and compute inclination lower limit from the semi-major and semi-minor axes  
  > "We thus fit a 2D Gaussian to this emission and obtain a disk radius _r_ _d_ _∼_ 31 au (0.08 _′′_ at 390 pc), corresponding to the semi-major axis divided by two, and a disk thickness _E_ d _∼_ 46 au (0.12 _′′_ ), corresponding to the semi-minor axis." (MUSE observations of d203-504)
   - assumption: Distance D = 390 pc  
  > "obtain a disk radius _r_ _d_ _∼_ 31 au (0.08 _′′_ at 390 pc)" (MUSE observations of d203-504)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit  
  > "We thus fit a 2D Gaussian to this emission" (MUSE observations of d203-504)
   - validation: Consistent with jet tilt angle >= 45 deg from [38]  
  > "This estimation is in agreement with the jet being tilted by at least 45 _◦_ [38]." (MUSE observations of d203-504)
2. **Measure ALMA 344 GHz continuum flux and derive disk mass**: Extract 344 GHz continuum flux using a 2D Gaussian fit and convert to disk mass range following [77] and [76]  
  > "We extract a flux for d203-504 _F_ 344 = 7 _._ 4 mJy using a 2D Gaussian fit." (ALMA observations of d203-504); "Following the method presented in [77] (see also [76]) we derive a disk mass range of 1.3–5.6 M Jup using this flux." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
   - assumption: 344 GHz emission is dominated by thermal dust rather than free-free emission; range of dust grain opacity kappa_nu and optical depth as in [76]  
  > "This range of values stems from the uncertainties on the value of the _κ_ _ν_ parameter, the dust grain opacity, and the optical depth of the emission at 344 GHz." (ALMA observations of d203-504); "Thus the ALMA 344 GHz emission must be largely dominated by the thermal dust rather than free-free emission." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit  
  > "We extract a flux for d203-504 _F_ 344 = 7 _._ 4 mJy using a 2D Gaussian fit." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
   - validation: Compared peak intensity (~3.0 mJy at disk position) with VLA 6.1 GHz peak intensity (0.1886 mJy) to rule out free-free domination  
  > "However, the 344 GHz ALMA data has a peak intensity of _∼_ 3 _._ 0 mJy and is peaked at the disk position, not at the ionization front." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
3. **Extract background-subtracted JWST NIRSpec+MIRI spectrum and measure emission line intensities**: Extract ON spectrum in 0.40 arcsec radius circular aperture on d203-504, subtract OFF background spectrum from 0.365 arcsec aperture, and fit Gaussian + linear continuum to atomic and H2 emission lines  
  > "To extract the d203-504 spectrum, we first take a circular aperture of 0.40" (Methods, JWST observations and data reduction); "Each detected emission line is fitted using a Gaussian with a FWHM corresponding to the instrument PSF plus a linear continuum." (Details on line identification.)
   - software: JWST pipeline
   - parameter: ON aperture radius = 0.40 arcsec; OFF aperture radius = 0.365 arcsec at RA=5:35:20.370, DEC=-05:25:04.97  
  > "we extract a spectrum of similar aperture (0.365 _′′_ ) at coordinates RA=5:35:20.370 and DEC=-05:25:04.97 where no obvious object can be detected" (JWST observations and data reduction.)
   - fitting: Gaussian with FWHM fixed to instrument PSF plus linear continuum  
  > "Each detected emission line is fitted using a Gaussian with a FWHM corresponding to the instrument PSF plus a linear continuum." (Details on line identification.)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Model stellar, dust, and PAH continuum and derive accretion rate and snowline radius**: Fit multi-component blackbody continuum (stellar, inner NIR dust, outer MIR dust) plus PAH bands (using PAHTAT), integrate NIR luminosity to compute accretion rate, and extrapolate radial dust temperature profile to 150 K to locate water snowline  
  > "We model the continuum flux of d203-504 observed in the NIRSpec and MIRI ranges using the sum of the emissions from the star _F_ _⋆_, near-infrared emission from the inner disk, _F_ NIR, and mid-infrared emission from the outer disk, _F_ MIR :" (Stellar, dust and PAH continuum emission); "Assuming a radial temperature profile for the dust, _T_ _d_ ( _r_ ) = _T_ NIR ( _r/r_ out ) _−_ 3 _/_ 4 [54], and a water freeze-out temperature of 150 K implies that the water snowline is located at _r_ snow = 0 _._ 73 au." (Results)
   - software: PAHTAT
   - parameter: r_in = 0.037 au, tau_1um = 4.8, tau_3um = 0.25  
  > "This yields _τ_ 1 _µm_ = 4 _._ 8 and _τ_ 3 _µm_ = 0 _._ 25." (Stellar, dust and PAH continuum emission)
   - assumption: T_star = 4160 K, A_V = 3, R_V = 5.5 extinction curve from [80], water freeze-out temperature = 150 K, radial dust temperature profile T_d(r) = T_NIR (r/r_out)^(-3/4)  
  > "The values of _τ_ 1 _µm_ and _τ_ 3 _µm_ are computed using the extinction curve from [80] with Rv = 5.5, for a visual line of sight extinction towards the star _A_ _V_ = 3 as derived from [40] for d203-504." (Stellar, dust and PAH continuum emission); "Assuming a radial temperature profile for the dust, _T_ _d_ ( _r_ ) = _T_ NIR ( _r/r_ out ) _−_ 3 _/_ 4 [54], and a water freeze-out temperature of 150 K implies that the water snowline is located at _r_ snow = 0 _._ 73 au." (Results)
   - fitting: Continuum and PAH decomposition using PAHTAT  
  > "We fit both continua in these spectra using a collection of black bodies with the PAHTAT [79] tool." (d203-504 disk PAH spectrum)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive carbon fraction locked in PAHs**: Measure 8.6 um PAH intensity from continuum-subtracted PAH spectrum and apply Eq. 5 following [81]  
  > "Using the extracted PAH spectrum _F_ PAH ( _λ_ ), we derive an intensity _I_ 8 _._ 6 = _F_ PAH (8 _._ 6 _µm_ ) = 862 MJy _/_ sr. Using _G_ 0 = 8 _×_ 10 4 and _N_ H = 5 _._ 5 _×_ 10 21 cm _−_ 2 as in [81], we compute a value _f_ _C_ PAH = 2 _._ 9 _×_ 10 _−_ 3 ." (Carbon fraction locked in PAHs)
   - software: PAHTAT
   - parameter: I_8.6 = 862 MJy/sr  
  > "Using the extracted PAH spectrum _F_ PAH ( _λ_ ), we derive an intensity _I_ 8 _._ 6 = _F_ PAH (8 _._ 6 _µm_ ) = 862 MJy _/_ sr." (Carbon fraction locked in PAHs)
   - assumption: [C/H] = 1.6e-4, epsilon_8.6 = 4.5e-18 MJy/sr/cm^-2/G0, G0 = 8e4, N_H = 5.5e21 cm^-2  
  > "where _f_ _C_ _P AH_ is the fraction of elemental carbon locked in PAHs, _N_ H is the column density of hydrogen atoms in the line of sight, [ [_H_ _C_ ] ] is the abundance of carbon relative to hydrogen atoms (1 _._ 6 _×_ 10 _−_ 4 ), and _ϵ_ 8 _._ 6 is the PAH emissivity at 8.6 µm, with _ϵ_ 8 _._ 6 = 4 _._ 5 _×_ 10 _−_ 18 MJy/sr/cm _−_ 2 / _G_ 0 [81]." (Carbon fraction locked in PAHs)
   - fitting: Direct algebraic calculation from Eq. 5  
  > "Using _G_ 0 = 8 _×_ 10 4 and _N_ H = 5 _._ 5 _×_ 10 21 cm _−_ 2 as in [81], we compute a value _f_ _C_ PAH = 2 _._ 9 _×_ 10 _−_ 3 ." (Carbon fraction locked in PAHs)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit H2 rovibrational lines with Meudon PDR code and compute photoevaporation mass-loss rate**: Fit 17 detected H2 line intensities against a grid of constant-density Meudon PDR models, then compute photoevaporation mass-loss rate across an ellipsoidal surface area  
  > "In order to determine the physical conditions of the H 2 emitting gas, we use the Meudon PDR code [87] to fit these intensities." (Fit of rovibrational H 2 lines with the Meudon code); "Following [76], we compute the photoevaporation mass-loss rate using _M_ PE = _S_ PE _µ_ _m_ H _n_ H _c_ _s_" (Photoevaporation mass loss rate)
   - software: Meudon PDR code
   - parameter: Grid ranges: G0 in [2e4, 8e4], geometrical scaling factor alpha in [0.1, 10], gas density n_H in [1e1, 1e10] cm^-3  
  > "We restrict our search of a best fitting model to _G_ 0 values between 2 _×_ 10 4 and 8 _×_ 10 4 to account for the previous constraint provided by [38], and to values of the geometrical scaling factor alpha between 0.1 and 10, as more extreme values would correspond to unlikely geometries. The range of gas densities explored goes from 1 _×_ 10 1 to 1 _×_ 10 10 cm-3." (Fit of rovibrational H 2 lines with the Meudon code)
   - assumption: R_V = 5.5, N_H/E(B-V) = 1.6e22 cm^-2, HD38087 extinction curve [89], power-law grain size distribution 3 nm to 0.3 um, optically thin H2 lines, mean molecular weight mu = 1.4, ellipsoidal emitting surface area S_PE  
  > "with extinction properties typical of dense molecular gas as used in [27] for the Orion Bar (Rv = 5.5, NH/E(B-V) = 1.6 _×_ 10 22 cm _−_ 2 ), an extinction curve using the fit of [89] for HD38087, and a power-law grain size distribution extending from 3 nm to 0.3 microns" (Fit of rovibrational H 2 lines with the Meudon code); "where _k_ B is the Boltzmann constant, _m_ H is the mass of hydrogen and _µ_ is the ratio of total mass over hydrogen mass of interstellar gas ( _µ_ = 1 _._ 4 [80])." (Photoevaporation mass loss rate)
   - fitting: Grid minimization assuming 50% total uncertainty on H2 line intensities  
  > "Error bars represent a total uncertainty of 50% used in the minimization to obtain the best fit model." (Figure 2 caption)
   - validation: Compared derived photoevaporation rate with earlier indirect estimates under photoionization equilibrium  
  > "and a photoevaporation mass-loss rate _M_ PE = 7 _._ 9 _×_ 10 _−_ 7 M _⊙_ yr _−_ 1, slightly higher than earlier indirect estimates under the assumption of photoionization equilibrium" (Results)
7. **Model H2O, CO, and CH3+ absorption and emission with LTE slab models and derive inner-disk C/O ratio**: Fit LTE slab models on a (T_ex, N) grid to NIRSpec H2O absorption (2.45-2.91 um), CO emission and absorption (4.38-5.02 um), and MIRI CH3+ emission (7.0-7.35 um), and compute inner-disk C/O = N_CO / (N_CO + N_H2O)  
  > "We then model the absorption in the _v_ = 1 _→_ 0 and _v_ = 2 _→_ 1 vibrational bands of CO, as well as the _v_ = 1 _→_ 0, _ν_ 1, and _ν_ 2 bands of H 2 O, observed against the bright infrared continuum, using a simple slab model at local thermodynamical equilibrium (see Methods)." (Results); "Therefore, we can derive an estimate of the C/O ratio in the inner disk using C/O= _N_ CO _/_ ( _N_ CO + _N_ H 2 O ) = 0 _._ 47 _±_ 0 _._ 07" (Results)
   - software: pgopher
   - parameter: Instrumental line spread function FWHM ~ 120 km/s; spectral ranges: H2O abs 2.45-2.91 um, CO abs 4.38-5.02 um, CH3+ em 7.0-7.35 um  
  > "(derived using a Gaussian fit on narrow emission lines, which typically gives a FWHM of _≈_ 120 km s _−_ 1 .)" (Analytical models); "For CO absorption, the model is fit in the NIRSpec range (4.38 _−_ 5.02 µm)." (Fitting procedure); "For H 2 O absorption, the model is fit in the NIRSpec range (2.45 _−_ 2.91 µm)." (Fitting procedure)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), optically thin slab, emitting surface S = pi * r_d^2 for CH3+ and CO emission, absorption occurs only against stellar + inner-disk NIR continuum (F_star + F_NIR), CO and H2O are the main carriers of gas-phase C and O in the inner disk  
  > "Thus, for the emissions, the column densities are computed assuming a realistic surface." (Analytical models); "Thus, we use _S_ = _πr_ d 2 ." (Analytical models); "The absence of other spectral signatures in absorption, indicates these molecules are likely the main carriers of carbon and oxygen." (Results)
   - fitting: Grid search over T_ex and N minimizing root-mean-square residual R; uncertainties defined at 10% above minimum residual  
  > "The minimum of the residuals provides the best fit model. The uncertainties on _T_ ex and _N_ correspond the values for these parameters found 10% above the minimum residual." (Fitting procedure)
   - validation: Tested water absorption predictions in the MIRI nu2 band near 6.7 um comparing absorption against F_star + F_NIR only (case 1) versus full continuum including F_MIR (case 2)  
  > "It can be seen in this figure that the model for the case 2 produces absorption features which are much stronger than observed, while case 1 agrees better with observations. This provides an additional confirmation that water absorption originates from the the inner disk." (Modeling of water absorption in the MIRI range)

## Qualitative findings

- H2O (v=1->0, nu1, nu2 between 2.5 and 2.9 um) and CO (v=1->0, v=2->1 around 4.4-5 um) are detected in absorption from the hot inner disk (r <= 0.73 au), while HCN, C2H2, and CO2 are not detected.  
  > "We also detect spectroscopic signatures from several molecules, with NIRSpec: H 2 O _v_ = 1 _→_ 0 _, ν_ 1 _, ν_ 2 symmetrical and asymmetrical stretching, in absorption, between 2.5 and 2.9 µm" (Results); "Finally, we note that HCN, C 2 H 2 or CO 2 emission signatures that are commonly detected in isolated disks are not seen in d203-504." (Results)
- d203-504 exhibits two distinct chemical zones: an inner disk (r <= 1 au) shielded from external UV radiation with a Solar C/O ratio, and an extended outer disk surface layer (r >= 1 au) hosting an active FUV-driven PDR with CH3+, H2, and PAH emission.  
  > "The analysis of the molecular emission and absorption observed with JWST in the spectrum of d203-504 points to the existence of two distinct chemical regions in the disk, as depicted in Figure 4." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous studies of strongly UV-irradiated disks in Orion relied on the edge-on disk d203-506, where the disk obscures the inner region; d203-504 has a more favorable viewing angle (i >= 54 deg) allowing direct observation of inner-disk chemistry alongside outer-disk PDR emission.  
  > "The current understanding is based on a single, edge-on disk (d203-506), restricting observations to the extended disk surface and preventing direct assessment of inner disk chemistry, including the C/O ratio." (Introduction); "The detection of water in absorption in d203-504, as opposed to its absence in d203-506, likely arises from d203-504’s geometry having a favorable vewing angle." (Discussion)
- Why this method: Deriving H2O and CO column densities from absorption against the hot inner-disk continuum is independent of the emitting surface area.  
  > "The inner disk column densities of H 2 O and CO are derived from absorption, and thus do not depend on an emitting surface area (Eq. 8)." (Results)
- Alternative: Free-free emission dominating the ALMA 344 GHz continuum flux was rejected because the VLA 6.1 GHz peak is only 0.1886 mJy and the ALMA 344 GHz emission (~3.0 mJy peak) peaks at the disk position rather than the ionization front.  
  > "However, the 344 GHz ALMA data has a peak intensity of _∼_ 3 _._ 0 mJy and is peaked at the disk position, not at the ionization front. Thus the ALMA 344 GHz emission must be largely dominated by the thermal dust rather than free-free emission." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
- Alternative: Water absorption occurring against both the near-infrared inner disk continuum and mid-infrared outer disk continuum (Eq. 9, case 2) was rejected because it overpredicts the 6.7 um MIRI nu2 absorption feature compared to observations.  
  > "It can be seen in this figure that the model for the case 2 produces absorption features which are much stronger than observed, while case 1 agrees better with observations." (Modeling of water absorption in the MIRI range)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): d203-504 is unresolved in the ALMA 344 GHz continuum observations and thus ALMA cannot independently constrain the disk size.  
  > "The disk, however, is not resolved in the ALMA observations and thus cannot provide with another estimate of the disk size." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
- Limitation (authors): Disk mass derived from ALMA 344 GHz flux has a broad range (1.3-5.6 M_Jup) due to uncertainties in dust grain opacity kappa_nu and optical depth.  
  > "This range of values stems from the uncertainties on the value of the _κ_ _ν_ parameter, the dust grain opacity, and the optical depth of the emission at 344 GHz." (ALMA observations of d203-504)
- Limitation (authors): For optically thin emission (CH3+ and CO emission), the emitting surface area S and column density N are degenerate and cannot be constrained independently.  
  > "All models are optically thin, thus the surface _S_ in equation 7 and associated column densities are degenerate and cannot be constrained independently." (Analytical models)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The inner-disk C/O ratio assumes CO and H2O are the sole major gas-phase reservoirs of carbon and oxygen without significant contributions from undetected species or optical depth saturation in individual ro-vibrational lines.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA 344 GHz disk mass depends on an assumed dust temperature and opacity law taken from earlier work [76, 77] that are not explicitly listed in this paper.
