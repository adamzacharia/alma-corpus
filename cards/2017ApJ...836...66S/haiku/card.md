1. **calibrate and image the Band 3 data**: Re-reduce delivered products in CASA, self-calibrate, image at Robust 0, 1, 2 (Robust 1 used), build continuum from line-free channels, subtract continuum in image plane, average 8 channels to 40 km/s  
  > "Following delivery of data products, the data were rereduced and imaged using the Common Astronomy Software Applications package (CASA)." (2. ALMA OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Robust = 1 used  
  > "only the Robust = 1 images are used here" (2. ALMA OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - parameter: 8-channel averaging to 40 km/s  
  > "Channel averaging over 8 of the original channels was done to reduce noise" (2. ALMA OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **measure continuum and CO line fluxes, peaks and source sizes**: 2D Gaussian fits to continuum and CO(1-0) integrated images; deconvolved sizes from maximum likelihood; peak brightness temperature from spectral cube within ±600 km/s of systemic velocity  
  > "Peak position and fluxes obtained from 2d Gaussian fits using the IDL routine CURVEFIT." (Table 1 (note))
   - software: IDL CURVEFIT, IDL MAXLIKELIHOOD
   - parameter: Gaussian fits to 112 GHz continuum and CO(1-0) maps; components listed in Table 2  
  > "The deconvolved sizes were obtained using the IDL routine" (Table 2 (note))
   - fitting: Parameter uncertainties are formal errors from the Gaussian fits  
  > "The uncertainties in the parameters were the formal errors from the Gaussian fitting." (Table 2 (note))
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **remove synchrotron and free-free contribution from 112.3 GHz continuum**: Extrapolate the 32.5 GHz flux with spectral index alpha = -0.60 measured between 7 and 32 GHz, scale to 112.3 GHz (about 0.47 of 32.5 GHz flux) and subtract

   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **derive dust optical depth and H2 column of the West nucleus**: Take dust temperature near 200 K, scale kappa from the 850 um calibration with nu^1.8, convert to kappa at 2.6 mm and to N_H2 via kappa = 4.51e-27 N_H2

   - parameter: Mean volume density n_H2 = 2.4e6 cm-3 over a 30 pc path  
  > "we infer a mean volume density" (3.5. West Nucleus ISM column densities and Mass)
   - assumption: Standard Galactic ISM dust properties and abundance apply in the nucleus  
  > "assuming that the dust there has similar properties to general ISM dust observed in the Galaxy" (3.5. West Nucleus ISM column densities and Mass)

   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **estimate luminosity of the West dust photosphere**: Stefan-Boltzmann luminosity L = 4 pi R^2 sigma T^4 for R = 15 pc and T = 200 K, giving 6.35e11 Lsun  
  > "with dust temperature T D ≃ 200 K and radius ≃ 15pc, must radiate" (3.4. West Nucleus Luminosity)
   - parameter: R = 15 pc; T = 200 K  
  > "with dust temperature T D ≃ 200 K and radius ≃ 15pc, must radiate" (3.4. West Nucleus Luminosity)
   - assumption: Spherical isotropic emission; a thin optically thick disk would be a factor 2 lower  
  > "the emergent luminosity would be a factor 2 lower than Equation" (3.4. West Nucleus Luminosity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Matches Wilson et al. (2014) estimate of 6.3e11 Lsun at 445 um  
  > "An identical estimate" (3.4. West Nucleus Luminosity)
6. **model CO(1-0) line profiles with inclined rotating disks**: Fit 2D inclined planar disks with parametrized rotation curve and single velocity dispersion to CO(1-0) profiles on a 60 mas grid, with emissivities allowed to differ on + and - offset sides  
  > "This model, including a parametrized rotation curve and gas velocity dispersion, was then fit to the observed CO line profiles on a well-sampled grid (60 mas spacing)" (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
   - parameter: West: V_sys 5337 km/s, FWHM 250 km/s, i = 30 deg, PA 265 deg, point mass 8e8 Msun  
  > "Systemic velocity V sys 5337 km s −1" (Table 4)
   - parameter: East: V_sys 5431 km/s, FWHM 120 km/s, i = 45 deg, PA 50 deg  
  > "Systemic velocity V sys 5431 km s −1" (Table 4)
   - assumption: 2D infinitesimally thin planar disk with constant velocity dispersion vs radius  
  > "This modeling is with a 2D, infinitesimally thin planar disk with constant velocity dispersion as a function of radius." (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
   - fitting: Maximum likelihood (Scoville et al. 1983 procedure); reduced chi-squared about 4 to 6 for both disks  
  > "This is a good fit, given the simplicity of the model" (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)

  > "The appropriateness of the model fit parameters (see Section 5) can be judged by comparison of the blue (observed) and model (red) spectra." (Appendix A)
7. **dynamical mass, disk thickness and turbulent dissipation time**: Spherical dynamical mass M = R V^2 / G; disk scale height from sigma_v and V_rot; dissipation time tau = H/(sigma_v f_a) with f_a = 1  
  > "The dynamical mass is obtained in the spherical approximation" (Figure 6 caption)

  > "with f a = 1" (Figure 8 caption)
   - parameter: Mean disk thickness 10 pc for density estimate

  > "We do this under the very simple assumption that the vertical velocity dispersion is constant with radius and that the disks are in equilibrium." (5.6. Disk Thickness)
   - assumption: Mestel disk surface density for self-gravitating case  
  > "For a Mestel disk" (5.5. Disk Area Covering)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spherical and disk-mass circular velocities differ significantly in an illustrative model  
  > "The proper estimation (solid curve) and the spherical one (dashed curve) have significantly different" (5.1. Dynamical Masses)
8. **test radiation pressure support of the disks**: Balance inward gravity against outward radiation pressure to obtain L/M, with kappa_IR from kappa_V and the IR-to-visible ratio  
  > "(Pollack et al. 1994; Scoville 2013; Thompson et al. 2015)" (6. RADIATION PRESSURE SUPPORT)

## Qualitative findings

- The CO(1-0) hole in the West nucleus coincides with the dust peak; the first or second explanation (thermalised or optically thin CO) is favoured over self-absorption  
  > "We thus favor the first or second explanations to reasonably account for the CO hole in the West nucleus." (4.1. CO Hole on the West Nucleus)
- The East nucleus is not axisymmetric; its CO peak is displaced NE of the dust peak  
  > "the peak in the CO emission is displaced" (4.2. CO Elongation in the East Nucleus)
- The two nuclear disks are highly symmetric, implying the merger timescale is long relative to disk rotation  
  > "The symmetry of the nuclear disk structures is impressive" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Higher resolution CO(1-0) data are important to publish now, so preliminary results are presented before the other transitions are available  
  > "In view of the major increase in resolution provided by the high resolution CO (1-0) data, publication of this data is important and we proceed here with those preliminary results." (2. ALMA OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
- Why this method: Higher order modelling (warps, feedback-driven dispersion gradients) is not warranted at the present resolution, so a simple disk model is used  
  > "given the limited resolution of the present observations relative to the scale of the disk, such higher order modeling is not yet warranted." (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
- Alternative: Three explanations for the CO hole were weighed: thermal equilibrium, optically thin CO, and self-absorption by foreground gas; self-absorption was disfavoured  
  > "There are three possible explanations for the drop in the strength of the CO emission at the center" (4.1. CO Hole on the West Nucleus)
- Alternative: Gas in discrete clouds was rejected in favour of a fairly continuous medium  
  > "We are thus led to the conclusion that the gas cannot be in discrete clouds but must be a fairly continuous medium" (5.8. Energy and Momentum Considerations)
- Alternative: Spherical dynamical mass estimates were compared against a disk-plus-point-mass model and judged potentially misleading  
  > "this mass estimate may be significantly different from that calculated from the observed kinematics" (5.1. Dynamical Masses)
- How they decided it was enough: Fit adequacy judged by reduced chi-squared and by visual comparison of observed and model spectra  
  > "The models developed for the high resolution CO (10) emission provide acceptable fits to the collection of" (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
- Limitation (authors): The data include only long baselines and no short spacings, so large-scale flux is not recovered  
  > "The observations reported in this paper do not include short spacing data and hence they do not recover flux associated with the large scale structures" (5.2. Physical Structure of the Nuclear Disks)
- Limitation (authors): Mass and luminosity estimates are uncertain, depending on the assumption of standard ISM dust properties  
  > "Clearly, these estimates are uncertain, given the assumption that the dust" (3.5. West Nucleus ISM column densities and Mass)
- Limitation (authors): The discussion is qualitative until full datasets are available  
  > "At this point, lacking the full datasets, our discussion is qualitative" (5.2. Physical Structure of the Nuclear Disks)
