## Analysis recipe

1. **measure continuum and CO fluxes, positions and sizes**: 2D Gaussian fits to the 112 GHz continuum and CO integrated-intensity images; deconvolution of sizes

   - software: IDL CURVEFIT, IDL MAX_LIKELIHOOD
   - fitting: Gaussian least squares; formal errors exclude calibration uncertainty  
  > "The uncertainties in the parameters were the formal errors from the Gaussian fitting." (Table 2)

2. **build continuum SED and isolate dust emission**: Compile <0.6 arcsec continuum fluxes, compute spectral indices, extrapolate synchrotron/free-free from 7-32 GHz index to 112 GHz and subtract to get dust-only flux and brightness temperature  
  > "determined between 7 and 32 GHz (see Figure 2)" (3.3. Dust Emission Fluxes and Brightness Temperatures); "The brightness temperature of the dust emission contribution at 112.3 GHz after removing the extrapolated synchrotron/free-free seen at longer wavelengths" (Table 3)

  > "determined between 7 and 32 GHz (see Figure 2)" (3.3. Dust Emission Fluxes and Brightness Temperatures)
   - assumption: Rayleigh-Jeans TB from peak flux per beam  
  > "Peak brightness temperature in the continuum calculated from" (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **estimate West nucleus luminosity**: Stefan-Boltzmann luminosity of an optically thick dust photosphere with T_D ~200 K and R ~15 pc  
  > "It is interesting to note that the implied dust photosphere, optically thick out to" (3.4. West Nucleus Luminosity)

## Qualitative findings

- Both nuclei host rotating nuclear disks; the East disk is elongated along the kinematic major axis, the West is compact and more face-on  
  > "The high resolution imaging presented here provides strong confirmation for the existence of nuclear disk structures in both nuclei of Arp 220" (5.2. Physical Structure of the Nuclear Disks)
- CO hole on the West dust peak is favored to be due to thermalization with optically thick dust or CO(1-0) being optically thinner than dust, not foreground self-absorption  
  > "We thus favor the first or second explanations to reasonably account for the CO hole in the West nucleus." (4.1. CO Hole on the West Nucleus)
- Gas must be a fairly continuous medium rather than discrete clouds  
  > "We are thus led to the conclusion that the gas cannot be in discrete clouds but must be a fairly continuous medium" (5.7. Disk Gas Properties)
- Radiation pressure on dust likely provides significant support, so dynamical masses may be underestimated  
  > "It is therefore clear that radiation on the dust in the central regions of Arp 220 is likely to be very important in providing significant pressure support" (6. RADIATION PRESSURE SUPPORT)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long-wavelength (2.6 mm) observations penetrate the optically thick dust; long baselines give 90 mas resolution  
  > "These high optical depths imply that the nuclear disk structures are best probed at" (1. INTRODUCTION); "A major virtue of the longer wavelength observations reported here is the ability to penetrate the optically thick dust envelope" (3.4. West Nucleus Luminosity)
- Why this method: Simple thin-disk model used because resolution does not warrant higher-order modeling  
  > "However, given the limited resolution of the present observations relative to the scale of the disk, such higher order modeling is not yet warranted." (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
- Alternative: Foreground/outflow self-absorption explanation for the West CO hole rejected  
  > "The third explanation appears inconsistent with the high spatial resolution spectra" (4.1. CO Hole on the West Nucleus)
- Alternative: Discrete virialized clouds rejected in favor of continuous medium; hybrid picture left viable  
  > "A hybrid picture – clouds of dense gas (with low areal covering factor) embedded in a diffuse molecular medium" (5.7. Disk Gas Properties)
- Alternative: Point mass could be gas concentration rather than black hole  
  > "the large mass could be an interstellar gas concentration and not necessarily a black hole" (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): No short-spacing data; extended flux (>~0.5 arcsec) resolved out  
  > "The observations reported in this paper do not include short spacing data and hence they do not recover flux associated with the large scale structures" (5.2. Physical Structure of the Nuclear Disks)
- Limitation (authors): Preliminary kinematic modeling; constant dispersion and thin disk simplifications  
  > "This is obviously a much simplified model to describe the central disks in a merger system" (5. CO EMISSION DISTRIBUTION AND KINEMATICS)
- Limitation (authors): Column/mass estimates rely on standard ISM dust properties  
  > "Clearly, these estimates are uncertain, given the assumption that the dust has standard interstellar dust properties and abundance" (3.5. West Nucleus ISM column densities and Mass)
- Limitation (authors): Spherical dynamical mass approximation may misestimate disk-dominated masses  
  > "the spherical approximation is likely to incorrectly estimate the actual mass in such a configuration" (5.1. Dynamical Masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Synchrotron subtraction relies on extrapolating a single 7-32 GHz index across different beams/epochs; free-free could flatten the spectrum.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Kinematic model uncertainties (on point mass, inclination) are not quantified; reduced chi^2 of 4-6 suggests underestimated noise or model inadequacy.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): West inclination of 30 deg conflicts with the SNR distribution, which affects dynamical masses.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing interferometric fluxes with very different beams and uv-coverage in Table 3 mixes spatial scales.
