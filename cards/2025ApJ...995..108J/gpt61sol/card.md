## Analysis recipe

1. **Produce continuum-subtracted CO cubes**: Combine TM1/TM2 for Cycles 8/10, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, and image with tclean; Cycle 9 uses its TM1 data.  
  > "We combined the TM1 and TM2 observations with the `concat` tool in CASA version 6.5.1." (ALMA Cycle 8 and Cycle 10 Observations: $^{12}$CO(2-1)); "We then fit the continuum emission from the spectral line-free channels across the four spectral windows using CASA task `uvcontsub`, assuming a zeroth-order polynomial." (ALMA Cycle 8 and Cycle 10 Observations: $^{12}$CO(2-1)); "The `tclean` task was then used to produce a continuum-subtracted cube containing only emission from the $^{12}$CO(1-0) line." (ALMA Cycle 9 Observations: $^{12}$CO(1-0))
   - software: CASA, concat, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: Cycles 8/10: Briggs robust=0.5; 7.8 MHz (∼10 km s−1) channels.  
  > "Each data cube was imaged with Briggs weighting (robust=0.5; (cite: briggs95))." (ALMA Cycle 8 and Cycle 10 Observations: $^{12}$CO(2-1)); "To improve the signal-to-noise of each channel, we smoothed the channel widths to 7.8 MHz ($\sim$ 10 km s$^{-1}$) in the final data cube." (ALMA Cycle 8 and Cycle 10 Observations: $^{12}$CO(2-1))
   - parameter: Cycle 9: natural weighting, robust = 2; 7.8 MHz (∼20 km s−1) channels.  
  > "imaging was performed using natural weighting (robust = 2) to maximize sensitivity to diffuse emission." (ALMA Cycle 9 Observations: $^{12}$CO(1-0)); "The channel widths were smoothed to 7.8 MHz ($\sim$20 km s$^{-1}$) for analysis purposes." (ALMA Cycle 9 Observations: $^{12}$CO(1-0))
   - assumption: Zeroth-order continuum polynomial for Cycles 8/10.  
  > "We then fit the continuum emission from the spectral line-free channels across the four spectral windows using CASA task `uvcontsub`, assuming a zeroth-order polynomial." (ALMA Cycle 8 and Cycle 10 Observations: $^{12}$CO(2-1))
   - fitting: Continuum fitted to line-free channels across four spectral windows for Cycles 8/10.  
  > "We then fit the continuum emission from the spectral line-free channels across the four spectral windows using CASA task `uvcontsub`, assuming a zeroth-order polynomial." (ALMA Cycle 8 and Cycle 10 Observations: $^{12}$CO(2-1))
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Define SMBH masses and aperture bounds**: Combine literature SMBH masses with MUSE Ca II stellar dispersions; apply the MBH–σ* relation and derive lower- and upper-bound SoI radii.  
  > "Masses of the central SMBHs were obtained from the literature where available. This is the case for NGC 6240, UGC 4211, and Mrk 739." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences); "We then measured $\sigma_*$ using the Ca II triplet absorption lines in the rest-frame 8350-8730Å range, following the method outlined by (cite: koss22)." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences); "To account for this uncertainty, we define upper- and lower-bound estimates of $r_{\text{SoI}}$ and conduct the same analysis at both extrema." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences)
   - software: MUSE instrument pipeline
   - parameter: log(MBH/M⊙) = 4.38 log(σ*/200 km s−1) + 8.49.  
  > "\text{log}\left(M_{\text{BH}}/M_\odot\right) = 4.38 \times \text{log}\left(\frac{\sigma_*}{200 \text{ km s$^{-1}$}}\right)+8.49," (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences)
   - parameter: Lower-bound rSoI = 33 pc (σ*/200 km s−1)^2.38; upper-bound approximately four times larger.  
  > "r_{\text{SoI}}=33 \textrm{pc} \left(\frac{\sigma_*}{200 \textrm{ km s}^{-1}}\right)^{2.38}." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences); "Based on the (cite: medling15) results for NGC 6240, we apply a factor of $\sim4$ to the lower-bound ($M_{\text{BH}}-\sigma_*$ SoI) to obtain our upper-bound SoI estimate." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences)
   - assumption: Virialized, isotropic and spherically symmetric gas motions.  
  > "If we assume that the nuclei have virialized, isotropic, and spherically symmetric gas motions" (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat the analysis at both SoI extrema to address methodology and definition uncertainty.  
  > "To account for this uncertainty, we define upper- and lower-bound estimates of $r_{\text{SoI}}$ and conduct the same analysis at both extrema." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences)
3. **Measure CO maps and integrated fluxes**: Integrate contiguous ≥3σ channels into moment 0 maps; sum >3σ pixels in the 25 arcsec field; separate resolved nuclear components where possible.  
  > "We produce these maps from the ALMA data cubes by integrating over contiguous channels with $\geq 3\sigma$ detections" (CO Maps and Properties); "by summing the flux densities of all pixels with $>3\sigma$ emission in the 25$''$ ALMA field of view." (CO Maps and Properties); "For Mrk 463, Mrk 739, and ESO 253-G003, we report fluxes for each nucleus separately because the associated $^{12}$CO emission is individually detected and spatially resolved." (CO Maps and Properties)
   - parameter: Velocity integration ranges: ±100 to ±1000 km s−1 around the expected redshifted line.  
  > "(velocity ranges of $\pm100$ to $\pm1000$ km s$^{-1}$ of the expected redshifted frequency of the $^{12}$CO emission line)." (CO Maps and Properties)
   - parameter: 1σ errors from emission-free map RMS plus assumed Band 3/6 flux uncertainties of 5%/10%.  
  > "We estimate 1$\sigma$ uncertainties by measuring the RMS in the emission-free regions of the intensity maps and assuming 5% and 10% flux uncertainties for ALMA Bands 3 and 6, respectively." (CO Maps and Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare global fluxes with single-dish measurements after checking that both observations cover the full line width.  
  > "We calculate these percentages by dividing the ALMA-observed global $^{12}$CO flux by the unresolved single dish measurements, after confirming that both observations had spectral coverage greater than the observed line width of the emission line." (CO Maps and Properties)
4. **Convert CO fluxes to molecular gas masses**: Calculate CO line luminosities using the Solomon prescription; convert CO(2-1) through r21 and CO(1-0) directly with αCO.  
  > "Following the prescription described in (cite: solomon05), we calculate the $^{12}$CO line luminosity of each galaxy" (CO Maps and Properties); "M_{\text{mol}} = \alpha_{\text{CO}} \frac{L^{'}_{\text{CO(2-1)}}}{r_{21}} = \alpha_{\text{CO}}L^{'}_{\text{CO(1-0)}}," (CO Maps and Properties)
   - parameter: L′CO = 3.25×10^7 SCOΔv νobs^−2 DL^2 (1+z)^−3.  
  > "L^{'}_{\text{CO}} = 3.25\times10^7S_{\text{CO}}\Delta v\, \nu_{\text{obs}}^{-2}\,D_L^2\,(1+z)^{-3}." (CO Maps and Properties)
   - assumption: r21 ≈ 1.09 for CO(2-1); no r21 conversion for ESO 253-G003.  
  > "We adopt the median galaxy-integrated $^{12}$CO line ratio $r_{21}\approx 1.09$ from (cite: MA2023)" (CO Maps and Properties); "we directly measure $M_{\text{mol}}$ from the line luminosity without applying the $r_{21}$ conversion factor." (CO Maps and Properties)
   - assumption: Explore αCO = 0.6–4.6 M⊙ (K km s−1 pc−2)−1.  
  > "we thus explore the full range of $^{12}$CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factors, $\alpha_{\text{CO}}$ = $0.6-4.6$ $M_\odot$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{-2}$)$^{-1}$." (CO Maps and Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Adopted r21 compared with the published NGC 6240 global line ratio.  
  > "This is also consistent with previous studies of NGC 6240, a dual AGN system in our sample, which has a mean global $^{12}$CO line ratio of $r_{21} = 1.17 \pm 0.19$ (cite: Cicone2018)." (CO Maps and Properties)
5. **Fit rotating molecular gas components**: Fit rotating disks to three-dimensional cubes with KinMS_fitter; generally fit two spatially separated components, but one for Mrk 975 and UGC 4211.  
  > "we employ the `KinMS_fitter`[^1] wrapper, which utilizes a Bayesian Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach to fit a rotating disk to the data cube." (Kinematics); "Generally, we fit two rotating disks to the $^{12}$CO line emission of each system by defining a spatial region for each component and fitting them separately." (Kinematics); "we report single-component models because the $^{12}$CO emission was either undetected near the second nucleus (Mrk 975) or only one velocity gradient was observed (UGC 4211)." (Kinematics)
   - software: KinMS, KinMS_fitter, skySampler, corner
   - parameter: 30,000–65,000 MCMC iterations.  
  > "At least 30,000 iterations were performed for each fitting, with some models having up to 65,000 iterations" (Kinematics)
   - parameter: Free parameters: Vmax, Rscale, Rturn, kinematic center x/y, Vsys, total flux, velocity dispersion, position angle and inclination.  
  > "ten free parameters for each rotating disk component: $V_{\text{max}}$, $R_{\text{scale}}$, $R_{\text{turn}}$, the $x$- and $y$-coordinates of the kinematic center, the systemic velocity $V_{\text{sys}}$, the total flux, the velocity dispersion $\sigma$, the position angle $\theta$, and the inclination angle $\varphi$." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: skySampler with uniform sampling and fixed moment 0 morphology for Mrk 463E, Mrk 739E/W and ESO 253-G003 SW.  
  > "using the KinMS plugin, `skySampler`, with a uniform sampling scheme." (Kinematics); "This was the case for Mrk 463E, Mrk 739E, Mrk 739W, and ESO 253-G003 SW." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Simple exponential surface brightness for most rotating components.  
  > "In the majority of our fittings, we assume a surface brightness with a simple exponential disk profile for each rotating component" (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Arctangential velocity profile V(r) = (2Vmax/π) arctan(r/Rturn).  
  > "V(r) = \frac{2V_{\text{max}}}{\pi}\arctan\frac{r}{R_{\text{turn}}}" (Kinematics)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC; parameter limits generally KinMS defaults; inclination 0–90°; maximum dispersion increased from 50 to 500 km s−1.  
  > "The maximum and minimum allowed values for each parameter were generally left at KinMS’s default settings" (Kinematics); "the inclination must be within 0 and 90 degrees" (Kinematics); "We did, however, increase the maximum velocity dispersion from 50 km s$^{-1}$ to 500 km s$^{-1}$ to account for the highly turbulent nature of these systems." (Kinematics)
   - validation: Inspect residuals for significant negative features and require fitted components to coincide with optical disks; inspect posterior corner plots.  
  > "we examined the model-subtracted residual images to confirm that significant negative features, a common indicator of overfitting or a poor choice of model, are not present." (Kinematics); "We also confirm that the fitted components are spatially coincident with the host galaxies’ optical disks" (Kinematics); "Here, we present corner plots showing the 1- and 2-dimensional projections of the posterior probability distributions of the KinMS-fitted MCMC parameters." (KinMS MCMC Corner Plots)
6. **Measure total and non-rotating gas within the SoI**: Extract >3σ residual CO flux within each SoI and convert to gas mass; repeat at both SoI bounds; use beam-sized apertures and upper limits for unresolved SoIs.  
  > "we extract $^{12}$CO line fluxes from $>3\sigma$ pixels within the SMBH SoIs and derive the molecular gas mass" (Kinematics); "We repeat this process for the upper- and lower-bound estimates of the SMBH SoI." (Kinematics); "In the case of systems with unresolved SMBH SoIs, we extract the $^{12}$CO flux using a beam-sized aperture at the SMBH position and report the molecular gas mass as an upper limit." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: Non-detection limits are 3σ; SoI fractions divide aperture gas mass by host global gas mass.  
  > "3$\sigma$ upper limits due to non-detections of $^{12}$CO within the SoI." (Table 6); "Fraction of the host galaxy’s global molecular gas mass that lies within the SoI" (Table 6)
   - assumption: All gas within the SoIs of NGC 6240 and NGC 985 is treated as non-rotating after unsuccessful rotating-disk fits.  
  > "For both NGC 6240 and NGC 985, we hereafter assume that all of the molecular gas within the SoIs are non-rotating." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Projected gas masses are upper limits to the true mass within the SoI; non-rotating gas masses are upper limits to infalling gas mass.  
  > "Formally, these are projected molecular gas masses and should be treated as upper-limits to the true mass within the SMBH SoI." (Kinematics); "These non-rotating gas masses serve as upper limits to the total infalling gas mass, given that they may include low velocity outflows, bar-driven streaming, and other components." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Estimate missing SoI flux assuming a smooth distribution over a ∼20–28 arcsec single-dish beam; inferred loss is below the cube 1σ RMS.  
  > "We estimated a maximum impact on the SoI gas measurements by assuming that the lost flux has a smooth distribution across the single-dish primary beam ($\sim20-28”$ diameters)." (Kinematics); "the maximum flux loss within the SoI corresponds to less than the 1$\sigma$ RMS of the ALMA data cubes" (Kinematics)
7. **Derive AGN luminosities and Eddington ratios**: Use published bolometric luminosities or convert intrinsic 2–10 keV luminosities; for ESO 253-G003 infer X-rays from ∼100 GHz continuum; divide bolometric by Eddington luminosity.  
  > "For Mrk 739 and UGC 4211, AGN bolometric luminosities of each nucleus were reported in (cite: koss11) and (cite: koss23)." (Bolometric Luminosities); "For the dual AGN system ESO 253-G003, we estimate intrinsic 2$-$10 keV X-ray luminosities from the ALMA continuum luminosities at $\sim$100 GHz (Droguett et al., in prep)." (Bolometric Luminosities); "We derive Eddington ratios ($L_{\text{Bol}}/L_{\text{Edd}}$) for the individual nuclei using the aforementioned AGN bolometric luminosities and SMBH masses" (Eddington Ratios)
   - parameter: κ2–10 = 20.  
  > "We once again assume a bolometric correction of $\kappa_{2-10} = 20$" (Bolometric Luminosities)
   - parameter: LEdd = 1.5×10^46 erg s−1 × MBH/(10^8 M⊙).  
  > "L_{\text{Edd}} = 1.5 \times 10^{46} \text{ erg s}^{-1} \frac{M_{\text{BH}}}{10^8 M_\odot}." (Eddington Ratios)
   - assumption: Solar metallicity for LEdd; AGN coronal synchrotron dominates ESO 253-G003 ∼100 GHz continuum.  
  > "This definition is in accordance with (cite: Koss2022b) and assumes solar metallicity." (Eddington Ratios); "At our $<$100 pc resolution, self-absorbed synchrotron emission from the AGN corona dominates the $\sim$100 GHz continuum" (Bolometric Luminosities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check consistency with luminosity-dependent bolometric corrections over the sample luminosity range.  
  > "While other studies have reported a luminosity dependence of the bolometric correction (cite: Duras2020), in the luminosity range covered by our sample" (Bolometric Luminosities); "we obtain similar and consistent values for the bolometric correction." (Bolometric Luminosities)
8. **Measure spatial offsets and gas concentration**: Compare AGN positions to CO peaks and modeled kinematic centers; compare gas surface densities within 50 and 200 pc for three systems.  
  > "we find that $\sim67\%$ (8/12) of the AGN are spatially offset from their $^{12}$CO emission line peaks" (Spatial misalignment with $^{12}$CO peaks); "In Table [7](#tab:offsets), we report the spatial offsets for each target, omitting NGC 6240 and NGC 985 for which we do not have KinMS models." (Off-nuclear SMBHs); "we show the ratio of molecular gas surface densities at $r<50$ pc and $r<200$ pc with respect to the AGN bolometric luminosity and Eddington ratio." (Spatial misalignment with $^{12}$CO peaks)
   - parameter: 50/200 pc surface-density comparison uses NGC 6240, UGC 4211 and ESO 253-G003.  
  > "the nuclei of NGC 6240, UGC 4211, and ESO 253-G003 — the three systems for which we have the necessary $\sim50$ parsec resolution." (Spatial misalignment with $^{12}$CO peaks)
   - assumption: UGC 4211 nuclei are not classified as offset because both are approximately equidistant from the single gas kinematic center.  
  > "Here, we do not consider the two nuclei of UGC 4211 to be offset, given that they are approximately equidistant from the single kinematic center of the molecular gas." (Off-nuclear SMBHs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Offset errors use KinMS 1σ uncertainties; Gaia–ALMA alignment checked with ALMA check calibrators.  
  > "The uncertainties in columns (2) and (3) were determined from the KinMS $1\sigma$ errors reported in Table [5](#tab:mcmc)." (Table 7); "We determined these uncertainties by comparing the coordinates of the ALMA check calibrator sources to the Gaia catalog." (CO Maps and Properties)
9. **Estimate potential short-term SMBH growth**: Express non-rotating SoI gas as a potential increase in SMBH mass, using resolved measurements at each SoI bound.  
  > "we can roughly estimate the short-term increase in $M_{\text{BH}}$ for each AGN." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes); "These ranges were derived from systems with resolved SoI and do not include the gas mass upper limits presented in Table [6](#tab:mol_SOI)." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - parameter: αCO = 0.6 M⊙ (K km s−1 pc−2)−1.  
  > "At the SoI lower-bound, assuming $\alpha_{\text{CO}}=0.6\,M_\odot$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{-2}$)$^{-1}$" (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - assumption: All non-rotating molecular gas within the SoI is eventually accreted.  
  > "if we assume all non-rotating molecular gas within the SMBH SoI (see Table [6](#tab:mol_SOI)) to be accreted onto the SMBH eventually" (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat the upper-bound growth estimate omitting the NGC 6240S outlier.  
  > "Omitting this outlier, the expected SMBH growth at upper-bounds range from a $\sim0-23\%$ mass increase, with the median remaining at 3%." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
10. **Test gas correlations and compare AGN classes**: Use Monte Carlo Kendall correlations with upper limits, report medians from 1000 bootstrap iterations, and perform partial Spearman correlations for resolved measurements.  
  > "This correlation analysis was performed with `pymccorrelation` (cite: Curran2014; Privon2020), which calculates the correlation coefficient $\tau$, the $p$-value, and their respective uncertainties using a Monte Carlo approach." (Molecular gas across merger stage); "The quantities reported here and in the remainder of this work are the median values determined after 1000 bootstrapping iterations." (Molecular gas across merger stage); "performing partial correlations using the Python package `Pingouin` to determine Spearman correlation coefficients for the resolved measurements in our sample." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - software: pymccorrelation, Pingouin
   - parameter: Include upper limits in Kendall calculations.  
  > "Note that Kendall’s tau method incorporates upper limit measurements that are hence included in these calculations." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - parameter: Partial-correlation covariates: stellar velocity dispersion and stellar mass.  
  > "even when the stellar velocity dispersion $\sigma_*$ and stellar mass $M_*$ are set as covariates" (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
   - parameter: Empirical projected-separation division at 1.5 kpc.  
  > "While this 1.5 kpc threshold was empirically selected" (Gas inside each SMBH’s sphere of influence)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare gas masses against aperture-size expectations, test surface densities, omit NGC 985E, and control for stellar dispersion and stellar mass.

## Qualitative findings

- CO generally concentrates near AGN, but is undetected near the non-AGN SMBHs in the two single-AGN systems.  
  > "the $^{12}$CO molecular gas is generally concentrated near the AGN position in all galaxies." (CO Maps and Properties); "In the single AGN systems (Mrk 975 and NGC 985), however, we find an absence of $^{12}$CO emission near the non-AGN SMBH." (CO Maps and Properties)
- Residual non-rotating gas is predominantly low-velocity, with no significant high-velocity components.  
  > "The residual moment 1 maps reveal that the non-rotating gas is predominantly low-velocity (line-of-sight velocity $\lesssim 200$ km s$^{-1}$)" (Kinematics); "there was no evidence of any significant high velocity components in the any of the residual cubes." (Kinematics)
- NGC 6240 and NGC 985 do not yield physically acceptable converged rotating-disk models.  
  > "Rotating disk models are not reported for NGC 6240 nor NGC 985." (Kinematics); "The best-fit models for both set-ups, however, were inconsistent with expected positions and orientations of the stellar disks" (Kinematics); "For the single AGN NGC 985, the velocity profile was similarly inconsistent with that of a rotating disk." (Kinematics)
- Larger SMBH masses are associated with higher non-rotating gas surface densities.  
  > "More massive SMBHs tend to have higher surface densities of non-rotating molecular gas within their SoIs." (Conclusions)
- Single and dual AGN molecular gas properties are tentatively consistent; variability and/or obscuration are proposed explanations.  
  > "Finally, we tentatively find no significant differences in the nuclear molecular gas masses of single AGN and dual AGN hosts, both within the SMBH sphere of influence and within the central kiloparsec." (Abstract); "Our results indicate that the probability of occurrence of the dual AGN phenomenon is likely dependent on AGN variability and/or obscuration rather than the availability of molecular gas in the nuclear regions." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High resolution probes SMBH SoIs; low-J CO estimates total molecular gas; ESO 253-G003 CO(1-0) avoids an assumed line ratio.  
  > "Studying the molecular gas kinematics within the SMBH sphere of influence (SoI) could, therefore, provide crucial insight into current and future SMBH growth, but this requires high angular resolution observations that can resolve the SoI." (Introduction); "are able to provide estimates of the total molecular gas mass and its distributions due to their low excitation temperatures" (Introduction); "we prioritized the $^{12}$CO(1-0) transition for which the line luminosity can be directly converted into a molecular gas mass" (Data Description)
- Why this method: Rotating-disk subtraction isolates non-rotating gas; skySampler accommodates non-central morphologies; arctangent profiles are motivated by prior sub-kpc CO fits.  
  > "We perform kinematic modeling of the ALMA data cubes to determine the rotational component of the $^{12}$CO line emission. This allows us to isolate the non-rotating component of the molecular gas." (Kinematics); "the standard analytic models available in `KinMS_fitter` (e.g., exponential disks, Gaussians) were not suitable because they assumed a centrally-peaked brightness profile." (Kinematics); "we select this model because arctangential velocity profiles have been shown to provide a good fit to (sub-)kiloparsec $^{12}$CO disks in galaxies across different evolutionary stages" (Kinematics)
- Alternative: For multiply observed systems generally use the newest data; use ESO 253-G003 CO(1-0) instead of CO(2-1) to avoid r21.  
  > "Generally, for systems that have been observed during multiple ALMA observing cycles, we analyzed the most recent data set." (Data Description); "we prioritized the $^{12}$CO(1-0) transition for which the line luminosity can be directly converted into a molecular gas mass, whereas the $^{12}$CO(2-1) transition would require assuming a line ratio" (Data Description)
- Alternative: Centrally peaked analytic brightness profiles rejected for ring-like and clumpy systems in favor of fixed moment 0 morphology.  
  > "the standard analytic models available in `KinMS_fitter` (e.g., exponential disks, Gaussians) were not suitable because they assumed a centrally-peaked brightness profile." (Kinematics); "Instead, we fixed the morphology of these systems from their moment 0 intensity maps" (Kinematics)
- Alternative: Single- and two-component rotating disks rejected for NGC 6240 and NGC 985.  
  > "we attempted to model the emission using both a single-component and a two-component rotating disk model." (Kinematics); "For the two-component model, we also attempted to manually constrain the parameter space" (Kinematics); "Both the single-component and two-component MCMC models failed to converge after 30,000 iterations." (Kinematics)
- Alternative: Aperture-size explanation for the SMBH–gas correlation tested against a uniform 3σ mass distribution and gas surface densities.  
  > "the positive trend between $M_{\text{BH}}$ and non-rotating molecular gas mass could be an artifact of differing SoI’s" (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes); "This suggests that the $M_{\text{BH}}$ correlation in Figure [11](#fig:SMBH_NR) is driven by differences in molecular gas concentration rather than an increase in spatial scale/aperture size." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
- Alternative: Recoiling SMBHs rejected as the interpretation of offsets because coalescence has not occurred.  
  > "Since SMBH coalescence has not yet occurred in these merger systems, we do not interpret these spatial offsets as evidence of recoiling SMBHs." (Off-nuclear SMBHs)
- Alternative: CO peak offsets could reflect higher excitation, feedback destruction, outflows or accretion-driven depletion.  
  > "The molecular gas in the vicinity of the AGN is either being destroyed by AGN feedback, depleted by nuclear outflows, accretion onto the SMBH, or it is at a higher excitation" (Conclusions)
- Alternative: An imbalanced gravitational potential cannot alone explain single-AGN systems because only the less massive Mrk 975 SMBH is active.  
  > "AGN activity is only detected for the *less* massive SMBH (Mrk 975 NE)." (Structural differences between Single and Dual AGN); "an imbalanced gravitational potential well cannot solely explain why only one SMBH is active." (Structural differences between Single and Dual AGN)
- How they decided it was enough: At least 30,000 and up to 65,000 fitting iterations to obtain reasonable 1σ errors; inspect residuals for overfitting.  
  > "At least 30,000 iterations were performed for each fitting, with some models having up to 65,000 iterations to obtain reasonable $1\sigma$ errors." (Kinematics); "we examined the model-subtracted residual images to confirm that significant negative features, a common indicator of overfitting or a poor choice of model, are not present." (Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Only two single-AGN systems provide an incomplete, preliminary comparison sample.  
  > "Though not a complete comparison sample, the inclusion of these two single AGN will serve as a preliminary point of comparison" (Sample Selection)
- Limitation (authors): SMBH masses and SoI radii depend on methodology; merger systems may depart from the MBH–σ* relation.  
  > "SMBH mass measurements can be highly uncertain, and vary substantially based on the method followed" (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences); "there can be up to a factor of $\sim4$ difference in the derived SoI based on methodology and definition." (Black Hole Masses & Sphere of Influences)
- Limitation (authors): Most lower-bound SoIs are unresolved, limiting correlation constraints.  
  > "the SoI lower-bound is unresolved in a high fraction of sources, resulting in the molecular gas measurements being dominated by upper-limits." (Gas inside each SMBH’s sphere of influence); "We therefore lack sufficient information to quantify a precise correlation at the SoI lower-bound." (Gas inside each SMBH’s sphere of influence)
- Limitation (authors): Galaxy-integrated r21 is used because robust 50–100 pc line ratios in major mergers are unavailable.  
  > "this value represents our best estimate given that $^{12}$CO line ratios at 50$-$100 pc resolutions have not been studied in a robust sample of major mergers." (CO Maps and Properties)
- Limitation (authors): Global missing flux can inflate SoI gas fractions by ∼3 for ESO 253-G003 and ∼1.2–1.4 for other systems.  
  > "a factor of $\sim3$ for ESO 253-G003, and a factor of $\sim1.2-1.4$ for the remaining systems" (Kinematics); "likely overestimate the true fraction by approximately the same order of magnitude as the flux loss on global/kiloparsec scales" (Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Some MCMC posteriors are bimodal or non-Gaussian.  
  > "We find that the MCMC probability distributions for some parameters are bimodal and/or non-Gaussian." (Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Projected SoI gas masses and non-rotating gas masses are upper limits to enclosed and infalling gas masses, respectively.  
  > "Formally, these are projected molecular gas masses and should be treated as upper-limits to the true mass within the SMBH SoI." (Kinematics); "These non-rotating gas masses serve as upper limits to the total infalling gas mass, given that they may include low velocity outflows, bar-driven streaming, and other components." (Feeding the Supermassive Black Holes)
- Limitation (authors): The nuclear concentration versus activity trend is not statistically significant and needs a larger sample.  
  > "the observed trend is not statistically significant ($\tau = -0.2$, $p$-value = 0.4)." (Spatial misalignment with $^{12}$CO peaks); "High resolution $^{12}$CO observations of a larger sample of AGN in major galaxy mergers are necessary to fully evaluate the observed trend." (Spatial misalignment with $^{12}$CO peaks)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Failed disk fits do not by themselves establish that all enclosed gas is non-rotating.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SoI aperture depends on SMBH mass, so residual coupling may remain despite the aperture and partial-correlation checks.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The missing-flux test assumes a smooth distribution; more concentrated filtered emission would behave differently.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The empirical 1.5 kpc division and small, censored sample limit the strength of merger-stage conclusions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cycle 9 text gives ∼20 km s−1 final channels, while Table 2 describes all RMS values per 10 km/s channel.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cycle 9 imaging text calls TM1 compact, but the observing setup identifies C43-9 and ∼15 km baselines.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): NGC 6240 has a 0.95 kpc separation in Table 1 and 1.4 kpc in its source description.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): NGC 985 has a Table 3 mass ratio of 6.9 despite the stated parent major-merger criterion below 5:1.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): NGC 985W gas fractions in Table 6 differ from the <0.05% value in the discussion.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mrk 975 NE upper-bound non-rotating mass range ends at 7.21 in Table 6, versus 7.51 at the lower bound; retain the printed values pending clarification.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 5 labels the eastern Mrk 463 fit as Mrk 436E; component identification needs confirmation.
