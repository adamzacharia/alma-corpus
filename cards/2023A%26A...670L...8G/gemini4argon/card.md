## Analysis recipe

1. **Check known Class II sources with large-scale CO structures for reflection nebulosity**: Visual inspection and literature check of archival optical and NIR images (DSS, Pan-STARRS, HST) around five Class II sources known to have kilo-au CO structures (AB Aur, SU Aur, RU Lup, GM Aur, DO Tau)  
  > "First we targeted Class II sources with known kilo-au scale gas structures – possibly due to late infall of material – and we searched for RNe in their vicinity in optical and near-infrared images." (Reflections on nebulae around young stars); "In order to test the hypothesis that RNe might indicate late infall, we looked for signs of nebulosity around these sources, as discussed below:" (Class II sources with large-scale CO structures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Compile and merge catalogues of reflection nebulae (RNe) in nearby star-forming regions**: Merge published RNe catalogues from Magakian (2003) and Connelley et al. (2007), remove duplicates using a 30'' cross-matching radius, and filter for sources within circular boundaries of eight nearby star-forming regions  
  > "As a first step, we compiled a catalogue of RNe using the published lists in (cite: Magakian2003) and (cite: Connelley2007)." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "Using a cross-matching radius of $30\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim\mu+1\sigma$), we found ten RNe present in both of the catalogues. After removing duplicates, we merged the two catalogues to have a final sample of 1009 RNe." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "We found 141 sources from our catalogue of 1009 RNe within these region boundaries." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - parameter: Cross-matching radius = 30''  
  > "Using a cross-matching radius of $30\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim\mu+1\sigma$), we found ten RNe present in both of the catalogues." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - parameter: SFR circular boundaries defined by central coordinates and radii in Table 1  
  > "The radii reported in the fifth column in Table 1 were used to define circular boundaries around the central coordinates listed in the second and third columns" (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compile YSO catalogue and classify Class II sources via infrared spectral indices**: Merge YSO catalogues from Marton et al. (2016) and Dunham et al. (2015) using a 5'' cross-matching radius, filter to SFR boundaries, compute infrared spectral index alpha (2-25 um) from WISE and 2MASS K-band photometry for Marton-only sources, apply median extinction correction of -0.31 to get alpha', and select Class II sources with -1.6 <= alpha' < -0.3  
  > "Using a cross-matching radius of $5\ensuremath{''}$, we found 781 common sources in both datasets. Our final catalogue consists of 136165 YSOs of which 4930 lie within the SFR boundaries." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "In order to estimate extinction-corrected spectral indices ($\alpha'$), we computed a correction factor of -0.31 as the median of differences between all the $\alpha'$ and $\alpha$ values reported in (cite: Dunham2015)." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "Following the same classification criteria as (cite: Dunham2015), that is $-1.6 \leq \alpha' < -0.3$, 2562 out of 4930 YSOs in our SFRs were classified as Class II sources." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - parameter: YSO cross-matching radius = 5''  
  > "Using a cross-matching radius of $5\ensuremath{''}$, we found 781 common sources in both datasets." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - parameter: Discard sources with alpha fitting uncertainties > 0.6  
  > "Similarly, we determined $\alpha$ for sources exclusively in the (cite: Marton2016) catalogue using the provided WISE and 2MASS (K band) photometry, and discarding those with fitting uncertainties $>0.6$" (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - parameter: Class II criterion: -1.6 <= alpha' < -0.3  
  > "Following the same classification criteria as (cite: Dunham2015), that is $-1.6 \leq \alpha' < -0.3$, 2562 out of 4930 YSOs in our SFRs were classified as Class II sources." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - assumption: Uniform extinction correction factor of -0.31 applied to alpha for sources only in Marton et al. (2016)  
  > "In order to estimate extinction-corrected spectral indices ($\alpha'$), we computed a correction factor of -0.31 as the median of differences between all the $\alpha'$ and $\alpha$ values reported in (cite: Dunham2015)." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - fitting: Slope of SED in infrared regime (~2-25 um) using WISE and 2MASS K-band photometry  
  > "The spectral indices, $\alpha$, or slope of the spectral energy distributions in the infrared regime ($\sim2$–$25~\mu$m) were given in the (cite: Dunham2015) catalogue." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "Similarly, we determined $\alpha$ for sources exclusively in the (cite: Marton2016) catalogue using the provided WISE and 2MASS (K band) photometry, and discarding those with fitting uncertainties $>0.6$" (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Cross-match Class II sources with RNe to identify late-infall candidates and compute association fractions**: Cross-match the 2562 Class II sources with the 141 RNe in the SFRs using a projected distance threshold of ~2000 au (Bondi-Hoyle accretion scale), and evaluate cumulative association fractions as a function of offset distance  
  > "The next step was to cross match our list of Class II sources with the merged catalogue of RNe." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "For a stellar mass of 1 M$_{\odot}$ and typical stellar velocity of 1 km s$^{-1}$, $L_{BH}\sim2000$ au. The 21 late-infall Class II candidates that we found using this threshold are listed in Appendix 8." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "Figure 4 plots the fraction of Class II sources and all YSOs associated with RNe as a function of the offset distance for different SFRs." (Discussion)
   - parameter: Distance threshold L_BH ~ 2000 au  
  > "For a stellar mass of 1 M$_{\odot}$ and typical stellar velocity of 1 km s$^{-1}$, $L_{BH}\sim2000$ au." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - assumption: Bondi-Hoyle length scale L_BH = 2GM_*/v^2 with M_* = 1 M_sun and v = 1 km s^-1  
  > "The typical length scale for this interaction is given as $L_{BH} = 2GM_{*}/v^{2}$, where $G$ is the gravitational constant, $M_{*}$ is the stellar mass, and $v$ is the stellar speed relative to the gas." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
   - assumption: Median SFR distances from Zucker et al. (2020) used for physical scale conversion  
  > "The median coordinates and distances are from (cite: Zucker2020)." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Query ALMA Science Archive and select adequate datasets for nearby candidate sources**: Search ALMA Science Archive for Band 6 and Band 7 observations of the 16 candidates in SFRs at d <= 200 pc, filtering for LAS >= 1000 au, rms noise < 2.3 mJy beam^-1 (over 10 km s^-1), and coverage of 12CO lines  
  > "To target higher-quality observations, we focussed on nearby SFRs ($d \lesssim$ 200 pc), where 16 out of the 21 Class II disks near RNe are located. (ALMA archive search:)
   - parameter: Distance d <= 200 pc  
  > "To target higher-quality observations, we focussed on nearby SFRs ($d \lesssim$ 200 pc), where 16 out of the 21 Class II disks near RNe are located." (ALMA archive search:)
   - parameter: LAS >= 1000 au  
  > "Among these datasets, we found 13 observations with the largest angular scale (LAS) corresponding to at least 1000 au to recover the expected large-scale structures (cite: Kuffmeier2020)." (ALMA archive search:)
   - parameter: rms noise < 2.3 mJy beam^-1 over 10 km s^-1 channel width  
  > "We selected a subset of five observations with rms noise (over a channel width of $10$ km s$^{-1}$) smaller than a $2.3$ mJy beam$^{-1}$, equal to the line sensitivity reported in the ALMA archive for SU Aur observations (cite: Ginski2021)." (ALMA archive search:)

## Qualitative findings

- All five Class II sources previously known to have kilo-au CO structures (AB Aur, SU Aur, RU Lup, GM Aur, DO Tau) also show prominent reflection nebulae or hints of reflection nebulosity in optical/NIR images.  
  > "To summarise, the five known Class II sources that have large-scale CO structures suggestive of an interaction with surrounding gas have either a prominent RN or some hints of reflection nebulosity." (Class II sources with large-scale CO structures)
- First discovery of at least one ~1000 au redshifted CO streamer (and hints of two more) northwest of the S CrA binary disks.  
  > "For S CrA, at least one $\sim1000$ au streamer is clearly visible (solid cyan line), north-west of the binary disks (green contours), as seen in the moment 0 map (Figure 3, panel b, middle panel)." (ALMA archive search:); "This is the first discovery of large-scale streamers around S CrA." (ALMA archive search:)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 12CO (J=2-1 and J=3-2) has high surface brightness and is a good tracer of large-scale diffuse gas structures; LAS >= 1000 au is required so interferometric filtering does not remove kilo-au streamers; rms < 2.3 mJy/beam matches the sensitivity needed to detect the SU Aur streamer.  
  > "We are primarily interested in $^{12}$CO emission ($J=2-1$ in Band 6 and $J=3-2$ in Band 7) since this molecule is expected to be a good tracer of large-scale diffuse gas structures (see e.g. Figure 1)." (ALMA archive search:); "Among these datasets, we found 13 observations with the largest angular scale (LAS) corresponding to at least 1000 au to recover the expected large-scale structures (cite: Kuffmeier2020)." (ALMA archive search:); "We selected a subset of five observations with rms noise (over a channel width of $10$ km s$^{-1}$) smaller than a $2.3$ mJy beam$^{-1}$, equal to the line sensitivity reported in the ALMA archive for SU Aur observations (cite: Ginski2021)." (ALMA archive search:)
- Why this method: Clouds close enough to gravitationally interact with Class II stars scatter stellar light in optical/NIR and appear as reflection nebulae; cross-matching within the Bondi-Hoyle radius (~2000 au) selects the most reliable candidates for cloud-star gravitational capture.  
  > "In order to find disks that are potentially undergoing late infall, one should first identify Class II sources that are close enough to clouds to gravitationally interact with them. Such clouds will scatter the protostellar light in optical and near-infrared (NIR) wavelengths and appear as reflection nebulae (RNe) (cite: Hubble1922)." (Motivation); "For the specific goal of this work, the cross-matching distance between a YSO and RN can be empirically estimated as the length scale from where ambient gas can be accreted onto an isolated star (cite: Bondi1944; Throop2008; Padoan2014)." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
- Alternative: Tidal interactions in binary/multiple stellar systems as an alternative origin for the large-scale spirals and streamers around HD 142527 and S CrA  
  > "As discussed in Section 2, such elongated structures can form due to the late infall of material, but they could also be a consequence of a binary interaction." (ALMA archive search:); "The two sources we found with large-scale structures, HD 142527 and S CrA (Section 3), are binaries, and thus some of the structures we observe around them (Figure 3, panel a and c) could be due to tidal interactions between protostars and surrounding gas." (Alternative explanations for large-scale structures)
- Alternative: Close encounters (stellar flybys) by neighbouring YSOs as an alternative cause of large-scale structures  
  > "Furthermore, such structures can also be created due to close encounters by neighbouring YSOs, as predicted by several hydrodynamic simulations (cite: Cuello2019; Vorobyov2020) and likely observed for a few sources (cite: Dong2022)." (Alternative explanations for large-scale structures)
- Alternative: Gravitational instabilities (GI) in massive disks as an alternative cause of spiral structures  
  > "Gravitational instabilities can be another possible way to form spiral-like structures, if the disks are massive enough (cite: Dong2015), as generally inferred by Toomre’s Q parameter (cite: Toomre1964)." (Alternative explanations for large-scale structures)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Existing reflection nebulae catalogues are significantly incomplete (missing e.g. SU Aur and HD 100546), preventing rigorous statistical conclusions.  
  > "The fact that $\gtrsim 40\%$ of RNe were not detected before suggests that the previous RNe catalogues were rather incomplete." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "The incompleteness of the available RNe catalogues is a major obstacle in providing reliable statistics at the moment (see discussion in Sec. 3)." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Applying a single median extinction correction (-0.31) to spectral indices for Marton et al. (2016) sources is a rough approximation that ignores individual source extinction.  
  > "We note that this method only provides a rough estimate of $\alpha'$ as it does not account for extinction values for individual sources." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae)
- Limitation (authors): The 2000 au cross-matching threshold is a lower limit that ignores the large physical extents (10^3 - 10^5 au) and asymmetric shapes of RNe as well as variations in stellar mass and velocity.  
  > "We note that this distance threshold is a lower limit for an interaction between clouds and YSOs because we do not account for the sizes of RNe." (Class II sources near reflection nebulae); "The exact distances between the centres of YSOs and RNe that result in late infall can vary greatly for different sources depending on their stellar (mass and velocity) and RNe properties (size and shape)" (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial filtering and foreground cloud absorption in interferometric 12CO observations can obscure large-scale structures, as seen in HD 97048.  
  > "However, significant negative emission was observed in the central channels (Figure 6, channels 5.32 to 3.57 km s$^{-1}$), suggesting that the source may be surrounded by large-scale gas that may absorb or otherwise obscure, through spatial filtering, kilo-au features around the star." (ALMA archive search:)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No kinematic modelling (e.g., PV diagrams or streamline fitting) was performed on the newly identified S CrA streamer in this Letter to confirm whether the gas is infalling, outflowing, or tidally stripped by the binary.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 3 new candidate sources had archival ALMA CO data meeting the LAS and sensitivity criteria, representing a very small sample size for testing the RNe selection method.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 2D sky-projected separations between YSOs and RNe do not guarantee 3D physical proximity required for Bondi-Hoyle accretion.
