## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and mosaic KCWI data and tie to MUSE**: KDRP reduction, Montage reprojection, registration by cross-correlation with MUSE, linear flux matching, Montage co-addition  
  > "The KCWI observations were reduced using the Version 1.0.1 Python implementation of the KCWI Data Extraction and Reduction Pipeline (KDRP)" (3 KCWI Data Reduction); "Instead, we performed image registration by maximizing the cross-correlation between individual KCWI fields and overlapping MUSE data in order to match the KCWI pointing astrometry to MUSE." (3.2 Image Registration)
   - software: KDRP, Montage
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux conservation checked after reprojection; KCWI flux compared to MUSE and SDSS  
  > "We validated the flux-conservation in our data before and after reprojection." (3.1 Image Re-Projection)
2. **Identify H II regions on KCWI H-beta maps**: Stellar continuum removed with LZIFU, H-beta map integrated over 4856-4876 A, regions segmented with HIIphot on the KCWI data  
  > "We therefore perform H II region identification directly on the KCWI data." (4 H II Region Catalog)
   - software: LZIFU, HIIphot
   - parameter: Same HIIphot termination gradient for every galaxy, as in Santoro et al. 2022  
  > "For each galaxy we apply the the same termination gradient" (4.2 H II Region Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit stellar continuum and emission lines, correct dust, clean sample**: Integrated KCWI and MUSE H II region spectra fit with pPXF using E-MILES SSP templates; Balmer-decrement extinction; DIG, BPT, S/N and edge cuts  
  > "We followed (cite: EmsellemMuse) and fit the emission lines simultaneously with the stellar continuum." (4.4 H II Region Stellar and Emission Line Fitting)
   - software: pPXF, PyNeb
   - parameter: Extinction curve of O'Donnell 1994  
  > "We apply the wavelength dependent extinction correction assuming a (cite: ExtinctionCorr1994ApJ...422..158O) extinction curve." (4.5 Dust Correction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure auroral line fluxes**: Monte Carlo fits of Gaussian plus linear offset to the continuum-subtracted spectrum, with [Fe II] 4360 removal for [O III] 4363  
  > "In each trial, we fit a single Gaussian (or double depending on the auroral line) plus a linear offset to the spectrum." (5 Measurement of Auroral Line Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive electron densities and temperatures**: PyNeb auroral-to-nebular ratio diagnostics with Monte Carlo uncertainties  
  > "Transitions Probabilities, Collision Strengths, and Critical Densities for the relevant emission lines used in the PyNeb Temperature Determinations." (Table of atomic references caption); "The uncertainty for each temperature measurement is the standard deviation of the distribution of temperatures constructed by Monte Carlo sampling of the error for each auroral and nebular line included in the temperature determination." (6.2 H II Region Electron Temperatures)
   - software: PyNeb
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Measure CO intensity, peak temperature and velocity dispersion inside each H II region**: Moment 0 and 2 of the ALMA 12m+7m+TP cubes, integrating the spectrum over pixels inside each H II region footprint, with a signal mask around the peak channel  
  > "Using the ALMA 12m+7m+TP datacubes, we calculate moment 0, and 2 (integrated intensity and velocity dispersion) for molecular gas near each H II region." (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion); "To measure the CO spectra, we reproject the H II region masks onto the grid of the ALMA datacubes and integrate the ALMA spectra for pixels located inside the footprint of each H II region." (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
   - parameter: Integration window around peak channel using channels above 1 sigma noise  
  > "we locate the velocity channel which contains the peak emission and construct an integration window around this channel by including velocity channels with signal above the $1\sigma$ noise." (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
   - assumption: Integrating CO within the H II region boundary captures molecular gas closest in projection to, and likely affected by, the ionized gas  
  > "We instead choose to integrate the ALMA spectrum contained in the H II region boundaries in order to measure the properties of the molecular gas closest in projection to the the ionized gas" (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with H II region-GMC velocity dispersions of Zakardjian et al.; ranges agree  
  > "which suggests that this method of extracting and measuring the CO properties is reasonable." (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
7. **Match HST clusters and associations to H II regions**: Clusters selected by position inside the footprint; associations matched as in Scheuermann et al. 2023; youngest and most massive match assigned  
  > "we assign to each H II region the age (mass) of the youngest (most massive) available cluster/association." (6.5 H II Region Compact Clusters and Associations)
   - assumption: Youngest and most massive populations dominate the ionization  
  > "because we expect that the youngest and most massive clusters or associations contribute most to the overall ionization of the H II region" (6.5 H II Region Compact Clusters and Associations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Fit Te-Te relations and test correlations with environment**: LINMIX Bayesian linear regression with intrinsic scatter for significant Te-Te relations; Pearson and Spearman correlation tests of temperature differences against ionization parameter, CO moments and stellar properties  
  > "we derive the best fitting linear relation using the Bayesian linear regression tool LINMIX" (7.1 Temperature-Temperature Relations); "To gauge the significance and monotonicity of each comparison we calculate the Pearson correlation coefficient" (7.3 Temperature Differences Compared to H II Region Ionized Gas, Stellar Population, and Molecular Gas Properties)
   - software: LINMIX
   - fitting: LINMIX_ERR linear mixture model with intrinsic scatter; sampler and priors not stated  
  > "The linear regression in LINMIX includes an additional term to represent the intrinsic scatter weighting each data point." (7.1 Temperature-Temperature Relations)
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Search high Te[O III] regions for evidence of shocks**: Compare [S II]/H-alpha, [O I]/H-alpha, He II/H-beta and [O III] line widths against CO velocity dispersion  
  > "To test this explanation, we search for evidence of shocks in enhanced optical line ratios and line broadening." (7.3.4 Investigating H II Regions for Presence of Low-Velocity Shocks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Software

- **KDRP (KCWI Data Extraction and Reduction Pipeline)** 1.0.1: KCWI data reduction to flux-calibrated cubes  
  > "The KCWI observations were reduced using the Version 1.0.1 Python implementation of the KCWI Data Extraction and Reduction Pipeline (KDRP)" (3 KCWI Data Reduction)
- **Montage** : Reprojection of KCWI cubes onto a square pixel grid and co-addition into mosaics  
  > "We reprojected the cubes onto a square pixel grid using the astronomical mosaic software" (3.1 Image Re-Projection)
- **LZIFU** : Stellar continuum removal and H-beta map construction for H II region identification  
  > "an emission line fitting code designed specifically for use with IFUs" (4.1 Construction of H-beta Maps)
- **HIIphot** : H II region segmentation of KCWI H-beta maps  
  > "was designed to identify H II regions and complexes (unresolved or blended H II regions) while also minimizing the inclusion of surrounding diffuse ionized gas (or DIG)." (4.2 H II Region Identification)
- **pPXF** : Joint stellar-continuum and emission-line fits of integrated KCWI and MUSE H II region spectra  
  > "we can input a wavelength dependent LSF function as well as fix the kinematics between emission lines of doublets and lines with similar levels of ionization." (4.4 H II Region Stellar and Emission Line Fitting)
- **PyNeb** : Electron density, temperature and extinction calculations  
  > "used in the PyNeb Temperature Determinations." (Table of atomic references caption)
- **LINMIX** : Bayesian linear regression of Te-Te relations  
  > "we derive the best fitting linear relation using the Bayesian linear regression tool LINMIX" (7.1 Temperature-Temperature Relations)
- **PHANGS-MUSE Data Analysis Pipeline** : MUSE spectral fitting and analysis in the PHANGS-MUSE reduction  
  > "and spectral fitting and analysis was performed using the Data Analysis Pipeline" (2.3 Multi-Unit Spectroscopic Explorer)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining KCWI+MUSE H II regions with PHANGS-HST and PHANGS-ALMA allows testing of environmental drivers of Te  
  > "The KCWI+MUSE H II regions combined with the PHANGS-HST and PHANGS-ALMA observations allow us to investigate how" (7.3 Temperature Differences Compared to H II Region Ionized Gas, Stellar Population, and Molecular Gas Properties)
- Why this method: CO integrated inside the H II region boundary instead of nearest-neighbour matching to molecular clouds, to capture gas closest in projection to the ionized gas  
  > "We instead choose to integrate the ALMA spectrum contained in the H II region boundaries in order to measure the properties of the molecular gas closest in projection to the the ionized gas" (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
- Alternative: Matching H II regions to molecular clouds via a nearest-neighbour algorithm was considered but not used  
  > "One possibility is to match H II regions to molecular clouds via a nearest-neighbor algorithm" (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Molecular and ionized gas are not co-spatial at the resolution used  
  > "Because molecular and ionized gas are not entirely co-spatial at our resolution, it is necessary to make a selection to capture gas near the H II region." (6.3 ALMA-CO: Intensity, Peak Temperature, and Velocity Dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): H II regions are not fully resolved, so surface brightness is PSF-averaged and the density-inhomogeneity link is uncertain  
  > "we acknowledge that the H II regions are not fully resolved which means we are measuring a PSF-averaged surface brightness." (7.2 The Impact of Density Inhomogeneities on [S II] and [O II] Temperatures)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO velocity dispersion is measured at ~1.3 arcsec (tens to ~100 pc) inside a mask defined at KCWI resolution, so it blends line-of-sight CO unrelated to the H II region
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 26 regions have Te[O III]; the shock interpretation rests on a small, low-S/N (average ~4) subsample and non-significant CO correlations
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No direct ALMA-based shock tracer or CO kinematic test of expansion is made
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA enters only as one environmental covariate, so conclusions about Te are driven by the optical IFU data
