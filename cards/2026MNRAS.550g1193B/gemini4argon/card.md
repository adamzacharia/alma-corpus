## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure radio and millimetre flux densities and upper limits (including ALMA and MeerKAT)**: Download ALMA QA2 archive images and inspect a 0.5 arcsec circle at the GOTO position to measure local image rms and 3-sigma upper limits; measure MeerKAT flux density and rms at the GOTO position using pwkit/imtool on SARAO pipeline images.  
  > "We downloaded the SARAO Science Data Processor pipeline images for all observations and used the `pwkit/imtool` program (cite: Williams2017) to measure the flux density and image RMS at the GOTO position." (Radio and millimeter/submillimeter); "We downloaded the final image products from the ALMA science archive. In the Quality Assurance Level 2 (QA2) images, no significant emission is seen in a $0.5\ensuremath{''}$ circle at the GOTO position." (Radio and millimeter/submillimeter)
   - software: pwkit/imtool
   - parameter: 0.5 arcsec radius inspection circle at GOTO position for ALMA  
  > "In the Quality Assurance Level 2 (QA2) images, no significant emission is seen in a $0.5\ensuremath{''}$ circle at the GOTO position." (Radio and millimeter/submillimeter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Reduce Keck/LRIS optical spectrum and determine redshift**: Reduce and co-add exposures using PypeIt (bias subtraction, flat-fielding, cosmic ray masking, wavelength calibration, 1D extraction, flux calibration, telluric correction), correct for Milky Way extinction, and align metal absorption lines (Fe II, Mg II, Mg I) to measure redshift.  
  > "We reduce and co-add the data using the Python Spectroscopic Data Reduction Pipeline (`PypeIt`; (cite: Prochaska2020joss; Prochaska2020zonedo))." (Spectroscopy); "We determine the redshift by identifying a consistent set of metal absorption lines, including Fe ii $\lambda\lambda2344, 2374, 2382$, the Mg ii doublet $\lambda\lambda2796, 2803$, and Mg i $\lambda2852$." (Spectroscopy)
   - software: PypeIt
   - assumption: E(B-V)_MW = 0.0633 mag and R_V = 3.1 Milky Way extinction law  
  > "adopting $E(B-V)_{\rm MW}=0.0633\,\mathrm{mag}$ from the Schlafly & Finkbeiner recalibration of the Schlegel et al. dust map (cite: Schlegel1998; Schlafly2011), and an $R_V=3.1$ Milky Way extinction law (cite: FitzpatrickMassa2007)." (Spectroscopy)
   - fitting: Maximising alignment of metal absorption transitions with absorption troughs  
  > "We refine the redshift by maximising the alignment of these transitions with absorption troughs in the spectrum, yielding $z = 1.216$." (Spectroscopy)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Perform time-resolved X-ray spectral analysis**: Download and bin Swift/XRT data into S/N = 10 intervals via SWIFTTOOLS API and simultaneously fit absorbed power-law models (TBABS*ZTBABS*CFLUX*POWERLAW) across three intervals.  
  > "Using the UK Swift Science Data Centre SWIFTTOOLS API (cite: Evans2009), we downloaded and binned the data into bins with a signal-to-noise threshold of S/N = 10" (X-rays); "We fit the *Swift*/XRT spectra to a power-law model using PYXSPEC version 2.1.4 (XSPEC version 12.15.0) using Cash statistic (cite: Cash1979) and a Wilms solar abundance (cite: Wilms2000)." (X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - software: SWIFTTOOLS API, PYXSPEC, XSPEC
   - parameter: S/N = 10 binning, 0.3 - 10 keV energy range  
  > "$N_{\text{H, Intr}}$ was tied between each spectrum as it is not expected to evolve with time, while the photon index $\Gamma$ was left free to vary between spectra, as was the flux (calculated between 0.3 – 10 keV)." (X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - assumption: Wilms solar abundance, fixed N_H,Gal = 6.49e20 cm^-2, fixed z = 1.216  
  > "TBABS accounts for the neutral hydrogen in the Milky Way, fixed to $N_{\text{H, Gal}} = 6.49 \times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (cite: Willingale2013), ZTBABS parameterises the absorption contribution from the host ($N_{\text{H, Intr}}$) with redshift fixed to the spectroscopic value of 1.216" (X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - fitting: Cash statistic (CSTAT/DoF = 0.24)  
  > "The absorbed power-law model provides an adequate description of the data (CSTAT/DoF = 0.24), with no systematic residuals" (X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - validation: Tested finer binning with SNR = 5 to check for additional spectral variability  
  > "We further investigated spectral variability using finer spectral bins with SNR = 5, but found no evidence for additional spectral evolution" (X-ray Spectral Analysis)
4. **Fit single-epoch optical SED for line-of-sight host extinction**: Subtract late-time DECam host flux from LT gri photometry at t_ref = 2.035 d and fit a dust-extinguished power-law model for MW and SMC extinction laws.  
  > "To constrain the line-of-sight extinction in the host galaxy, we constructed a single-epoch optical SED at $t_{\rm ref}=2.035$ d after the trigger using nearly contemporaneous LT $gri$ photometry and late-time DECam imaging to subtract the host contribution" (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction); "We consider a Milky Way law (cite: Fitzpatrick1999) with fixed $R_V=3.1$ and an SMC Bar law (cite: Gordon2003) with $R_V=2.74$, as implemented in `dust_extinction` (cite: Gordon2024)." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
   - software: dust_extinction, scipy.optimize.least_squares
   - parameter: t_ref = 2.035 d, 5% fractional systematic flux uncertainty added  
  > "We subtracted the DECam host flux in each band from the corresponding LT flux, propagated the statistical errors in quadrature, and added a $5\%$ fractional uncertainty in flux to account for cross-calibration and host-subtraction systematics." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
   - assumption: Milky Way law with R_V = 3.1 and SMC Bar law with R_V = 2.74; z = 1.216  
  > "We consider a Milky Way law (cite: Fitzpatrick1999) with fixed $R_V=3.1$ and an SMC Bar law (cite: Gordon2003) with $R_V=2.74$, as implemented in `dust_extinction` (cite: Gordon2024)." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
   - fitting: Chi-square minimisation in flux space with X-ray-informed Gaussian prior on beta ~ N(0.40, 0.30)  
  > "We therefore adopt a broad Gaussian prior $\beta \sim \mathcal{N}(0.40,\,0.30)$, which also allows the possibility that optical and X-rays lie on the same spectral segment. We fit Eq. [\[eq:dust_pl\]](#eq:dust_pl) in flux space using $\chi^2$ minimisation (`scipy.optimize.least_squares`; (cite: Virtanen2020))" (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
   - validation: Compared with joint optical+X-ray SED fits imposing beta_X = beta_opt + 0.5  
  > "We also investigated joint optical+X-ray SED fits at the same epoch imposing $\beta_X=\beta_{\rm opt}+0.5$, as expected for a standard slow-cooling synchrotron spectrum" (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
5. **Model broadband afterglow and compare jet/energy-injection models**: Fit broadband X-ray, optical/NIR, and radio detections and upper limits with a baseline top-hat jet model (afterglowpy) and a refreshed-shock two-component jet model (twocomponent_redback_refreshed in redback).  
  > "A rapid afterglow modelling tool is provided by `afterglowpy` (cite: ryan2020), and in combination with `redback` (cite: sarin2024) this enables end-to-end modelling and inference." (`afterglowpy` Baseline Modelling); "We use a refreshed shock, two-component model (cite: lamb2017; lamb2018; lamb2020) from `redback`, the `twocomponent_redback_refreshed` from their model library" (`redback` energy-injection test and model comparison)
   - software: afterglowpy, redback, nessai
   - parameter: For twocomponent_refreshed: fixed theta_obs = 0, log epsilon_e = -1, log epsilon_B = -2, xi_N = 1  
  > "| $\theta_{\rm obs}$ (rad) | | $0, \pi/2$ | $\theta_{\rm obs}/\theta_j \leq 3/2$ | $0.07^{+0.03}_{-0.02}$ | | 0 | fixed | 0 |" (`afterglowpy` Baseline Modelling); "| $\log \varepsilon_e$ | | $-5, 0$ | $\varepsilon_e + \varepsilon_B \leq 0.9$ | $-0.26^{+0.13}_{-0.19}$ | | $-1$ | fixed | $-1$ |" (`afterglowpy` Baseline Modelling)
   - assumption: Constant-density circumburst medium (n_0), z = 1.216, flat LambdaCDM cosmology (H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7)  
  > "Synchrotron forward-shock modelling favours a constant-density medium and strongly prefers refreshed (energy-injection) emission" (Abstract); "Throughout this paper, we assume a flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $H_{0} = 70~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}~Mpc^{-1}}$, $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.3$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.7$" (Introduction)
   - fitting: Nested sampling with nessai using priors and conditions in Table 7  
  > "We sample the posterior with the nested sampler `nessai` (cite: williams2023), adopting the priors listed in Table [\[tab:pri_post\]](#tab:pri_post)." (`afterglowpy` Baseline Modelling)
   - validation: Bayesian model comparison via log Bayes factor and information score from nessai, plus 250 posterior draws overplotted on multiband light curves  
  > "The sampler, `nessai`, provides a log Evidence and an information score (cite: williams2021); despite the increased number of parameters for the `twocomponent_redback_refreshed` model, this is the preferred model." (`redback` energy-injection test and model comparison)
6. **Evaluate host-galaxy association and model candidate host SED**: Compute Bayesian host association probabilities and empirical chance-coincidence probabilities (P_cc) for LS DR10 candidates within 0.1 deg, estimate local depth limits for an undetected coincident host, match photometry to COSMOS-Web DR1, and fit the candidate host SED with Prospector.  
  > "To quantify the probability of the host galaxy association to this event, we follow the Bayesian association method of (cite: Ackley2025)." (Association); "We modelled the host galaxy of GRB 250818B with PROSPECTOR (cite: Leja2017; Johnson2021), using the FSPS stellar-population models (cite: Conroy2009; Conroy2010), a Chabrier IMF (cite: Chabrier2003), and a Calzetti foreground dust law (cite: Calzetti2000)." (`Prospector` SED Modelling)
   - software: The Tractor, Prospector, FSPS
   - parameter: 0.1 deg search radius in LS DR10; delayed-tau SFH in Prospector  
  > "We identify our candidate hosts by querying the LS DR10 catalogue within a $0.1\ensuremath{^\circ}$ cone centred on the GRB" (Association); "The star-formation history was modelled with a parametric delayed-$\tau$ form." (`Prospector` SED Modelling)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF, Calzetti foreground dust law, fixed z = 1.216 for Prospector fit  
  > "We modelled the host galaxy of GRB 250818B with PROSPECTOR (cite: Leja2017; Johnson2021), using the FSPS stellar-population models (cite: Conroy2009; Conroy2010), a Chabrier IMF (cite: Chabrier2003), and a Calzetti foreground dust law (cite: Calzetti2000). We fix the redshift to $z=1.216$ from the afterglow absorption spectrum" (`Prospector` SED Modelling)
   - fitting: Bayesian posterior sampling in Prospector using DECam griz detections and WISE/SkyMapper 5-sigma upper limits  
  > "As photometric constraints we used DECam $griz$ detections from the DESI Legacy Surveys DR10 Tractor catalogue (Table 4), together with *WISE* $W1$–$W4$ and SkyMapper $u,v$ non-detections as $5\sigma$ upper limits." (`Prospector` SED Modelling)
   - validation: Repeated Prospector fit omitting upper-limit bands and performed independent photometric-analogue check with COSMOS-Web DR1  
  > "As a check, repeating the fit using only the DECam $griz$ detections (i.e. omitting the upper-limit bands) yields consistent posteriors for $M_{\star,\mathrm{formed}}$ and $A_V$." (`Prospector` SED Modelling); "While this COSMOS-Web comparison does not constitute a direct measurement for the specific LS DR10 source, it provides an independent sanity check that the candidate host is plausibly a low-mass galaxy with at most modest ongoing star formation." (COSMOS-Web host constraints)

## Qualitative findings

- GRB 250818B is unusually luminous across X-ray, optical, and radio bands compared to typical short GRBs, and is only the third short GRB with a detected radio afterglow at z > 1.  
  > "The afterglow is unusually luminous for a nominal short GRB, lying on the bright end of the short-GRB population in X-rays and optical and among the most luminous high-redshift short-GRB afterglows in the radio." (Abstract); "GRB 250818B is only the third short GRB to have a detected radio afterglow at $z > 1$" (Radio afterglow brightness in context)
- A single-component top-hat forward-shock model fails to reproduce the full X-ray and late-time optical/radio evolution, whereas a refreshed-shock two-component jet model is strongly preferred.  
  > "A single-component top-hat jet provides a reasonable overall description but does not reproduce the full broadband evolution, including mismatches in the X-ray light curve and late-time optical/radio behaviour. Bayesian model comparison favours a refreshed two-component jet scenario" (Conclusions)
- The host-galaxy association is ambiguous between an offset LS DR10 galaxy candidate (objid 5790 at 33.51 kpc) and an undetected fainter near-coincident galaxy below the LS DR10 threshold.  
  > "The host association is ambiguous: the nearest LS DR10 galaxy candidate ($r_{\rm AB}\sim24.7$) is offset by $\sim4\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim34$ kpc) with chance-alignment probability $P_{\rm cc}\sim0.2$, and current imaging does not exclude a fainter, near-coincident host." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Because public prompt-emission constraints are limited and host association is uncertain, broadband multi-wavelength follow-up (including low- and high-frequency radio/mm) is needed to anchor the synchrotron spectrum and constrain the explosion energetics and environment.  
  > "Because these scenarios imply very different physical offsets (and therefore limit the use of offset-based arguments), we place particular emphasis on constraints that arise directly from the broadband afterglow data." (Introduction); "In particular, when prompt-emission constraints are weak or unavailable, the afterglow becomes the primary handle on the explosion energetics and environment." (Radio afterglow brightness in context)
- Why this method: A refreshed two-component jet model was used because the baseline single-component top-hat model failed to reproduce the X-ray evolution and late-time optical/radio excess; an XRT-informed beta prior was used in optical SED fitting to break the beta-A_V degeneracy across three bands.  
  > "To reduce the strong $\beta$–$A_V$ degeneracy inherent to three-band photometry, we incorporate an X-ray-informed prior on $\beta$." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction); "A single-component top-hat jet provides a reasonable overall fit but does not reproduce the full evolution: it exhibits a noticeable mismatch to the X-ray light curve from $\sim 0.02$ d, underpredicts the optical emission at $\gtrsim 3$ d, and underpredicts the 36.8 GHz detection at $\sim 4$ – $5$ d (noting the large uncertainty on this point)." (Afterglow brightness and broadband interpretation)
- Alternative: More complex X-ray spectral models (rejected because the simple absorbed power-law model gave CSTAT/DoF = 0.24 with no systematic residuals and limited photon statistics).  
  > "The absorbed power-law model provides an adequate description of the data (CSTAT/DoF = 0.24), with no systematic residuals; given the limited photon statistics, we therefore do not explore more complex spectral models." (X-ray Spectral Analysis)
- Alternative: M14/LMC dust extinction law for the optical afterglow SED (rejected because it is undefined below rest-frame 3000 Angstrom, which is probed by observed g and r bands at z = 1.216).  
  > "We do not consider the M14/LMC family further because it is undefined below $\lambda_{\rm rest}\sim3000$ Å, whereas at $z=1.216$ the observed $g$ and $r$ bands probe $\lambda_{\rm rest}\lesssim3000$ Å." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
- Alternative: Joint optical+X-ray SED fits at t = 2 d with fixed cooling-break slope difference (rejected as baseline because the 1 keV X-ray point at 2 d is only marginal and introduces extra assumptions that dominate weak constraints).  
  > "However, at $t\simeq2$ d the contemporaneous 1 keV flux is only a marginal detection and does not meet the quality cuts adopted for our X-ray analysis." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction); "We therefore adopt the optical-only fit with the XRT-informed $\beta$ prior as our baseline." (Optical SED and line-of-sight extinction)
- Alternative: Single-component top-hat forward-shock model and reverse-shock component for late variability (rejected because the top-hat model is disfavoured by Bayesian model comparison and excess emission occurs after the afterglow peak, ruling out a reverse shock).  
  > "Bayesian model comparison prefers the refreshed two-component model over the single-component top-hat fit" (Afterglow brightness and broadband interpretation); "as the excess emission is seen at times later than the afterglow peak, we can rule out any reverse shock component for the afterglow variability." (Afterglow brightness and broadband interpretation)
