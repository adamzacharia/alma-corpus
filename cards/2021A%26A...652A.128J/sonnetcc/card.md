## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALCS cubes with common beam**: tclean cubes, natural weighting, primary-beam corrected, uv-tapered so all stacked sources share a similar beam  
  > "Data cubes were imaged using the CASA task TCLEAN down to a level of" (2.1 ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey); "maps so that all the stacked sources share a similar beam size, and hence a similar spatial resolution" (2.1 ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: channel size 60 km/s; taper 2 arcsec; pixel 0.16 arcsec  
  > "with a channel size of 60 km s$^{-1}$" (2.1 ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey); "with a tapering parameter of 2 arcsec (pixel size of 0.16 arcsec)" (2.1 ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: stacking also repeated on cubes at native resolution (appendix)  
  > "we also performed our stacking analyses on cubes in their native resolution" (2.1 ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey)
2. **Select the stacking sample and sub-samples**: merge catalogues in the redshift windows where [C II] falls in the bandwidth, de-duplicate within 1 arcsec, keep only sources not individually detected  
  > "only sources with spectroscopic redshifts or high precision photometric redshifts" (2.2.1 Sample selection); "sources are removed if they are closer than 1 arcsec from one another" (2.2.1 Sample selection); "we only select sources that are not individually detected in our ALMA data" (2.2.1 Sample selection)
   - software: AstroQuery
   - parameter: 52 sources (23 multiple images); good-z sub-sample of 36 spectroscopic sources; high-SFR sub-sample above the median SFR; SFR-z sub-sample is the intersection  
  > "After these considerations we selected a total of 52 sources (of which 23 are multiple images of same sources)" (2.2.1 Sample selection); "one sub-sample composed of only 36 sources with secure, spectroscopically determined redshifts" (2.2.1 Sample selection); "greater than the median SFR of the full sample" (2.2.1 Sample selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive SFR, stellar mass and magnification-corrected properties**: SExtractor catalogues and IRAC photometry, lensing magnification correction, SED fitting with BAGPIPES using four star formation histories; SFR from dust-corrected UV luminosity  
  > "We built our catalogues using SExtractor v2.19.5 (cite: SExtractor) in double image mode" (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting); "we ran BAGPIPES (cite: BAGPIPES) with four different star formation histories (SFHs): a burst, a constant, an exponential, and a combination of a young burst with a constant SFH" (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting); "The SFRs quoted in the rest of this paper were computed using the UV luminosity and corrected for dust attenuation (method (ii))." (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
   - software: SExtractor, TinyTim, GALFIT, BAGPIPES
   - parameter: age between 0 and 1.0 Gyr  
  > "the parameter spaces allowed for the age of the component, defined as the beginning of the star formation, was between 0 and 1.0 Gyr" (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
   - parameter: magnification from the online Frontier Fields magnification calculator (CATS models) and CLASH models  
  > "we used the online magnification calculator" (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting); "For the CLASH clusters, we used the updated models from (cite: Zitrin2015)." (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
   - assumption: IMF from Kroupa and Boily 2002  
  > "The IMF used in BAGPIPES is from (cite: KroupaandBoily2002)." (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
   - fitting: Bayesian SED fitting with BAGPIPES; best SFH chosen with the Bayesian information criterion  
  > "we applied the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) accounting for the difference in the number of parameters used in each SFH" (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
   - validation: stacked best-fit SEDs (mean and median) used to recover SFR_UV as a cross-check  
  > "we stacked all the best-fit SEDs of the sources obtained with BAGPIPES together (both using mean and median stacking) to recover the SFR$_\mathrm{UV}$" (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
4. **Stack cubes and continuum maps**: LINESTACKER pixel-by-pixel and channel-by-channel weighted-average and median stacking, aligned on redshift and [C II] rest frequency; same sources stacked in the continuum  
  > "Sources were stacked pixel to pixel and spectral channel to spectral channel. Both weighted average and median stacking were used." (3 Stacking method); "we performed continuum stacking of the same sources in the same way" (3 Stacking method)
   - software: LINESTACKER, CASA
   - parameter: weights proportional to inverse square of local RMS, noise estimated in an area of 12.96 arcsec^2 (81x81 pixels) around the source, sources not masked  
  > "we weighted each source proportionally to the inverse of the square of the local RMS" (3 Stacking method); "local noise was estimated in an area of 12.96 arcsec" (3 Stacking method)
   - parameter: sources discarded if the cube edge is closer than one beam from the stack centre  
  > "Sources are discarded if the cube edge is closer than one beam from the stack centre." (4.1 [C II] stack results)
   - assumption: stacking positions from catalogue coordinates; line aligned using catalogue redshift and [C II] rest frequency  
  > "Spatially, sources were stacked using their physical coordinate extracted from the corresponding catalogue." (3 Stacking method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: no bootstrapping or significance tests, because nothing is detected  
  > "Because none of the stacking analyses lead to a detection we do not perform the usual statistical tests designed to probe the significance of the stacking result." (4.1 [C II] stack results)
5. **Moment-0 maps and L[CII] upper limits**: moment-0 over 540 km/s centred on the expected line; 3 sigma of the stacked moment-0 map converted to L[CII] and divided by the sample (weighted-mean or median) magnification  
  > "The stacking results are presented as moment-0 maps, integrated over 540 km s$^{-1}$" (4.1 [C II] stack results); "line luminosity is then corrected for the weighted mean magnification of the studied sample when performing weighted-mean stacking, or the median magnification when performing median stacking" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
   - software: LINESTACKER
   - parameter: integration width about twice the ALPINE median FWHM of ~252 km/s  
  > "We decided to integrate over roughly two times their median linewidth in the moment-0 maps" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
   - assumption: L[CII] formula of Solomon and Vanden Bout 2005  
  > "is computed using the formula provided in (cite: SolomonVandenBout2005)" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: comparison with the local L[CII]-SFR relation and ALPINE  
  > "While upper limits from the median stacking analyses are still compatible with the local relationship" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
6. **Upper limit on molecular gas mass and gas fraction**: apply the Madden et al. L[CII]-to-M_H2 recipe to the L[CII] upper limits of the two extreme (sub-)samples, compare with SED stellar masses  
  > "to estimate the total molecular gas mass of low-metallicity dwarf galaxies" (4.1 [C II] stack results); "This range was derived using the two sample extremes" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
   - assumption: sub-solar metallicity of the sample  
  > "and with the assumption that the sample sources have sub-solar metallicity" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: compared with Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. gas fractions and Walter et al. extrapolation  
  > "Our mean results are mostly in good accordance with the average molecular gas fraction derived by (cite: Dessauges2020)" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
7. **Continuum upper limits on L_FIR, obscured SFR and dust mass**: 3 sigma continuum upper limit converted to L_FIR with a modified blackbody (T = 35 and 45 K) corrected for CMB, then SFR_IR and dust mass  
  > "well described by a modified blackbody with a temperature of $T=35$ K and $T=45$ K" (4.2 Continuum); "we adopt the prescription from (cite: daCunha13) to correct for the effect of the CMB" (4.2 Continuum); "Similarly, using the continuum upper limit we estimate an upper limit on the dust mass." (4.2 Continuum)
   - parameter: emissivity beta = 1.5; T_CMB ~ 20 K  
  > "both with dust emissivity $\beta = 1.5$" (4.2 Continuum); "the CMB temperature is $T_\mathrm{CMB}\sim20$ K" (4.2 Continuum)
   - assumption: SFR_IR ~ 1.1e-10 L_FIR (Kroupa IMF, constant SF over 100 Myr)  
  > "constant star formation over a timescale of 100 Myr; (cite: calzetti11)" (4.2 Continuum)
   - assumption: dust opacity kappa0 = 5.1 cm2/g at 1.2 THz, beta = 1.5 (alternative kappa0 = 0.43 cm2/g at 350 GHz)  
  > "$\kappa_0 = 5.1$ cm$^2$/g for $\nu_0=1.2$THz (cite: DraineLi07)" (4.2 Continuum); "If we assume $\kappa_0 =0.43$ cm$^2$/g for $\nu_0 = 350$ GHz (cite: Abergel14)" (4.2 Continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- No [C II] detection in the full sample or any sub-sample  
  > "The stacking analyses yielded non-detections for the full sample and all sub-samples." (4.1 [C II] stack results)

  > "upper limits derived from the mean stacking analyses seem to indicate a lower value of" (4.1 [C II] stack results)

  > "we would have expected at least a tentative detection in our stacked results" (5.2 Comparison to individual detections in high-z, low-SFR galaxies)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Cluster fields give lensing amplification to reach faint sub-L* z>6 galaxies  
  > "observations can be done more efficiently by targeting galaxy cluster fields as gravitational lensing will amplify the light from such distant galaxies" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Stacking improves sensitivity for individually undetected galaxies  
  > "the stacking of the data can enable a significant statistical improvement in the sensitivity" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Maximising the stacked line flux by randomising redshift offsets (Monte Carlo) was not done because with a non-detection it would maximise noise peaks  
  > "One could otherwise try to maximise the stacked line flux by randomising redshift offsets as a Monte Carlo process." (Footnote 8)
- Alternative: Bootstrapping and other significance tests were not performed for the non-detections  
  > "such tests would not serve much purpose if the stacked source is undetected" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
- How they decided it was enough: No further sub-samples because of the clear non-detection  
  > "given the clear non-detection we do not pursue further sub-samples" (4.1 [C II] stack results)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic offsets between Ly-alpha and [C II] redshifts may smear the stack and underestimate the upper limits  
  > "The first and probably main bias could come from systematic redshift uncertainties." (5.1 Potential biases)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is heterogeneous (several catalogues, different selections)  
  > "leading to a heterogeneous sample" (5.1 Potential biases)
- Limitation (authors): Multiple images of the same source reduce the number of independent objects  
  > "this intrinsically reduces the statistical significance of our results" (5.1 Potential biases)
- Limitation (authors): 19 sources not detected in HST, so no SFRs; stellar masses available for only about 2/3 of the sources  
  > "19 sources, mostly Lyman-alpha emitters from the MUSE GTO catalogues, are not detected in any of the HST bands and we could hence not derive reliable SFRs." (2.2.2 Sample properties and SED fitting)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits depend strongly on adopted lens models and per-source SFR (mean vs median differ mainly through magnification)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Individual redshift offsets (Ly-alpha vs [C II]) are not modelled; no injection-recovery test of the stack
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample of Ly-alpha emitters is biased and heterogeneous, so conclusions about the general z~6 population are weak
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single tapered beam assumed to cover all sources; source sizes and extended [C II] not tested beyond a native-resolution check
