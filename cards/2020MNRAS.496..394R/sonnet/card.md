## Analysis recipe

1. **Optical depth from isotopologue ratios**: Per-pixel, per-channel brightness temperature ratios of maps convolved to a common beam, solved for tau assuming equal Tex  
  > "assume that the excitation temperature is the same for both molecules" (3.1 Optical Depth)
   - parameter: [CO/13CO]=68±20, [CO/C18O]=570±130; masks 6/4/3 sigma for 12CO/13CO/C18O  
  > "$6\sigma$ for $^{12}$CO, $4\sigma$ for $^{13}$CO, and $3\sigma$ for C$^{18}$O" (Appendix: Optical depth maps)
   - assumption: same Tex for both species  
  > "assume that the excitation temperature is the same for both molecules" (3.1 Optical Depth)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Molecular column densities**: LTE column density with optical-depth correction factor, using JPL/LAMDA molecular parameters  
  > "is a correction factor for non-zero optical depth (cite: goldsmith1999)" (3.2 Molecular column density)
   - parameter: Tex = 20 K; median in 1.2"x0.9" ellipse, PA 60 deg  
  > "To compute the column density, we assume an excitation temperature, $T_{ex}=20$ K." (3.2 Molecular column density)
   - assumption: single Tex=20 K; higher or variable Tex changes results by factor <~2  
  > "changes our results by a factor of $\lesssim 2$" (3.2 Molecular column density)
   - fitting: Q(Tex) third-order polynomial fit to JPL values for variable-Tex test  
  > "we take the discrete values of $Q(T_{ex})$ for each isotopologue given in the JPL Spectral Line Catalog (cite: pickett1998) and fit a third-order polynomial." (Appendix: Column density maps)
   - validation: compared with variable Tex from brightness temperature; ratio <~2  
  > "values typically vary by a factor of $\lesssim 2$." (Appendix: Column density maps)
3. **Gas mass from C18O and globulette mass**: Mass = N(C18O)*[H2/C18O]*mu*m(H2)*pi*a_min*a_max  
  > "This gives a gas mass of $\sim 0.6$ M$_{\odot}$." (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: [H2/C18O]=4.85e6, mu=1.36, a_min~0.9", a_max~1.2"  
  > "$\left[\mathrm{H_2}/\mathrm{C^{18}O} \right] = 4.85 \times 10^6$ is the abundance of H$_2$ compared to C$^{18}$O" (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - assumption: C18O optically thin; lower limit if isotope-selective photodissociation  
  > "If isotope-selective photodissociation has reduced the abundance of C$^{18}$O, then this mass estimate will be a lower limit." (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Outflow properties**: Integrate 12CO J=2-1 line wings (|v-v0|>3.5 km/s) in lobe regions; velocity-moment method; dynamical age, mass-loss rate and momentum rate  
  > "We directly integrate the profile in the line wings — $|v-v_0|>3.5~\mbox{km~s$^{-1}$}$ — and correct for the low velocity outflow material following (cite: margulis1985)." (3.5 Molecular outflow)
   - parameter: Tex=100 K, [H2/CO]=1e4, optically thin wings  
  > "a single excitation temperature $T_{\rm ex}=100$ K, and a \[H$_2$/CO\] abundance ratio of $10^{4}$" (3.5 Molecular outflow)
   - assumption: inclination i<=62.5 deg from Cabrit; no inclination correction applied in table  
  > "we conclude that $i\le62.5^\circ$." (3.5 Molecular outflow)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tex=50 K lowers mass by ~40%  
  > "Using $T_{\rm ex}=50$ K decreases the mass estimate by $\sim40\%$." (3.5 Molecular outflow)
5. **Continuum flux, spectral index and dust masses**: Flux within 0.3" radius at four continuum bands; weighted least-squares power-law fit; spherical dusty core model rho~r^-2 with T(r)=T0 (r/r0)^-0.4; whole-globule mass from Band 6 continuum at 45 K  
  > "We obtain the best-fit model by minimizing the squared differences between the model and the fluxes, weighted by the inverse of their squared uncertainties." (3.6 Dust continuum emission)
   - parameter: nu0=230.61 GHz, kappa0=0.8 cm2/g, beta=1.0, gas-to-dust 100, r0=690 AU, 15% flux uncertainty  
  > "a $\beta=1.0$, and a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100." (3.6 Dust continuum emission)
   - assumption: 15 K for compact core; 45 K for extended emission  
  > "We adopt a higher temperature for the extended emission around the compact source (45 K, see Section [3.4](#s:tadpole_temp)) to compute a mass of $1.9~M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$" (3.6 Dust continuum emission)
   - fitting: chi-square minimisation, 1 sigma errors following Lampton et al.  
  > "we derive 1$\sigma$ error bars in the spectral index of $\pm0.5$ (cite: lampton1976)" (3.6 Dust continuum emission)
   - validation: ALMA flux recovery compared with APEX (~70%); fluxes treated as lower bounds  
  > "our ALMA observations recover approximately a 70% from the tadpole emission." (Appendix: Maximum recoverable scale)
6. **Virial mass, free-fall velocity and globule lifetime**: Virial mass for self-gravitating sphere with n=2 or constant density; lifetime = mass / constant photoevaporation rate from Paper I; P-V diagrams for kinematics  
  > "We estimate the virial mass of the tadpole globule as a whole assuming that it is a self-gravitating sphere." (3.8 Virial mass estimate)
   - parameter: DeltaV=1.4 km/s (C18O J=2-1), R=3000 AU  
  > "$\Delta V =1.4$ km s$^{-1}$ is the linewidth of C$^{18}$O J=2-1, $R=3000$ AU is the mean radius of the cloud" (3.8 Virial mass estimate)
   - assumption: constant photoevaporation rate  
  > "Assuming a constant photoevaporation rate, a 1.9 $\text{M}_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ globule will be completely ablated in $\sim 4$ Myr." (5 Globule survival)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of the bipolar molecular outflow in 12CO J=2-1, only inside the globule, joining the irradiated outflow.  
  > "our data reveal for the first time the bipolar molecular outflow in CO, seen only inside the globule" (abstract)
- CO lines are optically thick yet show no self-absorption, explained by a positive radial temperature gradient (external heating).  
  > "a positive radial thermal profile can explain the lack of self-absorption" (3.4 CO emission)
- 13CO and C18O P-V diagrams show C-shaped morphology; global dynamics unclear.  
  > "although the global dynamical behaviour of the gas remains unclear" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Only ALMA offers the angular resolution at southern declination to detect an embedded protostar and resolve the globule structure and kinematics.  
  > "only the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) provides the requisite angular resolution to detect an embedded protostar and the structure and kinematics of the surrounding globule." (Introduction)
- Why this method: C18O used for mass as least optically thick isotopologue; continuum used as most optically thin diagnostic.  
  > "We estimate the molecular mass of the tadpole from the C$^{18}$O as this is the least optically thick of the CO isotopologues." (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
- Alternative: Band 7 continuum not used for globule mass because the head size is a significant fraction of the MRS; Band 6 only.  
  > "To compute the mass of the entire globule, we use only the Band 6 continuum emission." (3.6 Dust continuum emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single excitation temperature is a large source of uncertainty.  
  > "Assuming a single excitation temperature is a large source of uncertainty as this is often a poor assumption" (3.2 Molecular column density)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral index poorly constrained because bands 6 and 7 are adjacent; fluxes and globule mass are lower bounds.  
  > "with adjacent bands (6 and 7) not being optimal to estimate accurate spectral indices." (3.6 Dust continuum emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass depends strongly on assumed dust temperature (1.9 vs 7.3 Msun), so lifetime is uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Constant photoevaporation rate from Paper I is imported without independent check
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral index uncertainty ±0.5 allows optically thick or beta~0 explanations; no SED peak constraint
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow parameters assume Tex=100 K and uncorrected inclination, which conflicts with the jet inclination
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing flux (~30%) affects extended emission masses
