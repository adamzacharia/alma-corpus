## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum images and CO cubes**: Image calibrated data with natural weighting; combine all spectral windows for continuum and use line-tuned windows for cubes; subtract detected continuum with uvcontsub.  
  > "The data was imaged both as continuum and spectral cube using natural weighting." (ALMA observations and data analysis); "A continuum image was produced combining all spectral windows, while a spectral cube was constructed for the spectral windows tuned to the redshifted CO line." (ALMA observations and data analysis); "The casa task `uvcontsub` was used to subtract the continuum from *uv* data for sources for which the continuum was detected." (ALMA observations and data analysis)
   - software: casa, uvcontsub
   - parameter: natural weighting  
  > "The data was imaged both as continuum and spectral cube using natural weighting." (ALMA observations and data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Per-source achieved rms and synthesized beams reported in Table 2.  
  > "The rms sensitivity and the synthesized beam size achieved by imaging with a natural weighting scheme are given in Table [2](#tab:obssum)." (ALMA observations and data analysis)
2. **Measure CO emission**: Estimate centers from moment-0 maps; fit a single Gaussian to obtain redshift, peak flux, linewidth, and integrated intensity.  
  > "Galaxy centers were estimated from the moment-0 maps." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum); "The emission lines were fitted with a single Gaussian line profile to estimate the redshift, peak flux density, line width, and integrated intensity." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - parameter: Nondetection peak and integrated-intensity limits: 3σ, assuming 300 km s−1 linewidth.  
  > "For the four nondetections, 3$\sigma$ upper limits of the peak flux density and integrated intensity (assuming a line width of 300 km s$^{-1}$) are reported in Table [4](#tab:results)." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - assumption: Single Gaussian line profile.  
  > "The emission lines were fitted with a single Gaussian line profile" (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian; optimizer and likelihood not stated.  
  > "The emission lines were fitted with a single Gaussian line profile" (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure millimeter continuum**: Fit 2D Gaussian profiles to continuum maps for flux densities and sizes.  
  > "The continuum flux density and size were measured by fitting 2D Gaussian profile to the maps." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - parameter: Continuum nondetections: 5σ upper limits, assuming compact sources.  
  > "$5\sigma$ upper limits for the continuum are calculated assuming the source is compact." (Table 4)
   - assumption: Compact source for continuum upper limits.  
  > "$5\sigma$ upper limits for the continuum are calculated assuming the source is compact." (Table 4)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian profile; optimizer and likelihood not stated.  
  > "The continuum flux density and size were measured by fitting 2D Gaussian profile to the maps." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Convert CO fluxes to molecular gas masses**: Calculate L′CO from integrated flux, observed frequency, luminosity distance and redshift; convert to CO(1-0) and apply αCO.  
  > "L^{'}_{\rm CO}=3.25\times10^7\,S_{\rm CO}\,\Delta v\,\nu_{\rm obs}^{-2}\,D_{\rm L}^2\, (1+z)^{-3}," (CO Emission Lines and Continuum); "The CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor, $\alpha_{\rm CO}$, relates the CO(1-0) luminosity to total molecular gas mass." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - parameter: L′CO(3-2)/L′CO(1-0) = 0.97; L′CO(4-3)/L′CO(1-0) = 0.87.  
  > "Utilizing line ratios of $L_{\rm CO(3-2)}^{'}$ / $L_{\rm CO(1-0)}^{'}$ = 0.97 and $L_{\rm CO(4-3)}^{'}$ / $L_{\rm CO(1-0)}^{'}$ = 0.87, as recommended for QSOs (cite: Carilli2013), we derived the CO(1-0) line luminosities." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - parameter: αCO = 0.8 M⊙ (K km s−1 pc²)−1.  
  > "We adopted $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 0.8\,\rm M_\odot$(K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$)$^{-1}$ for our galaxies" (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - assumption: ΛCDM: H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, ΩM = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7.  
  > "Throughout this work, we assume a Lambda cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) cosmology (cite: Komatsu2011) with $H_0 = 70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm M} = 0.3$, and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Starburst nature and high merger fraction motivate αCO.  
  > "given the starburst nature (cite: Fan2019) and the high merger fraction (cite: Fan2016a) of Hot DOGs." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare CO-based masses with dust-based predictions in Fan2016b.  
  > "Our estimations of molecular gas mass based on CO line observations is 0.56 dex higher than the values predicted by dust mass assuming a Milky Way dust-to-gas ratio of 0.01 in (cite: Fan2016b)." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
5. **Assemble UV-to-millimeter photometry**: Combine survey photometry and inherited IR measurements with ALMA continuum; measure W1/W2 aperture fluxes and inverse-variance-image errors.  
  > "The WISE W1 and W2 flux densities were obtained through aperture photometry on the WISE images [^9] (cite: Lang2014; Meisner2017), and the errors were estimated based on the inverse variance images." (UV to Millimeter Spectral Energy Distribution Data); "The IR broadband photometry at wavelengths ranging from 12 $\mu$m to the millimeter band were directly collected from their parent samples as reported in (cite: Fan2016b)." (UV to Millimeter Spectral Energy Distribution Data); "The ALMA continuum observations at rest-frame 3 mm have been included to constrain the cold dust component more accurately." (UV to Millimeter Spectral Energy Distribution Data)
   - software: NED, https://unwise.me/
   - parameter: W0248+2705, W0615-5716, W1248-2154 lack optical-NIR data.  
  > "The 3 remaining Hot DOGs, namely W0248+2705, W0615-5716, and W1248-2154, currently lack optical-NIR data." (UV to Millimeter Spectral Energy Distribution Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit stellar, dust, and AGN SED components**: BayeSED V3.0 three-component fitting; PCA compresses libraries, ANN or K-nearest-neighbor interpolation, MultiNest posterior sampling.  
  > "The SED of each galaxy in our sample was decomposed into three components: stellar, cold dust, and central AGN." (SED Analysis); "For SED models given as a SED library, principal component analysis is employed to reduce memory usage." (SED Analysis); "Finally, the MultiNest algorithm is employed to sample the parameter space and calculate the posterior probability distribution of the parameters." (SED Analysis)
   - software: BayeSED, MultiNest, CLUMPY
   - parameter: Graybody λ0 = 125 μm; Tdust and β free; final Tdust range 30–50 K.  
  > "where $\lambda_0$=125$\mu$m, and the dust temperature $T_{\rm dust}$ and the emissivity index $\beta$ are two free parameters in the SED fitting." (SED Analysis); "We constrained the $T_{\rm dust}$ parameter of the graybody component to be within the range 30-50 K and refitted the SEDs in our sample." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR)
   - parameter: 12 free parameters deferred to Table 3 of Fan2019; omit stellar component for three objects without optical-NIR photometry.  
  > "The 12 free parameters used in the fitting process are listed in Table 3 of (cite: Fan2019)." (SED Analysis); "For the three Hot DOGs without optical-NIR photometry, we excluded the stellar population component to prevent overfitting." (SED Analysis)
   - parameter: Upper limits encoded using negative flux-density errors.  
  > "The upper flux density limit was taken into account by setting the corresponding flux density error to a negative value according to the convention in BayeSED." (SED Analysis)
   - assumption: BC03 stellar populations, Chabrier IMF, exponentially declining SFH.  
  > "The stellar emission was modeled by using the (cite: bc03) simple stellar population library with a (cite: chabrier2003) initial mass function (IMF), and an exponential declining star formation history (SFH)." (SED Analysis)
   - assumption: Calzetti attenuation and energy balance: absorbed stellar energy entirely reemitted in IR.  
  > "The (cite: Calzetti2000a) dust attenuation law was used, and the energy of stellar emission absorbed by cold dust was assumed to be totally reemitted in the IR band." (SED Analysis)
   - assumption: CLUMPY torus for central AGN UV-IR emission.  
  > "The CLUMPY torus model (cite: Nenkova2008a; Nenkova2008b)" (SED Analysis); "has been utilized to model the UV-IR emission of the central AGN of our samples." (SED Analysis)
   - fitting: MultiNest; posterior medians and 68% intervals; final Tdust prior range 30–50 K; other priors and likelihood not stated.  
  > "Finally, the MultiNest algorithm is employed to sample the parameter space and calculate the posterior probability distribution of the parameters." (SED Analysis); "We adopted the median of the posterior probability distribution of each parameter as the fiducial value, and the uncertainties are reported as the 68% confidence intervals around the fiducial values." (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties); "We constrained the $T_{\rm dust}$ parameter of the graybody component to be within the range 30-50 K and refitted the SEDs in our sample." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR)
   - validation: Compare 30–50 K and 10–100 K fits; W0533-3401 reduced χ² improvement 0.10; reported Bayes-factor range 0.1–3.6.  
  > "Initially, we fitted our sample with BayeSED by setting the $T_{\rm dust}$ parameter range to default values (10-100K)." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR); "The reduced $\chi^2$ only improve by a value of 0.10 after we apply our prior knowledge of the $T_{\rm dust}$ for W0533-3401." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR); "We calculate the Bayes factor, defined as the difference in the Bayesian evidence between the two fitting methods, to be in the range of 0.1-3.6" (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR)
7. **Derive luminosities and star formation rates**: Integrate cold dust over 8–1000 μm and integrate the AGN component; average SFH over the last 100 Myr; calibrate SFR–LIR for objects without optical-NIR data.  
  > "The cold dust infrared luminosity $L_{\rm IR}$ was calculated by integrating the cold dust component from 8 to 1000 $\mu$m." (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties); "their AGN bolometric luminosity（$L_{\rm AGN}$）derived by integrating the AGN component of the best-fit UV to millimeter SED." (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction); "The SFRs of Hot DOGs were derived by averaging the modeled exponential SFH in the last 100 Myr." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
   - software: BayeSED
   - parameter: SFR [M⊙/yr] = 8×10−11 LIR [L⊙].  
  > "our sample generally follows the relation of SFR $[\rm M_{\odot}/\rm yr]$ = $8\times10^{-11}\,L_{\rm IR}\,[\rm L_{\odot}]$" (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
   - parameter: Use the remaining 13 Hot DOGs to calibrate SFR–LIR for the three optical-NIR nondetections.  
  > "For the three sources without an optical-NIR detection, we estimate their SFRs based on the relation between SFR and $L_{\rm IR}$ calibrated for the remaining 13 Hot DOGs." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
   - assumption: Use Tdust-constrained fitting for all physical-property analysis.  
  > "The analysis of the physical properties in this paper is entirely based on the $T_{\rm dust}$-constrained fitting." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SFR–LIR relation approximately 0.1 dex below the Chabrier-calibrated Kennicutt relation.  
  > "which is about 0.1 dex lower than the (cite: Kennicutt1998) relation calibrated by the (cite: chabrier2003) IMF." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
8. **Estimate dust masses**: Mdust = DL² Sνobs / [(1+z) κνrest B(νrest,Tdust)], with fitted Tdust and β.  
  > "M_{\rm dust}=\frac{D^2_{\rm L}}{(1+z)}\times\frac{S_{\nu_{\rm obs}}}{\kappa_{\nu_{\rm rest}} B(\nu_{\rm rest},T_{\rm dust})}," (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties)
   - parameter: κ850μm = 3.8 cm² g−1.  
  > "We adopted $\kappa_{850\mu m}$ = 3.8 cm$^2\,\rm g^{-1}$ by following (cite: Wu2014) and (cite: Fan2016b; Fan2019)." (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties)
   - assumption: κνrest = κ0(ν/ν0)^β.  
  > "$\kappa_{\nu_{\rm rest}}=\kappa_0(\nu/\nu_0)^\beta$ is the absorption coefficient at the corresponding rest-frame frequency" (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare dust masses with Fan2016b; repeat at 30 K to assess temperature sensitivity.  
  > "The estimated dust masses of our sample are consistent with previous IR SED decomposition results in (cite: Fan2016b)" (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties); "When we take a dust temperature of 30 K we found the dust mass increases by $\sim0.5$ dex and the corresponding $\delta_{\rm GDR}$ approximately decreases by a factor of 3." (Gas-to-dust ratio)
9. **Calculate gas fractions and gas-to-dust ratios**: Combine CO-based molecular masses with SED stellar and dust masses; fgas = MH2/(MH2+Mstar).  
  > "we calculated the molecular gas fraction, which is defined as $f_{\rm gas}$ = $M_{\rm H_2}/(M_{\rm H_2}\,+\,M_\star)$" (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction); "Most of our Hot DOGs exhibit a high $\delta_{\rm GDR}$ value $\sim$ $474_{-324}^{+711}$, with a median uncertainty of 43% (uncertainties propagated from $M_{\rm H_2}$ and $M_{\rm dust}$)." (Gas-to-dust ratio)
   - assumption: For three objects without stellar masses, adopt Mstar = 10^10.3 M⊙ to obtain upper limits on fgas.  
  > "we adopted $M_\star$ = $10^{10.3}\,\rm M_\odot$ which is the minimum $M_\star$ estimated for the other galaxies in our sample." (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction); "To put an upper limit on the molecular gas fraction for these three Hot DOGs" (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Propagate molecular-mass and dust-mass uncertainties; fgas median uncertainty approximately 60%.  
  > "with a median uncertainty of 43% (uncertainties propagated from $M_{\rm H_2}$ and $M_{\rm dust}$)." (Gas-to-dust ratio); "the median uncertainty of $f_{\rm gas}$ of our Hot DOG sample is approximately 60%, which results from the uncertainties in $M_{\rm H_2}$ and $M_{\star}$" (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction)
10. **Calculate starburst offsets, depletion times, and SFE**: Use Speagle2014 for ΔMS = SFR/SFRMS; tdepl = MH2/SFR; observational SFE = LIR/L′CO(1-0).  
  > "we can estimate the offset of our Hot DOGs from the main sequence $\Delta_{\rm MS}$ = $\rm {SFR}\,/\,\rm {SFR}_{\rm MS}$ relation." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency); "With the molecular gas masses and SFRs, we derived the gas depletion timescale $t_{\rm depl}\,=\,M_{\rm H_2}\,/\,\rm {SFR}$." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency); "to calculate the SFE as $L_{\rm IR}$ / $L_{\rm CO(1-0)}^{'}$." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
   - parameter: Speagle2014: log(SFR) = (0.84−0.026τuniv)log(Mstar)−(6.51−0.11τuniv), τuniv in Gyr.  
  > "\log({\rm SFR}/{\rm M}_{\ensuremath{\odot}}~{\rm yr}^{-1}) &=(0.84-0.026\times \tau_{\rm univ})\log(M_{\star}/{\rm M}_{\ensuremath{\odot}})" (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency); "& \qquad -(6.51-0.11\times \tau_{\rm univ})\, ," (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency); "where $\tau_{\rm univ}$ is the age of the Universe in gigayears" (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with literature SMGs/quasars and Sargent2014 main-sequence relations.  
  > "the compiled samples of SMGs and unobscured and obscured quasars from (cite: Perna2018)." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency); "The SFEs exhibit a tight correlation with $L_{\rm IR}$ for both MS galaxies (cite: Sargent2014) and SMGs." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
11. **Test SFE dependence on AGN luminosity**: Combine Hot DOGs, obscured quasars, WISSH quasars, and PG QSOs; calculate Spearman rank correlation of SFE and LAGN.  
  > "We plot the relation between SFE and ${L_{\rm AGN}}$ for Hot DOGs and obscured quasars in Figure [7](#fig:correlation)." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency); "We also included WISE-SDSS selected hyper-luminous (WISSH) quasars from (cite: Bischetti2021) and PG QSOs from (cite: Shangguan2020)."    - assumption: Comparison obscured-quasar LAGN from X-rays with Duras2020 luminosity-dependent bolometric correction.  
  > "The AGN bolometric luminosities of the obscured quasars from (cite: Perna2018) is calculated from their X-ray luminosities, assuming a luminosity-dependent bolometric correction from (cite: Duras2020)." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
   - fitting: Spearman rank correlation; p tests no correlation.  
  > "The Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient $\rho$ = 0.532 (p = 3.55e-8), where p is the probability of the null hypothesis that a correlation does not exist." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)

  > "we assume an Eddington ratio $\lambda_{\rm Edd}$ = 1.0 for our Hot DOG sample and infer the mass of the SMBH $M_{\rm BH}$ and the black hole to stellar mass ratio $M_{\rm BH}$/$M_\star$."    - assumption: λEdd = 1.0; η/(1−η) = 0.1.  
  > "by adopting $\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}$ = 0.1 and $\lambda_{\rm Edd}$ = 1.0, respectively." (Summary and Conclusion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Use published W2246-0526 MBH and fitted LAGN to estimate λEdd = 1.7.

## Qualitative findings

- Hot DOGs have lower molecular gas content and higher SFE than main-sequence galaxies.  
  > "Compared to main-sequence galaxies, they have a lower molecular gas content and higher SFEs, similar to quasars in the literature." (Abstract)
- Most objects are extreme starburst systems.  
  > "The high $\Delta_{\rm MS}$ values (Table [7](#table:properties)) demonstrate that most of our Hot DOGs are extreme starburst systems" (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
- SFE correlates positively with AGN luminosity across the four quasar samples.  
  > "Across all four quasar samples, there is a positive correlation between SFE and $L_{\rm AGN}$." (Star formation rate and star formation efficiency)
- Results are consistent with a short transition toward unobscured quasars.  
  > "Our results are consistent with the scenario that our sample represents a short transition phase toward unobscured quasars." (Abstract)
- W0248+2705 and W1814+3412 have tentative CO detections.  
  > "Among the four galaxies, W0248+2705 and W1814+3412 have a tentative detection, while no emission lines were found for the other two sources." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA CO constrains molecular gas; millimeter continuum improves cold-dust constraints.  
  > "This subsample has ALMA observations of CO emission lines, which were used to constrain their molecular gas content." (Introduction); "The ALMA continuum observations at rest-frame 3 mm have been included to constrain the cold dust component more accurately." (UV to Millimeter Spectral Energy Distribution Data)
- Why this method: Three-component SED decomposition separates host and AGN; constrain Tdust to 30–50 K to address AGN FIR contamination.  
  > "To decompose the host galaxy emission from the central AGN and estimate their physical properties, such as stellar mass $(M_\star)$ and SFR, we constructed UV to millimeter SEDs for all objects in our sample." (UV to Millimeter Spectral Energy Distribution Data); "However, we have restricted the $T_{\rm dust}$ parameter to a range of 30-50K to address AGN contamination in the FIR" (SED Analysis)
- Alternative: Initial Tdust = 10–100 K fitting replaced by 30–50 K fitting for physical-property analysis.  
  > "Initially, we fitted our sample with BayeSED by setting the $T_{\rm dust}$ parameter range to default values (10-100K)." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR); "The analysis of the physical properties in this paper is entirely based on the $T_{\rm dust}$-constrained fitting." (Influence of AGN contamination to FIR)
- Alternative: Earlier intermediate CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) ratio 0.8 considered; QSO ratios favored because AGN dominates bolometric luminosity.  
  > "a intermediate value of $L_{\rm CO(3-2)}^{'}$ / $L_{\rm CO(1-0)}^{'}$ = 0.8 between SMGs and QSOs was adopted in (cite: Banerji2017) and (cite: Fan2019)." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum); "our results in Section [4.2](#section4.2) indicate that the bolometric luminosities of our sample are dominated by central AGN emission, favoring the line ratios typical of QSOs." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
- Alternative: W0126-0529 excluded because uncertain redshift may explain the CO nondetection.  
  > "An uncertain redshift might be responsible for its nondetection in our observations. Thus, we chose to discard this source from our analysis." (CO Emission Lines and Continuum)
- Alternative: For W1248–2154 use the deeper data from 2017.1.00358.S rather than combining both projects.  
  > "From project 2017.1.00358.S; we note that W1248–2154 was observed in both projects, however we use these data as they are deeper." (Table 2)
- Alternative: Test a 30 K dust temperature to assess dust-mass and gas-to-dust-ratio sensitivity.  
  > "When we take a dust temperature of 30 K we found the dust mass increases by $\sim0.5$ dex and the corresponding $\delta_{\rm GDR}$ approximately decreases by a factor of 3." (Gas-to-dust ratio)
- How they decided it was enough: Observatory pipeline data deemed sufficient without further flagging or calibration; use deeper W1248–2154 project data.  
  > "The pipeline-reduced data delivered from the observatory was of sufficient quality such that no additional flagging and further calibration were necessary." (ALMA observations and data analysis); "we note that W1248–2154 was observed in both projects, however we use these data as they are deeper." (Table 2)
- Limitation (authors): No SED stellar masses for W0248+2705, W0615-5716, and W1248-2154 because optical-NIR photometry is absent.  
  > "Due to the lack of optical-NIR photometry data for W0248+2705, W0615-5716 and W1248-2154, we cannot obtain SED-based stellar masses $M_\star$ for these three Hot DOGs." (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction)
- Limitation (authors): Sample selection may favor intense starbursts.  
  > "therefore may be biased toward the most intense starbursting systems" (Gas-to-dust ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Warm graybody dust may miss the diffuse cold ISM and underestimate total dust mass.  
  > "The estimated $T_{\rm dust}\sim$ 45 K may trace a warmer dust component associated with photodissociation regions by starbursts, instead of the diffuse ISM with temperature below 30 K which represents the bulk of the dust mass" (Gas-to-dust ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Reported dust-mass uncertainties omit the full uncertainty in adopted κ850μm.  
  > "The uncertainties in the dust masses shown in Table [6](#table:SED fitting results) will be larger if we consider the adopted $\kappa_{850\mu m}$ value" (Results of the SED Fitting and Dust Properties)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar masses are more uncertain for the four objects with substantial optical-NIR AGN emission.  
  > "For these four BHDs, their stellar mass estimations are more uncertain." (The Applicability of the CLUMPY Model to Hot DOGs with Excess Blue Light)
- Limitation (authors): Comparison of stellar and dynamical masses deferred to future work.  
  > "We defer a comparative analysis of the stellar and dynamical masses of our sample to a future work." (Stellar mass and molecular gas fraction)
