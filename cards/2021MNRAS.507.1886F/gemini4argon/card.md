## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct continuum images, measure fluxes, fit 2D Gaussians, and derive dust/gas mass and H2 column density**: Average line-free channels, merge visibilities of 247+257 GHz (1.2mm) and 262+272 GHz (1.1mm), fit two 2D Gaussians to the 1.1mm image, and apply a gray-body model to calculate mass and H2 column density.  
  > "We have constructed continuum images at 1.2mm and 1.1mm by averaging the channels free from line emission in each band, and then merging the visibilities of the 247 GHz + 257 GHz and 262 GHz + 272 GHz bands, respectively." (Continuum images); "Based on our millimeter observations, we estimate the total gas and dust mass in Mon R2 IRS 3 using a gray-body model" (Continuum images); "We have fitted two 2D Gaussians to the 1.1mm image to estimate the contribution of each source to the total flux" (3.1 Continuum images)
   - software: CASA, CLASS/GILDAS
   - parameter: Briggs weighting robust=0.5  
  > "In the case of the higher signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) continuum images, we used Briggs weighting (robust=0.5) to achieve higher spatial resolution." (Observations)
   - assumption: D = 778 pc  
  > "Monoceros R2 (hereafter Mon R2) is an active massive star-forming cloud located at a distance of $\sim$ 778 pc (cite: Zucker2019)." (Introduction)
   - assumption: k_1.1mm = 0.0078 cm^2 g^-1, gas-to-dust ratio = 100, T_dust = 200 K  
  > "we adopt a grain emissivity, $k_{1.1mm}$ = 0.0078 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$, which is the value calculated by (cite: ossenkopf1994) for bare grains and dense gas, where gas-to-dust ratio of 100 is assumed. Adopting T$_{\rm dust}$ = 200 K (see Sect. 4), we derive a total mass of $\sim$0.14 M$_\odot$." (Continuum images)
   - fitting: Two 2D Gaussians fitted to the 1.1mm continuum image  
  > "We have fitted two 2D Gaussians to the 1.1mm image to estimate the contribution of each source to the total flux" (3.1 Continuum images)
   - validation: Compared continuum-derived N(H2) with independent estimate from NIR 13CO absorption lines assuming standard 13CO abundance of 2x10^-6  
  > "Assuming a standard $^{13}$CO abundance of $\sim$2$\times$10$^{-6}$ and that the amount of gas beyond the protostar is the same as that measured by the absorption lines, we derive N(H$_2$)$\sim$1.1$\times$10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$ along the line of sight which is in reasonable agreement with our estimate based on the 1.1mm continuum." (Continuum images)
2. **Extract spectra and identify molecular lines**: Extract interferometric spectra towards IRS 3 A and IRS 3 B and identify transitions above 4 sigma integrated intensity using CDMS and JPL spectroscopic catalogs.  
  > "To study the chemical content of IRS 3, we have extracted the interferometric spectra towards IRS 3 A and IRS 3 B." (Spectroscopic observations); "The lines have been identified using the $"$The Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy$"$ (CDMS) (cite: Muller2005; Endres2016) and the "Jet Propulsion Laboratory Line catalogue" (JPL) (cite: Pickett1998). We have adopted the 4$\sigma$ level in integrated intensity as the detection limit." (Spectroscopic observations)
   - software: CLASS/GILDAS
   - parameter: Detection limit = 4 sigma in integrated intensity  
  > "We have adopted the 4$\sigma$ level in integrated intensity as the detection limit." (Spectroscopic observations)
   - assumption: 32S/34S = 22.5  
  > "We have checked the identification of the S-bearing species by searching for the $^{34}$S isotopologues to test that the (non-)detections of all the $^{34}$S isotopologues are compatible with a standard ratio of $^{32}$S/$^{34}$S=22.5 (cite: Wilson1994; Chin1996)." (Spectroscopic observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked S-bearing species against 34S isotopologues assuming 32S/34S = 22.5; confirmed CH3OCH3 detection using LTE synthetic spectrum at T_ex = 170 K  
  > "We have checked the identification of the S-bearing species by searching for the $^{34}$S isotopologues to test that the (non-)detections of all the $^{34}$S isotopologues are compatible with a standard ratio of $^{32}$S/$^{34}$S=22.5 (cite: Wilson1994; Chin1996)." (Spectroscopic observations); "In order to confirm (reject) this detection, we produced a synthetic spectrum assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium and T$_{ex}$=170 K and checked that the obtained spectrum is compatible with the observed spectra" (Spectroscopic observations)
3. **Analyze spatial distribution and gas kinematics using moment maps and PV diagrams**: Compute zeroth-moment (integrated intensity) and first-moment (intensity-weighted velocity) maps, and extract Position-Velocity (PV) diagrams perpendicular to and along the reflection nebula axis to compare with Keplerian and solid-body rotation curves.  
  > "The integrated intensity maps (zeroth-moment maps) of the most intense lines are shown in Fig. 3." (Moment maps); "Relevant information on the structure and kinematics of the molecular gas can also be obtained from the first-moment maps shown in Fig. 5." (Moment maps); "To further explore the kinematics of the molecular gas and distinguish between a toroidal envelope from the circumstellar disk, we have performed Position-Velocity (PV) diagrams in the direction perpendicular to the reflection nebula ("toroid" in Figs. 5b)." (Moment maps)
   - software: CLASS/GILDAS
   - parameter: Reflection nebula direction P.A. ~ 40 deg; systemic velocity v_sys ~ 9 km s^-1  
  > "(the direction of the reflection nebula associated to IRS 3 A is P.A.$\sim$ 40$^\circ$ and we will refer to it as "RN", hereafter)." (Moment maps); "Moreover, the emission is red-shifted relative to the systemic velocity, v$_{sys}$ $\sim$ 9 km s$^{-1}$" (Moment maps)
   - assumption: Total stellar mass M_stars = 40 M_sun and inclination angle i = 45 deg for Keplerian comparison  
  > "We find a reasonable agreement with HCN and H$_2^{13}$CO observations assuming $M_{stars}=40 M_\odot$ and $i=45^\circ$." (Moment maps)
   - fitting: Visual comparison of PV diagrams with Keplerian rotation curve and solid-body rotation line  
  > "In Fig. 6, we compare these PV-diagrams with the velocity patterns expected in the case of Keplerian rotation (red line) and solid-body rotation (black line)." (Moment maps)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive rotation temperatures, molecular column densities, and fractional abundances**: Apply the rotation diagram technique to SO2 and CH3OH lines to determine T_rot and column densities; fit LTE synthetic spectra at T_k = 129 K and 170 K for remaining species; compute abundances relative to N(H2).  
  > "Therefore, we have used the SO$_2$ and CH$_3$OH lines to estimate the gas temperature. We obtained T$_{rot}$ = 175$_{-16}^{+15}$ K and N(SO$_2$)=3.4$_{-0.5}^{+0.6}$ $\times$ 10$^{16}$ cm$^{-2}$ using the three SO$_2$ lines observed towards IRS 3 A (see Table 2)." (Chemical content); "The molecular column densities of the rest of species have been derived by fitting the lines observed towards IRS 3 A assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)" (Chemical content); "Molecular abundances have been estimated adopting the value derived from our millimeter observations, N(H$_2$)=2.6$\times$10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$ (see Table 2)." (Chemical content)
   - software: CLASS/GILDAS
   - parameter: T_k = 129 K and T_k = 170 K; upper limit linewidth = 4 km s^-1 and T_b < 3 x rms in 1 km s^-1 channel  
  > "We estimate these uncertainties by calculating the column densities with two temperatures, 129 K -170 K, that are representative of the temperatures derived using different molecular tracers (see Table 2)." (Chemical content); "The rms has been derived in a channel of $\Delta v$=1 km s$^{-1}$. Then, the upper limit is calculated assuming a linewidth of 4 km s$^{-1}$ and imposing T$_{\rm b}$$<$3$\times$rms." (Discussion)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), N(H2) = 2.6 x 10^23 cm^-2, 12C/13C = 60, 16O/18O = 550, solar S/H = 1.5 x 10^-5  
  > "The molecular column densities of the rest of species have been derived by fitting the lines observed towards IRS 3 A assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)" (Chemical content); "This value has been calculated from thee H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{18}$O$^+$ column densities assuming $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C=60 (cite: Savage2002) and $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O=550 (cite: Wilson1994)." (Chemical content); "Following our calculations, SO$_2$ accounts for a significant fraction, $\sim$1%, of the sulphur budget assuming the solar abundance, S/H=1.5$\times$10$^{-5}$ (cite: Asplund2005)." (Chemical content)
   - fitting: Rotation diagram fitting for SO2 and CH3OH; LTE synthetic line fitting for other species  
  > "The excitation temperature can be easily derived using the rotation diagram technique as long as several transitions are observed." (Chemical content); "The molecular column densities of the rest of species have been derived by fitting the lines observed towards IRS 3 A assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)" (Chemical content)
   - validation: Checked optical depth of SO2 and OCS by fitting 34SO2 and OC34S isotopologues and verifying N(XS)/N(X34S) ~ 22.5  
  > "We have fitted the lines of the isotopologues $^{34}$SO$_2$, OC$^{34}$S and derived N(XS)/N(X$^{34}$S) ratios $\sim$22.5 (see Table 2, and Fig. 7 and 8), proving that the lines of the main isotopologues are optically thin." (Chemical content)

## Qualitative findings

- The 1.1mm continuum resolves two components, IRS 3 A and IRS 3 B, separated by ~0.65", with IRS 3 A containing ~80% of the continuum flux and hosting the hot core.  
  > "The continuum image at 1.1mm shows two components, IRS 3 A and IRS 3 B, that are separated by $\sim$0.65$"$. We estimate that IRS 3 A is responsible of $\sim$80 % of the continuum flux, being the most massive component." (Abstract); "The COMs CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$OCH$_3$ are only detected towards IRS 3 A proving that the hot core is associated to this protostar." (Spectroscopic observations)
- Chemical spatial differentiation is present around IRS 3 A: most species peak within R<0.3" of IRS 3 A, COMs (CH3OCHO) and H2CS peak ~0.5" SW in the southern NIR nebula, and HC3N peaks ~0.5" NE.  
  > "Most of the observed species peak in a region R$<$0.3" from IRS 3 A." (Moment maps); "The peak of the CH$_3$OCHO is located $\sim$0.5$"$ SW to IRS 3 A-mm, that is about a half of the HPBW of our observations. The peak of the HC$_3$N emission is displaced by $\sim$0.5$"$ NE from IRS 3 A-mm." (Moment maps)
- First-moment maps and PV diagrams show a SE-NW velocity gradient (7-11 km s^-1) perpendicular to the NIR reflection nebula, consistent with a rotating ring/toroid or unresolved circumstellar disk.  
  > "Most of the first-moment maps present a clear SE-NW velocity gradient in the v$_{lsr}$=7$-$11 km s$^{-1}$ velocity range." (Moment maps); "The detected velocity gradient is consistent with the emission coming from a toroidal envelope and/or a disk that is rotating in the direction perpendicular to the axis defined by the NIR nebula" (Moment maps)
- SO2 is the most abundant gas-phase S-bearing molecule detected (~1.3x10^-7), accounting for ~1% of the solar sulphur budget, implying >95% of sulphur remains locked in refractories or unobservable species like sulphur allotropes.  
  > "Following our calculations, SO$_2$ accounts for a significant fraction, $\sim$1%, of the sulphur budget. The abundance of SO$_2$ is a factor $>$5 higher than those of SO, OCS, and H$_2$CS. This implies that $>$95% of the sulphur should be locked in refractories or species such as S$_2$ and sulphur allotropes that cannot be observed." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous single-dish and lower-resolution interferometric observations could not resolve the individual members of the Mon R2 IRS 3 mini-cluster, requiring high spatial and spectral resolution (<0.3 km s^-1) ALMA observations.  
  > "All the mm studies carried out. thus far towards IRS 3 were performed with single-dish observations or interferometric observations which are unable to resolve the IRS 3 mini-cluster (see (cite: Boonmanvd2003; vandertak2003; Dierickx2015)." (Introduction); "A total of 18 spectral windows were observed, each with a spectral resolution $<$0.3 km s$^{-1}$ which allows to fully resolve the profiles of the detected lines" (Observations)
- Why this method: Briggs weighting (robust=0.5) was used for continuum to achieve higher spatial resolution, natural weighting for lines to maximize sensitivity, and LTE/rotational diagrams because high hot core densities (n(H2) > 10^7 cm^-3) thermalize the transitions.  
  > "Spectroscopic images of all spectral windows were produced in CASA using natural weighting. In the case of the higher signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) continuum images, we used Briggs weighting (robust=0.5) to achieve higher spatial resolution." (Observations); "The molecular column densities of the rest of species have been derived by fitting the lines observed towards IRS 3 A assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), which is a reasonable assumption for the high densities (n(H$_2$)$>$10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$) expected in a hot core." (Chemical content)
- Alternative: Fitting SO2 and CH3OH lines with multiple rotation temperatures was rejected due to the small number of observed transitions.  
  > "Given the small number of transitions observed, we do not consider that we could obtain realistic results using a more complex fitting with several rotation temperatures." (Chemical content)
- Alternative: Strong shock sputtering as the primary origin of gas-phase SO2 was rejected in favor of radiative heating (or mild shocks) because SO2 lines are narrow (~4 km s^-1) and follow the toroid PV pattern.  
  > "However, the SO$_2$ lines toward Mon R 2 IRS 3 A are substantially narrow $\Delta V \sim$ 4 km s$^{-1}$, compared with the high velocities observed in the bipolar outflow detected by (cite: Dierickx2015)." (Discussion); "Our results are therefore more consistent with the interpretation that sulphur has been released from the ices due to radiative heating (or perhaps mild shocks) rather than sputtered by strong shocks." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The angular resolution (~1.2" x 0.9" for lines, ~0.95" x 0.63" for 1.1mm continuum) barely resolves IRS 3 A and B, cannot separate NIR sources B and C, cannot resolve HCN v2=1 emission, and cannot distinguish an unresolved Keplerian disk from a solid-body rotating ring.  
  > "The angular resolution of the 1.1mm image is not enough to cleanly discern between IRS 3 B and IRS 3 A." (Continuum images); "In the cases of unresolved disks, where the sensitivity is not enough to detect the highest rotation velocities expected close to the star, a Keplerian disk and solid-body rotation would be difficult to differentiate." (Moment maps); "Given that the emission of the HCN $\nu_2$=1 is not resolved, we could not disentangle between these two physical processes." (Moment maps)
- Limitation (authors): Interferometric observations filter out extended emission, causing absorption and negative contours in C2H and HNC and missed flux signatures in HCN, H13CN, and SO.  
  > "It should be noticed that our observations are filtering the extended emission, thus producing negative contours in the emission of these molecules also far from the compact hot core. Signatures of missed flux are also found in the HCN 3$\rightarrow$2, H$^{13}$CN 3$\rightarrow$2, and to a lesser extent in the SO 6$_6$$\rightarrow$5$_5$ and 7$_6$$\rightarrow$6$_5$ images." (Spectroscopic observations)
- Limitation (authors): A single rotation temperature cannot fit all observed SO2 or CH3OH lines well because of a steep temperature gradient along the line of sight.  
  > "However, we do not obtain a good fit to the three millimeter lines observed, which is the consequence of the steep gas temperature gradient along the line of sight (see Figs. 7). We cannot obtain a good fit to all the methanol lines using a single rotation temperature, either." (Chemical content)
- Limitation (authors): Lines of abundant species like CH3OH, HCN, and SO (and possibly CH3OCHO) may be optically thick, making derived abundances lower limits and affecting abundance ratios.  
  > "We recall that the CH$_3$OH lines are very likely optically thick and the derived abundance a lower limit to the real value." (Chemical content); "One possibility is that the CH$_3$OCHO lines are optically thick and we are underestimating their column densities. Another possibility is that the N(SO)/N(SO$_2$) ratio is overestimated because the SO lines are optically thick, and the hot core is in an earlier evolutionary stage." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of interferometric observations of H2S and CS prevents a complete inventory of gas-phase S-bearing molecules.  
  > "We do not have interferometric observations of the abundant species, H$_2$S and CS, that would be useful to complete the inventory of S-bearing molecules." (Chemical content)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a discrepancy in the reported ALMA project code: Section 2 states 2015.1.00453.S (matching the ALMA archive manifest), whereas the Acknowledgements state ADS/JAO. 2016.1.00813.S.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a minor discrepancy in N(CH3OH): the text in Section 4 gives 1.1(+0.3/-0.2) x 10^17 cm^-2, while Table 2 lists 1.4 x 10^17 cm^-2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The spectral index alpha = 3.7 +/- 3.3 is derived over a very narrow lever arm (1.1 mm vs 1.2 mm) and has an uncertainty nearly equal to the value itself.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 2, the column density of HCN (1.1x10^15 cm^-2 at 170 K) is only ~3.7 times higher than H13CN (3.0x10^14 cm^-2), confirming severe optical depth in the main HCN 3-2 line, yet Table 2 lists the uncorrected main-line HCN abundance (4.2x10^-9) while the text in Section 4 states that HCN has an abundance of a few 10^-7.
