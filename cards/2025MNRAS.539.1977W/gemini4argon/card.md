## Analysis recipe

1. **Cross-match ALMACAL radio sources with optical counterparts**: Cross-match 1,047 ALMACAL radio coordinates with optical sources in NED, SIMBAD, and the AT20G optical catalogue using a 3-arcsecond matching threshold  
  > "We use the NASA Extragalactic Database (NED 1 ) and SIMBAD 2 databases to search for optical counterparts to the ALMACAL sources. Additionally, we perform a cross-match with an optical catalogue of sources in the Australia Telescope 20 GHz (AT20G) Survey (Mahony et al. 2011)." (2.2 Optical cross-matching); "Our choice of a 3-arcsecond threshold to determine whether an ALMA calibrator is matched to an optical source is somewhat arbitrary." (2.2 Optical cross-matching)
   - parameter: 3-arcsecond cross-matching threshold  
  > "Our choice of a 3-arcsecond threshold to determine whether an ALMA calibrator is matched to an optical source is somewhat arbitrary." (2.2 Optical cross-matching)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Assessed false positive mismatch rate by shifting radio coordinates by 1 arcminute in RA and Dec and re-matching with NED, finding 0.5% within 0.5 arcsec and 6.7% within 3 arcsec  
  > "To assess the likelihood of mismatches between radio and optical sources, we cross-matched the ALMACAL catalog with NED after shifting the radio coordinates by one arcminute in both RA and Dec." (2.2 Optical cross-matching); "We find that [0.5, 1.3, 3.5, 6.7] per cent of the radio sample with scrambled positions matches an optical source within [0.5, 1, 2, 3] arcseconds." (2.2 Optical cross-matching)
2. **Reduce VLT/X-Shooter spectroscopy and measure new redshifts**: Reduce UVB, VIS, and NIR nodding-mode spectra of 70 southern calibrators using the ESO X-shooter pipeline in ESO Reflex, visually inspect 2D spectra, extract 1D spectra, and determine redshifts with Marz  
  > "We processed the X-shooter data from the two programs using version 3.6.3 of the ESO X-shooter pipeline (Modigliani et al. 2010) within the ESO Reflex environment (Freudling et al. 2013)." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations); "After visually inspecting the two-dimensional spectra, we extract the one-dimensional spectra using the pipeline." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations); "We use marz 3 (Hinton et al. 2016) to determine redshifts." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations)
   - software: ESO X-shooter pipeline, ESO Reflex, Marz
   - parameter: Slit widths of 1.3, 1.2, and 1.2 arcsec for UVB, VIS, and NIR arms (R = 4100, 6500, 4300)  
  > "We chose slit widths of 1.3, 1.2 and 1.2 arcseconds for the UVB, VIS and NIR arms (covering 300−2500 nm) to account for poor seeing conditions. These widths correspond to a resolving power of 𝑅 = 4100, 6500, 4300, respectively." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations)
   - parameter: NODDING mode with exposure times of 900 to 2,700 seconds  
  > "Each target was observed in the NODDING mode to improve the removal of sky emission. Exposure times range from 900 to 2,700 seconds, depending on the magnitude of the target." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations)
   - fitting: Template/line matching in Marz  
  > "We use marz 3 (Hinton et al. 2016) to determine redshifts." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations)
   - validation: Visual inspection of 2D spectra and calculation of median SNR outside telluric bands  
  > "After visually inspecting the two-dimensional spectra, we extract the one-dimensional spectra using the pipeline. For the P101 data, we reach median SNR values of [7.4, 12.7, 3.0], while for the P113 data, the median SNR values are [9.4, 13.0, 3.1] for the UV, VIS, and NIR arms, respectively." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations)
3. **Compile and validate the ALMACAL-22 redshift catalogue**: Combine X-Shooter redshifts with archival spectra from large surveys (2dFGRS, 6dFGRS, SDSS, DESI DR1) and literature sources via NED/SIMBAD, track provenance, and assign redshift confidence flags (0-4, 6, 7)  
  > "The cross-matched ALMACAL sources were initially split into two distinct catalogues based on whether the optical counterpart belonged to large surveys such as the Two-degree-Field Galaxy Redshift Survey (2dFGRS; Colless et al. 2001), 6dF Galaxy Redshift Survey (6dFGRS; Jones et al. 2004), SDSS and DESI DR1 (DESI Collaboration et al. 2025)." (2.4 ALMACAL redshift catalogue construction); "In the ‘Confidence’ column, we assign a quality value from 0 to 4, or 6 or 7, classifying each source. This notation is adapted from the 6dF survey (Jones et al. 2009), with an additional quality flag 7 included to indicate a featureless BL Lac spectrum." (2.4.1 Format)
   - parameter: Confidence flags 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 (flag 4 for redshift error <= 300 km s^-1, flag 3 for > 300 km s^-1 or no public spectrum)  
  > "Quantitatively, sources with redshift errors ≲ 300 km s −1 are classified with confidence 4, whereas a confidence of 3 means that no error measurement is possible or errors are ≳ 300 km s −1 ." (2.4.1 Format)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pilot test on 20 calibrators by co-authors to standardise the process, and independent double-checking by two team members for objects outside large surveys  
  > "To refine the redshift determination method, the co-authors (CP, EK, EM, ES, HY, JA, JC, RS, SW) studied an initial sample of the same 20 calibrator sources." (2.4 ALMACAL redshift catalogue construction); "Spectroscopic redshift measurements for objects not belonging to large surveys were each checked independently by two members of the collaboration." (2.4 ALMACAL redshift catalogue construction)
4. **Classify calibrators using WISE infrared colours**: Retrieve WISE W1 (3.4 um), W2 (4.6 um), and W3 (12 um) magnitudes from NED for 774 sources using astroquery and construct a WISE colour-colour diagram with kernel density contours compared to the WISE blazar strip  
  > "Using astroquery (Ginsburg et al. 2019), we retrieve WISE magnitudes for the cross-matched optical positions discussed in section 2 from the NASA Extragalactic Database. In total, 774 sources have measurements of the W1, W2, and W3 magnitudes, corresponding to 3.4, 4.6, and 12 𝜇m, respectively, which are required to produce the WISE colour-colour plot (Wright et al. 2010)." (3.2 WISE classification); "As seen in the contours from a kernel density estimator, most ALMACAL sources fall within the QSO and Seyfert loci, consistent with these sources being active galactic nuclei (AGN), particularly quasars and blazars." (3.2 WISE classification)
   - software: astroquery
   - parameter: WISE W1 (3.4 um), W2 (4.6 um), and W3 (12 um) bands  
  > "In total, 774 sources have measurements of the W1, W2, and W3 magnitudes, corresponding to 3.4, 4.6, and 12 𝜇m, respectively, which are required to produce the WISE colour-colour plot (Wright et al. 2010)." (3.2 WISE classification)
   - fitting: Kernel density estimator for WISE colour-colour distribution  
  > "As seen in the contours from a kernel density estimator, most ALMACAL sources fall within the QSO and Seyfert loci, consistent with these sources being active galactic nuclei (AGN), particularly quasars and blazars." (3.2 WISE classification)
   - validation: Corroborated against earlier ALMACAL blazar classifications of Bonato et al. (2018)  
  > "This result independently corroborates the findings of Bonato et al. (2018), which, based on an earlier version of the ALMACAL catalogue (up to September 2017), determined that 97 per cent of the sources are consistent with being blazars" (3.2 WISE classification)
5. **Fit and classify radio spectral energy distributions and estimate linear sizes**: Fit radio SEDs of 897 RACS-low-detected ALMACAL sources using RadioSED with four analytical models (power law, simple peaked spectrum, curved peaked spectrum, re-triggered), classify into PL, PS, RT, or Complex, and convert rest-frame peak/trough frequencies to linear sizes  
  > "In this section, we present radio SED fits using the recently developed RadioSED 5 algorithm (Kerrison et al. 2024)." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION); "Out of the 1,047 ALMACAL sources, we perform SED fitting for 897 objects that are observed in the Rapid ASKAP Continuum survey (RACS-low; Hale et al. 2021)." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION); "We also include an approximation of the linear source size on the secondary 𝑦-axis using a scaling relation (Jeyakumar 2016)." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)
   - software: RadioSED
   - parameter: Four analytical SED models: power law, simple peaked spectrum, curved peaked spectrum, and re-triggered (power law + simple peaked spectrum)  
  > "The SED is fitted using four analytical models: i) a power law for steep, flat, or inverted spectra, ii) a simple peaked spectrum source model (Snellen et al. 1998), iii) a peaked spectrum model with curvature (Orienti et al. 2007) and iv) a re-triggered source modelled by a linear combination of the power law and simple peaked spectrum source models." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)
   - assumption: Scaling relation between turnover/peak frequency and linear source size from Jeyakumar (2016)  
  > "We also include an approximation of the linear source size on the secondary 𝑦-axis using a scaling relation (Jeyakumar 2016)." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)
   - fitting: Bayesian nested sampling in RadioSED  
  > "RadioSED offers an automated framework to retrieve data from large-area surveys and fit the corresponding data using a Bayesian nested sampling approach." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)
   - validation: Visual inspection of every SED to identify poorly fitted/variable ('complex') sources, check for dust emission, and search for high-frequency spectral breaks  
  > "We also introduce a final class labelled ‘complex’ to describe data that is poorly-fitted, has unusual model parameters or unnatural spectral indices." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION); "We note that after visually inspecting each SED, we find no evidence for dust emission that could not be explained by variability." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)

## Qualitative findings

- The fraction of sources with confident redshifts mildly increases or plateaus at fainter WISE W1 magnitudes (between 12.5 and 15 mag), because brighter objects include a larger fraction of featureless blazars.  
  > "Unlike typical redshift surveys where completeness decreases with increasing magnitude, we find a more varied relationship where completeness appears to mildly increase (or plateau, considering the errors) between W1 = 12.5 and 15 mag." (3.1 Redshift distribution); "This is likely due to a significant fraction of the brighter sources in the sample being blazars, where the non-thermal continuum masks emission features, making redshift determination difficult." (3.1 Redshift distribution)
- Most ALMACAL sources fall within the QSO and Seyfert loci of the WISE colour-colour diagram and align with the WISE blazar strip, confirming they are predominantly quasars and blazars.  
  > "As seen in the contours from a kernel density estimator, most ALMACAL sources fall within the QSO and Seyfert loci, consistent with these sources being active galactic nuclei (AGN), particularly quasars and blazars." (3.2 WISE classification); "According to the WISE classifications, the majority of ALMACAL sources are quasars and blazars, aligning with previous findings (Bonato et al. 2018)." (Figure 7 caption)
- Visual inspection of the radio SEDs shows no evidence for dust emission that cannot be explained by source variability, and no evidence for spectral breaks above the peak frequency.  
  > "We note that after visually inspecting each SED, we find no evidence for dust emission that could not be explained by variability." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION); "Estimating an age from the SED requires a spectral break above the peak frequency (see Allison et al. 2019, for an example), which, after visual inspection for all sources, is not evident in this sample." (5.1 A unique population of radio-loud AGN)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Accurate calibrator redshifts and properties are needed to ensure calibrators lie behind foreground galaxies in CGM absorption stacking and to prevent clustering bias in blind CO emitter and DSFG surveys in ALMACAL fields; bright (R < 21) quasars were chosen for X-Shooter to allow 'any-weather' observations.  
  > "To fully exploit the ALMACAL data, understanding the properties of the calibrators is crucial. For instance, obtaining accurate redshifts is necessary to ensure that the calibrator is at a higher redshift than foreground galaxies in stacking experiments (Klitsch et al. 2023)." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Moreover, verifying that CO emitters are at different redshifts from the calibrators is important to ensure that measured CO luminosity functions are not affected by clustering (Hamanowicz et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2023b)." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Optically bright (𝑅< 21) quasars were targeted to facilitate observations under ‘any-weather’ conditions." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations)
- Why this method: Wider X-Shooter slits (1.3, 1.2, 1.2 arcsec) were chosen for poor seeing and NODDING mode was used to improve sky emission removal; RadioSED was used because it automates multi-survey radio data retrieval and fits analytical models with Bayesian nested sampling.  
  > "We chose slit widths of 1.3, 1.2 and 1.2 arcseconds for the UVB, VIS and NIR arms (covering 300−2500 nm) to account for poor seeing conditions." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations); "Each target was observed in the NODDING mode to improve the removal of sky emission." (2.3 VLT X-Shooter UV–NIR observations); "RadioSED offers an automated framework to retrieve data from large-area surveys and fit the corresponding data using a Bayesian nested sampling approach." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)
- Alternative: Estimating AGN jet age from a high-frequency spectral break in the radio SED was considered, but no spectral breaks were found upon visual inspection; tracking sources with multi-epoch VLBI imaging to obtain a dynamical age is proposed instead.  
  > "Estimating an age from the SED requires a spectral break above the peak frequency (see Allison et al. 2019, for an example), which, after visual inspection for all sources, is not evident in this sample. An alternative is to track sources using imaging to obtain a dynamical age." (5.1 A unique population of radio-loud AGN)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): At a 3-arcsecond cross-matching separation, the probability of a false positive optical match is approximately 7 per cent, and optical astrometric accuracy in large databases is variable.  
  > "While ALMA calibrator positions are typically accurate to within 3 mas due to their very-long-baseline interferometric (VLBI) observations (Petrov & Kovalev 2025), the astrometric accuracy of optical sources is more variable, as they are drawn from large databases." (2.2 Optical cross-matching); "This suggests that at a 3-arcsecond separation, the probability of a false positive in the cross-match is approximately 7 per cent" (2.2 Optical cross-matching)
- Limitation (authors): Six sources lack optical counterparts within 3 arcseconds due to low Galactic latitudes in crowded stellar fields, very low declinations, or faintness at visible wavelengths.  
  > "Three sources (J1032-5917, J1220-5604 and J1555-4150) are found at low Galactic latitudes in crowded stellar fields, while the remainder are likely too faint at visible wavelengths." (2.2 Optical cross-matching)
- Limitation (authors): Separating blazars from the broader population of quasars using WISE colours alone is difficult because they occupy overlapping regions in colour-colour space.  
  > "Separating blazars that are dominated by non-thermal emission from the larger population of quasars is challenging based on WISE colours alone, as they occupy similar regions in the diagram." (3.2 WISE classification)
- Limitation (authors): Multi-epoch radio survey data span more than 20 epochs, so source variability causes poor SED fits (36% 'complex' class), and peaks near the upper edge of the frequency coverage have a higher false detection rate.  
  > "The data span more than 20 epochs which can allow sources to exhibit variability." (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION); "While we expect a higher false detection rate for peaks at higher frequencies near the edge of the measurement range (Kerrison et al. 2024)" (4 RADIO SPECTRAL ENERGY DISTRIBUTION)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-simultaneous radio survey flux densities of highly variable blazars can mimic peaked or re-triggered spectral shapes in static SED fits without simultaneous multi-band monitoring.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a minor discrepancy in the text regarding the highest redshift in the sample: the abstract and conclusions state z = 3.742, whereas Section 3.1 and Figure 6 caption state z = 3.789.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 1, the printed RA/Dec coordinates for J0304+3348 (00:32:51 +02:47:41), J0501-0159 (04:51:57 +00:06:11), and J1717-3948 (12:41:57 -02:10:53) do not match their J2000 IAU source names.
