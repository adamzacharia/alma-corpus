## Science

- Fields: evolved_stars
- Question: What is the morpho-kinematics of the wind escaping the gravity of the AGB star L2 Pup, including line broadening in the nascent wind near the star and the presence of predicted collimated radial outflows?  
  > "The present article uses ALMA observations of the $^{12,13}$CO(3-2), $^{29}$SiO(8-7), $^{12}$CO(2-1) and $^{28}$SiO(5-4) line emissions to shed new light on this issue." (Abstract); "These analyses predict the presence of radial outflows collimated by the disc and do not explicitly account for an important line broadening in the inner region of the CSE, in contrast with what is usually observed in other similar AGB stars. These are the two main issues that the present article is addressing." (Introduction)

  > "We used CASA to produce clean maps with natural weighting and no continuum subtraction." (Overall picture)

   - parameter: Natural weighting, no continuum subtraction, V_lsr = 33.3 km s^-1

2. **Measure absorption over the stellar disc and radial extent of self-absorption**: Extract spectra inside a circle of 12 mas radius centred on L2 Pup for 12,13CO(3-2) and 29SiO(8-7), compare with R Dor, and construct PV maps of V_z vs R in four quadrants (northern, eastern, southern, western).

   - assumption: Inner rim of gas disc is at R ~ 31 mas so r < 12 mas receives no contribution from rotation  
  > "They are located well within the inner rim of the gas disc, evaluated at $R$$\sim$31 mas by (cite: Homan2017) and should receive no contribution from rotation." (Absorption over the stellar disc)

  > "Figure 7 illustrates the evolution of the line profile when scanning along this line in steps of $y$$=$10 mas (0.64 au)." (High Doppler velocity wings); "To illustrate this point we show in Figure 9 the dependence on position angle of the mean Doppler velocity, averaged over $R$ in successive rings and integrated over $|V_z|$$<$20 km s$^{-1}$." (High Doppler velocity wings)
   - assumption: Gaussian line profile approximation to convert V_{z,20} to effective FWHM line width  
  > "In the approximation of Gaussian line profiles, we estimate that the effective line width (FWHM) remains below 10 km s$^{-1}$down to projected distances of $\sim$4 au and then increases linearly to reach $\sim$20 km s$^{-1}$ near the surface of the star." (High Doppler velocity wings)
   - fitting: Sinusoidal fit of mean Doppler velocity vs position angle (parameters A, B, omega_0 in Table 2)  
  > "| $A$ (km s$^{-1}$) | $B$ (km s$^{-1}$) | $\omega_0$ (degrees) |" (Table 2)
   - validation: Checked consistency with red-side evaluation and with a scan made at y = 0 in small steps of x; estimated +-1 km s^-1 uncertainties from different evaluation methods of V_{z,20}.

(High Doppler velocity wings); "We have checked that this evaluation is consistent with the results obtained on the red side and with a scan made at $y$$=$0 in small steps of $x$ (Figure 8)." 4. **Parameterize large-scale wind morpho-kinematics within 200 au using 12CO(2-1) and 28SiO(5-4)**: Construct intensity maps, radial distributions, moment 1 maps, and PV maps (V_z vs R and V_z vs omega) for 12CO(2-1) and 28SiO(5-4). In 0.5 arcsec radial intervals from R = 1 to 3.5 arcsec, model V_z vs omega flux density as the sum of three Gaussian-smeared sine waves (amplitudes a, b, c) and derive the inclination and expansion velocity of a flared expanding disc.  
  > "The central and right panels of Figure 5 display PV maps of the CO(2-1) emission, both in $V_z$ vs $R$ and in $V_z$ vs $\omega$." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "In order to obtain a simple parameterization of these features, we write the dominant sine wave as $V_z$ [km s$^{-1}$]$=$1.64sin($\omega$$-$57$^\circ$)and the weaker sine waves as 1.50sin($\omega$$-$230$^\circ$) and 2.43sin($\omega$$-$208$^\circ$), respectively." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "In each interval of $R$, the observed dependence of the flux density on $V_z$ and $\omega$ is described as the sum of these three smeared sine waves, multiplied by coefficients $a$, $b$ and $c$ respectively." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)
   - parameter: Sine wave 1 (dominant, coeff b): V_z = 1.64 sin(omega - 57 deg); Sine wave 2 (coeff a): 1.50 sin(omega - 230 deg); Sine wave 3 (coeff c): 2.43 sin(omega - 208 deg); Gaussian smearing sigma_Vz = 0.55 km s^-1, sigma_omega = 15 deg; radial intervals of 0.5 arcsec from 1.0 to 3.5 arcsec  
  > "In order to obtain a simple parameterization of these features, we write the dominant sine wave as $V_z$ [km s$^{-1}$]$=$1.64sin($\omega$$-$57$^\circ$)and the weaker sine waves as 1.50sin($\omega$$-$230$^\circ$) and 2.43sin($\omega$$-$208$^\circ$), respectively." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "Each sine wave is smeared with Gaussians having $\sigma$'s of 0.55 km s$^{-1}$ in amplitude and 15$^\circ$in phase." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)
   - assumption: Flared disc with Gaussian distribution of flaring angles (rms sigma_fl) and constant velocity smearing sigma_Vz = 0.55 km s^-1; V_exp = 1.6 / sin(i) km s^-1  
  > "To see that better, we use the simple picture of a flared disc having a Gaussian distribution of flaring angles with an rms value of $\sigma_{fl}$ and we approximate the smearing in $V_z$ as a constant $\sigma_{Vz}$$=$0.55 km s$^{-1}$, independent from $\omega$." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "The amplitude of the sine wave, 1.6$\pm$0.1 km s$^{-1}$, is the product of $V_{exp}$ by the sine of $i$: $V_{exp}$$=$$1.6/\sin i$ km s$^{-1}$." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)
   - fitting: Sine wave frequencies/phases/smearing estimated by eye; linear coefficients a, b, c found by best fit in each R interval; global sine wave fit to Figure 14 right panel.  
  > "All these parameters have been estimated by eye to approximately match the data." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "What can be stated with confidence is the dominance of a sine wave with amplitude 1.6$\pm$0.1 km s$^{-1}$ and a phase of 54$^\circ$$\pm$6$^\circ$, as obtained from a fit to the right panel of Figure 5" (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)

  > "Figure 6 illustrates the radial evolution of the $^{12}$CO(2-1) emission in successive intervals of $R$, each 0.5 arcsec wide, starting from $R$$=$1 arcsec." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)

   - validation: Compared with values obtained indirectly from single dish observations (1.4 to 2.0 x 10^-8 M_odot yr^-1).

## Qualitative findings

- Absorption spectra over the stellar disc peak at 0 km s^-1, showing no evidence of a significant wind with velocity > ~1 km s^-1 along the line of sight crossing the star's centre.  
  > "The L$_2$ Pup spectra peak at 0, giving evidence for the absence of significant wind along the line of sight crossing the star in its centre." (Absorption over the stellar disc); "The $^{12}$CO line emission must be probing the CSE over several arcseconds away from the star and this is therefore evidence for the absence of significant wind, with a velocity in excess of $\sim$1 km s$^{-1}$, along the line of sight crossing the star in its centre." (Absorption over the stellar disc)
- A northern blue-shifted blob of emission at Doppler velocities between -3 and -5 km s^-1 within < 1 arcsec of the star is identified in 12CO(2-1) and 28SiO(5-4) and tentatively associated with the northern loop observed by Kervella et al. (2014).  
  > "An unexpected feature is a blob of blue-shifted emission visible between $\sim$$-$3 and $\sim$$-$5 km s$^{-1}$ in the north direction, within less than 1 arcsec from the centre of the star." (A second search for north-south bipolar outflows at short distance from the star); "A blob of northern blue-shifted emission at Doppler velocities between $-$3 and $-$5 km s$^{-1}$ has been identified and tentatively interpreted as the "loop" observed in L band by (cite: Kervella2014)." (Summary and conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The high-resolution Band 7 data resolve the stellar disc and inner 20 au, while the unpublished medium-resolution Band 6 data (12CO(2-1) and 28SiO(5-4)) provide reliable imaging up to ~3 arcsec (~200 au) where the Band 7 data suffer from poor uv coverage beyond 0.15 arcsec.  
  > "However, beyond $R$$\sim$0.15 arcsec, such outflows cannot be detected using the data analysed in the preceding sections, the uv coverage being inadequate." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "Having shown the difficulty to detect reliably north/south bipolar outflows from the $^{12,13}$CO(3-2) and $^{29}$SiO(8-7) observations studied in the preceding sections, we now analyse the second set of observations presented in Section 2. They are of the emissions of the $^{12}$CO(2-1) and $^{28}$SiO(5-4) lines, the antenna configuration providing reliable imaging up to distances of $\sim$3 arcsec from the star." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)
- Why this method: Absorption spectra over the stellar disc probe the whole CSE unaffected by maximal resolvable scale constraints; scanning along x = 0 avoids rotation contributions when measuring inner line broadening; evaluating V_{z,20} on the blue wing avoids strong red-side absorption distortions.  
  > "In particular, it probes the whole CSE, unaffected by maximal resolvable scale constraints: it provides a direct measure of the terminal velocity in the form of a narrow absorption peak" (Absorption over the stellar disc); "The inner rim of the gas disc is located at $\sim$2 au from the centre of the star (cite: Homan2017) and the diameter of the beam (FWHM) is $\sim$1.2 au: when staying on the $x$$=$0 line, one may then expect to be free of significant contribution from rotation." (High Doppler velocity wings); "Yet, to be free of the effect of the strong red-side absorption, we evaluate its evolution from the value $V_{z,20}$ of $V_z$ at which 20% of the maximal flux density is reached on the blue side." (High Doppler velocity wings)
- Alternative: Full radiative transfer modelling of the inner line profiles was avoided because strong absorption distortions make speaking of a line width a crude approximation and faithful modelling of the complex reality difficult.  
  > "Very strong distortions caused by absorption illustrate the difficulty of making a faithful radiative transfer modelling of a very complex reality: speaking of a line width is but a crude approximation." (High Doppler velocity wings)
- Alternative: Evaluating a mass loss rate from the inner (< 0.2 arcsec) Band 7 low-velocity maps was rejected because of steep temperature gradients, boundary instabilities, and contamination from the upper part of the disc.  
  > "In such a context it is difficult to ascertain that the maps displayed in Figure 2, which also include emission from the upper part of the disc, are indeed evidence for a north/south outflow and we refrain from attempting from these an evaluation of the mass loss rate." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)
- Alternative: A wind driven exclusively by stellar pulsations with dust playing no dynamical role was rejected because the confinement of SiO within ~80 au indicates dust condensation and momentum transfer, and pulsations alone cannot produce relatively thin discs.  
  > "the observed confinement of the SiO emission supports the condensation of SiO molecules onto dust grains over a distance of some 80 au from the star, implying therefore that over this distance the dust transfers some of its radial momentum to the gas. Moreover, one does not see how pulsations alone could produce winds confined to relatively thin discs." (Summary and conclusion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): In the first set of observations (Band 7), sparse and uneven baselines < 500 m limit reliable imaging to R < ~0.2 arcsec and cause an isotropic wind to artificially appear as north-south emission beyond R ~ 0.15 arcsec.  
  > "Baselines smaller than 500 m are sparse and unevenly distributed, limiting the range of reliable imaging to projected distances from the star $R$$<$$\sim$0.2 arcsec." (Overall picture); "Beyond $R$$\sim$0.15 arcsec, the poor $uv$-coverage at short baseline lengths causes an isotropic wind to appear as a north-south emission" (The wind of L$_2$ Pup, 5.1 Searching for north/south bipolar outflows at short distance from the star)
- Limitation (authors): The parameterization of the emission in phase opposition to the dominant sine wave in terms of two weaker sine waves with eye-estimated parameters is somewhat arbitrary.  
  > "All these parameters have been estimated by eye to approximately match the data." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "While this provides a convenient parameterization of what is observed, its precise form, in particular the description of the emission in phase opposition to the dominant sine wave in terms of two weaker sine waves, is somewhat arbitrary" (The wind of L$_2$ Pup)
- Limitation (authors): The inclination angle i of the large-scale expanding wind disc is unknown, making the retained range of 40+-20 deg and the resulting mass loss rate estimate crude and dependent on somewhat arbitrary arguments.  
  > "Yet, too thin a disc would be highly improbable in the CSE of an AGB star and we retain as a reasonable range of acceptable values of $i$ the interval [20$^\circ$,60$^\circ$], which we write as 40$^\circ$$\pm$20$^\circ$, it being understood that this estimate rests on sensible but somewhat arbitrary arguments." (The wind of L$_2$ Pup); "While crude, the inclination angle of the wind mid-plane being unknown, it has the value of being the first direct measurement of the gas flow escaping the star gravity." (Mass loss rate)
- Limitation (authors): Below ~1 arcsec from the star, insufficient uv coverage in the first dataset, insufficient angular resolution in the second dataset, and complex dynamics prevent definitively ruling out north/south outflows.  
  > "Below $\sim$1 arcsec from the centre of the star, we failed to find any evidence for the presence of collimated outflows oriented north/south. However, difficulties inherent to the observations, uv coverage and angular resolution, and the complexity of the dynamics at stake in this region prevent us from ascertaining that there are no such outflows." (Summary and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The multi-sine-wave decomposition of the CO(2-1) position-velocity diagrams relies on parameters (amplitudes, phases, Gaussian smearing widths) fixed by eye rather than a simultaneous non-linear fit with formal parameter uncertainties.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The mass loss rate derivation assumes a uniform excitation temperature of 50 K and optically thin CO(2-1) emission at R = 2 arcsec without radiative transfer modelling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The inclination of the outer expanding disc (40+-20 deg) is weakly constrained due to the degeneracy between expansion velocity, disc inclination, and flaring angle.
