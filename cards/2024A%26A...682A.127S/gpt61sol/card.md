## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify sub-mm lines and compare epochs**: Use CASSIS with CDMS and JPL spectroscopic information; identify lines and compare CO moment-0 fluxes.  
  > "we used the CASSIS spectroscopic analysis tool" (Line identification); "to extract spectroscopic information from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (cite: CDMSref) and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory database (cite: JPLref)." (Line identification); "From the moment-0 maps of CO (3–2), the flux has increased from 181.5 Jy/(beam km s$^{-1}$) in 2016 to 226.0 Jy/(beam km s$^{-1}$) in 2019 (26% increase)." (Line identification)
   - software: CASSIS, Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy, Jet Propulsion Laboratory database
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with the mostly unchanged 2016 spectrum; HCO+ partially resolved in 2019.  
  > "the majority of lines seen in the 2019 spectrum could be identified without major effort." (Line identification); "This observation, coupled with the better spectral resolution of the 2019 ALMA spectrum, allowed HCO$^+$ to be (partially) resolved from SO$_2$." (Line identification)
2. **Measure the angular source size**: Fit elliptical Gaussians to moment-0 images representing all emission in all spectral windows; deconvolve the main beam.  
  > "Using moment-0 maps combined from the full measurement sets, we used the CASA routine `imfit` to fit elliptical Gaussians to the spatial distribution of the source in each epoch." (Source size and distance); "The images used represent all emission within all spectral windows." (Source size and distance); "The resulting source sizes (deconvolved from the main beam) measured were 178 ($\pm$21) $\times$ 125 ($\pm$36) and 87.9 ($\pm$1.8) $\times$ 59.8 ($\pm$1.7) mas respectively for epochs 1 and 2." (Source size and distance)
   - software: CASA, imfit, tclean
   - parameter: Smooth epoch 2 to 0.51″×0.45″ for comparison.  
  > "As each epoch had different beam sizes, we smoothed the epoch 2 data to the same beam size (0.51$''$$\times$0.45$''$) as epoch 1." (Source size and distance)
   - fitting: Elliptical Gaussians fitted with imfit.  
  > "we used the CASA routine `imfit` to fit elliptical Gaussians to the spatial distribution of the source in each epoch." (Source size and distance)
   - validation: Natural/uniform results agree within 1σ; smoothing preserves epoch 2 size.  
  > "As a sanity check, the images were cleaned using natural and uniform weightings to compare the results, with the different weightings providing the same results per epoch within 1$\sigma$ errors." (Source size and distance); "The `imfit` results provided the same results as those previously found for the unsmoothed epoch 2 data." (Source size and distance)
3. **Derive the kinematical distance**: Fit the 2019 CO spatial distribution; combine its adopted 5σ angular radius with expansion speed and elapsed time: d=(ve×t)/tan(θ).  
  > "The CO (3–2) distribution has a semi-major axis of 45.5 mas, assumed to be the semi-major FWHM/2 of the 2D Gaussian fitted to the CO (3–2) spatial distribution using `imfit`." (Source size and distance); "Converting the FWHM/2=0.0455$''$ to a value[^3] of 5$\sigma$ (=0.097$''$) and adopting this as the source size, $\theta$, we derive the distance $d=(\varv_e \times \textrm{t})/\tan(\theta)$." (Source size and distance)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: ve=232.5 km s−1.  
  > "take half the baseline width as the tangential velocity in one direction, which turned out to be $\varv_e$=232.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Source size and distance)
   - parameter: t=4012 d (=3.464×10^8 s).  
  > "We assume that the CO gas was ejected at the point of the merger, and so $t$=4012 d (=3.464$\times$10$^8$ s) had elapsed." (Source size and distance)
   - assumption: CO ejected at the merger.  
  > "We assume that the CO gas was ejected at the point of the merger," (Source size and distance)
   - assumption: Radial velocity equals the velocity in the far CO wings; half the baseline width supplies the tangential velocity.  
  > "We assume that the radial velocity is equal to the velocities observed in the far wings of the CO (3–2) line," (Source size and distance); "take half the baseline width as the tangential velocity in one direction," (Source size and distance)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitted to the CO spatial distribution with imfit.  
  > "the semi-major FWHM/2 of the 2D Gaussian fitted to the CO (3–2) spatial distribution using `imfit`." (Source size and distance)
   - validation: Distance consistent with (cite: mason2022).  
  > "The kinematic distance we determine is therefore 5.6 kpc, which is consistent with the distance estimate presented in (cite: mason2022)." (Source size and distance)
4. **Map the velocity structure**: Construct CO (3–2) and 29SiO (8–7) moment-0 and moment-1 maps.  
  > "As the dominant emission originates from the CO (3–2) and $^{29}$SiO (8–7) lines, both were used to probe the spatio-kinematic structure in 2019." (Moment maps); "Moment-0 and 1 maps were constructed using the CASA routine `immoment`." (Moment maps)
   - software: CASA, immoment
   - parameter: Velocity range −310 to 160 km s−1; exclude pixels below 5σ rms.  
  > "We extracted the emission across the same velocity range of –310 to 160 km s$^{-1}$." (Moment maps); "Any pixels with values \< 5$\sigma$ than the root–mean–square (rms) noise were neglected." (Moment maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Velocity gradient aligns approximately with CO elongation; 29SiO structure matches 2016 28SiO.  
  > "The gradient axis approximately matches the direction of a slight elongation seen in the CO (3–2) moment-0 maps." (Moment maps); "The $^{29}$SiO (8–7) moment-1 map shows an identical structure to the observed velocity gradient in the $^{28}$SiO (8–7) line from 2016 (cite: kaminski2018)." (Moment maps)
5. **Fit molecular gas conditions**: Fit SO2 first using LTE radiative transfer, then model the other molecules mainly by changing their column densities.  
  > "To characterise the molecular gas observed with ALMA, we used the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) radiative transfer tool available in CASSIS." (Radiative transfer modelling); "We modelled all detected molecules" (Radiative transfer modelling); "The key change made to fit the model was the gas column density for each molecule." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: 20 detected SO2 lines; stated upper-energy range 48–521 K.  
  > "simulating 20 detected SO$_2$ lines to get the best estimates of the SO$_2$ kinetic temperature and column density." (Radiative transfer modelling); "The upper energy level range of the SO$_2$ transitions cover 48–521 K," (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: Two gas components except single Gaussians for 29SiO, HCO+, and CO.  
  > "We model two gas kinematic components for all molecules except for $^{29}$SiO, HCO$^+$, and CO, which are better represented by a single Gaussian." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: 2016 SO2 widths and velocities fixed to K18; CO velocity −72.5 km s−1 and FWHM 200 km s−1.  
  > "For the 2016 observations, the FWHM and $\varv_{\rm LSR}$ were both fixed to values derived in **K18** for both components." (Table 1); "CO was modelled with $\varv_{\textrm{LSR}}$ equal to –72.5 km s$^{-1}$ and a FWHM of 200 km s$^{-1}$, as in **K18**." (Table 2)
   - assumption: All detected molecules share the same LTE conditions and temperature within each gas component.  
  > "We then assume that emission of all detected molecules is produced in the same LTE conditions, meaning we assume the same temperature for all molecules for each gas component." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - assumption: Bracketed column densities calculated from natural (Solar) isotopic abundances.  
  > "Values in brackets have been calculated from natural (Solar) isotopic abundances." (Table 2)
   - fitting: SO2: Monte-Carlo Multi-Chain (MCMC) χ2 fitting algorithm.  
  > "The initial model fitting was performed only on SO$_2$ using a Monte-Carlo Multi-Chain (MCMC) $\chi^2$ fitting algorithm," (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - validation: Red-wing excess in CO and 29SiO remains unmodelled; component assignments are inconsistent between epochs.  
  > "the line profiles of CO and $^{29}$SiO representing the entire source show an excess within the red wings of the lines that is not replicated in our CASSIS model" (CSE structure); "The automatic fit is not consistent in assigning the positions and widths of the components when the two epochs are compared." (CSE structure)
6. **Check line optical depths**: Calculate CO, 28SiO, and 29SiO optical depths from column density, temperature, line width, and transition quantum values.  
  > "All measurable values ($N$, $T$, $\Delta v$) were obtained from the LTE radiative transfer modelling" (Optical depth); "all other constants are associated quantum values of each transition (cite: goldsmith1999)." (Optical depth)
   - parameter: Table 3: N=1.20×10^19, 2.35×10^17, 1.20×10^16 cm−2; FWHM/2=100, 55.95, 55.95 km s−1 for CO, 28SiO, 29SiO.  
  > "| Column density       | 1.20$\times$10$^{19}$ | 2.35$\times$10$^{17}$ | 1.20$\times$10$^{16}$ |" (Table 3); "| FWHM/2 (km s$^{-1}$) | 100                   | 55.95                 | 55.95                 |" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Table 3 uses peak brightness temperatures; SiO isotopologues share temperature and partition function.  
  > "Excitation temperatures were measured as the peak brightness temperature of the spectral feature." (Table 3); "The values used for both isotopologues of SiO, such as temperature and partition function, are the same, with only column density varying between the calculations." (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Line centres are optically thick; line wings are judged optically thin enough to represent source structure.  
  > "The determined values of $\tau$ indicate optically thick emission." (Optical depth); "Therefore, the optically thin wings of the lines represent well the source structure." (Optical depth)
7. **Recover the HCO+ velocity profile**: Subtract the SO2 radiative-transfer model from the 2019 ALMA spectrum.

## Qualitative findings

- CO and 29SiO show a north-east–south-west velocity gradient; the north-east is receding.  
  > "the better spatial resolution reveals a velocity gradient spanning from north-east to south-west, with the north-east region moving away from the observer." (Moment maps)
- Marginally resolved elongation and velocity gradients are consistent with a bipolar configuration.  
  > "The spatial structure is not easy to recognize at the achieved resolution, but a slight elongation can be seen in mapped molecular lines." (CSE structure); "These features are consistent with a bipolar configuration." (CSE structure)
- HCO+ is identified for the first time in V1309 Sco; its 2016 line was blended with SO2.  
  > "Perhaps the most significant is the identification of HCO$^+$ for the first time in V1309 Sco" (Line identification); "the HCO$^+$ (4–3) line is blended with the SO$_2$ 10(4,6)–10(3,7) line in 2016 and could not be identified." (Line identification)
- H2 and HCO+ trace opposite extreme-velocity flows, suggesting differing shock or ambient-gas properties.  
  > "these shock tracers have very different kinematics with H$_2$ observed at extreme blueshifted flow and HCO$^+$ in the extreme redshifted flow." (The properties of the shocks); "This suggests that the shocks propagating in opposite directions interact with the ambient circumstellar gas in very different ways." (The properties of the shocks)
- Almost all accepted H2 models exhibit J-type shocks.  
  > "Finally, almost all models fulfilling our observational constraints exhibit J-type shocks." (H$_2$)
- 2022 optical lines weaken or disappear; increased dust extinction is the proposed explanation.  
  > "A significant difference is the absence of many atomic lines, including several forbidden iron and sulphur lines." (2022 spectrum); "The weakening of the optical spectrum can be attributed to increased extinction due to growing dust column densities, indicating continuing dust formation since 2019." (2022 spectrum)
- Near-infrared blueing is attributed to molecular emission rather than evolution into a blue straggler.  
  > "the apparent increase in blue luminosity in the near-infrared is attributed to the emergence of molecular emission rather than the evolution of V1309 Sco to a blue straggler." (V1309 Sco is not a blue straggler)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Longer 2019 baselines improve spatial resolution; shifted spectral windows target H13CN.  
  > "The longer baselines meant a spatial resolution of almost one order of magnitude better than in 2016." (ALMA / Observations); "in order to cover the H$^{13}$CN (4–3) line ($\nu_{\textrm{rest}}$=345.34 GHz), which was subsequently not detected." (ALMA / Observations)
- Why this method: Two kinematic components simplify overlapping profiles and were expected to constrain gas properties better than one; more components require too many parameters.  
  > "deciding on modelling two components is merely a simplification which was thought to produce more robust constraints on the gas properties than any single-component model." (CSE structure); "Fitting more than two components would require even more parameters, and is less informative." (CSE structure)
- Alternative: Apparent source contraction rejected as unlikely; epoch 1 size errors considered underestimated.  
  > "At face value, comparing the source size in each epoch would indicate that the source has contracted between 2016 and 2019." (Source size and distance); "However, we find this highly unlikely and rather consider the uncertainties in epoch 1 measurements to be largely underestimated." (Source size and distance)
- Alternative: Rotating-sphere interpretation inconsistent with the 29SiO velocity pattern.  
  > "The velocity structure of the $^{29}$SiO (8–7) emission shows a redshifted lobe in the north-east, with less extreme velocities at extended distances." (Moment maps); "This is inconsistent with a rotating sphere, which would show the most extreme velocities in the moment map at the most extended distances." (Moment maps)
- Alternative: UV/X-ray-driven HCO+ formation considered; shocks preferred because UV/X-ray sources are lacking.  
  > "Alternatively, ultraviolet and X-ray fluxes can influence HCO$^+$ abundances through photo-dissociation (cite: kimura2012; cleeves2017)." (HCO$^+$); "Due to a lack of UV or X-ray sources in V1309 Sco, shocks are the most likely candidate for the formation of HCO$^+$." (HCO$^+$)
- Alternative: Hot gas could be residual material cooling after the 2008 eruption.  
  > "The gas may be material that was heated during the 2008 eruption, cooling on a timescale of decades and recombining into molecular gas." (Role of shocks)
- Alternative: Opposite-lobe tracer differences may reflect different shocks, different ambient gas, obscured redshifted H2, or enhanced red-lobe CO.  
  > "This could depend on either the properties of the ambient gas into which the shocks are propagating, or even the shocks themselves." (The properties of the shocks); "It could also be that the NIR H$_2$ emission excited by shocks via the redshifted lobe is compact and could be obscured by circumstellar dust." (The properties of the shocks); "An alternative explanation would be that there is an increased column density of CO surrounding the redshifted lobe that could lead to increased HCO$^+$ formation." (The properties of the shocks)
- Alternative: High-temperature VO/CrO/ScO fit convergence rejected as unrealistic and visually worse.  
  > "We extended the model grids to higher temperatures, but only achieved convergence at unrealistic temperatures of $\sim$5000 K." (Molecule identification and modelling); "Furthermore, upon visual examination, the fit residuals were larger than those at T$_{vib}\leq$ 3500 K for many prominent features." (Molecule identification and modelling)
- How they decided it was enough: Accept H2 shock models agreeing with the detected ratio within 5σ and lying below all ratio upper limits.  
  > "in agreement with the observed ratios to 5$\sigma$ accuracy for the detected line ratio" (H$_2$); "and are below the ratio upper limits calculated for the simulated lines." (H$_2$)
- Limitation (authors): Spatially unresolved asymmetry and only marginal resolution of the molecular remnant.  
  > "Our ALMA maps only marginally resolve the cool component of the V1309 Sco remnant." (CSE structure); "There is some asymmetry in the gas distribution, which we are not able to resolve with the current sub-mm data." (CSE structure)
- Limitation (authors): Overlapping components and moderate/high optical depths undermine detailed component fits.  
  > "It is unlikely to be a real change in the physical parameters of the flow and rather reflects the large uncertainties in modelling the multiple overlapping components." (CSE structure); "the moderate to high optical depths of the mapped emission may be another source of uncertainty." (CSE structure)
- Limitation (authors): CASSIS saturation correction is limited.  
  > "The CASSIS software applies a limited correction on line saturation effects." (Optical depth)
- Limitation (authors): CO may have been ejected before the merger, changing the expansion-time assumption; paper describes the distance as an upper limit.  
  > "This also provides an upper limit on the distance, as the cold CO gas may have been ejected prior to the merger, meaning the time elapsed is potentially much longer and the CO emission radius is therefore larger." (Source size and distance)
- Limitation (authors): Atomic shock models cannot constrain density or shock velocity.  
  > "However, the errors on the line fluxes proved too large to constrain either the gas density or shock velocity." (Atomic line diagnostics)
- Limitation (authors): H2 models do not constrain external UV or cosmic-ray ionisation; density limit reaches the model boundary.  
  > "although the external UV radiation and H$_2$ cosmic-ray ionisation rate cannot be constrained." (H$_2$); "This is a lower limit as larger densities are not covered by the models, but may still be the true pre-shock density." (H$_2$)
- Limitation (authors): Reliable rotational and vibrational temperatures unavailable for VO, CrO, and ScO.  
  > "Due to issues with the fitting of VO, CrO, and ScO, we are unable to present reliable constraints on the rotational and vibrational temperatures of these molecules." (Molecule identification and modelling)
- Limitation (authors): 2022 optical physical-condition analysis not attempted because extinction makes constraints increasingly difficult.  
  > "Therefore, constraining the physical properties of the optical emitting region of the remnant is becoming increasingly difficult for V1309 Sco, and is not attempted using the 2022 observations." (2022 spectrum)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Epoch-to-epoch CO flux comparison needs matched imaging and calibration-error treatment before attributing the reported 26% increase to variability.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The distance requires uncertain velocity geometry, ejection timing, and a 5σ Gaussian radius; the stated upper-limit direction needs checking against the supplied distance formula.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optical-depth inputs are internally inconsistent: the prose uses LTE-fit quantities, Table 3 specifies peak brightness temperatures, and its 28SiO column differs from Table 2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The adopted 200 K and 2100 K temperatures are described as averages of 200/300 K and 1700/2400 K; the averaging or rounding prescription is unclear.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two H2 detections plus three upper limits give grid-dependent shock constraints; no unique shock velocity is established.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): HCO+ profile recovery depends directly on the SO2 blend model; subtraction uncertainty is not quantified.
