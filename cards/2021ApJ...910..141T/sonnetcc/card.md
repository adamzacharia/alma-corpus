   - parameter: Pixel 2.3'', image 250 x 250 pixels

   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Make continuum images and measure noise**: Continuum (2 mm) images created from the data cubes using IMCONTSUB  
  > "images were created from the data cubes using the IMCONTSUB task." (2 Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, imcontsub
   - parameter: Noise 4 mJy/beam (IRAS 03235, IRAS 03245), 1 mJy/beam (IRAS 03271)  
  > "The noise levels of the continuum images are 4 mJy beam$^{-1}$ in IRAS 03235 and IRAS 03245" (2 Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum peaks compared with Spitzer c2d source positions  
  > "the dust continuum peaks are consistent with the Spitzer sources" (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions)
3. **Moment 0 maps and 2D Gaussian fits for sizes and peak positions**: 2D Gaussian fitting to continuum and moment 0 maps; beam-deconvolved sizes, with the synthesized beam given as upper limit when deconvolution fails  
  > "We applied 2D gaussian fitting to the continuum and moment 0 maps." (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions); "In the case that beam-deconvolved sizes could not be obtained, we indicate the synthesized beam as their upper limits." (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: CH3OH moment 0 map made from the 1_0-1_-1 and 3_0-3_-1 lines because they are partially blended  
  > "because these lines are partially blended" (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions)
   - assumption: Distance 293 pc used to convert angular sizes to au  
  > "scaled to the newly measured distance of Perseus (293 pc)" (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: HC3N and CH3OH peaks compared with the Spitzer source positions and with continuum  
  > "the peak position of HC$_{3}$N is consistent with that of continuum image as well as the Spitzer source" (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions)
4. **Derive column densities and excitation temperatures from spectra**: Optically thin LTE model in the CASSIS spectrum analyzer, fitted with MCMC to spectra at the HC3N emission peak  
  > "we used the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) model available in the CASSIS spectrum analyzer, assuming that the lines are optically thin." (3.2 Spectral analyses); "show spectra of HC$_{3}$N and CH$_{3}$OH at the HC$_{3}$N emission peaks" (3.2 Spectral analyses)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Case 1: Tex of HC3N and CH3OH restricted to 15-30 K  
  > "The range of the excitation temperature of both HC$_{3}$N and CH$_{3}$OH is 15–30 K; hereafter Case 1." (3.2 Spectral analyses)
   - parameter: Case 2: Tex 35-40 K (IRAS 03235, IRAS 03245) and 35-50 K (IRAS 03271), comparable to dust temperatures  
  > "the excitation temperature ranges are set at 35–40 K in IRAS 03235 and IRAS 03245, and 35–50 K in IRAS 03271 (Case 2)" (3.2 Spectral analyses)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin lines  
  > "assuming that the lines are optically thin" (3.2 Spectral analyses)
   - fitting: MCMC with N, Tex, FWHM and VLSR as semi-free parameters; bounds define the range in which values are chosen randomly; priors/likelihood not stated  
  > "In the MCMC method, the minimum and maximum values set for parameter in a component define the bounds in which the values are chosen randomly." (3.2 Spectral analyses)
   - validation: Column densities compared with IRAM 30-m results; differences attributed to beam dilution  
  > "Again, this difference in column density could be caused by the beam dilution in the single-dish observations." (3.2 Spectral analyses)
5. **Run chemical network models of the CH3OH/HC3N ratio**: Nautilus hot-core model with a slow warm-up stage, varying cosmic-ray ionization rate and C/O ratio  
  > "We used the chemical network code Nautilus (cite: 2016MNRAS.459.3756R) and a hot-core model with a warm-up stage." (4.1 Chemical network simulations); "We changed the cosmic ray ionization rate ($\zeta$) and the initial C/O ratio" (4.1 Chemical network simulations)
   - software: Nautilus
   - parameter: Cosmic-ray rates 1.3e-17, 3.0e-16, 4.0e-14 s^-1 (Models 1-3)  
  > "$1.3 \times 10^{-17}$" (Table 6); "$3.0 \times 10^{-16}$" (Table 6); "$4.0 \times 10^{-14}$" (Table 6)
   - parameter: C/O ratio 0.4 (Models 1-3), 0.5 (Model 4), 1.2 (Model 5)  
  > "The C/O ratio of 0.4 corresponds to C$^{+}$ = $7.3 \times 10^{-5}$ and O = $1.76 \times 10^{-4}$" (Table 6 notes)
   - parameter: Initial T 10 K rising to 200 K during warm-up  
  > "The initial temperature is 10 K and rises to 200 K during the warm-up period." (4.1 Chemical network simulations)
   - parameter: Slow warm-up period of 1e6 yr  
  > "we utilized the model with a slow warm-up period ($1 \times 10^{6}$ yr)" (4.1 Chemical network simulations)
   - assumption: Dust temperature equal to gas temperature  
  > "The dust temperature is assumed to be equal to the gas temperature." (4.1 Chemical network simulations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare observed ratios with models to constrain age and temperature**: Observed CH3OH/HC3N ratios (with errors, Cases 1 and 2) overplotted on model ratio vs time; ages after the temperature reaches 25 K compared; model temperature checked against observed dust temperature  
  > "We now focus on comparison with observations, starting with ages after the temperature reaches 25 K" (4.2 Comparison); "The temperature scale arises from only the model." (4.2 Comparison)
   - parameter: For non-detections, ratio assumed lower than the IRAS 03245 value  
  > "the CH$_{3}$OH/HC$_{3}$N ratio should be lower than that in IRAS 03245" (4.2 Comparison)
   - assumption: Dust temperature of IRAS 03235 expected to be similar to the other two sources  
  > "it is expected that it is not so far from those in IRAS 03245" (4.2 Comparison)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model temperatures at matching ages compared with the dust temperatures of the sources  
  > "This dust temperature agrees with the dust temperature derived in IRAS 03245 (cite: 2009AA...493...89E)." (4.2 Comparison)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CH3OH and HC3N used as indicators of evolutionary stage; high-resolution imaging needed  
  > "In the present paper, we focus on methanol and cyanoacetylene as potential indicators to constrain evolutionary stages of low-mass YSOs." (Introduction); "high angular-resolution imaging observations of CH$_{3}$OH and HC$_{3}$N in low-mass YSOs are essential" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Case 2 added because lines may trace inner dense cores, so Tex may be underestimated in single-dish-like assumptions; slow warm-up chosen for low-mass YSOs  
  > "We adopt the second case because the observed lines may come from inner dense cores compared with previous single-dish observations" (3.2 Spectral analyses); "we utilized the model with a slow warm-up period" (4.1 Chemical network simulations)
- Alternative: Ages after ~1e6 yr rejected because model dust temperatures exceed 70 K, inconsistent with observed dust temperatures  
  > "Thus, we omit a possibility of ages after $\approx 10^{6}$ yr." (4.2 Comparison)
- Alternative: Model No. 2 rejected for IRAS 03245  
  > "Model No. 2 cannot reproduce the observed ratio in this source." (4.2 Comparison)
- Alternative: Matching temperature ranges of ~25-28 K and ~60-70 K judged unlikely  
  > "The other temperature ranges ($\sim 25-28$ K and $\sim 60-70$ K) are not consistent with the observed dust temperatures" (4.2 Comparison)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): HC3N peak offset from the Spitzer source not understood at this resolution  
  > "The origins of such discrepancies are not clear at the current angular resolution." (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity may be too low to detect weak CH3OH lines in IRAS 03235 and IRAS 03271  
  > "The noise levels of the ACA 7-m observations could be too high to detect weak lines of CH$_{3}$OH in IRAS 03235 and IRAS 03271." (3.1 Results: Spatial distributions)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperature may be underestimated in the data  
  > "the excitation temperature may be underestimated in our data." (3.2 Spectral analyses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only HC3N 16-15 (one line) constrains HC3N, so Tex and N are degenerate; the two cases differ by a factor of ~3-4 in N and the CH3OH/HC3N ratio changes from 3.7 to 16.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Resolution of ~11'' x 8'' (~3000 au) cannot separate hot-corino scales; CH3OH may be diluted so the ratio is a beam-averaged value.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CH3OH non-detections in two sources give no stated upper limits, so the ratio constraint there is qualitative.
