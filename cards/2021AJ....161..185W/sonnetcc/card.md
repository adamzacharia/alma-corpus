## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble surface brightness and velocity maps**: Use MUSE stellar mass and velocity maps, MUSE Halpha maps, and PHANGS-ALMA CO moment 0 and moment 1 maps (broad masks, highest resolution) with error maps  
  > "The cubes are then collapsed into standard moment 0 (integrated intensity) and moment 1 (intensity-weighted velocity), along with error maps." (2.2 PHANGS-ALMA); "(MUSE stellar mass and velocity, MUSE H$\alpha$, and ALMA CO, collectively referred to as “kinematic maps”)" (2 Data)
   - software: pPXF, pymusepipe
   - parameter: Broad masks for ALMA moment maps  
  > "We opt to use broad, rather than strict maps, to maximise our completeness (at the cost of increased noise)." (2.2 PHANGS-ALMA)
   - parameter: MUSE stellar kinematics and stellar populations fit on Voronoi bins to continuum S/N of 35  
  > "Both of these stages are performed on Voronoi binned data to a stellar continuum signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) of 35, to maximise reliability." (2.1 PHANGS-MUSE)
   - assumption: Single stellar population fit; averaging over populations does not bias results since stellar mass is dominated by old stars  
  > "The DAP fits only a single stellar population, so the stellar masses and velocities are an average of young and older stars." (2.1 PHANGS-MUSE)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Negligible impact on results from strict vs broad masking in most cases  
  > "We find a negligible impact on our results using these different masking schemes in most cases." (2.2 PHANGS-ALMA)
2. **Pre-process maps before integrals**: Mask MUSE foreground stars, subtract systemic velocity from ALMA moment 1, remove slits not crossing the bar, symmetrise each integral  
  > "We therefore remove any pixels with $|v| > 300\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}$." (3.4 Data Preparation); "We estimate the systemic velocity using PAFit (cite: 2006Krajnovic), and subtract it from the ALMA velocity field (moment 1) map." (3.4 Data Preparation); "This is done by removing any slits that do not at least partially cover the bar." (3.4 Data Preparation)
   - software: PAFit
   - parameter: |v| > 300 km/s relative to systemic removed (MUSE only)  
  > "We therefore remove any pixels with $|v| > 300\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}$." (3.4 Data Preparation)
   - parameter: Each integral symmetrised to run from -x0 to x0  
  > "we make sure each integral goes from $-x_0$ to $x_0$." (3.4 Data Preparation)
   - assumption: Foreground-star cut is arbitrary  
  > "This cut is arbitrary, and may need to be tailored for other data sets." (3.4 Data Preparation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Recovered pattern speeds with masked or non-masked stars not significantly different  
  > "However, we find that the recovered pattern speeds with masked or non-masked stars are not significantly different;" (3.4 Data Preparation)
3. **Compute Tremaine-Weinberg integrals and fit slope**: Place boxcar pseudo-slits parallel to the line of nodes, compute intensity-weighted velocity and position per slit, fit slope of <v> vs <x> = Omega_P sin(i)  
  > "In this work, we take $h(y)$ to be a boxcar function from $y-{\rm d}y$ to $y+{\rm d}y$, to represent a pseudo-slit parallel to the line of nodes." (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method); "Thus, taking a number of slits and plotting $\langle v \rangle$ versus $\langle x \rangle$ yields a straight line with a slope equivalent to $\Omega_{\rm P} \sin(i)$." (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method)
   - software: scipy.odr, NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: Slits as long as possible and 1 arcsec wide  
  > "Motivated by these results, we opt to make each individual slit as long as possible, and 1$''$ wide to approximately match the resolution of the data." (3.3 Effects of slit length and width on recovered pattern speeds)
   - parameter: Tested slit lengths 10 to 150 arcsec and widths 0.5 to 10 arcsec  
  > "We use a number of slit lengths, from 10$''$ to 150$''$" (3.3 Effects of slit length and width on recovered pattern speeds)
   - assumption: Disk is flat  
  > "The galaxy disk is flat;" (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method)
   - assumption: Single, well-defined pattern speed  
  > "The disk contains a single, well-defined pattern speed;" (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method)
   - assumption: Tracer obeys the continuity equation  
  > "The tracer obeys the continuity equation." (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method)
   - fitting: Orthogonal Distance Regression (scipy.odr) with per-pixel errors summed in quadrature in both <x> and <v>  
  > "These are then propagated into the fitting routine using Orthogonal Distance Regression (ODR), which allows us to effectively account for errors in both $\langle x \rangle$ and $\langle v \rangle$." (3.2 Uncertainties on the Pattern Speeds)
   - validation: Slit length/width convergence tests for each galaxy; measured pattern speeds converge as slits become longer, slit width has minimal impact  
  > "We find that the measured pattern speeds tend to converge as the slits become longer (typically, slightly longer than the bar)." (3.3 Effects of slit length and width on recovered pattern speeds); "We also find that the slit width has a minimal impact on the recovered pattern speed" (3.3 Effects of slit length and width on recovered pattern speeds)
4. **Propagate uncertainties by Monte Carlo**: Perturb line of nodes and galaxy centre by their measured errors, 1000 bootstraps, quote median and 16th/84th percentiles  
  > "We do this via a Monte-Carlo method, perturbing the line of nodes and the galaxy centre by the measured errors (cite: 2020Lang)." (3.2 Uncertainties on the Pattern Speeds); "We use 1000 bootstraps, measure the pattern speed for each of these iterations, and quote the pattern speed as the median of this distribution" (3.2 Uncertainties on the Pattern Speeds)
   - software: NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: 1000 bootstraps; inclination and distance errors not included  
  > "We do not include uncertainties from the inclination or distance in our uncertainty for the pattern speeds." (3.2 Uncertainties on the Pattern Speeds)
   - assumption: Position-angle error dominates the error budget  
  > "and that it is the error in the position angle that dominates" (3.2 Uncertainties on the Pattern Speeds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Quality-flag each pattern speed**: A posteriori flags Q=1 to 4 assigned independently by three authors per tracer; final flag is the mode, or highest flag if all disagree; Q=1 or 2 are 'well-constrained'  
  > "This flagging is performed independently on each tracer by three of the authors, and our final flag is the mode of these three flags." (3.5 Quality Flagging); "we will define “well-constrained” Tremaine-Weinberg values as having $Q=1$ or 2" (3.5 Quality Flagging)
   - parameter: Q=1 single well-defined slope converged and stable with decreasing slit width; Q=2 clear multiple slopes; Q=3 poor fit; Q=4 insufficient data  
  > "Single, well defined slope: integrals have converged, pattern speed is stable with decreasing slit width;" (3.5 Quality Flagging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Test ISM tracers with a null (no-pattern) axisymmetric model**: Axisymmetrise CO velocity (from Lang et al. rotation-curve fits) and surface brightness (annulus averages), blank pixels where emission is not detected, apply TW method to get Omega_null; compare with Omega_clump from real CO data; repeat with Halpha and with ~5 arcsec CO; toy density-wave models  
  > "For the velocity field, we project the fits from (cite: 2020Lang) into the frame of the galaxy, and for the CO data we take the average surface brightness within a number of annuli." (4 Application to ISM Tracers); "We then mimic the incomplete coverage of these maps by blanking pixels in these axisymmetrised profiles where emission is not detected" (4 Application to ISM Tracers)
   - parameter: Higher resolution (~1 arcsec) vs lower resolution (~5 arcsec) ALMA data compared  
  > "we compare $\Omega_{\rm clump}$ from the higher resolution ($\sim$1$''$) and lower resolution ($\sim$5$''$) ALMA data." (4 Application to ISM Tracers)
   - assumption: No pattern speed is present in the axisymmetrised maps, so any deviation is due to incomplete coverage and clumpy morphology  
  > "In this case, there is no pattern speed present in these maps, and any deviation is simply due to the incomplete CO coverage and the non-axisymmetry of the CO morphology." (4 Application to ISM Tracers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Points lie near the 1:1 relation of Omega_null vs Omega_clump; low and high resolution give much the same result  
  > "As we can see, the points tend to lie close to the ${1\!:\!1}$ relation" (4 Application to ISM Tracers); "This indicates that this effect is endemic to the use of CO, and not simply due to the high resolution of our data." (4 Application to ISM Tracers)
7. **Compare ISM-tracer and stellar pattern speeds**: Define pattern speed difference = (Omega_clump - Omega_P)/Omega_P for galaxies well-constrained in both stellar mass and the ISM tracer (9 galaxies for each of Halpha and CO)  
  > "We compare values flagged as well-constrained in both the stellar mass, and the ISM tracer in question (for both H$\alpha$ and CO, this leaves us with 9 galaxies)." (5.2 Comparisons Between Tracers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Omega_clump systematically higher than Omega_P by more than the ~10% errors  
  > "Typically, we find that $\Omega_{\rm clump}$ is higher than $\Omega_{\rm P}$" (5.2 Comparisons Between Tracers)
8. **Derive resonance radii and ratios**: Convert CO rotation curves (Lang et al.) to angular velocity and find where it equals Omega_P for co-rotation; ILR/OLR from Omega_P = Omega(R) -/+ kappa/2 with smooth splines; deproject bar length and form R_CR/R_bar and h_r/R_CR  
  > "By converting the rotation velocity to an angular velocity, $\Omega (R)$, the co-rotation radius is simply where the angular velocity is equal to the pattern speed." (6 Locations of Major Resonances); "In this case, to avoid issues with numerical derivatives, we use smooth splines fitted to the rotation curves." (6 Locations of Major Resonances)
   - software: NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: Consecutive crossing points within 2 sigma are associated with the same R_CR; mean taken as nominal value, range as uncertainty  
  > "For these points, we associate these to the same $R_{\rm CR}$ if they are within $2\sigma$, and we take the mean of them as the nominal values, with the range as the uncertainty." (6 Locations of Major Resonances)
   - assumption: Observed CO velocity profile is a proxy for the circular velocity  
  > "We use this observed velocity profile as a proxy for the true circular velocity of the galaxy" (6 Locations of Major Resonances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rotation curves from CO, Halpha and stars typically similar; R_CR errors similar to gravitational torque analysis  
  > "Typically, the rotation curves derived from the CO maps are very similar to those derived from H$\alpha$ and stars in our sample" (6 Locations of Major Resonances); "These errors are similar to those obtained using gravitational torque analysis (cite: 2016Querejeta)." (6 Locations of Major Resonances)
9. **Correlate pattern speeds and bar properties with global parameters**: Kendall tau correlations between Omega_P, R_CR, R_bar, R, S_bar and projection or global galaxy parameters, with Monte Carlo perturbation of values by errors  
  > "For $10{,}000$ realisations, we perturb each value by its associated errors in both $x$ and $y$, and repeat the $\tau$ calculation." (7 Correlations with Global Galaxy Parameters)
   - software: Seaborn, Matplotlib
   - parameter: Only points with relative Omega_P error <= 0.5 (removes NGC 7496)  
  > "but in this work we find that this removes only one galaxy (NGC 7496)." (7 Correlations with Global Galaxy Parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Correlation significant only if larger than its 1 sigma Monte Carlo spread; checked for dependence on inclination and Delta PA  
  > "If the correlation is larger than this 1$\sigma$ error, then the correlation is significant." (7 Correlations with Global Galaxy Parameters)

## Qualitative findings

- Applied to clumpy ISM tracers, TW gives an angular frequency at a representative radius set by the clump distribution (Omega_clump), not Omega_P  
  > "this method erroneously gives a signal that is simply the angular frequency at a representative radius set by the distribution of these clumps" (abstract)
- Later Hubble type has larger R_CR/R_bar; molecular-gas-rich galaxies have higher Omega_P; bulge-dominated galaxies have lower Omega_P; no clear trend between bar strength and Omega_P or stellar mass surface density and Omega_P  
  > "more molecular-gas rich galaxies have higher $\Omega_{\rm P}$, and more bulge-dominated galaxies have lower $\Omega_{\rm P}$." (abstract); "there are no clear trends between the bar strength and $\Omega_{\rm P}$" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: PHANGS-MUSE and PHANGS-ALMA give many galaxies with homogeneous data at cloud-scale resolution and allow cross-checks between tracers  
  > "this gives us an unprecedented opportunity not only to measure pattern speeds homogeneously for a large sample of galaxies" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: TW chosen for minimal model assumptions and purely kinematic basis  
  > "stands out as one of the most popular approaches, due to its minimal model assumptions, and the fact that it is purely based on the observed kinematics" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Strict masks tested instead of broad masks for CO moment maps: negligible impact in most cases; only matters for low-surface-brightness galaxies  
  > "This choice only matters for the low surface brightness galaxies in the sample, for which we typically find a poor fit in any case." (2.2 PHANGS-ALMA)
- Alternative: ISM tracers rejected for the main analysis because the continuity assumption is not valid for clumpy CO and Halpha  
  > "For this reason, the bulk of our analysis is performed on pattern speeds measured from stellar kinematics." (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method)
- How they decided it was enough: Slits as long as possible since measured pattern speeds converge with slit length; slit width 1 arcsec to match resolution  
  > "We find that the measured pattern speeds tend to converge as the slits become longer (typically, slightly longer than the bar)." (3.3 Effects of slit length and width on recovered pattern speeds)
- Limitation (authors): TW recovers only a single pattern speed; multiple pattern speeds (bars and spirals) not separated  
  > "it is important to note that the Tremaine-Weinberg method can only recover a single pattern speed in a galaxy." (9 Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Bias from secondary spiral pattern not quantified; needs simulations with known ground truth  
  > "Attempting to quantify the bias this may present would require detailed simulations of galaxies where we know a ground truth for the pattern speed." (3.1 The Tremaine-Weinberg Method)
- Limitation (authors): Small number statistics limit correlation analysis  
  > "Given that we are only confident in pattern speeds measured using stellar masses and velocities, this work is hindered by low number statistics." (9 Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Resonance radii approximate; observed CO velocity overestimates circular velocity where non-axisymmetry dominates; inclination and distance errors not in pattern speed uncertainty  
  > "As such, we highlight that these resonance locations are approximations, but represent our best estimation of the true value." (6 Locations of Major Resonances)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 10 galaxies with reliable stellar pattern speeds, 9 for correlations; correlation conclusions are fragile
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Quality flags depend on subjective by-eye judgement by three authors
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Omega_clump vs Omega_P offset is inferred from a handful of toy models and 9 galaxies; the true bias mechanism is not demonstrated with simulations
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Internal inconsistencies in the text: CO offset ~50% in abstract vs ~40% in body; R 1.1 vs 1.3 and h_r/R_CR 2.6 vs 2.7 in different sections
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rotation curves from CO used for resonances, which may overestimate circular velocity in bars
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA CO data are internal DR products and MUSE DR 2.0 is described only in a paper in preparation
