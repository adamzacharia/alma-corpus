## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate galaxy-integrated stellar masses, SFR_SED, and L_UV via multi-wavelength SED fitting**: Cross-match galaxy positions within 1'' to COSMOS2015 and ASTRODEEP catalogs and perform multi-wavelength SED fitting using CIGALE  
  > "we perform multi-wavelength SED fitting using the CIGALE code (Boquien et al. 2019)." (2.2. Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies); "All of the CRISTAL galaxies have counterparts within 1" (2.2 Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies)
   - software: CIGALE, CLOUDY
   - parameter: Delayed-τ SFH: τ_main = [100, 300, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000] Myr; Age_main = 50 Myr steps in [50: 1000] Myr; τ_burst = [10, 50, 100] Myr; Age_burst = [10, 20, 50] Myr; f_burst = [0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2]  
  > "τ_main [Myr] 100, 300, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000 Age_main [Myr] 50 Myr steps in [50: 1000] τ_burst [Myr] 10, 50, 100 Age_burst [Myr] 10, 20, 50 f_burst [Myr] 0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2" (Table A.1)
   - parameter: Metallicity Z = [0.2, 1] Z_⊙; Nebular log_10 U = -3.0, line width = 300 km s^-1; E(B-V) = 0.1 steps in [0.0: 2.0], E(B-V) factor = 0.44, R_v = 3.1  
  > "Z [Z_⊙] 0.2, 1 Nebular emission: CLOUDY log_10 U -3.0 line width [ km s^-1 ] 300 Dust attenuation: Calzetti00 E(B-V) 0.1 steps in [0.0: 2.0] E(B-V) factor 0.44 R_v 3.1" (Table A.1)
   - parameter: SFR_SED averaged over the last 100 Myr  
  > "We derive stellar masses and SFRs averaging the SFH over the last 100 Myr (SFR_SED) from the best-fit SED models." (2.2. Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies)
   - assumption: Bruzual & Charlot (2003) stellar population synthesis models, Chabrier (2003) IMF (0.1-100 M_⊙), Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation law, flat universe with Ω_M = 0.3, Ω_Λ = 0.7, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1  
  > "The model SEDs are generated with stellar population synthesis models of Bruzual & Charlot (2003) under a Chabrier (2003) initial mass function with a range of 0.1–100 M_⊙ assuming exponentially declining star formation histories (SFHs). We adopt Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation law and CLOUDY (Ferland et al. 1998) nebular emission line template." (2.2. Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies); "Throughout this paper, we assume a flat universe with the cosmological parameters of Ω_M = 0.3, Ω_Λ = 0.7, σ_8 = 0.8, and H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1." (1. Introduction)
   - fitting: SED grid fitting in CIGALE  
  > "we perform multi-wavelength SED fitting using the CIGALE code (Boquien et al. 2019)." (2.2. Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies)
   - validation: Consistent with LePhare results from the ALPINE program within 1σ uncertainty, and SFR_SED is compared against SFR_UV + SFR_IR  
  > "which are consistent with the results from LePhare code in the ALPINE program within the 1σ uncertainty." (2.2 Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies); "In Figure B.1, we compare SFR_UV + SFR_IR and SFR_SED." (Appendix B: comparison of SFR_SED and SFR_UV + SFR_IR)
2. **Combine visibilities, image rest-frame 158 µm dust continuum, and measure flux densities and S/N**: Concatenate visibilities with CASA/concat, exclude ±0.45 GHz around [C II] line, make dirty mfs continuum images (natural weighting and uv-tapered) without primary beam correction, pick peak flux within 0.5'' radius of [C II] position, and smooth 2.0''-tapered image to 3.0'' beam with imsmooth for total flux densities  
  > "We use the archive data by combining visibilities with the CASA/concat task." (2.3. Observation and Data products); "To ensure avoiding the contamination of [C II] lines to the dust continuum, we exclude ±0.45 GHz, which correspond to ±400 km s^-1 and ±450 km s^-1 for z = 4.5 and 5.5 respectively, from the [C II] central frequencies" (3.1. Dust continuum detected galaxies); "Here we make the images with a beam size of 3.0'' by applying CASA/imsmooth to the 2.0''-tapered images to transform the resolution of each source to a common beam size and to make sure that all of the flux densities are included within a single beam." (3.2. Dust size measurement)
   - software: CASA, concat, tclean, imsmooth
   - parameter: uv taper scales: 0.25'', 0.5'', 1.0'', 2.0''; final smoothed beam: 3.0''; search aperture radius: 0.5''; RMS calculated with 3σ clipping where PB response is 0.5 to 0.9; detection threshold S/N >= 4  
  > "we create low-resolution images with uv taper of 0.25'', 0.5'', 1.0'', and 2.0'' as well as high-resolution images with natural weighting. In all images with different resolutions, we pick maximum fluxes in dust continuum images within a circle of radius 0.5'' around the position of previous [C II] detection." (3.1. Dust continuum detected galaxies); "The noise is estimated by calculating 3σ clipped root-mean-square (RMS) values of all pixels within the Field-of-View (FoV) where the primary beam correction value is between 0.9 and 0.5 to avoid the impact of the target galaxy for sure." (3.1. Dust continuum detected galaxies)
   - assumption: Excluding ±0.45 GHz avoids broad [C II] line contamination (FWHM/2 ~ 250 km s^-1); 3.0'' beam includes all flux density within a single beam  
  > "we exclude ±0.45 GHz, which correspond to ±400 km s^-1 and ±450 km s^-1 for z = 4.5 and 5.5 respectively, from the [C II] central frequencies since broad component of [C II] emission lines could reach ~ 250 km s^-1 in FWHM/2 (Ginolfi et al. 2020b)." (3.1. Dust continuum detected galaxies)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of flux densities from tapered images against visibility fitting (UVMultifit) shows agreement within 1σ errors (except CRISTAL-21)  
  > "We see the visibility fitting recovered the fluxes of the sources in lower resolution images well within the 1-σ errors, except for CRISTAL-21 (see text)." (Figure 4 caption)
3. **Measure effective radii of rest-frame 158 µm dust continuum in the visibility plane**: Subtract continuum sources > 2'' away using CASA tclean (down to 1.5σ with 2'' masks) and uvsub; fit a 2D circular exponential disk profile (n = 1) directly to visibilities using UVMultifit

## Qualitative findings

- Typical star-forming galaxies at z = 4–6 have ~1 dex lower L_IR and ~10 times smaller Σ_IR than DSFGs/SMGs, indicating moderate, disk-wide star formation rather than compact central starbursts.  
  > "The CRISTAL galaxies have ∼1 dex smaller infrared luminosities ( _L_ IR ) and are slightly more extended in dust continuum emission than DSFGs or SMGs. Consequently the median IR surface densities Σ IR are ∼10 times smaller" (5 Summary and Conclusion); "This suggests that representative galaxy population at z ~4–6 undergoes moderate star formation with a lower Σ_IR compared with the dusty galaxies indicating intensive, centrally concentrated star formation." (5. Summary and Conclusion)
- The average obscured fraction of star formation (f_obs) correlates positively with stellar mass, showing no clear evolution from z = 0–2.5 at M_* < 10^10 M_⊙ but a possible decrease at M_* > 10^10 M_⊙ compared to z = 0–2.5.  
  > "On the other hand, we find the possible evolution of the obscured fraction from _z_ ∼ 5 to _z_ ∼ 0–2.5 at the range" (4.1 Fraction of obscured star formation)
- Dust continuum effective radii are on average ~2 times more extended than rest-frame UV radii, contrary to Illustris TNG50 predictions.  
  > "The effective radii of the dust emission are on average ~1.5 kpc and are ~ 2 times more extended than the rest-frame UV." (abstract); "Overall, CRISTAL galaxies possibly have a more extended distribution in dust thermal emission than in UV radiation, and it is contrary to the expectation from the simulation." (Figure 6 caption)
- CRISTAL galaxies follow the stellar mass-size relation of late-type galaxies (LTGs) and Lyman-break galaxies (LBGs) rather than early-type galaxies (ETGs), indicating they are growing their stellar disks via global star formation.  
  > "In contrast, as shown in Figure 10, the dust continuum sizes of CRISTAL galaxies follow a similar trend as the mass-size relation of LTGs or LBGs." (4.3.2. Stellar mass-size relation); "In contrast, as shown in Figure 10, the dust continuum sizes of CRISTAL galaxies follow a similar trend as the mass-size relation of LTGs or LBGs." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining extended (C43-4/5/6) and compact (C43-1/2) CRISTAL ALMA configurations with compact archival observations provides both high spatial resolution (~0.3'' / ~1.9 kpc) and a large maximum recoverable scale (~4.5'' / ~28.3 kpc) to recover extended dust emission.  
  > "CRISTAL observations cover a wide range of spatial frequencies in the uv plane with a combination of extended (C43-4/5/6) and compact (C43-1/2) array configurations to achieve not only high-resolution (~ 0.3'', corresponding to ~ 1.9 kpc in physical scale at z = 5) but also a large maximum recoverable scale (~ 4.5'', corresponding to ~ 28.3 kpc in physical scale at z = 5)." (2.3. Observation and Data products); "As these archival data typically cover shorter spatial frequency ranges in the uv plane compared with the CRISTAL observations, adding these archival data improves the sensitivities, especially to extended emissions." (2.3. Observation and Data products)
- Why this method: Measuring effective radii directly in the visibility plane with UVMultifit avoids systematic uncertainties from uv coverage, weighting, and non-linear clean deconvolution, which is critical when ~1/3 of sources have S/N < 5.  
  > "However, reconstructed images through the Fourier transform of the visibility data and non-linear clean algorithm strongly depend on the uv coverage of visibilities and its weight, which could lead to systematic uncertainties. Moreover, as about one-third of CRISTAL galaxies lack high S/N (S/N ≳ 5) in the dust continuum images, image-based analysis may make measurements uncertain. To achieve homogenous size measurements and to utilize full information from the observations, we directly measure the effective radii of dust continuum emission from the visibility data." (3.2. Dust size measurement)
- Alternative: Latest COSMOS2020 catalog (Weaver et al. 2022) was checked against COSMOS2015 (Laigle et al. 2016), and COSMOS2015 was chosen because it showed better target identifications without spatial offsets.  
  > "We find there are spatial offsets between the detected position in the COSMOS2015 catalog and the latest the COSMOS catalog (Weaver et al. 2022). For our target galaxies, COSMOS2015 catalog shows better identifications." (2.2. Properties of the CRISTAL galaxies)
- Alternative: Two-component visibility fitting for nearby continuum sources (>2'' away) was tested and showed no significant difference compared to subtracting clean components with tclean and uvsub.  
  > "Note that there is no significant difference if we conduct the two-component fitting to take into account the other continuum sources." (3.2. Dust size measurement)
