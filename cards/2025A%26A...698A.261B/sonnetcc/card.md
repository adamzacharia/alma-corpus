## Analysis recipe

1. **Make velocity-integrated moment-0 maps of isolated iCOM transitions**: Integrate isolated transitions (no adjacent contamination or contamination below 3 sigma) over the full line profile in the ALCHEMI continuum-subtracted cubes; group maps by Eu range and overlay contours  
  > "we looked for transitions isolated enough (i.e. with either no contribution from other adjacent species or with a contamination from adjacent features that are below 3$\sigma$ level)" (Emission distribution)
   - software: GILDAS MAPPING, GILDAS CLASS
   - parameter: Integration velocity range 70-380 km/s  
  > "The velocities used for the integration are within the range 70-380 km s$^{-1}$." (Integrated Intensity Images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectra and fit Gaussian lines**: Extract spectra from beam-sized regions centred on GMC 7, GMC 6, pSSC 5, pSSC 2; shift to LSRK of each transition; fit Gaussians to get integrated intensity, FWHM, Vpeak; reject lines contaminated at 15% or more  
  > "We then centred each spectrum at the local standard of rest velocity (LSRK) corresponding to the rest frequency of each transition used in this work before performing a Gaussian line fitting to the detected transitions." (Spectra and line parameters); "Transitions which were contaminated by more than 15% by a transition from another species (i.e., contributing to 15% or more of the total integrated intensity of our desired transition)" (Spectra and line parameters)
   - software: GILDAS CLASS, WEEDS
   - parameter: Detection threshold 3 sigma on the intensity peak  
  > "A threshold of 3$\sigma$ on the intensity peak was set to consider a line to be detected." (Spectra and line parameters)
   - parameter: Spectral rms from zeroth-order polynomial fit to line-free channels  
  > "For each spectrum, we fitted a zeroth order polynomial to the line-free channels from the continuum-subtracted data cubes to retrieve the information about the spectral root-mean-square (rms)." (Observations)
   - fitting: Single or multiple Gaussian line fits in CLASS (details of priors not stated)  
  > "We used these same packages to extract the spectra and perform Gaussian line fitting." (Observations)
   - validation: Line contamination checked with the ALCHEMI line identification and WEEDS (JPL and CDMS)  
  > "performed an additional inspection using WEEDS (cite: maret_2011), an extension of CLASS, which uses both the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL; (cite: pickett_1998)) and the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS; e.g. (cite: muller_2005; endres2016)) databases" (Spectra and line parameters)
3. **Derive T_rot and N_tot with rotational diagrams**: Rotational diagram per species and region assuming LTE, optically thin emission and Rayleigh-Jeans; one or two temperature components; blended transitions split 50/50  
  > "This method assumes Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) and optically thin emission." (Methodology); "We also assume the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation holds." (Methodology)
   - parameter: Beam-filling factor 0.5 for GMC scales (1.6'') and ~0.01 for pSSC scales (0.12'')  
  > "The beam filling factor, (see Sec. [4.1](#subsec:methodology)), is 0.5 for GMC scales and $\sim 0.01$ for pSSC scales." (Rotation diagrams)
   - parameter: Each of two blended transitions assigned 50% of total integrated intensity  
  > "we thus assumed that each transition accounts for 50% of the total line integrated intensity." (Methodology)
   - parameter: Species showing a single component evaluated for both emission sizes (1.6'' and 0.12'')  
  > "In these cases, since we do not know the region of emission of these species, we calculated column densities for the two emission sizes, i.e. for the GMC scale ($\theta_s=1.6\ensuremath{''}$) and the pSSC scale ($\theta_s=0.12\ensuremath{''}$)." (Results from the rotational diagrams)
   - assumption: Low-Eu component arises from GMC scales (1.6''), high-Eu component from pSSC scales (0.12'', ~2 pc)  
  > "we assumed that the first component, traced by the lower upper-level energy transitions, arises from the GMCs whilst the second component, traced by the higher upper-level energy transitions, arises from the pSSCs." (Methodology)
   - assumption: Ratio of 1 between symmetry states for one species (symbol missing in the text), since kinetic temperature exceeds the ~0.1 K level difference  
  > "we assumed a / ratio of 1 since the kinetic temperature is higher than the energy difference of $\sim 0.1$ K between the levels of the different symmetry states (cite: matthews1985)" (Methodology)
   - fitting: Best fit by minimizing the reduced chi-square; 15% calibration error and spectral rms included in integrated-flux uncertainties  
  > "The best fit was calculated by minimizing the reduced chi-square" (Methodology)
   - validation: Second component justified by shifts in FWHM/Vpeak with Eu and by emission becoming more compact with Eu  
  > "Our choice is supported by the fact that higher upper-energy level transitions showed often a different FWHM or $V_\mathrm{peak}$ compared to the lower upper-energy transitions" (Methodology)
4. **Test two-component interpretation with mean FWHM and Vpeak vs Eu**: Use relatively isolated transitions to compute mean FWHM and Vpeak per component and compare as a function of Eu towards GMC 6, pSSC 5 and pSSC 2  
  > "The uncertainties were derived by adding in quadrature the errors from the Gaussian fits with the spectral resolution of 10 km s$^{-1}$." (Gaussian fits and temperature components)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Two components clear for one species only, not clear for the others, given the 10 km/s resolution  
  > "the presence of two components is only clear for whilst it is not clear for and" (Gaussian fits and temperature components)
5. **Non-LTE LVG analysis of methanimine**: Grid of ~10,000 LVG models per region varying column density, H2 density and kinetic temperature, source size free, uniform sphere geometry  
  > "For each region we ran a grid of models ($\sim 10,000$) varying the column density, the gas density and temperature within $10^{13}-10^{18}$ , $10^4-10^8$ cm$^{-3}$, and $10-150$ K, respectively." (Non-LTE analysis); "We left the source size as a free parameter. Then, for the geometry, we chose a uniform sphere (cite: osterbrock1974) as the most physically realistic choice." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - software: grelvg
   - parameter: Lines with Eu >= 27 K used; the two lowest-Eu lines (225.554, 172.267 GHz) and the 312.336 GHz line excluded  
  > "We thus ran the LVG with the rest of the lines ($E_\mathrm{u}\geq 27$ K)." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Linewidth fixed at 50 km/s (average across regions)  
  > "the average linewidths of chosen is 50 km s$^{-1}$, which is the average linewidth across the regions" (Non-LTE analysis)
   - parameter: 15% calibration uncertainty in the integrated intensity  
  > "We also included a 15% calibration uncertainty in the integrated intensity" (Non-LTE analysis)
   - assumption: Ortho-to-para ratio set to the statistical value of 3  
  > "The ortho-to-para ratio of was set to the statistical value of 3." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - fitting: Grid search with chi-square; 1 sigma confidence levels correspond to a 68% range  
  > "The $1\sigma$ confidence levels constrained correspond to a 68% confidence range." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - validation: Initial fit with all lines gave chi2_r>5 (GMC 6, pSSC 5, pSSC 2) so low-Eu lines were dropped; population diagrams then used to test remaining non-LTE effects  
  > "However, this resulted in a bad fit ($\chi^2_r>5$) for GMC 6, pSSC 5, and pSSC 2." (Non-LTE analysis)
6. **Correct rotational diagram for opacity and source size (population diagram)**: Apply population diagram method using LVG optical depths and emission size to see whether opacity dominates the deviations  
  > "We corrected the rotation diagram for the optical depth and source size for all the regions using the population diagram method (cite: goldsmith_1999), taking the output values of the LVG analysis for these parameters" (Non-LTE analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Corrected diagrams still deviate from a straight line, with T_rot below the LVG minimum gas temperature, so non-LTE effects are present  
  > "Therefore, is also affected by non-LTE effects." (Non-LTE analysis)
7. **Compare column densities between species to infer chemical links and origin**: Plot column densities of iCOM pairs at GMC (1.6'') and pSSC (0.12'') scales and compare ratios and correlations with literature formation pathways  
  > "shows the relation between the column densities of the iCOMs, at both GMC scales (corresponding to a source size of $1.6\ensuremath{''}$) or pSSC scales" (Discussion)
   - assumption: A correlation does not necessarily imply a direct chemical link; it may reflect similar physical processes  
  > "However, it is worth noting that a correlation between two species does not always imply a direct chemical link." (Chemical link and formation pathways for iCOMs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- iCOM emission is concentrated in the inner CMZ, within ~6'' (~100 pc) of the kinematic centre, and becomes more compact with increasing Eu  
  > "within 6$\ensuremath{''}$ or $\sim 100$ pc around the kinematic centre" (Conclusions); "emission from iCOMs becomes more compact with increasing $E_{\mathrm{u}}$" (Conclusions)
- GMC 7 shows a deficiency in N-bearing iCOMs relative to the other regions at GMC scales  
  > "At GMC scales (governed by a cold extended component of the iCOMs), GMC 7 shows a deficiency in N-bearing iCOMs compared to the other regions." (Conclusions)
- O-bearing iCOMs are well correlated at GMC scales across the four regions  
  > "At GMC scales, O-bearing species are well correlated over the four regions" (Discussion)
- No hot (>100 K) rotational-temperature component is found in any region; iCOMs appear sub-thermally excited

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALCHEMI provides an unbiased spectral survey of the NGC 253 CMZ at resolution high enough to study the origin of iCOM emission  
  > "Therefore, ALCHEMI provides an ideal template to study iCOMs toward a nuclear extragalactic starburst, at a high enough angular resolution to investigate the origin of their emission for the first time." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Multiple gas components preferred over non-LTE/opacity effects for rotational-diagram curvature in most species, because of Eu-dependent line shapes and compactness; non-LTE LVG used for methanimine as it is the only species with collisional rates  
  > "We favoured the first option, i.e. the presence of multiple gas components." (Methodology); "is the only species from this work for which collisional excitation rates are available." (Non-LTE analysis)
- Alternative: Deviation of rotational diagrams could be due to non-LTE or opacity effects instead of multiple components; multiple components were favoured  
  > "This behaviour can be either due to the presence of multiple gas components with different physical conditions or to non-LTE or opacity effects (cite: goldsmith_1999)." (Methodology)
- Alternative: GMC 5 excluded from the analysis because of absorption features and faint iCOM emission  
  > "would require a specific radiative transfer modelling, which is beyond the scope of this paper. We thus excluded this GMC from our analysis." (Emission distribution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Spectral resolution (10 km/s) is insufficient to separate blended E/A states and limits identification of gas components  
  > "the E- and A- states are often completely blended together, the spectral resolution of 10 km s$^{-1}$ being insufficient to disentangle the two states." (Spectra and line parameters); "Higher spectral and angular resolution observations are needed to identify possible different gas components more clearly." (Gaussian fits and temperature components)
- Limitation (authors): Derived T_rot are not true gas temperatures because rotational diagrams deviate from LTE fits  
  > "Due to the fact that we observe deviations from the fits in the RDs, it is clear that the derived $T_{\text{rot}}$ are not representative of the true gas temperature." (Results from the rotational diagrams)
- Limitation (authors): Two-component treatment assumes the warm compact component contributes negligibly to the cold extended one  
  > "As a caveat, this implies that the contribution from the warm compact component to the cold extended one is negligible." (Rotational Diagrams)
- Limitation (authors): Abundance study not performed (column densities only)  
  > "An abundance study is beyond the scope of this paper." (Results from the rotational diagrams)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only methanimine has an LVG treatment; other species' column densities rely on LTE rotational diagrams that the paper itself says are not representative of true gas conditions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Splitting emission into 1.6'' and 0.12'' components is an assumption (pSSC size taken from literature) and the pSSC-scale column densities scale strongly with the assumed size.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No formal uncertainties on the correlations in the pair plots; p-value method unspecified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Moment-0 maps and Gaussian fits depend on ALCHEMI line identifications partly by private communication.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The chemical-link and shock/heating interpretations are qualitative; no chemical modelling is done.
