## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce ALMA data and make CO(4-3) cube and continuum maps**: Standard scriptForPI calibration in two CASA versions, mstransform to a common 25 km/s axis, statwt, uvcontsub, then tclean  
  > "We reduced the data with the standard `scriptForPI` scripts in the corresponding CASA versions 4.2.2 (for program 2013.1.00668.S) and 5.1.1 (for program 2017.1.01020.S)." (Target: zC406690); "Next, we recompute the visibility weights with `statwt` and subtract the continuum with `uvcontsub` using line-free channels." (Target: zC406690)
   - software: CASA, scriptForPI, mstransform, statwt, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: channel width 25 km/s  
  > "to convert the two datasets onto the same spectral axis with a channel width of 25 km/s" (Target: zC406690)
   - parameter: clean threshold 2-sigma, sigma from a previous clean run  
  > "The clean threshold is set to 2-sigma, where sigma is the RMS noise measured from a previous clean run." (Target: zC406690)
   - parameter: phase centre J2000 149.746250 deg, 2.084444 deg  
  > "to produce the clean data cube at the phase center ’J2000 149.746250$^\circ$ 2.084444$^\circ$" (Target: zC406690)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Obtain the H-alpha major-axis rotation curve**: Reuse earlier SINFONI-AO reduction; kinematic maps from pixel-by-pixel single-Gaussian fits; rotation curve along the kinematic major axis  
  > "The kinematic maps were obtained by a pixel-by-pixel single-Gaussian fitting (with H$\alpha$ $\rm S/N>5$ per pixel)" (Target: zC406690); "allow for the construction of an extended rotation curve along the kinematic major-axis, up to twice the effective radius" (Target: zC406690)
   - parameter: SINFONI-AO, 50 mas/pixel scale, effective spatial resolution 0.18 arcsec  
  > "at a scale of $\rm 50\, mas/pixel$ and an effective spatial resolution of 0.18$^{\prime \prime}$ for the reduced data cubes" (Target: zC406690)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit mass models (dynamical ring, flux ring, contracted halo) to the H-alpha rotation curve**: Parametric forward modelling of the major-axis rotation curve with DM halo, bulge, thick disk and/or razor-thin Gaussian ring; three models A (dynamical ring), B (flux ring), C (contracted halo); fitted with a 2D tool and with dysmalpy  
  > "We use a parametric forward-modelling approach, with a mass model consisting of a dark matter halo, a central stellar bulge, a thick disk and a razor-thin Gaussian ring" (Mass modelling); "we consider three different mass models representing three different scenarios" (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - software: emcee, dysmalpy
   - parameter: 400 walkers, 300 iterations each after 100 burn-in iterations  
  > "The MCMC fitting procedure is run using 400 walkers with 300 iterations each after a burn-in phase of 100 iterations." (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - parameter: NFW concentration fixed to 4; inclination fixed to 25 deg  
  > "The concentration of the NFW profile is fixed to $c_{\mathrm{NFW}}=4$" (Rotation Curve Fitting); "$i$ | $25^{o}$ | fixed" (Table 3)
   - parameter: baryon mass prior: initial log M = 11.2, Gaussian with 0.5 dex standard deviation  
  > "This gives $\log {\left( M_{\mathrm{baryon, init}}/M_\odot \right)} = 11.2$ which is then free to vary with a Gaussian prior with a 0.5dex standard deviation." (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - parameter: ring R_peak = 4.6 +/- 0.15 kpc and FWHM_ring = 5.0 +/- 0.3 kpc from the H-alpha flux used as initial values with Gaussian priors  
  > "We find $R_{\rm peak} = 4.6 \pm 0.15\, \rm kpc$ and $\rm FWHM_{\rm ring} = 5.0 \pm 0.3\, \rm kpc$" (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - parameter: free parameters: M_baryon, B/T, R_p, FWHM_ring, M_vir (or f_DM) and sigma_0  
  > "In total, our free parameters are $M_{\rm baryon}, B/T, R_{\rm p}, FWHM_{\rm ring}, M_{\rm vir}$ (or $f_{\rm DM}(R_{\rm eff})$) and $\sigma_0$." (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - assumption: constant velocity dispersion sigma_0 with pressure-support correction  
  > "taking a constant velocity dispersion $\sigma_0$ throughout the midplane" (Mass modelling)
   - assumption: razor-thin ring adequate for a toroidal distribution  
  > "an infinitely thin ring is an adequate approximation for a toroidal mass distribution, particularly along the mid-plane" (Ring Mass Distribution)
   - assumption: disk thickness fixed at q0 = 0.25  
  > "the intrinsic thickness of the disk is fixed to $q_0 = 0.25$" (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - assumption: bulge de Vaucouleurs profile with R_eff,bulge = 1 kpc  
  > "an effective radius of $R_{\mathrm{eff,bulge}} =1\, \mathrm{kpc}$ to represent a compact distribution" (Mass modelling)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; best fit = posterior median, 1-sigma from 16th and 84th percentiles; likelihood not stated  
  > "using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo Bayesian analysis exploring the posterior distribution of our model parameters simultaneously, using the `PYTHON` package `emcee`" (Rotation Curve Fitting); "The bestfit values are estimated as the median of the posterior distribution" (Rotation Curve Fitting)
   - validation: Walker trace plots checked (>50 autocorrelation times); chi2_red and AIC model comparison; dysmalpy and 2D fits agree  
  > "Reviewing the walker trace plots verifies that our parameter space is well explored with $>50$ times the autocorrelation time." (Rotation Curve Fitting); "We find that all best-fitting values agree well within their uncertainties for each of the three models respectively, when comparing both fitting procedures." (Rotation Curve fitting with dysmalpy)
4. **Derive CO(4-3) kinematics and compare with the H-alpha model**: Per-spaxel single Gaussian velocity map; rotation curve from circular apertures of 0.5 x PSF along the major axis with Gaussian centroid; compared with a mock Model A rotation curve at ALMA resolution  
  > "By performing individual Gaussian fitting for each spaxel, we construct a CO(4-3) velocity map" (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics); "We position circular apertures of $0.5\times$PSF along the major-axis that maximizes S/N and fit a Gaussian profile to determine the centroid velocity" (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics)
   - parameter: CO position calibrated with JWST/NIRCam (both on GAIA DR3), SINFONI moment-0 aligned to clump positions  
  > "we calibrate the CO position first using *JWST*/NIRCam, as both are registered to GAIA DR3 astrometry" (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics)
   - fitting: single-Gaussian fits per spaxel and per aperture; the CO rotation curve is not fitted directly  
  > "The resulting rotation curve obviously cannot be fitted for directly, as our model has more degrees of freedom than data points" (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics)
   - validation: Mock Model A rotation curve at ALMA resolution compared with the CO points  
  > "We create a mock model using our best-fit values for the Dynamical ring model (A), with the degraded resolution of the ALMA observation." (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics)
5. **Measure CO(4-3) flux and total molecular gas mass**: Circular aperture of radius 1 arcsec over the galaxy centre, single-Gaussian fit to the line; converted with the Tacconi2018 prescription  
  > "by placing a circular aperture with radius $1^{\prime \prime}$ over the center of the galaxy, and fitting a single-Gaussian for the line intensity" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas); "We convert it to mass following (cite: Tacconi2018):" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - parameter: D_L = 17.64 Gpc, R_41 = 2.4, alpha_CO = 3.6  
  > "with $D_L = 17.64\, \mathrm{Gpc}$, and standard conversion factor for the CO($4 \rightarrow 3$) emission ($R_{41} = 2.4$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}} = 3.6$)" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - assumption: metallicity function chi(Z) from Genzel2012 with 12+log(O/H) = 8.38  
  > "from (cite: Genzel2012), for a metallicity of" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - fitting: single Gaussian to the aperture spectrum  
  > "fitting a single-Gaussian for the line intensity" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive the total dust mass from the 142 GHz continuum**: Optically thin modified-blackbody conversion of the 142 GHz flux density following Magnelli2020  
  > "from which we can derive the total dust mass $M_{\mathrm{dust}}$ (cite: Magnelli2020)" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - parameter: rest-frame T = 25 K, beta = 1.8, kappa = 0.0431 m^2/kg at 352.6 GHz  
  > "(taking a rest-temperature of $25\, \text{K}$)" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas); "$\beta = 1.8$ is the dust emissivity, $\kappa_{\nu_0}=0.0431\, \mathrm{m^2\, kg^{-1}}$" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - assumption: CMB corrections negligible; optically thin emission  
  > "We assume corrections due to CMB photons typically negligible at this redshift and an optically thin emission." (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Dust-to-gas ratio and metallicity implied by it compared with the ionized-gas metallicity  
  > "in good agreement with the estimate based on the ionized gas" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
7. **Test whether dust and gas can hide the central bulge**: Compute A_V from the H2 column in a 2 kpc aperture scaled by the dust-to-gas ratio; attenuate bulge light in g, i and J bands for screen and mixed geometries; find the gas fraction needed to drop below 3 sigma  
  > "We consider two cases for the absorbing dust: (i) assuming the dust is distributed between the observer and the bulge (“screen")" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas); "The flux can drop bellow a $3\sigma$ (dotted red line) detection in all filters only when at least $\gtrsim 10$% of the total molecular gas" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - parameter: bulge aperture 2 kpc  
  > "the Hydrogen column density within an aperture of $2$ kpc (matching the assumed bulge size)" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - parameter: M/L_g = 1.1, M/L_i = 0.8, M/L_J = 0.45  
  > "We take $M/L$ ratios of $M/L_{\rm g} = 1.1$, $M/L_{i} = 0.8$, and $M/L_{\rm J} = 0.45$" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - assumption: N_H approximately N_H2 at z=2  
  > "it can be assumed that $\mathrm{N_H} \approx \mathrm{N_{H_2}}$" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - assumption: tau = A_lambda / 1.038  
  > "with the optical depth being $\tau_\lambda = A_\lambda / 1.038$" (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the upper limit on available cold gas set from the clump SFRs  
  > "This is consistent with the upper limit we have set earlier for the available cold gas." (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
8. **Estimate photometric redshift and mass of the western companion**: SED fit with Bagpipes to HST and JWST photometry in a 0.5 arcsec radius aperture; Kroupa IMF, Calzetti attenuation, BC03 models, solar metallicity, exponentially declining SFH  
  > "We fit the SED using the code BAGPIPES (cite: Carnall2018), assuming a (cite: Kroupa2001) initial mass function" (SED fitting of the western companion); "We fix the metallicity to a fiducial Solar value and adopt an exponentially declining star formation history." (SED fitting of the western companion)
   - software: Bagpipes
   - parameter: A_V bounded 0-5, z_phot bounded 0-4  
  > "The visual extinction is bound between $0<A_{\mathrm{V}}<5$ and the redshift $0<z_\mathrm{phot}<4$." (SED fitting of the western companion)
   - fitting: Bagpipes SED fit; median and 16th/84th percentiles reported  
  > "The median $z_\mathrm{phot} = 2.14^{+0.32}_{-0.42}$ (16$^{th}$ and 84$^{th}$ percentiles)" (SED fitting of the western companion)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO(4-3) velocity map shows a clear rotation pattern with a PA very similar to that of H-alpha  
  > "we construct a CO(4-3) velocity map that reveals a clear rotation pattern with a very similar PA as the H$\alpha$" (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics)
- CO(4-3) and the 142 GHz continuum peak between the dynamical centre and the western clumps; the ALMA resolution is too low to localise the emission  
  > "indicating that the CO(4-3) peaks between the center of the ring and the western clump." (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics); "The spatial resolution of these observations is too low to localize the origin of the emission" (Target: zC406690)
- No dust continuum or CO(4-3) detection of the western companion in the ALMA field  
  > "there is no detection of either dust continuum or CO(4-3) emission" (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA gas and dust data used to test whether enough dust exists to extinguish a central bulge  
  > "Using the observations taken with *ALMA* we can test if there is enough dust to allow for such high extinction of the bulge." (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
- Why this method: Major-axis rotation curve used because it is less sensitive to non-circular motions  
  > "We use the major-axis RC as it is less sensitive to deviations from circular motions which are more noticeable along the minor-axis" (Rotation Curve Fitting)
- Alternative: A flux-emitting ring over a faint exponential disk (Model B) and a cuspy/contracted dark matter halo (Model C) were considered and disfavoured against the massive ring model  
  > "We also consider a purely flux-emitting ring superposed over a faint exponential disk" (abstract); "both disfavored against a massive ring model" (abstract)
- Alternative: A companion piercing the centre (interaction-induced ring) is judged unlikely  
  > "With this data at hand, we conclude it is unlikely to be a satellite of zC406690." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ALMA resolution too low to locate the dust and CO emission  
  > "A specific determination as to the location of the dust is unclear given the low resolution" (Target: zC406690)
- Limitation (authors): CO rotation curve has too few points to be fitted directly  
  > "as our model has more degrees of freedom than data points" (CO(4-3) emission line kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Current ALMA data are too shallow to detect central emission  
  > "The current ALMA observations are x5 shorter than what would be needed to achieve a detection." (Hiding the Bulge - Dust and molecular gas)
- Limitation (authors): Low MCMC acceptance rate may indicate slow convergence  
  > "which might indicate slow convergence, but encourages parameter exploration" (Rotation Curve Fitting)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Continuum S/N of 4.5 at 142 GHz and 2.5 at 236 GHz is low, so dust mass and DGR rest on a marginal detection with an assumed 25 K temperature
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single dust temperature and emissivity are assumed for the whole galaxy, and the gas mass uses an assumed R_41 and alpha_CO
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO flux comes from a 1 arcsec aperture on a barely resolved source and the second ALMA dataset is not clearly used for the headline numbers
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The bulge-hiding argument relies on assumed M/L ratios and attenuation geometry and is untested at the centre
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model comparison relies on a single rotation curve with few independent points
