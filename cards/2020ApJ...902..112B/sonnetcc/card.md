## Analysis recipe

1. **Obtain 1.2 mm continuum flux densities and detections**: Use the ASPECS band-6 continuum mosaic and the tapered-image flux densities of González-López et al. (2020); count sources detected above 4 sigma among the 1362 UV-selected galaxies  
  > "González-López et al. (2020) make use of flux density measurements made from the tapered images" (Results); "over our sensitive ASPECS mosaic shows that 18 of these galaxies are detected at" (Results)
   - parameter: 9.3 uJy/beam peak 1-sigma sensitivity; 4 sigma detection threshold  
  > "The peak sensitivity in our 1.2 mm continuum observations is 9.3$\mu$Jy ($1\sigma$) per synthesized beam (1.53$''$ $\times$ 1.08$''$: González-López et al. 2020)." (Observations and Sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirms earlier pilot and Dunlop et al. (2017) detections; compared individual detections against earlier reports  
  > "Our new observations confirm all of our previously claimed detections at" (Previous Reported Continuum Detections)
2. **Select the UV-selected z=1.5-10 galaxy sample and measure HST/Spitzer photometry**: Lyman-break dropout color criteria plus photometric redshifts on HST photometry measured with SExtractor and EAZY; Spitzer/IRAC photometry using HST as a template  
  > "we utilize both Lyman-break selection criteria as well as a photometric redshift selection to ensure our samples are as comprehensive as possible." (Selections); "In performing photometry, a simultaneous fit of the flux of a source of interest and its neighbors is performed, the flux from neighboring sources is subtracted, and then aperture photometry on the source of interest is performed." (Flux Measurements)
   - software: SExtractor, EAZY
   - parameter: Source detection on sqrt(chi^2) image; Kron factors 1.6 (colors) and 2.5 (total)  
  > "color measurements are made in Kron-style (1980) scalable apertures with a Kron factor of 1.6." (Flux Measurements)
   - parameter: Stars excluded with SExtractor stellarity; expected low-z contamination 3-8%  
  > "Potential stars are excluded from our selection using the measured SExtractor (Bertin & Arnouts 1996) stellarity criterion." (Selections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Contamination by lower-z galaxies or stars estimated from earlier work  
  > "The expected contamination levels in these color-selected samples by lower-redshift galaxies (or stars) is estimated to be on the order of 3-8% (e.g., Bouwens et al. 2015)." (Selections)
3. **Derive UV slopes beta and stellar masses**: Power-law fit to HST UV photometry for beta (fixed at -2.2 for z=8-10); stellar-population SED fitting with FAST using HST, K-band and Spitzer/IRAC photometry, with nebular-line corrections to IRAC fluxes  
  > "we make use of the publicly-available code FAST (Kriek et al. 2009) to perform this fitting." (UV-continuum slopes and Stellar Masses); "to account for the impact of nebular emission lines on the observed IRAC fluxes." (UV-continuum slopes and Stellar Masses)
   - software: FAST
   - parameter: e^(-t/tau) star formation history with tau from 1 Gyr to 100 Gyr; rest-frame V extinction 0-2 mag; age from 10 Myr to age of universe  
  > "We assume an $e^{-t/\tau}$ star formation history and allow the $\tau$ parameter to have any value from 1 Gyr to 100 Gyr." (UV-continuum slopes and Stellar Masses)
   - assumption: Chabrier (2003) IMF, metallicity 0.2 Zsun  
  > "We assume a Chabrier (2003) IMF" (UV-continuum slopes and Stellar Masses); "Our fixing the fiducial metallicity to 0.2 $Z_{\odot}$ is motivated by studies of the metallicity of individual" (UV-continuum slopes and Stellar Masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: FAST masses compared with MAGPHYS and Prospector masses (Appendix A): FAST ~0.1 dex lower  
  > "with those derived from the MAGPHYS software (da Cunha et al. 2008)" (Appendix A)
4. **Adopt a far-IR SED and dust-temperature evolution to convert 1.2 mm flux to L_IR**: Modified blackbody SED with beta_d=1.6 and redshift-dependent T_d from a joint fit to literature measurements, with CMB correction C_nu; IR luminosity integrated over 8-1000 micron; Kennicutt (1998) conversion to SFR  
  > "As is common practice (e.g., Casey 2012), we will adopt a modified blackbody (MBB) form to model the far-IR spectral energy distributions of galaxies" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution); "we derive the following relationship between dust temperature and redshift:" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution)
   - parameter: T_d [K] = (34.6 +/- 0.3) + (3.94 +/- 0.26)(z-2); alternative fixed T_d of 35 K or 50 K  
  > "T_d [K] = (34.6 \pm 0.3) + (3.94 \pm 0.26)(z-2)" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution)
   - assumption: Dust emissivity index beta_d = 1.6; light-weighted dust temperatures; CMB effect included  
  > "all measurements have been converted to their equivalent values using an emissivity index $\beta_d$ of 1.6 and using the light-weighted dust temperatures" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution); "accounting for the impact of the CMB (e.g., da Cunha et al. 2013: §3.1.1)" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution)
   - fitting: Joint fit to all dust-temperature measurements (method details not stated)  
  > "we derive the following relationship between dust temperature and redshift:" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution)
   - validation: Checked that a mid-IR power-law SED template gives similar conversion factors; derived L_IR agree with MAGPHYS-based values of Aravena et al. (2020) to 0.01 dex in the mean  
  > "following e.g. Álvarez-Márquez et al.(2016) with a mid-IR power-law, yields similar 1.2mm to IR luminosity conversion factors" (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution); "Our derived IR luminosities are just 0.01 dex higher in the mean than those employed by Aravena et al. (2020)" (Continuum Detections of Individual Sources at 1.2 mm)
5. **Predict and count continuum detections; derive L_IR of detected sources**: Predict 1.2 mm flux for all 1362 galaxies from IRX-mass and IRX-beta relations with the MBB SED and compare predicted >4 sigma counts with the observed 18  
  > "Using the above procedure, we calculated the expected flux for our entire sample of 1362" (Expected Number of Continuum Detections); "15, 28, and 8 sources, respectively, are predicted to show" (Expected Number of Continuum Detections)
   - parameter: IRX_z=0 = 1.7(10^(0.4(1.86(beta+1.85))) - 1); Reddy and SMC variants with dA/dbeta 1.84 and 1.1  
  > "IRX_{z=0} = 1.7 (10^{0.4(1.86(\beta+1.85))} - 1)" (Expected Number of Continuum Detections)
   - assumption: IRX relations from z~0-3 valid at higher z (limited evolution)  
  > "Given the limited evolution in these relations, we expect the predicted results to be reasonably accurate in estimating the overall numbers from our program." (Expected Number of Continuum Detections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Observed count of 18 compared with predicted 15 / 28 / 8 (16 if only masses above 10^9.5 Msun are considered)  
  > "The total number of" (Continuum Detections of Individual Sources at 1.2 mm)
6. **Stack the 1.2 mm continuum in bins of stellar mass, beta and UV magnitude**: Image-plane stacking of ALMA continuum maps with inverse-variance weights; flux read off the stack via convolution with the primary beam  
  > "the ALMA continuum maps of the relevant sources are mapped onto the same position and stacked in the image plane, weighting each in proportion to the expected 1.2$\,$mm continuum signal divided by the noise squared (per beam)" (Stacked constraints on the Infrared Excess); "We derive a flux density from the stack based on a convolution of the image stack (3.3$''$$\times$3.3$''$ aperture) with the primary beam." (Stacked constraints on the Infrared Excess)
   - parameter: Six stellar-mass bins; five beta bins; weights proportional to inverse square of noise  
  > "We consider six different bins of stellar mass:" (IRX vs. Stellar Mass); "Each source is weighted according to the inverse square of the noise." (Table 9 notes)
   - assumption: Individually undetected sources are unresolved at the observed resolution  
  > "Individually undetected sources are assumed to be unresolved at the resolution of our observations." (Stacked constraints on the Infrared Excess)
   - fitting: Bootstrap and formal uncertainties both quoted  
  > "Both the bootstrap and formal uncertainties are quoted on the result (presented first and second, respectively)." (Table 4 notes)
   - validation: Three weighting schemes (UV flux, stellar mass, equal) for the low-mass stack all give non-detection  
  > "None of the stacks show a significant detection" (IRX vs. Stellar Mass)
7. **Fit IRX-stellar mass and IRX-beta relations to stacks**: Fit power law IRX_M = (M/M_s)^alpha to z=1.5-3.5 stacks; fit IRX-beta form with dA_UV/dbeta and beta_int by maximum likelihood, with 68% confidence intervals  
  > "In quantifying the dependence, we made use of the power law relation" (IRX vs. Stellar Mass); "Our maximum-likelihood derived values for" (Infrared Excess versus beta)
   - parameter: M_s = 10^9.15 (+0.18/-0.16) Msun, alpha = 0.97 +/- 0.17; dA_UV/dbeta = 1.81 (+0.18/-0.14), beta_int = -1.86 (+0.14/-0.10) for M>10^9.5 Msun at z~1.5-3.5  
  > "arrived at a best-fit value for $M_s$ and $\alpha$ of $10^{9.15_{-0.16}^{+0.18}}$ $M_{\odot}$ and 0.97$_{-0.17}^{+0.17}$, respectively." (IRX vs. Stellar Mass)
   - assumption: For lower-mass galaxies beta_int fixed to the high-mass value; alternative beta_int of -2.23 and -2.30 also explored  
  > "Fixing $\beta_{int}$ to be the same as for the higher-mass galaxies" (Infrared Excess versus beta)
   - fitting: Maximum likelihood with 68% confidence intervals (likelihood and priors not stated)  
  > "then computing 68% confidence intervals on the derived parameters." (Infrared Excess versus beta)
   - validation: Compared with pilot-study and literature relations; PROSPECTOR masses give a steeper relation (Appendix D)  
  > "The derived relationship is significantly steeper than our fiducial determination" (Appendix D)
8. **Derive the beta-stellar mass relation and the bivariate IRX(beta, M)**: Use CANDELS z=1.3-2.5 beta and Prospector masses; find M_s and alpha of the beta-M relation by balancing source counts above and below it; then fit the bivariate form IRX(beta,M) to the ASPECS stacks  
  > "compute the square of the difference in the number of sources on each side for each of the six regions, and then minimize the square of the differences." (Correlation with Stellar Mass and UV-continuum Slope); "The derived infrared excesses vs. $\beta$ and stellar mass were then fit using the expression given in Eq." (IRX as a Function of Stellar Mass and beta)
   - parameter: beta_int = -2.3 and dA/dbeta = 1.42 fixed; best fit M_s = 10^9.07 Msun, alpha = 0.92  
  > "The best-fit values of $M_s$ and $\alpha$ are 10$^{9.07}$ $M_{\odot}$ and 0.92, respectively." (Correlation with Stellar Mass and UV-continuum Slope)
   - fitting: Minimization of squared count differences in six regions (beta-M); fit of bivariate form to stack bins (method not detailed)  
  > "compute the square of the difference in the number of sources on each side for each of the six regions, and then minimize the square of the differences." (Correlation with Stellar Mass and UV-continuum Slope)
   - validation: beta-M parameters consistent at 1 sigma with the IRX-mass fit; compared with Alvarez-Marquez et al. (2019) dependence  
  > "demonstrating that the IRX-$\beta$ and IRX-stellar mass relations we derive are essentially equivalent." (Correlation with Stellar Mass and UV-continuum Slope)
9. **Compare IRX estimators against measured fluxes**: Predict 1.2 mm flux for detected sources and for sources expected at >2 sigma with eight estimators; compute mean, median, scatter and quartiles of observed-minus-predicted  
  > "divided the result by the measurement error $ef_{obs}$, and then determined the average as well as the upper and lower quartiles." (Predictive Power of Different Estimators for IRX)
   - parameter: Eight estimators including IRX_M99, IRX_z=0, IRX_SMC, IRX_M,0, IRX_beta, IRX_M, IRX(beta,M) and (IRX_beta IRX_M)^(1/2)  
  > "As one final predictor, we include a comparison against the flux density predicted taking the geometric mean of our derived" (Predictive Power of Different Estimators for IRX)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Scatter 0.65-0.81 dex; best for IRX(beta,M) and the geometric mean estimator  
  > "The scatter ranges from 0.65 to 0.81 dex" (Predictive Power of Different Estimators for IRX)
10. **Derive dust corrections and SFR densities at z>=3**: Apply IRX(beta,M) to CANDELS GOODS-N/S samples to get dust corrections per UV-luminosity bin, apply to UV LFs, convert to SFR density, and add ULIRG-like far-IR bright contributions  
  > "In deriving dust corrections for each bin in $UV$ luminosity, we make use of the stellar masses and $UV$-continuum slopes $\beta$ derived for our large CANDELS samples" (Dust Corrections for z>3 Samples); "is to simply include them based on dedicated searches for these sources in the IR." (Star Formation Rate Densities at z>=3)
   - software: FAST
   - parameter: IRX forced to zero below 10^9 Msun; z~3 average dust correction ~5 for UV-bright galaxies  
  > "we force the infrared excesses of sources with stellar masses less than 10$^9$ $M_{\odot}$ to be zero, consistent with our derived observational constraints." (Dust Corrections for z>3 Samples)
   - assumption: Constant SFR for 100 Myr and Chabrier IMF in the UV-to-SFR conversion  
  > "This relationship assumes a constant SFR for 100 million years." (Star Formation Rate Densities at z>=3)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The detection fraction rises steeply with stellar mass; galaxies below about 10^9.25 Msun show no dust continuum even in stacks  
  > "we find that the fraction of detected galaxies in our samples increases sharply with increasing stellar mass" (Summary)
- IRX-beta for high-mass z~1.5-3.5 galaxies follows a Calzetti-like relation, low-mass ones an SMC-like relation  
  > "For lower-mass $z\sim 1.5$–3.5 galaxies, the IRX-$\beta$ relation we derive is most consistent with an SMC-like relation." (Summary)
- Star formation transitions from mostly unobscured to mostly obscured near z~5  
  > "we can see that star formation is mostly unobscured at $z>5$, mostly obscured at $z<5$" (Star Formation Rate Densities at z>=3)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ASPECS covers the HUDF region with the deepest ancillary data and is sensitive enough to probe low obscured SFRs over a larger area than the pilot  
  > "The region chosen for targeting by ASPECS is that region of the HUDF containing the deepest near-IR, optical, X-ray, and radio observations available anywhere on the sky" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Selection and photometry follow the pilot study for consistency with earlier results  
  > "For consistency with earlier results from our pilot study (Bouwens et al. 2016), we have adopted essentially identical color-color and photometric redshift selection criteria" (Selections)
- Alternative: Less dust-temperature evolution (35 K fixed) also considered; gives lower IRX at z>3.5 and about half the dust correction  
  > "Given this, we also consider there being less evolution of the dust temperature of galaxies with cosmic time than in our fiducial models." (Fiducial SED Template and Dust Temperature Evolution)
- Alternative: Prospector stellar masses instead of FAST give a steeper IRX-mass relation  
  > "we derive a steeper IRX stellar mass relationship." (Summary)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): z>3.5 IRX conclusions depend on the assumed dust temperature evolution  
  > "However, we emphasize that this conclusion is sensitive to the dust temperature evolution we adopt." (IRX vs. Stellar Mass)
- Limitation (authors): Few massive z>3.5 sources in ASPECS to constrain the IRX-mass relation there  
  > "the ASPECS field only contains a few bright, massive sources to probe this well." (Comparison with Previous Determinations of the Infrared Excess)
- Limitation (authors): Individual-source IRX estimators show at least ~0.65 dex scatter  
  > "tend to show at least $\sim$0.65 dex scatter for individual sources." (Predictive Power of Different Estimators for IRX)
- Limitation (authors): IRX-mass relation depends on the stellar population code used for masses  
  > "the IRX versus stellar mass relation we derive does show some dependence on which stellar population we use to estimate stellar masses." (Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Key inputs (continuum mosaic, source fluxes, sample catalog, FAST masses) are not tabulated or released here, so the headline stacking numbers cannot be rederived from the paper alone
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stacking uncertainty is given as bootstrap plus formal error without a stated resampling recipe
- Caveat (inferred, ours): z>3.5 IRX results hinge on an adopted, debated T_d(z); results change by 0.4 dex for a fixed 35 K
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Maximum-likelihood fits to few stacked bins (some bins have 1-4 sources) are sensitive to bin choices
