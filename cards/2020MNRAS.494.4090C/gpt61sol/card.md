## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum images and line cubes**: Reduce [N ii] data in CASA and generate continuum images and line cubes with CLEAN.  
  > "The \[N ii\] data were reduced using the Common Astronomy Software Applications package (CASA) version 4.7 (cite: mcmullin07)." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "CASA’s CLEAN function was used to generate continuum images and line cubes." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, CLEAN
   - parameter: CLEAN depth 2 to 5σ, depending on source  
  > "The CLEAN depth varied depending on the source, but was between 2 to 5$\sigma$." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Typical pixel size 1 arcsec  
  > "The typical pixel size was 1$''$" (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Achieved continuum RMS 0.5 to 0.9 mJy per beam.  
  > "Our observations achieved RMS continuum noise of 0.5 to 0.9 mJy per beam." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
2. **Extract spectra and remove continuum**: Extract [N ii] spectra and continuum from a single pixel at the emission peak; subtract a zeroth order off-line baseline.  
  > "\[N ii\] spectral lines and the corresponding continuum were extracted at the peak of emission for all sources, with a single pixel extraction," (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "The continuum emission is subtracted using a zeroth order polynomial matched to the flux baseline of off-line regions." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Test elliptical apertures of 1× and 1.5× the beam size against single-pixel extraction.  
  > "we extracted both continuum and line flux from elliptical aperture regions corresponding to 1$\times$ and 1.5$\times$ the beam size, in addition to a point source, single pixel extraction." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - assumption: ACA observations are unresolved, even for the largest sources.  
  > "This figure illustrates that our ACA observations are unresolved even for the largest sources." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: Zeroth order polynomial matched to off-line flux baseline.  
  > "The continuum emission is subtracted using a zeroth order polynomial matched to the flux baseline of off-line regions." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - validation: Aperture extractions did not increase line flux.  
  > "These aperture extractions did not alter (or increase) the line flux measurement, indicating complete flux contained within the extraction pixel." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
3. **Measure integrated line fluxes and widths**: Fit CO profiles with a single Gaussian; sum [N ii] and [C ii] over 2×FWHM_CO; compare Gaussian [N ii] and [C ii] widths.  
  > "We first fit the CO line profiles with a single Gaussian function" (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "summing \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] over a velocity range $2\times$FWHM$_{\textrm{\small CO}}$, covering the full-width of our \[N ii\] line profiles." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "For sources with both \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] observations, we compared the FWHM determined from a Gaussian fit for each line." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Use CO(5-4) or CO(4-3), depending on redshift.  
  > "and either CO(5-4) or CO(4-3) from (cite: strandet16), depending on redshift." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: SPT0125–50, SPT0300–46, SPT0243–49 and SPT0550–53 have 50% to 70% coverage; truncate [C ii] integration to [N ii] coverage.  
  > "In 4 of the 40 cases, our spectral bandwidth only covers between 50% to 70% of the CO-defined line (SPT0125–50, SPT0300–46, SPT0243–49, and SPT0550–53)." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "In these cases, we only sum the \[C ii\] line to the end of the \[N ii\] line coverage." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - assumption: High-SNR CO widths define the velocity range for both fine-structure lines.  
  > "we use the FWHM$_{\textrm{\small CO}}$ of the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) CO lines to determine the velocity range over which to sum our \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] detections." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian for CO; Gaussian fits for [N ii] and [C ii] FWHM.  
  > "We first fit the CO line profiles with a single Gaussian function" (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "For sources with both \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] observations, we compared the FWHM determined from a Gaussian fit for each line." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - validation: Bright-source curve of growth captures >95% of line flux; [N ii] and [C ii] widths agree with significant scatter.  
  > "For brighter \[N ii\] sources we confirmed through a curve of growth analysis that this represents $>95$% of the line flux," (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "Over the full sample, there is a good one-to-one agreement between these two line widths," (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
4. **Assess detections, errors and upper limits**: Determine SNR from source peaks in uv-plane continuum-subtracted channel maps; use off-line central-pixel channel RMS for nondetection limits.  
  > "SNR determined from the source peak in *uv*-plane continuum subtracted channel maps." (Table 1); "In the 6 sources with undetected \[N ii\], an upper limit was estimated as 3$\times$ the channel RMS in the central pixel at velocities away from the line." (Results)
   - parameter: Robust detections >4.5σ; further detections ≥3σ.  
  > "at $>4.5\sigma$ in 22 of our sample of 41 SMG observations (including SPT2132-58 from (cite: bethermin16)), with a further 13 sources detected at $\geq3\sigma$." (Results)
   - parameter: Quoted errors are RMS from 1D spectra.  
  > "Errors quoted are RMS from the 1D spectra." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: >4.5σ criterion has 5% false-positive chance, below 1% within one-half beam of CO coordinates.  
  > "The $>4.5\sigma$ criteria results in a 5% false positive chance in our data, which drops to less than 1% chance when the measurement is constrained to within one-half beam of the CO-source coordinates." (Results)
5. **Obtain luminosities and demagnified quantities**: Report observed [N ii] luminosities and compare demagnified [N ii] luminosities with demagnified FIR luminosities and dust temperatures using literature magnifications.  
  > "the demagnified \[N ii\] luminosities are plotted against demagnified L$_{\textrm{\small FIR}}$ and T$_{\textrm{\small dust}}$, using magnification values ($\mu$) from (cite: spilker16)." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1; ΩΛ = 0.7; Ωm = 0.3  
  > "We assume a Hubble constant $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ and density parameters $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$ and $\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3$ throughout." (Introduction)
   - assumption: μ = 6.3 assumed for Table 1 sources marked f without a lensing model.  
  > "$\mu=6.3$ is assumed. No lensing model." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Characterize the line-ratio distribution**: Calculate median and interquartile range first treating lower limits as measurements, then use survival analysis to account for censoring.  
  > "These statistical values are calculated by treating the L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}$/L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$ lower limits (\[N ii\] non-detections) as real measured values at the measured limit." (Results); "we perform survival analysis using the LIFELINES Python package (cite: lifelines)." (Results)
   - software: LIFELINES
   - assumption: Use ratio lower limits as the last observation before the true line ratio.  
  > "For our line ratios, we utilize the lower limits as the last observation before the “event” occurs, where the event is the true line ratio." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Restricting to good-quality detections did not significantly change medians or interquartile ranges.  
  > "Furthermore, reducing our sample to include only sources with good quality \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] detections did not significantly alter our measured medians or interquartile ranges." (Results)
7. **Interpret ISM regimes and conditional metallicities**: Compare ratios with H ii/PDR/XDR/shock expectations and two literature metallicity grids.  
  > "corresponds to model expectations from XDR/PDR or shock regions determined by (cite: decarli14)." (Results); "We follow the analysis of (cite: nagao12) and include two metallicity grids for gas densities of $\log(n)=1.5$ and $=3$, each with ionization parameter $\log(U)=-3.5$" (Discussion)
   - parameter: [C ii]/[N ii] ≈2 for H ii; >≈9 for PDR/XDR.  
  > "(cite: decarli14) use L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}/$L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}\sim 2$ for H ii regions" (Results); "and greater than L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}/$L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}\sim 9$ for PDR/XDR regions" (Results)
   - assumption: Metallicity grids: log(n) = 1.5 or 3; log(U) = -3.5.  
  > "We follow the analysis of (cite: nagao12) and include two metallicity grids for gas densities of $\log(n)=1.5$ and $=3$, each with ionization parameter $\log(U)=-3.5$" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Compare with local LIRGs**: Assemble literature local-LIRG ratios and perform two-sample KS and T-tests.  
  > "in local LIRGs from (cite: diazsantos17), (cite: lu17), and (cite: zhao16)." (Results); "we perform a two-sample KS test and T-test." (Results)
   - parameter: Estimate missing literature dust temperatures using the Symeonidis relationship.  
  > "In our literature sample, when dust temperatures were not available they were estimated using the relationship shown in (cite: symeonidis13)." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Measure binned [N ii]/FIR trends**: Bin sources by FIR luminosity and dust temperature into three roughly equal groups; calculate median [N ii]/FIR.  
  > "We divide our sources with L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$ measurements into three bins of roughly equal size," (Results); "We also bin our sources into 3 bins of roughly equal number according to dust temperature:" (Results)
   - parameter: FIR luminosity covers 42.5 to 122.5 μm.  
  > "(42.5 to 122.5 $\mu$m)" (Results)
   - parameter: log10(L_FIR) bins: <12.71, 12.71–12.9, >12.9  
  > "ranging from log$_{10}($L$_{\textrm{\small FIR}})=$\[$<$12.71, 12.71–12.9,$>$12.9$]$." (Results)
   - parameter: T_dust bins: <39.3, 39.3–43.8, >43.8  
  > "T$_{\textrm{\small dust}}=$\[$<$39.3, 39.3–43.8, $>$43.8\]." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
10. **Test temperature dependence after FIR correction**: Multiply [N ii]/FIR by (T_dust/40 K)^4 and perform Kendall Tau tests on the full sample and two line-ratio subsamples.  
  > "To cancel this relationship, we plot $($L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$/L$_{\textrm{\small FIR}})\times ($T$_{\textrm{\small dust}}/40$ K$)^4$" (Discussion); "After removing this dependence, we perform a Kendall Tau test on the sample and calculate a *p*-value of 0.49." (Discussion); "For these samples, we repeat the Kendall Tau test and calculate *p*-values of 0.17 and 0.08, respectively." (Discussion)
   - parameter: [N ii] significant: [C ii]/[N ii] <10; insignificant: >10.  
  > "We define the \[N ii\] significant sources as those with L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}$/L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$$<10$," (Discussion); "and the \[N ii\] insignificant sources with L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}$/L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$$>10$." (Discussion)
   - assumption: L_FIR ∝ T_dust^4  
  > "as L$_{\textrm{\small FIR}}\propto$ T$_{\textrm{\small dust}}^4$." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat Kendall Tau tests in high- and low-[C ii]/[N ii] subsamples.  
  > "For these samples, we repeat the Kendall Tau test and calculate *p*-values of 0.17 and 0.08, respectively." (Discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- High-redshift SMGs span a wide [C ii]/[N ii] distribution; survival analysis does not significantly change the result.  
  > "We find that our SPT SMGs have a wide distribution of L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}$/L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$." (Conclusions); "Using survival statistics to account for our lower limits did not significantly alter our results." (Conclusions)
- Hybrid-regime sources require significant contributions to [C ii] from both H ii and PDR/XDR regions.  
  > "These sources suggest neither H ii regions nor PDR/XDR regions dominate the \[C ii\] flux. Instead the \[C ii\] flux has significant contributions from both regions." (Discussion)
- The highest-ratio sources likely have extreme ISM environments dominated by PDR/XDR contributions to [C ii].  
  > "These sources likely represent extreme ISM environments, where total \[C ii\] emission is dominated by the contribution from PDR/XDR regions." (Discussion)
- SMG and local-LIRG line-ratio distributions are not conclusively different.  
  > "These test results prevent us from conclusively determining the line ratios come from different underlying distributions." (Results)
- Dust-temperature independence remains undetermined after correcting the FIR–temperature dependence.  
  > "We cannot determine whether our measured \[N ii\] emission is independent of dust temperature," (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Select 3<z<6 to place [N ii] 205 μm in ACA bands 6 and 7; many sources already have [C ii] observations.  
  > "We selected a subset of sources with $3<z<6$ in order to enable observation of the \[N ii\] 205 $\mu$m line within bands 6 and 7 of ACA," (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "including many sources which had existing \[C ii\] observations (e.g. (cite: gullberg15))." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: CO-defined integration windows capture the line while avoiding noise fluctuations outside faint [N ii] profiles.  
  > "we use the FWHM$_{\textrm{\small CO}}$ of the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) CO lines to determine the velocity range over which to sum our \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] detections." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "while for fainter \[N ii\] sources this avoids large variations in the line flux measurement from integrating noise fluctuations outside the line frequencies." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Elliptical aperture extractions did not improve on single-pixel flux extraction.  
  > "we extracted both continuum and line flux from elliptical aperture regions corresponding to 1$\times$ and 1.5$\times$ the beam size, in addition to a point source, single pixel extraction." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "These aperture extractions did not alter (or increase) the line flux measurement, indicating complete flux contained within the extraction pixel." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Survival analysis was compared with treating lower limits as measured values.  
  > "These statistical values are calculated by treating the L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[C~{\sc ii}]}}}$/L$_{\textrm{\small \mbox{[N~{\sc ii}]}}}$ lower limits (\[N ii\] non-detections) as real measured values at the measured limit." (Results); "Using survival statistics to account for our lower limits did not significantly alter our results." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Restricting analysis to good-quality detections did not significantly change distribution statistics.  
  > "Furthermore, reducing our sample to include only sources with good quality \[N ii\] and \[C ii\] detections did not significantly alter our measured medians or interquartile ranges." (Results)
- How they decided it was enough: Apertures did not increase flux; bright-source curves of growth show >95% line-flux recovery.  
  > "These aperture extractions did not alter (or increase) the line flux measurement, indicating complete flux contained within the extraction pixel." (Sample selection, observations and data reduction); "For brighter \[N ii\] sources we confirmed through a curve of growth analysis that this represents $>95$% of the line flux," (Sample selection, observations and data reduction)

  > "In 4 of the 40 cases, our spectral bandwidth only covers between 50% to 70% of the CO-defined line (SPT0125–50, SPT0300–46, SPT0243–49, and SPT0550–53)."
