## Qualitative findings

- The overall distribution of [C I] (1-0) emission is consistent with CO (1-0), though their strongest peaks are in the eastern and western nuclei, respectively, and an arc-like feature in [C I] around the western nucleus is due to self-absorption.  
  > "However, the strongest [C ] (1–0) and CO (1–0) peaks are inconsistent, and they are located in the eastern and western nuclei, respectively. An arc-like feature in the [C ] (1–0) map seen around the western nucleus is due to self-absorption." (Distribution and Kinematics)
- While the integrated luminosities follow the linear ULIRG relation, the spatially-resolved [C I] (1-0) and CO (1-0) luminosities follow a sublinear relation, indicating non-constant [C I]/CO ratios across Arp 220.  
  > "While the [C ] (1–0) and CO (1–0) luminosities integrated across the system of Arp 220 follow the empirical linear relation for the unresolved ULIRG sample, we find a sublinear relation between $L'_{\rm [CI](1-0)}$ and $L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ using the spatially-resolved data of Arp 220, indicating that the [C ]/CO luminosity ratios are not constant in the system." (The relation between [C ] and CO)
- The average [C I] (1-0)/CO (1-0) luminosity ratio is roughly constant up to R_CO = 1.0 and increases with R_CO above 1.0, requiring variations in the C I/CO abundance ratio at ~100 pc scales.  
  > "As a result, the average $L'_{\rm [CI](1-0)}/L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ is roughly constant up to $R_{\rm CO}$ = 1.0 and then increases with $R_{\rm CO}$." (Variations in the C /CO abundance ratios); "Under the assumption that $N_{\rm CO}$ is constant in the observed region, the comparison between the observational and modeled data suggests that the C /CO abundance ratio varies at $\sim$100 pc scale in Arp 220." (Variations in the C /CO abundance ratios)
- High-velocity [C I] (1-0) components likely associated with the western nucleus outflow exhibit elevated [C I]/CO luminosity ratios, suggesting a C I-rich and CO-poor gas phase possibly driven by cosmic rays, shocks, or intense radiation fields.  
  > "A C -rich and CO-poor gas phase in outflows could be caused by the irradiation of the cosmic rays, the shock heating, and the intense radiation field." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Arp 220 is the closest ULIRG with proximity, extreme environment, brightness, and rich auxiliary data; two of three CO (1-0) SBs were selected and uv-clipped to match the uv range of the [C I] (1-0) data.  
  > "In this study, we investigate the relationship between [C ] (1–0) and CO (1–0) at $\sim$100 pc scale in Arp 220, which is a well-studied source because of its relative proximity, extreme environment and brightness." (Introduction); "Although there are three scheduling blocks (SBs) targeting the CO (1–0) emission in the project, we used two SBs whose $uv$ range matches the [C ] data." ($^{12}$CO (1–0))
- Why this method: Used velocity ranges from Barcos-Munoz et al. (2018) to identify high-velocity outflow components because coarse angular resolution makes morphological identification difficult; excluded pixels within 300 pc of nuclei to avoid self-absorption and pixels with log L'_[CI](1-0) < 5.3 to avoid noise domination; used RADEX to test whether gas excitation or abundance variations explain line ratios.  
  > "We created the CO (1–0) integrated intensity maps of high-velocity components (see Figure 3) by using the same velocity ranges used in (cite: Barcos2018) because it is difficult to identify the morphology of the outflowing components due to the coarse angular resolution." (High-velocity components in the western nucleus); "We also exclude pixels within 300 pc from each nucleus because self-absorption can affect our interpretation." (The relation between [C ] and CO); "We perform non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (non-LTE) analysis using the radiative transfer code RADEX (cite: vanderTak2007) to check whether this trend can be reproduced by changing the physical properties of gas." (Variations in the C /CO abundance ratios)
- Alternative: Changing only kinetic temperature or H2 gas density at a fixed C I/CO abundance ratio to explain the rising [C I]/CO vs R_CO trend (rejected based on RADEX calculations)  
  > "According to our RADEX calculation, the observed trend is not fully explained by changing only the temperature or gas density." (Variations in the C /CO abundance ratios)
- Alternative: Overestimation of M_CI in the redshifted component due to contamination from the main body gas or [C I] and CO tracing different components (considered as a possibility, though unable to explain the blueshifted component discrepancy alone)  
  > "One possibility is that $M_{\rm CI}$ of the redshifted component is overestimated due to the contamination of gas associated with the main body." (High-velocity components in the western nucleus); "However, even if we take into account this possibility, it is not possible to explain the difference between $M_{\rm CI}$ and $M_{\rm CO}$ of the blueshifted component." (High-velocity components in the western nucleus)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Data lack zero-spacing correction and have clipped visibilities, suffering from missing flux (recovering 66% of the JCMT [C I] flux) and limiting analysis to scales smaller than 1.75 kpc.  
  > "Since our data were not corrected with the zero-spacing information and visibilities are clipped, the data suffer from missing flux." (Molecular mass); "The shortest $uv$ range of our data is 26.9 k$\lambda$, which corresponds to the maximum recoverable scale of 1.75 kpc for Arp 220. Thus, we discuss components smaller than 1.75 kpc in this study." (Molecular mass)
- Limitation (authors): CO (3-2) spectra are contaminated by another molecular line, possibly H13CN (4-3), at velocities > 5630 km s^-1, restricting CO (3-2) analysis to 4845-5630 km s^-1 and allowing only an upper limit on R_CO in the redshifted outflow component.  
  > "We note that the CO (3–2) spectra are affected by contamination from other molecular lines, possibly H$^{13}$CN (4–3) ($\nu_{\rm rest}$ = 345.340 GHz) (cite: Wheeler2020), in the velocity range of $>$5630 km s$^{-1}$." (Supplemental data: $^{12}$CO (3–2)); "Since the CO (3–2) spectra of the redshifted component are affected by contamination from other molecular lines, possibly H$^{13}$CN (4–3), we calculate an upper limit for $R_{\rm CO}$ in the redshifted component and plot it with $L'_{\rm [CI](1-0)}/L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ of the redshifted component in Figure 9." (High line ratio in the redshifted component)
- Limitation (authors): Coarse angular resolution (~0.3'') prevents resolving the detailed morphology of the outflow components and measuring internal line widths of individual molecular clouds.  
  > "Furthermore, although the angular resolutions are not high enough to reveal the detailed distributions, the redshifted components of [C ] (1–0) and CO (1–0) seem to be slightly separated, implying that the [C ] (1–0) and CO (1–0) distributions are different in the outflow." (High-velocity components in the western nucleus); "However, we cannot measure the line width using our maps due to coarse angular resolutions." (Variations in the C /CO abundance ratios)
- Limitation (authors): Self-absorption around the nuclei affects the emission, requiring exclusion of pixels within 300 pc of each nucleus.  
  > "An arc-like feature in the [C ] (1–0) map seen around the western nucleus is due to self-absorption." (Distribution and Kinematics); "We also exclude pixels within 300 pc from each nucleus because self-absorption can affect our interpretation." (The relation between [C ] and CO)
- Limitation (authors): RADEX analysis assumes a constant N_CO; if N_CO varies significantly across regions, the cause of L'_[CI]/L'_CO variations is not straightforward to interpret.  
  > "If $N_{\rm CO}$ is significantly changing from one region to another in the science target, the cause of various $L'_{\rm [CI](1-0)}$/$L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ cannot be straightforward to interpret." (Variations in the C /CO abundance ratios)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Clipping only the shortest uv distances (>= 26.9 klambda) and smoothing the image beams to 0.3'' does not guarantee identical middle/long-baseline uv coverage across Band 3, Band 7, and Band 8 observations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a uniform N_CO / dV = 10^17 cm^-2 (km s^-1)^-1 across diverse ~100 pc environments in an extreme merger like Arp 220 can bias the inferred C I/CO abundance variations, since optical depth changes in CO (1-0) and CO (3-2) strongly affect R_CO and L'_[CI]/L'_CO.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 20% systematic flux calibration uncertainty in Band 8 ([C I]) directly shifts the normalization of the L'_[CI]/L'_CO ratio and the inferred C I/CO abundance ratios.
