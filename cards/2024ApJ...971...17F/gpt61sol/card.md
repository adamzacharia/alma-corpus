## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct the optical ionized-gas image**: Convert WFC3 counts to flux using inverse sensitivity and filter bandwidth; subtract F547M continuum from F502N.  
  > "We used a F547M image as the continuum to subtract from the line emission of the F502N image." (Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3)); "multiplying the data counts for each image by its filter’s inverse sensitivity and its filter bandwidth." (Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Prepare broad-line templates and spectral uncertainties**: Fit broad emission in the brightest-continuum nuclear spectrum separately for each epoch; construct noise arrays from NIFS variance and nuclear-row flux statistics for STIS/KOSMOS.  
  > "Specifically, we used the spectrum with the brightest continuum flux as our template for the broad components." (Fitting Broad Lines); "we utilized the variance data cube and take the square root of the variance for each point along each spectrum to approximate the noise." (Error Analysis); "we multiplied the standard deviation of flux of the nuclear row by the square root of the flux at each point along the spectrum divided by the average flux of the nuclear row." (Error Analysis)
   - software: BEAT
   - parameter: Broad-component FWHM > 1400 km s^-1.  
  > "the line widths must be greater than 1400 km s$^{-1}$ to avoid contamination from narrow line components." (Fitting Broad Lines)
   - parameter: Separate broad-line fits for different observing epochs.  
  > "We performed these fits separately for different epochs of observation because the broad lines in NGC 3227 are known to vary in both profile and flux (cite: bentz23)." (Fitting Broad Lines)
   - assumption: Unresolved broad component is a point source; fixed width and centroid along each slit, varying only in flux.  
  > "the broad component maintains a constant width and wavelength centroid along the slit, so the only allowed variation is in the flux of the profile as it changes according to the point spread function (PSF)." (Fitting Broad Lines)
   - fitting: Multiple Gaussian broad components, with fixed relative intensities in subsequent Hβ and Hα fits.  
  > "We allowed the routine to freely fit the broad emission with multiple Gaussian components" (Fitting Broad Lines); "The resulting broad line components with fixed relative intensities are used as templates in all subsequent fits for the H$\beta$ and H$\alpha$ regions." (Fitting Broad Lines)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Decompose emission lines into kinematic components**: Fit each STIS/KOSMOS slit spectrum and NIFS cube spectrum with multi-component Gaussians; derive velocity, FWHM, and integrated flux.  
  > "We applied BEAT to spectra from both long-slit spectroscopy (STIS and KOSMOS data) and IFU data cubes (NIFS data), fitting each spectrum in the slit and data cube, respectively." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT)); "Wavelength centroid, line width, and peak flux values of each component from our best fits are used to create kinematic plots that show the radial velocity, FWHM, and integrated flux profiles of the individual kinematic components." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT))
   - software: BEAT, MultiNest
   - parameter: Fit only S/N ≥ 3; component flux between 3σ continuum noise and line peak.  
  > "The lower limit for the flux levels of each component is 3$\sigma$ of the continuum noise, and the upper limit is given by the peak flux of the corresponding emission line data." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT)); "We do not fit data that fail the criterion of S/N $\ge$ 3." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT))
   - parameter: NLR FWHM upper limit 1400 km s^-1; lower limit set by instrumental spectral resolution.  
  > "The lower ends of the line widths were restricted by the instrument’s spectral resolution." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT)); "we set the upper limit of the NLR to 1400 km s$^{-1}$ (FWHM)" (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT))
   - parameter: [O III] 5007/4959 = 3.01; [N II] 6583/6548 = 2.95.  
  > "the relative intensities of certain lines such as \[O III\] $\lambda$5007/4959 and \[N II\] $\lambda$6583/6548 as 3.01 and 2.95, respectively (cite: osterbrock06)." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT))
   - assumption: Gaussian line profiles for simplicity and efficiency.  
  > "Although emission line profiles are not always purely Gaussian (cite: heckman81; veilleux91), treating them as such for simplicity and efficiency has been successfully employed for this type of study" (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT))
   - fitting: MultiNest Bayesian evidence selects the fewest significantly improving components; ln(Z_i+1/Z_i) ≥ 5.  
  > "BEAT utilizes the MultiNest sampling algorithm (cite: feroz08; feroz09; feroz19; buchner14) to compute Bayesian statistics and determine the fewest number of Gaussian components that significantly improve the fit of the model to the data." (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT)); "\mathrm{ln}\left[\frac{Z_{i+1}}{Z_{i}}\right] \geq 5" (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT))
   - validation: STIS kinematics match the earlier analysis where the same number of components is fitted.  
  > "In instances where we fit the same number of components as them at a given distance along a slit, the kinematics match." (HST STIS Kinematics)
4. **Separate rotation, outflow, and disturbed gas**: Compare slit kinematics with projected literature rotation curves; subtract the stellar rotation model from NIFS maps.  
  > "by comparing the observed large-scale velocities to the rotation curves from the stars or cold gas as discussed in Section" (Kinematic Properties of the Ionized Gas); "We have subtracted the rotational motion from the velocity maps using the stellar velocity model for NGC 3227 given in (cite: riffel17)" (Gemini NIFS Kinematics)
   - assumption: After subtraction of rotation, mapped NIFS velocities represent non-rotational, outflowing motions.  
  > "so it can be assumed that mapped velocities are due to non-rotational (i.e. outflowing) motions." (Gemini NIFS Kinematics)
   - fitting: STIS/KOSMOS velocity errors replaced by the median absolute deviation within manually selected position–velocity groups; NIFS uncertainties retained.  
  > "the errors for the BEAT fits were calculated by manually dividing the data in each slit into distinct kinematic groups based on position and velocity." (Error Analysis); "For each group, we calculated the median absolute deviation and used that value as the velocity uncertainty for each member in that group." (Error Analysis); "We did not redo the uncertainties for the NIFS data because they are reasonable values." (Error Analysis)
   - validation: NIFS ionized-gas velocity patterns resemble those in HST STIS.  
  > "This pattern is very similar to that seen in the HST STIS observations." (J-band Kinematics)
5. **Fit the NLR bicone geometry**: Generate over 300,000 models; maximize the mean weighted velocity fraction across seven STIS slits.  
  > "we used the bicone parameters given by (cite: travisthesis) as priors and generated over 300,000 bicone models" (Development of a New Model); "We average the weighted velocity fractions for all seven STIS slits corresponding to a given set of input parameters and deemed the set of parameters with the highest average to be the optimal model" (Development of a New Model)
   - software: kinematic modeling code from (cite: das05)
   - parameter: Vary inclination, inner HOA, outer HOA, turnover radius, and bicone PA; angular search described as ±10 degrees with 1-degree steps.  
  > "We iterated through five variable parameters (bicone inclination, inner opening angle, outer opening angle, turnover radius, and bicone PA)" (Development of a New Model); "within $\pm$10$^\circ$ of prior estimates for each STIS slit position, using step sizes of 1$^\circ$." (Development of a New Model)
   - parameter: Fixed maximum height = 150 pc; maximum velocity = 600 km s^-1.  
  > "Two parameters that we did not vary are the maximum height and maximum velocity, which we kept constant at 150 pc and 600 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Development of a New Model)
   - parameter: Exclude rotational points within ±20 km s^-1 of the rotation curve and outside model velocity space.  
  > "we assume that data that fall within $\pm$20 km s$^{-1}$ of the rotation curve and outside the model velocity space are rotational." (Development of a New Model)
   - assumption: Inclination from the LOS smaller than the inner opening angle to permit a Seyfert 1 view.  
  > "the bicone inclination angle from the LOS must have a smaller value than the inner opening angle, because the Seyfert 1 designation for NGC 3227 requires visibility of the central engine." (Development of a New Model)
   - assumption: Velocity rises linearly from zero to the turnover radius, then falls linearly to zero at maximum height.  
  > "starts at 0 km s$^{-1}$ in the center and increases linearly until a specified turnover radius, after which the velocity decreases linearly until zero at a predetermined maximum height along the bicone axis." (Bicone Model)
   - fitting: Normalize velocity fractions by weighted model velocity area; uncertainties from a 68% confidence interval.  
  > "divide the velocity fraction by the weighted model velocity area to determine the density of fitted points per unit model velocity space, which we call the “weighted velocity fraction.”" (Development of a New Model); "with uncertainties marking parameter values that reside within the 68% confidence interval for the average velocity fractions." (Development of a New Model)
   - validation: Compare model velocity envelopes with STIS data and geometry with independent BLR modeling.  
  > "The overlap between the model and data in this work is substantially improved from that in (cite: travisthesis), and we see generally decent agreement between data and model in all of the slits." (Comparison of Data to Model); "This value is in much better agreement with the measurement of $33.2^{+13.5}_{-9.1}$$^\circ$ from (cite: bentz23), based on modeling of the broad-line region (BLR)" (Development of a New Model)
6. **Compare cold, warm, and ionized gas**: Compare CO(2-1), [O III], H2, and He I flux contours and compare multiphase velocity structures separately for blueshifted and redshifted emission.  
  > "shows how the flux contours of CO(2-1), \[O III\], H$_2$, and He I significantly overlap with one another in the innermost couple of arcseconds in NGC 3227." (Multiphase Correlation); "As a result, we will analyze the blueshifted and redshifted kinematics separately." (Multiphase Correlation)
   - assumption: Overlapping positive and negative velocity contours can represent emission from both cones.  
  > "We notice several instances of overlap between positive and negative contours representing redshifted and blueshifted emission, respectively." (Multiphase Correlation); "in these instances, we are observing emission from both cones in the bicone structure." (Multiphase Correlation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare molecular-gas morphology and kinematics with earlier studies.  
  > "Our NIFS H$_2$ radial velocity, dispersion, and flux maps shown in Figure" (Connection to NGC 3227’s Circumnuclear Ring); "are similar to those from other studies (cite: riffel17; alonso19; davies14; hicks08; bianchin22)." (Connection to NGC 3227’s Circumnuclear Ring)
7. **Estimate recent and NLR-averaged luminosities**: Average corrected 5100 Å fluxes over 2007–2017; independently convert mean Lowell [O III] 5007 flux to bolometric luminosity.  
  > "All of these observations were obtained between 2007 and 2017, and the AGN flux at 5100 Å varied by a factor of 2.2 during this time." (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity); "We therefore measured the integrated fluxes of \[O III\] $\lambda$5007 in each of our 5 Lowell spectra obtained over 2009 – 2010" (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity)
   - parameter: L_bol = 9.8 λL_5100; L_bol = 3500 L_5007.  
  > "$L_{\mathrm{bol}} = 9.8 \lambda L_{5100}$ (cite: mclure04)" (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity); "Utilizing the relationship $L_{\mathrm{bol}}=3500L_{5007}$ (cite: heckman04)" (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity)
   - assumption: Distance = 23.7 Mpc; apply literature galaxy subtraction and reddening correction.  
  > "After subtraction of the galaxy contribution from the above sources (6.8 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$), the same reddening and bolometric corrections as above, and using a distance of 23.7 Mpc (cite: tonry01; blakeslee01)" (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity)
   - assumption: [O III] luminosity represents an average over at least 2,000 years.  
  > "which represents an average luminosity over at least 2,000 years" (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Calculate radiative trajectories and launch radii**: Compute the Sérsic disk/bulge enclosed-mass profile; solve the radiative-acceleration and gravitational-deceleration equation numerically using deprojected knot positions and velocities.  
  > "to calculate the mass density distribution for individual Sérsic components (cite: sersic68) that include the host galaxy disk and bulge." (Launch Radii); "We use Equation 10 of (cite: terzic05) and the mass density distribution to determine the enclosed mass, and consequently gravitational deceleration, at a given radius from the SMBH." (Launch Radii); "Given the velocity and distance of a gas knot from the SMBH, which has been corrected for projection effects, the launch distance $r_1$ can be solved for numerically." (Radiative Driving)
   - parameter: Force multiplier M = 500–3000.  
  > "force multipliers ranging from $\mathcal{M} = 500 - 3000$" (Launch Radii)
   - parameter: NLR-averaged L_bol = 2.25 × 10^44 erg s^-1; also compare recent and Eddington luminosities.  
  > "we find an average bolometric luminosity of $L_{\mathrm{bol}} = 2.25 \times 10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$." (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity); "For comparison, the range of turnover radii is $13-28$ pc for the 2007 – 2017 luminosity of 4.78 $\times 10^{43}$ erg s$^{-1}$ and $44 - 224$ pc for the Eddington luminosity." (Launch Radii)

## Qualitative findings

- The fitted bicone geometry explains the NLR morphology through extinction by the galactic disk.  
  > "These values place the bicone axis close to the galactic plane, and explain the morphology of the NLR (especially the lack of \[O III\] emission in the SW) as a result of extinction by dust in the plane." (Conclusions)
- The nuclear bridge may be an original and possibly continuing fueling flow.  
  > "This bridge is seen in He I, H$_2$, and CO(2-1) and is perpendicular to the outflowing axis, which may be the original (and potentially still present) fueling flow to the AGN." (Conclusions)
- The phase progression suggests hollowing of the cold reservoir by ionizing radiation.  
  > "This progression indicates the hollowing out of the cold gas reservoir at the nucleus by ionizing radiation as suggested by (cite: alonso19), which then flows along the cones." (Multiphase Correlation)
- Radiative driving plus gravity is favored as the dominant NLR dynamical mechanism.  
  > "our findings support radiative driving plus gravity as the dominant force behind NLR outflows" (Conclusions)
- No noteworthy tidal signature is detected in the KOSMOS Hα kinematics.  
  > "we see no sign of unusual kinematic signatures that would suggest a noteworthy tidal influence on the H$\alpha$ gas." (Influences from NGC 3226?)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine optical, near-IR, and radio data for multiphase kinematics on nuclear and galaxy-wide scales.  
  > "we add multiphase and multiscale observations comprising optical, near-IR, and radio data that exhibit kinematic details on both nuclear and galaxy-wide scales." (NGC 3227)
- Why this method: Bayesian component selection limits overfitting; model-area weighting counters preference for overly broad bicone envelopes.  
  > "Using the Bayesian likelihood to evaluate the best model, as BEAT does, minimizes the chances of overfitting." (HST STIS Kinematics); "Simply determining the model with the highest velocity fraction is insufficient to finding the optimal bicone model." (Development of a New Model); "Thus, we must apply a normalizing weight based on the size of the model velocity area to act as a counterbalance." (Development of a New Model)
- Alternative: Molecular outflow preferred over inflow; an inner disk warp is not excluded.  
  > "we prefer the interpretation of redshifted emission in the east and blueshifted emission in the west as molecular outflow." (Connection to NGC 3227’s Circumnuclear Ring); "Our observations do not rule out a warp in the disk inside of $\sim$30 pc" (Connection to NGC 3227’s Circumnuclear Ring)
- Alternative: Unweighted velocity-fraction maximization rejected because wider opening-angle ranges can overfit.  
  > "A bicone with a wide range in the opening and closing angles will always envelop a larger fraction of the data than a bicone with a narrower angular range." (Development of a New Model); "Simply determining the model with the highest velocity fraction is insufficient to finding the optimal bicone model." (Development of a New Model)
- Alternative: Higher maximum-velocity bicone models could fit discrepant points, but were avoided to limit empty model space.  
  > "We limited the value of $v\mathrm{_{max}}$ to 600 km s$^{-1}$ so that there wouldn’t be an inordinate amount of empty velocity model space" (Extents of the Outflows and Disturbed Gas); "a bicone model that successfully fit those data could surely be generated." (Extents of the Outflows and Disturbed Gas)
- How they decided it was enough: Additional Gaussian components require ln evidence ratio ≥ 5; optimal bicone maximizes the average weighted velocity fraction.  
  > "\mathrm{ln}\left[\frac{Z_{i+1}}{Z_{i}}\right] \geq 5" (Bayesian Evidence Analysis Tool (BEAT)); "deemed the set of parameters with the highest average to be the optimal model, for it maximizes the number of fit data points per unit area." (Development of a New Model)
- Limitation (authors): Rotation and outflow can have similar projected kinematic signatures.  
  > "rotational motion and outflowing motion can have similar kinematic signatures if the outflow is in the same direction as the rotation." (Kinematic Properties of the Ionized Gas)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties are unavailable for the adopted inner rotation model.  
  > "in the absence of uncertainties from that model" (Development of a New Model)
- Limitation (authors): Some observed velocities remain outside the fitted bicone envelopes.  
  > "There are two main discrepancies between the models and observed velocities." (Comparison of Data to Model); "a number of those points lie outside the model." (Comparison of Data to Model)
- Limitation (authors): STIS sampling can miss faint components detected by KOSMOS.  
  > "This is likely due to the much smaller areas sampled by STIS, which is therefore unable to detect this component." (HST STIS Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): NIFS PSF wings smooth spatial structure relative to HST.  
  > "The flux distribution is relatively smooth compared to the HST \[O III\] image, because the Gemini NIFS PSF has much broader wings than that of HST and its cameras." (Z-band Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): The [O III] luminosity cannot determine when dimming began.  
  > "the \[O III\] $\lambda$5007 emission does not reveal specific temporal information to provide a timeline of when the dimming started" (Estimates of Bolometric Luminosity)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Multiphase offsets and progression depend on unreported astrometric registration, resolution matching, and contour thresholds.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The bicone confidence construction and model-area weights are insufficiently specified for an exact statistical reproduction.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The fixed maximum velocity, empirical rotation cut, and manually assigned velocity uncertainties can affect the inferred geometry.
