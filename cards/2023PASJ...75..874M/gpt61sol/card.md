## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct representative continuum maps**: Combine measurement sets separately for each receiver; exclude line-targeted spectral windows and image the continuum  
  > "We make the representative images for each receiver (Band 3,6,7, and 9) by combining all the available measurement sets and using the data sampled in uv-distance of larger than $>300$ k$\lambda$" (ALMA Archival Data); "In each project, we avoided the spectral window (spw) in which line observations are targetted." (ALMA Archival Data)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: CASA 6.1.1.15  
  > "For imaging, we use `CASA` version of 6.1.1.15." (ALMA Archival Data)
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5; pixel size 5 mas; uv-distance >300 kλ  
  > "The maps were produced using the `tclean` task in `CASA` with Briggs weighting (robust = 0.5) and a pixel size of 5 mas." (ALMA Archival Data); "using the data sampled in uv-distance of larger than $>300$ k$\lambda$" (ALMA Archival Data)
   - parameter: Clean mask: 100 pix around S1  
  > "The clean masks were selected by 100 pix around position S1." (ALMA Archival Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Make continuum maps for every spectral window to check line contamination  
  > "In addition, we made continuum maps for every spw, confirming no line contamination in our representative map." (ALMA Archival Data)
2. **Measure S1 and assemble the broadband SED**: Fit ALMA S1 flux and deconvolved size with imfit; combine with literature VLA and VLBA measurements  
  > "We measure the flux of S1 ($F_{\rm S1}$) and beam deconvolved source size (represented by $\theta_{\rm maj}$, $\theta_{\rm min}$, and position angle: PA) using `imfit` task in `CASA` by ourselves." (SED); "For the VLA and VLBA measurements, we use the literature data." (Representative SED)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: σ = sqrt(σ_imfit^2 + (0.1 F_S1)^2)  
  > "\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma_\mathrm{imfit}^2+{(0.1F_{\rm S1})}^2}." (SED)
   - parameter: No restoring the synthesized beam, aperture masking or aperture photometry  
  > "We do not add any other corrections (i.e. restoring the synthesized beam, masking the aperture region, and aperture photometry)." (SED)
   - assumption: 10% systematic error  
  > "Therefore, we assume the $10\%$ systematic error" (SED)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit; imfit fitting errors  
  > "S1 measured by the 2D Gaussian fit" (Spectrum at $>10$ GHz); "The error ($\sigma_\mathrm{imfit}$) is estimated using the imfit task, which is based on the fitting error." (SED)
   - validation: Use the same minimum uv-distance in all ALMA bands  
  > "We use images whose minimum baseline used for imaging is the same among Band 3, 6, 7, and 9 ($>300$ k$\lambda$)." (SED)
3. **Assess beam-dependent spectral structure**: Compare fitted S1 flux with source size and synthesized beam size  
  > "we investigated the relation between source size and flux at S1 measured by the 2D Gaussian fit" (Spectrum at $>10$ GHz); "When we use the synthesized beamsize instead of the source size, the same positive trend is seen." (Spectrum at $>10$ GHz)
   - assumption: Resolution <0.1'' excludes host galaxy and downstream jet components NE and C  
  > "Since we restrict data sets with the angular resolution of $<0.1\ensuremath{''}$, we can safely ignore contamination from the host galaxy and compact radio emission components in the jet downstream such as components NE and C" (beamsize issues)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Larger flux occurs at larger fitted source size; the trend also appears with beam size  
  > "The positive relation is confirmed in which the larger flux is seen in the larger source size." (Spectrum at $>10$ GHz); "When we use the synthesized beamsize instead of the source size, the same positive trend is seen." (Spectrum at $>10$ GHz)
4. **Test a single free-free component**: Model free-free emission and self-absorption with uniform cylinders along the line of sight; estimate electron density  
  > "Here, we approximate the emitting regions by uniform cylinders whose axis is the line of sight" (Single component (free-free)); "Considering free-free self-absorption, the free–free spectral luminosity is given by" (Single component (free-free))
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: T_e = 10^6 K; d_s = 4 pc; l_L.O.S = 4 pc  
  > "Assuming the lower temperature and larger emitting region ($T_\mathrm{e}$, $d_\mathrm{s}$, $l_\mathrm{L.O.S}$) = ($10^6$ K, 4 pc, 4 pc)" (Single component (free-free))
   - assumption: Flux rise/fall above 10 GHz is due to beamsize effects  
  > "assuming that flux rise/fall in $>10$ GHz is due to beamsize effects" (Single component (free-free))
   - fitting: Bayesian parameter estimation developed by (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013PASP..125..306F); linked emcee documentation  
  > "based on Bayesian parameter estimation developed by (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013PASP..125..306F)" (Single component (free-free)); "https://emcee.readthedocs.io/en/v2.2.1/user/line/" (Single component (free-free))
   - validation: Compare with VLA and ALMA fluxes; attribute inverted Bands 6–9 structure to beam size effects  
  > "our VLA and ALMA measurements indicate the electron density of $n_\mathrm{e}\approx6\times10^4$ cm$^{-3}$" (Single component (free-free)); "In this model, the inverted spectral feature at the Bands 6, 7, and 9 ranges in the SED can be explained by beam size effects" (Single component (free-free))
5. **Test jet and dust alone**: Compare a jet power law plus dust grey body with the SED  
  > "tries to explain the SED by two components, i.e., jet PL (purple) and dust grey body (blue)." (Two components (jet + dust))
   - parameter: α_jet ≈ -0.5  
  > "Even if we assume the PL index of $\alpha_{\rm jet}\approx-0.5$ (without energy loss at the high frequency)" (Two components (jet + dust))
   - assumption: T_dust = 46 K; β = 2  
  > "Therefore, we assume $T_{\rm dust}=46$ K and $\beta=2$ according to (cite: Garc2016ApJ...823L..12G) for cold dust." (Multiple components)
   - assumption: κ_ν ~ κ_352GHz (ν[GHz]/352)^β; κ_352GHz = 0.09 m^2 kg^-1  
  > "$\sim\kappa_\mathrm{352GHz}\times(\nu\,\mathrm{[GHz]}/352)^\beta$ with $\kappa_\mathrm{352GHz}=0.09\,\mathrm{m^2 kg^{-1}}$" (Multiple components)
   - assumption: D_L = 13.97±2.10 Mpc  
  > "The nearby spiral galaxy NGC 1068 located at the distance of $D_{\rm L}=13.97\pm2.10$ Mpc (cite: Anand2021MNRAS.501.3621A)" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: VLA points lie below prediction and Bands 6 and 7 above it; offsets cannot be explained by the observed beamsize effects  
  > "the VLA data points are systematically lower than the predicted line (black) and Band 6 and 7 points are higher." (Two components (jet + dust)); "These systematic offsets can not be explained by beamsize effects" (Two components (jet + dust))
6. **Fit jet, corona and dust with an absorbing screen**: Add coronal SSA to jet power law and dust grey body; apply free-free absorption for the low-frequency drop  
  > "Because the PL at low frequency and grey-body at high frequency cannot explain the excess of 90-400 GHz emission, we consider the SSA components originally from the corona." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario); "to explain the rapid drop at $<10$ GHz, we consider that the emissions are screened by free–free absorption" (Screen by ionized gas)
   - parameter: Jet: S_100GHz = 6.5 mJy; α_jet = -0.75  
  > "We consider that the 22 GHz continuum is mainly from synchrotron emission with $(S_\mathrm{100\,GHz},\alpha_{\rm jet})=(6.5\,\mathrm{mJy}, -0.75)$" (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - parameter: Dust mass: 6.5×10^3 M_sun  
  > "For the high frequency (i.e., ALMA Band 9), we consider the grey-body with the dust mass of $M_\mathrm{dust}=6.5\times10^3\,M_{\odot}$" (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - parameter: Example screen: n_e = 3×10^3 cm^-3; T_e = 10,000 K; l_L.O.S = 20 pc  
  > "($n_\mathrm{e}$, $T_\mathrm{e}$, $l_\mathrm{L.O.S}$) = ($3\times10^3$ cm$^{-3}$, $10,000$ K, $20$ pc)" (Screen by ionized gas)
   - assumption: Electron-energy spectrum slope δ = 2.7  
  > "Assuming $\delta=2.7$" (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - assumption: T_dust = 46 K; β = 2; κ_352GHz = 0.09 m^2 kg^-1  
  > "Therefore, we assume $T_{\rm dust}=46$ K and $\beta=2$ according to (cite: Garc2016ApJ...823L..12G) for cold dust." (Multiple components); "$\kappa_\mathrm{352GHz}=0.09\,\mathrm{m^2 kg^{-1}}$" (Multiple components)
   - fitting: Bayesian parameter estimation of S_νSSA and ν_SSA; uncertainty from marginalized-distribution percentiles  
  > "($15^{+3}_{-3}$ mJy, $134^{+28}_{-22}$ GHz) based on Bayesian parameter estimation" (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario); "The error terms of $S_{\nu_\mathrm{SSA}}$, $\nu_\mathrm{SSA}$, $B$, and $R$ are calculated based on the 16th, 50th, and 84th percentiles of the samples in the marginalized distributions." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - validation: Includes the new 100 GHz point; fails to reproduce the inverse spectrum between Bands 6 and 7  
  > "our new analysis improves the overall behavior of the SED by including new 100 GHz data." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario); "The SED fit does not reproduce the inverse spectrum between Band 6 and 7." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
7. **Infer coronal magnetic field and size**: Convert fitted SSA turnover flux and frequency using the coronal model; estimate beam-based brightness temperature  
  > "Determining $S_\mathrm{SSA}$ and $\nu_\mathrm{SSA}$ enables us to estimate the coronal magnetic field strength ($B$) and the size ($R$)" (Multiple components); "According to the coronal model (cite: Inoue2018ApJ...869..114I)" (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario); "The brightness temperature is estimated to be $410\pm80$ K at $\nu_\mathrm{SSA}$ considering the synthesized beam size of 0.05$''$." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - parameter: Brightness-temperature beam: 0.05''  
  > "The brightness temperature is estimated to be $410\pm80$ K at $\nu_\mathrm{SSA}$ considering the synthesized beam size of 0.05$''$." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - assumption: Non-thermal electron energy fraction 0.03; coronal temperature 10 keV; Thomson optical depth 1.1; black hole mass 5×10^7 M_sun  
  > "we adopt the energy fraction of non-thermal electrons of 0.03, the coronal temperature of $10$ keV (cite: Pal2022MNRAS.517.3341P)" (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario); "the Thomson scattering optical depth value of 1.1, and the black hole mass of $5\times10^7\,M_{\odot}$ according to (cite: Inoue_2020)." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - fitting: 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles of marginalized samples; assumption-related systematic errors excluded  
  > "The error terms of $S_{\nu_\mathrm{SSA}}$, $\nu_\mathrm{SSA}$, $B$, and $R$ are calculated based on the 16th, 50th, and 84th percentiles of the samples in the marginalized distributions." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario); "The systematic errors regarding assumptions (e.g., several coronal parameters such as a typical energy fraction of non-thermal electrons and the Thomson scattering optical depth) are not considered." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
   - validation: Compare inferred magnetic field with other nearby Seyferts  
  > "We note that our new magnetic field estimate $B\approx20$ G is consistent with the values revealed in other nearby Seyferts (cite: Inoue2018ApJ...869..114I)." (Three components (jet + corona + dust) scenario)
