## Analysis recipe

1. **Create CO(12-11) channel maps and integrated intensity map, and [O III] 88 um and [N II] 205 um channel maps**: Clean channel maps at 100 km s^-1 resolution using the 1.5 mm continuum clean mask, and integrate CO(12-11) over -250 to +250 km s^-1 relative to systemic redshift z = 6.0244.  
  > "By applying a clean mask constructed for 1.5 mm continuum (white solid line in the data panel of figure 1; see Paper I for details), we make channel maps of the CO(12-11) line at 100$\:$km$\:$s$^{-1}$ spectral resolution with a Briggs weighting of robustness parameter of 2.0" (Observations and imaging); "The CO(12-11) line is integrated over a velocity range of $-$250$\:$km$\:$s$^{-1}$ to +250$\:$km$\:$s$^{-1}$." (Observations and imaging)
   - parameter: Spectral resolution = 100 km s^-1, Briggs robust = 2.0, integration range = -250 to +250 km s^-1  
  > "we make channel maps of the CO(12-11) line at 100$\:$km$\:$s$^{-1}$ spectral resolution with a Briggs weighting of robustness parameter of 2.0" (Observations and imaging); "The CO(12-11) line is integrated over a velocity range of $-$250$\:$km$\:$s$^{-1}$ to +250$\:$km$\:$s$^{-1}$." (Observations and imaging)
   - assumption: Systemic redshift z = 6.0244 +/- 0.0003  
  > "The velocity offset is calculated relative to the systemic redshift of $z=6.0244\pm0.0003$ (see section 3.2)." (Observations and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Peak SNR checks on arcs (7.8 sigma and 6.0 sigma for CO(12-11); > 4 sigma for [O III] and [N II] channels)  
  > "where the peak line fluxes and noise levels are $0.19\pm0.02\:\mathrm{Jy\:beam^{-1}\:km\:s^{-1}}\ (7.8\sigma)$ and $0.15\pm0.02\:\mathrm{Jy\:beam^{-1}\:km\:s^{-1}}\ (6.0\sigma)$ in the north-west and south-east arcs, respectively" (Observations and imaging); "We also show the the channel maps of \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and \[N \] $205\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ emissions with peak signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of larger than 4" (Observations and imaging)
2. **Source-plane reconstruction of CO(12-11) integrated intensity map**: Parametric gravitational lens modeling in the cleaned image plane inside the clean mask region with foreground lens parameters fixed from 1.5 mm continuum modeling.  
  > "We use the open-source code `GLAFIC` (cite: Oguri2010) to quantify the gravitational lensing effect and to reconstruct the source spatial structure with a parametric approach in the cleaned image plane." (Lens modeling); "Then, we reconstruct the background source, where all parameters of the foreground source are fixed." (Lens modeling)
   - software: GLAFIC
   - parameter: Free parameters: xy-position, total flux Sdv, and effective radius R_e; fixed parameters: Sersic index n = 1, axis ratio q = 0.93, position angle theta_q = 108 deg  
  > "we adopt an exponential disk profile with $n=1$. We fix the axis ratio and position angle to $q=0.93$ and $\theta_q=108$ deg, respectively, the values of which are derived from the 1.5-mm continuum data." (Integrated intensity map of the CO(12-11) emission); "Then, we fit the integrated intensity map of the CO(12-11) emission with the remaining free parameters of $xy$-position, $Sdv$, and $R_e$." (Integrated intensity map of the CO(12-11) emission)
   - assumption: Foreground lens at z = 0.776 follows a singular isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) with external shear; background source at z = 6.02 has an exponential disk (Sersic n = 1) profile.  
  > "We assume that the mass distribution of the foreground lens galaxy, which is situated at $z=0.776$ (cite: Fudamoto2017), follows a singular isothermal ellipsoid with external shear and that the brightness distribution of the background source galaxy at $z=6.02$ has a S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic profile." (Lens modeling)
   - fitting: 500 Monte Carlo runs adding 1 sigma noise map convolved with dirty beam to the clean image; uncertainties from 16th and 84th percentiles  
  > "To estimate the uncertainties of the derived source parameters, we add a $1\sigma$ noise map convolved with a dirty beam to the clean image and fit the noise-added images. Then, we take the 16th and the 84th percentile of the best-fit values from 500 Monte Carlo runs as the uncertainties." (Integrated intensity map of the CO(12-11) emission)
   - validation: Tested leaving Sersic index n free, imaging with different robust parameters (1.5, 1.0, 0.5, 0.0, -0.5), and running 500 Monte Carlo mock simulations varying R_true from 0.01'' to 0.14''  
  > "Therefore, we perform the fitting with the S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic index also free. Consequently, the obtained effective radius don’t change significantly ($R_e=0.09^{+0.07}_{-0.04}\ \mathrm{arcsec}$, $n=0.6^{+3.3}_{-0.06}$)." (CO(12-11) intrinsic source size); "In addition, we try the source reconstructions using clean images with different robust parameters of $1.5$, $1.0$, $0.5$, $0.0$, and $-0.5$ (the same as that of dust emission)." (CO(12-11) intrinsic source size); "Using `GLAFIC` code, we vary the effective radius of the S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic source from 0.01$''$ to 0.14$''$ in 0.01$''$ increments while fixing the other parameters at the best-fit parameters obtained in section 3.1 to create the mock lensed images." (CO(12-11) intrinsic source size)
3. **Source-plane reconstruction and kinematic analysis of [O III] 88 um and [N II] 205 um channel maps**: Reconstruct individual 100 km s^-1 channel maps in the source plane using circular Gaussian (n = 0.5) or point source models, and fit a single Gaussian to the reconstructed [O III] 88 um line profile to measure systemic redshift.  
  > "We adopt a circular Gaussian source with S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic index $n=0.5$ rather than $n=1$ as used in the modeling of the integrated intensity maps so that the fitting is more sensitive to the peak position." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission); "Thus, we also perform the source reconstruction using a point source instead of a Gaussian source and adopt a better model based on their chi-square values." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission); "Based on the single gaussian fit to the reconstructed profile of the \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ line, the redshift is determined to be $z=6.0244\pm0.0003$" (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
   - software: GLAFIC
   - parameter: Sersic index n = 0.5 (circular Gaussian), point source for -200 km s^-1 channel of [N II] 205 um, two circular Gaussian sources for +100 km s^-1 channel of [O III] 88 um  
  > "As a result, the point source model is adopted only for the $\SI{-200}{km.s^{-1}}$ channel of \[N \] $\SI{205}{\ensuremath{\mu}m}$." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission); "Then, fitting using two circular Gaussian sources is performed ($\SI{100}{km.s^{-1}}$ (2 sources row) panels)." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
   - assumption: Circular Gaussian source profile (n = 0.5) or point source  
  > "We adopt a circular Gaussian source with S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic index $n=0.5$ rather than $n=1$ as used in the modeling of the integrated intensity maps" (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
   - fitting: Chi-square model selection between extended Gaussian and point source; uncertainties from 500 Monte Carlo runs  
  > "Thus, we also perform the source reconstruction using a point source instead of a Gaussian source and adopt a better model based on their chi-square values. As a result, the point source model is adopted only for the $\SI{-200}{km.s^{-1}}$ channel of \[N \] $\SI{205}{\ensuremath{\mu}m}$. The uncertainties of the parameters are estimated in the same manner as described in section 3.1." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
   - validation: Inspection of residual maps to ensure peak residuals < 3 sigma; adding a second source at +100 km s^-1 in [O III] 88 um reduced > 5 sigma residuals to < 3 sigma  
  > "The residual maps demonstrate that the fitting with a circular Gaussian source has generally worked well, remaining $\lesssim 3\sigma$ peak residual." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission); "The **a** and **b** sources correspond to (**a1**, **a2**) and (**b1**, **b2**) components in the image plane and reduce the residuals to $<3\sigma$." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
4. **Non-LTE radiative transfer modeling of the CO SLED and evaluation of dust attenuation**: Two-component 1D non-LTE radiative transfer modeling of galaxy-integrated CO(2-1), CO(5-4), CO(6-5), and CO(12-11) fluxes using RADEX, plus analytical estimation of dust extinction on CO(12-11).  
  > "In order to investigate quantitatively the physical properties of the molecular gas, we model the CO SLED, using the 1D non-local thermal equilibrium (non-LTE) radiative transfer code `RADEX` (cite: vanderTak2007)." (Physical properties of highly excited gas); "In the modeling, we incorporate, in addition to the CO(12-11) line detected in this work, the CO(2-1) (Zavala et al. in prep), CO(5-4), and CO(6-5) (cite: Fudamoto2017; Zavala2018) lines detected in G09-83808 (Table [\[COdata\]](#COdata))." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Expanding sphere geometry, dV = 400 km s^-1, background temperature = 19.2 K (CMB at z = 6.02); search ranges: T_kin = 20-700 K, log(n_H2/cm^-3) = 2-6.7, log(N_CO/cm^-2) = 16-20.5  
  > "We assume an expanding sphere geometry and set $dV=\SI{400}{km.s^{-1}}$, which is roughly the FWHM of the CO lines, the background temperature to 19.2$\:$K, and the cosmic microwave background temperature to $z = 6.02$. We search for the minimum chi-square within the parameter space of $T_{\textrm{kin}}=20-700$ K, $\log(n_{\mathrm{H2}}/\mathrm{cm^{-3}})=2-6.7$, $\log(N_{\mathrm{CO}}/\mathrm{cm^{-2}})=16-20.5$." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
   - assumption: Differential magnification across CO transitions is negligible; molecular gas mass <= M_dyn (5 x 10^10 M_sun); L_CO(13-12) < L_CO(12-11) for both components; T_kin,cold < 100 K; dust attenuation model uses lambda_0 = 150 um and beta = 2.5.  
  > "A constraint that we set is that the molecular gas mass must not exceed the dynamical mass ($M_{\mathrm{dyn}}=5\times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ including $1\sigma$ error; (cite: Zavala2018)). Another constraint is that the CO SLED of both the warm and cool gas components satisfy $L_\mathrm{{CO(13-12)}} < L_\mathrm{{CO(12-11)}}$. We also assume that the temperature of the cold component is $T_{\mathrm{kin, cold}}<100\, \mathrm{K}$." (Physical properties of highly excited gas); "Adopting $\lambda_0=150\;\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and $\beta=2.5$ (Paper I), we find that the effect of dust attenuation on the CO(12-11) line at the rest-frame wavelength of $\sim\SI{200}{\ensuremath{\mu}m}$ is roughly 20%." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
   - fitting: Minimum chi-square search over parameter grid; 200 Monte Carlo iterations adding 1 sigma Gaussian noise to CO data to derive mean values and standard deviations  
  > "We search for the minimum chi-square within the parameter space of $T_{\textrm{kin}}=20-700$ K, $\log(n_{\mathrm{H2}}/\mathrm{cm^{-3}})=2-6.7$, $\log(N_{\mathrm{CO}}/\mathrm{cm^{-2}})=16-20.5$." (Physical properties of highly excited gas); "We add a $1\sigma$ Gaussian noise to the observed CO data and fit the noise-added CO SLED. This process is repeated 200 times to obtain the mean values and standard deviations." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
   - validation: Cold component kinetic temperature (55 +/- 16 K) compared and found consistent with dust temperature from SED modeling (51 K; Paper I)  
  > "The temperature of the cold component is consistent with that of the dust estimated from dust spectral energy distribution modeling (51$\:$K; Paper I)." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)

## Qualitative findings

- More than 99% of the CO(12-11) emission originates from the warm and dense molecular gas component, which is concentrated in the central ~0.5 kpc region and coincides with the dust continuum.  
  > "The results show that more than 99% of the CO(12-11) emission originates from the warm component (figure 6). This implies that the warm and dense molecular gas is concentrated in the center of this galaxy ($R_e=0.49^{+0.29}_{-0.19}\ \mathrm{kpc}$)." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
- Reconstructed [O III] 88 um and [N II] 205 um channel maps show an almost monotonic velocity gradient over ~2 kpc and double-horned intrinsic line profiles, plus two star-forming sub-components (a and b) in [O III] 88 um.  
  > "An almost monotonic velocity gradient is found over $\sim 2$ kpc centered at the compact dust continuum regions ($R_e\sim0.64$ kpc; Paper I) in both \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and \[N \] $205\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ line maps. And, the reconstructed line profiles of \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and \[N \] $205\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ have double-horn shapes as shown in figure 4." (Kinematics of ionized gas)
- The high L_CO(12-11)/L_CO(6-5) ratio suggests that G09-83808 may host a central dust-obscured AGN, though shocks and extreme starbursts cannot be excluded.  
  > "This comparison suggests that G09-83808 hosts a dust-obscured AGN in the center although we cannot exclude other possibilities, such as shock (cite: Meijerink2013; Saito2017) and extreme starburst (cite: Riechers2013), with the current data alone." (Heating source of CO(12-11) emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-J CO lines in high-z objects are redshifted to millimeter bands observable with ALMA at high sensitivity and angular resolution, and gravitational lensing magnification (mu ~ 8.4) enables spatially resolving the emission.  
  > "In contrast, for high-$z$ objects, high-$J$ CO lines are redshifted to a millimeter band and hence are observable with ground-based interferometers like ALMA." (Introduction); "Gravitational lensing magnifies the solid angles of background sources, enabling us to observe distant objects with increased spatial resolution." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Lens modeling is restricted to the 1.5 mm clean mask region to focus on the emitting region and reduce computation; Sersic parameters n=1, q=0.93, and theta_q=108 deg are fixed for CO(12-11) due to lower SNR and resolution than 1.5 mm continuum; n=0.5 circular Gaussians are used in channel maps to be more sensitive to peak positions.  
  > "We perform all lens modeling in the clean mask region (white solid line in the cleaned image panel of figure 1, 2, 3) to focus on the emitting region and reduce the amount of calculation." (Lens modeling); "Since the SNR and angular resolution of the integrated intensity map of the CO(12-11) emission are worse than those of the 1.5-mm continuum map (75$\sigma$ detection and $\times$), it is not straightforward to determine all the parameters of the background source" (Integrated intensity map of the CO(12-11) emission); "We adopt a circular Gaussian source with S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic index $n=0.5$ rather than $n=1$ as used in the modeling of the integrated intensity maps so that the fitting is more sensitive to the peak position." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
- Alternative: Single-component RADEX model for the CO SLED (rejected because the fit did not converge within the parameter search range)  
  > "Our fitting attempt with the CO SLED with a single component results in a solution that does not converge within the search range. Hence, the model requires an additional component." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
- Alternative: Point source model vs. extended circular Gaussian (n = 0.5) for [O III] 88 um and [N II] 205 um channel maps (point source rejected for all channels except -200 km s^-1 of [N II] 205 um based on chi-square)  
  > "Thus, we also perform the source reconstruction using a point source instead of a Gaussian source and adopt a better model based on their chi-square values. As a result, the point source model is adopted only for the $\SI{-200}{km.s^{-1}}$ channel of \[N \] $\SI{205}{\ensuremath{\mu}m}$." (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
- Alternative: Single Gaussian source for the +100 km s^-1 channel of [O III] 88 um (rejected in favor of two Gaussian sources due to > 5 sigma residual structure)  
  > "a non-negligible structure with SNR $>5$ remains at the $\SI{100}{km.s^{-1}}$ channel of \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ residual map using a single Gaussian source. Then, fitting using two circular Gaussian sources is performed" (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission)
- Alternative: Free Sersic index n and different Briggs robust parameters (1.5, 1.0, 0.5, 0.0, -0.5) for CO(12-11) size measurement (tested in Appendix 2; robust <= 0.0 rejected due to < 5 sigma integrated flux; n = 1 fixed model kept for fair comparison with other lines)  
  > "When imaging with robust parameters of $0.0$ or less, the integrated flux density is less than $5\sigma$, so we don’t perform the modeling." (CO(12-11) intrinsic source size); "Note that we used the measured size when the S$\acute{\mathrm{e}}$rsic index is fixed to 1.0, in order to make the size comparison with other emissions fairer." (CO(12-11) intrinsic source size)
- Alternative: Source reconstruction of individual CO(12-11) channel maps (not performed due to low SNR)  
  > "Due to the low SNR, we didn’t perform the source reconstructions for each channel." (CO(12-11) channel maps)
- How they decided it was enough: A second Gaussian source was added in the +100 km s^-1 channel of [O III] 88 um when a single source left > 5 sigma residuals, reducing residuals to < 3 sigma; a second RADEX gas component was added when a single-component CO SLED fit failed to converge.  
  > "a non-negligible structure with SNR $>5$ remains at the $\SI{100}{km.s^{-1}}$ channel of \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ residual map using a single Gaussian source. Then, fitting using two circular Gaussian sources is performed" (Channel maps of the [O ] 88 um and [N ] 205 um emission); "Our fitting attempt with the CO SLED with a single component results in a solution that does not converge within the search range. Hence, the model requires an additional component." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
- Limitation (authors): Differential magnification is not accounted for in the galaxy-integrated CO SLED modeling.  
  > "In other words, the present analysis does not take into account the dependency of the gravitational lensing effect on the source position." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
- Limitation (authors): Two-component RADEX fitting relies on only four observed CO transitions and requires imposed priors/constraints (M_gas <= M_dyn, L_CO(13-12) < L_CO(12-11), T_kin,cold < 100 K) to prevent parameters from exceeding search ranges.  
  > "In our case of G09-83808, the number of the observed CO line data is only four; thus, some additional constraints on the free parameters are necessary to perform two-component model-fitting and obtain meaningful results." (Physical properties of highly excited gas); "We note that the determined $T_{\mathrm{kin}}$ or $n_{\mathrm{H2}}$ would go beyond the search ranges without these constraints in the model fitting." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
- Limitation (authors): Dust attenuation is not included in the main RADEX fit (estimated to reduce CO(12-11) intensity by ~20%).  
  > "Finally, we discuss the potential effect of dust attenuation, which we have not considered in the analysis." (Physical properties of highly excited gas)
- Limitation (authors): Limited angular resolution and SNR prevent definitively distinguishing a rotating disk from mergers or outflows, or determining whether the [O III] sub-components are bound clumps or external accretion.  
  > "These results could be interpreted as a signature of a rotating disk, but it is difficult to reject the other possibilities such as mergers and outflows with the current data alone due to the limited angular resolution and SNR (cite: Simons2019; Rizzo2022)." (Kinematics of ionized gas); "It is difficult to determine from the current data alone whether the \[O \] $88\:\ensuremath{\mu}m$ sub-components are gravitationally bound clumps or have an external origin, such as clumpy gas accretion." (Kinematics of ionized gas)
- Limitation (authors): Shocks and extreme starbursts cannot be excluded as alternative heating mechanisms for CO(12-11) alongside a dust-obscured AGN.  
  > "This comparison suggests that G09-83808 hosts a dust-obscured AGN in the center although we cannot exclude other possibilities, such as shock (cite: Meijerink2013; Saito2017) and extreme starburst (cite: Riechers2013), with the current data alone." (Heating source of CO(12-11) emission)
- Limitation (authors): Source size reconstruction with GLAFIC cannot distinguish extended sources from point sources when R_true <= 0.04 arcsec.  
  > "This result implies that it is difficult to distinguish the extended source from the point source in the region of $R_{\text{true}}\lesssim 0.04\:$arcsec." (CO(12-11) intrinsic source size)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Lens modeling is performed in the cleaned image plane rather than the native interferometric uv-plane, where correlated noise and beam convolution effects are only approximated by convolving a noise map with the dirty beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitting a two-component non-LTE RADEX model (6 free parameters plus area/filling factors) to only 4 CO transitions is underconstrained and heavily dependent on the imposed ad-hoc priors.
