## Science

- Question: Test the invariance of the fine-structure constant alpha (and mu) by comparing the velocity offset between CH and H2O submillimeter absorption lines toward PKS 1830-211 (z_abs=0.89) and B 0218+357 (z_abs=0.68)

## Analysis recipe

1. **extract absorption spectra from visibilities**: Visibility fitting with UVMultiFit using a model of two point sources with free amplitudes, giving separate NE and SW spectra normalized to each image continuum  
  > "the spectra were extracted from visibility fitting using the Python tool UVMultiFit ((cite: mar14)), taking a model consisting of two point sources with free amplitudes" (Observations / PKS 1830-211); "were normalized to the continuum level of each image" (Observations / PKS 1830-211)
   - software: CASA, UVMultiFit
   - parameter: Relative positions of the two point sources fixed at the known locations of the lensed images  
  > "relative positions fixed at the known locations of the two lensed images of the quasar" (Observations / PKS 1830-211)
   - parameter: Visibilities self-calibrated before extraction  
  > "The visibilities of PKS 1830$-$211 were self-calibrated" (Observations / PKS 1830-211)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **fit a common multi-Gaussian opacity profile to CH and H2O**: Model I_abs = 1 - f_c (1 - exp(-G_tau)) with a common Gaussian-sum opacity profile for CH (convolved with hyperfine structure) and H2O (scaled by gamma_tau), plus a bulk velocity offset delta_v between species  
  > "We assume that the spectra of both CH and H$_2$O can be described by a common opacity profile $G_\tau(v)$, composed of the sum of individual Gaussian components." (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile); "I_{\rm abs}(v)  = 1 - f_c \times (1-{\rm e}^{-G_\tau(v)})," (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
   - software: scipy
   - parameter: Free parameters: centroid velocity, linewidth, peak opacity per Gaussian; covering factor if not fixed; gamma_tau; delta_v  
  > "the fitting procedure included the following parameters: centroid velocity, linewidth, and peak opacity for each Gaussian component, plus the covering factor, if not fixed, the global scaling ratio $\gamma_\tau$, and the bulk velocity offset $\delta v$" (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
   - parameter: Number of Gaussians: 17 (PKS 1830-211 NE), 11 (SW), 3 (SW v=+170 km/s component), 5 (B 0218+357 SW)  
  > "fixing the number of Gaussians to 17 for the line of sight toward PKS 1830$-$211(NE), 11 for PKS 1830$-$211(SW), 3 for the $v=+170$ km s$^{-1}$ isolated velocity component toward PKS 1830$-$211(SW), and 5 for B 0218$+$357(SW)" (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
   - parameter: Covering factor fixed at f_c = 1 for the NE sightline; free for SW (H2O saturated); varied 0.8-1.0 for B 0218+357  
  > "A fixed covering factor $f_c = 1$ was assumed for this line of sight, since the lines have low apparent optical depths and the profile does not appear to be saturated." (Analysis and results / Results for the PKS 1830-211(NE) sightline); "we have explored the effect of varying $f_c$ between 0.8 and 1.0, by fixing its value during the fit" (Analysis and results / Results for B 0218+357(SW))
   - assumption: CH hyperfine components have intensities following their statistical weights  
  > "convolved to the hyperfine structure of CH, with the relative intensities of hyperfine components following their statistical weights" (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
   - assumption: H2O hyperfine structure is negligible  
  > "is thus negligible in our work" (Table of spectroscopic parameters, notes)
   - fitting: Python scipy.curve_fit least squares; uncertainties from the covariance matrix scaled by reduced chi2; number of components set by trial until residuals are featureless and reduced chi2 near unity; no priors or sampler  
  > "For the fit, we used the Python scipy.curve_fit least-squares routine." (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile); "The uncertainties were derived as the square root of the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix multiplied by the reduced $\chi^2$." (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile); "The number of Gaussian velocity components was adjusted by successive trials, until the residuals became free from features above the noise level and the reduced $\chi^2$ close to unity." (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
   - validation: Reduced chi2 close to unity; fit opacity profile overlaid on CH and H2O opacity profiles (CH deconvolved from hyperfine structure with CLEAN)  
  > "All reduced $\chi^2$ are close to unity, suggesting that the CH and H$_2$O spectra are reasonably well represented by our assumption of a common opacity profile." (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
3. **combine epochs and sub-features into final velocity offsets**: Weighted average of per-epoch delta_v treated as statistically independent; 2016 spectra averaged before the v=+170 km/s fit; v=0 and v=170 km/s results combined for PKS 1830-211(SW)  
  > "Taking their weighted average for all individual epochs, we obtain a value $\delta v = -0.048 \pm 0.028$ km s$^{-1}$." (Analysis and results / Results for the PKS 1830-211(NE) sightline); "All spectra obtained during the 2016 monitoring were averaged together before fit, in order to improve the signal-to-noise ratio." (Analysis and results / Results for PKS 1830-211(SW))
   - assumption: Epoch measurements are statistically independent because the absorption profile varies with time  
  > "Since all velocity offset measurements have been made from different datasets, taken at different epochs and with clear time variations of the absorption profile (cite: mul23), we consider them statistically independent." (Analysis and results / Results for the PKS 1830-211(NE) sightline)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Changing f_c to 0.8 gave similar velocity offsets and scaling ratios within uncertainties (NE); varying f_c in B 0218+357 had no significant impact on delta_v  
  > "Changing the value of $f_c$ to 0.8 in our fits yielded similar velocity offsets and scaling ratios within uncertainties." (Analysis and results / Results for the PKS 1830-211(NE) sightline); "There was no significant impact on $\delta v$ within uncertainties." (Analysis and results / Results for B 0218+357(SW))
4. **test the fitting method on synthetic spectra**: Monte Carlo simulations of synthetic CH/H2O spectra with imposed delta_v and gamma_tau, Gaussian channel noise, and random per-component H2O amplitude variations of dispersion lambda; fitted exactly like the real data  
  > "we have run Monte Carlo simulations on synthetic spectra" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra); "The fitting procedure was then strictly identical to the treatment of real observational data" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra)
   - software: scipy
   - parameter: Input delta_v = 1 km/s, gamma_tau = 4.0 (NE) and 8.0 (SW), f_c = 1.0 (NE) and 0.95 (SW); 13 (NE) and 5 (SW) Gaussians; noise rms 0.5%  
  > "A fixed bulk velocity offset $\delta v$=1 km s$^{-1}$ and scaling ratios $\gamma_{\tau}$=4.0 and 8.0 were set between the two species, for the NE and SW sightlines, respectively." (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra); "composed of 13 Gaussian components for PKS 1830$-$211(NE), and 5 components for PKS 1830$-$211(SW)" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra); "All this process was iterated several hundred times" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra)
   - fitting: Same scipy.curve_fit procedure as for the real data  
  > "The fitting procedure was then strictly identical to the treatment of real observational data" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra)
   - validation: delta_v recovered with uncertainties similar to the real data for the NE sightline up to 20-30% amplitude variation; SW fits degrade quickly with amplitude variations  
  > "These simulations suggest that the bulk velocity offset can be recovered with typical uncertainties similar to the real observations" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra); "the fit uncertainties of $\delta v$ jump to values higher than 0.3 km s$^{-1}$, as soon as 10% variations are introduced" (Analysis and results / Test on synthetic spectra)
5. **check the CH hyperfine statistical-weight assumption**: Refit PKS 1830-211(SW) 2014-05-05 spectra (with H2(18)O) without bulk offset and with CH hyperfine relative strengths free, strongest 533 GHz component fixed to 0.83  
  > "but without the bulk velocity offset between CH and H$_2$O and with the relative strengths of the hyperfine components of CH set as free parameters" (Robustness of our constraints / Spectroscopy); "We have fixed the strength of the strongest hyperfine component of the 533 GHz transitions to 0.83" (Robustness of our constraints / Spectroscopy)
   - software: scipy
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Fitted relative strengths consistent with statistical weights within uncertainties  
  > "Within uncertainties, these results are consistent with the values of the statistical weights, and therefore, the initial assumption is validated, at least for this data set." (Robustness of our constraints / Spectroscopy)
6. **check CH submillimeter excitation**: RADEX excitation calculation for CH submillimeter lines under standard interstellar conditions with hyperfine-resolved collision data; excitation temperatures stay close to T_CMB  
  > "we have explored with RADEX (cite: vdtak07) the excitation of submillimeter lines of CH under standard interstellar conditions, using hyperfine-resolved collision data from (cite: dag18)" (Robustness of our constraints / Spectroscopy)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Example conditions: kinetic temperature 50 K, H2 density up to 10^4 cm^-3  
  > "within a few tenths of K from $T_\mathrm{CMB}$ for a kinetic temperature of 50 K and an H$_2$ volume density up to $10^4$ cm$^{-3}$" (Robustness of our constraints / Spectroscopy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **convert velocity offsets to constraints on Delta alpha/alpha and Delta mu/mu**: delta_v/c = 1.6 Delta alpha/alpha + 0.8 Delta mu/mu; upper limits built as |x| + 3 sigma_x; alpha limits derived assuming no mu variation or by combining with independent Delta mu/mu limits  
  > "\frac{\delta v}{c} = 1.6 \times \frac{\Delta \alpha}{\alpha} + 0.8 \times \frac{\Delta \mu}{\mu}." (Methylidyne and variations of alpha and mu); "All the upper limits below are built as $|x|+ 3\sigma_x$." (Discussion)
   - parameter: Delta K_alpha = 1.6 and Delta K_mu = 0.8 between CH and ortho-H2O  
  > "The sensitivity differences between those CH lines and a pure rotational line, such as the $1_{10}-1_{01}$ ground-state transition of ortho-H$_2$O, with $K_\alpha = 0$ and $K_\mu = -1$, are then $\Delta K_\alpha = 1.6$ and $\Delta K_\mu = 0.8$." (Methylidyne and variations of alpha and mu)
   - assumption: Independent Delta mu/mu limits from methanol (PKS 1830-211, 0.54 ppm) and from ammonia (B 0218+357, 7.1 ppm) are used for the combined alpha limits  
  > "Combining this independent constraint on $\Delta \mu / \mu$ from methanol and our new constraint from CH–H$_2$O lines, we derive" (Discussion); "the independent constraint $|\Delta \mu / \mu|<7.1$ ppm from (cite: kan11)" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **test the alpha dipole anisotropy**: Compare the two sightlines, lying about 39 and 38 degrees from the opposite poles of the reported dipole, to derive an upper limit on the difference in Delta alpha/alpha and on the dipole amplitude  
  > "With a dipole of $\Delta \alpha / \alpha$ described as $\mathcal{D}\cos{(\Theta)}$" (Discussion); "The direction to PKS 1830$-$211 is $\Theta \sim 39^\circ$ away from the pole in the southern hemisphere" (Discussion)
   - assumption: Dipole of the form D cos(Theta) as described by Webb et al. 2011  
  > "With a dipole of $\Delta \alpha / \alpha$ described as $\mathcal{D}\cos{(\Theta)}$" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CH and H2O spectra are well described by a common opacity profile with no significant bulk velocity offset in all three sightlines  
  > "consistent with no kinematics difference" (Analysis and results / Results for PKS 1830-211(SW)); "All reduced $\chi^2$ are close to unity" (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
- Opacity ratio gamma_tau in B 0218+357(SW) is closer to that in PKS 1830-211(NE) than in SW, suggesting similar absorber properties  
  > "adding to previous evidence collected by (cite: wal16; wal19) that the absorbing material in B 0218$+$357(SW) have properties close to PKS 1830$-$211(NE)" (Analysis and results / Results for B 0218+357(SW))

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Simultaneous CH and H2O observations in the same tuning minimize time-variability, quasar morphology and instrumental systematics  
  > "Our observational setup minimizes known specific systematics of radio absorbers, such as the time variations of the absorption line profile and the frequency-dependent morphology of the background quasars" (Conclusions)
- Why this method: CH mixed transitions have high K_alpha sensitivity relative to the pure rotational H2O line, and mu is already tightly constrained independently in these absorbers  
  > "the observation of CH and H$_2$O lines provides an exceptionally sensitive test of the variations of $\alpha$" (Methylidyne and variations of alpha and mu)
- Alternative: Saturated H2O central component of PKS 1830-211(SW) rejected for direct CH-H2O kinematic comparison; H2(18)O used to complement it  
  > "This region is therefore not useful for our purpose of comparing the kinematics of CH and H$_2$O directly." (Analysis and results / Description of spectra)
- Alternative: Alternative covering factor f_c = 0.8 for PKS 1830-211(NE) gave similar results  
  > "Changing the value of $f_c$ to 0.8 in our fits yielded similar velocity offsets and scaling ratios within uncertainties." (Analysis and results / Results for the PKS 1830-211(NE) sightline)
- How they decided it was enough: Number of Gaussian components was increased until residuals were featureless and reduced chi2 was near unity  
  > "The number of Gaussian velocity components was adjusted by successive trials, until the residuals became free from features above the noise level and the reduced $\chi^2$ close to unity." (Analysis and results / Fitting of a common multi-Gaussian profile)
- Limitation (authors): Alpha and mu variations cannot be disentangled from one velocity offset; an independent Delta mu/mu limit is needed  
  > "Equation [\[eq:df/f\]](#eq:df/f) shows that the variations of $\alpha$ and $\mu$ cannot be disentangled." (Methylidyne and variations of alpha and mu)
- Limitation (authors): Common-profile fitting may introduce systematics if CH and H2O profiles differ between components  
  > "Our method of fitting a common profile may introduce some systematics if the absorption profiles of CH and H$_2$O are not strictly identical" (Analysis and results / Results for the PKS 1830-211(NE) sightline)
- Limitation (authors): Co-spatiality of CH and H2O cannot be formally proven  
  > "Although we cannot formally prove that line kinematics do not suffer from chemical differentiation within individual velocity components and that the two species are co-spatial" (Robustness of our constraints / Co-spatiality of CH and H2O)
- Limitation (authors): B 0218+357 spectra are noisier and the dipole test rests on only two sources  
  > "The spectra toward B 0218$+$357 (Fig.[2](#fig:allfit), right column) are noticeably noisier than those toward PKS 1830$-$211" (Observations / B 0218+357)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No explicit systematic error budget (velocity/frequency calibration, Doppler tracking, bandpass) is propagated; only fit statistics enter the limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Combined alpha limit toward PKS 1830-211 pairs a CH-H2O offset from the NE sightline with a methanol Delta mu/mu limit from the SW sightline, assuming the same mu value for both
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Per-epoch offsets are treated as independent although all epochs share the same common-profile model and gamma_tau is assumed constant across components
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits are defined as |x|+3 sigma, which differs from the 3 sigma convention used in earlier cited limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table date 2014/07/19 differs from the 17 July 2014 visit stated in the text
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Component counts were tuned by trial until reduced chi2 near 1; no model comparison criterion is given
