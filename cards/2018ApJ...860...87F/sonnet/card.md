## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce GBT CO(1-0) spectrum**: Standard SubBeamNod reduction, polynomial baseline from adjacent blank-sky scans, co-add beams and windows  
  > "the blank-sky scans taken immediately before and after each target scan were averaged and used to derive a polynomial baseline that was removed from each target scan before co-addition." (2.2 GBT Data Reduction)
   - software: GBTIDL
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Profile checked with data subsets and different reduction methods  
  > "The shape of the CO(1-0) profile was confirmed using sub-sets of the data for the different beams and different spectral windows." (2.2 GBT Data Reduction)
2. **Fit double Gaussian to CO(1-0)**: Double Gaussian fit giving components A and B  
  > "Properties derived from fitting a double Gaussian to the CO(1-0) line profile." (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Image ALMA CO(3-2), CO(7-6) cubes**: Natural-weighted imaging, smoothed to 100 and 300 km/s  
  > "The data were imaged using natural-weighting to maximize the signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio." (2.3 ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive CO excitation**: Brightness temperature ratios r31, r71 compared with RADEX LVG models  
  > "we used the radiative transfer code RADEX (van der Tak et al. 2007)." (3.3 CO Excitation)
   - software: RADEX
   - assumption: Typical Galactic CO abundance ratios and velocity gradients  
  > "We adopted typical CO abundance ratios and velocity gradients used in Large-Velocity-Gradient modeling for Galactic molecular gas" (3.3 CO Excitation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive molecular gas masses and W1 dynamical mass**: Lens-corrected L'CO times alpha_CO per component; W1 mass from CO(3-2) fraction; M_dyn from turbulent support  
  > "the dynamical mass is $M_{dyn} =2.3\times10^5
 R_{\rm kpc} (3 \sigma_{v}^{2}) \,\textstyle\rm{M}_{\odot}$" (4.2 The Nature of W1)
   - assumption: alpha_CO 0.8 for B, 4.3 for A; magnification 2.38; W1 gas bound with isotropic turbulence  
  > "Assuming the ULIRG CO–to–H$_2$ conversion factor for component (B) and the Galactic conversion factor for component (A)" (4.1 Total Amount of Molecular Gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA CO(7-6) emission is associated only with component B (L1, L2SW); component A has much lower excitation.  
  > "There is no evidence of any CO(7-6) emission associated with velocity component (A)." (3.2 ALMA CO Results)
- CO(3-2) reveals extended component W1 west of L2SW and L1 matching component A in velocity and width.  
  > "The CO(3-2) spectrum for W1 has a velocity and line-width that closely matches component (A) of the GBT CO(1-0) profile (Fig. 6)." (3.2 ALMA CO Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Single-dish GBT used to capture extended emission  
  > "highlight the potential contribution that single-dish observations can have for studying molecular emission on large spatial scales" (5 Concluding Remarks)
- Alternative: Metal-poor inflowing gas argued against by lack of enhanced [CI] emission  
  > "The lack of a strong enhancement of \[C i\] emission for SMM02399 (Walter et al. 2011) argues strongly against the scenario of W1 resulting from metal-poor inflowing gas." (4.2 The Nature of W1)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): GBT cannot spatially disentangle excitation; alpha_CO for component A is highly uncertain.  
  > "With the single-dish GBT CO(1-0) data, we cannot fully disentangle the CO excitation spatially for the system" (3.3 CO Excitation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only three CO lines, so RADEX constraints are weak
- Caveat (inferred, ours): W1 mass depends on assumed r31 and alpha_CO
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Weak W1 detection; no imaging parameters or significance given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Interferometric CO(3-2) may resolve out flux, so the W1 fraction is uncertain
