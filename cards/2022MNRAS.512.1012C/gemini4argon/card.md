## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA CO(1-0) data cubes and generate moment and error maps**: Clean 3D RA-Dec-velocity cubes to the rms noise level, convolve to a circular beam, and fit each pixel spectrum with a Gaussian to produce peak intensity, velocity, and linewidth maps plus error maps; blank non-converging fits, interpolate isolated blanked pixels with neighbour median, and mask pixels with SNR < 3.  
  > "These images were cleaned until the residuals were consistent with the root-mean-square (rms) noise levels given in Table 1." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Moment maps (peak intensity, velocity, linewidth) were produced by fitting each pixel of the CO images with a Gaussian." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Pixels in the CO intensity, velocity, and velocity dispersion maps with SNR $<3$ based on the peak intensity and associated error maps are masked out." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, Miriad
   - parameter: velocity resolution = 2 km s^-1, SNR >= 3 mask, minimum velocity uncertainty = 6 km s^-1  
  > "The final images all have a velocity resolution of 2 km s$^{-1}$, and we convolved the images to a circular beam, which is listed in Table 1." (Observations and Data Reduction); "We impose a minimum velocity uncertainty of 6 km s$^{-1}$ to account for the random ISM motions ." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Minimum velocity uncertainty of 6 km s^-1 accounts for random ISM motions (~1/3 of typical CO linewidth)  
  > "We impose a minimum velocity uncertainty of 6 km s$^{-1}$ to account for the random ISM motions ." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to each spatial pixel spectrum  
  > "Moment maps (peak intensity, velocity, linewidth) were produced by fitting each pixel of the CO images with a Gaussian." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - validation: Combined ALMA and CARMA images with Miriad immerge to verify that missing short spacings do not significantly affect derived velocity fields  
  > "As a test of the effects of the missing short spacings on the CO kinematics, we combined the ALMA and CARMA images using the `immerge` task in Miriad (cite: miriad)." (Observations and Data Reduction); "The resulting combined velocity fields show no significant differences with the ALMA-only velocity fields." (Observations and Data Reduction)
2. **Derive CO rotation curves and geometric parameters**: First-order harmonic decomposition in concentric rings fitting for Vrot, Vrad, and ΔVsys; adjust centre, Vsys, PA, and inclination by hand to minimize ΔVsys and Vrad and match harmonic shapes.

## Qualitative findings

- Massive dwarf galaxies in the sample (M* = 10^9.3-9.7 Msun) have shallow cusp-like inner dark matter density profiles (<β*> = 0.6 ± 0.1), steeper than lower-mass dwarf galaxies.  
  > "We find $\langle\beta^*\rangle = 0.6$ with a standard deviation of $\pm0.1$ among the galaxies in this sample, in agreement with previous measurements in this mass range." (Abstract); "We find that the relatively massive dwarf galaxies (M$_\star = 10^{9.3-9.7}$ M$_\odot$) in this study show cuspier dark matter distributions than lower mass dwarf galaxies, in agreement with other observations at similar M$_\star$ and M$_\star$/M$_{\rm halo}$(Figures [\[fig:kde\]](#fig:kde) and [\[fig:beta_obs_sim\]](#fig:beta_obs_sim))." (Summary)
- When measured between 1-2% of Rvir to match the definition used in simulations, the observed dark matter slopes (β_1-2%Rvir = 0.8-1.2) are steeper than predicted by the FIRE and NIHAO simulations.  
  > "When the same definition of the slope is used, we observe steeper slopes than predicted by the FIRE and NIHAO simulations." (Abstract)
- CO J=1-0 is dynamically cold in these dwarf galaxies (Vrot/σV > 4) and yields rotation curves and β* values in good agreement with Hα measurements.  
  > "The velocity dispersions are quite low and the galaxies have $\frac{\rm V_{rot}}{\sigma_{\rm V}} > 4$, which both indicate that the CO is dynamically cold in these galaxies." (Derivation of CO Rotation Curves); "Given the uncertainties, the $\beta^*$ measurements from CO and H $\alpha$ are in excellent agreement with one another." (CO v. H\alpha as tracers of the total potential)
- There are no clear trends between β* and galaxy parameters such as inclination, distance, Rmax, Rvir, Mdyn, or M* within this sample.  
  > "Across this sample of galaxies, we find no trends between $\beta^*$ and galaxy parameters such as inclination, distance, ${\rm R_{max}}$, R$_{\rm vir}$, ${\rm M_{dyn}}$, or M$_\star$." (Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Dwarf galaxies have larger dark matter fractions than higher-mass systems; ALMA C36-1 Band 3 CO(1-0) provides ~2'' (~200 pc) resolution matched to PCWI Hα data and traces the dynamically cold molecular gas; Spitzer 4.5 μm closely traces the stellar mass distribution.  
  > "Dwarf galaxies are excellent systems to investigate the dark matter density distribution, as they tend to have larger fractions of dark matter compared to higher mass systems." (Abstract); "This configuration results in $\sim 2\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim 200$ pc for the mean distance to these galaxies) resolution that is well matched to the H $\alpha$ data from the Palomar Cosmic Web Imager presented by ." (Observations and Data Reduction); "As discussed by and , the 4.5$\mu$m data are preferred since they more closely trace the stellar mass distribution than the $r$-band data." (Stellar mass and velocity profiles)
- Why this method: First-order harmonic decomposition accounts for radial/non-circular motions (Vrad) and systemic offsets; forward-modelling MCMC with a lookup table for PA and inclination accounts for degeneracies between geometric parameters and halo properties without expensive re-fitting at every step.  
  > "In galaxy centres, there may be non-circular motions due to bars and/or inflows, and the V$_{\rm rad}$ component is sensitive to these non-circular motions." (Derivation of CO Rotation Curves); "There are known degeneracies between the geometric parameters and properties of the dark matter halo (for example, between the halo mass and inclination; e.g., (cite: maller97; oguri05; read16)). We therefore include the PA and inclination as (nuisance) parameters to be fit in the MCMC." (Decomposing the dark matter component); "Rather than re-fitting the rotation curve at each step in the MCMC, which would be computationally expensive, we first generate a grid of rotation curves by varying the PA and inclination." (Decomposing the dark matter component)
- Alternative: Combining ALMA and CARMA cubes with Miriad immerge to recover short spacings (rejected because combined velocity fields showed no significant differences from ALMA-only velocity fields).  
  > "The resulting combined velocity fields show no significant differences with the ALMA-only velocity fields. We therefore proceed using only the ALMA data in our analysis." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Relaxing the maximal disk assumption for NGC 4701 where V* > VCO (tested, yielded β* = 0.1 +0.6/-0.5, consistent with maximal disk β* = 0.4 ± 0.8).  
  > "As a test, we relax the maximal disk assumption for NGC 4701 and re-fit $\beta^*$. Though we find a slightly shallower slope ($\beta^*$ $=~0.1^{+0.6}_{-0.5}$), it is consistent with the slope measured for a maximal disk ($\beta^*$ $=~0.4^{+0.8}_{-0.8}$) well within the uncertainties." (Measuring the inner slope of the dark matter density profile)
- Alternative: Two-parameter power-law dark matter density profile instead of gNFW (tested, rejected because gNFW profiles soften at larger radii and better represent the data).  
  > "In some galaxies, the second factor in the gNFW profile does become important, as the power-law profiles continue to increase whereas the gNFW profiles soften and better represent the data. Therefore, we proceed with the results using the gNFW profiles." (Assumed functional form of the dark matter density profile)
- How they decided it was enough: Images cleaned until residuals matched rms noise; 6 of 13 galaxies kept after excluding 6 for insufficient SNR and 1 (NGC 4632) for group environment/tidal interaction; MCMC run for 300 walkers x 300 steps with 100 steps burn-in after observing chains forgot initial conditions after 50 steps.  
  > "These images were cleaned until the residuals were consistent with the root-mean-square (rms) noise levels given in Table 1." (Observations and Data Reduction); "We therefore exclude this galaxy from the sample as the presence of other close, large galaxies and the hints of tidal interaction may affect the kinematics and dark matter halo of this system." (Observations and Data Reduction); "By examining the chains, the MCMC has forgotten the initial conditions after 50 steps. To be conservative, we discard the initial 100 steps." (Decomposing the dark matter component)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA observations filter out flux on scales larger than ~21 arcsec.  
  > "These ALMA observations cover antenna spacings from $15-640$ m and filters out some of the flux on scales larger than $\approx 21\ensuremath{''}$." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Beam smearing is not corrected and affects the central regions of the rotation curves (within twice the beam HWHM).  
  > "For this analysis, we do not correct for beam smearing." (Derivation of CO Rotation Curves)
- Limitation (authors): Pixels and rings in the CO and stellar rotation curves are not perfectly independent due to the PSF/beam, though the decomposition assumes independent Gaussian errors.  
  > "Therefore, even though the rings used to derive the rotation curves do not overlap, pixels in those rings are not perfectly independent." (Correlations between points and uncertainties in the CO rotation curves)
- Limitation (authors): Stars are assumed to be in a thin disk rather than a more realistic oblate spheroid.  
  > "We assume that the stars are in a thin disk. In reality, the stars are likely in an oblate spheroid distribution." (Stellar mass and velocity profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Discretising PA and inclination on a 1-degree lookup grid introduces a small error in the MCMC.  
  > "We use the grid of rotation curves as a lookup table, choosing the CO rotation curve with the nearest PA and inclination to each MCMC trial, acknowledging that there is some (small) error introduced by discretising the PA and inclination in this way." (Decomposing the dark matter component)
- Limitation (authors): Galaxy distance uncertainties are >20 per cent, and uncertainties on extrapolated Rvir, M200, and c200 are large.  
  > "As shown in Table [\[tab:geomparams\]](#tab:geomparams), the uncertainties on the distances to these galaxies are $>20$ per cent." (Measuring the inner slope of the dark matter density profile)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size is small (6 galaxies) and sensitivity limits how far out in radius the CO rotation curves can be traced (Rmax = 1.5-3.3 kpc).  
  > "In comparing these distributions, it is important to keep in mind that our sample only includes six galaxies, whereas have 18 galaxies." (Results); "Analyses such as these are often limited by the sensitivity needed to probe out to large radii in faint, often gas-poor dwarf galaxies." (Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 2D harmonic decomposition on velocity fields without forward 3D cube modelling (e.g., 3DBarolo) does not account for beam smearing, which could systematically flatten inner rotation curves, especially in highly inclined galaxies like NGC 1035 (i = 79 deg).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating gNFW profiles fitted only within Rmax = 1.5-3.3 kpc out to Rvir ~ 64-90 kpc to determine R200, M200, and 1-2% Rvir involves a massive radial extrapolation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adjusting kinematic centre, Vsys, PA, and inclination 'by hand' introduces subjective human choices prior to the MCMC step.
