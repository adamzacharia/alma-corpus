## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate the ALMA data**: ALMA Science Pipeline calibration of all data sets  
  > "All data sets were calibrated using the ALMA Science Pipeline which includes Water Vapor Radiometer and system temperature correction, as well as bandpass, phase, and amplitude calibration." (2.2 Data analysis)
   - software: ALMA Science Pipeline
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **image and self-calibrate the 1.3 mm continuum**: TCLEAN of the high-resolution continuum spectral windows with manual masks, then two phase-only self-calibration iterations per epoch  
  > "Continuum images were produced using the continuum spectral windows of the high resolution data only" (2.2 Data analysis); "Two iterations of phase-only self-calibration were conducted on each epoch separately using a mask covering both sources." (2.2 Data analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "with Briggs weighting and robust parameter of 0.5." (2.2 Data analysis)
   - parameter: Final beam 0.02'' x 0.03'' centred at 225 GHz  
  > "yielding an image with synthesized beam of 0.02$''$ $\times$ 0.03$''$ centered at 225 GHz." (2.2 Data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-calibration raised the image S/N by a factor of 1.7  
  > "Self calibration improved the signal-to-noise ratio of the final image by a factor of 1.7." (2.2 Data analysis)
3. **build the 12CO line cubes**: Recentre epochs on the secondary disk, apply continuum self-cal solutions, UVCONTSUB, then combine the 0.02'', 0.2'' and 1.1'' datasets and TCLEAN with auto-masking and a uvtaper to make 1.5 km/s cubes  
  > "Self-calibration of the line data from all epochs and resolutions was done by applying the self-calibration solutions derived from the respective continuum datasets." (2.2 Data analysis); "We used the UVCONTSUB task to substract the continuum from the line data" (2.2 Data analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Auto-masking for regions up to 3 x rms, 1.5 km/s channels  
  > "after which the data was CLEANed using an auto-masking process (for regions up to 3$\times$rms) in TCLEAN to generate line cubes with 1.5 km s$^{-1}$ resolution." (2.2 Data analysis)
   - parameter: uvtaper uppertaper = 0.1arcsec  
  > "we applied an uvtaper with an uppertaper = 0.1arcsec." (2.2 Data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **estimate stellar masses of the two components**: Use primary M0 spectral type and 2MASS J-K to derive extinction, then BT-Settl models for mass and age of the primary; use the K-band flux ratio to get the secondary's absolute K magnitude, Teff and mass at the same age and extinction  
  > "then use the flux ratio in the K-band to estimate the mass of the secondary object assuming the same age and extinction." (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
   - parameter: A_K = 0.53 x [(J-K) - (J-K)_O]; result A_K = 1.3 mag, A_V = 14.5 mag  
  > "This approach gives A$_K$ = 1.3 mag and A$_V$ = 14.5 mag." (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
   - parameter: Distance 144 pc  
  > "a distance of 144 pc (cite: Gaia2018)" (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
   - assumption: Observed J-K colour is photospheric; same age and extinction for both components  
  > "We assume that the observed J-K color from 2MASS (cite: Cutri2003) is photospheric" (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors caution the masses and ages are model-dependent  
  > "stellar masses and ages of low-mass stars and brown dwarfs are model-dependent and notoriously difficult to estimate for individual objects" (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
5. **characterise the primary disk rings**: Search a grid of PA, i and centre for the most circular deprojected image, apply unsharp masking (Gaussian-smoothed copy subtracted), and extract radial and azimuthal profiles averaged over each ring  
  > "To perform the deprojection, we explored a grid of different $PA$, $i$, and centers." (3.2.1 Primary disk); "This sharper image is obtained by convolving the image with a circular Gaussian kernel to produce a smoothed version of the disk which is later subtracted from the original image." (3.2.1 Primary disk)
   - parameter: PA = 1.0 deg (E of N), i = 36.4 deg, centre from Gaia DR2 corrected for proper motion  
  > "We find that a $PA$ = 1.0 deg (E of N), and an $i$ = 36.4 deg, together with the coordinates from the Gaia Data Release 2" (3.2.1 Primary disk)
   - parameter: Ring radial ranges 0.3''-0.4'' (inner) and 0.4''-0.6'' (outer)  
  > "0.3$''$ – 0.4$''$ for the inner ring and 0.4$''$ – 0.6$''$ for the outer ring" (3.2.1 Primary disk)
   - assumption: The most circular deprojection indicates the right geometry; same basic assumptions as Cieza et al. 2019 for the inner disk dust mass limit  
  > "adopting the same basic assumptions as in (cite: Cieza2019)" (3.2.1 Primary disk)
   - fitting: Grid search for the most circular deprojected image (metric not stated)  
  > "provide the most circular deprojected image." (3.2.1 Primary disk)
   - validation: Deprojected image centre agrees with the Gaia position within about 15 mas; NACO contrast curve compared to rule out a companion at large separations  
  > "This suggests that the primary star is likely to be located at the center of the disk to within $\sim$15 mas or 2 au)." (3.2.1 Primary disk)
6. **measure the size and cavity of the secondary disk**: IMFIT for FWHM size and position; subtract the primary's CLEAN-model visibilities, shift the phase centre to the secondary, deproject the visibilities and read the first null of the real part (Bessel function) to get the cavity radius  
  > "(FWHM measured with the IMFIT routine within CASA)" (3.2.2 Secondary disk); "We also changed the phase center to the position of the secondary as measured with IMFIT in CASA" (3.2.2 Secondary disk)
   - software: CASA, IMFIT
   - parameter: i = 10 deg, PA = 122 deg for the secondary disk  
  > "The resulting visibility profile corresponding to an $i$ = 10 deg and $PA$ = 122 deg is shown in Fig. 3." (3.2.2 Secondary disk)
   - parameter: Null at 5000 +/- 500 klambda gives R_cav = 2.2 +/- 0.25 au  
  > "A null is seen at 5000 $\pm$ 500 k$\lambda$, which according to Equation 1 corresponds to cavity radius of 2.2$\pm$ 0.25 au." (3.2.2 Secondary disk)
   - assumption: Face-on narrow ring model: real visibility is a zeroth-order Bessel function and the imaginary part is zero  
  > "the Real component of the visibilities as a function of deprojected $u-v$ distance for a face-on narrow ring is a zeroth-order Bessel function" (3.2.2 Secondary disk)
   - fitting: i and PA refined by minimising the imaginary component of the visibility profile; null location read from the profile (no sampler or priors stated)  
  > "More precise values were obtained by minimizing the imaginary components in the imaginary part of the visibility profile." (3.2.2 Secondary disk)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **inspect the 12CO gas kinematics and morphology**: Position-velocity diagram, channel map and intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment-1) map of the 12CO cube  
  > "As shown in the Position-Velocity (P-V) diagram (panel b)" (3.3 Molecular gas data); "we show the intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment-1) map of the $^{12}$CO data." (3.3 Molecular gas data)
   - parameter: Bridge channel map spans 0.25 to 1.75 km/s  
  > "This bridge becomes even more evident in the channel map containing emission between 0.25 and 1.75 km/s (panel c)." (3.3 Molecular gas data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Moment-1 map consistent with Keplerian motion and kinematic centre coincides with the expected stellar location from Gaia  
  > "This map is consistent with Keplerian motion as the extreme velocities are observed along the major axis of the disk." (3.3 Molecular gas data)

## Qualitative findings

- The primary disk shows two closely packed rings with strong azimuthal asymmetries; the outer ring peaks to the south and the inner ring peaks to the north-west  
  > "The outermost structure peaks toward South of the star and presents a prominent decrement to the North-West." (3.2.1 Primary disk); "the innermost structure peaks toward the North-West and becomes nearly undetectable toward the South-East." (3.2.1 Primary disk)
- No continuum emission is detected near the centre of the primary cavity  
  > "We do not detect emission near the center of the cavity" (3.2.1 Primary disk)
- The 12CO emission shows no inner gas cavity in the primary disk and a possible bridge of gas between the two disks  
  > "We therefore conclude that there is no indication of an inner cavity in the gas" (3.3 Molecular gas data); "the P-V diagram also shows what seems to be a bridge of gas connecting both disks." (3.3 Molecular gas data)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ISO-Oph 2 is one of the 10 brightest ODISEA targets not covered by DSHARP, chosen for high-resolution follow-up  
  > "ISO-Oph 2 was observed with ALMA as part of the high-resolution follow-up of the 10 brightest ODISEA targets that were not included in the ALMA Cycle-4 Large Program DSHARP" (Introduction)
- Why this method: A visibility-plane analytic cavity estimate was used because the secondary disk is too small and faint to measure in the image; unsharp masking was used to accentuate the narrow rings; the uvtaper balances resolution and sensitivity  
  > "we estimate the size of the dust cavity adopting an analytical approach based on deprojecting the visibilities to 0.0 deg $i$ and 0.0 deg $PA$ in the $u-v$ plane." (3.2.2 Secondary disk); "which accentuates the narrow rings." (3.2.1 Primary disk); "To obtain an optimal balance between spatial resolution and sensitivity, we applied an uvtaper with an uppertaper = 0.1arcsec." (2.2 Data analysis)
- Alternative: An unseen planet-like disturber in the cavity versus a recent flyby of the secondary as the origin of the ring asymmetries; neither is tested  
  > "While the morphology of the rings around the primary might be due to an unseen disturber within the cavity," (abstract)
- Alternative: A close equal-brightness companion at the NACO resolution limit and a 0.08 Msun brown dwarf beyond 50 au are ruled out  
  > "it allow us to rule out the presence of an equal brightness binary at the resolution limit of the observations 0.05$''$ (7 au)" (3.2.1 Primary disk)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stellar and substellar masses and ages are model-dependent and uncertain for individual objects  
  > "stellar masses and ages of low-mass stars and brown dwarfs are model-dependent and notoriously difficult to estimate for individual objects" (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
- Limitation (authors): K-band excess cannot be ruled out for either component, which affects the extinction and mass estimates  
  > "the presence of K-band excess can not be ruled out for neither the A or B component in the system" (3.1 (Sub)stellar masses)
- Limitation (authors): Gaia DR2 gives proper motions only for the primary, so the flyby scenario cannot be tested  
  > "Unfortunately the Gaia Data Release 2 only provides proper motions for the primary star" (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): The secondary disk's small flux and size push the limit of ALMA capabilities, so its cavity is only marginally resolved  
  > "exhibits the double peak (Fig. 1, panel b) characteristic of a transition disk with a marginally resolved inner dust cavity." (3.2.2 Secondary disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 2.2 au cavity rests on a single visibility null at 5000 +/- 500 klambda for a marginally resolved disk, with inclination and PA fixed by minimising imaginary components and no model comparison or uncertainty on those angles
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The secondary mass (0.08 Msun) depends on one K-band flux ratio, assumed identical age and extinction, and no K-band excess; a small change moves it across the brown dwarf limit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 12CO bridge is seen at 1.5 km/s resolution with cloud contamination at 2-3 km/s and no quantified significance or kinematic modelling
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Phase centring of epochs on the secondary and the combination of three array configurations for CO are not documented well enough to judge artefacts
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The flyby interpretation is speculative; no hydrodynamic modelling or relative proper motion is available
