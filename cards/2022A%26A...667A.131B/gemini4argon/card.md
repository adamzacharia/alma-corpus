## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce ALMA Band 3 data and generate moment 0 maps of HCN(1-0) and HCO+(1-0)**: Initial reduction and imaging with uniform weighting in CASA, RMS calculation in a line-free channel towards the galaxy centre, and velocity integration to create moment 0 maps  
  > "Initial reduction of the data was carried out using the ALMA reduction package `CASA` (cite: CasaMcmullin), and then exported to PYTHON for plotting, making use of the `Matplotlib and Astropy`." (Data); "The 1 $\sigma$ threshold was determined in `CASA` by calculating the rms of the signal over an area towards the centre of the galaxy in a channel with no line detection." (Data); "Figures [1](#fig:HCO$^+$) and [2](#fig:HCN) show the velocity integrated maps at the original spatial resolution for HCO$^+$(1-0) and HCN(1-0), respectively." (Moment 0 maps)
   - software: CASA, PYTHON, Matplotlib, Astropy
   - parameter: uniform weighting, beam ~0.7" x 0.5" at PA -86 deg, conversion factor 32 K Jy^-1 beam  
  > "The angular resolution obtained using uniform weighting was $\sim0.7" \times 0.5"$ at a position angle of $-86\degree$ in the data cube. The conversion factor between Jy beam$^{-1}$ and K is 32 K Jy$^{-1}$ beam." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 3 sigma detection threshold applied for reliable intensity ratios  
  > "It must be noted, however, that for region NSB-c, both HCN(4-3) and HCO$^+$(4-3) emission is below the 3 $\sigma$ threshold necessary to derive a reliable intensity ratio." (HCN(4-3)/HCO+(4-3))
2. **Group multi-instrument observations by spatial resolution and sub-region, Correct for beam dilution, and compute molecular line ratios**: Group archival and new observations by spatial resolution (~40 pc high-res, >=100 pc low-res) and sub-region; deconvolve intensities using a beam-dilution scaling equation where the lowest resolution in the group defines the source size; compute intensity ratios HCN(1-0)/HCO+(1-0), HCN(4-3)/HCO+(4-3), HCN(4-3)/CS(7-6), and HCN(4-3)/CS(2-1)  
  > "Firstly, the archival data were grouped by spatial resolution. Within these groupings, there were variations of resolution of up to a factor of two. Therefore, we deconvolved the intensities by assuming the observation with the lowest resolution was the source size" (Comparison between observations); "I_{Source}=\left(\frac{(\theta_{s}^2+\theta_{b}^2)}{\theta_{b}^2}\right)I_{Beam}," (Comparison between observations)
   - parameter: High-resolution group (~40 pc), low-resolution group (>=100 pc and ~1000 pc), 10 sub-regions across CND and SB ring  
  > "High-resolution ($\sim$40 pc) HCN(1-0)/HCO$^+$(1-0)" (Table 2); "Low-resolution ($\gtrsim$100 pc) HCN(4-3)/CS(2-1)" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Source size theta_s is equal to the lowest-resolution observation in each spatial resolution category  
  > "where $I_{Source}$ is the source averaged peak intensity, $\theta_{s}$ is the source size (as defined by the lowest-resolution observation in the category), $\theta_{b}$ is the beam size, and $I_{Beam}$ is the original intensity" (Comparison between observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Calculate LTE column density bounds for HCN, HCO+, and CS**: Calculate upper-state column density N_u and total molecular column density N independently for each transition using LTE equations and CDMS partition functions to establish minimum and maximum column density limits for non-LTE grids  
  > "Using the observed line intensities from the archival data, we were able to estimate the column densities by assuming LTE and by using the same groupings of observations as discussed earlier, devolved to the lowest resolution of that group." (LTE analysis); "To compute the partition function, we made use of molecular data from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS) (cite: CDMS)." (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: T = 50 K to 400 K  
  > "We used temperatures in the range 50 K to 400 K and computed the column density from each transition independently" (LTE analysis)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Using the observed line intensities from the archival data, we were able to estimate the column densities by assuming LTE" (LTE analysis)
   - assumption: Beam filling factor Delta Omega_a / Delta Omega_s = 1 (source completely fills the beam)  
  > "Since we are investigating the regions themselves, we made the assumption that the source completely fills the beam, such that $\frac{\Delta\Omega_a}{\Delta\Omega_s}=1$." (LTE analysis)
   - assumption: Optically thin limit tau = 0  
  > "We set $\tau = 0$ for our lines, following the example of (cite: Krips_2008)." (LTE analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Perform non-LTE radiative transfer modelling and MCMC parameter estimation**: Run a grid of LVG non-LTE radiative transfer models using RADEX via SpectralRadex with LAMDA collisional data, compute modelled line ratios, and sample the chi^2 distribution using MCMC to infer posterior distributions of gas density, kinetic temperature, linewidth, column densities, and relative column density ratios  
  > "We used `RADEX` for this task via the `SpectralRadex` python package[^1]." (Non-LTE analysis); "We then fit these modelled ratios to their observed values by MCMC sampling the $\chi^2$ distribution of the parameters." (Non-LTE analysis); "we computed the posterior probability distributions of the ratios of the column densities. We did this by taking the ratio of the chains of column density samples for each region and resolution." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - software: RADEX, SpectralRadex
   - parameter: n_H2 = 10^3 to 10^7 cm^-3 (50 discrete values); T_K = 50 to 400 K (50 discrete values); N_Mol = N_min to N_max (50 discrete values); Delta v = 50 to 150 km s^-1 (3 discrete values)  
  > "$n_{H_2}$ (cm$^{-3}$) | $10^3$ | $10^7$ | 50" (Table 4); "$T_K$ (K) | 50 | 400 | 50" (Table 4); "$\Delta v$ (km s$^-1$) | 50 | 150 | 3" (Table 4)
   - assumption: Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) escape probability approximation with uniform spherical geometry for sub-regions  
  > "`RADEX` is a non-LTE radiative transfer code developed by (cite: Van_der_Tak_2007), which uses the escape probability method (cite: 1960_Sobolev). We assumed the sub-regions could be approximated as uniform spheres" (Non-LTE analysis)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling of chi^2 = sum_{i=1}^4 (R_o - R_m)/sigma^2 with uniform priors, 12 walkers, 100000 steps per walker, for regions/resolutions with at least 2 observed ratios  
  > "Assuming a uniform prior, sampling this distribution gives the posterior probability distribution of the parameters." (Non-LTE analysis); "Our MCMC sampler uses 12 walkers, each performing 100000 steps, to sample possible parameter combinations. We did this for all combinations of regions and resolutions with at least two of the investigated ratios." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - validation: Evaluated posterior 16th and 84th percentile confidence intervals across 40 pc and 100 pc resolution datasets  
  > "The resulting 16th and 84th percentile limits of the three ratio distributions are shown in Figure [11](#fig:grid_Abundance_ratios)." (Non-LTE analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN(1-0)/HCO+(1-0) and HCN(4-3)/CS(7-6) show overlap between AGN-dominated CND and starburst-dominated SB ring regions across spatial scales, making them insufficient as unique diagnostics.  
  > "For the HCN(1-0)/HCO$^+$(1-0) and HCN(4-3)/CS(7-6) ratios, we observe an overlap in the observed ranges of these ratios between AGN-dominated and SB-dominated regions." (Summary and conclusion)
- HCN(4-3)/HCO+(4-3) separates CND and SB regions at both high (~40 pc) and low (>=100 pc) resolutions, while HCN(4-3)/CS(2-1) reliably separates them only at high spatial resolution (< 50 pc).  
  > "We propose that at high spatial resolution (\< 50 pc) the HCN(4-3)/CS(2-1) is a reliable tracer of AGN activity." (abstract); "HCN(4-3)/HCO$^+$(4-3), on the other hand, seems to be distinctively higher in all the regions closer to the AGN position, at least at higher spatial resolutions. This distinction is even maintained when the ratio is viewed at lower-resolution scales, although the separation does decrease." (Summary and conclusion)
- Variations in molecular line ratios between CND and SB regions are driven by differences in relative chemical abundances (HCN relative to HCO+ and CS) rather than differences in gas density or kinetic temperature.  
  > "We also find that the variations in ratios are not a consequence of different densities or temperature but of different fractional abundances, yielding to the important result that it is essential to consider the chemical processes at play when drawing conclusions from radiative transfer calculations." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 1068 is a nearby (~14 Mpc), bright archetypal composite AGN/SB galaxy with extensive multi-resolution archival observations of dense gas tracers HCN, HCO+, and CS from parsec to kiloparsec scales.  
  > "NGC 1068 is a Seyfert 2 barred spiral galaxy; it is also the archetypal composite AGN/SB galaxy. Its close proximity ($\sim$14 Mpc) and significant brightness ($L_{Bol}$ $\approx$ 2.5 – 3.0 $10^{11}L_{\odot}$ ) (cite: Bland-Hawthorn_1997; Bock_2000) enable studies at many different resolutions, down to parsec scales" (Introduction); "Despite the plethora of available published data focused on NGC 1068, within this study we focus on the dense gas tracers: HCN, HCO$^+$, and CS. This focus is in an effort to validate previously proposed ratios and diagrams rather than to suggest new ones." (Data)
- Why this method: Grouping data by spatial resolution with beam-dilution deconvolution prevents mixing disparate scales, while non-LTE RADEX modelling with MCMC sampling separates physical excitation effects (temperature, density) from chemical abundance variations.  
  > "The different spatial resolutions make it necessary to ensure that only similarly obtained detections are compared. **This is especially true for the higher-resolution observations that cover only a small area of the galaxy.**" (Comparison between observations); "To achieve this, we used radiative transfer modelling to infer the gas conditions and molecular column densities at each location for each resolution. We were then able to determine whether there are physical differences in excitation conditions between locations, differences in the underlying chemical abundances, or even a combination of the two" (Physical and chemical properties of NGC 1068)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Not all sub-regions have been observed at all spatial resolutions for all molecules; NSB-b lacks consistent resolution observations and NSB-c has only a single high-resolution ratio (with HCN(4-3) and HCO+(4-3) below 3 sigma), excluding NSB-c from RADEX fitting.  
  > "We note that not all sub-regions listed in Table [1](#tab:Regions), and shown in Figures [1](#fig:HCO$^+$) and [2](#fig:HCN), have been observed at all resolutions for all molecules." (Comparison between observations); "The region NSB-c possesses fewer observed ratios than the other regions, with just a single high-resolution observation (see Table [2](#tab:Ratios)). As a result it is not included in the `RADEX` fitting discussed later in this paper." (Comparison between observations)
- Limitation (authors): Gas kinetic temperature, gas density (except in E Knot, W Knot, and NSB-a at 40 pc), and linewidth are poorly constrained by the MCMC RADEX fitting across resolutions.  
  > "Neither the CND nor the SB ring regions can be reasonably constrained in the temperature or density planes, at least with regards to showing distinguishing qualities within these parameters." (Non-LTE analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Follow-up studies of other nearby galaxies are required to confirm whether HCN(4-3)/HCO+(4-3) and HCN(4-3)/CS(2-1) hold as reliable diagnostics beyond NGC 1068.  
  > "whereas HCN(4-3)/HCO+(4-3) HCN(4-3)/CS(2-1) may show promise at certain resolutions but follow up studies, especially of other nearby galaxies, is required in order to confirm our findings." (Summary and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitting 6 free parameters (n_H2, T_K, Delta v, N_HCN, N_HCO+, N_CS) to at most 4 observed line ratios per region leaves the system underconstrained, explaining the wide posterior uncertainties in temperature and density.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a single uniform sphere with identical T_K, n_H2, and linewidth for transitions with very different critical densities and upper-state energies (e.g., CS(2-1) vs HCN(4-3)) ignores multi-phase gas structure along the line of sight.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deconvolving interferometric intensities using a simple Gaussian beam-dilution formula rather than uv-tapering or smoothing visibilities to a common beam can introduce systematic errors due to spatial filtering and clumpy emission morphology.
