## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract additional line fluxes and upper limits**: Circular aperture extraction centred on continuum peaks with manual radius and channel selection for detections (>5 sigma), and Gaussian fitting with fixed velocity width and aperture from detected lines for non-detections  
  > "Briefly, for the detected lines, circular apertures were centred on the peaks of the corresponding continuum emission, and the radii of the apertures were manually adjusted for each source in each image to include as much line emission as possible while still measuring that emission at higher than the $5\sigma$ level." (Line fluxes); "For non-detected lines, the same aperture and line velocity width as the detected lines were used. We then fit a Gaussian profile to the frequency of the undetected spectral lines with a fixed velocity width." (Line fluxes)
   - parameter: Detection threshold > 5 sigma; upper limits at 3 sigma  
  > "Figure 1 shows an overview of all the detected spectral lines and the $3\sigma$ upper-limits of undetected lines of all sources." (Line fluxes)
   - assumption: No correction for CMB effects on line fluxes  
  > "We do not correct for cosmic microwave background (CMB) effects (i.e., the observational contrast against the CMB and the enhanced excitation of the CO or other line transitions)" (Line fluxes)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fit with fixed velocity width for non-detected lines  
  > "We then fit a Gaussian profile to the frequency of the undetected spectral lines with a fixed velocity width." (Line fluxes)
   - validation: Apertures set several times larger than beam size to remove bias across SNR or beam sizes  
  > "These apertures were several times larger than the beam size, removing potential bias when comparing lines with different signal-to-noise ratios or with different beam sizes." (Line fluxes)
2. **Calculate CO SLEDs, line luminosities,infrared luminosities, SFRs, and line scaling relations**: Calculate line luminosities L' and L from integrated fluxes, derive L_IR from 151 GHz continuum using a single-temperature modified blackbody with CMB corrections, convert L_IR to SFR, and fit linear scaling relations using Orthogonal Distance Regression  
  > "We calculate the line luminosity for the spectral lines using the equation from (cite: solomon1997): $$\begin{equation} L^\prime =3.25\times10^7 S_{\rm CO}\Delta v f_{\rm obs}^{-2} D_{\rm L}^2(1+z)^{-3}~[\mathrm{K~km~s^{-1}~pc^2}]. \label{eq:lprime} \end{equation}$$" (CO spectral line energy distributions); "The infrared luminosity is calculated assuming a modified black body with an average dust temperature of $35~\mathrm{K}$ and a dust-emissivity index ($\beta$) of 2." (Spectral line comparison); "In order to estimate the scaling relations accounting for errors in both the line and infrared luminosity, we use a linear regression fitting technique of SciPy (cite: Scipy2020) ODR (Orthogonal Distance Regression) package" (Spectral line comparison)
   - software: SciPy ODR (ODRPACK)
   - parameter: T_dust = 35 K, beta = 2, nu_obs = 151 GHz  
  > "The infrared luminosity is calculated assuming a modified black body with an average dust temperature of $35~\mathrm{K}$ and a dust-emissivity index ($\beta$) of 2." (Spectral line comparison)
   - assumption: Flat LambdaCDM cosmology with Omega_m = 0.307, Omega_Lambda = 0.693, h = 0.693  
  > "Throughout this paper, we adopted a spatially flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with the best-fit parameters derived from the Planck results (cite: Planck2015), which are $\Omega_\mathrm{m} = 0.307$, $\Omega_\mathrm{\Lambda} = 0.693$ and $h = 0.693$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: SFR [M_sun yr^-1] = 1.47 x 10^-10 L_IR [L_sun]  
  > "Throughout this paper, we calculate the SFR assuming the (cite: Kennicutt2012) relation of $\mathrm{SFR~[M_\odot~yr^{-1}]}= 1.47 \times{} 10^{-10} L_\mathrm{IR}~[\mathrm{L_\odot}]$." (Spectral line comparison)
   - assumption: CMB dust heating and contrast corrected following da Cunha et al. (2013)  
  > "We account for the CMB using the appropriate equations from (cite: daCunha2013), which correct both for the dust emission in contrast to the CMB, as well as additional dust heating by the CMB." (Spectral line comparison)
   - fitting: Linear regression via Orthogonal Distance Regression (ODR) in log-log space  
  > "In order to estimate the scaling relations accounting for errors in both the line and infrared luminosity, we use a linear regression fitting technique of SciPy (cite: Scipy2020) ODR (Orthogonal Distance Regression) package" (Spectral line comparison)
   - validation: Validated single-component 151 GHz L_IR estimates against full SED luminosities from Bakx et al. (2020) and Bendo et al. (2023)  
  > "For single-component sources, we validate our method against the luminosities from (cite: Bakx2020Erratum) and (cite: Bendo2023), and find similar infrared luminosities within the typical uncertainty of the 151 GHz flux density." (Spectral line comparison)
3. **Measure continuum source sizes and derive dynamical masses**: Fit 2D Gaussians to Band 4 continuum images using CASA IMFIT to obtain deconvolved major and minor axes, compute effective radius r = sqrt(theta_maj * theta_min), and calculate virial and rotational dynamical masses from CO line FWHM and r  
  > "We calculate the effective radius from the Band 4 continuum image using the CASA tool IMFIT." (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies); "IMFIT returns major axis ($\theta_\mathrm{maj}$) and minor axis ($\theta_\mathrm{min}$) and we use the radius as $r=\sqrt{\theta_\mathrm{maj}\theta_\mathrm{min}}$, which turn the surface area into $\pi r^2$." (Size estimation)
   - software: CASA (IMFIT)
   - parameter: Minimum size set to 0.14 arcsec for 21 unresolved sources; inclination angle i = 55 deg for rotational mass  
  > "For these 21 galaxies, we set their sizes to $0.14~\mathrm{arcsec}$, the lower end of the reliable IMFIT size estimates." (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies); "$v_{\rm circ}$ is the circular velocity (using $v_{\rm circ} = 0.75 \Delta V_\mathrm{CO}/\sin i$, with the inclination angle set as the average of $i = 55 \deg$ following (cite: Wang2013))" (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies)
   - assumption: Systems are unlensed, self-gravitating, and dynamically relaxed; continuum size equals CO emitting size  
  > "We compare the effective radii and velocities with fixed-mass solutions to the dynamical mass equations, on the simplest assumption that the systems are unlensed." (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies); "A critical underlying assumption is that the systems are self-gravitating and relaxed, as discussed in (cite: Dunne2022)." (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies)
   - fitting: 2D image-plane Gaussian deconvolution via CASA IMFIT  
  > "Table 3 shows the deconvolved sizes obtained from CASA task IMFIT to Band 4 continuum." (Size estimation)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive molecular gas masses, dust masses, dust-to-gas ratios, and Schmidt-Kennicutt relation**: Convert [CI] (1-0) or CO line luminosities (scaled to CO 1-0 via Harrington et al. 2021 ratios) to M_H2 using alpha_[CI] and alpha_CO, compute surface densities using IMFIT continuum areas, and calculate M_dust from 151 GHz flux density  
  > "When available, we use the [C I] ($^3P_1$–$^3P_0$) line to calculate the molecular gas mass, otherwise we use the mean line luminosity ratios obtained from the turbulence model in (cite: Harrington2021) to calculate the CO (1–0) line luminosity" (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources); "We use the 151 GHz flux density from (cite: Bendo2023), and convert this to a dust mass assuming $S_{\nu} = \kappa_{\nu} B_{\nu}(T = 35~{\rm K}) M_{\rm dust} D_\mathrm{L}^{-2}$" (Dust-to-gas mass ratio)
   - parameter: r_2,1 = 0.88+-0.07, r_3,1 = 0.69+-0.12, r_4,1 = 0.52+-0.14, r_5,1 = 0.37+-0.15  
  > "specifically $r_{2,1} = 0.88\pm0.07$, $r_{3,1} = 0.69\pm0.12$, $r_{4,1} = 0.52\pm0.14$, $r_{5,1} = 0.37\pm0.15$." (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources)
   - assumption: alpha_[CI] = 17.0 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1, alpha_CO = 4.0 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 (including 1.36 helium factor)  
  > "$$\begin{eqnarray} \alpha_{\rm [CI]} &=& 17.0~\left[\mathrm{M}_\odot~\left(\mathrm{K~km~s^{-1}~pc^2}\right)^{-1}\right] \\ \alpha_{\rm CO} &=& 4.0~\left[\mathrm{M}_\odot~\left(\mathrm{K~km~s^{-1}~pc^2}\right)^{-1}\right]. \end{eqnarray}$$" (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources); "The $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 4.0~\mathrm{M}_{\odot}~(\mathrm{K~km~s}^{-1}~\mathrm{pc}^2)^{-1}$ value includes the additional helium correction (see (cite: Bolatto2013) for a review) of 1.36." (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources)
   - assumption: T_dust = 35 K, beta = 2, kappa_nu = kappa_* (nu/nu_*)^beta with kappa_* = 10.41 cm^2 g^-1 at nu_* = 1900 GHz  
  > "Here we assume a $\beta = 2$, and approximate the dust mass absorption coefficient ($\kappa_{\rm \nu}$) as $\kappa_{\rm \star} \left( \nu / \nu_{\star}\right)^{\beta}$, with ($\kappa_{\rm \star}, \nu_{\star})$ as (10.41 cm$^2~$g$^{-1}$, 1900 GHz) from (cite: Draine03)." (Dust-to-gas mass ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Sanity check comparing molecular gas mass estimates derived from [CI] and CO  
  > "As a sanity check, our sources also have a good agreement in their estimates of the molecular gas mass between [C I] and CO." (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources)
5. **Constrain photo-dissociation region (PDR) gas density and FUV radiation field**: Compare observed [CI](1-0)/CO(4-3) and [CI](2-1)/CO(7-6) vs [CI]/L_IR luminosity ratios against 1D plane-parallel PDR model tracks using PDRToolbox  
  > "Figure 9 shows the luminosity ratio of [C i] ($^3P_1$–$^3P_0$) to CO (4–3) (left panel) and [C i] ($^3P_2$–$^3P_1$) to CO (7–6) (right panel) against their respective [C i] luminosity over infrared luminosity." (Photo-dissociation regions inside DSFGs); "We investigate the differences in PDR conditions among our and reference samples using PDRToolbox (cite: Kaufman2006; Pound2008; Pound2011), which provides the line intensities for each combination of hydrogen density ($n_\mathrm{H}$) and far-ultraviolet (FUV) radiation intensity fields" (Photo-dissociation regions inside DSFGs)
   - software: PDRToolbox (PDRT)
   - assumption: 1D plane-parallel geometry with three discrete layers (Kaufman et al. 1999) without cosmic ray dissociation  
  > "We use the PDR model from (cite: Kaufman1999) that supposes the simple 1D geometry assuming three discrete layers, i.e., the [C I] emission comes only from the thin layer between two gas components traced by CO and singly-ionized carbon emission ([C II])." (Photo-dissociation regions inside DSFGs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Investigate super-thermalized CO line ratios in four companion sources (BEARS CUBS)**: Calculate r_{Ja,Jb} luminosity ratios, inspect Band 3 and 4 spectra extracted with matched apertures, check continuum ratios and off-source cube RMS, and compare against RADEX radiative transfer models and analytical differential lensing models  
  > "Figure 12 shows the Bands 3 (left column) and 4 (right column) spectra of these four sources extracted with the same aperture size." (Companion sources with Unusually-Bright lineS: The BEARS CUBS); "Basic RADEX (cite: vanderTak2007; ndRADEX) models suggest that high hydrogen density ($> 10^{4}~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$) and high gas temperature ($T_{\rm kin} > 100~\mathrm{K}$) can indeed reproduce such super-thermalized luminosity ratios until $\sim1.7$" (Physical interpretation)
   - software: RADEX, ndRADEX
   - parameter: n_H > 10^4 cm^-3, T_kin > 100 K  
  > "Basic RADEX (cite: vanderTak2007; ndRADEX) models suggest that high hydrogen density ($> 10^{4}~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$) and high gas temperature ($T_{\rm kin} > 100~\mathrm{K}$) can indeed reproduce such super-thermalized luminosity ratios until $\sim1.7$" (Physical interpretation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked Band 3 and 4 continuum ratios, companion A-source SLEDs in the same field/scheduling block, and off-source RMS vs frequency (<15% variation)  
  > "We do not find any significant ($< 15$ per cent) variation in the noise at the position of the spectral lines." 7. **Construct rest-frame composite spectrum and measure stacked line fluxes**: Automatically extract spectra from cubes using an aperture of twice the beam radius, subtract a power-law continuum masking +-1500 km/s around lines, scale each spectrum to z_common = 2.5 and L_IR = 3e13 L_sun, stack with inverse-variance weighting, and extract fluxes via Gaussian fitting and 600 km/s bin integration  
  > "We find the best extraction aperture to lie at twice the radius of the beam." (Method); "We subtract the continuum emission directly from the spectra assuming a power-law dust continuum emission ($S_{\nu} = \rm A_i \nu^{(2 + \beta)}$), with $\beta_{\rm dust} = 2$. We separately fit the spectra from Bands 3 and 4, while masking out the data within $1500~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}}$ around the CO, [C i] and H$_2$O lines." (Method); "The stacked spectrum is created at several velocity resolutions by adding the rest-frequency spectra of each source, corrected for luminosity and redshift. Each spectrum is additionally weighted by the inverse variance in each channel." (Method)
   - parameter: Aperture radius = 2 x beam radius; flux boost correction = 8% (1/0.92) with 6% extra noise in quadrature; z_common = 2.5; L_IR,norm = 3e13 L_sun; continuum mask = +-1500 km/s; optimal bin width = 600 km/s  
  > "We correct the stacked spectrum by boosting the flux by 8% ($= 1/0.92$), and accounting for 6% extra noise in the extracted values, added in quadrature." (Method); "We decide to stack the spectrum of each galaxy with the goal of representing a single archetypal galaxy at $z = 2.5$ (approximately the mean of this sample; (cite: Urquhart2022)) with an infrared luminosity of $3 \times{} 10^{13}~\mathrm{L}_{\odot}$." (Method); "The highest SNR of the velocity-integrated flux is found at a bin-size of $600~\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$." (Results)
   - assumption: Power-law dust continuum S_nu = A_i nu^(2+beta) with beta_dust = 2  
  > "We subtract the continuum emission directly from the spectra assuming a power-law dust continuum emission ($S_{\nu} = \rm A_i \nu^{(2 + \beta)}$), with $\beta_{\rm dust} = 2$." (Method)
   - fitting: Single-Gaussian fitting to the 70 km s^-1 stacked spectrum and direct integration in 600 km s^-1 bins  
  > ": Line properties fitted to the $70~\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$ stacked spectrum." (Table 1); "The highest SNR of the velocity-integrated flux is found at a bin-size of $600~\mathrm{km~s}^{-1}$." (Results)
   - validation: Compared extracted continuum against Bendo et al. (2023) 151 GHz fluxes (11% scatter), and evaluated SNR in 5000 random off-line 600 km s^-1 bins confirming a unit-width Gaussian white noise distribution  
  > "On average, our flux estimates agree with the 151 GHz continuum fluxes, and we find a point-to-point standard deviation of $11$%, although the scatter decreases for the brightest sources (to around $5$%)." (Method); "We extract the signal-to-noise ratio in 5000 bins of $600~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}}$ width at random off-line frequencies." (Method); "The off-line stacked spectrum is thus well-described by a white noise spectrum, and importantly confirms there are no issues with coherent processes with our spectrum, such as imperfect continuum subtraction." (Method)

## Software

  > "In order to estimate the scaling relations accounting for errors in both the line and infrared luminosity, we use a linear regression fitting technique of SciPy (cite: Scipy2020) ODR (Orthogonal Distance Regression) package, which is an implementation of the Fortran ODRPACK package (cite: Boggs1992)." (Spectral line comparison)
- **CASA (IMFIT)** : Measuring deconvolved source sizes from Band 4 continuum images  
  > "We calculate the effective radius from the Band 4 continuum image using the CASA tool IMFIT." (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies)
- **PDRToolbox (PDRT)** : Modelling photo-dissociation region hydrogen density (n_H) and FUV radiation field (G_0) from [CI], CO, and IR luminosities  
  > "We investigate the differences in PDR conditions among our and reference samples using PDRToolbox (cite: Kaufman2006; Pound2008; Pound2011), which provides the line intensities for each combination of hydrogen density ($n_\mathrm{H}$) and far-ultraviolet (FUV) radiation intensity fields" (Photo-dissociation regions inside DSFGs)
- **RADEX / ndRADEX** : Modelling CO excitation conditions to test super-thermalized line ratios

## Qualitative findings

- Most BEARS galaxies have sub-thermalized CO SLEDs peaking around J_up = 4-6, consistent with Planck-selected DSFGs from Harrington et al. (2021).  
  > "A handful of sources already appear to peak at or lower than $J_{\rm up} = 3$, with the majority of sources showing a peak in their CO SLED between $J_{\rm up} = 4$–$6$ or beyond." (CO spectral line energy distributions)
- For the majority of BEARS galaxies, the estimated molecular gas mass (assuming alpha_CO = 4.0) exceeds the apparent virial dynamical mass by roughly a factor of 4, implying the systems are not dynamically relaxed or line widths do not probe the full potential.  
  > "Remarkably, the majority of our galaxies have a molecular gas mass above that of the dynamical mass. We find a scatter of half an order of magnitude around the 4:1 scaling relation" (Molecular gas mass); "An alternative reading of this apparently unphysical result in Fig. 7 is that the underlying assumption of the dynamical mass estimates is false, i.e., that the systems are not dynamically relaxed (cite: Dye2018)" (Molecular gas mass)
- Dust-to-gas ratios of BEARS galaxies show low scatter and no redshift evolution between z = 1.4 and 4.6, similar to Milky Way values.  
  > "Unlike absorber systems, BEARS and other SFGs (cite: Shapley2020) do not show any redshift evolution, in agreement with a metallicity close to the Milky Way (cite: Draine2007; Draine2014)." (Dust-to-gas mass ratio)
- No high-velocity outflow tails (>3 sigma) are detected in the stacked CO line profiles despite a combined SNR > 100 sigma.  
  > "However, for our sample, both single-Gaussian fitting (Table 1) and visual inspection of the stacked lines (Figure 4) do not show any large velocity components above $3\sigma$." (Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Southern hemisphere Herschel Bright Sources (HerBS) in the South Galactic Pole field lacked spectroscopic redshifts and multi-line ISM characterization prior to ALMA BEARS observations.  
  > "Therefore, (cite: Urquhart2022) and (cite: Bendo2023) presented the result of the Bright Extragalactic Atacama Large Millimetre/submillimetre Array (ALMA) Redshift Survey (BEARS). They selected the 85 sources from HerBS which are located in the South Galactic Pole field and previously lacked spectroscopic redshifts." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used single-band 151 GHz ALMA continuum to derive L_IR because 40 of 71 sources have multiplicity that blends Herschel/SCUBA-2 photometry; normalized stacked spectra to a common redshift and intrinsic luminosity to avoid redshift-dependent K-correction bias in the composite SLED.  
  > "We choose to derive the infrared luminosity using only the 151 GHz flux density from the Band 4 observations with ALMA, since all galaxies are identified through their 151 GHz flux density, and over half of all sources discussed in this paper (40 out of 71) have multiples that make direct comparison to the Herschel and SCUBA-2 photometry unreliable." (Spectral line comparison); "Unlike previous methods, we provide a stacked spectrum normalized against the intrinsic properties of the observed galaxies." (Method)
- Alternative: Using deblended Herschel and SCUBA-2 multi-wavelength SED fits for L_IR across the full sample was deferred because 40/71 sources are multiples requiring extensive deblending.  
  > "The scope of this paper is to provide a comprehensive study of the gas properties of our sample, and we therefore take a simplified approach using the single data point all our sources share (i.e., the 151 GHz detection) and leave a deblending study for future work." (Spectral line comparison)
- Alternative: Differential magnification and multi-phase ISM mixing were rejected as explanations for super-thermalized CO ratios because linear combinations of sub-thermalized/thermalized regions cannot exceed unity.  
  > "Differential magnification of thermalised or sub-thermalised SLEDs only generates thermalised or sub-thermalised SLEDs." (Are we confident that these SLEDs are real?); "Therefore, no amount of differential magnification nor combination of regions with different ISM phases can create a super-thermalised line ratio ($k_\mathrm{obs}>1$) from individual thermalised or sub-thermalised components with each individual $k_i\leq 1$." (Why differential lensing or a multi-phase ISM cannot produce super-thermalized line profiles)
- Alternative: Self-absorption of CO (1-0) was rejected as an explanation for the BEARS CUBS super-thermalized ratios because the observed ratios involve J >= 3 transitions rather than CO (1-0).  
  > "Since our ratios do not include the CO (1–0) emission line, the second option is also not a likely solution, but the optical depth effects or thermal gradients can possibly explain our result." (Physical interpretation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single-point 151 GHz L_IR estimation assumes T_dust = 35 K and beta = 2, providing little constraint on warmer dust components.  
  > "However, we note that this data point provides little constraint on any warmer dust that could be present inside a small portion of these galaxies." (Spectral line comparison)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is biased towards higher gas surface densities because spectroscopic redshifts require CO line detections.  
  > "Here we note an intrinsic bias in our sample, since all galaxies have spectroscopic redshifts based on CO line measurements. Low gas-surface-density galaxies might remain without a spectroscopic redshift, which could bias our sample towards higher gas surface densities." (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources)
- Limitation (authors): Source sizes from IMFIT (~2 arcsec beam) are marginally resolved, use Band 4 continuum as a proxy for CO size, and are uncorrected for gravitational lensing or Einstein radius geometry.  
  > "The size estimates from IMFIT are on the same order as the beam size of the current ALMA observations ($\approx 2$ arcsec), and could be affected by the magnification of gravitational lensing." (Inferred Schmidt-Kennicutt relation of Herschel sources); "Here we note that the size estimation from Band 4 continuum might not equal the source size probed by the CO emission lines (cite: C.C.Chen2017)." (Dynamical properties of BEARS galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): 1D PDR models (PDRToolbox) neglect cosmic rays, clumpy 3D geometry, and shocks, potentially underestimating gas density by ~0.8 dex.  
  > "At the moment, it is hard to constrain the physical properties through only two emission lines and infrared luminosity, possibly leading us to underestimate the gas density by nearly one order of magnitude (0.8 dex)." (Photo-dissociation regions inside DSFGs)
- Limitation (authors): Super-thermalized CO line ratios in the four CUBS sources are only 1 to 2.3 sigma above unity due to the intrinsic SNR limit of dividing two ~5 sigma detections, so artefacts cannot be definitively excluded.  
  > "As you can see, although these are consistent with thermalized profiles by $1$–$2.3\sigma$, we note a fundamental limitation in assessing the super-thermalized line profiles using our data." (Are we confident that these SLEDs are real?); "Although we are unable to definitively exclude artefacts as the cause of these super-thermalized line profiles, we put these results into a larger cosmological perspective." (BEARS: a unique parent sample?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Use of a single 151 GHz continuum point with fixed T=35 K and beta=2 for both L_IR (and thus SFR) and M_dust introduces strong covariance in plots comparing L_IR, M_dust, and surface densities.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Marginally resolved ~2-3 arcsec ALMA beams cannot separate intrinsic galaxy radii from lensing Einstein radii, making individual dynamical masses and surface densities highly uncertain.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line blending between CH 532 / HCN (6-5) and CH 536 / HOC+ (6-5) at ~300-600 km/s resolution complicates individual species attribution in the stack.
