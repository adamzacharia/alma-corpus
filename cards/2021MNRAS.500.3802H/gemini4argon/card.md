## Analysis recipe

1. **Clean ALMA data cubes and extract global CO(1-0) spectra**: Clean dirty cubes interactively over ~50 channels centred on peak CO(1-0) emission using tclean. Smooth trimmed cubes spatially with a 2D Gaussian kernel (sigma_smooth = 1.5 spaxels), collapse spectrally, threshold at >1 sigma within an ~200x200 spaxel spatial region to build a spatial aperture mask, apply to original trimmed cube, and collapse spatially to extract integrated spectra.  
  > "The calibrated dirty cubes were cleaned using the `tclean` task over a range of $\approx 50$ channels centred on the peak of CO(1) emission using the interactive keyword." (ALMA sample and observations); "To extract the spectra (we note that we do not detect continuum emission in any of our objects), we define apertures by smoothing our cubes over the trimmed channel range with a 2D Gaussian kernel with $\upsigma_{smooth} = 1.5$ spaxels, using the `Gaussian2DKernel` and `convolve` sub-routines within the `astropy.convolution` package (cite: astropy13; astropy18)." (ALMA sample and observations); "We then collapse the smoothed cubes over their spectral axes and set all spaxels with a values over a standard deviation ($1\upsigma$) to 1 and those below to 0 to create a mask which we apply to each trimmed channel in our original cubes as an aperture (we also define a trimmed spatial region based on the collapsed smoothed cube of $\approx 200 \times 200$ spaxels outside which all spaxels are set to 0)." (ALMA sample and observations)

## Qualitative findings

- Galaxies powering large-scale ionised gas outflows do not have significantly different global molecular gas fractions or global star-formation efficiencies compared to matched control galaxies.  
  > "We find that the galaxies powering outflows do not possess significantly different global gas fractions or star-formation efficiencies when compared with a control sample." (Abstract)
- Molecular gas and star formation in outflow-type galaxies are more centrally concentrated (largely confined within r_eff) with a steeper radial decline than in control galaxies.  
  > "For our outflow-type objects, molecular gas and star-formation is largely confined within their inner effective radius ($\rm r_{eff}$), whereas in the control sample the distribution is more diffuse, extending far beyond $\rm r_{eff}$." (Abstract); "The gas and SFR distribution in the outflow-types generally fall within their inner $\approx \rm r_{eff}$ and decline with radius more steeply than the controls." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Outflow-type galaxies show an enhanced molecular gas fraction within their inner 0.5 r_eff compared to ALMA control galaxies, while resolved SFE shows no consistent enhancement.  
  > "We observe no obvious difference between the distribution of SFE between the outflow-type objects and their controls." (Summary and Conclusions); "There is an enhancement of molecular gas fraction in the outflow-types within their inner $0.5 \rm~r_{eff}$ compared to their controls (see Figure 15)." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Outflow-type galaxies do not show consistently higher spaxel values of Sigma_SFR or Sigma_CO at ~1 kpc resolution compared to control samples, nor extra-planar CO(1-0) emission.  
  > "Using our resolved maps from ALMA and data from the SAMI Galaxy Survey, we do not detect consistently higher density $\Sigma _{\rm CO}$ and $\Sigma _{\rm SFR}$ spaxels in the outflow-type objects compared to those in their controls." (Summary and Conclusions); "As the gas we observe is contained within a thin disk, without significant CO(1) emission outside the plane, it would seem that these objects are not entraining large quantities of molecular gas." (Equivalent Outflow-Control Global Gas Content)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Edge-on SAMI galaxies with 2'' optical IFU data allow unambiguous identification of extra-planar ionised winds, and ALMA Band 3 CO(1-0) at 1'' resolution matches the SAMI resolution down to 0.8 M_sun pc^-2 sensitivity while IRAM 30-m checks total flux.  
  > "For edge-on galaxies (i.e. $i \gtrapprox 70^{\circ}$) there is a second diagnostic that unambiguously identifies galaxies with strong winds: extra-planar emission from gas excited by the outflowing material can be detected by gas velocity dispersion and line ratios that increase with height above the disk plane (see Figure 1)." (Sample selection and data); "This observational setup was chosen to produce data with a resolution comparable to that of the SAMI observations." (ALMA sample and observations); "The on-source time was between 45 and 53 minutes for each galaxy, to ensure sensitivity to a molecular gas mass surface density of 0.8 $M_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$, well into the atomic-gas dominated regime of the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation (cite: bigiel08)." (ALMA sample and observations)
- Why this method: Supplemental xCOLD GASS and SAMI control samples were constructed because AGN contamination and non-detections left only 3 viable ALMA control galaxies; outflow-types were analysed individually due to their diverse kinematic and global properties.  
  > "Due to contamination of our ALMA control sample by AGN and CO(1) non-detections, there are only 3 viable control objects. We compensate for this by including additional suitable control objects where possible in both our integrated and resolved analyses to create a more robust comparative sample for our outflow-type objects." (Results); "Throughout our resolved analysis in Section 3.2, we treat our ALMA outflow-types individually due to the wide variety of global (e.g. metallicity and stellar mass) and resolved properties (i.e. some objects possess enhanced star-formation and gas density, while others do not) that we observe in the sample." (Effect of Timescales)
- Alternative: Using pre-computed SAMI SFR map data products was rejected in favour of deriving SFR maps directly from SAMI H-alpha emission maps with a less conservative BPT mask (using only [NII]/H-alpha and [OIII]/H-beta).  
  > "We use the emission line maps instead of the SFR map data products provided by SAMI in order to make a less conservative estimate on masked spaxels (cite: bryant15; medling18)." (Spatially Resolved Observations)
- Alternative: Using the observed ALMA control galaxies for the global integrated analysis was rejected in favour of a larger control sample drawn from xCOLD GASS.  
  > "In Section 3.1, we use a control sample derived solely from xCOLD GASS (referred to as the “xCOLD GASS controls”) for the interpretation of the global/integrated results (i.e. we do not include the original, viable ALMA controls)." (Results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only 3 viable ALMA control galaxies remain after removing CO(1-0) non-detections and AGN-contaminated objects, and they primarily match the low-M_* outflow-type subgroup.  
  > "Due to contamination of our ALMA control sample by AGN and CO(1) non-detections, there are only 3 viable control objects." (Results); "This may suggest that our ALMA control sample is more suitable as a control group for our low-$\rm M_*$ outflow-type objects." (Spatially Resolved Observations)
- Limitation (authors): GAMA 31452 shows warped arms and major morphological disruption in HSC optical images and may contaminate the outflow-type sample.  
  > "We note in our outflow-type sample some anomalies in the physical appearance of GAMA31452 in the HSC optical images in Appendix 7 (visually, the arms are warped and show clear signs of major disruption). It is included in our sample due to the small sample size, but it may contribute to some level of contamination." (Global Molecular Gas Contents)
- Limitation (authors): HI atomic gas measurements are not available for all ALMA targets, so total cold gas (HI + H2) depletion cannot be assessed directly.  
  > "However, we note the absence in this analysis of H I gas content in our outflow-types and controls (as we do not have H I for all our ALMA outflow-type/control sample)." (Equivalent Outflow-Control Global Gas Content)
- Limitation (authors): High disk inclinations mean CO velocity dispersion is dominated by disk rotation/in-plane motions along the line of sight, making minor-axis outflows difficult to detect in sigma_CO.  
  > "However, it should be noted that our ALMA objects are highly inclined meaning the $\rm \upsigma _{CO}$ values we measure are largely dominated by motions into/out of the plane of the disks. Outflows along the minor axes, therefore, would be difficult to detect in $\rm \upsigma _{CO}$." (Equivalent Outflow-Control Global Gas Content)
- Limitation (authors): The ~1 kpc physical resolution (after re-binning to SAMI resolution) is an order of magnitude larger than individual GMCs (15-90 pc), smearing compact dense gas and intense star-forming pockets.  
  > "Our ALMA CO(1) maps have a spatial resolution of $\approx$ 1 kpc (once re-binned to the SAMI resolution), approximately an order of magnitude larger than the average size of GMCs." (Dense vs. Diffuse Molecular Gas); "The contribution of gas in GMCs will, therefore, be smeared by the ALMA beam, reducing the $\Sigma_{\rm CO}$ measurements in our CO(1) maps on small spatial scales." (Dense vs. Diffuse Molecular Gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The resolved ALMA control sample contains only 3 galaxies (none in the high-mass regime), forcing reliance on an empirical Sigma_SFR-to-Sigma_CO scaling relation (Leroy et al. 2013) to infer molecular gas profiles for the SAMI high-mass control group.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Degrading the 1'' ALMA maps to the 2'' SAMI resolution in edge-on disks (i >= 70 deg) introduces severe beam smearing and projection effects along the line of sight, which can artificially flatten surface density histograms and obscure central concentrations or minor-axis features.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single global metallicity-dependent alpha_CO value (from a 3'' central aperture) is applied across the entire resolved disk of each galaxy, ignoring radial metallicity or excitation gradients that could reduce alpha_CO in galaxy centres and exaggerate or suppress central gas mass concentrations.
