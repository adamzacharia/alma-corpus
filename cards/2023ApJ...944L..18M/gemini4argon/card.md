## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract gas dynamical properties, surface densities, and velocity dispersions from PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) and mega-tables**: Adopt rotation curves from CO velocity fields (NGC 628, NGC 1365, NGC 7496) or stellar-mass semi-empirical model (IC 5332) and interpolate onto the JWST map grid. Adopt 1-kpc hexagonal aperture molecular gas surface densities and 150-pc cloud-scale velocity dispersions from PHANGS mega-tables (12m+7m+tp for NGC 628, NGC 1365, NGC 7496). For IC 5332, convolve the 7m+tp moment-0 map to 1.5 kpc resolution, sample at hex centers, apply alpha_CO, and adopt a global minimum velocity dispersion of 1.25 km/s (plus an optional exponential atomic gas component).  
  > "Each rotation curve is interpolated onto a finer radial grid so that each pixel (and resolution element) in the JWST map has an estimate of the circular velocity $V_c$ associated with it." (PHANGS-ALMA); "We therefore adopt the ‘large-scale’ area-weighted average molecular gas surface density measured in 1-kpc hexagonal apertures for NGC 628, NGC 1365 and NGC 7496." (PHANGS-ALMA); "For IC 5332, the flux recovery is considerably higher in the 7m+tp CO(2-1) cube (compared to the 12m+7m+tp cube), and so we construct our ‘large-scale’ area-weighted molecular surface densities from the 7m+tp moment-0 map convolved to 1.5 kpc resolution, sampling the convolved map at the center of each hex aperture." (PHANGS-ALMA)
   - parameter: 1-kpc hexagonal apertures; 150 pc scale for moment-0 and velocity dispersion (NGC 628, NGC 1365, NGC 7496)  
  > "In a given hex aperture, these values are calculated from the total flux traced in the 12m+7m+tp CO(2-1) 150 pc-scale integrated intensity (moment-0) map within the hex boundary, divided by the area of the aperture. (cite: sun22)" (PHANGS-ALMA); "As our fiducial estimate of the molecular gas velocity dispersion in each aperture, we choose the average velocity dispersion measured specifically on 150 pc scales from the 12m+7m+tp CO(2-1) cubes for NGC 628, NGC 1365 and NGC 7496 (cite: sun22)." (PHANGS-ALMA)
   - parameter: 1.5 kpc resolution convolved 7m+tp moment-0 map and minimum velocity dispersion sigma = 1.25 km s^-1 for IC 5332  
  > "We therefore select the minimum velocity dispersion $\sigma=1.25$ km s$^{-1}$ as a realistic upper bound on the velocity dispersion in the molecular gas at all locations in the map of IC 5332." (PHANGS-ALMA)
   - assumption: Aperture-dependent alpha_CO following Sun et al. (2022)  
  > "We then apply an $\alpha_{\mathrm CO}$ appropriate for each aperture following the prescription of (cite: sun22)." (PHANGS-ALMA)
   - assumption: Principal fragmentation happens at/near the cloud scale (150 pc)  
  > "This assumes that principal fragmentation happens at/near the cloud scale rather than above it; any scale-dependent evolution in turbulent velocity dispersion is assumed to occur below the cloud scale, e.g. where it can lead to secondary fragmentation and eventually lead to star formation." (PHANGS-ALMA)
   - assumption: Optional atomic gas in IC 5332: exponential decline from maximum 10 M_sun pc^-2 with 3.6 kpc scale length and sigma = 7 km s^-1  
  > "In this case, the total gas surface density is calculated by combining the molecular surface density with an atomic gas surface density that is assumed to exponentially decline from a maximum value of 10 M$_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$ with a scale length of 3.6 kpc." (PHANGS-ALMA); "When the atomic gas component included, the gas velocity dispersion is assigned a value of 7 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: leroy08)." (PHANGS-ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked impact of adopting an alternative global neutral gas velocity dispersion of sigma_gas = 11 km s^-1  
  > "An alternative choice would be to adopt a single value representative of the neutral gas as a whole, e.g. $\sigma_{gas}\approx$ 11 km s$^{-1}$ following (cite: leroy08) and others. We comment on the impact of this choice later in $\S$ [4](#sec:results)." (PHANGS-ALMA)
2. **Identify filamentary networks in JWST/MIRI F1130W images**: Create unsharp mask versions of the MIRI F1130W images by subtracting a Gaussian-smoothed version (sigma = 1.1" or 10 pixels) from the native image, then run FilFinder with global intensity thresholds and minimum width and length cuts to generate filament masks.  
  > "Filaments are identified using FilFinder (cite: KochRosolowsky)." (Identifying Filaments); "To lend objectivity to this choice, in this work FilFinder is applied to ‘unsharp mask’ versions of the original images, created as the difference between the native image and a smoothed version calculated via Gaussian convolution with a sigma of 1.1" (10 image pixels or roughly 3 resolution elements; i.e. adopting a fixed angular rather than physical smoothing scale)." (Identifying Filaments)
   - software: FilFinder
   - parameter: Unsharp mask Gaussian smoothing sigma = 1.1" (10 image pixels)  
  > "created as the difference between the native image and a smoothed version calculated via Gaussian convolution with a sigma of 1.1" (10 image pixels or roughly 3 resolution elements; i.e. adopting a fixed angular rather than physical smoothing scale)." (Identifying Filaments)
   - parameter: Minimum filament width = 50 pc; minimum filament length = 100 pc (NGC 628, IC 5332) or 500 pc (NGC 1365, NGC 7496)  
  > "Extracted features are selected to have widths not below 50 pc (roughly 3 resolution elements at the distance of NGC 628) and lengths not below 100 pc, although in the two most distant barred galaxies NGC 1365 and NGC 7496, this minimum length is increased to 500 pc, selected to avoid mildly resolved clumps not the focus of this work." (Identifying Filaments)
   - parameter: Unsharp mask global intensity threshold = 0.03 MJy/sr (NGC 628, IC 5332, NGC 7496) or 0.2 MJy/sr (NGC 1365)  
  > "The ‘unsharp mask’ threshold level is set to 0.03 MJy/sr in NGC 628, IC 5332 and NGC 7496. In NGC 1365, the threshold is raised to 0.2 MJy/sr to avoid selecting substructures in the principle filamentary features of interest." (Identifying Filaments)
   - assumption: Shorter or narrower lower-intensity elongated structures do not contribute to the main filamentary web and originate from a different mechanism  
  > "Those objects, which tend to be shorter or narrower than our selected FilFinder filaments, do not contribute to the visually striking filamentary web and are assumed to originate with a different mechanism." (Identifying Filaments)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested robustness to variations in unsharp mask smoothing scale and FilFinder parameters, and compared with native image extractions  
  > "The number and basic footprints of identified structures are found to be surprisingly robust to modest changes in the primary FilFinder parameters, with some small changes in filament edges possible." (Identifying Filaments); "(With selective tuning of the intensity threshold, however, FilFinder returns similar filaments when applied to the native images.)" (Identifying Filaments); "Provided the adaptive threshold scale and brightness threshold parameters are also adjusted to match the new properties of the narrower filaments in the $\theta_2$ map, we have found that FilFinder returns a very similar set of structures as in the nominal $\theta_1$ case." (Identifying Filaments)
3. **Measure characteristic azimuthal and radial spacings between filaments**: For NGC 628 and IC 5332, divide FilFinder masks into narrow elliptical annuli (width = 3 pixels or 1 resolution element) at the galaxy's inclination and PA, find intersections with >= 5 pixels, compute nearest-neighbor azimuthal separations in each ring, and average over 20 radial bins (300 pc-wide annuli). For NGC 1365 and NGC 7496, divide masks into 1-degree wide azimuthal segments, find radial intersections with >= 5 pixels, and calculate mean and standard deviation of nearest-neighbor radial separations in two radial zones (R < 2.5 kpc and R > 2.5 kpc) across azimuths away from bar ends.  
  > "To measure the azimuthal spacings of filaments in the FilFinder masks, we first divide the masks into a series of narrow elliptical annuli projected onto the sky plane at the orientation of the galaxy (see Table [\[tab:props\]](#tab:props))." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "We adopt a final radial averaging and measure the mean angular separation in every twenty radial bins, i.e. in 300 pc-wide annuli." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "To measure the radial spacings of the ring-like filaments in the FilFinder masks for NGC 1365 and NGC7496, a similar procedure is adopted but now the masks are divided into 1 degree wide azimuthal segments rather than annuli." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings)
   - parameter: Annulus width = 1 resolution element (3 pixels, 15 pc at NGC 628); radial averaging over 20 bins (300 pc-wide annuli); intersection threshold N_pix = 5 pixels  
  > "The width of the annuli is chosen to be one resolution element or 3 pixels in the MIRI images (15 pc at the distance of NGC 628)." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "All filament mask objects that intersect the ring with fewer than a threshold of $N_{\rm pix}=5$ pixels are ignored." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "We adopt a final radial averaging and measure the mean angular separation in every twenty radial bins, i.e. in 300 pc-wide annuli." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings)
   - parameter: Azimuthal segment width = 1 degree; radial zones R < 2.5 kpc and R > 2.5 kpc; intersection threshold N_pix = 5 pixels  
  > "In each azimuthal segment, the radial intersection between the filaments in the mask and the segment is sought, omitting any intersections consisting of less than $N_{\rm pix}=5$." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "To modestly account for the possibility of radial variation in the radial spacings, segments are analyzed in two pieces, $R$$<$2.5 kpc and $R$$>$2.5 kpc." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings)
   - assumption: Galaxy inclination and position angle from Table 1 used to project elliptical annuli  
  > "To measure the azimuthal spacings of filaments in the FilFinder masks, we first divide the masks into a series of narrow elliptical annuli projected onto the sky plane at the orientation of the galaxy (see Table [\[tab:props\]](#tab:props))." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Calculate Toomre length, 2D most unstable wavelength, Toomre Q, gas scale height, and 3D turbulent Jeans length, and compare with filament spacings**: In each 1-kpc hexagonal aperture, compute epicyclic frequency kappa from analytical rotation curve fits, Toomre length lambda_T = 4*pi^2*G*Sigma/kappa^2, 2D most unstable wavelength lambda_2D = 2*sigma^2/(G*Sigma), and Toomre Q. Estimate vertical gas scale height h either by assuming a fixed h = 100 pc or by solving the quadratic hydrostatic equilibrium equation with gas self-gravity and background stellar density rho_b, then compute mid-plane gas density rho = Sigma/(sqrt(2*pi)*h) and 3D turbulent Jeans length lambda_J = sigma*pi^(1/2)/(G*rho)^(1/2). Compare radial profiles of these scales against measured filament spacings.  
  > "We determine $\lambda_T$ at the locations of the mega-table hexagonal apertures (see $\S$ [2.2](#sec:almadata)) for each galaxy using the observed gas surface density and an estimate for the radial epicyclic frequency" (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length); "Where possible we do this in two ways: either assuming a uniform 100 pc scale height (i.e. the height of the MW disk, (cite: heyerdame15)) or solving for the scale height required for hydrostatic equilibrium in the presence of the self-gravity and a background potential, i.e." (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length); "Using either scale height, the mid-plane gas volume density is calculated as $\rho=\Sigma/(\sqrt{2\pi}h)$, assuming a Gaussian vertical distribution." (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)
   - parameter: Fixed gas scale height h = 100 pc (for Table 1 values) or hydrostatic equilibrium h (typically 30 to 100 pc)  
  > "The mean of the values measured in all hexagonal apertures (see $\S$ [2.2](#sec:almadata)) assuming a fixed gas scale height $h$=100 pc." (Table 1); "The gas scale heights estimated for our four targets lie typically in the range 30 to 100 pc and exhibit a modest amount of radial variation." (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)
   - assumption: Gas is isothermal and follows a Gaussian vertical distribution rho propto exp(-z^2/(2h^2)) such that Sigma = rho*sqrt(2*pi)*h  
  > "Assuming that the gas is isothermal and follows a Gaussian vertical distribution $\rho\propto exp(-z^2/(2h^2))$ with scale height $h$ (such that $\Sigma=\rho\sqrt{2\pi}h$) and making the typical assumption that the vertical terms dominate Poisson’s equation, this can be written as the approximation" (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)
   - assumption: Background gravitational potential is dominated by the stellar distribution, omitting atomic gas and dark matter halo (unless specified for IC 5332)  
  > "For this work, the background is assumed to be dominated by the underlying stellar distribution, omitting the contribution from atomic gas and the dark matter halo (unless otherwise specified)." (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)
   - fitting: Analytical quadratic equation solved for hydrostatic equilibrium scale height h  
  > "yielding the following quadratic equation to solve for $h$, $$\begin{equation}

\sqrt{8\pi}G\Sigma h +4\pi G\rho_b h^2-\sigma^2=0.\label{eq:scaleheight}
\end{equation}$$" (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)

## Qualitative findings

- JWST/MIRI imaging reveals a ubiquitous, quasi-periodic network of interconnected filaments, shells, and voids across all four target galaxies, with filaments ranging from ~50 pc to 1 kpc or longer.  
  > "As we will show here (cite: THILKER_PHANGSJWST), connecting and underlying these bubbles, there is a more extended and highly regular, quasi-periodic pattern of filamentary features that exhibit similar lengths, widths, spacings and orientations. These filaments range in size from $\sim$ 50 pc to 1 kpc or longer." (Introduction)
- Measured filament spacings closely match the local 3D turbulent Jeans length in all four galaxies and increase modestly with galactocentric radius, mirroring the radial behavior of the Jeans length.  
  > "In all cases, the filament spacings fall very near the Jeans length and agree less well with the other reference lengths, although the Toomre and Jeans lengths are often similar (as considered later in $\S$ [5](#sec:discussion))." (Results); "Consistent with the visual impression from the maps, filaments tend to modestly increase in separation moving outwards in galactocentric radius. A similar behavior is typical of the Jeans length in these targets, given observed gas properties." (Results)
- The gas disks in all four galaxies are generally Toomre stable in 2D (Q >= 1, with lambda_2D >= lambda_T), ruling out standard 2D Toomre fragmentation at lambda_2D and favoring 3D gravitational instability on the turbulent Jeans scale.  
  > "In the disks of the present target sample, $\lambda_{2D}$ lies near or above $\lambda_T$, which occurs when $Q\gtrsim1$ (see eq. [\[eq:toomrecriterion\]](#eq:toomrecriterion))." (Filaments as a product of gravitational instability); "This may be a sign that the observed structure originates as the product of 3D disk fragmentation predicted to occur on the Jeans length at the mid-plane in rotating gas disks, i.e. regulated by the balance between turbulent pressure and self-gravity, without strong sensitivity to shear ((cite: meidt22); and see e.g. (cite: elm87; ko06; elm11))." (Summary)
- Filament spacings are 1 to 1.5 orders of magnitude larger than the disk scale height (30-100 pc), whereas feedback-driven bubbles typically stall near the scale height, indicating the large-scale filament network is dynamical in origin rather than purely feedback-driven.  
  > "As alluded to already in $\S$ [4](#sec:results), the filamentary structure evident in the MIRI images is spaced with a characteristic scale that is 1-1.5 orders of magnitude larger than the disk scale height." (Filaments in relation to feedback); "With some exceptions (cite: BARNES_PHANGSJWST), many of the feedback-driven shells traced by MIRI appear to be stalling at or near the scale height, supporting the idea that structures present on larger scales (with separations $>$ 100 pc) are mostly dynamical in origin." (Filaments in relation to feedback)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MIRI F1130W (0.36" FWHM, 16-34 pc) offers the highest sensitivity to low column densities and extended filamentary PAH emission, which correlates tightly with CO; PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) traces the molecular gas that dominates the inner ISM (>70-90% in three targets) to provide rotation curves, surface densities, and cloud-scale velocity dispersions.  
  > "Our focus for this pilot study is on MIRI F1130W imaging (0.36” full-width-half-maximum resolution) where the sensitivity to low column densities is highest (cite: LEROY2_PHANGSJWST)." (PHANGS-JWST imaging); "We use PHANGS-ALMA CO (2-1) (cite: leroy21a; leroy21b) as our primary tracer of the dynamics of the gas, its global rotation, turbulent velocity dispersion and mass surface density." (PHANGS-ALMA)
- Why this method: Applying FilFinder to unsharp-masked images removes the need for radially varying intensity thresholds and enhances low-brightness interarm filaments; measuring azimuthal spacings in non-barred galaxies (NGC 628, IC 5332) and radial spacings in barred galaxies (NGC 1365, NGC 7496) matches the predominant orientation of the filaments.  
  > "This strategy was found to be an effective way to remove the need for varying thresholds within and between galaxies (due to underlying (radial) intensity gradients) while also enhancing the contrast of low brightness interarm filaments often found in interarm regions." (Identifying Filaments); "We thus measure either the azimuthal separation between filament features at a given radius – in NGC 628 and IC 5332 – or the radial separations between filaments at a given azimuth – in NGC 1365 and NGC 7496." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings)
- Alternative: Adopting a single global velocity dispersion representative of the neutral gas as a whole (sigma_gas ~ 11 km s^-1) instead of the 150-pc cloud-scale CO velocity dispersion.  
  > "An alternative choice would be to adopt a single value representative of the neutral gas as a whole, e.g. $\sigma_{gas}\approx$ 11 km s$^{-1}$ following (cite: leroy08) and others." (PHANGS-ALMA)
- Alternative: Applying FilFinder directly to native MIRI images with selective tuning of the intensity threshold instead of using unsharp-masked images.  
  > "(With selective tuning of the intensity threshold, however, FilFinder returns similar filaments when applied to the native images.)" (Identifying Filaments)
- Alternative: Assuming a uniform 100 pc gas scale height (MW value) vs. solving for the hydrostatic equilibrium scale height in the presence of gas self-gravity and the stellar potential.  
  > "Where possible we do this in two ways: either assuming a uniform 100 pc scale height (i.e. the height of the MW disk, (cite: heyerdame15)) or solving for the scale height required for hydrostatic equilibrium in the presence of the self-gravity and a background potential, i.e." (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)
- Alternative: Using spatial Fourier power spectra to characterize the quasi-periodicity of the filament network, deferred to future work due to contamination from large-scale galaxy structure, resolution effects, and non-Cartesian geometry.  
  > "Our approach, described below, misses a direct characterization of the quasi-periodicity visible in the images, but the determination of, e.g., Fourier power spectra might be fruitful in future." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "However, the large scale structure of the galaxy and the resolution of the observations can masquerade as characteristic features in power spectra (cite: koch20)." (A characteristic scale?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Interpolating a 1D rotation curve to estimate V_c can mis-estimate circular velocity where strong non-circular motions are present (such as in the barred regions of NGC 1365 and NGC 7496), leading to an underestimation of the Toomre length at those radii.  
  > "This approach may lead to mis-estimation of $V_c$ where strong non-circular motions are present and contribute to the measured rotation curve (within the barred regions of NGC 1365 and NGC 7496, for example). The result is that the Toomre length may be underestimated at these radii (see $\S$ [3.2](#sec:jeanstoomre))." (PHANGS-ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): In IC 5332, the 7m+tp moment-2 map at 1.5 kpc resolution contains unresolved bulk motion, requiring the use of a single minimum velocity dispersion (1.25 km s^-1) as an upper bound across the galaxy.  
  > "For IC 5332, we expect the velocity dispersion measured from the 7m+tp moment-2 map at 1.5 kpc resolution to contain unresolved bulk motion. We therefore select the minimum velocity dispersion $\sigma=1.25$ km s$^{-1}$ as a realistic upper bound on the velocity dispersion in the molecular gas at all locations in the map of IC 5332." (PHANGS-ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): With the chosen FilFinder parameters, many visibly long filaments are segmented, making the masks suitable for measuring spacings rather than individual filament lengths or geometries.  
  > "With these parameters, many visibly long filaments in all four of the targets are segmented by FilFinder, but the identified structures are suitable for our goal of measuring the spacing of structures rather than characterizing their individual lengths or geometries." (Identifying Filaments)
- Limitation (authors): The 1D azimuthal/radial spacing measurement ignores the covariance of radial and azimuthal spacings and does not measure spacings between spiral arm spurs.  
  > "However, the covariance of radial and azimuthal spacings and filament lengths is beyond the scope of the present work and we proceed with the simplest possible spacing measurement for each target, given the typical orientation of the observed filaments." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings); "It is worth noting that this approach does not measure the spacings between spur features adjacent to the spiral arms." (Measuring Characteristic Spacings)
- Limitation (authors): Omitting atomic gas and the dark matter halo from the background potential in the hydrostatic equilibrium equation may lead to an overestimation of the gas scale height and Jeans length.  
  > "For this work, the background is assumed to be dominated by the underlying stellar distribution, omitting the contribution from atomic gas and the dark matter halo (unless otherwise specified). This may lead to overestimation of the gas scale height and Jeans length determinations, but should be suitable for the preliminary estimates presented in this work." (Estimating the Turbulent Jeans Length and the Toomre Length)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of precise observational constraints on atomic gas surface density and velocity dispersion for NGC 1365 and NGC 7496 prevents a quantitative assessment of stability including atomic gas in those galaxies.  
  > "Since we lack precise observational constraints on the atomic gas surface density and velocity dispersion for NGC 1365 and NGC 7496, we do not make a quantitative assessment of stability including the atomic gas here." (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Measuring exclusively azimuthal spacings in unbarred galaxies and radial spacings in barred galaxies projects 2D filament separations onto a single coordinate axis without correcting for filament pitch angle, which systematically overestimates the true perpendicular separation between filaments.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In IC 5332, the molecular gas accounts for only ~10% of the ISM and its velocity dispersion is fixed globally to 1.25 km/s, so agreement between the molecular-only Jeans length and MIRI filament spacing may be coincidental unless the assumed atomic gas model is used.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The unsharp masking filter (Gaussian sigma = 1.1", or ~50-100 pc) and minimum length cuts (100 pc or 500 pc) impose a spatial bandpass that suppresses small-scale structure and could bias the recovered nearest-neighbor spacing distribution toward a few hundred pc.
