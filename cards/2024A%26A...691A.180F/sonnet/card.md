## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum source detection and flux**: Detect 3 mm and 2 mm continuum cores N and S; integrate flux inside 3 sigma contours  
  > "The flux densities, $F_{\nu}$, integrated inside the 3$\sigma$ rms contour levels are given in Table [\[tab:cont\]](#tab:cont) for both sources" (3.1 Millimeter continuum emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Check for resolved-out flux**: Compare ALMA spectra from a 27 arcsec circle with IRAM 30m spectra converted to flux density  
  > "we conclude that the amount of extended flux resolved out by ALMA is negligible and does not affect significantly our analysis." (3.4 Comparison between ALMA and IRAM spectra)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Agreement within <=10% for c-C3H2 and H13CO+  
  > "consistent with those measured with the IRAM-30m telescope in *c-*C$_3$H$_2$ and H$^{13}$CO$^+$ within a factor $\leq 10\%$" (3.4 Comparison between ALMA and IRAM spectra)
3. **LTE column densities**: AUTOFIT of SLIM for c-C3H2, CH3OH, H2CO, C4H, HCS+; CLASS integrated intensities for the rest, with Tex from CH3OH, optically thin  
  > "We decided to adopt for all species the $T_{\rm ex}$ measured from CH$_3$OH." (4.1 Spectral analysis)
   - software: MADCUBA, GILDAS/CLASS
   - parameter: No filling factor applied; Tex fixed to CH3OH value  
  > "we assumed that the emission is more extended than the extraction region in all tracers, and hence no filling factor is appliyed" (4.1 Spectral analysis)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin  
  > "We assumed also optically thin conditions." (4.1 Spectral analysis)
   - fitting: MADCUBA AUTOFIT, free N_tot, V, FWHM, common Tex  
  > "This function produces the synthetic spectrum that best matches the data assuming a constant excitation temperature ($T_{\rm ex}$) for all transitions of the same species." (4.1 Spectral analysis)
   - validation: Tex varied to 30 K; N_tot changes up to factor ~2  
  > "$N_{\rm tot}$ can vary up to a factor 2 depending on the species." (4.2 Fractional abundances in the continuum cores)
4. **H2 column density and abundances**: N(H2) from continuum flux with Battersby equation; abundances = N_tot/N(H2)  
  > "We show the molecular fractional abundances derived from the $N_{\rm tot}$-to-$N({\rm H_2})$ ratio in Table [4](#tab:lines-cont)." (4.2 Fractional abundances in the continuum cores)
   - parameter: beta=1.7, kappa=0.899 at 230 GHz, mu=2.8, gamma~3000  
  > "We also assume dust opacity index $\beta =1.7$, a typical value used for ice-coated dust grains in dense cores" (4.2 Fractional abundances in the continuum cores)
   - assumption: Td = CH3OH Tex (gas-dust coupling)  
  > "We adopt for $T_{\rm d}$ the excitation temperature derived from CH$_3$OH (Table [4](#tab:lines-cont)) for each core, assuming coupling between gas and dust." (4.2 Fractional abundances in the continuum cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative beta from 3mm/2mm fluxes gives N(H2) factor ~3 lower  
  > "i.e. a factor $\sim 3$ smaller than those given in Table" (4.2 Fractional abundances in the continuum cores)
5. **Chemical modelling comparison**: UCLCHEM static grid of 65536 models; compare column density ratios at 1e6 yr; select 50 lowest-MSE models  
  > "the 50 models with the lowest Mean Squared Error (MSE) are chosen." (5.4 Chemical modelling)
   - software: UCLCHEM
   - parameter: R=0.5 pc, Av=1 mag, gas-to-dust 100, 12C/13C=10^1.8  
  > "Each of the models is ran with an edge visual extinction of $A_v=1.0$ mag, and molecular ratios are computed at $10^6$ years." (5.4 Chemical modelling)
   - assumption: Isothermal constant-density static cloud  
  > "We considered a "static model", namely an isothermal cloud at constant density with a radius of $R=0.5$ pc" (5.4 Chemical modelling)
   - fitting: Grid sampled over density, T, zeta, F_UV, initial O and C abundances; ranking by MSE  
  > "| Density $n_\mathrm{H}\;(\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$ | $1 \times 10^3$ | $1 \times 10^7$ | log |" (Table)
   - validation: Test with gas-to-dust ~3000 at low and high density  
  > "In the low density case, the results do not show any significant differences." (5.4 Chemical modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- Emission is filamentary, SE-NW, with different morphology per tracer; narrow lines indicate quiescent gas  
  > "The narrow linewidths across the filament indicate quiescent gas, despite the two embedded protostars." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Natural weighting to be sensitive to source extension  
  > "To be sensitive to the source extension, the clean maps were created using natural weighting." (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Self-calibration rejected: no improvement due to faint continuum  
  > "the self-calibrated images did not show any significant improvement due to the faintness of the continuum emission" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Tex fixed to CH3OH value for all species; N_tot uncertain up to factor 2  
  > "$N_{\rm tot}$ can vary up to a factor 2 depending on the species." (4.2)
- Limitation (authors): Static models cannot reproduce all ratios; dynamical models needed  
  > "Overall, it is very unlikely to find a model that fits all ratios well." (5.4 Chemical modelling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single Tex from CH3OH applied to all species despite possible sub-thermal excitation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas-to-dust ratio extrapolated with huge uncertainty drives abundances
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optical depth of CS and SO not checked
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model selection of top 50 by MSE is ad hoc
