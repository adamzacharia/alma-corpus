## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: Which formation pathway (grain-surface versus gas-phase) dominates H2CO in the HD 163296 disk, constrained by radial, vertical, excitation-temperature and ortho-to-para-ratio (OPR) profiles  
  > "We determine empirically the radial and vertical distribution of H$_2$CO, as well as its excitation conditions and OPR as a function of radius." (Introduction); "Our resulting radial, vertical, and OPR constraints suggest an increased UV penetration beyond the dust millimeter edge, consistent with an icy origin but also with cold gas-phase chemistry." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and self-calibrate visibilities, subtract continuum**: Standard JAO pipeline output, pseudo-continuum from line-flagged channels, phase-only then phase-amplitude self-calibration on short baselines then combined data, applied to line data, then uvcontsub  
  > "we first applied the phase-only self-calibration to the short baseline visibilities, followed by a phase-amplitude self-calibration" (Data Reduction); "The different calibrations were applied to the data by using the `CASA` tasks `gaincal` and `applycal`." (Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, fixvis, fixplanets, gaincal, applycal, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Phase-only solints: inf, 360, 180, 60, 30, 18, 6 s (two to seven iterations depending on band and baseline set); one amplitude round with inf  
  > "we applied between two and seven iterations of phase-only self-calibration using different solution interval steps" (Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image line cubes to a common 0.6 arcsec circular beam with JvM correction**: tclean with Briggs weighting plus per-line UV taper, multiscale CLEAN with Keplerian mask, threshold 4x rms, then JvM correction  
  > "We used a Briggs weighting and multiscale deconvolver (cite: Cornwell2008) with scales of 0, 5, 15, and 25 pixels" (Data Reduction); "The images were CLEANed up to a threshold defined as four times the estimated rms of unmasked regions, and subsequently, were corrected for the *JvM effect* (cite: JvM1995)." (Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, keplerian_mask
   - parameter: Robust 0.0-0.5 and per-line UV taper (Table 4); pixel about 1/7 beam; channel width 0.2 km/s (0.5 km/s for Band 3)  
  > "All the final cubes have a channel width of $0.2$ km s$^{-1}$, with the exception of the Band 3 transition" (Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: JvM epsilon values reported (0.59-0.99); beam circularity achieved for all lines  
  > "Our derived $\epsilon$ values are listed in Table" (Data Reduction)
3. **Measure disk-integrated flux densities**: Extract disk-integrated spectrum within the Keplerian mask; uncertainty from 1000 off-source masks  
  > "we defined the uncertainty associated with the integrated flux density as the standard deviation of the integrated fluxes measured within 1000 off-source Keplerian masks" (3.1 Disk-integrated Emission)
   - software: GoFish
   - parameter: Integrated within outer radius of 4.5 arcsec and between -1.8 and 13.4 km/s  
  > "The integrated flux density is measured within an outer radius of $4\hbox{$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}5$ and integrating the emission between $-1.8$ and $13.4$ km s$^{-1}$." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Make zeroth-moment maps with bootstrapped noise**: Collapse cubes with bettermoments using one Keplerian mask for all lines, no clipping; rms from 1000 off-source moment maps  
  > "We did not apply a threshold clipping to recover accurate fluxes." (3.2 Spatial Distribution of H2CO Emission); "We created 1000 zeroth-moment maps, using the same Keplerian mask but centered at different off-source positions." (3.2 Spatial Distribution of H2CO Emission)
   - software: bettermoments
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: The 6_15-6_16 line not shown since no emission above 3 sigma in channels or unmasked moment map  
  > "there is no substantial line emission larger than $3\sigma$ neither in the channel maps nor the unmasked zeroth-moment map" (3.2 Spatial Distribution of H2CO Emission)
5. **Deproject radial intensity profiles and define the continuum edge**: Deproject and azimuthally average the moment maps; R_edge defined as the radius where continuum emission falls below 5 sigma  
  > "We defined $R_{\mathrm{edge}}$ as the radius where the emission falls below $5\sigma$." (3.3 Radial Distribution of H2CO Emission)
   - software: GoFish
   - parameter: Geometry from Table 1: inclination 46.7 deg, PA 133.3 deg, distance 101 pc  
  > "Inclination$^{\scriptsize{a}}$ | $46.7$ deg | (cite: Huang2018)" (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: R_edge consistent with higher-resolution previous results  
  > "consistent with previous results at higher angular resolution (cite: Carney2017; Law2021III)" (3.3 Radial Distribution of H2CO Emission)
6. **Fit disk-averaged LTE excitation conditions and OPR**: Simultaneous LTE fit of ortho and para lines for N_T, T_ex, OPR and line width on the Keplerian-corrected disk-averaged spectrum  
  > "we simultaneously model the emission of each line to determine the excitation temperature, the total (ortho + para) column density, and the OPR of H$_2$CO" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR); "we used the `average``_``spectrum` function from `GoFish`, which corrects the spectrum for Keplerian rotation before averaging" (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
   - software: GoFish, emcee
   - parameter: Free parameters N_T, T_rot, OPR and Delta v  
  > "the free parameters to fit are $N_{\mathrm{T}}$, $T_\mathrm{rot}$, $\mathrm{OPR}$, and $\Delta v$" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
   - assumption: LTE  
  > "If we assume that the line emission occurs under local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
   - assumption: Same excitation temperature for ortho and para  
  > "we assume the same excitation temperature since we detected only two o-H$_2$CO lines" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
   - assumption: Gaussian line profile centred at v_sys = 5.8 km/s  
  > "assuming a Gaussian line profile centered at $v_{\mathrm{sys}}=5.8$ km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Teague2019) with a certain $\Delta v$" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
   - assumption: Emitting layer in the midplane (z/r = 0) for the disk-averaged case  
  > "For simplicity, we assume that the emitting layer is in the midplane ($z/r = 0$)" (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
   - assumption: Background temperature is the maximum of dust continuum brightness temperature and the CMB (2.73 K)  
  > "where $T_{\mathrm{bg}}$ is the background temperature defined as the maximum between the dust continuum brightness temperature and the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature ($T_{\mathrm{CMB}} = 2.73$ K)" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC with a likelihood built from the LTE line intensity; best values from 16th, 50th, 84th percentiles; priors, walkers and steps not stated  
  > "and use the `emcee` package (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013) to explore the parameter space and get the posterior probability distributions" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR); "The best-fit excitation conditions and their corresponding uncertainties are deduced from the 16th, 50th, and 84th percentiles of the posterior distributions" (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
   - validation: Optically thin check (tau 0.003-0.074); consistent with 6_15-6_16 nondetection; repeated with Voigt profile giving consistent results  
  > "We also checked that these excitation conditions are consistent with the nondetection of the $6_{15}-6_{16}$ line" (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis); "Consequently, we performed the same analysis but using a Voigt profile." (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
7. **Fit radially resolved excitation conditions and OPR**: Deproject spectra into radial annuli with GoFish radial_spectra, shift and stack for Keplerian motion, and repeat the LTE fit in each bin  
  > "we repeat the same line fitting procedure but use the deprojected radial spectra instead of the disk-averaged one" (3.4.2 Radially Resolved Analysis)
   - software: GoFish, emcee
   - parameter: Annulus width equals one-quarter of the beam major axis  
  > "whose width corresponds to one-quarter of the beam" (3.4.2 Radially Resolved Analysis)
   - assumption: LTE, shared T_ex for ortho and para, same emitting region for all lines (acknowledged in footnote)  
  > "e.g., LTE emission, same $T_{\mathrm{ex}}$ for both isomers and the same emitting region for all the lines" (Footnote 7)
   - fitting: Likelihood and emcee fit per radial bin  
  > "we construct a likelihood function for each radial bin to determine the best-fit models at different radii" (3.4.2 Radially Resolved Analysis)
   - validation: Mean values compatible with disk-averaged results; emission confirmed optically thin throughout  
  > "In general, the mean values of each profile are compatible with the disk-averaged results" (3.4.2 Radially Resolved Analysis)
8. **Measure the vertical height of the emission**: ALFAHOR geometrical method on manually masked channel emission for the four brightest lines  
  > "masking manually the channel emission for each H$_2$CO transition" (3.5 Vertical Height of the Emission); "we applied this analysis only to the four brightest transitions in our sample" (3.5 Vertical Height of the Emission)
   - software: ALFAHOR
   - assumption: Stellar and geometric parameters from Table 1  
  > "Using the stellar and disk parameters from Table" (3.5 Vertical Height of the Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Value treated as a lower limit because optically thin emission blurs near/far surfaces; no notable differences between lines  
  > "Thus, the determined value should be interpreted as a lower limit." (3.5 Vertical Height of the Emission)
9. **Derive spin temperature from OPR and compare with T_ex**: Convert resolved OPR profile to spin temperature and compare with excitation temperature  
  > "We also extracted the $T_{\mathrm{spin}}$ radial profile from the resolved OPR values" (4.2.3 Ortho-to-para Ratio of H2CO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- H2CO emission shows a central depression, a peak near 60 au and ring-like structures near 250 and 380 au  
  > "is characterized by the presence of a central depression and a global peak at $\sim$$60$ au in the inner disk" (Conclusions)
- Voigt profile fits the line wings better, with slightly higher N_T and OPR but consistent within uncertainties  
  > "We find an improvement in the fit quality for the line wings, and recover slightly higher values for $N_{\mathrm{T}}$ and the OPR" (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
- HD 163296 differs from TW Hya in its OPR trend: roughly constant OPR rather than decreasing with radius  
  > "unlike the relatively constant OPR observed in HD 163296, the OPR profile of TW Hya has an apparent decreasing behavior" (4.3 H2CO in T Tauri versus Herbig Ae/Be disks)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HD 163296 is large, nearby and luminous with a well-defined CO snowline and dust edge; multiple ortho and para lines available  
  > "the Herbig Ae disk HD 163296 is an ideal source for a detailed study of H$_2$CO chemistry" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Multiple ortho and para lines over a wide E_u range allow simultaneous LTE fits; LTE justified by critical densities below typical disk gas densities  
  > "Since we have multiple ortho and para transitions that cover a wide range of upper-state energies" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR); "This should be a reasonable assumption for HD 163296 since the critical densities of the observed transitions" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
- Alternative: Separate T_rot for ortho and para not fit because only two o-H2CO lines were detected  
  > "we assume the same excitation temperature since we detected only two o-H$_2$CO lines" (3.4 H2CO Excitation Conditions and OPR)
- Alternative: Voigt line profile tested in addition to Gaussian for the line wings  
  > "Consequently, we performed the same analysis but using a Voigt profile." (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
- Alternative: Optically thick continuum favoured over temperature-tracing or line-energy explanations for the central depression  
  > "the scenario of a drop in the emission due to an optically thick continuum seems more likely" (4.2.1 Radial Distribution of H2CO)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Vertical height is a lower limit because optically thin emission blurs near and far surfaces  
  > "Thus, the determined value should be interpreted as a lower limit." (3.5 Vertical Height of the Emission)
- Limitation (authors): OPR uncertainties are purely statistical and ignore modelling assumptions  
  > "since the derived uncertainties are purely statistical and do not consider the assumptions mentioned in Section" (3.4.1 Disk-averaged Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Inner-disk column density drop is unresolved and fit quality is poorer there  
  > "the results should be treated with caution as we are on a region that cannot be resolved with the current angular resolution" (4.2.1 Radial Distribution of H2CO)
- Limitation (authors): Tentative OPR variations may reflect deviations from LTE, shared T_ex, or common emitting region assumptions  
  > "We cannot discard the possibility that the tentative variations observed in the OPR profile could be due to slight deviations from the assumptions made to derive" (Footnote 7)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single shared T_ex and same emitting layer for all lines may bias N_T and OPR, especially with lines of E_u up to 82 K
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Emission treated at midplane (z/r = 0) in the disk-averaged fit despite measuring z/r ~0.1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Circularization via strong UV tapers (e.g. 0.001 arcsec) discards resolution; impact on radial profiles not quantified
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No priors or convergence diagnostics for the MCMC reported
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-Gaussian line wings suggest the Gaussian model biases the quoted parameters
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fluxes from different programs combined with only 10% calibration uncertainty and no stated cross-band flux alignment
