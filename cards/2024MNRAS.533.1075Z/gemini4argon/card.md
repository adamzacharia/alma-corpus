## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure 0.82 mm continuum properties of MM2**: Fit a one-component two-dimensional Gaussian model to MM2's 0.82 mm continuum emission and compare with aperture/contour integration  
  > "To estimate the 0.82 mm properties of MM2, we fit a one-component two-dimensional Gaussian model to its 0.82 mm continuum emission using the CASA task imfit ." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission); "To check the robustness of the integrated flux density measurement from the Gaussian fit, we also use the CASA task imstat to measure the integrated flux density within the 30 𝜎 contour around MM2 and within a 2 [′′] . 0×1 [′′] . 0 ellipse centred on its fitted position." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission)
   - software: CASA imfit, CASA imstat
   - parameter: 30 sigma contour and 2.0" x 1.0" ellipse for imstat checks  
  > "measure the integrated flux density within the 30 𝜎 contour around MM2 and within a 2 [′′] . 0×1 [′′] . 0 ellipse centred on its fitted position." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission)
   - assumption: 10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty in Band 7  
  > "assuming 10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty for ALMA in Band 7 (Cortes et al. 2023)." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission)
   - fitting: One-component 2D Gaussian model fit via CASA imfit  
  > "we fit a one-component two-dimensional Gaussian model to its 0.82 mm continuum emission using the CASA task imfit ." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission)
   - validation: Inspected residual image (~27 mJy/beam peak surrounded by negative ring) and compared integrated flux with imstat measurements (within <3%-16%)  
  > "the residual image has a ∼27 mJy beam [−][1] peak, which is < 0 [′′] . 02 from the fitted centroid position, surrounded by a negative ring." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission); "These methods yield integrated flux density estimates of 401±40 mJy and 327±33 mJy, respectively, [3] within < 3%-16% of the value from the Gaussian fit." (3.1 ALMA 0.82 mm continuum emission)
2. **Decompose N2H+ (4-3) spectra into Gaussian velocity components**: Pixel-by-pixel multi-component Gaussian decomposition using SCOUSEPY in four stages with derivative spectroscopy and three sets of tolerance levels  
  > "To study the gas kinematics in the vicinity of MM2, we first apply Gaussian Decomposition to the N 2 H [+] (4-3) cube using the SCOUSEPY package (Henshaw et al. 2016, Henshaw et al. 2019)." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY); "We compile all the results from the three versions and select the models with the lowest AIC values as the best-fitting ones." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY)
   - software: SCOUSEPY
   - parameter: 5 sigma mask (~54 mJy/beam), SAA size = 20 pixels (~5300 AU), filling factor = 0.6  
  > "A 5 𝜎 mask (∼ 54 mJy beam [−][1] ) was first applied to remove emissionfree regions from the analyses that follow. As this dataset exhibits strong spatial variation, we chose a conservative SAA size of 20 pixels ( 5300 AU) with a filling factor of 0.6 [5] ." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY)
   - parameter: SNR = 3 to 5 (7-9 for four SAAs), sigma_ker = 1 to 3 channels  
  > "We vary the SNR from 3 to 5 and the 𝜎 ker from 1 to 3 channels to obtain satisfying fits for ≳ 96% of the SAA spectra; for an additional four SAA spectra, increasing the SNR to 7-9 yields reasonable fits." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY)
   - parameter: Tolerance levels: version 0 (T0=2.0, T1=3.0, T2=1.0, T3=2.5, T4=2.5, T5=0.5), version 1 (T3=4.0), version 2 (T4=1.0)  
  > "Parameter version 0 version 1 version 2 𝑇 0 2.0 2.0 2.0 𝑇 1 3.0 3.0 3.0 𝑇 2 1.0 1.0 1.0 𝑇 3 2.5 4.0 2.5 𝑇 4 2.5 2.5 1.0 𝑇 5 0.5 0.5 0.5" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Hyperfine structure does not dominate the multiple peaks (strongest hfs components grouped within <= 0.2 km/s); absorption features excluded  
  > "The strongest hfs components (with relative line strengths > 0.1) are tightly grouped within ≲0.2 km s [−][1] (i.e. within one channel in our image cube, which has Δv=0.2 km s [−][1] ; Section 2)." (3.2.2 N 2 H [+] (4-3)); "We note that SCOUSEPY is not designed to reproduce absorption and that Gaussian Decomposition is applied only to the N 2 H [+] emission." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY)
   - fitting: Automated multi-component Gaussian fitting initialized by derivative spectroscopy (or manual SAA fits), model selection via lowest Akaike Information Criterion (AIC)  
  > "SCOUSEPY first fits the spectra with initial guesses adopted from the measurements of derivative spectroscopy." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY); "In the final stage, SCOUSEPY selects the best-fitting models with Akaike information criterion (AIC) for the 48141 individual spectra." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY)
   - validation: Tested three tolerance level versions (versions 0, 1, 2) and compiled lowest AIC solutions; compared with multi-component hyperfine structure fitting on 10 spectra using pyspeckit  
  > "To test the performance of automated fitting, we run stage 3) with another two sets of tolerance levels labelled as version 1 and version 2." ## Qualitative findings

- MM2 lies on a north-south filamentary structure traced by both 0.82 mm continuum and N2H+ (4-3) emission, with integrated N2H+ peaks offset by 0.9" and 1.2" from the continuum peak.  
  > "MM2, which appears as a strong, compact source of 0.82 mm continuum emission, lies on a ∼north-south filamentary structure within the G11.92−0.61 massive protocluster. The filament is traced by both 0.82 mm continuum and N 2 H [+] (4-3) emission, but there are differences in the morphologies of the two tracers, with the peaks of the integrated N 2 H [+] emission offset by 0 [′′] . 9 and 1 [′′] . 2 (∼3000 and 4000 AU) from the 0.82 mm continuum peak." (8 CONCLUSIONS)
- H2D+ (1_1,0 - 1_1,1) is undetected towards MM2, consistent with internal heating from the embedded protobinary system.  
  > "H 2 D [+] is undetected towards MM2, to 4 𝜎 limits of 10.8 mJy beam [−][1] / 0.23 K. The non-detection of H 2 D [+] is likely due to internal heating from the recently-discovered protobinary system." (8 CONCLUSIONS)
- High-velocity-dispersion N2H+ components (sigma_v > 1.3 km/s) are spatially associated with known protostellar outflows from MM1/3/7/9 and MM2, showing the imprint of protostellar feedback on dense gas.  
  > "The spatial correlation between many high-velocity-dispersion components and known protostellar outflows indicates that we are observing the imprint of protostellar feedback on the dense molecular gas" (4.2 Results and basic statistics)
- The filamentary dust continuum structure contains multiple kinematically distinct substructures (Tree 0 and Tree 9) that partially overlap on the sky and may feed MM2 via multiple accretion flows.  
  > "In addition to the primary tree that is the focus of our analysis, we also identify a velocity gradient consistent with a filamentary accretion flow onto MM2 in a second tree, which is kinematically distinct but partially overlaps the primary tree on the plane of the sky. This finding suggests that the filamentary dust continuum structure contains multiple kinematic substructures, and that MM2 may be fed by multiple filamentary accretion flows." (8 CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: H2D+ (1_1,0 - 1_1,1) and N2H+ (4-3) trace cold, dense, and CO-depleted gas expected in high-mass prestellar cores at sub-arcsecond (~2000 AU) resolution.  
  > "To investigate the properties of a candidate high-mass prestellar core and its relationship to its environment, we proposed ALMA Cycle 3-5 observations of G11.92-0.61 MM2 targeting H 2 D [+] (1 1 , 0 -1 1 , 1 ) and N 2 H [+] (4-3), which trace dense and depleted gas (Ceccarelli et al. 2014)" (1 INTRODUCTION); "The expectation of extreme depletion motivated our targeting H 2 D [+] (1 1 , 0 -1 1 , 1 ) and N 2 H [+] (4-3) in our ALMA Band 7 observations." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: N2H+ (4-3) spectra are multi-peaked and too complex for moment analysis; pixel-by-pixel Gaussian decomposition (SCOUSEPY) and PPV clustering (ACORNS) disentangle multiple velocity components along the line of sight.  
  > "examination of the data cube indicates that the spectra are in general too complex for the gas kinematics to be captured by a moment analysis." (3.2.2 N 2 H [+] (4-3)); "To analyse the gas kinematics, we performed pixel-by-pixel Gaussian decomposition of the N 2 H [+] spectra using SCOUSEPY and hierarchical clustering of the extracted velocity components using ACORNS." (abstract)
- Alternative: Moment analysis for gas kinematics: rejected because spectra have multiple velocity components and absorption features.  
  > "The intensity-weighted velocity (moment 1) map of the N 2 H [+] (43) emission is shown in Figure 2c; however, examination of the data cube indicates that the spectra are in general too complex for the gas kinematics to be captured by a moment analysis." (3.2.2 N 2 H [+] (4-3))
- Alternative: Expanding shell driven by protostellar feedback as the cause of the Branch 8 velocity gradient: rejected because MM2 is at an early evolutionary stage and its west-east bipolar outflow is perpendicular to the south-north velocity gradient.  
  > "Compared with the SW core, MM2 is on an earlier evolutionary stage; its protostellar feedback is thus less likely to drive an expanding shell of dense gas with a velocity gradient of ∼10.5 km s [−][1] pc [−][1], which is higher than that reported by Henshaw et al. (2014) by a factor of ∼4. In addition, the observed ∼south-north velocity gradient in Branch 8 is unlikely to be caused by the ∼west-east bipolar outflow associated with MM2 (see Figure 7)." (6.2 Mass inflow rate)
- Alternative: Full-cube multi-component hyperfine structure fitting: rejected for the full cube because T_ex and tau are poorly constrained in complex spectra with >2 components.  
  > "This approach achieves good fits for spectra with one or two velocity components identified by Gaussian decomposition, but T 𝑒𝑥 and 𝜏 are poorly constrained for many components in more complex spectra." (7.3 Limitations and Future Work)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Missing short-spacing and zero-spacing information causes negative bowls in N2H+ spectra and cannot be quantified with existing data.  
  > "In addition to negative bowls associated with missing short-spacing information (panel D, see also Section 2), N 2 H [+] (4-3) absorption is detected against the submillimetre continuum towards MM2" (3.2.2 N 2 H [+] (4-3)); "The effects of missing short-spacing information cannot be quantified with existing data (since no short-spacing N 2 H [+] (4-3) observations are available) and this is a limitation of the present study." (7.3 Limitations and Future Work)
- Limitation (authors): Gaussian decomposition ignores hyperfine structure, overestimating intrinsic N2H+ line widths by ~37% on average (though centroid velocities agree within 0.1%).  
  > "In this limited sample, we find that in our data Gaussian Decomposition can overestimate the line width by 37% on average, while the fitted centroid velocities are consistent to within 0.1%." (7.3 Limitations and Future Work)
- Limitation (authors): SCOUSEPY does not model absorption features, which are present towards the MM2 continuum peak.  
  > "We note that SCOUSEPY is not designed to reproduce absorption and that Gaussian Decomposition is applied only to the N 2 H [+] emission." (4.1 Gaussian Decomposition with SCOUSEPY)
- Limitation (authors): Continuum flux and mass estimates for MM2 and Branch 8 do not separate the compact core emission from the background filament, and mass inflow rates have a factor of ~2 systematic uncertainty plus inclination and temperature uncertainties.  
  > "We estimate a factor of ∼2 systematic uncertainty, encompassing the uncertainties in the adopted 𝜅 and gas-to-dust mass ratio and the uncertainty associated with the isothermal assumption" (6.2 Mass inflow rate); "We also note that we have not attempted to impose a boundary between 0.82 mm continuum emission associated with the filament and with the MM2 core." (6.2 Mass inflow rate)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Direction of gas flow (inflow vs outflow along the filament) depends on the unknown 3D orientation/inclination of the filament relative to the plane of the sky.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line-of-sight confusion and projection effects in PPV space can create artificial coherent structures ('fibres') that do not correspond to 3D physical filaments.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Branch 8 extends close (~1700 AU) to the massive proto-O star MM1 (~40 M_sun), so the measured velocity gradient may be partially driven by MM1's gravitational potential rather than solely accreting onto MM2.
