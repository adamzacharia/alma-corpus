## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure continuum flux and size**: Elliptical Gaussian fit to visibilities; imfit 2D Gaussian for GSS30 sources  
  > "Phase centers and continuum fluxes are derived by fitting an elliptical Gaussian to the visibilities." (3.1 Dust continuum)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with single-dish and previous interferometric fluxes  
  > "Our flux densities are consistent with those values reported by (cite: delavillarmois19) for Elias 29 and GSS30I1." (3.1 Dust continuum)
2. **Derive 3 sigma upper limits to integrated line flux**: Spectrum over dust-disk mask and 25 au circular mask; noise scaled by pixels per beam; Gaussian line of FWHM 1 km/s; 3 sigma limit  
  > "An upper limit is set at 3$\sigma$ where $\sigma = \sigma_{\upsilon} \sqrt{N_{\rm chan}} \delta \upsilon$ in Jy km s$^{-1}$" (3.2 Water lines)
   - parameter: Line FWHM 1 km/s  
  > "corresponds to a Gaussian linewidth ($FWHM$) of 1 km s$^{-1}$ based on the width of the H$_2^{18}$O line observed toward the Class 0 objects" (3.2 Water lines)
   - assumption: Gaussian line profile  
  > "we assume that the underlying line profile is Gaussian" (3.2 Water lines)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stacking and matched filtering found no emission  
  > "Additional stacking analysis on the image plane (cite: flong17) and matched filtering (cite: loomis18) did not extract any water emission" (3.2 Water lines)
3. **Derive total disk masses**: Single-temperature dust mass from continuum flux; also power-law disk fits to visibilities after subtracting envelope  
  > "with a dust temperature $T_{\rm dust}$ of 30 K and gas-to-dust ratio of 100 to obtain the total disk mass (gas and dust)" (4.1)
   - software: RATRAN, DUSTY, GILDAS, CASA
   - parameter: q = 0.35, 0.4, 0.5; Sigma ~ R^-1  
  > "for a power-law index $q=0.4$, which is expected for an irradiated embedded disk" (4.1.1)
   - assumption: T_dust = 30 K, gas-to-dust 100, kappa 0.7-2.4 (204 GHz) and 2.2-5.0 (397 GHz) cm2/g  
  > "We explore a range of dust opacities ($\kappa_{\nu}$ between 0.7–2.4 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 204 GHz and 2.2–5.0 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 397 GHz" (4.1)
   - fitting: Greedy least-squares fit to visibilities  
  > "The best-fit of parameters are determined by performing a greedy least square fit to the observed visibilities." (Modelling of the disk structure)
   - validation: Power-law masses within a factor 2 of single-temperature masses  
  > "we obtain similar (within a factor of 2) disk masses" (4.1.1)
4. **Derive H2 18O column density and abundance upper limits**: Optically thin, thermalized emission; divide by N_H2 from disk mass; inner warm disk uses mass within 25 au (~25% of disk mass) or 100 K mass  
  > "An estimate is obtained by considering thermalized and optically thin water emission" (4.2)
   - parameter: T_ex = 200 K; 16O/18O = 540; mu_H2 = 2.8  
  > "an excitation temperature $T_{\rm ex}$ of 200 K (cite: coutens14)" (4.2)
   - assumption: 16O/18O = 540, ortho-to-para = 3  
  > "adopting a / = 540 (cite: wilson94)" (4.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Test optical depth/dust effects on predicted emission**: Power-law disk model with optically thick source functions for small vs large grains; comparison with Harsono 2015 models and Bosman model  
  > "Optically thick source functions are used to estimate the strength of the line and their emitting regions." (5.4)
   - assumption: Disk mass 0.03 Msun, gas-to-dust 100, water 1e-4 where T>100 K, kappa 0.7 vs 2 cm2/g  
  > "The water is assumed to be abundant ($10^{-4}$ w.r.t. ) where $T_{\rm dust} > 100$ K." (5.4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- No H2 18O emission detected toward any of the five Class I objects  
  > "No H$_2^{18}$O emission is detected toward any of our five Class I disks." (abstract)
- Class I water column density upper limits are at least two orders of magnitude lower than Class 0 disk-like structures  
  > "Upper limits to the water vapor column densities in Class I disks are at least two orders magnitude lower than values found in Class 0 disk-like structures." (abstract)

## Claim tuples

- H: Do Class I disks have abundant warm water vapor? | E: H2 18O 203 and 390 GHz observations of five Class I objects | R: No detection; disk-averaged abundance <~1e-6 | F: Water vapor is not abundant in warm Class I envelopes/disks on 100 au scales | **supported**
- H: Why is water not detected? | E: Power-law disk emission models with small and large grains | R: Large grains reduce the emitting region; line optically thick and beam diluted | F: Large dust grains plausibly suppress emission, consistent with hidden water inside 10 au | **partially**

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Same column-density method as Class 0 studies for comparison  
  > "In order to compare with previous water detections toward Class 0 objects (cite: jorgensen10a; persson12), a similar approach is adopted" (1)
- Alternative: Steeper surface density slope (R^-1.75) rejected as solution  
  > "Thus, a steeper power-law profile is not the solution." (5.4)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): GSS30 observed with incorrect phase center at ~10% of primary beam, giving much lower sensitivity  
  > "the observations toward the GSS30 system used an incorrect phase center such that the objects are located $\sim$12$''$ away" (2.3 ALMA observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits depend on assumed 1 km/s Gaussian line width and T_ex = 200 K
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disk masses from 750 um dust likely lower limits (optically thick)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Large-grain explanation not directly tested with data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Short ALMA integration (9.56 min)
