3. **Measure integrated line intensities**: Form moment-0 maps for each transition group, smooth, clip and measure velocity-integrated intensities  
  > "The moment-0 maps present the spatial distribution of line intensities integrated over velocity for each transition group" (Moment-0 maps); "All the moment-0 maps are subsequently smoothed to a common resolution of $0\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}8$ and applied with a $3.0 \sigma$ threshold clipping" (Moment-0 maps)

   - parameter: A- J_K = 2_-1 - 2_0: narrower integration range -130–64 km/s to avoid SO  
  > "The only exception is for the A- $J_K = 2_{-1}-2_{0}$ line, which has an SO $J = 7_{8}-6_{7}$ line nearby" (Moment-0 maps); "the velocity integration was performed in a narrower range ($-130-64$ km/s) to not exceed the midpoint between the target line and the SO line." (Moment-0 maps)
   - assumption: Use the NGC 253 contaminant contribution and uncertainty as an estimate for NGC 1068 at 145 GHz  
  > "we use an existing estimate of the contribution of this particular c- transition and the associated uncertainty based on observations towards another nearby galaxy, NGC 253 (cite: ALCHEMI_main_2021), as an estimate for NGC 1068." (Moment-0 maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat inference without the contamination correction; inferred gas properties remain consistent  
  > "We have verified the above with a separate set of gas property inference with no contamination" (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC)); "the inferred gas properties remain consistent with the case presented in this work (i.e. with aggressive correction), and the observed versus predicted line intensity are consistent." (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
4. **Infer gas excitation and isomer column densities**: Non-LTE RADEX modeling through SpectralRadex; compare summed predictions for blended transition groups with observed intensities  
  > "For the non-LTE analysis, we use the radiative transfer code `RADEX` (cite: radex_vandertak_2007) via the Python package SpectralRadex[^4] (cite: Holdship+2021_SpectralRadex)." (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "comparing the summation of the predicted line intensities from all blending lines in each "transition group" with the observed line intensity for each specific measurement." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
   - software: RADEX, SpectralRadex, emcee, UltraNest, MLFriends
   - parameter: Gas density prior: 10^2–10^8 cm^-3, log-uniform  
  > "| Gas density, $n_{\rm H2}$ [cm^-3^] |  $10^{2}-10^{8}$  |    Log-uniform    |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Gas temperature prior: 10–800 K, uniform  
  > "| Gas temperature, $T_{\rm kin}$ [K] |     $10-800$      |      Uniform      |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: N(A-) prior: 10^12–10^18 cm^-2, log-uniform  
  > "|          $N$(A-) [cm^-2^]          | $10^{12}-10^{18}$ |    Log-uniform    |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: N(E-) prior: 10^12–10^18 cm^-2, log-uniform  
  > "|          $N$(E-) [cm^-2^]          | $10^{12}-10^{18}$ |    Log-uniform    |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Beam filling factor prior: 0.0–1.0, uniform  
  > "|   Beam filling factor, $\eta_{ff}$   |     $0.0-1.0$     |      Uniform      |" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Measured intensity uncertainty is Gaussian  
  > "we assume that the uncertainty on our measured intensities is Gaussian" (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
   - assumption: Both A- and E- column densities are free parameters  
  > "The species column density from both isomers, $N_{A-}$ and $N_{E-}$, are, therefore, also explored as free parameters." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
   - fitting: Bayesian inference with emcee MCMC; uniform/log-uniform priors; likelihood ∝ exp(-χ²/2)  
  > "We coupled the RADEX modeling with the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler `emcee` (cite: FM+2013_emcee) to perform a Bayesian inference of the parameter probability distributions" (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "We assume priors of uniform or log-uniform distribution within the determined ranges" (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "our likelihood is given by $P(\theta | d) \sim \exp(-\frac{1}{2}\chi^2),$" (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
   - validation: Alternative UltraNest/MLFriends inference also failed to constrain gas temperature  
  > "a nested sampling Monte Carlo algorithm MLFriends (cite: ultranest16; ultranest19) using the `UltraNest` package" (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "The inference results with `UltraNest` still failed to constrain the gas temperature." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
5. **Check posterior predictions against the data**: Posterior predictive check comparing predicted and observed velocity-integrated intensities  
  > "We do this by performing a comparison of the distribution of the observational data, the measured velocity-integrated intensities, to the predicted distribution described by our Bayesian model." (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Sample predicted intensities from posteriors between 16–84 percentiles  
  > "We sample the predicted line intensities from our posteriors between 16-84 percentiles, and plot these against the observed line intensities." (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 145 GHz predictions systematically exceed corrected observations; removing the correction gives consistent inferred gas properties and observed/predicted intensities  
  > "in the 145 GHz group, the predicted line intensities are systematically higher than the observed intensities after correcting the line contamination" (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC)); "the inferred gas properties remain consistent with the case presented in this work (i.e. with aggressive correction), and the observed versus predicted line intensity are consistent." (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
6. **Assess the shock interpretation**: Compare inferred gas conditions with published UCLCHEM models and with SiO/HNCO spatial distributions and gas properties  
  > "Based on the chemical modeling with `UCLCHEM` for the physical condition of the CND of NGC 1068 performed by (cite: Huang_Viti_2023), both fast and slow shocks can enhance the methanol fractional abundance to comparable levels" (Are the gas properties consistent with shocks?); "We compared both spatial distribution of emissions from multiple tracers of different types of shocks (SiO and HNCO) versus , and the gas properties traced by these three species." (Conclusions)
   - assumption: Published no-shock models require T ≳ 100 K for comparable abundance enhancement  
  > "in the chemical models without shocks, a higher gas temperature is required (such that water ice sublimates, $T\gtrsim 100$ K) to boost the abundance to comparable levels (cite: Huang_Viti_2023)." (Are the gas properties consistent with shocks?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Temperature constraints cannot distinguish shock scenarios  
  > "Unfortunately in all four CND regions explored, none of the gas temperatures are constrained enough to discern among shock scenarios." (Are the gas properties consistent with shocks?)
7. **Interpret the E/A column-density ratio**: Inspect inferred N(E-)/N(A-) ratios and compare with Galactic and extragalactic measurements  
  > "In our measured $N_{E-}/N_{A-}$ ratio, the most probable ratio values are fairly close to unity" (E-/A- Ratio); "This is interestingly different from the generally expected overabundance of A- (cite: Wirstrom+2011) and the observed $N_{E-}/N_{A-}$ ratio from Galactic outflows reported by (cite: Holdship+2019)." (E-/A- Ratio)
   - assumption: A- and E- isomers formed and well mixed in the same environment; equilibrium E/A ratio linked to formation temperature  
  > "By assuming A- and E- isomers are formed and well mixed in the same environment, the equilibrium E/A ratio is linked to the temperature of the formation environment (cite: Wirstrom+2011; Holdship+2019)." (E-/A- Ratio)
   - assumption: Isomer abundance ratio fixed at formation because interconversion is strongly forbidden  
  > "Generally the conversion of one isomer to another is considered a strongly forbidden process; the abundance ratio of the two isomers is therefore often assumed to be fixed at the time of the formation" (E-/A- Ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Formation-temperature interpretation remains highly uncertain because inferred isomer column-density uncertainties are large  
  > "Note that the uncertainty derived from the inferred column densities of both isomers remain fairly large, so this is still highly uncertain." (E-/A- Ratio)

## Qualitative findings

- Gas density and A-/E-isomer column densities are generally well constrained; gas temperature is not  
  > "Generally the gas densities and the molecular column densities of both A- and E- isomers are well constrained." (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "The gas kinetic temperature, however, cannot be well constrained in any of the CND regions." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
- Methanol is concentrated toward the east of the CND, similar to SiO; HNCO is more extended  
  > "it is more concentrated towards the east of the CND and much less prominent in the west of the CND." (Comparison with other shock tracers - SiO and HNCO); "This is similar to the SiO emission reported by (cite: Huang+2022)." (Comparison with other shock tracers - SiO and HNCO); "In contrast, the HNCO reported by (cite: Huang+2022), even in the higher-J level (HNCO 6-5) is much more extended throughout the CND." (Comparison with other shock tracers - SiO and HNCO)
- Most probable E/A ratios are fairly close to unity  
  > "In our measured $N_{E-}/N_{A-}$ ratio, the most probable ratio values are fairly close to unity" (E-/A- Ratio)
- The adopted 145 GHz contamination correction may be too aggressive  
  > "This could suggest that this correction may be too aggressive so that it underestimates the true emission contribution" (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High spatial resolution probes GMC scales; multiple transitions span excitation energies needed to characterize the gas  
  > "This high spatial resolution provides physical scales comparable to giant molecular clouds (GMCs)." (Introduction); "Observations of transitions covering a range of excitation energies will be critical in quantitatively characterize the gas properties" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Line blending prevents individual-component separation; sum all blended-line predictions in non-LTE modeling  
  > "blending from different transitions is prevalent (both from A- and E- ) and hard to disentangle by individual component fitting" (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "comparing the summation of the predicted line intensities from all blending lines in each "transition group" with the observed line intensity for each specific measurement." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
- Alternative: Optically thin LTE rotation diagrams require uncontaminated individual transitions, which the blended observations do not provide  
  > "Such approach relies not only on the validity of the assumption of optically thin LTE, but also requires each transition to be free of contamination from other lines." (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "blending from different transitions is prevalent (both from A- and E- ) and hard to disentangle by individual component fitting" (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
- Alternative: Thermalize the gas and redistribute blended intensity among transitions; considered as an alternative  
  > "One option is to assume thermalization of the gas and to re-distribute the measured line intensity to all transitions involved in the line blending (cite: Martin+2006)." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
- Alternative: UltraNest with MLFriends tested instead of emcee; temperature remained unconstrained  
  > "a nested sampling Monte Carlo algorithm MLFriends (cite: ultranest16; ultranest19) using the `UltraNest` package" (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "The inference results with `UltraNest` still failed to constrain the gas temperature." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
- Alternative: Repeat gas-property inference without contamination correction; results remain consistent  
  > "We have verified the above with a separate set of gas property inference with no contamination" (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC)); "the inferred gas properties remain consistent with the case presented in this work (i.e. with aggressive correction)" (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
- How they decided it was enough: Check posterior-predicted intensities against observations, test another sampler and repeat inference without the contamination correction  
  > "This is to verify our posterior distribution produces a distribution for the data that is consistent with the actual data." (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC)); "The inference results with `UltraNest` still failed to constrain the gas temperature." (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "the inferred gas properties remain consistent with the case presented in this work (i.e. with aggressive correction), and the observed versus predicted line intensity are consistent." (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
- Limitation (authors): A- and E-isomer lines cannot be disentangled by individual-component fitting  
  > "we can not disentangle one from another by individual component fitting." (Line blending between E- and A- isomers)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature is poorly constrained; excitation-energy coverage may be insufficient and higher-Eu transitions may be needed  
  > "it is possible that the coverage of the excitation energy levels is not wide enough to fully constrain the gas temperature." (Inference of gas properties in the CND); "We speculate that transitions of higher $E_{u}$ may be needed to properly constrain gas temperature." (Inference of gas properties in the CND)
- Limitation (authors): The contamination estimate comes from a starburst galaxy unlike the AGN-dominated target  
  > "While we note that NGC 253 (a starburst) is very different from NGC 1068 (an AGN dominated galaxy)" (Moment-0 maps)
- Limitation (authors): The 145 GHz contamination correction may underestimate the true emission  
  > "This could suggest that this correction may be too aggressive so that it underestimates the true emission contribution" (Comparison of the predicted intensity from RADEX with observed values: A posterior predictive check (PPC))
- Limitation (authors): Gas temperatures cannot discriminate among shock scenarios  
  > "Unfortunately in all four CND regions explored, none of the gas temperatures are constrained enough to discern among shock scenarios." (Are the gas properties consistent with shocks?)
- Limitation (authors): Formation-temperature interpretation from E/A ratios is highly uncertain  
  > "Note that the uncertainty derived from the inferred column densities of both isomers remain fairly large, so this is still highly uncertain." (E-/A- Ratio)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The shock-origin argument depends on temperatures that the fit does not constrain well.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Transferring a contamination correction between different galaxies can introduce systematic error beyond its quoted uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Marginal column-density summaries alone cannot establish the E/A ratio posterior without their covariance.
