- Fields: transients
- Question: Why were optical afterglows without detected GRBs (AT 2023lcr, AT 2020blt, AT 2021any, AT 2021lfa) not seen with a GRB: off-axis jet, low initial Lorentz factor, or on-axis classical GRB missed by satellites?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Obtain Band 3 flux density and position of AT 2023lcr from ALMA**: ALMA data calibrated and imaged with the automated CASA-based pipeline; source detected in both epochs  
  > "Data were calibrated and imaged using the automated CASA-based pipeline (cite: CASA2022)." (2.4 Radio)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Band 3 (100 GHz), epochs 2023 June 29 and 2023 July 04  
  > "Both observations were in Band 3 (100 GHz) and yielded a detection with a centroid position of" (2.4 Radio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: ALMA centroid compared with the optical position  
  > "consistent with the optical position." (2.4 Radio)
2. **Fit optical light curve and measure optical spectral index**: Multi-band g, r, i, z extinction-corrected light curves fit with a smoothed broken power law with constant magnitude offsets between bands; MCMC fit  
  > "We fit the $g$-, $r$-, $i$-, and $z$-band extinction-corrected light curves using the following smoothed broken power law function (cite: Beuermann1999; Zeh2006)" (3.1 Optical); "From a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) fit, we find" (3.1 Optical)
   - parameter: smoothness n fixed to 2; t_0 allowed between last GOTO non-detection and first GOTO detection  
  > "We fixed $n=2$ and allowed $t_0$ to vary between the time of the last GOTO non-detection and the first GOTO detection." (3.1 Optical)
   - parameter: GOTO L-band point treated as the average of r- and g-band fluxes  
  > "We treat the GOTO $L$-band point as the average of the $r$- and $g$-band fluxes at that time." (3.1 Optical)
   - assumption: no host-galaxy or supernova contribution  
  > "We assume no contribution from the host galaxy or the underlying SN." (3.1 Optical)
   - fitting: MCMC; sampler and priors not named for this fit  
  > "From a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) fit, we find" (3.1 Optical)
   - validation: spectral index checked against the optical spectrum  
  > "consistent with the spectral index measured from the optical spectrum" (3.1 Optical)
3. **Measure X-ray spectral and temporal indices**: Swift/XRT detections fit simultaneously with the online Swift tool; Chandra spectrum fit with sherpa (absorbed power law, 0.5-6 keV); X-ray light curve fit with a single power law at 1 keV  
  > "Fitting the detections in the three epochs simultaneously using the online Swift tool (cite: Evans2007; Evans2009)" (2.3 X-rays); "was used to fit the spectrum in the range 0.5–6 keV, with the background subtracted, using a model with photoelectric absorption and a single-component power law" (2.3 X-rays); "We fit a single power law to the X-ray light curve of AT 2023lcr at 1 keV" (3.2 X-rays)
   - software: Swift online XRT tool, CIAO, sherpa
   - parameter: Galactic N_H = 4.12e20 cm^-2; Gamma = 2 adopted for the count-rate to flux conversion  
  > "with a Galactic hydrogen column density of $N_H=4.12\times10^{20}\,$cm$^{-2}$" (2.3 X-rays); "so for consistency we also adopt $\Gamma=2$." (2.3 X-rays)
   - parameter: Chandra flux multiplied by 1.77 to go from 0.5-6 keV to 0.3-10 keV  
  > "has been multiplied by a factor of 1.77 to correct from the 0.5–6 keV band to the 0.3–10 keV band" (2.3 X-rays)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Chandra photon index compared with the Swift value  
  > "consistent with the value from *Swift* but with much larger uncertainties" (2.3 X-rays)
4. **Derive analytical limits on the jet Lorentz factor**: Limits from deceleration time (rise time), radio spectrum (Gamma_avg) and interstellar scintillation size limits, following Perley et al. 2024  
  > "We follow (cite: Perley_2024) to obtain analytical estimates on the afterglow bulk Lorentz factors" (5.1 Analytical Constraints on the Lorentz Factors)
   - parameter: t_dec approximated by t_rise; upper limit on t_rise from first detection minus last non-detection  
  > "Therefore, we approximate $t_\text{dec} \approx t_\text{rise}$, where $t_\text{rise}$ is the afterglow rise time." (5.1 Analytical Constraints on the Lorentz Factors)
   - assumption: typical rest-frame deceleration time of about 200 s; uniform circumburst density; full filling factor  
  > "We assume a typical rest-frame deceleration time $t_\text{dec,rest} \approx t_\text{rise,rest} \approx 200$ s" (Table 3 caption); "We assume a full filling factor and use the time of the last non-detection as a lower limit on the explosion time." (5.1 Analytical Constraints on the Lorentz Factors)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: posterior Gamma_0 compared with the analytical limits  
  > "We also note that our posterior $\Gamma_0 \approx 166$ is consistent with analytical estimates from Section" (5.2 AT2023lcr)
5. **MCMC afterglow modelling of the radio, optical and X-ray data**: afterglowpy jet models (top hat, Gaussian, power law) fit with the emcee EnsembleSampler to radio, optical and X-ray data, for several data subsets and afterglowpy settings  
  > "We use `afterglowpy` (version 0.7.3; (cite: Ryan_2020)) and MCMC methods in `emcee` (version 3.1.4; (cite: Foreman_Mackey_2013)) to fit the radio, optical, and X-ray observations of AT 2023lcr" (4.1 Settings); "We fit each model only to optical observations, then only to optical and X-ray observations, then to all radio, optical, and X-ray observations" (4.1 Settings)
   - software: afterglowpy, emcee
   - parameter: most runs: 64 walkers, 75,000 iterations, 30,000 burn-in; if not converged: 64 walkers, 225,000 iterations, 125,000 burn-in  
  > "We perform most runs using 64 walkers and 75,000 iterations, discarding 30,000 iterations as the burn-in." (4.1 Settings); "If samples do not appear converged with these settings, we run using 64 walkers and 225,000 iterations, discarding 125,000 as the burn-in." (4.1 Settings)
   - parameter: broad uniform priors (Table of priors, e.g. log10 Gamma_0 in [0,3], theta_c in [0.02,0.78] rad, p in [2,3])  
  > "To minimize bias, our priors are broad and uniform" (4.1 Settings)
   - parameter: afterglowpy runs repeated with and without ICC and with Gamma_0 infinite and finite  
  > "To work around this, we run MCMC multiple times, with each combination: with and without ICC; and with $\Gamma_0 = \infty$ and $\Gamma_0 \neq \infty$." (4.2 afterglowpy Limitations)
   - parameter: X-ray fluxes converted to a 5 keV flux density with spectral index beta = -1  
  > "For all afterglows, we converted X-ray fluxes to a 5 keV flux density assuming a spectral index of $\beta = -1$, where $f_\nu \propto \nu^\beta$." (4.1 Settings)
   - assumption: homogeneous circumburst medium, single-shell approximation  
  > "to model a blast wave propagating through a homogeneous circumburst medium (cite: Ryan_2020)" (4.2 afterglowpy Limitations)
   - assumption: Galactic extinction with R_V = 3.1; redshift z = 1.0272 for AT 2023lcr  
  > "Optical observations were corrected for Galactic extinction with $E(B-V)=A_V/R_V$, assuming $R_V = 3.1$ (cite: Schlegel_1998)." (4.1 Settings); "we adopt $z=1.0272$ as the redshift of AT 2023lcr" (2.2 Optical Spectroscopy)
   - fitting: emcee EnsembleSampler, uniform priors, simple Gaussian likelihood per data point; radio upper limits for AT 2020blt penalized against a zero-flux point  
  > "We use a simple Gaussian likelihood for each data point." (4.1 Settings); "For AT2020blt, we penalize samples that do not satisfy radio upper limits by finding the log likelihood between the sample-generated radio data point and a zero flux point." (4.1 Settings)
   - validation: model compared with observations and with earlier modelling of the same events  
  > "The model is able to reproduce key features of all observations" (5.2 AT2023lcr)
6. **Compare configurations with goodness-of-fit metrics**: reduced chi^2 (minimum over 5,000 posterior samples) and WAIC-based elpd  
  > "In the results that follow, we report the minimum $\chi^2$ over 5,000 randomly selected posterior samples." (4.3 Goodness of Fit Metrics); "We also use the Widely Available Information Criterion (WAIC; (cite: Watanabe_2010; Cunningham_2020))" (4.3 Goodness of Fit Metrics)
   - parameter: 5% systematic error on VLA flux densities at 1-18 GHz and 15% at 18-40 GHz  
  > "we include a $5\%$ systematic error on the flux densities for the L, S, C, X, and Ku-bands (1-18 GHz), and a $15\%$ systematic error for the K and Ka-bands (18-40 GHz)" (4.3 Goodness of Fit Metrics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: elpd and chi^2/DoF used to choose among models  
  > "We report the result of other structures only if the afterglow is inconsistent with an on-axis top hat jet (by eye, or has a $\chi^2$/DoF significantly worse than other models)." (4.1 Settings)
7. **Compute gamma-ray energy limits and radiative efficiency**: KONUS-Wind limits converted to isotropic-equivalent luminosity and energy; eta_gamma = E_gamma,iso / (E_K,iso + E_gamma,iso) using E_K,iso from the model  
  > "where $E_\text{K,iso}$ is the isotropic-equivalent kinetic energy of the jet, found from modeling." (4.4 Radiative Efficiencies); "Assuming a similar scaling over longer time intervals (an observed duration of 40 s as in (cite: Perley_2024))" (2.5 Search for Gamma-ray Emission)
   - parameter: evaluated at z = 1.0272 and at the redshift upper limit z = 1.6  
  > "We perform the same computation at the redshift upper limit of AT 2023lcr ($z=1.6$)" (2.5 Search for Gamma-ray Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: efficiency compared with the LGRB population of Racusin et al. 2011  
  > "which is consistent with typical LGRB efficiencies (cite: Racusin_2011)" (5.2 AT2023lcr)

## Qualitative findings

- Early-time (rising-phase) optical data are needed to constrain the initial Lorentz factor and the origin of an orphan afterglow  
  > "Since early-time observations are more sensitive to initial physical conditions, such as the initial Lorentz factor, being able to capture early-time emission is extremely important to constraining an afterglow’s origin." (6 Conclusion)
- AT 2023lcr, AT 2021lfa and AT 2019pim all show significant interstellar scintillation below about 10 GHz  
  > "AT 2023lcr, AT 2021lfa, and AT 2019pim all exhibit significant scintillation at $\nu \lesssim 10$ GHz." (3.3 Radio)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Broad uniform priors chosen to minimize bias; each afterglow first described with the simplest structure, an on-axis top hat jet  
  > "To minimize bias, our priors are broad and uniform" (4.1 Settings); "we attempt to describe each afterglow with the simplest possible structure (an on-axis top hat jet)" (4.1 Settings)
- Alternative: Off-axis jet, low initial Lorentz factor, and on-axis classical GRB undetected by satellites were each tested  
  > "1) the jet was off-axis; 2) the jet had a low initial Lorentz factor; and 3) the afterglow was the result of an on-axis classical GRB" (abstract)
- Alternative: Models with finite Gamma_0 for AT 2020blt were inconsistent with the 25 d radio non-detection; Gamma_0 = infinity fits for AT 2021lfa struggled with the MASTER rising-phase data  
  > "models with a finite $\Gamma_0$ were inconsistent with the $\sim25$ d radio non-detection by a factor of 3" (5.3 AT2020blt); "We note that $\Gamma_0 = \infty$ configurations struggled to reproduce the MASTER observations." (5.5 AT2021lfa)
- How they decided it was enough: Longer runs (225,000 iterations) used when the standard 75,000-iteration chains did not appear converged  
  > "If samples do not appear converged with these settings, we run using 64 walkers and 225,000 iterations, discarding 125,000 as the burn-in." (4.1 Settings)
- Limitation (authors): afterglowpy lacks reverse-shock physics, which may affect radio observations  
  > "In addition to the above, `afterglowpy` does not support reverse shock physics, which might particularly affect radio observations." (4.2 afterglowpy Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): afterglowpy has no synchrotron self-absorption, so low-frequency radio data are overpredicted  
  > "Also, `afterglowpy` lacks support for synchrotron self-absorption." (4.2 afterglowpy Limitations); "However, the model somewhat overestimates radio observations in the radio L-band (1.39 GHz) and S-band (3.75 GHz)" (5.2 AT2023lcr)
- Limitation (authors): afterglowpy supports only a homogeneous medium, with no stellar wind  
  > "Finally, `afterglowpy` implements a homogeneous circumburst medium, with no support for a stellar wind medium." (4.2 afterglowpy Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Sparse early-time data leave viewing-angle and Lorentz-factor degeneracies unresolved  
  > "Ultimately, we lack the early-time data to resolve the viewing angle degeneracy." (5.3 AT2020blt)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA reduction is described in two sentences; no calibrators, beam, rms, flux-scale uncertainty or measurement method are given, so the 140 and 94 microJy values cannot be checked from the text.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Burn-in is 30,000 iterations in the text but table captions for 75,000-iteration runs say 25,000 were discarded.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The AT 2023lcr fit has chi^2/DoF of 14.0, which indicates a poor formal fit; conclusions rest on by-eye comparison and WAIC.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Redshift z = 1.0272 is only a lower limit from absorption lines, so the distance-dependent energies carry that systematic.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Tabulated redshift for AT 2021lfa (1.063) and the text value (1.0624) differ.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA points are only 4 of many radio points, so their individual influence on the fit is not isolated.
