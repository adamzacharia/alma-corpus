   - software: CASA
   - parameter: FWHM resolution = 12''  
  > "We compared the integrated intensities of IRAM and ALMA CO data that are smoothed to match the FWHM resolution of $12"$." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: IRAM 30-m forward efficiency = 0.92, main beam efficiency = 0.56  
  > "Following the methods of (cite: 2014A&A...567A.118D), we applied a forward efficiency of 0.92 and a main beam efficiency of 0.56 to convert the main beam temperature scale." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: No significant difference found within error margin; missing flux considered negligible  
  > "As a result, We found no significant difference within the error margin, and hence the missing flux density is considered to be negligible." (Observations and Data Reduction)
2. **Create moment maps, velocity channel maps, and position-velocity diagrams, and identify millimeter continuum sources**: Generate moment 0, 1, and 2 maps, velocity channel maps, and R.A.-integrated PV diagrams of CO lines; detect 1.3 mm continuum sources at >= 5 sigma and compute source sizes from 5 sigma contour area  
  > "Figures 2a, 2d, and 2g show the maps of moment 0 (integrated intensity) for each line emission toward M33GMC 37." M = m_{\mathrm{H}} \mu D^2 \Omega \sum_{i} [N_i(\mathrm{H}_2)],\\

\end{eqnarray}$$" (Results); "The total mass is estimated to be $\sim2.4 \times 10^5$ $M_\odot$ for the blue cloud and $\sim1.9 \times 10^5$ $M_\odot$ for the red cloud using equations (1) and (2)." (Results)

5. **Test whether stellar wind feedback can explain the cloud velocity separation**: Compare estimated total stellar wind momentum of ten O-type stars with the total expanding momentum of the two molecular clouds  
  > "First, we claim that the total momentum of stellar winds is significantly lower than that of the two molecular clouds." (Can stellar feedback explain the large velocity separation of the two clouds?)
   - parameter: Expanding velocity ~ 3 km s^-1 (half the velocity separation of the two clouds)  
  > "Here we assume that the two clouds show an expanding motion with the expanding velocity of $\sim$3 km s$^{-1}$, corresponding to a half value of the velocity separation of the two clouds." (Can stellar feedback explain the large velocity separation of the two clouds?)
   - assumption: 10 O-type stars, mass loss rate ~ 10^-6 M_sun yr^-1, wind duration ~ 10^6 yr, wind velocity ~ 2000 km s^-1  
  > "The typical wind momentum of an O-type star is calculated to be $\sim$$2 \times 10^{3}$ $M_\odot$ km s$^{-1}$, by considering a mass accretion rate of $\sim$$10^{-6}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$, a wind duration time of $\sim$10$^6$ yr, and a wind velocity of $\sim$2,000 km s$^{-1}$ (see (cite: 2000ARA&A..38..613K) and references therein)." (Can stellar feedback explain the large velocity separation of the two clouds?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked moment 0, PV diagram, and velocity channel maps for ring-like expanding shell signatures  
  > "We also have created velocity channel maps in order to justify the velocity and spatial distributions of clouds, because expanding clouds will be spatially shifted from inward to outward in the velocity channel maps (e.g., (cite: 2012ApJ...746...82F; 2017JHEAp..15....1S))." (Can stellar feedback explain the large velocity separation of the two clouds?)

## Qualitative findings

- Three 1.3 mm continuum sources (MMSs 1–3) are embedded within the densest C18O-bright parts of M33GMC 37 and correspond to up to ~10 high-mass stars of spectral types B0V–O7.5V.  
  > "Three continuum sources representing up to $\sim10$ high-mass stars with the spectral types of B0V–O7.5V are embedded within the densest parts of molecular clouds bright in the C$^{18}$O($J$ = 2–1) line emission." (Abstract)
- The blue and red clouds exhibit four observational signatures of cloud-cloud collision: supersonic velocity separation (~6 km s^-1), complementary spatial distribution with displacement (~6.2 pc), bridging feature in velocity space, and a V-shaped structure in the PV diagram.  
  > "To summarize, the red and blue clouds in M33GMC 37 fulfill four requirements of high-mass star formation triggered by cloud-cloud collisions as follows: (1) a super sonic velocity separation of two clouds, (2) complementary spatial distribution with a displacement of colliding clouds, (3) a presence of a bridging feature connecting the two clouds in velocity space, and (4) V-shaped structure in the position–velocity diagram." (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High spatial resolution (~2 pc) CO/isotopologue and continuum data are required to spatially resolve filamentary structures and cloud-cloud collision signatures in an external galaxy like M33.  
  > "We can therefore reveal sites of cloud-cloud collisions even in external galaxies if the spatial resolution of CO/Hi data is high enough (e.g., a few pc scales)." (Introduction); "Using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array, we spatially resolved filamentary structures of giant molecular cloud 37 in M33 using $^{12}$CO($J$ = 2–1), $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1), and C$^{18}$O($J$ = 2–1) line emission at a spatial resolution of $\sim2$ pc." (Abstract)
- Why this method: Calculating the spatial correlation coefficient as a function of displacement objectively evaluates complementary spatial distributions of colliding clouds when the collision angle is inclined to the line of sight.  
  > "To evaluate such complimentary distributions of colliding two clouds, it is useful to calculate the correlation coefficient between the intensity distributions of two clouds (c.f., S. Fujita et al. in preparation)." (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37); "By changing values of spatial displacement for one of the colliding clouds, we can obtain a map of correlation coefficient between the two clouds as a function of displacement." (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37)
- Alternative: Acceleration and expanding gas motion driven by stellar feedback (stellar winds and outflowing gas) from the embedded high-mass stars  
  > "In this section, we shall discuss whether the velocity separation of two clouds can be explained by acceleration due to the stellar feedback." (Can stellar feedback explain the large velocity separation of the two clouds?); "To summarize, the stellar feedback effect is negligible for the cloud kinematics, and therefore the velocity separation of two clouds cannot be explained by acceleration by stellar winds and outflowing gas." (Can stellar feedback explain the large velocity separation of the two clouds?)
- Alternative: Oblique cloud-cloud collision (rejected in favor of head-on collision due to presence of V-shaped PV structure)  
  > "Note that if the oblique collision—e.g., collisions with the edges of the clouds—occurred, we could not find such a V-shaped structure in the position–velocity diagram (cite: 2020PASJ..tmp..163F)." (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Further detailed photometric and spectroscopic observations are needed to clarify the exact number of high-mass stars and their spectral types (currently estimated assuming 10 stars of identical spectral type).  
  > "Further detailed photometric and spectroscopic observations are needed to clarify the number of high-mass stars and their spectral types." (Presence of high-mass stars and their spectral types)
- Limitation (authors): Velocity separation and collision timescale are subject to line-of-sight projection effects (collision angle assumed to be 45 degrees).  
  > "Although the velocity separation will be changed due to the projection effect, this is roughly consistent with the typical velocity separation in the Galactic high-mass star forming regions triggered by cloud-cloud collisions" (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37); "If the cloud-collision scenario is correct, we can also estimate the collision time scale as a dynamical time scale using the values of displacement and velocity difference of two clouds to be (spatial displacement) / (velocity difference) = 8.8 pc / 8.5 km s$^{-1}$ $\sim1$ Myr assuming the collision angle $\theta$ = 45 degree." (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37)
- Limitation (authors): The 13CO bridging feature at position A is not significantly detected because of the small velocity separation of the two colliding clouds.  
  > "In the case of M33GMC 37, the $V_\mathrm{LSR} \sim -135$ km s$^{-1}$ feature in the $^{13}$CO spectrum at the position A is possibly a bridging feature (see Figure 2b), but is not significantly detected because of the small velocity separation of the two colliding clouds." (An Alternative Idea: High-mass Star Formation Triggered by Cloud-Cloud Collisions in M33GMC 37)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The collision angle of 45 degrees is assumed without independent constraint, introducing roughly a factor of two uncertainty in the dynamical collision timescale.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The pixel size and exact spatial region over which the correlation coefficient (-0.44) and its P-value are computed are not fully specified, and spatial autocorrelation within the synthesized beam can inflate P-value significance if pixels are not independent.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO and the 12CO(2-1)/(1-0) ratio (~0.7) are applied uniformly across the GMC, although temperature and optical depth likely vary near the HII region and embedded continuum sources.
