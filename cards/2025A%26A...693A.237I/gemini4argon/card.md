## Analysis recipe

1. **Classify galaxies by multiplicity**: Visual classification into four categories (single, pair, multiple-UV, multiple-[C II]) based on HST/WFC3 F160W rest-frame UV images and ALMA [C II] line distributions.  
  > "In this study, we take a step forward to add information from the [C ii] line distribution to visually classify the CRISTAL sample into four categories" (3.1. Classification based on multiplicity)
   - parameter: projected angular separation < ~3 arcsec and velocity offset < 300 km/s for pairs; FIR S/N < 5 or overlapping UV blobs for multiple-UV  
  > "a pair of [C ii] line detections with a projected angular separation of less than ∼ 3 arcsec. The velocity offset among the pair is confirmed to be small (<300 km/s)" (3.1. Classification based on multiplicity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked against higher-resolution JWST images (Lines et al. 2024) and kinematical analyses (Posses et al. 2024).  
  > "three galaxies (CRISTAL-11, CRISTAL15, and CRISTAL-17) classified as single have pair-like morphology when looked with sharper images taken by James Webb Space Telescope" (3.1 Classification based on multiplicity)
2. **Extract [C II] spectra and create velocity-binned visibility datasets**: Extract 1D spectrum in a 1-arcsec aperture from the 20 km/s data cube, fit a 1D Gaussian (double Gaussian for CRISTAL-22a) to define the +-2 sigma (95% flux) velocity range, subtract continuum in uv-plane, and bin visibilities over this velocity range into a single channel.  
  > "(1) extract spectrum with a 1-arcsec aperture from the data cube (20 km/s width per channel)." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission); "contains 95 % (= [2.5 %, 97.5 %]) of the total [C ii] line flux, corresponding to 4σ (±2σ from the central frequency ν 0 ) width of Gaussian (≈ 1.7 Gaussian FWHM)." (3.2.1 [C ii] line emission); "We then binned the visibilities into a single channel by specifying `nchan` `=` `1` in the `CASA/mstransform` task." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - software: CASA/tclean, scipy (curve_fit), CASA/uvcontsub, CASA/mstransform
   - parameter: 1-arcsec aperture; 20 km/s channel width; +-2 sigma velocity range (95% flux); nchan = 1  
  > "(1) extract spectrum with a 1-arcsec aperture from the data cube (20 km/s width per channel)." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission); "We then binned the visibilities into a single channel by specifying `nchan` `=` `1` in the `CASA/mstransform` task." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - assumption: Single Gaussian line profile (double Gaussian for CRISTAL-22a)  
  > "As an exceptional case, we fit the [C ii] spectrum of CRISTAL22a, which shows a clear double-peaked line profile, with a double Gaussian." (3.2.1 [C ii] line emission)
   - fitting: 1D Gaussian fit using scipy curve_fit  
  > "(2) fit the extracted spectrum with a single Gaussian _f_(ν) ∝ exp[(ν − ν 0)2 /2σ2] using the `curve_fit` function within the `scipy` package (Virtanen et al. 2020)." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - validation: Visual inspection confirming the +-2 sigma velocity range includes almost all [C II] line emission.  
  > "The ±2σ velocity range includes almost all of the [C ii] line emission, which assures that the [C ii] size measurements will be performed by fitting the representative velocity range of the emission." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
3. **Measure [C II] structural parameters via visibility modeling**: Fit binned [C II] visibilities directly with 2D exponential disk models (1-component for single/multiple-UV, 2-component for pair/multiple-[C II]) and convert major-axis FWHM and axis ratio to circularized effective radius Re,[CII] and luminosity L_[CII].  
  > "We used the `UVMULTIFIT` (Martí-Vidal et al. 2014) to model the visibilities of the [C ii] line emission in the CRISTAL sample." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission); "For a 2-D exponential disk model, the major axis FWHM and major axis effective radius can be related as _R_ _e_,major = 1.21 × FWHM, therefore we obtained a circularized effective radius by calculating _R_ _e_,[CII] = 1.21 × FWHM √_q_, where _q_ is the minor-to-major axis ratio." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - software: UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: Sérsic index n = 1 (exponential disk); 6 free parameters (R.A., Decl., total flux density, major-axis FWHM, minor-to-major axis ratio q, position angle); q fixed to 1 when peak S/N < 8  
  > "exponential disk model ( _n_ = 1) for all of the [C ii] components and first allow six parameters (R.A., Decl., total flux density, major-axis FWHM, minorto-major axis ratio, and position angle of major axis) free." (3.2.1 [C ii] line emission)
   - assumption: 2D exponential disk profile (n = 1); standard LambdaCDM cosmology with H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "Throughout this paper, we assume a standard ΛCDM cosmology with _H_ 0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ω M = 0.3, and Ω Λ = 0.7." (1. Introduction); "We assumed a two-dimensional (2-D) exponential disk model (_n_ = 1) for all of the [C ii] components" (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - fitting: Visibility fitting in UVMULTIFIT with uncertainties from the post-fit covariance matrix and error propagation  
  > "We estimate the uncertainty by using error propagation. For the uncertainty of each parameter (FWHM and _q_), we adopt the output from the `UVMULTIFIT`, which is estimated from the post-fit covariance matrix (Martí-Vidal et al. 2014)." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - validation: Checked residual maps for absence of >2 sigma peaks in the central 2" region and compared with ALPINE-only visibility fits in Appendix B.  
  > "As can be seen in Figure 3, the best-fit model characterizes the observed [C ii] line emission well and no signal greater than 2σ significance level is present in the central 2′′-region, securing the validity of the fitting." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission); "To fairly compare with the fitting results from the low-resolution archival data alone, we iterate the above procedure by only using the data taken in the ALPINE Survey." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
4. **Compare [C II] sizes with rest-frame UV and FIR continuum sizes**: Calculate ratios of circularized [C II] effective radii to rest-frame UV (HST F160W GALFIT) and FIR (ALMA Band-7 UVMULTIFIT) effective radii from Mitsuhashi et al. (2024) and compare across multiplicity classes.  
  > "To evaluate the spatial extent of [C ii] line emission, we refer to two continuum size measurements (observing wavelengths of 1.6 µm and 870 µm), which were both performed in Mitsuhashi et al. (2024)." (3.2.2. Rest-frame UV and FIR continuum emission); "We start by comparing the measured effective radii among different tracers (rest-frame UV and FIR continua) to characterize the spatial extent of the [C ii] line emission in the context of star formation activity." (4.1. Size comparison)
   - software: GALFIT, SExtractor, UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: Excluded CRISTAL-21 from FIR size comparison due to unreliable FIR size measurement  
  > "One galaxy that is well below the one-to-one line is CRISTAL-21, which has an unreliable FIR size measurement due to the insufficient S/N of the visibility data, particularly at short _uv_ distances. Therefore, hereafter, we do not include the results of CRISTAL-21 when the FIR radius is related to the discussions." (4.1. Size comparison)
   - assumption: Single Sérsic n = 1 profile for UV (except CRISTAL-13) and circular exponential disk for FIR continuum  
  > "A single Sérsic model with an index of _n_ = 1 is assumed (except for CRISTAL-13), and nearby objects seen in the HST images are masked by using `SExtractor` (Bertin & Arnouts 1996)." (3.2.2. Rest-frame UV and FIR continuum emission)
   - fitting: Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to compare size distributions across multiplicity classes  
  > "Although we expect to find larger [C ii] sizes in pair and multiple-UV systems compared to single systems, we were unable to statistically distinguish their size distributions by performing the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (with _p_ values of 0.23 and 0.12 for the pair and multiple-UV, respectively), largely due to our limited sample size." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
   - validation: Checked positional offsets between [C II], UV, and FIR emission peaks.  
  > "We note that the median (average) positional offset between the [C ii] line and rest-frame UV continuum emission is 0.11 (0.14) arcsec (∼ 0.5 kpc in a physical scale), which is smaller than the typical synthesized beam size of the CRISTAL data." (4.1. Size comparison)
5. **Test for a secondary extended [C II] halo component via visibility stacking**: Extract real part of visibilities for 16 single galaxies (excluding CRISTAL-23c), stack visibilities and normalized residual visibilities, and perform single-component and concentric double-component ('galaxy' + 'halo') exponential disk fits.  
  > "To further confirm this, we stack the visibility data of 16 single galaxies and again perform the visibility modeling in the same manner. We perform both single component and double component fittings." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission); "In order to avoid the stacked visibility data being skewed by the bright objects in which the amplitude of residual visibility is also bright, we normalize the residual visibility by the total [C ii] flux obtained from the best-fit model before the stacking." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission)
   - software: casatool ms (getdata), UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: 16 single galaxies; 20 klambda and 100 klambda uv-distance bins; average redshift z = 5.22  
  > "we stack the visibility data of 16 single galaxies and again perform the visibility modeling in the same manner." (4.2 Constituent of [C ii] line emission); "We show the average values per both 20 kλ and 100 kλ bins, but no clear excess can be found in either case." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission)
   - assumption: Two concentric exponential disk components for double-component fit; average redshift z = 5.22  
  > "Here, we assume two concentric exponential disk components." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission)
   - fitting: Single and double exponential disk fitting in UVMULTIFIT evaluated with reduced chi^2  
  > "Since the fittings converge to the same reduced χ2 value, we cannot statistically favor either the single or double component model." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission)
   - validation: Checked residual visibility flux excess at the shortest uv-distance bin (20 klambda) against standard deviation.  
  > "At the smallest 20 kλ bin of the residual visibility, we find a flux excess of 0.15 mJy (1.2σ significance level)." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission)
6. **Measure radial surface density profiles of stacked [C II] and FIR continuum**: Shift phase centers to [C II] peaks, stack visibilities of 10 single galaxies detected in both [C II] and FIR using CASA/concat, image with CASA/tclean, and extract radial surface density profiles and their ratio Sigma_[CII]/Sigma_IR.  
  > "To inspect this, we stacked ten single galaxies, in which both [C ii] line and FIR continuum emission is detected. The stacking was performed in visibility using the `CASA/concat` task. The phase centers of individual visibility data are shifted to match the coordinates at which the [C ii] line emission peaks." (4.3.3. [C ii ] deficit in the central region of galaxies)
   - software: CASA/concat, CASA/tclean
   - parameter: 10 single galaxies; natural weighting; cleaned down to 1.5 sigma with 3" aperture mask; z = 5.22  
  > "We image both stacked [C ii] line and FIR continuum emission using the `CASA/tclean` task with natural weighting, and clean down to 1.5σ noise level by applying 3′′ aperture mask, where σ is the rms noise level of the dirty image." (4.3.3. [C ii ] deficit in the central region of galaxies)
   - assumption: Average redshift z = 5.22  
  > "The top axis is converted to a physical scale assuming an average redshift of _z_ = 5.22 among the stacked sample." (Figure 10 caption)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared profiles against noise levels from random apertures on blank regions and re-tested after excluding IR-bright galaxies (CRISTAL-21 and CRISTAL-24).  
  > "Beyond a beam-convolved radius of 1′′, the surface densities of [C ii] line and FIR continuum emission become comparable to the surface densities derived from setting random apertures, and the emission is likely not genuine." (4.3.3. [C ii ] deficit in the central region of galaxies); "However, our interest here is the radial variation in Σ [CII] /Σ IR, and so we have confirmed that the overall trend is unchanged when such exceptionally IR-bright galaxies are excluded from the stacking." (4.3.3. [C ii ] deficit in the central region of galaxies)
7. **Analyze correlations of [C II] properties with SFR surface density and Lyman-alpha equivalent width**: Perform linear regression fitting and Spearman's rank correlation tests between [C II] size, size ratios, and surface densities against Sigma_SFR_IR and rest-frame EW_Lya.  
  > "Figure 9 shows the [C ii] size and [C ii]-to-UV size ratio as a function of the SFR IR surface density (Σ SFR IR = SFR IR /2π_R_ 2 _e_,FIR) of the CRISTAL galaxies." (4.3.1. Outflows); "To study how the [C ii] line properties of our sample correlate with Lyα emission, we plot the [C ii] line surface density (Σ [CII] = _L_ [CII] /2π_R_ 2 _e_,[CII]), the [C ii]-to-IR surface density ratio (Σ [CII] /Σ IR) within the [C ii] effective radius, and the [C ii]-to-UV size ratio (_R_ _e_,[CII] /_R_ _e_,UV) as a function of the Lyα EW (EW Lyα) in Figure 11." (4.4. Connection to Ly α and H α emission)
   - parameter: Rest-frame EW_Lya obtained by multiplying observed-frame EW_Lya by (1 + z)^-1  
  > "Here, we convert the EW Lyα reported in the observed-frame to the rest-frame by applying a correction factor of (1+ _z_)−1." (4.4. Connection to Ly α and H α emission)
   - fitting: Linear regression fitting (y = m log10 EW_Lya + b) and Spearman's rank correlation test  
  > "We perform a linear regression fitting for each correlation and present the fitting results in Table 3." (4.4. Connection to Ly α and H α emission); "Parameters are determined from the form of _y_ = _m_ log 10 EW Lyα + _b_." (Table 3)
   - validation: Evaluated p-values of Spearman's rank correlation tests.  
  > "with a Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient of −0.51 and a _p_ value of 0.039." (4.4 Connection to Lyα and Hα emission)

## Qualitative findings

- [C II] line emission is systematically more extended than rest-frame UV continuum (by a factor of 2.90 on average) and comparable to or more extended than FIR continuum (by a factor of 1.54 on average).  
  > "We confirm that the [C ii] line emission is more spatially extended than the continuum emission, with average size ratios of ⟨_R_ _e_,[CII] /_R_ _e_,UV⟩ = 2.90 and ⟨_R_ _e_,[CII] /_R_ _e_,FIR⟩ = 1.54, although about half of the FIR-detected sample shows a comparable spatial extent between the [C ii] line and the FIR continuum emission (_R_ _e_,[CII] ≈ _R_ _e_,FIR)." (abstract)
- The [C II] visibility profiles of individual and stacked single galaxies are well characterized by a single exponential disk without statistically significant evidence for a secondary extended [C II] halo component.  
  > "To summarize, from the double component fitting in the stacked visibility data (Figure 7 right), we do not find a statistically significant evidence of [C ii] halo as a secondary component." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission); "Based on Figure 6, 7, and 8, we conclude that the [C ii] line emission of the CRISTAL galaxies can be adequately explained by a single exponential disk component." (4.2. Constituent of [C ii ] line emission)
- There is no statistically significant difference in [C II] sizes or size ratios between isolated single galaxies and interacting pair/multiple-UV systems.  
  > "In spite of a variety of merger states, we do not statistically confirm the difference in [C ii] sizes between single and pair (_p_ value = 0.23)/multiple-UV (_p_ value = 0.12) systems." (5. Conclusions)
- [C II] surface density anti-correlates with rest-frame Ly-alpha equivalent width, while the [C II]-to-IR surface density ratio tentatively correlates positively with Ly-alpha equivalent width.  
  > "We find a negative correlation between [C ii] surface density (Σ [CII]) and Lyα EW (_p_ value = 0.039), and a tentative positive correlation between [C ii]-to-IR surface density ratio (Σ [CII] /Σ IR) and Lyα EW (_p_ value = 0.090)." (5. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining compact and extended ALMA 12m configurations with archival ALPINE data provides both kpc-scale spatial resolution to separate interacting pairs and high sensitivity to large-scale extended structure.  
  > "The combination of multi-configuration observations is essential to achieve both sufficiently large maximum recoverable scale (MRS) and high spatial resolution." (2.1. The CRISTAL Survey); "Therefore, we combined the data from the ALPINE Survey (#2017.1.00428.L) with the CRISTAL data to achieve better sensitivity to the extended structure." (2.2.1. The ALPINE Survey)
- Why this method: Modeling directly in the interferometric visibility plane avoids image-domain biases at moderate S/N (noise fitting, clean beam smearing, dirty beam deviation from a single Gaussian) and avoids dependence on CLEAN imaging parameters.  
  > "In such cases, the measured sizes in the image domain could be overestimated, because either the best-fit model takes noise into account, or the smearing effect of the clean beam affects the fittings (Tadaki et al. 2020; Mitsuhashi et al. 2024)." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission); "The visibility analysis is also favorable assessing faint extended features in [C ii] line emission, as the imaging procedure requires several parameters to be considered (e.g., weighting of visibility data, threshold, size of the masked region for the CLEAN algorithm) and therefore complicates the optimization of the best parameter sets (Jones et al. 2023)." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
- Alternative: Ongoing star-formation-driven outflows as the main driver of extended [C II] emission (rejected/unsupported due to lack of correlation with Sigma_SFR_IR)  
  > "Therefore, regardless of the merging events, we fail to rule out the null hypothesis that there is no association between star-formation-driven (atomic) outflows and the extended [C ii] line emission." (4.3.1. Outflows)
- Alternative: Ionized gas as a dominant contributor to extended [C II] emission (inferred to have little impact, <~25%, based on stellar mass and metallicity relations)  
  > "Since the majority of the CRISTAL galaxies have lower stellar masses than this, we infer that the [C ii] line flux contributed from the ionized medium is not as significant as ∼ 25 %, indicating that the contribution from ionized gas has little impact on the spatial extent of [C ii] line emission." (4.3.2. Contribution from ionized gas)
- Alternative: Radial variation in the central [C II] deficit causing larger apparent [C II] sizes than FIR continuum (rejected because stacked radial Sigma_[CII]/Sigma_IR profile is relatively flat)  
  > "In summary, we suggest that radial variation in the [C ii] deficit is not the primary cause of the extended [C ii] line emission." (4.3.3. [C ii ] deficit in the central region of galaxies)
- Alternative: Shock heating induced by galaxy mergers driving extended [C II] emission (rejected because merging systems show moderate Re,[CII]/Re,FIR ~ 0.7-1.5 or normal L_[CII]/L_IR ~ 3e-3)  
  > "Shocked gas heating is not likely the driver of extended [C ii] line emission." (4.3.4. Shocked gas)

- Limitation (authors): Multiplicity classification is morphological and does not incorporate kinematical state (e.g., CRISTAL-05 appears single in UV/[C II] morphology but is a line-of-sight pair kinematically), and some HST-single sources are pairs in sharper JWST images.  
  > "We note, however, that this is still a crude classification, as we do not consider the dynamical state of each galaxy obtained from kinematical analysis." (3.1. Classification based on multiplicity); "three galaxies (CRISTAL-11, CRISTAL15, and CRISTAL-17) classified as single have pair-like morphology when looked with sharper images taken by James Webb Space Telescope" (3.1 Classification based on multiplicity)
- Limitation (authors): Limited sample size prevents statistically distinguishing [C II] size distributions across multiplicity classes and yields large uncertainties in size ratios.  
  > "Although we expect to find larger [C ii] sizes in pair and multiple-UV systems compared to single systems, we were unable to statistically distinguish their size distributions by performing the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (with _p_ values of 0.23 and 0.12 for the pair and multiple-UV, respectively), largely due to our limited sample size." (3.2.1. [C ii ] line emission)
- Limitation (authors): FIR continuum visibilities have lower S/N than [C II] and were modeled with a single circular exponential disk, preventing fair comparison in multiple-UV and multiple-[C II] systems and yielding an unreliable FIR size for CRISTAL-21.  
  > "Unlike [C ii] line emission, all of the FIR continuum emission was fit in a single exponential disk component due to the lower S/N detection compared to the [C ii] line emission." (3.2.2. Rest-frame UV and FIR continuum emission); "One galaxy that is well below the one-to-one line is CRISTAL-21, which has an unreliable FIR size measurement due to the insufficient S/N of the visibility data, particularly at short _uv_ distances." (4.1. Size comparison)
- Limitation (authors): Available Ly-alpha measurements come from slit spectroscopy and only probe global properties without spatial information.  
  > "However, we note that the Lyα properties currently available are taken by slit spectroscopy (Cassata et al. 2020), and only global properties without spatial information can be inferred." (4.4. Connection to Ly α and H α emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stacking visibilities of galaxies with diverse axis ratios (q = 0.31 - 0.90) without deprojecting or aligning major axes can artificially smear emission and mimic or dilute an extended halo.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing the Sérsic index to n = 1 for all [C II] visibility fits could mask steeper or flatter inner/outer profiles that a free Sérsic index might capture.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Binned single-channel visibility fitting over +-2 sigma velocity width integrates over kinematic structures (rotation, tidal tails, outflows), potentially conflating kinematic extent with morphological extent.
