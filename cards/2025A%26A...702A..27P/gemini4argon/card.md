## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and analyze Gemini-NIFS near-IR IFU cubes and map H2 and Br-gamma emission**: Reduce NIFS spectra using Gemini IRAF tasks, create 3''x3'' cubes with 0.05'' sampling, fit continuum to each spaxel, integrate over full line profiles of Br-gamma and H2 1-0 S(1), and compute H2/Br-gamma ratio map.  
  > "The standard NIFS tasks included in the Gemini IRAF package v1.16 were used for data reduction." (Near-IR observations using Gemini-NIFS); "For each emission line detected in the datacube, this analysis consists of fitting a continuum to each spaxel and then integrating over the full line profile." (Near-IR observations using Gemini-NIFS); "Figure [7](#ratio) presents the H$_{2}$/Br$\gamma$ ratio in Field 1 at the regions in which both emissions are observed." (The near-IR IFU spectroscopy)
   - software: Gemini IRAF package v1.16, DS9, QFITSview
   - parameter: FoV = 3'' x 3'', angular sampling = 0.05'' x 0.05''  
  > "Finally, data cubes were created for each field with a Field-of-View (FoV) of $3\ensuremath{''}\times3\ensuremath{''}$ and an angular sampling of $0.05\ensuremath{''}\times0.05\ensuremath{''}$." (Near-IR observations using Gemini-NIFS)
   - fitting: Continuum fitted to each spaxel prior to integrating over full line profile.  
  > "For each emission line detected in the datacube, this analysis consists of fitting a continuum to each spaxel and then integrating over the full line profile." (Near-IR observations using Gemini-NIFS)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image JVLA 10 GHz radio continuum, measure flux density, and classify radio source**: Calibrate JVLA visibilities with CASA pipeline, manually flag RFI, image with tclean (Briggs ROBUST = 0.25), integrate flux within 50 uJy/beam contour, extrapolate to 5.8 GHz to compare with L_bol, and compute Lyman continuum photon flux.  
  > "The image was obtained using the tclean task and Briggs weighting. We set ROBUST = 0.25 to reduce sidelobe effects" (Radio observations using JVLA); "The flux density integrated within the $50~\mathrm{\mu Jy\,beam^{-1}}$ contour level is $0.13\pm 0.01~\mathrm{mJy}$." (Discovering a compact radio continuum source); "We extrapolate our radio flux at $10\,\mathrm{GHz}$ to $5.8\,\mathrm{GHz}$ assuming some spectral behaviour and estimate the radio luminosity for a distance $D=6.2\,\mathrm{kpc}$." (Radio continuum source)
   - software: CASA pipeline v5.6.2-2.el7, tclean
   - parameter: ROBUST = 0.25, beam = 0.42'' x 0.17'', rms = 6 uJy beam^-1  
  > "We set ROBUST = 0.25 to reduce sidelobe effects from the nearby complex of bright HII regions" (Radio observations using JVLA); "The final image has a synthesized beam of $0.42\ensuremath{''} \times 0.17\ensuremath{''}$ (FWHM) and positional angle (P.A.) of $-51.6^{\circ}$, and a mean noise (rms) $\sigma_{rms}=6~\mathrm{\mu Jy\,beam^{-1}}$ (SNR $\geq$ 30)." (Radio observations using JVLA)
   - assumption: Spectral index alpha = 0.6 for ionised jet/knot and alpha = -0.1 for optically thin HC/UC HII region; D = 6.2 kpc; T_e = 10^4 K, a = 1  
  > "For an ionised jet/knot, we take a canonical spectral index $\alpha=0.6$, while for a HC/UC HII region, we use $\alpha=-0.1$" (Radio continuum source); "From (cite: kurtz94), using the radio flux at 10 GHz ($S_\nu = 86~\mu\mathrm{Jy}$), $T_e = 10^{4}$ K and the parameter $a=1$, we derived $N_{Ly} = 3.2\times 10^{44}$ ph s$^{-1}$." (Radio continuum source)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Imaged secondary calibrator J1832-105 to check astrometric error, finding offset < 0.1''.  
  > "Additionally, we generated an image of the secondary calibrator J1832$-$105 (the phase calibrator) to assess the astrometric error in the final image." (Radio observations using JVLA)
3. **Map ALMA CO isotopologues and derive physical parameters of the southern molecular outflow**: Generate moment 0 and channel maps of 12CO, 13CO, and C18O J=2-1; exclude self-absorbed velocities (97.2-102.0 km s^-1); integrate blueshifted (87.6-96.0 km s^-1) and redshifted (102-108 km s^-1) 12CO emission; compute CO column density, outflow mass, dynamical time, mass outflow rate, momentum, and momentum rate for the southern redshifted lobe.  
  > "To better appreciate red- and blue-shifted molecular gas associated with YSO-G29 we present in Fig. [13](#outflowsRB) the $^{12}$CO emission integrated between 87.6 and 96.0 km s$^{-1}$ (blue) and between 102 and 108 km s$^{-1}$ (red)" (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "We obtained the CO column density from the CO J=2–1 transition following the works of (cite: turner1991) and (cite: miao1995)." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "Then the outflow rate ($\dot{M} =$ M$_{out}$/$t_{dyn}$), momentum ($P$ $=$ M $\bar{v}$) and outflow momentum rate (${\dot{P}}= P/t_{dyn}$) were estimated." (The dust emission and the molecular environment)
   - software: Splatalogue Database for Astronomical Spectroscopy (SDAS)
   - parameter: Blue velocity range: 87.6 - 96.0 km s^-1; Red velocity range: 102 - 108 km s^-1; Excluded range: 97.2 - 102.0 km s^-1; Systemic velocity: 101 km s^-1; Outflow length L ~ 0.06 pc; Integrated intensity W ~ 0.5 Jy beam^-1 km s^-1  
  > "The systemic velocity of 101 km s$^{-1}$ for the molecular core was confirmed from the C$^{18}$O line." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "From the moment zero map of the outflow structure, we estimated an integrated intensity of about 0.5 Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "Dynamical time ($t_{dyn}$) is obtained from L/$v_{\rm max}$, where L is the length of the outflow (about 0.06 pc)" (The dust emission and the molecular environment)
   - assumption: LTE excitation temperature T_ex = 20 K, abundance ratio [CO/H2] = 10^-4, mean molecular weight mu_H2 = 2.72, distance = 6.2 kpc  
  > "Thus, assuming a T$_{\rm ex}$ = 20 K, and using the transition and molecular parameters obtained from the Splatalogue Database for Astronomical Spectroscopy (SDAS), we derived an average CO column density of about $6.7 \times 10^{16}$ cm$^{-2}$." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "where N($^{12}$CO) is the average column density of the lobe, ${\rm [CO/H_{2}]=10^{-4}}$ is the abundance ratio between the molecules, ${\rm \mu_{H_2}=2.72}$ is the mean molecular weight" (The dust emission and the molecular environment)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked spectra for self-absorption before computing mass of southern outflow; compared 12CO and 13CO maps to identify self-absorption/missing flux in northern shell-like structure.  
  > "After confirming that the southeastern CO structure does not exhibit line absorption issues, we estimated the mass of the southern molecular outflow" (The dust emission and the molecular environment)
4. **Estimate bolometric luminosity from archival mid-IR to mm SED**: Compile fluxes from allWISE (22 um), Hi-GAL (70-500 um), ATLASGAL (870 um), and BGPS (1100 um); perform linear interpolation between bands; integrate area under the curve to obtain bolometric flux and luminosity.  
  > "We estimated the bolometric luminosity of the region following the same procedure of R19, who used a linear interpolation of the fluxes between the mid-IR and the millimetre bands." (Bolometric luminosity of the region); "The area under the interpolation gives the bolometric flux $A = (3.06 \pm 0.01)\times 10^{-8}~\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}}$." (Bolometric luminosity of the region)
   - software: VOSA SED Analyzer
   - parameter: Wavelength range: 22 um to 1100 um; zero point f_0 = 8.36 Jy at 22 um  
  > "with the zero point $f_0=8.36~\mathrm{Jy}$ reported in the VOSA SED Analyzer (cite: bayo08)." (Table 5)
   - assumption: Distance d = 6.2 +/- 0.6 kpc  
  > "For a distance $d=6.2\pm0.6~\mathrm{kpc}$, the bolometric luminosity is $L_{bol} = 4\pi Ad^2 = (3.7\pm0.7) \times 10^{4}~\mathrm{L_{\odot}}$." (Bolometric luminosity of the region)
   - fitting: Linear interpolation of fluxes between mid-IR and mm bands; error estimated from interpolating f_lambda +/- delta_f_lambda.  
  > "To estimate the error, we obtained a lower and an upper limits of the area by interpolating $f_\lambda-\Delta f_\lambda$ and $f_\lambda+\Delta f_\lambda$, respectively" (Bolometric luminosity of the region)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Characterize chemical inventory, rotational temperatures, and gas mass of the main dust core**: Extract spectra from a circular region at the 1.3 mm continuum peak across 8 spws from ALMA projects 2021.1.00311.S and 2015.1.01312.S; identify lines using SDAS (CDMS/JPL); construct rotational diagrams for 6 species to fit T_rot; calculate core gas mass from 1.3 mm continuum flux.  
  > "Spectra were extracted from them using a circular region centred at the continuum emission peak (see green dashed circle in Fig. [20](#region))." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core); "The rotational diagram analysis was carried out for certain detected molecules $-$CH$_{3}$CN, CH$_{3}$CCH, E-CH$_{3}$OH, A-CH$_{3}$OH, HC$_{3}$N, and CH$_{3}$OCH$_{3}$$-$" (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core); "As a final step in the physical characterization of the chemically rich core, we estimated its mass using the approach described by (cite: kau08) (eq. B.1):" (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)
   - software: Splatalogue Database for Astronomical Spectroscopy (SDAS), CDMS, JPL
   - parameter: 1.3 mm integrated flux S_nu = 0.03 Jy  
  > "We used the integrated flux at 1.3 mm, S$_{\nu} = 0.03$ Jy, and assumed a dust temperature, T$_{\rm dust} = 361$ K" (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin emission for rotational diagrams; thermal coupling of gas and dust with T_dust = 361 K (from CH3CN); dust opacity per gram of matter kappa_nu = 0.013 cm^2 g^-1 at 1.3 mm; distance d = 6.2 kpc  
  > "Under the assumptions of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and optically thin emission, the inverse of the slope of the linear fit yields the rotational temperature (T$_{\rm rot}$)." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core); "We used the integrated flux at 1.3 mm, S$_{\nu} = 0.03$ Jy, and assumed a dust temperature, T$_{\rm dust} = 361$ K, derived from the CH$_3$CN emission and considering the thermal coupling of gas and dust." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core); "The dust opacity per gram of matter at 1.3 mm, $\kappa_{\nu}$, was adopted as 0.013 cm$^2$g$^{-1}$ (cite: lin2021)." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)
   - fitting: Linear fit to ln(N_u / g_u) vs. E_u in rotational diagrams.  
  > "we computed the natural logarithm of the column density per statistical weight (ln(N$_{\rm u}$/g$_{\rm u}$)) as a function of the E$_{\rm u}$ (Fig. [23](#Trots))." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core); "the inverse of the slope of the linear fit yields the rotational temperature (T$_{\rm rot}$)." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)
   - validation: Compared derived T_rot values with previous hot core studies in the literature.  
  > "These values are consistent with those reported in previous studies of hot molecular cores." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)

## Qualitative findings

- H2 1-0 S(1) and Br-gamma lines are detected in the near-IR, compatible with UV excitation and ionization in a perturbed ambient with some shock excitation, while no near-IR lines are detected towards the dark lane, ruling out an edge-on disc/toroid nature for the lane.  
  > "We conclude that the detected near-IR lines are compatible with excitation and ionization from UV radiation propagating in a highly perturbed ambient by the activity of YSO-G29, with some contribution of shocked gas." (Near-IR emission: lines from Gemini-NIFS); "As spectral lines can be detected towards discs and/or toroids (e.g., (cite: murakawa13)), we discard such a nature for the dark lane, proposing it to be just a region lacking near-IR emission." (Near-IR emission: lines from Gemini-NIFS)
- The northwestern 12CO shell-like structure is filled with 13CO emission and shows deep self-absorption/missing flux at its center, indicating it is not a true shell but a non-collimated blueshifted molecular outflow component impacting the near-IR cavity.  
  > "This demonstrates that the observed $^{12}$CO shell is mostly filled with $^{13}$CO emission." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "We conclude that the observed structure is not a molecular shell enclosed by near-IR emission but a non-collimated molecular outflow component, likely produced by one or multiple ejections from YSO-G29 that have impacted the feature observed at near-IR." (Molecular outflows)
- The main 1.3 mm continuum core is a chemically rich, thermally stratified hot molecular core with rotational temperatures spanning ~70 K to 361 K and vibrationally excited CH3CN and CH3OH lines, whereas adjacent dust cores lack COMs peaking on them and are likely colder and starless.  
  > "The main core is the only one with a rich abundance of complex molecules. In the others, some molecules are observed, but this is mostly due to extended emission that it is centred on the main core." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "In summary, the molecular inventory and excitation analysis of YSO-G29 reveal a chemically rich and thermally stratified hot core." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Gemini-NIFS provides near-IR IFU spectroscopy to test excitation mechanisms, JVLA A-array maps sub-arcsecond radio continuum to locate ionized gas/protostars, and higher-resolution archival ALMA Band 6 data resolves the dust cores and 12CO/13CO/C18O J=2-1 small-scale outflows.  
  > "We used the Jansky Very Large Array (JVLA) to map the radio continuum emission and the NIFS instrument at Gemini-North to perform near-IR spectroscopy." (Introduction); "In addition, we analysed more recent ALMA data with higher angular resolution than in our previous studies to examine the molecular gas distribution associated with the striking observed near-IR features." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used seeing-limited mode with PWFS2 on Gemini-NIFS because suitable AO guide stars were unavailable; set ROBUST = 0.25 in JVLA imaging to suppress sidelobes from bright HII regions 4' away; used allWISE 22 um flux instead of MIPSGAL 24 um due to saturation masking.  
  > "The observations were performed in seeing-limited mode using Peripheral WaveFront Sensor 2 (PWFS2) for guiding, rather than with adaptive optics correction via the ALTAIR system, due to the difficulty in finding suitable guide stars for this object." (Near-IR observations using Gemini-NIFS); "We set ROBUST = 0.25 to reduce sidelobe effects from the nearby complex of bright HII regions located at $\sim4^{\prime}$ to the northwest of YSO-G29." (Radio observations using JVLA); "in our case the catalogued source MIPSGAL MG029.8569-00.0434 overlaps with a masked out area of the MIPSGAL image, probably due to saturation. Instead, we used the flux at $22~\mathrm{\mu m}$ of the Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer" (Bolometric luminosity of the region)
- Alternative: Edge-on disc or toroid nature for the near-IR dark lane (rejected because no near-IR emission lines were detected towards it).  
  > "As spectral lines can be detected towards discs and/or toroids (e.g., (cite: murakawa13)), we discard such a nature for the dark lane, proposing it to be just a region lacking near-IR emission." (Near-IR emission: lines from Gemini-NIFS)
- Alternative: True hollow molecular shell enclosed by near-IR emission in the northwest (rejected because 13CO fills the interior and 12CO shows strong self-absorption/missing flux).  
  > "We conclude that the observed structure is not a molecular shell enclosed by near-IR emission but a non-collimated molecular outflow component, likely produced by one or multiple ejections from YSO-G29 that have impacted the feature observed at near-IR." (Molecular outflows)
- Alternative: Single-source scenario where the 10 GHz radio source is a thermal knot or non-thermal lobe along a jet from the 1.3 mm core (disfavoured in favour of a multiple-YSO scenario due to the complex multiwavelength morphology and lack of dust peak at the radio source).  
  > "In the former, the compact radio source may be a thermal knot or a non-thermal lobe located along the jet of a putative protostar likely situated at the centre of the 1.3 mm core" (Radio continuum source); "However, taking into account the complexity of the morphology of the different emissions, hereafter we will favour a scenario of multiple YSOs in the region." (Proposed scenario for YSO-G29)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Outflow dynamical parameters (P and P_dot) are affected by projection effects and should be considered lower limits because the low velocity (~4 km s^-1) suggests propagation nearly in the plane of the sky; the outflow may also be a blend from more than one source.  
  > "Caution is advised when interpreting these values due to potential projection effects. The small velocity range would indicate that such an outflow is observed mainly along the plane of the sky. In that case, the derived values for $P$ and ${\dot{P}}$ should be considered lower limits." (The dust emission and the molecular environment); "Moreover, one must also be careful with the parameters obtained, as the analysed outflow may be the product of outflows from more than one source." (Molecular outflows)
- Limitation (authors): 12CO J=2-1 emission suffers from high optical depth self-absorption and interferometric missing flux from extended spatial scales.  
  > "This can be due to high optical depth effects that usually $^{12}$CO lines suffer in dense gaseous structures. Additionally, the deep $^{12}$CO absorption spectral feature could indicate missing flux coming from more extended spatial scales that are filtered out by the interferometer." (Molecular outflows)
- Limitation (authors): Single-frequency 10 GHz JVLA observation lacks a spectral index measurement to definitively distinguish between a thermal UCHII region, an ionized jet, or a non-thermal lobe.  
  > "Conducting a radio spectral index study would provide valuable insights for discerning the nature of the radio source." (Radio continuum source)
- Limitation (authors): Bolometric luminosity L_bol is derived from low-resolution survey fluxes and may include contributions from multiple sources in the region.  
  > "However, taking into account the large dispersion of the diagram and the possibility that multiple sources in the region are contributing to $L_{bol}$, we cannot discard that the compact radio source may be a HC/UC HII region." (Radio continuum source)
- Limitation (authors): The HC3N rotational temperature fit is based on only two ground-state transitions and requires additional data for higher confidence.  
  > "It should be noted that our HC$_{\rm 3}$N fit is based on only two transitions. Although the derived T$_{\rm rot}$ is consistent with previous high-resolution studies, additional data would be necessary to confirm this estimate with higher confidence." (Chemical and physical characterization of the main dust core)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Typo in the reported mass outflow rate: M_dot = M_out / t_dyn = 0.2 M_sun / 8.3e3 yr is 2.4e-5 M_sun yr^-1, but the text prints 2.4 x 10^5 M_sun yr^-1 (missing minus sign in exponent).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistency in the 10 GHz flux density used for the radio source: Section 3.2 reports an integrated flux of 0.13 +/- 0.01 mJy within the 50 uJy/beam contour, whereas Section 4.3 uses S_nu = 86 uJy to compute N_Ly without explaining the difference.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Typo in the Table 5 note formula for WISE 22 um flux: printed as f_22 = f_0 10^(-2.5/m_22) instead of the standard Pogson relation f_0 10^(-m_22/2.5) (though the resulting 40.0 Jy matches the standard formula).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core gas mass (~1 M_sun) is calculated assuming a uniform dust temperature of T_dust = 361 K (from CH3CN in the innermost hot gas), which likely underestimates the total core mass if the bulk of the dust is cooler as traced by CH3CCH and CH3OCH3.
