## Science

- Fields: high_z_galaxies, star_formation, agn_and_quasars
- Question: Constrain the astrophysical properties (age, stellar mass, dust and gas content, AGN presence, sizes) of 11 spectroscopically confirmed DSFGs at 1.5 < z_spec < 3 in GOODS-S and characterize their role in galaxy evolution.  
  > "We present a panchromatic study of 11 (sub-)millimetre selected DSFGs with spectroscopically confirmed redshift ($1.5< z_{\rm spec}<3$) in the GOODS-S field, with the aim of constraining their astrophysical properties (e.g., age, stellar mass, dust and gas content) and characterizing their role in the context of galaxy evolution." ## Qualitative findings

- The 11 DSFGs lie on or slightly above the z ~ 2 star-forming main sequence, with younger objects located to the upper-left of the relation and older objects overlapping it.  
  > "Younger (i.e., bluer) objects are found to lie to the upper-left side of the main-sequence at the corresponding redshift, while the elder (i.e., redder) are found to lie in correspondence of it, as predicted by the *in-situ* scenario." (Discussion)
- The ISM attenuation law is shallower (median delta_ISM ~ -0.5) than the standard local Calzetti (2000) law, flattening at redder wavelengths.  
  > "As to the ISM attenuation law, it results shallower (median $\delta_{\rm ISM}\sim - 0.5$) than the standard (cite: Calzetti2000:2000ApJ...533..682C), constrained in local star-forming galaxies." (Discussion)
- Nine of 11 galaxies are detected in X-rays with L_2-10keV >= 10^42 erg s^-1 (two clearly AGN-dominated), while their radio emission is consistent with star formation alone and AGN contribution to total IR luminosity is negligible (<10%, median ~0.8%).  
  > "Nine objects out of eleven have an X-ray luminous (L$_{2-10 {\rm keV}}\gtrsim10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$) counterpart in the *Chandra* $\simeq7$ Ms catalog and two of them are clearly dominated by the active nucleus emission (L$_{2-10 {\rm keV}}\gtrsim10^{43}-10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$). The radio luminosity is consistent with the emission coming from galaxy star formation, suggesting that the AGN, if present, should be radio silent or quiet." (Summary and conclusions)
- Four galaxies show possible signatures of ongoing/forthcoming AGN feedback via extended radio emission (UDF11) or broad double-peaked CO line profiles (UDF3, UDF8, ALESS067.1).  
  > "For four objects we found some signatures of AGN feedback either in the radio band (UDF11), that appears more extended than the FIR one, or by the detection of possible AGN-driven molecular outflows (UDF3, UDF8, ALESS067.1)." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected GOODS-S for the widest and deepest multi-wavelength coverage (X-ray to radio) and required spectroscopic redshifts (1.5 < z_spec < 3) and >=2 Herschel detections to accurately sample the FIR dust peak and stellar emission, reducing parameter degeneracies.  
  > "In this work we follow the latter approach focusing on a sample of 11 spectroscopically-confirmed z $\sim2$ DSFGs, that we selected in the Great Observatories Origins Survey South (cite: Dickinson2001:2001AAS...198.2501D; Giavalisco2004:2004ApJ...600L..93G) field, in order to have the widest multi-wavelength coverage of their spectral/broad-band emission currently achievable (from X-rays to radio band)." (Introduction); "In this work, on the one hand, we reduce the uncertainties on the constrained parameters by both selecting solely the sources with spectroscopically confirmed redshift at the peak of Cosmic SFH and requiring an accurately-sampled SED from the X-ray to radio regime" (The sample)
- Why this method: Used CIGALE energy-balance SED fitting with a physically-motivated double power-law attenuation and a MIR power-law + single-T modified blackbody (Casey 2012) because the MIR is sparsely sampled (1-2 points per source), avoiding over-parameterized AGN/PAH templates.  
  > "This simple approach is very convenient in our case since the MIR SED of our 11 DSFGs is scarcely sampled (each source owns just one or two photometric data points in this spectral range)." (Dust emission); "We avoid to model the AGN MIR emission alone since the most quoted AGN models (cite: Fritz2006:2006MNRAS.366..767F; Nenkova2008:2008ApJ...685..147N; Feltre2012:2012MNRAS.426..120F) are extremely complex and characterized by a number of free parameters that would be hardly constrained by the under-sampled MIR emission of our galaxies." (Dust emission)
- Alternative: Multi-temperature modified blackbody dust emission model (rejected because it requires >5 FIR photometric points, not available for all sources)  
  > "However, fitting the dust thermal emission with a multi-temperature modified BB requires at least more than five photometric data points in the FIR regime, which are currently not available for every source of the sample." (Dust emission)
- Alternative: Detailed AGN (Fritz et al. 2006; Nenkova et al. 2008; Feltre et al. 2012) and PAH/silicate (Draine & Li 2007) MIR emission models in the primary fit (avoided due to sparse MIR sampling, though used as a fixed-parameter cross-check)  
  > "We avoid to model the AGN MIR emission alone since the most quoted AGN models (cite: Fritz2006:2006MNRAS.366..767F; Nenkova2008:2008ApJ...685..147N; Feltre2012:2012MNRAS.426..120F) are extremely complex and characterized by a number of free parameters that would be hardly constrained by the under-sampled MIR emission of our galaxies. In addition, considering the absence of any spectral information in the MIR band, we fairly avoid the exploitation of more sophisticated models that include PAH and silicate lines, such as (cite: DraineLi2007:2007ApJ...657..810D)." (Dust emission)
- Alternative: Including radio photometry directly in CIGALE SED fitting (rejected to avoid strong assumptions on the radio-to-submm spectral index given only 1 radio band for 10/11 sources)  
  > "We do not include radio photometry in SED fitting since we want to avoid any strong assumption on the radio-to-(sub)mm spectral index. The latter is well constrained when at least two fluxes in the radio band are available, but this requirement is satisfied only for one source, i.e. ALESS067.1." (Results)
- Alternative: Lower CO-to-H2 conversion factor alpha_CO ~ 0.8-1 (rejected as inappropriate for globally distributed molecular gas)  
  > "Although the topic is still under debate, a smaller value of $\alpha_{\rm CO}\sim0.8-1$ (often exploited for local ULIRGs and high-z compact DSFGs) is commonly thought to be inappropriate for the globally distributed molecular gas and more advisable for high-resolution observations which isolate the nuclear region" (Gas mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Requiring complete multi-wavelength coverage and spectroscopic redshift excludes UV/optically dark DSFGs and limits the sample to 11 objects, preventing statistical conclusions for the whole DSFG population.  
  > "On the other hand, the requirement for a complete multi-band coverage and the condition on the availability of spectroscopic redshift do not allowed us to include in our sample objects that are totally obscured in the UV/optical, and seriously limit our sample size and completeness." (The sample)
- Limitation (authors): Single-temperature modified blackbody fits yield luminosity-weighted dust temperatures that underestimate total dust mass by a factor of ~2.  
  > "However, dust masses derived from single-temperature fit to the FIR SED are *luminosity-weighted*. As a consequence, we select a dust temperature that is typically slightly higher than the mean and the resulting dust mass tends to be underestimated." (Dust mass)
- Limitation (authors): Some CIGALE parameters (f_att, delta_BC, Delta_star) do not always have single-peaked marginalized PDFs.  
  > "The most common exceptions are found for: the BC-to-ISM V-band attenuation, $f_{\rm att}$; the BC attenuation spectral index, $\delta^{\rm BC}$; the separation age between the old and young SSPs, $\Delta_\star$." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Total gas masses and gas metallicities have large systematic uncertainties (~0.3-0.4 dex) due to the assumed mass-metallicity and GDR calibrations.  
  > "Systematic uncertainties between different metallicity indicators and calibrators can reach $\sim0.3$ dex (cite: Kewley2008:2008ApJ...681.1183K) and clearly dominate over the statistical one." (Gas mass); "Uncertainties have been omitted for clarity: they are $\sim0.3$ dex for molecular gas masses and $\sim0.4$ dex for total gas masses and gas metallicities." (Gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): Broad, double-peaked CO profiles in UDF3, UDF8, and ALESS067.1 could trace either AGN-driven molecular outflows or unresolved rotating molecular disks; higher resolution imaging is required to distinguish them.  
  > "However, we need higher resolution images to provide the right interpretation of this evidence, that is also consistent with a rotating disk of molecular gas (i.e. unresolved; Pantoni et al. in preparation)." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The archival ALMA continuum and CO line data processing and flux measurements are deferred to a companion paper (Pantoni et al., in prep.), leaving the ALMA measurements in this paper undocumented.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Multi-wavelength flux densities used for the CIGALE fits are not tabulated in the paper (Table 2 only marks bands as detected or non-detected), requiring external catalog cross-matching to reconstruct the input SED table.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A discrepancy exists between Table 3 (which labels the SFH module 'Constant') and Section 3.1 (which states a delayed exponential / power-law SFH SFR(tau) propto (tau/tau_star)^0.5 was adopted).
