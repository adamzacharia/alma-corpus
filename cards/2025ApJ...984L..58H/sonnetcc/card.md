## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and line cubes**: tclean imaging of 3 mm continuum, N2H+ and CH3OH cubes at 61 kHz channels  
  > "task in CASA 6.5.4 (cite: casa:2022)" (Observations)
   - software: CASA 6.5.4
   - parameter: mask = auto-multithresh, deconvolver = hogbom, Briggs robust = 2.0  
  > "The mask parameter was set to “auto-multithresh,” the deconvolver was “hogbom,” and a Briggs robust weighting value of 2.0 was applied." (Observations)
   - parameter: thresholds 0.2 mJy (full-band continuum, ~0.857 GHz) and 10 mJy (per channel)  
  > "The imaging thresholds were 0.2 mJy for the full-band ($\sim$0.857 GHz) continuum and 10 mJy for individual channels" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Compare N2H+ and CH3OH morphology and find velocity jumps**: Visual inspection of channel maps and integrated-intensity maps; velocity of CH3OH vs. N2H+ compared at interfaces and a jump of 0.4-0.8 km/s compared with the 0.2 km/s sound speed at 10 K and the 0.45 km/s turbulent velocity dispersion  
  > "upon visually inspecting the channel map matrix" (Morphology and Kinematics); "are higher than the sound speed in cold cores (approximately 0.2 km/s at 10 K" (Morphology and Kinematics)
   - software: CASA 6.5.4
   - parameter: v_LSR of each core derived from the velocity shift of the N2H+ transition  
  > "is the system velocity, derived from the velocity shift of the" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Velocity jump above the cold-core sound speed implies shocks  
  > "suggesting the existence of shocks at the interface between CH$_3$OH and N$_2$H$^+$ gas components" (Morphology and Kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Velocity jumps compared with the turbulent velocity dispersion of Larson 1981  
  > "are consistent with the velocity dispersion (0.45 km/s) due to the turbulence activities" (Morphology and Kinematics)
3. **Estimate the mass assembly rate from CH3OH**: Mdot = rho A delta_v per pixel; N_u from integrated brightness temperature (optically thin), N(CH3OH) from LTE at 10 K, rho from N(CH3OH) / (X l)  
  > "We estimated the mass assembly rate ($\Dot{M}$) using our observations, following the relation" (Implications of the detected Shocks); "with the assumption of optically thin CH$_3$OH emission" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
   - parameter: delta_v = v - v_LSR, multiplied by sqrt(3) for projection  
  > "multiplied by a factor of $\sqrt{3}$ to account for the projection" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
   - parameter: Z(T) = 84 at T = 10 K; mean molecular weight mu = 2.35  
  > "is the gas temperature (assumed to be 10 K), $Z(T) = 84$ is the corresponding partition function" (Implications of the detected Shocks); "where $\mu = 2.35$ is the mean molecular weight per free particle" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
   - parameter: X ~ 1e-6, l ~ 0.02 pc  
  > "Adopting the $X \sim 10^{-6}$ from (cite: 2015Boogert_review_ice) and the $\ell \sim 0.02$ pc from (cite: 2021Sahu_ALMASOP_presstellar)" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin CH3OH, gas temperature 10 K  
  > "with the assumption of local-thermodynamic-equilibrium (LTE)" (Implications of the detected Shocks); "is the gas temperature (assumed to be 10 K)" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
   - assumption: Pixel area A = (400 pc x 0.5 arcsec)^2; assembly velocity equals the difference between quiescent core and turbulent ambient gas  
  > "We assumed that the assembly velocity is comparable to the velocity difference between the quiescent dense core and its turbulent ambient" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: not stated; the authors note the rate may be underestimated and that a 10% desorption efficiency would give 1e-6 Msun/yr, matching a 1e6 yr starless-core lifetime  
  > "This value may be underestimated." (Implications of the detected Shocks); "aligning with the expected lifetime of a solar-like starless core of $10^6$ years" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
4. **Run MHD simulation of a turbulent filamentary cloud**: ORION2 ideal MHD with AMR, driven turbulence at the largest scales, then gravity switched on; a starless core extracted when peak number density reached 1e7 cm^-3  
  > "The ideal MHD simulation was performed using the multiphysics Godunov code ORION2 with block-structured Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR) framework" (Core Evolution); "Gravity was turned on and the system was continued to be driven to maintain a global turbulence at a thermal Mach number of 10." (Core Evolution)
   - software: ORION2
   - parameter: Thermal Mach number 10, Alfvenic Mach number 1, virial parameter 1, box 4.55 pc, mass scale 3110 Msun, mean field 31.6 microgauss  
  > "The system was scaled by the virial parameter of 1 when gravity was on." (Core Evolution); "the length of the simulation region to be 4.55 pc" (Core Evolution); "The Alfvenic Mach number of 1 will define the mean magnetic field strength of $31.6~\mu {\rm G}$" (Core Evolution)
   - parameter: 512^3 base grid, 2 refinement levels, finest resolution 0.0022 pc (450 au), periodic boundaries, isothermal equation of state  
  > "Periodic boundary conditions and isothermal equation of state were used for the simulation on a base grid of $512^3$ with 2 levels of refinement." (Core Evolution); "The highest resolution is about 0.0022 pc (450 au)." (Core Evolution)
   - assumption: Core snapshot chosen to match the peak density of G205M3 (1e7 cm^-3)  
  > "when its peak number density reached $1\times10^7$ cm$^{-3}$" (Core Evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Make synthetic N2H+ and CH3OH images**: SPARX radiative transfer on the simulated core with N2H+ placed in dense gas and CH3OH placed only in shocked gas  
  > "We conducted radiative transfer calculations using" (Core Evolution)
   - software: SPARX
   - parameter: N2H+ abundance 1e-9 where n exceeds its critical density 6e4 cm^-3  
  > "we assumed it resides in gas with number densities exceeding its critical density ($6\times10^4$ cm$^{-3}$) and adopted a fractional abundance of $10^{-9}$" (Core Evolution)
   - parameter: CH3OH abundance 1e-6 where the 3D velocity jump exceeds 0.4 km/s  
  > "we assumed it is present in regions experiencing a velocity jump ($\Delta v$) greater than 0.4 km/s" (Core Evolution); "The velocity jump ($\Delta v$) is defined by the three-dimensional velocity differences at each location." (Core Evolution)
   - assumption: Dust and gas temperature fixed at 10 K; gas-to-dust mass ratio 100  
  > "The dust temperature and the gas temperature were fixed to 10 K." (Core Evolution); "The gas-to-dust mass ratio was set to 100." (Core Evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare synthetic and observed morphology and kinematics**: Qualitative comparison of simulated velocity jumps, channel maps and spatial distribution with observations  
  > "successfully reproduced the observed morphologically anti-correlated and kinematically mismatched" (Core Evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Simulated velocity jump 0.4-0.8 km/s matches the observed 0.4-0.8 km/s; CH3OH widespread around N2H+ as observed  
  > "the velocity jump reaches 0.4 – 0.8 km s$^{-1}$" (Core Evolution); "consistent with the observed spatial distribution" (Core Evolution)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Five densest starless cores of the ALMASOP catalogue, close to the time of forming a central protostar  
  > "Our five targets represent the densest starless cores in the catalogue" (Sample Selection); "The high densities imply that they are at the critical time for the subsequently forming central protostar." (Sample Selection)
- Why this method: The N2H+ F1=0-1 group was used because its hyperfine lines are unresolved (less blending in channel maps) and it is the weakest group (less optically thick); the 7 K CH3OH line was used because it is the strongest  
  > "its hyperfine lines have frequencies that are indistinguishable within our spectral resolution, reducing the impact of line blending in the channel maps" (Observations); "this group had the weakest intrinsic line strength among the three" (Observations); "We primarily used the one at 7 K as it has the strongest line intensity." (Observations)
- Alternative: Thermal desorption by slow-shock dust heating (10 to 30 K) considered but disfavoured relative to mechanical removal because the Eu = 20 K CH3OH line is not or weakly detected; in G208N2 the CH3OH lies on the NE side while the warmer gas is at the south  
  > "In the thermal processes, a slow shock with a velocity jump of 0.4 km/s can heat dust grains from 10 to 30 K" (Implications of the detected Shocks); "our observed CH$_3$OH is distributed on the northeastern side" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
- Alternative: CH3OH in G208N2 might instead be associated with outflows from nearby protostars (e.g., MMS 3)  
  > "the observed CH$_3$OH emission could potentially be associated with nearby protostars" (Discussion and Conclusion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Mass assembly rate may be underestimated: observed CH3OH may not trace all assembling mass and the adopted abundance (from low-mass protostellar ices) may be overestimated  
  > "This value may be underestimated." (Implications of the detected Shocks); "this fractional abundance may be overestimated" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
- Limitation (authors): More detailed simulations (collision geometry, chemical networks, desorption efficiency) are needed to refine the estimate  
  > "would help refine this estimate" (Implications of the detected Shocks)
- Limitation (authors): Survival timescale of gas-phase CH3OH is unknown; survey-type observations of other clouds are needed  
  > "A further question is the timescale over which CH$_3$OH gas can persist in the gas phase" (Discussion and Conclusion); "Further survey-type observations with high spatial and spectral resolution are essential" (Discussion and Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only five cores in one cloud; the shock interpretation rests largely on visual inspection of channel maps with no quantified significance of velocity offsets
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The simulation places CH3OH only where the velocity jump exceeds 0.4 km/s, so reproducing the CH3OH distribution is partly built into the model assumption
