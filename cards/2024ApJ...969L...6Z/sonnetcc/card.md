## Analysis recipe

1. **Search for high-velocity CO gas in channel maps**: Inspect CO J=2-1 channel maps around M17 MIR assuming a source velocity of 20 km/s  
  > "Assuming a radial velocity of $20\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ for M17 MIR, the ALMA data reveal high-velocity CO gas reaching relative velocity" (Results)
   - software: CASA, Spectral-Cube
   - assumption: Radial velocity of M17 MIR is 20 km/s  
  > "Assuming a radial velocity of $20\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ for M17 MIR" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify outflow knots in the PV diagram and integrated CO maps**: Build a PV diagram along the outflow axis, name knots N1-N4 and S1, and map CO integrated over |V-Vsys| >= 7 km/s  
  > "Four major outflow knots of the blueshifted outflow are separated along the outflow axis and are referred to as N1, N2, N3, and N4." (Results); "The integrated CO emission with velocities $|V-V_{\mathrm{sys}}|\geqslant7\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ is shown as the red and blue contours" (Results)
   - parameter: |V-Vsys| >= 7 km/s to exclude cloud emission; EHV defined as |V-Vsys| > 30 km/s  
  > "extremely high velocity (EHV; $|V-V_{\mathrm{sys}}|>30\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$)" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure knot properties and tangential velocities**: Calculate projected distance d, Vmax, Vavg and length L of each knot; L is the FWHM along the outflow axis (N3: between nearest local minima); correct velocities with an inclination of ~16 deg to tangential values  
  > "which is defined as the full width at half maximum along the outflow axis with an exception for N3" (Results); "The tangential velocity of each CO knot is then derived and listed in the brackets" (Results)
   - parameter: Vavg uses only emission with |V-Vsys| > 7 km/s  
  > "The estimation of $V_\mathrm{avg}$ only considers the high-velocity emission ($|V-V_{\mathrm{sys}}|>7\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$) of each knot to exclude the influence of the natal cloud." (Results)
   - assumption: Outflow inclination ~16 deg (derived in Appendix A)  
  > "The inclination angle of the CO outflow is $\sim16\ensuremath{^\circ}$" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive the outflow inclination**: Geometric relation i = arcsin(d/D) from the axial offset d between blue and red components of knot N2 and its perpendicular extent D; cross-checked against 20+-4 deg from SED-derived disk inclination  
  > "The projected offset along the outflow axis between the blue- and redshifted gas emission strongly depends on the inclination angle." (Inclination angle of the outflow); "This relatively small inclination angle ($\sim16\ensuremath{^\circ}\pm3\ensuremath{^\circ}$) agrees well with the value ($20\pm4\ensuremath{^\circ}$) estimated from the disk inclination above." (Inclination angle of the outflow)
   - parameter: D measured in the 24-30 km/s redshifted channel of N2  
  > "From the redshifted component of knot N2 in the $24-30\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ channel" (Inclination angle of the outflow)
   - assumption: Blue and red components symmetric along the outflow axis  
  > "We assume symmetrical blue- and redshifted components along the outflow axis." (Inclination angle of the outflow)
   - assumption: Outflow perpendicular to the disk  
  > "Assuming the outflow is perpendicular to the disk" (Inclination angle of the outflow)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with 20+-4 deg inferred from disk inclination of SED models  
  > "This relatively small inclination angle ($\sim16\ensuremath{^\circ}\pm3\ensuremath{^\circ}$) agrees well with the value ($20\pm4\ensuremath{^\circ}$) estimated from the disk inclination above." (Inclination angle of the outflow)
5. **Derive ejection velocity, dynamical times and burst durations**: Assume the nearest EHV subknots N1-1 and S1-1 trace ejecta from the ongoing outburst that began 9 yr before the 2019 observations to get ~421 km/s; apply this constant velocity to all knots for t_dyn = d/v and Delta t = L/v  
  > "It is reasonable to assume that the closest EHV subknots N1-1 and S1-1 represent the mass ejection caused by the ongoing accretion outburst, which started 9 yr before the ALMA observations in 2019 October." (Discussion); "We also estimate the duration ($\Delta t$) of each clustered accretion outburst by dividing the length ($L$) of each CO knot by the previously assumed tangential ejection velocity" (Discussion)
   - assumption: Same tangential ejection velocity (~421 km/s) for all outbursts; no slowing for N1 subknots  
  > "We assume that the tangential ejection velocity triggered by this earlier accretion outburst around the 1990s is $\sim421\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, the same as for the ongoing accretion outburst." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 35 yr dynamical time of N1-2/S1-2 agrees with the 1990s outburst start between 1984 and 1993 from earlier work  
  > "A dynamical time of 35 years agrees fairly well with that of the major accretion outburst that likely began between 1984 and 1993" (Discussion)
6. **Estimate CO knot masses**: Optically thin CO column density with Tex = 50 K, integrate emission above 7 km/s from Vsys inside the lowest contour per knot, convert to mass, then correct for missed flux assuming a 2D Gaussian knot  
  > "We utilize the methodology outlined in (cite: 2015PASP..127..266M) and (cite: 2019ApJ...886..130L) and assume a standard constant excitation temperature of 50 K to determine the CO column density of the outflow" (Outflow mass estimation); "We then recover the flux by assuming the knots extended as a 2D Gaussian distribution." (Outflow mass estimation)
   - parameter: Integration over emission exceeding 7 km/s relative to systemic velocity  
  > "We integrate emissions exceeding 7 $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ relative to the systemic velocity in the region to exclude the low-velocity emission from the cloud." (Outflow mass estimation)
   - parameter: Recovery fractions: 30% for N1 and S1; 41%, 57%, 49% for N2, N3, N4  
  > "Thirty percent of the mass for knots N1 and S1 were recovered, while 41%, 57%, and 49% of the mass for N2, N3, and N4, respectively, were recovered." (Outflow mass estimation)
   - assumption: Tex = 50 K; optically thin CO  
  > "Following the optical-thin assumption," (Outflow mass estimation)
   - assumption: CO-to-H2 abundance 1e-4; distance 2.0 kpc  
  > "We adopt the CO-to-H$_2$ abundance of $10^{-4}$ (cite: 1987ApJ...315..621B)" (Outflow mass estimation); "$d\approx2.0$ kpc is the distance to the source" (Outflow mass estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive outflow and ejection rates and compare with accretion rate**: Mdot_out = M_out/Delta t per knot; ejecta mass taken as one-tenth of the outflow mass; Mdot_ejec compared with a mean accretion rate of ~1e-3 Msun/yr  
  > "The mean outflow rate $\dot M_\mathrm{out}$ during each clustered accretion outburst is $M_\mathrm{out}/\Delta t$" (Episodic Mass Accretion and Ejection in the Formation Process of Massive Stars); "The mass of the ejecta is roughly one-tenth that of the outflow (cite: 2021MNRAS.500.3594R; 2022MNRAS.510.2552R)." (Episodic Mass Accretion and Ejection in the Formation Process of Massive Stars)
   - assumption: Outflow mass accumulates through episodic ejection during clustered outbursts of duration Delta t  
  > "We assume that the mass ($M_\mathrm{out}$) of a CO knot accumulates through episodic mass ejection during contemporary clustered accretion outbursts" (Episodic Mass Accretion and Ejection in the Formation Process of Massive Stars)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ejection-to-accretion ratio compared with S255IR NIRS 3 and low- to intermediate-mass YSOs  
  > "comparable to the ratio of the outbursting MYSO S255IR NIRS 3 estimated from its radio jet (cite: 2023A&A...680A.110C)" (Episodic Mass Accretion and Ejection in the Formation Process of Massive Stars)

## Qualitative findings

- CO J=2-1 shows a collimated bipolar north-south outflow with four blueshifted knots (N1-N4) and one redshifted knot (S1); EHV emission in N1 and S1 is jetlike with subknots  
  > "The ALMA CO $J=2-1$ data reveal a collimated and bipolar north–south outflow from M17 MIR." (abstract)
- First detection of a knotty outflow from an outbursting massive protostar  
  > "The first detection of the knotty outflow from an outbursting massive protostar suggests" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Outflow knots along the axis can record the history of accretion outbursts; no multi-epoch radio or near-IR high-resolution data exist for M17 MIR  
  > "molecular outflows launched during accretion outbursts could be recorded in the form of separated outflow knots or bullets along the outflow axis" (Introduction); "Currently, neither radio continuum nor near-infrared high-resolution data from multiple epochs are available for M17 MIR." (Discussion)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dynamical times of N2-N4 are lower limits because ejecta may slow down  
  > "the dynamical times of the CO knots N2 to N4 provide us the lower limits on the timescales of the ejecta traced by them" (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Early phase of the 1990s outburst poorly constrained for lack of earlier mid-IR light curves  
  > "the beginning phase of this earlier accretion outburst is poorly constrained due to the lack of mid-IR light curve data before the 1990s" (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Inclination uncertainty of 3 deg changes tangential velocities by about 20%  
  > "The $3^\circ$ uncertainty may lead to approximately a 20% underestimation or overestimation of the tangential velocities" (Inclination angle of the outflow)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 421 km/s ejection velocity rests on assuming the innermost EHV subknots trace the outburst that began 9 yr before 2019; no proper motions are measured
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-epoch data: knot ages and velocities are not independently verified
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow-to-ejecta mass ratio of one-tenth and Tex = 50 K are assumed without uncertainty propagation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties given for most derived masses and rates
