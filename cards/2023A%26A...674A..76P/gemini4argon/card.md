## Science

- Fields: star_formation, milky_way_ism
- Question: Investigate the variations of the core mass function (CMF) in the W43-MM2&MM3 mini-starburst ridge and establish the link between the CMF shape and local environmental conditions (cloud density structure, evolutionary state, and core mass segregation).  
  > "Among the most central open questions regarding the initial mass function (IMF) of stars is the impact of environment on the shape of the core mass function (CMF) and thus potentially on the IMF." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Decompose continuum images and define six subregions**: Complex wavelet multi-scale decomposition of the 1.3 mm and 3 mm bsens continuum images using MnGSeg; subregion boundaries visually defined by following minimum inflection points (minima or saddle points) on the wavelet-filtered scale of 0.11-0.19 pc (4''-7'').  
  > "To define the boundaries of these subregions, we used the MnGSeg technique developed by (cite: robitaille2019)." (Splitting the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge into six subregions); "Five subregions of $\sim$0.5–1 pc sizes were identified in both Figs. 2a–b; their boundaries were visually defined by following the minimum inflection points, corresponding to minima or saddle points." (Splitting the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge into six subregions)
   - software: MnGSeg
   - parameter: Wavelet-filtered spatial scale of 0.11-0.19 pc (4''-7'' at 5.5 kpc)  
  > "Figure 2 shows, for the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge, the wavelet filtered spatial scale tracing cloud structures of 0.11–0.19 pc ($4\ensuremath{''}-7\ensuremath{''}$ at 5.5 kpc)" (Splitting the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge into six subregions)
   - assumption: Distance d = 5.5 kpc  
  > "Figure 2 shows, for the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge, the wavelet filtered spatial scale tracing cloud structures of 0.11–0.19 pc ($4\ensuremath{''}-7\ensuremath{''}$ at 5.5 kpc)" (Splitting the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge into six subregions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Subregion uncertainties estimated by slightly varying the integration areas  
  > "Uncertainties are estimated by slightly varying the integration areas of Figs." (Sect. 2.2)
2. **Compute subregion column densities, masses, and volume densities**: Compute pixel-by-pixel H2 column density and subregion masses from 1.3 mm peak fluxes (bsens denoised image) and the Paper III dust temperature map using the Planck function, adding a Herschel-derived background column density offset; derive volume densities assuming spherical geometry.  
  > "Because the thermal dust emission of clouds is mostly optically thin at 1.3 mm, we computed the column densities and masses of the W43-MM2&MM3 subregions, under the assumption of optically thin thermal dust emission." (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "Subregion masses, $M^{\rm subregion}$, are computed from the 1.3 mm peak fluxes and dust temperatures measured for each pixel $i$ within the subregion area $A$" (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "Volume densities of subregions are computed assuming a spherical geometry and a line-of-sight radius equal to the equivalent plane-of-the-sky radius of its defined area, $A$:" (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
   - parameter: nu_1.3mm = 228.9 GHz, Omega_beam = pi * 0.51'' * 0.40'' / (4 ln 2), Omega_pixel = (0.1'')^2  
  > "where $\Omega_{\rm beam}=\frac{\pi \; 0.51\ensuremath{''} \times 0.40\ensuremath{''}}{4\,\ln{2}}$ is the beam solid angle" (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "The frequency $\nu_{\rm 1.3mm} = 228.9$ GHz is taken from Paper II (cite: ginsburg2022)" (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "where $d$ is the $5.5$ kpc distance of W43 (cite: zhang2014) and $\Omega_{\rm pixel}=(0.1\ensuremath{''})^2$ is the pixel area of our images." (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
   - parameter: Background offsets N_H2^bckg: (0.55 +/- 0.10) x 10^23 cm^-2 for Outskirts and MM51, (0.70 +/- 0.15) x 10^23 cm^-2 for MM10 and MM12, (1.0 +/- 0.2) x 10^23 cm^-2 for MM2 and MM3  
  > "We therefore assumed a different background column density for each subregion: $N_{\rm H_2}^{\rm bckg} = (0.55\pm 0.10) \times10^{23}~\rm cm^{-2}$ for Outskirts and MM51, $N_{\rm H_2}^{\rm bckg} = (0.70\pm 0.15) \times10^{23}~\rm cm^{-2}$ for MM10 and MM12, and $N_{\rm H_2}^{\rm bckg} = (1.0\pm 0.2) \times10^{23}~\rm cm^{-2}$ for MM2 and MM3." (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
   - assumption: Optically thin thermal dust emission at 1.3 mm; kappa_1.3mm = 0.01 cm^2 g^-1 (beta = 1.5); mu = 2.8 (10% helium abundance); d = 5.5 kpc; T_dust = 20 to 65 K from Paper III; spherical geometry for volume density  
  > "where $\Omega_{\rm beam}=\frac{\pi \; 0.51\ensuremath{''} \times 0.40\ensuremath{''}}{4\,\ln{2}}$ is the beam solid angle, $\mu = 2.8$ is the mean molecular weight per hydrogen molecule (assuming an helium abundance of 10%)" (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "we adopted a dust opacity per unit (gas $+$ dust) mass at 1.3 mm and adapted for cold cloud structures: $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm}=0.01\,\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}$ (cite: OssenkopfHenning1994)." (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with PPMAP column density map of Dell'Ova et al. (in prep.) and Herschel eta-PDF  
  > "It shows that a value of $N_{\rm H_2}^{\rm bckg}\sim 0.7\times10^{23}~\rm cm^{-2}$ indeed allows these $\eta$-PDF functions to be consistent with each other." (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "This column density image is consistent with the one of Dell’Ova et al. (in prep.), which was produced by the point process mapping of the temperature (PPMAP) procedure (cite: marsh2015) and achieves a $2.5\ensuremath{''}$ resolution." (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
3. **Characterize subregion core populations, protostellar fractions, and CMF slopes**: Assign the 205 cores from Paper III to the six subregions; compute protostellar fractions using Paper V classifications; perform two-sample KS test on MM3 core masses; fit cumulative CMFs with single power laws using a 5000-iteration bootstrap procedure coupled with the Clauset/Alstott maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) method.  
  > "We separated the 205 cores of W43-MM2&MM3 into six core populations associated with the subregions defined in Sect. 2.2." (Subregion core populations); "We ran a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test to assess the likelihood that two distributions are drawn from the same parent sample (null hypothesis)." (Subregion core populations); "In detail, for each subregion, we computed the bootstrapping probability density function of 5000 slopes of CMF high-mass ends, which are fitted by power laws using the (cite: alstott2014) method." (Varying subregion CMFs)
   - parameter: 5000 bootstrap iterations; fit range from 90% completeness limit (+/- 0.2 M_sun) to maximum core mass in each subregion  
  > "In detail, for each subregion, we computed the bootstrapping probability density function of 5000 slopes of CMF high-mass ends, which are fitted by power laws using the (cite: alstott2014) method." (Varying subregion CMFs); "we computed the bootstrapping probability density function of 5000 slopes of CMF high-mass ends, which are fitted by power laws using the" (Sect. 3.1.2)
   - assumption: Single power law cumulative CMF N(> log M) proportional to M^alpha above the 90% completeness limit; core masses vary in a 2-sigma Gaussian range [M_min - M_max]  
  > "The CMFs of all six subregions are presented in their complementary cumulative distribution form (cumulative form, for short) and fitted by single power laws of the form $N(> \log M) \propto M^\alpha$, from their 90% completeness limits to the mass of their most massive core" (Varying subregion CMFs); "To account for uncertainties associated with the core mass estimates, the mass of each randomly drawn core is allowed to vary in a $2\sigma$ Gaussian range of $[M_{\rm min}-M_{\rm max}]$." (Varying subregion CMFs)
   - fitting: Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) method based on the KS metric (Clauset et al. 2009; Alstott et al. 2014) within a 5000-draw bootstrap; bootstrap histograms fitted by exponentially modified Gaussians (EMGs) with negative skewness; 68.2% confidence intervals used for -sigma and +sigma.  
  > "To do so, we used the maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) method of (cite: clauset2009) and (cite: alstott2014), which is based on the KS metric and dedicated to probability laws fitted by power laws." (Varying subregion CMFs); "To account for the asymmetry of these bootstrapping functions, their histograms are fitted by exponentially modified Gaussians (EMGs) with a negative skewness." (Varying subregion CMFs); "For each subregion, uncertainties on the power-law index, $-\sigma$ and $+\sigma$, are estimated as the ranges of indices that are below and above, respectively, the peak of the bootstrapping probability density function and contain 68.2% of the fitted indices." (Varying subregion CMFs)
   - validation: Compared bootstrap CMF power-law index for total ridge (-0.93 +0.07/-0.10) with direct Alstott et al. (2014) method (-0.96 +/- 0.11) and linear regression (-0.95 +/- 0.04); tested constant dust temperature impact (changes index by at most 18%).  
  > "The power-law index we measured with our bootstrapping procedure, $\alpha=-0.93_{-0.10}^{+0.07}$, is consistent with those fitted by the method of (cite: alstott2014) and by linear regression, $\alpha_{\rm Alstott}=-0.96\pm0.11$ and $\alpha_{\rm LinReg}=-0.95\pm0.04$, respectively." (Varying subregion CMFs); "taking a constant dust temperature for all cores, including the most massive ones associated with hot cores, would change the power-law indices fitted to the subregion CMF by at most $18\%$." (Varying subregion CMFs)
4. **Construct and fit column density probability distribution functions (eta-PDFs)**: Construct histograms of the natural logarithm of normalized column density (eta-PDFs) for each subregion using the ALMA column density image, the getsf core-subtracted background image, and the Herschel W43-Main image; define departure/end points and fit two consecutive power-law tails (s_2 and s_3).  
  > "For each subregion, we analyzed the PDF of the ALMA image and used the PDF of the background image of its cores to define the column density range where cores dominate and estimate the variation in the power-law exponents of the PDF tail with and without cores." (Varying column density eta-PDFs); "Observations generally analyze the PDF of the natural logarithm of the normalized column density, $\eta \equiv \ln{(N_{\rm H_2}/ \overline{N_{\rm H_2}})}$ with $\overline{N_{\rm H_2}}$ the mean column density, and fit the PDF with power laws of exponent $s$:" (Varying column density eta-PDFs); "At column densities higher than their peak, the ALMA $\eta$-PDFs of most W43-MM2&MM3 subregions exhibit a tail with a continuous flattening that we describe by two consecutive tails and fit by power laws (see 6)." (Varying column density eta-PDFs)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: First departure point: first bin 50% higher than symmetrized ascending distribution (or 100 x 10^21 cm^-2); second departure point: first inflection point (maximum column density of getsf background image for MM51, MM10, MM12) or boundary between core-dominated and background-dominated column densities (MM2, MM3); end point: maximum column density in subregion  
  > "We first symmetrized the ascending distribution of each ALMA $\eta$-PDF, badly fitted by a lognormal, and define the first departure point of the $\eta$-PDF tail as the first bin higher by 50% with respect to the symmetrized function (and $100\times 10^{21}$ cm$^{-2}$ otherwise)." (Varying column density eta-PDFs); "Then, the second departure point is on the one hand considered the first inflection point observed on the $\eta$-PDF tail of the subregions MM51, MM10, and MM12 (see Figs. 6b–d)." (Varying column density eta-PDFs); "We therefore took as second departure point the boundary between ranges of column densities dominated by their cores and by their background image (see Figs. 6e–f)." (Varying column density eta-PDFs)
   - assumption: Power-law relation p_eta proportional to (N_H2)^s in the tail regimes  
  > "p_\eta = N_{\rm H_2}\times p_{N_{\rm H_2}} \propto (N_{\rm H_2})^s ." (Varying column density eta-PDFs)
   - fitting: Power-law fits to the second (s_2) and third (s_3) tails; Herschel eta-PDF fitted by the sum of a lognormal function (< 1.5 x 10^23 cm^-2) and two consecutive power-law tails following Schneider et al. (2022).  
  > "We used the procedure of (cite: schneider2022) and fit the Herschel $\eta$-PDF by the sum of a lognormal function at column densities lower than $1.5\times10^{23}~\rm cm^{-2}$ and two consecutive tails (see 13b)." (Varying column density eta-PDFs); "Finally, the range between the second departure point and, as an end point, the maximum column density detected in subregions" (Sect. 3.2)
   - validation: Uncertainties estimated by varying bin numbers, fit range limits, and background column density offsets; tested constant dust temperature assumption (changes s_2 and s_3 by <= 10%).  
  > "Uncertainties are estimated by varying the number of bins, the limits of the fitted ranges, and the column density offset of col. 4." (Table 3 notes); "The assumption on the dust temperature used to compute the column density image has a negligible effect on the $\eta$-PDFs since, with a constant temperature on all pixels, the power-law exponents of their $s_2$ and $s_3$ tails are not changed by more than $10\%$." (Varying column density eta-PDFs)
5. **Quantify core mass segregation in each subregion**: Compute two mass segregation indicators in tandem: (1) ratio of local core surface density of the five most massive cores (using 6 closest neighbors) to the subregion average, (Sigma_6cores)_massive / mean(Sigma_6cores); and (2) mass segregation ratio Lambda_MSR using the minimum spanning tree (MST) method.  
  > "The first one measures, for each core using its six closest neighbors, the local core surface density of a subregion, $\Sigma_{\rm 6\,cores}$ (cite: maschbergerClarke2011)." (Varying core mass segregation); "The first one measures, for each core using its six closest neighbors, the local core surface density of a subregion" (Sect. 3.3); "The second indicator we chose, the mass segregation ratio of cores, $\Lambda_{\rm MSR}$ (cite: allison2009b), has been used for cores in several high-mass star-forming studies" (Varying core mass segregation)
   - parameter: 6 closest neighbors for Sigma_6cores; 5 most massive cores for (Sigma_6cores)_massive; N_MST >= 3; 500 sets of N_MST uniformly randomly distributed cores for Lambda_MSR; thresholds: (Sigma_6cores)_massive / mean(Sigma_6cores) > 2 for significant segregation, and 1.5 < Lambda_MSR < 2 (moderate), 2 <= Lambda_MSR < 3 (high), Lambda_MSR >= 3 (very high)

## Qualitative findings

- The high-mass end of the subregion CMFs varies across W43-MM2&MM3 from close to or steeper than the Salpeter slope in quiescent/pre-burst subregions (Outskirts, MM51, MM10) to top-heavy in burst and post-burst subregions (MM12, MM2, MM3).  
  > "The high-mass end ($>$1 $\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$) of the subregion CMFs varies from close to the Salpeter slope (quiescent) to top-heavy (burst and post-burst)." (abstract); "The measured high-mass end of subregion CMFs in their cumulative form varies from being top-heavy, $\alpha\in [-0.95;-0.59]$, to close to the Salpeter slope, $\alpha\in [-1.54;-1.16]$" (Summary and conclusions)
- Subregions with flat second eta-PDF tails display top-heavy CMFs, whereas subregions with steep second eta-PDF tails have CMFs close to the Salpeter slope.  
  > "We find that subregions with flat second $\eta$-PDF tails display top-heavy CMFs." (abstract); "We find that subregions with flat second $\eta$-PDF tails display top-heavy CMFs." (abstract)
- Three out of six subregions (MM2, MM10, and MM3) show evidence of core mass segregation, with MM2 displaying a very high level, but there is no one-to-one correspondence between core mass segregation and a top-heavy CMF.  
  > "In conclusion, our analysis of the core mass segregation suggests that three out of the six W43-MM2&MM3 subregions could be mass-segregated, the MM2, MM3, and MM10 subregions (see 1)." (Varying core mass segregation); "In that respect, the link between the level of core mass segregation and the slope of the CMF high-mass end may exist but not as a one-to-one correspondence." (Correlation trends between the CMF and the basic cloud and core properties)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: W43-MM2&MM3 harbors a rich cluster with a statistically significant number of cores (205 cores) characterized in Paper III; the 1.3 mm continuum image was chosen over 3 mm for subregion properties because it is less contaminated by free-free emission.  
  > "W43-MM2&MM3 is a useful test case for environmental studies because it harbors a rich cluster that contains a statistically significant number of cores (specifically, 205 cores), which was previously characterized in Paper III." (abstract); "We chose the 1.3 mm image because, unlike the 3 mm image, it is less contaminated by free-free emission." (Splitting the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge into six subregions)
- Why this method: MnGSeg multi-scale decomposition separates the inhomogeneous ridge into 0.5-1 pc subregions with more homogeneous characteristics; a bootstrap MLE approach accounts for small-number statistics, core mass uncertainties, and completeness uncertainties; two mass segregation indicators (Sigma_6cores and Lambda_MSR) are used in tandem following Parker & Goodwin (2015).  
  > "We separated it into subregions of $\sim$0.5–1 pc scales, which, in the framework of dynamical cloud formation, would correspond to cloud subparts with more homogeneous characteristics and star formation activities than the whole cloud." (Splitting the W43-MM2&MM3 ridge into six subregions); "Given the small-number statistics for the core sample of each W43-MM2&MM3 subregion, we did not directly apply the (cite: alstott2014) method to their CMF but used it in a bootstrap procedure that simultaneously estimates the most likely CMF power-law index and its uncertainty." (Varying subregion CMFs); "We followed the recommendations of (cite: parkerGoodwin2015) and used two different indicators in tandem." (Varying core mass segregation)
- Alternative: Direct application of the Alstott et al. (2014) MLE method and linear regression on the cumulative CMF without bootstrapping (used as a validation check on the total ridge CMF).  
  > "To test the validity of our approach, we compared the power-law index fitted by the bootstrapping procedure described above with those directly fitted by the method of (cite: alstott2014) and a linear regression on its cumulative form." (Varying subregion CMFs)
- Alternative: Assuming a constant dust temperature across all cores or all pixels when computing core masses and column density eta-PDFs (tested and found to change CMF indices by at most 18% and eta-PDF tail slopes by <= 10%).  
  > "taking a constant dust temperature for all cores, including the most massive ones associated with hot cores, would change the power-law indices fitted to the subregion CMF by at most $18\%$." (Varying subregion CMFs); "The assumption on the dust temperature used to compute the column density image has a negligible effect on the $\eta$-PDFs since, with a constant temperature on all pixels, the power-law exponents of their $s_2$ and $s_3$ tails are not changed by more than $10\%$." (Varying column density eta-PDFs)
- Alternative: Applying the Paper III optical thickness correction to the column density map (would only change values by 15% toward the center of four cores).  
  > "Using the correction for optical thickness proposed by Paper III, would only change its values by 15% toward the center of four cores (cite: pouteau2022)." (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Missing short spacings (scales > 5.6'' at 1.3 mm) filter out large-scale background emission, creating negative bowls and making background-filtered mass assumptions invalid for MM51, Outskirts, and the Total area, as well as rendering the first eta-PDF tail unobservable with ALMA 12 m data alone.  
  > "Since, due to interferometer filtering, scales larger than $\sim$5.6$\ensuremath{''}$ are missing (see Sect. 2.1), we added a $N_{\rm H_2}^{\rm bckg}\sim 0.7\times10^{23}~\rm cm^{-2}$ level measured at the periphery of the cloud ridge on the Herschel column density image" (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "This assumption is correct for the centrally concentrated subregions MM2, MM3, MM10, and MM12, but totally incorrect for the MM51 and Outskirts subregions as well as the total imaged area." (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "These two ALMA $\eta$-PDF tails are called second and third tails ($s_2$ and $s_3$) because the first $\eta$-PDF tail ($s_1$) observed with Herschel data is filtered by the ALMA interferometer." (Varying column density eta-PDFs)
- Limitation (authors): Absolute uncertainties in column densities, subregion masses, and volume densities are a factor of about two due to poor knowledge of the absolute value of dust opacity per unit mass, and spherical geometry assumptions are questionable for MM10, MM51, and Outskirts.  
  > "The volume densities for MM10, MM51, and Outskirts are uncertain because the spherical assumption is more questionable for these subregions, and even more important for Outskirts" (Subregion column density and mass estimates); "We estimate that the absolute uncertainties in column densities, subregion masses, and volume densities arise primarily from our poor knowledge of the absolute value of the dust opacity per unit mass and are a factor of about two." (Subregion column density and mass estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Small-number statistics in the core catalogs of Outskirts, MM51, and MM12 mean their CMF high-mass ends are barely constrained, and having only six subregions prevents a reliable statistical estimate of the significance and strength of the observed correlations.  
  > "However, the small number of W43-MM2&MM3 subregions and the large uncertainties in the masses prevent us from making a reliable estimate of the significance and strength of these correlations." (Subregion core populations); "Since the core catalogs of the Outskirts, MM51, and MM12 subregions have small-number statistics, their CMF high-mass end is barely constrained." (Varying subregion CMFs); "We observed a correlation trend between these power-law indices but the small number of W43-MM2&MM3 subregions and the large uncertainties on $\alpha$ and $s_2$ prevent us from making a reliable estimate of the significance and strength of this correlation." (Comparison with observational studies in the literature)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Subregion boundaries were defined visually along inflection points of the wavelet-filtered map rather than via an automated segmentation algorithm, introducing subjective boundary choices.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adding a constant per-subregion column density offset from 25'' Herschel data to 0.46'' ALMA 12 m continuum images is only a first-order approximation for missing short-spacing (7 m and Total Power) flux.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlation claims between CMF slope, eta-PDF slope, and protostellar fraction rely on only 6 subregions (4 with measured s_2) with overlapping bootstrap uncertainty intervals.
