## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure source positions, flux densities, and sizes from continuum images**: Fit elliptical Gaussians to detected sources in ALMA and VLA continuum images and merge the ALMA and VLA catalogs  
  > "We fit elliptical Gaussians using the imfit task of CASA 4.7.2, measuring position, flux density, and source size." (Data Analysis); "We use a merged catalog from the ALMA and VLA observations to ensure that we include sources from both sets of observations in the event that a source was detected with the VLA and not with ALMA (and vice versa)." (Data Analysis)
   - software: CASA 4.7.2 (imfit)
   - fitting: Elliptical Gaussian fitting in image plane  
  > "We fit elliptical Gaussians using the imfit task of CASA 4.7.2, measuring position, flux density, and source size." (Data Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Estimate local YSO surface density around each protostar**: Compute local YSO surface density using the 11th nearest neighbor distance in infrared YSO catalogs (corrected with X-ray catalogs in Orion), adding ALMA/VLA detections if no catalog source is within 2.5''  
  > "We used the surface density of YSOs measured toward each protostar position, using the 11th nearest neighbor, and then dividing 10 by the area of a circle at the radius of the 11th nearest neighbor to determine the local YSO surface density (cite: gutermuth2005; gutermuth2011; megeath2016)." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "If there was no source in the catalog within 2.5$''$ of an ALMA or VLA detection, the ALMA or VLA source was added to the catalog for computation of the YSO surface density." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion)
   - parameter: 11th nearest neighbor; 2.5'' matching radius; 30 pc^-2 boundary between high and low YSO density  
  > "We used the surface density of YSOs measured toward each protostar position, using the 11th nearest neighbor, and then dividing 10 by the area of a circle at the radius of the 11th nearest neighbor to determine the local YSO surface density (cite: gutermuth2005; gutermuth2011; megeath2016)." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "If there was no source in the catalog within 2.5$''$ of an ALMA or VLA detection, the ALMA or VLA source was added to the catalog for computation of the YSO surface density." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "We set our boundary between low and high density regions at a YSO density of 30 pc$^{-2}$ such that there were comparable numbers of protostars in the regions assigned as high and low YSO densities." (Relationship Between Multiplicity and YSO Density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainty of 33% from Casertano & Hut (1985)  
  > "This results in an uncertainty of 33% in the $\Sigma_{YSO}$ measurement (cite: casertano1985); equations describing the surface density estimator are provided in (cite: megeath2016)." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion)
3. **Associate multiple systems hierarchically**: Iterative inside-out nearest-neighbor search in 100 logarithmically spaced radial bins from 15 au to 10^4 au, replacing paired sources with their unweighted geometric midpoint  
  > "Starting with 15 au as the smallest search radius, which is less than the most compact multiple in the sample such that we should not find companions, we search for companions with increasing separation in 100 logarithmically spaced radial bins, ending with a maximum separation of $10^4$ au for association of two continuum sources." (Associating Multiple Systems); "When two sources are associated, they are grouped as a multiple system, and we remove their individual catalog entries and replace them with a single entry at their average position, corresponding to the geometric midpoint between the components without any weighting." (Associating Multiple Systems)
   - parameter: Search radius: 15 au to 10^4 au in 100 logarithmically spaced radial bins  
  > "Starting with 15 au as the smallest search radius, which is less than the most compact multiple in the sample such that we should not find companions, we search for companions with increasing separation in 100 logarithmically spaced radial bins, ending with a maximum separation of $10^4$ au for association of two continuum sources." (Associating Multiple Systems)
   - assumption: Spatial association in projected separation alone assigns multiplicity; unweighted geometric midpoint represents paired position  
  > "Thus, regardless of the inherent limitations, we use spatial association alone to assign multiplicity." (Data Analysis); "When two sources are associated, they are grouped as a multiple system, and we remove their individual catalog entries and replace them with a single entry at their average position, corresponding to the geometric midpoint between the components without any weighting." (Associating Multiple Systems)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with manual primary-centered association used in Tobin et al. (2016a)  
  > "Our results would be different if we applied a simpler approach by assigning A as the primary based on its luminosity or flux density of dust emission, as had been done in (cite: tobin2016a)." (Associating Multiple Systems)
4. **Calculate companion probabilities and multiplicity/companion fractions (MF and CF)**: Compute Bayesian probability P(companion|detection) using Poisson expectation of unassociated YSOs, iteratively updating P(companion) with CF until convergence; round sum of component probabilities to assign system order and compute MF and CF with Wilson score or Poisson uncertainties  
  > "We ultimately need to determine the probability of whether a detected source is a companion or not. For this we make use of Bayes Theorem to determine" (Appendix A: Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "the multiplicity order is determined by the rounded sum of companion probabilities for all possible companions that comprise the system" (Multiplicity Statistics); "The uncertainties on the MF and CF are calculated using binomial statistics, specifically using the Wilson score interval (cite: wilson1927)" (Multiplicity Statistics)
   - parameter: P(detection|companion) = 0.75; probability cutoff = 0.001; 3 iterations for convergence (< 0.005 change); P(companion) = 0.14 (<500 au), 0.19 (<10^3 au), 0.46 (<10^4 au); z = 1.0 for Wilson interval; Poisson uncertainty for CF > 0.5  
  > "We find that running the multiplicity statistics three times is sufficient to reach convergence, such that the calculated MFs and CFs are no longer changing. $P(companion)$ depends on separation, so for separations less than 500, $10^3$, and $10^4$ au, $P(companion)$ = 0.14, 0.19 and 0.46, respectively; these values reflect the combined sample of Orion and Perseus." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "If a system has a probability below 0.001, then it is not included as a potential multiple because the probability is too low." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "Thus, instead use Poisson statistics for calculating $\sigma_{CF}$ when CF $>$ 0.5." (Multiplicity Statistics)
   - assumption: Survey sensitivity of ~1 M_earth detects 75% of disk-bearing YSOs and protostellar companions  
  > "Based on our survey dust mass sensitivity of $\sim$1 M$_{\ensuremath{\oplus}}$, compared to deeper surveys of YSOs in Lupus (cite: ansdell2016), we would detect 75% of disk-bearing YSOs." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "We assume that $P(detection|companion)$ = 0.75, because, as mentioned above, our sensitivity is expected to detect 75% of YSOs, which also applies to protostars, under the assumption that they are drawn from the same general population of dusty disks." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked sensitivity to P(detection|companion) between 0.25 and 0.9, verified against unrounded probability sum method (Appendix A.4), and compared with unweighted statistics (Appendix A.5)  
  > "We find that if we used values anywhere between 0.25 to 0.9, the probability of a given continuum detection being a companion is not significantly changed; in any event 0.75 is likely a reasonable estimate." (Probability of a Detected Source being a Companion); "There are alternative methods to calculate the MF and CF including the probabilities, and we describe one such method in Appendix A.4 that we used as a sanity check." (Multiplicity Statistics)
5. **Construct and statistically compare companion separation distributions and luminosity distributions**: Generate separation histograms (bin size 0.25 in log10(au)) and probability-weighted CDFs; randomly sample CDFs 1000 times according to companion probabilities and perform one-sided or two-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests  
  > "These histograms are generated with bin sizes of 0.25 for the log$_{10}$ of the projected separation in au." (Separation Distributions); "To evaluate this, we constructed (CDFs) from the separation distributions and used their respective companion probabilities to compare the distributions by randomly sampling the separation distribution CDFs 1000 times and comparing each of these randomly sampled CDFs using the KS test as described in the Appendix A.2." (Quantitative Separation Distribution Comparisons); "We make use of the scipy implementation of the KS test." (Comparing Separation Distributions)
   - software: scipy
   - parameter: Histogram bin size = 0.25 dex; 1000 Monte Carlo CDF realizations; significance threshold p <= 0.01  
  > "These histograms are generated with bin sizes of 0.25 for the log$_{10}$ of the projected separation in au." (Separation Distributions); "We then repeat this random CDF sampling and KS test 1000 times, recording the median likelihood, the quartiles of the distribution, and the fraction of realizations that have likelihoods in excess of our cutoff value for significant rejection of the null hypothesis that the two distributions are drawn from the same parent distribution." (Comparing Separation Distributions); "We consider low median likelihoods (p $\le$ 0.01) resulting from the KS test to be evidence that the two populations of multiples are not drawn from the same parent distribution, rejecting the null hypothesis." (Comparing Separation Distributions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared one-sided and two-sided KS tests for analytic functions, and tested assigning mixed-class pairs to the more evolved class instead of the younger class  
  > "The one-sided KS test agrees with the two-sided KS test when the number of samples in the analytic CDF are much larger." (Comparing Separation Distributions); "We tested creating our separation distributions where the more evolved Class was selected, rather then the less-evolved Class and the separation distributions do not change significantly." (Multiplicity Statistics Reporting Per Protostar Class)

## Qualitative findings

- The companion separation distribution for Orion protostars as a whole and for Class 0 protostars is double-peaked (peaks near ~100 au and ~10^3-4000 au with a local minimum near ~300 au) and statistically inconsistent with a log-flat distribution and with solar-type field stars.  
  > "The companion separation distribution as a whole is double peaked and inconsistent with the separation distribution of solar-type field stars, while the separation distribution of Flat Spectrum protostars is consistent solar-type field stars." (Abstract); "The histogram for the full sample (see Figure 9) shows some structure with a peak at $\sim$75 au and another peak at $\sim$4000 au. Multiples exist between these two peaks, but there is a local minimum that is visually apparent at $\sim$300 au, and the histogram does not begin rising again until separations $\ge$10$^3$ au." (Separation Distributions)
- Multiplicity statistics and separation distributions of the Perseus molecular cloud are statistically consistent with those of Orion.  
  > "The multiplicity statistics and companion separation distributions of the Perseus star-forming region are consistent with those of Orion." (Abstract); "We find that the null hypothesis cannot be ruled out for any of the Orion and Perseus separation distributions, meaning that there is no statistical evidence for the Perseus and Orion separation distributions to be drawn from different samples." (Orion and Perseus Comparisons)
- Three close multiple systems (HOPS-361-C, HOPS-361-E, HOPS-288, plus HOPS-384) have close companions detected only by the VLA at 9 mm and not by ALMA at 0.87 mm, likely due to optically thick 0.87 mm dust emission or angular resolution limits.  
  > "We find that three systems only have VLA (9 mm) detections of their companions that were undetected by ALMA at 0.87 mm. The non-detection by ALMA is most likely due to the high optical depth of the surrounding dust emission, obscuring the companion at shorter wavelengths." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Submillimeter to centimeter interferometry with ALMA and VLA provides high angular resolution (~20-40 au) and sensitivity to penetrate dense envelopes around Class 0 and Class I protostars where infrared observations are obscured.  
  > "Interferometry at submillimeter to centimeter wavelengths is hence required to examine multiplicity during the earliest stage of protostellar evolution where shorter wavelengths are highly obscured and longer wavelength imaging has low angular resolution." (Introduction); "The factor of $\sim$10 increases in sensitivity to continuum emission and routine observations at high angular resolution enables all the practical limitations of earlier multiplicity studies to be overcome." (Introduction)
- Why this method: The automated inside-out geometric-midpoint association algorithm avoids arbitrary primary selection (since mm/cm flux does not correlate directly with stellar mass), is less prone to human judgment bias, and is reproducible and directly comparable to simulation analyses.  
  > "In particular, the lack of direct correlation between mm/cm flux and protostellar mass precludes the use of the “center of light" as a center of mass proxy." (Associating Multiple Systems); "While neither of these methods distinguish bound pairs from chance alignments, our approach is less prone to bias and individual judgment when assigning the multiplicity status of particular systems. Moreover, it has greater reproducability, independent of millimeter flux density, and can easily be checked against simulation data as well." (Associating Multiple Systems)
- Alternative: Assigning a primary component based on luminosity or millimeter flux density and measuring all separations relative to the primary (as in Tobin et al. 2016a)  
  > "Our results would be different if we applied a simpler approach by assigning A as the primary based on its luminosity or flux density of dust emission, as had been done in (cite: tobin2016a)." (Associating Multiple Systems)
- Alternative: Calculating sample MF and CF directly from unrounded companion probabilities instead of rounding the sum of probabilities per system  
  > "There are alternative methods to calculate the MF and CF including the probabilities, and we describe one such method in Appendix A.4 that we used as a sanity check. We prefer to use our method of rounding per system because it produces results that are more directly comparable to previous work, and the calculated MF and CFs are consistent between our main method as described here and the alternative methods that are described in Appendix A." (Multiplicity Statistics)
- Alternative: Assigning composite-class pairs to the more evolved protostellar class rather than giving precedence to the younger evolutionary class  
  > "We tested creating our separation distributions where the more evolved Class was selected, rather then the less-evolved Class and the separation distributions do not change significantly." (Multiplicity Statistics Reporting Per Protostar Class)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cannot determine whether spatially associated continuum sources are gravitationally bound or infer true 3D orbital semi-major axes due to unknown orbital planes, phases, and eccentricity distributions.  
  > "We also do not know whether a collection of continuum sources constitutes a bound system or not. Thus, regardless of the inherent limitations, we use spatial association alone to assign multiplicity." (Data Analysis); "We also emphasize that we analyze the instantaneous projected separations of sources detected in our data, and we cannot infer the true orbital semi-major axes because we do not know the orbital plane or orbital phase for any companions observed." (Data Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Submillimeter/millimeter dust continuum can be optically thick, obscuring close companions at 0.87 mm that are only visible at 9 mm (e.g., HOPS-361-C, HOPS-288).  
  > "We find that three systems only have VLA (9 mm) detections of their companions that were undetected by ALMA at 0.87 mm. The non-detection by ALMA is most likely due to the high optical depth of the surrounding dust emission, obscuring the companion at shorter wavelengths." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Submillimeter/centimeter observations have an estimated ~20% incompleteness at 100-10^3 au (rising to 29% for Class I and Flat Spectrum) compared to HST near-IR detections, and suffer from resolution/contrast incompleteness at 10-60 au due to disk truncation and dust opacity.  
  > "Between $10^2$ and $10^3$ au, we estimate a submillimeter/centimeter incompleteness of $\sim$20% (as given by 1-[Number of detected companions/Total companions]; Total companions is the millimeter plus infrared companions). Considering only Class I and Flat Spectrum protostars, the incompleteness rises to 29%." (Near-infrared vs. ALMA/VLA Detections); "We instead argue that incompleteness in our survey between 10 and 60 au enhances the disagreement." (Evolution Beyond the Protostellar Phase)
- Limitation (authors): Independent SED classifications for individual components are only possible for separations >=2000 au; closer companions inherit the system classification and L_bol from lower-resolution infrared catalogs.  
  > "Thus, independent classifications for systems is generally only possible for systems with separations $\ensuremath{\gtrsim}$2000 au where they could be resolved in the mid and far-infrared." (Data Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Most protostars in the HOPS sample will become M-dwarfs rather than solar-type (FGK) stars, which complicates direct comparisons with the Raghavan et al. (2010) field solar-type binary sample.  
  > "While the field solar-type stars are a well-characterized sample to compare with, it is important to highlight that most of the protostars in the HOPS sample are likely to become M-stars rather than F, G, or K-type stars that make up the (cite: raghavan2010) sample." (Comparison Field Solar-type Multiples)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The evolutionary trends in MF/CF between Class I and Flat Spectrum at <1000 au and between high and low YSO density regions at 100-1000 au have low statistical significance (1.0 to 1.9 sigma) and are suggestive rather than definitive.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because VLA 9 mm pointings targeted primarily Class 0 sources while Class I and Flat Spectrum sources were mostly observed only with ALMA at 0.87 mm (which has slightly lower resolution and higher dust optical depth), close (<50-100 au) companions in Class I/Flat systems may be systematically under-detected relative to Class 0 systems.
