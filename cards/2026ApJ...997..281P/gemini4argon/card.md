## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST-MIRI MRS data and extract 1D spectrum**: Process raw data with JWST Calibration Pipeline and VIP routines using pair-wise dither background subtraction, extract 1D spectra with 1.5-FWHM aperture photometry, apply aperture correction factors, and perform residual fringe correction.  
  > "In short, the data were processed using version 1.14.1 of the JWST Calibration Pipeline (cite: Bushouse2024) and Calibration Reference Data System (CRDS) context `jwst_1253.pmap` in combination with dedicated routines based on the Vortex Image Processing (`VIP`) package for background subtraction, bad pixel correction and spectral extraction" (Observations and data reduction); "As the source is compact and unresolved, 1-D spectra were then extracted from the reduced datacubes with aperture photometry in 1.5-FWHM apertures centered on the centroid location, corrected for aperture size using correction factors from (cite: Argyriou2023)." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: JWST Calibration Pipeline, VIP
   - parameter: CRDS context jwst_1253.pmap; 1.5-FWHM aperture

   - assumption: Source is compact and unresolved for 1D extraction  
  > "As the source is compact and unresolved, 1-D spectra were then extracted from the reduced datacubes with aperture photometry in 1.5-FWHM apertures" (Observations and data reduction)

  > "In addition, one should note that the continuum level of the JWST spectrum is consistent when performing different background subtraction methods for the MINDS pipeline (see (cite: Christiaens2024) for more details)." (Dust continuum)
2. **Subtract dust continuum from MIRI MRS spectrum**: Estimate dust continuum using a Savitzky-Golay filter with a third-order polynomial via pybaselines, iteratively mask emission lines above 2 sigma, subtract continuum, and mask remaining 3 sigma downward spikes.  
  > "In short, the adopted method estimates the dust continuum emission using the `pybaselines` package (cite: Erb_2022). As a first step, a dust continuum level is guessed using a Savitzky-Golay filter with a third order polynomial." (Slab modelling); "Then, all emission lines extending above $2\sigma$ of the standard deviation are masked. Once no emission lines are left, the dust continuum is determined and subtracted. The last step of this procedure consists of masking the remaining $3\sigma$ downward spikes." (Slab modelling)
   - software: pybaselines
   - parameter: 3rd order polynomial Savitzky-Golay filter; 2 sigma emission masking; 3 sigma downward spike masking  
  > "As a first step, a dust continuum level is guessed using a Savitzky-Golay filter with a third order polynomial. Then, all emission lines extending above $2\sigma$ of the standard deviation are masked." (Slab modelling); "The last step of this procedure consists of masking the remaining $3\sigma$ downward spikes." (Slab modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Simultaneous LTE slab modelling and line flux measurement**: Generate 0D LTE synthetic spectra for H2O, CO2, 13CO2, C2H2, HCN, and OH between 13.4 and 18 um using LAMDA and HITRAN data, convolve to R~2500, resample with SpectRes, and fit all six species simultaneously with emcee MCMC. Integrate continuum-subtracted or best-fit synthetic spectra to obtain line fluxes.  
  > "We selected the major molecular emitters in the spectral region spanning from 13.4 to 18 $\mu$m: H$_2$O, CO$_2$, $^{13}$CO$_2$, C$_2$H$_2$, HCN, and OH." (Slab modelling); "The resulting synthetic spectra are convolved with the resolution of the selected region of the MIRI spectrum ($R\approx2500$, (cite: Labiano2021; Argyriou2023)) using the averaged values from (cite: Jones2023), then resampled to the same wavelength grid as the observed data with `SpectRes` (cite: carnall2017)." (Slab modelling); "The slab modelling procedure consists of performing an Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC; (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013)) fit of all the six selected species simultaneously, instead of fitting iteratively one species at the time" (Slab modelling)
   - software: SpectRes, emcee
   - parameter: Wavelength range 13.4 to 18 um; spectral resolution R ~ 2500; line width FWHM = 4.7 km/s (sigma = 2 km/s)  
  > "assuming a Gaussian line profile with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of $\Delta V = 4.7~$km s$^{-1}$ ($\sigma=2~$km s$^{-1}$)." (Slab modelling); "We selected the major molecular emitters in the spectral region spanning from 13.4 to 18 $\mu$m: H$_2$O, CO$_2$, $^{13}$CO$_2$, C$_2$H$_2$, HCN, and OH." (Slab modelling)
   - assumption: Plane-parallel slabs of gas in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE); Gaussian line profile with FWHM = 4.7 km/s; d = 140.26 pc  
  > "Next, we generate synthetic spectra to reproduce the continuum-subtracted spectrum assuming plane-parallel slabs of gas in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) using spectroscopic data from the LAMDA and HITRAN databases (cite: Schoier2005; Gordon2022)." (Slab modelling); "assuming a Gaussian line profile with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of $\Delta V = 4.7~$km s$^{-1}$ ($\sigma=2~$km s$^{-1}$)." (Slab modelling)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; uniform priors for 3 free parameters per species (N, T, R); 8 walkers per free parameter; ~10^5 steps  
  > "In particular, the fit is carried out using `emcee` (cite: emcee2013) and assuming a uniform prior for each of the free parameters (i.e., three per molecular species), as well as eight times the number of walkers with respect to the number of free parameters." (Slab modelling); "The fit convergence is checked by comparing the distribution of walkers as a function of steps, and making sure it had become constant for over $10^3$ steps; for J0438 it is reached after $\sim10^5$ steps." (Slab modelling)
   - validation: Checked walker distribution constancy over >10^3 steps and inspected fit residuals and posterior corner plots  
  > "The fit convergence is checked by comparing the distribution of walkers as a function of steps, and making sure it had become constant for over $10^3$ steps; for J0438 it is reached after $\sim10^5$ steps." (Slab modelling); "Finally, a careful analysis of the residuals (Fig. 17) reveals a few regions where the observed data are not particularly well fitted by the total model." (Slab modelling)
4. **Test effect of disk inclination on hydrocarbon visibility and estimate water iceline**: Run thermochemical disk models varying inclination from 45 deg to 70 deg to measure change in C2H2 peak flux density; calculate water iceline location using Eq. 19 of Liu et al. (2019).  
  > "To check whether the disk configuration could prevent the detection of hydrocarbons in J0438 we ran a set of thermochemical models following the framework of (cite: Kanwar2025) and enabled the disk inclination to vary from 45$^{\circ}$ to 70$^{\circ}$." (Disk inclination); "According to the formalism presented in (cite: Liu_Beibei_2019) (Eq. 19), we estimate the water iceline to lie at approximately $0.02~$au in the J0438 system (Fig. 5)." (Sub-structures)
   - parameter: Inclination varied from 45 deg to 70 deg  
  > "enabled the disk inclination to vary from 45$^{\circ}$ to 70$^{\circ}$." (Disk inclination)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

  > "The reduced dataset was downloaded from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) and the HST image was then sharpened by applying six iterations of the Lucy-Richardson algorithm (cite: Richardson1972) and using the nearby field star as the Point Spread Function (PSF)." ## Qualitative findings

- The inner disk of J0438 is O-dominated (H2O, CO2, HCN, H2, [Ar+], [Ne+]) and lacks abundant hydrocarbons except for C2H2, resembling T Tauri disks with C/O < 1 rather than typical hydrocarbon-rich VLMS/BD disks.  
  > "The JWST MIRI-MRS spectrum of J0438 resembles that of a disk around a T Tauri star with a C/O ratio below unity. The volatile reservoir is O-dominated, composed of H$_2$O, CO$_2$ and, contrarily to most VLMS and BD disks investigated so far, of only one hydrocarbon (i.e., C$_2$H$_2$)." (Summary and outlook)
- J0438 exhibits mid-IR seesaw continuum variability (pivot at ~11.5 um, 30% weaker at 6 um and 15% stronger at 20 um over 16 years) as well as line variability in [Ne+], H2, and H2O.  
  > "The largest offsets are found between the MIRI (2023-10-01) and the low-resolution IRS observations (2007-10-07) taken 16 years apart with the MIRI spectrum roughly $30\%$ weaker at $6~ \mu$m and $15\%$ stronger at $20~\mu$m." (Dust continuum); "The mid-IR variability of J0438 can be ascribed to a seesaw behaviour where the pivot point is at $\lambda\simeq11.5\,\mu$m." (Dust continuum)
- Marginally extended emission is detected in JWST-MIRI MRS for H2 S(1), S(3), S(5) and [Ne+], tracing a disk wind and/or disk surface rather than a collimated jet.  
  > "The inspection of the JWST data cubes shows marginally extended emission for the H$_2 \, \, S$(1), $S$(3) and $S$(5) lines, as well as of [Ne$^{+}$], for the first time in a BD disk." (Summary and outlook); "Interestingly, the emission patterns of H$_2$ and [Ne$^{+}$] are similar and are not attributable to a jet, but plausibly to a disk wind and/or to the disk surface." (Summary and outlook)
- ALMA Band 7 reveals non-bipolar extended CO (J=3-2) emission up to 420 au with low velocity dispersion (±2 km/s), and HST/ACS detects a faint point source 0.49'' E, 0.86'' S that is not detected in ALMA dust continuum.  
  > "The red-shifted NE lobe extends up to approximately 280 au whereas the blue-shifted emission spans up to $420~$au. The SE lobe appears to be disconnected from the central object possibly due to the presence of an interfering planetary-mass companion robustly detected with a S/N $>5$ in the HST/ACS images but not observed in the ALMA dust continuum maps (Fig. 6)." (Radio)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: J0438 is one of the very few highly inclined (~70 deg) brown dwarf disks known, has the most complete SED for a brown dwarf disk, and high-resolution JWST-MIRI MRS spectra paired with HST and ALMA allow characterization of both its inner disk chemistry and extended dynamical activity.  
  > "Interestingly, among all VLMS and BD disks targeted in JWST-MIRI Cycles 1 and 2 so far ($\sim$ 25) all of them appear to have a close to face-on configuration except for the target of this work." (Introduction); "Thanks to the high spectral resolving power of JWST-MIRI MRS ($R\!\sim\!3000$; (cite: Labiano2021)) we confirm the presence of faint water emission throughout the JWST mid-infrared (mid-IR) spectrum of J0438 as well as of a set of atomic, molecular and ionic emission lines" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Automated continuum subtraction via pybaselines avoids bias from visual selection in line-rich spectra; simultaneous MCMC fitting of all six species avoids bias from sequential species subtraction; pair-wise dither subtraction reduces noise for faint sources.  
  > "To subtract the background a direct pair-wise dither subtraction was carried out. This method is particularly suited for faint sources and guarantees the reduction of the noise level in the spectrum." (Observations and data reduction); "We followed the continuum subtraction procedure described in (cite: Temmink2024), instead of identifying line-free channels tracing the dust emission by eye. A visual selection of dust continuum points would inevitably result in an inaccurate continuum determination due to the line richness of the MIRI spectrum." (Slab modelling); "The slab modelling procedure consists of performing an Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC; (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013)) fit of all the six selected species simultaneously, instead of fitting iteratively one species at the time (i.e., subtracting the synthetic model of water first, then moving to the next species, and so on). This procedure has the advantage of reducing the bias introduced by the sequential subtraction of different synthetic spectra" (Slab modelling)
- Alternative: Spectrophotometric calibration uncertainties or aperture size differences between Spitzer-IRS and JWST-MIRI as the cause of the mid-IR continuum offset (rejected because offsets exceed calibration uncertainties and reverse sign around 11.5 um).  
  > "The reported spectrophotometric accuracy is 2–10% for SST-IRS (cite: Furlan2006; Watson2009) and $5.6 \pm 0.7\%$ for JWST-MIRI MRS (cite: Argyriou2023), which confidently rules out calibration uncertainties, especially for the shorter wavelengths." (Dust continuum); "However, this pattern is not observed for the full spectral coverage and instead, the MIRI spectrum is consistently stronger longwards of $12\, \mu$m, supporting the presence of both continuum and line variability (see also Appendix 9)." (Dust continuum)
- Alternative: High disk inclination (~70 deg) causing the non-detection of hydrocarbons weaker than C2H2 (rejected via thermochemical modeling showing only a ~33% drop in C2H2 peak flux density).  
  > "We found that the peak flux density of C$_2$H$_2$ decreases of approximately $33\%$ when the disk inclination changes from *i*=45$^{\circ}$ to *i*=70$^{\circ}$, which is well within our detection limit. This result made us confident that the inclination of our BD disk ($67^{\circ}-71^{\circ}$) is not responsible for the non-detection of hydrocarbons weaker than C$_2$H$_2$." (Disk inclination)
- Alternative: Visual selection of line-free continuum channels and sequential one-by-one slab fitting of molecular species (rejected in favor of automated pybaselines continuum subtraction and simultaneous 6-species MCMC fitting).  
  > "We followed the continuum subtraction procedure described in (cite: Temmink2024), instead of identifying line-free channels tracing the dust emission by eye." (Slab modelling); "The slab modelling procedure consists of performing an Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC; (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013)) fit of all the six selected species simultaneously, instead of fitting iteratively one species at the time" (Slab modelling)

- Limitation (authors): 0D LTE slab models do not account for the multi-dimensional physical structure of the disk, so R only represents an equivalent emitting area radius; C2H2 exhibits a degeneracy between column density and emitting radius, CO2 column density is only a lower limit due to limited S/N, and OH and 13CO2 are poorly constrained.  
  > "Note that $R$ should not be ascribed to a radius within the disk as the adopted models do not take into account the multi-dimensional physical structure of the disk." (Slab modelling); "Deviations from this behavior are seen for C$_2$H$_2$ ($N\!\sim\!10^{13}~$cm$^{-2}$ and $R\!\sim~0.9~$au), reporting a degeneracy between the column density and the emitting radius." (Slab modelling); "Due to the limited S/N, the column density of CO$_2$ remained challenging to constrain, and the current best slab is limited to a maximum column density of $10^{17}$cm$^{-2}$." (Slab modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Slab modeling is restricted to 13.4-18 um because shorter wavelengths are affected by photospheric absorption and silicate bands, and wavelengths beyond 18 um have considerably higher noise.  
  > "We did not produce synthetic spectra for the region below $13.4~\mu$m as it is largely dominated by the absorption lines from the central object up until $7.3~\mu$m (e.g., (cite: Li2024); Fig. 10) and by the silicate band in absorption and emission (Fig. 9). Beyond 18 $\mu$m, the noise level increases considerably, therefore we restrict the slab modelling analysis to wavelengths shorter than this." (Slab modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Current observations cannot distinguish whether the HST/ACS point source is a background field star or a planetary-mass companion, nor whether the ALMA extended CO emission is an outflow, binary interaction, or late-infall event without dedicated modeling and follow-up.  
  > "With our current data is not possible to disentangle whether this is a field star or a planetary-mass companion and further work is required to confirm its nature." (Summary and outlook); "The low CO velocities and the lack of a bipolar pattern makes it challenging to attribute the extended emission to an outflow. A late-infall event cannot be ruled out at this stage without dedicated modelling." (Summary and outlook)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA Band 7 data reduction and imaging parameters (velocity channel ranges for red/blue lobes, noise levels, contour thresholds in Fig. 6) are not specified in this paper and rely entirely on earlier works.
