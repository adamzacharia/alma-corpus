## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, agn_and_quasars, star_formation, astrochemistry
- Question: What are the physical and chemical properties of nuclear dense molecular gas in nearby ULIRGs at <=0.5 kpc resolution, and how do they spatially vary between the innermost (<=0.5 kpc) and outer nuclear (0.5-2 kpc) regions?  
  > "we attempt to investigate, with an aid of non-LTE calculations, (1) physical and chemical properties of nuclear dense molecular gas in a larger number of nearby ULIRGs, compared to the previous study by (cite: ima23), and (2) for the first time, how the properties spatially change between the innermost ($\lesssim$0.5 kpc) and outer nuclear (0.5–2 kpc) regions." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum subtraction and image reconstruction**: Subtract continuum using uvcontsub on line-free channels, then image continuum and line cubes with tclean using Briggs weighting  
  > "By choosing channels without showing obvious emission and absorption lines, we determined the continuum level, and subtracted it using the CASA task “uvcontsub”." (Observations and Data Analysis); "Then we applied the “tclean” task (Briggs-weighting; robust $=$ 0.5 and gain $=$ 0.1) for the continuum-only and continuum-subtracted dense molecular line data to create cleaned maps." (Observations and Data Analysis)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 0.5, gain = 0.1, velocity resolution ~20 km s^-1, pixel scale = 0.02'' pixel^-1  
  > "Then we applied the “tclean” task (Briggs-weighting; robust $=$ 0.5 and gain $=$ 0.1) for the continuum-only and continuum-subtracted dense molecular line data to create cleaned maps." (Observations and Data Analysis); "The final velocity resolution was $\sim$20 km s$^{-1}$ and the pixel scale was 0.02$''$ pixel$^{-1}$." (Observations and Data Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Create moment 0 and moment 1 maps at original beam resolution**: Integrate channels showing significant line signals relative to continuum level to produce integrated intensity (moment 0) and intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment 1) maps  
  > "Figure 1 shows continuum (contours) and integrated-intensity (moment 0) maps of newly taken HCN and HCO$^{+}$ lines with the original beam size (Table 3, column 2–4)." (Results); "The moment 1 maps in Figure 16 are created by integrating channels that show significant line signals, relative to continuum flux level." (Intensity-weighted Mean Velocity (Moment 1) Map)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verification of significant molecular line detection at or very close to the continuum emission peak position  
  > "These original-beam-sized moment 0 maps are primarily used for the verification of significant molecular line detection at or very close to the continuum emission peak position" (Table 5)
3. **Beam matching to fixed physical scales and spectral extraction**: Smooth cleaned images to circular beams of 0.5 kpc, 1 kpc, and 2 kpc diameter using imsmooth, extract spectra at continuum peak position, and subtract circular beam spectra to get 0.5-1 kpc and 1-2 kpc annular spectra  
  > "we modify the original synthesized beam sizes (Table 3) to a $\sim$0.5 kpc diameter circle, using the CASA task “imsmooth” (cite: CASA22) to cleaned images, for all ULIRGs, and then extract 0.5 kpc beam-sized integrated flux spectra at the continuum emission peak position." (Results); "we also modify the original beam to 1 kpc and 2 kpc diameter circles, and extract 1 kpc and 2 kpc beam-sized integrated flux spectra" (Results); "We also extract spectra of a 0.5–1 kpc (1–2 kpc) annular region, by subtracting the 0.5 kpc (1 kpc) beam-sized spectrum from the 1 kpc (2 kpc) beam-sized spectrum." (Results)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: beam diameters = 0.5 kpc, 1 kpc, 2 kpc; annular regions = 0.5-1 kpc, 1-2 kpc  
  > "we derive molecular line flux ratios, by extracting spectra in the central 0.5, 1, 2 kpc circular regions, and 0.5–1 and 1–2 kpc annular regions." (Abstract)
   - assumption: H_0 = 71 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.27, Omega_Lambda = 0.73  
  > "Throughout this paper, (1) we adopt the cosmological parameters, H$_{0}$ $=$ 71 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm M}$ = 0.27, and $\Omega_{\rm \Lambda}$ = 0.73" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure velocity-integrated line fluxes and line ratios via Gaussian fitting**: Fit single or double Gaussian profiles to detected HCN, HCO+, CS, and HC3N lines in extracted spectra and compute HCN/HCO+, high-J/low-J, HCN/CS, and HC3N/HCN flux ratios in Jy km s^-1  
  > "Gaussian fits are applied to significantly detected HCN and HCO$^{+}$ emission lines in the spectra at various regions." (Results); "We apply a double Gaussian fit only if an emission line displays a clear double-peaked profile with a deep central dip." (Results)
   - fitting: Single or double Gaussian profile fitting  
  > "Following (cite: ima23), to simplify our flux estimates, we try to apply a single Gaussian fit, as long as a line profile can be approximated by a single emission component." (Results)
   - validation: Confirming that single and double Gaussian fits provide fluxes which agree well within ~10%, and visual inspection of overplotted fits  
  > "we adopt a single Gaussian fitting result, after confirming that single and double Gaussian fits provide fluxes which agree well within $\sim$10%." (Gaussian fit); "These overplots can be used to visually inspect the goodness of the Gaussian fits and check the reliability of our discussion of dense molecular emission line flux ratios." (Gaussian fit)
5. **Non-LTE RADEX modeling with Levenberg-Marquardt fitting (fixed column density and abundance ratio)**: Fit observed high-J to low-J flux ratios of HCN and HCO+ with RADEX models using Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares fitting, both without flux scaling and with two-stage L-BFGS-B + Levenberg-Marquardt flux scaling within calibration uncertainties  
  > "We fit the observed high-J to low-J flux ratios of HCN and HCO$^+$ with RADEX to estimate molecular gas density and temperature." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios); "The least-squares fitting for log n$_{\rm H_2}$ (density) and log T$_{\rm kin}$ (temperature) is performed with the conventional Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm using the Python package `lmfit` (cite: lmfit)." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios); "in the first stage, gas density, temperature, and scaling of each emission line flux within the calibration uncertainty are left free, and the residuals are minimized using the L-BFGS-B method; in the second stage, the scaling factors are fixed to the obtained values." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
   - software: RADEX, pyradex, lmfit
   - parameter: log n_H2 grid from 2 to 6, log T_kin grid from 1 to 3; flux scaling max 5% for J=2-1 and 10% for J=3-2 and J=4-3  
  > "Confidence intervals for the parameters are examined by grid computing $\Delta\chi^2\equiv\chi^2-\chi^2_{\rm best}$ with log n$_{\rm H_2}$ ranging from 2 to 6 and log T$_{\rm kin}$ from 1 to 3." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios); "As our second calculations, we allow scaling of absolute flux within maximum 5$\%$ for J=2–1 and 10$\%$ for J=3–2 and J=4–3 ($\S$3)." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
   - assumption: One-zone uniform sphere, T_bg = 2.73 K, collisions with only H2, line width = 500 km s^-1, N_HCO+ = 1 x 10^16 cm^-2, [HCN]/[HCO+] = 3  
  > "For all RADEX calculations, (1) gas geometry is assumed to be a one-zone uniform sphere, (2) the cosmic microwave background with temperature of T$_{\rm bg}$ = 2.73 K is included, and (3) collisions with only H$_2$ are considered. The molecular line width is commonly set to 500 km s$^{-1}$" (Dense Molecular Gas Properties : Comparison with Non-LTE Model Calculations); "The HCO$^{+}$ column density, HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio, and molecular line width are fixed at N$_{\rm HCO^+}$ = 1 $\times$ 10$^{16}$ cm$^{-2}$, \[HCN\]/\[HCO$^+$\] = 3, and $\Delta$v = 500 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Table 7)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm and L-BFGS-B method; delta chi^2 grid confidence intervals  
  > "The least-squares fitting for log n$_{\rm H_2}$ (density) and log T$_{\rm kin}$ (temperature) is performed with the conventional Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm using the Python package `lmfit` (cite: lmfit)." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
   - validation: Tested alternative [HCN]/[HCO+] = 1, 7 and N_HCO+ = 1e15, 3e16 cm^-2; compared scaling on vs off and evaluated reduced chi^2  
  > "We also try different HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio (\[HCN\]/\[HCO$^+$\] = 7) and HCO$^{+}$ column density (N$_{\rm HCO+}$ = 1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 3 $\times$ 10$^{16}$ cm$^{-2}$)" (HCN-to-HCO$^+$ Flux Ratios); "The derived gas density and temperature are generally comparable between the first and second methods, but the reduced $\chi^{2}$ value is usually smaller in the second fitting result (scaling on) than the first one (scaling off)." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
6. **Bayesian MCMC analysis of all line ratios simultaneously**: Simultaneously fit HCN-to-HCO+ flux ratios (J=2-1, 3-2, 4-3) and HCO+ high-J to low-J flux ratios (J=3-2/2-1, 4-3/2-1) with RADEX using MCMC sampling in emcee with all parameters free  
  > "we fit both the HCN-to-HCO$^+$ flux ratios and HCO$^+$ high-J to low-J flux ratios simultaneously with RADEX to derive the gas physical parameters in detail, without fixing the HCO$^{+}$ column density and HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance, by using a Bayesian approach." (Bayesian Analysis of Both Types of Ratios); "We use a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler implemented in the `emcee` package (cite: emcee) to explore the parameter space." (Bayesian Analysis of Both Types of Ratios)
   - software: RADEX, pyradex, emcee, corner
   - parameter: 100 walkers, 100*tau steps, burn-in = 5*tau steps, thinned by 0.5*tau steps, 19,000 posterior samples  
  > "The chain is run with 100 walkers initialized around a first guess obtained by the L-BFGS-B solver ($\S$5.3) with the column density and abundance ratio unfixed." (Bayesian Analysis of Both Types of Ratios); "the chain is continued up to 100$\tau$ steps, with the first 5$\tau$ steps discarded as “burn-in”, and finally thinned out by 0.5$\tau$ steps to leave independent samples. The total number of sampling of the posterior probability distribution is thus $100\times(100-5)/0.5=19,\!000$." (Bayesian Analysis of Both Types of Ratios)
   - assumption: One-zone uniform sphere, T_bg = 2.73 K, collisions with H2 only, line width = 500 km s^-1

## Qualitative findings

- The innermost (<=0.5 kpc) and whole nuclear (<=1-2 kpc) molecular gas in ULIRGs is very dense (>=10^5 cm^-3) and warm (>=300 K), whereas in the starburst-dominated LIRG NGC 1614 it is modestly dense (10^4-4.5 cm^-3) and warm (~100 K).  
  > "We quantitatively found that molecular gas at ULIRGs’ innermost ($\lesssim$0.5 kpc) and whole nuclear ($\lesssim$1–2 kpc) regions is very dense ($\gtrsim$10$^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$) and warm ($\gtrsim$300 K), and that it is also modestly dense (10$^{4-4.5}$ cm$^{-3}$) and warm ($\sim$100 K) in one starburst-dominated LIRG’s nucleus ($\lesssim$1 kpc)." (Summary)
- HCO+ emission is spatially more extended than HCN emission at the same J-transition in the majority of observed ULIRGs.  
  > "We see that in the majority of the observed ULIRGs, HCO$^{+}$ flux increases more significantly than HCN flux when compared at the same J-transition, supporting a previous suggestion that HCO$^{+}$ emission is spatially more extended than HCN emission in nearby ULIRGs’ nuclei (cite: ima19)." (Results)
- A decreasing trend of HCN-to-HCO+ flux ratios and high-J to low-J flux ratios from the innermost (<=0.5 kpc) to outer nuclear (0.5-2 kpc) region is seen in a significant fraction of the observed ULIRGs.  
  > "We saw a signature that the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios at J=2–1, J=3–2, and J=4–3, and high-J to low-J flux ratios of HCN and HCO$^{+}$, decrease from the innermost ($\lesssim$0.5 kpc) to outer nuclear (0.5–2 kpc) region for some fraction of the observed ULIRGs." (Summary)
- Bayesian MCMC modeling shows an increasing trend of the HCN-to-HCO+ abundance ratio and gas kinetic temperature from the outer nuclear (0.5-2 kpc) to the innermost (<=0.5 kpc) regions significantly in two AGN-hosting ULIRGs (IRAS 16090-0139 and IRAS 22491-1808) and marginally in three others, while no spatial variation is seen in NGC 1614.  
  > "detected an increasing trend of the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio and gas kinetic temperature from the outer nuclear (0.5–2 kpc) to the innermost ($\lesssim$0.5 kpc) regions in two ULIRGs with luminous AGN signatures (IRAS 16090$-$0139 and IRAS 22491$-$1808) significantly and in additional three ULIRGs (IRAS 00456$-$2904, IRAS 01569$-$2939, and IRAS 03250$+$1606) marginally." (Summary); "Our Bayesian approach also demonstrated that the LIRG NGC 1614 displayed (a) much lower gas temperature and HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio than the observed other nearby ULIRGs’ nuclei, and (b) no discernible spatial change in these two parameters at 0.5 kpc physical scales within the central $\lesssim$1 kpc region." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiple J-transition lines (J=2-1, 3-2, 4-3) of high-dipole-moment tracers HCN and HCO+ at <=0.5 kpc resolution suffer minimal dust extinction and independently constrain excitation conditions (density, temperature) and chemical abundance variations inside ULIRG nuclei.  
  > "In the (sub)millimeter wavelength range at 0.3–3.5 mm where dust extinction effects are very small (cite: hil83), many rotational J-transition lines of abundant molecules are found." (Introduction); "By adding $\lesssim$0.5 kpc-resolution HCN and HCO$^{+}$ J=2–1 and J=4–3 line data to the existing J=3–2 data, we will be able to obtain three independent J-transition line data. They can be used to better constrain the possible spatial variation of (1) physical properties of dense molecular gas, based on excitation conditions (high-J to low-J flux ratios) of both HCN and HCO$^{+}$, and (2) chemical properties, by comparing HCN and HCO$^{+}$ emission line fluxes at the same J-transition." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Beam matching via imsmooth ensures identical physical scales are compared across transitions; combining fixed-parameter Levenberg-Marquardt fitting with all-free Bayesian MCMC accounts for parameter degeneracies and limited observational constraints.  
  > "To estimate nuclear dense molecular emission line (HCN and HCO$^{+}$) fluxes from the same physical scale, we modify the original synthesized beam sizes (Table 3) to a $\sim$0.5 kpc diameter circle, using the CASA task “imsmooth”" (Results); "First, we constrain molecular gas density and temperature, by fixing the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio and HCO$^{+}$ column density to fiducial values ($\S$5.2–5.3), because the number of observational constraints is limited. Next, we apply a Bayesian approach to constrain physical parameters, by making all parameters free ($\S$5.4)" (Dense Molecular Gas Properties : Comparison with Non-LTE Model Calculations); "the Bayesian technique is able to sample the posterior probability distribution naturally including the indeterminacy of the solution." (Bayesian Analysis of Both Types of Ratios)
- Alternative: Mechanical heating by shocks was noted as an alternative to AGN heating for producing strong high-J molecular emission.  
  > "with a caution that mechanical heating by shocks could also produce strong high-J CO emission lines (cite: hai12; mei13; pel13; ros15; kam16)." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Two Gaussian emission components were rejected in favor of one broad emission plus one narrow absorption Gaussian for lines showing negative continuum dips in IRAS 00091-0738.  
  > "We adopt one broad Gaussian emission and one narrow Gaussian absorption components, because negative signals below the continuum level at the HCO$^{+}$ central dip, observed in IRAS 00091$-$0738 (Figures 2 and 3), cannot be reproduced by two Gaussian emission components." (Table 9)
- Alternative: Alternative fiducial values in RADEX ([HCN]/[HCO+] = 1 and 7; N_HCO+ = 1x10^15 and 3x10^16 cm^-2) and flux scaling on vs off were tested.  
  > "We also try different HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio (\[HCN\]/\[HCO$^+$\] = 7) and HCO$^{+}$ column density (N$_{\rm HCO+}$ = 1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 3 $\times$ 10$^{16}$ cm$^{-2}$)" (HCN-to-HCO$^+$ Flux Ratios); "We derive molecular gas physical parameters, by (1) using the observed high-J to low-J flux ratios as they are (no flux scaling), and (2) allowing flux scale adjustment for individual J=2–1, J=3–2, and J=4–3 data" (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 11 ULIRGs are not statistically complete and are possibly biased toward AGN-important ULIRGs because sources with high (>=1) HCN-to-HCO+ J=3-2 flux ratios were selected.  
  > "The 11 ULIRGs are not statistically complete and are possibly biased to AGN-important ULIRGs because sources with high ($\gtrsim$1) HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 flux ratios are selected (cite: ima16b)." (Targets)
- Limitation (authors): Enlarging the synthesized beam from <=0.5 kpc to 1 and 2 kpc increases the rms noise in mJy beam^-1, making the 1-2 kpc annular and <=2 kpc circular spectra noisy and limiting outer annular detections to a fraction of the sample.  
  > "when we modify the originally very small beam size to a large beam size, the resulting rms noise in units of mJy beam$^{-1}$ increases. The resulting large-beam-sized spectrum can become noisy with large scatters." (Results); "we can obtain meaningful estimates of HCN and HCO$^{+}$ emission line fluxes at the 1–2 kpc annular region only for a limited fraction of the observed ULIRGs." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): In the Bayesian MCMC analysis, the number of independent emission line flux ratios is fewer than the total number of free parameters, introducing systematic uncertainties and indeterminacy.  
  > "with a caution that some parameters may have systematic uncertainties, given so many free parameters for the limited number of observational constrains." (Dense Molecular Gas Properties : Comparison with Non-LTE Model Calculations); "Although the number of available independent emission line flux ratios (HCN-to-HCO$^+$ flux ratio at J=2–1, J=3–2, and J=4–3, HCO$^+$ J=3–2 to J=2–1, and HCO$^{+}$ J=4–3 to J=2–1) is fewer than the total number of parameters" (Bayesian Analysis of Both Types of Ratios)
- Limitation (authors): Comparing overlapping circular apertures (<=0.5, <=1, <=2 kpc) rather than non-overlapping annuli can dilute spatial variations if nuclear emission is dominated by the innermost <=0.5 kpc region.  
  > "If nuclear ($\lesssim$2 kpc) molecular gas emission is dominated by the innermost $\lesssim$0.5 kpc region, possible spatial variation of the gas physical parameters can be diluted in the latter comparison." (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
- Limitation (authors): One-zone RADEX modeling cannot be applied to IRAS 00091-0738 due to self-absorption dips below the continuum level, and IRAS 10378+1108 and IRAS 12112+0305 lack all three J-transitions.  
  > "We exclude IRAS 10378$+$1108 and IRAS 12112$+$0305 because not all the three J-transition line data are available. We exclude also IRAS 00091$-$0738 because negative signals below the continuum levels, clearly detected at the HCO$^{+}$ central absorption dips in Figures 2 and 3, suggest self-absorption" (High-J to Low-J Flux Ratios)
- Limitation (authors): For broad molecular lines (>=300 km s^-1), vibrationally excited HCN v2=1, l=1f (HCN-VIB) emission (~400 km s^-1 redshifted) can blend with and contaminate HCO+ J=4-3 emission.  
  > "for sources with non-small molecular line widths ($\gtrsim$300 km s$^{-1}$ in full width at half maximum \[FWHM\]), it is often difficult to clearly separate HCO$^{+}$ and vibrationally excited HCN v$_{2}$=1, l=1f (HCN-VIB) emission lines" (Intensity-weighted Mean Velocity (Moment 1) Map)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The one-zone uniform sphere assumption with a fixed 500 km s^-1 line width for all targets and apertures ignores velocity gradients, clumpy structure, and radiative pumping (e.g., IR pumping of HCN, evidenced by the HCN-VIB detection in IRAS 00091-0738).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one starburst-dominated control object (the LIRG NGC 1614, which has lower L_IR than ULIRGs) is included, making it difficult to disentangle AGN effects from overall galaxy merger/L_IR effects.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Image-plane smoothing (imsmooth) from ~0.15 arcsec to ~1 arcsec (2 kpc) heavily upweights short baselines and inflates noise compared to uv-tapering during tclean.
