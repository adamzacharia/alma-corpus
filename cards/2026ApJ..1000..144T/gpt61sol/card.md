## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify molecular-cloud regions**: Use CO maps and cloud kinematics described in temi18; define displayed CO-cloud areas by emission-line signal-to-noise greater than 4.  
  > "Detailed CO maps and kinematic analyses are presented in (cite: temi18); here we provide a brief summary of their findings." (ALMA maps); "CO clouds, indicated with white contours, are defined as the area where the emission line signal-to-noise is greater than 4." (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Emission-line signal-to-noise >4.  
  > "the emission line signal-to-noise is greater than 4." (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - parameter: NGC 5813 upper limit evaluated over a 500 km s−1 linewidth.  
  > "over line widths of a $500 \ km \ s^{-1}$" (ALMA Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Model unobscured stellar light**: Model elliptical stellar isophotes with ELLIPSE and photutils; alternatively use I-band emission and a global I–V stellar color. NGC 4472 and NGC 5044 use elliptical isophotes.  
  > "we used the ELLIPSE function in the IRAF STSDAS library[^1] and a similar function in the Python library photutils[^2](cite: bradley19)." (Dust Absorption Maps); "Alternatively, when 2 HST filters are available we approximate the I band filter as the model for the stellar emission and fit a global stellar color between the I and V band filter." (Dust Absorption Maps); "Of the five galaxies in the sample, the dust absorption maps for NGC 4472 and NGC 5044 were produced using the elliptical isophotes method." (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - software: ELLIPSE, IRAF, STSDAS, Python, photutils
   - assumption: The alternative color method assumes no dust absorption in the I band.  
  > "this method reduces the dust absorption estimate by assuming no dust absorption in the I band and is sensitive to stellar color gradient" (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - fitting: Elliptical isophote models or a fitted global stellar color.  
  > "These codes calculate the underlying elliptical isophotes of the stellar emission, and from these isophotes they deduce a model of the stellar emission." (Dust Absorption Maps); "fit a global stellar color between the I and V band filter." (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure regional dust extinction**: Threshold smoothed image-minus-model residuals, cluster selected pixels, and calculate V- and I-band extinction from absorbed and unabsorbed fluxes.  
  > "We set a threshold of 0.03 count/s on the difference between images and models that were smoothed with a 0.35$''$ FWHM (to remove isolate noisy pixels)." (Dust Absorption Maps); "Finally we clustered the selected pixels into area of at least 50 pixels and obtain several high absorption clusters for each galaxy." (Dust Absorption Maps); "For each filter, we calculate the absorbed ($F_{abs}$) and unabsorbed ($F_{unabs}$) fluxes for each cluster and computed the extinction $A_{\lambda }=-2.5~log(F_{abs}/F_{unabs})$ in V and I–band" (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - parameter: Residual threshold 0.03 count/s; smoothing FWHM 0.35 arcsec; minimum region 50 pixels.  
  > "We set a threshold of 0.03 count/s on the difference between images and models that were smoothed with a 0.35$''$ FWHM (to remove isolate noisy pixels)." (Dust Absorption Maps); "Finally we clustered the selected pixels into area of at least 50 pixels" (Dust Absorption Maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Photon-noise errors plus stellar-model systematic uncertainty estimated from residual RMS where absorption is below 0.01 count/s.  
  > "The uncertainties on these magnitudes are calculated using the statistical photon noise of the dust absorption image and an estimate of the systematic uncertainty of the single-band model." (Dust Absorption Maps); "The latter uncertainty is estimated by calculating the root mean square of the dust absorption estimate where its amplitude is very low (less than 0.01 count/s)." (Dust Absorption Maps)
4. **Compare extinction curves**: Compare regional AI versus AV with G23 extinction laws parameterized by RV.  
  > "Plots of A$_I$ versus A$_V$, with their total errors, for each of the identified high dust absorption regions" (Dust Extinction Properties); "The adopted extinction curve uses the `G23` class from the `dust_extinction` Python package" (Dust Extinction Properties)
   - software: Python, dust_extinction, G23
   - parameter: RV = 2.3, 2.6, 3.1 and 5.0.  
  > "$R_V = A_V/E(B-V)$ = 2.3, 2.6, 3.1 and 5.0" (Dust Extinction Properties)
   - assumption: G23 mean dust-extinction behavior as a function of RV.  
  > "which provides the mean dust-extinction behavior as a function of $R_V$ at spectroscopic resolution from 912 Å to 32 $\mu$m (cite: Gordon2023)." (Dust Extinction Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Selected departures from the standard extinction law reported as statistically significant at ≳3σ.  
  > "The departures from the standard extinction law in these regions are statistically significant at the $\gtrsim3\sigma$ level." (Dust Extinction Properties)
5. **Measure ionized-gas emission and kinematics**: Fit each MUSE pixel with stellar templates and multiple Gaussian emission-line components.  
  > "Continuum modeling and emission line fitting was applied to each individual pixel of the MUSE cubes." (H$\alpha$ maps); "Sets of multiple Gaussian components, in combination with the stellar templates, were used for fitting the optical emission lines and extracting the gas kinematics components." (H$\alpha$ maps)
   - software: ppxf
   - parameter: Spectra truncated at 7400 Å.  
  > "we have truncated the spectra to 7400 Å where the spectrum is less contaminated by sky residuals" (H$\alpha$ maps)
   - assumption: E-MILES stellar population synthesis templates.  
  > "For the stellar continuum and stellar kinematics modeling the "E-MILES" stellar population synthesis templates (cite: Vazdekis2016) where used." (H$\alpha$ maps)
   - fitting: Penalized PiXel-Fitting with stellar templates and multiple Gaussian emission components.  
  > "The MUSE spectral maps were modeled and fitted using the latest version of the Penalized PiXel-Fitting (`ppxf`) code (cite: Cappellari2017; Cappellari2023)." (H$\alpha$ maps); "Sets of multiple Gaussian components, in combination with the stellar templates, were used for fitting the optical emission lines and extracting the gas kinematics components." (H$\alpha$ maps)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive dust masses**: Derive regional dust masses from V-band extinction following goud94.  
  > "we calculated the mass of the dust within the dust regions plotted in Figure [4](#fg:ratio) following the approach described in (cite: goud94)." (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation); "The table also reports the dust masses derived from V-band extinction measurements and the sizes of the “clouds” detected in dust absorption." (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation)
   - assumption: Grain sizes 0.005–0.22 μm; specific grain mass density 3 g/cm^3.  
  > "We assumed a grain size between 0.005 and 0.22 $\mu$m (cite: draine84), a specific grain mass density of 3 g/cm$^3$" (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation)
   - assumption: Graphite and silicate with equal absorption; size distribution proportional to a^−3.5.  
  > "a mixture of graphite and silicate (equal absorption from those), a grain size distribution proportional to a$^{-3.5}$ (cite: mathis77; goud94)." (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Construct X-ray comparison maps**: Reprocess Chandra data, filter background flares, detect point sources, align ObsIDs to the longest observation, and merge exposure-corrected, background-subtracted images.  
  > "All the data were reprocessed using the standard $chandra\_repro$ tool." (X-Ray maps); "the observations were first matched to the astrometry of the longest one by comparing the position of point sources in the images" (X-Ray maps); "We then created merged, exposure-corrected, background subtracted *Chandra* images in the 0.5-7 keV band with the $merge\_obs$ tool" (X-Ray maps)
   - software: CIAO, chandra_repro, lc_clean, wavedetect, wcs_match, wcs_update, merge_obs
   - parameter: 0.5–7 keV images; flare threshold matched to blank-sky background maps.  
  > "Periods of strong background flares were filtered using the $lc\_clean$ script, and the threshold was set to match the blank-sky background maps." (X-Ray maps); "in the 0.5-7 keV band" (X-Ray maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visually inspect detected X-ray point sources.  
  > "Point sources were detected using the CIAO task $wavedetect$ and later verified by visual inspection of the X-ray images." (X-Ray maps)
8. **Test for near-infrared excess**: Inspect 2MASS and Spitzer-IRAC observations at CO clouds; improve contrast with J- and K-band image subtraction.  
  > "We have conducted a detailed analysis of near-infrared observations from both 2MASS and *Spitzer*-IRAC for galaxies with ALMA molecular gas detections" (Is there Excess Emission in the near-IR?); "Contrast is much improved when an appropriate subtraction of J and K-band images is used." (Is there Excess Emission in the near-IR?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Compare gas, dust, and cavity structures**: Compare CO-cloud and dust regions with Hα velocity maps and X-ray cavity morphology.  
  > "Velocity maps of the H$\alpha$-emitting gas (Figure [6](#fg:velocity)) enable a comparison of the kinematic structure in regions spatially coincident with both molecular gas clouds and dust extinction." (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation); "Red contours mark areas of strong dust extinction, while white dashed ellipses outline CO-detected molecular clouds." (Role of X-ray Data in Interpreting Dust, CO and H$\alpha$ Emission); "Inner X-ray cavities are indicated by black ellipses" (Role of X-ray Data in Interpreting Dust, CO and H$\alpha$ Emission)
   - parameter: Kinematic tables include CO clouds within the detected Hα velocity field.  
  > "Only CO clouds within the detected velocity field of the H$\alpha$ emitting gas are included." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare spatial coincidence with the agreement and offsets of warm- and cold-gas velocity fields.  
  > "Although the velocity fields of the warm and cold gas phases exhibit strong overall agreement, their absolute velocities differ, indicating a significant offset between the two phases." (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation)
10. **Assess formation-timescale consistency**: Qualitatively compare dust growth, sputtering, molecular formation, and cloud dynamical timescales.  
  > "This very qualitative analysis shows that the hierarchy of timescales allows the dust and molecules to form in the cooled gas of the systems in our sample, although the uncertainties are large." (Role of X-ray Data in Interpreting Dust, CO and H$\alpha$ Emission)
   - assumption: Cold-cloud age of order the dynamical time, ≈10^7 yr.  
  > "it is dynamically transient, with a likely age of the order of the dynamical time, $\approx 10^7$ yr." (Role of X-ray Data in Interpreting Dust, CO and H$\alpha$ Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO is detected in NGC 4636, NGC 5044, and NGC 5846; NGC 5813 is undetected.  
  > "Emission is detected in NGC 4636, NGC 5044, and NGC 5846, while NGC 5813 shows no significant detection within the data sensitivity." (ALMA maps)
- CO clouds in the studied NGC 4636 and NGC 5846 regions coincide with dust and warm ionized gas.  
  > "Where CO is detected, the molecular clouds appear to be co-spatial with both the dusty structures and the warm, line-emitting gas." (Regions with Distinct Dust Properties)
- Warm- and cold-gas velocity structures broadly agree but have absolute velocity offsets.  
  > "Although the velocity fields of the warm and cold gas phases exhibit strong overall agreement, their absolute velocities differ, indicating a significant offset between the two phases." (H$\alpha$ and CO Kinematics and Dust Correlation)
- No localized near-IR excess is found at CO clouds.  
  > "However, our investigation did not provide any evidence in support of near-IR excess emission at specific CO cloud locations." (Is there Excess Emission in the near-IR?)
- Dusty molecular and ionized structures partially align with X-ray cavities.  
  > "The partial alignment of dusty, molecular, and ionized structures with X-ray cavities suggests a close connection with feedback" (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Select X-ray-bright group-central galaxies with complete multiwavelength coverage and use high-resolution Chandra data for morphological comparisons.  
  > "The sample contains 5 of the brightest galaxies in X-ray bright groups, where both observations and theory indicate significant hot gas cooling." (The galaxy sample); "These galaxies have the most complete observational coverage, with deep *Chandra* X-ray data, Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) H$\alpha$ observations, Spitzer IR data and detailed Hubble Space Telescope (HST) maps." (The galaxy sample); "comparing the morphology and properties of warm gas and dust with the morphology of the hot gas at high ($\sim$arcsec) resolution" (Chandra Data)
- Why this method: Suppress noisy extinction pixels; truncate MUSE spectra to reduce sky residuals; use G23 for broad-wavelength extinction behavior.  
  > "smoothed with a 0.35$''$ FWHM (to remove isolate noisy pixels)." (Dust Absorption Maps); "we have truncated the spectra to 7400 Å where the spectrum is less contaminated by sky residuals" (H$\alpha$ maps); "which provides the mean dust-extinction behavior as a function of $R_V$ at spectroscopic resolution from 912 Å to 32 $\mu$m (cite: Gordon2023)." (Dust Extinction Properties)
- Alternative: Gas-rich merger origin considered unsupported for the sample.  
  > "we believe that there is no supporting evidence for the merger hypothesis for the galaxies investigated in this work." (Dust Extinction Properties)
- Alternative: I-band emission from hot dust considered but not supported by the near-IR search.  
  > "However, our investigation did not provide any evidence in support of near-IR excess emission at specific CO cloud locations." (Is there Excess Emission in the near-IR?)
- Alternative: Low RV may reflect grain processing or incomplete growth in young clouds.  
  > "dust in these environments may be more heavily processed, fragmented, or otherwise evolutionarily distinct from dust in typical star-forming regions." (Summary and Conclusions); "Alternatively, clouds with low $R_V$ values may represent relatively young regions where dust growth remains incomplete, yielding a grain-size distribution dominated by small particles." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Alternative: Simple axisymmetric inflow disfavored by off-center orbiting molecular clouds.  
  > "the presence of molecular clouds in the form of off-center, orbiting structures argues against simple, axisymmetric inflow models" (Summary and Conclusions)
- Alternative: Recent direct stellar ejection disfavored as the origin of the large obscuring clouds.  
  > "Given their size and associated dust mass, it is highly unlikely that these relatively large, optically obscuring clouds are infalling material recently ejected by stellar processes." (Summary and Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: Restrict comparative analysis to regions meeting data-quality and completeness criteria.  
  > "The analysis is restricted to the galaxies and dust clouds appearing in Tables [2](#tbl:masshalpha_part2) and [3](#tbl:masshalpha) based on data quality and completeness criteria." (X-Ray maps)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar-isophote models are limited at galaxy centers and low-signal outer radii.  
  > "The accuracy of these models is limited at the center of galaxies due to the small number of pixels along the isophotes and at larger radii where the signal-to-noise is low." (Dust Absorption Maps)
- Limitation (authors): The I-band stellar-model approximation underestimates absorption and is sensitive to stellar color gradients.  
  > "this method reduces the dust absorption estimate by assuming no dust absorption in the I band and is sensitive to stellar color gradient" (Dust Absorption Maps)
- Limitation (authors): NGC 5044 lacks a second optical HST band, preventing confirmation of peculiar extinction near CO.  
  > "HST data for NGC 5044 are available only in one optical band." (Dust Extinction Properties); "Because of the limited HST data available for NGC 5044, we cannot confirm that dust associated with CO has peculiar extinction characteristics for this galaxy." (Regions with Distinct Dust Properties)
- Limitation (authors): Some molecular clouds lack complementary MUSE or HST coverage.  
  > "one CO cloud in NGC 5846 and one in NGC 4636 lie outside the field of view of the MUSE H$\alpha$ emission map and are therefore not discussed here." (Regions with Distinct Dust Properties); "Many unresolved CO features lie outside the field of view of the HST extinction map, preventing any analysis about their association with the dust." (Role of X-ray Data in Interpreting Dust, CO and H$\alpha$ Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Detecting small near-IR surface-brightness variations is difficult.  
  > "Revealing small variation in surface brightness at the centers of these bright galaxies has proven to be difficult using a single band image." (Is there Excess Emission in the near-IR?)
- Limitation (authors): Timescale arguments are qualitative and have large uncertainties.  
  > "This very qualitative analysis shows that the hierarchy of timescales allows the dust and molecules to form in the cooled gas of the systems in our sample, although the uncertainties are large." (Role of X-ray Data in Interpreting Dust, CO and H$\alpha$ Emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two-band extinction cannot uniquely distinguish grain size, composition, and mixed star–dust geometry.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reported ≳3σ extinction departures lack a reproducible fitting statistic and covariance treatment.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cavity alignment is mainly morphological; no quantitative chance-alignment or causal test is supplied.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 5.4 arcsec maximum recoverable scale complicates interpretation of CO absence around extended dusty structures.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The conclusions describe consistent radial velocities more strongly than the explicit warm–cold velocity offsets warrant.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The analysis reports RV ~2.1–2.5, while the conclusion summary reports ~2.0–2.5; these should remain distinct answer-key entries.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO-cloud count and association statements vary with field-of-view restrictions and should not be interpreted as uniform sample-wide completeness.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The acknowledgements print 2011.0.00735.SSB, whereas the ALMA section gives 2011.0.00735.S; verify archive selection using the latter explicit project code.
