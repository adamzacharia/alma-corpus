## Analysis recipe

1. **Select galaxy samples and homogenise spatial resolution and pixel grids**: Select massive (log(M_*/M_sun) >= 10), moderately inclined (i <= 70 deg) galaxies from VERTICO (excluding NGC 4772 and NGC 4698) and star-forming control galaxies from HERACLES/THINGS. Smooth CO(2-1) and H I cubes with CASA IMSMOOTH to match a 1200 pc physical beam and 650 pc spaxel size (15 arcsec beam, 8 arcsec spaxels at Virgo).  
  > "In this work, we focus on massive ( log ( M ⋆ /M ⊙ ) ≥ 10) galaxies to minimise metallicity-dependent uncertainties in tracing the molecular gas content of galaxies with CO(2-1) emission, and we enforce an optical inclination cut of i ≤ 70 ◦" (2.1 VERTICO sample); "At the assumed 16.5 Mpc distance to Virgo, the 15/8-arcsec beam/spaxels of the VERTICO data correspond to a 1200/650 pc physical resolution." (2.1 VERTICO sample); "Before calculating physical quantities, the HERACLES and THINGS data were smoothed using the IMSMOOTH routine of CASA to match the physical spaxel size and resolution of the VERTICO data, and circularise the synthesised beam." (2.2 HERACLES control sample)
   - software: CASA (IMSMOOTH)
   - parameter: log(M_*/M_sun) >= 10, i <= 70 deg, physical resolution = 1200 pc, spaxel size = 650 pc  
  > "In this work, we focus on massive ( log ( M ⋆ /M ⊙ ) ≥ 10) galaxies to minimise metallicity-dependent uncertainties in tracing the molecular gas content of galaxies with CO(2-1) emission, and we enforce an optical inclination cut of i ≤ 70 ◦" (2.1 VERTICO sample); "At the assumed 16.5 Mpc distance to Virgo, the 15/8-arcsec beam/spaxels of the VERTICO data correspond to a 1200/650 pc physical resolution." (2.1 VERTICO sample)
   - assumption: Virgo distance = 16.5 Mpc; Kroupa (2001) IMF; LambdaCDM cosmology with h = 0.7, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "all physical quantities used in this work were computed using a Kroupa (2001) stellar initial mass function, a Λ CDM cosmology with h = 0.7, Ω M = 0.3 and Ω Λ = 0.7, and we adopt a constant distance for Virgo galaxies of 16.5 Mpc" (1. Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare integrated properties of the 8 HERACLES control galaxies to star-forming galaxies from xCOLD GASS to verify no bias in H I or molecular gas mass fractions.  
  > "they span the same range in log M H I / M ⋆ and log M MG / M ⋆ as the xCOLD GASS sample, indicating that there is no bias toward H I- or molecule-rich systems in our control sample." (2.2 HERACLES control sample)
2. **Compute spatially resolved surface densities (Sigma_HI, Sigma_mol, Sigma_*) and mask to optical disc**: Apply signal masks to create moment 0 maps; convert integrated fluxes to H I mass (Eq. 1), CO luminosity (Eq. 2), and molecular gas mass (Eq. 3); compute WISE pixel-by-pixel stellar mass; divide by 650-pc pixel area; de-project by cos(i); and mask spaxels to within B-band R_25.  
  > "The H I and molecular gas mass of spaxels were converted to surface densities Σ H I and Σ mol in units of M ⊙ pc –2 using the 650-pc physical pixel size." (2.1 VERTICO sample); "All surface densities used in this work were de-projected to face-on values using a factor of cos ( i )." (2.1 VERTICO sample); "so we masked each galaxy to only include pixels/spaxels within the B -band R 25, where the radius of each spaxel was calculated from the photometric PA and inclination." (2.1 VERTICO sample)
   - parameter: R_21 = 0.8, alpha_CO = 4.35 M_sun pc^-2, intrinsic axial ratio q = 0.2  
  > "We used a constant R 21 = 0.8, as derived by Brown et al. (2021), and a constant α CO =" (2.1 VERTICO sample); "assuming an intrinsic axial ratio of q = 0.2" (2.1 VERTICO sample)
   - assumption: Optically thin H I emission; no Helium correction for H I in Sections 2-4 (36% Helium included in alpha_CO, and 36% Helium added to H I in Section 5)  
  > "The H I mass in each spaxel was calculated assuming optically thin emission (e.g., Meyer et al., 2017)" (2.1 VERTICO sample); "Unless otherwise specified, we do not correct our H I masses for the Helium abundance." (2.1 VERTICO sample); "To be consistent with previous works, in this section, we correct our H I masses for a 36 per cent contribution from Helium." (5. Physical drivers of Sigma_mol reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Determine H I deficiency categories and H I truncation radius stellar surface density (<log Sigma_*>_HI)**: Calculate def-H I from Eq. 4 and split VERTICO H I-deficient galaxies into moderate (def-H I = [0.3, 0.7) dex) and large (def-H I >= 0.7 dex) subsamples. Compute radial profiles of log Sigma_HI and log Sigma_* in 4-arcsec annuli to find R_HI where radial average Sigma_HI = 1 M_sun pc^-2, and record the average log Sigma_* at R_HI (<log Sigma_*>_HI).  
  > "We computed radial profiles of log Σ H I and log Σ ⋆ using 4 arcsec annuli, which we found gave the best consistency with the Chung et al. (2009) VIVA measurements." (2.1 VERTICO sample); "Defining the H I truncation radius ( R H I ) to be where the radial average Σ H I = 1 M ⊙ pc –2 (e.g., Wang et al., 2014, 2016; Stevens et al., 2019a), we used the value of log Σ ⋆ at this radius to determine the average log Σ ⋆ at R H I, ⟨ log Σ ⋆ ⟩ H I ." (2.1 VERTICO sample); "we split the VERTICO galaxies into two bins of global def-H I h, def-H I = [0.3, 0.7) dex and def-H I ≥ 0.7 dex, which we refer to these as the ‘moderate’ and ‘large’ def-H I samples, respectively." (4.1 Atomic hydrogen)
   - parameter: 4-arcsec annuli; Sigma_HI(R_HI) = 1 M_sun pc^-2; def-H I bins: [0.3, 0.7) dex and >= 0.7 dex  
  > "We computed radial profiles of log Σ H I and log Σ ⋆ using 4 arcsec annuli" (2.1 VERTICO sample); "Defining the H I truncation radius ( R H I ) to be where the radial average Σ H I = 1 M ⊙ pc –2" (2.1 VERTICO sample); "we split the VERTICO galaxies into two bins of global def-H I h, def-H I = [0.3, 0.7) dex and def-H I ≥ 0.7 dex" (4.1 Atomic hydrogen)

## Qualitative findings

- Cluster environmental processes not only strip H I at large radii, but also reduce the average H I surface density within the H I truncation radius R_HI, and in the most H I-deficient galaxies the densest H I regions are no longer present.  
  > "While the stripping of gas from galaxies impacts the H I gas at large radii, it has the additional effect of lowering the average H I surface density of the remaining gas within the H I-stripping radius, R H I ." (6. Summary)
- Four galaxies (NGC 4298, NGC 4689, NGC 4457, NGC 4450) exhibit co-spatial truncation of their H I and molecular gas discs.  
  > "First, the median rMGSM relation of four galaxies (NGC 4298, NGC 4689, NGC 4457, NGC 4450) transition from detections to non-detections coincidently with R H I, ⟨ log Σ ⋆ ⟩ H I, indicating that these galaxies show co-spatial H I and molecular gas truncation." (4.2 Molecular hydrogen)
- Molecular gas is less strongly affected than H I: lower surface density molecular gas at lower Sigma_* is removed first, while in large def-H I galaxies the average Sigma_mol within R_HI is reduced by a factor of ~2.8 (64%).  
  > "The weighted median detected spaxel is 0.45 dex lower (a factor of 2.8 or 64 per cent) than the control sample" (4.2 Molecular hydrogen); "Overall, the molecular gas is less affected than the H I, and we find evidence that environmental mechanisms first reduce the lower surface density molecular gas that exists preferentially at lower Σ ⋆" (6. Summary)
- There is no evidence for environment-driven elevation of Sigma_HI or Sigma_mol on 1.2-kpc scales in H I-deficient Virgo galaxies.  
  > "We find no evidence for environment-driven elevation of Σ H I or Σ mol in H I-deficient galaxies." (Abstract)
- Changes in ISM physical conditions (midplane hydrostatic pressure or total gas surface density) cannot explain the observed reduction in molecular gas content; direct stripping/ablation of molecular gas is required.  
  > "Using the ratio of Σ mol -to- Σ H I in individual regions, we show that changes in the ISM physical conditions, estimated using the total gas surface density and midplane hydrostatic pressure, cannot explain the observed reduction in molecular gas content. Instead, we suggest that direct stripping of the molecular gas is required to explain our results." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The Virgo cluster is close (16.5 Mpc), spans populations of galaxies at various stages of infall, and the combination of VERTICO CO(2-1) and VIVA H I observations enables studying cold gas properties on ~1.2-kpc scales.  
  > "At a distance of 16.5 Mpc (Mei et al., 2007) it is close, allowing for detailed studies of individual galaxies, and it has been observed comprehensively across nearly the entire electromagnetic spectrum" (1. Introduction); "Additionally, the VERTICO sample was selected from the VLA Imaging of Virgo galaxies in Atomic gas survey (VIVA, Chung et al., 2009) of H I emission. Together, these data enable us to study how the environmental processes influence the cold gas properties of galaxies in the Virgo cluster on ∼ 1.2-kpc scales." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Spatially resolved scaling relations on kpc-scales within the optical disc increase statistics and avoid averaging over physically distinct regions compared to azimuthally averaged radial profiles; equal galaxy weighting prevents larger or less-stripped galaxies from dominating the statistics.  
  > "Thus, comparing the physical quantities of kpc-scale regions within galaxies increases our statistics, and reduces the effects of averaging over larger areas that include many different physical regions." (1. Introduction); "Further, we must also ensure that any histograms and statistics we compute are not biased toward larger galaxies and galaxies that are less affected by the environment, as these systems contain more spaxels." (3. Combined Sigma_HI - Sigma_* and Sigma_mol - Sigma_* relations)
- Alternative: Using H I-normal VERTICO galaxies as the control sample was rejected because cluster environmental processes can begin to impact galaxies before they become globally H I-deficient.  
  > "While the VERTICO sample contains some H I-normal systems, environmental processes can begin to impact galaxies before they become H I-deficient. Thus, we chose to draw our control sample from a different survey to ensure that we had an H I-normal reference that was as free from environmental effects as possible." (2.1 VERTICO sample)
- Alternative: Unweighted spaxel statistics were rejected in favour of equal-galaxy weighting because individual galaxies form distinct sequences and unweighted statistics are biased toward larger, gas-rich galaxies.  
  > "Further, we must also ensure that any histograms and statistics we compute are not biased toward larger galaxies and galaxies that are less affected by the environment, as these systems contain more spaxels." (3. Combined Sigma_HI - Sigma_* and Sigma_mol - Sigma_* relations)
- Alternative: CO(2-1)-dark gas or variations in alpha_CO and R_21 due to metallicity or UV radiation field changes were rejected as explanations for the reduced Sigma_mol.  
  > "Metallicity variations cannot explain a reduction in CO(21) emission, as while at smaller metallicities there is more CO(21) dark gas, cluster galaxies show similar or elevated metallicity over isolated systems" (5. Physical drivers of Sigma_mol reduction); "Further evidence that VERTICO galaxies do not have substantial CO(2-1)-dark gas comes from studies of the KennicuttSchmidt (Schmidt, 1959; Kennicutt, 1998) star formation law." (5. Physical drivers of Sigma_mol reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): VERTICO CO(2-1) observation footprints were based on R_HI and vary in physical coverage (0.44 to 1 x R_25).  
  > "Subsequently, there is some variation in the physical coverage of the molecular gas in galaxies (0.44– 1 × R 25 ), and we accounted for this in our analysis of the molecular gas content." (2.1 VERTICO sample)
- Limitation (authors): HERACLES/THINGS control data are shallower than VERTICO/VIVA data, leading to a higher fraction of non-detected spaxels at low Sigma_* (< 1.25).  
  > "The HERACLES:THINGS data are shallower than the VERTICO:VIVA data in both H I ( Σ H I ≥ 1 – 3 M ⊙ pc –2 within B -band R 25 compared to Σ H I ≥ 1 M ⊙ pc –2 ) and molecular gas ( Σ mol ≥ 1–3 M ⊙ pc –2 compared to Σ mol ≥ 0.4–1 M ⊙ pc –2 )." (2.2 HERACLES control sample)
- Limitation (authors): Interacting galaxies NGC 4567 and NGC 4568 cannot be separated in velocity space, causing partial confusion in their H I observations.  
  > "NGC 4567 is interacting with NGC 4568 (row 2, column 2) and the galaxies cannot be separated in velocity space (Chung et al., 2009), causing an overlap of the rH ISM relation of each galaxy" (4.1 Atomic hydrogen)
- Limitation (authors): Central stellar structures (bars, bulges, rings in NGC 4535, NGC 4548, NGC 4579) alter the slope and shape of the rMGSM relation at high Sigma_*.  
  > "Beyond having interesting effects on the shape of the rMGSM relation, this suggests that we must use caution when comparing Σ mol properties at fixed Σ ⋆, particularly in the ranges where stellar structures could be important." (4.2 Molecular hydrogen)
- Limitation (authors): The 1.2-kpc resolution data cannot differentiate between diffuse molecular gas and dense GMC gas.  
  > "Our data cannot differentiate between diffuse and dense gas; however, the 64 per cent reduction (§4.2) in the typical molecular gas content within R H I observed in our large def-H I galaxies is greater than the ‘typical’ diffuse gas fractions mentioned above." (5. Physical drivers of Sigma_mol reduction)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Constant Milky Way alpha_CO and constant R_21 = 0.8 are assumed across all spaxels despite potential radial or environmental variations in excitation and conversion factor.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The control sample consists of only 8 field galaxies from HERACLES/THINGS, which may limit sample variance estimation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): VIVA H I beam sizes (15.2-20 arcsec, average 17 arcsec) are slightly larger than the 15-arcsec smoothed VERTICO CO(2-1) beam.
