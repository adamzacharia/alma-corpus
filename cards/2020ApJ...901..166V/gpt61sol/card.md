2. **Separate disk and envelope emission**: Inspect moment maps, major-axis cuts, position-velocity diagrams and spectra; compare visibility amplitudes with disk-only and disk-plus-envelope models  
  > "position-velocity (*pv*) diagrams are constructed along the disk major axis for the four single sources" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO morphology); "These spectra are extracted in a 6$^{\prime\prime}$ circular aperture and only include pixels with $> 3\sigma$ emission." (Envelope contribution)
   - parameter: Visibility profiles in 0.5 km s^-1 channels  
  > "we calculated the visibility amplitude profiles for a Keplerian disk in 0.5 km s$^{-1}$ channels using the modeling tools outlined in (cite: Sheehan2019)." (Envelope contribution)
   - parameter: Add a rotating infalling envelope of radius 3000 au when the disk cannot reproduce small-uv-distance emission  
  > "If there was a component at small uv-distances that could not be reproduced with the disk, we added a rotating infalling envelope with 3000 au radius using the prescription by (cite: Ulrich1976)." (Envelope contribution)
   - assumption: Literature stellar masses, disk radii, inclinations and position angles; constant molecular abundances throughout the disk  
  > "Values for the stellar mass, disk radius, inclination and position angle were adopted from the literature, and the C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO abundance were taken constant throughout the disk." (Envelope contribution)
   - fitting: Adjust disk mass to approximately match visibility profiles in each channel  
  > "The disk mass was adjusted to approximately match the visibility amplitude profiles in each channel." (Envelope contribution)
   - validation: IRAS 04302 disk-only visibility result agrees with position-velocity diagrams  
  > "For IRAS 04302, both the C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO visibility amplitude profiles can be reproduced without an envelope." 3. **Derive molecular column densities**: Measure integrated line flux over the dust-emitting area and calculate disk-averaged total column densities with Equation 1  
  > "we calculate disk-averaged total column densities, $N_T$, assuming optically thin emission in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances); "The integrated fluxes are measured over the dust emitting area" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances)
   - parameter: C17O: 30 K; H2CO: 100 K  
  > "A temperature of 30 K is adopted for C$^{17}$O and 100 K for H$_2$CO, as these are slightly above their freeze-out temperatures." (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission in LTE  
  > "assuming optically thin emission in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Test H2CO at 30 K; remeasure L1527 with a smaller aperture and central-channel exclusion for literature comparison  
  > "Changing the temperature for H$_2$CO to 30 K decreases the column densities by only a factor $\lesssim$3."    - assumption: Optically thin continuum  
  > "with the assumption of optically thin emission" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100

## Qualitative findings

- C17O and H2CO detected toward all sources  
  > "Both C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO are clearly detected toward all sources with a velocity gradient along the continuum structures" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO morphology)
- Line emission predominantly originates in disks, with larger envelope contributions for H2CO  
  > "In all sources, C$^{17}$O traces predominantly the disk, with some envelope contribution, especially in L1527 and L1489." (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO morphology); "H$_2$CO emission also originates in the disk, but has a larger envelope component." (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO morphology)
- L1527 shows no sign of CO freeze-out  
  > "The observations show no sign of CO freeze-out in L1527 and resemble the warm model" (Temperature structure in the edge-on disks)
- H2CO traces surface layers in IRAS 04302 and L1527  
  > "H$_2$CO emission originates primarily in the surface layers of IRAS 04302 and L1527." (Conclusions)
- No substantial CO processing inferred from C17O abundances  
  > "The derived C$^{17}$O abundances are thus within a factor 5 of the ISM abundance, suggesting that no substantial processing has happened" (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances)
- Young disks lack an extended hot-core-like region; compact HDO and CH3OH reservoirs remain possible  
  > "These results thus merely show that Class I disks do not have an extended hot-core like region, making the detection of COMs just as challenging as in Class II disks." (Chemical complexity in young disks); "These non-detections thus do not rule out the presence of HDO and CH$_3$OH" (Chemical complexity in young disks)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Different molecular freeze-out temperatures constrain global temperature structure; edge-on disks expose their midplanes  
  > "The different freeze-out temperatures of these species allow us to derive a global temperature structure." (Abstract); "IRAS 04302 and L1527 are seen edge-on, which allows a direct view of the midplane" (Observations)
- Why this method: Use position-velocity structure and visibility models to distinguish disk emission from envelope contamination  
  > "In these diagrams, disk emission is located at small angular offsets and high velocities, while envelope emission extends to larger offsets but has lower velocities." (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO morphology); "A first assessment of the envelope contribution to the line emission can be made by comparing generic models of either only a Keplerian disk or a disk embedded in an envelope to the observed visibility amplitudes." (Envelope contribution)
- Alternative: L1489 extended CO: non-thermal desorption, radial temperature inversion or warm inner envelope  
  > "This could be the result of non-thermal desorption of CO ice in the outer disk if the dust column is low enough for UV photons to penetrate" (Temperature structure in less-inclined disks); "or due to a radial temperature inversion resulting from radial drift and dust settling (cite: Facchini2017)." (Temperature structure in less-inclined disks); "Another possibility is that the extended emission is due to a warm inner envelope component." (Temperature structure in less-inclined disks)
- Alternative: Recent small accretion bursts or lower disk densities could explain extended CO snowlines  
  > "could thus indicate that small accretion bursts have occurred in the Class I systems and that the CO snowlines have not yet shifted back to their quiescent location." (Comparison with protostellar envelopes and protoplanetary disks); "Alternatively, these young disks may have lower densities than more evolved disks." (Comparison with protostellar envelopes and protoplanetary disks)
- Alternative: Dust opacity and excitation are disfavored as explanations for inner H2CO deficits  
  > "The decrease in H$_2$CO emission is also unlikely to be due to the continuum being optically thick because this would affect the C$^{17}$O emission as well" (Decrease in H$_2$CO in the inner disk); "An excitation effect thus seems unlikely, unless the C$^{17}$O emission is optically thick." (Decrease in H$_2$CO in the inner disk)
- Alternative: HDO and CH3OH may be hidden by optically thick dust, high inclination or beam dilution  
  > "the detection of HDO and CH$_3$OH may have been hindered by optically thick dust in the inner region, or by the high inclinations of these sources." (Chemical complexity in young disks); "if the region where HDO and CH$_3$OH are present is much smaller than the beam, they may have higher columns than the upper limits derived here." (Chemical complexity in young disks)
- Alternative: Alternative κ_1.3mm = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1 changes molecular abundances by ∼2  
  > "adopting $\kappa_{\rm{1.3mm}}$ = 0.899 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$, as often done for protostellar disks and envelopes (cite: Jorgensen2007; Andersen2019; Tobin2020), only affects the molecular abundances by a factor $\sim$2." (C$^{17}$O and H$_2$CO column densities and abundances)
