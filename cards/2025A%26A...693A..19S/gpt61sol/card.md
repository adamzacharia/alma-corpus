  > "In this calculation, we also consider only phase differences that fall within the $95\%$ confidence levels and outside the CoI of the wavelet cross-power spectra." ## Qualitative findings

- All three observables exhibit both standing and propagating waves.  
  > "all three parameters (brightness temperature, horizontal displacement, and width) exhibit a combination of standing and propagating waves." (Wavelet analysis)
- Standing-wave phase relationships dominate, especially in brightness temperature and horizontal displacement.  
  > "reveal a dominance of in-phase and anti-phase relationships, particularly for brightness temperature and horizontal displacement, suggesting a prevalence of standing waves." (Wavelet analysis)
- Transverse and width oscillations likely represent kink and sausage modes, respectively.  
  > "The transverse oscillations likely represent the presence of MHD kink modes (cite: 1982SoPh...75....3S), while the size fluctuations (width oscillations) likely indicate sausage modes (cite: 1981SoPh...69...39R; 2013A&A...555A..75M)." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- ALMA can sample dynamic dark fibrillar structures.  
  > "this work demonstrates ALMA’s capability to effectively sample such dynamic dark fibrillar structures, despite previous doubts." (Discussion and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: A long-lived fibril persists for the entire observation period, enabling wave studies along it.  
  > "here we present identification of a long-lived fibril (lasting over the entire observation period), enabling wave studies to be directly undertaken along this structure." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Wavelets localize spectral power in time and frequency; cross-power gives phase relationships and phase speeds.  
  > "Wavelet analysis localizes spectral power in both time and frequency by decomposing the signal into time-localized wavelets, revealing how the frequency content of the signal changes over time" (Wavelet analysis); "obtaining the phase relationships between the oscillations and, thus, the phase speeds of the waves propagating along the fibril." (Wavelet analysis)
- Alternative: Periods longer than 1000 s treated as likely slow structural evolution and removed.  
  > "Furthermore, periods larger than 1000 s (i.e., smaller than 1 mHz) were subtracted from all signals by means of wavelet filtering." (Wavelet analysis); "Such long periods are likely related to slow evolution of the magnetic structure, rather than wave signatures." (Wavelet analysis)
- Alternative: Standing-wave interpretation may include superposition of oppositely traveling waves.  
  > "However, standing waves can result from the superposition (interference) of two waves traveling in opposite directions, each with its own phase speed." (Phase speeds)
- Alternative: GONG Hα images did not resolve a corresponding feature.  
  > "While no clear counterpart was identified in the relatively low-resolution GONG H$\alpha$ images, we anticipate a correspondence with higher-resolution H$\alpha$ observations." (Identification of oscillatory signals)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only one long-lived fibril identified; other extended dark structures may contain unresolved fibrils.  
  > "Instead, we find a few extended dark structures, likely due to unresolved individual fibrils." (Analysis and results); "However, we identified only one long-lived fibril (along with a few short-lived ones)." (Analysis and results)
- Limitation (authors): Abrupt phase-angle changes may be noise or spurious signals.  
  > "We note that some of the abrupt changes in phase angles could be due to noise or spurious signals." (Wavelet analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Superposed wave modes complicate interpretation of cross-observable phase relationships.  
  > "However, interpreting such relationships is complex due to the potential superposition of multiple MHD wave modes, which current theoretical models are not yet sophisticated enough to fully disentangle" (Wavelet analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Waveguide asymmetry and cross-sectional density structure require future detailed modeling.  
  > "Such studies would require detailed modeling of the fibril’s cross-sectional shape and density structure, which could be achieved by combining high-resolution observations with advanced MHD wave modeling techniques." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reported 15:59–16:38 UTC span cannot contain five 10-minute scans plus the listed breaks; the analyzed 1568 s interval also needs clarification.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 1 gives median periods 241 and 224 s, whereas the abstract and conclusions give 240 and 225 s.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The conclusions call 74, 52 and 28 km/s mean absolute velocities, but Table 2 identifies them as medians; absolute means are 148, 103 and 86 km/s.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Interpolating calibration gaps may affect period and phase distributions; no gap-treatment robustness test is supplied.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Near-zero phase lags can produce extreme inferred speeds; the handling of these values is unspecified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distribution standard deviations are not uncertainty estimates on the medians.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): One selected fibril does not establish population-wide ubiquity.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mode identification is suggestive; no forward-model comparison or wave-energy deposition measurement is presented.
