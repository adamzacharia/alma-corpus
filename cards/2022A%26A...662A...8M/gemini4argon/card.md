- Question: Investigate the origin of the initial mass function (IMF) of stars and whether the core mass function (CMF) varies with cloud characteristics and evolutionary stage across 15 massive Galactic protoclusters.  
  > "One of the breakthroughs expected from ALMA, which is the basis of our Cycle 5 ALMA-IMF Large Program, is the question of the origin of the initial mass function (IMF) of stars." (abstract); "A detailed scrutiny and study of statistical samples of massive protoclusters is mandatory to test observationally whether the IMF origin is in fact independent of cloud characteristics or not." - ALMA projects: 2017.1.01355.L, 2013.1.01365.S, 2015.1.01273.S
- bands: Band 6 (1.3 mm) and Band 3 (3 mm)  
  > "The ALMA-IMF Large Program images each of the 15 massive protocluster clouds of [\[tab:sample\]](#tab:sample) both at 1.3 mm (ALMA Band 6) and 3 mm (Band 3)." (Observations and data reduction)
- frequencies and spectral setup: 8 SPWs at 228.4 GHz (Band 6, central frequency 228.965 GHz, bandwidths 117-1875 MHz, resolutions 0.33-1.5 km/s) and 4 SPWs at 99.66 GHz (Band 3, central frequency 100.713 GHz, bandwidths 117-938 MHz, resolutions 0.23-1.8 km/s)  
  > "The central frequencies of the ALMA-IMF bands are $\nu_{\textrm{B6}}=228.965$ GHz (1.3 mm) and $\nu_{\textrm{B3}}=100.713$ GHz (3 mm) (cite: ginsburg22), assuming a spectral index of $\alpha=3.5$" (Observing strategy); "The spectral setup chosen for the ALMA-IMF Large Program contains eight spectral windows at 228.4 GHz (1.3 mm) and four at 99.66 GHz (3 mm) (see [\[tab:lines\]](#tab:lines))." (Observing strategy)
- continuum bandwidth: 3.7 GHz at 1.3 mm (Band 6) and 2.9 GHz at 3 mm (Band 3) for bsens; 6.4 GHz total noncontinuous bandwidth  
  > "The first set of continuum images, called `bsens`, are derived using all observed spectral channels of the ALMA band 6 (1.3 mm or 228.965 GHz with a spectral index of $\alpha=3.5$) or band 3 (3 mm or 100.713 GHz with $\alpha=3.5$), summing up to 3.7 GHz and 2.9 GHz, respectively." (ALMA-IMF data set); "With a 6.4 GHz noncontinuous bandwidth, corresponding to 2.9 GHz covered by four spectral windows at 3 mm plus 3.5 GHz covered in eight spectral windows at 1.3 mm, the ALMA-IMF data will reveal the molecular content of a large population of objects" (Hot cores and molecular complexity in high- and low-mass protostars)
- arrays: 12 m array, 7 m (ACA) array, and Total Power Array (only 12 m array analyzed here)  
  > "The ALMA-IMF Large Program was observed from October 2017 to August 2018 with a total observation time of 69 hours and 172 hours with the 12 m and 7 m (ACA) arrays, respectively, and 595 hours with the Total Power Array." (ALMA-IMF data set); "The 7 m array data are therefore not used for the present analysis." (ALMA-IMF data set)

## Qualitative findings

- The 15 ALMA-IMF protoclusters are classified into 6 Young, 5 Intermediate, and 4 Evolved clouds based on 1.3 mm-to-3 mm continuum flux ratios and H41α free-free surface densities.  
  > "We classify ALMA-IMF protoclusters as Young (six protoclusters), Intermediate (five protoclusters), or Evolved (four protoclusters) based on the amount of dense gas in the cloud that has potentially been impacted by H ii region(s)." (abstract)
- The core catalog (~700 cores) exhibits no significant bias in core mass or size with cloud distance or evolutionary stage.  
  > "We show that this core sample has no significant distance bias and can be used to build core mass functions (CMFs) at similar physical scales." (abstract); "This core sample, published in Paper III (cite: louvet22), has no significant bias with cloud distance or cloud evolutionary stage (see Figs. [5](#fig:Mass-Size)-[6](#fig:CoreContent))." (Conclusion)
- Matter concentration from clouds (1 pc) to clumps/filaments (0.1 pc) to cores (0.01 pc) does not show significant evolution from Young to Evolved protoclusters, suggesting limited impact of stellar feedback on dense gas outside HII regions.  
  > "Our first analysis does not reveal any significant evolution of the matter concentration from clouds to cores (i.e., from 1 pc to 0.01 pc scales) or from the youngest to more evolved protoclusters, indicating that cloud dynamical evolution and stellar feedback have for the moment only had a slight effect on the structure of high-density gas in our sample." (abstract)
- Every targeted protocluster, including the youngest ones, hosts at least one core with line-rich COM spectra, indicating that the hot core phase emerges very early in high-mass star and cluster formation.  
  > "A first-look analysis of ALMA-IMF data cubes concerning hot molecular gas indicates that all the targeted protoclusters, even the youngest ones, contain at least one core with line-rich spectra." (Hot cores and molecular complexity in high- and low-mass protostars); "This trend suggests that the hot core phase would appear very early in the evolutionary sequence of the formation of high-mass stars and massive clusters." (Hot cores and molecular complexity in high- and low-mass protostars)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 15 massive ATLASGAL protoclusters at 2-5.5 kpc in ALMA Bands 3 and 6 to achieve matched ~2000 au spatial resolution and subsolar (~0.15-0.2 M_sun) point-mass sensitivity while covering parsec-scale clouds and key kinematic, ionized, and COM spectral lines.  
  > "In order to reach an angular resolution of a couple of thousand au and a subsolar mass sensitivity with reasonable ALMA integration times, while reaching the exceptional W43 mini-starburst region at 5.5 kpc (cite: nguyen11a), we applied a distance-limited criterion of $d\leq 5.5~\mbox{kpc}$." (The most massive protocluster clouds of the Milky Way); "We chose the 1.3 mm and 3 mm spectral bands primarily for their mostly optically thin emission in (massive) cores and their relatively well-defined dust opacities." (Observing strategy)
- Why this method: Used both 1.3 mm/3 mm continuum flux ratios and H41α recombination line surface densities to overcome mid-IR classification ambiguities, and used getsf on line-free (cleanest) continuum images to separate round cores from filaments and free-free peaks.  
  > "Owing to the uncertainties from the overall 1.3 mm-to-3 mm flux ratios, we introduced a second criterion based on estimates of the free-free continuum emission (shown in blue in the three-color images, [3](#fig:3col)) using cubes of the H41$\alpha$ hydrogen recombination line." (Evolutionary stage of the ALMA-IMF protoclusters); "We used here the multiscale, multiwavelength extraction method of sources and filaments *getsf* that spatially decomposes the observed images to separate relatively round sources from elongated filaments and their background cloud (cite: menshchikov21)." (Population of cores in the ALMA-IMF protoclusters)
- Alternative: Combining 12 m array data with 7 m (ACA) array data was rejected for the present continuum and core extraction analysis because it produced inconsistent quality, increased noise levels, and imaging artifacts across several fields.  
  > "For several of the target fields, incorporating the 7 m array data resulted in increased noise levels and/or imaging artifacts. The increased noise levels is particularly problematic for source extraction on the $\sim$2 100 au scales. The 7 m array data are therefore not used for the present analysis." (ALMA-IMF data set)
- Alternative: Using bsens continuum images or MnGSeg-filtered images for the initial core catalog was deferred to future work because extracting sources from bsens requires careful removal of line contamination.  
  > "Moreover, we showed that using both the `bsens` and `cleanest` images (see definitions in Sect. [3.2](#s:dataset)), the number of robust core detections could further increase by a factor of up to $\sim$2 (Pouteau et al. in prep.). Extracting these additional sources requires careful treatment of the line contamination to exclude emission peaks associated with line rather than continuum emission." (Population of cores in the ALMA-IMF protoclusters)
- How they decided it was enough: Sample of 15 protoclusters (~53 pc^2) yields ~700 cores in cleanest images (up to ~1500 with bsens/MnGSeg), providing sufficient statistical power to build individual CMFs per protocluster and test variations with cloud properties and evolution.  
  > "The ALMA-IMF core catalog that is summarized in [\[tab:concentration\]](#tab:concentration) and will be published in the companion paper Paper III (cite: louvet22) is already rich enough to investigate variations in the shape of the CMF." (Population of cores in the ALMA-IMF protoclusters)
- Limitation (authors): Kinematic distance estimates for clumps without maser parallaxes are subject to uncertainties from the Galactic rotation curve and near/far kinematic distance ambiguities.  
  > "These distance estimates are subject to uncertainties, such as the Galactic rotation curve and association with the near or far kinematic distance solutions." (The most massive protocluster clouds of the Milky Way)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA-IMF raw data downloaded from the archive before November 2020 were affected by a systematic error in spectral data normalization and system temperature, requiring reprocessing at JAO.  
  > "The ALMA-IMF data were indeed affected by a systematic error in the spectral data normalization and returned to the Joint ALMA Observatory (JAO) for further processing in November 2020. Any data downloaded from the archive before this time are therefore affected by these issues." (ALMA-IMF data set)
- Limitation (authors): Core masses have absolute uncertainties of a factor of a few and relative uncertainties of ~50% due to assumed uniform dust temperatures (20 K for most cores, 75 K for the 10 brightest) and partial optical thickness in the ~20 most massive cores (underestimating mass by 15%-40%).  
  > "According to (cite: motte18b) and Pouteau et al. (in prep.), we estimate that about 20 cores (i.e., the most massive 3% of the core sample) could be partially optically thick. Therefore, their masses would be underestimated by factors of 15%–40% when using [\[eq:mass\]](#eq:mass) (cite: motte18b). We estimated the absolute values of the core masses to be uncertain by a factor of a few, and the relative values between cores to be uncertain by $\sim$50%." (Population of cores in the ALMA-IMF protoclusters)
- Limitation (authors): For a strictly homogeneous comparison of core populations across all clouds, continuum images should be convolved to the exact same physical spatial resolution prior to source extraction.  
  > "To more robustly compare the core population of the ALMA-IMF clouds, one should convolve images to the same spatial resolution before extracting cores (cite: louvet21)" (Population of cores in the ALMA-IMF protoclusters)
- Limitation (authors): Low spatial dynamic range (1-2 orders of magnitude) in the 12 m-only continuum images restricts structural analysis to 3-scale mass ratios, which are affected by interferometric spatial filtering.  
  > "Due to the low spatial dynamic range (i.e., one to two orders of magnitude) of the ALMA-IMF continuum images currently available (12 m array; see Sect. [3.2](#s:dataset)), we limit our structural study here to ratios of masses measured at three different physical scales." (Distribution of gas mass from cloud to cores)
- Limitation (authors): Archetypical low-mass hot corinos (sizes of a few 100 au) remain undetected due to large beam dilution in the ~2000 au beam.  
  > "Archetypical hot corinos, such as IRAS 16293 or IRAS 4A with sizes of at most a few 100 au (cite: Jorgensen2016; belloche20), would themselves remain undetected in our survey because of the large beam dilution." (Hot cores and molecular complexity in high- and low-mass protostars)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core temperatures are simplified to a bimodal assumption (20 K for normal cores, 75 K for the 10 brightest cores with S > 400 mJy at 3.9 kpc), which could artificially distort the high-mass end of the core mass distribution and the cloud-mass vs. max-core-mass correlation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 12m-only recovered mass (M^recovered_1.3mm) depends on the uv-coverage and Largest Angular Scale (LAS), which varies by a factor of ~2.7-2.8 across the sample; absence of 7m and Total Power combination limits conclusions on intermediate-scale (0.1 pc) gas concentration.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the cleanest images of line-rich regions like W51 and W43, the effective line-free continuum bandwidth is small, degrading point-mass sensitivity at the low-mass end.

  > "The 12 m array `bsens` and `cleanest` continuum images at 1.3 mm and 3 mm are provided to the community, along with the present paper and Paper II." (ALMA-IMF data set); "The code is available on the github repository, <https://github.com/ALMA-IMF/reduction>, and ongoing work and data release updates can be found there and on the ALMA-IMF website (<https://almaimf.com>)." (ALMA-IMF data set)
- Products in archive: Raw data in ALMA archive (reprocessed by JAO in November 2020 to fix spectral normalization)  
  > "The ALMA-IMF data were indeed affected by a systematic error in the spectral data normalization and returned to the Joint ALMA Observatory (JAO) for further processing in November 2020." (ALMA-IMF data set); "This paper makes use of the following ALMA data: ADS/JAO.ALMA#2017.1.01355.L, #2013.1.01365.S, and #2015.1.01273.S." (Acknowledgements)
- Code: ALMA-IMF data reduction and analysis pipeline released at https://github.com/ALMA-IMF/reduction  
  > "The ALMA-IMF data processing pipelines and analysis are made public as described in (cite: ginsburg22). The code is available on the github repository, <https://github.com/ALMA-IMF/reduction>" (ALMA-IMF data set)
- Missing: Detailed imaging, self-calibration, and line-free channel selection parameters (residing in Paper II, Ginsburg et al. 2022, and the GitHub repository)
- Missing: Detailed getsf source extraction parameters, individual core flux catalogs, and free-free source rejection criteria (residing in Paper III, Louvet et al. 2022)
- Missing: Exact velocity integration ranges for the H41alpha line in each of the 15 clouds and the spatial masks used to exclude free-free dominated areas from 1.3 mm recovered flux measurements
- Data volume (inferred): Multi-TB (~10-50 TB for raw visibilities and full 12-SPW spectral cubes across 15 large mosaics in 12m, 7m, and TP arrays; a few GB for the released 12m continuum mosaics)
- Score (inferred): 75/5, Publicly released ALMA data, public reduction pipeline on GitHub, public continuum FITS products, and explicit analytic equations for mass and free-free calculations; however, this overview paper delegates the exact imaging parameters to Paper II and the core extraction catalog/parameters to Paper III.
- Rediscovery candidate: yes, All raw data and reduced continuum mosaics are public, the reduction pipeline is on GitHub, and key quantitative results (1.3mm/3mm flux ratios, H41alpha free-free surface densities, evolutionary classifications, and cloud-to-core mass concentration ratios) are explicitly tabulated and well-suited for automated reproduction.
