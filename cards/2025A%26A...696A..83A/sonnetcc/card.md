## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the ALMA data**: Pipeline calibration; TCLEAN cubes and continuum deconvolved to 2 sigma, matched circular 0.18 arcsec beam with Briggs weighting  
  > "Continuum and cube images were produced by the `TCLEAN` task and deconvolved down to ${2} \sigma$ noise level, initially measured from the dirty map." (2.1 ALMA); "For visibility calibration, we used the pipeline script provided by the ALMA Regional Center staff." (2.1 ALMA)
   - software: CASA 6.1.1.15, tclean
   - parameter: Matched circular beam 0.18 arcsec x 0.18 arcsec  
  > "a $\rm {630}~{\mu m}$ dust continuum map with a matched, circular beam of $0.18\ensuremath{''} \times 0.18\ensuremath{''}$ using Briggs weighting." (2.1 ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Make integrated intensity maps and measure the continuum flux**: bettermoments integrated intensity maps of CO(4-3) and [CI](1-0); imfit 2D Gaussian for the continuum flux  
  > "we used the PYTHON package `BETTERMOMENTS`" (2.1 ALMA); "Using the 2D-Gaussian fitting task `IMFIT`, the integrated flux of the dust continuum emission was found to be $0.18 \pm 0.01 ~ {\rm mJy}$." (2.1 ALMA)
   - software: bettermoments, imfit, CASA
   - parameter: Mask from 2 sigma clipping of the 2 x FWHM convolved spatial data  
  > "applying a mask based on a ${2} \sigma$ clipping of the $\rm 2 \times FWHM$ convolved spatial data." (2.1 ALMA)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit (imfit) to the continuum  
  > "Using the 2D-Gaussian fitting task `IMFIT`, the integrated flux of the dust continuum emission was found to be $0.18 \pm 0.01 ~ {\rm mJy}$." (2.1 ALMA)
   - validation: Continuum flux compared with earlier ALMA Band 4 data (agree within errors) and with PdBI (factor of about 2 lower)  
  > "this flux value is consistent with previous ALMA Band 4 observations (#2013.1.00059S, #2017.1.00856.S, #2017.1.01045.S) within the errors" (2.1 ALMA)
3. **Stack [CI](1-0) spectra in radial bins**: Shift each [CI] spaxel by the CO(4-3) velocity field, average in galactocentric radial bins, fit a single Gaussian  
  > "Next, each $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ spaxel is shifted according to the $\rm CO~({4} - {3})$ velocity field, to align the spectrum of each line of sight to zero velocity." (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra); "To extract the line intensity, we fit a single-Gaussian profile to the averaged-stacked spectra." (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra)
   - parameter: Radial bin width 0.09 arcsec (half the beam)  
  > "with a bin width of $0.09\ensuremath{''}$—half the beamsize of the data." (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra)
   - parameter: Only stacked spectra above 5 sigma are used  
  > "we only consider $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ averaged-stacked spectra that exceed the $5 \sigma$ level." (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra)
   - fitting: Single-Gaussian fit; uncertainty u = sigma sqrt(N) delta_v  
  > "where $\sigma$ is the RMS of the emission-free portion of the stacked spectrum, $N$ is the number of channels included in the mask, and $\Delta v$ is the channel width in $\rm km~s^{-1}$." (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra)
   - validation: Without stacking [CI] is detected to 0.7 R_eff; stacking extends detection beyond one R_eff  
  > "Without stacking, the $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ line is initially detected up to 0.7 times one effective stellar radius." (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra)
4. **Derive molecular gas masses from CO(4-3) and [CI](1-0)**: Line luminosities after Solomon+05; M_mol = alpha_CO R14 L'_CO(4-3); [CI] mass after Weiss+05 and an assumed X[CI]/X[H2]  
  > "Molecular gas masses are computed from the $\rm CO~({4} - {3})$ and $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ line luminosities, which are defined by (cite: Solomon+05) as," (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass); "The atomic carbon mass is estimated from the $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ line luminosity emission following the formula presented by (cite: Weiss+05)," (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 4.51 +/- 0.60 from 12+log(O/H) = 8.65 +/- 0.07  
  > "which yields an $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 4.51 \pm 0.60 ~ {\rm M_{\odot}~(K~km~s^{-1}~pc^{2})^{-1}}$." (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass)
   - parameter: R14 = 1.1 +/- 0.1 from the JVLA CO(1-0) luminosity of Bolatto+15  
  > "which yields a line luminosity ratio of $R_{14} = 1.1 \pm 0.1$." (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass)
   - assumption: alpha_CO includes a 36% contribution from helium  
  > "where $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ is the luminosity-to-molecular-gas-mass conversion factor, including a 36% contribution from helium" (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass)
   - assumption: [CI] excitation temperature 31.8 K (Brisbin+19); X[CI]/X[H2] = 1.5e-5 (Valentino+18 weighted mean)  
  > "Assuming this mean abundance, we measure a molecular hydrogen mass of" (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF; LCDM with H0 = 70, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "Throughout this paper, we assume a Chabrier initial mass function (IMF; (cite: Chabrier+03)) and a $\rm {\Lambda}CDM$ cosmology with $H_{0} = 70 ~ {\rm km~s^{-1}~Mpc^{-1}}$, $\Omega_{\rm M} = 0.3$, and $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.7$." (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CO-based and [CI]-based masses agree (1.2e11 vs 1.3e11 Msun) and agree with Bolatto+15; about a factor of 2 below Brisbin+19  
  > "which results in a molecular gas mass of $M_{\rm mol} = (1.3 \pm 0.1) \times 10^{11} ~ {\rm M_{\odot}}$ (i.e., consistent with our CO-based measurement)." (3.2 Molecular Gas Mass)
5. **Measure star formation rates (Halpha and dust continuum)**: Extinction-corrected Halpha SFR (Kennicutt+98 calibration for Chabrier IMF); central regions use the ALMA continuum scaled to rest-frame 70 um and the Calzetti+10 calibration  
  > "For this reason, in the central region of BX610, we use the ALMA dust continuum to measure the obscured star formation." (3.3 Star Formation Rate); "The resulting flux is $S_{\rm cont, \,70 ~ {\mu}m} = 13.2 ~ {\rm mJy}$." (3.3 Star Formation Rate)
   - parameter: Modified blackbody with beta = 2 and T_dust = 30 K  
  > "with a spectral emissivity index $\beta = 2$ and a dust temperature $T_{\rm dust} = 30 ~ {\rm K}$, as determined by (cite: Brisbin+19)" (3.3 Star Formation Rate)
   - assumption: E(B-V)_star/E(B-V)_gas = 0.44  
  > "We assume a $E(B-V)_{\rm star} / E(B-V)_{\rm gas}$ ratio of 0.44 (cite: Calzetti+00)." (3.3 Star Formation Rate)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Sensitivity of dust-based SFR to T_dust (x3.8 at 40 K) and beta (/3.0 at 1.5); obscured fraction 0.84 consistent with other z~2 galaxies  
  > "If we assume a dust temperature of $T_{\rm dust} = 40 ~ {\rm K}$, the resulting SFR increases by a factor of 3.8." (3.3 Star Formation Rate)
6. **Build radial profiles of line ratio, gas, SFR, gas fraction and depletion time**: Radial profiles from summed CO(4-3) luminosities and stacked [CI](1-0) luminosities, clump masked and shown separately  
  > "At each radius, the profile is calculated by taking the ratio of the summed $\rm CO~({4} - {3})$ line luminosities to the stacked $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ line luminosities." (4.1 Molecular Gas Excitation); "The emission from the star-forming clump is masked (based on the area marked by the red dashed aperture on the left panel) and shown separately (in red)." (4.1 Molecular Gas Excitation)
   - assumption: CO-based Sigma_mol profile used because CO extends farther than [CI]  
  > "We choose the CO-based over the \[CI\]-based profile because CO emission extends farther across the disk" (4.2 Stellar, Molecular, and Star Formation Rate Radial Profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Profiles compared with literature ratios for local (U)LIRGs, SMGs and z~1 main-sequence galaxies (Papadopoulos+12; Valentino+20)  
  > "In the central region $(\lesssim 0.5 ~ {R_{\rm eff, \,\star}})$ of BX610, the line luminosity ratio is comparable to that found in local (U)LIRGs" (4.1 Molecular Gas Excitation)
7. **Fit the resolved Kennicutt-Schmidt relation**: Beam-sized apertures spaced by half a beam; SFR from Halpha (disk, clump) or ALMA continuum (center); ordinary least-squares bisector fit to log Sigma_SFR vs log Sigma_mol  
  > "each with a diameter of $0.18\ensuremath{''}$ (matching the beam size) and centers separated by $0.09\ensuremath{''}$ to minimize flux loss from overlapping apertures." (4.3 Kennicutt-Schmidt Law); "Applying an ordinary least-squares bisector fit to all the regions in BX610, we find that $\log{(\Sigma_{\rm SFR})} = (1.27 \pm 0.05) \times \log{(\Sigma_{\rm mol})} - (3.68 \pm 0.17)$." (4.3 Kennicutt-Schmidt Law)
   - parameter: Aperture diameter 0.18 arcsec, center spacing 0.09 arcsec  
  > "each with a diameter of $0.18\ensuremath{''}$ (matching the beam size) and centers separated by $0.09\ensuremath{''}$ to minimize flux loss from overlapping apertures." (4.3 Kennicutt-Schmidt Law)
   - fitting: Ordinary least-squares bisector; priors/likelihood not applicable  
  > "Applying an ordinary least-squares bisector fit to all the regions in BX610" (4.3 Kennicutt-Schmidt Law)
   - validation: Slope compared with EGS13011166 (Genzel+13, N = 1.14 +/- 0.1)  
  > "which is comparable, within uncertainties and assumptions, to the measured slope in BX610 of $N = 1.27 \pm 0.1$." (4.3 Kennicutt-Schmidt Law)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Typical z~2 main-sequence galaxies are poorly studied at kpc scales; deep ALMA data on BX610 fill this gap  
  > "Despite significant progress, the molecular gas properties of more typical star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon on $\sim$kiloparsec scales remain relatively understudied." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: [CI] S/N falls faster than CO with radius, so spectra are stacked to extend detection  
  > "To enhance the detection of the $\rm [CI]~({1} - {0})$ line, we employ a spectral stacking method" (3.1 Stacking the [CI] Spectra)
- Alternative: CO-based Sigma_mol profile chosen over [CI]-based because CO extends farther  
  > "We choose the CO-based over the \[CI\]-based profile because CO emission extends farther across the disk" (4.2 Stellar, Molecular, and Star Formation Rate Radial Profiles)
- Alternative: In the dusty center the Halpha extinction-corrected SFR is replaced by the dust-continuum SFR because the Halpha correction likely underestimates star formation  
  > "a correction based on the attenuation $A_{\rm H{\alpha}}$ measured from HST imaging is likely to underestimate the total star formation activity significantly." (3.3 Star Formation Rate)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dust-continuum SFR is far from the IR SED peak, giving significant uncertainty; Band 8/9 or JWST Paschen-alpha needed  
  > "the infrared-based SFR here is derived from dust continuum observations at a wavelength away from the peak of the infrared SED, introducing significant uncertainties." (4.2 Stellar, Molecular, and Star Formation Rate Radial Profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Dust-based SFR depends strongly on T_dust and beta  
  > "Nevertheless, the dust continuum-based method has its own limitations." (3.3 Star Formation Rate)
- Limitation (authors): SED-derived stellar mass in the dusty center may be underestimated  
  > "in the dusty central region of BX610, the SED-derived stellar mass may be underestimated." (4.2 Stellar, Molecular, and Star Formation Rate Radial Profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of reliable obscured star formation measurements in the center limits interpretation  
  > "the absence of reliable measurements of obscured star formation in the central region limits our ability to interpret its physical conditions fully." (5 Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single galaxy, so conclusions about wet compaction and typical main-sequence behavior rest on one object
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas mass uses a single global R14 from low-resolution JVLA CO(1-0) and alpha_CO from a global metallicity relation, applied to resolved regions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Central SFR rests on a single-band 70 um extrapolation with T_dust = 30 K and beta = 2 and swings by factors of 3-4 under small changes
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mixed SFR tracers (Halpha in disk/clump, dust in center) feed one KS fit, so the slope depends on tracer switching
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stacking [CI] along the CO velocity field assumes the two lines share kinematics
