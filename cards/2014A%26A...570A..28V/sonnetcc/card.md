## Analysis recipe

1. **Build matched-resolution line intensity ratios**: Integrate maps over a common velocity window, degrade to common resolution, clip at 3 sigma, extract single-aperture intensities and form ratios  
  > "Intensities were calculated by integrating inside a common 460 km s$^{-1}$ velocity window (-230, 230 km s$^{-1}$ relative to the systemic velocity)." (2.1 Methodology); "all the maps have been first referred to the same tracking center and then degraded to the lower/common spatial resolution of the data used to derive each line ratio" (2.1 Methodology)
   - parameter: Intensity uncertainties 15% (Band 7), 30% (Band 9), 10% (PdBI)  
  > "which we take to be 15% for Band 7 data, 30% for Band 9 data, and 10% for the PdBI data" (2.2 The ALMA 'topology' of NGC1068)
   - assumption: Distance 14 Mpc  
  > "or 20–35 pc at the assumed distance of 14 Mpc" (2.2 The ALMA 'topology' of NGC1068)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux lost to missing short spacings estimated to matter only above 6 arcsec; single apertures of ~0.5 to ~5 arcsec used  
  > "we estimate that the amount of flux filtered in the interferometric images used in this work starts to be significant only on scales" (2.1 Methodology)
2. **LTE column densities per transition and region**: Boltzmann relation between upper-level and total column density for a grid of kinetic temperatures, assuming optically thin emission  
  > "We estimate the column densities of each species in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)." (3.1 LTE analysis); "If optically thin, and assuming a filling factor of unity" (3.1 LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Tk 50 to 250 K reported (calculated 10 to 300 K); 150 K adopted for the ALMA ratio comparison  
  > "(from 50 to 250 K, although the LTE calculations were performed from 10 to 300 K)" (3.1 LTE analysis); "Therefore taking an intermediate kinetic temperature of 150 K" (3.1 LTE analysis)
   - assumption: All transitions optically thin, so column densities are lower limits  
  > "Note that we have made the implicit assumption that all our transitions are optically thin." (3.1 LTE analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of column densities from different transitions of the same species shows inconsistencies  
  > "then the same total column density at a specific temperature should be obtainable from each transition. This is clearly not the case" (3.1 LTE analysis)
3. **CO rotation diagram per CND region**: Plot ln(Nu/gu) versus Eu/k for four CO transitions and fit a line to get rotational temperature and CO column density  
  > "The rotation diagram is a plot of the natural logarithm of $N_u/g_u$ versus $E_u/k$" (3.1.1 CO rotation diagram)
   - parameter: Level column density errors 10% (first two), 15% (J=3-2), 30% (highest J)  
  > "the error on the level column densities are 10% for the first two transitions, 15% for the $J$=3–2 and 30% for the highest $J$" (3.1.1 CO rotation diagram)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin lines, Rayleigh-Jeans regime  
  > "making the assumptions that LTE conditions hold, that the lines are optically thin, and that we are mostly in the Rayleigh-Jeans regime" (3.1.1 CO rotation diagram)
   - fitting: Single-component line fit; two-component fit also tried for AGN and CND-S  
  > "the AGN and CND-S regions may be better represented by a two component fit (not shown)" (3.1.1 CO rotation diagram)
   - validation: Rotational temperatures treated as lower limits to kinetic temperature  
  > "The rotational temperatures derived from the rotation diagrams ought to be considered as lower limits of the excitation and kinetic temperatures (cite: Gold99)." (3.1.1 CO rotation diagram)
4. **RADEX grid fit to CO ratios only**: Grid of uniform-sphere RADEX models fit to independent CO and 12CO/13CO ratios by reduced chi2-like minimisation function in log ratios  
  > "we therefore present the results from uniform sphere models" (3.2.1 CO fitting); "the number of degrees of freedom is taken to be equal to $N - n$ where $N$ is the number of intensity ratios, and $n$ is the number of fitted parameters" (3.2.1 CO fitting)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: n(H2) = 10^3-10^7 cm^-3  
  > "Gas density: n(H$_2$) = 10$^3$–10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$" (3.2.1 CO fitting)
   - parameter: Tkin = 10-500 K  
  > "Kinetic temperature: T$_{kin}$ = 10–500 K" (3.2.1 CO fitting)
   - parameter: over 14,000 models  
  > "for a total of over 14,000 models" (3.2.1 CO fitting)
   - parameter: Intrinsic 12C/13C = 40; background temperature 2.7 K  
  > "As an intrinsic $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio we used 40" (3.2.1 CO fitting); "We have used a background temperature of 2.7 K" (3.2 RADEX analysis of the CND)
   - assumption: All transitions sample the same gas; same 12CO/13CO ratio assumed for every region  
  > "Of course such modelling is limited by the assumption that all the molecular transitions sample the same gas." (3.2 RADEX analysis of the CND)
   - fitting: Grid search minimising a reduced chi2-like function of log ratios; no sampler  
  > "our reduced $\chi^2$ is in fact strictly speaking not a standard $\chi^2$ but a minimization function which we define as" (3.2.1 CO fitting)
   - validation: Varied 12C/13C for a subset of the grid without substantially changing conclusions  
  > "We did vary this ratio for a subset of our RADEX grid and did not find large enough differences to substantially change our conclusions." (3.2.1 CO fitting)
5. **RADEX grid with multi-species ratios**: Vary density and temperature for seven fixed sets of CO, HCO+, HCN and CS column densities per line width and fit all independent ratios  
  > "We have varied the gas density and temperature as in our first RADEX grid in Section 3.2.1 for several molecular column density ratios" (3.2.2 A RADEX grid computed with multi-species ratios)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: over 10000 models  
  > "(resulting in a grid of over 10000 models)" (3.2.2 A RADEX grid computed with multi-species ratios)
   - parameter: H12CN/H13CN = 18 and H12CO+/H13CO+ = 24 for all regions  
  > "ratios of, respectively, 18 and 24 (cite: Papa99; User04) for each region within the CND" (3.2.2 A RADEX grid computed with multi-species ratios)
   - assumption: Column-density sets guided by LTE column densities; sets are not exhaustive  
  > "they were guided by the range of column densities derived in LTE" (3.2.2 A RADEX grid computed with multi-species ratios)
   - fitting: Log of reduced chi2 minimised over the grid  
  > "for this RADEX grid we shall report results from the log of the reduced $\chi^2$ fitting" (3.2.2 A RADEX grid computed with multi-species ratios)
   - validation: Density lower limit of 10^4 cm^-3 from the CO analysis used to discard sets  
  > "we therefore use this lower limit as a further constraint and we are left with Sets 1 and 7" (3.2.2 A RADEX grid computed with multi-species ratios)
6. **RADEX using ALMA-resolution data only**: Repeat multi-species RADEX fit with only ALMA ratios (plus 12CO/13CO for column density) at the highest resolution  
  > "For this grid of models we used only ALMA data, apart from the intensities for the isotopic ratios." (3.2.3 RADEX analysis of ALMA data only)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Ratios: CO(6-5/3-2), HCN(4-3)/HCO+(4-3), HCN(4-3)/CO(3-2), CS(7-6)/CO(3-2), 12CO(3-2)/13CO(3-2)  
  > "CO(6-5/3-2); HCN(4-3)/HCO$^+$(4-3), HCN(4-3)/CO(3-2), CS(7-6)/CO(3-2), $^{12}$CO(3-2)/$^{13}$CO(3-2)" (3.2.3 RADEX analysis of ALMA data only)
   - assumption: Smaller emission region more likely to have similar physical conditions  
  > "by reducing the size of the emission region it is more likely that the emission may arise from gas with similar physical conditions" (3.2.3 RADEX analysis of ALMA data only)
   - fitting: Reduced log chi2 over the same set grid  
  > "In general we find that this grid gives a better reduced log $\chi^2$." (3.2.3 RADEX analysis of ALMA data only)
   - validation: CND-S could not be analysed because CS(7-6) was too weak at its original resolution  
  > "For the CND-S we could not perform this analysis as the CS(7-6) line intensity was too weak at its original spatial resolution." (3.2.3 RADEX analysis of ALMA data only)
7. **RADEX analysis of the SB ring knot**: Same sets applied to the ratios available for the SB ring  
  > "For the starburst ring only the following observations are available: CO (3-2), CO (1-0), HCO$^+$ (4-3), HCO$^+$ (1-0), HCN (4-3), and HCN (1-0)." (3.3 RADEX analysis of the SB ring)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Temperatures below ~30 K excluded because the SB ring is a site of high star formation  
  > "once we exclude temperatures below $\sim$ 30 K (being the SB ring a site of high star formation activity)" (3.3 RADEX analysis of the SB ring)
   - assumption: Isotopic ratios as in the CND analysis (Papa99; User04)  
  > "The isotopic ratios are again from (cite: Papa99; User04)." (3.3 RADEX analysis of the SB ring)
   - fitting: Best fit Sets 6 and 7  
  > "find that the best fits are for Sets 6" (3.3 RADEX analysis of the SB ring)
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Chemical modelling and comparison with RADEX column density ratios**: Run 27 UCL_CHEM gas-grain models (two phases, optional shock) and compare theoretical column density ratios with RADEX-derived ratios at the last time step of Phase II  
  > "we use the UCL_CHEM model (cite: Viti04; Viti11) to determine the best physical characteristics of each individual component within the CND" (4 Chemical modelling); "all the analysis and comparisons of the abundances from the chemical models are performed at the last time step of Phase II" (4 Chemical modelling)
   - software: UCL_CHEM
   - parameter: Phase II temperature 100 K and 200 K  
  > "(ii) the temperature (100 K and 200 K) during Phase II" (4 Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: Radiation field 1, 10 and 500 Draine  
  > "we picked three representative values of a low (1 Draine), medium (10 Draine) and high (500 Draine) radiation field" (4 Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: Shock velocity 40 km/s  
  > "we adopted a representative shock velocity of 40 km s$^{-1}$" (4 Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: Visual extinctions 2 and 10 mag  
  > "two representative visual extinctions of 2 and 10 mags" (4 Chemical modelling)
   - assumption: Solar-metallicity elemental abundances; cosmic-ray rate used to mimic enhanced X-rays; free-fall collapse in Phase I  
  > "We have kept fixed the initial elemental abundances at the standard solar metallicity ISM abundances compared to the total number of hydrogen nuclei" (4 Chemical modelling); "For simplicity we adopt free-fall collapse." (4 Chemical modelling)
   - fitting: Qualitative matching of model and observed log column density ratios; no formal fit  
  > "This attempt at chemical modelling was preliminary" (4 Chemical modelling)
   - validation: RADEX density limit (>10^4 cm^-3) used to exclude models; ratios compared only at one time step  
  > "we shall exclude *a priori* all models with a gas density of 10$^4$ cm$^{-3}$ to start with" (4 Chemical modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- Chemical models suggest a three-phase picture per CND region: a 10^5 cm^-3 phase, a >=10^6 cm^-3 phase, both with cosmic-ray rate enhanced by at least a factor 10, plus shocked gas  
  > "a simplified picture with three dominant gas phases emerges for all regions" (4 Chemical modelling)
- The HCN/CS ratio in best-fit chemical models seems to require shocks if HCN and CS are co-spatial  
  > "the HCN/CS ratio, for most best fit models, seems to require the presence of shocks" (4 Chemical modelling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Highest-resolution multi-line, multi-species chemical study of NGC 1068, resolving sub-regions within the CND  
  > "This is the highest resolution multi-line/multi-species chemical study of this object to date" (1.1 Summary of Paper I and aims of this paper)
- Why this method: Combine LTE, RADEX and chemical modelling to separate excitation from chemistry  
  > "Disentangling excitation from chemistry effects is crucial if we are to interpret the observed differences between the SB and the CND" (2.2 The ALMA 'topology' of NGC1068)
- Alternative: All three RADEX escape probability geometries (uniform sphere, LVG, slab) tried; no significant difference, uniform sphere reported  
  > "We used all three methods but, as (cite: Krip11), we did not find significant differences for the best fits" (3.2 RADEX analysis of the CND)
- Alternative: Time spent at constant density in Phase I (S versus L models) not discussed further because solutions were very similar  
  > "since models differing only in this parameter gave very similar solutions we shall not discuss this parameter any further" (4 Chemical modelling)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): RADEX assumes all transitions sample the same gas, and several ratios use degraded or low-resolution data  
  > "Of course such modelling is limited by the assumption that all the molecular transitions sample the same gas." (3.2 RADEX analysis of the CND)
- Limitation (authors): Chemical modelling is preliminary and compares time-dependent models only at one time step  
  > "This attempt at chemical modelling was preliminary" (4 Chemical modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Infrared pumping not considered  
  > "this analysis did not take into consideration infrared pumping" (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): No short-spacing correction in the maps  
  > "None of the maps used to derive the line ratios include short-spacing correction." (2.1 Methodology)
