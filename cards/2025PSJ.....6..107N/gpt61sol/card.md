## Analysis recipe

1. **Reconstruct spectral cubes**: Reconstruct the sky model with tclean after pipeline calibration and additional bandpass smoothing.  
  > "Additional bandpass calibration smoothing was performed, to improve the spectral noise per channel, and the tclean procedure was used to reconstruct the sky model." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract disk-averaged spectra**: Extract spectra from processed cubes using a circular aperture.  
  > "From the processed data cubes, disk-averaged spectra were extracted using a circular aperture with a radius of 1.8$''$" (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - parameter: Aperture radius 1.8''  
  > "a circular aperture with a radius of 1.8$''$" (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - assumption: Aperture includes the atmosphere plus half a beam to account for all emission.  
  > "which includes Titan up to the top of its atmosphere plus half a beam to account for all emission." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Prepare consistent spectroscopy**: Update NEMESIS to GEISA 211 format; recompute line intensities and partition functions consistently; fit tabulated partition functions with a third order polynomial.  
  > "The NEMESIS line database was updated to the GEISA 211 format (cite: geisa_2003) for additional precision on the rest frequencies and intensities." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "all of the values were re-computed without hyperfine or vibrational corrections in a self-consistent manner." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "The partition functions for all of the isotopologues were tabulated for inclusion in NEMESIS using a third order polynomial fit to the re-computed values" (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - software: NEMESIS, CDMS
   - parameter: Partition functions tabulated at 300.0, 225.0, 150.0, 75.00, 37.50, 18.75 and 9.375 K  
  > "| 300.0 | 10118.2635  |     10418.8811     |     10123.2613     |     10431.9556     |" (Table 2); "the re-computed values listed in Table [2](#tab:partition_fcn_vals)." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - parameter: Laboratory checks: K=0 to 9, J=14-13 at 257 GHz and J=15-14 at 275 GHz; room temperature, around 1 mTorr  
  > "by measuring $K$=0 to 9 transitions for $J$=14-13 at 257 GHz and $J$=15-14 at 275 GHz." (CH$_3$CN spectroscopic parameters and partition functions); "Measurements were made at room temperature, and at around 1 mTorr sample pressure" (CH$_3$CN spectroscopic parameters and partition functions)
   - assumption: Exclude v8=1 and nitrogen hyperfine structure from the parent partition function, matching the isotopic species.  
  > "we re-evaluated the partition function for the ground state of the parent at the same conditions as for the isotopic species, namely without the $v_8$=1 state, and without accounting for the nitrogen hyperfine structure" (CH$_3$CN spectroscopic parameters and partition functions)
   - assumption: Relevant hyperfine splittings are much smaller than the observed spectral resolution.  
  > "Note that the hyperfine splittings for all relevant transitions are much smaller than the resolution of our observed spectra." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - fitting: Third order polynomial fit to recomputed partition functions  
  > "using a third order polynomial fit to the re-computed values" (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - validation: Twenty laboratory transitions agree with CDMS predictions to an RMS deviation of 16 kHz.  
  > "CDMS predictions and currently measured frequencies for 20 different transitions were in agreement to a root mean square deviation of 16 kHz, which is well within the nominal uncertainty of the employed spectrometer." (CH$_3$CN spectroscopic parameters and partition functions)
4. **Construct the spectral response**: Convolve a wind-broadened profile with the Fourier transform of a padded Hann window.  
  > "We convolved the rotationally broadened profile with a line-shape function that mimics the ALMA correlator response, which is the Fourier transform of a (padded) Hann window, to get the new line spread function (cite: spec_resp_memo)." (Line Shape Function)
   - software: NEMESIS, modified code published in (cite: cordiner_2020)
   - parameter: Padding corresponds to two channels  
  > "we set this to correspond to two channels, according to the standard setup of the ALMA correlator." (Line Shape Function)
   - parameter: 488 and 976 kHz response functions; wind-broadened FWHM 604 and 1109 m s^-1  
  > "After the addition of wind broadening, the 488 kHz velocity FWHM increased to 604 m s$^{-1}$ (a difference of 36 m s$^{-1}$)" (Line Shape Function); "the 976 kHz velocity FWHM increased to 1109 m s$^{-1}$ (a difference of 19 m s$^{-1}$)" (Line Shape Function)
   - assumption: Use the observed May 2017 wind field  
  > "based on the observed wind field in May 2017." (Line Shape Function)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare FWHM before and after wind broadening; the LSF had a slight impact.  
  > "so the line shape function (LSF) had a slight impact on the results." (Line Shape Function); "corresponding to a velocity FWHM of 568 and 1090 m s$^{-1}$ at 257.4461 and 267.5854 GHz, respectively." (Line Shape Function)
5. **Build the atmospheric forward model**: Generate line-by-line NEMESIS spectra with disk annuli, atmospheric profiles, continuum opacity and thermal/pressure broadening.  
  > "Spectral models were generated using the line-by-line module of the Nonlinear optimal Estimator for MultivariatE spectral analySIS (NEMESIS) radiative transfer and retrieval tool (cite: irwin_2008)." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "NEMESIS includes the necessary physical parameters such as spontaneous emission and absorption of radiation, on and off-limb emission angles, continuum opacity and thermal and pressure broadening" (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - software: NEMESIS, Borysow CIA routines

## Qualitative findings

- CH3CN is strongly enriched in 15N relative to atmospheric N2.  
  > "We therefore show for the first time that $^{15}$N is strongly enhanced in CH$_3$CN compared to Titan’s main atmospheric nitrogen reservoir (N$_2$)." (Conclusions)
- Measured Titan nitriles have consistent 15N enrichment, suggesting a common fractionated nitrogen reservoir.  
  > "We find a consistent level of $^{15}$N enrichment within all Titan’s nitriles measured to-date, which implies they likely formed from a common, isotopically fractionated reservoir of atmospheric nitrogen." (Conclusions)
- Carbon isotope ratios agree with previous Titan and Solar System values.  
  > "our $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratios are in good agreement with the values previously derived for Titan and various other Solar System bodies" (Discussion)
- The ~250 km abundance feature is needed to fit cores and wings, but its physical origin and latitude are uncertain.  
  > "It therefore cannot be determined where exactly (latitudinally) in Titan’s atmosphere this peak originates." (Results); "this feature is found to be necessary to obtain a good fit to the observed spectra — otherwise, the line core and wing strengths cannot be simultaneously reproduced." (Results)
- Current data provide no strong evidence for nitrogen isotope variation with altitude.  
  > "Since these errors are so large, there is no strong evidence for variability in the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio with altitude, based on our ALMA data." (Continuous $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N Profile Retrieval)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Unresolved Titan maximizes sensitivity per beam for disk-averaged atmosphere measurements.  
  > "was not resolved, enabling the maximum sensitivity per beam for disk-averaged studies of Titan’s entire Earth-facing atmosphere." (Observations)
- Why this method: Scale the major-isotopologue profile because minor-isotopologue spectra contain less vertical information; replace inconsistent spectroscopic parameters.  
  > "The minor CH$_3$CN isotopologue spectra are noisier and contain less vertical information than the major isotopologue" (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "found discrepancies in the line intensities and partition function coefficients for the major isotopologue and $^{13}$C minor isotopologues." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
- Alternative: Initial CDMS line intensities and partition functions were replaced after inconsistencies were found.  
  > "found discrepancies in the line intensities and partition function coefficients for the major isotopologue and $^{13}$C minor isotopologues." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "These values were not sufficiently accurate in this frequency range and therefore, all of the values were re-computed without hyperfine or vibrational corrections in a self-consistent manner." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
- Alternative: Continuous minor-isotopologue profile retrieval was attempted but did not usefully constrain vertical isotope variation.  
  > "we also performed a continuously-variable CH$_3$C$^{15}$N VMR fit." (Results); "the spectroscopic signal-to-noise ratio of 16 was found to be insufficient to provide useful constraints" (Results)
- Alternative: Scaled default, Dobrijevic–Loison and gradient a priori profiles were tested.  
  > "including (1) scaled versions of our default a priori, (2) the CH$_3$CN profile from (cite: dobri_loison_2018), and (3) a gradient profile from (cite: thelen_2019)." (Sensitivity of Retrievals to A Priori Assumptions)
- Alternative: Dobrijevic–Loison models with and without GCRs were compared; usefulness of the no-GCR comparison is uncertain.  
  > "our observed CH$_3$CN/CH$_3$C$^{15}$N ratio is a closer match with the (cite: dobri_loison_2018) model without GCRs included." (Discussion); "However, cosmic rays are known to play a significant role in the photochemistry and ionization, primarily of Titan’s lower stratosphere and troposphere (cite: nixon_2024), so it is unclear whether this is a useful comparison." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: Fits match continuum, cores and wings within noise; SNR 16 does not support altitude-dependent ratios.  
  > "Within the noise, our spectral models provide an excellent fit to the observed Titan continuum, line cores and line wings." (Results); "the spectroscopic signal-to-noise ratio of 16 was found to be insufficient to provide useful constraints on the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio as a function of altitude" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Disk-averaged spectra cannot locate the abundance peak in latitude.  
  > "It therefore cannot be determined where exactly (latitudinally) in Titan’s atmosphere this peak originates." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Physical origin of the relatively narrow ~250 km abundance feature is uncertain.  
  > "there may be some doubt as to the physical origin of this relatively narrow feature" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Minor-isotopologue SNR is insufficient to constrain altitude-dependent nitrogen isotope ratios.  
  > "the spectroscopic signal-to-noise ratio of 16 was found to be insufficient to provide useful constraints on the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio as a function of altitude" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Future altitude-dependent measurements require at least five times higher minor-isotopologue SNR.  
  > "Future observations at higher signal-to-noise (by at least a factor of 5 for the minor isotopologue) will be required to investigate variability in the nitrile $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratios as a function of altitude." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA flux-scale uncertainty may reach ≈10%; continuum scaling also absorbs tropopause-temperature errors.  
  > "errors on the ALMA flux scale (which may be as much as $\approx10$%, due to uncertainty in the amplitude calibrator flux)" (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "as well as accounting for small errors in the assumed (a priori) temperature around the tropopause, which determines the model continuum level." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Excluding the CH3CN vibrationally excited state underestimates its partition function by 7%.  
  > "Omission of the CH$_3$CN vibrationally excited state amounts to a 7% underestimation of the partition function." (CH$_3$CN spectroscopic parameters and partition functions)
- Limitation (authors): Photochemical models still require improvement in the treatment of cosmic rays and electrons.  
  > "there is still room for improvement, so the precise importance of these processes should be revisited in future modeling efforts." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uniform minor-isotopologue scaling makes the principal ratios conditional on a shared vertical profile shape.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A disk-averaged abundance feature can reflect unresolved spatial structure; it is not a uniquely localized vertical layer.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 2017 wind field is applied to 2019 observations without a stated uncertainty test for temporal changes.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Discussion and spectroscopy appendix give inconsistent ratio revisions: nitrogen ~18% versus 20%, and CH3CN/CH3(13)CN ~34% versus 27%.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Introduction and Discussion quote different second Gurwell HCN ratios: 72±9 versus 108±20.
