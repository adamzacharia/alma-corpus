## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks
- Question: Are the spiral arms in the IM Lup dust disk driven by gravitational instability (moving at the local Keplerian speed) or by a planet (rigid rotation at the companion's orbit), tested by measuring spiral motion over a seven-year ALMA baseline  
  > "A robust way to distinguish the two mechanisms is to characterize the motion of spirals." (1 Introduction); "resulting in a seven-year time baseline." (2.1 Observations)
- Target **IM Lup**: coordinates _not stated_; z/distance 158 pc  
  > "located at 158 pc (cite: gaia2022) from the Earth" (1 Introduction); distance from Gaia (cite: gaia2022)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Image each epoch to a common beam**: CLEAN imaging per epoch with tuned weighting, then re-convolution to a common 150 x 101 mas beam  
  > "The image was re-convolved with a Gaussian to obtain a common beam size of $150 \times 101$ mas." (Methods 2.1 2017 image); "Several weighting schemes were tested to reduce the noise level with the required beam size being kept." (Methods 2.1 2017 image)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: 2017: uvtaper 0.08 arcsec, super-uniform npixels=10; 2019 and 2024: robust 0  
  > "We found that the super-uniform weighting with ${\tt npixels=10}$ achieves this." (Methods 2.1 2017 image); "We made an image with a robust parameter of $0$" (Methods 2.1 2019 images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Normalise and deproject the images**: Align on the disk centre (elliptical Gaussian fit), deproject, and divide each epoch by the ratio of its azimuthally averaged radial profile to that of 2017; iterate the disk-centre offsets with the image-fitting analysis four times  
  > "At each radius, the observed images are divided by a ratio of their azimuthally averaged profile to that of the 2017 image." (Methods 2.2.1 Image normalization); "We iterate this procedure four times and check the convergence of the offset parameters." (Methods 2.2.1 Image normalization)
   - parameter: PA 144.5 deg, inclination 47.5 deg  
  > "the position angle of 144.5 deg and inclination angle of 47.5 deg (cite: ober21; teag21)" (Methods 2.2.1 Image normalization)
   - assumption: Different frequencies trace the same dust surface density structure, so differences between epochs are temporal  
  > "we assumed that the different frequencies trace the same dust surface density structure" (Supplementary Information, Frequency effect)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Normalised images resemble each other except for a small rotation of the pattern  
  > "The normalized images indeed resemble each other except for a small rotation of the pattern" (Methods 2.2.1 Image normalization)
3. **Fit parametric angular velocity profile to the images**: Rotate the deprojected DSHARP reference image (convolved to a 65 mas circular beam) with omega(r) = omega_0 (r/1")^-gamma + omega_f, re-convolve to 150 mas, and compare to the 2019a, 2019b and 2024 images with a Gaussian log-likelihood, with free offsets (dx, dy) per epoch  
  > "The deprojected reference image is rotated according to Equation ([\[eq:omega\]](#eq:omega)) and convolved with a Gaussian again to match the common beam size of 150 mas." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis); "we can simultaneously treat the effect of the alignment accuracy by taking the offset $(dx, dy)$ as free parameters" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Radial range 0.065-0.7 arcsec  
  > "We only sum up the radial range of $r=0\mbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}065-0\mbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}7$" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - parameter: 128 walkers, 5000 steps, first 3000 discarded as burn-in  
  > "We employ 128 walkers and 5000 steps, with the first 3000 steps discarded as burn-in." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - parameter: Priors: log10 omega_0 ~ U(-4.7, 5.3), log10 omega_f ~ U(-4.7, 5.3), gamma ~ U(-5, 5)  
  > "$\log_{10} \omega_0$ | $\mathcal{U}(-4.7, 5.3)$ | $-0.97^{+0.32}_{-0.39}$ | $-0.82$" (Table 3); "$\gamma$ | $\mathcal{U}(-5, 5)$ | $1.9^{+0.49}_{-0.44}$" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Parametric power-law-plus-floor form for omega(r)  
  > "This parametric form is an assumption, but can be fitted to both cases: GI- and/or planet-induced spirals." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - assumption: Self-gravity of the disk neglected for the Keplerian profile  
  > "Note that we neglect the self-gravity of the disk." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC, Gaussian log-likelihood summed over pixels and the three later epochs with sigma = standard deviation of the residual map vs reference; broad non-informative uniform priors  
  > "To sample the posterior distribution of the free parameters, we use a Python package `emcee` (cite: emcee) in practice." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis); "We adopt broad non-informative priors" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - validation: Compared fitted curve with local Keplerian and rigid-body expectations; agreement with the radius-by-radius approach  
  > "The motion of the spirals is successfully detected in both approaches, where they agree well with each other." (2.2 Dust rotation curve)
4. **Model-independent radius-by-radius pattern speed**: At each radius, minimise chi^2 over omega summed over all epoch pairs of the azimuthally shifted normalised images; 1 sigma from |chi^2 - min chi^2| = 1, logarithmic radial bins  
  > "At each $r$, we calculate $\chi^2$ for various $\omega$ and find an optimal $\omega$ that minimizes $\chi^2$." (Methods 2.2.3 Radius-by-radius analysis); "Here, the radial bin is taken logarithmically." (Methods 2.2.3 Radius-by-radius analysis)
   - parameter: sigma_{i,j} = sqrt(sigma_i^2 + sigma_j^2) with sigma_i the standard deviation of epoch i  
  > "where $\sigma_i$ is the standard deviation of epoch $i$" (Methods 2.2.3 Radius-by-radius analysis)
   - assumption: No assumption on the radial angular velocity profile; measures the pattern speed of the dominant m=2 spirals  
  > "Therefore, what we measure in our analysis is the pattern speed of the dominant $m=2$ spiral arms." (Methods 2.2.4 The quantity we measured)
   - fitting: chi^2 minimisation per radius  
  > "At each $r$, we calculate $\chi^2$ for various $\omega$ and find an optimal $\omega$ that minimizes $\chi^2$." (Methods 2.2.3 Radius-by-radius analysis)
   - validation: Uncertainty may be underestimated because of signal correlation with the intensity gradient across radii  
  > "it is difficult to accurately estimate the uncertainty of $\omega$ if there is a signal correlation with intensity gradient across radii" (Methods 2.2.3 Radius-by-radius analysis)
5. **Compare to GI (local Keplerian) and planet-induced (rigid) cases**: Kernel density estimate of the MCMC sample, normalised by its maximum, evaluated at the local-Keplerian parameters and at Keplerian rotation at 117 au; converted to sigma equivalents  
  > "We perform the kernel density estimate on the MCMC sample with `scipy.stats.gaussian_kde` (cite: scipy)" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis); "showing that the GI-induced scenario is significantly preferable compared to the planet-induced scenario" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - software: scipy
   - parameter: GI case omega_kep = 0.19 (r/1")^-1.5 deg/yr for 1.1 Msun; planet case 0.30 deg/yr  
  > "\omega_{\rm kep}(r) \simeq 0.19 \left( \frac{r}{1''} \right)^{-1.5}\ {\rm deg\ yr^{-1}}," (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis); "\omega_{\rm planet}(r) \simeq 0.30 \ {\rm deg\ yr^{-1}}," (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - assumption: Planet-induced spirals rotate rigidly at the Keplerian rate at 117 au  
  > "the Keplerian rotation at 117 au" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Test whether the frequency difference can mimic motion**: Empirical comparison of epoch pairs (2019a/2019b vs 2024 vs 2017), plus analytic optically thick (z tan i) and optically thin two-slab opacity-centroid limits  
  > "the pairs of 2019a & 2024 and 2019b & 2024 are similar to 2017 & 2024. This means that the frequency effect is minimal." (Supplementary Information, Frequency effect); "we consider a model with two homogeneous dust slabs behind and beyond a spiral, where dust size distributions are different" (Supplementary Information, Optically thin case)
   - parameter: nu_I = 240 GHz and nu_II = 330 GHz; z = 0.05 r for the thick case  
  > "$\nu_{\rm I}= 240$ GHz and $\nu_{\rm I\hspace{-1.2pt}I}= 330$ GHz" (Supplementary Information, Optically thin case); "set $z = 0.05r$ (cite: ueda24)" (Supplementary Information, Optically thick case)
   - assumption: DSHARP dust opacity model; dust size distribution a power law with maximum size a_max; slabs optically thin with same temperature; maximum effect for equal surface densities  
  > "If the two slabs are optically thin and have the same temperature, the centroid of the emission" (Supplementary Information, Optically thin case); "For any combination of the dust sizes, $\Delta X_{\rm emis}$ is maximized when $\Sigma_1 = \Sigma_2$" (Supplementary Information, Optically thin case)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Frequency effect concluded negligible; positional difference ~0.05 arcsec at 30 au is not explained  
  > "Therefore, we conclude that the apparent positional difference is not due to the frequency effect but to the temporal variation." (Supplementary Information, Optically thin case)

## Qualitative findings

- Spiral arms are detected in each epoch and the dust rotation curve matches local Keplerian rotation and differs from rigid-body rotation  
  > "In each epoch, spiral arms are robustly detected." (2.1 Observations); "The dust rotation curve exactly matches the local Keplerian rotation, while it is significantly different from the rigid body rotation." (2.2 Dust rotation curve)
- Winding spirals are transient, implying continuous formation and a gravitationally self-regulated disk with Q ~ O(1)  
  > "it is more reasonable to consider that the spirals are continuously formed and diminished" (3 Implications and Future Direction)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Disk already observed several times at high resolution with ALMA, giving a baseline long enough to measure spiral motion  
  > "this disk has been observed several times with relatively high spatial resolutions using ALMA, spanning a period large enough to investigate the motion of the spirals" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Spiral motion discriminates GI (local Keplerian) from planet-driven (rigid) spirals; the radius-by-radius approach needs no assumed profile  
  > "A robust way to distinguish the two mechanisms is to characterize the motion of spirals." (1 Introduction); "it is advantageous that no assumption of the angular velocity profile is needed" (2.2 Dust rotation curve)
- Alternative: Shadow-induced spirals excluded (no significant shadow in infrared image); planet-induced spirals disfavoured by rigid-rotation test; frequency effect rejected  
  > "In the infrared image, there is no significant shadow (cite: aven18), excluding the shadow scenario." (1 Introduction); "showing that the GI-induced scenario is significantly preferable compared to the planet-induced scenario" (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
- Alternative: Several weighting schemes were tested for the 2017 image to reduce noise at the required beam size  
  > "Several weighting schemes were tested to reduce the noise level with the required beam size being kept." (Methods 2.1 2017 image)
- How they decided it was enough: Disk-centre offsets iterated four times until the offset parameters converged  
  > "We iterate this procedure four times and check the convergence of the offset parameters." (Methods 2.2.1 Image normalization)
- Limitation (authors): Parametric omega(r) form is an assumption  
  > "This parametric form is an assumption, but can be fitted to both cases: GI- and/or planet-induced spirals." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Radius-by-radius uncertainties are hard to estimate and may be affected by limited resolution  
  > "Although this approach may be affected by the limited spatial resolution" (2.2 Dust rotation curve); "it is difficult to accurately estimate the uncertainty of $\omega$ if there is a signal correlation with intensity gradient across radii" (Methods 2.2.3 Radius-by-radius analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Limited resolution may bias toward low azimuthal modes; disk self-gravity neglected; no direct evidence of infall  
  > "Since the observations have a limited spatial resolution, we might be biased to the spirals with low azimuthal modes ($m$) due to smoothing out (cite: dipi14)." (Methods 2.2.4 The quantity we measured); "Note that we neglect the self-gravity of the disk." (Methods 2.2.2 Image-fitting analysis)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only three independent epochs and one reference image; the radius-by-radius errors are acknowledged to be unreliable
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Pattern speed of a possibly stochastic, winding spiral may differ from the local Keplerian even when measured via the chosen metric
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Epochs at different frequencies/uv coverage are normalised only through azimuthally averaged profiles
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Planet-induced case tested only for a single companion radius of 117 au
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sigma values from KDE of an MCMC sample (e.g. 15 sigma from 7e-50) are not a calibrated hypothesis test
