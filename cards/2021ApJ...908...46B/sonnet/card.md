## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image line cubes**: Pipeline calibration, uv continuum subtraction, tclean with briggs 0.5, convolve to common beam, convert to brightness temperature  
  > "we converted the intensity in our data from units of Jy beam$^{-1}$ to brightness temperature in K" (3.3 Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - software: CASA, tclean, imsmooth
   - parameter: channel widths 0.17 km/s (2-1), 0.12 km/s (3-2), 0.22 km/s (CN)  
  > "The channel widths of 0.17 km s^−1^ and 0.12 km s^−1^ were adopted to generate images of the CO isotopologue $J$ = 2–1 and 3–2 lines, respectively." (3.3 Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Stack molecular lines**: Noise-weighted mean of cubes of different transitions/molecules  
  > "The image cubes were combined by calculating the noise weighted mean of them." (4.1 Stacking Molecular-Line Data)
   - assumption: Different molecules follow same Keplerian rotation; scale-height effect small (<3%)  
  > "because different molecules in a disk are expected to follow the same Keplerian rotation" (4.1 Stacking Molecular-Line Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared masses from individual lines; 80% agree within 1 sigma  
  > "80% of the measurements from different line data agree on a level better than 1$\sigma$" (5.3 Measurements with Individual Lines)
3. **Measure dynamical stellar mass**: Maximise S/N of velocity-aligned stacked spectrum over M* and vsys with i, PA, position, distance fixed  
  > "Thus, there are only two unknown parameters, $M_{\star}$ and $v_{sys}$ in our analysis." (4.2 Measuring Stellar Mass)
   - parameter: initial R = 1 arcsec, iterate until disk radius (95% flux) changes <10%; reject peak S/N <4 sigma or width <3 channels  
  > "any velocity aligned stacked spectrum with the S/N at its peak below 4$\sigma$ or with its line width narrower than three times the channel width was excluded" (4.2 Measuring Stellar Mass)
   - assumption: Geometrically thin disk  
  > "we adopted the assumption of a geometrically thin disk structure." (4.2 Measuring Stellar Mass)
   - fitting: Grid search; Gaussian fit to aligned spectrum, S/N of Gaussian-weighted integrated intensity  
  > "we first fitted a Gaussian line profile to the velocity aligned stacked spectrum, and calculated the S/N of the integrated intensity within a 1$\sigma$ Gaussian line width." (4.2 Measuring Stellar Mass)
   - validation: Synthetic DALI cubes; 700 noise realisations  
  > "we generated 700 synthetic ALMA data cubes from the disk models and included different random noise during the imaging simulations." (4.4 Robustness and Potential Bias)
4. **Estimate uncertainties**: Test spectra from varied parameters (incl. i, PA within errors); chi2 of residual vs best spectrum, accept at 50% cumulative probability; exclude solutions with <2.5% probability  
  > "the accumulated probability having a $\chi^{2}$ smaller than the obtained value was 50%." (4.3 Error Estimation)
   - assumption: Gaussian noise  
  > "On the assumption that the noise distribution in our spectra follows a Gaussian function" (4.3 Error Estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Error bars represent 1 sigma per simulations  
  > "we found that our error estimate indeed represents the $1\sigma$ uncertainty of our measurements." (4.4 Robustness and Potential Bias)
5. **Compare with spectroscopic masses from seven evolutionary models**: Teff and L* placed on tracks; mean deviation in three mass bins; unresolved-binary candidates (>3 mad from median per Teff bin) excluded  
  > "If the dynamical mass of a star in our sample deviates from the median mass by more than three times the median absolute deviation (mad), we consider this source a possible unresolved binary system." (6.1 Unresolved Binaries)
   - parameter: 20% L uncertainty; 2.5% Teff (<3500 K), 4.5% (>3500 K); 68% interval  
  > "The typical uncertainty of 20% in luminosity and 2.5% for an effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}<3500$ K and 4.5% for $T_{\mathrm{eff}}>3500$ K was adopted in the calculations." (6.2 Comparison between Dynamical and Spectroscopic Mass Measurements)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeated excluding i>70 deg sources  
  > "We also compared our dynamical measurement with the mass estimate from the stellar evolutionary models by excluding those sources." (6.2 Comparison between Dynamical and Spectroscopic Mass Measurements)

## Qualitative findings

- Mass bias for synthetic disks: overestimate 20-30% at i=80 deg; underestimate <10% face-on  
  > "the stellar mass could be overestimated by 20% to 30% with our method, when a disk is highly inclined ($i = 80^{\circ}$)" (4.4 Robustness and Potential Bias)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Large shallow ALMA surveys give large samples with several lines and resolved continuum disks  
  > "In these observations, several molecular lines are often simultaneously observed." (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Error estimation of Yen et al. 2018 rejected as it underestimates errors  
  > "we found that the method in (cite: Yen2018) tends to underestimate the errors" (4.3 Error Estimation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Accuracy limited by S/N; high-mass bin has only six stars; highly inclined disks may be overestimated  
  > "Nevertheless, the accuracy of our dynamical mass measurements is limited by the S/N of the data." (Comparison with Literature (Discussion))
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Thin-disk assumption biases masses at high inclination
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectroscopic masses depend on Teff/L and distances, with unresolved binaries only crudely screened
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Binning in three mass ranges chosen post hoc from the figure
