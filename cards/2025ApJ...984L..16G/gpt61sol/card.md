## Analysis recipe

1. **Align, rescale and image the combined observations**: Use a Cycle 6 image model to phase-align exoALMA; perform relative flux calibration; transfer continuum calibration and flux scaling to CO; spectrally average and image combined data.  
  > "To align the data, we first imaged the Cycle 6 data and created a model that was used to adjust the phase of exoALMA data to match that of the Cycle 6 data." (Data); "For the CO observations, we applied the same calibration table generated from the continuum to calibrate and align the data." (Data); "We then rescaled the flux of the CO using the scaling factor derived from the continuum." (Data)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Continuum Briggs robust=0.7; CO Briggs robust=0.2  
  > "We find that for the continuum, a *robust* parameter of 0.7 works best to achieve a good balance between signal-to-noise and spatial resolution." (Data); "For the same reasons, we utilize a *robust* parameter of 0.2 for the CO line." (Data)
   - parameter: Combined CO spectral resolution 0.85 km s−1  
  > "Our final spectral resolution is 0.85 km s$^{-1}$." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect continuum images across robust=0.5–0.9; combined short baselines reduce spatial-filtering artifacts.  
  > "images of the dust continuum emission obtained using robust parameters between 0.5 and 0.9" (Dust appearance vs Robust parameter); "Combining these data helped to reduce the effects of spatial filtering, a result of the cycle 6 data not having shorter baselines in the uv coverage." (CO channel maps)
2. **Measure disk geometry and continuum radial profiles**: Fit ellipses to ring/gap intensity extrema; deproject with average centers and orientation; azimuthally average the continuum.  
  > "This approach uses the Python-based Markov Chain Monte-Carlo sampler *emcee* (cite: Foreman-Mackey+2013) to find the best-fitting ellipse for intensity maxima or minima within a given radial interval." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings); "Using the average value of the ring centers and orientation, we then deproject the continuum emission and create the azimuthally averaged profile" (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
   - software: emcee
   - fitting: Markov Chain Monte-Carlo ellipse fitting with emcee  
  > "This approach uses the Python-based Markov Chain Monte-Carlo sampler *emcee* (cite: Foreman-Mackey+2013) to find the best-fitting ellipse for intensity maxima or minima within a given radial interval." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
   - validation: Compare fitted centers, inclinations and position angles across rings and gaps.  
  > "All the rings and gaps have similar centers, inclinations, and position angles, indicating that these substructures are concentric and coplanar." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
3. **Fit continuum components and identify asymmetries**: Fit Gaussian components in image and visibility space, including a broad diffuse ring; deconvolve image-plane widths and amplitudes; subtract the best-fit visibility model.  
  > "Both the image and visibility fitting require an additional diffused component to account for the extended emission observed beyond 150 au, which here is modeled as an additional broader ring." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings); "We then take the width of each component and deconvolve it via a simple sum of squares calculation using the geometrical mean of the synthesized beam size." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings); "an image obtained by subtracting the galario best fit model" (Continuum emission)
   - software: Galario, emcee
   - parameter: Gaussian ring profile I(r)=Ix exp[−(r−rx)^2/(2wx^2)]  
  > "Properties of the dust emission components modeled as Gaussian functions with intrinsic radial profiles $I(r) = I_x e^{-(r-r_x)^2/2w_x^2}$" (Table 1)
   - parameter: Deconvolve Gaussian amplitudes using measured σ/deconvolved σ  
  > "We also deconvolve the amplitudes of the Gaussians using a ratio of the measured $\sigma$ to the deconvolved $\sigma$." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
   - assumption: One center, inclination and position angle; concentric, coplanar rings  
  > "In both cases, we only fit for one center, inclination, and position angle, assuming that the rings are concentric and coplanar." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
   - fitting: emcee fits 1D image-plane Gaussians and compares observed visibilities with Galario model visibilities  
  > "In the case of the image plane, we use *emcee* to fit 1D Gaussian functions to the azimuthally averaged continuum intensity profile" (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings); "Galario takes an input model image, which in this case consists of the sum of Gaussian rings, and calculates the corresponding visibilities." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings); "Using *emcee*, we then find best-fitting model parameters by comparing observed and theoretical visibilities." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
   - validation: Image and visibility fits give consistent orientation and ring properties.  
  > "The image and visibility fittings deliver consistent results regarding the disk orientation and the ring properties." (Disk geometry and properties of the dust rings)
4. **Compare continuum and CO emission inside the cavity**: Construct continuum-subtracted CO peak brightness temperatures with the full Planck law; coadd velocity-offset cubes; compare continuum, CO and summed radial profiles.  
  > "the continuum-subtracted $^{12}$CO J=3-2 peak brightness temperature (Using the full Planck law)" (CO emission); "To improve the quality of the peak intensity map, we generated and coadded four CO data cubes, each offset in velocity by 0.17 km/s." (CO emission); "we plot both continuum and CO profiles, as well as their sum." (CO emission)
   - parameter: Four CO cubes offset by 0.17 km/s; continuum reference contour 5σ  
  > "To improve the quality of the peak intensity map, we generated and coadded four CO data cubes, each offset in velocity by 0.17 km/s." (CO emission); "As a reference, we overlay the 5$\sigma$ contour of the continuum emission." (CO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Velocity-offset coaddition largely removes coarse-channel peak-map artifacts.  
  > "This procedure largely removes image artifacts generated by the coarse velocity resolution when taking the peak intensity of each pixel and does not affect the physical interpretation of the data." (CO emission)
5. **Initialize the disk and calculate its temperature**: Initialize the α-disk surface-density profile; construct vertical gas/dust distributions; iterate RADMC3D temperature and aspect ratio to hydrostatic convergence.  
  > "Following the evolution of an $\alpha$ disk (cite: Lynden-Bell+1974; Pringle+1981; Hartmann+1998; Isella+2009), we assume an initial gas surface density profile" (Model Setup); "The vertical gas density profile is calculated assuming that the disk is vertically isothermal and in hydrostatic equilibrium" (Model Setup); "To find the disk temperature profile corresponding to the hydrostatic equilibrium, we start from an initial guess of the disk aspect ratio and iterate on the temperature calculated by RADMC3D until convergence." (Model Setup)
   - software: RADMC3D, LA-COMPASS
   - parameter: Σc=1.62 g cm−2; rc=150 au; γ=1  
  > "where $\Sigma_{c} = 1.62$ g cm$^{-2}$ is the surface density at the cutoff radius $r_{c} = 150$ au and $\gamma$ is the slope of the disk surface density which we set to 1." (Model Setup)
   - parameter: RADMC grid 205×100×80 (Nr×Nφ×Nθ); polar angle 60°–90°; midplane symmetry  
  > "Our RADMC grid is $205\times100\times80$ ($N_{r}\times N_{\phi}\times N_{\theta}$), covering the same radial and azimuthal extent of the LA-COMPASS simulations." (Disk-Planet Models); "In the polar angle we run from 60$^{\circ}$ to 90$^{\circ}$ (midplane) and assume that the disk is symmetric about the midplane." (Disk-Planet Models)
   - parameter: 2.5×10^8 thermal photons  
  > "We tried running the thermal calculation with up to $2\times10^{9}$ photons, but found that $2.5\times10^{8}$ photons achieved a good balance between computational time and reducing the noise." (Disk-Planet Models)
   - assumption: M⋆=1.25 M⊙; black body T⋆=4500 K, L⋆=0.95 L⊙  
  > "For all of our models we assume a stellar mass of 1.25 M$_{\odot}$ (cite: Donati+2019)." (Continuum Modeling); "For the irradiation flux, we assume a black body spectrum at a temperature $T_\star=$ 4500 K and total luminosity $L_\star=$ 0.95 L$_\odot$ (cite: Donati+2019)." (Model Setup)
   - assumption: Passively irradiated disk; negligible atomic/molecular contribution to interior thermal structure  
  > "Under the assumption of a passively irradiated disk" (Model Setup); "we assume that atomic and molecular gas play negligible roles in setting the disk’s interior thermal structure" (Model Setup)
   - assumption: DSHARP total absorption opacities  
  > "For the dust opacities, we assume the DSHARP total absorption opacities discussed in (cite: Birnstiel+2018) (see equation 6)" (Model Setup)
   - assumption: Thermal calculation: ρd=0.01ρg; maximum grain size 1 μm  
  > "to calculate the disk temperature, we assume $\rho_d = 0.01 \rho_g$ and a dust opacity corresponding to a grain size distribution with a maximum grain size of $1 \mu$m." (Model Setup)
   - assumption: Temperature calculated for the initial unperturbed disk and held constant; gas temperature equals dust temperature  
  > "Note that we calculate the disk temperature only for the initial unperturbed disk state and assume it to remain constant with time." (Hydrodynamic and Radiative Transfer Models); "As an additional caveat we calculate only the dust temperature, assuming that it is equal to the gas temperature." (Hydrodynamic and Radiative Transfer Models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Linear grid interpolation changes gas/dust mass by no more than ∼10%; thermal photon-number trials.  
  > "We linearly interpolate from the LA-COMPASS to RADMC grids, taking care to ensure that the interpolation does not change the gas or dust mass by more than $\sim10\%$." (Disk-Planet Models); "We tried running the thermal calculation with up to $2\times10^{9}$ photons, but found that $2.5\times10^{8}$ photons achieved a good balance between computational time and reducing the noise." (Disk-Planet Models)
6. **Evolve candidate planetary systems**: Evolve 2D gas and single-size dust with LA-COMPASS under three planets; initially hold orbital radii fixed and vary planet masses.  
  > "We use the 2D ($r, \phi$) version of the hydrodynamic code LA-COMPASS (cite: Li+2005; Li+2009) to calculate the evolution of circumstellar gas and dust under the gravitational influence of planets." (Model Setup); "All of our models include three planets." (Continuum Modeling); "Keeping the planets’ orbital radii fixed, we then run a grid of models allowing for three values for the mass of each planet." (Continuum Modeling)
   - software: LA-COMPASS
   - parameter: Radial domain 4–200 au; azimuth 0–2π; 1024 logarithmic radial cells and 3072 uniform azimuthal cells; open boundaries  
  > "Our simulation domain covers from 4 to 200 au in the radial direction and 0 to $2\pi$ in azimuth." (Model Setup); "We use 1024 logarithmically spaced cells in the radial direction and 3072 uniformly spaced cells in azimuth." (Model Setup); "Ultimately, we use open boundaries" (Model Setup)

## Qualitative findings

- CO is radially smoother than the dust and shows no gas-depleted cavity.  
  > "Conversely, the CO emission is radially smoother and shows no sign of gas depletion within the dust cavity." (Abstract)
- No mm-wave emission peak coincides with candidate planets LkCa 15 b or c.  
  > "none of the mm-wave emission peaks correspond to the location of the candidate planets LkCa 15 b and c" (Continuum emission)
- The Clump and Arc are positive Gaussian-model residuals; the Deficit is negative.  
  > "The Clump and the Arc are visible as positive residuals." (Continuum emission); "The Deficit appears instead as a negative residual." (Continuum emission)
- The continuum is likely optically thick at bright-ring radii.  
  > "Dashed lines indicate the locations of the bright continuum rings whose subtraction leaves clear signatures in the CO profile." (CO emission); "This shows that the continuum is likely optically thick at these radial locations." (CO emission)
- A planet-free disk model matches the observed CO emission.  
  > "In fact, a model that does not include any planets (see middle panel of Figure [6](#fig:mod_temp_im)) matches the observed CO emission." (CO Modeling)
- A single planet cannot simultaneously produce the large dust cavity and preserve the observed CO emission under the adopted assumptions.  
  > "The planet’s mass cannot be so low that it does not clear out a deep cavity in the continuum, but it cannot be so massive that it leaves a signature in the CO emission." (Discussion and Conclusion); "Our results indicate that such a delicate interplay cannot be obtained with a single planet." (Discussion and Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Gas observations at continuum angular scales test planet–disk interpretations; combine short and long baselines for sensitivity and reduced spatial filtering.  
  > "observations of molecular gas at the same angular scales of the continuum are essential to constrain the distribution of the circumstellar material and test the planet-disk interaction hypothesis." (Introduction); "The data were combined to increase sensitivity and reduce the effects of spatial filtering by utilizing the short baselines while retaining the high angular resolution achievable with the more extended configuration." (Data)
- Why this method: Radiative transfer is needed to distinguish density structure from optical-depth/vertical-distribution effects; visibility comparison accounts for ALMA resolution and uv sampling.  
  > "the different radial profiles might be caused by radiative transfer effects, such as the different vertical distribution and optical depths of dust and CO emission." (CO emission); "In this way, we naturally account for the angular resolution and discrete *uv* sampling of ALMA observations." (Continuum Modeling)
- Alternative: Initial planets at 20, 42 and 90 au fail to match the continuum satisfactorily.  
  > "Overall, none of these models provide a good match to the observations." (Continuum Modeling); "Following these results, we relaxed our assumptions on the planets’ orbital radii and tried several combinations of planets’ masses and orbital radii to reproduce the observed continuum radial profile." (Continuum Modeling)
- Alternative: Recalculating the temperature after cavity clearing does not rescue a massive-inner-planet model.  
  > "As a result, the gas temperature and CO emission within the cavity increase by approximately 10%." (CO Modeling); "However, this enhancement is insufficient to explain the observed emission in the presence of a massive planet." (CO Modeling)
- Alternative: Lower viscosity would require an even lower inner-planet mass.  
  > "A lower $\alpha$ would mean that massive planets would clear more gas within the dust cavity and excite instabilities" (Discussion and Conclusion); "Therefore, the inner planet mass would have to be lowered even more to match the observations." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- Alternative: Larger grains could allow less massive planets to trap dust without clearing as much gas.  
  > "A less massive inner planet may still be able to trap these larger dust grains while less effectively clearing a cavity in gas, therefore bringing us closer towards the differences in gas and dust that we observe." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- Alternative: Reducing gas surface density at fixed α and grain size makes the gas cavity clearer.  
  > "Doing this while holding $\alpha$ and $a$ fixed, however, would make the cavity more clear of gas which is the opposite of what we would want." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- Alternative: A low-mass planet chain or nonplanetary processes remain possible cavity origins.  
  > "Alternatively, a chain of properly spaced low-mass planets orbiting within 30 au from the central star could also explain the observed dust and gas morphology, as suggested by (cite: Leemker+2022)." (Discussion and Conclusion); "Alternatively, physical processes that do not require the presence of planets could be responsible for LkCa 15’s dust cavity." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- Alternative: 3D hydrodynamics rejected as computationally unfeasible for the required evolution time.  
  > "As we are interested in the evolution of the circumstellar disk on timescales of $10^{3}$ orbits in this study, 3D simulations are computationally unfeasible." (Disk-Planet Models)
- How they decided it was enough: Choose robust for signal-to-noise/resolution balance; evolve to quasi-steady state; choose thermal photon count for time/noise balance.  
  > "We find that for the continuum, a *robust* parameter of 0.7 works best to achieve a good balance between signal-to-noise and spatial resolution." (Data); "This allows for the simulations to achieve a quasi-steady state, meaning that the results do not strongly depend on the time frame used for the comparison." (Continuum Modeling); "We tried running the thermal calculation with up to $2\times10^{9}$ photons, but found that $2.5\times10^{8}$ photons achieved a good balance between computational time and reducing the noise." (Disk-Planet Models)
- Limitation (authors): The model parameter space is not explored fully.  
  > "However, a full exploration of the model parameter space is impossible due to the large computational time required to perform such simulations." (Hydrodynamic and Radiative Transfer Models)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature is fixed from the unperturbed disk, and gas temperature is assumed equal to dust temperature.  
  > "Note that we calculate the disk temperature only for the initial unperturbed disk state and assume it to remain constant with time." (Hydrodynamic and Radiative Transfer Models); "As an additional caveat we calculate only the dust temperature, assuming that it is equal to the gas temperature." (Hydrodynamic and Radiative Transfer Models)
- Limitation (authors): Simplified photodissociation omits a complete treatment of shielding, abundances and thermal chemistry.  
  > "The prescription for photodissociation that we take from (cite: Rosenfeld+2013) and (cite: Qi+2011) does not fully capture the complexity of this physical process." (Photodissociation); "Ultimately, in the absence of physical-chemical modeling, full radiative-hydrodynamic calculations, or chemical networks like those of (cite: vantHoff+2017), a number of assumptions had to be made about gas and dust abundances and temperatures." (Photodissociation)
- Limitation (authors): Synthetic compact emission at planets is an artifact of using the circumstellar scale height for circumplanetary material.  
  > "this feature is caused by an incorrect treatment of the circumplanetary material wherein the material is expanded vertically using the scale height of the PPD rather than the CPD." (Continuum Modeling); "Therefore, these sources of compact emission should be disregarded in the model images." (Continuum Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Continuum models overpredict middle/outer-ring brightness and make the inner cavity edge too steep.  
  > "The models reproduce the location of the dust rings and the overall morphology of the dust cavity, but over predict the intensity profile across the middle and outer rings by 30%-50%." (Continuum Modeling); "a massive Planet 1 generates a steep increase in the dust intensity profile between 30-40 au, while the observations show a more gradual increase." (Continuum Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Outer-disk CO brightness temperatures are overpredicted.  
  > "the synthetic models overpredict the CO peak brightness temperature beyond $\sim$100 au by about 30-50%." (CO Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): The faint continuum morphology depends on robust weighting.  
  > "The morphology of this emission varies depending on the robust parameter we use for imaging" (Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Current exoALMA 13CO resolution marginally resolves cavity structure.  
  > "As of now, the exoALMA $^{13}$CO resolution only allows for $\sim$1-2 beams across the disk cavity meaning that structures will only be marginally resolved at best." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ≲1 MJ limit is conditional on the explored disk models; no statistical confidence level or marginalized mass posterior is supplied.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO-section reference masses 3.8, 0.5 and 0.3 MJ differ from the continuum configuration’s 3–5, 0.4 and 0.25 MJ.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The simulation duration is described as 5000 orbits at 25 au, while the appendix describes a snapshot at 5000 innermost-planet orbits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 1 gives a declination offset of -0.0844 arcsec, while the visibility-fit appendix gives -84.0 mas.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The text identifies different near-side directions in different passages; reproduce projection conventions explicitly.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The joint circular/concentric fit imposes shared geometry; the separate ellipse fits provide the more independent geometry check.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The low-mass planet-chain explanation is proposed rather than tested with a dedicated chain simulation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Robust-dependent faint continuum structure and known synthetic CPD artifacts should not be used as secure planet detections.
