## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and image archival ALMA data and produce moment maps**: Image archival ALMA data of HCN, CN, CO, and HCO+ J=1-0 and 93 GHz continuum with uv-matching and tapering to a common beam per galaxy (following Wilson et al. 2022, and re-imaging NGC 4038/9, NGC 1808, and NGC 3351 at higher velocity resolution). Generate integrated intensity (Moment 0) and velocity dispersion (Moment 2) maps of CO and HCN J=1-0 using Spectral Cube with a 5-sigma peak / 3-sigma wing mask.  
  > "For each galaxy, we image archival ALMA data of the HCN, CN, CO, and HCO$^+$ $J=1-0$ transitions, in addition to the radio continuum emission at 93 GHz. The data are $uv-$matched and tapered to a common beam for each individual galaxy." (Data and Sample); "We produce maps of integrated intensity and velocity dispersion of the CO and HCN $J=1-0$ transitions using the Astropy Spectral Cube package (cite: Ginsburg:2019)." (Moment Maps)
   - software: CASA, SPECTRAL-CUBE, ASTROPY
   - parameter: Peak SNR >= 5 across two channels, expanded to SNR = 3, regions smaller than a beam area masked  
  > "Peaks of emission with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of at least five across two channels are identified within each datacube." (Moment Maps); "Masks are expanded around these peaks down to channels with emission at a SNR of three." (Moment Maps); "Emission from regions smaller than a beam area are masked." (Moment Maps)
   - parameter: Pixel selection cuts: SNR > 3 in integrated intensity and SNR > 2 in velocity dispersion  
  > "We require that all pixels have $\mathrm{SNR}>3$ in integrated intensity in addition to a $\mathrm{SNR}>2$ in velocity dispersion." (Moment Maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainties on moment 0 and moment 2 calculated analytically via Eqs. 23 and 27.  
  > "We calculate the uncertainty in integrated intensity and velocity dispersion using Eqs. [\[eq:unc_mom0\]](#eq:unc_mom0) and [\[eq:unc_mom2\]](#eq:unc_mom2), respectively." (Moment Maps)
2. **Derive molecular gas surface densities and dense gas fraction**: Convert CO and HCN integrated intensities into total and dense molecular gas surface densities (Sigma_mol and Sigma_mol,dense) and compute the observational dense gas fraction f_dense = (alpha_HCN / alpha_CO) * (I_HCN / I_CO).  
  > "To calculate the molecular gas mass surface density, $\Sigma_\mathrm{mol}$, we use: $$\begin{equation} \label{eq:Sigma_mol} \Sigma_\mathrm{mol} = \alpha_\mathrm{mol}\ I_\mathrm{mol}\ \mathrm{cos}(i). \end{equation}$$" (Molecular Gas Surface Densities); "If all of the above assumptions are true, then the fraction of dense gas traced by HCN/CO is given by: $$\begin{equation} f_\mathrm{dense} = \frac{\alpha_\mathrm{HCN}}{\alpha_\mathrm{CO}} \frac{I_\mathrm{HCN}}{I_\mathrm{CO}}. \end{equation}$$ with $\alpha_\mathrm{HCN}/\alpha_\mathrm{CO}=3.2$ implied from the above discussion." (Molecular Gas Surface Densities)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 1.1 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 for the ALMA sample; alpha_CO = 4.35 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 for EMPIRE sample  
  > "For ease of comparison with other studies our fiducial value is $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}=1.1$ \[M$_\odot$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$)$^{-1}$\] for our sample of galaxies, the (U)LIRG value including Helium (cite: Downes:1993)" (Molecular Gas Surface Densities); "We choose a fixed value of $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}=4.35$ \[M$_\odot$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$)$^{-1}$\] for the EMPIRE sample of galaxies" (Molecular Gas Surface Densities)
   - parameter: alpha_HCN / alpha_CO = 3.2  
  > "Furthermore, by using $\alpha_\mathrm{HCN}/\alpha_\mathrm{CO}=3.2$ our plots of $f_\mathrm{dense}$ are a direct reflection of the HCN/CO ratio." (Molecular Gas Surface Densities)
   - assumption: Inclination correction cos(i) applied only to disk galaxies, not mergers and (U)LIRGs  
  > "We apply inclination angles only to the disk galaxies in this sample, as inclination angles are typically uncertain in mergers and (U)LIRGs." (Molecular Gas Surface Densities)
   - assumption: Physical effects impacting alpha_CO impact alpha_HCN in a similar way so alpha_HCN / alpha_CO = 3.2 is constant  
  > "We do this assuming that the physical effects impacting $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ will impact $\alpha_\mathrm{HCN}$ in a similar way." (Molecular Gas Surface Densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive star formation rate surface densities, depletion times, free-fall times, and star formation efficiencies**: Calculate SFR surface density from 93 GHz continuum using the Murphy et al. (2011) composite thermal+non-thermal calibration (with a 10% dust subtraction and AGN masking), and from smoothed Spitzer 24 um maps using Rieke et al. (2009) for EMPIRE. Compute total and dense gas depletion times (t_dep = Sigma_mol / Sigma_SFR), free-fall times (t_ff), and star formation efficiency per free-fall time (epsilon_ff = t_ff / t_dep).  
  > "We adopt fixed values for $\alpha_\mathrm{NT}=0.84$ and $T_\mathrm{e}=10^4$ K, and we use the composite calibration from (cite: Murphy:2011) which accounts for both thermal and non-thermal contributions to the SFR:" (The Radio Continuum SFR); "We use the star formation rate (SFR) and molecular gas surface densities to estimate the depletion time of the total (mol$\,=\,$CO) and dense (mol$\,=\,$HCN) molecular gas content:" (Star Formation Timescales and Efficiency); "The efficiency of the star-formation process is then estimated by comparing the observed depletion timescales with estimates of the free-fall time: $$\begin{equation} \epsilon_\mathrm{ff}=\frac{t_\mathrm{ff}}{t_\mathrm{dep}} \end{equation}$$" (Star Formation Timescales and Efficiency)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: T_e = 10^4 K, alpha_NT = 0.84, nu = 93 GHz, 10% dust correction, 30% SFR uncertainty  
  > "We adopt fixed values for $\alpha_\mathrm{NT}=0.84$ and $T_\mathrm{e}=10^4$ K, and we use the composite calibration from (cite: Murphy:2011) which accounts for both thermal and non-thermal contributions to the SFR:" (The Radio Continuum SFR); "We adopt this correction factor of $\sim10\%$ for all sources, but we acknowledge that the emissivity of dust at 93 GHz may vary between our sources." (The Radio Continuum SFR); "We adopt an uncertainty of $30\%$ for SFR estimates derived from the radio continuum." (The Radio Continuum SFR)
   - assumption: Fixed half line-of-sight cloud depth R = 100 pc for all galaxies to estimate volume density rho = Sigma_mol / R  
  > "We calculate gas density assuming a fixed line of sight (LOS) depth via $\rho\approx\Sigma_\mathrm{mol}/R$, where $R$ is equivalent to half of the ‘cloud’ or gas depth along the line of sight (LOS). We do not know $R$, so we assume a fixed value of 100 pc for all galaxies when estimating $\rho$." (Star Formation Timescales and Efficiency)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared fixed-depth t_ff with Krumholz et al. (2012) GMC and Toomre regime prescriptions and found little qualitative difference.  
  > "We also explored estimating free fall time using the (cite: Krumholz:2012) prescriptions for $t_\mathrm{ff}$ in the GMC and Toomre regimes. We find little qualitative difference in the results between using $t_\mathrm{ff}$ as described above and the (cite: Krumholz:2012) prescription." (Star Formation Timescales and Efficiency)
4. **Calculate velocity dispersion, Mach number, and turbulent pressure**: Measure 1D CO J=1-0 velocity dispersion from moment 2 maps, deconvolve finite spectral channel resolution following Rosolowsky & Leroy (2006), convert to 3D velocity dispersion, and compute Mach number M and turbulent pressure P_turb.  
  > "We correct for broadening of the line due to the finite spectral resolution of our data using (cite: Rosolowsky:2006): $$\begin{equation} \sigma_v = \sqrt{\sigma_{v\mathrm{meas}}^2 - \frac{\delta{v}^2}{2\pi}} \end{equation}$$" (Velocity Dispersion); "As previous studies have done for Milky Way clouds (cite: Kainulainen:2017), we use CO linewidths as an indicator of the mach number of the gas in our galaxies:" (Velocity Dispersion as a Tracer of Mach Number); "| \(2\) | $P_\mathrm{turb,obs}$ | $(3/2)\,\Sigma_\mathrm{mol}\sigma_\mathrm{v}^2/R/k_\mathrm{B}$ | K cm$^{-3}$ |  |  |  |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: c_s = 0.4 km s^-1, T_kin = 45 K, mu = 2.33, R = 100 pc  
  > "We choose an intermediate value $c_s=0.4$ km s$^{-1}$, corresponding to $T\sim45$ K as our fiducial value." (Velocity Dispersion as a Tracer of Mach Number); "For a gas where hydrogen is primarily in molecular form $\mu = 2.33$ (assuming cosmic abundances, (cite: Kauffmann:2008))." (Velocity Dispersion as a Tracer of Mach Number)
   - assumption: Ideal gas equation of state with T_kin = 45 K and isotropic 3D velocity dispersion sigma_v,3D = sqrt(3) * sigma_v  
  > "This value is then converted to the 3-dimensional velocity dispersion via $\sigma_{v,3D} = \sqrt{3}\sigma_v$." (Velocity Dispersion); "To estimate $c_s$, we consider an ideal gas equation of state ($P_\mathrm{th} = n k_\mathrm{B} T / \mu m_\mathrm{H}$)" (Velocity Dispersion as a Tracer of Mach Number)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Construct gravoturbulent analytical star formation model grids and compare to observations**: Generate model grids for three gravoturbulent prescriptions (LN PN11, LN+PL B18, LN Fixed) over the observed Sigma_mol and sigma_v parameter space using the scaled Sun et al. (2018) empirical relation. Compute model Sigma_SFR, P_turb, t_dep, epsilon_ff, f_grav, f(n > 10^4.5 cm^-3), t_dep,grav, and t_dep,dense. Evaluate Spearman rank correlation coefficients for observed trends and compute chi^2 of data relative to the fiducial model that best fits the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation.  
  > "To compare models with data from our sample, we create a sets of models for each of the three, unique prescriptions. We produce LN models over the $\sigma_\mathrm{v}^2/R-\Sigma_\mathrm{mol}$ parameter space that encompasses our data in Fig. [1](#fig:grid)." (Connecting Observations to Theory); "We consider fiducial models which correspond to the parameters that best fit the observed Kennicutt-Schmidt relationship (see Fig. [3](#fig:KS_plots)). For each model, we calculate the $\chi^2$ of the data relative to this fiducial model, and this value is shown in each panel." ## Qualitative findings

- Observed f_dense (proportional to I_HCN/I_CO) shows a weak positive correlation with turbulent pressure P_turb, which matches model predictions for the gas fraction above a fixed density f(n > 10^4.5 cm^-3) rather than the self-gravitating gas fraction f_grav.  
  > "From this result, the observed positive trend in $I_\mathrm{HCN}/I_\mathrm{CO}$ with $P_\mathrm{turb}$ is more consistent with $I_\mathrm{HCN}/I_\mathrm{CO}$ being a better tracer of $f(n>10^{4.5}$ cm$^{-3})$ than $f_\mathrm{grav}$." (Does HCN/CO trace the Star-forming Gas Fraction?)
- Star formation efficiency per free-fall time (epsilon_ff) shows a weak negative correlation with P_turb and a weak positive correlation with f_dense across the sample.  
  > "$\epsilon_\mathrm{ff}$ shows a weak negative correlation with $P_\mathrm{turb}$ and a weak positive correlation with $f_\mathrm{dense}$ (Fig. [6](#fig:eff_vdisp_data)) when considering our sample as a whole." (The Star Formation Efficiency per Free-fall Time)
- Total molecular gas depletion time (t_dep) shows moderate negative correlations with both f_dense and P_turb.  
  > "We find moderate negative correlations between $t_\mathrm{dep}$ and the dense gas fraction traced by $I_\mathrm{HCN}/I_\mathrm{CO}$. $t_\mathrm{dep}$ estimated from the data appears relatively constant with $P_\mathrm{turb}$, but the Spearman rank coefficient of the combined data indicates a moderate, negative correlation (cf. Fig. [8](#fig:tdep_data))." (Total Gas Depletion Times)
- Dense gas depletion time (t_dep,dense) shows a weak positive correlation with f_dense in the combined dataset and a moderate positive correlation in ~3 sample sources and the EMPIRE galaxies.  
  > "We see a moderate positive correlation between $t_\mathrm{dep,dense}$ and $f_\mathrm{dense}$ in $\sim$ 3 of our sources and the EMPIRE galaxies. The Spearman rank coefficient shows a weak, positive correlation for the combined dataset." (Dense Gas Depletion Times)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: To test the relationship between HCN/CO and f_grav in more extreme star-forming environments where turbulence is stronger (mergers, starbursts, (U)LIRGs, barred galaxy centers), complemented by the EMPIRE survey of normal galaxy disks.  
  > "To assess the relationship between the HCN/CO ratio and $f_\mathrm{grav}$, we look to more extreme star-forming environments in which turbulence is stronger (e.g. mergers, starbursts, (U)LIRGs, barred galaxies)." (Introduction); "We use the EMPIRE sample of galaxies (cite: Jimenez_Donaire:2019) as a comparison, which predominantly targets normal regions of star formation within galaxy disks." (Introduction)
- Why this method: In external galaxies where spatial resolution is limited, combining molecular line ratios of dense gas tracers (sensitive to n-PDF shape) with CO kinematic measurements (sensitive to Mach number and turbulent pressure) provides a tool to test gravoturbulent n-PDF models.  
  > "In external galaxies where resolution is limited, molecular line ratios are an additional tool for assessing $n-$PDF shape." (Introduction); "Combining molecular line ratios of dense gas tracers with information on kinematics is therefore a promising tool for assessing $n-$PDF information of clouds in external galaxies." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Krumholz et al. (2012) prescriptions for free-fall time (t_ff) in the GMC and Toomre regimes were explored as an alternative to the fixed 100 pc line-of-sight depth prescription, yielding little qualitative difference.  
  > "We also explored estimating free fall time using the (cite: Krumholz:2012) prescriptions for $t_\mathrm{ff}$ in the GMC and Toomre regimes. We find little qualitative difference in the results between using $t_\mathrm{ff}$ as described above and the (cite: Krumholz:2012) prescription." (Star Formation Timescales and Efficiency)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Inclination angles are not applied to mergers and (U)LIRGs due to uncertainty, resulting in overestimates of Sigma_mol and other surface densities for systems with non-zero inclinations.  
  > "We apply inclination angles only to the disk galaxies in this sample, as inclination angles are typically uncertain in mergers and (U)LIRGs. Thus, the uncorrected measurements of galaxies with non-zero inclinations will result in overestimates of $\Sigma_\mathrm{mol}$ and other surface densities." (Molecular Gas Surface Densities)
- Limitation (authors): Fixed conversion factors (alpha_CO and alpha_HCN/alpha_CO = 3.2) are assumed, deferring pixel-by-pixel non-LTE radiative transfer modelling of emissivity and conversion factor variations to a companion paper.  
  > "We do not directly assess variations in emissivity in this paper, and defer an analysis of emissivity (and therefore conversion factors) to (cite: Bemis:2020) and Bemis & Wilson, in prep., where we present modelling of the HCN and CO emissivities for our sample of galaxies on a pixel-by-pixel basis." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): 93 GHz radio continuum SFR estimates are subject to ~30% uncertainty from variations in electron temperature (T_e), non-thermal spectral index (alpha_NT), and dust emissivity.  
  > "We adopt this correction factor of $\sim10\%$ for all sources, but we acknowledge that the emissivity of dust at 93 GHz may vary between our sources." (The Radio Continuum SFR); "We adopt an uncertainty of $30\%$ for SFR estimates derived from the radio continuum." (The Radio Continuum SFR)
- Limitation (authors): A fixed line-of-sight cloud depth of R = 100 pc is assumed for all galaxies when estimating mean volume density and free-fall time, which likely contributes to discrepancies between observed and modelled epsilon_ff vs. P_turb.  
  > "We do not know $R$, so we assume a fixed value of 100 pc for all galaxies when estimating $\rho$." (Star Formation Timescales and Efficiency); "The discrepancy between the models and data is likely in part due to inaccurate estimates of $t_\mathrm{ff}$ from our data. When estimating the mean density of the molecular gas traced by CO, we assume a fixed line-of-sight depth of 100 pc for all measurements" (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Using CO linewidths to trace cloud Mach number is limited by optical depth effects, disk rotation, large-scale motions, choice of fiducial sound speed (c_s = 0.4 km s^-1), and turbulent scale dependence across physical resolutions of ~50-900 pc.  
  > "There are limits to this approach, and the ability of the CO line to trace cloud turbulence may be limited by its optical depth (cite: Burkhart:2013; Goodman:2009), and observed line width can include disk rotation or other large-scale motions, but alternative measures of gas kinematics in extragalactic clouds are absent." (Velocity Dispersion as a Tracer of Mach Number); "A sample of galaxies at different physical scales will therefore be affected by this scale dependence, such that velocity dispersions will be smaller at smaller scales and vice versa." (Uncertainties From a Multi-scale Sample)
- Limitation (authors): Variations in the relative beam filling fractions of HCN and CO (Phi_HCN/CO) are a significant source of uncertainty.  
  > "A significant source of uncertainty instead comes from variations in the relative filling fractions of the HCN/CO transitions:" (Uncertainties From a Multi-scale Sample)
- Limitation (authors): Magnetic fields are neglected (beta -> infinity), turbulent forcing is fixed at b = 0.4, local star formation efficiency is fixed at epsilon_0 = 1%, and the virial parameter alpha_vir is not independently varied in the LN models.  
  > "Without a clear prescription for $\beta$ and $b$ in our sample, we simply take $\beta\rightarrow\infty$, which assumes that $B=0$ G. We take an intermediate value $b=0.4$, which assumes that turbulence is a mixture of compressive and solenoidal forcing." (Model Framework: Gravoturbulent Models of Star Formation); "One parameter that we did not vary in this analysis is $\alpha_\mathrm{vir}$." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Physical resolution varies by nearly a factor of 20 across the ALMA sample (48 pc in M83 to 903 pc in VV114) and even more when compared to the 33'' single-dish EMPIRE sample, mixing individual GMC scales with kpc-scale galaxy-integrated regimes where beam averaging and rotational broadening dominate sigma_v.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using a constant alpha_HCN/alpha_CO = 3.2 and constant alpha_CO = 1.1 across both normal spiral centers and extreme ULIRGs ignores known excitation and optical depth variations that directly affect both axes of plots involving f_dense, Sigma_mol, and P_turb.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): P_turb (~ Sigma_mol * sigma_v^2) and t_ff (~ Sigma_mol^-0.5) share mathematical dependencies on I_CO with t_dep (~ I_CO / L_93GHz) and f_dense (~ I_HCN / I_CO), which can induce artificial correlations from correlated measurement noise or conversion factor variations.
