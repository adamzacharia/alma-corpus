## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image Stokes I, Q, U**: Standard observatory scripts in CASA 4.5.0; clean with Briggs robust 0.5 (high res) and natural (low res); self-cal for low res  
  > "The Briggs weighting with robust parameter of 0.5, and natural weighting were used for the high and low angular resolution final images, respectively." (2.1 Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Polarization calibrator J0522-3627 observed every 25 minutes  
  > "removing instrumental polarization using a bright polarized calibrator, J0522-3627, which was observed every 25 minutes during each execution" (2.1 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Q and U rms near thermal noise; Stokes I dynamic-range limited  
  > "The Stokes $Q$ and $U$ image rms is near the expected thermal noise level." (2.1 Observations)
2. **Derive PI, E-vector angle and polarization fraction**: PI=sqrt(Q^2+U^2) with 5 sigma debiasing; EPA=0.5 arctan(U/Q); Dfrac=PI/I using pixels with PI>5 sigma and I>3 sigma  
  > "only those pixels that contain both more than 5$\sigma$ signal level in the $PI$ maps and 3$\sigma$ signal level in the Stokes $I$ maps, were used for calculating $D_{\rm frac}$" (2.1 Observations)
   - software: CASA immath
   - parameter: Delta PI = sqrt(dQ^2+dU^2+(0.002 I)^2); lower limit of Delta EPA about 1 deg  
  > "There is a lower limit of $\Delta E_{PA}$ of $\approx 1^\circ$" (2.1 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared high and low resolution images: 96% Stokes I flux recovered in central 0.5 arcsec  
  > "we are recovering 96% of Stokes $I$ flux in the central 200 AU" (3.2)
3. **Fit Stokes I structure with multiple Gaussians and estimate physical properties**: 2D three-component Gaussian fit; masses, column and number densities derived from fluxes assuming tau~1  
  > "we used a two-dimensional Gaussian fit with multiple components" (3.4)
   - parameter: Mean dust temperatures 25, 27, 36 K; beta=0.93; kappa(1.1mm)=0.014 cm2/g  
  > "Assuming the respective mean dust temperatures of 25, 27, and 36 K for the extended, elongated, and compact components, an optical depth of ${\tau}{\approx}1$, a gas-to-dust ratio of 100" (3.4)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100, tau≈1, spherical geometry for densities, distance 414 pc  
  > "A spherical geometry with the geometric mean of the major and minor axes of the source size is assumed in order to estimate the number densities." (3.4)
   - fitting: not stated (residual <1.5% of peak)  
  > "The residual level of the fitting result is less than 1.5% with respect to the observed peak flux" (3.4)
   - validation: Compared with SMA mass from earlier work  
  > "the estimated mass would be agreement within a factor of 1.3" (3.4)
4. **Compare polarization with three mechanisms**: Qualitative comparison of observed characteristics with magnetic alignment, self-scattering, anisotropic radiation predictions (Table 4)  
  > "Table 4 summarizes how each of the characteristics could fit in each proposed polarization mechanism." (4.2.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare with 3D non-ideal MHD simulation**: Simulated projected polarization map at viewing angle 60 deg, optically thin, compared qualitatively with ALMA maps  
  > "Optically thin condition was assumed when making this projection. A viewing angle of $i$=60$^{\circ}$ was used" (4.3)
   - parameter: Bonnor-Ebert core, n_c=2e5 cm-3, T=10 K, B0=26 uG, Omega0=1e-13 s-1, Mcore=8.7 Msun, finest cell 0.2 AU  
  > "The cell size of the finest grid is 0.2 AU." (4.3)
   - assumption: Viewing angle chosen so outflow lobes do not overlap and best fits Figure 5(c)  
  > "Also the inclination angle of 60$^{\circ}$ appears to be a better fit to explain our observed results" (4.3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Ring-like polarized structure (~80 AU radius) seen in PI but not Stokes I, with a depolarization gap and ~90-105 deg change in E-vector P.A.  
  > "A partial ring-like structure with a radius of $\approx$80 AU is detected in $PI$ but not in the Stokes $I$." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MMS 6 is bright and relatively close, a unique target for highest-resolution polarization  
  > "MMS 6 is a bright source and relatively close, so it is a unique target to observe the polarized emission at the ALMA highest angular resolutions." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Gold (1952) mechanical alignment parallel to flow rejected; E-vectors perpendicular to outflow cavity  
  > "The observational result does not support the scenario expected by (cite: 1952MNRAS.112..215G)" (4.2.2)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): MHD simulation with sink cell cannot resolve central region or include protostar radiation  
  > "Thus, we cannot resolve the central region or protostar itself." (4.3)
- Limitation (authors): About 25% Stokes I flux missing in central 2 arcsec, so Dfrac in extended structure may be overestimated  
  > "for the central $2''.0$, comparison with low resolution maps suggests that about 25% is missing in the Stokes $I$ emission" (3.3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stokes I is dynamic-range limited, affecting Dfrac
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mechanism comparison is qualitative; no quantitative scattering or radiative-transfer model fit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): MHD viewing angle chosen by fit to data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperatures and tau~1 make mass estimates lower limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Typo-level inconsistencies in beam and weighting descriptions between text and Table 1
