## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA spectral cubes and continuum**: Image pipeline-calibrated uv-data sets using tclean with natural weighting, a field of view of 1.5 times the primary beam, and 15-20 km s^-1 channel width.  
  > "The imaging is done using the standard tclean algorithm with a final field of view of 1.5 times the primary beam size." (2.2 ALMA observations); "A ‘natural’ weighting scheme is applied, which guarantees optimal sensitivity appropriate for a line detection experiment." (2.2 ALMA observations); "We use a channel width of 15 - 20 km s −1 ." (2.2 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: field of view = 1.5 x primary beam (1.4' in Band 3, 1.2' in Band 4)  
  > "with a final field of view of 1.5 times the primary beam size. This results in an image diameter of 1 . 4 ′ in ALMA Band 3 and 1 . 2 ′ in ALMA Band 4." (2.2 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: weighting = natural  
  > "A ‘natural’ weighting scheme is applied, which guarantees optimal sensitivity appropriate for a line detection experiment." (2.2 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: channel width = 15 - 20 km s^-1  
  > "We use a channel width of 15 - 20 km s −1 ." (2.2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Search for CO line emission and classify detection confidence**: Search for CO line emission in non-primary-beam-corrected image cubes both visually by-eye and using the line finder software SoFiA. Flag detections with FWHM < 300 km s^-1 as low significance (L) unless confirmed by independent observations, and FWHM >= 300 km s^-1 (or confirmed) as high significance (H).  
  > "We search for CO line emission in the ALMA image cubes before correcting for the primary beam response to ensure constant noise properties across the field of view." (3 RESULTS); "We search for line emission both by-eye and using the line finder software SoFiA and detect CO line emission in four out of six absorber fields." (3 RESULTS); "Therefore, we flag galaxies with a FWHM < 300 km s −1 as low significance detections unless the detection is confirmed by independent observations." (3 RESULTS)
   - software: SoFiA
   - parameter: FWHM threshold = 300 km s^-1 for high/low confidence flag  
  > "Therefore, we flag galaxies with a FWHM < 300 km s −1 as low significance detections unless the detection is confirmed by independent observations." (3 RESULTS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-check with independent optical/IFU/GRISM observations where available; perform subsequent trend analyses on both the high-significance sample and full sample.  
  > "Therefore, we flag galaxies with a FWHM < 300 km s −1 as low significance detections unless the detection is confirmed by independent observations." (3 RESULTS); "When studying trends and drawing general conclusions we regard both, the high significance sample only and the full sample." (3 RESULTS)
3. **Extract CO spectra and measure line fluxes and upper limits**: Extract CO spectra from primary beam corrected image cubes by integrating over the region defined by the 3 sigma contour in the integrated flux map. For non-detections, calculate 5 sigma upper limits assuming a 300 km s^-1 linewidth.  
  > "The CO spectra are obtained from the primary beam corrected image cubes. Since many sources are resolved we integrate the spectra over a region defined by the 3 𝜎 contour in the integrated flux map." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields); "Upper limits are calculated assuming a 5 𝜎 detection threshold and a linewidth of 300 km s −1 ." (Table 3)
   - parameter: extraction aperture = 3 sigma contour in integrated flux map  
  > "Since many sources are resolved we integrate the spectra over a region defined by the 3 𝜎 contour in the integrated flux map." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
   - parameter: upper limit threshold = 5 sigma over 300 km s^-1 linewidth  
  > "Upper limits are calculated assuming a 5 𝜎 detection threshold and a linewidth of 300 km s −1 ." (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare CO(2-1) upper limit in Q2131-1207 with the CO(3-2) detection from Szakacs et al. (2021).  
  > "We calculate an upper limit of the CO(2-1) emission at the position of this galaxy which is consistent with the detection by Szakacs et al. (2021)" (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
4. **Derive CO luminosities and molecular gas masses**: Convert observed CO line luminosities to CO(1-0) luminosity using Milky Way type line ratios from Carilli & Walter (2013), and multiply by a Galactic CO-to-H2 conversion factor alpha_CO = 4.36 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1.  
  > "We convert the CO emission line luminosities in the observed transitions to the CO(1-0) line luminosity using a Milky Way type line ratio as reported by Carilli & Walter (2013)." (3.2 Molecular gas masses); "We have used a CO-to-H 2 conversion factor of 𝛼 CO = 4 . 36M⊙(K km s −1 pc 2 ) −1 that is applicable for galaxies of solar metallicity that do not undergo a starburst (including a factor of 1.36 to account for the presence of helium Bolatto et al. 2013)." ## Qualitative findings

- No continuum emission is significantly detected from any of the CO-detected galaxies.  
  > "We do not detect significant continuum emission from any galaxy detected in CO." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
- In HE 0515-4414 G1, the molecular gas (CO(3-2)) and ionized gas ([OII]) velocity fields are coupled, showing rotation with similar orientation and maximum velocity.  
  > "However, a qualitative analysis shows that the molecular and ionized gas kinematics are coupled. Both show rotation with similar orientation and maximum velocity." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
- There is no significant correlation between absorber properties (HI column density, H2 column density, molecular gas fraction) and host galaxy molecular gas mass or impact parameter.  
  > "We do not report a significant correlation in any of these parameter spaces for both, the full sample and the high significance detections only." (4.3 Molecular gas mass relation to absorber properties); "This is also supported by the lack of a clear correlation between the absorber column density or molecular gas fraction with impact parameter or host galaxy molecular gas mass." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets were selected to have UV H2 absorption at z <= 1 to limit required ALMA observing time, and observed in the lowest accessible CO transition to minimize uncertainties from CO excitation conversion when deriving total molecular gas mass.  
  > "The absorbers were selected to have an H 2 molecular gas absorption detection at UV wavelength. Additionally, we selected absorbers observable with ALMA and at 𝑧 ≲ 1 to limit the required observing time." (2.1 Target selection); "The lowest CO transition is observed to minimize uncertainties from the line luminosity conversion, needed to evaluate the total molecular gas mass." (2.2 ALMA observations)
- Why this method: Natural weighting was chosen to guarantee optimal sensitivity for a line detection experiment, and the line search was performed before primary beam correction to ensure uniform noise across the field of view.  
  > "A ‘natural’ weighting scheme is applied, which guarantees optimal sensitivity appropriate for a line detection experiment." (2.2 ALMA observations); "We search for CO line emission in the ALMA image cubes before correcting for the primary beam response to ensure constant noise properties across the field of view." (3 RESULTS)
- Alternative: Excluding narrow-line (FWHM < 300 km s^-1) low-significance detections entirely was rejected to avoid biasing against low-mass galaxies; instead, both the high-significance subsample and full sample are analysed in parallel.  
  > "Therefore, we flag galaxies with a FWHM < 300 km s −1 as low significance detections unless the detection is confirmed by independent observations. These are still included in the further analysis to avoid a bias against low mass galaxies." (3 RESULTS)
- Alternative: Two K-band identified galaxies within < 2'' of Q0107-0232 from Crighton et al. (2013) were excluded as absorber hosts because they lack redshift information and show no CO emission, likely representing a chance alignment.  
  > "We do not find CO emission from the position of these galaxies. However, we note that there is no redshift information available for these galaxies and these could be a chance alignment. We therefore exclude these galaxies as the absorber hosts." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
- Alternative: Detailed forward morpho-kinematic modelling of HE 0515-4414 G1 was not performed because the CO emission is only marginally resolved and the MUSE [OII] emission has insufficient SNR.  
  > "The CO line emission is only marginally resolved and the [OII] emission is not significant enough to allow for a detailed analysis using state-of-the-art forward modelling techniques." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Some absorber metallicity estimates from the literature do not include ionization corrections and could therefore be somewhat higher.  
  > "We note that some of these metallicity estimates do not include ionisation corrections and could therefore be somewhat higher." (2.1 Target selection)
- Limitation (authors): Detections with narrow CO lines (FWHM < 300 km s^-1) are only significant over a few channels in the ALMA data and are flagged as low confidence when unconfirmed at other wavelengths.  
  > "While the narrow CO emission lines could trace low mass galaxies we also note that the detection is only significant over few channels in the ALMA data." (3 RESULTS)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming Milky Way type CO line ratios and alpha_CO = 4.36 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 yields an upper limit on molecular gas masses; CO(1-0) fluxes could be overestimated by a factor of 2-4 if ISM excitation is higher, and M_mol could be overestimated by up to a factor of 6 if a starburst alpha_CO = 0.8 applies.  
  > "The CO(1–0) line fluxes could therefore be overestimated by a factor of 2 – 4." (3.2 Molecular gas masses); "We caution, however, that the true molecular gas mass might be overestimated by up to a factor of six compared to 𝛼 CO = 0 . 8M⊙(K km s −1 pc 2 ) −1 , appropriate for starburst environments." (3.2 Molecular gas masses)
- Limitation (authors): The non-dust-corrected [OII] SFR for HE 0515-4414 G1 is a lower limit on the true SFR, making the 0.8 Gyr depletion time an upper limit.  
  > "Following the description by Kennicutt (1998) we measure a non dust-corrected SFR of 9.4 M ⊙ yr −1 which represents a lower limit on the true SFR. This translates to an upper limit for the molecular gas depletion time of 0.8 Gyr." (3.1 Notes on the individual quasar fields)
- Limitation (authors): The presence of low molecular gas mass galaxies below the ALMA detection limit at small impact parameters cannot be excluded.  
  > "We note that we cannot exclude the presence of low molecular gas mass galaxies below our detection limit at small impact parameters." (4.4 Do molecular gas absorbers probe disks of galaxies or pockets of dense gas in the CGM?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All three detections in the Q0107-0232 field and one in HE 0515-4414 are unconfirmed low-significance candidates without optical spectroscopic counterparts; if spurious, the multiplicity and detection rate claims weaken somewhat (though PG 1241+176 and Q2131-1207 still show overdensities).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sample size of six absorber fields is small, limiting statistical power in the correlation analyses.
