## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum image and H30alpha cube**: Continuum image from continuum bands plus line-free channels of the main SPW; line cube from the line SPW after continuum subtraction; Hogbom deconvolution with Briggs weighting  
  > "The continuum image was obtained using the continuum bands plus the channels of the main spectral window where the line is not present." (2 Observations); "The convolution method used the Hogbom algorithm with Briggs weighting." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA 5.4.0
   - parameter: Cell size 0.1 mas, image 2048 x 2048 pixels  
  > "the images have a cell size of 0.1 mas and a total size of" (2 Observations)
   - parameter: Cube velocity range -1187 to 1235 km/s, 1.262 km/s resolution  
  > "In velocity, the cube range goes from $-1187$ to 1235 km s$^{-1}$" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum flux densities and compare with 2012 data**: Integrate 230 GHz continuum over the region and over the compact source; compare with ALMA Cycle 0 at the same orbital position  
  > "The integrated 230 GHz continuum flux density of the observed region is $28.3 \pm 0.1$ Jy." (3.1 Continuum emission); "Therefore, both observations can be directly compared regarding the relative orbital position" (3.1 Continuum emission)
   - parameter: Binary period 2024 days; phases 13.61 (Cycle 5) and 12.70 (Cycle 0)  
  > "our ALMA Cycle 5 observations in 2017 Nov 20 corresponded to phase 13.61 and those of ALMA Cycle 0" (3.1 Continuum emission)
   - assumption: Binary orbit eccentricity about 0.9 so the two phases correspond to similar orbital positions  
  > "these two phases correspond to similar orbital positions of the companion star" (3.1 Continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Lower flux relative to 2012 attributed possibly to emission beyond the maximum recoverable scale  
  > "the lower flux density obtained in the present observations can be due to the existence of emission outside the maximum recovery scale of the ALMA configuration." (3.1 Continuum emission)
3. **Extract spectra and identify compact line components L1-L16**: Integrated spectra over the continuum region and a 0.09 arcsec radius compact region; iso-velocity channel maps, moment maps and peak maps; component spectra integrated over 66 mas radius  
  > "integrated over an area of $0.09\ensuremath{''}$ radius" (3.2 The H30alpha spectrum); "We identified sixteen of these individual components, labeled L1 to L16; their spectra, integrated over a region of 66 mas radius" (3.3 The strong narrow H30alpha line)
   - parameter: Channel width 1.262 km/s; moment-0 integrated between -25 and -116 km/s  
  > "Contour maps of the flux density, integrated over the line profile between $-25$ and $-116$ km s$^{-1}$ (zero momentum)" (3.3 The strong narrow H30alpha line)
   - assumption: LTE used for the emission measures in Table 1  
  > "assuming LTE conditions" (3.3 The strong narrow H30alpha line)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Table 1 estimated errors given (e.g. S(cont) = 0.04 Jy/beam)  
  > "Estimated errors: S(cont) = 0.04 Jy/beam" (Table 1)
4. **Decompose compact-source spectrum and derive thermal contribution**: Fit five Gaussians to the H30alpha spectrum within 90 mas radius; derive line EM and continuum EM from free-free relations (equations 1-6) and compare  
  > "was fitted by five Gaussian components that represent the negative and positive high velocity winds" (4.1 The Central Source); "equations 1-6 were used in the calculations" (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions)
   - parameter: Source size 180 mas  
  > "(in our case both equal to 180 mas)" (4.1 The Central Source)
   - assumption: Te = 1e4 K, optically thin to H30alpha  
  > "assuming a plasma with electron temperature $T_{\rm e}$ of $10^4$ K, optically thin to the H30$\alpha$ line radiation" (4.1 The Central Source)
   - fitting: Five Gaussian components (G1-G5); sampler and priors not stated  
  > "was fitted by five Gaussian components that represent the negative and positive high velocity winds" (4.1 The Central Source)
   - validation: Observed spectrum, fitted Gaussians and residuals shown in Figure 14  
  > "shows the observed spectrum, the fitted Gaussians and the residuals of the fitting" (4.1 The Central Source)
5. **Compare the wind emission with an ionized wind model**: Take total wind emission as twice the blueshifted component flux and compare with the Panagia and Felli free-free wind flux, then include latitude-dependent mass loss and velocity  
  > "we consider that the total wind emission is twice the value of the blueshifted component flux density" (4.2 The high velocity winds)
   - parameter: Mdot = 1e-3 Msun/yr, terminal velocity 500 km/s  
  > "Using $10^{-3}$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ for the mass loss rate $\dot{M}$ and 500 km s$^{-1}$ for the terminal wind velocity" (4.2 The high velocity winds)
   - assumption: Rotation axis coincides with the Homunculus axis; omega = 0.9  
  > "Assuming that the rotation axis of $\eta$ Car coincides with the Homunculus axis" (4.2 The high velocity winds); "assuming $\omega=0.9$" (4.2 The high velocity winds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted wind flux 3.2 Jy agrees with 3.7 Jy derived from observations  
  > "resulting in an expected flux density from the wind of 3.2 Jy, in agreement with the 3.7 Jy derived from the observations." (4.2 The high velocity winds)
6. **NLTE model of compact ionized sources L1 and L14**: Homogeneous ionized spheres; solve NLTE transfer for Te, ne and D matching continuum flux and maximum line intensity at several diameters  
  > "we assume that they are homogeneous ionized spheres, with diameter $D$ smaller than the HPBW" (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions); "We modeled two components, L1 and L14, because each one of them seems to arise from a single velocity region." (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions)
   - parameter: Te grid 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 x 1e4 K, ne solved to match continuum flux  
  > "three values for $T_e:$ (1.0, 1.1 and 1.2)$\times 10^4$ K" (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions)
   - assumption: Sources are homogeneous ionized spheres with diameter below the HPBW  
  > "we assume that they are homogeneous ionized spheres, with diameter $D$ smaller than the HPBW" (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions)
   - fitting: Grid in Te and diameter with interpolation to match model and observed line flux (no sampler or priors)  
  > "We then obtained, by interpolation, the values of $T_e$ and $n_e$ for which the model and observed line flux density coincide." (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions)
   - validation: ne compatible with earlier ALMA Cycle 0 modelling; turnover frequency close to observing frequency  
  > "The values of $n_e$ are compatible with what was found in" (4.3 The physical parameters of the NW compact regions)
7. **Test Pfgamma emission versus dust in the infrared blobs**: Scale H30alpha flux by emissivity ratio and frequency ratio to predict Pfgamma flux of a typical compact source and of the IR blob  
  > "To support this assumption we calculated the expected Pf$\gamma$ line flux density" (4.4 Comparison with observations at other wavelengths)
   - parameter: Emissivity ratio 8.7e3; source diameter 50 mas  
  > "The ratio of the emissivity coefficients is $8.7\times 10^3$" (4.4 Comparison with observations at other wavelengths); "for a source of 50 mas diameter" (4.4 Comparison with observations at other wavelengths)
   - assumption: Ionized H density 1e7 cm-3 and temperature 1e4 K for emissivities  
  > "for ionized H density of $10^7$ cm$^{-3}$ and temperature $10^4$ K given by (cite: sto95)" (4.4 Comparison with observations at other wavelengths)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted Pfgamma flux of 190 Jy for the IR blob compared with observed IR emission  
  > "showing that the observed IR emission can be explained by H line emission, and that the presence of dust emission is not required." (4.4 Comparison with observations at other wavelengths)
8. **Derive proper motions and ejection epochs of Weigelt blobs**: Linear fit to positions of blobs D (L1), C (L2) and B (L3) at previous epochs plus the ALMA position  
  > "The velocities, obtained from a linear fitting of their positions are" (4.5 The Weigelt blobs)
   - parameter: Distance 2300 pc; 6 mas/yr corresponds to 67 km/s  
  > "The linear velocity corresponding to an angular velocity of 6 mas yr$^{-1}$ is 67 km s$^{-1}$" (4.5 The Weigelt blobs)
   - assumption: Tentative identification of blob B with L3; blobs identified from position angle and distance  
  > "We tentatively identified Weigelt blob B with L3, although it was only present in the (cite: wei86) observations." (4.5 The Weigelt blobs)
   - fitting: Linear fit of position versus epoch  
  > "The velocities, obtained from a linear fitting of their positions are" (4.5 The Weigelt blobs)
   - validation: Ejection epochs compared with periastron epochs; only L1-L3 have compatible PA and distances  
  > "the only sources in the data with position angles and distances compatible with those of the blobs are L1, L2 and L3." (4.5 The Weigelt blobs)
9. **Test whether the 356.7 GHz line is H40delta rather than HCO+**: Compare the line rest frequency and implied velocity; compute H40delta intensity of L1 and L14 with the same NLTE procedure as H30alpha  
  > "we calculated the intensity of the H40$\delta$ line in the compact sources L1 and L14, as it was done for the H30$\alpha$ line." (4.6 HCO+ or H40delta emission line?)
   - parameter: H40delta rest frequency 356.658 GHz; implied velocity -63.7 km/s  
  > "the corresponding velocity would be $-63.7$ km s$^{-1}$" (4.6 HCO+ or H40delta emission line?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Superposed profiles of compact sources can explain the previously detected line profile  
  > "the profile of the line detected by (cite: bor19) can be explained by the superposition of the line profiles of the compact sources" (4.6 HCO+ or H40delta emission line?)

## Qualitative findings

- The 230 GHz continuum is resolved into a compact core at the binary position plus weaker extended emission to the NW  
  > "For the first time, the 230 GHz continuum image is resolved into a compact core, coincident with the binary system position, and a weaker extended structure to the NW of the compact source." (abstract)
- High positive and negative velocity H30alpha emission is aligned with the Homunculus axis  
  > "The positive and negative high-velocity emission seem to be aligned with the Homunculus axis." (3.5 High velocity H30alpha emission)
- The central source continuum can be explained by thermal processes  
  > "showing that all the continuum emission can be explained by thermal processes, at least during the orbital phases in which the stars are farther apart." (4.1 The Central Source)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous ALMA observations did not resolve the continuum or lines; new data have much higher resolution  
  > "In this paper, we present new ALMA observations with an order of magnitude higher resolution" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Thermal contribution estimated from the H30alpha line to test for non-thermal radio emission  
  > "To investigate this question, we calculate the thermal contribution of the ionized plasma" (4.1 The Central Source)
- Alternative: Considered whether the 356.7 GHz line reported as HCO+(4-3) is instead a hydrogen recombination line (H40delta) and concluded it is  
  > "and see if it could be instead a hydrogen recombination line" (4.6 HCO+ or H40delta emission line?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Lower flux than in 2012 may be due to emission outside the maximum recoverable scale  
  > "the lower flux density obtained in the present observations can be due to the existence of emission outside the maximum recovery scale of the ALMA configuration." (3.1 Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Unclear whether the narrow line in the compact-source spectrum belongs to the compact source  
  > "It is not clear if the narrow line belongs to the compact source or if it only represents the contribution of the superimposed extended NW region." (3.2 The H30alpha spectrum)
- Limitation (authors): Blob proper motions depend on identification of Weigelt blobs with ALMA sources  
  > "Of course, this result depends on the accuracy of the identification of the Weigelt blobs with the compact sources detected by ALMA" (4.5 The Weigelt blobs)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single 30-minute execution block; no uv-coverage or imaging parameters (robust, threshold, masks) given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cell size of 0.1 mas looks inconsistent with a 2048-pixel image covering 0.8 arcsec
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Proper-motion fits rest on three tentative blob identifications with scarce earlier-epoch positions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): NLTE modelling is done for only two of the 16 sources with a grid and interpolation, no uncertainties or code reference
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Continuum flux shortfall versus 2012 may reflect missing short spacings, which also affects EM comparisons
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Component positions and fluxes are beam-sized estimates (sources unresolved) so EM values assume source size equal to beam
