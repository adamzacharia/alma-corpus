## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and image the ACA CO(3-2) data**: Calibrated MS from the standard pipeline scriptForPI; cubes cleaned with tclean using interactive masks, no continuum subtraction; spectra from primary-beam corrected cubes  
  > "We obtained the calibrated measurement set by running the standard pipeline through the" (2 Observations); "Deconvolution and cleaning were executed using the task" (2 Observations); "All spectra were extracted from the primary-beam corrected cubes." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA v.5.6, tclean
   - parameter: native channel width 5.45 km/s  
  > "The data were imaged at their native spectral resolution of $\Delta v_{\rm channel} =5.45$ km s$^{-1}$" (2 Observations)
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "We applied Briggs weighting and set the robust parameter equal to 0.5." (2 Observations)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Reduce the archival ALMA CO(3-2) data and make a tapered cube**: CASA pipeline, uv-plane continuum subtraction with a linear polynomial, Briggs imaging, plus a 4 arcsec tapered cube (ALMA-t)  
  > "We reduced them using the CASA pipeline (v.5.1)" (2 Observations); "with a linear polynomial fit, estimating the continuum from the $\nu_{obs}<107.2$ GHz and $\nu_{obs}>107.6$ GHz channels" (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA pipeline v.5.1, uvcontsub
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; uv taper 4 arcsec; maximum spectral resolution 22 km/s  
  > "visibilities with Briggs weighting and robust=0.5" (2 Observations); "tapering of $4\ensuremath{''}$ to enhance any extended components" (2 Observations); "The maximum spectral resolution is 22 km s$^{-1}$." (2 Observations)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Check for ACA continuum**: Collapsed line-free spw17 map to place an upper limit on the continuum  
  > "We verified this by using a line-free spectral window (spw17) adjacent to the one containing the CO(3-2) line" (Appendix A.1)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 3 sigma upper limit S_106GHz < 0.5 mJy/beam  
  > "Using this map, we can place an ACA $3\sigma$ upper limit on the continuum level of $\rm S_{106~GHz}<0.5~mJy~beam^{-1}$" (Appendix A.1)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Upper limit is consistent with the ALMA detection of Circosta+21  
  > "which is consistent with the detection by (cite: Circosta+21)" (Appendix A.1)
4. **Extract spectra in apertures, fit Gaussians, build the curve of growth**: CO(3-2) spectra extracted from several apertures of ALMA (r = 0.6-10 arcsec) and ACA (r > 10 arcsec) cubes, fitted with Gaussians; fluxes plotted against aperture radius  
  > "For small apertures probed by the ALMA data ($r_{aperture}=0.6\ensuremath{''}-5\ensuremath{''}$), the CO(3-2) spectrum exhibits a single component" (Appendix A.3); "Apertures with $r>10\ensuremath{''}$ are only reliably probed by the ACA data." (Appendix A.3)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: ACA spectra rebinned to 160 km/s; single broad Gaussian per aperture; ALMA-t fluxes from elliptical apertures (a=15, b=10 arcsec and a=20, b=15 arcsec)  
  > "The ACA spectra were rebinned to $\Delta v_{channel}=160$ km s$^{-1}$ in order to maximise the S/N." (Appendix A.3); "All spectra were fitted using a single broad Gaussian component" (Appendix A.3)
   - assumption: The additional component at v~1500 km/s is too faint to fit and is excluded from the single-Gaussian fits  
  > "None of these single-Gaussian fits include the additional component centred at $v\sim1500$ km s$^{-1}$" (Appendix A.3)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits, no sampler or priors stated  
  > "A Gaussian fit returns a total flux of $8.7\pm2.6$ Jy km s$^{-1}$" (Results)
   - validation: Compared ACA and ALMA spectra from different apertures; flux curve plateaus beyond r_ap ~ 25 arcsec  
  > "by comparing ACA and ALMA spectra extracted from different apertures" (Results); "and it plateaus for larger apertures" (Results)
5. **Model the ACA uv visibilities**: uv visibilities modelled as a circular Gaussian plus a point source  
  > "the data are modelled using a circular Gaussian function with $\rm FWHM=300\pm80$ kpc, in addition to a point source (the unresolved ISM) which contributes very little to the total flux" (Results)
   - parameter: Gaussian FWHM = 300+-80 kpc  
  > "the data are modelled using a circular Gaussian function with $\rm FWHM=300\pm80$ kpc, in addition to a point source" (Results)
   - assumption: Unresolved ISM represented by a point source  
  > "in addition to a point source (the unresolved ISM)" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Size consistent with the curve-of-growth estimate; zero-baseline flux consistent with the spectral flux within its large error  
  > "This size estimate is consistent with the analysis of the" (Results); "which is consistent, within its (large) error, with the CGM flux measured from the spectrum" (Discussion and conclusions)
6. **Bound the molecular gas mass of the CGM (CO)**: Lower limits from the J=3 column density and from LTE; upper limit assuming optically thick CO, alpha_CO(1-0) = 3.6 and r31 = 0.5, using L'CO(3-2) = (24+-7)e10 K km/s pc^2  
  > "we use our fiducial estimate of the CGM CO(3-2) luminosity, $L^{\prime}_{\rm CO(3-2)}=(24\pm7)\times10^{10}$ K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$" (Appendix A.3); "If we assume $r_{31}\sim0.5$, combined with the $\alpha_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ quoted above, we obtain a total molecular gas mass of $1.7\times10^{12}~M_{\odot}$" (Appendix A.3)
   - parameter: LTE with T_kin = 50 K; g_3 = 7; E_1/k_B = 5.53 K; E_3/k_B = 33.19 K; mu = 1.36  
  > "Here we have assumed $T_{kin}=50$ K, which corresponds to the lower boundary of the cold neutral medium temperature range." (Appendix A.3); "$E_1/k_B = 5.53$ K and $E_3/k_B = 33.19$ K" (Appendix A.3)
   - assumption: [CO/H2] = 1e-4  
  > "We have assumed a typical CO abundance $\rm [CO/H_2]=10^{-4}$" (Appendix A.3)
   - assumption: All CO(3-2) emission treated equally (ISM and CGM share the same conditions)  
  > "We treat all of the CO(3-2) emission equally" (Appendix A.3)
   - assumption: Upper limit assumes optically thick CO throughout the CGM  
  > "by assuming optically thick CO emission throughout the entire CGM reservoir" (Appendix A.3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Convert the APEX [CI](2-1) non-detection into limits**: 3 sigma flux upper limit from the rms, assuming the same line profile as CO(3-2) (FWHM ~ 1000 km/s); mass limit from Tex and X_CI  
  > "lines share the same line profile, we can assume" (Appendix A.2); "If we adopt these values we obtain $M_{mol}< 9 \times 10^{11}~M_{\odot}$" (Appendix A.3)
   - software: CLASS, GILDAS
   - parameter: flux density conversion 40+-7 Jy/K; rms 7.0+-1.2 mJy per 50 km/s  
  > "we adopted a factor of $40\pm7$ Jy K$^{-1}$" (2 Observations); "down to a sensitivity of $7.0\pm1.2$ mJy per $\Delta v=50$ km s$^{-1}$ channel" (Appendix A.2)
   - assumption: [CI](2-1)/[CI](1-0) = 0.47 (Tex = 26 K) and X_CI = 1.6e-5 from literature averages  
  > "$X_{\rm CI}= 1.6\times 10^{-5}$ for the ISM of main sequence galaxies at $z>1$" (Appendix A.3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the CO-based limit; APEX FoV (27 arcsec) is smaller than the halo and an r=15 arcsec aperture holds half the CO flux, so the two limits are consistent  
  > "hence the two upper limits are consistent" (Appendix A.3)
8. **Make moment maps of the ACA cube**: CASA immoments with a velocity window and flux threshold  
  > "by selecting the velocity range $v\in[-300, 300]$ km s$^{-1}$and masking pixels below a flux threshold of 0.04 Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$" (Appendix A.5)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: v in [-300, 300] km/s; threshold 0.04 Jy/beam km/s  
  > "by selecting the velocity range $v\in[-300, 300]$ km s$^{-1}$and masking pixels below a flux threshold of 0.04 Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$" (Appendix A.5)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors caution that the ACA beam dilutes kinematic signatures and the features cannot be studied in detail  
  > "since the very large beam of ACA would dilute any kinematic signature of kiloparsec-scale outflows" (Appendix A.5)

## Qualitative findings

- The CO(3-2) emission is spread over tens of arcsec with a central peak offset ~3 arcsec south-east of the AGN and secondary peaks north, north-east and south-east; the authors say it is the most extended molecular CGM reservoir mapped  
  > "To our knowledge, this is the most extended molecular CGM reservoir that has ever been mapped." (Results)
- Beyond ~5 arcsec a second line at v~450 km/s dominates, and for r_ap>15 arcsec the ACA profile is very broad (v~-400 to 1000 km/s)  
  > "Larger apertures collect significant additional flux due to a second line centred at $v\sim450$ km s$^{-1}$" (Results)
- The moment maps show no regular velocity gradient and velocity dispersion peaks not centred on the AGN, which may favour accreting streams over outflows, though the authors caution against over-interpretation  
  > "The velocity map (middle panel of Fig. [9](#fig:co32_aca_moment_maps)) does not display a regular velocity gradient." (Appendix A.5); "This may favour accreting streams over the AGN-outflow interpretation for explaining the kinematics of the molecular CGM" (Appendix A.5)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The 1 arcsec snapshot ALMA data cannot probe large apertures, so the ACA data are used for scales beyond 10 arcsec  
  > "Apertures with $r>10\ensuremath{''}$ are only reliably probed by the ACA data." (Appendix A.3)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Stripping from satellites in a dense environment set aside because current data disfavour an over-density of galaxies  
  > "we set aside the hypothesis that this cold CGM reservoir coincides with a dense environment" (Discussion and conclusions)
- Alternative: AGN-driven outflow origin of the kinematics weighed against accreting streams; not resolved  
  > "This may favour accreting streams over the AGN-outflow interpretation for explaining the kinematics of the molecular CGM" (Appendix A.5)
- How they decided it was enough: The halo extent is set where the aperture flux curve of growth plateaus  
  > "and it plateaus for larger apertures" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral analysis limited by low S/N  
  > "Although our spectral analysis is limited by the low signal-to-noise (S/N) of the data" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA dataset has poor coverage of the redshifted side of the CO line  
  > "it does not provide good coverage of the redshifted side of the CO line" (2 Observations)
- Limitation (authors): ACA beam is too large to analyse kinematics in detail  
  > "Due to the poor resolution and low S/N of the data, none of these features can be studied in detail." (Appendix A.5)
- Limitation (authors): Mass estimates are highly uncertain, with no constraint on the gas physical conditions  
  > "The uncertainty is even higher in the absence of any (theoretical or observational) constraint on the physical conditions of the gas" (Discussion and conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one CO transition and a 9.3 arcsec beam: mass limits span two orders of magnitude and the halo could include blended unrelated sources or foreground/background emission at the same frequency
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux totals depend on single broad Gaussian fits to rebinned (160 km/s) low-S/N spectra; ACA cleaning used interactive masks that are not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA 12 m data are used only as a different dataset, not combined with the ACA data, so the ISM/CGM split relies on the Circosta+21 flux
- Caveat (inferred, ours): uv-fit uncertainty is large (flux 16+-11 Jy km/s), so size and flux from it are loose
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extended flux of 8.7 Jy km/s is compared to a point-source ISM flux of 0.63 Jy km/s, so a small flux-scale or baseline effect in a 5.2 h ACA dataset could matter
