## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, combine, and image 1.3 mm continuum and 12CO(2-1) line cube**: Calibrate Cycle 6 and Cycle 7 execution blocks with the ALMA Science Pipeline, combine the three measurement sets with CASA concat using visweightscale, image the 1.3 mm continuum with tclean (MFS) and fit source with imfit, subtract continuum in the uv plane with uvcontsub, and image the 12CO(2-1) cube with tclean (Högbom deconvolver)  
  > "We combined the visibility data from all three MS into a combined and calibrated MS using the `concat` task in CASA." (2.1 Data Reduction); "We produced the combined continuum image at 1.3mm using the CASA `tclean` task in multifrequency synthesis mode (cite: Rau11), combining the continuum SPWs with line-free channels from the targeted SPWs of all three MS." (2.2 Creating the Combined 1.3 mm Continuum Image); "The continuum was modeled with a linear power-law function and subtracted from the combined and calibrated visibilities in the *uv* plane to isolate the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) line emission. ## Qualitative findings

- The 12CO(2-1) emission in NGC 4061 forms a regularly rotating, unwarped, geometrically thin circumnuclear disk aligned with the prominent HST dust lane, without significant non-circular motions.  
  > "The molecular gas forms a regularly rotating circumnuclear disk aligned with the prominent dust lane seen in HST imaging." (Abstract); "The best-fitting KINMS model using the SKYSAMPLER tool yields residual velocities of $|V_{\rm residual}| \lesssim 15$ km s$^{-1}$ ($\lesssim$4%) across the CND, comparable to the spectral channel width of the reduced ALMA cube ($\approx$10 km s$^{-1}$), implying an absence of significant non-circular motions." (4.4 Results)
- The exceptionally large velocity dispersion (~459 km s^-1) reported in Hypercat and its implied ~1.2 x 10^10 M_odot black hole mass are ruled out by the ALMA CO kinematics.  
  > "Our results resolve the long-standing discrepancy between previous indirect mass estimates based on conflicting stellar velocity dispersion measurements and demonstrate that the exceptionally large dispersion reported in the literature is likely spurious." (Abstract); "Although their favored *M/L*$_{\rm F814W}$ values decrease with increasing BH mass, both models systematically overpredict the rotation of the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) CND—either within the inner $0.6\ensuremath{''}$ for $M_{\rm BH} = 2.0\times10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ or across the entire disk for $M_{\rm BH} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ M$_{\odot}$." (4.6 The Reliability of Our Measurements)
- Observed F555W-F814W color variations in the nuclear region are dominated by dust extinction rather than intrinsic stellar population gradients, and the SMBH mass is insensitive to plausible radial M/L variations.  
  > "This correspondence indicates that the observed color variations are dominated by dust extinction rather than intrinsic changes in the stellar population." (4.5.5 Mass-to-Light Ratio Model Variations); "These findings demonstrate that our ALMA-based measurement of $M_{\rm BH}$ is only weakly sensitive to the assumed stellar mass-to-light ratio profile, including plausible variations driven by dust attenuation or stellar population changes across the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) CND." (4.5.5 Mass-to-Light Ratio Model Variations)
- Scaling relations (M_BH-sigma and M_BH-L_K,bulge) derived purely from the 35 molecular-gas-based SMBH measurements agree well with Kormendy & Ho (2013) but diverge from van den Bosch (2016).  
  > "The $M_{\rm BH}$–$\sigma$ and $M_{\rm BH}$–$L_{K,\rm bulge}$ relations derived from molecular-gas-based measurements alone are consistent with the relations of (cite: Kormendy13), but not with those of (cite: Bosch2016)." (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 4061 has a well-defined dust disk and rapidly rotating gas but conflicting indirect SMBH mass estimates (1.4e9 vs 1.2e10 M_odot); combining high-resolution Cycle 6 (C43-7) and medium-resolution Cycle 7 (C43-5) ALMA 12CO(2-1) data achieves a 0.16'' x 0.13'' beam that resolves the SMBH sphere of influence (~0.12'') while recovering extended CND emission.  
  > "Despite these strong dynamical signatures, no published, peer-reviewed, high-precision $M_{\rm BH}$ measurement yet exists for NGC 4061. The geometry of its dust disk and the observed gas rotation provide a compelling case for high-spatial-resolution dynamical modeling (stellar or gas) to refine the mass estimate and test for systematic biases." (Introduction); "The resulting data set attains a synthesized beam of $0.16\ensuremath{''} \times 0.13\ensuremath{''}$, corresponding to $83.2 \times 67.6$ pc$^{2}$, which closely matches the estimated SOI radius of the SMBH ($r_{\rm soi} \approx 0.12\ensuremath{''}$)." (5 Conclusions)
- Why this method: 3D forward modeling with KinMS incorporates beam smearing, binning, and projection effects; using both SkySampler (data-driven CLEAN components, avoiding spatial distribution assumptions) and an analytic Gaussian disk tests sensitivity to gas morphology, while rescaling noise by (2N)^0.25 accounts for interferometric pixel covariance.  
  > "KinMS generates a mock data cube by simulating the gas distribution (Section 4.2) and kinematics while incorporating observational effects such as beam smearing, spatial and velocity binning, and LOS projection." (4.1 KINMS Tool); "SKYSAMPLER constructs the molecular gas clouds in the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) CND of NGC 4061 directly from the CLEAN components of the data cube, allowing the model to fit only the gas kinematics without assuming a specific spatial distribution." (4.2.1 Gas Distribution Assumed by SKYSAMPLER); "This scaling yields more realistic fit uncertainties by accounting for underestimated systematic effects commonly encountered in Bayesian analyses of large datasets such as ALMA (cite: Ngo2025a)" (4.3 Bayesian Inference and Priors)
- Alternative: Dust mass component and dark matter halo in the total mass model: both excluded because the dust mass is negligible and the compact (< 2'') CND region has negligible dark matter contribution.  
  > "Given the compactness of the continuum emission as seen in panel A of Figure 2, much smaller than the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) CND, and the negligible dust mass inferred from the HST optical image, we excluded the dust component from the total mass model." (2.5 Interstellar Medium (ISM) Mass Model); "We converted the deconvolved light-MGE into a stellar mass model for NGC 4061 by adopting a constant mass-to-light ratio parameter (from the best-fit value in Section 4.4) and neglecting the dark matter contribution in the central region (cite: Cappellari13a), given the compact extent of the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) disk." (3.2 Galaxy Mass Model)
- Alternative: No-SMBH model (M_BH = 0 M_odot) and high-mass SMBH models (M_BH = 2.0e9, 4.5e9, and 1.2e10 M_odot): tested and rejected because M_BH = 0 fails to reproduce the inner ~80 km s^-1 velocity rise while the higher-mass models overpredict the central or global disk rotation.  
  > "The first assumes no SMBH ($M_{\rm BH}$ $=0$ M$_{\odot}$) with $M/L_{\rm F814W}=3.6$ (M$_{\odot}$/L$_\odot$), which reproduces the extended CND kinematics beyond $r>0.4\ensuremath{''}$ but fails to match the slightly central rise in rotation velocity ($\approx$80 km s$^{-1}$)." (4.4 Results); "The second adopts a larger fixed SMBH mass of $M_{\rm BH}$ $=4.5\times10^9$ M$_{\odot}$ with $M/L_{\rm F814W}=3.0$ (M$_{\odot}$/L$_\odot$); it similarly fits the outer kinematics but overpredicts the central velocity increase at small radii." (4.4 Results); "Although their favored *M/L*$_{\rm F814W}$ values decrease with increasing BH mass, both models systematically overpredict the rotation of the $^{12}$CO(2$-$1) CND—either within the inner $0.6\ensuremath{''}$ for $M_{\rm BH} = 2.0\times10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ or across the entire disk for $M_{\rm BH} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ M$_{\odot}$." (4.6 The Reliability of Our Measurements)
- Alternative: Thick disk geometry, radially varying turbulent velocity dispersion sigma_gas(r), radially varying M/L_F814W(r), unmasked dust in F814W, and F555W stellar mass MGE: tested as alternatives to evaluate systematic uncertainties.  
  > "Several additional sources of uncertainty are inherent to dynamical modeling, including (i) the adopted distance to NGC 4061, (ii) the assumption of a thick disk (implemented via the $z$-coordinate perpendicular to the disk plane), (iii) the turbulent velocity dispersion of the gas, (iv) the disk inclination, (v) possible radial variations in the mass-to-light ratio, (vi) the construction of the stellar mass model without dust masking, and (vii) the use of an alternative photometric band." (4.5 Uncertainties)
- How they decided it was enough: Achieved resolving power zeta = 2 r_soi / theta_FWHM ~ 2 (matching the criterion zeta >= 2 for precise M_BH measurements), verified Gaussian-like MCMC posterior convergence over 10^5 iterations, confirmed low velocity residuals (<= 15 km s^-1), and ran extensive systematic tests across 7 potential error sources.  
  > "All distributions exhibit Gaussian-like shapes, demonstrating that the MCMC optimization of our KINMS models achieved robust convergence." (4.4 Results); "Observations with $\zeta \geq 2$ (i.e., $\theta_{\rm FWHM} \leq r_{\rm soi}$) are generally sufficient to yield precise $M_{\rm BH}$ determinations (cite: North19; Boizelle21)." (4.6 The Reliability of Our Measurements); "In the case of NGC 4061, our ALMA data correspond to $\zeta \approx 2$, indicating that the dynamical-$M_{\rm BH}$ measurement presented in this work is robust." (4.6 The Reliability of Our Measurements)
- Limitation (authors): The wide range of published distances to NGC 4061 (68–112 Mpc in NED) introduces a ~37% systematic uncertainty in M_BH, dominating the total error budget.  
  > "Since the inferred $M_{\rm BH}$ scales linearly with the assumed distance ($M_{\rm BH} \propto D$), the wide range of distance estimates for NGC 4061 (68–112 Mpc; NED)—primarily based on optical and near-infrared light curves of the Type Ia supernova SN 2008bf—constitutes the dominant source of uncertainty in our $M_{\rm BH}$ measurement." (4.5.1 Distances); "The spread in published distances introduces a systematic uncertainty of $\approx$37% in $M_{\rm BH}$." (4.5.1 Distances)
- Limitation (authors): Parameter degeneracies exist between M_BH and M/L_F814W, between y_cen and v_off (due to the beam size relative to the continuum peak), between inclination i and M/L_F814W, and between flux normalization f and sigma_gas.  
  > "While the other model parameters are well constrained, a clear anti-correlation between $M_{\rm BH}$ and $M/L_{\rm F814W}$ is evident (see both Figure 9 and Figure 10)." (4.4 Results); "resulting from the large beam size when the kinematic center is tied to the peak of the spatially resolved continuum emission (Section 2.2). Higher angular resolution observation will tighter constrain the CND disk center and suppress this degeneracy." (4.4 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Adopting the HST F555W band instead of F814W for the stellar mass model introduces an additional ~20% uncertainty in M_BH due to stronger dust attenuation in F555W.  
  > "Overall, adopting the F555W-based stellar mass model introduces an additional uncertainty of approximately 20% in the inferred SMBH mass for NGC 4061, likely reflecting the greater sensitivity of the F555W band to dust attenuation compared to the F814W band." (4.5.7 Using the Alternative F555W MGE Stellar Mass Model)
- Limitation (authors): Direct event-horizon-scale imaging of the NGC 4061 SMBH (~0.4 uas) is impossible even with ngEHT (~10 uas resolution).  
  > "We also should note that a $\sim$$10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ SMBH at $\sim$107 Mpc has an event-horizon angular size of only $\sim$0.4 $\mu$as, far below the $\sim$10 $\mu$as reach of ngEHT (cite: Johnson2023; Shlentsova2024), making direct horizon-scale imaging impossible at that distance." (4.6 The Reliability of Our Measurements)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistency in the reported 1.3 mm continuum RMS noise: Section 2.2 text states sigma_cont = 120 uJy beam^-1, whereas Table 1 lists Sensitivity = 0.02 mJy per beam (20 uJy beam^-1).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Section 2.3 states 'The final self-calibrated, cleaned cube shares the same synthesized beam as the 1.3 mm continuum image', but no self-calibration procedure is mentioned in Section 2.1 or 2.2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 4.5.6 (dust-unmasked F814W MGE test), the derived M_BH drops to (7.41 +/- 1.92) x 10^8 M_odot and (6.92 +/- 1.16) x 10^8 M_odot (~37-41% lower than the fiducial 1.17 x 10^9 M_odot) while M/L_F814W jumps from 3.46 to ~5.5, yet the text characterizes this as 'close agreement' and 'negligible impact'.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 2.4, the text states that velocity dispersion outside the central boxy region is 0 <= sigma_LOS <= 70 km s^-1 and that 'Section 4.4 best-fitting dynamical models yield an intrinsic velocity dispersion of 18-22 km s^-1', whereas Table 4 and Section 4.5.3 report best-fit sigma_gas = 13.91 km s^-1 (SkySampler) and 15.51 km s^-1 (Gaussian).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 4.7, the M_BH-sigma_e regression uses 35 galaxies, but Table 7 lists Fairall 49 with no sigma_e value ('-') and NGC 4061 with no sigma_e uncertainty ('290 +/- ...'); how missing values or uncertainties were handled in scipy.stats.linregress (which is an unweighted ordinary least squares routine) is not explained.
