## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure UV emission and black hole properties**: Normalise to z-band photometry; fit local power-law plus Fe II continua and 1–3 Gaussian line components; derive redshifts, virial masses, bolometric luminosities and Eddington ratios.  
  > "We construct a physically motivated model for the quasar UV emission spectrum, comprising a power law combined with a Fe II pseudo-continuum for the continuum, and multiple Gaussian components for the emission lines." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum); "The black hole masses are estimated from the (cite: vestergaard_mass_2009) and (cite: vestergaard_determining_2006) scaling relations for Mg ii and C iv lines, respectively." (Emission redshift, black hole mass, and bolometric luminosity); "From the 3000 Å monochromatic luminosity we evaluate the quasar bolometric luminosity $L_{\text{bol}}$ using the bolometric correction from (cite: richards_spectral_2006)" (Emission redshift, black hole mass, and bolometric luminosity)
   - software: ASTROCOOK
   - parameter: 50 km s−1 bins  
  > "we use ASTROCOOK to re-sample the X-Shooter spectra into 50 km s$^{-1}$-width bins to increase the S/N." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum)
   - parameter: 1000 realisations with Gaussian channel-flux errors  
  > "For each source, we generate N=1000 realisations of the C iv and Mg ii line profiles and underlying continua" (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum); "assuming that the flux in each spectral channel follows a Gaussian distribution with standard deviation equal to the associated spectral error." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum)
   - assumption: Spectroscopic–photometric differences primarily reflect slit losses  
  > "We attribute potential discrepancies between spectroscopic and photometric data primarily to slit losses rather than intrinsic quasar variability over different times of observations." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum)
   - assumption: Systematic uncertainties: black hole masses 0.5 dex; bolometric luminosities 0.3 dex  
  > "Black hole masses and bolometric luminosities are also affected by systematic uncertainties of 0.5 dex and 0.3 dex, respectively." (Quasar properties from UV emission lines)
   - fitting: Continuum subtraction followed by 1–3 Gaussian components; select lowest reduced χ²; median and standard deviation across realisations  
  > "After the subtraction of the best-fit continuum model from the data, we fit the residual emission lines with one to three Gaussian components." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum); "We then select the line model with the lowest reduced $\chi ^2$ value." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum); "adopt the median and standard deviation of the resulting continuum and line parameter distributions as best-fit values and associated errors, respectively." (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum)
   - validation: Check i/z photometry; compare overlapping XQz5 mass estimates after accounting for different Mg II calibrations  
  > "We verify the flux calibration against *i* and *z* photometry from Pan-STARRS DR1 (cite: chambers_pan-starrs1_2016)" (Modelling the quasar UV emission spectrum); "our black hole mass estimates are consistent with those provided in (cite: lai_xqz5_2024), once accounted for the different adopted Mg ii mass calibrations." (Emission redshift, black hole mass, and bolometric luminosity)
2. **Identify and group associated narrow absorbers**: Fit the continuum, identify doublets, manually review systems, fit Voigt profiles, retain significant associated C IV absorption and group nearby components.  
  > "We employ the NEW_SYSTEM_FROM_LIKELIHOOD recipe in ASTROCOOK to detect absorption peaks and estimate the probability that they correspond to the two components of the same doublet." (Identification and fit of metal narrow absorption lines); "This automatic doublet identification is followed by a manual review of the identified systems, where we add or remove components as necessary." (Identification and fit of metal narrow absorption lines); "Lastly, we group individual absorption components closer than 200 km s$^{-1}$ into single absorption systems" (Construction of the final absorber sample)
   - software: ASTROCOOK, FLUX_CLIP, NEW_SYSTEM_FROM_LIKELIHOOD
   - parameter: Associated systems within 10,000 km s−1 of z_em  
  > "We now focus on the associated high-ionisation absorbers that lie within $10,000$ km s$^{-1}$ of the quasar systemic redshift $z_{em}$" (Construction of the final absorber sample)
   - parameter: Initial doublets >2σ; final C IV λ1548 detection 3σ; minimum b = 7 km s−1  
  > "The system is retained if the doublet is identified with a significance greater than 2$\sigma$." (Identification and fit of metal narrow absorption lines); "we require a $3 \sigma$ detection of the C iv$\lambda 1548$ line" (Construction of the final absorber sample); "We impose a minimum $b$ value of $7$ km s$^{-1}$" (Identification and fit of metal narrow absorption lines)
   - assumption: All detected C IV+N V NAL systems treated as intrinsic ionised outflows  
  > "we will assume that all our detected C iv+N v NAL systems are intrinsic, specifically tracing ionised outflow." (Combining absorption and emission measurements)
   - fitting: Voigt profiles; free z_abs, log(N) and Doppler b  
  > "ASTROCOOK fits absorption lines using Voigt profiles, where the free parameters are the system absorption redshift $z_{abs}$, the ion column density $\log (N)$ and the Doppler parameter $b$." (Identification and fit of metal narrow absorption lines)
   - validation: Nearest-neighbour separation distribution supports 200 km s−1 grouping; at least three C IV+N V systems show partial coverage  
  > "the cumulative fraction steeply increases up to the value of 200 km s$^{-1}$, then it reaches a plateau." (Construction of the final absorber sample); "at least three systems in our sample of 19 C iv+N v absorbers display clear signs of partial coverage" (Combining absorption and emission measurements)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use available ALMA [C II] observations to derive host dynamical masses and assess the impact of quasar winds.  
  > "we exploit the ALMA data to derive the galaxy dynamical masses through the study of the \[C II\]$158 \mu$m emission line." (Summary and conclusions)
- Why this method: Fix disk inclination because the sources are only marginally resolved.  
  > "The choice of a fixed inclination angle is dictated by the fact that our sources are only marginally resolved." ([C II] luminosities and dynamical masses)
- Alternative: Zanella and Madden molecular-mass calibrations yield masses more than one order of magnitude higher than the adopted Salvestrini calibration.  
  > "more than one order of magnitude lower than those derived using the calibrations by (cite: zanella_c_2018) or (cite: madden_tracing_2020)." ([C II] luminosities and dynamical masses)
- Alternative: Much smaller disk inclinations could reconcile the local mass relation; inclination alone is considered an unlikely explanation.  
  > "A drastic decrease in $i$ by $\sim 25$° (and nearly $40$° for J0306+1853) could reconcile the deviation of our $z \sim 5$ quasars from the local relation" (Host galaxy – black hole (co)evolution); "it is unlikely that the assumption on the inclination angle alone accounts for this discrepancy" (Host galaxy – black hole (co)evolution)
- Alternative: Local analytic UV fits were rejected for BAL characterisation because they cover insufficient spectral range and would be strongly model dependent.  
  > "The local, analytic fits performed in Section [2.4](#section-x-shooter-modeling-uv-spectra) do not cover a sufficiently large portion of the quasar spectrum to be suitable for this purpose." (Identification of BALs); "even if considering wider spectral regions, such fits would result in strongly model-dependent BAL characterisation." (Identification of BALs)
- How they decided it was enough: Analyse four ≥5σ [C II] detections; exclude the ∼3σ, spatially offset J1022+2252 detection.  
  > "only four (J0131-0321, J0306+1853, J2207-0416 and J1335-0328) show \[C II\] emission with S/N $\geq 5$, for which we carry out 1D and 2D gaussian fits." ([C II] luminosities and dynamical masses); "For J1022+2252, the line is tentatively detected with a very low S/N ($\sim 3$) and a significant offset with respect to the quasar optical position." (ALMA data analysis); "Hence, we excluded it from further analysis." (ALMA data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Marginal resolution, moderate line significance, fixed inclination and non-circular beams limit dynamical masses.  
  > "Because our targets are marginally resolved and with a single emission line measurement of moderate significance, $M_{\text{dyn}}$ estimates are strongly affected by the assumption of a fixed inclination angle and by non-circular beam shapes." ([C II] luminosities and dynamical masses)
- Limitation (authors): Molecular-mass errors omit ∼0.1 dex intrinsic calibration dispersion.  
  > "The errors on $M_{mol}$ reported in Table [\[table-ALMA-cii-lines\]](#table-ALMA-cii-lines) are not taking into account the intrinsic dispersion of the relation, of $\sim0.1$ dex." ([C II] luminosities and dynamical masses)
- Limitation (authors): Quasar-property error propagation ignores parameter degeneracies.  
  > "Statistical uncertainties on quasar properties are evaluated by propagating errors from line and continuum parameter uncertainties" (Emission redshift, black hole mass, and bolometric luminosity); "without considering parameter degeneracies." (Emission redshift, black hole mass, and bolometric luminosity)
- Limitation (authors): BAL occurrence is unrepresentative because the original selection tried to avoid BALs.  
  > "Despite the attempt to avoid BAL quasars, some survived the original selection procedure and are still found in the sample" (X-Shooter data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N may suppress weak C IV detections and inflate the fraction with associated N V.  
  > "We, on the other hand, find a higher percentage of $\sim 54$%, likely due to the fact that our spectra have a lower S/N, preventing us from detecting the weaker C iv systems." (Comparison with previous literature absorption catalogues)
- Limitation (authors): Luminosity selection may mimic redshift evolution of emission-line velocity shifts.  
  > "selection biases could also play a significant role, as the vast majority of high-redshift quasars observed so far are extremely luminous and may not be representative of the entire quasar population" (Combining absorption and emission measurements)
