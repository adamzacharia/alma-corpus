## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: Test the universality of the 2D star-forming plane relating star formation rate surface density, stellar mass surface density, and molecular gas surface density across different galactic environments at 150 pc spatial resolution.  
  > "Here, we probe the interplay between these three quantities across different galactic environments at a spatial resolution of $150$ pc." (abstract); "In this paper, we aim to test the universality of a two-dimensional (2D) planar star formation relation, defined as $\Sigma_{\rm SFR} \propto \Sigma_{\star}^{a} \Sigma_{\rm mol}^{b}$." (ALMA CO mapping); "We adopt the local gas-phase metallicity (in solar units) prescription for our fiducial $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ conversion factor following (cite: Sun2020) and partially based on (cite: Accurso2017), following $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 4.35 (Z / Z_{\odot})^{-1.6}$ $M_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$, adopting a ratio CO(2–1)-to-CO(1–0) = 0.65" (ALMA CO mapping)
   - parameter: S/N cut > 1 for ALMA CO intensity  
  > "A signal-to-noise (S/N) cut of 1 is then applied to drop the most uncertain emission." (ALMA CO mapping)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.35 (Z / Z_sun)^(-1.6) M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "We adopt the local gas-phase metallicity (in solar units) prescription for our fiducial $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ conversion factor following (cite: Sun2020) and partially based on (cite: Accurso2017), following $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 4.35 (Z / Z_{\odot})^{-1.6}$ $M_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$" (ALMA CO mapping)
   - assumption: CO(2-1)-to-CO(1-0) ratio R_21 = 0.65  
  > "adopting a ratio CO(2–1)-to-CO(1–0) = 0.65 (cite: Leroy2013; denBrok2021; Leroy2021a)." (ALMA CO mapping)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested robustness against a constant alpha_CO(1-0) = 4.35 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 in Sec. 5.3  
  > "We test the robustness of our results against a constant $\alpha_\mathrm{CO(1-0)} = 4.35~M_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$, as the canonical value for our Galaxy (cite: Bolatto2013) in Sec. [5.3](#sec:ConvFact)." (ALMA CO mapping)

(Stellar mass surface density maps); "The stellar mass map is then reconstructed using the weights assigned by pPXF to each SSP spectrum, given that the current stellar mass of each template is known." (Stellar mass surface density maps); "Finally, we have identified foreground stars as velocity outliers in the SSP fitting and we have masked those pixels for the analysis carried out in this paper." (Stellar mass surface density maps)
   - software: ESOREX, pymusepipe, PHANGS DAP, GIST, pPXF
   - parameter: Voronoi bin minimum S/N ~ 35 in 5300-5500 Angstrom  
  > "First, we use a Voronoi tessellation (cite: Capellari2003) to bin our MUSE data to a minimum S/N of ${\sim}35$, computed at the wavelength range of $5300{-}5500$ Å." (Stellar mass surface density maps)
   - parameter: Fitting wavelength range 4850-7000 Angstrom with 13 logarithmically spaced ages and 6 metallicity bins  
  > "To fit our data, we used a grid of templates consisting of $13$ ages, logarithmically spaced and six metallicity bins. We fit the wavelength range $4850{-}7000$ Å, in order to avoid spectral regions strongly affected by sky residuals." (Stellar mass surface density maps)
   - assumption: Cardelli et al. (1989) Milky Way extinction law and Calzetti et al. (2000) extragalactic attenuation law  
  > "We assume a (cite: Calzetti2000) extinction law to account for extragalactic attenuation in the fitting." (Stellar mass surface density maps); "we correct the full mosaic for Milky Way extinction assuming a (cite: Cardelli89) extinction law" (Stellar mass surface density maps)
   - assumption: eMILES templates with Chabrier (2003) IMF and BaSTI isochrones  
  > "We used templates from the eMILES (cite: Vazdekis2010; Vazdekis2012) database, assuming a (cite: Chabrier2003) IMF and BaSTI isochrone (cite: Pietrinferni2004) with a Galactic abundance pattern." (Stellar mass surface density maps)
   - fitting: Linear combination of SSP templates fitted with pPXF  
  > "The PHANGS–MUSE DAP (cite: Emsellem2021) includes a stellar population fitting module, where a linear combination of single stellar population templates of specific ages and metallicities are used to reproduce the observed spectrum." (Stellar mass surface density maps)
   - validation: _not stated_

(Star formation rate measurements)
   - software: PHANGS DAP
   - parameter: S/N > 3 for Halpha and S/N > 1 for Hbeta; pixels with C_HII <= 0 or Halpha_obs/Hbeta_obs < 2.86 treated as nondetections  
  > "A S/N cut of 3 for H$\alpha$ and 1 for H$\beta$ was then applied before computing the star formation rate surface density map using Eq. (Star formation rate measurements); "Pixels below this S/N cut, pixels with $C_ \text{H\,\check@mathfonts\fontsize\sf@size\z@\selectfont II} \leq 0$ or pixels where $\mathrm{H}\alpha_\mathrm{obs} / \mathrm{H}\beta_\mathrm{obs} < 2.86$, are considered nondetections (see Sec. [3.2](#sec:fit))." (Star formation rate measurements)
   - assumption: Case B recombination Halpha_corr / Hbeta_corr = 2.86 (T = 10^4 K, n_e = 100 cm^-3) and O'Donnell (1994) extinction law (k_alpha = 2.52, k_beta = 3.66, R_V = 3.1)  
  > "In detail, we dereddened the H$\alpha$ fluxes, assuming that $\mathrm{H}\alpha_\mathrm{corr} / \mathrm{H}\beta_\mathrm{corr} = 2.86$, as appropriate for a case B recombination (cite: Osterbrock1989), temperature $T = 10^{4}$ K and density $n_\mathrm{e} = 100$ cm$^{-3}$" (Star formation rate measurements); "Opting for an (cite: ODonnell1994) extinction law, we use $k_{\alpha} = 2.52$, $k_{\beta} = 3.66$ and $R_{V} = 3.1$." (Star formation rate measurements)
   - assumption: Calzetti (2013) SFR calibration: SFR / (M_sun yr^-1) = 5.5e-42 Halpha_corr / (erg s^-1), scaled to a Kroupa (2001) universal IMF  
  > "$$\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sfr}
    \frac{\mathrm{SFR}} {M_{\odot}~\mathrm{yr}^{-1}} = 5.5\times10^{-42}\frac{\mathrm{H}\alpha_\mathrm{corr}}{\mathrm{erg~s}^{-1}}~.
\end{equation}$$" (Star formation rate measurements); "This equation is scaled to a Kroupa universal IMF (cite: Kroupa2001)." (Star formation rate measurements)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian profile fitting to emission lines in each pixel  
  > "As part of the PHANGS–MUSE DAP (cite: Emsellem2021), we fit single Gaussian profiles to a number of emission lines for each pixel of the final combined MUSE mosaic of each galaxy in our sample." (Star formation rate measurements)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Match spatial resolution, rebin across spatial scales, correct for inclination, and apply environmental masks and detection fraction cuts**: Convolve maps to common 150 pc resolution, rebin MUSE and ALMA maps to 150, 200, 300, 500, and 1000 pc pixels, apply cos(i) inclination correction, classify pixels into environments (disk, spiral arm, bar, ring, center) using Querejeta et al. ## Qualitative findings

- Significant variations (>1 sigma) exist in the posterior distributions of C_* and C_mol across galactic environments at 150 pc resolution, with C_* spanning negative to positive values (-0.41 in bars to 0.45 in spiral arms) and C_mol remaining positive (0.83 to 1.01).  
  > "The dependence of $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ on $\Sigma_{\star}$ spans a wide range of slopes, with negative and positive values, while the dependence of $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ on $\Sigma_{\rm mol}$ is always positive. Bars show the most negative value of $C_{\star}$ ($-0.41$), which is a sign of longer depletion times, while spiral arms show the highest $C_{\star}$ among all environments ($0.45$)." (abstract)
- Environmental differences in the star-forming plane homogenize toward coarser spatial resolutions and become smaller than 1 sigma at scales larger than 300-500 pc.  
  > "It shows that at spatial scales larger than $300$ pc, the differences between the posteriors of different environments become smaller than $1\sigma$ (with respect to the full-sample measurement), especially for $C_{\rm mol}$." (Effect of spatial resolution); "These variations between environments homogenize toward lower spatial resolutions. Differences are no longer significant at spatial scales larger than ${\sim}500$ pc." (Summary)
- Scatter around the star-forming plane decreases toward larger spatial scales and varies by environment at 150 pc, with centers and spiral arms showing lower scatter and disks showing larger scatter than the full sample.  
  > "The first noticeable feature is how the scatter decreases toward larger spatial scales." (Effect of spatial resolution); "The figure also shows that at ${\sim}150$ pc, the scatter in certain environments is significantly lower than in others. Centers and spiral arms show a particularly low scatter, whereas disk is the only environment that shows a scatter larger than the full sample." (Effect of spatial resolution)
- The 2D plane model is formally favored over the 1D resolved Kennicutt-Schmidt (rKS) power law at >3 sigma across all spatial scales via leave-one-out cross-validation, even though the scatter reduction is modest.  
  > "Despite offering only a modest improvement in the scatter, we find that the plane model is formally favored over the rKS at all spatial scales." (Plane versus single power law); "The figure shows that the LOO statistic for the plane model is higher than for the rKS model at all spatial scales at the ${>}3\sigma$ level, and that the preference for the plane model becomes stronger at high spatial resolution." (Plane versus single power law)
- Adopting a constant alpha_CO = 4.35 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 instead of a metallicity-dependent prescription does not qualitatively alter the environmental differences or relative predictive power of C_* and C_mol.  
  > "It is easy to see that even though some specific coefficients may change, the relative difference between environments, and the relative predictive power of $C_{\star}$ and $C_{\rm mol}$ remains qualitatively the same." (Choice of \alpha_{\rm CO} conversion factor)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The overlap of PHANGS-ALMA and PHANGS-MUSE provides high physical resolution (~150 pc) for 18 nearby (<20 Mpc), low-inclination (i < 60 deg) galaxies, making it possible to resolve the typical separation between star-forming regions, limit extinction, and isolate galactic environments.  
  > "The Physics at High Angular resolution in Nearby GalaxieS (PHANGS) surveys (cite: Leroy2021b; Emsellem2021; Lee2021) provide the opportunity to explore the relation between these three quantities at a high physical resolution (${\sim}150$ pc), making it possible to isolate galactic environments and study them separately." (Introduction); "In order to resolve the typical separation between star-forming regions (${\sim}100$ pc), and limit the effect of extinction, the galaxies studied in this paper have been selected to have distances less than $20$ Mpc and low inclinations ($i<60^{\circ}$)." (Data)
- Why this method: Hierarchical Bayesian linear regression represents a middle ground between treating galactic environments as completely independent or identical; rebinning rather than Gaussian convolution minimizes pixel-to-pixel covariance; a 60% detection fraction cut in Sigma_* bins avoids discretizing in 3D while minimizing bias from nondetections; a single C_norm avoids degeneracy with slopes.  
  > "After beginning with matched resolution 150 pc MUSE and ALMA data, we favor this rebinning approach rather than, for example, convolution to a coarser Gaussian beam, because it minimized pixel-to-pixel covariance and yields approximately statistically independent measurements." (Probing larger spatial scales); "We choose to fit a single $C_{\rm norm}$ value for all different galactic environments to avoid degeneracies between the normalization ($C_{\rm norm}$) and the slopes of the planes ($C_{\star}$ and $C_{\rm mol}$)." (Fitting technique); "The hierarchical linear regression represents the middle ground between assuming that different environments are completely independent populations, and assuming that they all are identical and described by the same model." (Fitting technique)
- Alternative: Binning data before fitting (as done in Pessa et al. 2021 for 1D relations) was rejected because discretizing data in higher dimensions for a 2D plane is problematic.  
  > "In (cite: Pessa2021), we overcame this issue by binning the data before fitting a power law. Here we are trying to fit a 2D plane, and discretizing the data in higher dimensions becomes problematic, so we do not proceed in the same way." (Nondetections in our data)
- Alternative: Convolving to a coarser Gaussian beam to probe larger spatial scales was rejected in favor of rebinning to minimize pixel-to-pixel covariance.  
  > "After beginning with matched resolution 150 pc MUSE and ALMA data, we favor this rebinning approach rather than, for example, convolution to a coarser Gaussian beam, because it minimized pixel-to-pixel covariance and yields approximately statistically independent measurements." (Probing larger spatial scales)
- Alternative: Exclusion of NGC 0628 from the 19 PHANGS-MUSE galaxies because its MUSE mosaic used a different observing strategy.  
  > "For the sake of homogeneity of the data set, one galaxy from the PHANGS–MUSE sample (NGC 0628) has been excluded because its MUSE mosaic was obtained using a different observing strategy." (VLT MUSE)
- Alternative: Global (full-sample) Sigma_* range cut across all environments was tested in Appendix 9 and rejected in favor of per-environment dynamic Sigma_* ranges because global truncation destabilizes spiral arm posteriors.  
  > "Due to the truncation of the $\Sigma_{\star}$ range, the posterior of the spiral arms becomes unstable, and highly dependant on the threshold level applied, reaching almost a value of $C_{\rm mol}\approx1$ for a detection fraction threshold of $70\%$. Thus, we opt for a “dynamic” $\Sigma_{\star}$ range, chosen for each environment separately, as our fiducial approach." (Global versus per-environment detection fraction threshold)
- How they decided it was enough: MCMC sampling used 4 chains with 2000 iterations + 2000 burn-in and converged at R_hat ~ 1; 60% detection fraction threshold selected via sensitivity tests in Appendix 8 showing 70% drops ~22,000 more points and destabilizes posteriors.  
  > "We use four Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) chains to sample the posterior distributions, each one of them having $2000$ iterations plus $2000$ additional burn-in iterations. The posterior is sampled using the NUTS algorithm (cite: homan2014). The convergence of the MCMC chains is ensured by using the $\hat{R} \approx 1$ criterion (cite: Gelman1992)." (Fitting technique); "We also note that only $5000$ data points are lost when going from $50\%$ to $60\%$, while going from $60\%$ to $70\%$ leads to drop ${\sim}22{,}000$ additional data points." (Choice of detection fraction threshold)
- Limitation (authors): At ~150 pc resolution, the lowest SFR pixels may not form enough stars to fully sample the IMF, making measured SFRs more uncertain in that regime.  
  > "We acknowledge that Eq. [\[eq:sfr\]](#eq:sfr) assumes a fully sampled IMF, and that the lowest SFR pixels (especially at the ${\sim}150$ pc resolution of our data) may not form enough stars to fully sample the IMF. Hence, the measured SFR is more uncertain in this regime." (Star formation rate measurements)
- Limitation (authors): Spiral arm masks from Querejeta et al. (2021) were identified partially using CO emission, which may introduce selection bias toward higher gas and SFR at fixed Sigma_*.  
  > "The spiral arms defined by (cite: Querejeta2021) tend to have more gas and more star formation at fixed $\Sigma_\star$. Because they were identified partially based on multiwavelength data that trace gas and SFR, this is somewhat by construction." (Nondetections in our data)
- Limitation (authors): Fixing a single normalization C_norm across all environments means vertical normalization offsets between environments can be absorbed into the derived slopes C_* and C_mol.  
  > "Because we keep $C_{\rm norm}$ fixed, our fitting method may allow some of the signal associated with normalization variation to the derived slopes ($C_{\star}$ and/or $C_{\rm mol}$)." (Fitting technique)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming a constant CO(2-1)-to-CO(1-0) ratio R_21 = 0.65 ignores central enhancements in R_21 (~0.18 dex), which can depress the measured C_mol slope by up to ~0.15.  
  > "Such anticorrelation of $R_{21}$ with galactocentric radius (and thus, correlation with $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$) implies our fiducial choice of $R_{21} = 0.65$ could potentially lead to an overestimation of the total molecular gas content in the inner and denser regions of the galaxy, and to an underestimation of the gas content in the outer regions. This can effectively depress the measured $C_{\rm mol}$ slope by up to ${\sim}0.15$." (Star-forming plane across galactic environments at 150 pc resolution)
- Limitation (authors): Covariance between C_* and C_mol is partially an artifact of the fitting procedure and sample size.  
  > "Secondly, this test shows that the covariance between $C_{\star}$ and $C_{\rm mol}$ discussed in Sec. [4](#sec:results) is partially an artifact resulting from the fitting, as we recover it even though the real coefficients were chosen independently." (Toy model to test the hierarchical fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Only 8 galaxies meet the criteria for single-galaxy multi-environment correlation analysis, which is too small to establish causal relations for third parameters.  
  > "Finally, we stress here that due to the relatively low number of galaxies where we can reliably probe several galactic environments (8), and since each one of the correlations has only as many points as different environments the galaxy has, we can only speculate about the influence of an additional parameter in the coefficients of the star-forming plane, rather than robustly establishing a causal relation." (What drives the environmental variations we see?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Forcing a single intercept C_norm across all environments while centering all variables on the global sample mean (rather than per-environment means) mathematically couples any environment-dependent offset in mean depletion time directly into the fitted slopes C_* and C_mol, particularly when an environment (e.g. bars or centers) does not sit at the global mean Sigma_*.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a per-environment detection fraction cut in Sigma_* bins rather than modeling censored data (upper limits) in the Bayesian likelihood can still leave residual truncation bias near the 60% threshold.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the single-galaxy correlation analysis (Sec. 5.2), correlating C_mol and C_* against <log SFE> and <tau / t_ff> is partially circular because SFE and tau are algebraically constructed from Sigma_SFR and Sigma_mol.
