- Question: How do the physical environment (gas temperature, column density, and dust continuum) and methanol emission in the high-mass young stellar object G24.33+0.14 change during the post-burst decay/cooling phase following its September 2019 accretion burst?

2. **Measure continuum flux densities, peak intensities, deconvolved sizes, and estimate heat wave propagation speed**: Generate SPW-specific and averaged continuum images and flux ratio maps; perform 2D Gaussian fitting on continuum images; calculate effective size as geometric mean of major and minor axes and divide spatial change by time interval (195 days)  
  > "To investigate the variations in flux density of the continuum, we performed 2D Gaussian fitting on the continuum images of four different SPWs and averaged SPWs." (Continuum emission); "From September 25, 2019 to April 7, 2020, spanning 195 days, considering the distance range from the continuum fitting center to the location where the flux ratio equals 1 is between 0.36$^{\prime \prime}$ and 1.77$^{\prime \prime}$ (2600–13000 AU), we estimate the propagation speed of the heat wave in this region to be between 0.08 $c$ and 0.38 $c$." (Continuum variability); "The effective size is calculated using the geometric mean of the major and minor axes of the deconvolved size." (Continuum variability)

   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate methanol rotational temperature, column density, and cooling timescale across radial zones (R0, R1, R2)**: Perform rotational temperature diagram analysis in three regions (R0, R1, R2) for ALMA 2016, ALMA 2019, and NOEMA epochs; calculate cooling timescale in R0 assuming uniform column density decrease rate  
  > "Based on the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), the rotational temperature diagram analysis can be used to estimate the rotational temperature and column density of the methanol molecule." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol); "We have delineated three specific regions – R0, R1, and R2 – ranging from the inner to the outer zones of the methanol emission area for an in-depth rotational temperature diagram analysis, as illustrated in panel (a) of Figure 6." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol); "Assuming a uniform decrease in column density from the 2019 burst to March 2020 at a rate of $r_{N} = \Delta N / \Delta t$, where $\Delta N$ is the change in column density and $\Delta t$ is the time separation, we can estimate the timescale for the methanol in the R0 region to cool down to its initial state." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
   - parameter: three regions: R0, R1, R2 from inner to outer zones  
  > "We have delineated three specific regions – R0, R1, and R2 – ranging from the inner to the outer zones of the methanol emission area for an in-depth rotational temperature diagram analysis, as illustrated in panel (a) of Figure 6." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Based on the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), the rotational temperature diagram analysis can be used to estimate the rotational temperature and column density of the methanol molecule." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
   - assumption: Partition function Q(T_rot) = 1.2327 * T_rot^1.5  
  > "The partition function for ${\rm CH_3OH}$ (cite: 2013ApJS..206...22C) is $$\begin{equation}
    Q(T_{\rm rot})=1.2327 T^{1.5}_{\rm rot}\, .
\end{equation}$$" (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
   - assumption: Uniform decrease in column density from 2019 burst to March 2020  
  > "Assuming a uniform decrease in column density from the 2019 burst to March 2020 at a rate of $r_{N} = \Delta N / \Delta t$, where $\Delta N$ is the change in column density and $\Delta t$ is the time separation, we can estimate the timescale for the methanol in the R0 region to cool down to its initial state." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
   - fitting: Linear fit to rotational temperature diagram (ln(3 k W / (8 pi^3 nu S mu^2)) vs E_u / k)  
  > "$$\begin{equation}
\ln\left(\frac{3\kappa W}{8\pi^3\nu S\mu^2}\right)=\ln\frac{N(T_{\mathrm{rot}}-T_{\mathrm{bg}})}{QT_{\mathrm{rot}}}-\frac{E_u/\kappa}{T_{\mathrm{rot}}}\, ,
\label{eq:T_rot}
\end{equation}$$" (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
   - validation: Checked that derived rotational temperatures and column densities decrease consistently with increasing distance from core center across all three epochs  
  > "The derived parameters exhibit consistent decrease trends with the increasing distance from the core center at three observation epochs, indicating rotational temperature diagram analysis yields reliable outcomes." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)

## Qualitative findings

- In the post-burst phase (March 2020), the 1.3 mm continuum emission in the inner core region of G24 decreased compared to the burst phase (September 2019), while the emission in the outer peripheral region increased, indicating inside-out cooling and outward propagation of a heat wave.  
  > "Following the burst, the continuum emission in the inner core region of G24 diminished by approximately 20%, while the emission in the outer region exhibited an increase by a factor of $\sim30\%$." (Abstract); "the continuum images of G24 from the NOEMA data indicate a cooling within the inner regions of the continuum emission, while the outer regions remain heated following the burst." (Summary)
- Methanol lines with higher upper state energies exhibit smaller emitting regions and suffered more pronounced integrated intensity reductions between September 2019 and March 2020, consistent with cooling proceeding from the inner core layers outward.  
  > "among the eight methanol lines, those with lower upper state energies (below 100 K) display larger emission regions, while those with higher upper state energies exhibit smaller regions." (Methanol variability); "there is a correlation between the magnitude of the integrated intensity reduction and the energy level of the upper state: as the energy of the upper state increases, the decrease in intensity becomes more pronounced." (Summary)
- Rotational temperature diagram analysis across three radial zones shows that gas temperature and methanol column density decreased in the inner layers (R0, R1) from September 2019 to March 2020, but increased in the outermost layer (R2).  
  > "by March 2020, there was a modest decrease in both the gas temperature and column density of methanol in the innermost layer of the methanol emission region, as opposed to a rise in these measurements in the outer layer when compared with the figures from September 2019." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining NOEMA 1.3 mm observations in March 2020 (post-burst decay phase) with archival ALMA 1.3 mm observations in September 2019 (burst phase) and August 2016 (quiescent pre-burst phase) allows tracking gas and dust evolution before, during, and after the accretion burst.  
  > "In this study, we have combined the 1.3 mm band observational data from NOrthern Extended Millimeter Array (NOEMA) in March 2020, which corresponds to the post-burst phase (Figure 1), with ALMA data collected during the burst phase in September 2019." (Introduction); "We further enhance our analysis by incorporating data from ALMA observations of G24 during its quiescent phase in August 2016 (pre-burst period)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: LTE rotational temperature diagram analysis across concentric regions estimates radial variations in rotational temperature and column density of methanol across the three epochs.  
  > "Based on the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), the rotational temperature diagram analysis can be used to estimate the rotational temperature and column density of the methanol molecule." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
- Alternative: Sublimation and subsequent refreezing of methanol ice on dust grains as the cause of column density changes was considered, but rejected because theoretical refreezing timescales (~100 years) are much longer than the observed ~200-700 day decrease.  
  > "According to the simulation results by (cite: 2024A&A...684A..51G) on the variation of methanol abundance in high-mass stars during the pre-burst, burst, and post-burst phases, the timescale for methanol gas to refreeze after a burst is about 100 years." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol); "We believe that the change in methanol column density is more likely caused by variations in local temperature in G24, which alter the proportion of excited methanol gas." (Rotational temperature diagram analysis for methanol)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The ~10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty and different uv coverages between NOEMA and ALMA affect the comparison and cause a slight offset in the minimum point of the continuum flux ratio.  
  > "The absolute flux error is estimated to be less than 10%." (Observation); "We noticed that the minimum point of the continuum flux ratio is slightly offset from the fitted center. We believe this is due to the uneven distribution of dust and the different uv coverage of the interferometer arrays." (Continuum variability)
- Limitation (authors): The standardized beam size (2.12'' x 0.61'') is much larger than the native ALMA resolution (~0.2''-0.3''), blending the three continuum peaks seen at high resolution into a single continuum peak.  
  > "Given that the beam size used in this study is $2.12'' \times 0.61''$, which exceeds the $0.3''$ beam size utilized in (cite: 2022PASJ...74.1234H), the continuum images from this study reveal only a single continuum peak, as opposed to the three peaks observed by (cite: 2022PASJ...74.1234H)." (Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral smoothing to 2 MHz may cause a one-channel discrepancy in peak velocity position for the 216.946 GHz methanol line.  
  > "For the line at 216.946 GHz, there is a one-channel discrepancy between the peak positions of NOEMA and ALMA 2019, potentially attributable to the spectral smoothing process." (Methanol lines)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing flux ratios and deconvolved sizes between ALMA (baselines up to 2.5 km, native beam ~0.2'') and NOEMA (baselines up to 368 m, elongated beam 2.12'' x 0.61'') purely by convolving to a common restoring beam without matching uv-coverage can introduce spatial filtering artifacts and bias radial ratio maps.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ~10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty in NOEMA (and ~5-10% in ALMA Band 6) is comparable in magnitude to the ~13% continuum peak decrease and ~14% integrated flux increase.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optical depth effects in abundant CH3OH lines and blending of the three internal cores within the 2.12'' x 0.61'' beam may bias single-component LTE rotational diagram fits.
