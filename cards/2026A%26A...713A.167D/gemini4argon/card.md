## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure multi-frequency radio continuum flux densities, spectral index, and deconvolved H II region sizes**: Measure integrated radio continuum flux densities above a 3σ threshold from 1.35 to 5.85 GHz maps to fit a power-law spectral index (S_ν ∝ ν^α), and fit 2D Gaussians to the 5 GHz CORNISH components (CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B) using CASA imfit.  
  > "Table [1](#tab:radio_flux) summarizes the integrated radio continuum flux densities ($S_\nu$) measured at the $3\sigma$-level from the corresponding continuum images. Using these measurements, we derived a spectral index ($\alpha$; $S_\nu \propto \nu^{\alpha}$) of $0.39 \pm 0.01$" (Radio continuum emission); "We employed the `imfit` task within the CASA package to estimate the angular extents of these components (see Fig. [2](#fig:r_cont))." (Radio continuum emission)
   - software: CASA (imfit)
   - parameter: 3σ flux integration threshold  
  > "Table notes: The $S_\nu$ values are estimated after applying a 3$\sigma$-level threshold to the corresponding radio continuum maps." (Table 1)
   - assumption: Distance D = 3.3 kpc  
  > "They have also put this star-forming region at a near kinematic distance of 3.3 kpc, which is also consistent with the distance estimations of (cite: 2006ApJ...653.1325S) and (cite: 2016ApJ...823...77R)." (Introduction); "Adopting the distance to G28.288–0.364 given in Sect. [1](#sect:intro), these angular dimensions correspond to physical sizes of $0.062 \pm 0.004$ pc $\times$ $0.033 \pm 0.002$ pc for CORNISH-A and $0.058 \pm 0.003$ pc $\times$ $0.033 \pm 0.002$ pc for CORNISH-B." (Radio continuum emission)
   - fitting: Power-law fit S_ν ∝ ν^α to multi-frequency flux densities; 2D Gaussian deconvolution via CASA imfit  
  > "Using these measurements, we derived a spectral index ($\alpha$; $S_\nu \propto \nu^{\alpha}$) of $0.39 \pm 0.01$, indicative of partially optically thick free–free emission." (Radio continuum emission); "The deconvolved source sizes returned by `imfit` are $3.89\ensuremath{''} \pm 0.28\ensuremath{''} \times 2.04\ensuremath{''} \pm 0.16\ensuremath{''}$ for CORNISH-A and $3.61\ensuremath{''} \pm 0.20\ensuremath{''} \times 2.05\ensuremath{''} \pm 0.12\ensuremath{''}$ for CORNISH-B." (Radio continuum emission)
   - validation: Compared derived spectral index with the in-band spectral index from the GLOSTAR-D catalog  
  > "This is in agreement with the in-band spectral index ($\alpha_{\rm in-band} = 0.16 \pm 0.02$) reported in the GLOSTAR-D radio source catalog (cite: 2019A&A...627A.175M) that also indicates the ionized gas to be partially optically thick." (Radio continuum emission)
2. **Extract and fit radio recombination line (RRL) spectra from GLOSTAR-D and ALMA Band 3**: Extract average RRL spectrum from a one-beam circular region in GLOSTAR-D, and extract individual H63δ spectra toward CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B from the ~1'' ALMA Band 3 cube, fitting Gaussian profiles to obtain central velocities and FWHMs.  
  > "Consequently, we extracted an average RRL spectrum (see the top panel of Fig. [3](#fig:glos_rrl)) from a circular region with a diameter equal to one synthesized beam of the GLOSTAR-D RRL map, chosen to fully encompass the $3\sigma$-level radio continuum emission seen in the CORNISH map." (Radio recombination line emission); "Next, we extracted average spectra from the ALMA Band 3 H63$\delta$ RRL data. These observations provide a substantially higher angular resolution ($\sim 1''$) than the GLOSTAR-D RRL data, enabling us to determine the RRL properties of CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B individually" (Radio recombination line emission)
   - parameter: Circular extraction aperture of diameter = 1 synthesized beam (25'') for GLOSTAR-D  
  > "extracted an average RRL spectrum (see the top panel of Fig. [3](#fig:glos_rrl)) from a circular region with a diameter equal to one synthesized beam of the GLOSTAR-D RRL map" (Radio recombination line emission)
   - assumption: Classification thresholds of Kurtz (2005), Sewilo et al. (2004), Keto et al. (2008): HC H II regions have sizes <= 0.03 pc and RRL FWHM >= 40 km s^-1; UC H II regions have sizes <= 0.1 pc and RRL FWHM ~ 30 km s^-1  
  > "adopting the classification criteria of (cite: 2005IAUS..227..111K), (cite: 2004ApJ...605..285S), and (cite: 2008ApJ...672..423K), which define HC and UC H II regions as having characteristic sizes $\lesssim$ 0.03 pc and $\lesssim$ 0.1 pc, respectively, and RRL linewidths of $\gtrsim$ 40 km s$^{-1}$ and $\approx$ 30 km s$^{-1}$" (On the evolutionary nature of CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fitting to extracted RRL spectra  
  > "For CORNISH-A, the fitted spectrum yields a central velocity of $46.16 \pm 0.29$ km s$^{-1}$ and an FWHM of $37.07 \pm 0.69$ km s$^{-1}$, whereas for CORNISH-B, the central velocity and FWHM are $42.86 \pm 0.48$ km s$^{-1}$ and $32.33 \pm 1.13$ km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Radio recombination line emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify compact 1.36 mm dust cores and measure their sizes and flux densities**: Run dendrogram analysis with astrodendro on the ALMAGAL 1.36 mm dust continuum map to identify candidate core leaves, then fit 2D Gaussians with CASA imfit using dendrogram leaf parameters as initial guesses and bounding boxes.  
  > "We used the ALMAGAL 1.36 mm dust continuum maps to identify and characterize compact cores associated with G28.288–0.364. Core extraction was performed using the `astrodendro` package together with the CASA `imfit` task." (Dust continuum emission); "To overcome this limitation, we employed the CASA task `imfit`, using the parameters derived from the dendrogram analysis as initial guesses for the fitting. All fit parameters were allowed to vary." (Dust continuum emission)
   - software: astrodendro, CASA (imfit)
   - parameter: min_value = 3σ, min_delta = 1σ, min_npix = 1 synthesized beam area  
  > "The identification of leaves is controlled by three parameters: (i) a minimum intensity threshold ($min\_value$) of 3$\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the rms noise of the map; (ii) a minimum intensity contrast ($min\_delta$) of 1$\sigma$, which sets the required significance for a structure to be treated as independent; and (iii) a minimum number of pixels ($min\_npix$), chosen to be equal to the synthesized beam area expressed in pixel units" (Dust continuum emission)
   - parameter: Post-fit filtering: F_peak > 5σ and aspect ratio <= 3  
  > "To avoid including spurious detections, we retained only sources satisfying $F_{\rm peak} > 5\sigma$." (Dust continuum emission); "Such cases typically correspond to filamentary features or diffuse emission, characterized by an aspect ratio greater than three between the major and minor axes." (Dust continuum emission)
   - fitting: Unconstrained 2D Gaussian fitting in CASA imfit within bounding boxes set by dendrogram leaf apertures  
  > "All fit parameters were allowed to vary. The fitting is performed within a bounding box whose dimensions were determined by the aperture sizes of the corresponding leaves identified in the dendrogram analysis." (Dust continuum emission)
   - validation: Visual inspection of 1.36-mm map overlaid with leaf structures, rejecting F_peak <= 5σ and aspect ratio > 3  
  > "In addition, we rejected cores with poorly fitted morphologies based on visual inspection of the 1.36-mm dust continuum map overlaid with the identified leaf structures." (Dust continuum emission)
4. **Derive isothermal core masses, surface densities, number densities, and correct for free-free contamination**: Calculate isothermal dust masses, surface densities, and number densities from 1.36 mm integrated fluxes using Eqs. (1)–(3). For cores with CORNISH 5 GHz counterparts (MM3 and MM4), extrapolate the 5 GHz flux density to 220.6 GHz assuming optically thin free-free emission (α = -0.1) and subtract it before recomputing dust masses.  
  > "Next, assuming that the dust emission is optically thin, we derived the isothermal core masses using the following expression" (Dust continuum emission); "We therefore estimate the free-free contribution by adopting the highest observed radio frequency as the turnover frequency and extrapolating the measured flux density to 220.6 GHz, assuming optically thin free-free emission with a spectral index of $\alpha=-0.1$." (Dust continuum emission); "Subtracting the extrapolated free-free contribution from the reported 1.36 mm flux densities, the resulting dust masses of MM3 and MM4 are reduced by factors of 4.23 and 2.43, respectively, yielding revised masses of 5.47 and 19.93 M$_\odot$." (Dust continuum emission)
   - parameter: ν = 220.6 GHz (1.36 mm)  
  > "However, it’s important to mention that the reported 1.36 mm (220.6 GHz) continuum flux density in Table [\[tab:dust_cont\]](#tab:dust_cont) is expected to arise from a combination of thermal dust emission and free-free emission from ionized gas." (Dust continuum emission)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission; gas-to-dust ratio δ_gd = 100; dust opacity κ_ν0 = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1 at 1.3 mm with β = 2.0; dust temperature T_d = 30.9 K; distance D = 3.3 kpc; mean molecular weight μ ≈ 2.8  
  > "where $\beta$ is the dust emissivity index, adopted as 2.0 (cite: 2013A&A...554A..42R) in our calculations." (Dust continuum emission); "For our calculations, we considered $\delta_{\rm gd} = 100$, $\kappa_{\nu_0}$ = 0.899 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at 1300 $\mu$m or 1.3 mm for a MRN-distribution of grain sizes (for the diffuse interstellar medium of gas density $=10^6$ cm$^{-3}$) with thin ice mantles (cite: 1994A&A...291..943O), and $T_{\rm d}$ = 30.9 K for the entire ATLASGAL clump (cite: 2022MNRAS.510.3389U)." (Dust continuum emission); "where the mean molecular weight ($\mu$) $\approx 2.8$, $m_{\rm H} = 1.67\times10^{-24}$ g, $R_{\rm core}$ is the effective core radius, defined as half of the geometric mean of FWHM$_{\rm maj}$ and FWHM$_{\rm min}$ evaluated at the distance of G28.288–0.364." (Dust continuum emission)
   - assumption: Optically thin free-free spectral index α = -0.1 above the 5 GHz CORNISH frequency for MM3 and MM4; zero free-free contamination for MM1, MM2, MM5  
  > "We therefore estimate the free-free contribution by adopting the highest observed radio frequency as the turnover frequency and extrapolating the measured flux density to 220.6 GHz, assuming optically thin free-free emission with a spectral index of $\alpha=-0.1$." (Dust continuum emission); "No CORNISH radio continuum counterparts are detected toward MM1, MM2, and MM5. Thus, it’s assumed that the cold dust emission is not contaminated by the free-free emission, and no correction is required for these cores." (Dust continuum emission)
   - fitting: Analytical evaluation of Eqs. (1)–(3) and power-law flux extrapolation  
  > "Extrapolating these values to 220.6 GHz under the assumption of optically thin free-free emission, we estimate that ionized gas accounts for approximately 76% and 59% of the 1.36 mm flux densities reported in Table [\[tab:dust_cont\]](#tab:dust_cont) for MM3 and MM4" (Dust continuum emission)
   - validation: Compared derived surface densities against the theoretical massive star formation threshold of >= 1 g cm^-2 (McKee & Tan 2003)  
  > "Based on the surface density threshold for massive star formation ($\gtrsim 1$ g cm$^{-2}$; (cite: 2003ApJ...585..850M)), all cores listed in Table [\[tab:dust_cont\]](#tab:dust_cont) satisfy the criterion for forming massive stars." (Dust continuum emission)
5. **Identify molecular transitions and model LTE rotational temperatures and column densities toward the five dust cores**: Identify molecular lines in ALMAGAL Band 6 cubes using CASSIS with CDMS and JPL catalogs, then fit 1D LTE radiative transfer models using XCLASS to derive rotational temperatures (from H2CO transitions) and column densities for DCN, H2CO, HC3N, C18O, 13CO, and SO, plus construct a DCN (3–2) moment 0 map.  
  > "Line identification was performed by comparing the observed rest frequencies with entries from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (cite: 2001A&A...370L..49M; 2005JMoSt.742..215M) and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory Molecular Spectroscopy Catalog (cite: 1998JQSRT..60..883P) using the spectral analysis package Centre d’Analyse Scientifique de Spectres Instrumentaux et Synthétiques (CASSIS; (cite: 2015sf2a.conf..313V))." (Molecular line emission); "The molecular emission was modeled using the eXtended CASA Line Analysis Software Suite (XCLASS; (cite: 2017A&A...598A...7M)), which solves the one-dimensional radiative transfer equation under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Molecular line emission); "We have also overplotted all detected cores on the moment 0 (velocity-integrated intensity) map constructed from the DCN $(3-2)$ line emission obtained as part of the ALMAGAL survey (see the right panel of Fig. [4](#fig:dcn))." (Molecular line emission)
   - software: CASSIS, XCLASS

## Qualitative findings

- High-resolution 5 GHz CORNISH observations resolve G28.288–0.364 into two compact H II regions (CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B) of ~0.06 pc size, with CORNISH-A in a rare transitional stage between HC and UC H II regions and CORNISH-B in a more evolved UC stage.  
  > "High-resolution 5 GHz data resolve the ionized emission into two compact components, CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B, with physical sizes of $\sim$0.06 pc." (Conclusions); "Combined with their physical sizes, these linewidths suggest that CORNISH-A is in a transitional stage between HC and UC H II regions, whereas CORNISH-B is more consistent with a comparatively evolved UC H II region." (Conclusions)
- Five compact dust cores (MM1–MM5) are identified in the ALMAGAL 1.36 mm map, where MM3 and MM4 host CORNISH-B and CORNISH-A respectively and have substantial free-free contamination (76% and 59%) at 1.36 mm.  
  > "Extrapolating these values to 220.6 GHz under the assumption of optically thin free-free emission, we estimate that ionized gas accounts for approximately 76% and 59% of the 1.36 mm flux densities reported in Table [\[tab:dust_cont\]](#tab:dust_cont) for MM3 and MM4" (Dust continuum emission); "The 1.36-mm dust continuum data resolve five cores (MM1–MM5) with masses ranging from $\sim$4 to 48 M$_\odot$ and mass surface densities exceeding 1 g cm$^{-2}$. MM3 and MM4 spatially coincide with CORNISH-B and CORNISH-A, respectively" (Conclusions)
- DCN (3–2) emission is strongly detected toward MM1 and MM5 (indicating early protostellar cores), absent or weak toward MM3 and MM4 (likely destroyed by H II region heating/radiation), and absent toward MM2 despite no radio continuum.  
  > "Significant DCN $(3-2)$ emission was detected toward MM1 and MM5, suggesting that they are likely to be early-stage protostellar cores. In contrast, MM3 and MM4 exhibited little to no DCN $(3-2)$ emission, likely reflecting the destruction of DCN molecules due to heating and enhanced radiation fields associated with the formation of H II regions." (Molecular line emission); "Surprisingly, MM2 showed no significant DCN $(3$–$2)$ emission despite the absence of any radio continuum counterpart." (Molecular line emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-angular-resolution (~1'') ALMA Band 3 H63δ and ALMAGAL Band 6 data are needed to resolve the individual ionized components (CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B) and compact dust cores that are blended in coarser-resolution surveys (~15''–25'').  
  > "These observations provide a substantially higher angular resolution ($\sim 1''$) than the GLOSTAR-D RRL data, enabling us to determine the RRL properties of CORNISH-A and CORNISH-B individually" (Radio recombination line emission); "We used the ALMAGAL 1.36 mm dust continuum maps to identify and characterize compact cores associated with G28.288–0.364." (Dust continuum emission)
- Why this method: Dendrograms (astrodendro) identify hierarchical core structures, while CASA imfit is combined with dendrogram initial guesses because dendrograms alone only measure flux above a saddle point rather than total integrated flux; XCLASS LTE modeling is chosen because core densities n >= 10^6 cm^-3 validate LTE.  
  > "Although the dendrogram technique is well-suited for investigating the hierarchical organization of emission within dense clumps, it cannot be used directly to derive fragment masses." (Dust continuum emission); "To overcome this limitation, we employed the CASA task `imfit`, using the parameters derived from the dendrogram analysis as initial guesses for the fitting." (Dust continuum emission); "Given the high number densities associated with the detected cores (see Table [\[tab:dust_cont\]](#tab:dust_cont)), with $n \gtrsim 10^6$ cm$^{-3}$ in the vicinity of the dust cores, the LTE approximation is expected to be valid for the observed transitions (cite: 2015PASP..127..266M)." (Molecular line emission)
- Alternative: Using CH3CN transitions as a molecular thermometer instead of H2CO (rejected because no CH3CN transitions were detected toward G28.288–0.364).  
  > "In such cases, methyl cyanide (CH$_3$CN) transitions are generally preferred as temperature tracers." (Molecular line emission); "Unfortunately, no CH$_3$CN transitions are detected toward G28.288–0.364." (Molecular line emission)
- Alternative: Using FUGIN 13CO/C18O (1–0) or ALMAGAL 13CO/C18O (2–1) cubes to isolate the outflow driving core (rejected due to ~20'' beam blending in FUGIN and spatial filtering of extended emission in ALMAGAL).  
  > "As a result, the FUGIN data are subject to similar beam dilution and blending effects, and thus do not provide additional constraints for isolating the driving source of the outflow." (Outflow activity); "Although the high-resolution ALMAGAL data products include $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O ($J = 2-1$) line emission, these interferometric data cubes, particularly the C$^{18}$O ($J = 2-1$) transition, are affected by spatial filtering of extended emission." (Outflow activity)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The radio continuum turnover frequency is unconstrained, preventing a formal SED decomposition of thermal dust and free-free emission at 1.36 mm; the α = -0.1 extrapolation from 5 GHz only gives a conservative minimum free-free estimate.  
  > "Since the turnover frequency is not constrained by either the available radio continuum data or dedicated modeling of the ionized component, a reliable decomposition of these contributions is not possible." (Dust continuum emission); "This approach provides a conservative estimate of the minimum free-free contribution to the 1.36 mm continuum flux density." (Dust continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): H2CO (3_0,3–2_0,2) can become optically thick at high density and temperature, causing T_rot estimates for MM3 and MM5 (~199–200 K) to saturate, and H2CO spectra toward MM3 and MM4 suffer from negative interferometric artefacts.  
  > "However, under conditions of high density and temperature, the H$_2$CO $(3_{0,3}-2_{0,2})$ transition can become optically thick, making temperature estimates based on H$_2$CO line ratios unreliable (cite: 2012A&A...545A..51R; 2019A&A...631A.142G)."
