## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA data**: CASA calibration, self-calibration on continuum, continuum subtraction, Briggs-weighted clean  
  > "Imaging was done using the CASA clean algorithm after a continuum subtraction was performed on the emission line data." (3.1 ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: ALMA/APEX 13CO(2-1) line intensity ratio 1.0±0.15 in central feature  
  > "The ALMA to APEX line intensity ratio is 1.0$\pm$0.15." (3.5 Missing flux in ALMA data)
2. **Decompose circumstellar components and measure line and continuum properties**: Channel maps, PV diagrams, aperture fluxes, Gaussian fits to lines and 2D source sizes  
  > "The different components are exemplified below through selected molecular-line channel maps and position-velocity (PV) diagrams" (4.2 Decomposition)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fits for sizes; Gaussian fits for line widths  
  > "7.0 mJy resides in a 0.027$''$$\times$0.026$''$ (PA = 100$^\circ$) source (FWHM of a Gaussian 2D fit)" (4.4 The central compact source, CCS)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Estimate HVO inclination angle**: SHAPE model of two expanding thin-walled ellipsoids compared with PV diagram and channel maps  
  > "The model is described by two, diametrically oriented, expanding, and thin-walled ellipsoids of homogeneous density" (4.9 Inclination angle of the HVO)
   - software: SHAPE
   - assumption: Symmetric source, constant axis direction, expansion velocity linear in distance (Hubble-like)  
  > "We further assume that the expansion velocity of the HGS/bubble structure is at each point linearly proportional to its distance from the centre." (4.9 Inclination angle of the HVO)
   - fitting: by eye comparison of models at 5, 10, 20 deg  
  > "We conservatively estimate, using inspection by the eye, that the inclination angle lies in the range 5 to 20 degrees" (4.9 Inclination angle of the HVO)
   - validation: Model channel maps compared to observed ones  
  > "The results of this are compared to the observed PV-diagram and channel maps." (Inclination angle of the HVO)
4. **Derive gas masses, temperatures and densities per component**: Optically thin C18O(2-1) column densities and mass equation; CO brightness temperature as kinetic temperature; ALI radiative transfer for e-EVS  
  > "We will use the C$^{18}$O(2–1) line data to estimate column densities assuming that this line is optically thin" (5.2.1 Molecular gas estimates)
   - software: Accelerated-Lambda-Iteration code
   - parameter: f_CO = 4e-4, f_C18O = 8e-7  
  > "4$\times$10$^{-4}$ (close to full association of carbon into CO assuming solar abundances" (5.2.1 Molecular gas estimates)
   - assumption: Thermalised, CO optically thick so Tk = Tb; solar CO/C18O = 480  
  > "If the CO(2–1) line emission is optically thick, which appears to be the case in most parts of the circumstellar medium of HD 101584" (5.2.1 Molecular gas estimates)
   - fitting: e-EVS: spherical homogeneous isothermal clump of radius 0.5"; best fit n_H2 = (5±2)e5 cm-3, Tk = 60±10 K  
  > "The best fit is obtained for $n_{\rm H_2}$ = (5$\pm$2)$\times$10$^5$ cm$^{-3}$ and $T_{\rm k}$ = 60$\pm$10 K" (5.2.5 The EVSs)
   - validation: Compared with CO brightness temperature and CO/13CO ratio of other studies  
  > "The resulting CO/$^{13}$CO ratio is 14$\pm$6, that is, in very good agreement with the value 13$\pm$6 estimated from C$^{17}$O and $^{13}$C$^{17}$O line emission from the CCS by (cite: olofetal17)." (The EVSs)
5. **Kinematical ages and energetics**: Age from max apparent velocity, length and inclination; kinetic energy and momentum from C18O line-of-sight velocities  
  > "A simple estimate of the age of an outflow is obtained by using its maximum apparent outflow velocity, its apparent length, and its inclination angle" (5.1 Kinematical ages)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Dust mass from continuum and SED modelling**: Optically thin dust mass equation; axisymmetric MCMax disk+envelope SED models compared by eye  
  > "The comparison between the model results and the data was carried out by eye." (6.5 SED model results)
   - software: MCMax
   - parameter: R_out = 3000 au, inclination 10 deg, Teff 8500 K, a_min 0.01 um, p = -3.5, silicate plus water ice  
  > "A value of $-3.5$ was used for the exponent of the size distribution, $p$" (6.4 SED modelling)
   - assumption: Dust opacity 0.5 cm2/g, Td = 50 K for simple estimate; gas-to-dust 200 canonical  
  > "Assuming a dust temperature of 50 K (the estimated gas temperature of the EDE component), and an opacity of 0.5 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$" (6.2 The EDE)
   - fitting: By eye; no sampler  
  > "The comparison between the model results and the data was carried out by eye." (6.5 SED model results)
   - validation: Radial profile of ALMA 1.3 mm image constrains B; simple Eq. estimate agrees within a factor two  
  > "only a factor of two different from the more sophisticated estimate above" (6.5 SED model results)
7. **Infer evolutionary status and energy budget**: Core-mass-luminosity relations, circumstellar mass, 17O/18O ratio; released gravitational energy from mass function and orbit vs kinetic energy  
  > "In the low-$L_\ast$ case we use a $M_{\rm c}$–$L_\ast$ ($M_{\rm c}$ is the core mass) relation for RGB stars (cite: bootsack88)" (7.1 Evolutionary status)
   - parameter: P = 144 d, K = 3 km/s, i = 10 deg; a_i = 1 au (post-RGB), 4 au (post-AGB)  
  > "We assume 1 and 4 au in the post-RGB and post-AGB cases, respectively" (7.2 The energetics and common-envelope evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Four circumstellar components (CCS, EDE, HVO, HGS) are identified, plus evidence of a second bipolar outflow  
  > "Four circumstellar components are identified" (abstract)
- Isotope ratios plus stellar mass estimates favour a post-RGB identification  
  > "Isotope ratios combined with stellar mass estimates suggest that the primary star’s evolution was terminated already on the first red giant branch (RGB)." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Simple analyses chosen because data constraints on density and temperature are limited  
  > "This means that the observational constraints on densities and temperatures are limited." (5 Quantitative estimates: gas)
- Alternative: Post-AGB high-luminosity case disfavoured as inconsistent with low 17O/18O  
  > "This is not compatible with the estimated low $^{17}$O/$^{18}$O ratio, which suggests a low-mass star." (7.1 Evolutionary status)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Flux uncertainties at least ~20%; ~40% of 13CO flux lost in ALMA data  
  > "they would not reflect the real uncertainties that we estimate are at least of the order 20 %." (3.1 ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Distance uncertain, so results scaled to 1 kpc  
  > "Therefore, we regard the Gaia estimate sufficiently uncertain to warrant giving all the distance-dependent results as their values at 1 kpc" (2.3 The distance)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SED fit by eye with no uncertainties; many degeneracies in dust model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas masses depend on assumed CO/C18O ratio, CO abundance and opacity of C18O
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing flux in ALMA data affects gas masses and morphology
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Post-RGB vs post-AGB cannot be decided without a reliable distance
