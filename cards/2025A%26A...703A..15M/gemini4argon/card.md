## Science

- Fields: debris_disks, protoplanetary_disks
- Question: Expand the sample of known CO-bearing debris disks around young intermediate-mass stars and explore the environmental conditions and origin (primordial vs. secondary) of their gas.  
  > "To expand the list of known CO-bearing debris disks and to improve our knowledge of the environmental conditions under which they can form, we targeted twelve dust-rich debris disks around young ($<$50 Myr) intermediate-mass stars." (abstract); "Using the new, unified sample, we investigate the possible origin of the CO gas by confronting the results with the predictions of both secondary and primary models (Sect. [6](#sec:discussion))." (Introduction)

  > "setting a list of scale sizes of 0 (point source), 5, 15, and 25 pixels (corresponding roughly to 1, 3, and 5 times the beam size)." ## Qualitative findings

- Five new CO-bearing debris disks were discovered around young A-type stars: two with low CO content (HD 152989 and HD 170116) and three CO-rich systems (HD 9985, HD 145101, and HD 155853).  
  > "We discovered CO gas in five disks. Two of them have a low CO content of a few times 10$^{-5}$ M$_\oplus$, similar to that of $\beta$ Pic. The other three disks, however, are CO-rich with $M_\mathrm{CO}>10^{-3}$ M$_\oplus$." (abstract); "While the CO content of the disks around HD 152989 and HD 170116 is $<$10$^{-4}$ M$_\oplus$, the other three gas-bearing systems, HD 9985, HD 145101, and HD 155853, have CO masses $>$10$^{-3}$ M$_\oplus$, and are therefore classified as CO-rich debris disks." (Summary and Conclusions)
- HD 152989 shows a potential double-ring structure in CO PV diagrams (at ~75 and ~130 au), and its continuum visibility data are equally well fit by a single Gaussian ring or two narrow rings at 83 and 120 au.  
  > "In contrast, the PV diagram of the object suggests that there is little gas between these two radial distances, and that the CO is instead mostly concentrated in two rings at $\sim$75 and $\sim$130 au (Fig. [4](#fig:pvd)c)." (Spatial distribution of CO gas); "Using BIC to assess the goodness-of-fit of the two assumed models – the single Gaussian ring and the double narrow ring, which have 7 and 8 free parameters, respectively – we found no significant difference between them." (Spatial distribution of CO gas)
- HD 170116 hosts one of the largest known debris disks (R_disk ~ 197 au) and exhibits strong azimuthal asymmetry in 12CO, with emission detected only on the southern side and peaking near a continuum clump.  
  > "Significant CO emission is detected only in the southern side of the continuum structure, indicating a strong asymmetry in the CO brightness distribution." (Imaging and CO line fluxes); "The disk has a peak radius of $\sim$200 au, which makes it one of the largest known debris disks (cite: matra2025)." (Summary and Conclusions)
- In HD 155853, the CO gas (45-140 au) is located closer to the star than the millimeter dust grains (60-175 au), whereas in HD 9985 the gas and dust are well co-located.  
  > "indicating that the densest part of the dust ring is confined between $\sim$55 au and $\sim$115 au, i.e. the dust and gas in this system are probably quite well co-located." (Spatial distribution of CO gas); "In the case of HD 155853, we found that the CO gas is mostly situated between 45 and 140 au (Fig. [4](#fig:pvd)b), closer to the star than large dust particles whose emission comes mainly from radial distances between 60 and 175 au (Table [\[tab:contdiskprops\]](#tab:contdiskprops))." (Spatial distribution of CO gas)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 12 young (<50 Myr), dust-rich (f_d > 5e-4) debris disks around F1-B9.5 stars within 200 pc without dust temperature restrictions to probe poorly sampled spectral types (F1-A7) and warmer belts; observed J=2-1 of 12CO, 13CO, and C18O in Band 6 because 12CO and 13CO can be optically thick and J=2-1 is optimal for 8-30 K gas.  
  > "In contrast to previous strategies, no criteria for dust temperature were applied in the target selection, but we restricted the sample to disks with $f_\mathrm{d}$ $>$5$\times10^{-4}$, as this proved to be a good indicator of the presence of CO gas (cite: moor2017)." (Sample selection); "An important lesson from previous observations of CO-bearing debris disks is that not only $^{12}$CO but even the $^{13}$CO line can be optically thick. Thus, reliable CO mass estimate requires the measurement of $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO, and C$^{18}$O lines." (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Used UVMULTIFIT in visibility space to model continuum emission and BIC to select the best profile; used curve-of-growth elliptical apertures on moment 0 maps for CO line fluxes; used survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier) to include upper limits when comparing CO luminosity distributions.  
  > "To model the spatial structure of the dust emission and to estimate the flux density of the disks at 1.33 mm, we used the UVMULTIFIT tool developed by (cite: martividal2014) to fit the observed visibilities." (Modeling of the continuum emission); "To infer the cumulative distribution functions of the given groups, we used the Kaplan-Meier estimator implemented in the `lifelines` package (cite: Davidson-Pilon2019), which considers not only detections, but also the measured CO line luminosity upper limits." (Detection rate of CO gas)
- Alternative: Tested point source, elliptical Gaussian, single Gaussian ring, and double narrow ring models for continuum visibility fitting; selected best models via BIC.  
  > "For the six spatially well resolved sources (HD 9985, HD 152989, HD 155853, HD 159595, HD 170116, and HD 176497), two surface brightness profile models were tested: an elliptical Gaussian and an azimuthally symmetric Gaussian ring model." (Modeling of the continuum emission); "To evaluate which of the best-fitting models obtained for the two different profile types has better quality, we used the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), which favored the Gaussian ring approach in all cases." (Modeling of the continuum emission)
- Alternative: Confronted both shielded secondary gas models and residual primordial (hybrid) disk models to explain CO-rich debris disks; neither could be ruled out.  
  > "By comparing the predictions of the latest model with the ages of known CO-rich disks, we found that for all of them it is conceivable that the gas is dominantly made by residual primordial material. Thus, the origin of the gas in CO-rich disks still remains an open question." (Summary and Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass uncertainties are formal only and do not include systematic uncertainties in dust opacity (which can reach a factor of several) or non-single dust temperatures.  
  > "We caution that the uncertainties given are formal errors only, taking into account the errors in the measured flux density, the distance of the system, and the derived dust temperature, but do not consider the possible systematic uncertainties associated with the value of the dust opacity, which can reach a factor of several (cite: krivov2021)" (Dust masses)
- Limitation (authors): CO mass estimates assume LTE at 20 K and ISM isotopic ratios; non-LTE/subthermal excitation, different kinetic temperatures, isotope-selective photodissociation, or fractionation can alter true CO masses by a factor of several.  
  > "For our adopted excitation temperature of 20 K and LTE, the $J=2$ rotational level is very efficiently populated, but a significantly different temperature and/or non-LTE environment may result in a lower fractional population than assumed and, accordingly, a higher CO mass." (CO mass estimates); "Taking all these effects into account, the actual mass of CO may differ by as much as several factors from what we have estimated, and is more likely to be higher." (CO mass estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Predicted collisional mass loss rates (M_dot_Dmin) have large uncertainties (40-60% formal, potentially much larger systematically) because smallest grain sizes may deviate from the blowout size in high-f_d or gas-bearing disks.  
  > "First, the typical formal uncertainty of the $\dot{M}_\mathrm{D_{min}}$ estimate is 40–60%, and the real uncertainty could be even substantially higher as there are a number of simplifying assumptions behind Eq. [\[eq:mdustprod\]](#eq:mdustprod)." (Second generation gas produced in a steady-state collisional cascade?)
- Limitation (authors): Secular planet stirring calculations assume a massless disk and neglect disk self-gravity, which would require a more massive planet if the disk mass exceeds the planet mass.  
  > "However, this calculation assumes a massless disk. If the self-gravity of the disk is taken into account, and if the mass of the disk exceeds that of the planet, the secular perturbation effect will be weaker, and a more massive planet than the ones inferred from the above approach is required for the sufficient excitation (cite: sefilian2024)." (Dynamical excitation of the disks)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO line fluxes are extracted in the image plane from moment 0 maps whereas continuum properties are fitted in the visibility plane with UVMULTIFIT.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The double-ring interpretation of HD 152989 is not statistically favored over a single Gaussian ring in the continuum by BIC and relies on visual inspection of the 12CO PV diagram at modest S/N.
