## Analysis recipe

1. **Align executions, rescale fluxes, self-calibrate, and image continuum and CO lines**: Fit 2D Gaussian to low-resolution continuum per execution to align to common phase centre; scale fluxes to concatenated LB executions with gencal; phase self-calibrate SB (2 rounds) then SB+LB (1 round); apply tables to line data, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, regrid with cvel2, and image with multi-scale tclean down to 1 sigma  
  > "All executions were aligned by fitting a 2D Gaussian to a low-angular-resolution image of the dust continuum in each execution, allowing us to identify the disc centre." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "The molecular line data were obtained by applying the same astrometric aligns, flux offset scales, and self-calibration tables to the original datasets without channel averaging, and subtracting the dust continuum using the task uvcontsub ." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "The dust continuum, 12 CO, and 13 CO datasets were imaged using the tclean task in CASA, employing the multi-scale deconvolution algorithm with scales corresponding to point sources, one beam size, and two beam sizes." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - software: CASA, gencal, gaingal, uvcontsub, cvel2, tclean
   - parameter: Phase centre: J2000 16h12m06.664505s -30d10m27.61789s  
  > "We then re-centred all images to a common phase centre, chosen as J2000 16h12m06.664505s -30d10m27.61789s" (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - parameter: Self-cal solint: 'inf', '360s' for SB; 'inf' for combined SB+LB  
  > "time interval solutions solint=’inf’, ’360s’ , and calibrating the data only using the task applymode=’calonly’" (2 OBSERVATIONS); "After one round of phase self-calibration where spectral windows and scans were combined and time interval solution of solint=’inf’ was applied" (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - parameter: Robust = 0.5 (continuum), 1.5 (12CO at 25 m/s), 2.0 (13CO at 50 m/s); clean threshold = 1 sigma  
  > "The fiducial products were generated with a spectral resolution of 25 m s −1 and a Briggs robust parameter of 1.5 for 12 CO, and 50 m s −1 with a robust parameter of 2.0 for 13 CO. For the dust continuum, we adopted a robust parameter of 0.5." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "however, we cleaned down to a 1 𝜎 threshold" (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to low-angular-resolution continuum image of each execution  
  > "All executions were aligned by fitting a 2D Gaussian to a low-angular-resolution image of the dust continuum in each execution, allowing us to identify the disc centre." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - validation: Checked that de-coherence is not an issue before flux rescaling; monitored SNR improvement at each self-cal iteration; explored grid of spectral resolutions and robust parameters  
  > "We double-check that de-coherence is not an important issue before applying the flux-rescaling." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "Additional tests using shorter solution intervals and amplitude self-calibration did not result in significant SNR improvements, so these were not applied to the final products." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
2. **Compute molecular moment maps, integrated fluxes, and disc radii (R68, R90)**: Generate moment 0, peak intensity, and Gaussian velocity maps using Discminer with sigma clipping; measure R68, R90, and integrated fluxes using curve of growth (COG) within 4 arcsec  
  > "The map of the dust continuum emission, and moment maps (computed using Discminer, Izquierdo et al. 2021) from 12 CO, and 13 CO are presented in Figure 1." (3.1 Dust continuum and molecular moment maps); "These values were derived using the emission curve of growth (COG), and omitting emission at radii greater than 4 ′′ from the disc centre to avoid contamination from a millimetre dust continuum background source" (3.1 Dust continuum and molecular moment maps)
   - software: Discminer
   - parameter: Clipping: 1 sigma for Moment 0; 3 sigma for peak intensity and peak velocity; outer radius cutoff = 4 arcsec  
  > "The moments were computed by clipping the data to 1 𝜎 for Moment 0 (Integrated intensity map), and 3 𝜎 for the peak intensity map, and the peak velocity." (3.1 Dust continuum and molecular moment maps); "omitting emission at radii greater than 4 ′′ from the disc centre to avoid contamination from a millimetre dust continuum background source" (3.1 Dust continuum and molecular moment maps)
   - assumption: Distance = 132.1 pc  
  > "J16120 is located in the Upper Scorpius star-forming region at a distance of 132.1 pc (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023)" (1 INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: Gaussian velocity centroid fitting in Discminer  
  > "First row: Dust continuum map (left), Moment 0 (middle-left), peak intensity (middle-right), and Gaussian velocity map (right) map of the 12 CO emission." (Figure 1 caption)
   - validation: Compared derived radii with Band 6 values from Agurto-Gangas et al. (2025)  
  > "Both the dust continuum and 12 CO radii are consistent (within the error bars and taking into account the beam difference) with the values reported in Agurto-Gangas et al. (2025) at ALMA Band 6." (3.1 Dust continuum and molecular moment maps)
3. **Model axisymmetric dust continuum visibilities and radial spectral index**: Extract continuum visibilities using export_uvtable normalising uv-distances per spw/channel; constrain inclination and PA by minimizing spread of real visibilities and centre by minimizing imaginary visibilities; fit non-parametric radial brightness profiles in Band 6 and Band 7 with Frankenstein; compute full Planck brightness temperature and spectral index profile  
  > "In both cases, the continuum visibilities were extracted from the self-calibrated measurement sets using the function export_uvtable in Tazzari (2017), but normalising the uv-distances with the wavelength of each spectral windows and channel." (3.2 Dust continuum emission morphology); "Before modelling the visibilities, we compute constraints to the disc geometry by minimizing the spread of the real part of the visibilities (Isella et al. 2019) in both Band 6 and 7." (3.2 Dust continuum emission morphology); "The Frankenstein visibility modelling of the self-calibrated dust continuum visibilities of the disc around J16120 at ALMA Band 6 ( 𝜆 = 1 . 3 mm) and 7 ( 𝜆 = 0 . 87 mm) are shown in the left panel of Figure 2." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
   - software: uvplot, frankenstein
   - parameter: Geometry fixed to Band 6 values: inc = 37.0 deg, PA = 45.1 deg; Frankenstein hyperparameters: alpha = 1.05, w_smooth = 0.1  
  > "inc=37 . 0 + − 0 0 . . 1 2 deg, PA=45 . 1 + − 0 0 . . 2 9 deg in Band 6. These values are identical. From here on out, we use the inclination and position angle inferred from the optically thinner Band 6 as the geometric constraints." (3.2 Dust continuum emission morphology); "Additionally, in both cases we fit the visibilities using the hyper-parameters 𝛼 = 1 . 05, and 𝑤 smooth = 0 . 1." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric dust disc; full Planck function (without Rayleigh-Jeans approximation)  
  > "In the former, we use frankenstein (Jennings et al. 2020), which assumes and axisymmetric disc" (3.2 Dust continuum emission morphology); "The middle panel of Figure 2 shows the brightness temperature radial profile obtained from the Frankenstein fit, without assuming the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
   - fitting: Non-parametric Gaussian process visibility fitting in Frankenstein; multi-wavelength solid distribution modelling following Sierra et al. (2024b)  
  > "We also use the multi-wavelength modelling described in Sierra et al. (2024b) to study the distribution of solids (such as maximum grain size, temperature, and dust surface density" (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
   - validation: Tested various Frankenstein hyperparameter combinations; imaged residual visibilities (Data - Frankenstein fit)  
  > "Tests with various hyper-parameter combinations revealed no significant differences." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc); "The left panel of Figure 3 shows the map of the residual visibilities (Data - Frankestein fit)." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
4. **Fit eccentric parametric dust disc model in the visibility plane**: Model dust continuum with three eccentric Gaussian rings (g0, g23, g75); compute model visibilities with Galario and sample 13-parameter posterior with emcee  
  > "We use the Python library emcee introduced in Foreman-Mackey et al. (2013) to explore the posterior distributions of the parameters describing the disc model, and Galario to compute their visibilities." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc); "The disc model consists of three Gaussians: 𝑔 0, 𝑔 23, 𝑔 75, which mimic the ring like structure inferred from the axisymmetric disc." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc)
   - software: Galario, emcee
   - parameter: Pixel size = 10 mas, image size = 1024 pixels; fixed inc = 37.0 deg, PA = 45.1 deg; 13 free parameters; 104 walkers (8 x 13), 8000 steps  
  > "The pixel size and number of pixels are fixed to 10 mas and 1024, respectively. The inclination and position angle are fixed at 37.0 deg and 45.1 deg, respectively, to avoid degeneracies with the disc periastron." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc); "The number of walkers is set to eight times the number of free parameters. Convergence is assessed by inspecting the walker chains and their autocorrelation times after 8000 steps." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc)
   - assumption: Constant eccentricity e and periastron omega across the three Gaussian rings  
  > "The centre ( 𝑟 𝑖 ), amplitude ( 𝐴 𝑖 ), and width ( 𝜎 𝑖 ) of each Gaussian are free parameters of the model, in addition to the disc offset (ΔRA, ΔDec), the eccentricity ( 𝑒 ), and periastron ( 𝜔 ), resulting in a total of 13 free parameters." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee using uniform (linear) priors: e in [0, 1], omega in [-pi, pi]  
  > "For the eccentricity, we explore values between 0 and 1, and for the periastron between − 𝜋 and 𝜋 . In all cases, we adopt uniform (linear) priors." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc)
   - validation: Assessed convergence via walker chains and autocorrelation times; compared residual map and chi-squared against axisymmetric model and models with additional rings  
  > "Convergence is assessed by inspecting the walker chains and their autocorrelation times after 8000 steps." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc); "We also tested models that include additional rings in the fit, but neither the residual maps nor the chi-squared values show significant improvement." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc)
5. **Model 12CO and 13CO channel maps and extract kinematic, line-width, and peak-intensity residuals**: Fit 3D Keplerian channel map models to 12CO and 13CO cubes using discminer with emcee; fit Gaussian line profiles to data and best-fit model cubes to extract centroid velocity, line width, peak intensity, residual maps, and 3D velocity components (v_r, v_phi, v_z); test warped disc model on velocity residuals  
  > "We use discminer (Izquierdo et al. 2021) to fit the intensities and rotation velocities encoded in the channel maps of the 12 CO (J=3–2) and 13 CO (J=3–2) emission from this target." (3.3.1 Disc kinematics); "Because the backside of the disc does not contribute significantly to the observed emission (see discussion in Izquierdo et al. 2025, or model images in Figure C1), we adopt Gaussian profiles to estimate centroid velocity, peak intensity, and line-width maps from both the data and the corresponding discminer model." (3.3.1 Disc kinematics); "We use the methodology and tools in Winter et al. (2025) to study the velocity residual maps in our work, and look for radial variations of the disc inclination and position angle." (4.2.1 Planet Kinematic Signatures in the Velocity Residual Map)

## Qualitative findings

- Visibility modelling of Band 7 and Band 6 continuum reveals an inner disc, two bright rings at 23 and 75 au (B23, B75), and two gaps at 14 and 39 au (D14, D39), with spectral index minima at the rings indicative of dust trapping.  
  > "Two bright rings at 23 and 75 au (B23, B75, respectively) are independently inferred in both wavelengths in addition to the inner bright disc, and two gaps at 14 and 39 au (D14, D39, respectively)." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc); "The right panel in Figure 2 presents the radial spectral index profile between the two wavelengths, revealing local minima associated with the bright rings and local maxima located in the gaps." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
- No compact circumplanetary disc emission is detected in Band 7 within the gap down to a 3-sigma limit of ~300 uJy, likely due to correlated noise in the gap or intrinsic faintness.  
  > "The point source is not detected in the Band 7 dust-continuum observations presented in this work." (4.1.1 No ALMA Band 7 CPD Detection); "Therefore, we can rule out Band 7 ( 𝜆 = 0 . 87 mm) point sources within the gap with a SNR > 3 𝜎 gap ∼ 300 𝜇 Jy." (4.1.1 No ALMA Band 7 CPD Detection)
- The J16120 disc is gravitationally stable across all gas mass estimates (M_disc / M_star ~ 1/2000 to 1/200), ruling out gravitational instability as the driver of the substructures and eccentricity.  
  > "Nevertheless, the gas disc mass estimated from different methodologies is smaller than the dynamical mass of the central star by a factor of ∼200–2000, confirming that the disc is gravitationally stable and the observed disc morphologies are not driven by gravitational instabilities." (3.3.3 Gas mass)
- Outer kink-like features in 12CO and 13CO channel maps (125-171 au) are reproduced by a Keplerian model combining front- and back-side disc emission surfaces, rather than an outer planet.  
  > "An inspection of the Keplerian discminer model (top panels of Figure C1) shows that the combined front- and backside emission can reproduce these structures, resulting in negligible residuals" (4.2 Signatures of a Planet in the Gas Emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-sensitivity, high-angular-resolution, and high-spectral-resolution ALMA Band 7 observations of continuum, 12CO, and 13CO were obtained to test previous millimetre, IR, and H-alpha hints of an embedded forming planet in J16120 across both dust and gas kinematics.  
  > "These observations reinforce earlier reports of a forming planet in the gap of J16120 and constitute a significant step forward in the detection of planetary signatures in protoplanetary discs." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Visibility-plane modelling (Frankenstein and Galario) resolves sub-beam continuum rings and tests axisymmetric vs. eccentric morphologies; 3D channel-map modelling (Discminer) isolates non-Keplerian velocity, line-width, and surface perturbations while accounting for front/back emission surfaces.  
  > "Therefore, to explore both scenarios, we study the dust continuum morphology in the visibility plane, assuming both an axisymmetric disc (Section 3.2.1) and an eccentric disc (Section 3.2.2)." (3.2 Dust continuum emission morphology); "To study the presence of perturbations in the disc, we compare the best-fit model channels with those of the data to extract residual maps that reveal deviations from Keplerian rotation, as well as gas substructures manifested as intensity and line-width fluctuations." (3.3.1 Disc kinematics)
- Alternative: Gravitational instability (GI) as the driver of disc substructures and eccentricity, rejected because the disc-to-star mass ratio is ~1/2000 to 1/200 (well below 0.1).  
  > "Nevertheless, the gas disc mass estimated from different methodologies is smaller than the dynamical mass of the central star by a factor of ∼200–2000, confirming that the disc is gravitationally stable and the observed disc morphologies are not driven by gravitational instabilities." (3.3.3 Gas mass)
- Alternative: A stellar binary companion within 0.1-10 au driving the disc eccentricity, disfavoured based on Gaia astrometry and near-IR/optical imaging.  
  > "However, using Gaia measurements, Vioque et al. (2026) found no evidence that the astrometry of J16120 is consistent with a stellar companion within ∼0.1–10 au, although spectroscopic binaries could still be present at separations below 0.1 au." (4.1.2 Eccentricity as a Signature of an Embedded Planet)
- Alternative: Shadowing by a misaligned/warped inner disc driving the eccentricity, rejected because the maximum warp inclination variation (~4 deg) is smaller than the gas opening angle (~5.7 deg).  
  > "We found that the highest inclination difference is ∼ 4 deg. This difference is smaller than the 12 CO gas opening angle estimated from the emission surface or the isothermal scale height in Section 3.3.2, 𝐻 / 𝑟> 0 . 1 (or ∼ 5 . 7 deg)." (4.1.2 Eccentricity as a Signature of an Embedded Planet)
- Alternative: A disc warp explaining the velocity residuals: a warp model (~4 deg inclination, ~3 deg PA variation) reproduces the large-scale velocity residuals inside 60 au, but fails to reproduce the localised positive velocity residual at ~50 au near the planet candidate.  
  > "The best-fitting model reproduces most of the observed residuals at 𝑟< 60 au, and the positive residuals on the western side of the disc (left panel of Figure D). However, it fails in reproducing the positive localised velocity residual in the vicinity of the planet candidate at ∼ 50 au" (4.2.1 Planet Kinematic Signatures in the Velocity Residual Map)
- How they decided it was enough: Stopped self-calibration when shorter solution intervals and amplitude self-cal gave no significant SNR improvement; selected fiducial line cubes from a grid of robust/velocity resolutions to optimize SNR for small-scale structures; stopped adding Gaussian rings in Galario when chi-squared and residuals stopped improving.  
  > "Additional tests using shorter solution intervals and amplitude self-calibration did not result in significant SNR improvements, so these were not applied to the final products." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "We also tested models that include additional rings in the fit, but neither the residual maps nor the chi-squared values show significant improvement." (3.2.2 Eccentric dust disc)
- Limitation (authors): Two millimetre wavelengths (Band 6 and Band 7) are insufficient to uniquely constrain dust grain size, temperature, and surface density due to strong degeneracies, and flux calibration uncertainties can shift the spectral index by ~0.5.  
  > "All these values are subject to uncertainties in the flux calibration, which can vertically shift the spectral index radial profile by ∼ 0 . 5." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc); "However, as discussed in Viscardi et al. (2025), using only two millimetre wavelengths is insufficient to constrain the dust properties due to significant degeneracies." (3.2.1 Axisymmetric dust disc)
- Limitation (authors): The radial separation between B23 and D39 is approximately one beam size, and radial/vertical velocity uncertainties at r < 75 au are large (consistent with zero), so local kinematic variations and meridional flow hints must be interpreted with caution.  
  > "The distance between B23 and D39 is approximately one beam size; therefore, local variations within this resolution element should be interpreted with caution." (3.3.1 Disc kinematics); "Note that the velocity uncertainties remain significant for 𝑟< 75 au and are also consistent with zero, so these measurements should be interpreted with particular care." (4.2.3 Hints of Gas Flow Towards the Planet’s Orbit)
- Limitation (authors): The gas mass derived from the 12CO emitting surface is only a lower limit because the model grid assumes a lower stellar mass (0.6 M_sun vs 0.7 M_sun) and a smooth disc without an inner cavity.  
  > "therefore our inferred disc mass from the height analysis is a lower boundary of the system, in agreement to previous constraints. Additionally, the models are full gas disc models (no substructures), however our system has a clear inner cavity" (3.3.3 Gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial smearing by the beam can artificially reduce the observed line-width enhancement, making the ~1 M_Jup mass estimate from comparison with Dong et al. (2019) a lower limit.  
  > "However, it is important to note that spatial smearing can artificially reduce the observed velocity dispersion, as demonstrated by Izquierdo et al. (2026b), implying that the inferred planet mass may be underestimated" (4.2.2 Line width residuals revealing an embedded planet)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties from the MCMC walker distributions in Discminer do not account for spatial pixel correlations and may be underestimated by up to an order of magnitude.  
  > "Using spatial correlations in the image plane, Hilder et al. (2025) showed that accurately quantifying the true errors is not straightforward, but that they may be up to an order of magnitude larger than those inferred from the Bayesian analysis." (APPENDIX B: BEST-FIT MODEL PARAMETERS)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The eccentric dust model fits a single global eccentricity and periastron to all three Gaussian rings, whereas hydrodynamic theory (and the authors' own discussion in Section 4.1.2) expects eccentricity to vary radially across the planet's orbit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The non-axisymmetric spiral-like velocity residual inside 60 au is largely reproduced by a mild (~4 deg) warp model (Appendix D), meaning the Doppler-flip interpretation is not unique without independent constraints on inner-disc misalignment.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The B23 dust ring sits at the exact deprojected radius (~23 au) of the previously reported SPHERE K-band and H-alpha protoplanet detections, creating tension with standard gap-carving expectations unless the dust is trapped in co-orbital horseshoe/Trojan orbits.
