## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble the extended sample**: Select 17 ALPAKA disks with robust M* and SFR, add literature cold-gas disk kinematics with M* >~ 1e10 Msun; low-z: Delta_MS >~ 0.3; z >~ 0.5: at least 3 resolution elements along the major axis  
  > "we selected the 17 ALPAKA disks with robust" (ALPAKA and the extended sample); "We specifically selected galaxies from the literature covered by at least 3 independent resolution elements along the major axis of the gas disk." (2.2 High-z subsamples)
   - parameter: Low-z selection: Delta_MS >~ 0.3 and CO kinematics (23 galaxies)  
  > "we select 23 galaxies with kinematic data obtained from CO observations and $\Delta_{\mathrm{MS}} \gtrsim 0.3$" (2.1 Low-z subsample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit 3D kinematics of PHANGS galaxies and define sigma and V/sigma**: Apply 3DBarolo to the public PHANGS 12-m+7-m CO(2-1) cubes; sigma is the radial average of the dispersion profile and V/sigma uses the maximum V  
  > "are derived after applying $^{\scriptstyle 3\mathrm{D}}$B (cite: DiTeodoro_2015) to the publicly available data cubes" (2.1 Low-z subsample); "The $V/\sigma$ values are obtained as a ratio between the maximum value of the rotation velocity and the average velocity dispersion." (ALPAKA and the extended sample)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compute offset from the star-forming main sequence**: Delta_MS = SFR/SFR_MS(M*, z) with the Popesso et al. 2023 MS prescription normalised to a Chabrier IMF  
  > "using the prescriptions recently published by (cite: Popesso_2023) and normalized to a Chabrier Initial Mass Function (cite: Chabrier_2003)" (ALPAKA and the extended sample)
   - assumption: SFR values from the literature are normalised to a common IMF  
  > "We normalized the SFR values to a common IMF" (6.3 Considerations on SFR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit sigma-z and V/sigma-z empirical relations**: Fit exponential forms sigma = a + b exp(-z0/z) + s and V/sigma = a + b exp(-z/z0) + s with a hierarchical Bayesian method  
  > "The fits are performed adopting a hierarchical Bayesian method and sampling the parameter space by means of an Hamiltonian Monte Carlo algorithm" (3.1 Empirical relations using the extended sample)
   - software: scattr
   - parameter: No U-Turn Sampler adaptation of the HMC step length  
  > "with No U-Turn Sampler (cite: Hoffman_2011) adaptation of the HMC step length" (9 Fitting empirical relations)
   - parameter: Wide uniform priors on a, b, z0 and s (Table 3)  
  > "For the prior probability of each model parameter, we assume wide uniform priors" (9.1 Fitting equations)
   - assumption: z has negligible uncertainties and no intrinsic scatter; measurement errors normal; data points independent  
  > "we assume $z$ to represent a set of measurements not affected by intrinsic scatter and characterized by negligible uncertainties" (9.1 Fitting equations)
   - fitting: HMC with NUTS; normal likelihood with intrinsic scatter s as a free parameter; product of per-point likelihoods; uniform priors  
  > "Since the data points are statistically independent, the full likelihood is the product of the likelihood function for the individual data points." (9.1 Fitting equations)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare cold-gas kinematics with H-alpha samples**: Compare medians of sigma and V/sigma from SAMI, DYNAMO, Hogan+21 and KAOSS H-alpha samples with extended-sample cold-gas medians in matching redshift bins and matched M* and Delta_MS  
  > "we make comparisons using samples that match the stellar masses and SFRs of our extended sample" (3.2 H-alpha observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Test the disk-instability model**: Compute sigma_exp,DI and (V/sigma)_exp,DI from Toomre-based equations with Q = 1 for main-sequence galaxies (M* = 10^10.5, V = 200 km/s) and for the extended sample with measured V and f_gas, then refit with the empirical forms  
  > "We fix the stellar mass at $10^{10.5} M_{\odot}$ and use $V = 200$ km/s, the average rotation velocity typically measured for massive main-sequence galaxies" (3.2 Models calibrated on H-alpha observations)
   - parameter: Q = 1 (marginal instability)  
  > "which is the typical value for marginal instability" (3.2 Models calibrated on H-alpha observations)
   - assumption: Cold and warm gas sigma are similar (Wisnioski assumption); disks marginally unstable  
  > "cold gas disks are marginally unstable to perturbations; and the cold gas $\sigma$ values are similar to the warm gas ones" (3.2 Models calibrated on H-alpha observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model compared with cold-gas data: overestimates sigma by factors 2-4 at z >~ 1  
  > "systematically overestimate the values of $\sigma$ from cold gas by factors of 2 - 4 at $z \gtrsim 1$" (3.2 Models calibrated on H-alpha observations)
7. **Compare sigma-SFR distribution with feedback models**: Overplot K18 feedback and transport+feedback models on the sigma-SFR plane using K18 fiducial high-z parameters and the sample ranges  
  > "we assume the fiducial parameters (i.e., rotation velocities and orbital times) for high-$z$ galaxies reported by K18 and we use the range of values of our extended sample" (4 Drivers of turbulence)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Calibrate the supernova-driven sigma-SFR relation**: Fit log sigma = 0.33 log SFR + b with slope fixed at 0.33 (F21 energy balance), extending the hierarchical model to uncertainties in both variables and SFR upper limits  
  > "we fixed its slope at 0.33, the value expected in the case of F21 feedback-driven turbulence" (5.2 Calibrating the model); "we thus encode the available upper limits in the probability function" (9.2 Fitting equation)
   - software: scattr
   - parameter: Uniform prior on offset b in [-100, 100]  
  > "we assume a wide uniform prior in the range $[-100, 100]$" (9.2 Fitting equation)
   - assumption: CC = [eps_SN h/M_gas]^(1/3) is approximately constant over cosmic time (CC ~ 1e-3, h ~ 200 pc)  
  > "the factor $CC = [\epsilon_{\mathrm{SN}} (h/\mathrm{pc})/(M_{\mathrm{gas}}/M_{\odot}]^{1/3}$ remains approximately constant over cosmic time" (5.1 Feedback-driven model)
   - fitting: Hierarchical Bayesian HMC; upper limits via the cumulative normal following Sawicki et al. 2012  
  > "following the prescription by (cite: Sawicki_2012)" (9.2 Fitting equation)
   - validation: Free-slope fit and leave-one-out cross-validation / WAIC favour the fixed 0.33 slope; residual correlation tests  
  > "the leave-one-out cross-validation and the widely applicable information criterion methods (cite: Vehtari_2015) indicate that the model with the fixed slope is favored over the one with the free value" (5.2 Calibrating the model)
9. **Predict sigma(z) for populations and V/sigma lower limits**: Combine the sigma-SFR relation with the Popesso et al. 2023 main-sequence evolution, and divide the local stellar Tully-Fisher velocity (Reyes et al. 2011) by the model sigma  
  > "By assuming the prescription of the redshift evolution of the SFR proposed by (cite: Popesso_2023)" (5.3 Turbulence across cosmic time); "we can use the local stellar TFR to determine the lower limits of the velocity of galaxies at fixed stellar masses" (5.3 Turbulence across cosmic time)
   - assumption: At fixed stellar mass, high-z galaxies have velocities at least those of z = 0 galaxies (LCDM expectation)  
  > "at fixed stellar mass, high-$z$ galaxies have higher velocity than $z = 0$ galaxies" (5.3 Turbulence across cosmic time)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model compared with the extended sample in three stellar mass bins; judged in good agreement  
  > "The model is in good agreement with observations," (5.3 Turbulence across cosmic time)
10. **Test residuals of the sigma-SFR relation**: Bootstrap datasets, Pearson correlation of orthogonal residuals with log sigma and log SFR, and partial correlation  
  > "we simulate multiple (N = 10000) datasets through bootstrapping" (12 Statistical analysis of the residuals)
   - parameter: N = 10000 bootstrap datasets  
  > "we simulate multiple (N = 10000) datasets through bootstrapping" (12 Statistical analysis of the residuals)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residuals show no significant correlation with log sigma and only weak with log SFR  
  > "there is only a weak and no statistically significant correlation between residuals and $\log\mathrm{SFR}$, and between residuals and $\log\sigma$, respectively" (12 Statistical analysis of the residuals)

## Qualitative findings

- Massive galaxy disks are dynamically cold (high V/sigma) up to z ~ 5 despite turbulence higher than local  
  > "The high values of $V/\sigma$ up to $z \sim 5$ indicate that massive galaxy disks are dynamically cold across cosmic time" (3.1 Empirical relations using the extended sample)
- Sigma from H-alpha is about 3 times higher (V/sigma lower) than from cold gas for similar M* and Delta_MS  
  > "The values of $\sigma$ ($V/\sigma$) from H$\alpha$ are a factor of $\approx 3$ higher (lower) than the ones from cold gas both for low and high-$z$ galaxies." (Summary and conclusions)
- The sigma-SFR distribution agrees with the K18 stellar feedback model while the transport+feedback model overpredicts sigma  
  > "The $\sigma$-SFR distribution of our sample aligns with the predictions of the stellar feedback model proposed by K18." (4 Drivers of turbulence)
- Predicted turbulence evolution comes entirely from main-sequence evolution; mild increase z = 0-2 and none at z > 2  
  > "The models shown in Fig. [6](#fig:model) suggest only a mild redshift evolution of turbulence motion within the range $z = 0$ - 2 and no significant evolution at $z > 2$." (5.3 Turbulence across cosmic time)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: No systematic high-resolution CO surveys exist at z = 0.5-4, so ALMA archival data mined for ALPAKA are combined with literature  
  > "there have been no high-resolution systematic surveys of CO at $z = 0.5 - 4$, but only observations of single targets" (ALPAKA and the extended sample)
- Why this method: F21 model chosen for its few free parameters rather than the K18 models  
  > "The F21 model has fewer free parameters, enabling us to better control and understand their impact on the results." (5.1 Feedback-driven model)
- Alternative: Slopes of 0.5 and 1 for the sigma-SFR relation (momentum-based) were fitted and rejected; only 0.33 reproduces the data  
  > "the relation with a slope of $m = 0.33$ is the only one that accurately reproduces the data" (11.2 Energy vs momentum)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Beam smearing and spectral resolution comparable to sigma may bias high-z sigma upward  
  > "velocity dispersion values may be slightly overestimated" (6.1 Uncertainties on the velocity dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): SFRs come from heterogeneous methods in the literature  
  > "the SFR values of the extended sample are derived from diverse methodologies and assumptions as they are taken from the literature" (6.3 Considerations on SFR)
- Limitation (authors): Small and heterogeneous sample limits detailed comparisons  
  > "the current limitations in sample size and statistics hinder such investigations at present" (2.3 Considerations on the extended sample)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample is heterogeneous in selection, tracers (CO, [CI], [CII]) and per-paper 3DBarolo analysis, so sigma values are not measured uniformly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): [CII] sigma at z > 4 may include contributions from non-molecular gas
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cold vs warm comparison uses different galaxies and mostly medians, not matched sources
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sigma-SFR slope of 0.33 is partly assumed (fixed) and the CC constancy argument relies on rough order-of-magnitude values
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High-z sigma near the spectral resolution (~30 km/s) may bias the sigma-z plateau
