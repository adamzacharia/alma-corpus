## Reasoning

- Why this data: FIR fine-structure lines like [C II] 158 μm and [O I] 63 μm are unaffected by dust extinction, have low excitation potentials (~100 K) weakly dependent on gas temperature, and together with FIR continuum constrain PDR gas physical conditions and kinematics at sub-arcsecond resolution.  
  > "Second, the excitation potential of fir fs lines, of order 100 K, is very low compared to that of optical lines, and comparable with physical gas temperatures, so fir line emissivities are only weakly dependent on gas temperature." (1. INTRODUCTION); "However, in combination with other spectral lines and the far-ir continuum emission, one can derive the physical conditions of the emitting gas and its cooling rate, and therefore the properties of the sources of gas heating." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Fitting the rotating disk model in the visibility (uv) plane via UVModelDisk avoids computing pixel covariance matrices because visibilities are statistically independent whereas image pixels are correlated.  
  > "Within UVModelDisk, the goodnessof-fit parameter is computed by transforming the disk model to the uv plane and comparing the resulting visibilities to the calibrated Alma visibilities. This is beneficial due to the fact that visibilities are statistically independent, while pixels in Alma images are not, and thus no covariance matrices need to be computed when running UVModelDisk." (3.2. Disk fitting)
- Alternative: Low-G PDR solution (n = 1.5 × 10^5 cm^-3, G = 3 G_0) rejected because it requires an implausible beam filling factor of 5000
