## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract and isolate the MIRI SO2 spectrum**: Extract at the 5.5 μm continuum peak; subtract a fourth-order local continuum and a Gaussian 7.25 μm ice feature.  
  > "only one spectrum was manually extracted from the peak of the mid-infrared continuum" (MIRI-MRS); "A fourth order polynomial was fitted through obvious line-free channels" (Continuum subtraction around SO$_2$ $\nu_3$ band); "Using this Gaussian fit, the contribution of the 7.25 $\mu$m ice feature was subtracted, providing a spectrum with only the contribution of the molecular emission" (Continuum subtraction around SO$_2$ $\nu_3$ band)
   - parameter: Extraction aperture diameter: 4×FWHM_PSF; no additional spectral stitching.  
  > "The diameter of the aperture was set to $4\times{\rm FWHM_{PSF}}$" (MIRI-MRS); "No additional spectral stitching was applied between the bands." (MIRI-MRS)
   - parameter: Continuum anchors: 7.015, 7.113, 7.162, 7.442, 7.489, 7.545, 7.635 μm.  
  > "7.015, 7.113, 7.162, 7.442, 7.489, 7.545, 7.635 $\mu$m" (Continuum subtraction around SO$_2$ $\nu_3$ band)
   - parameter: Fit the ice Gaussian over 7.1–7.26 μm.  
  > "a simple Gaussian model was fitted to the part of the spectrum where no clear molecular emission features are present ($7.1-7.26$ $\mu$m" (Continuum subtraction around SO$_2$ $\nu_3$ band)
   - assumption: Extracted spectrum contains only emission related to IRAS2A1.  
  > "is assumed to only contain emission related to IRAS2A1 (hereafter IRAS2A)." (MIRI-MRS)
   - fitting: Fourth-order polynomial and Gaussian ice-band fit.  
  > "A fourth order polynomial was fitted through obvious line-free channels" (Continuum subtraction around SO$_2$ $\nu_3$ band); "a simple Gaussian model was fitted" (Continuum subtraction around SO$_2$ $\nu_3$ band)
   - validation: SO2 model reveals an overestimated continuum in the excluded 7.4 μm region.  
  > "clearly shows that the local continuum was estimated too high in the 7.4 $\mu$m region, proving that in fact the 7.4 $\mu$m ice absorption feature is present." (MIRI-MRS)
2. **Extract ALMA spectra and compare emission sizes**: Extract aperture spectra; construct integrated-intensity maps; deconvolve emission sizes with the beam and compare with MIRI.  
  > "spectra are extracted from a $1.4''$ diameter aperture centered on the continuum position of IRAS2A1." (ALMA data); "After deconvolution with the beam, the size of the emission originating from the SO$_2$ $16_{4,12}-16_{3,13}$ transition is $\sim0.5''\times0.3''$ in diameter" (Comparison to ALMA data); "with the compact emission in the MIRI-MRS data (i.e., $0.5''\times0.3''$ in diameter" (Comparison to ALMA data)
   - parameter: 1.4'' diameter aperture; spectral noise 0.015 Jy; 5% flux calibration uncertainty.  
  > "spectra are extracted from a $1.4''$ diameter aperture centered on the continuum position of IRAS2A1." (ALMA data); "The noise level of the extracted spectrum is 0.015 Jy, and a flux calibration uncertainty of 5% is assumed." (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare SO2 extents with MIRI SO2, HDO, methanol and methyl formate.  
  > "These emitting areas are consistent with that of HDO $3_{3,1}-4_{2,2}$ ($E_{\rm up} = 335$ K) and complex organics such as methanol (CH$_3$OH) and methyl formate" (Comparison to ALMA data); "with the compact emission in the MIRI-MRS data (i.e., $0.5''\times0.3''$ in diameter" (Comparison to ALMA data)
3. **Construct the infrared extinction correction**: Separate differential ice/silicate extinction from a powerlaw absolute-extinction component.  
  > "The total extinction (in units of optical depth) toward IRAS2A ($\tau_{\rm tot}(\lambda) = A_{\rm\lambda}$/1.086) is therefore decomposed into two components" (Extinction correction); "The optical depth is computed with respect to a third-order polynomial fitted through obvious absorption free wavelengths (cite: Rochasubm)" (Extinction correction)
   - parameter: Powerlaw fit windows: 4.9–5.4, 7.2–7.3, 28–29 μm; AV=1 mag normalization.  
  > "Only the wavelengths outside of the major absorption features are taken into account (i.e., 4.9-5.4,7.2-7.3,28-29 $\mu$m)." (Extinction correction); "The extinction law is fitted for the case of $A_{\rm V}=1$ mag (cite: McClure2009)" (Extinction correction)
   - parameter: τext(λ) = 0.085 λ^-0.25 AV.  
  > "\tau_{\rm ext}(\lambda) = 0.085 \lambda^{-0.25} A_{\rm V}." (Extinction correction)
   - assumption: AV=55 from silicate absorption; McClure law for AK>1.  
  > "Here, $A_{\rm V} = 55$ is adopted based in the depth of the silicate absorption (cite: Rochasubm)" (Extinction correction); "the extinction law of (cite: McClure2009) for an extinction of $A_{\rm K} > 1$." (Extinction correction)
   - fitting: Fit the powerlaw exponent as well as its normalization.  
  > "In this case, the power of the powerlaw is also fitted." (Extinction correction); "\tau_{\rm ext}(\lambda) = 0.085 \lambda^{-0.25} A_{\rm V}." (Extinction correction)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit the MIRI SO2 rotational distribution**: LTE slab grid in column density and excitation temperature; optimize emitting area at each point.  
  > "The molecular emission features in the continuum-subtracted spectra were fitted with simple slab models assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (LTE slab model fitting); "For each grid point, the LTE spectrum of SO$_2$ was calculated at a spectral resolving power of $R=3500$ and the best-fitting emitting area was computed by minimizing the $\chi^2$ (cite: Grant2023)." (LTE slab model fitting)
   - software: slab model code, HITRAN, TIPS_2021_PYTHON
   - parameter: N: 10^14–10^19 cm^-2, steps 0.05 in log10; Tex: 50–250 K, steps 1 K.  
  > "creating a large grid covering $10^{14}-10^{19}$ cm$^{-2}$ in steps of 0.05 in $\log_{10}$ and $50-250$ K in steps of 1 K, respectively." (LTE slab model fitting)
   - parameter: Intrinsic ΔV=3.5 km s^-1; model resolving power R=3500.  
  > "The intrinsic velocity broadening $\Delta V$ was fixed to 3.5 km s$^{-1}$ based on ALMA data covering SO$_2$" (LTE slab model fitting); "the LTE spectrum of SO$_2$ was calculated at a spectral resolving power of $R=3500$" (LTE slab model fitting)
   - parameter: Fit channels: 7.23–7.255, 7.258–7.268, 7.2755–7.3535 μm.  
  > "$7.23-7.255$, $7.258-7.268$, $7.2755-7.3535$ $\mu$m" (LTE slab model fitting)
   - parameter: Noise windows: 7.06–7.13, 7.195–7.225, 7.47–7.50 μm; noise about 0.59 mJy; calibration uncertainty 5%.  
  > "the noise level was estimated from line-free channels ($7.06-7.13$, $7.195-7.225$, $7.47-7.50$ $\mu$m) to be about 0.59 mJy" (LTE slab model fitting); "a flux calibration uncertainty of 5% is assumed (cite: Argyriou2023)." (LTE slab model fitting)
   - assumption: Single Tex, column N, emitting area πR².  
  > "a column density $N$, and an emitting area equal to $\pi R^2$." (LTE slab model fitting); "the emission is assumed to arise from a slab of gas with an excitation temperature $T_{\rm ex}$" (LTE slab model fitting)
   - assumption: Absolute extinction AV=55 mag; negligible differential ice extinction on molecular emission.  
  > "The LTE models are corrected for an absolute extinction of $A_{\rm V} = 55$ mag (cite: Rochasubm)" (LTE slab model fitting); "The effect of differential extinction caused by the 7.25 $\mu$m and 7.4 $\mu$m ice absorption on the molecular emission is assumed to be negligible." (LTE slab model fitting)
   - fitting: Grid χ2 minimization for N, Tex, R and uncertainties; exclude higher N and temperatures by visual inspection.  
  > "Any higher column densities or temperatures were excluded by visual inspection of the spectrum." (LTE slab model fitting); "The best-fit parameters ($N$, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $R$) and their uncertainties were derived from the grid by minimizing the $\chi^2$." (LTE slab model fitting)
   - validation: Check Q/R branch shapes and optical-depth dependence; compare LTE emitting extent with the resolved map.  
  > "The fit slightly overestimates the $Q$-branch around 7.35 $\mu$m but provides a very reasonable fit to the $R$-branch around 7.3 $\mu$m." (MIRI-MRS); "the SO$_2$ emission is optically thin since optically thick emission would give a lower ratio between the measured $R$- and $Q$-branch fluxes." (MIRI-MRS); "the data can only be accurately fitted for very large emitting area of $\gtrsim5000$ au, which is in strong contrast with the extent of the emission" (MIRI-MRS)
5. **Fit ALMA ground-state SO2 and 34SO2**: Use the same LTE slab procedure with CDMS line lists and relatively unblended rotational transitions.  
  > "The pure rotational lines of SO$_2$ and $^{34}$SO$_2$ detected by ALMA were fitted using the same LTE slab model fitting procedure as for the MIRI-MRS data" (Comparison to ALMA data); "Fortunately, two transitions of SO$_2$ and one transition of $^{34}$SO$_2$ are (relatively) unblended." (Comparison to ALMA data)
   - software: slab model code, Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy

## Qualitative findings

- MIRI ν3 SO2 emission is optically thin; column density and emitting area are degenerate.  
  > "the SO$_2$ emission is optically thin since optically thick emission would give a lower ratio between the measured $R$- and $Q$-branch fluxes." (MIRI-MRS); "The column density and emitting area are therefore completely degenerate with each other" (MIRI-MRS)
- ALMA and MIRI likely trace the same molecular gas, with infrared-pumped ν3 emission.  
  > "The similarity in rotational temperatures suggests that MIRI-MRS and ALMA are in fact still tracing the same molecular gas." (Conclusions); "The $\nu_3$ asymmetric stretching mode of SO$_2$ detected around 7.35 $\mu$m with MIRI is not in LTE but rather radiatively pumped by a strong infrared radiation field" (Conclusions)
- Central hot-core ice sublimation is favored over an accretion shock.  
  > "A hot core origin is therefore a more likely explanation for the gaseous SO$_2$." (Physical origin of the SO$_2$); "the observed gaseous SO$_2$ could be sublimated from the ices in the central hot core" (Physical origin of the SO$_2$)
- Gas-phase SO2 is not the dominant sulfur carrier in IRAS2A.  
  > "The abundance of $\sim10^{-8}$ implies that the SO$_2$ gas is not the dominant sulfur carrier in IRAS2A" (Physical origin of the SO$_2$)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine extended and compact ALMA configurations to include larger-scale emission; match ALMA extraction to MIRI.  
  > "in a more compact configuration (C-3, $\theta_{\rm beam} = 0.58''\times0.34''$) to include larger-scale emission." (ALMA data); "In order for direct comparison to the MIRI data, spectra are extracted from a $1.4''$ diameter aperture centered on the continuum position of IRAS2A1." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: LTE slabs can constrain rotational temperature within a vibrational state even when MIR emission is not in LTE.  
  > "Nevertheless, LTE models can still constrain physical quantities such as the rotational temperature within a vibrationally excited state." (LTE slab model fitting)
- Alternative: Low line-to-continuum contrast does not explain the absent ν2 band.  
  > "This suggests that this cannot explain the absence of the $\nu_2$ band since a line-to-continuum ratio down to 0.01 should still be detectable on a strong continuum." (Absence of the $\nu_1$ and $\nu_2$ bands)
- Alternative: Dust opacity differences cannot explain the ALMA/MIRI molecule-count discrepancy.  
  > "This discrepancy cannot be explained by a difference in dust opacity, given that such an effect would only worsen the discrepancy in the detected number of molecules between ALMA and MIRI." (Comparison to ALMA data)
- Alternative: Extended outflow origin is less likely.  
  > "an extended outflow origin is less likely." (Physical origin of the SO$_2$); "the emission in the MIRI data is clearly centrally peaked with only little extended emission" (Physical origin of the SO$_2$)
- Alternative: Accretion shocks remain abundance-compatible but morphology and narrow lines favor a hot core.  
  > "the line width of $\sim3.5$ km s$^{-1}$ is consistent with those of the complex organics whereas accretion shocks are expected to show broader emission lines" (Physical origin of the SO$_2$); "the emission is extended more in the direction of the outflow than along the disk." (Physical origin of the SO$_2$)
- Alternative: H2 lines may not trace the same gas as SO2, motivating a continuum-derived H2 estimate.  
  > "H$_2$ does not only trace the warm inner regions but is also present in outflows and disk winds" (Physical origin of the SO$_2$); "the rotational temperature of the warm component is $>300$ K" (Physical origin of the SO$_2$)
- How they decided it was enough: Exclude higher grid temperatures/columns by visual inspection; omit MIRI outlier rejection because it did not significantly improve quality.  
  > "Any higher column densities or temperatures were excluded by visual inspection of the spectrum." (LTE slab model fitting); "the outlier rejection routine was skipped because it did not significantly improve the quality of the data." (MIRI-MRS)
- Limitation (authors): No SO2 MIR ro-vibrational collisional rate coefficients; full non-LTE analysis unavailable.  
  > "For SO$_2$ no collisional rate coefficients are available for its ro-vibrational transitions in the MIR, therefore not allowing for a full non-LTE analysis" (Introduction)
- Limitation (authors): MIRI column density and emitting area cannot be separately constrained.  
  > "Given the degeneracy, no accurate column density of SO$_2$ can be derived" (MIRI-MRS)
- Limitation (authors): MIRI P-branch excluded because the 7.4 μm ice contribution cannot be disentangled a priori.  
  > "The $P$-branch lines around 7.4 $\mu$m were not included in the fit because the contribution of the 7.4 $\mu$m ice feature" (MIRI-MRS); "could not be disentangled from the gas-phase emission of SO$_2$ a priori." (MIRI-MRS)
- Limitation (authors): Several ALMA SO2/isotopologue lines are blended with complex-organic emission.  
  > "several of these transitions are blended with strong emission of complex organics such as CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$CHO." (Comparison to ALMA data)
- Limitation (authors): Weak extended MIRI SO2 could arise from the outflow or IRAS2A2.  
  > "Some very weak extended SO$_2$ emission (on $\sim500$ au scales) is also present in the direction of the blue-shifted lobe of the north-south outflow" (MIRI-MRS); "the extended SO$_2$ emission could also be related to IRAS2A2" (MIRI-MRS)
- Limitation (authors): A larger sample is needed to test luminosity dependence of MIR SO2 emission.  
  > "a larger sample of JWST/MIRI-MRS spectra of low-mass and high-mass embedded protostellar systems is needed to further investigate the importance of luminosity (and other source properties) on the presence of MIR SO$_2$ emission." (Physical origin of the SO$_2$)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA molecule count is inconsistent between sections: 1.6±0.2×10^47 versus 1.8±0.2×10^47.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two relatively unblended SO2 transitions and one 34SO2 transition constrain the ALMA fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The text calls τ∼0.1 marginally optically thick; the resulting column–area constraint deserves direct verification.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abundance depends on assumed dust temperature, opacity, gas-to-dust ratio and density-profile scaling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The printed dust-opacity units need clarification before implementing the mass equation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The pumping calculation is an approximate consistency test rather than a full non-LTE fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The hot-core interpretation is favored but does not uniquely exclude weak accretion shocks.
