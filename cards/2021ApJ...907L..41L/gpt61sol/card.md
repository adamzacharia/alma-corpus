## Science

- Fields: star_formation
- Question: Does the SO rotating outflow trace an extended MHD disk wind interacting with the episodic jet in HH 212?  
  > "providing a confirmation for an extended disk wind as well as the first evidence of jet-disk wind interaction first predicted by (cite: Tabone2018)." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Separate the jet, shells, and wide-angle outflow**: Separate SO by velocity; compare SO, SiO, continuum, and an intensity-weighted velocity map  
  > "We can unveil the wide-angle outflow by separating the SO emission into two velocity components." (Results); "This emission shows up better in an intensity-weighted velocity map" (Results); "the SO map in comparison to the SiO map of the jet and the continuum map of the dusty disk (cite: Lee2019COM)" (Results)
   - parameter: High velocity: more than ±3 km s^-1 from systemic velocity ~1.7 km s^-1  
  > "At high velocity (more than $\pm3$ km s$^{-1}$ away from the systemic velocity of $\sim$ 1.7 km s$^{-1}$" (Results)
   - parameter: Low velocity: within ±3 km s^-1 of systemic velocity  
  > "At low velocity (within $\pm$ 3 km s$^{-1}$ of the systemic velocity" (Results)
   - parameter: Channel-map velocities are LSR velocities  
  > "The velocities in the channel maps are LSR velocities." (Observations)
   - assumption: Distance ~400 pc  
  > "The HH 212 protostellar system (cite: Zinnecker1992) is a young system located in Orion at $\sim$ 400 pc" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Shells connect to downstream bow shocks; wide-angle outflow is bounded by the HCO+ infalling-rotating envelope  
  > "They are now detected further away and seen to smoothly connect to the SiO/SO bow shocks downstream at larger distances ($\sim$ 600 au)" (Results); "The wide-angle outflow has an outer boundary outlined by the inner infalling-rotating envelope traced by the high-velocity emission of HCO$^+$" (Results)
2. **Construct a wind, shell, and jet model**: Adopt L5W30; truncate the extended wind; replace inner wind outflow velocity by radial shell expansion  
  > "The same model is thus adopted here to compare with the SO wide-angle outflow resolved at higher resolution and sensitivity." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "We thus assume an extended disk wind with a launching radius of $r_0 \sim
4-40$ au for the wide-angle outflow" (MHD Disk Wind Model); "with its outflow velocity replaced by a radially expanding velocity $v_r= r/t_\textrm{\scriptsize s}$" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - parameter: M_*~0.2 M_sun; λ~5.5; W~30  
  > "Assuming $M_\ast \sim 0.2$$M_\odot$, (cite: Tabone2017; Tabone2020) found their disk wind Model L5W30 (with $\lambda \sim 5.5$ and $W \sim 30$)" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - parameter: Shell corresponds to model streamlines launched from 4-8 au  
  > "The inner part of the wind bounded by the model streamlines launched from 4 to 8 au roughly coincides with the base of the shells" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - parameter: Initial SO jet launching radius 0.10-0.20 au  
  > "The SO jet is assumed to have a launching radius of 0.10 to 0.20 au in the dust-free zone (cite: Tabone2020)." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: Steady-state, axisymmetric, self-similar wind from a geometrically thin Keplerian disk  
  > "a steady-state, axisymmetric, self-similar wind launched from a geometrically thin Keplerian disk (cite: Blandford1982; Ferreira1997)." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: Wind symmetric about disk midplane; extends 185 au north and south  
  > "The wind is assumed to be symmetric with respect to the disk midplane and extend out to 185 au to the north and south." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: Outer wind 100 K; shell 200 K; jet 300 K  
  > "We assume a temperature of 100 K in the outer unperturbed part and 200 K in the inner perturbed part (shell)" (MHD Disk Wind Model); "It has a temperature of 300 K, which is a mean value adopted before to derive the SO abundance (cite: Podio2015)." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Observed opening structure agrees with model streamlines; 4-8 au streamlines coincide approximately with the shell base  
  > "the wide-angle outflow shows an opening structure in good agreement with the predicted model streamlines" (MHD Disk Wind Model); "The inner part of the wind bounded by the model streamlines launched from 4 to 8 au roughly coincides with the base of the shells" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
3. **Calculate the disk-wind mass-loss rate**: Use Mdot_DW=Mdot_in[(r_out/r_in)^ξ-1] with ξ~1/[2(λ-1)]  
  > "= \dot{M}_{in}[(\frac{r_\textrm{\scriptsize out}}{r_\textrm{\scriptsize in}})^\xi-1]" (MHD Disk Wind Model); "\xi\sim \frac{1}{2(\lambda-1)}." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - parameter: λ~5.5; r_out~40 au; r_in~4 au; Mdot_in~3×10^-6 M_sun yr^-1  
  > "With $\lambda \sim 5.5$, $r_\textrm{\scriptsize out}\sim 40$ au, $r_\textrm{\scriptsize in}\sim 4$ au, and $\dot{M}_\textrm{\scriptsize in}\sim 3\times10^{-6}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ (cite: Lee2020Jet)" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: Disk wind removes all accretion angular momentum  
  > "For the disk wind to remove all of the accretion angular momentum, we have (cite: Tabone2020)" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Compare synthetic and observed SO kinematics and brightness**: Generate LTE SO PV diagrams and compare transverse cuts at increasing distances from the protostar  
  > "A radiative transfer code (cite: Lee2014) adding the SO line is used to generate the position-velocity (PV) diagrams of the SO emission from the model, assuming LTE." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "the resulting model PV diagrams on the observed ones cut across the jet axis centered at increasing distance from the protostar to near the end of the wide-angle outflow" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - software: radiative transfer code (cite: Lee2014)
   - parameter: SO abundance relative to molecular hydrogen is free  
  > "The SO abundance (wrt molecular hydrogen) $x_\textrm{\scriptsize SO}$ is a free parameter to be derived by matching the observed intensity." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - parameter: Shell density ~4 times the unperturbed disk-wind model  
  > "Note that the density in the shell is $\sim$ 4 times that in the unperturbed disk-wind model in order to match the observed SO intensity there." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: LTE  
  > "A radiative transfer code (cite: Lee2014) adding the SO line is used to generate the position-velocity (PV) diagrams of the SO emission from the model, assuming LTE." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: Northern inclination ~-3°; southern inclination ~2° for the wide-angle outflow and jet  
  > "the inclination angles of the wide-angle outflow and jet are assumed to be $\sim
-3$$^\circ$ in the northern component and $\sim 2$$^\circ$ in the southern component." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - fitting: Match observed intensity through SO abundance and approximately match shell PV structure through dynamical age  
  > "The SO abundance (wrt molecular hydrogen) $x_\textrm{\scriptsize SO}$ is a free parameter to be derived by matching the observed intensity." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "can roughly match the observations with the dynamical age $t_\textrm{\scriptsize s}\sim 37$ yrs" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - validation: Approximate PV agreement; rotation overpredicted; shell age agrees approximately with cavity-apex travel distance  
  > "this model roughly reproduces the PV structures of the faint unperturbed wide-angle outflow (marked with blue brackets), even though it predicts a rotation velocity slightly larger than observed." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "This age roughly agrees with the axial distance ($\sim 600$ au) traveled by the cavity apex for a jet speed $\sim$ 64 km s$^{-1}$" (MHD Disk Wind Model); "However, since the model PV structures of the shell are tilted more than observed, the rotation velocity in the shell is also over-predicted." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
5. **Improve the wind rotation match**: Lower λ while retaining other parameters; decrease the jet inner launch radius to maintain maximum jet velocity  
  > "we reduce the rotation velocity and poloidal velocity in the model by lowering the $\lambda$ value, keeping the same for the other parameters." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "At the same time, we decrease the inner launch radius of the SO jet to 0.05 au to maintain the same maximum jet velocity." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - software: radiative transfer code (cite: Lee2014)
   - parameter: Modified λ~3.5; jet inner launch radius 0.05 au  
  > "this very simple “modified” model with $\lambda \sim 3.5$ provides a better match to the observations." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "At the same time, we decrease the inner launch radius of the SO jet to 0.05 au to maintain the same maximum jet velocity." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - assumption: Angular-momentum removal requires SO abundance close to that in the disk  
  > "The disk wind is thus dense enough to remove the disk angular momentum, if its SO abundance is close to that in the disk." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - fitting: Compare the modified model with observed PV diagrams; quantitative fitting criterion not stated  
  > "this very simple “modified” model with $\lambda \sim 3.5$ provides a better match to the observations." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
   - validation: Better observational match; compare low λ and SO chemistry with published simulations and thermo-chemical modeling  
  > "this very simple “modified” model with $\lambda \sim 3.5$ provides a better match to the observations." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "Such a low $\lambda$ value is favored by recent MHD simulations including stellar irradiation (cite: Wang2019)." (MHD Disk Wind Model); "This result is in excellent agreement with thermo-chemical modeling of dense Class 0 MHD disk winds (cite: Panoglou2012)" (MHD Disk Wind Model)
6. **Interpret cavity confinement and inner-disk transport**: Compare lateral jet ram pressure with wind magnetic pressure and their inward scalings  
  > "equilibrium at $z \sim 150$ au between the lateral ram pressure and magnetic pressure is reached on the streamline launched from 4 au, as observed." (Conclusions); "magnetic pressure in self-similar MHD disk winds increases inward faster ($\propto r_0^{-5/2}$) than the wind ram pressure ($r^{-2}$)" (Conclusions)
   - parameter: Jet mass-flux ~10^-6 M_sun yr^-1; sideways speed ~10 km s^-1  
  > "With a jet mass-flux $\sim 10^{-6}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ ejected sideways at a speed $\sim$ 10 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Lee2015)" (Conclusions)
   - parameter: Wind magnetic field required for Mdot_in~3×10^-6 M_sun yr^-1  
  > "the magnetic field strength required in our wind model to drive accretion at $\dot{M}_{in}\sim 3\times10^{-6}$ $M_\odot$yr$^{-1}$" (Conclusions)
   - assumption: Wind magnetic pressure confines sideways jet and bow-shock expansion  
  > "the twisting of field lines at the base of MHD disk winds creates a strong magnetic pressure that efficiently confines the sideways expansion of jets and jet bow shocks (cite: Meliani06; Matsakos09)." (Conclusions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pressure equilibrium matches the observed 4 au streamline; numerical interaction simulations proposed for confirmation  
  > "equilibrium at $z \sim 150$ au between the lateral ram pressure and magnetic pressure is reached on the streamline launched from 4 au, as observed." (Conclusions); "MHD numerical simulations of the jet/disk-wind interaction in HH 212 will be able to confirm and refine these results." (Conclusions)

## Qualitative findings

- SO resolves into a collimated jet, a wide-angle disk outflow, and an evacuated cavity created by a jet-driven bowshock  
  > "Here we spatially resolve this SO outflow into three components: a collimated jet aligned with the SiO jet, the wide-angle disk outflow, and an evacuated cavity in between created by a large jet-driven bowshock." (abstract)
- Outer wide-angle outflow is unperturbed by bow shocks; inner part coincides with the perturbed shell base  
  > "The inner part of the wide-angle outflow coincides with the base of the shells and is thus perturbed by the bow shocks." (Results); "Its outer part is unperturbed by the bow shocks, providing the best opportunity to check the previously proposed MHD disk wind interpretation (cite: Tabone2017; Tabone2020)." (Results)
- Wind can remove disk angular momentum if its SO abundance is close to the disk value  
  > "The disk wind is thus dense enough to remove the disk angular momentum, if its SO abundance is close to that in the disk." (MHD Disk Wind Model)
- Cavity width is consistent with a large-scale outer-disk magnetic field sufficient for the observed accretion  
  > "Hence, the cavity width appears consistent with the outer disk ($r_0 \ge 4$ au) hosting a large-scale magnetic field sufficient to extract angular momentum at the observed rate." (Conclusions)
- MRI is a possible angular-momentum transport mechanism inside the truncated disk wind  
  > "another mechanism such as magneto-rotational instability (MRI) (cite: Balbus2006) might thus be needed to transport disk angular momentum outward" (Conclusions)
