## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify the transient and measure infrared variability**: SQL amplitude selection, visual inspection and updated DoPhot profile-fitting photometry  
  > "an SQL query was very soon run to search for transients and variable sources with $>$3 magnitudes of variation." (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data); "The VVV photometry presented here are profile fitting photometry, performed with an updated version of DoPhot (cite: schechter93; alonso12)" (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data); "After passing visual inspection, it was classified as the first “WIT" source" (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data)
   - software: DoPhot, VISTA Science Archive
   - parameter: At least 4 epochs; H and K_s images; median K_s at least two magnitudes above the expected field limit  
  > "The query selected candidates having at least 4 epochs of observation, images in both the $H$ and $K_s$ bandpasses and a median $K_s$ magnitude at least two magnitudes brighter than the expected limiting magnitude for each field." (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data)
   - parameter: Photometric error floor: 0.02 mag  
  > "the quoted errors are those given by DoPhot, with a floor of 0.02 mag imposed as the estimated calibration uncertainty." (Table 1)
   - fitting: Profile fitting with updated DoPhot  
  > "The VVV photometry presented here are profile fitting photometry, performed with an updated version of DoPhot (cite: schechter93; alonso12)" (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data)
   - validation: Visual inspection  
  > "After passing visual inspection, it was classified as the first “WIT" source" (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data)
2. **Fit the March 2010 infrared SED**: Fit a reddened, uniform-temperature black-body library to near-simultaneous VVV and WISE photometry  
  > "We attempted to fit the SED using a reddened, uniform-temperature black body" (Infrared and optical data); "Each model was scaled to the same average flux as the data, measured as the average of log($\lambda F_{\lambda}$) at the six wavelengths." (Infrared and optical data)
   - parameter: 60<T<6000 K; 0<A_K_s<20  
  > "We constructed a library of black body SEDs with a range of temperatures and extinctions (60\<T\<6000 K and $0<A_{K_s}<20$)" (Infrared and optical data)
   - parameter: W2 saturation correction: +0.07 mag  
  > "The tabulated $W2$ data in fact require a positive correction for saturation of 0.07 mag (cite: cutri12)" (Infrared and optical data)
   - assumption: Koenig et al. high-extinction mid-infrared law; Cardelli et al. H-to-K_s law  
  > "we adopted the mid-infrared extinction law defined by (cite: koenig14) for fields with high extinction" (Infrared and optical data); "the (cite: cardelli89) extinction law was used to relate the extinction in $H$ and $K_s$" (Infrared and optical data)
   - fitting: Minimum reduced χ²; five degrees of freedom; models normalized in log(λF_λ)  
  > "located the area of parameter space with the minimum value of the reduced $\chi^2$ parameter, $\chi^2_\nu$, computed with five degrees of freedom." (Infrared and optical data); "Each model was scaled to the same average flux as the data, measured as the average of log($\lambda F_{\lambda}$) at the six wavelengths." (Infrared and optical data)
   - validation: Examined χ² over temperature/extinction space and tested an alternative near-infrared extinction law  
  > "combinations of temperatures and extinction in the ranges $720 < T < 2100$ K, $3.6 < A_{K_s} < 10.1$ can produce values of $\chi^2_\nu$ less than four times the minimum value." (Infrared and optical data); "Steeper near infrared extinction laws such as this lead to best-fit solutions with slightly lower values of both extinction and temperature, not significantly changing our conclusions." (Infrared and optical data)
3. **Measure later infrared fading and cooling**: W3 aperture photometry with nearby AllWISE zero-point standards; compare late-August K_s/W3 fluxes and the W2 upper limit  
  > "We performed aperture photometry on the $W3$ FITS image obtained from the NASA Infrared Science Archive with IRAF/DAOPHOT, bootstrapping the zero point using several nearby sources in the AllWISE source catalogue." (Infrared and optical data); "the ratio of $K_s$ and $W3$ fluxes from 29 August can be used to calculate a black body temperature." (Infrared and optical data)
   - software: IRAF, DAOPHOT
   - parameter: 2010 August 29 stack midpoint; W2>11 mag  
  > "We find $W3=6.78 \pm 0.14$ mag on 2010 August 29 (the mid-point date of the image stack)" (Infrared and optical data); "the upper limit of $W2>11$ at this epoch corresponds to a fade of over 5 mag at 4.6 $\mu$m" (Infrared and optical data)
   - assumption: Same extinction in early March and late August 2010  
  > "If we assume the same extinction was present in early March and late August 2010" (Infrared and optical data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Including the W2 upper limit rules out a single-temperature late-August black body  
  > "After including this upper limit in $W2$, the SED is not well described by a single temperature black body in late August" (Infrared and optical data)
4. **Test nova and collision hypotheses with cm continuum**: Reduce ATCA data, fit a radio power law and compare 2012 detections with 2019 upper limits  
  > "The *Miriad* software package was used to reduce the data, using standard procedures to flag and remove bad data." (Radio cm continuum data); "A power law fit to the detections and upper limit yields a spectral index $\alpha = -0.15 \pm 0.22$" (Radio cm continuum data); "The ATCA non-detection in 2019 at a level an order of magnitude fainter than the 2012 flux is therefore consistent with this interpretation." (The cm continuum test)
   - software: Miriad
   - parameter: 6 km configuration in both epochs  
  > "The observations used the longest baseline array configuration (6 km)" (Radio cm continuum data); "Again the 6 km configuration was used." (Radio cm continuum data)
   - parameter: 2019 self-calibration at 5.5 GHz using an adjacent source; not possible at 9 GHz  
  > "An adjacent, uncatalogued continuum source at 16:10:59.4 -51:49:24.0 was used for self-calibration at 5.5 GHz but this was not possible at 9 GHz." (Radio cm continuum data)

## Qualitative findings

- A highly obscured classical nova behind an IRDC is favoured  
  > "The hypothesis of a highly obscured classical nova with a bright dust peak appears to fit all the available data." (Conclusions)
- Rapidly fading radio continuum argues against a protostellar collision  
  > "The rapidly fading 4 cm continuum flux detected by ATCA appears to be inconsistent with this interpretation, causing us to favour the nova hypothesis." (Conclusions)
- ALMA detected two nearby sources rather than the transient  
  > "In bands 6 and 7 two sources were detected within the area of SDC G331.062$-$0.294, both of them offset from VVV-WIT-01 by a few arcseconds." (Radio mm/submm continuum data); "The other ALMA detection has no infrared counterpart and may well be an extragalactic object." (Radio mm/submm continuum data)
- The eastern ALMA counterpart is likely a YSO with a relatively massive or unusually warm disc  
  > "it is likely that VVV J161053.93-515532.8 is indeed a YSO." (A submm-bright variable YSO in SDC G331.062-0.294); "this indicates that the YSO hosts a relatively massive disc, or perhaps an unusually warm one." (A submm-bright variable YSO in SDC G331.062-0.294)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Near-simultaneous VVV/WISE measurements define the SED; ATCA evolution tests the two main hypotheses  
  > "The near simultaneity of the VVV and WISE measurements in March 2010 allows us to define an SED" (Infrared and optical data); "The cm continuum data from ATCA provide a valuable test of the protostellar collision and nova hypotheses." (The cm continuum test)
- Why this method: Account for the number of survey transients when assessing a chance IRDC projection  
  > "many transients have been detected in VVV so the chance of observing at least one transient in an IRDC over the course of the survey is rather higher" (VVV-WIT-01 discovery data)
- Alternative: Galactic supernova rejected from the absence of gamma-ray and neutrino emission  
  > "the absence of $\gamma$-ray and neutrino emission allows us to rule out a Galactic supernova" (Conclusions)
- Alternative: YSO episodic accretion unlikely given the amplitude and steep decline  
  > "the very high amplitude and steep decline in flux make it unlikely that this was an episodic accretion event in a YSO." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Very Late Thermal Pulse disfavoured by steep mid-infrared fading  
  > "The steep decline in mid-infrared flux also appears to rule out a Very Late Thermal Pulse in a post-AGB star." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Alternative steeper near-infrared extinction law changes fitted temperature/extinction slightly without changing conclusions  
  > "We also considered the modified near infrared extinction law of (cite: alonso17), derived for VVV data in the inner bulge." (Infrared and optical data); "Steeper near infrared extinction laws such as this lead to best-fit solutions with slightly lower values of both extinction and temperature, not significantly changing our conclusions." (Infrared and optical data)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Sparse light-curve sampling and no spectrum prevent definitive classification  
  > "the sparse sampling of the light curve and lack of a spectrum make a definitive classification difficult." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): High-extinction mid-infrared extinction-law systematics keep reduced χ² above one  
  > "$\chi^2_\nu > 1$ in all models due to systematic uncertainties in the mid-infrared extinction law that become important when extinction is high." (Infrared and optical data)
- Limitation (authors): Late-August SED cannot be represented by one black-body temperature  
  > "After including this upper limit in $W2$, the SED is not well described by a single temperature black body in late August" (Infrared and optical data)
- Limitation (authors): Nova distance poorly constrained because photometric maximum was missed  
  > "The photometric maximum was not in fact observed so the distance is not well constrained in this interpretation" (Classical Nova interpretation)
- Limitation (authors): Limited empirical merger sample and immature collision simulations  
  > "The main caveats are that our empirical knowledge of stellar mergers is limited to a few probable events and the present generation of numerical simulations is unlikely to be the final word." (Upper limit on the protostellar collision rate)
- Limitation (authors): YSO outburst could have heated the outer disc during ALMA observations  
  > "It is certainly possible that the infrared outburst was ongoing at the time of the ALMA detections in August and September 2015, resulting in heating of the outer disc (cite: johnstone13)." (A submm-bright variable YSO in SDC G331.062-0.294)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): The chance-projection calculation needs scrutiny of spatially varying transient detectability and IRDC coverage.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The collision-rate limit lacks a stated confidence level and a detailed completeness model.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The disc-mass estimate is sensitive to unknown distance, adopted temperature and possible outburst heating.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA fluxes and upper limits lack enough extraction and calibration detail for an exact numerical reproduction.
