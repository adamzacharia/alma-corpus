   - parameter: deconvolver = hogbom; weighting = natural or uniform; narrowest possible channel width  
  > "In tclean, we have used a hogbom deconvolver and produced images with the narrowest possible channel width." (2.3 Data reduction); "For our moments maps, we use a data cube made with natural weighting to minimise noise. In most cases, we extract our spectra from data cubes made with uniform weighting, which optimises the angular resolution of the data." (2.3 Data reduction)

   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Generate moment maps and extract spectra against the radio core**: Create moment 0, 1, and 2 maps using immoments and extract spectra at the position of the compact radio continuum source  
  > "We indicate the location of each galaxy’s compact and unresolved radio continuum source with a cross, against which we extract the spectra of CO(1-0), CO(2-1), CN(2-1), HCO [+] (2-1) and HCN(2-1)." (2.2 Moments maps and spectra); "We produce our maps using the CASA task immoments ." (2.3 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, immoments
   - parameter: includepix = [0,100] for moment 0 maps with overlapping absorption; >4 sigma threshold for moments 1 and 2 maps  
  > "However, for sources with a large beam and overlapping emission and absorption, we use ‘includepix = [0,100]’, i.e. all pixel values from 0 to an arbitrarily large value."   > "After producing these maps, we therefore estimate the summed value of the remaining positive noise in an emission free region, and subtract it from the whole image." (2.3 Data reduction)
3. **Fit Gaussian components to emission and absorption spectra**: Incrementally add Gaussian lines and fit with SciPy curve_fit, masking absorption while fitting overlapping emission first, then run 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations for uncertainties  
  > "To determine the optimal fit for each spectrum, we first identify the minimum number of components required by incrementally adding Gaussian lines. We do this until no channel contains > 5𝜎 emission and the RMS value of the fitting region is less than 1.5 times that of the remainder of the spectrum." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure); "We then perform an initial fit using Python ’s SciPy library. Specifically, we use the curve fit minimization algorithm." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure); "Then, 10,000 simulated spectra are generated based upon the observed spectrum and the initial best fit." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
   - software: Python, SciPy
   - parameter: Stopping criterion: no channel > 5 sigma and fitting region RMS < 1.5 times remainder RMS; 10,000 Monte Carlo realizations  
  > "We do this until no channel contains > 5𝜎 emission and the RMS value of the fitting region is less than 1.5 times that of the remainder of the spectrum." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure); "Then, 10,000 simulated spectra are generated based upon the observed spectrum and the initial best fit." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
   - assumption: Errors from fitting emission do not significantly propagate to overlapping absorption fits  
  > "Where the emission and absorption overlap, we do not account for errors from fitting to the emission propagating through to the absorption fits. This is because the former are generally much smaller." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
   - fitting: SciPy curve_fit minimization with 10,000 Monte Carlo realizations (15.865% and 84.135% percentiles for 1-sigma bounds)  
  > "The 1𝜎 upper and lower error bounds are then defined by the values that delineate the 15.865 per cent highest and lowest results among the 10,000 fits." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
   - validation: Tested range of visual masking boundaries for overlapping emission/absorption and found minimal changes  
  > "Additionally, we find that using a reasonable range of masking boundaries results in minimal changes to the ultimate fits." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
4. **Calculate excitation temperatures and absorption column densities**: Compute excitation temperatures from CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) velocity-integrated optical depth ratios (Eq. 2) and column densities using optical-depth corrected LTE formula (Eqs. 3 and 4)

  > "In the mean time however, we proceed to estimate molecular column densities by assuming the ground state excitation temperature of the molecular gas is 5 K for all of the molecular lines." (3.1 Excitation temperatures)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium, ground state excitation temperature T_ex = 5 K, optical depth correction factor tau / (1 - exp(-tau))  
  > "For optically thin gas in local thermodynamic equilibrium, the CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) velocity integrated optical depths are related" (3.1 Excitation temperatures); "In the mean time however, we proceed to estimate molecular column densities by assuming the ground state excitation temperature of the molecular gas is 5 K for all of the molecular lines." ## Qualitative findings

- Two distinct populations of molecular clouds are identified: narrow absorption features (<9 km/s dispersion) symmetrically distributed around 0 km/s in galaxies with edge-on/near-edge-on disks, and broad absorption features (>9 km/s dispersion) in non-disk galaxies that split into a symmetric CND component (-100 to 100 km/s) and a strongly redshifted inflowing component (150 to 550 km/s).  
  > "In galaxies where our line of sight passes through an edgeon or close to edge-on disk of molecular gas, we find low velocity dispersion clouds (typically <9 km/s) with no net inflow our outflow (Figures 14 and 15)." (5 CONCLUSIONS); "When excluding disks, we find that absorption regions have significantly different properties. They have wide velocity dispersions (typically >9 km/s), and in terms of their line of sight velocities, appear to split into two groups." (5 CONCLUSIONS)
- High CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) optical depth ratios (often exceeding the LTE limit of 4 and implying negative excitation temperatures) can be explained by hierarchical density substructure within molecular clouds combined with a continuum source that decreases in size at higher frequencies.  
  > "We find a wide range in the CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) ratios of the absorption regions. In around half of cases, the ratio implies a nonphysical negative excitation temperature. We show that the high CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) ratios can be explained by variation in the structure of the continuum source with frequency, combined with substructure in the molecular clouds." (5 CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Higher-order transitions and a wider mix of molecular tracers (CO(2-1), CN(2-1), HCO+(2-1), HCN(2-1)) provide higher sensitivity to intrinsic absorption and, combined with high-resolution CO emission, allow unambiguous determination of cloud motion relative to the galaxy.  
  > "Subsequent observations of the edge-on elliptical brightest cluster galaxy Hydra-A have shown that higher order transitions and a wider mix of molecular tracers can be particularly effective at detecting intrinsic molecular absorption (Rose et al. 2020)." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Combined with CO(2-1) observations, we can therefore study the bulk motions of the absorbing clouds in the context of each galaxy’s molecular emission." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Intrinsic absorption against a central continuum source is orders of magnitude more sensitive than emission and unambiguously reveals whether gas is moving towards or away from the nucleus; uniform weighting optimizes angular resolution to limit extended emission contamination.  
  > "This technique is advantageous because its sensitivity is many orders or magnitude higher than emission." (1 Introduction); "In most cases, we extract our spectra from data cubes made with uniform weighting, which optimises the angular resolution of the data. This limits contamination from spatially extended molecular emission in the spectra." (2.3 Data reduction)
- Alternative: Maser pumping as the cause of negative excitation temperatures in CO (rejected because maser pumping is not possible in CO)  
  > "The excitation temperature may be negative when 𝑛 u / 𝑔 u > 𝑛 l / 𝑔 l, which is possible if there is pumping by a maser. However, this effect is not possible in CO." (3.1 Excitation temperatures)
- Alternative: Non-LTE rotational level temperature differences as the sole cause of high CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) ratios (rejected because an extreme difference of 20 K vs 5 K only yields a ratio of 5.6, whereas ratios >10 are observed)  
  > "If the CO(1-0) excitation temperature is 20 K and the CO(2-1) excitation temperature is 5 K, then a ratio in the velocity integrated optical depths of 5.6 is expected. This temperature difference is at the more extreme end of what could be expected, but is still far from explaining the ratios of over 10 which we see in the absorption lines of some systems." (3.1 Excitation temperatures); "However, we ruled out a lack of non-local thermodynamic equilibrium as the cause because its effect is too small." (4.4 Cloud structure)
- How they decided it was enough: Gaussian components were incrementally added until no channel contained >5 sigma emission and the fitting region RMS was <1.5 times the remainder of the spectrum.  
  > "To determine the optimal fit for each spectrum, we first identify the minimum number of components required by incrementally adding Gaussian lines. We do this until no channel contains > 5𝜎 emission and the RMS value of the fitting region is less than 1.5 times that of the remainder of the spectrum." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
- Limitation (authors): Setting includepix = [0,100] and subtracting positive noise from an emission-free region may bias moment 0 intensity maps to be lower than reality.  
  > "This may bias the overall intensity map to be lower than reality because negative noise will effectively be subtracted from higher significance emission twice." (2.3 Data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Errors from fitting emission are not propagated into overlapping absorption fits.  
  > "Where the emission and absorption overlap, we do not account for errors from fitting to the emission propagating through to the absorption fits." (2.4 Line Fitting Procedure)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperature is the dominant uncertainty in column density estimates and had to be assumed as 5 K.  
  > "In the mean time however, we proceed to estimate molecular column densities by assuming the ground state excitation temperature of the molecular gas is 5 K for all of the molecular lines." (3.1 Excitation temperatures); "By far the most dominant error in our calculations of the column densities is the gas excitation temperature, 𝑇 ex ." (3.2 Absorption column density estimates)
- Limitation (authors): The Galactic CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO is a considerable source of uncertainty in molecular mass estimates.  
  > "To ensure our mass estimates are comparable with similar studies, we use the standard Milky Way CO-to-H 2 conversion factor of 𝑋 CO = 2 × 10 [20] cm [−][2] (K km s [−][1] ) [−][1] in our calculations. However, this is a considerable source of uncertainty." (3.3 Molecular mass estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Proximity of the absorbing clouds to the galaxy centre along the line of sight is unknown.  
  > "This is particularly important given that we lack information regarding their proximity to their galaxy’s centre, even if we know their RA, Dec and line of sight velocity with certainty." (4.2 The location of absorbing molecular clouds)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual masking of absorption regions before fitting overlapping emission introduces subjective bias when emission and absorption share similar velocities and widths.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) observations were taken years apart (e.g., 2018 vs 2021/2022) and with different angular resolutions; time variability of the background AGN continuum structure and differing beam dilution could affect the CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) optical depth ratios.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Column densities in Table 5 are computed assuming a uniform T_ex = 5 K and unit covering factor despite the paper demonstrating that covering factors and excitation conditions vary across transitions.
