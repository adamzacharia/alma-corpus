## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify molecular outflows and determine core systemic velocities**: Visual inspection of 12m-only CO PPV data cubes in DS9 to determine blue/red-shifted velocity ranges, followed by moment-0 maps, channel maps, line profiles, and PV diagrams along outflow axes to refine velocity ranges and lobes. Core systemic velocities measured from centroid velocity of N2D+ 3-2 (or DCO+ 3-2, C18O 2-1).  
  > "For the identification of outflows, we use the 12 m array data only." (3.1. Outflow Detection); "(1) Using `DS9` [1], we inspect the position-position-velocity (PPV) data cubes of the CO emission to determine the velocity ranges of the blueand red-shifted components with respect to the cloud emission." (3.1. Outflow Detection); "The core systemic velocity is determined by the centroid velocity, _v_ lsr, of the N 2 D [+] 3-2 line, while the DCO [+] 3-2 line is used when the N 2 D [+] line is undetected." (3.1. Outflow Detection)
   - software: DS9
   - parameter: Velocity range threshold > 2 sigma (rms in line-free channels); outflow lobe contour threshold > 4 sigma (rms of integrated intensity map)  
  > "The velocity range ( _△v_ ) of outflows are defined where the emission avoid environment gas around the system velocity and higher than the 2 _σ_ noise level, where _σ_ is" (3.1. Outflow Detection); "The outflow lobes are defined where the corresponding velocity integrated intensity is higher than the 4 _σ_ noise level, where _σ_ is the rms of the integrated intensity map." (3.1. Outflow Detection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Classified outflows into 'Definitive' (33) and 'Marginal' (10) based on whether they can be unambiguously separated from nearby overlapping outflows or ambient gas.  
  > "Among the 43 identified outflows, 33 are defined as definitive outflows, while the remaining are classified as marginal (Table 2)." (3.1. Outflow Detection)

Derive protostellar accretion rate from mechanical force assuming momentum conservation in a wind-driven outflow.

Outflow-driven turbulence); "For a continuous outflow, the outflow lobe length can be used to approximate the turbulence driving wavelength according to numerical simulations (Nakamura & Li 2007; Cunningham et al. 2009)." (3.3. Outflow-driven turbulence)
   - fitting: _not stated_

  > "Overall, the results from both approaches are consistent (Figure 8 and Table 3), the outflow luminosity is much smaller than the turbulent dissipation rate in the majority of clumps, except for G327.11 and G341.03, which have relatively larger _r_ L ." (4.3. Outflow Impact on the Clumps)

## Qualitative findings

- Most 70 um dark clumps already host protostars launching outflows and cannot be assumed to be purely prestellar without deep interferometric observations.  
  > "These results suggest that most of 70 _µ_ m dark clumps already host protostars, and thus they cannot be assumed to be prestellar without deep interferometric observations." (5. CONCLUSIONS)
- Episodic accretion-driven outflows begin at the earliest stages of protostellar evolution across low-, intermediate-, and high-mass cores, and the ejection episodicity timescale is likely not constant over time.  
  > "We discover 6 episodic molecular outflows associated with low- to high-mass cores, indicating that episodic outflows (and therefore episodic accretion) begin at extremely early stages of protostellar evolution for a range of core masses." (abstract); "The _△t_ knot is much longer than the value ( _∼_ 500 yr) found in more evolved cores in W43-MM1 (Nony et al. 2020), which indicates that the ejection episodicity timescale is likely not constant over time and accretion bursts may be more frequently at later stages of evolution in a cluster environment." (5. CONCLUSIONS)
- Strong thermal SiO emission unrelated to CO outflows or dense cores is present within 70 um dark clumps, likely produced by colliding or intersecting gas flows.  
  > "We often detect thermal SiO emission within these 70 _µ_ m dark clumps that is unrelated to CO outflows." (abstract); "This indicates that the shocks that produce this SiO emission most likely arise from colliding or intersecting gas flows." (4.4. SiO Emission Unrelated to CO Outflows)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-angular resolution ALMA observations of 12 massive 70 um dark clumps allow investigating the initial conditions and outflow feedback in the extreme early stages of high-mass star formation in cluster mode.  
  > "To investigate high-mass star formation in the cluster mode, we have carried out high-angular resolution observations towards 12 massive 70 _µ_ m dark clumps using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: CO 2-1 is used to compute outflow parameters because SiO abundances vary significantly from region to region and SiO emission is much weaker than CO.  
  > "Since the SiO abundances vary significantly from region to region and its emission is much weaker than the CO, we used the CO data to compute the outflow parameters by assuming that the CO line wing emission is optically thin." (3.2. Outflow Parameters)
- Alternative: Wide opening angle scenario for perpendicular bipolar CO lobes in #2-G327.11 and #1-G341.03 rejected in favor of multiple protostars with perpendicular accretion disks.  
  > "Alternatively, these two cores may be showing outflows with wide opening angles. However, we favour the first scenario because wide angles are expected in much more evolved sources and those expected in these IRDCs that properties are consistent with early stages of evolution (Arce et al. 2007)." (3.1. Outflow Detection)
- Alternative: Using lobe mean velocity (v_lobe) instead of individual knot velocity (v_knot) to calculate knot dynamical timescales.  
  > "Therefore, we use _v_ knot, instead of the mean velocity of detected knots ( _v_ lobe = _⟨v_ knot _⟩_ ) as used in Nony et al. (2020), when calculating the knot dynamical timescale." (4.2.3. Episodic Ejection)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low-velocity and/or weak outflows may be missed due to confusion with ambient CO gas around dense cores, potentially contributing to the high fraction of unipolar outflows.  
  > "Therefore, we might fail to identify some low-velocity and/or weak outflows because their emission can be confused with the ambient gas. We cannot completely rule out the possibility that this bias contributes to the high fraction of unipolar outflows." (3.1. Outflow Detection)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow parameters (v_max, lambda_max, M_out, P_out, E_out, etc.) are lower limits due to sensitivity limits, confusion with nearby outflows or line-of-sight emission, CO optical depth, failure of CO to trace low-density gas, and uncorrected inclination angles.  
  > "Note that both _v_ max and _λ_ max estimated here should be considered as lower limits because the outflow emission is limited by the sensitivity of the observations, can be confused by nearby outflows and/or line-of-sight emission that is not associated with the natal cloud, and these parameters have not been corrected for the outflow inclination." (3.1. Outflow Detection); "On average, the combined effects of the above mentioned biases could lead to a factor of _∼_ 10 underestimate in the outflow mass and dynamical properties (Dunham et al. 2014)." (4.1. Biases on Outflow Parameters)
- Limitation (authors): Difference in inter-knot timescales (Delta t_knot) compared to W43-MM1 could be affected by spatial resolution differences (~4000-7500 AU vs ~2400 AU) or nonconsecutive Hubble Wedge features in evolved clustered environments.  
  > "Given the different spatial resolutions between the two studies, we cannot fully rule out the possibility that the different _△t_ knot is due to the effect of spatial resolutions as higher spatial resolutions help to resolve more individual knots." (4.2.3. Episodic Ejection)
- Limitation (authors): Current data cannot distinguish between converging flows, large-scale collapse, or undetected low-mass protostars for SiO emission regions lacking CO velocity shears.  
  > "Unfortunately, we can not distinguish between these possibilities with the data at hand." (4.4. SiO Emission Unrelated to CO Outflows)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual identification of outflows and subjective velocity range selection in crowded protocluster environments with ambient gas confusion can introduce human bias.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming optically thin CO 2-1 emission in line wings without 13CO/C18O optical depth corrections systematically underestimates outflow masses and energetics.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dynamical timescales t_dyn = lambda_max / v_max assume constant velocity and represent lower limits if the outflow is not fully mapped or has decelerated.
