## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble mid-IR images and standard-star PSF images**: Use already published, fully reduced N-band images from VISIR, T-ReCS and CanariCam together with standard-star images  
  > "We used fully-reduced mid-IR imaging observations taken with 8-10 m class telescopes and already published in the literature" (2.1 Mid-IR observations); "For each galaxy we also obtained fully-reduced images of standard stars taken with the same filter and observed close in time." (2.1 Mid-IR observations)
   - parameter: Angular resolution close to the diffraction limit, 0.2-0.4 arcsec  
  > "All the observations have angular resolutions, as measured from the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the stars, close to the theoretical diffraction limit of the corresponding telescopes in the mid-IR ($0.2-0.4\ensuremath{''}$)." (2.1 Mid-IR observations)
   - parameter: Pixel sizes: 0.0453 arcsec upgraded VISIR, 0.075 arcsec VISIR, 0.0798 arcsec CanariCam, 0.09 arcsec T-ReCS  
  > "The pixel sizes are as follows, 0.0453$''$ for the upgraded VISIR, $0.075\ensuremath{''}$ for VISIR, $0.0798\ensuremath{''}$ for CanariCam and $0.09\ensuremath{''}$ for T-ReCS." (2.1 Mid-IR observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Separate unresolved and extended mid-IR emission by PSF scaling**: Fit 1D profiles along X and Y with one Gaussian for the standard star and two Gaussians for the galaxy, scale the star image by the unresolved-component amplitude, subtract it  
  > "We started by fitting separately the X and Y profiles of the corresponding standard stars with one Gaussian function" (3.1 Analysis of the observations); "We then used two Gaussians for the galaxy profiles along each of the X and Y directions." (3.1 Analysis of the observations)
   - parameter: 1D profiles of one pixel width centred at the emission peak along X and Y, on original (unrotated, unsmoothed) images  
  > "We extracted 1D profiles of one pixel width, centered at the peak of the emission along each of the X and Y axes of the mid-IR galaxy and standard star images." (3.1 Analysis of the observations)
   - parameter: Unresolved Gaussian sigma fixed to the stellar value (within +/-1 pixel); intensity and position free; second Gaussian fully free  
  > "and allowed its intensity and position to vary" (3.1 Analysis of the observations); "For the second Gaussian we allowed all the parameters to vary to model the extended emission." (3.1 Analysis of the observations)
   - assumption: A standard star observed close in time represents the unresolved nuclear component  
  > "The main assumption is that an imaging observation of a standard star taken close in time to the galaxy observation represents the galaxy unresolved nuclear component." (3.1 Analysis of the observations)
   - fitting: Two-Gaussian 1D profile fits per axis; fitting algorithm, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "We derived the scaling factors for the unresolved component Gaussian along the X and Y directions." (3.1 Analysis of the observations)
   - validation: Choose the PSF-subtracted image (X or Y scaling) that does not over-subtract the unresolved emission  
  > "From the two PSF-subtracted galaxy images, we chose the one that did not over-subtract the unresolved emission." (3.1 Analysis of the observations)
3. **Measure PA of extended emission and unresolved flux fractions**: Fit ellipses to PSF-subtracted images for PA; compute unresolved/total flux in a fixed aperture; contours from background sigma  
  > "task within iraf on the PSF-subtracted images to derive the position angle (PA) of the extended mid-IR emission" (3.2 Results); "The unresolved flux fraction is measured within $1.5-1.6\ensuremath{''}$ diameter apertures." (Table 2 notes)
   - software: iraf
   - parameter: Contours at 3, 5, 10, 15 and 20 x sigma_bk (background standard deviation on original images); smoothing Gaussians of FWHM 1.1-1.7 pixels (0.07-0.13 arcsec)  
  > "We also smoothed the images using Gaussian functions with FWHM of between 1.1 and 1.7 pixels, corresponding to 0.07 to 0.13$\ensuremath{''}$." (3.2 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with earlier work: good agreement on unresolved fractions with Asmus2014, GarciaBernete2016, Asmus2019  
  > "This is in good agreement with previous works (cite: Asmus2014; GarciaBernete2016; Asmus2019)." (3.2 Results)
4. **Register mid-IR to ALMA and compare with 870 um, CO(3-2) and NLR cones**: Align mid-IR peak with ALMA 870 um peak; overlay mid-IR contours on ALMA continuum; RGB composites with CO(3-2); cone PAs from visual inspection of literature images  
  > "We assumed that the peak of the mid-IR emission coincides with that of the ALMA $870\,\mu$m continuum emission and adjusted the mid-IR coordinates accordingly." (3.3 Comparison with ALMA 870 um and CO(3-2) observations); "We derived the PA of the cones by visual inspection of the" (3.4 Comparison with ionization cones, NLR, and nuclear outflows)
   - parameter: RGB: CO(3-2) red, 870 um continuum green, extended mid-IR blue, for the seven galaxies with extended mid-IR emission  
  > "We constructed red-green-blue (RGB) images using the ALMA CO(3-2) images in red and $870\,\mu$m continuum in green for the seven Seyferts with extended mid-IR emission." (3.3 Comparison with ALMA 870 um and CO(3-2) observations)
   - assumption: Mid-IR and ALMA peaks coincide (astrometric alignment)  
  > "We assumed that the peak of the mid-IR emission coincides with that of the ALMA $870\,\mu$m continuum emission" (3.3 Comparison with ALMA 870 um and CO(3-2) observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Place sample in N_H versus Eddington-ratio diagram**: Plot ALMA-derived N_H (upper limits where applicable) versus lambda_Edd and compare with predicted blowout and IR-dominated outflow regions  
  > "we show the hydrogen column densities or upper limits derived toward the AGN position of the GATOS Seyferts against the Eddington ratios" (4 Dependence of warm dust morphologies)
   - parameter: N_H2 from CO(3-2) with Milky Way CO-to-H2 factor, at 7-10 pc resolution  
  > "which were derived using the CO(3-2) data and the canonical CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor of the Milky Way" (4 Dependence of warm dust morphologies)
   - assumption: Molecular gas phase dominates the column on these scales  
  > "To derive $N_{\rm H}$ we assumed that the molecular gas phase is dominant on these scales (cite: Wada2016)." (4 Dependence of warm dust morphologies)
   - assumption: ALMA N_H of each galaxy is representative of clouds in the wind-launching region  
  > "We therefore assume that $N_{\rm H}$(ALMA) for each galaxy is representative of those clouds located in the dusty wind launching region." (4 Dependence of warm dust morphologies)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Generate CAT3D-WIND model images**: Compute mid-IR (8, 12 um) and far-IR (700 um) model images over a grid of disk/wind cloud distributions, wind-to-disk ratio, inclination, disk height and wind width  
  > "In this section we use the radiative transfer model of a clumpy disk and wind dubbed CAT3D-WIND and presented in (cite: Hoenig2017) to generate model images in the mid- and far-IR." (5 CAT3D-WIND mid- and far-IR model images); "we generated a total of 1728 + 9 model images" (5.2 Selection of model parameters)
   - software: CAT3D-WIND
   - parameter: f_wd = 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 and i = 30, 45, 60, 75 deg  
  > "We used four values of the wind-to-disk ratio of clouds: $f_{\rm wd} =0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2$ and four values of the inclination: $i=30^\circ$" (5.2 Selection of model parameters)
   - parameter: Fixed N0 = 10 and wind half-opening angle 45 deg; sigma_theta = 5, 10, 15 deg; h = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3  
  > "We fixed the following parameters, $N_0=10$ and the half-opening angle of the wind $\theta_w=45^\circ$." (5.2 Selection of model parameters); "We took ranges of the angular width of the wind $\sigma_\theta=5, 10, 15^\circ$ and height of the disk $h=0.1, 0.2, 0.3$." (5.2 Selection of model parameters)
   - parameter: Dust distributed over 2000 r_sub x 2000 r_sub; images resampled from 2 r_sub natural resolution to 10 r_sub pixels  
  > "We distributed the dust over a region of $2000\,r_{\rm sub} \times 2000\,r_{\rm sub}$ in size." (5.2 Selection of model parameters); "We resampled the images from the model natural resolution of $2\,r_{\rm sub}$ to a pixel size of $10\,r_{\rm sub}$." (5.2 Selection of model parameters)
   - parameter: Radial cloud distributions: compact/extended disk with compact/extended wind (a, a_w = -2 or -0.5)  
  > "compact disk - extended wind, $a=-2$ and $a_w = -0.5$" (5.2 Selection of model parameters)
   - assumption: Cloud optical depth tau_V = 50  
  > "The clouds have an optical depth of $\tau_V=50$" (5.1 Brief description of the models)
   - assumption: Sublimation radius r_mod = 0.3 pc at L_bol = 1e46 erg/s, scaled as L_bol^1/2  
  > "The inner radius is determined by the dust $r_{\rm sub}$, which the model takes as $r_{\rm mod}=0.3\,$pc at $L_{\rm bol} = 10^{46}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}$" (5.1 Brief description of the models)
   - fitting: No fitting; fixed parameter grid chosen from GonzalezMartin2019 ranges  
  > "we based our choice of the CAT3D-WIND parameters on the derived ranges" (5.2 Selection of model parameters)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Degrade model images to observed resolution and compare qualitatively**: Convolve 8 um and 700 um model images to 0.3 arcsec and 0.1 arcsec Gaussian beams, resample, apply same 1D-profile PSF-subtraction to mid-IR models, compare morphologies to observations  
  > "We convolved the $8\,\mu$m and $700\,\mu$m model images to the typical mid-IR and ALMA far-IR angular resolutions of $0.3\ensuremath{''}$ and $0.1\ensuremath{''}$, respectively, using a Gaussian function." (6.2 Fiducial model images for GATOS Seyferts); "We fitted 1D profiles along the equatorial and polar directions." (6.2.1 Mid-IR model images)
   - parameter: Unresolved Gaussian width fixed to the convolution width  
  > "For the unresolved component we fixed the Gaussian width to the value used to convolve the original CAT3D+WIND images." (6.2.1 Mid-IR model images)
   - parameter: Representative r_sub = 0.04 pc for the sample (0.029 pc for NGC 7213)  
  > "which for the typical bolometric luminosities of our sample provides $r_{\rm sub} = 0.04\,$pc" (6.2 Fiducial model images for GATOS Seyferts)
   - assumption: Peak of model compared qualitatively, not fitted per galaxy  
  > "In this section we adapt the resolutions of the CAT3D-WIND model images to those of our data, and make a qualitative comparison between models and observations." (6 Comparison between CAT3D-WIND model images and observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative visual comparison of model and observed morphologies  
  > "make a qualitative comparison between models and observations" (6 Comparison between CAT3D-WIND model images and observations)

## Qualitative findings

- Six of twelve galaxies show extended mid-IR emission roughly along the polar/NLR direction; in most of these it is perpendicular to the ALMA 870 um torus emission; NGC 6300 is the exception (equatorial/host)  
  > "In six galaxies there is extended mid-IR emission approximately in the polar direction and perpendicular to the extended $870\,\mu$m component detected with ALMA." (8 Summary)
- Galaxies with little or no extended mid-IR emission are the least luminous in 2-10 keV  
  > "The galaxies with little or no extended mid-IR emission are the least luminous in terms of their $2-10\,$keV luminosities" (7.2 The role of the IR radiation pressure)
- The X-shape wind signature is clearer in the far-IR than in the mid-IR in model images  
  > "Furthermore, this morphology is appreciated better for higher values of $f_{\rm wd}$ and thin cone walls, and in the far-IR rather than the mid-IR." (7.3 Disk-wind models)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GB21 ALMA observations of a volume-limited, complete sample of Seyferts drawn from the Swift-BAT survey  
  > "obtained ALMA observations of a volume-limited and complete sample of Seyfert galaxies to study their torus properties" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Model images explore mid- and far-IR morphologies for several disk and wind configurations informed by IR fits  
  > "The goal is to explore the morphologies at these wavelengths for several disk and wind configurations and geometries as informed by fits to their IR emission." (5 CAT3D-WIND mid- and far-IR model images)
- Alternative: Polar mid-IR emission could be AGN-driven dusty outflow and/or dust at NLR/cone edges; torus models without a wind can also give one-sided morphologies via self-obscuration  
  > "The extended mid-IR emission along the polar direction is likely associated with AGN-driven dusty outflows (cite: Wada2018; Williamson2020; Venanzi2020) and/or dust located in the edges of the NLR and ionization cones." (7.1 Observational considerations); "Torus models without the wind component can also produce this one-sided polar emission at intermediate inclinations and relatively large torus angular widths" (5.4 Thick geometries)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): PSF stability of ground-based mid-IR data limits extraction of extended emission  
  > "One of the main caveats from the analysis of the extended mid-IR emission with ground-based facilities is the stability of the PSF." (7.1 Observational considerations)
- Limitation (authors): Emission lines in imaging filters may contribute to polar alignment in some galaxies  
  > "might be partly responsible for the alignment along the polar direction" (7.1 Observational considerations)
- Limitation (authors): Model comparison depends on assumed sublimation radius; case-by-case comparison needs better r_sub and resolution  
  > "Detailed morphological comparisons on a case-by-case basis require accurate estimates of the sublimation radius as well as higher angular resolution observations, especially in the mid-IR." (7.3 Disk-wind models)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model-data comparison is qualitative only; no goodness-of-fit or per-galaxy model fitting

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extended-emission size range is given as 50-190 pc in Sect. 3.2 but 50-160 pc in Sect. 7.1 and the summary
- Caveat (inferred, ours): PSF-scaling choice (X vs Y) is subjective; model grid is a subset of the CAT3D-WIND parameter space
