## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image interferometric and Total Power CO (2-1) data and combine across spatial scales**: Reapply ALMA observatory pipeline calibration to 12 m and 7 m data, subtract continuum, jointly image and clean both 12 m configurations and 7 m ACA using modified PHANGS-ALMA pipeline (multi-scale then single-scale tclean), calibrate/image TP data with PHANGS-ALMA TP pipeline, correct for primary beam, convolve to circular beams (55, 80, 90, 120, 150 pc), feather interferometric and TP cubes, and regrid pixels to half beam FWHM.  
  > "The observations from the two 12 m-array configurations and the ACA were all imaged and cleaned together." (2.2 Calibration and imaging); "Using the feather task in casa, the TP cubes were combined with both main array configurations plus the ACA." (2.2 Calibration and imaging); "Following Sun et al. (2018) and Brunetti et al. (2021), after feathering the TP data with the convolved interferometric cubes, each cube was spatially regridded such that the pixels were half the synthesized beam FWHM on a side" (2.2 Calibration and imaging)
   - software: CASA, PHANGS-ALMA imaging pipeline, PHANGS-ALMA TP calibration and imaging pipeline
   - parameter: Multi-scale clean scales: point sources, 0.5, 1, 2.5, 5, and 10 arcsec  
  > "These modifications include implementation of different angular sizes of the model components during multi-scale cleaning, for which we use point sources, 0.5, 1, 2.5, 5, and 10 arcsec." (2.2 Calibration and imaging)
   - parameter: Circular synthesized beam FWHMs: 55, 80, 90, 120, and 150 pc  
  > "Several versions of the interferometric cube were made when the synthesized beam was made circular, with FWHM physical sizes of 55, 80, 90, 120, and 150 pc." (2.2 Calibration and imaging)
   - parameter: TP continuum subtraction: order-one polynomial (linear)  
  > "all continuum subtraction was carried out using order-one polynomial (linear) fits to the continuum levels." (2.2 Calibration and imaging)
   - assumption: Distance to NGC 4038/9 = 22 Mpc  
  > "At a distance of 22 Mpc (Schweizer et al. 2008), NGC 4038/9 is the nearest gas-rich major merger" (1 INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: Order-one polynomial (linear) fits for TP continuum subtraction  
  > "all continuum subtraction was carried out using order-one polynomial (linear) fits to the continuum levels." (2.2 Calibration and imaging)
   - validation: Visual inspection of observatory weblog diagnostic plots for calibration issues and visual inspection of cleaned cubes for artefacts before feathering  
  > "We inspected the diagnostic plots in the observatory weblogs to search for problematic data that were not properly calibrated or left unflagged by the ALMA pipeline." (2.2 Calibration and imaging); "Cleaned cubes were also visually inspected for artefacts related to calibration and cleaning errors before combining with the TP data." (2.2 Calibration and imaging)
2. **Calculate moment maps and derive pixel-by-pixel cloud-scale molecular gas properties**: Calculate moment 0, 1, and 2 maps and peak brightness temperature (T_B,max) following Brunetti et al. (2021). Convert integrated intensity to molecular gas mass surface density (Sigma_mol) using a constant Milky Way conversion factor, and compute virial parameter (alpha_vir) and internal turbulent pressure (P_turb/k_B) in each Nyquist-sampled square pixel using Equations 1 and 2.  
  > "Moment maps calculated from the CO (2–1) data cube as described in Brunetti et al. (2021) are shown in Figure 1." (2.2 Calibration and imaging); "Maps of total molecular gas mass surface density (Σmol), velocity dispersion (𝜎𝑣), and peak brightness temperature (𝑇B,max) were calculated in the same way as in Brunetti et al. (2021)." (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties); "From our measurements of Σmol and 𝜎𝑣 we also estimate the virial parameter and internal turbulent pressure in each pixel, following Sun et al. (2020), with" (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties)
   - parameter: alpha_CO(2-1) = 4.35 / 0.70 = 6.25 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "The only difference was that we adopted a single Milky-Way like conversion factor of 𝛼CO(2−1) = 4.35/0.70 = 6.25 M⊙ pc−2 (K km s−1)−1 for all measurements from NGC 4038/9, following Sun et al. (2018)" (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties)
   - assumption: 12CO J=2-1 / J=1-0 line ratio = 0.70 and Milky Way conversion factor (including helium and heavy elements) applies across NGC 4038/9  
  > "where 0.70 is the commonly used value for the 12CO 𝐽=2–1 / 𝐽=1–0 line ratio (cf. Leroy et al. 2022)." (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties); "This conversion factor includes a contribution from helium and other heavy elements." (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties)
   - assumption: A single cloud roughly fills each synthesized beam and cloud density profile follows rho(r) proportional to r^-1  
  > "As described in Sun et al. (2018), these equations assume that a single cloud roughly fills each synthesized beam along with the equation for the virial parameter assuming a cloud density profile that goes as 𝜌(𝑟) ∝ 𝑟−1." (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Separate pixel measurements into regional sub-samples and compare statistical distributions across galaxies**: Divide pixels into nuclear (< 1 kpc from either nucleus), non-nuclear (> 1 kpc), and Whitmore et al. (2014) CO (3-2) overlap region FoV sub-samples. Apply the same 1 kpc nuclear separation to PHANGS-ALMA galaxies. Compute mass-weighted medians, inner 68th percentiles, and 1D/2D Gaussian kernel density estimators (KDEs) to compare NGC 4038/9 with NGC 3256 and 70 PHANGS-ALMA galaxies.  
  > "Following Sun et al. (2018) and Brunetti et al. (2021), all pixels that are less than 1 kpc from either nucleus (shown as gray circles with radii of 1 kpc in Figures 2 and A1) are included in the nuclear pixel sample" (3.2 Separating measurements by region); "Qualitative comparisons between NGC 4038/9, NGC 3256, and the PHANGS-ALMA galaxies presented by Sun et al. (2018) at 120 pc resolution are shown as mass-weighted medians (symbols) and inner 68th percentiles (errorbars) in Figure 3." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties); "Figure 4 shows the mass-weighted distributions of the various sub-samples from NGC 4038/9 and NGC 3256 smoothed with Gaussian kernel density estimators" (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties)
   - software: SciPy
   - parameter: Nuclear region radius = 1 kpc  
  > "Following Sun et al. (2018) and Brunetti et al. (2021), all pixels that are less than 1 kpc from either nucleus (shown as gray circles with radii of 1 kpc in Figures 2 and A1) are included in the nuclear pixel sample" (3.2 Separating measurements by region)
   - parameter: Gaussian KDE bandwidth selection: Scott's Rule  
  > "PDFs are made from Gaussian KDEs, with bandwidths automatically chosen using the scipy implementation of Scott’s Rule (Scott 1992)." (4.2 Correlations between velocity dispersion and mass surface density)
   - fitting: Gaussian kernel density estimation using Scott's Rule for bandwidth selection  
  > "PDFs are made from Gaussian KDEs, with bandwidths automatically chosen using the scipy implementation of Scott’s Rule (Scott 1992)." (4.2 Correlations between velocity dispersion and mass surface density)
   - validation: Checked consistency of distributions across multiple spatial resolutions (55, 80, 90, 120, and 150 pc)  
  > "Measurements at 120 pc resolution are shown but comparisons at 80 pc and 55 pc resolution were also made with generally the same results as seen in Figures 3 and 4." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties); "We found very little difference between using measurements made at 90 pc and 150 pc resolution." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties)

## Qualitative findings

- Compared to 70 PHANGS-ALMA spiral galaxies at matched resolution, the molecular gas in NGC 4038/9 exhibits some of the highest mass surface densities, velocity dispersions, peak brightness temperatures, and internal turbulent pressures, overlapping most with the central regions of massive (primarily barred) spiral galaxies.  
  > "Compared to PHANGS-ALMA galaxies at matched spatial resolution, the molecular gas in the Antennae exhibits some of the highest surface densities, velocity dispersions, peak brightness temperatures, and turbulent pressures." (abstract); "The apertures from the centres of (primarily barred) PHANGS-ALMA galaxies populate a very similar part of the parameter space to NGC 4038/9 and NGC 3256" (4.2 Correlations between velocity dispersion and mass surface density)
- Virial parameters in NGC 4038/9 span a wide range but have median values consistent with typical PHANGS-ALMA spiral galaxies and less than half the median value measured in NGC 3256.  
  > "However, the virial parameters in the Antennae are consistent with many of the PHANGS-ALMA galaxies." (abstract); "The median virial parameter in NGC 4038/9 is less than half that in NGC 3256." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties)
- NGC 3256 and NGC 4038/9 have similar gas surface density distributions (with NGC 4038/9 reaching slightly higher maximum values), but NGC 3256 has significantly higher nuclear velocity dispersions and higher system-wide peak brightness temperatures and virial parameters.  
  > "NGC 3256 has similar gas surface densities but higher nuclear velocity dispersions than the Antennae, as well as higher system-wide peak brightness temperatures and virial parameters." (abstract); "Gas surface densities are similar between NGC 4038/9 and NGC 3256, though the highest values are measured in NGC 4038/9." (6 CONCLUSIONS)
- In the nuclear region of NGC 4038/9, the velocity dispersion versus surface density trend flattens above ~10^3 M_sun pc^-2, which may indicate a lower CO-to-H2 conversion factor near the nuclei or gas that is far from self-gravitating.  
  > "While a trend between the velocity dispersion and surface density in NGC 4038/9 is obvious up to about 103 M⊙ pc−2, for surface densities above this value there is a flattening of the trend that is most easily seen in the nuclei in Figure 7 (top panel)." (5.2 CO-to-H2 conversion factor considerations); "An alternative interpretation is that the gas in the nuclei is far from self-gravitating, in which case no correlation between velocity dispersion and surface density would be expected." (5.2 CO-to-H2 conversion factor considerations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Extending the sample of mergers observed at ~100 pc resolution beyond a single LIRG (NGC 3256) to NGC 4038/9 (at an earlier merger stage) with full spatial-scale coverage (12m + 7m + TP) across the central 9 kpc allows probing how cloud-scale molecular gas properties depend on merger phase.  
  > "However, a single merger that happens to be the nearest LIRG cannot be expected to be representative of all merging and starbursting systems. To extend the sample of mergers, we have observed NGC 4038/9 (Arp 244, “the Antennae”) at ∼100 pc spatial resolution to add it to these comparisons of molecular gas properties." (1 INTRODUCTION); "These observations have comparable sensitivity and resolution but are a significant improvement in area coverage (see Figure 1), and image fidelity (covering the full range of spatial scales) over earlier ALMA observations of the Antennae (Whitmore et al. 2014; Sun et al. 2018)." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Used a modified PHANGS-ALMA imaging pipeline to handle the complex emission morphology and multiple array configurations consistently with PHANGS-ALMA, and adopted the same pixel-based analysis, constant Milky Way conversion factor, and 1 kpc nuclear boundary definition to enable direct, homogeneous comparisons with Sun et al. (2018, 2020) and Brunetti et al. (2021).  
  > "Given the complex morphology of molecular line emission expected in the Antennae at the resolution of our observations, and the number of lines to be imaged, we requested access to an early version of the PHANGS-ALMA imaging pipeline1 for casa (Leroy et al. 2021a)." (2.2 Calibration and imaging); "Using the same conversion factor as Sun et al. (2018) means any differences in the estimated mass surface densities originate from different integrated intensities." (3.1 Measuring molecular gas properties); "We chose this approach to reduce variability in the comparison with NGC 4038/9 potentially brought on by differences in how pixels are assigned to the different regions." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties)
- Alternative: Using near-infrared morphological centre/disc designations from Sun et al. (2020) / Querejeta et al. (2021) instead of a fixed 1 kpc radius around galaxy centres  
  > "Note that Sun et al. (2020) separate their aperture measurements into central and disc regions within each galaxy based on distinct structures identified in near-infrared images (the full description of this methodology is given by Querejeta et al. 2021) rather than by a 1 kpc radius as we do in this work3." (3.2 Separating measurements by region); "Note that we made the separation of pixels into regions in the PHANGS-ALMA galaxies in the same manner as for NGC 4038/9, not using the centre and disc designations from Sun et al. (2020)." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties)
- Alternative: Using a starburst/RADEX-derived CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO(2-1) ~ 10 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1) instead of the Milky Way value (6.25)  
  > "Scaling this conversion factor to CO (2–1), with the same 𝑅21 we adopted, gives a value of 𝛼CO(2−1) ≈ 10 M⊙ pc−2 (K km s−1)−1. Given the observed spread in the conversion factor within starbursts of factors of ∼3 to 4 (Bolatto et al. 2013), we have opted to simplify comparisons to previous works by simply using the Milky Way value." (5.2 CO-to-H2 conversion factor considerations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Using a fixed 1 kpc radius to define central regions across PHANGS-ALMA galaxies means low stellar mass (smaller) galaxies have a larger fraction of contaminating disc pixels in their central 1 kpc, likely underestimating their central percentiles.  
  > "However, given the size-stellar mass relation of galaxies, the fraction of a galaxy included in the central region will increase as stellar mass decreases." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties); "This effect will likely result in the percentiles from the centres of low stellar mass galaxies being underestimated since contaminating disc pixels will make up a larger fraction of pixels in their central 1 kpc." (4.1 Distribution of cloud-scale molecular gas properties)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity limits truncate the detection of low surface density regions with large velocity dispersions, preventing determination of whether the velocity dispersion-surface density relation in mergers is broader at all surface densities or only at high surface densities.  
  > "However, the sensitivity limit in this parameter space roughly follows the upper-left edge of the contours (see Figure 1 from Sun et al. 2020), so that we do not have enough sensitivity to detect low surface density regions with large velocity dispersions." (4.2 Correlations between velocity dispersion and mass surface density); "To explore if the trends in the mergers truly broaden only at high surface densities or are possibly broader than the PHANGS-ALMA trend at all surface densities would require observations with a sensitivity a factor of two to four times better than the current dataset." (4.2 Correlations between velocity dispersion and mass surface density)
- Limitation (authors): Virial parameter estimates do not account for external pressure confinement (e.g., from stellar surface density or cloud-cloud collisions) that could help bind the gas.  
  > "We must also caution that the virial parameters shown in Figures 3 through 5 do not account for pressure confinement contributing to binding the observed molecular gas." (5.1 Comparison of the two mergers)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming a single Milky Way CO-to-H2 conversion factor across all of NGC 4038/9 is likely an oversimplification given expected spatial and temporal variations in mergers.  
  > "However, it is likely an oversimplification to assume all regions within NGC 4038/9 truly have the same conversion factor." (5.2 CO-to-H2 conversion factor considerations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adopting a constant Milky Way alpha_CO and a fixed R_21 = 0.70 across both the quiescent filaments and extreme starbursting overlap/nuclear regions of the Antennae may systematically bias Sigma_mol, alpha_vir, and P_turb.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beam-averaged pixel statistics at 55-150 pc assume a single spherical cloud fills each beam with an r^-1 density profile, which may break down in crowded merger nuclei and filaments where multiple velocity components or diffuse inter-cloud gas overlap along the line of sight.
