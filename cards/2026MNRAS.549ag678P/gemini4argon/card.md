## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure radio and millimeter continuum flux densities**: Download ALMA QA2 pipeline-calibrated images and report QA2 peak flux density and rms (verified with imtool); reduce VLA data with CASA VLA pipeline and VLA Imaging Pipeline and measure flux density and rms using pwkit/imtool  
  > "We reduced and analysed the data using the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: McMullin+2007; CASA2022) VLA pipeline and produced calibrated continuum images using the VLA Imaging Pipeline." (Very Large Array); "To determine the flux density of the transient and RMS for each image, we use pwkit/imtool (cite: 2017ascl.soft04001W)." (Very Large Array); "We downloaded the pipeline calibrated images from the ALMA archive and report the peak flux density and rms reported in the QA2 results in Table 4." (Atacama Large Millimetre Array)
   - software: CASA, VLA Imaging Pipeline, ALMA pipeline, pwkit/imtool
   - parameter: VLA sub-bands of 0.5 GHz, 1 GHz, or 2 GHz as well as full receiver bandwidth  
  > "Images and flux-density measurements were produced both for the full receiver bandwidth and also in sub-bands of 0.5 GHz, 1 GHz, or 2 GHz bandwidth (depending on the frequency and S/N)." (Very Large Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked ALMA QA2 results by re-measuring flux density and rms with imtool and found consistent values  
  > "To check the QA2 results, we also used imtool to measure the flux density and rms and found the values to be consistent with the QA2 values." (Atacama Large Millimetre Array)
2. **Model radio/millimeter SEDs and derive shock and CSM properties**: Fit a soft broken power-law to radio+ALMA SEDs at six epochs (30, 41, 66, 103, 119, 177 rest-frame days) to find peak flux and peak frequency, then infer shock radius, magnetic field, total energy, shock speed, and ambient electron density using synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) equations  
  > "To infer the properties of the forward shock and CSM, we model the radio emission from AT 2024wpp using the traditional synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) framework (cite: Chevalier+1998; Soderberg+2005)." (Radio evolution); "We first perform initial fits of a soft broken power-law ($F_\nu = F_{\nu,{\rm peak}}(({\nu/\nu_{\rm peak})^{-s\beta_1}+(\nu/\nu_{\rm peak})^{-s\beta_2}})^{-1/s}$) to the sub-band gridded radio data at 41  and 66 d (the only two epochs where the peak is continuously sampled) to estimate values of the spectral index below the peak, the spectral index above the peak, and the sharpness parameter" (Radio evolution); "We then fix $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$, and $s$ to the values above, and fit each SED using the band-integrated flux density measurements to determine the peak frequency ($\nu_{\rm p}$) and flux ($F_{\rm p}$) for all six epochs; the basic shock parameters are inferred from these values via equations 2–6 from (cite: Chrimes+2024b)." (Radio evolution)
   - parameter: beta_1 = +1.17, beta_2 = -0.90, s = 1.5  
  > "these are found to be $\beta_1 = +$1.17, $\beta_2 = -$0.90, and $s=$ 1.5, respectively." (Radio evolution)
   - parameter: Factor of 1.37 correction applied to 27d X and Ku measurements in first epoch SED  
  > "We apply a correction factor of 1.37 (consistent with a source rising as $F \sim t^{+2.5}$ between 27 and 31 days) to the X and Ku measurements, which removes the discontinuity" (Radio evolution)
   - assumption: Nonthermal power-law electron energy distribution with p = 3, volume filling factor f = 0.5, and equipartition epsilon_B = epsilon_e = 1/3  
  > "We assume an electron energy power-law index of $p=3$, a volume filling factor of $f=0.5$, and equipartition such that the magnetic energy density ($\epsilon_B$) and energy density of electrons ($\epsilon_e$) contribute equally to the post shock energy ($\epsilon_B$=$\epsilon_e$=1/3)." (Radio evolution)
   - assumption: 10% overall flux calibration uncertainty and interstellar scintillation modulations included  
  > "Flux modulations induced by interstellar scintillation (see following text) were included in the uncertainty analysis, as was an overall 10% flux calibration uncertainty component applied uniformly to all bands." (Radio evolution)
   - fitting: Soft broken power-law fit with uncertainties (95% confidence) determined from a Monte Carlo procedure  
  > "Uncertainties (95% confidence) on each parameter are determined from a Monte Carlo procedure." (Radio evolution)
   - validation: Compared low-frequency SED deviations against interstellar scintillation predictions from Walker 1998 and Walker 2001 maps  
  > "From the equations in (cite: Walker+1998), and assuming a source expanding radially at 0.2$c$, large ($m>0.3$) scintillation effects are expected below 13 GHz at 30 d, below 11 GHz at 41d, and below 6 GHz at 119 d (the expected modulation range is shown as a light grey envelope in Figure 16), in agreement with the deviations observed." (Radio evolution)
3. **Construct UVOIR light curves and fit blackbody evolution**: Perform image subtraction and forced/aperture/PSF photometry across UVOIR facilities, apply empirical filter offsets and Galactic extinction correction, interpolate light curves using polynomial fits and local regression, and fit Planck functions to co-eval SEDs from 2 to 111 rest-frame days  
  > "We interpolate the photometry shown in Figure 2 using a combination of direct polynomial fits and local regression, and sample the resulting model light curves at a list of selected times that roughly match the mean times of the Swift/UVOT epochs (at early times) or the HST and VLT multi-band observations (at late times) to form a series of co-eval SEDs between approximately 2 and 111 rest-frame days after the assumed explosion time." (Blackbody Evolution); "These are then fit to a Planck function at each epoch." (Blackbody Evolution)
   - software: HEAsoft, uvotsource, uvotimsum, SWARP, sep, SExtractor, ASTROMETRY.NET, SFFT, mirar, EsoReflex, AstroDrizzle, ccdproc
   - parameter: t_0 = MJD 60578.40  
  > "The time of first light is taken to be MJD 60578.40 based on a power-law fit to the early $g$-band data, shown in the figure inset." (UV/Optical Light Curve)
   - parameter: 10% systematic error term included in SED fits; PS1 y-band after 16 rest-frame days excluded  
  > "For all bands, we further include a 10% systematic error term to approximate the effect of calibration uncertainties, variation in the effective central wavelength, deviations from a perfect blackbody, and uncertainty in the interpolation model." (Blackbody Evolution); "Pan-STARRS 1 $y$-band measurements after 16 rest-frame days were excluded from the fit due to the possibility of NIR excess affecting these measurements." (Blackbody Evolution)
   - assumption: Cosmology: Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1; Galactic E(B-V) = 0.026 mag; zero host extinction  
  > "Throughout the paper we assume a cosmology with $\Omega_M=0.3$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.7$, $H_0$ = 70 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ and estimate Galactic reddening via the dust maps of (cite: Schlafly+2011) and extinction curve of (cite: Fitzpatrick1999); for AT 2024wpp we use a value of $E_{B-V} = 0.026$ mag." (Introduction); "(We correct for Galactic extinction but do not correct for any host extinction, for the reasons described in the previous subsection.)" (Blackbody Evolution)
   - fitting: Planck function fit to interpolated multi-band fluxes at 16 epochs  
  > "These are then fit to a Planck function at each epoch." (Blackbody Evolution)
   - validation: 10% systematic error chosen to achieve reduced chi-squared of approximately 1 on average across all fits  
  > "The value of 10% was was chosen to achieve $\hat{\chi}^2\approx1$ on average across all fits." (Blackbody Evolution)
4. **Analyze X-ray spectrum and light curve**: Extract Swift/XRT count rates using Swift team online tools, fit a power-law spectrum to the first ten days of observations, and convert count rates to unabsorbed 0.3-10 keV fluxes using WebPIMMS  
  > "To obtain XRT count rates we used the tools developed by the Swift team (cite: Evans2007; Evans2009)." (XRT); "We fit a single spectrum for all XRT observations within the first ten days (OBS ID 00016843002–00016848008), except for the first epoch (due to the cosmic ray hit)." (XRT); "From WebPIMMS, applying an intrinsic host $N_H = 6.3\times10^{20}\,$cm$^{-2}$ from our own analysis (§3.6), the conversion factor from count rate to unabsorbed flux is $4.865\times10^{-11}$ erg cm$^{-2}$." (XRT)
   - software: WebPIMMS
   - parameter: Conversion factor = 4.865e-11 erg cm^-2 per count  
  > "the conversion factor from count rate to unabsorbed flux is $4.865\times10^{-11}$ erg cm$^{-2}$." (XRT)
   - assumption: Milky Way N_H = 2.7e20 cm^-2 and intrinsic host N_H = 6.3e20 cm^-2  
  > "The fit assumed a Milky Way $N_H = 2.7 \times 10^{20}\,$cm$^{-2}$ (cite: Willingale2013)." (XRT); "From WebPIMMS, applying an intrinsic host $N_H = 6.3\times10^{20}\,$cm$^{-2}$ from our own analysis (§3.6), the conversion factor from count rate to unabsorbed flux is $4.865\times10^{-11}$ erg cm$^{-2}$." (XRT)
   - fitting: Power-law spectral fit with reduced chi-squared = 1.2  
  > "We found that the data are well described by a power law with index $\Gamma=1.7^{+0.3}_{-0.3}$ (90% confidence). The reduced chi squared of the fit is $\chi_r^2=1.2$." (XRT)
   - validation: Visual inspection of snapshots to exclude cosmic ray hit in OBS ID 00016843001  
  > "Visual inspection revealed that during the third snapshot (orbit) of the first observation (OBS ID 00016843001), the source position was impacted by a cosmic ray hit in the CCD baseline region." (XRT)
5. **Analyze UV and optical spectroscopy of transient**: Reduce ground-based and HST COS/STIS spectra, rescale to interpolated photometry, measure narrow ISM absorption equivalent widths via Gaussian fits or direct integration, fit damped Lyman-alpha profile, and measure late-time H and He emission line profiles  
  > "Following the initial reductions for each instrument, we apply an additional correction to each spectrum to match the flux scale from our interpolated photometry." (Ground-Based Spectroscopy); "We fit the high-S/N host lines in the COS spectrum (Si, O, C, S) with a Gaussian absorption model convolved with the COS line-spread function and find that the line is consistent with being saturated in all cases, so it is not possible to derive abundances from these observations." (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis); "The broad Lyman-$\alpha$ feature (the blue wing of which is cut off by geocoronal contamination) is well-fit by a damped Lyman-$\alpha$ profile and indicates a hydrogen column through the host of (6.5 $\pm$ 0.4) $\times$ $10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis)
   - software: DBSP-DRP, PypeIt, UCSC spectral pipeline, SimSpec, PyRAF, LPipe, numpy, scipy, astropy, matplotlib, lacosmic, ccdproc
   - parameter: UV+optical spectrum binned to R approx 200 (1400 km s^-1)  
  > "Individual spectra have been binned to $R\approx200$ (1400 km s$^{-1}$ resolution) to reduce noise and emphasize broad features, and regions of the spectrum affected by strong geocoronal absorption or emission have been removed." (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis)
   - fitting: Gaussian absorption model convolved with COS LSF; damped Lyman-alpha profile fit  
  > "We fit the high-S/N host lines in the COS spectrum (Si, O, C, S) with a Gaussian absorption model convolved with the COS line-spread function" (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis); "The broad Lyman-$\alpha$ feature (the blue wing of which is cut off by geocoronal contamination) is well-fit by a damped Lyman-$\alpha$ profile" (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis)
   - validation: Cross-checked broad and narrow spectral features across multiple independent instruments and reductions  
  > "However, there are several features seen consistently in spectra from multiple instruments (and reduced independently by different individuals using different standard-stars and independent techniques) that we are confident are real." (Spectroscopic Evolution)
6. **Derive host galaxy physical properties from SED and IFU spectroscopy**: Fit UV-to-MIR host galaxy photometry with prospector (using FSPS and dynesty) after removing nebular line contributions, and fit Gaussian profiles to continuum-subtracted KCWI IFU emission lines to derive Balmer decrement, SFR, and gas-phase metallicity  
  > "We use the python package prospector (cite: Leja+2017; Johnson+2021) to derive the host galaxy properties from photometry. This package models the galaxy’s SED using the Flexible Stellar Population Synthesis (FSPS; (cite: Conroy+2010FSPS)) package, and fits the model to the observations with dynesty." (Host galaxy properties); "To measure fluxes of major strong emission lines, we first subtract the stellar continuum using an SED model from the photometric fit. We then fit a Gaussian profile at the rest wavelength of each corresponding line of interest." (Host galaxy properties)

## Qualitative findings

- AT 2024wpp exhibits an early photospheric expansion at ~0.1-0.2c up to ~1.5e15 cm at ~5 days before smoothly contracting to ~1e14 cm at 100 days while remaining hot (>15000 K).  
  > "Specifically, between the inferred explosion time (shortly before the first P48 detection) and 2.1 d, we infer an expansion rate of $0.17c$." (Blackbody Evolution); "The photosphere reached a maximum radius of $\sim$1.5 $\times$ $10^{15}$ cm (100 AU) before the radius began to decrease." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Early optical and 20-day HST FUV spectra are devoid of broad ejecta or stellar wind emission/absorption features, consistent with strong X-ray photoionization and Compton heating of a low-density fast wind and inner CSM.  
  > "Ultraviolet spectroscopy at 20 days shows a featureless spectrum approximately consistent with a 21000 K blackbody with only minor deviations. P-Cygni or other spectral features from a stellar wind, or from the ejecta itself, are absent." (Summary and Conclusions); "The X-rays keep the low-density wind and inner CSM almost completely ionized, suppressing any line formation and allowing the transient to maintain a hot photosphere within the expanding wind, which slowly contracts with time as the wind gradually tapers off." (Summary and Conclusions)
- After 35 days, double-peaked narrow/intermediate-width H and He emission lines emerge with a systemic component and a -6600 km s^-1 blueshifted component, indicating stable asymmetric structures such as tidal streams or an ablated companion.  
  > "Weak H and He signatures do emerge in the spectra after 35 days in the form of double-peaked narrow lines. Each peak is individually narrow (full width $\delta v \sim 3000$ km s$^{-1}$) but the two components are separated by $\Delta v \sim 6600$ km s$^{-1}$, indicating stable structures of denser material, possibly representing streams of tidal ejecta or an ablated companion star." (Abstract)
- Radio and millimeter observations trace a ~0.2c shock expanding down a steep n_e propto r^-3 CSM density profile for the first ~100 days, followed by a rapid flux decline after 119 days at nearly constant peak frequency.  
  > "After $\sim$119 days the shock properties undergo a significant transition: the peak flux of the SED begins to decline dramatically, even as the peak frequency remains essentially constant." (Radio evolution); "The properties inferred by the first 100 days of radio observations indicate an energetic shock expanding down a steep density profile ($n \propto r^{-3}$)." (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Early rapid-response multi-band UV/optical imaging captured the pre-peak photospheric expansion; HST COS/STIS FUV spectroscopy tested for resonance metal and wind lines; ALMA+VLA observations sampled the high-frequency synchrotron peak to constrain shock speed and CSM density.  
  > "The observations presented here extend the optical temporal coverage both earlier and later in time than previous papers, and include other types of data that have not yet been reported for this event (and have rarely or never been reported for any previous LFBOT): FUV spectroscopy, multi-epoch late-time optical spectroscopy, and high-frequency submillimetre observations." (Introduction); "We first perform initial fits of a soft broken power-law ($F_\nu = F_{\nu,{\rm peak}}(({\nu/\nu_{\rm peak})^{-s\beta_1}+(\nu/\nu_{\rm peak})^{-s\beta_2}})^{-1/s}$) to the sub-band gridded radio data at 41  and 66 d (the only two epochs where the peak is continuously sampled) to estimate values of the spectral index below the peak, the spectral index above the peak, and the sharpness parameter" (Radio evolution)
- Why this method: Synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) modeling was used to infer shock and CSM properties consistently with previous LFBOT studies, while template image subtraction and host SED modeling were used to remove host galaxy contamination at late times.  
  > "The SSA framework has been assumed for previously discovered LFBOTs to infer the shock radius $R_{\rm p}$, shock speed $\beta_{\rm sh}$, magnetic field strength $B_{\rm p}$, total energy $U_{\rm tot}$, and density of the ambient medium $n_e$ (cite: Ho+2019; Coppejans+2020; Ho+2020koala; Ho+2022; Yao+2022; Chrimes+2024b)." (Radio evolution); "Our late-time measurements in particular should be more robust than those currently reported as we use host-galaxy template subtraction for all of our observations." (Additional constraints from recent publications)
- Alternative: Radioactive 56Ni decay as power source: rejected because explaining the peak luminosity would require at least 300 M_sun of 56Ni, whereas the inferred ejecta mass is ~0.1 M_sun.  
  > "Radioactive decay is clearly insufficient even without considering the low ejecta mass (at least 300 $M_\odot$ of $^{56}$Ni would be required to explain the peak luminosity)." (Ejecta mass constraints and inner pre-explosion material)
- Alternative: Multi-temperature blackbody fit to UV spectrum alone: rejected because the resulting fit substantially overpredicts the optical region of the spectrum.  
  > "We experimented fitting a multi-temperature blackbody to the UV alone, but the resulting fit (shown as the blue curve) substantially overpredicts the optical region of the spectrum." (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis)
- Alternative: Hidden Type Ic-BL supernova component explaining the 11-13 day broad optical features: rejected because the features disappear rather than strengthen at later times.  
  > "While it is thus tempting to attribute these features to some sort of “hidden” SN component, these features then disappear rather than strengthen, as would be expected if they truly represented optically-thick, radioactively-heated material expanding homologously (i.e., a traditional SN component)." (The origin of intermediate-time broad spectral features)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Empirical constant magnitude offsets applied to align multi-facility optical light curves do not account for the time-evolving color of the transient and leave a systematic flux calibration uncertainty.  
  > "It should be cautioned that this technique does not account for the time-evolving colour of the object, and because the choice of which instrument to treat as the baseline is arbitrary a systematic uncertainty in the flux calibration comparable to the characteristic offsets will remain in any resulting SED analysis." (Ground-based follow-up Imaging)
- Limitation (authors): Early blackbody temperature and radius estimates are somewhat uncertain because at T > 30 kK the true blackbody peak lies in the unobserved extreme UV.  
  > "While these measurements are somewhat uncertain due to the very high temperatures ($>30$kK, such that the true blackbody peak is in the extreme UV and thus poorly constrained), they are consistent with the notion that the photosphere is expanding at very high speeds of 0.1–0.2$c$ for a few days before rapidly transitioning into a phase of photospheric contraction." (Blackbody Evolution)
- Limitation (authors): Host ISM UV absorption lines in the COS spectrum are saturated, preventing abundance determinations.  
  > "We fit the high-S/N host lines in the COS spectrum (Si, O, C, S) with a Gaussian absorption model convolved with the COS line-spread function and find that the line is consistent with being saturated in all cases, so it is not possible to derive abundances from these observations." (Ultraviolet Spectroscopic Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): The simplified spherical forward-shock non-thermal SSA model cannot explain the rapid radio flux drop at constant peak frequency after 119 days and omits thermal electron or reverse-shock contributions.  
  > "As our simplified model assumes spherical symmetry, it is not possible to test this possibility in detail at this stage. The non-standard evolution could also indicate larger deviations from the basic model." (Radio evolution); "We leave modelling via a thermal relativistic-Maxwellian prescription (cite: Ho+2022; Margalit+2021; Margalit+2024) for future work." (Radio evolution)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High-frequency (>15 GHz) late-time (>30 d) VLA/ALMA flux densities reported here are systematically brighter than ATCA/radio measurements reported in Nayana et al. (2025), leading to a different inferred peak frequency evolution between 50 and 120 days.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Equipartition (epsilon_e = epsilon_B = 1/3) and non-thermal electron assumptions in the SSA model strongly affect the absolute values of inferred shock energy and CSM density.
