1. **Subtract the NIRSpec background and verify alignment**: Mask outside the IFU, sigma-clip emission, estimate channel backgrounds with Background2D, inspect and subtract; compare synthetic R100 filter images with HST.  
  > "First, we mask all data in the cube that falls outside the IFU footprint and apply sigma clipping ($2.5\sigma$) to exclude emission." (Observations and calibration); "Using the PHOTUTILS task *Background2D* with a $14\times14$ spaxel ($0.7''\times0.7''$) square window and a $5\times5$ spaxel median filter, we determine approximate background maps of each spectral channel in the data cube." (Observations and calibration); "Comparison images were created by convolving the R100 data cube with each HST/WFC3 filter bandpass function." (Observations and calibration)
   - software: PHOTUTILS, Background2D
   - parameter: 2.5σ clipping; 14×14 spaxel window; 5×5 spaxel median filter  
  > "First, we mask all data in the cube that falls outside the IFU footprint and apply sigma clipping ($2.5\sigma$) to exclude emission." (Observations and calibration); "Using the PHOTUTILS task *Background2D* with a $14\times14$ spaxel ($0.7''\times0.7''$) square window and a $5\times5$ spaxel median filter, we determine approximate background maps of each spectral channel in the data cube." (Observations and calibration)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visually inspect backgrounds; no HST/JWST offset >0.1''; confirm R100/R2700 alignment.  
  > "These background maps are visually inspected to verify that they do not contain emission (i.e., line or continuum), and are then subtracted from the observed data cube." (Observations and calibration); "No significant offset (i.e., $>0.1''$) between the HST and JWST images was found, so no astrometric correction was performed." (Observations and calibration); "We have also confirmed that the R100 and R2700 cubes are aligned to the same frame" (Observations and calibration)
2. **Fit component spectra**: Extract R100/R2700 spectra at three line peaks; fit continuum and emission lines, with narrow and broad components in R2700.  
  > "We begin by extracting integrated spectra from the R100 and R2700 cubes using the three emission line-focused apertures and fitting the line and continuum emission." (Component Analysis); "For the R2700 spectra, we allow each line to contain a narrow and broad model component at different redshifts" (Component Analysis)
   - software: LMFIT
   - parameter: Circular aperture radius 0.125'' at each component line peak  
  > "three circular apertures (radius $0.125''$) centred on the line peaks of each component" (Overview of continuum and line emission)
   - parameter: Four intrinsic FWHMs: narrow/broad Balmer and narrow/broad forbidden lines; intrinsic width and LSF added in quadrature  
  > "we fit four FWHM values (in km s$^{-1}$) for each extracted spectrum: the narrow and broad FWHMs for the Balmer lines, and the narrow and broad FWHMs for the other lines." (Component Analysis); "by adding the modeled intrinsic linewidth and instrumental line spread function (LSF[^6]) in quadrature." (Component Analysis)
   - parameter: R2700 single power-law continuum; R100 Heaviside Lyα break and separate continuum power laws  
  > "The continuum of the R2700 spectrum is taken into account by including a single power law in the model with slope $\alpha_{\rm opt}^{R2700}$." (Component Analysis); "For the R100 spectrum, the Ly$\alpha$ break was modeled using a Heaviside function." (Component Analysis); "To allow for a discontinuity around the Balmer break, we fit the continuum redwards and bluewards of a Balmer series line (H$\eta$, $\lambda_{\rm rest}=3837\textup{\AA}$) with separate power law models" (Component Analysis)
   - parameter: Additional R100 continuum power law bluewards of λrest=0.15 μm; continuous at the pivot  
  > "fitting the continuum blue-wards of a pivot wavelength ($\lambda_{\rm rest}=0.15\,\mu$m) with a separate power law component (slope $\beta_{\rm Ly\alpha}^{R100}$), such that the model is continuous at $\lambda_{\rm rest}=0.15\,\mu$m." (Component Analysis)
   - assumption: [N II] 6584/6548=2.94; [O III] 5007/4959=2.98  
  > "We adopt the standard assumptions of \[NII\]$\lambda 6584$/\[NII\]$\lambda 6548$$=2.94$ (e.g. (cite: dojc23)) and \[OIII\]$\lambda 5007$/\[OIII\]$\lambda 4959$$=2.98$ (e.g. (cite: dimi07)) for each fit." (Component Analysis)
   - assumption: [S II] 6716/6731∼0.55–1.45 at Te=10^4 K  
  > "The ratio \[SII\]$\lambda 6716$/\[SII\]$\lambda 6731$ is a strong tracer of electron density, and is restricted to a range of $\sim0.55-1.45$, assuming $T_e=10^4$ K (e.g., (cite: leti11; dell20))." (Component Analysis)
   - assumption: R100 lines unresolved with fiducial LSF; [O II] doublet represented by one Gaussian  
  > "The line widths of the R100 fits are set using the fiducial LSF (i.e., each line is assumed to be unresolved in velocity space)." (Component Analysis); "Due to the low spectral resolution, we model \[OII\]$\lambda\lambda 3726{,}3729$ as a single Gaussian." (Component Analysis)
   - fitting: LMFIT least_squares with inverse variance weighting; rescaled pipeline error spectra  
  > "Each spectrum is fit with LMFIT (cite: newv14) in ‘least_squares’ mode, using inverse variance weighting." (Component Analysis); "A corrected error spectrum is derived by rescaling the pipeline-output error array to match the standard deviation of sigma-clipped data (e.g., (cite: uble23b; jone24))." (Component Analysis)
   - validation: Low residuals; use R2700 redshifts because they agree better with ALMA  
  > "The spectra of all three components are well-fit with our models, resulting in low residuals and estimates of redshift, UV magnitude, spectral slopes, line-widths, and line fluxes." (Spectral fit results); "Because the R2700 redshift is in better agreement with the ALMA-based redshift, we only use redshifts based on the R2700 data in this work." (Spectral fit results)

4. **Map ionised gas kinematics and conditions**: Fit each R2700 spaxel with the component spectral model; iteratively remove components with S/N<3; derive line fluxes, continuum, CDF kinematics, extinction and metallicity.

(R2700 resolved analysis); "This results in estimates of $v_{50}$ , $w_{80}\equiv v_{90}-v_{10}$, and asymmetry$=|v_{50}-v_{90}|-|v_{50}-v_{10}|$ (e.g., (cite: herv23))." (R2700 resolved analysis)
   - software: LMFIT
   - parameter: Systemic redshift z=5.6543  
  > "the velocity (with respect to a systemic redshift of $z=5.6543$)" (R2700 resolved analysis)
   - parameter: Balmer decrement calculated where Hα and Hβ are each >1σ  
  > "E(B-V) is calculated using the observed Balmer decrement for each spaxel when both H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$ are well determined (i.e., $>1\sigma$)." (R2700 resolved analysis)
   - assumption: Same line and continuum model as component analysis  
  > "We explore the R2700 data cube by fitting the spectrum of each spaxel using the flexible line and continuum model detailed in Section [3.2](#CA)." (R2700 resolved analysis)
   - fitting: LMFIT least_squares; initially all variables free  
  > "Using LMFIT in ‘least_squares’ mode, we first fit each spectrum with all variables free." (R2700 resolved analysis)
   - validation: Compare against non-parametric [O III] kinematics, aperture results and integrated results

5. **Map the UV continuum slope**: Fit R100 spaxels with the spectral model; include lines to reduce continuum-fit bias; retain UV slopes when continuum normalisation is >1σ.  
  > "Thus, we focus on the continuum emission, and only include line emission in our fits in order to reduce their bias on our fits." (R100 resolved analysis); "The best-fit continuum power law slopes bluewards of the Balmer break ($\beta_{\rm UV}$) are recorded if the corresponding power law normalisations are well determined (i.e., $>1\sigma$)." (R100 resolved analysis)
   - software: LMFIT
   - assumption: Same spectral-fitting procedure as component analysis

## Qualitative findings

- Three line-emission peaks and an additional northern continuum component  
  > "The line emission is concentrated in three peaks (here called HZ10-E, HZ10-C, and HZ10-W), while the continuum emission overlaps these peaks and features additional emission to the north of HZ10-C." (Nature of components)
- Strong east-west velocity gradient; HZ10-E red asymmetry and HZ10-W blue asymmetry  
  > "a strong east-west velocity gradient with no major trends in $w_{80}$ (expect for a slightly wider linewidth in HZ10-W)." (R2700 resolved analysis); "HZ10-E and HZ10-W feature red and blue asymmetry, respectively, while HZ10-C straddles a separate gradient of asymmetry." (R2700 resolved analysis)
- Beam-matched Hα and [C II] agree in HZ10-C but are slightly offset in HZ10-W  
  > "When convolved to match the ALMA beam to compare to the \[C II\] morphology, we find a slight east-west offset between the two tracers for HZ10-W and a good match for HZ10-C(as found by (cite: teli24))." (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data)
- No strong [C II] peak toward HZ10-E  
  > "The \[C II\] emission does not feature an extension towards HZ10-E(as seen in H$\alpha$), suggesting the lack of a strong \[C II\] peak in HZ10-E." (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data)
- Northern UV-optical continuum may trace a gas-poor population  
  > "On the other hand, the rest UV-optical continuum emission to the north of HZ10-C is offset from the \[C II\], suggesting a gas-poor population." (Conclusions)
- HZ10-W is interpreted as an independent galaxy  
  > "we conclude that this is an independent galaxy with a coincident stellar and gas component." (HZ10-W)
- The system likely represents a merging group  
  > "Due to their close spatial and spectral association, they likely represent a merging group." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA [C II] probes areas whose UV emission may be hidden by strong dust obscuration  
  > "it is still possible that there are highly attenuated areas whose faint UV emission falls beneath our sensitivity limit." (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data); "To study areas possibly affected by strong dust obscuration, we leverage high-spatial resolution ALMA data targeting \[C II\] emission in this field from the CRISTAL survey (cite: herr25), which are analysed in a parallel work ((cite: teli24))." (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data)
- Why this method: Match spatial resolution because the ALMA restoring beam is larger than the NIRSpec PSF  
  > "so a direct comparison is slightly ambiguous" (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data); "To better compare these data, we convolve the NIRSpec dataset with a custom Gaussian kernel to match the ALMA PSF (photutils function create_matching_kernel; (cite: brad22))" (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data)
- Alternative: Do not apply the published NIRSpec wiggle correction because wiggles are not significant above 1σ  
  > "These patterns, caused by under-sampling of the PSF, do not appear in our data at high significance (i.e., $>1\sigma$), so we do not apply the correction of (cite: pern23b)." (Observations and calibration)
- Alternative: Single-Gaussian lines are less suitable; validate two-Gaussian fits with a non-parametric approach  
  > "The approach of fitting two components to each line (i.e., narrow and broad) is more suitable than a single-Gaussian approach, but may still simplify the distribution and source of flux in the HZ10 field." (R2700 resolved analysis)
- Alternative: Use R2700 rather than R100 redshifts  
  > "Because the R2700 redshift is in better agreement with the ALMA-based redshift, we only use redshifts based on the R2700 data in this work." (Spectral fit results)
- Alternative: A dust-obscured disk centre could produce two eastern line peaks  
  > "We note that two distinct peaks could also arise in a disc with a more dust-obscured kinematic centre." (HZ10-E and HZ10-C); "While we find little evidence of high obscuration in the spatial region between the two emission peaks, this is still a possibility." (HZ10-E and HZ10-C)
- Alternative: Broad emission may trace tidal features or outflows rather than an extreme BLR  
  > "it is likely that the broad component in these spectra does not originate from an extreme BLR, and instead represents a tidal feature or outflow." (Testing for the presence of a BLR)
- Alternative: Do not analyse a full-field R2700 spectrum because of complex morphokinematics  
  > "Due to the complex morphokinematics of the field (e.g., see Section [3.3.1](#r2700spx)), we do not consider the full-field spectrum from the R2700 data cube." (R100 resolved analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: Agreement with non-parametric kinematics justified retaining the two-Gaussian model  
  > "Since the results of the two-Gaussian and non-parametric approaches agree, the two-Gaussian approach is sufficient to capture the asymmetric morpho-kinematics of this field." (R2700 resolved analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot distinguish a single disturbed eastern disk from a multiple merger  
  > "The current data are not yet sufficient to state whether the emission east of HZ10-W represents a single disturbed disk or a multiple merger." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Weak BLR or AGN contribution cannot be excluded  
  > "However, we note that a weak BLR may still be present, contributing to the broad emission." (Testing for the presence of a BLR); "there is no strong evidence for the presence of an AGN, although AGN contribution cannot be ruled out" (Line ratio analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Low-resolution coverage limits high-ionisation-line searches  
  > "However, we note that the spectral range required for the detection of most of these lines is only covered by the low spectral resolution R100 data." (Lack of high-ionisation lines); "Higher spectral resolution observations may reveal fainter and narrower lines" (Lack of high-ionisation lines)
- Limitation (authors): Electron-density uncertainties are large; narrow-emission densities and resolved density maps are unavailable  
  > "While the resulting uncertainties are large (i.e., up to $\sim1\,$dex)" (Line ratio analysis); "Since no significant narrow \[SII\]$\lambda 6716$ emission is detected from any component, we may not constrain the electron density of the source of the narrow emission." (Line ratio analysis); "Due to the faintness of \[SII\]$\lambda\lambda 6716{,}6731$, we are not able to present maps of $n_e$." (R2700 resolved analysis)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA and NIRSpec resolution mismatch makes direct comparison ambiguous  
  > "so a direct comparison is slightly ambiguous" (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data)
- Limitation (authors): [C II] can arise from multiple ISM phases  
  > "which enables \[C II\] emission from multiple phases of the ISM (i.e., warm and dense molecular gas, warm or cold neutral gas; e.g., (cite: lang10; pine13; gurm24))." (Comparison with ALMA [C II] Data)
- Limitation (authors): Integrated R100 SFR uncertainty is dominated by extinction uncertainty  
  > "Correcting for this dust attenuation results in a large $SFR_{\rm H\alpha}=380\pm200$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, where the large uncertainty is due to the large uncertainty in $E(B-V)$." (Integrated spectral analysis)
