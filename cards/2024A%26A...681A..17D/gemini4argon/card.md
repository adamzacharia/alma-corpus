## Qualitative findings

- CO(2-1) and SiO v=3 J=5-4 rotational line emission and Band 6 continuum are detected in 5 out of 15 targeted stars in RSGC1 (F01, F02, F03, F04, and F13).  
  > "For five RSGs (F01, F02, F03, F04, and F13), the CO(2-1) line emission was detected, with a spatial extent <∼1′′ (see App. C)." (2. Observations and data reduction); "For the five sources in RSGC1 detected in the continuum and in CO v=0 J=2-1 emission, bright SiO v=3 J=5-4 line emission (at 212.582 GHz) is present as well." (Appendix B: ALMA SiO and continuum images)
- CO-derived gas mass-loss rates for F01, F02, and F03 are systematically lower by a factor of ~2 than previous SED-derived values primarily because their measured terminal wind velocities (8-15 km/s) are lower than the 25 km/s assumed in SED studies.  
  > "The CO gas mass-loss rate values of those red supergiants in common with Beasor et al. (2020) are systematically lower, on average by a factor of ∼2 (see Table 1) — although we here must provide the caveat of dealing with small number statistics." (4.1. CO-based mass-loss relation for RSGs); "The main reason for the difference in M˙ SED and M˙ CO for the 3 RSGC1 sources analysed in both this study and by Beasor et al. (2020) is the expansion velocity which was assumed to be 25 ± 5 km s−1 by Beasor et al. (2020), but which is lower for all RSGs in which CO(2-1) was detected." (4.1. CO-based mass-loss relation for RSGs)
- F01, F02, F03, and F04 have mass-loss rates below the nuclear burning rate (core-He burning dominated evolution), whereas F13 has an order-of-magnitude higher mass-loss rate (42 x 10^-6 Msun/yr) where wind mass loss dominates evolution.  
  > "The evolution of the four sources with mass-loss rate below ∼4×10−6 M⊙/yr (F01, F02, F03, and F04) is dominated by nuclear burning, while in the case of F13 the wind mass-loss rate is currently determining its RSG evolution (see Fig. 2)." (3.1. Radiative transfer analysis of the CO(2-1) emission)
- Quiescent RSG mass loss following the new CO-calibrated prescription is insufficient to strip the H-rich envelope of a single 25 Msun star, meaning such stars will explode as RSGs (Type II SNe) rather than evolving to Wolf-Rayet stars.  
  > "A reduction of the RSG mass-loss rate implies that quiescent RSG mass loss is not enough to strip a single star’s hydrogen-rich envelope. Upon core collapse such single stars would explode as RSGs." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: RSGC1 is a coeval cluster containing 14 RSGs with uniform initial masses (~25 Msun), chemical composition, and distance (~6600 pc), and ALMA's high sensitivity and resolution enable direct measurement of CO(2-1) wind expansion velocities and gas mass-loss rates without uncertain dust-to-gas ratios.  
  > "Now, with the immense gain in sensitivity provided by the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), we can directly measure the expansion velocity and gas mass-loss rates of homogeneous samples of RSGs with well-constrained distance and stellar parameters." (1. Introduction); "The cluster RSGC1 at a distance, d, of ∼6 600 pc contains 14 RSGs and one post-RSG, all with initial masses ∼25 M⊙ (Davies et al. 2008; Beasor et al. 2020)." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Non-LTE radiative transfer modelling with GASTRoNOoM accounts for stellar, CMB, and dust radiative excitation and spatially resolved beam convolution; joint multivariate fitting with MPFIT and Monte Carlo avoids correlated intercept errors and combines Mdot_CO with cluster Mdot_SED data despite small sample size.  
  > "To retrieve the stellar wind parameters from the CO(2-1) line profiles, we used the non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (non-LTE) radiative transfer code GASTR O NO O M (Decin et al. 2006) based on a multi-level approximate Newton-Raphson (ANR) operator." (3.1. Radiative transfer analysis of the CO(2-1) emission); "While mathematically equivalent to directly fitting the intercept ai instead of their difference ∆ai to a reference intercept (here arbitrarily chosen to be that of RSGC1, but see App. F), the errors on a1, ∆a2, ∆a3, ∆a4 are now uncorrelated, which allows for a better sense of whether the intercepts from each cluster vary from one another from a direct comparison of the ∆ai values and their errors." (3.2. Comparison to SED retrievals); "The limited and biased sample led us decide to fit both the (Lbol, M˙ CO) and (Lbol, M˙ SED) measurements together with a four parameter set of equations:" (4.1. CO-based mass-loss relation for RSGs)
- Alternative: Imaging CO(2-1) cubes at 1.3 and 10 km/s spectral resolution (tested, did not reveal more detections or significant details)  
  > "We also made images at 1.3 and 10 km s−1 resolution but these do not reveal any more detections or significant details." (2. Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Alternative fit to F01 assuming northeast emission blob in 129.4-135.4 km/s channels is part of F01 wind with v_LSR ~ 127 km/s and v_inf ~ 16 km/s (underpredicts peak around 115-125 km/s by a factor of ~2)  
  > "A good prediction of the (flux-corrected) red wing and peak flux around 135 km s−1 (with peak flux then being around 5 mJy) is achieved for a mass-loss rate of 2.2×10−6 M⊙/yr and vLSR around 127 km s−1, but the peak around 115 – 125 km s−1 is then underpredicted by a factor of ∼2 (see dotted black line in upper left panel in Fig. 1)." (Appendix C: ALMA CO v=0 J=2-1 total intensity and channel maps)
- Alternative: Alternative fit to F03 with terminal wind velocity of 17 km/s and Mdot_CO = 3.8e-6 Msun/yr (predicts two-horn profile too wide)  
  > "A terminal wind velocity of 17 km s−1 and M˙ CO of 3.8×10−6 M⊙/yr gives a better prediction of the red wing for flux values below ∼4 mJy, but then the typical two-horn profile is predicted too wide." (Appendix C: ALMA CO v=0 J=2-1 total intensity and channel maps)
- Alternative: MCMC analysis for full radiative transfer uncertainty estimation (rejected because non-LTE radiative transfer calculations are too computationally intensive)  
  > "To calculate the combined effect of the errors in the individual input parameters on M˙ CO, one should perform a Monte Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) analysis. However, this is not feasible since non-LTE radiative tranfser calculations are too computer-intensive to be embedded in a MCMC algorithm." (Appendix D: Uncertainty estimate on CO(2-1) line profile predictions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Small number statistics and sample bias towards the brightest/highest mass-loss RSGs in RSGC1 (only 5 of 14 RSGs detected in CO(2-1); Band 9 CO(6-5) was not observed, leaving only a single CO transition that cannot constrain the radial temperature profile independently).  
  > "We requested observations at both band 9, centred on the CO v=0 J=6-5 rotational line transition and band 6, centred on the CO v=0 J=2-1 rotational line transition, but only the latter were obtained." (2. Observations and data reduction); "Since we only have one rotational CO line, we cannot put constraints on the radial distribution of the kinetic temperature for the individual sources." (3.1. Radiative transfer analysis of the CO(2-1) emission); "although we here must provide the caveat of dealing with small number statistics" (4.1 CO-based mass-loss relation for RSGs)
- Limitation (authors): ISM CO contamination affects the blue-shifted velocities of the line profiles, and total CO envelope sizes are sensitivity-limited lower limits.  
  > "As visible both in the CO(2-1) channel maps (Fig. C.3–Fig. C.7) and line profiles, there is contamination by interstellar medium (ISM) emission at specific frequencies; see the dashed regions in Fig. 1." (2. Observations and data reduction); "are sensitivity-limited and provide a lower limit" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Terminal wind velocities derived from line widths can be sensitivity-limited lower limits.  
  > "We note that terminal wind velocities determined solely from the half line width at zero intensity might be lower limits since it was shown by Decin et al. (2018) that line widths are sensitivity limited, and hence that in some cases higher signal-to-noise data could indicate higher v∞ values, and hence higher M˙ CO values." (3.1. Radiative transfer analysis of the CO(2-1) emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 4 stars (F01-F04) in a single cluster (RSGC1) have CO detections used in the regression fit (Eq. 8), and all 4 lie in a narrow luminosity range (log L/Lsun = 5.24-5.56) at the top of the mass-loss distribution while 10 other cluster members are non-detections.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Consequently, the luminosity slope (b = 3.50) and initial mass dependence (S = -1.68) of the new prescription are entirely driven by the literature dust-SED measurements from Beasor et al., with the ALMA CO data only providing a constant zero-point offset (Delta_R = 0.49 ± 0.43) that is barely a ~1.1-sigma shift.
