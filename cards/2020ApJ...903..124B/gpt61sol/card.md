## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble matched disk measurements**: Select disks with millimeter dust radii and high-quality Spitzer molecular spectra; exclude Herbig A/B disks  
  > "1) dust disk radii measurements from recent high-resolution surveys using ALMA or the SMA" (Sample selection and properties); "2) high-quality $Spitzer$ spectra covering mid-infrared molecular gas emission" (Sample selection and properties); "We exclude from this work disks around Herbig A/B stars" (Sample selection and properties)
   - parameter: 63 disks  
  > "The sample analyzed in this work includes 63 protoplanetary disks around pre-main-sequence stars" (Sample selection and properties)
   - parameter: Distances from (cite: bj18)  
  > "Distances are taken from (cite: bj18)." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Associate unresolved binary spectra with primary-star dust radii  
  > "The primary (A) components dominate the observed flux at millimeter as well as infrared wavelengths (cite: dsharp2; long19; naj13)" (The impact of wide binaries on the -R$_{\rm{dust}}$ relation); "the molecular spectra used in this work are associated to the dust disk radii of the primaries" (The impact of wide binaries on the -R$_{\rm{dust}}$ relation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare binary and single-star distributions  
  > "the two distributions overlap and in this sample single stars outnumber the binaries." (The impact of wide binaries on the -R$_{\rm{dust}}$ relation)
2. **Harmonize literature dust radii**: Use visibility-space radii enclosing 90% or 95% of continuum flux; convert published 68% radii to 90%  
  > "In this work we use their measurements of the radius R$_{\rm{dust}}$ that encircles 90% or 95% (depending on what reported in the original papers) of the observed millimeter continuum flux." (Dust disk radii); "we use the relation reported by (cite: hend20) to derive the radius that encircles 90% of the flux" (Dust disk radii)
   - parameter: 90% or 95% enclosed millimeter continuum flux  
  > "encircles 90% or 95% (depending on what reported in the original papers) of the observed millimeter continuum flux." (Dust disk radii)
   - fitting: Upstream analysis in interferometric visibility space  
  > "The analysis of disk images has been performed in the interferometric visibility space" (Dust disk radii)
   - validation: Published conversion validated over the angular resolutions used  
  > "(cite: hend20) demonstrated the applicability of this relation for the range of angular resolutions used in this work;" (Dust disk radii)
3. **Measure molecular line fluxes**: Sum three water features; subtract local continuum and scaled water slab before integrating carbon-bearing features  
  > "the water line fluxes used for the analysis are taken as the sum of three well-separated emission features at 17.12 $\mu$m, 17.22 $\mu$m, and 17.36 $\mu$m." (Gas emission line fluxes); "The carbon-bearing molecular line fluxes are measured from the residuals after subtraction of the continuum + water emission model" (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - parameter: Continuum anchors: 13.38–13.41, 14.26–14.28, 15.00–15.03 μm  
  > "We measure the local continuum with two linear fits anchored over the following wavelength ranges: 13.38–13.41 $\mu$m, 14.26–14.28 $\mu$m, and 15.00–15.03 $\mu$m" (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - parameter: Water-model scaling lines: 13.43 μm and 14.19–14.35 μm  
  > "using lines at 13.43 $\mu$m and 14.19–14.35 $\mu$m" (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - parameter: Detection threshold >3σ  
  > "consider lines detected only if above 3$\sigma$." (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - assumption: LTE slab; T = 600 K; N = 10^18 cm^-2; emitting area scales the spectrum  
  > "We take a model of a slab of gas in LTE (cite: banz12)" (Gas emission line fluxes); "the emitting area is just a scaling factor for the whole spectrum" (Gas emission line fluxes); "We take T = 600 K and N = $10^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - fitting: Two linear continuum fits; scale water-model peak-to-continuum strength in each spectrum  
  > "We measure the local continuum with two linear fits" (Gas emission line fluxes); "We then scale the peak-to-continuum strength of the model to match the observed water emission around the HCN line" (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - validation: Ratio correlations persist with or without water correction and with alternative continuum pixels  
  > "these correlations are robust regardless of whether the carbon-bearing molecular features are corrected for water contamination or not" (Water/carbon-bearing molecule flux ratios); "They are also robust against different choices of the pixels used to measure the local continuum" (Water/carbon-bearing molecule flux ratios)
4. **Construct luminosities and the infrared index**: Convert fluxes using L = 4πd²F; measure n13-30 from molecular-emission-avoiding continuum ranges  
  > "defined from the measured line fluxes $F$ as $L = 4 \pi d^2 F$, where $d$ is the distance" (Gas emission line fluxes); "The spectral index $n_{13-30}$ is measured as in (cite: banz19) from narrow spectral ranges that avoid molecular emission at 13.1 $\mu$m and 30.1 $\mu$m." (Sample selection and properties)
   - parameter: Line luminosities in solar luminosity  
  > "we use molecular line luminosities $L$ in units of solar luminosity" (Gas emission line fluxes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit molecular correlations**: Pearson and Spearman comparisons plus Bayesian linear regression including upper limits and errors in both variables  
  > "correlations are assessed with both the Pearson’s correlation test for linear relations and Spearman’s rank correlation test for monotonic relations" (Correlation analysis and results); "we adopt the Bayesian method for linear regression by (cite: kelly07) using the `linmix_err` code, which does account for upper limits and uncertainties on both the dependent and independent variables." (Correlation analysis and results)
   - software: linmix_err
   - parameter: Pearson/Spearman probability <5%; Bayesian |ρ̂| ≥0.4  
  > "We adopt the common cut of a probability smaller than 5% to consider a correlation detected in the data." (Correlation analysis and results); "In this work we adopt a lower limit of 0.4 in the absolute value of the correlation coefficient to consider a correlation detected in the data" (Correlation analysis and results)
   - assumption: Linear relation with intrinsic scatter: y = α + βx + ε  
  > "The linear relation is in the form $y = \alpha + \beta x + \epsilon$" (Correlation analysis and results); "This method is particularly suited for multi-dimensional problems, as it accounts for an intrinsic scatter in the linear relation due to physical properties that are not explicitly included in the variables" (Correlation analysis and results)
   - fitting: linmix_err posterior medians; intercept and slope uncertainties are posterior standard deviations  
  > "provides posterior distributions for the model parameters and for the correlation coefficient ($\hat \rho$), from which we measure median values to represent the best fit results." (Correlation analysis and results); "For $\alpha$ and $\beta$ we report the median and standard deviation of the posterior distributions." (Table 1)
   - validation: Compare Bayesian fits with Pearson and Spearman tests  
  > "Table [4](#tab: correlations_appendix) reports results from the Pearson and Spearman correlation tests for comparison to those using the method by (cite: kelly07) reported in Table [1](#tab: correlations)." (Additional correlation tests)
6. **Remove the accretion-luminosity dependence**: Compare stellar, total, and accretion luminosity correlations; divide molecular luminosities by Lacc^0.6  
  > "Here, we repeat the correlation analysis and determine that the strongest correlation is with the accretion luminosity" (Luminosity normalization of IR molecular spectra); "To do so, we divide the measured line luminosities by $L_{\rm{acc}}^{0.6}$." (Luminosity normalization of IR molecular spectra)
   - software: linmix_err
   - parameter: Common power law index ~0.6 ± 0.1  
  > "All molecular luminosities in Figure [5](#fig: corr_Lacc) scale with $L_{\rm{acc}}$ with a similar power law index of $\sim0.6 \pm 0.1$" (Luminosity normalization of IR molecular spectra)
   - assumption: Similar molecular response to excitation from accretion luminosity  
  > "suggesting that they respond similarly to excitation from the accretion luminosity." (Luminosity normalization of IR molecular spectra)
   - fitting: Use the Bayesian linear regression of step 5  
  > "We use this method for the linear regressions included in Table [1](#tab: correlations)." (Correlation analysis and results)
   - validation: Check removal of correlations with stellar and total luminosity  
  > "We find that this luminosity normalization provides an effective correction in removing any correlation with $L_{\star}$ and $L_{\rm{tot}}$ too" (Luminosity normalization of IR molecular spectra)
7. **Test normalized luminosity against dust radius**: Regress accretion-normalized molecular luminosities against Rdust and test false positives with two null simulations  
  > "The different behavior of the four molecules, where only water shows a detectable correlation" (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$); "we run a bootstrapping procedure to estimate a false-positive rate." (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$)
   - software: linmix_err
   - parameter: 10,000 realizations for each of two null tests; fixed measured x-values  
  > "In both cases, we keep the measured x-values and run the bootstrap simulation 10,000 times, re-fitting the data in each realization with the Bayesian regression method used above." (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$)
   - parameter: Shuffle measured y-values or draw unrelated y-values with the measured intrinsic scatter  
  > "in one case by randomly shuffling the measured y-values" (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$); "in another case by randomly drawing y-values modeled as having the same intrinsic scatter as measured in the data, but no relation with the x-values." (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$)
   - fitting: Refit each realization with the same Bayesian regression  
  > "re-fitting the data in each realization with the Bayesian regression method used above." (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$)
   - validation: ~1% false-positive rate for |ρ̂| ≥0.4 in both null tests  
  > "In both cases, we measure a false-positive rate of $\sim1$% of finding an absolute value of the correlation coefficient equal or larger than 0.4" (Normalized molecular luminosity and R$_{\rm{dust}}$)
8. **Test molecular depletion against the infrared index**: Correlate molecular luminosities with n13-30 before and after normalization; separate cavity and full disks  
  > "A persistent anti-correlation, this time in common to all four molecules, is found between the accretion-normalized line luminosity and the infrared index $n_{13-30}$" (Normalized molecular luminosity and $n_{13-30}$); "By considering separately disks with or without an inner cavity, we find that this anti-correlation remains for all molecules in disks with cavities" (Normalized molecular luminosity and $n_{13-30}$)
   - software: linmix_err
   - assumption: n13-30 traces cavity presence and size, with an inclination caveat  
  > "n$_{13-30}$ should overall be a good tracer of the presence and size of an inner disk dust cavity (with the caveat of highly-inclined disks)" (n$_{13-30}$ as a probe of inner disk cavity size)
   - fitting: Use the Bayesian linear regression of step 5  
  > "We use this method for the linear regressions included in Table [1](#tab: correlations)." (Correlation analysis and results)
   - validation: Anti-correlations also occur before normalization; absent in full disks considered alone  
  > "these anti-correlations are detected even before normalizing line luminosities by the accretion luminosity" (Normalized molecular luminosity and $n_{13-30}$); "No correlation is found in the full disks if considered by themselves." (Normalized molecular luminosity and $n_{13-30}$)
9. **Compare correlations with physical interpretations**: Compare cavity-index data with SED models and literature water columns and CO emission radii  
  > "We adopt the fiducial model from (cite: ball19), here explored over a grid of inner disk cavity radii up to 10 au and over a range of disk inclinations." (n$_{13-30}$ as a probe of inner disk cavity size); "Therefore, despite their simplicity and degeneracy, for reference we report here the slab-model estimates from (cite: salyk11a)." (Current estimates of warm water columns in inner disks); "The column of warm water vapor shows a trend with R$_{\rm{co}}$ too" (Current estimates of warm water columns in inner disks)
   - parameter: SED cavity-radius grid up to 10 au  
  > "here explored over a grid of inner disk cavity radii up to 10 au and over a range of disk inclinations." (n$_{13-30}$ as a probe of inner disk cavity size)
   - assumption: Rdust primarily set by pebble drift; normalized molecular luminosities reflect inner-disk columns or elemental budgets  
  > "current R$_{\rm{dust}}$ measurements reflect a disk radius that is primarily set by pebble drift at the age of these systems (1–3 Myr)" (Discussion); "the range of measured molecular luminosities (once corrected for the measured accretion luminosity) reflects a range of column densities and/or elemental mass budgets in inner disks." (Discussion)
   - assumption: SED models assume a fully-devoid cavity without sophisticated cavity-wall treatment  
  > "they assume a fully-devoid inner cavity without a sophisticated treatment of the region around the inner cavity wall" (n$_{13-30}$ as a probe of inner disk cavity size)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative consistency with literature column-density trends; direct tests deferred

(Summary and Conclusions)
