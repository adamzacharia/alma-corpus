## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image Stokes I, Q, U**: CASA reduction following ALMA procedures; separate imaging of North and South fields for polarisation  
  > "The analysis of the linear polarisation was conducted on each target field imaged separately" (3 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: polarisation analysis above S/N of 4; analysis within inner third of primary beam  
  > "we conducted the polarisation analysis above a signal-to-noise ratio of 4" (3 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: instrumental polarisation below 0.1 mJy/beam  
  > "any remaining instrumental polarisation signatures are smaller than 0.1 mJy beam$^{-1}$" (3 Observations and data reduction)
2. **Extract core properties**: 2D Gaussian fits with IMFIT to find positions, fluxes, sizes of cores  
  > "The positions, peak flux densities, integrated fluxes, and sizes of the cores are obtained from two-dimensional Gaussian fits performed with CASA task IMFIT." (4.1 Detection of the cores)
   - software: CASA IMFIT
   - fitting: single Gaussian and point-like sources for extended emission  
  > "We observe the presence of large amount of extended emission that we model using single Gaussian and point-like sources." (4.1 Detection of the cores)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Spectral index alpha and beta**: Compare Band 6 and Band 7 integrated fluxes over matched uv range (20-360 klambda) and beam; beta=(alpha+Delta alpha)-2 with Rayleigh-Jeans correction  
  > "We can use an estimate of the temperature of dust grains within the cores to calculate the spectral index of the dust opacity $$\begin{equation} \beta=(\alpha+\Delta\alpha)-2" (4.2 Spectral index of the dust emission)
   - parameter: Td 100 K (hot cores), 35 K (starless): Delta alpha 0.06 and 0.20  
  > "we obtain from Eq. ([\[eq:delta-alfa\]](#eq:delta-alfa)) $\Delta\alpha_1$= 0.06 and $\Delta\alpha_2$=0.20 in the two environments" (4.2 Spectral index of the dust emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive column densities and masses**: Grey-body flux with Planck function to get tau_d, dust mass with Ossenkopf & Henning kappa, gas-to-dust 100  
  > "We consider a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100 and thus we obtained the column densities from" (4.3 Column densities and masses of the cores)
   - parameter: n=1e6 cm-3 for MM4, MM7, MM8; n=1e5 cm-3 for MM1a, MM3a, MM9, MM6c, MM11a  
  > "we adopted an average number density $n=$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$ for the denser cores MM4, MM7, and MM8" (4.3 Column densities and masses of the cores)
   - assumption: distance 5.2 kpc; Td 100/35 K; Ossenkopf & Henning ice-mantle opacity  
  > "We derived the dust opacity index $\kappa_\nu$ from (cite: Ossenkopf1994) for grains with ice mantles" (4.3 Column densities and masses of the cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: errors on masses estimated as factor 5-10  
  > "the errors of the masses and column densities are difficult to quantify and they could be more than a factor of 5 and less than a factor of 10." (4.3 Column densities and masses of the cores)
5. **Polarisation maps and field morphology**: Combine Q and U into polarised intensity and angle; B-field assumed perpendicular to polarisation vectors; debiased Ip  
  > "We combined the U and Q data cubes to produce cubes of polarised intensity (I$_P$=$\sqrt{Q^2+U^2-\sigma_{P}^2}$) and polarisation angle" (3 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: polarisation dominated by magnetic alignment, not scattering  
  > "At our densities and at the given scales the polarised signal is dominated by magnetic field effects and not by scattering" (4.4 Detection of linear polarisation and magnetic field morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Field strength by DCF**: B = xi sqrt(4 pi rho) sigma_v/sigma_psi with xi=0.5, mu=2.8, sigma_v from CH3OH widths  
  > "We also assume a mean molecular weight of $2.8 m_H$ and we use an average number density $n=10^6$ cm$^{-3}$ for denser cores MM4 and MM8" (4.5.1 Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi method)
   - parameter: sigma_psi from circular-mean variance over selected pixels in POLI images  
  > "The estimates of $\sigma_{\psi}$ were performed using the “classical” variance, calculated around the circular mean" (4.5.1 Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi method)
   - assumption: MM3 velocity dispersion assumed equal to MM4  
  > "therefore we assumed the same values of velocity dispersion used for MM4." (4.5.1 Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: considered order-of-magnitude estimates  
  > "the magnetic field values obtained from this method should be considered as order-of-magnitude estimates" (4.5.1 Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi method)
7. **Field strength by structure function**: Fit S^2(l)=b^2+m^2 l^2+sigma_M^2 for l>0.15"; B_SF = sqrt(8 pi rho) sigma_v/b; Bt/B0 from b; turbulent-cell beam correction  
  > "By fitting $S^2(l)$, we can derive the intercept of the fit $b^2$, and its square root gives an alternative measure for the dispersion of polarisation angles" (4.5.2 Structure function of the polarisation orientation angles)
   - parameter: bin width 0.15" with Nyquist sampling; N~2 turbulent cells  
  > "In the plot, the data are binned with a width of 0.15$\ensuremath{''}$ and using Nyquist sampling." (4.5.2 Structure function of the polarisation orientation angles)
   - assumption: Delta'~40 mpc, delta~10 mpc, W~3.7 mpc  
  > "We assumed a $\Delta'\sim40$ mpc, which is approximately the size of our cores and a $\delta\sim10$ mpc" (4.5.2 Structure function of the polarisation orientation angles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: beam correction ~1.4 does not change Bt/B0  
  > "the beam effect correction results $\sim\sqrt{N}=1.4$, which does not produce a significant change in our $B_t/B_0$ ratio." (4.5.2 Structure function of the polarisation orientation angles)
8. **Energy ratio and mass-to-flux ratio**: sigma_A from B~11 mG, n=1e6; gamma~3(sigma_v/sigma_A)^2; lambda ~7.6e-24 N(H2)/B  
  > "We estimate the ratio of turbulent-to-magnetic energy as $\gamma \sim 3(\sigma_\nu/\sigma_A)^2$" (4.5.3 Turbulent-to-magnetic field energy ratio)
   - assumption: line-of-sight component equals plane-of-sky component  
  > "assuming that the component along the line of sight is the same on the plane of the sky" (4.5.3 Turbulent-to-magnetic field energy ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: lambda is an upper limit, possibly an order of magnitude smaller  
  > "$\lambda$ is strictly an upper limit and could be more than an order of magnitude smaller" (4.6 Mass to magnetic flux ratio)

## Qualitative findings

- Magnetic field is ordered, follows the filament and is perpendicular to outflows  
  > "the magnetic field is orientated along the filament and appears perpendicular to the direction of the outflows." (abstract)
- Cores with polarised emission are less fragmented  
  > "the cores showing polarised emission are less fragmented" (abstract)
- Linear polarisation detected in thermal line emission of MM4a  
  > "We report a detection of linear polarisation from thermal line emission, probably from methanol or carbon dioxide" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: SF method used to overcome DCF underestimation from broad angle distributions  
  > "A possible way to overcome the problem of the underestimate of $B_{\perp}^\mathrm{DCF}$ is by measuring the structure function (SF)" (4.5.2 Structure function of the polarisation orientation angles)
- Alternative: Polarisation from MM6 and MM11c excluded as beyond inner third of primary beam  
  > "we have excluded from our analysis the polarised emission coming from MM6 and MM11c, since they are located beyond the one-third of the primary beam." (4.4 Detection of linear polarisation and magnetic field morphology)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): DCF/SF values are order-of-magnitude; flux loss 30-70% from missing short spacings  
  > "we estimate a flux loss between 30-60%, respectively, on a scale larger than $\theta_{MRS}$." (3 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Mass and column density errors could be factors of 5-10  
  > "they could be more than a factor of 5 and less than a factor of 10." (4.3 Column densities and masses of the cores)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Field strengths depend on assumed densities (1e5-1e6) taken from L17 and a velocity dispersion from CH3OH that may not trace the dust
- Caveat (inferred, ours): MM3 sigma_v borrowed from MM4
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core-scale evolutionary conclusion rests on two cores (MM4, MM8)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beta uncertainties likely underestimated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line identity ambiguous; 90 degree rotation assumption for GK effect
