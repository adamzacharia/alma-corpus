## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate and image continuum and CS/C33S in all Stokes**: Calibration in CASA 5.4; manual line-free channel extraction, phase-only self-calibration, tclean per Stokes parameter in CASA 5.6; primary beam correction and pixel-by-pixel debiasing  
  > "For calibration we used CASA version 5.4 and version 5.6 for imaging (cite: CASA2022)." (OBSERVATIONS); "The data were primary beam corrected and debiased pixel-by-pixel following (cite: Wardle1974) and (cite: Hull2015)." (OBSERVATIONS)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Phase-only self-calibration final solution interval 90 s  
  > "which we later phase-only self-calibrated using a final solution interval of 90 seconds" (OBSERVATIONS)
   - parameter: Spectral cubes with 2 km/s channel width  
  > "The spectral cubes were produced by using 2 km s$^{-1}$ channel width." (OBSERVATIONS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared the 250 GHz continuum and field morphology with the lower-frequency data of Liu et al.  
  > "The dust continuum map at 250 GHz is also consistent with the lower frequency data obtained by (cite: Liu2023b)" (3.1 The Dust Continuum Emission)
2. **Check flux and polarization variability between the two sessions**: Image each session independently in all Stokes and compare max, mean and rms of the images  
  > "To this effect, we imaged each session independently in all Stokes." (3.1 The Dust Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: Values extracted considering 1/3 FWHM for max and mean; rms from an emission-free region  
  > "The values where extracted from the images considering the 1/3 FWHM for the maximum and the mean while we used a region devoid of emission for $\sigma$." (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stokes I difference compared with ~10% ALMA Band 6 flux accuracy; polarization floor 0.1%  
  > "below the ALMA accuracy of band 6 ($\sim$ 10%)" (3.1 The Dust Continuum Emission)
3. **Derive plane-of-sky field orientation from polarized dust emission**: Rotate linear polarization position angle by 90 deg assuming dust grain alignment by the magnetic field  
  > "which assumes a 90$^{\circ}$ rotation in the linear polarization position angle" (3.3 The Magnetic Field Morphology from Polarized Dust Emission)
   - assumption: Grain alignment by magnetic fields; radiative alignment and self-scattering considered less favored  
  > "magnetic alignment emerges as the most probable process driving the polarized emission detected towards NGC6334I" (3.3 The Magnetic Field Morphology from Polarized Dust Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Regridded to 0.67'' x 0.52'' and compared with 220 GHz field morphology; angle difference at MM1 centre about 35 deg, otherwise agreement  
  > "it is clear that the field morphologies are remarkably similar over most of the extent of the Stokes I emission" (Comparison with lower frequency polarization results)
4. **Build column density and mass maps from dust continuum**: Hildebrand-type optically thin dust formulation using the Liu et al. temperature model, mass per pixel = mu m_H N A  
  > "we use the standard formulation developed by (cite: Hildebrand1983) but we make use of the NGC6334I temperature model obtained by (cite: Liu2023b)" (3.2 Column Density and Mass)
   - software: Savitzky-Golay filter
   - parameter: Dust emissivity kappa = 1 cm2 g-1 at 230 GHz; gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "we used a dust emissivity $\kappa_{\nu} = 1$ cm$^{2}$ gr$^{-1}$ for the dust grains at 230 GHz (cite: Ossenkopf1994), and a gas to dust ratio of a 100" (3.2 Column Density and Mass)
   - parameter: Temperature map extended to the 3 sigma dust contour assuming 30 K outside the model, then smoothed with a Savitzky-Golay filter with a window of one beam  
  > "we assumed that the dust temperature converged to 30 K outside the original model map" (3.2 Column Density and Mass); "The map was later smoothed using a Savitzky-Golay smoothing filter (cite: SavitzkyGolay1964) with a window of one beam to ensure continuity" (3.2 Column Density and Mass)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission; gas and dust in thermal equilibrium; mean molecular weight 2.8  
  > "we assume that the gas emission is optically thin and that the gas and dust are in thermal equilibrium" (3.2 Column Density and Mass); "$\mu = 2.8$ is the mean molecular weight" (3.2 Column Density and Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Core column densities compared with Brogan et al. 2016 SED results  
  > "the column density values at the core positions in our map appear to be in agreement with (cite: Brogan2016A)" (3.2 Column Density and Mass)
5. **Convert column density to number density with a cylindrical geometry**: Number density n = N / Z with cylinder thickness Z set from the drop in dust intensity with distance from the peak  
  > "The number density per pixel is determined then by computing $\mathrm{n}_{i,j} = \mathrm{N}_{i,j} / Z$, where $Z$ is the thickness of the cylinder." (3.2.1 The Number Density Map)
   - parameter: Thickness about 5'' = 0.03 pc at 1300 pc, from a 5 sigma intensity drop  
  > "we find a drop in the intensity at the $5\sigma$ level in the dust emission which corresponds to $\sim 5^{\prime\prime}$ or 0.03 pc at a distance of 1300 pc" (3.2.1 The Number Density Map)
   - assumption: Same cylinder height throughout NGC6334I (acknowledged as not accurate)  
  > "this simple geometrical assumption assumes the same height throughout NGC6334I which is not accurate" (3.2.1 The Number Density Map)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Values compared with SED-based densities of Brogan et al. 2016 and the 1e7-1e10 cm-3 range  
  > "The values presented in our number density map align with those established by (cite: Brogan2016A)" (3.2.1 The Number Density Map)
6. **Fit CS and C33S line spectra for excitation, opacity and systemic velocity**: MADCUBA LTE radiative transfer fit of a central-region spectrum; Tex from CH3OH, 13CH3OH and C2H5OH transitions; C33S fit scaled with 32S/33S = 102; Gaussian fit to C33S gives systemic velocity  
  > "we modeled the data using the MADCUBA package which fits spectroscopy models to the data using a complete radiative transfer formalism under a Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) assumptions (cite: Martin2019)" (3.4.1 The CS(J=5-4) Emission)
   - software: MADCUBA
   - parameter: Tex = 260 +/- 20 K; CS optically thick (tau = 6); C33S optically thin (tau = 0.3)  
  > "assuming an excitation temperature $T_\mathrm{ex} = 260 \pm 20$ K" (3.4.1 The CS(J=5-4) Emission); "which suggests that the emission is optically thin (tau=0.3)" (3.4.2 The C33S(J=5-4) Emission)
   - assumption: LTE; 32S/33S = 102; CS profile heavily self-absorbed  
  > "since the CS profile is heavily affected by self-absorption" (3.4.1 The CS(J=5-4) Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: C33S line profile nearly Gaussian with small deviations, taken as little outflow contamination  
  > "This is because the C$^{33}$S line profile is almost completely Gaussian with very small deviations around the -20 km s$^{-1}$ velocity channel." (3.4.2 The C33S(J=5-4) Emission)
7. **Compute outflow mass, momentum and energy from CS line wings**: Optically thin CS column density per channel (Zhang et al. 2016), H2 mass via CS abundance, then P = M v and E = M v^2/2 summed over channels per lobe  
  > "The total mass, momentum, and energy is obtained by adding all velocity channels per lobe." (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
   - parameter: Line wings -40 to -12 km/s (blue) and -2 to 20 km/s (red); systemic velocity -7.6 km/s; 3 sigma threshold with sigma = 0.2 K  
  > "The line-wings of the CS line cover the interval from -40 to -12 km s$^{-1}$  for the blue lobe and -2 to 20 km s$^{-1}$  for the red lobe" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
   - parameter: Tex = 260 K, beam filling factor 1, X(CS) = 1.2e-7  
  > "and $f$ is the beam filling factor assumed to be 1" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
   - assumption: CS optically thin in the line wings; emission at systemic velocity and filtered emission missed, so values are lower bounds  
  > "we assume that this emission is optically thin in the channels covering the CS line-wings" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows); "the values derived here appear to be a lower bound for the outflow mass, momentum, and energy" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with single-dish McCutcheon et al. 2000 (order of magnitude lower) and with surveys of high-mass outflows  
  > "while our estimates are an order of magnitude lower" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
8. **Map the dispersion of the polarization position angle**: Standard deviation with circular statistics in a moving window, corrected in quadrature for the angle error  
  > "The dispersion in the magnetic field lines is obtained by taking the standard deviation over a moving window of $1^{\prime\prime}.5$ in size." (4.2 The Magnetic Field Strength Map)
   - parameter: Window 1.5'' (about 16 beams, Nyquist sampling), 5 deg angle error, s ~ 10 deg from coherent regions  
  > "Thus, we obtain $N=16$ independent points as the required number (beams)" (The Magnetic Field Dispersion Map)
   - assumption: Gaussian distribution of angles, justified through the central limit theorem  
  > "To define the size of the window, we assume that the angles follow a Gaussian distribution." (The Magnetic Field Dispersion Map)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with a 4'' window, which gives dispersions over 25 deg, beyond the range where DCF is applicable  
  > "Analysis of the DCF technique done by comparing with simulations, suggest that the applicability of DCF decreases with dispersion values over $25^{\circ}$ (cite: Crutcher2004)." (The Magnetic Field Dispersion Map)
9. **Estimate plane-of-sky magnetic field strength and total field with DCF**: Standard DCF with the 1/2 correction using the number density map, C33S moment-2 velocity dispersion and the angle dispersion map; total field adds mean OH Zeeman B_los as a vector sum  
  > "\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{pos}} = 9.3 \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{H_{2}}}}\Delta\mathrm{V}/\delta\phi." (4.2 The Magnetic Field Strength Map); "This allow us to estimate the total magnetic strength field by computing the vector sum" (4.2.1 A total magnetic field estimation)
   - parameter: Delta V from C33S moment-2 map (optically thin); n from the cylinder density map  
  > "We use the C$^{33}$S emission because is optically thin" (4.2 The Magnetic Field Strength Map)
   - assumption: Equipartition between turbulence and magnetic energy; no self-gravity or anisotropic turbulence; C33S outflow contamination assumed minimal  
  > "The DCF technique provides an estimate of the field strength onto the plane of the sky by assuming equipartition between turbulence and magnetic energy" (4.2.2 Caveats)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Simple error propagation with sigma_n = 5e7 cm-3, sigma_v = 2 km/s, sigma_dphi = 5 deg; comparison with Li et al. 2015, Palau et al. 2021 and Zeeman results  
  > "The error estimates that we obtained are $\sigma_{\mathrm{n}} = 5\times 10^{7}$ cm$^{-3}$ for the number density, $\sigma_{v} = 2$ km s$^{-1}$ for the velocity dispersion, and $\sigma_{\delta \phi} = 5^{\circ}$ for the position angle dispersion." (4.2.2 Caveats)
10. **Compute kinetic, gravitational, thermal, magnetic, H II and outflow energies and the energy balance**: Per-pixel energy maps from mass, C33S velocity dispersion (thermal part removed), pairwise gravitational sum, thermal energy from temperature model, magnetic energy from B_pos; balance = magnetic minus the sum of the others  
  > "The energy balance against the magnetic field is calculated by subtracting the turbulent, thermal (both gas and UC H II), and gravitational energies to the magnetic field energy" (4.3 Energy Balance)
   - parameter: MM3 H II region size 3.3'' x 2.5'' from Gaussian fit; Te = 1e4 K, ne = 3e6 cm-3; geometrical thickness as in density model  
  > "which gives us a region of $3^{\prime\prime}.3 \times 2^{\prime\prime}.5$ in size" (4.3 Energy Balance)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust (gravitational energy a lower bound if thick); ideal gas for thermal energy; MM7 and MM9 excluded for lack of C33S coverage  
  > "If the dust emission is optically thick towards the more massive cores, the estimated gravitational energy will be a lower bound." (4.3 Energy Balance); "We do not compute values for MM7 and MM9 because we do not have sufficient C$^{33}$S coverage over these cores" (4.3 Energy Balance)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
11. **Derive sonic and Alfven Mach number maps**: Sonic Mach number M_s = sqrt(3) c_s / Delta V from the temperature model and C33S dispersion; Alfven speed from DCF B_pos (equals dV/dphi) and M_A = Delta V / V_A  
  > "we estimate the sonic Mach number map using $\mathcal{M}_{s}=\sqrt{3}c_{\mathrm{s}}/\Delta V$" (4.4 Effect of the Field in the Dynamics of the Gas)
   - assumption: Using B_pos alone as the field estimate makes V_A independent of density and M_A ~ dphi under DCF  
  > "we note that the Alfven Mach number depends only on the dispersion of the magnetic field lines when assuming DCF" (4.4 Effect of the Field in the Dynamics of the Gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Zernickel 2015, Li et al. 2020, Liu et al. 2023, Pattle et al. 2023, Roshi 2007 and Liu 2020  
  > "These results agree well with the study by (cite: Pattle2023) who found that the turbulence is mostly trans-Alfvenic when statistics are compiled over many sources." (4.4 Effect of the Field in the Dynamics of the Gas)

## Qualitative findings

- The field is predominantly radial from NW to SE with disturbances across major cores and hints of spiral structures  
  > "displays a predominantly radial pattern from North-West to South-East with intricate disturbances across major cores, hinting at spiral structures" (abstract)
- The NE-SW red outflow lobe appears to carve a cavity in the dust, with the field following its edges  
  > "From the map, the NE-SW outflow red-lobe appears to be carving a cavity in the dust" (3.4.1 The CS(J=5-4) Emission)
- Magnetic energy is far smaller than gravitational and combined other energies; outflows and the H II region may drive turbulence  
  > "The gravitational energy is clearly the dominant factor in the energetics of the region" (4.3 Energy Balance); "suggesing that protostellar feedback maybe the dominant driver behind the injection of turbulence in NGC6334I at the scales sampled by our data" (abstract)
- Gas is mostly supersonic and trans-Alfvenic, becoming super-Alfvenic toward dense cores  
  > "The gas in NGC6334I predominantly exhibits supersonic and trans-Alfvenic conditions, transitioning towards a super-Alfvenic regime" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CS used for outflow energy instead of CO because CO is filtered by the interferometer and may be self-absorbed  
  > "This is because the CO emission shows, what appears to be, significant filtering by the interferometer and possibly extensive self-absorption" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
- Why this method: Used the standard DCF method with minimal assumptions to limit biases  
  > "we aimed to mitigate biases in this work by simplifying our analysis and minimizing assumptions" (4.2 The Magnetic Field Strength Map)
- Alternative: 12CO emission rejected for outflow analysis because significant emission is missing  
  > "Therefore, we omit using CO emission for the outflow analysis here." (3.4.3 The 12CO(J=2-1) Emission)
- Alternative: Spherical geometry rejected for the density model in favor of a cylinder  
  > "Opting for a cylindrical rather than spherical geometry could be more appropriate for several reasons." (3.2.1 The Number Density Map)
- Alternative: Dust self-scattering and radiative alignment considered but judged less favored  
  > "the scales and physical conditions in massive star-forming regions (like the radiation anisotropy and large grain sizes), make them less favored" (3.3 The Magnetic Field Morphology from Polarized Dust Emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Outflow parameters are lower bounds with large uncertainty because of filtered emission and abundance uncertainty  
  > "the outflow parameters derived here may have a large uncertainty" (4.1 The Energy in the Outflows)
- Limitation (authors): DCF caveats: equipartition assumption, no self-gravity, velocity dispersion may include outflow, infall and rotation  
  > "we might be overestimating the non-thermal motions due to turbulence which will bias the estimation of the field strength as well as the sonic Mach number" (4.2.2 Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Density and temperature models carry assumptions that are hard to quantify, including interferometric filtering  
  > "The estimation of the density model is also subjected to a number of assumptions which are almost impossible to quantify." (4.2.2 Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): MM1 not resolved and clear rotation signatures not conclusive; ALMA configuration limits spiral resolution  
  > "However, clear rotation signatures are not conclusive from our data." (3.3 The Magnetic Field Morphology from Polarized Dust Emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cylinder thickness chosen to match literature densities, so density and B_pos are partly model-tuned
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Temperature model from possibly optically thick methanol may bias column density and DCF field
- Caveat (inferred, ours): C33S moment-2 used for DeltaV mixes bulk motions with turbulence and is not available over all cores
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Window size (1.5'') and Nyquist argument set the angle dispersion directly and so B_pos and M_A
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Red-lobe velocity range inconsistent between text (-2 to 20 km/s) and Table 4 notes (-4 to 12 km/s)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absolute flux calibrator, imaging parameters and polarization-vector cuts are not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Zeeman B_los from OH masers is not co-spatial with the DCF field; vector sum is an average combination
