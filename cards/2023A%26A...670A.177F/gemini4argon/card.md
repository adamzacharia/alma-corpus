## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure laboratory rotational spectrum and build spectroscopic catalogue for CHD2CHO**: Record rotational transitions between 82.5 and 450 GHz in the laboratory, combine with archival microwave transitions, fit with an effective IAM/Watson A-set Hamiltonian using weighted least-squares, and compute a JPL-format line catalogue up to J=28  
  > "The rotational transitions were recorded in the 82.5–450 GHz frequency range using the broadband Chirped-Pulse Fourier Transform Spectrometre (CP-FTS) as well as the high-resolution absorption experiment" (Experimental); "In the final analysis, experimental frequencies were introduced in a least-squares fit procedure where they were given a weight equal to the inverse of their experimental uncertainty squared." (Line assignment and line analysis); "Transitions were calculated up to $J=28$ and their line strength and line intensity were computed using the dipole moment components in Table 1." (Spectroscopic catalogue)
   - software: Gaussian 16
   - parameter: Frequency range = 82.5–450 GHz, 853 transitions fitted, 40 spectroscopic constants varied  
  > "A line position analysis is carried out allowing us to reproduce 853 transition frequencies with a weighted root mean square standard deviation of 1.7, varying 40 spectroscopic constants." (abstract); "Transitions were measured in the millimetre and submillimetre range (82.5 – 450 GHz)" (Conclusions)
   - parameter: Catalogue up to J = 28, LOGSTR0 = -8, LOGSTR1 = -8, minimum calculated error = 10 kHz  
  > "Transitions were calculated up to $J=28$ and their line strength and line intensity were computed using the dipole moment components in Table 1." (Spectroscopic catalogue); "where $F$ is the frequency in MHz, and LOGSTR0 and LOGSTR1 are two dimensionless constants both set to $-8$." (Spectroscopic catalogue); "A minimum value of 10 kHz was selected for the calculated error (ERR)." (Spectroscopic catalogue)
   - assumption: E_d = 14.487 cm^-1 computed from ab initio B3LYP/6-31G(d) ratio r = E_d/E_d' = 0.9311 and CH2DCHO E_d' = 15.55866(4) cm^-1  
  > "A calculation at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level of theory with the Gaussian 16 package (cite: frisch:16) yielded $r=0.9311$. Since $E_{d}'$, first estimated by (cite: turner:81) and (cite: cox:03) and determined later with a higher accuracy by (cite: coudert:19), is $15.558\,66(4)$ cm$^{-1}$, we obtain $E_d= 14.487$ cm$^{-1}$." (Theory)
   - assumption: Zero energy set at Out configurations 0_00,+ level; rotational partition function degeneracy factor = (2J+1)  
  > "The partition functions $Q_{{\rm rot}}$, listed in Table 3, were computed for several temperatures using degeneracy factors equal to $(2J+1)$. A zero energy was taken for the Out configurations $0_{00},+$ level." (Spectroscopic catalogue)
   - fitting: Weighted least-squares fit with weights equal to inverse of experimental uncertainty squared  
  > "In the final analysis, experimental frequencies were introduced in a least-squares fit procedure where they were given a weight equal to the inverse of their experimental uncertainty squared." (Line assignment and line analysis)
   - validation: Root mean square observed-minus-calculated residual of 83 kHz (81 kHz In, 88 kHz Out) and unitless standard deviation of 1.7; calculated rotational constants within 300 MHz of experimental values  
  > "The root mean square value of the observed minus calculated frequency is 81 kHz for transitions within the In configuration, 88 kHz for transitions within the Out configurations, and 83 kHz for all transitions. The unitless standard deviation of this final analysis is 1.7." (Line assignment and line analysis); "are within 300 MHz from the experimental values in Table" (Line assignment and line analysis)

(Astrophysical observations); "We adopted a similar approach to previous works by fitting synthetic spectra for CHD$_{2}$CHO calculated under the assumption that its excitation is characterised by local thermodynamical equilibrium (LTE), which is reasonable at the densities of the warm gas where these species are present (cite: jorgensen:16)." (Astrophysical observations)
   - parameter: Line width (FWHM) = 1 km s^-1, velocity offset (v_LSR) = 2.6 km s^-1, T_rot = 125 K  
  > "For the line width and velocity offset relative to the local standard of rest we adopt values of 1 km s$^{-1}$ (FWHM) and of 2.6 km s$^{-1}$ respectively, which match the spectra well at this position. For $T_{\rm rot}$ we assumed a temperature of 125 K similar to that of the non-deuterated and singly-deuterated isotopologues of acetaldehyde." (Astrophysical observations)
   - parameter: 68 transitions predicted to be brighter than 40 mJy beam^-1 km s^-1 inspected over 329.1–362.9 GHz

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of doubly deuterated acetaldehyde (CHD2CHO) in the interstellar medium towards IRAS 16293-2422 B  
  > "Using this catalogue we were able to detect for the first time towards the low-mass protostellar system IRAS 16293-2422 utilizing data from the ALMA Protostellar Interferometric Line Survey." (abstract)
- The D2/D ratio (~20%) of acetaldehyde matches those of methyl formate, dimethylether, and methanol towards IRAS 16293-2422, pointing to a common formation environment (likely the pre-stellar core phase) with enhanced deuterium fractionation  
  > "The resulting D$_{2}$/D ratio of $\sim$ 20% is found to be coincident with D$_{2}$/D ratios derived for other complex organic molecules towards IRAS 16293-2422, pointing at a common formation environment with enhanced deuterium fractionation." (abstract); "The similar doubly to singly deuterated column density ratios for acetaldehyde, methyl formate, dimethylether and methanol presented in Section 3 suggest doubly deuterated acetaldehyde shares a common origin and was formed in an environment resembling the physical conditions with which doubly deuterated methyl formate, dimethylether and methanol were formed. The pre-stellar core phase is a good candidate" (Discussion of astronomical observational results)
- Doubly deuterated acetaldehyde is unlikely to be directly detectable towards pre-stellar cores due to low predicted 3 mm line brightness (0.5 mK)  
  > "Based on our predictions it will be unlikely to detect the doubly deuterated acetaldehyde towards pre-stellar cores, and this is possibly true also for other COMs." (Discussion of astronomical observational results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The ALMA PILS survey offers high sensitivity, high spatial resolution zooming into the warm gas around protostars, and relatively narrow lines towards IRAS 16293-2422 B, enabling unambiguous identification of deuterated COMs; the 0.5'' offset position limits line and continuum opacity.  
  > "The high spatial-resolution of the interferometre allowed zooming-in into the warm gas around protostars where these molecules appear." (Introduction); "The high sensitivity of the PILS data and relatively narrow lines towards one component of IRAS 16293–2422 have enabled the detections of a number of species for the first time in the ISM" (Astrophysical observations); "For our search we analysed the position offset by one beam (0.5$''$) from the B component of IRAS 16293–2422, where line and continuum opacity is limited." (Astrophysical observations)
- Why this method: High-barrier internal axis method (IAM) accounts for the internal rotation of an asymmetrical partially deuterated methyl group where symmetrical rotor models fail; LTE synthetic spectrum fitting is justified by the high gas densities in the warm protostellar region.  
  > "These models cannot be used for molecules displaying internal rotation of a partially deuterated or methyl group. Alternate models were designed for such molecules" (Spectroscopic investigation of); "We adopted a similar approach to previous works by fitting synthetic spectra for CHD$_{2}$CHO calculated under the assumption that its excitation is characterised by local thermodynamical equilibrium (LTE), which is reasonable at the densities of the warm gas where these species are present (cite: jorgensen:16)." (Astrophysical observations)
- Alternative: Symmetrical methyl group internal rotation models were rejected because they cannot be applied to molecules with a partially deuterated asymmetrical methyl group.  
  > "The efforts to characterise the internal rotation of symmetrical methyl groups is still under study (cite: ilyushin:20; kleiner:20; xu:21). These models cannot be used for molecules displaying internal rotation of a partially deuterated or methyl group." (Spectroscopic investigation of)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The energy difference E_d between the In and Out configurations and the parameters describing the 1 -> 3 tunneling path could not be retrieved from the spectroscopic fit because no couplings/transitions between the In and Out configurations were observed.  
  > "No unaccountably large residuals, which could have been attributed to couplings between the In and Out configurations, were found. As a result, unlike in the monodeuterated species , the value of $E_d$ and of the parameters describing the $1\rightarrow3$ tunneling parameter could not be retrieved." (Line assignment and line analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Large J and K_a a-type transitions show residuals larger than the 50 kHz experimental uncertainty, possibly due to unaccounted effects of the 1 -> 3 tunneling motion.  
  > "$a$-type lines characterised by large $J$- and $K_a$-value tend to display residuals larger than this value and this may be due to the unaccounted for effects of the 1 $\rightarrow$ 3 tunneling motion." (Line assignment and line analysis)
- Limitation (authors): A few lines in the astrophysical spectrum are under- or over-produced by the LTE model due to blending with brighter species or absorption from optically thick emission.  
  > "The few lines that are either under- or over-produced with the synthetic spectra are due to blends with brighter lines of more prominent species (e.g the two lines seen at 330.71 GHz with an upper energy level of 202 K that are blended with glycolaldehyde) or absorption due to optically thick emission (e.g. the transition at 347.86 GHz falling close to a transition of formic acid)." (Astrophysical observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The rotational temperature (T_rot = 125 K), line width (1 km s^-1), and velocity offset (2.6 km s^-1) were fixed rather than fitted simultaneously with column density, which could affect the derived column density if excitation temperature differs from the main/singly-deuterated isotopologues.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The energy difference E_d between the In and Out conformers was not experimentally determined and relies on a B3LYP/6-31G(d) scaling of the monodeuterated species, introducing a small systematic uncertainty in the relative population of the In and Out states.
