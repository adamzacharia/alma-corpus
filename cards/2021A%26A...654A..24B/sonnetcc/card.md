## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the CO(2-1) cubes and continuum**: Observatory calibration script in CASA 4.7.0; post-processing in CASA 5.4.0 with tclean (hogbom, Briggs), auto-multithresh masking of line cubes, uv-plane continuum subtraction, primary-beam correction  
  > "The data were calibrated using the CASA version 4.7.0 and the calibration script provided by the observatory, while further post-processing was done using the CASA version 5.4.0." (2.2 Data reduction and imaging); "In all cases, the *hogbom* deconvolution algorithm was applied using *briggs* weighting and a robustness value 2.0 to reconstruct the final datacubes" (2.2 Data reduction and imaging)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 2.0, hogbom deconvolver, limiting flux threshold optimised per case  
  > "In all cases, the *hogbom* deconvolution algorithm was applied using *briggs* weighting and a robustness value 2.0 to reconstruct the final datacubes" (2.2 Data reduction and imaging)
   - parameter: Line cubes with channel width ~10 and ~30 km/s and pixel size about 1/5 to 1/4 of the synthesised beam  
  > "The spectral datacubes for the emission lines were produced with a channel width of $\sim 10$ and $\sim 30\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$ and a pixel size of about 1/5$^{th}$ to 1/4$^{th}$ of the synthesised beam size" (2.2 Data reduction and imaging)
   - parameter: Continuum image built from all continuum channels of the four spectral windows, discarding CO(2-1) channels  
  > "Continuum maps were constructed using all the continuum channels in the four spectral windows, that is discarding the channels with CO(2-1) emission." (2.2 Data reduction and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Compute moment maps from the CO(2-1) cube**: First three moments (integrated intensity, mean velocity, velocity dispersion) computed numerically from the final cube  
  > "the first three moments corresponding to the integrated intensity map of the line, the average velocity field and the average velocity dispersion map were computed numerically." (2.2 Data reduction and imaging)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Per spaxel, only channels with S/N above ~5 times the median absolute deviation are used  
  > "for each spaxel only those channels with a signal-to-noise (S/N) above $\sim$5 the median absolute deviation were considered" (2.2 Data reduction and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit tilted-ring rotating disc models to the cubes**: 3DBAROLO fits a 3D tilted-ring model to the continuum-subtracted CO(2-1) cube; geometry (centre, INC, PA) estimated first, then rings fitted  
  > "This software automatically fits 3D tilted-ring models to emission-line data cubes." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc); "The model requires the geometrical parameters of the disc, namely the centre position coordinates, the inclination angle (INC) and the kinematic position angle (PA)." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - software: 3DBAROLO
   - parameter: NGC 4968: 27 rings, radial separation 0.12 arcsec, input velocity range (-340, 340) km/s  
  > "To construct the disc model for NGC 4968, we use 27 rings with a radial separation of 0.12 arcsec." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc); "reducing the velocity range of the input NGC 4968 datacube to $(-340, 340)\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$" (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - parameter: NGC 4845: 22 rings, radial separation 0.55 arcsec, input velocity range (-510, 240) km/s  
  > "The velocity range of the input NGC 4845 datacube is reduced to $(-510, 240)\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$ and we use 22 rings with a radial separation of 0.55 arcsec." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - parameter: A model with 10 rings and 0.217 arcsec separation is stated generically (galaxy not named); input cube channel width 10 km/s  
  > "The model uses 10 rings with a radial separation of 0.217 arcsec." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc); "We use the ALMA CO(2-1) line datacube with a channel widths of $10\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - parameter: Centre: 2D Gaussian fit for NGC 4845; source-finder flux-weighted centroid for NGC 4968 and MCG-06-30-15  
  > "for maps without a central emission peak (the case of NGC 4968 and MCG-06-30-15) the code uses a source-finder algorithm to identify the emission region, and then calculates the geometrical centroid, weighted by the flux intensity" (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - parameter: INC = 60 deg (NGC 4968, ellipse fit to outer ring in intensity map); INC = 73 deg (NGC 4845, velocity field); INC 65-68 deg estimated by 3DBAROLO (MCG-06-30-15)  
  > "The inclination angle INC turns out to be 60$^{o}$ (NGC 4968) by fitting an ellipse to the outer ring in the intensity map." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc); "For MCG-06-30-15, 3D-Barolo estimates that the INC angle varies from 65$^{o}$ to 68$^{o}$." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - parameter: PA 234-256 deg (NGC 4968), 62-81 deg (NGC 4845), 115-122 deg (MCG-06-30-15)  
  > "The PA varies from 234$^{o}$ to 256$^{o}$ (NGC 4968) and from 62$^{o}$ to 81 $^{o}$ (NGC 4845)." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc); "For MCG-06-30-15, 3D-Barolo estimates that the PA varies from 115$^{o}$ to 122$^{o}$." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - assumption: Emitting material at each radius in a geometrically thin disc with pure rotational kinematics  
  > "The model assumes that the emitting material at each radius is confined within a geometrically thin disc and its kinematics are dominated by pure rotational motion" (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare model and data position-velocity diagrams along the major and minor axes; test letting NGC 4845 inclination vary between 73 and 80 deg gave no improvement  
  > "the comparison of the $p-v$ diagrammes of the host galaxy disc (blue contours) with the fitted $^{3D}$BAROLO model of a rotating disc (red contours) along the kinematic major and minor axes" (7.1 Kinematical perturbations); "the results do not improve letting the inclination angle to vary between 73 and 80 degrees." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
4. **Derive 3DBAROLO residual cubes and maximum residual velocities**: Subtract the model cube from the continuum-subtracted observed cube and read residual velocities from the residual kinematic maps; maximum residual taken as the mean of the blueshifted and redshifted components  
  > "by subtracting the 3D datacube produced by the model from the observations (the input continuum-subtracted CO(2-1) emission line datacube)" (4.1 Residuals from 3DBAROLO); "Therefore, the maximum residual velocity is the mean of the two components as we read them from the residual datacube $\sim$ 125 $\rm{km\,s^{-1}}$." (4.1 Residuals from 3DBAROLO)
   - software: 3DBAROLO
   - assumption: Residual velocities much smaller than rotation velocity imply non-circular motions are unlikely to trace powerful outflows  
  > "indicating that the non circular kinematics very unlikely are tracing powerful outflows" (4.1 Residuals from 3DBAROLO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual size compared with the rotation velocity (~200 km/s in each galaxy)  
  > "For NGC4968, the gas rotates at $\sim 200\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$ and there is a residual of $\sim 125\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$" (4.1 Residuals from 3DBAROLO)
5. **Fit DiskFit axisymmetric and non-axisymmetric velocity-field models**: DiskFit fits a set of models (flat pure rotation, rotation with warp, rotation plus radial motion, lopsided, bisymmetric) to the velocity field and the models are compared via residual maps and chi-square  
  > "Different axisymmetric and non-axisymmetric DiskFit kinematic models, such as pure rotation (flat disc), pure rotation (flat disc with warp), rotation plus radial motion, lopsided, and bisymmetric models" (3.2 DiskFit modeling); "DiskFit fits an entire velocity field with a physically motivated model." (3.2 DiskFit modeling)
   - software: DiskFit
   - parameter: Model choice judged by chi-square reduction relative to the flat pure-rotation model  
  > "The $\chi^2$ reduces by $\sim$50% for the radial motion model and by $\sim$63% for bisymmetric model with respect to its value for pure rotation (flat disk) model" (4.2 Residuals from DiskFit)
   - assumption: Perturbations expressed as harmonics (m = 0 radial flow, m = 1 lopsided, m = 2 bisymmetric/bar) on circular orbits  
  > "Physically, $m$ = 1 harmonics correspond to "lopsided" perturbations and $m$ = 2 harmonics correspond to bisymmetric (i.e. bar) perturbations." (3.2 DiskFit modeling)
   - fitting: Chi-square based model comparison; sampler, priors and likelihood details not stated  
  > "In this case the change in $\chi^2$ is small when bisymmetric flows are included, reducing $\chi ^{2}$ by $\sim$30%." (4.2 Residuals from DiskFit)
   - validation: Residual maps of each DiskFit model compared with the observed kinematics; bisymmetric adopted as best fit for NGC 4968 and NGC 4845, pure rotation for MCG-06-30-15  
  > "Pure rotation (flat disk) DiskFit model reproduces the observation well for MCG-06-30-15, leaving only very small residuals" (4.2 Residuals from DiskFit)
6. **Derive CO line luminosity and molecular gas mass**: Fit one or two Gaussians to the integrated spectrum, integrate flux over the moment-0 map pixels with S/N>3, compute L'CO and M(H2) = alpha_CO L'CO, repeated for the modelled disc and the residuals  
  > "The line profiles are generally smooth enough to be well fitted with a single (NGC 4968) or two (NGC 4845 and MCG-06-30-15) Gaussian profiles." (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates); "This was chosen by selecting those pixels where the S/N is larger than 3, roughly corresponding to 10% of the maximum values." (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 0.8 adopted for the tabulated masses (range 0.8-3.2)  
  > "may vary in the range 0.8-3.2. For simplicity we use here the lower value which is the average value in active galaxies." (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates)
   - parameter: L'CO(2-1)/L'CO(1-0) = 2/3 from Kamenetzky et al. average distribution; NGC 4968 uses the ratio measured by Strong et al. 2004  
  > "The resulting ratio is $\frac{{\rm L^{\prime}_{CO(2-1)}}}{{\rm L^{\prime}_{CO(1-0)}}}=2/3$." (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates); "For NGC4968 we use the ratio measured by (cite: strong2004molecular)" (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates)
   - assumption: Flat LCDM with H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Omega = 0.3 and 0.7; distances 42, 18 and 37 Mpc  
  > "We adopt a flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $\Omega _{\lambda}$ = 0.7, $\Omega _{\lambda}$ = 0.3, and $H_{0}$ = $70\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$ Mpc$^{-1}$." (1 Introduction)
   - assumption: Conversion factor depends on metallicity and environment and entails the CO(1-0) line luminosity  
  > "strictly speaking the conversion factor entails the CO(1-0) line luminosity, this means that in absence of similar CO(1-0) observations an assumption on the excitation status of the molecular gas must be made." (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fits to the total CO(2-1) spectrum (software, sampler, priors not stated)  
  > "The line profiles are generally smooth enough to be well fitted with a single (NGC 4968) or two (NGC 4845 and MCG-06-30-15) Gaussian profiles." (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates)
   - validation: Total CO flux compared with single-dish values for NGC 4968 and MCG-06-30-15  
  > "because of the integrated CO flux agrees with that measured with single dish observations (cite: strong2004molecular; Rosario), at least in NGC4968 and MCG-06-30-15, the amount of missing flux should not be significant." (6 Gas and continuum distribution)
7. **Compare CO and continuum distributions**: Overlay the collapsed CO intensity map on the continuum map averaged over the four spectral windows excluding line channels  
  > "the overlay of the collapsed CO intensity map with the map of the continuum at the observing frequencies, derived from the flux averaged over all the four spectral windows once neglecting the channels with the line emission" (6 Gas and continuum distribution)
   - assumption: Continuum emission mainly from dust, with synchrotron contributing a few percent at 1 mm (spectral index -0.6 to -0.8)  
  > "The origin of the majority of the continuum emission is very likely due to dust." (6 Gas and continuum distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Compute gravity torques and resonances for NGC 4968**: Derive the potential from the deprojected HST H-band stellar distribution via the Poisson equation, compute forces and torques on the CO gas per pixel, azimuthally average to get the relative angular momentum change per rotation  
  > "The forces are computed at each pixel, by derivating the potential, and then the torques are computed on the gas, taking into account the gas density at the given pixel." (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968); "From the torque map we compute azimuthal averages and obtain the effective torque at each radius, per unit mass." (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968)
   - parameter: Deprojection with inclination 60 deg and PA 250 deg; HST and CO images resampled to 0.03 arcsec = 6 pc pixels  
  > "We then de-projected the galaxy disc with the adopted inclination angle of 60$^\circ$ and PA 250$^\circ$." (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968); "have been resampled to the same pixel size of 0.03 arcsec = 6 pc" (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968)
   - parameter: Thin disc with scale ratio h_z/h_r = 1/12  
  > "assuming a thin disc of scale ratio $h_z/h_r$ =1/12" (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968)
   - assumption: Constant mass-to-luminosity ratio calibrated to the observed rotation curve; bulge assumed spherical and not deprojected  
  > "We assumed a constant mass-to-luminosity ratio and calibrated it to retrieve the observed rotation curve" (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968); "we have first isolated the bulge, assumed to be spherical, which should not be de-projected." (7.2 Torque computation for NGC 4968)
   - assumption: Black hole mass 7e6 Msun; corotation placed just outside the bar

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Galaxies chosen to span different AGN type, AGN luminosity and morphology, as part of the TWIST sample  
  > "These galaxies are different in AGN type, AGN luminosity, and morphology." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Non-circular motions can be isolated by subtracting the regular rotation pattern from the observed kinematics  
  > "Some non-circular motions can be isolated from the rotating disc simply by subtracting the regular rotation pattern from the observed kinematics" (4.1 Residuals from 3DBAROLO)
- Alternative: Letting the NGC 4845 inclination vary between 73 and 80 degrees did not improve the 3DBAROLO fit  
  > "the results do not improve letting the inclination angle to vary between 73 and 80 degrees." (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
- Alternative: Alternative causes of deviations from circular motion (AGN or star-formation outflows, warps, radial flows) discussed against the bar interpretation  
  > "Deviations from circular motions could be caused by different mechanisms, such as outflows driven by AGN or star formation." (7.1 Kinematical perturbations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Some flux may be lost due to high resolution and the lack of compact-array data  
  > "One may argue that some flux could be lost because of the high resolution of these observations and the lack of additional compact array observations." (6 Gas and continuum distribution)
- Limitation (authors): The conversion factor alpha_CO is highly uncertain  
  > "is highly uncertain, it depends on metallicity and environment (cite: bol+13)" (5 Line luminosity and molecular gas mass estimates)
- Limitation (authors): The 3DBAROLO fits for NGC 4845 velocity and dispersion are not optimal  
  > "The Barolo fits for N4845 for the velocity and the velocity dispersion are not optimal" (3.1 3D modeling of the main rotating disc)
- Limitation (authors): No firm evidence for gas inflow/outflow in the NGC 4845 nucleus with the present data  
  > "but with the present data there is not firm evidence of it." (7.1 Kinematical perturbations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Maximum residual velocity of NGC 4968 is ~125 km/s in Sect. 4.1 but ~115 km/s in the abstract and conclusions; the two are not reconciled.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Channel width is ~10 and ~30 km/s in the text but 2.5 km/s in the observation log table; the cube used for each product is ambiguous.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Residual velocities are read by eye from maps and averaged between components, with no uncertainties.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 3DBAROLO and DiskFit fit settings, errors and degeneracies are not reported, so the bisymmetric-vs-bar and non-circular-velocity claims lack quantitative uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO = 0.8 and a 2/3 CO excitation ratio are assumed; the quoted mass ranges mostly reflect the alpha_CO choice rather than measurement error.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The bar pattern speed depends on the assumption that corotation lies just outside the bar and on a M/L calibrated to the rotation curve.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beam sizes up to 0.56 arcsec (NGC 4845) at ~18 Mpc limit the inner kinematics; the same text parts appear duplicated and the cosmology line prints Omega_Lambda twice.
