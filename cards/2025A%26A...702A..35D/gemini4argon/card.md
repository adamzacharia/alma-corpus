## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract spectra from offset positions**: Inspect spectral cubes line-by-line and extract spectra from positions offset from the continuum peak where lines show Gaussian profiles and avoid strong continuum absorption and severe line blending  
  > "To improve line identification in such cases, spectra were extracted from offset positions (as shown in Fig. [6](#fig:fig. A1) in the Appendix), where most of the lines exhibit Gaussian profiles and are relatively bright compared to other positions." (Molecular Line Identifications)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify molecular lines and model synthetic spectra**: Simulate observed emission in GILDAS under LTE, fit Gaussian line profiles, subtract overlapping species using best-fit LTE synthetic spectra for partially blended lines, and require >=5 unblended transitions with S/N >= 3sigma for secure detection  
  > "The spectral modeling was performed using GILDAS[^3] (Grenoble Image and Line Data Analysis Software) to simulate the observed emission, with five parameters: source size, line width, velocity offset, rotational temperature, and column density." (Molecular Line Identifications); "For partially blended transitions, multi-component Gaussian fitting was applied, and the contribution from overlapping species was estimated and subtracted using the best-fit synthetic spectra based on local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) modeling." (Molecular Line Identifications); "CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ was considered confidently detected in ten HMCs (see Table [\[table:table 2\]](#table:table 2)), each showing at least five unblended transitions with signal-to-noise ratios (S/N) $\geq$ 3$\sigma$ that match the modeled rest frequencies and intensities." (Molecular Line Identifications)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Five model parameters: source size (fixed to deconvolved continuum angular size), line width (from Gaussian fitting), velocity offset (calibrated via CH3OH at 218440.063 MHz), rotational temperature (free), and column density (free)  
  > "Of these, only the rotational temperature and column density were treated as free parameters." (Molecular Line Identifications); "Source sizes were based on deconvolved continuum angular sizes; line widths were derived via Gaussian fitting; velocity offsets were calibrated using the CH$_{3}$OH line at 218440.063 MHz." (Molecular Line Identifications)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and uniform source-filling emission  
  > "For partially blended transitions, multi-component Gaussian fitting was applied, and the contribution from overlapping species was estimated and subtracted using the best-fit synthetic spectra based on local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) modeling." (Molecular Line Identifications); "Additionally, the adopted assumption of uniform source-filling emission for CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ may lead to systematic uncertainties." (Chemical links between NH2CHO and CH3CONH2)
   - fitting: Multi-component Gaussian fitting for line widths and partially blended transitions; LTE synthetic spectrum simulation  
  > "For partially blended transitions, multi-component Gaussian fitting was applied, and the contribution from overlapping species was estimated and subtracted using the best-fit synthetic spectra based on local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) modeling." (Molecular Line Identifications)
   - validation: Require at least five unblended transitions with S/N >= 3sigma matching modeled rest frequencies and intensities  
  > "CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ was considered confidently detected in ten HMCs (see Table [\[table:table 2\]](#table:table 2)), each showing at least five unblended transitions with signal-to-noise ratios (S/N) $\geq$ 3$\sigma$ that match the modeled rest frequencies and intensities." (Molecular Line Identifications)
3. **Derive excitation temperatures, column densities, and upper limits**: Use rotational diagrams for molecules with at least three unblended transitions spanning a broad energy range; for species with fewer transitions or narrow energy range, fix T_ex to the other amide and calculate N from individual lines; scale NH2^13CHO column densities by the 12C/13C ratio to obtain NH2CHO column densities; derive 3sigma upper limits for the 42 non-detections  
  > "Column densities and excitation temperatures were derived using rotational diagrams for molecules with at least three unblended transitions spanning a broad energy range (Fig. [2](#fig:fig. 2))." (Molecular Parameter Calculations); "Due to optical thickness of NH$_{2}$CHO lines, their column densities were instead derived from the optically thin NH$_{2}$$^{13}$CHO isotopolog, scaled by the appropriate $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio for each source." (Molecular Parameter Calculations); "For the 42 sources where CH$_3$CONH$_2$ was not detected, 3$\sigma$ upper limits for its column density has been derived." (The upper limits of column density for CH3CONH2 in undetected sources.)
   - parameter: Source sizes theta_source from 0.9 to 3.8 arcsec (Table 2) and 12C/13C ratios from Table 1  
  > "| Source     | $\theta_{\rm source}$ | NH$_{2}$$^{13}$CHO                                                                            | NH$_{2}$CHO                                                                                                  | CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$                                                                                                                | Ratio$^{\mathrm{c}}$ |   |" (Table 2); "These ratios were calculated following the equation described by (cite: Yan23) and are tabulated in Column 8 of Table [\[table:table 1\]](#table:table 1)." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin emission for CH3CONH2 and NH2^13CHO, and equal excitation temperatures between CH3CONH2 and NH2^13CHO when one cannot be independently fitted  
  > "Column densities (N) were calculated under the assumptions of LTE and optically thin emission, following the standard rotational diagram formalism (cite: Li22):" (Molecular Parameter Calculations); "Column densities are derived by assuming the excitation temperatures for CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$. For CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ in I18089, the column density is derived by assuming fixed excitation temperatures of NH$_{2}$$^{13}$CHO" (Table 2)

  > "Column densities and excitation temperatures were derived using rotational diagrams for molecules with at least three unblended transitions spanning a broad energy range (Fig. [2](#fig:fig. 2))." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
   - validation: Compare NH2CHO/CH3CONH2 ratios in I17175 MM1/MM2 against independent literature measurements of NGC 6334I MM1/MM2  
  > "Despite this, the NH$_{2}$CHO/CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ abundance ratios agree well between the two studies (4.5 for MM1 and 3.0 for MM2 in our work vs. 12.3 for MM1 and 3.1 for MM2 in (cite: Lig20)), supporting the reliability of our detections." (Chemical links between NH2CHO and CH3CONH2)

## Qualitative findings

- Amide (NH2CHO and CH3CONH2) emissions are compact and generally coincide with 1.3 mm continuum peaks in all detected HMCs except I16164, where spatial offsets are observed due to an ultracompact HII region.  
  > "Overall, amide emissions appear compact and generally coincide with the continuum peaks across all sources, except for I16164, where spatial offsets are observed." (Spatial distribution)
- In the 42 non-detected sources, 3sigma column density upper limits for CH3CONH2 generally fall within an order of magnitude of the detected values, indicating sensitivity limitations may explain many non-detections.  
  > "The upper limits generally fall within an order of magnitude of the detected values, suggesting that sensitivity limitations may account for many of the non-detections, rather than a true absence of CH$_3$CONH$_2$." (The upper limits of column density for CH3CONH2 in undetected sources.)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 52 HMC candidates from the ALMA Band 6 QUARKS survey, which resolves substructures within 3 mm core clusters at ~1.3 arcsec resolution with four 1.875 GHz spectral windows at 1.2 km s^-1 velocity resolution, sufficient to resolve broad molecular lines and conduct the first systematic search for acetamide.  
  > "To address this gap, we present the first systematic search for acetamide across 52 hot molecular cores (HMCs) using Band 6 observations from the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)." (Introduction); "The uniform spectral resolution of 976.56 kHz corresponds to a velocity resolution ($\delta$$V$) of 1.2 km s$^{-1}$, sufficient to spectrally resolve broad molecular lines." (Observations)
- Why this method: Extracted spectra at offset positions to avoid continuum absorption and severe line blending at continuum peaks; used optically thin NH2^13CHO scaled by 12C/13C ratios because main NH2CHO lines are optically thick; normalized abundances to CH3OH instead of H2 due to uncertainties in dust opacity.  
  > "To improve line identification in such cases, spectra were extracted from offset positions (as shown in Fig. [6](#fig:fig. A1) in the Appendix), where most of the lines exhibit Gaussian profiles and are relatively bright compared to other positions." (Molecular Line Identifications); "Due to optical thickness of NH$_{2}$CHO lines, their column densities were instead derived from the optically thin NH$_{2}$$^{13}$CHO isotopolog, scaled by the appropriate $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio for each source." (Molecular Parameter Calculations); "Abundances relative to H$_{2}$ are not reported due to uncertainties in dust opacity." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
- Alternative: Gas-phase formation routes for CH3CONH2 (addition of CH2 radical to NH2CHO, or radiative association between NH2CHO and CH3+), rejected due to high energy barriers (>1000 K for CH2 spin-flip) or predicted abundances (~10^-15) far below observed levels.  
  > "However, theoretical studies by (cite: Qua07) indicate that this reaction requires a spin-flip transition of CH$_{2}$ from the triplet to singlet state, with an energy barrier exceeding 1000 K—rendering it unlikely under typical interstellar conditions." (Formation Pathways of CH3CONH2); "Other proposed gas-phase reactions, such as radiative association between NH$_{2}$CHO and CH$^{+}_{3}$ (cite: Qua07), predict CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ abundances ($\sim$10$^{-15}$) far below observed levels." (Formation Pathways of CH3CONH2)
- Alternative: Extracting spectra at the continuum peak, rejected in favor of offset positions because of severe line blending and strong NH2CHO absorption against bright continuum emission at the center.  
  > "A detailed line-by-line inspection revealed that most CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ transitions are blended with other species near the continuum peak, while NH$_{2}$CHO transitions suffer from significant absorption against bright continuum emission." (Molecular Line Identifications); "To improve line identification in such cases, spectra were extracted from offset positions (as shown in Fig. [6](#fig:fig. A1) in the Appendix), where most of the lines exhibit Gaussian profiles and are relatively bright compared to other positions." (Molecular Line Identifications)
- Alternative: Rotational diagram fitting for CH3CONH2 in I18089, rejected because the five detected transitions span a narrow upper-level energy range (117.7-136.6 K).  
  > "For CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ in I18089, the rotational diagram was not employed due to the limited energy range (117.7-136.6 K) of five detected transitions, which precludes a reliable fit." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
- How they decided it was enough: Required at least five unblended transitions with S/N >= 3sigma matching modeled rest frequencies and intensities for a confident detection of CH3CONH2, and at least three unblended transitions spanning a broad energy range to apply rotational diagram fitting.  
  > "CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ was considered confidently detected in ten HMCs (see Table [\[table:table 2\]](#table:table 2)), each showing at least five unblended transitions with signal-to-noise ratios (S/N) $\geq$ 3$\sigma$ that match the modeled rest frequencies and intensities." (Molecular Line Identifications); "Column densities and excitation temperatures were derived using rotational diagrams for molecules with at least three unblended transitions spanning a broad energy range (Fig. [2](#fig:fig. 2))." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in the adopted 12C/13C ratios from distance effects, beam size variations, excitation temperature mismatches, isotope-selective photodissociation, and chemical fractionation.  
  > "While this method effectively avoids opacity-related biases, several factors may introduce uncertainties into the observed $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratios." (Molecular Parameter Calculations); "As noted by (cite: Yan23), these include distance effects, beam size variations, excitation temperature mismatches, isotope-selective photodissociation, and chemical fractionation." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
- Limitation (authors): Abundances relative to H2 cannot be reported due to uncertainties in dust opacity.  
  > "Abundances relative to H$_{2}$ are not reported due to uncertainties in dust opacity." (Molecular Parameter Calculations)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in excitation temperatures, optical depth, line blending, spatial structure, LTE assumptions, use of 13C isotopologs for NH2CHO, and uniform source-filling assumption for CH3CONH2 may introduce systematic uncertainties in derived column densities and ratios.  
  > "While uncertainties in excitation temperatures significantly affect the derived column densities, other contributors include optical depth, line blending, spatial structure, and assumptions of LTE." (Chemical links between NH2CHO and CH3CONH2); "Additionally, the adopted assumption of uniform source-filling emission for CH$_{3}$CONH$_{2}$ may lead to systematic uncertainties." (Chemical links between NH2CHO and CH3CONH2)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The exact R.A./Dec. coordinates of the offset spectral extraction positions (shown only visually in Fig. A1) are not tabulated, yet column densities at hot core edges can vary strongly with small positional shifts (as seen in the comparison of I17175 MM1/MM2 with Ligterink et al. 2020).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CH3OH column densities from Qin et al. (2022) were used to normalize the amide column densities, but it is not explicitly stated whether the CH3OH column densities were re-extracted at the exact same offset positions used for the amides in this paper.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For 9 of the 10 sources, NH2^13CHO column densities were computed assuming the rotational temperature of CH3CONH2 rather than an independently measured NH2^13CHO temperature.
