1. **Measure protostar continuum positions and proper motion**: 2D fitting of the 1.2 mm dust continuum emission and positional comparison with 2016 0.9 mm continuum images from Harris et al. (2018)

(Continuum emission)
   - software: IRAM-GILDAS
   - fitting: 2D fitting  
  > "The J2000 coordinates of the A, B, and W protostars can be obtained from a 2D fitting:" (Continuum emission)
   - validation: Shift for B agrees with the shift required to align A1+A2 positions with peak A  
  > "This shift is also in agreement with that required to align the A1+A2 positions in the map of (cite: Harris2018) with the A peak in the present map." (Continuum emission)
2. **Extract molecular spectra, measure line fluxes, and create integrated intensity maps**: Extract spectra at the continuum positions of protostars A and B, resample/smooth in velocity, and integrate emission over defined velocity intervals; consider lines with S/N >= 5 detected and derive 3-sigma upper limits otherwise  
  > "Only lines with a signal-to-noise of at least 5 are considered detected." (Methanol and methyl formate emission); "Figure 3 reports the CH$_3$OH spectra extracted at the positions of both the A and B protostars." (Methanol and methyl formate emission); "CH$_3$OH: For source A, the velocity interval is –3,+7 km s$^{-1}$. For source B the methanol emission has been integrated on the –11,+13 km s$^{-1}$ interval for all the lines but the 18$_{\rm 3,15}$–18$_{\rm 2,16}$ A one, for which we adopted –16,+19 km s$^{-1}$." (Table 1)
   - software: IRAM-GILDAS
   - parameter: S/N >= 5 detection threshold; 3-sigma upper limits; velocity resolution 1.2 km/s (smoothed to 6 or 8 km/s for weak lines); integration intervals: A (-3, +7 km/s), B (-11, +13 km/s or -16, +19 km/s for CH3OH; -22, +32 km/s and -20, +29 km/s for HCOOCH3)  
  > "Only lines with a signal-to-noise of at least 5 are considered detected." (Methanol and methyl formate emission); "All the spectra have been resampled to a velocity reolution of 1.2 km s$^{-1}$, with the exception of the weak CH$_{\rm 3}$OH(18$_{\rm 3,15}$–18$_{\rm 2,16}$) A and HCOOCH$_{\rm 3}$(19$_{\rm 17,2}$–18$_{\rm 17,1}$) A emission, smoothed to 8 km s$^{-1}$ and 6 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Table 1); "HCOOCH$_3$: The emission has been integrated from –22 km s$^{-1}$ to +32 km s$^{-1}$, and in the –20,+29 km s$^{-1}$ range for 19$_{\rm 17,2}$–18$_{\rm 17,1}$ E, and 21$_{\rm 3,19}$–20$_{\rm 3,18}$ E, respectively." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Non-LTE LVG radiative transfer analysis of CH3OH in VLA1623 B**: Run grids of non-LTE LVG models for the 5 CH3OH transitions with J <= 15 (E_u = 45-190 K) and simultaneously fit CH3OH-A and CH3OH-E line intensities  
  > "For the computations, we use the 5 lines with $E_{\rm u}$ in the 45–190 K range, and assume an H$_2$ ortho-to-para ratio equal to 3." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis); "We then simultaneously fit the measured CH$_3$OH-A and CH$_3$OH-E line intensities via comparison with those simulated by the LVG model, leaving $N$(CH$_{3}$OH), $n_{\rm H_2}$, $T_{\rm kin}$, and the emitting size $\theta$ as free parameters." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - parameter: Grid ranges: T_kin = 50 to 200 K, n_H2 = 10^7 to 10^11 cm^-3, N(CH3OH) = 10^15 to 10^18 cm^-2  
  > "We run grids of models varying the kinetic temperature ($T_{\rm kin}$) from 50 to 200 K, the volume density ($n_{\rm H_2}$) from 10$^{7}$ to 10$^{11}$ cm$^{-3}$, and the methanol column density ($N_{\rm CH_3OH}$) from 10$^{15}$ to 10$^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - assumption: H2 ortho-to-para ratio equal to 3; CH3OH-H2 collisional coefficients from Rabli & Flower (2010); 10% calibration uncertainty propagated with spectral rms  
  > "For the computations, we use the 5 lines with $E_{\rm u}$ in the 45–190 K range, and assume an H$_2$ ortho-to-para ratio equal to 3." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis); "The errors on the observed line intensities have been obtained by propagating the spectral r.m.s. with the uncertainties due to calibration (10%)." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - fitting: Reduced chi-squared minimization over model grid (lowest chi^2_r ~ 2)  
  > "The lowest $\chi^{2}_r$ values ($\sim$2) are obtained for sizes between 0.11$''$ (14 au) and 0.34$''$ (45 au), in agreement with the observed unresolved spatial distributions." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - validation: Derived sizes agree with unresolved spatial distribution; line opacities < 0.1 and n_H2 >= 10^8 cm^-3 confirm LTE and optically thin conditions  
  > "The lowest $\chi^{2}_r$ values ($\sim$2) are obtained for sizes between 0.11$''$ (14 au) and 0.34$''$ (45 au), in agreement with the observed unresolved spatial distributions." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis); "The volume densities found for the LVG analysis of methanol are indeed larger than 10$^{8}$ cm$^{-3}$, supporting the fact that the LTE condition is satisfied." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
4. **LTE Rotational Diagram and line profile fitting for CH3OH, HCOOCH3, and other iCOMs**: Construct LTE rotational diagrams for CH3OH in sources A and B; fit line profiles using GILDAS-Weeds to derive column densities for HCOOCH3 and upper limits for CH3CHO, NH2CHO, and CH3OCH3  
  > "Assuming an LTE population and optically thin lines (supported by the LVG analysis), we also construct rotational diagrams (RDs)." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis); "More specifically, the total column density is obtained by fitting the line profiles using the GILDAS–Weeds tool (cite: Maret2011), finding $N_{\rm HCOOCH_3}$ = 8 $\times$ 10$^{14}$ cm$^{-2}$ and $\leq$ 2–3 $\times$ 10$^{14}$ cm$^{-2}$ (not corrected for filling factor effects) for sources B and A, respectively (see Table 3)." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - software: IRAM-GILDAS, GILDAS-Weeds
   - parameter: Filling factor for VLA1623 B ranges from 7 x 10^-2 to 0.41  
  > "If we take as representative the LVG analysis of methanol emission towards VLA1623 B, the filling factor ranges from 7 $\times$ 10$^{-2}$ to 0.41." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - assumption: LTE population and optically thin lines; for HCOOCH3 and upper limits, assume same line profiles as CH3OH and same T_rot derived from CH3OH LTE analysis (177 K for B, 50-135 K for A)  
  > "Assuming an LTE population and optically thin lines (supported by the LVG analysis), we also construct rotational diagrams (RDs)." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis); "In order to take into account the multiple transitions we assume (i) the same line profiles for all transitions, and (ii) the same rotational temperature obtained from the LTE analysis of methanol." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - fitting: Boltzmann rotational diagram fit (chi^2_r = 4 for source B) and Weeds synthetic spectrum fitting  
  > "In addition, the RD fit for source B ($\chi^{2}_r$ = 4) shows that even the very high-$E_{\rm u}$ (338–537 K) lines do not require additional excitation mechanisms different from collisions, such as an IR radiation field from the protostar." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
   - validation: LTE estimates for source B are in very good agreement with LVG results  
  > "These estimates are in very good agreement with the LVG results confirming that LTE and optically thin conditions are satisfied." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
5. **Kinematic analysis and estimation of Keplerian emitting radii**: Map red- and blue-shifted CH3OH emission, construct Position-Velocity (PV) diagram along the disk equatorial plane of VLA1623 B, and calculate Keplerian radii from velocity shifts, disk inclinations, and protostellar masses  
  > "Figure 8 shows the Position-Velocity (PV) diagram of the CH$_3$OH(4$_{\rm 2,3}$–3$_{\rm 1,2}$) E emission (representative of the line sample) for VLA1623 B." (Kinematics: on the origin of chemical enriched gas in VLA1623 A+B); "If we assume methanol is tracing an inclined Keplerian disk, the bulk of the emission would arise at a radius of 33 au." (Kinematics: on the origin of chemical enriched gas in VLA1623 A+B); "Assuming for A1 a mass of 0.1–0.2 $M_{\rm \ensuremath{\odot}}$, the disk inclination, and that the methanol emission is coming on average from $\pm$ 2 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the envelope velocity, we derive a radius of 12–24 au." ## Qualitative findings

- First detection and imaging of methanol (CH3OH) and methyl formate (HCOOCH3) towards the VLA1623-2417 system; CH3OH is detected towards A1 (up to E_u = 61 K) and B (up to E_u = 537 K), while HCOOCH3 is detected only towards B.  
  > "Here we report the first detection of methanol (CH$_3$OH) and methyl formate (HCOOCH$_3$) emission towards the triple protostellar system VLA1623–2417 A1+A2+B, obtained in the context of the ALMA Large Program FAUST." (Abstract); "Compact methanol emission is detected in lines from $E_{\rm u}$ = 45 K up to 61 K and 537 K towards components A1 and B, respectively." (Abstract)
- The methanol emission around both VLA1623 A1 and B shows velocity gradients along the NE-SW main disk axes, but rotating in opposite directions.  
  > "The methanol emission around both VLA1623–2417 A1 and B shows velocity gradients along the main axis of each disk. Although the axial geometry of the two disks is similar, the observed velocity gradients are reversed." (Abstract)
- Within the coeval binary VLA1623 A1+A2, methanol emission is shifted towards the west and associated only with A1, indicating apparent chemical differentiation or high sub-mm dust opacity towards A2.  
  > "even though unresolved, it is possible to note that the methanol emission is shifted with respect to the continuum peak toward the west, where A1 lies. In other words, out of the coeval A1+A2 binary, only A1 appears to be associated with a hot-corino." (Kinematics: on the origin of chemical enriched gas in VLA1623 A+B)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Large Program FAUST provides high sensitivity and ~50 au (Solar System scale) angular resolution in the southern hemisphere to image iCOMs in the disk/envelope systems of protostars.  
  > "The ALMA interferometer, with its unprecedented combination of high-sensitivity and high-angular resolution, allows for (sub-)mm wavelength mapping of protostellar systems at Solar System scales." (Abstract); "This is one of the goals of the ALMA Large Program (LP) FAUST (Fifty AU STudy of the chemistry in the disk/envelope system of Solar-like protostars), focused on astrochemistry of protostars imaged at the Solar System spatial scale." (Introduction)
- Why this method: A combined non-LTE LVG and LTE rotational diagram approach is used because CH3OH collisional coefficients are only available for J <= 15 (E_u <= 190 K), while observed transitions reach E_u = 537 K.  
  > "Given the lack of collisional coefficients for methanol at transitions with rotational number $J$ $\geq$ 16 (cite: Rabli2010), our approach to deriving physical proporties is twofold: (i) a non-LTE (Local Thermodinamic Equilibrium) Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) model, as described by (cite: Ceccarelli2003), for the sub-sample of CH$_3$OH transitions associated with collisional rates ($J$ $\leq$ 15), and (ii) an LTE model hypothesis using all observed CH$_3$OH lines." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
- Alternative: Infrared radiative pumping from the protostar was considered for exciting the high-E_u (338-537 K) CH3OH lines in VLA1623 B, but rejected as unnecessary because the single-temperature LTE rotational diagram fit shows collisions alone suffice.  
  > "In addition, the RD fit for source B ($\chi^{2}_r$ = 4) shows that even the very high-$E_{\rm u}$ (338–537 K) lines do not require additional excitation mechanisms different from collisions, such as an IR radiation field from the protostar." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
- Alternative: Both thermal sublimation in the inner envelope (classical hot corino / protostellar illumination of flared disk surface layers) and accretion shocks near the centrifugal barrier remain viable alternatives for the origin of iCOMs in VLA1623 B.  
  > "As with HH212, two scenarios are possible for VLA1623 B: (i) iCOMs delimitate the accretion shock, similarly to what proposed for L1527, or (ii) they arise from portions of the flared disk illuminated directly by the protostar." (Complex organics around VLA1623-2417 B)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Collisional coefficients for CH3OH are lacking for transitions with J >= 16.  
  > "Given the lack of collisional coefficients for methanol at transitions with rotational number $J$ $\geq$ 16 (cite: Rabli2010), our approach to deriving physical proporties is twofold:" (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
- Limitation (authors): LVG analysis is challenged by the limited number of lines with collisional rates (5 lines, with 4 in the narrow E_u = 45-61 K range).  
  > "The limited number of methanol lines and the fact that 4 lines out of 5 are in the narrow $E_{\rm u}$ = 45–61 K range make this analysis challenging." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
- Limitation (authors): For source A, the E_u range of the 4 detected CH3OH lines (45-61 K) is too narrow for a free rotational diagram fit, only allowing an upper limit on T_rot.  
  > "For source A, the $E_{\rm u}$ range sampled by the 4 detected lines is too small to obtain a proper free fit." (Physical properties: LTE and LVG analysis)
- Limitation (authors): The ~0.4'' (50 au) angular resolution cannot disentangle the binary components A1 and A2 in the continuum and hampers detailed kinematic modeling of the spatially unresolved iCOM emission.  
  > "VLA1623 A is well detected, without disentangling the binary components A1 and A2 at the 0.4$''$ (52 au) angular resolution." (Continuum emission); "A detailed modelling is hampered by the spatial resolution (the beam size across the disk axis is 0.41$''$, 53 au) of the map." (Kinematics: on the origin of chemical enriched gas in VLA1623 A+B)
- Limitation (authors): Sub-mm dust opacity may hide iCOM emission around A2 and in the optically thick disk midplane of B.  
  > "Observations at cm-wavelengths are required to verify whether the detection of iCOMs emission towards A2 is prevented by dust opacity." (Summary and conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The non-LTE LVG model relies on a private/in-house code (Ceccarelli et al. 2003) that is not publicly distributed, though public LVG codes (e.g., RADEX) could approximate the check.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The two transitions CH3OH 4(2,3)-5(1,4) A and 5(4,2)-6(3,3) E are blended in VLA1623 B because of the broad double-peaked line profiles, introducing systematic uncertainty in their individual integrated fluxes.
