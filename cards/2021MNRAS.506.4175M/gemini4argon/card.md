## Analysis recipe

1. **Analyze 6.7 GHz methanol maser variability, velocity drifts, and periodicity**: Extract integrated flux density and peak velocity time series for five maser features from HartRAO single-dish monitoring; fit velocity drifts with linear regression; fit exponential decay to the +11.45 km s^-1 feature; determine periodicity using Period04 and Lomb-Scargle periodogram; compute relative amplitude variations.  
  > "Linear regression analysis is applied to each velocity time series to determine the velocity drift, and the results are included in this table." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability); "The period of each is determined using, firstly the programme Period04 developed by (cite: lenz2004period04) and secondly the Lomb-Scargle (LS) periodigram (cite: scargle82)." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability); "Finally, for $v_{LSR} =$+11.45 km s$^{-1}$, it is possible to fit an exponentially decaying function to the flux density time series, see Fig. 4." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability)
   - software: Period04
   - parameter: Five velocity features: +8.42, +9.65, +10.44, +10.70, and +11.45 km s^-1  
  > "For the purpose of analysis of these observations five features, two at $v_{LSR} =$+10.44 and +10.70 km s$^{-1}$ in the heavily line merged velocity regime, and others at $v_{LSR} =$+8.42, +9.65, and +11.45 km s$^{-1}$ are selected." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability)
   - fitting: Linear regression on velocity time series (goodness of fit R^2 > 50% for measurable drift) and exponential decay fit F_Int_6.7 = a * e^(MJD/b) + c  
  > "Two of the features have measurable velocity drifts, with "goodness of fit" values R$^{2} >$ 50$\%$." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability); "Finally, for $v_{LSR} =$+11.45 km s$^{-1}$, it is possible to fit an exponentially decaying function to the flux density time series, see Fig. 4." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability)
   - validation: Comparison of Period04 and Lomb-Scargle periods (agree within 3 sigma)

## Qualitative findings

- G188.95+0.89 is resolved into eight 1.3 mm dust continuum cores (MM1-MM8), with MM1 being the brightest and associated with the periodic 6.7 GHz CH3OH maser source.  
  > "G188.95$+$0.89 protocluster is resolved into eight 1.3 mm objects (MM1-MM8). MM1 and MM2 are the central objects and MM1 (the brightest dust continuum object) is associated with the periodic 6.7 GHz CH$_3$OH maser source." (Conclusion)
- All five selected 6.7 GHz CH3OH maser features are periodic with no detectable phase lags, while the +11.45 km s^-1 feature decays exponentially from 2003 and two features (+9.65 and +11.45 km s^-1) show measurable velocity drifts.  
  > "In these observations five features are periodically varying and at least two exhibit evidence of velocity drifts." (Abstract); "The 11.45 km s$^{-1}$ feature has decreased exponentially from 2003." (Abstract); "No phase lags between maser features were found." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability)
- MM2 likely hosts at least a binary YSO system, evidenced by two continuum cores separated by 0.1 arcsec in ALMA Band 7, double-peaked CH3OH emission, and multiple outflow orientations in 12CO.  
  > "First, the high-resolution ALMA band 7 dust continuum image resolves MM2 into 2 cores separated by 0.1$"$." (Binary System in G188.95+0.89-MM2); "An east-west outflow feature with an axis through the peak position of MM2, as well as a north-west and south-east outflow also intersecting with MM2, can be observed.   > "We noted that missing flux issue can give rise to such spectral feature but in this case the observed absorption feature is real.." (Binary System in G188.95+0.89-MM2)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Phase lags between maser features shorter than the 10-20 day observing cadence cannot be measured.  
  > "There may be lags shorter than our cadence that we cannot measure nor speculate about." (6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers Variability)
- Limitation (authors): Cores MM5-MM8 lie close to the edge of the primary beam, so their continuum fluxes are not primary-beam corrected and their line emission is affected by decreased sensitivity.  
  > "The integrated flux densities for MM5 to MM8, that were also used in the core mass estimates, are not primary beam corrected since MM5–MM8 are located close to the edge of the primary beam and will not be discussed in detail due to limited sensitivity." (G188.95+0.89 Continuum Emission); "The nature of the emission towards MM5–MM8 is contaminated by the effect of the decreased sensitivity towards the edge of the primary beam." (G188.95+0.89 Millimeter Line Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Only two maser features show measurable velocity drifts, which is insufficient to make a definitive comparison with disk infall models such as in G9.62+0.20E.  
  > "However, an insufficient number of features with measurable velocity drift are found thus rendering any comparison to G9.62+0.20E indeterminate." (Implications of the variability in the 6.7 GHz CH3OH Masers)
- Limitation (authors): No direct detection of accretion disks was possible with the current ALMA observations.  
  > "While no direct detection of accretion disks was possible with the current observations, the detected outflows suggest the presence of accretion disks in the source." (Conclusion)
