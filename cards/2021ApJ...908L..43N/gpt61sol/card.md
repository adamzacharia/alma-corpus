## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the CO observations**: Image the 12 m CO data with tclean, Briggs weighting and automatic masks; deconvolve to ∼1σ residual noise.  
  > "The weighting scheme of the `tclean` was $``$Briggs$"$ with a robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations/Data); "The `auto-multithresh` procedure (cite: Kepley20) automatically selected the emission mask in the dirty and residual images in `tclean`." (Observations/Data); "We continued the deconvolution process until the intensity of the residual image attains the $\sim$1$\sigma$ noise level." (Observations/Data)
   - software: CASA, tclean, auto-multithresh
   - parameter: CASA 5.4.1  
  > "We performed the imaging process using the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA) package (cite: McMullin07) version 5.4.1." (Observations/Data)
   - parameter: Velocity resolution 0.2 km s−1  
  > "at a velocity resolution of 0.2 km s$^{-1}$" (Observations/Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual intensity reaches ∼1σ noise  
  > "We continued the deconvolution process until the intensity of the residual image attains the $\sim$1$\sigma$ noise level." (Observations/Data)
2. **Check missing flux**: Image ACA data with the same flow; match the 12 m resolution to the 7 m data and form an integrated-intensity ratio map.  
  > "We applied the same flow described in the previous paragraph to the data reduction (imaging) process." (Observations/Data); "We converted the 12 m array data to be the same resolution as the 7 m array data, and then produced the integrated intensity ratio map in the pixels at more than 3$\sigma$ detection." (Observations/Data)
   - software: CASA, tclean, auto-multithresh
   - parameter: Pixels at more than 3σ detection  
  > "then produced the integrated intensity ratio map in the pixels at more than 3$\sigma$ detection." (Observations/Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux ratio almost 1; retain 12 m array alone  
  > "Since the flux ratio between the two data set is almost 1, we conclude that the 12 m array data does not suffer from the significant missing flux." (Observations/Data); "We used the 12 m array alone data throughout this manuscript." (Observations/Data)
3. **Separate velocity components**: Inspect channel maps, intensity-weighted first-moment map and component integrated-intensity contours; examine the PV diagram at the A–B intersection.  
  > "a) Velocity distribution of molecular gas in channel maps: we find multiple velocity components in channel maps." (Discussion); "shows the first-moment intensity weighted velocity (moment 1) map of $^{12}$CO(1–0) emission in N66N." (Velocity structures); "The position-velocity (PV) diagram at the intersection of filaments A and B shows a V-shape gas distribution" (Velocity structures)
   - parameter: A: 154.4–158.6 km s−1; B: 158.0–161.8 km s−1  
  > "Filament A shows a velocity range 154.4–158.6 km s$^{-1}$ and filament B shows a velocity range 158.0–161.8 km s$^{-1}$." (Velocity structures)
   - parameter: C: 161–165.0 km s−1  
  > "We find a third velocity component (Filament C) in a velocity range 161–165.0 km s$^{-1}$." (Velocity structures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Three diagnostics following Fukui18a; V-shape at the filament intersection indicates a physical connection.  
  > "To find proper evidence for a cloud–cloud collision, we use three methods discussed by (cite: Fukui18a)." (Discussion); "c) Position-velocity diagram: we find a V-shape gas distribution at the intersection of filaments A and B, indicating their physical interaction." (Discussion)
4. **Measure filament properties**: Identify filaments as dendrogram trunks in a three-dimensional cube; use trunk axes, velocity dispersion and CO flux to derive sizes, widths, column densities and masses.  
  > "We identify the filaments as the parent structures that are the trunks in the dendrogram." ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments); "We obtain the length and width of the filaments, A and B, from the respective major and minor axes of the trunks ($\sigma_x$, $\sigma_y$)." ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments); "**Astrodendro** characterizes the molecular gas as a structure tree with leaves, branches, and trunks in a three-dimensional data cube." ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments)
   - software: astrodendro
   - assumption: Gaussian velocity distribution; ΔV = 2√(2ln2) σv  
  > "We derive the apparent velocity width of each filament using the velocity dispersion, $\sigma_v$, assuming a Gaussian distribution ($\Delta V$=$2\sqrt{2ln2}\sigma_v$)." ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments)
   - assumption: CO-to-H2 conversion factor 7.5 × 10²⁰ cm−2 (K km s−1)−1  
  > "Assuming a CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor 7.5$\times$10$^{20}$cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ for the SMC (cite: Muraoka17)" ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Identify cores and derive virial masses**: Identify dendrogram leaves as molecular cores and determine virial masses following Naslim18 and nayana20.  
  > "We use **astrodendro** to identify the molecular cores and determine their virial masses, following the same method as in (cite: Naslim18; nayana20)." (CO cores in filaments and associated Young stellar population); "We identify the molecular cores as the dendrogram leaves, which are the brightest and smallest structures representing the top level of the dendrogram." (CO cores in filaments and associated Young stellar population)
   - software: astrodendro
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Associate gas with young stars**: Compare literature YSO and OB-star positions with CO cores; classify YSO 548 using its literature 8.0 μm magnitude and Chen09 selection criterion.  
  > "To investigate the ongoing star formation, we compare the positions of YSOs (cite: Sewilo13), and OB stars (cite: Dufton19) with the CO cores in filaments." (Discussion); "(cite: Sewilo13) provide an 8.0$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ magnitude of \[10.69\] for YSO 548" (Discussion); "indicating an intermediate-mass (mass range 5-10 M$_{\odot}$) object according to the selection criterion suggested by (cite: Chen09)." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Estimate collision timescale**: Use relative filament displacement and velocity, corrected for an assumed line-of-sight angle, to estimate collision timescale.  
  > "We estimate the timescale of cloud collision in N66N using the relative displacement and velocity of two interacting filaments." (Discussion); "We estimate the collision time scale to be $\sim$0.2 Myr." (Discussion)
   - parameter: Projected displacement 0.8 pc; relative velocity 3.6 km s−1  
  > "The displacement between two filaments on their relative location at the point of intersection is estimated to be 0.8 pc with a relative velocity of 3.6 km s$^{-1}$." (Discussion)
   - assumption: Relative motion at 45° to the line of sight  
  > "If we assume the relative motion of colliding clouds has an angle of 45$^{\circ}$ to the line of sight" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Assess the triggering interpretation**: Compare filament kinematics and stellar associations with MHD simulations and other collision candidates; compare the collision timescale with PMS ages and inspect the SNR expansion alternative.  
  > "Comparing our findings with the results of MHD simulations (cite: inoue18), molecular line observations in N159W-South and N159E of the LMC (cite: Fukui2019) and the NGC 604 of M33 (cite: Muraoka20)" (Conclusion); "We expect this low-mass PMS cluster has been formed prior to the cloud–cloud collision as it is found to be slightly older than the estimated collision timescale $\sim$0.2 Myr." (Discussion); "we do not find a continuous velocity gradient with an expanding shell of molecular gas that is blown from SNR B0057-724 in the direction of filament intersection." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative consistency with collision signatures reported in LMC and M33 clouds  
  > "These observational signatures are consistent with the cloud–cloud collision reported in N159W-South, N159E of the LMC, and NGC 604 of M33 (cite: Tokuda19; Fukui2019; Muraoka20)." (Discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- Filaments A and B are elongated structures; component C constitutes hub-filaments.  
  > "Two well-ordered filaments (A and B) appear as elongated structures extending from the dense clumps toward the north-east of N66" ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments); "A third velocity component in a velocity range 161–165.0 km s$^{-1}$ constitutes hub-filaments." (Abstract)
- A V-shape PV distribution at the A–B intersection indicates physical interaction.  
  > "c) Position-velocity diagram: we find a V-shape gas distribution at the intersection of filaments A and B, indicating their physical interaction." (Discussion)
- YSO 548 lies at the filament intersection; YSO 551 lies in a hub-filament.  
  > "Among these, the most reliable YSO 548 is at the filament intersection and YSO 551 is in a hub-filament." (Discussion)
- CO filaments resemble the 8.0 μm morphology.  
  > "The distribution of 8.0$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ emission in N66N shows multiple filaments, and the morphology resembles the spatial distribution of $^{12}$CO(1–0) emission." ($^{12}$CO(1–0) spatial distributions and filaments)
- The PMS cluster is inferred to predate the collision.  
  > "We expect this low-mass PMS cluster has been formed prior to the cloud–cloud collision as it is found to be slightly older than the estimated collision timescale $\sim$0.2 Myr." (Discussion)

(Abstract)
- Why this method: Use three published kinematic diagnostics to seek cloud–cloud collision evidence.  
  > "To find proper evidence for a cloud–cloud collision, we use three methods discussed by (cite: Fukui18a)." (Discussion)
