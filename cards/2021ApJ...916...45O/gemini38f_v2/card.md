## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate RMS noise in continuum images**: Mean RMS measured in 50 randomly placed synthesized-beam-sized areas with maximum intensity < 3.5 mJy beam^-1  
  > "We estimated the 1$\sigma$ root mean square (RMS) noise within each pointing by finding the mean value of $\sigma$ in 50 randomly-placed synthesized-beam-sized-areas of the pre-primary-beam corrected continuum images with a maximum intensity less than 3.5 mJy $\textrm{beam}^{-1}$." (Core Identification)
   - parameter: Max intensity threshold = 3.5 mJy beam^-1; 50 random beam-sized areas  
  > "50 randomly-placed synthesized-beam-sized-areas of the pre-primary-beam corrected continuum images with a maximum intensity less than 3.5 mJy $\textrm{beam}^{-1}$." (Core Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Identified 3 clumps with elevated noise due to cleaning residuals or emission outside the primary beam  
  > "We identified three clumps which had significantly higher noise levels: G316.80 ($\sigma = 1.05$ mJy $\textrm{beam}^{-1}$), 316.81 ($\sigma = 0.85$ mJy $\textrm{beam}^{-1}$), and G345.50 ($\sigma = 1.38$ mJy $\textrm{beam}^{-1}$)." (Core Identification)
2. **Identify cores using dendrograms**: Dendrogram extraction identifying leaves as cores on pre-primary-beam corrected images  
  > "We used the dendrogram algorithm (cite: rosolowsky_structural_2008) as implemented in the python package astrodendro to identify cores." (Core Identification); "We defined cores as the leaves of the dendrogram." (Core Identification)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: F_min = 4 sigma, delta = 1 sigma, S_min = 0.5 synthesized beam area  
  > "We required parent structures (trunks) in the dendrogram to have a minimum intensity $F_{\rm min}$ of $4\sigma$. We also required substructures (branches and leaves) to have a minimum increase in intensity $\delta$ of $1\sigma$ and a minimum area $S_{\rm{min}}$ of half the synthesized beam" (Core Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested parameter sensitivity with F_min = 3, 4, 5 sigma and S_min = 0.5, 1 beam  
  > "We kept $\delta$ constant and tested combinations of $F_{\rm{min}}=$ 3, 4, or 5 $\sigma$ and $S_{\rm{min}}=0.5$ beam or 1 beam." (Effects of Dendrogram Parameters, Binning Scheme, and Clump Distance)
3. **Estimate raw core masses from continuum fluxes**: Optically thin dust emission calculation  
  > "We estimated masses of cores by assuming optically thin thermal emission from dust, as in Papers I and II." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - parameter: kappa_1.3mm = 6.38e-3 cm^2 g^-1 (kappa_1.3mm,d = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1, gas-to-dust ratio 141), T_d = 20 K, lambda = 1.30 mm  
  > "assumed an opacity per unit dust mass $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm,d}=0.899\: {\rm cm^2 \ g}^{-1}$ (i.e., the value from the moderately coagulated thin ice mantle model of (cite: ossenkopf_dust_1994)), which results in $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm}= {\rm 6.38\times10^{-3}\:cm^2\:g}^{-1}$ using a gas-to-refractory-component-dust ratio of 141" (Core Mass Estimation); "In the absence of measurements of temperature for each source, we assumed dust temperatures $T_{d} =$ 20 K." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - assumption: Optically thin thermal dust emission at single uniform temperature T_d = 20 K  
  > "We estimated masses of cores by assuming optically thin thermal emission from dust, as in Papers I and II." (Core Mass Estimation); "In the absence of measurements of temperature for each source, we assumed dust temperatures $T_{d} =$ 20 K." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Evaluated impact of 15 K and 30 K temperature assumptions and 10% distance uncertainty  
  > "If temperatures of 15 K or 30 K were to be adopted, the mass estimates would differ by factors of 1.48 and 0.604, respectively." (Core Mass Estimation)
4. **Apply completeness corrections for flux recovery and core number recovery**: Core insertion and recovery experiments from Papers I and II mapped to normalized flux (true flux / RMS)  
  > "In Papers I and II, we derived correction factors to estimate a complete, i.e., “true”, CMF from an observed “raw” CMF. Artificial cores of varying masses were randomly inserted into real clump images to estimate the amount of the flux typically detected by the dendrogram algorithm as a function of inserted mass." (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections); "We adopted the median values of these factors as universal values with which to correct our observed “raw” CMFs from our entire clump sample." (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections)
   - parameter: f_flux (flux recovery fraction) and f_num (number recovery fraction) parameterized by true flux normalized by RMS noise  
  > "We then converted these correction factors to be a function of the “true” flux of the inserted core (using eq. [eq:Sigmamm] and eq. [eq:coremass]), and then a function of the “true” flux of the inserted core normalized by the RMS noise $\sigma$" (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections)
   - assumption: Universal recovery curves across Galactic clump source morphologies as a function of normalized flux  
  > "This similarity in functional form suggests that these factors may be applicable to a variety of Galactic clump source morphologies, such as those exhibited in our ALMAGAL clump sample." (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of curves derived in G286 and IRDCs showing close match when normalized by RMS noise  
  > "Although the correction factors vary between regions when considered as a function of inserted mass, they closely approximate each other when considered as a function of normalized flux." (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections)
5. **Fit power-law models and evaluate breaks and log-normal alternatives**: WLS, unbinned MLE, and binned MLE-B power-law fits; Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Anderson-Darling distance minimization with bootstrap resampling; AICc model selection between single, double, and triple piecewise power laws and log-normal distributions  
  > "First, we follow the fiducial weighted least squares (WLS) method of Papers I and II" (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF); "To estimate $\alpha$ for an unbinned sample of cores above some minimum mass $M_{\rm{min}}$ we applied an MLE" (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF); "We also use a second MLE method (MLE-B) to fit to binned data, i.e., fitting power-laws directly to the histograms" (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF); "The corrected Akaike Information Criterion AIC$_{c}$ (cite: doi:10.1080/03610927808827599) accounts for this effect by adding an additional penalty term to the AIC formula" (PPL fits and evidence for a break in the CMF)
   - software: powerlaw
   - parameter: Bins: 5 per dex, centered at 1 M_sun; 3000 bootstrap resamples for p-values; Vuong test for log-normal vs power-law comparison

## Qualitative findings

- Statistically robust detection of a break in the power-law behavior of the true CMF located between ~5 and 15 M_sun.  
  > "However, we find evidence of a break in this power-law behavior between $\sim5$ and $15\:M_{\odot}$, which is, to our knowledge, the first time such a break has been found in distant ($\gtrsim 1$ kpc) regions by ALMA." (abstract)
- The massive protocluster CMF is significantly more top-heavy, containing more massive cores, than the CMFs of G286 and IRDCs.  
  > "The nature of the difference is that we find that the CMF from our sample of ALMAGAL-observed massive protoclusters has a significantly larger population of high-mass cores than the CMFs derived from the G286 protocluster and the IRDC clumps." (Comparison of CMFs in Different Environments and Implications for Massive Star Formation)
- Clustering analysis shows Q parameters between 0.64 and 0.82, showing little evidence for centrally condensed structures in individual clumps.  
  > "We found little evidence for centrally condensed structures in these regions, with values of $Q$ ranging from 0.64 to 0.82, as summarized in Table 3." (Spatial Distribution of Cores)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sources selected within 3.5 kpc that had delivered 12m-array data from the ALMAGAL survey to allow probing down to low core masses (~1 M_sun)  
  > "To be able to probe down to relatively low core masses, i.e., $\sim1\:M_\odot$, we limited our analysis to sources within 3.5 kpc that had 12m-array observations delivered to the ALMA archive by May 2020." (Source Selection)
- Why this method: Adopted the exact dendrogram extraction and flux/number recovery correction methods of Papers I and II to enable direct, unbiased comparison between environments  
  > "To identify cores, we followed the methods used in our studies of the G286 protocluster (Paper I) and IRDCs (Paper II), thus allowing a direct comparison of the derived CMFs." (Core Identification)
- Alternative: Using clumpfind algorithm instead of dendrograms was explored in Paper I and rejected in favor of the fiducial dendrogram leaf method  
  > "We note that the effects of using the clumpfind algorithm (cite: williams_determining_1994) instead of the dendrogram method were explored in Paper I, and we do not repeat this analysis here." (Core Identification)
- Alternative: Identifying cores on primary-beam-corrected images was rejected because varying noise across pointings would compromise extraction  
  > "Attempting to identify cores in primary-beam-corrected images would be compromised as a result of this effect." (Core Identification)
- Alternative: Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance was supplemented/deprioritized relative to Anderson-Darling distance because KS is insensitive to distribution tails  
  > "We prefer the AD statistic due to the known issue of the KS statistic being insensitive to differences in the tails of compared distributions." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Core masses lack local background subtraction and assume a uniform dust temperature of 20 K without core-scale temperature measurements  
  > "The core masses we report have significant uncertainties due to no correction being made for the local backgrounds within which the cores are embedded, and also from a lack of temperature information on the scales of the cores." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Small number of cores per clump (Nc < 30) limits clustering analysis and prevents calculating mass segregation ratios on a per-clump basis  
  > "However, we note that the relatively small sample sizes of cores within each clump severely limit the conclusions that can be derived from these results." (Spatial Distribution of Cores); "Additionally, the small number of cores in each clump prevented the calculation of the frequently used “mass segregation ratio” $\Lambda_{MSR}$ and other typical methods to investigate this effect on a clump-by-clump basis." (Spatial Distribution of Cores)
- Limitation (authors): The sample of 28 clumps is an early subset and not a complete volume-limited sample of massive clumps within 3.5 kpc  
  > "We note that this is not a complete sample of all such massive clumps within 3.5 kpc of the Sun and is only a small fraction of the data that will eventually come from the ALMAGAL survey." (Source Selection)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Potential bias if warmer dust temperatures systematically exist in more massive cores, which would artificially flatten the high-mass CMF
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainties on number recovery corrections at the low-mass end (< 1 M_sun), especially in more distant (> 3 kpc) clumps, could artificially inflate the lowest-mass bins
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absence of line data to confirm that all dendrogram leaves are gravitationally bound cores rather than transient turbulent density fluctuations
