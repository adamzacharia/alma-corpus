- Question: Measure the core mass function (CMF) in 28 dense clumps within 3.5 kpc that are likely central regions of massive protoclusters, test for a break in its power-law behavior, and compare it with CMFs in other Galactic environments.

## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate continuum image RMS noise and identify dense cores**: Estimate 1-sigma RMS noise from 50 randomly-placed synthesized-beam-sized areas with maximum intensity < 3.5 mJy beam^-1 in pre-primary-beam-corrected images, then run the dendrogram algorithm and define leaves as cores.  
  > "We estimated the 1$\sigma$ root mean square (RMS) noise within each pointing by finding the mean value of $\sigma$ in 50 randomly-placed synthesized-beam-sized-areas of the pre-primary-beam-corrected continuum images with a maximum intensity less than 3.5 mJy $\textrm{beam}^{-1}$." (Core Identification); "We used the dendrogram algorithm (cite: rosolowsky_structural_2008) as implemented in the python package `astrodendro` to identify cores." (Core Identification); "We defined cores as the leaves of the dendrogram." (Core Identification)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: F_min = 4 sigma (minimum intensity for trunks)  
  > "We required parent structures (trunks) in the dendrogram to have a minimum intensity $F_{\rm min}$ of $4\sigma$." (Core Identification)
   - parameter: delta = 1 sigma (minimum intensity increase for branches and leaves)  
  > "We also required substructures (*branches* and *leaves*) to have a minimum increase in intensity $\delta$ of $1\sigma$" (Core Identification)
   - parameter: S_min = 0.5 synthesized beam area (minimum area)  
  > "and a minimum area $S_{\rm{min}}$ of half the synthesized beam to be considered independent entities." (Core Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Varied dendrogram parameters (F_min = 3, 4, 5 sigma and S_min = 0.5 or 1 beam) and confirmed CMF fit results (alpha between 0.94 and 1.10) are not very sensitive to parameter choice.  
  > "We kept $\delta$ constant and tested combinations of $F_{\rm{min}}=$ 3, 4, or 5 $\sigma$ and $S_{\rm{min}}=0.5$ beam or 1 beam." (Effects of Dendrogram Parameters, Binning Scheme, and Clump Distance); "For the true CMF, $M_{\rm{min}}$ typically ranged between 3 and $8\:M_{\odot}$ with $\alpha$’s between 0.94 and 1.10. This shows that our results using the fiducial method are not very sensitive to the choice of dendrogram parameters." (Effects of Dendrogram Parameters, Binning Scheme, and Clump Distance)
2. **Calculate raw core masses, radii, and surface densities**: Convert integrated 1.3 mm continuum flux and projected core area into raw mass and mean mass surface density assuming optically thin thermal dust emission.  
  > "We estimated masses of cores by assuming optically thin thermal emission from dust, as in Papers I and II." (Core Mass Estimation); "The median radius of the cores was $R_{c} \sim 0.01$ pc ($R_{c} = \sqrt{A/\pi}$), where $A$ is the core’s projected area). We evaluated the mean mass surface density of the cores, $\Sigma_{c} = M/A$." (Continuum Images and Average Core Properties)
   - parameter: lambda = 1.30 mm  
  > "$\lambda_{1.3}=\lambda/1.30\:{\rm mm}$ and $T_{d,20}=T_d/20\:{\rm K}$ with $T_d$ being the dust temperature." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - assumption: Optically thin thermal dust emission  
  > "We estimated masses of cores by assuming optically thin thermal emission from dust, as in Papers I and II." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - assumption: Dust temperature T_d = 20 K  
  > "In the absence of measurements of temperature for each source, we assumed dust temperatures $T_{d} =$ 20 K." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - assumption: Opacity per unit dust mass kappa_1.3mm,d = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1 (Ossenkopf & Henning 1994 moderately coagulated thin ice mantle model)  
  > "we assumed an opacity per unit dust mass $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm,d}=0.899\: {\rm cm^2 \ g}^{-1}$ (i.e., the value from the moderately coagulated thin ice mantle model of (cite: ossenkopf_dust_1994))" (Core Mass Estimation)
   - assumption: Gas-to-refractory-component-dust ratio of 141, giving kappa_1.3mm = 6.38e-3 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "which results in $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm}= {\rm 6.38\times10^{-3}\:cm^2\:g}^{-1}$ using a gas-to-refractory-component-dust ratio of 141 (cite: draine_physics_2011)." (Core Mass Estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Evaluated sensitivity to dust temperature: adopting 15 K or 30 K changes masses by factors of 1.48 and 0.604.  
  > "If temperatures of 15 K or 30 K were to be adopted, the mass estimates would differ by factors of 1.48 and 0.604, respectively." (Core Mass Estimation)
3. **Apply completeness corrections and construct raw, flux-corrected, and true CMFs**: Convert flux recovery (f_flux) and number recovery (f_num) curves from Papers I and II into functions of normalized flux (true flux divided by local RMS noise), adopt their median values to correct core fluxes/masses and bin counts, and bin masses at 5 bins per dex centered on 1 M_sun.  
  > "We then converted these correction factors to be a function of the “true” flux of the inserted core (using eq. [\[eq:Sigmamm\]](#eq:Sigmamm) and eq. [\[eq:coremass\]](#eq:coremass)), and then a function of the “true” flux of the inserted core normalized by the RMS noise $\sigma$ of the region into which the core was inserted." (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections); "We adopted the median values of these factors as universal values with which to correct our observed “raw” CMFs from our entire clump sample." (Core Flux Recovery and Completeness Corrections); "Masses were binned using the same methods as Papers I and II, with all bins equal in size in $\Delta \rm{log_{10}}M$ with five bins per dex, and a bin centered on 1 $M_{\odot}$." (Construction of the CMF)
   - parameter: Bin width: 5 bins per dex in log10(M), centered on 1 M_sun  
  > "with all bins equal in size in $\Delta \rm{log_{10}}M$ with five bins per dex, and a bin centered on 1 $M_{\odot}$." (Construction of the CMF)
   - assumption: N^1/2 Poisson counting uncertainties for each bin  
  > "We assumed $\textit{N}^{1/2}$ Poisson counting uncertainties for each bin." (Construction of the CMF)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Varied number of bins per dex and Split sample at d = 3 kpc (15 near vs 13 far clumps) and compared using KS tests.  
  > "We varied the number of bins per dex and fit a power-law to the resultant histogram using the fiducial binned MLE method (MLE-B)." (Effects of Dendrogram Parameters, Binning Scheme, and Clump Distance); "Figure [8](#fig:cmfs_distance) compares the CMFs of the 15 clumps (containing 127 cores) within 3 kpc and the 13 clumps (containing 95 cores) farther than 3 kpc." (Effects of Dendrogram Parameters, Binning Scheme, and Clump Distance)
4. **Fit single power-laws to the CMFs and determine the optimal minimum mass M_min**: Fit single power-laws using WLS, unbinned MLE, and binned MLE (MLE-B), first with fixed M_min = 0.79 M_sun, and then varying M_min to minimize Kolmogorov-Smirnov (D) and Anderson-Darling (A^2) statistics, with p-values evaluated via semi-parametric bootstrap resampling.  
  > "First, we follow the fiducial weighted least squares (WLS) method of Papers I and II, which involves minimizing differences in the log of $d N / d {\rm log} M$, normalized using the average of the asymmetric Poisson errors." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF); "We also use a second MLE method (MLE-B) to fit to binned data, i.e., fitting power-laws directly to the histograms of the raw, flux-corrected, and true CMFs." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF); "We then repeated this procedure $N=3000$ times, i.e., with 3000 different synthetic CMFs for each $M_{\rm min}$, recording the value of $\hat{D}$ or $\hat{A}^2$ resulting from the power-law fit to each synthetic CMF." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
   - parameter: Standard M_min = 0.79 M_sun (lower bound of bin centered at 1 M_sun)  
  > "The fiducial method of Papers I and II was to fit the CMF over a standard range from the mass bin centered at $1\:M_\odot$ (i.e., for cores down to a minimum mass $M_{\rm min} = 0.79\:M_\odot$) up to the highest mass bin that is populated." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
   - parameter: N = 3000 bootstrap synthetic CMFs per M_min (100 values of M_min between 0.1 and 10 M_sun for raw/flux-corrected; 11 mass bins for true CMF)  
  > "We then repeated this procedure $N=3000$ times, i.e., with 3000 different synthetic CMFs for each $M_{\rm min}$" (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF); "For the raw and flux-corrected CMFs, we performed this bootstrapping procedure for 100 values of $M_{\rm{min}}$ evenly distributed between 0.1 and 10 $M_{\odot}$. For the true CMF, we performed this procedure for 11 mass bins between 0.1 and 10 $M_{\odot}$." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
   - parameter: LOWESS smoothing parameter = 0.05  
  > "The black and blue curves, corresponding to the raw and flux-corrected CMFs, respectively, were smoothed using LOWESS regression (Locally Weighted Scatter-plot Smoothing) (cite: cleveland_robust_1979) with a smoothing parameter of 0.05 determined by cross-validation." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
   - assumption: Empty high-mass bin treated in WLS as 1/10th of 1-count level with error bar reaching 1-count level  
  > "For the one empty bin at the high-mass end, we again follow Papers I and II and treat it as an effective upper limit by assuming that the point is a factor of 10 lower than the level if the bin had 1 data point and set the error bar such that it reaches up to the level if there were 1 data point in the bin." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
   - fitting: Weighted least squares (WLS), unbinned maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), and binned maximum likelihood estimation (MLE-B) with semi-parametric bootstrap goodness-of-fit testing (p >= 0.10 threshold).  
  > "Considering the various results derived from the KS and AD based methods, we report final “best” fits that begin at the lowest significant ($p \geq .10$) $M_{\rm min}$ value as assessed by $A^2$." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
   - validation: Semi-parametric bootstrap resampling (3000 synthetic CMFs per M_min) using KS D and Anderson-Darling A^2 statistics.  
  > "We calculated the fraction of synthetic values of $\hat{D}$ or $\hat{A}^2$ that were larger than the original $D$ or $A^2$ fit to the real CMF and we estimate a $p$-value via $p = N(\hat{D} \geq D)/N$ and $p = N(\hat{A}^2 \geq A^2)/N$." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
5. **Fit piecewise power-laws (PPLs) and log-normal distributions to test for a break in the CMF**: Perform non-linear WLS regression for single, double, and triple PPLs as well as log-normal distributions across M_min bins from 1 to 16 M_sun, and compare models using corrected Akaike Information Criterion (AIC_c), Akaike weights w_i(AIC), and Vuong log-likelihood ratio tests R.  
  > "For the mass ranges defined by minimum mass bins $M_{\rm{min}}$ centered from 1 and 16 $M_{\odot}$ we fit a single power-law, a double PPL, and a triple PPL by performing non-linear WLS regression." (PPL fits and evidence for a break in the CMF); "We calculate the single, double and triple PPL fit AIC$_{\rm WLS,c}$ values as a function of $M_{\rm{min}}$ for each CMF." (PPL fits and evidence for a break in the CMF); "We explored the goodness of fit of log-normal distributions and single power-laws by calculating the ratio $R$ of their log-likelihoods (cite: vuong_likelihood_1989; clauset_power-law_2009)" (Comparison to log-normal distributions)
   - software: powerlaw
   - parameter: kappa_q = 2, 4, 6 parameters for single, double, and triple PPL; N = 7 to 13 bins  
  > "For a single, double, and triple PPL $\kappa_{q}=2$, $4$ and $6$, respectively. $N$ ranges between 7 and 13 depending on the $M_{\rm{min}}$ selected." (PPL fits and evidence for a break in the CMF)
   - parameter: 2000 iterations of random uniform in-bin mass sampling for binned log-likelihood ratio R  
  > "To assess the binned CMFs, we generated the corresponding number of random masses uniformly distributed in each mass bin and calculated the median $R$ over 2000 iterations of the same process." (Comparison to log-normal distributions)
   - fitting: Non-linear WLS regression for PPLs and log-normal fits; model selection via AIC_c Akaike weights and log-likelihood ratio R.  
  > "We calculated the AIC$_c$ values and Akaike weights $w_{i}$(AIC) for a log-normal fit and for single, double, and triple power-law fits to the CMF." (Comparison to log-normal distributions)
   - validation: Compared break masses found via PPL AIC_c weights with optimal M_min values from MLE A^2 minimization and tested sensitivity to minimum mass bin (0.79 vs 1.26 M_sun).  
  > "Note, the break mass bin $M_1$ for the raw and flux-corrected CMFs are identical to the bins selected by fitting a single power-law to the binned CMF through performing an MLE and minimizing $A^2$." (PPL fits and evidence for a break in the CMF)
6. **Quantify spatial clustering of cores and compare CMF across Galactic environments**: Construct minimum spanning trees (MSTs) and compute the Q parameter for clumps with N_c >= 10 cores; perform KS tests and MLE power-law fits to compare the massive protocluster CMF with the G286 and IRDC CMFs from Papers I and II.  
  > "For those clumps with at least 10 cores, we analyzed the clustering of cores within clumps by calculating minimum spanning trees (MST)" (Spatial Distribution of Cores); "For each clump, we calculated the dimensionless $Q$ parameter (cite: cartwright_statistical_2004)" (Spatial Distribution of Cores); "We performed Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests comparing CMFs from our previous studies in G286 and IRDCs to the CMF derived here in the ALMAGAL-observed massive protoclusters." (Comparison of CMFs in Different Environments and Implications for Massive Star Formation)
   - software: networkx
   - parameter: Minimum core count for MST analysis: N_c >= 10; Q = 0.8 threshold between fractal and centrally concentrated  
  > "With some variation with the density profile assumed, large $Q$ values ($Q>0.8$) indicate that structures are centrally concentrated, while small $Q$ values ($Q<0.8$) indicate that clusters have fractal substructures." (Spatial Distribution of Cores)
   - parameter: M >= 0.79 M_sun cutoff for environmental KS tests and MLE comparisons  
  > "We only include cores with $M \geq 0.79$ $M_{\odot}$, i.e., more massive than the lower bound of the bin centered at $1\:M_{\odot}$, in these tests" (Comparison of CMFs in Different Environments and Implications for Massive Star Formation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Noted degraded accuracy of Q parameter for N < 30 members based on published simulations.  
  > "while (cite: gouliermis_clustered_2012) found through the use of simulated clusters that clusters containing $N<30$ members experience especially pronounced degradation in the accuracy of $Q$ estimations." (Spatial Distribution of Cores)

## Qualitative findings

- A break in the power-law behavior of the massive protocluster CMF is detected between ~5 and 15 M_sun.  
  > "However, we find evidence of a break in this power-law behavior between $\sim5$ and $15\:M_{\odot}$, which is, to our knowledge, the first time such a break has been found in distant ($\gtrsim 1$ kpc) regions by ALMA." (Abstract)
- For the true CMF above M_min = 0.79 M_sun, a double piecewise power-law is preferred over a single power-law, triple PPL, or single log-normal distribution.  
  > "However, for the true CMF a log-normal fit was not the best description ($w_{i}$(AIC)$_{\rm{ln}}=0.03$) and a double PPL was preferred ($w_{i}$(AIC)$_{\rm{double}} = 0.85$)." (Comparison to log-normal distributions)
- The massive protocluster CMF is significantly more top-heavy (containing more high-mass cores) than the CMFs in the G286 protocluster and IRDC clumps, likely due to higher clump mass surface densities (Sigma_cl > 1 g cm^-2).  
  > "The nature of the difference is that we find that the CMF from our sample of ALMAGAL-observed massive protoclusters has a significantly larger population of high-mass cores than the CMFs derived from the G286 protocluster and the IRDC clumps." (Comparison of CMFs in Different Environments and Implications for Massive Star Formation); "In comparison to G286 and the IRDC clumps, the main difference is the typically much higher value of mass surface density, $\Sigma_{\rm cl}$, of the massive protocluster sample: these sources generally have $\Sigma_{\rm cl}>1\:{\rm g\:cm}^{-2}$" (Comparison of CMFs in Different Environments and Implications for Massive Star Formation)
- Little evidence is found for centrally condensed core clustering in the 7 clumps with at least 10 cores, nor any significant radial mass segregation.  
  > "We found little evidence for centrally condensed structures in these regions, with values of $Q$ ranging from 0.64 to 0.82, as summarized in Table [3](#qparams)." (Spatial Distribution of Cores); "We compared all core masses to the radial separation of each core from the center of its image and observed a slight, although non-significant trend of higher mass cores being located nearer to the center of images ($r=-0.12$)." (Spatial Distribution of Cores)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected ALMAGAL 12m-array observations of dense clumps within 3.5 kpc delivered by May 2020 in order to probe down to low core masses (~1 M_sun) in central regions of massive protoclusters.  
  > "To be able to probe down to relatively low core masses, i.e., $\sim1\:M_\odot$, we limited our analysis to sources within 3.5 kpc that had 12m-array observations delivered to the *ALMA* archive by May 2020." (Source Selection)
- Why this method: Used the exact same dendrogram core identification and completeness correction methods as Papers I and II to allow direct, unbiased comparison of CMFs across environments; identified cores in pre-primary-beam-corrected images so spatially varying noise does not compromise detection; preferred Anderson-Darling over KS statistic because AD is sensitive to distribution tails.  
  > "To identify cores, we followed the methods used in our studies of the G286 protocluster (Paper I) and IRDCs (Paper II), thus allowing a direct comparison of the derived CMFs." (Core Identification); "Attempting to identify cores in primary-beam-corrected images would be compromised as a result of this effect." (Core Identification); "We prefer the AD statistic due to the known issue of the KS statistic being insensitive to differences in the tails of compared distributions." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
- Alternative: clumpfind algorithm (explored in Paper I and not repeated here in favor of the fiducial dendrogram algorithm)  
  > "We note that the effects of using the `clumpfind` algorithm (cite: williams_determining_1994) instead of the dendrogram method were explored in Paper I, and we do not repeat this analysis here." (Core Identification)
- Alternative: Pearson's chi^2 cumulative test statistic (rejected in favor of KS and Anderson-Darling statistics, which are more powerful)  
  > "Other distance metrics such as Pearson’s $\chi^{2}$ cumulative test statistic are also potentially applicable for this goal, but have been found to be less powerful than KS or AD based approaches (cite: virkar_power-law_2014)." (Single Power-Law Fits to the CMF)
- Alternative: Herschel-derived dust temperature maps for core temperatures (rejected because angular resolution >= 30'' is much coarser than ALMA cores and subject to Galactic plane background subtraction uncertainties)  
  > "We note that *Herschel*-derived dust temperature maps have much lower angular resolution ($\gtrsim 30\ensuremath{''}$) than the sizes of the ALMA-identified cores." (Core Mass Estimation)
