## Analysis recipe

1. **Verify continuum streamer structures and map molecular emission**: Image 1.2 mm continuum with different beam tapering to verify northern/southern streamers against cleaning artifacts; produce moment 0 maps (integrated over the whole velocity interval) and velocity-binned channel maps for HDO, SO2, and NH2CHO.  
  > "In order to verify that these structures are not artifacts introduced by the cleaning process, we test different beam tapering. All the elongated arc-like structures are still present when using a larger circular beam." (2 The protostellar binary system SVS13-A); "In particular, the line emission is integrated over the whole velocity interval and reported in color scale superposed to the continuum emission in white contours." (4 Results); "More specifically, line emission is integrated in five different velocity channels and showed in cyan contours superposed to the continuum emission in color scale." (4 Results)
   - software: IRAM-GILDAS
   - parameter: HDO velocity intervals: 2.4-4.4, 4.6-6.5, 6.7-8.7, 8.8-10.5, and 10.6-11.8 km s^-1  
  > "For HDO the blue-shifted emission in the velocity intervals 2.4-4.4 km s − 1 and 4.6-6.5 km s − 1 shows the same distribution." (4 Results); "In the velocity intervals 6.7-8.7 km s − 1, 8.8-10.5 km s − 1 and 10.6-11.8 km s − 1 the line emission is associated with both protostars, peaking in between." (4 Results)
   - parameter: SO2 velocity intervals: 6.6-8.7, 8.9-10.4, and 10.5-11.9 km s^-1 (velocities < +6.5 km s^-1 excluded due to CH2DOH contamination)  
  > "SO 2 emission is also associated to both protostars in the velocity intervals 6.6-8.7" (4 Results); "Even if the blue-shifted part of the line (velocities lower than +6.5 km s − 1 ) is contaminated by the CH 2 DOH(4 3 , 1 –5 1 , 5 )e1 line at 241.61928840 GHz (see Table 1), the SO 2 emission in the other available channels shows a spatial distribution similar to that of HDO." (4 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested different beam tapering (using a larger circular beam) to confirm elongated continuum structures are not cleaning artifacts.

  > "In order to distinguish which of the two hypothesis is correct, one has to evaluate the thermal sublimation temperature of deuterated water, SO 2 and formamide." ## Qualitative findings

- Continuum emission at 1.2 mm reveals three elongated structures extending north from VLA4A and VLA4B, interpreted as dust accretion streamers feeding the binary system.  
  > "In addition, we detect three elongated structures which extend from the northern edge of the continuum associated with VLA4A and B towards the north." (2 The protostellar binary system SVS13-A); "We conclude that the dust emission is tracing accretion streamers extending towards the north and the south and feeding VLA4A and VLA4B." (2 The protostellar binary system SVS13-A)
- HDO and SO2 emission are associated with both VLA4A and VLA4B with an emission peak in between, whereas NH2CHO is compact and predominantly associated with VLA4A, confirming chemical segregation.  
  > "As already noticed in [1], the NH 2 CHO emission is more compact and centered in VLA4A while HDO and SO 2 have a broader emission, extended also towards VLA4B. This finding confirms the chemical segregation reported in the binary system using iCOMs [1,18] ." (4 Results); "The radial profiles show that HDO and SO 2 are extended in a region covering both protostars with a peak of emission in between." (4 Results)
- HDO channel maps reveal a blue-shifted (+2.42–4.39 km s^-1) emission component at ≥ 120 au spatially coincident with the northern dust accretion streamer, with hints of NH2CHO at the same position.  
  > "This component is perfectly aligned with the northern streamer traced by the dust, thus revealing molecular gas associated with the streamer itself." (4 Results); "In addition, the channel maps of HDO emission reveal a further component at blue-shifted velocities (+2.42–4.39 km s − 1 ) spatially coincident with the dust accretion streamer. The streamer traced by HDO is located at a distance ≥ 120 au from the VLA4A and VLA4B protostars." (6 Conclusions)
- Thermal sublimation at ≥ 120 au is excluded for formamide based on updated binding energies unless VLA4A is undergoing an active accretion burst; an accretion shock at the streamer-disk interface is proposed as the origin of the molecular emission.  
  > "The presence of formamide at this position, considering the information on the binding energies and the corresponding sublimation temperatures (see 5.1 and Fig. 5), suggests that formamide is not thermally desorbed."   > "The spatial distribution of HDO in SVS13-A demonstrates that in Class I disks water is not necessarily confined in the inner 10 au, as suggested by previous ALMA and NOEMA H 18 2 O observations in 5 Class I disks [56] ." (6 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SVS13-A is a close Class I protobinary system at ~299 pc with known hot corino chemistry, chemical segregation, and dust streamers; high-angular resolution (~50 au) ALMA observations of HDO and SO2 clarify the origin of the elongated molecular distribution.  
  > "SVS13-A is a perfect target to perform astrochemical studies and tackle some of the questions above. It is very well studied, in Perseus, at a distance of ∼ 299 pc ( Gaia, [11] )." (2 The protostellar binary system SVS13-A); "The origin of such molecular line distribution needs to be clarified using different molecular tracers. To this purpose we report here the analysis of sulfur dioxide SO 2 and deuterated water HDO in the SVS13-A system." (2.1 The prototype Class I hot corinos)
- Why this method: Evaluating thermal sublimation temperatures from updated quantum chemical binding energy distributions allows distinguishing between thermal sublimation from warm dust grains and non-thermal shock injection into the gas phase.  
  > "In summary, the presence of HDO, SO 2 and NH 2 CHO, implies that either they (or their precursors) are thermally sublimated or they are injected from the frozen mantles into the gas-phase by a shock." (5.1 Binding energies and sublimation temperatures); "In order to distinguish which of the two hypothesis is correct, one has to evaluate the thermal sublimation temperature of deuterated water, SO 2 and formamide." (5.1 Binding energies and sublimation temperatures)
- Alternative: Cleaning artifacts as the origin of the northern continuum streamer structures (rejected via beam tapering tests).  
  > "In order to verify that these structures are not artifacts introduced by the cleaning process, we test different beam tapering. All the elongated arc-like structures are still present when using a larger circular beam." (2 The protostellar binary system SVS13-A)
- Alternative: Chemical desorption or photo-desorption as the release mechanism for HDO (rejected as inefficient).  
  > "Other possible mechanisms, such as chemical desorption or photo-desorption, would not be efficient here." (5.1 Binding energies and sublimation temperatures)
- Alternative: Thermal sublimation driven by an active accretion burst on VLA4A (considered possible and not completely excluded due to unknown individual protostellar luminosities).  
  > "Thermal desorption is not completely excluded in case the source is actively experiencing an accretion burst." (abstract); "In order to definitely exclude the possibility that thermal sublimation is responsible for the chemical enrichment at distances larger than 120 au, it is crucial to have an estimate of the individual VLA4A and VLA4B luminosities." (5.2 Accretion streamers feeding the disks)
- Alternative: Self-gravitating substructures causing local temperature enhancements (rejected because the disks and circumbinary material in SVS13-A are expected to be gravitationally stable).  
  > "However, in the case of SVS13-A the circumstellar disks around VLA4A and VLA4B, as well as the circumbinary material are expected to be stable" (5.2 Accretion streamers feeding the disks)
