## Analysis recipe

1. **Image and smooth the ALMA data**: Image the 32 new visibility sets with tclean and natural weighting; Hanning-smooth the cubes; export FITS  
  > "The imaging of the visibility sets for all 32 sources was performed using the ”tclean” task in the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: mcm07) package version 5.4.0." (New ALMA observations); "Applying a natural weighting resulted in data cubes" (New ALMA observations); "After calibration and imaging within CASA, all image cubes were converted into FITS format for further analysis." (New ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Hanning smoothing to 20 km s^-1  
  > "For analysis purposes we Hanning-smoothed the data cubes to a velocity resolution of 20 km s$^{\rm -1}$." (New ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure nuclear continuum flux and size**: Fit two-dimensional Gaussians to continuum maps; deconvolve the synthesized beam; retain the strongest continuum component  
  > "We obtained estimates for the continuum properties from two-dimensional Gaussian fits to the continuum maps using the CASA task IMFIT which deconvolves the synthesized beam from the fitted component size" (New ALMA observations); "In sources with multiple continuum components, we use the results from the strongest one, as this is where a potential CON would be most likely to be found." (New ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, IMFIT
   - assumption: The strongest continuum component is the most likely location of a potential CON  
  > "In sources with multiple continuum components, we use the results from the strongest one, as this is where a potential CON would be most likely to be found." (New ALMA observations)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fits with beam deconvolution; error-estimate discussion in (cite: con97)  
  > "two-dimensional Gaussian fits to the continuum maps using the CASA task IMFIT which deconvolves the synthesized beam from the fitted component size" (New ALMA observations); "see (cite: con97) for a discussion of the error estimates in this process." (New ALMA observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract HCN-vib fluxes and upper limits**: Integrate narrow separated lines over ±150 km s^-1; extract within twice the HCO+ Gaussian FWHM diameter; fit integrated spectra for broad blended lines; set 3-sigma nondetection limits  
  > "we integrated the data cubes over the velocity range $\pm150$ km s$^{-1}$ around the systemic velocity of the transition" (New ALMA observations); "We then extracted the fluxes from a region with a diameter of twice the Gaussian full width at half maximum of the velocity integrated HCO$^{+}$ emission." (New ALMA observations); "To compensate for line blending in sources with strong and broad lines, we instead obtained the flux through a fit to the spatially integrated spectrum extracted from the same region." (New ALMA observations)
   - parameter: Systemic velocities from tabulated redshifts in the heliocentric frame  
  > "calculated from the redshifts given in Table" (New ALMA observations); "in the heliocentric frame." (New ALMA observations)
   - parameter: 3-sigma upper limits using integrated-map rms and ground-state line widths  
  > "In the case of a nondetection of the HCN-vib line, we set a $3$-$\sigma$ upper limit to the flux based on the rms in the velocity integrated map and the line width of the ground state transitions." (New ALMA observations)
   - fitting: Fit the spatially integrated spectrum for strong broad blended lines  
  > "To compensate for line blending in sources with strong and broad lines, we instead obtained the flux through a fit to the spatially integrated spectrum extracted from the same region." (New ALMA observations)
   - validation: Confirm the IRAS F14378-3651 detection using its integrated intensity map; re-extract pre-existing ALMA fluxes consistently  
  > "The transition is clearly visible in the spectra of all sources except for IRAS F14378-3651, in which the integrated intensity map was used to confirm the detection." (Results); "We have instead extracted these in the same way as for newly observed sources, using the calibrated data sets obtained from the principal investigators of the projects in question." (Archival and literature data)
4. **Derive luminosities and identify CONs**: Convert integrated line flux to luminosity using Equation 1 of (cite: sol05); calculate surface brightness using the 1.1 mm continuum size; apply the CON threshold  
  > "Line luminosities are calculated using Eq. 1 of (cite: sol05), applied to HCN-vib:" (Results); "To avoid issues related to line blending, we use the size of the $1.1$ mm continuum when computing the HCN-vib surface brightness." (How to find compact obscured nuclei); "we identify as CONs galaxies with $\Sigma_{\mathrm{HCN-vib}}> 1$ L$_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ pc$^{-2}$ in the $J=3\text{--}2$ transition over a region with a radius of at least $5$ pc." (How to find compact obscured nuclei)
   - parameter: L_HCN-vib = 1.04 × 10^-3 S_HCN-vib Delta v nu_rest (1+z)^-1 D_L^2; Jy km s^-1, GHz, Mpc give L_sun  
  > "L_{\mathrm{HCN-vib}}=1.04\times10^{-3}S_{\mathrm{HCN-vib}}\,\Delta v\,\nu_{\mathrm{rest}}(1+z)^{-1}D_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}" (Results); "where $L_{\mathrm{HCN-vib}}$ is the HCN-vib luminosity measured in $L_{\odot}$, $S_{\mathrm{HCN-vib}}\,\Delta v$ is the velocity integrated flux in Jy km s$^{-1}$, $\nu_{\mathrm{rest}}$ is the rest frequency in GHz, and $D_{\mathrm{L}}$ is the luminosity distance in Mpc." (Results)
   - assumption: A CON also requires a power source strong enough to excite HCN-vib through its mid-infrared radiation field

## Qualitative findings

- CONs are common in local (U)LIRGs and much less prevalent in lower-luminosity galaxies  
  > "a remarkably high fraction of local LIRG and ULIRG systems, $\sim20\%$ and $\sim40\%$, respectively, are categorized as CONs." (How common are compact obscured nuclei?); "in lower luminosity galaxies the CON prevalence goes down significantly to a value close to zero." (How common are compact obscured nuclei?)
- No statistically significant preference for highly inclined hosts  
  > "There is no statistically significant evidence that CONs are found primarily in highly inclined systems" (Where do compact obscured nuclei occur?)
- Most CONs have lower f25/f60 ratios; NGC 4418 is a warm outlier  
  > "most of the CONs having lower $f_{25}/f_{60}$ ratios than the non-CONs." (Distributions of host galaxy properties); "We note also that one of the CONs, NGC 4418, stands out with a substantially higher $f_{25}/f_{60}$ ratio of $0.22$." (Where do compact obscured nuclei occur?)
- CONs have similar PAH equivalent widths but stronger silicate features than the rest of the sample  
  > "it seems that CONs generally have similar EQW$_{6.2\mathrm{\mu m}}$ but stronger $s_{9.7\mathrm{\mu m}}$ when compared to the rest of the sample." (Where do compact obscured nuclei occur?)
- All seven CONs show possible submillimetre molecular outflow signatures  
  > "At (sub)millimeter wavelengths, all seven CONs in our survey show possible signatures of molecular outflows." (Do all compact obscured nuclei have in- and outflows?)
- Cold observed infrared emission is consistent with warmer interiors obscured by cooler outer regions  
  > "This is consistent with the notion that the large dust columns in CONs work to gradually shift the radiation to longer wavelengths, making the mid- and far-infrared “photospheres” significantly cooler than the interior regions." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Systematic far-infrared-selected survey to measure CON prevalence; declination and distance limits keep integrations and sample sizes feasible  
  > "Due to the limited statistical data, it is still uncertain how common the CONs are, and how their prevalence depends on luminosity." (Sample); "Declination and distance limits were set to ensure reasonable integration times ($t_{\mathrm{int}}\lesssim 2$ h) and sample sizes ($\leq20$ in each luminosity bin) to make the survey feasible." (Sample)
- Why this method: Use nuclear HCN-vib surface brightness to avoid dilution by host infrared emission; use continuum size to avoid line blending; limit velocity integration to avoid HCO+ blending  
  > "This means that it may miss CONs in systems where the infrared emitting region is spatially extended or where multiple nuclei are present." (How to find compact obscured nuclei); "To avoid issues related to line blending, we use the size of the $1.1$ mm continuum when computing the HCN-vib surface brightness." (How to find compact obscured nuclei); "This velocity range was set to avoid blending issues with the HCO$^{+}$ transition that is blueward of the HCN-vib line by approximately $400$ km s$^{-1}$." (New ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Previous L_HCN-vib/L_IR > 10^-8 criterion can miss nuclei diluted by host infrared emission; retained as an option when surface brightness cannot be measured  
  > "This means that it may miss CONs in systems where the infrared emitting region is spatially extended or where multiple nuclei are present." (How to find compact obscured nuclei); "the criterion based on $L_{\mathrm{HCN-vib}}/L_{\mathrm{IR}}$ ratio may be used when the surface brightness is hard to derive." (Alternative tracers of compact obscured nuclei)
- Alternative: High-resolution millimetre continuum brightness as an alternative tracer, with synchrotron/free-free false positives  
  > "one possible way to find sources with very high column density is to examine the continuum brightness at (sub)millimeter wavelengths" (Alternative tracers of compact obscured nuclei); "However, one caveat is that the millimeter continuum may also be affected by synchrotron or free-free emission (cite: aal19), a problem that gets worse at longer wavelengths." (Alternative tracers of compact obscured nuclei)
- Alternative: Total-infrared-selected complementary sample to distinguish rare warm CONs from a second warm population  
  > "either NGC 4418 is an outlier, and in general warm CONs are rare; or there is a second population of warm CONs." (Effects of the selection bias); "In order to discern between the two scenarios, a sample selected on the total infrared luminosity could be observed." (Effects of the selection bias)
- Alternative: Below-threshold HCN-vib sources may be less extreme CONs, former CONs or transition objects  
  > "it is possible that these objects are related to the CONs, either as less extreme versions, as former CONs, or as objects in a transition phase to become CONs." (Sources just below the limit)
- Alternative: Line asymmetries may arise from lopsided material distributions rather than noncircular motions  
  > "We note, however, that these asymmetries may also be due to lopsided distributions of material in the nucleus rather than noncircular motions." (Do all compact obscured nuclei have in- and outflows?)
- How they decided it was enough: Three sub-LIRGs with existing HCN-vib 4-3 nondetections were not reobserved in 3-2  
  > "as the line was not detected in any of the sources we did not reobserve them in the $J=3\text{--}2$ transition." (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Far-infrared selection biases the sample toward cooler SEDs  
  > "The fact that our sample is based on far-infrared luminosities means that it is biased toward sources with cooler SEDs." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): The high-luminosity parent sample is not fully volume limited at the adopted threshold  
  > "For the distance limit in the high-luminosity bin, however, the RBGS is only volume limited for $L_{\mathrm{FIR}}\gtrsim1.8\times10^{12}$ L$_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$." (Sample)
- Limitation (authors): IC 4687/6 and NGC 4568/7 were not observed  
  > "However, observations have not been obtained of the galaxy pairs IC 4687/6 in the LIRG sample and NGC 4568/7 in the sub-LIRG sample." (Sample)
- Limitation (authors): Empirical CON threshold is somewhat arbitrary and depends on a sufficiently strong exciting power source  
  > "With an empirically based, but still somewhat arbitrary, limit separating CONs from non-CONs" (Sources just below the limit); "a CON also has a power source that is strong enough to produce the mid-infrared radiation field required to excite HCN-vib." (How to find compact obscured nuclei)
- Limitation (authors): HCN-vib surface brightness does not uniquely determine physical column density or size  
  > "a measured HCN-vib surface brightness can not be directly translated into physical properties such as the column density and size of an observed source." (How to find compact obscured nuclei)
- Limitation (authors): Host inclinations are uncertain in disturbed systems and may not trace nuclear orientations  
  > "An important caveat is that inclinations are difficult to determine in the disturbed interacting galaxies that are common in the most luminous systems." (Distributions of host galaxy properties); "However, we caution that the nuclear orientation may not be well connected to that of the larger scale structure (cite: pja17)." (Distributions of host galaxy properties)
- Limitation (authors): IRAS 17208-0014 line blending hinders faint-outflow searches  
  > "For IRAS 17208-0014, this is hindered by the large line widths which cause severe blending with nearby lines of CH$_{2}$NH and vibrationally excited HC$_{3}$N" (Noncircular motions in galaxies with compact obscured nuclei)
- Limitation (authors): The compared greenhouse models assume spherical symmetry and no clumpiness
