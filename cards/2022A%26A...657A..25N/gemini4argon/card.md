## Science

- Fields: high_z_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: What are the radial profiles and exponential scale lengths of the star formation rate, stellar mass, molecular gas mass, and dust mass surface densities in two strongly lensed galaxies at z ~ 1 on sub-kiloparsec scales, and how do they compare to local and other high-redshift galaxies?  
  > "We study the radial profiles of SFR, $M_\star$, and $M_{\mathrm{mol}}$ surface densities of these high-redshift galaxies and compare the corresponding exponential scale lengths with those of local galaxies." (abstract); "For both galaxies, we combine observations obtained with HST and ALMA and observations of the Cosmic Snake obtained with SINFONI and the Herschel space observatory in order to measure the radial profiles of their surface densities" (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct galactocentric radius masks in the image plane via gravitational lens modelling**: Create concentric elliptical rings centred on the galaxy centre in the source plane, project the mask onto the image plane using the Lenstool gravitational lens model, and compute ring areas in the image plane  
  > "We created a mask of concentric elliptical rings centred on the centre of the galaxy in the source plane (ellipses on the left of Fig. 5). Then, we projected this mask onto the image plane using the lens model of the galaxy" (Recovery of the galactocentric radius); "The surface of each ring ($S_{\rm ring}$) was computed following $S_{\mathrm{ring}} = N_{\mathrm{pix, ring}} \cdot p^2$, where $N_{\mathrm{pix, ring}}$ is the number of pixels in a given ring in the image plane and $p$ is the pixel size in physical units." (Recovery of the galactocentric radius)
   - software: Lenstool
   - parameter: Ring width in image plane >= half the beam size (350 pc for CS HST, 400 pc for A521 HST, 300 pc for CS ALMA, 400 pc for A521 ALMA, 800 pc for CS SINFONI)  
  > "Following the prescriptions of (cite: leroy_star_2008) for the local galaxies, we required that the width of the rings in the image plane was at least equal to half the size of the beam." (Recovery of the galactocentric radius)
   - assumption: Surface brightness is constant inside each ring and conserved through gravitational lensing  
  > "We assumed the surface brightness to be constant inside each ring, hence it is conserved through gravitational lensing. We can thus infer the surface density of various physical quantities without lensing corrections." (Recovery of the galactocentric radius)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with pixel-by-pixel lensing-corrected radial profiles; deviations are <5% for most points and never >10%  
  > "The resulting radial profiles deviate by less than $5\%$ for most data points and by never more than $10\%$ (only for a few data points) from the radial profiles obtained when a constant surface brightness was assumed." (Recovery of the galactocentric radius)
2. **Subtract foreground cluster galaxy contamination and extract radial HST photometry**: Model bright foreground cluster members with a sum of two 2D Sersic profiles recursively and subtract them (or mask out overlapping galaxy with a 2-sigma contour ellipse in A521), then measure surface brightness in each elliptical ring across HST bands  
  > "We computed the contamination by modelling the cluster members with a sum of two 2D Sérsic profiles each." (Cosmic Snake arc); "we traced a contour at $\mathrm{2\sigma}$ detection around the foreground galaxy and fitted an ellipse to it (using a least-squares algorithm). All pixels inside the fitted ellipse were excluded from the flux integration of the A521 arc." (A521 arc); "We defined a mask of 20 elliptical rings covering the range from $\SI{0}{kpc}$ to $\SI{7}{kpc}$ in the source plane with a step of $\SI{350}{pc}$, corresponding to our average HST spatial resolution." (Cosmic Snake arc)
   - parameter: 20 elliptical rings of 350 pc step (0 to 7 kpc) for Cosmic Snake; 20 elliptical rings of 400 pc step (0 to 8 kpc) for A521  
  > "We defined a mask of 20 elliptical rings covering the range from $\SI{0}{kpc}$ to $\SI{7}{kpc}$ in the source plane with a step of $\SI{350}{pc}$, corresponding to our average HST spatial resolution." (Cosmic Snake arc); "Therefore we used a mask covering the range from $\SI{0}{kpc}$ to $\SI{8}{kpc}$ of 20 elliptical rings with a step of $\SI{400}{pc}$ to match half of the average HST spatial resolution." (A521 arc)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm and least-squares statistic for 2D Sersic profile fitting, masking galactocentric radii below 4 kpc  
  > "The fit minimisation was performed using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm and least-squares statistic. A mask was applied on the Cosmic Snake arc to exclude from the fit the regions at galactocentric radii below $\SI{4}{kpc}$" (Residuals of the HST images of the Cosmic Snake)
   - validation: Tested 1-sigma and 3-sigma detection ellipses for the masked A521 cluster member to compute uncertainty; weighted residual RMS for Sersic subtractions  
  > "To compute the uncertainty associated with the ellipse defining the pixels to be excluded from the flux integration over the A521 arc, we integrated the fluxes considering ellipses at $\mathrm{1\sigma}$ and $\mathrm{3\sigma}$ detection limits." (A521 arc)
3. **Fit FIR SED with modified black-body and derive radial dust mass and IR luminosity profiles**: Fit a modified black-body (MBB) law over the integrated Herschel + ALMA rest-frame 650 um FIR SED of the Cosmic Snake, integrate from 8 to 1000 um for L_IR, and scale by ALMA 650 um continuum flux in 4 radial rings to get Sigma M_dust and ring L_IR  
  > "We performed a MBB fit over the integrated FIR SED of the Cosmic Snake, including the Herschel data and the ALMA rest-frame $\SI{650}{\mu m}$ measurement (Fig. 6)." (Molecular gas and dust mass surface densities); "we scaled the best-fitted FIR SED according to the ALMA rest-frame $\SI{650}{\mu m}$ dust continuum flux measurement in each ring in order to compute the radial profile of $\Sigma M_{\mathrm{dust}}$." (Molecular gas and dust mass surface densities)
   - parameter: 4 rings of width 300 pc from 0 to 1.2 kpc; integration wavelength range 8 to 1000 um; observer-frame frequency 230 GHz  
  > "We used a mask of four rings with a width of $\SI{300}{pc}$ ranging from $\SI{0}{kpc}$ to $\SI{1.2}{kpc}$ where we detect the rest-frame $\SI{650}{\mu m}$ continuum." (Molecular gas and dust mass surface densities)
   - assumption: Dust opacity kappa_0 = 1.6 m^2 kg^-1; integrated MBB parameters (nu_0, beta, T_dust) are valid in each concentric ring; absorbed UV flux is reprocessed locally within the same ring

## Qualitative findings

- Despite having similar galaxy-integrated M_star, SFR, and M_mol, the Cosmic Snake has steep radial profiles with short scale lengths (0.5-1.5 kpc), whereas A521 has much flatter profiles with scale lengths 3 to 10 times larger (4-6 kpc).  
  > "We find that the scale lengths in the Cosmic Snake are about $\SI{0.5}{kpc}-\SI{1.5}{kpc}$, and they are 3 to 10 times larger in A521. This is a significant difference knowing that the two galaxies have comparable integrated properties." (abstract)
- The scale lengths of both z ~ 1 galaxies fall within the wide distribution observed for local galaxies (0.5 to 5.5 kpc), with the Cosmic Snake at the short end and A521 at the long end.  
  > "The Cosmic Snake lies at the short end of the local scale length distribution, whereas A521 is at the long end of the local scale length distribution." (Conclusions)
- Both the Cosmic Snake and A521 satisfy the local empirical scaling relation between stellar mass surface density within the half-light radius (mu_star^HLR) and total M_star.  
  > "Both the Cosmic Snake and A521 satisfy the relation between $\mu_\star^{\mathrm{HLR}}$ and $M_\star$ defined empirically by the distribution of local galaxies (cite: kauffmann_dependence_2003; gonzalez_delgado_califa_2015)." (Morphological parameters of the Cosmic Snake and A521)
- The normalisations of the Sigma M_mol and Sigma SFR radial profiles in the Cosmic Snake and A521 are offset upwards by up to a factor of 20 (~20 for Cosmic Snake, ~4-10 for A521) compared to z=0 counterparts, consistent with the Kennicutt-Schmidt law.  
  > "the normalisation of the $\Sigma M_{\mathrm{mol}}$ profiles of the $z=1$ galaxies is offset towards high surface densities by $\sim 20$ times for the Cosmic Snake and $\sim 4-10$ times for A521, and this is similar for the $\Sigma \mathrm{SFR}$ radial profiles." (Comparison of radial profiles with local galaxies and other high-z objects)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Strong gravitational lensing combined with matched 0.15''-0.2'' angular resolution HST and ALMA observations enables sub-kiloparsec (~30 pc and ~100 pc) spatial resolution at z ~ 1, comparable to observations of nearby galaxies.  
  > "HST and ALMA observations have similar angular resolutions of $0.15\ensuremath{''}-0.2\ensuremath{''}$, which with the help of strong gravitational lensing enable us to reach spatial resolutions down to $\sim \SI{30}{pc}$ and $\sim \SI{100}{pc}$ in these two galaxies, respectively." (abstract)
- Why this method: Projecting source-plane concentric elliptical rings to the image plane exploits surface brightness conservation under gravitational lensing, allowing surface densities to be measured without pixel-by-pixel magnification corrections.  
  > "Because the surface brightness is conserved through lensing, surface densities of physical quantities can be compared without the need to correct for lensing." (Introduction); "We assumed the surface brightness to be constant inside each ring, hence it is conserved through gravitational lensing. We can thus infer the surface density of various physical quantities without lensing corrections." (Recovery of the galactocentric radius)
- Alternative: Computing pixel-by-pixel lensing-corrected radial profiles instead of assuming constant surface brightness per ring; tested and found to deviate by <5% for most points (<10% max).  
  > "To verify whether this hypothesis is correct, we derived for each quantity the lensing-corrected radial profile. To do this, we divided the brightness and the size of each pixel by its magnification obtained from the gravitational lens model" (Recovery of the galactocentric radius)
- Alternative: Modelling the overlapping cluster galaxy ('+') on the A521 arc with a Sersic profile was rejected because it would result in a large uncertainty; instead, pixels inside a 2-sigma contour ellipse were masked out.  
  > "Modelling this last cluster member would result in a large uncertainty. Therefore we instead cut out part of the mask at the location of the cluster galaxy, so that the contaminated pixels were left out from the flux integration." (A521 arc)
- Alternative: Using SINFONI H-alpha data for A521 and Herschel FIR data for A521; both were not used because A521 is too faint in H-alpha for spatial study and has no 1.3 mm ALMA continuum detection.  
  > "A521 was also observed with SINFONI (cite: patricio_resolved_2019), but the galaxy is not bright enough in $\mathrm{H\alpha}$ to spatially study its emission." ($\mathrm{H\alpha}$ observations with SINFONI); "A521 was also observed with Herschel, but we did not use Herschel data for A521." (Herschel data)
- Alternative: Fitting the exponential scale length of Sigma M_mol in A521 only over the inner 0 to 2.1 kpc (before the spiral arm bump at 4-5 kpc) instead of the full 6 kpc detection radius, yielding l_mol = 1.3 +/- 0.2 kpc.  
  > "Another way of measuring the scale length is to fit an exponential only on the points before the bump at radii from $\SI{0}{kpc}$ to $\SI{2.1}{kpc}$." (Radial profiles and scale lengths of the Cosmic Snake and A521)
- How they decided it was enough: Computed convergence radii (r_conv) and verified they are below about half of the detection radii (r_det), and checked that convolving maps and changing ring widths altered scale lengths by at most 25%.  
  > "All values we found are within $\SI{25}{\%}$ of the scale length presented in this work. The spatial resolution therefore does not bias the measurement of the scale length" (Radial profiles and scale lengths of the Cosmic Snake and A521); "All our $r_{\mathrm{conv}}$ are found to be below about half of the respective $r_{\mathrm{det}}$, with the exception of $\Sigma M_{\mathrm{mol}}$ in A521, which we discuss below. We conclude that the sensitivity of our observations is good enough to allow accurate scale length measurements." (Radial profiles and scale lengths of the Cosmic Snake and A521)
- Limitation (authors): Slightly less than 50% of the Cosmic Snake source galaxy is not visible in the highly magnified arc (only the northern part is lensed into the arc).  
  > "All of them correspond to the northern part of the source galaxy. The northern and southern regions are images of slightly more than half of the galaxy, whereas the central region shows two highly magnified counter-images, both with fewer than 20% of the galaxy. Slightly less than 50% of the source galaxy is not visible in the arc." (Cosmic Snake and A521 galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): No 1.3 mm dust continuum is detected in A521, preventing a radial profile of Sigma M_dust or an L_IR constraint on the radial SED fits for A521.  
  > "No $\SI{1.3}{mm}$ continuum is detected in A521." (CO and dust continuum observations with ALMA); "As we have no $L_{\mathrm{IR}}$ radial profile to constrain the SED fit for A521, we assumed that this choice of priors is valid at all radii." (A521 arc)
- Limitation (authors): The choice of SED priors is the main source of uncertainty on Sigma SFR and Sigma M_star.  
  > "The HyperZ code yields errors on the derived $\Sigma \mathrm{SFR}$ and $\Sigma M_\star$, but our choice of priors is the main source of uncertainty here." (Cosmic Snake arc)
- Limitation (authors): The PdBI CO(2-1) scale length in the Cosmic Snake (4.3 kpc) is very uncertain because it relies on only two radial data points with ring spacing smaller than half the beam.  
  > "We find a significant difference between the CO(4–3) and CO(2–1) scale lengths, as we measure a scale length for CO(2–1) of $\SI{4.3}{kpc}$. However, this result is very uncertain because we have only two datapoints for CO(2–1)." (Radial profiles and scale lengths of the Cosmic Snake and A521)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size of high-redshift galaxies with resolved radial profiles is small, so more measurements are needed to confirm the scale length distribution and morphological trends.  
  > "More measurements are necessary to support these preliminary results and to highlight possible morphological trends, such as spiral arms or starbursts (as in sub-millimetre galaxies), or any other trend tracing the evolutionary stage of the galaxy." (Comparison of radial profiles with local galaxies and other high-z objects)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Molecular gas masses and radial profiles rely on CO(4-3) rather than CO(1-0) or CO(2-1), assuming a spatially uniform excitation ratio r_4,1 = 0.33 and constant Milky Way alpha_CO; radial excitation gradients (hinted at by the longer PdBI CO(2-1) scale length in the Cosmic Snake) could artificially steepen the CO(4-3) profile.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass radial profile in the Cosmic Snake assumes a radially constant dust temperature (T_dust = 38 K) and emissivity (beta = 2.2) derived from the galaxy-integrated FIR SED.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For the Cosmic Snake arc, radial profiles are measured only on the northern half of the galaxy that is lensed into the fold/cusp arc, whereas integrated properties come from the northeast counter-image.
