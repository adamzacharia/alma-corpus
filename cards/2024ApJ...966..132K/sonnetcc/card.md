## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and image ALMA CO(2-1) data into cubes**: Calibrate visibilities in CASA and image into CO(2-1) cubes with 20 km/s channels (NGC 4786, robust 0.5, 0.05 arcsec pixels) and 10 km/s channels (NGC 5193, 0.035 arcsec pixels)  
  > "and then imaged into a data cube with 20 $\mathrm{km}\, \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ velocity channel spacings" (2.1 ALMA Observations); "The $uv$-plane visibilities were calibrated in CASA version 5.1.1, and then imaged into a data cube with 10 $\mathrm{km}\, \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ velocity channel spacings, with a pixel size of $0\hbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}{035}$." (2.1 ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: NGC 4786 robust = 0.5; other tclean settings not stated  
  > "using a robust parameter of 0.5." (2.1 ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Process HST WFC3 images and build PSFs**: Process through calwf3, combine and align with AstroDrizzle (H-band to 0.08 arcsec pixel scale, J-band aligned to it), construct dithered and drizzled TinyTim PSFs  
  > "To start, we drizzled and aligned the flat-fielded $H$-band images to a pixel scale of $0\hbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}{08}$" (2.2 HST Observations); "model point-spread functions (PSFs) that were dithered and drizzled in the same fashion as the $H$-band image." (2.2 HST Observations)
   - software: calwf3, AstroDrizzle, TinyTim
   - parameter: H-band drizzled pixel scale 0.08 arcsec  
  > "To start, we drizzled and aligned the flat-fielded $H$-band images to a pixel scale of $0\hbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}{08}$" (2.2 HST Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: H-J alignment to within ~0.2 subpixels based on inspection of the J-H maps  
  > "interpolated to align them to within ${\sim}0.2$ subpixels of accuracy based on inspection of the $J-H$ maps" (2.2 HST Observations)
3. **Fit unmasked and dust-masked H-band MGE host models**: Mask neighbors, stars and artifacts (and, for dust-masked, regions where J-H > 0.88 mag); initial MgeFit MGE then used as starting guesses for a GALFIT MGE fit including the TinyTim H-band PSF; 14 Gaussians for NGC 4786 and 8 for NGC 5193  
  > "We first modeled the 2D surface brightness with routines from the" (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models); "typically corresponding to areas where $J-H > 0.88$ mag" (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models); "Our MGE solutions contain fourteen Gaussian components for NGC 4786 and eight Gaussian components for NGC 5193" (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models)
   - software: MgeFit, GALFIT, TinyTim
   - parameter: lower bound on MGE axial ratios of 0.69 (NGC 4786) and 0.75 (NGC 5193), allowing deprojection to inclinations as low as 46 and 51 deg  
  > "we set a lower bound on the possible MGE component axial ratios of 0.69 and 0.75 for NGC 4786 and NGC 5193." (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models)
   - assumption: oblate, axisymmetric galaxy; all Gaussians share the same center and position angle; no central PSF (AGN) component  
  > "Our MGE models assume that the galaxy has an oblate and axisymmetric shape, and that each Gaussian component shares the same center and position angle." (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models); "we decided not to include a central PSF component in our model for either galaxy’s central surface brightness distribution." (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: model and data isophotes, major-axis surface brightness profiles and ellipticity profiles compared  
  > "Examination of the 2D isophotes in Figure [2](#fig:Isophotes) shows that the model isophotes are an excellent match to those seen in the" (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models)
4. **Build dust-corrected MGE from Nuker fit and J-H extinction**: Fit 2D Nuker model to the central 10x10 arcsec with GALFIT; estimate A_H from J-H color excess using a thin embedded-screen model with a third-order polynomial inversion of the Delta(J-H) versus A_V curve and A_H = 0.175 A_V; boost the central nine pixels, refit Nuker with fixed alpha, beta, r_b and tuned gamma; replace dust-disk pixels with the Nuker model and refit an MGE  
  > "We fit a 2D Nuker model (cite: 1997AJ....114.1771F) to the innermost" (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models); "We fit the color excess curve with a third-order polynomial up to the turnover point." (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models); "This was done to anchor the model fit to the observed values at the center." (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
   - software: GALFIT, MgeFit
   - parameter: median J-H outside the disk 0.81 mag (NGC 4786) and 0.82 mag (NGC 5193); f = b = 0.50 along the major axis; A_H = 0.175 A_V  
  > "a median $J-H$ color of 0.81 mag and 0.82 mag outside the dust disks of NGC 4786 and NGC 5193, respectively" (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models); "Finally, we set $A_H = 0.175A_V$ based on the standard interstellar extinction law described in (cite: 1985ApJ...288..618R)" (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
   - assumption: embedded-screen model with an infinitely thin disk; equal light fractions in front of and behind the disk; lower A_V root chosen  
  > "Along the major axis of each disk, the fractions of light originating in front of and behind of the dust disk are assumed to be equal ($f = b = 0.50$)." (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models); "we assumed the lower $A_V$ value, as the higher value implies that effectively all of the light originating behind the disk is lost due to extinction." (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
   - fitting: Nuker gamma tuned so that central model pixels match the boosted data pixels: gamma = 0.11 (NGC 4786), 0.75 (NGC 5193)  
  > "For NGC 4786, this value is $\gamma = 0.11$, and for NGC 5193 it is $\gamma = 0.75$." (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
   - validation: tested with the central four pixels instead of nine and found no significant difference  
  > "We also tested this procedure with the central four pixels as well and found no significant difference between the two cases." (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
5. **Derive gas mass profiles from the CO(2-1) flux**: Multiply the cube by a 3DBarolo 3D mask and sum along the spectral axis for an integrated-flux map, average on elliptical annuli, convert to CO(1-0) luminosity, apply alpha_CO and helium correction  
  > "multiply the data cube with a 3D mask generated by the" (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties); "we average the flux on elliptical annuli centered on the disk centers." (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = 0.7; alpha_CO = 3.1; f_He = 0.36  
  > "assuming a CO(2$-$1)/CO(1$-$0) line ratio of 0.7 (cite: 1999AJ....117.1995L)" (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties); "where we set $f_{\mathrm{He}} = 0.36$" (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties)
   - assumption: most appropriate alpha_CO for ETGs is unknown; M_gas values are approximations  
  > "though it should be noted that the most appropriate $\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}$ value for ETGs is unknown" (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: statistical uncertainty of integrated flux from Monte Carlo simulations; gas mass has a small effect on the M_BH error budget  
  > "These statistical uncertainties are calculated through Monte Carlo simulations" (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties)
6. **Fit thin-disk gas-dynamical models to the ALMA cubes**: Build circular-velocity field from BH (point mass), deprojected MGE stars (scaled by sqrt(Upsilon_H)) and gas disk; project to LOS with Gaussian line profiles; weight by a deconvolved CO flux map; convolve each channel with the synthesized beam; minimize chi^2 against block-averaged data within elliptical regions  
  > "The BH masses are measured by modeling the observed gas kinematics in the ALMA data cubes with a thin, rotating disk model." (4 Dynamical Modeling); "At each point in the disk, we model the emergent CO line profile as a Gaussian" (4 Dynamical Modeling); "On these block-averaged scales, we minimized $\chi^2 = \sum_{i = 1}^{N} \left[(d_i - m_i)/\sigma_i\right]^2$" (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - software: JamPy, scikit-image, astropy, 3DBarolo
   - parameter: nine free parameters minimum: M_BH, Upsilon_H, i, Gamma, (x_c, y_c), F0, sigma_0 (plus systemic velocity)  
  > "Our models use a minimum of nine free parameters which include BH mass $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$, $H$-band mass-to-light ratio $\Upsilon_H$, disk inclination angle $i$, disk position angle $\Gamma$" (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - parameter: model grid oversampled by factor s = 3; five Richardson-Lucy iterations to deconvolve the flux map; data cubes block-averaged by a factor of 4  
  > "on a grid that is oversampled by a factor of $s = 3$ relative to the size of the spatial pixels in the data cube" (4 Dynamical Modeling); "applied five iterations of the Richardson-Lucy algorithm implemented in the" (4 Dynamical Modeling); "we further block-averaged the cubes by a factor of 4" (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - parameter: fit regions: ellipse q = 0.50 with semimajor axis 0.55 arcsec (NGC 4786) and 1.0 arcsec (NGC 5193); 31 channels / 372 data points (NGC 4786), 55 channels / 3190 data points (NGC 5193)  
  > "Our elliptical fitting regions have an axial ratio of $q = 0.50$ and semimajor axis lengths of $a = 0\hbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}{55}$ and $a=1\hbox{\ensuremath{.\!\!^{\prime\prime}}}{0}$ for NGC 4786 and NGC 5193, respectively." (4 Dynamical Modeling); "We fit our dynamical models to 31 frequency channels in the NGC 4786 data cube" (4 Dynamical Modeling); "which gives an overall total of 3190 data points used in the $\chi^2$ minimization." (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - assumption: MGE deprojection assumes oblate axisymmetric galaxies with inclinations of 70 deg (NGC 4786) and 60 deg (NGC 5193) from initial fits; distance fixed  
  > "are oblate, axisymmetric, and have inclination angles of $70^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$, respectively" (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - assumption: dark matter neglected; spatially uniform turbulent dispersion sigma_0; thin gas disk  
  > "We neglect the contribution of dark matter as the enclosed mass profiles on scales comparable to the circumnuclear disks are thought to be dominated by the stars." (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - fitting: chi^2 minimization with a 3D noise model (per-channel rms of emission-free pixels divided by the primary beam); optimizer, priors and bounds not stated  
  > "We first calculated the noise in each frequency channel as the standard deviation of emission-free pixels on the block-averaged scale." (4 Dynamical Modeling); "The 3D noise model is then generated by dividing the uniform rms noise value by the primary beam for each frequency channel." (4 Dynamical Modeling)
   - validation: data and model compared via PVDs, moment maps and CO line profiles; reduced chi^2 reported with degrees of freedom

## Qualitative findings

- Both galaxies host circumnuclear CO disks in orderly rotation with projected line-of-sight velocities of about 270 km/s, but neither shows a central rise in rotation speed  
  > "The central region in each galaxy contains a circumnuclear disk that exhibits orderly rotation" (abstract); "neither galaxy exhibits a central rise in rotation speed" (abstract)
- Both disks show a deficit of CO emission at the center; the models predict higher central CO surface brightness than observed  
  > "An additional factor in the measurement uncertainty is the apparent deficit of CO emission in the central region of both galaxies." (6.1 The Impact of Resolving the BH Sphere of Influence)
- Dynamically derived Upsilon_H values are consistent with a Salpeter IMF  
  > "The dynamically determined $\Upsilon_H$ values are consistent with single stellar population models assuming a (cite: 1955ApJ...121..161S) IMF." (5.2 NGC 5193 Dynamical Modeling Results)
- The unmasked MGE shows an unphysical bump in the enclosed stellar mass profile, a limitation of MGEs aggravated by dust  
  > "The bump is unphysical and is a limitation of MGEs that is exacerbated by both the annular extinction of the dust" (6.1 The Impact of Resolving the BH Sphere of Influence)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Galaxies selected for a smooth, round circumnuclear dust disk in HST images, with CO(2-1) showing disklike rotation, to probe scales near r_SOI  
  > "The selection was based on the identification of a smooth and morphologically round circumnuclear dust disk from optical Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: H-band used for the host-galaxy model because dust attenuation is reduced at longer wavelengths; GALFIT chosen for the final MGEs because it allows an asymmetric PSF  
  > "because dust attenuation is reduced at longer wavelengths." (3 Host Galaxy Modeling); "because it allows for an asymmetric 2D PSF to be incorporated in the modeling process" (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models)
- Alternative: A central PSF component for an unresolved nuclear source was considered and rejected in the GALFIT models  
  > "Based on these findings, we decided not to include a central PSF component in our model for either galaxy’s central surface brightness distribution." (3.1 Dust-Masked and Unmasked MGE Models)
- Alternative: Adjusting HST pixel fluxes directly and fitting an MGE to the adjusted image was rejected in favor of Nuker-model adjustments, because it can create unphysical discontinuities  
  > "We prefer using Nuker models to perform these adjustments as opposed to simply adjusting the observed flux values in the HST image and fitting an MGE to this adjusted image directly" (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
- How they decided it was enough: Fit region extended to nearly the whole disk because of the small number of resolution elements; best-fit (lowest chi^2) dust-corrected models adopted as fiducial  
  > "Given the small number of resolution elements across each disk, we opted to fit our models to nearly the full extent of each disk" (4 Dynamical Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Models do not tightly constrain M_BH because the SOI is unresolved  
  > "our models do not tightly constrain the central black hole mass in either galaxy" (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): The extinction correction is a simple method; proper treatment would need radiative transfer models  
  > "a proper treatment of determining the intrinsic stellar light distribution in the presence of a dusty circumnuclear disk likely requires the usage of radiative transfer models" (3.2 Dust-Corrected MGE Models)
- Limitation (authors): The fiducial models are not formally acceptable fits (reduced chi^2 above the 0.05 significance threshold)  
  > "a formally acceptable fit would achieve $\ensuremath{\chi^2_{\mathrm{\nu}}}\leq 1.042$ assuming a level of significance of 0.05" (5.2 NGC 5193 Dynamical Modeling Results)
- Limitation (authors): The most appropriate alpha_CO for ETGs is unknown, so gas masses are approximations  
  > "though it should be noted that the most appropriate $\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}$ value for ETGs is unknown" (2.1.1 Circumnuclear Disk Properties)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reduced chi^2 of 1.4 and 2.1 indicate the model is incomplete (central CO deficit, line-profile structure), so the quoted statistical errors from noise-only Monte Carlo are optimistic.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The final systematic ranges are partly asymmetric and defined by combining three discrete MGE models; the combination rule is not a likelihood or posterior.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): F0 = 1.54 for NGC 4786 differs strongly from unity and the alternate flux map changes inclination by more than 10 deg, suggesting degeneracy among flux map, inclination and M_BH.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distance for NGC 4786 comes from a Hubble-flow estimate with H0 = 73 that carries ~15% uncertainty, and M_BH scales linearly with it.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust correction uses a thin embedded-screen model with f = b = 0.5 and no radiative transfer.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed MGE deprojection inclination (70 and 60 deg) differs from fitted inclination in some models.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): sigma_0 below channel width is poorly constrained at these channel spacings.
