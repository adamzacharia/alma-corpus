## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and flux-calibrate the H-alpha maps**: Background plane subtraction, seeing matching, Gaia DR2 astrometry check, iterative R-band continuum subtraction, filter-transmission and [NII] correction, Galactic extinction correction (same steps for WFI and SINGS data)  
  > "For consistency, the same steps have been applied both to the WFI and SINGS data." (2.3 Star formation tracer); "We then fit the residual sky background with a plane." (2.3 Star formation tracer); "We repeat this procedure until the successive continuum estimates differ by less than 1 per cent." (2.3 Star formation tracer)
   - parameter: Sky masking above 3 sigma over median flux; astrometric precision target 0.3 arcsec, achieved 0.1-0.2 arcsec  
  > "masking all emission more than $3\sigma$ above the median flux of the masked image" (2.3 Star formation tracer); "we adopt a target value of $0.3\ensuremath{''}$ for the absolute astrometric precision" (2.3 Star formation tracer); "The resulting astrometric precision is $0.1\ensuremath{''}{-}0.2\ensuremath{''}$" (2.3 Star formation tracer)
   - assumption: Uniform 30 per cent [NII] contamination for all galaxies  
  > "we first assume a uniform contamination of 30 per cent due to the" (2.3 Star formation tracer)
   - assumption: Galactic foreground extinction with Fitzpatrick reddening law, R_V = 3.1; no spatially resolved internal extinction correction  
  > "assuming a (cite: Fitzpatrick1999) reddening law with $R_V = 3.1$" (2.3 Star formation tracer); "Note that we do not carry out a spatially-resolved correction for internal extinction of the H$\alpha$ line" (2.3 Star formation tracer)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Astrometry checked against Gaia DR2 stars  
  > "has been assessed by matching stellar sources to the Gaia DR2 catalogue" (2.3 Star formation tracer)
2. **Prepare the CO and H-alpha maps and masks**: Convolve both maps to a common resolution and pixel grid; mask galaxy centre (and bar for NGC3351, NGC4535), outskirts, stars and background galaxies; use the PHANGS broad integrated-intensity CO map  
  > "Both maps are convolved to the same resolution and matched to the same pixel grid before running the analysis." (3.2 Application of analysis method); "We use the "broad" integrated intensity maps of the PHANGS v1.0 data release" (2.2 Molecular gas tracer)
   - software: HEISENBERG
   - parameter: Central region defined by eye; extended over the bar for NGC3351 and NGC4535  
  > "We define a central region by eye to exclude the galactic centre (where independent regions are the most prone to blending)." (3.2 Application of analysis method); "For NGC3351 and NGC4535, this mask is extended to cover the bar region" (3.2 Application of analysis method)
   - parameter: Outskirts excluded beyond the galactocentric radius of the outermost emission peak; NGC628 'headlight' cloud masked  
  > "We also exclude the galaxy outskirts beyond the galactocentric radius of the outermost emission peak identified across both maps" (3.2 Application of analysis method); "We have masked this particular cloud in our analysis in Section" (5.3 Influence of galaxy morphology)
   - assumption: CO emission traces molecular gas, and young stars form from molecular gas  
  > "we assume that CO and molecular gas coexist in time and space, so that CO emission can be used to trace molecular gas" (3.3 Input maps and parameter choices)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive the global SFR per field of view and scale the H-alpha map**: SFR from GALEX far-UV plus WISE 22 micron maps (15 arcsec resolution) with published prescriptions, then a conversion factor between H-alpha flux and total extinction-corrected SFR; used only for the star formation efficiency  
  > "we calculate the SFR from multi-wavelength mapping, combining the Galaxy Evolution Explorer (GALEX) far-ultraviolet band" (2.4 Global SFR); "we use the SFR prescription provided by (cite: Kennicutt2012) and (cite: Jarrett2013)" (2.4 Global SFR); "We note that this conversion factor has no impact on the evolutionary timeline derived in Section" (2.4 Global SFR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Set the reference time-scale and CO-to-H2 conversion from metallicity**: Scale t_star,ref (4.3 Myr at solar metallicity) with gas mass-weighted metallicity; alpha_CO from Sandstrom et al. where available, otherwise a Bolatto et al. metallicity scaling  
  > "We therefore scale the reference time-scale for each galaxy by the mean gas mass-weighted metallicity" (3.5 Metallicity and reference time-scale); "we simply scale the conversion factor with metallicity as suggested by (cite: Bolatto2013)" (3.5 Metallicity and reference time-scale)
   - parameter: Resulting t_star,ref range 4.19-4.53 Myr across the sample  
  > "which translates into a narrow range of associated reference time-scales of $t_{\rm star,ref}=4.19{-}4.53$ Myr" (3.5 Metallicity and reference time-scale)
   - assumption: Fixed ratio CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = 0.7; solar 12+log(O/H) = 8.69  
  > "we adopt a fixed ratio CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = 0.7" (3.5 Metallicity and reference time-scale); "with $12+\log{({\rm O}/{\rm H})}_{\ensuremath{\odot}} = 8.69$ (cite: Asplund2009)" (3.5 Metallicity and reference time-scale)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Identify emission peaks and measure the CO-to-H-alpha flux-ratio bias versus aperture size**: CLUMPFIND peaks in both maps; top-hat convolution to N_ap aperture sizes spaced logarithmically; apertures centred on CO or H-alpha peaks; summed fluxes give the gas-to-SFR ratio relative to the galactic mean (tuning-fork diagram); Monte-Carlo sub-sampling of non-overlapping apertures  
  > "The emission peaks on which the apertures are placed are identified in both maps at the best common resolution, using the algorithm CLUMPFIND (cite: Williams1994)." (3.2 Application of analysis method); "we place apertures on each peak and measure the gas and SFR fluxes within these apertures" (3.2 Application of analysis method); "the flux ratio bias is calculated 1000 times on different Monte-Carlo realisations of sub-samples of independent, non-overlapping apertures, for each peak type and aperture size" (3.2 Application of analysis method)
   - software: HEISENBERG, CLUMPFIND
   - parameter: Per-galaxy l_ap,min (pc): NGC628 50, NGC3351 80, NGC3627 100, NGC4254 140, NGC4303 150, NGC4321 130, NGC4535 130, NGC5068 30, NGC5194 70; l_ap,max, N_ap (15; 12 for NGC5194), N_pix,min and CLUMPFIND flux ranges per galaxy in the input-parameter table  
  > "$l_{\rm ap,min}$ \[pc\] | 50 | 80 | 100 | 140 | 150 | 130 | 130 | 30 | 70" (Table (input parameters)); "The parameters for the peak identification listed here are valid for the diffuse-emission filtered maps" (Table (input parameters) notes)
   - parameter: Non-default HEISENBERG flags: mask_images on, mstar_int mask galaxy centre, mgas_ext mask outer parts where CO not detected  
  > "Flags set to a different value than the default (as listed in table 1 of (cite: Kruijssen2018))." (Table 1 caption); "Mask the centre of the galaxy" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Independent regions are randomly distributed in two dimensions and star formation history is roughly constant over the timeline duration  
  > "We also assume that the regions are randomly distributed in each other’s vicinity" (3.3 Input maps and parameter choices)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Small changes of the peak-identification parameters do not strongly affect the results, as long as physically relevant peaks are identified  
  > "small variations of these numbers do not strongly affect the constrained quantities" (3.3 Input maps and parameter choices)
6. **Fit the statistical model to obtain t_CO, t_fb and the separation length lambda**: Fit the Kruijssen et al. model to the tuning-fork flux-ratio biases (effective uncertainties account for covariance between apertures); absolute times from the metallicity-scaled reference time-scale  
  > "we systematically fit the model from (cite: Kruijssen2018) to the observed gas-to-SFR flux ratios measured as a function of the aperture size" (3.1 General concept); "the duration of one of the two phases needs to be provided as a reference time-scale in order to convert the relative time-scales into absolute ones" (3.2 Application of analysis method)
   - software: HEISENBERG
   - parameter: Three free parameters t_gas (t_CO), t_fb, lambda  
  > "These three quantities are non-degenerate, as they affect the predictions of the model in different ways" (3.2 Application of analysis method)
   - assumption: Reference time-scale of the unembedded H-alpha phase from Haydon et al. instantaneous-burst SPS calibration  
  > "calibrate $t_{\rm star, ref}$ using an SPS model describing an instantaneous burst of star formation, to avoid any dependence on the duration of star formation" (3.2 Application of analysis method)
   - fitting: Reduced-chi2 minimisation over three parameters; marginalised 1D PDFs; uncertainty from 32nd and 68th percentiles of the PDF on each side of the best fit  
  > "The model is fitted to the data points by minimising the reduced-$\chi^2$ over the above three free parameters." (3.2 Application of analysis method); "The resulting three-dimensional probability distribution function (PDF) is marginalised to obtain the one-dimensional PDF for each free parameter." (3.2 Application of analysis method)
   - validation: Check of the Kruijssen et al. 2018 applicability criteria (phase ratio, resolved lambda, at least 35 peaks per bin, no tracer deficit after filtering, roughly constant SFH)  
  > "To validate the accuracy of these values, we verify here that we fulfill the requirements listed in sect. 4.4 of (cite: Kruijssen2018)." (6.1 Accuracy of the results); "we have $\lambda \geq 1.7 l_{\rm ap,min}$ for all galaxies" (6.1 Accuracy of the results)
7. **Filter diffuse emission iteratively and refit**: Gaussian high-pass filter in Fourier space with cut-off 10-12 times lambda, applied to both maps; lambda re-measured and filtering iterated until converged  
  > "For our analysis, we use a Gaussian high-pass filter, which is the best compromise between the selectivity of the filter and the undesired appearance of artefacts around compact regions." (3.4 Diffuse emission); "We then set the characteristic cut-off wavelength of this Gaussian filter to be between $10{-}12\times\lambda$" (3.4 Diffuse emission)
   - software: HEISENBERG
   - parameter: Convergence when lambda changes by less than 5 per cent for at least four successive iterations; n_lambda,iter 10-12 per galaxy (table)  
  > "until convergence is reached (when $\lambda$ varies by less than 5 per cent from the previous iteration, for at least four successive iterations)" (3.4 Diffuse emission)
   - parameter: Filter FWHM set to the smallest multiple of lambda giving q_con >= 0.9; compact-flux fractions corrected with q_con and q_overlap  
  > "we ensure that $q_{\rm con}\geq0.9$, so that the correction to be applied to the compact fraction is relatively small" (Fractions of compact and diffuse emission)
   - assumption: Emission on scales above the independent-region separation does not take part in the evolutionary cycle  
  > "does not participate in the evolutionary cycle of emission peaks described in Section" (3.4 Diffuse emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Criterion that a peak never shows a tracer deficit relative to the galactic mean is satisfied only after filtering  
  > "After the filtering of the diffuse emission (Section" (6.1 Accuracy of the results)
8. **Derive secondary quantities (feedback velocity, star formation efficiency, timeline)**: v_fb = r_CO/t_fb; eps_sf = t_CO Sigma_SFR / Sigma_gas, with Sigma_gas from the filtered CO map; other quantities per Kruijssen et al. 2018  
  > "where $r_{\rm CO}$ is the mean radius of the CO emission peaks determined with HEISENBERG as the standard deviation of a two-dimensional Gaussian" (4.3.3 Feedback velocity); "we obtain $\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ from the filtered CO map, using the conversion factor $X_{\rm gas}$ from Table" (4.3.4 Star formation efficiency)
   - software: HEISENBERG
   - assumption: Only compact (filtered) CO is counted in Sigma_gas, assuming diffuse CO belongs to gas not forming massive stars  
  > "For $\Sigma_{\rm gas}$, we thus take only the compact CO emission into account, because this is the emission for which $t_{\rm CO}$ describes the lifetime." (4.3.4 Star formation efficiency)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Repeat the analysis in galactocentric radial bins**: Non-overlapping radial bins of at least 1 kpc width with at least 50 peaks per tracer; peaks taken from full-map runs and run on the diffuse-filtered maps  
  > "we divide each galaxy into non-overlapping radial bins of a minimum width of 1 kpc" (5.1 Radial profiles of the molecular cloud lifetime); "we supply the peaks identified across the full maps as input for the analysis in each radial bin" (5.1 Radial profiles of the molecular cloud lifetime)
   - software: HEISENBERG
   - parameter: Bin widths increased when needed to reach 50 peaks per tracer  
  > "we require that each bin contains a minimum of 50 peaks identified in our full-galaxy runs for each tracer" (5.1 Radial profiles of the molecular cloud lifetime)
   - assumption: alpha_CO and t_ref evaluated at the mean metallicity in each bin  
  > "we use the appropriate values of ${\alpha}_{\rm CO}$ and $t_{\rm ref}$ corresponding to the mean metallicity in each bin" (3.5 Metallicity and reference time-scale)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: lambda approximately constant between bins supports using the globally filtered maps  
  > "This approach is validated a posteriori by the fact that $\lambda$ (which sets the filtering scale) is approximately constant between each bin for a given galaxy." (5.1 Radial profiles of the molecular cloud lifetime)
10. **Compare t_CO with free-fall, crossing and galactic dynamical time-scales**: Compute t_ff from HEISENBERG GMC radius and mass, t_cr from CPROPS velocity dispersions, and tau_gal from the Jeffreson & Kruijssen harmonic-sum theory using observed Omega, Q, surface densities, shear and spiral pattern speeds  
  > "The GMC radius and mass are derived using the output from HEISENBERG by defining $r_{\rm GMC}\equiv1.91r_{\rm CO}$ (cite: Kruijssen2019)" (5.2 Comparison with analytical models); "Within this theory, the cloud lifetime is set by the large-scale dynamics of the ISM and calculated as the harmonic average of characteristic time-scales associated with the gravitational collapse of the ISM" (5.2 Comparison with analytical models)
   - software: HEISENBERG, CPROPS
   - parameter: Inputs for tau_gal: angular velocity, Toomre Q, gas and stellar surface densities and dispersions, shear, number of spiral arms, pattern speed  
  > "All of the time-scales taken into account in $\tau_{\rm gal}$ depend on observable parameters." (5.2 Comparison with analytical models)
   - assumption: Spherical clouds for the free-fall time; CO flux-weighted averages per radial bin  
  > "for spherical clouds of radius $r_{\rm GMC}$ and molecular gas mass $M_{\rm GMC}$, where $G$ is the gravitational constant" (5.2 Comparison with analytical models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainties on tau_gal obtained by propagating input uncertainties  
  > "including the uncertainties on the predictions obtained by propagating the uncertainties on the input quantities" (5.2 Comparison with analytical models)
11. **Find the gas surface density separating galactic and internal dynamical regimes**: Split radial bins at a varied kpc-scale molecular gas surface density threshold and compare median logarithmic offsets and scatter of t_CO against t_cr, t_ff and tau_gal; adopt the threshold where offsets and scatter change  
  > "we have systematically varied the density threshold at which the sample of radial bins is divided into low and high densities" (Gas surface density threshold appendix); "we adopt a threshold value of $\Sigma_{{\rm H}_2}=8\,M_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$" (Gas surface density threshold appendix)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Result stable to small threshold changes and to outlier removal; transition confined to 7-9 Msun/pc^2  
  > "we have verified that the result is robust against the removal of outliers in the data" (5.4 Galactic dynamics versus internal dynamics)

## Qualitative findings

- All nine galaxies show a pronounced de-correlation between CO and H-alpha that grows toward smaller apertures  
  > "All galaxies in our sample exhibit a pronounced de-correlation between gas emission and SFR emission, which becomes stronger as the aperture size decreases." (4.1 A universal de-correlation)
- No dependence of the timelines on spiral arm strength or number  
  > "We do not find any dependence of the measured evolutionary timelines on the strength or the number of spiral arms in the galaxies of our sample." (6.2 Comparison with previous work)
- Pixel-statistics CO/H-alpha ratios underestimate the lifetime by about a factor of 2  
  > "systematically underestimates the GMC lifetime by a factor of $\sim2$ on average" (6.2 Comparison with previous work)
- Cloud lifetimes can be elevated near the co-rotation radius  
  > "In addition to these general trends, we find that GMC lifetimes can be elevated near the co-rotation radius." (7 Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO(2-1) chosen because it is brighter and easier to map across whole galaxies at arcsecond resolution than dense-gas tracers  
  > "it is brighter and easier to observe than these high density gas tracers, allowing entire galaxies to be mapped efficiently at arcsecond resolution" (2.2 Molecular gas tracer)
- Why this method: The statistical method avoids subjective cloud classification and needs only the region separation length, not individual GMCs, to be resolved  
  > "Rather than needing to resolve individual GMCs, as was the case in previous methods, this new technique only requires resolving the mean separation length of the combined population of GMCs and Hii regions" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Using a CPROPS cloud catalogue for GMC radii and masses was rejected in favour of HEISENBERG outputs  
  > "By contrast, using a cloud catalogue would rely on subjective classification." (5.2 Comparison with analytical models)
- Alternative: The very bright NGC628 'headlight' cloud was masked; leaving it in raises t_CO and makes one bin unconstrained  
  > "and becomes unconstrained in the bin including the ‘headlight’ cloud, with a lower limit of 25.1 Myr" (5.3.1 Co-rotation radius)
- How they decided it was enough: Each radial bin needs at least 50 peaks per tracer for adequate statistics  
  > "we require that each bin contains a minimum of 50 peaks identified in our full-galaxy runs for each tracer, to ensure sufficient statistics to constrain the derived quantities to sufficiently high precision" (5.1 Radial profiles of the molecular cloud lifetime)
- Limitation (authors): No spatially resolved internal extinction correction, so embedded star formation may be missed  
  > "our lack of a spatially-resolved extinction correction may cause us to underestimate emission from young, embedded Hii regions, or fail to detect them at all" (2.3 Star formation tracer)
- Limitation (authors): Feedback times and separation lengths for NGC4254, NGC4303, NGC4321 and NGC4535 may be upper limits because of coarser resolution  
  > "We therefore advise some caution in the interpretation of the feedback time-scales measured for NGC4254, NGC4303, NGC4321, and NGC4535." (4.3.1 Feedback time-scale)
- Limitation (authors): Spatially resolved star formation histories are unknown, so the constant-SFH requirement cannot be tested  
  > "Unfortunately, the spatially-resolved SFHs of the galaxies in our sample are not known." (6.1 Accuracy of the results)
- Limitation (authors): Bursty bins at bar tips violate the method assumptions and bias t_CO  
  > "Both of these extremes bias the measured cloud lifetimes." (5.3.2 Influence of the bar)
- Limitation (authors): The analytical model is simple and does not separate gas phases  
  > "The analytical predictions from (cite: Jeffreson2018) are based on a simple analytical model, which limits the direct comparison with our measurement." (5.4 Galactic dynamics versus internal dynamics)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Peak-identification and aperture parameters were hand-optimised per galaxy; no systematic sensitivity table is given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A uniform 30 per cent [NII] correction and a single global extinction rescaling may bias H-alpha peak fluxes
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 8 Msun/pc^2 regime boundary rests on a few dozen radial bins with no formal significance test
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heavy reliance on unpublished in-prep inputs (PHANGS maps, H-alpha maps, inclinations, CPROPS catalogues)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absolute time-scales depend on one calibrated reference time-scale from a separate simulation paper
