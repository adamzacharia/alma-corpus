- Question: What are the fragmentation properties of the massive star-forming region W51 IRS2 (W51 North) on scales of ~200 AU, and what physical process (e.g. thermal Jeans) controls them?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum**: CASA pipeline calibration, iterative phase and amplitude self-calibration, tclean continuum imaging using line-free channels  
  > "Using the pipeline in CASA (cite: McMullin2007) (Version 4.7), we obtained the visibilities to perform self-calibration and generate the final continuum images." (2 Observational data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5  
  > "a “briggs” weighting ROBUST parameter of 0.5 was set" (2 Observational data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: rms reduction tracked through self-calibration  
  > "The phase-only self-calibrations decreased the root mean square (rms) noise of continuum image from 0.5 mJy beam$^{-1}$ to 0.15 mJy beam$^{-1}$." (2.1 ALMA 3 mm data)
2. **Spectral index map**: CASA immath spix mode on 3 mm and 1.3 mm images tapered to common resolution, 3 sigma cut-off  
  > "The spectral index map was derived from the “spix” mode in the “immath” task of the CASA package" (3.1 Dust emission and spectral index)
   - software: CASA immath
   - parameter: common beam 0.079"x0.053", pixel 0.01"  
  > "we tapered them to the same resolution of 0.079$^{\prime\prime}\times$0.053$^{\prime\prime}$ using a pixel size of 0.01$^{\prime\prime}$." (3.1 Dust emission and spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify fragments**: uv>910 klambda 1.3 mm image; 12 sigma threshold with closed-contour criterion; 2D Gaussian fits; isolated/point-like sources excluded  
  > "we constrained the 1.3 mm high-resolution continuum image by using only visibilities with a *uv* range larger than 910 k$\lambda$" (3.2 Fragment identification); "we assumed an identification threshold of 12$\sigma$[^1] in the *uv*-range constrained continuum image." (3.2 Fragment identification)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: threshold 12 sigma (sigma=0.11 mJy/beam)  
  > "This restriction on the *uv* range results in a rms noise of $\sigma$ = 0.11 mJy beam$^{-1}$." (3.2 Fragment identification)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit per source  
  > "A two-dimensional Gaussian fitting was performed toward each identified source" (3.2 Fragment identification)
   - validation: simobserve simulations and astrodendro comparison (32 vs 33 sources)  
  > "A total of 32 continuum sources were identified by the dendrogram algorithm" (8 Dendrogram)
4. **Fragment masses**: Optically thin mass M = S D^2/(B(Td) kappa); also optically thick estimate  
  > "The masses of the optically thick sources were estimated by $M_{\rm t}$ = $\pi R^{2} N_{\rm H_{2}}m_{\rm H} \mu$" (3.2 Fragment identification)
   - assumption: Td = Tgas = 200 K, kappa=0.008991 cm2/g, D=5128 pc, mu=2.8  
  > "we assume a dust temperature of $T_{\rm d}$ = $T_{\rm gas}$ = 200 K" (3.2 Fragment identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare optically thin and thick median masses (0.4 vs 0.5 Msun)  
  > "This indicates that the optically thin assumption does not strongly affect our results." (3.2 Fragment identification)
5. **MSDC analysis and breaks**: Pair separations in log bins of 0.1; MSDC compared with 1000 uniform random realizations; breaks where MSDC departs from random; linear fit below first break  
  > "we ran 1000 rounds of calculations of MSDC for a uniformly random distribution of sources within the same area." (4.1 Typical separations)
   - parameter: logarithmic bins of log(theta)=0.1  
  > "grouped the angular separations of all companions into different annuli with logarithmic bins of log($\theta$) = 0.1" (4.1 Typical separations)
   - fitting: linear fit to MSDC below first break  
  > "We performed a linear fit toward these points and found a slope of –0.8($\pm$0.1)" (4.1 Typical separations)
   - validation: Compared slope to Pipe Nebula and Lupus I  
  > "very consistent with the values obtained for the linear regime of the MSDC in the Pipe Nebula (–0.68) and the Lupus I cloud (–0.72)" (4.1 Typical separations)
6. **Define ensembles and Jeans analysis**: Circles with radii at the breaks group fragments into SEs/LEs; ensemble masses from 1.3 mm low-res uv 9-910 klambda image; densities, Jeans lengths and masses at 200 K; Jeans number with CFE  
  > "we generated a 1.3 mm low-resolution continuum image using only the *uv* range of 9.164 $\sim$ 910 k$\lambda$." (4.3 Fragmentation)
   - assumption: uniform-density spheres, T=200 K (200-400 K tested)  
  > "Assuming that the identified ensembles are spheres with a uniform density distribution" (4.3 Fragmentation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare Jeans length/mass with typical separations/masses; CFE calibrated on SE1 and applied to other SEs  
  > "Thus, one can calculate $N_\mathrm{Jeans}$ for the other SEs, adopting the same CFE obtained for SE1." (5.1 Thermal Jeans fragmentation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Restrict to uv>910 klambda to isolate compact fragments from extended emission  
  > "to identify the real compact and small-scale fragments, we constrained the 1.3 mm high-resolution continuum image by using only visibilities with a *uv* range larger than 910 k$\lambda$" (3.2 Fragment identification)
- Alternative: Turbulence and magnetic fields as extra support in SE3 considered but disfavored versus higher temperature  
  > "it seems that the most plausible explanation for the low fragmentation found in SE3 is that it might be warmer than the other SEs naturally inhibiting fragmentation." (5.1 Thermal Jeans fragmentation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Fragments may merge in time, changing MSDC slope; no stellar-activity counterparts; temperature assumed uniform  
  > "if a fraction of the fragments merge with time, the slope of the linear regime could change." (5.2 A cluster-like spatial distribution)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uniform 200 K temperature drives masses and Jeans results; temperature tuned to fit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distance uncertainty (~36%) large
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fragment status of 12 sigma sources not confirmed by kinematics or protostellar tracers
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Few ensembles (5 SE, 2 LE), small number statistics
- Caveat (inferred, ours): MSDC breaks defined by eye relative to random distribution; edge effects
