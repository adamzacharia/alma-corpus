## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image the continuum of each project**: Pipeline calibration in CASA, then phase-only self-calibration rounds and one amplitude round; continuum imaged with TCLEAN at Briggs robust 0.5  
  > "we obtained the visibilities to perform self-calibration and generate the final continuum images" (Observational data)
   - software: CASA pipeline 4.7, TCLEAN
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "a “briggs” weighting ROBUST parameter of 0.5 was set" (Observational data)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-calibration reduced rms: 3 mm noise decreased from 0.5 to 0.15 mJy/beam in phase-only rounds  
  > "The phase-only self-calibrations decreased the root mean square (rms) noise of continuum image from 0.5 mJy beam" (ALMA 3 mm data)
2. **make the 3 mm to 1.3 mm spectral index map**: Taper both high-res continuum images to a common beam and compute the spectral index per pixel with immath spix; cut at 3 sigma  
  > "we tapered them to the same resolution of 0.079$^{\prime\prime}\times$0.053$^{\prime\prime}$ using a pixel size of 0.01" (Results, Dust emission and spectral index)
   - software: CASA immath (spix mode)
   - parameter: Common resolution 0.079'' x 0.053''; pixel 0.01''; 3 sigma cut  
  > "we also included a cut-off with rms noises higher than 3" (Results, Dust emission and spectral index)
   - assumption: Spectral index near 2 interpreted as partly optically thick dust; index 0.2 regions interpreted as free-free  
  > "suggesting that the dust emission may be partly optically thick" (Results, Dust emission and spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **identify compact fragments in the uv-constrained 1.3 mm image**: Use 1.3 mm high-res visibilities with uv > 910 kλ; identify sources as 12 sigma contour regions split by at least one closed contour; fit 2D Gaussians to each source  
  > "we assumed an identification threshold of 12$\sigma$" (Fragment identification); "A two-dimensional Gaussian fitting was performed toward each identified source" (Fragment identification)
   - parameter: uv range > 910 kλ; threshold 12 sigma (1.32 mJy/beam)  
  > "the typical intensity of the identified continuum sources" (ALMA simulations)
   - assumption: Source identification by contour criterion is adequate; the 12 sigma threshold is set by the most negative lobe  
  > "12$\sigma$ is the most negative lobe found in the uv-restricted image" (Fragment identification)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fit per source (code not named)  
  > "A two-dimensional Gaussian fitting was performed toward each identified source" (Fragment identification)
   - validation: SIMOBSERVE simulations showed no significant artifacts from the 910 kλ cut; dendrogram cross-check found 32 sources, most matching  
  > "cutting off baselines shorter than 910 k$\lambda$ does not create significant artifacts" (Fragment identification); "A total of 32 continuum sources were identified by the dendrogram algorithm" (Fragments identified using the dendrogram algorithm)
4. **classify isolated and point-like sources**: Sources inside an individual 6 sigma contour of the full-uv image with no extended structure are isolated; intensity to flux ratio above 0.5 marks point-like sources; these are excluded from fragments  
  > "if a source has a ratio of intensity to flux density higher than 0.5, it is considered as an unresolved point-like source" (Fragment identification)
   - parameter: Intensity/flux density ratio > 0.5; 6 sigma full-uv contour  
  > "Finally, 29 out of 33 continuum sources were identified as fragments." (Fragment identification)
   - assumption: Isolated sources are already decoupled from parental structure  
  > "which means that the source was already decoupled from its parental structure" (Fragment identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **estimate fragment masses**: Optically thin dust mass from Gaussian-fit flux density, with optically thick alternative (column density from kappa)  
  > "Assuming that the 1.3 mm continuum emission of the identified sources is optically thin, their masses can be estimated using the flux densities" (Fragment identification)
   - parameter: Distance 5128 pc; dust temperature 200 K; kappa = 0.008991 cm^2 g^-1 at 1.3 mm  
  > "we assume a dust temperature of $T_{\rm d}$ = $T_{\rm gas}$ = 200 K" (Fragment identification)
   - assumption: Dust temperature 200 K uniform across the surveyed region  
  > "that the dust temperature is uniformly distributed in the surveyed region" (Fragment identification)
   - assumption: Optically thick alternative with N_H2 = 2.6e25 cm^-2 at 1.3 mm, mu = 2.8  
  > "the column density of these sources can be estimated by using" (Fragment identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Optically thick masses give median 0.5(±0.3) Msun vs 0.4(±0.3) Msun thin, so the thin assumption does not strongly affect results  
  > "This indicates that the optically thin assumption does not strongly affect our results." (Fragment identification)
6. **measure fragment separations and mean surface density of companions (MSDC)**: Pairwise separations of 29 fragments (406 companions) grouped in logarithmic bins of 0.1; MSDC computed per annulus and compared with 1000 random realizations; breaks identified where MSDC leaves the random level  
  > "Thus, the 29 identified fragments generate 406 companions" (Typical separations between fragments); "we ran 1000 rounds of calculations of MSDC for a uniformly random distribution of sources within the same area" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - parameter: Log bin width 0.1; 1000 random rounds  
  > "logarithmic bins of log($\theta$) = 0.1" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - assumption: Fragments in the uv-constrained image are the relevant population; random reference uses the same surveyed area  
  > "our random sources were generated within the entire surveyed region" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - fitting: Linear fit to MSDC points below the first break, giving slope -0.8(±0.1)  
  > "We performed a linear fit toward these points and found a slope of –0.8($\pm$0.1)" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - validation: Comparison against random-distribution MSDC; slope compared with Pipe Nebula and Lupus I values  
  > "two breaks are found at log($\theta$) = –4.0 (1845 AU) and log($\theta$) = –3.4 (7346 AU)" (Typical separations between fragments)
7. **group fragments into ensembles**: Circles of radius equal to each break scale drawn around each fragment; fragments whose circles contain another fragment's centre are grouped; ensemble centres are geometric means  
  > "If a circle includes the center of any other fragment, we consider them to be related fragments" (Ensembles defined as groups of fragments)
   - parameter: Radii 1845 AU (small-scale ensembles, SEs) and 7346 AU (large-scale ensembles, LEs)  
  > "Based on the above criteria, a total of five ensembles were identified with a spatial scale of 1845 AU." (Ensembles defined as groups of fragments)
   - assumption: Break scales set the size of parental structures  
  > "these breaks reflect the beginnings of clustering phenomena" (Ensembles defined as groups of fragments)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **derive ensemble masses, densities, Jeans lengths and Jeans masses**: Ensemble masses from total flux in each circle of the low-res 1.3 mm image (uv 9-910 kλ) using the optically thin formula; densities for uniform spheres; Jeans length and mass with T = 200 K  
  > "This data set has the largest recoverable angular scale of $\sim10^{\prime\prime}$, which covers the surveyed region very well." (Fragmentation)
   - parameter: mu = 2.8; T = 200 K; spherical uniform density  
  > "Assuming that the identified ensembles are spheres with a uniform density distribution" (Fragmentation)
   - assumption: Ensembles are uniform spheres; T = 200 K  
  > "A dust temperature of $T$ = 200 K was used to estimate the masses of the ensembles" (Fragmentation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Jeans lengths compared with median fragment separations within each ensemble  
  > "The Jeans length obtained for SE1 is consistent (within errors) with the typical separations of fragments within SE1." (Fragmentation)
9. **test thermal Jeans fragmentation with Jeans numbers and core formation efficiency**: N_Jeans = M_ens CFE / M_Jeans; CFE calibrated on SE1 (N_Jeans = 20 observed) and applied to other SEs; temperature raised to 400 K for SE3  
  > "In order to have $N_{\rm Jeans}$ = 20 in SE1, the CFE must be $\sim$24%" (Discussion, Thermal Jeans fragmentation)
   - parameter: CFE ~24% from SE1 calibration  
  > "the CFE must be $\sim$24%, which is consistent with the values found by (cite: Palau2015)" (Discussion, Thermal Jeans fragmentation)
   - assumption: Core formation efficiency same for all SEs; temperatures 200-400 K  
  > "adopting the same CFE obtained for SE1" (Discussion, Thermal Jeans fragmentation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted fragment numbers 4, 5, 4, 2 for SE2-SE5 compared with observed  
  > "The resulting $N_\mathrm{Jeans}$ are 4, 5, 4, and 2 for SE2, SE3, SE4, and SE5, respectively." (Discussion, Thermal Jeans fragmentation)
10. **compare the MSDC slope and clustering-to-binary scale with other clouds and the stellar-density anticorrelation**: Estimate stellar density as fragment count over assumed spherical volume; place W51 IRS2 and OMC-1S on the Simon1997 clustering-to-binary scale versus stellar density relation; compute median fragment density  
  > "by dividing the number of identified fragments in each region by the volume corresponding to the area where these fragments have been identified" (Discussion, A cluster-like spatial distribution)
   - parameter: 50% uncertainty assumed on stellar density  
  > "a 50% uncertainty is assumed for the stellar density estimation" (Discussion, A cluster-like spatial distribution)
   - assumption: Fragments are precursors of protostellar objects  
  > "the fragments identified here are true (precursors of) protostellar objects" (Discussion, A cluster-like spatial distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Fragmentation in W51 IRS2 is consistent with thermal Jeans fragmentation at 200-400 K, with the high temperature attributed to stellar feedback from nearby massive stars  
  > "can be explained through a thermal Jeans process operating at high temperatures of 200–400 K" (abstract)
- Fragmentation in SE1 (W51 North) is the densest and most fragmented ensemble; W51 North appears to be forming a protocluster  
  > "SE1 has the highest fragmentation level and is also the densest ensemble" (Fragmentation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The 1.3 mm high-resolution image has the highest angular resolution, so it is the best option to identify fragments  
  > "The 1.3 mm high-resolution image is the best option to do this job as it has the highest angular resolution of 0.028" (Fragment identification)
- Why this method: The 3 mm image uses only line-free channels to avoid line contamination, and the 1.3 mm low-res data are used for masses because the 3 mm data are affected by free-free emission  
  > "In order to avoid line contamination, the 3 mm continuum image was only generated using the line-free channels." (ALMA 3 mm data); "as the 3 mm data are affected by the free-free emission" (Fragmentation)
- Alternative: Optically thick mass estimate rejected for the main analysis, as thin and thick medians agree  
  > "Hereafter, our calculations are based on the assumption that the dust emission is optically thin." (Fragment identification)
- Alternative: Dendrogram algorithm checked as alternative fragment identification; sources largely consistent  
  > "We found that the majority of continuum sources identified by the dendrogram algorithm are indeed consistent" (Fragments identified using the dendrogram algorithm)
- Alternative: Turbulence and magnetic fields considered as extra support in SE3 and judged less likely than warmer temperature  
  > "In conclusion, it seems that the most plausible explanation for the low fragmentation found in SE3 is that it might be warmer" (Discussion, Thermal Jeans fragmentation)
- How they decided it was enough: Simulations were used to decide that the 910 kλ uv cut does not create significant artifacts while recovering the real compact sources  
  > "Based on the results of our simulations, it seems that cutting off baselines shorter than 910 k$\lambda$ does not create significant artifacts" (Fragment identification)
- Limitation (authors): Fragments may merge with time, which could change the linear-regime slope  
  > "However, one should keep in mind that if a fraction of the fragments merge with time, the slope of the linear regime could change." (Discussion, A cluster-like spatial distribution)
- Limitation (authors): The thermal Jeans result is preliminary and needs further tests  
  > "While this is the first result consistent with thermal Jeans fragmentation, further tests need to be performed to make this result more robust." (Fragmentation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperature 200 K vs 200-400 K range drives all Jeans masses and numbers; the chosen value is essentially a free parameter
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1.3 mm high-res beam (0.031'' x 0.025'') is compared to a uv-cut image without stated beam, so fragment sizes may be uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar density estimate assumes all fragments are protostellar with a 50% uncertainty, which dominates the anticorrelation placement
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one ensemble (SE1) provides a clean match to the Jeans prediction; the others rely on a CFE calibrated on SE1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1.3 mm low-res beam is given as 0.237 x 0.217 arcsec in one place and 0.227 arcsec in another
