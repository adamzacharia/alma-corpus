## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image continuum**: CASA pipeline calibration, iterative phase and amplitude self-calibration, tclean imaging with robust 0.5 of line-free continuum  
  > "Using the pipeline in CASA (cite: McMullin2007) (Version 4.7), we obtained the visibilities to perform self-calibration and generate the final continuum images." (Observational data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "a “briggs” weighting ROBUST parameter of 0.5 was set" (Observational data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: rms reduction after self-calibration  
  > "The phase-only self-calibrations decreased the root mean square (rms) noise of continuum image from 0.5 mJy beam$^{-1}$ to 0.15 mJy beam$^{-1}$." (ALMA 3 mm data)
2. **spectral index map**: immath spix mode on 3 mm and 1.3 mm images tapered to common 0.079"x0.053" resolution, 3σ cutoff  
  > "The spectral index map was derived from the “spix” mode in the “immath” task of the CASA package" (Dust emission and spectral index); "we also included a cut-off with rms noises higher than 3$\sigma$." (Dust emission and spectral index)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: common resolution 0.079"x0.053", pixel 0.01"  
  > "we tapered them to the same resolution of 0.079$^{\prime\prime}\times$0.053$^{\prime\prime}$ using a pixel size of 0.01$^{\prime\prime}$." (Dust emission and spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with SMA spectral index of 2.8 for W51 North  
  > "which is comparable to the previously reported spectral index of 2.8 in W51 North using Submillimeter Array observations (cite: Zapata2009)." (Dust emission and spectral index)
3. **identify compact fragments**: Image 1.3 mm high-res data with uv > 910 kλ; 12σ threshold plus closed-contour criterion; 2D Gaussian fits; exclude isolated and point-like sources  
  > "In this work, we assumed an identification threshold of 12$\sigma$[^1] in the *uv*-range constrained continuum image." (Fragment identification); "Second, if a source has a ratio of intensity to flux density higher than 0.5, it is considered as an unresolved point-like source." (Fragment identification)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: uv > 910 kλ, 12σ = 1.32 mJy/beam  
  > "which is certainly below our 12$\sigma$ (1.32$\times$10$^{-3}$ Jy beam$^{-1}$) identification threshold." (ALMA simulations)
   - parameter: isolation criterion: individual 6σ full-uv contour  
  > "the red contour in Fig. [2](#1.3mm_910klambda) is 6$\sigma$ of the 1.3 mm continuum with full *uv* range" (Fragment identification)
   - fitting: two-dimensional Gaussian fitting of each source  
  > "A two-dimensional Gaussian fitting was performed toward each identified source, and the fitting results are listed in Table" (Fragment identification)
   - validation: simobserve simulations of uv-cut artifacts and comparison with astrodendro dendrogram identification (32 sources, only CB10 unmatched)  
  > "Based on the results of our simulations, it seems that cutting off baselines shorter than 910 k$\lambda$ does not create significant artifacts, but it allowed us to recover the real compact sources." (Fragment identification); "A total of 32 continuum sources were identified by the dendrogram algorithm" (Fragments identified using the dendrogram algorithm)
4. **fragment masses**: Optically thin dust mass M = S D^2/(B(Td) kappa); also optically thick estimate Mt = pi R^2 N_H2 m_H mu with N_H2 = 1/kappa  
  > "Assuming that the 1.3 mm continuum emission of the identified sources is optically thin, their masses can be estimated using the flux densities" (Fragment identification); "The masses of the optically thick sources were estimated by $M_{\rm t}$ = $\pi R^{2} N_{\rm H_{2}}m_{\rm H} \mu$" (Fragment identification)
   - parameter: kappa = 0.008991 cm2 g-1 at 1.3 mm  
  > "($\kappa$ = 0.008991 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 1.3 mm)" (Fragment identification)
   - assumption: Td = Tgas = 200 K uniform  
  > "we assume a dust temperature of $T_{\rm d}$ = $T_{\rm gas}$ = 200 K and that the dust temperature is uniformly distributed in the surveyed region" (Fragment identification)
   - assumption: D = 5128 pc; mu = 2.8  
  > "D = 5128($\pm$1867) pc is the distance to W51 IRS2 (cite: Reid2019)" (Fragment identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Optically thin and thick median masses agree  
  > "This indicates that the optically thin assumption does not strongly affect our results." (Fragment identification)
5. **separations and MSDC**: Histogram of pairwise separations (log bins 0.1) and MSDC per Simon 1997, compared with 1000 random uniform distributions; break identification; linear fit below first break  
  > "we grouped the angular separations of all companions into different annuli with logarithmic bins of log($\theta$) = 0.1" (Typical separations between fragments); "We performed a linear fit toward these points and found a slope of –0.8($\pm$0.1)" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - parameter: 29 fragments, 406 companions  
  > "the 29 identified fragments generate 406 companions" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - fitting: linear fit in log-log space  
  > "We performed a linear fit toward these points" (Typical separations between fragments)
   - validation: comparison with 1000 random distributions  
  > "we ran 1000 rounds of calculations of MSDC for a uniformly random distribution of sources within the same area." (Typical separations between fragments)
6. **define ensembles and estimate density**: Circles of radius 1845 AU / 7346 AU around fragments to group into SEs/LEs; masses from 1.3 mm low-res image with uv 9.164-910 kλ; uniform sphere density  
  > "we draw a circle with a radius corresponding to the identified breaks for every fragment." (Ensembles defined as groups of fragments); "we generated a 1.3 mm low-resolution continuum image using only the *uv* range of 9.164 $\sim$ 910 k$\lambda$." (Fragmentation)
   - parameter: radii 1845 AU (SE) and 7346 AU (LE)  
  > "which has been considered to be 1845 AU for SEs and 7346 AU for LEs" (Fragmentation)
   - assumption: T = 200 K, uniform spheres, mu = 2.8  
  > "A dust temperature of $T$ = 200 K was used to estimate the masses of the ensembles" (Fragmentation)
   - assumption: fragmentation level Poisson error

## Qualitative findings

- Most sources have spectral index ~2 (partly optically thick dust); W51 d2 and CC1 ~0.2 (free-free).  
  > "with only two regions, W51 d2 and CC1, associated with spectral indices around 0.2 indicative of free-free emission" (Conclusions)
- Two-level hierarchical fragmentation: 2 LEs and 5 SEs.  
  > "Five small-scale ensembles (SEs, corresponding to a radius of 1845 AU) and two large-scale ensembles (LEs, corresponding to a radius of 7346 AU) are defined in our surveyed region." (Conclusions)
- SE3 low fragmentation explained by higher temperature (~400 K), supported by vibrationally excited CH3CN absorption.  
  > "it seems that the most plausible explanation for the low fragmentation found in SE3 is that it might be warmer than the other SEs naturally inhibiting fragmentation." (Thermal Jeans fragmentation could be at work in W51 IRS2)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 1.3 mm high-res has highest resolution for fragments; low-res 1.3 mm used for ensemble masses due to large MRS and 3 mm free-free contamination  
  > "The 1.3 mm high-resolution image is the best option to do this job as it has the highest angular resolution of 0.028$^{\prime\prime}$" (Fragment identification); "as the 3 mm data are affected by the free-free emission, the data set at 1.3 mm with low angular resolution" (Fragmentation)
- Why this method: uv-cut to isolate compact structures since sources <0.2"; 12σ threshold equals most negative lobe  
  > "to identify the real compact and small-scale fragments, we constrained the 1.3 mm high-resolution continuum image by using only visibilities with a *uv* range larger than 910 k$\lambda$" (Fragment identification); "12$\sigma$ is the most negative lobe found in the *uv*-restricted image, and hence we do not trust any structure below this threshold" (Fragment identification)
- Alternative: Turbulence and magnetic fields as additional support in SE3 disfavoured  
  > "the magnetic field does not seem to be particularly strong in SE3 compared to SE1." (Thermal Jeans fragmentation could be at work in W51 IRS2)
- Alternative: Optically thick mass estimate considered; optically thin adopted  
  > "Hereafter, our calculations are based on the assumption that the dust emission is optically thin." (Fragment identification)
- Alternative: Dendrogram identification as alternative to closed-contour method  
  > "This indicates that our identification method used in Section [3.2](#fragments) yields results comparable to other identification methods." (Fragments identified using the dendrogram algorithm)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Mass errors only include flux and distance, not temperature or opacity  
  > "however, here, only the flux density and distance were taken into account." (Table notes)
- Limitation (authors): Fragments may merge, changing MSDC slope; kinematics needed  
  > "if a fraction of the fragments merge with time, the slope of the linear regime could change." (A cluster-like spatial distribution of fragments in W51 North (SE1))
