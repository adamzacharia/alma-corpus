## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate VLA data and flag**: CASA VLA pipeline on each two-hour observation set, Hanning smoothing disabled, then manual flagging of RFI at 29.43 GHz and bad antenna data  
  > "To calibrate the data from each two-hour observation set, we used the casa pipeline version 5.6.2 as described in" (2.3 Data reduction); "After examining the calibrated data, we performed additional flagging, removing the RFI at 29.43 GHz remaining in the observation sets of J2348–3054" (2.3 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Calibrators 3C48 / 3C147 for bandpass and flux; J0201+0343, J0348-2749, J2359-3133 for phase and amplitude  
  > "Phase and amplitude calibrations were performed using observations of J0201+0343, J0348-2749, and J2359-3133 (for P036+03, J0305–3150 and J2348–3054 respectively)." (2.2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image cubes and search for CO(2–1)**: Three-channel cubes (channel = 1.2 x FWHM[CII]) centred on CO(6–5) redshift, natural weighting, cleaned to 2 sigma within 3 arcsec; moment-0 maps and 100 km/s cubes with single-pixel spectra at the peak  
  > "We applied a natural weighting scheme and cleaned down to $2\sigma$ within $3\ensuremath{''}$ of the source centre." (2.3 Data reduction); "The spectra extracted from the single pixel corresponding to the peak in the moment-0 maps of these cubes, are shown in the right panels" (2.3 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Assumed CO(2–1) linewidth equals [C II] FWHM; velocity range encompasses 84% of the emission  
  > "Assuming that the CO(2–1) emission has the same linewidth as" (2.3 Data reduction)
   - assumption: CO(2–1) emission has the same linewidth as [C II] and the systemic velocity of the other CO transitions  
  > "Assuming that the CO(2–1) emission has the same linewidth as" (2.3 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked for peaks of similar significance in neighbouring channels and for offset emission or continuum; searched for companions at known positions  
  > "We also searched for evidence of the companions reported in (cite: 2020ApJ...904..130V), but find nothing exceeding $\sim2.5 \,\sigma$ at the expected positions and frequencies." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
3. **Measure CO(2–1) line flux and 4-sigma upper limits**: Detection: peak-pixel value in the central channel times channel width plus 16% for line wings. Upper limits: moment-0 rms times 1.2 x FWHM[CII], plus 16%, times 4 sigma, plus a missing-extended-flux correction from mock VLA observations (15% for P036+03, 19% for J0305-3150, none for J2348-3054)  
  > "multiplying by the channel width, and accounting for the extra 16% of the line flux not included in $1.2\,\times$ FWHM." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits); "To derive the upper limits, we measured the root mean square (rms) of the moment-0 map, also multiplying this value by $1.2\,\times$ FWHM$_\mathrm{[CII]}$, accounting for the extra 16%, and multiplying by our detection threshold of $4\sigma$." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits); "Based on this modelling, we found that for P036+03, we would miss 15% of the flux if the CO(2–1) emission is as extended as the" (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits); "Similarly, for J0305-3150, we would miss 19% of the flux." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Detection threshold 4 sigma  
  > "we treated the CO(2–1) emission from J2348–3054 as a significant detection and derived $4\sigma$ upper limits for the other two sources, consistent with our detection threshold." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
   - assumption: CO(2–1) line width equals the [C II] width (highest S/N prior)  
  > "Given the low S/N of these observations, we derived the CO(2–1) line flux density and upper limits assuming that the CO(2–1) line width is the same as that measured for the much higher S/N" (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
   - assumption: CO(2–1) emission has the same size as the [C II] emission  
  > "assuming that the CO(2–1) emission arises from the same size region as the" (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mock VLA observations of point and Gaussian sources under the same configuration; checked with a 2.5 arcsec circular aperture for J2348–3054; repeated with [C II] FWHM values from the original papers  
  > "We then performed mock VLA observations under the same configuration used for the real observations and compared the flux extracted from the peak pixel for both the point source and extended source." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits); "we also tested our results using the values derived in the original studies that reported the" (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
4. **Compute line luminosities with CMB correction**: L' = 3.25e7 S dv D_L^2 / ((1+z)^3 nu_obs^2); CMB correction f_CMB from da Cunha et al. 2013 non-LTE high-density, high-temperature case  
  > "where $D_L$ is the luminosity distance in Mpc, $\nu_\mathrm{obs}$ is the observed central frequency of the line in GHz and $S_\mathrm{line} \Delta v$ is the line flux in Jy km s$^{-1}$." (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties); "To perform the CMB corrections, we adopted their non-LTE, high-density ($n_\mathrm{H_2}=10^{4.2}$ cm$^{-3}$) and high-temperature ($T_\mathrm{kin}=40$ K) scenario." (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties)
   - parameter: f_CMB for CO(2–1): 0.56, 0.55, 0.53 (P036+03, J0305-3150, J2348-3054)  
  > "for P036+03, J0305-3150, and J2348-3054 is 0.56, 0.55, and 0.53 (respectively)" (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties)
   - assumption: Cosmology H0=70, Omega_M=0.3, Omega_Lambda=0.7  
  > "we adopt a Lambda–cold dark matter cosmology, with $H_0 = 70$  km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_M = 0.3$, and $\Omega_\lambda = 0.7$." (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Both CMB-corrected and uncorrected values derived  
  > "We therefore derived both CMB-corrected and uncorrected values for the CO line luminosities." (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties)
5. **Refit dust SEDs with CMB effects**: Modified blackbody with optically thin dust at lambda>40 um, correcting heating by and contrast against the CMB; only data at rest lambda>70 um fit; Bayesian MCMC for log Mdust, Tdust, beta  
  > "We fit the dust SEDs with a modified blackbody, assuming optically thin dust emission at $\lambda>40$ μm." (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties); "Because we are assuming thermal dust, we only included observations sampling the dust SED at $\lambda_\mathrm{rest}>70$ μm." (Appendix: Dust SED fitting)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Dust opacity kappa = kappa_nu0 (nu/nu0)^beta with nu0=c/125 um and kappa_nu0=2.64 m^2/kg  
  > "Following (cite: 2003MNRAS.341..589D) we take $\nu_0 = c/(125$μm) and $\kappa_{\nu_0} = 2.64~\mathrm{m^2 kg^{-1}}$." (Appendix: Dust SED fitting)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust at lambda>40 um (may bias T_dust low and M_dust high if thick)  
  > "our dust temperatures and masses may be biased to slightly lower and higher values, respectively." (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC; uniform priors 2.73(1+z)<T_dust<100, 6<log M_dust<11, 1<beta<3; median and 16th/84th percentiles reported  
  > "We apply uniform priors of $2.73 (1+z) < T_\mathrm{dust} < 100$, $6 < \log{M_\mathrm{dust}} < 11$ and $1 < \beta < 3$." (Appendix: Dust SED fitting); "The resulting likelihood posteriors as well as the median, 16th and 84th percentile values are shown for our sample and the literature sample in" (Appendix: Dust SED fitting)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive molecular gas masses from CO(2–1), [C I], [C II] and dust**: CO(2–1): thermalised L'CO(2-1)=L'CO(1-0), alpha_CO=0.8. Dust: gas-to-dust 100. [C I]: optically thin LTE carbon mass, T_ex=47 K, [C I]/H2=(6.82±3.04)e-5. [C II]: alpha_[C II]=31 Msun/Lsun  
  > "we scaled the dust masses by the local gas-to-dust ratio of 100 (cite: 2007ApJ...663..866D)" (3.2 Molecular gas masses); "We assumed the atomic carbon is in thermal equilibrium with the dust, with the average dust temperature typically quoted in the literature, $T_\mathrm{ex} = 47$  K" (3.2 Molecular gas masses); "we applied the mean conversion factor from the calibration of (cite: 2018MNRAS.481.1976Z), $\alpha_\mathrm{[C II]}=31\,\mathrm{M}_\odot/\mathrm{L}_\odot$," (3.2 Molecular gas masses)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 0.8 (K km/s pc^2)^-1, r_2,1 = 1  
  > "assuming $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 0.8$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$)$^{-1}$ and $r_\mathrm{2,1}=1$." (abstract)
   - assumption: ULIRG-like alpha_CO=0.8 (alpha_CO,tot = alpha_CO,intr / f_CMB with the CMB correction)  
  > "In effect, this additional CMB correction leads to an increased $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$, that is $\alpha_\mathrm{CO, tot.} = \alpha_\mathrm{CO, intr.} / f_\mathrm{CMB}$." (3.2 Molecular gas masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Gas masses from the four tracers compared; T_ex = 30 K or 100 K changes [C I]-derived masses by 1.5x or 0.8x

## Qualitative findings

- CO(2–1) detected in J2348–3054 (z=6.9) but not in P036+03 or J0305–3150, the highest-redshift CO(2–1) observations to date  
  > "We reduced and analysed observations of CO(2–1), taken with the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array, in three $z=6.5-6.9$ QSO hosts—the highest redshift observations of CO(2–1) to date." (abstract)
- Non-detections in P036+03 and J0305–3150 imply more highly excited CO than in J1148+5251  
  > "Two of our three QSOs, P036+03 and J0305–3150, were not detected in CO(2–1), implying more highly excited CO than in the well-studied $z=6.4$ QSO J1148+5251." (abstract)
- Dust-based gas masses mostly agree with CO(2–1) masses  
  > "We find that the molecular gas masses inferred from the dust mass measurements are mostly consistent with those inferred from CO(2–1)" (4.1 Molecular gas mass comparison)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets chosen because they already had CO(6–5), CO(7–6), [C I] and [C II] data, so low-J CO would anchor excitation and gas mass  
  > "To continue systematically exploring the molecular gas reservoirs of $z\gtrsim6$ QSO host galaxies, we used the vla to observe the CO(2-1) emission of three QSOs with existing observations of CO(6–5), CO(7–6)," (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Low-J CO is needed to calibrate CO excitation and the cold/diffuse gas fraction  
  > "To test how much molecular gas is in the cold/diffuse state and hence correctly calibrate the CO excitation for these $z\gtrsim6$ QSO hosts, it is critical to also observe their low-$J$ ($J=1,2$) CO emission." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Tested T_ex = 30 K and 100 K for [C I] masses, found small impact (0.8x for 100 K)  
  > "whereas if we assume $T_\mathrm{ex} =$ 100 K the molecular gas masses would be 0.8$\times$ our values, therefore making little difference to the derived values." (3.2 Molecular gas masses)
- Alternative: Retested fluxes with [C II] FWHM from original detection papers; fluxes consistent within errors  
  > "we also tested our results using the values derived in the original studies that reported the" (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
- Alternative: Treated the 3.3 sigma peak near P036+03 as an upper limit, since stronger peaks appear elsewhere in the maps  
  > "Thus, we treat it as an upper limit." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
- How they decided it was enough: 4-sigma detection threshold; the two sources below it get upper limits  
  > "we treated the CO(2–1) emission from J2348–3054 as a significant detection and derived $4\sigma$ upper limits for the other two sources, consistent with our detection threshold." (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N of the CO(2–1) data forces an assumed line width equal to [C II]  
  > "Given the low S/N of these observations, we derived the CO(2–1) line flux density and upper limits assuming that the CO(2–1) line width is the same as that measured for the much higher S/N" (2.4 Line fluxes and upper limits)
- Limitation (authors): Dynamical masses are highly uncertain and could be systematically underestimated  
  > "in reality, the measured dynamical masses are highly uncertain and could be systematically underestimated if the measured sizes and/or circular velocities are too small, or if the assumed geometry is incorrect" (4.2 Mass budget)
- Limitation (authors): Dust temperatures and emissivity indices mostly unconstrained due to few continuum points  
  > "We note that in most cases, the dust temperatures and emissivity indices remain unconstrained due to the limited number of continuum data points." (3.1 Line luminosities and dust properties)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one of three targets is detected (5.1 sigma), so r_6,2 and mass conclusions rest on upper limits and on literature fluxes with heterogeneous calibration
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO=0.8 and r_2,1=1 are assumed; the conclusion that alpha_CO must be low depends on uncertain dynamical masses
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The [C I]-based consistency requires a high carbon abundance taken from another sample
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stated 4-sigma upper limits in the conclusions (1.0-1.5e10) differ from the table values (<1.2e10, <1.6e10)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data are used only through earlier publications, so ALMA processing is not reproducible from this paper
