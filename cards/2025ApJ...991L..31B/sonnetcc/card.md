## Analysis recipe

1. **JWST/MIRI light curves and SED photometry**: Aperture photometry with a 4.07 pixel (50% encircled energy) aperture on calibrated images, integration-level (5.99 s) and group-level (~0.9 s binned) light curves; MAST stage 3 products for four post-TSO filters, local reduction for F2550W  
  > "We chose to use a 4.07 pixel aperture corresponding to 50% encircled energy to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio" (James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)); "we have developed a scheme to achieve higher temporal resolution light curves of JWST/MIRI data by reducing the images at the group level rather than the default integration level" (James Webb Space Telescope (JWST))
   - software: JWST calibration pipeline, photutils
   - parameter: Integrations affected by cosmic rays in source or background regions discarded  
  > "Any integrations which were affected by cosmic rays in the source or background regions were discarded" (James Webb Space Telescope (JWST))
   - parameter: Group-level data binned to 3 groups, ~0.9 s resolution  
  > "these were binned to 3 groups, yielding a $\sim 0.9$ second time resolution light curve" (James Webb Space Telescope (JWST))
   - assumption: Dereddened with A_V = 4.0, R_V = 3.1 for Table 1 flux densities  
  > "We correct the observed MIR flux densities for interstellar extinction adopting $A_V = 4.0$ (cite: hynes09; cas93; 2021ApJ...916...33G) and $R_V = 3.1$." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Raw-image reduction of the other four filters matched the MAST stage three products  
  > "As a check, we performed the same calibration and analysis routine on the raw images in the other four filters, which produced results consistent with the stage three data products retrieved from the MAST portal." (James Webb Space Telescope (JWST))
2. **ALMA 97.5 GHz light curve**: Standard pipeline calibration, then a custom python script runs per-time-bin tclean multi-frequency synthesis imaging and imfit source fitting  
  > "To compute flux density time-domain variability, we pass the calibrated interferometric data through a custom python script" (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)); "We defined a bin size of 60 seconds and used a Briggs weighting scheme which produced the cleanest results." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA))
   - software: CASA, tclean, imfit, custom python script
   - parameter: 60 s time bins, Briggs weighting  
  > "We defined a bin size of 60 seconds and used a Briggs weighting scheme which produced the cleanest results." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of calibrated data for atmospheric lines and instrumental artifacts  
  > "The calibrated data were then visually inspected to ensure all atmospheric lines and artifacts from instrumental effects were properly removed." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA))
3. **VLA 6 GHz light curve**: Drop A-configuration antennas, peel J2025+3343 with two rounds of self-calibration and uvsub, then 5-minute tclean images at 6.0 GHz with robust=0 and imfit point-source peak fluxes  
  > "We thus opted to exclude all seven ‘A configuration’ antennas, leaving an array of 20 antennas in close to D configuration" (Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA)); "We then created a light curve of V404 Cyg by creating images every 5 minutes, at a reference frequency of 6.0 GHz and using" (Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA))
   - software: CASA, VLA pipeline, tclean, uvsub, imfit
   - parameter: Self-calibration: phase-only on 10 min intervals, then phase and amplitude on 20 min intervals  
  > "In the first round, we applied phase-only gain solutions on 10 min intervals, and in the second-round we applied phase and amplitude gain solutions over 20 min intervals" (Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA))
   - parameter: 2 Taylor terms in tclean  
  > "using 2 Taylor terms to account for the wide fractional bandwidth" (Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA))
   - assumption: V404 Cyg is a point source in all images, so point source forced in imfit  
  > "We checked that V404 Cyg was a point source in all images" (Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **X-ray spectral fits**: Absorbed power-law (wabs*powerlaw) fits in XSPEC with N_H fixed, Chandra via CIAO and XMM-Newton via SAS, 0.3-7.0 keV  
  > "We extracted source events with energies 0.3–7.0 keV from a 3.2 arcsec radius aperture and the background from a 23 arcsec circle near the target." (Chandra X-ray Observatory); "We jointly fit the three spectra using the same model as for Chandra." (XMM-Newton)
   - software: CIAO, XSPEC, SAS
   - assumption: Luminosities computed at 2.226 kpc  
  > "corresponding to an average luminosity of $L_X = 8.9\times 10^{32}$ erg s^-1^ at 2.226 kpc" (Chandra X-ray Observatory)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Optical photometry calibration**: HiPERCAM pipeline aperture photometry with contaminating star subtracted via count ratios; Skinakas data scaled to match HiPERCAM mean flux in the overlap  
  > "The data were reduced using the HiPERCAM pipeline" (Gran Telescopio Canarias (GTC) HiPERCAM); "we elected to calibrate the Skinakas data by applying an offset so that the mean flux density as measured by Skinakas matched the mean flux density as measured by HiPERCAM during the time that the observations overlapped" (Skinakas Observatory)
   - software: HiPERCAM pipeline, IRAF, daophot
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: HiPERCAM calibration agrees with Pan-STARRS-derived SDSS magnitudes at the ~20% level  
  > "We found that they agreed at the $\sim 20\%$ level." (Gran Telescopio Canarias (GTC) HiPERCAM)
6. **Radio/mm/MIR spectral index**: Spectral slope from the simultaneous portions of the VLA, ALMA and JWST data (fit method not given)  
  > "Considering only the simultaneous portions of the VLA, ALMA, and JWST observations, the radio/mm/MIR spectral slope is $\alpha = 0.04 \pm 0.01$" (Results)
   - assumption: A_V = 4.0, R_V = 3.1 for MIR dereddening  
  > "We correct the observed MIR flux densities for interstellar extinction adopting $A_V = 4.0$ (cite: hynes09; cas93; 2021ApJ...916...33G) and $R_V = 3.1$." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test of a thermal blackbody origin of the flare**: Flare rise time limits emitting size; brightness temperature of a blackbody of that size at 21 micron is predicted into the optical and compared with the Skinakas R_C increase  
  > "the flux density increases by about 50% ($\sim 1.0$ mJy) over just 3.6 seconds" (Discussion); "A hypothetical blackbody of diameter 3.6 light-seconds producing the dereddened (cite: 2021ApJ...916...33G) 21 $\mu$m flux density of $\sim 0.84$ mJy would have a temperature of about $8 \times 10^5$ K." (Discussion)
   - parameter: JWST data binned to 60 s to match Skinakas time resolution  
  > "we bin the JWST data to 60 seconds, to match the time resolution of the Skinakas observatory data" (Discussion)
   - assumption: Emitting region diameter <= 3.6 light-seconds from the flare rise time  
  > "This constrains the size of the emitting region to $\le$3.6 light-seconds" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted optical flux ~1 Jy versus observed ~0.9 mJy, inconsistent by three orders of magnitude  
  > "is only approximately 0.9 mJy. This is inconsistent with the hypothetical blackbody by three orders of magnitude, ruling out such an origin for the large flare" (Discussion)
8. **Minimum-energy and cooling-time estimate for the jet flare**: Treat the flare as a discrete synchrotron ejection and apply the Longair minimum-energy formulae; volume from the flare rise time and a 0.8 deg jet opening angle; compare with the available accretion kinetic power  
  > "If we adopt a similar opening angle of 0.8 deg for our calculations" (Discussion); "We can then estimate the maximum available accretion kinetic power to be $\sqrt{L_X L_{\rm trans}}=1.2\times 10^{35}$ erg s^-1^." (Discussion)
   - parameter: Opening angle 0.8 deg; observed flux density and radio parallax distance  
  > "use the observed flux density and radio parallax measured distance to V404 Cyg" (Discussion)
   - assumption: Equipartition, eta=1 (all energy in electrons), uniform filling, optically thin with spectral index -0.75  
  > "The assumptions of this model include 1) source components are close to equipartition, 2) $\eta=1$ (i.e., all energy in electrons, none in protons or nuclei)" (Jet energetics); "4) the emission region is optically thin (spectral index $\alpha=-0.75$" (Jet energetics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Estimated kinetic power is less than 10% of the available accretion kinetic power  
  > "The estimated kinetic power is then less than 10% of the available kinetic power of the accretion flow" (Discussion)
9. **Check of the hot accretion flow alternative**: Follow the Veledina et al. method with the 3.6 s rise-time constraint and extrapolate to quiescence assuming L_X scales as Mdot squared and B scales as sqrt(Mdot)  
  > "we follow the method of (cite: 2013MNRAS.430.3196V) using the constraint imposed by the 3.6 second rise time of the large flare" (Discussion); "the magnetic field strength should scale as $B\propto \sqrt{\dot{M}}$" (Discussion)
   - assumption: Optically thin flow luminosity scales as Mdot squared; vertical extent and magnetic/radiation pressure equipartition independent of accretion rate  
  > "If we assume that the luminosity of optically thin flows scales as $L_X \propto \dot{M}^2$" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Intense, strongly correlated variability appears across the electromagnetic spectrum, with a pronounced feature shared by X-ray, optical, 21 micron and mm light curves  
  > "Intense variability strongly correlated across the electromagnetic spectrum is clearly present in this new dataset." (Results); "there is a pronounced feature common between the X-ray, optical, 21 $\mu$m, and millimeter light curves" (Discussion)
- The ALMA 97.5 GHz flux density is similar to the VLA 6 GHz flux density, implying an approximately flat spectrum  
  > "As the ALMA 97.5 GHz flux density is similar to that measured with the VLA at 6 GHz implying an approximately flat spectrum" (Discussion)
- The 3.6 s flare rise time rules out a blackbody origin and supports a jet  
  > "The large amplitude 21 $\mu$m variability on short timescales is inconsistent with a thermal blackbody-like emitter" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Simultaneous ALMA Band 3 data was chosen to search for correlated variability at longer wavelengths than the JWST MIR  
  > "Simultaneous Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) Band 3 (97.5 GHz) observations were crucial to search for correlated variability at longer wavelengths." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Variability study to distinguish thermal from synchrotron origins  
  > "The primary aims of this study were to quantify the variability of the MIR emission and identify correlations with variability at longer wavelengths which are thought to arise from a jet." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Circumbinary material or viscously heated accretion disk: rejected on rapid large-amplitude 21 micron variability  
  > "ruling out circumbinary dust or viscously heated accretion disk origins" (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Extended hot accretion flow (Veledina et al. model): rejected as implausible  
  > "Thus, it is implausible that this model can account for the observed MIR radiation." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Thermal bremsstrahlung from a disk wind (suggested for A0620-00): disfavoured for V404 Cyg because the wind is warm while the inferred brightness temperature is an order of magnitude higher  
  > "while we infer a brightness temperature of an order of magnitude higher based on the amplitude of seconds-timescale variability" (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): A small thermal contribution to the MIR excess at wavelengths shorter than 21 micron cannot be ruled out  
  > "we cannot rule out a small thermal contribution to the MIR excess at wavelengths short of 21 $\mu$m" (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Post-TSO MIRI images are non-simultaneous and the source varies, limiting inference on the MIR SED shape  
  > "non-simultaneity of the JWST MIRI images taken after the time-series observations and the significant variability on short timescales, there are some limitations on how much we can infer about the shape of the SED in the MIR" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): The minimum-energy method gives only a lower limit if the emission region is partially optically thick  
  > "Should the MIR emission arise in a region which is partially optically thick, this method instead gives us a lower limit on the minimum energy." (Jet energetics)
- Limitation (authors): Detailed timing analysis and the full SED are deferred to later work  
  > "Detailed timing analyses will be explored in a future study." (Discussion); "We will present and study the full SED in a later work." (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA flux densities and light-curve values are shown only in a figure; no numeric ALMA flux or uncertainty is tabulated, so the key alpha value cannot be traced to ALMA numbers
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral index alpha = 0.04 +/- 0.01 uses non-detailed fitting and a very variable source; a +/- 0.01 error looks small and its derivation is undescribed
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlation between bands is asserted by eye, with timing analysis deferred; no lag or significance statistics
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Jet energetics depend on an adopted opening angle (0.8 deg from other sources) and equipartition, and the cooling time exceeds the rise time
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dereddening with a single A_V = 4.0 strongly affects MIR flux densities
