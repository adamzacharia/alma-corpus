## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble rotation curves**: Use PHANGS-ALMA CO tilted-ring rotation curves; supplement with literature H I rotation curves for eight galaxies, and use H I only for three galaxies; correct to the PHANGS distance and inclination  
  > "we supplemented the CO rotation curves with literature H i rotation curves when available." (2.1 Rotation curves); "Where needed, we corrected the rotation curve data to match the distance and inclination reported in (cite: lang_phangs_2020) to ensure homogeneity." (2.1 Rotation curves)
   - assumption: H I and CO corotate  
  > "we note that H i and CO are expected to corotate, and this has been corroborated with observations in nearby galaxies" (2.1 Rotation curves)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Including H I rotation curves changes the slope within the uncertainties of the fiducial relation  
  > "Any resulting changes in the slope lie within the uncertainties of our fiducial relation, confirming the robustness of our approach" (Including H i rotation curves)
2. **Derive H2 surface density profiles with a master alpha_CO**: Convert CO(2-1) intensity profiles to Sigma_H2 using R21 and alpha_CO(1-0); average four alpha_CO prescriptions (S20, MW, N12, B13) into a master alpha_CO, with the standard deviation as uncertainty and standard error propagation  
  > "The master conversion factor is derived by averaging the four prescriptions, and we adopt the standard deviation as uncertainty." (2.2 Gas surface densities); "to derive our final $\Sigma_{\rm H_2}(R)$ profile, with uncertainties determined through standard error propagation." (2.2 Gas surface densities)
   - parameter: R21 = 0.65  
  > "where $R_{21} = 0.65$ is the adopted CO(2-1) to CO(1-0) line ratio (cite: den_brok_new_2021; leroy_low-j_2022)" (2.2 Gas surface densities)
   - parameter: alpha_CO,MW = 4.35 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1 for the Galactic-average prescription  
  > "\alpha_{\text{CO, MW}}= 4.35 \, M_{\odot}\, \mathrm{(K\,km\,s^{-1}\, pc^{2})}^{-1}~." (2.2 Gas surface densities)
   - assumption: Combining the four calibrations is treated as a conservative way of capturing realistic uncertainties  
  > "Combining the calibrations into a master $\alpha_{\text{CO}}$ is a conservative approach that manages to capture realistic uncertainties better" (2.2 Gas surface densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: M_H2 from the integrated profiles agrees with the PHANGS-ALMA CO luminosities, median difference 0.03 dex  
  > "We find great agreement when comparing our values to the CO luminosities reported in (cite: leroy_surveyphangs-alma_2021), with a median difference of 0.03 dex." (footnote 3)
3. **Fit rotation curves with analytic functions for extrapolation**: Fit an arctan function first; use the multi-parameter function for five galaxies (NGC 300, NGC 1365, NGC 2903, NGC 3521, NGC 3627) with complex kinematics  
  > "For simplicity, we first fitted the rotation curves with the arctan function, finding satisfactory fits for most cases." (3.1 Rotation curve fitting); "For five galaxies (NGC 300, NGC 1365, NGC 2903, NGC 3521, and NGC 3627) with complex kinematics the multi-parameter function works better." (3.1 Rotation curve fitting)
   - software: emcee
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee, flat priors on the fitting parameters  
  > "we retrieved posterior distributions for the best-fitting parameters using a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) routine with the Python package" (3.1 Rotation curve fitting); "assuming flat priors on the fitting parameters" (3.1 Rotation curve fitting)
   - validation: Fitted models reproduce the observed kinematics (shown for one representative galaxy)  
  > "Our models successfully reproduced the observed kinematics" (3.1 Rotation curve fitting)
4. **Fit H2 surface density profiles**: Fit a polyexponential profile (Sigma_0, R_Sigma, c1, c2, c3); fit a sum of two polyexponential profiles when one is not flexible enough  
  > "In those cases, we fit instead a combination (sum) of two polyexponential profiles." (3.2 Surface density profile fitting)
   - software: emcee
   - assumption: Gas surface densities decay exponentially at large radii  
  > "we extrapolate the observed data to capture all $j$. For this, we fit $\Sigma(R)$ and $V(R)$ with functional forms." (3 Computing j)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC, as for the rotation curves  
  > "Similar to our approach with the rotation curves, we used" (3.2 Surface density profile fitting)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compute j_H2 and M_H2 by Monte Carlo and apply the convergence cut**: Compute j(<R) = J/M by integrating R'^2 Sigma V and R' Sigma over radius up to 50 kpc for 100000 random MCMC realisations of the rotation-curve and surface-density parameters; take medians and 1 sigma as values and uncertainties; M_H2 from integrating the denominator; exclude galaxies with convergence factor R < 0.7  
  > "calculating $j_{\rm H_2}$ for 100000 random MCMC realisations of both the rotation curves and surface density profiles fitting parameters" (3.3 Specific angular momentum); "up to 50 kpc" (3.3 Specific angular momentum); "We establish a threshold such that galaxies with $\mathcal{R}<0.7$ are excluded from the derivation of the molecular $j - M$ relation (see below)." (3.3 Specific angular momentum)
   - parameter: Convergence threshold R_min = 0.7; integration limit 50 kpc; 100000 realisations  
  > "Applying the convergence cutoff results in a final sample of 51 galaxies with converged $j_{\rm H_2}$ profiles." (3.3 Specific angular momentum)
   - assumption: Flat rotation curves and exponentially decaying Sigma beyond the data, so extrapolation captures all of j  
  > "Measuring $j$ from insufficiently extended data can lead to its underestimation" (3 Computing j)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Results are robust to the choice of R_min (0.2, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9 give consistent fit parameters)  
  > "all the fitting parameters remain consistent within their uncertainties regardless of the specific convergence criterion employed." (Convergence criteria)
6. **Fit the j_H2-M_H2 power law and look for secondary dependences**: Fit log j = alpha [log M_H2 - 9] + beta with orthogonal intrinsic scatter; analyse residuals against Hubble type, R_e, SFR, M_* and M_H2/M_*  
  > "We include the orthogonal intrinsic scatter ($\sigma_{\perp}$) following (cite: bacchini_volumetric_2019) and (cite: mancera_pina_baryonic_2021)." (4.1 Shape and dependences); "The parameters considered include Hubble type, effective radius ($R_{\rm e}$), star formation rate (SFR), stellar mass ($M_{\ast}$), and molecular gas fraction $M_{\rm H_2} / M_\ast$." (4.1 Shape and dependences)
   - fitting: Bayesian framework; sampler, priors and likelihood not given  
  > "We quantify the scaling law by fitting a power law with a Bayesian framework" (abstract)
   - validation: Slope unchanged when H I rotation curves are excluded or when four outliers are excluded (j_H2-M_* case)  
  > "excluding these four galaxies does not affect the slope of either the $j_{\rm H_2}$–$M_{\rm H_2}$ or the $j_{\rm H_2}$–$M_\ast$ relations" (The j_H2-M_* relation)
7. **Compare with previous observations and with models**: Compare with Obreschkow & Glazebrook (2014) for five overlapping galaxies; test the Romeo (2020) disc-stability scaling; compare with SHARK v2.0 disc galaxies  
  > "To match this stability relation to our observed $j_{\rm H_2}$–$M_{\rm H_2}$ relation with a slope $\alpha\approx0.5$, we need the scaling $\sigma \propto M_{\rm H_2}^{0.5}$" (5 Testing theoretical models); "To compare our $j_{\rm H_2}-M_{\rm H_2}$ relation with SHARK, we selected only SHARK galaxies with $M_\ast$ within $\pm 3 \sigma$ of our own $M_\ast$ range and $B/T<0.3$" (5 Testing theoretical models)
   - software: SHARK
   - assumption: H2 velocity dispersion scaling gamma ~ 0.05-0.2 taken from kinematic measurements in the literature  
  > "Instead, for our data, the dependence is much weaker, $\sigma \propto M_{\rm H_2}^{\gamma}$, with $\gamma\approx0.05-0.2$" (5 Testing theoretical models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The H2 relation has a slope close to the stellar j-M relation and shallower than the H I relation  
  > "The slope of the molecular relation aligns closely with that of the $j_\ast$-$M_\ast$ relation" (4.1 Shape and dependences)
- Galaxies with higher M_H2/M_* tend to have lower j_H2 at fixed M_H2; no clear dependence on Hubble type or R_e; no significant SFR correlation  
  > "This relationship indicates that galaxies with higher $M_{\rm H_2}/M_\ast$ tend to have lower $j_{\rm H_2}$ at a given $M_{\rm H_2}$." (4.1 Shape and dependences); "We find no clear dependence with Hubble type or $R_{\rm e}$ in our $j_{\rm H_2}-M_{\rm H_2}$ relation." (4.1 Shape and dependences)
- Four lenticular or intermediate-spiral galaxies (NGC 1317, NGC 3626, NGC 4293, NGC 4457) form a tail with low R_e,H2, j_H2 and M_H2/M_*  
  > "The tail is formed by the galaxies NGC 1317, NGC 3626, NGC 4293, and NGC 4457." (The j_H2-M_* relation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Deep high-resolution CO observations of local galaxies with rotation curves and profiles available from the PHANGS-ALMA collaboration; scarcity of such CO data had left the molecular j-M relation unquantified  
  > "the picture for the molecular gas (H$_2$) is largely incomplete due to the scarcity of deep and high-resolution CO data" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Extrapolate rotation curves and surface densities with functional forms so that all of the angular momentum is captured  
  > "we extrapolate the observed data to capture all $j$. For this, we fit $\Sigma(R)$ and $V(R)$ with functional forms." (3 Computing j)
- Alternative: Arctan versus multi-parameter rotation-curve function; single versus summed polyexponential surface density profile  
  > "For five galaxies (NGC 300, NGC 1365, NGC 2903, NGC 3521, and NGC 3627) with complex kinematics the multi-parameter function works better." (3.1 Rotation curve fitting); "In those cases, we fit instead a combination (sum) of two polyexponential profiles." (3.2 Surface density profile fitting)
- Alternative: Four alpha_CO prescriptions combined into one master alpha_CO rather than adopting a single one  
  > "The master conversion factor is derived by averaging the four prescriptions, and we adopt the standard deviation as uncertainty." (2.2 Gas surface densities)
- Alternative: Six galaxies excluded because of irregular surface density profiles or a compact, non-flattening rotation curve  
  > "From our 66 galaxies we also excluded NGC 1512, NGC 2566, NGC 2775, NGC 4569, and NGC 4826 since their surface density profiles are too irregular to allow a robust quantification of their angular momentum." (2.2 Gas surface densities)
- How they decided it was enough: Galaxies are kept only if the convergence factor R is at least 0.7, to avoid over-reliance on extrapolation  
  > "which allows us to avoid over-reliance on the extrapolated data." (3.3 Specific angular momentum)
- Limitation (authors): alpha_CO is hard to constrain and the calibrations differ by more than their quoted uncertainties  
  > "remains challenging to constrain precisely due to its sensitivity to local conditions such as gas density, temperature, and metallicity" (2.2 Gas surface densities)
- Limitation (authors): Insufficiently extended data lead to underestimated j, so results depend on the extrapolation for non-converged galaxies  
  > "Measuring $j$ from insufficiently extended data can lead to its underestimation" (3 Computing j)
- Limitation (authors): Results for the non-converged sample are less reliable  
  > "but we note that these results are less reliable" (Molecular angular momentum catalogue)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is predominantly low-bulge galaxies with few lenticulars, so conclusions on lenticulars are limited  
  > "a more representative sample, including a greater number of lenticular galaxies, would be necessary to draw clearer conclusions." (The j_H2-M_* relation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Result rests entirely on two inherited PHANGS products (rotation curves, Sigma_H2 profiles) whose systematics (inclination, beam smearing at 150 pc, alpha_CO) are not re-examined
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A uniform R21 = 0.65 and an averaged alpha_CO that is not independent of its components may bias M_H2 systematically and correlate errors with j
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Orthogonal-scatter fit details (priors, treatment of correlated j and M_H2 errors from the same MCMC) are not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample is limited to star-forming main-sequence galaxies at M_H2 >~ 1e8 Msun, so the relation is not shown to hold across the galaxy population
