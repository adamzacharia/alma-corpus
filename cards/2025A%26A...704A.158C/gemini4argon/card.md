## Analysis recipe

1. **Design and apply Lyman-break color selection criteria for z ~ 15-20 and z ~ 20-28 candidates**: Test color cuts on EGG and JAGUAR mock catalogs perturbed with observational noise, then apply SNR and color thresholds to the ASTRODEEP-JWST multi-band catalogs and HST stacks  
  > "We defined color selection criteria for galaxies in two different redshift ranges, z$\sim$15-20 and z$\sim$20-30 on the basis of mock catalogues of objects at z$=$0-30 tailored to match the noise properties of our observations" (Color selection criteria); "After extensive testing, we first define a detection threshold corresponding to SNR$>$10 in the detection band used by M24, i.e. F356W+F444W." (Color selection criteria)
   - software: EGG, SEXTRACTOR, A-PHOT, Rainbow JWST pipeline, GALIGHT
   - parameter: SNR > 10 in F356W+F444W detection band; SNR < 2.0 in bands blueward of the break; (F200W-F277W) > 1.5 for z~15-20; (F277W-F356W) > 1.5 for z~20-28  
  > "After extensive testing, we first define a detection threshold corresponding to SNR$>$10 in the detection band used by M24, i.e. F356W+F444W." (Color selection criteria); "&(F200W-F277W)>1.5\\ &-0.4<(F277W-F356W)<0.4\\ &(F356W-F444W)<0.5\\" (Color selection criteria); "&(F277W-F356W)>1.5\\ &-0.5<(F356W-F444W)<0.5" (Color selection criteria)
   - assumption: z = 18 assumed to convert observed F277W magnitude to rest-frame M_UV for the 5 F200W-dropout candidates  
  > "We estimated the M$_{UV}$ of the five candidates by converting to the rest-frame the observed F277W magnitude assuming a redshift z=18 where our redshift selection function peaks" (Selected sources over $\sim$0.2 deg$^2$)
   - fitting: Sersic profile fitting in F277W with GALIGHT to measure half-light radii, and power-law fit to F277W, F356W, F444W for UV slope beta  
  > "We have measured their half-light radius by fitting the light distribution in the F277W band with GALIGHT (cite: Ding2020; Birrer2021) assuming a (cite: Sersic1968) profile with free index $n$ and fixing the redshift at the best-fit solution at $z>10$." (Selected sources over $\sim$0.2 deg$^2$)
   - validation: Visual inspection to remove hot pixels and stellar spikes; multi-aperture SNR checks on NIRCam SW stacks and MIRI mosaics  
  > "When analysing the observed dataset (Sect. 3.2), we will also exclude objects classified as spurious by M24, or after visual inspection, such as hot pixels and stellar spikes." (Color selection criteria); "We performed additional checks on the reliability of the five objects in the reference sample of F200W-dropout sources." (Selected sources over $\sim$0.2 deg$^2$)
2. **Compute photometric redshift probability distributions P(z) and evaluate low-z interloper solutions**: Multi-wavelength SED fitting using four independent codes across eight configurations  
  > "To alleviate the impact of specific flavours of the adopted techniques, and to broaden the range of spectral libraries explored, we have exploited four different codes: ZPHOT (cite: Fontana2000), EAZY (cite: Brammer2008), *BAGPIPES* (cite: Carnall2018; Carnall2019b) and CIGALE (cite: Boquien2019)." (The photometric redshift probability distribution); "To summarise, from the combination of codes and assumptions adopted we have obtained eight different $P(z)$ for all our candidates." (The photometric redshift probability distribution)
   - software: ZPHOT, EAZY, BAGPIPES, CLOUDY, CIGALE
   - parameter: BAGPIPES live points increased from 400 to 2000, ionization parameter up to log U = -1  
  > "Following (cite: Gandolfi2025), we increased the number of live points (i.e., the walkers used by BAGPIPES in the Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling) from the default 400 to 2000 to enhance sensitivity to strong line emitter solutions, and allowed the ionization parameter to reach log\,$U = -1$." (The photometric redshift probability distribution)
   - assumption: Chabrier (2003) IMF (or BPASS IMF cutoff 300 M_sun in BAGPIPES), Calzetti (2000) attenuation law, flat LambdaCDM (H0=70, Omega_M=0.30)  
  > "Throughout the paper we adopt AB magnitudes (cite: Oke1983), a (cite: Chabrier2003) initial mass function (IMF) in the range 0.1-100 M$_{\odot}$, the (cite: Calzetti2000) attenuation law, and a flat $\Lambda$CDM concordance model (H$_0$ = 70.0 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_M=0.30$)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Chi-square and MCMC SED fitting across BC03, EAZY/Larson, BPASS+CLOUDY, and CIGALE+DALE2014 template sets  
  > "Rather than focusing on the best-fitting photometric redshift, we have used all these codes to compute the redshift probability distribution ($P(z)$)." (The photometric redshift probability distribution)

## Qualitative findings

- Five galaxy candidates are identified at 15 <= z <= 20 and none at z >= 20, but all exhibit multimodal redshift probability distributions with alternative solutions at z ~ 3-7.  
  > "We identify five candidates at $15 \leq z \leq 20$, while no objects are found based on the $z\gtrsim20$ color selection criteria. Despite exhibiting a $>$1.5 mag break, all the objects display multimodal redshift probability distributions across different SED-fitting codes and methodologies." (abstract)
- Five F200W-dropout objects observed with NIRSpec PRISM are confirmed to be lower-redshift interlopers: four dusty star-forming galaxies at z ~ 2.2-6.6 and one low-mass passive galaxy at z = 4.91.  
  > "This conclusion is supported by the analysis of five F200W-dropout objects that we find to be interlopers on the basis of NIRSpec PRISM spectra: four dusty star-forming galaxies at z$\sim$2.2-6.6, and a passive galaxy at z=4.91 with log$(M_{\rm star}/{\rm M}_{\odot}) \lesssim$ 9." (abstract)
- Archival ALMA 870 um continuum observations of the z = 6.56 interloper UDS_56824 reveal a ~3 sigma dust continuum detection, confirming it as a heavily dust-obscured starburst galaxy.  
  > "A $\sim$3$\sigma$ detection is found at the NIRCam position of UDS_56824, with an integrated flux density of $S_{870_{\rm \mu m}}=0.54 \pm 0.19\,$mJy (see Fig. 6)." (UDS_56824 at $z_{spec}$=6.56); "and a highly attenuated ($E(B-V)=0.8$) starburst galaxy at $z=6.56$ with mass $\log M_*/M_\odot = 9.8$." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The ASTRODEEP-JWST catalog provides consistent photometric measurements across the seven major deep JWST surveys (~615 sq. arcmin), and archival ALMA Band 7 data covers the NIRSpec-confirmed dusty interloper UDS_56824.  
  > "In this paper we analyse the ASTRODEEP-JWST photometric sample (cite: Merlin2024), which provides consistent measurements on the major JWST deep surveys, to select bright galaxy candidates at z$\sim$15-30." (Introduction); "We checked the ALMA archive finding that the position of UDS_56824 has been observed by project #2015.1.01074.S (PI H. Inami) that we analyse as follows." (UDS_56824 at $z_{spec}$=6.56)
- Why this method: Used four SED-fitting codes across eight configurations to avoid bias from specific template sets, and bracketed the UV LF with two extreme contamination cases instead of P(z) weighting because the number density of faint red interlopers is unconstrained.  
  > "To alleviate the impact of specific flavours of the adopted techniques, and to broaden the range of spectral libraries explored, we have exploited four different codes: ZPHOT (cite: Fontana2000), EAZY (cite: Brammer2008), *BAGPIPES* (cite: Carnall2018; Carnall2019b) and CIGALE (cite: Boquien2019)." (The photometric redshift probability distribution); "we believe that the knowledge of the population of potential interlopers in LBG selections at these extreme redshifts is too uncertain at the moment, and we prefer to bracket the various options with the two opposite scenarios described above." (The computation of the LF)
- Alternative: Weighting the UV luminosity function by the photometric redshift probability distribution P(z) (rejected due to high uncertainty in the poorly known interloper populations)  
  > "We underline that our procedure for estimating the binned LF does not attempt to include the effect of contamination by leveraging the information contained in $P(z)$ to weight the number of observed objects (cite: Donnan2024)." (The computation of the LF)
- Alternative: Adopting a strict Delta chi^2 > 4 selection criterion between high-z and low-z SED solutions (none of the faint z > 15 candidates pass this threshold)  
  > "We remark that, given the resulting $P(z)$, none of our candidates would pass a selection criterion such as $\Delta\chi^2 >$4 between different redshift solutions that has been often adopted to build luminosity functions at $z>9$ (cite: Finkelstein2023b; Harikane2022b)." (The photometric redshift probability distribution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): None of the five z ~ 15-20 candidates can be unambiguously confirmed photometrically because all exhibit viable lower-redshift solutions (z ~ 3-7) across all SED-fitting codes.  
  > "The first is that all our objects are, in principle, credible candidates at $15<z<20$, but none of them are solid enough to be unambiguously assigned these extreme redshifts." (The photometric redshift probability distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Existing MIRI imaging is too shallow by 1-4 magnitudes to rule out low-redshift solutions for most candidates.  
  > "In fact, the MIRI imaging should be 1-2 mag deeper in the F770W and F1000W bands, and $>$3-4 mag deeper at $\lambda_{obs}>$15$\mu$m to yield meaningful constraints for the other candidates." (The photometric redshift probability distribution)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA 870 um continuum detection of UDS_56824 is only ~3 sigma, and a Single-band submillimeter flux requires an assumed dust temperature (25 K vs 50 K) to derive dust mass.  
  > "A $\sim$3$\sigma$ detection is found at the NIRCam position of UDS_56824, with an integrated flux density of $S_{870_{\rm \mu m}}=0.54 \pm 0.19\,$mJy (see Fig. 6)." (UDS_56824 at $z_{spec}$=6.56); "Assuming a higher dust temperature of T$_d=50$\,K would lower the dust-to-stellar mass ratio to log$(M_d/M_{star}) \sim$ -2.0" (UDS_56824 at $z_{spec}$=6.56)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA continuum imaging of UDS_56824 mentions testing a uvtaper in tclean, but does not state whether the reported 0.54 +/- 0.19 mJy flux was measured on the untapered natural-weighted map or a tapered map, nor the resulting beam size.
