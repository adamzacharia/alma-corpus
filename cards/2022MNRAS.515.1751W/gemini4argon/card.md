## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA line and continuum visibilities**: Calibrate visibilities with the CASA pipeline, subtract continuum in the uv plane with uvcontsub where continuum is detected, and image line cubes and mfs continuum maps with tclean using natural/Briggs weighting and uv tapers.  
  > "All data, including previous observations discussed above (see Table 2), were calibrated and reduced with the automated pipeline of the Common Astronomy Software Application (casa; McMullin et al. 2007) version 5.6 (with the exception of 2015.1.00540.S and 2015.1.01111.S, which required version 4.7)." (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction); "separate the continuum and line visibilities specifically for imaging the [C ii] emission in COS-3018555981, UVISTA-Z-001, and UVISTA-Z-019 as well as the [O iii] emission in UVISTA-Z-001, where the underlying dust continuum is confidently detected" (Section 2.2.2); "Continuum images were created by averaging (i.e. the mfs mode in tclean) over all available spectral channels that were well outside the relevant emission line range (i.e. |𝑣| > 1000 km/s)." (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: tclean threshold set to 3x theoretical sensitivity  
  > "These sensitivities, which agree well with the empirically measured RMS noise, were computed for use in the tclean task, where the threshold parameter was set to three times this value." (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction)
   - parameter: 0.6'' uv taper for total [O III] and [C II] line fluxes; |v| > 1000 km/s for mfs continuum  
  > "For measuring the total [O iii] and [C ii] line fluxes, a moderately (0.6′′) tapered image was chosen to avoid resolving out extended emission" (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction); "Continuum images were created by averaging (i.e. the mfs mode in tclean) over all available spectral channels that were well outside the relevant emission line range (i.e. |𝑣| > 1000 km/s)." (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction)
   - assumption: Antenna efficiency factor η ~ 0.7 (A_eff ~ 80 m^2 for 12-m dishes)  
  > "which is equal to ∼ 80 m2 for the 12-meter ALMA antennae assuming an efficiency factor of 𝜂 ∼ 0.7 (Cortes et al. 2020)." (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared empirically measured RMS noise in naturally weighted images to theoretical interferometric sensitivity  
  > "Furthermore, the measured root-mean-square (RMS) noise in naturally weighted images is compared in Table 2 to the theoretically expected value of the sensitivity of an interferometric image (Thompson et al. 2017)" (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction)
2. **Measure dust continuum fluxes, source sizes, and upper limits**: Measure continuum flux densities and deconvolved source sizes with 2D Gaussian fits (CASA imfit) or matched apertures on beam-matched tapered maps; place 3σ upper limits from RMS noise in beam/source-sized apertures.  
  > "we place 3𝜎 upper limits at ∼ 90 μm by empirically measuring the RMS noise in a beam-shaped aperture matched in (convolved) size to the source extent at ∼ 160 μm" (Section 3.1); "For detections, deconvolved sizes 𝐴dust as measured by the casa imfit procedure are given." (Table 3)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: 3σ upper limits; 10% systematic flux error (Bands 5, 6) and 20% (Band 8)  
  > "In estimating the uncertainties on our continuum detections as well as upper limits, we conservatively take a systematic flux calibration uncertainty into account (10% in band 5 and 6, 20% in band 8)." (3.1 Dust continuum)
   - assumption: Cosmological parameters Ω_m = 0.3, Ω_Λ = 0.7, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1  
  > "In our analysis, we adopt the cosmological parameters Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7, and 𝐻0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1 throughout (implying an angular scale of 5.2 kpc/arcsec at 𝑧 = 7)." (1 INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fit via CASA imfit

## Qualitative findings

- All five z ~ 7 LBGs are confidently detected (> 5σ) in [O III] 88 μm, with compact [O III] morphology aligning well with HST rest-frame UV while [C II] 158 μm is more spatially extended.  
  > "We perform a multi-wavelength comparison with new deep HST images of the rest-frame UV, whose compact morphology aligns well with [O iii] tracing ionised gas. By contrast, we find more spatially extended [C ii] emission likely produced in neutral gas" (abstract); "Defining the SNR as that measured for the peak pixel, the [O iii] and [C ii] lines are detected with a significance of at least SNR > 5 in all five sources." (3.3 [O iii] 88 μm and [C ii] 158 μm)
- The [N II] 205 μm non-detection in COS-2987030247 sets a lower limit L_[C II]/L_[N II] > 4.8 (3σ), consistent with [C II] originating in neutral PDR-like gas rather than purely in H II regions.  
  > "The non-detection of [N ii] 205 μm in COS-2987030247 allows us to set a lower limit on the [C ii]/[N ii] ratio (𝐿[C ii]/𝐿[N ii] > 4.8 at 3𝜎), which is fully consistent with [C ii] production in a PDR-like medium, and renders H ii regions the unlikely primary origin of the [C ii] emission" (6 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Cloudy photoionisation models require an enhanced nebular α/Fe ratio (X_α/Fe,neb = 4) and near-solar nebular oxygen abundance to reproduce the observed [O III] 88 μm strength relative to SFR and EW([O III] + Hβ).  
  > "Surprisingly, however, our models fail to reproduce the strength of [O iii] 88 μm, unless we assume an α/Fe enhancement and near-solar nebular oxygen abundance." (abstract); "Out of a large range of models, we find the most consistent are those where specifically α elements have near-solar nebular abundances, i.e. [α/H]neb ∼ 0" (5.5 Optical and FIR lines of [O iii])

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected five UV-bright z ~ 7 LBGs previously confirmed via [C II] 158 μm rather than Lyα to avoid selection bias towards overdense ionised bubbles, and observed [O III] 88 μm, [N II] 205 μm, dust continuum, and HST rest-frame UV to jointly constrain dust and ionised/neutral gas properties.  
  > "Moreover, the sample considered in this work arguably has a weaker selection bias since, in contrast to the existing studies, all galaxies have previously been spectroscopically confirmed through [C ii] instead of Lyα." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Observations were performed with WFC3 using the F140W (𝐽𝐻140) filter with a 5 ks exposure per target, motivated by the goal of properly resolving the spatial substructure of the rest-frame UV." (2.3 HST imaging)
- Why this method: Used a 0.6'' uv taper for total line fluxes to avoid resolving out extended emission while maintaining SNR; developed mercurius to treat continuum detections and upper limits uniformly under self-consistent opacity; used peak temperature T_peak to compare dust temperatures without opacity model degeneracies.  
  > "For measuring the total [O iii] and [C ii] line fluxes, a moderately (0.6′′) tapered image was chosen to avoid resolving out extended emission (see e.g. Carniani et al. 2018a, 2020) as best as possible while maintaining a good signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)." (2.2.2 ALMA data reduction); "The full code of this routine which treats detections and upper limits uniformly and we therefore refer to as mercurius (Multimodal Estimation Routine for the Cosmological Unravelling of Rest-frame Infrared Uniformised Spectra), is available online." (4.1 Dust SED fitting procedure); "Importantly, the peak temperature offers a way to compare observations of the dust temperature consistently since this approach avoids degeneracies introduced by the chosen opacity model" (APPENDIX A: DUST PEAK TEMPERATURE MEASUREMENTS)
- Alternative: Tested optically thin, self-consistent λ_0, and fixed λ_0 = 200 μm opacity models, as well as higher dust emissivity (1.8 <= β_IR <= 2) and flat vs. gamma temperature priors in dust SED fitting.  
  > "we note that there is little difference when assuming a flat prior, but a maximum temperature has to be assumed" (4.1 Dust SED fitting procedure); "either an entirely optically thin SED or the general opacity model" (Section 4.1); "Finally, we find yet a lower dust temperature and higher mass and yield increase if we instead assume a higher dust emissivity 1.8 ≲ 𝛽IR ≲ 2" (4.2 The build-up of dust: masses and yields)
- Alternative: Considered AGB stars as the main source of dust production at z ~ 7, rejected because the required dust yield per AGB star far exceeds theoretical expectations (~0.02 M_sun).  
  > "This renders AGB stars an unlikely candidate as the main source of dust formation in this early cosmic epoch" (Section 4.2)
- Alternative: Explored variations in Cloudy models: varying gas density (log10 n_H = 0.5 to 3), stripping the PDR (stopping at H_2 fraction 10^-6), enhancing α/Fe, lowering C/O, and increasing cosmic ray rate by 10x.  
  > "In addition to these “vanilla” simulations of a H ii region combined with a PDR, we also consider various deviations from the default scenario, whose impact will be discussed further in the next sections." (5.3 Comparing observed emission line strengths to the predictions of photoionisation models)

- Limitation (authors): Dust mass estimates are subject to systematic uncertainty from the unknown dust grain composition (κ_ν,ref can lower M_dust by ~3x or increase it by ~1.5x) and from not sampling the Rayleigh-Jeans tail.
