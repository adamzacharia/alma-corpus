## Analysis recipe

1. **Gravitational lens modelling and pixelised source-plane reconstruction of continuum and line cubes**: Simultaneously fit Band 4 continuum visibilities (time-averaged to 20s) and Band 7 visibilities in the uv-plane using an elliptical power-law mass profile and linear inversion on a randomized Voronoi source grid; reconstruct channel slices by averaging 50 random Voronoi pixelisations onto a 100x100 grid.  
  > "The linear combination of the sets of model visibilities that best fits the observed visibilities then recovers the source surface brightness distribution for a given lens model." (Lens modelling); "To determine our optimal lens model, we simultaneously fitted the visibilities from all four SPWs in the band 4 continuum data from this paper and the band 7 visibilities from D18." (Lens Model); "All 50 random Voronoi pixelisations were then individually binned onto a fine grid of $100 \times 100$ square pixels across the source plane and their mean computed to arrive at the final source reconstruction for a given cube slice." (Source Reconstruction)
   - software: MIRIAD, uvmodel, NeuralNest
   - parameter: 0.8 x 0.8 arcsec source plane with 400 Voronoi pixels, 7 randomisations per likelihood evaluation, 50 randomisations averaged onto 100 x 100 square pixels for final reconstruction, 20s visibility time-averaging in Band 4  
  > "We reconstructed the lensed source on a $0.8 \times 0.8$ arcsec source plane consisting of 400 Voronoi pixels formed using the method described in D18." (Source Reconstruction); "When optimising the lens model, we computed seven randomisations of the source plane pixelisation for each trial set of lens model parameters and returned the median likelihood of these seven randomisations to `NeuralNest`." (Source Reconstruction); "We used the band 7 visibilities as presented in D18 but we time-averaged the more numerous band 4 visibilities into bins of 20 s to improve computational efficiency." (Lens Model)
   - assumption: Elliptical power-law volume mass density profile for the foreground lens with zero external shear  
  > "Following the lens modelling in D18, we used an elliptical power-law density profile for the foreground lens, using the form given by (cite: kassiola1993)" (Lens Model); "This agrees with the findings of D18 and hence a parameterisation of external shear was not included in the final lens model which comprised six parameters in total." (Lens Model)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling with neural network proposals via NeuralNest, returning median likelihood of 7 random source pixelisations per trial  
  > "In this work, lens model optimisation was performed with `NeuralNest` (cite: moss2020) which uses a neural network to generate highly efficient proposals for Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling." (Lens modelling)
   - validation: Tested adding external shear and a two-component lens model (no significant improvement, ~5% magnification difference); tested varying temporal bin size by a factor of several; quantified spatially-varying source-plane PSF using a 3x3 grid of simulated point sources  
  > "We also tried a lens model comprising two separate mass components as used by (cite: bussmann2012) and as in D18, we found no significant improvement in the fit compared to a single ellipsoidal power-law." (Lens Model); "First, we generated a set of model visibilities of a lensed image of a point source created using the best fit lens model." (Source Reconstruction)
2. **Far-IR SED fitting to derive dust mass, FIR luminosity, and star formation rate**: Fit rest-frame continuum photometry with an optically thick single-temperature modified black-body SED and an optically thin two-temperature modified black-body SED, integrate from 8 to 1000 um for LFIR, and convert to SFR.  
  > "Following D18, we fitted the rest-frame photometry with a modified black-body SED (referred to hereafter as the ’optically thick SED’ ) comprising a single dust temperature component as well as an optically thin SED with two dust temperature components." (Far-IR Spectral Energy Distribution); "Integrating these SEDs from 8-1000 $\mu$m gives a far-IR luminosity, $L_{\rm FIR}$, of $1.6\times10^{13}$ L$_\odot$ and $1.5\times10^{13}$ L$_\odot$ for the optically thin and thick SEDs respectively." (Far-IR Spectral Energy Distribution)
   - parameter: 4 degrees of freedom for optically thin two-component SED; 3 degrees of freedom for optically thick single-component SED; integration range 8-1000 um  
  > "We allowed the temperature and normalisation of both components to vary in the fit for the optically thin SED giving four degrees of freedom. For the optically thick SED, temperature, normalisation and the 100 $\mu$m-opacity, $\tau_{100}$, were varied in the fit, giving three degrees of freedom." (Far-IR Spectral Energy Distribution)
   - assumption: Fixed emissivity index beta = 2.0; dust mass absorption coefficient kappa_168um = 1.7 m^2 kg^-1; Kennicutt & Evans (2012) SFR conversion with Kroupa (2003) IMF  
  > "For this, we took the observed ALMA 880 $\mu$m flux density of $91\pm5$ mJy from the band 7 data and a dust mass absorption coefficient at the corresponding rest-frame wavelength, 168 $\mu$m, of 1.7 m$^2$kg$^{-1}$ from (cite: james2002). Both SEDs were fitted with a fixed emissivity index of 2.0 (cite: smith2013)." (Far-IR Spectral Energy Distribution); "Taking the mean of these luminosities and converting it to a star formation rate (SFR) according to the prescription given by (cite: kennicutt2012), which uses the initial mass function given by (cite: kroupa2003), gives a value of $2400\pm500$ M$_\odot$yr$^{-1}$." (Far-IR Spectral Energy Distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared results against full multi-wavelength MAGPHYS SED fitting  
  > "based on our own SED derived from solely the far-IR photometry" (The stellar mass)
3. **Measure spatial variation of ISM excitation temperature and molecular mass fraction from [CI] lines**: Divide reconstructed [CI](2-1) and [CI](1-0) source images into a stack of five east-west slices, compute line luminosity ratio R = L'(2-1)/L'(1-0), and convert to excitation temperature and molecular mass fraction per slice.  
  > "Therefore, we divided the \[ C i \] (2-1) and \[ C i \] (1-0) source images in Fig. 2 into a stack of five slices running east-west, each 0.2 arcsec long and 0.08 arcsec wide, with the stack positioned to cover the main \[ C i \] emission." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM); "Assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium, we then converted the line ratio into an excitation temperature using T$_{\rm ex}=38.8{\rm K}/\ln(2.11/R_{\rm CI})$ (cite: stutzki1997)." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM)
   - parameter: 5 slices running east-west, each 0.2 arcsec long and 0.08 arcsec wide  
  > "Therefore, we divided the \[ C i \] (2-1) and \[ C i \] (1-0) source images in Fig. 2 into a stack of five slices running east-west, each 0.2 arcsec long and 0.08 arcsec wide, with the stack positioned to cover the main \[ C i \] emission." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM)
   - assumption: Optically thin [CI] lines and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Since the \[ C i \] lines are optically thin, it is reasonably straightforward to use the ratio of the \[ C i \] (2-1) and \[ C i \] (1-0) lines to investigate the variation of the temperature in the ISM." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM); "Assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium, we then converted the line ratio into an excitation temperature using T$_{\rm ex}=38.8{\rm K}/\ln(2.11/R_{\rm CI})$ (cite: stutzki1997)." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified world coordinate system alignment between Band 3 and Band 4 continuum images to confirm [CI](1-0) spatial offset is real  
  > "Initially concerned that this may simply be the result of an offset in the world co-ordinate system between the band 3 data (where the \[ C i \] (1-0) line is located) and band 4 data, we confirmed that the continuum images between both bands align perfectly." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM)
4. **Estimate total molecular ISM mass using four tracers and five methods**: Calculate de-magnified luminosities of CO(1-0), [CI](1-0), [CII] 158um, and rest-frame 850um dust continuum (extrapolated from 3290um), and apply Galactic-calibrated conversion factors individually and via a combined CO/[CI]/dust method.  
  > "In this section, we estimate the mass of the ISM in ID141 using four different tracers and five different methods." (First Estimates of the Mass of the ISM); "(cite: dunne2021a) have used several samples of calibration galaxies to derive an optimal technique for estimating the calibration factors ($\alpha_{\rm CO}$, $\alpha_{[CI]}$, $\alpha_{\rm dust}$) for an individual galaxy and for producing a best estimate of the ISM mass in the galaxy that uses all three." (Combining Three Methods)
   - parameter: Magnification factors: mu = 5.5 for CO(1-0) and 3290um dust, mu = 5.6 for [CI](1-0), mu = 5.9 for [CII]  
  > "We have corrected this using the magnification factor of 5.5 derived for band 3." (The gas mass from CO); "Instead, we simply used the global line flux given by (cite: cox2011), corrected it for the magnification factor of 5.9 we measured for the dust continuum emission in band 4" (The gas mass from [CII]); "$\mu_{\rm tot}$ : 6.2 5.8 6.3 5.6" (Table 4)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 3.0 +/- 0.5 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 with 1.36 helium factor and mean GDR = 135; alpha_[CI] = 18.8 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1; alpha_CII = 30 M_sun/L_sun; alpha_850um = 6.7 +/- 1.7 x 10^19 ergs s^-1 Hz^-1 M_sun^-1 with T_d = 25 K and beta = 1.8  
  > "They derive a calibration factor $\alpha_{\rm CO}= 3.0\pm0.5\,$M$_{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$pc$^2$)$^{-1}$. Using this value and including a factor of 1.36 to allow for the elements other than hydrogen gives an estimate of the mass of the ISM of $(3.3\pm0.7) \times 10^{11}$M$_{\odot}$" (The gas mass from CO); "Using their calibration sample (see above), (cite: dunne2021a) have derived a value for the \[ C i \] calibration factor of $\rm \alpha_{[CI]} = 18.8\ M_{\odot} (K\ km\ s^{-1} pc^2)^{-1}$." (The gas mass from [CI]); "Zanella et al. derive a \[ C ii \] to $H_2$ conversion ratio of $\alpha_{\rm CII} \simeq 30$ M$_{\odot}/$L$_{\odot}$." (The gas mass from [CII]); "We estimated the luminosity at rest-frame 850 $\mu$m, using a dust temperature of 25K and an emissivity index of ($\beta=1.8$), the same values used by (cite: scoville2016)." (The gas mass from dust emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Quantified potential differential magnification error by testing a source with twice the Band 3 diameter (only 3% lower magnification); compared five methods against each other  
  > "We have quantified a typical size of the effect by computing the magnification factor of a source that has twice the diameter of the band 3 source shown in Fig. 1, finding this to be only 3 per cent lower than the original magnification." (Results)

## Qualitative findings

- The [CI](1-0) emission is spatially offset by ~1 kpc to the south compared to [CI](2-1), CO(7-6), H2O, and dust continuum, revealing a large reservoir of cool (~40 K) molecular gas that dominates the molecular mass.  
  > "This demonstrates that although the gas is much hotter in the north, most of the mass of the molecular phase is in the colder gas to the south." (The Temperature Distribution in the ISM); "The \[ C i \] (1-0) emission is offset by $\simeq 1$ kpc from the other lines, including the \[ C i \] (2-1) line." (Conclusions)
- The kinematics of ID141 are dominated by a rotationally-supported unstable gas disk (Q_gas <= 0.6), with asymmetric northern emission that may be a minor disturbance, a second merging disk, or two additional clumps.  
  > "Our modelling of the kinematics reveals that the system appears to be dominated by a rotationally-supported gas disk with evidence of a nearby perturber." (Abstract); "imply that $Q\simeq$0.6 in the central few hundred parsecs of the galaxy, falling to a lower value beyond a radius of 1 kpc" (How quickly will ID141 become a quiescent galaxy?)
- All four ISM tracers (CO, [CI], [CII], dust) agree on an ISM mass ~4 times larger than the dynamical mass because they are all benchmarked to Milky Way calibrations, whereas rapid chemical evolution with a top-heavy IMF lowers gas-to-tracer ratios by ~4x.  
  > "We suggest that this conflict and other similar conflicts reported in the literature is because the gas-to-tracer ratios are $\simeq$4 times lower than the Galactic values used to calibrate the ISM in high-redshift galaxies." (Abstract); "We demonstrate that this could result from a top-heavy initial mass function and strong chemical evolution." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ID141 is strongly lensed (magnification ~6), intrinsically hyper-luminous, and at z=4.24 where both [CI] lines, CO(7-6), and H2O fall into atmospherically transparent ALMA Bands 3 and 4, enabling high-resolution (~350 pc) mapping of both warm and cool ISM phases.  
  > "To circumvent these shortfalls, we have therefore embarked on a second high-resolution study of the ISM using a galaxy at a redshift such that all of the key spectral lines are visible to ALMA." (Introduction); "At this redshift, both \[ C i \] lines, the bright \[ C ii \]  line at 158 $\mu$m, multiple CO lines (including the CO(1-0) line in the near future) and water lines, and many other lines are visible to ALMA." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Visibility-plane lens modelling with randomized Voronoi pixelisation and NeuralNest avoids clean-image correlated noise and stagnation in local likelihood optima; 3DBarolo fits the full 3D cube directly to avoid beam-smearing flattening of 2D velocity fields; four different ISM tracers test whether the gas-vs-dynamical mass discrepancy is specific to CO excitation or due to global chemical enrichment.  
  > "This alleviates the effect reported in (cite: nightingale2018) whereby lens model optimisation can occasionally stagnate when exceptionally good pixelisations occur by chance." (Source Reconstruction); "Instead of fitting a kinematical model to the 2D velocity field, this code fits directly to the full datacube. This avoids the effect of artificial flattening of velocity fields due to inadequate spatial resolution" (The kinematics of the source); "In this work, we find that four different tracers yield the same excessive gas mass. It is unlikely that all four tracer conversion factors would need to be scaled by the same amount and so we have sought a more natural explanation in the form of a chemical evolution model" (Introduction)
