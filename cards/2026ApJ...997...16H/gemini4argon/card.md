## Science

- Fields: star_formation, astrochemistry, milky_way_ism
- Question: What are the detection rates, spatial distributions, and chemical segregation of CH3OH, N2H+, CCS, and c-C3HD in starless cores of the Orion A and B molecular clouds?  
  > "We present a chemical survey of 16 starless cores (including five prestellar cores) in the Orion A and B molecular clouds, targeting CH$_3$OH, N$_2$H$^+$, CCS, and c-C$_3$HD, using the Atacama Compact Array (ACA) and the Yebes 40-m telescope." (Abstract); "Imaging observations similar to those in (cite: 2025Hsu_ALMASOP_starless) will allow one to further investigate the critical role of COMs in elucidating the physical and chemical processes within the starless cores in a cloud environment that may be different from those in the Taurus and Perseus clouds." 1. **Image ACA Band 3 continuum and molecular line datacubes**: Clean continuum and spectral line visibilities using tclean with Briggs weighting

  > "The Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 2.0 was applied for better illustrating the extended emission and improving the sensitivities." (ACA Band 3 (3 mm) Observations)
   - parameter: threshold = 1.2 mJy (continuum), 100 mJy (spectral channels)  
  > "The imaging thresholds were 1.2 mJy for the full-band ($\sim$0.857 GHz) continuum and 100 mJy for individual channels (with a spectral resolution of 61 kHz per channel)." (ACA Band 3 (3 mm) Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured channel and continuum rms noise levels across all sources (Table [tab:appx:noise])  
  > "show the ACA observational beam sizes and noise levels, respectively, for the continuum and each spectral window (SPW)." (Observational Parameters)
2. **Identify molecular line detections in ACA cubes**: Search for voxels exceeding 5 times the rms noise level within a spatial and velocity sub-cube on non-primary-beam-corrected cubes  
  > "First, we adopted the datacube without primary beam correction so that the voxels across the entire image have similar noise levels." (Detection); "A molecule is considered detected if any voxel has a flux density above five times the noise level within a sub-cube centered on the target position within a circular region of 25$''$ (10,000 au) and centered on the source $v_\mathrm{LSR}$ covering $\pm$3 km s$^{-1}$ in velocity." (Detection)
   - parameter: Circular region radius/aperture = 25'' (10,000 au), velocity range = v_LSR +/- 3 km s^-1, SNR threshold = 5  
  > "A molecule is considered detected if any voxel has a flux density above five times the noise level within a sub-cube centered on the target position within a circular region of 25$''$ (10,000 au) and centered on the source $v_\mathrm{LSR}$ covering $\pm$3 km s$^{-1}$ in velocity." (Detection)
   - assumption: Distance of ~400 pc for converting 25'' to 10,000 au  
  > "These cores are distributed in the Orion A or B clouds with a distance of $\sim400$ pc (cite: 2011Lombardi_2MASS_extinction_IV; 2018Kounkel_APOGEE2)." (Sample Selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Generate integrated intensity (moment 0) maps**: Integrate emission over the longest contiguous velocity channel range with signal > 3 sigma (and also a fixed v_LSR +/- 1 km s^-1 interval), then apply primary beam correction  
  > "While the SNR for determining the molecular detection was five for a more robust criteria, integration interval was defined as the longest contiguous range of velocity channels in which the signal exceeded three times the noise level." (Morphology); "We finally applied the primary beam correction on the integrated maps." (Morphology); "In the main text, we present Figures 3 and 4, where a fixed integration interval of $v_\mathrm{LSR}\pm1$ km s$^{-1}$ is adopted." (Morphology)
   - parameter: 3 sigma contiguous channel threshold within 25'' and v_LSR +/- 3 km s^-1; fixed v_LSR +/- 1 km s^-1 for main text figures  
  > "We then extracted sub-cubes covering $\pm 3$ km s$^{-1}$ around the source $v_\mathrm{LSR}$, within a 25$''$ (10,000 au) circular region centered on the target position." (Morphology); "While the SNR for determining the molecular detection was five for a more robust criteria, integration interval was defined as the longest contiguous range of velocity channels in which the signal exceeded three times the noise level." (Morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_

  > "The overall morphologies are consistent with those derived from the noise-based intervals." (Morphology)

  > "Assuming optically thin and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with a fixed rotational temperature of 10 K (cite: 2024Scibelli_COM_Perseus), we derive the molecular column density maps for each species." (Morphology); "First, assuming optically thin and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), we derive the upper upper state column density $N_\mathrm{u}$ from the integrated intensity of transition in terms of the products of brightness temperature and velocity $W$ via:" (Integrated Intensity Images and Column Densities); "Table 1 lists the peak of the derived column density ($N^{p}_\mathrm{tot}$) within a 25$''$ (10,000 au) circular aperture centered on the target position." (Integrated Intensity Images and Column Densities)
   - parameter: T_rot = 10 K; aperture = 25'' (10,000 au)  
  > "Assuming optically thin and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with a fixed rotational temperature of 10 K (cite: 2024Scibelli_COM_Perseus), we derive the molecular column density maps for each species." (Morphology); "Moreover, we list in Table 1 the peak column densities for each species within a circular aperture of 25$^{\prime\prime}$(10,000 au) centered on each target position." (Morphology)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and LTE at fixed rotational temperature T_rot = 10 K  
  > "Assuming optically thin and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with a fixed rotational temperature of 10 K (cite: 2024Scibelli_COM_Perseus), we derive the molecular column density maps for each species." (Morphology)

(Comparisons with Single-dish Observations)
   - software: GILDAS, CLASS, CASA, imsmooth
   - parameter: Convolved circular beam FWHM = 36''; Yebes extraction window = 5 MHz; modeled T_rot = 5 K and 10 K (and evaluated at 7.5 K)  
  > "We conducted baseline subtraction for each source and extracted the spectrum centered at the expected line frequency with a 5 MHz window." (Yebes Observation at Q Band); "First, we used the `imsmooth` task in `CASA` to convolve the ACA datacube to a circular beam comparable to the Yebes 40-m aperture (FWHM = 36$''$ at 48.4 GHz)." (Comparisons with Single-dish Observations)

## Qualitative findings

- CH3OH is detected toward all 16 targeted starless cores in ACA observations, confirming its ubiquity in Orion starless cores.  
  > "CH$_3$OH was detected toward all targets, confirming its ubiquity in starless cores, consistent with previous surveys in Taurus and Perseus." (Abstract)
- CH3OH, CCS, and c-C3HD generally trace the outer layers of the dense cores outlined by N2H+, each showing distinct spatial distributions.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Expanded starless core COM surveys from Taurus and Perseus to Orion, which experiences stronger supersonic turbulence and more intense UV radiation fields; combined ACA for spatially resolved maps (~18'') with Yebes 40-m single-dish observations to evaluate interferometric missing flux.  
  > "While being at a farther distance of 400 pc (as compared to Taurus at 150 pc and Perseus cloud at 300 pc) (cite: 2010Lombardi_2MASS_extinction_III; 2011Lombardi_2MASS_extinction_IV; 2018Zucker_Perseus_distance), the Orion molecular clouds are known to experience stronger supersonic turbulence and more intense UV radiation fields (cite: 2022Ha_SFR_turbulence; 2022Xia_SFR_UV)." (Introduction); "To assess the amount of missing flux, we compare our data with observations from the Yebes 40-m telescope." (Comparisons with Single-dish Observations)
- Why this method: Used Briggs robust = 2.0 in tclean to better illustrate extended emission and improve sensitivity; focused on the N2H+ F1=0-1 hyperfine group at 93.176 GHz to minimize line blending at 61 kHz resolution and reduce optical depth effects.  
  > "For N$_2$H$^+$ $J=1-0$, we focus only on the hyperfine line group at 93.176 GHz ($F_1=0-1$) because (1) its hyperfine components are indistinguishable at our spectral resolution (61 kHz or 0.18  km s$^{-1}$), minimizing the effects of line blending in the channel maps, and (2) this group has the weakest intrinsic line strength of the three, making the emission comparatively less optically thick." (ACA Band 3 (3 mm) Observations); "The Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 2.0 was applied for better illustrating the extended emission and improving the sensitivities." (ACA Band 3 (3 mm) Observations)
- Alternative: Excitation / critical density effects as the cause of the layered CCS and c-C3HD distribution: rejected because c-C3HD has a lower critical density than CCS and would trace more diffuse gas if excitation dominated.  
  > "Consequently, $c$-C$_3$HD emission has a lower critical density than CCS emission and can trace more diffuse gas, which can not explain the observed morphology." (Segregation between Carbon-chain Molecules)
- Alternative: Deuterium enhancement or interferometric spatial filtering as the cause of centrally concentrated c-C3HD relative to CCS: considered as possible contributing factors, though single-dish maps of other cores also show non-coincident c-C3H2 and CCS.  
  > "Admittedly, spatial filtering by interferometers may affect the observed morphology. The more centrally concentrated distribution of c-C$_3$HD might also be explained by deuterium enhancement in dense, cold environments (cite: 2001Rodgers_deuterium; 2022Giers_L1544_cC3H2)." (Segregation between Carbon-chain Molecules)

- Limitation (authors): Limited number of detected transitions per molecule (single transition per species in ACA), requiring fixed rotational temperature (10 K) and LTE/optically thin assumptions.  
  > "Despite the limited number of detected transitions for each molecule, we estimated the column density associated with each core. Assuming optically thin and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with a fixed rotational temperature of 10 K (cite: 2024Scibelli_COM_Perseus), we derive the molecular column density maps for each species." (Morphology)
- Limitation (authors): Peak column densities (N^p_tot) within the 25'' aperture may be overestimated due to contamination from nearby bright protostellar sources.  
  > "Note that the density of the column $N^{p}_\mathrm{tot}$ may be overestimated due to nearby bright sources within the 25$^{\prime\prime}$aperture." (Morphology)
- Limitation (authors): Significant interferometric spatial filtering (missing flux up to an order of magnitude for CH3OH) in the ACA data, which may also affect observed morphologies.  
  > "At this temperature, six sources in our sample show column density ratios greater than $\sqrt{10}$, indicating that the ACA observations may be missing an order of magnitude of CH$_3$OH flux." (Comparisons with Single-dish Observations); "Admittedly, spatial filtering by interferometers may affect the observed morphology." (Segregation between Carbon-chain Molecules)
- Limitation (authors): Detection statistics alone cannot rule out specific CH3OH desorption mechanisms, and single-dish mapping is still needed to directly image the inferred extended CH3OH component.  
  > "Given the uniformly high detection rates under diverse physical conditions, detection statistics alone do not allow any desorption mechanism to be ruled out." (The Ubiquitous CH$_3$OH Gas in Starless Cores); "Images from single-dish observations are necessary to confirm this." (The Extended CH$_3$OH Component inferred from the Missing Flux)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 1 (coordinates and v_LSR) omits two of the 16 sources (G208.68-19.20N2 and G209.29-19.65S1), which appear in the Appendix tables and figures but lack listed J2000 coordinates and v_LSR values in the paper.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing ACA CH3OH 2(0,2)-1(0,1) (E_u = 6.96 K) to Yebes CH3OH 1(0,1)-0(0,0) (E_u = 2.32 K) to quantify missing flux conflates spatial filtering with potential sub-thermal (non-LTE) excitation differences between the two transitions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using deuterated c-C3HD as a proxy for main-isotopologue c-C3H2 when comparing with CCS introduces temperature- and density-dependent fractionation bias.
