## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate visibilities and extract 3mm spectra and continuum fluxes in uv space**: Calibrate ALMA data with observatory CASA script, export to uvfits/uv tables, and extract spectra in uv space with GILDAS (using a fixed Gaussian kernel from continuum for ALMA ID9316, and point-source models at ALMA positions for NOEMA targets)  
  > "We therefore extracted the spectra by fitting a point source model in the $uv$ space at the known spatial positions from the high-resolution ALMA imaging." (NOEMA); "We then exported the data into uvfits format to generate $uv$ tables to perform further analysis with the IRAM GILDAS working on the $uv$ space (visibility)." (ALMA); "The spectrum of each setup is extracted in the $uv$ space on the position of the continuum peak using a Gaussian kernel determined from the continuum and kept fixed in size and position in each spectral channel." (ALMA)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Gaussian kernel determined from continuum (for ID9316 ALMA); point source model at ALMA positions (for NOEMA)  
  > "The spectrum of each setup is extracted in the $uv$ space on the position of the continuum peak using a Gaussian kernel determined from the continuum and kept fixed in size and position in each spectral channel." (ALMA)
   - assumption: NOEMA sources are unresolved at 3mm  
  > "no source is expected to be resolved given the resolution from the compact NOEMA configurations used." (NOEMA)
   - fitting: Visibility-space fitting in GILDAS  
  > "We emphasize that we analyzed both NOEMA and ALMA data in $uv$ space using the same algorithms that were applied in (cite: Jin2019alma), (cite: Puglisi2019), and (cite: Valentino2018CI; Valentino2020CO; Valentino2020CI)." (ALMA)
   - validation: Compared ALMA 870 um photometry with SCUBA2 850 um super-deblended photometry and ID3117 SED fit with ACA 630 um photometry  
  > "As a sanity check, we compared the ALMA 870$\mu$m photometry for all COSMOS sources with the SCUBA2 850$\mu$m photometry from the super-deblended catalog, finding that they are in excellent agreement" (ALMA); "The source ID3117 has ACA (Atacama Compact Array) 630$\mu$m observations (2019.1.01832.S, PI: J. Zavala), and its 630$\mu$m flux from our SED fitting also agrees well with the ACA photometry." (ALMA)
2. **Blindly search for emission/absorption lines and measure spectroscopic redshifts and line fluxes**: Sweep through full 3 mm continuum-subtracted spectrum using line-search algorithm from Jin et al. (2019)  
  > "Adopting the line-search algorithm from (cite: Jin2019alma), we blindly searched for lines by sweeping through the full 3 mm continuum-subtracted spectrum." (Line detection and redshift identification)
   - assumption: For ID9316 CO(5-4) on the edge of the spectrum, line flux is estimated assuming an identical line profile to CO(4-3)  
  > "Notes: $^a$The CO(5-4) line is on edge of the spectra, we estimated the line flux assuming an identical line profile to CO(4-3)." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Every galaxy is detected in at least two transitions at 4.5-30 sigma  
  > "Every galaxy is detected in at least two transitions, where the brightest lines are detected with 6–14$\sigma$ and secondarily bright ones are detected at 4.5–6.0$\sigma$." (Line detection and redshift identification)
3. **Fit FIR/(sub)mm dust SEDs to derive L_IR, beta, T_d, M_d, and lambda_eff**: Fit deblended FIR photometry and ALMA/NOEMA continuum fluxes using three MBB setups: (1) optically thin without CMB, (2) optically thin with CMB correction, and (3) optically thick with CMB correction  
  > "Following the identical SED recipes adopted in (cite: Jin2019alma), we fitted the FIR/(sub)mm photometry of this sample in three ways, using: (1) optically thin MBB (modified black body) models from (cite: Magdis2012SED); (2) optically thin MBB models from (cite: Magdis2012SED) accounting for the CMB impact on the dust continuum following (cite: daCunha2013CMB); and (3) optically thick MBB models from (cite: Magdis2012SED) also including CMB following (cite: daCunha2013CMB)." (Cold dust SEDs and CMB impact)
   - parameter: L_IR integrated over 8-1000 um rest-frame; lambda_eff where optical depth tau = 1  
  > "Notes: $L_{\rm IR}$: IR luminosity at 8–1000${\rm \mu m}$ rest-frame corrected for CMB effect" (Table 4); "$\lambda_{\rm eff}$: effective wavelength of optical depth $\tau=1$." (Table 4)
   - assumption: Radio data points excluded from fit; SFR = L_IR * 10^-10  
  > "The radio data points are not directly used for the fittings, but we extrapolated the expected radio emission based on the measured IR luminosities using the mass-stratified IR–radio correlation from (cite: Delvecchio2021)." (Cold dust SEDs and CMB impact); "SFRs are converted from IR luminosity from the FIR SED fitting, that is, ${\rm SFR}=L_{\rm IR} \times 10^{-10}$." (High star formation efficiency from [CI](1-0))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Extrapolated radio emission from IR-radio correlation agrees with observed radio fluxes  
  > "The predicted radio fluxes are in good agreement with the observations for most cases in this sample, showing little evidence for radio AGN emission." (Cold dust SEDs and CMB impact)
4. **Measure dust continuum FWHM sizes and inclination angles in uv space**: Fit elliptical models to 870 um and/or 1mm ALMA visibilities in uv space following Puglisi et al. (2019) and Valentino et al. (2020)  
  > "We measure the sizes of the dust continuum in the $uv$ space using 870$\mu$m and/or 1mm observations, following the same method applied in (cite: Puglisi2019) and (cite: Valentino2020CI)." (Size measurements); "The inclinations are measured from the sizes of major and minor axes, which are obtained by fitting elliptical models to the ALMA emission of the galaxies[^1]." (Gas masses from optically thin dust models are overestimated)

## Qualitative findings

- Five dusty galaxies selected with FIR photometric redshifts z_phot > 6 are spectroscopically confirmed at lower redshifts (3.5 < z < 4.2), because optically thick dust and CMB effects make their SEDs appear abnormally cold with steep Rayleigh-Jeans slopes.  
  > "Using NOEMA and ALMA 3mm line scans, we measured spectroscopic redshifts of six new dusty galaxies at $3.5<z<4.2$ by solidly detecting \[CI\](1-0) and CO transitions." (abstract); "Dust SED analyses explain the $z_{\rm phot}$ overestimate from seemingly cold dust temperatures ($T_{\rm d}$) and steep Rayleigh-Jeans (RJ) slopes, providing additional examples of cold dusty galaxies impacted by the cosmic microwave background (CMB)." (abstract)
- Combining with literature samples, dust continuum FWHM size anti-correlates with IR luminosity: log(FWHM/kpc) = -0.38 * log(L_IR / 10^10 L_sun) + 1.42.  
  > "Interestingly, an anti-correlation is seen in the figure, that is, $$\begin{equation}
   {\rm log({\rm FWHM~size /kpc})} = -0.38\times{\rm log}(L_{\rm IR}/10^{10}L_{\odot})+1.42,
\end{equation}$$ with 1$\sigma$ scatter of 0.24 dex, indicating galaxies with higher IR luminosity tend to have a more compact dust morphology." (Size measurements)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NOEMA and ALMA 3mm line scans were chosen to spectroscopically confirm redshifts and detect [CI](1-0) and CO lines in optically dark, FIR-bright galaxies selected with z_phot,FIR > 6, while archival ALMA 870um and 1mm data provided high-resolution dust continuum sizes.  
  > "We re-observed the two sources ID5340 and 6309 with one NOEMA tuning setup in January and February 2021 (ID: W20DM, PIs: E. Daddi & S. Jin) in order to obtain solid \[CI\](1-0) detections." (NOEMA); "The ancillary 870$\mu$m and 1mm data from ALMA provide high-resolution imaging for our sample." (Size measurements)
- Why this method: Analyzing visibilities in uv space and using Super-deblended photometry matched the exact methodology of Jin et al. (2019), Puglisi et al. (2019), and Valentino et al. (2018, 2020), allowing direct comparison and sample merging; stellar and dust SEDs were fitted separately because stellar and dust emission are often spatially unassociated in high-z dusty galaxies.  
  > "We emphasize that we analyzed both NOEMA and ALMA data in $uv$ space using the same algorithms that were applied in (cite: Jin2019alma), (cite: Puglisi2019), and (cite: Valentino2018CI; Valentino2020CO; Valentino2020CI)." (ALMA); "Note that we are fitting stellar and dust SEDs separately rather than using an energy balance between optical and FIR. This is because dust and stellar components are often found to be spatially unassociated in high-z dusty galaxies" (Near-infrared and IRAC photometry and stellar mass)
- Alternative: Low star formation efficiency (long depletion timescale) or very high metallicity as the cause of cold dust temperatures and low <U>; rejected based on high [CI](1-0)-derived SFEs and dynamical mass constraints  
  > "Therefore, the low SFE/high-$Z$ explanation for cold dust is strongly disfavored, and the optically thick dust becomes the only rational explanation left." (High star formation efficiency from [CI](1-0))
- Alternative: Optical-FIR energy balance SED fitting (such as MAGPHYS); rejected because stellar and dust components are often spatially unassociated in high-z dusty galaxies  
  > "Note that we are fitting stellar and dust SEDs separately rather than using an energy balance between optical and FIR. This is because dust and stellar components are often found to be spatially unassociated in high-z dusty galaxies, e.g., (cite: Franco2018GOODSS; Hodge2019)." (Near-infrared and IRAC photometry and stellar mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stellar ages from NIR+IRAC SED fitting are not reliably constrained due to high dust attenuation  
  > "The age is not reliably constrained because of the high attenuation." (Near-infrared and IRAC photometry and stellar mass)
- Limitation (authors): Dynamical gas masses cannot be constrained for ID3117 (due to low and uncertain inclination i = 16 +/- 40 deg) and ID6309 (where M_* > M_dyn,2Re)  
  > "This is because ID3117 has a low and uncertain inclination angle of $i=16\pm40\degree$, making it impossible to obtain a reasonable constraint on its $M_{\rm gas,dyn}$. ID6309 has a reliable inclination measurement of $i=60\pm6\degree$ but shows excessive stellar mass with respect to the dynamical mass $M_{*}>M_{\rm dyn,2Re}$." (Footnote 1)
- Limitation (authors): Dynamical masses assume high-J CO(4-3) velocity widths and 1mm dust continuum sizes trace the same region, which could only be directly verified for ID9316  
  > "We were only able to verify this equivalence for the ALMA 3mm-detected ID9316 in our sample, and find that its CO(4-3) size ($0.39''\pm0.07''$) is in agreement with the dust continuum size (Table 6)." (Gas masses from optically thin dust models are overestimated)
- Limitation (authors): Global dust opacity tau_100um assuming spherical geometry is an upper limit because galaxies may be clumpy/composed of clouds, while global Sigma_IR is a lower limit  
  > "The SFR densities derived from global size measurements are lower limits to the actual $\Sigma_{\rm IR}$." (Cold dust temperature against high IR surface brightness $T_{\rm d}$–$\Sigma_{\rm IR}$); "Similarly to what was discussed in terms of the Stefan-Boltzmann law, because the galaxy might actually be composed of clouds, the opacity derived in this way is an upper limit." (High dust opacity at FIR)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Five of the six new spectroscopic redshifts and [CI](1-0) detections come from NOEMA rather than ALMA; only ID9316 has an ALMA 3mm line scan, although ALMA provides the crucial high-resolution 870um/1mm continuum sizes and fluxes for the COSMOS targets.
