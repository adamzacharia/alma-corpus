- Question: What are the physical and chemical properties of a star-forming core in a Galactic low-metallicity environment in the extreme outer Galaxy, and how do its molecular abundances compare to inner Galactic and LMC hot cores?  
  > "It is thus vital to understand universal characteristics of interstellar chemistry by studying chemical compositions of star-forming cores in diverse metallicity environments." (Introduction); "The low metallicity environment is in common with the Magellanic Clouds, and thus the extreme outer Galaxy is an ideal laboratory to test the universality of the low metallicity molecular chemistry observed in the LMC and SMC." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract spectra and measure continuum flux and source sizes**: Extract spectra and continuum flux from circular apertures (0.50'' and 1.93'' diameter for SMM1; 0.6'' diameter for SMM2) and fit 2D Gaussians to emission images to measure source sizes  
  > "The spectra and continuum flux are extracted from the 0.50$''$ diameter circular region centered at RA = 06$^\mathrm{h}$17$^\mathrm{m}$24.073s and Dec = 14$\ensuremath{^\circ}$54$\ensuremath{'}$42.27$''$ (ICRS)" (Data reduction); "We have also repeated the analysis for the spectra extracted from a 0.1 pc (1.93$''$) diameter region at the hot core position" (Fractional abundances and isotope abundance ratios); "We have estimated the spatial extent of observed emission by fitting a two-dimensional Gaussian to the continuum center (Table 3)." (Distribution of molecular line emission and dust continuum)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Aperture diameters: 0.50'' (0.026 pc) and 1.93'' (0.1 pc) for SMM1; 0.6'' for SMM2  
  > "The spectra and continuum flux are extracted from the 0.50$''$ diameter circular region" (Data reduction); "We have also repeated the analysis for the spectra extracted from a 0.1 pc (1.93$''$) diameter region at the hot core position" (Fractional abundances and isotope abundance ratios); "Figure 13(a) shows the SiO(6-5) spectrum extracted from a 0.6$''$ diameter region centered at the above position." (Another embedded protostar traced by high-velocity SiO gas)
   - assumption: Distance d = 10.7 kpc  
  > "It is located at the galactocentric distance of 19.0 kpc and the distance from Earth is 10.7 kpc (cite: Bra07)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting to continuum center for spatial extent  
  > "We have estimated the spatial extent of observed emission by fitting a two-dimensional Gaussian to the continuum center (Table 3)." (Distribution of molecular line emission and dust continuum)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify spectral lines and measure line parameters**: Identify spectral lines using CDMS and JPL databases; fit 1D Gaussian profiles to measure peak brightness temperature, FWHM, LSR velocity, and integrated intensity, or directly integrate over the emission frequency region when Gaussian fits poorly  
  > "Spectral lines are identified with the aid of the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (cite: Mul01; Mul05) and the molecular database of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (cite: Pic98)." (Spectra); "Line parameters are measured by fitting a Gaussian profile to detected lines." (Spectra); "For spectral lines for which a Gaussian profile does not fit well, their integrated intensities are calculated by directly integrating the spectrum over the frequency region of emission." (Spectra)
   - parameter: Assumed line width Delta V = 4 km s^-1 for 2sigma upper limits  
  > "The tabulated uncertainties and upper limits are of 2$\sigma$ level and do not include systematic errors due to continuum subtraction. Upper limits are estimated assuming $\Delta$$V$ = 4 km s$^{-1}$." (Measured line parameters)
   - fitting: 1D Gaussian profile fitting  
  > "Line parameters are measured by fitting a Gaussian profile to detected lines." (Spectra)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive molecular column densities and rotation temperatures**: Perform LTE rotation diagram analysis for molecules with multiple transitions, single-temperature LTE calculation for other molecules, and non-LTE RADEX calculations for selected molecules  
  > "Column densities and rotation temperatures are estimated based on the rotation diagram analysis for the molecular species where multiple transitions with different excitation energies are detected (Figure 6)." (Rotation diagram analysis); "Column densities of molecular species for which rotation diagram analysis is not applicable are estimated from Equation" (3.3.2 Column densities of other molecules); "We have also estimated column densities of selected species based on non-LTE calculations with RADEX (cite: vdT07)." (Column densities of other molecules)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Exclude transitions with large S*mu^2 at E_u < 300 K for CH3OH, CH3CN, HNCO, SO2, and HCOOCH3  
  > "For CH$_3$OH, CH$_3$CN, HNCO, SO$_2$, and HCOOCH$_3$, transitions with relatively large $S\mu^2$ values at low $E_{u}$ ($<$300 K) are excluded from the fit in order to avoid possible effect of optical thickness" (Rotation diagram analysis)
   - parameter: RADEX inputs: n_H2 = 2.1 x 10^7 cm^-3, T_bg = 2.73 K, 10% line intensity uncertainty  
  > "For input parameters, we use the H$_2$ gas density of 2.1 $\times$ 10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$ according to our estimate in Section 3.3.3 and the background temperature of 2.73 K." (Column densities of other molecules); "We assume an empirical 10$\%$ uncertainty for input line intensities." (Column densities of other molecules)
   - assumption: Optically thin condition and LTE for rotation diagram analysis  
  > "We here assume an optically thin condition and the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Rotation diagram analysis)
   - assumption: Assumed T_rot = 35 K for extended cold molecules, 200 K for compact hot core molecules without rotation diagrams, 36 K for CS/C34S, 39 K for H2CO/HDCO, 166 K for 34SO2  
  > "We assume a rotation temperature of 35 K for those species, which is roughly equivalent to that of C$^{33}$S." (Column densities of other molecules); "Average temperature of those species is $\sim$200 K. We assume this temperature for column density estimates (including upper limit) of c-C$_2$H$_4$O, HC$_3$N, $^{13}$CH$_3$CN, $^{13}$OCS, and CH$_3$SH." (Column densities of other molecules)
   - fitting: Linear fit in log(N_u/g_u) vs E_u/k space  
  > "Most molecular species are well fitted by a single temperature component." (Rotation diagram analysis)
   - validation: Comparison between LTE and non-LTE RADEX column densities  
  > "The calculated non-LTE column densities are reasonably consistent with the LTE estimates." (Column densities of other molecules)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of a hot molecular core in the extreme outer Galaxy (WB89-789 SMM1) at a galactocentric distance of 19 kpc.  
  > "Here we report the first detection of a hot molecular core in the extreme outer Galaxy, which is an excellent laboratory to study star formation and interstellar medium in a Galactic low-metallicity environment." (Abstract)
- Complex organic molecules up to nine atoms (including CH3OH, C2H5OH, HCOOCH3, CH3OCH3, CH3CHO, HCOOH, H2CCO, c-C2H4O, CH3CN, C2H5CN, NH2CHO) are detected in a warm (>100 K) and compact (<0.03 pc) region.  
  > "Our ALMA observations have detected a variety of carbon-, oxygen-, nitrogen-, sulfur-, and silicon-bearing species, including complex organic molecules (COMs) containing up to nine atoms, towards a warm ($>$100 K) and compact ($<$ 0.03 pc) region" (Abstract)
- Fractional abundances of COMs relative to H2 scale with the four-times-lower metallicity of WB89-789, and abundances relative to CH3OH are remarkably similar to the inner Galactic intermediate-mass hot core NGC7192 FIRS2.  
  > "Fractional abundances of CH$_3$OH and other COMs relative to H$_2$ generally scale with the metallicity of WB89-789, which is a factor of four lower than the solar value." (Summary); "A comparison of fractional abundances of COMs relative to the CH$_3$OH column density between the outer Galactic hot core and a Galactic intermediate-mass hot core show a remarkable similarity." (Summary)
- Molecular abundances in WB89-789 SMM1 do not resemble those of LMC hot cores, showing much lower SO2 and SO abundances and higher CH3OH abundance.  
  > "In WB89-789 SMM1, however, the abundances of SO$_2$ and SO relative to H$_2$ are lower by a factor of 28 and 5 compared with LMC hot cores." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with LMC hot cores); "The CH$_3$OH abundance of WB89-789 SMM1 is higher than those of any known LMC hot cores." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with LMC hot cores)
- Another compact embedded protostar (WB89-789-SMM2) driving extremely high-velocity (V_sys +/- 25 km/s) bipolar SiO outflows is detected 2.7'' east of SMM1.  
  > "We have also detected a compact source associated with high-velocity SiO gas at the east side of WB89-789 SMM1." (Another embedded protostar traced by high-velocity SiO gas); "The spectral characteristics of the observed high-velocity SiO resemble those of extremely high velocity (EHV) outflows observed in Class 0 protostars" (Another embedded protostar traced by high-velocity SiO gas)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: WB89-789 is a young, active star-forming region in the extreme outer Galaxy (D_GC = 19 kpc, metallicity ~1/4 solar), providing an ideal laboratory to test whether low-metallicity hot core chemistry seen in the LMC is universal.  
  > "The low metallicity environment is in common with the Magellanic Clouds, and thus the extreme outer Galaxy is an ideal laboratory to test the universality of the low metallicity molecular chemistry observed in the LMC and SMC." (Introduction); "Among star-forming regions in the extreme outer Galaxy, WB89-789 (IRAS 06145+1455; 06$^\mathrm{h}$17$^\mathrm{m}$24.2s, 14$\ensuremath{^\circ}$54$\ensuremath{'}$42$\ensuremath{''}$, J2000) has particularly young and active nature (cite: Bra94)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Normalizing COM abundances by CH3OH cancels out the overall metallicity effect and H2 column density uncertainties, while re-extracting spectra over a 0.1 pc diameter region matches the physical scale of LMC hot core observations.  
  > "The fractional abundances normalized by the CH$_3$OH column density are also discussed in Sections 4.3-4.4, because of the non-negligible uncertainty associated with $N_{\mathrm{H_2}}$" (Fractional abundances and isotope abundance ratios); "Furthermore, the normalization by CH$_3$OH can cancel the metallicity effect in the abundance comparison." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with Galactic hot cores); "For a fair comparison, we have re-calculated $N_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ and molecular column densities of SMM1 for a 0.1 pc (1.93$''$) diameter region." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with LMC hot cores)
- Alternative: Optical thickness as the cause of the low SO2 abundance in SMM1 was rejected because estimating SO2 from 34SO2 increases the column density by at most a factor of three.  
  > "However, the SO$_2$ column density of SMM1 increases only by a factor of up to three when it is estimated from $^{34}$SO$_2$ (see Section 3.3.4). Thus the low SO$_2$ abundance in the outer Galactic hot core would not be due to the optical thickness." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with LMC hot cores)
- Alternative: Low elemental sulfur abundance as the sole cause of low SO2 in SMM1 was rejected because C-S bond species (CS, H2CS, OCS) do not show a significant abundance decrease.  
  > "In contrast to the S-O bond bearing species, the C-S bond bearing species such as CS, H$_2$CS , and OCS do not show significant abundance decrease in WB89-789 SMM1." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with LMC hot cores)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Far-infrared data for SMM1 are insufficient (only upper limits from low-resolution AKARI FIS survey), so the derived bolometric luminosity and stellar mass may be overestimated.  
  > "Note that far-infrared data, which is important for the luminosity determination of embedded sources, is insufficient for SMM1. Only upper limits are provided due to the low angular resolution of available *AKARI* FIS all-sky survey data." (Hot molecular core and protostar associated with WB89-789 SMM1)
- Limitation (authors): The CH3CN rotation diagram is scattered and its 13C isotopologue is not detected, causing large abundance uncertainty due to potential optical thickness.  
  > "The rotation diagram of CH$_3$CN is rather scattered. Although its isotopologue line is not detected, optical thickness might affect the column density estimate, as CH$_3$CN is often optically thick in hot cores (cite: Fue14)." (Fractional abundances and isotope abundance ratios)
- Limitation (authors): H2CO column density may be a lower limit due to line optical thickness, so deuteration ratios relative to H2CO are not discussed.  
  > "The H$_2$CO column density derived in this work may be a lower limit because the line is often optically thick, thus we do not discuss the abundance ratio relative to H$_2$CO." (Molecular abundances: Comparison with LMC hot cores)
- Limitation (authors): The detailed structure of the high-velocity SiO outflow in SMM2 is not spatially resolved at the 5000 au beam size, and CO outflow lines are not covered in the present ALMA setup.  
  > "We note that the detailed structure of high-velocity SiO gas is not spatially resolved, and CO lines, which often trace high-velocity outflows, are not covered in the present data." (Another embedded protostar traced by high-velocity SiO gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolation of Galactic 12C/13C and 32S/34S isotope gradients to D_GC = 19 kpc introduces systematic uncertainty into the main-isotopologue abundances of CH3OH, OCS, CS, SO, HCN, and HCO+.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adopting a single effective dust temperature Td = 60 K from an LMC high-mass hot core (L ~ 3.1 x 10^5 L_sun) for a much lower luminosity source (L ~ 8.4 x 10^3 L_sun) may bias the dust-derived N_H2.
