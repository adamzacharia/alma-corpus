## Analysis recipe

1. **Cross-match radio and far-IR catalogues and select high-z candidates**: Cross-match VLA 1.4 GHz and HerMES catalogues within 18 arcsec, filter for S/N > 5, compact single radio sources (< 10 arcsec), 250/350/500 um flux errors < 50%, f_350um/f_250um > 1, and z > 3 from literature.  
  > "In more detail, we selected galaxies that present relatively compact radio morphologies at 1.4 GHz (i.e. an angular size smaller than 10 arcsec) as well as a far-IR spectrum that peaks in flux at $\lambda \geq 350 \, \mu m$" (abstract); "To identify potential high-redshift hybrid AGN-SFG systems, we conducted a cross-match analysis between the VLA COSMOS and ECDF-S 1.4 GHz catalogues with those of HerMES, applying a maximum separation of 18 arcsec" (Sample selection); "Furthermore, only sources with 250, 350, and 500 $\mu m$ flux errors smaller than 50 $\%$ were accepted, which ensured that the shape of the far-IR emission was properly determined." (Sample selection)
   - software: TOPCAT
   - parameter: maximum separation = 18 arcsec, radio S/N > 5, angular size < 10 arcsec, far-IR flux errors < 50%, f_350um/f_250um > 1, redshift match radius = 2 arcsec  
  > "applying a maximum separation of 18 arcsec (motivated by the beam size of Herschel at 250 $\mu m$ of 18.1 arcsec)." (Sample selection); "retrieving redshift values from sources that are within 2 arcsec of our candidates." (High-redshift candidates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of 1.4 GHz and 250 um images and comparison with Cosmos2015, Cosmos2020, and HELP catalogues.  
  > "The radio 1.4 GHz and far-IR ($250 \, \mu m$) images of each of these sources were visually inspected in order to secure the robustness of the matching process" (Sample selection)
2. **Extract mm spectra from IRAM 30m and ALMA observations to determine spectroscopic redshifts**: Scan 3 mm and 2 mm bands with IRAM 30m (removing outliers and correcting platforming) and extract ALMA Band 3 and Band 4 spectra using CASA to identify CO emission lines.  
  > "After removing these outliers and correcting for the platforming issue (cite: 2008stt..conf..192K; 2009stt..conf..199K), we generated the final spectrum for the 3 mm band" (IRAM observations); "By analysing the spectrum, extracted using the CASA software (cite: 2022PASP..134k4501C), we detected a feature at 107.3 GHz which, considering the numerous CO emission lines, assisted in constraining the redshift of the source." (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Gaussian filter of 50 km/s and redshift step of 0.01 (for IRAM spectrum search)  
  > "In this plot, the total spectrum (blue line) and its filtered version for a Gaussian filter of $50 \, km/s$ (orange line) are presented" (IRAM observations); "Essentially, the approach of this code is to sum the observed spectra at the positions where a CO detection was expected at a given redshift, and subsequently vary the redshift in steps of 0.01." (IRAM observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of line-derived redshift with photometric redshift estimates from CIGALE and literature catalogues.  
  > "This proposed redshift possibility closely agrees with the suggested solution of $z=3.15$ obtained from our photometric analysis and the referenced literature catalogues (i.e. HELP, Cosmos2015, and cosmos2020)." (ALMA observations)
3. **Perform multi-wavelength SED fitting to derive physical properties and AGN fractions**: Fit multi-wavelength photometry (excluding radio and nebular emission) using CIGALE to obtain best-fit and Bayesian estimates of stellar mass, SFR_IR, L_IR, AGN fraction, and photometric redshift.

(SED fitting results)

(COSMOSVLA J100233.16+020626.5); "according to equation 5 by (cite: 2022ApJ...927..204E): $\rm q_{TIR}=log(L_{IR}/3.75 \cdot 10^{12})-logL_{1.4GHz}$" (COSMOSVLA J095845.94+024329.2)
   - assumption: Radio spectral index alpha = 0.8 for pure radio synchrotron emission  
  > "When we assume a typical radio spectral index of 0.8 for both sources, the corresponding radio flux density at 1.4 GHz is equal to $f_{\rm 1.4GHz-QSO}=0.1 \pm 0.04 \, mJy$" (COSMOSVLA J100256.53+021158.4)

## Qualitative findings

- Five out of eight galaxies (63%) in the sample show morphological indications of multiple interacting or merging galaxies.  
  > "A thorough investigation of available optical, near-IR, and millimetre (mm) imaging reveals a possible merging scenario in five out of eight cases in our sample." (abstract); "Although the morphology of these sources still remains uncertain, five out of eight (63 $\%$) might consist of multiple galaxies, with significant levels of both SF and AGN activity." (Comparison with previous work)
- Seven out of eight sources (87.5%) show indications of an AGN component from either radio excess or mid-IR SED fitting, accompanied by intense star formation (~10^3 M_sun/yr).  
  > "Furthermore, for 87.5 $\%$ of the sources in our sample (seven out of eight), an AGN is indicated either by the radio excess or by the mid-IR signature obtained from the best SED fit." (Comparison with previous work); "An additional highly star-forming component seems to be present in all cases, with a typical SFR of $\sim 10^3 \, M_{\odot}/yr$." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: COSMOS and ECDF-S are two of the most extensively observed fields across wavelengths, and 3 mm / ALMA Band 3-4 spectroscopy provides a good trade-off of weather versus line detection to search for mid-J CO lines at z >= 3.  
  > "For these selection criteria, we used the COSMOS and ECDF-S fields, two of the most extensively observed astronomical fields currently available, which provide high spectral and spatial resolution at a multi-wavelength scale." (abstract); "The expected redshift span for this candidate is $z \geq 3$, and therefore, the 3 mm spectral range provides an excellent trade-off of weather versus line detection to search for ideally two emission lines required to determine the source redshift." (IRAM observations)
- Why this method: Combining radio compactness and f_350um/f_250um > 1 avoids local extended galaxies while tracing redshifted dust emission and uncovering SFG+AGN merging systems that might be omitted when AGN-contaminated sources are removed.  
  > "Therefore, in this work, we question the strategy of removing AGN-contaminated radio sources from selected samples as this approach may overlook merging systems in which the AGN emission conceals an additional SFG population" (Introduction); "While the compactness criterion merely serves as a method for avoiding local extended galaxies, the far-IR and radio criteria have been employed extensively in the past in the study of the distant Universe." (Validity of the selection criteria)
- Alternative: Separating the SED fitting analysis for the two components of CVLA1002 was rejected due to sub-arcsec separation and insufficient angular resolution in most bands.  
  > "A separation of the SED fitting analysis for the two sources would be non-trivial and would yield high uncertainties in the fluxes of each source." (COSMOSVLA J100256.53+021158.4)
- Alternative: Using WILMA backend data from the IRAM 30m observations was rejected in favour of the FTS units, which provide higher spectral resolution.  
  > "Nevertheless, in the following analysis, only the data from the FTS units were investigated since they provide a higher spectral resolution than WILMA, which is necessary to resolve faint emission lines." (IRAM observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size (eight galaxies) limits statistical comparisons and only provides a lower limit on the star formation rate density at z > 3.  
  > "However, it is important to emphasise that a comparison of our results with studies involving larger sample sizes should be approached with caution, considering the limited statistics that our data provides." (Comparison with previous work); "This infrequency, along with the complexity of the adopted selection method, will unavoidably provide a less accurate estimation of the SFRD at $z>3$, which can be only considered as a lower limit (cite: 1980ApJ...235..694A)" (Comparison with previous work)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial resolution of current instrumentation is insufficient to definitively confirm the merging nature of the sub-arcsec components or deblend their multi-wavelength photometry.  
  > "It has to be noted that the low angular extent of these galaxies did not permit confirmation of this statement due to the spatial resolution of the current instrumentation." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Dust temperature and redshift degeneracy in far-IR selection, and Herschel sensitivity bias towards the brightest dust-rich galaxies.  
  > "However, this emission is affected by the temperature of the dust as well, which consequently introduces a degeneracy that is non-trivial to break. Another point of attention is the sensitivity of the Herschel surveys, which are inherently biased towards the detection of the brightest dust-rich far-IR galaxies." (Validity of the selection criteria)
- Limitation (authors): Single emission line detection in ALMA spectrum of CVLA100 provides a plausible redshift solution rather than a two-line unique determination.  
  > "One plausible solution indicates that this feature corresponds to the redshifted CO(4-3) emission line at $z=3.3$." (ALMA observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-line redshift identification for CVLA100 at 107.3 GHz relies on photometric redshift priors to assign the transition to CO(4-3).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Classification of 5/8 sources as merging systems is based on visual inspection of multi-wavelength cutouts without kinematic or spectroscopic confirmation of both components.
