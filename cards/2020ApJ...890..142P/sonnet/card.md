## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate and image lines**: Self-calibration on continuum, uvcontsub, clean with Briggs weighting  
  > "After self-calibration, we subtracted out the continuum using CASA’s `uvcontsub` function and imaged each targeted line using CASA’s `clean` algorithm." (2.3 Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust 0.5 (2 for weak lines); solint 10-100 s  
  > "Solution intervals were 10-100 seconds." (2.3 Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Build Keplerian masks and moment-0 maps, spectra and radial profiles**: Keplerian masks from disk geometry with thermal+turbulent broadening; sum within masks; rms maps from 1000 random samples; azimuthal averaging in 0.1" rings  
  > "We created a velocity-integrated emission map (a.k.a. a moment-0 map) for each line and disk pair by summing up the emission within the Keplerian masks across the velocity channels in the data cube." (3 Image Analysis)
   - software: kepmask
   - parameter: r0 = 100 AU, q = -0.3, v0 = 0.3 km/s (0.6 for 4_14, 4_13; 0.15 for J1604-2130)  
  > "For all disks, we fixed $r_0$ and $q$ to be 100 AU and -0.3, respectively." (Table 6 notes)
   - fitting: Position and inclination angles from grid search using masks and C18O (12CO or H2CO where necessary)  
  > "we empirically fit for the position and inclination angles using the masks, C$^{18}$O emission towards each disk, and a grid-search algorithm" (3 Image Analysis)
   - validation: Detection requires peak >=3 sigma and >=3 sigma in at least 4 channels  
  > "Emission within the Keplerian masks is $\geq 3\sigma$ in at least 4 velocity channels of the channel map" (4.1 Detection Statistics)
3. **Disk-averaged and azimuthally-averaged rotational diagrams**: Opacity-corrected rotational diagram linear fit with iterative opacity correction for four disks with >=3 lines  
  > "we initially solve for the opacity-uncorrected $\overline{T_{ex}}$ and $\overline{N_{tot}}$ using a linear fit assuming $C_{\tau_{ul}}=1$, and then we correct the rotational diagram for the opacity using an iterative approach" (4.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - software: Python, NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: convergence when tau and Tex change at most 0.001% (5000 iterations max for LkCa 15)  
  > "at most 0.001% (or, in the case of the uncertain spike in excitation temperature for LkCa 15, until 5000 iterations have passed)" (4.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - assumption: LTE, all lines from same environment, emission fills beam; mu=2.37, t0~0.01  
  > "This method assumes that the lines are in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), that all lines originate from the same disk environment, and that the emission fills the beam." (4.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - fitting: Linear fit of ln(Nu/gu) vs Eu; includes 10% flux uncertainty  
  > "Errors and fits include 10% absolute flux uncertainties, added in quadrature to the velocity-integrated flux uncertainties." (Table 4 notes)
   - validation: Energy ranges similar across disks; critical densities below disk densities supporting LTE  
  > "Notably the energy ranges covered by the H$_2$CO lines for each disk are similar, implying that these results are not due to excitation effects from using different H$_2$CO lines." (4.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
4. **Estimate column density profiles for all disks**: Apply the rotational-diagram equation to single lines with assumed Tex 20-50 K  
  > "we estimate a range of azimuthally-averaged column density profiles using Equation [\[eq_fitline\]](#eq_fitline) for H$_2$CO excitation temperatures between 20-50K." (4.4 Radial H2CO Column Density Profiles)
   - software: Python
   - parameter: Tex = 20-50 K  
  > "demonstrating that they are quite insensitive to excitation temperature assumptions in this range" (4.4 Radial H2CO Column Density Profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Profiles insensitive to Tex in 20-50 K  
  > "demonstrating that they are quite insensitive to excitation temperature assumptions in this range" (4.4 Radial H2CO Column Density Profiles)
5. **Compare H2CO with CO snowline, dust and C18O**: Compare radial profiles with literature/toy-model CO snowline ranges, continuum and C18O profiles  
  > "we compare the observed H$_2$CO emission profiles with estimated locations of CO snowlines at the disk midplane." (5.2 H2CO and the CO Snowline)
   - assumption: Freeze-out temperature range 17-25 K for model-based snowlines  
  > "we estimate midplane snowlines at locations corresponding to a similar temperature range of 17-25K" (5.2 H2CO and the CO Snowline)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- 8/13 detected disks are centrally peaked; about half show outer rings/plateaus  
  > "The majority (8/13) of the disks are centrally peaked in H$_2$CO." (4.2 Spatial Distributions)
- H2CO emission is present both interior and exterior to the CO snowline in all disks, implying gas-phase and grain-surface formation  
  > "There is significant H$_2$CO emission both interior and exterior to the CO snowline of all disks." (5.2 H2CO and the CO Snowline)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Keplerian masks reduce noise in extracted emission  
  > "ultimately reducing the noise incorporated into the extracted emission" (3 Image Analysis)
- Alternative: Dust opacity as cause of central H2CO depressions, rejected for all but IM Lup  
  > "For all disks except IM Lup, optically thick dust is likely not a major cause for the H$_2$CO emission substructure in the sample." (4.2 Spatial Distributions)
- Alternative: Inheritance of H2CO ice alone cannot explain morphologies  
  > "which suggests that inheritance alone cannot explain the observed H$_2$CO emission morphologies." (5.2 H2CO and the CO Snowline)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Non-uniform initial calibration across the sample  
  > "we note that this initial calibration was non-uniform across the sample." (2.3 Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Model-based snowline estimates are very uncertain  
  > "Note that these model-based estimates are very uncertain" (5.2 H2CO and the CO Snowline)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two Herbig Ae disks, so the stellar-type trend is weak
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uniform Tex of 20-50 K assumed for single-line disks
