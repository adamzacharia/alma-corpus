## Analysis recipe

1. **Create continuum and velocity-integrated (moment 0) emission maps and extract spectra**: Integrate spectral line emission over specific velocity ranges relative to systemic velocity to produce moment 0 maps for envelope, jet, outflow, cavity wall, inner envelope, and disk tracers; extract spectra at selected positions.  
  > "All moment 0 maps are integrated from -2.5 to 2.5 km s [−][1]" (Figure 2 caption); "Spectra obtained at the cavity wall positions for hydrocarbons (red) and ice-mantle tracers (blue)." (Figure 8 caption)
   - parameter: Velocity integration ranges: e.g., -2.5 to 2.5 km s^-1 (envelope), -70 to -40 and 40 to 70 km s^-1 (B1-c EHV jet), -10 to 10 km s^-1 (low-velocity outflow), -5 to -1 and 1 to 5 km s^-1 (cavity walls), -3 to 3 km s^-1 (hot core)  
  > "All moment 0 maps are integrated from -2.5 to 2.5 km s [−][1]" (Figure 2 caption); "B1-c ntegrated from -70 to -40 and from 40 to 70 km s [−][1]" (Figure 3 caption); "Moment 0 maps shown in colorscale integrated from -3 to 3 km s [−][1]" (Figure 9 caption)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Estimate expected CO snowline radii and EHV jet dynamical ages**: Calculate expected CO snowline radii from current luminosity using Equation 1 of Frimann et al. (2017); calculate EHV jet dynamical ages from bullet spacing and terminal velocity.  
  > "Equation 1 from Frimann et al. (2017) allows to calculate the expected CO snow line for the current luminosity of the example sources B1-c and TMC1 presented in Fig. 2 in the absence of an outburst." (4.1.1. C18O); "The new EHV sources B1c and SMM3 show bullet spacings of 1200 au in B1c and 3200 au in SMM3, which can be converted using the terminal velocity of the jets (not corrected for inclination) to the dynamical ages of 80 and 250 years, respectively." (4.2.1. Extremely high-velocity (EHV) jet)

C18O); "which can be converted using the terminal velocity of the jets (not corrected for inclination) to the dynamical ages of 80 and 250 years, respectively." (4.2.1. ## Qualitative findings

- The protostellar envelope is traced by C18O, DCO+, and N2D+, with CO freeze-out governing the chemistry at envelope scales (N2D+ peaks offset from continuum where CO is frozen out, while DCO+ peaks near the CO snowline).  
  > "The protostellar envelope is well traced by C [18] O, DCO [+] and N 2 D [+], with the freeze-out of CO governing the chemistry at envelope scales." (abstract); "As DCO [+] requires gas-phase CO for its formation, it peaks close to the CO snowline, which is within the resolution of our observations (∼1800–2500 au radius), while N 2 D [+] is only located where CO is not present in the gas phase." (4.1.2. N2D+ and DCO+)
- Extremely high-velocity (EHV) molecular jets are detected in 6 out of 7 Class 0 sources (including new detections in B1-c and SMM3, and a third known H2CO bullet in SMM3) and are absent in Class I sources.  
  > "What is especially important to highlight is the third detection of a H 2 CO bullet in SMM3 after IRAS 04166 (Tafalla et al. 2010) and Emb8N (Tychoniec et al. 2019)." (3.3. Outflows and jets); "In our sample 6 out of 7 sources have high-velocity component and none is detected in Class I (Section 4.2.1)" (4.7. Class 0 vs Class I in different components)
- Outflow cavity walls in Class 0 protostars are traced by UV-irradiation tracers (C2H, c-C3H2, CN) and high-density tracers (13CS, H13CO+).  
  > "To summarize, we observe the hydrocarbons C 2 H and c-C 3 H 2, as well as CN, H [13] CO [+] and [13] CS in the outflow cavity walls of Class 0 protostars." (4.3. Outflow cavity walls)
- In Class I disks, C17O traces the disk midplane whereas CN traces the upper irradiated layers of the disk and residual cavity walls/winds.  
  > "In comparison with the C [17] O emission, which traces the midplane disk, CN thus appears in the upper layers and in the outflow, therefore in most cases the two molecules are mutually exclusive." (4.4.2. Embedded disks)
- COMs and small hydrocarbons (C2H, c-C3H2) coexist in the same protostellar sources, with COMs tracing the hot core and outflows and hydrocarbons tracing the UV-irradiated cavity walls.  
  > "COMs can coexist with hydrocarbons in the same protostellar sources, but they trace different components." (abstract); "Hydrocarbons and COMs are found to co-exist routinely in protostellar sources: the former are present in the UV-irradiated cavity walls and the latter in the hot core and in the outflows." (5. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining ALMA 12m (Bands 3, 5, 6 at 0.3''-3'') and ACA 7m (Band 6 at 6'') provides both low- and high-excitation lines and preserves sensitivity from sub-arcsecond inner envelope/disk scales (35-110 au) to large envelope scales (800-2000 au, MRS up to 30'').  
  > "The availability of both low- and high-excitation lines, as well as data on small and larger scales, are important to unravel the full picture." (abstract); "The ALMA observations presented here target different spectral setups across Band 3, 5, and 6. This allows to probe the strongly varying physical scales and conditions." (2.2. Spectral setup of the observations)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Top-down destruction of PAHs as the formation route for small hydrocarbons in cavity walls (rejected because PAHs are rare in low-mass protostars and require UV fields > 10^3 G0).  
  > "The top-down production of hydrocarbons due to PAHs destruction does not appear to be an efficient route here, as PAHs are not commonly observed in low-mass protostellar systems (Geers et al. 2009), and the UV-fields required for this process are above 10 [3] (Abergel et al. 2003)." (4.3. Outflow cavity walls)
- Alternative: Thermal desorption of COMs in the S68N outflow (rejected because dust temperatures at a few 10^3 au are well below COM desorption temperatures and there is no enhancement closer to the source).  
  > "Thermal desorption of molecules from grains is not likely as dust temperatures at distances of few times 10 [3] au from the protostar are well below the desorption temperatures for most COMs." (4.2.3. Shock sputtering products)
- Alternative: Accretion shock origin for SO in TMC1 (ruled out by narrow linewidths, pointing instead to emission along the cavity walls).  
  > "However, narrow linewidths of SO toward TMC1 seem to rule out the accretion shock scenario, and points to emission along the cavity walls (Harsono et al. 2021)." (4.4.1. Compact emission)
- Alternative: Warm carbon-chain chemistry (WCCC) to explain C2H and c-C3H2 (rejected in favor of PDR chemistry in UV-irradiated outflow cavity walls).  
  > "Both c-C 3 H 2 and C 2 H can be solely explained by PDR chemistry (Guzmán et al. 2015; Cuadrado et al. 2015) even in moderate UV fields of low-mass protostars and therefore WCCC chemistry does not need to be invoked for the sources presented in this work." (4.6. Carbon-chains and other hydrocarbons vs COMs)

- Limitation (authors): Spatial and kinematic resolution is insufficient to confirm Keplerian rotation for Class 0 embedded disks (such as SMM3).

  > "Thus, the sub-arcsecond ALMA 12m array observations tend to resolve-out the envelope emission." (3.2. Protostellar envelope); "In all cases, we do not capture the entirety of the outflows as they extend beyond the primary beam of observations (∼ 30 [′′] )." (3.3. Outflows and jets)
