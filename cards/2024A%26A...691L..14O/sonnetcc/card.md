## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate and image the ALMA data**: ALMA pipeline calibration in CASA, self-calibration on the W Hya continuum, tclean imaging with robust 0.5  
  > "following standard calibration procedures with the ALMA pipeline. We also self-calibrated the data with the continuum of W Hya." (Observations and data reduction); "The image reconstruction was carried out with the task tclean with a robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "with the task tclean with a robust parameter of 0.5" (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure the 268 GHz continuum size and brightness**: Fit the 268.2 GHz continuum image with a uniform elliptical disk  
  > "Fitting the continuum image with a uniform elliptical disk results in a major and minor axis of $59.1 \pm 0.3$ mas and $57.7 \pm 0.2$ mas" (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
   - assumption: Adopted stellar angular diameter of 41.4 mas (20.7 mas radius R*) from literature UD diameter times 1.15  
  > "This resulted in a stellar angular diameter of 41.4 mas" (Introduction)
   - fitting: Uniform elliptical disk fit (algorithm not stated)  
  > "The uniform elliptical disk fitting also results in an intensity of $36.87 \pm 0.10$ mJy/beam." (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
   - validation: Compared with 338 GHz continuum of earlier work: no hot spot found  
  > "We do not find a hot spot as found in the 338 GHz continuum by Vlemmings et al." (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
3. **Map the 268 GHz H2O line kinematics and extract spatially resolved spectra**: Continuum-subtracted channel maps in velocity relative to systemic; spectra extracted at four positions over the stellar disk and four emission spots off the limb  
  > "We extracted spatially resolved spectra of the H$_2$O line at four positions over the stellar disk and on four salient emission spots away from the limb of the stellar disk" (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
   - parameter: Vsys = 40.4 km/s (LSR), Vrel = VLSR - Vsys  
  > "We adopted a systemic velocity ($V_{\rm sys}$) of 40.4 km s$^{-1}$ in the local standard of rest (LSR)" (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
   - assumption: Absorption over the disk at negative relative velocity arises from cooler gas in front of the disk  
  > "which is expected because of the absorption due to the cooler gas in front of the stellar disk" (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with infall and outflow seen in o Cet with ALMA by Wong et al.  
  > "also found the presence of both infall and outflow in $o$ Cet observed with ALMA near minimum light" (3 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 268 GHz)
4. **Compare the 251 GHz and 268 GHz H2O lines to test thermal versus nonthermal excitation**: Channel maps and spatially resolved spectra of the tentatively identified 251 GHz line compared with the 268 GHz line (similar upper-level energies)  
  > "Both the 251 and 268 GHz $\varv_2$ = 2 H$_2$O lines have similar upper-level energies of $\sim$6040–6140 K. Nevertheless, the former line is only seen in absorption." (5 Nonthermal origin of the 268 GHz H2O line)
   - assumption: Images at Vrel > 3.0 km/s of the 251 GHz line are excluded because of blending with the Si17O line at 250.744695 GHz  
  > "are affected by the blend due to the adjacent Si$^{17}$O line at 250.744695 GHz" (4 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 251 GHz)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Line identification of the 251 GHz line is tentative  
  > "We also identified – albeit tentatively – another vibrationally excited H$_2$O line" (4 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 251 GHz)
5. **Check maser plausibility against maser pumping models and atmospheric model densities**: Compare maser-depth versus gas temperature and ortho-H2O density from Gray et al. models with densities and temperatures at 2-3 R* from Bladh et al. models, converted to H2O densities  
  > "These mass densities correspond to H$_2$ number densities of $1.5 \times 10^{8}$–$1.5 \times 10^{9}$ cm$^{-3}$, which translates into H$_2$O densities of $8 \times 10^{4}$–$8 \times 10^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$" (5 Nonthermal origin of the 268 GHz H2O line)
   - parameter: ortho-H2O abundance 6e-4; dust temperature >~ 900 K required for radiative pumping at these densities  
  > "This is consistent with the radiative pumping regime if the dust temperature is $\ensuremath{\gtrsim}\,$900 K, which is indeed the case as presented below." (5 Nonthermal origin of the 268 GHz H2O line)
   - assumption: Ortho-H2O abundance of 6e-4 relative to H2  
  > "with an ortho-H$_2$O abundance of $6 \times 10^{-4}$ (Khouri et al. (cite: khouri14b))" (5 Nonthermal origin of the 268 GHz H2O line)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with similar figure of Baudry et al. (their Fig. 17)  
  > "similar to Fig. 17 of Baudry et al. ((cite: baudry23))" (5 Nonthermal origin of the 268 GHz H2O line)
6. **Register ZIMPOL polarimetric images to ALMA and compare dust and H2O emission**: Fit the ZIMPOL total-intensity image with a uniform elliptical disk and align its centre with the ALMA continuum disk centre; overlay 268 GHz H2O integrated intensity contours on polarized intensity and polarization-degree maps  
  > "The central position of the fitted elliptical disk was registered to the center of the ALMA continuum image" (6 Comparison between the 268 GHz H2O emission and dust distribution); "In both panels, the integrated intensity of the 268 GHz H$_2$O line near the systemic velocity is shown with the contours." (6 Comparison between the 268 GHz H2O emission and dust distribution)
   - software: SPHERE-ZIMPOL pipeline 0.40.0
   - assumption: Optically thin dust so that polarized intensity traces dust distribution  
  > "in an optically thin case, a polarized intensity map reflects the spatial distribution of the dust grains" (6 Comparison between the 268 GHz H2O emission and dust distribution)
   - assumption: Gas and grains kinematically coupled when inferring dust infall from H2O kinematics  
  > "and the grains and gas are kinematically coupled" (Discussion and conclusion)
   - fitting: Uniform elliptical disk fit to the ZIMPOL total intensity map (method not stated)  
  > "we fitted the SPHERE images with a uniform elliptical disk" (6 Comparison between the 268 GHz H2O emission and dust distribution)
   - validation: Comparison repeated with the 748 nm ZIMPOL data, found very similar  
  > "The results are very similar to those shown in Fig." (Comparison of the ALMA image of the 268 GHz H2O line with the ZIMPOL data obtained at 748 nm)

## Qualitative findings

- Strong spotty 268 GHz H2O emission is seen along and just outside the stellar limb at ~40 mas (~1.9 R*), extending to ~60 mas (~2.9 R*)  
  > "Strong, spotty emission was also detected along and just outside the stellar disk limb at an angular distance of $\sim$40 mas ($\sim$1.9 $R_{\star}$), extending to $\sim$60 mas ($\sim$2.9 $R_{\star}$)." (abstract)
- The 251 GHz H2O line shows absorption over the stellar surface in contrast to the 268 GHz emission  
  > "the 251 GHz H$_2$O line predominantly shows absorption over the stellar disk" (4 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 251 GHz)
- The 268 GHz H2O emission correlates spatially with visible polarized intensity (dust clouds), including the south cloud, east and NNW spots  
  > "The strong H$_2$O emission in the south overlaps very well with the large dust cloud showing high polarized intensity." (6 Comparison between the 268 GHz H2O emission and dust distribution)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Contemporaneous ALMA and ZIMPOL data (ZIMPOL taken nine days before) allow direct comparison of gas and dust at nearly the same epoch  
  > "just nine days before our ALMA observations" (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Compare the H2O emission image with ZIMPOL polarimetric data to test for a correlation between H2O emission and dust formation  
  > "To examine whether there is a correlation between the H$_2$O emission and dust formation, we compared the 268 GHz H$_2$O emission image with the visible polarimetric imaging data obtained with VLT/SPHERE-ZIMPOL." (6 Comparison between the 268 GHz H2O emission and dust distribution)
- Alternative: Excess emission from hot gas in front of the star rejected, because the 251 GHz line with similar upper-level energy shows only absorption  
  > "Therefore, the excess emission in the 268 GHz line is unlikely caused by hot gas in front of the star" (5 Nonthermal origin of the 268 GHz H2O line)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 251 GHz H2O line identification is tentative and its rest-frequency uncertainty is deferred to a later paper  
  > "The uncertainty in the rest frequency of this H$_2$O line will be discussed in greater detail in a forthcoming paper." (4 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 251 GHz)
- Limitation (authors): 251 GHz channel maps at Vrel > 3.0 km/s are blended with the Si17O line  
  > "are affected by the blend due to the adjacent Si$^{17}$O line at 250.744695 GHz" (4 Vibrationally excited H2O line at 251 GHz)
- Limitation (authors): Dust-gas kinematic coupling is assumed for the infall interpretation of the dust clouds  
  > "and the grains and gas are kinematically coupled" (Discussion and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar angular diameter is given as 41.4 mas in the Introduction but 40.7 mas in the continuum appendix; the R* scaling of the quoted radii depends on which is used
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 251 GHz line is only tentatively identified, so the thermal-versus-maser contrast rests on an uncertain identification
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Maser conclusion relies on a private-communication model grid and on atmospheric model densities, not on measured line brightness temperatures versus saturation or on polarization
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Image registration of ZIMPOL to ALMA assumes the centres of the elliptical disk fits coincide, which is not independently tested
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties are given on most velocity extents and positions
