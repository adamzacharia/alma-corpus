## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the visibilities**: Standard ALMA pipeline calibration in CASA 5.1.15, imaging with tclean in CASA 5.3  
  > "We calibrated the visibility data set using CASA software version 5.1.15 with the standard pipeline provided by the ALMA Observatory and imaged it with the TCLEAN task in CASA 5.3." (ALMA Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: SPW 31 and SPW 29 used for line analysis; resolution ~0.8-1.2 arcsec; ~1.2 mJy/beam continuum and ~4.7 mJy per channel line sensitivity  
  > "We analyzed the spectral lines in two spectral windows (SPW 31 and SPW 29) because of their wide frequency band" (ALMA Observations and data reduction); "a high sensitivity of $\sim$ 1.2 mJy beam$^{-1}$ for continuum and $\sim$ 4.7 mJy per channel for lines" (ALMA Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_

  > "$\eta=100$ is a generic gas-to-dust ratio for interstellar matter with solar metallicity (cite: 1991ApJ...380..429L; 1992ApJS...82..167H)" (Core Parameters)

  > "$\mu\approx2.8$ is the mean particle weight per $\rm H_2$ molecule (cite: 2008A&A...487..993K)" (Core Parameters)

## Qualitative findings

- H2CS is detected in line-rich, warm and cold cores, all with N(H2) above the star-formation threshold, so it is widely distributed in star-forming cores  
  > "suggesting that H$_{2}$CS can be widely distributed in star-forming cores with different physical environments." (abstract)
- H2CS emission comes from extended regions around line-rich and warm cores and also covers much of the cold cores  
  > "The observations revealed that the emission of H$_{\rm 2}$CS is originated from extended regions surrounding both compact line-rich cores and warm cores and also can cover plenty of regions of the cold cores." (H$_2$CS Spatial Distribution)
- Abundance increases with temperature, and higher gas temperatures go with higher H2CS column densities  
  > "The results show the H$_{2}$CS abundances increase as temperature rises and higher gas temperatures are usually associated with higher H$_{2}$CS column densities." (abstract)
- Warm cores lie above and cold cores below the N-f trend line; line-rich cores have the highest N(H2CS) and f(H2CS)  
  > "Additionally, warm cores are largely scattered above the line, while cold cores are below the line." (Column Density-Abundance Relation)
- H2CS temperatures match CH3OCHO temperatures within uncertainties in line-rich cores  
  > "H$_{2}$CS shows no significant temperature differences within uncertainties" (Temperature Structure)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Collapsing, massive and luminous ATOMS clumps selected; Band 7 and SPW 31/29 chosen for wide bandwidth with a rich set of lines  
  > "Finally, 11 massive and luminous sources with collapsing signature were observed with the ALMA." (Sample Selection); "We analyzed the spectral lines in two spectral windows (SPW 31 and SPW 29) because of their wide frequency band" (ALMA Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Multiple H2CS transitions over a wide Eu range (91-419 K) allow accurate rotation temperature and column density  
  > "The E$_{u}$ of H$_{2}$CS covers a wide range of energy levels from 91 to 419 K, allowing accurately determine the rotation temperature and column density." (H$_2$CS Line Emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Several H2CS lines are blended with other species and could not be used as isolated lines  
  > "The $\rm 10_{2,9}-9_{2,8}$ lines are blended with $\rm H_{2}^{13}CO$ in both line-rich cores and warm cores." (Table 4 notes)
- Limitation (authors): Further observations and studies are needed to understand the physical and chemical properties of H2CS  
  > "Further observations and studies are still needed to better understand the physical and chemical properties of H${_2}$CS in space." (Column Density-Abundance Relation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The N(H2CS) vs f(H2CS) correlation (r = 0.88) is partly built in because f = N(H2CS)/N(H2) shares the numerator; the claimed enhancement needs a test independent of this.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): T_d (and so N(H2) and f) comes from the same H2CS fit that provides T, and cold-core temperatures are fixed to the clump temperature, so the T-f trend is partly circular for cold cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-line (cold-core) N(H2CS) rests on an assumed temperature with 20% error; no fit uncertainty on size or LTE assumptions is propagated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectra come from the continuum peak position and the deconvolved continuum size is used as H2CS source size; beam dilution and different H2CS vs dust extent are not tested.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No short-spacing or maximum recoverable scale discussion for C43-1 at 0.8-1.2 arcsec; extended H2CS emission claims rely on moment 0 maps alone.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Text says N(H2) is source-averaged while table notes say peak column densities; the definition affects abundances.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-detections (73 cores without H2CS) are not included in the abundance trends, so the sample is biased toward detections.
