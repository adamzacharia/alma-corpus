## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum subtraction and imaging of line and continuum data**: CASA uvcontsub with a constant continuum level, then clean (Briggs, robust=0.5, gain=0.1) of line and continuum data, starting from ALMA-calibrated data  
  > "We first determined a constant continuum flux level by using data that were not affected by emission and absorption lines, and used the CASA task “uvcontsub” to subtract the derived continuum emission from the data." (2 Observations and Data Analyses)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust=0.5, gain=0.1  
  > "(Briggs-weighting; robust=0.5, gain $=$ 0.1)" (2 Observations and Data Analyses)
   - parameter: velocity resolution 10–20 km/s; pixel 0.003'' (data-a), 0.005'' (data-b)  
  > "The adopted velocity resolution was 10–20 km s$^{-1}$." (2 Observations and Data Analyses); "The pixel scale was 0.003$''$ pixel$^{-1}$ and 0.005$''$ pixel$^{-1}$ for data-a and -b, respectively." (2 Observations and Data Analyses)
   - assumption: Continuum is constant across the band and can be set from line-free data  
  > "We first determined a constant continuum flux level by using data that were not affected by emission and absorption lines" (2 Observations and Data Analyses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Torus fluxes compared with earlier Cycle 4 and 2 data containing shorter baselines  
  > "We found that their fluxes roughly agree (Appendix B), supporting our view that we recover virtually all dense molecular line emission flux from the torus in our Cycle 6 data." (3 Results)
2. **Compare continuum at matched resolution**: Smooth data-a continuum to the data-b beam with imsmooth and compare peak flux  
  > "We modified the beam size of data-a to match that of data-b, using the CASA task “imsmooth”" (3 Results)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Smoothed data-a peak (8.1 mJy/beam) agrees with data-b (8.2 mJy/beam)  
  > "which agrees well with that of data-b (8.2 mJy beam$^{-1}$)" (3 Results)
3. **Make moment 0, 1 and 2 maps and read torus morphology and kinematics**: Intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment 1) maps of HCN and HCO+ J=3-2 shown against moment 0 and moment 2 maps; velocity display range matched to earlier 0.04'' maps  
  > "The intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment 1) maps of the HCN J=3–2 and HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 lines are also displayed in Figure 2 (bottom panels)." (3 Results)
   - parameter: Continuum peak (~260 GHz) adopted as the SMBH position  
  > "We adopt the $\sim$260 GHz continuum peak position as the location of the mass-accreting SMBH" (3 Results)
   - assumption: V_sys = 1130 km/s, optical LSR velocity  
  > "we adopt the systemic velocity of NGC 1068 to be V$_{\rm sys}$ = 1130 km s$^{-1}$ in the optical LSR velocity" (3 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Main Cycle 6 features reproduced in the Cycle 5+6 combined data (Appendix A)  
  > "Main features detected in our Cycle 6 data are reproduced in the combined data as well." (3 Results)
4. **Extract beam-sized and area-integrated spectra and fit Gaussians**: Spectra at C-, E- and W-peaks and integrated over 0.2'' x 0.1'' torus, 0.1'' x 0.1'' E-torus and W-torus regions; Gaussian fits for velocity, peak, FWHM and flux  
  > "Gaussian fits to several clearly detected emission lines are shown in Figure 5, and estimated molecular line fluxes are tabulated in Table 3." (3 Results); "we also extract area-integrated spectra within (a) a 0.2$''$ (east-west) $\times$ 0.1$''$ (north-south) (14 pc $\times$ 7 pc) rectangular region" (3 Results)
   - parameter: At the W-peak, single and two-Gaussian fits both applied; single Gaussian adopted for discussion  
  > "At the W-peak, two Gaussian fits are applied because a very broad component is observed, in addition to a narrow component." (Table 3)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits to emission lines  
  > "Gaussian fits are applied to estimate the fluxes of these emission lines (Figure 8 and Table 4)." (3 Results)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Quantify outflow from the C-peak absorption wing**: Correct C-peak spectra for innermost-torus contamination, fit a Gaussian to the broad blueshifted HCN absorption wing, convert equivalent width to HCN J=2 column density with Eq. (3), then to H2 mass and energy  
  > "We apply a Gaussian fit to the broad blueshifted HCN J=3–2 absorption wing in the raw beam-sized spectrum at the C-peak in Figure 6a" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
   - parameter: A_ul = 8.4e-4 s^-1 for HCN J=3-2  
  > "where A$_{\rm ul}$ = 8.4 $\times$ 10$^{-4}$ (s$^{-1}$) is adopted for HCN J=3–2 (cite: mul05)" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
   - parameter: HCN spread over the beam, 1.3 pc x 1.2 pc  
  > "If these outflowing HCN molecules spatially distribute in the beam size (0.019$''$ $\times$ 0.017$''$ or 1.3 pc $\times$ 1.2 pc; Table 2)" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
   - assumption: HCN-to-H2 abundance ratio of 1e-8 because outflow is confined to the nuclear region  
  > "Assuming a high HCN-to-H$_{2}$ abundance ratio of 10$^{-8}$ ($\S$4.1) because the outflow is supposed to be confined to the nuclear region" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
   - assumption: Continuum dust attenuation neglected at ~260 GHz  
  > "where continuum flux attenuation by dust extinction is neglected at $\sim$260 GHz ($\sim$1.2 mm)" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian to the absorption wing in the raw C-peak spectrum  
  > "We apply a Gaussian fit to the broad blueshifted HCN J=3–2 absorption wing in the raw beam-sized spectrum at the C-peak in Figure 6a" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
   - validation: Blueshifted HCN wing becomes clearer and an HCO+ wing appears after contamination correction  
  > "What is more important is that the blueshifted absorption tail of HCN J=3–2 becomes clearer" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
6. **Derive line luminosities and dense molecular gas mass**: Gaussian-fit fluxes in the 14 pc x 7 pc region converted to L and L' with the Solomon & Vanden Bout equations, then J=1-0 luminosity assumed equal to J=3-2 and multiplied by HCN or HCO+ conversion factors  
  > "using the following equations (cite: sol05)" (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass); "= 6–12 $\times$ HCN J=1–0 luminosity" (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass)
   - parameter: HCO+ conversion: 2–5 x HCO+ J=1-0 luminosity  
  > "= 2–5 $\times$ HCO$^{+}$ J=1–0 luminosity" (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass)
   - assumption: Torus HCN and HCO+ emission almost thermalized up to J=3 and optically thick at J=3-2 and lower, so J=1-0 luminosity equals J=3-2 luminosity  
  > "Assuming that HCN and HCO$^{+}$ emission in the dense and warm NGC 1068 torus is almost thermalized at up to J=3 and optically thick at J=3–2 and lower J-transitions" (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass)
   - assumption: HCO+-derived mass adopted because HCN is overabundant  
  > "By considering the suggested HCN overabundance and the resulting possible overestimatation of HCN-derived H$_{2}$ mass" (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Independent size check from assumed 1e6 cm^-3 density and clump filling factor 0.01–0.1 gives 3–6 pc, comparable to the observed size  
  > "This independent, simple comparison also suggests that the estimated torus dense molecular mass is reasonable." (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass)
7. **Constrain HCN-to-HCO+ abundance ratio from 13C isotopologue absorption**: Compare H13CN and H13CO+ J=3-2 absorption at the C-peak, correct the ratio for B-coefficient and RADEX excitation differences  
  > "We thus use the isotopologue H$^{13}$CN J=3–2 and H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ J=3–2 line data to constrain the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio" (4.1 HCN-abundance-enhanced dense molecular torus)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: B23 of H13CO+ ~1.7 times that of H13CN  
  > "The B-coefficient from J=2 to J=3 (B$_{\rm 23}$) of H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ is a factor of $\sim$1.7 higher than that of H$^{13}$CN" (4.1 HCN-abundance-enhanced dense molecular torus)
   - assumption: H13CN/H13CO+ column density ratio equals the HCN/HCO+ abundance ratio; absorption comes from the foreground torus  
  > "where we assume that the H$^{13}$CN-to-H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ column density ratio (= abundance ratio) is comparable to an HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio." (4.1 HCN-abundance-enhanced dense molecular torus)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency with other lines of evidence (flux ratio, spatial extent, mass ratio, VIB lines)  
  > "Summarizing, various observational results described in this section consistently suggest an enhanced HCN abundance, relative to HCO$^{+}$, in the NGC 1068 torus." (4.1 HCN-abundance-enhanced dense molecular torus)
8. **Analyse torus rotation along the torus axis and compare with Keplerian and maser curves**: Beam-sized spectra at various distances from the SMBH along PA = 115 deg fitted with Gaussians for peak velocity and width (Figure 14); position-velocity diagrams (Figure 13); compare with Keplerian and H2O maser curves  
  > "Figure 13 shows position velocity diagrams of HCN J=3–2 and HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 lines along the position angle of PA = 115$^{\circ}$ east of north." (4.3 Redshifted dense molecular emission from the innermost northwestern torus); "based on the Gaussian fit of beam-sized spectra extracted at various distances from the central SMBH along PA = 115$^{\circ}$" (4.4 Dynamical properties of the torus)
   - parameter: Keplerian curve V ∝ r^-0.5 for a point mass of 1e7 Msun, and sub-Keplerian V ∝ r^-0.31 from H2O masers  
  > "overplotted with (1) Keplerian rotation (V $\propto$ r$^{-0.5}$) dominated by a point source with mass of 1 $\times$ 10$^{7}$M$_{\odot}$" (4.3 Redshifted dense molecular emission from the innermost northwestern torus)
   - assumption: Torus inclination i ~ 90 deg (edge-on); even i = 34–66 deg raises intrinsic velocity by only 1.1–1.8  
  > "it is very likely that the torus inclination is *i* $\sim$ 90$^{\circ}$ (edge-on)" (4.4 Dynamical properties of the torus)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits to beam-sized spectra  
  > "based on the Gaussian fit of beam-sized spectra extracted at various distances from the central SMBH along PA = 115$^{\circ}$" (4.4 Dynamical properties of the torus)
   - validation: Innermost HCN and HCO+ dynamics roughly agree with VLBI H2O maser dynamics at <0.7 pc  
  > "Our ALMA data demonstrate that HCN J=3–2 and HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 emission line dynamics roughly agrees with the VLBI-detected H$_{2}$O maser dynamics at $<$0.7 pc." (4.3 Redshifted dense molecular emission from the innermost northwestern torus)
9. **Characterise vibrationally excited HCN absorption and emission**: Gaussian fit to the HCN-VIB absorption at the C-peak converted to column density via equivalent width; Gaussian fit to HCN-VIB emission at the W-peak converted to luminosity with Eqs. (1)-(2); moment 0 map over 1170–1430 km/s  
  > "we obtain the absorption equivalent width to be EW $\sim$ 2 $\times$ 10$^{6}$ (cm s$^{-1}$)" (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines); "Using Equations (1) and (2), the HCN-VIB J=3–2 emission line luminosity is estimated" (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines)
   - parameter: A_ul = 7.3e-4 s^-1 for HCN-VIB J=3-2  
  > "The A$_{ul}$ value for HCN-VIB J=3–2 is 7.3 $\times$ 10$^{-4}$ (s$^{-1}$) at Splatalogue (cite: mul05)." (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines)
   - assumption: HCN-VIB line luminosity from the W-peak taken as the torus total because it is detected clearly only there  
  > "Since the HCN-VIB J=3–2 emission line is detected clearly only in the beam-sized W-peak spectrum, we use the luminosity as that from the NGC 1068 torus." (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits to the absorption feature and the W-peak emission line  
  > "A Gaussian fit to this line is shown in Figure 5e." (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines)
   - validation: Moment 0 map of redshifted component detects HCN-VIB at ~4.8 sigma near the W-peak  
  > "The HCN-VIB J=3–2 emission line is detected with $\sim$4.8$\sigma$ at the northwestern side of the C-peak" (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN and HCO+ J=3-2 are bright within ~3 pc and low-surface-brightness emission extends to 5–7 pc, with the western side brighter than the eastern side  
  > "Bright emission is compact ($\lesssim$3 pc), and low-surface-brightness emission extends out to 5–7 pc." (abstract)
- HCN and HCO+ are in emission at the E- and W-peaks and in absorption at the continuum peak  
  > "Both HCN J=3–2 and HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 lines are clearly detected in emission at the E- and W-peaks, but in absorption at the C-peak." (3 Results)
- Line widths are larger in the W-peak than the E-peak, taken as higher turbulence in the western torus  
  > "The molecular line widths are larger in the W-peak than in the E-peak, suggesting larger turbulence at the W-peak." (3 Results)
- HNC J=3-2 is much weaker than HCN and HCO+, suggesting low HNC abundance; H13CO+ is not significantly detected  
  > "The HNC J=3–2 emission from the torus is much weaker than HCN J=3–2 and HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 emission (Figure 4), suggesting a low HNC abundance there." (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines); "while no significant signal is recognizable for H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ J=3–2 ($<$3$\sigma$)" (3 Results)
- Authors propose that a compact massive gas clump collided with the western torus, producing the counter-rotation and turbulence (interpretation, not directly measured)  
  > "We propose a simple scenario that a compact massive gas clump fell into the pre-existing torus and collided with the western torus from the far side." (4.8 Schematic picture of the NGC 1068 torus)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Only long baselines were requested, to isolate the compact torus  
  > "Since our main interest is dense molecular gas properties of the compact torus, only long baselines were used." (2 Observations and Data Analyses)
- Why this method: 13C isotopologue lines used for the abundance ratio because they are more likely optically thin; Cycle 6 data alone used for line comparisons to avoid systematics from combining data  
  > "and so the isotopologue lines are more likely to be optically thin." (4.1 HCN-abundance-enhanced dense molecular torus); "we want to avoid possible systematic uncertainties resulting from combining data taken with largely different observing parameters" (3 Results)
- Alternative: Outflow-only interpretation (Garcia-Burillo et al. 2019) of the innermost redshifted component not adopted, because the match with the H2O maser disk dynamics favors counter-rotation  
  > "(cite: gar19) prefer an outflow scenario, rather than the counter-rotating molecular gas scenario" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas); "there is no strong reason why the dynamical properties of the H$_{2}$O maser rotating disk and outflowing molecular gas agree" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
- Alternative: Almost Keplerian rotation of the outer torus (Impellizzeri et al. 2019) not adopted; outer torus is slowly rotating  
  > "This slowly rotating outer ($\gtrsim$2 pc) torus scenario is different from the argument of almost Keplerian rotation there by (cite: imp19)." (4.4 Dynamical properties of the torus)
- Alternative: HCN-derived dense H2 mass not adopted because HCN overabundance may inflate it; HCO+-derived mass adopted  
  > "By considering the suggested HCN overabundance and the resulting possible overestimatation of HCN-derived H$_{2}$ mass" (4.2 Estimated torus dense molecular mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Emission larger than the maximum recoverable scale may be resolved out  
  > "Emission with a spatial extent larger than these MRSs may be resolved out and may not be recovered in our ALMA data." (2 Observations and Data Analyses)
- Limitation (authors): Extended host-galaxy emission is not recovered in the Cycle 6 data, so only compact torus emission is discussed  
  > "We will thus discuss only compact torus emission." (3 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Continuum peak flux uncertainties exclude absolute flux calibration and continuum frequency-range choice  
  > "Possible systematic uncertainties coming from absolute flux calibration ambiguity in individual ALMA observations and choice of frequency range to determine the continuum level are not taken into account." (Table 2)
- Limitation (authors): C-peak spectra can be contaminated by innermost torus emission because the beam is not much smaller than the separation  
  > "the beam-sized spectra at the C-peak (Figure 6) can be contaminated by emission from the innermost torus" (4.7 Outflowing dense molecular gas)
- Limitation (authors): HCO+-VIB emission candidate is below 3 sigma and may be HOC+  
  > "However, our Gaussian fit of this possible emission line provides detection significance of $<$3$\sigma$." (4.5 Vibrationally excited dense molecular emission lines)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The HCN abundance enhancement rests on a ~4.5 sigma H13CN absorption versus a <2 sigma H13CO+ limit at one position, plus an assumed equality of isotopologue column-density ratio and abundance ratio
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dense gas masses depend on assumed HCN/HCO+ J=1-0 conversion factors and on assuming J=1-0 luminosity equals J=3-2; the factor 4–5 offset between tracers is explained by assumed overabundance rather than tested
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Counter-rotation versus outflow remains degenerate with a single ~0.02'' dataset; no dynamical model fit is performed and the 1/sin(i) correction relies on an assumed edge-on geometry
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow mass and energy are lower limits tied to an assumed HCN/H2 of 1e-8 and a uniform beam-filling area
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties are given for several continuum values, and no flux comparison between independent executions is reported
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gaussian-fit procedure and the exact line-free channels are not documented, so velocity and flux errors cannot be independently reproduced
