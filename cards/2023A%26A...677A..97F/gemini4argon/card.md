## Analysis recipe

1. **Align and combine e-MERLIN and VLA C-band visibilities and image all radio bands**: Correct e-MERLIN C-band visibilities for proper motion and phase offsets using field source XEST 22-054 and CASA fixvis, combine with matched-bandwidth VLA C-band data, split Ku and K bands into two sub-bands each, and image with Briggs robust +0.5. Re-image VLA/e-MERLIN bands with common uv-cutoff and restoring beam for flux measurements.  
  > "Therefore, the e-MERLIN data was shifted so that the position of L1551 IRS 5 would match the position in the VLA data using the CASA task *fixvis*." (Observations and Data Reduction); "For all of the VLA and VLA+e-MERLIN bands, a Briggs weighting of $+0.5$ was used for imaging as this seemed to give the best compromise between angular resolution and sensitivity." (Observations and Data Reduction); "When measuring the total flux densities of the sources in the e-MERLIN and VLA observations, the data at each frequency was re-imaged, using the same outer uv-cutoff and synthesised beam for each band." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, fixvis
   - parameter: Position shift: Delta_alpha = +37.1 mas, Delta_delta = -87.8 mas  
  > "When combining the proper motion and the difference due to phase errors, this gives a total difference in position between the two observations of $\Delta_{\alpha} = +37.1\ \mathrm{mas}$ and $\Delta_{\delta} = -87.8\ \mathrm{mas}$." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: Briggs weighting +0.5; outer uv-cutoff 1.375 x 10^6 lambda (L1551 IRS 5) and 1 x 10^6 lambda (L1551 NE)  
  > "For L1551 IRS 5, this was the X Band VLA observation, which gives a uv-cutoff of $1.375 \times 10^6\ \lambda$ and a beam of $0.20\ensuremath{''} \times 0.18\ensuremath{''}$ with a PA of $49\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (Observations and Data Reduction); "For L1551 NE, this was the C Band VLA observation, which gives a uv cut-off of $1\times 10^6\ \lambda$ and a beam of $0.37\ensuremath{''} \times 0.30\ensuremath{''}$ with a PA of $61\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Field source XEST 22-054 is extragalactic with no significant proper motion  
  > "The source was assumed to be an extragalactic source based on its steep negative spectral index ($\alpha = -2.02 \pm 0.06$) in the VLA C Band image and the lack of any IR detections at this position when checked using SIMBAD. Therefore it should have no significant proper motion." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure multi-frequency continuum flux densities for each binary component**: Integrate flux within a polygon region drawn around each source above a sigma_rms threshold in CASA (or using CASA task imfit for L1551 NE), passing through the saddle point where components overlap.  
  > "The total flux densities of the sources at each frequency were measured in CASA by drawing a region around each source containing all of the emission above a certain threshold and integrating the flux within that region." (Observations and Data Reduction); "In these cases, the polygon was drawn so that it passes through the saddle-point of the brightness distribution between the two sources." (Observations and Data Reduction); "The flux densities were measured for component A and component B at each frequency using the CASA task *imfit* and are shown in [\[tab:L1551_NE_fluxes\]](#tab:L1551_NE_fluxes)." (L1551 NE)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: Thresholds: 3 sigma_rms (VLA and e-MERLIN), 10 sigma_rms (ALMA Band 3 and 4), 25 sigma_rms (ALMA Band 6 and 7)  
  > "The thresholds used were $3\sigma_{\mathrm{rms}}$ for the VLA and e-MERLIN images, $10\sigma_{\mathrm{rms}}$ for the ALMA Band 3 and Band 4 images, and $25\sigma_{\mathrm{rms}}$ for the ALMA Band 6 and Band 7 images." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Higher thresholds in ALMA images exclude circumbinary ring dust emission  
  > "Higher thresholds were used for the ALMA images to avoid including dust emission from the circumbinary rings around the sources." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: imfit used for L1551 NE components  
  > "The flux densities were measured for component A and component B at each frequency using the CASA task *imfit* and are shown in [\[tab:L1551_NE_fluxes\]](#tab:L1551_NE_fluxes)." (L1551 NE)
   - validation: Archival ALMA images and calibration inspection plots were inspected for quality  
  > "The archival ALMA images were inspected, along with the corresponding calibration inspections plots from the archive, to ensure that the data was of sufficiently high quality to use for our study." (Observations and Data Reduction)
3. **Fit radio-to-submm spectral energy distributions (SEDs)**: Fit measured multi-frequency flux densities (and previous epoch data from Rodriguez et al. 1998) with a combined thermal free-free conical jet model (spectral index fixed to 0.6) and a dust power-law model: S_nu = K_ff * nu^0.6 + K_dust * nu^alpha_dust.  
  > "Both components were fitted with a model spectrum consisting of a combination of a thermal free-free emission component and a dust emission component." (Spectrum); "The dust emission component is modelled using a simple power-law $S_{\nu} \propto \nu^{\alpha}$ giving a total model spectrum of: $$\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \left( \frac{S_{\nu}}{\mathrm{mJy}} \right) = K_{\mathrm{ff}} \left( \frac{\nu}{\mathrm{GHz}} \right)^{0.6} + K_{\mathrm{dust}} \left( \frac{\nu}{\mathrm{GHz}} \right)^{\alpha_{\mathrm{dust}}}
\end{split}
\end{equation}$$" (Spectrum)
   - parameter: Conical jet parameters: epsilon = 1, q_n = -2, free-free spectral index = 0.6  
  > "A conical jet model was used for which $\epsilon=1$, $q_n = -2 \epsilon = -2$ and the electron temperature and velocity of the jet is constant ($T_e(r) = T_0$, $v(r) = v_0$)." (Spectrum)
   - assumption: Standard conical isothermal constant-velocity jet with optically thin turnover frequency nu_m > 40 GHz where dust emission dominates  
  > "However, for typical parameters of a jet this is usually estimated to be $\nu_{\mathrm{m}} > 40\ \mathrm{GHz}$ (cite: Anglada2018), at which point the spectra of the sources are clearly dominated by dust emission." (Spectrum)
   - fitting: Fit for K_ff, K_dust, and alpha_dust (sampler/optimizer not stated)  
  > "where $K_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is a constant of proportionality for the dust emission component and $\alpha_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is the spectral index of the dust emission." (Spectrum)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive ionised mass-loss rates and ionisation fractions**: Calculate ionised mass-loss rates from fitted K_ff values using Eq. 3 from Reynolds (1986), and compare with atomic [O I]63 mass-loss rates to estimate lower limits on jet ionisation fractions.  
  > "From the fit to the spectra of the sources, the ionised mass-loss rates of the jets can be estimated." (Ionised Mass-loss Rate); "We can compare the values of $\dot{M}_{\mathrm{ion}}$ estimated with the total mass-loss rate of L1551 IRS 5 to obtain values for the ionisation fraction of the jets." (Ionised Mass-loss Rate)

## Qualitative findings

- In L1551 IRS 5 S at 5 GHz, thermal free-free emission is elongated along the jet axis with a double-lobe structure on either side of the central star, while L1551 IRS 5 N shows weak emission close to the star with no extended jet lobes.  
  > "Two main emission lobes are seen either side of the central star, with the NE lobe corresponding to the red-shifted jet and the SW lobe corresponding to the blue-shifted jet." (L1551 IRS 5); "In the N component on the other hand, the emission detected appears to be very weak and close to the position of the central star with no extended emission seen along the jet axis." (L1551 IRS 5)
- A stationary emission region between the two outflows ~0.6'' SW of L1551 IRS 5 is detected at a similar position to 2002 observations, supporting a shock interaction zone between the two outflows.  
  > "In [1](#fig:L1551_IRS_5_C_to_K_Band) (a) and (b), a region of emission is also detected between the outflows to the SW of the two sources at a similar position to that of the emission detected by (cite: Rodriguez2003)." (Morphology); "If this emission corresponds to the same emission region as the one they detected, this would indicate a lack of proper motion and back up their hypothesis of this being a region of shock interaction between the two outflows." (Morphology)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution cm-wavelength radio observations penetrate dust extinction and resolve free-free emission at the base of the jet (~15 au scales), while archival ALMA mm/sub-mm observations constrain the dust emission component in the SED.  
  > "In order to learn more about the launching and collimation of jets, high resolution observations near the base of the jet are necessary. Optical or IR emission from this region is often obscured by high levels of extinction, particularly in more embedded sources. Therefore, radio observations of the free-free emission are ideally suited for this purpose." (Introduction); "The resolution of these observations allows us to discern emission on scales of only $\sim 15\ \mathrm{au}$." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Combining e-MERLIN and VLA C-band visibilities provides both high angular resolution and short-baseline sensitivity; re-imaging all VLA/e-MERLIN bands with a matched uv-cutoff and beam avoids spatial-filtering bias in SED measurements; 3D radiative transfer modelling tests whether an injection gap r_0 reproduces the 5 GHz double-lobe structure.  
  > "In order to achieve good uv-coverage and create a high resolution image of L1551 IRS 5 at C Band, the visibility data from the e-MERLIN C band observation and the VLA C band observation were combined. This takes advantage of the high resolution of e-MERLIN and the sensitivity and short baseline coverage of the VLA." (Observations and Data Reduction); "When measuring the total flux densities of the sources in the e-MERLIN and VLA observations, the data at each frequency was re-imaged, using the same outer uv-cutoff and synthesised beam for each band. This was in order to measure and compare the total flux densities at different frequencies more accurately and avoid any issues with differences in uv-coverage and synthesised beam size between the images." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Disk wind model considered as an alternative to the X-wind model, but disfavoured because it is very difficult for a disk wind to produce the high velocity, small launch radius (<= 0.2 au), high ionisation, and high density within w_0 ≈ 2.5 au.  
  > "The high jet velocity of $v_0 \sim 150$ km/s suggests that the underlying magnetocentrifugal wind comes from the innermost $0.2$ au or smaller. It is very difficult to produce these findings by a disk wind. Our data and the modelling analysis thus favour the X-wind model." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Two alternative physical interpretations considered for the r_0 ≈ 10 au gap in L1551 IRS 5 S: either a recent mass-loss rate variation on a ~4 month timescale, or the jet material only becoming ionised at 10 au due to a shock front.  
  > "The value obtained for $r_0$ could be interpreted as being due to variability in the mass loss rate of the jet." (Radiative Transfer Modelling); "Alternatively, it could be the case that the material in the jet is only ionised at a distance of 10 au, possibly due to a shock in the jet, or a new ionising event due to variability." (Radiative Transfer Modelling)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Proper motion of jet knots and relative orbital proper motion between the binary components of L1551 IRS 5 could not be corrected when combining the 2020 e-MERLIN and 2021 VLA C-band data.  
  > "It should be noted that there would also be proper motion of the emission knots in the jet relative to the star and disk, which cannot be corrected for since the knots in the red-shifted and blue-shifted portions would be moving in opposite directions." (Observations and Data Reduction); "There is also relative proper motion of the two components with respect to each other due to orbital motion which also could not be corrected for" (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Significant uncertainty in the 5 GHz flux density of L1551 IRS 5 N due to low flux and spatial overlap with the S component.  
  > "Although this mean that there is a significant degree of uncertainty in the flux density of the N component of L1551 IRS 5 at 5 GHz, due to the low level of its flux density and the high degree of overlap with the emission of the S component." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): No proper motion measurements were available for L1551 NE to align the ALMA Band 7 and VLA positions, and L1551 NE only had ALMA data in a single band plus insufficient angular resolution to directly measure the jet opening angle.  
  > "Unlike in the case of L1551 IRS 5, the peak in the dust emission seen in the ALMA 336 GHz image was not used as there was proper motion between the epochs of the ALMA observation (Oct 2019) and the VLA observations (Dec 2019 - Jan 2020), which was not corrected for, as no proper motion measurements were available for L1551 NE." (L1551 NE); "For L1551 NE, we do not have as many data points as for L1551 IRS 5, particularly at higher frequencies where we only have ALMA observations in one band." (Spectrum); "For L1551 NE, neither our observations nor the observations from (cite: Reipurth2002) have sufficient resolution to estimate the major and minor axes of the jet." (Ionised Mass-loss Rate)
- Limitation (authors): SOFIA [O I]63 observations do not resolve the two individual jets in L1551 IRS 5, providing only a combined system mass-loss rate and rough estimates of the ionisation fractions.  
  > "Unfortunately, the two individual jets are unresolved and so we only have the total mass-loss rate for the whole system." (Ionised Mass-loss Rate); "However, these are only rough estimates for the ionisation fractions given the large errors involved." (Ionised Mass-loss Rate)
- Limitation (authors): The 3D radiative transfer model assumes a symmetric jet and does not account for the asymmetry seen in the SW blue-shifted lobe residuals.  
  > "Although in both the 5 GHz and 10 GHz images, there is some emission to the SW of the source in the blue-shifted jet which is still present in the residuals. This is likely as the jet is asymmetric, which was not accounted for in the model." (Radiative Transfer Modelling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA images were NOT re-imaged to a common uv-cutoff and beam size unlike the VLA/e-MERLIN data, and instead used subjective sigma thresholds (10 sigma and 25 sigma) to exclude circumbinary dust, which could systematically bias the high-frequency SED points and dust spectral indices.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The free-free spectral index was fixed to 0.6 (standard conical isothermal jet) during SED fitting rather than fitted as a free parameter, which forces a specific geometry on the derived K_ff values.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For L1551 NE, fitting a 3-parameter model (K_ff, K_dust, alpha_dust) with only one sub-mm data point (336 GHz) means alpha_dust is heavily dependent on the leveraged 20-24 GHz points where dust and free-free emission mix.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is an internal text inconsistency for the radiative-transfer derived ionised mass-loss rate of L1551 IRS 5 S: Section 4.5 reports 4.3e-9 M_sun/yr whereas the Conclusions report 5.7e-9 M_sun/yr.
