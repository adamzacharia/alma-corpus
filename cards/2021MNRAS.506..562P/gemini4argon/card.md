## Analysis recipe

1. **Register multi-wavelength optical/UV HST and near-IR VLT-NaCo images to a common frame**: Align optical and IR images to VLT-NaCo Ks or H band reference frame using point-like star-forming clusters and minimum square fit  
  > "Briefly, per each galaxy, the set of optical and IR images were aligned to a common reference frame, this being defined by the VLT-NaCo *Ks* or *H* band images." (Image registration); "These sources were used for image registration using a minimum square fit, the IRAF[^4] IMALIGN task was used." (Image registration)
   - software: IRAF (IMALIGN)
   - parameter: Point-like source FWHM <= 5 pc; positional errors 10 to 40 mas  
  > "To that aim, all the images were examined for several point-like sources, typically compact (FWHM $\lesssim 5\, \rm{pc}$) star forming clusters other than the galaxy nucleus, common to the FoV of all of them." (Image registration); "The positional errors range between $10$ and $40\, \rm{mas}$ (Table [\[tab:general_info\]](#tab:general_info), column 9), and are measured as the standard deviation of the offsets found per each point-like position and filter." (Image registration)
   - assumption: For NGC 3081 and NGC 3783, registration relies on the nucleus only due to lack of other point-like sources  
  > "By default the nucleus is not used in the alignment process, with the exceptions of NGC 3081 and NGC 3783 (Table [\[tab:general_info\]](#tab:general_info)) with no point-like sources other than the nucleus in the field of view." (Image registration)
   - fitting: Minimum square fit of Gaussian centroids of point-like sources  
  > "These sources were used for image registration using a minimum square fit, the IRAF[^4] IMALIGN task was used." (Image registration); "Positional errors depend on the quality of the Gaussian fit to determine the source’s centroid." (Image registration)
   - validation: Cross-checked IRAF IMALIGN results using custom scripts  
  > "We also used our own scripts to cross-check the IRAF results." (Image registration)
2. **Construct dust colour and extinction (A_V) maps and measure nuclear dust extinction**: Compute ratio of IR (VLT-NaCo Ks or H) to optical (HST) images, and compare colours in circular apertures on dust filaments vs nearby dust-free regions  
  > "To uncover the dust location and morphology, colour maps of the central kpc of the galaxies in the sample were constructed as a ratio of an IR to an optical image, usually VLT-NaCo *Ks* band to *HST* *V* band." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc); "Briefly, for each galaxy, colours are measured at the locations of the dust filaments and lanes in regions as close as possible to the nucleus, and then compared with equivalent measurements in dust-free regions in the in the neighbourhood selected by visual inspection." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
   - parameter: Circular aperture radius 0.1 - 0.3''  
  > "In all cases, colours are measured in circular apertures with radius that match the smallest dimension of the dust structures, typically a few tens of parsec, i.e. in the 0.1 – 0.3$''$ radius range, in line with the angular resolution of the images." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
   - assumption: Selected reference regions are dust-free (otherwise inferred A_V is a lower limit)  
  > "It should be noted that selected dust-free regions may still contain some dust, in which case the inferred $A_{\rm{V}}$ is a lower limit." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared A_V across multiple independent colour maps (B-K, V-K, V-H, V-I, I-K, J-K), finding agreement within < 0.5 mag  
  > "In most cases, it was possible to construct various K-band / optical colour maps depending on the availability of *HST* continuum images. This allowed us to check the reliability of the estimated $A_V$, values can be compared in Table [\[tab:extinction\]](#tab:extinction)." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc); "Still, the agreement in $A_{\rm{V}}$ from independent colour maps is remarkable, within less than $0.5\, \rm{mag}$ in most cases." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
3. **Extract continuum-free nuclear ionised gas maps**: Rescale closest-wavelength HST continuum image iteratively and subtract from narrow-band FR533N ([OIII]) and F658N (H-alpha+[NII]) images  
  > "Specifically, continuum-free line images were extracted from *HST* narrow-band FR533N and F658N images centred in \[OIII\] and H$\alpha$+\[NII\], respectively." (Nuclear ionised gas); "To estimate the continuum contribution, the continuum image was multiplied by a scaling factor chosen iteratively until the continuum-free line image did not show negative values." (Nuclear ionised gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Iterative check until continuum-free line image showed no negative values  
  > "To estimate the continuum contribution, the continuum image was multiplied by a scaling factor chosen iteratively until the continuum-free line image did not show negative values." (Nuclear ionised gas)
4. **Extract ALMA CO(3-2) maps for NGC 1566, register to dust maps, and estimate molecular gas mass inflow rate**: Register ALMA data to optical/IR frame using central continuum point source; integrate CO(3-2) channels above 2-sigma and selectively over blueshifted velocities (20 to 80 km/s); compute inflow rate from filament width, extinction-derived density, and kinematic velocity  
  > "To register this image to the dust map, ALMA line-free continuum emission maps at distinct frequencies were evaluated, all showing a single point-like source at the centre of the field which was assumed to be the nucleus." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre); "For best illustration, Fig. [4](#fig:alma)-right-panel shows a selective integration map in velocity channels, namely the blueshifted velocity channels from $20\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$ to $80\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$, which show one of the most prominent dust filaments with counterpart in CO(3–2)" (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre); "The volume density and filament width are estimated from the extinction map." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre)
   - parameter: Blueshifted velocity channels 20 to 80 km/s; filament width W = 20 pc (FWHM <= 200 mas); V_flow = 80 km/s  
  > "For best illustration, Fig. [4](#fig:alma)-right-panel shows a selective integration map in velocity channels, namely the blueshifted velocity channels from $20\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$ to $80\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}}$, which show one of the most prominent dust filaments with counterpart in CO(3–2)" (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre); "The filament width, W, corresponds to a FWHM $\lesssim 200\, \rm{mas}$, or $20\, \rm{pc}$." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre)
   - parameter: Two sigma level signal threshold for channel sum  
  > "The CO(3–2) map presented here is a sum up of the velocity channels with two sigma level signal in the line and no background suppression applied to unveil all possible emission counterpart to the dust map also at larger radii from the centre" (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre)
   - assumption: Central ALMA continuum point source is the nucleus; inclination angle = 28 deg; dust-to-gas ratio = 2e21 cm^-2 mag^-1; filament depth equals width; filling factor = 1; factor of 2 for second filament  
  > "For velocity de-projection, an inclination angle to the line of sight of 28 degrees is assumed (e.g. (cite: Garcia-gomez1991) and references therein)." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre); "Assuming a dust to gas ratio of $2 \times 10^{21}\, \rm{cm^{-2}\,mag^{-1}}$ (cite: Savage-Mathis1979) and the filament width equal to its depth, the volume density in the central few tens of parsec is $n_{\rm e} \sim 50\, \rm{cm^{-3}}$. As the filaments are spatially separated, a filling factor in the filament equal to one is assumed." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre); "The factor 2 accounts for a second much shorter filament seen South-West the nucleus at the redshifted velocities in (cite: Combes2019) kinematic map, presumably also approaching the centre." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre)

## Qualitative findings

- Long, narrow dust filaments and lanes (extending hundreds of pc to kpc) are ubiquitous in the central parsecs of type 1–1.9 AGN, with M87 as the sole dust-free exception.  
  > "The dust maps (Section [2.3](#sec:DustMaps_extinction)) show the ubiquity presence of obscuring material in the central parsecs of all the study galaxies, the sole exception is M87." (Nuclear dust: morphology, location, and extinction)
- In type 1–1.9 AGN, optical and IR nuclear peaks coincide within astrometric precision (10-40 mas), whereas in type 2 AGN they are shifted by a few parsecs due to optically thick crossing filaments.  
  > "Yet, in the present sample the optical and IR peak, amid the filaments and lanes crossing the centre, always coincide in position, within our precision astrometry of few tens of mas, i.e. parsec to sub-parsec scales (Table [\[tab:extinction\]](#tab:extinction))." (Nuclear dust: morphology, location, and extinction)
- Because the dust extinction in type 1–1.9 filaments is moderate (typically A_V < 2 mag), nuclear ionised gas overlaps and interlaces with dust structures rather than showing the strict spatial anti-correlation seen in type 2 AGN.  
  > "On the contrary, in the present sample, the ionised gas spreads all over the nuclear region in most cases regardless of the dust location. This overlap may be due to two reasons: 1) the low extinction of the filaments and lanes, typically $A_V < 2\, \rm{mag}$" (Conclusions)
- Nuclear dust filaments have direct counterparts in molecular gas (e.g. ALMA CO(3-2) in NGC 1566), acting as streamers channeling material from galactic scales into the nucleus.  
  > "Filaments and lanes are traced by equivalent structures in molecular cold and warm gas in CO, HCN and $H_2$. A single filament can be seen either getting straight- or spiralling- to the centre, suggesting their role as streamers to channel material from galactic scales to the centre." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Comparable high angular resolution (<200 mas) of VLT-AO near-IR and HST optical/UV images allows registration at the mas level to locate the nucleus and parsec-scale dust filaments; ALMA CO(3-2) traces the molecular gas counterpart and kinematics.  
  > "The comparable resolution of the VLT-AO images in the IR to that of the HST in the optical-UV allows us to produce an accurate registration of the whole set of images at the mas level." (Image registration); "Accordingly, we look for their counterpart in molecular tracers to infer on the kinematics." (Dust filaments traced by molecular gas: streaming material to the centre)
- Why this method: Eclipse was preferred over ESOreflex for VLT-NaCo reduction due to extensive knowledge of the package and ability to modify background subtraction and frame registration parameters; colour maps are more sensitive to dust than single-band images.  
  > "The use of this package is preferred to the standard ESOreflex due to our extensive knowledge of the package and the possibility to modify parameters for background subtraction and frames registration optimization." (Data acquisition and reduction); "in the present work, the high angular resolution of the IR / optical colour maps and the fact that colours rather than single images are more sensitive to dust, allow us to trace these structures down to the inner few parsec in type 2 and 1 alike" (Nuclear dust: morphology, location, and extinction)
- Alternative: Standard ESOreflex pipeline was rejected in favour of the Eclipse package for VLT-NaCo data reduction.  
  > "The use of this package is preferred to the standard ESOreflex due to our extensive knowledge of the package and the possibility to modify parameters for background subtraction and frames registration optimization." (Data acquisition and reduction)
- Alternative: Compact parsec-scale donut-like dusty torus as the universal cause of AGN 1/2 dichotomy and NLR ionised gas collimation is challenged in favour of hundred-pc to kpc scale host-galaxy dust filaments and lanes.  
  > "Their location with respect to the nucleus and increasing gradient in optical depth towards the centre could naturally lead to the canonical type 1 / 2 AGN classification, making these filaments to play the role of the torus." (Abstract); "Thus, it was concluded that the typical nuclear ionisation cones and collimated morphologies that characterise the narrow line region in the sample are not caused by a parsec-scale collimating torus as envisaged by the AGN unified model but simply by the location / morphology of the filaments and lanes in the central region ((cite: Prieto2014))." (Nuclear ionised gas location relative to the dust)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Selected dust-free reference regions may still contain some dust, making the inferred A_V values lower limits.  
  > "It should be noted that selected dust-free regions may still contain some dust, in which case the inferred $A_{\rm{V}}$ is a lower limit." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
- Limitation (authors): Reported A_V values cannot measure extinction at the nucleus itself, only in its surroundings (tens of pc away), because nuclear colours are contaminated by AGN non-thermal emission and hot dust emission.  
  > "Also we note that the reported $A_{\rm{V}}$ values do not measure any extinction at the nucleus itself but at its surrounding, typically within ten of parsec from the centre: nuclear colors are not representative of extinction as both non-thermal emission and presumably dust in emission from the AGN also contribute to these colors." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
- Limitation (authors): In star-forming regions, jets, or the steep UV nuclear continuum where optical emission is intrinsically stronger than IR, the colour maps do not measure dust extinction.  
  > "At these locations, included the nucleus as just discussed above, the maps do not measure dust extinction but simply the stellar or non-thermal contribution." (Dust maps and extinction in the central kpc)
- Limitation (authors): For NGC 3081 and NGC 3783, no point-like clusters other than the nucleus were available in the FoV, so image registration had to rely on the nucleus itself.  
  > "By default the nucleus is not used in the alignment process, with the exceptions of NGC 3081 and NGC 3783 (Table [\[tab:general_info\]](#tab:general_info)) with no point-like sources other than the nucleus in the field of view. In these cases the registration relies on the nucleus only." (Image registration)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because NGC 3081 and NGC 3783 were registered using the nucleus itself, the conclusion that optical and IR nuclear peaks coincide cannot be independently tested in those two galaxies.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The mass inflow rate estimate in NGC 1566 relies on strong geometric and physical assumptions (filament depth equal to width, volume filling factor of 1, dust-to-gas ratio from Milky Way) and an inflow velocity read off a literature CO(2-1) map rather than fitted directly to the CO(3-2) cube.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extinction values A_V are measured 0.5'' to 1.5'' (tens to hundreds of pc) away from the AGN, so extrapolating their optical depth to sub-parsec nuclear scales to replace the torus remains indirect.
