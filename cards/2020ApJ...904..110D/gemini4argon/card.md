## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify and fit vibrationally excited AlO lines in SMA spectra of VY CMa**: Extract spectra from the central pixel (~1'' beam) of SMA data and perform least-squares fits of Gaussian profiles (for v = 0, 1) and composite synthetic hyperfine profiles (for v = 2).  
  > "All measurements were done on spectra obtained from the central pixel of the SMA data, corresponding to a restoring beam of ~ 1'' centred on the continuum peak of VY CMa" (3.1. VY CMa); "The observed frequencies nu_obs in the ground vibrational state and the v = 1 level and the FWHM line width, Delta v, were derived from Gaussian profiles that were least squares fit to the spectra (see Fig. 2)." (3.1. VY CMa); "The synthetic profiles of the lines in the v = 2 vibrational state were obtained by representing each hyperfine component with a Gaussian profile whose HWHM was 2.5 km s^-1." (3.1. VY CMa)
   - parameter: HWHM = 2.5 km s^-1 for hyperfine components in v = 2  
  > "The synthetic profiles of the lines in the v = 2 vibrational state were obtained by representing each hyperfine component with a Gaussian profile whose HWHM was 2.5 km s^-1." (3.1. VY CMa)
   - assumption: Relative intensities of hyperfine components equal to theoretical values in Launila & Banerjee (2009)  
  > "The relative intensities of the hyperfine components were equivalent to the theoretical relative intensities in Launila & Banerjee (2009)." (3.1. VY CMa)
   - fitting: Least squares fit with intensity scaling factor and center frequency as variables for v = 2  
  > "The variables in the least squares fit of the synthetic profile to the observed profile were: an intensity (scaling) factor of the composite theoretical synthetic profile and the center frequency." (3.1. VY CMa)
   - validation: Comparison of derived LSR velocity with stellar velocity and ground state AlO velocity, and comparison of line width ratios across v states with theoretical spin-rotation interaction predictions.  
  > "The mean central LSR velocity v_LSR = 21.5 +- 3.3 km s^-1 for the four rotational transitions in the excited vibrational levels of AlO in Table 1 is in good agreement with the stellar velocity (v_LSR = 22 km s^-1, Kaminski et al. 2013a)" (3.1. VY CMa); "As expected, the FWHM of the lines are 3.3 +- 0.4 times smaller in the v = 1 level, and 1.6 +- 0.3 times smaller in the v = 2 level than those in the ground vibrational state — i.e., close to the theoretical predictions discussed in Sect. 2.1" (3.1. VY CMa)
2. **Extract ALMA spectra and construct moment maps for R Dor and IK Tau**: Extract spectra in circular apertures from calibrated spectral cubes, measure peak and integrated fluxes, fit Gaussians to TiO lines, and create zeroth-order moment maps over channels exceeding the rms.  
  > "We extracted spectra in an aperture of 150 mas radius for AlO and 300 mas for TiO towards R Dor and in an aperture of 320 mas for AlO or 160 mas for TiO for IK Tau." (3.2. R Dor and IK Tau); "We made zeroth-order moments (integrated flux density) over the channels where the line signal exceeded the rms." (3.2. R Dor and IK Tau); "Based on the line frequencies reported in Table 4, we find the mean central velocity of the TiO lines towards R Dor is v_cent(TiO) = 7.8 +- 0.4 km s^-1, based on fitting Gaussians to the emission for lines with v <= 2 and excluding the line blended with SO2." (3.2.2. TiO)
   - parameter: Extraction aperture radii: 150 mas (R Dor AlO), 300 mas (R Dor TiO), 320 mas (IK Tau AlO), 160 mas (IK Tau TiO)  
  > "We extracted spectra in an aperture of 150 mas radius for AlO and 300 mas for TiO towards R Dor and in an aperture of 320 mas for AlO or 160 mas for TiO for IK Tau." (3.2. R Dor and IK Tau)
   - parameter: Moment map threshold: channels where line signal exceeded rms  
  > "We made zeroth-order moments (integrated flux density) over the channels where the line signal exceeded the rms." (3.2. R Dor and IK Tau)
   - fitting: Gaussian fitting to TiO emission lines  
  > "Based on the line frequencies reported in Table 4, we find the mean central velocity of the TiO lines towards R Dor is v_cent(TiO) = 7.8 +- 0.4 km s^-1, based on fitting Gaussians to the emission for lines with v <= 2 and excluding the line blended with SO2." (3.2.2. TiO)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive vibrational temperatures of AlO and TiO in R Dor using vibrational temperature diagrams**: Construct vibrational temperature diagrams using integrated fluxes of ground and excited vibrational transitions and the standard expression (Eq. A5 in Snyder et al. 2005).  
  > "The VT diagram was constructed with the integrated flux of the N = 9 - 8 transition in the v = 2 excited vibrational level, and three transitions in the ground vibrational state (Fig. 4), and the standard expression (see Eq. A5 in Snyder et al. 2005) on the assumption the emission fills the synthesized beam of the telescope." (3.2.1. AlO); "The vibrational temperature for TiO in R Dor was derived by the same procedure adopted for AlO in Sect. 3.2.1, except the partition function is from McKemmish et al. (2019)." (3.2.2. TiO)
   - assumption: Emission fills the synthesized beam of the telescope; LTE  
  > "The VT diagram was constructed with the integrated flux of the N = 9 - 8 transition in the v = 2 excited vibrational level, and three transitions in the ground vibrational state (Fig. 4), and the standard expression (see Eq. A5 in Snyder et al. 2005) on the assumption the emission fills the synthesized beam of the telescope." (3.2.1. AlO)
   - fitting: Linear fit in log(Nu/gu) vs Energy diagram  
  > "Shown in Fig. 4 is a vibrational temperature (VT) diagram for AlO in R Dor." (3.2.1. AlO); "From the derived T_vib of 1800 +- 170 K (see diagram in Fig. 6)" (3.2.2. TiO)
   - validation: Comparison of predicted v = 3 peak flux of TiO from T_vib with observed spectral features.  
  > "From the derived T_vib of 1800 +- 170 K (see diagram in Fig. 6), we estimate the peak flux of the J = 11 -> 10 rotational transitions in the v = 3 excited vibrational level (E_u = 4275 K) of 19 mJy is within a few mJy of the peak flux in the spectra centered on the Omega = 2 and Omega = 3 spin components" (3.2.2. TiO)
4. **Perform 1D non-LTE radiative transfer modeling of AlO in R Dor**: Model the circumstellar envelope using a 1D spherically symmetric non-LTE ALI radiative transfer code without hyperfine structure, then distribute integrated flux among hyperfine components according to quantum mechanical line strengths and sum Gaussians to obtain line profiles.  
  > "For this, we used a 1D spherically symmetric non-LTE radiative transfer program based on the accelerated lambda iteration method (ALI; Rybicki & Hummer 1991; Maercker et al. 2008) to calculate the abundances and line profiles of AlO and TiO." (4.1. Method); "The calculated total integrated flux for each transition was then distributed among the individual hyperfine components according to their quantum mechanical line strengths (see Yamada et al. 1990; Goto et al. 1994; Launila & Jonsson 1994; Launila & Banerjee 2009)." (4.1.1. AlO)
   - software: ALI
   - parameter: 30 shells logarithmically spaced from r_0 = 3.3 x 10^13 cm to r_max = 4 x 10^14 cm; circular model beam radius = 150 mas  
  > "For modelling purposes, we analyzed spectra extracted with a circular aperture with a radius of 150 mas, for AlO, and 300 mas, for TiO, because this included all the observed emission (see Sect. 3.2 for details)." (4.1. Method); "Our model circumstellar envelopes (CSEs) were divided into 30 shells, evenly spaced on a logarithmic scale from an inner radius r_0 = 3.3 x 10^13 cm, out to an outer radius r_max = 4 x 10^14 cm." (4.1. Method)
   - parameter: Levels included: N < 20 and v <= 10; hyperfine Gaussian HWHM = 3 km s^-1  
  > "We used Gaussians with a HWHM of 3 km s^-1, close to the innermost velocity as derived from the sound speed, to represent the hyperfine components." (4.1.1. AlO); "Hence, we included all levels with N < 20 and v <= 10 as tabulated in ExoMol (Patrascu et al. 2015; Tennyson et al. 2016)." (4.3. AlO results)
   - parameter: Stellar/CSE parameters from Table 5: R_* = 3.3 x 10^13 cm, T_eff = 2400 K, D = 59 pc, L_* = 6500 L_sun, M_dot = 1.6 x 10^-7 M_sun yr^-1, v_inf = 5.7 km s^-1, v_0 = 3 km s^-1, v_LSR = 7.0 km s^-1, v_turb = 1 km s^-1, epsilon = 0.65, beta = 1.5, r_e = 1 x 10^14 cm  
  > "R_* cm 3.3 x 10^13 T_eff K 2400 D pc 59 L_* L_sun 6500 M_dot 10^-7 M_sun yr^-1 1.6" (Table 5); "v_inf km s^-1 5.7 v_0 km s^-1 3 v_LSR km s^-1 7.0 v_turb km s^-1 1 epsilon ... 0.65 beta ... 1.5 r_0 cm 3.3 x 10^13 r_e cm 1 x 10^14" (Table 5)
   - assumption: Gaussian fractional abundance profile with r_e = 1 x 10^14 cm; He-SiO collisional rates scaled by mass as surrogate for H2-AlO  
  > "Instead the rates for He-SiO (Dayou & Balanca 2006) and He-NaCl (Quintana-Lacaci et al. 2016) — appropriately scaled to account for the difference in mass of these systems (see Schoier et al. 2005, for details) — were used as surrogates." (4.2.1. Molecular parameters); "The fractional abundance of AlO and TiO were represented by a Gaussian profile" (4.2.2. Stellar and circumstellar parameters); "For both molecules, an e-folding radius of 1 x 10^14 cm was used." (4.2.2. Stellar and circumstellar parameters)
   - fitting: Single free parameter f_AlO adjusted to reproduce observed line profiles

## Qualitative findings

- Rotational transitions of vibrationally excited AlO (v = 1, 2) are identified in VY CMa, AlO (v = 2) and TiO (v = 1, 2) in R Dor, and TiO (v = 1) in IK Tau.  
  > "The J = 11 -> 10 transition of TiO in the v = 1 and 2 levels, and the N = 9 -> 8 transition in the v = 2 level of AlO were identified towards R Dor; the J = 11 -> 10 line of TiO was identified in the v = 1 level towards IK Tau; and two transitions in the v = 1 and 2 levels of AlO were identified towards VY CMa." (abstract)
- Infrared radiative pumping plays a critical role in populating vibrationally excited levels of AlO and TiO in the inner wind of R Dor.  
  > "Our radiative transfer calculations confirm the importance of infrared radiative pumping on the excitation of AlO and TiO in the inner wind of R Dor when observed at high angular resolution." (5.1. Our results in context)
- TiO emission towards R Dor is spatially unresolved (within ~75 mas or <= 2 R_*) and exhibits broadened and slightly redshifted line profiles, providing indirect evidence that TiO resides in a rotating disk close to the star.  
  > "The observations provide indirect evidence that TiO is present in a rotating disk close to the star." (abstract); "However, since our observations are not spatially resolved, we are unable to properly constrain the extent of the TiO emission, other than it is within ~ 75 mas of the star, based on the ALMA beam size of ~ 150 mas." (5.1.2. TiO)
- Weak, spatially unresolved gas-phase TiO2 lines are detected towards R Dor with intensities 4-5 times lower than TiO, preventing conclusive determination of whether TiO/TiO2 are nucleation seeds for Al2O3 dust.  
  > "The rotational lines of TiO2 are 4-5 times less intense than those of TiO, and the line profile shape may be narrower. However, the low intensity and lower signal to noise ratio of the TiO2 lines makes it difficult to conduct any conclusive analysis" (5.2. Evidence for gas-phase TiO2 towards R Dor)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: R Dor is the closest AGB star to the Sun (59 pc) with a large stellar diameter (54 mas) and rich molecular spectrum, and existing sensitive interferometric spectral line surveys of R Dor, IK Tau, and VY CMa have high enough ground-state peak fluxes and gas temperatures (~1400 K) to make vibrationally excited AlO and TiO observable.  
  > "The semiregular (SR) variable oxygen rich star R Dor is the closest AGB star to our Sun (59 pc, Knapp et al. 2003). It has a very low mass loss rate (1.6 x 10^-7 M_sun yr^-1, Maercker et al. 2016), the stellar diameter is large (54 mas, Norris et al. 2012), it is molecule rich, and there is a 27 GHz wide spectral line survey in Band 7 with ALMA" (1. INTRODUCTION); "First, the lowest vibrational levels of both molecules will be populated in the inner wind, because the vibrational frequencies of AlO (1389 K) and TiO (1438 K) are comparable to the kinetic temperature of the gas (T_gas) within a few stellar radii of the star" (3. ASTRONOMICAL OBSERVATIONS); "And finally, the peak fluxes of the ground state lines in sensitive interferometric spectra are high enough so lines in the lowest few excited vibrational levels should be observable in existing surveys." (3. ASTRONOMICAL OBSERVATIONS)
- Why this method: Explicitly including all hyperfine levels of AlO or fine-structure ladders of TiO in non-LTE radiative transfer is computationally prohibitive, so radiative transfer was solved without hyperfine/fine structure and the resulting fluxes were distributed/scaled among components afterwards; a smaller 150 mas aperture was chosen for AlO in R Dor to exclude the asymmetric SE extension that a 1D spherical model cannot reproduce.  
  > "Explicitly including all the AlO hyperfine levels and the connecting transitions in the radiative transfer analysis is computationally prohibitive, even if the included levels are restricted to a relatively small number of rotational levels (e.g., N <= 20)." (4.1.1. AlO); "To analyze TiO in the most efficient way possible, we first ran a radiative transfer model excluding the fine structure — i.e., TiO was treated as though it were a closed shell molecule." (4.1.2. TiO); "In the case of AlO, a smaller aperture was selected to avoid including the SE extension seen in the zeroth moment maps of Fig 3. The extension breaks spherical symmetry and hence cannot be reproduced by a 1D model." (A.2. Impact of the south-eastern extension in AlO emission)
- Alternative: Different collisional rate prescriptions for AlO (SiO-He, NaCl-He, NaCl-He multiplied by 100, and zero collisions) were tested and found to have negligible impact on v = 0 lines and only small impact on the v = 2 line.  
  > "The difference between the calculated lines profiles and the observed lines for the different sets of collisional rates was negligible in the ground vibrational state and was small for the line in the excited v = 2 level." (A.1. Choice of collisional rates for AlO)
- Alternative: Using a 300 mas spectral extraction aperture for AlO in R Dor was tested, showing that including the asymmetric SE extension causes the 1D model to under-predict the N = 9 -> 8 and 8 -> 7 lines.  
  > "The differences between Figures 8 and 14 clearly show the impact of the SE extension: the ground state lines of (9 -> 8) and (8 -> 7) are slightly under-predicted when additional emission from the SE extension is included in the observed spectra" (A.2. Impact of the south-eastern extension in AlO emission)
- Alternative: Including low-lying electronically excited singlet and triplet states of TiO (up to 9 electronic states) and extending vibrational levels to v <= 10 were tested, but only caused small improvements and could not reproduce the vibrationally excited TiO lines without an additional IR radiation field.  
  > "Indeed, a test with TiO vibrational levels up to v <= 10 confirmed this." (4.4. TiO results); "We found that including more electronic levels only caused incremental differences and small improvements in the model, and the model did not adequately reproduce the vibrationally excited lines." (4.4. TiO results)
- Alternative: Including silicate dust with optical depth 0.05 at 10 um (Maercker et al. 2016) was tested and found to have no noticeable effect on TiO or AlO emission.  
  > "Including silicate dust with their derived opacity (optical depth of 0.05 at 10 um, Maercker et al. 2016) did not have a noticeable effect on the TiO (or AlO) emission." (4.4. TiO results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
