## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks
- Question: Does the MP Mus disk show a ring in ALMA Band 7 (0.89 mm) dust continuum, and what do the spectral index and the gas versus dust extent imply about radial drift and grain growth?  
  > "In this paper, we analyzed new ALMA Band 7 observations of MP Mus in order to investigate the details of its disk substructures." (abstract); "We conclude that there are strong indicators for an active radial drift process within the disk." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and self-calibrate the visibilities**: ALMA staff pipeline calibration, then phase-only self-calibration of the extended configurations (continuum, line channels excluded)  
  > "we used the standard pipeline calibration provided by the ALMA staff using the Common Astronomy Software Applications package" (2 Observations and data reduction); "we performed selfcalibration to the extended configurations for each band; first excluding all channels with emission lines and then, using `mtmfs` deconvolver, `Briggs` weighting, a `robust` value of 0.5 and -0.5, and `nterms`=2." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Band 7: five phase-only steps, 60 s (x4) and 45 s (x1)  
  > "We sought solutions over 60 s intervals on the first four steps and solutions over 45 s on the fifth step." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Band 6: four phase-only steps, 35 s (x2) and 25 s (x2)  
  > "sought solutions over 35 s for the first and second steps, and solutions over 25 s for the third and fourth steps." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Band 7 rms improved by 60% and peak S/N from 125 to 394 at r = 0.5; Band 6 S/N from ~40 to ~320  
  > "we improved the root mean square (rms) noise by 60% and S/N of the emission peak by 215% (from 125 to 394)" (2 Observations and data reduction); "S/N is improved by 800% (from $\sim$40 to $\sim$320)" (2 Observations and data reduction)
2. **Combine compact and extended data for line analysis**: Fit 2D Gaussian to find the continuum peak in each dataset, set a common phase center with fixvis/fixplanets, then concat  
  > "we found the peak of the disk continuum emission by fitting a 2D Gaussian shape to each dataset." (2 Observations and data reduction); "we applied CASA tasks `fixvis` and `fixplanets` to set the phase center to a common coordinate to finally use `concat` task to concatenate both data sets." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to the continuum peak (details not stated)  
  > "we found the peak of the disk continuum emission by fitting a 2D Gaussian shape to each dataset." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - validation: Combining compact and extended configurations for continuum gave no improvement in rms or image quality, so only extended data were used for continuum  
  > "After combining the compact and extended configurations we did not see an improvement in the rms nor in the image quality" (2 Observations and data reduction)
3. **Image the dust continuum at 0.89 mm and 1.3 mm**: tclean with mtmfs deconvolver, nterms=2, Briggs robust 0.5 and -0.5 on self-calibrated extended-configuration data  
  > "We applied the `tclean` algorithm with ` mtmfs` deconvolver and "nterms=2"." (3.1.1 Continuum images); "We only used selfcalibrated extended configurations to produce the continuum images and deprojected brightness profiles." (3.1.1 Continuum images)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5 (S/N and resolution compromise) and -0.5 (finer structure)  
  > "Additionally, we applied $r=-0.5$ to explore the disk with a better angular resolution" (3.1.1 Continuum images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Maximum recoverable scales (2.7" and 1.8") larger than the disk angular size  
  > "Both values are larger than the observed angular size of the disk." (3.1.1 Continuum images)
4. **Measure continuum fluxes**: Aperture photometry with a 5-sigma mask on the continuum images  
  > "To estimate continuum fluxes, we applied aperture photometry on these images with a 5-$\sigma$ mask." (3.1.1 Continuum images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive disk geometry and deprojected radial profiles**: 2D Gaussian fit to the Band 7 continuum for inclination, PA and center; deproject and take azimuthal average in concentric rings  
  > "based on a 2D Gaussian fit to the 0.89 mm dust continuum in Band 7" (3.1.2 Continuum radial profiles); "We then extracted the azimuthal average radial profile, as the average intensity within concentric rings centered at the peak intensity of the source." (3.1.2 Continuum radial profiles)
   - parameter: i = 32 deg, PA = 10 deg  
  > "were measured as $32^\circ$ and $10^\circ$" (3.1.2 Continuum radial profiles)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit (details not stated)  
  > "based on a 2D Gaussian fit to the 0.89 mm dust continuum in Band 7" (3.1.2 Continuum radial profiles)
   - validation: Inclination and PA agree with Band 6 estimates and scattered-light values  
  > "which is in total agreement with estimations made from Band 6 at 1.3 mm dust continuum (cite: Ribas2023) and scattered light observations (cite: Avenhaus2018)" (3.1.2 Continuum radial profiles)
6. **Reconstruct a super-resolution 1D profile from visibilities**: Frank (frankenstein) nonparametric Gaussian-process fit of the Band 7 continuum visibilities  
  > "we used the frankenstein software (cite: Jennings2020) to construct a 1D radial profile from the visibilities of the continuum in Band 7" (3.1.3 Frank radial profile)
   - software: frankenstein
   - assumption: Azimuthal symmetry  
  > "Frank calculates super resolution radial profiles of protoplanetary disks assuming azimuthal symmetry" (3.1.3 Frank radial profile)
   - fitting: Gaussian process fit to visibilities, nonparametric radial brightness reconstruction  
  > "the Frank software fits the visibilities directly and reconstructs a 1D radial brightness profile nonparametrically." (3.1.3 Frank radial profile)
   - validation: Frank inclination and PA consistent with adopted values; ring bump at ~45-50 au agrees with image and radial profile  
  > "The inclination and position angle values derived from Frank are consistent with the previously adopted results." (3.1.3 Frank radial profile); "we noticed that the bump at $\sim$45-50 au is present" (3.1.3 Frank radial profile)
7. **Measure gas disk radii from CO**: Deproject moment 0 images, extract azimuthally averaged radial profiles, take the radius enclosing 95% of the integrated flux  
  > "we deprojected moment 0 images using position angle and inclination previously estimated by a 2D Gaussian fit of the continuum" (Gas disk radii); "We estimated the gas disk radius, $R_{gas}$, as the radius that contains 95$\%$ of the total flux." (Gas disk radii)
   - parameter: Combined compact+extended line data used for line analysis  
  > "for line analysis, we used the combined compact and extended configurations of each data set" (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared to R_CO >= 2 R_mm relation from the literature  
  > "which results in total agreement with the relation found by (cite: Andrews2020) of $R_{\rm CO} \geq 2R_{mm}$" (3.1.4 Dust continuum versus 12CO profiles)
8. **Estimate dust and total disk masses**: Optically thin, Rayleigh-Jeans dust mass M_dust = F d^2 / (kappa B(T)) at each band; total disk mass from Cieza et al. flux relations  
  > "Here, we use the following relation to estimate the disk dust mass (cite: Hildebrand1983):" (3.2.1 Disk masses); "we use the linear relations derived by (cite: Cieza2008) to estimate the total disk mass from the observed flux" (3.2.1 Disk masses)
   - parameter: d = 98 pc (Gaia DR3)  
  > "where $d = 98 pc$ is the distance to MP Mus from Gaia DR3." (3.2.1 Disk masses)
   - parameter: T = 20 K, kappa ~ 3 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "a temperature of $T = 20 K$ and $\kappa_{\nu} \sim 3cm^{2}g^{-1}$ as the dust opacity at 0.89 mm" (3.2.1 Disk masses)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission at scales much larger than 10 au  
  > "the continuum emission is assumed to be optically thin" (3.2.1 Disk masses)
   - assumption: Rayleigh-Jeans Planck function  
  > "since the Planck function is close to the Rayleigh-Jeans regime" (3.2.1 Disk masses)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100 in the Cieza relations  
  > "simply assumes a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100" (3.2.1 Disk masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Masses are stated as upper limits; opacities noted as highly uncertain  
  > "as upper limits." (3.2.1 Disk masses)
9. **Derive the integrated and radially resolved spectral index**: Integrated alpha from the two band fluxes via F1/F2 = (nu1/nu2)^alpha; spectral index map from joint tclean mtmfs nterms=2 imaging of both bands after aligning phase centers, deprojected to radial profiles  
  > "we can fit the observed submillimeter and millimeter SED between two frequencies with $F_{\nu 1}/F_{\nu 2} = (\nu_{1}/\nu_{2})^{\alpha_{mm}}$" (3.2.2 Spectral index); "we set a common phase center estimated by a 2D Gaussian fit, and then we used the `tclean` task with `mtmfs` deconvolver, `nterms`=2, which internally sets a common beam size between the two visibility data sets." (3.2.2 Spectral index)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust 0.5, 0.0 and -0.5 tested for the alpha map  
  > "We also probe with different `robust` values (0.5, 0.0, and -0.5)." (3.2.2 Spectral index)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust in the Rayleigh-Jeans limit, alpha = beta + 2  
  > "where the sub-mm/mm spectral index is related to the dust opacity index by $\alpha_{mm} = \beta_{mm} + 2$." (3.2.2 Spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Maps compared across robust values; alpha range depends on robust  
  > "depending on the robust value we used" (4.2 Indicators of grain growth and radial drift on the disk)
10. **Test whether the Band 7 ring is visible at Band 6 resolution**: Convolve the 0.89 mm image to the 1.3 mm beam after aligning phase centers, and inspect convolved and residual images; also make an r^2-weighted brightness profile  
  > "we convolved the 0.89 mm continuum image with the beam size of the 1.3 mm continuum image and then corrected the images to a common phase center, estimated by a 2D Gaussian fit." (4.1 Millimeter substructure in Band 7); "we made a brightness profile multiplying the brightness intensity by $r^{2}$" (4.1 Millimeter substructure in Band 7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ring vanishes after convolution but remains in the residual image, taken to mean Band 6 lacks resolution  
  > "The ring structure seems to disappear with the convolution to the larger beam size" (4.1 Millimeter substructure in Band 7); "However, the ring structure is clearly presented in the residual image" (4.1 Millimeter substructure in Band 7)
11. **Interpret gas versus dust extent and compare with opacity theory**: Compare R_gas/R_dust to the criterion for dust evolution and compare scattered-light and mm profiles with Birnstiel et al. 2015 predictions  
  > "we obtained an estimated ratio of $R_{gas}/R_{dust} \sim 2$" (4.2 Indicators of grain growth and radial drift on the disk); "(cite: Birnstiel2015) proposed that the inconsistency between the near infrared and submillimeter observed disk profiles may be an effect of dust opacity due to dust evolution." (4.3 Comparison with disk opacity theory)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note disk modeling including total CO content is needed to identify the dust evolution process  
  > "However, identifying the dust evolution process from $R_{gas}/R_{dust}$ requires modeling the disk structure, including the total CO content." (4.2 Indicators of grain growth and radial drift on the disk)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Band 7 is the focus for continuum; the extended Band 6 configuration was used for comparison as it has the best resolution of the two Band 6 datasets  
  > "we focus on Band 7 observations, and for comparison, we used the extended configuration of 2017.1.01167.S in Band 6 since it has the best resolution between the two datasets." (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: robust = 0.5 chosen as a compromise between resolution and S/N and for consistency with Ribas et al. 2023  
  > "We adopted a robust value of $r = 0.5$, which is an intermediate value between the angular resolution and S/N" (3.1.1 Continuum images)
- Alternative: Combining compact and extended configurations for the continuum was tried and rejected  
  > "After combining the compact and extended configurations we did not see an improvement in the rms nor in the image quality" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Disk shadowing / dust-opacity (Birnstiel et al. 2015) explanation judged incompatible with the 50 au ring  
  > "whose results are incompatible with the predictions for this effect" (4.3 Comparison with disk opacity theory)
- How they decided it was enough: Compact configurations were dropped for continuum because they gave no improvement in rms or image quality  
  > "we did not see an improvement in the rms nor in the image quality, thus we decided to use only the extended configurations" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Dust opacities are highly uncertain, affecting the dust masses  
  > "their values still remain highly uncertain" (3.2.1 Disk masses)
- Limitation (authors): Compact-configuration Band 7 data had phase calibration issues  
  > "the compact configurations Band 7 dataset showed issues during the phase calibration" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Second bump at ~60 au is a very low S/N feature  
  > "but this is a very low S/N feature." (3.1.2 Continuum radial profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Identifying dust evolution requires disk modeling including total CO content  
  > "requires modeling the disk structure, including the total CO content." (4.2 Indicators of grain growth and radial drift on the disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ring significance is not quantified (no statistical test or uncertainty on ring radius/contrast); the Band 7 r=-0.5 peak S/N is only 141
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust masses use a single T = 20 K and kappa = 3 cm^2/g although the inner disk is optically thick, so masses are lower limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux uncertainty appears to be a flat 10% with no stated derivation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Radial drift versus dust evolution conclusion rests on R_gas/R_dust ~ 2 with no disk modeling
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral index map values depend on robust and on beam matching; no uncertainty map is given
