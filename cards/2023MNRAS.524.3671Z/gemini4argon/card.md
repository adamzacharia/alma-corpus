## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and astrometrically align multi-band archival observations**: Stack HST ACS F814W images with SWARP; calibrate and image ALMA 300 GHz, VLA 8.4 GHz, VLA 33 GHz, and e-Merlin 1.5 GHz visibility data in CASA with tclean; fit peak positions of the four lensed images in HST and ALMA using SAOimageDS9 and shift HST astrometry to match ALMA.  
  > "We stacked all ACS images with SWARP (Bertin et al. 2002)." (2.1 HST F814W Image); "We invert the combined visibility data by task tclean with the ‘mfs’ mode, a gridder of mosaic, a cell size of 0.02'', and a Briggs weighting with a robust of 1.5." (2.2 ALMA 300 GHz continuum); "We fit the peak positions of the four images with SAOimageDS9 (Joye & Mandel 2003) for both the HST data and the ALMA continuum data. From these, we get an average offset of 0.126'' in the right ascension (R.A.) and 0.219'' in the declination (Dec.)." (2.5 Astrometric correction)
   - software: SWARP, CASA, tclean, imfit, imregrid, AIPS, SAOimageDS9
   - parameter: Astrometric offset applied between HST and ALMA: 0.126'' in R.A. and 0.219'' in Dec.  
  > "From these, we get an average offset of 0.126'' in the right ascension (R.A.) and 0.219'' in the declination (Dec.)." (2.5 Astrometric correction)
   - parameter: Uniform restoring beam of 0.25'' x 0.25'' for radio images in spectral index analysis  
  > "In the spectral slope index analysis, we use a uniform restoring beam of 0.25′′ × 0.25′′ for all radio images, to analyse these data with the same reconstructed source plane." (2.4 E-Merlin 1.5 GHz continuum data)
   - assumption: Four lensed images follow Gaussian distributions for size measurements in imfit  
  > "We use imfit to measure the sizes (FWHM) of the four components (see Table 2), by assuming that they follow Gaussian distributions." (3.2.1 The HST optical data)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting with CASA imfit and peak fitting with SAOimageDS9  
  > "We use imfit to measure the sizes (FWHM) of the four components (see Table 2), by assuming that they follow Gaussian distributions." (3.2.1 The HST optical data)
   - validation: Radio coordinate offsets between bands are checked (< 13 mas, smaller than pixel scale); aligned HST and ALMA peaks match within one HST pixel.  
  > "After we corrected the offset, the peak positions of the images obtained from HST and ALMA (see Fig. 1) are still not yet perfectly aligned (though the current offsets are within one pixel of the HST image)" (2.5 Astrometric correction); "The largest offset is lesser than 13 mas, which happens between the VLA 8.4 GHz and 33 GHz data." (2.5 Astrometric correction)
2. **Parametric lens mass and source modelling of HST optical and ALMA 300 GHz continuum images**: Fit the HST image in PyAutoLens with an isothermal ellipsoid plus external shear lens model and a point-source model; use the highest-likelihood result as prior/initial input to fit the ALMA 300 GHz continuum image with a power-law ellipsoid plus external shear lens mass model and an elliptical Sérsic source profile, fixing the lens mass centre to the HST result.  
  > "Fit the HST data using a simple lens model which assumes point-source emission for the source galaxy. This provides an initial estimate for the parametric lens mass model." (3.1 Overview of Lens Modelling Strategy); "Take the highest likelihood results from the previous step as the initial input to fit the ALMA 300 GHz continuum data with another parametric mass model, using an extended source model which follows a smoothly parameterized form of elliptical Sérsic profile." (3.1 Overview of Lens Modelling Strategy); "We adopt a power-law ellipsoid (Tessore & Metcalf 2015) with an external shear as the lens galaxy mass profile and model the source with an elliptical Sérsic profile. We fix the centre of the lens mass model (from the result of the HST data modelling) to reduce the dimensions of parametric space." (3.2.2 The ALMA 300 GHz continuum)
   - software: PyAutoLens, dynesty, pyautofit
   - parameter: Lens plane redshift z = 1.7; source redshift z = 2.56  
  > "In this case, 1'' corresponds to ~8.7 kpc in the lens plane at z = 1.7 and ~8.22 kpc in the source plane at z = 2.56." (1 INTRODUCTION); "In our lens model, the redshift of the lens plane is set as 1.7 (Kneib et al. 1998)." (3.1 Overview of Lens Modelling Strategy)
   - assumption: Cosmology: \Omega_m = 0.310, \Omega_\Lambda = 0.689, H_0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1  
  > "Throughout the paper, we assume a cosmology with \Omega_m = 0.310, \Omega_\Lambda = 0.689, and H_0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 (Planck Collaboration et al. 2020)." (1 INTRODUCTION)
   - assumption: Lens system is a single galaxy and distant cluster at z ~ 1.7 modelled as a power-law ellipsoid with external shear  
  > "We take the assumption from Kneib et al. (1998) that the lens system is contributed by a single galaxy (the narrow absorption systems) and a distant cluster both at z ~ 1.7." (3 LENS MODELLING); "We adopt a power-law ellipsoid (Tessore & Metcalf 2015) with an external shear as the lens galaxy mass profile and model the source with an elliptical Sérsic profile." (3.2.2 The ALMA 300 GHz continuum)
   - fitting: Bayesian inference using nested sampler Dynesty integrated within PyAutoLens  
  > "With the Bayesian inference (Dynesty) integrated within PyAutoLens, we adopt the best-fit results and their associated errors as the lens profile (Table 3), which is further used for the non-parametric modelling of the radio data." (3.3.1 The ALMA 300-GHz continuum)
   - validation: Invert simple point source to lens plane using fitted mass models and verify simulated positions match the four HST images within 0.5 pixel.  
  > "For both parametric and non-parametric models, the positions of the simulated point source (Fig. 1) match well with all four images in the HST data, within 0.5-pixel size. This indicates that our models are reliable." (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum)
3. **Non-parametric source-plane reconstruction of ALMA 300 GHz and radio continuum bands**: Reconstruct the 300 GHz source plane iteratively using a magnification-weighted Voronoi grid followed by a brightness-weighted Voronoi mesh while fitting a new power-law ellipsoid plus shear lens mass model; then fix the lens mass model parameters to the 300 GHz non-parametric fit and reconstruct the 1.5, 8.4, and 33 GHz source planes using a magnification-weighted Voronoi mesh.  
  > "To fit the non-parametric source, we first fix the parameters of the lens object according to our parametric mass model (see Sect. 3.2.2), and then use a pixelized Voronoi grid that adapts to the mass model’s magnification pattern, giving higher resolutions to areas with higher magnification." (3.3.1 The ALMA 300-GHz continuum); "Then, we perform a fit where the source plane is reconstructed by a brightness-weighted Voronoi (Fig. 2 left) mesh. We fit a new mass model, a power-law ellipsoid (Tessore & Metcalf 2015) with an external shear (shown in Table 3)." (3.3.1 The ALMA 300-GHz continuum); "The ALMA 300-GHz continuum image provides strong constraints on our lensing galaxy mass model, therefore we fix the mass model parameters to values derived from this data in order to perform non-parametric fitting at radio bands." (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum)
   - software: PyAutoLens, dynesty, pyautofit, pylops
   - parameter: Magnification-weighted Voronoi mesh for 1.5, 8.4, and 33 GHz reconstructions to ensure identical Voronoi cell grids  
  > "For the data from VLA and e-Merlin, the source plane is reconstructed with a magnification-weighted Voronoi mesh instead of a brightness-weighted one. This is because a magnification-based mesh produces an identical grid of Voronoi cells across all three frequency channels" (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum)
   - assumption: Fixed lens mass model parameters from ALMA 300 GHz non-parametric fit across all radio bands  
  > "The ALMA 300-GHz continuum image provides strong constraints on our lensing galaxy mass model, therefore we fix the mass model parameters to values derived from this data in order to perform non-parametric fitting at radio bands." (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum)
   - fitting: Bayesian inference with Dynesty in PyAutoLens; Gaussian profile fitting to measure FWHMs of source-plane components  
  > "With the Bayesian inference (Dynesty) integrated within PyAutoLens, we adopt the best-fit results and their associated errors as the lens profile (Table 3), which is further used for the non-parametric modelling of the radio data." (3.3.1 The ALMA 300-GHz continuum); "The FWHMs of this radio clump, fitted with Gaussian profiles" (4.2 The discovery of a radio jet)
   - validation: Inspect residual maps (Appendix B), perform mock Sérsic source lensing simulations across caustics (Appendix A), and Monte Carlo sample lens model parameter probability distributions (~5% error contribution).  
  > "The residual maps at multiple wavelengths are presented in Appendix B." (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum); "The error compromises a 15% absolute flux uncertainty, a ~ 5% error from the uncertainty of the model by running multiple models with all parameters randomly sampled with their probability density distributions, and a statistical error measured in emission-free regions." (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum)
4. **Extract multi-band radio fluxes and fit spectral indices in the lens and source planes**: Extract radio fluxes at 1.5, 8.4, and 33 GHz from elliptical apertures in the source plane and lens plane; fit single power-law SEDs and pixel-by-pixel spectral index maps in the source plane and from nterm=2 e-Merlin imaging in the lens plane.  
  > "We first measure radio fluxes from regions shown in the lens plane (Fig. 1) and the source plane (Fig. 2) for 1.5, 8.4, and 33 GHz, respectively." (4.3 Spectral indices of the radio continuum); "Then we fit spectral indices with fluxes from all three radio bands, which are shown in orange lines of Fig. 3. All SEDs can be fitted with one single power-law profile." (4.3 Spectral indices of the radio continuum); "As shown in Fig. 5, we fit power-law profiles for all pixels at maps of 8.4 GHz, 1.5 GHz, and 33 GHz, with a mask of S/N > 3.5 at the 33 GHz source plane image." (4.3 Spectral indices of the radio continuum)
   - software: CASA, python, scipy, numpy
   - parameter: 5-\sigma cutoff mask for e-Merlin lens-plane spectral index map; S/N > 3.5 mask at 33 GHz for source-plane spectral index map  
  > "We use a 5-\sigma mask from the e-Merlin continuum to mask the spectral index map and the error map." (4.3 Spectral indices of the radio continuum); "As shown in Fig. 5, we fit power-law profiles for all pixels at maps of 8.4 GHz, 1.5 GHz, and 33 GHz, with a mask of S/N > 3.5 at the 33 GHz source plane image." (4.3 Spectral indices of the radio continuum)

## Qualitative findings

- A well-separated, kpc-scaled, single-sided radio jet is located at a projected distance of ~1.15-1.2 kpc to the northwest of the Cloverleaf host galaxy in the reconstructed source plane.  
  > "With both parametric and non-parametric lens models and with reconstructed images in the source plane, we find a well-separated, kpc-scaled, single-sided radio jet located at projected ~1.2 kpc to the northwest of the host galaxy in the source plane." (ABSTRACT); "All reconstructed radio images show extended emission to the northwest of the dust continuum core, at a projected distance of ~1.15 kpc (0.14'') away from the centre of the host galaxy." (4.2 The discovery of a radio jet)
- The radio jet exhibits a steep power-law spectral index (~1.11) dominated by synchrotron emission, whereas the host galaxy exhibits a flatter spectral index (~0.70-0.80) likely combining free-free, dust continuum, and supernova-remnant synchrotron emission.  
  > "As shown in Fig. 3 and Table 5, the SEDs of the radio jet in both lens plane and source plane exhibit power-law profiles, and have similar spectral indices, which indicates that the emission is dominated by the synchrotron from electrons in the jet material." (5.4 The Power-Law Profiles of the SEDs); "On the position of the host galaxy, the SED can be also fitted with one single power-law profile with a flatter spectral index (see Fig. 5 and Table 5) compared with the one at the position of the radio jet." (5.4 The Power-Law Profiles of the SEDs)
- The co-existence of broad absorption line winds and the newly discovered radio jet suggests AGN feedback in the Cloverleaf quasar operates via both radiation-driven wind and kinetic jet modes.  
  > "together with the broad absorption line and the newly discovered radio jet, we suggest that the AGN feedback in Cloverleaf consists of coexistent contributions from both radiation-driven wind and AGN-launched jet." (5.5 AGN Feedback of Cloverleaf)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Optical (HST) traces point-like QSO emission, sub-millimetre (ALMA 300 GHz) resolves the extended dusty host galaxy disk free of microlensing to tightly constrain the lens mass model, and multi-band radio (e-Merlin and VLA) traces synchrotron emission from relativistic particles in the jet.  
  > "This work focuses on observational data at three wavelengths: (i) optical, which is dominated by the QSO emission and is seen as almost four point sources from HST images (Kneib et al. 1998); (ii) Sub-millimetre, which traces the gaseous dusty disk of the host galaxy" (1 INTRODUCTION); "The extended lensed arc at the submillimeter wavelength is not subject to the micro-lensing effect and its entire brightness distribution can be used to improve the precision of the lens model." (3.2.1 The HST optical data)
- Why this method: Non-parametric Voronoi mesh modelling adapts resolution to source morphology and magnification, overcoming parametric limitations for irregular sources; using a magnification-weighted mesh with a fixed ALMA-derived mass model produces identical Voronoi cells across radio bands for pixel-by-pixel spectral index mapping.  
  > "The traditional method (e.g. Kneib et al. 1998), which is based on parametric modelling, has limitations in the cases of irregular sources. The newly developed non-parametric modelling, which is required for high angular resolution and high sensitivity analysis, greatly improves the reconstructed source plane" (1 INTRODUCTION); "This is because a magnification-based mesh produces an identical grid of Voronoi cells across all three frequency channels, ensuring consistent sampling of the source when calculating the spectral indices pixel by pixel to obtain a spectral indices map." (3.3.2 The 1.5-, 8.4-, and 33-GHz continuum)
- Alternative: Rejected the possibility that the radio jet feature originates from the foreground lens galaxy rather than the background lensed quasar, because multiple components appear across all radio bands and their merging morphology between components B and D matches a background source lying on the inner caustics.  
  > "One may also ask if the radio feature comes from the foreground lens galaxy, instead of the background source galaxy. First, the multiple components shown in all radio bands indicate that it is unlikely to be multiple jets from the centre." (5.2 Possibility to be a jet in the foreground?); "Second, the spatial distributions of all radio components are highly consistent with the scenario that the radio structure locates on the caustics, i.e. merging between lensed components (Appendix A)." (5.2 Possibility to be a jet in the foreground?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Three of the 18 VLA 33 GHz execution blocks taken before 22 Oct. 2013 were corrupted and unusable due to incomplete execution.  
  > "However, three of them, taken before 22 Oct. 2013, were corrupted and not usable due to incomplete execution." (2.3.2 VLA 33 GHz continuum)
- Limitation (authors): The four optical point-source images may suffer from microlensing, which is not included in the strong lens modelling, and provide limited constraints on the lens model.  
  > "The four optical images of Cloverleaf point sources may suffer from micro-lensing (Kayser et al. 1990), which may lead to a biased result since the micro-lensing effect is not included in the strong lens modelling." (3.2.1 The HST optical data)
- Limitation (authors): Several reconstructed 300 GHz emission clumps outside the caustics have lower fidelity due to lens model uncertainty.  
  > "Due to the uncertainty of the lens model, several emission clumps outside the caustics have less fidelity." (4.2 The discovery of a radio jet)
- Limitation (authors): A counter-jet on the opposite side of the host galaxy may be too faint to detect with current sensitivity if it lies away from the caustics.  
  > "Depending on the geometry, the other side of the radio jet might be too faint to be detected due to being away from the caustics, or simply does not exist." (5.3 Why One-sided?)
- Limitation (authors): A numerical plotting issue caused the inner critical curve in Figures A1 and B1 to be drawn as an ellipse rather than a point.  
  > "The solid black line and the red solid line are the outer and the inner critical curve (which is incorrect due to the numerical issue and should be a point) in the lens plane respectively." (Figure A1 caption)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Astrometric alignment relies on shifting the HST image to match the ALMA continuum peaks, assuming the optical AGN and sub-mm dust continuum peaks are spatially coincident, which may introduce small systematic offsets in the fixed lens centre.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Lens modelling appears to be performed on cleaned images with correlated noise rather than directly in the interferometric uv-plane.
