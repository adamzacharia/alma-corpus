## Qualitative findings

- First detection of the rotational emission lines of t-HC(O)SH towards the hot corino IRAS 16293 B, arising from its high-density, warm inner region.  
  > "In this article, we present the first detection of the SH-bearing molecule t-HC(O)SH towards the hot corino object IRAS 16293 B." (Conclusion); "The integrated emission maps indicate that the t-HC(O)SH emission lines originate from the high-density, warm inner region of IRAS 16293 B." (Spatial distribution of t-HC(O)SH)
- The fractional abundance of t-HC(O)SH towards IRAS 16293 B is lower than that towards G+0.693–0.027 and G31.41+0.31, indicating lower chemical richness of SH-bearing molecules towards IRAS 16293 B.  
  > "Our estimated abundance of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B is 8.50$\times$10$^{-11}$, which is several times of magnitude lower than the abundance of t-HC(O)SH towards the G+0.693–0.027 and G31.41+0.31." (Fractional abundance of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B); "That comparison also indicates that the chemical richness of SH-bearing molecules towards the IRAS 16293 B is less than G+0.693–0.027 and G31.41+0.31." (Fractional abundance of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B)
- Other searched SH-bearing molecules (including c-HC(O)SH, vinyl mercaptan, ethyl mercaptan, dithioformic acid, ethynethiol, and metal hydrosulfides) were not detected towards IRAS 16293 B.  
  > "We did not find any evidence of those SH-bearing molecules towards the IRAS 16293 B within the limit of LTE analysis." (Searching of other SH-bearing molecules towards the IRAS 16293 B)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: IRAS 16293 B is chemically rich in complex organic molecules and has narrow line widths (1.0–1.5 km s^-1) compared to IRAS 16293 A (>=3.0 km s^-1), reducing line blending and enabling the identification of complex species.  
  > "We extract the millimeter-wavelength molecular spectra from IRAS 16293 B because IRAS 16293 B is highly chemically rich compared to IRAS 16293 A." (Identification of emission lines of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B); "The spectral line width of IRAS 16293 B is $\sim$1.0–1.5 km s$^{-1}$ but the spectral line width of IRAS 16293 A is $\geq$3.0 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: jo16)." (Identification of emission lines of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B)
- Why this method: LTE assumptions are valid in the inner region of IRAS 16293 B because the gas density of the warm inner region of the low-mass protostellar envelope is high (>=10^10 cm^-2 [sic]).  
  > "The LTE assumptions are reasonable in the inner region of IRAS 16293 B because the gas density of the warm inner region of the low-mass protostellar envelopes is $\geq$10$^{10}$ cm$^{-2}$ (cite: jo16; cou18)." (Identification of emission lines of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B)
- Alternative: Searching for complex molecular species towards IRAS 16293 A was rejected because its broad spectral lines (>=3.0 km s^-1) result in severe line blending.  
  > "So, the searching of complex molecular species from the broad spectral lines towards the IRAS 16293 A is difficult, and there are high chances of detecting the blended spectral lines rather than the non-blended spectral lines (cite: jo12; jo16)." (Identification of emission lines of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The detected t-HC(O)SH transition lines are not spatially resolved or are only marginally resolved, making it impossible to determine the spatial distribution morphology without higher angular resolution observations.  
  > "This demonstrates that the detected t-HC(O)SH transition lines are not spatially resolved or are only marginally resolved towards the IRAS 16293 B. As a result, determining the morphology of the spatial distribution of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B is impossible." (Spatial distribution of t-HC(O)SH)
- Limitation (authors): No transitions of c-HC(O)SH are present in the observed frequency ranges, so its upper limit had to be estimated with respect to c-HC(S)SH.  
  > "We can not perform the search of emission lines of c-HC(O)SH (cis isomer monothioformic acid) because we can not find any transitions of c-HC(O)SH in this data set. So we estimate the upper limit column density of c-HC(O)SH with respect to c-HC(S)SH." (Searching of other SH-bearing molecules towards the IRAS 16293 B)
- Limitation (authors): High upper-state energy transitions (E_u = 505.06 K, 572.03 K, 644.28 K) are below the noise level (< 1.5 sigma) and blended, and several other transitions are blended with other molecules.  
  > "We also found that the LTE spectra of J = 20(12,8)–19(12,7), J = 20(13,7)–19(13,6), and J = 20(14,6)–19(14,5) transition lines of t-HC(O)SH at frequencies 234.085 GHz, 234.121 GHz, and 234.161 GHz did not generate properly (below 1.5 $\sigma$) due to high upper state energies ($E_{u}$) of 505.06 K, 572.03 K, and 644.28 K." (Identification of emission lines of t-HC(O)SH towards the IRAS 16293 B)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The detection relies on only four non-blended features (two of which at 234.074 and 234.075 GHz are blended with each other into a single spectral feature, leaving only three distinct unblended emission peaks) in a line-rich hot corino spectrum.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The excitation temperature (125 +/- 15 K) is poorly constrained empirically because the unblended detected transitions span a very narrow range of upper-state energies (185.30 K to 249.91 K).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The extraction aperture (2.07'' diameter) is much larger than the assumed source size (0.5'') and the beam (~0.5'' x 0.4''), which dilutes the line emission relative to a single-beam extraction at the offset position.
