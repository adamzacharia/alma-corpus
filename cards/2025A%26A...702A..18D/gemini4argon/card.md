## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate, align, and image multi-frequency ALMA visibility data**: Perform automatic user-independent phase and amplitude self-calibration with snow (using gpuvmem and CASA gaincal/applycal), align visibility datasets in position and flux scale using VisAlign, concatenate array configurations (except Band 7 TM2, and uv-clipping Band 9 TM1 < 0.4 Mlambda), and restore continuum images.  
  > "For each band and array configuration, automatic (user-independent) self-calibration was performed using the `snow` package." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment); "Alignment was performed using the `VisAlign` package (cite: CasassusCarcamo2022MNRAS.513.5790C; Casassus2023MNRAS.526.1545C), selecting the datasets with the largest baselines as an astrometric reference (i.e. TM1), and a final round of self-calibration with `snow` was applied to the concatenated dataset." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
   - software: snow, CASA, gpuvmem, VisAlign
   - parameter: Briggs robustness parameter r = 2 during self-calibration; final images restored with r = 1.0 (Band 3), r = 1.0 (Band 4), r = 0.0 (Band 7), r = 2.0 (Band 9)  
  > "Image restoration was performed with a Briggs robustness parameter of $r = 2$." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment); "$r$ $^{a}$ | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.0 | 2.0" (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
   - parameter: Band 9 TM1 uvrange clipped to discard baselines shorter than 0.4 Mlambda; TM1 intensity scaled by 1/0.78 and TM2 shifted by dRA = 25.6 mas, dDec = 0.13 mas  
  > "We opted to discard baselines shorter than 0.4 M$\lambda$." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment); "An application of `VisAlign` to align TM2 to TM1 results in a shift of $\Delta \alpha$ = 25.6 mas along the right ascension, and $\Delta \delta= 0.13$ mas along the declination, with a correction in the intensity scale of 78%." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment); "We applied the correction factor of 1/78% to TM1." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
   - assumption: Point source at Band 3 phase centre coincides with GAIA ICRS position of PDS 70 and is due to accretion free-free emission on the central star  
  > "As is shown in Fig. 1a, a bright point source is detected at the phase centre, which is coincident with the position of PDS 70 given by the GAIA ICRS co-ordinates (cite: Gaia2023A&A...674A...1G)." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment); "We therefore assume that this point source is due to accretion free-free emission on the central star, following (cite: Doi2024ApJ...974L..25D)." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
   - fitting: Maximum entropy deconvolution via gpuvmem and iterative gaincal/applycal keeping solution table with highest peak S/N  
  > "The `gaincal` solution interval was reduced by half in each iteration, starting with the scan length, and stopping when the peak signal-to-noise ratio (PS/N) decreases. The solution table with the highest PS/N was kept." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)

## Qualitative findings

- PDS 70c is detected in Band 4 (5.2 sigma) and Band 7 (9 sigma), tentatively detected in Band 3 (2.6 sigma), and not detected in Band 9, while PDS 70b and PDS 70d are not detected at any frequency.  
  > "New detections in Bands 3 (tentative at $2.6\,\sigma$), 4 ($5\,\sigma$), and 7 (re-detected at $9\,\sigma$) are consistent with optically thick thermal emission from PDS 70c (spectral index $\alpha = 2\pm0.2$)." (abstract); "No counterparts were detected for PDS 70b or d, at any frequency." (Point source measurements of PDS 70c)
- The Band 7 flux density of PDS 70c is constant within ~10% across seven epochs between May and November 2023.  
  > "Therefore the flux density of PDS 70c is constant across all seven epochs within $\sim$10%." (Variability study)
- The CPD of PDS 70c is strongly depleted of sub-millimetre-emitting dust (dust-to-gas ratio < 10^-4.80, depleted by a factor of at least 1000).  
  > "If so, PDS 70c is depleted of sub-millimetre-emitting dust by a factor of at least 1000." (abstract); "Given an optically thick free-free spectrum, any steeper component is negligible, and the dust-to-gas mass ratio $\zeta$ must be very low, $\log(\zeta)<-4.80$." (Conclusion)
- H I free-free emission from an accretion shock at the surface of the CPD is the only viable mechanism that reproduces both the optically thick radio SED up to Band 7 and the optically thin turnover in Band 9.  
  > "However, the turnover can only be accounted for by H i free-free from an accretion shock at the surface of a CPD." (abstract); "The CPD surface shock model is the closest one to the uniform slab requirements, and is therefore the only viable interpretation of the observed SED, including the Band 9 drop." (Conclusion)

(2018) for pre-shock velocities of 30 and 50 km/s. | R: A 2 R_Jup emitting region requires log(T/K) = 6.83 (+0.04/-0.10), which is unphysical for planetary shocks; a 7000–10^4 K slab requires R_max ~ 52–62 R_Jup (~0.025–0.078 au) and EM ~ 10^11–10^12 cm^-6 pc, and CPD surface shock simulations produce tau > 1 at 300 GHz turning over to tau ~ a few times 0.1 at 600 GHz. | F: Photospheric shocks and accretion funnels are ruled out, whereas H I free-free emission from a thin shocked layer at the CPD surface reproduces both the optically thick lower-frequency SED and the Band 9 turnover. | **supported**

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-frequency ALMA observations across Bands 3, 4, 7, and 9 acquired within ~two months avoid variability bias and span the frequency range needed to distinguish thermal dust (alpha ~ 3) from free-free emission (alpha <= 2) and test predictions of bright Band 9 CPD dust emission.  
  > "The global hydrodynamic simulations of embedded thermal-mass bodies by (cite: Szulagyi2018MNRAS.473.3573S), as well as the parametric viscous disc models by (cite: Zhu2018MNRAS.479.1850Z), suggest that higher frequencies, such as those in ALMA Bands 9 and 10 ($\sim$671 GHz and $\sim$850 GHz), are more suitable for CPD observations in the dust continuum." (Introduction); "Measurements involving Bands 3, 4, 7, and 9 in 2023 are spread over two months (except for the November 2023 execution blocks in Band 7 and the December 2023 TM2 execution block in Band 9), and are the least biased by variability compared to the 2017 and 2019 data." (Point source measurements of PDS 70c)
- Why this method: Forced photometry incorporates prior knowledge of PDS 70c's orbit without the bias of arbitrary upper limits; uv-plane subtraction of the circumstellar ring separates PDS 70c in coarse natural-weight single-epoch Band 7 beams; single-dominant-element Saha approximation makes metal and H- ionisation computationally tractable for nested sampling.  
  > "In turn, incorporating these non-detections as 2 or 3 $\sigma$ upper limits injects a bias in attempts to fit this SED, by setting the expectation value for the flux densities to zero (or any other arbitrary value)." (Point source measurements of PDS 70c); "However, the natural weight beams, of $\sim$0.12$''$, are too coarse to separate PDS 70c from the bright ring. We therefore proceeded to subtract the disc emission from the visibility data." (Variability study); "This allows for an approximation of the real number density of electrons without requiring an excessively long computing time." (Magnetic disc)
- Alternative: Concatenating Band 7 TM1 and TM2 configurations was rejected to preserve angular resolution  
  > "We avoided concatenation with TM2 to preserve angular resolution." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
- Alternative: Amplitude and phase self-calibration for the final Band 7 TM1 image was rejected because amplitude gains reduced the flux density of PDS 70 by 20% compared to TM2 and phase-only self-calibrated TM1  
  > "However, we selected the phase-only self-calibration for our final dataset, as the amplitude gains result in a drop in flux density for PDS 70 by 20%, compared to both TM2 and the phase self-calibrated TM1." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
- Alternative: Using 2 or 3 sigma upper limits for the Band 3 and Band 9 non-detections was rejected in favour of forced photometry to avoid biasing SED fits toward zero  
  > "In turn, incorporating these non-detections as 2 or 3 $\sigma$ upper limits injects a bias in attempts to fit this SED, by setting the expectation value for the flux densities to zero (or any other arbitrary value)." (Point source measurements of PDS 70c)
- Alternative: Single-parameter (gamma) optically thin approximation for the dusty CPD model was rejected after finding that the bulk of the flux originates from the outer radius where T_ext ~ T_floor cannot be neglected  
  > "This indicates that the SED is highly dependent on the floor temperature, and our approximation using the single parameter, $\gamma$, was invalid ($T_{\rm ext}$ cannot be neglected)." (Fitting the data)
- Alternative: Dusty CPD with a low floor temperature (T_floor < 15 K) was rejected because Band 9 brightness temperatures and CO observations require T >= 21–30 K at 34 au  
  > "Band 9 shows a lower temperature limit of 21 K at 34 au, and the presence of CO in the PDS 70 cavity suggests temperatures of at least 30 K (cite: Law2024ApJ...964..190L). Therefore, we conclude that it is not possible to fit the observed SED using only a dusty disc model with the current derivations of $T_{\rm floor}$." (Which models fit the data)
- Alternative: Photospheric shock or accretion funnels (R_max ~ 2 R_Jup) as the radio source were rejected because they require T ~ 10^6.83 K, which requires an object > 200 M_Jup and quenches H-alpha emission  
  > "Therefore a photospheric shock, or free-free emission from the accretion funnels, cannot be the source of the radio signal as such shocks would require excessively high temperatures, given the free-fall velocities in the planetary regime." (Surface shock on the CPD)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration iterations were stopped when peak S/N decreased; point-source injection tests in the uv plane confirmed that an optically thick extrapolation from Band 7 would have been detectable in Band 9.  
  > "The `gaincal` solution interval was reduced by half in each iteration, starting with the scan length, and stopping when the peak signal-to-noise ratio (PS/N) decreases." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment); "As the absence of PDS 70c in Band 9 is particularly surprising, we performed point-source injections tests in the $uv$ plane, using optically thick spectra tied to Band 7. As is shown in Fig. 2, such point sources should indeed be detectable, at least in the form of a protrusion of the cavity wall." (Point source measurements of PDS 70c)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar spectral index between Band 3 and Band 4 (-0.7 +/- 0.8) is too noisy to distinguish partially thick free-free emission, variability, or a synchrotron component.  
  > "Comparing $24\pm5\,\mu$Jy in Band 3 with $18\pm4\,\mu$Jy in Band 4 corresponds to a spectral index of $\alpha = -0.7\pm0.8$, which is too noisy to distinguish partially thick free-free emission (cite: WrightBarlow1975MNRAS.170...41W), variability, or a synchrotron component." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
- Limitation (authors): Inter-band alignment with VisAlign could not be applied to Bands 7 and 9 because the central star is not detected and the ring morphology differs dramatically from Band 3, requiring reliance on absolute pointing accuracy.  
  > "There is no counterpart of the star in Bands 7 or 9 with which to cross-check the inter-band alignment, and the ring appears dramatically different in radius and width compared to Band 3, hampering the application of `VisAlign`. We simply rely on the absolute pointing accuracy, of $\sim$10 mas in Band 7 and $\sim$5 mas in Band 9." (Imaging, self-calibration and alignment)
- Limitation (authors): Diffuse emission near the cavity edge in Band 9 was not subtracted from the forced-photometry measurement, and PDS 70c's flux in Band 7 is affected by confusion with the cavity wall.  
  > "However its flux density is difficult to measure because of confusion with the cavity wall." (Point source measurements of PDS 70c); "We did not attempt to correct for the diffuse emission near the edge of the cavity, which would lower the flux density reported in Table" (Point source measurements of PDS 70c)
- Limitation (authors): The jet flux formula was developed for stellar jets and is extrapolated into the planetary mass regime.  
  > "It should be borne in mind that these formulae were developed for stellar jets, rather than protoplanets, and that they are being extrapolated into the planetary mass regime." (PDS 70c SED models)
- Limitation (authors): Magnetic disc viscosity estimates neglect external ionisation sources (interstellar UV and cosmic rays) at large radii.  
  > "However, these viscosity estimates neglect other sources of ionisation (i.e. interstellar UV and cosmic rays), and $\alpha$ values can be unrealistically low at large radii." (Magnetic disc)
- Limitation (authors): The atomic plasma model depends heavily on extrapolating H- free-free opacities from Gray (2022) (valid up to 10 um and T = 2500–10000 K) to millimetre wavelengths and cooler temperatures (T ~ 1000–2000 K).  
  > "(cite: Gray2022oasp.book.....G) was inspired by works that calculated opacities of up to $10\,\mu$m and in a range of temperatures from 2500 to 10000 K. This is very important here, since we are calculating the opacity at millimetre wavelengths, and in a cold disc where the majority of the flux comes from temperatures of $T\approx1000$–2000 K." (Fitting the data)
- Limitation (authors): Achieving a pre-shock free-fall velocity > 30 km/s at 62 R_Jup requires a minimum planetary mass of 15.7 M_Jup (above the 2 sigma dynamical upper limit of 13.6 M_Jup) and spatially constant mass flux models yield very high mass influx rates, as radially varying v_0 and mass flux were not modelled.  
  > "A minimum planetary mass of 15.7 $M_{\rm Jup}$ is required, which is 2.1 $M_{\rm Jup}$ above the $2\sigma$ upper limit of 13.6 $M_{\rm Jup}$ estimated for the dynamical mass of PDS 70c (cite: Trevascus2025A&A...698A..19T). This minimum planetary mass could be reduced by considering the increasing $\varv_0$ at smaller radii, and its resulting radio flux, but this is beyond the scope of this work." (Surface shock on the CPD)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Band 9 turnover relies on a 2.6 sigma discrepancy from an optically thick extrapolation in a dataset where TM1 suffered weather-related low-spatial-frequency phase noise (requiring uv-clipping < 0.4 Mlambda and a 1/0.78 cross-calibration scaling with TM2).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Band 7 flux density of PDS 70c differs between the restored map peak (111 +/- 14 uJy) and the deconvolved gpuvmem Gaussian fit (157 +/- 27 uJy) due to confusion with the cavity wall.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The favoured CPD surface shock scenario with spatially uniform pre-shock velocity requires a planetary mass (15.7 M_Jup) exceeding dynamical upper limits unless radially dependent infall velocities and concentrated mass loading are invoked.
