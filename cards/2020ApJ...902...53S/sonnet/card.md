## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA CO(1-0) and combine arrays**: Multiscale CLEAN of 12-m and 7-m data separately with uvtaper on 12-m, then feather with TP  
  > "We finally combined the cleaned data of 12- and 7-m array datasets and calibrated the TP array data by using the *feather* task." (3.1 CO)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: scales 0'', 3.18'', 9.54'' (12-m); 0'', 14.64'', 43.92'' (7-m)  
  > "The scale parameters are $0''$, 3.18$''$, and 9.54$''$ for the 12-m array and $0''$, 14.64$''$, and 43.92$''$ for the 7-m array." (3.1 CO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive cloud masses and sizes**: Sum N(H2)=A*W(CO) over pixels; size is effective diameter from half-maximum integrated-intensity contour; spectra fit with single or double Gaussians  
  > "The size of each molecular cloud is defined as an effective diameter, determined by the contour of the half level of maximum integrated intensity." (4.3 Detailed CO Distribution with ALMA)
   - assumption: A = 7.0e20 cm^-2 (K km/s)^-1, mu = 2.72, D = 50 kpc  
  > "where $m_\mathrm{p}$ is the mass of atomic hydrogen, $\mu = 2.72$ is the mean molecular weight" (4.3 Detailed CO Distribution with ALMA)
   - fitting: single or double Gaussian fits to CO(1-0) spectra  
  > "Cols. (2–9): Observed physical properties of the clouds obtained by single or double Gaussian fitting with $^{12}$CO($J$ = 1–0) emission line." (Table 2)
   - validation: ~30% relative error from conversion factor and distance uncertainties  
  > "have $\sim$30 % relative errors due to uncertainties in the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor and distance to the LMC" (4.3 Detailed CO Distribution with ALMA)
3. **CO 3-2/1-0 ratio map**: Intensity ratio of ASTE 3-2 to ALMA 1-0  
  > "Figure [6](#fig6) shows an intensity ratio map of $^{12}$CO($J$ = 3–2) / $^{12}$CO($J$ = 1–0) (hereafter $R_{3\text{--}2/1\text{--}0}$) using ASTE and ALMA" (4.4 CO 3–2 / 1–0 Ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: compared with quiescent LMC value ~0.4 in GMC-south  
  > "of N132D GMC-south, south of the SNR, is $\sim$0.4" (4.4 CO 3–2 / 1–0 Ratio)
4. **X-ray spectral fits of cloud F region vs reference region**: Fit unbinned Chandra ACIS-S3 spectra 0.3-10 keV with TBabs*TBvarabs*(vpshock+vnei+vnei), C statistic, with explicit background model  
  > "we fitted each source spectrum with a plane-parallel shock model (cite: 2001ApJ...548..820B) plus two NEI components (`vnei` $+$ `vnei`)." (4.6 X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - software: XSPEC, HEASOFT, CIAO
   - parameter: vpshock fixed at kTe=0.86 keV, net=1.94e11 cm^-3 s; normalization free  
  > "We fix these parameters at these values in our fits and only allow the normalization to vary." (4.6 X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - assumption: MW NH 1.47e21 fixed; LMC ISM He = 0.9 Zsun and other elements 0.5 Zsun (Wilms scale)  
  > "we set the elemental abundance for the ISM in the LMC with He = 0.9 $Z_\odot$ and the other elements = 0.5 $Z_\odot$ on the (cite: Wilms2000) scale." (4.6 X-ray Spectral Analysis)
   - fitting: C statistic minimization, Pearson chi2 for goodness of fit, 1 sigma errors  
  > "We use the C statistic (cite: Cash1979) as the minimization statistic" (3.3 X-rays)
   - validation: Pearson reduced chi2 0.99 (center), 1.12 (southwest)  
  > "the C statistic is 1350 with 1302 degrees of freedom (DOF) and the Pearson reduced $\chi^2$ is 0.99" (4.6 X-ray Spectral Analysis)
5. **Estimate cosmic-ray proton energy**: W_p ~ 1e52 (n_H/1 cm^-3)^-1 erg using cloud densities; alternative HI wind-shell case using N(HI)=1.823e18 W(HI)  
  > "Adopting proton densities of molecular clouds of 260–1980 cm$^{-3}$ (see Table [\[tab:mc\]](#tab:mc)), we then obtain $W_\mathrm{p} \sim$0.5–$3.8 \times 10^{49}$ erg." (Discussion: Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)
   - parameter: HI shell diameter ~25 pc, thickness ~5 pc, W(HI) ~500 K km/s  
  > "We here assume that the diameter and thickness of the H i wind shell are $\sim$25 pc and $\sim$5 pc, respectively." (5.2 Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)
   - assumption: Hadronic origin of gamma rays; clouds inside wind bubble are the targets  
  > "Assuming that this hypothesis is correct, we derive the total energy of accelerated cosmic-ray protons" (5.2 Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: compared with Galactic gamma-ray SNRs  
  > "This is comparable to the values obtained for the Galactic gamma-ray SNRs" (5.2 Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)

## Qualitative findings

- Diffuse CO inside the X-ray shell resolved into nine clouds; no apparent trend between cloud and O-rich ejecta distributions  
  > "We find no apparent trend between the spatial distributions of the molecular clouds and O-rich ejecta." (4.5 Comparison with O-rich Ejecta)
- Hard X-rays concentrate inside the SNR where clouds B–G lie  
  > "We note that the hard X-ray emission are concentrated inside the SNR, where the molecular clouds B–G are located." (4.7 Comparison with hard X-ray emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA CO needed to resolve clouds illuminated by shocks and CR protons  
  > "The high angular resolution of 5$''$ in the ALMA CO data will allow us to resolve molecular clouds illuminated by shock waves and cosmic-ray protons in N132D." (Overview of the Magellanic SNR N132D)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Alternative HI wind-shell target case considered, giving W_p ~3e50 erg  
  > "We also discuss an alternative case that the shock front of N132D has reached the cavity wall of the wind-blown bubble." (5.2 Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Detailed spatial comparison of CO and gamma rays not possible due to H.E.S.S. resolution  
  > "Although a detailed spatial comparison between the CO data and the gamma-ray emission could not be carried out" (5.2 Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)
- Limitation (authors): HI integrated intensity toward SNR uncertain due to radio continuum absorption  
  > "Since $W$(H i) toward N132D has large uncertainty due to the radio continuum absorption" (5.2 Is N132D the Energetic Accelerator of Cosmic-Ray Protons?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cloud F near-side argument rests on a single pair of X-ray regions and a fixed forward-shock model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO-to-H2 factor of 7e20 is assumed with no independent check for these small clouds
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gamma-ray hadronic origin is assumed; leptonic contribution is not excluded
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 3-2/1-0 ratio interpretation relies on different beams (23'' vs 5'') and the regridding is not described
