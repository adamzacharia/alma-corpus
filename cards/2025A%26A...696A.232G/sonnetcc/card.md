## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and prepare continuum data**: ALMA pipeline calibration, then separate line and continuum SPWs and average continuum channels  
  > "All datasets were calibrated using the pipeline and scripts provided by the ALMA staff." (2 Observations); "We separated the line and continuum emission spectral windows for each source and averaged the channels in the continuum spectral windows." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: CASA 6.5.4.9  
  > "Version 6.5.4.9 of the Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA; (cite: 2007ASPC..376..127M)) was utilized to analyze and process the data" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-calibration attempted but not achievable for the new data  
  > "Self-calibration was attempted on the new datasets, but due to the limited observing time per source and the low signal-to-noise ratios (SNR), it was not possible to achieve satisfactory results." (2 Observations)
2. **Create continuum images**: tclean with MTMFS deconvolver and multiscale, weighting chosen per source  
  > "We used the TCLEAN task in CASA with the MTMFS deconvolver (cite: 2011A&A...532A..71R), employing scales of 0, 1, 3, and 5 times the beam size to create the final images of each disk." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA tclean
   - parameter: Weighting selected per source for highest resolution with sufficient sensitivity  
  > "For each source, we selected the weighting that offered the highest resolution while maintaining sufficient sensitivity to capture and distinguish the entire disk structure effectively." (2 Observations)
   - parameter: Pixel size 0.001-0.003 arcsec  
  > "For all sources, pixel sizes between 0.001 and 0.003 arcseconds were used to ensure the pixel size was approximately ten times smaller than the beam size for all images." (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure total continuum flux in the image plane**: Aperture photometry with aperture extended until the flux flattens, inclination corrections applied; uncertainty from standard deviation inside each aperture  
  > "The total flux for all targets is determined through aperture photometry, where the aperture is extended until the flux flattens, and inclination corrections are applied." (3.1 Dust continuum images); "The uncertainty is calculated as the standard deviation inside each aperture." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Fluxes compared with low-resolution values of Ansdell et al. 2018 and found within ~10% for most  
  > "Most flux measurements fall within an 10$\%$ uncertainty range compared to those reported at low resolution by (cite: 2018ApJ...859...21A)." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
4. **Fit visibilities with axisymmetric brightness models**: GALARIO visibility modelling with Gaussian, asymmetric-ring, Gaussian+ring or binary (summed two-Gaussian) brightness profiles, plus a delta-like Gaussian for V856ScoA, choosing the simplest structure seen in the image  
  > "we used the GALARIO code (cite: 2018MNRAS.476.4527T) to model the visibilities of the observations with the best fit possible." (3.2 Visibility modeling); "we employ three different models for the isolated disks choosing the most simple structure we observed for each disk in the continuum images" (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - software: GALARIO, emcee, CASA UVMODELFIT
   - parameter: 48 to 120 walkers, 2,000 to 30,000 steps; first 1,000 steps discarded as burn-in; binaries needed more walkers and iterations  
  > "we employed between 48 and 120 walkers, running the chains for 2,000 to 30,000 steps to ensure convergence" (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - parameter: Initial parameters from UVMODELFIT for single disks  
  > "For all the single disks, we used UVMODELFIT within CASA to obtain the initial parameter estimates." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - parameter: Final values are posterior medians with 16th and 84th percentile uncertainties  
  > "The final model parameters are represented by the median of the posterior probability distributions for each parameter, while the uncertainties are given by the 16th and 84th percentiles." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - parameter: Inclination and PA fixed to face-on / P.A. fixed to 0 for unresolved or poorly constrained disks  
  > "the inclination and P.A. were fixed to face-on values" (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric brightness profile; features not visible in the continuum images were not fitted  
  > "We did not fit any features that were not already visible in the continuum images." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC with emcee ensemble sampler; Gaussian likelihood; priors not stated  
  > "employing the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) ensemble sampler provided by EMCEE (cite: 2013PASP..125..306F)" (3.2 Visibility modeling); "Posterior distributions are obtained by assuming a Gaussian likelihood." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
   - validation: Convergence checked after burn-in by examining corner plots; visibility plots in appendix  
  > "To verify convergence, we discarded the first 1,000 steps (burn-in phase) and examined the corner plots." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
5. **Derive total flux, dust radii and the substructure census**: Total flux and R68/R90 measured from the modelled images; census of cavities, gaps and rings combining image and visibility analysis  
  > "Subsequently, we derived the total flux and dust disk radius from the modeled images." (3.2 Visibility modeling); "We define disk sizes as the radius where the cumulative flux equals 68% of the total flux" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
   - software: GALARIO
   - parameter: R68 definition (68% of total flux)  
  > "We define disk sizes as the radius where the cumulative flux equals 68% of the total flux" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
   - assumption: Compact disks defined as dust radius < 30 au  
  > "defined as having dust disk radii smaller than 30 au for the purposes of this paper" (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Faint Sz73 ring recovered above 3-sigma; resolution limit of ~4 au acknowledged  
  > "Even the faint ring in Sz73, with surface brightness between 0.2 and 0.4 mJy, was detected above the 3-$\sigma$ level." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
6. **Fit the size-luminosity relation**: Bayesian linear regression of log10 R68 against log10 F_mm with fluxes scaled to 160 pc  
  > "We plot the disk radii versus their total flux density (scaled to 160 pc)" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution); "we apply a Bayesian linear regression method implemented in the LINMIX package" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
   - software: LINMIX
   - parameter: log10(R68) = alpha + beta log10(F_mm)  
  > "\log_{10}(R_{68}) =\alpha+\beta\log_{10}(F_{\rm mm})." (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
   - assumption: Flux scaled to a common distance of 160 pc  
  > "We plot the disk radii versus their total flux density (scaled to 160 pc)" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
   - fitting: LINMIX Bayesian linear regression with intrinsic dispersion; priors not stated  
  > "with an intrinsic dispersion of $0.116 \pm 0.02$" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
   - validation: Slope compared with drift-, fragmentation- and trap-dominated predictions and earlier work  
  > "The observed slope falls between the drift-dominated and the trap- and frag-dominated regimes" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
7. **Build radiative transfer grid of model disks**: RADMC-3D ray-traced images at 1.3 mm for 1728 models; total flux and R68 measured as for observations; optically thin mass compared with input mass; power-law fits outside the saturated region defined by mean optical depth  
  > "We performed radiative transfer at 1.3 mm for the generic protoplanetary disk model in the RADMC-3D software package (cite: 2012ascl.soft02015D)" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling); "we create a ray-traced image assuming a distance of 160 pc and face-on disk inclination" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - software: RADMC-3D, optool
   - parameter: Grid: 12 dust masses 4e-6 to 5.1e-3 Msun, 12 radii 0.5 to 30 au, 12 stellar luminosities 0.0025 to 3 Lsun, log-uniform  
  > "These parameters ranged from 4e-6 to 5.1e-3 $M_{\odot}$, 0.5 to 30 au, 0.0025 to 3 $L_{\odot}$" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - parameter: Dust opacities from optool with DSHARP opacities, grain sizes 0.050 um to 3 mm, full scattering matrix  
  > "The dust opacity values were calculated using grain sizes from $a_{\text{min}} = 0.050  \mu$m to $a_{\text{max}} = 3$ mm, incorporating the full scattering matrix." (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - assumption: Dust surface density proportional to 1/r out to outer radius r_out, Gaussian vertical profile  
  > "\Sigma(r)= \Sigma_0\left(\frac{r}{r_{\rm out}}\right)^{-1}" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - assumption: Distance 160 pc, face-on inclination  
  > "we create a ray-traced image assuming a distance of 160 pc and face-on disk inclination" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - assumption: Stellar effective temperature omitted because it has minimal impact on flux-derived dust mass  
  > "the star’s effective temperature has minimal impact on the flux-derived dust mass of the models" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - assumption: Optically thin mass uses kappa = 2.3 cm2/g and uniform T_dust = 20 K  
  > "we assume a uniform dust temperature, $T_{\rm dust} = 20$ K" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - fitting: Power-law fits to the model grid for M_flux versus M_dust away from the saturated regime (tau < 0.25 relation given)  
  > "we fit power laws to the model grid away from the saturated region, defined through the mean optical depth" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
   - validation: Flux-derived mass compared with model input mass, showing saturation with optical depth  
  > "Figure [6](#fig:mass_comparison) primarily highlights the saturation of the disk flux as the emission becomes optically thick" (3.4 Radiative transfer modeling)
8. **Infer dust mass and optical depth for compact disks**: Invert the model grid on the flux-radius plane using each disk's stellar luminosity, propagating observational uncertainties  
  > "Using the stellar luminosities tabulated in Appendix [6](#appendix:a), we can create a bespoke radius-flux grid for each disk and thereby determine its dust mass." (3.5 Dust mass estimates); "The uncertainties in each observable are readily propagated through." (3.5 Dust mass estimates)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: 28 disks with radii < 30 au fit within the grid and have known luminosity  
  > "The distribution of dust masses for the 28 disks with well determined values and known luminosities" (3.5 Dust mass estimates)
   - assumption: Stellar luminosities from the stellar-parameters table; disks at the grid edge (binaries, tau >> 5) excluded as indeterminate  
  > "resulting in inferred masses that were either indeterminate or exceptionally large with substantial uncertainties" (3.5 Dust mass estimates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Estimate planet masses for disk substructures**: DBNets convolutional neural network applied to continuum FITS images with gap centre positions, inclination, PA and distance  
  > "We employed DBNets, a deep-learning tool that utilizes convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to analyze observations of dust continuum emission and predict the mass of the gap-opening planets" (3.6 The exoplanet population in the Lupus substructures); "In total, we analyzed 25 substructures within our sample." (3.6 The exoplanet population in the Lupus substructures)
   - software: DBNets
   - parameter: alpha-turbulence range 1e-2 to 1e-4  
  > "The results, obtained for a range of $\alpha$-turbulence values between $10^{-2} - 10^{-4}$" (3.6 The exoplanet population in the Lupus substructures)
   - assumption: Observed gaps and cavities are carved by planets  
  > "Under the assumption that the observed rings and gaps are produced by planets, we estimate their masses using DBNets." (5 Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with planet masses in Taurus and sigma Orionis disks; results flagged where uncertainties exceed DBNets rejection threshold  
  > "is comparable to similarly derived estimates from observations of disks in Taurus (cite: 2023ApJ...952..108Z) and the $\sigma$ Orionis cluster (cite: 2024arXiv241003823H)" (3.6 The exoplanet population in the Lupus substructures)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Highest-resolution ALMA data of small, faint disks are needed to reduce observational biases in the link between planet formation and substructures  
  > "observational biases should be reduced through observations of these small, faint disks at the highest resolution using the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)" (abstract)
- Why this method: Visibility modelling reveals features not seen in images, such as the faint Sz73 ring  
  > "The faint ring in Sz73 was not detected during the initial imaging process with CASA, but became apparent when analyzing the visibilities." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
- Alternative: Internal photoevaporation or dead zones as alternatives to planets for the smallest cavities (photoevaporation judged less likely)  
  > "Alternatively, though less likely, we could be observing a different regime where the smallest gaps in our sample are caused by internal photoevaporation rather than planet formation" (5.4 Impact of compact disks in the exoplanet population)
- Alternative: Self-calibration was attempted and abandoned for the new datasets  
  > "Self-calibration was attempted on the new datasets, but due to the limited observing time per source and the low signal-to-noise ratios (SNR), it was not possible to achieve satisfactory results." (2 Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: MCMC chains judged converged after discarding 1,000 burn-in steps and inspecting corner plots  
  > "To verify convergence, we discarded the first 1,000 steps (burn-in phase) and examined the corner plots." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Resolution limits substructure detection to about 4 au and small substructures may be missed  
  > "Given our resolution, which limits detection to structures as small as approximately 4 au" (3.1 Dust continuum images); "which points to a potential bias introduced by resolution where very small substructures may remain undetected in the smallest disks" (3.3 Disk size and substructure distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Sz130 extended emission below 300 kilo-lambda could not be fit; flux 41% lower than literature  
  > "In the case of Sz130, this resulted in a lower flux (41% lower) compared to the literature values." (3.2 Visibility modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Flux discrepancies may result from missing short baselines  
  > "Some discrepancies could arise from the absence of short baselines in our observations, potentially leading to the loss of extended emission flux." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
- Limitation (authors): Planet masses for several disks exceed DBNets rejection threshold and should be interpreted with caution  
  > "the planet masses for these disks should be interpreted with caution" (Table 2)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Most disks are fitted with a single Gaussian even when marginally resolved; radii of unresolved disks rely on fixed face-on geometry and are model dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust masses depend on fixed assumptions in the RADMC-3D grid (Sigma proportional to 1/r, DSHARP opacities, face-on, no stellar temperature dependence), so the optical depth conclusion is model dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heterogeneous sample: images and parameters from many programmes with different resolution and processing; five disks lack very high resolution
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abstract planet separations 2 to 74 au disagree with Table 2 (up to 117 au for IM Lup) and the Discussion (14-117 au)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Planet masses from DBNets assume planet-carved gaps and cavity positions chosen from fitted ring parameters; many exceed the rejection threshold
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No self-calibration, and flux scale uncertainty is not propagated into radii or masses
