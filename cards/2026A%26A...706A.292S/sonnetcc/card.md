## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate and image continuum Stokes I, Q, U**: Three rounds of phase self-calibration, line-excluded continuum, tclean imaging at 3mm, 1mm-combine and 1mm-high  
  > "employing three rounds of phase self-calibration (cite: Pearson1984) for each dataset to minimize image noise and enhance fidelity." (ALMA observations and data reductions); "The continuum maps were then produced using the `tclean` task in CASA." (ALMA observations and data reductions)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5 (K04166 1mm-high also robust +2.0)  
  > "3mm | 12-m | 94.5 | 0.5" (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-calibration improved 1.4 mm 12-m image quality by a factor of about 7.0  
  > "This process typically improved the quality of the 1.4 mm data from the 12-m array by a factor of approximately 7.0, for instance." (ALMA observations and data reductions)
2. **Image-plane Gaussian fits of Stokes I**: 2D elliptical Gaussian fit to each of the three datasets per source  
  > "we performed 2D Gaussian fits with CASA `imfit`, for each of the three datasets in each source" (Results 3.2)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - assumption: Distance 156 pc for au conversion  
  > "The radii in astronomical units are given, using a distance of 156 pc." (Table 3 notes)
   - fitting: Minimization of an elliptical Gaussian model (imfit)  
  > "Parameters obtained by minimizing an elliptical Gaussian model to the dust continuum emission maps" (Table 3 notes)
   - validation: Compared 1mm-high sizes with eDisk dust disk radii  
  > "these sizes are consistent with the dust disk radii reported by the eDisk collaboration" (Results 3.2)
3. **Fit visibility amplitudes to separate disk and envelope**: Azimuthally averaged amplitude profiles of the 7-m + 12-m Band 6 continuum visibilities fit with Plummer envelope alone or combined with Gaussian or uniform disk; best model chosen by reduced chi2  
  > "The analytical models were used alone or combined, then minimized to reproduce the azimuthally averaged amplitude profiles of each source." (Results 3.2); "summarizes the results from modeling the visibility amplitudes with Plummer envelopes, both including and not including Gaussian disks and uniform disks components." (Results 3.2)
   - parameter: Envelope flux, p+q, R_in, R_out, disk flux and disk radius per model; reduced chi2 used to pick best model  
  > "Envelope flux | Envelope $p+q$ | Envelope $R_{in}$ | Envelope $R_{out}$ | Disk flux | Disk radius $^{b}$ | $\chi^2$" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Distance 156 pc  
  > "The radii in astronomical units are given, using a distance of 156 pc." (Table 3 notes)
   - fitting: Minimization against azimuthally averaged amplitudes; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "then minimized to reproduce the azimuthally averaged amplitude profiles of each source." (Results 3.2)
   - validation: Reduced chi2 comparison between models (PG best for both sources)  
  > "models with a Plummer envelope and a disk show reduced $\chi^2$ 2 to 3 orders of magnitude better than models including only a disk or an envelope" (Results 3.2)
4. **Derive polarization maps**: Debiased polarized intensity, fractional polarization P/I and polarization angle; sigma cutoffs applied to masks  
  > "We then calculate the fractional linear polarization of $P_{\mathrm{frac}} = P/I$." (ALMA observations and data reductions); "We adopted a 3$\sigma$ cutoff in total intensity 2$\sigma$ cutoff in polarized intensity for polarization fraction mapping" (ALMA observations and data reductions)
   - parameter: Super-Nyquist pixel sampling at about one-third of the beam major axis; 3 sigma threshold on polarized intensity for vector maps  
  > "pixels sampling the polarization direction (position angles) at approximately one-third of the major axis of the beam" (ALMA observations and data reductions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit polarization position-angle histograms and offsets**: Two-component Gaussian fit to the K04166 PA histogram; polarized-peak vs Stokes I-peak offsets with positional uncertainties  
  > "fitted with a two-component Gaussian, shows peaks separated by" (Results 3.3)

   - parameter: nu = 218.4 GHz  
  > "is the frequency (218.4 GHz here)" (Discussion 4.1)

## Qualitative findings

- K04166 has extended Stokes I and polarized emission tracing a tentative hourglass magnetic field in its envelope; inner envelope and disk polarization changes  
  > "K04166 shows extended Stokes $I$ and polarized emission tracing a tentative hourglass magnetic field morphology in its envelope." (abstract)
- K04169 shows compact Stokes I and polarized emission consistent with disk self-scattering  
  > "K04169 exhibits compact Stokes $I$ and polarized emission, consistent with self-scattering from the disk." (abstract)
- Both disks are extremely compact; K04166 keeps a substantial envelope while K04169 envelope is largely dissipated  
  > "Both disks are extremely compact, yet K04166 retains a substantial envelope while the one of K04169 is largely dissipated." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Needed deep multiscale polarimetry across envelope and disk scales of young nearby protostars  
  > "A promising way forward is to jointly study the polarized emission at envelope and disk scales around young nearby protostars." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Fourier-space modeling is preferred for disk sizes in embedded protostars  
  > "It has been demonstrated that the best method to measure disk sizes in embedded protostars is to directly model the data in the Fourier space" (Results 3.2)
- Alternative: Image-plane Gaussian fits cannot separate envelope from disk; models with envelope only or disk only were compared and rejected in favour of envelope + disk for K04166  
  > "does not allow us to disentangle the emission due to the inner envelope from the one due to the disk" (Results 3.2)
- Alternative: Two scenarios for K04166 inner polarization: toroidal magnetic field around the disk or self-scattering from large grains in the inner envelope  
  > "First, the observed pattern may trace a toroidal magnetic field in the envelope" (Discussion 4.2); "indicating that large grains exist outside the disk, in the inner envelope" (Discussion 4.2)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Observing program not fully completed  
  > "While full completion of the observational program was not achieved" (ALMA observations and data reductions)
- Limitation (authors): Visibility models cannot characterize outer envelope beyond about 3000 au  
  > "these models cannot faithfully characterize the properties of the outer envelope(s) at scales" (Results 3.2)
- Limitation (authors): Location of the polarization-mechanism transition depends on the discrimination criterion  
  > "the precise location of the transition between polarization mechanisms depends on how both mechanisms are discriminated" (Discussion 4.2)
- Limitation (authors): Grain size from self-scattering is highly uncertain  
  > "the typical grain size needed to produce the self-scattering signal is subject to large uncertainties" (Discussion 4.2)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3% polarization-fraction threshold is ad hoc and the mechanism transition is only tentative
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visibility fits give very small envelope inner radii and unconstrained parameters (for example R_in 0.01 (7) arcsec for K04169)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Polarization calibration and leakage treatment are not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one Stokes I model family (Plummer + Gaussian/uniform disk) was tried
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 3 mm and 1 mm datasets have different resolution, so comparisons across bands are confounded by beam dilution
