## Science

- Fields: evolved_stars, astrochemistry, stellar_physics
- Question: (1) to identify and map the emission and absorption of H2O in several vibrational states, and of OH in Lambda-doubling transitions with similar excitation energies; and (2) to determine the physical conditions and kinematics in gas layers close to the extended atmosphere in a sample of asymptotic giant branch stars (AGBs) and red supergiants (RSGs).  
  > "Our goals are: (1) to identify and map the emission and absorption of H 2 O in several vibrational states, and of OH in Λ-doubling transitions with similar excitation energies; and (2) to determine the physical conditions and kinematics in gas layers close to the extended atmosphere in a sample of asymptotic giant branch stars (AGBs) and red supergiants (RSGs)." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract spectra and measure line peak flux densities, velocity extents, and rest frequencies**: Extract spectra from image cubes using circular apertures; measure peak flux density and velocity extent between 2.5 sigma line wings; average peak frequencies across stars corrected for systemic LSR velocity to derive rest frequencies.  
  > "We extracted H 2 O and OH spectra from our image cubes for various aperture sizes (larger than the synthesised beam and smaller than the maximum recoverable scale), using circular apertures of diameters typically 0 ′′ . 08, 0 ′′ . 4 and 4 ′′ . 0 for the high, mid and low resolution cubes." (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products); "The velocity extent of the identified H 2 O lines is determined from the blue and red line wing velocities at the 2.5σ level of each detected line" (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products); "The average of our frequency measurements in different stars for a given transition, corrected for the stellar velocity used during the observations, is our observed rest frequency." (3.2. Identification of highly excited water lines)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Aperture diameters: 0.08 arcsec (high), 0.4 arcsec (mid), 4.0 arcsec (low); 2.5 sigma threshold for velocity extent  
  > "using circular apertures of diameters typically 0 ′′ . 08, 0 ′′ . 4 and 4 ′′ . 0 for the high, mid and low resolution cubes." (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products); "The velocity extent of the identified H 2 O lines is determined from the blue and red line wing velocities at the 2.5σ level of each detected line" (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products)
   - assumption: Adopted stellar LSR systemic velocities from Table 1  
  > "The average of our frequency measurements in different stars for a given transition, corrected for the stellar velocity used during the observations, is our observed rest frequency." (3.2. Identification of highly excited water lines)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-checked with CDMS, JPL, and W2020 catalogs to rule out spectral confusion; compared apertures of different sizes to confirm no flux is resolved out.  
  > "Comparisons between these (and with the ACA data, Sec. 2.3) showed that in most cases no additional flux was detected in larger apertures except where variability is likely (Sec. 6.1), confirming that we are not resolving out significant emission from these lines." (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products); "In addition, we used the CDMS 6 (Müller et al. 2005; Endres et al. 2016) data base to check for possible misidentifications due to the spectral proximity with the molecular species in the atomium chemical inventory" (3.2. Identification of highly excited water lines)
2. **Improve spectroscopic OH Lambda-doubling rest frequencies**: Combine ALMA OH transition frequencies from this work and Khouri et al. (2019) with the laboratory line list of Drouin (2013) and perform a Dunham fit using SPFIT.  
  > "We added the transition frequencies from our present study and from Khouri et al. (2019), summarized in Table 4, to the line list. We then carried out the line fit using Pickett’s SPFIT program (Pickett 1991), as done earlier (Drouin 2013)." (Appendix A)
   - software: SPFIT
   - parameter: 21 Lambda-doubling parameters for OH plus 5 isotopic breakdown parameters (2 poorly determined parameters out of 97 omitted)  
  > "Drouin (2013) employed 21 Λ−doubling parameters for OH itself plus five parameters to accomodate the breakdown of the Born-Oppenheimer approximation through the isotopic substitutions." (Appendix A); "Two poorly determined parameters (out of 97) were omitted." (Appendix A)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: Dunham fit in SPFIT  
  > "Drouin (2013) had carried out a Dunham fit pertaining to a plethora of laboratory data." (Appendix A); "We then carried out the line fit using Pickett’s SPFIT program (Pickett 1991), as done earlier (Drouin 2013)." (Appendix A)
   - validation: O-C residuals compared with observational uncertainties in Table 4.  
  > "Nevertheless, as can be seen in Table 4, the changes were large enough to reproduce" (Appendix A)
3. **Measure angular sizes of H2O and OH emitting/absorbing regions and stack weak OH lines**: Determine H2O sizes from geometric mean of max/min radii within 3 sigma contour and azimuthal averaging on moment 0 maps; fit 2D Gaussians to OH moment 0 maps within 3 sigma contour mask in CARTA; stack OH visibilities of four hyperfine transitions aligned in velocity to detect weak high-J OH lines.  
  > "In OH, despite often irregular emission or absorption contours, we have used a 3σ contour mask in CARTA 4 to fit 2D Gaussians to the observed regions (see Sects. 8.2.1 and 8.2.2)." (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products); "In a first approach, sizes are derived from the geometric mean of the maximum and minimum angular radii of the emission centered on the star and enclosed within the 3σ contour" (4.3. Channel maps, angular sizes, ring-like structures); "For each group of four transitions, the visibility data were extracted for the same velocity range centered on each hyperfine transition and combined by aligning the velocities." (8.2.3. Current and further identification of OH sources)
   - software: CARTA, CASA
   - parameter: 3 sigma contour threshold for size measurement and CARTA mask  
  > "we have used a 3σ contour mask in CARTA 4 to fit 2D Gaussians to the observed regions" (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products)
   - assumption: No beam deconvolution applied to irregular clean image sizes  
  > "The angular extent of the emitting or absorbing H 2 O and OH regions is determined without beam de-convolution from our channel maps or from the velocity-integrated intensity maps (zeroth moment or mom 0 maps)" (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting in CARTA within 3 sigma mask for OH moment 0 maps  
  > "The OH extent of these clean, emission (or absorption, see Sect. 8.2.2) images is obtained by fitting 2D Gaussians with CARTA (see end of Sect. 2.2) within the 3σ level contour of our mom 0 maps" (8.2.1. OH channel maps, angular sizes)
   - validation: Validated OH visibility stacking by comparing stacked maps with single-frequency channel maps of the strongest sources.  
  > "We have validated this approach by comparing the stacked maps with the single frequency channel maps of our strongest sources." (8.2.3. Current and further identification of OH sources)
4. **Fit compact H2O maser components and derive brightness temperatures**: Fit 2D Gaussian components to channel maps using AIPS task SAD above 5 sigma threshold, group into maser features, and compute brightness temperatures from channel map peaks and deconvolved Gaussian component sizes.  
  > "To better trace the deepest motions of the inner stellar wind, we have conducted a Gaussian analysis of the most compact, nondiffuse regions of water emission mapped in different transitions using the task SAD in AIPS 9 ." (4.5. Tracing the inner-wind regions at small scales); "Firstly, a good approximation to the brightness temperature, T b , of compact sources is derived from the peak flux density per beam in our channel maps and the synthesized beam." (5.1. Brightness temperature); "Secondly, another approach to estimating the brightness temperature is provided by the de-convolved sizes of the Gaussian components fitted, as described in Sect. 4.5, to the 268.149 GHz features with a peak S/N >∼10." (5.1. Brightness temperature)
   - software: AIPS, SAD
   - parameter: 5 sigma extraction threshold; >= 3 neighboring components with ~2 km/s velocity coherence; peak S/N >= 10 for T_Gauss  
  > "times the r.m.s. noise level in the spectral channels as our typical threshold to extract components." (4.5. Tracing the inner-wind regions at small scales); "comprising at least 3 neighboring components with ∼2 km s −1 velocity coherence." (4.5. Tracing the inner-wind regions at small scales)
   - assumption: Maser emission has a Gaussian angular profile  
  > "Maser emission usually has a genuinely Gaussian angular profile (e.g., Richards et al. 1999)" (4.5. Tracing the inner-wind regions at small scales)
   - fitting: Least-squares 2D Gaussian fitting via AIPS SAD  
  > "For the most compact H 2 O emission sources, we have also used in the AIPS (Astronomical Image Processing System) package 3 a specific task to fit Gaussian models by least squares to our images (see Sect. 4.5)." (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive excitation temperatures, column densities, line opacities, and OH/H2O abundance ratios from population diagrams**: Construct population diagrams from velocity-integrated flux densities in 0.08 arcsec apertures for H2O and OH, perform linear least-squares fits to obtain T_ex (or T_rot) and N, calculate line-center opacities, and compute the N(OH)/N(H2O) ratio.  
  > "We have constructed population diagrams for a few sources in our sample with at least six lines and with known velocity-integrated flux densities using the spectra extracted for an aperture diameter of 0 ′′ . 08 from our spectral cubes. A linear least-squares fit to the data in various vibrational states gives the excitation temperature, T ex , and the quantity ln( N /Q)" (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates); "An estimate of the OH rotational temperature, T rot , and column density, N (OH), can be obtained from a population diagram" (9.2. Population diagrams and opacity estimates); "From the high resolution observation of high-lying energy levels of three AGBs (R Hya, S Pav and R Aql), all performed within a few weeks, and from the population diagram analysis described in Sects. 5.2 and 9.2, we obtain N (OH)/ N (H 2 O) ∼(0.7−2.8)×10 −2 ." (10. Observed OH/H2O abundance ratio and chemical models in the inner wind)
   - parameter: Circular aperture diameter = 0.08 arcsec; at least 6 lines for H2O; exclude masing 268.149 GHz line in AH Sco and IRC+10011  
  > "We have constructed population diagrams for a few sources in our sample with at least six lines and with known velocity-integrated flux densities using the spectra extracted for an aperture diameter of 0 ′′ . 08 from our spectral cubes." (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates); "In AH Sco and IRC+10011 we have excluded line number 14 which is masing" (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates)
   - assumption: Optically thin LTE emission uniformly filling a 0.08 arcsec circular aperture; spin degeneracy 3/4 (ortho) and 1/4 (para) for H2O; H2O and OH excited in similar regions; fractional abundance H2O/H2 = 3e-5 when estimating n(H2)  
  > "we assume that each line uniformly fills a circular aperture of 0 ′′ . 08 in diameter with a rough estimate of the filling factor uncertainty. The H 2 O partition function includes the spin degeneracy (3/4 and 1/4 for ortho and para H 2 O)" (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates); "For a fractional water abundance of 3×10 −5 (used in Sect.7) the H 2 density is then (3.7−5.3)×10 10 cm −3" (5.2.    - fitting: Linear least-squares fit to population diagram  
  > "A linear least-squares fit to the data in various vibrational states gives the excitation temperature, T ex, and the quantity ln( N /Q) where N" (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity by randomly discarding lines in the R Hya population diagram (~25% statistical uncertainty on N(H2O)) and varying assumed errors for OH (~30% uncertainty on N(OH)).  
  > "Discarding any line in the R Hya diagram for example, does not change significantly the N (H 2 O) estimates and, from random trials we suggest a statistical uncertainty of the column density at the level of ∼25%." (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates); "However, tests made with various assumed errors in the observables suggest that N (OH) can vary by at least 30%." (9.2. ## Reasoning

- Why this data: High sensitivity and high angular resolution (~0.02-1.0 arcsec) of the ALMA ATOMIUM Band 6 survey allow probing hot, dense gas in the inner wind (1-30 R_*) close to the photosphere without resolving out compact high-energy H2O and OH lines; ACA follow-up provides an additional epoch and higher spectral resolution (0.07 km/s) to test 268.149 GHz time variability.  
  > "These and more recently discovered transitions can now be observed at a high sensitivity and angular resolution in the inner wind close to the stellar photosphere with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)." (abstract); "The main goals of the ACA observations are to cover H 2 O lines at 268.149, 254.040, and 254.053 GHz (see Table 2) at a higher spectral resolution and to measure the H 2 O line flux densities at an additional epoch." (2.3. ACA observations)
- Why this method: Population diagrams across a wide range of excitation energies (~4000-9000 K) allow estimating excitation temperatures and column densities when multiple lines in a single vibrational state are unavailable; visibility stacking of four hyperfine transitions reduces noise by a factor of two to detect faint high-J OH lines.  
  > "However, an estimate of the excitation gas temperature and column density can be made from a population diagram (Goldsmith & Langer 1999) provided that the range of line energy used in such a diagram is large enough and that we are not too far from the optically thin case." (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates); "This allows us to image four stacked hyperfine lines, reducing the noise by a factor of two, but at the expense of the identification by another approach to know which exact hyperfine transition(s) is(are) excited." (8.2.3. Current and further identification of OH sources)
- Alternative: Photodissociation of H2O by UV radiation from fast shocks (>40 km/s) as the origin of high-J OH excitation was rejected in favor of direct chemical formation of OH from O and H2 behind slower circumstellar shocks.  
  > "However, the shock speeds invoked in this work, at least 40 km s −1 , are higher than those observed in AGBs. Therefore, we favor the direct formation of OH from oxygen and H 2 after the passage of the circumstellar shock" (10. Observed OH/H2O abundance ratio and chemical models in the inner wind)
- Alternative: Excitation of H2O by collisions with electrons was rejected as negligible because the electron mole fraction in AGB atmospheres is <= 1e-5 where water is excited.  
  > "15 Excitation by electrons is expected to be small in AGBs. The mole fraction of electrons estimated from the equilibrium thermochemical data of Agúndez et al. (2020) is <∼5 10 −6 ." (7.1. General comments)
- How they decided it was enough: Compared spectra extracted across increasing aperture sizes (0.08, 0.4, 4.0 arcsec and ACA 18 arcsec) to verify that no additional flux was resolved out.  
  > "Comparisons between these (and with the ACA data, Sec. 2.3) showed that in most cases no additional flux was detected in larger apertures except where variability is likely (Sec. 6.1), confirming that we are not resolving out significant emission from these lines." (2.2. Main array observations, data reduction, and products)
- Limitation (authors): Modest spectral resolution (~1 MHz or 1.1-1.3 km/s) of the main array observations limits rest frequency precision and can blend narrow maser components.  
  > "Our main sources of error most probably come from limited spectral resolution, ∼1 MHz, and the stellar velocity uncertainty." (3.2. Identification of highly excited water lines); "Maser lines can be narrower than 1 km s −1 , so, together with the extended array, a higher spectral resolution would be desirable to reveal the presence of multiple unresolved spectral components" (6.2. Line narrowing at 268.149 GHz)
- Limitation (authors): Population diagrams assume optically thin LTE emission uniformly filling a 0.08 arcsec aperture and span a restricted energy range for OH, yielding column density uncertainties of ~25% for H2O and >=30% for OH.  
  > "Discarding any line in the R Hya diagram for example, does not change significantly the N (H 2 O) estimates and, from random trials we suggest a statistical uncertainty of the column density at the level of ∼25%." (5.2. Population diagrams, opacity estimates); "The main limitations in the determination of N (OH) come from the rather restricted energy range and the fact that the observed regions are assumed to have uniform properties. However, tests made with various assumed errors in the observables suggest that N (OH) can vary by at least 30%." (9.2. Population diagrams and opacity estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of multi-epoch monitoring across the stellar pulsation cycle prevents testing the predicted phase dependence of the OH/H2O abundance ratio and high-J OH variability.  
  > "However, our current observations, lacking data at different epochs are unable to estimate how the OH/H 2 O ratio varies with the optical phase and if variations match the model predictions." (10. Observed OH/H2O abundance ratio and chemical models in the inner wind)
- Limitation (authors): H2O radiative transfer maser models only include vibrational states up to (0,2,0), (1,0,0), and (0,0,1) and treat ortho and para H2O separately without cross-species line overlap; chemical kinetic models are not yet available for RSGs.  
  > "(We point out that at the present time we have no model outputs available for RSGs.)" (10. Observed OH/H2O abundance ratio and chemical models in the inner wind); "(2) An in-depth development of H 2 O line excitation models awaits newer collision rates and needs to incorporate higher vibrational states, up to the (0,3,0), (1,1,0) and (0,1,1) states at least, together with line overlap effects between para and ortho water." (11.3. Chemical abundances and prospective remarks)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a uniform 0.08 arcsec filling factor for both H2O and OH when computing N(OH)/N(H2O) can bias the abundance ratio because the high-resolution maps show ring-like, clumpy, and partially absorbed structures against the stellar continuum disk.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing peak flux densities between mid and extended array configurations to infer 268.149 GHz time variability can be affected by ~15% flux calibration uncertainties and differing sensitivity to diffuse extended emission.
