## Reasoning

- Why this data: High spectral and spatial resolution data from ALMA allow identification of complex organic molecules and their spatial distribution in hot molecular cores.  
  > "Using high spectral and spatial resolution data like ALMA, VLA, etc., we can identify the different complex organic molecules and the spatial distribution of those complex molecules in the hot molecular cores." (Introduction)
- Why this method: The LTE assumption in LTE modelling and rotational diagram analysis is valid because the gas density in the warm inner region of G10.47+0.03 is 7x10^7 cm^-3.  
  > "The LTE assumption is valid in the warm inner region of G10.47+0.03 because the gas density of the hot core region of G10.47+0.03 is 7$\times$10$^{7}$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: rol11)." (Identification of C2H5CN towards the G10.47+003)
- Alternative: Rejected the higher column density (1.7x10^17 cm^-2) and lower excitation/rotational temperatures (150 K LTE / 92 K rotational diagram) reported by (cite: mondal23), arguing that their baseline polynomial fitting above second order biased the column density and their 92 K temperature contradicts the hot core environment.  
  > "(cite: mondal23) fit above the second-order polynomial to reduce the noise level of the spectra, which contributed to the measurement of a higher column density of ." (Identification of C2H5CN towards the G10.47+003); "But, using the rotational diagram method, (cite: mondal23) found the temperature of the emitting region to be 92 K, indicating that the molecule is arising from the cold region of the hot core. So, the estimated temperature of by (cite: mondal23) is confusing." (Identification of C2H5CN towards the G10.47+003)
- Alternative: Excluded the J = 15(1,15)–14(1,14) transition from the rotational diagram fit because its upper state energy E_up is nearly identical to that of J = 15(0,15)–14(0,14), which has higher intensity.  
  > "We observe that the $E_{up}$ of the J = 15(1,15)–14(1,14) and J = 15(0,15)–14(0,14) transition lines of are nearly similar. So, we use only the J = 15(0,15)–14(0,14) transition line for the rotational diagram because the line intensity of J = 15(0,15)–14(0,14) is higher than J = 15(1,15)–14(1,14)." (Rotational diagram analysis of)

- Limitation (authors): The synthesised beam size of the continuum images is not sufficient to resolve the continuum source.  
  > "After fitting the 2D Gaussian, we observe that the synthesised beam size of the continuum image of G10.47+0.03 is not sufficient to resolve the continua." (Millimeter wavelength continuum emission towards the G10.47+0.03)
- Limitation (authors): Blended emission lines of C2H5CN are not properly fitted by the single-species LTE model.  
  > "We also observe that all non-blended emission lines of are properly fitted with the LTE model, but the blended lines are not properly fitted with the LTE model." (Identification of C2H5CN towards the G10.47+003)
- Limitation (authors): The observed C2H5CN transitions are not well spatially resolved (or at best marginally resolved), so no conclusions can be drawn about the morphology of the spatial distribution.  
  > "We notice that the estimated emitting regions of C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN are comparable to or slightly greater than the synthesised beam size of the integrated emission maps, which indicates the observed transitions of C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN are not well spatially resolved or, at best, marginally resolved. As a result, no conclusions can be drawn about the morphology of the spatial distribution of towards the G10.47+0.03." (Spatial distribution of)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The rotational diagram uses only 5 transitions spanning a narrow upper-state energy range (E_u = 50.79 K to 77.61 K), which poorly constrains a high rotational temperature of 352.9 ± 66.8 K.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The non-blended transitions have moderate optical depths (tau ~ 0.10 to 0.155 in Table 3), which may slightly affect the optically thin assumption in the rotational diagram.
