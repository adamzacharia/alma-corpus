- Fields: star_formation, astrochemistry
- Question: How do the millimeter CH3OH maser rings excited during the accretion outburst in G358.93-0.03 MM1 evolve in size and intensity over time to trace the propagation and deceleration of the outburst-driven heat wave?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure exponential decay timescales of the 199 and 217 GHz maser lines**: Fit exponential decay curves to the peak intensities and integrated flux densities of the 199 and 217 GHz maser lines across epochs  
  > "Both millimeter maser lines (199 and 217 GHz) decayed exponentially (Fig. 3a), with fitted $e$-folding times of 19.7$\pm$0.38 and 61.7$\pm$7.4 days, respectively, for their peak intensities, and 20.0$\pm$0.5 and 44.9$\pm$8.5 days for their integrated flux densities." (Exponential decay into thermal emission)
   - assumption: Exponential decay of maser intensity over time  
  > "Both millimeter maser lines (199 and 217 GHz) decayed exponentially (Fig. 3a), with fitted $e$-folding times of 19.7$\pm$0.38 and 61.7$\pm$7.4 days, respectively, for their peak intensities, and 20.0$\pm$0.5 and 44.9$\pm$8.5 days for their integrated flux densities." (Exponential decay into thermal emission)
   - fitting: Exponential decay fit  
  > "Both millimeter maser lines (199 and 217 GHz) decayed exponentially (Fig. 3a), with fitted $e$-folding times of 19.7$\pm$0.38 and 61.7$\pm$7.4 days, respectively, for their peak intensities, and 20.0$\pm$0.5 and 44.9$\pm$8.5 days for their integrated flux densities." (Exponential decay into thermal emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit circular rings to maser spots in the highest resolution ALMA cubes**: Independently fit several maser spots around the ring in three high-resolution ALMA epochs (2019.451, 2019.538, 2019.552) and fit those positions with a circle accounting for position uncertainties  
  > "Image cubes from three ALMA epochs have sufficient angular resolution to independently fit several maser spots around the ring." (Maser position fitting technique); "To measure the ring diameter, we subsequently fit those positions with a circle using `scipy.odr` accounting for the uncertainties of those fitted positions, with results summarized in Table 3 and Fig. 2b,c." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - software: SciPy, scipy.odr
   - parameter: 6 to 8 maser spots per epoch  
  > "| 2019.451 | 217.2992 | 8 | 0.336$\pm$0.047 | 2270$\pm$310 |" (Table 3); "| 2019.538 | 217.2992 | 6 | 0.335$\pm$0.051 | 2265$\pm$345 |" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Maser spots trace a circular ring  
  > "To measure the ring diameter, we subsequently fit those positions with a circle using `scipy.odr` accounting for the uncertainties of those fitted positions, with results summarized in Table 3 and Fig. 2b,c." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - assumption: Distance of 6.75 kpc to convert angular diameter to au  
  > "with a near kinematic distance of 6.75 kpc (cite: Brogan2019)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Orthogonal distance regression using scipy.odr accounting for uncertainties in fitted spot positions  
  > "To measure the ring diameter, we subsequently fit those positions with a circle using `scipy.odr` accounting for the uncertainties of those fitted positions, with results summarized in Table 3 and Fig. 2b,c." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - validation: Checked offsets of the centers of the fitted circles from the continuum peak, which range from 7-16 mas (50-110 au)  
  > "The offsets of the centers of the fitted circles from the continuum peak range from 7–16 mas (50-110 au)." (Maser position fitting technique)
3. **Perform 2-component Gaussian fitting on smoothed ALMA and SMA cubes and infer ring diameters**: Smooth higher-resolution ALMA cubes to match lower-resolution epochs (SMA beam for 217 GHz; 2019.286 ALMA beam for 199 GHz epoch 2019.552), fit two fixed-beam Gaussians per channel using CASA imfit, filter fits by residual and R.A. separation criteria, and take the 90th percentile chord length as the ring diameter  
  > "We ran the CASA task ` imfit` to fit two Gaussians to each channel of the smoothed cube using these initial position estimates." (Maser position fitting technique); "We fixed the size and position angle of each component to the synthesized beamsize and solved for their positions and intensities." (Maser position fitting technique); "Since the diameter is the largest chord of a circle, we take the 90th percentile measurement as the best estimate of the diameter, with the estimated uncertainty being the difference between it and the 80th and 100th percentile values." (Maser fitting results)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: Component size and position angle fixed to synthesized beamsize  
  > "We fixed the size and position angle of each component to the synthesized beamsize and solved for their positions and intensities." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - parameter: Peak absolute intensity in 2-component fit residual < 0.4 times that of single-component fit  
  > "First, the peak absolute intensity in the residual image of the 2-component fit must be $<0.4$ times that of a single-component fit to the same channel." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - parameter: Integrated absolute flux in 2-component fit residual < 0.6 times that of single-component fit  
  > "Second, the integrated absolute flux in the residual image of the 2-component fit must be $<0.6$ that of a single-component fit." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - parameter: Separation of fitted components in right ascension between 0.09'' and 0.40''  
  > "Third, the separation of the fitted components in right ascension must be between $0.09\ensuremath{''}$ and $0.40\ensuremath{''}$." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - parameter: Diameter taken as 90th percentile of separations; uncertainty from 80th and 100th percentiles added in quadrature with theta / median SNR  
  > "Since the diameter is the largest chord of a circle, we take the 90th percentile measurement as the best estimate of the diameter, with the estimated uncertainty being the difference between it and the 80th and 100th percentile values." (Maser fitting results); "To reflect the additional uncertainty of fitting relative separations smaller than the observed beamsize ($\theta$), we add in quadrature the ratio of $\theta$ to the median SNR of the individual separation measurements." (Maser fitting results)
   - assumption: Ring-like structure was pervasive in time across epochs  
  > "Assuming the ring-like structure was pervasive in time, we performed the same spatial smoothing to the SMA cube resolution for the other ALMA epochs at 217.2992 GHz, followed by 2-component fitting using the same initial estimates as epoch 2019.451 with the same process and success criteria." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - fitting: 2-component Gaussian fitting in each spectral channel via CASA imfit with fixed beam shapes  
  > "We ran the CASA task ` imfit` to fit two Gaussians to each channel of the smoothed cube using these initial position estimates." (Maser position fitting technique)
   - validation: Proof-of-concept test on smoothed 2019.451 cube overlaid on native-resolution peak intensity image; also tested alternative initial estimates separated by 0.18'' straddling the single component fit, confirming nearly identical results  
  > "To check the fit robustness, 2-component fits were also run with the initial estimates set to two locations separated by 0.18$''$ straddling the single component fit; we confirmed that nearly identical fit results were still obtained." (Maser position fitting technique); "To check the fit reliability, we overlaid the reliable 2-component fit results on the peak intensity image of the native-resolution parent data, demonstrating that the fitted positions generally follow the true underlying structure in the two highest resolution ALMA epochs (Fig. 2b,c) to within the native beamsize." (Maser position fitting technique)
4. **Fit power-law expansion model to maser ring diameters over time**: Fit a fractional power law R = a(t - t0)^m to the inferred diameters from the 2-component fits of the millimeter maser images, and compare with 6.7 GHz VLBI maser spot diameters  
  > "Fitting the inferred diameters from the 2-component fits to the millimeter maser images using a fractional power law, $R=a(t-t_0)^m$, assuming $R$=0 at $t$=$t_0$=2019.0479 corresponding to the maser flare inception (cite: Sugiyama19) yields $m$ = 0.39$\pm$0.06." (Maser fitting results)
   - parameter: t0 = 2019.0479  
  > "assuming $R$=0 at $t$=$t_0$=2019.0479 corresponding to the maser flare inception (cite: Sugiyama19) yields $m$ = 0.39$\pm$0.06." (Maser fitting results)
   - parameter: 50000 Monte Carlo trial fits with bootstrap resampling  
  > "The width of the 90% confidence region of the fit shown in Fig. 3b corresponds to the interdecile range of $50000$ Monte Carlo trial fits with bootstrap resampling." (Maser fitting results)
   - assumption: R = 0 at t = t0 = 2019.0479 (maser flare inception)  
  > "assuming $R$=0 at $t$=$t_0$=2019.0479 corresponding to the maser flare inception (cite: Sugiyama19) yields $m$ = 0.39$\pm$0.06." (Maser fitting results)
   - fitting: Fractional power law fit with 50000 Monte Carlo trials and bootstrap resampling for 90% confidence region  
  > "Fitting the inferred diameters from the 2-component fits to the millimeter maser images using a fractional power law, $R=a(t-t_0)^m$, assuming $R$=0 at $t$=$t_0$=2019.0479 corresponding to the maser flare inception (cite: Sugiyama19) yields $m$ = 0.39$\pm$0.06." (Maser fitting results); "The width of the 90% confidence region of the fit shown in Fig. 3b corresponds to the interdecile range of $50000$ Monte Carlo trial fits with bootstrap resampling." (Maser fitting results)
   - validation: Compared power-law fit against the observed extent of 6.7 GHz VLBI maser spots from Burns et al.  
  > "This fit is consistent with the size measurement of the first epoch of 6.7 GHz masers." (Maser fitting results)

## Qualitative findings

- The highest resolution (24 mas) ALMA image at 217.2992 GHz reveals a nearly complete circular ring of maser spots circumscribing the 0.24'' (~1600 au) dust continuum of MM1, with a northern gap containing a secondary continuum peak.  
  > "The highest angular resolution image (24 mas $\approx$ 160 au) reveals a nearly complete, circular ring of strong maser spots in the 217.2992 GHz ($v_{t}$=1) maser line that closely circumscribes the dust continuum emission from MM1." (Abstract); "The ring is nearly complete except on the northern edge, where the gap contains a secondary peak of continuum emission." (Maser morphology at highest resolution)
- By epoch 2021.597, the 199 and 217 GHz CH3OH transitions had decayed into non-masing thermal emission with Gaussian line profiles and brightness temperatures well below their upper state excitation energies.  
  > "By the time of our ALMA observations on 2021.597, the 199 and 217 GHz lines were considerably weakened, with peak intensities similar to the other bright thermal hot core lines in MM1 (Table 1)." (Exponential decay into thermal emission); "Their brightness temperatures were significantly below the excitation energy levels of these transitions (Table 2), and their line profiles were Gaussian shaped in the central 5 km s$^{-1}$ instead of the prior multi-peaked profile (Figure 1e,f)." (Exponential decay into thermal emission)
- High-excitation thermal CH3CN K=7 lines and the warmest thermal CH3OH peak arise mostly inside the former maser ring, where high gas densities likely collisionally quench the millimeter masers.  
  > "Other hot core species tracing dense gas with a similar $E_{\rm up}$ as the 217 GHz line, such as CH$_3$CN $K$=7 from $J$=11–10 and $J$=12-11 (Fig. 2b,d), arise mostly inside the (former) maser ring." (Exponential decay into thermal emission); "The dearth of masers toward the peak of the warmest thermal gas tracer (199 GHz, Fig. 1b) likely indicates the density there is high enough to collisionally quench them." (Propagation of the heat wave)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The 199 and 217 GHz lines were the strongest millimeter CH3OH maser lines observed in at least 3 epochs, allowing multi-epoch tracking of the ring size and intensity decay over 200 days.  
  > "The strongest CH$_3$OH maser lines observed in $\geq3$ epochs were the 199 and 217 GHz lines, thus we use their data in our analysis." (Exponential decay into thermal emission)
- Why this method: Because maser emission appears on both sides of the ring in many velocity channels, single-component fitting in low-resolution images blends the emission and biases extent estimates; smoothing all cubes to a common resolution and fitting two components per channel provides a globally self-consistent diameter measurement across epochs.  
  > "In many channels, 217 GHz maser emission appears on both sides of the ring, which will produce blending in lower resolution images." (Maser morphology at highest resolution); "This method ensures a globally self-consistent fitting approach across epochs and observatories while not imposing any assumption of a smaller diameter at earlier epochs." (Maser position fitting technique)
- Alternative: Single spatial component fitting to low-resolution maser images (as done in Brogan et al. 2019) was rejected because emission on opposite sides of the ring blends together and biases the inferred extent.  
  > "In hindsight, fitting a single spatial component to such images (cite: Brogan2019) will lead to biased estimates of the emission extent." (Maser morphology at highest resolution)
- Alternative: Combining direct circle-fit diameters from high-resolution cubes with 2-component fit diameters from smoothed cubes was rejected because the 2-component fits systematically agree with the -1 sigma diameter of the circle fit.  
  > "However, the 2-component fit results systematically tend to agree with the $-1\sigma$ diameter from the ring fit rather than the fitted diameter, hence the results from these two distinct methods should not be combined when fitting for the time variation." (Maser position fitting technique)
- Alternative: Combining size measurements across all epochs of the 6.7 GHz VLBI masers and the millimeter masers into a single fit was rejected because different transitions invert under different physical conditions.  
  > "These results demonstrate that one should be cautious and not simply combine size measurements from maser transitions that likely exhibit population inversion in different ranges of physical conditions, particularly when detailed theoretical models are not available for all transitions." (Maser fitting results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 199.5749 GHz maser in the final 2019 ALMA epoch (2019.552) was too weak to achieve 2-component fits when smoothed to the SMA resolution, requiring smoothing to the 2019.286 ALMA resolution instead.  
  > "The same method was applied to the 199.5749 GHz line, but the maser was too weak in the final ALMA epoch to achieve 2-component fits after smoothing it to the SMA resolution." (Maser position fitting technique)
- Limitation (authors): 2-component fit diameters systematically underestimate the direct ring-fit diameters by ~1 sigma.  
  > "However, the 2-component fit results systematically tend to agree with the $-1\sigma$ diameter from the ring fit rather than the fitted diameter, hence the results from these two distinct methods should not be combined when fitting for the time variation." (Maser position fitting technique)
- Limitation (authors): Whether the accretion rate remained steady or declined during the decay phase cannot be determined because it is unclear whether the disk or the protostar dominates the total luminosity in massive YSO outbursts.  
  > "Because it remains unclear whether the disk or the protostar dominates the total luminosity in massive YSO outbursts, we cannot say whether the subsequent accretion rate remained steady or declined, only that the total luminosity of the disk plus protostar did eventually decay." (Connection to the infrared continuum decay and the accretion event)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 199.5749 GHz epoch 2019.552 cube was smoothed to a smaller beam (0.366'' x 0.311'') than the SMA beam (~0.6'' x 0.4'') used for the other 2-component fits, introducing a resolution mismatch in one data point of the time-series expansion fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 2-component Gaussian fitting relies on initial position guesses derived from the 2019.451 native-resolution ring and hard cutoffs on right ascension separation (0.09'' to 0.40''), which could truncate smaller separations at early epochs if the ring were smaller than 0.09''.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 1, the beamsize for ALMA C4 (epoch 2019.286) is printed with a typo as '0.366x311' instead of '0.366x0.311' and the K RMS noise range for ALMA C8 (epoch 2019.552) is inverted ('12.2-4.5').
