## Analysis recipe

1. **Denoise continuum maps and extract core catalogues**: Apply MnGSeg wavelet denoising to continuum maps, run getsf using denoised bsens/bsens* for detection and denoised bsens/bsens* and cleanest for flux and size measurements, apply post-selection filters, and replace contaminated fluxes with cleanest measurements for hot cores.  
  > "In short, the denoising process relies on MnGSeg (cite: Robitaille19), a wavelet-based method which decomposes an image into a Gaussian component associated with the cloud structure and the noise and a coherent component containing the hierarchical star-forming structures." (W43-MM2 and MM3); "Following the approach developed by (cite: Pouteau22a) towards W43-MM2&MM3 (see 2.2.1), we performed the source extraction with getsf using the denoised $\texttt{bsens}^*$ image for the detection step and the denoised $\texttt{bsens}^*$ and cleanest images for measurements." (W43-MM1)
   - software: MnGSeg, getsf
   - parameter: Post-selection filters: size < 4x beam, ellipticity < 2, peak and integrated flux S/N > 2; flux ratio threshold > 3 sigma for hot core line contamination correction in W43-MM1  
  > "The catalogue of the combined regions contains 208 sources passing the recommended post-selection filters (i.e. size lower than four times the beam, ellipticity lower than 2, peak and integrated flux signal-to-noise ratios above 2)." (W43-MM2 and MM3); "The five corrected cores shown in red stand out with flux ratios above 3$\sigma$." (W43-MM1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared new W43-MM1 catalogue with previous getsources catalogue of Motte et al. (2018b) and cleanest catalogue of Louvet et al. (subm.)  
  > "59 of these (87%) are also detected in the cleanest catalogue of Louvet et al. (subm.) and 48 (71%) are in the core catalogue of (cite: Motte18b) (see also 7)." (Completeness)
2. **Derive core masses, deconvolved sizes, volume densities, and completeness limits**: Calculate core masses from continuum fluxes and dust temperatures using an optical depth corrected formula; deconvolve FWHM sizes from the 0.44'' beam; inject synthetic Gaussian sources into getsf background images across 10 mass bins to determine 90% completeness.  
  > "We used temperatures from the map presented by (cite: Motte18b) and calculated masses similarly as in W43-MM2&MM3, using an equation correcting for the dust optical thickness (cite: Motte18b; Pouteau22a)." (W43-MM1); "We estimated the completeness level of W43-MM1 by injecting populations of about 1400 synthetic sources over the background image of W43-MM1." (Completeness of the core catalogue)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: 0.44'' beam for deconvolution; 1400 synthetic sources (~800 in ridge) with 0.6'' Gaussian FWHM in 10 mass bins from 0.16 to 2.4 M_sun across 4 synthetic images  
  > "Cores have diameters ranging from 1200 to 9300 au once deconvolved from the 0.44$\ensuremath{''}$-beam and masses ranging from 0.2$\,\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$ to 109$\,\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$." (W43-MM1); "Synthetic sources were produced with a Gaussian profile whose FWHM size corresponds to the median size of observed cores, 0.6$\ensuremath{''}$." (Completeness of the core catalogue); "The synthetic sources of each population were split in ten bins of mass from 0.16 to 2.4 $\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$, with a constant number of about 140 sources per bin." (Completeness of the core catalogue)
   - assumption: Distance of 5.5 kpc; dust temperatures from Motte et al. (2018b) and Pouteau et al. (2022a); critical Bonnor-Ebert sphere relation at median T_dust = 23 K  
  > "The molecular cloud complex W43, located at 5.5 kpc from the Sun (cite: zhang14) at the junction point of the Scutum-Centaurus spiral arm and the galactic bar, is known as a particularly active star-forming region." (Introduction); "Figure 6 also displays for comparison the mass-size relation of critical Bonnor-Ebert spheres at the median dust temperature of cores ($T_{\rm dust}=23$ K)." (Physical properties of cores in regions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Synthetic source recovery rate evaluated across 4 independent realisations to determine 90% completeness  
  > "The 90% completeness level, excluding sources with bad mass measurements, is found to be 1.6 $\pm$ 0.2 $\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$." (Completeness of the core catalogue)
3. **Identify protostellar and prestellar cores via molecular outflows and hot core emission**: Visually inspect CO (2-1) cubes channel by channel and integrated high- and low-velocity wing maps to identify outflow lobes associated with cores, supplement with hot core emission surveys, and classify cores without either signature as prestellar candidates.  
  > "Cores with neither outflows nor hot core emission are classified as prestellar candidates." (abstract); "The identification of outflow lobes has been done through a detailed inspection of the CO cube." (Protostellar cores in W43-MM2&MM3)
   - parameter: Outflow lobe detection threshold: > 5 sigma in 3 consecutive channels; V_LSR = 92 km s^-1; high-velocity windows: 53.6-61.2 and 119.6-127.2 (or 125.9-133.6) km s^-1; low-velocity windows: 73.9-75.1 and 110.7-112.0 km s^-1  
  > "The minimum requirement for the detection of a lobe is to show emission above 5$\sigma$ in three consecutive channels." (Protostellar cores in W43-MM2&MM3); "In these zoomed-in figures, both high and low velocity emission are shown in contours, with integrations over 53.6-61.2 $\ensuremath{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ and 119.6-127.2 $\ensuremath{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, 73.9-75.1 $\ensuremath{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ and 110.7-112.0 $\ensuremath{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, respectively." (Protostellar cores in W43-MM2&MM3)
   - assumption: Protostellar core sample has the same mass completeness as the total core sample  
  > "We assume the protostellar core sample has the same mass completeness as the total core sample" (Identification of protostellar cores using outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured detection intensity S/N of outflow lobes from low-mass cores (0.8-1.6 M_sun) in at least 2 consecutive channels, confirming all are between 5.7 sigma and 49 sigma (median 17 sigma)  
  > "Their detection intensity, excluding a lobe with confusion, range from 5.7$\sigma$ to 49$\sigma$, with a median of 17$\sigma$." (Measurements of low-mass outflows detection)
4. **Compare core populations and fit prestellar and protostellar Core Mass Functions (CMFs)**: Compare core mass distributions using Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, bin cores into 7 mass intervals to compute protostellar fraction f_pro, and fit high-mass slopes of cumulative CMFs using polyfit and Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) with 3000-iteration bootstrap resampling.  
  > "In 8, histograms comparing the counts of the prestellar, protostellar, and total core populations are represented and fitted with the polyfit method (cite: Sciutto89)." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores); "For the fit, we applied the Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE) method of (cite: Clauset09) implemented in the powerlaw python package (cite: Alstott14)." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores); "In detail, we built $N=3000$ synthetic set of mass generated from a random draw with discount of the measured core masses." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores)
   - software: powerlaw, polyfit
   - parameter: 7 mass bins from 0.8 to 110 M_sun; x_min = 1.6 M_sun (fixed at completeness) or free parameter; N = 3000 bootstrap iterations with x_min uniformly varying by +/- 0.2 M_sun around 1.6 M_sun  
  > "In 7 we compare the numbers of prestellar and protostellar cores in seven mass intervals, ranging from 0.8 to 110$\,\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores); "The uncertainties associated with the sample incompleteness are accounted by allowing $x_{\rm min}$ to uniformly vary by $\pm 0.2\,\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$ from $1.6\,\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$, the 90% completeness level." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores)
   - assumption: 50% core-to-star mass efficiency for defining 16 M_sun high-mass threshold; ergodicity when interpreting CMF evolution  
  > "The latter delimits the last bin and corresponds to the minimum mass of a core able to form high-mass stars, assuming a 50% core-to-star mass efficiency." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores); "In the following, we first assume that the statistics performed in space reconcile with temporal statistics (i.e. the hypothesis of ergodicity)." (CMF and core mass growth)
   - fitting: Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE) minimising KS distance, and Exponentially Modified Gaussian (EMG) fit to bootstrap probability density functions  
  > "When $x_{\rm min}$ is set as a free parameter, the couple of parameters ($x_{\rm min}$, $\alpha_{\rm fit}$) which minimises the KS distance between the data and the fit is provided." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores); "The histograms of the bootstrapping probability density functions are fitted by exponentially modified Gaussians (EMG) with a negative skewness." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores)
   - validation: Compared fixed x_min MLE slopes, free x_min MLE slopes, polyfit slopes, and 3000-iteration bootstrap EMG peak slopes  
  > "For the prestellar CMF, the bootstrap slope $\alpha_{\rm BS,pre}=-1.46_{-0.19}^{+0.12}$ is identical to the fitted slope and compatible at 1$\sigma$ with the Salpeter slope, -1.35." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores)

## Qualitative findings

- Protostellar cores are on average more massive, smaller in deconvolved size, and about 10 times denser than prestellar cores in W43.  
  > "Protostellar cores are found to be, on average, more massive and smaller in size than prestellar cores." (abstract); "As a consequence, the population of protostellar cores is about 10 times denser than that of prestellar cores, with median volume densities of 3.3 $\times$ 10$^{7}$ cm$^{-3}$ versus 4.7 $\times$ 10$^{6}$ cm$^{-3}$." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores)
- At most two to three massive (> 16 M_sun) cores in W43-MM1 and W43-MM2&MM3 are plausible prestellar candidates (MM1#6, MM1#134, and MM2&MM3#22), while MM1#132 (101 M_sun) is likely part of gas inflows rather than a gravitationally bound core.  
  > "From its position at the immediate neighbourhood of the W43-MM1 centre (see 16), source MM1#132 could be rather part of gas inflows." (High-mass prestellar core candidates); "In summary, we conclude that at most two to three massive cores in W43-MM1 and W43-MM2&MM3 could be prestellar." (High-mass prestellar core candidates)
- The high-mass slope of the prestellar CMF in W43 is consistent with the canonical Salpeter slope, whereas the top-heavy global CMF is driven by the protostellar core population.  
  > "Our analysis also revealed that the high-mass slope of the prestellar CMF in W43, $\alpha=-1.46_{-0.19}^{+0.12}$, is consistent with the Salpeter slope, and thus the top-heavy form measured for the global CMF, $\alpha=-0.96 \pm 0.09$, is due to the protostellar core population." (abstract)
- Cores grow in mass during the protostellar phase through inflow from their clump environment (clump-fed model), with high-mass cores growing more strongly than low-mass cores.  
  > "Our results could be explained by ’clump-fed’ models in which cores grow in mass, especially during the protostellar phase, through inflow from their environment." (abstract); "The difference between the slopes of the prestellar and protostellar CMFs moreover implies that high-mass cores grow more in mass than low-mass cores." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: W43-MM1 and W43-MM2&MM3 are massive clumps in the active W43 star-forming complex where top-heavy global CMFs were previously measured; 12-m only data were chosen over 12-m + 7-m combined data because the combined data have higher noise without improving compact core or collimated outflow detection.  
  > "In this work, we aim to further discriminate protostellar and prestellar cores using outflow detections, in order to address the question of the origin of the top-heavy CMFs measured by (cite: Motte18b) and (cite: Pouteau22a)." (Introduction); "The combined 12-m + 7-m data indeed have higher noise levels than the 12-m only data (cite: Pouteau22a) without bringing information significant for this work, whether on the detection of compact cores or the identification of collimated outflows." (Continuum and line observations)
- Why this method: MnGSeg denoising reduces noise by ~30% and increases core detections without introducing spurious sources; getsf is suited for extracting compact cores in complex environments; molecular outflows in CO (2-1) and hot core emission are the primary and secondary signatures of the protostellar phase.  
  > "(cite: Pouteau22a) showed that applying a "denoising" process on the continuum map before running the core extraction increases the number of detected cores thanks to an increased sensitivity, without introducing spurious sources or degrading the quality of flux measurements." (W43-MM2 and MM3); "getsf is the successor of the sources and filaments extraction methods getsources and getfilaments (cite: Mensh12; Mensh13) and is well suited to extract compact cores in complex environments." (W43-MM2 and MM3); "Molecular outflows are the most common signature of the protostellar phase of cores in the millimetre wavelength range (cite: Bontemps96)." (Identification of protostellar cores using outflows)
- Alternative: Combined 12-m + 7-m array configurations were rejected in favour of 12-m only data because the combined data had higher noise levels.  
  > "Our observations with the 7-m array configuration are not used in this work." (Continuum and line observations); "The combined 12-m + 7-m data indeed have higher noise levels than the 12-m only data (cite: Pouteau22a) without bringing information significant for this work, whether on the detection of compact cores or the identification of collimated outflows." (Continuum and line observations)
- Alternative: Visual frequency selection (bsens*) was used for W43-MM1 continuum instead of the ALMA-IMF bsens-nobright method (which excludes the entire CO spectral window) to maximise continuum bandwidth while excluding bright lines.  
  > "Frequency selection was done visually to exclude the brightest lines (such as CO isotopolgues, SiO or SO) while maximising the total bandwidth." (Continuum selection for the bsens$^*$ 1.3 mm continuum map of W43-MM1); "This method is an alternative to the solution presented by (cite: Ginsburg22), which excludes the whole CO spectral window ("bsens-nobright", see their Appendix G)." (Continuum selection for the bsens$^*$ 1.3 mm continuum map of W43-MM1)
- Alternative: Core-fed models (where protostellar core mass decreases monotonically from its initial prestellar mass) were rejected as an explanation for the W43 core populations because protostellar cores are observed to be more massive than prestellar cores.  
  > "If the total mass reservoir available to form a star is mainly determined by the mass of its prestellar core, as assumed in "core fed" models, one would expect the mass of a protostellar core to be lower than that of the prestellar core at the time of protostar formation, ($M_{\rm pre,end}$)." (CMF and core mass growth); "The existence of protostellar cores more massive than prestellar cores thus implies that cores are able to grow in mass during the protostellar phase through inflow and accretion from their environment, which can be included in the previous equation with an additional term $\dot{M}_{\rm core}$:" (CMF and core mass growth)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Bonnor-Ebert spheres are not the most appropriate models for evaluating the gravitational boundedness of the massive and dense cores in these observations.  
  > "Furthermore, this evaluation of cores gravitational boundedness should be considered with caution, as Bonnor-Ebert spheres are not the most appropriate models for the massive and dense cores in our observations." (Physical properties of cores in regions)
- Limitation (authors): The cumulative protostellar CMF of W43 is poorly represented by a single power-law across 1.6 to 109 M_sun.  
  > "This result indicates that the protostellar CMF of W43, in its cumulative form, is poorly represented by a single power-law from 1.6 to 109 $\,\ensuremath{M_{\odot}}$." (Mass distribution of prestellar and protostellar cores)
- Limitation (authors): W43-MM1 has a worse completeness limit (1.6 M_sun vs 0.8 M_sun) and stronger sidelobes than W43-MM2&MM3, which may produce more spurious low-mass prestellar core candidates below 1.6 M_sun.  
  > "The continuum emission is more centrally concentrated and brighter in W43-MM1 compared to W43-MM2&MM3, which produces brighter sidelobes." (Completeness of protostellar core detection); "It likely prevents for good detections of weakest sources and possibly produces more spurious low-mass prestellar core candidates." (Completeness of protostellar core detection)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow identification is relatively insensitive to low-velocity outflows (within ~10 km s^-1 of V_LSR) due to CO self-absorption and cloud contamination, and suffers from confusion in the dense central areas, making the number of protostellar cores a lower limit.  
  > "First, we are relatively insensitive to outflows emitting only at low velocity, where the CO spectra suffer from strong self-absorption or contamination (about 10$\,\ensuremath{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ from the central velocity, see 3)." (Completeness of protostellar core detection); "We conclude that, although the number of detected protostellar cores is formally a lower limit, our outflow-based method does not introduce a significant bias as a function of core mass." (Completeness of protostellar core detection)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow identification and core attribution rely on visual inspection of CO (2-1) channel maps rather than an automated or reproducible algorithm.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperatures used to compute core masses are taken from lower-resolution maps (Motte et al. 2018b; Pouteau et al. 2022a) rather than measured at the 0.5'' core scale, which could bias the masses of internally heated protostellar cores vs externally heated prestellar cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Evolutionary interpretation of the prestellar-to-protostellar CMF flattening relies on the assumption of ergodicity, which the authors note may be violated if W43 is undergoing a time-dependent burst of star formation.
