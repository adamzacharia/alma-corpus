## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA 3.3 mm continuum and measure FRII core flux density**: Multi-frequency synthesis continuum imaging of the three-pointing Band 3 mosaic excluding line-contaminated channels, followed by flux density measurement of the FRII core  
  > "Thus, in order to avoid any possible contamination from line emission in the field, we performed the continuum imaging in the available bandwidth of the observations, excluding a range of $\pm$1500 km/s from the mean redshift of the protocluster." (ALMA Photometry); "In the ALMA continuum map at 3.3 mm, we detected the FRII core and the Western hot spot at $>$3$\sigma$ level (Figure [\[fig:alma_cont\]](#fig:alma_cont)). The measured flux density of the core is $55 \pm 9$ $\upmu$Jy." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
   - software: CASA 5.4.0-70
   - parameter: Excluded velocity range: +/- 1500 km/s around z = 1.694  
  > "excluding a range of $\pm$1500 km/s from the mean redshift of the protocluster." (ALMA Photometry)
   - parameter: Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5, mfs mode at 92.04 GHz  
  > "The imaging was performed with the CASA task in multi-frequency synthesis (*mfs*) mode, resulting in an observed-frame frequency of the image equal to 92.04 GHz. We used a Briggs weighting scheme with robustness parameter equal to 0.5," (ALMA Photometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit the broadband optical-to-mm SED of the FRII host galaxy and derive physical parameters**: Simultaneous three-component SED fitting (SSPs for stellar emission, dusty AGN torus, and cold dust heated by starburst) using two cold dust models (cold dust template vs. gray-body), excluding the ALMA 3.3 mm point; derive SFR, sSFR, intrinsic X-ray luminosity, gas depletion time, and SMBH accretion rate  
  > "We modeled the SED of the FRII host galaxy emission using the fitting code originally presented by (cite: fritz_2006) and improved by (cite: feltre_2012)." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building); "In addition, we performed a second SED fitting with the same models except for the cold dust contribution, which has been fitted with a simple gray-body model with $\beta=2$" (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building); "In both fits, we excluded the ALMA data point, due to the uncertain nature of the ALMA continuum emission (i.e., either due to thermal or synchrotron emission, see Section [0.2](#sec:alma_results))." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
   - software: SED fitting code of Fritz et al. (2006) / Feltre et al. (2012)
   - parameter: Gray-body emissivity index beta = 2 (fixed)  
  > "which has been fitted with a simple gray-body model with $\beta=2$" (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
   - assumption: Fiducial uncertainties of 20% on L_bol and L_FIR, and 30% on stellar mass following Circosta et al. (2019)  
  > "The fiducial errors are 20% for the bolometric and far-infrared (FIR) luminosity, and 30% for the stellar mass; these uncertainties are based on the error analysis of (cite: circosta_2019)," (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
   - assumption: Linear L_FIR - SFR calibration of Kennicutt (1998) after subtracting AGN fraction  
  > "thus, the SFRs derived exploiting the $L_{\mathrm{FIR}}$ – SFR linear calibration presented by (cite: kennicutt_1998) and subtracting the AGN fraction are in agreement within 1$\sigma$ for the two fittings (570–630 $\mathrm{M_{\odot}}$/yr)." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
   - assumption: AGN bolometric corrections from Duras et al. (2020) and BH accretion efficiency eta = 0.3  
  > "Adopting, as bolometric corrections, the values derived by (cite: duras_2020) (see also (cite: lusso_2012)) for a large samples of AGN spanning seven dex in luminosity, we predict an intrinsic 2–10 keV luminosity" (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building); "Moreover, assuming that the BH is accreting with an efficiency $\eta=0.3$ (as assumed by (cite: gilli_2019) and as generally thought for BHs powering jetted AGN; refs. (cite: blandford_1977; ghisellini_2014)), from the bolometric luminosity, we derive an accretion rate for the SMBH of $\dot{M} = L_{\mathrm{bol,~AGN}}/(\eta c^2) \approx 0.6$ $\mathrm{M_{\odot}}$/yr." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
   - fitting: Chi-squared minimization (reduced chi^2 = 1.2 for cold dust template, 1.0 for gray-body model)  
  > "The reduced $\chi^2$ is equal to 1.2 and 1.0 for the fitting using the cold dust template and the simple gray-body model, respectively." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
   - validation: Comparison between the two dust models and comparison of SED-derived intrinsic 2-10 keV AGN luminosity with X-ray spectrum results from Gilli et al. (2019)  
  > "we may conclude that the AGN luminosity derived from the SED-fitting is broadly consistent with that derived using X-rays, especially in the case of the gray-model body." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
3. **Determine the physical origin (thermal dust vs. non-thermal synchrotron) of the 3.3 mm ALMA core emission**: Compare measured ALMA 3.3 mm flux density against gray-body and template SED predictions, and estimate non-thermal synchrotron contribution at 3 mm by subtracting SFR-driven 1.4 GHz flux from measured JVLA 1.4 GHz core flux and extrapolating to 3 mm  
  > "This measurement is nicely in agreement with the flux density expected from the dust thermal emission, considering the best fit using the gray-body model (1$\sigma$), while it is incompatible at the 3$\sigma$ level with the expected flux density in the case of dust-template fitting, which is $\times$2.4 higher." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm); "If we subtract $S_{1.4~\mathrm{GHz, SFR}}$ from the measured $S_{1.4~\mathrm{GHz}}$, and we assume a wide synchrotron spectral index range $\alpha$ = 0.5–1.0 ($S_\nu \propto \nu^{-\alpha}$, valid from the jet base to the oldest electron population; refs. (cite: giovannini_2001; ruffa_2019; padovani_2017)), we derive an expected synchrotron emission at 3 mm $S_{3~\mathrm{mm, sync}}$ = 4–30 $\upmu$Jy." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
   - parameter: Synchrotron spectral index alpha = 0.5 - 1.0 (S_nu proportional to nu^-alpha)  
  > "and we assume a wide synchrotron spectral index range $\alpha$ = 0.5–1.0 ($S_\nu \propto \nu^{-\alpha}$, valid from the jet base to the oldest electron population; refs. (cite: giovannini_2001; ruffa_2019; padovani_2017))," (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
   - assumption: Stellar mass- and redshift-dependent L_8-1000um - L_1.4GHz relation from Delvecchio et al. (2021)  
  > "Exploiting the stellar mass- and redshift-dependent $L_{8-1000~\upmu\mathrm{m}} - L_{1.4~\mathrm{GHz}}$ relation recently presented by (cite: delvecchio_2021), we derive a SFR-driven flux density $S_{1.4~\mathrm{GHz, SFR}}{\sim}$90 $\upmu$Jy." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Calibrate and image JVLA 1.4 GHz linear polarization, fractional polarization, and magnetic field orientation**: Calibrate instrumental cross-hand delays and leakage using unpolarized calibrator 3C147; calibrate polarization angle and fraction using in-field NVSS source J102921+051938 in two 42 MHz IFs; image Stokes I, Q, U; compute debiased linear polarization, fractional polarization, and magnetic field orientation  
  > "Thus, we initially calculated the cross-hand delays using the task of CASA, and, subsequently, we calculated the leakage terms, applying, on the fly, the cross-hand delays table." (Instrumental Calibration); "Thus, we split our JVLA dataset in two subsets, identical to the NVSS IFs. For each subset, we set the polarized emission model for the calibrator field accordingly to the fractional polarization and polarization angles derived for J102921+051938 (using the task)." (Polarization Calibration); "The debiased linear polarization image is calculated as $\sqrt{U^2+Q^2-2.3\sigma_{UQ}^2}$, where $\sigma_{UQ}$ is the average *rms* of the $U$ and $Q$ images, following Equation (5) of (cite: george_2012)." (Polarization Calibration)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: IF1 centered at 1365 MHz (42 MHz bandwidth) with tau_1 = 8.4 deg; IF2 centered at 1435 MHz (42 MHz bandwidth) with tau_2 = 5.2 deg; mean polarized fraction F = 4.73 +/- 0.3%  
  > "The mean polarized fraction ($\overline{F}\sim 4.73\pm0.3\%$) and mean polarization angle ($\overline{\tau}=6.8\pm0.9$ deg) derived from the averaging of the two NVSS intermediate frequencies (IFs, 42 MHz bandwidth centered at 1365 MHz – IF1 – and 1435 MHz – IF2) of this source have been reported by (cite: condon_1998)," (Polarization Calibration); "we derived the two position angles in the IF1 ($\tau_1 = 8.4$ deg) and IF2 ($\tau_2= 5.2$ deg) of J102921+051938." (Polarization Calibration)
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5, uv-taper < 50 klambda (~2.5 arcsec beam), blanked at 3 sigma, B-field orientation = 0.5 arctan(U/Q) + pi/2 in steps of 3 pixels  
  > "We performed the target (i.e., the FRII HzRG) imaging of the Stokes $I$, $U$ and $Q$, separately for the two IFs subsets, with a robustness parameter of 0.5; we applied a mild tapering of $<$50 k$\lambda$ (corresponding to a ${\sim}$2.5 arcsec restoring beam)," (Polarization Calibration); "The magnetic field orientation is calculated as $0.5\arctan(U/Q)+\pi/2$, in steps of three pixels, and is indicated by the cyan dashes." (Polarization Calibration)
   - assumption: No magnetic field wrapping between IF1 and IF2 (valid for RM < 400 deg/m^2)  
  > "We note that we assumed no wrapping of the magnetic field between the IF1 and IF2, as appropriate for sources with an $RM <$ 400 $\mathrm{deg/m^2}$ (cite: taylor_2009)." (Polarization Calibration)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Re-imaged 3C147 after leakage/delay correction (fractional polarization reduced from ~1% to ~0.01%, below <0.05% limit); re-imaged J102921+051938 in target field and verified <1 sigma agreement with model polarization; tested 5, 10, 50, 100 MHz bandwidths to verify negligible bandwidth depolarization in 42 MHz IFs  
  > "We re-imaged the total intensity and linear polarization of 3C147 after the corrections, finding that the total intensity was conserved, while the polarized emission was strongly reduced (fractional polarization ${\sim}$0.01%, consistent with the tabulated value of $<$0.05%)." (Instrumental Calibration); "In order to check whether the polarized fraction and polarization angle had been correctly applied, we performed a quick imaging of J102921+051938 in the target field, and measured the polarized emission (from the linear polarization image, i.e., $\sqrt{U^2+Q^2}$, where $U$ and $Q$ are the Stokes parameters) and polarization angle, finding a good agreement ($<$1$\sigma$) with the model values." (Polarization Calibration); "We found that the bandwidth depolarization started to decrease the S/N of the polarized flux between 50 MHz and 100 MHz; thus, in the two 42 MHz IFs bandwidth, depolarization can be considered negligible." (Polarization Calibration)

## Qualitative findings

- The z = 1.7 FRII host galaxy is a starburst galaxy caught in a brief major stellar mass building phase on its way to becoming a BCG.  
  > "The high sSFR = $1.5 \pm 0.5$ $\mathrm{Gyr^{-1}}$ derived for the FRII host galaxy classifies this object as a starburst galaxy when compared with other SFGs at the same redshift (cite: schreiber_2015), implying that the proto-BCG is currently being observed during the brief phase (few $10^8$ yr; ref. (cite: lapi_2018)) of its major stellar mass building." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building)
- The 3.3 mm ALMA core emission is dominated by thermal cold dust emission rather than non-thermal synchrotron emission, although a minor non-thermal contribution cannot be ruled out.  
  > "In summary, the ALMA flux density is likely dominated by the dust emission, despite the fact that a minor non-thermal contribution may be present." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
- The 1.4 GHz polarized emission shows enhanced fractional polarization and magnetic field alignment along bending morphologies in the FRII lobes, consistent with compression by the external ICM and supporting a positive AGN feedback scenario.  
  > "In general, we found an increased polarization fraction in the regions where the total intensity shows a bending morphology, and a magnetic field orientation that seems to follow the direction of the bendings. Coupled with the X-ray diffuse emission detected in several spots around the FRII lobes (in part ascribed to shock-heated ICM, see (cite: gilli_2019)), we interpret these features as possible signatures of compression produced by the external ICM." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Excluded +/- 1500 km/s around the protocluster mean redshift (z = 1.694) during ALMA continuum imaging to avoid CO(2-1) line contamination from protocluster members; excluded the ALMA 3.3 mm point from SED fitting because its thermal vs. synchrotron nature was a priori uncertain; used a 3C147 leakage + in-field NVSS calibrator strategy for JVLA polarization because no dedicated polarized calibrator was observed; applied <50 klambda tapering in JVLA polarization imaging to recover extended emission while resolving lobe components.  
  > "Thus, in order to avoid any possible contamination from line emission in the field, we performed the continuum imaging in the available bandwidth of the observations, excluding a range of $\pm$1500 km/s from the mean redshift of the protocluster." (ALMA Photometry); "In both fits, we excluded the ALMA data point, due to the uncertain nature of the ALMA continuum emission (i.e., either due to thermal or synchrotron emission, see Section [0.2](#sec:alma_results))." (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building); "we applied a mild tapering of $<$50 k$\lambda$ (corresponding to a ${\sim}$2.5 arcsec restoring beam), in order to retrieve the extended emission with sufficient resolution to distinguish the different components in the FRII galaxy lobes." (Polarization Calibration)
- Alternative: Cold dust template SED model vs. simple gray-body (beta=2) model: the cold dust template model was disfavored because it gave a slightly worse reduced chi^2 (1.2 vs. 1.0), an unrealistically low AGN MIR/FIR fraction (3.1% vs. 24%) for a Compton-thick Type II AGN, and predicted a 3.3 mm flux 2.4x higher than measured by ALMA (incompatible at 3 sigma).  
  > "The AGN fraction in the 8–1000 $\upmu$m range of the gray-body model fitting is significantly higher (24%) that that derived from the dust-template fitting (3.1%)," (SED Fitting: A Proto-BCG Caught at the Peak of Its Stellar Mass Building); "This measurement is nicely in agreement with the flux density expected from the dust thermal emission, considering the best fit using the gray-body model (1$\sigma$), while it is incompatible at the 3$\sigma$ level with the expected flux density in the case of dust-template fitting, which is $\times$2.4 higher." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
- Alternative: Non-thermal synchrotron emission dominating the 3.3 mm ALMA core flux: disfavored because even a flat synchrotron slope (alpha = 0.5) extrapolated from the AGN 1.4 GHz flux yields at most 30 uJy, well below the measured 55 +/- 9 uJy.  
  > "Despite that the upper value (corresponding to $\alpha$ = 0.5) is barely compatible, within 3$\sigma$, with the flux density measured in our ALMA map, we argue that, even in this limited case with a flat slope, the expected flux is considerably lower than that measured." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
- Alternative: Peculiar motion of the galaxy through the ICM as the sole cause of the radio lobe bendings: noted as the simplest explanation if multi-wavelength evidence for shock-heated ICM is neglected.  
  > "However, we point out that the simplest explanation for the observed bending morphology in the lobes would be the peculiar motion of the galaxy through the ICM, if evidence for the shock-heating mechanism found in previous and upcoming works (cite: gilli_2019; damato_2020; damato_subm) is neglected" (Polarized Emission at 1.4 GHz: Signatures of AGN Feedback?)
- How they decided it was enough: Selected 42 MHz IF bandwidths for JVLA polarization imaging after testing 5, 10, 50, and 100 MHz bandwidths and finding that bandwidth depolarization only begins reducing S/N between 50 and 100 MHz.  
  > "We found that the bandwidth depolarization started to decrease the S/N of the polarized flux between 50 MHz and 100 MHz; thus, in the two 42 MHz IFs bandwidth, depolarization can be considered negligible." (Polarization Calibration)
- Limitation (authors): A minor non-thermal synchrotron contribution to the 3.3 mm ALMA flux density cannot be completely ruled out due to 20% uncertainty on L_FIR, 30% on stellar mass, ~0.2 dex scatter in the L_FIR - L_1.4GHz relation, and fixed gray-body beta = 2.0.  
  > "However, we cannot completely rule out some minor contribution of synchrotron emission, due to the large uncertainties on the involved quantities (20% on the $L_{8-1000~\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ and 30% on the stellar mass), and inherent scatter of the relation (${\sim}$0.2 dex, ref. (cite: delvecchio_2021)) used to derive $S_{1.4~\mathrm{GHz, SFR}}$." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm); "In addition, in the Rayleigh–Jeans tail of the gray-body, the flux density is a function of the $\beta$ index, as $S_\nu \propto \nu^{2+\beta}$, where $\beta$ is assumed to be fixed at 2.0 during the fitting." (Continuum Emission at 3.3 mm)
- Limitation (authors): The George et al. (2012) polarization debiasing formula is suited for S/N >= 4, whereas extended regions outside the hot/warm spots only have S/N >= 3, making absolute fractional polarization values less reliable in low-S/N regions.  
  > "This formula is suited for S/N $\geq$ 4 polarized emission. In our map, the only regions above S/N = 4 are the hot and warm spots, while the rest of the emission has S/N $\geq$ 3. Despite this implying that the absolute value of fractional polarization is less reliable in the low-S/N regions, we stress that the debias applies a general offset to all pixels." (Polarization Calibration)
- Limitation (authors): Hot spots and knots in the radio lobes may be partially optically thick, which could affect the observed magnetic field orientation.  
  > "In addition the hot spots and knots may be partially optically thick, affecting the orientation of the observed magnetic field." (Polarized Emission at 1.4 GHz: Signatures of AGN Feedback?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing the dust emissivity index to beta = 2.0 directly controls the Rayleigh-Jeans slope and predicted 3.3 mm thermal dust flux; a slightly lower beta (e.g., 1.5-1.8) would increase the predicted 3.3 mm dust flux or alter the required dust temperature.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Off-axis instrumental polarization leakage in the JVLA L-band can be significant if the in-field NVSS calibrator (J102921+051938) or the target is far from the pointing center.
