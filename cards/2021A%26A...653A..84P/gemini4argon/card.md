## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive dust masses for individual ALPINE continuum-detected targets, non-detections, and serendipitous sources**: Single Modified Black Body (MBB) in optically thin regime evaluated at rest-frame 250 um (1.2 THz), extrapolated from observed Band 7 (rest-frame ~157 um) continuum flux  
  > "We derive the dust masses using a single Modified Black Body (MBB) curve, under the approximation of an optically thin regime (see Bianchi 2013)" (3. Dust mass estimates); "Dust masses are measured from the continuum emission at 250 um (see Gilli et al. 2014), extrapolated from the 157 um ALMA data, assuming a mass-weighted temperature of 25 K and a dust emissivity of beta=1.8" (6. Summary)
   - parameter: T = 25 K (fiducial), tested T = 20 K to 35 K  
  > "For the temperature of the dust, we assume T= 25 K." (3. Dust mass estimates); "For the temperarure of the cold dust, we explore a range of values, from T=20 K up to T=35 K." (3. Dust mass estimates)
   - parameter: k_nu0 = 4 cm^2 g^-1 at nu0 = 1.2 THz (lambda_rest = 250 um), beta = 1.8  
  > "We adopt k_nu0 = 4 cm^2 g^-1 with nu0 =1.2 THz (see Bianchi 2013) and beta = 1.8, i.e. the Galactic value from the Planck data (Planck Collaboration et al. 2011)." (3. Dust mass estimates)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission at rest-frame 250 um  
  > "Considering the rest-frame frequency assumed to compute S_nu_obs, we have considered 1.2 THz, corresponding to lambda_rest = 250 um (see Gilli et al. 2014). At this wavelength our galaxies are expected to be optically thin" (3. Dust mass estimates)
   - assumption: Single diffuse cold dust component dominates the dust mass budget  
  > "The adopted method relies on the assumption that the diffuse cold dust component accounts for the bulk of the dust budget." (3. Dust mass estimates)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_lambda = 0.7, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-3  
  > "Throughout the paper, we assume a LambdaCDM cosmology with Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_lambda = 0.7 and H_0 =70 km s^-1 Mpc^-3." (1. Introduction)
   - fitting: Analytical evaluation of single MBB equation; error bars computed by summing in quadrature relative uncertainties on 157 um flux (~25%), beta (~15%), and band conversion (~30%)  
  > "We calculate the error bars on the dust masses by summing in quadrature the relative uncertainties on the 157 um continuum flux (~25%), the systematic uncertainties due to the beta emissivity (~15%) and to the band conversion emissivity (~30%)." (3. Dust mass estimates)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to beta (1.5 to 2.0 changes mass by <15%), temperature (T=20 K changes by 20%, T=35 K decreases by 60%), and rest-frame reference wavelength (250 um vs 850 um gives unchanged masses)  
  > "we test how extreme values of beta=1.5 (beta=2) would affect our dust mass estimates finding an increase (decrease) of the dust masses by a negligible factor of less than ~15% for beta=1.5 (beta=2)" (3. Dust mass estimates); "A temperature of T=20 K would increase the dust masses by a factor within the uncertainties considered (20%), while assuming T=35 K would produce a decrease of the order of 60%." (3. Dust mass estimates); "In fact, we have tested that our dust masses do not change if one considers 250 or 850 um and if the flux S_nu_obs in Eq. 1 is derived by means of an extrapolation at higher frequencies." (3. Dust mass estimates)
2. **Compare observed dust scaling relations with chemical evolution models**: Compare M_dust, M_gas, SFR, M_stars, and age of the 23 continuum-detected targets (and SFR vs age for non-detections) against chemical evolution tracks for proto-spheroids (PSPH) and spiral galaxies  
  > "In Fig. 2 we compare estimates derived for the galaxies of our sample with results obtained with the chemical evolution models presented in Sect. 4.1." (4.2. Dust scaling relations)
   - software: LePhare
   - parameter: PSPH final baryonic masses of 3e10, 1e11, and 1e12 M_sun; spiral final stellar masses from 2e9 to 1e11 M_sun  
  > "The models describe three PSPH of final baryonic mass 3 x 10^10 M_sun, 10^11 M_sun and 10^12 M_sun." (4.1. Chemical evolution models); "The final stellar masses of the spirals range from 2 x 10^9 M_sun to 10^11 M_sun." (4.1. Chemical evolution models)
   - assumption: Rescale observed M_stars and SFR from Chabrier (2003) IMF to Salpeter (1955) IMF by multiplying by 1.7  
  > "For consistency with the models, we rescale M_stars and SFR to a Salpeter (1955) IMF by multiplying the quantities by a factor of 1.7 (see Speagle et al. 2014)." (4.2. Dust scaling relations)
   - assumption: PSPH models tested with Salpeter (1955) IMF and Larson (1998) top-heavy IMF (m_c = 1.2 M_sun); spiral models use Scalo (1986) IMF  
  > "The stellar initial mass function (IMF) assumed for spirals is the one of Scalo (1986)" (4.1. Chemical evolution models); "For the IMF of PSPH we test two different forms, i.e. the Salpeter (1955) IMF and the one of Larson (1998), a top-heavy IMF (THIMF)." (4.1. Chemical evolution models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked against age of the Universe at z = 4.5 and 5.5, independent morphological classifications (Le Fèvre et al. 2020), and continuum non-detections in the SFR-age plane  
  > "In fact, in the extreme and unlikely case that the ages are all systematically underestimated, they can not be larger than the age of the Universe at the redshift of our sample" (4.2. Dust scaling relations); "A further indication supporting our conclusion, is the galaxy classification of the ALPINE targets, performed by Le Fèvre et al. (2020)" (4.2. Dust scaling relations); "In Fig. 2 (panel c)), we report the SFR as a function of the age also for the ALPINE galaxies not detected in continuum (empty squares)." (4.2. Dust scaling relations)
3. **Stack ALMA continuum images in redshift and UV luminosity bins**: Median stacking of rest-frame 158 um continuum images centered on UV counterpart positions, including both detections and non-detections, split into two redshift bins (z < 5 and z > 5) and three L_UV bins (~20 sources per bin)  
  > "We perform stacking using the lambda_158um rest-frame continuum images centered on the UV counterpart positions, including both individual detections and non-detection in order to avoid biases." (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies); "We use median stacking on the UV counterpart positions of both detected and non detected objects." (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies); "We performed the stacking in two redshift bins, at z < 5 and z> 5, and we further split the sample in bins of UV luminosity." (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies)
   - parameter: Detection threshold 3.5 sigma; 3 sigma upper limits for non-detections (3 * background RMS + local maximum); L_UV bins: 10.4-10.9, 10.9-11.1, 11.1-11.6 log(L_sun)  
  > "As for the detections, we adopt a 3.5sigma threshold. In case of non detection, we use the conservative 3sigma upper limits by adding three times the background RMS to the local maximum (see Béthermin et al. 2020.)" (5.1. ## Qualitative findings

- ALPINE galaxies have dust masses, gas masses, and SFRs consistent with intermediate- and low-mass proto-spheroid (PSPH) chemical evolution models (precursors of local ellipticals or spiral bulges) rather than disc galaxy models.  
  > "The comparison of the observed dust scaling relations with chemical evolution models suggests that ALPINE galaxies are not likely progenitors of disc galaxies, but of intermediate and low mass proto-spheroids, resulting in present-day bulges of spiral or elliptical galaxies." (abstract)
- A standard Salpeter IMF is generally sufficient to explain the dust masses of ALPINE galaxies in proto-spheroid models without requiring a top-heavy IMF, except for a couple of the most dust-massive galaxies.  
  > "A couple of galaxies with particularly high M_dust are reproduced by the PSPH models with a THIMF (green dashed-dotted lines)." (4.2. Dust scaling relations); "In most cases, a Salpeter IMF is sufficient to account for the observed dust masses, i.e. in general there is no particular need to invoke mechanisms such as a top-heavy IMF to enhance dust production" (4.2. Dust scaling relations)
- The cosmic dust mass density at z ~ 5 derived from UV-selected galaxies is ~30% of that derived from blind FIR-selected galaxies, demonstrating that UV selection misses the most dust-rich, UV-obscured galaxies.  
  > "The DMD obtained at z ~ 5 from UV-selected sources is ~30 % of the value obtained from blind far-IR selected sources, showing that the UV-selection misses the most dust-rich, UV-obscured galaxies." (abstract)
- The phenomenological model of Gioannini et al. (2017) best reproduces the cosmic evolution of the dust mass density from z ~ 5 to the local Universe, whereas cosmological semi-analytical and hydrodynamic models overestimate the low-redshift (z < 1) dust mass density.  
  > "The phenomenological model from Gioannini et al. (2017) is the model that best reproduces the observed evolution of rho_dust." (6. Summary); "On the other hand, the ab-initio cosmological, semi-analytical models of Popping et al. (2017) and Vijayan et al. (2019) and and the cosmological simulations of Li et al. (2019) roughly account for the observed evolution of rho_dust dust at z > 1, but they overestimate the dust mass budget at lower redshift, failing in reproducing the decreasing trend observed at z < 1." (6. Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALPINE provides the first statistically robust sample of 118 UV-selected normal star-forming galaxies at 4.4 < z < 5.9 with high sensitivity and spatial resolution, avoiding the source confusion of earlier FIR instruments.  
  > "The dust content of normal galaxies and the dust mass density (DMD) at high-z (z > 4) are unconstrained given the source confusion and the sensitivity limitations of previous observations. The ALMA Large Program to INvestigate [CII] at Early Times (ALPINE), which targeted 118 UV-selected star-forming galaxies at 4.4<z<5.9, provides a new opportunity to tackle this issue for the first time with a statistically robust dataset." (abstract)
- Why this method: Single optically thin cold MBB (T=25 K, beta=1.8) is used because cold diffuse dust dominates the total dust mass budget; median stacking is required to include the ~80% continuum non-detections without bias; proxy LF convolution is required because ALPINE is not a volume-limited sample.  
  > "The adopted method relies on the assumption that the diffuse cold dust component accounts for the bulk of the dust budget." (3. Dust mass estimates); "Among the 118 ALPINE targets, 23 sources (~ 20% of the sample) are far-IR continuum detected and, as a consequence, for deriving the average properties of the parent UV-selected population, we need to take into account also the contribution of the non-detected galaxies." (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies); "since the ALPINE sample is not a volume-limited sample, to derive the comoving density of our physical property (M_dust), we use L_UV as proxy of M_dust" (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies)
- Alternative: Warmer mass-weighted dust temperature (T = 35 K instead of fiducial T = 25 K), which reduces derived dust masses and DMDs by 60%  
  > "A temperature of T=20 K would increase the dust masses by a factor within the uncertainties considered (20%), while assuming T=35 K would produce a decrease of the order of 60%." (3. Dust mass estimates)
- Alternative: Alternative dust emissivity index values (beta = 1.5 and beta = 2.0 instead of 1.8), which change dust masses by less than ~15%  
  > "we test how extreme values of beta=1.5 (beta=2) would affect our dust mass estimates finding an increase (decrease) of the dust masses by a negligible factor of less than ~15% for beta=1.5 (beta=2)" (3. Dust mass estimates)
- Alternative: Using SFR(SED) from UV/optical SED fitting instead of SFR(UV+IR), rejected because a few outliers have SFR(UV+IR) up to ~5 times higher than SFR(SED)  
  > "Anyway, since there are few outliers for which SFR(UV+IR) results to be a factor up to ~5 higher than SFR(SED), we prefer to consider the SFR(UV+IR) in the present analysis to properly account for dust-obscured SFR." (2. Observations)
- Alternative: Top-heavy IMF (Larson 1998) vs standard Salpeter (1955) IMF in proto-spheroid chemical evolution models  
  > "For the IMF of PSPH we test two different forms, i.e. the Salpeter (1955) IMF and the one of Larson (1998), a top-heavy IMF (THIMF)." (4.1. Chemical evolution models)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single band observation (ALMA Band 7, rest-frame ~157 um) requires assuming a fixed dust temperature (T = 25 K) and emissivity (beta = 1.8) and extrapolating to 250 um; if cold dust is at T = 35 K, dust masses and DMDs decrease by 60%.  
  > "Morever, we discuss the uncertainties on the derived dust masses related to three (non-independent) factors: the 'band-conversion', linked to the extrapolation of the 250 um flux from higher frequencies (i.e. 157 um), the emissivity beta and the temperature T." (3. Dust mass estimates); "As stated in Sec. 3, a warmer dust mass (T= 35 K) would reduce the DMDs by a factor of 60%." (5.3. Discussion on the DMD derivation)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar ages derived from SED fitting are highly uncertain, though bounded by the age of the Universe at z ~ 4.5-5.5.  
  > "We are aware that the age is one of the most uncertain parameters of the SED-fitting analysis (i.e., Thomas et al. 2017)." (4.2. Dust scaling relations)
- Limitation (authors): The linear fit of log(M_dust) vs log(L_UV) from stacked images has poor statistical significance (constrained at 1-2 sigma, p-value = 0.08) and must be extrapolated to fainter UV luminosities not sampled by ALPINE.  
  > "The fit results with a poor significance level with the coefficient constrained at 1-2sigma (Pearson rank coefficient of 0.76 and p-value of 0.08, estimated considering all data as detections)." (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies); "Two main caveats could affect our method and should be mentioned: first, we consider the ALPINE targets as a fair representative sample of the UV-sources; second, we extrapolate to a broader L_UV range, the log(M_dust)-log(L_UV) relation found for 10.4 <log(L_UV) < 11.5." (5.1. Contribution to the DMD from UV-selected galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): The IR-selected DMD determination has large uncertainty (~0.3-0.5 dex, up to 50%) due to the small sample (10 sources, 5 after removing overdensity candidates) used to constrain the IRLF.  
  > "The large uncertainty (up to 50%) affecting our determination of rho_dust for IR selected galaxies reflects the small sample used to estimate the IRLF." (5.3. Discussion on the DMD derivation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rest-frame 158 um is near the peak of the dust SED rather than deeply on the Rayleigh-Jeans tail, making single-band dust mass estimates highly sensitive to the assumed mass-weighted dust temperature.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The log(M_dust)-log(L_UV) regression is fit to only 6 stacked points (2 of which are upper limits), resulting in large slope uncertainty (+0.68/-0.57) that strongly affects the extrapolation to 0.03 L*.
