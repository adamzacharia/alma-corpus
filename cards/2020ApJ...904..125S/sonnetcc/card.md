## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum images**: Continuum images with robust and super-uniform weighting after pipeline calibration and self-calibration  
  > "After standard calibration, we additionally applied self-calibration to the measurements in order to improve the images." (Observations and Imaging); "We adopt npixels = 4 which optimizes the flux density among npixels = 2, 4, 6." (Footnote 1)
   - parameter: SU weighting with npixels = 4  
  > "We adopt npixels = 4 which optimizes the flux density among npixels = 2, 4, 6." (Footnote 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Test large-scale disk geometry with single-Gaussian azimuthal segments**: Robust image split into 10 deg azimuthal segments (36); single Gaussian fit to each radial profile; ellipse fit to peak locations assuming a circular disk  
  > "we split the continuum emission in segments of 10$$^\circ$$in azimuth to have a sufficient sampling rate." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry); "The profile of intensity versus radius for each segment can be fit with one Gaussian function." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry); "Subsequently, an ellipse is fit to the peak locations, which allows us to determine the disk geometry assuming that the disk is circular." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry)
   - parameter: 10 deg segments, 36 segments  
  > "we split the continuum emission in segments of 10$$^\circ$$in azimuth to have a sufficient sampling rate." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry)
   - assumption: Dust ring is circular  
  > "an ellipse is fit to the peak locations, which allows us to determine the disk geometry assuming that the disk is circular." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per segment; ellipse to peak positions (algorithm not stated)  
  > "The profile of intensity versus radius for each segment can be fit with one Gaussian function." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry)
   - validation: Compared with gas-kinematics geometry; checked other numbers of segments  
  > "We note that we have compared this analysis for different numbers of segments, and the results are consistent (within the uncertainties) with the ones presented in the following." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry)
3. **Fit two Gaussians to radial profiles of the de-projected SU image**: De-project SU image (PA 62 deg, i = 21 deg), split into 72 segments of 5 deg, fit inner and outer Gaussians to each radial profile; use amplitudes and positions  
  > "We first de-project the continuum image with the major axis of the disk-plane of 62$$^\circ$$, which is consistent with the value used in (cite: 2018Boehler), and the inclination of 21$$^\circ$$" (Three Structural Continuum Features); "we split the continuum emission into 72 segments where every segment is 5$$^\circ$$in azimuth." (Three Structural Continuum Features); "the radial profiles of the segments in the de-projected SU-weighted image are better described by two-Gaussian functions" (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - parameter: 5 deg segments, 72 segments; 95% confidence error bars  
  > "we split the continuum emission into 72 segments where every segment is 5$$^\circ$$in azimuth." (Three Structural Continuum Features); "error bars covering the 95\% confidence level." (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - assumption: Inclination 21 deg and major axis 62 deg for de-projection  
  > "We first de-project the continuum image with the major axis of the disk-plane of 62$$^\circ$$, which is consistent with the value used in (cite: 2018Boehler), and the inclination of 21$$^\circ$$" (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - fitting: Two-Gaussian fit per segment (sampler/optimizer, priors not stated)  
  > "the radial profiles of the segments in the de-projected SU-weighted image are better described by two-Gaussian functions" (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - validation: Compared 2- and 3-Gaussian fits for azimuth 140-165 deg  
  > "Hence, the main conclusions in this paper remain based on the 2-Gaussian fits while acknowledging the possibility of a 3-Gaussian structure in this azimuth range." (Rings and Spirals?)
4. **Identify spiral-like arms from azimuthal trends**: Arms 1, 2, 3 identified by comparing positions and amplitudes of the inner and outer Gaussians as a function of azimuth; pitch angles estimated from radial shift over azimuth range  
  > "In order to identify the starting and ending of a feature, we compare positions and amplitudes" (Three Structural Continuum Features); "This first structural continuum feature is characterized by its peak positions moving outward from about 0.45\ensuremath{''} to 0.60\ensuremath{''} over a range in azimuth of 115$$^\circ$$with a pitch angle of about 7$$^\circ$$." (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - parameter: Arm 2: 0.3'' to 0.4'' over 115 deg, pitch angle ~9 deg  
  > "Over a range of 115$$^\circ$$in azimuth, the peak positions move outward from 0.3\ensuremath{''} to 0.4\ensuremath{''} with a pitch angle of$\~$9$$^\circ$$." (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - parameter: Arm 1: 0.34'' to 0.49'' over ~140 deg, pitch angle ~10 deg  
  > "move outward from about 0.34\ensuremath{''} to 0.49\ensuremath{''}" (Three Structural Continuum Features); "with a pitch angle of$\~$10$$^\circ$$." (Three Structural Continuum Features)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Identification said to be robust for a high threshold of 10 (in units of noise) where fit uncertainties are large  
  > "We note that the identification of the structural features as detailed out in the following is not based on weak or marginal features in low SN data" (Three Structural Continuum Features)
5. **Check gravitational stability and turbulence at the two brightest peaks**: Toomre Q from brightness temperature, optical depth, surface density, sound speed and orbital frequency; 13CO linewidths compared with sound speeds  
  > "With gas temperatures of 34 K and 78 K (cite: 2018Boehler), the optical depths are estimated to be 0.54 and 0.19 at the locations of the brightest and second brightest emission." (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence); "The level of turbulence at the locations of the two brightest emission peaks is tested by comparing their linewidths of$\^13$CO with their sound speeds." (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence)
   - parameter: Dust opacity 2.74 cm^2 g^-1; gas-to-dust ratio 100 (also considered ~10)  
  > "assuming a dust opacity of 2.74" (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence); "Adopting a gas-to-dust ratio of 100, the values for Q are reduced to 2 and 26." (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence)
   - assumption: Gas temperatures 34 K and 78 K from literature; gas-to-dust ratio 100 as the baseline  
  > "With gas temperatures of 34 K and 78 K (cite: 2018Boehler)" (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence); "Adopting a gas-to-dust ratio of 100, the values for Q are reduced to 2 and 26." (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Considered lower gas-to-dust ratios of order 10; Q stays above 1  
  > "Given these gas-to-dust ratios of the order of 10, values for Q at the two brightest emission locations remain larger than 1" (Gravitational Stability and Turbulence)
6. **Fit spiral density-wave model to each arm**: WKB spiral wave model (Rafikov 2002); three free parameters (planet azimuth, radius, aspect ratio) fit to each arm; two solution sets found  
  > "The model was first introduced by (cite: 2002Rafikov), using a WKB approximation." (Modeling of Spiral Features); "are determined by a best-fit via a downhill simplex method to each of the three features" (Modeling of Spiral Features); "We find two sets of best-fit results which fit the data similarly well." (Modeling of Spiral Features)
   - parameter: Aspect-ratio upper bound 0.2; solutions with h_c above 0.2 discarded  
  > "consequently, the models with $h_c$ larger than 0.2 are discarded." (Modeling of Spiral Features)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation; second index 0.45 following Andrews 2011; linear / weakly non-linear regime  
  > "We fix$$=1.5 (i.e., we assume that the disk is in Keplerian rotation)" (Modeling of Spiral Features); "One possible reason for this is that the spiral wave model is only suitable for the linear and weakly non-linear regime." (Modeling of Spiral Features)
   - fitting: Downhill simplex minimisation; uncertainties shown at 95% confidence level (likelihood and priors not stated)  
  > "are determined by a best-fit via a downhill simplex method to each of the three features" (Modeling of Spiral Features); "with a 95\% confidence level interval shown with the black arcs." (Figure 5 caption)
   - validation: Fitted h_c compared with an independent estimate from the sound speed (0.07 to 0.09) and with NIR values  
  > "values for $h_c$ become larger with $\sim$0.07 to 0.09 across the MWC 758 disk." (Modeling of Spiral Features)
7. **Compare arms with NIR, VLA and higher-resolution ALMA results**: Overlay peak locations on the NIR image and on the 0.04'' ALMA image of Dong et al.; compare 2- versus 3-Gaussian fits  
  > "We overlay the peak locations obtained from our submm analysis on the NIR image in Figure [3](#fig:NIR+Gauss2)" (Submm versus NIR Emission); "Overlaid are the three structural features and the peak positions identified in this paper." (Rings and Spirals?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 3-Gaussian fits for 140 to 165 deg shift the second component ~0.03'' outward and do not change arm 1 identification  
  > "and the original identification of submm arm 1 based on the 2-Gaussian fit would not be affected by this." (Rings and Spirals?)

## Qualitative findings

- Single-Gaussian fits of the robust image do not recover the known gas-disk geometry, so the 0.9 mm emission does not trace a circular structure  
  > "the continuum emission observed at 0.9 mm is not tracing a circular structure." (Large-Scale Continuum Disk Geometry)
- Arm 1 and arm 2 have NIR counterparts; arm 3 does not  
  > "While two of the submm spirals (arm 1 and arm 2) have counterparts in the NIR image, the third one (arm 3) is absent at NIR wavelengths." (Summary and Conclusion)
- Northern 9 mm VLA clump coincides with the northern tip of arm 3; no clear radial shift near the southern clump  
  > "This clump seen at 9 mm is at the northern tip of arm 3 seen at 0.9 mm." (Correspondance with 9mm Dust Trapping Vortices?)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SU weighting gives a sharper image that reveals additional structure  
  > "provides a sharper image revealing additional important features." (Summary and Conclusion)
- Why this method: Azimuthal segments preserve radial trends that azimuthal averaging would erase  
  > "our analysis relies on splitting the disk into segments by azimuth, so that we can obtain information on the azimuthal structure of the disk" (Rings and Spirals?)
- Alternative: Models with h_c larger than 0.2 rejected because they overproduce infrared flux in the SED fit  
  > "consequently, the models with $h_c$ larger than 0.2 are discarded." (Modeling of Spiral Features)
- Alternative: Three-Gaussian fits tested for azimuth 140-165 deg; two-Gaussian retained  
  > "Hence, the main conclusions in this paper remain based on the 2-Gaussian fits while acknowledging the possibility of a 3-Gaussian structure in this azimuth range." (Rings and Spirals?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Spiral wave model valid only in the linear and weakly non-linear regime; low-h_c fit lower than independent estimate  
  > "One possible reason for this is that the spiral wave model is only suitable for the linear and weakly non-linear regime." (Modeling of Spiral Features)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot distinguish single twisted spiral from ring plus outer spiral for arm 1  
  > "we are not able to unambiguously distinguish this scenario" (Rings and Spirals?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Arm identification is by eye from Gaussian-fit trends; no objective criterion or false-positive test
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Resolution (0.11-0.19'') is coarse versus segment size and the quoted resolution varies in the text (0.18, 0.12, 0.11)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spiral fit has two degenerate solutions and one pinned at a prior bound; no sampler or posterior shown
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Toomre Q uses literature temperatures and a dust opacity of unknown applicability
