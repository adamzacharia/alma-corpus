## Analysis recipe

1. **Set the distance**: Invert the Gaia DR2 parallax and combine the parallax and pulsation distance estimates.  
  > "We can therefore simply invert the parallax to provide $d = \varpi^{-1} = 1851^{+204}_{-184}$ pc." (The distance to RU Vul); "we adopt a fixed distance of $d = 2000 \pm 165$ pc, based on the combination of pulsation and parallax data, which offer two independent distance estimates." (The distance to RU Vul)
   - assumption: Fixed d = 2000 ± 165 pc  
  > "we adopt a fixed distance of $d = 2000 \pm 165$ pc" (The distance to RU Vul)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pulsation and parallax distances agree within uncertainties.  
  > "the distances from the pre-decline pulsation period ($2070 \pm 130$ pc; Section [2.1.1](#PropBackDiscSect); (cite: UGT16)) and parallax (*Gaia*) agree within uncertainties" (The distance to RU Vul)
2. **Derive spectroscopic stellar parameters**: Fit Gaussian equivalent widths of Fe I/II lines; analyse with moog abfind and atlas9; iteratively remove abundance trends.  
  > "A standard equivalent-width analysis of Fe i and Fe ii lines was performed, and Gaussian profiles were fit to lines present in the continuum-normalised spectra." (Spectral synthesis); "The resulting equivalent widths were analysed using the `abfind` task in moog[^8] (cite: Sneden73), and compared to $\alpha$-element-enhanced stellar atmosphere models (atlas9; (cite: Kurucz93))." (Spectral synthesis); "Models were tuned using a nested, iterative process, to derive three atmospheric parameters: $v_{\rm t}$, $T_{\rm eff}$ and \[Fe/H\]." (Spectral synthesis)
   - software: moog, abfind, atlas9, bt-settl
   - parameter: λ > 6000 Å; blended lines fit with multiple Gaussian components  
  > "Lines were restricted to $\lambda > 6000$ Å, as shorter wavelengths have much lower signal, causing scatter in the derived parameters." (Spectral synthesis); "Blended lines were fit with multiple Gaussian components." (Spectral synthesis)
   - assumption: log(g) = 0 dex; α-element-enhanced atmospheres; solar abundances from (cite: AGSS09)  
  > "Assuming log($g$) = 0 dex, a fit is found for $T_{\rm eff} = 3620$ K, with \[Fe/H\] = –1.15 dex and $v_{\rm t} = 1.59$ km s$^{-1}$." (Spectral synthesis); "compared to $\alpha$-element-enhanced stellar atmosphere models (atlas9; (cite: Kurucz93))." (Spectral synthesis); "Solar abundances from (cite: AGSS09) were used to convert the absolute ($\epsilon$) abundances to relative (square-bracket notation) abundances." (Spectral synthesis)
   - fitting: Adjust vt against equivalent-width trends, Teff against excitation-potential trends and [Fe/H] to average ε(Fe).  
  > "First, $v_{\rm t}$ was adjusted to minimise the correlation between equivalent width and $\epsilon$." (Spectral synthesis); "Secondly, $T_{\rm eff}$ was adjusted to minimise the correlation between excitation potential and $\epsilon$, then $v_{\rm t}$ adjusted accordingly." (Spectral synthesis); "Finally, the model \[Fe/H\] is adjusted to match the average $\epsilon$(Fe)" (Spectral synthesis)
   - validation: Test higher log(g), inspect Fe I abundance scatter and visually compare bt-settl TiO spectra; visual comparison disagrees.  
  > "These values are relatively stable against excursions to higher log($g$)" (Spectral synthesis); "The standard deviation in iron abundance among the 37 Fe i lines in the final best fit is 0.278 dex." (Spectral synthesis); "This is in contrast to the above analysis and the fitting by (cite: UGT16) of the 705 nm TiO bandhead." (A visual check)
3. **Fit the stellar SED**: Fit Hipparcos mean and 2MASS JHKs photometry independently of the spectroscopic parameters; derive luminosity and radius.  
  > "To ensure a fully independent derivation from the spectroscopic determination, we do not use the metallicity, temperature or surface gravity derived in the fit." (SED fitting); "photometry restricted to the *Hipparcos* mean magnitude (cite: vanLeeuwen07) and the 2MASS $JHK_{\rm s}$ magnitudes (cite: SCS+06)" (SED fitting); "Via $L \propto R^2 T^4$, this therefore provides $R_\ast = 142 \pm 14$ R$_\odot$ (0.66 $\pm$ 0.07 AU)." (SED fitting)
   - software: code of (cite: MvLD+09), with additions of (cite: MZB12) and (cite: MZW17)
   - assumption: [Fe/H] = –1.6 dex; M = 0.6 M⊙; d = 2 kpc  
  > "Instead, we assume the original \[Fe/H\] = –1.6 dex from (cite: UGT16)." (SED fitting); "To set the model’s surface gravity, we further assume $M$ = 0.6 M$_\odot$ and $d = 2$ kpc." (SED fitting)
   - assumption: E(B−V) = 0.096 mag; (cite: Draine03) reddening law  
  > "We also assumed $E(B-V) = 0.096$ mag (cite: SF11) and a (cite: Draine03) reddening law." (SED fitting)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Vary metallicity, mass and reddening; compare photometric and spectroscopic temperatures.  
  > "assuming \[Fe/H\] = –1.0 dex raises the fitted parameters by 27 K and 13 L$_\odot$" (SED fitting); "assuming $M = 0.9$ M$_\odot$ has no effect on temperature but decreases the luminosity by 7 L$_\odot$" (SED fitting); "the close agreement with the spectroscopic temperature (3639 K versus 3620 K) suggests that reddening by circumstellar dust is negligible." (SED fitting)
4. **Constrain the evolutionary state and mass**: Compare observed stellar properties with existing mesa tracks, exploring metallicity and initial mass.  
  > "we compared our observed properties of RU Vul to mesa (cite: PBD+11; PCA+13) stellar evolution models computed for (cite: MZ15b)." (Stellar evolution modelling); "Two parameters are explored: metallicity and mass" (Stellar evolution modelling)
   - software: mesa
   - assumption: Globular-cluster-calibrated mass loss; Population II remnant mass approximately 0.53 ± 0.02 M⊙

## Qualitative findings

- First CO J=3→2 detection around a truly metal-poor evolved star  
  > "We report the first detection of CO $J = 3 \rightarrow 2$ around a truly metal-poor evolved star." (Abstract)
- CO J=2→1 is treated as a probable non-detection  
  > "We treat it as a probable non-detection in further discussion but allow for its possible contribution to the 222 GHz continuum flux." (Line detections)
- Current circumstellar material need not be an outflowing wind  
  > "we cannot *conclusively* state whether the material currently around the star represents an outflowing wind or material orbiting in the circumstellar environment." (A summary of the evidence)
- Metallic iron favoured over amorphous carbon as the continuum opacity source  
  > "We therefore favour metallic iron as the underlying continuum dust opacity source for RU Vul over amorphous carbon." (Dust mineralogy)
- Aspherical or clumpy dust geometry leaves a clear line of sight to the star  
  > "This implies an aspherical or clumpy geometry to the wind that affords us a clear line of sight towards the stellar surface." (Optical dust absorption and dust inhomogeneity)
- A binary-shaped disc is possible but unconvincing  
  > "Consequently, while there is a reasonable possibility that a binary companion could be shaping a disc around RU Vul, it is not convincing at this time." (Is RU Vul a binary?)
- Rapid dust formation is proposed as a consequence of stellar fading during the thermal pulse  
  > "we suggest it is a consequence of rapid, clumpy dust formation that is occurring as the star fades, following the extinguishment of hydrogen burning by the thermal pulse." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: RU Vul's unusually emissive dust envelope made it the best metal-poor star detectable by ALMA.  
  > "Its unusually emissive dust envelope is the reason we targetted it, as best metal-poor star detectable by ALMA." (A summary of the evidence)
- Why this method: By-eye dusty fits because parameters are highly correlated; independent photometric fitting to check spectroscopy.  
  > "The resulting fits are heavily parameterised, and parameters are highly correlated, meaning a fit is best achieved “by eye”."
