## Analysis recipe

1. **Stack molecular lines by upper energy level and produce total intensity maps and PV diagrams**: Divide detected lines into two ranges of upper level energies (E_u < 120 K and E_u > 120 K), stack them to produce mean channel maps, then integrate to get total line intensity maps and extract position-velocity (PV) diagrams across the upper and lower disk atmospheres  
  > "In order to increase the sensitivity for better detections, we divided them into 2 ranges of upper level energies: $E_u < 120$ K and $E_u > 120 K$, and then stacked them to produce the mean channel maps, and then the total line intensity maps, and the position-velocity (PV) diagrams." (Results); "The spatio-kinematic relationship among these molecules can be studied with the PV diagrams cut across the upper and lower disk atmospheres, as shown in Figure [\[fig:pv_atms\]](#fig:pv_atms)." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: E_u < 120 K and E_u > 120 K energy bins  
  > "we divided them into 2 ranges of upper level energies: $E_u < 120$ K and $E_u > 120 K$" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure vertical heights and outer disk radii of molecules**: Measure vertical height along the jet axis in the lower atmosphere using E_u < 120 K maps; determine outer disk radius from linear PV structures in the upper disk atmosphere where they intersect the Keplerian rotation curve  
  > "We can also measure the vertical height of these molecules (using lines with $E_u < 120$ K) along the jet axis in the lower atmosphere where the emission is brighter, and find it to be $\sim$ 15, 19, 20, 24, and 26 au, respectively for NH$_2$CHO, HNCO, CH$_3$CHO, CH$_3$OH, and H$_2$CO." (Stratified Distribution of Molecules); "From the location of their PV structure on the Keplerian curve, we find that the disk radius of these molecules increases from $\sim$ 24 au for NH$_2$CHO/HNCO/t-HCOOH, to $\sim$ 36 au for CH$_3$CHO, to $\sim$ 40 au for CH$_3$OH/D$_2$CO, and then to $\sim$ 48 au for H$_2$CO." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: Lines with E_u < 120 K  
  > "Here we use the emission with $E_u < 120$ K, where all molecules are detected." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation around a central mass of ~ 0.25 M_sun  
  > "Previously, the disk was found to be rotating roughly with a Keplerian rotation due a central mass of $\sim$ 0.25 $M_\odot$ (including the masses of the central protostar and the disk) (cite: Codella2014; Lee2017COM)." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Distance of ~ 400 pc  
  > "The hot corino in the HH 212 protostellar system (cite: Codella2016) in Orion at a distance of $\sim$ 400 pc is particularly interesting" (Introduction)
   - fitting: Linear PV structures matched to Keplerian rotation curve  
  > "In the upper disk atmosphere, their emissions form roughly linear PV structures (as marked by the magenta lines), indicating that they arise from rings rotating at certain velocities." (Kinematics)
   - validation: Comparison of vertical height trend with outer radius trend expected for a flared disk in hydrostatic equilibrium  
  > "This trend is the same as the increasing order of the vertical height measured earlier for these molecules, indicating that the height increases with increasing radius, as expected for a flared disk in hydrostatic equilibrium." (Kinematics)
3. **Derive CH3OH physical properties via non-LTE LVG modeling and rotation diagram**: Non-LTE Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) modeling of 7 CH3OH lines with E_u < 200 K at the lower disk atmosphere peak using GRELVG, combined with LTE rotation diagram linear fit of 5 CH3OH lines with E_u > 200 K, then taking the mean of the two methods  
  > "In order to understand the nature and spatial origin of the detected methanol, we analyzed the observed methanol lines (Table 1) via a non-LTE Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) approach, using the code GRELVG, initially developed by (cite: Ceccarelli2003)." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "We also derived the excitation temperature and column density from rotation diagram using the remaining five transition lines with $E_u > 200$K (see Figure [\[fig:LVG\]](#fig:LVG)c), assuming optically thin emission and LTE (cite: Goldsmith1999)." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "Taking the mean values from the two methods, we have $T\sim 92^{+48}_{-37}$ K and N(CH$_3$OH)$=1.5^{+4.5}_{-0.8}\times 10^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
   - software: GRELVG, BASECOL
   - parameter: N(A-CH3OH) and N(E-CH3OH) varied from 2x10^15 to 1x10^19 cm^-2 (step factor of 2); n_H2 from 10^6 to 10^9 cm^-3 (step factor of 2); T from 50 to 120 K (step of 5 K); linewidth = 4 km s^-1  
  > "Specifically, we varied the methanol column density N(A-CH$_3$OH) and N(E-CH$_3$OH) simultaneously from $2\times 10^{15}$ to $1\times 10^{19}$ cm$^{-2}$ (with a step of a factor of 2), the H$_2$ density $n_{\textrm{\scriptsize H}_2}$ from $10^{6}$ to $10^{9}$ cm$^{-3}$ (with a step of a factor of 2) and the gas temperature T from 50 to 120 K (with a step of 5 K)." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "To compute the line escape probability as a function of the line optical depth we adopted the semi-infinite slab geometry (cite: Scoville1974) and a linewidth equal to 4 km s$^{-1}$, following the observations." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
   - assumption: A-/E- CH3OH ratio = 1; semi-infinite slab geometry; 40% measurement uncertainty; optically thin and LTE for E_u > 200 K lines  
  > "We assumed an A-/E- CH$_3$OH ratio equal to 1. To compute the line escape probability as a function of the line optical depth we adopted the semi-infinite slab geometry (cite: Scoville1974)" (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "where the errors are estimated considering the 1$\sigma$ confidence level and the uncertainties of $\sim$ 40% in our measurements" (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "We also derived the excitation temperature and column density from rotation diagram using the remaining five transition lines with $E_u > 200$K (see Figure [\[fig:LVG\]](#fig:LVG)c), assuming optically thin emission and LTE (cite: Goldsmith1999)." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
   - fitting: Grid sampling of chi^2 surface for LVG; linear equation fit for rotation diagram  
  > "We ran several grids of models to sample the $\chi^2$ surface in the parameter space." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "In particular, we fit the data with a linear equation, and then derived the temperature from the negative reciprocal of the slope and the column density from the y-intercept." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
   - validation: Consistency between non-LTE LVG (E_u < 200 K) and LTE rotation diagram (E_u > 200 K) results  
  > "We found that $T\sim 109\pm31$ K and N(CH$_3$OH)$=(1.4\pm0.7)\times 10^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$. Taking the mean values from the two methods, we have $T\sim 92^{+48}_{-37}$ K and N(CH$_3$OH)$=1.5^{+4.5}_{-0.8}\times 10^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
4. **Derive excitation temperatures, column densities, and abundances for NH2CHO, HNCO, H2CO, D2CO, H2(13)CO, and CH3CHO**: Measure integrated line intensities averaged over a 68 au x 20 au (0.17" x 0.05") rectangular region in the lower atmosphere with a 2 sigma cutoff; fit LTE rotation diagrams for NH2CHO, HNCO, and CH3CHO (with T fixed to 100 K); calculate column densities for H2CO, D2CO, and H2(13)CO at assumed temperatures; divide by model H2 column density to obtain abundances  
  > "Table 1 lists the integrated line intensities averaged over a rectangular region (with a size of 68 au $\times$ 20 au) that covers most of the emission in the lower atmosphere, measured with a cutoff of 2$\sigma$." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "For NH$_2$CHO and HNCO, we fit the data points to obtain the temperature and column density." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "In addition, the abundance of these molecules are also estimated by dividing the column density of the molecules by the mean H$_2$ column density derived from a dusty disk model (cite: Lee2021Pdisk) in the same region, which is found to be $\sim$ 1.08$\times 10^{25}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
   - parameter: Rectangular region size 68 au x 20 au (0.17" x 0.05"); cutoff of 2 sigma  
  > "Table 1 lists the integrated line intensities averaged over a rectangular region (with a size of 68 au $\times$ 20 au) that covers most of the emission in the lower atmosphere, measured with a cutoff of 2$\sigma$." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "they are the mean values averaging over a rectangular region (with a size of $0^{\prime\prime}_{^\textrm{.}}17$$\times$$0^{\prime\prime}_{^\textrm{.}}05$ covering most of the emission) centered at the lower atmosphere." (Table 1)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and LTE; T_ex = 92 K for D2CO, 60 K for H2CO and H2(13)CO, 100 K for CH3CHO; [12C]/[13C] = 50; H2 column density = 1.08 x 10^25 cm^-2; 40% line intensity uncertainty  
  > "Since D$_2$CO has roughly the same radial extent as CH$_3$OH, it is assumed to have an excitation temperature of 92 K, the same as that found for CH$_3$OH. Since H$_2$CO has a slightly larger radius than CH$_3$OH, it and its $^{13}$C isotopologue H$_2$$^{13}$CO are assumed to have an excitation of 60 K. CH$_3$CHO has a smaller radial extent than CH$_3$OH and is thus assumed to have an excitation temperature of 100 K." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "Indeed, the H$_2$CO column density can be better derived from the H$_2$$^{13}$CO line assuming \[$^{12}$C\]/\[$^{13}$C\] ratio of $\sim$ 50, as estimated in the Orion Complex (cite: Kahane2018)." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "The line intensities here are assumed to have an uncertainty of 40%." (Table 1)
   - fitting: Linear fit to rotation diagram (blended NH2CHO lines and lowest E_u optically thick HNCO line excluded; slope fixed for CH3CHO)  
  > "The blended lines of NH$_2$CHO are excluded from the diagram. The HNCO line at the lowest $E_u$ (marked with an open square) seems to be optically thick with an intensity much lower than the line next down the $E_u$ axis, and is thus excluded from the fitting." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere); "As for CH$_3$CHO, we fixed its temperature to 100 K by fixing the negative reciprocal of the slope in the linear equation and then derived its column density from the y-intercept of the linear fit to the rotation diagram" (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
   - validation: Comparison of H2CO column density from main isotopologue vs H2(13)CO confirms main H2CO lines are optically thick  
  > "As can be seen from Table 2, the H$_2$CO column density derived this way is $\sim$ 3 times that derived from the H$_2$CO lines." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)

## Qualitative findings

- Organic molecules in the HH 212 disk atmosphere show a stratified radial and vertical distribution, with outer radius and height increasing from NH2CHO/HNCO, to CH3CHO, to CH3OH, and to H2CO.  
  > "Interestingly, we find a stratified distribution of these molecules, with the outer emission radius increasing from $\sim$ 24 au for NH$_2$CHO and HNCO, to 36 au for CH$_3$CHO, to 40 au for CH$_3$OH, and then to 48 au for H$_2$CO." (Abstract)
- NH2CHO, HNCO, D2CO, H2(13)CO, and CH3CHO trace mainly the disk atmosphere, whereas H2CO and CH3OH trace both the disk atmosphere and the disk wind.  
  > "In summary, NH$_2$CHO, HNCO, D$_2$CO, H$_2$$^{13}$CO and CH$_3$CHO trace mainly the disk atmosphere, while H$_2$CO and CH$_3$OH trace not only the disk atmosphere, but also the disk wind." (Stratified Distribution of Molecules)
- The lack of molecular emission in the disk midplane within the centrifugal barrier is explained by high dust continuum optical depth (tau >= 3 at 1.33 mm).  
  > "As can be seen, the optical depth is $\mathrel{\hbox{\rlap{\hbox{\lower 4pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}\hbox{$>$}}}$ 3 toward the midplane within the CB, where no molecular emission is detected, supporting this possibility." (Lack of Molecular Emission in Disk Midplane)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Band 6 (~ 1.33 mm) covers more NH2CHO lines along with HNCO, H2CO, D2CO, H2(13)CO, CH3OH, and CH3CHO, and has lower dust continuum optical depth than 0.85 mm, allowing molecular line mapping closer to the midplane and central source.  
  > "Here we map it at a longer wavelength $\lambda \sim$ 1.33 mm, with spectral windows set up to cover more NH$_2$CHO lines in order to derive the physical properties of NH$_2$CHO." (Introduction); "At longer wavelength, since the continuum emission of the disk is optically thinner, we can also map the molecular line emission in the disk atmosphere closer to the midplane and the central source." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Lines were stacked into two E_u bins to increase sensitivity for better detections; non-LTE LVG was used for CH3OH lines with E_u < 200 K because they are optically thick and previous LTE rotation diagram estimates were overestimated.  
  > "In order to increase the sensitivity for better detections, we divided them into 2 ranges of upper level energies: $E_u < 120$ K and $E_u > 120 K$, and then stacked them to produce the mean channel maps" (Results); "More importantly, the excitation temperature was also overestimated because almost all the lines had E$_u$ $<$ 200 K and were thus likely optically thick." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
- Alternative: HNCO and HCOOH coming from the decomposition of desorbed organic salts (NH4+OCN- and NH4+HCOO-) which have binding energies similar to NH2CHO.  
  > "On the other hand, HNCO and HCOOH may come from the decomposition of the desorbed organic salts (NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ and NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$), which have similar BE to that of NH$_2$CHO (cite: Kruckiewicz2021; Ligterink2018)." (Distribution of Molecules and Binding Energy)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Higher spectral and spatial resolution observations are needed to verify the possible ~ 0.5 km s^-1 velocity shift (infall/accretion) between the upper and lower disk atmospheres for NH2CHO/HNCO/t-HCOOH.  
  > "Note that for NH$_2$CHO/HNCO/t-HCOOH, there seems to be a small velocity shift of $\sim$ 0.5 km s$^{-1}$ between the upper and lower disk atmosphere. This velocity shift could suggest an infall (or accretion) velocity of $\sim$ 0.25 km s$^{-1}$, which is $\sim$ 8% of the rotation velocity at $\sim$ 24 au. However, observations at higher spectral and spatial resolution are needed to verify this possibility." (Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperatures of H2CO, D2CO, H2(13)CO, and CH3CHO cannot be derived from rotation diagrams due to narrow E_u range (< 120 K), few detected lines, and optical thickness, requiring assumed temperatures.  
  > "On the other hand, since H$_2$CO, D$_2$CO, and CH$_3$CHO are only detected with a narrow range of $E_u < 120$ K and their emission can be optically thick there, we can not derive their excitation temperature from the rotation diagram." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperatures derived for NH2CHO and HNCO have large uncertainties (~ 226 +/- 130 K).  
  > "It is interesting to note that NH$_2$CHO and HNCO have roughly the same excitation temperature of $\sim$ $226\pm130$ K, although with a large uncertainty." (Physical Properties in the Disk Atmosphere)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of observational information on NH2 prevents constraining whether the gas-phase reaction H2CO + NH2 -> NH2CHO + H is responsible for formamide formation.  
  > "Unfortunately, we have no information on the other reactant, NH$_2$." (Formamide, HNCO, and H2CO); "In conclusion, based on the current observations, it is not possible to constrain the formation route of formamide in the disk atmosphere of HH 212." (Formamide, HNCO, and H2CO)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Molecular abundances depend directly on the H2 column density adopted from an external dusty disk model (Lee et al. 2021b) rather than a direct gas mass tracer.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Column densities for H2CO, D2CO, H2(13)CO, and CH3CHO rely on fixed, assumed excitation temperatures (60 K, 92 K, 100 K).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line intensities are assumed to have a uniform 40% uncertainty, which dominates the error bars on derived column densities and temperatures.
