## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum and line cubes**: ALMA pipeline calibration; CASA clean with natural weighting and tapers; continuum from line-free data; cubes at 50/100 km/s  
  > "The data were reduced and imaged using the Common Astronomy Software Application package `CASA`" (2.2 Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: taper 1.0'' (SPT0529-54), 0.5'' (Cloverleaf); natural weighting  
  > "An outer taper of 1.0$^{\prime\prime}$ and 0.5$^{\prime\prime}$ is applied to SPT0529-54 and the Cloverleaf respectively" (2.2 Data Reduction and Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Manual inspection of pipeline reduction  
  > "We inspected the quality of the reduction manually and found no major problems." (2.2 Data Reduction and Imaging)
2. **Fit dust SED to get L_FIR**: Modified blackbody with MCMC to unresolved photometry, rest-frame >=50 um, beta fixed to 2; free amplitude, T_d, lambda_0  
  > "we fix $\beta$ to 2 and let the amplitude ($\rm \Omega/(1+z)^3$), $\rm T_{d}$ and $\lambda_{o}$ vary." (3.1 Estimating L_FIR)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: L_FIR integrated 42.5-122.5 um rest frame  
  > "total far-infrared luminosity ($\rm L_{FIR}$) from 42.5$-$122.5 $\rm \mu m$ in rest frame" (1 Introduction)
   - assumption: single T_d, uniform source; beta=2  
  > "this simplistic modified blackbody function applies only with the assumption that the entire source has a single T$\rm _{d}$ and that the source is uniform" (3.1 Estimating L_FIR)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC posterior sampling  
  > "We fit the modified blackbody function given by Equation 1 with Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm using the `emcee`" (3.1 Estimating L_FIR)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Resolved L_FIR per pixel**: Scale total L_FIR by each pixel's fraction of the continuum flux  
  > "the $\rm L_{FIR}$ for each pixel is obtained by scaling the $\rm L_{FIR}$ of the entire source using the flux contribution of each pixel to the total continuum flux" (3.1 Estimating L_FIR)
   - assumption: uniform dust temperature and opacity across source  
  > "we assume that the galaxies have uniform temperature and opacity distribution." (3.1 Estimating L_FIR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Extract spectra and line luminosity**: Apply continuum mask to each channel, integrate; error = std of line-free channels; non-parametric second-moment FWHM; L_H2O from Solomon & Vanden Bout relation; per-pixel from moment 0  
  > "We use the standard deviation of flux density in line-free channels as the error in each velocity bin." (3.3 Spectral Analysis)
   - parameter: L_H2O = 1.04e-3 I nu_rest D_L^2 (1+z)^-1  
  > "\rm L_{H_{2}O}  = (1.04 \times 10^{-3}) \ I_{H_{2}O} \ \nu_{rest}\ D_{L}^{2}\ (1+z)^{-1}" (3.3 Spectral Analysis)
   - assumption: Planck 2015 cosmology h=0.677, Om=0.307  
  > "We use Planck 2015 flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology where $h$ = 0.677, $\Omega_{m}$ = 0.307 and $\Omega_{\Lambda}$ = 0.693" (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mask recovers 95-100% of H2O flux  
  > "the mask selected from the continuum includes 95$-$100$\%$ of the total H$_{2}$O flux (depending on the source)" (2.2 Data Reduction and Imaging)
5. **Fit L_H2O-L_FIR correlation global and resolved**: MCMC fits of log L_H2O vs log L_FIR including literature; slope-fixed-to-zero fit of ratio; resolved pixels binned in Sigma_LFIR; F-test between free and fixed slope  
  > "On comparing the two fits using a F-test, we conclude that the fit with varying slope does not provide significantly more information than the fit with slope fixed to zero." (4.1)
   - parameter: bins of 0.05e12 Lsun kpc^-2  
  > "pixels binned within (0.05 x $\rm 10^{12}$) $\rm L_{\odot}kpc^{-2}$" (4.1)
   - assumption: H2O and FIR co-spatially lensed; differential lensing negligible  
  > "we assume that H$_{2}$O and FIR are co-spatially lensed and hence, the issue of differential lensing" (4.1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: F-test; comparison of global and resolved ratios  
  > "This fit is shown as a thick black line and the grey shaded region in Figure [3](#mainplot)B which is consistent with the global $\rm L_{H_{2}O}$/$\rm L_{FIR}$" (4.1)
6. **Calibrate SFR and test dependence on physical properties**: SFR=1.47e-10 L_IR (Kennicutt & Evans), L_IR/L_FIR~1.38; compare ratio vs lambda_max, Sigma_gas, tau_100; compare with CO(6-5)  
  > "To convert $\rm L_{IR}$ to $\rm L_{FIR}$, we use $\langle$$\rm L_{IR}$/$\rm L_{FIR}$$\rangle$ $\sim$1.38" (4.1)
   - parameter: kappa_lambda = 2.92e5 (lambda/um)^-2 cm^2/g  
  > "We use $\rm \kappa_{\lambda}$ = 2.92 $\times\ 10^{5} (\lambda / \mu m)^{-2}$ $\rm cm^{2}g^{-1}$ (cite: li01)" (4.3)
   - assumption: dust and gas sizes similar  
  > "For a simple calculation, we assume that the dust and gas sizes are similar." (4.3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Three SPT sources chosen at similar redshift and nearby on sky to observe same line and ease Band 9 follow-up  
  > "The three SPT targets were selected such that they are at a similar redshift and within 10$^{\circ}$ of each other on the sky." (2.1 Sample Selection)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): No lens modeling; uniform T and opacity assumed; resolved L_FIR scaled from non-FIR continuum  
  > "It should be noted that the resolved $\rm L_{FIR}$ is estimated by scaling the continuum which is not in the FIR regime" (3.1 Estimating L_FIR)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small sample of four sources, one low S/N
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Resolved L_FIR is just scaled continuum so the correlation is partly built in
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Image-plane pixels are correlated and multiply imaged
- Caveat (inferred, ours): F-test null described oddly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Large uncertainty on resolved slope (±1.44)
