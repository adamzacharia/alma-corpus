## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct mean Halpha spectrum and reduce VLTI visibilities**: Piece-wise linear interpolation of 15 archival VLT Halpha spectra onto 200 equidistant points between 654.5 and 658.0 nm to compute a point-wise mean and standard deviation; reduce VLTI/GRAVITY data using Gravity Pipeline in EsoReflex.  
  > "A piece-wise linear interpolation has been performed in 200 equidistant points in the range 654.5 to 658.0 nm for all the datasets, and a point-wise average and standard deviation from this average has been computed." (H$\alpha$ spectroscopy); "The reduction was performed using the Gravity Pipeline in EsoReflex (cite: freudling2013automated), to obtain the calibrated squared visibilities $V^2$ and closure phases arg$T^3$." (VLTI interferometry)
   - software: Gravity Pipeline, EsoReflex
   - parameter: 200 equidistant points in 654.5 to 658.0 nm  
  > "A piece-wise linear interpolation has been performed in 200 equidistant points in the range 654.5 to 658.0 nm for all the datasets, and a point-wise average and standard deviation from this average has been computed." (H$\alpha$ spectroscopy)
   - assumption: Mean Halpha profile corresponds approximately to an axially-symmetric mean state of the system  
  > "For this reason, a mean profile was computed (see Fig. 1), corresponding approximately to an axially-symmetric mean state of the system." (H$\alpha$ spectroscopy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit simple analytic visibility models to VLTI and ALMA data**: Fit a homogeneous ellipse + point source + halo model to VLTI/GRAVITY visibilities, and a narrow elliptical delta-ring + halo model to ALMA Band 7 visibilities using MCMC, then rescale VLTI and ALMA visibilities into unit-less quantities.  
  > "1. A homogeneous ellipse described by $\alpha, \beta, \omega$, together with a point source and a background halo for the VLTI/GRAVITY visibilities, to model the inner disc." (Simple analytic models); "2. A narrow elliptical ring with a background halo for the ALMA visibilities, to model the outer disc." (Simple analytic models); "The $A$ and $B$ parameters were used to rescale the visibilities in physical units (Jy) into a unit-less quantity for further analysis." (Simple analytic models)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Initial MCMC: 32 walkers, 10^4 steps; second iteration: 64 walkers, 10^5 steps, 30% burn-in; omega < 180 deg  
  > "Initial simulations were performed with 32 walkers and $10^4$ steps." (Simple analytic models); "The marginalized posteriors were used as a starting value for the next iteration: these new simulations ran with 64 walkers for a total of $10^5$ steps, with a $30\%$ burn-in phase." (Simple analytic models)
   - assumption: Uninformative prior; infinitely thin elliptical ring (delta-ring) for outer disc; homogeneous ellipse for inner disc  
  > "The best-fit parameters and their uncertainties (68% confidence intervals of the marginalized posterior distributions) were obtained via a MCMC simulation with an uninformative prior." (Simple analytic models)
   - fitting: MCMC simulation with uninformative prior using emcee (32 walkers x 10^4 steps, then 64 walkers x 10^5 steps with 30% burn-in); 68% confidence intervals of marginalized posteriors.

   - parameter: Fixed inner disc inclination i = 40 deg and position angle = 147 deg; stellar parameters r_* = 2.0 R_sun, T_* = 4750 K, M_* = 1.4 M_sun  
  > "The geometry of the disc was taken from the MCMC analysis of the analytic models, the inclination of $i = 40^\circ$ is fixed, together with the position angle of 147$^\circ$." (Accretion region model); "$r_\star = 2.0\,R_\odot$, $T_\star = 4750\,{\rm K}$, $M_\star = 1.4\,M_\odot$." (The central star)
   - assumption: LTE (Saha equation for electron density), single-scattering radiative transfer, constant shell temperature, spherical shell geometry  
  > "One limitation is that `Pyshellspec` is a single-scattering radiative-transfer tool." (The `SHELL` object)
   - fitting: Sub-space parameter scan over R_outsh, v(R_insh), e_velsh, T_sh, and rho_sh minimizing chi^2_SPE and chi^2_V2.

## Qualitative findings

- The inner accretion region is described by an optically thin spherical shell of gas at ~9100 K with velocities up to 385 km/s, surrounded by an innermost disc (0.07 to 0.16 au) that accounts for the VLTI/GRAVITY K-band visibilities.  
  > "1. The geometry of the inner accretion region can be described using a shell of gas, heated to about 9 000 K, surrounding the young star. This gas is moving at speeds up to $\SI{385}{\kilo \meter\,\second^{-1}}$." (Conclusions); "This shell is surrounded by an innermost disc ($16\,R_\odot$ to $35\,R_\odot$, i.e., 0.07 to 0.16 au), which is responsible for the VLTI/GRAVITY K-band visibilities." (Conclusions)
- An unresolved warm intermediate disc (0.2 to 1 au, 1000 K to 380 K) is required to account for the mid-infrared SED and is the probable origin of the observed water vapour emission.  
  > "However, a deficit in the SED is present in the mid-IR, which has to be compensated for by introducing a warm intermediary region in the 0.2 – 1 au range." (Intermediate disc model); "6. An intermediate disc, for the moment unresolved by ALMA, extending from 0.2 to 1 au or beyond, must be present, according to the SED constraints. From this region, the observed water vapour emission probably originates." (Conclusions)
- The outer dust ring at ~48 au has temperatures of 50-60 K, which is too warm for H2O or CO condensation lines in the emitting layers, leaving CO2 as the only candidate volatile condensation line.  
  > "4. The ring at ($48\pm1$) au does not correspond to the condensation line of water ice or carbon monoxide due to high temperature (50 – 60 K). The only possible condensation line that could cause such a ring is carbon dioxide." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Archival VLT Halpha spectra constrain the inner accretion region kinematics, VLTI/GRAVITY K-band interferometry constrains the spatial extent of the inner accretion region, ALMA high-resolution Band 6/7 interferometry constrains the outer and intermediate disc dimensions and temperature, and multi-wavelength SED photometry constrains the global temperature and density structure.  
  > "To constrain the physical properties of the accretion region around the star, all available H$\alpha$ datasets from the ESO Archive were utilized." (H$\alpha$ spectroscopy); "To constrain the spatial extent of the inner accretion region, we utilized archival VLTI/GRAVITY interferometric data of DoAr 44 (cite: bouvier2020probing)." (VLTI interferometry); "To constrain the dimensions and temperature of the outer disc, the newest continuum Band 7 (870 μm) ALMA interferometric data was used (project code 2019.1.00532.S, observation date 2021-08-19, (cite: arce2023radio))." (ALMA interferometry)
- Why this method: Simple analytic visibility models provide a benchmark, angular scales, and visibility rescaling factors; a mean Halpha profile approximates an axially-symmetric state since the 3D equilibrium radiative-transfer code Pyshellspec/Shellspec does not capture temporal variability; chi^2 contributions were optimized separately per region because global optimization is exceedingly difficult.  
  > "This standard deviation from the mean spectrum has been treated as the denominator in the $\chi^2$ minimization, as our model will not capture the temporal variability of the system." (H$\alpha$ spectroscopy); "These analytic models were used as a benchmark for the more complex radiative-transfer models, providing the angular extent of the observed structures as a guide." (Simple analytic models); "However, the total $\chi^2$ was not optimised globally, as this is exceedingly difficult." (`Pyshellspec` radiative-transfer models)
- Alternative: X-ray data from Montmerle et al. (1983) were rejected because X-ray emission forms under non-LTE conditions outside the model approximations.  
  > "The X-ray data from (cite: montmerle1983einstein) were not used, as the X-ray emission is formed under non-LTE conditions, which is outside the approximations of our models." (Spectral energy distribution)
- Alternative: Axially symmetric conical shapes for the inner optically thin Halpha-emitting gas were tested, but optimization always converged to large opening angles or a spherically symmetric shell.  
  > "We have modelled the inner optically thin matter responsible for the averaged H$\alpha$ emission using axially symmetric conical shapes, but optimising always resulted in large opening angles, or a spherically symmetric shell of gas." (What is the geometry of the accretion region?)
- Alternative: Monotonic density profile without a central cavity was rejected as incompatible with ALMA visibilities and CLEANed images.  
  > "To account for the ALMA visibilities and CLEANed images, we were forced to introduce a cavity." (Outer disc model); "The observations are not compatible with a monotonic density profile, and a large central cavity must be present." (Conclusions)

- Limitation (authors): The radiative-transfer model is static/axially-symmetric and does not capture the strong temporal variability of the Halpha profile.  
  > "This standard deviation from the mean spectrum has been treated as the denominator in the $\chi^2$ minimization, as our model will not capture the temporal variability of the system." (H$\alpha$ spectroscopy)
- Limitation (authors): Pyshellspec is a single-scattering radiative-transfer tool and does not compute full multiple scattering.  
  > "One limitation is that `Pyshellspec` is a single-scattering radiative-transfer tool." (`Pyshellspec` radiative-transfer models)

  > "However, the total $\chi^2$ was not optimised globally, as this is exceedingly difficult." (`Pyshellspec` radiative-transfer models)
- Limitation (authors): The outer disc is vertically isothermal in the model and the complex ring-within-a-ring morphology seen in ALMA images was simplified to a single power-law ring.  
  > "The density profile was simplified to a power-law, and the complex “ring-within-a-ring" morphology was not fitted in detail." (Outer disc model); "On the contrary, our model is vertically isothermal, and requires about 50 – 60 K in the outer regions, in order to account for the Spitzer/MIPS ($\SI{70}{\micro \meter}$) and Herschel/PACS ($\SI{100}{\micro \meter}$) far infrared fluxes." (Outer disc model)
- Limitation (authors): The outer disc is optically thick, so the dust density and dust mass only have a lower bound and the mid-plane temperature is inaccessible.

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Shellspec imposes parametric power-law temperature profiles and vertical isothermality rather than solving self-consistent Monte Carlo dust thermal radiative equilibrium, which likely drives the high 50-60 K mid-plane temperature needed to fit the far-IR SED.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a factor of 10 discrepancy between the accretion rate in the text/Conclusions (6.1e-8 M_sun/yr) and the statement that it is 'surprisingly similar' to Bouvier et al. 2020a (quoted in the Introduction as 6.5e-9 M_sun/yr), suggesting a possible typo in the exponent.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitting the inner, intermediate, and outer regions separately rather than globally may miss radiative shadowing effects from the misaligned inner disc onto the outer ring.
