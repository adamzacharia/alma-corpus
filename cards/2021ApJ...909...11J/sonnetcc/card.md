## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate and image the combined 2015+2016 data**: CASA calibration of passband, flux and gain, phase-only self-calibration from continuum (uv < 1000 m), CONCAT of both epochs, then CLEAN line cubes at ~1 km/s resolution with robust 2 and robust 0.5  
  > "The CASA package was used to calibrate the data for passband, flux, and gain." (2 Observations); "We used CONCAT to combine both the 2015 and 2016 data, and then CLEANed the combined data" (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 2 (main maps) and 0.5 (high-resolution maps)  
  > "we used a robust weighting factor of 2 for the visibility to generate the maps." (2 Observations); "We also used a robust weighting factor of 0.5 for the visibility to generate higher-resolution maps to search for jet rotation and to study shock structures in knot BK3." (2 Observations)
   - parameter: self-calibration from continuum restricted to uv distance < 1000 m  
  > "we only used the continuum data with $uv$ distance $< 1000$ m to derive the solution for the self-calibration" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure proper motion of the inner-jet knots**: Compare knot peak positions between the 2008 SMA and 2016 ALMA maps after degrading ALMA to 0.25 arcsec and aligning on the continuum peaks; 8 knots (4 per side); transverse velocity from proper motion at 321 pc; inclination from transverse and mean radial velocity  
  > "We measured the peak positions of these knots (Figure [5](#convol)) and calculated the position shift of the knots." (3.2 Proper Motion); "The time interval is 7.72 years." (3.2 Proper Motion)
   - parameter: time baseline 7.72 yr; position error = one third of the beam size along the jet axis  
  > "The time interval is 7.72 years." (3.2 Proper Motion); "The error bar of position shift is assumed to be one third of the beam size along the jet axis." (3.2 Proper Motion)
   - assumption: Distance 321 pc; mean radial velocity ~20 km/s used for inclination  
  > "Thus, the inclination angle is refined to be $\sim$11 degrees, using a mean radial velocity of $\sim$20 km s$^{-1}$ estimated later in Section 3.5." (3.2 Proper Motion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the refined outer-jet SMA proper motions (2004 epoch removed); velocity roughly constant from 1000 to 5000 AU  
  > "The figure shows that the velocity of the jet is roughly constant at a distance from 1000 to 5000 AU" (3.2 Proper Motion)
3. **Compare knot peaks with the orbiting jet source wiggle model**: Overlay the previously proposed orbiting jet source model (with previous best-fit parameters, adjusted for proper motion) on the ALMA SiO map and compare with SMA at the same resolution  
  > "After adjusted for proper motion, the previous best-fit parameters still roughly fit the pattern of the wiggle but with some deviations" (3.3 Wiggle Structure)
   - assumption: Circular orbit of the jet source in the model  
  > "In the orbiting jet source model (cite: 2010ApJ...713..731L), the orbit of the jet source was assumed to be circular." (4.1 Wiggle Structure and Protobinary?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Deviation of ALMA knot peaks to the north not seen in the 2008 SMA map; possible missing flux (MRS ~0.75 arcsec vs knot size ~1 arcsec)  
  > "One possible reason is that our ALMA observation results suffer from missing flux, so some flux of the knots is missing." (4.1 Wiggle Structure and Protobinary?)
4. **Analyse PV diagrams across and along the jet axis and derive ejection periods**: PV diagrams perpendicular to the jet axis (centred on SiO peaks) to identify velocity components and search for rotation, and along the jet axis to identify knots, sub-knots and velocity variations; period from sub-knot spacing and jet velocity; internal-shock formation distance from v_j, velocity amplitude and period  
  > "To study the jet kinematics perpendicular to the jet axis, we presented the position-velocity (PV) diagrams" (3.4 Kinematics Perpendicular to the Jet Axis); "thus the period of the sub-knot ejection is about 4.5 years, using a jet velocity of" (3.5 Kinematics along the Jet Axis)
   - parameter: v_j ~102 km/s; velocity amplitude ~30 km/s; period of outer-knot velocity variation ~20 years  
  > "is the period of the velocity variation (based on the mean interknot spacing and the proper motion)" (3.5 Kinematics along the Jet Axis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted lack of SiO between 0.1 and 0.5 arcsec of the source is consistent with the observed CO-only emission there  
  > "This is consistent with our observations, which show CO but no SiO emission between" (3.5 Kinematics along the Jet Axis)
5. **Derive column densities, mass-loss rate and SiO/SO abundances in knot BK3**: CO column density assuming LTE and optically thin emission; two-sided mass-loss rate from 2 v_j m_H2 N b; SiO and SO column densities assuming optically thin emission at 300 K, converted to abundances relative to H2  
  > "Assuming that the CO emission is LTE and optically thin" (3.6 Column Density and SiO and SO Abundances in Knot BK3); "Assuming that both emissions are optically thin" (3.6 Column Density and SiO and SO Abundances in Knot BK3)
   - parameter: CO excitation temperature 200 K; SiO and SO excitation temperature 300 K; jet beam size ~0.4 arcsec (~128 AU)  
  > "we assume the excitation temperature to be 200 K, as adopted in (cite: 2010ApJ...713..731L)" (3.6 Column Density and SiO and SO Abundances in Knot BK3); "is thus assumed to be 300 K." (3.6 Column Density and SiO and SO Abundances in Knot BK3)
   - assumption: CO/H2 abundance ~4e-4 (gas-phase formation in an initially atomic jet)  
  > "the abundance of CO relative to H$_{2}$ is $\sim$4$\times$10$^{-4}$" (3.6 Column Density and SiO and SO Abundances in Knot BK3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mass-loss rate consistent with the previous measurement after adjusting for the new proper motion  
  > "This mass-loss rate is consistent with the previous measurement (cite: 2010ApJ...713..731L) after adjusted for the new proper motion." (3.6 Column Density and SiO and SO Abundances in Knot BK3)
6. **Model PV structure of backward/forward shocks and sideways ejection in knot BK3**: Simple kinematic model of a jet beam (unshocked beam, backward shock, internal working surface, forward shock, sideways ejection) plus a jet-driven bow shock, producing model PV diagrams along and perpendicular to the jet axis  
  > "this model has two components, a jet beam and a bow shock structure." (4.2.1 Illustrative Model For Shock Structures in Knot BK3)
   - parameter: R_j = 0.2 arcsec; z_bo = 0.5 arcsec; c_s = 8 km/s; beta = 4; v_s = 14 km/s; bow shock thickness 0.3 arcsec  
  > "where z$_{bo}=$$0^{\prime\prime}_{^\textrm{.}}5$ is z position of the bow shock tip" (Appendix I: Shock Model for knot BK3); "is the shock velocity to roughly match the observation." (Appendix I: Shock Model for knot BK3)
   - fitting: Parameters chosen by hand to roughly match the observation, no formal fit  
  > "is the shock velocity to roughly match the observation." (Appendix I: Shock Model for knot BK3)
   - validation: Model PV diagrams roughly consistent with observed PV structures  
  > "These features are also similar to those seen in the observation." (4.2.1 Illustrative Model For Shock Structures in Knot BK3)

## Qualitative findings

- SiO traces the jet; CO and SO trace the jet at high velocity and the outflow shell at low velocity  
  > "SiO traces the jet. CO and SO trace the jet at high velocity and outflow shell at low velocity." (5 Conclusions)
- Orbiting jet source model still fits the wiggle trend but knot peaks deviate to the north  
  > "However, the peaks of the knots show some deviations from the model." (5 Conclusions)
- No clear velocity gradient across the fast jet knots, so no jet rotation detected at this resolution  
  > "However, no clear velocity gradient can be seen across these knots at our current resolution." (3.4 Kinematics Perpendicular to the Jet Axis)
- Backward and forward shocks and sideways ejection resolved in knot BK3; SiO traces the high-density outer bow shock layer while CO and SO trace the low-density inner layers  
  > "In knot BK3, the SiO traces the high-density outer layer of the bow shock, while CO and SO trace the low-density inner layers of the bow shock." (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Higher resolution resolves the jet and knots to separate the lines and search for jet rotation  
  > "Now, we have higher resolution" (1 Introduction); "but with higher angular resolution to search for jet rotation." (2 Observations)
- Why this method: Robust 2 weighting chosen to better show jet and outflow shell structure; robust 0.5 for higher resolution to search for rotation and study BK3 shocks  
  > "In order to better show the structure of the jet and outflow shells, we used a robust weighting factor of 2 for the visibility to generate the maps." (2 Observations)
- Alternative: 2004 SMA epoch removed from the outer-jet proper-motion refinement because of its poorest resolution and uv coverage  
  > "We first refined the previous SMA measurements by taking out the 2004-epoch data due to its poorest resolution" (3.2 Proper Motion)
- Alternative: Self-calibration restricted to uv distance < 1000 m because long baselines have S/N lower than 3  
  > "Since the $uv$ data in long baselines have a signal to noise ratio lower than 3" (2 Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Possible missing flux: MRS ~0.75 arcsec versus knot size ~1 arcsec; impact on the knot peak deviations unknown  
  > "but we are not sure how and to what extent the missing flux impacts our results." (4.1 Wiggle Structure and Protobinary?)
- Limitation (authors): No clear velocity gradient across the jet knots, so jet rotation not detected at the current resolution  
  > "However, no clear velocity gradient can be seen across these knots at our current resolution." (3.4 Kinematics Perpendicular to the Jet Axis)
- Limitation (authors): Sub-knots are not spatially resolved, so shock structure analysis is limited to the knots  
  > "Since sub-knots are not spatially resolved, here we focus on the knots." (4.2 Backward and Forward Shocks and Sideways Ejection.)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Proper-motion uncertainty (one third of the beam) and knot-peak picking at degraded 0.25 arcsec resolution are coarse; peak positions of resolved knots may be biased by missing flux
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Excitation temperatures (200 K CO, 300 K SiO/SO) and optically thin assumption drive the column densities and abundances with no stated uncertainty
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The BK3 shock model is hand-tuned with no fit or goodness-of-fit, so it is illustrative rather than constraining
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sub-knot period assumes the same jet velocity as the outer knots
