## Analysis recipe

1. **Determine source positions and continuum flux densities**: Fit two-dimensional Gaussians in the image plane to the continuum images and to the moment 0 images integrated over the circumstellar line velocity range (using 12CO(3-2) for canonical positions).  
  > "The source positions, both in lines and continuum, were determined by fitting two-dimensional Gaussians to the images (in the line case using the moment 0 images integrated over the velocity range of the circumstellar line)." (ALMA data); "We determined the positions and flux densities of the continuum by fitting two-dimensional Gaussians to the data in the image plane." (Continuum)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Effective continuum frequencies: 107, 233, and 338 GHz  
  > "Within each band the four different sub-bands were added to give the effective frequencies 107, 233, and 338 GHz." (ALMA data)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting in the image plane  
  > "We determined the positions and flux densities of the continuum by fitting two-dimensional Gaussians to the data in the image plane." (Continuum)
   - validation: Astrometric accuracy estimated to be better than 0.1''; continuum peak coincides with CO line peak within ~0.1''  
  > "We estimate the astrometric accuracy to be better than 0.1$''$." (ALMA data); "When detected, the continuum peak coincides with the CO line peak in all cases within $\approx$ 0.1$''$." (Continuum)
2. **Characterize infrared SEDs, variability, and classify objects into four categories**: Correct Spitzer, MSX, and WISE photometry for extinction; construct mid-IR colour-colour diagrams to separate standard (SdE) and detached (DdE) dust envelopes; fit single-period sinusoids to VVV Ks and WISE W1/W2 light curves to separate regular large-amplitude pulsators (P) from non-periodic/low-amplitude (nP); inspect 12CO moment 8 maps and line profiles to separate standard (SgE) and complex (CgE) gas envelopes.  
  > "Based on these data, we constructed two colour-colour diagrams: $S_{7.87}/S_{4.49}$ vs $S_{22.1}/S_{7.87}$ and $S_{14.65}/S_{8.28}$ vs $S_{21.34}/S_{14.65}$." (Dust-CSE characteristics); "We used maximum intensity $^{12}$CO images (moment 8 in CASA), over the circumstellar velocity ranges, to characterize their morphologies." (Gas-CSE characteristics); "We regard an object as a large-amplitude, regular pulsator (P) if the period is well-determined and the amplitude is $>$ 0.5 mag, with an uncertainty lower than 20 % of its value, in at least one band." (Variability characteristics)
   - software: CASA, VizieR Photometry tool
   - parameter: Colour borders: S_22.1/S_7.87 = 30 * (S_7.87/S_4.49)^-0.8 and S_21.34/S_14.65 = 40 * (S_14.65/S_8.28)^-3  
  > "These borders are mathematically described by the expressions $S_{22.1}/S_{7.87}$ = 30$\times$($S_{7.87}/S_{4.49})^{-0.8}$ and $S_{21.34}/S_{14.65}$ = 40 $\times$ ($S_{14.65}/S_{8.28})^{-3}$." (Dust-CSE characteristics)
   - parameter: Counterpart search radius: 6'' from ALMA coordinates  
  > "Using the ALMA-based coordinates nearby counterparts within 6$''$ were retrieved." (Photometry and light curves)
   - assumption: Single pulsation period assumed for light curve fitting  
  > "The $K_{\rm s}$-band light curves were analysed using the codes and methodology outlined in (cite: groeetal20) assuming a single period." (Photometry and light curves)
   - fitting: Light curve period fitting following Groenewegen et al. (2020) and Groenewegen (2022)  
  > "The $K_{\rm s}$-band light curves were analysed using the codes and methodology outlined in (cite: groeetal20) assuming a single period. For the WISE data a very similar approach was adopted to analyse the light curves in the W1 (3.4 $\mu$m) and W2 (4.6 $\mu$m) bands, as outlined in (cite: groe22), also assuming a single period." (Photometry and light curves)
   - validation: Visual inspection of SEDs, light curves, and CO line profiles/maps

## Qualitative findings

- The 74 CO-detected OH/IR stars divide into four distinct categories based on dust-CSE colour (SdE vs DdE), gas-CSE CO morphology/profile (SgE vs CgE), and near-IR variability (P vs nP): SSP (40), SSnP (7), DSnP (10), and DCnP (17).  
  > "There are 40 objects in category SdE/SgE/P (SSP), seven objects in category SdE/SgE/nP (SSnP), ten objects in category DdE/SgE/nP (DSnP), and 17 objects in category DdE/CgE/nP (DCnP)." (Division into categories)
- SgE objects (57 stars across SSP, SSnP, DSnP) form a very homogeneous group with sharply centrally peaked CO brightness distributions well-fitted by Moffat profiles and soft-parabola line shapes.  
  > "When combining these results, we find 57 objects with brightness distributions that are sharply peaked at the centre and have soft-parabola line profiles." (Gas-CSE characteristics); "It is clear that the dataset is very homogeneous (the scatter around the average is in all cases small), implying very similar large-scale circumstellar properties for all SgE objects." (Azimuthally averaged CO line radial brightness profiles)
- All 17 CgE objects (extended axi-symmetric/ring CO morphologies and/or high-velocity line wings of 50-150 km s^-1) have detached dust envelopes (DdE) and lack regular large-amplitude pulsations (nP).  
  > "The remaining 17 objects have complex brightness distributions, or, in the two cases with sharply centrally peaked brightness distributions, the line profiles have prominent extended line wings. These objects will be analysed using a more complex gas-CSE (CgE). These are all of the DdE type." (Gas-CSE characteristics); "and 17 objects in category DdE/CgE/nP (DCnP)." (Division into categories)
- About 20% of the sample shows extended CO emission structures (including at least seven bow-shock structures) attributed to interaction between the expanding circumstellar envelope and the surrounding ISM.  
  > "We note here that about 20 % of the objects show surrounding structure, in both $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO, that suggests an interpretation in the form of CO line emission coming from a region where expanding circumstellar gas interacts with the surrounding interstellar medium. In at least seven cases, there is a bow-shock structure with the star at the expected position if this is due to emission from interacting gas caused by stellar motion through the ISM." (Gas-CSE characteristics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selecting OH/IR stars in the inner Galactic bulge with OH 1612 MHz flux > 0.3 Jy and luminosity within a factor of two of 5400 L_sun provides an equidistant (~8.2 kpc), statistically significant sample of 77 high-mass-loss O-rich AGB stars in a narrow initial-mass range, overcoming the small sample size (15 stars) and limited S/N and transition coverage of O+22.  
  > "The study of O+22 had limitations. Only two $^{12}$CO lines and one $^{13}$CO line were observed, often with limited S/N. The limited sample size (15 objects) prevented statistical comparisons. In the present study we remedy these shortcomings by observing 77 objects in the inner Galactic bulge in different CO isotopologue lines with ALMA and a sub-sample with the Atacama Pathfinder EXperiment telescope (APEX)." (Introduction); "We selected all objects, brighter than 0.3 Jy in the OH 1612 MHz line, that have an estimated luminosity within a factor of two of the median luminosity of the O+22 sample, 5400 $L_\odot$. This minimized contamination by foreground objects, and helped ensure that the sample covers a narrow range in initial mass." (Sample)
- Why this method: Convolving cubes to 0.5'', 1'', 2'', and 3'' circular Gaussian beams and extracting central-pixel spectra balances S/N degradation at larger apertures (due to non-white interferometer noise) against capturing extended emission, while Moffat profile fits to azimuthally averaged radial profiles boost S/N on weak extended emission.  
  > "In order to study them in more detail we derived azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles (AARPs) by azimuthally averaging the data cubes for all objects and for all lines within the velocity range $\pm$20 % of the gas expansion velocity centred on the systemic velocity. This increases the S/N and provides more reliable information on the weak, extended emission." (Azimuthally averaged CO line radial brightness profiles); "The CO line brightness distributions are sharply peaked for SgE objects, but they contain extended low-intensity emission as shown above. This, together with the limited S/N of the data and the fact that the S/N decreases for a compact source when using increasingly larger apertures (cite: tsuketal23), makes it difficult to estimate the total intensities of the interferometer data. We therefore summarise the observational $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO line results in a way suitable for the radiative transfer analysis of SgE objects." (Line profiles)
- Alternative: Excluding short baselines (spatial scales > 3-5'') to filter out interstellar CO contamination was tested on uncontaminated strong sources and rejected (except for two objects: OH359.161-0.055 and OH000.319-0.041) because it caused significant loss of circumstellar flux.  
  > "Because the lines of sight to the Galactic centre are crowded with interstellar CO line emission, the imaging was done twice for the CO lines, first with full range of visibilities and then excluding short baselines corresponding to spatial frequencies lower than 3–5 $''$, in order to exploit the filtering capabilities of the interferometer and limit the line-of-sight contamination from interstellar CO line emission. The latter approach was tested on a number of objects with strong circumstellar lines and no interstellar contamination and showed significant amounts of lost circumstellar flux in all cases. Hence, except for two objects, we refrained from using short-baseline removal as a solution to this problem." (ALMA data)
- Alternative: Using the Moffat SWHM (Theta_0.5) or surface integral of the Moffat fit to measure source size and total flux was rejected because Theta_0.5 is close to the synthesised beam FWHM and average beta values are < 2 (so the integral does not converge at reasonable radii); Theta_0.1 was used instead.  
  > "The $\Theta_{0.5}$ values are, on average, close to the FWHMs of the synthesised beams, and not a good measure of the extent of the brightness distribution (cite: ramsetal20)." (Azimuthally averaged CO line radial brightness profiles); "The average $\beta$:s are all below 2, and we cannot use these fits to reliably estimate the total flux densities. We instead chose the angular extent at which the fit to the AARP reaches 10 % of its peak value, $\Theta_{0.1}$, as a measure of the size of the brightness distribution" (Azimuthally averaged CO line radial brightness profiles)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Lines of sight toward the Galactic centre suffer from interstellar CO line emission (~10% of objects) and extended CSM/ISM interaction emission (~20% of objects), complicating line profile extraction and ALMA-APEX comparisons.  
  > "About 20 % of the objects show extended structures, often with low surface brightness, that can possibly be attributed to interaction between circumstellar and interstellar CO gas (CSM/ISM). Another about 10 % of the objects show clear presence of interstellar CO line emission in the velocity range of the circumstellar emission." (Discussion of some individual objects)
- Limitation (authors): Part of the 13CO(3-2) line fell outside the spectral window for seven objects despite splitting Band 7 into two velocity tunings.  
  > "Despite this, part of the $^{13}$CO(3–2) line was missed for seven objects (for three of these objects the data of O+22 were used instead)." (ALMA data)
- Limitation (authors): Some spread in source distances along the line of sight around the adopted 8.2 kpc is likely, and none of the sources have reliable Gaia EDR3 parallaxes.  
  > "The adopted distance to all of our sources is 8.2 kpc (cite: gravcoll19), even though some spread along the line of sight is most likely. Unfortunately, none of our sources have a measured reliable parallax in Gaia Early Data Release 3 (cite: gaiacoll23)." (Sample)
- Limitation (authors): Extended low-surface-brightness dust continuum emission at large radii (> 1.9'' MRS at 338 GHz) may be resolved out by the interferometer or missed by 2D Gaussian fits.  
  > "It is likely that there is continuum flux not captured by the fitted Gaussians nor ‘seen’ by the interferometer. Extended low-surface brightness emission from colder dust at large radii may contribute (maybe even significantly) to the flux measured by ALMA." (Continuum)
- Limitation (authors): For CgE objects with high-velocity wings, limited S/N means reported FWZPs (50 to 150 km s^-1) are likely lower limits, and some bipolar outflows may be misclassified as SgE due to orientation or weak wings.  
  > "There may be objects with higher-velocity wings that are too weak to be detected or the orientation is such that the wings are not seen if the outflow is bipolar, i.e. there may be border line objects between SgE and CgE." (Gas-CSE characteristics); "For CgE objects with line wings, the FWZPs range from about 50 to 150  km s$^{-1}$. The S/N of our spectra are in general limited, so the reported values are likely to be lower limits to the true FWZPs." (Kinematics)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Classification between SgE and CgE relies partly on visual inspection of moment 8 images and line profiles, which can be sensitive to S/N and viewing inclination.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No radiative transfer modelling is performed in this paper; physical mass-loss rates and 12C/13C abundance ratios are deferred to forthcoming papers.
