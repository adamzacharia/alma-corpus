## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum and line cubes**: CASA calibration, tclean continuum and cube imaging with briggs weighting (robust 0.5), hogbom, self-calibration from continuum applied to cubes, pblimit 0.2  
  > "Continuum imaging and line cubes were generated with the TCLEAN task in CASA 5.3." (2 Observations); "The self-calibration solutions derived from the continuum images were applied to the line cubes." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: briggs, robust = 0.5, hogbom  
  > "The deconvolution was performed using the “hogbom” algorithm with a weighting parameter of “briggs,” and the robust parameter was set to 0.5 to balance sensitivity and resolution." (2 Observations)
   - parameter: pblimit = 0.2  
  > "Primary beam correction was applied using pblimit = 0.2." (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit LTE synthetic spectra per core to get T, N for each molecule**: Extract a single-pixel spectrum at each core continuum peak and fit LTE models with XCLASS; CH3OH is handled through 13CH3OH and a 12C/13C ratio  
  > "Spectra were extracted from a single pixel at the continuum peak position of the dense core and averaged over the beam." (3.1 Line identifications); "Under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), XCLASS solves the radiative transfer equation and generates synthetic spectra for specific molecular transitions" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - software: XCLASS, MAGIX, CDMS, JPL
   - parameter: Free parameters per molecule: source size, rotation temperature, column density, line width, velocity offset  
  > "rotation temperature (T), column density (N), line width" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - parameter: Source size fixed to the deconvolved continuum size  
  > "In our study, the source size was taken as the deconvolved size of the continuum emission" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - parameter: Upper level energy ranges: 93-485 K CH3COCH3, 227-459 K CH3OH, 36-144 K 13CH3OH, 64-441 K C2H5CN; CH3CHO two lines at 166 K  
  > "The upper level energy ranges of molecular transitions are 93$-$485 K for CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$, 227$-$459 K for CH$_{3}$OH, 36$-$144 K for $^{13}$CH$_{3}$OH, and 64$-$441 K for C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN." (3.1 Line identifications)
   - parameter: C2H5CN partition function includes only the ground state, so its column densities are corrected by f_vib  
  > "while the partition function of C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN only considers the contribution from the ground state in the XCLASS fitting" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - assumption: LTE  
  > "Under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), XCLASS solves the radiative transfer equation" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - assumption: CH3CHO fit temperature fixed to the CH3COCH3 excitation temperature  
  > "We assumed that the fitting temperature of CH$_{3}$CHO is equal to the excitation temperature of CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$, as CH$_{3}$CHO is considered a precursor to CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$ (cite: 2022ApJS..259....1G)." (3.1 Line identifications)
   - assumption: CH3OH rotation temperature equal to that of 13CH3OH  
  > "The rotation temperature of CH$_{3}$OH was assumed to be the same as that of $^{13}$CH$_{3}$OH." (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances)
   - assumption: CH3OH column density = 13CH3OH column density times 12C/13C from the R_GC relation  
  > "which were calculated from the $^{12}$C / $^{13}$C ratios based on the column densities of $^{13}$CH$_{3}$OH" (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances)
   - fitting: MAGIX with the Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm; priors, chains and likelihood not stated  
  > "We obtained the final fitting parameters and error estimates for the molecules using the Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm." (3.1 Line identifications)
   - validation: A transition counts as detected if above 3 sigma and consistent with the XCLASS model across frequencies  
  > "Transitions are considered to be detected if their line intensities exceed 3" (3.1 Line identifications); "and the observed line intensities at different frequencies are consistent with the XCLASS model predictions" (3.1 Line identifications)
3. **Derive H2 column density from continuum flux**: Optically thin dust continuum formula for N(H2) using integrated flux density, solid angle, kappa and the Planck function  
  > "The source-averaged H$_{2}$ column densities of the 16 cores are derived from the continuum flux using the following formula under the LTE assumption (cite: 2008A&A...487..993K)" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - parameter: kappa(870 um) = 1.89 cm2/g  
  > "= 1.89 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ is the dust mass absorption coefficient (cite: 1994A&A...291..943O)" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - parameter: Dust temperature taken from Chen et al. 2024  
  > "T is taken from (cite: 2024ApJ...962...13C), and it is assumed to be equal to the gas temperature of CH$_{3}$OCHO" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - assumption: gas-to-dust mass ratio = 100  
  > "= 100 is the gas-to-dust mass ratio (cite: 1991ApJ...380..429L; 1992ApJS...82..167H)" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - assumption: mean molecular weight = 2.8  
  > "= 2.8 is the mean molecular weight of the gas (cite: 2008A&A...487..993K)" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - assumption: dust temperature equal to the CH3OCHO gas temperature  
  > "T is taken from (cite: 2024ApJ...962...13C), and it is assumed to be equal to the gas temperature of CH$_{3}$OCHO" (3.1 Line identifications)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Compute abundances relative to H2 and test correlations between molecules**: Fractional abundance = N / N(H2) per core; correlation coefficients and significance between acetone and the other molecules; comparison of mean temperatures  
  > "where N represents the column density of specific molecule and N (H$_{2}$) denotes the column density of molecular hydrogen" (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances); "The correlation coefficients of CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$ with CH$_{3}$CHO, CH$_{3}$OH, and C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN are 0.90, 0.61, and 0.91, respectively." (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Temperature correlations checked to exclude temperature as the driver of abundance correlations  
  > "This indicates that the abundance correlations of these molecules are not affected by temperature." (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances)
5. **Map line integrated intensities against the continuum**: Integrated intensity maps of one transition per molecule toward nine clumps, compared with continuum peaks and UC Hii regions  
  > "integrated intensity maps of CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$ at 342485 MHz, CH$_{3}$CHO at 343779 MHz, CH$_{3}$OH at 342730 MHz, and C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN at 343195 MHz" (3.2 Spatial distribution)
   - parameter: Transitions marked with asterisks in the transition tables are the ones used for the maps  
  > "Asterisks mark the transitions of the molecules that are used for Fig." (Table 1 notes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare with literature sources and a grain-surface chemical model**: Compare mean T, N and abundance of acetone in H, I, L cores with published high/intermediate/low-mass sources, and the mean abundance with Garrod et al. 2022 model predictions  
  > "These comparisons suggest that the CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$ emitting gas in the H, I, and L cores may have similar physical and chemical conditions" (4.1 Comparison of the physical parameters); "The average abundance of CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$ is (6.2$\pm$1.3)$\times$10$^{-8}$, which is comparable to the slow timescale model prediction." (4.2 Chemistry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Integrated intensity maps of CH3COCH3, CH3CHO, CH3OH and C2H5CN look similar  
  > "The integrated intensity maps reveal similar spatial distributions for CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$, CH$_{3}$CHO, CH$_{3}$OH, and C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN." (abstract)
- Line peaks coincide with continuum peaks in regions without UC Hii regions; in clumps with UC Hii regions line peaks are offset  
  > "The line emission peaks of all four molecules coincide with the continuum emission peaks in regions without ultra-compact Hii regions." (abstract); "the observed line offsets are likely influenced by UC Hii regions" (3.2 Spatial distribution)
- I16060 C2 has CH3COCH3 without detected C2H5CN, contradicting earlier claims that acetone needs C2H5CN  
  > "Nonetheless, I16060 C2 provides a counterexample" (3.2 Spatial distribution)
- Column densities of all four molecules in H cores are 1 to 2 orders of magnitude above those in I and L cores  
  > "In general, the column densities of the four molecules in the H cores are 1 to 2 orders of magnitude higher than those in the I and L cores." (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances)
- No significant correlations among the rotation temperatures of CH3COCH3, CH3OH and C2H5CN  
  > "showing no significant correlations in temperatures among these molecules" (3.3 Rotation temperatures, column densities, and abundances)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier acetone work was limited to single targets or small single-dish samples, so a larger interferometric sample with matched resolution and spectral setup was needed  
  > "comprehensive interferometric studies targeting CH$_{3}$COCH$_{3}$ and chemically associated molecules across a larger sample with a similar angular resolution and spectral setup are essential" (Introduction)
- Why this method: 13CH3OH is fitted instead of CH3OH because CH3OH lines are optically thick and blended  
  > "Due to the optical thickness and blending of certain CH$_{3}$OH lines (see Appendix [8](#sec:mt)), we fit the isotopolog $^{13}$CH$_{3}$OH as an alternative in the 16 cores." (3.1 Line identifications)
- Alternative: Fitting CH3OH directly was replaced by fitting 13CH3OH because of optical depth and blending  
  > "we fit the isotopolog $^{13}$CH$_{3}$OH as an alternative in the 16 cores" (3.1 Line identifications)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Source size from the continuum may underestimate the line-emitting size and so overestimate fitted column densities  
  > "This may lead to an underestimation of the source size, resulting in an overestimate of the fitted column densities." (3.1 Line identifications)
- Limitation (authors): Poor continuum sensitivity because fewer than 10% of channels are line-free  
  > "resulting in relatively poor continuum sensitivity" (2 Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Resolved scales above 2000 au are too coarse to see the Orion-KL-like differences between molecules  
  > "whereas the resolved linear scales of our samples exceed 2000 au" (3.2 Spatial distribution)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abundances share the same N(H2) denominator and CH3CHO shares the CH3COCH3 temperature, so the abundance correlations may be partly built in; no test against N(H2) alone is shown
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperature is set equal to the CH3OCHO gas temperature, which directly drives N(H2) and all abundances
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LTE with a single temperature and a continuum-based source size is applied to line emission that the authors say is more extended in some cores
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The intermediate-mass core count is 3 in the abstract and table but 4 in Section 3.1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several CH3OH abundance uncertainties in the table exceed the value (for example 3.1 +/- 9.6 e-6), suggesting an error propagation problem
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The correlation method (Pearson or rank) and how errors enter it are not described, with only 16 cores of heterogeneous distance (2.7 to 10.1 kpc)
