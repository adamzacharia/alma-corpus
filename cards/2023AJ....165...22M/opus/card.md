## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA continuum and line data and derive upper limits**: CASA calibration and observatory-script imaging with Briggs weighting; 3σ limits for compact source and for a face-on resolved disc 3 beams in diameter  
  > "whilst the ‘Extended’ measurement for ALMA references the 3-$\sigma$ limit on the presence of a face-on, spatially resolved disc 3 beams in diameter." (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs weighting  
  > "Image reconstruction was carried out using scripts supplied by the observatory, adopting Briggs weighting as a compromise between signal-to-noise and resolution." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Decompose far-infrared emission into compact and extended components**: Point-source PSF model for compact component plus 2D Gaussian for extended component; convolved model subtracted and residuals examined  
  > "The compact component is modelled as a point source using a wavelength appropriate PSF model, whilst the extended component is modelled as a 2D Gaussian profile." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting); "The convolved model is then subtracted from the observation and significant residuals identified in the image." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency with SOFIA/HAWC+ non-detection checked  
  > "The brightness of both components are consistent with the non-detection in the SOFIA/HAWC+ 54 $\mu$m image due to the much greater sensitivity of *Herschel*/PACS at 70 $\mu$m." (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
3. **Convert CO and SiO non-detections to gas mass limits**: 5σ integrated line flux limits converted to gas masses assuming optically thin LTE emission  
  > "we derive 5-$\sigma$ upper limits to the integrated line fluxes of 2.3$\times10^{-17}$ W/m$^{2}$ for SiO (5-4) and 3.1$\times10^{-17}$ W/m$^{2}$ for SiO (5-4)." (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: line width 10 km/s, T_gas = 100 K  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas ($T_{\rm gas} = 100~K$)," (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: optically thin, LTE  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas ($T_{\rm gas} = 100~K$)," (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit two-component modified blackbody SED**: 10,000 random parameter sets (T_bb, λ0, β) scaled to measured fluxes; models violating upper limits discarded; error-weighted least squares best fit plus mean/std of valid sets  
  > "To do this we create 10,000 sets of values drawn from the appropriate ranges for each parameter ($T_{bb}$, $\lambda_{0}$, $\beta$) and scale the resultant modified blackbodies to the measured flux density of the system." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting); "Models that violated the upper limits for either component were discarded from the final ensemble." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - parameter: compact T fixed at 102 K; β between 0 and 4; λ0 ≥ 24 μm (compact), ≥ 70 μm (extended)  
  > "The beta exponents for both components must lie between 0 and 4, which covers the range observed for debris discs." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting); "i.e. 24 $\mu$m for the compact component and 70 $\mu$m for the extended component" (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - assumption: Spitzer/IRS rising spectrum entirely from compact component; extended component colder than compact  
  > "The rising *Spitzer*/IRS spectrum at mid-infrared wavelengths is entirely accounted for by the compact component." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - fitting: random sampling with rejection of models violating upper limits, then error weighted least-squares  
  > "we calculated the best-fit values for the two components in combination through error weighted least-squares fitting, and the mean and standard deviation of the valid parameter sets in isolation." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Radiative transfer modelling of compact component grain sizes and comparison with ALMA limit**: Optically thin disc models with power-law radial and grain size distributions; simulated ALMA image for each SED model compared to ALMA upper limit  
  > "For each model fit to the SED, a spatially resolved ALMA image of the emission was simulated and compared to the ALMA upper limit." (Radiative transfer modelling (Results)); "We used simple power-law radial dust and grain size distributions and realistic dust emissivities commonly employed in debris disc modelling." (Radiative transfer modelling (Results))
   - parameter: Models A-D: R_in 30-60 au, R_out 50-90 au, a_min 2-60 μm, a_max 20-1000 μm, γ = 3.5  
  > "Summary of model parameters used to calculate SEDs presented in Figure [4](#fig:comparison_seds)." (Table 3)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust (L_dust/L_WD ≃ 10^-3); L_WD = 67.6 Lsun  
  > "We assume the dust emission is optically thin, consistent with the observed fractional luminosity at infrared wavelengths ($L_{\rm dust}/L_{\rm WD} \simeq 10^{-3}$)" (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - fitting: Exploration of a broad parameter grid (Models A-D); no formal sampler stated  
  > "A broad parameter space of the spatial and grain size distribution was explored" (Radiative transfer modelling (Results))
   - validation: Comparison of simulated ALMA images with ALMA upper limit  
  > "For each model fit to the SED, a spatially resolved ALMA image of the emission was simulated and compared to the ALMA upper limit." (Radiative transfer modelling (Results))
6. **Estimate required cometary disruption rate**: Convert dust mass to number of Hale-Bopp equivalent comets deposited over 10^5 yr  
  > "Further assuming that the dust we currently observe has been deposited over the past $10^{5}~$yrs without any loss due to radiation pressure, etc." (Discussion)
   - parameter: dust mass 0.13 M_Earth; timescale 10^5 yr  
  > "Given the observed dust mass of 0.13 $M_{\oplus}$ we can equate this to $\simeq~500\times10^{6}$ bodies with a mass equivalent to that of the Hale-Bopp comet" (Discussion)
   - assumption: no dust loss over 10^5 yr; complete disruption of comets  
  > "Again, a conservative assumption as many cometary bodies will not be completely broken up." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Herschel 70 μm emission around the WD is extended and decomposes into compact and extended components  
  > "In contrast, archival *Herschel*/PACS 70 $\mu$m imaging data (PI: K. Su) surprisingly show extended emission associated with the white dwarf’s position." (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
- The steep SED slope requires micron-sized grains below the blow-out size that must be continually replenished  
  > "The steep slope of the SED can only be explained by the presence of small, micron sized grains that dominate the emission but have a very low emissivity at long wavelengths" (Radiative transfer modelling (Results))
- Nature of the extended component is unconstrained  
  > "The available data offer no real constraint on the nature of the extended component seen in the *Herschel* 70 $\mu$m map with weak upper limits either side of that to constrain its behaviour." (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA resolution sufficient to resolve a Solar-system scale disc; CO and SiO diagnose post-AGB vs icy planetesimal origins  
  > "sufficient to spatially resolve a Solar-system scale disc around the white dwarf" (Observations); "These lines are diagnostic of emission from post-AGB envelopes and icy planetesimal belts." (Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
- Why this method: Debris disc modelling tools appropriate for optically thin dust  
  > "This is appropriate to determine the spatial and grain size distribution of any accumulation of optically thin circumstellar dust" (Radiative transfer modelling (Results))
- Alternative: In-situ sublimation of remnant planetesimals rejected because sublimation timescale <1,000 yr  
  > "This suggests that the observed dust emission is not the product of in-situ destruction of remnant planetesimals." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Extended component could be a blow-out halo, superimposed nebula, or post-AGB envelope remnant  
  > "Multiple interpretations could fit the evidence equally well, such as it being a blow-out halo from the compact component, part of the wider Helix nebula coincidentally superimposed upon the white dwarf" (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ALMA blind to the extended Herschel component due to ~4'' largest angular scale  
  > "The largest angular scale for the array configuration was about $4\ensuremath{''}$ which rendered the ALMA observations blind to the extended component seen in the *Herschel*/PACS map." (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Compact component mm spectral slope unconstrained by non-detections  
  > "However, the non-detection at (sub-)millimetre wavelengths of the compact component leaves its spectral slope unconstrained" (Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Reduction summary is incomplete; details in scripts  
  > "The reduction and analysis summarised here is not a complete and detailed overview of the process." (Observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line flux limits both labelled SiO(5-4); one presumably CO, ambiguous
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Quoted 3σ line sensitivities (1.38 mJy/km/s) and rms (0.38 mJy/km/s) are mutually inconsistent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass 0.13 M_Earth used in discussion differs from Table 3 model masses (8-10×10^-3 M_Earth)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas mass limits depend strongly on assumed T_gas=100 K and 10 km/s line width; CO photodissociation not modelled
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Radiative transfer modelling explores only four hand-picked models, no formal fit or uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): WD temperature quoted inconsistently (103600 K vs 110000 K)
