## Analysis recipe

1. **Decompose 70 µm Herschel emission into compact and extended components**: Compact component modelled as a point source with a PSF model; extended component as a 2D Gaussian; convolved model subtracted and residuals examined  
  > "The compact component is modelled as a point source using a wavelength appropriate PSF model, whilst the extended component is modelled as a 2D Gaussian profile." (Modelling)
   - parameter: Compact flux 36 ± 4.5 mJy; extended flux 222 ± 12 mJy at 70 µm  
  > "with a flux density of 36 $\pm$ 4.5 mJy, and a 2D Gaussian for the extended emission, with a flux density of 222 $\pm$ 12 mJy" (Results)
   - assumption: Extended component not resolved by SOFIA and Herschel at 24-70 µm disc scales  
  > "this component is therefore unlikely to be spatially resolved in the SOFIA and *Herschel* far-infrared observations." (Modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residuals inspected for significant structure  
  > "The convolved model is then subtracted from the observation and significant residuals identified in the image." (Modelling)
2. **Fit the excess SED with two modified blackbody components**: Modified blackbody with break wavelength and beta exponent; compact temperature fixed at 102 K; 10,000 random parameter sets scaled to measured fluxes and filtered by upper limits  
  > "To do this we create 10,000 sets of values drawn from the appropriate ranges for each parameter" (Modelling)
   - parameter: Compact T fixed at 102 K; beta between 0 and 4 for both components; lambda_0 at least 24 µm (compact) and 70 µm (extended)  
  > "is fixed as 102 K and the temperature of the extended component must be lower than the compact component." (Modelling)
   - assumption: Rising Spitzer/IRS mid-infrared spectrum fully accounted for by the compact component  
  > "The rising *Spitzer*/IRS spectrum at mid-infrared wavelengths is entirely accounted for by the compact component." (Modelling)
   - fitting: Random sampling of 10,000 parameter sets, rejection by upper limits, error-weighted least-squares for best fit, mean and standard deviation of valid sets  
  > "we calculated the best-fit values for the two components in combination through error weighted least-squares fitting, and the mean and standard deviation of the valid parameter sets in isolation." (Modelling)
   - validation: Parameter sets violating upper limits discarded  
  > "Models that violated the upper limits for either component were discarded from the final ensemble." (Modelling)
3. **Infer minimum grain size and size distribution of the compact dust**: Analytical radiative transfer / debris disc modelling of power-law dust distributions; simulated ALMA images compared to upper limit  
  > "We used simple power-law radial dust and grain size distributions and realistic dust emissivities commonly employed in debris disc modelling." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - software: Standard debris disc modelling tools (name not stated)
   - parameter: Models A-D: disc inner radius 30-60 au, outer 50-90 au, surface brightness exponent 0 or -1, a_min 2-60 µm, a_max 20-1000 µm, size exponent 3.5, dust mass 8-10e-3 Earth masses  
  > "Summary of model parameters used to calculate SEDs presented in Figure [4](#fig:comparison_seds)." (Table 3)
   - assumption: Dust emission optically thin; power-law size distribution with minimum and maximum grain sizes  
  > "We assume the dust emission is optically thin, consistent with the observed fractional luminosity at infrared wavelengths" (Modelling)
   - assumption: White dwarf luminosity 67.6 Lsun and T 103 600 K used for blow-out size of 60 µm  
  > "For the Helix white dwarf ($T_{\rm WD} = 103\,600~\pm~5\,500$ K, $L_{\rm WD} = 67.6~L_{\odot}$), this size is 60 $\mu$m." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Models compared with SED shape and ALMA upper limit  
  > "A broad parameter space of the spatial and grain size distribution was explored" (Radiative transfer modelling)
4. **Constrain gas mass from CO and SiO line non-detections**: 5-sigma integrated line flux upper limits converted to gas masses  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas" (Results)
   - parameter: Line width 10 km/s; T_gas = 100 K  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas ($T_{\rm gas} = 100~K$)" (Results)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and LTE at 100 K  
  > "optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate comet disruption rate needed to supply the dust**: Convert dust mass to Hale-Bopp equivalent bodies and divide by a 10^5 yr deposition time  
  > "Given the observed dust mass of 0.13 $M_{\oplus}$ we can equate this to" (Discussion)
   - parameter: Deposition time 10^5 yr; no loss by radiation pressure assumed  
  > "Further assuming that the dust we currently observe has been deposited over the past $10^{5}~$yrs without any loss due to radiation pressure, etc." (Discussion)
   - assumption: Hale-Bopp comet mass as the body unit; conservative estimate  
  > "Considering that the vast bulk of cometary bodies is icy matter rather than dust alone, this may be considered a conservative estimate of the number required." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Data are incompatible with a compact remnant planetesimal belt or post-AGB disc  
  > "We find the data are incompatible with a compact remnant planetesimal belt or post-asymptotic giant branch disc" (abstract)
- Dust most likely originates from deposition by a cometary cloud  
  > "and conclude the dust most likely originates from deposition by a cometary cloud." (abstract)
- ALMA CO and SiO non-detections rule out substantial gas mass expected from a remnant post-AGB disc  
  > "The non-detection of CO or SiO line emission from the white dwarf rules out the presence of substantial gas mass, contrary to expectations if the excess emission originated from a remnant post-AGB disc." (Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine detections and upper limits from infrared and millimetre data to model the excess and infer its origin  
  > "In the next section we will combine the available detections and upper limits to model the excess emission and infer its likely origin." (Observations)
- Why this method: Dust assumed optically thin, consistent with the fractional luminosity  
  > "We assume the dust emission is optically thin, consistent with the observed fractional luminosity at infrared wavelengths" (Modelling)
- Alternative: Post-AGB circumbinary disc rejected  
  > "We therefore discard the idea of a post-AGB circumbinary disc as the potential origin for the excess emission." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Debris disc (planetesimal belt) disfavoured by small grain size and ALMA non-detection  
  > "The main constraint that speaks against a debris disc as the origin for the observed excess around the Helix Nebula white dwarf is the small grain size required to fit the shape of the SED" (Discussion)
- Alternative: Herschel extended component interpretations (blow-out halo, nebula superposition, post-AGB remnant) left open  
  > "Multiple interpretations could fit the evidence equally well, such as it being a blow-out halo from the compact component" (Results)
- How they decided it was enough: Broad parameter space explored and models checked against the ALMA upper limit  
  > "A broad parameter space of the spatial and grain size distribution was explored, as illustrated in Figure" (Radiative transfer modelling)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA blind to the extended component seen in Herschel/PACS map due to largest angular scale  
  > "which rendered the ALMA observations blind to the extended component seen in the" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Available data offer no real constraint on the nature of the extended component  
  > "The available data offer no real constraint on the nature of the extended component seen in the *Herschel* 70 $\mu$m map" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Comet budget is a conservative estimate; dust loss not modelled  
  > "Again, a conservative assumption as many cometary bodies will not be completely broken up." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO and SiO line sensitivities differ between sections (1.74 mJy/km/s in Observations vs 0.38 mJy/km/s in Results) and continuum rms differs (9 and 11 µJy vs about 10 µJy).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SiO and CO upper limits are both labelled SiO (5-4) in the Results text, so the line assignments are ambiguous.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The modelling tool, dust opacities and priors are not named, so the radiative transfer results cannot be reproduced from the text alone.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The paper calls the ALMA data new observations but also says they were obtained from the archive; data provenance is unclear.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass 0.13 Earth masses is taken from literature yet drives the comet count; the comet number is labelled conservative without error bars.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Herschel and SOFIA photometric and extended-component interpretation is left open, weakening the compact-component inference.
