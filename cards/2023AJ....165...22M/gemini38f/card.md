## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA data**: Calibrate visibility data in CASA and reconstruct continuum and line images using Briggs weighting scripts supplied by the observatory.  
  > "Calibration and reduction of the ALMA observations were carried out in CASA using the appropriate CASA version (4.5.3 in C3 and 4.7.6 in C4). Image reconstruction was carried out using scripts supplied by the observatory, adopting Briggs weighting" (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs weighting  
  > "adopting Briggs weighting as a compromise between signal-to-noise and resolution." (2 Observations)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of residual maps and check of rms sensitivity across continuum and line channels  
  > "In the C3 data, the measured continuum sensitivity was 9 μJy with 3-σ line sensitivities of 1.38 mJy/km/s for 12CO (2-1) and 1.53 mJy/km/s for 29SiO (5-4)." (2 Observations)
2. **Measure fluxes and spatial components in far-IR and mm data**: Decompose emission into a compact point-like component convolved with instrument PSF and an extended component modeled as a 2D Gaussian profile; derive 3-σ upper limits for non-detections in SOFIA and ALMA.  
  > "The compact component is modelled as a point source using a wavelength appropriate PSF model, whilst the extended component is modelled as a 2D Gaussian profile. The convolved model is then subtracted from the observation and significant residuals identified in the image." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - parameter: Compact: point source; Extended: 2D Gaussian  
  > "The compact component is modelled as a point source using a wavelength appropriate PSF model, whilst the extended component is modelled as a 2D Gaussian profile." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - assumption: ALMA extended disc 3-sigma limit assumes a face-on spatially resolved disc 3 beams in diameter  
  > "whilst the ‘Extended’ measurement for ALMA references the 3-σ limit on the presence of a face-on, spatially resolved disc 3 beams in diameter." (2 Observations)
   - fitting: Error-weighted least-squares fitting of 2D models to image data  
  > "we calculated the best-fit values for the two components in combination through error weighted least-squares fitting" (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - validation: Evaluation of image residuals after model subtraction  
  > "The convolved model is then subtracted from the observation and significant residuals identified in the image." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
3. **Calculate gas mass upper limits from ALMA line non-detections**: Derive 5-sigma integrated line flux limits for CO (2-1) and SiO (5-4) and convert to gas masses under LTE and optically thin assumptions.  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas (T_gas = 100 K), we derive 5-σ upper limits to the integrated line fluxes of 2.3×10^-17 W/m^2 for SiO (5-4) and 3.1×10^-17 W/m^2 for SiO (5-4)." (4.1 Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: Line width 10 km/s, T_gas = 100 K  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas (T_gas = 100 K)" (4.1 Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Assuming a line width of 10 km/s (typical for debris discs), optically thin emission, and local thermodynamic equilibrium for the gas (T_gas = 100 K)" (4.1 Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - fitting: Analytical conversion from flux limit to mass  
  > "we derive 5-σ upper limits to the integrated line fluxes of 2.3×10^-17 W/m^2 for SiO (5-4) and 3.1×10^-17 W/m^2 for SiO (5-4). These limits are equivalent to gas masses of 1.2×10^-4 M_⊕ in CO and 2.7×10^-2 M_⊕ in SiO." (4.1 Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit SED with two-component modified blackbodies**: Simultaneous grid/Monte Carlo search over 10,000 parameter combinations (T_bb, lambda_0, beta), filtering out models violating upper limits and performing error-weighted least-squares fitting on valid models.  
  > "To do this we create 10,000 sets of values drawn from the appropriate ranges for each parameter (T_bb, λ_0, β) and scale the resultant modified blackbodies to the measured flux density of the system. Models that violated the upper limits for either component were discarded from the final ensemble." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - software: NumPy, astropy
   - parameter: T_bb_compact = 102 K fixed; beta in [0, 4]; lambda_0 >= 24 um (compact) and >= 70 um (extended)  
  > "The temperature of the compact component, being strongly constrained by the Spitzer/IRS spectrum (cite: 2007Su), is fixed as 102 K and the temperature of the extended component must be lower than the compact component." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting); "The break wavelength (λ_0) for both components must be at least the shortest wavelength for which a detection exists (i.e. 24 μm for the compact component and 70 μm for the extended component)." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - assumption: Mid-IR rising spectrum accounted for entirely by compact component; optically thin modified blackbody emission  
  > "The rising Spitzer/IRS spectrum at mid-infrared wavelengths is entirely accounted for by the compact component." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - fitting: Error-weighted least-squares fitting and mean/standard deviation of valid parameter sets  
  > "we calculated the best-fit values for the two components in combination through error weighted least-squares fitting, and the mean and standard deviation of the valid parameter sets in isolation." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
   - validation: Comparison to SOFIA and ALMA upper limits  
  > "Models that violated the upper limits for either component were discarded from the final ensemble." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
5. **Simulate dust disc SED and ALMA images using radiative transfer**: Use analytical debris disc radiative transfer modeling with power-law radial and grain size distributions to simulate SED and spatially resolved ALMA images.  
  > "We used standard debris disc modelling tools to fit the far-infrared SED of the compact excess (cite: 2012Ertel) and simulate spatially resolved observations (cite: 2011Ertel)." (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling); "For each model fit to the SED, a spatially resolved ALMA image of the emission was simulated and compared to the ALMA upper limit." (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: R_in, R_out, alpha, a_min, a_max, gamma = 3.5, M_dust  
  > "Summary of model parameters used to calculate SEDs presented in Figure 4." (Table 3)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission, power law size distribution, power law surface brightness distribution  
  > "We assume the dust emission is optically thin, consistent with the observed fractional luminosity at infrared wavelengths (L_dust/L_WD ≃ 10^-3)" (3.2 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - fitting: Exploration of discrete model configurations (Models A, B, C, D)  
  > "A broad parameter space of the spatial and grain size distribution was explored, as illustrated in Figure 4, and the exact model parameters for each scenario are summarised in Table 3." (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - validation: Simulated ALMA images compared directly against ALMA millimetre upper limits  
  > "For each model fit to the SED, a spatially resolved ALMA image of the emission was simulated and compared to the ALMA upper limit." (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA Band 6 observations do not detect continuum emission or CO (2-1) / 29SiO (5-4) line emission from the Helix white dwarf.  
  > "The ALMA observations of the Helix white dwarf are also non-detections, both in continuum and line measurements." (4.1 Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
- The steep SED slope and ALMA millimetre non-detection require small, micron-sized dust grains far below the radiation blowout size (60 μm), ruling out a steady-state collisional debris disc or remnant post-AGB disc.  
  > "The steep slope of the SED can only be explained by the presence of small, micron sized grains that dominate the emission but have a very low emissivity at long wavelengths" (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling); "These grains cannot be on stable orbits around the Helix central star due to their removal by radiation pressure and Poynting-Robertson drag, and must be replenished continuously." (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling)
- The observed dust emission is most likely fueled by the ongoing disruption of thousands of Hale-Bopp equivalent comets per year, originating from a disrupted Kuiper belt analogue.  
  > "The measured dust mass, and lifetime of the constituent grains, implies disruption of several thousand Hale-Bopp equivalent comets per year to fuel the observed excess emission around the Helix Nebula’s white dwarf." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 6 provides high angular resolution and sensitivity to detect cool dust continuum and diagnostic gas lines (CO, SiO) indicative of post-AGB discs or debris belts.  
  > "Two common species, CO and SiO, were covered by spectral windows in the ALMA observations. These lines are diagnostic of emission from post-AGB envelopes and icy planetesimal belts." (3.1 Image analysis and modified blackbody fitting)
- Why this method: Separating compact and extended components on PACS images and simulating resolved ALMA synthetic observations tests whether spatial dilution or small grain sizes cause the mm non-detection.  
  > "For each model fit to the SED, a spatially resolved ALMA image of the emission was simulated and compared to the ALMA upper limit." (4.2 Radiative transfer modelling)
- Alternative: Remnant post-AGB circumbinary disc: rejected due to non-detection of CO and SiO lines and absence of mm dust continuum.  
  > "The non-detection of CO or SiO line emission from the white dwarf rules out the presence of substantial gas mass, contrary to expectations if the excess emission originated from a remnant post-AGB disc." (5 Discussion); "We therefore discard the idea of a post-AGB circumbinary disc as the potential origin for the excess emission." (5 Discussion)
- Alternative: In-situ collisional destruction or sublimation of remnant planetesimals (classical debris disc): rejected because grain blowout size is ~60 μm, inconsistent with the required micron-sized grains.  
  > "Steep sub-millimetre slopes have been observed at far-infrared or millimetre wavelengths for a handful of debris disc systems... However, in each case the minimum dust grain sizes inferred for those debris disc systems is consistent with the blow out grain size expected from radiation pressure... which is explicitly not the case here." (5 Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The nature of the extended component seen in Herschel 70 μm is poorly constrained due to weak upper limits on either side.  
  > "The available data offer no real constraint on the nature of the extended component seen in the Herschel 70 μm map with weak upper limits either side of that to constrain its behaviour." (4.1 Imaging and spectral energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): SOFIA/HAWC+ Band A observation suffered from low sensitivity (rms 37 mJy/beam), resulting in a non-detection.  
  > "The new SOFIA/HAWC+ 53 μm observation has a higher angular resolution than the previous Spitzer and Herschel 70 μm observations, but has much lower sensitivity and is a non-detection for the expected excess emission." (2 Observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA non-detections could in principle arise from extreme extended spatial dilution beyond the 4 arcsec maximum recoverable scale if dust was very widely distributed.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-detection of CO gas could alternatively be attributed to rapid photodissociation in the intense UV field of the 100,000 K central white dwarf rather than low initial gas production.
