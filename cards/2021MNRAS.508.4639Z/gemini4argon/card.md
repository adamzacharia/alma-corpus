## Qualitative findings

- The 'blue profile' of HCO+ lines in single-dish observations of G286 is caused by emission from two spatially separated sub-clumps at different velocities rather than global infall.  
  > "We find that the "blue profile" (an indicator of gas infall) of HCO$^+$ lines in single dish observations of G286 is actually caused by gas emission from the two sub-clumps rather than gas infall." (Abstract)
- Although the blue profile does not trace infall, G286 has a virial mass (~126 M_sun) much smaller than its total mass (~1021 M_sun) and is still gravitationally bound and likely undergoing overall collapse.  
  > "Then the virial mass of G286 clump is $M_{Vir} \sim 126~$M$_{\odot}$, which is significantly smaller than the total mass of G286 clump. This indicates that the G286 clump is still bounded by gravity and may be undergoing overall collapse." (Is G286 undergoing global collapse?)
- Energetic outflows driven by G286c1 carve a cavity in the clump and possibly trigger core G286c3, but lack the momentum and energy to drive expansion of the two sub-clumps.  
  > "Energetic outflows are identified in G286 but the outflows are not strong enough to drive expansion of the two sub-clumps." (Abstract); "These evidences suggest that the formation of G286c3 is possibly triggered by the outflow driven by G286c1." (Molecular Outflows and shocked gas)
- The 'NW-SE' and 'NE-SW' filaments exhibit strong velocity gradients perpendicular to their major axes, likely formed by compression from nearby HII regions A and B, and collide to form the 'L' type structure and massive core G286c1 at the junction.  
  > "The two parts of the "L" type filament (“NW-SE” and “NE-SW” filaments) show prominent velocity gradients perpendicular to their major axes, indicating that they are likely formed due to large-scale compression flows." (Abstract); "The “NW-SE” and “NE-SW” filaments seem to be in collision, and a large amount of gas has been accumulated in the junction region where the most massive core G286c1 forms." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA 12-m array and ACA 7m Band 3 observations from the ATOMS survey provide high angular resolution (<2'', ~0.024 pc) to resolve dense cores and internal kinematics while ACA 7m recovers larger spatial scales (~73'') comparable to single-dish beams.  
  > "The achieved angular resolutions of the 12-m array observations for molecular lines are usually better than 2$\ensuremath{''}$ (or $\sim$0.024 pc at 2.5 kpc distance), enabling us to resolve dense cores ($\sim0.1$ pc in size) in G286." (ALMA observations); "The FOV of ACA 7m array is about two times larger than the ALMA 12-m array, and thus ACA 7m array data are useful to study gas kinematics at a larger scale." (Molecular line emission from single dish and ACA 7m observation)
- Why this method: Narrow velocity range [-23, -16.5] km s^-1 used for moment 1 maps to reduce outflow contamination; HC3N (11-10) used to trace dense cores and filaments; CCH (1-0) used to trace low-density PDR gas near HII region A.  
  > "To reduce outflow contamination, we make moment 1 maps in the narrow velocity range of -23 km s$^{-1}$ to -16.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Molecular line emission from combined ALMA 12-m array and ACA data); "HC$_{3}$N(11-10) is a good tracer of dense molecular gas (cite: Liu2020)." (Molecular line emission from combined ALMA 12-m array and ACA data); "CCH is a good tracer of low density PDR regions (cite: Cuadrado2015)." (Formation of the "L" type filament and the G286 protocluster)
- Alternative: Global infall as the sole cause of the single-dish HCO+ blue profile (rejected in favor of two velocity-separated sub-clumps)  
  > "We find that the "blue profile" (an indicator of gas infall) of HCO$^+$ lines in single dish observations of G286 is actually caused by gas emission from the two sub-clumps rather than gas infall." (Abstract)
- Alternative: Outflow feedback driving the expansion of the two sub-clumps and large-scale velocity gradients (rejected based on momentum and energy ratios)  
  > "From above analysis, we argue that the outflow feedback in G286 is not sufficient to drive expansion of the two sub-clumps or to cause the large-scale velocity gradients across the whole G286 region." (Feedback of outflows)
- Alternative: Large-scale shocked flows induced by supersonic turbulence causing the SW-NE velocity gradient (considered as an alternative to HII region B compression that cannot be ruled out)  
  > "However, we cannot rule out the possibility that the large-scale velocity gradient along south-west to north-east direction is caused by large-scale shocked flows induced by supersonic turbulence (cite: Chen2020)." (Formation of the "L" type filament and the G286 protocluster)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The collision origin for SiO knots C, D, E, and F cannot be fully confirmed with the present data.  
  > "As discussed in Sec. 4.3, the SiO knots C, D, E, and F may originate from a collision scenario. However, we cannot fully confirm this scenario with present data." (Molecular Outflows and shocked gas)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in excitation temperature, optical depths, and abundances affect the outflow-to-subclump momentum and energy analysis, and projection effects are not considered in the dynamical time calculation.  
  > "Although there are many uncertainties (e.g., excitation temperature, optical depths and abundances) in above analysis, the momentum of the outflows seems much smaller than that of the two gas clumps." (Feedback of outflows); "However, here we don’t consider the projection effect." (Feedback of outflows)
- Limitation (authors): Complex environment around G286c1 makes it difficult to accurately measure the filament spine there.  
  > "Based on the star formation history we described in Sec. 4.3, the surrounding environment of G286c1 is very complex, which may make it difficult to accurately measure the spine of filament." (Width of the longest filament)
- Limitation (authors): Compression from HII region B cannot be fully determined due to its distance from HII region A and G286.  
  > "Hii region B is at a certain distance from Hii region A and G286 clump. Therefore, we cannot fully determine the compression from Hii region B." (Probable interactions between G286 clump, HII region A and HII region B)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow mass and momentum ratios assume optically thin HCO+ (1-0) line wings and constant excitation temperature equal to H13CO+ (1-0), which can underestimate outflow momentum if the wings are moderately optically thick.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination angles are not accounted for in the outflow dynamical time, momentum, energy, or PV velocity gradient estimates.
