## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure dust continuum properties of HOPS-288-A and HOPS-288-B**: 2D Gaussian fitting on the 1.3 mm dust continuum image from the high-resolution dataset (#2018.1.01038.S)  
  > "We applied 2D Gaussian fitting on the dust continuum image and summarize the 2D Gaussian fitting results in Table [\[tab:fit2D\]](#tab:fit2D)." (Dust)
   - assumption: Source A treated as a single system since neither dataset resolves its local binarity  
  > "Since neither of the two data sets resolves the local binarity of source A, in this study we treat source A as a single system." (Observations)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting  
  > "We applied 2D Gaussian fitting on the dust continuum image and summarize the 2D Gaussian fitting results in Table [\[tab:fit2D\]](#tab:fit2D)." (Dust)
   - validation: Comparison of 1.3 mm dust continuum images from high-resolution (#2018.1.01038.S) and wide-bandwidth (#2018.1.00302.S) datasets  
  > "For completeness, we show the comparisons of the 1.3 mm dust continuum images from both high-resolution (#2018.1. 01038.S) and wide-bandwidth (#2018.1.00302.S) in Figure [17](#fig:appx_img)." (Dust)
2. **Model circumbinary disk rotation and estimate central mass**: Extract C18O position-velocity diagram along cut penetrating source A and B (PA = 127 deg) and fit Keplerian rotation model  
  > "To model the possible disk motion, we extracted a position–velocity (PV) diagram along the cut penetrating source A and B (PA = $127^{\circ}$), as indicated by the purple line in Figure [3](#fig:img_CBD)(a)." (Circumbinary Disk); "We assumed that the circumbinary disk follows Keplerian rotation around a central mass ($M_\mathrm{cen}$) as $v = \left ( GM_\mathrm{cen}/R \right )^{1/2}$, where $R$ is the radius." (Circumbinary Disk)
   - parameter: PA = 127 deg; systemic velocity v_CBD = 5.0 km s^-1; outer radius = 1.25" (500 au)  
  > "To model the possible disk motion, we extracted a position–velocity (PV) diagram along the cut penetrating source A and B (PA = $127^{\circ}$), as indicated by the purple line in Figure [3](#fig:img_CBD)(a)." (Circumbinary Disk); "The reference velocity, defined as the system velocity of the circumbinary disk $v_\mathrm{CBD}$, was set at 5.0 km s$^{-1}$ based on the best symmetry of the PV diagram." (Circumbinary Disk); "Based on the spatial extent of the blueshifted component at $\Delta p = -0\hbox{$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}75$, we adopt an outer radius of $1\hbox{$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}25$ (500 au) for the circumbinary disk." (Circumbinary Disk)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation around a central mass M_cen; distance of 400 pc  
  > "We assumed that the circumbinary disk follows Keplerian rotation around a central mass ($M_\mathrm{cen}$) as $v = \left ( GM_\mathrm{cen}/R \right )^{1/2}$, where $R$ is the radius." (Circumbinary Disk)
   - fitting: Grid search over M_cen from 1 to 10 M_sun (step 0.25 M_sun) and inclination angle phi from 75 deg to 105 deg (step 5 deg), evaluated by sum of squared error between relative velocity at each relative position (5 sigma maximum contour)  
  > "We constructed a grid of parameters by varying the central mass ($M_\mathrm{cen}$) from 1 to 10 $M_\odot$ in steps of 0.25 $M_\odot$." (Circumbinary Disk); "We also varied the inclination angle ($\varphi$), defined such that $90^\circ$ corresponds to a perfectly edge-on geometry, between $75^\circ$ and $105^\circ$ in steps of $5^\circ$." (Circumbinary Disk); "The fitting was evaluated by the sum of squared error between the relative velocity ($\Delta v$) at each relative position ($\Delta p$)." (Circumbinary Disk)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Analyze COM morphology and kinematics and derive individual stellar masses**: Generate moment 0 maps and extract PV diagrams of CH3OH and stacked COM transitions along major axes; fit linear velocity-position relation to Keplerian rotation  
  > "To investigate the kinematics of the COM-rich gas components, we extracted PV diagrams of CH$_3$OH along the major axis of each source." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol); "We further model the rotation motion for source A and source B, which are based on the black dashed lines in Figures [6](#fig:PV_CH3OH)(a) and (e)." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol); "For CH$_2$DOH, CH$_3$CHO, HCOOCH$_3$, C$_2$H$_5$OH, C$_2$H$_5$CN, multiple transitions were stacked to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)." (Diversities and Similarities between Complex Organics)
   - parameter: Reference velocities: 3.5 km s^-1 (source A), 6.5 km s^-1 (source B); outer rotation radii: 200 au (0.50", source A), 40 au (0.1", source B)  
  > "The values are 3.5 and 6.5  km s$^{-1}$ for source A and source B, respectively." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol); "Given a distance of 400 pc (cite: 2011Lombardi_2MASS_extinction_IV) and adopting the outer boundaries of the PV diagrams as the sizes of the rotating features, 200 au (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$50) for source A and 40 au (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$1) for source B, the inferred stellar masses are 2.9 $M_\odot$ for source A and 1.1 $M_\odot$ for source B." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol)
   - assumption: Disk mass much smaller than stellar mass; edge-on geometry; Keplerian rotation; distance = 400 pc  
  > "Assuming that the disk mass is much smaller than the stellar mass and an edge-on geometry, the relative velocity along the line of sight ($\Delta v$) at a relative position ($\Delta p$) following of a Keplerian rotation is:" (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol); "Given a distance of 400 pc (cite: 2011Lombardi_2MASS_extinction_IV) and adopting the outer boundaries of the PV diagrams as the sizes of the rotating features, 200 au (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$50) for source A and 40 au (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$1) for source B, the inferred stellar masses are 2.9 $M_\odot$ for source A and 1.1 $M_\odot$ for source B." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol)
   - fitting: Visual inspection of linear correlation between Delta p and Delta v in PV diagrams  
  > "The black dashed lines, selected by visual inspection, are intended to illustrate the linearity." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol)
   - validation: Sum of the two stellar masses (4.0 M_sun) compared with central mass (~5.00 M_sun) estimated from C18O circumbinary disk motion  
  > "The sum of the two stellar masses (4.0 $M_\odot$) is comparable to the value ($\sim$5.00 $M_\odot$) estimated from the circumbinary disk motion traced by C$^{18}$O in Section [3.3](#sec:CBD)." (Kinematics Revealed by Methanol)
4. **Synthesize molecular spectra and derive excitation temperatures and column densities**: Extract spectra at dust continuum peaks of source A and B from wide-bandwidth data (#2018.1.00302.S) and fit synthetic LTE spectra using XCLASS  
  > "To minimize mutual contamination between the two sources, given the comparable beam size (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$37) and their close separation (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$5), we extracted spectra at the dust continuum peaks of each source." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "We used `XCLASS` (cite: 2017Moller_XCLASS) to evaluate the excitation temperature and total column density for each molecular species." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization)
   - software: XCLASS
   - parameter: Fixed source size = 0.37"; fixed FWHM line width = 5 km s^-1; fixed velocity offsets = 2.0 km s^-1 (source A) and 6.5 km s^-1 (source B)  
  > "To reduce degeneracy in the parameter fitting, we fixed the source size to 0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$37, the line width FWHM to 5 km s$^{-1}$, and the velocity offsets to 2.0 and 6.5 km s$^{-1}$ for source A and source B, respectively." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE); fixed T_ex = 200 K for species with insufficient transitions (DCN, H2S, HCCCN, and CH3OH v12=1 in source B)  
  > "`XCLASS` calculates the synthetic spectra of molecular species under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), given molecular parameter inputs such as source size, excitation temperature ($T_\mathrm{ex}$), total column density ($N_\mathrm{tot}$), line width ($\delta v$), and velocity offset ($v_\mathrm{off}$)." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "Table notes: $\dagger$The temperature is assumed to be 200 K in the simulations." (Diversities and Similarities between Complex Organics)
   - fitting: Step 1: Manual N_tot adjustment at fixed T_ex = 200 K; Step 2: Independent Nested Sampling per species (T_ex in [100, 300] K, N_tot in [0.01, 100] x initial); Step 3: Simultaneous multi-species Nested Sampling (T_ex within +/-50 K of Step 2, N_tot in [0.1, 10] x Step 2); Step 4: MCMC for error estimation  
  > "2.  Independent Fitting per Species. For each molecular species, we independently optimized $T_\mathrm{ex}$ and $N_\mathrm{tot}$ using the “Nested Sampling (NS)” algorithm." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "3.  Simultaneous Multi-Species Fitting. All molecular species were then fitted simultaneously using the NS algorithm." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "4.  Error Estimation. Finally, we estimated the uncertainties of the values derived from Step3 using the “Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)” method." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization)
   - validation: Visual inspection of observed vs. synthesized spectra across all spectral windows  
  > "Figures [13](#fig:appx_spec_spw3)-[16](#fig:appx_spec_spw1) in appendix show the observed and the synthesized spectra for both sources." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization)
5. **Compare COM column density ratios and isotopic ratios**: Compute 12C/13C and D/H ratios of CH3OH and compare COM column density ratios between source B and source A and with literature Orion hot corinos  
  > "The $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratios of CH$_3$OH, derived from the column density ratio between CH$_3$OH $v=0$ and $^{13}$CH$_3$OH, are 8 and 13 for source A and source B, respectively." (Column Densities of COMs); "To further examine the observed chemical similarity between source A and source B, we compare the COM column densities between source A and those of other hot corinos in Orion that are known to exhibit rotation motions or be disk-origin" (Hot corinos exhibiting rotation motion in the Orion)
   - assumption: ISM 12C/13C ratio of 68 adopted when deriving adjusted D/H ratios from 13CH3OH  
  > "Adopting the column density of $^{13}$CH$_3$OH with a $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio of 68 (cite: 2005Milam_12C13C), the D/H ratios become 0.05 and 0.02 for source A and source B, respectively." (Column Densities of COMs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- HOPS-288 hosts two CO outflows launched from sources A and B and a C18O circumbinary disk viewed nearly edge-on.  
  > "1.  We identified two CO outflows, each associated with one source, along with a C$^{18}$O circumbinary disk." (Conclusions)
- Three COM-rich features are identified: two rotating hot corinos at HOPS-288-A and HOPS-288-B, and an intervening component between them traced by low-E_u CH3OH and tentatively CH3CHO.  
  > "Three COM-rich features are revealed: two hot corinos associated with the two members, rich in a variety of COMs, and an intervening component between the two members traced by CH$_3$OH and tentatively by CH$_3$CHO." (Abstract)
- COM column density ratios between HOPS-288-B and HOPS-288-A cluster around 0.1–0.2, indicating similar complex organic compositions between the two binary members.  
  > "As shown in Figure [12](#fig:colDensRatio_lit), these ratios cluster around a value of $\sim$0.1–0.2." (Hot corinos exhibiting rotation motion in the Orion)
- NH2CHO is concentrated in a more compact inner region (~0.1"–0.2") of HOPS-288-A compared to other COMs.  
  > "These characteristics suggest that NH$_2$CHO resides in the more inner part (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$1 in Figure [9](#fig:PV_COM_Cmajor)(h)) of source A, which is further supported by its limited spatial extent that less extended (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$2 in Figure [8](#fig:mom0_COM)(h)) along the major axis of source A=." (Diversities and Similarities between Complex Organics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (#2018.1.01038.S) data are used to resolve morphologies and kinematics of selected tracers, while wide-bandwidth (#2018.1.00302.S) data are used for multi-line chemical composition fitting.  
  > "Owing to its higher spatial and spectral resolutions, this dataset is primarily used to investigate the morphologies and kinematics of selected molecular tracers." (Observations); "Given its broad spectral coverage, this dataset is primarily used for deriving chemical compositions." (Observations)
- Why this method: Spectra were extracted at dust continuum peaks to minimize mutual contamination between the two close sources (0.5" separation vs 0.37" beam), and source size, line width, and velocity offset were fixed in XCLASS to reduce fitting degeneracy.  
  > "To minimize mutual contamination between the two sources, given the comparable beam size (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$37) and their close separation (0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$5), we extracted spectra at the dust continuum peaks of each source." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "To reduce degeneracy in the parameter fitting, we fixed the source size to 0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$37, the line width FWHM to 5 km s$^{-1}$, and the velocity offsets to 2.0 and 6.5 km s$^{-1}$ for source A and source B, respectively." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization)
- Alternative: Quiescent bridge scenario (as in IRAS 16293-2422) rejected for the intervening CH3OH component because the tail is not seen in dust continuum and a quiescent bridge cannot easily explain CH3OH desorption.  
  > "Therefore, the quiescent bridge scenario seen in IRAS 16293–2422 does not match HOPS-288." (CH$_3$OH-rich feature between source A and source B)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Neither ALMA dataset resolves the 50 au inner binary of HOPS-288-A, so source A is treated as a single source.  
  > "Since neither of the two data sets resolves the local binarity of source A, in this study we treat source A as a single system." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot rule out that the tail-like morphology of the intervening CH3OH component is an artifact caused by beam convolution.  
  > "The intervening component that appears as a tail connecting the two sources in Figure [4](#fig:mom0_CH3OH)(a) could represent a physical bridge between them, although we cannot rule out the possibility that this structure is an artifact caused by beam convolution." (Morphology of Methanol)
- Limitation (authors): Fixed source size (0.37"), line width, and velocity centroid in XCLASS fitting do not capture species-dependent spatial extents or unresolved compact sizes, potentially underestimating column densities.  
  > "We note that in source A, the COM emission extends slightly beyond the beam, meaning that our analysis captures only the bright central region." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "However, in our analysis, these parameters were fixed, which could introduce uncertainties in the derived column densities." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Dust opacity and radiative pumping of high-E_u transitions are not accounted for and could lead to underestimated or uncertain column densities.  
  > "In addition, high–upper-energy transitions of CH$_3$OH may be affected by radiative pumping in addition to collisional excitation." (Molecular Spectrum Synthesization); "Meanwhile, we do not account for the potential effect of dust opacity, which could lead to underestimated molecular column densities." (Caveats)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The linear velocity-position relations for the hot corinos in sources A and B are selected by visual inspection rather than quantitative kinematic modeling (e.g., 3DBarolo or radiative transfer disk models).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing the emitting source size to 0.37" for both sources in XCLASS ignores differential beam dilution between the more extended source A and more compact source B, which directly scales the B/A column density ratio.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a minor text inconsistency between Section 5.2 (12C/13C ratios of 8 and 13 for sources A and B) and Section 6.3 (12 and 8 for sources A and B).
