## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce, image and continuum-subtract the cubes**: Pipeline-reduced data, natural-weighted imaging, continuum subtraction with line-free channels, 60 km/s channels  
  > "For our imaging process we applied natural weighting." (ALMA observations and analysis); "For both sources, the channel width was set to 60 km s$^{-1}$." (ALMA observations and analysis)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Channel width 60 km/s  
  > "For both sources, the channel width was set to 60 km s$^{-1}$." (ALMA observations and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Estimate emission extent and extract spectra**: Moment-0 map of each line, imfit for size estimate, spectrum extracted from a region equal to two times the size estimate  
  > "We created the moment-0 map of each emission line, and used the imfit tool of casa to get an estimate of the extent of the emission." (ALMA observations and analysis); "We then extracted the spectrum from a region that is equivalent to two times the size estimate of the source." (ALMA observations and analysis)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: Region = 2x imfit size  
  > "We then extracted the spectrum from a region that is equivalent to two times the size estimate of the source." (ALMA observations and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit line profiles and derive fluxes and luminosities**: Single versus double Gaussian fits to H2O; for W0410-0913 a simultaneous fit of a double Gaussian CO(9-8) and a single Gaussian OH+; integrated flux for lower limit when truncated (W0149+2350)  
  > "we integrate the measured flux of the line, to calculate a lower limit of the total integrated line flux" (H2O(2_02-1_11) in W0149+2350); "We do this by using a double Gaussian fit for the CO(9$-$8), and a single Gaussian for the OH$^+$ emission." (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
   - parameter: Central velocity of each CO component restricted within 400 km/s and of OH+ within 200 km/s of expected value  
  > "restricting the central velocity ($\upsilon_{\rm cen}$) of each component of the CO line within a range of 400 km s$^{-1}$, and for the OH$^+$ line within 200 km s$^{-1}$" (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
   - assumption: OH+ assumed to be in emission based on the observed spectrum  
  > "We note that in our analysis we have assumed that the OH$^+$ is in emission based on the observed spectrum" (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits with all three parameters free; priors/sampler/likelihood not stated  
  > "We allow all three parameters of the Gaussians to vary during the fit" (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
   - validation: Double versus single Gaussian compared via chi2 improvement (22% for W0149+2350, 12% for W0410-0913)  
  > "A double Gaussian fit to the line improves the $\chi^2$ by 22% compared to the single Gaussian fit." (H2O(2_02-1_11) in W0149+2350); "A double Gaussian fit to the line improves the $\chi^2$ by 12% compared to the single Gaussian fit." (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
4. **Compare spatial extents of H2O, CO(9-8) and OH+**: Velocity-limited moment-0 maps (-300 to 750 km/s for W0149+2350, -500 to 500 km/s for W0410-0913) fitted with imfit, sizes converted to kpc  
  > "we create moment-0 maps limited to the velocity range of $-300 < \upsilon < 750$ km s$^{-1}$, for both the H$_2$O and CO emission" (H2O(2_02-1_11) in W0149+2350); "we create moment-0 maps limited to the velocity range of $-500 < \upsilon < 500$ km s$^{-1}$" (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Constrain H2O excitation with radiative transfer**: Non-LTE RADEX modelling using peak fluxes of H2O and CO(9-8)  
  > "we take a simplistic modelling approach using the non-LTE molecular radiative transfer code radex (cite: vanderTak07)" (Excitation of the H2O and OH+ emission); "We use the peak fluxes for each of the observed H$_2$O lines and the corresponding CO($9-8$) line, for each source." (Excitation of the H2O and OH+ emission)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: CO column densities 1e16-1e17 cm^-2  
  > "CO column densities in the range of $10^{16}-10^{17}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Excitation of the H2O and OH+ emission)
   - assumption: Spherical symmetry; AGN torus dust temperature 450 K, star-formation dust temperatures 51 K and 63 K  
  > "we expect an AGN torus dust temperature of 450 K, and a dust temperature due to star-formation of 51 K and 63 K" (Excitation of the H2O and OH+ emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare line-to-IR luminosity and line ratios with literature**: L_H2O/L_IR (using L_IR,tot and L_IR,SF) and H2O/CO, OH+/H2O ratios compared with ULIRGs, SMGs, AGN and Mrk231  
  > "For this reason we examine both of the possible scenarios when comparing to the literature." (The H2O-IR luminosity ratio of Hot DOGs)
   - assumption: Only sources with the same H2O transition are compared  
  > "We only compare to sources with the same H$_2$O transition available, as each of the sources." (The H2O-IR luminosity ratio of Hot DOGs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- H2O line profiles resemble the CO(9-8) profiles, but H2O emission is more compact than CO(9-8)  
  > "However, the H$_{2}$O emission seems to be more compact than the CO(9$-$8)." (abstract)
- OH+ is detected in emission in W0410-0913, and H2O/CO and OH+/H2O ratios are comparable to luminous AGN in the literature  
  > "The H$_2$O/CO line ratio of both Hot DOGs and the OH$^+$/H$_2$O line ratio of W0410$-$0913 are comparable to those of luminous AGN found in the literature." (abstract)
- Cannot confidently separate AGN and star-formation excitation of H2O with a single transition  
  > "We cannot confidently distinguish between excitation due to the AGN or star-formation or both for the H$_2$O emission." (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Lines are serendipitous in sidebands of Band-6 observations aimed at CO(9-8)  
  > "These detections were serendipitous, falling within the side-bands of Band-6 observations aimed to study CO(9$-$8) in these Hot DOGs." (abstract)
- Why this method: Only one H2O transition per source, so a simplistic RADEX approach is used  
  > "As we only have a single H$_2$O line detection for each source, it is not possible to make an extensive analysis." (Excitation of the H2O and OH+ emission)
- Alternative: OH+ P-Cygni profile or a massive excited molecular outflow considered as alternative interpretations of the W0410-0913 feature, judged unlikely  
  > "Such a peculiar line profile is unlikely, and is not seen for the lower$-J$ CO (cite: Fan18) and H$_2$O transitions." (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913); "Furthermore, there is no evidence for such a massive outflow in the lower$-J$ CO transitions (cite: Fan18)." (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): OH+ is blended with CO(9-8) and its profile may differ from the assumed emission profile  
  > "Due to their large linewidths the CO(9$-$8) and OH$^+$ lines are partially blended." (H2O(3_12-3_03) and OH+(1_1-0_1) in W0410-0913)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot confirm AGN origin of OH+ by radiative transfer modelling  
  > "we cannot confirm that the OH$^+$ emission is due to the AGN through radiative transfer modelling" (Excitation of the H2O and OH+ emission)
- Limitation (authors): W0149+2350 H2O line is truncated, so only lower limits on its flux and luminosity  
  > "Although the line is truncated, it covers $\rm \sim1000\,km\,s^{-1}$" (H2O(2_02-1_11) in W0149+2350)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one H2O line per source, so excitation conclusions rest on a simplistic RADEX grid with unreported inputs
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Short integrations (6 min for W0410-0913) and low-S/N double-Gaussian fits with large errors on component parameters
