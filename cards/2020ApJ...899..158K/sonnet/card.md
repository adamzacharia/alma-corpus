## Analysis recipe

1. **match datasets**: smooth to common beam, reproject to common grid, match spectral resolution, restrict FoV, add beam-correlated Gaussian noise to GC data  
  > "we add additional beam-correlated[^1] Gaussian noise to both GC images to match them to the higher noise of the NGC253 observations." (Data)
   - software: spectral-cube, Astropy
   - parameter: FoV 1500x750 pc (CO 1-0), 800x400 pc (CO 3-2); velocity ±250 km/s  
  > "the FoVs are 1500 pc by 750 pc for the wider FoV in CO(1–0) and 800 pc by 400 pc in CO(3–2)." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **identify structures with dendrograms**: ASTRODENDRO with SNR>5, min delta of 1x rms, min volume 3 resolution elements x channel width  
  > "We only consider emission with SNR $>5$ (cf. Table [\[table1\]](#table1)). The minimum difference in intensity between nested structures is set to one times the rms noise." (3.1 Dendrogram structure identification)
   - software: astrodendro
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: tested alternative size and line-width definitions  
  > "We confirm with tests that this definition of size do not affect our analyses as other definitions merely shift the normalization of the sizes" (3.2.1 Size)
3. **measure size, line width, luminosity, mass, column density**: size=radius, line width=v_rms, L=integral of I dA, M=alpha_CO L (r31 for CO 3-2), N=M/A_eff  
  > "For CO(3–2) we calculate $M = \ensuremath{\alpha_\mathrm{CO}} r_{31} L$" (3.2.4 Mass)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: r31=0.67  
  > "we adopt $r_{31} = I_{3-2}\,I_{1-0} ^{-1} = 0.67$ for both galaxies" (3.2.4 Mass)
   - assumption: alpha_CO=2.2 (GC), 1.1 (NGC253) Msun pc-2 (K km/s)-1  
  > "We adopt a lower value $\ensuremath{\alpha_\mathrm{CO}}= 1.1$ M$_\odot$ pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$, i.e., one quarter the Solar Neighborhood value, for NGC253." (3.2.4 Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **bin and fit power laws**: bin by size (0.1 dex) and by surface density (0.25 dex), medians and 16–84th percentiles; weighted least squares in log-log  
  > "To do this we fit the bin centers and median values as lines in log log space using a weighted least squares minimization." (3.4 Fitting)
   - software: SciPy, NumPy
   - fitting: weighted least squares; errors from covariance diagonals  
  > "We adopt the square root of the diagonals of the covariance matrix as the uncertainties" (3.4 Fitting)
   - validation: degraded CO(3-2) resolution test  
  > "As a test, we degrade the resolution of the CO(3–2) data in NGC253 and repeat the dendrogram analysis." (4.1.3 Comparison of the size – line width relations)
5. **assess virial state**: compare sigma^2/R vs N with virial and pressure-bounded models  
  > "Figure [3](#fig4) plots $\sigma^2/R$ as a function of $N$ for both galaxies and and both lines." (5.1 Virial state of the molecular gas)
   - parameter: Gamma=0.73, f=5/3  
  > "we here use $\Gamma=0.73$ for an isothermal spherical cloud of critical mass" (5.1 Virial state of the molecular gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Low column density gas in NGC253 (N<3e22) shows constant sigma^2/R, not self-gravitating, likely transient or pressure-bound  
  > "This high-velocity dispersion, not gravitationally self-bound gas is likely in transient structures created by the combination of high average densities and feedback in the starburst." (abstract)

## Claim tuples

- H: Do starbursts show increased turbulence at fixed scale? | E: Matched dendrogram size–line width comparison | R: Similar slopes, NGC253 line widths larger by ~2-3 | F: NGC253 has higher velocity dispersion at fixed size | **supported**
- H: Is gas gravitationally bound? | E: sigma^2/R vs N compared to virial models | R: GC bound above 3e21; NGC253 only above 3e22 | F: Excess line width in NGC253 lies in low-N, unbound gas | **supported**

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: dendrograms suit hierarchical, multi-scale data  
  > "The hierarchical nature of the dendrogram approach is ideal to extract multi-scale information from our high spatial dynamic range data." (3.1 Dendrogram structure identification)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Formal fit errors underestimate uncertainty; alpha_CO uncertain and affects virial state  
  > "but note that these statistical errors are often small and systematic errors are non-negligible." (3.4 Fitting)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO adopted per galaxy with no uniform treatment drives virial conclusions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dendrogram structures are not clouds; results depend on astrodendro parameters
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beam and channel not deconvolved; galactic rotation not removed
