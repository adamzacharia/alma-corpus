## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum imaging and subtraction, spectral extraction, and line identification**: Image continuum from line-free channels across 85-116 GHz using multi-frequency synthesis, subtract continuum from visibilities, extract spectra from 12.5'' radius circular apertures centred on continuum peaks (and molecule-specific apertures of radius R_p + 2.5*HWHM), and identify lines using spectroscopic catalogues.  
  > "Continuum maps were produced from the line-free channels across the survey bandwidth (85-116 GHz) using multifrequency synthesis (Rau & Cornwell 2011)." (4.1 Line detection and identification); "The full line-labelled ALMA Band 3 spectra of the three sources, extracted by integrating the intensity in a circular aperture of 12.5'' radius centred on the respective continuum peaks, so as to recover emission from all molecules, are presented in App. A." (4.1. Line detection and identification); "We used the Weeds (Maret et al. 2011) package in GILDAS [5] to identify the emission lines." (4.1. Line detection and identification)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS, Weeds, CDMS, Splatalogue
   - parameter: Survey aperture radius = 12.5''; molecule-specific aperture radius = R_p + 2.5 * HWHM; detection threshold = 3 sigma  
  > "We detect, above the 3σ noise level, a total of 314 spectral emission features across the three stars" (4.1. Line detection and identification); "For each molecule, the aperture from which line spectra are extracted is set to a circular region with a radius equal to the peak position + 3σ of the Gaussian fit to the AARP (i.e., R p + 2.5×HWHM) of the corresponding molecule" (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles)
   - assumption: All observed SiS line emissions are assumed to be primarily thermal in nature.  
  > "In this work, we have therefore assumed that all of the observed SiS line emissions are of primarily thermal nature." (4.1. Line detection and identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Assess line variability between observation epochs**: Compare spectra produced from a subset of visibilities sharing the same uv-coverage between the 2015 and 2016 datasets to isolate time-dependent amplitude variations.  
  > "Hence, to check for potential variability, we compared spectra produced from a subset of visibilities that have the same uv -coverage in both datasets." (4.2. Line Variability)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Relative calibration uncertainty threshold = ~11%  
  > "we estimate a relative calibration uncertainty of ∼11% between the two observations." (4.2. Line Variability)
   - assumption: Spatial expansion of the circumstellar gas over <=1 yr (~6 mas) is negligible compared to the beam size.  
  > "We note that any spatial variation in the circumstellar gas distribution occurring during the interval between these observations (≤ 1 yr) will be far below the spatial resolution of both datasets." (3.3. Data combination)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Increased line intensity uncertainties for variable species (C2H, HC3N) by the observed percentage of variability.  
  > "while increasing the uncertainty in the line intensities of these species by the observed percentage of variability." (3.3. Data combination)
3. **Stack systemic-velocity emission maps to study CSE substructure**: Regrid line cubes to a common 3.3 km/s spectral resolution and velocity grid, and stack systemic-velocity channels across all detected Band 3 transitions per molecule using rms-weighted mean.  
  > "We regridded the line cubes to be stacked to ensure that they have the same spectral resolution (3.3 km/s) and velocity grid, and used the software LineStacker (Jolly et al. 2020) to stack the cubes to extract the rms-weighted mean emission of the channels centred on the systemic velocity for each molecule." (4.3.1. Complex morphology in the CSEs of IRAS 15194 − 5115 and IRAS 15082 − 4808)
   - software: LineStacker
   - parameter: Spectral resolution = 3.3 km/s; no channel averaging; radial cuts at PA = 0 deg and PA = 90 deg  
  > "No channel averaging was performed as it would lead to the substructure visible at the systemic velocity to be smeared out." (4.3.1. Complex morphology in the CSEs of IRAS 15194 − 5115 and IRAS 15082 − 4808); "along a line that intersects the star at PA=0 [◦] (left), and PA=90 [◦] (right)." (Figure 7 caption)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Determine radial extents (R_i, R_e) of molecular emitting regions via AARPs**: Convolve non-blended line cubes to a common circular beam, average channels within ±20% of v_exp around v_sys, compute azimuthally-averaged radial profiles in concentric rings of width equal to the common beam, average line profiles weighted by inverse squared uncertainty, and fit Gaussians to derive beam-deconvolved inner and outer radii.  
  > "the emission from within a range of ±20% of the expansion velocity centred on the systemic velocity was averaged together to increase the signal-to-noise ratio" (4.3.2 Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles); "circular rings of increasing radii, centred on the continuum peak, with widths equal to the common restoring beam." (4.3.2 Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles); "In order to estimate the radial extents of the emitting regions, the mean AARPs of each molecule were fit with Gaussian profiles." (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles)
   - parameter: Velocity range = v_sys ± 0.2 * v_exp; ring width = common restoring beam; centrally-peaked: R_i = 0, R_e = HWHM; shell: R_i, R_e = half-maxima points on either side of peak R_p  
  > "For centrally-peaked morphologies, the peak position ( R p ) of the emission, and hence the inner radius ( R i ) is set to 0. In this case, the outer radius ( R e ) is estimated as the half width at half maximum (HWHM) of the Gaussian fit to the mean AARP." (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles); "In the case of shell emission, the inner and outer radii of the emission regions are given by the half maxima points on either side of the peak position of the Gaussian fit to the AARP." (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles)
   - assumption: Rarer isotopologues of HCN, CS, SiO, and HC3N are co-spatial with their main counterparts since photodissociation occurs via broadband continuum.  
  > "Since the photodissociation of these molecules occurs throughout the broadband continuum (van Dishoeck 1988), and is not limited to narrow frequency ranges of discrete UV lines, their rarer isotopologues are expected to be present in the same spatial range as the main counterparts." (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fitting to the inverse-variance-weighted mean AARP, followed by beam deconvolution.  
  > "The common restoring beam was used to obtain the beam-deconvolved sizes of the brightness distributions." (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles)
   - validation: Verified for each species that different transitions of all isotopologues are emitted from the same region around the central star.

## Qualitative findings

- Molecular brightness distributions divide into centrally-peaked (CS, SiO, SiS, HCN), shell-like (CN, HNC, C2H, C3H, C4H, C3N, HC5N, c-C3H2), and compound central+shell (HC3N, SiC2) morphologies, with identical relative radial ordering across all three stars and IRC +10 216.  
  > "There are two main morphologies of the brightness distributions: centrally-peaked (CS, SiO, SiS, HCN) and shell-like (CN, HNC, C 2 H, C 3 H, C 4 H, C 3 N, HC 5 N, c-C 3 H 2 ). The brightness distributions of HC 3 N and SiC 2 have both a central and a shell component." (abstract); "The qualitative behaviour of the brightness distributions of all detected molecules, in particular their relative locations with respect to the central star, is the same for the three stars, and consistent with those observed towards IRC +10 216." (abstract)
- Cyanopolyynes (HC3N, HC5N) peak at slightly smaller radii (by 10-40%) than hydrocarbons (C2H, C4H), while CN, HNC, and hydrocarbons show the most extended emission.  
  > "Of the shell distributions, the cyanopolyynes peak at slightly smaller radii than the hydrocarbons, and CN and HNC show the most extended emission." (abstract); "Compared to C 2 H and C 4 H, the HC 3 N and the HC 5 N emissions peak at slightly smaller radii in all three CSEs, by an estimated 10 − 40% (Fig. 9)." (4.3.4. Shell species)
- Co-spatial arcs traced by multiple chemically unrelated species (e.g. HC3N, SiC2, C2H, HNC) in IRAS 15194-5115 and IRAS 15082-4808 indicate density enhancements rather than chemical abundance variations, possibly induced by binary companions.  
  > "The arcs seen in the HC 3 N and SiC 2 emissions at the systemic velocity for IRAS 15194−5115 are clearly co-spatial (Fig. 6, top left panel), though these molecules themselves do not share any common chemistry." (5. Discussion); "This suggests that the arc structures seen are, predominantly, the result of density enhancements rather than abundance variations." (5. Discussion)
- First detections of 29SiO, 30SiO, 29SiS, 30SiS, Si34S, and C33S towards the three stars, plus first detections of Si13CC, doubly 13C-substituted HC3N, C6H, C8H, and l-C4H2 towards IRAS 15194-5115.  
  > "We report the first detections of lines of [29] SiO, [30] SiO, [29] SiS, 30 SiS, Si 34 S, and C 33 S towards the three sources." (4.1. Line detection and identification); "We report the first detection of Si [13] CC, and doubly [13] C−substituted isotopologues of HC 3 N towards IRAS 15194−5115, along with the long-chain hydrocarbons C 6 H, C 8 H, l−C 4 H 2, which have not been detected towards this source in previous observations." (4.1. Line detection and identification)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Spatially resolved interferometric spectral surveys in Band 3 (3 mm) directly measure the spatial extents of rotational molecular emitting regions (which single-dish surveys had to infer indirectly) across three C-type AGB stars spanning an order of magnitude in mass-loss rate and a broad range of 12C/13C ratios.  
  > "Our interferometric observations make it possible to directly obtain the spatial extents of the emitting regions for individual stars." (1. Introduction); "They possess expanding, largely spherical CSEs with similar expansion velocities, sampling roughly an order of magnitude in MLRs. They sample a broad range of [12] C/ [13] C ratios (Table 1)" (2. The sources)
- Why this method: Azimuthally-averaged radial profiles (AARPs) with Gaussian fits provide a systematic first-approximation of emitting shell radii to constrain chemical stratification, and LTE population diagrams (supplemented by single-line calculations) provide order-of-magnitude abundance estimates prior to full non-LTE radiative transfer modelling.  
  > "In the current work, we adopt a qualitative approach using simplistic LTE models to obtain order-of-magnitude estimates of the abundances." (1. Introduction); "We believe that Gaussian fits to the AARPs provide a reasonable way to systematically estimate the extent of the various emitting regions, to a first approximation." (4.3.2. Azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles)
- Alternative: Using default archival Ceres flux calibration — rejected and replaced with quasar phase calibrators because the Ceres model in CASA 4.3/4.4 was poorly defined and caused intensity mismatches between overlapping tunings.  
  > "In addition, we found that the flux scales used for the calibration of the archival data of several tunings of IRAS 15194-5115 and IRAS 15082-4808 were obtained using a model of the solar system object Ceres that was poorly defined" (3.2. Data reduction); "This led us to use quasars (Table 3), originally serving as phase calibrators, instead of Ceres to calibrate the flux scales of these tunings." (3.2. Data reduction)
- Alternative: Self-calibration on bright compact spectral lines or continuum — attempted and rejected because line self-calibration yielded no significant SNR improvement and continuum emission was too weak.  
  > "Self-calibration was attempted in tunings with bright and compact lines available, but did not result in any significant improvement in signal-to-noise ratio, and was hence not implemented. The observed continuum emission could not be used for self-calibration due to its low intensity." (3.2. Data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gaussian fits to AARPs of centrally-peaked species in IRAS 15194-5115 and IRAS 15082-4808 underestimate the true outer radii because the brightness profiles peak sharply at the centre and fall off gradually.  
  > "For IRAS 15194−5115 and IRAS 15082−4808, the emissions from these molecules peak sharply at the centre, and then gradually fall off at larger radii. Due to this, the Gaussian fits underestimate the radii of the actual emitting regions" (4.3.3. Centrally-peaked species)
- Limitation (authors): Interferometric filtering out of extended emission (negative bowls at large radii) affects SiC2, C4H, HC5N, CN, and CO due to the lack of very short baselines / single-dish total-power combination.  
  > "We note that towards IRAS 15082−4808, the SiC 2 AARPs show negative dips at larger radii (see Fig. 9), which is evidence of interferometric filtering out of extended emission. A similar behaviour is exhibited by C 4 H towards both IRAS 15194−5115 and IRAS 15082−4808, and possibly HC 5 N towards IRAS 15194−5115." (4.3.4. Shell species); "However, this is due to the most extended CN emission being filtered out due to the lack of very short baselines in our observations" (5. Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Population diagrams for centrally-peaked species (except SiS) yield unrealistically low rotational temperatures (10-20 K) due to limited upper-energy coverage, radially varying excitation conditions, and high optical depths, making their derived abundances lower limits.  
  > "However, for all centrally-peaked species, except SiS, the population diagrams yield much lower temperatures than expected (∼10 − 20 K; Fig. 12, left panel)." (4.4. Column density and rotation temperature estimates); "estimated rotational temperatures are much lower than expected for these centrally-peaked species (see Table 6 and Fig. 12, left panel), and we therefore believe that for these species the reported abundances are lower limits." (4.5 Abundances)
- Limitation (authors): Abundance calculations assume LTE and a smooth, isotropic, constant mass-loss wind (r^-2 density), neglecting non-LTE radiative transfer effects and the observed arc/shell density enhancements; overall abundance uncertainties are a factor of 3-5 (population diagrams) to an order of magnitude (single lines).  
  > "The arcs seen in the molecular emission towards IRAS 15194−5115 and IRAS 15082−4808 suggest that there are density enhancements (see Sect. 4.3.4). These are not considered in the assumed smooth H 2 flow" (4.5.1. Uncertainties of abundance estimates); "In general, we estimate that the overall uncertainty, including all parameters and model assumptions, is about a factor of 3 − 5 for the absolute abundances estimated using population diagrams, depending on the quality of the population diagram. The single-line estimates are, at best, order-of-magnitude estimates." (4.5.1. Uncertainties of abundance estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Lower spatial resolution towards IRAS 07454-7112 prevents resolving inner CSE central components or arc substructures.  
  > "The lower spatial resolution in the maps of IRAS 07454−7112 limits the detection of any similar sub-structure around this star." (4.3.1. Complex morphology in the CSEs of IRAS 15194 − 5115 and IRAS 15082 − 4808)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Combining ALMA 12 m interferometric line fluxes (which suffer from spatial filtering for extended species like CN, C4H, SiC2) directly with single-dish APEX fluxes in the same population diagrams without primary-beam or missing-flux corrections can bias the fitted slopes (T_rot) and intercepts (N_tot).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optical depth corrections in the population diagrams are computed iteratively from the optically thin (C_tau = 1) fit; for heavily saturated lines like HCN (tau up to ~22), this single-step first-order correction does not converge to the true column density.
