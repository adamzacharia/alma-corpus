## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and image ALMA 1.3 mm continuum data and derive SFR upper limit**: Combine two ALMA Band 6 execution blocks in CASA tclean, measure 5 sigma rms upper limit at phase center, and convert to SFR upper limit using redshifted and renormalized Chary & Elbaz (2001) IR SED templates and the Kennicutt (1998) relation.  
  > "Using the standard infrared SED library of Chary & Elbaz (2001), we translated the 1.3 mm flux upper limit into an SFR upper limit." (3.3. ALMA millimeter observations); "The infrared luminosities (i.e., L IR ) of these renormalized templates were then converted into SFRs using the standard SFR( M ⊙ yr − 1 ) = 10 − 10 ( L IR / L ⊙ ) relation from Kennicutt (1998)." (3.3. ALMA millimeter observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 2.0  
  > "combining both runs (Briggs robust parameter = 2.0)" (3.3 ALMA millimeter observations)
   - assumption: z = 0.1606, H_0 = 68 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.31, Omega_Lambda = 0.69  
  > "Throughout this paper, we adopt a flat cosmological model with H 0 = 68 km s − 1 Mpc − 1 , Ω M = 0.31, and Ω Λ = 0.69 (Planck Collaboration XIII 2016)." (1. Introduction)
   - assumption: SFR(M_sun yr^-1) = 10^-10 (L_IR / L_sun)  
  > "The infrared luminosities (i.e., L IR ) of these renormalized templates were then converted into SFRs using the standard SFR( M ⊙ yr − 1 ) = 10 − 10 ( L IR / L ⊙ ) relation from Kennicutt (1998)." (3.3. ALMA millimeter observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with Spitzer 24 um SFR upper limit  
  > "From this analysis, we inferred SFR < 4.5 M ⊙ yr − 1 , in agreement with the Spitzer data (Sect. 3.1)." (3.3. ALMA millimeter observations)
2. **Reduce Spitzer 24 um data and derive mid-IR SFR upper limit**: Reduce MIPS 24 um data with MOPEX, measure sky noise in ten empty regions to derive 3 sigma flux upper limit following Dale et al. (2012), and convert to SFR using Tzanavaris et al. (2010) and Murphy et al. (2011) relations.  
  > "The M1/MIPS images (M1 band = MIPS 24 micron), downloaded from the Spitzer archive 4 , were reduced with MOPEX (Makovoz & Marleau 2005)." (3.1. Spitzer Space Telescope mid-infrared observations); "To derive the flux upper limit we followed the procedure detailed in Dale et al. (2012, their page 7)." (3.1. Spitzer Space Telescope mid-infrared observations); "Following Tzanavaris et al. (2010), the corresponding SFR is SFR 24 µm ( M ⊙ yr − 1 ) = 2.14 × 10 − 42 ν L ν 24 µm , (1)" (3.1. Spitzer Space Telescope mid-infrared observations)
   - software: MOPEX
   - parameter: 10 empty sky regions, N_pix = N_sky slightly larger than MIPS PSF  
  > "In particular, we measured the sky and its standard deviation from ten empty sky regions around the host position, and we used as N pix and N sky (= N pix ) the area subtended by a radius slightly larger than the M1/MIPS point spread function (PSF)." (3.1. Spitzer Space Telescope mid-infrared observations)
   - assumption: Spectral slope beta between 3 and 4 in L_nu ~ nu^-beta  
  > "Assuming that the SED of the host is similar to the SED of the host of the long-duration GRB 980425, then in the 24 µm window the spectral slope, β, can have values between 3 and 4 (cf. Fig. 3 in Michałowski et al. 2009)." (3.1. Spitzer Space Telescope mid-infrared observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Reduce ATCA radio continuum data and measure fluxes/upper limits**: Reduce ATCA 5.5 and 9.0 GHz visibilities in MIRIAD, image with robust weighting (0.0 to 2.0) and tapering, check astrometry against cataloged quasars, and derive upper limits on point-source afterglow/kilonova emission and extended emission flux.  
  > "Data reduction was performed in a standard manner using the Multichannel Image Reconstruction, Image Analysis and Display (MIRIAD) software package for ATCA radio interferometry (for details, see Sault et al. 1995) 5" (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations); "The Briggs “robust” parameter (Briggs 1995) was varied between 0.0 and 2.0 (natural weighting for maximum sensitivity)." (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations); "The corresponding results attained following the procedure outlined in Klose et al. (2019), using 5σ upper limits (corresponding to 25 µJy at 5.5 and 9.0 GHz), and assuming an isotropically radiating source are listed in Table 2." (5.3. Constraints on GRB late-time emission components)
   - software: MIRIAD
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.0 to 2.0; uv taper FWHM = 5 arcsec  
  > "The Briggs “robust” parameter (Briggs 1995) was varied between 0.0 and 2.0 (natural weighting for maximum sensitivity)." (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations); "Adding some tapering (full width half maximum = 5 arcsec) increases the noise level to 8 µJy and the extended emission level to 40 µJy;" (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations)
   - assumption: Isotropic emission, spectral slope beta = -1/3 or 0.7 for radio AG and beta = 0.7 for kilonova flare  
  > "Here, for the radio AG we considered its spectral slope ( F ν ∼ ν − β ) to be either β = −1/3 or 0.7, and for a kilonova radio flare we set β = 0.7." (5.3. Constraints on GRB late-time emission components)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Astrometry checked against two known quasars B2258-391 and NVSS J230132-385927  
  > "We double-checked the astrometry of the resulting radio images by comparing the position of two known radio sources in the field with their published coordinates." (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations)
4. **Reduce VLT/MUSE IFU data and separate stellar and gas-phase emission**: Reduce MUSE exposures with the ESO MUSE pipeline, remove telluric absorption with molecfit, align astrometry to HST/F814W with SAOimage DS9 using point source Q, correct for Galactic foreground extinction, and fit and subtract stellar continuum using Starlight with Bruzual & Charlot (2003) SSP models.  
  > "The data were reduced in a standard fashion following Krühler et al. (2017), using version 1.2.1 of the MUSE data reduction pipeline provided by ESO (Weilbacher et al. 2012, 2014)." (3.4. VLT/MUSE spectroscopy); "Telluric absorption lines in the spectrum were taken into account by using the molecfit software package (Smette et al. 2015)." (3.4. VLT/MUSE spectroscopy); "To summarize, we used the Starlight software package (Cid Fernandes et al. 2005, 2009) to model the stellar continuum using a combination of single stellar population models (Bruzual & Charlot 2003) and then subtracted the fitted stellar continuum model to obtain the gas-phase-only data cube." (3.4. VLT/MUSE spectroscopy)
   - software: MUSE data reduction pipeline, molecfit, SAOimage DS9, Starlight
   - parameter: S/N >= 4 for bright lines (Halpha, [O III] 5007) and S/N >= 2 for faint lines (Hbeta, [N II] 6584, [S II] 6718)  
  > "We required S / N ≥ 4 for the bright lines (Hα, [O iii ] λ 5007) and S / N ≥ 2 for the faint lines (Hβ, [N ii ] λ 6584, [S ii ] λ 6718)." (4. Results)
   - assumption: Galactic foreground E(B-V) = 0.01 mag, Milky Way extinction law (Pei 1992), R_V = 3.08  
  > "The data were corrected for Galactic foreground reddening ( E ( B − V ) = 0.01 mag; Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011), assuming an average Milky Way extinction law (Pei 1992) and R V = 3.08." (3.4. VLT/MUSE spectroscopy)
   - fitting: Starlight stellar continuum fit using Bruzual & Charlot (2003) single stellar population models  
  > "To summarize, we used the Starlight software package (Cid Fernandes et al. 2005, 2009) to model the stellar continuum using a combination of single stellar population models (Bruzual & Charlot 2003) and then subtracted the fitted stellar continuum model to obtain the gas-phase-only data cube." (3.4. VLT/MUSE spectroscopy)
   - validation: Visual inspection of Balmer absorption removal in gas-phase cube  
  > "Figure 3 does show some indication of absorption close to the Balmer lines; the Starlight fits then remove these absorption features, such that the gas-phase-only cube no longer contains any absorption lines." (3.4. VLT/MUSE spectroscopy)
5. **Map host galaxy reddening, SFR, EW(Halpha), metallicity, kinematics, and BPT diagnostics**: From spaxel emission line fluxes in the MUSE gas cube, compute E(B-V)_host via Balmer decrement, extinction-corrected SFR(Halpha) via Murphy et al. (2011), EW(Halpha), O3N2 oxygen abundance via Pettini & Pagel (2004), Halpha radial velocity and intrinsic velocity dispersion, and [N II] and [S II] BPT diagrams.  
  > "We calculated the internal host-galaxy reddening via the Balmer decrement 6 by assuming a Milky Way extinction law with a ratio of total-to-selective extinction R V = 3.08 after correcting for Galactic foreground extinction (Sect. 3.4): E ( B − V ) host = 1.98 log((Hα/Hβ)/2.85), (3)" (4.2. Host-galaxy reddening E(B – V)); "The SFR was then calculated via the measured flux in the Hα emission line (Kennicutt 1998; Kennicutt & Evans 2012) using the prescription given in Murphy et al. (2011, their Eq. (2)), SFR(Hα) = 5.37 × 10 − 42 L (Hα) M ⊙ yr − 1 , (4)" (4.3. Star formation rate); "We follow here Pettini & Pagel (2004, PP04), according to whom the nebular oxygen abundance can be calculated as 12 + log (O/H) = 8.73 − 0.32 × O3N2, with (5)" (4.5. Metallicity)
   - parameter: 2 arcsec radius aperture for global SFR; continuum flux density >= 2e-20 erg s^-1 cm^-2 A^-1 for EW(Halpha); sigma_instr = 42 km s^-1, sigma_T,gas = 9.1 km s^-1  
  > "For the entire galaxy, inside a circle with a radius of 2 arcsec centered at the central part of the host, the SFR is 0.15 ± 0.02 M ⊙ yr − 1 ." (4.3. Star formation rate); "In addition, we required EW(Hα) - 0 and a measurable continuum level; a flux density ≥2 × 10 − 20 erg s − 1 cm − 2 Å − 1 turned out to be a reasonable choice." (4.4. Equivalent width (H α )); "the redshifted Hα line, the LSF is about 2.5 Å, corresponding to8 σinstr = 42 km s−1, while for a T ∼104 K warm gas, σT,gas = 9.1 km s−1." (4.6 Velocity pattern)
   - assumption: Case B recombination at T = 10^4 K, n_e ~ 10^2 - 10^4 cm^-3, intrinsic Halpha/Hbeta = 2.85, R_V = 3.08  
  > "Adopting Case B recombination at T = 10 4 K and an electron density of n e ∼ 10 2 −10 4 cm − 3 (Osterbrock 1989)." (4.2. Host-galaxy reddening E(B – V)); "E ( B − V ) host = 1.98 log((Hα/Hβ)/2.85), (3)" (4.2. Host-galaxy reddening E(B – V))
   - assumption: Solar oxygen abundance 12 + log(O/H) = 8.69 (Asplund et al. 2009)  
  > "The measured median of 12 + log(O/H) corresponds to a metallicity Z = 0.51 − + 0 0 . . 05 07 Z ⊙ , for a solar value of 12 + log(O/H) = 8.69 (Asplund et al. 2009;" (4.5. Metallicity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of E(B-V) and SFR with previous long-slit spectroscopy and broadband SED results

## Qualitative findings

- The GRB 050709 host galaxy is undetected in ALMA Band 6 (1.3 mm) and Spitzer 24 um, ruling out substantial optically obscured star formation.  
  > "The galaxy is detected neither by ALMA nor by Spitzer , excluding a substantial amount of optically obscured star formation activity." (abstract)
- The host is a metal-poor (~0.5 solar) emission-line galaxy lying on the star-forming main sequence and the local mass-metallicity relation.  
  > "As is evident in Fig. 7, the location of the GRB 050709 host galaxy in the MZR is entirely consistent with the behavior of local normal star-forming galaxies." (4.5. Metallicity); "The locus of the GRB 050709 host is within the ±1σ variation of the main sequence as shown by the gray regions." (5.1. The GRB host galaxy)
- BPT diagrams, faint diffuse 5.5 GHz radio emission in excess of star formation, irregular optical morphology with a double peak and tidal tails, and smooth gas kinematics together indicate the host is in an advanced merger stage.  
  > "This potential evidence for shocked gas, the very irregular morphological appearance of the host, and a comparison of the observed kinematics of the Hα-emitting gas with analogous results for merging field galaxies led us to conclude that the GRB 050709 host is most likely a galaxy in an advanced merger stage." (6. Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GRB 050709 is at z = 0.1606, making it one of the nearest short-GRB hosts and suitable for spatially resolved VLT/MUSE spectroscopy; ALMA Band 6 (and requested Band 9) observations were obtained to test whether the faint ATCA radio source is due to dust-obscured star formation.  
  > "At a redshift of z = 0.16, it belongs to one of the cosmologically nearest short-GRB hosts identified to date. Consequently, it represents a promising target for sensitive, spatially resolved observational studies." (abstract); "In order to clarify if the faint ATCA radio source is related to star-forming activity in the eastern part of the galaxy, we applied for ALMA observations in bands 6 (1.1–1.4 mm, 211–275 GHz) and 9 (0.4–0.5 mm, 602–720 GHz)." (3.3. ALMA millimeter observations)
- Why this method: Used O3N2 and BPT line ratios because lines are close in wavelength and insensitive to dust extinction or instrumental response; used Briggs robust = 2.0 in ALMA and varied robust 0.0-2.0 in ATCA to maximize sensitivity.  
  > "The Briggs “robust” parameter (Briggs 1995) was varied between 0.0 and 2.0 (natural weighting for maximum sensitivity)." (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations); "Since the flux ratios refer to lines that are very close to each other, the line ratios are basically insensitive to extinction by dust along the line of sight or instrumental response." (4.5. Metallicity)
- Alternative: Star-formation origin for the ATCA 5.5 GHz extended emission (rejected by ALMA, Spitzer, and MUSE SFR constraints in favor of nonthermal/shocked gas emission)  
  > "Using Eq. (1) in Greiner et al. (2016), the observed 5.5 GHz flux would then correspond to an unobscured SFR in the eastern part of the host of ∼2−3 M ⊙ yr − 1 . However, our VLT/MUSE observations do not support this picture (Sect. 3.4)." (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations)
- Alternative: GRB radio afterglow or late-time kilonova radio flare origin for the ATCA 5.5 GHz extended emission (rejected because those sources would be < 1 mas and point-like)  
  > "If it is real, the radio source is not point-like and therefore cannot be the GRB radio AG. Analogously, it cannot be the potential late-time radio flare from non-relativistic ejecta of the double" (3.2 ATCA radio-continuum observations)
- Alternative: Infall of external metal-poor gas clouds or stellar wind feedback as alternative explanations for the lower metallicity and higher SFR in region B  
  > "If correct, this, in combination with the fact that the SFR peaks in region B (Fig. 5), could indicate that star formation is triggered here by the infall of metal-poor gas." (4.5. Metallicity); "Feedback from stellar winds, which is especially efficient in the low potential well of low-mass galaxies, could be an alternative scenario because regions that have consumed their gas more quickly (and so have lower SFRs) have had a higher metal production rate" (4.5. Metallicity)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Scheduled ALMA Band 9 observations could not be performed due to weather constraints.  
  > "Unfortunately, due to weather constraints observations in band 9 could not be performed despite being scheduled." (3.3. ALMA millimeter observations)
- Limitation (authors): The faint ATCA 5.5 GHz extended emission near the GRB site is at the ~5 sigma level, so there is no guarantee that it is real and it is too faint to split in time or frequency.  
  > "We conclude that there may be some extended emission at the GRB explosion site at the 5σ level. This is too faint to perform a more detailed analysis (e.g., splitting in time or frequency). It does not look like a spurious source, but at the S/N level at which we detect it there is no guarantee that it is real." (3.2. ATCA radio-continuum observations)
- Limitation (authors): Small angular sizes (< 0.2''), faintness, and strong host foreground emission prevent MUSE from clarifying the nature of individual clumps C-G and K-M.  
  > "While the HST image seems to provide compelling evidence that these are star-forming regions, young luminous stellar clusters, or background (foreground) galaxies, their individual nature cannot be clarified with certainty using the MUSE data cube." (4.1. The nature of objects C-G and K-M)
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N in the outskirts of the galaxy prevents measuring E(B-V)_host, EW(Halpha), or a secure SFR at the GRB explosion site.  
  > "Unfortunately, there is no data point close to the GRB explosion site." (4.2. Host-galaxy reddening E(B – V)); "Our data sensitivity is not good enough, however, to measure the corresponding SFR in this part of the galaxy with certainty." (5.2. The GRB progenitor and the GRB explosion site)
- Limitation (authors): Apparent lower metallicity in region B and high velocity dispersions (~60 km s^-1) in the outskirts could be affected by low-number statistics or low S/N.  
  > "Region B appears to be less metal-rich than the outer parts of the galaxy, but we caution that such a conclusion could also be a result of low-number statistics (a small number of spaxels with data)." (4.5. Metallicity); "values of up to about 60 km s−1 occur at the outskirts of the galaxy, though we cannot rule out that this is due to a lower S/N." (4.6 Velocity pattern)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA Band 6 combined image has a high angular resolution (0.22'' x 0.18''), which could resolve out diffuse dust continuum emission across the ~3-4'' host galaxy; a uv-tapered ALMA upper limit is not reported.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Setting E(B-V)_host = 0 for outer spaxels with low Hbeta S/N systematically underestimates the extinction-corrected SFR in the galaxy outskirts.
