## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract core-integrated spectra and select the chemically richest template core**: Integrate emission in spw0 and spw1 over the continuum core ellipse defined in the ALMAGAL core catalogue, then sort cores by intensity and S/N of CH3CN K=2 at 220.730 GHz and MF 17(3,14)->16(3,13) at 218.298 GHz, line density (~80 lines GHz^-1), simple kinematics, and core mass.  
  > "To study the richness of the spectral line emission of each core, we extracted the spectra in the two broad spectral windows (spw0 and spw1) by integrating the emission over the ellipse that describes the size of each continuum core." (Method); "We sorted the spectra of all the ALMAGAL cores using the intensity of the $K=2$ component of methyl cyanide (CH$_3$CN), where $K$ denotes the projection of the total rotational angular momentum onto the principal axis of the molecule, at 220.730 GHz." (Identification of the richest chemical core); "Since the forthcoming study will focus on GA, EG, and MF, we further considered the intensity of one of the brightest MF transitions in the observed band, the $\rm 17_{3,14}\rightarrow 16_{3,13}$ at 218.298 GHz." (Identification of the richest chemical core)
   - software: CuTEx
   - parameter: CH3CN K=2 at 220.730 GHz; MF 17(3,14)->16(3,13) at 218.298 GHz  
  > "Since the forthcoming study will focus on GA, EG, and MF, we further considered the intensity of one of the brightest MF transitions in the observed band, the $\rm 17_{3,14}\rightarrow 16_{3,13}$ at 218.298 GHz." (Identification of the richest chemical core)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Excluded the two more massive cores due to prominent self-absorption features in the most massive core and highly noisy spectra in the second  
  > "The two more massive cores were excluded because the most massive core shows prominent self-absorption features, while the second core exhibits highly noisy spectra." (Identification of the richest chemical core)
2. **Identify molecular lines and fit LTE synthetic spectra to derive physical parameters and abundances**: Search for molecular transitions with S/N >= 5 (~1.35 K) using Splatalogue (CDMS and JPL catalogues) and fit LTE synthetic spectra (accounting for line opacity) using SLIM and AUTOFIT in MADCUBA, both manually and via an automated two-round MADCUBA macro. Compute abundances relative to H2 and scale optically thick main isotopologues from rarer isotopologues using Galactocentric isotopic ratios.  
  > "For the line identification in AG318$-$c9, we searched for molecular species with an S/N $\geq5$ (corresponding to $\sim 1.35\,$K) to ensure reliable detections." (Line identification); "This software allowed us to identify and fit different species using the tool called spectral line identification and modeling (SLIM), which generates a synthetic spectrum under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) while accounting for line opacity." (Line identification); "For species for which isotopologues were also detected ($\rm H_2CO$, $\rm SO_2$, $\rm SO$, $\rm HC_3N$, $\rm HNCO$, $\rm HC(O)NH_2$, $\rm CH_3OH$, $\rm CH_3CN$, $\rm t-HCOOH$, $\rm CH_3OCH_3$, $\rm CH_3CHO$, and $\rm C_2H_5CN$), the column densities of the main isotopologues were obtained by scaling those of the isotopologues by the measured isotopic ratios." (Molecular inventory)
   - software: Splatalogue, MADCUBA, SLIM, AUTOFIT, RichValues
   - parameter: S/N >= 5 (~1.35 K)  
  > "For the line identification in AG318$-$c9, we searched for molecular species with an S/N $\geq5$ (corresponding to $\sim 1.35\,$K) to ensure reliable detections." (Line identification)
   - parameter: Initial guesses: V = -34.4 km s^-1, FWHM = 5 km s^-1, theta_s = 0 (no beam dilution), initial T_ex from rotational diagram of 41 MF transitions (T_ex^CH3OCHO = 167 K)  
  > "For AG318$-$c9, we assumed as initial guesses $V$ = $-$34.4 km s$^{-1}$, which is the systemic velocity of the clump, and FWHM = 5 km s$^{-1}$, which is the typical width of the lines for this source, as obtained from simple Gaussian fits to some species in our setup." (Automatization with MADCUBA); "The parameter $\theta_s$ was fixed to 0, which in MADCUBA means that the source emission size is equal to or larger than the synthesised beam and hence unaffected by beam dilution." (Automatization with MADCUBA); "When $T_{\rm ex}$ is fixed, $T_{\rm ex}^{\rm CH_3OCHO}=167\,\rm K$ is assumed (see Sect. [8](#macro))." (Spectral line analysis)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), justified by n_H2 = 1.72 x 10^8 cm^-3  
  > "Given the high gas density of AG318$-$c9, $n\rm _{H_2}=1.72 \times 10^8$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: almagal3), the assumption of LTE is well justified." (Line identification)
   - assumption: N_H2 = 7.35 x 10^24 cm^-2 derived from M_core = 124.2 M_sun, D_core = 6396 au, m_H = 1.67 x 10^-24 g, mu = 2.8  
  > "The H$_2$ column density, $N_{\rm H_2}=7.35\times 10^{24}\,\rm cm^{-2}$ (Table [\[tab:properties\]](#tab:properties)), was derived using $N_{\rm H_2} = \frac{4\,M_{\rm core}}{\pi\,\mu\,m_{\rm H}\,D{\rm core}^2}$, where $M_{\rm core}$ is the mass of the core, and $D_{\rm core}=6396\,\rm au$ is the geometric mean size of AG318$-$c9, as reported by (cite: almagal3)." (Line identification); "Here, $m_{\rm H}=1.67\times 10^{-24}\,\rm g$ is the hydrogen atom mass, and $\mu=2.8$ is the mean molecular weight per particle." (Line identification)
   - assumption: Isotopic ratios at D_GAL = 6.84 kpc: 12C/13C = 46.6 +/- 10, 32S/33S = 88.9 +/- 7.7, 32S/34S = 20.7 +/- 1.2  
  > "Specifically, we adopted $\rm ^{ 12}C/^{13}C = 46.6\pm10$ (cite: Yan_2019), $\rm ^{32}S/^{33}S = 88.9\pm7.7$, and $\rm ^{32}S/^{34}S = 20.7\pm1.2$ (cite: yan2023)." (Molecular inventory)
   - fitting: Non-linear least-squares minimisation based on the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm in AUTOFIT (two rounds: individual species with fixed FWHM, then joint fit with ALL SPECIES enabled and FWHM free; hierarchical parameter fixing if non-convergent: FWHM, then V, then T_ex)  
  > "Best-fit solutions were obtained using the AUTOFIT routine, which performs a non-linear least-squares minimisation based on the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm (cite: lm1; lm2)." (Line identification); "In the first round, the FWHM remains fixed, and each species is fitted individually without taking into account the contribution from other species (disabling the ALL SPECIES flag in AUTOFIT)." (Automatization with MADCUBA); "In this second round, the FWHM is left free and the contribution of all blended species is properly taken into account (enabling the ALL SPECIES flag in AUTOFIT)." (Automatization with MADCUBA)
   - validation: Visual inspection of global and zoomed-in synthetic spectra against observed spectra across spw0 and spw1  
  > "Although this procedure is largely automated, the resulting fit must be visually inspected to ensure consistency with the observations, and this last step should be done manually." (Automatization with MADCUBA)

## Software

- **MADCUBA** : Spectral line identification, LTE synthetic spectrum modelling (SLIM/AUTOFIT), rotational diagram analysis, moment maps, and pixel-by-pixel N and T_ex maps  
  > "Spectral line identification and local thermodynamic equilibrium modelling were performed using the software called MAdrid Data CUBe Analysis (MADCUBA)." (Integrated intensity, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $N$ maps of EG, GA, and MF)
- **SLIM** : Module within MADCUBA for spectral line identification and LTE synthetic spectrum generation  
  > "This software allowed us to identify and fit different species using the tool called spectral line identification and modeling (SLIM), which generates a synthetic spectrum under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) while accounting for line opacity." (Line identification)
- **AUTOFIT** : Routine within MADCUBA/SLIM for non-linear least-squares Levenberg-Marquardt fitting of molecular parameters  
  > "Best-fit solutions were obtained using the AUTOFIT routine, which performs a non-linear least-squares minimisation based on the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm (cite: lm1; lm2)." (Line identification)
- **Splatalogue** : Querying CDMS and JPL spectroscopic catalogues to search for candidate molecular transitions  
  > "For the remaining unidentified lines, we used `Splatalogue` to search for additional molecular species with transitions consistent with the observed frequencies, drawing spectroscopic data from the CDMS (cite: cdms2001; cdms2005; cdms2016) and the JPL (cite: jpl) catalogues." (Line identification)
- **CuTEx** : Structure identification algorithm used in ALMAGAL catalogue to extract compact continuum cores  
  > "The compact cores embedded in the ALMAGAL clumps were identified with the structure identification algorithm CUrvature Thresholding EXtractor (CuTEx; (cite: cutex)) based on their integrated dust continuum flux." (Core 9)
- **RichValues** : Python library used to generate the tables of molecular transitions  
  > "The table of molecular transitions were made thanks to the Python library `RichValues` created by Andrés Megías." (Acknowledgements)

## Qualitative findings

- AG318-c9 hosts a rich HMC chemical inventory with 65 detected species (31 main, 34 isotopologues and vibrationally excited states; 44 O-bearing, 28 N-bearing, 8 S-bearing, 2 Si-bearing; 33 COMs) within a 4 GHz Band 6 setup.  
  > "We report the detection of 65 molecular species, including 31 main species and 34 isotopologues and vibrationally excited species." (ALMAGAL); "Of these, 44 are O-bearing species, 28 are N-bearing, 8 are S-bearing, and 2 are Si-bearing." (ALMAGAL)
- Compared to G31.41+0.31, AG318-c9 generally has lower column densities and abundances (especially for O-bearing COMs), but shows detections of species not reported in G31 (such as ethyl formate, C2H5OCHO, and HOCH2CN) and relative overabundances of 12 species including NH2CN and H2CNH, suggesting AG318-c9 is a younger or less thermally processed HMC.  
  > "While AG318$-$c9 exhibits lower abundances than G31 overall, it shows detections of species that have not yet been reported in G31, such as ethyl formate ($\rm C_2H_5OCHO$)." (ALMAGAL); "The chemical comparison with G31 suggests that AG318$-$c9 is a less evolved hot core dominated by the recent sublimation of ice mantles, in contrast to the higher abundances observed in the possible more evolved G31." (ALMAGAL)
- MF is the most abundant and spatially extended of the three target COMs and traces a clear temperature gradient (~100-250 K), whereas EG and GA are compact and confined to the innermost core region; EG and MF radial extents are sensitivity-limited while GA reflects an intrinsically central distribution.  
  > "MF emerges as an excellent tracer of the gas temperature in HMCs owing to its wide spatial extent and wide dynamic range of the excitation temperature." (ALMAGAL); "In contrast, EG and GA are more compact and preferentially trace the innermost high-density regions of the core, suggesting a high sublimation temperature threshold and supporting a shared grain-surface formation pathway." (ALMAGAL); "While EG and MF seem to be partly limited by sensitivity, GA reflects an intrinsically central distribution." (ALMAGAL)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMAGAL provides a uniformly observed, statistically significant sample of 1013 dense clumps in Band 6; spw0 and spw1 provide ~3.74 GHz of broad spectral coverage suitable for line identification, and AG318-c9 was chosen for its top CH3CN K=2 and MF S/N, high line density (~80 lines GHz^-1), high mass, and simple single-velocity kinematics.  
  > "This source was selected because it combines an exceptionally high line density with a relatively simple kinematic structure, making it an optimal template for chemical analysis in the survey." (ALMAGAL); "The remaining two spectral windows (spw2 and spw3), which cover narrower bandwidths of 0.468 GHz, were not considered here as they correspond to higher spectral resolution subwindows within the frequency ranges already covered by spw0 and spw1 and do not provide additional constraints for line identification, especially for weak species." (Observations)
- Why this method: MADCUBA SLIM/AUTOFIT models synthetic LTE spectra accounting for line opacity and blending from all species simultaneously, and can be scripted into an automated macro for survey-wide application; LTE is justified by the high gas density (n_H2 = 1.72 x 10^8 cm^-3).  
  > "This software allowed us to identify and fit different species using the tool called spectral line identification and modeling (SLIM), which generates a synthetic spectrum under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) while accounting for line opacity." (Line identification); "Given the high gas density of AG318$-$c9, $n\rm _{H_2}=1.72 \times 10^8$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: almagal3), the assumption of LTE is well justified." (Line identification)
- Alternative: Rejected the two most massive ALMAGAL cores as templates because the most massive shows prominent self-absorption and the second has highly noisy spectra.  
  > "The two more massive cores were excluded because the most massive core shows prominent self-absorption features, while the second core exhibits highly noisy spectra." (Identification of the richest chemical core)
- Alternative: Rejected narrow spectral windows spw2 and spw3 because they lie within the frequency range of spw0 and spw1 and do not provide additional constraints for weak line identification.  
  > "The remaining two spectral windows (spw2 and spw3), which cover narrower bandwidths of 0.468 GHz, were not considered here as they correspond to higher spectral resolution subwindows within the frequency ranges already covered by spw0 and spw1 and do not provide additional constraints for line identification, especially for weak species." (Observations)
- Alternative: Rejected using CH3CN to estimate the common initial T_ex seed in the automated macro because its lowest K transitions are optically thick and saturated; used 41 optically thin MF transitions instead.  
  > "Although we initially wanted to use CH$_3$CN to estimate such a common initial $T_{\rm ex}$, we realised that the lowest $K$ transitions were saturated, indicating that they are optically thick, leading to an overestimate of the rotational temperature ($T_{\rm rot}$) and an underestimate of the rotational column density ($N_{\rm rot}$)." (Automatization with MADCUBA)
- Alternative: Focused on the a-EG conformer rather than g-EG because g-EG has many blended lines and its few unblended transitions have similar energies (~130 K).  
  > "We focused on the a-EG conformer because it shows a significantly larger number of transitions within the ALMAGAL spectral setup compared to the g-EG conformer." (Ethylene glycol)
- Alternative: Used a reduced subset of least-blended transitions for EG and GA pixel-by-pixel maps rather than the full transition list used in the core-integrated fit to ensure convergence in lower-S/N outer regions.  
  > "However, for EG and GA, a reduced set of transitions was selected, prioritising the least blended transitions while preserving a broad energy coverage." (Integrated intensity, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $N$ maps of EG, GA, and MF)
- How they decided it was enough: Adopted a 5 sigma (~1.35 K) threshold for line identification, a 3 sigma threshold in moment 0 maps for pixel-by-pixel fitting, and truncated the EG T_ex map at r = 0.9'' where uncertainties became too large.  
  > "For the line identification in AG318$-$c9, we searched for molecular species with an S/N $\geq5$ (corresponding to $\sim 1.35\,$K) to ensure reliable detections." (Line identification); "The procedure performs the analysis only on pixels with a S/N above a given threshold in the moment 0 map, which in our case was $\rm S/N > 3$." (Generation of the maps ($N$)); "For clarity, the map is truncated at a radius of 0.9$''$ beyond which the uncertainties become too large for a reliable interpretation." (Integrated intensity, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $N$ maps of EG, GA, and MF)
- Limitation (authors): 91 observed spectral lines remain unidentified because transitions may be missing from current CDMS and JPL databases.  
  > "A total of 91 lines remained unassigned and are labelled unidentified (U) in the spectra." (Molecular inventory)
- Limitation (authors): Transitions of CH3CN (K=0,1,2,3), H2CO (218.222 GHz), and 13CO are not well reproduced by a single LTE model due to optical depth/self-absorption, non-interconverting A/E symmetry ladders in CH3CN, and interferometric spatial filtering of extended 13CO emission.  
  > "This behaviour arises because the $\rm CH_3CN$ transitions with $K=0,1,2$ are optically thick and show absorption features, as is also the case for the $\rm H_2CO$ $\rm 3_{0,3}\rightarrow 2_{0,2}$ transition at 218.222 GHz." (Molecular inventory); "In addition, the $\rm ^{13}CO$ emission is spatially extended and is therefore filtered out by the interferometer, resulting in missing flux." (Molecular inventory)
- Limitation (authors): Core gas mass (~120 M_sun) is likely overestimated because it assumed a uniform temperature of 49 K, whereas T_ex maps show higher temperatures and a gradient; core luminosity (~1.2 x 10^5 L_sun) is indirectly inferred by scaling the host clump luminosity.  
  > "However, this mass is likely an overestimate because it was obtained by assuming a uniform core temperature of 49 K, whereas our $T_{\rm ex}$ maps reveal a temperature gradient in the core with temperatures significantly $>$49 K (Sect. [6.4](#subsect:physical_maps)), implying a lower total gas mass." (Comparison with the hot core G31.41+0.31); "The core mass is likely overestimated, while the luminosity is indirectly inferred from the host clump, introducing additional uncertainties." (Comparison with the hot core G31.41+0.31)
- Limitation (authors): Pixel-by-pixel N and T_ex maps fit only the target molecule without subtracting or jointly fitting blending species.  
  > "To generate $N$ and $T_{\rm ex}$ maps, only the target species was considered in each case, without considering the contribution of other molecules (Appendix [9](#sec:maps))." (Integrated intensity, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $N$ maps of EG, GA, and MF)
- Limitation (authors): Beam dilution at radii <= 0.21'' and high central dust opacity may cause underestimation of column densities and excitation temperatures in the innermost core region.  
  > "At radii comparable to or smaller than the beam size ($\sim 0.21\ensuremath{''}$), the observed brightness temperature might be affected by beam dilution." (Integrated intensity, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $N$ maps of EG, GA, and MF); "In addition, dust opacity might become non-negligible towards the core centre, potentially attenuating line emission from the deepest layers." (Integrated intensity, $T_{\rm ex}$, and $N$ maps of EG, GA, and MF)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Relative abundances X = N_species / N_H2 all depend on a single core-averaged H2 column density derived assuming T_dust = 49 K, whereas the molecular gas traces 100-250 K; any revision to T_dust systematically shifts all reported abundances X.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Pixel-by-pixel maps ignore line blending from other species and use a single source size assumption (theta_s = 0), which could bias T_ex and N in partially blended transitions of EG and GA.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing Band 6 (~217-221 GHz) detections in AG318-c9 with Band 3 (84-116 GHz) GUAPOS detections in G31.41+0.31 samples different upper-state energy regimes and dust optical depths, which could contribute to systematic column density offsets.
