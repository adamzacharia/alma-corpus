## Analysis recipe

1. **Preliminary search for COMs and source selection**: Extract spectra at the 1.3 mm continuum peak pixel for all 56 Class 0/I protostellar cores and select sources with at least two CH3OH transitions detected at SNR > 5 (including at least one with E_up > 100 K).  
  > "To have a quick search of the presence of COMs, we first extract the spectra at the continuum peak pixel for the protostellar cores from the ALMASOP sample." (Data and Tools); "Among the 56 protostellar cores, we find 11 sources with at least two CH$_3$OH transitions including at least one transition with upper energy $E_\mathrm{up}$ $>100$ K getting detected at a level above 5$\sigma$." (Results)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Detection threshold SNR > 5  
  > "We select the sources where at least two methanol transitions are detected (SNR $>$ 5)" (Data and Tools)
   - parameter: At least one transition with E_up > 100 K  
  > "including at least one transition with upper energy $E_\mathrm{up}$ $>100$ K getting detected at a level above 5$\sigma$." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure source emission size and H2 column density, and extract source-averaged spectra**: Perform 2D Gaussian fitting on the CH3OH-46K (218440 MHz) moment-0 images in CASA to define the emission extent, extract full spectra within this extent, and compute H2 column density N(H2) from 1.3 mm continuum flux within the same extent.  
  > "define their emission extent based on the 2D Gaussian fitting of their CH$_3$OH moment–0 images. The CH$_3$OH transition we use is at $J=4-3$; $K_a=2-1$; $K_c=3-2$; $E_\mathrm{up}$ $=46$ K; and $f_\mathrm{rest}$ $= 218440$ MHz (hereafter CH$_3$OH–46K transition)" (Data and Tools); "We then extract the full spectra within the same extent toward each source." (Data and Tools); "Based on the 2D Gaussian fitting in CASA to the CH$_3$OH–46K transition moment–0 images, we infer the extent of the methanol emission and calculate the molecular hydrogen column density $N(\mathrm{H_2})$ using the 1.3 continuum flux within the extent." (2D Gaussian Fitting)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Reference line: CH3OH J=4-3, Ka=2-1, Kc=3-2 at 218440 MHz (E_up = 46 K)  
  > "The CH$_3$OH transition we use is at $J=4-3$; $K_a=2-1$; $K_c=3-2$; $E_\mathrm{up}$ $=46$ K; and $f_\mathrm{rest}$ $= 218440$ MHz (hereafter CH$_3$OH–46K transition)" (Data and Tools)
   - assumption: Optically thin 1.3 mm continuum emission  
  > "We use the formulae in (cite: Hsu2020_ALMASOP) and (cite: 2008Kauffmann_CONTFormula) for optically thin continuum emission." (2D Gaussian Fitting)
   - assumption: Dust opacity law kappa_nu = 0.1 (nu / 1 THz)^beta cm^2 g^-1 with beta = 1.70  
  > "The dust opacity is in the form of $\kappa_\nu = 0.1(\nu / 1\,\mathrm{THz})^\beta \mathrm{cm}^2 \mathrm{g}^{-1}$. The dust opacity index $\beta$ was assigned to be 1.70" (2D Gaussian Fitting)
   - assumption: Dust temperature = 100 K  
  > "We assumed a (dust) temperature of 100 K, which is the typical value for hot corinos." (2D Gaussian Fitting)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting to moment-0 images  
  > "Based on the 2D Gaussian fitting in CASA to the CH$_3$OH–46K transition moment–0 images" (2D Gaussian Fitting)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify molecular transitions and fit LTE synthetic spectra**: Use XCLASS LineIdentification to identify molecular carriers and simulate 1D LTE synthetic spectra to fit T_ex, N, delta_v, and v_LSR for each species, first individually and then simultaneously for all species to account for line blending.  
  > "we use XCLASS (cite: 2017Moller_XCLASS) to search for the molecular carrier candidates with its `LineIdentification` function, and to simulate synthetic spectra with input molecular parameters including the excitation temperature ($T_\mathrm{ex}$), total column density ($N$), line width in velocity ($\delta v$), and local-standard–of–rest velocity ($v_\mathrm{LSR}$)." (Data and Tools); "We then run overall fittings for all species simultaneously with Bees algorithm and LM algorithm for each source to take into account blending of lines." (Data and Tools)
   - software: XCLASS, MAGIX
   - parameter: T_ex range limited to (0, 400) K for initial fitting  
  > "To have a first estimation, we make individual fittings for each molecular species with the Bees algorithm, and the range of $T_\mathrm{ex}$ is limited to be (0, 400)." (Data and Tools)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) for each gaseous molecular component  
  > "Each (gaseous) molecular component is assumed to be in its individual local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Data and Tools)
   - assumption: Emission size of all species equals the deconvolved 2D Gaussian extent of the CH3OH-46K transition  
  > "We assume the emission size of all species to be the (2D Gaussian) extent of the CH$_3$OH–46K transition deconvolved by the beam size." (Data and Tools)
   - assumption: T_ex = 100 K fixed for molecules detected with only one transition  
  > "For molecules detected with only one transition, we assume their $T_\mathrm{ex}$ to be 100 K, the typical temperature of a hot corino." (Data and Tools)
   - fitting: Bees swarm optimization and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) algorithms in MAGIX, with uncertainties estimated via Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) in XCLASS  
  > "The optimization algorithms in MAGIX we use are “Bees algorithms” and “Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) algorithms”." (Data and Tools); "Tables 6 – 16 display the molecular parameters (i.e. $T_\mathrm{ex}$, $N$, $\delta v$, and $v_\mathrm{LSR}$) and their errors estimated by the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method in XCLASS of these 11 sources." (Results)
   - validation: Comparison of derived isotopologue ratios and column density ratios with literature hot corinos  
  > "the ratio between the column densities of $^{13}$CH$_3$OH and CH$_2$DOH appears to be similar among the hot corino sources in this study and in the literature" (Isotope Ratios of Methanol: $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O, and D/H,)
4. **Fit YSO spectral energy distributions (SEDs) and reconstruct thermal structure**: Combine archival 3.6-870 um photometry with 1.3 mm ALMASOP ACA and combined peak flux densities, fit against the Robitaille et al. (2006) YSO model grid using SED Fitter with observational constraints on disk outer radius and outflow cavity opening angle, and reconstruct the 2D thermal structure using HO-CHUNK (2008 version) to measure the >100 K warm region size.  
  > "On top of these SED data points, we append two data points at 1.3 mm based on the continuum images made with the ALMASOP ACA–only data and the combined data (TM1+TM2+ACA)." (Data); "We employed the SED Fitter (cite: 2007Robitaille_sedfitter) for deriving the YSO models best matching with the observed SED data points." (Tools and Methods); "We further reconstruct the physical model using the 2008 version of the HO-CHUNK code with the best-fit parameters obtained from the SED Fitter" (Warm Region Sizes in the YSO Models)
   - software: SED Fitter, HO–CHUNK
   - parameter: Foreground extinction A_V range [0, 250] mag  
  > "In our study, the range of $A_V$ was set to [0, 250] mag" (Tools and Methods)
   - parameter: Fixed distances D = 398 pc (Orion A), 404 pc (Orion B), 404 pc (lambda Orionis)  
  > "and the distance $D$ was fixed to be 398 pc, 404 pc, and 404 pc, respectively, for sources in the Orion A, Orion B, and $\lambda$ Orionis clouds (cite: 2018Kounkel_Orion_distance)." (Tools and Methods)
   - parameter: Disk outer radius constraint < 140 au (except G192)  
  > "First, the disk size (disk outer radius) should be less than 140 au due to the fact that there were no clear disk signatures in the C$^{18}$O $J=2-1$ moment–0 images for all the hot corino sources (except G192)." (Tools and Methods)
   - parameter: Outflow cavity effective opening angle constraint |Theta_400^Fitter - Theta_400^obs| < 1 deg  
  > "We ruled out the YSO models with the effective opening angle 1 degree apart from our observed values (i.e. kept those with $|\Theta^\mathrm{Fitter}_{400}-\Theta^\mathrm{obs}_{400}|<1^\circ$)." (Additional Constraint to the Cavity of YSO Model)
   - parameter: Magnetospheric truncation radius R_trunc = 5 R_* and hot spot fractional area f_spot = 0.007  
  > "We therefore applied the default value of HO-CHUNK 2004 release according to the change log of HO-CHUNK 2008 version: $R_\mathrm{trunc}=5R_{\star}$ and $f_\mathrm{spot}=0.007$." (Limitation for Reproducing YSO Model SEDs)
   - assumption: 2D axisymmetric YSO geometry (central star, accretion disk, envelope, bipolar cavity) with solar metallicity PHOENIX stellar atmospheres  
  > "The physical structural setup of the YSO model in the HO-CHUNK package includes a central star, an accretion disk, an envelope, and an optional outflow cavity" (Tools and Methods); "the metallicity was assumed to be the solar metallicity." (Limitation for Reproducing YSO Model SEDs)
   - assumption: Cavity wall polynomial index b = 1.5  
  > "$b$ is the polynomial index assumed to be 1.5" (Additional Constraint to the Cavity of YSO Model)
   - fitting: Chi-square minimization over the 200,000-model R06 grid in SED Fitter, selecting top 5 best-fit models per source  
  > "The SED Fitter will evaluate the $\chi^2$ from the observed flux and the modeled flux under a combination of a SED grid model, $A_V$, and $D$, and list the best combinations of parameters with the minimum $\chi^2$ values." (Tools and Methods); "We present in Table 20 the five best-fit YSO models exported by the SED Fitter for each source" (Results)
   - validation: Positive correlation with ratio of order unity between the modeled >100 K warm region size and the observed deconvolved CH3OH-46K source size  
  > "Fig. 2 plots the deconvolved source size of the CH$_3$OH–46 transition versus the warm region size ($>$ 100 K, the typical temperature of a hot corino) inferred from the SED fitting. There exists a positive correlation between the two quantities with a ratio on the order of unity" (Warm Region Sizes in the YSO Models)
5. **Evaluate statistical correlations, detection rates, and chemical abundance ratios**: Calculate total gas-phase methanol molecule numbers from 13CH3OH (scaled by 12C/13C = 50), correlate with L_bol, compare hot corino detection rates with ACA-only data and the Perseus PEACHES survey under matched criteria, and fit linear proportional relations to COM column densities.  
  > "We derive the total number of molecule ($\mathcal{N}$) within the emission extent with: $\mathcal{N} = N \times (\theta_S \times D)^{2}$, where $N$ is the column density, $\theta_S$ is the source size, and $D$ is the distance." (Luminosity and Warm Region in Hot Corino); "To clarify the influence of these two factors, we also append the hot corino candidates under the same line detection criteria and apply the same sample selection method as PEACHES." (Comparisons between ALMASOP and PEACHES); "We explore possible correlations between the column densities of CH$_3$OH, CH$_3$CHO, HCOOCH$_3$, and C$_2$H$_5$OH in Fig. 7 and fit the observed positive correlation trend by a linear proportional function." (Complex Organic Molecules (COMs))
   - software: astropy
   - parameter: PEACHES-matched criteria: L_bol > 1 L_sun and 3 sigma methanol detection  
  > "Number of sources under PEACHES sample selection method ($L_\mathrm{bol}$ $>1~L_\odot$) and line detection criteria (3 $\sigma$) is given in parentheses." (Table 2)
   - assumption: 12C/13C = 50 in the Orion cloud to rescale 13CH3OH column density to main CH3OH  
  > "We use $^{13}$CH$_3$OH with $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C $=$ 50 (cite: 2018Kahane_13C12C_Orion) to derive the methanol column density, given the main isotopologue (CH$_3$OH) is likely optically thick" (Luminosity and Warm Region in Hot Corino)
   - fitting: Linear proportional function fitting to column density correlations  
  > "fit the observed positive correlation trend by a linear proportional function." (Complex Organic Molecules (COMs))
   - validation: Cross-comparison of COM abundance ratios with CALYPSO, PEACHES, and individual hot corinos in the literature  
  > "Using the main isotopologue of methanol (CH$_3$OH), we also compare our results with the column densities of the corresponding species in the Perseus protostars from PEACHES (cite: 2021Yang_PEACHES) and the three Serpens hot corinos from (cite: 2019Bergner_Ser-emb_COM)." (Complex Organic Molecules (COMs))

## Qualitative findings

- All 11 detected hot corino sources in the ALMASOP sample are likely Class 0 protostellar cores, as the single nominally Class I object (G208E) lacks far-IR (70-160 um) data and exhibits a Class 0-like SED.  
  > "All the hot corino sources discovered are likely Class 0 and their sizes of the warm region ($>$ 100 K) are comparable to 100 au." (Abstract); "All the hot corinos we detect are therefore likely Class 0 protostellar cores." (Conclusion)
- The sizes of the >100 K warm regions inferred from YSO SED modeling positively correlate with the deconvolved CH3OH-46K emission sizes with a ratio near unity, and are larger than the inferred disk sizes, indicating COM emission extends into the inner envelopes.  
  > "There exists a positive correlation between the two quantities with a ratio on the order of unity, indicating that the YSO model exported by the SED fitting is reflecting a coherent trend seen in the real observations." (Warm Region Sizes in the YSO Models); "In addition, the sizes of the warm region are larger than the inferred disk sizes, hinting that the warm regions, where COM emissions originate, extend to the inner envelopes." (Warm Region Sizes in the YSO Models)
- Main isotopologue CH3OH emission is optically thick across the hot corino sample, as evidenced by the low apparent [CH3OH]/[13CH3OH] ratio of 4.  
  > "The low $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio for our hot corino sources most likely suggests high optical depths of the main CH$_3$OH isotopologue" (Isotope Ratios of Methanol: $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O, and D/H,)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining ALMA 12-m (TM1 and TM2) and 7-m (ACA) array data provides ~0.35 arcsec (~140 au) angular resolution, reducing beam dilution by ~300x compared to ACA-only data to resolve compact hot corino emission.  
  > "Following the successful identifications of the hot corino sources with the ACA data in (cite: Hsu2020_ALMASOP), we further combined the ALMA and ACA data for gaining a better angular resolution." (Observations); "In contrast, the beam filling factor of this study is about 300 times higher than that of (cite: Hsu2020_ALMASOP), leading to much less beam dilution and much ease for the detection." (Sensitivity and Hot Corino Detection)
- Why this method: Simultaneous multi-species LTE fitting in XCLASS/MAGIX accounts for line blending in line-rich spectra, and using 13CH3OH circumvents the high optical depth of main CH3OH.  
  > "We then run overall fittings for all species simultaneously with Bees algorithm and LM algorithm for each source to take into account blending of lines." (Data and Tools); "Since there is no CH$_3$CN transition within our spectral coverage, and to circumvent the potential optical depth issue of CH$_3$OH, we consider the column densities of their $^{13}$C isotopologues, namely $^{13}$CH$_3$CN and $^{13}$CH$_3$OH." (CH$_3$CN vs CH$_3$OH)
- Alternative: Applying SED fitting to G196A, G203W2, and G208E was rejected because they lack 70-100 um photometry needed to constrain total luminosity.  
  > "We applied the SED fitting analysis only to eight sources, including G192, G205S1A, G206W2, G208N1, G209N1B, 209S1, G210WA, and G211S, as the other three targets lack photometric data at [70 — 100] $\mu$m band, the critical wavelengths for constraining the total luminosity." (Tools and Methods)
- Alternative: Fitting the CO J=2-1 transition in XCLASS was rejected due to its non-Gaussian line profile.  
  > "The CO $J=2-1$ transition is detected in all of the sources but not fitted by XCLASS due to the non-Gaussian line profile." (Molecular parameters of hot corino sources)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): For molecules detected with only a single transition, T_ex is fixed to 100 K, making their column density estimates less precise.  
  > "For molecules detected with only one transition, we assume their $T_\mathrm{ex}$ to be 100 K, the typical temperature of a hot corino. The estimate of column density ($N$) in these cases hence may not be less precise." (Data and Tools)
- Limitation (authors): Reconstructing the exact R06 YSO radiative transfer models is limited because the 2006 version of HO-CHUNK is no longer available and some grid input parameters (e.g., magnetospheric truncation radius, hot spot area) were not directly provided.  
  > "These limitations are: (1) the version of the HO-CHUNK code for making the R06 grid was released in 2006 but is no longer available; (2) part of the HO-CHUNK R06 model grid parameters, such as the magnetosphere co-rotation radius, are not advertised, and (3) some input models (e.g. the grain dust model and the stellar photosphere model) used by HO-CHUNK are not directly provided." (Warm Region Sizes in the YSO Models)
- Limitation (authors): Inferred YSO thermal structures are model-dependent and simplified compared to real protostellar cores.  
  > "While the inferred physical (thermal) structures of the sources are model dependent and the real situation may be far more complicated than what has been assumed in the SED models, the results from our analysis are nevertheless indicative." (Warm Region Sizes in the YSO Models)
- Limitation (authors): Missing photometric data at 70-160 um for G196A, G203W2, and G208E makes their T_bol and L_bol uncertain or severely underestimated.  
  > "Therefore, the reported $L_\mathrm{bol}$ values of these sources (G203W2 and G208E) may be severely underestimated and represent the lower limits." (SED and Classification of Hot Corino)
- Limitation (authors): High dust continuum optical depth may obscure molecular line emission, causing hot corino detection rates in both ALMASOP and PEACHES to be underestimated.  
  > "Finally, we note that the dust continuum opacity may prevent the detection of molecular line emissions (cite: 2019Sahu_IRAS4A1; 2020DeSimone_dust_opacity). The detection rates of hot corinos in both ALMASOP and PEACHES are therefore possibly underestimated." (Comparisons between ALMASOP and PEACHES)
- Limitation (authors): Main isotopologue CH3OH lines are optically thick, and even 13CH3OH column density in G208N1 may be slightly underestimated.  
  > "It appears much closer, though still less than the local ISM value, implying possibly an underestimate of the $^{13}$CH$_3$OH column density." (Isotope Ratios of Methanol: $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O, and D/H,)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming the same source size (the deconvolved CH3OH-46K moment-0 Gaussian FWHM) for all 36 molecular species in XCLASS ignores chemical stratification and excitation gradients across transitions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several species in Tables 6-16 hit or exceed the stated initial T_ex upper bound of 400 K (e.g., HCOOCH3 at 480 K in G192, 13CH3CN at 429 K in G211S), suggesting poorly constrained excitation temperatures or optical depth effects.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using robust = 2.0 (natural weighting) maximizes point-source sensitivity but degrades angular resolution (~0.41-0.46 arcsec), leaving most COM emission regions only marginally resolved.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The chi-square values of the best-fit R06 SED models (Table 20) are very large (hundreds to >10,000), indicating poor formal fits or underestimated photometric uncertainties.
