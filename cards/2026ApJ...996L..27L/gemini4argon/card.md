   - parameter: robust = 0.5 for continuum; natural weighting and 4-channel averaging (13.5 km/s) for CO(3-2)

2. **Measure dust continuum and CO(3-2) line polarization and validate against instrumental polarization**: Select pixels with PI/sigma_PI >= 4 (and I_CO/sigma_I_CO >= 140 for CO(3-2)), rotate dust polarization angles by 90 deg to obtain plane-of-sky B-field orientation, rotate CO(3-2) polarization angles by 90 deg based on offset from dust, and measure integrated quantities in elliptical apertures of 0.5'' x 0.6''  
  > "For the polarimetric analysis, we only use polarization measurements with $PI/\sigma_{\rm PI} \ge 4$, where $PI$ is the polarized intensity and $\sigma_{\rm{PI}}$ is its uncertainty. For the CO(3-2) line emission polarization analysis, we apply the additional constraint $I_{\rm{CO}}/\sigma_{\rm{I}_{\rm{CO}}} \ge 140$." (Observations and Data Reduction); "For dust polarization, the measured polarization angles were rotated by $90^{\circ}$ to show the plane-on-the-sky B-field orientation." (Results); "Using an elliptical aperture of $200\times240$ pc$^{2}$ ($0.5\ensuremath{''} \times0.6\ensuremath{''}$) centered at the peak of each core and tilted at $+25^{\circ}$ and $-25^{\circ}$ for the East and West nuclei, we compute integrated polarization fractions of $0.4\pm0.1$% and $0.2\pm0.1$%, respectively." (Dust polarization of Arp 220)
   - parameter: PI/sigma_PI >= 4; I_CO/sigma_I_CO >= 140; elliptical apertures 0.5'' x 0.6'' tilted at +25 deg (East) and -25 deg (West)  
  > "For the polarimetric analysis, we only use polarization measurements with $PI/\sigma_{\rm PI} \ge 4$, where $PI$ is the polarized intensity and $\sigma_{\rm{PI}}$ is its uncertainty. For the CO(3-2) line emission polarization analysis, we apply the additional constraint $I_{\rm{CO}}/\sigma_{\rm{I}_{\rm{CO}}} \ge 140$." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Using an elliptical aperture of $200\times240$ pc$^{2}$ ($0.5\ensuremath{''} \times0.6\ensuremath{''}$) centered at the peak of each core and tilted at $+25^{\circ}$ and $-25^{\circ}$ for the East and West nuclei, we compute integrated polarization fractions of $0.4\pm0.1$% and $0.2\pm0.1$%, respectively." (Dust polarization of Arp 220)
   - assumption: Both nuclei are nearly optically thin at 345.8 GHz (nu^2.4 flux dependence); both dust and CO(3-2) trace the same dominant B-field component in the outflow so CO(3-2) polarization is perpendicular to B-field  
  > "We estimate that both nuclei are nearly optically thin at $345.8$ GHz after obtaining a flux dependence of $\sim\nu^{2.4}$ in the spectral range of $100-691$ GHz using fluxes within apertures of $0.1\ensuremath{''}-0.5\ensuremath{''}$ from (cite: Sakamoto2008; wilson2014; Scoville2016; Martin2016)." (Dust polarization of Arp 220); "The main assumption is that both tracers are sensitive to the dominant B-field component in the outflow, i.e., parallel to the outflow as directly measured by dust polarization. Thus, a $90^{\circ}$ rotation has been applied to the CO(3–2) polarization angles displayed in Figs. 1 and 3." (Summary and Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Split visibilities into two Hour Angle / parallactic angle ranges (0.0h-2.0h and 2.0h-3.4h) and verify consistency of Stokes Q, U, and V in apertures  
  > "To investigate the possible contributions of the residual instrumental polarization, we split the visibilities into two different Hour Angle (HA), or parallactic angle, ranges: from $0.0\text{h}$ to $2.0\text{h}$ ($140\ensuremath{^\circ}-180\ensuremath{^\circ}$) and from $2.0\text{h}$ to $3.4\text{h}$ ($125\ensuremath{^\circ}-140\ensuremath{^\circ}$)." 3. **Estimate magnetic field strengths in the molecular outflows of Arp 220 W**: Calculate B-field strengths using turbulent kinetic energy equipartition (B_eq), classical Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi (B_DCF), modified DCF for shear flows in outflows (B'_DCF), and SFR scaling (B_SFR), with uncertainties from bootstrapping 1,000 random iterations  
  > "We compute the median and standard deviation using bootstrapping with 1,000 random iterations for the blue and redshifted outflows." (Summary and Discussion); "We estimate B-field strengths of $4.0\pm 1.7$ mG and $18.2\pm 6.8$ mG for the blue and redshifted outflows, respectively, using the Classical Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi (DCF) method (cite: Davis1951; CF1953)." (Summary and Discussion); "We use the outflow velocities of $U_{0} = 460$ and $500$ km s$^{-1}$, and estimate B-field strengths of $1.1\pm 1.0$ mG and $9.5\pm 5.6$ mG in the blue and redshifted outflows, respectively." (Summary and Discussion)
   - parameter: sigma_CO = 220+-20 km/s; M_CO = 10^7 M_sun (red) and 10^6 M_sun (blue); V = 121x50x50 pc^3 (red) and 90x50x50 pc^3 (blue); U_0 = 500+-50 km/s (red) and 460+-50 km/s (blue); sigma_theta = 12+-5 deg (red) and 20+-10 deg (blue)  
  > "| $\sigma_{\rm{CO}}$ | Velocity dispersion CO(3-2) | $220\pm20$ km s$^{-1}$ | $220\pm20$ km s$^{-1}$ |" (Table 3); "| U$_{o}$ | Velocity molecular outflow CO(3-2) | $500\pm50$ km s$^{-1}$ | $460\pm50$ km s$^{-1}$ |" (Table 3); "| $\sigma_{\theta}$ | Angular dispersion | $12\pm5^{\circ}$ | $20\pm10^{\circ}$ |" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric outflow volume; factor of 2 uncertainty in outflow molecular mass; dust and molecular gas are cospatially embedded in the same B-field of the outflow  
  > "The angular dispersion, $\sigma_{\theta}$, was estimated as the standard deviation of the polarization angles of the dust and molecular line polarization measurements, which intrinsically assume that both the dust and molecular gas are cospatially embedded in the same B-field of the outflow." ## Qualitative findings

- First detection of CO(3-2) emission line polarization through the Goldreich-Kylafis effect in an extragalactic molecular outflow  
  > "We perform ALMA $870\,\mu$m dust continuum polarization and CO(3-2) emission line polarization, and report the first detection of CO(3-2) emission line polarization through the Goldreich-Kylafis effect in an outflow." (Abstract); "We report the first detection of CO(3–2) line polarization in the outflow of an external galaxy." (Summary and Discussion)
- Arp 220 E exhibits a spiral-like B-field structure on the disk, while Arp 220 W has a vertical B-field parallel to its bipolar molecular outflow  
  > "Dust polarization shows that Arp 220 E has a spiral-like B-field on the disk with a linear polarization fraction of $0.4\pm0.1$% that may produce the detected circular polarization passing through foreground aligned dust grains. Arp 220 W reveals a B-field parallel to the red- and blueshifted outflows in both the dust and emission line polarization maps." (Abstract)
- A highly polarized (3-5%) dusty bridge ('overlap' region) with a B-field orientation of ~110 deg connects both nuclei  
  > "A highly polarized ($3-5$%) dusty bridge has a B-field orientation of $\sim110^{\circ}$ connecting both nuclei." (Abstract)
- Likely detection of continuum circular polarization (Stokes V) of 0.3+-0.1% in Arp 220 E, whereas Stokes V in Arp 220 W is consistent with instrumental polarization  
  > "We report the likely detection of circular polarization (Stokes V) of $0.3\pm0.1$%, in Arp 220 E within a $0.5\ensuremath{''}$ ($200$ pc) aperture." (Circular polarization in the core of Arp 220 E); "However, the Stokes V in the Arp 220,W is consistent with instrumental polarization." (Circular polarization in the core of Arp 220 E)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Arp 220 is the closest ULIRG and an extremely dusty, molecular merger with prominent high-velocity molecular outflows, while previous radio polarimetry missed the cold dense ISM and previous dust polarimetry was spatially unresolved.  
  > "Given its extremely dusty and molecular nature, Arp 220 serves as an excellent system to quantify the effect of B-fields on both its ISM and outflows." (Introduction); "In all cases, these B-fields measurements were obtained via radio polarimetric observations sensitive to the diffuse and hot ISM, consequently missing the B-field structure within the cold and dense ISM, star-forming regions, and outflows of Arp 220." (Introduction); "These previous works utilized unresolved polarimetric observations and consistently reported a polarization fraction in the range of $\sim1-2$% and a plane-of-the-sky B-field orientation at a position angle (PA) of $\sim80-100^{\circ}$, which effectively traces the dust ridge (‘overlap’) connecting the two nuclei." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Combining dust continuum polarization and Goldreich-Kylafis molecular line polarization resolves the 90 deg GK ambiguity and traces the B-field in the dusty and molecular outflow, while the modified DCF method accounts for large-scale shear flows where classical DCF fails.  
  > "The combination of these two effects—dust polarization and molecular line polarization—can be utilized to provide a comprehensive description of the B-field properties within the dusty and molecular medium (cite: Girart1999; Lai2003; Cortes2021)." (Introduction); "The dust polarization, from magnetically aligned dust grains, provides an alternative approach to estimate the B-field orientation of the CO(3-2) molecular outflow." (Summary and Discussion); "However, the Classical DCF method cannot be applied to outflows due to the effects of shearing and compression." (Summary and Discussion)
- Alternative: Classical DCF method is rejected as a direct measure in outflows because of shearing and compression, and is treated only as an upper limit  
  > "However, the Classical DCF method cannot be applied to outflows due to the effects of shearing and compression. These B-field strengths should be considered as upper-limits." (Summary and Discussion)
- Alternative: Foreground aligned dust grain conversion of linear to circular polarization in Arp 220 E is noted as a possible interpretation of Stokes V, but not pursued further due to detection reliability uncertainty  
  > "As Arp 220 E is linearly polarized, the circular polarization can be interpreted as the conversion from linear to circular polarization by a foreground of dust grains aligned by a B-field. Given the uncertainty in the reliability of the detection, however, we do not consider this option further." (Circular polarization in the core of Arp 220 E)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Higher spatial resolution observations are needed to separate the northern spiral arm and overlap region in Arp 220 E, and to resolve the central disk of Arp 220 W and the blueshifted polarized outflow  
  > "Both the continuum and polarized dust emission are also elongated along this direction, though the central disk of the galaxy remains unresolved in our observations." (Dust polarization of Arp 220); "Higher spatial resolution observations will be required to resolve both contributions spatially." (Dust polarization of Arp 220); "The blueshifted polarized outflow is unresolved." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): A detailed radiative transfer analysis is required to break the 90 deg Goldreich-Kylafis ambiguity directly rather than relying on the assumption of cospatial dust polarization  
  > "However, line emission polarization angle has a $90^{\circ}$ ambiguity, and a detailed radiative transfer analysis using the physical conditions of the outflow is required to estimate the exact orientation of the B-field in the molecular outflow (cite: Lankhaar2020; Lankhaar2022; Lankhaar2023)." (Summary and Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Small number of independent Nyquist polarization measurements in the outflows means DCF B-field strength estimates should be considered upper limits and order-of-magnitude estimates  
  > "Higher angular resolution observations are required to spatially resolve the outflows and enable the use of more sophisticated approaches (cite: Hildebrand2009), so our measurements should be considered upper limits and order-of-magnitude estimates." (Summary and Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Linear polarization in the West core (~0.2%) is close to the 0.1% ALMA instrumental accuracy, so a slight instrumental influence cannot be definitively ruled out, and Stokes V (0.3%) is well below the nominal 1.8% ALMA circular polarization accuracy  
  > "In the case of the West core, the linear polarization fraction is at the level of the ALMA accuracy, but the consistency between the two HA ranges suggests that it appears to be real, although we cannot definitively rule out a slight influence from instrumental polarization. Regarding the circular polarization, the measured values are much lower than the ALMA accuracy ($1.8\%$)." (On the residual instrumental polarization)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The bandwidth of the four spectral windows is printed as '1.875 MHz' in Section 2, which is an obvious typo for 1.875 GHz in standard ALMA Band 7 continuum/TDM-like or FDM setups.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 2 and Section 4c, the equipartition equation is written inconsistently: Table 2 writes B_eq = sqrt(4*pi*rho_CO)*sigma_CO whereas the text writes B_eq = sqrt(4*pi*rho_CO)*sigma_CO^2 (dimensionally incorrect) and also defines 3D dispersion sigma_{v,CO} = sqrt(3)*sigma_CO.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The angular dispersion sigma_theta combines dust and CO(3-2) polarization angles under the assumption that both trace the exact same B-field, with very few Nyquist samples (2 dust and 4 CO in the blueshifted outflow).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The HCO+(4-3) spectral window was observed in the setup but no results or analysis for it are presented.
