## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the archival VLA data**: Standard CASA calibration with the VLA pipeline; imaging with robust 0 and a (u,v) cut at >100 kilolambda to suppress structure larger than 2 arcsec  
  > "The images were made with a $(u,v)$ range set to $>100~k\lambda$, to suppress structures larger than 2$''$ that limit the signal-to-noise ratio in the Orion field." (VLA Observations); "The data were calibrated in the standard manner using the CASA (Common Astronomy Software Applications; (cite: McMullin+2007)) package of NRAO" (VLA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 0  
  > "A robust weighting of 0 (cite: Briggs_1995) was used in the imaging" (VLA Observations)
   - parameter: (u,v) range > 100 kilolambda  
  > "The images were made with a $(u,v)$ range set to $>100~k\lambda$" (VLA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Search ALMA archive data for millimeter counterparts of the JuMBOs**: Searched archival ALMA data sets for continuum counterparts; used the 2015.1.00669.S Band 3 mosaic rms to set a 5-sigma limit  
  > "We searched for JuMBO counterparts in the Atacama Large Millimeter/Submillimeter Array archive." (ALMA Observations); "so we do not detect any counterpart at a 5-$\sigma$ level of 750 $\mu$Jy Beam$^{-1}$." (ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: 5-sigma level of 750 uJy/beam from rms 150 uJy/beam  
  > "The rms noise for this large mosaic is 150 $\mu$Jy Beam$^{-1}$, so we do not detect any counterpart at a 5-$\sigma$ level of 750 $\mu$Jy Beam$^{-1}$." (ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure flux density and position of the JuMBO 24 radio counterpart in each VLA epoch**: Flux density, position and circular polarization limit listed per epoch in Table 1; the near-infrared source coincides within 0.05 arcsec with a radio source in all three images  
  > "we also give the mean epoch of the observations, the configurations used, the central frequency and the bandwidth of the observations, the parameters of the synthesized beam, the radio flux density and position of JuMBO 24" (VLA Observations); "coincides within 0.05 arcsec with a radio continuum source present in all three VLA images" (VLA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the Forbrich et al. 2016 source list, where the radio source first appeared  
  > "This radio source first appeared in the list of (cite: Forbrich+2016)." (VLA Observations)
4. **Estimate chance-coincidence probability**: Radio source surface density from Forbrich et al. 2016 times the positional error box, multiplied by 42 JuMBOs  
  > "assuming that these possible coincidences are independent events we have to multiply the probability of a given JuMBO source being spatially associated times 42." (The Probability of a Random Association)
   - parameter: 358 of 556 sources with flux density >= 50 uJy; density 3.5e-3 per arcsec^2  
  > "These results give a solid angle density of $3.5 \times 10^{-3}$ radio sources per arcsec$^2$." (The Probability of a Random Association)
   - parameter: 1-sigma error box 0.027 x 0.031 arcsec from SD0630 statistical errors plus 0.01 arcsec systematic error  
  > "we obtain a $\pm 1-\sigma$ error box of $0\rlap.{''}027 \times 0\rlap.{''}031$." (The Probability of a Random Association)
   - assumption: Coincidences with the 42 JuMBOs are independent events  
  > "assuming that these possible coincidences are independent events" (The Probability of a Random Association)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit proper motion from the three epochs**: Least-squares fit to the three radio positions with 0.01 arcsec systematic error added in quadrature; result registered to the Orion rest frame by subtracting the mean radio-star proper motion  
  > "A least-squares fit to the positions given in Table 1, after adding in quadrature a systematic error of $0\rlap.{''}01$, yields the following values:" (Upper Limits to the Proper Motion)
   - parameter: Same gain calibrator J0541-0541 in all epochs for relative astrometry  
  > "The gain calibrator, J0541$-$0541, was the same for all observations so the astrometry can be directly compared more easily." (VLA Observations)
   - assumption: Distance of 388 pc to convert proper motion to transverse velocity  
  > "that corresponds to a transverse velocity of $\sim 15$ km s$^{-1}$ at a distance of 388 pc (cite: Kounkel+2017)." (Upper Limits to the Proper Motion)
   - fitting: Least-squares  
  > "A least-squares fit to the positions given in Table 1" (Upper Limits to the Proper Motion)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Test flux variability over days and years**: Compared flux densities from the two 6.1 GHz epochs a decade apart and from the five individual SD0630 sessions (Table 2)  
  > "The two measurements at 6.1 GHz given in Table 1 are separated by a decade and, within the noise, they are consistent with being equal." (Time Monitoring); "all flux densities are consistent within noise with a value of $\simeq 45~ \mu$Jy" (Time Monitoring)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive the radio spectral index**: Power-law S ~ nu^alpha from the 4.8 and 7.4 GHz sub-bands of SD0630, and separately from the Table 1 flux densities assuming a steady source; weighted mean of the two  
  > "Assuming that the continuum spectrum is described by a power law, $S_{\nu} \propto \nu^{\alpha}$, we derive a spectral index of $\alpha = -0.52\pm0.40$." (Nature of the Radio Emission)
   - parameter: SD0630 sub-bands of 1 GHz each centered at 4.8 and 7.4 GHz  
  > "The observations of SD0630 were made using two bandwidths of 1 GHz each, centered at 4.8 and 7.4 GHz, respectively." (Nature of the Radio Emission)
   - assumption: Power-law continuum spectrum; steady flux density for the second determination  
  > "We can also derive a spectral index assuming that the source has a steady flux density and combining the values given in Table 1." (Nature of the Radio Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Measure the radio source size and compare with JWST morphology**: Gaussian ellipsoid fit with CASA IMFIT to the concatenated SD0630 image, compared with a fit to the JWST 2.77 micron image  
  > "A Gaussian ellipsoid fit to the radio image using the task IMFIT of CASA gives a deconvolved angular size of" (Position and Morphology of the Radio and Infrared Sources)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: Gaussian ellipsoid (IMFIT)  
  > "A Gaussian ellipsoid fit to the radio image using the task IMFIT of CASA" (Position and Morphology of the Radio and Infrared Sources)
   - validation: Consistent with Gaussian fit to the JWST 2.77 micron image (0.170 x 0.132 arcsec, PA 97 deg)  
  > "these angular dimensions and the orientation of the major axis are consistent with the value obtained from Gaussian ellipsoid fits to the JWST images." (Position and Morphology of the Radio and Infrared Sources)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sensitive archival VLA observations of Orion were searched for counterparts to the JuMBOs  
  > "using sensitive observations from the archive of the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) of the National Radio Astronomy Observatory." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Robust 0 chosen to balance sensitivity and angular resolution; (u,v) cut to suppress extended structure limiting S/N  
  > "A robust weighting of 0 (cite: Briggs_1995) was used in the imaging to optimize the compromise between sensitivity and angular resolution." (VLA Observations)
- Alternative: Optically thick Rayleigh-Jeans blackbody (alpha = 2) and pulsar-like steep negative indices (alpha <= -2) ruled out by the spectral index  
  > "we can rule out an optically-thick blackbody in the Rayleigh-Jeans regime ($\alpha$ = 2) and the negative steep indices characteristic of pulsars ($\alpha \leq$ –2)." (Nature of the Radio Emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Large spectral index uncertainty prevents discriminating emission mechanisms  
  > "Given the large uncertainty, it is difficult to discriminate between possible emission mechanisms" (Nature of the Radio Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Significant radial velocities cannot be excluded by the plane-of-sky limits  
  > "although it should be pointed out that significant velocities could be present in the radial direction." (Upper Limits to the Proper Motion)
- Limitation (authors): Radio source only marginally resolved  
  > "Although the radio source is only marginally resolved" (Position and Morphology of the Radio and Infrared Sources)
- Limitation (authors): More intensive observations needed to establish time behavior  
  > "While more intensive observations are needed to establish firmly the time behavior of the radio emission from JuMBO 24" (Time Monitoring)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only three VLA epochs plus five sessions in one project, so the proper motion and variability limits are weak
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA non-detection is only quantified for one mosaic; limits for the other six data sets and for individual JuMBOs are not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Chance-coincidence estimate uses a surface density from a different catalog and treats the 42 JuMBOs as independent; the radio source density is not uniform across the field
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux-density and position extraction method is not described, and the marginal size measurement has PA uncertainty of 89 deg
