## Analysis recipe

1. **Compute Gaia proper motion anomaly and significance**: PMA = change in proper-motion velocity between Gaia DR2 and DR3, with significance |dmu|/sigma  
  > "We used the proper motion anomaly defined in (cite: 2022MNRAS.513.2437P) as the change in velocity between" (2 Methodology); "The significance of the proper motion anomaly is defined as $|\Delta \mu|/\sigma_{|\Delta \mu|}$." (2 Methodology)
   - parameter: Detection threshold |dmu|/sigma >= 3  
  > "We consider $|\Delta \mu|/\sigma_{|\Delta \mu|}\geq3$ a significant detection of astrometric acceleration" (3.1 Sources with significant astrometric accelerations)
   - parameter: Most detections at separations ~0.1 to 30 au when combined with RUWE  
  > "simulations suggest most detections occur for orbital separations between $\sim0.1$ to $30$ au, approximately" (2 Methodology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive stellar masses homogeneously**: Stellar masses from Teff and L using Baraffe evolutionary tracks below 1.35 Msun and PARSEC V2.0 above  
  > "we derive stellar masses homogeneously for all sources, using (cite: 2015A&A...577A..42B) evolutionary tracks for stars less than 1.35" (2 Methodology); "(cite: 2022A&A...665A.126N) PARSEC V2.0 for masses above 1.35" (2 Methodology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cited as accurate for YSOs in earlier work  
  > "This approach has proven to give accurate stellar masses in YSOs" (2 Methodology)
3. **Simulate Gaia two-body astrometry per source**: Simulate Gaia DR2 and DR3 observations at the real scanning-law epochs and scan angles for a two-body system, 40,000 runs per source  
  > "we performed $40\,000$ simulations modelling a two-body interaction" (2 Methodology); "under the assumption of a two-body system with varying mass ratios and orbital periods" (2 Methodology)
   - software: astromet
   - parameter: Log-uniform mass ratio 0.0001 to 1 and period 0.01 to 2512 yr  
  > "we randomly sampled the log-uniform space of mass ratios (from 0.0001 to 1) and periods (from 0.01 to 2512 yr)." (2 Methodology)
   - parameter: Eccentricity uniform 0 to 1; viewing angles and orbital phases random  
  > "Viewing angles and orbital phases are also taken at random." (2 Methodology)
   - assumption: Astrometric signal dominated by a single companion  
  > "we assume that, even if multiple companions are present, the astrometric signal is dominated by a single companion." (2 Methodology)
   - assumption: Light ratio l = q^3.5, extended from main-sequence stars to Class II YSOs  
  > "We extend this commonly used heuristic for main-sequence stars to Class-II YSOs" (2 Methodology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Recovery of known companions  
  > "We recovered 85% of the known companions within our sensitivity range." (abstract)
4. **Constrain companion mass and separation for detections**: Keep simulations whose |dmu|, its significance and RUWE are consistent with the observed values, then take 10th, 50th, 90th percentiles of mass and semi-major axis  
  > "we considered the simulated companions that produce both a proper motion anomaly and a RUWE consistent with the observations." (3.1 Sources with significant astrometric accelerations); "we derive the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of the distribution of possible companion masses and semi-major axes" (3.1 Sources with significant astrometric accelerations)
   - parameter: RUWE single-star limit 1.25  
  > "The 1.25 threshold for the RUWE was proposed as the upper limit for a single star solution by" (3.1 Sources with significant astrometric accelerations)
   - assumption: Broad range around observed RUWE because disks may affect RUWE  
  > "We consider a broad range around the observed RUWE as it has been suggested protoplanetary disks can impact the RUWE" (3.1 Sources with significant astrometric accelerations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with independently characterised companion of HD 142527  
  > "are in agreement with the independently characterised separation and mass of the known companion in this system." (3.3 Comment on individual sources)
5. **Exclusion limits for non-detections**: For sources without significant PMA, find simulated companions that would have produced a significant PMA or higher RUWE, and treat these as excluded  
  > "For these, we consider the simulated companions that should have produced a significant proper motion anomaly or a RUWE higher than the one observed." (3.2 Constraints from non-detections)
   - assumption: Each system's astrometry dominated by a single companion  
  > "A non-detection does not imply the absence of companions in a system." (3.2 Constraints from non-detections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Population analysis against comparison samples**: Compare the 32% companion fraction with random Gaia stars at 100-200 pc, Sco-Cen and Taurus YSO catalogues and known-binary catalogues; two-sided KS test on stellar mass  
  > "is similar to the fraction found in random populations of main-sequence and YSO stars." (4.1 Companions in the transition disk population); "A two-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test shows no significant difference in stellar mass" (4.1 Companions in the transition disk population)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test whether detected companions can carve the cavities**: Bootstrap stellar mass and companion mass and separation from the step 4 results, apply the Rosotti et al. 2025 cavity-size prescription over all inclinations and eccentricities  
  > "we apply the theoretical prescription described in (cite: 2025A&A...698A.102R) to derive the cavity size that each companion could carve." (4.2 About the detected companions carving the dust cavities); "we bootstrap the stellar mass of the primary star and the companion’s mass and separation from the results of Sect." (4.2 About the detected companions carving the dust cavities)
   - parameter: 20% cavity size uncertainty  
  > "considering a 20% cavity size uncertainty" (4.2 About the detected companions carving the dust cavities)
   - assumption: All possible companion inclinations and eccentricities considered  
  > "We consider all possible companion inclinations and eccentricities over the full range of companion mass and separation." (4.2 About the detected companions carving the dust cavities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Test other astrometric noise sources (false positives)**: FARGO3D hydro simulations for disk gravity; Monte Carlo simulations of point-like accretion and scattered-light elements  
  > "We investigate the impact of a massive, eccentric disk produced by a companion by conducting 2D locally-isothermal hydrodynamic simulations with FARGO3D" (Details of the hydrodynamic simulations); "We run $1\,000\,000$ simulations uniformly covering these ranges of $l$, separation, and stellar mass." (5 Other possible sources of astrometric signal)
   - software: FARGO3D, astromet
   - parameter: Disk masses 1% to 10% of stellar mass  
  > "a range of disk masses from 1% to 10% the stellar mass." (5 Other possible sources of astrometric signal)
   - parameter: 1000 orbits simulated  
  > "The simulations are run for 1000 orbits at" (Details of the hydrodynamic simulations)
   - assumption: Star at 150 pc in the hydro simulations  
  > "the star is assumed to be at a distance of 150 pc." (Details of the hydrodynamic simulations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Most inferred companions have M > 30 MJ, many in or near the stellar mass regime  
  > "Most inferred companions have $M > 30$ M$\rm{_{J}}$, placing many within or near the stellar mass regime." (abstract)
- GG Tau (hierarchical triple) and PDS 70 are hard to model with a two-body interaction; no solution for GG Tau  
  > "no two-body simulation could reproduce the astrometry of GG Tau" (3.1 Sources with significant astrometric accelerations)
- HD 142527 companion masses above 300 MJ are more likely  
  > "Our results indicate that companion masses above $300$ M$\rm{_{J}}$ are more likely." (3.3 Comment on individual sources)
- Transition disks host as many companions in the sensitivity range as random groups of YSOs and main-sequence stars  
  > "Transition disks host as many companions within our sensitivity range as do randomly sampled groups of YSOs and main-sequence stars." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Gaia astrometry bridges the 0.1-30 au gap where direct imaging and spectroscopy are inefficient  
  > "bridging the gap where neither direct imaging nor spectroscopy can efficiently detect them." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: PMA is mostly sensitive to asymmetric perturbations, so less affected by symmetric YSO variability  
  > "It is mostly sensitive to asymmetric perturbations, and hence it is less affected by the different types of azimuthally symmetric variability typical of YSOs." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Sampling orbit orientation from disk inclination and position angle (companions in disk plane) was tested but not used, to allow companions outside the disk plane  
  > "However, we find that this assumption does not reduce the space of companion solutions significantly" (2 Methodology)
- Alternative: Gaia DR3 proper motion corrections were tested and not applied, for consistency with DR2  
  > "find our results are mostly unchanged." (Footnote 1)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): A non-detection does not rule out companions; only a narrow range of mass and separation is excluded  
  > "A non-detection does not imply the absence of companions in a system." (3.2 Constraints from non-detections)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity to low-mass companions falls as primary mass increases, biasing inferred companion probability  
  > "loses sensitivity to low mass companions as the mass of the primary star increases" (4.1 Companions in the transition disk population)
- Limitation (authors): Two-body, single dominant companion assumption  
  > "we assume that, even if multiple companions are present, the astrometric signal is dominated by a single companion." (2 Methodology)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar masses for pre-main-sequence stars carry large (often 30-50%) uncertainties that feed directly into companion masses
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Companion masses depend on the assumed l = q^3.5 light ratio, which is untested for YSOs
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two-body assumption fails for PDS 70 and GG Tau, so the same may bias other multiples
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison populations are not matched in magnitude, distance and RUWE selection in a documented way
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA data play no quantitative role; cavity sizes are inherited from heterogeneous literature measurements
