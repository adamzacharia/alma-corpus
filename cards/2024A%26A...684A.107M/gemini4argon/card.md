## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract optical emission line profiles and determine systemic and expansion velocities**: Extract GTC MEGARA spectra of the central ejecta from a 1.8 arcsec radius circular aperture, subtract surrounding nebular contamination averaged from four corner apertures of the same size, and measure systemic and expansion velocities from double-peaked profiles in MEGARA and CTIO echelle spectra.  
  > "The profiles of the H-poor ejecta (red dashed histograms in Fig. 1) have been extracted from a circular region 1′′.8 in radius around the brightest region." (3.1. Spectral Line Profiles); "This emission is estimated by averaging four apertures located in each quadrant of MEGARA’s FoV with the same size as that used for the central ejecta." (3.1. Spectral Line Profiles); "Considering the average of the nebular red and blue components to be the systemic velocity, Vsys, we derive velocities in the Local Standard of Rest (LSR) from the CTIO echelle data of 101.8 km s−1 and 104.4 km s−1 for the Hα and [N ii] lines, respectively." (3.1.1. Systemic velocities of A 58 and its central ejecta)
   - software: megaradrp, megararss2cube, IRAF
   - parameter: Aperture radius = 1.8 arcsec  
  > "The profiles of the H-poor ejecta (red dashed histograms in Fig. 1) have been extracted from a circular region 1′′.8 in radius around the brightest region." (3.1. Spectral Line Profiles)
   - assumption: Expansion velocity of the nebula is half the separation between the red and blue components; systemic velocity is the average of the red and blue components.  
  > "If the expansion velocity of the nebula, Vexp, is assumed to be half the separation between the red and blue components, we derive velocities of 30 and 35 km s−1 for Hα and [N ii], respectively" (3.1. Spectral Line Profiles); "Considering the average of the nebular red and blue components to be the systemic velocity, Vsys, we derive velocities in the Local Standard of Rest (LSR) from the CTIO echelle data of 101.8 km s−1 and 104.4 km s−1 for the Hα and [N ii] lines, respectively." (3.1.1. Systemic velocities of A 58 and its central ejecta)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency between GTC MEGARA dips, CTIO echelle double-peak averages, and ALMA/APEX molecular line profiles.  
  > "These values are found to be consistent to the observed dips in the GTC" (3.1.1 Systemic velocities of A 58 and its central ejecta)
2. **Sub-resolution spatial-kinematic tomography of optical emission and alignment with HST and ALMA**: Determine the position of the central star V605 Aql by fitting a 2D Gaussian to collapsed line-free continuum images from MEGARA cubes, then fit 2D Gaussians to the [O III] and [N II] emission in each spectral channel (-250 to +350 km s^-1) to measure channel centroids and FWHMs, and overlay onto HST and ALMA CO maps.  
  > "The position of V605 Aql was then determined by adjusting a two-dimensional Gaussian to the stellar emission located near the center of the image." (3.2.1. Relative location of V605 Aql and the optical outflow); "The high S/N achieved in the MEGARA data actually allows increasing the accuracy of the spatial location of the emission at each velocity channel by a factor ≈10 by the determining the position of the centroid of the optical emission through a two-dimensional Gaussian fit (Condon 1997)." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution); "Finally the lower panels of Fig. 3 show the CO (J = 3 →2) first momentum image presented by Tafoya et al. (2022) overplotted by the MEGARA [O iii] and [N ii] centroids presented in the upper panels." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution)
   - parameter: LSR velocity range = -250 to +350 km s^-1; exclude centroids > 1 arcsec from V605 Aql or with Gaussian FWHM > 1.5 arcsec  
  > "These fittings were conducted for channels within the LSR velocity range from −250 to +350 km s−1." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution); "Channels with associated centroids located more than 1′′ away from the position of V605 Aql or with Gaussian FWHM exceeding 1′′.5 in any of its axes were excluded." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution)
   - assumption: V_sys = +103 km s^-1  
  > "The centroids and FWHMs are represented with a color code indicating their velocity with respect to the adopted systemic velocity of +103 km s−1." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fit per spectral channel  
  > "The high S/N achieved in the MEGARA data actually allows increasing the accuracy of the spatial location of the emission at each velocity channel by a factor ≈10 by the determining the position of the centroid of the optical emission through a two-dimensional Gaussian fit (Condon 1997)." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **3D spatio-kinematic modeling of the ionized and molecular ejecta**: Construct a 3D morpho-kinematic mesh model in SHAPE combining a radially expanding molecular disk and hourglass-shaped ionized bipolar lobes, applying an ad hoc emission law to mimic dust extinction behind the disk and radial falloff, and render a synthetic [O III] image.  
  > "The upper-left panel of this figure shows a spatio-kinematic model created with the software SHAPE (Steffen et al. 2011) consisting of a bipolar structure and a radially expanding disk inclined at an angle of i = 63◦± 4◦ with respect to the line of sight." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58); "Instead an arbitrary emission law has been included in the model to simulate both the effects of extinction (emission decreasing with the optical depth along the line of sight and very particularly behind the molecular disk) and distance to the CSPN (emission decreasing with radial distance to the CSPN)." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
   - software: SHAPE
   - parameter: inclination i = 63 +/- 4 deg; PA = 63 deg  
  > "The upper-left panel of this figure shows a spatio-kinematic model created with the software SHAPE (Steffen et al. 2011) consisting of a bipolar structure and a radially expanding disk inclined at an angle of i = 63◦± 4◦ with respect to the line of sight." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58); "These structures, oriented along a PA of 63◦ (as proposed by Tafoya et al. 2022), produce the image shown in the upper-right panel of the figure." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
   - assumption: Radial velocity vector with homologous expansion at each point; molecular disk does not emit optically but absorbs optical emission behind it.  
  > "The molecular disk is not expected to emit in the optical emission line, but it rather extincts the emission behind it." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58); "Considering this physical structure for the ionized material and assuming a radial velocity vector with homologous expansion at each point, it is possible to interpret the behavior of the [O iii] emission centroids shown in Fig. 3." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual comparison of the synthetic [O III] image rendered from SHAPE with the HST [O III] image.  
  > "The bottom panels show the HST [O iii] image (left) and a synthetic image rendered from the SHAPE model (right)." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)

## Qualitative findings

- H-alpha emission from the born-again H-poor ejecta is unambiguously detected for the first time, showing only the blueshifted component because the redshifted component is absorbed by the dusty molecular disk.  
  > "This is the first time that the Hα emission of the born-again ejecta is unambiguously detected, although a revision of figure 2 in Pollacco et al. (1992) reveals arguable evidence for its detection." (3.1. Spectral Line Profiles); "Only the blue component is detected, whereas the red component is deemed to be completely absorbed behind a high-density molecular and dusty disk." (5. Conclusions)
- The spatial velocity gradient of optical [O III] and [N II] emission (blueshifted to NE, redshifted to SE/SW) is opposite to that of the ALMA CO molecular disk (redshifted to NE, blueshifted to SW), showing that the molecular disk does not emit significantly in the optical.  
  > "According to this model based on the CO (J=3→2) spatiokinematic information, the redshifted emission from the disk is found towards the NE of the CSPN, while the blueshifted emission is found towards the SW." (4.1 Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58); "This is exactly the opposite to the spatial distribution of the [O iii] and [N ii] emission (Fig. 3), thus indicating that the molecular disk is not emitting in the optical" (4.1 Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
- The ionized ejecta form an hourglass-like bipolar structure that surrounds the compact high-velocity CO molecular components and is bisected by the expanding molecular and dusty torus.  
  > "We propose an hourglass structure for the ionized material that embraces molecular high-velocity polar components, while bisected by an expanding toroidal molecular and dusty structure." (abstract); "The high-velocity components observed in the CO (J = 3 → 2) emission line are located within the cavities formed by the ionized gas, which is escaping around it, most likely transferring momentum to the molecular material and dragging it outwards." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
- Unlike the compact high-velocity CO components, the high-velocity component of ALMA HCN (J=4->3) emission is diffuse and more extended, tracing highly excited gas closer to the central source.  
  > "Contrary to the CO molecule, the spatial distribution of the high-velocity component of HCN is not compact, but it is rather diffuse and more extended." (4.2. Spatial distribution of the HCN and CO high-velocity components)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GTC MEGARA provides the high-dispersion integral field spectroscopy needed to spatially and kinematically resolve the H-deficient optical ejecta, while ALMA molecular observations are unaffected by dust extinction and provide the underlying disk-outflow geometry.  
  > "Here we present results for A 58 that in conjunction with the available molecular emission detected by ALMA provide an unprecedented view of this born-again PN." (1. Introduction); "On the other hand the radio emission of molecular material, which is mostly unaffected by the extinction, allows a more reliable determination of the systemic velocity of the recent ejecta." (3.1.1. Systemic velocities of A 58 and its central ejecta)
- Why this method: Fitting 2D Gaussians to individual velocity channels boosts the centroid localization accuracy by a factor of ~10, overcoming the ~1.1 arcsec spatial resolution limit of the MEGARA seeing and spaxel scale.  
  > "The high S/N achieved in the MEGARA data actually allows increasing the accuracy of the spatial location of the emission at each velocity channel by a factor ≈10 by the determining the position of the centroid of the optical emission through a two-dimensional Gaussian fit (Condon 1997)." (3.3. Boosting the Tomography Spatial Resolution)
- Alternative: Assigning the position of the centroid of the optical emission at the systemic velocity to V605 Aql (rejected because it showed a noticeable shift compared to the stellar continuum centroid).  
  > "It is worth noting that we also explored the possibility of assigning the position of the centroid of the emission at the systemic velocity to V605 Aql." (3.2.1. Relative location of V605 Aql and the optical outflow); "However, this method revealed a quite noticeable shift with the position of the stellar continuum." (3.2.1. Relative location of V605 Aql and the optical outflow)
- Alternative: Simple expanding sphere model with azimuthally varying extinction from Clayton et al. (2013) (rejected because MEGARA velocity centroids are not distributed symmetrically around the CSPN and do not follow spherical predictions).  
  > "The variations in the MEGARA centroids of [O iii] and [N ii] (Fig. 3) do not follow that expected from a simple spherical model, although Clayton et al. (2013) noted variations in the profiles of these emission lines that attributed to small-scale dust inhomogeneities or material clumps." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Subtraction of the surrounding old nebula emission from the central ejecta spectrum is imperfect due to spatial surface brightness variations in the outer nebula.  
  > "These residuals most likely result from spatial variations of the emission of the surrounding old nebula (see, e.g., figure 2 in Guerrero & Manchado 1996)." (3.1. Spectral Line Profiles)
- Limitation (authors): MEGARA data cubes lack absolute astrometric positioning, and there is a 0.26 arcsec (1.3 pixel) offset in the fitted stellar continuum position between VPH481-MR and VPH665-HR.  
  > "At any rate there is no information on the absolute positioning of the MEGARA data cubes, which compromises a comparison of the spatial properties of the optical outflow with the HST and ALMA observations." (3.2.1. Relative location of V605 Aql and the optical outflow); "data cubes of 1.3 pixels, i.e., 0 [′′] . 26, which is most likely due" (3.2.1 Relative location of V605 Aql and the optical outflow)
- Limitation (authors): SHAPE does not include radiative transfer physics, so the spatially varying dust extinction from the disk had to be approximated with an arbitrary emission law.  
  > "In this sense it should be noted that SHAPE does not account for radiative transfer physics, thus the effects of the spatially-varying extinction caused by the disk cannot be modeled." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58); "Instead an arbitrary emission law has been included in the model to simulate both the effects of extinction (emission decreasing with the optical depth along the line of sight and very particularly behind the molecular disk) and distance to the CSPN (emission decreasing with radial distance to the CSPN)." (4.1. Physical Structure of the H-poor Ejecta of A 58)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 2D Gaussian centroiding per velocity channel assumes a single dominant emission peak per channel; if both lobes of the hourglass or clumpy structures contribute simultaneously at a given velocity, the single-Gaussian centroid will reflect a flux-weighted blend rather than a physical structure's location.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The alignment between ALMA, HST, and MEGARA relies on matching the optical continuum centroid, the HST F547M peak, and the ALMA center, which may be affected by severe, wavelength-dependent dust scattering and extinction near V605 Aql.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SHAPE model is qualitative and relies on an unparameterized ad hoc extinction/emission law rather than radiative transfer.
