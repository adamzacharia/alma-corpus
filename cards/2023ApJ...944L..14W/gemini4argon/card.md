## Analysis recipe

1. **Source selection and multi-band aperture photometry**: Select sources on the JWST F335M image and measure aperture photometry at corresponding positions across JWST and HST bands using a 0.155'' radius aperture without aperture corrections.  
  > "Aperture photometry was performed using a $0.155\ensuremath{''}$ radius aperture with a sky annulus between $0.217\ensuremath{''}$ and $0.310\ensuremath{''}$, as described in (cite: rodriguez22)." (Observations and Reductions); "Source selection was performed based on the F335M image, and the corresponding positions were then measured in the other bands. The ABmag system is used throughout." (Observations and Reductions)
   - parameter: Aperture radius = 0.155'' (5 pixels in F200W); sky annulus = 0.217'' to 0.310''; ABmag system  
  > "Aperture photometry was performed using a $0.155\ensuremath{''}$ radius aperture with a sky annulus between $0.217\ensuremath{''}$ and $0.310\ensuremath{''}$, as described in (cite: rodriguez22)." (Observations and Reductions); "This subtends a five pixel radius for the F200W NIRCAM observations" (Observations and Reductions)
   - assumption: JWST magnitudes are used in a relative sense without aperture corrections  
  > "For this reason, the JWST magnitudes are used in a relative rather than absolute sense throughout this paper." (Observations and Reductions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Compile census of massive young star clusters and separate clusters from stars**: Combine 19 optically selected HST clusters (Age < 10 Myr, Mass > 10^6 M_sun, excluding 3 faint F2100W old interlopers), 2 historical radio continuum clusters (M4, M6), and 16 new JWST embedded clusters selected via PAH filter brightness, F200W brightness, and F200W Concentration Index.  
  > "we start by identifying all optically selected clusters that have estimated ages younger than 10 Myr and masses higher than $10^6~M_{\odot}$, from the cluster catalog and age-dating analysis based on multi-band HST optical images" (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365); "Finally, we add 16 new, partly or heavily embedded massive young clusters identified in the new JWST images. These have been selected to be brighter than 20.0 mag in at least one of the three filters which contain PAH emission (F335M, F770W and F1130W)." (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365); "We also require that the Concentration Index (CI - defined as the magnitude difference measured in 1 and 4 pixel aperture radii) in the F200W band to be larger than that of a point source: CI$_{\rm F200W} > 1.4$ mag." (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365)
   - parameter: m < 20.0 mag in F335M, F770W, or F1130W; m_F200W < 20.0 mag; CI_F200W (1 to 4 pix) > 1.4 mag; optical detection threshold < 26 mag in B, V, I  
  > "we also require the F200W magnitude to be brighter than 20.0 mag, to insure there is a bright stellar continuum source present" (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365); "We also require that the Concentration Index (CI - defined as the magnitude difference measured in 1 and 4 pixel aperture radii) in the F200W band to be larger than that of a point source: CI$_{\rm F200W} > 1.4$ mag." (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365); "$^g$ - Brighter than 26th mag in relevant band." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Validated star/cluster separation using training sets (RSG stars vs clusters), showing no overlap in CI_F200W.  
  > "Figure 4 shows that while there is a large overlap between stars and clusters in CI$_{\rm I}$, there is no overlap using the CI$_{\rm F200W}$ measurements, and the orange line is able to cleanly separate stars from clusters in all cases for our training sets." (Separation of Clusters and Stars)
3. **Estimate extinction, mass, and age for embedded clusters**: Derive empirical linear correlations between HST SED-fitted E(B-V) and (m_I - m_F360M) for extinction, and between HST log Mass and m_F200W / m_F300M for mass (using log Age < 6.7 clusters); assign log Age = 6.5 (3 Myr) to clusters lacking HST ages based on F335M PAH brightness and Draine dust models.  
  > "$A_V (\mbox{JWST) = [(m$_I$ - m$_{F360M}$)}  \times 0.810 + 0.99] \times 3.1$, which provides estimates of extinction for our partially embedded clusters." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters); "The resulting formula is $\mbox{log}~(M/M_{\odot}) = - 0.4068 \times m_{\rm F200W} + 13.83$. We again use the sample with log Age $<$ 6.7 clusters to make it similar to our sample of young massive clusters in NGC 1365. A version of this fit based on the F300M magnitude is nearly as good: $\mbox{log}~(M/M_{\odot}) = - 0.3639 \times m_{\rm F300M} + 13.25$" (Mass Estimates for Embedded Clusters); "Since the scatter in the relationship between F335M magnitudes and log Age based on HST is fairly large ($\sim$0.6 dex), we have chosen to simply assign log Age values of 6.5 (3 Myr) for the clusters without HST age estimates." (Age Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
   - parameter: Calibration sample cut: log Age < 6.7; Assigned embedded age: log Age = 6.5 (3 Myr)  
  > "Only the log Age $<$ 6.7 clusters are included to make the sample similar to our program sample of young massive clusters in NGC 1365." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters); "we have chosen to simply assign log Age values of 6.5 (3 Myr) for the clusters without HST age estimates." (Age Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
   - assumption: All young clusters in the sample have similar intrinsic colors and mass-to-light ratios; R_V = 3.1  
  > "The underlying assumption needed to estimate extinction using the I - F360M color index is that all the (young) clusters in our sample have similar intrinsic colors, hence the redder color is caused by extinction rather than due to age." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters); "This is because similar age clusters have similar mass-to-light ratios." (Mass Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
   - fitting: Linear correlation fitting (correlation coefficient r = 0.49, RMS = 0.95 mag for A_V; r = 0.90, RMS = 0.35 dex for F200W mass; r = 0.87 for F300M mass)  
  > "The RMS scatter (0.95 mag) is relatively large and the correlation coefficient (0.49) is only fair, hence our estimates of extinction will only be approximate." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters); "As shown in the top panel in Figure 5 we find a very good correlation in NGC1365 as well, with a correlation coefficient of 0.90, and an RMS scatter of just 0.35 dex." (Mass Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
   - validation: Compared empirical extinction slope (2.51) with Galactic extinction law prediction (2.11), and compared derived masses for M4, M5, M6 with literature estimates from Galliano et al. (2008, 2012).  
  > "Hence the coefficient connecting $A_v$ and I - F360M is 0.810 $\times$ 3.1 = 2.51, relatively similar to the estimate from the Galactic extinction law discussed above (i.e., 2.11)." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters); "Our estimates for clusters M4, M5, and M6 range from log Mass = 6.0 to 6.6, in fair agreement with (cite: galliano08) who estimate values “on the order of 10$^7$ M$_{\odot}$" for the three clusters." (Mass Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
4. **Calculate duration of the embedded phase and examine gas streamer kinematics**: Compute embedded duration as (# missing in optical band / 30 clusters with M >= 10^6 M_sun) * 10 Myr sample interval; visually compare HST/JWST images of Region 2 with PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) moment-1 velocity, CO cloud catalog, and velocity residual maps.  
  > "We calculate the duration of the deeply embedded phase for massive clusters in NGC 1365 as: #missing $/$ total $\times$ Age (i.e., sample interval) = 4/30 $\times$10 Myr = 1.3 $\pm$ 0.7 Myr, a very short timescale." (Duration of the Embedded Phase); "The third panel shows CO (2-1) moment-1 velocity from the PHANGS-ALMA observations (cite: leroy21), color-coded from -220 km/sec (dark blue) to +220 km/sec (dark red and white)." (Triggered Star Formation); "The bottom panel of Figure 9 shows the CO velocity residuals based on a model of the inner rotating disk (Schinnerer et al. 2022 - this volume)." (Triggered Star Formation)
   - parameter: Total massive cluster count = 30; Sample age interval = 10 Myr  
  > "We calculate the duration of the deeply embedded phase for massive clusters in NGC 1365 as: #missing $/$ total $\times$ Age (i.e., sample interval) = 4/30 $\times$10 Myr = 1.3 $\pm$ 0.7 Myr" (Duration of the Embedded Phase)
   - assumption: 30 clusters in the sample have masses of at least 10^6 M_sun and ages < 10 Myr  
  > "Going forward, for various estimates we assume that 30 clusters from our sample have masses of at least log Mass $\mathrel{>\kern-1.0em\lower 0.9ex\hbox{$\sim$}}$ 6.0." (Mass Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared embedded phase duration with literature values (1 - 5 Myr) for lower-mass clusters in other galaxies.  
  > "Our estimates for how long very massive clusters in NGC 1365 remain embedded in their natal ISM are consistent with those found by previous works, without being obviously longer or shorter relative to estimates made for lower mass clusters in other galaxies." (Duration of the Embedded Phase)

## Qualitative findings

- NGC 1365 has the richest population of massive (> 10^6 M_sun), young (< 10 Myr) star clusters of any known galaxy within 30 Mpc.  
  > "We find that this galaxy has the richest population of massive young clusters of any known galaxy within 30 Mpc, with $\sim$ 30 star clusters that are more massive than $10^{6}~M_{\odot}$ and younger than 10 Myr." (Abstract)
- The JWST NIRCAM F200W filter concentration index cleanly separates barely resolved compact star clusters from individual stars with no overlap in the training sets, outperforming HST I-band.  
  > "Figure 4 shows that while there is a large overlap between stars and clusters in CI$_{\rm I}$, there is no overlap using the CI$_{\rm F200W}$ measurements, and the orange line is able to cleanly separate stars from clusters in all cases for our training sets." (Separation of Clusters and Stars)
- PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) kinematics show coherent gas velocities (~0 km/s) along dark filamentary streamers and intermediate velocities (~ -50 to -100 km/s) where they intersect the bar (-100 to -200 km/s), supporting cluster formation triggered by streamer-bar collisions.  
  > "Gas velocities along the bar (the upper blue arrows) are more mixed, with values $\approx -100$ to $-200~{\rm km~s}^{-1}$ along most of the bar, but with velocities $\approx -50~{\rm km~s}^{-1}$ in regions where the streamers intersect the bar." (Triggered Star Formation)
- JWST reveals previously unknown dynamical structures such as the NE Bridge and overshoot regions, created by stars and gas overshooting the inner star-forming ring along bar dust lanes.  
  > "We believe the NE bridge feature is a clear example of material that has overshot the inner ring after falling towards the center along the southern bar dust lane (bottom-right of Fig 1)." (Clues to Orbital Dynamics in NGC 1365 - the NE Bridge and other Overshoot Regions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 1365 has one of the highest star formation rates within 20 Mpc and is known to form very massive young clusters; JWST infrared imaging penetrates the heavy dust lanes that obscure clusters in HST optical images.  
  > "The dusty, barred spiral galaxy NGC 1365, at a distance of 19.57 $\pm$ 0.78 Mpc ((cite: gagandeep21)), has one of the highest rates of star formation (16.9 M$_{\odot}~\mbox{yr}^{-1}$) of any galaxy within 20 Mpc" (Introduction and Motivation); "The main goal of this paper is to use new infrared imaging of NGC 1365, taken with the recently launched JWST, plus our optical HST images to collect a complete census of these very young massive clusters" (Introduction and Motivation)
- Why this method: Because aperture corrections and photometric calibrations were still preliminary and multi-band HST+JWST SED fitting was deferred to future work, empirical correlations with HST-derived properties and a fixed 3 Myr age for PAH-bright sources were used.  
  > "For this reason, the JWST magnitudes are used in a relative rather than absolute sense throughout this paper." (Observations and Reductions); "Since the scatter in the relationship between F335M magnitudes and log Age based on HST is fairly large ($\sim$0.6 dex), we have chosen to simply assign log Age values of 6.5 (3 Myr) for the clusters without HST age estimates." (Age Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
- Alternative: Using only JWST observations (without HST I-band) to determine the empirical extinction correlation was rejected because the smaller wavelength baseline resulted in considerably more scatter.  
  > "In principle it would be advantageous to use only JWST observations to determine the correlation, since four of our clusters do not have I band photometry. However, we found that the smaller baseline resulted in considerably more scatter, and hence was not practical." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
- Alternative: Selecting clusters using a single JWST filter (such as F335M, F1000W, or F2100W as done in other PHANGS works) was noted as an alternative to the multi-band PAH + F200W selection used here.  
  > "Different cluster selection criteria are possible, and in fact several other works within the PHANGS consortium have selected clusters using a single filter: the F335M filter (cite: rodriguez22), F1000W (Schinnerer et al. 2022, this volume), and the F2100W filter (cite: hassani22)." (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365)
- Alternative: Using the HST V filter instead of I or F200W for Concentration Index size measurements gave intermediate scatter, but could not penetrate dust as well as F200W.  
  > "If we use the V filter from HST instead the results are intermediate between F200W and I, but we are not able to penetrate the dust as well." (Separation of Clusters and Stars)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): JWST aperture photometry does not include aperture corrections, image alignment, geometric solutions, photometric zeropoints, and background subtraction are preliminary, so the data and cluster catalog are preliminary.  
  > "In the future, aperture corrections will be determined for both stars and clusters. A variety of other observational parameters will also improve with time, including image alignment and geometric solutions, photometric zeropoints, and background subtraction, as discussed in (cite: lee22b)." (Observations and Reductions); "For these reasons the data and massive cluster catalog we compile here should be considered preliminary." (Observations and Reductions)
- Limitation (authors): Some of the brightest embedded clusters (M4, M5, M6) are saturated in the JWST F770W and F2100W images.  
  > "Three different JWST$+$HST filter combinations (as indicated along the top right corner of each panel) reveal embedded young clusters, some of which are very bright and saturated (e.g., M4, M5, M6 in the F2100W and F770W images)" (A Compilation of Massive Young Clusters in NGC 1365)
- Limitation (authors): Age, mass, and extinction estimates for embedded clusters are approximate because they rely on empirical correlations with significant scatter (0.95 mag in A_V, 0.35 dex in log Mass, 0.6 dex in log Age) rather than full HST+JWST SED fitting.  
  > "The age, mass, and extinction estimates in this paper are approximate and preliminary, since they are based on correlations between optical estimates and JWST observations for the more embedded clusters." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Four deeply embedded clusters lack HST I-band detections, so their extinction A_V cannot be estimated from the (m_I - m_F360M) formula.  
  > "In principle it would be advantageous to use only JWST observations to determine the correlation, since four of our clusters do not have I band photometry." (Extinction Estimates for Embedded Clusters)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Empirical relations for mass and extinction are calibrated on optically detected, less-obscured clusters (log Age < 6.7) and extrapolated to deeply embedded sources (up to A_V ~ 19 mag) where F200W and F300M fluxes themselves suffer significant dust attenuation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assigning a constant age of 3 Myr (log Age = 6.5) to all JWST-selected clusters lacking HST ages ignores age variations within the 0-10 Myr window and directly impacts the inferred mass-to-light ratio.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA CO(2-1) kinematic comparison in Region 2 is purely visual/qualitative without quantitative modeling of shock velocities or cloud-cloud collision timescales.
