## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and match resolution of CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) TP cubes**: Calibrate to antenna temperature scale via chopper-wheel method, remove atmospheric O3 residuals, subtract linear baselines, grid onto HPBW/9 pixels with prolate spheroidal function, compute pairwise relative flux scales across EBs by inverting the design matrix and coadd, smooth CO(2-1) to 56.6" with Gaussian kernel, and bin both cubes to 10 km s-1.  
  > "The data were calibrated into the antenna temperature ( T A - ) scale with the chopper-wheel method. The residual features caused by the atmospheric O 3 lines were removed by applying the method of T. Sawada et al. (2021). Linear spectral baselines were subtracted." (2.2. Data Reduction); "We calculated the relative flux scales among the cubes, by calculating the flux ratios and errors of all of their pairs, and solved for the scales by inverting the design matrix." (2.2. Data Reduction); "The CO 21 image was then smoothed to the CO 10 beam size 56.6  using a Gaussian kernel in the CASA task imsmooth. For the purpose of this paper, the velocity was binned to 10km s [−][1] for both lines." (2.2. Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, imsmooth
   - parameter: Grid size HPBW/9 (5.62" for CO 10, 2.81" for CO 21), prolate spheroidal support radius 6 pixels  
  > "The calibrated data were mapped onto a regular grid of HPBW/9 ( » 5.62  for CO 10, » 2.81  for CO 21 ) using the prolate spheroidal function with a support radius of 6 pixels (F. R. Schwab 1980, 1984) following the standard imaging procedure for TP Array." (2.2. Data Reduction)
   - parameter: Target beam 56.6", velocity bin 10 km s-1  
  > "The CO 21 image was then smoothed to the CO 10 beam size 56.6  using a Gaussian kernel in the CASA task imsmooth. For the purpose of this paper, the velocity was binned to 10km s [−][1] for both lines." (2.2. Data Reduction)
   - fitting: Linear spectral baseline subtraction; design matrix inversion for relative EB flux scales  
  > "Linear spectral baselines were subtracted." (2.2. Data Reduction); "We calculated the relative flux scales among the cubes, by calculating the flux ratios and errors of all of their pairs, and solved for the scales by inverting the design matrix." (2.2. Data Reduction)
   - validation: Standard deviation of normalized relative scaling factors across EBs reported in Table 2 (1.0% to 5.9%)  
  > "The standard deviation of the normalized coefficients is given in Table 2." (2.2. Data Reduction)
2. **Create moment maps, spatial r21 maps, and measure luminosity-weighted r21**: Integrate channels with CO detection to make moment 0 maps; create moment 1 maps where moment 0 S/N > 5; measure total flux of both lines within the whole FOV of the CO 21 moment 0 map to compute luminosity-weighted r21; divide moment 0 maps where both lines have S/N > 5 to make spatial r21 maps.  
  > "The integrated intensity (moment 0) map of the CO 10 line is shown in panel b) of Figure 1, created using only channels where CO was detected." (2.2. Data Reduction); "We first estimate the ratio r 21 derived as a ratio between the total flux of CO within the images. The total CO flux for both lines was measured within the whole field of view of the integrated intensity map of CO 21 ." (3.1. Luminosity-weighted CO Ratio); "The map of the CO line ratio r 21 derived from the integrated intensity maps of the two CO lines is shown in panel (d) of Figure 1. Note that r 21 is shown only for pixels where both CO lines are above an S/N of 5." (3.2. Ratio Map)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: S/N > 5 threshold for moment 1 and spatial r21 map display  
  > "The intensity weighted velocity (moment 1) map is shown in panel (c), which was created using regions detected at over 5σ in the integrated intensity map." (2.2. Data Reduction); "Note that r 21 is shown only for pixels where both CO lines are above an S/N of 5." (3.2. Ratio Map)
   - assumption: Absolute flux calibration uncertainty of 2.4% for both CO lines, giving 3% uncertainty in quadrature on luminosity-weighted r21  
  > "Errors are assumed to be 3%, assuming an absolute flux calibration uncertainty of 2.4% for both CO lines and adding them in quadrature." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared luminosity-weighted mean r21 = 0.61 +/- 0.06 with literature values  
  > "The average value of luminosity-weighted mean is r 21 = 0.61 with a standard deviation of 0.06, consistent with previous studies (Y. Yajima et al. 2021; J. S. den Brok et al. 2021; A. K. Leroy et al. 2022)." (3.1. Luminosity-weighted CO Ratio)
3. **Generate major-axis position-velocity (PV) diagrams of r21**: Extract PV diagrams along the major axis for CO 10 and CO 21 using CASA impv with a 1' slit width, exclude pixels within 28" of map edges, measure CO 10 PV rms in emission-free regions, and divide the CO 21 PV diagram by the CO 10 PV diagram for regions above 20 sigma (10 sigma for NGC 1512 and NGC 4579).  
  > "The PV diagrams for both CO 10 and CO 21 data are created using CASA task impv, using the position angles presented in Table 1. The widths of the slits are set to 1 ¢, comparable to the beam size of the TP array data." (3.3. PV Diagram); "Pixels within half a beam (28″) from the edges of the maps are shown but were excluded from further analysis." (3.3. PV Diagram); "The CO 21 PV diagram was then divided by the CO 10 PV diagram for regions over 20σ to obtain the distribution of r 21 . For NGC 1512 and NGC 4579, a threshold of 10σ was used because the noise levels for these two galaxies were relatively high." (3.3. PV Diagram)
   - software: CASA, impv
   - parameter: Slit width = 1', edge exclusion = 28", threshold = 20 sigma (10 sigma for NGC 1512 and NGC 4579)  
  > "The widths of the slits are set to 1 ¢, comparable to the beam size of the TP array data." (3.3. PV Diagram); "Pixels within half a beam (28″) from the edges of the maps are shown but were excluded from further analysis." (3.3. PV Diagram); "The CO 21 PV diagram was then divided by the CO 10 PV diagram for regions over 20σ to obtain the distribution of r 21 . For NGC 1512 and NGC 4579, a threshold of 10σ was used because the noise levels for these two galaxies were relatively high." (3.3. PV Diagram)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Average r21 in PV diagram compared with luminosity-weighted r21 over the whole map and found consistent within standard deviation  
  > "The two estimates therefore do not necessarily have to match, but they are consistent and well within the standard deviation." (3.3. PV Diagram)
4. **Separate circularly rotating disk from kinematic outliers via tilted-ring modeling**: Fit the CO 10 cube with a tilted-ring model in 3DBarolo using concentric 28.8" rings, create a major-axis PV diagram from the model cube, and define regions inside the 30 sigma model contour (10 sigma for NGC 1512 and NGC 4579) as 'disk' and regions on/outside as 'outlier'.  
  > "In order to distinguish between the edges of the PV diagrams and the normal galactic rotation in a physically motivated manner, we use 3DBarolo (E. M. Di Teodoro & F. Fraternali 2015) to fit the data cube with a tilted-ring model (D. H. Rogstad et al. 1974)." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk); "The CO 10 data cube was modeled using the position angle and inclination given in Table 1 as initial parameters, with concentric rings 28.8  in width." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk); "A contour in the model PV diagram was drawn at 30σ (i.e., 10σ above the threshold level used in calculating the observed ratio), inside which the observed data were assigned to be the “disk” component." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: Ring width = 28.8", radial gas inflow/outflow fixed to 0, PA, inclination, and rotation velocity free in each ring  
  > "The CO 10 data cube was modeled using the position angle and inclination given in Table 1 as initial parameters, with concentric rings 28.8  in width. The position angle, inclination, and rotation velocity were allowed to change in each ring. The radial gas inflow/outflow was fixed to 0." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk)

## Qualitative findings

- Kinematic outliers deviating from pure circular rotation systematically exhibit higher r21 than the rotating disk component across all sample galaxies and morphological types.  
  > "We find that r 21 is systematically higher in outliers compared to the disk." (abstract); "For all galaxies, r 21 is larger for the outlier components compared to the disk." (4.2. Number Distribution of r 21)
- Average r21 values do not differ significantly between SA, SAB, and SB galaxy morphologies.  
  > "We compare r 21 between SA, SAB, and SB galaxies, and find no significant difference in the average r 21 depending on the presence of galactic bars." (abstract); "The mean and standard deviation of r 21 values in Table 3 indicate that there are no significant differences between galaxies with and without bars." (4.2. Number Distribution of r 21)
- The radial gradient of r21 in the disk is bimodal: SA galaxies (plus NGC 3627 and NGC 4321) have a constant or shallow gradient out to 0.4 R25, whereas SB galaxies (plus NGC 4536 and NGC 4579) decrease by ~20% before 0.4 R25 (corresponding to the stellar bar radius) and flatten beyond.  
  > "We find, however, that the radial gradient in r 21 is bimodal, where a group containing all SA galaxies prefer constant or very shallow r 21 gradients out to 40% of the optical radius, while another group containing all SB galaxies have a steep r 21 gradient, decreasing by ∼20% before 40% of the optical radius, which also corresponds to the radius of the stellar bar." (abstract); "All SA galaxies and two in the SAB classification (i.e., NGC 3627 and NGC 4321) maintain a constant or very shallow gradient in r 21 out to 0.4R 25, decreasing only by ∼ 5%. On the other hand, all SB galaxies and the remaining SABs (i.e., NGC 4536 and NGC 4579) quickly decrease in r 21 by 15% already before 0.3R 25, reaching 20% before 0.4R 25 ." (4.3. Radial Gradient)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA TP array observations of both CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) provide zero-spacing total fluxes free from interferometric imaging uncertainties and superior flux calibration (~2.4%-5%) compared to conventional single-dish telescopes (20%-30%).  
  > "The calibration precision offered by the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) largely surpasses most conventional radio telescopes." (1. Introduction); "These single dish observations provide zero-spacing information, so are free from uncertainties that can potentially be introduced in the imaging process of interferometer data." (2. ALMA-FACTS)
- Why this method: Because the ~3 kpc TP spatial resolution smears out spatial structures, high-S/N major-axis PV diagrams and 3DBarolo tilted-ring models are used to spectrally resolve r21 variations and separate noncircular/nuclear kinematic outliers from the rotating disk.  
  > "We exploit the high S/N afforded by ALMA to spectrally resolve the variation in r 21, and investigate its relation to kinematic signatures." (3.3. PV Diagram); "In order to distinguish between the edges of the PV diagrams and the normal galactic rotation in a physically motivated manner, we use 3DBarolo (E. M. Di Teodoro & F. Fraternali 2015) to fit the data cube with a tilted-ring model (D. H. Rogstad et al. 1974)." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk)
- Alternative: Modeling galaxy kinematics using CO 21 instead of CO 10 in 3DBarolo, which yielded nearly identical PV diagrams and consistent results, though CO 10 was preferred for its lower critical density and wider mapped area.  
  > "We used CO 10 to model the galaxy instead of CO 21 because we can expect its lower critical density to allow us to trace the bulk of gas and also because the area mapped in CO 10 is wider compared to CO 21, thereby giving a better representation of the whole galaxy." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk); "The PV diagrams of both CO lines were nearly identical, and using CO 21 to model the galaxy parameters produced consistent results in the analyses, even though we use the S/N ratio for the distinction of disks and outliers." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk)
- How they decided it was enough: A 20 sigma cut (10 sigma for NGC 1512 and NGC 4579) on CO 10 PV diagrams ensures S/N > 10 over most of CO 21 to detect r21 variations <= 0.05; disk/outlier boundary tested by varying model contour threshold by +/- 5 sigma.  
  > "Considering that the typical range of r 21 is 0.4 to 0.8 within and between galaxies, our high noise level cuts enable us to detect small variations in r 21 of  0.05." (3.3. PV Diagram); "In Figure 2 we also show the contour level 5σ above and below the threshold explained above to illustrate how our choice of the sigma threshold affects the results." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk)
- Limitation (authors): NGC 628 had to be excluded from the PV analysis due to its very small velocity width (~70 km/s).  
  > "We exclude NGC 628 from this paper because the very small velocity width (∼70km/s) of this galaxy severely limited our analyses using position–velocity (PV) diagrams in Section 3.3." (2.2. Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): The coarse spatial resolution of the TP array (56.6" or ~3.1 kpc) smears out smaller-scale variations (such as star-forming regions, bars, and bar ends) and prevents identifying the exact physical mechanism causing the high-r21 PV outliers.  
  > "It should be noted that the variations we find in this study using the TP array are the minimal variations within the galaxy; larger variations at smaller spatial scales (e.g., with individual star-forming regions) are smeared out." (3.2. Ratio Map); "The spatial resolution of our TP array does not allow for detailed investigation on what process is responsible for the elevated r 21 at the edges of our PV diagram, and these will be treated in forthcoming papers using high spatial resolution data sets." (3.5. Physical Origin of High r 21 at Edges of the PV Diagram); "This is likely because we lack the angular resolution to allow us to distinguish the rapid change in r 21 expected from the center, bar, and bar ends." (4.3. Radial Gradient)
- Limitation (authors): The S/N contour thresholds used to separate disk and outlier components in the PV diagram are somewhat arbitrary, and for NGC 3521 and NGC 4579 the outlier separation is sensitive to the threshold choice.  
  > "The S/N cuts used above are somewhat arbitrary." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk); "For NGC 4579, changing the threshold to a lower value (5σ instead of 10σ) results in the whole PV diagram being defined as a disk; the increase in r 21 at the edges of the PV diagram is not prominent in this galaxy, and should be interpreted with care." (4.1. Modeling the Galactic Disk)
- Limitation (authors): Adjacent radial bins (5.6" width) are not completely independent because they are 1/10 of the 56.6" beam size.  
  > "The width of the radial bins is 5.6 , corresponding to the pixel size, i.e., 1/10 of the beam. Thus, the adjacent radial bins are not completely independent." (4.3. Radial Gradient)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Differential beam dilution within the 56.6" (~3.1 kpc) TP beam can artificially produce or alter radial gradients and PV-edge ratios if the intrinsic spatial/velocity distributions of CO(2-1) and CO(1-0) differ on sub-kpc scales (e.g., compact nuclear rings vs. extended disks).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The PV analysis is restricted to a 1'-wide slit along the kinematic major axis; for galaxies where the stellar bar is misaligned with the major axis, the major-axis radial profile does not uniformly sample the bar.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reclassifying NGC 3521 from SABbc to SA reduces the nonbarred SA sample to 4 galaxies and SB to 3 galaxies, making the morphological bimodality rest on a small sample size (11 galaxies total).
