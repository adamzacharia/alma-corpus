## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct spectral cubes**: Concatenate calibrated uv observations, reweight with statwt and image with tclean.  
  > "The *uv* observations were concatenated into a single file to re-calibrate the uv weights using CASA’s `statwt` (cite: casateamCASACommonAstronomy2022)." (ALMACAL$-22$ and sample selection); "Imaging is performed with CASA’s `tclean`, using linear interpolation, natural weighting, and 0.5$''$ uv tapering." (ALMACAL$-22$ and sample selection)
   - software: CASA, statwt, tclean
   - parameter: 31.2 MHz channels; natural weighting; 0.5 arcsec uv taper.  
  > "The channel width is 31.2 MHz, a value that balances spectral resolution, SNR and data volume." (ALMACAL$-22$ and sample selection); "Imaging is performed with CASA’s `tclean`, using linear interpolation, natural weighting, and 0.5$''$ uv tapering." (ALMACAL$-22$ and sample selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare PSF shape with a 2D Gaussian fit; minimal deviations.  
  > "By comparing the PSF shape to a 2D Gaussian fit, we found minimal deviations." (ALMACAL$-22$ and sample selection)
2. **Find and inspect emission-line candidates**: SoFiA2 smooth and clip; friends-of-friends linking; visual inspection of spectra and moment maps.  
  > "We chose the smooth and clip (S+C) algorithm to identify sources, while smoothing to suppress noise and outliers." (Source searching algorithm); "Then, the ‘linker’ combines detected pixels in the binary mask into coherent detections using a friends-of-friends algorithm." (Source searching algorithm); "Three co-authors (VB, CP, MZ) visually inspected the spectra and moment maps of the candidates to ensure that they were not confused with noise peaks." (Source searching algorithm)
   - software: SoFiA2
   - parameter: Flag channels >5σ from median noise; MAD threshold 3σ.  
  > "We flag spectral channels where the noise deviates from the median by more than $5\sigma$." (Source searching algorithm); "In SoFiA2, we apply spatial and spectral smoothing based on the median absolute deviation (MAD) with a $3\sigma$ threshold." (Source searching algorithm)
   - parameter: Local-noise window: 25 pixels and 15 channels; merging radius three.  
  > "SoFiA2 measures and adjusts for the local noise level using a running window of 25 pixels and 15 channels in the spatial and spectral domain." (Source searching algorithm); "It links all pixels within a merging radius of three, treating sources as three-dimensional collections of pixels." (Source searching algorithm)
   - parameter: S/N >4; clear moment-map peak; exclude duplicates.  
  > "We select candidates with S/N $> 4$ and a clear peak in the moment map compared to the background noise." (Final catalogue); "Duplicate detections – e.g. those offset by only a few pixels but originating from the same region – were excluded." (Final catalogue)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Three-author inspection of spectra and moment maps.  
  > "Three co-authors (VB, CP, MZ) visually inspected the spectra and moment maps of the candidates to ensure that they were not confused with noise peaks." (Source searching algorithm)
3. **Estimate completeness and reject unusable cubes**: Inject mock sources; rerun identical source finder; match detected and injected positions.  
  > "We add artificial sources to each data cube and run the source finder with the same parameters used to look for real emissions to quantify the recovery fraction." (Completeness); "We estimate the percentage of recovered sources by comparing the position of detected sources with the injected mock sources." (Completeness)
   - software: SoFiA2
   - parameter: S/N 2–18; widths 100–1600 km s−1; 20 injections per cube, ten repetitions.  
  > "The S/N values of the synthetic sources range from 2 to 18, and the line widths vary between 100 and 1600 km s$^{-1}$." (Completeness); "In each cube, 20 mock sources are randomly injected with varying properties." (Completeness); "The process is repeated ten times per cube to build up statistically significant samples" (Completeness)
   - parameter: Retain non-zero completeness cubes; exclude zero-completeness cubes.  
  > "However, cubes with low but non-zero completeness are kept" (Completeness); "Ultimately, we kept 1107 cubes, removing 401 cubes with zero completeness." (Completeness)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Recovery fraction versus SNR and line width; investigate failures in narrow or structured-noise cubes.  
  > "We analyse the detection rate, or completeness factor ($c_i$), based on the SNR and line width of the mock sources across two categories: high-completeness and low-completeness cubes." (Completeness); "Further investigation revealed that these cubes were either extremely narrow (significantly less than 4 GHz) or exhibited structured noise." (Completeness)
4. **Estimate line fidelity**: Search inverted cubes identically; F(S/N,σ)=1−Nnegative/Npositive.  
  > "We invert the cubes and run the source finder (SoFiA2) with the same parameters, again searching for emission lines (which are, of course, absorption features in the physical cube)." (Fidelity); "F(\text{S/N}, \sigma) = 1 - \frac{N_{\text{negative}}(\text{S/N}, \sigma)}{N_{\text{positive}}(\text{S/N}, \sigma)}" (Fidelity)
   - software: SoFiA2
   - assumption: Absorption features not expected; inverted-cube emission represents random noise.  
  > "As we do not expect absorption features, any emission lines in the inverted cubes must represent random noise peaks." (Fidelity)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO LF normalisation decreases with redshift; characteristic luminosity increases to z∼2.  
  > "The normalisation factor, $\Phi^{*}_{\text{CO}}$, decreases with redshift." (Conclusions); "The characteristic luminosity, defined as the knee of the luminosity function, shows an increasing trend from the local Universe to $z\sim2$" (Conclusions)
- Molecular gas density higher than previous observations at low redshift and lower at z≥1.  
  > "The molecular gas mass density reported by ALMACAL$-22$ shows higher values than previous observations in the lower redshift bins ($z\lesssim1$)." (Conclusions); "It presents a slight tension at higher redshift ($z\geq1$), where ALMACAL$-22$ finds lower values" (Conclusions)
- Lowest-J and probabilistic redshift approaches agree, with larger probabilistic uncertainties.  
  > "These estimates match the previous results using the redshift probability calculator built from the semi-analytical model, `SHARK-2`" (Lowest$-J$ possible); "The main difference is the uncertainty, which is larger for the estimates derived using a probabilistic approach." (Lowest$-J$ possible)
- Many independent calibrator fields reduce cosmic variance.  
  > "The main reason that ALMACAL$-22$ has such low values of cosmic variance is that the survey comprises many independent calibrator fields instead of one contiguous area." (Cosmic variance)
- Depletion times approximately constant above z∼2 and increasing toward z=0.  
  > "At redshifts above $z\sim 2$, we find a relatively constant depletion timescale" (Census of the baryon cycle); "The depletion timescale increases from $z\sim 2$ to $z=0$" (Census of the baryon cycle)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use many ALMA calibrator fields to alleviate cosmic variance.  
  > "This paper aims to alleviate the limitation introduced by cosmic variance when estimating the molecular gas mass density by exploiting ALMA calibrator data" (Introduction)
- Why this method: SoFiA2 handles numerous cubes with varying noise; probabilistic transition assignments retain redshift ambiguity.  
  > "Detecting emission lines in more than $10^3$ cubes with significant noise variations is challenging." (Line search); "To address this, we use the source-finding algorithm SoFiA2 (cite: westmeierSOFIAAutomatedParallel2021), to identify potential candidates." (Line search); "This method allows us to account for the uncertainty in identifying the exact $J$ transition." (Probabilistic approach)
- Alternative: Threshold finder considered; smooth and clip selected.  
  > "offering two different algorithms: a simple threshold finder and the smooth and clip (S+C) finder." (Source searching algorithm); "We chose the smooth and clip (S+C) algorithm to identify sources" (Source searching algorithm)
- Alternative: Use all plausible transition probabilities rather than only the highest-probability transition.  
  > "Instead of simply selecting the $J-$transition with the highest probability for each detection, we adopted a more comprehensive approach." (Probabilistic approach); "We included all plausible $J$ transitions, each weighted according to the probabilities evaluated by `SHARK-2`." (Probabilistic approach)
- Alternative: Lowest-J assignments tested as an alternative.  
  > "We now explore the conservative approach of assuming the lowest$-J$ CO transition detectable for all sources." (Lowest$-J$ possible)
- Alternative: Unreliable sparse-photometry redshifts rejected in favour of the probabilistic approach.  
  > "For the remaining objects, there are fewer than five photometric bands available, resulting in broad photo$-z$ probability distributions that cannot reliably estimate the redshift or assign a redshift probability greater than $10\%$." (Existing spectroscopic and photometric data); "For these cases, we keep the redshift probability described earlier" (Existing spectroscopic and photometric data)
- Alternative: [C ii] contamination tested; maximum two interlopers do not change overall results.  
  > "We also explored the possibility that we are detecting other lines than CO, particularly the bright \[C ii\] emission line" (Possible biases and uncertainties); "even if two of them in the high-redshift bin were removed, it would not affect the overall results, as the values in that bin are lower limits." (Possible biases and uncertainties)
- How they decided it was enough: Select sensitivity consistent with theory, reject zero-completeness cubes, and fit only above luminosity limits.  
  > "Only observations with noise levels consistent with theoretical sensitivity were selected." (ALMACAL$-22$ and sample selection); "Ultimately, we kept 1107 cubes, removing 401 cubes with zero completeness." (Completeness); "We only include bins where the median luminosity exceeds the calculated luminosity limit" (Schechter fits)
- Limitation (authors): Single-line detections leave most redshifts probabilistic.  
  > "Ultimately, we did not identify any potential transitions in these fields, so we are reliant on detections of one emission line." (Redshift estimation); "We emphasise the need for additional telescope time to confirm the redshift of our sources" (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Poisson errors and high-J transition misclassification affect sparse bright-end bins.  
  > "One significant factor is the Poisson error, particularly in bins with very few objects ($\lesssim 5$)" (Possible biases and uncertainties); "the redshift uncertainties, particularly in converting high$-J$ CO transitions into CO(1–0), could lead to the misclassification of transitions." (Possible biases and uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Uneven sensitivity and incomplete understanding of completeness/reliability uncertainties.  
  > "Another uncertainty stems from the uneven sensitivity of the data cubes." (Possible biases and uncertainties); "we still allow for the possibility that some uncertainties are not fully understood." (Possible biases and uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Faint-end data insufficient to fit slope; fixed slope may be inappropriate at low redshift.  
  > "due to the limited number of data points at the faint end, we adopted a fixed slope at $\alpha = -0.2$" (Schechter fits); "the assumption of a fixed faint slope at $\alpha =-0.2$ may not be appropriate" (Comparison with other observations)
- Limitation (authors): Highest-redshift Schechter fit weakly constrained; molecular gas density is a lower limit.  
  > "The highest redshift panel contains only one data point above the luminosity limit with significant statistical weight." (Schechter fits); "none of the individual measurements is completely above the luminosity limit, so we provide a lower limit." (Comparison with other observations)
- Limitation (authors): Broad profiles may be unresolved blends.  
  > "there are a few sources with a FWHM $\gtrsim 1000$ km/s, such as J0334-4008.4, most likely explained by blended sources for which the achieved resolution is insufficient to resolve them." (Final catalogue)
- Limitation (authors): Driver cosmic variance prescription does not account for luminosity-moment variance.  
  > "it does not take into account the variance in luminosity moments since they use galaxies in a restricted range of magnitude at $z\sim 0$." (Cosmic variance)
- Limitation (authors): Lowest-J method may overestimate molecular gas content.  
  > "As a result, we may be over-estimating the molecular gas content through this approach." (Lowest$-J$ possible)
- Limitation (authors): Constant αCO changes all inferred masses and densities linearly.  
  > "A different constant value affects our results linearly." (Molecular gas mass density)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Agreement with SHARK-2 predictions is not a fully independent test because SHARK-2 also supplies most transition probabilities.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Resolve the stated pruning RMS <0.01 mJy/channel versus mean cube sensitivity ∼0.78 mJy/channel before implementing selection.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distinguish 401 initial fields, 299 independent survey sightlines and 46 fields containing detections.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several appendix entries have S/N below the stated >4 catalogue cut; source identifiers also repeat.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The molecular-density integration phrase 'up to the luminosity limit' needs explicit numerical bounds and direction.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 2 luminosity units conflict with the positive pc² power used in the luminosity equation; retain printed values and seek clarification.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The supplied CO(6–5) rest frequency and excitation-factor convention require verification within the source materials before encoding an answer key.
