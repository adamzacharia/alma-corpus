## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract and fit 1D [CII] spectra**: Extract spectrum in circular aperture centred on the quasar; aperture radius set where flux in concentric apertures (0.1'' to 3.0'') plateaus; fit flat continuum plus Gaussian line  
  > "We determine the best aperture by extracting the flux of the quasars in concentric apertures from 0.1$''$ up to 3.0$''$, and choosing the radius at which the flux encounters a plateau." (3.1 [CII] line measurements); "Once the best aperture is found, we extract the one dimensional spectra, and fit them with a flat continuum and a Gaussian line." (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - parameter: Aperture radii listed per source in the 1D table (1.3-2.3'')  
  > "The spectra were extracted from different apertures" (Table (1D [CII] fit) notes)
   - assumption: Sources may be extended, so a source-centred aperture is used  
  > "In order to properly account for potentially extended sources, we extract our one dimensional spectrum for each source in an aperture centered on the quasar position" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - fitting: Flat continuum plus single Gaussian line; routine, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "we extract the one dimensional spectra, and fit them with a flat continuum and a Gaussian line." (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Build continuum-subtracted [CII] cubes and moment-0 maps; measure line flux with 2D Gaussian**: Choose line window at Gaussian-fit peak +/-1.4 sigma, uvcontsub in uv space, re-image with tclean (robust=2), moment-0 maps, 2D Gaussian fit in CASA, correct flux by ~1.20  
  > "The continuum subtraction is done in the *uv* space using the CASA task *uvcontsub*." (3.1 [CII] line measurements); "selecting a small rectangular region around the source." (3.1 [CII] line measurements); "In the remainder of the paper, we use the" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Line window = Gaussian-fit peak +/-1.4 sigma (~83% of line flux)  
  > "this recovers $\sim 83\%$ of the total line flux and maximizes the SNR, in case of a perfectly Gaussian line profile (e.g. (cite: decarli2018), (cite: novak2020), appendix A)" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - parameter: Flux correction factor ~1.20  
  > "therefore the 2D flux need to be corrected by a factor of $\sim$1.20 (e.g. (cite: decarli2018))" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - assumption: Gaussian line profile  
  > "in case of a perfectly Gaussian line profile" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit within CASA to the moment-0 map in a small rectangular region  
  > "selecting a small rectangular region around the source." (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
   - validation: Sizes consistent with beam; PSO352-15 results consistent with Rojas et al.  
  > "our results for the quasar PSO352-15 are consistent with those presented in (cite: rojas2021)" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
3. **Measure continuum flux densities**: Make pure continuum maps for lower and upper sidebands by collapsing line-free channels and fit a 2D Gaussian in CASA; Band 3 gives no detection  
  > "We model each continuum emission from the maps with a 2D Gaussian function, using CASA." (3.3 Continuum measurements); "The measured sizes are consistent with the beam size." (3.3 Continuum measurements)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Flux at 1900 GHz rest frame used for IR quantities  
  > "to calculate the corresponding IR quantities (see Section [4](#sec:analysis)), we use the continuum emission flux obtained at 1900 GHz from the 2D Gaussian fit" (3.3 Continuum measurements)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian; uncertainties method not stated  
  > "We model each continuum emission from the maps with a 2D Gaussian function, using CASA." (3.3 Continuum measurements)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive CO(6-5) and continuum upper limits for Band 3 sources**: Extract 1D spectrum from the central pixel; continuum 2 sigma upper limit from mean rms of the spectrum; line flux 3 sigma upper limit with 300 km/s width; then luminosity and FIR/TIR limits via the same formulae  
  > "We can place upper limits to the continuum emission at 2$\sigma$ by considering the mean rms noise on the extracted 1D spectrum." (3.2 CO(6-5) line measurements); "We also estimate upper limits on the emission line flux at 3$\sigma$, and considering a typical line width of 300 km s$^{-1}$" (3.2 CO(6-5) line measurements)
   - parameter: Line width 300 km/s; 3 sigma line limit; 2 sigma continuum limit  
  > "and considering a typical line width of 300 km s$^{-1}$ (e.g. (cite: decarli2022))" (3.2 CO(6-5) line measurements)
   - assumption: Typical line width 300 km/s from literature  
  > "and considering a typical line width of 300 km s$^{-1}$ (e.g. (cite: decarli2022))" (3.2 CO(6-5) line measurements)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate redshifts from [CII] and velocity offsets of UV lines**: Redshift from [CII] peak; compare to literature Mg II, Lya and template redshifts as velocity offsets  
  > "We estimate the redshifts of the quasars in our sample from the peak of the" (4.2 Redshift estimations)
   - assumption: [CII] defines the systemic redshift  
  > "with respect to lines arising from the ISM in the host galaxy, considered the rest-frame of the system" (4.2 Redshift estimations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targeted z>5 radio-loud quasars observable from ALMA (Dec<20 deg), since almost all high-z quasars observed in sub-mm were radio-quiet  
  > "we focused on high-redshift ($z>5$) RL quasars, observable from ALMA (Declination $<20$ deg)." (2 Sample and Observations); "almost all have been radio-quiet (RQ), due to the absence of dedicated ALMA follow-up of radio-loud quasars." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Compact configuration to avoid flux losses; robust=2 to maximise SNR following similar literature  
  > "We chose a compact configuration C(43-2) to avoid flux losses, with 44 antennas used." (2 Sample and Observations); "to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), following similar works from the literature (cite: decarli2018)" (2 Sample and Observations)
- Alternative: Two scenarios for IR-derived quantities: all continuum as dust (upper limit) vs synchrotron-subtracted (lower limit); no synchrotron-subtracted values for J0131-0321 (virtually all synchrotron) or J2228+0110 (non-detection)  
  > "we derive such quantities in two ways. We firstly consider the total ALMA recovered continuum data as purely produced by dust, which will provide upper limits to IR luminosities, SFR and dust/gas masses." (4.3 Radio and (sub-)mm SED)
- Alternative: Full kinematic modelling not done because [CII] and continuum are unresolved  
  > "the quality of our data prevents us from performing a full modeling of the kinematics of our sources" (4.6 Mass Budget)
- How they decided it was enough: Aperture radius chosen where concentric-aperture flux plateaus; companion candidates require fidelity>0.9 and SNR>4  
  > "choosing the radius at which the flux encounters a plateau." (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
- Limitation (authors): Low SNR and limited spatial resolution prevent firm conclusions on kinematics  
  > "due to the low SNR and the limited spatial resolution of the maps, we are not able to draw stronger conclusions on their kinematics" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
- Limitation (authors): Radio data are non-simultaneous so SED shapes may be affected by variability  
  > "hence the radio intrinsic SED shape might be altered by variability effects" (4.3 Radio and (sub-)mm SED)
- Limitation (authors): PSO055-00 [CII] line lies at the edge of the bandwidth; derived quantities uncertain  
  > "The emission line for the quasar PSO055-00 is found at the edge of the bandwidth" (3.1 [CII] line measurements)
- Limitation (authors): A full SED fit of synchrotron and dust components is out of scope given limited data  
  > "Given the limited data at hands, a full spectral fit of the different components is currently out of the scope of this work." (7 Conclusions and Outlooks)
- Limitation (authors): The data are about 3 times shallower than those used to determine the blank-field number density  
  > "We note that our data is shallower, by a factor of $\sim$3, than those used to determine the blank field number density." (Footnote 7)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Internal inconsistencies: PSO135+16 redshift is 5.63 in the sample table but 5.68 in the text; 1D-fit table gives F_cont 0.064+/-0.10 mJy and PSO352-15 F_line 1.20+/-0.040 Jy km/s, which differ from the 2D-fit table values
- Caveat (inferred, ours): M_dust for PSO055-00 with synchrotron subtracted (3.8e7) exceeds the total-continuum value (2.8e7), which should not happen
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Synchrotron fractions rely on non-simultaneous radio data and extrapolated power laws without a full SED fit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed T_dust=47 K and beta=1.6 and a single alpha_CII conversion carry large systematic uncertainty for AGN hosts
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The L_CII difference rests on p=0.04 with only five new RL sources plus a handful from the literature and a heterogeneous RQ comparison sample; multiple KS tests are not corrected
- Caveat (inferred, ours): M_dyn assumes a single median inclination of 46 deg for unresolved sources and a dispersion-dominated geometry
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Companion non-detection is weakly informative because the data are shallow
