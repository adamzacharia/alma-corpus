## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure spatially integrated strong-line and direct Te-based oxygen abundances**: Extract optical emission lines ([O II], Hbeta, [O III], Halpha, auroral [O III]4363, [O II]7322,7332, [S II]6718,6732) from JWST/NIRSpec IFU cubes within a 3-sigma NIRCam continuum mask, correct for dust via Balmer decrement, apply Sanders et al. (2024) strong-line calibration, and compute direct Te-based abundances for 5 galaxies using PyNeb.  
  > "From the new JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy, we measured spatially integrated oxygen abundances (“metallicity”, defined as _Z_ _≡_ 12+log(O _/_ H)) of the 18 galaxies using strong optical emission lines with applied _T_ _e_ calibration from R. L. Sanders et al. (2024)." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement); "All emission lines are extracted from the spectral cubes in a mask defined by the region of the 3 _σ_ JWST/NIRCam rest-frame optical continuum flux ( _i.e._ tracing the stellar mass)." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement); "We derived the oxygen abundances using the _getTemDen_ module of the `PyNeb` Python package (V. Luridiana et al. 2015) from the electron temperatures ( _T_ _e_ (O [+] ) and _T_ _e_ (O [++] )) and the electron density ( _n_ _e_ ) by following these steps." (A. DETAILS ON METALLICITY CALCULATIONS)
   - software: PyNeb
   - parameter: 3-sigma JWST/NIRCam rest-frame optical continuum mask  
  > "All emission lines are extracted from the spectral cubes in a mask defined by the region of the 3 _σ_ JWST/NIRCam rest-frame optical continuum flux ( _i.e._ tracing the stellar mass)." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
   - assumption: Case B recombination for Balmer decrement dust correction  
  > "Dust correction was applied via the Balmer decrement (H _α_ /H _β_ ) assuming case B recombination." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
   - assumption: ne = 400 cm^-3 assumed for Te(O++) derivation, Te = 2 x 10^4 K assumed for ne derivation  
  > "1. We derived _T_ _e_ (O [++] ) from the [O III ] 4363 /[O III ] 5007 line ratio. For this we assumed _n_ _e_ = 400 cm _[−]_ [3]" (A. DETAILS ON METALLICITY CALCULATIONS); "2. We derived _n_ _e_ using the [S II ] 6732 /[S II ] 6718 line ratio, assuming an electron temperature of 2 _×_ 10 [4] K" (A. DETAILS ON METALLICITY CALCULATIONS)
   - fitting: Monte-Carlo samplings for metallicity uncertainties  
  > "_d_ Metal abundance uncertainties include uncertainties from calibrations and line flux measurements, derived from extensive Monte-Carlo samplings." (Table 1)
   - validation: Compared strong-line metallicities with Maiolino et al. (2008) and Hirschmann et al. (2023) calibrations and verified against direct Te-based measurements for 5 galaxies.  
  > "We use the metallicity parameterization and calibration by R. L. Sanders et al. (2024), which we found to lead to consistent metallicities as the parameterization by R. Maiolino et al. (2008) and M. Hirschmann et al. (2023)" (2.2. Metallicity Measurement); "For these galaxies, we find consistent metallicity measurement from the strong-line method an the _T_ _e_ method (see Appendix A for details)." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
2. **Fit the z = 4-6 Mass-Metallicity Relation (MZR) and evaluate FMR scatter**: Combine ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST sample with literature z = 4-6 data across log(M*/Msun) = 7.5-11.0, fit quadratic MZR functional form using scipy.optimize, and measure intrinsic scatter around the Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR).  
  > "The thick black dashed line in the middle panel of Figure 1 shows a fit using the `scipy.optimize` _Python_ package to all available data at _z_ = 4 _−_ 6 and log(M _⋆_ _/_ M _⊙_ ) = 7 _._ 5 _−_ 11 _._ 0 including the ALPINECRISTAL-JWST sample:" (3.1. The Mass-Metallicity Relation (MZR)); "For a _µ_ 0 = 0 _._ 66 (B. H. Andrews & P. Martini 2013), we measure a scatter of _σ_ FMR _∼_ 0 _._ 24 dex based on the data at _z_ = 4 _−_ 6." (4.2. Scatter of the FMR and its Universality)
   - software: scipy.optimize
   - parameter: mu_0 = 0.66 and mu_0 = 0.22 for FMR projection mu = log(M*) - mu_0 * log(SFR)  
  > "by defining _µ ≡_ log(M _⋆_ ) _−µ_ 0 _×_ log(SFR) where _µ_ 0 = 0 _._ 66 based on a fit to galaxies at _z_ = 0 (B. H. Andrews & P. Martini 2013)." (3.2. The Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR)); "We found that _µ_ 0 = 0 _._ 22 decreases the scatter to _σ_ FMR _∼_ 0 _._ 22 dex, which is negligibly smaller than for _µ_ 0 = 0 _._ 66." (4.2. Scatter of the FMR and its Universality)
   - assumption: Solar oxygen abundance 12 + log(O/H) = 8.69, Chabrier (2003) IMF, LCDM cosmology (H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, Omega_m = 0.3), metallicity measurement uncertainty sigma_meas = 0.2 dex  
  > "Throughout this work, we assume a ΛCDM cosmology with _H_ 0 = 70 km s _[−]_ [1] Mpc _[−]_ [1], Ω Λ = 0 _._ 7, and Ω m = 0 _._ 3 and magnitudes are given in the AB system (J. B. Oke 1974)." (1. INTRODUCTION); "We assume here a solar value of 12 + log(O _/_ H) = 8 _._ 69 (C. Allende Prieto et al. 2001)" (3.1. The Mass-Metallicity Relation (MZR)); "assuming a conservative uncertainty in the metallicity measurement of _σ_ meas = 0 _._ 2 dex (M. Curti et al. 2024)." (4.2. Scatter of the FMR and its Universality)
   - fitting: scipy.optimize fit to 12 + log(O/H) = B(log(M*/Msun) - log(M0))^2 + A  
  > "12+log(O _/_ H) = _B_ (log( _M_ _⋆_ _/_ M _⊙_ ) _−_ log( _M_ 0 )) [2] + _A,_ (1)" (3.1. The Mass-Metallicity Relation (MZR))
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Model metal build-up and FMR scatter with an analytical chemical evolution model and cosmological simulations**: Integrate continuity equations forward in time with AGB and Type II SNe yields, EAGLE-derived mass-loading factors, and stochastic SFHs generated with DELightcurveSimulation; compare MZR/FMR with cosmological simulations and trace progenitors/descendants in DUSTY-GAEA.  
  > "We derive the stellar mass, SFR, gas and dust mass empirically by running the model forward in time, thereby computing these parameters in every time step" (4.1. The Fast Build-up of Metals in the View of a Simple Analytical Model); "we use the `DELightcurveSimulation` Python package [10] (S. D. Connolly 2016), which is based on the D. Emmanoulopoulos et al. (2013) light-curve simulation algorithm." (4.3. Can a Bursty Star Formation Explain the Scatter of the FMR?); "We use the DUSTY-GAEA simulation model (O. Osman et al. 2025) to characterize the evolutionary path of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST galaxies on the MZR and FMR plane." (4.5. The progenitors and descendants of the ALPINE galaxies)
   - software: DELightcurveSimulation
   - parameter: t_depl = 5 x 10^8 yr, Mdot_gas,in = 100 Msun yr^-1, R = 0.46, r_Z = 0 to 0.5  
  > "where _M_ ˙ ej _,_ Z is the total metal ejection rate, _R_ = 0 _._ 46 (M. R. Krumholz & A. Dekel 2012; R. Feldmann 2015)" (4.1. The Fast Build-up of Metals in the View of a Simple Analytical Model); "assuming reasonable average values of _t_ depl = 5 _×_ 10 [8] yr (e.g.," (4.1. The Fast Build-up of Metals in the View of a Simple Analytical Model); "The width of the swaths shows a range in the metallicity of the inflowing gas from metal free ( _r_ Z = 0) to 50% ( _r_ Z = 0 _._ 5) of the metallicity of the ISM gas." (Figure 4 caption)
   - parameter: Stochastic SFH parameters: alpha_PSD = 2, sigma_MS = 0.1, 0.3, 0.6 dex, tau_break = 5, 50, 200 Myrs  
  > "We assume a generalized power spectrum density (PSD, with a slope _α_ PSD = 2; A. L. Faisst & T. Morishita 2024)." (4.3. Can a Bursty Star Formation Explain the Scatter of the FMR?); "We simulated SFHs with _σ_ MS = 0 _._ 1, 0 _._ 3, and 0 _._ 6 dex and _τ_ break = 5, 50, and 200 Myrs." (4.3. Can a Bursty Star Formation Explain the Scatter of the FMR?)
   - assumption: Z_sun ~ 0.014, AGB yields for 1-7 Msun (Dell'Agli et al. 2019), SNe II yields for 7-120 Msun (Limongi & Chieffi 2018)  
  > "The oxygen abundance metallicity is then computed as 12 + log(O _/_ H) = 8 _._ 69 + log( _ζ /_ 0 _._ 014) (C. Allende Prieto et al. 2001; R. Feldmann 2015) using the calibration _Z_ _⊙_ _∼_ 0 _._ 014 (M. Asplund et al. 2009)." (4.1. The Fast Build-up of Metals in the View of a Simple Analytical Model); "We include stars in the range of 1 _−_ 7 M _⊙_ for AGB yields and 7 _−_ 120 M _⊙_ for SNe II yields [7] and use their mass" (4.1. The Fast Build-up of Metals in the View of a Simple Analytical Model)

## Qualitative findings

- At the massive end of the MZR, metal abundance evolves on average from 40% to 60% solar between z ~ 5 and cosmic noon (z ~ 2), indicating fast early metal enrichment.  
  > "We find that the metal abundance evolves, on average, from 40% to 60% solar between _z_ _∼_ 5 and cosmic noon ( _z_ _∼_ 2) at the massive end of the MZR, suggesting already significant metal enrichment at early times." (abstract)
- The FMR at z = 5 exhibits a 5x larger intrinsic scatter (preferentially towards lower metallicities) compared to the local FMR.  
  > "The FMR at _z_ = 5 exhibits a 5 _×_ larger scatter (preferentially to lower metallicities) compared the local FMR relation." (abstract); "We find the intrinsic scatter of the FMR at _z_ _∼_ 5 to be approximately five times larger compared to the local FMR (Figure 5 and Table 3)." (5. SUMMARY & CONCLUSIONS)
- A factor of two lower outflow efficiency (mass-loading factor) than predicted by EAGLE/FIRE simulations is preferred to reproduce the observed MZR at z = 5.  
  > "However, we find that a _∼_ 2 _×_ lower outflow efficiency ( _i.e._ lower massloading factor) than found by the EAGLE and FIRE simulations is preferred to reproduce the MZR at _z_ = 5 (Figure 4)." (5. SUMMARY & CONCLUSIONS)
- ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST galaxies likely built up their metals rapidly at z = 15-10, joined the FMR around z ~ 10, and will evolve into massive (log(M*/Msun) ~ 11.4) galaxies with super-solar metallicities by z = 0.  
  > "We show that the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST galaxies likely joined the current FMR at _z_ _∼_ 10 and will evolve into massive (log(M _⋆_ _/_ M _⊙_ ) _∼_ 11 _._ 4) galaxies with super-solar metallicities by _z_ = 0." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous JWST studies at z ~ 5-7 only probed low stellar masses (log(M*/Msun) <= 9.5); the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST sample provides 18 massive (log(M*/Msun) = 9.5-11) main-sequence galaxies with joint JWST/NIRSpec IFU and ALMA [C II]/far-IR continuum coverage.  
  > "Current JWST observations referenced above have derived the MZR at stellar masses of log(M _⋆_ _/_ M _⊙_ ) ≲ 9 _._ 5 at high redshifts. The relation (and its evolution) for more massive galaxies in the early Universe are still unknown." (1. INTRODUCTION); "The galaxies are massive at log(M _⋆_ _/_ M _⊙_ ) _>_ 9 _._ 5, thus complement the samples at lower stellar masses observed by other JWST surveys." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Strong optical lines ([O II], Hbeta, [O III]) are detected in all 18 galaxies and calibrated with the high-z Te-based relation of Sanders et al. (2024), avoiding Halpha/[N II] blending and AGN contamination, and verified against direct Te measurements where auroral lines are detected.  
  > "We do not use H _α_ and [N II ] in the case of contamination by an AGN (W. Ren et al. 2025) and the blending of the two lines in the medium resolution spectra." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement); "The strong-line metallicity measurements were also compared to direct _T_ _e_ -based measurements derived from fainter auroral lines ([O III ] 4363 and [O III ] 7322 _,_ 7332 ) for a subset of five galaxies." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
- Alternative: Using Halpha and [N II] lines for metallicity (rejected due to potential AGN contamination and line blending in medium resolution spectra)  
  > "We do not use H _α_ and [N II ] in the case of contamination by an AGN (W. Ren et al. 2025) and the blending of the two lines in the medium resolution spectra." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
- Alternative: Maiolino et al. (2008) and Hirschmann et al. (2023) strong-line metallicity calibrations (compared and found consistent with Sanders et al. 2024)  
  > "We use the metallicity parameterization and calibration by R. L. Sanders et al. (2024), which we found to lead to consistent metallicities as the parameterization by R. Maiolino et al. (2008) and M. Hirschmann et al. (2023)" (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties due to dust attenuation corrections are not included in the metal abundance uncertainties.  
  > "However, uncertainties due the dust attenuation corrections are not included." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): Six galaxies are potential type 1 AGN candidates and one is a BPT AGN candidate, which could potentially affect line ratios.  
  > "As mentioned previously, six of the ALPINECRISTAL-JWST galaxies are potential type 1 AGN candidates." (2.2. Metallicity Measurement)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in Te and ne parameters are substantial due to the low signal-to-noise ratio of the faint auroral lines.  
  > "The uncertainty in these parameters and the derived metal abundances are substantial due to the low signal-to-noise ratio of the auroral lines." (A. DETAILS ON METALLICITY CALCULATIONS)
- Limitation (authors): Galaxy age measurements from SED fitting have large uncertainties (factors of two or more) and trace time since the last starburst rather than onset of star formation.  
  > "We note that the measurement of ages comes with large uncertainties (factors of two or more), which could explain some of the discrepancies between the observed and model ages." (4.1. The Fast Build-up of Metals in the View of a Simple Analytical Model)
- Limitation (authors): The Millennium simulation only sparsely populates z > 9 (only 22 snapshots saved at z > 5).  
  > "One caveat is that the Millennium simulation only sparsely populates the regime at _z_ _>_ 9." (4.5. The progenitors and descendants of the ALPINE galaxies); "Again, we note that for the Millennium simulation, only 22 snapshots were saved at redshift _>_ 5." (4.5. The progenitors and descendants of the ALPINE galaxies)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating lower-mass literature MZR trends at z > 4 to the stellar mass range of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST sample assumes a power-law slope that is poorly constrained at the high-mass end for z > 6.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Lowering the outflow efficiency by a factor of two to match the MZR may create tension with reproducing extended [C II] halos observed around the same galaxies.
