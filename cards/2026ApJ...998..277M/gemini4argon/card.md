## Science

- Fields: astrochemistry, star_formation, nearby_galaxies
- Question: Investigate the physical and chemical properties, molecular abundances, and origin of cold CH$_3$OH gas in two dense molecular cloud cores (N79S-1 and N79S-2) offset from the SSC candidate H72.97-69.39 in the low-metallicity N79 region of the LMC.  
  > "In this study, we report the detection of cold ($\leq$ 15 K) CH$_3$OH gas towards two dense cores, which are located $\sim$ 1.2 pc offset from the super star cluster (SSC) candidate (H72.97-69.39) in the N79 region of the LMC." (Introduction); "The possible origins of cold CH$_3$OH gas in these dense cores are discussed, along with a possible explanation for the non-detection of CH$_3$OH in the SSC candidate." (Abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure dust continuum source sizes and flux densities**: Fit 2D Gaussian within the 50% peak intensity contour to measure source sizes, and extract average 0.87 mm continuum flux density within a 0.42'' (0.1 pc) diameter circular region centered on CH3OH peak positions.  
  > "We estimate the source size of the dust continuum using a two-dimensional Gaussian fitting within a region that encloses 50% of the peak intensity (see Table 2)." (Continuum Emission); "We measure the average flux density within a circular region of 0.42$''$ (0.1 pc) diameter centered at CH$_3$OH peak positions toward N79S-1 and N79S-2" (Column Density of H2)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 0.42'' (0.1 pc) diameter extraction region  
  > "We measure the average flux density within a circular region of 0.42$''$ (0.1 pc) diameter centered at CH$_3$OH peak positions toward N79S-1 and N79S-2" (Column Density of H2)
   - parameter: 50% peak intensity contour for 2D Gaussian size fit  
  > "We estimate the source size of the dust continuum using a two-dimensional Gaussian fitting within a region that encloses 50% of the peak intensity (see Table 2)." (Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fitting  
  > "We estimate the source size of the dust continuum using a two-dimensional Gaussian fitting within a region that encloses 50% of the peak intensity (see Table 2)." (Continuum Emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectra, identify molecular transitions, and fit line profiles**: Extract spectra from 0.42'' (0.1 pc) diameter regions at CH3OH peaks, identify lines using CASSIS with CDMS and JPL databases (S/N >= 3, matching systemic velocity, unblended), and fit single Gaussian profiles to unblended transitions.  
  > "The spectra are extracted from the 0.42$''$ (0.1 pc) diameter region centered at CH$_3$OH peak positions toward N79S-1 and N79S-2." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions); "a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $\geq$ 3; the velocity coincides with the systemic velocity, and the line is not severely blended with another molecular line" (Spectra Toward the CH3OH Peak Positions); "The line parameters are measured by fitting a single Gaussian profile to the observed spectral profile of each unblended transition." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: 0.42'' (0.1 pc) diameter spectral extraction region  
  > "The spectra are extracted from the 0.42$''$ (0.1 pc) diameter region centered at CH$_3$OH peak positions toward N79S-1 and N79S-2." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions)
   - parameter: Detection threshold S/N >= 3  
  > "We detect any line based on the following criteria: a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $\geq$ 3; the velocity coincides with the systemic velocity, and the line is not severely blended with another molecular line." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions)
   - parameter: Fitting velocity range: 229 to 236 km s^-1 (N79S-1) and 230 to 238 km s^-1 (N79S-2)  
  > "The Gaussian fitting is performed for each transition in the velocity range between 229 and 236 km s$^{-1}$ for N79S-1 and between 230 and 238 km s$^{-1}$ for N79S-2." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian profile fit to each unblended transition  
  > "The line parameters are measured by fitting a single Gaussian profile to the observed spectral profile of each unblended transition." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions)
   - validation: Checked line blending using Einstein A_ij and upper-state energy E_u  
  > "Blended lines are identified by checking the position of the line with other lines, depending on the value of, Einstein coefficient (A$_{ij}$) and upper-state energy (E$_u$)." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions)
3. **Create moment zero maps and measure molecular emission source sizes**: Integrate spectra over the emission velocity range (typically 229 to 238 km s^-1) to make moment zero maps, and fit 2D Gaussians within the 50% peak intensity region to derive deconvolved source sizes.  
  > "The moment zero maps of each molecular line are created by integrating each spectrum in the velocity range where the emission line is seen, typically between 229 and 238 km s$^{-1}$." (Line Images); "We estimate the deconvolved source size of each molecular line using a two-dimensional Gaussian fitting within a region that encloses 50% of the peak intensity" (Line Images)
   - parameter: Velocity integration range: typically 229 to 238 km s^-1 (205 to 257 km s^-1 for CO(3-2), 205-220 km s^-1 blue lobe, 242-257 km s^-1 red lobe)  
  > "The moment zero maps of each molecular line are created by integrating each spectrum in the velocity range where the emission line is seen, typically between 229 and 238 km s$^{-1}$." (Line Images); "Figure 9 (b) shows the spatial distribution of blue-shifted emission integrated over 205-220 km s$^{-1}$ and red-shifted emission integrated over 242-257 km s$^{-1}$." (Spatial Distribution of Molecular Emission)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fitting within 50% peak intensity contour  
  > "We estimate the deconvolved source size of each molecular line using a two-dimensional Gaussian fitting within a region that encloses 50% of the peak intensity" (Line Images)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive rotational temperatures and column densities of CH3OH and SO, and column densities of other species**: Perform LTE rotation diagram analysis (straight-line fit of log(N_u/g_u) vs E_u/k) for CH3OH and SO; validate CH3OH and H2CO with non-LTE RADEX modeling; calculate LTE optically thin column densities for CS, H2CS, H2CO, CN, and CCH by averaging values at T_rot = 10 K and 20 K.  
  > "Thus, we perform the rotation diagram analysis to obtain the rotational temperatures by assuming the observed transitions are optically thin and in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density); "We have also conducted non-LTE calculations using RADEX for CH$_3$OH in two sources, N79S-1 and N79S-2, to test potential differences from an LTE approach (cite: vand07)." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density); "Column densities of other molecular species (CS, H$_2$CS, H$_2$CO, CN, and CCH) are derived by solving Eqn." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: T_rot varied from 10 K to 20 K for CS, H2CS, H2CO, CN, and CCH  
  > "However, we vary the T$_{rot}$ from 10 K to 20 K to account for the uncertainty of the column density." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
   - parameter: RADEX inputs: T_bg = 2.73 K, n(H2) = 1.8e6 cm^-3 (N79S-1) and 3.6e6 cm^-3 (N79S-2), line width = 2.0 km s^-1 (N79S-1) and 2.3 km s^-1 (N79S-2)  
  > "As input parameters, we use a background temperature of 2.73 K and H$_2$ gas densities of 1.8 $\times$ 10$^{6}$ cm$^{-3}$ for N79S-1 and 3.6 $\times$ 10$^{6}$ cm$^{-3}$ for N79S-2" (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density); "and the average line width is set to 2.0 km s$^{-1}$ for N79S-1 and 2.3 km s$^{-1}$ for N79S-2" (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin conditions  
  > "Thus, we perform the rotation diagram analysis to obtain the rotational temperatures by assuming the observed transitions are optically thin and in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
   - assumption: Gas temperature of 13 K (N79S-1) and 15 K (N79S-2) with 10-20 K bounds for single-transition species  
  > "The gas temperature at N79S-1 and N79S-2 is assumed to be 13 K and 15 K, respectively, based on the rotation diagram analysis of CH$_3$OH and SO." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
   - fitting: Straight-line fit to rotation diagram; uncertainties reported at 2 sigma level  
  > "The column density and rotational temperature are obtained by fitting a straight line." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density); "The derived temperatures, column densities, and their uncertainties (2$\sigma$ level) are summarized in Table" (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
   - validation: Tested LTE assumptions against non-LTE RADEX calculations for CH3OH and H2CO, excluding optically thick CH3OH 1_1-0_0 A+ transition from rotation diagram  
  > "We have also conducted non-LTE calculations using RADEX for CH$_3$OH in two sources, N79S-1 and N79S-2, to test potential differences from an LTE approach (cite: vand07)." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density); "Among these, three have optical depths of approximately 1, while the 1$_1$-0$_0$ A$^+$ line has a significantly higher optical depth of 5.3. This line was already excluded from the rotational diagram analysis in our study." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density)
5. **Derive H2 column densities, gas densities, masses, visual extinctions, and relative molecular abundances**: Compute N(H2) from optically thin 0.87 mm dust continuum emission at T_d = 10, 13/15, and 20 K (averaging 10 K and 20 K values as final N(H2)), derive n(H2), gas mass, and A_V assuming a 0.1 pc spherical geometry, and divide molecular column densities by N(H2) to obtain relative abundances.  
  > "The H$_2$ column density ($N_{H_2}$) is estimated from the optically thin dust continuum emission using the following equation:" (Column Density of H$_2$); "Assuming a source diameter of 0.1 pc and a spherical distribution of gas, the gas density and visual extinction are derived to be $n_{\mathrm{H_2}} = 1.85 \times 10^6$ cm$^{-3}$ and $A_V \sim 128$ mag for N79S-1, and $n_{\mathrm{H_2}} = 3.59 \times 10^6$ cm$^{-3}$ and $A_V \sim 196$ mag for N79S-2." ## Qualitative findings

- Two dense, cold (13-15 K), well-shielded (A_V > 100 mag) molecular cores (N79S-1 and N79S-2) without IR/radio counterparts are identified ~1.0-1.2 pc north and northwest of the SSC candidate H72.97-69.39.  
  > "We identified two dense molecular cloud cores, named N79S-1 and N79S-2, which are separated by $\sim$ 1.0-1.2 pc from the SSC candidate." (Conclusions); "We derive the physical properties of two molecular cloud cores and found that these are very dense ($n_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ $\sim$ 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$), well shielded (A$_v$ $>$ 100 mag) and are not associated with an IR source." (Conclusions)
- CH3OH and H2CS are only detected at N79S-1 and N79S-2 and are absent toward the SSC continuum peak, where CN, CCH, H13CO+, NO, and hydrogen recombination lines are strong.  
  > "Emissions from CH$_3$OH and H$_2$CS are not detected at the SSC continuum peak; they are only observed at locations N79S-1 and N79S-2." (Spatial Distribution of Molecular Emission); "In this work, we also detect the emission lines of CN, CCH, NO, and H$^{13}$CO$^+$ toward the SSC candidate (see Figure 7)." (Non-detection of CH$_3$OH Toward SSC Continuum Peak)
- The compact distribution of cold CH3OH and continuous velocity structure between N79S-1 and N79S-2 suggest non-thermal desorption driven by localized grain-grain collisions in interacting gas clumps.  
  > "So, the continuous velocity structure from the core N79S-1 to the core N79S-2 would support the physical interaction between the gas clumps." (Possible Origin of CH$_3$OH in N79S-1 and N79S-2 Core); "The observed compact emission of CH$_3$OH suggests that grain collisions may play a significant role in the desorption of CH$_3$OH from dust grain mantles." (Possible Origin of CH$_3$OH in N79S-1 and N79S-2 Core)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The LMC provides a nearby low-metallicity (30-50% solar) environment with strong UV fields to study COM formation, and ALMA 0.4'' (~0.1 pc) resolution in Bands 6 and 7 resolves individual dense cores and multiple molecular transitions in the N79 South GMC.  
  > "The Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) is an excellent target for studying the formation and survival of COMs in low metallicity regions." (Introduction); "The measurement sets are cleaned by the CASA task “tclean” with the Briggs weighting and the robustness parameter of 0.5, using a common circular restoring beam size of 0.4$''$ for Band 6 and 7 data in order to accommodate the spectral analyses in separated frequency regions." (ALMA Observations)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: External UV photodesorption from the interstellar radiation field or the SSC candidate H72.97-69.39 was considered but disfavored due to high visual extinction (A_V > 50 mag from the SSC and A_V > 100 mag in the cores) and low experimental photodesorption yields.  
  > "However, the high visual extinction at the CH$_3$OH emission peak in N79S-1 and N79S-2 attenuated the interstellar radiation field." (Possible Origin of CH$_3$OH in N79S-1 and N79S-2 Core); "This suggests that the N79S-1 and N79S-2 cores are well shielded (Av $>$ 50) from the external radiation field." (Possible Origin of CH$_3$OH in N79S-1 and N79S-2 Core)
- Alternative: Reactive (chemical) desorption was considered as an origin for gas-phase CH3OH, but rejected as the primary mechanism because it would produce widespread emission rather than the observed compact distribution.  
  > "If chemical desorption of CH$_3$OH from dust surfaces is taking place in the present sources, we would expect to observe widespread cold CH$_3$OH emission rather than a compact distribution." (Possible Origin of CH$_3$OH in N79S-1 and N79S-2 Core)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Spectral resolution (0.86-1.1 km s^-1) is insufficient to clearly resolve hyperfine components of CN and CCH or potential multiple velocity components in the cores.  
  > "The transitions were not clearly resolved because of the low spectral resolution of the present spectra." (Spectra Toward the CH$_3$OH Peak Positions); "The velocity resolution in present observations (0.9-1.1 km s$^{-1}$) may not be sufficient to resolve multiple velocity components, possibly smoothing the observed linewidths." (Physical Properties of N79S-1 and N79S-2 Core)
- Limitation (authors): Some CH3OH transitions (especially 1_1-0_0 A+) and the H2CO transition are moderately to highly optically thick according to RADEX, making the H2CO column density a lower limit.  
  > "Among these, three have optical depths of approximately 1, while the 1$_1$-0$_0$ A$^+$ line has a significantly higher optical depth of 5.3." (Gas-phase Molecular Column Density); "Therefore, the H$_2$CO line is moderately optically thick in both sources. In this study, the derived column density of H$_2$CO may be considered a lower limit for both sources." (Relative abundance of H$_2$CO)
- Limitation (authors): Derived molecular abundances can be affected by spatial filtering, excitation temperature assumptions, optical depth, and beam filling factors, while only dust temperature uncertainty (10-20 K) is folded into N(H2).  
  > "The derived abundances of the observed molecules may be significantly affected by several factors, including spatial filtering effects, assumptions about excitation temperature, optical depth, and beam filling factors. In this work, we consider only the temperature uncertainty when estimating H$_2$ column densities from dust continuum observations." (Relative Molecular Abundance)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Three CH3OH lines (5_-1-4_-1 E, 5_0-4_0 A+, 4_0-3_-1 E) in N79S-1 have optical depths ~1 in RADEX but were retained in the optically thin LTE rotation diagram fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two SO transitions in N79S-1 and three in N79S-2 were used to fit a straight line in the SO rotation diagram.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using the arithmetic mean of 10 K and 20 K column densities as the nominal value biases the central estimate toward the 10 K value because the Planck function and partition functions are non-linear in temperature.
