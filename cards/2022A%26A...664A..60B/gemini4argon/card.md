## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation, high_z_galaxies
- Question: How do the spatial extents (effective radii) of the cold molecular gas (CO(2-1)) and 1.3 mm continuum in local LIRGs compare with their stellar and ionized gas sizes, and with other low-z and high-z galaxy populations?  
  > "To shed more light on this scenario, we test for the first time the relation among the spatial extents (defined by the half-light radius) of different tracers in a sample of local LIRGs at high spatial resolution ($\sim$90 pc)" (Introduction); "compare the size of the host galaxy (stellar component) with that derived for the ongoing star formation (ionized gas) as well as the size of the regions where stars are forming (molecular gas) in a local sample of LIRGs" (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive galaxy distances, infrared luminosities, star formation rates, and stellar masses**: Derive luminosity distances from CO(2-1) redshifts using Wright cosmology calculator; scale L_IR from Díaz-Santos et al. (2017) to new D_L (splitting multiple systems via 24 um MIPS flux ratios); convert L_IR to SFR; convert 2MASS K-band magnitudes to M_star.  
  > "(5): Redshift derived from the CO(2–1) emission line; (6): Luminosity distance assuming a $\Lambda$DCM cosmology with H$_0$ = 67.8 km sec$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{M}$ = 0.308, $\Omega_{\Lambda}$ = 0.692, using the E. L. Wright Cosmology calculator" (Table 1); "Finally, we scaled these values according to the D$_L$ used in this work." (L$_{IR}$ range); "We then converted the magnitude to luminosity in the K band, L$_K$, and assuming a (mean) M$_{star}$/L$_K$ ratio of 0.4 as derived in (cite: Zibetti09), we derived the stellar mass in the K band, M$_{star}$" (SFR – M$_{star}$: location in the *Main Sequence* plane)
   - software: E. L. Wright Cosmology calculator
   - parameter: H0 = 67.8 km sec^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.308, Omega_Lambda = 0.692  
  > "Throughout the paper, we consider the cosmology corrected quantities: H$_0$ = 67.8 km sec$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{M}$ = 0.308, $\Omega_{\Lambda}$ = 0.692." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Mean M_star/L_K = 0.4 with 0.4 dex scatter  
  > "assuming a (mean) M$_{star}$/L$_K$ ratio of 0.4 as derived in (cite: Zibetti09), we derived the stellar mass in the K band, M$_{star}$" (SFR – M$_{star}$: location in the *Main Sequence* plane)
   - assumption: Kroupa IMF for SFR from L_IR; SFR_Kroupa ~ 0.7 * SFR_Salpeter  
  > "L$_{IR}$ has been then converted to SFR following (cite: Kennicutt12) relation for a Kroupa ((cite: Kroupa01)) IMF (see (cite: Murphy11))." (SFR – M$_{star}$: location in the *Main Sequence* plane); "This relation used the Salpeter IMF, which has been converted to Kroupa according to the formula: SFR$_{Kroupa}$$\sim$0.7$\times$SFR$_{Salpeter}$" (SFR – M$_{star}$: location in the *Main Sequence* plane)
   - assumption: Small AGN contribution to L_IR (~5% on average)  
  > "This parameter can be considered a good tracer of star formation for all our systems because the AGN contribution in our sample to the total L$_{IR}$ is small ($\sim$5% on average; see also (cite: AAH12))." (SFR – M$_{star}$: location in the *Main Sequence* plane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared Elbaz et al. (2007) MS relation converted to Kroupa IMF with Whitaker et al. (2012) MS relation using Chabrier IMF, finding the same MS relation.  
  > "We derived the same *MS* relation when using the power law defined by (cite: Whitaker12) at z$\sim$0 using a Chabrier IMF." (SFR – M$_{star}$: location in the *Main Sequence* plane)
2. **Classify galaxy morphology and dynamical phase (composite classification)**: Combine visual morphological classification from Spitzer/IRAC 3.6 um and HST/WFPC3 images with visual and kinemetry classifications of H-alpha and CO(2-1) kinematic maps into four composite classes: 0 (RD), 0 (PD), 1, and 2.  
  > "In this work, the sources have been classified taking into account both the morphological information from *Spitzer*/IRAC and *Hubble Space Telescope (HST)* images and the kinematic information obtained from the ionized (H$\alpha$) and molecular (CO) gas phases." (Activity, morphology and dynamical phase classification); "Furthermore, following the same scheme used for the H$\alpha$ emission, we visually classify the kinematic maps produced for the molecular CO(2–1) line." (Classifying the LIRG sample using morphological and kinematic information)
   - parameter: Nuclear separation thresholds: >= 1.5 kpc to 15 kpc for class 1 (interacting); <= 1.5 kpc for class 2 (merger); > 15 kpc for 1a/isolated  
  > "`1`: objects in a pre-coalescence phase with two well differentiated nuclei separated by a projected distance of at least 1.5 kpc up to a maximum distance of 15 kpc" (Activity, morphology and dynamical phase classification); "`2`: objects with two nuclei separated by a projected distance $\leq$1.5 kpc or a single nucleus with a relatively asymmetric morphology, with perturbed or complex kinematics (hereafter, `merger`)." (Activity, morphology and dynamical phase classification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-checked visual kinematic classifications between H-alpha and CO(2-1) and against kinemetry classifications.  
  > "The visual kinematic classification proposed for our galaxies using the ionized and molecular gas tracers allowed a direct comparison between the two. Furthermore, the visual classification could be also useful to check the results obtained with `kinemetry`." (Classifying the LIRG sample using morphological and kinematic information)
3. **Generate CO(2-1) and 1.3 mm continuum maps and measure effective radii (R_eff)**: Generate CO(2-1) and 1.3 mm continuum maps selecting emission above 5 sigma; determine effective radii (half-light radii) and enclosed fluxes using the curve-of-growth (CoG) method in circular apertures centered on the near-IR HST/NICMOS peak (or 1.3 mm peak).  
  > "We generated CO(2–1) and 1.3 mm continuum maps for the whole sample, selecting the emission above 5$\sigma$ for both maps." (Molecular gas and 1.3 mm continuum distributions); "In our specific case, we consider `CoG` method (i.e., the flux emission contained in circular apertures of increasing size are derived to compute the radius within which half of the total emission is contained)" (Selection of the methodology); "We select the center of the (circular) aperture choosing the peak emission observed in the near-IR using *HST/NICMOS* F160W ($\lambda_c$ = 1.60 $\mu$m, FWHM = 0.34 $\mu$m) images." (Selection of the methodology)
   - parameter: Detection threshold > 5 sigma; uncertainty estimated from 4 sigma and 6 sigma thresholds: Delta R_eff = (R_eff,4sigma - R_eff,6sigma)/2  
  > "We generated CO(2–1) and 1.3 mm continuum maps for the whole sample, selecting the emission above 5$\sigma$ for both maps." (Molecular gas and 1.3 mm continuum distributions); "The uncertainties associated to the derivation of the R$_{eff}$ have been derived as half the difference between the R$_{eff}$ derived at 4$\sigma$ and 6$\sigma$, considered as the major source of uncertainty for the derivation of this parameter" (Table 4)
   - assumption: Circular aperture is a good approximation of elliptical aperture; observed sizes are not deconvolved for the beam; no inclination correction applied.  
  > "We considered that an equivalent circular aperture as a good approximation of the (major) elliptical aperture." (Selection of the methodology); "The CO(2–1) and 1.3 mm continuum sizes presented in this work are observed, that is, not corrected for the beam." (Selection of the methodology); "Under this assumption, we also expect a small conversion at longer (mm) wavelengths: for this reason, no inclination correction has been applied to our sample." (Extinction impact: optical *versus* near-IR ionized gas tracers. Inclination effects.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested circular vs elliptical apertures on ESO 320-G030 (<5% difference) and NGC 7130 (<12% difference); tested shifting aperture center to CO peak in NGC 7130 (<4% change in R_CO, <15% in R_cont); checked beam deconvolution effect (<1% for CO, <4% average for continuum); compared integrated ALMA CO fluxes with single-dish for 2 galaxies (<15% difference).  
  > "In the first case, the difference between the effective radius derived using a circular or elliptical aperture agrees within 5% while in the latter the difference agrees within 12%." (Selection of the methodology); "Then, in such an extreme case, the variation of the effective radius would be of $\lesssim$4% and $\lesssim$15% for the CO and continuum distributions, respectively." (Selection of the methodology); "For these systems, good agreement is found between the integrated ALMA fluxes and single dish observations, within a factor of $<$15%." (Molecular and 1.3 mm continuum emissions)
4. **Harmonize stellar and ionized gas effective radii and evaluate systematic effects (method conversion, extinction, instrumental bias)**: Convert 2MASS K-band stellar A/2 effective radii to CoG using the empirical CoG/(A/2) relation derived from 27 (U)LIRGs in H-alpha; compare H-alpha and HST/NICMOS Pa-alpha sizes on 12 LIRGs to assess extinction; compare HST/NIC2, HST/WFPC3, 2MASS, and Spitzer/IRAC1 on 3 mergers to assess FoV and resolution bias.  
  > "we need to correct the effective radii of the stellar component, that were derived using the `‘A/2’` method. These radii can be indeed converted to `CoG` measurements using the relation shown in Fig. [4](#Conversions)." (Correction factors and systemic effects associated to the choice of a method); "we compared the effective radii derived for the ionized gas component traced by the strongest hydrogen emission lines in the optical (H$\alpha$) and near-IR (Pa$\alpha$) for a subsample of 12 sources" (Extinction impact: optical *versus* near-IR ionized gas tracers. Inclination effects.); "We investigate these effects in three galaxies (NGC 1614, NGC 7130 and NGC 3256) classified as post-coalescence systems, that show a quite complex structure (e.g., tidal tails)." (Instrumental bias: limitation in the FoV, angular resolution and sensitivity)
   - parameter: Median R_eff(CoG)/R_eff(A/2) = 1.1 for H-alpha (27 galaxies); mean (median) CoG/(A/2) = 1.3 +/- 0.2 (1.3) for CO(2-1) (9 galaxies)  
  > "For this subsample, we found that 0.8 $\lesssim$ R$_{eff}$(`CoG`)/R$_{eff}$(`A/2`) $\lesssim$ 2.2, with median ratio of 1.1." (Correction factors and systemic effects associated to the choice of a method); "Among these sources we selected isolated, interacting and mergers systems deriving a mean (median) ratio `CoG/‘A/2’` $\sim$ 1.3 $\pm$ 0.2 (1.3), which is quite similar to that derived for the ionized component." (Correction factors and systemic effects associated to the choice of a method)
   - fitting: Linear regression fit in Fig. 4 relating R_eff(A/2) and R_eff(CoG)  
  > "These radii can be indeed converted to `CoG` measurements using the relation shown in Fig. [4](#Conversions)." (Correction factors and systemic effects associated to the choice of a method)
   - validation: Checked CoG/(A/2) conversion derived from H-alpha against CO(2-1) maps for a subsample of 9 LIRGs.  
  > "To check the validity of the results achieved using the H$\alpha$ tracer, we also derived the R$_{eff}$ using the `CoG` and `‘A/2’` methods to a subsample of nine LIRGs using the CO(2–1) maps." (Correction factors and systemic effects associated to the choice of a method)
5. **Fit IR SEDs to estimate 1.3 mm continuum flux loss in ALMA observations**: Fit single-temperature gray-body models to Herschel PACS and SPIRE photometry and compare extrapolated 1.3 mm SED flux densities with ALMA >5 sigma 1.3 mm continuum fluxes.  
  > "In this Appendix we present the IR spectral energy distribution (SEDs) for the whole LIRG sample, using *Herschel* PACS and SPIRE data ((cite: Chu17))." (IR SED fit: ALMA flux loss estimation at 1.3 mm); "The aim of this analysis is to quantify the flux loss derived when using ALMA data at 1.3 mm with respect to the extrapolated flux at 1.3 mm (through the SED fitting analysis)." (IR SED fit: ALMA flux loss estimation at 1.3 mm)
   - parameter: Gray-body emissive index beta = 2 (except IRAS F17138-1017), single dust temperature T_dust

## Qualitative findings

- Local LIRGs have a very compact cold molecular gas distribution (median R_CO ~ 0.7 kpc), which is ~2 times smaller than the H-alpha ionized gas and ~3.5 times smaller than the stellar size.  
  > "LIRGs show an extremely compact cold molecular gas distribution (median R$_{CO}$ $\sim$0.7 kpc), which is a factor 2 smaller than the ionized gas (median R$_{H\alpha}$$\sim$1.4 kpc), and 3.5 times smaller than the stellar size (median R$_{star}$$\sim$2.4 kpc)." (abstract)
- There is no relation between the molecular gas size R_CO and the morphological/dynamical class of the LIRGs.  
  > "We can thus claim that no relation has been found between the molecular size, R$_{CO}$, and the galaxy types." (No relation between the molecular size and the morphological type)
- LIRGs hosting an AGN have median molecular gas sizes a factor of ~2 larger than LIRGs without an AGN (KS test p-value = 0.08).  
  > "In our particular case, larger (median) molecular radii by a factor of 2 are derived when considering galaxies with an AGN" (The impact of AGN on the molecular gas); "For the molecular emission the test suggests that the two subsamples (AGN *versus* non-AGN) are not drawn from the same parent sample (p-value=0.08)" (The impact of AGN on the molecular gas)
- When ionized gas is traced by near-IR Pa-alpha (less affected by dust extinction than H-alpha), its effective radius agrees tightly with the CO(2-1) effective radius.  
  > "In this case we found a tight correlation between the two tracers: indeed, for this subsample (see § [4.2.3](#EXTINC)) the effective radius derived for CO(2–1) emission are very similar to that derived for the Pa$\alpha$ emission." (Molecular *versus* ionized (Pa$\alpha$) emissions)
- Local LIRGs share similar stellar masses, stellar sizes, and molecular sizes with local ETGs, while being ~6 times more compact in CO and ~1.6 times more compact in stellar size than local Spirals.  
  > "LIRGs are indistinguishable from ETGs in the M$_{star}$-R$_{star}$ plane, sharing a similar range in their stellar mass and size. However, LIRGs have a stellar host more compact than that of local Spirals, by a factor 1.6." (Conclusions); "the molecular size of LIRG is $\sim$6 times more compact than local Spirals of similar stellar mass, while similar to local ETGs, although characterized by higher SFR." (Conclusions)
- Local LIRGs have stellar masses and sizes similar to intermediate-redshift (z~1-4) MS SFGs, but their molecular gas distributions are ~2.6 times smaller than high-z MS SFGs and ~7.8 times smaller than high-z SMGs.  
  > "LIRGs, representing the population of bursty above-*MS* SFGs at low-z, appear to be similar to the bulk population of *MS* SFGs at intermediate redshfit (z$\sim$1-4) in terms of the stellar mass and the size of their host galaxies;" (Conclusions); "LIRGs appear to be more compact than high-z SFGs in their molecular gas distribution (factor 2.6 smaller) while having a slightly smaller size (factor 1.3 smaller) in their stellar host." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA high-resolution observations (<100 pc) resolve molecular emission in local LIRGs at giant molecular cloud (GMC) scales with high S/N, while combining extended and compact configurations provides a 3-5 kpc maximum recoverable scale.  
  > "With the advent of high resolution instrument as the Atacama Large Millimeter/Submillimeter Array (ALMA), we are now able to study the molecular emission in local galaxies at spatial resolution similar to that covered by typical giant molecular cloud (GMCs, $\sim$100 pc)." (Introduction); "A combination of the extended and compact configurations was used to achieve the 0.2$''$ angular resolution and a maximum recoverable scale of 10$''$-12$''$ (3-5 kpc)." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: The curve-of-growth (CoG) method in circular apertures is robust for galaxies with irregular or clumpy emission (such as interacting and merger systems) where parametric surface brightness fits like GALFIT fail, and is commonly applied at both low and high redshift.  
  > "However, if the galaxies show irregular or clumpy emission (as in the case of interacting or merger systems) the half-light radii can be obtained from the `CoG` of the flux at increasingly large apertures." (Selection of the methodology); "In our specific case, we consider `CoG` method (i.e., the flux emission contained in circular apertures of increasing size are derived to compute the radius within which half of the total emission is contained) as a robust way to derive the R$_{eff}$ and it is commonly applied both at low- and high-z." (Selection of the methodology)
- Alternative: GALFIT parametric surface brightness fitting and the 'A/2' pixel-area method were considered as alternative size measurement methods; CoG was selected for the primary analysis and an empirical conversion was derived from A/2 to CoG.  
  > "To derive the size of a system, different methodologies can be applied: `GALFIT`, the curve-of-growth `(CoG)` and the so-called `‘A/2’` methods can be used according to the objects considered." (Selection of the methodology)
- Alternative: Elliptical apertures vs circular apertures for CoG; circular apertures were adopted after testing showed agreement within 5-12%.  
  > "We considered that an equivalent circular aperture as a good approximation of the (major) elliptical aperture." (Selection of the methodology); "In the first case, the difference between the effective radius derived using a circular or elliptical aperture agrees within 5% while in the latter the difference agrees within 12%." (Selection of the methodology)
- Alternative: Deconvolving the beam and applying inclination corrections to R_eff were considered and rejected because the effects are small (<1% for CO beam, ~1.05 in K-band for inclination).  
  > "The CO(2–1) and 1.3 mm continuum sizes presented in this work are observed, that is, not corrected for the beam. The intrinsic CO and 1.3 mm continuum sizes are also resolved (larger than the beam) and (on average) smaller than the observed size by $\lesssim$1% and $\lesssim$4%, respectively." (Selection of the methodology); "Under this assumption, we also expect a small conversion at longer (mm) wavelengths: for this reason, no inclination correction has been applied to our sample." (Extinction impact: optical *versus* near-IR ionized gas tracers. Inclination effects.)
- Alternative: ALMA 1.3 mm continuum effective radii were measured but excluded from the physical discussion because SED fitting showed 2-15x flux loss of faint extended emission due to sensitivity limits.  
  > "With that in mind, we assume that the effective radii estimation of the 1.3 mm continuum emission is affected by the flux loss of the outer regions at this frequency: for this reason, we will not discuss the results derived for this tracer." (Molecular and 1.3 mm continuum emissions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ALMA 1.3 mm continuum observations at >5 sigma miss a large fraction (<70%, Flux(SED)/Flux(ALMA) ~ 2-15) of faint extended dust emission due to surface brightness sensitivity limits, biasing R_cont to smaller values.  
  > "The 1.3 mm continuum fluxes derived with ALMA at $>$5$\sigma$ are well below the SED emission (Flux(SED)/Flux(ALMA)$\sim$2-15) as a result of the sensitivity effects, for which the faint emission at larger radii is not observed." (Molecular and 1.3 mm continuum emissions); "The gap between the two values clearly suggests that a considerable part of the 1.3 mm emission ($<$70%) is not detected by our ALMA observations." (IR SED fit: ALMA flux loss estimation at 1.3 mm)
- Limitation (authors): Limited VIMOS FoV (~30''x30'') causes H-alpha effective radii for extended galaxies to be lower limits.  
  > "For extended objects, the limited VIMOS FoV only allowed a lower limit to the R$_{eff}$ estimation to be derived." (Ancillary data)
- Limitation (authors): 2MASS K-band images have low angular resolution (~2''), whereas HST/NICMOS images have high resolution (0.15'') but a small FoV (~19.5''x19.5'') that can underestimate sizes in extended systems and may miss diffuse Pa-alpha emission.  
  > "2MASS images do not have any limitation by the FoV although they are characterized by a low angular resolution ($\sim$2$''$)." (Ancillary data); "However, *NICMOS/HST* images may be insensitive to diffuse Pa$\alpha$ emission (e.g., (cite: AAH06; Calzetti07))" (Extinction impact: optical *versus* near-IR ionized gas tracers. Inclination effects.)
- Limitation (authors): The number of high-z galaxies with simultaneous high-resolution measurements of molecular, dust, ionized, and stellar sizes is still small.  
  > "Up to now, the number of high-z galaxies with available measurements of the molecular, ionized and stellar light distributions is quite low, such that the relations found here should be investigated further with larger samples to draw firm conclusions." (LIRGs *versus* high-z SFGs. Sizes of the (un)obscured star formation traced by molecular and ionized ISM)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 4, the units for CO(2-1) Flux(R_eff) are printed in the column header as [Jy], whereas integrated line fluxes are normally in Jy km s^-1; a reproducer must verify whether these values are velocity-integrated fluxes (Jy km s^-1) mislabeled as Jy.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The outer radius used to define 100% of the flux in the curve-of-growth (CoG) method is not explicitly specified for each galaxy, though emission >5 sigma is selected.
