## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and line cubes and combine arrays**: CASA clean with natural weighting; image-plane feather for continuum, C18O, N2D+, DCN; uv-plane concat for CO  
  > "After calibration, clean images were made using the CASA task ``clean''." (2 Observations); "Natural weightings were used for the final images." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA, clean, feather, concat
   - parameter: Cube velocity widths 0.1 (C18O, N2D+), 0.5 (DCN), 1.0 (CO, SiO) km/s  
  > "Velocity widths of 0.1 km s$\^-1$(C$\^18$O and N$\_2$D$\^+$), 0.5 km s$\^-1$(DCN), and 1.0 km s$\^-1$(CO and SiO) were used to produce the image cubes." (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: No data combination for SiO since there was no total flux difference between ACA and low-resolution images  
  > "No data combination was performed since there was no total flux difference in the image between the ACA and ALMA low resolution data sets." (2 Observations)
2. **Measure continuum flux, size and inclination**: 2D Gaussian fitting of each continuum image for total flux, peak flux and deconvolved size; inclination from the major to minor axis ratio  
  > "Using 2D Gaussian fitting, we also obtained the total flux and peak flux for each image" (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission); "Using the ratio between the major and minor axes of the 1.3\,mm continuum emission, we can estimate the inclination angle of the disk" (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
   - software: 2D Gaussian fitting (tool not named)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Tobin et al. (2020): size within a factor of 2 and inclination within about 5 degrees  
  > "Thus, our result is in agreement with (cite: Tobin2020) within a factor of 2." (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission); "Thus, our results are consistent with (cite: Tobin2020) within$\~$5$\^$." (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
3. **Derive dust and gas mass, column density and number density from the continuum**: Optically thin, isothermal dust emission, M_dust = F d^2 / (kappa B(T_dust)); gas mass with gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "Assuming optically thin dust thermal emission and an isothermal dust temperature, we estimate the dust mass with the total 1.3 mm flux," (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: T_dust = 20 and 40 K  
  > "Assuming a dust temperature of$T\_dust =20$and 40\,K, we obtain the dust mass," (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: kappa_1.3mm = 0.9 cm2/g; d = 392 pc  
  > "We adopt$\_1.3mm = 0.9$\,cm$\^2$\,g$\^-1$from the dust coagulation model of the MRN (cite: Mathis77)" (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100  
  > "in which a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100 is assumed." (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and isothermal dust  
  > "Assuming optically thin dust thermal emission and an isothermal dust temperature, we estimate the dust mass with the total 1.3 mm flux," (3.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive envelope column density and mass from C18O**: Optically thin LTE C18O column density formula, then mass from solid angle, distance and mean molecular weight  
  > "Assuming optically thin C$\^18$O emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), the envelope column density" (3.2 Dense Gas Tracers)
   - parameter: X(C18O) = 1.7e-7; S mu^2 = 0.02 Debye^2; Eu/k = 15.8 K; Tex = 20 K; mean molecular weight 2.8; flux above 7 sigma  
  > "We assumed that the excitation temperature is$T\_ex$=20 K." (3.2 Dense Gas Tracers); "is a mean molecular weight (cite: Kauffmann08)" (3.2 Dense Gas Tracers)
   - assumption: Optically thin C18O, LTE, Tex = 20 K  
  > "We assumed that the excitation temperature is$T\_ex$=20 K." (3.2 Dense Gas Tracers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive outflow mass, momentum, energy, timescale, mass-loss rate, force and kinetic luminosity from CO**: Same LTE optically thin formulas with X(CO) = 1e-4 and Eu/k = 16.59 K; blue and red lobes integrated separately; pixels above 3 sigma per channel; inclination 45 deg corrections; t_dyn = l_flow / v_max  
  > "we derive outflow physical parameters from equations~(\ref{eq;column}) and (\ref{eq;mout}) assuming LTE and an optically thin condition," (Outflow Parameters); "We only use the pixels above 3$$in a given channel." (Outflow Parameters); "is the outflow dynamical timescale estimated from the outflow length$l\_flow$and the maximum velocity$v\_max$as$t\_dyn= l\_flow/v\_max$." (Outflow Parameters)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: X(CO) = 1e-4, Eu/k = 16.59 K, Tex = 20 K, pixels above 3 sigma  
  > "abundance ratio$X\_CO$of$10\^-4$(cite: Frerking82)" (Outflow Parameters); "We only use the pixels above 3$$in a given channel." (Outflow Parameters)
   - assumption: Disk inclination i = 45 deg; LTE; optically thin; Tex = 20 K  
  > "We adopt an inclination angle of the disk$i$= 45$\^$." (Outflow Parameters); "We also assumed that the excitation temperature is$T\_ex$=20 K (cite: Aso00)." (Outflow Parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare MMS 3 outflow with other regions to infer evolutionary stage**: Compared outflow size, mass, force and bolometric luminosity with Class 0/I outflows in OMC-2/3 and Taurus  
  > "we compare the outflow size, mass, force (or momentum flux), and bolometric luminosity of MMS 3 with those of other outflows associated with Class 0 and Class I sources in the OMC-2/3" (Outflow Parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Literature outflow parameters said to agree within a factor of about 3 on average  
  > "Outflow parameters in OMC-2/3 estimated by (cite: Feddersen20) plotted here are consistent with previous several studies within factor of $\sim$3 on average" (Figure 6 caption)

## Qualitative findings

- C18O envelope is flattened and elongated roughly perpendicular to the CO outflow, while N2D+ traces the larger-scale filament  
  > "The spatial distribution of the C$\^18$O emission is very different from that of the N$\_2$D$\^+$emission that traces the filament." (4.1 Observational Characteristics of MMS 3)
- A compact red-shifted high-velocity CO component near the continuum peak may be a jet; no blue-shifted counterpart was detected  
  > "The detected emission shows a compact structure associated with the 1.3 mm continuum peak, which may correspond to a recently ejected jet." (3.3.1 Morphology); "In our observations, no blue-shifted high-velocity component was detected." (3.3.1 Morphology)
- Faint compact red-shifted SiO marginally detected on the CO outflow lobe  
  > "A compact and faint red-shifted SiO emission was marginally detected on the CO outflow lobe." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Contradictory prestellar and protostellar characteristics of MMS 3 motivated high-resolution, high-sensitivity imaging  
  > "These observational characteristics have motivated us to study the evolutionary stage of MMS 3 through direct imaging with high angular resolution and high sensitivity observations." (4.1 Observational Characteristics of MMS 3)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Two scenarios considered: (1) very early phase after protostar formation, disfavored by outflow velocity > 10 km/s; (2) low-mass star with low accretion rate, not contradicted  
  > "To explain the characteristics of MMS 3, we proposed two possible scenarios: (1) a very early star formation phase and (2) low-mass star formation with a low accretion rate." (4.2 Possible Evolutionary Scenarios)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Origin of the SiO emission unresolved; needs more observations  
  > "More observations are necessary to identify the origin of the SiO emission." (3.3.1 Morphology)
- Limitation (authors): Higher-sensitivity SiO data needed to distinguish a jet from outflow-envelope interaction  
  > "High sensitivity SiO observations will be needed to confirm either scenario." (4.1 Observational Characteristics of MMS 3)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass errors include only flux uncertainties  
  > "The errors for the mass are obtained by propagating only the errors for the measured flux densities." (Table 4)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Masses depend on assumed kappa, T_dust (20 or 40 K) and Tex = 20 K; no uncertainty on temperature is propagated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow parameters assume inclination 45 deg and optically thin LTE CO, which likely underestimates mass; the 35 km/s maximum velocity in the text versus ~42 km/s in the Summary is inconsistent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Resolved-out ambient emission in C18O and N2D+ (interferometer filtering) may bias envelope mass and systemic velocity
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-epoch short observations (4 to 16 min) limit sensitivity for faint jet and SiO detections (5 sigma only)
