## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA and VLA visibility data (both disk-subtracted and non-disk-subtracted)**: Apply standard pipeline/MIRIAD calibration, perform iterative phase-only self-calibration, subtract a uniform limb-darkened disk model in the UV plane for contrast imaging, and invert/deconvolve both disk-subtracted and non-disk-subtracted visibilities, applying a CMB correction.  
  > "The disk-subtracted data were inverted into the image plane and deconvolved using CASA’s `tclean` function." (ALMA Data); "Images were also produced from the calibrated but non-disk-subtracted data; these images were used to measure absolute fluxes across the disk for radiative transfer modeling." (ALMA Data); "The total flux measurements were corrected for the cosmic microwave background (CMB) according to the prescription detailed in Appendix A of (cite: depater14)." (ALMA Data)
   - software: CASA, tclean, MIRIAD
   - parameter: Self-calibration solution intervals of 20, 10, 5, and 1 minutes  
  > "using solution intervals of 20, 10, 5, and 1 minutes in that order." (ALMA Data)
   - assumption: Flux calibration errors of ~5% for ALMA, 3% for longest four VLA wavelengths, and 7% at 0.9 cm  
  > "The CASA pipeline retrieved flux calibration errors of $\sim$5% in all three bands." (ALMA Data); "the absolute flux calibration error was assumed to be 3% at the longest four wavelengths and 7% at 0.9 cm (cite: butler01; perley13; perley17)." (VLA Data)
   - fitting: UV-plane Bessel function fit to confirm total flux  
  > "These flux measurements were confirmed by fitting the visibility data to a Bessel function" (ALMA Data)
   - validation: Compared UV-plane Bessel function fit to image-plane total flux; differences were much smaller than flux calibration error.  
  > "These flux measurements were confirmed by fitting the visibility data to a Bessel function; the difference between the UV-plane-derived and image-plane-derived total flux measurements was much smaller than the flux calibration error at all wavelengths." (ALMA Data)
2. **Project maps and extract spatially resolved brightness temperature profiles**: Project images cylindrically onto a latitude-longitude grid, extract a 30-degree longitude slice centered on the sub-observer point to create 1-D latitude profiles, and average non-disk-subtracted profiles over 10-degree latitude bins for 25 deg N (20-30 deg N) and 75 deg N (70-80 deg N).  
  > "These images are also shown cylindrically projected onto a latitude-longitude grid in Figure 2." (ALMA Data); "a vertical slice of width 30$^\circ$ longitude centered on the sub-observer point was taken from the longitude-smeared, disk-subtracted, projected maps (shown in Figure 2 for the ALMA data) at each frequency, then averaged into a 1-D brightness versus latitude profile." (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures); "To extract spatially-resolved brightness temperatures for radiative transfer modeling, we simply averaged non-disk-subtracted latitude profiles over ten degrees latitude; that is, the 25$^\circ$N region represents latitudes from 20-30$^\circ$N and the 75$^\circ$N region represents latitudes from 70-80$^\circ$N." (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures)
   - parameter: Slice width: 30 deg longitude; latitude bin width: 10 deg (20-30 deg N and 70-80 deg N)  
  > "a vertical slice of width 30$^\circ$ longitude centered on the sub-observer point was taken" (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures); "averaged non-disk-subtracted latitude profiles over ten degrees latitude; that is, the 25$^\circ$N region represents latitudes from 20-30$^\circ$N and the 75$^\circ$N region represents latitudes from 70-80$^\circ$N." (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Computed latitude-bin errors by taking standard deviation of brightness measurements across several different 30 deg longitude ranges.  
  > "The error on the extracted brightness temperatures was determined by making latitude profiles over several different 30$^\circ$ longitude ranges and computing the standard deviation in the brightness measurements from those profiles." (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures)
3. **Fit radiative transfer models to the enriched region (25 deg N) and depleted north polar region (75 deg N) via MCMC**: Couple the Radio-BEAR radiative transfer code with the emcee MCMC sampler to fit the 8-band spectrum at 25 deg N using a simplified cloud-physics model, then fit the 75 deg N spectrum using a step-function depletion model above a mixing pressure P_mix.  
  > "Radiative transfer modeling was carried out using the radio-BEAR (radio-BErkeley Atmospheric Radiative transfer) code" (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "Radiative transfer modeling was carried out within a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) framework, implemented using the `emcee` Python package (cite: dfm13)." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "we employ a model for the depleted region that prescribes a step function in the H$_2$S and NH$_3$ abundances." (North Pole)
   - software: radiobear, emcee, scipy
   - parameter: 500 iterations, 40 walkers, burn-in of 250 iterations (first half discarded)  
  > "Each of the MCMC runs presented in this paper used 500 iterations and 40 walkers. As is standard practice with MCMC (cite: dfm13), we cut out the “burn-in” phase by using only the second half of the iterations to describe the posterior distribution" (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium; dry adiabat with T = 76.4 K at 1 bar (Lindal et al. 1987) and Orton et al. (2015) T-P profile at higher altitudes; cloud opacity ignored; deep H2O fixed at 30x Solar; protosolar abundances from Asplund et al. (2009); simplified model ignores deep water-solution cloud.  
  > "Radio-BEAR assumes that the atmosphere is in local thermodynamic equilibrium; its temperature follows an adiabat with a temperature of 76.4 K at 1 bar as determined by radio-occultation experiments with Voyager 2 (cite: lindal87)." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "RadioBEAR therefore ignores cloud opacity and considers only gas absorption." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "we set the deep H$_2$O abundance to a fixed 30$\times$ Solar. The difference between a wet and dry adiabat is small; we assumed a dry adiabat." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - fitting: Gaussian log-likelihood function (Eq. 1) sampled with emcee; for 75 deg N, priors on deep abundances are set equal to the posterior PDFs from the 25 deg N fit.  
  > "Letting $\theta$ represent the set of free parameters in the model, the likelihood function $\ln p$ is given by $$\begin{equation} \label{lnlike} \ln p(T|\nu, \sigma, \theta) = -\frac{1}{2} \sum_n \Big[ (T_n - T_m(\theta))^2 \sigma_n^{-2} + \ln(2 \pi \sigma_n^{-2}) \Big] \end{equation}$$" (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "We set the prior probability distribution functions of the deep abundances equal to the posterior probability distribution functions found for the enriched region; that is, the deep abundances are constrained to agree with the enriched region." (North Pole)
   - validation: Checked simplified cloud-physics model against full model (difference at least an order of magnitude smaller than flux calibration errors); plotted MCMC parameter values vs iteration to confirm burn-in removal.  
  > "plotting the parameter values as a function of iteration confirmed that this procedure had worked as intended." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "We checked our simplified implementation against the full cloud-physics model, and found that the difference in brightness temperature between them was at least an order of magnitude smaller than the flux calibration errors at all frequencies." (Enriched Region)
4. **Derive true deep abundances below the water-solution cloud and bulk N/S ratio**: Tune the full cloud-physics model (including the deep water-solution cloud) so that the NH3 and H2S abundances between the solution cloud and the NH4SH cloud match the best-constrained retrieved abundances from the MCMC fits, and compute the N/S ratio.  
  > "Thus, to make inferences about the true deep abundances of nitrogen- and sulfur-bearing species, we returned to the full cloud-physics model of (cite: depater91), which includes prescriptions for the deep water and water-solution clouds from (cite: atreya85). We tuned the model such that the NH$_3$ and H$_2$S abundances between the solution cloud and the NH$_4$SH cloud matched our retrieved abundances of those species." (RT Modeling Discussion)
   - software: radiobear
   - assumption: Solution cloud removes ~5% of deep H2S and ~25% of deep NH3 following Atreya & Romani (1985); atmosphere is well-mixed below the mixing layer.  
  > "The solution cloud in the full model removes $\sim$5% of the deep H$_2$S and $\sim$25% of the deep NH$_3$" (Enriched Region); "we used the best-fitting H$_2$S abundance below the NH$_4$SH cloud from the enriched region and the best-fitting NH$_3$ abundance below $P_{mix}$ from the depleted region, since those were the best-constrained values and the two regions are assumed to be well-mixed below the mixing layer." (RT Modeling Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested alternative meridional kinetic temperature gradients and integrated the thermal wind equation to verify that composition, rather than temperature, drives the brightness differences.  
  > "To achieve a reasonable fit to the observed north polar spectrum using only kinetic temperature changes requires many unphysical kinks in the temperature structure (Figure 13)." (Meridional Temperature Gradients); "However, they stop short of integrating the thermal wind equation vertically to produce a geostrophic wind profile. We have done this, and find that the winds must be supersonic deeper than $\sim$20 bar, which is unphysical." (Meridional Temperature Gradients)

## Qualitative findings

- The north polar region northward of ~50 deg N is very bright across all millimeter and radio wavelengths, similar in brightness and extent to the south polar region observed 30 years ago, indicating large-scale downwelling of dry air from ~0.1 to ~50 bar.  
  > "The north polar region is very bright at all frequencies northward of $\sim$50$^\circ$N, which we attribute to strong depletions extending down to the NH$_4$SH layer in both NH$_3$ and H$_2$S relative to the equatorial region" (Abstract); "The north polar region is approximately equal in brightness and extent to the south polar region observed three decades ago, with a radio brightness temperature $\sim$35 K brighter at 2 cm than the midlatitudes." (Conclusions)
- Alternating radio-bright and radio-dark zonal bands are detected at ~20 deg S, ~0 deg, and ~20 deg N, as well as zonal banding within the north polar region at ~60 deg N and ~75 deg N in the highest-resolution VLA bands.  
  > "The highest-resolution maps reveal zonal radio-dark and radio-bright bands at 20$^\circ$S, 0$^\circ$, and 20$^\circ$N, as well as zonal banding within the north polar region." (Abstract); "At 0.9 cm and 2 cm wavelengths, the highest-resolution VLA bands, a polar collar is observed at $\sim$60$^\circ$N" (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures)
- No large vortex-like disturbances at the scale of Jupiter's Great Red Spot are detected in the longitude-resolved VLA maps.  
  > "Nevertheless, if any vortex-like disturbances at the scale of Jupiter’s Great Red Spot were present on Uranus, these maps should have detected them. No such structures are found" (VLA Data)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Millimeter and centimeter wavelengths probe beneath Uranus's upper tropospheric cloud layers from ~0.1 to ~50 bar, and combining ALMA and VLA provides high spatial resolution (0.1" to 0.8") across 1.3 mm to 10 cm.  
  > "We present ALMA and VLA spatial maps of the Uranian atmosphere taken between 2015 and 2018 at wavelengths from 1.3 mm to 10 cm, probing pressures from $\sim$1 to $\sim$50 bar at spatial resolutions from 0.1” to 0.8”." (Abstract); "Radio observations provide a unique tool for probing the atmosphere of Uranus beneath its tropospheric cloud layers, permitting inferences about its tropospheric properties" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Subtracting a limb-darkened disk model in the UV plane reduces deconvolution ringing from the sharp-edged planet; MCMC with Radio-BEAR explores the joint posterior distribution of coupled atmospheric parameters; 25 deg N was chosen for small emission angles and minimal beam smearing.  
  > "To reduce ringing in the image plane from the presence of the bright planet, a uniform limb-darkened disk model of Uranus was subtracted from the data in the UV plane in each band" (ALMA Data); "We first fit the radio-dark region at 25$^\circ$N, chosen as the representative “enriched” or volatile-rich region because of its location at small emission angles with respect to the observer as well as relatively similar brightnesses at nearby latitudes, minimizing beam smearing effects." (Enriched Region)
- Alternative: Explaining the polar-to-midlatitude brightness difference solely via meridional kinetic temperature gradients rather than compositional differences; rejected because it requires unphysical temperature profile kinks or supersonic geostrophic winds deeper than ~20 bar.  
  > "To achieve a reasonable fit to the observed north polar spectrum using only kinetic temperature changes requires many unphysical kinks in the temperature structure (Figure 13)." (Meridional Temperature Gradients); "However, they stop short of integrating the thermal wind equation vertically to produce a geostrophic wind profile. We have done this, and find that the winds must be supersonic deeper than $\sim$20 bar, which is unphysical." (Meridional Temperature Gradients)
- Alternative: Tuning the N/S ratio to very near unity as in Hofstadter (1992); rejected in favor of a sulfur-rich model where H2S survives above the NH4SH cloud.  
  > "The nitrogen-to-sulfur (N/S) ratio does not need to be tuned to very near unity as required by the H$_2$S-inclusive models of (cite: hofstadter92)." (RT Modeling Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Planet rotation during observations smears features in longitude (~8 deg smearing in 20 min ALMA observations at 1.3 and 2.1 mm; 3.1 mm combines two 40 min observations at different longitudes).  
  > "It should be noted that the planet’s rotation smears out features in longitude: the $\sim$20 minute observations at 2.1 mm and 1.3 mm are smeared by $\sim$8$^\circ$, and the 3.1 mm image is a sum of two $\sim$40 minute observations taken at different sub-observer longitudes." (ALMA Data)
- Limitation (authors): VLA 0.9 cm images suffer from imaging artefacts due to proximity to a telluric water absorption band and poor phase stability.  
  > "It should be noted that the 0.9 cm images are more strongly affected by imaging artefacts than the other bands because this wavelength sits near a telluric water absorption band, leading to relatively poor phase stability." (VLA Data)
- Limitation (authors): Longitude-resolved VLA maps have significant artefacts and warping near 300 deg W due to limited UV-plane coverage.  
  > "Limited UV-plane coverage leads to significant artefacts in these maps, including large-scale alternating bright and dark regions (e.g., bright at 60$^\circ$W and 200$^\circ$W in the 2 cm and 3 cm images) that cross many latitude bands. An apparent warping near 300$^\circ$W is also present, caused by poor zonal coverage at those longitudes." (VLA Data)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic errors from inverting and CLEANing a bright, extended, sharp-edged disk make latitude-bin errors larger than background per-beam RMS.  
  > "It is worth noting that they are larger than the per-beam RMS error extracted from background regions of the radio maps (except at 0.9 cm). This is due to systematic errors arising from inverting and CLEANing visibility data of a very bright, extended, and sharp-edged source." (Spatially Resolved Brightness Temperatures)
- Limitation (authors): Combined temperature and composition changes between midlatitudes and poles cannot be completely ruled out and could affect the retrieved deep NH3 abundance.  
  > "However, the possibility that both temperature and composition change between the midlatitudes and poles cannot be ruled out. A different assumed polar temperature profile would have a relatively small but perhaps non-negligible effect on the deep NH$_3$ abundance retrieved in this paper." (Meridional Temperature Gradients)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absolute flux calibration uncertainties (~3-7%) dominate the error budget for broadband spectral fitting across non-simultaneous VLA (2015) and ALMA (2017-2018) epochs.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The MCMC chain length (500 iterations with 40 walkers, 250 burn-in) is relatively short, though the authors state trace plots were inspected.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deep NH3 and H2S abundances below the ~100 bar water-solution cloud rely on an unprobed theoretical cloud-physics prescription (Atreya & Romani 1985).
