## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the archival data**: Manual CASA calibration of four measurement sets, combined, then tclean line cubes (multiscale) and continuum map (Hogbom)  
  > "We manually calibrated the data using CASA (cite: mcmullin2007casa)." (2 Data and data reduction); "After calibration, spectral cubes were extracted for each molecular line across all channels using the multiscale deconvolution algorithm" (2 Data and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5, pixel 0.07 arcsec  
  > "The chosen size of a pixel is $0.07\,\textmd{arcsec}$ and Briggs weighting was applied to all images with $robust\,$=$\,0.5$." (2 Data and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure integrated line spectra and fluxes**: Integrated 12CO, 13CO and C18O spectra fitted with Gaussians to get peak, FWHM and total flux  
  > "The integrated $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O spectra and their respective Gaussian fittings are shown in Fig. [3](#fig:Spectral profile)." (3.2 Line intensity)
   - fitting: Gaussian fitting of the integrated spectra; fitting method not stated  
  > "The integrated $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O spectra and their respective Gaussian fittings are shown in Fig. [3](#fig:Spectral profile)." (3.2 Line intensity)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive excitation temperature and CO optical depths**: Tex from the 12CO peak assuming optically thick 12CO; 13CO and C18O optical depth maps from Nishimura et al. formulae after converting intensity to brightness temperature via Rayleigh-Jeans; 12CO optical depth scaled from 13CO using a 12CO/13CO ratio of ~10  
  > "Under the assumption of Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), and assuming that $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O have the same excitation temperature T$_\textmd{ex}$ and that $^{12}$CO is optically thick" (3.3 Optical depth); "Using the Rayleigh-Jeans law, the maps in units of intensity are converted to maps in units of brightness temperature with (cite: thompson1986)" (3.3 Optical depth)
   - parameter: Masks at 3 sigma_13 (13CO) and 2 sigma_18 (C18O) on the integrated intensity maps  
  > "with cut-offs at $3\sigma_{13}$ and $2\sigma_{18}$ for $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O, respectively." (3.3 Optical depth)
   - assumption: LTE, a single excitation temperature for all three CO isotopologues, 12CO optically thick  
  > "Under the assumption of Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), and assuming that $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O have the same excitation temperature T$_\textmd{ex}$ and that $^{12}$CO is optically thick" (3.3 Optical depth)
   - assumption: 12CO/13CO optical-depth ratio of ~10 taken from the literature  
  > "Using the observed $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO ratio of $\sim$10 found by (cite: bachiller1997) for the entire nebula, we estimate the $^{12}$CO optical depth to be up to $\sim$10." (3.3 Optical depth)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested a higher Tex of 60 K, which lowers the maximum 13CO optical depth to 0.34  
  > "With an excitation temperature of 60$\,$K, we obtain a maximum $^{13}$CO optical depth of 0.34." (4 Isotopic ratios)
4. **Derive isotopic ratios 12C/13C and 16O/18O**: Ratio maps of integrated intensity with 13CO above 3 sigma at a common beam and pixel grid; ratio taken in the 12CO optically thin extended tail; converted with frequency^-3 relations; 16O/18O from 13CO/C18O ratio combined with the 12CO/13CO ratio  
  > "We create a binary mask that picks only the regions where the $^{13}$CO emission is above the $3\sigma_{13}$ level." (4 Isotopic ratios); "As $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O are both mostly optically thin, we use the $^{13}$CO/C$^{18}$O ratio to derive the optical depth-corrected C$^{16}$O/C$^{18}$O ratio." (4 Isotopic ratios)
   - assumption: Underlying 12C/13C isotopic ratio uniform across the globule  
  > "Given the age and the physical conditions inside the Helix nebula, the underlying isotopic ratio $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C is expected to be uniform." (4 Isotopic ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with AGB models, presolar grains and AGB carbon stars  
  > "The AGB models of (cite: Karakas2016) are used to compare these ratios with what is predicted by those models." (4 Isotopic ratios)
5. **Constrain progenitor mass using AGB models**: Compare the derived isotopic ratios, and the 16O/18O to 12C/13C ratio, with Karakas (2016) model surface abundances at the end of the AGB  
  > "Comparing this to the models gives a possible mass around 2$\,$M$_\odot$." (4 Isotopic ratios)
   - assumption: Dust in the Helix is carbonaceous, requiring C/O > 1  
  > "Carbonaceous dust requires that C/O $> 1$." (4 Isotopic ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Requires 12CO optical depth to be higher than derived, which the data cannot confirm  
  > "We cannot confirm this with the available data." (4 Isotopic ratios)
6. **Derive molecular mass from 13CO and C18O column densities**: J=2 column densities from optical depth maps, total column densities via partition function, scaled to 12CO by optical-depth-corrected isotopic ratios, converted to H2 mass at 201 pc  
  > "The derived column densities $N(\textmd{$^{13}$CO})$ and $N(\textmd{C$^{18}$O})$ are converted to $^{12}$CO column densities by multiplying them with the corresponding isotopic ratios corrected for optical depth derived in section [4](#sec:ratio)." (5.1 Molecular mass)
   - parameter: Tex = 30 K, dv = 0.05 km/s  
  > "where $T_\textmd{ex}=30\,\textmd{K}$ is the excitation temperature, $v$ is the velocity centre and $dv=0.05$ $\textmd{km}\,\textmd{s}^{-1}$ is the velocity width." (5.1 Molecular mass)
   - assumption: Distance 201 pc; CO/H2 = 6e-4  
  > "assume a CO/H$_2$ ratio of 6$\,\times 10^{-4}$ based on the CO/H abundance ratio of $\sim$3$\,\times 10^{-4}$ in the Helix nebula in (cite: huggins1992; huggins2002)" (5.1 Molecular mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with earlier CO masses; uncertainty from CO/H2 and LTE up to ~30 percent  
  > "Uncertainties lie in the CO/H$_2$ conversion factor and the LTE assumption, which have been found to estimate the molecular mass with a discrepancy of up to $\sim30$ percent (cite: szHucs2016)." (5.1 Molecular mass)
7. **Derive gas mass from 1.2 mm dust continuum**: Total continuum flux density with a 2 sigma cut-off, converted to gas mass with a Rayleigh-Jeans optically thin dust formula  
  > "We do not consider emission below 2$\sigma$ ($\sigma=20\,\mu$Jy$\,$beam$^{-1}$)." (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation); "to find the mass of the gas (e.g. (cite: evans2004) (cite: evans2004)), assuming that the emission is under the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation." (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation)
   - parameter: T_dust = mean 30.8 K; K_d = 20 cm2/g; dust-to-gas 1:100  
  > "We use $K_\textmd{d}=20\,\,\textmd{cm}^2\,\textmd{g}^{-1}$ taken from (cite: mennella1998) for amorphous carbon dust grain at $1\,\textmd{mm}$ and at $20\,\textmd{K}$" (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation); "Here we use $\chi_\textmd{d}=1\textmd{:}100$ as in (cite: meaburn1992)." (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation)
   - assumption: Free-free emission negligible; dust emission optically thin (gamma = 1)  
  > "Free-free emission is not expected to be important in the knot, so the continuum emission is entirely associated with dust" (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation); "At this wavelength, dust emission is optically thin." (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Sensitivity to dust-to-gas ratio (1:150 and 1:55) explored  
  > "With a dust–to–gas mass ratio of $1\textmd{:}150$ in the Helix" (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation)
8. **Test tail-formation models with kinematics**: Channel maps, declination velocity cuts and right ascension velocity cuts of 12CO and 13CO used to look for a head-to-tail velocity gradient expected from wind ablation (~10 km/s)  
  > "We look for a velocity gradient between the head and the tail of the C1 globule to test the ablation of the head by an ambient wind." (6.1 Tail formation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Gradient of ~0.4-0.5 km/s versus ~10 km/s expected; compared with Huggins et al. gradient  
  > "Our result is in agreement with (cite: huggins2002) who found an observable velocity gradient of $0.2\,\textmd{km}\,\textmd{s}^{-1}$ in the line of sight" (6.1 Tail formation)

## Qualitative findings

- The C1 globule is molecular with a multi-peak structure; CO emission is strong in both head and extended tail  
  > "Our findings confirm the molecular nature of the globule with a multi-peak structure." (abstract)
- The molecular head is offset by ~1 arcsec from the ionized crescent-shaped head seen in the optical  
  > "The molecular head of the globule is ellipsoidal and is $\sim1^{\prime \prime}$ offset to the crescent-shaped ionized head observed in the optical" (3.1 Morphology)
- Most of the CO mass is carried by the tail  
  > "This means that the majority of the CO in the knot is carried by the tail." (5.1 Molecular mass)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: New ALMA CO data with much better resolution than previous observations  
  > "We report and analyse new CO data of the knot with significantly better resolution than previous observations." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: LTE with a shared Tex and optically thick 12CO, using optically thin isotopologues to correct for optical depth  
  > "However, with an optically thick $^{12}$CO and making use of its optically thin isotopes, $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O, we derive the molecular mass considering the $^{12}$CO column density corrected for optical depth." (5.1 Molecular mass)
- Alternative: Stream (wind-swept) model alone rejected for the tail  
  > "We conclude that the stream model alone does not describe the formation of the tail of the C1 globule." (6.1 Tail formation)
- Alternative: Photoevaporation alone rejected  
  > "The photoevaporation model alone does not explain the molecular properties of the knot." (6.1 Tail formation)
- Alternative: Excitation temperature of 60 K tried instead of 30 K, giving a maximum 13CO optical depth of 0.34  
  > "With an excitation temperature of 60$\,$K, we obtain a maximum $^{13}$CO optical depth of 0.34." (4 Isotopic ratios)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 12CO optical depth is not well constrained, so the isotopic ratios are uncertain  
  > "The derived isotopic ratios depend on the optical depth of the $^{12}$CO line which is not well constrained." (4 Isotopic ratios)
- Limitation (authors): CO/H2 conversion factor and LTE assumption can give up to ~30 percent discrepancy in mass  
  > "Uncertainties lie in the CO/H$_2$ conversion factor and the LTE assumption, which have been found to estimate the molecular mass with a discrepancy of up to $\sim30$ percent (cite: szHucs2016)." (5.1 Molecular mass)
- Limitation (authors): Unresolved clumps and non-constant excitation temperature are possible; higher resolution data needed  
  > "Higher angular resolution data, in the optically thin C$^{18}$O line in particular, are needed in order to investigate the likelihood of clumping in the C1 knot." (5.3 Clumping)
- Limitation (authors): 2 sigma cut-off could underestimate the dust-derived gas mass  
  > "As the detected dust emission is weak, the 2$\sigma$ cut-off could result in an underestimation of the total gas mass." (5.2 Gas mass from dust observation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 12CO optical depth of ~10 is not measured but scaled from 13CO using a ratio taken from a literature whole-nebula value
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties are given on most derived masses, ratios or optical depths
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single constant Tex of 30 K from the 12CO peak is applied to all gas, yet the paper itself says 13CO and C18O may trace warmer gas
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optically thin region used for the 12C/13C ratio is chosen by eye from Fig. 5 with no objective criterion
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust-derived gas mass depends on K_d at 1 mm and 20 K applied at 1.2 mm and T_dust 30.8 K
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Calibration, flux scale and CASA version are not recorded, so absolute flux uncertainty cannot be assessed
