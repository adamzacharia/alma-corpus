## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA CO(1-0) and [CI](1-0) cubes and create moment maps**: Perform standard calibration in CASA, clean cubes with natural weighting and primary-beam correction using tclean, and generate moment maps with immoments using a 4x RMS threshold.  
  > "We use the Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA) package (cite: McMullin2007a) to perform the standard calibration. (ALMA data); "We create the CO and $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $ moment maps with the CASA task *immoments* by applying a threshold of 4 $\times$ the RMS." (ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, tclean, immoments
   - parameter: natural weighting; pixel size ~0.1''; channel width 5 km s^-1; immoments threshold = 4 x RMS (RMS = 1.2 mJy/beam for CO(1-0), 1.3 mJy/beam for [CI](1-0))  
  > "The pixel sizes in CO and $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $ images are about 0.1$\ensuremath{''}$. The RMS values are about 1.2 mJy/beam and 1.3 mJy/beam for CO(1-0) and $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $(1-0) data at a channel width of 5 km s$^{-1}$ , respectively." (ALMA data); "We create the CO and $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $ moment maps with the CASA task *immoments* by applying a threshold of 4 $\times$ the RMS." (ALMA data)

  > "To derive the flux of pure emission lines, we employ the STARLIGHT package (cite: CidFernandes2005) to reproduce the stellar continuum." (VLT/MUSE data); "The total stellar mass is estimated by integrating the values in each pixel with continuum S/N larger than 5.0, and then converted to the (cite: Kroupa2001) IMF by adding 0.15 dex (cite: Bell2003; Shangguan2019a). (VLT/MUSE data)

   - parameter: 45 SSPs (3 metallicities, 15 stellar ages); continuum S/N > 5.0 threshold  
  > "In this process, we assume the (cite: Chabrier2003) initial mass function (IMF), and perform a combination of 45 single stellar populations (SSPs) from (cite: Bruzual2003) model, which consist of three different metallicities and 15 stellar ages." ## Qualitative findings

- Molecular gas in Haro 11 is concentrated around the central two star-forming knots B and C (containing ~55% and ~4% of the gas mass, respectively), while no significant CO(1-0) emission is detected toward knots A and T.  
  > "We note that molecular gas is concentrated around knots B and C, which contain about $55\%$ and $4\%$ of the gas mass, respectively." (CO(1-0) distribution and kinematics); "No CO was detected at a significant level toward knots A and T." (CO(1-0) distribution and kinematics)
- There is a spatial offset between the CO(1-0) and Halpha intensity peaks, indicating a delay between molecular gas compression and current star formation.  
  > "We also detect an offset between the CO and $\hbox{H$\alpha$}$ intensity peaks, indicating the delay between compression of molecular gas and current star formation." (CO(1-0) distribution and kinematics)
- The kinematics of molecular gas, ionized gas, and stars are complex and non-circular, suggesting a combined stage of cloud-cloud collision and stellar feedback/outflows.  
  > "The molecular/ionized gas and stellar components show complex kinematics, indicating that the gas is probably at a combined stage of collision of clouds and feedback from star formation." (abstract); "Thus, the molecular gas between knots B and C might be the complex/combined stage of collision and feedback." (CO(1-0) distribution and kinematics)
- [CI](1-0) emission closely resembles CO(1-0) in spatial distribution and velocity field across the bridge from knot B to C, indicating [CI] traces the same bulk molecular gas as CO.  
  > "We note the \[CI\](1-0) line emission is detected throughout the bridge from knot B to C, in which the peak location and distribution strongly resemble the CO line emission." ([CI] distribution); "These results seem to suggest that \[CI\] can trace the same molecular gas as CO in such a dwarf merger starburst galaxy." ([CI] distribution)
- Haro 11 has a molecular gas fraction of ~80%, significantly higher than normal SFGs at similar sub-solar metallicity and comparable to massive (U)LIRGs, driven by high molecular gas surface density.  
  > "Within these sub-solar metallicity dwarf galaxies, we note the molecular gas fraction ($\sim 80\%$) of Haro 11 is rare, significantly larger than in normal SFGs, while is similar to the fraction ($\sim 50\% - 90\%$) of (U)LIRGs." (Neutral gas)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous single-dish observations (Mopra, APEX) had large beam sizes that could not resolve the distribution of molecular gas or individual star-forming knots; archival ALMA Band 3 and 8 plus VLT/MUSE provide ~460 pc spatial resolution.  
  > "However, limited by the large beam size of the single antenna in previous studies, we cannot answer the following questions:" (Introduction); "To address these questions We collect and analyse the high-resolution sub/millimeter ALMA observation of CO($J=$1-0) (hereafter CO(1-0)) and \[CI\]($^3$P$_1 - ^3$P$_0$) (hereafter \[CI\](1-0)) emission lines and the archival optical VLT/MUSE data and investigate the starburst activities and gas consumption in Haro 11 at 500 pc scales." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Ionization-based Halpha SFR is preferred because intense starburst regions in Haro 11 have SFR >= 1 M_sun yr^-1 (where stochastic fluctuations are negligible) and trace short-timescale SFR variations in mergers; Astrodendro reliably identifies star-forming cores.  
  > "This clump-finding algorithm is based on the dendrogram and has been used to identify reliable star-forming cores in galaxies (see (cite: Li2020c) for the detailed comparison of different clump-finding packages)." (Star formation knots); "The ionization-based tracers (e.g., $\hbox{H$\alpha$}$) will be preferable since we will focus on the intense starburst regions in Haro 11, which have high SFR ($\ge 1 \  M_{\odot}\rm \ yr^{-1}$)." (Measurements of molecular gas mass and star formation rate)
- Alternative: Strong emission line metallicity diagnostics vs electron temperature method: selected the temperature-based metallicities from James et al. (2013).  
  > "(cite: james2013) and (cite: Menacho2021) mentioned the variations in spatially resolved oxygen abundances between electron temperature method and strong emission line diagnoses (cite: Marino2013) in Haro 11. Here, we select the temperature-based metallicities in (cite: james2013) and adopt the area size weighted average metallicity value of $12+ \log({\rm O/H}) \sim 8.12$." (Measurements of molecular gas mass and star formation rate)
- Alternative: Galactic conversion factor (alpha_CO = 4.3) vs metallicity-dependent conversion factor (alpha_CO = 60): Galactic value underestimates H2 mass in low-metallicity galaxies due to CO-dark gas.  
  > "The former value is smaller than our H$_2$ gas mass estimation ($3.8^{+3.3}_{-3.1} \times 10^9\  M_{\odot}$) in this work, because the conversion factor of (1 times of) Galactic value would underestimate the molecular gas mass in low-metallicity galaxies (cite: shi2016a; madden2020)." (Molecular gas properties in previous studies)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Large CO velocity dispersion in the collision/overlap region might be affected by beam-smearing.  
  > "However, the large velocity dispersion might also be caused by the beam-smearing effect." (CO(1-0) distribution and kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Discrepancy between [CI]-based and CO-based H2 masses may be due to the large adopted alpha_CO or missing flux in the [CI] observation.  
  > "This difference may be due to the larger $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ we adopted or the amount of missing flux in the $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $ observation." ([CI] distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Rebinned pixel size (0.4'') for the [CI]/CO comparison is smaller than the smoothed beam size (1.5'').  
  > "We rebinned the pixels in $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $ and CO(1-0) intensity maps as 0.4$\ensuremath{''}$. Though the pixel size is smaller than the beam sizes of CO and $[\rm C {\sc I}]\ $, it is instructive to perform such a comparison." ([CI] distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Non-detection of CO in knots A and T is limited by ALMA sensitivity, requiring deeper observations.  
  > "The exciting finding is the relatively high sSFRs within knots A and T, with no significant CO detections, limited by the ALMA detection sensitivity." (Molecular gas in knots A and T); "Much deeper observations with the ALMA telescope are needed to investigate the accurate mass distribution and kinematics of molecular gas." (Molecular gas in knots A and T)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO = 60 +/- 50) spans an order of magnitude (10 to 110), dominating the error budget of M_H2, Sigma_H2, SFE, P_turb, and alpha_vir.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a single galaxy-wide average alpha_CO (60) ignores knot-to-knot metallicity differences (12+log(O/H) = 8.25 in knot B vs 7.80 in knot C).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO(1-0) beam radius (~220 pc) is larger than typical individual GMCs, so beam-scale virial parameters and turbulent pressures average over inter-cloud gas and multiple clouds.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Maximum recoverable scales (4.2'' for Band 3, 5.3'' for Band 8) are comparable to the galaxy's R_80 (~5''), meaning extended diffuse flux may be resolved out.
