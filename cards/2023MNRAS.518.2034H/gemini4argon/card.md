## Analysis recipe

1. **Phase-centre shift, self-calibration, and continuum/line imaging**: Shift the phase centre of the Band 6 high-resolution visibilities to the centre of the continuum emission, perform phase self-calibration on the continuum data, apply the solutions to the line data, and produce cleaned images without visibility-level continuum subtraction.  
  > "The phase centre was shifted to the centre of the continuum emission and the visibilities were changed accordingly. Phase self-calibration was done for the continuum data and the results were applied to the line data." (Observations and data reduction); "In all cases of line emission imaging, continuum emission has not been subtracted at visibility level in order to preserve the possibility to account properly for its contribution in the image plane." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS/IMAGER
   - parameter: Phase centre origin: RA=09h47m33.492315s, Dec=11d25m42.9081536s  
  > "The coordinates of the origin are: RA=09h47m33.492315s and Dec=11$^\circ$25’42.9081536”." (Continuum emission)
   - assumption: Systemic velocity V_z = -0.5 km s^-1  
  > "The Doppler velocity $V_\text{z}$ spectrum is centred on a star systemic velocity of $-$0.5 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Teyssier2006), which, however, is not known to better than $\sim$$\pm$0.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Simulated visibilities for an isotropic 1/R disc of radius 0.5 arcsec with the same observational coverage; natural-weighted image brightness deviated by less than a few percent  
  > "We have checked that such strong anisotropy is not an artefact of the imaging process by simulating visibilities, for the same observational coverage, for a disc source having a radius of 0.5 arcsec and an isotropic brightness decreasing radially as 1/$R$." (Observational results); "The brightness of the image, obtained with natural weighting, was found to deviate from the source brightness by less than a few percent." (Observational results)
2. **Morpho-kinematic analysis of the expanding CO(2-1) shell and quantification of inhomogeneity**: Construct channel maps, radial intensity profiles, position-velocity (V_z vs R and V_z vs omega) maps, and distributions of the elliptical shell parameter rho = sqrt((V_z/5.5)^2 + (R/6)^2). Divide the shell into twelve 30-degree position angle sectors and compute the rms deviation from unity of the ratio of each sector's rho distribution to the mean distribution.  
  > "the data are compared with three ellipses defined as $\rho$=$\sqrt{(V_\text{z}/5.5)^2+(R/6)^2}$=1/$\sqrt{2}$, 1 and $\sqrt{2}$, respectively; they correspond to spherical shells of radius $6 \times \rho$ arcsec and expansion velocities $5.5\times\rho$ km s$^{-1}$." (CO(2-1) line emission: a radially expanding shell); "In order to quantify the inhomogeneity of the shell structure, we have compared the $\rho$ distributions of the CO(2-1) intensity measured in twelve sectors of position angle, each 30$^\circ$ wide, with the mean distribution in Figure 6." (CO(2-1) line emission: a radially expanding shell)
   - parameter: Shell mean radius = 6 arcsec, mean expansion velocity = 5.5 km s^-1, 12 sectors of 30 deg width, intervals |rho - 1| < 0.25 and |rho - 1| < 0.5  
  > "Figure 3 gives evidence for a significant enhancement of emission in a shell having a mean radius of some 6 arcsec and expanding at a mean velocity of some 5.5 km s$^{-1}$, namely having been ejected 6-7 centuries ago." (CO(2-1) line emission: a radially expanding shell); "On average, they reach 37% and 73% for \|$\rho-1$\|\<0.25 and \|$\rho-1$\|\<0.5, respectively, illustrating the importance of inhomogeneity." (CO(2-1) line emission: a radially expanding shell)
   - assumption: Distance d = 114 pc  
  > "In our analysis we use 114 pc, a value consistent with the distance obtained from the period-luminosity relation (cite: Haniff1995)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Kinematic and intensity characterization of three inner outflows (SE, SW, NW)**: Define three 3D data cube regions (SE, SW, NW) between R = 60 and 300 mas, each with volume 0.48 arcsec^2 km s^-1, and compute the integrated intensity, mean and rms Doppler velocity <V_z>, and mean and rms position angle <omega> for CO(2-1), 29SiO(5-4), SO(5_5-4_4), SO2(22_2,20-22_1,21), and SO2(13_2,12-12_1,11)/H13CN(4-3).  
  > "we define three regions, called SE, SW and NW, with angular separations from the centre of the star between 60 and 300 mas" (5.1 Observational results); "Table 4 lists the mean and rms values of the Doppler velocity and position angle distributions measured in each region for each line, together with the associated intensity." (Observational results)
   - parameter: Radial range: 60 to 300 mas; cube volume: 0.48 arcsec^2 km s^-1; SE: -5 < V_z < 2 km s^-1, 90 < omega < 180 deg; SW: -2 < V_z < 5 km s^-1, 180 < omega < 270 deg; NW: -2 < V_z < 5 km s^-1, 270 < omega < 360 deg  
  > "They have a same data cube volume of 0.48 arcsec$^2$ km s$^{-1}$ and are indicated by rectangles on the $V_\text{z}$ vs $\omega$ maps of Figures 7 to 11." (Observational results); "$-$5\<$V_\text{z}$\<2 km s$^{-1}$ | \<$\omega$\>" (Table 4); "90$^\circ$\<$\omega$\<180$^\circ$ | Rms($\omega$)" (Table 4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare rms deviations of V_z and omega in each region to a uniform distribution (~80% of uniform value)  
  > "In each region, the rms deviations of $V_z$ and $\omega$ from their mean is $\sim$80% of the value corresponding to a uniform distribution, showing that the selected regions cover well the observed outflows." (Discussion of the results)
4. **Derive relative molecular abundances in the inner outflows under LTE approximation**: Calculate the ratio of observed intensity in each region (SE, SW, NW) to the LTE factor F = A_ji (2J+1) k exp(-E_u/T) evaluated at T = 1000 K, and normalize to the 29SiO abundance as reference.  
  > "We calculate accordingly, for each of the three regions and for each line, the ratio between the observed intensity" (5.3 Discussion of the results); "We list in Table 5, for each region and for each line, the calculated ratio of the relevant molecular abundance to the $^{29}$SiO abundance chosen as reference." (Discussion of the results)
   - parameter: Reference temperature T = 1000 K; F = 8.92e-3 (CO(2-1)), 4.53 (29SiO(5-4)), 14.3 (H13CN(4-3)), 0.44 (SO(5_5-4_4)), 0.069 (SO2(22_2,20-22_1,21)), 0.124 (SO2(13_2,12-12_1,11))  
  > "the factor $F=A_{ji}(2J+1)k\text{e}^{-Eu/T}$ calculated at $T$=1000 K using the line parameters listed in" (5.3 Discussion of the results); "$F$ | 8.92$\times$10$^{-3}$ | 4.53 | 14.3 | 0.44 | 0.069 | 0.124" (Table 5)
   - assumption: LTE approximation, optically thin (neglecting absorption in 60-300 mas), T = 1000 K  
  > "In the LTE approximation, neglecting absorption, this ratio is proportional to the abundance of the excited molecule, namely to the global density in each region and to the relative molecular abundances." (Discussion of the results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check opacity by extracting spectra over the stellar disc (R < 30 mas) and in the surrounding ring (30 < R < 50 mas); compare derived ratios with single-dish literature values  
  > "To get some insight into the effect of opacity, we display in Figure 3 Doppler velocity spectra observed over the stellar disc ($R$\<30 mas) and around it (30\<$R$\<50 mas)." (Discussion of the results); "We also list, in the same table, abundances obtained from single dish observations using broad and isotropic radial distributions." (Discussion of the results)
5. **Analyze vibrationally excited SiO maser emission and geometry**: Map channel maps, PV diagrams, and Doppler velocity spectra integrated within R < 0.3 arcsec and over the stellar disc (R < 30 mas and 30 < R < 50 mas) for 28SiO(v=1,5-4), 28SiO(v=2,5-4), and 28SiO(v=1,8-7), and compare line ratios with LTE predictions.  
  > "Figure 4 (left) illustrates the peculiarity of the $^{28}$SiO($\nu$=1,5-4) emission by comparing its Doppler velocity spectrum, integrated within $R$\<0.3 arcsec, with those of the $\nu$=0 and $\nu$=2 lines: its emission is an order of magnitude more intense and it covers a much broader interval of Doppler velocities, reaching beyond $\sim$$\pm$16 km s$^{-1}$." (Vibrationally excited SiO lines); "the LTE approximation predicts the intensity of the $\nu$=1, $J$=8-7 line to be a factor $\sim$7 larger than that of the $\nu$=1, $J$=5-4 line in the optically thin limit" (Vibrationally excited SiO lines)
   - parameter: Expansion velocity = 10.6 km s^-1 (from Vlemmings 2019), observed |V_z| ~ 6 km s^-1  
  > "If the actual expansion velocity of the SiO masers seen on the $\nu$=1 line is the same as the 10.6 km s$^{-1}$ inferred for the continuum, then a $|V_z|\sim$6 km s$^{-1}$ would place the masing clumps at an inclination angle of 30-40$^\circ$ with respect to the plane of the sky." (Vibrationally excited SiO lines)
   - assumption: Actual expansion velocity of SiO maser layer equals the 10.6 km s^-1 continuum expansion velocity from Vlemmings (2019)  
  > "If the actual expansion velocity of the SiO masers seen on the $\nu$=1 line is the same as the 10.6 km s$^{-1}$ inferred for the continuum, then a $|V_z|\sim$6 km s$^{-1}$ would place the masing clumps at an inclination angle of 30-40$^\circ$ with respect to the plane of the sky." (Vibrationally excited SiO lines)

## Qualitative findings

- The NE/SW elongation of the Band 6 continuum reported previously by Fonfria et al. (2019b) was an artefact of a phase-centre offset and disappears after phase-centre correction and self-calibration, leaving a nearly circular continuum source.  
  > "However, this elongation is caused by a significant offset of the phase-centre and disappears after having corrected for it (Figure 1)." (Continuum emission)
- Within 40 mas of the star, simultaneous in-falling and outflowing gas is present, with Doppler velocities exceeding 10 km s^-1 over the stellar disc.  
  > "In summary, within 300 mas from the centre of the star, we observe a very complex morpho-kinematics giving evidence for anisotropic emission and, within 40 mas from the centre of the star, we find evidence for simultaneous presence of outflowing and in-falling gas." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Continuum emission was not subtracted in the uv-plane so that its contribution and radiative transfer/absorption effects could be properly accounted for in the image plane.  
  > "In all cases of line emission imaging, continuum emission has not been subtracted at visibility level in order to preserve the possibility to account properly for its contribution in the image plane." (Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Spiral wake from a binary companion (Fonfria et al. 2019a): mapping the V_z vs omega intensity along the presumed spiral arc in CO(2-1) shows that a simple spiral interpretation cannot be reliably supported or refuted.  
  > "However, when mapping the $V_\text{z}$ vs $\omega$ map of the intensity along such a presumed spiral (right panel of the lower row of Figure 4), one sees that one cannot make simply sense of such an interpretation." (CO(2-1) line emission: a radially expanding shell)
- Alternative: Rotating torus in the close neighbourhood of the star (Fonfria et al. 2019b): cannot be separated with confidence from the contribution of the south-western outflow.  
  > "to separate a possible effect of rotation from the contribution of the south-western outflow and evidence for a possible rotation cannot be stated with reasonable confidence." (5.3 Discussion of the results)
- Alternative: Evaporating Jovian planet orbiting at ~30 mas (Wiesemeyer et al. 2009): predicted to lie south-east of the star and blue-shifted by 4-5 km s^-1 at the epoch of observation, where no corresponding emission feature is seen.  
  > "According to their definition of the orbit parameters, such a planet would have been south-east of the star at the time of the present observations and blue-shifted by some 4-5 km s$^{-1}$, in a region where we observe no feature that might be revealing its presence" (Discussion of the results)
- Alternative: UV photodissociation as the cause of SO and SO2 confinement within ~0.15 arcsec: rejected because UV photodissociation is equally probable for SO and SiO, yet SiO extends to much larger radii.  
  > "As remarked by (cite: Massalkhi2020) photodissociation by UV radiation is equally probable for SO and SiO molecules: Figure 2 excludes an interpretation of the confinement in terms of photodissociation of the SO and SO$_2$ molecules." (Discussion of the results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The distance to R Leo is uncertain (ranging from 71 pc to 114 pc in earlier literature, with a recent measurement of 100 +/- 5 pc published after submission).  
  > "Its distance to Earth is not well measured, varying from 71 pc (cite: Gaia2018) to 114 pc (cite: vanLeeuwen2007)." (Introduction)
- Limitation (authors): The star systemic velocity (-0.5 km s^-1) is not known to better than +/- 0.5 km s^-1.  
  > "The Doppler velocity $V_\text{z}$ spectrum is centred on a star systemic velocity of $-$0.5 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Teyssier2006), which, however, is not known to better than $\sim$$\pm$0.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): The SO2(13_2,12-12_1,11) line in Band 7 is blended with H13CN(4-3) (blue-shifted by ~1.1 km s^-1), requiring an unblended HCN line for a reliable evaluation of the HCN abundance in the SE region.  
  > "The Band 7 SO$_2$-HCN line is a blend of the SO$_2$(13$_{2,12}$-12$_{1,11}$) line with the H$^{13}$CN(4-3) line, which is blue-shifted by $\sim$1.1 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the SO$_2$ line." (Discussion of the results); "This is consistent with observations in the other regions, but a reliable evaluation of the HCN abundance in the SE region would require observing the emission of other, unblended, lines." (Discussion of the results)
- Limitation (authors): Because SO and SO2 emission is confined within ~0.15 arcsec, close to the bright stellar continuum, a reliable quantitative abundance analysis requires higher angular resolution and sensitivity.  
  > "the emission of these molecules does not extend radially much beyond continuum emission: better angular resolution and sensitivity would be required for a reliable analysis." (5.3 Discussion of the results)
- Limitation (authors): The complexity and strong anisotropy of the morpho-kinematics and non-LTE chemistry prevent the development of accurate, comprehensive models, restricting interpretations to crude and approximate modelling.  
  > "But it also makes it hard to develop accurate, comprehensive models with confidence, restricting reliable interpretations to considerations based on very crude and approximate modelling." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abundance ratios in Table 5 are derived under an optically thin, single-temperature (T = 1000 K) LTE slab approximation without radiative transfer modelling, even though significant continuum absorption is demonstrated over the stellar disc and non-LTE shock chemistry is inferred for SO and SO2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Merging Band 6 observations taken across a full year (Oct 2016 to Oct 2017, spanning >1 pulsation period of 310 days) for CO(2-1) imaging without explicitly detailing proper-motion/phase-centre alignment per epoch could introduce smearing on small scales.
