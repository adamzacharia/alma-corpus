## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce ACA Band 8 data and image continuum**: Default pipeline calibration in CASA, then tclean in MFS mode with natural weighting and a 3 sigma threshold  
  > "The calibrated visibilities have been obtained by reducing the raw data using the default pipeline scripts executed in the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: 2022PASP..134k4501C)." (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations); "adopting a 3 $\sigma$ cleaning threshold (with $\sigma$ being the rms of the noise)" (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting; MFS; 3 sigma threshold  
  > "and a natural weighting scheme, in order to minimize the noise" (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure Band 8 continuum fluxes**: 2D Gaussian fit to each continuum map with imfit  
  > "We exploit the continuum maps obtained this way to derive a flux measurement by fitting a 2D Gaussian to the sources via CASA task IMFIT." (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: 10% systematic flux calibration uncertainty added in quadrature  
  > "A 10% systematic flux calibration uncertainty was added in quadrature to the nominal uncertainty." (Table ALMA Band 8 observations notes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: For PJ231-20, the combined companion and QSO flux in the high-resolution map agrees with the low-resolution measurement  
  > "In this map, the combined flux of the companion and QSO host agrees with the flux measured in the lower-resolution observation." (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements)
3. **Assemble multi-wavelength fluxes and apply corrections**: Collect literature and archival ALMA fluxes; reduce archival ALMA data with the same procedure; de-magnify J0439+1634 flux by 4.5; use 2022.1.00321.S Band 8 data to separate the PJ231-20 quasar from its companion (8.4 +/- 0.4 mJy at 398 GHz)  
  > "Here we measured for the quasar a flux of 8.4 $\pm$ 0.4 mJy at the observed frequency of 398 GHz." (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements); "we therefore scaled down the fitted flux value by the galaxy-integrated average magnification factor $\mu=4.5$" (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: Magnification of the host galaxy taken as the galaxy-integrated average factor  
  > "the galaxy-integrated average magnification factor $\mu=4.5$" (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Reduce NOEMA 158 GHz data and measure continuum**: Reduce with GILDAS, image cubes with 3 sigma threshold, natural weighting and 50 km/s channels, then measure continuum on line-free channels with the same procedure as Band 8; Gaussian fits to CO(9-8) and CO(10-9)  
  > "We imaged the cubes adopting a 3 $\sigma$ cleaning threshold, natural weighting, and a channel width of 50 km/s." (2.3 NOEMA Band 2 complementary data); "A continuum flux measurement was obtained from these maps with the same procedure adopted for ALMA Band 8 observations." (2.3 NOEMA Band 2 complementary data)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: 3 sigma threshold, natural weighting, 50 km/s channels  
  > "We imaged the cubes adopting a 3 $\sigma$ cleaning threshold, natural weighting, and a channel width of 50 km/s." (2.3 NOEMA Band 2 complementary data)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fitted at the expected line frequency  
  > "obtained by fitting a Gaussian profile to the data centered at the expected frequency of the transition" (2.3 NOEMA Band 2 complementary data)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive source angular size Omega**: Omega = pi * 9ab/(8 ln2) from the FWHM axes of a 2D Gaussian fitted to resolved images; r_e converted to FWHM for J0439+1634; physical size converted for PJ083+11  
  > "This equation corresponds to the area of an ellipse that subtends $\sim98.9 \%$ of the total volume (flux) of that Gaussian." (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements); "the angular size was computed from the physical size reported in (cite: 2020ApJ...903...34A)" (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements)
   - parameter: n = 1.58 Sersic index for the r_e to FWHM conversion  
  > "where $n = 1.58$ is the Sersic index and $k = 1.9992n-0.3271$" (2.2 Multi-wavelength data and size measurements)
   - assumption: Emitting region is an ellipse enclosing 98.9% (3 sigma) of the Gaussian flux  
  > "we defined the source size as that of an ellipse encompassing 98.9% (3$\sigma$) of the total flux (volume) of the Gaussian" (4.1 SED fitting results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Sensitivity to the size definition tested via R_sigma and the optically thin fit  
  > "we decided to investigate how changing the definition of the angular size of the source $\Omega$ impacts our results" (4.1 SED fitting results)
6. **Fit single-component modified black body SED**: Single MBB with CMB contrast and finite optical depth, free T_d, M_d and beta, sampled by MCMC  
  > "We leave as free parameter the dust temperature, the dust mass, and the emissivity index, and explore the 3D parameter space using a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm implemented in the EMCEE package" (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 45 chains, 5000 steps, burn-in of 1000 steps  
  > "We run the algorithm using 45 chains, 5000 steps and a burn-in phase of 1000 steps." (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - parameter: k0 = 0.4 cm2/gr, nu0 = 250 GHz, T0 = 2.73 K  
  > "where $k_0=0.4$ cm$^2$/gr, $\nu_0=250$ GHz, and $\beta$ is called emissivity index" (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - assumption: Spatially unresolved analysis with a single dust component  
  > "In this paper we carry out a spatially unresolved analysis, assuming that the emission arises from a single dust component" (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - fitting: emcee; uniform priors on T_d (1 to 150 K) and log Md/Msun (6 to 10); Gaussian prior on beta (mean 2.2, sigma 0.6); likelihood not stated  
  > "We assume uniform priors probability distribution for $T_d$ and $M_d$ in the ranges" (3 Methods and data analysis); "while for $\beta$ we model the prior probability distribution as a Gaussian with mean value $\mu_G=2.2$ and standard deviation $\sigma_G = 0.6$" (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - validation: Beta posterior scatter (0.3) much smaller than the prior width (0.6), so the prior has little influence; PJ158-14 did not converge with only two fluxes  
  > "the distribution of the derived emissivity indices exhibits a standard deviation much smaller than that of the adopted prior (0.3 and 0.6, respectively)" (4.1 SED fitting results); "the fitting algorithm did not converge due to the availability of only two flux measurements" (3 Methods and data analysis)
7. **Derive L_IR, SFR and optical depth at 1900 GHz**: L_IR integrated 8-1000 micron from the best-fit SED; SFR = 1.496e-10 L_IR/Lsun; tau_1900 from the fitted M_d and beta  
  > "\dfrac{SFR}{M_\odot \cdot yr^{-1}} = 1.496 \cdot 10^{-10} \dfrac{L_{IR}}{L_\odot}" (3 Methods and data analysis); "we derive the optical depths at the rest-frame frequency of 1900 GHz ($\tau_{1900}$)" (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - assumption: Kroupa IMF in the SFR conversion  
  > "which relies on the assumption of a Kroupa initial mass function (cite: 2003ApJ...598.1076K)" (3 Methods and data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Test optically thin approximation and compare with literature**: Refit under the optically thin MBB, compare T_d and M_d versus assumed source size R_sigma; plot Sigma_SFR vs Sigma_Md, T_d vs Sigma_SFR, T_d vs z, and L[CII]/L_FIR vs T_d  
  > "The subscript $thin$ refers to the quantities derived by running again the fit under the optically thin MBB emission approximation of equation" (4.1 SED fitting results)
   - software: emcee
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100 for Sigma_Md to gas mass surface density  
  > "assuming a gas-to-dust mass ratio (GDR) of 100 (cite: 2022A&A...662A..60D; 2023ApJ...954L..10F)" (4.1 SED fitting results)
   - assumption: T_CMB(z=6.5)=20.5 K in the optically thick theoretical relation  
  > "assuming a CMB temperature $T_{CMB}(z=6.5)=20.5$ K" (4.1 SED fitting results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Optically thin fit approaches the general result as the assumed size grows  
  > "increasing the assumed size causes the quantities derived in the general case to asymptotically approach those obtained under the optically thin approximation" (4.1 SED fitting results)

## Software

- **CASA** : Pipeline calibration, tclean imaging and imfit flux measurement  
  > "default pipeline scripts executed in the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: 2022PASP..134k4501C)" (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations)
- **tclean** : Continuum imaging in MFS mode  
  > "imaged running the TCLEAN task in *multi-frequency-synthesis* mode" (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations)
- **imfit** : 2D Gaussian fit to measure flux  
  > "by fitting a 2D Gaussian to the sources via CASA task IMFIT" (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations)
- **GILDAS** : Reduction and imaging of NOEMA data  
  > "by reducing the raw data using the Grenoble Image and Line Data Analysis Software (GILDAS" (2.3 NOEMA Band 2 complementary data)
- **emcee** : MCMC sampling of the MBB SED fit  
  > "Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm implemented in the EMCEE package" (3 Methods and data analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- Gaussian prior on beta has little influence on its derived values  
  > "Also, the Gaussian prior implemented in the fit has little influence on the derivation of this parameter" (4.1 SED fitting results)
- Compact size drives the optical thickness; PJ231-20 is most compact and PJ009-10 most extended  
  > "the value of their optical depths is explained by the fact that these sources are either very compact, in the case of PJ231-20 or very extended, in the case of PJ009-10" (4.1 SED fitting results)
- Dust temperatures of IR-luminous galaxies stay constant with redshift and are comparable to the QSO hosts  
  > "we find that the dust temperature appears to remain constant across all redshifts, while also being comparable to the values we derive for the QSO hosts in this study" (4.2 On the dust temperature in QSOs and galaxies across redshift)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets selected as bright at rest-frame 158 micron and lacking public Herschel/SPIRE photometry; rest-frame ~80 micron coverage is scarce  
  > "selected so that their flux at the rest-frame wavelength of 158 $\mu$m is greater than 1.5 mJy and had no public flux measurement performed by *Herschel/SPIRE*" (2.1 ALMA Band 8 observations); "given the scarce amount of data sampling the rest-frame wavelength of $\sim80$ $\mathrm{\mu m}$" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Band 8 plus Rayleigh-Jeans fluxes let the SED be fitted without assuming a T_d  
  > "allows us to pin down the IR SED of these sources without the need of the assumption of a given $T_d$" (Introduction)
- Alternative: Optically thin MBB approximation, refitted and compared; found to bias T_d low and M_d high  
  > "this approximation systematically underestimates the dust temperature and overestimates the dust mass" (4.1 SED fitting results)
- Alternative: J0439+1634 excluded from plots, discussion and averages because of strong lensing and a possibly hotter, more magnified inner component  
  > "J0439+1634 is not included in the plots, nor in general discussion of the dust physical properties of the sample or in the calculation of their average values." (4.1 SED fitting results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): All fits rely on a single Band 6 size measurement  
  > "our fits rely entirely on a single size measurement from ALMA Band 6" (4.1 SED fitting results)
- Limitation (authors): PJ158-14 has only two fluxes and the fit did not converge  
  > "the fitting algorithm did not converge due to the availability of only two flux measurements" (3 Methods and data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Contribution of AGN-heated central hot dust remains unconstrained for the rest of the sample  
  > "the contribution of this gradient to the rest of the sample remains unconstrained" (4.4 Contribution of the central AGN to surrounding dust heating)
- Limitation (authors): T_d of J0439+1634 is truncated by the 150 K prior upper limit  
  > "the confidence value we report for the dust temperature is restricted by the upper limit of 150 K that we set as a prior in the fit" (4.1 SED fitting results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-component MBB with a size derived from one band may be degenerate with tau; results depend on the Omega definition (98.9% ellipse)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mean T_d excludes the hottest source J0439+1634, which biases the sample mean low
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table lists tau_1900 = 0.02 for PJ009-10 while the text gives 0.03; the text also writes J0439+1439 in one place
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Many fluxes come from heterogeneous literature with different calibration and uv coverage; ACA Band 8 beams of several arcsec may include companions or extended emission
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Likelihood and treatment of the 10% systematic flux error in the MCMC are not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 2 to 5 photometric points per source against 3 free parameters leave little leverage
