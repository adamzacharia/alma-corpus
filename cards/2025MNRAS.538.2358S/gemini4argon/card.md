## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate, and image continuum data in Bands 3, 6, and 7, and determine disc geometry and radial profiles**: Iterative phase self-calibration of SB and SB+MB executions in CASA, flux re-scaling of MB visibilities, concatenation with LB executions, multi-scale CLEAN imaging with uv-tapering and imsmooth to a 60 mas circular beam, disc geometry estimation via visibility spread minimization and 10sigma ellipse fitting, and azimuthal averaging of brightness temperature radial profiles  
  > "We create a final Band 3 dust continuum map by cleaning the self calibrated data with a Briggs robust parameter of 0 and a Gaussian uvtaper of 27 mas × 42 mas; 0 deg, resulting in a beam size of 59 mas × 55 mas. Then, a convolution was applied using the task imsmooth in CASA to get a circular beam of 60 mas." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "The right panel is the brightness temperature radial profile (without assuming Rayleigh-Jeans approximation) for each map, computed with Gofish (Teague 2019)." (3.1 Dust continuum images); "Firstly, they were estimated by minimizing the spread of the de-projected visibilities for circular annuli in the visibility space (e.g., Isella et al. 2019)." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
   - software: CASA, Gofish
   - parameter: robust = 0.0 (Band 3, Band 6), robust = 0.5 (Band 7); circular beam = 60 mas; inc = 50.1 deg, PA = 61.4 deg; DeltaRA = 0 mas, DeltaDec = 0 mas (Band 3)  
  > "The adopted geometry of the disc at all wavelengths is fixed to inc = 50.1 deg, and PA = 61.4 deg." (3.1 Dust continuum images); "The offset of the disc centre with respect to the phase centre in the Band 3 observations is set to ΔRA = 0 mas, ΔDec = 0 mas." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
   - assumption: Full Planck function (no Rayleigh-Jeans approximation) for brightness temperature  
  > "The right panel is the brightness temperature radial profile (without assuming Rayleigh-Jeans approximation) for each map, computed with Gofish (Teague 2019)." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
   - fitting: Minimization of spread of de-projected visibilities in circular annuli, and ellipse fit in the image plane to a 10sigma iso-contour  
  > "Firstly, they were estimated by minimizing the spread of the de-projected visibilities for circular annuli in the visibility space (e.g., Isella et al. 2019)." (3.1 Dust continuum images); "Secondly, we fit an ellipse in the image plane to a 10σ iso-contour, which follows the brightest ring at 69 au." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
   - validation: Compared total Band 6 and 7 fluxes against archival IRAM PdBI, CARMA, and SCUBA measurements; verified astrometric alignment across executions via 2D Gaussian fits  
  > "We checked the astrometric alignment between different executions by creating low angular resolution images of each execution and fitting a 2D Gaussian to each dust continuum map." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "These values are consistent within the 10% flux calibration uncertainties when compared with the fluxes of 140 ± 3 mJy at 214 GHz reported by Piétu et al. (2006)" (2 OBSERVATIONS)
2. **Perform non-parametric visibility modelling with Frankenstein, fit Gaussian rings to radial profiles, and image azimuthal residuals**: Extract normalized visibilities, fit de-projected visibilities with Frankenstein, convolve radial profiles to a common 35 mas resolution, fit 5 symmetric Gaussian rings to both image-plane and Frankenstein radial profiles, and image visibility residuals (data minus Frankenstein model) in CASA  
  > "Then we model the de-projected visibilities using the non-parametric visibility fit in the Python package Frankenstein (Jennings et al. 2020)." (3.2 Frankenstein fit); "Before comparing the morphology inferred at each wavelength, the radial profiles are convolved using to the lowest angular resolution: 35 mas (5.5 au)." (3.2 Frankenstein fit); "using five symmetric Gaussian rings (grey lines). This model is chosen to account for the three main rings, the inner disc, and the extended emission beyond the outer ring." (3.2 Frankenstein fit)
   - software: uvplot, Frankenstein, Scipy, CASA
   - parameter: Frankenstein hyper-parameters: alpha = 1.3, w_smooth = 10^-1; common resolution = 35 mas (5.5 au)  
  > "The hyper-parameters used in the Frankenstein fits are α = 1.3 and w_smooth = 10^-1, which are conservative when inferring substructures from the data Jennings et al. (2020)." (3.2 Frankenstein fit)
   - assumption: Axi-symmetric disc emission for Frankenstein fit; five symmetric Gaussian components (inner disc fixed at r_0 = 0, three main rings, and extended emission)  
  > "using five symmetric Gaussian rings (grey lines). This model is chosen to account for the three main rings, the inner disc, and the extended emission beyond the outer ring." (3.2 Frankenstein fit)
   - fitting: Non-linear least squares minimization of chi-squared via scipy.optimize.curve_fit for 5-Gaussian ring profiles and linear FWHM-frequency slopes  
  > "The fit is performed using curve_fit, a non-linear least squares minimisation method based on minimising the chi-squared statistic, implemented in the scipy.optimize module (Virtanen et al. 2020)." (3.2 Frankenstein fit)
   - validation: Quantified Frankenstein resolution by fitting input delta rings at the same uv-coverage; tested sensitivity of residual maps to disc centre offsets in Appendix A  
  > "fitting the visibilities of an input delta ring at the same uv-coverage of each observation, and then measuring the FWHM of the Frankenstein output profile." (3.2 Frankenstein fit); "A Frankenstein model was calculated in both cases, and the residual maps were computed using the same imaging parameters as in Figure 4." (APPENDIX A: DISC CENTRING)
3. **Constrain radial profiles of dust temperature, dust surface density, and maximum grain size via multi-wavelength SED fitting**: MCMC fitting of multi-wavelength continuum intensities (Bands 3, 6, 7) at each radius (> 0.1 arcsec) using the radiative transfer equation including dust scattering for DSHARP and Ricci opacities  
  > "The dust property constraints are computed using the solution for the radiative transfer equation in Sierra et al. (2019); Sierra & Lizano (2020), where the contribution of the scattering opacity is taken into" (3.4 Dust properties); "The dust opacities are estimated by two models: the DSHARP opacities (Birnstiel et al. 2018), and Ricci's opacities (Ricci et al. 2010)." (3.4 Dust properties)
   - software: emcee, dsharp_opac, DustProperties
   - parameter: Parameter space: 1 K < T_d < 300 K, 10^-6 g cm^-2 < Sigma_d < 10 g cm^-2, 10 um < a_max < 30 cm; 24 walkers, 10,000 steps; sampled every 1/3 resolution element  
  > "We explore the space parameter between 1 K < T_d < 300 K, 10^-6 g cm^-2 < Σ_d < 10 g cm^-2, and 10 μm < a_max < 30 cm, and use the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) implemented in the Python library emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013)." (3.4 Dust properties); "the parameter space at each radius is explored using 24 walkers and 10,000 steps. The best fits at each radius are then used to sample the radial profile of dust properties" (3.4 Dust properties)
   - assumption: Spherical grains, grain size distribution n(a) da = a^-p da with p = 3, bulk density 1.6 g cm^-3 (DSHARP) or 0.85 g cm^-3 (Ricci), absolute flux uncertainties of 10% (Band 6, 7) and 5% (Band 3)  
  > "We assume the particle size distribution follows n(a)da = a^-p da, with p = 3." (3.4 Dust properties); "and we also consider the absolute flux uncertainty for each ALMA Band according to the ALMA proposer's guide: 10% for Band 6 and 7, and 5% for Band 3." (3.4 Dust properties); "The bulk densities for the DSHARP and Ricci’s dust grain compositions are 1.6 and 0.85 g cm" (5.2 Dust trap efficiency)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee (24 walkers, 10,000 steps); log-uniform priors on a_max and Sigma_d; prior on T_d from passively irradiated disc T_prior = (0.02 L_* / 8 pi sigma_B r^2)^(1/4) with 50 K uncertainty  
  > "The prior for the maximum grain size and dust temperature are given by a log-uniform distribution, while for the dust temperature we include a prior temperature radial profile given by the expected temperature from a passively irradiated disc" (3.4 Dust properties); "and use a wide temperature uncertainty around the prior temperature (50 K) to ensure that the prior is not dominating the posterior probability distribution." (3.4 Dust properties)
   - validation: Checked optical depths (absorption vs scattering) and compared predicted VLA Band Q (44 GHz) flux between 25 and 125 au against observed flux (0.72 mJy)  
  > "between 25 au and 125 au is approximately 0.72 mJy. The expected flux at Band Q (within the same radial extent) based on the inferred dust properties shown in Figure 6 is ∼ 0.70 mJy" (5.3 Dust opacities)
4. **Extract integrated and inner-disc fluxes and constrain non-dust (free-free) emission**: Compute total fluxes using curve of growth on image-plane radial profiles, measure Band 3 inner disc flux from peak value and CASA imfit, and extrapolate Band 6-7 dust spectral index to Band 3 to isolate free-free contribution and Band 3 to Band X spectral index limits  
  > "The total fluxes were computed using the flux curve of growth, extracting the asymptotic flux at large radii where the integrated flux oscillates only around the RMS value." (3.5 Non-dust emission); "while the Band 3 flux is estimated from the peak value in the compact inner disc region, which is consistent with the result obtained using the imfit task in CASA." (3.5 Non-dust emission); "We follow the methodology of Rota et al. (2024) to constrain the upper and lower limits of the spectral index between Band 3 and the centimetre observations in Band X in the inner disc." (3.5 Non-dust emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Inner disc spectral index alpha_B6-B7 = 2.4 +/- 0.3  
  > "The spectral index between Bands 6 and 7 is 2.4 ± 0.3 (Long et al. 2022), decreasing to α = 0.30 ± 0.37 between Bands 3 and 6." (3.5 Non-dust emission)
   - assumption: Inner disc emission in Bands 6 and 7 originates purely from dust continuum emission, and total flux at Band X is dominated by the inner disc  
  > "Assuming that the emission in Bands 6 and 7 originates from dust continuum" (3.5 Non-dust emission); "and the lower limit for the spectral index between Band 3 and Band X (where the total flux is presumably dominated by the inner disc) is α_extrap = -0.1." (3.5 Non-dust emission)

## Qualitative findings

- The ring-to-gap contrast increases with wavelength and ring widths decrease linearly with wavelength for B42, B69, and B101, consistent with dust trapping.  
  > "We found that the width of the three rings decreases linearly with frequency, and the spectral indices show local minima at the locations of the rings, consistent with dust trap models." (abstract); "The contrast between the rings and gaps clearly increases with wavelength, suggesting a dust trap origin (Pinilla et al. 2012; Long et al. 2020)." (3.1 Dust continuum images)
- No azimuthal asymmetries > 3sigma are detected in Band 3 at the Lagrangian point structures seen in Bands 6 and 7, suggesting that only smaller (sub-mm) grains populate the Lagrangian points.  
  > "No azimuthal asymmetries above 3σ were detected in the Band 3 dust continuum observations at the locations of the Lagrangian structures observed in Band 6 and 7 (Section 3.3)." (5.1 No Lagrangian structures in Band 3); "However, since this is not observed with the current sensitivity, it is likely that only smaller grains (sub-millimetre-sized), which are bright in ALMA Bands 6 and 7 (Draine 2006), are dominating the emission of the potential Lagrangian points." (5.1 No Lagrangian structures in Band 3)
- The bright unresolved inner disc emission at Band 3 has flat spectral indices consistent with free-free emission from an ionised jet or disc wind.  
  > "The inner disc shows bright and unresolved emission at 3 mm, exhibiting a spectral index of α_1.3-3mm = 0.3 ± 0.37, and α_3mm-3cm ranging from -0.1 to 0.0. These properties are consistent with free-free emission from an ionised jet or disc wind." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution (60 mas) and long-wavelength (Band 3, 3 mm) optically thin observations are needed to resolve the three rings with high contrast and break degeneracies in radiative transfer modelling that affected lower-resolution (150 mas) 7 mm studies.  
  > "However, the 150 mas (23.6 au) angular resolution, limited by the 7 mm observations, made it challenging to resolve the three rings, and analyse the radial profiles with high contrast between rings and gaps. Long-wavelength observations (λ ≳ 1.3 mm), where optically thin emission is detected, are essential for disentangling degeneracies in radiative transfer modelling" (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Non-parametric visibility fitting with Frankenstein achieves higher angular resolution (35 mas vs 60 mas CLEAN) from the longest baselines; image-plane curves of growth are used for total/inner fluxes because Frankenstein fits have larger uncertainties in the innermost 1-2 beams.  
  > "In order to exploit the highest angular resolution from the data, we extract the disc visibilities at Band 3 using the self-calibrated measurement set and a modified version of the export_uvtable function in Tazzari (2017)" (3.2 Frankenstein fit); "We did not use the intensity profiles derived from the Frankenstein fits, as constraints on the inner region can have substantial uncertainties (Jennings et al. 2020, 2022)." (3.5 Non-dust emission)
- Alternative: Disc centre offsets obtained from visibility imaginary part minimization (DeltaRA = 15.3 mas, DeltaDec = 12.9 mas) and ellipse fitting (DeltaRA = 15.7 mas, DeltaDec = -6.9 mas) were tested and rejected in favour of fixing DeltaRA = DeltaDec = 0 mas, which minimizes azimuthal residuals.  
  > "We decided to fix offset to 0 because this particular value minimize the azimuthal residuals after subtracting an axi-symmetric model (Section 3.3 and Appendix A)" (3.1 Dust continuum images); "The residual maps are shown in Figure A1, where it is clear that the residual structures are minimized at the disc phase centre. Therefore, we assume ΔRA = ΔDec = 0 when fitting the Band 3 data." (APPENDIX A: DISC CENTRING)
- Alternative: The dust evolution model combination v_frag = 10 m s^-1 and alpha = 10^-4 was discarded because it produces maximum grain sizes > 100 cm in the rings, contradicting observations.  
  > "We discard the combination of v_frag = 10 m s^-1 and α = 10^-4, as this leads to a_frag within in the rings higher than 100 cm, contradicting the observations." (4 DUST EVOLUTION MODELS FOR LKCA15)
- Alternative: Ricci dust opacities were tested alongside DSHARP opacities and disfavoured because they yield an unrealistically high gas-to-dust ratio (~2500), underestimate the 7 mm Band Q flux, and overpredict brightness temperatures in RADMC3D forward models.  
  > "The expected flux at Band Q (within the same radial extent) based on the inferred dust properties shown in Figure 6 is ∼ 0.70 mJy and 0.13 mJy for the DSHARP and Ricci's opacity models, respectively. The latter value is particularly low compared to the observed flux" (5.3 Dust opacities); "After computing the dust continuum maps of the dust evolution models using the DSHARP and Ricci's opacities, we found that the former do a better work in reproducing the observations" (6 CONCLUSIONS)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration iterations were continued only if the SNR improved by at least 5%.  
  > "The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is measured after each self-calibration iteration, and we proceed to apply additional iterations if the SNR improves by at least a factor of 5%." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
- Limitation (authors): The innermost region (within 1-2 beam sizes, < 0.1 arcsec) has larger uncertainties due to angular resolution limits and Frankenstein fitting artifacts.  
  > "We note that the innermost region (within 1–2 beam sizes) may have larger uncertainties due to angular resolution limitations or uncertainties in the Frankenstein fitting process (see Jennings et al. 2022). Consequently, conclusions drawn for this region should be interpreted with caution." (3.4 Dust properties)
- Limitation (authors): The maximum grain size in the dust evolution models scales linearly with the assumed gas surface density (0.1 M_sun); if the gas mass were an order of magnitude lower (as in Sturm et al. 2023), lower turbulence and higher fragmentation velocities would be favoured.  
  > "Therefore, if the gas mass would be one order of magnitude lower (as estimated by Sturm et al. 2023), the maximum grain size would be one order of magnitude lower, favouring models with lower turbulence and higher fragmentation velocities." (4 DUST EVOLUTION MODELS FOR LKCA15)
- Limitation (authors): The Band 3 rms noise (5.4 uJy beam^-1) is comparable to the extrapolated intensity (~5 uJy beam^-1) of the Band 6/7 Lagrangian structures if their spectral index is 3.0.  
  > "Extrapolating the clump and arc fluxes to 97.5 GHz using a spectral index of 3.0 (computed at the asymmetry locations), results in an expected intensity of ∼ 5 μJy beam^-1. This value is approximately equal to the rms noise of the Band 3 observations (Table 1), making it difficult to distinguish them from thermal noise." (5.1 No Lagrangian structures in Band 3)
- Limitation (authors): The ~3 au radial shift of the B101 ring peak between Bands 7 and 3 is smaller than the angular resolution (5.5 au), preventing robust conclusions on radial drift vs optical depth effects.  
  > "Although the peak position appears to increase with wavelength, the difference between the radial position of B101 at Bands 7 and 3 is only about 3 au (Table 3). This difference is smaller than the angular resolution and limits robust conclusions about the origin of the apparent shift" (5.2 Dust trap efficiency)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 3, the uncertainties on log10(A_i) are printed as log10(sigma_A) rather than linear uncertainties on log10(A_i) (e.g., -0.8 +/- -2.1 for Clean and 9.7 +/- 8.3 for Frankenstein), which could confuse automated comparisons.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The dust evolution models keep the gas surface density static over 1 Myr and use ad-hoc fixed Gaussian amplitudes (A_1=1, A_2=5, A_3=3) rather than self-consistently modelling planet-disc hydrodynamical gap opening.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The self-calibrated measurement sets for Band 6 and Band 7 from Long et al. (2022) and for Band 3 in this work are not hosted in a public repository (available on request), requiring full recalibration from raw ALMA archive data to reproduce from scratch.
