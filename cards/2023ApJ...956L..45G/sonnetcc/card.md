   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Dynamic range ~1,100 (rms = 0.08 mJy/beam) reached after self-calibration  
  > "A high dynamic range for the continuum image of $\sim$1,100 (rms = 0.08 mJy beam$^{-1}$) was reached after self-calibration" (Observations)
2. **Image continuum and line cubes**: tclean with Briggs weighting, robust 0.5, primary-beam correction  
  > "For imaging, we used task tclean of CASA, with a Brigg’s weighting of visibilities, and a robust parameter of 0.5 (unless otherwise noted)" (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Synthesized beam ~0.17'' x 0.08'' (PA ~ -2 deg)  
  > "resulting in a synthesized beam of $\sim 0.17''\times 0.08''$ (PA $\simeq -2^{\circ}$)" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify compact continuum sources**: Images excluding baselines shorter than 200 klambda to filter extended emission; 40 unresolved sources identified  
  > "we obtained images that exclude baselines shorter than 200 k$\lambda$, thus filtering out large-scale structures" (Dust continuum emission); "we have detected 40 compact mm-continuum sources, three of them coinciding with VLA1, VLA2, and VLA3" (abstract)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Beam 0.13'' x 0.06'' (PA = -2 deg), rms ~0.06 mJy/beam; positional uncertainty ~0.02''  
  > "with a beam size of $0.13''\times0.06''$ (PA = $-2^{\circ}$)" (Table 2); "Positional uncertainties are $\sim$0.02$''$." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with cm continuum VLA sources (R-K20); Bc[E], Bc[W], Bd, VLA4 lack mm emission  
  > "have associated continuum emission at millimeter wavelengths, further reinforcing that VLA1, VLA2, VLA3, VLA\[SW\], and VLA\[NE\] are embedded protostars" (Dust continuum emission)
4. **Estimate dust and total masses of continuum sources**: Optically thin isothermal dust mass equation M_dust = S_nu D^2 / (kappa_nu B_nu(T_dust)), then x100 gas-to-dust  
  > "assuming that it is optically thin and that the dust is isothermal within the beam size" (1.3 mm continuum sources); "assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100" (1.3 mm continuum sources)
   - parameter: kappa(1.3 mm) = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1; D = 1.3 kpc; T_dust = 50 K (300 K for VLA2)  
  > "Adopting a dust opacity $\kappa_\nu$(1.3 mm) = 0.899 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Ossenkopf94; D-R22)" (1.3 mm continuum sources); "excepting for source ID 28 (VLA2), where we assume $T_{\rm dust}$ = 300 K" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission (otherwise lower limits), gas-to-dust 100, isothermal dust  
  > "These dust mass values should be taken with caution and as a first approximation, in particular because if the emission is not optically thin they represent lower limits." (1.3 mm continuum sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with disk masses of low-mass YSOs in GGD 27 and Orion A/B  
  > "similar to the values obtained for protostellar disks of low-mass YSOs in massive star-forming regions, e.g., GGD 27 (cite: Busquet19)" (1.3 mm continuum sources)
5. **Map H2CO and SiO integrated emission, velocity field and PV diagrams**: Integrated-intensity and velocity maps of H2CO and SiO; PV diagrams along major axis (PA = -53 deg, averaging width 0.14'') and minor axis (width 0.6'') of the SiO structure; blue/red lobe maps  
  > "Figure [4](#fig:siokine)a shows the position velocity (PV) diagram of the SiO emission along the major axis of the toroid-like structure (PA = $-$53$^{\circ}$) with an averaging width of 0.14$''$" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2); "which is more apparent in the PV diagram along the minor axis of the SiO structure with an averaging width of $0.6''$" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: H2CO integrated over V_LSR ~ -4 to +23 km/s  
  > "in the velocity range $V_{\rm LSR} \simeq  -4$ to +23 km s$^{-1}$" (H2CO line emission)
   - parameter: Blueshifted SiO lobe V_LSR = -16.6 to -3.1 km/s; redshifted +23.9 to +37.4 km/s  
  > "blueshifted ($V_{\rm LSR} = -16.6$ to $-3.1$ km s$^{-1}$) and redshifted ($V_{\rm LSR}$ = +23.9 to +37.4 km s$^{-1}$)" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SiO structure compared with the maser-shell axis and large-scale CO outflow direction  
  > "This displacement is consistent with the direction observed in the CO bipolar outflow at parsec scales" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
6. **Estimate central mass and toroid mass from SiO kinematics**: Keplerian estimate from a 6 km/s velocity shift at ~130 au; boundedness test of the 27 km/s high-velocity gas at 260 au; lower-limit toroid mass from SiO critical density and observed size  
  > "those motions could be gravitationally bound by a central mass of $\sim$10 M$_{\odot}$ assuming Keplerian conditions" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2); "a central mass of around 210 M$_{\odot}$ would be required" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
   - parameter: Critical density 1.5e6 cm^-3 taken as minimum density of the toroid; toroid size ~780 au x 390 au  
  > "Assuming this critical density of SiO as the minimum density for the entire toroid-like structure and given its observed size" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation of the inner toroid; ambient V_LSR = +10 km/s  
  > "If the observed velocity shift of 6 km s$^{-1}$ is due to rotating motions at the inner parts ($\sim$130 au) of the toroid" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Central mass consistent with a B1-B0.5 star expected for VLA2  
  > "consistent with the expected mass of a B1-B0.5 espectral type star for VLA2 (cite: Shepherd01)" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
7. **Estimate density of the obstacle halting the maser shell**: Balance between thermal pressure of the clump and ram pressure of the isotropic wind: n k T_k = Mdot_w V_w / (4 pi R^2)  
  > "assuming a balance between the thermal pressure of the gas in the clump and the ram pressure of the wind that drives the shell" (A wind emerging from the toroid, interacting with a high-density gas obstacle)
   - parameter: T_k = 300 K, R = 260 au, Mdot_w and V_w from Carrasco et al. 2015  
  > "together with $T_k$ = 300 K and R = 260 au (cite: Surcis23)" (A wind emerging from the toroid, interacting with a high-density gas obstacle)
   - assumption: Result likely an upper limit: kinetic pressure only, normal shock, projected distance  
  > "is likely to be an upper limit to the actual density" (A wind emerging from the toroid, interacting with a high-density gas obstacle)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the nearby H2CO clump and outer SiO toroid location  
  > "This is a very high density, but consistent with the fact that this obstacle is located within a very high-density region" (A wind emerging from the toroid, interacting with a high-density gas obstacle)

## Qualitative findings

- H2CO is clumpy around VLA1-3 with no main clump on a protostar, while SiO is concentrated almost exclusively on VLA2  
  > "the SiO emission is strongly and almost exclusively concentrated around VLA2" (General distribution of the SiO line emission)
- Bc[E], Bc[W], Bd and VLA4 have no mm continuum, supporting that they are obscured Herbig-Haro objects  
  > "that these four objects are not embedded protostars, but obscured Herbig-Haro (HH) objects excited by the massive protostar VLA3" (Dust continuum emission)
- SiO blue and red lobes surround VLA2, indicating a wide-angle outflow incorporated into the large-scale CO outflow  
  > "indicates the presence of a wide-angle outflow associated with this massive protostar" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
- A highly collimated bipolar SiO jet NE of VLA2 is reported, likely powered by continuum source ID 32  
  > "we identify one of them (source ID 32 in Table [2](#tab:possources)) that is well-centered between the two jet lobes" (Highly collimated bipolar SiO jet)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Test the presence of the predicted toroid and study gas and dust around the massive protostars at 1.3 mm  
  > "The main goal of our ALMA observations was to test the presence of the molecular toroid around VLA2 predicted by previous works (cite: Carrasco15)" (Introduction)
- Why this method: A baseline cut separates weak compact sources from extended emission  
  > "In order to better distinguish weak compact mm continuum sources from extended emission present in the region" (Dust continuum emission)
- Alternative: Two protostars embedded in the SiO toroid (explaining the two rabbit-ear PV features) judged very unlikely  
  > "However, the fact that no other continuum source is detected, despite the high sensitivity of the data" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
- Alternative: High-velocity SiO gas as bound rotation rejected because it would require ~210 Msun; interpreted as unbound outflow  
  > "However, such a mass is not observed, leading us to conclude that these high velocities traced by the weakest SiO emission represent unbound motions" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Resolution is insufficient to resolve the inner toroid kinematics (<~0.1'')  
  > "observations at submillimetre wavelengths, with higher angular resolution than currently available in this work, are needed" (The SiO toroid surrounding W75N(B)-VLA2)
- Limitation (authors): Dust masses are first approximations and lower limits if emission is optically thick  
  > "These dust mass values should be taken with caution and as a first approximation" (1.3 mm continuum sources)
- Limitation (authors): Obstacle density is likely an upper limit  
  > "is likely to be an upper limit to the actual density" (A wind emerging from the toroid, interacting with a high-density gas obstacle)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Toroid/rotation interpretation rests on a ~6 km/s gradient over ~0.1'', about one beam width, and is not fitted with a disk or kinematic model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SiO emission traces shocks and outflows, so a toroid identification from an elongated SiO structure is not unique
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Obstacle density depends on assumed wind parameters, T_k = 300 K and projected distance, with no uncertainties propagated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Source identification criteria are not given, so the count of 40 sources is not reproducible without the selection method
