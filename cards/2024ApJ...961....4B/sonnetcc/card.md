## Analysis recipe

1. **Image and self-calibrate the four pointings**: tclean imaging with iterative masked cleaning interleaved with phase then amplitude+phase self-calibration  
  > "In summary, our standard self-calibration procedure consisted of the following steps:" (Observations and data reduction); "The initial cleaned images contained many imaging artifacts, such as radial streaks, circular patterns, and parallel “wavy" streaks." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, gaincal
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "The data were imaged using CASA version 5.7.0-134.el7 `tclean` with robust parameter set to 0.5 (cite: McMullin2007)." (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Phase self-cal solint inf, then solint int, then phase+amp solint inf  
  > "Complete a phase self-calibration with `solint` set to `inf`." (Observations and data reduction); "Self-calibrate the image with `solint` set to `int`." (Observations and data reduction); "Complete a round of phase and amplitude self-calibration with `solint` set to `inf`." (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Sgr B2 N Band 6: cleaning threshold raised to 10 sigma until amplitude self-cal, then extra phase+amplitude step with solint 15 s  
  > "Thus, we opted to increase the cleaning threshold up to ten times the noise until the amplitude self-calibration step was completed." (Observations and data reduction); "After, we cleaned to three times the noise and added an extra amplitude and phase self-calibration step with `solint` set to 15 s, which we found to produce the best results." (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that self-calibration does not shift positions or change flux; compared against a more conservative self-calibration giving flux ratio scatter up to 40% and mean ratio close to 1  
  > "We made sure that the phase self-calibration does not introduce spatial shifts and amplitude self-calibration does not change the flux erroneously." (Observations and data reduction); "This indicates that we can expect a large flux uncertainty for individual sources due to radiointerferometric imaging, but there is no systematic error in the approach we used to create the final catalogs" (A.2.4 Final result)
2. **Check line contribution to the continuum**: Extract spectra at core peak pixels in Sgr B2 N cubes, sigma-clip lines, compare mean before and after line removal  
  > "We extracted spectra at the location of the brightest pixels of the cores cataloged in this work." (Appendix: Line contribution to continuum); "We removed line emission and absorption from the spectra using the `astropy’s` implementation of sigma clipping with `sigma`=2 and `maxiters`=5." (Appendix: Line contribution to continuum)
   - software: astropy
   - parameter: sigma clipping with sigma=2, maxiters=5  
  > "We removed line emission and absorption from the spectra using the `astropy’s` implementation of sigma clipping with `sigma`=2 and `maxiters`=5." (Appendix: Line contribution to continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mean relative line contribution -4.4% (Band 3) and -1.2% (Band 6); significant changes only for SNR < 10  
  > "We find that the mean relative line contribution for all cores is $-4.4\%$ for Band 3 and $-1.2\%$ for Band 6." (Appendix: Line contribution to continuum)
3. **Extract sources with dendrograms**: Noise measured in 30 concentric annuli with 7-sigma clipping, then a separate astrodendro run per annulus on the primary-beam-corrected image  
  > "We then use 7-sigma clipping to remove most of the signal from each of the annuli and calculate the standard deviation for each region." (3.1 Source extraction); "We generate a separate dendrogram for each concentric annulus using noise estimation for input parameters." (3.1 Source extraction)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: min_value = 4 x noise, min_delta = 0.7 x noise, min_npix = 3 pixels  
  > "We set *min_value* to be equal to four times the noise estimate, *min_delta* to be 0.7 times the estimated noise, and *min_npix* to be 3 pixels." (3.1 Source extraction)
   - parameter: 30 concentric annuli, widths ~7.8" (Band 3) and 1.1" (Band 6)  
  > "The width of the concentric annuli is $\sim$$7.8$$''$ and $1.1''$ for Band 3 and Band 6 respectively." (3.1 Source extraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Combinations of annuli number, min_value, min_delta and min_npix inspected by hand for a compromise between false positives and likely positives  
  > "We inspected each combination by hand to find a compromise between reducing the number of false positives due to artifacts and noise and increasing the number of likely positives." (Footnote 1)
4. **Vet sources and build the core catalog**: Manual exclusion polygons around extended emission, cross-match with known HII region catalogs, by-eye scoring 0/1/2 using 1 mm and 3 mm morphology, spectral index map  
  > "We rank every source as not-a-core (0), core candidate (1), or core (2)." (3.1 Source extraction); "We draw polygons" (3.1 Source extraction); "To further identify regions based on their spectral properties, we create a 1 mm to 3 mm spectral index map of the central, most dense parts of Sgr B2 N and M." (3.1 Source extraction)
   - parameter: Score 1 and 2 both kept (complete catalog); robust and complete catalogs gave no significant difference  
  > "Thus, we report and use only the “complete" catalog in the rest of this work." (3.1 Source extraction)
   - assumption: A 3 mm filamentary structure without a 1 mm counterpart indicates free-free emission  
  > "Such a filamentary structure at 3 mm without a strong counterpart at 1 mm is indicative of free-free emission." (3.1 Source extraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Difference between scores 1 and 2 admitted to be subjective  
  > "We note that the difference between a score of 1 and 2 is subjective." (3.1 Source extraction)
5. **Measure source fluxes and sizes**: Integrated flux within the dendrogram leaf contour and brightest pixel; peak-to-contour flux ratio used to judge whether a source is resolved  
  > "We use the integrated flux within the dendrogram structure contour and the brightest pixel within the contour as the primary observables." (3.2 Photometry); "Thus, we use a ratio of brightest pixel flux and summed flux within dendrogram structure to serve as a metric of whether a source is resolved or not." (3.3 Source sizes)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: Aperture correction factor 1.465 for a 1-sigma aperture (used to compare with G18); frequency correction ~0.92  
  > "We calculate the aperture correction factor as the fraction of the Gaussian distribution that is cut off after using a 1-$\sigma$-sized aperture to be 1.465." (3.2.1 Photometry comparison); "the frequency correction factor is $\sim$0.92." (3.2.1 Photometry comparison)
   - assumption: Optically thick part of the core with spectral index of two for the G18 frequency correction  
  > "Assuming that the majority of the flux within the G18 beam is coming from the optically thick part of the core or an region and it has an average spectral index of two" (3.2.1 Photometry comparison)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting with gaussfit_catalog was tested and abandoned  
  > "During testing, we attempted to perform 2D Gaussian photometry using the `gaussfit_catalog` python package." (3.2 Photometry)
   - validation: Aperture photometry comparison with G18 fluxes  
  > "We compare our and G18 images by performing aperture photometry on G18 data." (3.2.1 Photometry comparison)
6. **Derive 3 mm to 1 mm spectral indexes**: Band 6 convolved to the Band 3 beam; brightest-pixel fluxes at 3 mm positions compared at both bands; limits for single-band detections  
  > "To calculate the spectral index, we take the brightest pixel values for detections at 3 mm and take the corresponding positions on the sky at 1 mm." (3.4 Spectral indexes); "In addition to calculating spectral indexes for sources present in both bands, we are also able to place upper and lower limits for sources detected only at 3 mm and 1 mm respectively." (3.4 Spectral indexes)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Errors only for sources with SNR > 10 in both bands; all catalog sources SNR > 4  
  > "We calculate the spectral index errors only for sources with SNR over 10 in both bands." (3.4 Spectral indexes); "All sources in our catalog have the SNR $>$ 4." (3.4 Spectral indexes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested using 1 mm brightest-pixel positions instead; results very similar; in-band tclean alpha maps too noisy to use  
  > "We find that using the 1 mm data’s brightest pixel locations produces very similar individual measurements, much less than the calculated errors." (3.4 Spectral indexes); "Thus, our data is not sensitive enough to produce in-band spectral indexes." (3.4 Spectral indexes)
7. **Compute lower-limit core masses and fit the core mass function**: Optically thin modified blackbody in the Rayleigh-Jeans limit, M = T_B d^2 / (kappa T_KE), applied to total dendrogram leaf flux with aperture correction; power law fit above completeness limit  
  > "Using a number of assumptions about the properties of a core, including that the dust is optically thin, we can derive the mass of such a core." (3.5 Source masses); "We then incorporate an aperture correction factor for the source flux." (3.5 Source masses)
   - software: plfit
   - parameter: Uniform core temperature T_KE = 50 K  
  > "Thus, in this work we take the average core temperature to be 50 K." (4.1.1 Protostellar cores)
   - parameter: Distance 8.4 kpc; 8.127 kpc would give 7% lower masses  
  > "Using the most recently measured distance of 8.127 kpc (cite: GRAVITYCollaboration2018) would result in 7% lower masses." (Footnote 3)
   - parameter: Dust opacity kappa(92 GHz) = 0.0017 cm^2/g, kappa(226 GHz) = 0.0083 cm^2/g, beta_dust = 1.75  
  > "as a power law, assuming $\beta_{\text{dust}} = 1.75$, we obtain $\kappa_{92 GHz} = 0.0017\ \frac{cm^2}{g}$ and $\kappa_{226 GHz} = 0.0083\ \frac{cm^2}{g}$" (3.5 Source masses)
   - parameter: Completeness threshold 6 sigma (13.5 Msun); also 8 sigma (18 Msun)  
  > "The core mass distributions have the turnover point around our completeness threshold over the whole imaged area at 6$\sigma$ (13.5 M$_\odot$)." (3.5 Source masses); "If we increase the fitting threshold to 8$\sigma$ (18 M$_\odot$)" (3.5 Source masses)
   - assumption: Dust optically thin (so masses are lower limits); 3 mm masses adopted as lower limits  
  > "In addition, since the cores are at least partially optically thick, we can obtain only a lower limit on their dust masses." (3.5 Source masses); "Thus, we use the masses based on the 3 mm data as the lower limits in the rest of the paper." (3.5 Source masses)
   - fitting: Maximum-likelihood power-law fit with plfit to masses above completeness limit  
  > "Using `plfit`, a Python implementation of the general power law fitting algorithm (cite: Clauset2009), we fit a power law to the masses above the completeness limit." (3.5 Source masses)
   - validation: Compared fitted slope with Kroupa IMF slope and tested a higher fitting threshold  
  > "The resulting fit of 191 sources is $\alpha = 2.4\pm 0.1$, which is consistent with the Kroupa IMF $\alpha = 2.35$." (3.5 Source masses)
8. **Test the nature of the sources (Stage 0/I vs other options)**: Simple model of an optically thick sphere inside optically thin dust for expected spectral indexes; Stromgren-sphere test for HII regions; free-fall time and Bonnor-Ebert mass for prestellar nature; mass extrapolation for Stage II  
  > "To test our assumptions, we create a simple model of our observed sources." (4.1.1 Protostellar cores); "Using a Strömgren sphere as an approximation, we know that:" (4.1.2 regions?); "We can further estimate the effects of the high-pressure environment using the Bonnor-Ebert mass:" (4.1.3 Prestellar cores?)
   - parameter: Average dust temperature 50 K, gas-to-dust ratio 100, gas column ~3e24 cm^-2  
  > "Assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100, we represent the dust surface density using gas column density, with an average over the Sgr B2 N and M cluster of around $3\times10^{24}$ $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ (cite: Sanchez-Monge2017)." (4.1.1 Protostellar cores)
   - assumption: Magnetic fields do not contribute to core collapse support  
  > "Thus, in this work we assume that the magnetic fields do not contribute to the core collapse support." (4.1.3 Prestellar cores?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Conclusion: sources are most consistent with Stage 0/I YSOs; very few HII regions contaminate; no prestellar cores  
  > "Thus, we claim that there are very few to none regions contaminating our catalog." (4.1.2 regions?)
9. **Infer total stellar mass and star formation rate**: Sum core masses above completeness limit within cluster radii, extrapolate the Kroupa IMF down to 0.3 Msun, add HII regions at 45 Msun each, divide by cluster age  
  > "We take the calculated masses from Section [3.5](#sec:source_masses) and extrapolate the low-mass end of the Kroupa initial mass function (cite: Kroupa2001) to obtain the core-based inferred stellar mass." (4.2 Inferred stellar mass); "Taking the star-forming age of the cloud as 0.74 Myr (cite: Kruijssen2015), we estimate the star formation rate (SFR) for each cluster." (4.2 Inferred stellar mass)
   - parameter: Cluster radii 0.4 pc (N) and 0.5 pc (M); 6 sigma completeness 0.3 mJy (M), 0.2 mJy (N); upper core limit 20 Msun  
  > "we take the radii of Sgr B2 N and M clusters to be 0.4 pc and 0.5 pc, respectively." (4.2 Inferred stellar mass); "We take 6$\sigma$ as our completeness limit within the cluster boundaries" (4.2 Inferred stellar mass)
   - assumption: One third of a core mass ends up in a star; each HII region powered by a >20 Msun star with IMF-weighted mean 45 Msun  
  > "We further assume that roughly one third of a core mass will end up in a star (cite: Lada2003)." (4.2 Inferred stellar mass); "Assuming that each region is powered by a $>$ 20M$_\odot$ star, the IMF-weighted average mass of such star is 45M$_\odot$." (4.2 Inferred stellar mass)

## Qualitative findings

  > "The sources seen at low resolution are observed to fragment into an average of two objects." (abstract)

  > "Most of the sources we report are marginally resolved and are at least partially optically thick." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Follow-up at higher resolution to the earlier ALMA Band 3 survey of the region  
  > "We report follow-up ALMA observations to ALMA project 2013.1.00269.S" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Fully automated source detection is inefficient in this high dynamic range, complex field, so dendrograms plus manual vetting were used  
  > "Combining this with the irregular structure of the observed objects makes fully automated source detection inefficient." (3.1 Source extraction); "Therefore, we utilized the automated source-finding dendrogram algorithm (cite: Rosolowsky2008) augmented with a by-hand approach described below." (3.1 Source extraction)
- Alternative: 2D Gaussian photometry rejected in favor of dendrogram contours  
  > "Thus, we concluded that our sources are not well-represented by simple 2D Gaussians and decided to use dendrogram contours to define sources." (3.2 Photometry)
- Alternative: Mosaic gridder for the overlapping Band 3 pointings rejected  
  > "We find no significant benefit of imaging the two pointings together with mosaic gridder when compared to using standard gridder on each pointing individually." (A.3 Imaging overlapping pointings)
- Alternative: RMS-based clean masks replaced by hand-drawn polygons  
  > "At first, we tried to use RMS-based masks." (A.1 Cleaning and self-calibration testing)
- Alternative: More conservative self-calibration tested but gives 20% higher noise  
  > "With the self-calibration approach used in this paper, we are able to achieve 20% lower noise compared to the more conservative self-calibration." (A.2.4 Final result)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration stopped at the step where noise stopped improving; extra step added only for Sgr B2 N Band 6  
  > "Running this extra self-calibration step on the other pointings did not reduce the noise in the images." (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Hand-drawn exclusion polygons can remove true sources  
  > "Excluding detections within polygons that were drawn by eye can potentially exclude true positives." (3.1 Source extraction)
- Limitation (authors): Local imaging artifacts (negative bowls, wave patterns) remain after self-calibration  
  > "Even after extensive self-calibration and cleaning, the data still include local artifacts such as negative bowls around bright sources and wave-like patterns on different scales." (3.1 Source extraction)
- Limitation (authors): Masses are lower limits and may be underestimated, especially in crowded regions  
  > "Thus, even with this correction some masses might be underestimated." (3.5 Source masses)
- Limitation (authors): Core scoring (1 versus 2) is subjective  
  > "We note that the difference between a score of 1 and 2 is subjective." (3.1 Source extraction)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single assumed T = 50 K and optically thin kappa for all cores; masses are lower limits by construction and the mass function slope depends on this.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 60% of 3 mm sources lie in the 1 mm field, so spectral-index conclusions are extrapolated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Hand scoring of cores and hand-drawn masks make the catalog hard to reproduce exactly.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux scatter up to 40% per source from self-calibration choices; no ALMA absolute flux uncertainty quoted.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass function compared with Kroupa IMF without a completeness-corrected treatment of fragmentation or unresolved multiplicity.
