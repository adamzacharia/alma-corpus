## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce ALMA data and extract basic data products**: Reduce ALMA data with standard CASA pipeline subroutines and extract total CO(1-0) emission, moment maps, and PVDs as in H21.  
  > "We use standard CASA (Common Astronomy Software Applications) pipeline subroutines (cite: mcmullin07) to reduce the ALMA data." (Sample selection and data); "We then extract total CO(1-0) emission (and total molecular gas masses), moment maps and position velocity diagrams (PVDs) as described in H21." (Sample selection and data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: synthesised beam = 1'', spectral resolution ~ 10 km/s  
  > "These observations were done in Band 3 with a synthesised beam of 1$''$ ($\approx 0.5$ kpc - $1$ kpc) and a spectral resolution of $\approx$ 10 ${\rm km~s}^{-1}$, with an on-source integration time between 45-53 minutes for each object." (Sample selection and data)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m0 = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "Throughout this paper we adopt a standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $H_0=70\ $${\rm km~s}^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega _{m_0}=0.3$, $\Omega _{\Lambda}=0.7$ and a (cite: chabrier03) IMF." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **3D kinematic modelling of CO(1-0) data cubes**: Fit simulated 3D interferometric data cubes to ALMA CO(1-0) cubes using multiple candidate surface brightness profiles combined with an arctangent velocity curve.  
  > "The KinMS tool allows us to create simple simulated interferometric data cubes by defining arbitrary surface-brightness and velocity profiles." (Kinematic Modelling); "For each object, we fit multiple different surface brightness profiles and combinations of surface brightness profiles with the arctan velocity profile (i.e. exponential disc, exponential disc + central hole, Gaussian ring, two exponential discs, exponential disc + Gaussian ring, two Gaussian rings)." (Kinematic Modelling)
   - software: KinMS, KinMS_fitter, GAStimator
   - parameter: Position angle, RA centre, Dec centre, velocity centre, inclination, total flux, velocity dispersion, surface brightness parameters, arctan velocity parameters (V_max, R_turn); total flux fixed for GAMA228432, GAMA238125, and GAMA574200.  
  > "In addition to the parameters associated with the surface brightness profiles and arctan velocity profile, we also fit the position angle, RA centre, Dec centre, velocity centre, inclination, total flux and velocity dispersion of the gas (see Appendices [7](#appendix: corner) & [8](#appendix: params))." (Kinematic Modelling); "However, in the cases of GAMA228432, GAMA238125 and GAMA574200, we fix the total flux parameter in the models to our initial guess (calculated by integrating the flux of the cube) due to overestimation of this parameter when the gas profiles are significantly asymmetric." (Kinematic Modelling)
   - assumption: Gas is dynamically cold and relaxed, symmetric about spatial and spectral kinematic centres, following an arctangent rotation curve.  
  > "Intrinsically, KinMS assumes that the gas in our observations is dynamically cold and relaxed, so the models are subsequently symmetric about their spatial and spectral kinematic centres." (Kinematic Modelling); "In order to capture the rotation-dominated gas, we use an arctangent velocity curve in each case." (Kinematic Modelling)
   - fitting: Initial least-squares fit for first guesses, followed by Python MCMC Gibbs-sampler with adaptive stepping (100000 runs) using boxcar priors.  
  > "It also provides an interface to the GAStimator package, which implements a `Python` MCMC Gibbs-sampler with adaptive stepping employed to fit the mock interferometric data cubes generated by KinMS, with predefined surface-brightness and velocity profiles, to the original data cubes." (Kinematic Modelling); "Prior to using the MCMC fitting process, we perform a least squares fit to the data in order to obtain first guesses at the model parameters." (Kinematic Modelling); "Presenting all initial and final parameters from our fitting procedure and the boxcar priors used in each case." (KinMS Parameters)
   - validation: Best-fit surface brightness model selected by minimising the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC); convergence checked via corner plots.  
  > "Once we have implemented MCMC fits using all prospective surface brightness models, we select a best-fit model that minimises the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) (i.e. the model with the greatest likelihood with respect to a penalty incurred for larger numbers of parameters)." (Kinematic Modelling); "In addition, we find well-converged solutions using our pure rotation models for each object (see Appendix [7](#appendix: corner))." (Major Axis Position Velocity Diagrams)
3. **Derive stellar circular velocity curves via Multi-Gaussian Expansion (MGE)**: Mask dust regions by eye in HSC r-band images, fit MGE surface brightness models, circularise components for edge-on deprojection, and compute circular velocity curves with upper/lower limits by including/excluding unresolved central components.  
  > "Our MGE models are derived using the `MgeFit` package of (cite: cappellari02)." (Circular Velocity); "Having obtained MGE surface brightness models for the r-band photometry of our galaxies, we extract circular velocity curves using the `mge_vcirc` function in the `JamPy` package (cite: cappellari08)." (Circular Velocity); "For the lower limits, we exclude MGE components that have $\rm \upsigma _i \lesssim \upsigma_{PSF}$, where $\rm \upsigma _{i}$ are the dispersions of the MGE Gaussian components and $\rm \upsigma _{PSF}$ is the dispersion of the HSC PSF (see Figure [3](#fig: mge))." (Circular Velocity)
   - software: MgeFit, JamPy
   - parameter: Circularised MGE input [S_i, sigma_i * sqrt(q_obs,i), 1]  
  > "Using the method outlined in (cite: cappellari09) and (cite: smith19), we circularise the MGE model components by transforming the input to `JamPy` from $[S_i,\upsigma _i,q_{obs,i}]$ $[S_i,\upsigma _i \sqrt{q_{obs,i}},1]$, where $S_i$, $\upsigma _i$ and $q_{obs,i}$ are the surface brightness, width and axis ratio of the $i^{th}$ Gaussian component respectively." (Circular Velocity)
   - assumption: Constant mass-to-light ratio (M/L) from Bell & de Jong (2001) colour calibration using SDSS DR16 g and r magnitudes (log M/L = -0.66 + 1.222(B-V) +/- 0.1 dex); dust acts as a foreground screen.  
  > "The `mge_vcirc` function assumes a constant mass-to-light ratio (M/L) within a galaxy, which we derive for each galaxy using the colour calibration outlined in (cite: bell01):" (Circular Velocity); "We also perform a first-order dust-correction of the r-band images by masking pixels containing dust regions by-eye; assuming the dust acts as a screen in front of the stellar emission (cite: carollo97; cappellari02)." (Circular Velocity)
   - fitting: MGE fitting via MgeFit  
  > "Our MGE models are derived using the `MgeFit` package of (cite: cappellari02)." (Circular Velocity)
   - validation: Visual comparison of original HSC r-band photometry contours with output MGE surface brightness model.  
  > "An example of the MGE models produced by implementing the `MgeFit` package on the r-band photometry on our objects is given in Figure [3](#fig: mge), where we contrast the original photometry for GAMA106389 with the output surface brightness model." (Circular Velocity)
4. **Extract and compare major- and minor-axis PVDs and compute V/sigma**: Rotate ALMA cubes and 2D SAMI maps by KinMS position angle and centre on kinematic centre; extract major-axis PVDs (2 kpc slit) and minor-axis PVDs (r_e slit), compute normalised data-model residuals, and calculate V/sigma from KinMS parameters.  
  > "We extract our PVDs from the original ALMA cubes by rotating the spatial axes by the position angle found by our KinMS fitting procedure (see Section [3.1](#subsec: Kin)) and centred on the kinematic centre also found using the results from our fit. ## Qualitative findings

- Four of the seven modelled outflow-type galaxies possess ring-type or central-hole molecular gas distributions indicative of resonance systems (such as bars or minor mergers), while the remaining three are best fit by exponential discs.  
  > "Four of the seven outflow-type objects we model with `KinMS` appear to possess ring-type gas distributions, which are indicative of a resonance system." (Summary and Conclusions)
- The bulk of the molecular gas in the sample is dynamically cold and rotation-dominated (V/sigma >> 1, sigma_vel <= 30 km/s except for unmodelled flaring in GAMA593680), tracing the stellar potential derived from MGE models.  
  > "The bulk of the gas in our objects is consistent with our dynamically stable models. Furthermore, the gas rotation generally traces the velocity curves generated from MGE models derived from r-band photometry." (Summary and Conclusions); "The whole sample falls consistently in the very highly rotation-dominated regime (i.e. $\rm V/\upsigma>>1$), implying again that the molecular gas in these objects is largely dynamically cold and rotation-supported." (Dynamically Cold Discs)
- Minor flux asymmetries are present in the major-axis PVDs of the outflow-type galaxies, but their magnitude falls within the 25-75% quartiles of a resolution-matched PHANGS-ALMA control sample (except GAMA618906), and there is little evidence of velocity asymmetry.  
  > "In all cases, and especially for the high mass galaxies, this analysis reveals little evidence for velocity asymmetries." (Dynamically Cold Discs); "We find that our objects fall within the inner (i.e. 25-75%) quartiles of the high-$\rm M_*$ and low-$\rm M_*$ KDE profiles extracted from PHANGS-ALMA, with the exception of GAMA618906 in the high-$\rm M_*$ distribution." (Dynamically Cold Discs)
- Two objects (GAMA228432 and GAMA593680) exhibit a twisting feature in their minor-axis PVDs indicative of radial gas motion in the plane of the disc, while the rest of the sample shows no clear radial motion.  
  > "We note a clear “twisting” feature in only two of our objects; GAMA228432 and GAMA593680." (Radial Gas Motion); "However, we note that we do not see evidence of radial gas motion in the remainder of our sample." (Summary and Conclusions)
- No direct evidence of extra-planar molecular gas entrained in the ionised winds is detected at the sensitivity of the observations.  
  > "In our investigation, while we do not find direct evidence of extra-planar molecular gas entrained in the ionised winds of these objects, we do find evidence that that gas is more centralised and provide a potential mechanism for gas to be driven inwards in a subset of these galaxies." (Molecular Gas Outflows)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA CO(1-0) observations at ~1 kpc and ~10 km/s resolution trace the molecular gas kinematics in SAMI-selected edge-on galaxies known to host large-scale ionised outflows.  
  > "We investigate the kinematics of the molecular gas in a sample of seven edge-on (i>60$^\circ$) galaxies identified as hosting large-scale outflows of ionised gas, using ALMA CO(1-0) observations at $\sim$ 1 kpc resolution." (Abstract)
- Why this method: Full 3D kinematic modelling with KinMS is required to overcome projection and beam-smearing effects in highly inclined galaxies and to pinpoint the kinematic centre for PVD extraction.  
  > "By finding the kinematic centre of our objects through modelling, we can more accurately centre the PVDs and assess kinematic features. Due to the high inclination of our objects, we have to use full 3D kinematic modelling to overcome projection effects." (Kinematic Modelling)
- Alternative: Multiple candidate surface brightness profiles (exponential disc, exponential disc + central hole, Gaussian ring, two exponential discs, exponential disc + Gaussian ring, two Gaussian rings) were tested for each galaxy and rejected if they did not minimise the BIC.  
  > "For each object, we fit multiple different surface brightness profiles and combinations of surface brightness profiles with the arctan velocity profile (i.e. exponential disc, exponential disc + central hole, Gaussian ring, two exponential discs, exponential disc + Gaussian ring, two Gaussian rings)." (Kinematic Modelling); "Once we have implemented MCMC fits using all prospective surface brightness models, we select a best-fit model that minimises the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) (i.e. the model with the greatest likelihood with respect to a penalty incurred for larger numbers of parameters)." (Kinematic Modelling)
- Alternative: Fitting a radial bar-flow model to GAMA228432 was attempted/considered but rejected because its lower S/N could not constrain the additional parameters.  
  > "In order to test the hypothesis that the molecular gas in our two objects where we identify “twisting” features in their PVDs is undergoing radial motion, we re-model the highest S/N object of the two, GAMA593680, with a radial bi-symmetric bar-flow component (we are unable to produce a compelling solution with the lower S/N object GAMA228432 due to the number of parameters required)." (Radial Gas Motion)
- How they decided it was enough: Best-fit surface brightness and radial-flow models were selected by minimising the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) across 100,000 MCMC runs.  
  > "Once we have implemented MCMC fits using all prospective surface brightness models, we select a best-fit model that minimises the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) (i.e. the model with the greatest likelihood with respect to a penalty incurred for larger numbers of parameters)." (Kinematic Modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Two targeted galaxies (GAMA31452 and GAMA567624) could not be modelled because CO(1-0) was either not detected or the gas was too diffuse.  
  > "Additional notes: Where rows are left blank, we either had no detection of CO(1-0) for the objects or the gas was too diffuse to model." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N in several objects (GAMA228432, GAMA238125, GAMA574200, GAMA618906) limits the ability to discern subtle kinematic features or fit multi-parameter radial flow models.  
  > "GAMA238125: we do not observe asymmetry in this object, but we note there are deviations from the minor-axis PVD extracted from the model. The low S/N of this object may restrict our ability to discern kinematic features clearly." (Minor Axis PVDs); "(we are unable to produce a compelling solution with the lower S/N object GAMA228432 due to the number of parameters required)" (Radial Gas Motion)
- Limitation (authors): High inclination (edge-on) prevents direct optical identification of stellar bars and requires circularising MGE models to bypass JamPy deprojection inclination limits.  
  > "As all of the objects in our sample are highly inclined ($i>60^\circ$), the disc inclination parameter in most cases falls below the lower limit of the inclination prior built into `JamPy` when analytically deprojecting our 2D MGE models into 3D space (cite: baldry18)." (Circular Velocity); "We are unable to identify whether these objects have optical bars due to their high inclination." (Radial Gas Motion)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size and observation depth limit firm statistical conclusions and cannot rule out molecular gas outflows or radial inflows below the sensitivity limit.  
  > "These findings are, however, subtle and if there is molecular gas being expelled by the ionised wind, it is at levels below the sensitivity of our observations." (Molecular Gas Outflows); "The conclusions of this study are limited by sample size and depth of the observations, but mostly by the high inclination of the galaxies, as required by the diagnostics of (cite: ho16) to identify ionised gas winds." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA project code in the Acknowledgements (S/JAO.ALMA#2011.0.01234.S) is the standard template placeholder rather than the actual Cycle 5 project code (2017.1.00601.S).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In edge-on galaxies, ring distributions and central holes produce linear (solid-body) major-axis PVD traces due to line-of-sight projection, making it degenerate whether inner velocity discrepancies with MGE curves are purely geometric or dynamical.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The KinMS models were only performed on the outflow-type galaxies and not on the 7 ALMA control galaxies from H21, so the incidence of ring profiles or minor-axis PVD twisting is not directly compared against the matched SAMI control sample.
