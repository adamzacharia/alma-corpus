## Analysis recipe

1. **Make line and continuum images**: Clean calibrated ALMA products with three Briggs weighting schemes; average native channels  
  > "We also used CASA (v5.4.0-70) to process and clean the ALMA products." (ALMA observations and data processing); "The cleaning algorithm was run using three different Briggs weighting schemes for the *uv* visibility plane" (ALMA observations and data processing)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Robust = 0.5; robust = 2; robust = 2 with taper to approximately twice the untapered resolution  
  > "robust parameter set to 0.5 (nearly half-way between uniform and natural weighting)" (ALMA observations and data processing); "robust parameter set to 2 (similar to natural weighting)" (ALMA observations and data processing); "robust parameter set to 2 and a *uv*-taper with a resolution approximately twice that provided by scheme (2)." (ALMA observations and data processing)
   - parameter: Approximately 50 km s−1 channels; cleaning threshold 2 σ_rms, lowered to 0.5 σ_rms for W0410–0913 and W0831+0140  
  > "The native channels were averaged to a width of $\sim$ 50 km$\,$s$^{-1}$" (ALMA observations and data processing); "The line and continuum cubes were cleaned down to a level of 2 $\sigma_{\rm rms}$ over the general background" (ALMA observations and data processing); "W0410–0913 and W0831+0140 had to be cleaned down to a levels of 0.5 $\sigma_{\rm rms}$, respectively" (ALMA observations and data processing)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect residuals for dirty-beam patterns; deeper cleaning required for two sources  
  > "patterns from the dirty beam were still clearly identifiable in the residual images when cleaning down to 2.0 $\sigma_{\rm rms}$." (ALMA observations and data processing)
2. **Measure integrated line and continuum emission**: Subtract continuum in the uv plane, extract aperture spectra, fit Gaussian lines and a first-order continuum polynomial; also directly integrate the line and collapsed continuum cube  
  > "the cubes were continuum-subtracted in the *uv* plane using representative frequency ranges from the continuum SPW adjacent to the reference SPW" (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes); "The emission line profile was fitted by a Gaussian function with the central position, peak intensity and FWHM as free parameters." (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes); "The second measurement is derived from a first-order polynomial fit to the extracted spectrum of the entire cube and interpolated to the central wavelength" (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes)
   - parameter: Extraction diameter 1″; W0410–0913: 2.5″; centered on emission peak  
  > "an aperture of 1$''$  diameter centered at the peak of the galaxy emission (an aperture of 2.5$''$  was used for W0410–0913 due to its large extent)." (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes)
   - parameter: Direct line integration within ±2.5 σ of the fitted peak  
  > "directly integrating the observed continuum-subtracted spectrum over a frequency range defined by $\pm\,$2.5$\sigma$ from the line’s peak frequency as obtained from the fit" (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes)
   - assumption: For W0410–0913, fix the Gaussian center to the CO redshift and fit only the available blue wing  
  > "the fit to the line was performed using the available portion of the profile and fixing the central position of the Gaussian to the redshift obtained from the CO emission line" (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes)
   - fitting: Gaussian line: center, peak intensity and FWHM free; first-order polynomial continuum  
  > "The emission line profile was fitted by a Gaussian function with the central position, peak intensity and FWHM as free parameters." (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes); "a first-order polynomial fit to the extracted spectrum of the entire cube" (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes)
   - validation: Two continuum measurements agree within uncertainties; re-derived W2246–0526 quantities agree with earlier work  
  > "These measurements can be found in Table" (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes); "and are all consistent within the uncertainties." (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes); "All the newly derived quantities are within the uncertainties of those given in the previous work." (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes)
3. **Measure core sizes and extended emission**: Fit beam-deconvolved 2D Gaussians; scale and subtract the clean PSF; measure residual flux fractions and elliptical radial profiles  
  > "The intrinsic sizes of the line and continuum emission were measured by fitting a 2D-Gaussian to the sources, from which the FWHM of the beam is subtracted in quadrature." (Sizes and extended structure); "iteratively scaling the well-defined, clean PSF image of the observations to the \[C ii\]  and dust continuum images until finding the ratio that minimizes the dispersion in the central region of the residual map" (Sizes and extended structure); "increasingly expanding ellipse-shaped rings with the same major-to-minor axis ratio and position angle (P.A.) as the best 2D-Gaussian fit to the source." (Sizes and extended structure)
   - parameter: PSF-scaling objective evaluated inside an ellipse with PSF semi-major and minor axes  
  > "within an aperture defined by an ellipse with semi-major and minor axes equal to those of the PSF." (Sizes and extended structure)
   - parameter: Extended fraction: residual/original flux in the same manually selected circular aperture; avoid W2246–0526 companion 1″ north-east  
  > "the fraction of the flux remaining in the residual image, measured in a fixed circular aperture equal to that used in the original image." (Sizes and extended structure); "The aperture is set manually for each Hot DOG with the goal of encompassing the total flux" (Sizes and extended structure); "The only exception is W2246–0526, for which we defined an aperture that purposefully avoids the companion galaxy located 1$''$  north-east of the central Hot DOG." (Sizes and extended structure)
   - assumption: Gaussian sizes represent core emission only  
  > "these luminosity-weighted Gaussian fits are representative only of the *core emission* of the Hot DOGs." (Sizes and extended structure)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian sizes; iterative PSF scaling minimizes central residual dispersion  
  > "fitting a 2D-Gaussian to the sources, from which the FWHM of the beam is subtracted in quadrature." (Sizes and extended structure); "finding the ratio that minimizes the dispersion in the central region of the residual map" (Sizes and extended structure)
   - validation: Repeat extended-continuum comparison at a common 0.5″ tapered resolution  
  > "if we use a *uv*-tapering of 0.5$''$  to place all the cubes on a common beam size" (Spatial distribution and extended emission)
4. **Map velocities and assess rotation support**: Compute moments 1 and 2; for sources without successful modeling estimate maximum projected velocity from moment-1 maps and dispersion from integrated FWHM  
  > "The velocity and dispersion maps of the line were obtained by computing the moment 1 and 2 statistics of the continuum-subtracted cubes." (Measurement of line and continuum fluxes); "visual inspection of the velocity field maps" (Rotation or dispersion supported?); "$\sigma_V$ is calculated as FWHM$_{\rm [CII]}$/2.355." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
   - parameter: |Vmax| = (|V+| + |V−|)/2; velocities per resolution element at SNR = 3–4 contours  
  > "$|V_{\rm max}|$ is calculated as $(|V_{\rm +}|$ + $|V_{\rm -}|)$/2" (Rotation or dispersion supported?); "maximum positive and negative projected mean velocities per resolution element identified in the moment 1 line maps, evaluated at the SNR = 3–4 contours" (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
   - assumption: |Vmax|/σV > 1 indicates rotation support; < 1 indicates dispersion/turbulence support  
  > "supported by rotation, when $|V_{\rm max}|$/$\sigma_V$ $>$ 1, or by dispersion/turbulence, when $|V_{\rm max}|$/$\sigma_V$ $<$ 1." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare map-based velocities with modeled velocities; map estimates appear underestimated by approximately a factor of 2  
  > "the projected $|V_{\rm max}|$ measured in the moment 1 maps seem to be systematically underestimated with respect to the modeled rotation velocities by a factor of $\sim$ 2." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
5. **Fit resolved gas kinematics**: SEARCH 3D signal masks followed by 3DBarolo tilted-ring fits to W0220+0137 and W2246–0526  
  > "After trimming, these cubes were fit with tilted ring models using the code $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo (cite: DiTeodoro2015)" (Kinematic modeling); "First, the line emission in each cube was identified using the `SEARCH` algorithm in BBarolo, which is based on the code DUCHAMP (cite: Whiting2012)." (Kinematic modeling); "the two sources for which the angular resolution and quality of the data allow such a detailed characterization: W0220+0137 and W2246–0526." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
   - software: 3DBarolo, SEARCH, 3DFIT, CASA, im.moments, im.fitcomponents
   - parameter: Robust = 2, no uv-taper; only line SPW; central 200 × 200 spaxels  
  > "considering natural weighting –robust parameter equal to 2– and no $uv$-tappering" (Kinematic modeling); "containing only the line SPW, were spatially trimmed to the central 200 $\times$ 200 spaxels, centered on the galaxy." (Kinematic modeling)
   - parameter: Ring width = restoring-beam minor-axis FWHM/2.5; maximum radius = morphological major-axis FWHM  
  > "the width of each ring is set to the FWHM of the minor axis of the restoring beam divided by 2.5, while the maximum radius of the model is set to the morphological major axis FWHM." (Kinematic modeling)
   - parameter: Fixed morphological center; initial dispersion = moment-2 maximum; initial inclination 45°, allowed 10–80°  
  > "The central position of all model rings is fixed to the morphological central position, while the initial guess for the velocity dispersion is the maximum value in the moment 2 map" (Kinematic modeling); "The inclination is allowed to vary between $10-80^{\circ}$, with an initial guess of $45^{\circ}$." (Kinematic modeling)
   - assumption: Thin disk, z₀ = 0.01″  
  > "a thin disk is assumed ($z_{\circ}=0.01''$)." (Kinematic modeling)
   - fitting: Beam-convolve cloud models; match azimuthally averaged flux; minimize absolute residuals ring by ring. First fit rotation, dispersion, systemic velocity, inclination and PA; then fix the latter three to ring averages and refit rotation and dispersion.  
  > "convolved with the synthesized beam of the data cube , normalized by setting the azimuthally-averaged flux in the model and data cubes equal" (Kinematic modeling); "Each parameter is varied, until the absolute residual (i.e., $|$model–observation$|$) is minimized." (Kinematic modeling); "The three latter variables are then set to the average values between all rings, and the fit algorithm is run again, with only the rotational velocity and velocity dispersion as variables." (Kinematic modeling)
   - validation: Inspect moment maps, spectra and position–velocity diagrams; W0220+0137 fits well, while W2246–0526 retains large residuals  
  > "the model is able to reproduce the kinematic properties of W0220+0137 to an excellent level, with very small moment 0 residuals." (Kinematic modeling); "The spectral residuals (bottom-right panel) are small as well." (Kinematic modeling); "The intrinsic kinematics of the galaxy are not well recovered by the model, as evidenced by the large residuals in all moment maps" (Kinematic modeling)
6. **Derive dynamical masses**: Use outermost-ring radius and velocities to compute rotation- and dispersion-supported masses  
  > "We use these outputs to generate two estimates of the dynamical mass of the system." (Kinematic modeling); "where $r$ and $v$ are the radius and velocity of the outermost ring, respectively" (Kinematic modeling); "where $\sigma_v$ is the velocity dispersion of the outermost ring" (Kinematic modeling)
   - parameter: M_dyn^rot = v²r/G; M_dyn^disp = α σ_v²r/G  
  > "M_{dyn}^{rot}=\frac{v^2r}{G}" (Kinematic modeling); "M_{dyn}^{disp}=\alpha\frac{\sigma_v^2r}{G}" (Kinematic modeling)
   - assumption: Adopt α = 3.4 and use dispersion-supported mass estimates  
  > "Here we follow (cite: Stott2016) and adopt a value of $\alpha$ = 3.4." (Kinematic modeling); "the two systems analyzed here seem to be dispersion-dominated" (Kinematic modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: W2246–0526 dispersion-supported mass agrees within uncertainties with DS2018; compare dynamical, stellar and literature black-hole masses  
  > "This value is within the uncertainties of the $M_{\rm dyn}$  obtained by (cite: DS2018)" (Dynamical masses from modeling); "which we can compare to the stellar masses estimated in Section" (Dynamical masses from modeling); "and to BH masses published in the literature" (Dynamical masses from modeling)
7. **Compare ISM diagnostics and velocity distributions**: Compare [C ii]/IR ratios, equivalent widths, luminosity surface densities, sizes and line widths with OpQs, IRQs, ALPINE and GOALS; use a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test  
  > "for our sample of Hot DOGs as well as for the comparison populations of high-*z* QSOs and the GOALS sample." (The [C ii] deficit); "Histograms of the integrated FWHM$_{\rm [CII]}$ for our sample of luminous Hot DOGs and for the comparison quasar samples are presented" (Velocity fields and dispersion maps); "A two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test performed over the Hot DOG and the combined quasar samples" (Velocity fields and dispersion maps)
   - parameter: Physical areas = π R_160μm,eff²; intrinsic continuum half-light radius = FWHM/2  
  > "physical areas are calculated as $\pi$ $R^2_{\rm 160\mu m, eff}$" (Data homogenization); "the intrinsic (PSF-corrected) 160 $\mu$m  dust continuum emission half-light radius (= FHWM/2, for a 2D Gaussian)." (Data homogenization)
   - assumption: Flat cosmology: ΩM = 0.28, ΩΛ = 0.72, H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1  
  > "Throughout the paper, the following flat cosmology is used: $\Omega_{\rm M}$ = 0.28, $\Omega_{\rm \Lambda}$ = 0.72, H$_{\rm 0}$ = 70 km$\,$s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: For comparison samples: L_IR/L_FIR ≃ 1.7 where needed; ALPINE line and continuum sizes assumed equal  
  > "we use the average $L_{\rm IR}$/$L_{\rm FIR}$ $\simeq$ 1.7 ratio found for nearby (U)LIRGs to estimate their $L_{\rm IR}$." (Data homogenization); "no correction is performed to their \[C ii\]  sizes, such that the line and continuum sizes are assumed to be the same." (Data homogenization)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Test Hot DOG line widths against both combined and individual quasar comparison samples  
  > "Individual tests comparing the Hot DOGs with each OpQ and IRQ sample also provide *D* $\geq$ 0.9 and *p* $\leq$ 3 $\times$ 10$^{-4}$." (Velocity fields and dispersion maps)
8. **Separate stellar and AGN photometric emission**: Fit optical-to-mid-IR photometry with empirical AGN and galaxy templates; sum E, Sbc and Im components to obtain host H-band flux  
  > "There are four empirical templates in the suite: one used to fit the AGN component and three representative of galaxy hosts" (SED modeling); "After the best SED fit is found, the three galaxy templates are added together to obtain the rest-frame $H$-band flux density of the host, free from AGN contamination." (SED modeling)
   - parameter: Templates span 0.03–30 μm; 1000 Monte Carlo realizations per source  
  > "spanning the wavelength range from 0.03 to 30 $\mu$m" (SED modeling); "we run 1000 Monte Carlo simulations for each Hot DOG, sampling from the errors of the photometric data-points assuming they follow a normal distribution" (SED modeling)
   - assumption: No additional host extinction beyond template reddening; AGN attenuation combines SMC and Milky Way laws; include intergalactic absorption  
  > "it is not necessary to account for additional dust extinction at the time of fitting, other than the intrinsic reddening the templates already have." (SED modeling); "a combination of a Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) reddening law at shorter wavelengths and a Milky Way reddening law at longer wavelengths." (SED modeling); "Intergalactic medium absorption is also considered." (SED modeling)
   - fitting: Four empirical templates; additional AGN obscuration allowed  
  > "There are four empirical templates in the suite" (SED modeling); "the AGN template is allowed to have additional obscuration" (SED modeling)
   - validation: Host H-band uncertainty from standard deviation of Monte Carlo realizations; inspect imaging for photometric contamination  
  > "The uncertainty is obtained as the standard deviation of the simulated results." (SED modeling); "no other source of emission is evident in the IRAC imaging within the apertures." (SED modeling)
9. **Convert host luminosity to stellar mass**: Use ezgal and Galaxev to derive H-band mass-to-light ratios for four exponentially declining star-formation histories  
  > "To derive it, we use the code *ezgal* (cite: Mancone2012) and the Galaxev stellar population SED models (cite: Bruzual2003)" (SED modeling); "We consider exponentially declining SFRHs that depend only on two parameters" (SED modeling)
   - software: ezgal, Galaxev
   - parameter: Δt = {500, 100} Myr; τ = {0.1, 1} Gyr  
  > "we assume that star formation began $\Delta t$ = {500, 100} Myr before the age of each galaxy, $t$, and two time-scales, of $\tau$ = {0.1, 1} Gyr." (SED modeling)
   - assumption: Z = 0.008 = 0.4 Z⊙; Chabrier IMF; TP-AGB contribution included  
  > "which include a contribution from thermally pulsing-asymptotic giant branch (TP-AGB) stars" (SED modeling); "with a metallicity Z = 0.008 ($\equiv$ 0.4 $Z_{\odot}$, where $Z_{\odot}$  is the metallicity of the Sun) and a Chabrier intial mass function (IMF) (cite: Chabrier2003)." (SED modeling)
   - fitting: Four limiting star-formation-history combinations; scale model H-band mass-to-light ratios to host photometry  
  > "The integral of the SFRH provides the $M_{\star}$  of the model together with the resulting SED, from which the \[M/L\]$_{H}$ is derived." (SED modeling); "we decide to consider four simple limiting cases that are selected to represent reasonable lower and upper boundaries for $\Delta t$ and $\tau$" (SED modeling)
   - validation: Report the full range across assumed histories; compare with dynamical masses  
  > "the error bars around the average values (displayed as big solid squares) represent the full extent of the range of estimates and not the standard deviation." (Hot DOGs on the main sequence); "the stellar mass of the Hot DOG hosts appears to exceed their dynamical mass by a factor of $\sim$ 5" (Dynamical masses from modeling)

  > "Both observables are corrected by multiplying by the fractions of extended emission" (Hot DOGs on the main sequence); "one is the starburst galaxy M82 and the other is a typical Sd spiral galaxy, which we scale to the rest-frame 158 $\mu$m  dust continuum to obtain the SFR." (Hot DOGs on the main sequence); "we use $L_{\rm [C\,{\sc II}]}$  and two representative values of the \[C ii\]-deficit seen in local, non AGN-dominated (U)LIRGs" (Hot DOGs on the main sequence)
   - parameter: [C ii]/L_IR = [5,0.2] × 10−3; SFR/L_IR = 1.48 × 10−10 [M⊙ yr−1]/[L⊙]

## Qualitative findings

- Four robust [C ii] detections; all seven sources detected in dust continuum  
  > "The \[C ii\]  line is robustly detected in four out of the seven EL Hot DOGs (W0134–2922, W0220+0137, W0831+0140 and W2246–0526)." (Summary); "All Hot DOGs are detected in dust continuum emission at $\simeq$ 158 $\mu$m." (Summary)
- Diverse velocity fields share large [C ii] velocity dispersions  
  > "The velocity fields of EL Hot DOGs are very diverse: from barely rotating sources, to resolved hosts with ordered, circular motions, to complex, disturbed systems that are likely the result of ongoing mergers." (Summary); "all Hot DOGs are characterized by large line-velocity dispersions, FWHM$_{\rm [CII]}$ $\gtrsim$ 500 km$\,$s$^{-1}$" (Summary)
- [C ii] cores are larger and have larger extended fractions than the dust continuum  
  > "In all cases, when both the line and continuum are detected, the size of the \[C ii\]  emission is larger than that of the continuum." ([C ii] equivalent widths, Σ_[CII], and core sizes); "These values are always smaller than $f_{\rm ext}$$_{\rm ,[CII]}$, implying that the continuum emission is arising from a volume closer to the central AGN than that responsible for \[C ii\]." (Spatial distribution and extended emission)
- Hot DOG hosts do not lie systematically above or below the star-formation main sequence  
  > "when considered as a whole population, they do not all seem to be lying systematically below or above the star-formation MS." (Hot DOGs on the main sequence)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Accessible declinations and rest-UV redshifts place [C ii] in transparent regions of Bands 7 and 8  
  > "have declinations accessible to ALMA and are at rest-UV redshifts that put the \[C ii\]$_{158}$ emission line" (The sample); "within the frequency range of ALMA’s band 8 and 7, in regions of high atmospheric transparency." (The sample)
- Why this method: Average channels to improve SNR while retaining kinematics; detailed modeling only where resolution and data quality allow  
  > "to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) while keeping the final spectral elements sufficiently small to derive kinematic information." (ALMA observations and data processing); "the two sources for which the angular resolution and quality of the data allow such a detailed characterization: W0220+0137 and W2246–0526." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
- Alternative: W0236+0528: test expected-frequency and redder-SPW candidate lines; retain only a tentative expected-frequency detection  
  > "We attempted to create an image of the line using two velocity ranges encompassing the frequencies of the two possible feature identifications, but no source could be found in either." (Line detection, velocity shifts and luminosities); "a SNR $\simeq$ 3 detection at the SPW where the line was expected, while nothing can be found in the redder SPW." (Line detection, velocity shifts and luminosities)
- Alternative: Reject detailed tilted-ring interpretation for W0134–2922 and W0831+0140  
  > "the analysis resulted in an unconstrained morphological inclination and position angle for the former" (Rotation or dispersion supported?); "position-velocity diagrams of the minor and major axis for the latter that indicates clear non-rotation." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
- Alternative: Calculate both rotation- and dispersion-supported masses; favor the dispersion-supported W2246–0526 value  
  > "Since the modeling of W2246–0526 strongly favors a dispersion-supported system, we refer from here on to the value derived from this assumption as the most likely $M_{\rm dyn}$." (Dynamical masses from modeling)
- Alternative: High gas content and additional turbulent heating are non-exclusive explanations for excess [C ii]  
  > "An alternative possibility is the existence of an additional heating source, other than star formation, that enhances the \[C ii\]  emission." (What emission is the SMBH in extremely luminous Hot DOGs powering?); "Because of the size constraint, the most likely scenario is a combination of these two possibilities." (What emission is the SMBH in extremely luminous Hot DOGs powering?)
- Alternative: Consider smooth cosmic-web accretion, mergers and recurrent episodes rather than a unique single-merger explanation  
  > "It is possible that there is more than one mechanism triggering the EL Hot DOG phenomenon." (An evolutionary perspective); "this process could occur once, *or it may be recurrent*." (An evolutionary perspective)
- How they decided it was enough: Robust line detections have SNR ≥20; W0236+0528 remains tentative after tapered imaging; residual dirty-beam patterns motivate deeper cleaning  
  > "The \[C ii\]  line is robustly detected (SNR $\geq$ 20) in four out of the seven Hot DOGs in the sample" (Line detection, velocity shifts and luminosities); "Given the large uncertainties, we thus consider this only as a tentative detection" (Line detection, velocity shifts and luminosities); "patterns from the dirty beam were still clearly identifiable in the residual images when cleaning down to 2.0 $\sigma_{\rm rms}$." (ALMA observations and data processing)
- Limitation (authors): W0116–0505 misses [C ii] entirely; W0410–0913 samples only its blue wing  
  > "for two sources, W0116–0505 and W0410–0913, the line is redshifted completely and partially, respectively, out of the frequency range covered by the reference SPW" (Line detection, velocity shifts and luminosities)
- Limitation (authors): W0236+0528 is tentative and excluded from later figures and discussion  
  > "refrain from including this source in the figures or discussion throughout the remainder of the paper." (Line detection, velocity shifts and luminosities)
- Limitation (authors): Map-based rotation-support estimates can be biased by beam smearing  
  > "We warn that since kinematic modeling was not possible for these two sources given the current data, beam smearing can be significant." (Rotation or dispersion supported?)
- Limitation (authors): W2246–0526 tilted-ring model does not recover the intrinsic kinematics well  
  > "The intrinsic kinematics of the galaxy are not well recovered by the model, as evidenced by the large residuals in all moment maps" (Kinematic modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar masses may include companions and extended emission absent from the ALMA dynamical aperture  
  > "the near-IR photometry could include emission from companion galaxies and extended structures not captured by the ALMA-derived $M_{\rm dyn}$." (Dynamical masses from modeling)

  > "it is extremely difficult to infer any information regarding the SFRH of galaxies based on sparse photometric data" (SED modeling); "A larger number of Hot DOGs and a better sampling of their photometric SEDs are necessary to obtain more precise stellar masses and SFRs" (Hot DOGs on the main sequence)
- Limitation (authors): Resolution is insufficient to isolate SMBH-scale gas and dust  
  > "the physical resolution of the current ALMA data is $\sim$ 1.5–2 kpc" (Spatial distribution and extended emission); "much higher resolution observations are needed to further isolate the gas and dust structures located under the sphere of influence of the SMBH" (Spatial distribution and extended emission)

  > "the picture put forward here may not be valid for lower luminosity Hot DOGs and it is possible that not all accretion events produce an EL, hot obscured phase." (An evolutionary perspective)
