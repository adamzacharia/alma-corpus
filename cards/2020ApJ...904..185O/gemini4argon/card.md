## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify continuum substructures and measure peak positions and intensities**: 2D Gaussian fitting on 1.3 mm continuum images (Briggs robust=0.5 and uniform weighting) using the 2D Fit Tool of casa viewer, and comparison with extrapolated VLA proper motions  
  > "We identify five intensity peaks, as shown in Figure 1(b). Their positions and peak intensities are listed in Table 3. These values are obtained by using the 2D Fit Tool of casa viewer ." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum); "Figure 2(c) shows the extrapolation of the proper motion from the VLA observation in February 2014 (2014.15) to our ALMA observation in August 2017 (2017.64)." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - software: CASA viewer
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5 and uniform (robust = -2.0) continuum maps  
  > "Panels (a) and (b) are obtained with the Briggs weighting with a robustness parameter of 0.5, while panel (c) is with the uniform weighting (i.e. robustness parameter of − 2 . 0)." (Figure 1 caption)
   - fitting: 2D Fit Tool in casa viewer  
  > "These values are obtained by using the 2D Fit Tool of casa viewer ." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - validation: Comparison of Briggs and uniform weighting spatial profiles along A1-A1a, and comparison with VLA 15 GHz and 33 GHz continuum positions  
  > "Figure 1(d) shows the spatial profile of the continuum emission along the line passing the peak A1 and A1a. The separation between A1 and A1a is clearer in the image with the uniform weighting." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
2. **Determine morphological parameters of the circummultiple disk/envelope system**: Calculate the intensity centroid of 1.3 mm continuum data points above 3 sigma (0.9 mJy/beam) and fit those positions to an ellipse using intensity-weighted least-squares  
  > "First, we determine the centroid of the continuum intensity distribution using the data points with 3 σ (0.9 mJy beam − 1 ) detection or higher to be the following: ( α ICRS , δ ICRS ) = (16 h 32 m 22 s . 873 , − 24 ◦ 28 ′ 36 ′′ . 614)." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System); "Then, we fit the positions of the data points used above to an ellipse by using the least-squares method weighted by the intensity at each position." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System)
   - parameter: 3 sigma threshold = 0.9 mJy/beam; pixel size = 0''.025  
  > "using the data points with 3 σ (0.9 mJy beam − 1 ) detection or higher" (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System); "within the pixel size of the map (0 ′′ . 025)." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System)
   - assumption: Flat disk structure without thickness for inclination angle lower limit; dust emission intensity proportional to dust mass  
  > "If a flat disk structure without thickness is assumed, its inclination angle is roughly estimated to be 66 ◦ (0 ◦ for a face-on configuration) from the ratio of the sizes of the major and minor axes." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System); "The intensity centroid can be regarded as a rough estimate of the center of gravity for the elliptic structure, assuming that the intensity of the dust emission is proportional to the dust mass at each position." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System)
   - fitting: Intensity-weighted least-squares ellipse fit  
  > "fit the positions of the data points used above to an ellipse by using the least-squares method weighted by the intensity at each position." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System)
   - validation: Ellipse centroid coincides with intensity centroid within 1 pixel (0''.025)  
  > "The centroid of the ellipse is found to coincide with the intensity centroid obtained above within the pixel size of the map (0 ′′ . 025)." (4.2. Morphological Structure of the Disk/Envelope System)
3. **Model the kinematic structure of the circummultiple structure using C17O (J=2-1)**: Extract PV diagrams centered on the continuum intensity centroid and fit 3D Keplerian disk and infalling-rotating envelope (IRE) models using reduced chi-squared tests on the blue-shifted component  
  > "We here use three-dimensional physical models of a flat disk/envelope with the Keplerian motion or the infalling-rotating motion." (4.3. Kinematic Structure of the C 17 O Line); "We calculate the reduced χ 2 value ( χ 2 /DOF), which is the sum of the square of the difference between the modeled and observed PV diagrams along the envelope mid-plane direction at each pixel normalized by the square of the rms in the observation" (Appendix A. Reduced Chi-Squared Test for the Position-Velocity Diagrams); "Since the C 17 O emission notably suffers from the self-absorption effect, we only use the blue-shifted component from − 3 km s − 1 to +3 km s − 1 to calculate χ 2 ." (Appendix B. Analysis of C 17 O)
   - parameter: Inner radius = 50 au (or r_CB = 50 au), outer radius = 300 au, P.A. = 50 deg, V_sys = 3.9 km/s, flared angle = 30 deg, intrinsic FWHM = 1 km/s, emissivity proportional to r^-1.5  
  > "Here, the inner and outer radii of the model are fixed to be 50 au and 300 au according to the observed molecular distribution." (4.3.1. Keplerian Model for the C 17 O Line); "The emissivity in the model is assumed to be proportional to r − 1 . 5 , where r denotes the distance from the center of gravity, and is to be zero for r < 50 au and r > 300 au. The scale height of the disk is assumed to increase as increasing the distance from the center of gravity with the flared angle of 30 ◦ ." (Figure 8 caption)
   - assumption: Optically thin line emission, no radiative transfer, ballistic motion conserving energy and specific angular momentum for IRE model  
  > "In these models, the line emission is assumed to be optically thin, and the radiation transfer is not considered." (4.3. Kinematic Structure of the C 17 O Line); "In this model, the velocity field is approximated by the ballistic motion. The energy and specific angular momentum of the gas is assumed to be conserved" (4.3.2. Infalling-Rotating Envelope Model for the C 17 O Line)
   - fitting: Grid search minimizing reduced chi-squared (chi^2/DOF) over central mass and inclination angle; parameter ranges defined where reduced chi^2 increases by 1  
  > "the parameter ranges are determined from the range where the reduced χ 2 value increases by 1." (Appendix A. Reduced Chi-Squared Test for the Position-Velocity Diagrams)
   - validation: Visual comparison of model contours on PV diagrams along 6 position angles  
  > "As depicted in Figure 8(a), the PV diagram along the envelope mid-plane direction is reasonably reproduced. However, the PV diagrams along the other directions are not well explained" (4.3.1. Keplerian Model for the C 17 O Line)
4. **Model the kinematic structure of the circumstellar disk around protostar A1 using H2CS**: Extract PV diagrams centered on continuum peak A1 and fit 3D Keplerian disk and infalling-rotating envelope models to H2CS (7_4,3-6_4,2, 7_4,4-6_4,3) along P.A. 50 deg and 140 deg using reduced chi-squared tests  
  > "First, we perform the χ 2 test for the Keplerian model and the H 2 CS (7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 , 7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 ) line, as the C 17 O case in Section 4.3.1" (4.4.2. Keplerian Model for the H 2 CS Line); "In contrast to the C 17 O analysis, we use the two PV diagrams of the H 2 CS (7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 , 7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 ) line prepared along the disk mid-plane direction (P.A. 50 ◦ ) and the direction perpendicular to it (P.A. 140 ◦ ), and use both the red- and blue-shifted components with the velocity range from − 6 km s − 1 to +11 km s − 1 ." (Appendix C. Analysis of H 2 CS)
   - parameter: V_sys = 2.5 km/s, outer radius = 30 au, inner radius = 0.56 au (1 pixel) for Keplerian or r_CB = 5, 10, 20 au for IRE, P.A. = 50 deg, flared angle = 30 deg, intrinsic FWHM = 1 km/s, emissivity proportional to r^-1.5  
  > "where the systemic velocity is 2.5 km s − 1 . The inner and outer radii of the model are fixed to be ∼ 0 au and 30 au according to the observed molecular distribution, respectively." (4.4.2. Keplerian Model for the H 2 CS Line); "The intensity in the models is convolved with the synthesized beam size for each molecular line (Table 1) and the Gaussian line profile with the intrinsic line width (FWHM) of 1 km s − 1 ." (Figure 10 caption)
   - assumption: Optically thin line emission, center of gravity at continuum peak A1  
  > "The center of gravity for the model is assumed to be at the continuum peak A1" (Figure 10 caption)
   - fitting: Grid search minimizing reduced chi-squared over central mass, inclination angle, and (for IRE) centrifugal barrier radius (5676 pixels)  
  > "The pixels with the angular offset from − 0 ′′ . 4 to +0 ′′ . 4 and the velocity from − 6 km s − 1 to +11 km s − 1 are taken into account. 5676 pixels are used for the calculation." (Table A4)
   - validation: Comparison of Keplerian vs IRE models across multiple P.A.s and H2CS transitions (7_0,7-6_0,6 and 7_2,5-6_2,4), and checking PV diagrams centered on A2, A3, A4  
  > "As the result, we find that the kinematic structure traced by H 2 CS is worse reproduced by the infalling-rotating envelope model than by the Keplerian model" (4.4.3. Infalling-Rotating Envelope Model for the H 2 CS Line)
5. **Derive the H2CS rotation temperature distribution and line-of-sight maximum temperature profile**: Calculate the intensity ratio of H2CS (7_4,3-6_4,2, 7_4,4-6_4,3) to H2CS (7_2,5-6_2,4) from integrated intensity maps, 1 km/s velocity channel maps, and peak along the velocity axis (using CASA immoments moments=[8]), and convert to rotation temperature  
  > "Figure 12(a) shows the distribution of the ratio of the integrated intensity of the H 2 CS (7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 , 7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 ) line relative to that of the H 2 CS (7 2 , 5 –6 2 , 4 ) line, while Figure 12(b) shows the distribution of the rotation temperature of H 2 CS calculated from the integrated intensity ratio." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "Figures 16(a) and (b) show the maps of the highest intensity ratio of the two H 2 CS lines and the highest rotation temperature along the velocity channels, which are derived from the cube data. In other words, the maximum values along the line of sight are shown in these maps. These maps are obtained by using the immoments task of casa with the option “ moments = [8] ”." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)
   - software: CASA, RADEX
   - parameter: High-temperature limit of intensity ratio = 1.47 for T = infinity; rms noise = 6 mJy/beam km/s (integrated) and 2 mJy/beam (cube)  
  > "Since the intensity ratio at these positions are close to the high-temperature limit (1.47 for T = ∞ ) as shown in Figure 13(b), the derived temperature is sensitive to a small change in the ratio" (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "The rms noise level for the integrated intensity used in panel a is 6 mJy beam − 1 km s − 1 , while that for the line intensitiesusedinpanelbis2mJybeam − 1" (Figure 13 caption)
   - assumption: LTE condition and optically thin emission for both H2CS lines; dust continuum contribution ignored  
  > "Here, we assume the LTE condition and the optically thin conditions for the two lines." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "Here, we ignore the contribution of the dust emission, because the treatment of this effect requires detailed radiation transfer and is out of the scope of this paper." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)
   - fitting: Analytical conversion from two-line intensity ratio under optically thin LTE  
  > "Here, we assume the LTE condition and the optically thin conditions for the two lines." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)
   - validation: Calculated optical depths (median 0.28 and 0.10, mostly < 0.5), verified LTE consistency with RADEX (n_H2 = 10^7-10^9 cm^-3), and compared with 7_2,5-6_2,4 / 7_0,7-6_0,6 ratio  
  > "The derived optical depths are mostly lower than 0.5, and its median is 0.28 and 0.10 for the H 2 CS (7 2 , 5 –6 2 , 4 ) and (7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 , 7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 ) lines, respectively." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "The rotation temperature of H 2 CS derived from the 7 2 , 5 –6 2 , 4 and 7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 /7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 lines is confirmed to be consistent with the gas kinetic temperature derived by using RADEX code (van der Tak et al. 2007) with the H 2 density range of 10 7 − 9 cm − 3" (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)

## Qualitative findings

- The 1.3 mm dust continuum of IRAS 16293-2422 Source A resolves into 5 intensity peaks (A1, A1a, A2, A3, A4) within 100 au, where A1 and A2 match extrapolated cm-wavelength positions and A3, A4, and A1a are newly detected.  
  > "The continuum emission of the binary component, Source A, reveals the substructure consisting of 5 intensity peaks within 100 au from the protostar." (abstract); "The positions of two continuum peaks (A1 and A2) are consistent with those previously observed at cm wavelength, if their proper motions are considered. They also agree with the recent report on the 3 mm continuum emission. We also detect new continuum peaks (A3, A4, and A1a)." (7. Summary)
- C17O (J=2-1) traces a rotating circummultiple structure on a 300 au scale centered at the continuum intensity centroid and is strongly depressed within a radius of 50 au.  
  > "The C 17 O emission mainly traces the circummultiple structure on a 300 au scale centered at the intensity centroid of the continuum, while it is very weak within the radius of 50 au from the centroid." (abstract)
- H2CS lines trace a compact (r ~ 30 au) rotating circumstellar disk centered on continuum peak A1, which has a different rotation centroid, systemic velocity (2.5 km/s vs 3.9 km/s), and central mass than the circummultiple structure.  
  > "The H 2 CS emission, in contrast, traces the rotating disk structure around one of the continuum peaks (A1). Thus, it seems that the rotation centroid of the circummultiple structure is slightly different from that of the disk around A1." (abstract); "As well, the difference of the systemic velocity between the two models, 3.9 km s − 1 for the circummultiple structure and 2.5 km s − 1 for the circumstellar structure, implies a potential motion of the protostar A1 in Source A." (4.4.5. Comparison between the Circumstellar and Circummultiple Structures)
- The H2CS rotation temperature steeply rises to >= 300 K at the 50 au transition zone between the circummultiple structure and the A1 circumstellar disk, suggesting a ring-like hot corino heated by accretion shocks and/or protostellar radiation on piled-up gas.  
  > "As approaching to the protostar A1, the rotation temperature steeply rises up to 300 K or higher at the radius of 50 au from the protostar. It is likely due to a local accretion shock and/or the preferential protostellar heating of the transition zone from the circummultiple structure to the disk around A1." (abstract); "‘Hot corino’ may have a ring-like structure, instead of a simple spherical or disk-like structure." (7. Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous observations at ~0''.5 (70 au) resolution could not resolve possible substructures and detailed temperature profiles in the 50 au transition zone of IRAS 16293-2422 Source A; ~0''.1 (14 au) resolution is needed.  
  > "However, the previous observations (Oya et al. 2016; van’t Hoff et al. 2020) do not have a sufficient spatial resolution ( ∼ 0 ′′ . 5; 70 au) for this purpose. In this study, we make use of the ALMA data observed at a high spatial resolution ( ∼ 0 ′′ . 1; 14 au)" (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: H2CS K-structure lines have very slow radiative transitions between different K_a levels, making their rotation temperature a good measure of gas kinetic temperature; using the 7_2,5-6_2,4 and 7_4,3-6_4,2/7_4,4-6_4,3 pair avoids line blending affecting the 7_0,7-6_0,6 line, and taking the maximum along velocity channels disentangles envelope and disk components along the line of sight.  
  > "The rotation temperature derived from these lines is a good measure of the gas kinetic temperature, because the radiation processes between the different K a levels (∆ K a = ± 2 , ± 4) are very slow." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "We here employ the H 2 CS (7 2 , 5 –6 2 , 4 ) and (7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 , 7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 ) lines for the analysis, because the H 2 CS (7 0 , 7 –6 0 , 6 ) line suffers from the contamination by the H 2 CS (7 5 , 2 –6 5 , 1 , 7 5 , 3 –6 5 , 2 ) lines." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "In Figure 12, the envelope and disk components are contaminated with each other along the line of sight, because the line emission is integrated for the velocity axis. On the other hand, they are expected to be disentangled to some extent in the velocity channel maps." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)
- Alternative: Self-calibration was tried in various ways but rejected because it did not improve or even deteriorated the images, and preserving absolute positions was needed for proper motion analysis.  
  > "We tried self-calibration in various ways. However, the images are not improved or even deteriorated. Since we discuss the proper motion of the continuum peaks, we employ the images without self-calibration in this paper." (2. Observations)
- Alternative: Uniform weighting continuum image was used to check peak separation (A1 vs A1a), but Briggs weighting (robust=0.5) was adopted for the main analysis because uniform weighting suffers from stronger resolved-out effects.  
  > "the resolved-out effect increases due to less contribution of short-baseline data. Because we are interested in the structure surrounding Source A, we hereafter employ the continuum image with the Briggs weighting (Figures 1b), which better" (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
- Alternative: Centering the C17O kinematic analysis on continuum peak A1 was tested and rejected because C17O did not show a symmetric velocity feature around A1.  
  > "We found that the C 17 O emission does not show a symmetric feature around A1 in contrast to Figure 8. Therefore, we concluded that the center for the kinematic structure of the C 17 O emission should be at the center of gravity of the elliptic structure traced by the dust continuum emission, rather than at A1." (4.3. Kinematic Structure of the C 17 O Line)
- Alternative: Combining Keplerian and infalling-rotating envelope models into a composite model was rejected due to having too many free parameters for the current data.  
  > "Finer tuning of the model by, for instance, combining the two simple models is practically difficult, because such an analysis includes too many free parameters for our current observational data of this source, and it is out of scope of this work." (4.3.3. Circummultiple Structure)
- Alternative: Using the H2CS (7_0,7-6_0,6) line for rotation temperature derivation was rejected because it samples colder gas and is contaminated by the H2CS (7_5,2-6_5,1, 7_5,3-6_5,2) doublet.  
  > "This is probably because the 7 0 , 7 –6 0 , 6 line would sample colder regions and is partly contaminated by the H 2 CS (7 5 , 2 –6 5 , 1 , 7 5 , 3 –6 5 , 2 ) line (Section 4.4.4). Hence, we use the rotation temperature derived from the 7 2 , 5 –6 2 , 4 and 7 4 , 3 –6 4 , 2 /7 4 , 4 –6 4 , 3 lines in the following discussions." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)

  > "These are contaminations of the (CH 3 ) 2 CO (18 5 , 13 –17 6 , 12 EE and 18 6 , 13 –17 5 , 12 EE at 224.700 GHz) transitions, the CH 2 DOH (7 2 , 6 –7 1 , 7 , e 1 at 224.701 GHz; 20 1 , 19 –20 0 , 20 , e 1 at 224.725 GHz) lines, and/or an unidentified line.   > "Here, we ignore the contribution of the dust emission, because the treatment of this effect requires detailed radiation transfer and is out of the scope of this paper." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS); "It should be noted that very high temperatures obtained at some positions would be artificial. Since the intensity ratio at these positions are close to the high-temperature limit (1.47 for T = ∞ ) as shown in Figure 13(b), the derived temperature is sensitive to a small change in the ratio and suffers from a large error." (5. Distribution of the Rotation Temperature of H 2 CS)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3D kinematic models neglect radiative transfer and continuum optical depth in a source where 1.3 mm dust brightness temperature reaches ~70 K.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The maximum-along-line-of-sight (moment 8) ratio method is susceptible to positive noise bias in low-S/N channels, which can artificially inflate the derived H2CS rotation temperature near the 1.47 asymptotic limit.
