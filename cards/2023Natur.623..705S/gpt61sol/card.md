## Qualitative findings

- Gaps have higher polarization fractions and a significant azimuthal polarization component.  
  > "We find that the gaps have polarization angles with a significant azimuthal component and a higher polarization fraction than the rings." (Abstract)
- Polarization fractions and intensities are larger along the major axis.  
  > "The polarized fractions and intensities are larger along the major axis than the minor axis." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk)
- Scattering dominates rings; aligned grains dominate gaps.  
  > "both grain scattering and alignment are at play, with scattering dominating the polarization in the rings and aligned grains in the gaps." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk)
- Optically thick rings likely contain most of the mass and are reservoirs for future planetesimal formation.  
  > "Since the rings are much more optically thick, most of the mass must be in these optically thick rings." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk); "These rings are thus the likely reservoirs for the future formation of planetesimals." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk)
- Polarization reveals asymmetries not reproduced by the models or evident in non-polarimetric continuum.  
  > "even these models cannot explain polarization asymmetries across the major and minor axis" (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk); "Asymmetries and dust features are revealed in the polarization observations that are not seen in non-polarimetric observations." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Resolve polarization in rings and gaps; add previous polarization observations for sensitivity.  
  > "Here we show deep, high-resolution 870 $\mu$m polarization observations of HL Tau, resolving polarization in both the rings and gaps." (Abstract); "The linear polarization data (Stokes $Q$ and $U$) were combined with data from previous observations(cite: Stephens2017c) for increased sensitivity" (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk)
- Why this method: Plane-parallel slabs simplify transfer for a thin dust layer; prolate grains reproduce the D14 minor-axis orientations.  
  > "The radiative transfer simplifies significantly if each local patch of the dust disk is approximated by a plane-parallel slab(cite: Lin2022MNRAS.512.3922L)." (Linear Polarization Modeling); "with a dust scale height of approximately 1 au at a radius of 100 au, based on the lack of an azimuthal variation of the gap width(cite: Pinte2016)." (Linear Polarization Modeling); "they naturally produce the parallel-to-the-major-axis polarization orientations observed in the first gap at the locations along the minor axis." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Alternative: Adding Science Verification data reduced Stokes I signal-to-noise.  
  > "We also attempted to combine these Stokes $I$ data with HL Tau Science Verification data(cite: ALMA2015), but we found that these data reduced the signal to noise." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: C-4 excluded from display Stokes I images because it added noise and sidelobes.  
  > "The Stokes $I$ image of this track had significant side-lobes, and only added additional noise to the 2019.1.01051.S Stokes $I$ data" (Observations and Data Reduction); "we did not include the C-4 data for the Stokes $I$, but we did for the polarization vectors." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Radially aligned oblate grains with s = 1.2 fail the D14 minor-axis polarization orientation.  
  > "We also rerun this identical analysis, except we use oblate grains with an aspect ratio of $s = 1.2$ and their short axes aligned radially in the disk." (Linear Polarization Modeling); "This model is inconsistent with the observations since the polarization direction is opposite to what is observed in D14 along the minor axis." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Alternative: Toroidally aligned oblate grains not considered because they predict radial polarization.  
  > "We do not consider oblate grains that are toroidally aligned, because the polarization would be radial and completely different from what is observed." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Alternative: Scattering-only radiation anisotropy can produce an intensity–polarization anticorrelation but struggles with D14 direction and polarized intensity.  
  > "pure scattering of spherical or randomly aligned grains with the radiation anisotropy due to the existence of rings and gaps can also produce the anti-correlation between Stokes $I$ and $p$ along the major axis." (Linear Polarization Modeling); "However, solely relying on radiation anisotropy will be difficult to reproduce both the polarization direction and the high level of polarized intensity along the minor axis for the first gap (D14)." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- How they decided it was enough: Adopted parameters broadly reproduce the observations; uniqueness is explicitly not established.  
  > "Although we find that the adopted parameters appear adequate enough to broadly reproduce the observations, we caution that the parameters may not be unique in producing such features(cite: Tazaki2019ApJ...885...52T; YangLi2020; Lin2023MNRAS.520.1210L)." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Single-wavelength polarization does not precisely determine grain properties.  
  > "Polarization data for a single wavelength do not allow a precise determination of these properties in general; our case is no exception." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Grain size, composition, albedo, porosity, alignment efficiency and shape are degenerate; ~100 μm grains are not unique and underpredict longer-wavelength intensity.  
  > "Grains of $\sim$100 $\mu$m size are likely not a unique solution and would underestimate the intensity of the image at longer wavelengths (e.g., 3 mm)." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk); "Models could be further fine-tuned, but they are degenerate due to several different parameters, including grain size, composition, albedo, porosity, alignment efficiency, and shape(cite: Tazaki2019ApJ...885...52T; YangLi2020; Lin2023MNRAS.520.1210L)." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk)
- Limitation (authors): Plane-parallel model omits radiation anisotropy; its effect on inferred s is unclear.  
  > "Radiation anisotropy is not included given the plane-parallel nature of the model, and it is unclear how it will affect the inferred $s$ (also degenerate with the alignment efficiency)." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Plane-parallel slabs cannot capture near–far asymmetry of the inner disk.  
  > "The model cannot capture the near-far side asymmetry since the image is built from plane-parallel slabs that is more applicable for geometrically thin disks." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Higher resolution and multi-wavelength observations are needed to reduce beam averaging and constrain grain properties.  
  > "Multi-wavelength high resolution observations are necessary to overcome these degenerencies and accurately constrain grain sizes." (Aligned Grains and Scattered Light Found in Gaps of Planet-Forming Disk); "Going to even higher angular resolution and better sensitivity should mitigate the beam averaging effects and uncover the thermal polarization from aligned grains if any." (Linear Polarization Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): P > 10% outer-disk vectors may be interferometric artifacts or random noise.  
  > "The vectors that have $P > 10\%$ are few and toward the outer disk, and thus are potentially interferometric artifacts or random noise above the 3$\sigma$ level." (Observations and Data Reduction)
