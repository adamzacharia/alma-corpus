## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble stellar properties and infer dust masses**: Use Gaia distances, PMS tracks and a scaled Phoenix photosphere; convert literature 887 μm ALMA fluxes to dust masses.  
  > "The stellar masses and ages were computed through a set of PMS tracks (cite: Siess2000; Bressan2012; Baraffe2015; Choi2016)" (Sample and stellar properties); "the stellar luminosity calculated from a Phoenix model of the stellar photosphere (cite: Hauschildt1999) scaled to the de-reddened optical photometry from the literature." (Sample and stellar properties); "a crude estimate of the disk dust mass was derived from the ALMA fluxes at 887 $\mu m$ by (cite: Pascucci2016)" (Sample and stellar properties)
   - assumption: Gaia distance; optically thin emission; dust temperature 20 K; Beckwith1991 dust opacity  
  > "using the $Gaia$ distance and the typical assumption (optically thin emission, dust temperature of 20 K, and dust opacity by (cite: Beckwith1991))." (Sample and stellar properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure disk geometry and scattering-surface height**: Extract radial profiles; fit Gaussian ring peaks or trace the 3σ disk edge; fit ellipses, then maximize flux over minor-axis-offset annuli or apertures.  
  > "For the ringed disks, we fit 1d-Gaussian profiles to extract the ring locations from the radial profiles." (Fitting procedure); "For the disks without ringed substructure, we trace the outer disk edge at which the disk signal drops below 3$\sigma$ as determined from the image background." (Fitting procedure); "We then find the annulus or aperture for which the contained disk or ring flux is maximized." (Fitting procedure)
   - parameter: 1° azimuthal extraction bins; bin measurements in 10° increments  
  > "In both cases we use a sliding aperture, to extract radial profiles in azimuthal bins of 1$^\circ$." (Fitting procedure); "After the initial measurement, we then bin the data in either case in azimuthal direction in increments of 10$^\circ$." (Fitting procedure)
   - parameter: Offset grid step 0.1 pixel (1.25 mas)  
  > "with offsets along the minor axis increasing in steps of 0.1 pixel (1.25 mas)." (Fitting procedure)
   - assumption: Constrain inclination and PA to ALMA literature values where available; fix semi-major axis; allow offsets only along the minor axis.  
  > "For this second step, we constrain the disk inclination and position angle, either to literature measurements from ALMA millimeter observations (typically gas observations) or based on the prior fitting step when no ALMA data is available." (Fitting procedure); "We additionally constrain the ring or disk-edge semi-major axis to the value found in the previous step." (Fitting procedure); "We then only allow ellipse offsets from the stellar position along the minor axis" (Fitting procedure)
   - fitting: Unconstrained least-squares ellipse fit; LSMC with 10^5 fits drawing point positions from normal distributions; parameter uncertainties from run-to-run standard deviations.  
  > "Initially, we use the least squares algorithm by (cite: oy1998NUMERICALLYSD) to find the best-fitting ellipse, without any prior constraints to the disk parameters." (Fitting procedure); "We repeat the fit 10$^5$ times. Each time we draw the location of each data point from a normal distribution with the width of the data points uncertainty." (Fitting procedure); "We then use the standard deviation of each ellipse parameter across all runs as the uncertainty for that parameter." (Fitting procedure)
   - validation: Tested on convolved, noise-added radiative-transfer images; inclination and PA recovered above approximately 10°; aspect ratios biased low by approximately 0.05.  
  > "We then ran the two-step fitting procedure, as described in section [4.2](#sec: ellipse-fitting) on all model images." (Geometric fitting approach and results); "We find that for inclinations larger than $\sim10^\circ$ the measured inclination and position angle are well consistent with the expected results." (Geometric fitting approach and results); "We do however observe a small systematic offset of $\sim0.05$ (i.e., the retrieved aspect ratio is slightly too low)." (Geometric fitting approach and results)
3. **Measure polarized contrast and population trends**: Divide polarized flux by incident stellar flux, measure along the major axis and average detected separations; compare with SED slopes, dust mass, accretion lifetime and age; calculate Kendall τ.  
  > "This method consists of dividing the observed polarized flux at a certain location by the net stellar flux that is virtually incident on that disk region (that is the stellar flux diluted by the distance)." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast); "Finally, a unique number is obtained for each disk by averaging the values obtained over the separations with detected flux." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast); "To quantify this trend we calculated the Kendall $\tau$-coefficient ((cite: 10.2307/2332226))." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast)
   - parameter: SED slopes: 2.2–5 μm and 5–22 μm  
  > "the near (2.2 $\mu m$ to 5 $\mu m$) and mid-infrared (5 $\mu m$ to 22 $\mu m$) SED slopes for each system." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast)
   - assumption: Major-axis measurements approximate 90° scattering to minimize phase-function dependence.  
  > "Also, the measurement is performed along the major axis (approximately 90$^\circ$ scattering) to minimize the dependence on the scattering phase function." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Constrain CS Cha scattering-grain sizes**: Measure integrated disk polarized-flux to stellar-intensity ratios by band; compare with RADMC3d models using Optool Mie optical properties.  
  > "We then computed the brightness ratio of polarized scattered light to stellar total intensity, as measured in the flux calibration frames of each epoch." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "To model this we used the radiative transfer code RADMC3d ((cite: 2012ascl.soft02015D))." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "Optical properties were computed using the Mie theory by utilizing the public code *Optool* ((cite: Dominik2021))." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
   - software: RADMC3d, Optool
   - parameter: IRDIS annulus 0.09–1.0 arcsec; ZIMPOL annulus 0.07–1.0 arcsec  
  > "we computed the integrated polarized scattered light in an annulus between 0.09" and 1.0" for the IRDIS, near-infrared images." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "For the ZIMPOL R and I-band data we measured the integrated polarized scattered light in an annulus between 0.07" and 1.0", taking into account the smaller coronagraph size." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
   - parameter: Maximum grain size free; minimum grain size 0.1 μm; size-distribution power-law index -3.5  
  > "with a size distribution with a minimum grain size of 0.1$\mu m$ and a power law index of -3.5." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "The maximum grain size $a_{max}$ was left as a free parameter." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
   - assumption: Compact spherical pyroxene-silicate grains  
  > "We assumed compact spherical grains made of pyroxene silicate (refractive index taken from (cite: Dorschner1995))" (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
   - assumption: SPHERE inclination approximately 22°; flaring exponent 1.09; remaining parameters from Tazaki et al.  
  > "We used the inclination of CS Cha of $\sim$22$^\circ$, as measured from the SPHERE data with a small flaring exponent of 1.09, to adjust the overall disk brightness to our observations." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "The remaining model parameters are identical to the ones presented in (cite: 2019MNRAS.485.4951T)." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
   - fitting: Ignore H-band data in color interpretation because of possible calibration issues; vary maximum grain size.  
  > "Ignoring the H-band data, which may suffer from calibration issues discussed in detail in (cite: Ginski2018)" (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "The maximum grain size $a_{max}$ was left as a free parameter." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
   - validation: 0.32 μm model reproduces the I–J decline but predicts a slightly blue J–K color rather than the observed slightly red color.  
  > "From model calculations, we found that a model with $a_{max}=0.32 \mu m$ successfully reproduces the steep decline in polarized flux between I and J bands." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "However, we note that the model predicts a slightly blue J-K color, while our observations found a slightly red color." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
5. **Measure CS Cha binary photometry and astrometry**: Resolve the binary in ZIMPOL calibration images; correct catalog photometry using component flux ratios; infer masses from AMES-COND; fit two Moffat functions for astrometry.  
  > "Since the binary was unresolved for these measurements we corrected the primary star magnitude following (cite: 2020A&A...635A..73B), using the flux ratio between the two components as input." (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "Assuming an average system age of 4.2 Myr (see table [\[tab: sample\]](#tab: sample)) and AMES-COND model isochrones ((cite: Allard2001)), we find masses of 1.2 M$_\odot$ and 0.8 M$_\odot$ for Aa and Ab" (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "To extract the astrometry from the ZIMPOL images we simultaneously fitted two Moffat functions to the binary star." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - assumption: Average system age 4.2 Myr; AMES-COND isochrones  
  > "Assuming an average system age of 4.2 Myr (see table [\[tab: sample\]](#tab: sample)) and AMES-COND model isochrones ((cite: Allard2001))" (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - fitting: Simultaneous two-Moffat-function astrometric fit  
  > "To extract the astrometry from the ZIMPOL images we simultaneously fitted two Moffat functions to the binary star." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - validation: Recovered total stellar mass consistent with the literature gas-kinematic total mass.  
  > "The total mass of the two components that we recover is well consistent with the total mass extracted from gas kinematics in the disk by (cite: Kurtovic2022)." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
6. **Fit the CS Cha binary orbit**: Jointly fit literature radial velocities and the new astrometric measurement with orbitize! MCMC.  
  > "We combined the radial velocity measurements given by (cite: Guenther2007) with our new astrometric measurement to derive the orbit of the CS Cha Aab pair." (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "We use the *orbitize!* python package for the fit with the included Markov-Chain Monte-Carlo sampler ((cite: Blunt2020; Foreman-Mackey2013))." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - software: orbitize!
   - parameter: 1.2 × 10^6 orbit solutions; 50 walkers  
  > "We ran $1.2 \times 10^{6}$ orbit solution with 50 walkers." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - assumption: Adopted distance 190 pc, corresponding to parallax 5.26 mas  
  > "For the purpose of this orbit fit we adopted the distance of 190 pc (i.e., a parallax of 5.26 mas)." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - fitting: Gaussian total-mass prior centered at 2.0 M_sun with σ = 0.2 M_sun; fitted parallax with Gaussian σ = 0.7 mas.  
  > "We set the prior of the combined system mass using a Gaussian distribution with the peak at 2.0 M$_\odot$ and the conservative standard deviation of 0.2 M$_\odot$" (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "However we allow the fit to include the parallax and set a Gaussian prior with a standard deviation of 0.7 mas, which then includes the nominal Gaia DR3 parallax of 5.93 mas." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test whether the stellar binary explains the ALMA cavity**: Compare the observed ALMA cavity radius at 190 pc with the cavity edge predicted from the fitted binary semi-major axis and literature tidal calculations.  
  > "The cavity radius from ALMA found by (cite: 2020ApJ...892..111F) and scaled to our adopted distance of 190 pc is 40 au." (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "Given the peak of the semi-major axis distribution recovered by our orbit fit of 5 au we would then expect for the inner cavity edge to be located at 15.5 au." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - parameter: CS Cha mass parameter μ = 0.4; comparison calculation μ = 0.3, e = 0.4, α_v = 10^-3, edge = 3.1a  
  > "The mass parameter $\mu =M_2/(M_1+M_2)$ for the CS Cha system is 0.4." (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "The calculations by (cite: 1994ApJ...421..651A) indicate that for an eccentric binary system with $e=0.4$" (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "with $\mu = 0.3$ as well as a disk viscosity of $\alpha_v = 10^{-3}$, the outer edge of the cavity should be located at 3.1$a$." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - assumption: Coplanar binary orbit and circumbinary disk  
  > "This assumes that the binary orbit is co-planar with the circumbinary disk, which is consistent with at least some of the orbits that we find as discussed earlier." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Compare SY Cha scattered light with ALMA morphology**: Fit the K-band scattered-light structure with inclination and PA constrained to ALMA values; compare the northern clump and arc with Band 6 over-brightness.  
  > "we overlay the extracted ellipse from our fit of the scattered light data with inclination and position angle constraint to the ALMA values" (SY Cha); "The fit was performed on the K-band data only, as the faint ring structure appears more clear in this data set." (SY Cha); "Both of these asymmetric structures roughly coincide with the over-brightness in the ALMA Band 6 data" (SY Cha)
   - assumption: Western side is the near side, based on stronger polarized intensity in H and K bands  
  > "Due to the higher polarized intensity signal in the west in both H- and K-band we assume that the western side is the near side of the disk." (SY Cha)
   - fitting: Two-step ellipse procedure with ALMA inclination and PA constraints  
  > "we overlay the extracted ellipse from our fit of the scattered light data with inclination and position angle constraint to the ALMA values" (SY Cha)
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Measure HD97048 brightness asymmetry**: Divide the original disk surface-brightness image by an image flipped along the disk minor axis.  
  > "There we show the ratio of the disk surface brightness between the original image and an image flipped along the disk minor axis." (HD 97048)
   - assumption: An axis-symmetric disk would give a ratio of 1.  
  > "For a perfectly symmetric disk, we would expect a ratio of 1 (i.e., axis symmetry with respect to the disk minor axis)." (HD 97048)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Most detected disks are smooth or faint; SZ Cha has multiple rings and WW Cha interacts with its environment.  
  > "The observations found typically smooth or faint disks, often with little substructure, with the notable exceptions of SZ Cha, which shows an extended multiple-ringed disk, and WW Cha, which shows interaction with the cloud environment." (abstract)
- Dust mass correlates with polarized contrast, but inner-disk shadowing can dominate brightness differences.  
  > "In particular the polarized contrast seems to increase with the logarithm of the dust mass." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast); "This suggests that the brightness in scattered light is at least in some cases dominated by geometric shadowing effects by inner-system material rather than overall depletion." (Summary and discussion)
- CS Cha has an ALMA millimeter cavity but no resolved scattered-light cavity.  
  > "It is interesting that the large cavity seen in ALMA continuum is not detected in the scattered light observations of the system." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
- SY Cha Band 6 continuum shows an asymmetric outer ring, inner cavity and unresolved inner disk; the scattered-light structures are tentative.  
  > "In the ALMA data the disk is resolved into an outer ring with an azimuthal asymmetry between the north-east and the south-west, an inner cavity and an unresolved inner disk component." (SY Cha); "Given the low S/N of the the scattered light data, we can at this point only speculate on the origin of the described features." (SY Cha)
- HD97048 brightness asymmetries may trace small-scale disk warps.  
  > "Following this logic the asymmetries observed in the HD 97048 system may be interpreted as small-scale warps within the disk, which change alignment moving radially within the disk." (HD 97048)
- SZ Cha has the most extreme recovered aspect ratio in the sample.  
  > "Our results indicate that this disk shows the most extreme aspect ratio within our sample" (Sample aspect ratio)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine scattered light with ALMA continuum and stellar properties to study age and dust-mass trends.  
  > "We combined the scattered light observations with existing literature data on stellar properties and with archival ALMA continuum data to study trends with system age and dust mass." (abstract)
- Why this method: Use ALMA geometry for CS Cha because it is unaffected by scattered-light illumination and phase-function effects; measure contrast along the major axis to reduce phase-function dependence.  
  > "Since the ALMA data are not affected by illumination and phase function effects we adopt the ALMA values for further analysis." (CS Cha); "Also, the measurement is performed along the major axis (approximately 90$^\circ$ scattering) to minimize the dependence on the scattering phase function." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast)
- Alternative: SY Cha Band 6 asymmetry may reflect enhanced material rather than increased temperature.  
  > "That the asymmetry is still present in the optically thinner Band 6 data may suggest that it is caused by an enhancement of material rather than an increase of temperature." (SY Cha)
- Alternative: SY Cha outer-disk faintness could arise from inner-disk misalignment or a puffed-up inner disk without misalignment.  
  > "If this inner disk is slightly misaligned with the faint outer disk structure that we traced in the ellipse fitting, then shadowing might explain the large brightness contrast between these two disk zones" (SY Cha); "Alternatively, the inner disk could simply be strongly puffed up due to stellar radiation, which may lead to full azimuthal shadowing of the outer disk, without the need to invoke a misalignment." (SY Cha)
- Alternative: The larger scattered-light inner component in SY Cha may reflect radial drift or an optical-depth effect.  
  > "That the signal is more extended in scattered light than in ALMA millimeter continuum may be explained by radial drift of the larger dust particles ((cite: Weidenschilling1977; Villenave2019))." (SY Cha); "However, it may also be possible that we trace an optical depth effect" (SY Cha)
- Alternative: CV Cha brightness dips could be a steep scattering phase function or shadows from disk misalignment.  
  > "With our data it is ultimately not possible to decide whether we see a steep phase function effect or shadows caused by disk misalignment." (CV Cha)
- Alternative: CS Cha retrograde and low-inclination orbits cannot be ruled out.  
  > "Somewhat unsurprisingly, given our limited astrometric data, we can not rule out retrograde orbits with an inclination of $\sim$140$^\circ$." (CS Cha: Stellar binary); "However, we note that we cannot rule out lower inclination orbits with our existing data" (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
- How they decided it was enough: Trace ring peaks or the 3σ disk edge; omit height-refinement fits for faint disks lacking sufficiently constrained geometry or complementary ALMA data.  
  > "For the disks without ringed substructure, we trace the outer disk edge at which the disk signal drops below 3$\sigma$ as determined from the image background." (Fitting procedure); "for the faint disks around Sz 45, TW Cha and VZ Cha the uncertainty of the disk inclination or position angle was large and no complementary ALMA data were available, thus we omitted the second fitting step" (Fitting procedure)
- Limitation (authors): Band 7 flux-derived dust masses may be affected by optical thickness and viewing geometry.  
  > "the disk may well be partially optically thick in band 7 and the measured fluxes may depend not just on dust mass and temperature but also on the viewing geometry." (Disk polarized scattered light contrast)
- Limitation (authors): Six of eleven disk aspect-ratio measurements have large uncertainties.  
  > "the aspect ratios for 6 of these systems (CR Cha, CS Cha, SY Cha, SZ 45, TW Cha, VZ Cha) have large uncertainties" (Sample aspect ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Edge-based aspect ratios are systematically low by approximately 0.05.  
  > "We do however observe a small systematic offset of $\sim0.05$ (i.e., the retrieved aspect ratio is slightly too low)." (Geometric fitting approach and results)
- Limitation (authors): SY Cha scattered-light features have low S/N and their origins are speculative.  
  > "Given the low S/N of the the scattered light data, we can at this point only speculate on the origin of the described features." (SY Cha)
- Limitation (authors): CS Cha H-band photometry may have a systematic calibration effect.  
  > "This may have introduced a systematic effect in the photometric calibration of this data set, as was already discussed for the same data in (cite: Ginski2018)." (CS Cha)
- Limitation (authors): The CS Cha dust model does not match the observed J–K color; a larger parameter exploration is outside scope.  
  > "However, we note that the model predicts a slightly blue J-K color, while our observations found a slightly red color." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis); "A large exploration of the parameter space may be necessary to fully reconcile our observations with the radiative transfer models, which is outside of the scope of this study." (CS Cha: Scattered light color analysis)
- Limitation (authors): CS Cha component-mass errors may omit differences among isochrone models.  
  > "This uncertainty may be underestimated, given that different isochrone models may well yield slightly different results." (CS Cha: Stellar binary)
- Limitation (authors): The optical selection limits the sample to roughly solar-like and intermediate-mass stars.  
  > "The main selection criterion was the optical brightness of the targets (Gmag \< 13 mag)" (Sample and stellar properties); "We are therefore nearly complete within the population of solar-like and intermediate-mass stars" (Sample and stellar properties)
- Limitation (authors): A larger unbiased sample is required to establish an age–brightness connection.  
  > "A larger unbiased sample size is needed in order to draw a clear conclusion on the connection between disk brightness and stellar age." (Summary and discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reported dust-mass correlation needs an explicit account of upper limits, nondetections and shared distance or selection effects.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CS Cha cavity comparison uses a coplanar calculation with a different mass parameter from the inferred system; it does not uniquely establish an additional planet.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SY Cha planet and vortex interpretations rely on low-S/N morphology without a quantitative cross-band significance test.
