## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify closure phase residuals on intermediate-baseline triangles**: Compare observed closure phases on the ALMA-PV-NOEMA and ALMA-SMT-KP triangles (April 18, 2021, non-self-calibrated data) against predictions from compact ring-only images reconstructed by Difmap, DoG-HIT, and Comrade.  
  > "To overcome this problem, we analyze data that were not self-calibrated, and use closure phases that are insensitive to station-based gain errors (e.g., Blackburn et al. 2020) to probe the jet base emission at scales accessible with SMT-KP and PV-NOEMA." (3.1. Bump and offset in closure phases); "We thus focused on the ALMA-SMT-KP and ALMA-PV-NOEMA triangles in this analysis." (3.1. Bump and offset in closure phases); "The middle and bottom panels display the triangles of interest obtained for the small-scale ring images for different methods—one representative image is chosen for each framework— Difmap (CLEAN), DoG-HIT (RML) and Comrade (Bayesian)." (3.1. Bump and offset in closure phases)
   - software: Difmap, DoG-HIT, Comrade
   - parameter: Triangles: ALMA-PV-NOEMA and ALMA-SMT-KP; Date: April 18, 2021  
  > "The ALMA-PV-NOEMA (middle) and ALMA-SMT-KP (bottom) closure phases on M87* observed on April 18, 2021 (figure is adapted from EHTC et al. (2025)) are shown together with ring-image models obtained with different imaging algorithms (see text for details)." (2. EHT observations of M87* in 2021)
   - assumption: Small-scale ring emission is fixed by long-baseline reconstructions and intermediate-scale jet base emission is derived from residuals between observed closure phases and the ring-only image.  
  > "Since the small-scale information of the long ALMA baselines in those triangles cannot be disentangled from the intermediate-scale information, the jet base information has to be derived from the difference between the data and the ring-only image." (3.1. Bump and offset in closure phases)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-validation across seven imaging teams and synthetic data tests with an extended jet component in EHTC et al. (2025).  
  > "In EHTC et al. (2025), the data were analyzed by seven teams using different imaging algorithms. The robustness of the reconstruction was demonstrated via cross-validation." (2. EHT observations of M87* in 2021); "Finally, synthetic data tests were performed in EHTC et al. (2025). These tests also include the validation of a ring image with an extended jet component." (4.2. Symmetric Gaussian component)
2. **Grid search and parameter exploration of an offset Gaussian component**: Add a symmetric Gaussian component onto the DoG-HIT compact ring image and evaluate closure phase fits and reduced chi-square across a grid of positions, flux densities, and FWHM sizes.  
  > "We base our analysis on a DoG-HIT reconstruction: since the algorithm separates small and large scale emission naturally, it is optimized to fit to the closure phases and closure amplitudes in the 2021 data." (4.1. Impact of the model parameters); "Figure 3 shows the ring image obtained by DoG-HIT with an additional Gaussian added on top, varying some of the parameters described in Section 3.2." (4.1. Impact of the model parameters); "In the left panel we show an example of reduced χ2 (χ2ν) of the ALMA-PV-NOEMA triangle as a function of the location of the Gaussian component with a fixed flux density of 60 mJy and a size of 180 µas." (4.1. Impact of the model parameters)
   - software: DoG-HIT
   - parameter: Fixed flux density 60 mJy and size 180 microarcseconds for spatial chi-square map over [-400, 400] microarcseconds in relative RA and Dec  
  > "In the left panel we show an example of reduced χ2 (χ2ν) of the ALMA-PV-NOEMA triangle as a function of the location of the Gaussian component with a fixed flux density of 60 mJy and a size of 180 µas." (4.1. Impact of the model parameters)
   - assumption: Source consists of the compact DoG-HIT ring plus a single faint symmetric Gaussian component.  
  > "Based on these simple considerations, we propose the following source model: the compact ring emission as seen on long baselines, with an additional, faint, Gaussian component located at scales 100 − 2000 µas from the center of the ring emission." (3.2. Model)
   - fitting: Reduced chi-square (chi^2_nu) evaluation on ALMA-PV-NOEMA, ALMA-SMT-KP, and combined triangles.  
  > "The left and middle panels show the χ2ν for the ALMA-PV-NOEMA and the ALMA-SMT-KP triangles, respectively, and the right panel shows the combined χ2ν." (Fig. 4 caption)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **MCMC posterior estimation for symmetric, asymmetric, and double Gaussian models**: Fit the parameters of an additional Gaussian component (4-parameter symmetric, 6-parameter elliptical/asymmetric, and 12-parameter double Gaussian) on top of the DoG-HIT ring reconstruction using MCMC sampling.  
  > "To obtain the maximum posterior position for a Gaussian component, we use the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013)." (4.2. Symmetric Gaussian component); "To explore the possibility of asymmetry of the jet base emission we extend the model by two additional parameters: the ratio of fwhm_maj and fwhm_min (R) and the rotation angle of the asymmetric Gaussian component Φ, see Appendix C for additional details." (4.3. Asymmetric Gaussian component); "In this way, we test the presence of the second component in the northern region, but allow all parameters to vary (12 parameters)." (4.4. Two Gaussians)
   - software: emcee, DoG-HIT
   - parameter: 4-parameter symmetric model; 6-parameter asymmetric model; 12-parameter double Gaussian model  
  > "Figure A.1 displays the corner plot of the four-parameter Gaussian model, Figure A.2 displays the corner plot of the six-parameter Gaussian model, and Figure A.3 displays the fit with a twelve-parameter double Gaussian model." (Appendix A: Corner plots)
   - assumption: Compact ring emission is held fixed to the DoG-HIT reconstruction while Gaussian component parameters vary.  
  > "We base our analysis on a DoG-HIT reconstruction: since the algorithm separates small and large scale emission naturally, it is optimized to fit to the closure phases and closure amplitudes in the 2021 data." (4.1. Impact of the model parameters)
   - fitting: MCMC sampler emcee with uniform priors: F0 in [0, 0.2] Jy, x, y in [-500, 500] microarcseconds, fwhm_s in [50, 250] microarcseconds, R in [0, 1], Phi in [-180, 180] deg, and y2 in [0, 500] microarcseconds for the second Gaussian.  
  > "We adopt uniform priors for the flux (F0 ∈ [0, 0.2] Jy), the location (x, y ∈ [−500, 500] µas), and the width (fwhm_s ∈ [50, 250] µas)." (4.2. Symmetric Gaussian component); "We again use uniform priors (R ∈ [0, 1], and Φ ∈ [−180◦, 180◦])." (4.3. Asymmetric Gaussian component); "However, given the complexity of the model and limitations of the data, we constrain the second component in the positive y direction, that is, we allow the y position to vary only within [0, 500µas]." (4.4. Two Gaussians)
   - validation: Checked reduced chi-square across all closure triangles and verified that visibility amplitudes on baselines > 2 Glambda are unaffected and the Gaussian component is absorbed into amplitude self-calibration.  
  > "The component has almost negligible impact on all other triangles; the χ2ν over all triangles improves from 1.5 to 1.39." (4.2. Symmetric Gaussian component); "Amplitudes on baselines longer than ∼ 2 Gλ stay mostly unaffected by the additional Gaussian component. This is reflected by the fit to the self-calibrated amplitudes shown in panel g and j in Figure 5 and the left panel in Figure B.2." (4.2. Symmetric Gaussian component)
4. **Test alternative physical and imaging scenarios and systematic errors**: Test (1) stacking the wavelet-filtered 86 GHz GMVA jet structure onto the 230 GHz EHT ring and grid-searching its rescaled flux density, (2) gradient-descent closure-phase fitting with DoG-HIT under three masking regimes (unmasked, 1% ring-contour localized, and wavelet scale+localization constrained), (3) forward-modeling the 2021 Gaussian onto 2017/2018 Chile-LMT-SMT closure phases, and (4) checking RR - LL closure phase differences for polarization leakage.  
  > "we filter out the ‘ring-components’ in the 86 GHz image by setting all smallscale wavelets to zero, then we add the 230 GHz EHT ring image and the jet emission at 86 GHz seen by the GMVA." (5.1 GMVA jet structure); "For each approach, we take the ring-only image from DoG-HIT and try to improve the fit quality to the closure phases only, using a gradient descent approach with a small step size." (5.2. Imaging with weakened assumption on compactness); "One data-driven test to rule out leakage as the cause of the bump and offset is to compare the phase-residuals of the RR − LL closure phases on the respective triangles (Figure D.1)." (Appendix D: Impact of non-closing errors)
   - software: DoG-HIT
   - parameter: GMVA jet flux density grid-searched between 0.0 Jy and 0.2 Jy; 1% intensity contour mask for localized ring test  
  > "Motivated by the findings in Section 4.1, the GMVA jet image is rescaled to add a flux density between 0.0 Jy and 0.2 Jy to match the 230 GHz ring image." (5.1. GMVA jet structure); "Here, we add a constraint on the emission location by performing the same gradient descent fit to the closure phases but only allowing the pixels on the ring to vary, defined by the 1% intensity contour in the DoG-HIT imagesy." (5.2.2. Fit with localization constraint)
   - assumption: Ring features at 230 GHz and 86 GHz are aligned when stacking; intrinsic circular polarization approaches zero for the RR - LL leakage test.  
  > "The images are stacked by aligning the ring features observed at 230 and 86 GHz." (6. Discussion); "Under the assumptions that there is no significant leakage and that circular polarization is −→ 0, the difference between the parallel hands closure phases is zero." (Appendix D: Impact of non-closing errors)
   - fitting: Grid search over [0.0, 0.2] Jy for GMVA jet scaling; gradient descent on closure phases for DoG-HIT imaging tests.  
  > "We grid-search for the best flux density in this interval and find the best χ2 at an additional flux density of 0.06 Jy, consistent with the findings in Section 4.2." (5.1. GMVA jet structure); "For each approach, we take the ring-only image from DoG-HIT and try to improve the fit quality to the closure phases only, using a gradient descent approach with a small step size." (5.2. Imaging with weakened assumption on compactness)
   - validation: Compared predicted closure phases on 2017 and 2018 Chile-LMT-SMT triangle against observational noise and evaluated RR vs LL parallel-hand closure phases.  
  > "In both years, the effect of the additional Gaussian component on the Chile-LMT-SMT closure phases is an order of magnitude smaller than the respective noise." (Fig. 10 caption); "While there is a small offset on the ALMA-PV-NOEMA triangle, neither the bump nor the offset can be explained by a difference between the parallel hands, and we thus conclude that the signal is not caused by polarization leakage." (Appendix D: Impact of non-closing errors)

## Qualitative findings

- Compact ring-only models leave small but significant closure phase residuals (a bump on ALMA-PV-NOEMA and an offset on ALMA-SMT-KP) of a few degrees in the 2021 EHT observations.  
  > "The image models are discrepant with the observed data by a few degrees, which may be attributable to intermediate-scale emission observed on the PV-NOEMA and SMT-KP baselines." (2. EHT observations of M87* in 2021); "In particular, the ALMA-PV-NOEMA triangle features a ‘bump’ that is not well captured by the ring images, and, similarly, ALMA-SMT-KP has a visible ‘offset’." (3.1. Bump and offset in closure phases)
- The closure phase residuals are well explained by a faint (~60-70 mJy), ~170-180 microarcsecond FWHM Gaussian component located southwest of the compact ring (~300 microarcseconds West, ~60-70 microarcseconds South), which aligns with the southern arm of the 86 GHz GMVA edge-brightened jet.  
  > "By visual inspection, the jet base Gaussian component studied here aligns to first order with the southern arm of the edge-brightened jet observed with the GMVA." (6. Discussion); "Fitting a simple geometric model – a single offset Gaussian – yields a flux density of ∼ 60 mJy at a location ≈ (−60, −320) µas relative to the ring center. The Gaussian component has fwhm ≈ 180 µas." (7. Conclusions)
- Most of the ~1 Jy missing flux in EHT observations of M87* does not reside on the intermediate spatial scales (~250 to 2500 microarcseconds) probed by PV-NOEMA and SMT-KP, and must originate from larger spatial scales.  
  > "Our analysis revealed a Gaussian feature centered at (∆R.A. ≈ 320 µas, ∆Dec. ≈ 60 µas), projected separation of ≈ 5500 AU, with an estimated flux density of only ∼ 60 mJy, implying that most of the missing flux identified in previous EHT studies had to originate from different, larger scales." (abstract)
- The faint intermediate-scale Gaussian component has a negligible impact on baselines longer than ~2 Glambda and did not affect the 2017 and 2018 EHT ring reconstructions.  
  > "Amplitudes on baselines longer than ∼ 2 Gλ stay mostly unaffected by the additional Gaussian component." (4.2. Symmetric Gaussian component); "Additionally, the Gaussian component identified in this work is not detectable on any baseline in the EHT longer than the PVNOEMA baseline, and thus was not detectable/had no measurable impact on observations conducted in 2017 and 2018." (5.3 Impact on previous EHT observations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The 2021 EHT campaign added NOEMA and the Kitt Peak 12-m telescope, introducing two intermediate baselines (PV-NOEMA ~1100 km and SMT-KP ~100 km) sensitive to ~250 and ~2500 microarcsecond scales; April 18 was selected because NOEMA did not observe on April 13 and April 14 and 17 had shorter tracks.  
  > "The 2021 EHT observations offer the best (u, v)-coverage to date, providing a new opportunity to detect the faint, extended emission associated with the jet. In particular, this is aided by the addition of two new stations; the NOrthern Extended Millimeter Array (NOEMA) in France and the Kitt Peak 12-m telescope (KP) in the USA." (1. Introduction); "Since April 14 and 17 were relatively shorter tracks and NOEMA did not participate in April 13, we used only April 18." (2. EHT observations of M87* in 2021)
- Why this method: Because (u, v) coverage at intermediate scales consists of only two single baselines, direct imaging is too underconstrained; geometric model-fitting on closure phases formed with high-sensitivity ALMA avoids station-based phase/gain errors and self-calibration biases.  
  > "Due to the limited number of baselines sensitive to intermediate angular scales, the (u, v) coverage is too sparse to directly image the faint, extended structure." (1. Introduction); "As a consequence, much like in the case of other very sparse VLBI data sets (e.g., Wielgus et al. 2020), we resort to a geometric model-fitting approach to constrain the presence of extended emission in the 2021 EHT data." (1. Introduction); "To overcome this problem, we analyze data that were not self-calibrated, and use closure phases that are insensitive to station-based gain errors (e.g., Blackburn et al. 2020) to probe the jet base emission at scales accessible with SMT-KP and PV-NOEMA." (3.1. Bump and offset in closure phases)
- Alternative: Asymmetric (elliptical) Gaussian and double Gaussian models: considered and fitted, but rejected as unnecessary because they do not significantly improve reduced chi-square over a single symmetric Gaussian.  
  > "The χ2ν values are 1.28 for the bump and 1.10 for the offset, i.e., there is no significant improvement to the fit." (4.3. Asymmetric Gaussian component); "The χ2ν values are 1.29 for the bump and 1.10 for the offset. Thus, there is no statistical evidence for the inclusion of a secondary Gaussian component." (4.4. Two Gaussians)
- Alternative: Stacking the rescaled 86 GHz GMVA jet structure onto the 230 GHz ring: improves the fit to the bump and offset (best at 0.06 Jy) but performs worse than the simple symmetric Gaussian model.  
  > "The fit to the offset and the bump is improved by including the jet component. However, our model of the 86 GHz jet is not able to provide a good fit, performing worse than the simpler" (5.1 GMVA jet structure)
- Alternative: Unmasked DoG-HIT gradient-descent imaging of closure phases: fits the bump and offset but produces a nonphysical 'waffle-like pattern' corresponding to the PV-NOEMA and SMT-KP baseline frequencies.  
  > "we observe that the improved precision has been achieved by fitting a ‘waffle-like pattern’ associated with the PVNOEMA and SMT-KP baselines, which is nonphysical." (5.2.1 Fit without spatial scale and localization constraints)
- Alternative: Ring-localized DoG-HIT gradient-descent imaging without spatial scale constraints: fits the bump via sub-resolution (< 20 microarcsecond) ring asymmetries, but fails to fit the ALMA-SMT-KP offset.  
  > "In this case, we can fit the bump reasonably well but have issues fitting the offset. A closer inspection of the structures that fit the bump in the top right panel of Figure 9 indicates that this fitting is achieved by asymmetries in the ring itself through a number of components." (5.2.2. Fit with localization constraint)
- Alternative: Polarization leakage as the cause of the closure phase bump and offset: tested via RR - LL parallel-hand closure phase comparison and ruled out.  
  > "While there is a small offset on the ALMA-PV-NOEMA triangle, neither the bump nor the offset can be explained by a difference between the parallel hands, and we thus conclude that the signal is not caused by polarization leakage." (Appendix D: Impact of non-closing errors)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): With only two intermediate baselines (PV-NOEMA and SMT-KP), the (u, v) coverage is too sparse to robustly reconstruct the morphology of the jet base emission, and the model remains weakly constrained with posterior degeneracies.  
  > "While our findings point to detectable jet base emission at 230 GHz, the limited coverage provided by only two intermediate baselines limits our ability to robustly reconstruct its morphology." (abstract); "However, the presence of multiple regions with low χ2ν indicates that the current model remains weakly constrained and degenerate." (4.1. Impact of the model parameters)
- Limitation (authors): Other unknown sources of non-closing systematic errors corrupting the single-baseline/small-triangle measurements cannot be completely ruled out.  
  > "Nevertheless, we cannot rule out the possibility that other unknown sources of systematic error may corrupt the data." (5.2.4. Data corruptions and systematic errors); "While we rule out polarization leakage as the origin of the signal and have performed imaging and synthetic data checks, unknown systematic errors cannot be completely ruled out." (7. Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): The detected component (~300 microarcseconds from the core) is too far out to draw conclusions about jet launching physics within the first ten Schwarzschild radii.  
  > "While this is an anticipated observable for the EHT and its successors to connect the jet dynamics to the black hole and study jet launching mechanisms (EHTC 2024), we note that the jet base component found in this study is too far out to make reliable conclusions about the physics of the jet within the first ten Schwarzschild radii." (6. Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The entire constraint on intermediate-scale emission rests on small closure-phase deviations (a few degrees) on just two triangles (ALMA-PV-NOEMA and ALMA-SMT-KP) from a single observing track (April 18, 2021).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a sign/axis-label inconsistency in the text when summarizing the Gaussian position: Table 1 and Fig. 4 give (Delta RA, Delta Dec) = (x, y) around (-304, -68) microarcseconds (southwest), whereas the abstract writes (Delta R.A. ~ 320 microarcseconds, Delta Dec. ~ 60 microarcseconds) and the conclusions write (-60, -320) microarcseconds.
