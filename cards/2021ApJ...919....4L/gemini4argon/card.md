## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure and model laboratory rotational spectrum of propionamide and compute partition function**: Measure microwave/mm/sub-mm transitions (9-461 GHz) in three laboratories and fit rotational transitions of ground (v=0) and excited torsional states (v=1) simultaneously with methyl torsion states using the Rho axis method (RAM); calculate rotational and vibrational partition functions.  
  > "The new laboratory measurements of the propionamide spectrum were carried out in the 9– 461 GHz range, providing a good opportunity to check directly for the transition frequencies of detected interstellar lines of propionamide." (2. SPECTROSCOPY); "Analysis of the spectra was performed using the Rho axis method (RAM) and the RAM36hf program (Ilyushin et al. 2010; Belloche et al. 2017)." (2. SPECTROSCOPY)
   - software: RAM36hf
   - parameter: K_a <= 10, J <= 50 for ground state; F fixed at 5.55 cm^-1; predictions limited to uncertainty < 0.1 MHz  
  > "In the ground state (v 29, v 30 ) = (0, 0) (denoted as v=0), the rotational transitions up to K a = 10, and J up to 50 were included in the fit." (2. SPECTROSCOPY); "We fixed the F parameter at 5.55 cm [−1], a value that corresponds to methyl top moment of inertia I α ≈ 3.2 a.m.u. ." (2. SPECTROSCOPY); "We limit our predictions to the transitions with calculated uncertainties lower than 0.1 MHz." (2. SPECTROSCOPY)
   - assumption: Dipole moment components mu_a = 0.6359(60) D and mu_b = 3.496(30) D from Marstokk et al. (1996); vibrational modes up to 1000 cm^-1  
  > "We used dipole moment components obtained by Marstokk et al. (1996) via Stark measurements μ a = 0.6359(60) D and μ b = 3.496(30) D" (2. SPECTROSCOPY); "For the calculation of the vibrational partition function, we used the excitation energies E i of all normal modes of propionamide up to 1000 cm [−1] ." (2. SPECTROSCOPY)
   - fitting: Least-squares Hamiltonian fit in RAM36hf (26 parameters for v=0 with rms 0.035 MHz; 25 parameters for v=1 with rms 0.046 MHz)  
  > "The RAM Hamiltonian model included 26 parameters (with one fixed parameter F ). The final set of molecular parameters is presented in Table 1. The overall root mean square deviation of the fit was 0.035 MHz" (2. SPECTROSCOPY)
   - validation: Checked all clean and partially blended ISM lines directly against laboratory experimental records  
  > "To exclude all doubts in rest frequencies of the propionamide transitions detected in the ISM, we have checked against laboratory experimental records all clean and partially blended lines detected." (2. SPECTROSCOPY)
2. **Select low-blending target position Sgr B2(N1E) using integrated intensity maps**: Map integrated intensity of the clean 92.143 GHz propionamide line (92142 to 92143.5 MHz) and compare with CH13CN contours to pick a position offset from Sgr B2(N1) where propionamide is strong and blending/continuum opacity is weak.  
  > "By mapping the integrated intensity of 92.143 GHz line of propionamide and comparing to that of CH [13] CN (Fig. 2), we selected a position in which propionamide is relatively strong and other molecular emissions are relatively weak." (4.2.1. Position selection)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Selected position 17:47:19.99, -28:22:18.72 (J2000), ~1.5'' east of Sgr B2(N1), region size ~0.3''  
  > "The selected region is about 1.5 [ʹʹ] to the east of the hot core Sgr B2(N1). The coordinate is 17:47:19.99, -28:22:18.72(J2000). The size of selected region is ∼ 0.3 [ʹʹ] ." (4.2.1. Position selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Subtract continuum emission in the image plane**: Select 3-5 groups of line-free channels in each spectral window and fit and subtract a first-order baseline in the image plane.  
  > "For each spectral window, we selected 3-5 groups of channels that seem to be free of line emission. A first-order baseline was fitted to these channels." (4.2.2. Removal of continuum emission.)
   - software: GILDAS, CLASS
   - parameter: 3-5 groups of line-free channels per spectral window; first-order baseline  
  > "For each spectral window, we selected 3-5 groups of channels that seem to be free of line emission. A first-order baseline was fitted to these channels." (4.2.2. Removal of continuum emission.)
   - assumption: Continuum emission follows the spectral energy distribution of free-free emission  
  > "The subtracted continuum emission of lines that is obviously offset from the spectrum energy distribution of free-free emission, is regarded as being affected by line confusion or absorption." (4.2.2. Removal of continuum emission.)
   - fitting: First-order baseline fit  
  > "A first-order baseline was fitted to these channels." (4.2.2. Removal of continuum emission.)
   - validation: Checked that subtracted continuum fluxes across frequencies match the spectral energy distribution expected for free-free emission  
  > "The overall flux dependence on the frequencies is well consistent with the spectrum energy distribution that is expected by free-free emission." (4.2.2. Removal of continuum emission.)
4. **Derive C18O and H2 column densities toward Sgr B2(N1E)**: Model ALMA C18O spectrum with Weeds under LTE and optically thin assumptions, then scale by adopted N(H2)/N(C18O) ratio.  
  > "The C [18] O column density has been obtained with WEEDS by assuming optically thin emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium" (4.2.3. H 2 column density)
   - software: GILDAS, Weeds
   - parameter: T_ex = 150 K  
  > "An uniform excitation temperature T ex of 150 K (Belloche et al. 2019) was adopted." (4.2.3. H 2 column density)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission, LTE, T_ex = 150 K, N(H2)/N(C18O) = 1.4 x 10^7 (two times the Orion KL value of 7 x 10^6)  
  > "The C [18] O column density has been obtained with WEEDS by assuming optically thin emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium" (4.2.3. H 2 column density); "We adopt an abundance ratio N(H 2 )/N(C [18] O) two times of that in Orion KL (7 × 10 [6] cm [-2], Castets & Langer (1995))." (4.2.3. H 2 column density)
   - fitting: LTE synthetic spectrum modeling in Weeds  
  > "The C [18] O column density has been obtained with WEEDS by assuming optically thin emission and local thermodynamic equilibrium" (4.2.3. H 2 column density)
   - validation: _not stated_

  > "The 3-mm emission of propionamide toward Sgr B2(N1E) is modeled assuming LTE conditions with five parameters: column density, temperature, source size, velocity offset, and linewidth. We model each spectral window of each observed setup separately to account for the varying angular resolution." (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide); "With the derived size, a linewidth measurement of 4.2(.5) km s [−1 ] with Gaussian fit of 92.408 and 103.342 GHz lines in CLASS, and an assumption of excitation temperature of 150 K" (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide)
   - software: GILDAS, CLASS, Weeds
   - parameter: Linewidth = 4.2(0.5) km s^-1, T_ex = 150 K, source size ~ 0.3''; same parameters used for v=0 and v=1 states  
  > "A same set of the five parameters is used to model the C 2 H 5 CONH 2 v=0 and v=1 states." (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide); "With the derived size, a linewidth measurement of 4.2(.5) km s [−1 ] with Gaussian fit of 92.408 and 103.342 GHz lines in CLASS, and an assumption of excitation temperature of 150 K" (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide)
   - assumption: LTE conditions with T_ex = 150 K  
  > "The 3-mm emission of propionamide toward Sgr B2(N1E) is modeled assuming LTE conditions with five parameters: column density, temperature, source size, velocity offset, and linewidth." (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit in CLASS for linewidth; 5-parameter LTE modeling in Weeds  
  > "With the derived size, a linewidth measurement of 4.2(.5) km s [−1 ] with Gaussian fit of 92.408 and 103.342 GHz lines in CLASS, and an assumption of excitation temperature of 150 K" (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide)
   - validation: Checked consistency across all ~110 expected v=0 and ~40 expected v=1 transitions >3sigma so that blended lines do not contradict expected intensities

## Qualitative findings

- Tentative detection of propionamide (C2H5CONH2) toward Sgr B2(N1E) based on 6 clean v=0 transitions, ~17 partially blended v=0 transitions, and 8 partially blended v=1 transitions.  
  > "Six clean lines were detected toward Sgr B2(N1E). These lines are presented in Fig. 5 and Table 3. Approximately 17 partially blended lines were also detected." (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide); "About 40 C 2 H 5 CONH 2 v=1 lines with expected intensities above 3σ noise level were expected. Of these, eight partially blend lines were detected (Fig. 7)" (4.2.4. Detection of propionamide)
- Formamide, acetamide, and propionamide exhibit very similar spatial distributions in Sgr B2(N1), suggesting a consistent formation mechanism for the amide family.  
  > "The integrated intensity maps of CH 3 CONH 2 and NH 2 CHO indicate that the amide family shows quite similar spatial distributions, implying a consistent formation mechanism in the ISM." (4.2.5. Related molecules)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: IRAM 30m single-dish observations (~24'' beam) suffered from severe line blending and confusion from multiple hot cores in Sgr B2(N), requiring high angular resolution (0.6'' x 0.5'') ALMA ReMoCA archival data to isolate a less-blended offset position (Sgr B2(N1E)).  
  > "That is why as the next step of our search we concentrated on ALMA archive data (see Section 3.2) which provided the HPBW for a synthesized beam of order of 0.6" × 0.5" thus allowing a search for a position in which propionamide is less contaminated by line blending from other molecules." (4.1. IRAM 30m result)
- Why this method: Continuum subtraction was performed in the image plane rather than the Fourier domain because the high density of spectral lines, varying systemic velocities, and velocity gradients make uv-plane splitting impossible.  
  > "As stated in Belloche et al. (2019), because of the large number of spectral lines, their different systemic velocities, and the velocity gradients, it is impossible to split the line and continuum emission in the Fourier domain. Therefore we performed the splitting in the image plane." (4.2.2. Removal of continuum emission.)
- Alternative: Identifying propionamide using IRAM 30m single-dish data or at the Sgr B2(N1) continuum peak was rejected due to severe line blending and optically thick free-free continuum emission.  
  > "For the IRAM data we have considered a number of propionamide emission lines among which two lines at 86954 MHz and 87980 MHz looked most promising. Unfortunately our pre-analysis showed that even these two lines suffer from serious line blending from other molecules." (4.1. IRAM 30m result); "However, the spectrum toward the continuum peak position of Sgr B2(N1) is severely affected by the optically thick free- free emission from the HII regions, and the weak COM transitions also suffer from serious line blending from other molecules (Belloche et al. 2019)." (4.2.1. Position selection)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Detection is reported as tentative because only six transitions are completely unblended; remaining transitions are partially or heavily blended with other species.  
  > "Based on the six clean lines of propionamide toward Sgr B2(N1E), which are consistent with the LTE synthetic spectrum of propionamide, we reported the tentative detection of propionamide in Sgr B2." (5. DISCUSSION)
- Limitation (authors): High column density and optical depth cause saturation in several NH2CHO transitions toward Sgr B2(N1) and Sgr B2(N1E).  
  > "Due to the high column density and large optical depth of NH 2 CHO toward Sgr B2(N1), some lines are saturated, such as the 102065 MHz, 105465 MHz, and 106108 MHz lines." (4.2.5. Related molecules)
- Limitation (authors): Calculated transition frequency uncertainties do not include systematic errors from intervibrational interactions between methyl torsion and skeletal torsion modes, requiring restriction to K_a < 10.  
  > "It should be noted that calculated uncertainties for transition frequencies can not predict possible systematic errors due to intervibrational interactions between methyl torsion and skeletal torsion modes in the molecule, since those interactions are not taken explicitly into account by our current Hamiltonian model." (2. SPECTROSCOPY)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Excitation temperature of 150 K is assumed rather than fitted independently for propionamide and C18O.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): H2 column density relies on an assumed Galactic Center C18O-to-H2 conversion factor (twice the Orion KL value) which carries substantial systematic uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): With only 6 clean lines in a line-rich hot-core spectrum, line confusion remains a major caveat, justifying the 'tentative' label.
