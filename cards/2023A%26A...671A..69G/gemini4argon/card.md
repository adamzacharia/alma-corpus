- Fields: sun
- Question: Identify and study magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) waves and their energy flux in bright small-scale magnetic features in the solar chromosphere using ALMA Band 3 and Band 6 observations alongside Bifrost simulations.

## Analysis recipe

1. **Synthesize and degrade simulation continuum brightness temperature maps to match ALMA observations**: Solve radiative transfer with ART on Bifrost simulation snapshots to get Band 3 and Band 6 brightness temperatures, subsample frames to 2 s cadence with scan gaps matching ALMA, convolve with the median ALMA synthetic elliptical beam, and rebin pixels to ALMA pixel sizes.  
  > "We used the Advanced Radiative Transfer code (cite: 2021_art) which solves the radiative transfer equation under the assumption of Local Thermodynamics Equilibrium (LTE)." (Simulation); "For this, we have arbitrary chosen the snapshot corresponding to 700 s as starting point and so on removed every other frame, constructing a new time series wit the frames 700, 702, 704 and etc, until eventually we matched both ALMA observations time stamps." (Simulation); "The spatial resolution was amended through convolving each snapshot with the corresponding ALMA synthetic elliptical median beam to simulate ALMA’s angular resolution." (Simulation)
   - software: Bifrost, ART
   - parameter: Band 3 simulation snapshots: [700-1288, 1428-2022, 2172-2764]; Band 6 simulation snapshots: [700-1182, 1294-1770, 1913-2389, 2499-2981, 3120-3354]  
  > "In the case of Band 3, the 3 scans are composed of the snapshots [700-1288, 1428-2022, 2172-2764]. For the case of Band 6, the 5 scans correspond to the snapshots [700-1182, 1294-1770, 1913-2389, 2499-2981, 3120-3354]." (Simulation)
   - parameter: Degraded pixel sizes: 0.34 arcsec (Band 3) and 0.14 arcsec (Band 6)  
  > "Subsequently, the pixel size of the simulation was changed from 0.066 arcsec to the respective 0.34 arcsec in Band 3 and 0.14 arcsec in Band 6." (Simulation)
   - assumption: LTE radiative transfer using non-equilibrium electron densities from Bifrost; perfect spatial Fourier space sampling approximated by Gaussian beam convolution  
  > "We used the Advanced Radiative Transfer code (cite: 2021_art) which solves the radiative transfer equation under the assumption of Local Thermodynamics Equilibrium (LTE). In this work, ART uses the non-equilibrium electron densities provided by the Bifrost simulations" (Simulation); "Please note that this approach assumes a perfect sampling of the spatial Fourier space while, in reality, ALMA's Fourier space coverage is sparse." (Simulation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested a different starting snapshot for time series matching and confirmed it did not change final results  
  > "As a side note, we also did a test with a different starting snapshot and it did not change the final results" (Simulation)
2. **Automatically identify, segment, track, and classify oscillating small-scale bright features**: Apply a Difference of Gaussians (DoG) filter to each frame, find local maxima, segment features via Flood fill on normalized inverted images, filter out elongated/large masks, track overlapping masks frame-to-frame, and cluster extracted peak-temperature light curves using DBSCAN to select oscillating features.  
  > "we applied to each frame a Difference of Gaussians (DoG) filter with Gaussian widths of half and double the median major axis of the ALMA beam." (Feature Identification and Tracking); "Then, for each frame, we saved the position of all local maxima separated by at least 1.5 times the median major beam. Subsequently, we used the positions of the local maxima as seed points in the Flood fill method implemented in the Python Scikit-image library (cite: scikit-image)." (Feature Identification and Tracking); "These light curves are classified by means of the Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) algorithm which is part of the Python Machine Learning Scikit-learn package (cite: scikit-learn)." (Feature Identification and Tracking)
   - software: SALAT, Scikit-image, Scikit-learn
   - parameter: DoG filter Gaussian widths: 0.5 and 2.0 times the median major axis of the ALMA beam; local maxima minimum separation: 1.5 times the median major beam  
  > "we applied to each frame a Difference of Gaussians (DoG) filter with Gaussian widths of half and double the median major axis of the ALMA beam." (Feature Identification and Tracking); "Then, for each frame, we saved the position of all local maxima separated by at least 1.5 times the median major beam." (Feature Identification and Tracking)
   - parameter: Flood fill tolerance: ~2.4 standard deviations (FWHM halfway between seed peak temperature and FoV median temperature); max border distance filter: 4 times median beam major axis; frame-to-frame overlap: >= 2 pixels  
  > "This range was chosen such that for each seed point the method would flood all surrounding pixels within $\sim2.4$ standard deviations, corresponding to the full width at half maximum understood as all the pixels where the temperature is halfway between the pixel seed position (local maximum) temperature and the median temperature of the complete FoV." (Feature Identification and Tracking); "all the features with distances between any two points on their borders larger than 4 times the median beam major axis were discarded." (Feature Identification and Tracking); "if the mask seen at the time $t_{i-1}$ shares at least 2 pixels with the mask seen at the time $t_i$ then they are considered as belonging to the same feature." (Feature Identification and Tracking)
   - assumption: Merged features terminate the lifespan of the individual merging features at the previous frame  
  > "In the case that two or more masks merge in the following frame, each individual feature is considered alive until the previous frame." (Feature Identification and Tracking)
   - fitting: DBSCAN clustering on feature light curves  
  > "These light curves are classified by means of the Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) algorithm which is part of the Python Machine Learning Scikit-learn package (cite: scikit-learn)." (Feature Identification and Tracking)
   - validation: Toy model control experiment with synthetic 2D Gaussian features placed via Poisson Disk Sampling with 5% noise and convolved with the ALMA beam: yielded ~60% detection success rate and showed systematic ~20% size underestimation.  
  > "However, the method systematically underestimates the true size of the detected features by $\sim$20% for most common sizes in Band 3 and 6." (Discussion and Conclusions); "Therefore, the intention of this appendix is to asses the limitation of the algorithm with regard to the definition of the features borders. To this end, we designed a control experiment consisting of a toy model image filled with Gaussian-like fake features." (On the feature tracking method); "The success rate is roughly 60% for both bands, meaning that the algorithm is capable of picking 6 out of 10 features present in the image." (On the feature tracking method)
3. **Extract physical properties, sizes, and horizontal velocities of tracked features**: For each feature and frame, extract peak brightness temperature, compute equivalent circular diameter from mask area, track intensity-weighted centre of gravity to compute instantaneous horizontal velocities, and take the FFT of horizontal velocity oscillations to extract velocity amplitudes.  
  > "For each individual feature, the algorithm returns its shape, area, value of the brightest pixel, average pixel value within the feature, initial and final time, and the position of the centre of gravity (of intensity) as computed from the pixel values within the feature's border as weights. For the purpose of this paper, all the areas were converted to the diameter of a circle of equal area." (Occurrence and general properties); "Each displacement of the centre of gravity in $x$ and $y$ directions between 2 consecutive frames was used to calculate the instantaneous horizontal velocities $v_x$ and $v_y$ and the corresponding instantaneous horizontal velocity $v_t = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}$ for each feature." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity); "The velocity amplitudes are computed by taking the fast Fourier transform of each horizontal velocity oscillation and subsequently choosing those amplitude values greater than two standard deviations over the average." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity)
   - parameter: FFT velocity amplitude threshold: > 2 standard deviations above the average  
  > "The velocity amplitudes are computed by taking the fast Fourier transform of each horizontal velocity oscillation and subsequently choosing those amplitude values greater than two standard deviations over the average." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity)
   - assumption: 1 arcsec ~ 727 km; features are approximately circular (blob-like) so diameter of a circle of equal area represents feature size  
  > "($1600$ km $\times$ $1235$ km assuming that $1$ arcsec $\approx$ 727 km)" (Observations); "Although the shape of the feature changes with time, it remains close to a blob-like shape, making the diameter of a circle of equal area an excellent proxy for its size and its corresponding evolution." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity)
   - fitting: Log-normal fits to size and horizontal velocity distributions  
  > "The blue and black thick lines shows a log-norm fit in each case." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity); "Again, the grey distributions correspond to results from simulations, the blue distributions to results from observations and the blue and black thick lines correspond to log-norm fits." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity)
   - validation: Verified that feature sizes exceed the median minor and major axes of the ALMA beam  
  > "In both panels, the vertical maroon dashed lines are the median minor and major axes of the ALMA beam in each corresponding band, showing that the features found by the method were completely resolved by ALMA." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity)
4. **Determine oscillation periods and temperature-size phase differences via wavelet analysis**: Detrend temperature, size, and horizontal velocity time series with a linear fit, apodize with a Tukey window, and compute Continuous Wavelet Transforms (CWT) and cross-wavelet transforms between temperature and size.  
  > "In order to determine the oscillation periods, we performed a continuous wavelet analysis (CWT) for temperature, size and velocity for each individual feature." (Wavelet analysis and periods); "To proceed with the analysis of these oscillations, all time series corresponding to temperature, size and horizontal velocity were detrended (using a linear fit) and apodised (using a Tukey window with a length of 0.1)." (Wavelet analysis and periods); "The wavelet analysis described above is applied to each individual feature, resulting in the dominant oscillation periods (i.e., all periods corresponding to wavelet areas with a confidence level of 95% or larger and outside the cone of influence) as well as the phase differences between temperature and sizes." (Wavelet analysis and periods)
   - parameter: Linear detrending; Tukey window length = 0.1; CWT significance threshold >= 95% confidence level outside the cone of influence  
  > "To proceed with the analysis of these oscillations, all time series corresponding to temperature, size and horizontal velocity were detrended (using a linear fit) and apodised (using a Tukey window with a length of 0.1)." (Wavelet analysis and periods); "The wavelet analysis described above is applied to each individual feature, resulting in the dominant oscillation periods (i.e., all periods corresponding to wavelet areas with a confidence level of 95% or larger and outside the cone of influence) as well as the phase differences between temperature and sizes." (Wavelet analysis and periods)
   - fitting: Linear fit for detrending time series prior to CWT  
  > "all time series corresponding to temperature, size and horizontal velocity were detrended (using a linear fit)" (Wavelet analysis and periods)
   - validation: Restricted dominant period and phase extraction to wavelet regions with >= 95% confidence level and outside the cone of influence  
  > "resulting in the dominant oscillation periods (i.e., all periods corresponding to wavelet areas with a confidence level of 95% or larger and outside the cone of influence)" (Wavelet analysis and periods)
5. **Estimate wave energy flux of sausage modes**: Compute filling factor f, radial velocity perturbation v_r from radius amplitude and size period, phase speed v_ph from temperature and area fractional perturbations, and combine with external mass density rho_e from the Bifrost simulation using Eq. 1.

(Energy flux estimation); "Each value is shown with its respective uncertainty from the error propagation analysis." (Energy flux estimation)
   - parameter: Observing frequencies: nu = 100 GHz (Band 3) and 240 GHz (Band 6); External mass density rho_e = 2.5e-8 kg m^-3 (Band 3) and 4.0e-7 kg m^-3 (Band 6)  
  > "The densities are taken directly from the simulation as the average of the mean values within circles of 3 times the radii of each feature excluding the densities inside the feature, being $\approx 2.5\times 10^{-8}$ kg m$^{-3}$ for Band 3 and $\approx 4.0\times 10^{-7}$ kg m$^{-3}$ for Band 6." (Energy flux estimation); "$\nu$ is the frequency of observation ($100$ GHz for Band 3 and $240$ GHz for Band 6)" (Energy flux estimation)
   - parameter: Degraded simulation median formation heights: 1563 km (Band 3) and 1260 km (Band 6)  
  > "of 1563 km for Band 3 and 1260 km for Band 6, the slope of the energy fluxes between the two heights in the observation is 12" (Energy flux estimation)
   - assumption: External mass densities rho_e for ALMA observations are taken from the Bifrost simulation (within circles of 3x feature radius outside the feature); ALMA brightness temperature in Rayleigh-Jeans limit is a proxy for emission intensity  
  > "The densities are taken directly from the simulation as the average of the mean values within circles of 3 times the radii of each feature excluding the densities inside the feature, being $\approx 2.5\times 10^{-8}$ kg m$^{-3}$ for Band 3 and $\approx 4.0\times 10^{-7}$ kg m$^{-3}$ for Band 6." ## Qualitative findings

- Small-scale bright features in both ALMA Band 3 and Band 6 observations and Bifrost simulations are magnetic in nature (representing cross-sections of magnetic flux tubes) and form higher in the chromosphere than surrounding quieter regions.  
  > "Interestingly, the radiation is formed higher in the solar atmosphere within the features both in the degraded and original simulations. This higher formation is more noticeable in Band 6 than in Band 3." (Location, lifetimes, size and horizontal velocity); "Additional support is provided by the fact that the simulations exhibit very similar features which are clearly connected to magnetic field structures (i.e., they are cross sections of magnetic flux tubes)." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Events are 5-6 times more frequent in Band 6 than in Band 3 in both observations and simulations, and oscillate with shorter periods in Band 6 than in Band 3.  
  > "We also note that the events are a factor 5-6 more frequent in Band 6 than in Band 3, which is seen in both the observations and simulations." (Occurrence and general properties); "The distributions for Band 6 are narrower than for Band 3, with smaller periods, indicating that in general features oscillate faster at the chromospheric heights mapped by Band 6 as compared to the heights for Band 3." (Wavelet analysis and periods)
- Temperature and size oscillations exhibit a predominant anti-phase relationship (-180 deg or 180 deg) in both bands, consistent with compressible fast-sausage MHD modes, while horizontal velocity oscillations indicate transverse (kink) modes.  
  > "We find a predominant anti-phase behaviour between temperature and size oscillations suggesting that the variations of the bright features are caused by compressible fast-sausage Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) modes." (abstract); "Furthermore, we speculate that the oscillations in horizontal velocities may be associated to MHD transverse (kink) waves." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Estimated fast-sausage wave energy flux decreases with height from Band 6 to Band 3, which could suggest wave damping in the chromosphere, though these waves alone are unlikely to balance total chromospheric radiative losses.  
  > "The decrease of wave energy-flux with height (from Band 6 to Band 3) could possibly suggest energy dissipation at chromospheric heights, thus wave heating, with the assumptions that the identified small-scale waves are typical at each band and they propagate upward through the chromosphere." (abstract); "In light of estimated chromospheric radiative losses of between 1000 - 10,000 W m$^{-2}$ (cite: Withbroe1977), it is unlikely that these waves alone can provide sufficient energy to balance the chromosphere." (Discussion and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA millimeter continuum measures brightness temperature analogous to gas temperature in the chromosphere with high cadence (2 s), and datasets D2 and D8 sample a plage region with vertical/near-vertical chromospheric magnetic fields ideal for searching for MHD waves.  
  > "ALMA records brightness temperature, which is analogous to the actual gas temperature of the continuum forming layer, providing unprecedented accuracy in chromospheric plasma temperature measurements." (Introduction); "These data products are presented in (cite: 2021RSPTA.37900174J) as data sets D2 and D8, where they showed that the magnetic topology at chromospheric heights represents vertical or near-vertical fields in a large portion of the FoVs with more inclined field towards the edges. This magnetic field topology verifies that these data sets are good candidates to search for MHD waves." (Observations)
- Why this method: Previous work only studied three isolated features in Band 3, so an automated DoG + Flood fill + DBSCAN algorithm was needed to obtain a statistically significant sample, and CWT/cross-wavelet analysis captures time-varying dominant periods and phase lags between linked physical signals.  
  > "However, these isolated cases were not sufficient to make a statistically significant conclusions. Accordingly, in this study we aimed to analyse the oscillatory behaviour of as many features as possible, in both the two observed ALMA bands, (Sect. 2.1) and the two synthetic continuum map time series from the Bifrost simulation (Sect. 2.2). Therefore, it was necessary to implement an algorithm to automatically identify and track the features across the time series" (Feature Identification and Tracking); "This allows for the analysis of a time-varying signal in both frequency and time domains by determining the dominant modes of oscillation in the power spectrum." (Wavelet analysis and periods)
- Alternative: Using individual ALMA sub-band images instead of full-band reconstructions; full-band images were chosen for their higher signal-to-noise ratio due to better spatial Fourier space sampling.  
  > "For the purpose of this study, we have used the full-band image reconstruction mode in which all the sub-bands of a particular band are combined into one, resulting in time series for frequencies around 3 mm for Band 3 (100 GHz) and 1.25 mm (240 GHz) for Band 6. The full-band ALMA images have a higher signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) compared to the sub-bands S/N because of the better sampling of the spatial Fourier space during the image reconstruction." (Observations)
- Alternative: Simulating sparse ALMA spatial Fourier space coverage when degrading the Bifrost simulation; rejected as beyond the scope of the study in favor of convolving with the median synthetic elliptical beam.  
  > "The spatial resolution was amended through convolving each snapshot with the corresponding ALMA synthetic elliptical median beam to simulate ALMA's angular resolution. Please note that this approach assumes a perfect sampling of the spatial Fourier space while, in reality, ALMA's Fourier space coverage is sparse. Simulating these effects in detail, however, would go beyond the scope of the study presented here." (Simulation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Degrading the simulation by Gaussian beam convolution assumes perfect spatial Fourier space sampling rather than ALMA's true sparse uv coverage.  
  > "Please note that this approach assumes a perfect sampling of the spatial Fourier space while, in reality, ALMA's Fourier space coverage is sparse." (Simulation)
- Limitation (authors): The feature tracking algorithm has a ~60% detection success rate when features overlap and systematically underestimates feature sizes by ~20%, which can affect radial velocity and energy flux estimates by a factor of sqrt(2).  
  > "20% for most common sizes in Band 3 and 6. We note that it does not affect the derived oscillation periods, or the measured temperatures and horizontal velocities." (Discussion and Conclusions); "The success rate is roughly 60% for both bands, meaning that the algorithm is capable of picking 6 out of 10 features present in the image." (On the feature tracking method)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA Band 3 and Band 6 observations were not simultaneous and lack direct wave propagation measurements between the two bands.  
  > "the fact that the ALMA data for Band 3 and Band 6 used here were not obtained simultaneous prevents drawing any strong conclusion regarding the differences in the found oscillations period distributions and its implications for any related decrease in the energy flux." (Discussion and Conclusions); "Although, with the observations not being co-temporal, and with no propagation statistics, it cannot be concluded whether this is damping in identical wave trains due to upward propagation." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Non-unity filling factors can over- or under-estimate energy flux, and external mass densities cannot be measured from ALMA data so simulation densities had to be used.  
  > "It is worth noting that a non-unity filling factor can result in over- or under-estimation of the energy flux (cite: 2008ApJ...676L..73V; 2013ApJ...768..191G)." (Energy flux estimation); "in which all quantities are derived directly from our measurements except for the mass density. Hence, we have used the densities directly extracted from the simulations as explained in Sect." (Discussion and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Standard photospheric cylinder models used to infer sausage wave modes from phase relations assume density and structural uniformity that may not hold in the chromosphere.  
  > "However these models depend on density and structural uniformity seen only in the photosphere, and have been shown to no longer be valid in the chromosphere (cite: 2022ApJ...938..143G)." (Phase relations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 1 in the manuscript text is partially corrupted/truncated (columns for ALMA observations and degraded simulations are missing from the rendered table), making some intermediate summary statistics (such as median feature sizes and temperatures for the observational datasets) unavailable in the text.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a minor typographical discrepancy in the text for the ALMA Band 3 energy flux: Section 4.5 states '1839 +/- 464 W m^-2' and Band 6 uncertainty '1348 W m^-2', whereas Table 3 and Section 5 state '1838 +/- 429 W m^-2' and '5341 +/- 1448 W m^-2'.
