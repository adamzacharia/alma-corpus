## Analysis recipe

1. **Process and align HST/WFC3 imaging and subtract foreground lens galaxy**: Coadd WFC3 F160W archival frames with MultiDrizzle to 0.064'' pixel scale, calibrate astrometry against 46 SDSS DR12 sources, and fit/subtract two Sersic profiles for the foreground lens galaxy using galfit  
  > "We retrieved the calibrated, flat-fielded individual exposure frames of WFC3 with the F160W filter (H-band) from the archive, and then coadded them by ourselves using the iraf MultiDrizzle package." (2.2 HST Data); "We used the software galfit (cite: peng02) to deblend the background lensed source from the foreground lens galaxy." (2.2 HST Data)
   - software: iraf, MultiDrizzle, galfit
   - parameter: pixel scale = 0.064''  
  > "The final pixel scale was set to 0.064'' to improve the resolution and quality of the image." (2.2 HST Data)
   - parameter: Sersic indices: n = 4.42 and n = 1.03  
  > "As a result, the brighter component of the foreground lens galaxy has the Sersic index of n=4.42 (elliptical), while the fainter component has the index of n=1.03 (exponential)." (2.2 HST Data)
   - assumption: Foreground lens galaxy light profile is well described by two Sersic components  
  > "According to (cite: negr14), two Sersic profiles were simultaneously fitted to the foreground lens galaxy to cleanly subtract it." (2.2 HST Data)
   - fitting: Simultaneous two-component Sersic fitting in galfit  
  > "two Sersic profiles were simultaneously fitted to the foreground lens galaxy to cleanly subtract it." (2.2 HST Data)
   - validation: Sersic indices and lens center checked for consistency with literature and ALMA continuum  
  > "The profiles are consistent with those obtained by (cite: negr14)." (2.2 HST Data); "We checked that the coordinates of the foreground lens galaxy in the SDSS catalog match the coordinates of the continuum emission detected in Band 4, 6, and 7 (cite: alma15b) within 0.015''." (2.2 HST Data)
2. **Identify molecular clumps in the CO(5-4) data**: Identify peaks >=3.5 sigma in CO(5-4) velocity-integrated map (created with >1.5 sigma line cube pixels) and run clumpfind on the 21 km s^-1 line cube, retaining 14 clumps detected by both methods  
  > "We first searched for the peak positions with >=3.5sigma in the velocity-integrated intensity map created by including pixels above 1.5sigma in the line cube. We then used the clumpfind package (cite: will94) to search for clumps in the line cube. We identified 14 molecular clumps along the arcs detected by both methods" (4.1 Clump Identification)
   - software: clumpfind
   - parameter: 3.5 sigma integrated map threshold, 1.5 sigma line cube threshold, 21 km s^-1 spectral resolution  
  > "using the line cube (spectral resolution of 21 km s^-1) and the velocity-integrated intensity map of CO(5-4). We first searched for the peak positions with >=3.5sigma in the velocity-integrated intensity map created by including pixels above 1.5sigma in the line cube." (4.1 Clump Identification)
   - assumption: Clumps along the arcs identified by both integrated map peaks and 3D clumpfind represent physical or magnified clump structures  
  > "We identified 14 molecular clumps along the arcs detected by both methods (Figure [fig:image]a)." (4.1 Clump Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison between 2D peak finding and 3D clumpfind algorithm, and examination of source-plane positions and velocities to identify counter-images  
  > "We use the positions in image plane and source plane, and the mean velocities, as a guide to identify multiple images, and we suggest that clumps 1 and 13 and clumps 4 and 5 are possibly counter images of the same source." (4.1 Clump Identification)
3. **Reconstruct clump and stellar component positions in the source plane**: Map positions of molecular clumps, dust clumps, and selected HST stellar knots back to the source plane using glafic with the Tamura et al. best-fit isothermal ellipsoid lens model  
  > "This lens model is used to compute the positions on the source plane (Section 3.2)." (3 Lens Model); "By using our lens model (cite: tamu15) and the glafic software, we determined the positions of molecular clumps on the source plane (Figure 2)." (5.2 The Nature of SDP.81)
   - software: glafic
   - parameter: Isothermal ellipsoid with 400-pc core  
  > "The lensed images are explained by the best-fit mass model with a mass profile of an isothermal ellipsoid with a 400-pc core." (3 Lens Model)
   - assumption: Gravitational lensing conserves surface brightness  
  > "We reiterate that the magnification correction is not necessary when discussing the surface density of molecular gas and SFR, because the gravitational lensing conserves the surface brightness for the identified clumps." (5.1 Star-formation Properties)
   - fitting: Lens model fitting performed in cited paper (Tamura et al. 2015) using glafic  
  > "The analysis of gravitational lensing was conducted by (cite: tamu15) using the glafic software package (cite: ogur10)." (3 Lens Model)
   - validation: Core radius and predicted mass centroid of lens model match HST stellar light and ALMA continuum center  
  > "The core radius of the isothermal ellipsoid agrees well with that of the stellar light in the HST/WFC3 image, and the position of the predicted mass centroid is consistent with that of the emission detected in the ALMA continuum images." (3 Lens Model)
4. **Measure fluxes and derive surface densities of molecular gas and SFR**: Perform photometry in regions >2 sigma in CO(5-4) moment-0 map on both CO and 1.0 mm continuum; compute CO luminosity, molecular gas mass, FIR luminosity, dust mass, SFR, and corresponding surface densities  
  > "We used regions above 2sigma in the CO(5-4) velocity-integrated map for conducting photometry and estimating the physical quantities of each clump. The photometry of dust continuum was conducted on the 1.0 mm image in the same regions used for the CO photometry." (4.2 Surface densities of molecular gas and SFR)
   - parameter: 2 sigma photometry aperture threshold; CO(5-4)/CO(1-0) = 0.3; alpha_CO = 0.8 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1; T_dust = 39.3 K; beta = 1.5; kappa_d(125 um) = 2.64 m^2 kg^-1  
  > "We used a CO line ratio of CO(5-4)/CO(1-0) = 0.3 (cite: alma15b). The molecular gas mass is derived from M_H2 = \alpha_CO L'_CO(1-0), where \alpha_CO is the CO-to-molecular gas mass conversion factor including He mass. We adopted a conversion factor of \alpha_CO = 0.8 M_\odot (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1" (4.2 Surface densities of molecular gas and SFR); "adopt \kappa_d(125 \mu m) = 2.64 \pm 0.29 m^2 kg^-1 (cite: dunn03) and eta = 1.5. The dust temperature of T_dust = 39.3 K was derived by (cite: negr14)" (4.2 Surface densities of molecular gas and SFR)
   - assumption: Filling factor of unity and negligible differential magnification between CO and dust  
  > "We assume that there is no significant difference in the distribution of CO and dust emission within the measured region for each clump, and thus the effect of differential magnification (e.g., (cite: blai99; serj12; heza12)) is negligible. Because the distribution of molecular clouds or star-forming regions smaller than the beamsize is unknown, we assume a filling factor of unity in this study." (4.2 Surface densities of molecular gas and SFR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: High magnification clumps (>100) verified to share the same locus as the other clumps on the Schmidt-Kennicutt plane  
  > "We note that the four clumps with high magnification (>100; Section 2.1) which are not presented in Figure 1 share the same region with the other clumps." (5.1 Star-formation Properties)

## Qualitative findings

- Molecular gas surface density and SFR surface density in SDP.81 clumps are more than three orders of magnitude higher than in local spirals and follow the starburst sequence.  
  > "The surface density of molecular gas (Sigma_H2) and star-formation rate (Sigma_SFR) of the clumps are more than three orders of magnitude higher than those found in local spiral galaxies. The clumps are placed in the 'burst' sequence in the Sigma_H2-Sigma_SFR plane" (abstract)
- Molecular and dust clumps are concentrated within ~2 kpc, while stellar components extend over ~6 kpc and are offset to the west.  
  > "The molecular and dust clumps are confined within a ~2 kpc region, while the spatial extent of the stellar components is as large as ~6 kpc and offset toward the west." (abstract)
- A systematic north-south velocity gradient in the molecular clumps suggests either a rotating gas disk with central dust obscuration or a galaxy merger with tidal stellar features.  
  > "The molecular clumps have a systematic velocity gradient in the north-south direction, which may indicate a rotating gas disk. One possible scenario is that the components of molecular gas, dust, and stars are distributed in a several-kpc scale rotating disk, and the stellar emission is heavily obscured by dust in the central star-forming region. Alternatively, SDP.81 can be explained by a merging system" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Long Baseline Campaign Science Verification observations provided unprecedented angular resolution up to 0.03'' (~200 pc at z = 3.042), enabling spatially resolved clump studies in a lensed SMG  
  > "Recently, the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observed SDP.81 at 1-2 mm in the Science Verification observations with an angular resolution up to 0.03'' (which corresponds to ~200 pc at z=3.042), offering an excellent opportunity to study the spatially-resolved properties of an SMG." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Gravitational lensing conserves surface brightness, allowing direct measurement of intrinsic surface densities without magnification uncertainty  
  > "We reiterate that the magnification correction is not necessary when discussing the surface density of molecular gas and SFR, because the gravitational lensing conserves the surface brightness for the identified clumps." (5.1 Star-formation Properties)
- Alternative: Galactic alpha_CO = 4.3 was considered, which would place clumps on the normal star-forming galaxy sequence instead of the burst sequence  
  > "If we adopt the Galactic conversion factor (alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1; (cite: bola13)), these clumps are located on the sequence of normal star-forming galaxies." (5.1 Star-formation Properties)
- Alternative: Rotating disk scenario vs. galaxy merger scenario for explaining the velocity gradient and gas/stellar spatial offset  
  > "One possible scenario is that the components of molecular gas, dust, and stars are distributed in a several-kpc scale rotating disk, and the stellar emission is heavily obscured by dust in the central star-forming region. Alternatively, SDP.81 may be a merging system" (5.2 The Nature of SDP.81)
- How they decided it was enough: Excluded four clumps close to the critical curve with magnification >100 because of large magnification variation across the clump region  
  > "The magnification of the four clumps close to the critical curve (ID 4, 5, 7, and 8) is large (>100) and varies significantly within each clump. It is possible that they have properties with different physical scales compared to the other clumps, and we exclude these clumps in the discussion of star formation properties." (4.1 Clump Identification)
- Limitation (authors): Some clumps may be unresolved, and gravitational lensing may produce multiple counter-images that cannot all be confirmed due to low SNR  
  > "We caution here that the gravitational lensing can possibly produce two (or multiple) clumps in the image plane that are in fact the same clump in the source plane. However, because of the low SN, it may be difficult to identify all of the multiples in the CO(5-4) image." (4.1 Clump Identification); "We found that some of the clumps do not appear to be extended in the transverse direction, and it is likely that they are unresolved." (4.1 Clump Identification)
- Limitation (authors): CO(5-4) has a high critical density (~10^5 cm^-3) and may trace only dense gas rather than the diffuse gas of the entire disk  
  > "The CO(5-4) line we used in this study has a relatively high critical density (10^5 cm^-3) and it may not trace the gas of the entire disk." (5.2 The Nature of SDP.81)
- Limitation (authors): Filling factor is assumed to be unity but could be smaller if clumps are not spatially resolved  
  > "Because the distribution of molecular clouds or star-forming regions smaller than the beamsize is unknown, we assume a filling factor of unity in this study. We note that the filling factor might be less than unity if they are not spatially resolved." (4.2 Surface densities of molecular gas and SFR)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Clump identification relies on a specific SNR threshold in integrated maps combined with clumpfind, which could suffer from noise artifacts in low SNR regions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Derivation of gas mass assumes uniform CO(5-4)/CO(1-0) ratio and uniform dust temperature across all individual sub-kpc clumps.
