## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure disk extent and orientation**: Measure major-axis extent on Q_phi images; fit ellipses to ten brightness contours; take median parameters and standard deviations.  
  > "We measured the extension of the disks directly on the $Q_\phi$ frames" (Preliminary contour fitting); "Then, we fitted an ellipse to each brightness contour level to find the PA of the disk, using ten levels to average over the whole disk surface." (Preliminary contour fitting); "Finally, we computed the best-fit value as the median of each ellipse parameter among the ten levels, with the standard deviation as the uncertainty." (Preliminary contour fitting)
   - software: least-squares ellipse fitting algorithm
   - parameter: IRDIS pixel scale 12.251 mas/pix; Gaia DR3 parallaxes  
  > "using the pixel scale of IRDIS of 12.251 mas/pix and Gaia DR3 parallaxes to each object to convert to au." (Preliminary contour fitting)
   - parameter: 3 pixel smoothing; more than 20 points per contour; exclude coronagraph and dust below midplane  
  > "We selected the levels on a 3 pixel smoothed $Q_\phi$ frame" (Preliminary contour fitting); "We also imposed having more than 20 points to fit a level, and we discarded all the points behind the coronagraph area or belonging to the dust below the disk midplane." (Preliminary contour fitting)
   - assumption: Approximately constant intensity at a given radial distance  
  > "Considering the general symmetry of the disks, the signal intensity should be approximately constant at a given radial distance from the central star." (Preliminary contour fitting)
   - fitting: Least-squares ellipse fitting  
  > "With these conditions, we fit each contour level with the least-squares ellipse fitting algorithm by (cite: Hammel&Molina2020)." (Preliminary contour fitting)
   - validation: Mirror-symmetry PA check agrees within 1°  
  > "We find that with this procedure we obtain PA values compatible with the previous ones resulting from the ellipse fit within a margin of 1°." (Preliminary contour fitting)
2. **Fit disk geometry and micron-dust mass**: Compare axisymmetric parametric disk synthetic scattered-light images with observations pixel by pixel using MCMC.  
  > "we decided to adopt an axisymmetric analytical parametric model and to perform regressions to obtain the best-fit parameters." (Parametric disk model); "We used the Python module `emcee` (cite: emcee2013) to perform our MCMC regression, comparing the disk observation with our synthetic model at each step, pixel by pixel." (MCMC modelling set-up)
   - software: RADMC-3D, emcee
   - parameter: r0 = 115 au; rin = 1 au; rout = 120 au for V721 CrA and 190 au for BN CrA  
  > "the reference radius $r_0$, which we chose to be 115 au." (Parametric disk model); "These parametric models extend radially from $r_\mathrm{in} = \SI{1}{au}$ to $r_\mathrm{out} = \SI{120}{au}$ for V721 CrA and to $r_\mathrm{out} = \SI{190}{au}$ for BN CrA" (Parametric disk model)
   - parameter: 200 logarithmic radial cells; 200 uniform zenith cells, 30°–150°; axisymmetric grid  
  > "The model grids have 200 logarithmically spaced radial cells from $r_\mathrm{in}$ to $r_\mathrm{out}$ and 200 uniformly spaced cells for the zenith angle $\theta$ in the interval $[\ang{30}, \ang{150}]$." (RADMC-3D set-up)
   - parameter: dust_2daniso_nphi=90; full anisotropic polarised scattering; 10^5 photon packages; sloppy flag  
  > "We limited the precision of azimuthal resolution using the flag `dust_2daniso_nphi=90`." (RADMC-3D set-up); "For the synthetic images, we imposed full anisotropic polarised scattering treatment, with $10^5$ photon packages" (RADMC-3D set-up); "we reduce the default sub-pixelling settings by using the “`sloppy`” flag when creating the synthetic image." (RADMC-3D set-up)
   - parameter: Fixed preliminary PA; Gaussian convolution FWHM = 42.4 mas  
  > "The PA of the disk model in the image was fixed to be the best-fit PA from the preliminary analysis" (RADMC-3D set-up); "we calculated a convolution scale $\mathrm{FWHM} = 1.025 \cdot \lambda_0 / D = \SI{42.4}{mas}$" (RADMC-3D set-up)
   - parameter: ~2000 steps, 32 walkers; discard first 500 steps; posterior medians  
  > "Due to time constraints we had to limit our MCMC runs to $\sim$2000 steps and 32 walkers for each run." (MCMC modelling set-up); "Thus, we discarded the first 500 steps from the chains and computed the final best-fit values as the median of the marginalised posteriors." (MCMC modelling set-up)
   - parameter: Flat prior log10(Md/M_sun): [-8, -2]  
  > "| $\log_{10}(M_\mathrm{d} / \si{M_\odot})$ |  \[-8, -2\]   |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Flat prior hr0: [0.01, 1.0]  
  > "| $hr_0$                                   | \[0.01, 1.0\] |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Flat prior beta: [0.01, 0.5]  
  > "| $\beta$                                  | \[0.01, 0.5\] |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Flat prior inclination: [20, 80]°  
  > "| $i$ \[°\]                                |  \[20, 80\]   |" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Vertically Gaussian density; surface density proportional to r^-1; geometric scale-height power law  
  > "\rho_\mathrm{d} (r, z) =  \frac{ \Sigma_\mathrm{d} (r) }{ \sqrt{2 \pi h_\mathrm{d}^2} } \cdot \exp{ \left[ -\frac{ z^2 }{2 h_\mathrm{d}^2} \right] }" (Parametric disk model); "\Sigma_\mathrm{d} (r) = \Sigma_\mathrm{0} \left( \frac{r}{r_0} \right)^{-1}" (Parametric disk model); "h_\mathrm{d} (r) = \frac{h_0}{r_\mathrm{0}} r \left( \frac{r}{r_0} \right) ^{\beta}" (Parametric disk model)
   - assumption: Adopt RADMC-3D 0.1 um amorphous olivine silicates  
  > "The RADMC-3D default grains, labelled ‘silicates’, instead consist of mono-dispersed grains of 0.1 um made of amorphous olivine (50% Fe and 50% Mg)." (Dust opacities); "Eventually, we adopted the silicate grains for our MCMC models" (Dust opacities)
   - assumption: Neglect millimetric midplane grains and disk temperature profile  
  > "For this reason, we can neglect the larger millimetric dust grains on the midplane and the temperature profile of the disk." (Parametric disk model)
   - fitting: Gaussian residual likelihood, flat priors; mask coronagraph and signal below background standard deviation after Gaussian smoothing  
  > "we masked with “NaNs” the areas behind the coronagraph or below the standard deviation of background noise, after applying a Gaussian smoothing to the $Q_\phi$ data." (MCMC modelling set-up); "The logarithmic likelihood was chosen to be a standard Gaussian distribution of the residuals between the model and the data, and we employed flat priors on our free parameters" (MCMC modelling set-up)
   - validation: Inspect walker consistency and stability; V721 CrA inclination agrees with literature millimetre-ring inclination within ±3°  
  > "We made sure to have at least a general consistency in the evolution of the ensemble of independent walkers along the whole chain." (MCMC modelling set-up); "the chains do not show a significant evolution of the walker distribution after the removal of the first hundred steps" (MCMC modelling set-up); "the inclination found by (cite: Francis&vanDerMarel2020) for the millimetre dust ring." (V721 CrA results)
3. **Identify disk substructures**: Inspect best-model residuals and multiply observed images by disk-surface r^2 maps using best-fit geometry.  
  > "in the inner regions of the disk, two spiral arms appear with a strong S/N contrast above our model, winding clockwise" (V721 CrA results); "Adopting the best-fit parameters, we generated the flux-corrected images" (Discussion); "Multiplying the original data by a map of the $r^2$ of the whole disk surface, it is possible to more accurately assess the structural differences in the dust distribution." (Discussion)
   - software: diskmap
   - parameter: Best-fit geometry; correction for F proportional to 1/r^2  
  > "Adopting the best-fit parameters, we generated the flux-corrected images" (Discussion); "the correction refers to the natural flux decay from the central star, going as $F \propto 1 / r^2$" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: BN CrA gap or shadow visible in both model residuals and corrected image  
  > "First, there is evidence for a gap or shadow in the middle of the disk, which can be recovered even more clearly from the image in Figure [13](#fig:BN_r2)." (BN CrA results)
4. **Extract and compare scattering phase functions**: Extract disk-averaged scattering phase functions from flux-corrected images using diskmap.  
  > "To assess this difference quantitatively, we extracted the SPF of both disks, with `diskmap`." (Scattering phase functions); "we tried to use the maximum available disk surface, to extract the SPF averaged on the whole disk" (Scattering phase functions)
   - software: diskmap
   - parameter: V721 CrA: 40 < r < 100 au; BN CrA: 40 < r < 190 au  
  > "we used the surface between $\SI{40}{au} < r < \SI{100}{au}$ for V721 CrA and between $\SI{40}{au} < r < \SI{190}{au}$ for BN CrA." (Scattering phase functions)
   - assumption: Azimuthally homogeneous dust distribution  
  > "the SPF analysis described in this subsection implicitly assumes that the dust is homogeneously distributed in the azimuthal dimension." (Scattering phase functions)
   - assumption: Previously fitted disk geometry  
  > "extracting the SPF requires assuming a disk geometry in the first place." (Scattering phase functions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative comparison with published disk phase functions  
  > "If we compare their Fig. 3 to our SPFs, we can see that most of their sample has an SPF qualitatively similar to that of BN CrA" (Scattering phase functions)
5. **Check disk structures against the SEDs**: Monte Carlo radiative-transfer SED modelling with fitted disk masses and inclinations.  
  > "To model the SED we use the dust radiative transfer model developed by (cite: Whitney+2003b; Whitney+2003a)." (SED modelling); "For the disk mass and inclination we use the best-fit values reported in Table [1](#tab:best-mcmc) for V721 CrA and BN CrA, multiplying the dust mass by a factor of 100 to estimate the total disk mass." (SED modelling)
   - software: dust radiative transfer model (cite: Whitney+2003b; Whitney+2003a)
   - parameter: V721 CrA: small grains ≤200 Å throughout disk; large grains ≥200 Å from 30 to 120 au  
  > "In the SED modelling we assume that small grains (grain size $\leq 200\AA$) are uniformly distributed along the disk." (SED modelling); "The large grains ($\geq 200 \AA$) are distributed from 30 au up to 120 au" (SED modelling)
   - parameter: BN CrA: 1–200 au; gap 70–100 au; no inner-disk gap  
  > "The disk around BN CrA extends from 1 au to 200 au, and shows a gap between 70 au and 100 au." (SED modelling); "No inner-disk gap is modelled in order to reproduce the observed SED." (SED modelling)
   - assumption: Total disk mass = fitted dust mass ×100  
  > "multiplying the dust mass by a factor of 100 to estimate the total disk mass." (SED modelling)
   - assumption: V721 CrA large-grain inner edge constrained by literature ALMA gap  
  > "with the inner edge chosen in order to be in agreement with the millimetre-dust gap found with ALMA by (cite: Francis&vanDerMarel2020)." (SED modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative consistency of modelled SEDs with the relative evolutionary stages  
  > "the SED modelling and the disk properties are consistent with a younger age for V721 CrA" (SED modelling); "an older age for BN CrA, with a smoother disk, less massive and with a potential gap in the middle." (SED modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- Both disks are resolved, extended and structured in near-IR scattered light.  
  > "Both stars show resolved and extended disks with substructures in the near-IR." (abstract)
- V721 CrA has two clockwise-winding inner spiral arms.  
  > "in the inner regions of the disk, two spiral arms appear with a strong S/N contrast above our model, winding clockwise" (V721 CrA results)
- BN CrA has a dark lane consistent with either a dust gap or a self-cast shadow.  
  > "The disk of BN CrA shows a dark circular lane, which could be either an intrinsic dust gap or a self-cast shadow." (abstract)
- V721 CrA may have smaller grains; BN CrA may have larger, more compact grains.  
  > "Their polarised scattering phase functions suggest that the two disks have slightly different dust populations, with V721 CrA possibly having smaller grains and BN CrA having larger and more compact ones." (Summary)
- No spiral structure detected in BN CrA.  
  > "Lastly, we did not detect any spiral structure in BN CrA." (Surrounding environment)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Bright, extended near-infrared structures suited to morphological characterisation  
  > "We specifically chose these two disks among the DESTINYS targets of Corona Australis in virtue of their bright and extended structures in the near-infrared (NIR), which are best suited for a morphological characterisation." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Simple axisymmetric models constrain basic properties from NIR scattered light  
  > "In order to infer a few basic properties of our disks, we decided to adopt an axisymmetric analytical parametric model and to perform regressions to obtain the best-fit parameters." (Parametric disk model)
- Alternative: DIANA opacities rejected for the MCMC models because of dark stripes, poorer inclination fits and worse chains  
  > "using DIANA opacities resulted in two dark stripes on the closer disk side, resulting in a poorer fit to the disk inclination and worse MCMC chains overall." (Dust opacities)
- Alternative: Axisymmetric grid preferred over a 3D grid for speed and scattering performance  
  > "We noted that setting a lower number of scattering photons with axisymmetry gave better results and a faster run time than with a 3D grid and a thousand times more photons." (RADMC-3D set-up)
- Alternative: BN CrA dark lane could be an intrinsic dust gap or a self-cast shadow  
  > "The disk of BN CrA shows a dark circular lane, which could be either an intrinsic dust gap or a self-cast shadow." (abstract)
- Alternative: V721 CrA north-west feature could be a feeding streamer or sparse outer spiral  
  > "This might be a streamer of dust feeding the circumstellar disk" (Surrounding environment); "or otherwise a sparse outer spiral structure." (Surrounding environment)
- How they decided it was enough: Walker distributions appeared stable after the first hundred steps; first 500 steps discarded  
  > "the chains do not show a significant evolution of the walker distribution after the removal of the first hundred steps" (MCMC modelling set-up); "Thus, we discarded the first 500 steps from the chains and computed the final best-fit values as the median of the marginalised posteriors." (MCMC modelling set-up)
- Limitation (authors): Scattered-light-only analysis has uncertainties and degeneracies  
  > "analysing it based on the scattered light signal alone carries its own limitations, due to many uncertainties and possible degeneracies, which can lead to misinterpretation of a disk feature." (Parametric disk model)
- Limitation (authors): Brightness-contour ellipse fitting biases inclination high  
  > "Thus, an ellipse fitting of brightness contours will be biased to suggest higher inclinations than the real ones." (Preliminary contour fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Inner radius cannot be constrained through image analysis because of the coronagraph  
  > "the coronagraph covers the innermost regions of both disks, thus making it impossible for us to constrain $r_\mathrm{in}$ through image analysis." (Parametric disk model)
- Limitation (authors): Computational cost limited MCMC runs to ~2000 steps and 32 walkers  
  > "Due to time constraints we had to limit our MCMC runs to $\sim$2000 steps and 32 walkers for each run." (MCMC modelling set-up)
- Limitation (authors): BN CrA inclination may depend on the chosen dust opacity  
  > "Even this inclination value could be affected by the specific choice of dust opacity for the regression." (BN CrA results)
- Limitation (authors): Substructures and shadows add uncertainty to phase-function extraction  
  > "Any substructure or shadow would add uncertainty to the extraction of the SPF, as could be the case for both V721 CrA and BN CrA." (Scattering phase functions)
- Limitation (authors): Dust opacity was not fitted as a free parameter  
  > "The basic disk modelling with these parameters was already too computationally intensive to leave dust opacity as another free parameter." (Dust opacities)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA contribution is a literature comparison; this paper does not provide an independent ALMA reduction recipe.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Quoted MCMC intervals are conditional on the adopted opacity and axisymmetric density model.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual chain stability alone does not establish effective sample size or quantitative convergence.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 42.4 mas synthetic-image convolution differs from the reported approximately 50–55 mas observed PSFs.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SED mass conversion applies a factor of 100 to a mass fitted only for micron-sized dust.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two disks establish consistency with subgroup evolution, rather than a causal environmental effect.
