## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract compact millimeter continuum cores and measure integrated fluxes and sizes**: Apply CuTEx (second-derivative curvature thresholding followed by 2D elliptical Gaussian plus 1st/2nd order polynomial background fitting) to 219 GHz (7M+TM2 and 7M+TM2+TM1) and 150 GHz (Band 4) continuum images; identify disappearing cores present at 7600 au but absent at 1400 au.  
  > "CuTEx identifies compact sources as maxima of curvature in maps by analyzing their second derivatives." (Millimeter continuum emission); "Once the peaks in the curvature maps are identified, they are fitted (in the original map) with 2D elliptical Gaussians plus a background that can be of first or second order polynomial" (Millimeter continuum emission); "while from the CuTEx algorithm extraction we performed on this continuum image the compact core #5 is resolved into two cores, due to the better angular resolution." (Millimeter continuum emission)
   - software: CuTEx
   - parameter: r = d tan(theta) where theta = sqrt(theta_A * theta_B)  
  > "as derived from CuTEx ($r=d \tan\theta$, where $\theta=\sqrt{\theta_A \theta_B}$ and $d=8.5\,$kpc is the source distance)" (Millimeter continuum emission)
   - assumption: Source distance d = 8.5 kpc  
  > "where $	heta=\sqrt{	heta_A 	heta_B}$ and $d=8.5\,$kpc is the source distance) of cores from #2 to #9." (Millimeter continuum emission)
   - fitting: 2D elliptical Gaussians plus 1st or 2nd order polynomial background (automatically assessed by CuTEx)  
  > "Once the peaks in the curvature maps are identified, they are fitted (in the original map) with 2D elliptical Gaussians plus a background that can be of first or second order polynomial" (Millimeter continuum emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Characterize the centimeter continuum SED, dust emissivity index, and ionizing photon rate of the H II region**: Perform 2D Gaussian fit in CARTA to check point-like nature, extract aperture photometry in ~20" circular aperture across SMGPS (0.9-1.6 GHz) and RACS (0.88, 1.35, 1.65 GHz), fit spectral index alpha with MCMC, extrapolate and subtract free-free contribution at 150 and 220 GHz to derive dust spectral index and beta, and compute Lyman continuum photon rate N_Ly.  
  > "The source is point-like, as a preliminary 2D Gaussian fit performed in Cube Analysis and Rendering Tool for Astronomy (CARTA, (cite: CARTA)) matches the angular resolution of the data. We then perform aperture photometry using a circular aperture of 2 times the resolution of the data ($\sim$20")." (Centimeter continuum emission); "We calculated the spectral index between the point at 150 GHz and at 220 GHz, first removing the contribution from free-free emission extrapolated from the two possible fits of the first part of the SED." (Centimeter continuum emission); "The number of Lyman continuum photons emitted by the ionizing source and that generate the H ii region can be calculated using the radio flux density, following the relation given by (cite: matsakis1976)" (Centimeter continuum emission)
   - software: CARTA
   - parameter: Circular aperture diameter ~20" (2 times resolution)  
  > "We then perform aperture photometry using a circular aperture of 2 times the resolution of the data ($\sim$20")." (Centimeter continuum emission)
   - parameter: S_nu = 10.5 mJy at 1.36 GHz from SMGPS  
  > "Considering the flux density from the average continuum map of SMGPS to be 10.5 mJy at 1.36 GHz, and using the source distance of 8.5 kpc the rate of ionizing photon is in the range $5.9-6.5	imes10^{46}$ photons s$^{-1}$" (Centimeter continuum emission)
   - assumption: S_nu proportional to nu^alpha; Rayleigh-Jeans regime beta = alpha - 2  
  > "The spectral index $\alpha$ (assuming $S_{\nu}\propto\nu^{\alpha}$) from the fit of the data is $-0.4\pm0.4$" (Centimeter continuum emission); "The value of the exponent of the power-law of dust emissivity $eta$ (with $eta=\alpha-2$ in the Rayleigh-Jeans regime) for this source is in the range of 0.7-2.2." (Centimeter continuum emission)
   - assumption: Electron temperature T_e = 8000-10000 K (at galactocentric distance ~10 kpc) and distance D = 8.5 kpc  
  > "Given the presence of some scatter in the relation, we considered a range of $T_e=8000-10000\,$K." (Centimeter continuum emission)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo Markov Chain linear fit between 0.8 GHz and 1.6 GHz using 100 walkers and 500 steps  
  > "The spectral index values and their error have been derived with a Monte Carlo Markov Chain linear fit to the points between 0.8 GHz and 1.6 GHz, using 100 walkers and 500 steps." (Centimeter continuum emission)
   - validation: Compared derived alpha values with known H II regions in SMGPS from Goedhart et al. (2024)  
  > "Both values of $\alpha$ are consistent with optically thin free-free emission, as seen from $\alpha$ values derived in the same range from known H ii regions using SMGPS from (cite: goedhart2024)." (Centimeter continuum emission)
3. **Create masked moment maps, PV diagrams, and core-averaged spectra of molecular lines**: Compute pixel-by-pixel rms from first 500 channels; build 3D mask requiring >5 sigma seed voxels connected to two consecutive channels >2 sigma, expanded by 0.25x beam FWHM spatially and 2 channels spectrally; blank moment-0 pixels with S/N < 3; extract radial PV cuts of DCO+(2-1) and core-averaged spectra with Gaussian fits for FWHM.  
  > "1) we calculated the root mean squared ($\sigma$) of the cube for each pixel, using the first 500 channels of the cubes, which were determined to be free of any emission;" (Molecular emission); "5) we enlarge the mask obtained from point 4 of $0.25	imes$FWHM of the beam in the spatial directions and of two channels at the beginning and at the end of the range of integration in the spectral direction" (Molecular emission); "obtained with a Gaussian fit of the mean spectrum over the size of each core." (Mass estimates and virial parameter)
   - parameter: 500 emission-free channels for rms; 5 sigma seed threshold; 2 consecutive channels > 2 sigma; expanded by 0.25*FWHM spatially and 2 channels spectrally; moment-0 blanked at S/N < 3; spectral detection threshold S/N > 5  
  > "As a last step, from the spectral rms and the number of channels of integration of each pixel, we constructed a map of the moment-0 rms, and blanked from the moment maps the pixels where the moment-0 signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) was below 3." (Molecular emission); "using the first 500 channels of the cubes, which were determined to be free of any emission" (Molecular emission)
   - assumption: Hyperfine splitting of deuterated species is neglected in Gaussian FWHM fits, so FWHM values are upper limits  
  > "We do not took into consideration the hyperfine splitting of the deuterated species, so the FWHM values reported are upper limits." (Molecular emission)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to core-averaged spectra  
  > "obtained with a Gaussian fit of the mean spectrum over the size of each core." (Mass estimates and virial parameter)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit CH3CCH (9-8) spectrum on Core #4 to derive gas temperature and column density**: Extract average spectrum towards core #4 and fit synthetic LTE spectra using MADCUBA SLIM and CDMS catalog entries.  
  > "We extracted an average spectrum towards this core, and used the Spectral Line Identification and Modelling (SLIM) tool of the MAdrid Data CUBe Analysis package (MADCUBA (cite: MADCUBA)) to perform a line fitting." (Molecular emission); "To analyze CH$_3$CCH we selected the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy catalog (CDMS, (cite: cdms))[^5], and used SLIM to generate synthetic spectra under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Molecular emission)
   - software: MADCUBA (SLIM)
   - parameter: Free parameters: column density N, excitation temperature T_ex, central velocity, FWHM  
  > "The free parameters fitted by the LTE model are the molecular column density ($N$), excitation temperature ($T_{m{ex}}$), central velocity of the line emission, and FWHM of the Gaussian line profiles." (Molecular emission)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and Gaussian line profiles  
  > "and used SLIM to generate synthetic spectra under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (Molecular emission)
   - fitting: Non-linear least-squares LTE fit using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm  
  > "SLIM allows to fit the spectrum using non-linear least-squares LTE fit to the data using the Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm, comparing the modeled spectra with the observed one." (Molecular emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Construct and model radial continuum intensity profiles of the cores**: Using CuTEx ellipse parameters (x_0, y_0, PA, theta_A/theta_B) on ALMA Band 6 7m+tm2 continuum, bin pixel intensities by elliptical semi-major axis for cores #1 to #6 and visually compare with line-of-sight integrated singular isothermal sphere and Bonnor-Ebert sphere profiles convolved with the Band 6 7m+tm2 beam.  
  > "Using the four parameters x$_0$, y$_0$, PA, and $	heta_{A}/	heta_{B}$ fixed to the value defined by CuTEx for the core, it is possible to assign to each pixel of the core the semi-major axis (that we refer to as the profiles radius) of the ellipse passing through that pixel." (Radial profile of continuum emission); "We approximated the Bonnor-Ebert sphere using the analytic radial profile $ho(r)\propto (1+(r/R_0)^{\alpha})^{-1}$, with $\alpha=2$ (see e.g. (cite: fischera2014)) and using three different values of $R_0$: 500 au, 1500 au, and 2000 au." (Radial profile of continuum emission); "the radial profile of the flux density emitted is given by the convolution of the surface density profile $\Sigma(R)$ with a gaussian profile with FWHM the size of the beam of the ALMA Band6 *7m+tm2* data" (Radial profile of continuum emission)
   - software: CuTEx
   - parameter: Bonnor-Ebert critical radius R_0 = 500 au, 1500 au, 2000 au; power-law exponent alpha = 2  
  > "We approximated the Bonnor-Ebert sphere using the analytic radial profile $ho(r)\propto (1+(r/R_0)^{\alpha})^{-1}$, with $\alpha=2$ (see e.g. (cite: fischera2014)) and using three different values of $R_0$: 500 au, 1500 au, and 2000 au." (Radial profile of continuum emission)
   - assumption: Cores are isothermal and dust properties do not vary with radius (kappa_nu(r) = const.)  
  > "Assuming that the cores are isothermal and that the dust properties are not varying with the radius (i.e. $\kappa_{\nu}(r)=\rm{const.}$)" (Radial profile of continuum emission)
   - fitting: Visual comparison of observed profiles with convolved analytic models

## Qualitative findings

- Three out of four disappearing cores (#2, #5, and #6) exhibit bright, peaked DCO+(2-1) and N2D+(2-1) emission, no SiO (5-4) outflow emission, and (for #2 and #5) shallow Bonnor-Ebert radial profiles with ~1500-2000 au flat inner regions, confirming they are young prestellar cores.  
  > "From the analysis of the radial profiles and of the emission of DCO$^+$(2$-$1), N$_2$D$^+$(2$-$1), and SiO $(5-4)$ we confirm that cores #2, #5, and #6 (i.e. 3 out of 4 disappearing cores) are young prestellar objects." (Conclusions); "while the profiles of cores #2 and #5 are better reproduces by Bonnor-Ebert spheres with a central flattening over a larger area, up to a radius of 1500-2000 au." (Radial profile of continuum emission)
- Core #1 does not have a compact morphology in ALMA Band 4 continuum and spatially coincides with the centimeter H II region, likely representing dispersed material or a relic dust envelope.  
  > "The other disappearing core, core #1, does not have a compact morphology when observed in ALMA Band 4, and it likely corresponds to material dispersed by the expansion of the H ii region or the relic of the dust envelope around the ionizing star." (Conclusions)
- Core #4 is the most chemically and physically evolved core, showing peaked DCN(2-1), CH3CCH(9-8) (T_ex = 39 +/- 5 K), and SiO(5-4) outflow emission with a NE-SW velocity gradient, and fragmenting into three subfragments at 1400 au resolution.  
  > "Therefore, we conclude that core #4 is the most chemically evolved and we assume that the temperature in the rest of the cores along the arch is lower than towards core #4." (Evolutionary stages of the cores from chemistry); "Among the cores inside the ALMAGAL FOV, only the core #4 is associated to SiO emission, which shows a velocity gradient in the North East-South West direction." (Molecular emission)
- DCO+(2-1) moment-1 and radial position-velocity diagrams show a radial velocity gradient centered on the H II region with breaking points at cores #5 and #6, consistent with an expanding shell.  
  > "In the moment-1 map we identify a velocity gradient along the radial direction centered on the H ii region position, signature of an expanding shell." (Molecular emission); "For the other cores we observe a radial velocity gradient, which in the case of cores #5 and #6 has a breaking point at their position." (Molecular emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected AG286.0716-1.8229 from >1000 ALMAGAL clumps because it contains four disappearing cores within 1 pc of a candidate H II region; observed Band 4 deuterated species (DCO+, N2D+, DCN) and CH3CCH to probe cold (<20 K), dense (>10^5 cm^-3) prestellar chemistry and gas temperature, and archival cm data (SMGPS, RACS) to confirm free-free emission from the H II region.  
  > "Moreover, to have good candidates for this pilot study we searched for H ii regions or candidate H ii regions in the ALMAGAL fields in the catalog of (cite: anderson2014) closer than 1 pc to one or more disappearing cores." (Introduction); "In fact, in the early stages of star formation in regions with temperature below 20 K and density above $\sim10^5\,$cm$^{-3}$ the deuteration process takes place (cite: caselli2012; fontanideuteration; fontani2014timescale)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Compared intermediate (~7600 au) and high (~1400 au) resolution continuum maps because prestellar cores with flat radial profiles spread their flux over many small beams and disappear below the noise at high resolution; used CuTEx curvature thresholding to isolate compact cores from diffuse background.  
  > "Comparing the two sets of images it is possible that a compact source in the maps at $\sim 5000\,$au is not identified above the noise limits in the $\sim1000\,$au if the radial profile of the source is flat, like in the case of prestellar cores (cite: caselli2019flat)." (Introduction); "This method allows an easier identification of compact sources, which stand out in the curvature images, while diffuse and background emission is strongly dampened." (Millimeter continuum emission)
- Alternative: Fitting hyperfine splitting of the deuterated transitions (DCO+, N2D+, DCN) was rejected because satellite hyperfine components were rarely detected, leaving hyperfine fits poorly constrained.  
  > "We did not considered the hyperfine splitting because only in a few cases more transitions other than the main were detected in the spectrum, thus making the results of the fit including the hyperfine splitting not well constrained." (Mass estimates and virial parameter)
- Alternative: Core #1 was rejected as a compact prestellar core candidate because its 150 GHz continuum emission shows a diffuse morphology and radial profile flatter than any Bonnor-Ebert model.  
  > "However, given the more diffuse morphology of core #1 from its 150 GHz emission, we do not consider it to be a compact core and we exclude it from further analysis to determine whether this is a prestellar core." (Centimeter continuum emission)
- Alternative: Pure thermal Jeans fragmentation was tested against the collect-and-collapse model and rejected as the primary explanation because the thermal Jeans length is 2.5-3.4 times smaller than the observed core separation.  
  > "The thermal Jeans length is a factor 2.5 lower than the mean observed separation, and a factor 3.4 lower than the mean deprojected separation and not compatible within the errors." (Triggered star-formation?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gas temperatures could not be directly measured for cores other than Core #4 because CH3CCH (9-8) was only detected towards Core #4, requiring assumed temperature ranges (10-20 K and 20-30 K).  
  > "We were able to estimate the gas temperature of only the most evolved core, using the CH$_3$CCH emission. The resulting value of $39\pm5$ K is also an upper limit for the other cores." (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): Core masses may be underestimated by up to a factor of two because a standard gas-to-dust ratio of 100 was assumed, whereas at the galactocentric distance of ~10 kpc the ratio could be ~200.  
  > "In particular, at the galactocentric distance of AG286.0716$-$1.8229 ($\sim10$ kpc) the gas-to-dust ratio from the relation found in (cite: giannetti2017) would be of $\sim200$. Thus, these masses could be underestimated up to a factor two from the uncertainties on this parameter." (Mass estimates and virial parameter)
- Limitation (authors): Neglecting hyperfine splitting in the Gaussian fits to deuterated lines makes the derived line FWHMs and virial parameters upper limits.  
  > "Moreover, as stated above, the FWHM and therefore $\sigma$ for most of the cores are upper limits of the real value, since we did not take into account the hyperfine splitting, while the masses could be lower limits if the real gas-to-dust ratio in the region is above 100. Therefore the values of $\alpha_{m{vir}}$ reported here are also upper limits." (Mass estimates and virial parameter)
- Limitation (authors): There is a spatial gap between the 23 cm radio continuum emission and the millimeter dust/gas arch, and no bright PDR rim is detected at 3.6 um while 5.8 and 8.0 um IRAC data and PDR molecular tracers are unavailable.

(Triggered star-formation?)
