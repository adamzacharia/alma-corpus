## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure FIR dust continuum emission sizes**: Convert ALMA measurement sets to GILDAS/MAPPING uv-tables, mask emission lines to create continuum visibilities, fit a single 2D Gaussian source model in the visibility plane, and compute the circularized dust radius as the geometric mean of best-fit major and minor axis FWHMs.  
  > "After creating continuum visibility by masking emission lines, we used a single 2D Gaussian source model for data fitting. The free parameters of the fittings are source positions, major/minor axis FWHMs, position angles, and source fluxes." (Size Measurement); "Geometric means were calculated for the best fit major and minor axis FWHMs and used as the radii of the dust continua: $r_{\rm dust} = \sqrt{a\,b}/2$, where $a$ is the major axis FWHM and $b$ is the FWHM of the minor axis." (Size Measurement)
   - software: GILDAS, CASA, IMFIT
   - parameter: 2D Gaussian model with free source positions, major/minor axis FWHMs, position angles, and source fluxes  
  > "The free parameters of the fittings are source positions, major/minor axis FWHMs, position angles, and source fluxes." (Size Measurement)
   - parameter: Clean threshold of 3 times dirty image RMS for image-plane check  
  > "To measure FWHM in the image plane, we created images using the weighting scheme and cleaned these down to three times RMSs, where RMSs are calculated using dirty images." (Size Measurement)
   - assumption: Spherical dust emission geometry (r_dust = sqrt(a*b)/2)  
  > "Geometric means were calculated for the best fit major and minor axis FWHMs and used as the radii of the dust continua: $r_{\rm dust} = \sqrt{a\,b}/2$, where $a$ is the major axis FWHM and $b$ is the FWHM of the minor axis. This simplification is required because the I20 method assumes only spherical dust emission geometry." (Size Measurement)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting to visibility data  
  > "After creating continuum visibility by masking emission lines, we used a single 2D Gaussian source model for data fitting." (Size Measurement)
   - validation: Compared visibility-plane sizes with 2D Gaussian fits (CASA IMFIT) on cleaned images; found good agreement.  
  > "2D Gaussian fitting using CASA task IMFIT was performed on the cleaned images, showing that the measured sizes match well with those measured from visibility data." (Size Measurement)
2. **Calibrate the ISM clumpiness parameter using multiwavelength ALMA continuum galaxies**: Fit the Inoue et al. (2020) radiative equilibrium clumpy sphere dust model to multiwavelength FIR continuum fluxes, UV luminosity, redshift, and dust size for 6 calibration galaxies (HZ4, HZ6, HZ9, HZ10, A1689-zD1, B14-65666) to derive individual and sample-averaged clumpiness parameter log(xi_clp), dust temperature, dust mass, and IR luminosity.  
  > "To calibrate the ISM geometry of the model, we measured the average value of $\xi_{\rm clp}$ for high-redshift galaxies, using the method presented in I20, to galaxies with multiwavelength continuum detections in our sample, specifically HZ4, HZ6, HZ9, HZ10, A1689_zD1, and B14-65666." (Clumpy Medium Model Fitting using Multiwavelength ALMA observations); "The input parameters are dust emission size, measured flux, UV luminosity, and redshift, and the routine output are dust temperature, dust mass, and ISM clumpiness ($\xi_{\rm clp}$). IR luminosities were calculated using dust mass and dust temperature." (Clumpy Medium Model Fitting using Multiwavelength ALMA observations)
   - software: Python, scipy.optimize, FIS22sed
   - parameter: 1000 Monte-Carlo realizations fluctuating UV luminosity, IR size, and fluxes within 1-sigma Gaussian errors  
  > "Uncertainties in dust temperature, dust mass (IR luminosity), and the clumpiness parameter were estimated using the Monte-Carlo technique by fluctuating the measurements (i.e., UV luminosity, IR size, fluxes of galaxies) 1000 times and assuming Gaussian distributions with measured one sigma errors." (Fitting)
   - assumption: Radiative equilibrium in a clumpy sphere ISM with high density contrast (negligible interclump opacity)  
  > "We used the dust emission model presented in I20, in which dust absorption and emission assume radiative equilibrium." (ISM Geometry and Radiation Model: the Clumpy Sphere Distribution); "To simplify the model, I20 further assumed a high-density contrast case, neglecting opacity in the interclump medium, and denoting an extremely tenuous interclump medium." (ISM Geometry and Radiation Model: the Clumpy Sphere Distribution)
   - assumption: Optically thin modified blackbody dust emission with fixed emissivity index beta = 2  
  > "Dust emission is assumed to be optically thin modified black body radiation, as is typical used for high-redshift galaxies (cite: Casey2012), with fixed dust emissivity index ($\beta=2$)." (Fitting)
   - assumption: Mass absorption coefficient kappa_UV = 5.0e4 cm^2/g and dust emissivity kappa_0 = 30 cm^2/g at 100 um  
  > "The following fiducial dust properties were selected based on a compilation of theoretical, empirical models, and experimental measurements (see Appendix of I20): mass absorption coefficient ($\kappa_{\rm UV}=5.0\times10^4\,{\rm cm^2/g}$) and dust emissivity ($\kappa_{0}=30\,\rm{ cm^2/g}$ at $100\,\rm{\mu m}$)." (Fitting)
   - assumption: Cosmology (Omega_m, Omega_Lambda, h) = (0.3, 0.7, 0.7) and gravitational lensing magnification factors mu = 9.3 (A1689-zD1) and mu = 1.43 (MACS0416-Y1)  
  > "Throughout this paper, we assume a cosmology with $(\Omega_m,\Omega_{\Lambda},h)=(0.3,0.7,0.7)$." (Introduction); "$\ast$ A16889_zD1 and MACS0416-Y1 are gravitationally magnified with magnification factors of $\mu=9.3$ and $\mu=1.43$, respectively. Demagnified properties are listed in this table." (Table 1)
   - fitting: Chi-square minimization using scipy.optimize.minimize with 1000 Monte-Carlo iterations; median, 16th, and 84th percentiles taken as best values and uncertainties.  
  > "For the fitting, we used `minimize` from the Python package `scipy.optimize` to perform chi-square minimization." (Fitting); "The resulting output distributions were used to estimate the median, 16th, and 84th percentiles, which are treated as the final estimated values and uncertainties, respectively." (Fitting)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to dust emissivity index beta between 1.5 and 2.5; found variations within 1-sigma uncertainty (Delta T_d ~ 5 K, Delta log xi_d ~ 0.1).  
  > "We tested the impact of varying $\beta$ in our fitting by considering different values of $\beta$ between $1.5$ to $2.5$. Indeed, although we found systematic changes in the derived median dust temperature and $\xi_{\rm clp}$, these variations are within the $1\,\sigma$ uncertainty derived when fixing $\beta=2.0$ (i.e., typical $\Delta T_{\rm d}\sim5\,\rm{K}$ and $\Delta \rm{log}\,\xi_{\rm d}\sim0.1$)." (Clumpy Medium Model Fitting using Multiwavelength ALMA observations)
3. **Estimate dust temperature, dust mass, and IR luminosity from single-band ALMA continuum observations**: Apply the clumpy sphere radiative equilibrium model with fixed average clumpiness (log xi_clp = -1.02 +/- 0.41) to single-band continuum fluxes, sizes, and UV luminosities of J0217-0208, J1211-0118, and MACS0416-Y1.  
  > "By assuming the ISM clumpiness obtained in the previous step, we applied our dust emission model to galaxies with only single-band dust continuum detection (i.e., J2017-0208, J1211-0118, and MACS0416-Y1) to estimate their dust temperatures and IR luminosities." (Applying the Clumpy Medium Model using Single-Wavelength ALMA observations); "$\ast$: We used ${\rm log}\,\xi_{\rm clp}=-1.02\pm0.41$ for fixed $\xi_{\rm clp}$ objects." (Table 2)
   - software: Python, scipy.optimize, FIS22sed
   - parameter: Fixed clumpiness parameter log(xi_clp) = -1.02 +/- 0.41  
  > "$\ast$: We used ${\rm log}\,\xi_{\rm clp}=-1.02\pm0.41$ for fixed $\xi_{\rm clp}$ objects." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Constant ISM clumpiness log(xi_clp) = -1.02 +/- 0.41 across high-z star-forming galaxies  
  > "We find that a dust distribution model assuming a clumpiness of ${\rm log}\,\xi_{\rm clp}=-1.02\pm0.41$ may accurately represent the ISM of high-redshift star-forming galaxies." (Abstract)
   - fitting: Chi-square minimization using scipy.optimize.minimize with Monte-Carlo error propagation fluctuating input parameters assuming Gaussian distributions.  
  > "We used the same input and output parameters shown in §[1.1](#sec:calibration), but used only input single-band continuum fluxes. Uncertainties were estimated using the Monte-Carlo technique by fluctuating input parameters assuming Gaussian distributions." (Applying the Clumpy Medium Model using Single-Wavelength ALMA observations)
   - validation: Tested single-band fixed-clumpiness method on the 6 multiwavelength calibration galaxies using their highest-SNR single band, and compared single-band results against [CII]-based temperatures from Sommovigo et al. (2021) and multi-band limits.  
  > "We compared their dust temperatures derived by fitting multiple wavelength data, and those derived assuming ${\rm log}\,\xi_{\rm clp}=-1.02\pm0.41$ using only single dust continuum measurements. For single-band dust temperature estimations, we used the dust continuum measurements with the highest signal to noise ratios." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry); "In particular, we compared with the dust temperatures derived by (cite: Sommovigo2021), who used \[CII\] $158\,\rm{\mu m}$ emission line luminosity as an additional parameter for dust temperature and IR luminosity." (Comparison with other methods)
4. **Fit tentative correlation between dust temperature and ISM clumpiness**: Perform orthogonal linear regression between dust temperature and log(xi_clp) including uncertainties in both axes.  
  > "Nevertheless, we derived this tentative relationship between dust temperatures and $\rm{log\,}\xi_{\rm clp}$ using orthogonal linear regression (the python `scipy.odr` package) including the uncertainties inherent to both axes." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)
   - software: Python, scipy.odr
   - assumption: Linear relationship between Td and log(xi_clp)  
  > "We found that ${\rm Td} = 0.070\,(\pm0.017)\,{\rm log\,}\xi_{\rm clp} + 1.58\,(\pm0.61)$; this relationship entails that galaxies with high dust temperatures have very low $\xi_{\rm clp}$, i.e., ISM geometries similar to that of a homogeneous distribution without any clumps." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)
   - fitting: Orthogonal linear regression using scipy.odr  
  > "Nevertheless, we derived this tentative relationship between dust temperatures and $\rm{log\,}\xi_{\rm clp}$ using orthogonal linear regression (the python `scipy.odr` package) including the uncertainties inherent to both axes." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)

## Software

- **GILDAS** : Visibility-based 2D Gaussian size measurements of dust continuum emission  
  > "In particular, we performed visibility-based size measurements using the task in the software package GILDAS[^3]." (Size Measurement)
- **CASA** : Converting measurement sets and performing 2D Gaussian image-plane fitting with IMFIT to validate visibility-based sizes  
  > "2D Gaussian fitting using CASA task IMFIT was performed on the cleaned images, showing that the measured sizes match well with those measured from visibility data." (Size Measurement)
- **IMFIT** : 2D Gaussian fitting on cleaned images to validate visibility size measurements  
  > "2D Gaussian fitting using CASA task IMFIT was performed on the cleaned images, showing that the measured sizes match well with those measured from visibility data." (Size Measurement)
- **Python** : Custom routines for fitting the clumpy dust emission model and estimating dust temperature, dust mass, IR luminosity, and clumpiness  
  > "For ease of operation, we devised two customized routines using Python: (1) a routine to the estimate dust geometry parameter ($\xi_{\rm clp}$), dust temperature, dust mass, and IR luminosity using multiwavelength FIR continuum measurements (see §[\[sec:calibration\]](#sec:calibration)), and (2) a routine to estimate the dust temperature, dust mass, and IR luminosity assuming a fixed dust geometry parameter $\xi_{\rm cl}$, using single-wavelength FIR continuum measurements (see §[\[sec:application\]](#sec:application))." (Fitting)
- **scipy.optimize** : Chi-square minimization using the minimize function during SED and dust model fitting  
  > "For the fitting, we used `minimize` from the Python package `scipy.optimize` to perform chi-square minimization." (Fitting)
- **scipy.odr** : Orthogonal linear regression between dust temperature and log(xi_clp)  
  > "Nevertheless, we derived this tentative relationship between dust temperatures and $\rm{log\,}\xi_{\rm clp}$ using orthogonal linear regression (the python `scipy.odr` package) including the uncertainties inherent to both axes." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)
- **FIS22sed** : Publicly released Python package implementing the single-band and multi-band dust temperature and IR luminosity fitting routines  
  > "The code used herein has been made publicly available[^2] and is readily applicable for deriving dust temperatures and IR luminosities using upcoming ALMA single-band continuum observations." (Introduction); "[^2]: <https://github.com/yfudamoto/FIS22sed.git>" (Fitting)

## Qualitative findings

- A clumpy sphere ISM model with an average clumpiness parameter of log(xi_clp) = -1.02 +/- 0.41 represents high-redshift star-forming galaxies and allows estimation of dust temperature and IR luminosity from single-band ALMA continuum flux and size.  
  > "We find that a dust distribution model assuming a clumpiness of ${\rm log}\,\xi_{\rm clp}=-1.02\pm0.41$ may accurately represent the ISM of high-redshift star-forming galaxies." (Abstract)
- Dust temperatures derived from single-band continuum using fixed clumpiness generally agree with multiwavelength SED fitting and [CII] 158 um line-based methods within uncertainties.  
  > "In comparison with other methods, such as multiwavelength fittings (cite: Bakx2020) and the \[CII\] line-based method introduced in (cite: Sommovigo2021), our method, fixing ${\rm log}\,\xi_{\rm clp}=1.02\pm0.41$ was found to accurately estimate the dust temperatures of high-redshift galaxies; this was confirmed by demonstrating general agreements with several established methods." (Conclusion)
- A tentative correlation exists between ISM clumpiness and dust temperature, driven largely by HZ6, suggesting hotter dust galaxies may have more homogeneous ISM geometries.  
  > "Although we simply fixed $\rm{log\,}\xi_{\rm clp}$ to be constant, we also found a tentative correlation between $\xi_{\rm clp}$ and dust temperature (left panel of Fig. 1). This correlation mostly reflects the low dust temperature and high $\xi_{\rm clp}$ of HZ6." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected public ALMA archive observations of z > 5 galaxies with < 1 arcsec angular resolution and significant continuum detections to ensure secure dust emission size measurements, including a subset with >= 3 bands to calibrate the model.  
  > "We selected high-redshift ($z>5$) galaxy observations with relatively high resolution ($<1^{\prime\prime}$) ALMA observations showing significant dust continuum detection in order to assure secure dust emission size measurements." (Data); "We used galaxies with multiwavelength dust detections in order to calibrate the dust emission model (i.e., HZ4, HZ6, HZ9, HZ10, A1689-zD1, and B14-65666), and applied this model to predict the dust temperatures of galaxies with only single-wavelength continuum detection (i.e., J2017-0208, J1211-0118, and MACS04160-Y1)." (Data)
- Why this method: The clumpy sphere analytical radiative equilibrium model of Inoue et al. (2020) incorporates the complicated multi-phase geometry of high-z star-forming galaxies while requiring only single-band continuum flux and size once clumpiness is calibrated, avoiding time-consuming multi-band or line observations.  
  > "Herein, we used the clumpy sphere distribution model with an analytical multi-phase medium (cite: Neufeld1991; Hobson1993; Varosi1999; Inoue2005) because it incorporates the complicated geometry of star-forming galaxies at high redshift." (ISM Geometry and Radiation Model: the Clumpy Sphere Distribution); "Therefore, assuming a reasonable value of $\xi_{\rm cl}$, the method is readily applicable and requires only a relatively small amount of observation time compared with observations obtaining multiple data points across a wide wavelength range." (ISM Geometry and Radiation Model: the Clumpy Sphere Distribution)
- Alternative: Uniform shell and uniform sphere ISM-to-stellar distributions from Inoue et al. (2020) were not used in favor of the clumpy sphere distribution.  
  > "I20 considered three different assumptions regarding ISM-to-stellar distributions, i.e., the uniform shell, uniform sphere, and clumpy sphere distributions. Herein, we used the clumpy sphere distribution model with an analytical multi-phase medium (cite: Neufeld1991; Hobson1993; Varosi1999; Inoue2005) because it incorporates the complicated geometry of star-forming galaxies at high redshift." (ISM Geometry and Radiation Model: the Clumpy Sphere Distribution)
- Alternative: Image-plane 2D Gaussian size fitting with CASA IMFIT was evaluated as an alternative to visibility-plane fitting, found to agree, and visibility-plane sizes were adopted for the analysis.  
  > "2D Gaussian fitting using CASA task IMFIT was performed on the cleaned images, showing that the measured sizes match well with those measured from visibility data. For the following analysis, we used FIR sizes obtained from visibility data." (Size Measurement)
- Alternative: Varying dust emissivity index beta between 1.5 and 2.5 was tested, showing minor systematic shifts within 1-sigma uncertainties; fixed beta = 2.0 was adopted.  
  > "We tested the impact of varying $\beta$ in our fitting by considering different values of $\beta$ between $1.5$ to $2.5$. Indeed, although we found systematic changes in the derived median dust temperature and $\xi_{\rm clp}$, these variations are within the $1\,\sigma$ uncertainty derived when fixing $\beta=2.0$ (i.e., typical $\Delta T_{\rm d}\sim5\,\rm{K}$ and $\Delta \rm{log}\,\xi_{\rm d}\sim0.1$)." (Clumpy Medium Model Fitting using Multiwavelength ALMA observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The dust emission geometry is simplified as spherical using the geometric mean of major and minor axes, ignoring more complex geometries.  
  > "Geometric means were calculated for the best fit major and minor axis FWHMs and used as the radii of the dust continua: $r_{\rm dust} = \sqrt{a\,b}/2$, where $a$ is the major axis FWHM and $b$ is the FWHM of the minor axis. This simplification is required because the I20 method assumes only spherical dust emission geometry." (Size Measurement)
- Limitation (authors): Fixing the ISM clumpiness parameter introduces a scatter of Delta T_d = 6 +/- 11 K and a possible systematic overestimation of dust temperature.  
  > "Although the comparison shows a relatively large uncertainty, and although a possible systematic overestimation of $T_{\rm d}$ was identified when fixing $\xi_{\rm clp}$, the general agreement of dust temperature suggests that the method works effectively for high-redshift galaxies, especially in the average sense." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)
- Limitation (authors): The calibration sample is small (6 galaxies), making the correlation between clumpiness and dust temperature only tentative.  
  > "Thus, due to the lack of statistically significant samples to confirm this relationship, we only tentatively propose this correlation." (Uncertainty of Fixing Dust Geometry)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sign typo in Sections 4.1, 4.2, and 6 where log(xi_clp) is written as +1.02 +/- 0.41 instead of -1.02 +/- 0.41 (as correctly stated in the Abstract, Table 2, and Section 5.1, and required physically since xi_clp < 1).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Calibration sample size of 6 galaxies is very small, and one galaxy (B14-65666) is an ongoing two-clump merger being modeled as a single spherical distribution.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The model assumes optically thin modified blackbody FIR emission, which may break down at rest-frame ~50-100 um for compact, dusty galaxies or very high temperatures like MACS0416-Y1.
