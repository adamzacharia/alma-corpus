## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure and correct stellar positions**: PSF-sized Gaussian positions; L-band linear transformation using positional deviations from linear trajectories  
  > "data points derived from a PSF-sized Gaussian fit." (Keplerian fit); "we apply a linear transformation to the investigated sources observed in the L-band." (Linear transformation); "In the final step, we apply $\sigma_{pos}$ to the Gaussian estimated positions of the dusty sources." (Linear transformation)
   - parameter: NACO K- and L-band observations, 2002–2018  
  > "We chose the K- and L-band data for the orbital analysis of these sources because the data covers almost two decades of continuous observations between 2002 and 2018." (Orbits)
   - assumption: Local linear trajectory over the analyzed baseline  
  > "the assumption of a local linear trajectory for the analyzed data baseline is justified." (Linear transformation)
   - fitting: PSF-sized Gaussian fit  
  > "data points derived from a PSF-sized Gaussian fit." (Keplerian fit)
   - validation: Discard data with deviations larger than one pixel  
  > "for deviation values larger than one pixel, we apply a quality control and discard the data due to poor quality." (Linear transformation)
2. **Estimate the cluster–Sgr A* distance**: Extract a core Brγ spectrum; combine LOS velocity and proper motion using r = GM/(v_LOS² + v_prop²)  
  > "we furthermore place a circular-annular aperture" (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*); "r_{\rm IRS13}\,=\,GM_{\rm SgrA*}/(\sqrt{v_{\rm LOS}^2+v_{\rm prop}^2})^2" (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
   - parameter: Core aperture radii 0.25″ and 0.26″  
  > "The aperture consists of a circular area with a radius of 0.25"." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*); "The total radius of the circular annular aperture is 0.26"." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
   - parameter: Brγ rest wavelength 2.1661 μm; shifted wavelength 2.1635 μm  
  > "we indicate the Br$\gamma$ rest wavelength at 2.1661$\mu$m, the related Doppler-shifted emission line at 2.1635$\mu$m." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
   - assumption: Close-to-circular bound cluster trajectory  
  > "we assume a close-to-circular bound trajectory of IRS 13 around the central black hole." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
   - assumption: Connection between cluster-member radial velocity and gas  
  > "we assume a connection between the radial velocity of the members of the cluster and the gas." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
   - assumption: 8 kpc for proper-motion conversion  
  > "For the conversion from mas/year to km/s, we assumed a distance of 8 kpc." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare distances inferred from literature E-star proper motions and radial velocities  
  > "the listed proper motion and radial velocity in (cite: Paumard2006), (cite: 2009ApJ...697.1741B), and (cite: Jia2023)" (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*); "a similar 3d distance for the E-stars" (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
3. **Fit stellar orbits and local uncertainties**: Keplerian fits with SLSQP; select minimized reduced χ² solutions; MCMC uncertainties  
  > "We use the Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP) method to estimate a Keplerian solution" (Keplerian fit); "we select the orbits with the highest probability given by the minimized reduced $\chi ^2$" (Cluster analysis); "The associated uncertainties for the orbital elements are extracted from the provided corner plots." (Supplementary material)
   - parameter: E-stars, DS sources, and Greek-named sources; exclude ι and ϑ because of blending  
  > "For the Keplerian orbits, we select all E-stars (seven sources) and the DS sources (33 sources)" (Orbits); "In addition to crowding effects, $\iota$ and $\vartheta$ are blended with nearby sources that hinder a confusion-free analysis." (Orbits); "Hence, we exclude both sources from the Keplerian analysis." (Orbits)
   - assumption: All fitted sources bound to Sgr A*; black-hole mass 4.02 × 10^6 M⊙  
  > "we assume that all the sources are gravitationally bound to Sgr A\*" (Orbits); "adopt the mass of 4.02$\,\times\,10^{6}$M$_{\odot}$ for the supermassive black hole (cite: eht2022; Peissker2022)." (Orbits)
   - fitting: SLSQP minimizes reduced χ²; MCMC uses listed Keplerian elements as a prior  
  > "The SLSQP method calculates the reduced $\chi ^2$ and aims to minimize/optimize the distance between the calculated fit and the input positions (cite: Kraft1988)." (Keplerian fit); "we present the MCMC simulations using the Keplerian elements" (MCMC simulations); "as a prior." (MCMC simulations)
   - validation: Compare alternative orbit solutions and inspect eccentricity–semi-major-axis parameter space  
  > "we selected another Keplerian solution with an increased $\chi^2$ indicating a statistically poor solution for the given data set." (Orbits); "we want to complete this discussion by inspecting possible values for the eccentricity e and the semi-major axis a." (Parameter space of the eccentricity and semi-major axis)
4. **Identify ordered orbital structure**: Inspect inclination, LOAN, and position-angle distributions; assign N1/N2 using LOAN  
  > "we use the inclination and the LOAN as reliable quantities for estimating the distribution of the investigated sources in the 3-dimensional space." (Cluster analysis); "by using the LOAN as a distinguishing parameter." (Cluster analysis); "With the definition of the PA using atan2, which is the 2nd argument of the arctangent (cite: Ali2020), we estimate the orientation of all the dusty sources including the E-stars." (Cluster analysis)
   - parameter: Uncertainties on group means based on standard deviation  
  > "The related uncertainties are based on the standard deviation." (Cluster analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with Paper I anisotropy and inspect a–e distribution for a second structure  
  > "the clustering of the objects is in agreement with the non-randomized distribution of the dusty sources" (Cluster analysis); "we do not find traces of a second kinematic structure, which challenges the proposed classification of the IRS 13 cluster members into two groups" (IRS 13 and the (C)CWS)
5. **Test cluster migration timescales**: AMUSE N-body simulations in a static enclosed-mass potential; impose reduced virial velocities  
  > "For the model, we use the modules AMUSE-PhiGRAPE (cite: Harfst2007), AMUSE-BHtree (cite: Barnes1986), and AMUSE-ph4 (cite: Portegies-Wart2018) to perform the simulations." (N-body simulations); "we use a static potential as a function of distance" (N-body simulations); "We mimic the in-spiral motion caused by the loss of angular momentum of the cluster by setting $v_{\rm virial}$ to 20$\%$ of v$_{\rm relax}$" (N-body simulations)
   - software: Astrophysical Multipurpose Software Environment, AMUSE-PhiGRAPE, AMUSE-BHtree, AMUSE-ph4
   - parameter: Cluster mass 4.5 × 10^4 M⊙; King parameter 16; size 0.1 pc; initial location (0,3) pc  
  > "|      Cluster Mass       | 4.5$\times$10$^4 M_{\odot}$ |" (Table 4); "|     King parameter      |             16              |" (Table 4); "is located at a distance of 3 pc from Sgr A\* with an initial size of 0.1 pc (cite: Petts2017)." (N-body simulations)
   - parameter: N = 64, 248, 512; simulated time span about 0.0–0.3 Myr  
  > "we set the number of particles used for the model to 64, 248, and 512." (N-body simulations); "we limit the simulations to a time span of about 0.0-0.3 Myr" (N-body simulations)
   - parameter: Sgr A* mass 4 × 10^6 M⊙; cusp about 5 × 10^5 M⊙; enclosed region 3 pc; α = 1.2  
  > "We set the mass of Sgr A\* to $4\times10^6\,M_{\odot}$" (N-body simulations); "We adapt the Ansatz of (cite: Hobbs2009) using a stellar cusp mass of about $5\,\times\,10^5\,M_{\odot}$." (N-body simulations); "we set to 3 pc whereas the mass distribution exponent is fixed to $\alpha\,=\,1.2$" (N-body simulations)
   - parameter: Virial velocity reduced to 10% upon entering the NSC  
  > "we mimic by setting $v_{\rm virial}$ to 10$\%$ as soon as the cluster enters the NSC." (N-body simulations)
   - assumption: Equal particle masses; no stellar winds or local density fluctuations  
  > "stellar winds or local density fluctuations are not included" (N-body simulations); "the modeled cluster encloses particles with the same mass" (N-body simulations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Vary particle number and compare migration timescales with published simulations  
  > "By using different particle numbers, we can furthermore investigate if the migration timescales depend on the number of stars in the cluster." (N-body simulations); "we find a migration timescale for a cluster of $\leq$ 0.3 Myrs in agreement with the numerical simulations presented in (cite: Bonnell2008), (cite: Hobbs2009), and (cite: Jalali2014)." (N-body simulations)
6. **Assess the extended cluster membership**: Use dereddened J−H and H−K colors and Q-factor to assess IRS 2L membership  
  > "With the estimated derredened J-H and H-K colors, we incorporate the Q factor" (Dimensions of IRS 13)
   - parameter: Q = (J−H)·a − (H−K); a = 1.0 after dereddening  
  > "$Q\,=\,(J-H)\cdot a\,-\,(H-K)$" (Dimensions of IRS 13); "we set $a=1.0$ due to the obliterated reddening." (Dimensions of IRS 13)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare membership indications with literature polarization and dust structures  
  > "the polarization observations (cite: Witzel2011) presented in (cite: Buchholz2013) strongly suggest that IRS 2L is indeed an embedded source in the dust feature that also encloses the IRS 13 cluster (cite: Roche2018)" (Dimensions of IRS 13)
7. **Estimate the E3 virial mass from ALMA gas**: Represent the velocity-resolved H30α ring; calculate M_IMBH = R_gas v_R²/G  
  > "choosing a different representation of the ionized ring of the H30$\alpha$ line distribution around the position of the E star E3" (IRS 13E3, a colliding wind product?); "M_{\rm IMBH}=\frac{R_{\rm gas}v_{\rm R}^2}{G}\sim 31434 \pm 7422\,M_{\odot}" (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
   - parameter: R_gas = 0.10 ± 0.01″ ≈ 825 ± 83 AU  
  > "$R_{\rm gas}=0.10 \pm 0.01''\sim 825 \pm 83\,{\rm AU}$" (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
   - parameter: v_R = 130 ± 30 km s−1  
  > "The line-of-sight velocity traced by the line is $v_{\rm R}=130 \pm 30\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$." (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
   - assumption: Gas fully virialized  
  > "Assuming that the gas is fully virialized" (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
   - fitting: Uncertainty from propagation of errors  
  > "where the uncertainty is given by the propagation of errors." (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
   - validation: Independent confirmation of the literature H30α ring representation  
  > "we independently confirm the results of (cite: tsuboi2019; Tsuboi2021)" (IRS 13E3, a colliding wind product?)
8. **Compare the E3 broadband SED with accretion models**: Combine literature radio/mm, infrared, and X-ray constraints; calculate self-similar ADAF SEDs  
  > "The low-frequency mm/radio flux densities at 340, 232, and 42 GHz are adopted from (cite: Tsuboi2017b) and (cite: tsuboi2019)." (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?); "we use the self-similar model of (cite: 2021ApJ...923..260P) (cite: 1995ApJ...452..710N; 1997ApJ...477..585M)" (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)

## Qualitative findings

- IRS 13 has a non-randomized, disk-like orbital distribution  
  > "a non-randomized orientation of the PA for all the investigated cluster members in favor of a disk-like distribution." (Cluster analysis)
- A distinct second kinematic structure is not established; a warped disk could explain the projected distributions  
  > "we do not find traces of a second kinematic structure" (IRS 13 and the (C)CWS); "A possible explanation for the arrangement of the LOAN, inclination, and PA" (IRS 13 and the (C)CWS); "could be projection effects that result in a warped disk." (IRS 13 and the (C)CWS)
- IRS 13 is interpreted as a tidally stable core remnant with an evaporating extended part  
  > "the nature of the IRS 13 core can be described as the tidally stable remnant of a massive inspiralling cluster that is suffering from ongoing evaporation due to the tidal forces of Sgr A\*" (Dimensions of IRS 13)
- H30α kinematics and the broadband ADAF comparison provide tentative IMBH support  
  > "we provide tentative support for the hypothetical presence of the IMBH in the IRS 13 E3 region." (Conclusions)
- Lower-accretion-rate ADAF case is preferred, with additional dust and stellar infrared emission  
  > "The case IMBH I with the lower accretion rate appears more plausible since the infrared emission is likely enhanced by dust and stellar emission" (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long NACO baseline for orbits; velocity-resolved H30α for the hypothetical IMBH mass  
  > "We chose the K- and L-band data for the orbital analysis of these sources because the data covers almost two decades of continuous observations between 2002 and 2018." (Orbits); "The IMBH mass can be estimated from the rotation of the gas traced by H30$\alpha$ line." (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
- Why this method: Keplerian approximation justified by binding to Sgr A*; simplified N-body setup targets migration timescales  
  > "we assume that all cluster members are gravitationally bound to Sgr A\* which justifies the inference of Keplerian orbits" (Conclusions); "Our main objective is a quantitative estimate about the migration timescales of a potential cluster into the NSC." (N-body simulations)
- Alternative: Linear trajectories also represent the measured stellar positions  
  > "We emphasize that a linear fit applied to the data points represents the trajectory of the DS sources as well." (Orbits)
- Alternative: Two warped disks or one projected disk with ascending and descending parts  
  > "The distribution of the groups implies either two warped disks that are inclined by about 50$^{\circ}$ to each other or one disk for which we observe ascending and descending part in projection." (Table 3)
- Alternative: Colliding stellar winds as the E3 explanation; can coexist with an IMBH scenario  
  > "In principle, these two scenarios can coexist with each other" (IRS 13E3, a colliding wind product?); "we note that the collision of stellar winds to explain the nature of E3 is a stationary solution that relies on the distance of E2 and E4." (IRS 13E3, a colliding wind product?)
- Alternative: Stellar rejuvenation cannot be excluded  
  > "we cannot exclude the possibility of rejuvenation." (Alternative interpretations)
- Alternative: Foreground/background superposition assessed with colors and a randomized fake-cluster simulation  
  > "we did not find photometric outliers that would suggest a population of background stars that significantly impact the analysis." (Background stars); "we model a foreground fake cluster that consists of stars with a randomized velocity and direction." (Foreground stars)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stellar Keplerian solutions lack individual radial velocities  
  > "The Keplerian solutions for the projected on-sky positions of the investigated sources lack radial velocities." (Limitations of the analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Some stellar orbits have less than 2% coverage  
  > "the orbital data coverage is less than 2$\%$ for some of the cluster members." (Orbits)
- Limitation (authors): Multiple orbital solutions are acceptable; quoted uncertainty ranges describe local solutions  
  > "multiple orbital solutions with comparable probabilities are acceptable." (Cluster analysis); "The listed error spectrum represents a local solution" (Cluster analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Gas–star radial-velocity link used for the cluster distance may be premature  
  > "the assumed link between the gas and the cluster members may be premature." (3d distance of IRS 13 to Sgr A\*)
- Limitation (authors): Simplified migration simulations omit stellar winds and ambient medium and use equal particle masses  
  > "For these naive N-body simulations, we neglected stellar winds or the ambient medium, which should impact the simulations" (IRS 13, an in-spiraling cluster); "Another limitation is the same mass of all particles used." (IRS 13, an in-spiraling cluster)
- Limitation (authors): IMBH confirmation requires more detailed multiwavelength timing, spectroscopy, and rotating-gas studies  
  > "for the confirmation of the IMBH presence in IRS 13, a more detailed multiwavelength timing and spectral analysis is required in combination with the study of the rotating ionized material" (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
- Limitation (authors): Current X-ray sensitivity cannot access the expected IMBH variability timescale  
  > "the expected variability timescale of the proposed IMBH is on the order of a minute." (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?); "No current X-ray instrument is sensitive enough to provide access to the necessary timescales." (IRS 13E3, an IMBH?)
