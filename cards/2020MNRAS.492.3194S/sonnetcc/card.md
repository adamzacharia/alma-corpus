## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the ALMA continuum at two resolutions**: Fourier transform the calibrated visibilities to dirty images, then clean with tclean (technique like Hodge et al. 2013), once tapered to the IFU resolution (IFM) and once untapered (HR)  
  > "The uv-visibilities in the measuring set were Fourier transformed to create dirty images and these dirty images were subsequently cleaned using a similar technique to that described by (cite: Hodge13)" (2.3.2 Imaging the ALMA data)
   - software: CASA 5.1.2, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting whenever possible  
  > "We created two sets of clean images using natural weighting whenever possible" (2.3.2 Imaging the ALMA data)
   - parameter: Gaussian taper to match the beam to the Halpha IFU PSF (0.6-1.0 arcsec); HR maps untapered, 0.15-0.35 arcsec  
  > "This was done by applying a Gaussian taper" (2.3.2 Imaging the ALMA data); "which have a final resolution of 0.15–0.35 arcseconds" (2.3.2 Imaging the ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that continuum spectral windows contain no visible emission lines  
  > "We verified that the spectral windows used to create the continuum images did not contain any visible emission lines" (2.3.2 Imaging the ALMA data)
2. **Measure FIR flux density and size from the visibilities**: Phase-centre the data, bin visibility amplitudes in uv distance, fit a constant (point source) or a zero-centred Gaussian (resolved source) and pick the model with BIC  
  > "Our preferred method to obtain total flux density measurements and sizes from the ALMA data is to use the visibilities directly, as it does not rely on the choices made during the imaging process." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data); "We modelled these binned visibility amplitudes either as a constant over $uv$-distance (describing a point source) or as Gaussian centred at 0 k$\lambda$ (describing a resolved 2D Gaussian source)." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - software: CASA fixvis, CASA uvmodelfit, Scipy curve_fit
   - parameter: phase centre taken from the peak of the High Resolution images, using fixvis  
  > "We first phase centred our data to the objects’ central coordinates using CASA’s `fixvis`." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - parameter: uv-distance bins of 50 klambda  
  > "binning across the $uv$ distance in steps of 50 k$\lambda$" (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - parameter: Gaussian model accepted only if Delta BIC >= 2; unresolved sources get the beam size as an upper limit  
  > "only accepting the Gaussian extended model if $\Delta$BIC $\geq 2$" (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data); "For these objects, we used the size of the beam as a conservative upper limit on the size." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - fitting: Least-squares fit with Scipy curve_fit; 1 sigma errors from the covariance matrix; BIC model selection  
  > "We fitted these models using the Scipy’s `curvefit`" (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data); "We used the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) to choose the best-fit model" (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - validation: Same extended/point-source classification from CASA uvmodelfit  
  > "Reassuringly we obtain consistent result on which of the sources are extended by using CASA’s `uvmodelfit` routine which directly fits to the calibrated $uv$ visibilities." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
3. **Check flux density and measure FIR size in the image plane**: Fit a single elliptical Gaussian with imfit for total flux density; curve of growth on the IFU-matched maps for half-light size, deconvolved with the beam curve of growth  
  > "To obtain the total flux density measurements we used CASA’s `IMFIT` routine to fit a single elliptical Gaussian model convolved with the synthesised beam." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data); "we calculated the total flux in the ALMA maps using apertures with increasing size where the apertures were centred on the location of peak emission" (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: curve of growth normalised to the imfit total flux densities; not done for the two unresolved low-SNR (<8) sources  
  > "The COG are normalised to the total flux densities obtained from the `IMFIT` fitting results." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: imfit flux densities agree with the visibility-derived ones within 1 sigma; the two size methods disagree beyond 1 sigma only for ID1 and ID7  
  > "These fits reproduced consistent flux densities (within the 1$\sigma$ errors) that were obtained directly from the visibilities described above." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data); "there are only two targets where the the two different size measurements are not consistent within their 1 $\sigma$ uncertainties: ID1 and ID7" (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
4. **Derive FIR luminosity, stellar mass and SFR(FIR) from SED fitting**: Bayesian SED template fitting of UV to submm photometry (including the ALMA fluxes) with four components, then SFR(FIR) from L_IR,SF with the Kennicutt & Evans 2012 calibration  
  > "The multi-wavelength SEDs of the targets were modelled using the Bayesian SED code `FortesFit` (cite: Fortes). Four SED components were used in the modelling:" (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties); "we estimate star formation rates (SFR(FIR)) using the calibration from (cite: Kennicutt12)" (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
   - software: FortesFit
   - parameter: stellar: Bruzual & Charlot library, delayed exponential SFH, ages 0.001-1 Gyr, timescales 0.01-2 Gyr, A_V < 10 mag  
  > "with a star-formation history modelled as a delayed exponential with a range of ages (0.001-1 Gyr) and exponential timescales (0.01-2 Gyr)" (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties); "A variable screen extinction following a Milky Way law was applied (A$_{\rm V}<$ 10 mag)." (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
   - parameter: star-formation dust emission: Dale et al. one-parameter template sequence (0-4)  
  > "following the one-parameter template sequence (0–4) from (cite: dale14)" (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
   - assumption: Chabrier initial mass function  
  > "assume a (cite: Chabrier03) initial mass function (IMF)" (1 Introduction)
   - assumption: ALMA 870 and 1100 um emission is dominated by dust-obscured star formation (no significant synchrotron)  
  > "we argue that the ALMA 870 and 1100 $\mu$m emission provides a good tracer of the dust dust obscured star formation in these sources." (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
   - fitting: Bayesian fit with probabilistic priors on accretion disc and AGN dust luminosity from the X-ray luminosity; marginalised posteriors; sampler not stated  
  > "Probabilistic priors were used to constrain the luminosity of the accretion disc and AGN dust emission components based on the X-ray luminosity." (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
   - validation: Radio spectral-index and flux-ratio checks for synchrotron contamination of the ALMA flux  
  > "For the radio-quiet sources, this is supported by the fact that the sub-mm fluxes (Table [\[Table:Sample_ALMA\]](#Table:Sample_ALMA)) would imply unrealistic (highly inverted) spectral indices of $\alpha>0.4$ if they arose from synchrotron emission." (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
5. **Extract IFU spectra and fit emission lines**: Extract spectra in a 5 kpc nuclear aperture and a maximum aperture around the continuum peak; fit [O III], Halpha, [N II] and Hbeta with one or two Gaussians, choosing the second component by BIC  
  > "From each data cube we extracted spectra from two different circular apertures centred on the continuum peak: (a) a nuclear aperture within 5 kpc diameter" (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure); "each line was fitted with one or two Gaussian components, with the centroids, FWHM and fluxes (normalisation) as free parameters." (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
   - software: lmfit
   - parameter: second Gaussian accepted if Delta BIC > 2; residuals also inspected visually  
  > "(using $\Delta$BIC$>2$ as boundary for choosing a more complex model)" (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
   - parameter: [O III] 5007/4959 flux ratio fixed at 2.99; [N II] 6583/6548 ratio fixed at 3.06  
  > "We tied the line widths and central velocities of the two lines and fixed the \[O iii\]$\lambda$5007/\[O iii\]$\lambda$4959 flux ratio to be 2.99" (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure); "flux ratio was fixed to be 3.06 (based on the atomic transition probability; (cite: Osterbrock06))" (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
   - parameter: sky-line residual wavelengths masked; noise taken as the RMS of the emission-line free region  
  > "The noise of the spectra was estimated as the RMS of the emission-line free region in the spectrum." (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
   - assumption: Halpha and [N II] come from the same gas (tied velocity and width)  
  > "This approach, which assumes that the H$\alpha$ and \[N ii\] emission comes from the same gas, is commonly used in high-redshift observations to limit the number of free parameters" (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
   - fitting: Least-squares fit with lmfit; Monte Carlo errors from 500 noise realisations  
  > "Best-fit solutions, and the uncertainties, for the free parameters were obtained using the Python `lmfit` least-square library." (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure); "We repeated this 500 times to build a distribution of all free parameters." (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
   - validation: Monte Carlo errors consistent with lmfit errors  
  > "are consistent with the errors estimated by the `lmfit`" (3.1.1 Extracting spectra and emission-line fitting procedure)
6. **Derive Halpha SFR with and without dust correction**: Convert Halpha luminosity to SFR with Kennicutt & Evans 2012; correct with the Balmer decrement where narrow Hbeta is available (ID3, ID5, ID8)  
  > "To estimate SFR(H$\alpha$) we converted from the measured L$_{\rm H\alpha}$ by using the calibration from (cite: Kennicutt12)." (3.1.2 Dust-corrections); "The preferred approach to constrain this is to measure the nebular dust attenuation (A$_{\rm V,HII}$) using the Balmer decrement (cite: Reddy15)." (3.1.2 Dust-corrections)
   - parameter: Calzetti extinction curve, intrinsic Halpha/Hbeta = 2.86  
  > "We assume the (cite: Calzetti00) extinction curve and an intrinsic H$\alpha$/H$\beta$ ratio of 2.86" (3.1.2 Dust-corrections)
   - assumption: Global star formation unchanged over the past 100 Myr, so Halpha and FIR SFRs are comparable  
  > "we assume that the global star formation has not changed significantly in the past 100 Myr ($\sim$ 1 dynamical time)" (4.2 Comparison of star-formation rates from FIR and Halpha)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with SFR(FIR); AGN photoionisation noted as an upper-limit caveat on SFR(Halpha)  
  > "the SFR(H$\alpha$) should be considered an upper limit on the SFR, due to possible photo-ionisation from the AGN" (4.2 Comparison of star-formation rates from FIR and Halpha)
7. **Map narrow Halpha and the [O III] outflow**: Spaxel-by-spaxel line fitting on spectra binned over 0.2 arcsec for Halpha; for [O III] a narrow-band image of the blue-shifted outflow velocity slice, because a second component could not be fitted per spaxel  
  > "We binned the spectra by averaging the nearby spaxels within radius of 0.2 arcsec." (3.1.3 Emission-line maps); "we employed a different method to map the outflow for these 5 targets by creating a narrow-band image in the spectral region of the outflow." (3.1.3 Emission-line maps)
   - software: lmfit

## Qualitative findings

- Halpha is not a reliable star-formation tracer in these AGN hosts: SFR(Halpha) is below SFR(FIR), FIR emission is more compact, and Halpha and FIR peaks are offset in half the sample  
  > "we conclude that H$\alpha$ is unreliable to map SF inside our AGN host galaxies" (abstract)
- No spatial anti-correlation between ionised outflows and star formation is found with either Halpha or FIR; any instantaneous impact must be subtle  
  > "any instantaneous impact on SF by ionised outflows must be subtle, either occurring on scales below our resolution, or on long timescales" (abstract)
- For ID6 (XID 2028) the Halpha cavity reported earlier appears only with the original shallower HK-band cube, pointing to differences in intermediate reduction steps  
  > "This implies that the differences found in this work to those in (cite: Cresci15) are not dominated by the adopted analyses methods but, instead, in the intermediate data reduction steps" (4.4.1 No clear evidence of feedback in ID 6)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sample chosen to have spatially resolved Halpha and [O III] from IFU data plus rest-frame FIR from ALMA  
  > "we select a sample of AGN host galaxies with spatially-resolved H$\alpha$ and \[O iii\] emission from integral field spectroscopy and with ancillary rest-frame FIR data from ALMA" (2 Sample Selection, observations and source properties)
- Why this method: Visibility-plane fitting preferred for flux and size because it does not depend on imaging choices  
  > "Our preferred method to obtain total flux density measurements and sizes from the ALMA data is to use the visibilities directly, as it does not rely on the choices made during the imaging process." (3.2 Flux density and size measurements from ALMA data)
- Alternative: SED-derived stellar attenuation not used to correct Halpha, as poorly constrained for AGN hosts and different from nebular attenuation  
  > "we choose not to use these to correct the H$\alpha$ luminosities because: (1) they are poorly constrained due to the challenges with fitting the uv–optical SEDs of AGN host galaxies" (3.1.2 Dust-corrections)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Sample limited to sources with existing FIR and Halpha detections, so all lie on or above the star-forming main sequence  
  > "it is limited to sources with existing detections in FIR and H$\alpha$ emission, resulting in all of the sources lying on, or above, the ‘main sequence’ of star formation" (4.5 Implications of our results)
- Limitation (authors): Effects of outflows could occur below the resolution of the data (a few kpc) or on long timescales  
  > "However, impact from these outflows could be occurring on spatial scales below those to which we are sensitive (i.e., $<$a few kiloparsec)" (4.5 Implications of our results)
- Limitation (authors): ID2 FIR flux may be partly synchrotron contaminated  
  > "some contamination to the ALMA flux for this single source does not influence our main conclusions in this work" (2.4 SED fitting and sample properties)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only eight targets, selected on both ALMA and IFU detections, so the null result has little statistical power
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Seeing-limited IFU data (0.6-1.0 arcsec) limit the outflow-versus-SF comparison to about 4 kpc scales
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow maps use a velocity slice and brightest-pixel peaks, which are sensitive to noise and to the IFU astrometric correction (0.1 arcsec)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Peak-pixel positions are used instead of model centroids for extended or complex emission
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optical astrometry correction in CDFS relies on a private communication
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ID6 conclusion depends on data cubes that are not publicly available
