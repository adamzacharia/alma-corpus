## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate each field with user masks**: Modified auto_selfcal: first pass with automasking, then manual masks made from local peaks in uniform/briggs0.5/natural maps; solint reduced by factor 2 from inf to int; fall-back mode combines scans and SPWs  
  > "and modified the pipeline such that it takes in a self-calibration mask that can be specified by the user" (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing); "are reduced iteratively by a factor of 2 until the solution no longer satisfies the above criteria" (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing); "We then identified local peaks in these 3 maps and used them as a guide to create self-calibration masks centered on sources with clear detection." (Self-calibration and Imaging processing)
   - software: CASA, tclean, auto_selfcal
   - parameter: Fall-back mode when solint=inf fails: combine all scans and spectral windows  
  > "then the pipeline goes to fall-back mode and combines all the observational scans and different spectral windows to do the self-calibration" (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing)
   - parameter: Mask version 2: all sources above 4 times the rms plus surrounding extended emission  
  > "In the second version, the mask includes all the sources above 4 times the rms as well as the extended emission surrounding it." (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing)
   - parameter: Self-cal weighting must match final imaging weighting (natural, briggs 0.5, uniform)  
  > "we found in order to have the best S/N ratio the the weighting used in the self-calibration" (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared images from default automasking and user-mask pipelines; user masks gave better S/N and cleaner noise  
  > "we found that images from the modified pipeline have a better S/N ratio and a cleaner noise pattern" (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing)
2. **Two-stage imaging in three weightings**: Stage 1 tclean with self-cal masks to make images for drawing final masks on natural maps; stage 2 tclean with multi-scale deconvolver and larger final masks on natural, briggs0.5 and uniform self-calibrated data  
  > "Once the self-calibration was complete, we then proceeded with a two-stage imaging process." (Self-calibration and Imaging processing); "The purpose of the first step is to produce images from which we can draw the final clean masks on all the identified sources." (Self-calibration and Imaging processing); "to produce three different images with different sensitivity and angular resolution" (Self-calibration and Imaging processing)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Cleaned down to 2 sigma in both stages  
  > "and cleaned down to" (Self-calibration and Imaging processing)
   - parameter: Multi-scale deconvolver used to recover 1-2 arcsec emission  
  > "in order to better recover the large-scale emission" (Self-calibration and Imaging processing)
   - parameter: Each weighting imaged from visibilities self-calibrated with the same weighting  
  > "For each weighting scheme, we used the self-calibrated visibilities with the same weighting scheme to achieve the best signal-to-noise ratio." (Self-calibration and Imaging processing)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Detect sources in the images**: scipy ndimage.maximum_filter with beam-sized filter; local maxima kept if S/N >= 4 in all three maps or >= 5 in at least one; one extra source with S/N >= 4 in two of three maps  
  > "We set the filter scale to be the same size as the beam size to filter out structures smaller than the beam size." (Source Detection process); "Lastly, we also added one more source that has" (Source Detection process)
   - software: SciPy
   - parameter: Conservative detection threshold of S/N >= 5 adopted rather than 3  
  > "we adopted a more conservative detection threshold" (Source Detection process)
   - parameter: Field of view set by the 0.2 primary-beam cutoff (37 arcsec); about 1.1e5 beams per map  
  > "the radius at which the map reaches a sensitivity that is 20% of that of the phase center" (Source Detection process)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Expected false positives estimated from beam count at 3, 4, 5 sigma; all but one detected source are above 5 sigma  
  > "All the sources detected in our survey except one are detected at least above" (Source Detection process)
4. **Assign evolutionary class and Tbol**: Class and Tbol taken from eHOPS SEDs (1-850 micron photometry and spectra); Dunham et al. 2015 values when no eHOPS entry; unresolved multiples inherit the same Tbol  
  > "for each source was obtained by constructing Spectral Energy Distribution (SED) plots using photometry and spectra from 1 to 850" (Source Detection Statistics); "In instances where CAMPOS sources lacked corresponding entries in the eHOPS survey" (Source Detection Statistics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Measure dust disk radii and inclinations**: CASA imfit 2D elliptical Gaussian on all three weighted maps; radius = 2 sigma deconvolved major axis (FWHM x 2/2.355, R95); uniform-map value used first, then briggs0.5, then natural; 5 sigma contour on the briggs0.5 map for non-Gaussian, edge-on or low-S/N sources; inclination from axis ratio  
  > "to fit a 2D Gaussian to the continuum data" (Measuring disk radii); "We multiplied the FWHM of the Gaussian deconvolved radius by a factor of 2/2.355 to obtain the disk radius" (Measuring disk radii); "we adopted the measurements from the Briggs 0.5 weighted maps and, in cases where those were not usable, we used the results from the naturally weighted maps as a final resort." (Measuring disk radii)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: Uniform-weighted map used first because it has the smallest beam  
  > "by first adopting the measurements from the uniform weighted maps which have the smallest beam size." (Measuring disk radii)
   - parameter: Unresolved sources get an upper limit of half the beam size  
  > "Triangular markers denote the upper limit of unresolved point sources, set at half the beam size." (Evolution of disk radii)
   - parameter: 5 sigma contour on briggs0.5 maps for 24 listed sources  
  > "contour in the Briggs 0.5 weighted maps to measure the radius" (Measuring disk radii)
   - assumption: Thin circular disks when converting axis ratio to inclination  
  > "assuming thin circular disks, the major-to-minor axis ratio gives inclination angles" (Circumbinary disks); "The inclination angle is derived from the ratio of major and minor axes of the disk, assuming a circular flat disk." (Edge-on Disks)
   - fitting: imfit elliptical Gaussian; no sampler or priors stated  
  > "to fit an elliptical Gaussian on the three sets of images obtained with different weighting schemes" (Measuring disk radii)
   - validation: Compared radii and PA between weightings; consistent for most sources; Gaussian radius may be biased by envelope  
  > "measurements from the three maps are consistent for most sources" (Measuring disk radii); "the 2D Gaussian fitting might not reflect the exact radius of the disks, potentially due to contamination with envelope material" (Measuring disk radii)
6. **Compare radius distributions across class and cloud**: Left-censored Kaplan-Meier cumulative distributions (lifelines), Gaussian kernel density estimates, exponentially modified Gaussian fits per class, Anderson-Darling tests between classes and clouds; circumbinary and Class II disks excluded  
  > "The left-censored fitting function of the Kaplan-Meier estimator for survival analysis was employed to account for the upper limits of unresolved sources." (Evolution of disk radii); "We computed the kernel density distribution of disk radii with a Gaussian Kernel" (Evolution of disk radii); "Lastly, we conducted the Anderson-Darling test to assess the statistical differences among the disk radii distributions of each evolutionary class." (Evolution of disk radii)
   - software: lifelines, SciPy
   - parameter: Unresolved sizes set to half the beam in the KDE  
  > "The sizes of the unresolved point sources are adopted to be half of the beam size." (Evolution of disk radii)
   - parameter: Cloud tests only for clouds with more than 5 disks  
  > "We conducted the Anderson-Darling test between clouds that have more than 5 disks" (Disk radii comparison across molecular clouds)
   - parameter: Circumbinary disks (SMM 2, IRAS16293-2422 A, VLA1623A) and Class II excluded  
  > "In both figures, we excluded the circumbinary disks (SMM 2, IRAS16293-2422 A and VLA1623A) and Class II disks." (Evolution of disk radii)
   - fitting: EMG (mu, sigma, lambda) fitted to the KDE of each class; method and priors not stated  
  > "we fit them with an exponentially modified Gaussian distribution (EMG)" (Evolution of disk radii)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test radial drift as the cause of radius decrease**: Order-of-magnitude drift timescale tau = dr Vk / (St cs^2) / gamma compared with the Class 0 half-life  
  > "We conducted an order of magnitude analysis and compared the two timescales to assess this scenario." (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory); "This timescale is a factor of three longer than the half-life of the Class 0 stage" (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory)
   - parameter: Isothermal midplane at 10 K (sound speed 0.2 km/s)  
  > "Assuming an isothermal midplane temperature of 10 K" (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory)
   - parameter: Grain radius about 1 mm  
  > "Our ALMA 1.3 mm continuum maps mostly trace millimeter dust grains, so we adopt" (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory)
   - assumption: Epstein drag, compact spherical grains, isothermal gas density profile, grain internal density 1.6 g cm-3, Class 0 mass 0.1 Msun, surface density about 10 g cm-2  
  > "Assuming Epstein drag law, compact spherical particles, an isothermal gas density profile" (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory); "is the internal density of dust grains which is adopted to be" (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Fit the Rgas/Rmm versus Tbol trend from literature**: Compile literature R_gas,Kep and R_mm with Tbol, then fit a Bayesian power law with MCMC  
  > "We compiled the existing literature for disks with both a measurement of the gas disk radius" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase); "to draw 2,000 posterior samples in 50 sampling chains to conduct the Monte Carlo Markov chain (MCMC) modeling" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 2,000 posterior samples, 50 chains, burn-in 1,000  
  > "We set the burn-in period to be 1,000" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase)
   - parameter: Half the beam size used as radius uncertainty when none given  
  > "we adopted half of the beam size as the uncertainty in the disk radius measurement" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC, simple Bayesian power law; priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "to draw 2,000 posterior samples in 50 sampling chains" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase)
   - validation: Authors caution the trend is not robust owing to mixed resolutions, tracers and methods  
  > "is not robust due to different angular resolutions, different line tracers" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase)
9. **Compare Class 0 radii with disk formation models**: Treat Class 0 dust radii as gas radii (Rgas/Rmm about 1) and compare the observed distribution with hydrodynamic, Hall-effect and ambipolar-diffusion predictions  
  > "In our CAMPOS results, only 1 out of 52 Class 0 sources are unresolved and we do not see a bimodal distribution of Class 0 disk radii." (The formation of protostellar disks); "the average disk radius is a factor of 2.2 times larger than the ambipolar diffusion models" (The formation of protostellar disks)
   - assumption: Class 0 dust disk radius traces the gas disk radius  
  > "we see that Class 0 gas disk radii can be traced by the dust disk radii" (The formation of protostellar disks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Disk radii vary strongly within every class and differ between clouds (Ophiuchus smallest, then Corona Australis; Serpens, Aquila, Chamaeleon larger)  
  > "We find that Ophiuchus has the smallest disks followed by Corona Australis." (Disk radii comparison across molecular clouds)
- VLA 1623Aa and Ab circumstellar disks appear co-planar, challenging the misaligned-disk interpretation  
  > "the resolved circumstellar disks around VLA 1623Aa and VLA 1623Ab in the center of the circumbinary disks are aligned" (Circumbinary disks)
- Six streamer candidates are reported from extended continuum structures, needing line data to confirm  
  > "Note that these 6 sources are only candidates and line observations are needed to analyze the kinematics of these structures" (Streamer Candidates)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Complete the sample of protostellar disks in nearby clouds at 0.1 arcsec resolution, complementing the Orion VANDAM survey  
  > "To complete the sample of protostellar disks in nearby molecular clouds within 450 pc at" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Same radius definition and fitting as the Orion survey for easier comparison  
  > "Thus for easier comparison between younger protostellar disks, we adopted the same definition and methods employed in the Orion Class 0/I disk survey" (Measuring disk radii)
- Alternative: Automasking in tclean for self-calibration, rejected because it adds masks on artifacts or noise  
  > "we found that the auto-masking algorithm would add masks in features that are artifacts or noise" (Self-calibration pipeline modifying and testing)
- Alternative: Envelope contamination as the cause of larger Class 0 radii, judged minimal  
  > "Thus we expect minimal envelope contamination, as large envelope emission would be filtered out by our interferometric observations." (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory); "This implies that most of the continuum structures detected in our survey are not contaminated by the isotropic dense inner envelope." (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory)
- Alternative: Radial drift as the cause of radius shrinkage, rejected as insufficient  
  > "the decrease in dust disk size between Class 0 and Class I in the Ophiuchus and Serpens molecular clouds cannot be explained by radial drift alone" (Evolution of Dust disk size: Comparison with theory)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Sample is severely incomplete for Class II sources, so they are excluded from the analysis  
  > "our sample is severely incomplete for Class II sources" (Sample Selection)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is biased against the lowest-mass (luminosity) protostars  
  > "Our sample lacks representation of protostars nestled within low-mass cores, leading to a bias against the lowest-mass (luminosity) protostars." (Sample Selection)
- Limitation (authors): Gaussian-fit radii may not reflect true disk radii and can include envelope emission  
  > "the 2D Gaussian fitting might not reflect the exact radius of the disks, potentially due to contamination with envelope material" (Measuring disk radii)
- Limitation (authors): Inclinations from axis ratios are lower limits, especially for edge-on disks  
  > "The lack of highly inclined disks is due to the bias of deriving the inclination angle." (Edge-on Disks)
- Limitation (authors): Rgas/Rmm trend is not robust owing to mixed resolutions, tracers and methods in the literature  
  > "is not robust due to different angular resolutions, different line tracers" (The decoupling of dust and gas disk in early Class I phase)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Class 0 dust radii are compared with gas-radius model predictions while the paper's own Rgas/Rmm trend rests on about 25 heterogeneous literature disks, so the model-tension claim is only as strong as that assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gaussian FWHM-based radii at 0.1 arcsec resolution are biased for sources near the beam size and for non-Gaussian profiles; mixed 15 and 18 au samples and a 436 pc cloud group probe different scales
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Anderson-Darling p-values of 0.23 and 0.06 for the pooled classes are weak, so the headline Class 0 to Class I shrinkage rests on per-cloud subsamples (Serpens and Aquila p = 0.03)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Tbol classes come partly from an unpublished catalog, so class assignment cannot be independently checked
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Per-cloud statistics mix different distances, sensitivities and PWV conditions (Chamaeleon has fewer antennas and higher PWV)
