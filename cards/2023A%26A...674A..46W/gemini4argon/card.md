## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and smooth JVLA NH3 cubes and retrieve ALMA continuum image**: Calibrate JVLA data in CASA 4.8.2, image NH3 (1,1) to (7,7) cubes using multiscale CLEAN in TCLEAN with Briggs robust=0.5, smooth all cubes to the NH3 (1,1) beam, and retrieve the archival ALMA 870 um continuum image.  
  > "Image cubes of $\rm{NH_3}$ lines are made using the multi-scale CLEAN algorithm (cite: 2008ISTSP...2..793C) in CASA task TCLEAN with Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations and Data); "To achieve ~~a same synthesized beam from~~ $\rm{NH_3}$ (1,1) to (7,7), we smooth all image cubes ~~into the beam in~~ $\rm{NH_3}$ (1,1) ~~." (Observations and Data); "We ~~retrieve~~ the 870 $\mu\mathrm{m}$ continuum image of G35.20 from the ALMA science archive." (ALMA)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "Image cubes of $\rm{NH_3}$ lines are made using the multi-scale CLEAN algorithm (cite: 2008ISTSP...2..793C) in CASA task TCLEAN with Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations and Data)
   - parameter: common beam = 2.9'' x 1.9'', PA = 67.9 deg  
  > "The synthesized beam in $\rm{NH_3}$ (1,1) is $2.9''\times1.9''$, PA=67.9~~$^ \circ$~~ with the rms noise at 2.8 mJy $\rm{beam}^{-1}$." (Observations and Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit NH3 (1,1) to (7,7) spectral lines simultaneously**: Simultaneously fit the NH3 (1,1) to (7,7) lines using PySpecKit with a six-parameter single velocity component model (excitation temperature, kinetic temperature, column density, ortho/para ratio, centroid velocity, velocity dispersion) via MCMC.  
  > "We use PySpecKit (cite: 2011ascl.soft09001G) to simultaneously fit the NH$_3$ (1,1) to (7,7) lines." (NH3 Fitting); "By limiting the range of parameters and using Monte Carlo Markov-Chain (MCMC) method, we obtain the best fitted result with the lowest relative residuals with the mean value at about 3%." (NH3 Fitting)
   - software: PySpecKit
   - parameter: T_k range: 3 K to 10^2 K; OPR range: 0 to infinite; column density range: 10^14 - 10^17 cm^-2  
  > "We also adopt a number of criteria for parameters to ensure fitted results are reasonable: the kinetic temperature is from 3 K to $10^2$ K, the ortho-to-para ratio is from 0 to infinite and the column density is ${10^{14} - 10^{17}}\ {\rm{cm}^{-2}}$." (NH3 Fitting)
   - assumption: Single velocity component dominates G35.20; optically thin assumption  
  > "Therefore, we adopt the fitting result from one velocity component throughout this paper." (NH3 Fitting); "The lower temperature and the column density also make sure that the thin optical depth assumption is suitable~~$\rm{NH_3}$." (Temperature and Column density)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo Markov-Chain (MCMC) over uniform initial parameter grid  
  > "By limiting the range of parameters and using Monte Carlo Markov-Chain (MCMC) method, we obtain the best fitted result with the lowest relative residuals with the mean value at about 3%." (NH3 Fitting)
   - validation: Checked relative residuals (mean ~3%), local SNR (>3), fitted error maps, and tested single vs multiple velocity components.  
  > "By limiting the range of parameters and using Monte Carlo Markov-Chain (MCMC) method, we obtain the best fitted result with the lowest relative residuals with the mean value at about 3%." (NH3 Fitting); "We check emission lines of those ends and thing that the lower fitted temperature is not due to the relatively lower local SNR: the SNR of each pixel is higher than 3 and the fitted temperature is slowly changed to the two ends." (Temperature and Column density)
3. **Extract position-velocity diagram, fit velocity gradient, and analyze periodicity**: Construct 1-D position-velocity diagram along the filament, exclude pixels within 1 beam of cores A and B, fit a linear model to derive the large-scale velocity gradient, and apply the Lomb-Scargle method to the velocity and column density residuals to determine periodicity and phase separation.  
  > "As ~~core B and core A~~ have obvious different velocities with the host filament, we ~~remove~~ pixels around these two cores within ~~1~~ beam size and fit the rest data in the ~~p-v~~ map with a ~~linear model~~." (Position-Velocity diagram); "The residual is fitted by~~ Lomb-Scargle method to study the ~~period-amplitude distribution." (Position-Velocity diagram)
   - parameter: Rectangular central region [-0.12 - 0.12 pc]; 1 beam exclusion mask around cores A and B  
  > "To investigate the most active region in the central region, we also chose a rectangular region in the central part of G35.20 which corresponds to ~~the~~ \[-0.12~~-~~ 0.12 pc\] in the complete ~~p-v~~ plot (0 point is between ~~Core~~ A and B)." (Position-Velocity diagram); "As ~~core B and core A~~ have obvious different velocities with the host filament, we ~~remove~~ pixels around these two cores within ~~1~~ beam size and fit the rest data in the ~~p-v~~ map with a ~~linear model~~." (Position-Velocity diagram)
   - fitting: Linear model fit for velocity gradient; Lomb-Scargle periodogram for residuals  
  > "The linear fitting ~~shows a small velocity gradient at~~ 3.96 km ${\rm{s}^{-1}}$ ${\rm{pc}^{-1}}$ $\rm{NH_3}$." (Position-Velocity diagram); "The residual is fitted by~~ Lomb-Scargle method to study the ~~period-amplitude distribution." (Position-Velocity diagram)
   - validation: Compared derived core separation period with previous measurement in (cite: 2014A&A...569A..11S)  
  > "Form this distribution, the~~ most likely period is ~~0.0246 pc with error at 0.0011 pc. (cite: 2014A&A...569A..11S) found the separation among those cores is about~~ 0.023 pc ~~which is a little bit smaller than ours but still within the error." (Position-Velocity diagram)
4. **Calculate intrinsic non-thermal velocity dispersion and Mach number map**: Subtract broadening due to channel width, thermal velocity dispersion, and large-scale velocity gradient in quadrature from the fitted velocity dispersion, then divide by the sound speed to obtain the Mach number map.  
  > "To calculate the Mach number, we first correct the line width ~~which be~~ broadened by the effect from the channel width:" (Mach Number); "Then we calculate the non-thermal velocity dispersion from the fitted temperature and the $\sigma _{{V_{obs}}}$ (cite: 1983ApJ...270..105M):" (Mach Number); "As the correction is based on each pixel which has ~~limited~~ resolution, we need to deduct the velocity gradient from the large scale motion to get the line width ~~which is~~ purely caused by turbulence." (Mach Number)
   - parameter: channel width = 0.21 km s^-1 (or 0.2 km s^-1 in Eq. 2); large-scale gradient = 3.96 km s^-1 pc^-1  
  > "Here the $\sigma _V^2$ is driven from the fitted result and the $\sigma _{{V_{channel}}}^2$ is channel width (in this work is 0.21 km ${\rm{s}^{-1}}$)." (Mach Number); "The large scale gradient (3.96 km ${\rm{s}^{-1}}$ ${\rm{pc}^{-1}}$) which is driven from the linear fitting is adopted to correct the turbulence as we mentioned in section. 4." (Position-Velocity diagram)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested smoothing channel width by factors of 2 and 3 to evaluate channel broadening impact  
  > "If we smooth the channel width to 2 and 3 times than current one which is similar to that of (cite: 2013MNRAS.432.3288S; Lu_2014; Lu_2018) , the channel width would contribute more “turbulence” than that from the thermal motion under the typical temperature of 25 K." (Mach Number)
5. **Calculate clump Jeans properties and re-measure ALMA 870 um core fluxes and masses**: Calculate thermal and turbulent Jeans mass and length for the clump assuming spherical geometry, and re-measure the ALMA 870 um continuum fluxes and masses of the 6 cores using the NH3 temperature and SNR criteria.

## Qualitative findings

- Turbulence in G35.20 is overall supersonic (peak Mach number ~2.8), but dissipates toward the dense ALMA cores where local Mach number minima occur.  
  > "Interestingly, most of ~~ALMA continuum cores have the local minimum value in the Mach number map." (Supersonic Turbulence and its Dissipation Towards Cores); "The peak value ~~locates~~ around 2.8~~(similar as the inner of the central part) which indicates the turbulence is overall supersonic." (Supersonic Turbulence and its Dissipation Towards Cores)
- The phase difference between periodic column density and velocity oscillations along the filament is nearly zero, favoring MHD wave theory over gravitational instability.  
  > "The difference of the phase between the column density and the velocity is almost zero which prefers the MHD wave theory." (Position-Velocity diagram)
- Because G35.20 is an evolved IRDC with active outflows, its supersonic turbulence is affected by protostellar feedback and is not in a pristine state.  
  > "However, because G35.20 is evolved, the turbulence is not at a pristine state." (NH3 Ortho/Para Ratio Along Outflow)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA 870 um continuum resolves the thermal Jeans length and core fragmentation, while JVLA NH3 (1,1)-(7,7) lines provide high spectral resolution to resolve the thermal linewidth and measure kinetic temperature across high temperatures.  
  > "We use ALMA dust continuum emission to resolve fragmentation, and JVLA $\rm{NH_3}$ (1,1) to (7,7) inverse transition lines to trace linewidth, temperature, and dynamics." (abstract)
- Why this method: Simultaneous fitting of NH3 (1,1) to (7,7) including ortho (3,3) and (6,6) lines allows fitting OPR and high kinetic temperatures (>50 K) where cold-ammonia models fail, and subtracting channel width and velocity gradient broadening avoids overestimating non-thermal turbulence.  
  > "We do not use the cold-ammonia model presented in (cite: 2012ApJS..200....2L) because of the relatively high temperature which is more than 50 K (in Fig. [\[re\]](#re)) in the center of G35.20." (NH3 Fitting); "Therefore, we stress the necessity of measuring the broadeningof the channel width~~, otherwise the non-thermal motion would be overestimated." (Mach Number)
- Alternative: Cold-ammonia model (rejected because the central kinetic temperature of G35.20 exceeds 50 K).  
  > "We do not use the cold-ammonia model presented in (cite: 2012ApJS..200....2L) because of the relatively high temperature which is more than 50 K (in Fig. [\[re\]](#re)) in the center of G35.20." (NH3 Fitting)
- Alternative: Multiple velocity components in NH3 fitting (tested in Appendix A and rejected in favor of a single velocity component because G35.20 is dominated by one component).  
  > "To avoid the possible existence of other gas components, we have fitted all~~ $\rm{NH_3}$ data cubes with one and more velocity components, and find that G35.20 is dominated by a single velocity component (~~details can be found~~ in appendix A)." (NH3 Fitting)
- Alternative: Gravitational instability (GI) model for periodic filament fragmentation (rejected in favor of MHD wave model because the phase separation between column density and velocity is nearly zero rather than pi/4).  
  > "The difference of the phase between the column density and the velocity is almost zero which prefers the MHD wave theory." (Position-Velocity diagram)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gas accretion rate cannot be accurately estimated due to complex motions in the central region and inability to set a fixed NH3-to-H2 conversion factor.  
  > "Due to the complex motion in the central region, we can not estimate the accurate accretion rate by the X factor." (Fragmentation , Gas properties and Star formation); "But due to the complex gas motion, we can not set a fixed factor for convert $NH_3$ into $H_2$ so we can hardly calculate the gas accretion rate to verify this assumption." (Fragmentation , Gas properties and Star formation)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA observations do not cover the northern part of the filament, so it is unknown whether dense cores exist there.  
  > "The density in the northern part is similar to the southern part but ALMA have not done similar observation here so we don’t know if there are cores or not." (Fragmentation , Gas properties and Star formation)
- Limitation (authors): G35.20 is an evolved cloud with active outflows, so its turbulence does not represent pristine initial conditions of massive star formation.  
  > "However, because G35.20 is evolved, the turbulence is not at a pristine state." (NH3 Ortho/Para Ratio Along Outflow)
