3. **Estimate dust disk outer radius under optically thick assumption**: Calculate radial temperature profile T(r) following Isella et al. (2014, 2019) and integrate Planck function B_nu(T(r)) from r_in to r_out to match observed flux density  
  > "In brief, we substitute a radial profile of the disk temperature $T(r)$ into the Planck function $B_\nu$ and integrate $B_\nu(T(r))$ from the disk inner radius $r_{\rm in}$ to the outer radius $r_{\rm out}$ to calculate the flux density $F_\nu$, i.e., $F_\nu = \frac{2\pi \langle\cos i \rangle}{D^2}\int^{r_{\rm out}}_{r_{\rm in}} B_\nu(T(r))r\,dr$, where $D$ is the distance to the source and $i$ is the disk inclination whose mean value is $\langle \cos i \rangle=2/\pi$." (Dust Disk Radius); "We follow (cite: Isella14; Isella19) to calculate the temperature profile as $T(r)^4 = T_{\rm \ast,\,irr}^4 + T_{\rm PMC,\,irr}^4 + T^4_{\rm acc}$, where $T_{\rm \ast,\,irr}$ and $T_{\rm PMC,\,irr}$ accounts for the irradiation from the star and the PMC, respectively, and $T_{\rm acc}$ comes from viscous heating due to accretion." (Dust Disk Radius)
   - software: MATLAB
   - parameter: r_in = 1.6 R_Jup (PMC radius), D = 112.5 pc, <cos i> = 2/pi  
  > "The SED fitting in (cite: SM18) yields a spectral type of L$0\pm1$, log *g* $=4\pm0.5$, and an effective temperature of $2600\pm100$ K, so the radius of SR 12 c using the Stefan–Boltzmann law is expected to be about 1.6 $R_{\rm Jup}$." (SR 12); "We note that in this analysis, the disk inner radius is taken to be the PMC radius because the disk temperature there is still below the dust sublimation temperature of $\sim$1500 K." (Dust Disk Radius)
   - assumption: Optically thick continuum emission; irradiation from host binary ignored at 980 au; mean disk inclination <cos i> = 2/pi  
  > "Because optically thick emission scales with surface area, we can apply this assumption to estimate the disk radius needed to account for the observed emission." (Dust Disk Radius); "At 980 au, we can safely ignore the irradiation from the host binary." (Dust Disk Radius)

## Qualitative findings

  > "This suggests that disks around bound and unbound planetary-mass objects can span a wide range of masses." (Abstract)

  > "Compared with Figure 4 in (cite: W20), both the PDS 70 c and SR 12 c disks are in line with the relationship between the disk flux density and the host mass in the stellar regime." (Scaling Relation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SR 12 c exhibits active H-alpha accretion and Spitzer infrared excess, and its ultrawide 980 au separation allows deep ALMA Band 7 imaging (~5 times deeper than previous surveys) to detect a faint PMC disk free from host star contamination.  
  > "Deep ALMA imaging is thus essential to detect PMC disks and examine their physical properties." (Introduction); "With an rms of 10 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$, $\sim$5 times deeper than the previous 0.88 mm survey in (cite: W20), this is the most sensitive ALMA imaging of a PMC to date, and the first submillimeter detection of a circumplanetary disk around a widely separated PMC." (Introduction); "These disk markers,[^2] together with the ultrawide orbit, make SR 12 c a prime target for ALMA deep imaging." (SR 12)
- Why this method: Natural weighting was chosen to maximize S/N; diffuse cloud CO channels were flagged and merged with continuum windows to improve continuum sensitivity; a surface density model was used to trace the optical depth transition as a function of disk outer radius.  
  > "We hence flagged channels that have diffuse CO emission and merged the rest of the window with the three 2 GHz windows." (Observations and Data Reduction); "We adopted natural weighting for a better S$/$N and used the CASA task `tclean` to generate the final continuum map (Figure 1), which has an rms of 10 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ around the map center." (Observations and Data Reduction); "A more comprehensive approach to infer the dust mass and trace the transition of the optical depth is to fit a surface density model to match the observed flux density, as demonstrated by (cite: Isella14; Isella19)." (Dust Mass)
- Alternative: Background submillimeter galaxy contamination rejected based on 0.02% chance alignment probability and proper motion match  
  > "The probability of one galaxy being within 0.1$''$ from SR 12 c is 0.02%." (The SR 12 c Dust Disk); "Thus, this 0.88 mm source is most likely a dust disk around SR 12 c." (The SR 12 c Dust Disk)
- Alternative: CO freeze-out onto dust grains rejected as the primary cause of 12CO(3-2) nondetection because calculated disk temperatures exceed the 20 K CO desorption temperature  
  > "Indeed, the dust temperature calculation in Section 4.2 shows that most parts of the disk are warmer than 20 K, the temperature at which most CO desorbs into the gas phase (cite: Bergin95)." (Gas-to-dust Ratio and Small Gas Disk); "Therefore, CO depletion may not play an important role in the SR 12 c disk." (Gas-to-dust Ratio and Small Gas Disk)
- Alternative: Briggs weighting (robust = 0.5) tested in tclean to check if the disk could be spatially resolved at higher resolution (0.108'' x 0.066'')  
  > "The disk is not resolved with Briggs weighting (robust $=$ 0.5) in `tclean` either, where the beam size is $0.108\ensuremath{''}\times0.066\ensuremath{''}$." (Footnote 3)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The inferred optically thick disk radius depends on the assumed radial temperature profile  
  > "Although the inferred radius depends on the assumed temperature profile, our ALMA observations suggest that the dust disk is probably very small." (Dust Disk Radius)
- Limitation (authors): Optically thin dust mass estimates may significantly underestimate the true dust mass if the continuum emission is optically thick  
  > "Applying the optically thin assumption to what is actually optically thick emission may significantly underestimate the true dust mass (e.g., (cite: BE19))." (Dust Mass)
- Limitation (authors): There is no tight constraint on the OTS 44 disk size to independently calculate its mean disk temperature  
  > "There is no tighter constraint on the OTS 44 disk size to calculate the mean disk temperature (the beam size in (cite: B17) is $1.6\ensuremath{''} \times 1.6\ensuremath{''}$)." (Footnote 6)
- Limitation (authors): The actual accretion rate of SR 12 c may be higher based on newer planetary accretion shock models  
  > "The actual accretion rate may be higher based on the new accretion shock models in (cite: Aoyama21)." (Footnote 7)
- Limitation (authors): High gas-to-dust ratio remains uncertain due to potential optical thickness of the dust and variability of the accretion rate  
  > "However, as the disk probably contains more dust than computed for the optically thin assumption and the accretion rate may be highly variable, it remains unclear if the actual ratio is indeed inconsistent with the canonical value." (Gas-to-dust Ratio and Small Gas Disk)
- Limitation (authors): The constraint on gas disk size from the 12CO(3-2) nondetection assumes foreground cloud absorption is not significant  
  > "If cloud absorption is not significant, a nondetection of ${}^{12}$CO(3–2) implies a compact gas disk around SR 12 c." (Abstract); "Assuming the ${}^{12}$CO (3–2) emission from SR 12 c is not absorbed by foreground clouds, our nondetection favors a small gas disk." (Gas-to-dust Ratio and Small Gas Disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Diffuse ambient cloud emission is explicitly seen across 0.5-8 km/s LSRK, which likely overlaps the systemic velocity of SR 12 in rho Ophiuchi and could absorb or mask compact 12CO(3-2) emission from SR 12 c.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because the continuum source is unresolved at a single wavelength, dust mass and disk radius are degenerate without assuming either the optical depth or a surface density profile.
