## Analysis recipe

1. **Calculate thermodynamic and spectroscopic parameters of C6H6O isomers**: Perform quantum chemical calculations to optimize geometries, verify non-negative vibrational frequencies, and compute electronic energies with zero-point vibrational energy (G4 composite method), enthalpies of formation and dipole moments (DFT-B3LYP/6-31G(d,p)), and rotational constants and partition functions at 150 K (MP2/6-311G++(d,p)).  
  > "To carry out a comprehensive study of thermodynamic data among the ten considered species from C 6 H 6 O isomeric group, we perform quantum chemical calculations using the Gaussian 09 suite of programs (Frisch et al. 2013)." (Appendix); "The electronic energy values with zero-point vibrational energy correction (E 0 +ZPVE) for all the species are calculated. The G4 composite method (Curtiss et al. 2007) is utilized for these computations." (A.1. Electronic Energy with Zero-point Vibrational Energy Correction); "Here, Møller–Plesset of second order perturbation (MP2) method (Møller & Plesset 1934), with the 6-311G++(d,p) including a diffuse function are used to calculate the rotational constants (A, B, and C) of the species of C 6 H 6 O isomeric group (see Table A3)." (A.4. Rotational Constants)
   - software: Gaussian 09, freqchk
   - parameter: T = 298 K for enthalpy of formation; T = 150 K for rotational partition function  
  > "The Enthalpy of Formation at 298 K, Electronic Energy (E 0 ) with Zero-point Vibrational Energy (ZPVE), and Relative Energy with the G4 Composite Method for ten Species of the C 6 H 6 O Isomeric Group" (Table A1); "Calculated Rotational Constant and Rotational Partition Function (Q rot ) at 150 K for Ten Species of the C 6 H 6 O Isomeric Groups with the MP2/6-311G++(d,p) Method" (Table A3)
   - assumption: Rotational symmetry number sigma = 1 for all asymmetric top isomers  
  > "Here σ is the rotational symmetry number having the value 1 for all species, A, B, and C are the rotational constants for the molecule (in MHz) and T is the temperature (in K)." (A.4. Rotational Constants)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare calculated enthalpies of formation, dipole moments, and rotational constants against experimental literature values where available (e.g., 0.4%-0.5% deviation in rotational constants for phenol).

  > "Here, Chemical Model of Molecular Cloud is implemented (Das et al. 2019, 2021; Gorai et al. 2020; Mondal et al. 2021; Sil et al. 2021; Bhat et al. 2022) to study the chemical evolution of phenol in high-mass star-forming regions and dark cloud environments." Hot Core Model); "During the initial warm-up phase, the cloud is allowed to warm up (gas and grain both) to 200 K in 10 [5] yr. Then another 2.12 × 10 [4] yr is used to warm up to 400 K. So, in total, our simulation continues up to 3.212 × 10 [5] yr." (4.1. Hot Core Model)
   - parameter: Dark cloud (DC): n_H = 2.0 x 10^4 cm^-3, T_gas = T_ice = 20 K, A_V = 10, time = 1.0 x 10^7 yr

   - parameter: Binding energies: 2056 K for C6H5 and 5476 K for C6H5OH (scaled by 1.416)

   - assumption: Destruction of phenol in gas phase via photodissociation (alpha = 2.9 x 10^-9, beta = 0, gamma = 1.7), cosmic ray dissociation (alpha = 1.3 x 10^-17, beta = 0, gamma = 263.5), and major ions (C+, He+, HCO+, H3O+)

## Qualitative findings

- Phenol is the most energetically favorable isomer of the C6H6O group, followed by 2,5-cyclohexadienone and cyclohexadienone, while propargyl ether is the least favorable.  
  > "It is noticed that phenol is the most energetically favorable isomer of this group. In contrast, propargyl ether is the least favorable (having relative energy ∼103 kcal mol [−][1] compared to phenol) species of this group." (Abstract); "However, based on the reaction energy, dipole moment, and enthalpy of formation, it shows that phenol, 2,5-cyclohexadienone, and cyclohexadienone are the probable candidates and propargyl ether (the only non-cyclic structure among all the isomers considered here) is the least probable candidate for future astronomical detection in the C 6 H 6 O isomeric group." (5. Conclusions)
- No conclusive detection of phenol is obtained in G10.47+0.03; the tentative transition at 148.489 GHz is blended with other phenol transitions and glycolonitrile (HOCH2CN).  
  > "We notice that this transition is blended with some other transitions of phenol along with a transition of glycolonitrile (HOCH 2 CN)." (2. Astronomical Search for Phenol); "Some transitions (148.6068, 148.9386, 149.2565, and 159.3413 GHz) are marked with blue vertical lines which might have appeared due to phenol, but no conclusive transitions are obtained." (2. Astronomical Search for Phenol)
- Ice-phase hydrogen abstraction of benzene (C6H6 + OH -> C6H5 + H2O), if allowed at ~10 K, followed by gas-phase radiative association (C6H5 + OH -> C6H5OH + hnu) is the primary pathway to form high abundances of phenol in hot cores; otherwise, gas-phase C6H6 + O -> C6H5OH controls its formation.  
  > "It is noticed that the phenyl (−C 6 H 5 ) formation by the ice-phase hydrogen abstraction reaction of benzene (i.e., C 6 H 6 + OH → C 6 H 5 + H 2 O if allowed at ∼10 K) could serve as the starting point for the formation of phenol in the gas phase by radiative association reaction C 6 H 5 + OH → C 6 H 5 OH + hν." (Abstract); "The gasphase reaction C 6 H 6 + O → C 6 H 5 OH significantly contributes to the formation of phenol, when the ice-phase reaction C 6 H 6 + OH → C 6 H 5 + H 2 O is not considered at low temperature." (Abstract)
- Phenol formation in starless dark cloud environments is inadequate even under optimistic rate constant assumptions.  
  > "We notice that even with the upper limit of the rate constant and no temperature limitation for the ice-phase reactions, the formation of phenol is inadequate in the dark cloud condition." (5. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G10.47+0.03 is a Galactic high-mass star-forming hot core where many complex organic molecules and various alcohols have recently been identified, making its ALMA Cycle 4 Band 4 archival data suitable to search for the simplest aromatic alcohol, phenol.  
  > "G10 is another hot core source where many COMs are already reported (Rolffs et al. 2011; Gorai et al. 2020; Mondal et al. 2021)." (2. Astronomical Search for Phenol); "Based on the recent identifications of various alcohols (Mondal et al. 2021) in G10, we search for the simplest alcohol of the aromatic family, phenol in G10." (2. Astronomical Search for Phenol)
- Why this method: Upper limit calculations via LTE spectral analysis are vital for predicting future detections of COMs and inferring reaction pathways and rates, while gas-grain chemical modeling is needed because interstellar chemistry is far from equilibrium and controlled by kinetics and ice-phase pathways.  
  > "Based on the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) analysis, an upper limit on the abundance of phenol is predicted in G10. Upper limit calculations are vital for predicting future detections of complex organic molecules (COMs), including prebiotic molecules (Sahu et al. 2020), and inferring reaction pathways and reaction rates." (1. Introduction); "Interstellar chemistry is far from equilibrium, and the reaction pathways mainly control it." (5. Conclusions)
- Alternative: Direct formation of phenol via C6H6 + OH -> C6H5OH + H (reaction 2) is rejected because it is negligible over 200-2500 K.  
  > "However, Tokmakov & Lin (2002) indicated that the direct formation of phenol with an H atom abstraction from reaction (2) is negligible over the temperature region 200−2500 K" (3.1. At High-temperatures)
