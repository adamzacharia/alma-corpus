## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure continuum flux densities**: Mask emission lines, create continuum visibilities, and fit a single 2D Gaussian in the visibility domain using GILDAS.  
  > "after creating continuum visibilities by masking emission lines, if present, following the same procedure as in §[2.2](#sec:ancillary)." (Continuum Flux Measurements); "We performed visibility-based flux measurements using the task from the software package GILDAS" (Continuum Flux Measurements); "We used a single 2D Gaussian for visibility fitting, keeping source positions, source sizes, and integrated flux densities as free parameters." (Continuum Flux Measurements)
   - software: GILDAS, MAPPING
   - parameter: Exclude channels closer than ±3σ width of [N ii] 205 μm or [C ii] 158 μm.  
  > "To create continuum maps, we excluded channels closer than $\pm3\sigma$ width of the \[N ii\] ($205\,\rm{\mu m}$) or \[C ii\] ($158\,\rm{\mu m}$) emission lines from the calibrated data." (Ancillary ALMA data)
   - assumption: Single 2D Gaussian source model.  
  > "We used a single 2D Gaussian for visibility fitting" (Continuum Flux Measurements)
   - fitting: Free source positions, source sizes, and integrated flux densities.  
  > "We used a single 2D Gaussian for visibility fitting, keeping source positions, source sizes, and integrated flux densities as free parameters." (Continuum Flux Measurements)
   - validation: Confirm individual image detections; ancillary positions consistent across frequencies within beam uncertainties.  
  > "After we confirmed individual detections in all images, we performed continuum flux density measurements in the visibility domain." (Continuum Flux Measurements); "All maps show significant ($\rm{S/N} \simeq 5-30$) continuum detections, with spatial positions that are consistent across different frequencies given the beam uncertainties" (Ancillary ALMA data)
2. **Choose the optical-depth prescription**: Compare Band 6-normalized fluxes with modified-blackbody models for λ0=100 and 200 μm over a range of SED temperatures.  
  > "with fixed $\alpha=2$ and $\beta_{\rm d}=2$) for $\lambda_{0}=100\,{\rm \mu m}$ (left) and $\lambda_{0}=200\,{\rm \mu m}$ (right) for a range of SED temperatures" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "The observed fluxes of our galaxies normalised to Band 6 (at $205\,{\rm \mu m}$) are shown by symbols." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - parameter: Comparison grid: α=2, βd=2, λ0=100 or 200 μm.  
  > "with fixed $\alpha=2$ and $\beta_{\rm d}=2$) for $\lambda_{0}=100\,{\rm \mu m}$ (left) and $\lambda_{0}=200\,{\rm \mu m}$ (right)" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - assumption: Adopt λ0=100 μm; retain both λ0 choices for HZ6.  
  > "The emission at $\sim100\,\mu m$ is therefore likely optically thin and we therefore assume $\lambda_{0}=100\,{\rm \mu m}$ in the following." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "With the current data, it is not possible to make further conclusions and we therefore show in the following derivations using both values of $\lambda_0$ for *HZ6*." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Reasonable changes in α and βd do not change the optical-depth conclusion.  
  > "Different values of $\alpha$ and $\beta_{\rm d}$ in a reasonable range do not change this conclusion." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
3. **Fit the infrared SED**: Fit all three ALMA continuum measurements with a single modified black body plus mid-infrared power-law using PyMC3 MCMC.  
  > "We use the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method provided by the Python package PyMC3" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "to fit the infrared SEDs of our four galaxies including all the three ALMA continuum measurements described above" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "The SED is parameterised as the sum of a single modified black body and a mid-infrared power-law as described in (cite: CASEY12) (cite: BLAIN03)" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - software: PyMC3
   - parameter: 18 000 samples; target acceptance 0.99.  
  > "We draw $18\,000$ samples in total with a target acceptance of $0.99$, which we found to provide the best performance." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - parameter: Fit in linear space.  
  > "We found that fitting in linear space is more appropriate given the errors of the data." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - assumption: α=2.0; λ0=100 μm, with an additional λ0=200 μm solution for HZ6.  
  > "we fix the mid-IR power-law slope to $\alpha=2.0$ as suggested by the measurements in (cite: CASEY12)." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "we therefore assume $\lambda_{0}=100\,{\rm \mu m}$ in the following." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "we therefore show in the following derivations using both values of $\lambda_0$ for *HZ6*." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - assumption: Flat dust-temperature prior; Gaussian βd prior centered on 1.8 with σ=0.5.  
  > "we adopt a flat prior for the dust temperature, and a Gaussian prior for $\beta_{\rm d}$ with a $\sigma(\beta_{\rm d})=0.5$ centered on $1.8$ (cite: HILDEBRAND83)." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - assumption: Gaussian normalization prior in linear space around a Band 7-derived initial guess.  
  > "The normalisation is also sampled with a Gaussian prior in linear space around an initial guess derived by the normalisation in Band 7." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - fitting: No-U-Turn Sampler, an extension to Hamilton Monte Carlo.  
  > "To perform the fitting, we use the *No-U-Turn Sampler* (cite: HOFFMAN11), which is an extension to the Hamilton Monte Carlo algorithm (cite: NEAL12) and is less sensitive to tuning." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive temperatures and infrared luminosities**: Derive Tpeak from the SED peak using Wien’s law; integrate best-fit SEDs over 3–1100 μm for LIR and 42.5–122.5 μm for LFIR.  
  > "The peak temperature is derived using Wien’s law" (Conclusions); "The total infrared luminosities ($L_{{\rm IR}}$) are derived by integrating the best-fit SED between $3-1100\,{\rm \mu m}$" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "We also quote far-IR luminosities ($L_{{\rm FIR}}$) measured by integrating the flux between $42.5\,{\rm \mu m}$ and $122.5\,{\rm \mu m}$ for easier comparison with the literature." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
   - parameter: Tpeak [K] = 2.898×10^3 [μm K] / λpeak [μm].  
  > "T_{\rm peak}\,{\rm [K]} = \frac{ 2.898\times10^3\,{\rm [\mu m\,K]}}{ \lambda_{\rm peak}\,{\rm [\mu m]}}." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)

## Qualitative findings

- Three galaxies require optically thin emission near rest-frame 100 μm; HZ6 also permits λ0=200 μm.  
  > "our new Band 8 observations cannot be fit with $\lambda_0=200\,{\rm \mu m}$ for three out of four galaxies." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "The observations of *HZ6* can be reconciled with optically thick emission up to rest-frame $200\,{\rm \mu m}$." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
- Peak temperatures are warmer than average local galaxies, with little further evolution suggested beyond z=4 at fixed LIR.  
  > "the peak temperatures of high-$z$ galaxies are warmer compared to average local galaxies" (Rising Temperature towards High Redshifts?); "our observational data suggest that the peak temperature (i.e., the wavelength at peak emission of the infrared SED) does not evolve anymore strongly beyond redshifts $z=4$ for a fixed total infrared luminosity." (Rising Temperature towards High Redshifts?)
- Dust-based gas masses generally agree with dynamical estimates; HZ6 depends strongly on the optical-depth assumption.  
  > "Generally, we find an agreement within a factor of two ($<1\sigma$) between gas masses derived from dust continuum and dynamical masses." (Molecular Gas Masses from the RJ dust continuum); "However, assuming $\lambda_0=100\,{\rm \mu m}$ for the fit of *HZ6* results in a $2\sigma$ discrepancy." (Molecular Gas Masses from the RJ dust continuum)
- Main-sequence galaxies are warmer at their far-IR luminosities than the DSFG-normalized L∝T^4 relation predicts.  
  > "However, our galaxies seem to be significantly warmer than predicted by this relation, or, for a given temperature their far-IR luminosity is too faint." (Comparison with Dusty Star-Forming Galaxies at $z>5$)
- Decreasing dust opacity can expose hot dust and produce a plateau in emergent peak temperature.  
  > "Specifically, as the dust opacity drops, hot dust becomes more optically thin and is visible to the external observer." (Conclusions); "Our model shows that once the dust density falls below a certain value, the emergent peak temperature ceases to rise." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Band 8 constrains the SED peak while minimizing observing time; 406.4 GHz maximizes atmospheric transmission.  
  > "Note that Band 8 provides the strongest constraints on the location of the peak of the infrared SED (and hence luminosity weighted dust temperature) while minimising the observation time with ALMA." (Introduction); "At that frequency, the Band 8 atmospheric transmission is maximised and going to higher frequencies would increase the integration times significantly." (New ALMA Band 8 Observation)
- Why this method: Visibility fitting reduces dependence on image reconstruction; linear-space SED fitting suits the measurement errors; NUTS is less sensitive to tuning.  
  > "The visibility domain is less affected by these parameters." (Continuum Flux Measurements); "We found that fitting in linear space is more appropriate given the errors of the data." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "To perform the fitting, we use the *No-U-Turn Sampler* (cite: HOFFMAN11), which is an extension to the Hamilton Monte Carlo algorithm (cite: NEAL12) and is less sensitive to tuning." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
- Alternative: Higher observing frequencies require longer integrations; Band 7 alone gives insufficient SED constraints with the archival data.  
  > "At that frequency, the Band 8 atmospheric transmission is maximised and going to higher frequencies would increase the integration times significantly." (New ALMA Band 8 Observation); "On the other hand, Band 7 observes too low frequencies for robust constraints on the infrared SED together with the archival ALMA observations (cite: FAISST17b)." (New ALMA Band 8 Observation)
- Alternative: λ0=200 μm rejected for three galaxies, retained as an alternative for HZ6.  
  > "our new Band 8 observations cannot be fit with $\lambda_0=200\,{\rm \mu m}$ for three out of four galaxies." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "The observations of *HZ6* can be reconciled with optically thick emission up to rest-frame $200\,{\rm \mu m}$." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
- Alternative: Mass-weighted temperature 35 K instead of 25 K lowers molecular masses by <10%.  
  > "For the latter, we adopt $25\,{\rm K}$, but assuming higher temperatures such as $35\,{\rm K}$ lowers the inferred molecular masses by less than $10\%$." (Molecular Gas Masses from the RJ dust continuum)
- Alternative: High αCO values above 20 excluded for HZ10.  
  > "However, large $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ values above $20$ that are expected for metal-poor environments such as in the Small Magellanic Cloud (cite: LEROY11) can be excluded for *HZ10*." (Molecular Gas Masses from the RJ dust continuum)
- How they decided it was enough: Plan for S/N=10; achieved Band 8 S/N~8–25; select 18 000 MCMC samples and target acceptance 0.99 for best performance.  
  > "This frequency was chosen to optimise the constraints on the infrared SED and to minimise the integration time to reach a S/N of $10$." (New ALMA Band 8 Observation); "All sources are significantly detected ($\rm{S/N}\simeq8-25$) as expected from our observing strategy" (New ALMA Band 8 Observation); "We draw $18\,000$ samples in total with a target acceptance of $0.99$, which we found to provide the best performance." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Only four galaxies, selected among the infrared brightest at z~5.5 because of observing-time constraints.  
  > "However, our conclusions are currently based on a sample of only four galaxies, which are, due to observing time constraints, among the infrared brightest galaxies at $z\sim5.5$." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): The sample likely misses more metal-poor systems.  
  > "It is therefore likely that we are missing more metal poor systems, which could be the more common type of galaxies." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): No constraints blueward of rest-frame ~110 μm; α fixed; HZ4 and HZ6 have larger fitting uncertainties.  
  > "Since our data do not constrain the SED blueward of rest-frame $\sim110\,{\rm \mu m}$ (Band 8), we fix the mid-IR power-law slope to $\alpha=2.0$" (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature); "The galaxies *HZ4* and *HZ6* are fainter, resulting in larger uncertainties of the fit." (Infrared SED Fits and Dust Temperature)
