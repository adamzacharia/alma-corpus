## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce single-dish APEX and NRO 45-m spectra**: Flag bad scans/atmospheric absorption lines (near 595 and 600 GHz for APEX), subtract first-order baselines, average scans, smooth/bin velocity channels (82 km/s for APEX, 50 km/s for NRO 45-m), and convert antenna temperature to flux density using calibrated Jy/K gain factors.  
  > "For each scan, we excluded ∼ 0.3 GHz of the spectrum at the frequency of each atmospheric line. A first-order baseline was then fitted to each scan and subtracted from the spectrum." (2.1. APEX observations); "A first-order baseline was then subtracted from the spectra and the intensities were corrected with a point-source Jy/K conversion factor of Γ=(6.54 ± 0.33) Jy/K. Finally, we integrated the spectra over all schedules and smoothed the resulted spectrum to a velocity channel of bin width 50 km s [−][1] ." (2.2. NRO 45 -m dish observations)
   - software: GILDAS/CLASS, NEWSTAR
   - parameter: APEX channel width = 82 km s^-1; RMS = 0.66 mK (47 mJy); gain Gamma = 72 +/- 8 Jy/K; HPBW = 9.89'' +/- 0.11''  
  > "The RMS noise of this spectrum is 0.66 mK per channel with channel width of 82 kms [−][1], measured using CLASS on continuum-free line-free channels." (2.1. APEX observations); "The flux ratio of this calibrator source was applied to cross scans of Mars obtained in 2022 June with SEPIA660, yielding the antenna gain of [3] Γ=(72 ± 8)Jy/K for 2021 which we used to convert the observed brightness temperature to flux density." (2.1. APEX observations)
   - parameter: NRO 45-m channel width = 50 km s^-1; RMS = 2.6 mJy; gain Gamma = 6.54 +/- 0.33 Jy/K; HPBW ~ 18''  
  > "At this frequency, the HPBW of the 45-m telescope with FOREST is ∼18 [′′] ." (2.2. NRO 45 -m dish observations); "The source was detected in each observing run, however, it was heavily affected by the atmospheric absorption with an RMS noise of 2.6 mJy per 50 km s [−][1] channel." (2.2. NRO 45 -m dish observations)
   - fitting: First-order polynomial baseline subtraction per scan/schedule  
  > "A first-order baseline was then fitted to each scan and subtracted from the spectrum." (2.1. APEX observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit spectral line profiles, apply differential beam corrections, and compute line luminosities and ratios**: Fit both Gaussian and Lorentzian profiles to the BCG and CGM tail velocity components, estimate uncertainties via Monte Carlo realisations adding Gaussian noise equal to channel RMS, apply Gaussian beam response corrections for the offset tail, and compute L' luminosities and ratios combining with archival ALMA CO fluxes.  
  > "In each realisation, we added an artificial Gaussian error to the data with a standard deviation equal to the RMS of the data (47 mJy per channel). Then, we fitted Gaussian functions to both lines and measured all of the line properties in each realisation." (2.1. APEX observations); "To extract the line properties over an integrated region as large as the HPBW (∼ 10 [′′] and ∼ 18 [′′] for APEX and NRO 45m, respectively), we used a Lorentzian fitting function as well as a Gaussian function." (3. Results); "Considering that the APEX HPBW is 9.89 [′′], the tail flux drops by ∼ 40% at the distance of 4 [′′] in a Gaussian beam." (3. Results)
   - software: Astropy, Specutils
   - parameter: Fixed tail velocity offset = 315.8 km s^-1 and fixed tail FWHM = 123 km s^-1 (from ALMA CO(3-2))  
  > "The FWHM of all lines for the tail are assumed to be the same as the FWHM of the [12] CO(3-2) line detected by Fogarty et al. (2019) for the tail of MACS1931." (Table 1)
   - parameter: Beam response correction: +40% for APEX tail fluxes ([CI](2-1) and CO(7-6)) and +15% for NRO 45-m tail CO(1-0) upper limit  
  > "Considering that the APEX HPBW is 9.89 [′′], the tail flux drops by ∼ 40% at the distance of 4 [′′] in a Gaussian beam." (3. Results); "Through a similar analysis for NRO 45-m dish with a HPBW of 18 [′′], we added ∼ 15% to our upper limit estimation of the tail flux in [12] CO(1-0)." (3. Results)
   - assumption: Tail velocity offset and FWHM match the ALMA 12CO(3-2) second velocity component (C2) from Fogarty et al. (2019)  
  > "Assuming that the velocity offset between the two peaks and the FWHM of the small peak are the same as the [12] CO(3 − 2) line from Fogarty et al. (2019), we extracted the line properties, including line widths, fluxes, and luminosities, reported in Table 1." (3. Results)
   - assumption: Tail is approximated as a point source at 4'' distance from the BCG and beam centre within a Gaussian single-dish beam  
  > "Therefore, in order to account for the Gaussian beam response, we assumed the tail to be a point source at a distance of ∼ 4 [′′] from BCG and the beam centre and increased the tail flux by a fraction for the beam response correction." (3. Results)
   - fitting: Gaussian and Lorentzian line profile fitting with Monte Carlo error propagation  
  > "After data reduction within GILDAS/CLASS, we performed a Monte Carlo simulation using the Astropy and Specutils Python packages (Astropy Collaboration et al. 2022; Earl et al. 2023) to estimate the uncertainty of these measurements." (2.1. APEX observations)
   - validation: Comparison of single-dish NRO 45-m CO(1-0) flux (2.5 +/- 0.6 Jy km s^-1) with archival ALMA 12-m CO(1-0) flux (3.02 +/- 0.42 Jy km s^-1) to verify no missing extended flux  
  > "The single-dish flux value of the [12] CO(1-0) line (2.5 ± 0.6 Jykms [−][1] using a Lorentzian fit) is in agreement with the one detected with ALMA 12-m array reported in Fogarty et al. (2019) (3.02 ± 0.42 Jykms [−][1] ) within the error bars." (3. Results)
3. **Model non-LTE molecular gas excitation (T_kin, n_H2, K_vir) for BCG and CGM tail**: Run non-LTE LVG radiative transfer models over a 3D grid of [n_H2, T_kin, K_vir] using MyRadex and LAMDA molecular data, constrained by observed CO line luminosity ratios and the [CI](2-1)/CO(1-0) ratio, and sample the posterior via MCMC.  
  > "To this aim, we used a non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (non-LTE) radiative transfer code, i.e. MyRadex [5] (Du 2022) to map the [ _n_ _H_ 2, _T_ kin, _K_ vir ] parameter space for the CO lines." (3.2. Line ratio modelling); "Then, we fitted a model for each point in our parameter space using MyRadex, and calculated the likelihood of each model based on the observations of CO and CI, following the method of Papadopoulos et al. (2014)." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)
   - software: MyRadex, LSODE, ODEPACK
   - parameter: Grid: 0.7 < log(T_kin [K]) < 3 (step 0.11 dex), 1 < log(n_H2 [cm^-3]) < 7 (step 0.1 dex), 1 < K_vir < 100 (step 0.1 dex)  
  > "We made a grid of numbers in the [ _n_ _H_ 2, _T_ kin, _K_ vir ] parameter space, composed of 0.7 < log [T] [K] [kin] [<] [3] [with] [an] [increment] [of] 0.11 dex, 1 < log [cmn H [−] 2 [3] ] [<] [7] [with] [an] [increment] [of] [0.1] [dex,] and 1 < K vir < 100 with an increment of 0.1 dex." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)
   - parameter: T_back(z) = T_CMB(z) = 3.69 K  
  > "We used the CO and CI molecular data from the Leiden atomic and molecular database (LAMDA [8] ; Schöier et al. 2005) and assumed background temperature as the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature at the cluster redshift, _T_ back(z) = _T_ CMB(z) = 3.69 K." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)

(2023) as" (3.2. Line ratio modelling); "Using the definition of the line luminosity from Equation 1 and optically thin assumption, we can derive the [CI](2-1) luminosity" (3.2. Line ratio modelling)
   - fitting: Gaussian likelihood P = exp(-chi^2 / 2) with MCMC sampling and flat logarithmic priors: 5 < T_kin [K] < 10^3, 1 < log(n_H2 [cm^-3]) < 7, 1 < K_vir < 100  
  > "The probability for each observation follows a Gaussian distribution, using _P_ = exp(−χ [2] /2), while χ [2] =Σ _i_ (1/σ _i_ ) [2] [ _R_ obs( _i_ ) − _R_ model( _i_ ) ] [2], where _R_ obs is the ratio of the measured line luminosities, σ i the error of the measured line ratio, and _R_ model the ratio of the line brightness temperatures calculated by the LVG model." (3.2.   > "However, a temperature of more than 1000 K is not physical for the BCG gas. Thus, we do not consider the second solution as a valid model." (3.2. Line ratio modelling); "Moreover, the model predictions for the physical conditions of these two galaxy components are sufficiently separate by 2σ in the _n_ _H_ 2 - _T_ kin plane." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- The ALMA 12CO(1-0) interferometric observations do not filter out extended flux compared to the NRO 45-m single-dish observations, ruling out missing interferometric flux as the cause of the low gas-to-dust ratio.  
  > "It would imply that the ALMA interferometric observations do not filter out flux from an extended component of this system." (3. Results); "Consequently, missing [12] CO(1-0) flux in ALMA observations is not the main reason for the low gas-to-dust ratio of MACS1931." (3. Results)
- The molecular gas in the MACS1931 BCG is highly excited, comparable to local ULIRGs, whereas the CGM tail gas has a different CO SLED and is likely colder, less dense, and less bound.  
  > "Our study shows that the gas in the BCG is highly excited, comparable to the gas in local ultra luminous infrared galaxies (ULIRGs), while the CGM is likely less excited, colder, less dense, and less bound compared to the ISM of the BCG." (abstract); "Our study shows that the gas in the BCG is highly excited, comparable to the gas in local ultra luminous infrared galaxies (ULIRGs), while the CGM is likely less excited, colder, less dense, and less bound" (abstract)
- Part of the molecular gas in the MACS1931 system is CO-poor (or CO-dark) and traced by [CI](2-1), yielding a ~10 times higher gas-to-dust ratio that alleviates tension with Galactic values.  
  > "suggesting that part of the molecular gas in this system might be CO-poor and the gas-to-dust ratio might be higher than the value calculated previously" (5. Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Single-dish NRO 45-m CO(1-0) observations were obtained to test whether ALMA filtered out extended emission, while APEX high-frequency CO(7-6) and [CI](2-1) observations were obtained to probe high-J gas excitation and trace potentially CO-poor H2 gas alongside archival ALMA CO(1-0), CO(3-2), and CO(4-3) data.  
  > "To investigate this matter further, we obtained additional single-dish 12 CO(1-0) observations to be able to recover the total flux, which might be filtered out in interferometric observations, along with high-frequency CO and CI observations in order to probe the gas physical conditions." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Used Lorentzian line profile fits in addition to Gaussian fits for direct comparison with the Lorentzian fluxes reported by Fogarty et al. (2019); used non-LTE LVG radiative transfer code MyRadex (with LSODE solver) to avoid LTE biases and RADEX convergence issues while adding [CI](2-1) as a constraint.  
  > "We employed the Lorentzian fits because in the following we compare the fluxes extracted from these singledish observations with those reported by Fogarty et al. (2019) using a Lorentzian fitting function." (3. Results); "MyRadex is a revised version of RADEX (van der Tak et al. 2007), that fits the CO SLED with a large velocity gradient (LVG) model and adds as an additional constraint the CI flux value (Papadopoulos et al. 2018). It solves the statistical equilibrium problem with the LSODE [6] solver in ODEPACK [7] and avoids unconverged solutions under some parameters compared to the original RADEX." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)
- Alternative: High-temperature (>1000 K) diffuse LVG solution for the BCG was rejected as physically unrealistic for BCG molecular gas.  
  > "It is noteworthy that the top panel of Figure 3 shows two possible solutions for the BCG (white contours), one colder and denser and one hotter and more diffuse with lower probability. However, a temperature of more than 1000 K is not physical for the BCG gas. Thus, we do not consider the second solution as a valid model." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)
- Alternative: APEX data taken in April 2023 were omitted from the analysis due to not meeting quality standards.  
  > "The data taken in April 2023 did not meet good quality standards and were omitted from the analysis." (2.1. APEX observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single-dish observations lack the spatial resolution to resolve the BCG from the CGM tail, requiring fixed kinematic priors from ALMA and a point-source approximation at 4'' offset to correct for beam response.  
  > "These single-dish observations do not have the required spatial resolution to distinguish between the BCG and the tail of MACS1931 and we use the ALMA observations of Fogarty et al. (2019) as a prior to understand the molecular gas geometry and kinematics." (3. Results); "The exact extent of the tail-like CGM component is not measurable and the emission is not spatially resolved in these single-dish observations." (3. Results)
- Limitation (authors): Single-dish detections have low S/N (e.g. S/N = 1.2 for the tail [CI](2-1) component and S/N = 2.4 for BCG CO(7-6)) due to atmospheric absorption at ~595-600 GHz (APEX) and low elevation ~30 deg (NRO 45-m).  
  > "The source was detected in each observing run, however, it was heavily affected by the atmospheric absorption with an RMS noise of 2.6 mJy per 50 km s [−][1] channel." (2.2. NRO 45 -m dish observations); "Despite the atmospheric noise affecting the APEX high-frequency data and the NRO 45-m low elevation data of MACS1931, the lines are detected, albeit at low statistical significance." (3. Results)
- Limitation (authors): The archival ALMA Band 7 spectrum of 12CO(4-3) for the CGM tail is partially truncated by a spectral window gap around 300-500 km s^-1.  
  > "It is noteworthy that the ALMA Band 7 spectrum of MACS1931 is truncated around the relative velocity of 300 − 500 kms [−][1] for the [12] CO(4-3) line, and the reported line flux in Fogarty et al. (2019) is based on the best fitted Lorentzian function on this partially obscured spectrum." (3.1. Spectral line energy distribution and profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of [CI](1-0) and high-J CO (e.g., CO(5-4)) detections for the tail leaves degeneracies in the n_H2-T_kin parameter space and leaves K_vir poorly constrained.  
  > "This degeneracy in the [ _n_ _H_ 2 - _T_ kin ] space is inherent to this kind of models and can only be broken with additional independent observations, as the ratio of the two atomic carbon lines, [CI](1-0)/[CI](2-1) and a better sampling of the CO ladder." (3.2. Line ratio modelling); "However, K vir is not well constrained for any of the galaxy components." (3.2. Line ratio modelling)
- Limitation (authors): The [CI](2-1)-based H2 mass depends strongly on the uncertain Q_21 excitation factor and assumed carbon abundance X_CI.  
  > "However, a larger uncertainty lies within the _Q_ 21 factor and the final result significantly depends on this factor, specifically for the tail." (4.2. Molecular hydrogen mass inferred from [CI](2-1))
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CGM tail [CI](2-1) detection has S/N = 1.2 (Table 1), which is marginal; the large derived CGM tail H2 mass (13.4 +/- 9.6 x 10^10 M_sun) rests heavily on this 1.2-sigma feature combined with a very small excitation factor Q_21 = 0.03 +/- 0.02.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the BCG, the non-beam-corrected [CI](2-1) flux (41.6 Jy km s^-1) appears to be used in Equation 4, whereas for the tail it is not explicitly stated whether the uncorrected (8.4 Jy km s^-1) or beam-corrected (14.2 Jy km s^-1) flux is used (plugging numbers into Eq. 4 shows 8.4 Jy km s^-1 gives ~13.4 x 10^10 M_sun).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Values reported in the main text for the best-fit parameters (e.g., T_kin = 22.6 K for BCG, 15.3 K for tail) differ slightly from the 50th percentile values shown in the Figure A.1 corner plot headers (log T_kin = 1.33 -> 21.4 K for BCG; log T_kin = 1.04 -> 11.0 K for tail), likely reflecting maximum-likelihood grid points vs. marginalized MCMC medians.
