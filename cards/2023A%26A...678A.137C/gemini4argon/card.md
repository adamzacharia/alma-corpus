## Analysis recipe

1. **Generate moment 0 maps and extract single-pixel spectra**: Make integrated intensity (moment 0) maps of the unblended CH3OH 19_{3,17}-19_{2,18} line and extract spectra from the peak methanol pixel (or brightest pixel on the ring for G34.30+0.20, or offset by ~9 pixels for G34.41+0.24 and ~3 pixels for G23.21-0.37 to reduce line blending)  
  > "Figure [1](#fig:maps) shows the continuum maps and the integrated intensity (moment 0) maps of the 19$_{3,17}$–19$_{2,18}$ transition with upper energy $E_\mathrm{up}$ = 490 K and Einstein $A$ coefficient $A_\mathrm{ij} = 9.27\times10^{-5}$ s$^{-1}$." (Spectral analyses); "The spectra are extracted from the peak pixel of the methanol intensity maps." (Spectral analyses); "For the two bright sources G34.41+0.24 and G23.21-0.37, the methanol emission peaks at the same location as the continuum, but the spectrum extracted from the central pixel has too much line blending, so we deliberately chose a pixel offset from the actual peak ($\sim$9 pixels offset for G34.41 and $\sim$3 pixels offset for G23.21)." (Spectral analyses)
   - parameter: CH3OH 19_{3,17}-19_{2,18} transition (E_up = 490 K, A_ij = 9.27e-5 s^-1); extraction coordinates in Table 1  
  > "Figure [1](#fig:maps) shows the continuum maps and the integrated intensity (moment 0) maps of the 19$_{3,17}$–19$_{2,18}$ transition with upper energy $E_\mathrm{up}$ = 490 K and Einstein $A$ coefficient $A_\mathrm{ij} = 9.27\times10^{-5}$ s$^{-1}$." (Spectral analyses)
   - assumption: Other O-COMs peak at similar locations to methanol emission  
  > "The moment 0 maps of other selected O-COMs of one example source G19.88-0.53 are shown in Fig. [7](#fig:OCOM_mom0_G19.88), which confirmed that their emission peaks at similar regions to the methanol emission." (Spectral analyses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirmed co-spatial emission using moment 0 maps of other O-COMs in G19.88-0.53  
  > "The moment 0 maps of other selected O-COMs of one example source G19.88-0.53 are shown in Fig. [7](#fig:OCOM_mom0_G19.88), which confirmed that their emission peaks at similar regions to the methanol emission." (Spectral analyses)
2. **Identify lines and fit LTE synthetic spectra for O-COMs and methanol isotopologues**: Identify transitions using JPL and CDMS catalogs in CASSIS and fit LTE models (N, T_ex, FWHM, v_lsr) per species using chi^2 grid-fitting on unblended lines for narrow/weak sources or interactive visual inspection for blended/bright sources; use 2-component fits where lines are double-peaked and restrict MF fits to A_ij < 10^-4 s^-1 where optically thick  
  > "After extracting all the spectra, we performed line identification and spectral fitting using the spectral analysis software CASSIS[^2] (cite: Vastel2015CASSIS)." (Spectral analyses); "We adopted two methods for fitting the spectra: $\chi^2$ minimization or visual inspection when the former is not applicable." (Spectral analyses); "In this case, the fitting was only based on weak lines with $A_\mathrm{ij} < 10^{-4}$ s$^{-1}$." (Spectral analyses)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: 3-sigma detection threshold for unblended lines; T_ex fixed to MF when unconstrained; T_ex of g-EG fixed to a-EG  
  > "The identified transitions of the selected O-COMs are listed in Table [1](#table:transition), where transitions that are above 3$\sigma$ and not fully blended with other strong lines are considered as “identified.”" (Spectra and detection); "As a solution, $T_\mathrm{ex}$ was fixed to that of MF, which has the most identified transitions and therefore the best constraint on $T_\mathrm{ex}$, and only $N$ was fitted and estimated for uncertainties." (Spectral analyses); "Besides, the $T_\mathrm{ex}$ of $g$-EG is always set as the same as that of $a$-EG." (Spectral analyses)
   - assumption: Single excitation temperature (LTE) per species; vibrational corrections applied to GA and EG  
  > "Here we assume one $T_\mathrm{ex}$ for each species, that is, the populations of all levels can be characterized by a single $T_\mathrm{ex}$, which is often called “local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)” in radio astronomy." (Spectral analyses); "Vibrational corrections were applied to GA and EG (cite: Jorgensen2016)." (Spectral analyses)
   - fitting: Chi^2 minimization on a coarse-to-fine grid in (N, T_ex, FWHM) with 2-sigma uncertainty contours, or interactive visual inspection with upper/lower limit comparisons  
  > "Finally, we ended up with a fine grid around the best-fit grid point, from which we could make the contour plot and estimate the 2$\sigma$ uncertainties." (Spectral analyses); "When it came to fitting by visual inspection, we started with an initial guess of the parameters and adjusted them to a better fit until no improvement could be made." (Spectral analyses)
   - validation: More than 70% of line features in G19.88-0.53 are fitted well with uncertainties < 30%; changing fixed T_ex by 20-50 K changes N within a factor of two  
  > "More than 70% of the line features can be fitted quite well with uncertainties of \< 30% ." (Spectra and detection); "We consider the fitting results of $N$ still representative since the difference would be within a factor of two if we change the fixed value of $T_\mathrm{ex}$ by 20–50 K in the range of 100–250 K" (Spectral analyses)
3. **Infer main methanol column density and compute O-COM column density ratios**: Calculate 16O/18O ratio from Galactocentric distance D_GC for each source, multiply N(CH3^18OH) by 16O/18O to get N(CH3OH), sum a-EG and g-EG, and compute ratios N(O-COM)/N(CH3OH) and pairwise O-COM ratios to compare with low-mass protostars, comets, and Garrod et al. (2022) models  
  > "The isotope ratios of $\mathrm{^{16}O/^{18}O}$ and $\mathrm{^{12}C/^{13}C}$ can be calculated from the distance to the Galactic center ($D_\mathrm{GC}$) using the equations in (cite: Wilson1994ARAA) and (cite: Milam2005)" (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol); "Therefore, only CH$_3^{18}$OH is used to calculate the column density of methanol." (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol); "The column densities of the two conformers of EG, $a$-EG and $g$-EG, are summed up in the last subplot, although they were fitted separately." (Column density ratios)
   - parameter: (16O/18O) = (58.8 +/- 11.8) D_GC + (37.1 +/- 82.6); intercept error excluded from main error propagation  
  > "(\mathrm{^{16}O/^{18}O}) & = & (58.8\pm11.8)D_\text{GC} + (37.1\pm82.6)" (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol); "Considering that only the slope in Eq. \[\[eqn:18O_ratio\]\](#eqn:18O_ratio) contains the information of the trend between the $\mathrm{^{16}O/^{18}O}$ ratio and $D_\mathrm{GC}$, and the intercept error only represents the scatter of the sources from which the equation was originally fitted, we did not include the intercept error in the error propagation." (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol)
   - assumption: CH3^18OH lines are optically thin while 13CH3OH lines are marginally optically thick  
  > "If both $^{13}$ and CH$_3^{18}$OH are optically thin, we can expect their column density ratio to be 7–8. However, our fitting results show a ratio of 2–5, which indicates that $^{13}$ is also (marginally) optically thick." (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared results with and without the 16O/18O relation intercept error in Fig. 14  
  > "Results that include the intercept error are shown in Fig. [14](#fig:O-COM ratio full error)." (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol)

- Average column densities of the six O-COMs rank as DME ~ MF > C2H5OH > (a+g)EG > CH3CHO > GA.

  > "The column density ratios of the six selected O-COMs with respect to methanol show no clear difference between the five low-mass studied previously and the 14 high-mass protostellar objects observed with ALMA, suggesting that these species are mainly formed under similar conditions." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 6 observations at 0.3'' resolution of 14 high-mass hot cores within 1-4 kpc provide the spatial resolution and sensitivity needed to detect optically thin CH3^18OH lines and multiple O-COM transitions across a diverse sample.  
  > "We emphasize the importance of detecting optically thin lines of minor isotopologues of methanol, which requires higher sensitivity, in order to better constrain the column density of methanol and hence the ratios with respect to other O-COMs." (Introduction); "These frequency ranges were chosen to maximize both the number of transitions and the range of upper-state rotational energy levels of key target molecules including those highlighted here." (Overview)
- Why this method: Using CH3^18OH avoids optical depth saturation of main CH3OH and 13CH3OH; combining chi^2 grid-fitting for clean spectra with interactive visual inspection for blended spectra handles diverse line widths across the sample.  
  > "If both $^{13}$ and CH$_3^{18}$OH are optically thin, we can expect their column density ratio to be 7–8. However, our fitting results show a ratio of 2–5, which indicates that $^{13}$ is also (marginally) optically thick. Therefore, only CH$_3^{18}$OH is used to calculate the column density of methanol." (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol); "When it came to fitting by visual inspection, we started with an initial guess of the parameters and adjusted them to a better fit until no improvement could be made. This is more efficient and reliable for complex spectra with blended lines, since we can monitor the change intuitively and interactively." (Spectral analyses)
- Alternative: Using 13CH3OH to infer methanol column density was rejected because the 13CH3OH/CH3^18OH ratio is 2-5 instead of 7-8, showing 13CH3OH is marginally optically thick.  
  > "If both $^{13}$ and CH$_3^{18}$OH are optically thin, we can expect their column density ratio to be 7–8. However, our fitting results show a ratio of 2–5, which indicates that $^{13}$ is also (marginally) optically thick. Therefore, only CH$_3^{18}$OH is used to calculate the column density of methanol." (Isotope ratio calibration for methanol)
- Alternative: Extracting spectra at the continuum/methanol peak was rejected for G34.41+0.24 and G23.21-0.37 due to severe line blending, and for G34.30+0.20 due to dust optical depth creating a ring morphology.  
  > "However, in some bright sources such as G34.30+0.20 (Fig. [1](#fig:maps)), the methanol emission shows a ring shape around the continuum peak, which is likely due to the high optical depth of dust (cite: DeSiomone2020; MvG2022). In this case, we picked the brightest pixel on the ring to extract the spectra." (Spectral analyses); "For the two bright sources G34.41+0.24 and G23.21-0.37, the methanol emission peaks at the same location as the continuum, but the spectrum extracted from the central pixel has too much line blending, so we deliberately chose a pixel offset from the actual peak ($\sim$9 pixels offset for G34.41 and $\sim$3 pixels offset for G23.21)." (Spectral analyses)
- How they decided it was enough: The upper tuning alone covers ~70% of the COM lines found in both tunings, making results from the 14 completed sources in the upper tuning robust without waiting for the lower tuning reduction.  
  > "The upper tuning covers about 70% of the COM lines found in both tunings (not including ), while the lower one is more abundant in strong lines of and CH$_3$CN, which are not directly relevant to our project. Therefore, we consider our results to be robust using only the data in the upper tuning." (Data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Emitting regions of O-COMs are barely or not spatially resolved at 0.3'' resolution, causing species- and source-dependent beam dilution.  
  > "This is likely the case in the CoCCoA observations since the moment 0 maps of selected O-COMs given in Fig. [7](#fig:OCOM_mom0_G19.88) show that the emission of these molecules are barely or not spatially resolved." (Column density ratios)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperatures of CH3^18OH, GA, and g-EG could not be independently constrained in most sources due to few unblended lines across E_up, requiring T_ex to be fixed.  
  > "For species such as CH$_3^{18}$OH, GA, and $g$-EG, there are few unblended lines covering a wide range of $E_\mathrm{up}$." (Spectral analyses); "As a solution, $T_\mathrm{ex}$ was fixed to that of MF, which has the most identified transitions and therefore the best constraint on $T_\mathrm{ex}$, and only $N$ was fitted and estimated for uncertainties." (Spectral analyses)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size for comparing O-COMs across evolutionary stages (protostars, disks, comets) is too small for robust statistical conclusions.  
  > "However, the sample here is too small to draw any robust conclusion. A larger sample of sources at different evolutionary stages are needed to study the chemical inheritance throughout star formation." (O-COMs from clouds to comets)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual inspection fitting for bright blended sources is subjective and harder to reproduce identically than automated chi^2 minimization.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing T_ex of CH3^18OH to that of MF (120-200 K) assumes co-spatial emission and identical excitation, whereas CH3CHO and DME show lower T_ex (100-130 K).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Offsetting extraction pixels in G34.41+0.24 and G23.21-0.37 probes different physical radii than the peak-pixel extractions used for the rest of the sample.
