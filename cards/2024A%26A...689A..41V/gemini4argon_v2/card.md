## Analysis recipe

1. **Mitigate spectral line and compact continuum source contamination**: Identify bright spectral lines in Band 3 and Band 4 images and remove the higher-frequency half of the corresponding spectral windows; bin remaining frequencies into a single frequency bin; jointly model and subtract the off-center dusty galaxy point source using ALMA Band 3 and Band 4 visibilities; perform a blind point-source search with a lower uv-cut of 11 klambda  
  > "Hence, we removed the higher-frequency half of the corresponding spectral window before continuum modeling the pressure distribution of the ICM." (Mitigating point-source contamination in the ACA and ALMA observations); "Thus, we utilize Band 4 data to broaden the spectral coverage and remove the dust-continuum emission originating from this interfering source. We did this by modeling the point source using both ALMA Band 3 and 4 observations." (Mitigating point-source contamination in the ACA and ALMA observations); "Finally, we ran a point source search with our forward modeling routine using both the ALMA+ACA Band 3 and ALMA Band 4 observations with a lower *uv*-cut of 11 k$\lambda$ to exclude extended emission." (Mitigating point-source contamination in the ACA and ALMA observations)
   - software: dynesty
   - parameter: Lower uv-cut of 11 klambda for point source search; 7 parameters for point source (RA, Dec, Amplitude, spectral slope, and 3 calibration uncertainty parameters)  
  > "Finally, we ran a point source search with our forward modeling routine using both the ALMA+ACA Band 3 and ALMA Band 4 observations with a lower *uv*-cut of 11 k$\lambda$ to exclude extended emission." (Mitigating point-source contamination in the ACA and ALMA observations); "We model any possible emission features with a point source which is described by ~~7~~ parameters, namely: RA, Dec, Amplitude, spectral slope, and the three calibration uncertainty parameters of the observations." (Mitigating point-source contamination in the ACA and ALMA observations)
   - assumption: Unresolved compact sources follow a Dirac delta spatial profile with a first-order polynomial spectrum  
  > "Unresolved, compact sources are modeled using point-like (Dirac delta) emission models with a first-order polynomial to describe their spectral behavior." (Interloper and cluster member removal)
   - fitting: Bayesian forward modeling with static nested sampling in dynesty; priors in Table 1  
  > "The implementation uses the static nested sampling method implemented in the `dynesty` package (cite: Speagle2020Dynesty; sergey_koposov_2023_7600689)." (Visibility-based modeling of the ICM)
   - validation: Tested adding a point source frozen at the BCG location and rejected its presence at 4.1 sigma via the Bayes factor  
  > "Through the Bayes factor, we can reject the presence of a point source at the location of the BCG in the ALMA+ACA data by $4.1\sigma$." (The theoretical formalism)
2. **Single-component Bayesian forward modeling of the ICM pressure profile**: Map 3D parametric pressure profiles (theoretical gNFW and empirical A10/M14 CC, MD, UPP templates, both spherical and elliptical) onto a 3D grid, integrate along the line of sight to get Compton y, convert to surface brightness per band, Fourier transform to uv-space with primary beam attenuation per pointing, and evaluate Gaussian log-likelihoods on ALMA+ACA visibilities alone and jointly with ACT DR6 100 and 150 GHz maps  
  > "In brief, the routine flows as follows: (1) From a parametric description of the pressure distribution, we map the ICM on a three-dimensional grid which is then integrated along the line of sight to produce a projected Compton $y$ map" (Methodology); "Regarding handling the ALMA+ACA mosaic, we model each pointing individually and correct for the primary beam attenuation per field. The fields are combined at the likelihood level of our routine, adopting a Gaussian likelihood." (Visibility-based modeling of the ICM); "We treat the ACT and ACA+ALMA observations as independent when computing the final posterior distribution of the model parameters. This is done by adding the log-likelihoods linearly." (Joint likelihood modeling of the ICM via ALMA, ACA, and ACT observations)
   - software: dynesty
   - parameter: Stopping criterion dlnB = 0.01; shape parameters alpha, beta, gamma frozen or thawed per Table 1 and Table 2  
  > "Sampling of the posterior distribution continues until the log difference of the Bayesian evidence is less than an arbitrary threshold, in our case set to $\Delta\ln\mathcal{B}=0.01$." (Joint likelihood modeling of the ICM via ALMA, ACA, and ACT observations)
   - assumption: Flat LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 and Omega_m = 0.307 (1'' = 7.855 kpc at z=1.98); non-relativistic thermal SZ spectrum with T_CMB = 2.7255 K; zero covariance between ACT and ALMA+ACA; stationary ACT noise/signal covariance estimated from 8 adjacent patches  
  > "For all calculations, the assumed cosmology is based on (cite: Planck2014_XIII), a spatially flat, $\Lambda$ cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) model with $H_0=67.7$ kms$^{-1}$Mpc$^{-1}$ and $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.307$. Here $1\ensuremath{''} = 7.855$ kpc for XLSSC 122 ($z=1.98$)." (Introduction); "As we do not expect the electron temperature in XLSSC 122 to exceed 5 keV ~~ (cite: Mantz2018)~~ , we have neglected the temperature-dependent relativistic corrections of the thermal SZ in this work." (The thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect); "Hence, we assume no covariance between the ACA+ALMA observations and the ACT maps." (Joint likelihood modeling of the ICM via ALMA, ACA, and ACT observations)
   - fitting: Static nested sampling in dynesty with Gaussian likelihoods in uv/Fourier space; Bayesian evidence integrated via trapezoidal rule and compared to null model via Bayes factor  
  > "Hence, we compute the Bayesian evidence with a simple quadratic integration scheme using trapezoids over the initial samples (cite: Speagle2020Dynesty), thus enabling a tool for fair model comparison." (Joint likelihood modeling of the ICM via ALMA, ACA, and ACT observations); "In this work, we normalize the Bayesian evidence by the evidence of a null model to compute the Bayes factor $\mathcal{Z}$." (Joint likelihood modeling of the ICM via ALMA, ACA, and ACT observations)
   - validation: Inspected image-plane and radially binned uv-plane residuals after subtracting best-fit models from visibilities  
  > "The residuals in Figure [1](#fig:best_image) are obtained by subtracting the model in the visibility plane from the data." (The theoretical formalism); "Figure [12](#fig:2comp_model-res)  shows the residuals in the *uv*-plane between the data, shown in Fig [2](#fig:uv-radial), and the likelihood weighted reconstruction of the spherical and elliptical gNFW models" (Residuals)
3. **Deconvolved image reconstruction and two-component ICM modeling**: Construct a 'cleaned' image by subtracting the best-fit gNFW model from the visibilities and adding the imaged residuals to the synthesized-beam-smoothed (5''), primary-beam-attenuated likelihood-weighted model; then fit a two-component gNFW model (spherical and elliptical, shape parameters frozen to A10-UPP) in uv-space to ALMA+ACA and ALMA+ACA+ACT data  
  > "Then, similar to clean, we add the imaged residuals (shown on the right panel of Fig. [1](#fig:best_image)) to the likelihood-weighted model, which is smoothed with the synthesized beam ($5\ensuremath{''}$) and attenuated by the primary beam, to create the image reconstruction." (“Cleaned” image reconstruction); "After obtaining the ~~“cleaned ”~~ interferometric image, we ran a two-component model to confirm via *uv*-based modeling that these filamentary-like structures are not an imaging artifact." (Modeling asymmetric pressure distributions)
   - software: dynesty
   - parameter: Shape parameters frozen to A10-UPP; ordered prior on the declinations of the two component centroids  
  > "The shape parameters of the gNFW are frozen to the A10-UPP values, similar to what is done in Section [0.1.1](#sec:gnfw_single). To let each run converge to a single solution, we used an ordered prior on the declinations of the centroids of the two SZ components" (Modeling asymmetric pressure distributions)
   - assumption: Both components follow gNFW pressure profiles with A10-UPP shape parameters  
  > "The shape parameters of the gNFW are frozen to the A10-UPP values, similar to what is done in Section [0.1.1](#sec:gnfw_single)." (Modeling asymmetric pressure distributions)
   - fitting: Static nested sampling in dynesty; model comparison via Bayes factor relative to single-component gNFW fit; likelihood-weighted posterior marginalization for model images  
  > "For the elliptical one, we find $|\Delta\ln{\mathcal{Z}}| = 68.6$. The latter translates to a Bayesian evidence difference of 6.6 with respect to the best single elliptical gNFW model, indicating a tentative $3.6\sigma$ detection of the second component" (Modeling asymmetric pressure distributions)
   - validation: Compared marginalized two-component model images against contours of the cleaned image reconstruction and examined uv-radial residuals  
  > "By marginalizing over the posterior distribution, we get a smoother model which closely resembles the ~~“cleaned ”~~ image, as indicated in Figure [8](#fig:2comp_modeling) where we plot the contours from the cleaned image reconstruction" (Modeling asymmetric pressure distributions)
4. **Derive SZ, X-ray, and dynamical halo masses M_500,c**: Compute the evidence-weighted average of M_500,c across the empirical formalism ALMA+ACA+ACT runs; derive an independent X-ray M_500,c from kT = 5.0 +/- 0.7 keV via the Vikhlinin et al. (2009) M_500,c - T_X relation; compute the biweight velocity dispersion from 32 'Gold' cluster members within r_200,c and convert to M_500,c^dyn using the Munari et al. (2013) scaling relation and colossus

## Qualitative findings

- XLSSC 122 has a flattened inner pressure profile consistent with a local non-cool-core (morphologically disturbed) cluster classification (or a high-z relaxed profile from McDonald et al. 2014), disfavoring a local cool-core profile at >=3 sigma.  
  > "We detect the SZ effect with $11\sigma$ significance in the ALMA+ACA observations and find a flattened inner pressure profile that is consistent with a ~~non-cool~~ core classification with a significance of $\geq 3\sigma$." (abstract); "Based on its radial pressure distribution, XLSSC 122 is classified as a ~~non-cool-core~~ when compared with local observations (cite: Arnaud2010)." (Conclusions)
- The ALMA+ACA SZ centroid agrees much better with the X-ray peak and BCG than earlier CARMA measurements (offset of 10'' +/- 1'' southward rather than 35'' +/- 8'').  
  > "In contrast to the previous works, we find better agreement between the SZ effect signal and the X-ray emission as well as the cluster member distribution." (abstract); "Second, the peak of the pressure distribution, as mapped by the SZ signal, is offset from the BCG and the peak of the X-ray emission by $10\ensuremath{''}\pm 1\ensuremath{''}$ ($80\pm7$ kpc) when projected on the sky mainly along declination axis." (Morphological implications)
- There is an excess of SZ flux and a ~3x pseudo-temperature enhancement in the south/southeast where no X-ray emission is detected, tentatively attributed to an infalling group or filamentary structure shock-heating low-density ICM gas.  
  > "Further, XLSSC 122 exhibits an excess of SZ flux in the south of the cluster where no X-ray emission is detected." (abstract); "Figure [11](#fig:ratio) shows this ratio scaled to arbitrary units and indicates that XLSSC 122 is far from isothermal, with a pseudo temperature increase of $\sim3\times$ in the south, indicative of a disturbance." (Thermodynamical implications)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA and ACA Band 3 provide sub-arcminute (2'' to 11'') resolution to map ICM morphology and remove compact source contamination, ACT DR6 100/150 GHz single-dish maps constrain large angular scales (>1') and total Compton Y filtered out by the interferometer, and ALMA Band 4 provides spectral leverage to subtract dusty galaxy continuum contamination.  
  > "In this paper, we treat this issue by combining archival SZ observations from both the main ~~12-m array (ALMA~~ ) ~~and the 7-m Atacama Compact Array (ACA )~~ with single-dish data from ACT. This comprehensive approach enables us to probe the broad range of spatial scales needed to provide the first detailed, sub-arcminute view of the ICM" (Introduction); "Furthermore, we make use of ancillary ALMA Band 4 observations (PI: J. Zavala, 2018.1.00478.S) to get better spectral constraints on the contaminating emission from dusty galaxies." (The Atacama Large Millimeter/Submillimeter Array)
- Why this method: Forward modeling directly in the native uv-plane avoids deconvolution artifacts, correlated image-space noise, and spatial filtering biases; standard CLEAN is unsuitable because the SZ signal is concentrated at short baselines rather than point-like or multi-scale Gaussian sources.  
  > "Modeling in the *uv*-plane avoids issues related to the deconvolution of interferometric data (e.g. heavily correlated image-space noise, the filtering of large spatial scales, and the ~~non-uniform~~ weighting of the signal across the baselines)" (Visibility-based modeling of the ICM); "However, this routine assumes that the emission distribution is well described by an arbitrary set of point-like or ~~multi-scale~~ Gaussian sources. Figure [2](#fig:uv-radial) directly shows that this assumption is invalid in our case" (“Cleaned” image reconstruction)
- Alternative: Standard CLEAN deconvolution algorithm (rejected for ICM reconstruction because SZ emission is concentrated at small uv-distances rather than point-like or multi-scale Gaussian components)  
  > "The use of the clean algorithm has been the de facto standard in radio astronomy for half a century ~~ (cite: Hogbom1974)~~ . However, this routine assumes that the emission distribution is well described by an arbitrary set of point-like or ~~multi-scale~~ Gaussian sources." (“Cleaned” image reconstruction)
- Alternative: Chandra X-ray data for diffuse ICM study (rejected in favor of XMM-Newton because Chandra collected only ~200 source counts in 182.2 ksec, or <=4 photons per hour)  
  > "However, the combined *Chandra* data translates to $\lesssim 4$ source photons per hour of observation time, which is not uncommon in high-redshift observations of clusters of galaxies in the X-ray regime." (Auxiliary Data Comparison); "Therefore, to study the diffuse ICM in XLSSC 122 from the X-ray perspective, we henceforth only rely on the *XMM-Newton* observations." (Auxiliary Data Comparison)
- Alternative: Point-source emission from the BCG explaining the flat inner SZ profile (tested and rejected at 4.1 sigma via Bayes factor)  
  > "Through the Bayes factor, we can reject the presence of a point source at the location of the BCG in the ALMA+ACA data by $4.1\sigma$." (The theoretical formalism)
- Alternative: Elliptical gNFW models with thawed (gamma, beta) or (gamma, beta, alpha) in ALMA+ACA+ACT fits (rejected due to convergence to unphysical solutions with e > 0.9 and beta < 2)  
  > "Regarding the modeling based on the theoretical formalism, the first point to note is that the two gNFW runs in which we additionally unfroze the parameters $\gamma$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$, $\beta$, and $\alpha$ for elliptical morphologies both resulted in highly unphysical solutions for the best-fit model parameters." (ALMA+ACA+ACT joint likelihood modeling)
- How they decided it was enough: Nested sampling in dynesty was run until the log difference in Bayesian evidence reached dlnB = 0.01, and models were selected/compared using the Bayes factor relative to a null model.  
  > "Sampling of the posterior distribution continues until the log difference of the Bayesian evidence is less than an arbitrary threshold, in our case set to $\Delta\ln\mathcal{B}=0.01$." (Joint likelihood modeling of the ICM via ALMA, ACA, and ACT observations)
- Limitation (authors): Low SNR on intermediate spatial scales (3-10 klambda) and parameter degeneracies prevent distinguishing between local morphologically disturbed (A10-MD) and high-z relaxed (M14-UPP/CC) pressure profile templates.  
  > "Furthermore, we still cannot significantly differentiate between the several profile classifications by adding ACT data. This is because of the high-dimensional parameter space, the complex degeneracies, the relatively low ~~SNR~~ in the spatial scales $3-10~\mathrm{k}\lambda$ of the ACA and ALMA observations, and the similarity between the different profiles." (ALMA+ACA+ACT joint likelihood modeling)
- Limitation (authors): An elliptical gNFW profile is not an ideal physical model for elongated filamentary structures, leading to extreme eccentricities (e ~ 0.8) for the secondary component.  
  > "Regarding the eccentricity, the modeling done on both the ACA+ALMA and ACA+ALMA+ACT observations imply an eccentricity for the smaller component of $\sim0.8$. This is extreme; however, the cause is most likely because of the model choice." (Modeling asymmetric pressure distributions); "We are aware that this specific model is not ideal for modeling filamentary-like structures, but it is sufficient to probe the significance of this feature in the native, *uv*-space of ACA and ALMA" (Morphological implications)
- Limitation (authors): SZ halo mass estimates rely on local (z < 0.3) scaling relations from Arnaud et al. (2010) and are subject to hydrostatic mass bias.  
  > "Here, we make use of local scaling relations from (cite: Arnaud2010) to convert $Y_{\rm 500,c}$ to an SZ-derived halo mass, as robust scalings derived from high$-z$ clusters do not exist." (Mass estimates); "Furthermore, our analysis leads to an improved precision of the SZ mass estimate to $M_{500,\mathrm{c}} = 1.66^{+0.23}_{-0.20} \times 10^{14}~\rm M_\odot$, though we note the overall accuracy could still be affected by the hydrostatic mass biases common to SZ mass estimates." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line-of-sight geometry is fixed by assuming the cluster extent along the line of sight is not compressed when squeezing the 3D grid to model projected eccentricity, introducing a direct degeneracy between projection angle, eccentricity, and integrated Compton Y.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ACT DR6 SNR map contours used in Figure 1 are cited as 'Hilton et al. (in prep.)', and the exact sky patches used for the ACT empirical covariance matrix are not specified.
