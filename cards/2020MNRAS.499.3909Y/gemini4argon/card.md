## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate and image ALMA and VLA recombination line cubes without uv-plane continuum subtraction**: Combine or split spectral windows, perform iterative phase (and for Band 6, final amplitude and phase) self-calibration on Sgr A*, and image line cubes directly prior to continuum subtraction.  
  > "The dataset was first combined along all spectral windows prior to deriving and applying phase self-calibration solutions three times with decreasing time intervals before a final phase and amplitude self-calibration solution was derived." (2.1.1 H30alpha ALMA Data); "Continuum subtraction was not performed in the uv plane, instead the image cube was constructed immediately after self-calibration." (2.1.1 H30alpha ALMA Data); "We performed two rounds of phase self-calibration, deriving solutions per spectral window in the first round and then for all spectral windows combined." (2.1.2 H39alpha ALMA Data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: H30a channel width 45 km s^-1 from -3770 to +700 km s^-1; H39a channel width 15.625 MHz; VLA H52a and H56a channel width 2 MHz  
  > "The data cube is made up of 45 km s−1 channels ranging from −3770 to +700 km s−1 over two spectral windows relative to the rest frequency of H30α at 231.9 GHz." (2.1.1 H30alpha ALMA Data); "The calibrated dataset provided by the ALMA archive consists of 4 spectral windows, each with 2 GHz bandwidth and 15.625 MHz channel width." (2.1.2 H39alpha ALMA Data); "The broad 8 GHz bandwidth is comprised of 64 spws, each having 64 channels of 2 MHz width." (2.1.3 H52alpha VLA Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectra toward Sgr A*, equalize flux across spectral windows, and subtract continuum in the image plane**: Extract spectrum in aperture centered on Sgr A*, equalize adjacent spectral windows by taking the ratio of mean fluxes in edge channels (5 channels for ALMA H30a, 10 channels for VLA H52a/H56a) and scaling, concatenate spws, and subtract a linear continuum fit over selected line-free velocity intervals.  
  > "In order to equalize the flux between the two spectral windows that are used here, the five channels on the edge of each window were averaged to determine the relative difference." (2.1.1 H30alpha ALMA Data); "(1) determine the ratio of the mean flux of the last ten channels of spectral window j with the mean flux of the first ten channels of the next (higher frequency) spectral window j + 1" (2.1.3 H52alpha VLA Data); "Channels between velocities of –1300 km s−1 and –1800 km s−1 were used in a linear χ-squared fit in the image plane to subtract the continuum." (2.2.1 H30alpha Line Emission)
   - parameter: Extraction apertures: inner 0.23'' (or 0.46'' diameter) for H30a, inner 0.75'' (or 1.5'') for H39a, 0.20'' x 0.11'' for H52a, 0.22'' x 0.27'' for H56a; scaling factor 1.0052 for H30a lower velocity window, 0.9832 to 1.003 for H52a  
  > "A spectrum (which includes the continuum) was obtained from the inner 0.23′′ towards the position of Sgr A* with a synthesized beam of 0.30′′ × 0.33′′." (2.1.1 H30alpha ALMA Data); "This resulted in the need to multiply the lower velocity window by a factor of 1.0052 before concatenating the spectra together." (2.1.1 H30alpha ALMA Data); "The derived ratios used to normalize the spws ranged from 0.9832 to 1.003." (2.1.3 H52alpha VLA Data)
   - assumption: Selected velocity ranges at band edges are sufficiently free of line emission to define a linear continuum  
  > "Despite having significantly broader spectral coverage than Murchikova et al. (2019), it is still not clear where the true continuum is because of a limited set of line free channels." (2.2 Spectroscopic Results)
   - fitting: Linear chi-squared / least squares straight-line fit to selected line-free channels  
  > "Channels between velocities of –1300 km s−1 and –1800 km s−1 were used in a linear χ-squared fit in the image plane to subtract the continuum." (2.2.1 H30alpha Line Emission); "The overlayed line is the least squares straight-line fit to the continuum in the shaded region." (Figure 1 caption)
   - validation: Confirmed the double-peaked spectrum of Murchikova et al. (2019) at +/- 500 km s^-1 across 4 independent RRL transitions (H30a, H39a, H52a, H56a).  
  > "To confirm the results presented by Murchikova et al. (2019), we used the four independent measurements of H30α, H39α, H52α and H56α with different spectral setups." (2.2 Spectroscopic Results)
3. **Map spatial distribution of extreme velocity (-2500 and +4000 km s^-1) ionized gas**: Select spectral windows containing -2500 and 4000 km s^-1 emission, make moment/average maps of 5 channels near low and high velocity band edges (channels 5-10 and 50-55), and subtract them from each other to form continuum-subtracted line emission contour maps.  
  > "Using two spectral windows, we made moment maps of five channels centered near the low and high velocity channels (channels 5-10 and 50-55), determined the slope between the low and high velocity features for each spectral window before subtracting them from each other." (2.2.3 H52alpha Line Emission); "From these windows five channels were averaged near both the low and high velocity band edges (channels 5-10 and 50-55), before subtracting them from each other." (2.2.4 H56alpha Line Emission)
   - parameter: Channels 5-10 and 50-55 in spectral windows covering -2500 and 4000 km s^-1  
  > "From these windows five channels were averaged near both the low and high velocity band edges (channels 5-10 and 50-55), before subtracting them from each other." (2.2.4 H56alpha Line Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Both H52a and H56a independently show the same diagonal NE-SW spatial orientation matching the +/- 500 km s^-1 H30a orientation.  
  > "These high velocity features show a similar distribution to that found for H52α line emission, i.e., diagonal NE-SW orientation." (2.2.4 H56alpha Line Emission)
4. **Image multi-band VLA radio continuum from 1 to 48 GHz to identify persistent jet and outflow structures**: Self-calibrate VLA A-array broadband visibility data in phase and amplitude on Sgr A*, split bandwidth into multiple channels to account for frequency response, and construct high-dynamic-range continuum images and spectral channel cubes across Q, Ka, Ku, X, and L bands.  
  > "We then combined all 14 useful spectral windows with the task VBGLU in AIPS before we applied additional phase self-calibration." (3.1.2 Broadband Ku band 13–15 GHz); "procedures were applied to the data in OBIT (Cotton 2008) using the bright radio source Sgr A* before MFImager was used to construct a cube of 16 channels corresponding to each spectral window." (3.1.4 Broadband L Band 1.4–2 GHz); "We used OBIT by splitting the 2 GHz bandwidth of 9 GHz data into 46 channels to account for frequency response of Sgr A* in each frequency channel before the final image was made." (Figure 8 caption)
   - software: AIPS, VBGLU, OBIT, MFImager
   - parameter: L-band imaged in 16 channels (1-2 GHz), split into low (1-1.4 GHz) and high (1.5-1.9 GHz) sub-bands; X-band split into 46 channels across 2 GHz; Ka-band split into 24 channels across 8 GHz  
  > "Low frequency (1–1.4 GHz) and high frequency images (1.5–1.9 GHz) are shown in red and blue whereas green shows an image using the entire range (1–1.9 GHz)." (3.2.2 Low Frequency Radio Data); "The 8-GHz bandwidth was imaged in 24 different frequency channels and this map corresponds to channel 16." (Figure 8 caption); "We used OBIT by splitting the 2 GHz bandwidth of 9 GHz data into 46 channels to account for frequency response of Sgr A* in each frequency channel before the final image was made." (Figure 8 caption)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare features across multiple epochs, frequencies, and array configurations to distinguish real persistent structures from phase/amplitude artifacts.  
  > "Given that high frequency features near Sgr A* are contaminated by phase and amplitude errors, we analyze numerous data sets and describe features that persist and appear at different frequencies, epochs, and VLA array configurations, as these are unlikely to be generated by imaging artifacts." (1 INTRODUCTION)
5. **Derive physical properties of the sub-arcsecond ionized gas, pc-scale jet magnetic field, and 3D jet/disk orientation**: Compute volume emission measure (VEM), electron density, and ionized mass of feature J1 from its 36 GHz optically-thin free-free flux density; estimate equipartition magnetic field of the pc-scale jet from its 1.6 GHz surface brightness; infer 3D jet and inner accretion disk orientation angles from PA and radial velocity signs.  
  > "The free-free emissivity at 36 GHz is jν/ne2 ∼ 2.2 × 10−40 erg cm3 Hz−1 sr−1, yielding a flux Sν = jν VEM/d2 ∼ 0.32 VEM/(1058 cm−3) mJy." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites); "we deduce an equipartition magnetic field ∼ 0.2 mG using standard (but uncertain)" (4 DISCUSSION); "Thus the red shifted arm of the jet emerges to the NE of Sgr A* with PA Ωj ≈ 60◦ E of N and makes an angle θj ≈ 45◦ to the line of sight (which is directed away from the observer)." (4.1 Orientation of the Jet)
   - parameter: J1 36 GHz integrated flux ~ 0.2 mJy; J1 dimensions 0.2'' x 0.02'' x 0.02''; jet diameter ~ 2'', 1.6 GHz surface brightness ~ 0.2 mJy beam^-1  
  > "J3 lies in an area confused by extended continuum emission, making it difficult to extract reliable fluxes, so we focus on J1, which has an integrated flux ∼ 0.2 mJy at 36 GHz." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites); "Adopting dimensions 0′′.2 × 0′′.02 × 0′′.02 consistent with Fig. 6(b) this implies ne ≈ 1 × 105 cm−3 and mass ≈ 6 × 10−5 M⊙." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites); "J1, which has an integrated flux ∼ 0.2 mJy at 36 GHz." (4 DISCUSSION)
   - assumption: Galactic center distance d = 8 kpc; nominal electron temperature Te = 10^4 K; optically-thin thermal bremsstrahlung for J1 at 36 GHz  
  > "where a Galactic center distance d=8 kpc has been adopted." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites); "Thus, we infer VEM ≈ 6 × 1057 cm−3 for nominal temperature 104 K." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites)
   - assumption: Equipartition between electrons and protons with an E^-2 electron energy spectrum between 10 MeV and 100 GeV  
  > "electron spectrum running between 10 MeV and 100 GeV and equal energy density in electrons and" (4 DISCUSSION)
   - assumption: Jet emerges perpendicular to the equatorial plane of the inner accretion flow  
  > "The orientation, and mass flow rate of the jet is consistent with jet-launching models, which predict that the jet emerges perpendicular to the equatorial plane of the accretion flow near the event horizon of Sgr A*." (4.1 Orientation of the Jet)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inferred VEM (~6 x 10^57 cm^-3) is consistent with the independent upper limit (< 2 x 10^58 cm^-3) from Br-gamma observations; inferred inner disk inclination and ascending node match one of the GRAVITY infrared flare hotspot orbital solutions.  
  > "This implies that the volume emission measure of the material is ∼< 2 × 1058 cm−3, and that stimulated emission amplifies the H30α line luminosity by at least a factor of 80 (Murchikova et al. 2019)." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites); "Further, astrometric observations of infrared flares with the GRAVITY instrument on board the VLT suggest a hotspot source orbiting Sgr A* with inclination i ≈ 130◦ and Ω most likely to be either ≈ 130◦ – consistent with the clockwise stellar disk, or ≈ 300◦ – consistent with our observed jet orientation (Gravity Collaboration 2020)." (4.1 Orientation of the Jet)

## Qualitative findings

- Independent ALMA and VLA hydrogen recombination line observations (H30a, H39a, H52a, H56a) confirm the broad double-peaked spectrum at +/- 500 km s^-1 toward Sgr A* and reveal more extreme velocity components extending to ~ -2500 and +4000 km s^-1 within 0.2''.  
  > "Our analysis of sub-arcsecond H30α, H39α, H52α and H56α line emission towards Sgr A* confirm the recently published broad peak ∼ 500 km s−1 spectrum toward Sgr A*." (abstract); "We also detect emission at more extreme radial velocities peaking near −2500 and 4000 km s−1 within 0.2′′." (abstract)
- At low radio frequencies (1 to 1.4 GHz), thermal emission from the mini-spiral becomes optically thick and appears in absorption against Sgr A East, revealing a symmetric, nonthermal linear jet structure emanating from Sgr A* along PA ~ 60 deg.  
  > "We note a nonthermal elongated structure on an arcminute scale emanating from Sgr A* at low frequencies between 1 and 1.4 GHz where thermal emission from the mini-spiral is depressed by optical depth effects." (abstract); "A prominent linear structure at PA∼ 60◦ is detected symmetrically only at the lowest frequencies of the broad band." (3.2.2 Low Frequency Radio Data)
- Multiple linear continuum features (J1-J5) on sub-arcsecond to arcsecond scales at 9-44 GHz align along NE-SW position angles (23 to 59 deg) consistent with the recombination line velocity gradient and the large-scale low-frequency jet.  
  > "The PAs of J1 and J5 range between 23◦ and 59◦ and are similar to the PAs of highly red-shifted RRL components, as shown in Figures 3 and 4, and mmRL reported by Murchikova et al. (2019)." (3.2.1 High Frequency Radio Data)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining multi-transition recombination lines (H30a, H39a, H52a, H56a) with wider velocity coverage and broadband multi-frequency continuum data (1 to 48 GHz) allows independent confirmation of broad line emission, identification of persistent features against time-variable artifacts, and separation of nonthermal jet emission from optically thick thermal mini-spiral emission below 1.4 GHz.  
  > "In particular, to detect recombination line emission from Sgr A*, we use a different technique, multiple transitions of hydrogen atom and a wider spectral coverage." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Given that high frequency features near Sgr A* are contaminated by phase and amplitude errors, we analyze numerous data sets and describe features that persist and appear at different frequencies, epochs, and VLA array configurations, as these are unlikely to be generated by imaging artifacts." (1 INTRODUCTION); "One approach to identify both thermal and nonthermal sources is to employ broad bandwidths at multiple frequencies." (4 DISCUSSION)
- Why this method: Continuum subtraction was performed in the image plane toward Sgr A* rather than in the uv-plane to avoid contamination from multiple confusing sources across the field and frequency-dependent variability of Sgr A*.  
  > "We use a technique in which the continuum subtraction is applied in the image plane toward Sgr A* unlike the uv plane where the subtraction is done over the entire field with contributions from multiple sources." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Alternative: Highly inclined cool Keplerian accretion disk model for the broad double-lobed recombination line emission (Murchikova et al. 2019); rejected because the newly detected >~ 4000 km s^-1 line widths correspond to Keplerian motion at 1/5 the observed angular scale.  
  > "These have previously been interpreted as the red and blue lobes of a highly inclined Keplerian disk (Murchikova et al. 2019)." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites); "These have previously been interpreted as the red and blue lobes of a highly inclined Keplerian disk (Murchikova et al. 2019). However, our observations have wider frequency coverage and find significantly broader line widths," (4 DISCUSSION)
- Alternative: Colliding stellar winds in the IRS 13E cluster as the sole origin of its X-ray emission; the authors propose jet collision with the minicavity as an alternative.  
  > "colliding winds of masslosing stars in the cluster are responsible for production of X-rays" (4 DISCUSSION); "Alternatively, we note that X-ray emission from the minicavity can also be explained by the violent collision with the jet shocking the gas to high temperatures." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites)
- Alternative: Phase and amplitude calibration errors as the cause of increased rms noise within 1'' of Sgr A* at 44 GHz; considered alongside the alternative of intrinsic diffuse synchrotron emission.  
  > "It is possible that phase and amplitude errors are responsible for the increased noise." (3.2.1 High Frequency Radio Data); "Alternatively, the excess noise is due to diffuse emission at 44 GHz and is intrinsic due to residual synchrotron emission detected at mm and X-rays." (3.2.1 High Frequency Radio Data)

- Limitation (authors): True line-free channels cannot be conclusively identified because the detected recombination lines are extremely broad relative to the available bandwidth.  
  > "Amazingly, the lines we detect are so broad that the measurements are still limited by not being able to conclusively identify line free channels from Sgr A*." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Despite having significantly broader spectral coverage than Murchikova et al. (2019), it is still not clear where the true continuum is because of a limited set of line free channels." (2.2 Spectroscopic Results)
- Limitation (authors): High-frequency continuum imaging near Sgr A* is dynamic-range limited by strong, hourly time-variable continuum emission, phase and amplitude errors, confusing sources, and limited uv coverage.  
  > "Many high frequency features that are described in the vicinity of Sgr A* are several thousand times fainter than the bright source Sgr A*, thus errors generated by the variable, strong continuum from Sgr A* contaminate structural details in its vicinity." (3.2 Radio Continuum Results); "The Galactic center is a challenging site to search for a jet near Sgr A* because the dynamic range of high resolution images is limited by confusing sources, limited uv coverage, and the intrinsic hourly time variability of Sgr A*, especially at high frequencies." (4 DISCUSSION)
- Limitation (authors): Mass of accelerated ionized gas cannot be directly inferred from recombination-line fluxes due to dominant stimulated emission.  
  > "The mass of accelerated material cannot easily be inferred from the recombination-line fluxes because of the dominant contribution of stimulated emission." (4.0.1 Collimated Outflow and Interaction Sites)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Manual flux equalization across adjacent spectral windows (scaling each spw to match the edge channels of its neighbor) forces continuity across spw boundaries and could artificially create or distort ultra-broad (several thousand km/s) spectral baselines if there are bandpass roll-offs at spw edges.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The extreme velocity peaks (-2500 and +4000 km/s) depend heavily on which narrow velocity windows at the band edges are assumed to be line-free for linear continuum subtraction, and the text and figure captions report slightly different velocity ranges for these continuum windows.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High-frequency sub-arcsecond continuum features (J1-J5) are ~10^3 to 10^4 times fainter than the time-variable point source Sgr A* and lie in regions where rms noise is elevated by a factor of ~3.
