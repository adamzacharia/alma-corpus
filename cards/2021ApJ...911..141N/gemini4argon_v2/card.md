## Analysis recipe

1. **Subsample classification based on [C II] morphology**: Divide the 27 quasars into 'disturbed' (9 sources with close companions or complex merger kinematics) and 'undisturbed' (18 sources without ongoing merger signs or close companions).  
  > "We further apportion this sample into two subsamples based on the morphology of the \[C II\] emission." (Sample Selection); "A total of nine quasars fall within this subgroup labelled as ‘disturbed’. The second ‘undisturbed’ subgroup consists of the remaining 18 galaxies whose \[C II\] emission shows no signs of ongoing merger activity or close companion." (Sample Selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **3D kinematic modeling of [C II] data cubes**: Generate 3D model cubes for a 9-parameter thin rotating disk and a 6-parameter dispersion-dominated bulge, convolve with a 2D Gaussian approximation to the synthesized beam, and fit to a small region of the cleaned data cube using MCMC.  
  > "We therefore run the fitting code on a small region containing the emission in the *cleaned* data cube for which the beam can be approximated by a 2D Gaussian." (qubefit); "The thin disk model is described by nine parameters and has been previously summarized in Appendix C of (cite: Neeleman2019)." (Thin Disk Model); "In total, the bulge model is described by six parameters." (Dispersion-Dominated Bulge Model)
   - software: qubefit, emcee, scipy
   - parameter: Mask = pixels >= 3 sigma expanded by a 1-beam buffer; beam oversampling factor f_beam = pi / (A_beam ln 2); 30% burn-in removal; 420,000 realizations (example P183+05)  
  > "The mask used for the analysis in this paper is generated from all pixels with at least a 3$\sigma$-significant signal, which is then expanded to contain a buffer of at least one independent measurement around the 3$\sigma$ features." (qubefit); "Under the assumption that the beam can be Nyquist-sampled at approximately 2 samples per FWHM of the beam, the adjustment factor is $f_{\rm beam} =  \pi / (A_{\rm beam}\ln2)$, where $A_{\rm beam}$ is the area of the synthesized beam in pixels." (qubefit); "We further remove the first 30 % of the ensemble as a ‘burn-in’ period, where the chain has not converged on its steady-state solution." (qubefit)
   - assumption: Gravitationally supported gas; axisymmetric exponential intensity profile; constant rotational velocity and constant Gaussian velocity dispersion across the galaxy; negligible ALMA line-spread function (1 MHz vs 30 MHz channels)  
  > "In this paper, we wish to explore how well we can determine the overall kinematics, under the assumption that the gas is gravitationally supported." (Models); "The emission of the disk is assumed to be axisymmetric and exponentially decreasing with distance from the center." (Thin Disk Model); "We note that in this equation we have explicitly kept both the velocity and dispersion constant over the full disk." (Thin Disk Model)
   - fitting: Modified chi-square log-likelihood accounting for correlated beam pixels, sampled with affine-invariant MCMC sampler emcee using flat uninformed priors and two independent ensembles.  
  > "For this paper we use a modified $\chi^2$ likelihood function of the form: $$\begin{equation}
\ln \mathcal{L} = -0.5 f_{\rm beam}  \sum_i^{i=n} \frac{(\mathcal{M}_i - \mathcal{D}_i)^2}{\mathcal{R}_i^2}
\end{equation}$$" (qubefit); "To accomplish this, we use the affine-invariant MCMC ensemble sampler, `emcee`, to sample the multidimensional parameter space (cite: ForemanMackey2013). We assume flat, uninformed priors on all of the parameters, and run two independent ensembles using different initial values." (qubefit)
   - validation: Convergence checked between and within the two MCMC ensembles; goodness of fit evaluated via reduced-chi^2 and one-sample KS test on independent (1 per beam) residual pixels inside the union of the fitting mask and 1-sigma model contour.  
  > "To check for the convergence of the ensemble, we compare the ensemble both with itself and with the other ensemble." (qubefit); "To provide a measure of the goodness of fit, we calculate two goodness-of-fit statistics: a simple reduced-$\chi^2$ statistic and a one-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) probability." (Goodness of Fit); "To mitigate the effects of correlated noise in the data due to oversampling of the beam, we only take one measurement per beam." (Goodness of Fit)
3. **Generate 2D mean velocity and velocity dispersion fields and radial profiles**: Fit a single 1D Gaussian to the spectrum at each spatial pixel using astropy routines inside qubefit, then deproject velocities and bin by galactocentric radius (excluding within 30 deg of minor axis) to form radial profiles.

## Qualitative findings

- Roughly one-third (9/27) of z >= 6 quasar host galaxies have disturbed [C II] morphologies due to companions or mergers, one-third (10/27) show smooth velocity gradients consistent with rotating disks, and one-third (8/27) lack velocity gradients and appear dispersion-dominated.  
  > "One third (9 out of 27) of quasar host galaxies have disturbed \[C II\] emission profiles." (Summary and Concluding Remarks); "About one third of quasar host galaxies (10 out of 27) show a smooth velocity gradient consistent with the emission arising from a rotating disk, and the remaining one third (8 out of 27) show a velocity profile without a clear velocity gradient, which is consistent with the emission arising from a dispersion-dominated system." (Summary and Concluding Remarks)
- Observed radial increases in rotational velocity and central peaks in velocity dispersion are explained by beam smearing, so constant rotational velocity and constant velocity dispersion profiles are sufficient at ~0.25'' resolution.  
  > "As the figure shows, this constant velocity model shows a similar rise in the rotational velocity with radius, indicating that the increase in rotational velocity for all galaxies is largely driven by the effects of beam smearing." (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion); "Most quasar host galaxies with a strong velocity gradient have higher velocity dispersion at the center. However, this increase is also seen in their beam-convolved model data cubes, again indicating that beam-smearing is the dominant cause for this increase." (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion)
- The z ~ 2 main-sequence [C II]-to-molecular gas conversion factor (alpha_[CII] = 31 L_sun/M_sun) overpredicts molecular gas masses above the dynamical masses at z >= 6 and is therefore likely invalid for z >= 6 quasar hosts.  
  > "We find that if we apply the molecular mass conversion from the \[C II\] luminosity assuming the conversion in (cite: Zanella2018), we get molecular mass estimates that are on-average greater than the dynamical mass estimates with a median molecular-to-dynamical mass ratio of 1.7." (Gas Masses versus Dynamical Masses); "This suggests that the conversion factor derived for main-sequence galaxies at $z \sim 2$ is not valid for quasar host galaxies at $z \gtrsim 6$." (Gas Masses versus Dynamical Masses)
- The diversity of [C II] kinematics (mergers, rotating disks, turbulent dispersion-dominated systems) suggests that gas accretion onto z >= 6 SMBHs is not triggered by a single precipitating factor.  
  > "Finally, we speculate that the large variety in \[C II\] kinematics is an indication that gas accretion onto $z \gtrsim 6$ super massive black holes is not caused by a single precipitating factor." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sub-0.25'' (<1.2 kpc at z ~ 6) ALMA [C II] observations are required to spatially resolve the ~0.4'' (~2 kpc) extent of z >= 6 quasar host galaxies, overcome optical/NIR quasar glare, and constrain morphology, inclination, and kinematics.  
  > "Unlike the optical and near-infrared, where the brightness of the quasar inhibits detection of the quasar host galaxy, the Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) capitalizes on the relative faintness of the quasar at millimeter wavelengths in order to search for the quasar host." (Introduction); "Previous observations have shown that the extent of the \[C II\] emission from these galaxies is $\approx$$0.4\ensuremath{''}$ corresponding to $\approx$2 kpc (cite: Walter2009; Wang2013; Venemans2016; Decarli2018). We have therefore obtained $<$$0.25\ensuremath{''}$ (which is $<$1.2 kpc at $z \approx 6$) \[C II\] imaging of $z \gtrsim 6$ quasar host galaxies using ALMA over several observing programs (in particular recent program IDs: 2017.1.01301.S and 2018.1.00908.S)." (Sample Selection)
- Why this method: Direct 3D cube forward modeling with qubefit accounts for finite beam resolution/smearing and allows flexible non-disk models, while spaxel-by-spaxel 1D Gaussian fitting avoids the clipping artifacts that bias moment-based velocity dispersion maps at low S/N.  
  > "In order to capture the increasing complexity of far-infrared emission line observations, and to allow for maximum flexibility in emission models, we have designed the Python-based code `qubefit`." (qubefit); "Because of the robust nature of the Gaussian fitting and its independence on the noise properties of the data, we opt to use this method for estimating the velocity and velocity dispersion fields for all quasar host galaxies in our sample." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
- Alternative: 2D moment 1 and moment 2 maps with sigma-clipping were tested and rejected for deriving velocity and dispersion fields because clipping at >= 2 sigma artificially lowers the velocity dispersion at the low-S/N edges of the galaxy.  
  > "Aggressive clipping can therefore significantly alter the velocity dispersion field, resulting in low S/N regions having artificially low velocity dispersions." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields); "Because of the robust nature of the Gaussian fitting and its independence on the noise properties of the data, we opt to use this method for estimating the velocity and velocity dispersion fields for all quasar host galaxies in our sample." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
- Alternative: More complicated emission profiles (e.g., double exponential) and radial velocity/dispersion profiles were tested in qubefit, but rejected because they did not improve the fit or change conclusions at ~0.25'' resolution.  
  > "Although in (cite: Novak2020) we find that the \[C II\] emission is best described by a compact and extended component, introducing a more complicated emission profile does not improve the fit." (Thin Disk Model); "We have tested more complicated velocity and dispersion profiles, but these do not change our conclusions." (Thin Disk Model)
- Alternative: Dispersion-dominated spherical bulge model was fit to all galaxies, but provided a poorer fit than the thin disk model for systems with rotation or asymmetric morphologies, so the thin disk model was used for subsequent analysis.  
  > "In general, the dispersion-dominated bulge model provides a poorer fit to the data than the thin disk model." (Kinematic Modeling Results); "For the remainder of the paper, we will therefore use the results from the thin disk model." (Kinematic Modeling Results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Convolving the model with a frequency-independent 2D Gaussian beam on a small region of the cleaned cube and using a scalar f_beam factor are approximations to the true dirty beam and pixel-to-pixel covariance matrix.  
  > "We note that this is an approximation in two ways: 1) The actual beam (often referred to as the ‘dirty’ beam) is much more complicated in interferometric data, hence the need for removing the beam before analyzing the data in a process known as *cleaning*." (qubefit); "We note that this is an approximation to the true correlated nature of the observations, which requires accounting for the covariance matrix describing the pixel-to-pixel correlations as well as the uncertainty in the $\chi^2$ statistic (cite: Davis2017; Smith2019)." (qubefit)
- Limitation (authors): Four undisturbed galaxies (J0842+1218, J1044-0125, J2100-1715, J2348-3054) are only barely resolved and one (P323+12) is detected at very low significance, making kinematic modeling and inclination constraints difficult.  
  > "We note that the \[C II\] emission from four of these galaxies (see Table 1) is only barely resolved, and one quasar host galaxy (P323$+$12) is only detected at very low significance." (Sample Selection); "However, some of the quasar host galaxies are only barely resolved in these observations, and have large uncertainties on the inclination of the \[C II\] emission (i.e., J0842$+$1218, J1044$-$0125, J2100$-$1715, P323$+$12 and J2348$-$3054)." (Kinematic Modeling Results)
- Limitation (authors): The ~0.25'' spatial resolution is insufficient to resolve the rising part of the rotation curve (which requires at least 4 beams along the major axis) or the SMBH sphere of influence.  
  > "The resolution of these observations is therefore insufficient to measure the shape of the rotation curve, which requires at least four independent measurements along the major axis." (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion); "We note in passing here that the current resolution remains too coarse to resolve the SMBH’s sphere of influence." (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): For galaxies in the disturbed/merger subsample, the assumption of gravitationally supported motion in a single smooth disk breaks down and yields unreliable inclinations and dynamical masses.  
  > "In some cases (i.e., J1342$+$0928, P308$-$21 and J2318$-$3113), the model tries to fit multiple distinct \[C II\] components with a single smooth disk. This causes unreasonably large inclinations (the median inclination is about 20$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ larger) in the merger subsample, and hence results in inaccurate kinematic parameters for these galaxies." (Kinematic Modeling Results); "We measure the dynamical masses for all galaxies in the sample, although we caution that the estimates for galaxies in the disturbed sample are not very reliable due to non-gravitationally supported motions." (Summary and Concluding Remarks)
- Limitation (authors): Equating [C II] dynamical mass to stellar bulge mass carries large systematic uncertainties that could alleviate the 2.4 sigma offset from the local M_bulge - M_BH relation.  
  > "A detailed discussion on the magnitude of this systematic is beyond the scope of this work, but if the results from NGC 3091 are typical, then much of the tension between high redshift quasars and the local bulge mass - black hole mass relation could be alleviated by accounting for this systematic." (Comparison between Dynamical and SMBH Mass)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The inclination angles for several low-inclination or barely resolved disks have only upper limits, resulting in only lower limits on v_rot and M_dyn for 5 of the 18 undisturbed sources.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using a single isotropic pressure-support factor eta = 3.4 for both rotation-dominated and dispersion-dominated systems introduces substantial systematic uncertainty in circular velocity.
