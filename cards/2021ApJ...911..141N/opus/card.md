## Analysis recipe

1. **Classify [C II] morphology**: Split sample into 'disturbed' (close companion or merger) and 'undisturbed' subsamples by visual morphology  
  > "We further apportion this sample into two subsamples based on the morphology of the [C II] emission." (Sample Selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit 3D kinematic models to [C II] cubes**: qubefit forward modeling: thin disk (9 params, exponential intensity, constant v_rot and σ_v) and dispersion-dominated bulge (6 params); model cube convolved with 2D Gaussian beam; modified χ² likelihood with f_beam correction over 3σ-based mask  
  > "We model the beam with a 2D-Gaussian with size and position angle as determined from a Gaussian fit to the beam of the data cube." (Methods: Kinematic Modeling); "We therefore run the fitting code on a small region containing the emission in the cleaned data cube for which the beam can be approximated by a 2D Gaussian." (Methods: Kinematic Modeling)
   - software: qubefit, emcee
   - parameter: Mask from ≥3σ pixels expanded by one independent measurement  
  > "which is then expanded to contain a buffer of at least one independent measurement around the 3σ features" (Methods: Kinematic Modeling)
   - parameter: f_beam assumes Nyquist sampling at ~2 samples per beam FWHM  
  > "Under the assumption that the beam can be Nyquist-sampled at approximately 2 samples per FWHM of the beam" (Methods: Kinematic Modeling)
   - assumption: Gas gravitationally supported; constant rotation and dispersion over disk; no radial motion  
  > "In this paper, we wish to explore how well we can determine the overall kinematics, under the assumption that the gas is gravitationally supported." (Models)
   - fitting: emcee affine-invariant ensemble; flat uninformed priors; two independent ensembles; 30% burn-in; medians with 16th–84th percentiles; 3σ limits for unconstrained parameters  
  > "We assume flat, uninformed priors on all of the parameters, and run two independent ensembles using different initial values." (Methods: Kinematic Modeling); "We further remove the first 30 % of the ensemble as a ‘burn-in’ period" (Methods: Kinematic Modeling)
   - validation: Convergence checked by comparing ensembles with themselves and each other; second chain initialized within 20% of first  
  > "The second chain was initialized with parameter values that were chosen randomly but within 20 % of the first chain." (Analysis of the Kinematic Modeling)
3. **Assess goodness of fit**: Reduced χ² and one-sample KS probability of residuals vs Gaussian noise, using one measurement per beam within union of fitting mask and model 1σ contour  
  > "To mitigate the effects of correlated noise in the data due to oversampling of the beam, we only take one measurement per beam." (Goodness of Fit); "The KS-statistic is converted to a value between 0 and 1, using standard recipes within scipy" (Goodness of Fit)
   - software: SciPy
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual channel maps inspected; thin disk preferred over bulge  
  > "For the remainder of the paper, we will therefore use the results from the thin disk model." (Kinematic Modeling Results)
4. **Make velocity and dispersion fields**: Single-Gaussian fit to each spaxel spectrum (initial guesses from moments); compared with moment-map method  
  > "In this manuscript we use the fitting routines in astropy (cite: Astropy2013; Astropy2018) to fit a single Gaussian to the spectrum of each individual spaxel." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
   - software: Astropy, qubefit
   - assumption: Line profile per spaxel is Gaussian  
  > "The main drawback with this method is the assumed function form of the spectral line." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with moment maps at several clipping thresholds for P183+05  
  > "There is no evidence that the assumption of a Gaussian profile is systematically skewing the measurements in either field." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
5. **Derive radial velocity and dispersion profiles**: Deproject per-pixel velocities using fitted PA and inclination, bin by deprojected radius, exclude near minor axis; compare to beam-convolved constant-velocity model; 13 undisturbed resolved galaxies  
  > "of the minor axis, because uncertainties increase for these measurements due to larger velocity corrections and larger beam-smearing effects." (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion); "we apply the same method to the model cube with constant velocity which has been convolved with the beam." (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rise in rotation curve reproduced by beam-smeared constant model  
  > "The resolution of these observations is therefore insufficient to measure the shape of the rotation curve" (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion)
6. **Estimate circular velocities**: v_circ = sqrt(v_rot² + η σ_v²) with η = 3.4 at Re; compared with 0.52 FWHM and 0.75 FWHM/sin i  
  > "We will take this value as our fiducial value to facilitate comparison with literature values at lower redshift" (Methods for Estimating Circular Velocities)
   - assumption: Exponential turbulent pressure-supported disk (Burkert2010); 40% systematic covers η range  
  > "the reported systematic uncertainties in the dynamical mass include a 40 % contribution that encompasses the expected range in" (Methods for Estimating Circular Velocities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compute dynamical masses**: M_dyn = v_circ² R / G = 2.33e5 v_circ² R evaluated at 2Re (Re = 1.678 R_D); systematic uncertainties added  
  > "Under the assumption that the mass distribution is spherically symmetric, the dynamical mass enclosed within a radius" (Dynamical Masses)
   - parameter: +20% lower systematic for disk geometry; 50% lower systematic for [C II] vs stellar extent; ~40% for extended component  
  > "we therefore conservatively increase the dynamical mass uncertainty by 20 % toward lower masses to account for this systematic uncertainty." (Dynamical Masses); "we will conservatively add a 50% systematic uncertainty to the lower bound of the dynamical mass estimate" (Radial Extent for Dynamical Mass Estimate)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Need resolution that spatially resolves ~0.4'' [C II] extent  
  > "requires a resolution such that the [C II] emission is easily spatially resolved." (Sample Selection)
- Why this method: Existing codes assume disks; qubefit allows flexible models with beam convolution and MCMC; Gaussian fitting chosen for velocity fields  
  > "In order to capture the increasing complexity of far-infrared emission line observations, and to allow for maximum flexibility in emission models" (Methods: Kinematic Modeling); "Because of the robust nature of the Gaussian fitting and its independence on the noise properties of the data, we opt to use this method" (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
- Alternative: Dispersion-dominated bulge model fitted but rejected as poorer than thin disk  
  > "In general, the dispersion-dominated bulge model provides a poorer fit to the data than the thin disk model." (Kinematic Modeling Results)
- Alternative: Double-exponential emission profile tested; did not improve fit  
  > "introducing a more complicated emission profile does not improve the fit." (Thin Disk Model)
- Alternative: Moment-map velocity fields rejected in favor of Gaussian fitting  
  > "The choice of clipping can significantly alter the velocity field." (Two Methods for Deriving Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion Fields)
- Alternative: Scoville2016 dust-based gas masses would exceed dynamical masses  
  > "we would get molecular mass estimates above the mass estimates based on [C II], much larger than the dynamical mass estimates." (Gas Masses versus Dynamical Masses)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Model-choice systematics not included in MCMC uncertainties  
  > "Systematic uncertainties from the choice of the model are not included in these uncertainty estimates." (Fitting Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Resolution insufficient for rotation curve shape  
  > "The resolution of these observations is therefore insufficient to measure the shape of the rotation curve" (Radial Profiles of the Mean Velocity and Velocity Dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): Disturbed-sample dynamical masses unreliable  
  > "we caution that the estimates for galaxies in the disturbed sample are not very reliable due to non-gravitationally supported motions." (Summary and Concluding Remarks)
- Limitation (authors): Dynamical mass as bulge-mass proxy is uncertain  
  > "One possible caveat to this result is the implicit assumption that the dynamical mass is a good proxy for the bulge mass in these systems." (Summary and Concluding Remarks)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination–v_rot degeneracy at low inclination leaves several v_circ only as limits, affecting the mean M_dyn
- Caveat (inferred, ours): f_beam approximation to correlated noise may misestimate statistical uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-exponential, constant-velocity models may bias Re and v_circ for compact, barely resolved hosts
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison to local relation hinges on M_dyn≈M_bulge and on single-epoch BH mass systematics
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Printed code 2015.1.00339.S differs from archive-linked 2015.1.00399.S; one may be a typo
