## Analysis recipe

1. **combine, align and self-calibrate the continuum data**: Flag line channels, average, recompute weights, align and rescale executions, then self-calibrate in two stages (compact first, then combined)  
  > "we apply the task `statwt` to recalculate the visibility weighting according to the observed scatter." (2.1 ALMA observations); "To enhance the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N), self-calibration was performed in two stages." (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA 5.6.2, statwt
   - parameter: Continuum channels averaged to 125 MHz after flagging ±25 km/s around each line  
  > "The remaining channels from all spectral windows are averaged into 125 MHz channels." (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: Visibilities averaged to 250 MHz channels and 30 s time bins for the visibility analysis  
  > "we averaged the continuum emission into channels of 250 MHz in width and 30 s time-binning." (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: Flux rescaling: 10% for 2015.1.00934.S in Band 7; 19% for the first extended execution in Band 4  
  > "In Band 4, we corrected a discrepancy of $19\%$ between the first observation of the extended baselines and all others." (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux calibration of each execution compared for consistency  
  > "we aligned the datasets and compared the flux calibration of each individual execution to ensure they were consistent." (2.1 ALMA observations)
2. **make continuum images and measure fluxes**: Total fluxes measured from the images within a circular aperture of 0.5'' radius from the centre; radial intensity profiles from deprojected images  
  > "The fluxes from this table are obtained from the images assuming all the emission within a circular area of" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - parameter: Deprojection uses inclination and PA from the visibility fits; profiles normalized to the ring peak and restored with a common 0.05'' x 0.034'' beam  
  > "the images are previously restored with the same beam before deprojection" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **image the CO and CS lines and make moment maps**: Apply continuum calibration to line channels, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, image 0.5 km/s channels with robust 1 and uv-tapering, make peak-intensity and velocity maps with bettermoments, clipped at 3 sigma  
  > "We used the package `bettermoments` (cite: bettermoments) to create the kinematic map." (2.1 ALMA observations); "these were obtained by processing the channel maps with the Python package `bettermoments` (cite: bettermoments), and clipping the emission at 3$\sigma$." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - software: CASA 5.6.2, uvcontsub, bettermoments
   - parameter: 0.5 km/s channels, robust 1, taper 0.03'' (12CO) and 0.04'' (13CO)  
  > "we chose to apply a uv-tapering with a Gaussian of $0.03''$ on the $^{12}$CO, and $0.04''$ on the $^{13}$CO." (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: CS non-detection: channels imaged at 1.0 km/s and three moment 0 images (1-4, 5-8, 9-12 km/s)  
  > "Three moment 0 images were generated from these channel maps, spanning the velocities from 1-4, 5-8, and 9-12 km s$^{-1}$." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - fitting: bettermoments fits a quadratic function to the line peak in each pixel  
  > "This package fits a quadratic function to find the peak intensity of the line emission in each pixel, and the velocity associated to it." (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **derive optical depth, dust disk mass and spectral index**: Optical depth from the brightness temperature of the ring peak; optically thin dust mass from M_dust = d^2 F_nu / (kappa_nu B_nu(T)); integrated spectral index from the Band 7 / Band 4 total flux ratio  
  > "Under the assumption of optically thin emission, we calculate the dust disk mass as" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index); "With the total flux from the images at the two wavelengths, we obtained the spatially integrated spectral index as $\alpha_{\mathrm{mm}}$=$\log(F_{\rm{B7}}/F_{\rm{B4}})/\log(\nu_7/\nu_4)=2.0\pm0.2$" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - parameter: Distance 137.5 pc  
  > "We use the distance to CIDA 1 of 137.5$\pm$1.1 pc derived by (cite: bailer-jones2018) using Gaia DR2" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - parameter: Dust temperatures of 20 K (constant), 14.7 K (luminosity relation) and about 30 K (radiative transfer midplane at 20 au) tried for optical depth  
  > "We used a radially constant physical temperature of 20 K and we find that the optical depth at the peak is $\tau_{\rm{peak}, B7}=0.48$ and $\tau_{\rm{peak}, B4}=0.28$ for Band 7 and Band 4, respectively." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission for the dust mass  
  > "We therefore assume that most of the emission is optically thin to calculate the dust disk mass." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - assumption: kappa_nu = 2.3 cm^2/g (nu/230 GHz)^0.4; dust temperature 20.0 K or 14.7 K  
  > "Taking a mass absorption coefficient ($\kappa_\nu$) at a given frequency as $\kappa_\nu=2.3\,$cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}\times(\nu/230\,\rm{GHz})^{0.4}$ (cite: beckwith1990; andrews2013)" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spectral index checked in a beam-sized region at the centre and in an area encircling the ring (both around 2.0)  
  > "the integrated spectral index in each region is around $2.0$." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
5. **fit the dust morphology in the uv plane**: Axisymmetric parametric intensity profiles (central Gaussian plus broken-Gaussian ring, plus an extra Gaussian in Band 7) fitted to the visibilities with a free offset, inclination and PA; Gaussian-process code frank used for comparison  
  > "We describe the emission on both ALMA bands with axisymmetric parametric models, and for comparison we also use the Gaussian Processes modeler Python package `frank` (cite: jennings2020)." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting); "Therefore, each model has four extra free parameters in addition to those that describe the intensity profile." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
   - software: galario, emcee, frank
   - parameter: galario pixel size 0.2 mas  
  > "The Fourier Transforms to obtain the models visibilities and the $\chi^2$ calculation are computed with the `galario` Python package (cite: tazzari2018) using a pixel size of 0.2 mas." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
   - parameter: More than 250000 steps after convergence; 16th and 84th percentiles as error bars  
  > "We ran more than 250000 steps after convergence to find the most likely parameter set for each model, as well as taking the 16th and 84th percentile for the error bars." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric dust emission  
  > "We describe the emission on both ALMA bands with axisymmetric parametric models" (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; flat priors over a wide range (inc in [0,90], PA in [0,180], sigma >= 0); chi^2 likelihood from galario  
  > "We sample the posterior probability distribution with a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) routine based on the `emcee` Python package (cite: emcee)." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting); "We used a flat prior probability distribution over a wide parameter range, such that the walkers would only be initially restricted by geometric considerations (inc $\in [0,90]$ , PA $\in [0,180]$, $\sigma \geq 0$)." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
   - validation: Residual images show no clear additional substructure; a Band 7 fit without the extra outer component left significant residuals; visibility ratio of the two bands constant within uncertainties  
  > "Our residuals do not show any clear sign of additional substructures, either radial or azimuthal at the current resolution and sensitivity of the observations." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting); "a fit in Band 7 without this additional ring in the outer disk (e.g., the model used for Band 4) produced significant residuals, suggesting the presence of a further ring." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
6. **derive stellar and accretion properties from the X-Shooter spectrum**: Fit photospheric templates plus slab accretion models and extinction; break the degeneracy by deriving accretion luminosity from emission line fluxes while varying A_V from 0 to 4 mag in 0.25 mag steps; adopt evolutionary tracks  
  > "We fitted the spectrum following the procedure described by (cite: manara13)." (3.3 Stellar properties from the X-shooter spectrum); "The accretion luminosity derived from the lines shows the minimum dispersion with $A_V\sim3.5$ mag" (3.3 Stellar properties from the X-shooter spectrum)
   - software: X-Shooter pipeline, ESOReflex, molecfit
   - parameter: A_V constrained to 3-3.8 mag; best fit SpT M5, A_V = 3.5 mag  
  > "with SpT M5, $A_V$= 3.5 mag, resulting in a value of stellar luminosity $L_\star$ = 0.15 $\pm$ 0.03 $L_\odot$" (3.3 Stellar properties from the X-shooter spectrum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mass accretion rate compared with earlier values  
  > "This value of mass accretion rate is in line with the results of (cite: HH08)" (3.3 Stellar properties from the X-shooter spectrum)
7. **compare with planet-disk dust evolution and radiative transfer models**: Dust evolution simulations with an analytic planet gap (2.4 MJup at 8 au and 0.5 MJup at 12 au), post-processed with RADMC-3D into synthetic images and radial profiles at 0.1, 0.5 and 1 Myr, compared with the deprojected observed profiles and spectral index  
  > "The dust density distribution of the dust evolution simulations are then combined with radiative transfer calculations using the Monte Carlo code RADMC-3D[^1] as explained in previous works (cite: pohl2016)." (4 Comparison with dust evolution models); "Figure [11](#fig:rad_profile_LMP) shows the comparison of the deprojected radial profile from Fig. [3](#fig:radial_profile) and the models assuming a 0.5 $M_{\rm{Jup}}$ planet at 12 au" (4 Comparison with dust evolution models)
   - software: DustPy, RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Initial dust-to-gas ratio 1/100, all grains initially micron-sized  
  > "For this simulation, we assumed an initial dust-to-gas ratio of 1/100 where all the grains are initially micron-sized particles." (4 Comparison with dust evolution models)
   - parameter: Fragmentation velocity 10 m/s; alpha_visc = 1e-3; disk mass 5.5 MJup; star 0.1 Msun  
  > "The fragmentation velocity of the particles is 10 m s$^{-1}$ in the entire disk. We assumed $\alpha_{\rm{visc}}=10^{-3}$ and a disk mass of 5.5 $M_{\rm{Jup}}$ around a stellar object of 0.1 $M_\odot$ mass" (4 Comparison with dust evolution models)
   - assumption: A single planet opens the gap; the dust-emission minimum co-locates with the gas gap minimum in the baseline experiment  
  > "Under the assumption that it is one single planet creating the large gap" (4 Comparison with dust evolution models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model profiles compared with deprojected observed radial profiles in both bands; model spectral index compared with observed  
  > "This comparison of the radial profiles illustrates how the models at 0.1 Myr nicely reproduce the contrast between the inner disk and the peak of the ring" (4 Comparison with dust evolution models)

## Qualitative findings

- The continuum images in both bands show a very bright unresolved inner disk, a large gap and a ring peaking at about 20 au  
  > "The dust continuum images at both wavelengths reveal unresolved emission from the inner disk, a large gap, and a ring-like structure peaking at around 20 au." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
- Band 7 visibilities require an additional outer component, suggesting a shoulder or an unresolved extra ring; no other substructure is found  
  > "There is a hint of a shoulder beyond the ring-like structure from our fit of the visibilities in Band 7." (6 Conclusions)
- The spectral index is roughly constant across the disk within uncertainties  
  > "Within the uncertainties, the spectral index seems to be relatively constant throughout the disk" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier ALMA data only marginally resolved the cavity, so higher resolution at two wavelengths was needed to resolve the cavity, ring structure and spectral index variations  
  > "Nevertheless, in previous ALMA observations the cavity of CIDA1 was marginally resolved and the nature of this structure was unclear." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: A single central Gaussian describes the inner disk; several ring profile combinations were tried and the best parametric models adopted  
  > "While a single centrally peaked Gaussian is enough to describe the inner emission of CIDA 1, for the ring emission we tried several different combinations of Gaussian profiles and/or radially broken Gaussian profiles" (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
- Alternative: Several Gaussian and broken-Gaussian ring profile combinations tried; a Band 7 fit without the extra outer component was rejected for significant residuals  
  > "a fit in Band 7 without this additional ring in the outer disk (e.g., the model used for Band 4) produced significant residuals, suggesting the presence of a further ring." (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
- Alternative: A 2.4 MJup planet at 8 au rejected because it empties the inner disk and is unlikely to form by core accretion around such a low-mass star  
  > "This experiment demonstrates that such a massive planet is unlikely to exist in this disk" (4 Comparison with dust evolution models)
- Alternative: Photoevaporation excluded; dead-zone edge trapping, snow-line effect, a close-in super-Earth and multiple overlapping planets discussed as alternatives  
  > "These include a traffic jam expected at the snow line due to variation of fragmentation velocities of the particles, the inner edge of dead zones, and/or multiple planets inside the cavity" (6 Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: MCMC run for more than 250000 steps after convergence  
  > "We ran more than 250000 steps after convergence to find the most likely parameter set for each model" (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Band 4 S/N is about half that of Band 7, so the shallower Band 4 gap needs deeper data to confirm  
  > "this result needs to be taken with care because the S/N of Band 4 is around half that of Band 7" (3.2 Dust morphology from visibility fitting)
- Limitation (authors): The dust opacity is poorly known, and the dust mass depends strongly on the assumed temperature  
  > "Therefore, the inferred dust disk mass is consistent between the two wavelengths with a large uncertainty that depends on the assumed temperature." (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral index in the gap is unreliable owing to very low flux  
  > "The very low fluxes within the gap do not allow us to obtain reliable values of the spectral index in this region" (3.1 Images, dust disk mass, and spectral index)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Physical temperature for optical depth and mass spans 14.7-30 K, so mass and tau are only loosely constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model-observation comparison is by eye with a single hand-picked parameter set (alpha 1e-3, fixed fragmentation velocity), so the planet-mass exclusion depends on those choices
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Planet at 12 au is chosen ad hoc to move the pressure bump out, with no fit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extra Band 7 outer Gaussian is justified by residuals only; no model-comparison statistic is given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visibility-fit parameter uncertainties are statistical only and ignore the 10-19% inter-execution flux discrepancies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Band 7 and Band 4 beams and flux scales were matched only approximately, which can affect the spectral-index profile
