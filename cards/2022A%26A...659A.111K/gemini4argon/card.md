## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure and analyze laboratory rotational spectra of acrylamide and compute partition functions**: Record absorption spectra from 75 to 480 GHz, assign lines using Loomis-Wood plots in AABS, fit Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian (and two-state RAS Hamiltonian for skew conformer), and compute partition functions numerically with SPCAT  
  > "The individual spectral sections were merged into a single spectrum and subjected to the analysis using the Assignment and Analysis of Broadband Spectra (AABS) package (cite: aabs; Kisiel2012)." (Experiments); "The fits and predictions were made in terms of Watson’s $A$-reduced Hamiltonian in I$^{\text{r}}$-representation (cite: Watson1977)." (Syn conformer); "We used the SPCAT program (cite: Pickett1991) to undertake this summation numerically employing the spectroscopic constants from Table [\[T1\]](#T1) and all rotational states up to $J=120$." (Syn conformer)
   - software: AABS, SPCAT
   - parameter: Frequency range 75-480 GHz, sample pressure 10-20 ubar, room temperature, J up to 120 for partition function  
  > "The room-temperature rotational spectrum was measured in the frequency region 75–480 GHz using two spectrometers." (Experiments); "All spectra were registered with the sample pressure between 10 and 20 $\mu$bar by upward and downward frequency scanning and averaged." (Experiments)
   - assumption: Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian in I^r representation; harmonic approximation for vibrational partition function  
  > "The fits and predictions were made in terms of Watson’s $A$-reduced Hamiltonian in I$^{\text{r}}$-representation (cite: Watson1977)." (Syn conformer); "The vibrational partition function ($Q_{\text{vib}}$) was obtained using the Eq. 3.60 of (cite: Gordy1970) by taking into account the frequencies of twenty-four normal vibrational modes" (Syn conformer)
   - fitting: Least-squares Hamiltonian fit to transition frequencies  
  > "The broad coverage of transition types and quantum numbers ensured the precise determination of the rotational constants and full sets of the quartic and sextic centrifugal distortion constants." (Syn conformer)
   - validation: Fit rms (sigma_fit) of 0.024-0.040 MHz and dimensionless weighted deviation (sigma_w) of 0.93-1.08  
  > "$\sigma_{\text{fit}}$$^{d}$/ MHz | 0.028 | 0.024 | 0.035" (Table 1); "$\sigma_{\text{fit}}$$^{d}$/ MHz | 0.040 | 0.035" (Table 3)
2. **Model ALMA ReMoCA spectra and derive column density upper limits for acrylamide and propionamide**: Compute LTE synthetic spectra with Weeds for each molecule and compare with continuum-subtracted ALMA ReMoCA spectra toward Sgr B2(N1S), Sgr B2(N2), and the East offset position  
  > "We modeled the spectra of Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2) with the software Weeds (cite: Maret11) under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)" (Observations); "A best-fit synthetic spectrum of each molecule was derived separately, and then the contributions of all identified molecules were added together." (Observations)
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: Sgr B2(N1S): theta_s = 2.0'', T_rot = 160 K, Delta V = 5.0 km s^-1, V_off = 0.0 km s^-1, V_sys = 62 km s^-1  
  > "with respect to the assumed systemic velocity of the source, $V_{\rm sys}=62$ km s$^{-1}$ for Sgr B2(N1S) and $V_{\rm sys}= 74$ km s$^{-1}$ for Sgr B2(N2)." (Observations); "*Acrylamide* | *syn*-C$_2$H$_3$C(O)NH$_2$ | n | 0 | 2.0 | 160 | $<$ 1.6 (16) | 2.26 | 5.0 | $0.0$ | $>$ 26" (Table 4)
   - parameter: Sgr B2(N2): theta_s = 0.9'', T_rot = 180 K, Delta V = 5.0 km s^-1, V_off = 0.0 km s^-1, V_sys = 74 km s^-1  
  > "*Acrylamide* | *syn*-C$_2$H$_3$C(O)NH$_2$ | n | 0 | 0.9 | 180 | $<$ 2.1 (16) | 2.67 | 5.0 | $0.0$ | $>$ 6.3" (Table 5)
   - parameter: East offset position (1.58'', -2.72''): theta_s = 2.3'', T_rot = 150 K, Delta V = 4.2 km s^-1, V_off = 0.0 km s^-1  
  > "C$_2$H$_5$C(O)NH$_2$ | n | 0 | 2.3 | 150 | $<$ 1.3 (16) | 3.77 | 4.2 | $0.0$" (Table 6)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE); source size, temperature, linewidth, and velocity offset fixed to acetamide values (or Li et al. 2021 values for East offset); skew conformer neglected in partition function (<3% contribution)  
  > "we computed LTE synthetic spectra of this molecule on the basis of the parameters derived for acetamide, CH$_3$C(O)NH$_2$, by (cite: Belloche2019) and (cite: Belloche2017), respectively. Only the column density of acrylamide was kept as a free parameter." (Nondetection of acrylamide); "We did not take into account the contribution of the *skew* conformer of acrylamide to its partition function." (Nondetection of acrylamide)
   - fitting: Column density varied as the only free parameter to determine upper limits against observed spectra and 3-sigma level  
  > "Only the column density of acrylamide was kept as a free parameter." (Nondetection of acrylamide); "This illustrates the additional uncertainty that arises from the continuum fitting and affects the position of the level used to assess the significance of a line detection (e.g., the $3\sigma$ level in the figures shown in Appendix [8](#a:spectra))." (Nondetection of propionamide)
   - validation: Visual comparison of synthetic spectra across multiple transitions in v=0 and vibrationally excited states against observed spectra and full molecular background model  
  > "The nondetection toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2) is illustrated in Figs. [5](#f:spec_c2h3conh2-s_ve0_n1s)–[6](#f:spec_c2h3conh2-s_v24e1_n1s) and Figs. [7](#f:spec_c2h3conh2-s_ve0_n2)–[8](#f:spec_c2h3conh2-s_v24e1_n2), respectively" (Nondetection of acrylamide)
3. **Compare observational upper limits with astrochemical kinetics model predictions**: Use solid-phase abundance ratios of CH4 to C2H2, C2H4, and C2H6 at the end of cold collapse from Garrod et al. (2021) as proxies for acetamide to propiolamide, acrylamide, and propionamide ratios  
  > "Here we extend this analysis to consider also acrylamide and propionamide, using C$_2$H$_4$ and C$_2$H$_6$ as proxies, respectively. As before, values are taken from the end-time of the cold collapse stage of evolution used in the models (cite: Garrod21)." (Formation mechanisms for acrylamide and related species)
   - assumption: Amides form on cold grain surfaces via addition of hydrocarbon radicals to NH2CO in proportion to simple hydrocarbon abundances, neglecting hot-stage formation/destruction and binding energy differences  
  > "This suggests that, if formed through cold addition of C$_2$H$_3$ to NH$_2$CO on grain surfaces, acrylamide should be around 440 times less abundant than acetamide." (Formation mechanisms for acrylamide and related species); "We note, however, that the ratios from the models do not consider any formation or destruction that could occur during the hot stage, nor do they take account of differences in binding energies" (Formation mechanisms for acrylamide and related species)

## Qualitative findings

- Tunneling splitting through a low energy barrier between two symmetrically equivalent configurations was discovered for the skew conformer of acrylamide.  
  > "Tunneling through a low energy barrier between two symmetrically equivalent configurations has been revealed for this higher-energy species." (abstract)
- Neither acrylamide nor propionamide were detected toward Sgr B2(N1S) or Sgr B2(N2).  
  > "Neither acrylamide nor propionamide were detected toward the two main hot molecular cores of Sgr B2(N)." (abstract)
- The recent tentative detection of propionamide toward a position East of Sgr B2(N1) by Li et al. (2021) is not confirmed.  
  > "We did not detect propionamide either toward a position located to the east of the main hot core, thereby not confirming the recent claim of its interstellar detection toward this position." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sgr B2(N) is a spectral-line rich high-mass star-forming region where other amides (formamide, acetamide, N-methylformamide) have been detected, and Sgr B2(N1S) has lower continuum opacity allowing a deeper look into the molecular content.  
  > "The new measurements and analyses presented here provided precise laboratory information to search for this amide in the high-mass star-forming region Sgr B2(N), a possible source of large peptide molecules." (Introduction); "With its location about 1$\ensuremath{''}$ to the South of the main hot core Sgr B2(N1), its continuum emission has a lower opacity, which allows us to look deeper into the molecular content of Sgr B2(N1)." (Observations)
- Why this method: LTE modeling is appropriate because of the high densities characterizing Sgr B2(N)'s hot cores, and automatic pixel-intensity distribution baseline removal handles line-confusion better than manual channel selection.  
  > "We modeled the spectra of Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2) with the software Weeds (cite: Maret11) under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), which is appropriate given the high densities that characterize Sgr B2(N)’s hot cores (cite: Bonfand19)." (Observations); "The automatic algorithm that we used to fit the continuum level for each pixel of the data cube relies on the distribution of intensities of each pixel and removes a zeroth-order baseline (cite: Belloche2019)." (Nondetection of propionamide)
- Alternative: Manual first-order baseline subtraction using 3-5 groups of channels (as done by Li et al. 2021) was contrasted with the authors' automatic zeroth-order pixel intensity distribution baseline removal, noting manual selection in line-confused spectra leads to different continuum levels.  
  > "In contrast, (cite: Li2021) fitted first-order baselines after a manual selection of 3–5 groups of channels that seemed to be free of line emission. These different approaches can lead in some cases to different fitted continuum levels." (Nondetection of propionamide)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Perturbations in the v24=3, v24=4, v23=1, and v17=1 excited vibrational states of syn-acrylamide could not be treated within the semi-rigid rotor Hamiltonian.  
  > "Many lines for these states could be confidently assigned using the Loomis–Wood-type plots but could not be treated in the scope of the semi-rigid rotor Hamiltonian due to clear evidences of perturbations." (Syn conformer)
- Limitation (authors): Perturbation contributions in the skew conformer of acrylamide may be treated incompletely due to high spectral density and line blending.  
  > "It is therefore possible that the perturbation contributions are treated incompletely." (Skew conformer)
- Limitation (authors): Continuum level determination in spectra close to the confusion limit is difficult and introduces uncertainty in the 3-sigma detection threshold.  
  > "Second, and more importantly, the determination of the continuum level is notoriously difficult in spectra close to the confusion limit." (Nondetection of propionamide); "This illustrates the additional uncertainty that arises from the continuum fitting and affects the position of the level used to assess the significance of a line detection (e.g., the $3\sigma$ level in the figures shown in Appendix [8](#a:spectra))." (Nondetection of propionamide)
- Limitation (authors): Astrochemical proxy ratios do not include hot-stage formation/destruction, binding energy differences, or spatial variations after thermal desorption.  
  > "We note, however, that the ratios from the models do not consider any formation or destruction that could occur during the hot stage, nor do they take account of differences in binding energies or any associated variations in spatial arrangement in the gas phase, following thermal release from the grains." (Formation mechanisms for acrylamide and related species)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits on column densities depend on the subjective visual comparison against the 3-sigma noise level and the completeness of the background molecular model in a highly line-confused spectrum.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming identical source size, rotational temperature, and linewidth as acetamide for undetected species is standard but introduces systematic uncertainty if spatial distributions or excitation conditions differ.
