3. **Measure sub-millimetre source size in the uv plane**: Extract signal amplitude vs uv distance from each ALMA tracer using GILDAS scripts, scale uv-distances to a common frequency, marginalize over normalization, and fit circular Gaussian models  
  > "This procedure uses GILDAS-based (<http://www.iram.fr/IRAMFR/GILDAS>) scripts to extract the signal amplitude as a function of the *uv* distance from each tracer. (Size measurement); "The size and its $1 \sigma$ uncertainty are determined by comparing the *uv* distance vs. amplitude distribution to circular Gaussian models." (Size measurement)

   - parameter: R_eff = FWHM / 2; resolved threshold P_unres <= 10%  
  > "The galaxy best-fit size is defined as the effective radius R$_{\rm eff}$ = FWHM/2." (Size measurement); "A source is considered to be resolved when P$_{\rm unres} \leqslant 10 \%$." (Size measurement)

  > "The size and its $1 \sigma$ uncertainty are determined by comparing the *uv* distance vs. amplitude distribution to circular Gaussian models." (Size measurement)

  > "The goodness of the fit is estimated from the $\chi^2$ minimization as a function of the size." (Size measurement)

(Spectral analysis)

\end{equation}$$" (Mass outflow rate computation); "We use a \[OII\] emissivity j$_{\rm [OII]} = 1.641 \times 10^{-21} \ {\rm erg s^{-1} cm^{-3}}$ as derived with the TEMDEN package in IRAF at T$_{\rm e} = 10^4 \ {\rm K}$ and n$_{\rm e} = 300 \ {\rm cm^{-3}}$." \end{equation}$$" (Mass outflow rate computation); "We thus define R$_{\rm OF} =$ R$_{\rm eff, ALMA} = 1.54 \ {\rm kpc}$." (Mass outflow rate computation)

  > "We use a \[OII\] emissivity j$_{\rm [OII]} = 1.641 \times 10^{-21} \ {\rm erg s^{-1} cm^{-3}}$ as derived with the TEMDEN package in IRAF at T$_{\rm e} = 10^4 \ {\rm K}$ and n$_{\rm e} = 300 \ {\rm cm^{-3}}$." (Mass outflow rate computation)

## Qualitative findings

- A broad, redshifted emission component is detected in CO(2-1), CO(5-4), [CI](2-1), and [OII]3726,3729 with consistent kinematics, tracing 46 +/- 13% of the total molecular gas mass being ejected from ID2299.  
  > "Here we present a massive starburst galaxy at $z=1.4$ which is ejecting $46 \pm 13$% of its molecular gas mass at a startling rate of $\gtrsim 10,000$ M$_{\odot}{\rm yr}^{-1}$." (Abstract); "The remarkable consistency of the kinematics in the ionized and molecular phases implies that these are tracing different phases of the same phenomenon." (A disruptive event in a massive galaxy at z=1.4)
- The ejection event is not driven by AGN or starburst feedback, nor is it a fossil outflow or a second merging galaxy, but is most plausibly a merger-induced tidal ejection.  
  > "However, our observations provide compelling evidence that this is not a feedback-driven wind, but rather material from a merger that has been probably tidally ejected." (Abstract); "We thus conclude that the ID2299 observations can be explained by an exceptional tidal ejection episode triggered by the merger." (Triggering mechanism of the disruptive event)
- The ejected gas has low CO excitation similar to high-redshift disks and is forming stars at 180 +/- 60 M_odot yr^-1 (30 +/- 11% of the total SFR), consistent with a tidal tail rather than a feedback-driven wind.  
  > "From the luminosity of the broad CO(5-4) component, we estimate that the ejected material is forming stars at a rate of SFR$_{\rm broad} = 180 \pm 60$ M$_{\odot} {\rm yr}^{-1}$. This corresponds to $30 \pm 11 \%$ of the total SFR derived from the far-IR bolometric luminosity of the dust." (Triggering mechanism of the disruptive event); "In addition, the molecular gas emission in the ejected component is significantly less excited than that of its host (Figure 6)." (Triggering mechanism of the disruptive event)
- With the remaining molecular gas depleted by star formation in ~40 Myr and the ejected gas unavailable for several hundred Myr, the galaxy is about to rapidly quench.  
  > "At the observed SFR, the gas remaining in the host would be consumed by star formation in $\tau_{\rm depl, SB} =$ M$_{\rm mol, n} /$ SFR $\sim 40$ Myr. Therefore, the residual material will be rapidly consumed long before the expelled gas could be re-accreted." (Impact on the host galaxy)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiple low- and high-J CO transitions (CO(2-1), CO(5-4), CO(7-6)) and [CI](2-1) together with rest-frame optical [OII] spectroscopy simultaneously constrain the cold molecular gas mass, dense star-forming gas, CO excitation SLED, and warm ionized phase of the outflow.  
  > "A broad component that is red-shifted from the galaxy emission is detected in four (low- and high-J) CO and \[CI\] transitions and in the ionized phase, which ensures a robust estimate of the expelled gas mass." (Abstract); "The CO(2-1) and \[CI\](2-1) transitions trace the bulk of the cold molecular gas reservoir while the CO(5-4) and CO(7-6) transitions sample the dense molecular gas." (A disruptive event in a massive galaxy at z=1.4)
- Why this method: Extracting spectra and subtracting narrow-line and continuum models directly in the uv plane accounts for the measured source size and minimizes contamination from the bright host galaxy when isolating the fainter broad component; adopting Fiore et al. (2017) outflow equations minimizes systematic differences when comparing to scaling relations.  
  > "To determine the spatial centroid and the physical extension of the broad emission while minimizing the contamination from the stronger galaxy emission we subtract the narrow components from the CO(2-1), CO(5-4) and \[CI\](2-1)+CO(7-6) data-cubes" (Imaging the broad component in the ALMA cubes); "In the assumption that the broad component is associated to a feedback-driven wind, we compute the mass outflow rate following the prescription adopted to compute the scaling relation in the left panel of Figure 4(cite: Fiore17). This is to minimize the effects of geometry assumptions and systematics when comparing with the literature results." (Mass outflow rate computation)
- Alternative: Feedback-driven wind from current AGN or starburst activity: rejected because the mass outflow rate is 1.5-2 orders of magnitude above empirical scaling relations and >= 2 orders of magnitude above hydrodynamical simulations of AGN winds.  
  > "This putative wind lies $\sim$ 2 (1.5) orders of magnitudes above literature scaling relations(cite: Fiore17; Fluetsch19) implying a wind that would carry $\sim$ 2 (1.5) orders of magnitudes more mass and energy than expected from the AGN (starburst) activity." (Triggering mechanism of the disruptive event); "We show in Fig. 5 that the mass outflow rate, total mass expelled, and fraction of ISM mass expelled from the parent galaxy predicted by the simulations are all at least two orders of magnitude higher in ID2299 than what AGN-feedback can produce" (Simulations of feedback-driven outflows)
- Alternative: Fossil outflow from a recently faded hyper-luminous AGN or extreme starburst: rejected because SFR >= 14000 M_odot yr^-1 exceeds the brightest proto-clusters, and a past AGN with L_bol ~ 10^47.8 erg s^-1 is disfavored by the lack of radio AGN emission (VLBA log L_radio <= 23.55) and the ~10^-9 Mpc^-3 space density of such QSOs.  
  > "However, the SFR $\geqslant 14000$ M$_{\odot}$yr$^{-1}$ implied by the scaling relation in the right panel of Figure 4 cannot be ascribed to a single galaxy, as this is larger than the SFR of the brightest proto-clusters in the Universe(cite: Negrello20)." (Triggering mechanism of the disruptive event); "The lack of radio AGN signatures at high and low resolution disfavours the possibility that the AGN hosted in ID2299 was as powerful as L$_{\rm bol} \geqslant 10^{47.8}$ erg/s in the past." (Rejecting the “fossil outflow” interpretation)
- Alternative: Cold accretion inflow from the cosmic web or galactic fountain: rejected because halos of M_halo ~ 10^12-10^13 M_odot at z = 1.4 have at most 10-50 M_odot yr^-1 cold accretion over ~50 kpc scales, and a fountain cannot contain half the galaxy's ISM on compact scales.  
  > "Even allowing for a substantial scatter in the stellar-to-halo mass ratio, and assume that the hosting halo mass is M$_{\rm halo} \sim 10^{12}$ M$_{\odot}$ we would expect cold accretion rates of $\dot{\rm M}_{\rm inf, gas} \sim (10 - 50)$ M$_{\odot}$yr$^{-1}$ spread over a $\sim 50$ kpc scale (cite: Goerdt10; Dekel13)." (Excluding inflow scenarios); "Similarly, assuming that this is a fountain-like event with previously ejected gas returning to the galaxy would appear implausible when considering that about half of the ISM of the system is present in the extended gas" (Excluding inflow scenarios)
- Alternative: Unresolved merging companion galaxy or kinematically perturbed disk: rejected because the implied dynamical mass (M_dyn,tot,b = 2.1 x 10^11 M_odot) exceeds the available baryonic mass in the ALMA area by ~0.85 dex (~7x) and the broad component has much lower CO excitation than the narrow component.  
  > "However, it is hard to reconcile the broad component of ID2299 with a kinematically perturbed disk since its excitation conditions are dramatically different than in the narrow component (Figure 6, see also Methods)" (Triggering mechanism of the disruptive event); "Conservatively assuming a 50% splitting between the two putative galaxies and adding to the gas mass, the total falls short by a factor of 0.85 $dex$ ($\sim 7\times$) with respect to M$_{\rm dyn, tot, b}$." (Arguments against a two-galaxy merging scenario)
- How they decided it was enough: A spectral scan accounting for 47 independent trials yielded a 1.7x10^-4 spurious probability for the CO(2-1) broad component (integrated SNR = 6.2), and independent detection of consistent broad kinematics in [OII] (>15sigma), CO(5-4) (3.5sigma), and [CI](2-1) (2.7sigma) reduced the chance of a spurious detection to essentially zero.  
  > "We thus conclude that the CO(2-1) spectrum requires two components with high confidence (spurious probability at the level of $1.7\times10^{-4}$)." (Significance of the broad component detection in the sub-millimetre); "We note that the broad component parameters derived from the CO(2-1) fit are consistent with those of the \[OII\] emission. The chances of obtaining a spurious multiple detection with consistent kinematics are basically zero." (Significance of the broad component detection in the sub-millimetre)
- Limitation (authors): The physical size and geometry of the broad emission cannot be directly constrained because of the low SNR of the broad component detection.  
  > "We note that we cannot estimate a physical size for the broad emission because of the low SNR of the detection." (Imaging the broad component in the ALMA cubes)
- Limitation (authors): The cosmic rate of disruptive events is derived from a single object and therefore carries a large Poissonian uncertainty of ~0.6 dex.  
  > "The error associated to this measurement is $\sim 0.6 \ {\rm dex}$ reflecting the large uncertainty associated with a single event observation." (Frequency of disruptive events and connection with the quenched galaxy population)
- Limitation (authors): Ionized gas mass and outflow rate estimates are subject to large uncertainties from assumed electron density, metallicity, and lack of dust attenuation correction.  
  > "Since the molecular gas substantially dominates the mass outflow rate and because the physical quantities associated to the ionized gas are affected by large uncertainties due to metallicity, electron density and dust attenuation assumptions, we refer only to the molecular phase of the gas through our discussion in the main text." (Mass outflow rate computation)
- Limitation (authors): Inclination of the outflow and galaxy is unknown.  
  > "The range of outflow velocities (likely a lower limit to the intrinsic distribution of ejection velocities because of the unknown inclination) reaches higher than the galaxy escape velocity" (Impact on the host galaxy)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The broad component in CO(5-4) and [CI](2-1) is not fitted independently; its velocity centroid and FWHM are fixed to the CO(2-1) best-fit values.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The spatial extent of the broad component is unresolved at the current ALMA resolution (~0.7''-1.5''), so R_OF = 1.54 kpc is assumed equal to the narrow-component effective radius rather than directly measured as a resolved tidal tail.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adopting a ULIRG-like alpha_CO = 0.8 for the broad component is in tension with its low-excitation disk-like SLED (which suggests alpha_CO = 3.6), although the authors note this makes their ejected mass fraction conservative.
