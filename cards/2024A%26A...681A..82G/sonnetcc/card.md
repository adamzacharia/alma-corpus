## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and self-calibrate the ALMA data**: ALMA-staff calibration restored by allegro, then per-spw phase (and limited amplitude) self-calibration with solution intervals inf, 60s, 30s  
  > "The calibration of the data was performed by the ALMA staff and was restored by the allegro team at Leiden University." (Observations); "Self-calibration techniques were employed for each spectral window individually using solution intervals of inf, 60s and 30s." (Observations)
   - software: CASA 5.7.0
   - parameter: Band 4 short-baseline SNR 88 to 380; Band 6 short-baseline SNR 61 to 69  
  > "significant improvements for Band 4 data (signal-to-noise ratio, from 88 to 380)" (Observations)
   - assumption: Flux calibration errors set to nominal 5% at Bands 4 and 6  
  > "The flux calibration errors are set to the nominal values of 5$\%$ at Bands 4, 6." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SNR improvement after self-calibration  
  > "In any case, sufficient self-calibration solutions were found, leading to enhanced data quality." (Observations)
2. **Make continuum images at common resolution**: tclean with MTMFS (nterms=2) and multiscale, Briggs weighting, Band 4 uv-taper, convolution of Band 4 and 6 to 78 mas, plus VANDAM VLA 9.1 mm image  
  > "we used the MTMFS deconvolver (cite: 2011A&A...532A..71R) with nterms=2" (Observations); "Both, the Band 4 and Band 6 images were convolved to have the same 78 (milliarcsecond) beam." (Observations)
   - software: CASA tclean
   - parameter: scales 0, 10, 30, 50 pixels; pixel 0.003 arcsec (ALMA), 0.01 arcsec (VLA); Band 4 uvtaper 0.058 arcsec  
  > "scales of 0, 10, 30, and 50 times the pixel size" (Observations); "A smooth tapering was applied by setting *uvtaper* to 0.058" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Several Robust values explored; Briggs chosen as best compromise between sensitivity and resolution  
  > "it provided the best compromised between sensitivity and resolution, and several Robust values were explored when making the final images." (Observations)
3. **Extract radial brightness-temperature and spectral-index profiles**: Average emission in elliptical rings for A1 and A2; brightness temperature from the full Planck equation; spectral index profiles 9.1-2.1 mm and 2.1-1.2 mm  
  > "The radial profiles from these images were obtained by averaging the emission in elliptical rings for both sources" (Results); "two additional radial profiles were generated, representing the spectral indices between 9.1 mm and 2.1 mm, as well as between 2.1 mm and 1.2 mm." (Results)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: A1 centre from a Gaussian fit with imfit; A2 centre at peak emission  
  > "the central position of the radial profiles was determined by a Gaussian fit using *imfit*." (Results); "the central position of the radial profile of A2 was determined based on the peak emission in the respective images" (Results)
   - assumption: A1 i = 20 deg, PA = 96 deg; A2 i = 14 deg, PA = 122 deg (from outflows)  
  > "the inclination and position angle values were set to 20 degrees and 96 degrees" (Results); "for A2 we took the inclination to be 14 degrees (cite: 2016ApJ...819..159C)" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with CALYPSO spectral indices of Galametz et al.  
  > "This is in agreement with our spectral index values of the IRAS4A1 inner regions." (Results)
4. **Fit dust temperature, surface density and maximum grain size per radius for A1**: Bayesian grid comparison of observed azimuthally averaged intensities at three wavelengths with a scattering-including slab model; expected values from marginalised posteriors  
  > "A Bayesian approach was employed to obtain the posterior probability distributions of the model parameters" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis); "a model grid of intensities was created using various dust parameters." (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - parameter: a_max 0.001-10 cm, T_dust 0.1-250 K, Sigma_dust 0.1-1000 g cm^-2  
  > "($a_{max}$ from 0.001 - 10 cm, $T_{dust}$ from 0.1 - 250 K, $\Sigma_{dust}$ from 0.1 - 1000 $gcm^{-2}$)" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - parameter: Uncertainty combines error of the mean and flux calibration error at each frequency  
  > "where $\sigma^{2}_{\bar{I,i}}$ is the error of the mean, obtained from the azimuthally averaged intensity profiles" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - assumption: Emission originates primarily from a disk rather than the envelope  
  > "we will adopt the hypothesis that the emission detected in our high-resolution images originates primarily from a disk rather than the envelope." (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - assumption: Power-law size distribution n(a) proportional to a^-p with p = 3.5  
  > "where p is commonly assumed to be 3.5 according to measurements of the ISM" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - assumption: DSHARP composition, no porosity: 20% water, 32.91% silicates, 7.43% troilite, 39.66% refractory organics  
  > "which considers particles without porosity and a composition of 20 $\%$ water fraction by mass, 32.91 $\%$ astronomical silicates, 7.43 $\%$ troilite, and 39.66 $\%$ refractory organics." (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - assumption: Single temperature at each radius; isotropic scattering with effective scattering coefficient  
  > "this model assumes a single temperature at each radius within the disk." (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis); "for these equations isotropic scattering is assumed" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - fitting: Standard log-normal likelihood; expected value of each parameter computed over the grid posterior  
  > "a standard log-normal likelihood function was used, which is defined as follows:" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis); "The expected value of each parameter was obtained by:" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
   - validation: Observed intensities compared with model intensities from the fitted parameters; multiple near-degenerate solutions noted  
  > "Figure 3 shows the dust parameters, the optical depths at each wavelength, and the intensities of the observations compared with the ones obtained from the model." (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis); "Multiple equally likely solutions close to each other were found for the A1 source" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
5. **Generic gap models showing substructure disappearing with scale height**: RADMC-3D HD163296-inspired disk with two gaps at 49 and 86 au, scale height H_p = H_100 (r/100 au)^(1+Psi) increased until substructures disappear  
  > "The scale height parameter was increased in the generic gap models until the substructures disappeared due to shadowing, obscuration, and/or contrast effects." (3.2 Generic gap models); "The positions of the two most prominent gaps were taken to be 49 and 86 au with a fixed width of 10 and 8 au, respectively." (3.2 Generic gap models)
   - software: RADMC-3D, optool
   - parameter: Psi = 0.14; disk size 110 au; dust mass 0.039 Msun; i = 46.7 deg, PA = 133.3 deg  
  > "where $\Psi$ is the flaring index, with a predefined value 0.14" (3.2 Generic gap models); "In addition, a size of 110 au for the disk was chosen." (3.2 Generic gap models)
   - assumption: Stellar parameters of HD163296: 2.04 Msun, 17 Lsun, 9332 K, 101 pc; DSHARP opacities via optool  
  > "To incorporate the DSHARP dust particle opacities, the optool software (cite: 2021ascl.soft04010D) was utilized" (3.2 Generic gap models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative: image and major/minor-axis cuts versus scale height

6. **IRAS4A1-specific flared-disk models reproducing the 1.2 mm asymmetry**: Nine RADMC-3D models: base H/R = 0.3, three fixed scale heights, three with high flaring index Psi = 1.3, two with reduced gap widths; compare normalised major/minor-axis cuts with observed 1.2 mm image  
  > "The scale height in the RADMC-3D models for the IRAS4A1 disk was initially set to H/R = 0.3" (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1); "three with a fixed high flaring index ($\Psi$ = 1.3), and two models with reduced gap widths." (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: i = 20 deg, PA = 99 deg; dust mass 0.11 (+0.08/-0.04) Msun from the multi-wavelength analysis  
  > "The inclination and position angle of the disk were fixed at 20° and 99°, respectively." (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1); "The dust mass in the disk was taken from the multi-wavelength analysis" (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1)
   - assumption: Stellar properties from average Class I values: 1.55 Msun, 2.1 Rsun, 3700 K  
  > "These average values include the stellar mass (1.55 $M_{\odot}$), radius (2.1 $R_{\odot}$), and effective temperature (3700 K)." (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1)

## Qualitative findings

- Faint emission lies between and around A1 and A2, evident at 2.1 and 1.2 mm but not at 9.1 mm  
  > "There is, however, additional faint emission observed between and surrounding A1 and A2" (Results)
- A distinct asymmetry is seen in A1 at 1.2 mm but not at 2.1 or 9.1 mm  
  > "a distinct asymmetry is observed for A1 in the 1.2 mm image, which is not apparent in the 2.1 mm and 9.1 mm images." (Results)
- No additional compact objects found in the field of view  
  > "no additional compact objects, such as low-mass companions or distant galaxies, have been detected within the field of view" (Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The data are comparable in resolution, time and rms to the HL Tau long-baseline data designed to find substructures  
  > "The data for HL Tau was specifically designed to search for substructures, a goal that was also intended for the observation of IRAS4A1." (Observations)
- Why this method: Radiative transfer modelling used to test whether a flared disk with large scale height explains the asymmetry and missing substructure  
  > "To test this assumption, a model of the dust continuum emission at 1.2 mm was constructed using RADMC-3D." (3.2 Generic gap models)
- Alternative: Dust self-scattering, rather than a flared disk, may affect the inner A1 disk and the low spectral index  
  > "Furthermore, dust self-scattering might be affecting the inner regions of the IRAS4A1 disk." (Results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Simple model may not capture a Class 0 object; inclination and position angle strongly affect the asymmetry  
  > "the simplicity of the model employed in this study may limit its ability to reproduce accurately the complexities of a Class 0 young stellar object like IRAS4A1." (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1); "The inclination and position angle in the models greatly influence the resulting asymmetry" (3.3 Large scale height and very flared disk models of IRAS4A1)
- Limitation (authors): High resolution may resolve out the extended envelope; envelope emission may still contribute  
  > "our high-resolution image might be causing the extended component of the envelope to be resolved out" (Results); "Separating the continuum from the envelope is challenging" (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Single-temperature slab assumption may fail for envelope or flared-disk emission from different layers  
  > "In cases where the emission is originating from an envelope or a flared disk involving different layers, however, this assumption may not be valid." (3.1 Multi-wavelength analysis)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Band-to-wavelength labels are swapped in the Observations text (Band 4 given as 1.2 mm, Band 6 as 2.1 mm), against the tables and later text
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Models are normalised and compared by eye; no quantitative goodness of fit for the RADMC-3D profiles
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust-parameter fit assumes a single-temperature isothermal slab while also arguing the disk is flared, which is internally inconsistent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar parameters for the IRAS4A1 models are averages of Class I sources, not measured for A1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Envelope contribution and free-free emission in the VLA image are not removed and may bias the three-band fit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination (20 deg) from the outflow is low, so the asymmetry interpretation is sensitive to geometry
