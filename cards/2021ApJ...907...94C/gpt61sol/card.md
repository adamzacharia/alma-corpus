## Analysis recipe

1. **Produce continuum and polarization mosaics**: Phase-only self-calibration; primary-beam correction; pixel-by-pixel polarized-intensity debiasing; plot final maps with APLpy  
  > "We imaged both mosaics by performing phase-only self-calibration with a final solution interval of 90 s." (OBSERVATIONS); "All images were primary-beam corrected, and the polarized intensity image was debiased pixel-by-pixel (cite: Wardle1974; Hull2015)." (OBSERVATIONS); "The final maps were produced using the Python-based *APLpy* plotting package (cite: Robitaille2012)." (OBSERVATIONS)
   - software: CASA, APLpy
   - parameter: Rectangular mosaic; factor 2 over-sampling relative to Nyquist along both axes; factor 4 total beam overlap  
  > "The observations were configured as a rectangular, super-sampled mosaic with a factor of 2 over-sampling (with respect to Nyquist) along both axes, or a factor of 4 in total beam overlap." (OBSERVATIONS)
   - parameter: 3.1 mm: ~2.20 × 1.75 arcmin, 15 pointings; 1.3 mm: ~1.25 × 0.94 arcmin, 45 pointings  
  > "The mosaics cover regions of $\sim$ $2.20^{\prime}\,\times\,1.75^{\prime}$ (15 pointings) at 3.1 mm and $\sim$ $1.25^{\prime}\,\times\,0.94^{\prime}$ (45 pointings) at 1.3 mm" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - parameter: Final phase-only self-calibration solution interval: 90 s  
  > "a final solution interval of 90 s." (OBSERVATIONS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Recovery of all previously detected continuum sources  
  > "We recover all of the continuum sources previously detected by others (cite: Eisner2006; Friedel2011; Hirota2015)." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
2. **Estimate column densities and total masses**: Estimate N_H2 independently from each continuum band using S_nu/[B_nu(T_dust) Omega kappa_nu mu m_H]; obtain total masses using map pixels  
  > "We estimate the column density independently from the 3.1 and 1.3 mm data by assuming optically thin dust emission:" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL); "N_{\mathrm{H}_{2}} = \frac{S_{\nu}}{B_{\nu}(T_\textrm{dust}) \Omega \kappa_{\nu} \mu m_{\mathrm{H}}}" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL); "By using all the pixels in the two maps shown in Figure [1](#fig:stokesI), we obtained total masses of 35 and 88.2 M$_{\odot}$" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - parameter: Omega = pi/(4.0 log 2) b_maj b_min  
  > "The area is given by the solid angle, calculated as $\Omega=\pi/(4.0\log{2})\,b_\textrm{maj}\,b_\textrm{min}$." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - parameter: Column density evaluated only in the filament region used for field-strength estimation  
  > "we calculate the column density only in the region of the filament relevant to the estimation of the magnetic field strength" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - parameter: Stokes I cutoffs: 900 microJy beam^-1 at 3 mm; 7.3 mJy beam^-1 at 1 mm  
  > "We obtained cutoffs for the 3 and 1 mm Stokes $I$ maps of 900 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ and 7.3 mJy beam$^{-1}$ respectively." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission  
  > "by assuming optically thin dust emission" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - assumption: kappa_nu = 0.01 cm^2 g^-1 at 1.3 mm; 2.6 × 10^-3 cm^2 g^-1 at 3 mm  
  > "The dust opacity $\kappa_{\nu}$ is set as 0.01 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 1.3 mm and $2.6\times 10^{-3}$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 3 mm (cite: Ossenkopf1994)." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100; mean molecular weight mu = 2.33  
  > "These values assume a gas-to-dust ratio of 100." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL); "We use $\mu = 2.33$ as the mean molecular weight of hydrogen" (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - assumption: T_dust = 20–50 K for column densities; 25 K average for total masses  
  > "Based on that work, we use a range for $T_\textrm{dust}$ of between 20 and 50 K." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL); "by using $T_\textrm{dust} = 25$ K as an average dust temperature." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Preliminary spectral index ~3 in the column-density region used to justify optical thinness  
  > "The optically thin assumption is justified, as preliminary spectral index estimations from these data suggest a spectral index of $\approx$ 3 in the region where we estimate the column density." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
3. **Infer the magnetic field morphology**: Rotate polarization position angles by 90 degrees; map the plane-of-sky field and extrapolate central field lines  
  > "Here we refer to the polarization position angle pattern, not to the magnetic field pattern; we obtain the latter by rotating the former by 90$^{\circ}$." (Grain alignment); "By extrapolating the field lines through the center of Orion-KL, we see that they intersect inside the $3^{\prime \prime}\,\times\,5^{\prime \prime}$ ellipse reported to be origin of the explosive CO outflow (cite: Bally2017)." (The polarized dust emission from Orion-KL)
   - assumption: Dust grains magnetically aligned by radiative torques  
  > "under the assumption that dust grains are magnetically aligned by radiative torques (cite: Lazarian2007)." (The polarized dust emission from Orion-KL)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent field pattern between wavelengths; agreement with previous coarser observations  
  > "The magnetic field pattern is consistent between both wavelengths." (The polarized dust emission from Orion-KL); "the derive magnetic field morphology is in agreement with previous observations done at coarser angular resolution and/or lower sensitivity (cite: Rao1998; Tang2010; Hull2014)." (The polarized dust emission from Orion-KL)
4. **Compare field orientations with CO streamers**: Overlay field orientations on literature red- and blueshifted CO moment 0 maps; apply RHT to identify and compare linear structures  
  > "The magnetic field is superposed over the $^{12}$CO$\,(J=2\rightarrow1)$ moment 0 maps of both the red- and blueshifted outflowing CO emission (cite: Bally2017)." (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation); "We applied this algorithm to the CO streamers to highlight the linear structures in the data and to correlate them with our polarization map." (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation)
   - software: Rolling Hough Transform
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Linear structures identified in both lobes; RHT confirms the dust–CO spatial anti-correlation  
  > "The linear structures in the CO streamers are clearly identified by the RHT algorithm in both the red- and blue-shifted lobes of the outflow" (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation); "The RHT maps also confirm the anti-correlation between the dust and CO emission that is seen to the West of the Orion-KL nebula" (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation)
5. **Estimate magnetic field strengths**: Apply three DCF variants in the NE filament; derive independent estimates for both bands; average field-strength estimates  
  > "we use three versions of the Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi method (DCF; (cite: Davis1951; Chandrasekhar1953)), as previously employed in (cite: Cortes2016; Cortes2019)." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "Instead, we use the NE region of the filament, where the field appears to be unperturbed by the explosion" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "Because the 3.1 and 1.3 mm data trace different length scales, we derive independent estimations of the field strength from each dataset" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: n = N/L; L = 2 arcsec  
  > "we assume a thin slab with a depth of half the region’s minor axis, or $n = N/L$ with $L = 2^{\prime \prime}$" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: Adopted NH3 line-width: 1.4 km s^-1  
  > "They obtained a value of 1.4 km s$^{-1}$ at the location of Orion-KL" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "we used that value for the line-width in our field-strength estimation." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: delta phi calculated directly from polarization data  
  > "The value of $\delta \phi$ is calculated from our polarization data directly." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: Average of Column 10 estimates; reported 1.3 mm average 9.4 mG  
  > "By averaging all of the estimates from Column 10 in Table [2](#table:B), we obtain an average strength at 1.3 mm value of $\left<B\right>=9.4$ mG." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Field-line perturbations caused by non-thermal turbulent motions; avoid the explosion-affected central region  
  > "The DCF technique assumes that the magnetic field lines are perturbed by non-thermal turbulent motions" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "we cannot directly estimate the field strength from the central region of Orion-KL that has been affected by the explosion." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Thin slab with depth half the region's minor axis  
  > "we assume a thin slab with a depth of half the region’s minor axis" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Adopted NH3 resolution approximately matches the region's minor axis; field estimate treated as an upper limit  
  > "Given that their resolution approximately matches the minor axis of our region of interest, we used that value for the line-width in our field-strength estimation." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "we consider this estimate as an upper limit of the magnetic field strength." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
6. **Compare gravitational, magnetic, and outflow energies**: Calculate E_W = GM^2/R and E_B = B^2 R^3/(12 pi); compare with the literature maximum outflow energy  
  > "a gravitational potential energy of E$_{W} = GM^{2}/R = 3.52 \times 10^{46}$ ergs" (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field); "E$_{\mathrm{B}}$ = $B^{2}R^{3}/12\pi \sim 2 \times 10^{45}$ erg, where we used a magnetic field strength of 9.4 mG." (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field); "the maximum energy estimated in the outflow, or $\sim 10^{48}$ erg (cite: Bally2017)." (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field)
   - parameter: B = 9.4 mG  
  > "we used a magnetic field strength of 9.4 mG." (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field)
   - assumption: Sphere of radius 12 arcsec (5000 au)  
  > "when considering a sphere of $12^{\prime \prime}$ (5000 au) in radius enclosing the region where the field appears perturbed." (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field)
   - assumption: Orion-KL complex mass ~100 M_sun from Bally2020  
  > "(cite: Bally2020) estimated a total mass for the Orion-KL complex of $\sim$ 100 M$_{\odot}$" (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- A U-shaped dust cavity surrounds the explosion center and may have been created by the explosive event  
  > "This cavity has a “U” shape and surrounds the center of the explosion; it is likely that it was created by the explosive event." (The total intensity mosaic of Orion-KL)
- Quasi-radial central field transitions smoothly to a quasi-perpendicular, hourglass-like morphology  
  > "The transition from the quasi-radial morphology into an orientation quasi-perpendicular to the filament is smooth in both the northern and southern parts of the filament." (The polarized dust emission from Orion-KL)
- Field and CO streamer orientations correlate, while dust polarization locations and CO streamers are spatially anti-correlated  
  > "Comparing these structures with the magnetic field, we can clearly see a correlation in the orientations, in particular as we move radially away from the center of the explosion." (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation); "the locations where we detect dust polarization seem to be anti-correlated with the locations of the CO streamers." (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation)
- Gas and magnetic field inferred to be strongly coupled  
  > "there is strong coupling between the gas and the magnetic field; under such a scenario, the gas and the magnetic field should move as a single fluid." (The magnetic Reynolds Number)
- H2 emission is attenuated beyond the proposed shock front along the NE filament  
  > "the H$_{2}$ emission appears significantly attenuated, in particular as we move NE along the main axis of the dust filament and away from the post-shock region" (A Shock in the Orion-KL Magnetic Field)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: EOC observations test off-axis instrumental polarization errors using an extended, highly polarized source  
  > "The primary goal of these EOC observations was to assess the effects of off-axis instrumental polarization errors in maps of an extended, highly polarized source of scientific interest." (OBSERVATIONS)
- Why this method: RHT identifies linear structures; DCF is restricted to the apparently unperturbed NE filament because central perturbations violate its assumptions  
  > "The RHT is a machine-vision algorithm that is well suited for the identification of linear structures in data" (The CO outflow and the magnetic field orientation); "the field lines perturbations are likely non-linear, which makes the assumptions in the DCF technique not applicable." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "Instead, we use the NE region of the filament, where the field appears to be unperturbed by the explosion" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
- Alternative: Classical Gold mechanical alignment disfavored because the expected polarization direction is opposite to the observed azimuthal pattern  
  > "(cite: Tang2010) discarded the classical Gold mechanism, arguing that the polarization pattern should be aligned with the flow; this is the opposite of the azimuthal polarization pattern that we see in Orion-KL." (Grain alignment)
- Alternative: Mechanical Alignment Torques disfavored by the spatial anti-correlation between CO and polarized dust  
  > "The second flavor was introduced by (cite: Hoang2018) and is called “Mechanical Alignment Torques” (or MATs)" (Grain alignment); "the CO and dust emission would need to be co-spatial for mechanical alignment to be plausible." (Grain alignment)
- Alternative: Gravity pulling field lines inward considered less likely than outward stretching by the outflow  
  > "it is more likely that magnetic field morphology is the result of the field being stretched outward by the outflow rather than being pulled inward by gravity." (Energetics comparison: outflow, gravity, and magnetic field)
- Alternative: Direct DCF field estimation in the central explosion-affected region rejected  
  > "we cannot directly estimate the field strength from the central region of Orion-KL that has been affected by the explosion." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "the field lines perturbations are likely non-linear, which makes the assumptions in the DCF technique not applicable." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): DCF variants omit finite-resolution and line-of-sight smoothing; reported field strength is an upper limit  
  > "these three flavors of the DCF method do not take into account the effect of finite angular resolution and integration along the line of sight" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "both of which tend to smooth the magnetic field and reduce angle dispersions" (Estimation of the magnetic field strength); "we consider this estimate as an upper limit of the magnetic field strength." (Estimation of the magnetic field strength)
- Limitation (authors): Shock type cannot be determined from these data alone  
  > "Unfortunately, from our data alone, we cannot conclude what type of shock is manifesting itself through the magnetic field morphology in Orion-KL." (A Shock in the Orion-KL Magnetic Field)
- Limitation (authors): Available one-dimensional shock models do not provide a suitable comparison  
  > "current models are only one-dimensional, with the field orthogonal to the shock propagation direction (cite: Pilipp1994); therefore, we do not have a model to which we can refer for comparison." (A Shock in the Orion-KL Magnetic Field)
- Limitation (authors): Electron fraction is most likely a lower limit because radiation may have increased electron density  
  > "This yields $X(e) \sim 10^{-7}$, which is most likely a lower limit, as the radiation field produced by the nearby stars and enhanced by the explosion might have increased the electron density." (The magnetic Reynolds Number)
- Limitation (authors): Proposed shock need not be spherically symmetric because propagation speed depends on ambient density  
  > "the shock does not have to be spherically symmetric because the shock speed will depend on the ambient density in which the perturbation is propagating." (A Shock in the Orion-KL Magnetic Field)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The NE DCF field is applied to the explosion-affected volume without a direct central field-strength measurement.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The two continuum bands sample different spatial scales; their differing masses require scrutiny of spatial filtering, apertures, and line contamination.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Orientation correlations and the proposed shock boundary lack a stated statistical estimator or uncertainty budget.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Reynolds-number appendix uses n_H in the equation but supplies n_H2, leaving the density convention ambiguous.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stated Alfvén speed, electron fraction, Reynolds number, and magnetic-energy normalization should be independently checked from the printed inputs.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The inferred shock speed depends on associating the morphology transition with propagation since the explosion.
