## Analysis recipe

1. **Build molecular gas surface density maps from CO**: CO moment 0 maps from PHANGS-ALMA (spirals) or CASA immoments with archive pipeline masks (NGC 6822, WLM), converted to Sigma_mol  
  > "We construct the integrated intensity map of CO (i.e., moment 0 map) using" (2.2.2 CO data); "using the mask cubes, provided by the ALMA archive, which are created at the last stage of cleaning during the pipeline" (2.2.2 CO data)
   - software: CASA immoments
   - parameter: alpha_CO(1-0) = 4.35 Msun pc-2 (K km/s)-1 for all galaxies  
  > "we assume a constant MW $\alpha_{\rm CO}^{1-0}$ of 4.35 $M_{\rm \odot}$pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ for the entire galaxy sample" (3.1 Molecular gas surface density)
   - parameter: R21^-1 = 0.65; CO(1-0) maps used directly for NGC 6822  
  > "We adopt $R_{21}^{-1}$ = 0.65, following the assumptions by (cite: denbrok2021new) for CO (2-1) observations" (3.1 Molecular gas surface density); "For the CO (1-0) moment maps (used for NGC 6822)" (3.1 Molecular gas surface density)
   - assumption: Milky Way CO-to-H2 conversion factor constant across metallicity  
  > "we assume a constant MW $\alpha_{\rm CO}^{1-0}$ of 4.35 $M_{\rm \odot}$pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ for the entire galaxy sample" (3.1 Molecular gas surface density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Smooth and resample Hi and ancillary maps to a common resolution**: Hi cubes smoothed to a symmetric Gaussian beam and resampled; other maps convolved to the Hi beam and resampled with SWARP  
  > "we perform image convolution and resampling to match the spatial resolution of all datasets to the coarsest resolution, which corresponds to the Hi beam size" (2.3 Convolution and resampling); "and resampled to have a pixel size of 1/3 to 1/4 of the beam size" (2.3 Convolution and resampling)
   - software: CASA imsmooth, SWARP, Aniano et al. convolution kernels
   - parameter: Hi pixel size 1/3 to 1/4 of the beam  
  > "and resampled to have a pixel size of 1/3 to 1/4 of the beam size" (2.3 Convolution and resampling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Decompose Hi line profiles into Gaussian components**: BAYGAUD-PI Bayesian MCMC multi-Gaussian decomposition plus polynomial baseline, with Bayes-factor selection of component number and SNR cuts  
  > "is a robust kinematic decomposition tool for Hi 21 cm data, employing the Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
   - software: BAYGAUD-PI
   - parameter: N_max = 4 for NGC 5068 and NGC 1566; 3 for the others  
  > "We set $N_{\rm max} = 4$ for NGC 5068 and NGC 1566; $N_{\rm max} = 3$ for the other galaxies." (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
   - parameter: Model selection: Bayes factor at least 10 times larger than second-best model  
  > "defined as having a Bayes factor at least 10 times larger than the second-best model" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
   - parameter: SNR_amp > 2 and SNR_area > 2 per component  
  > "SNR$_{\rm amp}$ ($> 2$) and SNR$_{\rm area}$ ($> 2$)" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
   - assumption: Polynomial baseline of order n fitted with the Gaussians  
  > "$b_{j}$ is the coefficient of the $n^{\rm th}$ order polynomial used for the baseline fit" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC; priors and sampler settings not stated  
  > "is a robust kinematic decomposition tool for Hi 21 cm data, employing the Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
   - validation: SNR cuts remove spurious components; profiles failing them are replaced by a single-Gaussian fit  
  > "These criteria help avoid including spurious low-amplitude components that could be fitted" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI); "we replace the multiple Gaussian fit results with the result from a single Gaussian fit (cite: park2022gas)" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
4. **Classify components as narrow (colder) or broad**: Components with N_Gauss>=2, not the broadest, sigma_V below 6 km/s and aligned with bulk motion are narrow; everything else is broad  
  > "For the remaining components, we apply a velocity dispersion threshold of 6  km s$^{-1}$, classifying those with velocity dispersions smaller than this value as colder components." (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components)
   - software: BAYGAUD-PI
   - parameter: sigma_V threshold = 6 km/s  
  > "For the remaining components, we apply a velocity dispersion threshold of 6  km s$^{-1}$, classifying those with velocity dispersions smaller than this value as colder components." (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components)
   - parameter: Narrow component centre must lie within mu_sg +/- sigma_sg of a forced single-Gaussian fit  
  > "Specifically, if a narrow component from a multi-Gaussian fit has a central velocity ($\mu_{i}$) outside the range $\mu_{\rm sg} \pm \sigma_{\rm sg}$, we exclude it from the colder Hi category and instead classify it as broad." (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compute Hi column density maps and narrow fraction f_n**: Hi column density from integrated brightness temperature; f_n = I_narrow/I_total  
  > "assuming optically thin conditions for Hi" (5.1 Narrow and Broad Hi Components); "where $I_{\rm narrow HI}$ and $I_{\rm total HI}$ are the intensity of narrow Hi and total Hi" (5.1 Narrow and Broad Hi Components)
   - assumption: Optically thin Hi  
  > "assuming optically thin conditions for Hi" (5.1 Narrow and Broad Hi Components)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive SFR surface density from FUV and WISE W4**: Hybrid FUV+W4 SFR with W1-dependent W4 coefficient following Belfiore et al.; background subtraction and foreground-star masking of images first  
  > "We map out the SFR of galaxies at 100 Myr timescale, traced by the FUV band" (3.2 Star formation rate (SFR)); "we follow the (cite: belfiore2023calibration) method to take into account the possible overestimate of the standard hybrid SFR" (3.2 Star formation rate (SFR))
   - software: photutils.background, PTS, SKIRT toolkit
   - parameter: log10 C_FUV = -43.42  
  > "is -43.42 (cite: leroy2019z)" (3.2 Star formation rate (SFR))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive gas-phase metallicity maps from TYPHOON IFS**: lzifu emission-line fits, Balmer-decrement dust correction, BPT star-forming selection, Scal calibration  
  > "we use the Scal metallicity diagnostic (cite: pilyugin2016new)" (3.3.2 Oxygen abundance); "we adopt the (cite: kauffmann2003host) line and classify pixels below this line as star-forming regions" (3.3.1 BPT diagram)
   - software: lzifu
   - assumption: R(V) = 3.1 MW extinction curve  
  > "We assume $R(V) = 3.1$, based on the average MW extinction curve" (2.2.1 IFS - the TYPHOON survey)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Cross-correlate Hi components with molecular gas and SFR**: Correlation coefficient between apodised 2D power spectra of narrow and broad Hi maps and Sigma_mol or Sigma_SFR as a function of spatial scale k  
  > "we perform a cross-correlation analysis to identify the spatial scale ($k$) where the correlations are strongest" (5.2 Correlation at different spatial scale)
   - parameter: Cosine apodisation kernel, central 70% of image untapered  
  > "we use a cosine kernel with a rectangular boundary following (cite: blagrave2017ghigls) that smoothly tapers the edges (excluding the central 70% of the image) along both the x- and y-axes" (5.2 Correlation at different spatial scale)
   - parameter: Hi maps cropped to CO or SFR field of view  
  > "we crop the narrow and broad Hi gas maps to match the FoV of the CO observations" (5.2 Correlation at different spatial scale)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Radially binned f_n versus metallicity and SFR**: Equal-count radial bins; median with 16th and 84th percentiles compared with 12+log(O/H) and Sigma_SFR  
  > "The $f_{\rm n}$ values are binned radially, with each bin containing an equal number of regions" (6.1 Radial trend); "We take the median value and the corresponding 16% and 84% percentile of the values within the radial bin." (6.1 Radial trend)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
10. **Test the effect of physical resolution on median f_n**: Degrade Hi cubes of Sextans A, NGC 7793 and WLM, rerun BAYGAUD-PI, bootstrap the median and scatter  
  > "degrading the spatial resolution of Hi data cubes of three galaxies with a different range of inclination (Sextans A, NGC 7793, and WLM)" (6.2 Limitations of this study); "From 10,000 bootstrap samples of the median and scatter" (6.2 Limitations of this study)
   - software: CASA imsmooth, CASA imrebin, BAYGAUD-PI
   - parameter: 10,000 bootstrap samples  
  > "From 10,000 bootstrap samples of the median and scatter" (6.2 Limitations of this study)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Software

- **CASA immoments** : Moment 0 maps of ALMA CO cubes using archive mask cubes  
  > "We construct the integrated intensity map of CO (i.e., moment 0 map) using" (2.2.2 CO data)
- **CASA imsmooth** : Convolving CO maps and degrading Hi cubes  
  > "assuming the Gaussian shape of the beam of original data cubes" (2.3 Convolution and resampling)
- **CASA imrebin** : Rebinning Hi cubes in the resolution test  
  > "degrading the spatial resolution of Hi data cubes of three galaxies with a different range of inclination (Sextans A, NGC 7793, and WLM)" (6.2 Limitations of this study)
- **BAYGAUD-PI** : Bayesian MCMC Gaussian decomposition of Hi cubes  
  > "is a robust kinematic decomposition tool for Hi 21 cm data, employing the Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach" (4.1 BAYGAUD-PI)
- **lzifu** : Emission line fitting of TYPHOON spectra  
  > "fits a single, kinematically-aligned Gaussian to the emission lines and calculates their flux and error" (2.2.1 IFS - the TYPHOON survey)
- **SWARP** : Resampling smoothed maps to the Hi grid  
  > "Finally, we resample the smoothed data to the Hi beam size using" (2.3 Convolution and resampling)
- **photutils.background** : Background estimation and subtraction in photometric images  
  > "we construct a background image for each band separately by estimating the background level and small-scale fluctuations using the Python package" (2.2.3 Photometric data)
- **PTS (for SKIRT)** PTS-7/8: Foreground star masks for GALEX FUV and WISE images  
  > "software, a Python toolkit for the SKIRT radiative transfer code" (2.2.3 Photometric data)
- **IDL sky.pro** : Sky scatter and sky aperture area for photometric uncertainty  
  > "derived using the IDL tool" (2.2.3 Photometric data)
- **Aniano et al. convolution kernels** : Matching GALEX and WISE PSFs to the Hi beam  
  > "we use the kernel sets from (cite: aniano2011common)" (2.3 Convolution and resampling)
- **Astropy** : General analysis (Python package)  
  > "a community-developed core Python package and an ecosystem of tools and resources for astronomy" (Acknowledgements)
- **Numpy, Matplotlib, SciPy** : General analysis and plotting  
  > "This work also used Numpy ((cite: numpy)), Matplotlib ((cite: matplotlib)), and Scipy ((cite: scipy))." (Acknowledgements)

## Qualitative findings

- Narrow Hi is clumpier and more filamentary than broad Hi  
  > "The colder, or narrow, Hi components show a clumpier and more filamentary distribution compared to the broad Hi components." (7 Conclusion)
- Multiple Hi components occur in star-forming regions and spiral arms; diffuse gas is mostly single-component  
  > "Multiple Hi kinematic components are preferentially found in star-forming regions and spiral arms of galaxies, whereas diffuse gas is more commonly observed with a single Hi component." (7 Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sample required IFS, CO (not strictly for dwarfs), FUV/NIR/MIR images and Hi cubes with spectral resolution better than 4 km/s  
  > "Hi 21 cm data with a spectral resolution, better than 4 km s$^{-1}$, to enable finding colder Hi components" (2.1 Sample galaxies)
- Why this method: A consistent decomposition across galaxies of different metallicity was needed  
  > "it is necessary to apply a consistent method for distinguishing kinematically distinct components for multiple galaxies, across a wide range of metallicity" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: A 2 km/s dispersion threshold for cold Hi was not used because of channel resolution  
  > "our ability to adopt 2 km s$^{-1}$ as the defining threshold for cold components is limited by the channel resolution of our Hi data" (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components)
- Alternative: Spatially coherent decomposition (as in ROHSA) not adopted  
  > "This issue can be mitigated by incorporating spatial coherence when decomposing velocity profiles into multiple Gaussians" (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components); "However, such an approach is beyond the scope of this study." (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Pixel-to-pixel discontinuities in the decomposition can miss colder Hi  
  > "We find some discontinuities in the Gaussian decomposition results between adjacent pixels, occasionally missing colder Hi components" (4.2 Classification of narrow/broad Hi components)
- Limitation (authors): Limited CO field of view prevents direct comparison in some galaxies  
  > "Since CO observations are only available for the inner region, a direct spatial comparison between narrow Hi and molecular gas content is not feasible." (5.1 Narrow and Broad Hi Components)
- Limitation (authors): Effect of inclination is inconclusive  
  > "As a result, the impact of inclination remains inconclusive in this study." (6.2 Limitations of this study)
- Limitation (authors): Effect of sensitivity is inconclusive  
  > "it is inconclusive from the figure whether sensitivity influences our ability to identify colder Hi components" (6.2 Limitations of this study)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Seven galaxies with heterogeneous Hi datasets (VLA, ATCA, MeerKAT) confound resolution, sensitivity and galaxy type
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Constant Milky Way alpha_CO is unlikely to hold in the metal-poor dwarfs where the key CO comparison is made
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 6 km/s threshold and bulk-motion cut are not tested against alternatives or simulations
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlations are mostly low (between -0.5 and 0.5) with no significance estimates
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA CO is a minor input and its data quality and sensitivity are not discussed

## Reproducibility

- Data public (paper): CO cubes downloaded from the ALMA archive; TYPHOON cubes not yet public  
  > "The reduced, primary beam-corrected data cubes were downloaded from the ALMA data archive" (2.2.2 CO data); "The TYPHOON optical IFS datacubes will be made publicly available in a forthcoming release by Seibert et al. (in prep.) through Data Central" (Data Availability)
- Products in archive: Archive reduced cubes and pipeline mask cubes used  
  > "using the mask cubes, provided by the ALMA archive, which are created at the last stage of cleaning during the pipeline" (2.2.2 CO data)
- Missing: TYPHOON IFS cubes not public (emission-line products only on request)
- Missing: BAYGAUD-PI priors and sampler settings
- Missing: Tabulated numeric f_n, Sigma_mol or cross-correlation values (results shown mainly as figures)
- Missing: Mapping of ALMA project codes to galaxies and product versions
- Data volume (inferred): Several to tens of GB for the NGC 6822 and WLM ALMA 7m cubes plus Hi cubes; PHANGS moment maps are small; TYPHOON cubes unavailable
