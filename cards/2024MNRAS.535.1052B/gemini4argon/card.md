## Analysis recipe

1. **Map radio continuum and mid-IR [S IV] emission of ionized gas**: Clean and image JVLA 15 and 22 GHz continuum data with natural and Briggs (robust = 0) weighting; scan Gemini North TEXES slit across target to build [S IV] 10.5 um spectral cube; collapse cube and extract spectrum and 2-Gaussian fit.  
  > "The data were cleaned and imaged with CASA and AIPS. The galaxy was mapped with natural weighting for greatest sensitivity and Briggs weighting (robust = 0) for higher resolution." (2.1 Radio Continuum Maps); "The slit was aligned north-south and stepped east-west across the targets and the scan combined into a data cube of 23 0.25 [′′] pixels in R.A., 32 0.14 [′′] in Dec, and 256 0.95 km s [−][1] wide velocity channels." (2.2 Mid-Infrared Spectroscopy with TEXES); "The line profile could be formally fit by 2 gaussians at ∼ 744 , 775 km s [−][1] consistent with the IRTF results" (4.1 Ionized Gas:Double Peak or Extended Wing?)
   - software: JVLA pipeline 4.7.1, CASA, AIPS
   - parameter: robust = 0 (Briggs weighting) and natural weighting for JVLA maps  
  > "The galaxy was mapped with natural weighting for greatest sensitivity and Briggs weighting (robust = 0) for higher resolution." (2.1 Radio Continuum Maps)
   - parameter: TEXES cube: 23 x 0.25 arcsec pixels in RA, 32 x 0.14 arcsec pixels in Dec, 256 x 0.95 km s^-1 channels  
  > "the scan combined into a data cube of 23 0.25 [′′] pixels in R.A., 32 0.14 [′′] in Dec, and 256 0.95 km s [−][1] wide velocity channels." (2.2 Mid-Infrared Spectroscopy with TEXES)
   - assumption: T_e ~ 10^4 K giving S^+++ thermal broadening of ~3.5 km s^-1  
  > "Thermal broadening in an H ii region at 𝑇 e ∼ 10 [4] K will give any hydrogen line FWHM about 20 km s [−][1]" (2.2 Mid-Infrared Spectroscopy with TEXES); "The 𝑆 [+++] thermal broadening of ≈ 3 . 5 km s [−][1], added in quadrature to the instrumental resolution, gives the true resolution of ≈ 4 . 5 km s [−][1] ." (2.2 Mid-Infrared Spectroscopy with TEXES)
   - fitting: 2-Gaussian formal fit to the [S IV] line profile  
  > "The line profile could be formally fit by 2 gaussians at ∼ 744 , 775 km s [−][1] consistent with the IRTF results [4], although it does not rule out large scale gas flows in addition to the two peaks." (4.1 Ionized Gas:Double Peak or Extended Wing?)
   - validation: Compared spatial distribution of integrated [S IV] map to 15 GHz radio map of similar resolution  
  > "Comparing the [S IV] map to the 15 GHz radio map of Figure 1a, which has close to the same resolution, shows very similar spatial distribution." (3.1.1 The [S IV] Agrees with the Radio Continuum)
2. **Analyze spatial distribution and kinematics of CO(3-2) molecular gas**: Generate moment 0, 1, and 2 maps, position-velocity diagrams (PVDs) along RA and Dec, and region-integrated line profiles (5 positions) from the continuum-subtracted ALMA CO(3-2) spectral cube.  
  > "Figure 2 displays the first 3 moments of the CO(3-2) data cube (integrated line intensity, intensity-weighted velocity, and velocity dispersion)." (3.2 Spatial Structure of Dense Molecular Gas); "More insight into this complicated velocity field may be found in the Position-Velocity Diagrams (PVDs) of Figure 7" (4.2 Molecular Gas: Two Elongated Clouds at Different Velocities); "Top, line profiles of CO(3-2) in 5 positions across the source. Bottom, the MOM0 map of CO(3-2) with circles identifying the location and showing the area over which the data was summed for each line profile." (Figure 6 caption)
   - software: CASA 4.2.2
   - parameter: 2 km s^-1 spectral channels, 0.34 x 0.25 arcsec beam  
  > "13/08/14 ALMA 345 GHz 0 . 34 × 0 . 25 [′′] 2 km s [−][1] 3 . 5 × 10 [−][5]" (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared CO peak positions with Consiglio et al. (2017) 3 mm continuum and CO peaks and overlaid [S IV] and 15 GHz radio contours  
  > "The dominant molecular peak marked as ’W4’ in Figure 6, agrees with Consiglio et al. (2017)’s CO and 3 mm continuum peak ’W’, and is co-incident with the radio and infrared cluster." (3.2 Spatial Structure of Dense Molecular Gas)
3. **Simulate individual star cluster evolution prior to collision**: Evolve two isolated star clusters (large and small) for 2 Myr using AMUSE coupling N-body (ph4), Tree-SPH (GADGET-2), stellar evolution (SeBA), and stellar winds ('heat' mode) via a Bridge scheme.  
  > "AMUSE couples all the codes, N-body and hydrodynamical, via a Bridge scheme (Fujii et al. 2007). For these simulations we used the community codes ph4 (Dolcetta et al. 2011) for the N-body calculations and GADGET-2 (Springel 2005) for Tree-SPH calculations." (5.1 Numerical Methods: the AMUSE Environment); "The clusters were allowed to evolve for a total of 2Myr with a 10kyr timestep." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
   - software: AMUSE, ph4, GADGET-2, SeBA
   - parameter: Cluster total masses: 4.5 x 10^5 M_sun (large) and 2.2 x 10^5 M_sun (small); O star counts: 4300 and 2150  
  > "stars respectively. The model clusters therefore have total masses of 4 _._ 5 × 10 [5] M ⊙ and 2 _._ 2 × 10 [5] M ⊙" (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
   - parameter: SPH particle mass = 1.5 M_sun; initial gas T = 10^4 K; evolution time = 2 Myr; timestep = 10 kyr  
  > "for the gas we assumed an idealized Plummer sphere Plummer (1911), a Maxwell-Boltzman velocity distribution matching a temperature of 𝑇 = 10 [4] K and a particle mass of 1 . 5M ⊙ ." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution); "The clusters were allowed to evolve for a total of 2Myr with a 10kyr timestep." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
   - assumption: Star cluster has already formed in each clump prior to collision; gas comprises 70% of total cluster mass  
  > "In this paper we start with the assumption that a star cluster has already formed in each clump, and follow the appearance of the resulting system through the collision." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution); "where we assumed the gas comprises 70% of the total mass as the star formation efficiency in SSCs is often quite high (Lada & Lada 2003)." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
   - assumption: Plummer sphere for gas; King model for stars; Salpeter IMF in mass range [15, 120] M_sun  
  > "for the gas we assumed an idealized Plummer sphere Plummer (1911), a Maxwell-Boltzman velocity distribution matching a temperature of 𝑇 = 10 [4] K and a particle mass of 1 . 5M ⊙ . For the stars, we assumed a King model King (1966) and a Salpeter initial mass function Salpeter (1955) in a mass range of [15 , 120]M ⊙ ." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Examined rate of mass loss, radius, mass, and energy of each cluster; tested different SPH particle masses and stellar wind modes.  
  > "After the evolution of each cluster, we examined the rate of mass loss (SPH particles blown away due to the stellar winds), radius, mass and energy to make sure that they were behaving in a typical manner of stellar clusters." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution); "Additionally, the mass of the SPH particles was chosen by running the cluster evolutions using different masses and determining which one provided the sufficient accuracy for our case." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
4. **Simulate cluster-cluster collisions and generate synthetic CO(3-2) and [S IV] observations**: Trim evolved clusters to slightly above virial radius, collide them over a grid of impact parameters (b = 0 to 6 pc), interpolate SPH gas properties onto a 3D grid, run RADMC-3D at inclinations of 45, 60, and 75 deg, convolve with observed beam, and compare moment 0, PVDs, and line profiles to ALMA and TEXES observations.  
  > "starting from 0, the head-on collision, and increasing by 0.5 pc increments to 6pc, where the cluster interaction is minimal." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision); "After all the collisions were finished, the AMUSE results were used as input for the radiative transfer software RADMC-3D Dullemond et al. (2012) to produce 3D line profiles." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision); "The CO(3-2) moment map and PVDs were convolved with the beam size of the observations" (Figure 10 caption)
   - software: AMUSE, ph4, GADGET-2, SeBA, RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Initial collision velocity ~ 120 km s^-1; impact parameter b = 0 to 6 pc in 0.5 pc increments; runtime = 0.5 Myr with 10^4 yr timestep  
  > "the large cluster was placed at the center of the coordinates system and the small cluster was set some distance away with initial velocity of approximately 120 km s [−][1] ." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision); "starting from 0, the head-on collision, and increasing by 0.5 pc increments to 6pc, where the cluster interaction is minimal." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision); "The time step is the same as in the evolution simulations, 10 [4] yr, and the total runtime for each collision was 0.5Myr." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision)
   - parameter: RADMC-3D grid: 256 x 256 x 256 covering 60 x 60 x 60 pc; inclinations: 45, 60, and 75 deg  
  > "The properties of the SPH gas particles were interpolated to a grid of size 256x256x256 that covered the entire range of gas distribution which was 60x60x60 pc. Each collision was observed at an inclination angle of 45, 60 and 75°" (5.2.2 Cluster Collision)
   - assumption: Large-scale spatial distribution and kinematics of CO and [S IV] are similar as they roughly trace the same gas mass  
  > "the large scale behaviour of gas in these conditions, in terms of the spatial distribution and kinematics, would be very similar in the CO and [S IV] as they roughly trace the same gas mass." (5.3.2 Cluster Collision)
   - fitting: Visual comparison across grid of impact parameters (0 to 6 pc), timesteps (0 to 0.5 Myr), and inclinations (45, 60, 75 deg) to match shape, spatial/kinematic separation, and peak ratio.  
  > "When comparing the CO and [S IV] observations to all the simulation results, specifically in terms of the the overall shape of the two peaks, the spatial and kinematic distance between them and the ratio between the two peaks in the line profile. We determine that the best match to the CO and [S IV] observations occurs for the 𝑏 = 4pc impact parameter, at the 𝑇 = 0 . 5Myr timestep, when observed at a 45° inclination angle." (5.3.2 Cluster Collision)
   - validation: Initial collision velocity determined by running multiple low-scale simulations and comparing to the [S IV] line profile  
  > "This velocity was determined by running multiple low-scale simulations, approximating the resulting line profile, and comparing it to the [S IV] line profile." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision)

## Qualitative findings

- The JVLA 15 and 22 GHz continuum maps show an asymmetric extended ionized envelope dominated by a single bright resolved cluster peak at RA 05:55:42.60 DEC 03:23:32.0 rather than multiple secondary sub-clusters.  
  > "the current data show that ionized gas has an unusual and asymmetric extended structure and that the spatial distribution is dominated by one large cluster." (3.1 Radio Continuum: Multiple Clusters?)
- Both velocity components of [S IV] appear in every spatial pixel across the source without a separate spatial clump for the red component, indicating that if two distinct clusters exist they overlap along the line of sight.  
  > "We conclude that if there are two distinct clusters, as the [S IV] profile strongly suggests, they so overlap in the line of sight that they cannot be separated." (4.1 Ionized Gas:Double Peak or Extended Wing?)
- The ALMA CO(3-2) data show two velocity features along the main molecular streamer and a V-shaped / check-mark position-velocity diagram consistent with two elongated molecular clouds overlapping in the line of sight and colliding.  
  > "The Position-Velocity diagram (PVD) along the Dec axis resembles the V-shape observational signature of a cloud-cloud collision Torii et al. (2017) (highlighted in a subfigure in Figure 5)." (4.2 Molecular Gas: Two Elongated Clouds at Different Velocities); "with two elongated clouds overlapping in the line of sight, consistent with the double line peaks of Figure 5." (4.2 Molecular Gas: Two Elongated Clouds at Different Velocities)
- Stellar winds are the primary factor shaping the simulated pre-collision clusters, blowing away gas, increasing cluster radii, and distorting the outer regions of the larger cluster while the denser small cluster remains mostly spherical.  
  > "We find that the stellar winds were the most significant factor in shaping the clusters, which is consistent with the results of simulations run by (Pelupessy & Portegies Zwart 2012); they blew away some of the gas particles, resulting in an overall increase of the clusters radii, and a decrease in the gas density, temperature and average rms velocity." (5.3.1 Cluster Evolution)
- In the grazing collision model (b = 4 pc), the two clusters are not gravitationally bound to each other and exchange gas across a bridge region that will thin out as the clusters separate unless a new SSC forms.  
  > "The clusters in this collision are not gravitationally bound to each other; if no new SSC forms the bridge area between the clusters (W3) will thin out until the two clusters separates completely." (6.1 Clusters in II Zw 40 as Modelled: Positions, Trajectories, and Possible Future Development)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: [S IV] 10.5 um is less affected by extinction and thermal broadening than H recombination lines, probing ionized gas kinematics at ~4.5 km s^-1 resolution; high-resolution Gemini/TEXES and JVLA data were obtained to test the two-cluster hypothesis from lower-resolution IRTF data, while ALMA CO(3-2) probes the dense warm molecular gas at comparable resolution.  
  > "an ALMA spectral cube of the CO(3-2) line that probes the dense warm molecular gas with spatial and velocity resolution comparable to the ionized gas." (abstract); "[S IV] has two great advantages over H i recombination lines; it is less affected by extinction than many, including Br 𝛾, and it is much less affected by thermal broadening than any hydrogen line can be." (2.2 Mid-Infrared Spectroscopy with TEXES); "Beck et al. (2013) accordingly suggested that two super star clusters offset in velocity were included in the same beam, a model which motivated the higher resolution observations of Gemini." (4.1 Ionized Gas:Double Peak or Extended Wing?)
- Why this method: AMUSE was chosen to run N-body gravity, SPH hydrodynamics, stellar evolution, and stellar winds in parallel via a Bridge scheme; the stellar wind 'heat' mode was selected because stellar winds develop differently in dense embedded clusters; clusters were trimmed to slightly over the virial radius and Salpeter IMF restricted to [15, 120] M_sun to keep computational runtime realistic while preserving core structure and wind effects.  
  > "The Astrophysical Multipurpose Software Environment (AMUSE) assembles community codes and makes it possible to run computations of all these aspects in parallel and to communicate the results between codes." (5.1 Numerical Methods: the AMUSE Environment); "we accordingly use the stellar wind module’s ’heat’ mode, which follows the energy rather than the velocity of the wind particles, as most appropriate for this embedded source." (5.1 Numerical Methods: the AMUSE Environment); "The clusters were then trimmed to a little over the virial radius of each (which was also higher than the half-mass radius)." (5.2.2 Cluster Collision)
- Alternative: Multiple secondary sub-clusters seen in earlier radio maps (Beck et al. 2002; Kepley et al. 2014) were found to be ~2 sigma fluctuations or deconvolution artifacts when deeper JVLA maps revealed a single dominant cluster with asymmetric extended emission.  
  > "Where Beck et al. (2002) saw ≈ 2 𝜎 brightness fluctuations as possible secondary clusters and could not detect most of the extended emission, the current data show that ionized gas has an unusual and asymmetric extended structure and that the spatial distribution is dominated by one large cluster." (3.1 Radio Continuum: Multiple Clusters?)
- Alternative: Alternative explanations for why the small cluster lies on the outer edge of the [S IV] distribution: (1) the small clump contained no stars prior to the collision, or (2) a new SSC formed in the bridge area relatively closer to the large cluster's core.  
  > "Alternatively, this could be due to the small cluster clump containing no stars prior to the collision, so that only the large clump contained already formed stars." (6.1 Clusters in II Zw 40 as Modelled: Positions, Trajectories, and Possible Future Development); "A third option is the formation of a new SSC in the bridge area between the two cluster cores; for this solution to work it would have to be formed relatively closer to the large cluster’s core." (6.1 Clusters in II Zw 40 as Modelled: Positions, Trajectories, and Possible Future Development)
- Alternative: Star formation triggered after the cloud-cloud collision rather than star clusters forming prior to the collision (left as a valid alternative for future work).  
  > "(We intend in future work to examine separately the alternate case of star formation starting after the collision)." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution); "The major unanswered question is whether the star clusters were formed before the collision as we posited here, or are a result of the collision of two clouds." (6.2 Summary and Comments)
- How they decided it was enough: Cluster evolution time (2 Myr) was set by the maximum dynamical relaxation time of the gas; SPH particle mass (1.5 M_sun) was chosen by convergence testing of cluster evolution runs; impact parameter grid was stopped at b = 6 pc where cluster interaction becomes minimal.  
  > "We chose the total evolution time based on the maximum dynamical relaxation time of the gas for both clusters based on their mass, number of particles and virial radius Spitzer (1987)." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution); "Additionally, the mass of the SPH particles was chosen by running the cluster evolutions using different masses and determining which one provided the sufficient accuracy for our case." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution); "We chose the total evolution time based on the maximum dynamical relaxation time of the gas for both clusters based on their mass, number of particles and virial radius" (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
- Limitation (authors): Magnetic field effects are omitted from the simulations due to lack of observational data.  
  > "(and ideally the magnetic field, for which there is at present no data)." (5.1 Numerical Methods: the AMUSE Environment); "As the initial model was very general (with the one major omission of not yet including magnetic field effects) we expect these findings to be valid for typical clusters." (5.3.1 Cluster Evolution)
- Limitation (authors): Low-mass stars (< 15 M_sun) are omitted from the Salpeter IMF to keep computational demands realistic.  
  > "We chose these ’top heavy’ mass limits to keep the total stellar mass and the computational demands realistic; the winds of low mass stars are so much weaker than those of massive stars that they can be omitted with little effect on the large-scale results." (5.2.1 Cluster Evolution)
- Limitation (authors): The simulations do not explicitly model the elongated molecular clouds, their chemistry/cooling, or their interactions with the stars and ionized gas, nor do they include accretion, fragmentation, or ongoing star formation.  
  > "This discrepancy between the CO and [S IV] distributions could be solved by obtaining higher resolution observations of the ionized gas, as well as running higher resolution simulations that account for fragmentation, accretion and star formation." (6.1 Clusters in II Zw 40 as Modelled: Positions, Trajectories, and Possible Future Development); "While the elongated appearance and two-component velocity structure of the moleculargas motivatedthemodelsand helpestablish the velocities of the components, the present simulations do not attempt to model the molecular component nor its interactions with the stars and ionized gas." (6.2 Summary and Comments)
- Limitation (authors): The simulations cannot currently distinguish whether the star clusters formed before the collision or as a result of the cloud collision.  
  > "The major unanswered question is whether the star clusters were formed before the collision as we posited here, or are a result of the collision of two clouds. As the upper range of the age of SSC-N is 3Myr Vanzi et al. (2008), both options are at present valid and the simulations cannot at present distinguish." (6.2 Summary and Comments)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Synthetic CO(3-2) and [S IV] emission in RADMC-3D are generated from the same isothermal (initially 10^4 K) SPH gas distribution without explicit multiphase ISM thermodynamics, molecular chemistry, or photoionization modeling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The initial relative collision velocity (~120 km s^-1) is much higher than the observed line-of-sight velocity separation (~25-30 km s^-1) even at a 45 deg viewing inclination, and the gravitational binding or galactic potential driving such a high relative velocity in a dwarf galaxy is not dynamically modeled.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model selection (b = 4 pc, T = 0.5 Myr, i = 45 deg) is performed by qualitative visual inspection rather than a quantitative goodness-of-fit metric.
