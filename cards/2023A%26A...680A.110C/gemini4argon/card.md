1. **Measure multi-epoch radio and millimetre continuum flux densities**: Integrate flux density inside a polygonal shape encompassing the compact source across VLA bands and ALMA 3 mm maps; subtract fiducial large-scale lobe flux (1.8 mJy at 6 GHz, 1.0 mJy at 10 GHz) in D-array when needed; separate NE and SW components at 3 mm.  
  > "The flux density of the compact, variable source centred on S255IR NIRS 3 has been estimated inside a polygonal shape encompassing the compact, unresolved radio source" (Very Large Array); "$^c$ Corrected by subtracting a fiducial value of 1.8 mJy for the two large-scale lobes (see main text)." (Very Large Array); "When estimating the flux density at wavelengths of 3 mm or shorter, it is thus necessary to distinguish between the NE lobe and the rest of the source." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission)
   - software: CASA, STATCONT
   - parameter: Relative flux error = 10%  
  > "Under the reasonable assumption that this object is not variable, by comparing the flux density measurements obtained at different epochs at the same frequency, we estimated a relative error of 10% in all bands." (Very Large Array)
   - assumption: Large-scale lobes have constant flux in time; check source at 06h12m53.61s +18d00m26.4s is non-variable  
  > "In a few cases, as indicated in the footnotes of the table, we attempted a correction to the flux, under the assumption that the flux of the lobes is constant in time and thus equal to the value measured (at a different epoch) with the A array." (Very Large Array); "Under the reasonable assumption that this object is not variable, by comparing the flux density measurements obtained at different epochs at the same frequency, we estimated a relative error of 10% in all bands." (Very Large Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared flux density measurements of a nearby compact check source in the primary beam across epochs to estimate 10% relative error  
  > "Under the reasonable assumption that this object is not variable, by comparing the flux density measurements obtained at different epochs at the same frequency, we estimated a relative error of 10% in all bands." (Very Large Array)
2. **Track spatial expansion of the radio jet peak**: Fit a 2D Gaussian to sub-arcsecond VLA K-band (1.3 cm) maps to determine peak positions over time, and combine with the ALMA 3 mm NE peak positions and the OLHKP peak position to compute projected expansion velocities.  
  > "For this purpose we fitted a 2D Gaussian to the K-band maps with sub-arcsecond resolution." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm); "For our analysis we also included the NE peak of our ALMA data and that of Obonyo et al. ((cite: obonyo); hereafter OLHKP)." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm)
   - parameter: Position uncertainty represented as 1/5 of the synthesised beam  
  > "To give an idea of the uncertainty on these positions, we also draw ellipses corresponding to one-fifth of the synthesised beams." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm)
   - assumption: Protostar is located at the stationary SW peak of the 3 mm continuum emission (06h 12m 54.012s, +17d 59m 23.04s); distance d = 1.78 kpc  
  > "In this study we focus on the outburst from the massive (proto)star S255IR NIRS 3, located at a distance of $1.78^{+0.12}_{-0.11}$ kpc (Burns et al. (cite: burns16))." (Introduction); "With all the above in mind, in the following we assume that the (proto)star powering the radio jet is located at the SW peak of the 3 mm continuum emission, namely at $\alpha$(J2000)=$06^{\rm h}\,12^{\rm m}\,54.012s$, $\delta$(J2000)=17$^\circ$ 59$'$ 23.04$''$." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to K-band maps  
  > "For this purpose we fitted a 2D Gaussian to the K-band maps with sub-arcsecond resolution." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm)
   - validation: Compared derived mean expansion velocity (436 km s^-1) with independent IR proper motion measurement (450 +- 50 km s^-1) by Fedriani et al. (2023)  
  > "Using the same approach, Fedriani et al. ((cite: fedr23)) estimated an expansion speed of $450\pm50$ km s$^{-1}$ from their IR data." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm)
3. **Compute 3 mm spectral index maps and decompose dust vs. free-free emission**: Create continuum maps for the four ALMA Band 3 spectral windows (85.2, 87.2, 97.2, 99.2 GHz) with identical clean beams, fit pixel-by-pixel spectral index alpha where all four fluxes exceed 5 sigma, and calculate the free-free flux fraction R_S/(1+R_S).  
  > "For this purpose, we used the maps obtained from the four correlator units centred at 85.2, 87.2, 97.2, and 99.2 GHz (see Sect. 2.2), created with the same clean beam." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission); "For each pixel of the maps, the spectral index was computed from a least-square fit to the four fluxes in a $\log S_\nu$–$\log\nu$ plot. The fit was performed only in those pixels where all of the four fluxes were above the 5$\sigma$ level." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission); "and, from this, the fraction of the total flux due to free-free emission, $R_{\rm S}/(1+R_{\rm S})$." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission)
   - parameter: 5 sigma threshold across all 4 bands; nu_1 = 85.2 GHz, nu_2 = 99.2 GHz  
  > "The fit was performed only in those pixels where all of the four fluxes were above the 5$\sigma$ level." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission); "As previously explained, the spectral index between $\nu_1=85.2$ GHz and $\nu_2=99.2$ GHz was estimated assuming $S_\nu\propto\nu^\alpha$, and hence we have" (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission with S_nu^d propto nu^gamma (gamma = 2 to 4, dust opacity index beta = 0 to 2) and optically thin free-free emission with S_nu^ff propto nu^-0.1  
  > "At 3 mm it is reasonable to assume that dust emission is optically thin and the flux density is $\propto\nu^\gamma$, with $\gamma=2$–4, because the dust absorption coefficient is believed to vary as $\nu^\beta$ with $\beta=0$–2 (see e.g. D’Alessio et al. (cite: dale01), Sadavoy et al. (cite: sada13)), where $\beta=0$ corresponds to the case of large grains (‘pebbles’) in disks (Testi et al. (cite: testi))." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission); "The total flux can be written as $S_\nu=S_\nu^{\rm d}+S_\nu^{\rm ff}$, where $S_\nu^{\rm ff}\propto\nu^{-0.1}$ is the optically thin free-free flux and $S_\nu^{\rm d}\propto\nu^\gamma$, with $\gamma=2$–4, is the dust flux." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission)
   - fitting: Pixel-by-pixel linear least-squares fit in log S_nu - log nu space  
  > "For each pixel of the maps, the spectral index was computed from a least-square fit to the four fluxes in a $\log S_\nu$–$\log\nu$ plot." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission)
   - validation: Consistency with the ~500 K brightness temperature measured at 3 mm towards the SW peak  
  > "This implies that 15–20 mJy out of 23 mJy emitted by the SW component (see Table 4), are contributed by free-free emission, also consistent with the brightness temperature of $\sim$500 K measured at 3 mm towards the SW peak, probably too large to be due only to dust emission." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission)
4. **Fit 2D spatial morphology of the NE lobe in ALMA 3 mm maps and derive jet expansion law**: Generate synthetic 2D brightness temperature maps of an optically thin jet (T_B = T_0 (y/y_0)^-3), convolve with the ALMA beam, fit to the observed NE lobe at t = 1061 and 1881 days to find y_0, y_m, and theta_0, and solve the momentum-conserving deceleration equation for T and y_m0.  
  > "In order to obtain a reliable estimate of $y_{\rm m}$, we have fitted the NE lobe in the two ALMA maps of Fig. 1 assuming that the 3 mm emission is optically thin all over the jet surface." (Expansion law of the jet); "For a given set of $y_0$, $y_{\rm m}$, and $\theta_0$ (the opening angle of the jet) a map was generated, convolved with the instrumental beam, and the model brightness temperature was computed for each pixel of the observed map." (Expansion law of the jet); "one obtains a system of two equations in the two unknowns $T$ and $y_{\rm m 0}$, the solutions to which are $T$=124 days and $y_{\rm m 0}$=251 au." (Expansion law of the jet)
   - parameter: t = 1061 days and t = 1881 days; v_0 = 900 km s^-1; psi = 10 deg  
  > "As in Paper I, we chose $\varv_0$=900 km s$^{-1}$, while the two parameters $T$ and $y_{\rm m 0}$ can be determined from the values of $y_{\rm m}$ estimated from the two ALMA maps at $t$=1061 days and $t$=1881 days." (Expansion law of the jet); "assuming $\psi$=10$^\circ$ (see Paper I, where the complementary angle $i=90\ensuremath{^\circ}-\psi=80\ensuremath{^\circ}$ was used)." (Expansion law of the jet)
   - assumption: 3 mm emission in the NE lobe is optically thin everywhere (T_B = T_0 (y/y_0)^-3); jet expands conserving momentum into an ambient medium with density propto r^-2  
  > "For this purpose, we assume that the jet is expanding in a medium with density $\propto r^{-2}$, with $r$ the distance from the star." (Expansion law of the jet); "In order to obtain a reliable estimate of $y_{\rm m}$, we have fitted the NE lobe in the two ALMA maps of Fig. 1 assuming that the 3 mm emission is optically thin all over the jet surface." (Expansion law of the jet)
   - fitting: Grid search over y_0, y_m, and theta_0 minimising pixel-by-pixel chi^2 = sum (T_B^i(model) - T_B^i(data))^2

## Qualitative findings

- The massive (proto)star S255IR NIRS 3 is located at the stationary SW peak of the 3 mm continuum emission rather than midway between the two peaks, while the NE peak is an expanding jet lobe.  
  > "Careful comparison between Fig. 1a and 1b reveals that, while the NE peak is moving away from the centre, the SW peak stays still, consistent with this being the location of the (proto)star." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm)
- A new SW free-free jet lobe emerged between May 2018 and June 2019 (22-35 months after the NE lobe), likely originating from the same 2015 accretion event but delayed by denser ambient material to the SW.  
  > "We thus conclude that in all likelihood the SW jet lobe appeared only recently, between May 2018 and June 2019." (Nature of the 3 mm continuum emission); "We conclude that both jet lobes could arise from the same accretion event, although with a time lag between them of 22–35 months." (Origin of the SW lobe); "The weakness of the IR emission to the SW is thus indicative of an asymmetry in the density distribution along the jet axis, a fact that could naturally also explain the delay of the expansion of the SW lobe with respect to the NE lobe." (Origin of the SW lobe)
- The progressive change in jet position angle from 75 deg on 0.35 pc scales down to 44-48 deg on <1000 au scales indicates that the jet outflow is undergoing precession.  
  > "Further support for this hypothesis is given by the progressive change of the position angle of the jet from the large to the small scale, as shown in Table 3. This trend is indeed consistent with a jet outflow undergoing precession." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm)
- Radio emission below ~6 GHz exhibits a different spectral slope and temporal behaviour than higher frequencies, indicating a non-thermal (likely synchrotron) contribution.  
  > "Therefore, the radio emission at 1.5 and 3 GHz, and probably also part of the 6 GHz flux, might not be due to free-free radiation but to another mechanism, such as synchrotron emission, which has been detected towards a number of extended radio jets from YSOs (e.g. Carrasco-Gonzalez et al. (cite: cargon10), Moscadelli et al. (cite: mosca13), Brogan et al. (cite: brog18), Sanna et al. (cite: sanna19) and references therein)." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm)
- The jet was actively fed by the outburst for ~250 days (until mid-2017), after which mass injection ceased and the inner radius r_0 began expanding, forming a detached radio knot.  
  > "From the onset of the radio outburst up to $\sim$250 days, $r_0$ remains approximately constant, whereas the jet mass increases. After that, the reverse occurs: as soon as the inner radius starts increasing, the jet stops growing in mass, with the grey area marking the transition between these two phases." (Modelling the jet variability); "After this date, no more mass is injected into the lobes and the inner jet radius expands, which, over the long term, is bound to give rise to one of the knots that characterise thermal jets from YSOs." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-band VLA monitoring was chosen to track the temporal evolution of the radio continuum spectrum after the 2016 outburst, while two-epoch sub-arcsecond (<0.1'') ALMA Band 3 imaging was chosen to spatially resolve the expanding jet lobes and separate thermal dust from free-free emission.  
  > "Although the emission was basically unresolved, we predicted that in a few years the jet expansion should make it possible to resolve its structure. With this in mind, we performed both monitoring of the radio emission and sub-arcsecond millimetre imaging at two epochs, several years after the beginning of the outburst." (Introduction); "We prefer to use the peak position of our maps instead of that of the sub-millimetre maps of Liu et al. ((cite: liu20)), because of the higher angular resolution (0.087$''$ instead of 0.14$''$)." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm)
- Why this method: K-band (1.3 cm) was chosen over Q-band (7 mm) for Gaussian peak tracking because of higher S/N and lack of dust contamination; frequencies below 6 GHz were excluded from thermal jet fitting due to synchrotron contamination; Reynolds (1986) equations were re-derived for pyramidal geometry because the original small-angle approximation fails for opening angles up to 50 deg.  
  > "We prefer the 1.3 cm data to the 7 mm data, which would provide us with better resolution, because in the K band the S/N is higher and in the Q band contamination by dust thermal emission might be present." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm); "For all these reasons, in the following we focus our study on the emission above 6 GHz, with the caveat that even the 6 GHz flux might be partly contaminated by a non-thermal contribution, as suggested by OLHKP." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm); "In order to overcome this limitation we re-wrote Reynolds’ equations under suitable assumptions, as detailed in Appendix 7, so that they are now valid for any $\theta_0<90\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (Modelling the jet variability)
- Alternative: Star located at the geometrical centre between the NE and SW 3 mm peaks (rejected because the SW peak is stationary, coincides with the sub-mm dust core and molecular absorption dips, and has a dust-like positive spectral index).  
  > "At first look, the figure seems to outline a bipolar structure, where the star might be located close to the geometrical centre, between the two jet lobes. However, this is not the case. Careful comparison between Fig. 1a and 1b reveals that, while the NE peak is moving away from the centre, the SW peak stays still, consistent with this being the location of the (proto)star." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm)
- Alternative: Two separate jets from two different YSOs vs. a single precessing jet (single precessing jet adopted because only one core lies along the jet axis and the jet PA changes progressively with scale).  
  > "The fact that the two axes have different orientations and do not intersect at the position of the star (i.e. at the SW peak of the 3 mm continuum) can be interpreted in two ways: either we are dealing with two jets originating from two different YSOs, or the jet is precessing and the star is moving on the plane of the sky at a different speed with respect to the large-scale lobes." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm); "Although it is impossible to rule out one of the two hypotheses, in the following we assume that all lobes belong to the same jet, because there is only one core lying along the jet axis and evidence for precession has been found in S255IR NIRS 3 (Wang et al. (cite: wang); Fedriani et al. (cite: fedr23)), as well as other similar objects (see e.g. Shepherd et al. (cite: shep00), Cesaroni et al. (cite: cesa05), Sánchez-Monge et al. (cite: sanch14), Beltrán et al. (cite: bel16))." (Continuum emission at $\lambda=3$ mm)
- Alternative: Delayed SW lobe caused by a second independent accretion burst vs. higher ambient density on the SW side (second burst rejected based on lack of new IR or methanol maser bursts; higher SW density favoured OVER intrinsically asymmetric disk ejection due to faint SW near-IR emission despite blue-shifted orientation).  
  > "However, we point out that the IR monitoring performed by Uchiyama et al. ((cite: uchi)) and Fedriani et al. ((cite: fedr23)) between November 2015 and February 2022 as well as the methanol maser observations of the Maser Monitoring Organization (M2O) have not revealed any other burst after that of Caratti o Garatti et al. ((cite: cagana)) and before the ejection of the SW lobe." (Origin of the SW lobe); "In our opinion, two explanations are possible: either the accretion (and hence ejection) event is intrinsically stronger on the NE side of the disk, or the expansion towards the SW is hindered by the presence of denser material. We favour the latter hypothesis since the near-IR emission is much fainter from the SW lobe than from the NE lobe (see Caratti o Garatti et al. (cite: cagana) and Fedriani et al. (cite: fedr23))." (Origin of the SW lobe)
- Alternative: Constant-velocity jet expansion as in Paper I (rejected because late-time VLA and ALMA peak positions show significant deceleration).  
  > "While this assumption could hold for the first few months after the onset of the radio outburst, it is inconsistent with the most recent data. In fact, in Sect. 3.2 we show that the jet expansion is slowing down with time." (Expansion law of the jet)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): In compact VLA configurations (B, C, D) at long wavelengths (1.5-10 GHz), the central compact source cannot be fully separated from the 8'' large-scale lobes, making those flux measurements upper limits even when subtracting a fiducial A-array lobe flux.  
  > "At the longest wavelengths it was possible to resolve the central source from the lobes only in the most extended array configuration. In the other cases, we could only measure the total flux density from all components" (Very Large Array); "With this approach a caveat is in order, because a compact configuration may be sensitive to lobe structures that are resolved out in a more extended configuration. This implies that the corrected flux densities could be still an upper limit." (Very Large Array)
- Limitation (authors): The 6 GHz flux densities at the last five to six VLA epochs are underestimated by the thermal model, likely due to contamination by non-thermal (synchrotron) emission.  
  > "For all these reasons, in the following we focus our study on the emission above 6 GHz, with the caveat that even the 6 GHz flux might be partly contaminated by a non-thermal contribution, as suggested by OLHKP." (Continuum emission at $\lambda\ge7$ mm); "While most of the fits look to be in agreement with the data within the uncertainties, the 6 GHz fluxes appear to be underestimated by the model at the last five to six epochs. In our opinion, such a discrepancy could indicate contamination from non-thermal emission at this frequency, as already suggested by OLHKP." (Modelling the jet variability)
- Limitation (authors): The momentum-conserving deceleration equation is a simple parametrisation rather than the exact equation of motion of the jet.  
  > "While this expression provides us with a more realistic description of the jet expansion than the constant velocity assumption adopted in Paper I, we stress that it is not to be taken as the real equation of motion of the jet but as the simplest way to parametrise the observed deceleration of it." (Jet expansion law)
- Limitation (authors): The modified Reynolds model uses a pyramidal geometry and integrates radially from y_0 to y_m without azimuthal integration, which is an approximation valid only for small inclination angles psi.  
  > "We note that it is not strictly correct to integrate from $y_0$ to $y_{\rm m}$ because the projections of the two circles with radii $r_0$ and $r_{\rm m}$ on the plane of the sky are ellipses, and the integral should be made not only in the variable $R$ but also in the azimuthal angle. However, the approximation adopted by us is acceptable for small $\psi$." (Description of the model)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The opening angle theta_0 hits the upper boundary of the allowed grid (50 deg) from day 170 onwards in the SED fits, whereas the spatially resolved ALMA maps at days 1061 and 1881 yield theta_0 = 9 deg and 2.4 deg; this tension is attributed to jet re-collimation rather than model degeneracy.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The deceleration parameters T = 124 days and y_m0 = 251 au are solved algebraically from just two ALMA epochs while fixing v_0 = 900 km s^-1 from Paper I, with no formal uncertainty propagation into the multi-epoch SED fits.
