## Science

- Fields: debris_disks, exoplanets
- Question: What is the radial structure of the HD 206893 debris disc (is there a gap, asymmetry, CO gas, dust around the brown dwarf B), and what does it imply for the dynamics of the companions?  
  > "In this paper we present ALMA observations of the debris/planetesimal disc surrounding HD 206893, a system that also hosts two massive companions at 2 and 11 au." (abstract); "These two companions make this system an ideal target to image its disc with ALMA, in order to both constrain the dynamics of this system and look for additional companions that could shape the distribution of planetesimals." (1 Introduction)
- Target **HD 206893**: coordinates _not stated_; z/distance 40.8 pc  
  > "Located at 40.8 pc (cite: Gaiadr2), the F5V star HD 206893 is known to host a companion, HD 206893 B, at a separation of $\sim11$ au" (1 Introduction); distance from Gaia DR2

## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the continuum and measure total flux**: tclean Briggs imaging with Gaussian uv taper; integrate flux in an ellipse oriented as the disc  
  > "Continuum images at wavelengths of 0.88 and 1.3 mm are obtained using the TCLEAN task in CASA with Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 2.0 (to maximise sensitivity)." (2.1 Dust continuum); "These fluxes are computed by integrating all emission within an ellipse of semi-major axis $5\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim200$ au) and oriented as the disc on the sky" (2.1 Dust continuum)
   - software: CASA tclean
   - parameter: robust = 2.0  
  > "a robust parameter of 2.0 (to maximise sensitivity)" (2.1 Dust continuum)
   - parameter: Gaussian taper 0.9'' at 0.88 mm and 0.4'' at 1.3 mm  
  > "of $0.9\ensuremath{''}$ at 0.88 mm and $0.4\ensuremath{''}$ at 1.3 mm, which leads to a loss of resolution" (2.1 Dust continuum)
   - assumption: Central point source is photospheric emission (30 uJy at 0.88 mm, 13 uJy at 1.3 mm)  
  > "The stellar flux is consistent with Rayleigh-Jeans extrapolations of its flux at shorter wavelengths (i.e. 30 $\mu$Jy at 0.88 mm and 13 $\mu$Jy at 1.3 mm), and thus we attribute it to photospheric emission." (2.1 Dust continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Azimuthal radial profiles and asymmetry tests**: Deproject with best-fit PA and inclination, azimuthally average; compare disc halves and subtract their radial profiles  
  > "In order to study the radial structure of the disc, we azimuthally average the deprojected emission (cite: Marino2016) using the best fit disc position angle and inclination" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure); "We first compare the integrated flux of one half of the disc against the other while varying the angle of the axis that divides the two halves." (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - parameter: Band 6 untapered image and band 7 image with 0.4'' taper (similar beams)  
  > "we use the band 6 clean image without tapering and the band 7 image with a $0.4\ensuremath{''}$ tapering" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - assumption: Disc orientation from the parametric fit  
  > "using the best fit disc position angle and inclination" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Radial profiles of the two halves show the flux difference is spread over a broad region, so it is not a single compact SMG  
  > "They do reveal nevertheless that the flux difference does not arise from a single compact SMG" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
3. **Reconstruct radial profile in visibility space**: Subtract constant stellar flux from visibilities, deproject, reconstruct intensity profile with Frankenstein (Gaussian process)  
  > "In order to further constrain the radial structure, we use the PYTHON module Frankenstein (cite: Jennings2020) that uses Gaussian process to reconstruct the intensity radial profile of a disc." (2.1.1 Dust radial structure); "We first subtract the stellar emission from the visibilities by simply subtracting a constant value of 30 $\mu$Jy at 0.88 mm and 13 $\mu$Jy at 1.3 mm" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - software: Frankenstein
   - parameter: maximum radius 400 au, alpha = 1.01, w_smooth = 1e-2  
  > "presents the derived profiles using a maximum radius of 400 au and hyperparameters $\alpha=1.01$ and $w_\mathrm{smooth}=10^{-2}$" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - assumption: No primary beam correction (effect < 10% within 150 au)  
  > "no primary beam correction is taken into account, although its effect is smaller than 10% within 150 au in both band 7 and 6 data" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - fitting: Gaussian-process reconstruction in Frankenstein; no further details  
  > "uses Gaussian process to reconstruct the intensity radial profile of a disc" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
   - validation: Profiles confirm a gap near 70 au, slightly deeper than in image-space profiles  
  > "These profiles confirm the presence of a gap centred around 70 au" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
4. **Upper limit on dust around the brown dwarf B**: Search for a point source at the expected BD position; 3 sigma flux limits converted to dust mass with radiative transfer  
  > "Nevertheless, we can place $3\sigma$ upper limits at 0.88 and 1.3 mm of 27 and 15 $\mu$Jy, respectively." (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit); "Using the same radiative transfer tools as in (cite: Perez2019protolunar), we estimate a dust mass upper limit" (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit)
   - parameter: BD expected at ~0.25'' separation towards the NE  
  > "We do not detect any emission arising from a point source towards the NE at the BD separation of $\sim0.25\ensuremath{''}$" (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit)
   - parameter: Dust accretion rate onto BD from the debris disc ~4 kg/s using Bonsor et al. 2018 with corrected K_ej = 5.14e8  
  > "We find that the BD could be accreting $\mu$m-sized dust at a rate of $\sim4$ kg s$^{-1}$." (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit); "There is a typo in Table 1 in (cite: Bonsor2018) where $K_\mathrm{ej}$ should be $5.14\times10^{8}$ (private communication with Amy Bonsor)." (Footnote 5)
   - assumption: Disc size >~0.1 au (a smaller, optically thick disc could hide more mass)  
  > "which is equivalent to a dust to planet ratio of $5\times10^{-8}$ for a disc with a size $\gtrsim0.1$ au" (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit)
   - assumption: Accreted dust: 1 um radius grains, 3 g/cm3, accreted for 700 Myr  
  > "Assuming the accreted dust is all made of grains of 1 $\mu$m in radius, have a density of 3 g cm$^{-3}$, and have been accreted for 700 Myr" (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Search for CO emission and derive CO mass and ice fraction limits**: Continuum-subtract, image cubes with tclean, apply a Keplerian mask (both rotation senses), integrate within deprojected radius of 150 au, convert flux limits to CO mass with a non-LTE tool, then to CO+CO2 ice fraction  
  > "we applied a Keplerian mask as in (cite: Matra2015; Marino2016; Matra2017fomalhaut)" (2.2 CO gas emission); "After integrating the emission within a deprojected radius of 150 au" (2.2 CO gas emission); "we use the tool developed by (cite: Matra2018) to derive CO mass upper limits for a wide range of collisional partner densities" (2.2 CO gas emission)
   - software: CASA tclean, CO mass tool of Matra et al. 2018
   - parameter: Band 7 cubes 0.43 km/s channels (1.1 km/s effective); Band 6 cubes 1.3 km/s channels (2.0 km/s effective)  
  > "This produces data cubes with channel widths of 0.43 km s$^{-1}$(effective bandwidth of 1.1 km s$^{-1}$due to Hanning smoothing) in band 7" (2.2 CO gas emission); "1.3 km s$^{-1}$(effective resolution of 2.0 km s$^{-1}$due to Hanning smoothing and averaging every 2 channels) in band 6" (2.2 CO gas emission)
   - parameter: Kinetic temperatures 20 to 200 K  
  > "a range of kinetic temperatues from 20 to 200 K" (2.2 CO gas emission)
   - assumption: Optically thin CO emission, non-LTE excitation  
  > "These CO flux upper limits can be translated to CO masses assuming optically thin emission." (2.2 CO gas emission)
   - assumption: Fractional luminosity ~3e-4, stellar mass 1.3 Msun, luminosity 2.9 Lsun, disc radius 70 au and width 100 au, photodissociation timescale 120 yr  
  > "the fractional luminosity of the system ($\sim3\times10^{-4}$)" (2.2 CO gas emission); "disc radius and width (approximately 70 and 100 au)" (2.2 CO gas emission); "an expected photodissociation rate of 120 yr (cite: Visser2009)" (2.2 CO gas emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Both rotation directions tested; no significant emission recovered  
  > "assuming a disc orientation (derived in §[3](#sec:parmodel)) and sense of rotation— we test both possible directions" (2.2 CO gas emission)
6. **Fit parametric gap disc model to visibilities**: Two-power-law surface density with Gaussian gap, radiative transfer with RADMC-3D, MCMC fit in visibility space  
  > "we fit a parametric disc model with a gap as in (cite: Marino2018hd107; Marino2019), combining radiative transfer simulations (cite: radmc3d) and an MCMC fitting procedure in the visibility space." (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Gap depth fixed to 1  
  > "here we fixed the gap depth to 1 (i.e. the surface density is zero at $r_\mathrm{g}$)" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - parameter: Vertical extent fixed to 5% of radius, uniform vertical mass distribution  
  > "We thus choose to fix the vertical extent of the disc to 5% of the radius and use a uniform vertical mass distribution" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - parameter: r_min lower limit of 14 au; outer edge fixed at 250 au  
  > "we impose a lower limit for $r_{\min}$ of 14 au" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model); "we set a fixed outer edge of 250 au" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - parameter: Free parameters include disc inclination and PA, phase centre offsets, and position, size (2D Gaussian) and flux of a background SMG ~11.5'' to the SW; spectral index alpha free and uniform  
  > "we also fit the disc inclination $i$ and position angle PA, and nuisance parameters such as phase center offsets, and the position, size (modelled as a 2D gaussian) and flux of a SMG" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model); "which we leave as a free parameter that is uniform across the disc" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - parameter: Reported values are posterior medians with 16th and 84th percentile uncertainties  
  > "The quoted values correspond to the median, with uncertainties based on the 16th and 84th percentiles of the marginalised distributions." (Table 1)
   - assumption: Dust mass counts only grains smaller than 1 cm, set by assumed opacities  
  > "Note that this dust mass only includes the mass in grains smaller than 1 cm, which is set by the assumed dust opacities (cite: Marino2019)." (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - assumption: Star modelled with Teff = 6500 K and R = 1.3 Rsun template spectrum  
  > "The star is modelled with a template spectrum corresponding to an effective temperature of 6500 K and a radius of 1.3 $R_\odot$" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - fitting: MCMC in visibility space; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "an MCMC fitting procedure in the visibility space" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
   - validation: Residual maps show no residuals above 3 sigma within 200 au; model without a gap fails to reproduce visibility wiggles beyond 50 klambda  
  > "We do not find any residuals stronger than $3\sigma$ within 200 au." (3.1 Results); "The model without a gap fails to reproduce the amplitude of the wiggles beyond 50 k$\lambda$." (3.1 Results)
7. **Convert gap width to planet mass**: Gap width equated to chaotic zone width w_g = 3 a_p mu^(2/7), using posterior of r_g and w_g  
  > "Roughly speaking, the width of the gap is expected to be the same as the size of the chaotic zone where mean motion resonances overlap, i.e. $w_\mathrm{g}\cong 3 a_\mathrm{p}\mu^{2/7}$" (3.1 Results)
   - parameter: Stellar mass 1.3 Msun  
  > "Given the estimated stellar mass of 1.3 $M_\odot$ (cite: Delorme2017)" (3.1 Results)
   - assumption: Gap truly empty and cleared by a planet on a circular orbit through scattering  
  > "assuming the gap is truly empty (as assumed in our model to derive its width) and was cleared by a planet on a circular orbit through scattering" (3.1 Results)

## Qualitative findings

- No dust is detected around B; accretion of dust from the disc cannot cause its reddening  
  > "Therefore we conclude that if dust in the atmosphere is indeed responsible for its reddening, it cannot be supplied in sufficient quantity by the circumstellar disc." (2.1.2 BD dust emission upper limit)
- CO non-detection is consistent with a comet-like volatile composition  
  > "Therefore the CO non-detection is consistent with HD 206893’s exoKuiper belt having a volatile composition similar to that of comets." (2.2 CO gas emission)
- Secular resonances between the two inner companions cannot open the 74 au gap  
  > "Therefore this scenario cannot explain the gap at 74 au." (5.2.2 Secular resonances between planet at 2 au and BD at 11 au)
- 4-5 of 6 well resolved wide debris discs show gaps  
  > "Out of 6 wide debris discs observed with ALMA with enough resolution, 4–5 show radial substructure in the form of gaps." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The system hosts a directly imaged BD and a likely inner companion, making it ideal for imaging the disc to constrain dynamics and search for more companions  
  > "These two companions make this system an ideal target to image its disc with ALMA, in order to both constrain the dynamics of this system and look for additional companions that could shape the distribution of planetesimals." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Gap depth fixed in the model because leaving it free left gap location and width poorly constrained; tapering used to gain S/N per beam  
  > "we opt to leave it fixed after a few tests where we found that if the depth is not fixed, the location and width of the gap are not well constrained" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model); "allows for an increase of the signal-to-noise per beam" (2.1 Dust continuum)
- Alternative: Gap depth left free in the model: rejected because location and width were not well constrained  
  > "if the depth is not fixed, the location and width of the gap are not well constrained" (3 Parametric axisymmetric disc model)
- Alternative: Asymmetry caused by a single compact background SMG: disfavoured by the broad radial extent of the flux difference  
  > "the flux difference does not arise from a single compact SMG" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
- Alternative: Gap opened by secular resonances with BD and a 30 au planet: ruled out by direct imaging non-detections  
  > "This is ruled-out by direct imaging which did not detect any companion more massive than 3 M$_\mathrm{Jup}$ at 30 au" (5.2.3 Secular resonances between BD and planet at 30 au)
- Alternative: Gas-drag origin of the gap: ruled out using the CO mass limit  
  > "we can also rule out that for this system gas drag could be important for mm-sized grains" (5.2.6 Planet-less scenarios)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Planet mass assumes the gap is empty at its centre; a shallower, broader gap would change the inferred width and mass  
  > "A caveat in this interpretation is that we have assumed the gap is empty at its center." (3.1 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Frankenstein profiles lack a primary beam correction  
  > "no primary beam correction is taken into account" (2.1.1 Dust radial structure)
- Limitation (authors): Lower S/N makes gap width and depth comparison with other discs difficult  
  > "Given the lower S/N in HD 206893 observations, it is hard to compare the gap width and depth with the other two." (5.4 Are gaps common among exoKuiper belts?)
- Limitation (authors): Derived planet masses must be taken with caution since the gap may be inherited or the planet may have migrated  
  > "Therefore, the derived planet masses must be taken with caution." (5.2.1 Gap clearing by planet at 74 au)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitting recipe (sampler, priors, likelihood) for the visibility model is not given, so the quoted gap parameters cannot be independently checked
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gap depth fixed to 1 may bias the gap width and hence the planet mass
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral index differs between text (2.53+-0.16) and Table 1 (2.54+-0.17)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Marginal 2 sigma asymmetry and a nearby SMG are modelled with limited detail
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Companion C constraints rely on literature RV and PMa values and a simple Hill-radii stability criterion
