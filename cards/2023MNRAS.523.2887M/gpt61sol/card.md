## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct DMA spectroscopic predictions**: Fit laboratory rotation-tunneling frequencies with interacting tunneling-state Hamiltonians; calculate rotational spectra.  
  > "Fitting and calculation of the rotational spectrum of DMA was carried out with the SPFIT and SPCAT programs (cite: spfit_1991)." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - software: SPFIT, SPCAT
   - parameter: Watson’s S reduction, representation Ir  
  > "Watson’s S reduction was used in the representation $I^r$." (Table 2)
   - parameter: Use central EE internal-rotation components  
  > "We did not use the internal rotation splitting of ground state DMA in almost all of our fits and only used the central, strong internal rotation peak ($EE$)." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - assumption: Positive Fbc for positive μb and μc; negative χbc  
  > "This is best described with $F_{bc}$ being positive when $\mu _b$ and $\mu _c$ are chosen to be positive." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters); "Its value needed to be negative once $F_{bc}$ was chosen to be positive." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - fitting: Iteratively add physically reasonable parameters that improve the fit; omit poorly determined parameters when removal does not greatly deteriorate the fit.  
  > "If at least one parameter improved the quality of the fit sufficiently, we chose the one that improved the quality the most and searched for additional parameters." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters); "It was omitted from the fit, often only temporarily, if its omission did not cause a large deterioration of the fit." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - validation: 1294 new transitions reproduced with normalized rms 0.952; rms 28.7 kHz  
  > "Our 1294 transitions were reproduced to an rms error, also known as weighted or normalized rms, of 0.952." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters); "As we employed a substantial range of different uncertainties, the rms value of 28.7 kHz is dominated by the data with larger uncertainties." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
2. **Calculate DMA partition functions**: Sum ground-state energies for Qrot; evaluate Qvib in the harmonic approximation with quantum-chemical fundamentals.  
  > "The partition function $Q_{\rm rot}$ was calculated for selected temperatures beyond the standard temperatures in the CDMS by summation over the ground state energies up to $J = 130$ and $K_a = 60$." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters); "We evaluated the vibrational partition function for a posteriori correction in the harmonic approximation." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - software: Gaussian 09
   - parameter: J ≤ 130; Ka ≤ 60  
  > "by summation over the ground state energies up to $J = 130$ and $K_a = 60$." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - parameter: B3LYP/aug-cc-pVTZ  
  > "We employed the B3LYP hybrid density functional (cite: Becke_1993; LYP_1988) for that matter together with the correlation consistent basis set augmented with diffuse basis functions commonly denoted aug-cc-pVTZ (cite: cc-pVXZ_1989)." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
   - assumption: Include spin-statistics and 14N spin-weight; reduced ortho:para weights 9:7  
  > "Spin-statistics and the spin-weight from the $^{14}$N nucleus were taken into account." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters); "We employ a reduced spin-weight of 9 : 7 for *ortho* and *para* states, respectively, instead of the full spin-weight of 72 : 56" (Table 4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare calculated fundamentals with isolated, sufficiently strong experimental fundamentals.  
  > "Wherever fundamentals are isolated and not too weak, there is usually good to reasonable agreement between experimental and calculated values." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
3. **Select ReMoCA spectra**: Analyze spectra at Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b) after improved line/continuum separation.  
  > "For this work we analyzed the spectra of two positions." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations); "We further improved the process that separates the line and continuum emission by adding two reference positions to the pool of positions that were used to find the frequency ranges that contain absorption features (cite: Belloche19)." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit dimethyl ether as the DMA template**: Fit population diagrams for rotational temperature; adjust synthetic LTE spectra for column density.  
  > "A fit to these population diagrams yields rotational temperatures of $\sim$170 K and $\sim$130 K, respectively" (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b)); "Assuming these temperatures, we adjusted synthetic LTE spectra to the observed spectra" (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: Five model parameters: source size, column density, rotational temperature, linewidth and velocity offset  
  > "Each species was modeled with a set of five parameters: size of the emitting region ($\theta_{\rm s}$), column density ($N$), temperature ($T_{\rm rot}$), linewidth ($\Delta V$), and velocity offset ($V_{\rm off}$)" (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
   - parameter: Sgr B2(N1S): emission size 2″; Sgr B2(N2b): ∼0.8″ from uncontaminated integrated-intensity maps  
  > "we assumed an emission size of 2$\ensuremath{''}$ to model the emission of dimethyl ether toward Sgr B2(N1S)." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b)); "In the case of Sgr B2(N2b), integrated intensity maps of lines of dimethyl ether that are free of contamination suggest an emission size on the order of 0.8$\ensuremath{''}$." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
   - assumption: LTE; finite angular resolution and transition optical depth included  
  > "Like in our previous ReMoCA studies (cite: Belloche19; Belloche22), we compared the observed spectra to synthetic spectra computed under the assumption of LTE with the astronomical software Weeds (cite: Maret11)." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations); "The calculations take into account the finite angular resolution of the observations and the optical depth of the rotational transitions." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
   - fitting: By-eye best-fit synthetic spectra for each molecule; sum contributions of identified molecules.  
  > "For each position, we derived by eye a best-fit synthetic spectrum for each molecule separately, and then added together the contributions of all identified molecules." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
   - validation: Dozens of ground-state lines identified at both positions; excited-state lines also detected.  
  > "Dimethyl ether is well detected toward both Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b), with dozens of lines in its vibrational ground state easily identified" (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
5. **Estimate methylamine toward Sgr B2(N2b)**: Fit a synthetic LTE spectrum using methanol’s rotational temperature and emission size.  
  > "Methylamine has a similar rotational temperature as methanol toward Sgr B2(N1S), which led us to assume the same rotational temperature and emission size as methanol toward Sgr B2(N2b)." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: Size 0.5″; Trot 140 K; FWHM 3.5 km s−1; velocity offset 0.0 km s−1  
  > "| CH$_3$NH$_2$, $\upsilon=0$ | t | 2 | 0.5 | 140 | 2.7 (17) | 1.07 | 3.5 | 0.0 | 299 |" (Table 9)
   - assumption: Methylamine shares methanol’s rotational temperature and emission size at N2b.  
  > "assume the same rotational temperature and emission size as methanol toward Sgr B2(N2b)." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
   - fitting: By-eye synthetic LTE fit  
  > "For each position, we derived by eye a best-fit synthetic spectrum for each molecule separately" (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
   - validation: Only two relatively uncontaminated transitions, at 84306 and 112273 MHz; detection classed as tentative.  
  > "Only two transitions are relatively free of contamination (at 84 306 and 112 273 MHz)." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b)); "As a result, we only claim a tentative detection of methylamine toward Sgr B2(N2b)." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
6. **Set ALMA DMA column-density limits**: Compute synthetic LTE spectra with the dimethyl ether size and temperature; vary DMA column density by hand and compare by eye.  
  > "we computed synthetic LTE spectra assuming the same emission size and rotational temperature as those derived for dimethyl ether." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b)); "by varying the column density by hand and comparing the synthetic and observed spectra by eye" (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: N1S: size 2.0″, Trot 170 K, FWHM 5.0 km s−1, Voff 0.0 km s−1, Fvib 1.40  
  > "| CH$_3$NHCH$_3$, $\upsilon=0$ | n | 0 | 2.0 | 170 | $<$ 9.8 (16) | 1.40 | 5.0 | 0.0 | $>$ 204 |" (Table 8)
   - parameter: N2b: size 0.8″, Trot 130 K, FWHM 3.5 km s−1, Voff 0.0 km s−1, Fvib 1.18  
  > "| CH$_3$NHCH$_3$, $\upsilon=0$ | n | 0 | 0.8 | 130 | $<$ 5.9 (16) | 1.18 | 3.5 | 0.0 | $>$ 1360 |" (Table 9)
   - parameter: Systemic velocities: N1S 62.0 km s−1; N2b 74.2 km s−1  
  > "$V_{\rm sys}=62.0$ km s$^{-1}$ for Sgr B2(N1S) and $V_{\rm sys}=74.2$ km s$^{-1}$ for Sgr B2(N2b)." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
   - assumption: DMA shares dimethyl ether’s emission size and rotational temperature.

## Qualitative findings

- DMA was not detected toward either ALMA hot-core position.  
  > "We did not find any evidence for dimethylamine in either source." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
- DMA was not detected in the G+0.693 survey.  
  > "The molecule is not detected in the observational survey." (Nondetection of dimethylamine toward G+0.693–0.027)
- Modeled DMA is formed mainly by early external-UV processing of young ices under low extinction.  
  > "DMA in the models is formed mainly through an early mechanism that relies on photoprocessing of the young dust-grain ices by external UV under low-extinction conditions." (Conclusion and outlook)
- Ethylamine overproduction suggests that modeled DMA abundances could also be too high.  
  > "Due to the similarities in the chemistry between ethylamine and DMA, this could indicate that DMA is also over-produced, rendering a possible future detection of this molecule even more challenging." (Comparison of chemical models with observations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: N1S has lower continuum opacity; N2b balances narrower lines against sufficient H2 column density.  
  > "This position was chosen by (cite: Belloche19) for its lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations); "This position was chosen as a compromise between getting narrow line widths to reduce the level of spectral confusion and keeping a high enough H$_2$ column density to detect less abundant molecules." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations)
- Why this method: High hot-core densities justify LTE; molecular ratios provide useful model/observation comparisons.  
  > "This assumption is justified by the high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (cite: Bonfand19)." (Search for dimethylamine toward Sgr B2(N) — Observations); "As with the observational data themselves, molecular abundance ratios may be the most useful means of comparison between models and observations." (Comparison of chemical models with observations)
- Alternative: Including internal-rotation splitting in the spectroscopic fit was attempted and failed.  
  > "attempts to introduce internal rotation into the treatment of the rotation-tunneling spectrum of DMA failed." (Spectroscopic results and determination of spectroscopic parameters)
- Alternative: Explicit G+0.693 shock modeling was recognized as preferable but replaced with a simpler pre-shock collapse comparison.  
  > "the most appropriate modeling approach would include an explicit treatment of the passage of a shock" (Astrochemical modeling); "Here, to allow a simpler comparison between the modeled abundances in the ices and the post-shock gas-phase abundances, we run collapse models similar to the first stage of the hot-core models" (Astrochemical modeling)
- Alternative: DMA proton transfer to ammonia excluded because DMA has greater proton affinity.  
  > "the proton affinity of DMA is greater than that of ammonia (cite: Hunter98), meaning that protonated DMA cannot transfer its proton in this way; such a mechanism is therefore not included in the present chemical network." (Astrochemical modeling)
- Alternative: Gas-phase DMA formation considered possible but unsupported by known experimental studies.  
  > "There remains also the possibility that DMA could be formed in the gas phase" (Comparison of chemical models with observations); "the authors are unaware of any experimental studies (cite: Anicich03)" (Comparison of chemical models with observations)
- How they decided it was enough: ALMA upper limits guided by 3σ, blends and baseline uncertainty; G+0.693 limit uses brightest unblended predicted emission.  
  > "using the 3 $\sigma$ level (dashed lines in Figs. [9](#f:remoca_ch3nhch3_n1s) and [10](#f:remoca_ch3nhch3_n2b)) as a guide, but also accounting for the blends with other species and the uncertainties on the baseline level." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b)); "To derive the upper limit to the column density, we have used the brightest emission according to the simulated LTE model that appears unblended" (Nondetection of dimethylamine toward G+0.693–0.027)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA limits do not constrain whether the analogous formation routes operate differently.  
  > "This limit is not constraining enough to infer whether or not the formation routes that possibly relate methanol to dimethyl ether and methylamine to dimethylamine operate differently." (Sgr B2(N))
- Limitation (authors): Methylamine toward N2b is only tentatively detected.  
  > "As a result, we only claim a tentative detection of methylamine toward Sgr B2(N2b)." (Search for DMA toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2b))
- Limitation (authors): G+0.693 DMA emission falls at a local baseline minimum; its upper limit can change by less than a factor of two.  
  > "Unfortunately, the predicted emission just appears in a spectral region which shows a local minimum in the data with respect to the adjusted broad band baseline." (Nondetection of dimethylamine toward G+0.693–0.027); "The upper limit to the column density of DMA could be affected by the local baseline by less than a factor of two when the local baseline uncertainty is considered." (Nondetection of dimethylamine toward G+0.693–0.027)
- Limitation (authors): DMA chemical production rates are poorly constrained.  
  > "The rates used to simulate the production of DMA and related species are only poorly constrained, largely by comparison between photodissociation rates of similar molecules (under gas-phase conditions)." (Comparison of chemical models with observations)
- Limitation (authors): The dominant early formation mechanism depends on uncertain low initial visual extinction.  
  > "It remains to be seen whether such a low initial visual extinction is appropriate to all regions of a hot core, thus DMA abundances could again be lower than the models predict." (Comparison of chemical models with observations)
- Limitation (authors): The G+0.693 models omit shock chemistry and shock-induced mantle release.  
  > "a fair comparison between models and observations should include the substantial influence of the shock chemistry and shock-induced grain-mantle release, which the present models do not do." (Comparison of chemical models with observations)
- Limitation (authors): Neglected internal rotation may matter near 100 GHz for linewidths below 2 km s−1.  
  > "Internal rotation may need to be considered around 100 GHz for sources with line widths less than 2 km s$^{-1}$." (Discussion of the spectroscopic parameters)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): By-eye upper limits lack a deterministic acceptance rule, limiting exact numerical replication.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DMA limits depend on adopting dimethyl ether’s temperature and emission size.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The N2b methylamine/DMA ratio inherits uncertainty from a tentative methylamine detection.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The supplied reaction table loses species identities, preventing reconstruction of the complete new network from this text.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The text states a hot-core methylamine/DMA model range of 37–75, but the tabulated slow ζ0 case is 79.
