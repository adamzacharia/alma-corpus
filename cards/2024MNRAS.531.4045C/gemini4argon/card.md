## Analysis recipe

1. **Generate moment maps and define galactic sub-regions**: Smooth-moment masking method: convolve 12CO(1-0) cube spatially with a Gaussian of synthesised beam width and Hanning-smooth spectrally, select pixels > 3x RMS of smoothed cube as mask, apply to original cube to generate moment 0, 1, 2 maps; divide central region into nucleus, arcs, nodes, and dust lanes.  
  > "These were generated using a smooth-moment masking method (e.g. Dame 2011). In summary, we convolved the 12CO(1-0) data cube spatially with a Gaussian of width equal to that of the synthesised beam and Hanning-smoothed the cube spectrally." (2.2 Moment maps); "To create a mask we then selected only pixels with intensities exceeding three times the RMS noise of the smoothed cube, and applied this mask to the original cube to generate the moment maps." (2.2 Moment maps); "The bottom-right panel of Figure 1 illustrates our definitions of four distinct regions based on the moment maps: nucleus (blue), arcs (green), nodes (red) and dust lanes (yellow)." (2.2 Moment maps)
   - parameter: Mask threshold = 3x RMS noise of smoothed cube; Nucleus radius <= 180 pc; Arcs velocity dispersion 0 - 25 km s^-1; Nodes velocity dispersion >= 25 km s^-1  
  > "To create a mask we then selected only pixels with intensities exceeding three times the RMS noise of the smoothed cube, and applied this mask to the original cube to generate the moment maps." (2.2 Moment maps); "The nucleus encompasses the inner 180 pc in radius. The arcs refer to the regions of relatively low velocity dispersions (0 – 25 km s−1) of the nuclear ring, while the nodes refer to the regions of relatively high velocity dispersions (≳ 25 km s−1) of the nuclear ring," (2.2 Moment maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify giant molecular clouds (GMCs)**: Run modified CPROPSTOO algorithm on the central 15'' x 15'' region of the 12CO(1-0) cube to identify 3D cloud structures and classify them as spatially and spectrally resolved or unresolved.  
  > "In this work, we run cpropstoo on a 15 [′′] × 15 [′′] region of the 12 CO(1-0) cube centred on the galaxy centre" (3.1 Cloud identification); "In this work, we run cpropstoo on a 15′′ × 15′′ region of the 12CO(1-0) cube centred on the galaxy centre, thus encompassing all of the emission detected in the zeroth-moment map (see Figure 1)," (3.1 Cloud identification)
   - software: cpropstoo
   - parameter: high-threshold = 4.5 sigma_rms, low-threshold = 2.0 sigma_rms, minvchan = 2, convexity = 0.65, Delta T_max = 2 sigma_rms = 2.20 K, minarea = 120 to 24 spaxels (step 24)  
  > "We set the minimum contrast between a cloud’s peak and its boundary at Δ𝑇max = 2𝜎rms = 2.20 K." (3.1 Cloud identification); "adopting high-threshold = 4.5𝜎rms, low-threshold = 2.0𝜎rms, minvchan = 2 and convexity = 0.65. We assign minarea a range of 120 – 24 spaxels (the synthesised beam area) with a step size of 24 spaxels in descending order," (3.1 Cloud identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified that reasonable variations in CPROPSTOO input parameters do not affect overall results and conclusions.  
  > "We note that while different sets of parameters yield slightly different cloud identification results, we have verified that for reasonable parameters the overall results and conclusions remain unaffected." (3.1 Cloud identification)
3. **Calculate GMC physical properties and uncertainties**: Extrapolate moments to infinite sensitivity (T_edge = 0 K), deconvolve spatial beam and spectral channel width to compute cloud radius R_c, observed linewidth sigma_obs,los, gradient-subtracted linewidth sigma_gs,los, CO luminosity L_CO(1-0), gas mass M_gas, and surface density Sigma_gas; estimate uncertainties via 500 bootstrap resamplings.  
  > "where 𝜎v is the second moment extrapolated to the limit of infinite sensitivity (𝑇edge = 0 K) along the velocity axis and Δ𝑉chan is the channel width of 2 km s−1." (3.2 Cloud properties); "The uncertainties of the above quantities are derived via 500 bootstrap resampling." (3.2.1 General properties)
   - software: cpropstoo
   - parameter: 500 bootstrap resamplings; Delta V_chan = 2 km s^-1  
  > "where 𝜎v is the second moment extrapolated to the limit of infinite sensitivity (𝑇edge = 0 K) along the velocity axis and Δ𝑉chan is the channel width of 2 km s−1." (3.2 Cloud properties); "The uncertainties of the above quantities are derived via 500 bootstrap resampling." (3.2.1 General properties)
   - assumption: Geometric parameter eta = 1.91; Distance D = 17.5 Mpc; CO-to-molecule conversion factor X_CO = 2 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 (alpha_CO(1-0) = 4.4 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1)  
  > "where 𝜂 is a geometric parameter, 𝜎maj,dc and 𝜎min,dc are the deconvolved RMS spatial extent along the major and the minor axis of the cloud, respectively, and we adopt 𝜂 = 1.91 for consistency with earlier studies" (3.2 Cloud properties); "To obtain the molecular gas mass, we adopt a CO-to-molecule conversion factor (including helium) 𝑋CO = 2 × 1020 cm−2 (K km s−1)−1, equivalent to a conversion factor 𝛼CO(1−0) ≈ 4.4 M⊙ (K km s−1 pc2)−1, yielding" (3.2 Cloud properties)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits overlaid on number distributions of cloud properties  
  > "the black histogram and overlaid black Gaussian fit show the full cloud sample" (3.2.1 General properties)
   - validation: Compare observed velocity dispersion (sigma_obs,los) with gradient-subtracted velocity dispersion (sigma_gs,los) to check for contamination by large-scale bulk motions/shear.  
  > "By comparing the observed velocity dispersions (𝜎obs,los) with the gradient-subtracted velocity dispersions (𝜎gs,los), we can assess whether bulk motions (due to e.g. galaxy rotation and/or shear) contribute significantly to the observed velocity dispersions of the clouds." 4. **Fit size-linewidth and virial mass-gas mass scaling relations**: Compute Spearman rank correlation, fit power-law relations between R_c and sigma_obs,los using Linmix and ODR, compute virial mass M_obs,vir and virial parameter alpha_obs,vir, fit power law between M_obs,vir and M_gas, and evaluate external pressure tracks in Sigma_gas vs sigma_obs,los R_c^-1/2 plane.  
  > "To calculate the best-fitting power-law relation taking into account both 𝑅c and 𝜎obs,los uncertainties, and to be consistent with previous GMC studies (e.g. Liu et al. 2021; Choi et al. 2023), we use a hierarchical Bayesian method called Linmix (Kelly 2007)." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation); "The top panel of Figure 7 shows the virial masses of the resolved clouds of NGC 613 (calculated using the observed velocity dispersion 𝜎obs,los; see Equation 10) as a function of their gas masses, overlaid with the best-fitting power law (black solid line)." (4.2 Cloud virialisation); "The bottom panel of Figure 7 shows the correlation between molecular gas mass surface density (Σgas) and 𝜎obs,los𝑅c−1/2 for all the resolved clouds of NGC 613, providing another perspective to assess the dynamical states of the clouds (Field et al. 2011)." (4.2 Cloud virialisation)
   - software: Linmix
   - assumption: Geometrical factor b_s = 1/5 assuming homogeneous spherical clouds  
  > "and 𝑏s is a geometrical factor that depends on the internal structure of a cloud, for which we adopt 𝑏s = 1/5 assuming the clouds are homogeneous and spherical." (4.2 Cloud virialisation)
   - fitting: Hierarchical Bayesian linear regression (Linmix) and Orthogonal Distance Regression (ODR)  
  > "To calculate the best-fitting power-law relation taking into account both 𝑅c and 𝜎obs,los uncertainties, and to be consistent with previous GMC studies (e.g. Liu et al. 2021; Choi et al. 2023), we use a hierarchical Bayesian method called Linmix (Kelly 2007)." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation); "The grey solid line in Figure 6, which shows an even steeper slope, is that from the best-fitting relation derived using the orthogonal distance regression (ODR) algorithm (𝜎obs,los ∝ 𝑅c1.58±0.15)." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation)
   - validation: Compare Linmix slope with ODR slope and binned median trend  
  > "The black curve with filled black circles shows the binned trend of NGC 613 from this work. It connects the median velocity dispersions measured in ten bins along the abscissa, each containing an equal number of clouds." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation)
5. **Estimate energetics, timescales, and azimuthal cloud evolution in the nuclear ring**: Calculate bar-driven mass inflow rates, kinetic energy injection rate E_dot_in,total, turbulent energy dissipation rate E_dot_diss, turbulence dissipation timescale t_diss, inter-node travel time t_travel, cloud-cloud collision timescale t_coll, shear timescale t_shear, and characteristic cloud lifetime t_lifetime from azimuthal cloud number drop between nodes.

   - parameter: Inflow width W_in = 100 pc; Nuclear ring radius R_NR = 270 pc; Circular velocity V_c,NR = 170 km s^-1; Annulus inner semi-major axis = 240 pc, outer = 385 pc, ellipticity = 0.3  
  > "where ⟨Σgas⟩≈ 400 M⊙ pc−2 and 𝑊in ≈ 100 pc are our measured mean molecular gas mass surface density and width of the gas inflow in both dust lanes, respectively." (4.1.2 Large-scale bars and nuclear rings); "where 𝑉c,NR is the circular velocity at the radius of the nuclear ring (𝑉c,NR ≈ 170 km s−1) and Ωp is the pattern speed of the large-scale bar." ## Qualitative findings

- Observed GMC velocity dispersions are dominated by internal turbulent motions rather than bulk motions inherited from large-scale galaxy rotation or shear.  
  > "suggesting that the observed velocity dispersions are dominated by internal turbulent motions rather than bulk motions inherited from larger-scale galaxy rotation." (3.2.2 Cloud kinematics)
- GMC rotation axes are not well aligned with the galaxy isovelocity contours.  
  > "We also confirm that the rotation axes of the NGC 613 clouds are not well aligned with the isovelocity contours, which is consistent with the case of NGC 5806, supporting that the galaxy rotation does not affect the internal rotation of the clouds." (3.2.2 Cloud kinematics)
- Clouds become denser and more massive as they migrate from the offset dust lanes into the nuclear ring.  
  > "In particular, the clouds in the dust lanes have smaller gas mass surface densities than those in the other three regions (see the double-peaked black histogram in the bottom-right panel of Figure 3). This implies that the clouds get denser as they migrate into the nuclear ring." (3.2.1 General properties)
- The number of GMCs decreases azimuthally downstream from one node to the other along the nuclear ring, while individual cloud properties (mass, size, linewidth, surface density, virial parameter) show no discernible azimuthal trend.  
  > "Interestingly, the number of clouds decreases from one node to the other (see panel (a) of Figure 8), yet other cloud properties show no discernible trend and exhibit large scatters as a function of azimuthal angle (see panels (b) – (f) of Figure 8)." (4.3 Nuclear ring clouds)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 613 is a barred spiral galaxy with nuclear ring size (300 pc), stellar mass, and molecular gas mass very similar to NGC 5806, and has ~20 pc resolution ALMA 12CO(1-0) data capable of spatially resolving individual GMCs.  
  > "This leads us to expect the nuclear ring of NGC 613 to have conditions similar to those of the NGC 5806 nuclear ring, making a comparison highly relevant." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Exploiting high spatial resolution (≈ 20 pc) Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array 12CO(1-0) observations, we study the giant molecular clouds (GMCs) in the nuclear ring and its vicinity, identifying 158 spatially- and spectrally-resolved GMCs." (ABSTRACT)
- Why this method: The modified CPROPSTOO algorithm and Linmix Bayesian regression were chosen to ensure consistency with previous WISDOM GMC studies and because the modified CPROPSTOO has fewer free parameters and is more robust in complex, crowded environments.  
  > "Due to fewer free parameters, our version is more efficient and leads to a more robust cloud identification in complex and crowded environments." (3.1 Cloud identification); "To calculate the best-fitting power-law relation taking into account both 𝑅c and 𝜎obs,los uncertainties, and to be consistent with previous GMC studies (e.g. Liu et al. 2021; Choi et al. 2023), we use a hierarchical Bayesian method called Linmix (Kelly 2007)." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation)
- Alternative: Orthogonal Distance Regression (ODR) was computed for the size-linewidth relation (giving slope 1.58 +- 0.15), but Linmix was adopted for the main discussion to maintain consistency with previous GMC studies.  
  > "The grey solid line in Figure 6, which shows an even steeper slope, is that from the best-fitting relation derived using the orthogonal distance regression (ODR) algorithm (𝜎obs,los ∝ 𝑅c1.58±0.15)." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation); "We however again note that it is the slope yielded by the Linmix algorithm that will be used for the following discussion, to be consistent with previous GMC studies." (4.1 Steep size – linewidth relation)
- Alternative: Galaxy centre environment alone as the primary driver of the steep size-linewidth relation was rejected because several other galaxy centres show shallow or no correlations.  
  > "Therefore, while the centres of galaxies may partially contribute to steep size – linewidth relations, they are not likely to be the primary driver of them." (4.1.1 Size – linewidth relation in galaxy centres)
- Alternative: AGN feedback as the dominant driver of cloud turbulence across the nuclear ring was rejected because jet-ISM interactions and SiO(2-1) shocks are spatially confined to the nucleus and two small spots.  
  > "Therefore, the impact of the AGN feedback on the molecular gas in the nuclear ring and its vicinity appears limited to small regions and thus only a portion of the NGC 613 clouds." (4.1.3 Other mechanisms inducing the high velocity dispersions of the clouds)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainty in the distance to NGC 613 is not propagated into cloud property uncertainties (causes systematic scaling).  
  > "The uncertainty of the distance to NGC 613 is not propagated through, as a distance error translates to a systematic scaling of some of the estimated quantities: 𝑅c ∝ 𝐷, 𝐿CO(2−1) ∝ 𝐷2, 𝑀gas ∝ 𝐷2, 𝑀vir ∝ 𝐷 and 𝛼vir ∝ 𝐷−1 (see Section 4)." (3.2.1 General properties)
- Limitation (authors): Kinetic energy injection and turbulent energy dissipation rate estimates carry large uncertainties, and the injection rate assumes 100% conversion of inflow kinetic energy into turbulence.  
  > "We note that this is an upper limit, as it assumes that the kinetic energy transported by the inflows is entirely converted into turbulent energy in the nuclear ring." (4.1.2 Large-scale bars and nuclear rings); "Although Equation 7 and Equation 8 contain large uncertainties, the estimated input rate is approximately half of the estimated dissipation rate." (4.1.2 Large-scale bars and nuclear rings)
- Limitation (authors): The bar pattern speed of NGC 613 has not been directly measured and cannot be derived from the central ALMA field alone, requiring an assumption of a flat outer rotation curve.  
  > "The bar pattern speed of NGC 613 has never been measured, and it is not possible to estimate it from the current observations as they cover only the central region of the galaxy." (4.1.2 Large-scale bars and nuclear rings)
- Limitation (authors): Cloud masses, surface densities, and virial parameters depend strongly on the assumed CO-to-molecule conversion factor X_CO, which could be 4-15 times lower in barred galaxy centres.  
  > "These results imply that the virial parameters of the clouds of NGC 613 could be at least four times larger than currently calculated, which would in turn imply that the clouds are not gravitationally bound but either confined by external forces or short-lived." (4.2 Cloud virialisation)
- Limitation (authors): GMC properties and scaling relation slopes can depend on the choice of cloud-finding algorithm.  
  > "However, GMC properties and in particular the slopes of scaling relations can change depending on how individual clouds are defined (e.g. Pan et al. 2015; Dobbs et al. 2019)." (5 SUMMARY)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 2 header and Section 3.2.1 text contain a typo labeling the CO luminosity as L_CO(2-1) with units 10^4 K km s^-1 pc^-2, whereas the observations and Equation 3 are for 12CO(1-0) in K km s^-1 pc^2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using a standard Galactic X_CO = 2e20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 in a barred galaxy nuclear ring likely overestimates GMC masses by a factor of several compared to both Sato et al. (2021) and Teng et al. (2022, 2023).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No single-dish (Total Power) or 7-m ACA data are included; with MRS of 5.5'' (C43-6) and 1.7'' (C43-8), extended diffuse emission in the nuclear ring and dust lanes may be spatially filtered out, affecting cloud boundary definitions and masses.
