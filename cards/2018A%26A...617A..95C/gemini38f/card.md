## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract spectra and map spatial emission of CH3NC and CH3CN**: Extracted spectra at two offset positions (0.5'' SW from B and 0.6'' NE from A); created VINE map for CH3CN and integrated emission map for CH3NC (18_1 - 17_1 v=0 line).  
  > "In this work we analyse spectra extracted from two positions, where a large number of complex organic molecules have been previously detected and the line emission is not obscured by dust." (Section 2 Observations); "In the left panel, a velocity-corrected integrated emission (VINE) map shows the emission from methyl cyanide (the 18_+-4 - 17_-+4 v8 = 1 I = +1 transitions, which are unresolved). The right panel of Fig. 1 shows a zoom in of the methyl cyanide emission and an integrated emission map of methyl isocyanide (the 18_1 – 17_1 v=0 line)." (Section 3.1 Spatial extent)
   - parameter: Target extraction positions: IRAS 16293B (16h32m22s58, -24d28m32.8s) and IRAS 16293A (16h32m22s90, -24d28m36.2s)  
  > "The first position at alpha_J2000=16h32m22s58, delta_J2000=-24deg28'32.8''... We therefore choose a position at alpha_J2000=16h32m22s90, delta_J2000=-24deg28'36.2''" (Section 2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Lines identified and checked against CDMS / updated laboratory spectroscopy predictions.  
  > "The resulting predictions will be made available in the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS; Endres et al. 2016)." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
2. **Fit synthetic LTE spectra to determine excitation temperature and column density**: Calculated synthetic LTE spectra over a parameter grid using CASSIS, comparing models to the most optically thin lines by computing minimum chi^2; corrected for dust continuum background radiation temperature; computed 3-sigma upper limit for IRAS 16293A.  
  > "To determine abundances and excitation temperatures, the spectral modelling software CASSIS was used to calculate synthetic spectra and determine best-fit spectral models to the observations assuming LTE." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures); "A large grid of models was run with column densities between 1x10^13 – 9x10^14 cm^-2 and excitation temperatures between 80 – 300 K." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures); "We use 1.05 x 3 x RMS x sqrt(Delta V x FWHM) to compute the 3sigma limit, where 1.05 is a factor to account for a 5% flux calibration uncertainty." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Grid ranges: N = 1x10^13 - 9x10^14 cm^-2, Tex = 80 - 300 K, FWHM = 0.8 - 1.2 km s^-1, Vpeak = 2.5 - 2.9 km s^-1; background radiation correction factor = 1.1 at 150 K  
  > "A large grid of models was run with column densities between 1x10^13 – 9x10^14 cm^-2 and excitation temperatures between 80 – 300 K. Line widths were varied between 0.8 – 1.2 km s^-1... The peak velocities were varied between 2.5 – 2.9 km s^-1" (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures); "The correction factor is 1.1 at 150 K towards IRAS 16293B." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), source size of 0.5''  
  > "All models assume LTE and a source size of 0.5''." (Table 2 caption)
   - assumption: Vibrational partition function correction factor of 1.17 at 150 K  
  > "Vibrational contributions to the partition function at 150 K were estimated from nu_8 and its overtones from that earlier IR study as 1.17." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: Grid search minimizing chi^2 compared against optically thin lines; error bars determined from minimum parameter change producing significant goodness-of-fit difference  
  > "The results of these models were compared to the spectra of the most optically thin lines to determine the best-fit model by computing the minimum chi^2." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures); "We therefore determine the errors by determining the minimum change to each quantity which produces a significant difference to the goodness of fit." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - validation: Visual comparison of LTE model overlaid on all detected transitions in IRAS 16293B spectra  
  > "Fig. 2 shows the LTE model overlaid on the data for all detected transitions in IRAS 16293B overlaid with the best-fit LTE model." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
3. **Model CH3NC chemistry with kinetics model**: Three-phase chemical kinetics simulation using MAGICKAL with cold collapse followed by static warm-up to 400 K at two uniform final densities.  
  > "In order to investigate the chemistry of CH3NC, we use an updated version of the three-phase chemical kinetics model MAGICKAL (Garrod 2013)." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling); "The two-stage physical model used is also similar to that of (Belloche et al. 2017), in which a cold collapse is followed by a static warm-up to 400 K." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling)
   - software: MAGICKAL
   - parameter: Collapse phase densities: n_H = 6x10^10 cm^-3 and n_H = 1.6x10^7 cm^-3; warm-up to 400 K at 2.8x10^5 yr  
  > "we run two models, using two different final densities for the collapse phase: n_H=6x10^10 cm^-3, corresponding to the continuum peak of IRAS 16293B and n_H=1.6x10^7 cm^-3, corresponding to the density of the filament between IRAS 16293A and IRAS 16293B" (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling); "The subsequent warm-up phase starts at a dust temperature of 8 K, and reaches a final temperature of 400 K at 2.8x10^5 years." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling)
   - assumption: 15% of CH3CNH+ isomerises to CH3NCH+; CH3NCH+ electron recombination branching ratio of ~65% to CH3NC + H; H + CH3NC reaction barrier of 1200 K  
  > "This intermediate is formed with enough energy that a certain percentage of these molecules can isomerise to CH3NCH+; we use the suggestion by DeFrees et al. of 15%." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling); "CH3NCH+ then recombines with free electrons, forming CH3NC and H as the primary channel (~65% of recombinations)" (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling); "This reaction is assumed to have a barrier of 1200 K, based on recent calculations of the barrier for H + HNC (Graninger et al. 2014)." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared model-predicted CH3CN/CH3NC abundance ratios at 150 K to observed ratios and limits in IRAS 16293B and A.  
  > "In the low-density case, the ratio at 150 K is ~450, which is the same order as the observed ratio in IRAS 16293B. The ratio at the same point in the high-density model is ~2.5x10^6, which is consistent with the upper limits for IRAS 16293A." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- Methyl isocyanide (CH3NC) is detected for the first time towards a solar-type protostar (IRAS 16293B), with 10 unblended and 6 blended lines above 3 sigma.  
  > "Methyl isocyanide is detected towards IRAS 16293B, with 10 unblended lines and 6 blended lines above 3sigma present in the spectra... This is the first detection of this molecule in a solar-type protostar." (Section 3 Results and analysis)
- CH3NC emission in IRAS 16293B traces the disk component with similar morphology and extent to CH3CN.  
  > "The emission traces the disk component of IRAS 16293B and shows a similar morphology and extent to methyl cyanide emission." (Section 3.1 Spatial extent)
- Chemical kinetics modelling indicates that gas-phase destruction of CH3NC (e.g. by H) is needed to reproduce high CH3CN/CH3NC ratios, and the final ratio is strongly sensitive to density.  
  > "This result shows that there needs to be some efficient mechanism for destruction of CH3NC in the gas-phase that doesn’t operate on CH3CN." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling); "The chemical modelling has also shown that the CH3CN/CH3NC abundance ratio is very sensitive to the final collapse density." (Section 5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High sensitivity and high spatial resolution of ALMA PILS Band 7 data allow detection of weak molecular emission from rare complex organic molecules without dust obscuration at offset positions.  
  > "To achieve the first point, high sensitivity observations are needed as it has shown very weak emission in the objects it has been detected in so far. In this work, we therefore utilise high sensitivity ALMA observations from the Protostellar Interferometric Line Survey (PILS)" (Section 1 Introduction); "In this work we analyse spectra extracted from two positions, where a large number of complex organic molecules have been previously detected and the line emission is not obscured by dust." (Section 2 Observations)
- Why this method: CASSIS LTE modelling allows systematic exploration of column density and excitation temperature while correcting for background continuum dust temperature.  
  > "To determine abundances and excitation temperatures, the spectral modelling software CASSIS was used to calculate synthetic spectra and determine best-fit spectral models to the observations assuming LTE." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
- Alternative: JPL line catalog for CH3NC was considered initially but rejected for final analysis because predictions had large uncertainties (>0.5 MHz or 0.4 km s^-1) and deviated to lower frequencies; replaced with new CDMS line catalogue using recent laboratory data.  
  > "The initial modelling of the methyl isocyanide emission lines was performed using the JPL catalog (Pickett et al. 1998) entry. Lines were assigned tentatively, but the predictions had large uncertainties (more than 0.5 MHz or 0.4 km s^-1), deviated to lower frequencies by almost one line width at K = 0, rapidly increasing to higher K." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
- Alternative: Positions closer to the IRAS 16293A peak continuum position were tested, but rejected due to severe line blending.  
  > "We additionally searched positions closer to the A peak continuum position, however, no lines could be identified as blending became too severe." (Section 3 Results and analysis)
- Alternative: Searching for isotopologues of CH3NC was considered but rejected because line lists are not available.  
  > "Isotopologues were not searched for as the line lists are not available." (Section 3 Results and analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Spectroscopic predictions for higher K levels were historically uncertain; new predictions rely on IR parameters.  
  > "The rotational parameter A-B and the centrifugal distortion parameter D_K, necessary for describing the K level spacing, were taken from an earlier IR study (Pliva et al. 1995)." (Section 3.2 Column densities and excitation temperatures)
- Limitation (authors): H + CH3NC reaction barrier in the chemical model is speculative (assumed 1200 K by analogy to H + HNC) and lacks experimental verification.  
  > "In particular, the reaction of H+CH3NC, which is included in both the gas-phase and the grain-surface networks, is quite speculative... but the lack of experimental work makes it difficult to produce a better prediction." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Physical chemical model uses uniform single-point density where collapse finishes before warm-up begins, which fails for extreme densities.  
  > "The chemical model is a single-point model, thus it has a uniform density." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling); "Models in which the maximum density is reached prior to any warm-up seem to provide a poor representation of the chemistry in cases where densities reach extreme values." (Section 3.3 Chemical modelling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line blending in IRAS 16293A could potentially hide faint CH3NC features or make upper limit estimates sensitive to baseline/continuum placement.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The assumed excitation temperature of 150 K for IRAS 16293A might differ from source B, affecting the derived abundance limit.
