## Analysis recipe

1. **extract spectra and identify CH3NC lines**: Spectra extracted at offset positions near A and B; CH3NC lines identified using new CDMS predictions and LTE model  
  > "Methyl isocyanide is detected towards IRAS 16293B, with 10 unblended lines and 6 blended lines above 3$\sigma$ present in the spectra." (Results and analysis)
   - software: CASSIS, CDMS
   - parameter: 3σ detection threshold  
  > "10 unblended lines and 6 blended lines above 3$\sigma$ present in the spectra." (Results and analysis)
   - assumption: Isotopologues not searched (no line lists)  
  > "Isotopologues were not searched for as the line lists are not available." (Results and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: All lines predicted by LTE model are detected  
  > "All of the lines that are predicted by a local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) spectral model are detected in the spectra." (Results and analysis)
2. **map spatial extent**: VINE map of CH3CN and integrated emission map of CH3NC 18_1-17_1  
  > "The right panel of Fig. [1](#fig:ch3ncmap) shows a zoom in of the methyl cyanide emission and an integrated emission map of methyl isocyanide (the 18$_1$ – 17$_1$ $\varv$=0 line)." (Spatial extent)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Other CH3NC lines show same extent and morphology  
  > "The other lines of methyl isocyanide also show the same extent and morphology." (Spatial extent)
3. **fit LTE column density and excitation temperature**: CASSIS LTE synthetic spectra grid compared to optically thin lines via minimum chi2  
  > "The results of these models were compared to the spectra of the most optically thin lines to determine the best-fit model by computing the minimum $\chi^2$." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: N 1e13-9e14 cm^-2; Tex 80-300 K  
  > "A large grid of models was run with column densities between 1$\times$10$^{13}$ – 9$\times$10$^{14}$ cm$^{-2}$ and excitation temperatures between 80 – 300 K." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - parameter: FWHM 0.8-1.2 km/s; peak velocity 2.5-2.9 km/s (VLSR 2.7 km/s)  
  > "The peak velocities were varied between 2.5 – 2.9 km s$^{-1}$, corresponding to one channel on either side of the $V\rm{_{LSR}}$ (2.7 km s$^{-1}$)." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - parameter: Source size 0.5"  
  > "All models assume LTE and a source size of 0.5$''$." (Table 2)
   - assumption: LTE  
  > "determine best-fit spectral models to the observations assuming LTE." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - assumption: Continuum background correction factor 1.1 at 150 K for B  
  > "The correction factor is 1.1 at 150 K towards IRAS 16293B." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - assumption: Vibrational partition function contribution 1.17 at 150 K  
  > "Vibrational contributions to the partition function at 150 K were estimated from $\nu _8$ and its overtones from that earlier IR study as 1.17." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: Grid search, minimum chi2  
  > "to determine the best-fit model by computing the minimum $\chi^2$." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **estimate uncertainties**: Minimum change to each quantity producing significant difference in goodness of fit  
  > "We therefore determine the errors by determining the minimum change to each quantity which produces a significant difference to the goodness of fit." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **upper limit for source A**: 3σ limit on brightest line 17_3-16_3 at 341.637 GHz with 1.05×3×RMS×sqrt(ΔV×FWHM)  
  > "We use $1.05{\times}3{\times}RMS{\times}\sqrt{{\Delta}V{\times}FWHM}$ to compute the 3$\sigma$ limit, where 1.05 is a factor to account for a 5% flux calibration uncertainty." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - parameter: FWHM 2.2 km/s; Tex 150 K  
  > "We assume a line width of 2.2 km s$^{-1}$ based on the line width found for nitriles towards this source by (cite: Calcutt2017), and an excitation temperature of 150 K." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - assumption: Tex of A same as B  
  > "The excitation for IRAS 16293A is adopted to be the same as IRAS 16293B and similar to other molecules detected towards this source." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **compute CH3CN/CH3NC ratios**: Ratio of CH3CN column density (Calcutt2017) to CH3NC column density  
  > "as well as the CH$_3$CN/CH$_3$NC abundance ratio using the methyl cyanide abundances determined in (cite: Calcutt2017)." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **chemical modelling of CH3NC/CH3CN**: MAGICKAL three-phase single-point model: cold collapse then static warm-up to 400 K at two final densities  
  > "The two-stage physical model used is also similar to that of (cite: Belloche2017), in which a cold collapse is followed by a static warm-up to 400 K." (Chemical modelling)
   - software: MAGICKAL
   - parameter: Final densities nH=6e10 and 1.6e7 cm^-3  
  > "$n_{\mathrm{H}}=6\times10^{10}$ cm$^{-3}$, corresponding to the continuum peak of IRAS 16293B" (Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: Collapse gas 10 K, dust 16 to 8 K; warm-up to 400 K at 2.8e5 yr  
  > "The cold collapse in the model has an isothermal gas temperature (10 K), and the dust temperature cools from an initial value of 16 K to a final value of 8 K." (Chemical modelling); "reaches a final temperature of 400 K at $2.8\times10^{5}$ years." (Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: 15% isomerisation; ~65% recombination to CH3NC+H; H+CH3NC barrier 1200 K  
  > "CH$_3$NCH$^+$ then recombines with free electrons, forming CH$_3$NC and H as the primary channel ($\sim$65% of recombinations)" (Chemical modelling)
   - assumption: Uniform density single point  
  > "The chemical model is a single-point model, thus it has a uniform density." (Chemical modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ratio at 150 K compared with observed ratios  
  > "In the low-density case, the ratio at 150 K is $\sim$450, which is the same order as the observed ratio in IRAS 16293B." (Chemical modelling)
8. **test model variants**: Adding CH3+ + HNC radiative association with various branching ratios; disabling H+CH3NC on grains and in gas  
  > "Additionally, we have explored disabling the H+CH$_3$NC grain surface reaction and gas-phase reaction, to understand how important this speculative reaction is on the CH$_3$CN/CH$_3$NC ratio." (Chemical modelling)

   - parameter: HNC association given same rate as HCN association  
  > "This process was given the same rate as the equivalent association of HCN with CH$_{3}$$^{+}$, and results in CH$_{3}$NCH$^{+}$." (Chemical modelling)

   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CH3NC emission traces the disk component of B with similar morphology to CH3CN  
  > "The emission traces the disk component of IRAS 16293B and shows a similar morphology and extent to methyl cyanide emission." (Spatial extent)
- JPL entry predictions were inaccurate; new CDMS predictions used  
  > "Lines were assigned tentatively, but the predictions had large uncertainties (more than 0.5 MHz or 0.4 km s$^{-1}$)" (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
- Disabling grain-surface H+CH3NC had little effect; disabling gas-phase reaction keeps ratio near unity  
  > "Disabling the grain surface reaction had practically no effect, however, disabling the gas-phase reaction had a significant impact at higher temperatures" (Chemical modelling)
- Ratio varies greatly between 130-170 K due to desorption and gas-phase destruction  
  > "The CH$_3$CN/CH$_3$NC ratio varies greatly in the temperature range of 130 $-$ 170 K." (Chemical modelling)
- Possible interpretation: B younger/less evolved than A  
  > "Such a scenario, if applied to IRAS 16293, suggests that the B source is younger than the A source or at least less evolved." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-sensitivity observations needed because CH3NC emission is weak  
  > "To achieve the first point, high sensitivity observations are needed as it has shown very weak emission in the objects it has been detected in so far." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Offset positions chosen for bright lines without absorption or high continuum optical depth; A position chosen to reduce blending  
  > "This position is used as the lines are particularly bright, do not have strong absorption features, and do not suffer from high continuum optical depth" (Observations); "making line blending a significant problem for identification and analysis of molecular emission." (Observations)
- Alternative: Positions closer to A continuum peak searched but too blended  
  > "We additionally searched positions closer to the A peak continuum position, however, no lines could be identified as blending became too severe." (Results and analysis)
- Alternative: Additional CH3+ + HNC formation route tested; little effect  
  > "However, it was observed that the inclusion of this process did not affect the abundance of CH$_{3}$CN in any significant way" (Chemical modelling)
- Alternative: JPL catalog entry rejected for CDMS predictions  
  > "These early laboratory data, however, extend only to 141 GHz with $J = 7-6$ and $K \le 6$." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
- How they decided it was enough: All LTE-predicted lines detected  
  > "All of the lines that are predicted by a local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) spectral model are detected in the spectra." (Results and analysis)
- Limitation (authors): H+CH3NC reaction and its barrier are speculative  
  > "In particular, there is a large uncertainty on the barrier of the CH$_3$NC+H reaction as it likely does not equal the barrier on the HNC+H reaction." (Chemical modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Low-density model may not represent probed region; need simultaneous collapse/warm-up models  
  > "Also, although the low-density model fits the observations better than the high-density model, it may not be a representative density for the regions being probed by these observations." (Chemical modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Literature comparison ratios often tentative or not robust
