## Analysis recipe

1. **identify CH3NC lines in the spectra**: Both offset positions towards A and B were searched for methyl isocyanide; lines above 3σ were compared with an LTE spectral model using CDMS-based predictions  
  > "Both offset positions towards A and B were searched for methyl isocyanide. Isotopologues were not searched for as the line lists are not available." (Results and analysis)
   - software: CASSIS, CDMS
   - parameter: Detections above 3σ; 10 unblended and 6 blended lines in B  
  > "Methyl isocyanide is detected towards IRAS 16293B, with 10 unblended lines and 6 blended lines above 3$\sigma$ present in the spectra." (Results and analysis)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium  
  > "assuming LTE" (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: All lines predicted by the LTE model are detected in the spectra  
  > "All of the lines that are predicted by a local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) spectral model are detected in the spectra." (Results and analysis)
2. **measure the spatial extent of CH3NC relative to CH3CN**: Integrated emission maps; VINE map for CH3CN to remove contamination from nearby lines  
  > "VINE maps are a method for determining the spatial scale of molecules in spectrally dense regions, with large velocity gradients." (Spatial extent)
   - parameter: CH3NC 18_1-17_1 v=0 line for integrated map  
  > "an integrated emission map of methyl isocyanide (the 18$_1$ – 17$_1$ $\varv$=0 line)" (Spatial extent)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **derive excitation temperature and column density of CH3NC in B**: Grid of synthetic LTE spectra compared to the most optically thin lines; best fit by minimum chi-squared  
  > "The results of these models were compared to the spectra of the most optically thin lines to determine the best-fit model by computing the minimum $\chi^2$." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Column density grid 1×10^13 – 9×10^14 cm^-2; Tex grid 80 – 300 K  
  > "A large grid of models was run with column densities between 1$\times$10$^{13}$ – 9$\times$10$^{14}$ cm$^{-2}$ and excitation temperatures between 80 – 300 K." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - parameter: Line widths 0.8 – 1.2 km s-1; peak velocity 2.5 – 2.9 km s-1  
  > "The peak velocities were varied between 2.5 – 2.9 km s$^{-1}$" (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - parameter: Source size 0.5''  
  > "All models assume LTE and a source size of 0.5$''$." (Table 2)
   - assumption: LTE; background radiation temperature raises the CMB value, giving a column-density correction factor of 1.1 at 150 K towards B  
  > "The correction factor is 1.1 at 150 K towards IRAS 16293B." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: Minimum chi-squared over the model grid against optically thin lines  
  > "computing the minimum $\chi^2$." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - validation: Errors from minimum change in each quantity giving a significant change in goodness of fit  
  > "We therefore determine the errors by determining the minimum change to each quantity which produces a significant difference to the goodness of fit." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
4. **set 3σ upper limit on CH3NC column density in A**: Upper limit from the brightest CH3NC line (17_3-16_3 at 341.637 GHz) using the 3σ formula with a 5% flux factor  
  > "The upper limits are determined for the brightest methyl isocyanide line in the frequency range of the observations (the 17$_3$ – 16$_3$ line at 341.637 GHz)." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - parameter: FWHM 2.2 km s-1; Tex 150 K  
  > "We assume a line width of 2.2 km s$^{-1}$ based on the line width found for nitriles towards this source by (cite: Calcutt2017), and an excitation temperature of 150 K." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - assumption: Tex for A adopted equal to B  
  > "The excitation for IRAS 16293A is adopted to be the same as IRAS 16293B" (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **compute CH3CN/CH3NC abundance ratio**: Ratio of CH3CN column density from Calcutt et al. 2018 to the CH3NC column density from step 3 (B) or step 4 (A)  
  > "as well as the CH$_3$CN/CH$_3$NC abundance ratio using the methyl cyanide abundances determined in (cite: Calcutt2017)." (Column densities and excitation temperatures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **model CH3NC and CH3CN chemistry for two collapse densities**: Single-point three-phase gas-grain model: isothermal 10 K collapse to final density, then static warm-up to 400 K; CH3NC formed via CH3+ + HCN isomerisation; destroyed by H on grains and in gas  
  > "The two-stage physical model used is also similar to that of (cite: Belloche2017), in which a cold collapse is followed by a static warm-up to 400 K." (Chemical modelling)
   - software: MAGICKAL
   - parameter: Final densities n_H=6×10^10 cm^-3 and 1.6×10^7 cm^-3  
  > "$n_{\mathrm{H}}=6\times10^{10}$ cm$^{-3}$, corresponding to the continuum peak of IRAS 16293B" (Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: Dust temperature 16 K to 8 K during cold collapse; warm-up reaches 400 K at 2.8×10^5 years  
  > "The subsequent warm-up phase starts at a dust temperature of 8 K, and reaches a final temperature of 400 K at $2.8\times10^{5}$ years." (Chemical modelling)
   - parameter: Isomerisation fraction 15%; H + CH3NC barrier 1200 K  
  > "we use the suggestion by DeFrees et al. of 15%." (Chemical modelling)
   - assumption: Water-ice binding energies CH3CN 6150 K, CH3NC 5686 K  
  > "The binding energies on water ice for methyl cyanide and methyl isocyanide were updated in the network using new values from Bertin et al. (2017a; 6150 K and 5686 K, respectively)." (Chemical modelling)
   - assumption: Barrier for CH3NC + H assumed equal to H + HNC barrier  
  > "This reaction is assumed to have a barrier of 1200 K, based on recent calculations of the barrier for H + HNC (cite: Graninger2014)." (Chemical modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Low-density model fits the observations better than the high-density model  
  > "although the low-density model fits the observations better than the high-density model" (Chemical modelling)
7. **test the role of the H + CH3NC destruction reaction**: Models rerun with the grain-surface and gas-phase H + CH3NC reactions disabled separately  
  > "Additionally, we have explored disabling the H+CH$_3$NC grain surface reaction and gas-phase reaction" (Chemical modelling)
   - software: MAGICKAL
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CH3NC is detected for the first time in a solar-type protostar, in IRAS 16293B; no lines are detected toward A  
  > "No lines are detected towards the offset position of IRAS 16293A." (Results and analysis)
- Source A and B show significant chemical differentiation, with B's ratio far lower than A's  
  > "The lack of detection of methyl isocyanide in the second component in the IRAS 16293 binary, IRAS 16293A, shows that there is significant chemical differentiation between these sources." (Discussion)
- CH3NC spatial morphology follows the disk component of B and resembles CH3CN  
  > "The emission traces the disk component of IRAS 16293B and shows a similar morphology and extent to methyl cyanide emission." (Spatial extent)
- Gas-phase destruction of CH3NC by H not acting on CH3CN is needed to reproduce the observed ratios  
  > "The chemical modelling of methyl isocyanide has also shown that the gas-phase destruction mechanism, H+CH$_3$NC, is necessary to increase the CH$_3$CN/CH$_3$NC ratio" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-sensitivity ALMA PILS data are needed because CH3NC emission is very weak in known sources  
  > "high sensitivity observations are needed as it has shown very weak emission in the objects it has been detected in so far." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Source A lines are broad, so a second position near B was chosen to limit line blending  
  > "The FWHM of lines around source A are much broader ($\sim$2$-$3 km s$^{-1}$) than around source B ($\sim$1 km s$^{-1}$) making line blending a significant problem" (Observations)
- Alternative: Radiative association of CH3+ with HNC added to the model; did not significantly change CH3CN and only modestly changed CH3NC in the low-density model  
  > "it was observed that the inclusion of this process did not affect the abundance of CH$_{3}$CN in any significant way" (Chemical modelling)
- Alternative: Searching closer to the A peak continuum position; abandoned due to blending  
  > "We additionally searched positions closer to the A peak continuum position, however, no lines could be identified as blending became too severe." (Results and analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Many uncertainties in formation and destruction pathways; initial models not expected to reproduce observations  
  > "There are many uncertainties in the formation and destruction pathways of methyl isocyanide, and it is therefore not surprising that the initial modeling attempts do not reproduce observations." (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): The H + CH3NC reaction is speculative with a large uncertainty on its barrier  
  > "In particular, there is a large uncertainty on the barrier of the CH$_3$NC+H reaction as it likely does not equal the barrier on the HNC+H reaction." (Chemical modelling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abstract gives >5517 for source A while Table 2 gives >5317; the reproducer must choose one value
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper-limit calculation depends on an assumed FWHM (2.2 km/s) and Tex (150 K) borrowed from other work, not measured in A
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two spectral positions are analysed, so the B and A ratios rest on one position each
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Chemical model is single-point with uniform density and an uncertain H + CH3NC barrier, so ratio conclusions are model-dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Column density errors are described qualitatively ('minimum change ... significant difference') without a stated threshold
