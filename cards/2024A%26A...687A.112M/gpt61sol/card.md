## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct combined spectral cubes**: Merge measurement sets with concat, adjust array visibility weights, and image spectral cubes with multiscale tclean.  
  > "The data were merged using the task ‘*concat*’. During this process, the visibility weights of the different arrays were adjusted for uniformity." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array); "Subsequently, spectral cubes were generated using the CASA task ‘*tclean*’. Here, the ‘*deconvolver*’ parameter was set to ‘*multiscale*’ mode." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)
   - software: CASA, concat, tclean
   - parameter: CASA 6.4  
  > "Following this, data combination and imaging tasks were carried out with CASA version 6.4." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5–0.8; mosaic gridding for R Scl and S Sct  
  > "For imaging, the Briggs weighting scheme was employed, with the ‘*robust*’ parameter set to values between 0.5 and 0.8, optimising the balance between sensitivity and resolution." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array); "For the sources R Scl and S Sct, imaging was gridded in mosaic mode." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract shell structure diagnostics**: Inspect moment 0 maps, systemic-velocity AARPs and PV-diagrams; extract spectra centred on each star.  
  > "azimuthally-averaged radial profiles (AARPs) of the brightness distribution at the $\varv_{\rm{LSR}}$, and position-velocity diagrams (PV-diagrams)" (Kinematical and spatial structures); "Spectra extracted centred on the star are shown" (Kinematical and spatial structures)
   - parameter: Spectral apertures approximately 10% of shell diameters  
  > "The spectra are extracted from apertures that correspond to $\approx$10% of the shell diameters" (Kinematical and spatial structures)
   - parameter: Use 12CO (2-1) for U Ant  
  > "For U Ant we use the $^{12}\rm{CO}\,(2-1)$ data for the analysis, which provides the highest angular resolution in the data presented by (cite: kerschbaumetal2017)." (Kinematical and spatial structures)
   - assumption: High shell excitation temperatures make 12CO (1-0) and (2-1) structurally comparable.  
  > "Owing to the relatively high excitation temperatures estimated for the shells, the excitation will not lead to different information in the $^{12}\rm{CO}\,(1-0)$ and$^{12}\rm{CO}\,(2-1)$ lines regarding the structure of the shells." (Kinematical and spatial structures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Apertures selected to contain present-day mass loss while retaining the distinct two-peaked profile.  
  > "chosen to be large enough to contain all the present-day mass-loss but small enough to make the distinct two-peaked profile visible" (Kinematical and spatial structures)
3. **Measure shell radii, widths and velocities**: Fit two Gaussian shells to AARPs in each velocity bin; derive RV-plots, systemic-velocity radii and beam-deconvolved widths; measure expansion velocities in extracted spectra.  
  > "we create azimuthally averaged radial profiles (AARPs) in each velocity bin for all sources and fit two gaussian shells to the profiles" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "The measured FWHM of the shells are additionally deconvolved by the observational beams." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "Values for $\varv_{\rm{sh}}$  are measured in the $^{12}\rm{CO}\,(1-0)$  spectra extracted from the ALMA observations (in $^{12}\rm{CO}\,(2-1)$ for U Ant)." (Table 3)
   - parameter: Radii from 360-degree AARPs; widths from single two-degree position-angle intervals  
  > "we therefore measure the shell radii $R_{\rm{sh}}$ in the AARPs over 360 degrees." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "we measure the shell widths $\Delta R_{\rm{sh}}$ over position-angles covering only a single two-degree interval." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
   - parameter: Width position angles: U Ant 350-352; R Scl 225-227; V644 Sco 315-317; S Sct 240-242; DR Ser 265-267 deg.  
  > "| U Ant    | 260      | 24                 | inner | 41.2          | 1.5                  | 350-352" (Table 3); "| R Scl    | 360      | -19                | inner | 16.3          | 1.7                  | 225-227" (Table 3); "| V644 Sco | 700      | -21.5              | inner | 9.0           | 0.5                  | 315-317" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Largest spatial separation at systemic velocity; largest velocity separation at line-of-sight shell caps.  
  > "The largest *spatial* separation of the two observed shells in the RV-plots is expected to be at the systemic velocity" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "The largest *velocity* separation is expected at the caps of the shells along the line of sight" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
   - fitting: Two Gaussian shell profiles per velocity bin; optimizer and statistical uncertainties not stated.  
  > "fit two gaussian shells to the profiles, providing the projected shell radii as a function of velocity" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
   - validation: Compare measured RV tracks with theoretical spherical-shell loci and extracted expansion velocities.  
  > "we indicate the location of theoretical, spherical shells consistent with the parameters of the shells presented in Table" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "for all sources the measured radii and expansion velocities are consistent with two nearly spherical shells, with a faster inner and a slower outer shell." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
4. **Estimate shell ages**: Use shell radii and expansion velocities to estimate age ranges.  
  > "Given the radii and expansion velocities of the shells, we can estimate the age of the shells using their velocities as upper and lower limits." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
   - assumption: Outer emission traces decelerated material; inner emission is less affected by interaction with previous winds.  
  > "Since the outer emission likely traces material that has slowed down since it was ejected, the velocities from the outer shells provide upper limits to the shell ages." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "The inner emission has not been as affected by the interaction with the previous winds" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Check missing flux and interior emission**: Convolve ALMA data with single-dish beams and compare spectra; inspect channel maps and AARPs for interior emission.  
  > "To address the possibility of resolved-out flux, we compare the ALMA data convolved with the single-dish beam to the single-dish spectra" (DR Ser: a filled shell); "Significant emission inside the shell can be seen at all velocities" (DR Ser: a filled shell)
   - parameter: DR Ser maximum recoverable scale approximately 25'', shell diameter 15''  
  > "in the observations of DR Ser is $\approx$ 25$''$ – significantly larger than the shell diameter (15$''$)." (DR Ser: a filled shell)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: V644 Sco and S Sct recover single-dish flux; U Ant loses flux; DR Ser has no 12CO (1-0) single-dish comparison.  
  > "Unfortunately we do not have spectra in $^{12}\rm{CO}\,(1-0)$ towards DR Ser." (DR Ser: a filled shell); "However, for V644 Sco and S Sct the comparison shows that ALMA is not resolving out any flux, while for U Ant the opposite is clearly the case (cite: kerschbaumetal2017)." (DR Ser: a filled shell)
6. **Prepare complementary single-dish spectra**: Average calibrated APEX spectra, subtract first-degree polynomial baselines fitted to emission-free regions, and correct for main-beam efficiencies.  
  > "were post-processed only through averaging of the separate spectra and subtracting a first-degree polynomial baseline fitted to the emission-free parts of the spectrum." (Single-dish observations); "Lastly, we corrected the spectra for the relevant main-beam efficiencies" (Single-dish observations)
   - parameter: Final rms 11-16 mK at 0.9 km s^-1  
  > "The final spectra have characteristic rms noise levels of $11-16$ mK at a velocity resolution of 0.9 km s$^{-1}$." (Single-dish observations)
   - parameter: SEST beams 44'', 23'', 15'' with efficiencies 0.75, 0.50, 0.25; APEX beams 17'', 13'' with efficiencies 0.79, 0.58.  
  > "| Beam     | SEST      | 44$''$ (0.75)         | 23$''$ (0.50)         | 15$''$ (0.25)" (Table 2); "|          | APEX      |                       |                       | 17$''$ (0.79)         | 13$''$ (0.58)" (Table 2)
   - assumption: 20% uncertainty in peak intensities  
  > "We assume an uncertainty of 20% in the peak intensities." (Table 2)
   - fitting: First-degree polynomial baseline fitted to emission-free spectrum  
  > "subtracting a first-degree polynomial baseline fitted to the emission-free parts of the spectrum." (Single-dish observations)

## Qualitative findings

- Brighter, more coherent outer CO emission surrounds fainter, more filamentary inner emission; the inner component expands faster.  
  > "The observed emission is separated into two distinct components: a more coherent, bright outer shell and a more filamentary, fainter inner shell." (abstract); "The kinematic information shows that the inner sub-shells move at a higher velocity relative to the outer sub-shells." (abstract)
- R Scl interior structures appear as intersecting arcs.  
  > "the improved sensitivity shows that the structures around R Scl rather are a series of intersecting arcs." (Arc-structures around R Scl)
- V644 Sco has partial shell-like emission outside its detached shell; DR Ser has smooth interior emission.  
  > "V644 Sco shows emission from clear shell-like structures at distances from the star outside the detached shell." (V644 Sco: additional shells); "for DR Ser there is clear smooth emission also at radii smaller than the shells." (DR Ser: a filled shell)
- Earlier shell masses are consistent with the brighter outer CO component but may miss substantial mass.  
  > "While our models show that the masses determined in earlier work are consistent with the emission from the outer shell, this emission likely misses a significant amount of mass in the shells." (Radiative transfer models)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution resolves shell spatial and kinematic splitting; multiple CO transitions constrain masses and temperatures.  
  > "We expected the high-angular resolution data to probe in detail the spatial and kinematical properties of the shells" (Introduction); "Modelling multiple transitions of CO is an effective way of constraining the masses and temperatures in the detached shells (cite: schoieretal2005; maerckeretal2016a)." (Single-dish observations)
- Why this method: RV-plots use the entire data set at each velocity; narrow position-angle intervals reduce width broadening from asymmetry.  
  > "However, while the PV-diagrams are only along a cut in one direction, the RV-plots use information in the entire data set at each velocity." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "deviations from symmetry artificially broaden the shell-profiles." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
- Alternative: Initially attempted two-shell radiative transfer modelling, then restricted modelling to the outer shell because CO-to-H2 structure and velocity evolution are uncertain.  
  > "Initially, we attempted to model the emission from two shells." (Radiative transfer models); "However, it is likely that the emission does not trace two shells, but rather inner and outer regions of one shell" (Radiative transfer models); "In addition, the complexity of the velocity fields and the evolution of the expansion velocities complicates the modelling." (Radiative transfer models)
- Alternative: A single shell with radially reduced CO abundance and a velocity gradient was too degenerate for meaningful modelling.  
  > "We investigated the possibility to model a single-shell with a $f_{\rm{CO}}$$(r)$ that reduces the amount of CO in the centre of the shell and a velocity gradient across the shell." (Comparison to hydrodynamical models: single vs. double shells); "the problem contains too many free parameters and is too degenerate to model to obtain meaningful results." (Comparison to hydrodynamical models: single vs. double shells)
- Alternative: High optical-depth effects cannot explain the double CO structure.  
  > "the optical depths are $\lesssim$ 1.3 and hence too low for the structures to be explained by high optical-depth effects" (Comparison to hydrodynamical models: single vs. double shells)
- Alternative: A Gaussian H2 shell could imply an implausibly large total mass; a flatter density profile is preferred.  
  > "For a single *Gaussian* shell of H$_2$, the CO emission would originate from the wings of the Gaussian distribution, and the total mass may be significantly higher." (Comparison to hydrodynamical models: single vs. double shells); "such a large total mass is unlikely, even if mass is swept-up from the pre-pulse mass-loss." (Comparison to hydrodynamical models: single vs. double shells); "A more flat-topped H$_2$ density distribution is more likely" (Comparison to hydrodynamical models: single vs. double shells)
- Alternative: An earlier thermal pulse is essentially excluded as the origin of the V644 Sco outer arc.  
  > "Assuming a velocity of 25 km s$^{-1}$, the time between this outer arc and the detached shell corresponds to $\approx$500 years." (V644 Sco: additional shells); "This essentially excludes the possibility that the arc would have been created in an earlier thermal pulse." (V644 Sco: additional shells)
- How they decided it was enough: An average radius suffices for this analysis; model agreement is described qualitatively.  
  > "An average radius suffices for the analysis of the shells we provide here" (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser); "Overall the models fit reasonably well." (Radiative transfer models)
- Limitation (authors): CO brightness does not necessarily trace H2 density.  
  > "the observed CO emission, that is the brightness distribution, does not necessarily trace the gas density distribution of H$_2$" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial splitting is less apparent for V644 Sco and DR Ser.  
  > "For the smaller shells, V644 Sco and DR Ser, the separation is less apparent." (Observed double-shell structures in U Ant, V644 Sco, S Sct, and DR Ser)
- Limitation (authors): U Ant has resolved-out flux; DR Ser may also lose some flux and lacks the direct 12CO (1-0) single-dish check.  
  > "Unfortunately we do not have spectra in $^{12}\rm{CO}\,(1-0)$ towards DR Ser." (DR Ser: a filled shell); "for V644 Sco and S Sct the comparison shows that ALMA is not resolving out any flux, while for U Ant the opposite is clearly the case" (DR Ser: a filled shell); "some minor negative features in the AARP of DR Ser in Fig. [1](#f:momentpv) indicate that possibly some of the flux is resolved out." (DR Ser: a filled shell)
- Limitation (authors): Unknown additional components and velocity profiles complicate the spectra.  
  > "all with unknown velocity profiles, contribute to the observed spectra, and complicate the interpretation." (Radiative transfer models)
- Limitation (authors): CO-based shell masses are uncertain lower limits.  
  > "Hence, any mass estimate from the radiative transfer models is only a lower limit and very uncertain." (Radiative transfer models)
- Limitation (authors): CO destruction in shell centres is unconfirmed; detailed three-dimensional modelling and complementary observations are needed.  
  > "it is unclear whether CO would be destroyed at the centre of a shell." (Conclusions); "Full 3-dimensional hydrodynamical models, coupled with radiative transfer, will be necessary to gain a full understanding of the shells." (Conclusions); "Observations of CI at high resolution towards the detached-shell sources may shed light on the distribution of CO in the shells" (Conclusions)
