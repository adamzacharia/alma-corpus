## Analysis recipe

1. **Reconstruct continuum maps, extract radial profiles, and compute continuum SED and spectral indices for disk and envelope**: Reconstruct continuum maps across the three setups with a common 0.35 arcsec clean beam, extract radial brightness profiles in elliptical annuli (PA = 167 deg), measure disk fluxes via 2D Gaussian fits (or closed contours at 15, 120, 230 sigma) and envelope fluxes via contours (>5 sigma excluding the disk), and perform a linear fit in log-log scale to compute the SED spectral index.  
  > "The radial profile of the continuum emission, taken from increasing elliptical annuli oriented at a position angle of 167$^\circ$ East of North (the orientation of the inner envelope), is displayed in Fig. 3." (Dust emission); "With the continuum data aligned in position across the three FAUST setups, we reconstructed the continuum maps with a common clean beam of $0.35\ensuremath{''}$. We then computed the Spectral Energy Distribution (SED) from a linear fit (in log-log scale) over the fluxes observed in the three setups (Fig. 4)." (Dust emission); "Note that the flux density (and error) used to construct the SED are derived from 2D Gaussian fits over the disk emission, where the emission is centrally peaked, while the envelope flux is derived from a contour excluding the disk." (Dust emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Common clean beam = 0.35 arcsec; elliptical annuli PA = 167 deg East of North; disk closed contours at 15, 120, 230 x rms (setups 3, 1, 2); envelope contour > 5 x rms  
  > "With the continuum data aligned in position across the three FAUST setups, we reconstructed the continuum maps with a common clean beam of $0.35\ensuremath{''}$." (Dust emission); "The circumbinary disk emission over which the SED was computed is determined as the first closed contour around it (i. e., not encompassing the extended N-S emission): 15, 120 and 230 times the rms noise of the beam-matched maps in setups 3, 1, and 2, respectively." (Dust emission)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fits over disk emission; linear fit in log-log scale over the three setup frequencies (100.6, 225.4, 253.0 GHz)  
  > "We then computed the Spectral Energy Distribution (SED) from a linear fit (in log-log scale) over the fluxes observed in the three setups (Fig. 4)." (Dust emission)
   - validation: Compared derived circumbinary disk spectral index (3.0 +/- 0.1) with Alves et al. (2019) covering Bands 3, 6, and 7.  
  > "Table 2 reports the peak flux and flux density for the circumbinary disk, for which we find a spectral index of $3.0 \pm 0.1$. The same index is reported by (cite: Alves2019), whose observations cover ALMA Bands 3, 6 and 7." (Dust emission)
2. **Create moment maps, extract spectra, identify molecular transitions, and fit Gaussian velocity components**: Create integrated intensity (moment 0) and velocity field (moment 1) maps for methanol lines using 12-m data; extract average spectra over the >4 sigma polygon of the 218.440 GHz moment 0 map; identify transitions using CASSIS linked to CDMS/JPL databases; fit 3 Gaussian velocity components (-2.0, 2.8, 9.9 km/s) using Levenberg-Marquardt fitting in CASSIS; fit 2D Gaussians to channel maps using CASA imfit to measure deconvolved component sizes.  
  > "We extracted the average spectrum over the region above 4$\sigma$ in the integrated intensity map for each molecular transition (either in Jy beam$^{-1}$ or in Kelvin) into the CASSIS software" (CH$_{3}$OH line identification); "shows the results of the best-fit adjustment using the Levenberg-Marquardt fitting within CASSIS using a large number of iterations" (CH3OH line identification); "Within CASA (*imfit* command), we fitted a 2D gaussian to the integrated intensity maps of the three high spectral resolution transitions, taking into account the three velocity components." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification)
   - software: CASA, CASSIS, GILDAS
   - parameter: Polygon extraction threshold > 4 sigma in integrated intensity map; 3 velocity components at V_LSR = -2.0, 2.8, and 9.9 km/s  
  > "To achieve that, we defined a polygon extracted from the average spectrum over a region above 4$\sigma$ in the integrated intensity map of the 218.440 GHz methanol transition." (Modelling of the methanol and dust emission); "From these figures we can clearly distinguish three components, which are visible at -2 km s$^{-1}$, 2.8 km s$^{-1}$ and 9.9 km s$^{-1}$." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification)
   - assumption: Systemic velocity V_LSR = 3.6 km/s  
  > "The systemic velocity of [BHB2007] 11 with respect to the local standard of rest is V$\rm_{LSR}$ = 3.6 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Onishi1999)." (Line identification)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt spectral fitting within CASSIS; 2D Gaussian image fitting with CASA imfit  
  > "shows the results of the best-fit adjustment using the Levenberg-Marquardt fitting within CASSIS using a large number of iterations" (CH3OH line identification); "Within CASA (*imfit* command), we fitted a 2D gaussian to the integrated intensity maps of the three high spectral resolution transitions, taking into account the three velocity components." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification)
   - validation: Compared 12-m array spectrum with combined 7-m + 12-m spectrum for the 243.916 GHz line to confirm negligible missing flux; checked for line blending with CH3OCHO.  
  > "In fact, we confirmed that the flux of the data combined with the 7-m and 12-m array data (maximum recoverable scale: 16$^{\prime\prime}$–19$^{\prime\prime}$) is comparable with that of the 12-m array data (see Fig. 9 for the 243.916 GHz transition)." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification); "Due to the molecular complexity of this source we explored the line blending from other species." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification)
3. **Estimate beam-averaged methanol column density and rotational temperature via LTE Rotational Diagram analysis**: Construct rotational diagrams in CASSIS for each of the three velocity components from the integrated intensities extracted in the 4-sigma polygon of the 218.440 GHz transition, excluding the blended 261.7 GHz component at -2 km/s, and fit a straight line (order 1 polynomial) accounting for rms and 10% calibration uncertainty.  
  > "From the CASSIS Line Analysis module, the user can fit the lines and produce a detailed *.rotd* file with the integrated area and *rms* values for each transition." (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis); "All the transitions are shown, although the blended transition of the 261.7 GHz methanol transition at -2 km s$^{-1}$ is not used in the fit." (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Calibration uncertainty = 10%  
  > "We estimate an overall systematic uncertainty of 10% for each dataset." (Observations); "where cal is the calibration value ($\%$), W is the integrated area (in K km s$^{-1}$), rms is the noise around the selected species (in K), FWHM is the full width at half maximum (km s$^{-1}$) and $\delta$V is the bin size (in km s$^{-1}$)." (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), optically thin emission, beam-filling factor of 1 (beam-averaged)  
  > "For a molecule in LTE, all excitation temperatures are the same" (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis); "where the column density is the beam-averaged one and the lines are predicted to be optically thin." (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis)
   - fitting: Order 1 polynomial (straight line y = ax + b) fit to ln(N_u/g_u) versus E_u  
  > "The error bars should be taken into account for the order 1 polynomial fit, in order to obtain a reliable value for the uncertainty on the rotational temperature as well as the total column density." (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis)
   - validation: Tested removing transitions with E_up < 150 K and confirmed results do not change.  
  > "Removing the transitions with upper energy less than 150 K does not change the results because of the wide dispersion in these points." (LTE Rotational Diagram analysis)
4. **Derive gas kinetic temperature, H2 volume density, methanol column density, and source size via non-LTE LVG radiative transfer modelling**: Model the 12C and 13C methanol transitions up to J=15 simultaneously for each of the three velocity components using the LVG code grelvg over a grid of ~10000 models, minimizing chi^2 over N(CH3OH), n_H2, T, and source size theta_s.

## Qualitative findings

- Faint extended dust continuum emission oriented north-west to south-east is detected at the 3-sigma level in Band 6 beyond the previously known envelope.  
  > "At the present resolution, the proto-binary system reported by (cite: Alves2019) is not resolved, but faint, extended emission oriented in a north-west south-east direction is detected (at a 3$\sigma$ level) in the $\sim 1$ mm bands (setups 1 and 2) beyond the envelope." (Dust emission)
- Twelve CH3OH transitions (E_up = 45-537 K) and one 13CH3OH transition are detected, all showing three velocity components at -2.0, 2.8, and 9.9 km/s.  
  > "Twelve methanol lines have been detected with upper energies in the range [45–537] K along with one $^{13}$CH$_3$OH transition and one methyl formate (CH$_3$OCHO) line blended with one of the methanol transitions." (abstract); "From these figures we can clearly distinguish three components, which are visible at -2 km s$^{-1}$, 2.8 km s$^{-1}$ and 9.9 km s$^{-1}$." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification)
- The -2.0 and +9.9 km/s methanol components are located north-west and south-east of 11B and are likely associated with shocked gas from accretion streamers onto 11B, whereas the 2.8 km/s component is associated with 11A or the region between 11A and 11B.  
  > "The three positions are clearly identified in the moment 0 maps: the 2.8 km s$^{-1}$ component in between [BHB2007] 11 A and [BHB2007] 11 B, while the -2 and 9.9 km s$^{-1}$ are located North-West and South-East from [BHB2007] 11 B, respectively." (Origin of the observed methanol components); "This could either be due to the fact that the dust emission is very optically thick towards the hot corinos and/or the hot methanol gas is associated with shocked gas within an accretion streamer falling onto source [BHB2007] 11 B." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: The ALMA Large Program FAUST provides uniform frequency settings, angular resolution (~50 au), and sensitivity designed to survey how chemical complexity is transferred from the large-scale envelope to the inner disk/envelope system of Class 0/I protostars.  
  > "In this context, the ALMA (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array) Large Program FAUST (Fifty AU STudy of the chemistry in the disk/envelope system of Solar-like protostars) is designed to survey the chemical composition of a sample of 13 Class 0/I protostars at the scale of about 50 to 1000 au (all sources have a distance lower than 250 pc)." (Introduction); "The FAUST survey provides a uniform sample in terms of frequency setting, angular resolution and sensitivity." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used 12-m only data for methanol because the emission is compact (<3 arcsec) and missing flux is negligible; used non-LTE LVG modelling (including 13CH3OH) because LTE rotational diagrams suffer from beam dilution, high line optical depth, and sub-thermal excitation; used 2D Gaussian centroid fitting per velocity channel because at high SNR it achieves astrometric precision better than the synthesised beam (~0.02 arcsec).  
  > "Since the CH$_3$OH emission is compact and its size is less than 3$^{\prime\prime}$, the missing flux in the 12 m array data is negligible." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification); "Finally, even though the source size and the optical depths of the lines can be corrected a posteriori (using the rotational diagram analysis), non-LTE effects (e.g. sub-thermally populated lines), if present, cannot be corrected unless performing a non-LTE analysis." (Non-LTE LVG analysis); "Centroid fitting to the compact molecular emission yields astrometry with precisions better than those of the spatial resolution in the case of high SNR." (Origin of the observed methanol components)
- Alternative: Combining 7-m and 12-m array data for the methanol line analysis was tested and rejected in favor of 12-m array data only, because the methanol emission is compact (<3 arcsec) and the combined flux was comparable to the 12-m only flux.  
  > "In fact, we confirmed that the flux of the data combined with the 7-m and 12-m array data (maximum recoverable scale: 16$^{\prime\prime}$–19$^{\prime\prime}$) is comparable with that of the 12-m array data (see Fig. 9 for the 243.916 GHz transition). We therefore used in the following the 12-m array data only." (CH$_{3}$OH line identification)
- Alternative: LTE Rotational Diagram analysis was performed and rejected as a reliable estimator of column density and gas temperature because of beam dilution, high optical depths in 12CH3OH lines, and non-LTE excitation effects.  
  > "In summary, in the RD analysis, all of the above mentioned effects lead to an underestimation of the methanol column density and incorrect gas temperature." (Non-LTE LVG analysis)
- Alternative: Origin of the observed methanol from thermal desorption inside classic hot corinos centered on 11A and 11B was considered and disfavoured based on high foreground dust optical depth and the spatial offsets of the velocity centroids.  
  > "However, the above analysis on the location of the gas (§ 7.1 and 7.2) and the dust optical depth towards 11A and 11B (§ 6.3) is not in favour of the hypothesis that the observed hot methanol originates in the 11A or 11B cores, therefore invalidating their hot corino nature." (Origin of the gaseous methanol: shock versus thermal desorption)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The ~0.4 arcsec ALMA synthesised beam cannot spatially resolve the binary components 11A and 11B, which are separated by 0.2 arcsec (28 au).  
  > "The methanol emission is compact (FWHM $\sim$ 0.5$^{\prime\prime}$) and encompasses both protostars, which are separated by only 0.2$^{\prime\prime}$ (28 au). In addition, the overall methanol line emission presents three velocity components, not spatially resolved by our observations." (abstract); "The beam encompasses both 11A and 11B, separated by 0.2$^{\prime\prime}$, which therefore cannot be resolved." (Molecular emission)
- Limitation (authors): The three methanol velocity components are not completely spectrally independent and suffer from mutual velocity blending, which can bias the channel-by-channel 2D Gaussian centroid positions.  
  > "It is clear from Fig. 6, 7 and 8 that the three velocity components are not completely spectrally independent and that they are affected by blending from one another." (Origin of the observed methanol components); "Also, Fig. 18 (right panel), must be analysed with caution as the overlap of velocity ranges from one component to another can be misleading to conclude that we have an elongated structure for the methanol emission." - Limitation (authors): Non-LTE LVG modelling is restricted to methanol transitions up to J=15 because collisional coefficients with para-H2 are only available for the first 256 levels (10-200 K).  
  > "We used the collisional coefficients with para-H$_2$, computed by (cite: Rabli2010) between 10 and 200 K for the first 256 levels and provided by the BASECOL database (cite: Dubernet2012; Dubernet2013). We therefore used the methanol transitions up to J=15 for the non-LTE analysis." (Non-LTE LVG analysis)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because the 0.4 arcsec beam is twice the binary separation (0.2 arcsec) and the three velocity components spectrally blend with one another, single-Gaussian 2D centroid fitting in overlapping velocity channels will measure an intensity-weighted photocenter rather than true distinct physical positions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The non-LTE LVG analysis extracts all line intensities from a single common polygon (>4 sigma of the low-excitation 218.44 GHz line) while assuming a single uniform spherical source size theta_s per velocity component, even though low-E_up transitions trace more extended gas (~1 arcsec) than high-E_up transitions (~0.5 arcsec).
