## Analysis recipe

1. **Map the basic outflow morphology**: Integrate blue- and red-shifted SO and SiO emission separately, excluding absorption pixel by pixel  
  > "The pixel-dependent integration starts from the first channel that no longer shows absorption (starting from the systemic velocity, $\varv_{\rm sys}$) and progresses up to the respective outer integration limits." (SiO and SO emission)
   - parameter: SO outer limits: 15 and 115 km s^-1; SiO: 5 and 130 km s^-1  
  > "For blue- and red-shifted SO emission, these outer limits are fixed at 15 and 115 km s$^{-1}$, respectively, while for SiO emission, these are at 5 and 130 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (SiO and SO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare SO and SiO morphologies and spectra; inspect another SO transition that is only in emission  
  > "Despite the difference in angular resolution of their maps, both molecules trace generally the same morphology." (SiO and SO emission); "a second SO transition at 109.25 GHz ($J=3_2-2_1$, orange), which is only in emission, is shown for comparison of the line profiles." (SiO and SO emission)
2. **Separate outflow emission from hot-core emission and blends**: Construct LVINE maps using velocity/linewidth templates and paired transitions; integrate the lower-intensity transition channel by channel with a sensitivity-dependent exception  
  > "For each molecule, the integration strategy uses both selected transitions such that for a given channel the transition with the lowest intensity is used." (Integration strategy); "The procedure is slightly more complicated when the intensity of one or both transitions is below 2$\sigma$" (Integration strategy); "In theses cases, we use the spectrum obtained with better sensitivity for integration." (Integration strategy)
   - parameter: Inner limits at v_t ± FWHM_t, shifted two channels farther from source velocity  
  > "The channels where the velocity is $\varv_t\pm FWHM_t$ were first computed for each pixel." (Integration strategy); "We pushed these limits by two additional channels away from the source velocity to define the inner integration limits of the blue- and red-shifted emission" (Integration strategy)
   - parameter: Inspect spectra every 0.5'' over 8''×4''  
  > "We compared the spectra of both transitions over an area of $8\ensuremath{''}\times 4$$''$ around Sgr B2 (N1) by extracting spectra every 0.5$''$." (Integration strategy)
   - parameter: Templates: ethanol 108.44 GHz, Eu/k=88 K; methanol 95.91 GHz, Eu/k=21 K; 5σ detection threshold  
  > "We selected one transition of ethanol at 108.44 GHz with an upper level energy $E_u/k$ of 88 K" (Integration strategy); "we used a transition of methanol at 95.91 GHz and an upper level energy of 21 K." (Integration strategy); "Where neither ethanol nor methanol are detected above an intensity threshold of 5$\sigma$" (Integration strategy)
   - parameter: Match paired-cube resolutions with a two-dimensional Gaussian kernel  
  > "the cube of higher angular resolution was smoothed with a two-dimensional Gaussian kernel to the resolution of the other cube." (Integration strategy)
   - parameter: Fixed species-dependent outer limits; low/high-velocity maps split each integration interval into equal channel halves  
  > "The outer integration limits are set to a fixed value based on the comparison of the spectra of the two transitions for each molecule" (Integration strategy); "The low-velocity maps show emission integrated from the inner integration limits up to half the number of channels used for the full integration interval" (Details on the outflow morphology)
   - assumption: Paired transitions of similar upper-level energies and Einstein A coefficients have similar intensities; contaminants are unlikely at identical velocities in both  
  > "For each molecule, we made use of two transitions of similar upper-level energies and Einstein A coefficients, because these have similar intensities." (Integration strategy); "such contaminating emission is unlikely to appear at exactly the same velocities in both spectra" (Integration strategy)
   - fitting: Use Paper I template maps with improved methanol fits at larger distances  
  > "the methanol line at these positions was not properly fitted during the derivation of the peak-velocity and linewidth template maps." (Integration strategy); "Therefore, we improved the procedure and used the updated peak-velocity and linewidth maps" (Integration strategy)
   - validation: Overplot paired spectra across the region to distinguish target emission from contaminants  
  > "Overplotting both spectra within the same velocity range helped us identifying which part of the blue- and red-shifted emission belongs to the molecule of interest and what is contamination from other species." (Integration strategy)
3. **Identify kinematic features and estimate dynamical ages**: Compare position-velocity diagrams, integrated maps, and velocity-channel maps; estimate ages from projected lengths and velocities over an inclination range  
  > "Positions close to the hot core’s centre and those containing emission from other species are masked." (Position-velocity diagrams); "These most extreme values of inclination result in a lower limit on the dynamical age of 2 kyr and an upper limit of 57 kyr." (Morphology of blue- and red-shifted emission)
   - parameter: Velocity-channel maps integrated over 10 km s^-1  
  > "Each channel map contains integrated intensities over a 10 km s$^{-1}$ velocity interval" (Velocity-channel maps)

## Qualitative findings

- Bipolar SO/SiO emission: blue southeast, red northwest; collimated high-velocity features coexist with wider-angle lower-velocity emission  
  > "The morphology of the SO and SiO maps shows that blue-shifted emission extends towards the southeast and red-shifted emission towards the northwest." (Summary); "Most striking are highly collimated features in both blue- and red-shifted emission that are observed at high velocities, which may be associated with molecular jets." (Summary); "However, the bulk of emission is found at lower velocities with a larger opening angle." (Summary)
- O-bearing molecules are depleted at N1SE1, most strongly in OF; most N-bearing abundances remain similar to N1S  
  > "purely O-bearing molecules are less abundant in N1SE1:HC and even less in N1SE1:OF than in N1S, while most values for N-bearing molecules are similar in these components." (Summary)
- HC3N is enhanced; HC5N is only detected at N1SE1:OF  
  > "HC$_3$N is more abundant in both components at N1SE1 and in N1NW3:OF than in N1S and HC$_5$N is only detected in N1SE1:OF." (Summary)
- Outflow positions do not show elevated rotational temperatures compared with the previously analysed hot-core positions  
  > "the outflow positions do not stand out compared to the previously analysed positions (N1S, N1S1, and N1W1)." (Temperatures and velocities)
- No general abundance correlation with G0.693 or L1157-B1  
  > "No general trend between the two other sources and any position in Sgr B2 (N1) can be observed." (Comparison to chemical composition of other sources impacted by shocks)
- A hot-core phase preceding the shock is proposed; chemical confirmation requires models adapted to these conditions  
  > "We propose a scenario for N1SE1 in which the pre-shock material went through a phase of hot-core chemistry" (Summary); "we would need to run shock chemical models that take into account higher initial densities and temperatures than typically assumed" (Summary)

- Why this data: ReMoCA supplies high angular resolution and sensitivity for molecular outflow maps and abundance analysis

- Why this method: LVINE accounts for velocity and linewidth gradients; paired transitions reduce line confusion; radiative-transfer modelling is preferred over population diagrams because of continuum and correction uncertainties  
  > "Subsequently, the peak velocities and linewidths were used to adjust the integration limits in each pixel of the LVINE maps."
