## Qualitative findings

- An inner Keplerian disk and outer free-falling envelope reproduce the large-scale molecular kinematics.  
  > "This suggests that the structure of the Sgr C disk can be described by the composite model: an inner Keplerian disk embedded in an outer, free-falling envelope." (Fitting the PPV cube)
- N-bearing molecules are concentrated in the compact component; O-bearing molecules occur in both components, with CH3OCHO dominant in the extended component.  
  > "nitrogen-bearing molecules (e.g., CH$_3$CN, $^{13}$CH$_3$CN) mainly contribute to Component 1." (NMF analysis of COMs); "In contrast, oxygen-bearing molecules (e.g., CH$_3$OH, CH$_3$OCHO) are found in both components, with $\mathrm{CH_3OCHO}$ being the primary contributor to Component 2." (NMF analysis of COMs)
- Component position-angle misalignment suggests a possible warp.  
  > "This position angle misalignment between the two components suggests the possible presence of a warped structure (cite: Kraus2020; Sai2020)." (NMF analysis of COMs)
- Spiral trajectories are consistent with rotating, gravitationally collapsing accretion streamers.  
  > "These streamline models reproduce the observations, supporting the interpretation as streamers undergoing gravitational collapse and rotation toward the central protostar." (Spirals in the Sgr C disk as infalling streamers)
- Estimated inward envelope and streamer forces exceed outward radiation pressure.  
  > "Therefore, the inward force of the infalling envelope or streamers is sufficient to overcome the outward radiation pressure from the protostar" (The evolution and fate of the high-mass protostar)
- The inferred infall rate is an upper limit to the final accretion rate onto the protostar.  
  > "Therefore, our derived infall rate serves as an upper limit for the final accretion rate onto the protostar." (Accretion rate)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution observations of an early O-type Keplerian disk candidate test accretion in the CMZ.  
  > "As a candidate for a Keplerian disk around an early O-type protostar, the Sgr C disk provides a chance to test models of mass accretion in extreme environments." (Introduction)
- Why this method: NMF supplies non-negative physical components; CH3OCHO traces compact and extended structures; stacking improves kinematic S/N.  
  > "This constraint is physically motivated by the additive nature of astronomical emission, preventing nonphysical negative fluxes and ensuring that each extracted component represents a distinct, localized physical environment rather than a mere statistical residual." (NMF analysis of COMs); "The analysis in [3.3.1](#subsec:NMF) revealed that the CH$_3$OCHO transitions trace both an inner, compact structure and a more extended, outer component." (Fitting the PPV cube); "In line-rich regions, stacking multiple transitions effectively enhances the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N), which facilitates a more robust extraction of kinematic information (cite: Ginsburg2018; Schw2019)." (Fitting the PPV cube)
- Alternative: Standalone pure Keplerian disk and standalone Ulrich infall models fit less adequately than the composite model.  
  > "We also performed fits using a standalone pure Keplerian disk or an Ulrich infall model, respectively. However, neither could adequately reproduce the observed kinematics." (Fitting the PPV cube)
- Alternative: PCA contrasted with NMF because PCA permits negative components.  
  > "Unlike Principal Component Analysis (PCA), which relies on orthogonal eigenvectors that can take negative values, NMF imposes a strict non-negativity constraint (H, W $\ge$ 0)." (NMF analysis of COMs)
- Alternative: Gravitational instability as a spiral origin is disfavored by earlier high Toomre stability estimates.  
  > "However, the Sgr C disk maintains a sufficiently high Toomre stability parameter (cite: LuXing2022), indicating that the substantial thermal and turbulent pressure in this warm environment could effectively suppress GI (cite: Toomre1964)." (Spirals in the Sgr C disk as infalling streamers)
- Alternative: Tidal interactions from an unseen binary companion remain possible; no companion is resolved.  
  > "The $m=2$ modes could also be driven by tidal interactions from an unseen binary companion (cite: Dong2015), yet no companion has been resolved at the current observational resolution." (Spirals in the Sgr C disk as infalling streamers)
- Alternative: Anisotropic tidal forces from the central object are another proposed streamer mechanism.  
  > "Furthermore, recent theory suggests that anisotropic tidal forces from the central object can drive the formation of spiral accretion streamers (cite: GuangXing2024)." (Spirals in the Sgr C disk as infalling streamers)
- Alternative: Dust opacity and radiative transfer can mimic intrinsic chemical depletion.  
  > "Breaking the degeneracy between intrinsic chemical depletion and dust opacity will require future high-resolution, multi-band observations" (Chemical dichotomy in protostellar disks)
- How they decided it was enough: Composite model reproduces large-scale velocities; pure models fail; PV masses agree with PPV modeling.  
  > "Despite the localized residuals in the right panels, the model reproduces the large-scale velocity field of CH$_3$OCHO." (Fitting the PPV cube); "We also performed fits using a standalone pure Keplerian disk or an Ulrich infall model, respectively. However, neither could adequately reproduce the observed kinematics." (Fitting the PPV cube); "the estimated central mass, $M_* \approx 40.7~M_{\odot}$, remains to be consistent with the result from the PPV dynamical modeling" (PV diagram across the Sgr C disk)
- Limitation (authors): Low-SNR temperature fits are unreliable at boundaries and in the southwest; some southwestern fits do not converge.  
  > "Spurious high-temperature pixels are observed at the disk boundaries; these likely arise from the low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in the outer regions, which compromises the reliability of the temperature fit." (Rotational temperature and disk mass); "We noted that in the southwestern (SW) region, the derived temperatures are also anomalously high, and in certain pixels, the fit fails to converge." (Rotational temperature and disk mass)
- Limitation (authors): No high-resolution radio continuum directly constrains protostellar free-free emission.  
  > "However, we do not have high resolution radio continuum data to constrain the free-free emission directly from the protostar." (Rotational temperature and disk mass)
- Limitation (authors): Optically thick inner dust emission makes the 5.5 M⊙ estimate a lower limit.  
  > "our 5.5 $\mbox{$M_\odot$}$ estimate represents a lower limit to the true disk mass." (Rotational temperature and disk mass)
- Limitation (authors): Axisymmetric PPV model excludes spirals and leaves localized residuals.  
  > "This is because our model does not include the spirals" (Fitting the PPV cube); "The negative residual is where the model is greater than the observed data, mainly distributed in the central part." (Fitting the PPV cube)
- Limitation (authors): Infall rate is an order-of-magnitude estimate affected by model symmetry, fitting errors, and mass errors.  
  > "Considering the uncertainties in the estimate, such as the use of an idealized symmetric model, the fitting errors of *emcee*, and the error in the disk mass, the result should be considered as an order-of-magnitude estimate." (Estimating the accretion rate)
- Limitation (authors): Envelope infall measures mass supply rather than instantaneous accretion onto the protostar.  
  > "While these techniques measure the mass supply rather than the instantaneous accretion onto the protostar itself, they provide crucial constraints on the mass flow in these obscured systems." (Estimating the accretion rate)
- Limitation (authors): Southeastern extension is not modeled because molecular line emission is absent.  
  > "We do not model this feature due to the lack of corresponding molecular line emission." (Spirals in the Sgr C disk as infalling streamers)
- Limitation (authors): West spiral mass cannot be estimated without temperature or continuum measurements.  
  > "The mass of Ws cannot be estimated, because it does not have available temperature or continuum emission measurement." (The evolution and fate of the high-mass protostar)
- Limitation (authors): Chemical segregation remains entangled with radiative transfer effects.  
  > "However, this chemical interpretation must be treated with caution, as it is fundamentally entangled with radiative transfer effects." (Chemical dichotomy in protostellar disks)
