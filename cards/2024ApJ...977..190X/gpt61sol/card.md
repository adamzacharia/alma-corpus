## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure new continuum emission**: Extract NOEMA line-free uv continuum, clean with HOGBOM and NATURAL weighting, and measure peak surface brightness.  
  > "We extracted the continuum from all channels free of spectral lines in the uv plane with UV$\_$AVERAGE." (Observations); "The uv table of the continuum and spectral lines underwent cleaning with the HOGBOM algorithm, while NATURAL weighting was applied to achieve the maximum signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)." (Observations); "The unresolved continuum emission was also detected at high S/N by taking the peak surface brightness value on the continuum map averaging over line-free channels" (Results)
   - software: Grenoble Image and Line Data Analysis System, CLIC, MAPPING, UV_AVERAGE
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum emission unresolved and detected at high S/N.  
  > "The unresolved continuum emission was also detected at high S/N" (Results)
2. **Measure new CO lines**: Subtract continuum in uv plane; combine CO(9-8) tunings; extract peak spectra and fit single Gaussian profiles.  
  > "The uv table for spectral lines was produced via UV$\_$SUBTRACT with the subtraction of the underlying continuum." (Observations); "The CO(9-8) line data from two tunings were combined to improve the S/N ratio." (Observations); "For the detections, we extracted the line spectra from the peak position and fitted a single Gaussian profile to the line spectra." (J0129-0035)
   - software: Grenoble Image and Line Data Analysis System, CLIC, MAPPING, UV_SUBTRACT
   - parameter: Bin spectra to 64–77 km/s.  
  > "The processed data were binned to spectral resolutions of 64 km/s to 77 km/s to optimize the sensitivity." (Observations)
   - parameter: Integrate intensity maps over 1.5 × FWHM_line.  
  > "created by integrating over a velocity range of $1.5 \times \rm FWHM_{\rm line}$ around the redshifted CO line" (J0129-0035)
   - parameter: J0129-0035 CO (10-9) 3σ upper limit: channel width 74 km s−1 and line range 275 km s−1.  
  > "$\Delta v_{\rm line}$ is adopted as the velocity range $275 \, \rm km\, s^{-1}$ from the ALMA CO (6-5) measurement" (J0129-0035); "$\Delta v_{\rm channel}$ is the binned channel width of $74 \, \rm km\, s^{-1}$" (J0129-0035)
   - assumption: Use ALMA CO (6-5) linewidth priors: 229 km s−1 for J2054-0005; 183 km/s for J0129-0035.  
  > "adopt the CO (6-5) line width of $229 \, \rm km \, s^{-1}$ as a prior to fit the line width." (J2054-0005); "using the ALMA CO (6-5) FWHM line width of 183 km/s as a prior constraint and employing the MCMC method in the fitting." (J0129-0035)
   - fitting: Least-square Gaussian fitting; Bayesian MCMC with CO (6-5) linewidth priors for weak lines; also MCMC-fit CO(9-8).  
  > "Therefore, for these two lines, we employ a Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method" (J2054-0005); "using the ALMA CO (6-5) FWHM line width of 183 km/s as a prior constraint and employing the MCMC method in the fitting." (J0129-0035); "To ensure consistency, we also applied MCMC to the CO(9-8) line and found that the result is consistent with the least-square fitting within the uncertainties." (J2054-0005)
   - validation: CO(9-8) MCMC and least-square fits agree within errors; emission peaks match optical positions.  
  > "For completeness, we also performed an MCMC fit to the CO(9-8) line and confirmed that the result agrees with the least-square fit, falling within the margin of error." (J0129-0035); "all of which coincide with the optical position (cite: Shao2019)." (J2054-0005); "all of which match the quasar optical position (cite: Shao2019)." (J0129-0035)
3. **Fit J2054-0005 dust and AGN emission**: Fit Gτ-MBB plus each CAT3D template; select smallest χ² template; refine dust and torus normalization parameters.  
  > "First, we separately fit the SED by combining the G$\tau$-MBB model with each torus template from the CAT3D model." (Continuum Emission); "We identified the optimal torus template by choosing the one with the smallest $\chi^2$ value." (Continuum Emission); "In the second step, we refined the SED fitting by incorporating the chosen torus template alongside the G$\tau$-MBB model to extract the best-fitting parameters." (Continuum Emission)
   - software: lmfit, CAT3D
   - parameter: Fixed dust continuum size 0.9 × 0.7 kpc².  
  > "using the dust continuum size ($0.9 \times 0.7 \, \rm kpc^2$) measured in (cite: Venemans2020) as a non-fitting parameter." (Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: L_TIR integrated over 8–1000 μm; L_FIR over 42.5–122.5 μm.  
  > "$L_{\rm TIR}$ refers to the luminosity integrated from 8 to 1000 $\rm \mu m$, while $L_{\rm FIR}$ is integrated over the range from 42.5 to 122.5 $\rm \mu m$ (cite: Helou1985)." (Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: κν = κ0 (ν/ν0)^β; κ0 = 0.77 cm² g−1 at 353 GHz.  
  > "$\kappa_{\nu} = \kappa_0 (\frac{\nu}{\nu_0})^{\beta}$ is the frequency-dependent dust opacity." (Continuum Emission); "we adopt $\kappa_0 = 0.77\, \rm cm^2 \, g^{-1}$ at $\nu_0 = 353\, \rm GHz$" (Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: Include CMB dust heating.  
  > "The dust heating from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) at the quasar redshift with a temperature of $T_{\rm CMB}(z)$ is also taken into account (cite: DaCunha2013)." (Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: ΛCDM: H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7.  
  > "Throughout this work, we adopt a $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $H_0 = 70 \rm \; km\, s^{-1}\, Mpc^{-1}$, $\Omega_{m}$=0.3, and $\Omega_{\Lambda}$=0.7." (Introduction)
   - fitting: lmfit; free torus scale k_t, dust temperature, dust mass and β; torus shape selected from templates.  
  > "using the Python lmfit package." (Continuum Emission); "our free parameters included the torus component scale factor, $k_{\rm t}$, the torus SED shape determined by the selected template, along with the dust temperature $T_{\rm dust}$, dust mass $M_{\rm dust}$, and emissivity index $\beta$." (Continuum Emission)
   - validation: Compare fitted dust temperature and β with Hashimoto2019.  
  > "While their $T_{\rm dust}$ is similar to ours, they found a lower $\beta$, which can be attributed to their assumption of thin optical depth and the limited number of data points used in their fit." (Continuum Emission)
4. **Fit J0129-0035 dust and derive SFRs**: Fit dust mass with fixed temperature, β and size; derive TIR luminosity and SFR; vary temperature and β to assess sensitivity.  
  > "Therefore, during our fitting procedure, the free parameter to fit is the dust mass $M_{\rm dust}$." (Continuum Emission); "we also show the SED models adopting different values of $\beta$ and $T_{\rm dust}$" (Continuum Emission); "The SFR estimated from $L_{\rm TIR}$ is $733 \pm 59\, \rm  M_{\odot}\, yr^{-1}$." (Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: β = 1.6; T_dust = 47 K; dust continuum size 1.2 × 1.0 kpc².  
  > "We adopted the graybody with $\beta = 1.6$ and $T_{\rm dust}= 47 \, \rm K$ that was often assumed in the previous optically thin model (cite: Beelen2006; Venemans2020)." (Continuum Emission); "We use the size of the dust continuum ($1.2\times 1.0 \, \rm kpc^2$) measured in (cite: Venemans2020) as the galaxy size." (Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: Kroupa IMF; SFR_IR/(M⊙ yr−1) = 3.88 × 10−44 L_TIR/(erg s−1).
