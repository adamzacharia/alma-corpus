## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure and assign laboratory rotational spectrum of methylamine and fit nuclear quadrupole hyperfine structure**: Record absorption spectra from 150 to 1520 GHz using the Lille fast-scan hybrid spectrometer, assign transitions using Loomis-Wood diagrams, and fit hyperfine components to derive hyperfine-free frequencies and quadrupole coupling constants  
  > "All the spectra were recorded in absorption mode using the Lille fast-scan hybrid spectrometer (Zakharenko et al. 2015; Motiyenko et al. 2019). The measurements cover the frequency range 150 – 1520 GHz with a few small gaps." (2. Experimental details); "The assignment of the rotational spectrum of methylamine in excited vibrational states was aided by Loomis-Wood-type diagrams (Loomis & Wood 1928)." (3.1. Microwave, millimeter, and submillimeter data); "rotational transition frequencies, each hyperfine component was fitted to Eq. (1). The linear least-squares Gauss-Newton algorithm was applied using a dedicated Python code." (3.3. Hyperfine structure analysis)
   - software: BELGI-hybrid, Python, home-made software for Loomis-Wood plots
   - parameter: 1456 hyperfine transitions (including 324 v_t = 1-1 components) fitted to yield 653 hyperfine-free frequencies  
  > "to yield 653 hyperfine-free rotational transition frequencies and two hyperfine constants" (3.3. Hyperfine structure analysis)
   - assumption: Perturbation approach for nuclear quadrupole coupling using expectation values from the two-step diagonalization of H_WTR  
  > "We used a perturbation approach similar to that used for acetamide, a _C_ _s_ molecule with one methyl internal rotor large-amplitude motion, and one nitrogen quadrupole nucleus (see Eq. (1) of Suenram et al. 2001)." (3.3. Hyperfine structure analysis)
   - fitting: Linear least-squares Gauss-Newton algorithm  
  > "The linear least-squares Gauss-Newton algorithm was applied using a dedicated Python code." (3.3. Hyperfine structure analysis)
   - validation: Root-mean-square deviation of the hyperfine fit (0.0129 MHz) and comparison of chi_+ and chi_- with previous studies  
  > "rms [(] _[b]_ [)] (MHz) 0.0129 0.0082" (Table 1); "As can be seen from Table 1, the χ + values match well, whereas the χ − values are outside the confidence intervals of both studies." (3.3. Hyperfine structure analysis)
2. **Globally fit the torsion-wagging-rotation Hamiltonian and generate spectral line predictions**: Two-step diagonalization of the hybrid Hamiltonian matrix using BELGI-hybrid to globally fit microwave, mm/submm, and far-infrared transitions for v_t = 0, 1, 2 up to J = 40, and compute line strengths and hyperfine intensities  
  > "Using the BELGI-hybrid code, we obtained the rotation-torsion eigenvalues and eigenvectors from the usual two-step diagonalization of the Hamiltonian" (3.3. Hyperfine structure analysis); "The BELGI-hybrid code was modified to provide the line strength calculations for methylamine transitions." (4.2. Intensity calculation)
   - software: BELGI-hybrid
   - parameter: 28802 transitions with J <= 40, K <= 17; 79 floated parameters and 8 fixed parameters  
  > "Overview of a fit of 28 802 transitions of methylamine with _J_ ≤ 40, _K_ ≤ 17, using 79 parameters floated to give a weighted standard deviation of 1.53." (Table 2); "The 79 floated molecular parameters (plus eight additional parameters, which were fixed at values from previous fits" (4.1. Fit with the hybrid formalism)
   - assumption: RAM dipole moment components mu_a = -0.226 D and mu_c = 1.275 D; neglect torsional (alpha) and tunneling (gamma) dependence of dipole moments; nuclear spin statistical weights W_st = 1 for A1, A2, E2 and 3 for B1, B2, E1  
  > "the values we used for the dipole moments in this system, namely µ _a_ = −0.226 D and µ _c_ = 1.275 D, are very close to the PAM ones." (4.2. Intensity calculation); "For the intensity calculation we also had to take into account the nuclear spin statistical weight ( _W_ st ), which is equal to 1 for _A_ 1, _A_ 2, and _E_ 2 species and to 3 for _B_ 1, _B_ 2, and _E_ 1 ." (4.2. Intensity calculation)
   - fitting: Global least-squares fit in BELGI-hybrid  
  > "The column contains the operator used in the hybrid Hamiltonian multiplied by the parameter adjusted in the least-square fit." (Table A.1)
   - validation: Weighted standard deviation of 1.53 and comparison of predicted spectrum with experimental spectrum around 1.38 THz  
  > "As can be seen from Table 2, we achieve a global unitless standard deviation of 1.53 for 28802 transitions." (4.1.   > "We compared the observed spectrum to synthetic spectra computed under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with the astronomical software Weeds (Maret et al. 2011)." (5.1. ## Qualitative findings

- First interstellar detection of methylamine (CH3NH2) in its first torsionally excited state (v_t = 1) toward Sgr B2(N1S).  
  > "We report the first interstellar detection of methylamine in its first torsionally excited state. The detection was obtained with ALMA toward the offset position Sgr B2(N1S)" (7. Conclusions)
- Accounting for nuclear quadrupole hyperfine structure broadens several methylamine lines and reduces their peak brightness temperatures compared to models without hyperfine structure, reducing the number of firmly detected v = 0 lines from 15 to 10.  
  > "A detailed comparison of Fig. A.1 to Fig. A.5 of Kisiel et al. (2022) shows that the hyperfine structure does indeed broaden several lines of methylamine and in turn reduces their peak temperature compared to the previous study, where the hyperfine structure was not accounted for" (5.2. Detection of CH3NH2 in its first torsionally excited state); "With these criteria, we count 10 lines of methylamine in its torsional ground state as detected in Fig. C.1 while 15 were counted in the previous study that" (5.2. Detection of CH3NH2 in its first torsionally excited state)
- The emission of methylamine in both v = 0 and v_t = 1 states is well described by a single LTE excitation temperature and column density.  
  > "the observed data points are roughly distributed along a straight line, which indicates that the emission of methylamine in both torsional states can be described by a single temperature component." (5.2. Detection of CH3NH2 in its first torsionally excited state)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Given the strong v_t = 0 intensities and ~230 K rotational temperature toward Sgr B2(N1S) in the ReMoCA survey, the strongest v_t = 1 transitions were expected to be detectable above 3 sigma; the offset position Sgr B2(N1S) was chosen for its lower continuum opacity compared to the main hot core peak.  
  > "This position was chosen for its lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core." (5.1. Observations); "This position was chosen for its lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core." (5.1. Observations)
- Why this method: The hybrid Hamiltonian approach was used because the high-barrier tunneling approach showed limitations for the v_t = 1 state; LTE modeling with Weeds is justified by the high gas densities (> 1e7 cm^-3) in Sgr B2(N) hot cores, and LSD hyperfine predictions were used because hyperfine splitting broadens lines at the 5 km s^-1 linewidth of Sgr B2(N1S).  
  > "To overcome these limitations, we use a new hybrid model and the BELGI-hybrid code described in Kleiner & Hougen (2015)" (1. Introduction); "This assumption is justified by the high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (> 1 × 10 [7] cm [−][3], see Bonfand et al. 2019)." (5.1. Observations); "However, we found that the spectral broadening due to the hyperfine structure is not negligible for some methylamine lines given the typical line width of 5 km s [−][1] (about 1.7 MHz) toward Sgr B2(N1S)." (5.2. Detection of CH3NH2 in its first torsionally excited state)
- Alternative: Including previously published ground-state v_t = 0 combination differences (GSCDs) and far-infrared pure rotational transitions in the global fit was rejected because GSCDs caused parameters to drift and FIR pure rotational lines had frequency discrepancies up to 30 MHz.  
  > "as their large number tends to decrease the impact of the more accurate microwave transitions, thus causing the parameters to drift" (3.2. Far-infrared data)
- Alternative: Including v_t = 1-1 transitions with K = 0 and 1 in the A and B nondegenerate species in the global fit was rejected because avoided/unavoided crossings caused large residuals up to 1-2 MHz that could not be reduced with ∆K = +/-1 perturbation terms.  
  > "We used several Hamiltonian terms with matrix elements connecting energy levels differing by ∆ _K_ = ±1 to take into account this perturbation, such as DAB, AXG, DAC (see Table A.1) and so on, but we did not succeed in decreasing the observed-calculated values for those lines with _K_ = 0 and 1 and we finally omitted them in the fit." (4.1. Fit with the hybrid formalism)
- Alternative: Replacing the Motiyenko et al. (2014) tunneling model predictions for v_t = 0-0 in the Lille Spectroscopic Database with the new hybrid model predictions was rejected because the tunneling model covers up to J <= 50 (versus J <= 40) and achieves a slightly better rms for v_t = 0-0.  
  > "First, the effective high-barrier tunneling Hamiltonian model from Motiyenko et al. (2014) covers the wider range of rotational quantum numbers up to" (4.2. Intensity calculation)
- Alternative: Using JPL entry 31008 (without hyperfine structure) for astronomical LTE modeling of v = 0 methylamine was replaced with LSD entry 31802 (with hyperfine structure) because hyperfine broadening is non-negligible.  
  > "This entry does not account for the hyperfine structure of methylamine. However, we found that the spectral broadening due to the hyperfine structure is not negligible for some methylamine lines given the typical line width of 5 km s [−][1] (about 1.7 MHz) toward Sgr B2(N1S). Therefore, here we used the spectroscopic entry 31802 (version 2021.08) of the LSD" (5.2. Detection of CH3NH2 in its first torsionally excited state)
- How they decided it was enough: In the spectroscopic fit, J was limited to <= 40 to keep matrix sizes and computation time reasonable; in the astronomical analysis, 5 uncontaminated v_t = 1 lines matching the pre-existing v = 0 LTE parameters were judged sufficient for a robust detection of an excited state of an already securely detected molecule.  
  > "In the present work, because of the size of the matrix that we need to take into consideration in our global approach and the computer time required subsequently, we decided to fit only up to _J_ = 40." (4.1. Fit with the hybrid formalism); "Five transitions are even sufficiently free of contamination, which means that this match is unlikely due to chance." (6. Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): The global hybrid Hamiltonian fit does not achieve residuals completely within experimental uncertainty for the microwave transitions (global weighted rms of 1.53; microwave WRMS of 2.38 for v_t = 0 and 3.35 for v_t = 1), possibly due to convergence to a local minimum, unmodeled high-order terms for high J/K, or unmodeled intervibrational resonances from low-lying nontorsion modes.  
  > "The quality of the fit is satisfactory, although not completely within experimental uncertainty for the microwave transitions." (4.1. - Limitation (authors): The statistical uncertainty on the rotational temperature derived from the population diagram (222 +/- 8 K) may be underestimated.  
  > "As explained in Sect. 3 of Belloche et al. (2016) and in Sect. 4.4 of Belloche et al. (2019), this uncertainty is purely statistical and should be viewed with caution. It may be underestimated." (Table 4)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 5 lines of v_t = 1 CH3NH2 are classified as uncontaminated detections in a highly line-confused Band 3 spectrum, and one of them (86657 MHz) lies directly between two strong ethylene glycol transitions whose wings are slightly overestimated by the full model.
