## Qualitative findings

- No broad mm/submm molecular emission lines from nova outflows were detected in any of the 94 classical novae and several dwarf novae surveyed with APEX, IRAM 30 m, SMA, or ALMA.  
  > "Except for CK Vul, known to have broad molecular emission from earlier studies, none of our survey targets shows broad features that can be associated with a nova system." (Single-dish survey results: no detection of nova lines); "Our single-dish survey of the 94 old classical novae and several dwarf novae demonstrates that circumstellar envelopes of post-novae systems are poor in molecular gas." (Summary and conclusions)
- The CO emission around GK Per is narrow and forms an extended ridge of a quiescent translucent interstellar cloud rather than a circumstellar outflow or planetary nebula.  
  > "Our maps reveal that the emission they found is only a fragment of a longer ridge that most likely is much larger than the field covered by our map and is part of the large-scale complex mapped by Hessman." (Results of CO mapping); "With new maps of CO emission near GK Per, we debunked the long-standing conviction that the remnant of Nova 1901 is associated with a circumstellar molecular cloud. We find only interstellar emission in the direction of the nova." (Summary and conclusions)
- V1148 Sgr lacks circumstellar CO emission, arguing against its proposed classification as a red nova.  
  > "Although all the disputed positions of the remnant were well within the APEX beam, we do not detect any strong circumstellar emission in the CO transitions. This strongly advocates against a red nova classification of V1148 Sgr." (Red-nova connection)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Recent millimeter detections of broad molecular lines in CK Vul, eta Car, and SN 1987A using modern wide-bandwidth receivers motivated a systematic search in classical novae, including archival ALMA and new SMA interferometric observations that provide flatter baselines and higher sensitivity than single-dish telescopes.  
  > "Encouraged by the discoveries in CK Vul, \eta Car, and SNR 1987A, we decided to revise the view on the presence of molecular emission in classical novae by undertaking an observation campaign aimed to detect molecules at mm wavelengths using modern instruments, including single-dish telescopes and interferometers." (Introduction); "Millimeter-wave interferometric arrays are usually more sensitive and offer flat spectroscopic baselines, and thus provide the most optimal way to test classical novae for the presence of molecules (Sect. 1)." (Interferometric search of molecules in novae)
- Why this method: Lowest rotational transitions of CO and HCN were targeted because they are typically the strongest lines in cool circumstellar envelopes, and HCN should be enhanced in CNO-processed nova ejecta; spectra were smoothed to ~30 km s^-1 to optimize sensitivity to broad nova outflow lines.  
  > "We searched for lowest rotational transitions of CO and HCN, because these are usually the strongest lines in cool circumstellar envelopes. Particularly, emission of HCN might be expected to be strong if the gas is enhanced in CNO-processed material, as expected for classical novae." (Single-dish observations); "To decrease the noise to levels appropriate for the expected broad lines, we rebinned the spectra to a much lower resolution, typically near 30 km s^{-1}." (APEX observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Optical and near-infrared molecular bands (such as C2, CN, CO) were rejected for measuring isotopic ratios because high intrinsic line widths and modest spectral resolutions prevent resolving isotopic shifts.
