## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the continuum and search for emission**: Image the continuum with tclean using natural weights, correct for the primary beam, and look for emission; search for edge-on disc emission with a rectangular aperture of variable length and position angle  
  > "We image the continuum with the task tclean in CASA, using natural weights to produce a reconstructed image with the lowest possible noise." (2 ALMA observations); "We also search for extended emission from an edge-on disk by computing the flux in a rectangular aperture centred on the star" (2 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting  
  > "using natural weights to produce a reconstructed image with the lowest possible noise" (2 ALMA observations)
   - assumption: edge-on disc co-planar with TRAPPIST-1 b-h for the aperture search  
  > "since we assume an edge-on disc co-planar with TRAPPIST-1 b–h" (2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Only detected source identified as likely sub-mm galaxy from ALMA number counts  
  > "that is likely sub-mm galaxy given ALMA number counts" (2 ALMA observations)
2. **Derive 0.88 mm flux upper limits versus disc size**: 3 sigma limit for an unresolved disc; for resolved discs, integrated flux uncertainty over a rectangular aperture accounting for number of beams and noise growth away from the phase centre  
  > "also taking into account the number of beams in this area and how the noise level increases away from the phase center." (2 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: unresolved disc radius < 4 au  
  > "For an unresolved disc with a radius smaller than 4 au" (2 ALMA observations)
   - assumption: disc centred on the star in a rectangular aperture  
  > "estimating the integrated flux uncertainty over a rectangular aperture centred on the star as described above" (2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Convert flux limits to fractional luminosity limits**: Assume blackbody dust temperature and a 1/lambda opacity beyond 200 micron; add a 3.6 K interstellar radiation field floor to the dust temperature and recompute the limit  
  > "In order to take this into account, we calculate the dust temperature as" (2 ALMA observations); "significantly higher than the blackbody equilibrium temperature of 3.6 K" (2 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: T_ISRF fixed at 3.6 K, applied to all calculations in the Discussion  
  > "significantly higher than the blackbody equilibrium temperature of 3.6 K" (2 ALMA observations)
   - assumption: blackbody grains with opacity declining as 1/lambda beyond 200 micron  
  > "and an opacity that declines with wavelength as" (2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: ALMA limit compared with WISE 22 micron and MIPS 24 micron limits  
  > "We find that the ALMA limit is significantly more constraining than the WISE and MIPS limit beyond 0.4 au." (2 ALMA observations)
4. **Set CO 3-2 upper limit and CO gas mass**: Measure rms per channel, set a 3 sigma limit for unresolved emission within 4 au, convert to CO mass with a non-LTE tool  
  > "The achieved rms per 0.42 km s$^{-1}$ channel is 0.5 mJy beam$^{-1}$." (2 ALMA observations); "Using the non-LTE tool by (cite: Matra2018) we translate this upper limit to a CO gas mass" (2 ALMA observations)
   - software: non-LTE tool of Matra et al. 2018
   - parameter: maximum Doppler shift of 10 km/s (beyond 1 au if Keplerian)  
  > "with a maximum Doppler shift of 10 km s$^{-1}$ (i.e. beyond 1 au if in Keplerian rotation)" (2 ALMA observations)
   - assumption: unresolved emission interior to 4 au  
  > "for any unresolved CO 3-2 emission interior to 4 au" (2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Search for flaring (variable) emission**: Image the data in 12 s time intervals and measure flux at the stellar position  
  > "we also search for unresolved variable emission at the stellar position by imaging the data in time intervals of 12s." (2 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: 12 s time bins; rms 0.23 mJy per window  
  > "(rms of 0.23 mJy over 12s window)" (2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Number of 3 sigma peaks compared with expected false positives for a normal distribution  
  > "which is roughly consistent with the expected number of false positives for a normal distribution" (2 ALMA observations)
6. **Compare dust limits with collisional evolution of a planetesimal belt around TRAPPIST-1**: Run collisional cascade model, convert predicted size distributions to surface density/mass in mm-sized grains (<1 cm), compare with the 3 sigma upper limit assuming a belt of fractional width 0.5  
  > "The output from these simulations are size distributions at different radii that we translate to surface densities or disc masses in mm-sized grains, here defined as all grains smaller than 1 cm." (3 Discussion)
   - software: collisional evolution model (Wyatt et al. 2011; Marino et al. 2017)
   - parameter: maximum planetesimal size 100 km  
  > "a maximum planetesimal size of 100 km," (3 Discussion)
   - parameter: internal density 2.7 g/cm3  
  > "internal densities of 2.7 g cm$^{-3}$," (3 Discussion)
   - parameter: mean eccentricity 0.05  
  > "mean orbital eccentricities of 0.05 and inclinations of" (3 Discussion)
   - parameter: dust opacity 3 cm2/g at 0.88 mm  
  > "a dust opacity of 3 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 0.88 mm," (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
   - assumption: initial solids surface density equal to a MMSN extrapolated to 100 au  
  > "an initial surface density of solids equivalent to a Minimum Mass Solar Nebula (MMSN) extrapolated out to 100 au" (3 Discussion)
   - assumption: belt edge-on and co-planar with the planets  
  > "the belt is edge-on, i.e. co-planar to TRAPPIST-1 b–h," (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
   - assumption: age of about 8 Gyr  
  > "after 8 Gyr of evolution it could have lost most of its mass through collisions" (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Conclusions checked against varied maximum planetesimal size and stirring level  
  > "These conclusions hold when varying model parameters such as the maximum planetesimal size and level of stirring" (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
7. **Re-image archival Proxima Cen data and compare with the same model**: Re-image the ALMA data after applying the ALMA calibration script, derive ACA and 12m+ACA azimuthally averaged limits for a 45 deg inclined disc, compare with the collisional model  
  > "To derive upper limits we re-imaged the data after applying the calibration script provided by ALMA." (3.2 Proxima Cen collisional evolution)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: inclination 45 deg, azimuthal averaging  
  > "assuming a disc inclined by $45\deg$ and azimuthally averaging" (3.2 Proxima Cen collisional evolution)
   - assumption: same assumptions as for TRAPPIST-1  
  > "We applied the same model to Proxima Cen (cite: Bazot2016)" (3.2 Proxima Cen collisional evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Varied the initial disc mass to find the lowest mass detectable  
  > "By varying the initial disc mass we find that discs with a mass lower than a tenth of a MMSN" (3.2 Proxima Cen collisional evolution)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: TRAPPIST-1's efficient planet formation, potentially young age and proximity made it an ideal target for a planetesimal belt search  
  > "the efficient planet formation in this system, its potentially young age (highly unconstrained until recently) and its proximity made it an ideal target to look for a planetesimal belt." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Natural weights chosen for the lowest possible noise in the reconstructed image  
  > "using natural weights to produce a reconstructed image with the lowest possible noise" (2 ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Dust temperature from stellar blackbody alone was re-evaluated by adding an interstellar radiation field contribution  
  > "Note that assuming blackbody equilibrium temperatures only based on the stellar radiation might not be a good assumption" (2 ALMA observations)
- Alternative: A dynamical instability that scattered and depleted a massive belt is an alternative explanation of the non-detection  
  > "The non-detection of a disc could also be explained by an instability in the system that scattered and depleted a massive planetesimal belt." (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Collisional model assumes the system has been stable for 8 Gyr  
  > "A caveat in the use of our collisional evolution model is that it assumes the system has been stable for 8 Gyr." (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
- Limitation (authors): Minimum mass nebula from planets assumes planet orbits did not evolve significantly  
  > "Note that this approach to derive an initial mass in solids assumes planets’ orbits do not evolve significantly" (3.1 TRAPPIST-1 collisional evolution)
- Limitation (authors): Proxima Cen outer belt signal is only marginal and flaring may bias earlier analysis; a visibility-space analysis is needed  
  > "A future detailed analysis of more sensitive observations in the visibility space, taking into account the time variable emission of Proxima Cen" (3.2 Proxima Cen collisional evolution)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits depend on adopted dust opacity (3 cm2/g), temperature law and belt width fraction (0.5), whose uncertainty is not propagated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Collisional model is not released or fully specified, so conclusions about belts beyond 40 au cannot be independently reproduced
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Age of 7.6+-2.2 Gyr is drawn from the literature and dominates the collisional depletion conclusion
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Proxima Cen limits rely on an azimuthally averaged, assumed 45 deg inclination disc with few imaging details given
