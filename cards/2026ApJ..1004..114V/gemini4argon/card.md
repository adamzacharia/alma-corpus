## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure ring radius and ALMA dust continuum flux and mass**: Fit an ellipse around the ring in CARTA to estimate the radius; measure the 261.3 GHz continuum flux density from the ALCS map, scale it to 870 um, and convert to dust mass using a scaling relation.  
  > "After correcting for the beam factor, we obtain a flux density of 240 _±_ 80 _µ_ Jy for the dust continuum." (2. OBSERVATIONS); "We estimate that the ring has a radius of _≈_ 0 _._ 25 _[′′]_ by roughly fitting an ellipse around the ring using the `CARTA` software (Comrie et al. 2021)." (3. RESULTS); "We scale the 1.15mm flux density to an 870 _µ_ m flux density assuming _β_ = 1 _._ 8, yielding _S_ 870 _µ_ m = 0 _._ 7 _±_ 0 _._ 2 mJy, which we convert into a dust mass" (3. RESULTS)
   - software: CARTA
   - assumption: Dust emissivity spectral index beta = 1.8  
  > "We scale the 1.15mm flux density to an 870 _µ_ m flux density assuming _β_ = 1 _._ 8, yielding _S_ 870 _µ_ m = 0 _._ 7 _±_ 0 _._ 2 mJy" (3. RESULTS)
   - assumption: Redshift-independent dust mass scaling relation from Dudzeviciute et al. (2020)  
  > "which we convert into a dust mass using the redshift-independent scaling relation in Dudzeviˇci¯ut˙e et al. (2020) to obtain _M_ dust = (9 _±_ 4) _×_ 10 [7] _M_ _⊙_ ." (3. RESULTS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **PSF matching and multi-band aperture photometry**: Generate and resample HST ACS and JWST NIRCam PSFs to 0.02'', create matching kernels to convolve all bands to the F444W resolution (FWHM ~ 0.16''), and extract photometry in apertures for the center, three clumps, and total system.  
  > "For the JWST NIRCam PSFs, we use the `stpsf` (formerly `WebbPSF;` Perrin et al. 2014) Python software package to generate simulated point-spread functions (PSFs) for each JWST band, oversampling the PSF to match a target pixel size of 0.02 _[′′]_ ." (4. ANALYSIS); "Because the F444W band has the lowest spatial resolution, with a FWHM _∼_ 0 _._ 16 _[′′]_, we create a shared kernel between each respective band’s PSF via the `psf.matching` sub-module in `photutils` (Bradley et al. 2020)" (4. ANALYSIS); "We construct four separate apertures for the photometric analysis. One aperture surrounds the red center of the galaxy, and three of them (A, B, C) are centered on bright clumps along the ring." (4. ANALYSIS)
   - software: stpsf, photutils
   - parameter: Aperture radius = 0.08'' for clumps A, B, C and center; 0.48'' for total system  
  > "These apertures all have a radius of 0.08 _[′′]_, with the aperture size roughly corresponding to the lowest resolution element. We additionally apply a wide aperture that covers the entire size of the system, covering a radius of 0.48 _[′′]_ ." (4. ANALYSIS)
   - assumption: 5% systematic error added in quadrature to photometric measurements  
  > "We additionally apply a systematic error of 5% in quadrature to the photometric measurements before performing SED modeling." (4. ANALYSIS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **SED modeling of the CRG, clumps, and nearby companions**: Fit multi-band HST and JWST photometry using bagpipes with a delayed star-formation history model (with both free redshift and fixed z = 4.0148); subtract foreground galaxy light in F277W using GALFIT to place apertures and fit SEDs of five faint companions.  
  > "To determine whether the CRG is a ring galaxy or a case of gravitational lensing, we employ the `bagpipes` software (Carnall et al. 2018) to model the SED of the total galaxy and individual clumps along the ring" (4.    - parameter: Six delayed SFH parameters: log10(M_*/M_sun), SFR, A_V, system age, metallicity (Z/Z_sun), ionization parameter (U)  
  > "we employ a delayed star-formation history model in `bagpipes`, consisting of six parameters:" (4. ANALYSIS)
   - parameter: GALFIT single Sersic profile: R_e ~ 0.21'', n ~ 1.2, b/a ~ 0.7  
  > "using the `GALFIT` software (Peng et al. 2002) to fit a single S`ersic profile with _R_ _e_ _∼_ 0 _._ 21 _[′′]_, _n_ _∼_ 1 _._ 2, and _b/a_ _∼_ 0 _._ 7 after masking the faint companions." (4. ANALYSIS)

  > "with the HST mosaics produced using the ‘mosaicdrizzle’ pipeline (developed and presented by Koekemoer et al. 2011)" (2. OBSERVATIONS)

  > "The JWST mosaics used the JWST pipeline [3] version 1.13.4 (Bushouse et al. 2024)," (2. OBSERVATIONS)
- **CARTA** 2.0.0: Fitting an ellipse around the ring to estimate its radius  
  > "We estimate that the ring has a radius of _≈_ 0 _._ 25 _[′′]_ by roughly fitting an ellipse around the ring using the `CARTA` software (Comrie et al. 2021)." (3. RESULTS)
- **bagpipes** : SED modeling of multi-band photometry for the total galaxy, ring clumps, central region, and nearby companions  
  > "To determine whether the CRG is a ring galaxy or a case of gravitational lensing, we employ the `bagpipes` software (Carnall et al. 2018) to model the SED of the total galaxy and individual clumps along the ring" (4. ANALYSIS)
- **stpsf** : Generating simulated PSFs for each JWST NIRCam band  
  > "For the JWST NIRCam PSFs, we use the `stpsf` (formerly `WebbPSF;` Perrin et al. 2014) Python software package to generate simulated point-spread functions (PSFs) for each JWST band" (4. ANALYSIS)
- **photutils** 1.0.0: Creating PSF matching kernels via the psf.matching sub-module  
  > "we create a shared kernel between each respective band’s PSF via the `psf.matching` sub-module in `photutils` (Bradley et al. 2020) before convolving the F444W-matched kernel with each band of observations." (4. ANALYSIS)
- **nautilus** : Nested sampling for photometric SED fitting in bagpipes  
  > "The photometric fitting is performed using nested sampling, specifically using `nautilus` (Lange 2023), as implemented in `bagpipes` ." (4. ANALYSIS)
- **GALFIT** : Fitting and subtracting a 2D Sersic profile of the main galaxy in F277W to isolate faint companions  
  > "To figure out where to place apertures, we first remove the foreground light in F277W, using the `GALFIT` software (Peng et al. 2002) to fit a single S`ersic profile" (4. ANALYSIS)
- **MyFilter** 1.0.0: Data analysis / filter transmission curve visualization

## Qualitative findings

- The candidate ring galaxy at z_spec = 4.0148 consists of a red central component surrounded by a clumpy blue ring with three main clumps, a tidal tail, and two faint companions at consistent photometric redshifts.  
  > "The galaxy is characterized by a blue ring surrounding a red center; the ring is comprised of three main clumps and has a radius of 0 _._ 25 _[′′]_ ." (6. CONCLUSION); "Of the five companions visible in Figure 4, we find that two have photometric redshifts in agreement with the spectroscopic redshift of the system; both of these lie along an apparent tidal arm" (4. ANALYSIS)
- The system is more likely a collisional ring galaxy at z = 4.0148 than a galaxy-galaxy gravitational lens, though lensing cannot be completely ruled out without high-resolution kinematic observations.  
  > "Although we argue strongly in favor of the collisional ring explanation, we cannot entirely rule out a galaxy-galaxy strong lensing explanation for the system’s morphology" (abstract); "to confirm the nature of this source, we require kinematic information via high spectral resolution observations." (abstract)
- High-redshift ring galaxies represent a significant contaminant in current and future strong gravitational lens surveys.  
  > "From all of the above, we infer that galaxies dominated by a ring morphology remain a significant contaminant in searches for gravitational lenses, which poses challenges to current and future optical and near-infrared surveys." (5.2. Implications of ring galaxies for future gravitational lens searches)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-band HST ACS and JWST NIRCam imaging plus CANUCS NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy and archival ALCS ALMA continuum data were combined to resolve the morphology, measure clump colors/SEDs, and confirm the spectroscopic redshift of a candidate lens/ring galaxy.  
  > "Using multi-band HST ACS and JWST NIRCam imaging, along with NIRSpec PRISM observations, we argue in this work that it is most likely a collisional ring galaxy." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: PSF-matched multi-band SED fitting with bagpipes was used to test whether the clumps and central core share a common redshift, while a simple thin-lens Einstein mass calculation was chosen over full lens modeling because a lens modeling failure would not conclusively rule out lensing.  
  > "To determine whether the CRG is a ring galaxy or a case of gravitational lensing, we employ the `bagpipes` software (Carnall et al. 2018) to model the SED of the total galaxy and individual clumps along the ring" (4. ANALYSIS); "To further assess the lensing hypothesis, we do not attempt to perform detailed gravitational lens modeling, primarily because a lens modeling failure would not necessarily mean that the system is not a galaxy-galaxy lens." (4.1. Is this system a gravitational lens?)
- Alternative: Third scenario where the foreground lens is at z = 4.0148 and the blue ring is an even higher-redshift background source (rejected as highly implausible due to extreme rarity of z > 2 lenses and because emission lines peak at the center of the slit).  
  > "A third scenario, which we find highly implausible, is that the foreground galaxy is at _z_ = 4 _._ 0148, and that the blue emission is coming from a background galaxy." (3. RESULTS)
- Alternative: Detailed gravitational lens modeling (not performed because a modeling failure would not conclusively disprove the galaxy-galaxy lens hypothesis).  
  > "To further assess the lensing hypothesis, we do not attempt to perform detailed gravitational lens modeling, primarily because a lens modeling failure would not necessarily mean that the system is not a galaxy-galaxy lens." (4.1. Is this system a gravitational lens?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low spectral resolution of the NIRSpec PRISM data prevents accurate line flux constraints, deblending of H-alpha and [N II], or kinematic confirmation of the ring vs. lens nature.  
  > "to confirm the nature of this source, we require kinematic information via high spectral resolution observations." (abstract); "Due to the low spectral resolution of the NIRSpec data, it is challenging to accurately constrain line fluxes, or to deblend some line emission, e.g., the H _α_ flux with the neighboring [N II ] lines." (3. RESULTS)
- Limitation (authors): Low spatial resolution and low (~3 sigma) significance of the archival ALMA continuum detection prevent determining whether the dust emission originates from the red central core or the blue ring.  
  > "The faint ALMA detection of dust continuum, along with its relatively low spatial resolution, makes it difficult to determine whether the bulk of emission arises from the red component or blue ring." (3. RESULTS)
- Limitation (authors): Simple delayed SFH photometric model in bagpipes cannot strongly constrain stellar ages or metallicities.  
  > "We emphasize that for this exercise, we have employed a rather simple photometric model, and as such we cannot place strong constraints on certain parameters (e.g., age, metallicity)." (4. ANALYSIS)
- Limitation (authors): Targeting [C II] 158 um with ALMA to kinematically confirm the source is not feasible because at z = 4.0148 it falls at 379 GHz, in the atmospheric gap between ALMA Bands 7 and 8.  
  > "however, it would emit at an observed frequency of 379 GHz, which is outside the frequency coverage of the Band 7 (275 _−_ 373 GHz) and Band 8 (385 _−_ 500 GHz) receivers on ALMA." (6. CONCLUSION)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The target lies on the periphery of the strong lensing cluster MACS0416, yet the paper does not state the cluster magnification factor mu at the source position or correct the derived stellar mass, SFR, ring radius, or dust mass for cluster magnification.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 0.08'' radius apertures used for the center and clumps A, B, and C have a diameter of 0.16'', which equals the FWHM of the F444W PSF, meaning adjacent components and central/ring emission may suffer from cross-contamination and aperture losses.
