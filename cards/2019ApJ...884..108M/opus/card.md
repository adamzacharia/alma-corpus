## Analysis recipe

1. **measure continuum flux and geometry**: uvmodelfit single-component fit to visibilities with CO channels flagged and weights recomputed with statwt; elliptical Gaussian for 49 Cet, point source for HD 32297  
  > "we applied the `uvmodelfit` task in CASA that allows to fit a single component source model to the *uv* data. Channels with CO emission were flagged and the data weights were recomputed using the `statwt` task." (Continuum Emission); "Therefore in this case we applied a point source model in the visibility domain fitting" (Continuum Emission)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: uvmodelfit; uncertainty = quadratic sum of measurement error and 10% calibration error  
  > "the quoted uncertainty was computed as the quadratic sum of the measurement error and the typical ALMA calibration error (10% of the signal)" (Continuum Emission)
   - validation: Compared with geometry of Hughes et al. 2017, extrapolated (sub)mm photometry (4.7 mJy) and earlier MAMBO/SMA/ALMA fluxes  
  > "Our measurement is consistent with this prediction." (Continuum Emission); "This result matches well the flux derived from our low resolution observation" (Continuum Emission)
2. **derive dust masses**: Optically thin isothermal dust mass formula at 1.33 mm  
  > "Assuming isothermal, optically thin dust emission and using our obtained millimeter flux densities, we estimated the dust mass of the disks" (Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: kappa = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "we adopted a value of 2.3 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ for the dust opacity" (Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: T_dust = 56 K (49 Cet, blackbody at 100 au) and 47 K (HD 32297)  
  > "we took the equilibrium temperature of blackbody grains located at a radial distance of 100 au, and obtained a $T_{\rm dust}$ of 56 K" (Continuum Emission); "derived a characteristic temperature of 47 K for the emitting large grains in the outer belt" (Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **extract CO line spectra and integrated fluxes**: Beam-shaped elliptical apertures of growing size; sum consecutive channels with peak SNR>3; minimum aperture enclosing all emission; C18O over same aperture and velocity range  
  > "We applied different-sized elliptical apertures with an axis ratio and a position angle identical to the appropriate beam to extract the line spectra from the data cubes" (CO Line Emission); "In the case of C$^{18}$O data the integration was performed with the same aperture radius and over the same velocity range as established for the more abundant species." (CO Line Emission)
   - parameter: channels with peak SNR > 3  
  > "where the peak SNR is higher than 3 (within the aperture)" (CO Line Emission)
   - fitting: Statistical error from line-free spectral regions plus 10% flux calibration error  
  > "To estimate the statistical error of the derived line fluxes we used the line-free regions of the spectra, the final uncertainty was computed by adding a 10% flux calibration error to this." (CO Line Emission)
   - validation: Compared with Hughes 2017 (49 Cet 3-2 profile) and MacGregor 2018 (HD 32297 flux 1.02 Jy km/s); offset C18O source in 49 Cet treated as upper limit  
  > "the derived integrated line flux of 1.02 Jy km s$^{-1}$ are consistent with our results" (CO Line Emission); "the integrated line flux is considered as an upper limit in the following analysis" (CO Line Emission)
4. **assess optical depth from line ratios**: Compare measured isotopologue flux ratios with optically thin expectations from ISM isotope ratios  
  > "one would expect line ratios of $S_{\rm ^{12}CO} / S_{\rm ^{13}CO} \sim 85-91$ and $S_{\rm ^{13}CO} / S_{\rm ^{18}CO} \sim 7.3$" (CO Line Emission)
   - assumption: [12C]/[13C]=77, [16O]/[18O]=560; identical excitation temperatures 10-100 K  
  > "assuming that all three isotopologues are optically thin and have identical excitation temperatures between 10 and 100 K" (CO Line Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **derive CO isotopologue and total CO masses**: Optically thin LTE mass formula on 13CO (49 Cet) and C18O (HD 32297), scaled by isotope ratios and molecular masses  
  > "We assumed that local thermodynamical equilibrium (LTE) holds and utilized the Boltzmann equation to compute the fractional populations." (CO Line Emission); "the obtained CO isotopologue masses were multiplied by the corresponding isotope ratios and took into account the difference in the molecular masses" (CO Line Emission)
   - parameter: excitation temperature 20 K  
  > "for the excitation temperature we adopted 20 K for both our disks" (CO Line Emission)
   - assumption: LTE; ISM isotope ratios  
  > "Taking isotope ratios of \[$^{12}$C\]/\[$^{13}$C\]=77 and \[$^{16}$O\]/\[$^{18}$O\]=560" (CO Line Emission)
   - fitting: Uncertainties from line fluxes and distances only  
  > "The uncertainties account only for errors in the integrated line fluxes and distances of the disks." (CO Line Emission)
   - validation: Masses would not exceed derived values by more than factor two for 7-74 K in LTE  
  > "for gas temperatures between 7 and 74 K the gas mass estimates would not exceed our derived CO masses by more than a factor of two" (CO Line Emission)
6. **estimate minimum shielding factors**: Steady-state M_CO = Mdot_loss gamma t_ph eps_CO with mass loss from Matra 2017b formula  
  > "If we know $t_{\rm ph}$, $\gamma$, and $\dot{M}_{\rm loss}$ then the minimum necessary shielding factor in the 49 Cet and HD 32297 systems can be estimated." (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - parameter: 49 Cet R=112 au, ΔR=82 au; HD 32297 R=100 au, ΔR=40 au  
  > "This resulted in $R=112$ au and $\Delta R = 82$ au." (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - assumption: t_ph ~120 yr (interstellar UV only), gamma = 0.27  
  > "As a lower threshold for the UV flux, we consider only the influence of interstellar UV photons" (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **test CO self-shielding**: Interpolate Visser 2009 tab. 5 for required column; compare with max vertical CO column assuming gas follows dust  
  > "Assuming that the gas follows the same spatial distribution as the dust component and taking the total CO mass derived in Sect." (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **model with 1-D shielded secondary gas disk**: 1-D viscous evolution of CO, C0, 13CO, C18O with C0 shielding, self-shielding and isotope-selective photodissociation; 10x10 logarithmic grid in Mdot_CO and alpha compared with observed 13CO/C18O masses, plus inner CO radius, CI flux (49 Cet) and 12CO flux (HD 32297) computed with LIME in LTE  
  > "This new 1-D model (which will be presented in detail in a forthcoming paper) thus solves in parallel the viscous diffusion equations (cite: lynden-bell1974) for CO and C$^0$" (Upgraded shielded secondary gas disk model); "we run a large grid of 100 models to explore the parameter space in $\dot{M}_{\rm CO}$ and $\alpha$" (Modelling results); "In the computation of CI model fluxes we used the LIME radiation transfer code (cite: brinch2010) assuming LTE conditions." (Modelling results)
   - software: LIME
   - parameter: Mdot_CO 1e-3 to 1e-1 M_earth/Myr; alpha 1e-5 to 1e-2; 10 log-spaced elements each  
  > "comprised of 10 elements going from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-1}$ M$_\oplus$/Myr for $\dot{M}_{\rm CO}$ and from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-2}$ for $\alpha$" (Modelling results)
   - parameter: run times 40 Myr (49 Cet), 25 Myr (HD 32297), starting 5 Myr after birth; belts 71-153 au and 80-120 au  
  > "our simulations are run for 40 Myr and 25 Myr for 49 Cet and HD 32297, respectively" (Modelling results); "For HD 32297, we adopted a planetesimal belt between 80 and 120 au" (Modelling results)
   - assumption: Uniform gas temperature 20 K; pre-stirred disk; constant mass input with radius; ISM isotope proportions in released gas; radiation pressure on gas neglected  
  > "We took a uniform gas temperature of 20 K (Sect." (Modelling results); "which we assume to be released at the ISM proportion compared to $^{12}$CO" (Upgraded shielded secondary gas disk model); "Therefore this effect was not considered in our modelling." (Upgraded shielded secondary gas disk model)
   - assumption: HD 32297 inclination 88° or 84°  
  > "In the calculations we assumed two different inclination values, 88$^\circ$ (dashed line) and 84$^\circ$ (solid)" (Modelling results)
   - fitting: Grid search; acceptable region where observables agree within 3σ contours  
  > "where all three observational constraints, represented by the blue (inner CO disk radius), green (CI emission), and black ($^{13}$CO mass) lines, are simultaneously satisfied within their 3$\sigma$ uncertainties" (Modelling results)
   - validation: Required ice fractions compared with solar system comets (2-27%)  
  > "the required $\gamma$ values are not unrealistically high" (Modelling results)
9. **assess gas-dust coupling**: Stokes number using peak total (C0+CO) model gas surface density for beta=0.5 grains  
  > "Concerning $\Sigma_{\rm g}$, we took the highest total (C$^0$+CO) gas surface density of our secondary gas disk models" (Gas-dust interaction)
   - parameter: beta = 0.5; grain density 2 g cm^-3  
  > "$\beta$ is equal to 0.5 for the smallest bound grains (cite: krivov2010)" (Gas-dust interaction); "Taking a grain density of 2 g cm$^{-3}$" (Gas-dust interaction)
   - assumption: H and O daughter products neglected (St are upper limits)  
  > "These values, however, should be considered as upper limits since additional daughter products, H, O, from photodissociation of CO, CO$_2$, and H$_2$O have not been taken into account" (Gas-dust interaction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- 12CO is highly optically thick in both disks; 13CO probably optically thick in HD 32297 and thin in 49 Cet  
  > "Thus the measured values imply highly optically thick $^{12}$CO emission in both disks, while the $^{13}$CO line is probably optically thick in HD 32297 and optically thin in 49 Cet." (CO Line Emission)
- HD 32297 continuum is unresolved; 49 Cet marginally resolved

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Optically thin rare isotopologues needed for CO mass since 12CO is optically thick  
  > "We used the optically thin line observations to estimate the CO gas mass." (CO Line Emission)
- Why this method: H2 is hard to detect, so they test whether a secondary gas model can explain CO; fit rare isotopologues to avoid abundance-ratio uncertainties  
  > "Unfortunately, the detection of molecular hydrogen is notoriously difficult." (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297); "we rather have to reproduce the results of the optically thin $^{13}$CO or C$^{18}$O line observations" (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
- Alternative: JCMT 12CO line of Greaves 2016 rejected as discrepant lower-S/N measurement  
  > "Therefore the somewhat deviating line observation of (cite: greaves2016), derived from a lower S/N measurement, will not be taken into account in the further analysis." (CO Line Emission)
- Alternative: Hybrid primordial-gas model not ruled out  
  > "we cannot rule out their presence based on our current results" (A possible primordial origin of the gas component)
- Alternative: Offset C18O source in 49 Cet treated as upper limit  
  > "since we have no explanation for the observed large offset, the integrated line flux is considered as an upper limit" (CO Line Emission)
- How they decided it was enough: Aperture size chosen as minimum enclosing all disk CO emission from flux-vs-aperture curve  
  > "we defined the minimum aperture size that includes all CO emission associated to the disk" (CO Line Emission)
