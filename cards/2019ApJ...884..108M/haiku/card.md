## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image the 7-m data**: Calibrate and flag with the CASA pipeline script, then subtract continuum with uvcontsub and image CO cubes with tclean (natural weighting)  
  > "Both data sets were calibrated and flagged with the ALMA reduction tool Common Astronomy Software Applications" (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Channel width 0.7 km/s for 12CO; 1.4 km/s for 13CO and C18O  
  > "The channel width was set to 0.7 km s" (Observations)
   - assumption: Continuum is a first-order polynomial in line-free channels  
  > "to fit a first order polynomial to line-free channels in the uv space" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **measure the continuum flux density and disk geometry**: Fit a single-component source in the visibility plane; elliptical Gaussian for 49 Cet, point source for HD 32297; channels with CO flagged and weights recomputed with statwt  
  > "For 49 Cet an elliptical Gaussian model was fitted to the non-flagged visibility data." (Continuum Emission); "Therefore in this case we applied a point source model in the visibility domain fitting" (Continuum Emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Continuum flux 5.3±0.7 mJy (49 Cet); 3.4±0.4 mJy (HD 32297)  
  > "Using uvmodelfit we measure a flux density of 5.3" (Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: Least-squares visibility fitting in CASA (uvmodelfit); uncertainty as quadratic sum of measurement error and 10% calibration error  
  > "the quoted uncertainty was computed as the quadratic sum of the measurement error and the typical ALMA calibration error" (Continuum Emission)
   - validation: 49 Cet flux compared with straight-line log-log extrapolation of earlier photometry (4.7 mJy)  
  > "we can infer a flux density of 4.7 mJy at 1.33 mm. Our measurement is consistent with this prediction." (Continuum Emission)
3. **derive dust masses**: Optically thin isothermal dust mass from flux, distance, Planck function and opacity  
  > "Assuming isothermal, optically thin dust emission and using our obtained millimeter flux densities" (Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: T_dust 56 K (49 Cet, blackbody at 100 au); 47 K (HD 32297, from macgregor2018); opacity 2.3 cm^2/g  
  > "we took the equilibrium temperature of blackbody grains located at a radial distance of 100 au" (Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: Isothermal, optically thin dust emission  
  > "Assuming isothermal, optically thin dust emission" (Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainties exclude opacity factor of about 2–3  
  > "do not account for the probable factor of" (Continuum Emission)
4. **extract CO line spectra and integrated fluxes**: Elliptical apertures matched to beam shape, sized to include all disk CO emission; integrate consecutive channels with peak SNR above 3; C18O integrated over same aperture and velocity range as more abundant species  
  > "where the peak SNR is higher than 3 (within the aperture)" (CO Line Emission); "In the case of C$^{18}$O data the integration was performed with the same aperture radius and over the same velocity range" (CO Line Emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Identical aperture sizes for 12CO and 13CO at both disks  
  > "We obtained identical aperture sizes for" (CO Line Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Statistical error from line-free regions plus 10% flux calibration error  
  > "we used the line-free regions of the spectra" (CO Line Emission)
5. **derive CO isotopologue and total CO masses**: Optically thin LTE mass from C18O and 13CO line fluxes, excitation temperature 20 K, scaled by ISM isotope ratios  
  > "We assumed that local thermodynamical equilibrium (LTE) holds" (CO Line Emission); "for the excitation temperature we adopted 20 K for both our disks" (CO Line Emission)
   - software: LIME
   - parameter: Excitation temperature 20 K  
  > "for the excitation temperature we adopted 20 K for both our disks" (CO Line Emission)
   - assumption: All isotopologues optically thin with identical excitation temperatures; LTE; ISM isotope ratios  
  > "assuming that all three isotopologues are optically thin and have identical excitation temperatures between 10 and 100 K" (CO Line Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: LTE gas temperature range 7–74 K would change masses by no more than a factor of two  
  > "gas mass estimates would not exceed our derived CO masses by more than a factor of two" (CO Line Emission)
6. **estimate shielding factor from steady-state CO budget**: Compute mass loss rate from collisional cascade formula, then the shielding factor epsilon_CO needed to reproduce the CO mass  
  > "Assuming a steady-state collisional cascade in the planetesimal belt" (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - parameter: Ice mass fraction 0.27; t_ph ~120 yr; R=112 au and ΔR=82 au (49 Cet); R=100 au and ΔR=40 au (HD 32297)  
  > "Based on (cite: macgregor2018) for the outer belt of HD 32297 we adopted a radius" (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - assumption: Steady-state collisional cascade; CO2 photodissociation contributes CO  
  > "Here we consider that photodissociation of CO$_2$ molecules also contribute to the observed CO gas." (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Factors described as lower limits due to possible lower ice fraction and stellar UV effects  
  > "these factors should be considered as lower limits" (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
7. **fit secondary gas evolution model to observed 13CO and C18O masses**: Grid over CO production rate and alpha; compare total 13CO (49 Cet) or C18O (HD 32297) masses and inner CO radius, CI flux and 12CO flux constraints  
  > "we run a large grid of 100 models to explore the parameter space" (Modelling results)
   - software: LIME
   - parameter: Grid 10×10 logarithmic from 1e-3 to 1e-1 M_earth/Myr and 1e-5 to 1e-2 alpha; initiation 5 Myr; run 40 Myr (49 Cet), 25 Myr (HD 32297)  
  > "then our simulations are run for 40 Myr and 25 Myr for 49 Cet and HD 32297, respectively" (Modelling results)
   - assumption: Uniform gas temperature 20 K; disk pre-stirred; constant mass input with radius  
  > "We took a uniform gas temperature of 20 K (Sect. [4.2](#coemission)) in the whole disks." (Modelling results)
   - fitting: Grid search, comparing model masses and CI/CO line fluxes with observed values within 3 sigma  
  > "Solid black contours correspond to the observed" (Modelling results)
   - validation: Consistency of inner CO radius, CI flux and 13CO mass in 3 sigma space  
  > "we found that there exists a region in the parameter space" (Modelling results)
8. **assess gas-dust coupling**: Compute dimensionless stopping time (Stokes number) for blowout-size grains using the maximum total CO+C0 gas surface density of the model  
  > "we took the highest total (C$^0$+CO) gas surface density of our secondary gas disk models" (Gas-dust interaction)
   - parameter: beta = 0.5 for smallest bound grains; grain density 2 g/cm^3  
  > "Taking a grain density of 2 g cm" (Gas-dust interaction)
   - assumption: Circular parent-body orbits; compact spherical grains with radiation pressure efficiency unity  
  > "Assuming compact spherical grains with radiation pressure efficiency of unity" (Gas-dust interaction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stokes numbers described as upper limits because H and O daughter products are not included  
  > "These values, however, should be considered as upper limits since additional daughter products" (Gas-dust interaction)

## Qualitative findings

- Both disks have CO masses above 0.01 M_earth with optically thick 12CO, implying high CO shielding  
  > "detected similarly high CO content (>0.01 M" (abstract)
- Shielded secondary gas model with C0 and self-shielding can explain CO in both systems without primordial gas

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The observations are part of a long-term project to study the nature of young dust-rich debris disks with high 12CO line luminosity.  
  > "As part of our long term project to study the properties and nature of all known young dust-rich debris disks" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Because the H2 content is hard to detect, the authors test whether the observed CO can be explained by a secondary gas production scenario instead.  
  > "Therefore here we use a different strategy and investigate whether the observed CO gas quantity" (Shielding of CO gas in 49 Cet and HD 32297)
- Alternative: Hybrid disk model with primordial H2 shielding CO (kospal2013; pericaud2017) is not required for the observed CO, though it cannot be ruled out.  
  > "we cannot rule out their presence based on our current results" (A possible primordial origin of the gas component)
- Alternative: Radiation pressure on carbon was not considered because the disk becomes optically thick to UV.  
  > "Therefore this effect was not considered in our modelling." (Upgraded shielded secondary gas disk model)
- How they decided it was enough: A model is accepted when inner CO radius, CI flux and 13CO mass constraints overlap within 3 sigma.  
  > "where all three observational constraints, represented by the blue" (Modelling results)
- Limitation (authors): The 49 Cet C18O line flux is taken at an offset source with no explanation for the large offset, so it is an upper limit.  
  > "since we have no explanation for the observed large offset, the integrated line flux is considered as an upper limit" (CO Line Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Total CO masses depend on isotope ratios that may be altered by isotope-selective photodissociation.  
  > "This can introduce serious uncertainties in the estimates of the total CO gas mass" (CO Line Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass uncertainty does not include the factor of about 2–3 from the dust opacity.  
  > "do not account for the probable factor of" (Continuum Emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The paper gives two different ALMA project codes (2016.2.00200.S in Observations and 2017.2.00200.S in Acknowledgments); the archive link lists only 2016.2.00200.S.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1.33 mm continuum and CO fluxes are not corrected for the gas excitation or beam dilution; the excitation temperature of 20 K is adopted, not derived.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1-D shielded model is described as to be presented in a forthcoming paper, so the new model cannot be checked from this text alone.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust opacity, CO-to-H2 and isotope-ratio assumptions carry large systematic uncertainties not in the quoted errors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The HD 32297 model fit is described as marginal and depends on very low alpha values.
