## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum**: CASA pipeline calibration (WVR, Tsys, bandpass, phase, amplitude); line-free channel selection with uvcontsub; concatenate scheduling blocks; natural-weighted CLEAN  
  > "The procedure included offline water vapor radiometer calibration, system temperature correction, and bandpass, phase and amplitude calibrations." (2.2 ALMA Observations); "The 100 GHz observations were concatenated in CASA" (2.2 ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA 5.4.1
   - parameter: natural weighting; CLEAN threshold 1/2 the RMS noise  
  > "CLEANed using the *CLEAN* algorithm down to a threshold of $\frac{1}{2}$ the RMS noise" (2.2 ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum total flux**: CASA uvmodelfit with a disk model on the visibilities  
  > "We used the CASA task *uvmodelfit* and a disk model to obtain a total flux of $0.050\pm0.008~\rm  mJy$." (2.1 VLA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive 232-100 GHz spectral index**: Spectral index from the 232 GHz and 100 GHz total fluxes, with 10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty at each frequency  
  > "The spectral index between 232 and 100 GHz is $\alpha_{1.3-3.0 {\rm mm}} = 2.20\pm 0.11$" (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the value reported by Hales et al. calculated within the ALMA Band 6 spectral windows  
  > "The spectral index is consistent with $2.19\pm0.47$ as reported in (cite: hales18)" (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
4. **Fit radiative transfer disk model to 232 and 100 GHz images**: Ray-traced RADMC-3D continuum images (mctherm then image), attenuated by the primary beam and convolved with the dirty beam, compared to data in the image plane  
  > "we use RADMC-3D and the *mctherm* command to calculate the dust temperature and then use the *image* command to generate a ray-traced continuum image" (3 Model Fitting); "The image is then attenuated by the primary beam and convolved with the dirty beam for a given observational setup." (3 Model Fitting)
   - software: RADMC-3D 0.41, radmc3dPy, OpacityTool
   - parameter: Free parameters: total disk mass, characteristic radius Rc, surface-density exponent gamma, scale height ratio hc at 100 au  
  > "The free parameters considered in the modeling are: the total disk mass with a gas-to-dust ratio of 100:1" (3 Model Fitting)
   - parameter: Equal weighting of the two datasets; 100x1000 link chains minus 100 each for burn-in; grains 0.1-10000 um  
  > "We adopted an equal weighting for the two data sets and ran an MCMC fit with $100\times1000$ link chains minus 100 each for burn-in." (3 Model Fitting); "change the particle size population to $0.1 - 10000~\mu$m grains" (3 Model Fitting)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100:1  
  > "the total disk mass with a gas-to-dust ratio of 100:1" (3 Model Fitting)
   - assumption: Dust opacities from OpacityTool with 60% amorphous silicates, 15% amorphous carbon, 25% porosity, Bruggeman mixing, hollow spheres maximum hollow ratio 0.8, size distribution s^-3.5  
  > "Bruggeman mixing is used to calculate an effective refractory index and a distribution of hollow spheres with a maximum hollow ratio of 0.8 (cite: min05)" (3 Model Fitting); "following a power-law size distribution of $s^{-3.5}$" (3 Model Fitting)
   - fitting: Metropolis-Hastings MCMC; chi2 per wavelength with sigma = observed rms times synthetic beam size in pixels, wavelength chi2 values averaged with equal weights; priors not stated  
  > "we use a Metropolis-Hastings Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) model fitting approach" (3 Model Fitting); "We adopt an equal weighting for each wavelength, and all of the $\chi^{2}$ values are averaged together." (3 Model Fitting)
   - validation: Best-fit model and residual maps compared with the data; 95% credible regions from posteriors  
  > "This approach of only fitting the ALMA data was able to well reproduce the observations." (3 Model Fitting)
5. **Test inclusion of 15 GHz data and a 15 GHz-only thermal fit**: Joint 232+100+15 GHz fit with larger grains and varied weights failed; 15 GHz fitted alone with the same RT approach, giving implausible parameters  
  > "Therefore, we decided to exclude the 15 GHz data" (3 Model Fitting); "we tried fitting the VLA 15 GHz data alone with the same RT approach as for the ALMA datasets." (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
   - software: RADMC-3D 0.41
   - parameter: Dust grains 0.1-21000 um for the joint fit  
  > "We further assume the disk is populated by $0.1 - 21000~\mu$m grains" (3 Model Fitting)
   - fitting: Same MCMC approach  
  > "with the same RT approach as for the ALMA datasets" (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
   - validation: Rejected because the 15 GHz-only best-fit disk has an unphysical scale height and a mass 3.5x larger  
  > "The scale height, however, seems un-physically large at 89 au." (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
6. **Fit a central point source on top of the thermal disk at 15 GHz**: Take the ALMA-only best-fit RT model scaled to 15 GHz (about 0.001 mJy) and add a central point source, fitting its flux and offsets with MCMC  
  > "This leaves only 3 free parameters: the flux of the central emission, the X-offset, and the Y-offset." (4.3 Long Wavelength Central Emission); "we use RADMC-3D to calculate the emission at 15 GHz, resulting in a very faint disk with a total flux of only $\sim0.001$ mJy" (4.3 Long Wavelength Central Emission)
   - software: RADMC-3D 0.41
   - assumption: Additional emission assumed to come from approximately the center of the disk  
  > "the "additional" emission required to reproduce the data can be assumed to be coming from approximately the center of the disk" (4.3 Long Wavelength Central Emission)
   - fitting: MCMC similar to Sec. 3, adding the point-source flux on top of the RT model  
  > "We adopt an MCMC modelling approach similar to the one outlined in Sec. [3](#sec:models)" (4.3 Long Wavelength Central Emission)
   - validation: Best-fit model and residuals shown in a figure  
  > "the best fit model and residuals are shown in Fig. [6](#fig:resid_cent)" (4.3 Long Wavelength Central Emission)
7. **Assess ice line position and disk relaxation timescale**: Take ice line from the best-fit RT model and compute disk relaxation time versus radius using density and temperature from the RT model  
  > "The density and temperature we take from our best-fit RT model, the gas-to-dust ratio is set to 100:1" (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
   - software: RADMC-3D 0.41
   - parameter: Cooling-wavelength opacity at 18 um of 52.7 cm^2/g  
  > "we choose a representative cooling wavelength opacity at $\rm 18\,\mu m$ of $\kappa=52.7 \rm \,cm^2/g$" (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
   - assumption: Water ice condensation temperature typically 150 to 175 K in the midplane (literature)  
  > "is typically between 150 to 175 K in the midplane of a disk (cite: lecar06)" (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with outburst-time ice line locations from Abraham et al. and Banzatti et al.  
  > "consistent with data from during the outburst (cite: abraham09; banzatti12)" (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
8. **Infer maximum grain size from millimeter detection and spectral index**: Argue that thermal grains emit inefficiently at wavelengths longer than their size, so detection at 100 GHz with an optically thin spectral index implies grains of at least 3 mm  
  > "we infer that grains of at least $3\rm~mm$ may be present in the disk post-outburst." (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
   - assumption: Spectral index indicates an optically thin disk  
  > "indicating the disk is optically thin" (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: RT models include grains up to 1 cm; stated as consistent but not a confirmation of 1 cm grains  
  > "While this is not a confirmation of $1\rm~cm$ grains it does indicate the data is at least consistent with the presence of up to $1\rm~cm$ grains." (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
9. **Evaluate origin of the 15 GHz excess emission**: Compute brightness temperature, synchrotron critical frequency and electron temperature, gyrosynchrotron emitting-region size, and expected free-free flux from X-ray luminosity; reason about ECMI, jets and winds  
  > "Adopting a magnetic field strength of 3 kG gives $\nu_{crit} = 8.4$ GHz." (4.3.1 Role of the Magnetic Field); "we find an expected flux of 4.3 $\mu$Jy." (4.3.2 Radio Jets and Non-thermal Disk Emission)
   - parameter: Lorentz factor ~1 (or 1.34); angle theta ~90 deg; delta ~2 lower limit; B of 3-6 kG  
  > "where $\gamma$ is the Lorentz factor and is assumed to be $\sim1$" (4.3.1 Role of the Magnetic Field); "where $\theta$ is assumed to be $\sim90^{\circ}$ (cite: dulk85)" (4.3.1 Role of the Magnetic Field)
   - assumption: Emitting region taken uniformly spread over the stellar surface for the brightness temperature  
  > "assuming the size of the emitting region is uniformly spread out over the surface of the star" (4.3.1 Role of the Magnetic Field)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Order-of-magnitude consistency checks only; the electron delta cannot be constrained with available data  
  > "(which cannot be constrained with the available data)" (4.3.1 Role of the Magnetic Field)

## Qualitative findings

- Disk mass is nearly an order of magnitude higher than typical Lupus disks from ALMA surveys, assuming gas-to-dust 100:1  
  > "Our model fitting finds a total disk mass nearly an order of magnitude higher, assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100:1." (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
- The 45 GHz non-detection does not constrain thermal disk properties  
  > "This calculated flux shows that the non-detection at 45 GHz is not useful for constraining the thermal disk properties." (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
- Most likely 15 GHz scenario is a combination of (gyro)synchrotron and free-free emission  
  > "we find that the most likely scenario is a combination of (gyro)synchrotron and free-free emission" (4.3.2 Radio Jets and Non-thermal Disk Emission)
- Compact dust disk is in line with other studies of EXor/FUor disks  
  > "Other ALMA studies have found that EXor/FUor disks tend to be more compact than that of typical protoplanetary disks (cite: cieza18)." (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long wavelength observations of EX Lupi in its post-outburst state  
  > "In this paper, we present long wavelength observations of EX Lupi." (Introduction)
- Why this method: RT fit follows the earlier Hales et al. approach; 15 GHz data excluded from the joint fit because it could not be reproduced  
  > "we followed a radiative transfer (RT) model fitting approach similar to (cite: hales18)" (3 Model Fitting); "Therefore, we decided to exclude the 15 GHz data" (3 Model Fitting)
- Alternative: Joint fit of 232, 100 and 15 GHz with thermal disk emission rejected  
  > "This indicates that the approach is not well suited for fitting all three datasets simultaneously, or that there are different emission mechanisms at longer wavelengths." (3 Model Fitting)
- Alternative: Thermal 15 GHz-only disk model judged highly improbable  
  > "the resulting disk parameters to do so are highly improbable" (4.1 Model Fitting Results)
- Alternative: ECMI judged an unlikely source of 15 GHz emission  
  > "ECMI is a highly unlikely source of the 15 GHz emission due to the required stability" (4.3.1 Role of the Magnetic Field)
- Alternative: Radio jet origin judged unlikely  
  > "we note the likelihood of the 15 GHz emission being jet-driven is low" (4.3.2 Radio Jets and Non-thermal Disk Emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 45 GHz non-detection due to low declination, poor phase calibration and less on-source time than requested  
  > "led to a non-detection at 45 GHz" (2.1 VLA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): 15 GHz beam too large to resolve the disk  
  > "Due to the large beam, the emission is only marginally resolved along the minor axis of the beam." (2.1 VLA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot separate a point source from a few-au extended region at 15 GHz  
  > "we are unable to differentiate emission from a point source and a moderately extended (i.e., a few au) region" (4.3 Long Wavelength Central Emission)
- Limitation (authors): No pre-outburst ALMA and VLA data for comparison  
  > "although we note that there is no pre-outburst ALMA and VLA data to compare it to." (4.2 Millimeter Observations & Grain Growth)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Text and Table 1 disagree on units and values (100 GHz peak flux given as Jy and mJy, total flux as uJy and mJy; spectral index error 0.11 vs 0.01)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fit is to images with equal chi2 weights, and the authors suggest the 100 GHz beam may inflate Rc by a factor of 2 versus Hales et al.; visibility-plane fitting not done
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disk mass depends on assumed dust opacity (fixed composition, size distribution) and gas-to-dust 100:1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Priors, convergence diagnostics and number of independent samples are not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Grain size lower limit of 3 mm is an argument from the spectral index, not a direct fit; spectral index of a marginally resolved source with different beams may be biased
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 15 GHz excess interpretation rests on a single marginally resolved detection with no spectral index
