## Qualitative findings

- The continuum emission at ~43 GHz shows an elongated north-south jet-like structure that is perpendicular to the inner dust ring traced by ALMA at ~345 GHz.  
  > "The continuum emission at $\sim$ 43 GHz shows an elongated jet-like structure perpendicular to the $\sim$ 345 GHz observation." (Abstract); "In the inset we show the central part of M 2-9, where the elongated structure (north-south direction) from the JVLA image is observed perpendicular to the contours overlapped by ALMA (tracing a more inner dust ring as is reported by (cite: Castro-Carrizo2017))." (Results and discussion)
- The radio-continuum jet bends in opposite directions between the 1999 (~23 GHz) and 2006 (~43 GHz) epochs, showing morphological spatial variability over a ~7-year interval.  
  > "Both RC emissions have the same morphology in the central part (elongation at the north-south direction), but the external emissions change in direction, presenting a morphological variability; the $\sim$ 23 GHz emission blends to the left side or direction, while the $\sim$ 43 GHz does so towards the right." (Results and discussion); "The jet traced at 43.34 GHz is blended in opposite directions from 1999 to 2006 (7 years at least)." (Conclusion)
- The white dwarf moved from the left to the right of the red giant star from our perspective between 1999 and 2006.  
  > "Furthermore, since the jet exhibited a change in the direction of its bending during that interval of time, it can be inferred that the white dwarf star moved from the left to the right of the red giant star as seen from our perspective (see Fig. 6)." (Results and discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution JVLA (~43 GHz) and ALMA (~345 GHz) continuum observations at sub-arcsec scales are needed to resolve the central collimated ionized wind and the surrounding dust ring in the inner core of M 2-9.  
  > "We studied the inner part of M 2-9 at arc-sec scales by using new continuum observations from the JVLA and Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) radio interferometers at $\sim$ 43 GHz ($\sim$0.7 cm) and $\sim$345 GHz ($\sim$0.9 mm), respectively." (Introduction); "The aim was to investigate the presence of this collimated fast wind suggested by (cite: Livio2001), with the JVLA, in a disk-like structure modelled by (cite: Lykou2011) and traced by ALMA." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Adding the 43 GHz measurement to the multi-frequency SED and comparing multi-epoch radio maps allows testing whether the emission follows the theoretical spectral indices of an isothermal wind and whether the jet bends over the binary orbit.  
  > "This study also includes a spectral energy distribution (SED) analysis to complement the work of (cite: Lim2003) and determine the nature of RC observations." (Introduction); "Thus, to investigate the nature of the 43 GHz RC emission shown on Fig. 3, we present the SED reported in (cite: Lim2003), adding the new $\nu$ $\sim$ 43 GHz emission (see Fig. 5 and Tab. 1)." (Results and discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Observations do not have sufficient angular resolution to directly determine dynamical parameters such as the orbital period.  
  > "Although our observations do not have sufficient angular resolution to determine dynamical parameters like the orbital period, we can say that since the time interval between the two observations is around 7 years and taking into account an orbital period of 86 years (cite: Doyle2000; Corradi2011), the angle subtended by the white dwarf in those epochs, as seen from the red giant star, is around 30 degrees." (Results and discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA imaging procedure, continuum extraction, and final ALMA image noise/beam parameters are completely omitted from the text, and no quantitative measurements (such as 345 GHz flux density or ring size) are extracted from the ALMA data.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a mismatch between the ALMA project code stated in Section 2 (2013.1.00458.S) and the one in the acknowledgments (2015.1.01539.S).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 2, the JVLA Set II (~43 GHz) spectral window central frequencies are printed as 4885.1 MHz and 4835.1 MHz (which correspond to 4.86 GHz C-band), likely a typographical error in the paper.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 1, the frequency cell for the first row (the new ~43.34 GHz data point) is left blank.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison of jet bending between 1999 and 2006 is made at two different frequencies (~23 GHz vs ~43 GHz), which trace slightly different optical depth regimes and angular scales.
