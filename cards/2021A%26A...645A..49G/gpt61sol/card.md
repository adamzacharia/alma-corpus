  > "together with the adopted baselines and Gaussian fits to the profiles." (Herschel/PACS data); "Sinc functions on top of baselines of order 1 were fitted around the spectra" (Herschel/SPIRE data); "We consider in the fit 3 continuum flux densities, at 30, 428, and 660 $\mu$m, as constraints for fitting the SED."    - software: ipipe, HIPE
   - parameter: PACS: HIPE 14.0.1, calibration tree 72, oversampling four, upsample 1  
  > "The data reduction was performed using the PACS reduction and calibration pipeline (ipipe) included in HIPE 14.0.1, with calibration tree version 72, using an oversampling of four fully independent channels (an upsample parameter of 1)." (Herschel/PACS data)

  > "We therefore include the line in the fit with a high 1$\sigma$ uncertainty of 200 Jy km s$^{-1}$" (Defining the data set)

  > "the very high-lying H$_2$O $8_{18}-7_{07}$ line at $63.32$ $\mu$m" (Defining the data set); "is not detected in ESO 320-G030, with $S<70$ Jy km s$^{-1}$ (2 $\sigma$)." (Defining the data set)

  > "the adopted baselines and Gaussian fits to the profiles." (Herschel/PACS data); "Sinc functions on top of baselines of order 1 were fitted around the spectra" (Herschel/SPIRE data)

3. **Build excitation-model grids**: Compute nonlocal, non-LTE H2O excitation, line fluxes and profiles, and dust SEDs in spherical gas-and-dust components.  
  > "the model components consist of spherically symmetric distributions of gas and dust, for which the statistical equilibrium populations of the H$_2$O rotational levels are calculated through nonlocal, non-LTE radiative transfer calculations." (A library of model components); "The fluxes and profiles of all involved lines and the spectral energy distribution (SED) of the dust continuum from the mid-IR to the mm are subsequently computed." (A library of model components)
   - parameter: Vary T_dust, tau_100, N_H2O, and n_H2; fix Delta V = 100 km s^-1 and T_gas = 150 K  
  > "The physical parameters that are modified from model to model are $T_{\mathrm{dust}}$, $\tau_{100}$, $N_{\mathrm{H2O}}$, and $n_{\mathrm{H2}}$, and we keep fixed $\Delta V=100$ km s$^{-1}$ and $T_{\mathrm{gas}}=150$ K." (A library of model components)
   - parameter: Core: tau_100 >= 4 and T_dust >= 75 K  
  > "The “core” models are all optically thick in the far-IR ($\tau_{100}\ge4$) and very warm ($T_{\mathrm{dust}}\ge75$ K)." (Groups of model components)
   - parameter: Disk: tau_100 >= 0.7 and T_dust = 45–75 K; envelope: tau_100 <= approximately 2 and T_dust = 40–60 K  
  > "The “disk” models have lower $\tau_{100}$ but are still (nearly) optically thick ($\tau_{100}\ge0.7$) with $T_{\mathrm{dust}}=45-75$ K." (Groups of model components); "The “envelope” models mainly cover optically thin conditions but can reach optically thick values ($\tau_{100}\lesssim2$), and have moderate $T_{\mathrm{dust}}=40-60$ K." (Groups of model components)
   - parameter: Three n_H2 grid values spanning (1.7–15) × 10^4 cm^-3; N_H2O multiplicative spacing 1.5–2  
  > "While $N_{\mathrm{H2O}}$ is varied by more than 1 dex within each group with multiplicative factors of $1.5-2$" (Groups of model components); "the grid for $n_{\mathrm{H2}}$ is coarser with only 3 values, representing typical densities of buried galactic nuclei ($(1.7-15)\times10^4$ cm$^{-3}$" (Groups of model components)
   - assumption: Uniform physical properties within each component; mixed gas and dust  
  > "We assume for each component uniform physical properties" (A library of model components); "The gas and dust are assumed to be mixed." (A library of model components)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Vary density to test collisional excitation; absence of a high-density trend supports insensitivity to the fixed gas temperature.  
  > "That we do not find such a trend below (Section [3.2.2](#excit)) indicates that our results are insensitive to our choice of $T_{\mathrm{gas}}$." (A library of model components)
4. **Fit component sizes and infer parameter distributions**: Search all core–disk–envelope combinations; minimize reduced chi-squared for solid angles; marginalize Bayesian posterior distributions for physical and derived parameters.  
  > "We then consider all possible combinations, in a number of $2.2\times10^8$, that are obtained by taking 1 component of each group." (Minimizing chi_red^2); "The minimization is performed by a standard procedure and yields both $R_j$ for each component of the combination and then the minimum $\chi_{\mathrm{red}}^2$." (Minimizing chi_red^2); "The posterior distribution of eq. ([\[prob2\]](#prob2)) is marginalized over to obtain the likelihood distribution of a specific parameter $a_i$, and of any function of parameters $f(\mathbf{a})$ (cite: war03)." (Likelihood distributions)
   - parameter: Three components; 24 fitted line and continuum quantities; 12 free physical parameters; sizes treated as derived  
  > "$N_L=24$ is the number of H$_2$O lines and continuum flux densities that are fitted" (Minimizing chi_red^2); "Our model for ESO 320-G030 has a total of $N_f=12$ free physical parameters, ($T_{\mathrm{dust}}$, $\tau_{100}$, $N_{\mathrm{H2O}}$, $n_{\mathrm{H2}}$) for each of the 3 model components." (Likelihood distributions); "the sizes $R_j$ for each combination are treated as derived rather than free parameters" (Likelihood distributions)
   - parameter: Add 15% Herschel and 10% ALMA systematic uncertainties in quadrature  
  > "we sum in quadrature the errors in Table [\[tab:obs\]](#tab:obs) and the systematic uncertanties of 15% and 10% for the *Herschel* and ALMA measurements, respectively." (Minimizing chi_red^2)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio 100  
  > "which implicitly assumes a gas-to-dust ratio of 100 by mass." (Likelihood distributions)
   - fitting: Gaussian detection likelihood with an error-function upper-limit term; prior zero for T_dust < 60 K and X_H2O > 3 × 10^-5, unity otherwise; no sampler named.  
  > "$N_d=23$ corresponds to the line and continuum detections and $N_u=1$ to the undetected H$_2$O $8_{18}-7_{07}$ line at 63.32 $\mu$m" (Likelihood distributions); "we use the prior $P(\mathbf{a})=0$ whenever a model component with $T_{\mathrm{dust}}<60$ K and $X_{\mathrm{H2O}}>3\times10^{-5}$ is found, and $P(\mathbf{a})=1$ otherwise." (Likelihood distributions); "\left[1+\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{\sigma_i-|S_i(\mathbf{a})|}{\sqrt{2}\sigma_i}" (Likelihood distributions)

## Qualitative findings

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine H2O excitation constraints from Herschel with ALMA spatial maps to constrain nuclear luminosity, extent, temperature, and column density.  
  > "exploit at the maximum level the *Herschel*/ALMA synergy to infer the distribution of luminosity sources, their spatial extent, dust temperatures, and ISM column densities" (Analysis)
- Why this method: Use 3D maps to test spherical-model sizes and resolve poorly constrained component flux contributions.  
  > "to check the reliability of our model fits, and in particular of the calculated sizes of the three components." (A 3D approach); "the contributions by the several model components to the H$_2$O448 line and to the 454 GHz continuum emission are poorly determined" (A 3D approach)
- Alternative: Two nuclear components give a poorer fit than three.  
  > "the minimum value of $\chi_{\mathrm{red}}^2$ significantly increases to 2.3 when only 2 components are used." (Results)
- Alternative: An initially uniform prior admits high-column, low-temperature disk solutions; an abundance prior excludes these.  
  > "We started running calculations with the prior probability density function $P(\mathbf{a})=1$ for all sets of parameters." (Likelihood distributions); "some solutions for the disk, characterized by extremely high $N_{\mathrm{H2O}}$ and low $T_{\mathrm{dust}}<50$ K, yielded significant likelihood." (Likelihood distributions); "we use the prior $P(\mathbf{a})=0$ whenever a model component with $T_{\mathrm{dust}}<60$ K and $X_{\mathrm{H2O}}>3\times10^{-5}$ is found" (Likelihood distributions)
- Alternative: Central coherent core versus distributed star-forming cores; spatial maps favor the central-core model.  
  > "our fit to the H$_2$O fluxes cannot distinguish between a physically coherent region at the center of the galaxy" (Summary and limitations of the model); "a discrete set of star-forming cores spread out over the disk volume (model B) or even beyond." (Summary and limitations of the model); "model A more closely resembles the observed map than model B" (The 454 GHz continuum)
- Alternative: The core may be powered by a buried AGN or by a compact starburst.  
  > "an AGN with $\sim10$% of the total galaxy luminosity is still possible." (Discussion and conclusions); "The nuclear core may still be primarily powered by a starburst" (Discussion and conclusions)
- Alternative: Insufficient nuclear dynamical mass may reflect lower inclination or nonrotational support.  
  > "This discrepancy can be attributed to a lower inclination of the nuclear disk relative to that of the host galaxy" (The velocity field); "Alternatively, $V_{\mathrm{rot}}$ could underestimate $M_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ if the nuclear gas is not rotationally supported, but supported by radiation pressure and turbulence."
