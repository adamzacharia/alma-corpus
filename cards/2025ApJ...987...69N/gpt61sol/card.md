## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify molecular clouds**: Run astrodendro on the 5 km s−1 cube; retain leaf structures as molecular clouds.  
  > "First, we used the `astrodendro` software package (cite: Astrodendro2019) to identify virtually all molecular clouds in the data cube" (Method for Detecting HVCs); "Among the identified structures, only the *leaf* structures were considered molecular clouds." (Method for Detecting HVCs); "instead re-identified clouds using the 5 km s$^{-1}$ resolution cube as described above." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: min_value = 2σ; min_delta = 3.5σ; min_npix = 200 voxels  
  > "`min_value` was set to 2 $\sigma$, `min_delta` to 3.5 $\sigma$, and `min_npix` to 200 voxels, equivalent to about four times the beam area." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - parameter: FoV diameter 9.4′ (~12.3 kpc)  
  > "The field of view (FoV) spans a diameter of 9.4$^\prime$ ($\sim$12.3 kpc), providing extensive spatial coverage for detailed analysis." (Observation Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the 1 km s−1 catalog; finer channels artificially split broad HVCs.  
  > "This is because the catalog based on the 1 km s$^{-1}$ resolution data cube was found to artificially split the HVCs with a wide velocity dispersions into multiple components." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
2. **Construct the disk velocity field**: Smooth the cube to 400 pc with CASA imsmooth; calculate intensity-weighted velocity.  
  > "by using the `imsmooth` task in CASA (cite: McMullin2007; CASA2022) to smooth the cube to 400 pc and then calculating the intensity-weighted velocity." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - software: CASA, imsmooth
   - parameter: Smoothing scale: 400 pc  
  > "The smoothing scale of 400 pc was chosen as it approximately matches the arm thickness, helping to remove local flows associated with the arm." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - assumption: 400 pc smoothing removes local arm flows; central velocities are not representative of the disk.  
  > "The smoothing scale of 400 pc was chosen as it approximately matches the arm thickness, helping to remove local flows associated with the arm." (Method for Detecting HVCs); "the velocity field from the 400-pc-smoothed data cube does not represent true disk velocities in this region." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Select high-velocity clouds**: Subtract the disk velocity field from astrodendro v_cen; select |Δv| ≥ 50 km s−1 within 0.4 < Rgalcen < 5.5 kpc.  
  > "$v_\text{LSR}^\text{HVC}$ is the mean velocity of the cloud from the `v_cen` parameter in `astrodendro` output" (Method for Detecting HVCs); "of molecular clouds located at 0.4 kpc $<R_\text{galcen}<5.5$ kpc by subtracting the disk velocity field from the center velocity of the clouds." (Method for Detecting HVCs); "we define HVCs as clouds with absolute velocity deviations $|\Delta v|$ of $50\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ or more from the disk velocity field." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: Exclude Rgalcen < 0.4 kpc and Rgalcen > 5.5 kpc  
  > "We therefore exclude molecular clouds at $R_\text{galcen}<0.4$ kpc from the following analysis" (Method for Detecting HVCs); "We also excluded identified clouds whose distance from the galactic center $R_\text{galcen}$ exceeds 5.5 kpc as the noise level increases around the border of FoV." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - assumption: Selected HVCs trace motion perpendicular to the plane rather than in-disk non-circular motion.  
  > "In this study, we focus on HVCs moving perpendicular to the galactic plane and exclude non-circular motions within the galactic disk." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Most clouds have |Δv| < 40 km s−1; the 50 km s−1 threshold selects kinematically peculiar objects.  
  > "The figure clearly shows that most clouds have velocity deviations of $|\Delta v| < 40$ km s$^{-1}$." (Properties of HVCs); "Therefore, the definition of HVCs as those with $|\Delta v| > 50$ km s$^{-1}$ effectively identifies kinematically peculiar objects." (Properties of HVCs)
4. **Derive cloud masses, sizes and binding indicators**: Mmol = αCO LCO; r = 1.91√(σmaj σmin); σv from v_rms; Mvir = 5rσv²/G; αvir = Mvir/Mmol.  
  > "M_{\text{mol}} &=\alpha_{\text{CO}}L_{\rm CO}" (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs); "($r=1.91\times\sqrt{\sigma_{maj}\times\sigma_{min}}$)" (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs); "`Astrodendro` also outputs the intensity-weighted second moment of velocity `v_rms` ($\sigma_v$ in Table [\[table\]](#table))." (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: Mvir = 5rσv²/G; αvir = Mvir/Mmol  
  > "M_{\rm vir} = \frac{5r\sigma_v^2}{G}." (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs); "\alpha_{\rm vir} = \frac{M_{\rm vir}}{M_{\rm mol}}." (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs)
   - assumption: Galactic αCO = 4.3 M⊙ (K km s−1 pc−2)−1; molecular mass includes helium and heavier elements  
  > "To compute the molecular gas mass (including helium and heavier elements)" (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs); "we adopted the Galactic CO-to-$\text{H}_2$ conversion factor $\alpha_{\text{CO}} = 4.3 \,M_\odot\,(\mathrm{K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{-2}})^{-1}$ (cite: Bolatto2013)" (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs)
   - assumption: M83 distance: 4.5 Mpc  
  > "M83 is a barred spiral galaxy located at a distance of 4.5 Mpc (cite: Thim2003)." (M83)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cloud radii and masses largely agree with Hirota2024; smaller clouds are missed and dispersions tend to be larger.  
  > "The radii and masses of the clouds identified in this study are largely consistent with those cataloged by (cite: Hirota2024)" (Properties of HVCs); "except that smaller clouds in terms of radius and mass are not detected." (Properties of HVCs); "Additionally, compared to the catalog of (cite: Hirota2024), the velocity dispersions tend to be larger, likely due to the difference in velocity resolution between the datasets." (Properties of HVCs)
5. **Estimate statistical and systematic uncertainties**: Bootstrap cloud properties with dendroplot; assess finite-resolution broadening and αCO systematics.  
  > "We estimated the statistical uncertainties of cloud properties ($r$, $\sigma_v$, $M_{\rm mol}$, $M_{\rm vir}$) using bootstrap resampling (cite: Rosolowsky2006)." (Uncertainties); "We performed 1000 bootstrap iterations with the Python package `dendroplot` (cite: dendroplot; Wong2017)" (Uncertainties); "systematic uncertainties such as the effects of finite resolution and the choice of the $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ conversion factor." (Uncertainties)
   - software: dendroplot
   - parameter: 1000 bootstrap iterations; ±1σ range centered on the bootstrap median  
  > "We performed 1000 bootstrap iterations with the Python package `dendroplot` (cite: dendroplot; Wong2017) and adopted the $\pm 1\sigma$ range centered on the median of the bootstrap resamplings as the measurement uncertainty." (Uncertainties)
   - assumption: σch = δv/√12; σv = √(σv,int² + σch²)  
  > "Here we adopt $\sigma_{\mathrm{ch}}$ = $\frac{\delta v}{\sqrt{12}}$ following (cite: Hirota2024)." (Uncertainties); "\sigma_v = \sqrt{\sigma_{v, \mathrm{int}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{ch}}^2}," (Uncertainties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Estimated spectral broadening ~1.5% for the HVC median versus ~8% for disk clouds; dispersion contrast remains robust.  
  > "When the observed dispersion is 8.5 km s$^{-1}$ (the median value for HVCs), the broadening contributes only $\sim 1.5\%$ to the observed value." (Uncertainties); "In contrast, for a dispersion of 3.8 km s$^{-1}$ (the median for disk clouds), the observed value can be overestimated by as much as $\sim 8\%$." (Uncertainties); "the conclusion that HVCs have systematically larger velocity dispersions than disk clouds remains robust." (Uncertainties)
6. **Inspect spatial and spectral separation**: Make integrated-intensity maps, average spectra over each identified HVC region and extract major-axis position–velocity diagrams.  
  > "The central position of each HVC was set to the center of the map, and the velocity channels were integrated over a width of each $4\sigma_v$, centered at the `v_cen`." (Properties of HVCs); "The blue lines represent spectra averaged over each HVC’s identified region." (Properties of HVCs); "shows the position–velocity diagrams of the HVCs, extracted along their major axes." (Position-Velocity Diagrams of HVCs)
   - parameter: Integration width 4σv, centered at v_cen  
  > "the velocity channels were integrated over a width of each $4\sigma_v$, centered at the `v_cen`." (Properties of HVCs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spectra show separate disk and HVC velocity components.  
  > "The spectra within these HVCs confirm a double-peak structure in the line-of-sight velocity, consisting of both the disk velocity field and the HVC component." (Properties of HVCs)
7. **Compare HVC and disk-cloud scaling relations**: Compare r–σv, Mmol–Mvir, Mmol–σv and r–Mmol; calculate medians and surface densities.  
  > "We compared HVCs with disk molecular clouds in M83 based on the relationship between radius and velocity dispersion $\sigma_{v}$ (cite: Larson1969)" (Scaling Relation); "We next plotted the relationship between the CO luminosity based gas mass and the virial mass" (Scaling Relation); "shows (a) the $M_{\rm mol}$–$\sigma_v$ and (b) $r$–$M_{\rm mol}$ relations, respectively." (Other scaling relations)
   - parameter: Exclude unresolved dispersions below approximately half the channel width from radius-binned medians  
  > "When the channel width of the velocity is taken as the FWHM, velocity dispersions smaller than approximately half of the channel width are unresolved." (Scaling Relation); "These data points, marked with crosses, were excluded from the median calculation." (Scaling Relation)
   - parameter: Compare virial parameters with disk clouds of order 10^5 M⊙  
  > "disk clouds with masses comparable to those of the HVCs (i.e., on the order of $10^5\,M_\odot$)" (Scaling Relation)
   - parameter: Surface density Σ = Mmol/(πr²)  
  > "using $\Sigma = M_{\rm mol}/(\pi r^2)$" (Other scaling relations)
   - assumption: Virial parameter indicates gravitational binding through the kinetic-to-gravitational energy ratio.  
  > "The virial parameter is a measure of the ratio of kinetic to gravitational energy, and serves as an indicator of whether a cloud is gravitationally bound." (Calculation of the Properties of HVCs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mmol–σv comparison also shows elevated HVC dispersions.  
  > "From the $M_{\rm mol}$–$\sigma_v$ relation, we confirm—similar to the $r$–$\sigma_v$ relation" (Other scaling relations); "that the HVCs tend to have higher velocity dispersions than the disk clouds." (Other scaling relations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Nearly face-on M83 and high-sensitivity CO(1–0) mapping covering nearly the entire disk enable a complementary HVC search.  
  > "With an inclination angle of $26^{\circ}$ (cite: Koda2023), it is nearly face-on, making it highly suitable for this study." (M83); "we use high-sensitivity CO(1–0) mapping data from (cite: Koda2023), which cover nearly the entire disk of M83" (M83); "This provides a complementary view to the previous H$\;$i and CO(2–1) studies." (M83)
- Why this method: Use 5 km s−1 channels to avoid splitting broad HVCs; smooth to the arm thickness to remove local flows.  
  > "This is because the catalog based on the 1 km s$^{-1}$ resolution data cube was found to artificially split the HVCs with a wide velocity dispersions into multiple components." (Method for Detecting HVCs); "The smoothing scale of 400 pc was chosen as it approximately matches the arm thickness, helping to remove local flows associated with the arm." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
- Alternative: 1 km s−1 cloud catalog rejected because it artificially splits broad HVCs.  
  > "This is because the catalog based on the 1 km s$^{-1}$ resolution data cube was found to artificially split the HVCs with a wide velocity dispersions into multiple components." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
- Alternative: Bar-driven explanation rejected for HVC 1 because its blueshift opposes the local redshifted bar streaming.  
  > "according to (cite: Hirota2014), the streaming motion of the bar at this location acts toward the redshifted direction, while HVC 1 shows a blueshift relative to the disk velocity field." (Properties of HVCs); "We thus regard that HVC 1 is not bar-driven." (Properties of HVCs)
- Alternative: Single supernova origin rejected; multiple-supernova fountains and superbubbles considered energetically possible.  
  > "we claim that single supernova explosions are insufficient to explain their origin." (Discussion); "Note that galactic fountains and superbubbles, driven by multiple supernovae, can in principle reach such energies" (Discussion)
- Alternative: Smaller inner-disk αCO would increase inferred virial parameters; low-metallicity external gas could instead increase true masses.  
  > "If we adopt smaller $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ in the inner 3 kpc disk, most HVCs would shift toward the left" (Uncertainties); "the virial parameter would increase accordingly." (Uncertainties); "when the HVCs originated from outside the galaxy and have significantly lower metallicities compared to disk clouds, their true gas masses could be underestimated" (Uncertainties)
- How they decided it was enough: The 50 km s−1 threshold separates HVCs from most clouds, which have |Δv| < 40 km s−1.  
  > "The figure clearly shows that most clouds have velocity deviations of $|\Delta v| < 40$ km s$^{-1}$." (Properties of HVCs); "Therefore, the definition of HVCs as those with $|\Delta v| > 50$ km s$^{-1}$ effectively identifies kinematically peculiar objects." (Properties of HVCs)
- Limitation (authors): The smoothed disk field is unreliable in the central region; the FoV border has increased noise.  
  > "the velocity field from the 400-pc-smoothed data cube does not represent true disk velocities in this region." (Method for Detecting HVCs); "We also excluded identified clouds whose distance from the galactic center $R_\text{galcen}$ exceeds 5.5 kpc as the noise level increases around the border of FoV." (Method for Detecting HVCs)
- Limitation (authors): Finite spectral resolution broadens measured dispersions; finite spatial resolution broadens radii.  
  > "The finite spectral resolution of the data leads to artificial broadening of the observed line widths." (Uncertainties); "Similarly, the cloud radius may also be affected by the artificial broadening due to the finite spatial resolution." (Uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Uniform αCO may overestimate inner-disk HVC masses or underestimate low-metallicity external-cloud masses.  
  > "Since most of the HVCs are located within the central 3 kpc, their masses may be overestimated when the uniform $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ is assumed." (Uncertainties); "when the HVCs originated from outside the galaxy and have significantly lower metallicities compared to disk clouds, their true gas masses could be underestimated" (Uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Kinetic energies use uncorrected line-of-sight velocities and are treated as lower limits.  
  > "the projection effect of their velocity vectors

has not been taken into account." (Discussion); "As we assume that the contribution from non-circular motion to the $\Delta v$ is negligible, the derived kinetic energy values should be considered as lower limits." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Specific inflow origins cannot be determined; higher-resolution, higher-sensitivity H i data would help.  
  > "we cannot determine specifically where our HVCs are inflowing from." (Discussion); "To further constrain where the HVCs are inflowing from, H$\;$i data with high spatial resolution and sensitivity will be helpful." (Summary and Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inflow is an indirect interpretation; velocity-sign asymmetry alone does not establish three-dimensional trajectories.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Selection sensitivity to the 400 pc smoothing scale and 50 km s−1 threshold is not quantified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ten HVCs provide a small sample; no formal asymmetry or population-comparison significance test is specified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SNR non-associations lack an explicit matching tolerance and catalog-completeness analysis.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The table gives HVC 9 a dispersion of 22.9 km s−1, beyond the rounded 3–20 km s−1 summary range.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stated virial-mass equation and tabulated virial masses should be checked for consistent normalization before using them as reproduction acceptance criteria.
