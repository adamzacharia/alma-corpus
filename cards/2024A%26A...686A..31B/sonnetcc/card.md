## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract line moment maps and integrated intensities from ALCHEMI cubes**: CubeLineMoment masks cubes using a brighter main-isotopologue line of the same transition as velocity template and outputs moment 0, 1, 2 maps  
  > "`CubeLineMoment` works by extracting integrated intensities for a given list of targeted spectral frequencies by applying a set of spectral and spatial masks (defined by the user)." (Spectral-Line Extraction)
   - software: CubeLineMoment
   - parameter: Moment maps masked below a chosen sigma threshold (channel-based); value not given  
  > "masked below a chosen $\sigma$ threshold (channel-based)" (Spectral-Line Extraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Define six SSC* apertures and measure line intensities**: Six beam-sized apertures covering all Leroy et al. 2018 SSCs with no overlap; intensities extracted over a beam-sized aperture for all available J transitions; converted from Jy/beam to K km/s  
  > "We have selected six SSC regions using the aperture size of the beam of the ALCHEMI observations." (Region Selection); "the conversion to brightness temperature units from Jy/beam was accomplished using the" (Region Selection)
   - software: astropy
   - parameter: Regions where absorption (TH2) affects weak lines: no HC15N transitions used for SSC-9* or SSC-13*  
  > "no transitions of HC$^{15}$N were used for either SSC-9\* or SSC-13\*" (Region Selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **LTE column densities to set priors for non-LTE modelling**: Upper-state column density from integrated intensity with beam filling factor and optical-depth correction, converted to total N with the partition function over a grid of temperature (up to 500 K) and optical depth  
  > "These total column densities were estimated over a range of temperatures and optical depth." (LTE Analysis); "with the upper limit being 500 K" (LTE Analysis)
   - software: CDMS (partition functions)
   - parameter: Optical depth 0 to 10 in steps of 0.1 (tau<1) and 0.5 (tau>1)  
  > "We used a range of values for optical depth ($\tau$) from 0 to 10 in increments of 0.1 in the optically thin regime, $\tau < 1$ and 0.5 in the optically thick regime $\tau > 1$." (LTE Analysis)
   - assumption: Source size for the beam filling factor taken as the size of the regions in Leroy et al. 2018  
  > "For the purposes of the LTE estimation, $\theta_{S}$ was defined as the size of the regions observed in (cite: 2018Leroy)." (LTE Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rotational diagrams used to constrain temperature ranges for the non-LTE priors  
  > "This analysis drove the initial temperature ranges for each species seen in the non-LTE analysis" (LTE Analysis)
4. **Non-LTE RADEX modelling with nested sampling to get column densities per isotopologue group**: Each isotopologue group (CO, HCN, HCO+ families) modelled together with RADEX, fitting density, kinetic temperature, column densities and beam filling factor; posteriors from MLFriends nested sampling  
  > "The posterior probability distributions are derived with the nested sampling Monte Carlo algorithm MLFriends" (Non-LTE Radex Analysis); "The beam filling factor has been allowed to be a free parameter between 0 and 1" (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
   - software: RADEX, SpectralRadex, UltraNest
   - parameter: Uniform or log-uniform priors; column-density priors based on LTE N_u results (ranges in Tables 3-5)  
  > "we assume prior distributions of our parameters to be uniform or log-uniform, where the column density priors are based upon the $N_u$ results obtained during the LTE analysis" (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
   - assumption: All fitted transitions in each group arise from the same gas component  
  > "under the assumption that all of the fitted transitions in each group arise from the same gas component." (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
   - assumption: HCO+ collisional data used for its isotopologues  
  > "Collisional data of HCO$^{+}$ thus were used for its respective isotopologues, recommended by the LAMDA database (cite: LAMDA_2020)" (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
   - fitting: Gaussian uncertainties on intensities; likelihood exp(-chi^2/2)  
  > "We assume that the uncertainty on our measured intensities is Gaussian" (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
   - validation: RADEX models for CO isotopologues with available data compared under the same parameters to justify using HCO+ collisional data for its isotopologues  
  > "models were computed under the same physical parameters of the different isotopologues of CO, whose data are available, and the results were then compared." (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
5. **Fit H39alpha line and derive ionizing photon rate**: Simultaneous Gaussian fits to H39alpha and He39alpha (one H component in SSC-1*, 4*, 7*, 14*; two in SSC-9* and 13*), Gaussian areas converted to Q0 via Emig et al. 2020 equations  
  > "In SSC-1\*, 4\*, 7\*, and 14\*, we fit one Gaussian component to hydrogen and one Gaussian component to helium." (SSC Age Determination); "By combining Equations A4 and A14 from (cite: Emig_2020), the total ionizing photon rate expressed in terms of the flux density of the recombination line is" (H39alpha Recombination Line Fitting)
   - parameter: Fit velocity range barycentric [-200, 750] km/s  
  > "we used the spectra over barycentric velocity ranges \[$-$200, 750\] km s$^{-1}$ to fit and measure the line emission" (Spectral-Line Extraction)
   - assumption: Te = 6000 K, b_(n+1) = 0.8  
  > "We assume a temperature of $T_e = 6000$ K and an LTE departure coefficient of $b_{\mathsf{n}+1} = 0.8$ (cite: Bendo_2015; 2021_Mills)." (H39alpha Recombination Line Fitting)
   - fitting: Gaussian profiles fit simultaneously to H39alpha, He39alpha (and C2H3CN in SSC-13*)  
  > "We simultaneously fit Gaussian profiles to H39$\alpha$ and He39$\alpha$ emission" (H39alpha Recombination Line Fitting)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Measure Br-gamma equivalent width in the SSC* apertures**: Br-gamma line emission measured over SSC* apertures from the Rosenberg et al. 2013 Br-gamma image and combined with K continuum  
  > "The equivalent width was obtained by using this line emission in combination with the K continuum." (SSC Age Determination)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive SSC* ages by fitting Starburst99 models**: Starburst99 models of EW(Br-gamma) and ionizing photon rate vs age fitted to observations; test of formation type, IMF slope and upper mass cutoff  
  > "To do this we fit the ionizing photon rates of H39$\alpha$ and the equivalent width of Br$\gamma$, as both are available outputs of S99." (SSC Age Determination)
   - software: Starburst99
   - parameter: Varied parameters: IMF power law, stellar mass cutoff, formation type; time range 1e4-1e9 yr  
  > "S99 allows for various input parameters, but the ones most prudent to this study are IMF power law, stellar mass cutoff and formation type." (SSC Age Determination); "`Starburst99` operates by populating a main sequence of stars under specified initial conditions and allowing the evolution of these populations to occur over a user-defined timescale (in this study, $10^4-10^9$ yrs)." (SSC Age Determination)
   - assumption: Cluster mass ~1e5 Msun from Leroy et al. 2018; Continuous models discarded  
  > "We found that, when setting appropriate masses to be achieved $10^5$ M$_\odot$ the Continuous models were not able to reproduce the observed values of Br$\gamma$ EW or ionizing photon rate over the entire time range and thus were discarded in favour of Instantaneous models." (SSC Age Determination)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Best-fit IMF and cutoff chosen as that reproducing both H39alpha and Br-gamma data  
  > "A top-heavy IMF power-law of 1.5 and a upper stellar mass cutoff of 150 M$_{\odot}$ with instantaneous star formation was shown to be the best fit to both the H39$\alpha$ and Br$\gamma$ data." (SSC Age Determination)
8. **Compare isotopic column-density ratios with SSC* age**: Qualitative assessment of ratio versus age plots; no correlation statistics given because of 4 to 6 regions  
  > "Given the low sample size of 4 to 6 regions (depending on the ratio) quantified statistics of any possible correlation between age and column density ratio are not given." (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?); "Instead a qualitative assessment of each ratio vs age plot has been completed." (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?)
   - assumption: SSC-9* and SSC-13* excluded from some ratios because of absorption near TH2  
  > "once we disregard the absorption affected regions of SSC-9\* and SSC-13\*" (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of ratios to Martin et al. 2019, 2021, Meier et al. 2015 and fractionation models of Viti et al.  
  > "Taking the most suitable model from this study, representing starburst galaxies (see Figure 10 in (cite: 2020Viti))" (Isotopic Ratios and ISM Fractionation)

## Qualitative findings

- No significant age trend for CO and HCN 12C/13C ratios; HCO+/H13CO+ shows a tentative negative correlation with age if SSC-13* is disregarded  
  > "HCO$^{+}$ may show a correlation with age over these timescales, in $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C." (abstract); "This ratio does indeed show a negative correlation with age, with the exception of SSC\*-13." (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?)
- Temperature is largely unconstrained by the lines observed  
  > "The kinetic temperature, $T_{\rm K}$, however, remains largely unconstrained regardless of the region or isotopologue group." (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
- HCO+ column densities exceed HCN, consistent with HCO+ being more abundant in high star formation regions  
  > "HCO$^{+}$ and its isotopologues have larger column densities than their HCN counterparts." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA resolves compact SSCs in nearby starbursts, with many CNO isotopologue transitions available  
  > "Thanks to the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA), it is possible to observe compact energetic sources, such as SSCs, on a resolvable scale in the starburst regions within nearby galaxies." (Introduction)
- Why this method: RADEX column densities include optical depth effects so are considered more reliable than line intensity ratios  
  > "`RADEX` takes into account the optical depth effects, as such they give typically a more reliable result than individual line intensity ratios." (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?)
- Alternative: Continuous star formation Starburst99 models discarded because they could not reproduce Br-gamma EW or ionizing photon rate  
  > "the Continuous models were not able to reproduce the observed values of Br$\gamma$ EW or ionizing photon rate over the entire time range and thus were discarded in favour of Instantaneous models." (SSC Age Determination)
- Alternative: HC15N not used for SSC-9* and SSC-13* due to absorption near TH2  
  > "no transitions of HC$^{15}$N were used for either SSC-9\* or SSC-13\*" (Region Selection)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Kinetic temperature largely unconstrained; multiple temperature components likely  
  > "The kinetic temperature, $T_{\rm K}$, however, remains largely unconstrained regardless of the region or isotopologue group." (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample (4 to 6 regions) so no quantitative correlation statistics  
  > "Given the low sample size of 4 to 6 regions (depending on the ratio) quantified statistics of any possible correlation between age and column density ratio are not given." (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?)
- Limitation (authors): Conclusions limited to the narrow age range of these SSCs  
  > "Though it must be noted that this is only the age range covered by these SSCs." (Is There a Correlation Between SSC Age and Isotopic Ratio?)
- Limitation (authors): RADEX struggles to converge at the highest HCO+ column densities (SSC-14*)  
  > "these high column densities approach the parameter space under which `RADEX` struggles to converge as a result of line saturation at high opacities" (Non-LTE Radex Analysis)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ages rest on a coarse Starburst99 grid (3 IMF slopes, 3 cutoffs) with fit method and age uncertainties not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 1.6 arcsec beam (~28 pc) blends 1 to 4 SSCs, so regions are not individual clusters
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-component RADEX treatment of gas that is likely multi-temperature
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ratio uncertainties are not propagated into the age comparison and no correlation statistic is given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absorption at TH2 removes SSC-9* and SSC-13* from several ratios, leaving 4 points
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Collisional data of HCO+ reused for its isotopologues
