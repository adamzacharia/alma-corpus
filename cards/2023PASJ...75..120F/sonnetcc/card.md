## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JCMT POL-2 data and form polarization maps**: Starlink data reduction with the Aug 2019 instrumental polarization model; Stokes I, Q, U used to derive P and ANG  
  > "Instrumental polarization was calibrated by using the model released on August 3, 2019." (2.1 Polarimetry with SCUBA-2/POL-2 at JCMT); "was used for data reduction." (2.1 Polarimetry with SCUBA-2/POL-2 at JCMT)
   - software: Starlink
   - parameter: Flux conversion factors 516 (850 um) and 531 (450 um) Jy/pW/beam  
  > "Flux Conversion Factor values were 516 Jy pW$^{-1}$ beam$^{-1}$ and 531 Jy pW$^{-1}$ beam$^{-1}$ at $\lambda$= 850 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and 450 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$, respectively." (2.1 Polarimetry with SCUBA-2/POL-2 at JCMT)
   - parameter: Data kept only for satisfactory weather (tau225 between 0.03 and 0.07)  
  > "Data were selected only when the weather conditions were satisfactory for submillimeter polarimetry" (2.1 Polarimetry with SCUBA-2/POL-2 at JCMT)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Reduce and image ALMA Band 3 data, search for polarization**: CASA reduction; Stokes I continuum image made; no significant polarization detected  
  > "Data were reduced using the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA) package" (2.2 ALMA polarimetry); "Significant polarization was not detected in this dataset." (3.2 Millimeter dust continuum polarimetry)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Select magnetic field segments**: B segments from polarization vectors rotated by 90 deg, selected by S/N criteria, plotted in independent 12 arcsec pixels  
  > "Black lines represent the magnetic field directions derived from polarization vectors rotated by 90$^\circ$." (3.1 Submillimeter dust continuum polarimetry); "Note that the B segments are plotted in 12$^{\prime\prime}$ pixels in order to ensure that every data point is independent" (3.1 Submillimeter dust continuum polarimetry)
   - parameter: PI/DPI > 3 and I/DI > 10  
  > "These B segments were selected according to the following criteria; signal-to-noise ratio ($S/N$) in polarized intensity ($PI$/$DPI$) \> 3 and $S/N$ in intensity ($I$/$DI$) \> 10." (3.1 Submillimeter dust continuum polarimetry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 850 um segments connect smoothly to the Planck global field direction  
  > "which smoothly connects to the global magnetic field direction observed with Planck at $\lambda$= 850 $\mu$m." (3.1 Submillimeter dust continuum polarimetry)
4. **Measure multi-band flux densities by aperture photometry**: Single 6 arcsec aperture photometry with background subtraction on JCMT, Herschel, IRAM and ALMA images  
  > "of the IRAF package in order to subtract background emissions" (2.3 Data selection for SED analysis); "To derive the respective fluxes at each band, a single aperture of 6$^{\prime\prime}$ was applied." (2.3 Data selection for SED analysis)
   - software: IRAF apphot
   - parameter: Aperture chosen to limit contribution from the reflection nebula; 3.3 mm flux excluded from the SED because of missing flux  
  > "were not used because this is the only data taken with an interferometer and it suffers significantly from a missing flux issue" (2.3 Data selection for SED analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate dust masses of the core at 850 and 450 um**: Optically thin dust mass M = F d^2 / (kappa B(T_dust))  
  > "To estimate dense core masses traced at $\lambda$= 850 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and 450 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$, the following equation was used:" (4.1 Mass estimated from dust continuum)
   - parameter: d = 140 pc; kappa = 1.89 (850 um) and 6.14 (450 um) cm^2/g  
  > "is distance (140 pc)" (4.1 Mass estimated from dust continuum); "the corresponding dust opacities obtained by the thin ice mantle model at these wavelengths adopted as 1.89 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ and 6.14 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$, respectively" (4.1 Mass estimated from dust continuum)
   - assumption: T_dust = 10 K, gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "Assuming typical values of $T_{\rm dust}$ = 10 K and a gas-to-dust ratio of 100" (4.1 Mass estimated from dust continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit the core SED with YSO models**: Robitaille (2017) model grid fitted with sedfitter; best match s-ubhmi (central star, Ulrich-like envelope, bipolar cavity)  
  > "package was used to assess the Spectral Energy Distribution (hereafter SED) of the L1521 F core." (2.3 Data selection for SED analysis); "i.e., an SED model with a YSO was found to have the best match for L1521 F" (4.2 SED analysis)
   - software: sedfitter, Astropy
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model inclination of the outflow cavity compared with the observed near edge-on cavity  
  > "the estimated inclination of the bipolar outflow cavity of 88.5$^\circ$ (nearly edge-on) matches with the observation" (4.2 SED analysis)
7. **Estimate plane-of-sky magnetic field strength with the DCF method**: Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi method with Q = 0.5, densities from dust data, N2H+ velocity dispersion and polarization angle dispersion  
  > "Magnetic field strengths in L1521 F were estimated using the Davis$-$Chandrasekhar$-$Fermi method" (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio); "Based on the results by Ostriker et al. (2001), we used $Q$ value of 0.5." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - parameter: Gas densities (1.14 +/- 0.08)e-18 and (4.5 +/- 0.8)e-18 g/cm^3  
  > "gas densities are estimated as (1.14 $\pm$ 0.08) $\times$ 10$^{-18}$ g cm$^{-3}$ and (4.5 $\pm$ 0.8) $\times$ 10$^{-18}$ g cm$^{-3}$, respectively" (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - parameter: Velocity dispersion (0.12 +/- 0.01)e5 cm/s from N2H+ J=1-0  
  > "The velocity dispersion is derived to be (0.12 $\pm$ 0.01) $\times$ $10^{5}$ cm s$^{-1}$" (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - parameter: Polarization angle dispersion 20 +/- 1 deg (850 um) and 11 +/- 9 deg (450 um)  
  > "It is found to be $20 \pm 1$ $^\circ$ and $11 \pm 9$ $^\circ$ measured at $\lambda$= 850 $\mu$m and 450 $\mu$m, respectively." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note the 450 um estimate rests on few segments  
  > "Please note that the field strength derived at $\lambda$= 450 $\mu$m is based on the limited number of detected segments." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
8. **Derive the mass-to-flux ratio relative to critical**: mu_c = (M/Phi)obs/(M/Phi)crit from column density and B_pos  
  > "Mass-to-flux ratios, $\mu_{\rm c}$, are derived by using the estimated magnetic field strengths, $B_{\rm pos}$:" (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - parameter: Column densities (2.82 +/- 0.17)e22 and (7.29 +/- 1.3)e22 cm^-2  
  > "Column densities derived from the observations are (2.82 $\pm$ 0.17) $\times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$ for $\lambda$= 850 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and (7.29 $\pm$ 1.3) $\times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$ for $\lambda$= 450 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - assumption: Critical value (M/Phi)crit = 1/(2 pi G^1/2) for a magnetically critical cloud  
  > "represents the value for magnetically critical cloud (Nakano and Nakamura 1978)" (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Compare the observed field pattern with resistive MHD simulations**: Synthetic polarization from the 3D MHD model by integrating density and field; best model T85 (initial misalignment angle 85 deg) chosen among 9 models  
  > "Calculating relative Stokes parameter by integrating density and magnetic field, we obtain the expected polarization pattern from the 3D MHD simulation" (4.5 Gravitational infall scenario); "we found that a model with an initial misalignment angle $\theta_0 =85^{\circ}$" (4.5 Gravitational infall scenario)
   - parameter: Observer viewing angle theta = 80 deg, phi = 30 deg  
  > "one looks at the object with a polar angle from the $z$-axis of $\theta$ = 80$^{\circ}$ and the azimuth angle from $x$-axis of $\phi$ = 30$^{\circ}$" (4.5 Gravitational infall scenario)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Field direction changes sharply within and outside the central N2H+ clump, where a gap in velocity gradient is seen  
  > "Note that magnetic field directions change drastically within and outside the central N$_2$H$^+$ clump, where a significant gap in velocity gradient is detected." (4.3 Morphology of the magnetic field within the dense core)
- Central N2H+ clump and 450 um dust likely correspond to a pseudo-disk  
  > "The central clump traced with N$_2$H$^+$ emissions and using the $\lambda$= 450 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ dust continuum most likely corresponds to a pseudo-disk as shown in the simulation." (Conclusion)
- Minor condensations MMS-2 and MMS-3 are not expected to twist the field  
  > "Considering their masses and distances from the central protostar, MMS-1, both MMS-2 and MMS-3 cannot significantly contribute to the twist of magnetic field lines." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Dual-band polarimetry traces the cold peripheral region at 850 um and the central warm region at 450 um  
  > "Dual band polarimetry enables magnetic field morphology to be traced in the peripheral cold region of the core at" (2.1 Polarimetry with SCUBA-2/POL-2 at JCMT)
- Why this method: Daisy scans chosen as suitable for compact sources  
  > "Daisy scans, which are suitable for observing compact sources, were utilized to take the data." (2.1 Polarimetry with SCUBA-2/POL-2 at JCMT)
- Alternative: ALMA non-detection attributed either to insufficient sensitivity or to complex field structure in the 3.3 mm region (not discriminated)  
  > "The reason for which no significant polarization detected could either arise from a lack of sensitivity of the observation or from complex magnetic field structures in the region traced by the" (3.2 Millimeter dust continuum polarimetry)
- Alternative: MMS-2 and MMS-3 rejected as significant contributors to the field twist  
  > "Considering their masses and distances from the central protostar, MMS-1, both MMS-2 and MMS-3 cannot significantly contribute to the twist of magnetic field lines." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 450 um field strength rests on a limited number of detected segments  
  > "Please note that the field strength derived at $\lambda$= 450 $\mu$m is based on the limited number of detected segments." (4.4 Magnetic field strength and mass-to-flux ratio)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA 3.3 mm flux suffers from missing flux and was excluded from the SED  
  > "it suffers significantly from a missing flux issue" (2.3 Data selection for SED analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Beam sizes differ between bands in the SED photometry  
  > "Note that the beam sizes of the data at each band vary, as shown in Table 1." (2.3 Data selection for SED analysis)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two 450 um segments are tabulated, so the 200 uG value and the 11 +/- 9 deg dispersion are weakly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single T_dust = 10 K is applied to both bands, although the 450 um region is called warm; this biases mass, density and mu_c
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Same N2H+ velocity dispersion is used for both regions, with a beam and extent unrelated to the JCMT segments
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison with MHD models is qualitative: one viewing angle, 9 models, no goodness-of-fit, non-uniqueness not tested
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA non-detection gives no upper limit on polarization fraction, so it cannot constrain the models
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DCF Q=0.5 and the angle-dispersion method have systematic uncertainties not propagated into the quoted errors
