   - parameter: Dust: n(a)∼a−3.5, 0.8 μm–1 cm; material density 3.3 g cm−3  
  > "Each superparticle was then assigned a representative mass (cite: Baruteau2019) assuming a dust number density profile of $n(a) \!\sim\! a^{-3.5}$ over the size range $a_{\min} \!=\! 0.8\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ to $a_{\max} \!=\! 1\,\mathrm{cm}$." (Numerical setup); "For the material density of the particles, we used $\rho_{\rm mat}\!=\!3.3\,{\rm g\,cm}^{-3}$, which represents silicate grains." (Numerical setup)
   - assumption: Locally isothermal gas; T∼r−0.5; α-viscosity  
  > "We assumed a locally isothermal equation of state and adopted a gas temperature profile of $T\sim r^{-0.5}$, a standard choice for debris discs (cite: Kral2017)." (Disc setup); "Here, we employed the $\alpha$-viscosity model (cite: Shakura1973) for the kinematic viscosity" (Disc setup)
   - assumption: Fiducial T0=38 K, h=0.07, μ=2.3  
  > "| Temperature at $r_0$ | $T_0$ | 38 K |" (Table 1); "| Aspect Ratio | $h$ | 0.07 |" (Table 1); "| Mean-molecular weight | $\mu$ | 2.3 |" (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ring location matched observations; simulated width compared with CO-model constraint  
  > "The ring is centred at $r_0 \!=\! 75\,\mathrm{au}$, matching the location observed in HD 121617 (cite: overview_arks; gas_arks)." 2. **Evolve gas and size-dependent dust dynamics**: Eulerian gas and Lagrangian dust with gas drag, dust feedback, stochastic diffusion and size-dependent radiation pressure  
  > "In this framework, the gas is defined as an Eulerian fluid on a fixed grid, while the dust is represented by Lagrangian particles, i.e. discrete fluid elements that are tracked as they move through the flow field." (Hydrodynamical simulations); "Finally, because of the potentially high dust-to-gas ratio in debris discs, we include both the frictional effect of the gas onto the dust and the back-reaction of the dust onto the gas dynamics." (Hydrodynamical simulations); "In addition to frictional interactions with the gas and the central star’s gravitational force, we implemented the effect of radiation pressure on the dust dynamics." (Gas drag and radiation pressure)
   - software: Dusty FARGO-ADSG
   - parameter: Gas grid: 0.1r0–3r0, 512 logarithmic radial cells; −π–π, 1024 azimuthal cells  
  > "The grid on which the gas density was evaluated extended from $0.1\,r_0$ to $3\,r_0$ with $N_r\!=\!512$ logarithmically spaced grid cells." (Numerical setup); "In the two-dimensional case, the azimuthal angle, $\varphi$, spanned from $-\pi$ to $\pi$ on $N_\varphi\!=\!1024$ cells." (Numerical setup)
   - parameter: 10^5 superparticles; initial dust positions 0.9r0–1.1r0; 200 orbits with output every orbit  
  > "We model the dust population using $10^5$ superparticles." (Two-dimensional fiducial model); "For the initial setup, we positioned the particles inside the gas ring, between $0.9\,r_0$ and $1.1\,r_0$." (Numerical setup); "We ran the simulation for 200 orbits at $r_0$ and output density and velocity fields for every orbit." (Numerical setup)
   - parameter: α=10−4; β1μm=0.4; initial local Keplerian dust velocity  
  > "| Viscosity | $\alpha$ | $10^{-4}$ |" (Table 1); "| Radiation strength | $\beta_{1\mu{\rm m}}$ | 0.4 |" (Table 1); "For simplicity, we initialised the dust grains with their local Keplerian velocity." (Gas drag and radiation pressure)
   - parameter: Cloud-in-cell interpolation and stochastic dust diffusion  
  > "We used the implemented cloud-in-cell method for interpolation." (Gas drag and radiation pressure); "We further activated dust diffusion due to stochastic kicks between the dust particles and the gas (cite: Fuente2017; Charnoz2011), following (cite: Youdin2007)" (Gas drag and radiation pressure)
   - assumption: Neglect disc self-gravity, Poynting–Robertson drag and dust-particle collisions  
  > "We neglected the disc’s self-gravity and the Poynting–Robertson drag (cite: Burns1979), which operates on a much longer time scale than what is relevant in our simulations (cite: Burns1979)." (Gas drag and radiation pressure); "Our numerical treatment did not consider collisions between the dust particles themselves." (Numerical setup)
   - assumption: Optically thin stellar irradiation; radiation pressure represented by a size-dependent effective gravitational potential  
  > "If the disc is optically thin to stellar irradiation, we can approximate the stellar flux at a certain location independently of the dust distribution." (Gas drag and radiation pressure); "In practice, we modified the code in a way that each dust particle experiences this effective gravitational potential, which depends on the particle size via $\beta(a)=\beta_{1\mu {\rm m}}\times (\mu{\rm m}/a)$." (Gas drag and radiation pressure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Discrete-size drift tests matched analytical velocities; continuous-size test compared with 1000-bin semi-analytic solution at ten orbits  
  > "In all those tests, the analytical velocity profiles are well matched by the numerical solution." (Multi-sized grains); "Specifically, we discretised the continuous distribution into a large number of bins ($N_{\rm dust} \!=\! 1000$) and numerically solve Eqs. 70–71 of (cite: Benitez2019)." (Continuous size distribution); "We then compare these solutions with the hydrodynamical results from Dusty FARGO-ADSG at ten orbits." (Continuous size distribution)

  > "Here, we binned the particles around $2.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ (${\rm St}\sim0.01$), 1 mm (${\rm St}\sim3.5$) and $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ (${\rm St}\sim14$), respectively, summing those within $\pm 10\%$ of each central particle size." (Two-dimensional fiducial model)

(Radiative transfer and image synthesis); "We then used RADMC-3D to compute the dust temperature with the `mctherm` task, employing $n_{\rm phot} \!=\! 10^8$ photon packages." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis); "Next, we performed ray-tracing simulations of the thermal emission at $\lambda_{\rm obs} \!=\! 0.89\,\mathrm{mm}$ and of scattered stellar light at $\lambda_{\rm obs} \!=\! 1.25\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, using anisotropic scattering based on the scattering matrices calculated with `optool`." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis)
   - software: RADMC-3D, mctherm, optool
   - parameter: Spherical grid 512×30×1024; fifty logarithmic dust-size bins; 10^8 photon packages  
  > "Accordingly, we constructed a three-dimensional spherical grid with $N_{r}\times N_{\theta}\times N_{\varphi} = 512 \times 30 \times 1024$ cells." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis); "For the dust component, we separated the superparticles into fifty logarithmically spaced representative size bins and transformed the discrete superparticle distribution into a grid-based dust density via cloud-in-cell interpolation." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis); "We then used RADMC-3D to compute the dust temperature with the `mctherm` task, employing $n_{\rm phot} \!=\! 10^8$ photon packages." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis)
   - assumption: Gaussian vertical density with Hd=√[αD/(αD+St)] Hg; αD equal to viscous α  
  > "H_{\mathrm{d}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{D}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{D}} + \mathrm{St}}}\;H_{\mathrm{g}}" (Radiative transfer and image synthesis); "We assumed that the diffusion coefficient $\alpha_{\rm D}$ is equal to the viscous $\alpha$ parameter used in the simulations, which in our case served as a numerical convenience rather than a physically constrained quantity." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis); "\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(r,\varphi,z) \;=\; \frac{\Sigma_{\mathrm{d}}(r,\varphi)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,H_{\mathrm{d}}}\;\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{z^{2}}{2\,H_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}\right)" (Radiative transfer and image synthesis)
   - assumption: Compact pyroxene-silicate grains  
  > "We computed the opacities for each representative dust size using the `optool`[^4] package (cite: Dominik2021), assuming compact grains with a material composition of pyroxene silicates." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis)
   - assumption: Inclination 44.1°; position angle 58.7°  
  > "We assumed an inclination of 44.1$^\circ$ and a position angle of $58.7^\circ$, as reported in (cite: overview_arks)." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- An unstable gas ring produces size-dependent radial and azimuthal dust trapping  
  > "We find that an unstable gas ring can create a size-dependent radial and azimuthal dust trap." (abstract)
- Both 50 M⊕ and 5 M⊕ models reproduce the three key observables  
  > "They are reproduced by both our fiducial model with $M_{\rm gas}\!=\!50\,M_\oplus$ and the lower-mass model with $M_{\rm gas}\!=\!5\,M_\oplus$." (50 M⊕ versus 5 M⊕)
- At CO-only gas mass, dust feedback prevents vortex development  
  > "Here, we find that if we initiate the simulation above that value, the vortex does not even develop." (Mgas=MCO); "Even if we decrease the ring width considerably to $\sigma\!=\!H$, the instability remains suppressed by the dust." (Mgas=MCO)
- Higher-mass model predicts increasing arc contrast with wavelength and stationary azimuth; lower-mass model predicts non-monotonic contrast and azimuthal shifts  
  > "a monotonic increase of arc contrast with wavelength and a stationary arc azimuth favour the higher gas mass" (50 M⊕ versus 5 M⊕); "a non-monotonic contrast (peaking near band 6) together with a wavelength-dependent azimuthal shift point to the lower-mass scenario." (50 M⊕ versus 5 M⊕)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combined ALMA asymmetry, CO content, scattered-light symmetry and radial offset test dust–gas interactions  
  > "The combination of azimuthally confined continuum emission (cite: asym_arks; hd121617_arks), substantial CO gas content (cite: Moor2017; gas_arks), lack of an arc in the distribution of small grains, and the observed radial offset in scattered light (cite: scat_arks)" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Lagrangian dust permits multiple streams; optically thin dust requires radiation pressure; high dust-to-gas ratios require feedback  
  > "multiple streams of dust moving in multiple directions can be present at a single location, making it inappropriate to treat dust as a pressureless fluid with a single velocity at a given location (cite: Lynch2024)." (Hydrodynamical simulations); "Meanwhile, stellar irradiation exerts a significant radiation pressure on dust particles throughout the disc that can become relevant for small dust grains of a high surface-to-mass ratio, necessitating an explicit numerical treatment of this force, as done here." (Hydrodynamical simulations); "Finally, because of the potentially high dust-to-gas ratio in debris discs, we include both the frictional effect of the gas onto the dust and the back-reaction of the dust onto the gas dynamics." (Hydrodynamical simulations)
- Alternative: Outward-migrating interior planet could generate resonant clumps; remains an alternative  
  > "An alternative explanation is that the disc asymmetry arises from interactions with an unseen planet (Pearce et al. in prep.)." (Planet versus gas drag); "As the planet migrates, its mean-motion resonances sweep through the disc, capturing debris bodies in resonances and creating asymmetric clumps in their distribution." (Planet versus gas drag)
- Alternative: 250 M⊕ gas model fails to reproduce the scattered-light offset and sharpness  
  > "This model of increased gas density would fail to reproduce both the outward shift and the sharpness of the ring seen in scattered light." (The gas mass upper limit)
- Alternative: CO-only 0.15 M⊕ gas model suppresses instability through dust feedback  
  > "First, we probed the scenario where the reported CO mass of 0.15 $M_\oplus$ represents the total gas mass, $M_{\rm gas}\!=\!0.15\,M_\oplus$." (Mgas=MCO); "Even if we decrease the ring width considerably to $\sigma\!=\!H$, the instability remains suppressed by the dust." (Mgas=MCO)
- Alternative: 2.5 M⊕ low-gas case produces the wrong combination of mm and scattered-light asymmetries  
  > "Consequently, this model fails to reproduce the observed combination in HD 121617 of an asymmetric ALMA continuum arc and a symmetric SPHERE scattered-light image" (Mgas=MCO); "As argued in Sec. [4.3](#subsec:lowlimit), the observations are incompatible with the gas drag scenario for total gas masses of $M_{\rm gas}\!\lesssim\! 2.5\,M_\oplus$." (Hybrid rather than debris disc)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gas-mass bounds depend on viscosity, temperature, scale height, ring width, dust sizes and opacities  
  > "We note that the quoted gas mass bounds are model-dependent." (Conclusions); "They are expected to vary with assumptions about the turbulent viscosity, gas temperature and scale height, ring width, and the dust size distribution and opacities that set the relative contributions of trapped versus background grains." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Narrow ring imposed as an arbitrary initial condition  
  > "An obvious question is about the origin of the radially confined ring, which we imposed as an arbitrary initial condition, assuming that the ring is an inherited structure from the protoplanetary disc phase." (Caveats and prospects)
- Limitation (authors): Dust growth, fragmentation and planetesimal production omitted  
  > "Another important flaw is the omission of dust growth, fragmentation, and production by planetesmals (if present) in the numerical evolution of the ring." (Caveats and prospects)
- Limitation (authors): Vertical dust scale heights are a numerical convenience rather than physically constrained  
  > "We assumed that the diffusion coefficient $\alpha_{\rm D}$ is equal to the viscous $\alpha$ parameter used in the simulations, which in our case served as a numerical convenience rather than a physically constrained quantity." (Radiative transfer and image synthesis)
- Limitation (authors): Synthetic arc contrast is stronger than observed; synthetic scattered-light ring is too radially extended  
  > "This combination results in a slightly stronger contrast between the arc and the rest of the ring (see Sec. [5.2](#subsec:observe_predictions)) in the synthetic image compared to the real observation."   > "Similar to the azimuthal asymmetry in the gas, the azimuthal accumulation of dust grains is also time-dependent and both the shift and the contrast of the asymmetry depend on the snapshot at which the simulation is evaluated." (Two-dimensional fiducial model)
- Limitation (authors): Gas asymmetry dissipates without external forcing  
  > "However, the instability leads to a radial spreading of the dust ring and, without external forcing, the asymmetry dissipates with time." (Evolution of the asymmetry in the gas ring)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Qualitative morphology agreement does not establish a unique dynamical mechanism.
