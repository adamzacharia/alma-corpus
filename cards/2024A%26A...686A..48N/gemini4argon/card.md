## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract resolved and global spectra and generate moment maps**: Extract spectra from a synthesized-beam-sized aperture ('resolved') and a 10x10 beam elliptical aperture ('global') centered on each galaxy's kinematic center; integrate over visually determined velocity ranges to create moment 0 (no clipping) and moment 1 and 2 maps (3-sigma clipping).  
  > "we extracted spectra from two kinds of apertures: an aperture the size of the synthesized beam (hereafter “resolved”) and an elliptical aperture 10 × 10 times the size of the synthesized beam (hereafter “global”)." (4.1. Spectra); "No clipping was applied for the integrated intensity maps in order to salvage the faint emission, while we used a 3σ threshold clipping to properly derive the velocity field and the dispersion." (4.2. Moment maps)
   - software: astropy, spectral-cube, pvextractor
   - parameter: Resolved aperture = 1x1 beam; Global aperture = 10x10 beam; 3-sigma threshold clipping for moment 1 and 2; velocity integration ranges from ±150 to ±500 km s^-1 (Table 3)  
  > "an aperture the size of the synthesized beam (hereafter “resolved”) and an elliptical aperture 10 × 10 times the size of the synthesized beam (hereafter “global”)." (4.1. Spectra); "while we used a 3σ threshold clipping to properly derive the velocity field and the dispersion." (4.2. Moment maps)
   - assumption: No correction for line blending/overlapping; for IRAS 17578-0400, continuum levels of 0.02 Jy (resolved) and 0.07 Jy (global) assumed  
  > "We did not correct for potential line overlapping." (4.2. Moment maps); "continuum subtraction was done by assuming continuum levels of 0.02 Jy and 0.07 Jy for the resolved and global spectra, respectively, based on the lowest intensity channels (Fig. A.11)." (4.3. Line luminosities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested a larger 15'' aperture for spectral extraction to check if emission outside the global aperture affects L_IR-L'_mol trends  
  > "We also note that if we use a much larger aperture for spectral extraction, for instance 15 [′′] (corresponds to 2.5–20 kpc for our sample), to include the emission outside the global aperture (Sect. 4.2.1), the observed trend is not significantly changed." (4.4. Correlation with infrared luminosity)
2. **Calculate line luminosities and global/resolved HCN/HCO+ line ratios, and correlate with galaxy properties**: Compute L'_HCN and L'_HCO+ using Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005) formula; fit power-law relations with L_IR; compute L'_HCN/L'_HCO+ ratios and test correlations with alpha_AGN, L_AGN^bol, L_IR, SFR, S60/S100, and [N II]122/[O III]88 using Spearman rank correlation with Monte Carlo perturbation and bootstrapping.  
  > "These luminosities were calculated in units of K km s [−][1] pc [2] following the equation from Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005): L [′] = 3.25×10 [7] S ∆v ν obs [−][2] [D] L [2] [(1][+] z ) [−][3]" (4.3. Line luminosities); "We performed a Spearman rank correlation analysis for the six above-mentioned quantities with each of the global and resolved L HCN [′] [/] [L] HCO [′] [+] [using] pymccorrelation [6] [(Privon] [et] [al.] 2020)" (5.2. Comparison with galaxy properties)
   - software: pymccorrelation
   - parameter: Excluded non-detected galaxies (UGC 11763, UGC 2982, NGC 5734) from correlation analysis; 12 galaxies used for [N II]122/[O III]88  
  > "The galaxies in which either HCN or HCO [+] was not detected (i.e., UGC 11763, UGC 2982, and NGC 5734) were excluded in the analysis." (5.2. Comparison with galaxy properties); "We only considered 12 galaxies in which both the [N ii] 122 and [O iii] 88 lines are detected with good significance" (5.2. Comparison with galaxy properties)
   - assumption: L_bol = 1.15 L_IR; SFR = (1 - alpha_AGN) x 10^-10 L_IR (Chabrier IMF)  
  > "where L bol = 1.15 L IR is assumed; Veilleux et al. 2009)." (5.2. Comparison with galaxy properties); "via the relation SFR = (1 − α AGN ) × 10 [−][10] L IR on the same assumption as Sturm et al. (2011), that is, the SFR– L IR relation calibrated by Kennicutt (1998) but using a Chabrier initial mass function." (5.2. Comparison with galaxy properties)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo perturbation plus bootstrapping for Spearman rank correlation; linear regression in log-log space for L_IR vs L'_mol  
  > "We performed Spearman rank correlation analyses using the Monte Carlo perturbation plus bootstrapping method as implemented by pymccorrelation (Privon et al. 2020, see Sect. 5.2 for detail)." (Table 5); "The fitting results of the L mol [′] [–] [L] [IR] [relations] [are] [log] [ L] [IR] [=] (0.53 ± 0.03) log L HCN [′] [+] [(7][.][5] [±] [0][.][3)] [and] [log] [ L] [IR] [=] [(0][.][61] [±] 0.03) log L HCO [′] [+] [ +][(6][.][9][±][0][.][2)" (4.4. Correlation with infrared luminosity)
   - validation: Checked L_IR correlation after correcting for AGN contribution by (1 - alpha_AGN) L_IR  
  > "If the L IR is corrected for the AGN contribution by (1−α AGN ) L IR, the correlations are not largely changed ( r = 0.86 for both L HCN [′] and L [′] HCO [+] [)" (4.4. Correlation with infrared luminosity)
3. **Spatially and spectrally resolved (pixel-by-pixel and spaxel-by-spaxel) line ratio analysis in ppV space**: Compute L'_HCN/L'_HCO+ on a pixel-by-pixel basis (spectral integration then 3-sigma clipping) and spaxel-by-spaxel basis (3-sigma clipping on both cubes), extract statistical percentiles, and visualize spaxels exceeding the 90th percentile in 3D position-position-velocity (ppV) space.  
  > "For a pixel-by-pixel analysis, we initially performed spectral integration followed by applying a 3σ threshold clipping. In the spaxel-by-spaxel analysis, we first adopted a 3σ threshold clipping to both HCN and HCO [+] cubes" (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio); "In Figs. 5–8, we present visualization of spaxels with L HCN [′] [/] [L] HCO [′] [+] [exceeding the] 90th percentile in the position-position-velocity (ppV) space" (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio)
   - parameter: Pixel size = 0.05'' x 0.05''; spaxel size = 0.05'' x 0.05'' x 20 km s^-1; clipping = 3-sigma; structure threshold = 90th percentile  
  > "Each pixel and spaxel has dimensions of 0.05 [′′] ×0.05 [′′] and 0.05 [′′] ×0.05 [′′] ×20 km s [−][1], respectively, for all galaxies." (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio); "For simplicity, we used the 90th percentile as a threshold for all galaxies." (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visually compared 90th percentile threshold against 85th and 95th percentiles  
  > "Through visual inspection, we found the 90th percentile generally produces suitable results to extract characteristic structures, as compared to the other neighboring values such as the 85th and 95th percentile." (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio)
4. **Non-LTE radiative transfer modeling of excitation conditions**: Run one-zone non-LTE radiative transfer calculations using RADEX with uniform sphere LVG geometry to predict L'_HCN/L'_HCO+ as a function of H2 volume density across grids of T_kin, N_H2, X_HCN, and electron abundance X_e-.  
  > "Practically, we used a publicly available code, RADEX [7] (van der Tak et al. 2007), to predict the line intensity." (6.1. Excitation conditions); "We ran RADEX with all combinations of parameters described above and calculated L HCN [′] [/] [L] HCO [′] [+] [as] [a] [function] [of] [n] [H] [2] [.]" (6.1. Excitation conditions)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: T_bg = 2.7 K; delta_v = 50 km s^-1; N_H2 = 10^22, 10^23, 10^24 cm^-2; T_kin = 20, 50, 100 K; n_H2 = 10^1 to 10^9 cm^-3 (100 log-spaced values); X_HCO+ = 10^-8; X_HCN = 1e-8, 5e-8, 1e-7, 3e-7, 5e-7; X_e- = 0, 10^-5, 10^-4  
  > "In all of our calculations, the background temperature was fixed to be the temperature of the cosmic microwave background (i.e., 2.7 K)." (6.1. Excitation conditions); "For N H 2, we considered three plausible values for U/LIRGs: 10 [22], 10 [23], and 10 [24] cm [−][2] . We varied X HCN to cover a wide range of values: 1 × 10 [−][8], 5 × 10 [−][8], 1 × 10 [−][7], 3 × 10 [−][7], and 5 × 10 [−][7] ." (6.1. Excitation conditions); "The line width (∆v) was set to be 50 km s [−][1] . This value is somewhat smaller than the observed values, but we can consider the emission as arising from an ensemble of such clouds. We explored three values for the kinetic temperature ( T kin ): 20, 50, and 100 K." (6.1. Excitation conditions)
   - assumption: Uniform sphere geometry with LVG; HCO+ electron collisional rate coefficients for J>=3 scaled by dipole moment squared (mu^2) from HCN rates  
  > "we adopted the non-local thermal equilibrium (non-LTE) radiative transfer model, assuming uniform sphere geometry with a large velocity gradient (LVG, see e.g., Goldreich & Kwan 1974)." (6.1. Excitation conditions); "We provisionally employed the rate coefficients for J ≥3 levels generated by scaling with µ [2] based on the rate coefficients for HCN provided by Faure et al. (2007)." (6.1. Excitation conditions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested different reference abundance X_HCO+ = 10^-7 and found qualitatively similar trends  
  > "We also tested different reference abundances of HCO [+], such as X HCO [+] = 10 [−][7], and found qualitatively similar trends." (6.1. Excitation conditions)
5. **Time-dependent chemical modeling of C-shocks**: Run two-phase (pre-shock and post-shock) time-dependent gas-grain chemical models using the C-shock module of UCLCHEM to track gas temperature, density, and fractional abundances of HCN, HCO+, and electrons as a function of post-shock time.  
  > "we experimentally ran the time-dependent gas-grain chemical code, UCLCHEM [8] (Holdship et al. 2017), which is publicly available. We adopted the “C-shock” model of UCLCHEM" (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN); "In Fig. 10, the evolution of the gas temperature and density as well as the abundances of HCN, HCO [+], and the electrons are shown as a function of post-shock time." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN)
   - software: UCLCHEM
   - parameter: Pre-shock: n_H2 = 10^3 cm^-3, T = 10 K, A_V = 2 mag, radiation field = 1 Habing, cosmic-ray ionization rate = 1.3 x 10^-17 s^-1, duration = 10^5 yr; Post-shock: v_shock = 10, 20, 30, 40 km s^-1  
  > "all physical conditions were kept constant at standard Galactic values (H 2 density 10 [3] cm [−][3], temperature 10 K, visual extinction 2 mag, radiation field 1 Habing, cosmic ionization rate 1.3 × 10 [−][17] s [−][1]" (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN); "all physical conditions were kept constant at standard Galactic values (H 2 density 10 [3] cm [−][3], temperature 10 K, visual extinction 2 mag, radiation field 1 Habing, cosmic ionization rate 1.3 × 10 [−][17] s [−][1]" (Sect. 6.2 chemical model (UCLCHEM C-shock))
   - assumption: Standard Galactic elemental abundances; constant radiation field and cosmic-ray ionization rate during shock  
  > "As a pre-shock condition, we considered the typical condition of “standard” molecular gas with Galactic elemental abundances." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN); "Our model maintained constant values for the radiation field and the cosmic-ray ionization rate" (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN)

## Qualitative findings

- Both HCN and HCO+ J=3-2 lines were detected in 21 of 23 galaxies, only HCN in NGC 5734, and neither in UGC 2982.  
  > "We detected both HCN and HCO [+] in 21 galaxies, only HCN in one galaxy, and neither in one galaxy." (abstract); "For NGC 5734, HCN was clearly detected, but HCO [+] is totally absent (Fig. A.23). Neither HCN nor HCO [+] was detected in UGC 2982 (Fig. A.19)." (4.1. Spectra)
- Global and resolved HCN/HCO+ line ratios show no statistically significant correlation with AGN fraction, bolometric AGN luminosity, infrared luminosity, star formation rate, or dust temperature.  
  > "These line ratios appear to have no systematic trend with bolometric AGN luminosity or star formation rate." (abstract)
- In eight out of ten galaxies with known molecular outflows and/or inflows, spaxels with HCN/HCO+ above the 90th percentile form spatially and kinematically symmetric structures in ppV space (collimated bicones in two galaxies and thin spherical shells in six galaxies).  
  > "In eight out of ten galaxies known to have outflows and/or inflows, we found spatially and kinematically symmetric structures of high line ratios. These structures appear as a collimated bicone in two galaxies and as a thin spherical shell in six galaxies." (abstract)
- Non-LTE RADEX modeling indicates X(HCN)/X(HCO+) >= 10 is required to achieve L'_HCN/L'_HCO+ >= 1 unless gas density exceeds 10^6 cm^-3, and electron collisions can further enhance the ratio by more than a factor of two at moderate densities (10^3-10^5 cm^-3) when X_e- = 10^-4.  
  > "X HCN / X HCO [+] ≳ 10 is necessary to get L HCN [′] [/] [L] HCO [′] [+] [ ≳] [1 unless the density is very high] (≳10 [6] cm [−][3] )." (6.1. Excitation conditions); "The effect is especially noticeable when the electron abundance is highest ( X e [−] = 10 [−][4] ) and the HCN abundance is also high ( X HCN - 1 × 10 [−][7] ), in which case L HCN [′] [/] [L] HCO [′] [+] [can be more than twice as much as the ratio with-] out electron excitation." (6.1. Excitation conditions)
- Chemical shock modeling in moderately-dense gas (10^3-10^4 cm^-3) shows C-shocks with velocities of 30-40 km s^-1 enhance HCN abundance by a factor of 10-100 at 10^3-10^5 yr post-shock via the high-temperature gas-phase reaction CN + H2 -> HCN + H.  
  > "As shown in Fig. 10, the HCN abundance is enhanced by a factor of ∼10–100 at ∼10 [3] – 10 [5] yr after the shock, depending on the shock velocity." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN); "Instead, the enhancement of HCN is mainly due to the gas-phase production via the high-temperature reaction: CN+H 2 → HCN+H." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN); "As shown in Fig. 10, shock velocities of 30 and 40 km s [−][1] can reproduce our HCN and HCO [+] abundances, accounting for the observed line ratio, while 10 and 20 km s [−][1] shocks seem to be insufficient." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Submillimeter molecular lines (HCN and HCO+ J=3-2) are less affected by severe dust extinction in compact obscured nuclei, and the sample was restricted to two dedicated ALMA programs with homogeneous sensitivity and ~0.3'' spatial resolution to allow proper galaxy-to-galaxy and spatially/spectrally resolved comparisons.  
  > "Molecular lines at (sub)millimeter wavelengths are less affected by dust extinction and thus have been explored for useful diagnostic methods." (1. Introduction); "This sample selection was done to ensure that the source properties could be compared properly by only studying sources that have HCN and HCO [+] data with similar sensitivities and spatial resolutions." (2. Sample)
- Why this method: Spectrally and spatially resolved (spaxel-by-spaxel) analysis in ppV space disentangles shocked outflow/inflow components from surrounding disk gas along the line of sight; non-LTE RADEX modeling accounts for subthermal excitation and electron collisions; UCLCHEM models C-shocks in the moderately-dense gas (10^3-10^4 cm^-3) that dominates extragalactic beams.  
  > "As detailed in Sect. 5.3, spectrally resolved analysis would be helpful to disentangle the nuclear region from the surrounding disk regions." (5.1. Global and resolved line ratio); "Given that both HCN and HCO [+] are likely to be subthermally excited (see Sect. 4.4), we adopted the non-local thermal equilibrium (non-LTE) radiative transfer model" (6.1. Excitation conditions); "However, given that the majority of the gas is in a density range of ∼10 [3] –10 [4] cm [−][3] in the beam of extragalactic observations, it is essential to consider the chemistry in the moderately-dense regime." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN)
- Alternative: Clumpy and non-axisymmetric circumnuclear disks as the cause of asymmetric double-peaked line profiles (favored self-absorption by outflows/inflows instead)  
  > "Although we cannot exclude the possibility that these features are caused by clumpy and non-axisymmetric circumnuclear disks, the double-peaked profiles are more likely to be absorption features resulting from self-absorbing gas near the nuclei (i.e., outflows or inflows), as explained below." (4.1. Spectra)
- Alternative: 85th and 95th percentile thresholds for extracting high line ratio structures in ppV space (90th percentile selected as most suitable)  
  > "Through visual inspection, we found the 90th percentile generally produces suitable results to extract characteristic structures, as compared to the other neighboring values such as the 85th and 95th percentile." (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio)
- Alternative: Thermal desorption or sputtering of HCN ice in high-density (10^5 cm^-3) shock models as the main HCN enhancement pathway (found negligible at 10^3-10^4 cm^-3 where gas-phase CN + H2 -> HCN + H dominates)  
  > "In our model, unlike the chemistry under the high density conditions, thermal desorption or sputtering do not contribute to the HCN enhancement because HCN ice is almost absent in pre-shock conditions." (6.2. Chemical pathways to the enhancement of HCN)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Inclusion of six additional galaxies at slightly larger distances breaks the sample completeness held in Paper I.  
  > "Inclusion of these galaxies does not change the range of the infrared luminosity of the sample as a whole but breaks the completeness of the sample held in Paper I." (2. Sample)
- Limitation (authors): Mid-infrared AGN diagnostics (alpha_AGN) have limitations in heavily obscured nuclei ([Ne V] and [O IV] can be attenuated, and 6.2 um PAH EQW may overestimate AGN fraction in starbursts).  
  > "We used these values as best estimates while keeping in mind the limitations of mid-infrared diagnostics when dealing with obscured nuclei." (2. Sample)
- Limitation (authors): Interferometric missing flux beyond the effective MRS (~3-4'') and the fixed ~3'' global aperture may systematically underestimate line fluxes in the closer LIRG sample, and single-dish data are not homogeneously available.  
  > "Given that the LIRG sample is located at smaller distances compared to the ULIRG sample, the potential impact of interferometric missing flux may be systematically more significant for the LIRG samples." (3. Observations and data reduction); "At this moment, single-dish measurements are not homogeneously available for all sample galaxies to calibrate this missing flux issue." (3. Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): No correction was applied for line blending with neighboring CH2NH, HC3N-vib, and HCN-vib lines, which may overestimate HCN and HCO+ fluxes in galaxies with broad lines (>=300 km s^-1).  
  > "For simplicity, we took into account all channels within the integration range and did not apply any correction for line blending. Hence, the values should be taken with caution, particularly for the galaxies with broad line profiles (≳300 km s [−][1] )" (4.3. Line luminosities)
- Limitation (authors): For IRAS 17578-0400, continuum could not be subtracted in the visibility/cube due to the line forest, causing large uncertainties in its line luminosities and spaxel-by-spaxel ratios.  
  > "For IRAS 17578−0400, it is hard to find line-free channels due to the large number of emission lines and the complex line profiles; hence, the continuum was not subtracted." (3. Observations and data reduction); "We note that the values for IRAS 17578−0400 have large uncertainties because we could not identify line-free channels" (4.3. Line luminosities)
- Limitation (authors): Adopted kinematic parameters (center, PA, v_sys) were roughly estimated from the outer velocity field and may not be robust enough for detailed kinematic modeling.  
  > "The parameters are summarized in Table 2, although these may not be robust enough for a more detailed kinematic analysis." (3. Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): 3-sigma threshold clipping in spaxel-by-spaxel analysis ignores faint but real emission below 3-sigma.  
  > "We note, however, that faint but real emission contained in spaxels with a less than 3σ significance may be ignored by the clipping." (5.3. Spectrally resolved line ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Collisional rate coefficients for HCO+ with electrons at J>=3 were provisionally approximated by scaling HCN rates by the square of the dipole moment.  
  > "We provisionally employed the rate coefficients for J ≥3 levels generated by scaling with µ [2] based on the rate coefficients for HCN provided by Faure et al. (2007). For a robust discussion in the future, more accurate rate coefficients for HCO [+] will be necessary." (6.1. Excitation conditions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spaxels of 0.05'' x 0.05'' within the ~0.3'' synthesized beam are strongly spatially correlated (as noted in Table 6), which affects statistical distributions of spaxel-by-spaxel ratios.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Analyzing only the single J=3-2 transition of HCN and HCO+ leaves a degeneracy between excitation (temperature/density) and abundance without multi-transition constraints.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing short-spacing (ACA/single-dish) data can alter spatial line ratios if HCO+ (which has a lower critical density) is more spatially extended than HCN and selectively filtered out.
