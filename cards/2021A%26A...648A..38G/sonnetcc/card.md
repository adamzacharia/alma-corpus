## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract absorption spectra**: Extract absorption spectra at the continuum peak in each spectral window from standard pipeline-processed products  
  > "Absorption spectra were extracted from the standard pipeline-processed data products at the peak of the continuum map in each spectral window." (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - software: ALMA standard pipeline
   - parameter: Channel spacing 244 kHz (0.31 km/s); spectral resolution is twice the channel spacing  
  > "The channel spectral resolution is twice the channel spacing." (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Integrate opacity and derive column densities / limits**: Integrate line profiles over fixed velocity intervals; 3-sigma upper limits for non-detections; convert integrated opacity to column density  
  > "Upper limits are 3$\sigma$." (Table 2); "Therefore, the CO$^+$ column densities have been derived assuming an excitation temperature of 2.73 K." (3.1 The sight-lines toward J1717-3342 and J1744-3116)
   - parameter: Integration velocity intervals -14..10 km/s (J1717-3342) and -20..20 km/s (J1744-3116)  
  > "J1717-3342 | 352.7333 | 2.3911 | -14..10" (Table 2); "J1744-3116 | 357.8635 | -0.9968 | -20..20" (Table 2)
   - parameter: N(HCO+) = 1.12e12 cm^-2 x integral tau dv  
  > "using N(HCO$^+$) = $1.12\times 10^{12}~{\rm cm}^{-2}\int \tau ~{\rm dv}$ as before" (2.4 Other data discussed here)
   - assumption: CO+ excitation set by the CMB at 2.73 K  
  > "assuming that the energy level population is determined by the cosmic microwave background at 2.73 K" (2.1 Spectroscopy)
   - assumption: 13CO and C18O excitation temperature 5 K  
  > "we assume an excitation temperature of 5 K (Table 1)" (3.1 The sight-lines toward J1717-3342 and J1744-3116)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive CO+/HCO+ ratio and X(CO+) limits**: Ratio of N(CO+) limits to N(HCO+) and to N(H2) = N(HCO+)/3e-9  
  > "$^a$N($\mathrm{{\rm H}_2}$) = N(HCO$^+$)/$3\times 10^{-9}$." (Table 3)
   - assumption: Standard HCO+ abundance relative to H2 of 3e-9 in diffuse molecular gas  
  > "using the standard value for the HCO$^+$ abundance relative to H$_2$ in the diffuse molecular gas, $3\times 10^{-9}$ (cite: lucas:96; gerin:19)" (3.1 The sight-lines toward J1717-3342 and J1744-3116)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Test CO+ + H2 as the HCO+ source (analytic balance)**: Equate HCO+ formation via CO+ + H2 with HCO+ recombination with electrons and solve for required n(CO+)/n(HCO+) and X(CO+); compare with observed limits  
  > "Equating formation and recombination of HCO$^+$ , neglecting photodissociation that is four orders of magnitude slower according to the Kinetic Database for Astrochemistry (KIDA)" (3.2 CO+ as a source of HCO+)
   - software: KIDA
   - parameter: x(e) = 2e-4 from n(C+)/n(H) = 1.6e-4; f_H2 = 0.6, T = 40 K  
  > "Taking $x(e)= 2\times10^{-4}$ corresponding to the observed free gas-phase carbon abundance $n({\rm C}^+)/n({\rm H}) = 1.6\times10^{-4}$ (cite: SofLau+04)" (3.2 CO+ as a source of HCO+); "This is some 25 times higher than our upper limit" (3.2 CO+ as a source of HCO+)
   - assumption: Steady state; photodissociation neglected; HCO+ destroyed by thermal-electron recombination  
  > "neglecting photodissociation that is four orders of magnitude slower according to the Kinetic Database for Astrochemistry (KIDA)" (3.2 CO+ as a source of HCO+)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Model the coupled CO+/HOC+/HCO+ thermal chemistry**: Self-consistent atomic/molecular, ionization and thermal equilibrium in gas spheres with column density varied and abundances integrated along the central line of sight; test variants of rates and abundances (baseline, HOC+ isomerization rate, HOC+ formation from CO+ + H2, C+ + H2O channel, X(CH+))  
  > "In brief we calculated the self-consistent global atomic/molecular, ionization and thermal equilibrium in gas spheres of constant total hydrogen number and column density" (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+); "The hydrogen column density was varied and molecular abundances were integrated along the central line of sight to form the results that are displayed here." (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
   - software: KIDA
   - parameter: X(OH) = 1.0 +/- 0.14 x 10^-7  
  > "X(OH) $= 1.0 \pm 0.14 \times10^{-7}$ varies only narrowly" (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
   - parameter: X(H2O) = 2.4e-8  
  > "whose fixed relative abundance X($\mathrm{{\rm H}_2}$O) $=2.4\times10^{-8}$ we take from (cite: GerNeu+16)" (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
   - parameter: X(CH+) free parameter, default 1e-9  
  > "We take X(CH$^+$) as a free parameter with X(CH$^+$) $= 10^{-9}$ by default in the following discussion." (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
   - assumption: Interstellar radiation field (Draine field G0 = 1 elsewhere); HOC+ formation via O + CH3+ ignored as non-thermal  
  > "We ignore HOC$^+$ formation by the reaction of O with CH$_3$$^+$ because the formation of CH$_3$$^+$ is an aspect of the non-thermal chemistry." (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared model CO+ and HOC+ abundances with the observed CO+ upper limits and the observed N(HOC+)/N(HCO+)  
  > "The model produces CO$^+$ relative abundances about 2.5 times smaller than our observed upper limits, and about twice as much HOC$^+$ as observed." (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
6. **Derive n(H)/G0 for CO formation from N(CO)/N(HCO+)**: Balance CO formation by HCO+ dissociative recombination against CO photodissociation for a uniform slab and solve for n(H)/G0 as a function of E(B-V) for the QSO sight-line sample  
  > "Balancing the formation of CO through dissociative recombination of HCO$^+$ and destruction of CO by photodissociation can constrain the physical conditions where CO might form in diffuse molecular gas." (5 Conditions for the growth of CO)
   - parameter: T = 40 K, x(e) = 2e-4  
  > "a uniform temperature of 40K and a uniform electron fraction $x(e) = 2 \times 10^{-4}$." (5 Conditions for the growth of CO)
   - parameter: A_V = 3.1 E(B-V), E(B-V) from Green et al. 2019, nominal error 0.02 mag  
  > "The error bar in E(B-V) is a nominal value 0.02 mag." (5 Conditions for the growth of CO)
   - assumption: Draine radiation field defined as G0 = 1; photodissociation rate 2.43e-10 s^-1 attenuated as exp(-gamma A_V) with gamma = 3.88; line-of-sight averages replace local quantities  
  > "replacing local quantities with line of sight averages" (5 Conditions for the growth of CO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived densities with those inferred from HCO+ emission brightness and typical diffuse-gas thermal pressures  
  > "are higher by a factor $\sim$ 5 than are inferred from the brightness of HCO$^+$ emission in these directions (cite: Lis20Hotspots)" (5 Conditions for the growth of CO)

## Qualitative findings

- CO+ non-detection confirms HCO+ is mainly made via CH2+/CH3+ + O (supra-thermal), while CO+ and HOC+ come from C+ + OH and H2O  
  > "The non-detection of CO$^+$ confirms that HCO$^+$ is mainly produced in the reaction between oxygen and carbon hydrides CH$_2^+$ or CH$_3^+$ induced by supra-thermal processes while CO$^+$ and HOC$^+$ result from reactions of C$^+$ with OH and H$_2$O." (abstract)
- Sight lines span diffuse to translucent gas; the positive-velocity component toward J1744-3116 is probably translucent  
  > "could be associated with translucent rather than diffuse gas" (3.1 The sight-lines toward J1717-3342 and J1744-3116)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: N=2-1 near 236 GHz chosen because it is accessible from the ground and remains populated in diffuse gas; targets are bright quasars with known high Galactic foreground molecular columns  
  > "We chose to search for the $N=2\rightarrow1$ transitions because the frequencies of this first excited transition near 236 GHz are easily accessible from ground-based radio observatories" (2.1 Spectroscopy); "that were known to have high intervening column densities of Galactic neutral gas detected in molecular absorption" (2.2 Target directions)
- Why this method: Absorption spectroscopy is the best probe of diffuse-cloud molecular content because excitation is weak and levels are in the lowest rotational states  
  > "Absorption spectroscopy represents the best method to probe the molecular content of diffuse clouds because the molecular excitation is weak at the low densities" (2.1 Spectroscopy)
- Alternative: CO+ ground-state transitions at 117.7 and 118.1 GHz rejected because of the nearby atmospheric O2 line at 118.75 GHz  
  > "The CO$^+$ ground state rotational transitions at 117.7 GHz and 118.1 GHz are close to a strong atmospheric line from molecular oxygen at 118.75 GHz, rendering their observation from the ground very difficult." (2.1 Spectroscopy)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The sample of sight lines used for n(H)/G0 includes CO and HCO+ detections, which may bias densities high  
  > "which may bias the derived $n({\rm H}/G_0$ to somewhat high values as a higher density implies a faster CO production rate" (5 Conditions for the growth of CO)
- Limitation (authors): Aspects of the C+ + H2O reaction (rate, branching ratio) may not be fully understood, so the HOC+ comparison is uncertain  
  > "or perhaps some aspect of the reaction of C$^+$ and $\mathrm{{\rm H}_2}$O is not fully understood" (4 The thermal chemistry of CO+, HOC+ and HCO+)
