## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: Characterize the spatial distribution, column densities, and excitation temperatures of the small organic molecules HCN, C2H, and H2CO on planet-forming scales in five protoplanetary disks.  
  > "Small organic molecules, such as C$_2$H, HCN, and H$_2$CO, are tracers of the C, N, and O budget in protoplanetary disks." (Abstract); "Here, we focus on molecular line emission from the small organic molecules HCN, C$_2$H, and H$_2$CO in five nearby and otherwise well-studied protoplanetary disks." (Introduction); "We derive column density and excitation temperature profiles for HCN and C$_2$H, and find that the HCN emission arises in a temperate ($20 - 30$ K) layer in the disk, while C$_2$H is present in relatively warmer ($20-60$ K) layers." (Abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Generate zeroth-moment maps and measure disk-integrated line fluxes**: Integrate velocity channels to create zeroth-moment maps and compute disk-integrated fluxes using elliptical masks based on disk inclination, PA, and observed emission extent.  
  > "For HCN $J=3-2$ and $J=1-0$, all hyperfine lines were included to generate the zeroth-moment maps, while for C$_2$H only the two brightest (and blended) lines are included in the $N=3-2$ line, and the first and brightest component in the $N=1-0$ line." (Zeroth-moment maps); "The fluxes were computed using elliptical masks that were constructed using the disk inclinations and position angles listed in Table 1, and an outer radius based on the observed extent of the emission of each line." (Disk-integrated line fluxes)
   - parameter: Disk inclinations and position angles from Table 1  
  > "The fluxes were computed using elliptical masks that were constructed using the disk inclinations and position angles listed in Table 1, and an outer radius based on the observed extent of the emission of each line." (Disk-integrated line fluxes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainties estimated as the standard deviation of integrated fluxes in 500 random line-free positions using the same elliptical mask.  
  > "The uncertainties were estimated by computing integrated fluxes using the same elliptical mask but centered at 500 random positions that are free from line emission in the map." (Disk-integrated line fluxes); "The listed uncertainties correspond to the standard deviation of these line-free integrated fluxes." (Disk-integrated line fluxes)
2. **Extract deprojected radial intensity profiles and radial spectra**: Deproject zeroth-moment maps using GoFish radial_profile and extract Keplerian-shifted deprojected spectra in radial bins of 1/4 beam major axis using GoFish radial_spectra.  
  > "To better characterize the spatial distribution of the emission we used the `radial_profile` function of the Python package `GoFish` (cite: Teague2019) to produce deprojected radial intensity profiles from the zeroth-moment maps, considering the disk inclination and position angle (see Table 1)." (Radial profiles); "To derive column density profiles, we first produce deprojected spectra at different radii using the `radial_spectra` function of `GoFish`." (Column densities); "Before averaging the spectra in each radial bin, the velocity is shifted according to the Keplerian rotation of the disk, so that all the spectra are aligned." (Column densities)
   - software: GoFish
   - parameter: Radial bin size = 1/4 of the beam major axis  
  > "The radial bins are set to 1/4 of the beam major axis, and the disk is assumed to be vertically flat." (Column densities)
   - parameter: Averaging wedge = 30 degrees along disk major axis (or full azimuthal average for low S/N: C2H in IM Lup and GM Aur, and H2CO in all sources)  
  > "The spectra are azimuthally averaged in those disks with low S/N (C$_2$H for IM Lup and GM Aur, and H$_2$CO for all sources), and averaged within a $30^{\circ}$ wedge along the disk major axis for the others." (Column densities)
   - assumption: Vertically flat disk geometry  
  > "The radial bins are set to 1/4 of the beam major axis, and the disk is assumed to be vertically flat." (Column densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with lower angular resolution profiles from literature  
  > "The radial profiles are consistent with profiles previously observed at lower angular resolution (0.5$''$) for HCN and C$_2$H in IM Lup, AS 209, HD 163296, and MWC 480 (cite: bergner2019), and for H$_2$CO in all sources (cite: pegues2020)." (Radial profiles)
3. **Retrieve HCN and C2H column density and excitation temperature profiles**: Two-step LTE hyperfine spectral fitting with thermal line width flux redistribution: first simultaneously fit 0.3'' 1-0 and tapered 3-2 spectra to constrain Tex,0; second fit 0.15'' 3-2 spectra using priors around Tex,0 (and fixed CO temperatures in inner blended regions).  
  > "To derive the column densities, we assume that the emission is in local thermal equilibrium (LTE) and that the emission fills the beam." (Column density retrieval); "First, we fit the $1-0$ and $3-2$ lines simultaneously to obtain a better constraint on the excitation temperature." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method); "In the second step, and to obtain a better-resolved column density profile, we fit the high angular resolution $3-2$ lines only." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Free parameters: N_tot, FWHM line width Delta V, excitation temperature T_ex, velocity offset v_lsr  
  > "The free parameters in the fit are the total column density N$_{\rm{tot}}$, the FWHM $\Delta V = 2 \sqrt{2 \ln2} \sigma$, and the excitation temperature in the case of HCN and C$_2$H, as their lines present hyperfine structure." (Column density retrieval); "We also allow for a small offset from the source velocity $v_{lsr}$, constrained between $-$0.25 and 0.25$~\rm{km~s}^{-1}$." (Column density retrieval)
   - parameter: Background temperature T_bg fixed to max(continuum brightness temperature, CMB 2.73 K)  
  > "The background temperature $\rm{T}_{\rm{bg}}$ is fixed to the maximum of the continuum brightness temperature or the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature (2.73 K)." (Column density retrieval)
   - assumption: Local thermal equilibrium (LTE), beam filling factor of 1, and identical excitation temperature for all hyperfine components  
  > "To derive the column densities, we assume that the emission is in local thermal equilibrium (LTE) and that the emission fills the beam." (Column density retrieval); "We fit the observed spectra assuming that all the hyperfine lines have the same excitation temperature." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
   - assumption: Flux computed with purely thermal line width and redistributed over observed broader line width to account for beam smearing  
  > "A way to alleviate this problem is to first compute the line intensity assuming a purely thermal line width, then compute the total flux, and finally redistribute this flux in the line but now for the observed and broader line width." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling using emcee; Step 1 prior: 10 K < T_ex < T_gas(12CO); Step 2 prior: T_ex,0 - 10 K < T_ex < T_ex,0 + 10 K (or fixed to T_gas(12CO)/T_gas(13CO) in inner disk); velocity offset prior [-0.25, 0.25] km/s; 50th percentile for best fit and 16th/84th percentiles for uncertainties.  
  > "We explore the parameter space using the `emcee` package (cite: Foreman2013). The best fit is taken as the 50th percentile of the samples in the marginalized distributions, and the associated errors are computed from the 16th and 84th percentiles." (Column density retrieval); "We thus restrict the rotational temperature between 10 K and the gas temperature inferred from the peak brightness temperature of the optically thick $^{12}$CO 2–1 line, $\rm{T}_{\rm{gas}}$($^{12}$CO) (cite: law21_surf), for both HCN and C$_2$H." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method); "We used the excitation temperature found in the previous step (T$_{\rm{ex},0}$) as an initial parameter, and constrain $\rm{T}_{\rm{ex},0}- 10$ K $< \rm{T}_{\rm{ex}}<  \rm{T}_{\rm{ex},0}+ 10$ K for both HCN and C$_2$H." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
   - validation: Compared against independent HCN retrievals in Bergner et al. (2021) and Cataldi et al. (2021) (including non-LTE fits), finding consistent column density profiles.  
  > "(cite: Cataldi21) performed a non-LTE fit to the HCN lines and found similar column densities, suggesting our LTE assumption is a good approximation, at least for HCN." (Column density retrieval); "An independent retrieval of the HCN column density and excitation temperature is presented in (cite: bergner21) and (cite: Cataldi21), using a method similar to the one presented here but with different constraints on the excitation temperature." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
4. **Retrieve H2CO column density profiles**: Fit a single Gaussian to the 0.3'' H2CO 3_03-2_02 line in each radial bin under LTE for fixed excitation temperatures between 20 and 50 K.  
  > "For H$_2$CO, we fit a single Gaussian to the $J=3-2$ line in each radial bin." (H$_2$CO fitting method); "Since we only observed one line, the excitation temperature is fixed and the column density was computed for a range of excitation temperatures between 20 and 50 K so that we are not *a priori* assuming the vertical location of the H$_2$CO emitting layer." (H$_2$CO fitting method)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Fixed excitation temperatures T_ex between 20 and 50 K  
  > "Since we only observed one line, the excitation temperature is fixed and the column density was computed for a range of excitation temperatures between 20 and 50 K so that we are not *a priori* assuming the vertical location of the H$_2$CO emitting layer." (H$_2$CO fitting method)
   - assumption: LTE and excitation temperature between 20 and 50 K  
  > "Since we only observed one line, the excitation temperature is fixed and the column density was computed for a range of excitation temperatures between 20 and 50 K so that we are not *a priori* assuming the vertical location of the H$_2$CO emitting layer." (H$_2$CO fitting method)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC fit for N_tot, FWHM Delta V, and velocity offset v_lsr with fixed T_ex  
  > "For H$_2$CO, the excitation temperature is fixed because only a single line is available." (Column density retrieval); "We explore the parameter space using the `emcee` package (cite: Foreman2013)." (Column density retrieval)
   - validation: Sensitivity check across T_ex = 20-50 K shows column density varies by only a factor ~2; consistent with Pegues et al. (2020).  
  > "We found that the column density is not very sensitive to $\rm{T}_{\rm{ex}}$; the difference in column density is only a factor $\sim2$ for this range of temperatures." (H$_2$CO fitting method); "The H$_2$CO column density profiles are consistent with the ones derived by (cite: pegues2020) for all sources using different H$_2$CO transitions and at lower angular resolution." (HCN, C$_2$H, and H$_2$CO column density profiles)
5. **Estimate inner 50 au organic gas and ice masses and abundances relative to water**: Integrate column density profiles within r < 50 au (extrapolating inward at constant column density where inner disk fits fail), multiply by an ice-to-gas ratio of 1000, and divide by modeled water ice mass inside 50 au.  
  > "Because of the uncertainties of the fit in the inner disk, we extrapolated the column density profiles inward, assuming a constant column density starting from the radius where we obtain a good fit (see Fig. 4)." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au); "Assuming a conservative ice-to-gas ratio of 1000 (cite: oberg21), we find a large amount ($10^{23} - 10^{26}$ g) of gas phase plus ice mantle HCN, C$_2$H, and H$_2$CO in the inner disks." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au); "The total water mass inside $50$ au was estimated from the models presented in (cite: zhang21), and are listed in Table 1 of (cite: oberg21)." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au)
   - parameter: Outer integration radius = 50 au  
  > "The gas masses within $50$ au are listed in Table [\[tab:organic_reservoir\]](#tab:organic_reservoir)." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au)
   - assumption: Constant column density extrapolation inward in the inner disk where fits are poor  
  > "Because of the uncertainties of the fit in the inner disk, we extrapolated the column density profiles inward, assuming a constant column density starting from the radius where we obtain a good fit (see Fig. 4)." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au)
   - assumption: Ice-to-gas ratio of 1000  
  > "Assuming a conservative ice-to-gas ratio of 1000 (cite: oberg21), we find a large amount ($10^{23} - 10^{26}$ g) of gas phase plus ice mantle HCN, C$_2$H, and H$_2$CO in the inner disks." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived organic-to-water ratios to Solar System cometary ice abundances from Mumma & Charnley (2011).  
  > "Typical abundances with respect to water in cometary ices range from 0.1% to 0.6% for HCN and from $0.1-1\%$ for H$_2$CO (cite: mumma2011), which are consistent with our organic fraction estimates." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN and C2H emission is more compact than H2CO emission by a factor of ~2 and shows rich substructures (rings, central holes, gaps), especially in HD 163296 and MWC 480.  
  > "In general, the HCN and C$_2$H emission is more compact than that of H$_2$CO — their radial extents are smaller by a factor $\sim2$." (Radial profiles); "The line emission reveals substantial substructure, in particular toward the warmer disks around HD 163296 and MWC 480, where several rings are observed in HCN and C$_2$H." (Conclusions)
- HCN emission originates in colder, vertically deeper disk layers (20-30 K) than C2H emission (20-60 K).  
  > "We derive column density and excitation temperature profiles for HCN and C$_2$H, and find that the HCN emission arises in a temperate ($20 - 30$ K) layer in the disk, while C$_2$H is present in relatively warmer ($20-60$ K) layers." (Abstract); "The temperatures are higher for C$_2$H, however, suggesting that the HCN emission arises in a colder and deeper layer than C$_2$H, and is thus more sensitive to density variations." (Conclusions)
- There is no universal spatial correlation between millimeter dust gaps and HCN or C2H column density peaks.  
  > "The second column density peak seen at $\sim110$ au toward HD 163296 does not spatially coincide with any of the dust gaps, revealing that there is not a universal connection between dust depletion and C$_2$H or HCN formation." (Photochemistry of HCN and C$_2$H)
- H2CO column densities show enhancements near the CO snowline and near the outer edge of the millimeter dust continuum disk.  
  > "The H$_2$CO column densities rise near the location of the CO snowline and/or millimeter dust edge, suggesting an efficient release of H$_2$CO ices in the outer disk." (Abstract); "We find enhanced H$_2$CO column densities at the CO snowline location and another column density enhancement around the millimeter dust edge." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Provides high angular resolution (0.15''-0.3'', 10-50 au) and high brightness sensitivity across both 1-0 (Band 3) and 3-2 (Band 6) transitions to resolve chemical substructures and constrain excitation temperatures.  
  > "The MAPS data presented in this paper provide a combination of high angular resolution with excellent brightness sensitivity, which enables us to identify substructure in the line emission of these small organics." (Introduction); "In addition, the $1-0$ line observations now allow us to better constrain the molecular excitation temperature." (Location of the HCN and C$_2$H emission surfaces)
- Why this method: Deprojecting within a 30 degree wedge along the major axis avoids emission surface height uncertainties; thermal line width flux redistribution mitigates beam smearing in hyperfine fits; two-step fitting combines multi-band Tex constraints with 0.15'' spatial resolution.  
  > "The main motivation for using a $30^{\circ}$ wedge when possible is to avoid the uncertainty of the emission surface height, which strongly influences the spatial location of emission near the minor axis." (Column densities); "A way to alleviate this problem is to first compute the line intensity assuming a purely thermal line width, then compute the total flux, and finally redistribute this flux in the line but now for the observed and broader line width." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method); "First, we fit the $1-0$ and $3-2$ lines simultaneously to obtain a better constraint on the excitation temperature." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
- Alternative: Taking vertical emission height into account when deriving radial profiles (rejected in favor of a flat disk assumption with a 30 degree major-axis wedge).  
  > "Although it is possible to take into account the vertical height of the emission to derive the radial intensity profiles (cite: law21_rad), here we assume a flat disk but produce the deprojected profiles by averaging the emission within a $30^{\circ}$ wedge along the disk major axis." (Radial profiles)
- Alternative: Using 0.15'' resolution H2CO images (rejected in favor of 0.3'' tapered images for all sources due to low S/N at 0.15'' except in GM Aur).  
  > "For consistency, here we use the tapered version of the H$_2$CO images (with 0.3$''$ resolution) for all sources, which are more sensitive to low surface brightness and extended features." (Observations)
- Alternative: Using T_gas(12CO) as the fixed inner-disk excitation temperature for HCN in Herbig Ae disks (rejected in favor of T_gas(13CO) because 12CO produced a large column density discontinuity).  
  > "For the two disks around Herbig Ae stars, however, the $\rm{T}_{\rm{gas}}$($^{13}$CO) was used instead, since the $\rm{T}_{\rm{gas}}$($^{12}$CO) produced a large discontinuity in the column density profile." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Spectral line fits perform poorly in the inner disk (<30 au) where lines are broadened by beam smearing and back-side contamination, completely blending hyperfine components.  
  > "The lines are fit well in most of the disk, but poorly fit in the inner disk, where the lines are substantially broader, resulting in the individual hyperfine components being completely blended." (HCN and C$_2$H fitting method); "As was found for HCN and C$_2$H, the lines are fit well in most of the disk, but poorly fit in the inner $\sim30$ au, especially in HD 163296 and MWC 480. This is probably due to contamination from the back side of the disk." (H$_2$CO fitting method)
- Limitation (authors): Only a single H2CO transition was observed, preventing a direct constraint on the H2CO excitation temperature.  
  > "Since we only observed one line, the excitation temperature is fixed and the column density was computed for a range of excitation temperatures between 20 and 50 K so that we are not *a priori* assuming the vertical location of the H$_2$CO emitting layer." (H$_2$CO fitting method)
- Limitation (authors): Inner 50 au HCN masses for GM Aur, HD 163296, and MWC 480 may be underestimated due to inward constant-column-density extrapolation.  
  > "This means that the HCN masses estimated for GM Aur, HD 163296, and MWC 480, which tend to have centrally peaked N profiles, could be larger than what we have estimated here." (Small organics reservoir in the inner 50 au)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Total gas+ice organic masses and water-relative abundances in the inner 50 au rely on an assumed uniform ice-to-gas ratio of 1000 and inward extrapolation where lines are optically thick/blended.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a flat disk geometry during radial spectral extraction may introduce minor radial smearing for elevated layers, even within a 30 degree wedge.
