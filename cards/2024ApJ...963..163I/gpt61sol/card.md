## Analysis recipe

1. **Image calibrated data and assess missing flux**: Image continuum and lines; use 12m7m for main analyses and compare with 12m7mTP  
  > "The data calibration was performed using the CASA software package version 4.5.3, 4.6, and 4.7, while imaging was carried out using CASA 5.4 (cite: Mcmullin2007)." (Observations); "the combined 12 m, 7 m, and TP data (hereafter 12m7mTP) are also used to assess the missing flux in images without TP data." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, YCLEAN
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5 for continuum; 2 for lines  
  > "We adopted a Briggs robust weighting of 0.5 and 2 for the visibilities of continuum and lines in the imaging process, respectively." (Observations)
   - parameter: Compare H2CO fluxes above 3σ over the whole mapping area  
  > "The mean ratio of 12m7m flux over 12m7mTP flux in the whole mapping area where the emission is more than 3 $\sigma$ for H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) emission is 0.66." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measure 12m7m/12m7mTP flux ratios separately for the three H2CO transitions  
  > "The mean ratio of 12m7m flux over 12m7mTP flux in the whole mapping area where the emission is more than 3 $\sigma$ for H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) emission is 0.66." (Observations); "While the ratio for H$_2$CO(3$_{2,2}$–2$_{2,1}$) and H$_2$CO(3$_{2,1}$–2$_{2,0}$) emission is 1.00 and 0.96, respectively." (Observations)
2. **Measure line emission and core detection rates**: Gaussian-fit para-H2CO spectra; measure peak intensity, linewidth, integrated intensity, and detections in dendrogram leaves  
  > "The peak intensities, line widths, and integrated intensities of the para-H$_2$CO emission were derived using Gaussian fitting (red curve in Figure [1](#Spectrum))." (Results); "the results presented in the main text, which use dendrogram leaves." (Core detection rate)
   - parameter: Detection threshold 3σ  
  > "We note that the detection threshold is 3$\sigma$." (Core detection rate)
   - fitting: Gaussian fitting  
  > "The peak intensities, line widths, and integrated intensities of the para-H$_2$CO emission were derived using Gaussian fitting (red curve in Figure [1](#Spectrum))." (Results)
   - validation: Repeat with simplified elliptical core apertures; detection rate 60% and main trends align  
  > "The detection rate based on this simplified structure is 60 % (175/294)" (Core detection rate); "the main trends align with those described in the main text." (Core detection rate)
3. **Compare H2CO with dust continuum**: Compare spatial distributions and Spearman correlations of integrated intensities with 1.3 mm flux density by core class  
  > "The spatial distributions of all H$_2$CO transition lines differ from the material traced by the 1.3 mm dust continuum emission." (Spatial distribution); "The Spearman’s correlation coefficient for each classification of cores is summarized in Table [\[CC-Ih2co-S1mm\]](#CC-Ih2co-S1mm)." (Correlation with 1.3mm dust continuum)
   - fitting: Spearman rank-order correlation  
  > "The protostellar cores have a Spearman rank-order correlation (Spearman’s correlation) [^3] coefficient of $r$ = 0.25 with a $p$-value of 0.02, while the prestellar candidates have $r$ = 0.07 and $p$-value = 0.49." (Correlation with 1.3mm dust continuum)
   - validation: Elliptical-aperture check preserves main trends  
  > "the main trends align with those described in the main text." (Core detection rate)
4. **Identify the gas kinematics and outflow association**: Make moment 1/2, blue/red wing, and position-velocity maps; compare H2CO with C18O dispersions and published CO outflows  
  > "The H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) intensity-weighted mean velocity (moment 1; left panel), and velocity dispersion (moment 2; right panel) maps for two representative IRDC clumps: G010.991-00.082 and G337.541-00.082 are shown in Figure [6](#MOM12)." (Velocity distribution); "The direction of the blue- and red-shifted components roughly agrees with these components detected in CO emission that is considered to trace molecular outflows, as investigated by (cite: Li2020) in the ASHES survey." (Velocity distribution); "Figure [10](#PV) shows examples of the position-velocity map of the outflows." (Velocity distribution)
   - parameter: Moment-map velocity ranges approximately 5–25 km s^-1; visually selected per clump  
  > "The velocity range used to generate these moment maps for the 12 IRDC clumps is approximately 5–25 km s$^{-1}$ ." (Velocity distribution); "We note that these velocity ranges were determined through visual inspection of the spectra for each clump, ensuring that they encompassed all of the line emission." (Velocity distribution)
   - parameter: Re-bin pixels from 0.2 to 0.6 arcsec  
  > "The pixel scale was re-binned to 0.6$''$, approximately half of the synthesized beam, from the original value (0.2$''$)." (Velocity distribution)
   - parameter: C18O/H2CO comparisons require both lines above 3σ  
  > "Therefore, we only investigate the core and pixel where both lines are detected above the 3$\sigma$ level." (Velocity distribution)
   - parameter: Blue/red velocity ranges manually selected from spectra  
  > "The velocity range of the blue- and red-shifted components, which are selected manually by visually inspecting the spectra, are written in the upper-right on Figure [9](#WING)." (Velocity distribution)
   - fitting: Spearman correlation of H2CO and C18O velocity dispersions  
  > "The protostellar cores show a Spearman rank-order correlation coefficient of $r$ = 0.10 with $p$-value = 0.37." (Velocity distribution)
   - validation: Check spatial directions against CO outflows from Li2020  
  > "The direction of the blue- and red-shifted components roughly agrees with these components detected in CO emission that is considered to trace molecular outflows, as investigated by (cite: Li2020) in the ASHES survey." (Velocity distribution)
5. **Fit LTE temperatures**: Fit all three H2CO line profiles following Mangum2015  
  > "We fit the three H$_2$CO lines assuming LTE conditions and estimate best-fitting parameters." (LTE assumption); "Under this assumption, the fitting routine constructs line profiles with a range of input parameters, including peak velocities, temperatures, line-widths, and column densities, following (cite: Mangum2015)." (LTE assumption)
   - software: Python, lmfit, FFTL
   - parameter: Require all three transitions above 5σ  
  > "Temperatures were derived only for those cores and pixels where all three transitions of H$_2$CO were detected at more than 5$\sigma$." (Kinetic temperature from H$_2$CO lines)
   - parameter: Stated fitting ranges: 10–300 K; 1.0 × 10^10–1.0 × 10^20 cm^-2; 0.14–1.37 km s^-1  
  > "The fitted temperatures, column densities, and line-widths are in the range of 10 – 300 K, 1.0 $\times$ 10$^{10}$ – 1.0 $\times$ 10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$, and 0.14 – 1.37 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (LTE assumption)
   - parameter: Core peak velocities manually selectable; pixel velocities set by H2CO 3_0,3–2_0,2 peak  
  > "The peak velocities of the lines can be selected manually and are fixed according to their known rest frequencies." (LTE assumption); "When we derive the temperature for each pixel, the peak velocities are automatically selected as the position of H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) peak intensity and are fixed according to their known rest frequencies." (LTE assumption)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium  
  > "We fit the three H$_2$CO lines assuming LTE conditions and estimate best-fitting parameters." (LTE assumption)
   - fitting: lmfit non-linear least squares; minimize constructed–observed line differences; return errors  
  > "The code then minimizes the differences between the constructed and observed lines, using the non-linear least-squares python fitting routine *lmfit* [^4]." (LTE assumption); "The best fit is returned, along with the estimated temperature, line-width, column density, and associated errors, which we then take as the optimized fitting results [^5]." (LTE assumption)
   - validation: Compare with RADEX temperatures and TP-inclusive temperatures  
  > "The $T_{\rm LTE}$ and $T_{\rm Radex}$ are roughly similar, but in almost all cases, the $T_{\rm Radex}$ is slightly higher than the $T_{\rm LTE}$." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature); "In order to investigate the impact of the missing flux on the line emission, we compare the temperatures derived from 12m7m and 12m7mTP data." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
6. **Derive non-LTE temperatures**: Use RADEX through ndRADEX and the Gaussian-fitted H2CO 3_2,1–2_2,0 / 3_0,3–2_0,2 integrated-intensity ratio  
  > "We employ the Radex code (cite: Vandertak2007), by means of the ndRADEX tool[^6], to generate a set of different models covering a wide range of conditions." (Radex modeling); "With the above parameters and the H$_2$CO(3$_{2,1}$–2$_{2,0}$)/H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) integrated intensity ratio derived from the Gaussian fitting, we then compute the temperatures using a model grid for the H$_2$CO lines encompassing 580 temperatures ranging from 10 to 300 K." (Radex modeling)
   - software: RADEX, ndRADEX
   - parameter: 580 temperatures from 10 to 300 K  
  > "With the above parameters and the H$_2$CO(3$_{2,1}$–2$_{2,0}$)/H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) integrated intensity ratio derived from the Gaussian fitting, we then compute the temperatures using a model grid for the H$_2$CO lines encompassing 580 temperatures ranging from 10 to 300 K." (Radex modeling)
   - parameter: Linewidth from Gaussian fitting  
  > "The line width is derived from the Gaussian fitting." (Radex modeling)
   - assumption: Background temperature 2.73 K  
  > "For the background temperature, we adopt 2.73 K." (Radex modeling)
   - assumption: Continuum-derived H2 densities and column densities from Sanhueza2019  
  > "For the H$_2$ density and H$_2$ (or H$_2$CO) column density, we adopt the values derived from the 1.3 mm dust continuum (cite: Sanhueza2019)." (Radex modeling)
   - assumption: N(H2CO)/N(H2) = 1.0 × 10^-10  
  > "The fractional abundance of N(H$_2$CO)/N(H$_2$) was adopted as 1.0 $\times$ 10$^{-10}$ from (cite: Tang2017a), which investigated the fractional abundance in IRDCs." (Radex modeling)
   - assumption: Pixel modeling uses whole-IRDC average density, column density, and linewidth  
  > "When we derive the temperature of each pixel, we use the averaged values of H$_2$ density, column density, and linewidth over the whole IRDC for simplicity." (Radex modeling)
   - fitting: Model-grid temperature determination; error propagated from optimum Gaussian fitting  
  > "The temperature error is propagated from the optimum of the Gaussian fitting." (Radex modeling); "With the above parameters and the H$_2$CO(3$_{2,1}$–2$_{2,0}$)/H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) integrated intensity ratio derived from the Gaussian fitting, we then compute the temperatures using a model grid for the H$_2$CO lines encompassing 580 temperatures ranging from 10 to 300 K." (Radex modeling)
   - validation: Compare against LTE and test density/column-density ranges  
  > "The $T_{\rm LTE}$ and $T_{\rm Radex}$ are roughly similar, but in almost all cases, the $T_{\rm Radex}$ is slightly higher than the $T_{\rm LTE}$." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature); "n(H$_2$) = 10$^5$ – 2.0 $\times$ 10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$ and N(H$_2$) = 10$^{22}$ – 10$^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
7. **Test temperature systematics**: Vary density and column density; compare 12m7m and 12m7mTP temperatures  
  > "To investigate the uncertainty, we calculate the minimum and maximum temperature" (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature); "In order to investigate the impact of the missing flux on the line emission, we compare the temperatures derived from 12m7m and 12m7mTP data." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: n(H2) = 10^5–2.0 × 10^7 cm^-3; N(H2) = 10^22–10^24 cm^-2  
  > "n(H$_2$) = 10$^5$ – 2.0 $\times$ 10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$ and N(H$_2$) = 10$^{22}$ – 10$^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
   - assumption: Density/column-density ranges span continuum-derived core extrema  
  > "These ranges come from the minimum and maximum values of the cores derived from the 1.3 mm continuum emission (cite: Sanhueza2019), which include both protostellar cores and prestellar candidates." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- H2CO 3_0,3–2_0,2 is more extended than the two higher-excitation transitions  
  > "H$_2$CO(3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) is more spatially extended than H$_2$CO(3$_{2,2}$–2$_{2,1}$) and H$_2$CO(3$_{2,1}$–2$_{2,0}$)." (Spatial distribution)
- H2CO spatial distributions differ from 1.3 mm continuum  
  > "The spatial distributions of all H$_2$CO transition lines differ from the material traced by the 1.3 mm dust continuum emission." (Spatial distribution)
- More than half the H2CO-traced regions exceed 50 K under all tested assumptions  
  > "In all models and under all assumptions, more than half of the regions traced by H$_2$CO show high temperatures of more than 50 K." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
- High-temperature/high-dispersion pixels tend to trace outflow edges  
  > "However, the data points in the sub-area tend to trace the edges of the outflows." (Non-thermal velocity dispersion)
- Some embedded cores have entered the protostellar phase  
  > "Altogether, these results confirm that some of the embedded cores in 70 $\mu m$ dark IRDCs have already entered the protostellar phase." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 70 μm dark IRDCs probe early high-mass star formation; H2CO probes kinetic temperatures and possible hot-core precursors  
  > "Among IRDCs, those that are 70 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ dark are especially important because they are the coldest, most quiescent clouds (cite: Guzman2015)." (Introduction); "From the above, the three transitions of H$_2$CO allow us to probe the kinetic temperature of IRDCs and unveil potential precursors of future massive hot cores." (Introduction)
- Why this method: H2CO ladder populations are collisionally controlled; use two temperature methods and matched-resolution NH3 comparisons  
  > "Since the relative population of the $K_a$ ladders of H$_2$CO are almost exclusively determined by collisional processes (cite: Mangum1993), the line ratios involving different $K_a$ ladders are good tracers of the kinetic temperatures (cite: Mangum1993; Muhle2007)." (Introduction); "We derived the kinetic temperature of the molecular gas in the IRDC clumps traced by H$_2$CO lines using two methods: (1) under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and (2) with the RADEX non-LTE modeling program (cite: Vandertak2007)." (Kinetic temperature from H$_2$CO lines)
- Alternative: Elliptical apertures versus dendrogram leaves; main trends agree  
  > "The detection rate based on this simplified structure is 60 % (175/294)" (Core detection rate); "the main trends align with those described in the main text." (Core detection rate)
- Alternative: LTE versus non-LTE RADEX temperatures  
  > "The $T_{\rm LTE}$ and $T_{\rm Radex}$ are roughly similar, but in almost all cases, the $T_{\rm Radex}$ is slightly higher than the $T_{\rm LTE}$." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
- Alternative: 12m7m versus 12m7mTP; missing flux less important than model assumptions  
  > "Compared to the uncertainty from the assumption, the impact of the missing flux is likely negligible." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
- Alternative: Repeat temperature–dispersion analysis excluding G014.49  
  > "The right panel of Figure [16](#linewidth-temp_core) shows the same plot as the left panel but excludes the data from G014.492-00.139 (hereafter, G014.49)." (Non-thermal velocity dispersion)
- How they decided it was enough: Require all three H2CO transitions above 5σ for temperature derivation  
  > "Temperatures were derived only for those cores and pixels where all three transitions of H$_2$CO were detected at more than 5$\sigma$." (Kinetic temperature from H$_2$CO lines)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature sample requires three >5σ lines and covers only 26 cores  
  > "Temperatures were derived only for those cores and pixels where all three transitions of H$_2$CO were detected at more than 5$\sigma$." (Kinetic temperature from H$_2$CO lines); "Under this 5$\sigma$ threshold, we could derive the temperature toward 26 cores among all the 294 cores." (Kinetic temperature from H$_2$CO lines)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature errors exceed 20 K above 100 K  
  > "For both $T_{\rm LTE}$ and $T_{\rm Radex}$, the temperature error becomes larger than 20 K for temperatures of more than 100 K." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature depends significantly on model and assumed H2 density  
  > "These results indicate that the temperature determination can vary significantly depending on the assumed models and n(H$_2$)." (Comparison between LTE temperature and Radex temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Pixel RADEX calculations use whole-IRDC average density, column density, and linewidth  
  > "When we derive the temperature of each pixel, we use the averaged values of H$_2$ density, column density, and linewidth over the whole IRDC for simplicity." (Radex modeling)
- Limitation (authors): G014.49 fits are uncertain because of complicated, non-single-Gaussian spectra  
  > "The spectra for many of the cores in G014.19 are different from a single Gaussian (e.g., double-peak structure)." (Non-thermal velocity dispersion); "Therefore, the fits for the cores in G014.49 are likely more uncertain than for other cores." (Non-thermal velocity dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): 300 K pixel temperatures are lower limits and excluded from power-law fitting  
  > "Pixels with $T_{\rm kin}$ = 300 K are discarded because these values are considered to be lower limits." (Table 5)
- Limitation (authors): Larger samples needed for robust correlation conclusions  
  > "However, a larger sample is needed for more robust statistical conclusions." (Correlation with 1.3mm dust continuum)
- Limitation (authors): Smoothing dilutes G028.273–00.167 H2CO emission below temperature-measurement requirements  
  > "The H$_2$CO temperature of G028.273–00.167 is not derived in the smoothed data because the H$_2$CO components in G028.273–00.167 detected with a 1.2$''$ resolution (see Figure [19](#A-MOM0) in appendix) are diluted at a 5$\ensuremath{''}$ beam." (Comparison of temperatures derived from H$_2$CO and NH$_3$)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The three-line 5σ selection favors bright, excited gas; temperature statistics do not describe all 294 cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The RADEX methods specify the 3_2,1–2_2,0 ratio, but the LTE/RADEX comparison attributes a difference to the 3_2,2–2_2,1 ratio; resolve this before reproduction.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stated LTE linewidth range of 0.14–1.37 km s^-1 is difficult to reconcile with the broader reported core dispersions; clarify its meaning.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Mach-number subsection reports different ranges and means from the preceding thermal-motion section without clearly identifying the population change.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Pixels re-binned to half a beam remain spatially correlated, which matters for correlation significance.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Temperature and derived non-thermal dispersion share fitted inputs; their correlation is not an independent heating diagnostic.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow-edge shocks and hot-core precursors remain interpretations rather than uniquely established mechanisms.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Exact imaging and manual velocity selections require earlier papers or unreleased task-specific artifacts.
