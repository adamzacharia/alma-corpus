## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure dust-ring locations and profiles**: Extract deprojected radial and azimuthal continuum profiles using outer-disk position angle and inclination; normalize asymmetric profiles to the opposite side.  
  > "The azimuthal and radial profiles are extracted for each image, using the position angle and inclination of the outer disk." (Dust structure); "In the asymmetric curves the profiles are normalized to the flux at the opposite side of the asymmetric peak" (Dust structure)
   - parameter: Radial profiles average ±30 degrees around the asymmetry peak, or around the major axis for axisymmetric disks.  
  > "The radial profiles are taken by averaging the $\pm$30 degrees on either side of the angle of the peak of the asymmetry if present, or around the major axis position angle for axisymmetric disks." (Dust structure)
   - parameter: Azimuthal extraction radial width: half the beam size.  
  > "At each radial location, the azimuthal profile is extracted using a radial width of half the beam size." (Dust structure)
   - parameter: Opposite-side nondetections: 3sigma upper limit for normalization.  
  > "in case of a non-detection on that side, a 3$\sigma$ upper limit is assumed for the normalization." (Dust structure)
   - assumption: Optical-depth effects considered insignificant for most moderate-to-face-on disks; IRS48 is an exception.  
  > "As most asymmetric disks have a moderate to face-on inclination, optical depth effects are not considered to be affecting the results significantly." (Dust structure); "The only exception is IRS 48 with a 50$^{\circ}$ inclination, which implies that the continuum contrast might be overestimated by a factor of a few." (Dust structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare ring widths with the deprojected beam; combine radial locations with literature analysis corrected to Gaia distances.  
  > "In the radial plots, the deprojected beam profile is overplot at the location of the dust ring, to show how well the ring is resolved radially." (Dust structure); "The radial profiles provide the radial locations of the dust rings, in combination with more detailed analysis from the literature (after correction for the new *Gaia* distances)." (Dust structure)
2. **Fit intrinsic azimuthal widths**: Fit Gaussian rings/asymmetries in the visibility plane using real and imaginary components; IRS48 uses a fourth-power radial profile; convert phi_w to FWHM.  
  > "The fitting is performed in the visibility plane comparing both the Real and Imaginary components, using the `galario` tool set to Fourier transform and sample the model (cite: Tazzari2018)." (Visibility analysis); "IRS 48 can be described by a single 2D Gaussian, where the radial profile is found to be best fit with a 4th power rather than a second power" (Visibility analysis); "The $\phi_w$ value provides the estimate for the FWHM of each asymmetry by multiplication with 2.36." (Visibility analysis)
   - software: galario, emcee
   - parameter: 70 walkers; 2000 steps.  
  > "In the MCMC, 70 walkers were used in combination with 2000 steps." (Visibility analysis)
   - parameter: Position angle and inclination fixed to sample-table values; phase center fitted.  
  > "The position angle and inclination are fixed, taken from Table" (Visibility analysis); "The phase center is fit as well and listed in the table." (Visibility analysis)
   - parameter: Axisymmetric disks assigned FWHM 360 degrees.  
  > "For the axisymmetric disks, the FWHM is set to 360$^{\circ}$." (Dust structure)
   - assumption: One or two rings and asymmetries represented by radial/azimuthal Gaussian components.  
  > "The disks can be described by a combination of one or two rings and asymmetries." (Visibility analysis); "The asymmetric disks are fit to a 2D profile $I(r,\phi)$, describing the radial and azimuthal features as Gaussians." (Visibility analysis)
   - fitting: Manual exploration for initial estimates, then emcee MCMC; priors and likelihood not stated.  
  > "The best-fit parameters are found by careful exploration of the parameter values for initial estimates, followed by a fit to the visibilities with the Markov-chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) code `emcee` (cite: Foreman2013) for constraining each asymmetry." (Visibility analysis)
   - validation: Inspect posterior distributions and visibility curves; image model-subtracted residuals. Typical residual peaks 12–22%; HD142527 did not converge.  
  > "The best-fit models are mapped onto the observed visibilities, imaged and subtracted to image the residuals." (Visibility analysis); "The residuals contain typically 12-22% of the peak image, similar to the best fits of (cite: Cazzoletti2018) for HD135344B." (Visibility analysis); "For HD 142527 it was not possible to find convergence" (Visibility analysis)
3. **Estimate gas-gap radii**: Measure peaks of normalized azimuthally averaged integrated 13CO profiles; infer R_CO and R_gap using Facchini2017gaps relations; use target-specific alternatives when unresolved.  
  > "we re-evaluate the gas surface density profiles and the gap radii $R_{\rm gap}$ by analyzing the normalized azimuthal averaged intensity profiles of $^{13}$CO for each target" (Gas structure); "As an alternative, we measure the location of $R_{\rm peak}$, the peak in the integrated $^{13}$CO emission." (Gas structure); "For HD 169142, DM Tau, HD 163296 and TW Hya no gas gaps were resolved and the gap locations are estimated to be located in between the dust ring radii." (Gas structure)
   - parameter: R_peak approximately 1.4 R_CO; R_CO/R_gap = 1.3 for 5–15 M_Jup.  
  > "Inspection of the results in (cite: Facchini2017gaps) shows that $R_{\rm peak}$ is approximately 1.4 times larger than $R_{\rm CO}$." (Gas structure); "for planet masses 5-15 $M_{\rm Jup}$ the ratio $R_{\rm CO}/R_{\rm gap}$ = 1.3." (Gas structure)
   - assumption: All disks harbor gas gaps; radial gap shape assumed similar even if eccentric.  
  > "we assume in this study that all disks in fact harbor gas gaps." (Gas structure); "we have to make the assumption here that the radial gap shape remains similar." (Gas structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Conversion also recovers the directly visible PDS70 12CO gap at almost the same location.  
  > "We notice that the ratios above still recover the gap radius at almost the exact same location for the $R_{\rm peak}$ of $^{12}$CO in this case." (Gas structure)
4. **Construct comparable gas surface density profiles**: Gaussian profiles centered on R_dust, with inner width consistent with R_gap; scale to literature densities at peak, gap and outer disk.  
  > "we present the estimated gas surface density profile, described as a Gaussian centered on $R_{\rm dust}$ and an inner width consistent with the $R_{\rm gap}$ location." (Gas gaps); "The profile is scaled to the derived gas surface density profile from the literature based on combined $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O data" (Gas gaps); "to match the surface density at peak, gap and outer disk locations." (Gas gaps)
   - parameter: Literature gap radii rescaled to Gaia distances.  
  > "The gap radii in the literature profiles were rescaled to the *Gaia* distances." (Gas gaps)
   - parameter: DM Tau and SR21: R_peak estimated at approximately 75% of dust-ring radius.  
  > "For DM Tau and SR 21, no $R_{\rm peak}$ could be measured from the CO profile and it was estimated to be located at $\sim$75% of the dust ring radius." (Gas gaps)
   - assumption: R_dust marks the outer gas-gap edge and pressure maximum.  
  > "$R_{dust}$ is chosen as the outer edge of the gas gap as the pressure maximum is thought to be located there." (Gas gaps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Overplot literature profiles and mark radii inside the beam radius as unconstrained.  
  > "The literature surface density profile is overplotted as blue-dotted line" (Gas gaps); "We indicate the unconstrained regime with a gray area between 0 and the beam radius" (Gas gaps)
5. **Estimate inner gas density from accretion**: Compute Sigma_acc at 1 au from stellar accretion rate using the stated viscous-disk surface density equation and irradiation temperature law.  
  > "The expected gas surface density close to the star can be estimated indirectly assuming a viscous disk model from the stellar accretion rate by (cite: Manara2014)" (Gas gaps); "We compute the expected local gas surface density $\Sigma_{acc}$ at 1 au derived from the accretion rate assuming $\alpha=10^{-3}$" (Gas gaps)
   - parameter: alpha = 10^-3; flaring angle phi = 0.02; radius 1 au.  
  > "$\phi$ the flaring angle (taken as 0.02)" (Gas gaps); "We compute the expected local gas surface density $\Sigma_{acc}$ at 1 au derived from the accretion rate assuming $\alpha=10^{-3}$" (Gas gaps)
   - assumption: Viscous disk; Dullemond2001 temperature profile; stellar properties from Francis2020.  
  > "assuming a viscous disk model" (Gas gaps); "the temperature profile, for which we use (cite: Dullemond2001)" (Gas gaps); "The stellar properties are taken from (cite: Francis2020)." (Gas gaps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Overplot accretion-based density on reconstructed gas profiles.  
  > "and overplot this value on the derived profiles" (Gas gaps)
6. **Compare azimuthal width with observational Stokes number**: Evaluate St_obs = lambda_obs rho_s / [4 Sigma_gas(R_dust)] and compare with visibility-derived azimuthal FWHM.  
  > "we construct a plot of the azimuthal FWHM as a function of Stokes number of the traced dust grains, for the radial location of the dust." (Stokes numbers); "St_{obs} = \frac{\lambda_{\rm obs}\rho_s}{4\Sigma_{\rm gas}(R_{\rm dust})}" (Stokes numbers)
   - parameter: Intrinsic dust density 1 g cm^-3.  
  > "with $\rho_s$ the intrinsic dust density (taken as 1 g cm$^{-3}$)" (Sample)
   - parameter: Factor-3 uncertainty on gas surface density.  
  > "For the uncertainties we assume an uncertainty of a factor 3 on $\Sigma_{\rm gas}(r)$" (Stokes numbers)
   - assumption: Characteristic emitting grain size lambda_obs/2pi; similar grain-size distributions across the sample.  
  > "particles with size $a_{\rm grain}=\lambda_{obs}/2\pi$ (cite: Draine2006) are the primary contributor at observing wavelength $\lambda_{obs}$" (Stokes numbers); "As it is reasonable that the grain size distributions are similar across the sample" (Stokes numbers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Image-derived azimuthal contrasts also distinguish symmetric and asymmetric disks, but the trend is not pursued because of possible imaging artefacts.  
  > "In addition we show the dependence of the azimuthal contrast from the images on the observational Stokes number which also shows a distinction between symmetric and asymmetric disks." (Stokes numbers); "As this trend might be affected by imaging artefacts it is not further discussed." (Stokes numbers)
7. **Model the width–Stokes trend**: Construct grain-size distributions, solve azimuthal drift–mixing equilibrium, then calculate optical-depth-dependent dust intensity profiles and their FWHM.  
  > "1) constructing a particle size distribution, 2) calculation of the azimuthal equilibrium density distribution of each particle size according to (cite: Birnstiel2013) and 3) calculating the dust intensity profiles and thus the azimuthal dust intensity contrast." (Stokes numbers); "The final azimuthal FWHM is computed directly for the intensity profile of the model." (Stokes numbers)
   - parameter: Fiducial alpha = 10^-3; gas-to-dust ratio = 10; gas contrast = 1.2; a_max = 1 mm; azimuthal sigma_y = 10 degrees.  
  > "The fiducial model (orange) shows a possible combination based on a manual fitting procedure: $\alpha=10^{-3}$, gas-to-dust ratio = 10, gas contrast = 1.2, $a_{\rm max}$=1 mm and azimuthal $\sigma_y$ of 10$^{\circ}$." (Stokes numbers)
   - parameter: Compare truncated MRN and steady-state size distributions; vary fragmentation velocity, viscosity, gas-to-dust ratio, gas contrast and gas extent.  
  > "first a truncated power-law size distribution with an MRN exponent (cite: Mathis1977) up to a maximum particle size $a_\mathrm{max}$ and secondly, the steady-state distributions of (cite: Birnstiel2011)." (Stokes numbers); "We explore a number of parameters, including $\alpha$, gas-to-dust ratio, gas contrast and gas azimuthal extent" (Stokes numbers); "Furthermore we explore whether the FWHM is set by either fragmentation (different fragmentation velocities) or by a default grain size distribution with a maximum grain size." (Stokes numbers)
   - assumption: Constant gas density plus Gaussian overdensity; absorption opacity; face-on, vertically isothermal disk.  
  > "parameterized the azimuthal gas density as a constant density plus a Gaussian overdensity" (Stokes numbers); "The emission profile was then calculated assuming absoption opacity and a face-on, vertically isothermal disk" (Stokes numbers)
   - fitting: Manual fitting; neighboring parameter values plotted to illustrate dependence.  
  > "and find the best fit, accompanied by two values on either side to show the dependence of the curve on the parameter." (Stokes numbers); "a possible combination based on a manual fitting procedure" (Stokes numbers)
   - validation: Model reproduces the observed step-like trend; both grain-size prescriptions can reproduce the curve.  
  > "found a step-like trend which can be matched to a simple dust evolution model with an azimuthal pressure bump in the gas." (Diversity asymmetries); "Both the equilibrium model using a fragmentation velocity and a grain size distribution with a fixed maximum grain size can reproduce the curve." (Diversity asymmetries)
8. **Assess warps from gas kinematics**: Inspect first-moment CO velocity maps for warps; use 12CO where available, otherwise 13CO.  
  > "We compare the kinematics of each disk in the first moment map of the $^{12}$CO emission" (Kinematics); "We use the $^{12}$CO data where available, and otherwise $^{13}$CO." (Kinematics)
   - parameter: Assess SNR, spectral resolution and beam/R_gap.  
  > "The detectability of a warp is determined by a combination of signal-to-noise ratio, spectral resolution and spatial resolution compared with the companion orbit radius." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Inner velocity twist can indicate misalignment/warp; radial flows are observationally difficult to distinguish.  
  > "A twist pattern (deviation from Keplerian rotation) in the inner part of the disk points towards a misalignment between inner and outer disk, or a warp." (Gas structure); "it is almost impossible to distinguish observationally from a misalignment (cite: Rosenfeld2014)" (Gas structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Four warp detections confirm literature results; assess nondetections against observing limitations.  
  > "In 4 disks (IRS 48, HD 142527, MWC 758, J1604-2130) a warp is clearly detected, confirming the results from the literature (cite: Calcino2019; Casassus2013; Boehler2018; Mayama2018)." (Kinematics); "For the other disks no warp is detected, but this is possibly due to the low spatial resolution compared to the location of the gap." (Kinematics)
9. **Constrain companions and asymmetry mechanisms**: Compare dust-ring/gas-gap separation with planet-model relations and literature companion contrast limits at the inferred gap locations.  
  > "In order to estimate the companion mass assuming a single planet, the millimeter dust radius $R_{\rm dust}$ is compared with the derived gas gap radius $R_{\rm gap}$."    - assumption: Single circular-orbit companion for the Facchini relation; compare eccentric Super-Jovian and multiple-companion alternatives.  
  > "for the assumption that the gaps are cleared by a single companion on a circular orbit." (Implications for companions); "We hypothesize that the wide transition disk cavities in our sample are also caused by eccentric, Super-Jovian planets" (Implications for companions); "It is also possible that multiple companions (such as seen in PDS70) are responsible for the wide gaps." (Implications for companions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check consistency with direct-imaging limits; circular model calibration stops at 15 M_Jup and excludes eccentric orbits.  
  > "as no models were run for higher mass companions, this relation cannot be used to estimate accurate masses for these ratios." (Gas gaps); "Also, Facchini et al. do not consider eccentric orbits which significantly increase the separation between the dust ring and the gas gap (cite: Muley2019)." (Gas gaps); "On the other hand, the contrast curves generally rule out companion masses $>50 M_{\rm Jup}$ at the gap location." (Implications for companions)

  > "When no spiral arms were detected, the pitch angle is set to 1$^{\circ}$." (Link with asymmetries); "No difference is made between ‘single’ and ‘double’ spirals as the secondary spiral can easily be hidden in part of the disk." (Link with asymmetries)
   - parameter: Micron-sized grains evaluated over scattered-light feature radii using the gas profiles.  
  > "the Stokes number of a $\mu$m-sized dust grain is computed using the gas surface density profile in that range" (Link with asymmetries)
   - assumption: Ages, NIR excess and stellar masses from Garufi2018.  
  > "Ages, NIR excess and stellar masses are taken from (cite: Garufi2018)." (Link with asymmetries)

  > "Targets are taken from the scattered light demographics study by (cite: Garufi2018) for which high resolution ALMA data are available in (cite: Francis2020) and (cite: Andrews2018dsharp) to estimate the dust gap width." ## Software

- **galario** : Fourier transform and sample continuum models for real/imaginary visibility fitting.  
  > "The fitting is performed in the visibility plane comparing both the Real and Imaginary components, using the `galario` tool set to Fourier transform and sample the model (cite: Tazzari2018)." (Visibility analysis)
- **emcee** : MCMC fitting of asymmetric continuum visibility models.  
  > "followed by a fit to the visibilities with the Markov-chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) code `emcee` (cite: Foreman2013) for constraining each asymmetry." (Visibility analysis)
- **DALI** : Named as an example of physical-chemical CO modeling to derive gas surface density; execution in this study not stated.  
  > "Gas surface density profiles can be derived from spatially resolved CO isotopologue data in combination with physical-chemical modeling" (Sample); "e.g. DALI (cite: Bruderer2012; Bruderer2013)." (Sample)
- **BT-SETTL** : Evolutionary models underlying adopted literature companion mass limits.  
  > "The mass upper limit curves are computed in these works by comparing the brightness limit with evolutionary models such as BT-SETTL (cite: Allard2014) or COND (cite: Baraffe2003) assuming the age of the system and hot-start models." ## Qualitative findings

- Dust asymmetries are linked to low local gas surface density through observational Stokes number.  
  > "Asymmetries in the dust appear to be linked to a low local gas surface density through the observational Stokes number." (Conclusions)
- Single-ring observations do not distinguish vortices from gas horseshoes.  
  > "Current dust and gas observations cannot distinguish between vortices (caused by planetary companions) and gas horseshoes (caused by (sub)stellar companions with a mass ratio $q>$0.05) in single-ring systems." (Conclusions)
- Gas horseshoes cannot explain multiple asymmetries or asymmetric structures in multiple rings.  
  > "Gas horseshoes only appear at the inner edge of a wide, eccentric gap and are unable to trigger secondary asymmetries" (Horseshoe, RWI or spiral?); "so they can be excluded for asymmetries in disks with multiple rings with asymmetries and multiple asymmetries" (Horseshoe, RWI or spiral?)
- AB Aur and MWC758 show evidence of eccentric dust cavities through offset inner disks.  
  > "Both MWC 758 and AB Aur show a clear indication of an eccentric dust cavity, as the inner dust disk detected in the ALMA continuum image is offset from the center of the disk." (Dust structure)
- The observed diversity does not require short vortex or horseshoe lifetimes.  
  > "The diversity in asymmetries does not require dissipation of vortices or gas horseshoes and their lifetimes may be much longer than previously thought." (Conclusions)
- Detected scattered-light spirals preferentially accompany luminous stars and wide gaps.  
  > "The detection of spiral arms in scattered light images is linked to high luminosity stars with wide gaps" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Require modeled, resolved CO isotopologues and radially resolved dust rings; choose archive programs balancing resolution and SNR.  
  > "We select transition disks for which the gas surface density has been derived using detailed CO modeling and resolved CO isotopologue observations, preferably a combination of $^{13}$CO and optically thin C$^{18}$O." (Sample); "Furthermore, we require that the dust rings are at least marginally spatially resolved in the radial direction, in order to make a proper assessment of the azimuthal structure in the dust." (Sample); "The programs with the best combination of spatial resolution and signal-to-noise ratio are chosen for this study" (Sample)
- Why this method: Use visibility-derived widths because image contrasts depend on beam and SNR; use CO peaks because gap edges are unresolved.  
  > "The azimuthal contrast in the images cannot be compared reliably across the different images, as its value depends on the beam size and SNR along the ring." (Dust structure); "The low spatial resolution of our $^{13}$CO observations does not allow to measure this parameter directly" (Gas structure); "As an alternative, we measure the location of $R_{\rm peak}$, the peak in the integrated $^{13}$CO emission." (Gas structure)
- Alternative: Vortices, gas horseshoes and spiral density waves considered as gas-asymmetry origins; millimeter features belonging to spiral waves remain uncertain.  
  > "The disks with single dust rings and asymmetric features (HD 142527, IRS 48 and AB Aur) could still be explained by either gas horseshoe or vortices as the result of Rossby Wave Instability." (Horseshoe, RWI or spiral?); "Whether asymmetric features in the millimeter continuum really can be part of a spiral density wave remains a question." (Horseshoe, RWI or spiral?)
- Alternative: Fixed maximum grain size and fragmentation-limited distributions both fit the trend.  
  > "Both the equilibrium model using a fragmentation velocity and a grain size distribution with a fixed maximum grain size can reproduce the curve." (Diversity asymmetries)
- Alternative: Circular brown-dwarf companions, eccentric Super-Jovians and multiple companions considered for wide gaps.  
  > "This limits the companion masses (at $R_{\rm gap}$) at the gap location to the brown dwarf regime." (Implications for companions); "We hypothesize that the wide transition disk cavities in our sample are also caused by eccentric, Super-Jovian planets" (Implications for companions); "It is also possible that multiple companions (such as seen in PDS70) are responsible for the wide gaps." (Implications for companions)
- Alternative: Velocity twists can arise from misalignment or radial flows; maps do not provide quantitative companion masses.  
  > "it is almost impossible to distinguish observationally from a misalignment (cite: Rosenfeld2014)" (Gas structure); "no quantitative information can be derived from these maps." (Gas structure)
- How they decided it was enough: Simple intensity modeling judged sufficient to reproduce the trend; HD142527 fit accepted despite weak constraints.  
  > "Although this is a rather simple approach, it is sufficient for the purpose of reproducing the trend of azimuthal extent w.r.t. observational Stokes number." (Stokes numbers); "The best-fit parameters are thus not as well constrained as the other disks, but sufficient for our purposes." (Visibility analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Most observations cannot distinguish an inner gas gap from a cavity.  
  > "This representation reveals that for none of the disks, except PDS 70, the distinction between a gas gap or gas cavity can be made." (Gas gaps)
- Limitation (authors): CO-derived gas densities remain uncertain because of the carbon budget; AB Aur's constant-abundance profile is especially uncertain.  
  > "The derivation of gas surface density from CO isotopologue data remains uncertain, in particular due to problems with our knowledge of the carbon budget in disks (cite: Kama2016; Miotello2019)." (Stokes numbers); "For AB Aur no detailed physical-chemical model was used and the CO abundance was taken to be constant throughout the disk, so the derived profile remains highly uncertain." (Gas gaps)
- Limitation (authors): HD142527 visibility fit did not converge; Gaussian models leave substantial residual structure.  
  > "For HD 142527 it was not possible to find convergence" (Visibility analysis); "These residuals are due to the structures that cannot be well represented by a simple double Gaussian as used to model the asymmetry." (Visibility analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Circular-orbit planet models lack higher-mass calibration and eccentric CO predictions.  
  > "as no models were run for higher mass companions, this relation cannot be used to estimate accurate masses for these ratios." (Gas gaps); "Unfortunately no grid of models exists for planet-disk interaction models including eccentricity with predictions for the CO emission" (Gas structure)
- Limitation (authors): Companion limits depend on evolutionary models, age, extinction and circumplanetary emission; inner separations may be unconstrained.  
  > "Also extinction, age estimate and choice of evolutionary model may affect the derived mass estimates and limits." (Companions); "The evolutionary models for upper limits and most candidates do not include contributions from a circumplanetary disk (CPD)" (Companions); "However, high mass ratios are still possible in the inner parts of the disk where no contrast could be measured" (Implications for companions)
- Limitation (authors): Dust-trapping model parameters are degenerate.  
  > "Several parameters are redundant with each other: a lower viscosity requires a lower gas overdensity and/or gas-to-dust ratio." (Diversity asymmetries)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is biased toward long-lived disks; spiral detectability depends on sensitivity, resolution and geometry.  
  > "The sample is intrinsically biased as it only contains disks with ages $\sim$4-10 Myr" (Link with asymmetries); "the detection of spirals in these disks is hindered by the observational sensitivity, angular resolution, and disk geometry, respectively." (Link with gaps and stellar properties)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The common Stokes-number threshold depends on heterogeneous literature gas models and a shared characteristic-grain approximation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gap radii inferred from fixed model ratios should not be treated as independently resolved companion orbits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): HD142527's unconverged width fit weakens its use as a precise answer key.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assigning 360-degree widths and 1-degree nondetection pitch angles mixes conventions with measurements.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual luminosity/gap-width thresholds do not establish a selection-corrected physical boundary.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The manual dust-model fit demonstrates plausibility rather than unique parameter recovery.
