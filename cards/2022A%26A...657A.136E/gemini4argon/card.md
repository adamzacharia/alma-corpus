## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate interferometric flux recovery for NOEMA HCN isotopologue cubes**: Extract H13CN and HC15N spectra from the NOEMA cube within a 28 arcsec polygon matching the IRAM 30m beam and compare to velocity-resampled IRAM 30m single-dish spectra  
  > "To this end, we extracted the HCN lines from the NOEMA cube within a polygon corresponding to the beam size of the single-dish observations (i.e.$~\sim$ 28$^{\prime\prime}$); this was compared to the IRAM 30m spectrum that had been resampled in velocity to match the resolution of our NOEMA observations (i.e.$~\sim 6.9$ km s$^{-1}$)." (Observations)
   - software: GILDAS MAPPING, GILDAS CLASS
   - parameter: Polygon size ~ 28 arcsec; velocity resolution resampled to ~6.9 km s^-1  
  > "To this end, we extracted the HCN lines from the NOEMA cube within a polygon corresponding to the beam size of the single-dish observations (i.e.$~\sim$ 28$^{\prime\prime}$); this was compared to the IRAM 30m spectrum that had been resampled in velocity to match the resolution of our NOEMA observations (i.e.$~\sim 6.9$ km s$^{-1}$)." (Observations)
   - assumption: Flux filtering affects H13CN and HC15N homogeneously if their spatial distributions are similar  
  > "As a result of the homogeneous effect of flux filtering on both lines, this caveat will have negligible impact on the calculated $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratios, assuming that their distribution is similar." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Approximately 20% of the flux is recovered for both H13CN and HC15N  
  > "The resulting comparison can be seen in Fig. 1 and showed that we recovered approximately 20% of the flux from both the H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{15}$N observations." (Observations)
2. **Define sub-region polygons and extract H13CN, HC15N, N2H+, 15NNH+, and N15NH+ spectra**: Identify five gaseous cores with average H13CN (1-0) emission > 3 sigma rms, decompose by eye into non-overlapping polygons (NW, NE, S, E, FIR3), extract spectra in Jy using GILDAS MAPPING, and convert to temperature units (K) using GILDAS CLASS  
  > "The H$^{13}$CN ($1-0$) emission map suggests the presence of five gaseous cores in which the average emission is above 3$\sigma$ rms (see Fig. 4)." (Observations); "Using the MAPPING package of GILDAS (see Sect. 3), the spectra were extracted in flux density units (Jy) from the five regions illustrated in Fig. 4, which have been labelled depending on the position of the peak with respect to the phase centre, as: north-west (NW), north-east (NE), south (S), east (E) and FIR3." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis); "The CLASS package of GILDAS was employed to convert our spectra into temperature units (K) before they were imported into CASSIS." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis)
   - software: GILDAS MAPPING, GILDAS CLASS, CASSIS
   - parameter: Detection threshold > 3 sigma rms on average emission map (-1 to 18 km s^-1 for H13CN, 5 to 15 km s^-1 for HC15N)  
  > "Figure 2 shows maps of the emission of both the H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{15}$N ($1-0$) lines averaged over the channels with signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) larger than 3, corresponding to an approximate velocity range of -1 to 18km s$^{-1}$ for H$^{13}$CN and 5 to 15 km s$^{-1}$ for HC$^{15}$N." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Verify LTE condition by calculating critical densities**: Calculate critical density n_CRIT = A_ul / C_ul at 40 K for each transition and compare with the average H2 volume density of OMC-2 FIR4  
  > "In order to check our LTE assumption, which is satisfied if the H$_2$ density is larger than the critical density of the analysed transition, we computed the critical density of each of our lines from the equation:" (Extraction of the spectra and analysis); "The critical density at 40 K was calculated to be 1.62$\times$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$ for H$^{13}$CN, 1.59$\times$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$ for HC$^{15}$N, 1.61$\times$10$^5$ cm$^{-3}$ for $^{15}$NNH$^+$, 1.66$\times$10$^5$ cm$^{-3}$ for N$^{15}$NH$^+$ and 1.77$\times$10$^5$ cm$^{-3}$ for N$_2$H$^+$." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Temperature = 40 K; average H2 density = 1.2x10^6 cm^-3  
  > "In the case of N$_2$H$^+$ and its isotopologues, we can safely assume LTE as these values for critical density are smaller than the average H$_2$ volume density of OMC-2 FIR4 (1.2$\times$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$; (cite: Ceccarelli2014b))" (Extraction of the spectra and analysis)
   - assumption: 15N and 13C isotopologues have the same collisional coefficients as their main isotopologues for the 1-0 transition  
  > "For the $1-0$ transition we can assume that the $^{15}$N isotopologues (and $^{13}$C-isotopologues) have the same collisional coefficients as their respective main isotopologues." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Critical densities for N2H+ are below the average H2 volume density, and for HCN isotopologues are of the same order of magnitude, validating the LTE assumption  
  > "for H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{15}$N, the calculated critical density values are the same order of magnitude as the volume density and so the approximation of LTE conditions is reasonable." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis)
4. **Fit H13CN and HC15N spectra and derive HCN 14N/15N ratios**: Fit single Gaussians in CASSIS using Levenberg-Marquardt fitting to obtain FWHM, then perform LTE radiative transfer modelling at fixed upper and lower excitation temperatures to determine column densities and 14N/15N ratios  
  > "As can be seen in Fig. 8, the lines of H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{15}$N show generally a Gaussian shape; hence, each line was fitted with a single Gaussian in CASSIS using Levenberg-Marquardt (L-M) fitting." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis); "The column density was repeatedly varied until the model matched the observations, taking into account a 10% calibration error in the latter." (HCN isotopologues)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: T_ex = 35 K and 45 K for FIR4 regions; T_ex = 100 K and 150 K for FIR3; T_bg = 2.73 K; calibration error = 10%  
  > "Therefore, the spectra were modelled for two fixed temperatures, corresponding to the range of temperatures found for FIR4 using previous data: an upper temperature of 45 K and a lower temperature of 35 K (taken from (cite: Ceccarelli2014b), (cite: Favre2018)). For FIR3, the upper temperature was fixed at 150 K and the lower temperature was fixed at 100 K (assuming that FIR3 is a hot corino; (cite: Tobin2019), Ceccarelli et al. in prep.)." (HCN isotopologues); "We take the background temperature (T$_{\rm bg}$) as that of the cosmic microwave background (CMB; i.e. 2.73 K)." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis)
   - assumption: Lines are optically thin (opacities 8.1x10^-4 to 1.6x10^-2) and in LTE; 12C/13C = 50 +- 5  
  > "Our analysis obtained opacities ranging from 8.1$\times$10$^{-4}$ to 1.6$\times$10$^{-2}$, enabling us to assume that the lines are optically thin and use the method described in Sect. 4.1." (HCN isotopologues); "However, the column density ratio $N_{\rm TOT}$(H$^{13}$CN)/$N_{\rm TOT}$(HC$^{15}$N) has to be corrected by the $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio, which was assumed to be 50$\pm$5 as measured by (cite: Kahane2018) using CN, HCN and HC$_3$N lines." (HCN isotopologues)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt single Gaussian fitting for line parameters, followed by manual grid variation of N_TOT at fixed T_ex  
  > "hence, each line was fitted with a single Gaussian in CASSIS using Levenberg-Marquardt (L-M) fitting." (Extraction of the spectra and analysis); "The column density was repeatedly varied until the model matched the observations, taking into account a 10% calibration error in the latter." (HCN isotopologues)
   - validation: Derived 14N/15N ratios in inner FIR4 regions are compared and found consistent with the low-angular-resolution single-dish value of 270 +- 50 from Kahane et al. (2018)  
  > "In fact, the derived $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratios are consistent with each other (within the uncertainties) as well as with the value obtained at low angular resolution by (cite: Kahane2018): 270$\pm$50." (HCN)
5. **Fit N2H+, 15NNH+, and N15NH+ hyperfine spectra and derive column densities using CLASS HFS routine**: Fit hyperfine structure using the CLASS HFS routine, obtain total integrated intensity W (by summing all HF components, scaling the strongest component by 9/5 for faint 15N isotopologues, or scaling the isolated faintest J'F'-JF = 01-12 component by 27/3 for optically thick N2H+), and compute column densities assuming optically thin LTE  
  > "Due to the aforementioned presence of HFS, we first used the specific HFS fitting routine that is provided within CLASS to model our spectra." (CLASS); "therefore, we divided its integrated intensity by $\frac{5}{9}$ in order to obtain the total integrated intensity, $W$, for the $^{15}$N isotopologues." (CLASS); "We measured its integrated intensity and divided it by the factor $\frac{3}{27}$ to derive the total one." (CLASS)
   - software: GILDAS CLASS
   - parameter: T_ex = 35 K and 45 K; T_bg = 2.73 K; FWHM = 0.8 km s^-1; velocity resolution = 0.2 km s^-1; cal = 10%  
  > "We fixed $T_{\rm ex}$ to 35 K and 45 K ((cite: Ceccarelli2014b), (cite: Favre2018)." (CLASS); "From fitting our spectra using L-M fitting in CASSIS, FWHM is obtained as 0.8 km s$^{-1}$." (CLASS)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin conditions hold for 15N isotopologues and for the isolated faintest HF component (3/27 weight) of N2H+  
  > "For the $^{15}$N isotopologues, the opacity derived from the fit confirms that we have optically thin lines ($\tau \leq 0.1$) in all but one case: N$^{15}$NH$^+$ towards the NW region." (CLASS); "However, the faintest component, $J'F'-JF=01\rightarrow12$, has, according to the relative intensity ratio, an opacity that is $\frac{3}{27}$ of the total, providing opacities lower than 1 in all spectra." (CLASS)
   - fitting: CLASS HFS fitting routine; for E Region where only main component is visible, single Gaussian fit to the main HF component  
  > "Due to the aforementioned presence of HFS, we first used the specific HFS fitting routine that is provided within CLASS to model our spectra." (CLASS); "Therefore, we fitted the main HF component (the only one detected) with a single Gaussian with the resultant brightness temperature" (Results, N2H+ (E Region))
   - validation: Compared against independent CASSIS MCMC HFS fitting results  
  > "The values obtained with the two methods are the same within error bars except for the cases with higher opacity (i.e. NW Region) or with low S/N (i.e. E Region in both $^{15}$N isotopologues and S Region in N$^{15}$NH$^+$)." (CASSIS)
6. **Fit N2H+, 15NNH+, and N15NH+ spectra using CASSIS MCMC LTE modelling and derive N2H+ 14N/15N ratios**: Run Jython LTE modelling scripts (LineAnalysisScripting.py) in CASSIS using MCMC to fit N_TOT, T_ex, FWHM, and V_LSR; for three very low S/N spectra (E Region of both 15N isotopologues and S Region of N15NH+), fix V_LSR from L-M Gaussian fit and T_ex to 35-45 K and vary N_TOT manually  
  > "Within CASSIS, there is a Python library (LineAnalysisScripting.py) that allows users to create Jython scripts to model sources in LTE using the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method." (CASSIS); "We used this value to fix V$_{LSR}$ in a line analysis while fixing T$_{ex}$ to 35 K and 45 K to give upper and lower limits. The N$_{TOT}$ was then varied manually until good fits were obtained, taking into account a 15% calibration error." (CASSIS)
   - software: CASSIS, LineAnalysisScripting.py
   - parameter: N_TOT: 10^10 to 10^15 cm^-2 (15N isotopologues), 10^12 to 10^15 cm^-2 (main N2H+); FWHM: 0.3 to 1.2 km s^-1; T_ex: 30 to 50 K (or +-3 sigma of main isotopologue for 15N); V_LSR: 8 to 15 km s^-1  
  > "Therefore, N$_{TOT}$ is varied between 10$^{10}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ for the $^{15}$N isotopologues and between 10$^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ for the main isotopologue." (CASSIS); "From this we also vary FWHM between 0.3 km s$^{-1}$ and 1.2 km s$^{-1}$ in each case. For T$_{ex}$, once again we use previous research of this source to determine our upper and lower limits: 50 K and 30 K, respectively" (CASSIS); "Also from previous research, the accepted value for the V$_{LSR}$ of OMC-2 FIR4 is 11.4 km s$^{-1}$ (e.g. (cite: Kahane2018), (cite: Ceccarelli2017)); therefore, this parameter is varied between 8 and 15 km s$^{-1}$." (CASSIS)
   - assumption: LTE conditions  
  > "Within CASSIS, there is a Python library (LineAnalysisScripting.py) that allows users to create Jython scripts to model sources in LTE" (CASSIS)
   - fitting: MCMC sampler with bounded priors in CASSIS; manual variation with L-M V_LSR fit for low-S/N spectra  
  > "using the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method. As part of the script, we define a number of parameters that will be varied and give upper and lower limits for the variation range." (CASSIS)
   - validation: Cross-checked between CASSIS MCMC and CLASS HFS methods; double peak in S Region checked by comparing peak velocities of isolated F1=0-1 N2H+ component with 15N isotopologues to rule out self-absorption  
  > "The values obtained with the two methods are the same within error bars except for the cases with higher opacity (i.e. NW Region) or with low S/N (i.e. E Region in both $^{15}$N isotopologues and S Region in N$^{15}$NH$^+$)." (CASSIS); "Therefore, we can conclude that the double peak seen both in N$^{15}$NH$^+$ and in the isolated HF component of N$_2$H$^+$ is likely due to two velocity features along the line of sight." (N$_2$H$^+$)

## Qualitative findings

- H13CN (1-0) emission is systematically stronger than HC15N (1-0) across OMC-2 FIR4 and FIR3, with both showing a central emission hole in FIR4 that is absent in continuum and other molecular tracers.  
  > "It can be seen from these maps that the H$^{13}$CN ($1-0$) emission is ubiquitously stronger than the HC$^{15}$N ($1-0$) emission across all regions of both FIR4 and FIR3." (Observations); "It should also be noted that there is a hole in the H$^{13}$CN (and possibly HC$^{15}$N) emission towards the central region of FIR4 that is absent from the continuum emission and from any molecular line studied so far" (Observations)
- Local changes in physical conditions and cosmic-ray ionisation rate at core scales (~600-860 au) in OMC-2 FIR4 do not significantly affect the 14N/15N ratio in either HCN or N2H+.  
  > "These results suggest that local changes in the physical parameters occurring on the small linear scales probed by our observations in the protocluster do not seem to affect the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio in either HCN or N$_2$H$^+$ and hence that this is independent of the molecule used." (abstract); "Moreover, the high level of irradiation due to cosmic rays does not affect the N fractionation either." (abstract)
- Selective photodissociation does not appear to regulate nitrogen fractionation at the linear scales probed in OMC-2 FIR4, likely because the source is deeply embedded.  
  > "The absence of significant variations in both species also indicates that selective photodissociation has no effect at the linear scales probed by our observations." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: OMC-2 FIR4 is one of the closest analogues to the birth environment of the Solar System, and combining NOEMA HCN isotopologue observations with archival ALMA N2H+ isotopologue data provides the first high-angular-resolution comparison between HCN and N2H+ (which should behave oppositely under selective photodissociation).  
  > "In order to better investigate this, we obtained observations of isotopologues of HCN towards OMC-2 FIR4 in order to provide the first high angular resolution comparison of the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio between HCN and N$_2$H$^+$ using interferometric data in one of the best analogues of the environment in which our SS was born." (Introduction); "The analysis of these spectra will allow us to compare, for the first time at high angular resolution, the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio simultaneously in HCN and N$_2$H$^+$ (i.e. the two molecules in which nitrogen fractionation should behave in an opposite way) if both are regulated by selective photodissociation." (Observations)
- Why this method: H13CN is used instead of main HCN because HCN is expected to be optically thick; for optically thick main N2H+, the isolated faintest hyperfine component (J'F'-JF = 01-12, 3/27 of total opacity) is used because it is optically thin and unblended; both CLASS HFS and CASSIS MCMC methods are applied to cross-check N2H+ column densities.  
  > "It should be noted that H$^{13}$CN is potentially better than HCN for computing the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio because HCN is expected to be optically thick." (HCN isotopologues); "However, the faintest component, $J'F'-JF=01\rightarrow12$, has, according to the relative intensity ratio, an opacity that is $\frac{3}{27}$ of the total, providing opacities lower than 1 in all spectra. In addition, this component is well separated from the others in the spectrum, therefore its integrated intensity is not contaminated by emission from any other component." (CLASS)
- Alternative: Using the HCN-specific 12C/13C ratio of 36 +- 15 from Kahane et al. (2018) instead of the average value of 50 +- 5; rejected because the H13C15N detection was weak (2.5 sigma) and likely underestimated the ratio.  
  > "The $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio measured from HCN specifically in this paper is tentative; the authors calculated their HCN-specific value of 36$\pm$15 by comparing HC$^{15}$N to H$^{13}$C$^{15}$N. They point out in this paper that due to the weakness (2.5$\sigma$) of the H$^{13}$C$^{15}$N detection, it is possible that the obtained $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio is underestimated (see (cite: Kahane2018) for further discussion). Taking this into account, we decided to use the average value of 50$\pm$5." (HCN isotopologues)
- Alternative: Using CASSIS MCMC fitting for the E Region 15NNH+ and N15NH+ spectra and S Region N15NH+ spectrum; rejected because low S/N caused MCMC to underestimate N_TOT and overestimate 14N/15N, replaced by manual fitting with fixed V_LSR and T_ex.  
  > "For the latter, the fit obtained using MCMC with the aforementioned limits was not optimal and underestimated N$_{TOT}$ with respect to the observations, causing overestimations of the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio. Therefore, we concluded that these three spectra had insufficient S/N to be analysed using MCMC" (CASSIS)
- Alternative: Self-absorption as the cause of the double peak in the S Region N2H+ F1=0-1 component and N15NH+; rejected in favour of two overlapping velocity features along the line of sight because the peak velocities match between the optically thin 15N isotopologues and N2H+.  
  > "To probe whether the profile observed in the F$_1=0-1$ component of N$_2$H$^+$ and in N$^{15}$NH$^+$ is due to multiple velocity features or self-absorption, we compared the velocities of the two peaks in the F$_1=0-1$ component of N$_2$H$^+$ ($1-0$) with those of N$^{15}$NH$^+$ and $^{15}$NNH$^+$." (N$_2$H$^+$); "Therefore, we can conclude that the double peak seen both in N$^{15}$NH$^+$ and in the isolated HF component of N$_2$H$^+$ is likely due to two velocity features along the line of sight." (N$_2$H$^+$)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): About 80% of the single-dish flux is filtered out by the NOEMA interferometer (only ~20% recovered), though this is argued to affect both isotopologues equally.  
  > "The resulting comparison can be seen in Fig. 1 and showed that we recovered approximately 20% of the flux from both the H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{15}$N observations." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Only a single transition (1-0) was observed for each species, so excitation temperature T_ex cannot be constrained directly from the data and must be assumed from previous literature.  
  > "Due to the fact that we only observed one transition for both H$^{13}$CN and HC$^{15}$N, the excitation temperature ($T_{\rm ex}$) cannot be derived directly from the data and it has to be assumed." (HCN isotopologues); "More specifically, in the case of FIR4, the observation of higher transitions will allow us to better constrain parameters such as $T_{\rm ex}$ and $N_{\rm TOT}$ in order to create more accurate models to compare with our observations in both LTE and non-LTE." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): N2H+ and 15NNH+ are undetected towards FIR3 (likely resolved out or destroyed by CO), preventing a derivation of the N2H+ 14N/15N ratio in FIR3.  
  > "For this reason, the $^{14}$N/$^{15}$N ratio in FIR3 from N$_2$H$^+$ cannot be derived and will not be discussed further." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): In the NW Region, the opacity of N15NH+ has a large uncertainty (1.4 +- 1.4), which may introduce inaccuracies into the optically thin column density estimate.  
  > "In the NW Region, as stated before, the opacity derived in CLASS that resulted from using the HFS fitting routine is 1.4$\pm$1.4. With such a large error associated with a faint line, the opacity cannot be well constrained and this may be causing inaccuracies in our estimates performed assuming optically thin conditions." (N$_2$H$^+$)
- Limitation (authors): In the E Region, the 15NNH+ and N15NH+ lines are so faint that only the main hyperfine component is visible, preventing a full HFS fit.  
  > "In the case of the E Region, the line is so faint that only the main component of the HFS is visible in the $^{15}$NNH$^+$ and N$^{15}$NH$^+$ spectra. For this reason, the fit to the whole HFS cannot be performed." (N$_2$H$^+$)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The HCN 14N/15N ratio depends directly on assuming a spatially uniform 12C/13C ratio of 50 +- 5 across all sub-regions including FIR3 and the outer E region; any local carbon fractionation would bias the inferred nitrogen fractionation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The NOEMA H13CN and HC15N critical densities (~1.6x10^6 cm^-3) slightly exceed the adopted average H2 density (1.2x10^6 cm^-3), so sub-thermal (non-LTE) excitation could affect the derived column densities if densities vary between cores and the outer E region.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 2 lists T_ex = 40 +- 5 K for FIR3 in H13CN and HC15N, whereas Section 4.2 states that FIR3 was modelled with T_ex between 100 K and 150 K.
