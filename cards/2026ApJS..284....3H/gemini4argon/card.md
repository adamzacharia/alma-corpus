## Analysis recipe

1. **Convolve multiwavelength JWST and HST images to common catalog resolutions and subtract diffuse emission**: Convolve maps to 0.36'' (F1130W) for the 10 um catalog and 0.67'' (F2100W) for the 21 um catalog using jwst_kernels, mask saturated galaxy centers in NGC 1365 and NGC 7496 using STPSF, and decompose F1000W and F2100W maps using Constrained Diffusion (CD)  
  > "For a 10 $\mu$m catalog, all maps are convolved to match the resolution of the F1130W filter (0.36$''$) using the `jwst_kernels` code." (JWST Imaging); "Similarly, for the 21 $\mu$m catalog, we convolve all maps to the resolution of F2100W (0.67$''$)." (JWST Imaging); "In this study, we use Constrained Diffusion (cite: Li2022), a related approach that ensures the sum of all decomposed component fluxes matches the original input map and are non-negative." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction)
   - software: pjpipe, jwst_kernels, STPSF
   - parameter: Use scale 2 of CD decomposition for F2100W and sum of scales 1 and 2 for F1000W; central mask radius ~18'' for NGC 1365 and NGC 7496  
  > "In this study, we use the second scale of our F2100W images for source detection in the 21 $\mu$m maps, as smaller scales are dominated by pixel-to-pixel noise." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction); "In the case of the 10 $\mu$m maps, we used the sum of the first and second scales, which improved the detection of faint sources." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction); "The radius of the masked region is approximately 18$''$." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared CD-filtered native resolution maps against maps convolved to 0.85'' and previous unfiltered catalog from (cite: hassani23)  
  > "We compare the second component of our F2100W maps with the sum of the 0, 1, and 2 components from the F2100W maps convolved to 0.85$\ensuremath{''}$ in (cite: Pathak2024)." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction)
2. **Identify compact 10 and 21 um sources and measure multiwavelength photometry and completeness limits**: Run ASTRODENDRO on CD-filtered F1000W and F2100W maps, measure peak surface brightness on unfiltered convolved JWST/HST maps with annular background subtraction, sample native ALMA CO(2-1) and MUSE maps at source positions, and run artificial source tests (AST)  
  > "We use the ASTRODENDRO package to identify and measure the structure of mid-infrared emission peaks." (Source Identification); "After subtracting the median background, we convert the background-subtracted surface brightness to flux units ($\mu$Jy) by multiplying it by the solid angle subtended by the target PSF (see Table 4 of (cite: lee2023)), which was calculated using the STPSF package (cite: Perrin)." (Source Photometry); "We also included the surface brightness of CO(2-1) and H$\alpha$ EW from (cite: Leroy2021) and MUSE data from (cite: phangs-muse), respectively." (Source Photometry)
   - software: ASTRODENDRO, STPSF, WEBBPSF, Photutils, Neloura
   - parameter: Dendrogram threshold = min + 5 * step; min_npix = 41 (F2100W) or 10 (F1000W); min_delta = 3 * step; min separation = 0.33'' (F1000W) or 0.17'' (F2100W); background annulus = 2x to 3x PSF size  
  > "By scaling this step by a factor of 5, the function ensures that only features significantly brighter than the background noise are included in the analysis." (Source Identification); "We adopt three criteria to define independent structures: (1) each leaf must contain at least as many pixels as the area subtended by the PSF, which corresponds to $\sim$ 41 pixels for F2100W and $\sim$ 10 pixels for F1000W, calculated from the PSF FWHM (cite: lee2023), (2) the difference between the minimum value in a leaf and the peak intensity is at least three times as large as the “step” size defined previously, and (3) the local maximum of the leaf is separated by all other local maxima by at least one half-width of the PSF for the band we are using: 0.33$''$ ($\sim$3 pixels) for F1000W and 0.17$''$ ($\sim$1.5 pixels) for F2100W." (Source Identification); "The background was estimated using an annulus with radii set to $2\times$ and $3\times$ the PSF size: 1.34$''$ and 2.01$''$ for the F2100W catalog, and 0.72$''$ and 1.08$''$ for the F1000W catalog." (Source Photometry)
   - fitting: Fit detection probability P(f_nu) of 2000 injected fake sources per galaxy with a logistic function to determine 50% completeness limit c2  
  > "We assess the recovery rate of injected fake sources in relation to their flux by fitting the probability of detection $P$ with a logistic function" (Completeness Limits)
   - validation: Evaluated completeness limits across sub-galactic environments and local background bins  
  > "We specifically check whether the completeness limit varies with local background level by dividing the data into four bins of background level (units of MJy/sr): $(0, 10^{-0.5})$, $(10^{-0.5}, 1)$, $(1, 10^{0.5})$, and $(10^{0.5}, \infty)$, then calculating the completeness limit for fake sources within these background ranges." (Completeness Limits)
3. **Classify sources and analyze correlations and luminosity functions**: Classify sources into exposed/embedded young clusters, RSGs, O-AGBs, C-AGBs, CPNs, B[e], WRs, foreground stars, and background galaxies using flux ratio cuts from CIGALE, PARSEC/COLIBRI, and SAGE-LMC; fit Halpha-F2100W linear relation and Pareto-lognormal luminosity functions  
  > "We use the `CIGALE` code (section [4.1.1](#sec:s_cigale)) to model dusty, young stellar clusters and establish band ratio thresholds to find “ISM sources” that we divide into optically exposed or embedded based on the presence of H$\alpha$ emission (Section [4.2](#sec:ismsources))." (Source Classification); "We use the `PARSEC` stellar evolutionary tracks (section [4.1.2](#sec:parsec)) to classify RSGs, O-AGBs, and C-AGBs, which we validate with observations from the SAGE-LMC survey (cite: Jones2017; Jones2018)." (Source Classification); "To characterize the slope of the luminosity functions, we employ a Pareto-lognormal model to fit over distribution." (Luminosity Functions)
   - software: CIGALE, PARSEC, COLIBRI, CMD 3.7, ASTRODENDRO
   - parameter: Flux ratio cuts in Table 3; Gaia DR3 parallax_over_error > 3 within 2''; F200W semi-major/semi-minor axis ratio > 1.5 for background galaxies  
  > "Table [\[tab:flux_ratios_sources\]](#tab:flux_ratios_sources) presents the source classifications used in the catalog." (Source Classification); "We applied the criterion $\texttt{parallax\_over\_error} > 3$ to identify foreground stars and performed a cross-match with our 10 and 21 $\mu$m catalogs using a 2$\ensuremath{''}$ separation to account for proper motion." (Foreground Stars); "We select elongated F200W sources with a semi-major to semi-minor axis ratio greater than 1.5." (Background Galaxies)
   - assumption: BC03 SSP with Z=0.02, Chabrier IMF, A_V = 1 and 10 mag, R_V = 3.1, q_PAH = 0.95 to 3.8%, U_min = 10, alpha = 2 for CIGALE cluster models; Kroupa IMF for PARSEC models  
  > "We adopt the BC03 stellar population synthesis models from (cite: Bruzual03) with a metallicity of $Z = 0.02$ and a Chabrier initial mass function (cite: Chabrier)." (Young Stellar Clusters); "We also incorporate the dust emission model from (cite: DraineLi2007; Draine2014), considering a PAH mass fraction, $q_{\mathrm{PAH}}$, ranging from 0.95 to 3.8 %, and a minimum radiation field intensity of $U_{\mathrm{min}} = 10$." (Young Stellar Clusters); "We also adopt the Kroupa IMF which is corrected for unresolved binaries (cite: Kroupa2001; Kroupa2002), which was used to compute the integrated magnitudes." (Evolved Stars)
   - fitting: Log-linear regression between L_Halpha,corr and nu L_nu,21um; Pareto-lognormal PDF fit to luminosity distributions with bootstrap resampling for slope uncertainties  
  > "We fit a linear relation in log space between the attenuation-corrected HST H$\alpha$ luminosity and the F2100W luminosity" (Correlation); "The uncertainties in the slope ($\beta$) were estimated for each galaxy using bootstrap by resampling the luminosity, and the standard deviation of the slopes derived from the Pareto–lognormal fits was reported as the error in $\beta$." (Luminosity Functions)
   - validation: Validated stellar color cuts against SAGE-LMC spectroscopically classified stars and cross-matched exposed clusters with MUSE nebular catalog  
  > "We further compare the colors of our sources to SAGE-LMC sources in Figure [18](#fig:class_figure), finding good agreement for RSGs and O-AGBs using the color criteria F200W$-$F360M $< -0.3$ and F200W$-$F770W $< -1.8$ (see black solid lines in Figure [18](#fig:class_figure))." (RSG and O-AGB detections); "By cross-matching our exposed sources with the “nebular” catalog from (cite: neb_catalog) using a maximum separation of 1$''$ (comparable to the typical MUSE PSF), we find that about 77% of the exposed clusters are associated with MUSE nebular regions across our sample of 19 galaxies." (Exposed Sources)

## Qualitative findings

- Large-scale diffuse emission accounts for ~50% of the total galaxy flux in F2100W and ~56% in F1000W, while cataloged compact sources contribute ~20% at 21 um and ~5% at 10 um.  
  > "We find that 21 $\mu$m compact sources contribute $\sim$20% of the total galaxy emission in that band, but only contribute $5\%$ at 10 $\mu$m." (Abstract); "The median $f_{\text{LS}}$ is about 50% in the F2100W band and slightly higher, around 56%, in the F1000W band." (Completeness Limits)
- In regions of intense central CO(2-1) emission (I_CO > 100 K km s^-1), the log(L_Halpha,corr / nu L_21um) ratio decreases below 0.1, indicating a higher prevalence of clusters with relatively weak Halpha emission associated with bright CO intensity.  
  > "However, in regions of intense central CO emission ($I_{\mathrm{CO}} > 100$ K km s$^{-1}$), the log ratio decreases to below 0.1, indicating a higher prevalence of clusters with relatively weak H$\alpha$ emission associated with bright CO intensity." (Correlation)
- 21 um source luminosity correlates with both attenuation-corrected Halpha emission and ALMA CO(2-1) intensity, though the correlation with CO is weaker and more scattered than with Halpha.  
  > "Additionally, we compare with CO intensity, which shows a weaker correlation than H$\alpha$, becoming more scattered when considering only embedded sources." (Correlation); "Both plots illustrate that the more luminous 21 $\mu$m sources are associated with more H$\alpha$ and CO emission though the correlation with H$\alpha$ is stronger." (Correlation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST NIRCam/MIRI provides the resolution and sensitivity needed to isolate compact mid-IR star-forming regions and dusty evolved stars in galaxies at 5-20 Mpc, complemented by PHANGS HST, MUSE, and ALMA CO(2-1) data.  
  > "New observations with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) provide an opportunity to isolate and characterize high mass star-forming regions and bright, dusty stars in in more distant targets." (Introduction); "These observations and catalog are part of the Physics at High Angular Resolution in Nearby GalaxieS (PHANGS) survey, which also provides high resolution ancillary data of molecular gas from the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (cite: Leroy2021)" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Constrained Diffusion removes diffuse mid-IR background while preserving flux and non-negativity without wavelet artifacts, and ASTRODENDRO captures hierarchically nested and irregular mid-IR structures better than CLUMPFIND or SExtractor.  
  > "In this study, we use Constrained Diffusion (cite: Li2022), a related approach that ensures the sum of all decomposed component fluxes matches the original input map and are non-negative." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction); "Given the limitations of the source-finding algorithms discussed above, we adopt a dendrogram-based approach for source identification, following (cite: Williams) and (cite: hassani23)." (Source Identification)
- Alternative: Fourier filtering and wavelets for diffuse emission subtraction (rejected because they produce negative, non-physical artifacts in high-contrast areas)  
  > "However, these methods can produce artifacts in decomposed maps, especially in areas with large contrasts in emission, resulting in negative, non-physical emission (cite: Coifman1995)." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction)
- Alternative: Convolving F2100W maps to 0.85'' resolution before CD filtering (rejected because it degrades physical resolution by 20 pc in the most distant targets)  
  > "Despite this advantage, the convolution to 0.85$\ensuremath{''}$ introduces a 20 pc increase in physical resolution for the most distant targets. Given this limitation, we opted to use the filtered, native-resolution maps." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction)
- Alternative: CLUMPFIND, FELLWALKER, and SEXTRACTOR for source finding (rejected because CLUMPFIND blends diffuse emission and SEXTRACTOR struggles with irregular structures)  
  > "In the case that medium consists of structured diffuse emission blended with compact sources, CLUMPFIND will often identify larger structures that reflect the diffuse emission rather than cleanly isolating the compact sources (cite: Williams)." (Source Identification); "Other algorithms, such as SEXTRACTOR (cite: Bertin), are better suited for identifying elliptical sources but struggle with irregular structures (cite: Williams)." (Source Identification)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Constrained Diffusion can fragment saturated sources and exacerbate diffraction spike artifacts in galaxy centers (requiring 18'' central masks in NGC 1365 and NGC 7496).  
  > "However, one limitation is that saturated sources may be fragmented into several parts, where the wings and core of the PSF blend together, potentially confusing the source detection algorithm." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction); "Additionally, this method can exacerbate diffraction spike artifacts in the central region of galaxies and result in numerous false detections, notably impacting two of our targets: NGC 1365 and NGC 7496." (Diffuse Emission Subtraction)
- Limitation (authors): Varying noise levels and distances (5-20 Mpc) across the 19 galaxies result in non-homogeneous sensitivity and luminosity detection thresholds, limiting RSG and O-AGB detection in more distant galaxies.  
  > "We note that since the “step” values vary for each galaxy, the corresponding noise levels and detection limits also differ across our sample, resulting in a non-homogeneous sensitivity." (Source Identification); "While many types of evolved stars are expected to be detectable in this nearby galaxy, we are unlikely to detect RSGs and O-rich AGBs in our more distant targets ($D\gtrsim 10~\mathrm{Mpc})$ (see Section [4.3.1](#sec:rsg-oagbs) for discussion)." (Example Model SEDs)
- Limitation (authors): PAH ionization and destruction processes are not included in the CIGALE cluster models, and the post-AGB phase is not included in the PARSEC models.  
  > "PAH destruction processes are not included in our current modeling of young clusters." (Example Model SEDs); "We note that the post-AGB phase is not included in our `PARSEC` models, limiting our ability to estimate the properties of these stars." (C-AGBs)
- Limitation (authors): CPNe and C-PAGB stars have overlapping infrared colors and cannot be distinguished with the current NIRCam and MIRI filter sets.  
  > "Some bright C-PAGB sources can exhibit $r_7 > 1$, making it difficult to distinguish CPNs from C-PAGBs with the current NIRCam and MIRI filter sets." (CPN/C-PAGB); "Given these overlaps, we treat CPNe and C-PAGB candidates as a blended category." (CPN/C-PAGB)
- Limitation (authors): MUSE spectral coverage and AO laser gaps limit optical spectroscopic confirmation of Wolf-Rayet Blue Bump and Red Bump features.  
  > "While we do have optical spectroscopy of our targets from MUSE, our spectral coverage is not well suited for WR identification." (WR Stars)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Physical resolution varies by a factor of ~4 across the sample (5.2 to 19.6 Mpc), meaning 'compact' sources at ~20 Mpc (~60 pc) can blend multiple clusters and evolved stars that would be resolved in NGC 5068.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A few typos exist in the text and tables (e.g., Table 1 swaps the F1000W and F2100W column values for matched flux ranges, and a couple of LaTeX macros for source counts in Sections 4.3.2-4.3.5 failed to render in the text).
