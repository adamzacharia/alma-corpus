- Fields: nearby_galaxies, agn_and_quasars
- Question: Can radio-jet AGN feedback lower the star-formation efficiency and perturb the molecular gas in a massive spiral galaxy that lacks a bulge, bar, rich environment and strong radiative AGN, so that competing quenching mechanisms are ruled out?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA CO(1-0) cube**: CASA 5.6 reduction with automatic flagging and bandpass/phase/amplitude/flux calibration; CLEAN with Briggs weighting  
  > "We used the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA) version 5.6 to reduce the data, applying the automatic flagging of visibilites, to calibrate the bandpass, phase, amplitude and flux." (2.2 ALMA CO(1–0) interferometry)
   - software: CASA, CLEAN
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; cell 0.2''; channel 11 km/s  
  > "using briggs weighting with $robust=0.5$. The spatial sampling of the final data cube is 0.2$''$, and spectral channel sizes are 11 km s$^{-1}$." (2.2 ALMA CO(1–0) interferometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure IRAM 30-m single-dish CO(1-0) flux**: CLASS reduction, first-order baselines after masking the CO range, conversion from brightness temperature to Jy with EMIR website efficiencies  
  > "we used simple first-order polynomials to remove the baselines, after carefully masking the spectral range where we expected CO(1–0) to fall." (2.1 IRAM/EMIR single-dish observations); "We used the values given on the EMIR website[^3] to translate the measured brightness temperatures into units of flux density (Jy)." (2.1 IRAM/EMIR single-dish observations)
   - software: CLASS (GILDAS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with an independent single-dish measurement of 14.0 Jy km/s  
  > "A similar spectrum has recently been obtained by (cite: dabhade20b) who measured a somewhat greater integrated line flux of 14.0 Jy km s$^{-1}$." (2.1 IRAM/EMIR single-dish observations)
3. **Check ALMA flux recovery against single dish**: Convolve the ALMA map with a 2D Gaussian equal to the 30-m beam centred on the pointing position and compare integrated flux  
  > "we convolve the ALMA map with a two-dimensional Gaussian distribution with FWHM widths corresponding to the beam size of the 30-m telescope, and centered on the position at which we pointed the telescope." (4.1 CO(1–0) morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Convolved flux 13.0 Jy km/s vs measured 12.6+-0.8; at least 94% of single-dish flux recovered; missing flux neglected  
  > "We find a total flux of $I_{CO}=13.0$ Jy km s$^{-1}$." (4.1 CO(1–0) morphology); "we can conclude that the amount of missing flux is small, and we will neglect it in our following analysis." (4.1 CO(1–0) morphology)
4. **Bulge-disk decomposition of 2MASS J-band image**: Azimuthally averaged surface-brightness profile fit with two seeing-convolved Sersic functions; light within 3'' assigned to bulge  
  > "we construct an azimuthally averaged surface-brightness profile with the Python implementation of the Ellipse package (cite: jedrzewski87), and use the LM-fitting routine of (cite: markwardt12) to model this profile with two Sersic functions" (3.1 Bulge-disk decomposition); "In the following we will consider all light emitted from within 3$''$ of the nucleus as coming from the bulge, and light from outside that radius as coming from the disk." (3.1 Bulge-disk decomposition)
   - software: Ellipse (Python implementation), LM-fitting routine of Markwardt (2012)
   - parameter: Gaussian seeing kernel FWHM=0.96 (as printed)  
  > "convolved with a Gaussian kernel of $FWHM=0.96$, to approximate the size of the seeing disk in the 2MASS image" (3.1 Bulge-disk decomposition)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt least squares; priors not stated  
  > "use the LM-fitting routine of (cite: markwardt12) to model this profile with two Sersic functions" (3.1 Bulge-disk decomposition)
   - validation: Sersic parameters compared with optical values of Bagchi et al.; radii systematically larger  
  > "These Sersic parameters are within the range of those previously found by (cite: bagchi14) in the optical, however, the effective radii are systematically larger." (3.1 Bulge-disk decomposition)
5. **Derive stellar masses and surface densities**: Convert J-band luminosity with a population-synthesis mass-to-light ratio  
  > "For these parameters we find $\Upsilon=0.5~M_{\odot}/L_{\odot}$." (3.2 Stellar masses, and stellar mass surface densities)
   - parameter: Fiducial 10 Gyr population formed in a 200 Myr burst of 50 Msun/yr; D_L=354.6 Mpc  
  > "of a fiducial 10-Gyr old stellar population formed in a fairly short (200 Myr) period of constant star formation of 50 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$" (3.2 Stellar masses, and stellar mass surface densities)
   - assumption: Both bulge and disk have old stellar populations  
  > "assuming that both have old stellar populations" (3.2 Stellar masses, and stellar mass surface densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with K-band estimate of Walker et al. (4.6e11 Msun), a 25% discrepancy judged acceptable  
  > "A systematic discrepancy of 25% is acceptable for the purpose of our analysis" (3.2 Stellar masses, and stellar mass surface densities)
6. **Measure star-formation rate and SFR surface density**: Integrated GALEX FUV flux converted with Kennicutt (1998) using Kroupa IMF; cross-checked with a combined FUV+22um estimator of Leroy et al. (2008)  
  > "We integrated the far-UV flux in the GALEX image of J2345$-$0449, finding $F_{FUV}=92.1\pm1.1\ \mu$Jy" (3.3 Star formation); "We estimated a star-formation rate by combining the UV and IR measurements into a single estimator, using the relationship of (cite: leroy08)." (3.3 Star formation)
   - parameter: Disk area from deconvolved UV size 28.6'' x 14.0'' (43 x 21 kpc) used for average Sigma_SFR  
  > "Diffuse UV emission is detected over an area with a deconvolved size of 28.6$''$ $\times$ 14.0$''$, corresponding to 43 kpc $\times$ 21 kpc" (3.3 Star formation)
   - assumption: Kroupa IMF  
  > "but adopted the today more widely used Kroupa (cite: kroupa01) stellar initially mass function instead of that of Salpeter" (3.3 Star formation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: UV-only and UV+IR estimates agree (SFR 1.25 vs 1.1), so UV misses little star formation  
  > "This suggest that the UV does not miss significant amounts of star formation in this galaxy." (3.3 Star formation)
7. **Extract spatially resolved CO spectra and fit line profiles**: Spectra in 1'' x 1'' square apertures from an 11 km/s cube, each fitted with a single Gaussian for pixels with SNR>3 in an integrated map  
  > "we constructed spectra from square apertures with 1.0$''$$\times$1.0$''$ in size in a data cube with 11 km s$^{-1}$ wide spectral channels" (4.2 Gas kinematics); "This was done for all pixels with signal-to-noise ratio $SNR>3$ in an emission-line map constructed from directly integrating all flux in the redshift range where line emission was detected." (4.2 Gas kinematics)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per aperture; multi-component fits attempted but S/N insufficient  
  > "We then fitted each spectrum with a single Gaussian component." (4.2 Gas kinematics); "the signal-to-noise ratios in individual small apertures are not sufficient to probe the global kinematic properties of multiple components robustly and in more detail." (5.2 Search for outflow signatures)
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Model the velocity field**: Kinemetry (Fourier analysis along concentric ellipses) with externally fitted centre, PA and inclination from ellipse fitting to the gas morphology  
  > "Kinemetry performs a fifth-order Fourier analysis of the kinetic moments of the line-of-sight velocity distribution of a gas disk or ring along concentric ellipses" (4.3 Fourier-analysis of the large-scale velocity field); "To do so, we used the gas morphology, which we fitted with the Python implementation of the IRAF task ellipse (cite: jedrzewski87)." (4.3 Fourier-analysis of the large-scale velocity field)
   - software: Kinemetry, ellipse (Python implementation of IRAF task)
   - parameter: PA = 94 deg; inclination 59 +- 2 deg  
  > "The position angle on the sky is $PA=94^\circ$, and the inclination angle $59^\circ \pm 2^\circ$" (4.3 Fourier-analysis of the large-scale velocity field)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual map: below about 10 km/s in most of the disk (1.5% of the velocity offset), >=30 km/s along the outer rim  
  > "Residuals are less than about $\pm10$ km s$^{-1}$ in most of the disk, or $1.5$% of the measured total velocity offset" (4.3 Fourier-analysis of the large-scale velocity field)
9. **Derive circular velocity and dynamical mass from PV diagram**: PV diagram along PA=94 deg compared with Kinemetry model; v_c from the projected velocity at the turnover radius corrected for inclination; M_dyn = V^2 R / G  
  > "Distances are measured from the nucleus of the galaxy along the morphological and kinematic major axis of the galaxy at $PA=94^\circ$." (4.4 Position-velocity diagram); "The enclosed dynamical mass within the turnover radius, $M_{dyn}= V^2\ R / G$" (4.4 Position-velocity diagram)
   - parameter: Delta v/2 = 316 km/s at turnover radius 5.2 kpc; i = 59 deg  
  > "derived from the projected (observed) velocity of $\Delta v / 2$ = 316 km s$^{-1}$ at the turnover radius of 5.2 kpc" (4.4 Position-velocity diagram)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with the Halpha velocity of Bagchi et al. at the same radius  
  > "this velocity is consistent with the velocity previously found by (cite: bagchi14) from H$\alpha$ at the same radius" (4.4 Position-velocity diagram)
10. **Convert CO flux to molecular gas mass and surface densities**: Solomon et al. (1997) L'_CO from I_CO, then M_H2 = alpha_CO x L'; local surface densities in 1.5 kpc apertures  
  > "We use Equation 3 of (cite: solomon97) to estimate a molecular gas mass from the CO flux measurement" (4.6 Integrated molecular gas mass and gas mass surface densities); "we can also estimate the local gas mass surface densities in individual 1.5 kpc apertures, corresponding to the 1$''$ beam size." (4.6 Integrated molecular gas mass and gas mass surface densities)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 4.3 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1; no helium correction (would be 36% higher)  
  > "We did not include a correction for He and other metals, if we had, this mass estimate would be 36% higher" (4.6 Integrated molecular gas mass and gas mass surface densities)
   - assumption: A single CO-to-H2 conversion factor applies throughout the galaxy  
  > "Assuming that a single CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor of $\alpha_{CO}=4.3\times$ M$_{\odot}$" (4.6 Integrated molecular gas mass and gas mass surface densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
11. **Quantify jet-perturbed gas, beam smearing, outflow limit and kinetic energy**: Split gas at FWHM=70 km/s; toy cube from the modeled velocity field with same S/N and beam to test beam smearing; E_kin = 3/2 M v^2 from local line widths; comparison with jet power  
  > "We used the modeled velocity field to construct an artificial data cube with the same signal-to-noise ratio and beam size as observed in our data" (5.1 Radio jets as origin of the broad line emission); "We can give a rough estimate of the kinetic energy corresponding to the line broadening in this region, by setting $E_{kin} = 3/2\ M\ v^2$, i.e., assuming isotropic gas broadening in three dimensions." (5.3 Kinetic energy)
   - parameter: 3 sigma outflow limit from peak rms 1.2 mJy and assumed FWHM=180 km/s  
  > "We did not identify any such component down to a peak rms = 1.2 mJy in our data cube." (5.2 Search for outflow signatures)
   - assumption: Same alpha_CO for broad and narrow gas; lower limit alpha_CO=0.34 for optically thin gas  
  > "Assuming that similar CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factors apply to both regions" (5.1 Radio jets as origin of the broad line emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Beam smearing doubles widths of FWHM=10 km/s lines but broadens FWHM=25 km/s lines by at most 2 km/s  
  > "lines with an intrinsic width of $FWHM= 25$ km s$^{-1}$ are broadened by at most $2$ km s$^{-1}$" (5.1 Radio jets as origin of the broad line emission)
12. **Compare with star-formation laws and Toomre stability**: Place average/extreme Sigma_gas and Sigma_SFR on the Kennicutt-Schmidt, Silk-Elmegreen and extended Schmidt diagrams; compute Sigma_crit = kappa sigma / (pi G) for sigma=10, 30 and 75 km/s  
  > "the critical mass surface density, $\Sigma_{gas,crit}$ for gas to become Toomre-unstable is $\Sigma_{gas,crit}=\kappa\ \sigma / \pi\ G$" (6.2 Rotational support in the ring with and without radio jet)
   - parameter: a = 1.4; sigma = 10 km/s for gas unaffected by the jet; R_inner=4.2 kpc, R_outer=11.8 kpc; v_c 366 and 378 km/s  
  > "We adopt $a=1.4$, appropriate for the flat part of the rotation curve." (6.2 Rotational support in the ring with and without radio jet); "setting $R_{inner}=4.2$ kpc and $R_{outer}=11.8$ kpc for the inner and outer edge of the molecular disk" (6.2 Rotational support in the ring with and without radio jet)
   - assumption: Unperturbed dispersion sigma=10 km/s, similar to other massive spirals  
  > "We will therefore adopt $\sigma=10$ km s$^{-1}$ as typical value for the gas velocity dispersion in absence of the radio source." (6.2 Rotational support in the ring with and without radio jet)

## Qualitative findings

- CO(1-0) forms a large, irregular molecular ring (outer radius 7.9'' x 4.4'') associated with the stellar disk that avoids the bulge region  
  > "We can therefore safely conclude that the molecular gas is associated with the inner disk of J2345$-$0449, and avoids the bulge." (4.1 CO(1–0) morphology)
- The velocity field is regular rotation; Kinemetry residuals are small  
  > "gas velocities in the ring as probed by the line centroids do not show significant deviations from rotational motion within the gravitational potential of J2345$-$0449" (4.3 Fourier-analysis of the large-scale velocity field)
- Broad CO lines coincide spatially with the region intercepted by the radio source; no escaping outflow detected  
  > "We do not detect any outflow motion at velocities exceeding the circular velocity of the ring" (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The ALMA program was meant only to detect CO in the galaxy and offers arcsecond-resolution resolved molecular gas data  
  > "which was only meant to yield a detection of CO line emission in this galaxy" (5.2 Search for outflow signatures)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Multi-component, non-parametric and prior-based line fits to isolate outflow components were tried but not robust at the available S/N  
  > "we have tried to perform a multi-component fit to the line profiles, and also adopted non-parametric methods." (5.2 Search for outflow signatures)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Observations are shallow and low resolution; deeper data are needed to probe non-rotational components  
  > "Probing non-rotational components would therefore require deeper observations with higher spatial and spectral resolution" (5.2 Search for outflow signatures)
- Limitation (authors): GALEX resolution (5'') only allows average star-formation surface densities  
  > "Currently available data can only provide us with average values, because GALEX has a relatively low spatial resolution of only 5$''$." (6.1.1 Standard Kennicutt-Schmidt relation)
- Limitation (authors): The extent of 2MASS resolution and S/N may affect the measured bulge-disk radii  
  > "it might affect the detailed fit results, in particular the measured radii" (3.1 Bulge-disk decomposition)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO = 4.3 is assumed uniform though the perturbed gas may need a much lower value (the paper itself spans 0.34 to 4.3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SFR is a single galaxy-wide GALEX FUV estimate; Sigma_SFR per aperture is not measured, so the KS offset compares ranges rather than matched local pixels
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Alternatives (bar, environment, structure) are excluded from literature properties, not tested with the data here
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Jet kinetic power and jet lifetime come from a cluster cavity relation and carry order-of-magnitude uncertainty
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sigma_crit values in the text for sigma=30 and 75 km/s are printed with wrong units
