# Black hole mass measurement using ALMA observations of [CI] and CO emissions in the Seyfert 1 galaxy NGC 7469

## Science

- Fields: agn_and_quasars, nearby_galaxies
- Question: Measure the supermassive black hole mass in the Seyfert 1 galaxy NGC 7469 using ALMA observations of [CI](1-0) and 12CO(1-0) gas kinematics, test whether [CI](1-0) is a better dynamical tracer than CO in AGNs, and constrain the broad-line region virial factor and inclination.  
  > "We present a supermassive black hole (SMBH) mass measurement in the Seyfert 1 galaxy NGC 7469 using Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observations of the atomic-${\rm [CI]}$(1-0) and molecular-$^{12}$CO(1-0) emission lines at the spatial resolution of $\approx0.3\ensuremath{''}$ (or $\approx$ 100 pc)." (Abstract); "As is expected from X-ray dominated region (XDR) effects that dramatically increase an atomic carbon abundance by dissociating CO molecules, we suggest that the atomic \[CI\](1-0) emission is a better probe of SMBH masses than CO emission in AGNs." (Abstract); "Given this new $M_{\rm BH}$, we are able to constrain the specific RM dimensionless scaling factor of $f=7.2^{+4.2}_{-3.4}$ for the AGN BLR in NGC 7469." (Abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce HST images and construct PSF models**: Download raw flt frames of HST ACS F814W and WFC3 F547M, re-reduce ACS F814W with Astrodrizzle to 0.04'' pixel scale, align to the ALMA galaxy centre from Izumi et al. (2020), and generate combined PSF models (FWHM = 0.08'') by inserting Tiny Tim PSFs into flc frames and drizzling.  
  > "We thus downloaded the raw `flt` frames of the ACS F814W image from *HST*/Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) and re-reduced these images using the `drizzlepac/Astrodrizzle` package (cite: Avila12) to a final pixel scale of $0.04\ensuremath{''}$." (Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images); "We aligned these *HST* images to the galaxy centre determined from I20. Next, we used the `Tiny Tim` routine (cite: Krist95; Krist11) to create point spread function (PSF) models for individual *HST* exposure frames of the involved filters." (Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images); "The final PSF model is the combination of these PSF models created by `Astrodrizzle` (cite: Rusli13; denBrok15; Nguyen17; Nguyen18; Thater19a), which has a FWHM of $0.08\ensuremath{''}$ ($\approx$ 27 pc) and will be used to decompose the mass model (Section 4.2)." (Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images)
   - software: drizzlepac/Astrodrizzle, Tiny Tim
   - parameter: Final pixel scale = 0.04'', PSF FWHM = 0.08''  
  > "re-reduced these images using the `drizzlepac/Astrodrizzle` package (cite: Avila12) to a final pixel scale of $0.04\ensuremath{''}$." (Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images); "The final PSF model is the combination of these PSF models created by `Astrodrizzle` (cite: Rusli13; denBrok15; Nguyen17; Nguyen18; Thater19a), which has a FWHM of $0.08\ensuremath{''}$ ($\approx$ 27 pc)" (Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images)
   - assumption: Galactic extinction A_V = 0.184 (A_F547M = 0.075 mag, A_F814W = 0.105 mag) and Cardelli et al. (1989) extinction law  
  > "All quantities are quoted with the Galactic extinction correction to recover their intrinsic values assuming $A_V=0.184$ (cite: Schlafly11) and the interstellar extinction law of (cite: Cardelli89)." (INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Create ALMA 3D cubes, moment maps, PVDs, and radial PA profiles**: Create 3D (R.A., decl., velocity) cubes for 12CO(1-0) and [CI](1-0) using CASA; generate moment 0, 1, and 2 maps using the moments masking technique (spatial smoothing by factor alpha and clipping at beta*sigma on the smoothed cube to mask the full-resolution cube); extract major- and minor-axis PVDs (3-pixel / 0.81'' width); extract radial position angle profiles from velocity fields using Kinemetry.  
  > "We created a three-dimensional (3D) cubes of (R.A., decl., velocity) for these $^{12}$CO(1-0) and \[CI\](1-0) emissions using the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: McMullin07)." (ALMA observations); "We varied $\alpha$ and $\beta$ for their appropriate choices to obtain the best moment maps, which yields $\alpha=3$ and $\beta=0.5$." (ALMA observations); "we extracted their radial PA profiles along the major axis via the `Kinemetry` code (cite: Krajnovic06), then used them as an additional input in the same manner of using the circular velocity curve." (KinMS model)
   - software: CASA, Kinemetry
   - parameter: Smoothing factor alpha = 3, clipping threshold beta = 0.5 sigma; PVD cut width = 3 pixels (0.81'') along major axis PA = 308 deg and minor axis PA = 218 deg  
  > "We varied $\alpha$ and $\beta$ for their appropriate choices to obtain the best moment maps, which yields $\alpha=3$ and $\beta=0.5$." (ALMA observations); "Similarly, Fig. 5 shows the PVDs of $^{12}$CO(1-0) and \[CI\](1-0) extracted from a cut of three pixels in width ($0.81\ensuremath{''}\approx270$ pc) through the major axis (${\rm PA}=308^{\circ}$)." (ALMA observations); "This argument is strengthened when we examine these emissions in the galaxy-minor-axis (${\rm PA}=218^{\circ}$) PVDs as shown in Fig. 6." (ALMA observations)
   - fitting: Harmonic expansion of velocity fields along ellipses using Kinemetry to obtain radial PA profiles (295-325 deg for 12CO(1-0) and 300-320 deg for [CI](1-0) across 4'')  
  > "These PA profiles vary in the range of (295–325)$^\circ$ for $^{12}$CO(1-0) and (300–320)$^\circ$ for \[CI\](1-0) across $4\ensuremath{''}$ as shown in Fig. 4." (KinMS model)
   - validation: Visual inspection of moment maps, double-horn integrated spectra, and symmetry in major/minor-axis PVDs  
  > "These profiles show a classical double-horn shape of a rotating disc but asymmetric with some missing blue-shifted velocity channels." (ALMA observations)
3. **Construct the default galaxy stellar and ISM mass models**: Fit the HST WFC3 F547M PSF with a circular Multi-Gaussian Expansion (MGE), then fit the F547M galaxy image with an axisymmetric MGE using mge_fit_sectors while masking the central AGN (r < 0.06''), starburst ring, and bright stars. Estimate H2 mass from 12CO(1-0) aperture fluxes and combine with literature dust mass via the KinMS gasgrav mechanism.  
  > "We applied the `IDL mge_fit_sectors` routine, version 4.14 (cite: Cappellari02) of the MGE model, to parametrise the stellar-mass distribution." (The default-galaxy-mass model); "We also masked out pixels contaminated by the AGN (unresolved point source at the centre, $r<0.06\ensuremath{''}$ or $\approx$ 20 pc), the prominent starburst ring and bright stars." (The default-galaxy-mass model); "We then accounted for these ISM mass (molecular gas + dust) in the mass model by turning on the `gasgrav` mechanism in the KinMS model." (The default-galaxy-mass model)
   - software: IDL, MgeFit (mge_fit_sectors v4.14), KinMS
   - parameter: 6 Gaussian components for F547M PSF (Table 2); 7 Gaussian components for F547M stellar light (Table 3); central AGN mask r < 0.06''; M_H2(r <= 3'') = 2.7 x 10^9 M_sun, M_H2(r <= 7'') = 4 x 10^9 M_sun; M_dust(r <= 7'') = 2.5 x 10^7 M_sun  
  > "We also masked out pixels contaminated by the AGN (unresolved point source at the centre, $r<0.06\ensuremath{''}$ or $\approx$ 20 pc), the prominent starburst ring and bright stars." (The default-galaxy-mass model); "We found the same amount of $M_{\rm H_2}$ within $3\ensuremath{''}$ and $M_{\rm H_2}(r\lesssim7\ensuremath{''})\approx4\times10^9$ M$_{\odot}$." (The default-galaxy-mass model); "(cite: Papadopoulos00) also estimated the dust mass of $\approx2.5\times10^7$ M$_{\odot}$ in the same region of $r\leq7\ensuremath{''}$ ($\lesssim2.3$ kpc)." (The default-galaxy-mass model)
   - assumption: Starburst CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO = 1.0 +- 0.3 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1; gas-to-dust ratio ~ 160; ISM distributed according to 12CO(1-0) and [CI](1-0) surface brightness; constant stellar M/L_F547M  
  > "We converted these aperture fluxes into $M_{\rm H2}$ by assuming the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor for starburst galaxies: $X_{\rm CO}=1.0\pm0.3\times10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: Kuno00; Kuno07; Bolatto13)." (The default-galaxy-mass model); "Here, we assumed the molecular gas and dust are distributed accordingly to the surface brightness densities of $^{12}$CO(1-0) and \[CI\](1-0)." (The default-galaxy-mass model)
   - fitting: 2D axisymmetric MGE photometric decomposition using mge_fit_sectors  
  > "Then, we included this PSF MGE in the second axisymmetric-MGE fit for the photometric MGE." (The default-galaxy-mass model)
   - validation: Comparison of 2D surface brightness contours between HST F547M data and MGE model (Fig. 7)  
  > "This best-fitting MGE model is tabulated in Table 3 and shown in Fig. 7, which illustrates the agreement between the data and the model in the form of two dimensional (2D) contours at the same radii and contour levels." (The default-galaxy-mass model)
4. **Fit 3D gas kinematics with KinMS and MCMC to measure M_BH**: Simulate 3D gas cubes in KinMS using circular velocities from mge_circular_velocity (JAM package), radial PA profiles from Kinemetry, and a Gaussian radial gas surface brightness profile; fit 9 free parameters (log M_BH, M/L_F547M, sigma_gas, G_sigma, F, i, x_c, y_c, v_off) to the 12CO(1-0) and [CI](1-0) cubes via KINMSpy_MCMC and emcee.  
  > "The gas is assumed to move on circular orbits governed by a circular velocity curve, calculated from the `mge_circular_velocity` procedure within the Interactive Data Language (IDL) Jeans Anisotropic Modelling (cite: Cappellari08) package." (KinMS model); "In practice, this is all accomplished by using the `Python` code `KINMSpy_MCMC`." (KinMS model); "Overally, the KinMS model matches the observations with nine free parameters, including $G_\sigma$, $i$, $F$, $x_{\rm c}$, $y_{\rm c}$, $M_{\rm BH}$, $M/L$, $\sigma_{\rm gas}$, and a velocity offset ($v_{\rm off}=V-V_{\rm sys}-V_{\rm rot}$)" (KinMS model)
   - software: KinMS, KINMSpy_MCMC, emcee, IDL, JAM (mge_circular_velocity)
   - parameter: Fit region = 60 x 60 pixels (6'' x 6'') and 60 velocity channels covering (-300, 300) km s^-1 (N = 60 x 60 x 60 constraints, P = 9 parameters); 3 x 10^5 MCMC iterations with 20% burn-in  
  > "We performed the KinMS fit in an area of 60 pixels $\times$ 60 pixels ($6\ensuremath{''}\times6\ensuremath{''}\approx2\times2$ kpc$^2$)." (Results of the default-mass model); "We limit the velocity channels ($-$300, 300) km s$^{-1}$ related to the systemic velocity of $\approx4850$ km s$^{-1}$ and $\approx4831$ km s$^{-1}$ for $^{12}$CO(1-0) and \[CI\](1-0), respectively." (Results of the default-mass model); "To ensure our fit converges, we ran the model with $3\times10^5$ iterations, then exclude the first 20% of the calculations as the burn-in phase and used the rest 80% to produce the final posterior PDF of all nine free parameters." (Results of the default-mass model)
   - assumption: Gas moves on circular orbits in a dynamically thin, cold disc (asymmetric drift ignored); axisymmetric Gaussian gas surface brightness profile; D_A = 68.4 Mpc  
  > "That means the dynamically asymmetric drift from turbulent velocity dispersion of the gas can be ignored (cite: Davis20)." (KinMS model); "Here, we assumed the axisymmetric SBs for \[CI\](1-0) and $^{12}$CO(1-0) and modelled them using a Gaussian." (KinMS model)

## Qualitative findings

- NGC 7469 hosts a dynamically settled circumnuclear gas disc (CND) with a ring-like structure and a two-arm/bi-symmetric spiral pattern inside a starburst ring.  
  > "These emissions reveal that NGC 7469 hosts a circumnuclear gas disc (CND) with a ring-like structure and a two-arm/bi-symmetric spiral pattern within it, surrounded by a starbursting ring." (Abstract)
- Atomic [CI](1-0) emission is more centrally peaked (< 5'') and symmetric in its PVD than 12CO(1-0) (~7''), making [CI](1-0) a better tracer for measuring central SMBH masses in AGNs where XDR effects dissociate CO.  
  > "It is clear that the atomic-\[CI\](1-0) emission is more compact and strongly peaked toward the centre ($<$ 5$\ensuremath{''}$ or 1.7 kpc) than the molecular-$^{12}$CO(1-0) emission when we solely look at the central kpc regions (i.e. inside the starburst ring)." (ALMA observations); "However, this rotating-asymmetric feature is not present in the \[CI\](1-0) PVD, suggesting this emission line is actually a more appropriate line to measure the $M_{\rm BH}$ than the molecular-$^{12}$CO(1-0) line for this AGN NGC 7469." (ALMA observations)
- While [CI](1-0) better constrains M_BH without M_BH-M/L covariance, 12CO(1-0) is more extended and better constrains the galaxy stellar mass-to-light ratio M/L_F547M.  
  > "These features again tell us that \[CI\](1-0) is a best bet to estimate the centrally compact mass (i.e. black hole mass), while the $^{12}$CO(1-0) line is preferable in tracing the enclosed mass (i.e. mass-to-light ratio, $M/L$) of NGC 7469 out to $\approx7\ensuremath{''}$ (cite: Izumi20)." (ALMA observations); "There is no covariance found among parameters of the \[CI\](1-0) kinematics. For $^{12}$CO(1-0), covariance of $M_{\rm BH}$ vs. $M/L$$_{\rm \rm F547M}$ is present as the result of the degeneracy between the potentials of the SMBH and galaxy itself" (Results of the default-mass model)
- The unresolved BLR in NGC 7469 is oriented nearly face-on (i ~ 11 deg), consistent with the unified AGN model for a Seyfert 1 galaxy.  
  > "The gas within the unresolved BLR thus has a Keplerian virial velocity component and the inclination of $i\approx11.0^\circ$$_{-2.5}^{+2.2}$, confirming its face-on orientation in a Seyfert 1 AGN by assuming a geometrically thin BLR model." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: [CI](1-0) is compact and strongly peaked at the AGN centre due to XDR dissociation of CO, probing close to the SMBH, while 12CO(1-0) has ~5% higher spatial resolution and is more extended than other CO lines, helping to constrain the extended galaxy mass.  
  > "Owing to the XDR effect (i.e. dissociation of CO into C), \[CI\](1-0) surely probes the close vicinity of this AGN as evidenced by the position-velocity diagram (PVD)." (ALMA observations); "Regarding this aspect, the $\approx5$% higher in spatial resolution and more extended of $^{12}$CO(1-0) make it a better transition for dynamical modelling than other CO lines. We thus chose the atomic-\[CI\](1-0) and molecular-$^{12}$CO(1-0) emissions among these as the best bets to perform $M_{\rm BH}$ measurement that compromises both these requirements." (ALMA observations)
- Why this method: Cold-gas dynamical modelling with KinMS and radially varying position angles from Kinemetry accounts for the minor-axis velocity warps; since sigma_gas/V_rot is low (0.35 inside 0.5'' and 0.19 outside 0.5''), the CND is dynamically settled and thin, allowing circular-orbit modelling without asymmetric drift corrections.  
  > "Using the dynamics of \[CI\](1-0) and $^{12}$CO(2-1), I20 found $\sigma_{\rm gas}/V_{\rm rot}\approx0.35$ ($r\lesssim0.5\ensuremath{''}$) and $\sigma_{\rm gas}/V_{\rm rot}\approx0.19$ ($r>0.5\ensuremath{''}$) (see panels a and b of their Fig. 11), which indicates a dynamically thin ($d_{\rm t}\approx0$) and cold ($V_{\rm rot}\approx V_{\rm circ}$) CND for NGC 7469." (KinMS model); "Accounting for PA variations in our dynamical models help to mimic the warps seen along the galaxy-minor axis of the velocity-field maps of $^{12}$CO(1-0) (panel b of Fig. 2) and \[CI\](1-0) (panel b of Fig. 3)." (KinMS model)
- Alternative: Dynamical modelling using 12CO(2-1) and 12CO(3-2) lines observed in the same ALMA program was tested/considered, but rejected because they have slightly lower spatial resolution than 12CO(1-0), exhibit a similar central hole in their PVDs, and did not significantly improve the M_BH uncertainty.  
  > "It is also worth mentioning that the dynamical modeling of the other CO lines observed in this program did not improve the inferred-$M_{\rm BH}$ uncertainty significantly comparing to the chosen $^{12}$CO(1-0)." (Results of the default-mass model); "Additionally, the appearance of the $^{12}$CO(2-1) and $^{12}$CO(3-2) PVDs are similar to that of $^{12}$CO(1-0) PVD. i.e., there would be a “hole” at the AGN position (cite: Smith19; Nguyen20)" (Results of the default-mass model)
- Alternative: Dynamical models with constant position angles (PAs) were rejected because they produce axisymmetric kinematic maps that cannot reproduce the minor-axis warps and bias M_BH estimates.  
  > "Dynamical models with constant PAs will create axisymmetric kinematic maps that cannot well reproduce the data, which are asymmetric along the minor axis. This mismatch between the data and the model biases the $M_{\rm BH}$ estimates and produces some large residuals or artificial non-circular motions in the `Data–Model` maps of the velocity fields." (KinMS model)
- Alternative: Dust extinction as the cause of the blueshifted/redshifted asymmetry in the 12CO(1-0) PVD was ruled out because the colour map shows less extinction on the blueshifted side.  
  > "Here, we rule out the possibility of dust extinction because there is less extinction in this blueshifted component (Section 4.4)." (ALMA observations)
- How they decided it was enough: Ran MCMC for 3 x 10^5 iterations (excluding the first 20% as burn-in) to ensure convergence, and rescaled log-likelihood by sqrt(2N) to prevent underestimation of uncertainties from correlated ALMA spaxels.  
  > "To ensure our fit converges, we ran the model with $3\times10^5$ iterations, then exclude the first 20% of the calculations as the burn-in phase and used the rest 80% to produce the final posterior PDF of all nine free parameters." (Results of the default-mass model); "Accordingly, in the Bayesian framework, we rescaled the model uncertainties by dividing the model $\log$ likelihood by $\sqrt{2N}$ or equivalently multiplying the `rms` by $(2N)^{1/4}$ (Table \[tab_alma\])." (Results of the default-mass model)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA synthesised beam (~0.3'' or ~100 pc) is ~30 times larger than the black hole sphere of influence r_SOI (~3 pc or ~0.01''), making this the lowest relative angular resolution M_BH measurement and susceptible to stellar-mass systematics.  
  > "Given $M_{\rm BH}\approx1.8\times10^7$ M$_{\odot}$ and $\sigma_\star\approx150$ km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Onken04) gives $r_{\rm SOI}\equiv GM_{\rm BH}/\sigma_\star^2\approx3$ pc ($\approx0.01\ensuremath{''}$), a factor of $\approx30$ times smaller than what our ALMA observations can resolve." (Results of the default-mass model); "Because of the poorly resolvable scale of our data, we argue this consistency applies to the case of NGC 7469 only." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Central saturation in the HST ACS F814W image prevents an accurate pixel-by-pixel colour-based stellar mass-surface density measurement at the very centre where the SMBH potential dominates.  
  > "However, the central saturation found in F814W prevents us from creating an accurate mass-surface density at the galaxy centre, where the SMBH’s gravitational potential dominates." (The stellar-mass models account for spatial variations of populations and extinction)
- Limitation (authors): High-velocity residuals (~30 km s^-1) near the galaxy centre in both [CI](1-0) and 12CO(1-0) velocity fields may indicate non-circular motions, asymmetric rotation, or beam smearing, which could mean the black hole is slightly more massive.  
  > "However, there is a large residual ($\approx30$ km s$^{-1}$) on the northwest and southeast sides of the galaxy centre for \[CI\](1-0) and $^{12}$CO(1-0), respectively, which might be interpreted as real non-circular motions." (Results of the default-mass model); "or perhaps due to the beam smearing effects because of the limited-spatial resolutions of our data, and consequently, the black hole could be a bit more massive." (Results of the default-mass model)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The spatial resolution (~100 pc) is ~30 times larger than the SMBH sphere of influence (~3 pc), so the inferred M_BH relies heavily on the inner stellar MGE profile and the assumption of a simple Gaussian gas surface brightness distribution inside the beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The central region of NGC 7469 hosts a bright Seyfert 1 AGN and a young starburst ring, making the stellar M/L and AGN point-source subtraction in HST F547M uncertain right at the nucleus.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Wait for higher angular resolution ALMA observations (resolving r_SOI) and tests in additional AGNs before generalizing [CI](1-0) as a universally superior SMBH mass tracer.
