## Analysis recipe

1. **Reconstruct the lensed source**: Infer maximum a posteriori lens models and source surface brightness for gradient, curvature and area-weighted gradient regularisation.  
  > "In an extension of the analysis by (cite: Stacey:2024), we infer *maximum a posteriori* lens models and their associated sources for the three regularisation types, casting every pixel into the source plane." (Data and lens modelling); "We consider three types of source regularisation: gradient, curvature and area-weighted gradient." (Data and lens modelling)
   - software: pronto
   - parameter: Ellipsoidal power-law, external shear and multipole expansions up to 4th order.  
  > "The lens model comprises an ellipsoidal power-law, external shear and multipole expansions up to 4th order." (Data and lens modelling)
   - parameter: Delaunay source grid adapted to magnification; neighbouring-point regularisation.  
  > "The source is constructed on a Delaunay grid adapted to the lens model magnification and a regularisation hyper-parameter enforces a correlation between neighbouring points of the source." (Data and lens modelling)
   - parameter: Area-weighted gradient weights regularisation by triangle area.  
  > "area-weighted gradient is a modification of the former whereby the regularisation is weighted by the area of each triangle rather than each triangle being weighted equally." (Data and lens modelling)
   - assumption: Bayesian priors on lens mass distribution and source smoothness.  
  > "In the lens modelling procedure, assumptions must be made about the mass distribution in the lens and the smoothness of the source in the form of Bayesian statistical priors." (Data and lens modelling)
   - assumption: Flat ΛCDM cosmology (cite: PlanckCollaboration:2020).  
  > "Throughout, we assume flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology (cite: PlanckCollaboration:2020)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Semi-linear inversion jointly solving lens parameters and source surface brightness; maximum a posteriori solutions.  
  > "The lens modelling was performed by (cite: Stacey:2024) using `pronto`, a semi-linear inversion methodology adapted for interferometric data solving simultaneously for the lens model parameters and source surface brightness" (Data and lens modelling); "we infer *maximum a posteriori* lens models and their associated sources for the three regularisation types" (Data and lens modelling)
   - validation: Compare regularisation types with Stacey:2024; gradient and curvature agree within 2σ, with limited angular discrepancies for area-weighted gradient.  
  > "Gradient and curvature regularisation types produce model parameters consistent within 2$\sigma$ of those reported in (cite: Stacey:2024)." (Data and lens modelling); "The area-weighted gradient regularisation model is consistent except for the shear and ellipticity position angles which differ by 2 and 10 degrees, respectively." (Data and lens modelling); "These discrepancies have a minimal effect on the source as the shear is small ($\approx1$ percent of the convergence) and the lens is very round (axis ratio of $\approx0.9$)." (Data and lens modelling)
2. **Map source uncertainties and magnification**: Invert 1000 mock visibility data sets; form pixel mean, standard deviation and signal-to-noise maps; calculate magnification from mapped triangle-area ratios.  
  > "We inferred an uncertainty in the source plane by generating 1000 mock visibility data sets from the maximum a-posteriori lens model and realisations of the noise and performing linear inversions." (Data and lens modelling); "We then created mean, standard deviation and signal-to-noise ratio maps for each pixel of the reconstructed source." (Data and lens modelling); "Additionally, we created source-plane magnification maps by mapping the triangulated image pixels into the source-plane and calculating the ratio of the triangle areas." (Data and lens modelling)
   - parameter: 1000 mock visibility data sets for each maximum a-posteriori model.  
  > "We inferred an uncertainty in the source plane by generating 1000 mock visibility data sets from the maximum a-posteriori lens model and realisations of the noise and performing linear inversions." (Data and lens modelling)
   - assumption: Neglect lens-model parameter uncertainties.  
  > "We generate uncertainty maps for all three models neglecting the uncertainties in lens model parameters." (Data and lens modelling)
   - assumption: Source uncertainty dominated by data noise and phase/amplitude-error artefacts.  
  > "the source surface brightness uncertainty is dominated by noise and artefacts in the data caused by phase and amplitude errors" (Data and lens modelling)
   - fitting: Linear inversions of mock visibilities.  
  > "We inferred an uncertainty in the source plane by generating 1000 mock visibility data sets from the maximum a-posteriori lens model and realisations of the noise and performing linear inversions." (Data and lens modelling)
   - validation: Assess whether source clumps are genuine or noise artefacts.  
  > "Here, our source error analysis gives due diligence to questions of source structure significance, such as whether individual clumps are genuine or noise artefacts." (Discussion)
3. **Measure resolution and recovered flux**: Estimate brightness-weighted source-plane beam FWHM; compare Delaunay vertex spacing; compute lensed flux from source flux density times magnification map.  
  > "Assuming the beam is uniformly magnified, we infer a surface-brightness-weighted mean FWHM of $55$ pc (consistent for all three regularisation types)." (Results); "This is very similar to the median distance between the Delaunay vertices when every pixel is cast back into the source plane; $\approx50$ pc." (Results); "The total lensed flux density we compute from the product of flux density of the reconstructed source and the magnification map" (Results)
   - parameter: Include nominal ±10% ALMA flux scale accuracy.  
  > "including nominal $\pm10$% flux scale accuracy for ALMA calibration." (Results)
   - assumption: Uniformly magnified beam for the mean-FWHM estimate.  
  > "Assuming the beam is uniformly magnified, we infer a surface-brightness-weighted mean FWHM of $55$ pc (consistent for all three regularisation types)." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Resolution agrees with Delaunay spacing; recovered flux agrees with unresolved APEX/LABOCA measurement.  
  > "This is very similar to the median distance between the Delaunay vertices when every pixel is cast back into the source plane; $\approx50$ pc." (Results); "These are consistent with the measurement of $0.125\pm0.005$ Jy with $\pm15$% flux scale accuracy quoted by (cite: Reuter:2020) from an unresolved flux density measurement with APEX/LABOCA at the same frequency." (Results)
4. **Derive star formation rates and densities**: Compute brightness-weighted mean magnifications, correct the inherited SED-based star formation rate, and map star formation rate density.  
  > "We find a surface-brightness-weighted mean magnification of $16.6^{+0.3}_{-0.2}$ ($16.5^{+0.2}_{-0.2}$; $15.8^{+0.3}_{-0.2}$) for gradient (curvature; area-weighted) source regularisation types." (Results); "With these magnification corrections, we infer an obscured star formation rate" (Results); "The star formation rate density for each source reconstruction is shown in Fig. [2](#fig:reconstruction) (fourth panels), assuming that the dust temperature and emissivity are uniform over the source." (Results)
   - parameter: Reuter:2020 SED-based SFR 700±200 M⊙ yr⁻¹ and original magnification 20±3.  
  > "An obscured star formation rate of $700\pm200$  M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ was computed by (cite: Reuter:2020) for SPT 0538$-$50 by fitting the far-infrared–mm broad-band spectral energy distribution" (Results); "with a quoted magnification of $20\pm3$ based on lens modelling with observations at lower angular resolution." (Results)
   - assumption: Uniform dust temperature and emissivity over the source.  
  > "The star formation rate density for each source reconstruction is shown in Fig. [2](#fig:reconstruction) (fourth panels), assuming that the dust temperature and emissivity are uniform over the source." (Results)
   - assumption: Inherited modified black-body SED and Kroupa initial mass function.  
  > "a modified black-body and (cite: Kroupa:2001) initial mass function" (Results)
   - fitting: SED fitting inherited from Reuter:2020.  
  > "An obscured star formation rate of $700\pm200$  M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ was computed by (cite: Reuter:2020) for SPT 0538$-$50 by fitting the far-infrared–mm broad-band spectral energy distribution" (Results)
   - validation: Revised rates consistent with Reuter:2020; SED-fitting uncertainty dominates.  
  > "consistent with (cite: Reuter:2020) within the uncertainties." (Results); "Note that the uncertainties in the star formation rate from SED fitting dominate the overall uncertainties." (Results)
5. **Fit the compact disc and inspect substructure**: Fit a 2D Sérsic profile to the source and inspect departures from a smooth profile.  
  > "We fit a 2D Sérsic profile to the source using a Markov-Chain Monte Carlo sampler (emcee; (cite: emcee))" (Results); "the reconstructions for all regularisation types show a dual spiral arm morphology and several clumps with sizes of $\lesssim100$ pc at 3–9$\sigma$ significance." (Results)
   - software: emcee
   - assumption: Sérsic index 1 represents an exponential disc.  
  > "Sérsic index of $1.2\pm0.2$ (where an index of 1 is an exponential disc)" (Results)
   - fitting: Markov-Chain Monte Carlo using emcee.  
  > "We fit a 2D Sérsic profile to the source using a Markov-Chain Monte Carlo sampler (emcee; (cite: emcee))" (Results)
   - validation: Sérsic fit is poor; substructure appears across regularisations and in lensed images.  
  > "the source structure is not smooth and the Sérsic provides a poor fit" (Results); "the reconstructions for all regularisation types show a dual spiral arm morphology and several clumps with sizes of $\lesssim100$ pc at 3–9$\sigma$ significance." (Results); "These features can also be noted in the lensed images" (Results)
6. **Search for a bar signature**: Fit isophotes and inspect radial ellipticity and position-angle changes for all three regularisations.  
  > "To further characterise the source morphology, we fit isophotes using the isophote tool in the Python package photutils (cite: Bradley:2023)" (Results); "The ellipticity and position angle of the fitted isophotes for all three regularisation types are shown in Fig. [3](#fig:isophotes)." (Results)
   - software: photutils
   - parameter: Free centroid, ellipticity, position angle, 3rd and 4th order harmonics.  
  > "All isophote parameters were free to optimise (centroid, ellipticity, position angle, 3rd and 4th order harmonics)." (Results)
   - fitting: photutils isophote tool; all isophote parameters free.  
  > "To further characterise the source morphology, we fit isophotes using the isophote tool in the Python package photutils (cite: Bradley:2023)" (Results); "All isophote parameters were free to optimise (centroid, ellipticity, position angle, 3rd and 4th order harmonics)." (Results)
   - validation: Position-angle swing with ellipticity peaks interpreted as a typical bar signature.  
  > "The ellipticity position angle swings from $\approx25$ deg at 0.2 kpc to $-20$ deg at 0.6 kpc, with accompanying peaks in ellipticity" (Results); "which is a typical signature of a bar (e.g. (cite: Tsukui:2024; LeConte:2024))." (Results)
7. **Test morphology robustness and resolution needs**: Compare regularisation and lens angular freedom; smooth source to native 30 mas resolution.  
  > "We find that changes in the regularisation type and additional lens model freedom in the form of angular structure do not significantly alter the inferred source properties" (Discussion); "Crucially, we find that smoothing the source to the native resolution of the observations (30 mas) erases any evidence of the spiral morphology, suggesting that gravitational lensing is the only feasible approach to resolving these features." (Discussion)
   - parameter: 30 mas smoothing resolution.  
  > "smoothing the source to the native resolution of the observations (30 mas) erases any evidence of the spiral morphology" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Morphology robust to tested lens and regularisation choices; spiral evidence lost at native resolution.  
  > "Here, we have demonstrated that the lensed source reconstructions are robust to a combination of angular flexibility in the lens model and different choices of source regularisation." (Conclusions); "smoothing the source to the native resolution of the observations (30 mas) erases any evidence of the spiral morphology" (Discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- Dual spiral arms and a potential nuclear bar in the central kpc.  
  > "For SPT 0538$-$50, we find the central kpc of the galaxy exhibits a dual spiral arm morphology and a potential nuclear bar that could facilitate gas inflow, feeding the nuclear starburst and supermassive black hole." (Conclusions)
- Central emission resolves into two clumps.  
  > "The central region of the source is also not smooth or point-like but resolved into two clumps." (Results)
- Curvature and area-weighted gradient more effectively recover low surface brightness emission.  
  > "Additionally, the low surface brightness emission is more apparent where the magnification is lower, with curvature and area-weighted gradient regularisation types more effectively recovering this emission." (Results)
- Exponential profile, spiral arms and possible bar suggest a dynamically cold source.  
  > "Furthermore, the characteristic exponential profile, spiral arms and possible bar suggest that SPT 0538$-$50 may be dynamically cold, like local nuclear discs (cite: Gadotti:2020)." (Discussion)
- Tested regularisation and lens angular flexibility do not significantly change inferred source properties.  
  > "We find that changes in the regularisation type and additional lens model freedom in the form of angular structure do not significantly alter the inferred source properties" (Discussion)
- Spiral morphology disappears after smoothing to native resolution.  
  > "smoothing the source to the native resolution of the observations (30 mas) erases any evidence of the spiral morphology" (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long-baseline ALMA plus gravitational magnification accesses central DSFG structure at tens-of-pc scales.  
  > "High-resolution imaging ($\lesssim100$ pc) could answer this question and, thus, provide key insights into the structural evolution of DSFGs." (Introduction); "At cosmic noon, these scales are accessible only with the aid of gravitational lensing magnification." (Introduction); "We have shown how sophisticated gravitational lens modelling tools and long-baseline ALMA observations can be combined to produce observations with spatial resolution sufficient to resolve the inner structure of DSFGs." (Conclusions)
- Why this method: Test regularisation-dependent biases and characterise a possible bar with isophotes.  
  > "different types of regularisation may be appropriate for different sources, lead to stronger or weaker regularisation of the source surface brightness elements and introduce different biases on lens model parameters." (Data and lens modelling); "a technique commonly employed to characterise the structure of barred spirals in the local Universe (e.g. (cite: Gadotti:2007))." (Results)
- Alternative: Gradient, curvature and area-weighted gradient source regularisation.  
  > "We consider three types of source regularisation: gradient, curvature and area-weighted gradient." (Data and lens modelling)
- Alternative: Smooth Sérsic source gives a poor fit.  
  > "the source structure is not smooth and the Sérsic provides a poor fit" (Results)
- Alternative: Gas-rich major merger or inward clump migration as routes to central gas build-up.  
  > "The final stages of a gas-rich major merger could result in gas build-up in the central kpc (cite: Jogee:2006), or potentially an inward migration of gas clumps (cite: Dekel:2014)." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Transient tidally induced spirals versus long-lived secular spirals.  
  > "transient two-arm spirals reaching into the centre of the galaxy may be induced by tidal interactions (e.g. (cite: Dobbs:2010)) or they could be long-lived and produced secularly, such as via swing amplification (cite: Toomre:1981)." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Compact dust emission partly heated by an AGN remains possible.  
  > "Another consideration with SPT 0538$-$50 is whether the compact central dust emission could be attributed to heating by an AGN." (Discussion); "While there is no clear evidence for an AGN in the broad-band SED for this object (cite: Bothwell:2013), it also cannot be ruled out." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Observed spiral may be the centre of larger arms extending through unobscured stellar emission.  
  > "it could be that the spiral morphology of SPT 0538$-$50 represents only the centre of spiral arms that extend out to several kpc in unobscured stellar emission" (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: Agreement across reconstructions, source-noise analysis and recovered-flux comparison support the morphology; kinematics remain necessary for stronger physical conclusions.  
  > "Here, our source error analysis gives due diligence to questions of source structure significance, such as whether individual clumps are genuine or noise artefacts." (Discussion); "Here, we have demonstrated that the lensed source reconstructions are robust to a combination of angular flexibility in the lens model and different choices of source regularisation." (Conclusions); "This suggests that there is no significant contribution (\<10%) from undetected, extended low surface brightness dust emission." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Source-plane resolution is non-uniform because magnification varies.  
  > "As the magnification varies over the source, the effective angular resolution in the source plane is not uniform." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Lens-parameter uncertainties omitted from source uncertainty maps.  
  > "We generate uncertainty maps for all three models neglecting the uncertainties in lens model parameters." (Data and lens modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Uniform dust temperature and emissivity assumption is likely inaccurate.  
  > "we assumed that the dust emissivity and temperature are uniform over the source, but in reality, these are likely non-uniform." (Discussion); "Multi-frequency dust continuum observations at matched angular resolution would be required to test whether there is such a temperature gradient and compute the star formation rate density more robustly." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): AGN cannot be ruled out; X-ray and radio observations are lacking.  
  > "While there is no clear evidence for an AGN in the broad-band SED for this object (cite: Bothwell:2013), it also cannot be ruled out." (Discussion); "X-ray or radio observations (currently lacking) are warranted to test for the presence of radio jets or an accretion disc corona which definitively characterise AGN activity." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Matched-resolution stellar observations and emission-line kinematics are needed.  
  > "Greater clarity would be achieved with observations at matched angular resolution of stars and emission line kinematics." (Discussion); "More definitive answers on the role of nuclear spirals in the structural evolution of DSFGs can only come from gas kinematics." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Group-related mass sheet transform-like effects are untested and could change size and SFR.  
  > "our analysis does not consider the effect of a mass sheet transform-like effect that may result if the lens is part of a group." (Discussion); "This would reduce or inflate the size of the lensed source, along with its inferred star formation rate, without changing its structure" (Discussion); "It is unknown whether the lens of SPT 0538$-$50 is part of a group (cite: Stacey:2024), so we leave such investigation to future work." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Instrumental flux calibration and SED modelling limit overall source-property uncertainties.  
  > "We found that the factors limiting the overall uncertainties on the lensed source properties are instrumental flux calibration and spectral energy distribution modelling." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Continuum morphology alone does not measure rotation, inflow or a bar pattern speed.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The three regularisation tests share the same data and modelling framework, so their agreement is not independent confirmation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The compact central SFR density inherits both the global SED calibration and an assumed spatial conversion from dust brightness.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reported Sérsic parameter errors need caution because the paper describes the smooth profile as a poor fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Noise-realisation tests are difficult to assess without the visibility-noise and calibration-error prescriptions.
