1. **Make the HCN spectral images**: Separate continuum and line emissions in the u-v dataset, then CLEAN with natural weighting and a circular region slightly larger than Neptune

(Image synthesis of ALMA archived data)
   - software: Common Astronomy Software Applications, CLEAN
   - parameter: 320 × 320 pixels; 0.025″ spacing; natural weighting; 0.1 mJy threshold; gain 0.1; csclean  
  > "320$\times$320 pixels with 0.$\ensuremath{''}$025 pixel spacing, "natural" weighting, 0.1 mJy threshold, 0.1 gain and csclean mode." (Image synthesis of ALMA archived data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Achieved 0.42″ × 0.39″ beam resolves Neptune’s disk  
  > "The achieved synthesized beam size was 0.$\ensuremath{''}$42$\times$0.$\ensuremath{''}$39, which was fine enough to resolve the Neptune’s disk spatially." (Image synthesis of ALMA archived data)
2. **Map integrated HCN emission**: Integrate ±30 MHz covering the entire HCN emission line  
  > "To illustrate the spatial distribution of HCN on Neptune’s disk, an integrated-intensity map that integrates the $\pm$30 MHz frequency range, which covers the entire HCN emission line, was produced." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)

   - assumption: Ring structure arises from increasing atmospheric line-of-sight path length with emission angle

   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compare latitudes at fixed emission angle**: Measure intensities along 1.05″ and 0.75″ radius circles; average eastern and western hemispheres  
  > "shows the HCN intensity measured at the same emission angle along the 1.$''$05– and 0.$''$75– radius circle" (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission); "Eastern and western hemispheric intensities are averaged." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)

   - validation: Intensity errors from off-disk r.m.s.; latitude errors from the synthesized beam

4. **Remove the radial intensity trend**: Fit a radially averaged profile and map measured intensity divided by the averaged intensity; derive a latitudinally averaged ratio profile  
  > "The radially averaged profile was produced by the polynomial fitting method." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)

   - fitting: Polynomial fitting of the radially averaged intensity profile  
  > "The radially averaged profile was produced by the polynomial fitting method." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)
   - validation: Compare with the fixed-emission-angle structure; assess the localized dark feature against ~10% systematic intensity accuracy  
  > "The derived structure is similar to that measured along the same emission angle" (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission); "In turn, difference of intensity between bright and dark region on 60$^\circ$ S arc is $\sim$10$\%$, which is similar to the systematic error value of ALMA’s intensity measurement accuracy." 5. **Construct atmospheric spectra**: Use PSG radiative transfer to model spectra at 60° S and the equator  
  > "For the calculation, we employed the open-source software Planetary Spectrum Generator (PSG) (cite: Villanueva2018)." (Radiative transfer analysis); "We attempted to obtain the HCN abundance at two points on 60$^\circ$ S and the equator." (Radiative transfer analysis)

  > "For the temperature profile, we employed a disk-averaged result retrieved from (cite: Fletcher2010)." (Radiative transfer analysis)
   - assumption: Constant HCN VMR above p0 = 1.0 mbar  
  > "As the vertical HCN profile, a constant molecular VMR above a specific pressure level, $p_0$, was employed in this study." (Radiative transfer analysis); "we employed 1.0 mbar pressure level as $p_0$ because the value could reproduce the observed spectra to a relatively better extent." (Radiative transfer analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Select p0 = 1.0 mbar for relatively better reproduction of the observed spectra  
  > "we employed 1.0 mbar pressure level as $p_0$ because the value could reproduce the observed spectra to a relatively better extent." (Radiative transfer analysis)
6. **Retrieve HCN abundance**: Search VMR parameter space for the best-fit spectrum at each point  
  > "The best-fit spectrum for each point was identified within the VMR parameter space by the least-square method." (Radiative transfer analysis)
   - software: Planetary Spectrum Generator (PSG)
   - parameter: 1–sigma errors at Δχ² = 1.0  
  > "The 1–$\sigma$ significance level was used as the error value, corresponding to $\Delta\chi^2$ = 1.0." (Radiative transfer analysis)
   - assumption: Constant HCN VMR above 1.0 mbar  
  > "As the vertical HCN profile, a constant molecular VMR above a specific pressure level, $p_0$, was employed in this study." (Radiative transfer analysis); "we employed 1.0 mbar pressure level as $p_0$ because the value could reproduce the observed spectra to a relatively better extent." (Radiative transfer analysis)
   - fitting: Least-square VMR fit; Δχ² errors with Δχ² = 1.0 for 1–sigma  
  > "The best-fit spectrum for each point was identified within the VMR parameter space by the least-square method." (Radiative transfer analysis); "The 1–$\sigma$ significance level was used as the error value, corresponding to $\Delta\chi^2$ = 1.0." (Radiative transfer analysis)
   - validation: Inspect observed-minus-modeled residuals; equatorial line center within ±1.5 MHz is poorly fitted  
  > "Residuals between the observed and modeled spectra are also shown as red curves with an offset of -5 K." (Radiative transfer analysis); "For the equatorial spectra, the $\pm$1.5 MHz frequency region from the line center could not fit well." (Radiative transfer analysis)
7. **Evaluate temperature and source interpretations**: Compare the HCN intensity gradient with published temperature variations, circulation morphology and the cometary-impact hypothesis  
  > "Above 1.0 mbar, where HCN molecules are present, the inferred temperature variations were no more than 5 K, which are not enough to explain the observed HCN intensity variation." ## Qualitative findings

- HCN is enhanced in an equatorial belt  
  > "After correcting the effect of different optical path lengths, a spatial distribution of HCN emissions is derived over Neptune’s disk, and it clearly shows a band-like HCN enhancement at the equator." (Abstract)
- A weaker HCN intensity peak occurs at the south pole  
  > "In addition, a relatively weak peak is found at the south pole." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)
- The localized western depletion spot at 60° S is marginal  
  > "At the southern mid-latitude of $\sim$60$^\circ$ S, a low intensity ratio region is found in the western hemisphere." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission); "Thus, detection of the HCN depletion spot on 60$^\circ$ S arc is marginal." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)
- HCN intensity variation is attributed mainly to abundance variation  
  > "Thus, in this study, we consider that the variation in HCN line intensity was caused mainly by a variation in HCN abundance." (Possible sources of HCN intensity gradient)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Spatially resolved ALMA observations can reveal HCN structure unavailable from disk-averaged single-dish spectra  
  > "The ALMA achieves high spatial resolution observation of solar system objects in millimeter and submillimeter wavelength, whereas previous observations using single-dish telescopes were able to obtain only the disk-averaged spectra of HCN." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Fixed-emission-angle comparisons isolate latitudinal intensity variation; p0 = 1.0 mbar better reproduces spectra  
  > "which can exhibit latitudinal intensity variation without variation of the emission angle." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission); "we employed 1.0 mbar pressure level as $p_0$ because the value could reproduce the observed spectra to a relatively better extent." (Radiative transfer analysis)
- Alternative: Temperature variation alone is insufficient to explain the observed HCN intensity variation  
  > "Above 1.0 mbar, where HCN molecules are present, the inferred temperature variations were no more than 5 K, which are not enough to explain the observed HCN intensity variation." (Possible sources of HCN intensity gradient)
- Alternative: Decreasing high-altitude HCN abundance, temperature, or both could explain the equatorial line-center mismatch; detailed profiles were not retrieved  
  > "This result leads to three possible scenarios, that the HCN abundance or temperature decreases, or both decrease in high altitude region." (Radiative transfer analysis); "we did not attempt to obtain more specific abundance or temperature profile" (Radiative transfer analysis)
- Alternative: Simple meridional diffusion from a single cometary collision does not fit the full latitudinal distribution  
  > "Such a latitudinal distribution does not fit well with a simple meridional diffusion of HCN from a single collision." (External source model)
- How they decided it was enough: No more detailed vertical retrieval because the line-center residual area was small and its possible column-density effect was considered limited  
  > "because the area of the line center residual is quite small compared with the total area of the emission spectra and the possible effect on the retrieved column density is very limited." (Radiative transfer analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Localized HCN depletion-spot detection is marginal  
  > "Thus, detection of the HCN depletion spot on 60$^\circ$ S arc is marginal." (Analysis of spatial distribution of HCN emission)
- Limitation (authors): Equatorial line center within ±1.5 MHz is not fitted well  
  > "For the equatorial spectra, the $\pm$1.5 MHz frequency region from the line center could not fit well." (Radiative transfer analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Circular 0.41″ model beam approximates the slightly elliptical observed beam  
  > "Because PSG can employ a horizontally symmetrical beam, a 0.$''$41 diameter beam was employed while the true beam shape is slightly elliptical." (Radiative transfer analysis)
- Limitation (authors): HCN probes the upper stratosphere, whereas the earlier circulation model is mainly based on upper-tropospheric observations  
  > "our study probes the upper stratosphere while the previously suggested two-cell meridional circulation model has been mainly based on observations of the upper troposphere." (Implication for global circulation)
- Limitation (authors): A CS latitudinal-distribution analysis is needed to evaluate the impact hypothesis  
  > "To evaluate the cometary impact hypothesis, a new analysis of the latitudinal distribution of CS is crucial." (External source model)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abstract gives an equatorial VMR of 1.92 ppb, while the main text gives 1.66 ppb above the equivalent pressure level; these are conflicting answer keys.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reported VMR errors do not establish robustness to the adopted temperature profile, pressure cutoff, beam approximation or calibration systematics.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The temperature argument relies on literature observations from different epochs rather than a simultaneous temperature map.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The circulation and impact interpretations are morphological comparisons, without fitted transport or impact models.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The supplied conversion omits some atmospheric species and reactions, preventing recovery of the complete atmospheric model from this text.
