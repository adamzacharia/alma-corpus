## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct continuum-subtracted line cubes**: Subtract continuum with uvcontsub; CLEAN with robust weighting and a 2.5× off-source-rms stop threshold; compare fluxes with 15″ tapered images.  
  > "The continuum is subtracted with `uvcontsub`, fitting channels away from the line emission." (Sample and Observations); "Line images were constructed using the task `CLEAN` with robust weighting (`robust = 0.5`) and a stop threshold 2.5 times the root mean square (rms) of the off-source channels." (Sample and Observations)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, CLEAN
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; stop threshold = 2.5× off-source-channel rms.  
  > "with robust weighting (`robust = 0.5`) and a stop threshold 2.5 times the root mean square (rms) of the off-source channels." (Sample and Observations)
   - parameter: 15″ tapered beam for flux comparison.  
  > "we extracted fluxes using a 15$''$ tapered beam" (Sample and Observations)
   - fitting: Continuum fitted in channels away from line emission.  
  > "The continuum is subtracted with `uvcontsub`, fitting channels away from the line emission." (Sample and Observations)
   - validation: Noise within ∼30% of theoretical limit; robust/tapered fluxes agree 1:1 within 3σ for all objects.  
  > "The measured 1 $\sigma$ noise level per beam per channel (typically 1.5–5 mJy) is consistent within $\sim 30 \%$ of the theoretical noise limit." (Sample and Observations); "we extracted fluxes using a 15$''$ tapered beam, finding a 1:1 ratio within 3 $\sigma$ for all objects" (Sample and Observations)
2. **Measure CO fluxes and extract spectra**: Form moment 0 maps from spectral channels above 1σ; sum pixels inside the 2σ source contour; extract integrated spectra from the same line-emitting region.  
  > "We use the channels above the 1 $\sigma$ level of the spectrum to generate the CO intensity (moment 0) map" (CO Luminosity); "The integrated CO flux is measured from the intensity map by summing up the pixels within the 2 $\sigma$ contour of the source emission." (CO Luminosity); "is extracted from the line-emitting region above 2 $\sigma$ of the intensity map." (CO Luminosity)
   - parameter: Flux uncertainty: standard deviation of 20 off-source, equal-pixel-count circular-aperture measurements.  
  > "We estimate the uncertainty from the standard deviation of 20 repeated off-source measurements using a circular aperture containing the same number of pixels as those within the 2 $\sigma$ contour of the source." (CO Luminosity)
   - parameter: Exclude ∼5%–10% absolute-flux-scale uncertainty.  
  > "The uncertainty of the absolute flux scale, $\sim 5\%-10\%$ (cite: Fomalont2014Msngr; Bonato2018MNRAS), is not included in our final flux uncertainty." (CO Luminosity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Prior JCMT/IRAM line fluxes agree to ≲50%; PG 1341+258 remains ∼4σ under different velocity channel widths.  
  > "Our line fluxes are reasonably consistent, with deviations $\lesssim 50\%$." (CO Luminosity); "We believe PG 1341+258 is marginally detected, because we always detect the source with $\sim 4\,\sigma$ significance when we clean the data with different velocity channel widths." (CO(2–1) Measurements for Individual Objects)
3. **Fit integrated line widths**: Fit double-peak Gaussian or standard Gaussian profiles; derive W50 and W20 from the fitted profile.  
  > "The double-peak Gaussian function is defined as (cite: Tiley2016MNRAS)" (CO Line Width); "Under these circumstances, the data should be fit with a standard Gaussian function" (CO Line Width); "Table [2](#tab:alma) lists measurements of both $W_{50}$ and $W_{20}$" (CO Line Width)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: −500 < v0 < 500 km s−1; w >0 km s−1; σ >0 km s−1; AG >0.  
  > "$-500\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}} < v_0 < 500\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ is the central velocity" (CO Line Width); "$w$ ($>0\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$) is the half width of the central parabola, $\sigma$ ($>0\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$) is the width of the edge half-Gaussian profile" (CO Line Width); "$A_\mathrm{G}>0$ is the peak flux of the half-Gaussian edges" (CO Line Width)
   - parameter: Choose standard Gaussian if its reduced χ² is closer to unity or AG/AC <2/3.  
  > "(1) the reduced chi-square of the standard Gaussian fit is closer to unity than that of the double-peak Gaussian function;" (CO Line Width); "(2) $A_\mathrm{G}/A_\mathrm{C} 
< 2/3$." (CO Line Width)
   - parameter: Double-peak: W50 = 2(w + √(2 ln 2)σ); W20 = 2(w + √(2 ln 5)σ).  
  > "W_{50}=2(w+\sqrt{2\ln 2} \sigma)," (CO Line Width); "W_{20}=2(w+\sqrt{2\ln 5} \sigma)." (CO Line Width)
   - parameter: Standard Gaussian: W50 = 2√(2 ln 2)σ; W20 = 2√(2 ln 5)σ.  
  > "for which $W_{50}=2\sqrt{2\ln2}\sigma$ and $W_{20}=2\sqrt{2\ln5}\sigma$." (CO Line Width)
   - assumption: Use optical redshifts in final analysis.  
  > "The systemic velocities of the CO line agree closely ($<5\%$ difference) with the optical redshifts, and for our final analysis we simply adopt the latter." (CO Line Width)
   - fitting: Markov chain Monte Carlo with emcee.  
  > "We use a Markov chain Monte Carlo method in the `emcee` package (cite: ForemanMackey2013PASP) to perform the fit." (CO Line Width)
   - validation: All detections fit successfully except low-S/N PG 1341+258; CO systemic velocities differ by <5% from optical redshifts.  
  > "all of which were successfully fit, apart from the tentative detection of PG 1341+258, which suffers from exceptionally low signal-to-noise ratio." (CO Line Width); "The systemic velocities of the CO line agree closely ($<5\%$ difference) with the optical redshifts" (CO Line Width)
4. **Calculate luminosities and homogenize literature data**: Convert integrated line fluxes to luminosities; recalculate published CO(1–0) luminosities using the adopted cosmology; homogenize literature line widths with the ALMA median W20/W50.  
  > "L^\prime_\mathrm{CO} = 3.25 \times 10^7\, S_\mathrm{CO}\Delta\nu\,
\nu_\mathrm{obs}^{-2}\, D_L^2\, (1+z)^{-3}," (CO Luminosity); "The published CO fluxes were converted to luminosities according to our adopted cosmological parameters." (Measurements from the Literature); "We homogenize the line widths adopting $W_{20}/W_{50} = 1.17 \pm 0.19$, the median ratio measured in our ALMA sample." (Measurements from the Literature)
   - parameter: Flux in Jy km s−1; observed frequency in GHz; luminosity distance in Mpc; luminosity in K km s−1 pc².  
  > "$L^\prime_\mathrm{CO}$ is the CO line luminosity in units of $\mathrm{K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^2}$" (CO Luminosity); "$S_\mathrm{CO}\Delta\nu$ is the integrated line flux in units of $\mathrm{Jy\,km\,s^{-1}}$" (CO Luminosity); "$\nu_\mathrm{obs}$ is the observed frequency of the CO(2–1) line in GHz, and $D_L$ is the luminosity distance in Mpc." (CO Luminosity)
   - parameter: Treat PG 0003+199 and PG 2214+139 literature CO(1–0) detections as upper limits.  
  > "According to (cite: Shangguan2018ApJ), the 3 $\sigma$ CO(1–0) detections of PG 0003+199 (cite: Maiolino1997ApJ) and PG 2214+139 (cite: Scoville2003ApJ) were likely overestimated." (Measurements from the Literature); "We regard them as upper limits." (Measurements from the Literature)
   - assumption: Ωm = 0.308; ΩΛ = 0.692; H0 = 67.8 km s−1 Mpc−1.  
  > "We adopt the following cosmological parameters: $\Omega_m = 0.308$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.692$, and $H_{0}=67.8$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ (cite: Planck2016AA)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate excitation ratios**: Compare CO(2–1) with literature CO(1–0); calculate R21 percentiles with the Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator; inspect relations with radiation-field intensity and AGN luminosity.  
  > "The $50^{+25}_{-25}$th percentile value, calculated with the Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator `kmestimate` in `IRAF.ASURV` (cite: Feigelson1985ApJ; Lavalley1992ASPC), is $R_{21} = 0.62^{+0.15}_{-0.07}$." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio); "we checked but failed to find a clear correlation between $R_{21}$ and $\langle U \rangle$ for the quasar host galaxies" (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio); "$R_{21}$ seems unrelated to the AGN luminosity" (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
   - software: IRAF.ASURV, kmestimate
   - parameter: 15 objects with both transitions observed; eight detections in both lines; 50th and 25th/75th percentiles.  
  > "Of the 15 quasars with both CO(1–0) and CO(2–1) observations, eight are detected in both lines." (Measurements from the Literature); "The $50^{+25}_{-25}$th percentile value" (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
   - assumption: Thermalized, optically thick emission has R21 = 1.  
  > "If the CO emission is thermalized and optically thick, the intrinsic brightness temperature and luminosity of the line are independent of $J$ and rest frequency, and $R_{21}=1$." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
   - fitting: Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimation; no formal correlation test because too few statistically meaningful measurements.  
  > "calculated with the Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator `kmestimate` in `IRAF.ASURV`" (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio); "Unfortunately, the number of objects with statistically meaningful measurements is too small to perform a formal statistical test." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
   - validation: Check ACA flux recovery with 15″ taper; compare R21 with global nearby inactive and active galaxies.  
  > "which is confirmed by our 15$''$ tapered measurements" (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio); "Therefore, our low-redshift quasars exhibit $R_{21}$ values fully consistent with those derived from global measurements of nearby inactive and active galaxies." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
6. **Derive molecular gas masses**: Convert new CO(2–1) luminosities to CO(1–0) with median R21 = 0.62; derive molecular masses with αCO = 3.1.  
  > "We adopt this median value of $R_{21}$ to convert all the new ALMA CO luminosities" (CO Luminosity); "(9) CO line luminosity, converted from $J=$ (2–1) to $J=$ (1–0) with a line ratio of 0.62." (Table 2); "(10) Molecular gas mass derived from CO line luminosity, assuming $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}=3.1\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{(K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{2})^{-1}}$." (Table 2)
   - parameter: Do not propagate R21 uncertainty.  
  > "We do not consider the uncertainty on $R_{21}$, as it is hardly well-constrained by our data." (CO Luminosity)
   - assumption: All new ALMA luminosities use R21 = 0.62.  
  > "We find a median value of $R_{21} = 0.62^{+0.15}_{-0.07}$" (CO Luminosity); "We adopt this median value of $R_{21}$ to convert all the new ALMA CO luminosities" (CO Luminosity)
   - assumption: αCO = 3.1 M⊙ (K km s−1 pc²)−1 with 0.3 dex uncertainty.  
  > "we adopt $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ = 3.1 $M_\odot\,\mathrm{(K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{2})^{-1}}$ with 0.3 dex uncertainty, as recommended by (cite: Sandstrom2013ApJ)" (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: R21 agrees with nearby-galaxy/AGN values; varying R21 from 0.5 to 1.0 could add ∼0.3 dex mass uncertainty.  
  > "if $R_{21}$ varies from 0.5 to 1.0, it could contribute to the final uncertainty of $M_\mathrm{H_2}$ as significantly as $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ ($\sim 0.3$ dex)." (CO Luminosity); "It is reassuring that our estimated value of $R_{21}$ agrees well with values found in nearby galaxies and AGNs" (CO Luminosity)
7. **Assess sample representativeness**: Compare 40 CO-measured quasars with 70 parent z<0.3 PG quasars using two-sample Peto-Prentice tests.  
  > "Figure [1](#fig:smp) compares the CO-measured subsample with the parent sample of 70 PG quasars with $z<0.3$." (Sample and Observations); "a two-sample Peto-Prentice test" (Sample and Observations); "The same holds for the distributions of 5100 Å AGN luminosity, BH mass, and IR luminosity" (Sample and Observations)
   - parameter: Compare redshift, 5100 Å AGN luminosity, BH mass, and IR luminosity distributions.  
  > "the two redshift distributions are not statistically different" (Sample and Observations); "The same holds for the distributions of 5100 Å AGN luminosity, BH mass, and IR luminosity" (Sample and Observations)

   - validation: _not stated_

   - parameter: LIR independent; L′CO(1–0) dependent.

   - parameter: Assign 0.1 dex uncertainty where literature errors are unavailable.

   - assumption: Use LIR,host from Shangguan2018ApJ.

## Qualitative findings

- CO-measured quasars are representative of the z<0.3 PG parent sample.  
  > "We conclude that the CO-measured sample is representative of the parent sample of $z<0.3$ PG quasars." (Sample and Observations)
- Low-J CO is subthermally excited, similar to low-redshift galaxies and AGNs.  
  > "We conclude that, similar as low-$z$ galaxies and AGNs, the low-$J$ CO emission of our quasars is subthermally excited." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
- No clear R21 correlation with radiation-field intensity or AGN luminosity.  
  > "No clear correlation is found between $R_{21}$ and the intensity of the interstellar radiation field or the luminosity of the active nucleus." (Abstract)
- Quasar hosts follow a tight, approximately linear IR–CO relation consistent with starbursts.  
  > "is consistent with a linear relation between $L_\mathrm{IR}$ and $L^\prime_\mathrm{CO\mbox{\scriptsize (1--0)}}$." (IR versus CO Relation); "Both the slope and the zero point are consistent with the relation for starburst galaxies." (IR versus CO Relation)
- Quasar-host αCO is not too dissimilar from ordinary star-forming galaxies and lower-luminosity AGNs.  
  > "The above analysis, while far from a rigorous derivation, does suggest that the host galaxies of low-redshift quasars have an $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ value not too dissimilar from that of ordinary star-forming galaxies and lower luminosity AGNs." (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor)
- Almost all continuum sources are unresolved; nearly half of CO(2–1) sources are resolved.  
  > "the continuum emission of almost all of the quasars are unresolved with our ACA measurements, while CO(2–1) of nearly half of the quasars is resolved." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
- Literature quasars have higher molecular gas fractions than the new ALMA sample because of observational selection.  
  > "The quasars gathered from the literature on average have a higher molecular gas fraction than those newly observed using ALMA." (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor); "This is an obvious observational selection effect." (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: UV-selected PG quasars avoid selection on dust/gas properties; nearby source brightness permits ACA detections with moderate resolution.  
  > "It is important to recognize that the PG sample was originally ultraviolet-selected, and hence was not selected based on the dust or gas properties of the quasars." (Introduction); "The brighness of these nearby quasars allows to obtain significant detections using ACA with moderate resolving power (FWHM $\approx 6\ensuremath{''}$) in relatively short exposure times." (Sample and Observations)
- Why this method: Model profiles improve low-S/N line widths; Linmix variable ordering accommodates CO upper limits; Peto-Prentice handles censoring.  
  > "For spectra with relatively low signal-to-noise ratio, we can obtain more accurate line widths by fitting a model line profile to the data instead of measuring them directly from the observed spectrum." (CO Line Width); "Since `Linmix` only allows upper limits on the dependent variable, we treat $L_\mathrm{IR}$ as the independent variable and $L^\prime_\mathrm{CO\mbox{\scriptsize (1--0)}}$ as the dependent variable." (IR versus CO Relation); "The Peto-Prentice test is adopted to work with samples including censored data (in our case, IR luminosity)." (Sample and Observations)
- Alternative: Standard Gaussian replaces double-peak Gaussian when reduced χ² is closer to unity or AG/AC <2/3.  
  > "(1) the reduced chi-square of the standard Gaussian fit is closer to unity than that of the double-peak Gaussian function;" (CO Line Width); "(2) $A_\mathrm{G}/A_\mathrm{C} 
< 2/3$." (CO Line Width)
- Alternative: Low R21 could reflect subthermal excitation or gas temperature ≲10 K; authors favor subthermal excitation.  
  > "The low values of $R_{21}$ for our quasars indicate that the molecular gas is optically thick but either is sub-thermally excited (cite: OcanaFlaquer2010AA; Husemann2017MNRAS) or has low temperature ($\lesssim 10$ K; (cite: Braine1992AA))." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio); "We conclude that, similar as low-$z$ galaxies and AGNs, the low-$J$ CO emission of our quasars is subthermally excited." (The CO(2–1)/CO(1–0) Ratio)
- Alternative: ULIRG-like αCO = 0.8 would give improbably low molecular gas fractions.  
  > "Such a low value of $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ would result in molecular gas mass fractions substantially lower than those of star-forming galaxies" (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor); "This seems improbable." (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor); "$\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ = 0.8 $M_\odot\,\mathrm{(K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{2})^{-1}}$, a value commonly advocated for ultraluminous IR galaxies (ULIRGs; (cite: Downes1998ApJ))." (CO-to-H$_2$ Conversion Factor)
- Alternative: A 15″ tapered imaging check gives fluxes consistent with the robust-weighted images.  
  > "we extracted fluxes using a 15$''$ tapered beam, finding a 1:1 ratio within 3 $\sigma$ for all objects" (Sample and Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: CLEAN stopped at 2.5× rms; achieved noise checked against theoretical limits and recovered flux checked with a tapered beam.  
  > "a stop threshold 2.5 times the root mean square (rms) of the off-source channels." (Sample and Observations); "The measured 1 $\sigma$ noise level per beam per channel (typically 1.5–5 mJy) is consistent within $\sim 30 \%$ of the theoretical noise limit." (Sample and Observations); "we extracted fluxes using a 15$''$ tapered beam, finding a 1:1 ratio within 3 $\sigma$ for all objects" (Sample and Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Absolute flux-scale uncertainty is excluded from final flux errors.  
  > "The uncertainty of the absolute flux scale, $\sim 5\%-10\%$ (cite: Fomalont2014Msngr; Bonato2018MNRAS), is not included in our final flux uncertainty." (CO Luminosity)
- Limitation (authors): R21 uncertainty is poorly constrained and not propagated into masses.  
  > "We do not consider the uncertainty on $R_{21}$, as it is hardly well-constrained by our data." (CO Luminosity)
- Limitation (authors): Single-dish CO(1–0) may underestimate emission and bias R21.  
  > "Meanwhile, CO(1–0) emission may still be underestimated to some extent, when the emission size is comparable to the beam size but its spatial distribution is unknown from the single-dish observation." - Limitation (authors): Too few meaningful measurements for formal R21 correlation tests.
