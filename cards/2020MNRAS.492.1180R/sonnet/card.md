## Analysis recipe

1. **Map SO, PN, PO emission and identify P-spots**: Integrated maps in velocity ranges; seven P-spots identified visually in PN map  
  > "peaking towards several visually-identified spots (P-spots, hereafter) located along the cavities of the bipolar outflow" (3.1.2)
   - software: CASA, MADCUBA
   - parameter: Velocity ranges [-6.5,-4.5], [-4.5,-2.0], [-2.0,0] km/s  
  > "three different velocity ranges (\[-6.5,-4.5\], \[-4.5,-2.0\] and \[-2.0, 0\] km s$^{-1}$)" (3.1.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectra**: Spectra from circular regions at P-spot, MM, HV positions  
  > "The spectra extracted in a circular region of 3$^{\prime\prime}$ diameter centered on the positions of the P-spots" (3.1.2)
   - software: MADCUBA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **LTE fit of column densities, velocities, linewidths**: MADCUBA-AUTOFIT synthetic LTE spectra with CDMS entries incl. 14N hyperfine  
  > "we have used the MADCUBA-AUTOFIT tool that compares the observed spectra with the LTE synthetic spectra of the different species" (3.1.3 LTE analysis)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM, AUTOFIT
   - parameter: Tex fixed: 5 K for PN/PO, 34 K for SO; free N, v, FWHM  
  > "Then, fixing $T_{\rm ex}$, we run MADCUBA-AUTOFIT leaving $N$, $\varv$, and $FWHM$ as free parameters." (3.1.3 LTE analysis)
   - assumption: LTE, single transition so Tex cannot be derived  
  > "Since our data contain only a single rotational transition of the three species (PN, PO and SO), the excitation temperature cannot be derived, and hence we assumed fixed values." (3.1.3 LTE analysis)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt least squares; errors from covariance matrix  
  > "This tool provides the best non-linear least-squared fit using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm." (3.1.3 LTE analysis)
   - validation: Tex sensitivity: column densities vary by factor 2.5 (PN), 2.1 (PO) over 5-50 K  
  > "the column densities of PN and PO can vary by a factor of 2.5 and 2.1, respectively, assuming different values of $T_{\rm ex}$ in the range of 5$-$50 K" (3.1.3 LTE analysis)
4. **Non-LTE test of PO/PN ratio**: RADEX with n(H2)=1e4,1e5,1e6 cm-3 deriving column densities matching line fluxes  
  > "we have run RADEX assuming three different values: $n$(H$_2$) = 1$\times$10$^{4}$, 1$\times$10$^{5}$ and 1$\times$10$^{6}$ cm$^{-3}$." (3.1.4 Non-LTE analysis)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Tkin = 20 K, background 2.73 K  
  > "The calculations assume a kinetic temperature of $T_{\rm kin}$ = 20 K and a background temperature of 2.73 K." (3.1.4 Non-LTE analysis)
   - assumption: He collision rates scaled to H2 (x1.385 PN, x1.4 PO)  
  > "For PO, the recent collision data with He (described in Appendix [7](#appendix-PO-collision-rates)) were scaled by 1.4 to mimic H$_2$." (3.1.4 Non-LTE analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with LTE ratios  
  > "In all cases, the PO/PN ratio clearly increases with increasing distance from the central protostar, supporting the role of photochemistry to explain the observed relative abundances of PN and PO." (Discussion, Hints from molecular abundance ratios)
5. **Analyse ROSINA masses for P-bearing species**: Peak analysis at masses 31, 34, 43, 45, 47 Da; correlation of m46.696 with P and 35Cl; PO vs SO correlation  
  > "we checked the correlation of the ratio of this peak with the P peak on mass 31 Da and with the $^{35}$Cl peak on mass 35 Da." (3.2)
   - assumption: PO ionization cross section equal to NO; same sensitivity for SO and PO  
  > "we assume that the ionization cross section of PO is the same as for NO" (3.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CCl excluded because ratio to 35Cl not constant while ratio to P is  
  > "This is the case for P, but not for $^{35}$Cl." (3.2)

## Qualitative findings

- PN and PO arise in low-velocity, narrow-line gas in outflow cavity walls, not at high-velocity outflow axis, hot core or starless core  
  > "The ALMA maps show that the emission of PN and PO arises from several spots associated with low-velocity gas with narrow linewidths in the cavity walls of a bipolar outflow." (abstract)
