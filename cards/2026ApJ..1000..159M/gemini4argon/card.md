(3.1. ALMA data); "We simply used a dirty image without a primary beam correction because the S/Ns of our target lines are not expected to be high enough to require cleaning procedure and we require uniform noise distribution within a field of view." (3.1. ALMA data)

   - parameter: natural weighting

  > "After removing channels _±_ 300 km s _[−]_ [1] around the detected [O iii]88 _µ_ m and expected [O iii]52 _µ_ m line frequencies" (3.1. ALMA data)

  > "We simply used a dirty image without a primary beam correction because the S/Ns of our target lines are not expected to be high enough to require cleaning procedure" (3.1. ALMA data)

3. **Derive total gas mass, ionized gas mass, and dust-to-gas ratio (D/G)**: Compute total gas mass by subtracting magnification-corrected stellar mass from dynamical mass (M_gas = M_dyn - M_*), estimate ionized gas mass from Hbeta luminosity and electron density using Chávez et al.   > "Here, we computed the gas mass by subtracting the magnificationcorrected stellar mass from the dynamical mass ( _M_ gas = _M_ dyn _−_ _M_ _∗_ ) assuming negligible dark matter contribution within galaxy centers (≲ _r_ _e_ )." ## Qualitative findings

- Galaxies at z > 12 (GHZ2 and GS-z14-0) have lower dust-to-stellar mass ratios than galaxies at z = 4-7, even if a lower dust temperature of T_dust = 50 K is assumed.  
  > "Our results indicate a lower dust content in galaxies at _z_ _>_ 12 compared to those at _z_ = 4–7, a trend that persists when compared with samples from Fudamoto et al. (2023), which were derived using a similar methodology." (4.1. Dust production and evolution at cosmic dawn)
- The low dust masses and D/G ratios at z > 12 disfavor models with extreme dust condensation efficiency in stellar ejecta, and instead support models with moderate condensation efficiency and short metal accretion timescales (τ_acc ~ 5-100 Myr) that later build up dust at z ~ 7.  
  > "These constraints are in tension with a high dust condensation efficiency in stellar ejecta but are consistent with models with a short metal accretion timescale at _z_ _>_ 12." (abstract); "If our target galaxies at _z_ _>_ 12 are progenitors of these _z_ _∼_ 7 massive galaxies, our results support the short _τ_ acc model with _τ_ acc _∼_ 5–100 Myr, while the high _f_ cond model is unsupported." (4.1. Dust production and evolution at cosmic dawn)
- A top-heavy IMF would require a very low (< 4%) dust survival rate in GS-z14-0, suggesting a top-heavy IMF is not preferred to explain the overabundance of UV-bright galaxies at z > 10.  
  > "Specifically, ≲ 4% dust survival rate is required in GS-z14-0 under _M_ ch = 10 instead of _M_ ch = 0 _._ 35 in the Larson IMF (comparable to the top-heavy IMF), which is smaller than the expectations from several models (e.g., Marassi et al. 2015)." (4.1. Dust production and evolution at cosmic dawn); "it may suggest that the top-heavy IMF scenario is not preferred to explain the overabundance of UV-bright galaxies at _z_ _>_ 10 from their low dust contents." (4.1. Dust production and evolution at cosmic dawn)
- Both GHZ2 and GS-z14-0 exhibit starburst-like star formation efficiencies (~ 10 Gyr^-1) on 10 Myr timescales, suggesting starburst phases are common among UV-bright galaxies at z > 12.  
  > "The star formation efficiencies, defined as the SFR divided by molecular gas mass, reach _∼_ 10 Gyr _[−]_ [1] in a 10 Myr timescale, aligning with the expected redshift evolution of ‘starburst’ galaxies" (abstract); "both GHZ2 and GS-z14-0 are likely under a starburst episode, which may suggest that this ‘starburst’ phase with efficient gas consumption is more ubiquitous among UV-bright galaxies at _z_ _>_ 12 than at _z_ _∼_ 6." (4.2. Star formation efficiency)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Ultra-deep ALMA observations of rest-frame 88 µm and 52 µm continuum and [O III] emission in two of the highest-redshift galaxies known (z = 12.3 and z = 14.2), combined with JWST metallicities and star formation histories, probe dust emergence and gas consumption when the Universe was < 300 Myr old.  
  > "In this paper, we examine the interstellar medium of two _z_ _>_ 12 galaxies using ultra-deep ALMA observations of the [O iii] emission and the dust continuum." (1. INTRODUCTION); "Leveraging the gas phase and stellar metallicities as well as stellar assembly histories observed at _z_ _>_ 12, which have been available thanks to the exceptional sensitivity of the _James_ _Webb_ _Space_ _Telescope_ (JWST), we aim to explore dust production, dust growth, and star formation efficiency in these early galaxies" (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Dirty continuum images with natural weighting and no primary beam correction were used to maximize S/N and maintain uniform noise across the field; the Inoue et al. (2020) clumpy radiative equilibrium model was chosen because clumpy geometry is a reliable approximation for high-z galaxies and yields conservative dust mass upper limits.  
  > "we created continuum images with a natural weighting to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio. We simply used a dirty image without a primary beam correction because the S/Ns of our target lines are not expected to be high enough to require cleaning procedure and we require uniform noise distribution within a field of view." (3.1. ALMA data); "Among the three available models for the dust geometry provided in I20 (see also, Imara et al. 2018), we specifically employed the “clumpy” geometry as it is considered a reliable approximation for high- _z_ galaxies (e.g., Fudamoto et al. 2023; Kohandel et al. 2023). Indeed, this ‘clumpy’ geometry provided looser constraints on _M_ dust than other geometries in I20" (3.2. Mdust measurement)
- Alternative: Spherical or shell dust geometries in the Inoue et al. (2020) model (rejected in favor of clumpy geometry, which gives looser, more conservative constraints on M_dust)  
  > "Indeed, this ‘clumpy’ geometry provided looser constraints on _M_ dust than other geometries in I20, i.e., ‘spherical’ or ‘shell’ under the same assumptions, and therefore our analysis is considered to be conservative." (3.2. Mdust measurement)
- Alternative: Fixed dust temperature T_dust = 50 K modified blackbody assumption (shown for comparison, yields ~1 dex larger M_dust limit and disagrees with observed A_V)  
  > "We also illustrated the 3 _σ_ upper limit under the assumption of _T_ dust = 50 K in the pink circles to just demonstrate how I20 model gives tight constraints. Note also that these dust mass constraints do not agree with the observed _A_ _V_ values (contrary to the values inferred using the I20 model)." (Figure 2 caption); "This conclusion holds even when assuming _T_ dust = 50 K (e.g., Sommovigo et al. 2022a,b; Mitsuhashi et al. 2024), implying a dust mass limit about _∼_ 1 dex larger than our fiducial value" (4.1. Dust production and evolution at cosmic dawn)
- Alternative: Smaller UV effective radius r_e,UV = 39 ± 10 pc from Ono et al. (2023) for GHZ2 (tested; gives tighter M_dust and M_gas constraints than the fiducial 105 ± 9 pc)  
  > "Note that in the case of GHZ2, if we use the effective radius of _r_ _e,_ UV = 39 _±_ 10 pc from Ono et al. In their model, the super-Eddington star formation occurs with significant radiation pressure.
