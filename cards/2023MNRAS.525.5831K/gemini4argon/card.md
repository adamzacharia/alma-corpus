## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate systemic redshifts from MUSE He II 1640 spectra**: Extract 1D He II 1640 spectra from MUSE cubes in 1 arcsec (5 pixel) diameter apertures at host galaxy positions (set by VLA radio cores and Spitzer IRAC continuum) and fit single or double Gaussian profiles  
  > "The 1D spectra are extracted from aperture diameters of 5 pixels or 1 ′′ at the positions of the host galaxies which are determined from the radio cores (Carilli et al. 1997; Pentericci et al. 2000) and the Spitzer imaging" (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation); "We began by applying a Gaussian fit to the He ii profiles of MRC 0943-242, TN J0205+2242, TN J0121+1320 and 4C+04.11" (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation)
   - parameter: Aperture diameter = 5 pixels (1 arcsec)  
  > "The 1D spectra are extracted from aperture diameters of 5 pixels or 1 ′′ at the positions of the host galaxies" (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation)
   - parameter: Gaussian fit bounds: peak A free, width sigma constrained to 2 - 15 Angstrom, centre lambda_c allowed to deviate +- 5 Angstrom from initial value  
  > "the Gaussian peak ( 𝐴 ), width ( 𝜎 ) and centre ( 𝜆 𝑐 ) were fit to the data with 𝐴 as a free parameter and sigma constrained within the wavelength range 2 − 15 Å and 𝜆 𝑐 is allowed to deviate ±5 Å from its initial value." (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation)
   - assumption: He II component spatially overlapping with Spitzer/IRAC2 K-band continuum traces the host galaxy systemic redshift  
  > "We therefore look for a spatial overlap between the He ii and 𝐾 −band continuum from Spitzer /IRAC2 to confirm which emission component in He ii traces the host galaxy and should thus represent the systemic redshift." (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian fit (double Gaussian for TN J1338-1942); fixed to [C I] redshift for 4C+03.24 and 4C+19.71  
  > "As a result, a double Gaussian profile, with components that are independent of one another, is fit to the spectrum." (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation); "Here, the [C i] line is used to constrain the systemic redshift for the 4C+03.24 and 4C+19.71." (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation)
   - validation: Consistency check between He II and [C I] redshifts where both are detected  
  > "Where [C i] is detected, the He ii and [C i] redshifts are fully consistent within the margin of error." (4.1 Systemic Redshift Estimation)
2. **Create [C I](1-0) moment-0 narrow-band images**: Integrate continuum-subtracted ALMA cubes over velocity intervals by summing consecutive channels exceeding the zero-flux level using CASA immoments()  
  > "The moment-0 maps (i.e. narrow-band images) were created with casa immoments() in which we images were integrated over frequency ranges shown in Fig. 3." (3.1 ALMA); "The maps are spectrally integrated over velocity intervals which display the spatial extent of [C i] emission by summing the consecutive channels which exceed the zero-flux level." (4.2 [C i] narrow-band imaging)
   - software: CASA immoments()
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check spatial overlap of [C I] contours with Spitzer host galaxy and VLA radio core positions  
  > "The [C i] contours in Fig. 3 are rather close to the AGN host galaxies detected with Spitzer, indicating that our faint [C i] detections are likely to be associated with the host galaxies." (4.2 [C i] narrow-band imaging)
3. **Extract and fit 1D [C I](1-0) spectra**: Extract 1D spectra from primary-beam corrected ALMA cubes at VLA radio core or K-band continuum peak positions and fit single Gaussian profiles; calculate 5-sigma upper limits for non-detections  
  > "In this study, we trace molecular gas via the [C i] lines which we fit using single Gaussian models. Our [C i] line spectra are extracted from primary-beam corrected image cubes in 2 ′′ diameter apertures (∼15 kpc at 𝑧 = 3 . 5)" (4.3 Neutral carbon spectral line fits); "For non-detections, we estimate 5 𝜎 flux density upper limits of 𝑆 [C i] dV = 5 𝜎 rms √ 𝛿 𝑣Δ𝑣 where 𝜎 rms is the root-mean-square (RMS) level in the 1D spectrum estimated over a velocity range of -50 to 50 km s −1 ." (4.4 Neutral carbon line luminosity and molecular gas mass)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Aperture diameter = 2 arcsec (stated as 1 arcsec in Fig. 3 caption and Sec. 5.2)  
  > "For the 1D spectra, pixels were averaged over 2 ′′ diameter apertures." (3.1 ALMA); "All spectra have been extracted from the data cubes with a 1 ′′ aperture centred at the peak of the 𝐾 -band continuum." (Figure 3 caption)
   - parameter: Channel velocity binning = 9 km s^-1 (MRC 0943-242), 11 km s^-1 (TN J0205+2242), 64 km s^-1 (TN J0121+1320), 43 km s^-1 (4C+03.24, 4C+19.71), 48 km s^-1 (TN J1338-1942), 13 km s^-1 (4C+04.11)  
  > "The ALMA [C i] channel binning is 9 and 11 km s −1 for MRC 0943-42 and TN J0205+2242, respectively." (Figure 3 caption); "The ALMA [C i] binning are 64 and 43 km s −1 for TN J0121+1320 and 4C+03.24, respectively." (Figure 3 caption); "The [C i] binnings are 43, 48 and 13 km s −1 for 4C+19.71, TN J1338-1942 and 4C.04+11, respectively." (Figure 3 caption)
   - assumption: Assumed line width Delta_v = 100 km s^-1 for 5-sigma non-detection upper limits  
  > "This velocity width, Δ𝑣 ≃ 100 km s −1 , is based on the assumption that emission from the cold gas is traced by line dispersions of the order ∼100 km s −1 ." (4.4 Neutral carbon line luminosity and molecular gas mass)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian fit independent of He II fit  
  > "On the extracted 1D spectra, we fit the lines in a similar manner as that for the single-peaked He ii lines (see Section 4.1)." (4.3 Neutral carbon spectral line fits); "The He ii and [C i] line fits are completely independently of one another." (4.3 Neutral carbon spectral line fits)
   - validation: Comparison of [C I] redshift and line width with He II and previous CO detections  
  > "Its observed line width of 565 ± 194 km s −1 is consistent with that of the CO(3-2) detection in this galaxy (De Breuck et al. 2003)." (5.1 Neutral carbon within the host galaxies); "In the case of MRC 0943-242, however, Gullberg et al. (2016b) report a CO(8-7) FWHM of 43±13 km s −1, fully consistent with the 40±14 km s −1 we find in [C i]." (5.1 Neutral carbon within the host galaxies)
4. **Derive [C I] line luminosities, H2 masses, star-formation efficiencies, and gas fractions**: Compute L'_[CI], L_[CI], and M_H2,[CI] using equations (1)-(3); combine with literature SFR and M_* to calculate SFE = SFR / M_gas and f_gas = M_gas / (M_gas + M_*)  
  > "From the [C i] spectral line fitting, we obtain [C i] line flux density measures to infer line luminosities and [C i]-derived H 2 masses for the radio-AGN host galaxies." (4.4 Neutral carbon line luminosity and molecular gas mass); "Using the lower 𝐻 2 masses when concentrating on the AGN host galaxies only from our ALMA [C i] data, we now compare HzRGs with other high redshift galaxies in terms of SFE and gas fraction 𝑓 gas = 𝑀 gas /( 𝑀 gas + 𝑀 ★ )." (5.3 Star-formation efficiency)
   - parameter: Einstein coefficient A_10 = 7.93 x 10^-8 s^-1, excitation factor Q_10 = 0.48, carbon abundance X_[CI] = 3.0 x 10^-5  
  > "In equation 3, the luminosity distance, 𝐷 L , is in Mpc, the Einstein A-coefficient is A 10 = 7 . 93×10 −8 s −1 . We use a [C i](1-0) excitation factor of 𝑄 10 = 0 . 48 and a carbon abundance of 𝑋 [C i] = 3 . 0 × 10 −5 (Weiß et al. 2005)." (4.4 Neutral carbon line luminosity and molecular gas mass)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 67.8 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 and Omega_m = 0.308  
  > "Throughout, we have made use of ΛCDM cosmology as defined by Planck Collaboration et al. (2016) where H 0 = 67 . 8 km s −1 Mpc −1 and Ω m = 0 . 308 ." (1 INTRODUCTION)

## Qualitative findings

- Faint [C I](1-0) emission (2-3 sigma) is detected in 4 out of 7 high-z radio galaxies, while 3 out of 7 are undetected.  
  > "We detect faint [C i] emission in four out of seven sources." (abstract); "At least three HzRGs in our observations are not detected in [C i], while the remaining four have 2 − 3 𝜎 level line detections." (5.1 Neutral carbon within the host galaxies)
- Two detected sources (MRC 0943-242 and 4C+03.24) exhibit very narrow [C I] line widths (40-50 km s^-1) that likely trace individual molecular gas clouds rather than full host galaxy rotation, whereas TN J0121+1320 and 4C+19.71 show broader widths (179-565 km s^-1).  
  > "In two of these galaxies, we discover narrow line emission of full width at half maximum ≲ 100 km s −1 which may trace emission from bright kpc-scale gas clouds within the ISM." (abstract); "In MRC 0943-242 and 4C+03.24, the dispersions range from 40-50 km s −1 and are insufficiently broad to cover the rotational velocity of the host galaxy and can therefore only trace single molecular clouds within the galaxy." (5.1 Neutral carbon within the host galaxies)
- HzRGs in this sample have lower molecular gas masses and lower gas fractions (f_gas < 0.2) and higher star-formation efficiencies than typical high-z star-forming and submillimeter galaxies, caused either by gas depletion from earlier starbursts or removal via jet-driven AGN feedback.  
  > "Our results imply that the observed faintness in carbon emission is representative of a decline in molecular gas supply from previous star-formation epochs and/or a displacement of molecular gas from the ISM due to jet-powered outflows." (abstract); "Generally, we find that high- 𝑧 radio galaxies have 𝑓 gas < 0 . 2 and relatively high SFE’s of 4 − 45 Gyr −1 ." (6 CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Bands 3 and 4 trace [C I](1-0) at z = 2.9 - 4.5, which is resilient to cosmic-ray photodissociation in jetted AGN and matches the redshift window where VLT/MUSE detects rest-UV lines (Ly-alpha, He II).  
  > "Selecting [C i] as a tracer may be especially wise given that radio galaxies have jetted AGN that produce high-energy cosmic rays which are capable of photodissociating CO in molecular gas." (1 INTRODUCTION); "the Atacama Large Millimeter-submillimeter Array (ALMA) has frequency bands 3 and 4 which are best suited for probing emission from this atomic transition at a redshift range of 𝑧 = 2 . 9 − 4 . 5 where rest-UV lines are detectable within the observational window of the Multi-Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE)." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Natural uv-weighting (robust = 2) without uv-tapering was chosen to maximise S/N, and fixed apertures at known radio core / Spitzer host positions were used to include the central host galaxy and ensure stable Gaussian fit convergence.  
  > "For the imaging, we used the data reduction pipeline (casa-6.2.1.7 and a natural uv -weighting ( robust parameter 2) to optimise the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) at the expense of spatial resolution." (3.1 ALMA); "We select this aperture size to ensure that (i) the full central part of the host galaxy is included, and (ii) the extracted 1D spectra have sufficient S/N to provide a stable convergence of the fitting algorithm." (4.3 Neutral carbon spectral line fits)
- Alternative: Gaussian uv-tapering of the 12m ALMA data (with 2, 3, and 4 arcsec widths) was attempted to recover extended CGM emission, but rejected because increased noise reduced the overall S/N for all sources except MRC 0943-242.  
  > "we also made an attempt at tapering the 12m uv -data with 2 ′′, 3 ′′, and 4 ′′ Gaussian width options. For all sources, with the exception of MRC 0943-292, an increase in noise due to the reduced amount of data decreased the S/N data overall. Hence, we opted to continue forward with the untapered images" (3.1 ALMA)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N (2-3 sigma) of the [C I](1-0) detections leads to large redshift and line width uncertainties and prevents detailed kinematic modelling or distinguishing between starburst depletion and AGN feedback.  
  > "Our current data is too shallow to allow for a proper conclusion on which mechanism is primarily responsible for the low molecular gas fractions we have observed in our sample of radio galaxies." (6 CONCLUSIONS)
- Limitation (authors): Neglecting the neutral H I gas component when computing M_gas may underestimate total gas mass by up to a factor of two.  
  > "Due to the lack of systematic measurements, we neglect the H i component, which may lead to an under-estimate of 𝑀 gas by a factor up to two." (5.3 Star-formation efficiency)
- Limitation (authors): Assumed conversion factors (alpha_CO, X_[CI], Q_10) have large uncertainties for individual galaxies.  
  > "As expected, the [C i] and CO gas estimates do not lead to equivalent results due to the assumptions on 𝛼 CO and X [C i] and Q 10 , which are more appropriate for populations of galaxies, but have large uncertainties for individual galaxies." (5.2 Comparing CO and [C i]-derived molecular gas in high- 𝑧 radio galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar masses from Spitzer photometry are often conservative upper limits due to possible hot dust torus contamination at rest-frame 1.6 um.  
  > "We note that the stellar masses reported by Seymour et al. (2007) and De Breuck et al. (2010) are often upper limits due to potential contributions from the hot dust torus emission to the rest-frame 1.6 𝜇 m photometry." (3.3 Ancillary data)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All four [C I](1-0) detections are marginal (2-3 sigma), meaning line fluxes, FWHMs, and derived H2 masses are highly sensitive to channel binning and baseline/continuum subtraction choices.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is an internal inconsistency in the text regarding the [C I] spectral extraction aperture: Sections 3.1 and 4.3 specify a 2 arcsec diameter aperture, whereas the Figure 3 caption and Section 5.2 state a 1 arcsec aperture.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Simultaneously observed 13CO(4-3) spectral windows are mentioned in Sections 2 and 3.1, but no results or upper limits are reported for 13CO(4-3).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the abstract and Conclusions, the upper limit on M_H2 for non-detections is summarized as < 0.65 x 10^10 M_sun, whereas Table 3 and Section 4.4 list upper limits up to < 1.08 x 10^10 M_sun (0.65 x 10^10 M_sun is actually the detection value for 4C+03.24).
