## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate and image the eDisk data**: Manual pipeline run for semi-pass EBs; per-EB imaging and shift to a common phase center from imfit; visibility-amplitude scale factors between EBs; two-pass phase and amplitude self-calibration on short baseline then combined data; imaging with tclean  
  > "We then imaged each execution block (EB) individually and shifted the brightest source in the image to common phase center which was obtained from the *imfit* task in CASA." (Observation and Data Reduction); "Namely, we first completed the self-calibration process and determined the scale factors after self-calibration." (Observation and Data Reduction); "We then reran the entire procedure from the beginning, applying these scale factors a priori." (Observation and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, ALMA calibration pipeline, tclean, imfit
   - parameter: robust = 0.0 for continuum, robust = 2.0 for lines; robust = 2.0 plus 1000 klambda uv taper for extended dust emission  
  > "we present the image of dust emission produced with a robust parameter of 0.0, while all the lines have been produced with a robust parameter of 2.0." (Observation and Data Reduction); "we also present the dust emission image produced using a robust parameter of 2.0 and an $uv$ taper of 1000$k \lambda$" (Observation and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Azimuthally averaged visibility amplitudes of each dataset plotted together to check scale factors between EBs  
  > "we computed the azimuthally averaged visibility amplitude of each dataset and plotted them together to determine the average scale factors between each EB." (Observation and Data Reduction)
2. **Fit the continuum disk geometry and flux**: 2D Gaussian fit with imfit to the robust = 0.0 image; inclination from cos^-1 of minor/major axis ratio  
  > "We fit the continuum image produced with the *robust=0.0* with a model of 2D Gaussian using *imfit* task in CASA." (4.2 Dust emission); "the inclination of the disk is $\sim47^\circ$ from $\cos^{-1}(94/138)$ under the assumption of a vertically thin circular disk geometry" (4.2 Dust emission)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: Fit on robust = 0.0 image without uv tapering  
  > "We note that the fit was performed with the image produced using *robust=0.0* without any $uv$ tapering." (4.2 Dust emission)
   - assumption: Vertically thin circular disk geometry  
  > "under the assumption of a vertically thin circular disk geometry" (4.2 Dust emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Total flux summed above 3 sigma in the robust 2.0, 1000 klambda tapered image is 73.5 mJy; compared with 59 +/- 2 mJy from IRAM PdBI  
  > "the total flux density increases to 73.5$\pm$0.15 mJy" (4.2 Dust emission); "than the value of $59\pm2$ mJy by IRAM PdBI observations at 1.3 mm (cite: Santiago+etal_2009)" (4.2 Dust emission)
3. **Derive the dust (disk) mass**: Optically thin, isothermal dust mass equation M_d = S_nu d^2 / (kappa_nu B_nu(T_d)) with S_nu = 70.8 mJy, d = 156 pc, nu = 225 GHz; total mass with gas-to-dust ratio of 100  
  > "Assuming that the dust emission is optically thin and isothermal, we can estimate the disk dust mass using the following equation:" (4.1 Disk mass); "where $\nu$ is the observed frequency (225 GHz), $S_\nu$ is the flux density of dust emission (70.8 mJy)" (4.1 Disk mass)
   - parameter: kappa(1.3 mm) = 2.3 cm^2 per g; T_d = 20 K (Taurus median) or ~34 K from T_d = 43 (L_bol/Lsun)^0.25 with L_bol = 0.41 Lsun  
  > "We adopt the dust opacity of $\kappa_{1.3\,mm}$=2.3 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Beckwith+etal_1990)" (4.1 Disk mass); "we obtain the dust temperature of $\sim$34 K in this disk" (4.1 Disk mass)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio of 100; optically thin emission (otherwise mass is a lower limit)  
  > "Assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100, we obtained the disk mass of 0.015 M$_{\odot}$." (4.1 Disk mass); "If it is optically thick, the derived mass would be a lower limit." (4.1 Disk mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Santiago-Garcia et al. (0.02 Msun, kappa = 1.0 cm^2/g; reproduced as 0.028 Msun) and a 0.027 Msun SED model value  
  > "Adopting their value of opacity, we obtained 0.028 M$_\odot$ for the disk mass, in good agreement with the value quoted by (cite: Santiago+etal_2009)." (4.1 Disk mass); "(0.027 M$_\odot$) from RADMC-3D modeling and MCMC fit using a wide range SED set data including CARMA mm data (cite: Sheehan+Eisner_2017)" (4.1 Disk mass)
4. **Search the continuum disk for rings/gaps via radial profile**: Deproject the image, azimuthally average in 0.01" annuli, fit two Gaussian functions to the radial profile, then fit Gaussian functions to the residual rings  
  > "the radial intensity profile of the 1.3 mm dust emission azimuthally averaged in annuli of 0.01$''$ ($\sim$ 1/4 of the beam) in the plane of the disk" (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk); "A single Gaussian function did not adequately fit the data. Therefore we performed a two Gaussian function fit to the radial profile." (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk)
   - parameter: Deprojection with PA = 121.5 deg and inclination = 47 deg from the 2D Gaussian fit  
  > "The deprojection was performed using a position angle of 121.5$^\circ$ and an inclination of 47$^\circ$, as derived from the 2D Gaussian fit" (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk)
   - assumption: Flat, thin disk geometry  
  > "assuming a flat-thin disk geometry" (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk)
   - fitting: Two-Gaussian fit to the radial profile, then Gaussian fits to the residual; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "To characterize the rings, we fit the residual with Gaussian functions." (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk)
   - validation: Ring significance judged by amplitude in sigma; authors say features are near sensitivity and resolution and cannot be confirmed  
  > "because these features are close to our sensitivity level and resolution, we are unable to confirm them unambiguously." (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk)
5. **Fit edge and ridge of 13CO and C18O PV diagrams**: PV diagrams along the disk major axis (PA 122 deg) within one beam width; edge and ridge extracted with pvanalysis of SLAM and fitted with a power law V_rot = v_b (r/r_b)^-p  
  > "along the major axis of the dust disk (P.A.=122$^\circ$ east of north), within the width of one beam" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1)); "The ridge is determined from the peak derived by the 1D Gaussian fitting to the emission along the positional (or velocity) axis, while the edge is determined as the outermost contour of the emission above a given threshold." (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
   - software: SLAM
   - parameter: Ridge threshold 6 sigma for 13CO and 3 sigma for C18O; edge threshold not stated  
  > "The ridge was derived using a threshold of $6\sigma$ for the case of $^{13}$CO (2–1)." (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1)); "we derived the ridge using a threshold of $3\sigma$" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
   - fitting: Power-law fit of (r, v) points; fit algorithm, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "The derived positions and velocities $(r,v)$ can then be fitted with a power-law function:" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
   - validation: Power-law indices compared with the Keplerian value of 0.5 within uncertainties  
  > "The power-law fit indices agree with each other within their mutual uncertainties for the rotating gas motions to be closely consistent with Keplerian rotation" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
6. **Derive protostellar mass limits from Keplerian motion**: Keplerian rotation v = sqrt(G M / r) at inclination 47 deg applied to edge and ridge results; lowest and highest values adopted as lower and upper mass limits  
  > "Thus the stellar mass can be estimated under the assumption that the gases are in the Keplerian motion ($v=\sqrt{GM_\star/r}$) in the disk with inclination of $i=47^\circ$ around the central star." (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1)); "we have simply adopted the lowest and highest of derived values as the lower and upper limits of our stellar mass estimate" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
   - software: SLAM
   - parameter: Inclination i = 47 deg  
  > "in the disk with inclination of $i=47^\circ$ around the central star" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
   - assumption: Gas in the small region is in Keplerian motion; ridge fitting underestimates and edge fitting overestimates the mass  
  > "it is well known that the ridge fitting systematically underestimates, while the edge fitting overestimates the stellar mass (cite: Maret+Maury_2020; Aso+etal_2015)" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Keplerian trajectories for 0.15 and 0.39 Msun overlaid on H2CO and CH3OH PV diagrams; H2CO consistent with Keplerian motion  
  > "we overlaid the Keplerian trajectories of gas motions by a central source of 0.15 M$_\odot$ and 0.39 M$_\odot$ in the PV diagrams for these tracers" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
7. **Map jets/outflows and date the ejection events**: Moment 0/1 maps of 12CO and SiO in velocity bins, PV diagrams along the jet axis (PA 32 deg), and knot-distance/velocity timescales  
  > "we present the moment 0 maps of the $^{12}$CO (2–1) (top) and SiO (5–4) (bottom) emissions integrated in the three velocity ranges of" (4.4 Jets and Outflows); "along the jets/outflows axis (P.A.=32$^\circ$)" (4.4 Jets and Outflows)
   - parameter: SiO moments computed in -70 to 70 km/s with 3 sigma threshold  
  > "The moments were computed in a velocity range of –70 to 70 km s$^{-1}$ with a threshold of 3 $\sigma$." (3.2 Molecular line emission)
   - parameter: Average jet velocity 40 km/s within 11 arcsec radius; inclination 47 deg for the ejection period  
  > "with an average velocity of 40 km s$^{-1}$ within a radius of $11''$" (4.4 Jets and Outflows)
   - assumption: Distance 156 pc and i = 47 deg used to convert projected distance and velocity to the ejection period  
  > "where $i=47^\circ$" (4.4 Jets and Outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spatial overlap of SiO and 12CO high-velocity jets; comparison with previous observations of knots  
  > "indicating that the SiO (5–4) and $^{12}$CO (2–1) jets spatially overlap, and thus they might arise from the same mass ejection events" (4.4 Jets and Outflows)
8. **Measure continuum brightness asymmetry**: Integrated flux ratio between northeastern and southwestern hemispheres; intensity profiles along major and minor axes; compared qualitatively with RADMC-3D synthetic images of Takakuwa et al.  
  > "We measured an integrated flux ratio of 1.17 between the northeastern (NE) and southwestern (SW) hemispheres" (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability); "This is broadly consistent with the asymmetry seen in the synthetic continuum image from (cite: Takakuwa+eDisk_2024)." (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Assess disk gravitational stability with Toomre Q**: Q = c_s Omega / (pi G Sigma) = 2 (M_star / M_disk)(H/R) with R = 22 au, M_star = 0.15-0.39 Msun, M_disk = 0.015 and 0.008 Msun  
  > "The stability of a disk against gravitational collapse can be examined by using Toomre’s criterion of (cite: Toomre_1994)" (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability); "and the sound speeds $c_s$ for the dust temperature of 20 K and 34 K" (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability)
   - assumption: Disk is geometrically thin; instability for Q < 1-1.5  
  > "A geometrically thin disk would be gravitationally unstable if its Toomre number of $Q<1-1.5$ depending on the type of disturbances in disk (cite: Durisen+etal_2007)." (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note significant uncertainties from optical depth, dust opacity and dust temperature assumptions  
  > "there are still significant uncertainties due to the assumptions about optical depth, dust opacity, and dust temperature" (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability)

## Qualitative findings

- Two faint arm-like features extend to ~1.0" in the tapered continuum image  
  > "there are also two faint arm-like features extending out to $\sim 1.0''$" (4.2 Dust emission)
- A new pair of knots (R0, B0) is resolved at ~0.5"-2" from the protostar  
  > "particularly resolve another pair of knots at the distance of $\sim0.5''-2''$ from the protostar" (4.4 Jets and Outflows)
- SiO and 12CO high-velocity jets show a saw-tooth slow-head, fast-tail pattern  
  > "both $^{12}$CO (2–1) and SiO (5–4) show a saw-tooth feature exhibiting episodic “slow head” and “fast tail” properties" (4.4 Jets and Outflows)
- SiO jet shows a stretched S-shape morphology suggestive of wiggling or precession  
  > "suggesting wiggling or precession motions in the jet" (3.2 Molecular line emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The eDisk program aims to search for primordial signatures of planet formation in disks around young protostars  
  > "The main objective of the eDisk program is to search for possible primordial signature of planet formation, the substructures in the disks surrounding these young protostars." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Robust 0.0 continuum image chosen as best compromise between resolution and sensitivity; two-pass self-calibration needed because EB amplitude profiles differed  
  > "which is found to provide the best compromise between the angular resolution and sensitivity" (Observation and Data Reduction); "At this stage, we realized that the amplitude profiles of these EBs exhibited different behaviors." (Observation and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Binary companion explanation for the wiggling SiO jet judged implausible because no binary component was resolved at 8 au  
  > "we could not resolve any binary component in the IRAS 04166+2706 system, thus the former hypothesis may not be plausible to explain the wiggling jets observed in the SiO (5–4) emission" (5.2 Wiggling motion in the jets and knotty jets)
- Alternative: Central dip in 13CO and C18O intensity attributed possibly to continuum absorption; velocity pattern interpreted as rotation plus infall in a flattened disk/envelope rather than only outflow  
  > "suggesting that velocity pattern can be also interpreted as a mixture of rotation and infalling motion in the flattened disk/envelope" (3.2 Molecular line emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Possible ring and gap features near sensitivity and resolution limit  
  > "because these features are close to our sensitivity level and resolution, we are unable to confirm them unambiguously." (4.2 Identifying a possible substructure in the continuum disk)
- Limitation (authors): Poor velocity resolution of CH3OH and H2CO (1.34 km/s) prevents PV power-law fitting  
  > "the poor velocity resolution of 1.34 km s$^{-1}$ of CH$_3$OH (4$_2$–3$_1$) and H$_2$CO (3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$) prevents us from performing a similar analysis" (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
- Limitation (authors): Uncertain which of edge or ridge technique is more reliable for the stellar mass  
  > "It is not clear which of the two techniques is more reliable." (4.3 An analysis of Position-Velocity diagrams of 13CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1))
- Limitation (authors): Disk mass and Toomre Q depend on optical depth, dust opacity and dust temperature assumptions  
  > "there are still significant uncertainties due to the assumptions about optical depth, dust opacity, and dust temperature" (5.1 Brightness asymmetry, dust mass in the disk, and disk stability)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass range 0.15-0.39 Msun spans a factor of 2.6 and is set by method bias (ridge vs edge), not statistical error
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass assumes optically thin emission while the asymmetry is attributed to high optical depth, an internal tension
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination of 47 deg comes from the Gaussian axis ratio under a thin-disk assumption yet is used for all kinematic and jet calculations
- Caveat (inferred, ours): C18O edge p uncertainty differs between text (0.20) and Table 3 (0.22)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 13CO and C18O PV diagrams show emission in all four quadrants (infall/outflow contamination) yet Keplerian fit uses restricted velocity ranges
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ring/gap significance rests on a residual after a two-Gaussian fit that is near the sensitivity limit, with width a fraction of the beam
