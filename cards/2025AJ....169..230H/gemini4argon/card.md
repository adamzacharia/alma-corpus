## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify SW knots in 12CO channel maps and apply un-sharp masking**: Apply an un-sharp masking code to the 12CO image cube and sum three channels corresponding to the velocities of the respective knots to compare with HST images.  
  > "The $^{12}$CO images have four times lower resolution, thus for comparison, we have applied an un-sharp masking code to the $^{12}$CO image cube and show three summed channels corresponding to the velocities of the respective knots marked in each image." (The ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: 3 summed channels per image; 0.04''/pixel scale  
  > "we have applied an un-sharp masking code to the $^{12}$CO image cube and show three summed channels corresponding to the velocities of the respective knots marked in each image." (The ALMA Observations); "The scale of the $^{12}$CO frames is 0.04$''$/pixel where a pixel here is one unit in the x,y coordinate frame in each channel." (Line Profiles for the knots)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract 12CO line profiles and measure LSR radial velocities of the SW knots**: Extract single-pixel spectra (in Jy/beam) at the x,y pixel coordinates of each knot in the 12CO cube, measure the LSR velocity from the peak in each line profile and uncertainty from the FWHM, and compare with HST/STIS K I velocities corrected to the LSR frame.  
  > "The LSR velocities of the $^{12}$CO emission at the corresponding knots or condensations are from the peak in their respective line profiles shown in the Appendix. The quoted uncertianties are from the FWHM of th profiles." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star); "In each relevant frequency channel, we simply measured the individual pixel value (Jy/beam) at each stated location." (Line Profiles for the knots)
   - parameter: Single-pixel extraction (0.04'' pixel size vs 0.25'' FWHM beam)  
  > "In these data, a pixel is 0.04 arcsec, compared to the 0.25 arcsec FWHM of the Gaussian beam produced by the inteferometry reduction procedure." (Line Profiles for the knots)
   - assumption: Solar motion correction from Heliocentric to LSR at VY CMa's position is -17.8 km s^-1 (following Schonrich, Binney & Dehnen 2010).  
  > "To compare with the $^{12}$CO velocities, we correct the Heliocentric velocities for the motion of the Sun relative to the LSR (cite: Binney). At the position of VY CMa this is -17.8 km s$^{-1}$" (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of independent Doppler velocities from HST/STIS K I emission and ALMA 12CO emission.  
  > "The Doppler velocities from the two independent sources, the K I emission and the $^{12}$CO molecular emission for knots S-D and SW-A, agree within the uncertainties" (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
3. **Derive knot ages, 3D velocities, orientations, and deprojected distances**: Estimate ages directly from HST proper motions (arcsec/yr) and angular distance from the star in 1999; combine proper motions with 12CO LSR velocities relative to the star to compute transverse velocity, total 3D velocity, projection angle relative to the plane of the sky, and deprojected distance.  
  > "Their ages are estimated directly from their proper motions in arcsec per year and the distance from the star in arcsec" (Proper Motions and Ages); "Table 4 includes the transverse velocity in the plane of the sky from the proper motions, and the line of sight velocity relative to the star (Vel$_{rel*}$) using the $^{12}$CO Doppler velocities." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
   - parameter: Reference epoch 1999 for ages and distances  
  > "Since the reference frame for the motions is 1999, the ages are relative to that year." (Proper Motions and Ages)
   - assumption: Distance to VY CMa = 1.15 kpc and stellar LSR velocity = 22 +- 1 km s^-1.  
  > "To derive these values, we adopt the distance of 1.15 kpc and an LSR velocity of 22$\pm$1 km s$^{-1}$ for VY CMa, as discussed in (cite: RMH2024)." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Projection angles near the plane of the sky are consistent with high polarization found from HST and near-IR imaging polarimetry.  
  > "Although the errors are large, especially for the S-E positions, this orientation is consistent with the high polarization found for the SW knots (cite: Shenoy15) that requires an optimum scattering angle of 90 degrees, near the plane of the sky." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
4. **Compare observed 2021.96 12CO knot positions with expected positions from proper motions**: Extrapolate 1999.2 HST angular distances over the 22.8 yr baseline using measured proper motions and compare with the observed 2021.96 12CO angular distances from the star.  
  > "To compare the images of the knots at the two epochs, we show the measured distances of the knots from the star in Table 5, and compare the observed position in 22.8 yrs in the $^{12}$CO image with the knot’s expected position based on its proper motion." (Comparison with the 12CO Images and a Possible Position Offset)
   - parameter: Time baseline = 22.8 yrs (1999.2 to 2021.96); CO position uncertainty <= 0.04 arcsec  
  > "The uncertainty in the CO positions is $\leq$ 0.04 arcsec or approximnately 1 pixel." (Table 5)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested method on knot W2, where the expected distance in 22.8 yrs (0.57'' +- 0.02) agrees with the observed 12CO distance (0.58'' +- 0.02).  
  > "As a test we compared the expected position of the W2 knot (cite: RMH2019) with its observed position in the corresponding $^{12}$CO images. Its projected distance from the star using its proper motion agrees with its distance from the star in $^{12}$CO" (Comparison with the 12CO Images and a Possible Position Offset)
5. **Estimate dust and total mass of the S-arc and SW knots from 249 GHz continuum flux**: Measure the 249 GHz continuum flux density in the S-arc contour and calculate the dust mass and total mass following the method of O'Gorman et al. (2015) and Humphreys et al. (2024), then combine with the NIR/MIR mass lower limit for knots SW-A and S-D.  
  > "The flux density of the emission feature in the 249-GHz continuum associated with the S-arc, is 1.146 mJy in 0.35 arcsec$^{2}$, i.e. the blue contour in the middle panel of Figure 6. Following the method in (cite: OGorman) and adopted in (cite: RMH2024), we can estimate the dust mass corresponding to this region." ## Qualitative findings

- The SW Clump is not a single ejection or expanding bubble, but consists of three discrete knots plus an extended arc that are spatially and kinematically distinct.  
  > "Initially, in the near-infrared, they looked like a separate feature or single ejection from VY CMa (Figure 1). But the HST and ALMA images reveal three separate knots plus an extended arc that are spatially and kinematically distinct." (Summary)
- The SW knots were ejected more than 200 years ago, with knot SW-A and the S-E arc consistent with ejection over an active period lasting about 30 years.  
  > "The SW knots were ejected more than 200 years ago with an active period lasting about 30 years, and with a total mass in the Clump $>$ 2 $\times$ 10$^{-2}$ M$_{\odot}$." (Abstract); "The SW knots were ejected more than 200 years ago, so there is no record in VY CMa’s historic light curve for comparison. Knot SW-A and the S-E arc are consistent with ejection over a 30 year time span, but probably in separate outflows." (Summary)
- Observed 12CO knot positions in 2021.96 show a small but systematic outward offset (0.11 to 0.27 arcsec) compared to positions expected from HST proper motions, which is likely due to excitation differences (optical scattered light illuminating the inner star-facing side vs collisional/IR CO excitation).  
  > "The difference is more likely due to the difference in the excitation mechanisms. The visual knots are due to scattered light while the CO emission is attributed to collisional and infrared excitation." (Comparison with the 12CO Images and a Possible Position Offset)
- The 249 GHz mm continuum peak is displaced southwest of the NIR/MIR peak (near the S-E feature and S-arc at ~2'' from the star), revealing an additional significant, cooler (T <= 150 K) dust mass shielded from the star's direct radiation.  
  > "The peak in the mm continuum emission just to the southwest of the NIR and MIR emission is indicative of the presence of significant dust mass that is likely blocked from a clear view of the star, reducing the scattered light." (The Dust Properties and Mass Estimates); "This explanation suggests that there is an additional, significant dust mass associated with the SW knots a bit further to the southwest that is mostly not seen at near and mid-infrared wavelengths." (The Dust Properties and Mass Estimates)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Observations at wavelengths longer than 1 um and in ALMA 12CO and mm continuum penetrate deeper into the dusty circumstellar wind of VY CMa to reveal 3D kinematics and cooler dust features hidden by extinction at shorter wavelengths; 12CO has the most complete coverage of the SW knots among the observed transitions.  
  > "In the following discussion we use the $^{12}$CO observations which have more complete coverage of the SW knots." (The ALMA Observations); "By observing at wavelengths longer than $1~\ensuremath{\mu}m$, we can penetrate deeper into the mass-loss wind of VY CMa, and see features that are hidden by dust extinction at shorter wavelengths." (The Dust Properties and Mass Estimates)
- Why this method: Un-sharp masking was applied to the 12CO cube because the ALMA images have four times lower spatial resolution than HST; single-pixel spectrum extraction was used because in an interferometric map each pixel represents the entire spatial beam and summing pixels would not improve S/N and would increase contamination from nearby features.  
  > "The $^{12}$CO images have four times lower resolution, thus for comparison, we have applied an un-sharp masking code to the $^{12}$CO image cube" (The ALMA Observations); "Instead each pixel represents the entire spatial beam centered at that location, and noise is spatially distributed. Hence the S/N would not be improved by taking a sum of multiple pixels centered at that location." (Line Profiles for the knots)
- Alternative: Using the HST/STIS K I emission line velocity directly on the star as the systemic velocity of VY CMa was rejected in favor of the radio LSR velocity (22 km s^-1) because 12CO observations reveal an optically thick foreground outflow remnant from the late 19th century that may affect optical line profiles.  
  > "In our 2021 paper (cite: RMH2021) we used a velocity for the star based on the velocity of the K I emission lines in an HST/STIS spectrum directly on the star." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star); "We do not know how the optical emission and absorption lines from VY CMa would be affected by passage through such dense and dusty material. For that reason we adopt the velocity of VY CMa from the radio observtions." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
- Alternative: Physical acceleration of the ejecta by gas pressure or radiation pressure on dust/dust annealing was considered and rejected as the primary cause of the 22.8 yr positional offset between HST and ALMA 12CO knots because both mechanisms are insufficient at distances > 1000 AU.  
  > "This may seem small, but at distances more then 1000 AU, accleration by gas pressure, given the slow wind from VY CMa, would not be sufficient." (Comparison with the 12CO Images and a Possible Position Offset); "Acceleration due to radiation pressure on dust and dust annealing has been suggested (e.g. (cite: Chap), (cite: RichardsY)) but this effect is unlikely to be sufficient at these distances." (Comparison with the 12CO Images and a Possible Position Offset)
- Alternative: Summing multiple pixels around each knot to extract 12CO spectra was rejected because interferometric noise is spatially correlated across the beam and a larger aperture would increase contamination from unrelated nearby features.  
  > "Hence the S/N would not be improved by taking a sum of multiple pixels centered at that location. Moreover, in principle a larger sampling radius would slightly increase the amount of contamination by unrelated nearby spatial features." (Line Profiles for the knots)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The origin of the significant velocity discrepancy between K I optical emission and 12CO emission for the S-E feature (+17.5 to +17.9 km s^-1) and especially knot SW-B (+33 km s^-1) is not understood.  
  > "The Doppler velocities from the two independent sources, the K I emission and the $^{12}$CO molecular emission for knots S-D and SW-A, agree within the uncertainties, although the K I emission is redshifted relative to the S-E feature and much moreso for the SW-B knot. The origin for this significant difference isn’t understood." (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties on the projection angles (orientation) are large, especially for the S-E positions.  
  > "Three of the SW knots have projection angles near the plane of the sky. Although the errors are large, especially for the S-E positions, this orientation is consistent with the high polarization found for the SW knots" (The Line of Sight Velocities and Motion Relative to the Star)
- Limitation (authors): The Heliocentric-to-LSR velocity conversion has a ~2 km s^-1 convention discrepancy because standard ALMA convention uses an older solar motion of 20 km s^-1 whereas -17.8 km s^-1 from Schonrich et al. (2010) was used here.  
  > "The standard convention for ALMA observations uses an older value for the Solar motion of 20 km s$^{-1}$. Consequently, the velocity discrepancy could be uncertain by $\approx$ 2 km s$^{-1}$." (Footnote 2)
- Limitation (authors): Knots S-D and SW-A have a marginal projected separation of 0.25'' (comparable to the 0.25'' FWHM beam), so they are not fully spatially resolved from each other.  
  > "S-D and SW-A have peak fluxes at very different frequencies although their projected separaton at 0.25$''$ is marginal." (Line Profiles for the knots)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All ALMA calibration and imaging parameters are omitted and deferred to companion papers (Singh et al. 2023; Humphreys et al. 2024).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line velocities and uncertainties are read from peak and FWHM of multi-component single-pixel profiles rather than deblended Gaussian fits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The dust mass estimate for the S-arc relies on an assumed dust temperature of 150 K without multi-wavelength mm SED fitting to constrain temperature and dust opacity index independently.
