## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure 1.3 mm continuum fluxes and derive inner envelope masses (M_Env)**: Fit 1.3 mm ACA continuum emission above the 3sigma contour with one or more 2D Gaussians using CASA imfit task and compute lower limit of inner envelope mass from integrated flux using Planck blackbody equation.  
  > "To determine the properties of the continuum sources, we fit the emission above the 3 σ contour using CASA imfit task with one or more 2-dimension Gaussians for one or more, respectively visually identified peaks." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum); "we assume the emission is optically thin to infer the lower limit of inner envelope mass (M Env ) following the equation:" (3.1 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - software: CASA (imfit)
   - parameter: 3sigma contour threshold for fitting  
  > "To determine the properties of the continuum sources, we fit the emission above the 3 σ contour using CASA imfit task" (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - assumption: Optically thin 1.3 mm dust continuum emission  
  > "For simplicity, however, we assume the emission is optically thin to infer the lower limit of inner envelope mass (M Env ) following the equation:" (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - assumption: Dust mass opacity kappa_nu ~ 0.00899(nu/231 GHz)^beta cm^2 g^-1, gas-to-dust mass ratio = 100, dust opacity spectral index beta ~ 1.5  
  > "The assumed dust mass opacity is κ ν ∼ 0.00899( ν /231 GHz) β cm 2 g − 1 , assuming a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100 (e.g., Lee et al. 2018). We also assume a dust opacity spectral index β ∼ 1.5." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - assumption: Dust temperature T_dust ~ 15 K, distances D = 389 +/- 3 pc (Orion A), 404 +/- 5 pc (Orion B), 404 +/- 4 pc (lambda-Ori)  
  > "Following Dutta et al. (2020), we adopt slightly different distances D for the sources in Orion A ( ∼ 389 ± 3 pc), Orion B (404 ± 5 pc), and λ -Ori (404 ± 4 pc)." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum); "We assume an optimal T dust ∼ 15 K for all the inner envelopes (e.g., Dutta et al. 2022b)." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting via CASA imfit  
  > "To determine the properties of the continuum sources, we fit the emission above the 3 σ contour using CASA imfit task with one or more 2-dimension Gaussians for one or more, respectively visually identified peaks." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
   - validation: Assessed sensitivity to T_dust variations (+15 K decreases mass by 60%, -5 K increases mass by 85%)  
  > "For an increase of +15 K in temperature, the derived mass will decrease by 60%, and a decrease of − 5 K will increase the derived mass by 85%." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
2. **Determine systemic velocity (V_sys) of each protostar**: Extract C18O (2-1) spectra at the continuum peak in high-resolution maps and take the velocity of peak emission, or average the 1st and 2nd brightest velocity channels when peak spans more than one channel.  
  > "The systemic velocities ( V sys ) of the sources were estimated from the C 18 O emission observed at the continuum peak in high-resolution maps, where C 18 O is most likely tracing the disk/envelope emission around the protostars." (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O); "Therefore, we chose the velocity of the peak emission as the V sys (Figure 3) or we average the velocities of the 1st and 2nd brightest velocity-channels from the spectra in cases where the peak emission spanned over more than one channel" (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O)
   - parameter: Velocity resolution ~ 2.0 km s^-1 per channel  
  > "In most cases, C 18 O spread only over 2-3 channels (one channel corresponding to velocity resolution ∼ 2.0 km s − 1 ), making it challenging to get a reasonable Gaussian fit." (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O)
   - assumption: C18O emission at the continuum peak traces the disk/envelope systemic velocity  
  > "The systemic velocities ( V sys ) of the sources were estimated from the C 18 O emission observed at the continuum peak in high-resolution maps, where C 18 O is most likely tracing the disk/envelope emission around the protostars." (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked by eye against the spectra  
  > "All these determined V sys values were checked by eye against the spectra." (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O)
3. **Fit outflow shell morphology and derive outflow inclination angle (i)**: Fit a parabola z = c R^2 to the outermost shell of the CO (2-1) moment zero map above 3sigma to obtain curvature c, then fit a parabola in the position-velocity (PV) diagram along the jet axis using Equation A3 to determine inclination angle i.  
  > "We assume that the molecular CO outflow shell is a radially expanding parabolic shell of the underlying wide-angle wind, and then accordingly fit a parabolic shape to the moment zero map of CO emission using Equation A1 to obtain the curvature ( c ) at the launching point." (3.3. Physical Structure of outflow); "Using these c values, we fitted parabolas to the “outflow” emission in PV space as shown in Figure 4b with Equation A3 to create a complete 3D paraboloid structure, where the fitting equations include the inclination of the structure in the plane-of-sky" (3.3. Physical Structure of outflow)
   - parameter: Low-velocity CO emission above 3sigma  
  > "we estimated inclination angles i a using the low-velocity CO emission above 3 σ" (3.3 Physical Structure of outflow)
   - assumption: Molecular CO outflow shell is a radially expanding parabolic shell with expansion velocity proportional to radial distance (v_R = R/t_0)  
  > "The molecular CO outflow shell is assumed to be a radially expanding parabolic shell generated by the underlying wide-angle wind." (A. INCLINATION ANGLE FROM CO OUTFLOW SHELL); "For a radially expanding shell with a velocity proportional to the radial distance v R = R/t 0 , where t 0 is the dynamical time of expansion." (A. INCLINATION ANGLE FROM CO OUTFLOW SHELL)
   - fitting: Analytical parabolic fitting to moment zero map (z = c R^2) and PV diagram (Equation A3) by varying i and t_0  
  > "The curvature c is determined from fitting to the integrated emission (zeroth moment) maps (see equation A1). By varying i and t 0 we can obtain a parabolic fit of the PV diagram." (A. INCLINATION ANGLE FROM CO OUTFLOW SHELL)
   - validation: Visual inspection of individual velocity channels and comparison of CO PV diagram with SiO PV diagram to distinguish outflow shell from ambient material and high-velocity jet  
  > "The likely ambient material near the systemic velocity is distinguished after visually inspecting individual velocity channels in the data cube." (3.3. Physical Structure of outflow); "If the objects exhibit high-velocity jets blended with the low-velocity outflow emission, we distinguish the outflow shell by comparing the CO PV diagram with that of SiO emission (Figure 5), assuming SiO traces mostly jet components." (3.3. Physical Structure of outflow)
4. **Calculate CO outflow mass, momentum, and outflow force (F_CO)**: Convert beam-averaged CO emission above 3sigma in each velocity channel (excluding channels near V_sys) into channel mass M_k, sum momentum P = sum(M_k V_k), and divide by dynamical time T_dyn,out = R_CO / V_CO,max with inclination correction f_ia.  
  > "Outflow force F CO was calculated from the beam-averaged CO emission across the whole velocity range above 3 σ on each lobe (blue and red) separately. To avoid contamination from ambient material, we removed a few channels near the systemic velocity after inspecting channel maps." (3.4. Outflow Force); "T dyn,out is the length of the jet divided by the velocity of the outflow. Outflow force F CO was calculated from the beam-averaged CO emission across the whole velocity range above 3 σ on each lobe (blue and red) separately." (3.4 Outflow Force)
   - parameter: 3sigma threshold; channels near systemic velocity removed  
  > "Outflow force F CO was calculated from the beam-averaged CO emission across the whole velocity range above 3 σ on each lobe (blue and red) separately. To avoid contamination from ambient material, we removed a few channels near the systemic velocity after inspecting channel maps." (3.4. Outflow Force)
   - assumption: CO-to-H2 abundance ratio X_CO ~ 10^-4 and excitation temperature T_ex ~ 50 K in the outflow; conservative error of 40%  
  > "We assumed a CO-to-H 2 abundance ratio of X CO ∼ 10 − 4 in the outflow for a excitation temperature of T ex ∼ 50 K." (3.4. Outflow Force); "Since the above information could not be recovered simultaneously from the present observations, we assume a conservative error of 40% in all plots in the subsequent sections." (3.4. Outflow Force)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared F_CO values from combined maps (FOV ~ 40'') with those derived from ACA-only maps (FOV ~ 60''), finding changes only by factors of 1 to 1.5  
  > "We also compared the present F CO values with those derived from ACA maps (FOV ∼ 60 ′′ ), and we see only changes by factors of 1 to 1.5 from the smaller primary beam values." (3.4. Outflow Force)
5. **Identify molecular jet knots, measure jet velocity (V_j, V_j,cor), and compute episodic ejection timescales (T_knot) and Keplerian perturbation radii**: Search CO and SiO data cubes and PV diagrams along the flow axis to identify discrete knots; take mean velocity of brightest peak knot emission as V_j, deproject via V_j,cor = V_j / sin(i), compute mean timescale between consecutive knots as T_knot = (delta_R / V_j) tan(i), and convert T_knot to Keplerian orbital radius via Kepler's third law.  
  > "The mean of the velocity of the brightest peak knot emission is considered to be the observed jet-velocity V j , and these are listed in Table 3." (3.5. Molecular jet detection and episodes of accretion); "The mean timescales between knots are measured as" (3.5 Molecular jet detection and episodes of accretion); "The corresponding orbital radii for those periods can be estimated from Kepler’s third law of orbital motion following equation:" (4.4 Episodic ejection)
   - assumption: Central protostellar mass M_* = 0.05 - 0.30 M_sun (or 0.1 M_sun in Figure 13)  
  > "where T = T knot is the mean episodes of ejection and M ∗ is the mass of the central protostars, which is assumed to be 0.05 − 0.30 M ⊙ ." (4.4.3. Episodic ejection: dependence on luminosity and envelope mass); "A stellar mass M ∗ = 0.1 M ⊙ is assumed for all protostars." (Figure 13 caption)
   - fitting: Linear least-squares fits to correlations of log(V_j,cor) and log(T_knot) with log(M_Env)  
  > "A linear fit is consistent with the equation: log( V j , cor ) = (0 . 21 ± 0 . 14) log( M Env ) + (2 . 0 ± 0 . 06) . (7)" (4.4.1. ## Qualitative findings

- Out of 42 outflows detected in CO emission, SiO emission was detected in 21 fields, with 19 sources driving confirmed molecular jets (16 in this sample/study context plus previously published ALMASOP jets) and 6 sources exhibiting monopolar or nearly-monopolar SiO jets.  
  > "We detected SiO emission in 21 fields, where 19 sources are driving confirmed molecular jets with high abundances of SiO." (abstract); "Six of the sources exhibit monopolar or nearly-monopolar SiO jets (G203.2111.20W2, G191.90-11.20S, G205.46-14.56M1, G205.4614.56S1, G209.55-19.68S2, G211.47-19.27S B )." (3.5. Molecular jet detection and episodes of accretion)
- SiO emission within knots is optically thicker than CO (tau_SiO > tau_CO), meaning CO traces deeper interior layers of the knots as well as low-density outflow shells.  
  > "We estimated the optical depth for SiO ( τ SiO ) and CO ( τ CO ) emission from the peak emission within the knots, assuming a temperature T ex ∼ 150 K, and find that the SiO emission is optically thicker than that of CO ( τ SiO > τ CO ) within the knots." (4.1. Which emission traces Which component?)
- Redshifted monopolar SiO jets can be explained by a layered internal bow-shock structure where a dense, cool compressed layer shields the middle SiO+CO layer in the blueshifted lobe from the observer.  
  > "For the blue-shifted lobe, the scenario is opposite, where an observer can see only a forward CO emission front (layer i ). Emission from layers- iii and iv might be shielded by the compressed and coolest layer- ii ." (Figure 8 caption)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMASOP provides a statistically significant, unbiased sample of young, cold protostars in the Orion molecular cloud at a similar distance (~400 pc) and environment, observed at high resolution and sensitivity in 1.3 mm continuum, CO, C18O, and SiO.  
  > "In this paper, we describe ALMA observations of a statically significant sample of protostars in the Orion molecular cloud. Their location in common puts them at a similar distance ( ∼ 400 pc) and in a similar environment, minimizing the effects of environmental differences on the observed parameters." (1. INTRODUCTION); "Here, we combined data from three configurations (TM1, TM2, and ACA) to improve the sensitivity of the line cubes." (2.2. Observations)
- Why this method: ACA low-resolution continuum probes large-scale envelope mass, C18O traces systemic velocity of the inner envelope/disk, parabolic fitting to CO shells constrains inclination angle, and high-critical-density SiO (5-4) isolates dense jet knots from the CO outflow shell.  
  > "In brief, we generated 1.3 mm continuum images using low-resolution ACA observations of typical beam size 7 ′′ . 6 × 4 ′′ . 1 to probe large-scale envelope emission." (2.2. Observations); "Given its higher critical density and an expected increase in SiO from dust grains shattered in shocks, the SiO emission is more suitable for detecting the high-density material and shocks in the jets, where as the CO traces both high-density jet as well as low-density outflow structures." (3.5. Molecular jet detection and episodes of accretion)
- Alternative: Using ACA-only maps (FOV ~ 60'') instead of combined TM1+TM2+ACA maps (FOV ~ 40'') for F_CO measurements was rejected due to poorer sensitivity of ACA-only maps.  
  > "In addition, we could not recover the whole outflow emission from the ACA maps due to their relatively poor sensitivity compared with the combined high-resolution maps. Therefore, we present only the F CO measurements derived from the high-sensitivity combined maps." (3.4. Outflow Force)
- Alternative: Gaussian fitting to C18O spectra to determine V_sys was rejected because the emission spans only 2-3 velocity channels.  
  > "In most cases, C 18 O spread only over 2-3 channels (one channel corresponding to velocity resolution ∼ 2.0 km s − 1 ), making it challenging to get a reasonable Gaussian fit." (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O)
- Alternative: Binary orbital dynamics driving the accretion/ejection episodes was rejected for the wide binary systems because their separations (> 100 au) do not match the derived Keplerian perturbation radii (2 - 25 au).  
  > "the Keplerian perturbation radius. Hence, it is unlikely these respective accretion/ejection events are driven by binary orbital dynamics." (4.4 Episodic ejection)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 1.3 mm dust continuum emission may be optically thick in the central core region of extremely young protostars, so derived envelope masses represent lower limits.  
  > "For an extremely young protostar, the 1.3 mm emission could be optically thick, especially in the central core region. Hence, this emission may represent a lower limit to the total dust column density from the inner envelope around the protostars." (3.1. 1.3 mm Continuum)
- Limitation (authors): The 2 km s^-1 velocity resolution is insufficient to probe the kinematics and geometry of resolved disks or to obtain Gaussian fits to C18O lines.  
  > "In most cases, C 18 O spread only over 2-3 channels (one channel corresponding to velocity resolution ∼ 2.0 km s − 1 ), making it challenging to get a reasonable Gaussian fit." (3.2. Systemic Velocity from C 18 O); "In a few cases, we may have resolved the larger disks but the velocity resolution of our observations is not sufficient to probe their kinematics and geometry." (4.1. Which emission traces Which component?)
- Limitation (authors): The parabolic outflow shell model is only suitable for objects with well-defined outflow shells that are distinguishable from ambient material or multiple outflows.  
  > "Here, we note that this model is suitable only for those objects that have well-defined outflow shells, i.e., outflow walls that are well distinguished from the ambient material or outflows of multiple sources." (3.3. Physical Structure of outflow)
- Limitation (authors): The TM1/TM2 primary beam (~40'') does not cover the full spatial extent of some outflows, and interferometric observations suffer from missing flux, optical depth, and temperature uncertainties.  
  > "Here we note that we do not cover the full outflow extents within the TM1/TM2 primary beam ( ∼ 40 ′′ )." (3.4. Outflow Force); "Errors of F CO estimation depend on the amount of missing flux in interferometric observations, the lack of coverage of the full length of the outflow lobe, calibration, the optical depth of CO, the temperature variation of the outflow, and errors in the inclination angle measurements." (3.4. Outflow Force)
- Limitation (authors): Jet widths are not spatially resolved even at ~0.38'' x 0.35'' resolution, requiring the transverse beam size to be adopted as the jet width.  
  > "Since the jet widths are not spatially resolved even at the high spatial resolution achieved, the beam size b m is taken to be the jet width." (3.6. Jet Mass-Loss Rate and Kinetic Luminosity)
- Limitation (authors): SiO (5-4) and CO (2-1) can be optically thick in dense knots, making SiO-to-CO abundance ratios and jet mass-loss rates lower limits.  
  > "Therefore, we consider our measured values to be lower limits to the mass loss rates." (3.6. Jet Mass-Loss Rate and Kinetic Luminosity); "We note that SiO (5-4) could be optically thicker towards the dense shock than CO (2-1) in the same region. Therefore the SiO emission is likely the lower limit and the X SiO /X CO is the lower limit in this case." (4.3. Origin of gas-phase SiO)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The linear correlations reported for log(V_j,cor), log(Mdot_j), and log(T_knot) versus log(M_Env) have large uncertainties on their slopes (0.21 +/- 0.14, 0.47 +/- 0.27, and -0.14 +/- 0.19), making them statistically weak (< 2 sigma).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination angles derived from fitting parabolas to CO shells carry large uncertainties, especially for nearly edge-on sources (i <= 10 deg), which propagate nonlinearly into V_j,cor (= V_j / sin i) and T_knot (= delta_R tan i / V_j).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a single fixed dust temperature (15 K), fixed stellar mass (0.1 M_sun), and fixed stellar radius (2 R_sun) across all Class 0 and Class I protostars may introduce systematic biases in M_Env and Mdot_acc.
