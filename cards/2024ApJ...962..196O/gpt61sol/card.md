## Analysis recipe

1. **Build MIR feature maps**: Fit each IRS spaxel with dust continuum, PAH and emission-line models; generate feature flux maps  
  > "First, we fit each individual spaxel using a combination of dust continuum, PAH feature, and emission line models" (Spitzer Spectral Maps); "Next, we use the best fit spectral parameters for each spaxel to generate feature flux maps." (Spitzer Spectral Maps)
   - software: CUBISM, lmfit
   - parameter: Central 55.5'' × 55.5'' covered by both gratings  
  > "We utilize only the central $55.5\ensuremath{''} \times 55.5\ensuremath{''}$ regions of the maps that were covered by both gratings." (Spitzer Spectral Maps)
   - assumption: Instrumental PSF not considered in spectral-cube fitting  
  > "We do not consider the instrumental PSF when fitting the spectral cube." (Spitzer Spectral Maps)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares fitting with lmfit  
  > "utilizing the python package lmfit for Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares fitting (cite: 2023zndo...8145703N)." (Spitzer Spectral Maps)
   - validation: Link associated-feature parameters and combine spaxels to mitigate sampling and sub-PSF variations  
  > "linking the fit parameters of associated spectral features (e.g., all PAH features or all H$_2$ lines) helps to mitigate spatial and spectral sampling issues at the initial spaxel-fitting stage" (Spitzer Spectral Maps); "combining multiple spaxels in the subsequent spectral extractions and model fitting largely averages over any sub-PSF variations." (Spitzer Spectral Maps)
2. **Define spectral zones and measure integrated features**: Select zones with PAH 7.7/11.3 and H2 S(3)/PAH 11.3 maps; sum and model each zone spectrum  
  > "Next, we use the PAH 7.7/11.3 and $H_2$ S(3) / PAH 11.3 $\mu$m ratios measured from these maps to identify zones with distinct MIR spectra" (Discussion); "We model the summed spectra of each zone, allowing all PAH feature fluxes to vary independently" (Discussion)
   - parameter: SF: PAH 7.7/11.3 >0.9; inner disk: <=0.9  
  > "Spaxels in the SF Zone were selected to have PAH 7.7/11.3 $>0.9$, and those in inner disk Zones 1-3 PAH 7.7/11.3 $<=0.9$." (Discussion)
   - parameter: Zones 1, 2, 3: H2 S(3)/PAH 11.3 <0.25, 0.25–0.7, >0.7  
  > "Zones 1-3 were further selected to have $H_2$ S(3) / PAH 11.3 in the ranges $<0.25$, 0.25–0.7, and $>0.7$, respectively." (Discussion)
   - parameter: NUC: 5×5 spaxel box, 934 pc × 934 pc, centered on AGN  
  > "Instead, we sum the spectrum of the spatially unresolved nucleus (NUC) inside a $5\times5$ spaxel (934 pc $\times$ 934 pc) box centered on the AGN point source, covering the central peak of the PSF." (Discussion)
   - fitting: All PAH feature fluxes vary independently in summed-zone fits  
  > "We model the summed spectra of each zone, allowing all PAH feature fluxes to vary independently" (Discussion)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit warm molecular gas temperatures and masses**: Fit H2 excitation diagrams with dN/dT~T^-n following Togi and Smith  
  > "We fit the H$_2$ excitation diagram for each spectral zone with a power-law temperature distribution $dN/dT \sim T^{-n}$ (Fig. 5; Table 1), following the method of (cite: 2016ApJ...830...18T)." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - parameter: Power-law index n, minimum detectable temperature Tl, total warm H2 mass  
  > "The power-law model is parameterized by the power-law index $n$, the minimum detectable H$_2$ temperature T$_l$, and the total warm H$_2$ mass." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - assumption: Distance 21 Mpc for sizes and luminosities  
  > "We adopt the SN 1989M (type Ia) distance of 21 Mpc (cite: 1996ApJ...465L..83R) to compute sizes and luminosities." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Continuous temperature distribution for gas with a range of densities  
  > "A continuous temperature distribution is expected for molecular gas with a range of densities, and a power-law distribution provides a good fit to the data." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - fitting: Power-law temperature-distribution fit; sampler, likelihood and priors not stated  
  > "We fit the H$_2$ excitation diagram for each spectral zone with a power-law temperature distribution $dN/dT \sim T^{-n}$" (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - validation: Power-law distribution described as a good fit  
  > "A continuous temperature distribution is expected for molecular gas with a range of densities, and a power-law distribution provides a good fit to the data." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
4. **Measure CO emission and estimate cold molecular mass**: Use PHANGS-ALMA CO 2-1 products; extract zone profiles and estimate cold molecular mass from CO flux  
  > "We present ALMA CO 2-1 line profiles, extracted from the zones of the inner galaxy where we performed our analysis of the *Spitzer* spectral maps" (Highly Turbulent CO Kinematics Driven by Jet Feedback); "Similarly, we estimate a cold molecular mass of $5.2 \times 10^{8} M_\odot$ from the ALMA CO 2-1 flux in the same region" (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - parameter: Comparison aperture r=2.1 kpc  
  > "the total H$_2$ mass within $r=2.1$ kpc, estimated from the CO 1-0 flux" (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas); "from the ALMA CO 2-1 flux in the same region" (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - assumption: αCO=4 M⊙/(K km s^-1 pc^2); R21=0.6  
  > "assuming the standard $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 4 M_\odot$ / (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$) and $R_{21}=0.6$." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with literature CO 1-0 mass  
  > "the total H$_2$ mass within $r=2.1$ kpc, estimated from the CO 1-0 flux is $\sim 50$ times greater, at $5 \times 10^8 M_\odot$ (cite: 2009A&A...496...85G)." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas); "Similarly, we estimate a cold molecular mass of $5.2 \times 10^{8} M_\odot$ from the ALMA CO 2-1 flux in the same region" (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
5. **Fit dust SEDs and compare gas-mass tracers**: Fit zone UV-to-FIR photometry with a CIGALE model grid; compare dust-based gas masses with CO and warm H2  
  > "We fit each SED with a grid of cigale models" (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance); "Assuming a standard dust mass percentage of 1%, this corresponds to a total gas mass of $3\times 10^8 M_\odot$." (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance); "The molecular gas mass estimated above from CO 1-0 and 2-1 is a factor of 1.8 greater than this" (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: Nebular emission turned off  
  > "We turned off the nebular emission, which would otherwise require shock modeling." (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF, solar metallicity, delayed star formation history and dust extinction  
  > "using a Chabrier initial stellar mass function (IMF) with solar metallicity and delayed star formation history for the stellar population, modified by dust extinction." (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
   - assumption: Draine dust emission models; grain-density factor 0.81  
  > "We use the dust emission models of (cite: 2014ApJ...780..172D), with dust grain densities reduced by a factor of 0.81 compared to (cite: 2007ApJ...657..810D)." (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
   - assumption: Dust mass percentage 1%; most inner-disk gas molecular for warm-H2 percentages  
  > "Assuming a standard dust mass percentage of 1%" (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance); "Using the lower gas mass estimated from dust and assuming that most of the gas in the inner disk is molecular yields warm H$_2$ percentages of $1-4\%$ in Zones 1 and 2." (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
   - fitting: Grid of CIGALE models; detailed grid and fit statistic not stated  
  > "We fit each SED with a grid of cigale models" (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
   - validation: Compare CO-to-dust mass ratios and apply an analogous M31 bulge-SED correction to PAH abundance  
  > "Zone 1 and the SF zone have $M$(CO 2-1)/$M_\mathrm{dust}$ values that are consistent with the Galatic value of $\sim 100$" (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance); "Assuming a similar correction applies at the center of M58, the actual $q_\mathrm{PAH}$ appears to be consistent with the Galactic ISM and M31, with no indication of PAH depletion." (Dust Mass and PAH Abundance)
6. **Estimate star formation and assess PAH changes**: Use PAH 7.7 luminosity for SFR and compare PAH ratios across zones  
  > "We estimate the star formation rate using the prescription SFR(PAH 7.7) $= 2.4\times10^{-9} L$(PAH 7.7) (cite: 2001A&A...372..427R)." (PAH Ratios Unaffected by Jet Feedback); "Notably, there is no significant variation in the PAH 7.7/11.3 or other PAH ratios across Zones 1-3" (PAH Ratios Unaffected by Jet Feedback)
   - assumption: Inner-disk PAH excitation most likely from old bulge stars  
  > "The PAH emission from the inner disk of M58 (and elliptical galaxies) is most likely excited by the interstellar radiation field from old stars in the galaxy bulge" (PAH Ratios Unaffected by Jet Feedback)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check FUV surface brightness and HST Hα morphology  
  > "Furthermore, the FUV continuum surface brightness of the inner disk is low (Fig. 3a) and there are no obvious clumps in the HST H$\alpha$ + \[Nii\] image attributable to star formation" (PAH Ratios Unaffected by Jet Feedback)
7. **Measure CO kinematics and turbulent energy**: Compare zone-integrated CO profiles and spatially resolved dispersions; estimate warm-H2 turbulent energy and dissipation time

## Qualitative findings

- Warm H2 follows dust lanes but does not closely match CO clumps  
  > "The $\sim 10 \times$ higher resolution Gemini image clearly shows that the warm H$_2$ follows the dust lanes resolved by *HST*." (Results); "The H$_2$ emission shows similar clumping in the inner disk but lacks a detailed correspondence to the clumps in the CO 2-1 maps." (Results)
- Zone 3 blue CO wing might indicate a molecular outflow  
  > "Of note is the broad blue wing in Zone 3, which can be seen as anomalously blue-shifted emission against the regular rotation of the galaxy disk, which might indicate a molecular outflow component." (Highly Turbulent CO Kinematics Driven by Jet Feedback)
- Radio-jet shocks and turbulence are suggested to power molecular and ionized emission  
  > "We suggest that the molecular and ionized gas emission are excited by shocks and turbulence driven by the radio jet cocoon and radio-jet-driven outflows into the multi-phase ISM." (Summary and Conclusions)
- No indication of PAH destruction by jet-driven shocks or AGN X-rays  
  > "There is no indication of PAH destruction by jet-driven shocks or X-rays from the AGN." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Warm-gas conditions may indicate negative radio-jet feedback on star formation  
  > "The high molecular gas temperatures and turbulence in the inner disk of M58 most certainly are not conducive to star formation, and may be a signature of negative radio-jet feedback on star formation." (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO 2-1 provides molecular-gas spatial structure and kinematics alongside warm-H2 maps  
  > "There is a weaker association of H$_2$ with molecular gas traced by the ALMA CO 2-1 map" (Results); "We present ALMA CO 2-1 line profiles, extracted from the zones of the inner galaxy where we performed our analysis of the *Spitzer* spectral maps" (Highly Turbulent CO Kinematics Driven by Jet Feedback)
- Why this method: Empirical spectral zones reduce aperture-selection bias; power-law H2 temperatures avoid arbitrary two-temperature bins  
  > "We note that this is a less-biased, empirical way of identifying, grouping, and extracting like spaxels than the more common method of extracting spectra in circular or elliptical apertures." (Spitzer Spectral Maps); "This is preferable to two-temperature models, which arbitrarily split the temperature distribution into two bins." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
- Alternative: Two-temperature H2 models rejected as arbitrary temperature bins  
  > "This is preferable to two-temperature models, which arbitrarily split the temperature distribution into two bins." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
- Alternative: Prefer H2 S(3)/PAH 11.3 to H2 S(0-3)/PAH 7.7 as a shock indicator  
  > "We recommend using $H_2$ 0-0 S(3)/ PAH 11.3 instead, which is less sensitive to PAH ionization and only requires the measurement of two adjacent spectral features." (Shock-Heated Molecular Gas)
- Alternative: Optical line ratios also admit LINER photoionization  
  > "At all locations in the inner disk and nucleus of M58, we find \[Nii\]/H$\alpha = 1.5-2.1$, \[O i\]/H$\alpha = 0.16-0.38$ and \[S ii\]/H$\alpha = 0.7-1.6$, characteristic of shocked gas or LINER photoionization." (Jet Shock-Ionized Gas with Disturbed Kinematics)
- Alternative: Morphological quenching and bulge-related gas velocity dispersion are alternative suppression mechanisms  
  > "Star formation may be suppressed in such an environment by radio-jet feedback, morphological quenching (cite: 2009ApJ...707..250M), or the increased velocity dispersion of gas in the gravitational field of the bulge (cite: 2020MNRAS.495..199G)." (PAH Ratios Unaffected by Jet Feedback)
