## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: How does the gas-phase elemental C/O ratio vary azimuthally in the protoplanetary disk around HD 100546, and what explains the spatial and kinematic asymmetries in CS and SO emission?  
  > "Using ALMA observations of CS and SO, we have unearthed evidence for an entirely novel type of C/O variation in the protoplanetary disk around HD 100546: an azimuthal variation from a typical, oxygen-dominated ratio (C/O 0.5) to a carbon-dominated ratio (C/O ≳ 1.0)." (Abstract); "To investigate the origin of the azimuthal asymmetries in various molecular species in the HD 100546 disk, we ran source specific models using the 2D physical-chemical code DALI (Bruderer et al., 2012; Bruderer, 2013)." (3.3 Chemical modelling)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and extract spectra and upper limits from ACA Band 7 observations**: Calibrate ACA visibilities with the ALMA pipeline, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, image with tCLEAN using natural weighting, generate moment 0 maps between -14.5 and 25.5 km s-1, extract spectra using 5.0" elliptical and Keplerian masks with specflux, and apply uv-plane matched filtering using DALI templates for non-detected lines.  
  > "Continuum and line imaging were performed with the tCLEAN algorithm using natural weighting in order to maximize the S/N ratio of the data." (3.1 Data reduction); "The total disk-integrated flux was extracted using the CASA task specflux, and determined to be 0.62 Jy km s–1 for the Keplerian-masked cube, where the mask was cut at ± 4 km s–1 either side of the source velocity in order to remove noisy channels." (3.1 Data reduction); "Finally, we applied a matched filter to the visibility data, to maximize the S/N in the uv -plane (Loomis et al., 2018)." (3.1 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, ALMA Pipeline, tCLEAN, uvcontsub, specflux, Keplerian mask (Teague, 2020), VISIBLE / matched filter (Loomis et al., 2018)
   - parameter: cell size = 0.5", image region = 20"x20", velocity integration range = -14.5 to 25.5 km s-1, elliptical aperture radius = 5.0"  
  > "We used a cell size of 0.5" to ensure that the beam is well sampled." (3.1 Data reduction); "The CS 7-6 integrated intensity map (Figure 2, top right) was generated from a 20"x20" region centred on the source, where the integrated intensity is determined between -14.5 to 25.5 km s–1" (3.1 Data reduction); "We extracted the spectrum from the CLEAN cube using an elliptical aperture with a 5.0" radius centred on the source (approximately the same size as the disk as traced by 12 CO emission (Walsh et al., 2014)), where the edges of the mask were smoothed by the beam." (3.1 Data reduction)
   - assumption: V_LSRK = 5.7 km s-1; M_* = 2.4 M_sun for Keplerian mask  
  > "The mask identifies which pixels in the image cubes have Doppler shifted line velocities that match the Keplerian velocity, based on the velocity profile of a disk rotating around a star of mass M ⋆ = 2.4 M ⊙ ." (3.1 Data reduction); "corresponding to channels expected to contain significant emission ( ∼± 20 km s–1 from the source velocity V LSRK = 5.7 km s–1 )." (3.1 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared elliptical and Keplerian masked spectra; used elliptically-masked spectrum to avoid removing real non-Keplerian/asymmetric emission.  
  > "Due to the peculiar nature of the CS line profile, our analysis utilises only the elliptically-masked spectrum, in order to not mistakenly remove any real emission." (3.1 Data reduction)
2. **Measure and validate the spatial offset of CS 7-6 emission from the host star**: Fit a Gaussian to the CS image and deproject using disk inclination and PA; compare CS peak position against ACA continuum and C18O 3-2 reference positions; calculate beam centroid uncertainty; fit a Gaussian directly to continuum-subtracted visibilities within ±10 km s-1 of V_LSRK using CASA uvmodelfit.  
  > "Fitting a Gaussian, we find the peak of the CS emission to be significantly offset from the host star by ∼ 1"." (1.1 Data & observational findings); "Next, we assess the offset in the visibility plane, using the CASA task uvmodelfit to fit a model directly to the UV-data." (Supplementary Information 1)
   - software: CASA, uvmodelfit
   - parameter: uvmodelfit channels = ±10 km s-1 from source velocity, 20 iterations (converging after ∼8)  
  > "We first split out the spectral windows containing the CS 7-6 emission from continuum-subtracted measurement set, using only the channels centered ± 10 km s–1 from the source velocity." (Supplementary Information 1); "We run 20 iterations, with the model converging after ∼ 8." (Supplementary Information 1)
   - assumption: Continuum and C18O 3-2 emission peaks trace the central star location within the central 0.5" pixel  
  > "It is therefore reasonable to utilise the continuum and C 18 O emission peaks as reference locations for the host star." (Supplementary Information 1)
   - fitting: Gaussian visibility-plane fitting with CASA uvmodelfit  
  > "We then fit a Gaussian to the data, where the initial guess for shape is based on the synthesized beam properties, and the initial guess for the emission peak is the image centre (ie. location of the star)." (Supplementary Information 1)
   - validation: Repeated uvmodelfit with a range of initial peak location guesses (converging within ∼8%); checked archival high-resolution C2H data (2017.1.00845.S) which also shows redshifted enhancement.  
  > "Since uvmodelfit can be sensitive to the initial conditions, we repeat the fitting using a range of initial guesses for the location of the emission peak. In all cases the models converge to close to the same result, deviating by a maximum of ∼ 8 % from the value reported here." (Supplementary Information 1); "The total flux on the red-shifted side of the disk is a factor of ∼ 1.5 greater that that on the shifted side, which may be indicative of an elevated C/O ratio." (Supplementary Information 1)
3. **Construct baseline axisymmetric 2D physical-chemical disk model (C/O = 0.5)**: Run a grid of 1728 DALI models to fit the SED for dust mass, constrain maximum gas mass from HD 56 µm and 112 µm upper limits, constrain global [C/H]gas and [O/H]gas from the CO ladder, [CI], C2H upper limits, and CO 3-2 radial profile, and fit radially varying [S/H] in dust rings using CS and SO fluxes and sulfur upper limits.  
  > "We begin by fitting the SED using a grid of 1728 models, in which the parameters R gap, ψ, h c, δ dust and ∆ g/d are varied." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure); "[C]/[H] gas and [O]/[H] gas are constrained by modelling the CO ladder, the line profiles of the CO 3-2, CO 6-5 and [CI] transitions, and the radial profile of the CO 3-2 emission from (Walsh et al., 2014)." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure); "Finally, we constrain the gas-phase elemental sulfur abundance using the disk-integrated CS 7-6, SO 7 7 -6 6 and SO 7 8 -6 7 fluxes and radial intensity profiles, and upper limits for other sulfur-bearing species listed in Table 2." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: Table 1 fiducial parameters: R_sub=0.25 au, R_gap=1.0 au, R_cav=13 au, R_out=1000 au, R_c=50 au, M_gas=1.45e-1 M_sun, M_dust=1.12e-3 M_sun, [C/H]gas=1.0e-5, [O/H]gas=2.0e-5, t_chem=5 Myr; [S/H]=10^-9 (13-30 au) and 10^-8 (150-230 au)  
  > "For this study, we adopt a radially varying sulfur abundance profile, in which [S/H]= 10–9 between 13-30 au and [S/H]= 10–8 between 150-230 au, coincident with prominent millimeter dust rings." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure); "[C/H] gas Initial carbon abundance (relative to hydrogen) 1.0 × 10–5 (where C/O=0.5)" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Interstellar gas-to-dust ratio ∆_g/d = 100; UMIST 06 chemical network (122 species, 1701 reactions); d = 110 pc; L_* = 36 L_sun  
  > "This is not a tight enough constraint to uniquely determine the gas-to-dust ratio, so from this point on we adopt the interstellar value of ∆ g/d = 100, equivalent to a total gas mass of 1.45 × 10–1 M ⊙ ." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure); "The chemical network used in our model is based on a subset of the UMIST 06 (Woodall et al., 2007) network. It consists of 122 species (including neutral and charged PAHs) and 1701 individual reactions." (3.3.3 Chemical network)
   - fitting: Grid search over 1728 SED models followed by chemical abundance grid fitting against multi-wavelength line fluxes and upper limits  
  > "We begin by fitting the SED using a grid of 1728 models, in which the parameters R gap, ψ, h c, δ dust and ∆ g/d are varied." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure)
   - validation: Verified consistency of best-fit dust mass and C, O abundances with Kama et al. (2016).  
  > "We find a best fit total dust mass of 1.12 × 10–3 M ⊙, consistent with previous studies (Kama et al., 2016)." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure)
4. **Model azimuthal C/O variations by splicing 2D DALI models and simulating ALMA observations**: Use output abundances from baseline C/O=0.5 model (model A) as input for shadowed model (model B) with depleted H2O, atomic O, and CO (placing removed C into neutral atomic C), evolve for 5 years, raytrace both models, splice a wedge (θ, φ) from model B into model A using a custom Python script, and generate synthetic ALMA observations with CASA simobserve and simanalyze.  
  > "Using a custom Python script, for each transition the spectral cube from model B is spliced together with its counterpart from model A, following the geometry outlined in Figure 4." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries); "We process the model cubes using the CASA tasks simobserve and simanalyze in order to create simulated ALMA observations, using parameters that match the observations." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries); "The model presented here uses a CO depletion factor of 0.3, an atomic oxygen depletion factor of 0.3, and an H 2 O depletion factor of 0 (i.e. total removal), resulting in a gas-phase C/O ratio of 1.5." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries)
   - software: DALI, Python, CASA, simobserve, simanalyze
   - parameter: Wedge size θ = 60 deg, wedge orientation φ = 10 deg north of west, wedge C/O = 1.5 (CO depletion factor = 0.3, O depletion factor = 0.3, H2O depletion factor = 0), chemical age = 5 years; grid of ∼570 models with θ = 10-90 deg, φ = 0-180 deg, C/O = 0.3-3.0  
  > "The model presented here uses a CO depletion factor of 0.3, an atomic oxygen depletion factor of 0.3, and an H 2 O depletion factor of 0 (i.e. total removal), resulting in a gas-phase C/O ratio of 1.5." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries); "Using these values, we find that a wedge size of θ = 60o centred ten degrees north of west west ( φ = 10o ) accurately reproduces both the CS and SO emission morphology and line profiles." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries)
   - assumption: Angles measured in cube plane rather than projected disk plane; shadow transit time in outer disk is ∼5 years  
  > "Angles are measured in the plane of the cube i.e. they are not projected on to the disk, and as such do not directly correlate to angles measured in the disk plane." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries); "The model is run to a chemical age of 5 years, based on the approximate shadow transit time (Supplementary Information 5)." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries)
   - fitting: Grid exploration of ∼570 models varying C/O, wedge size θ, wedge position φ, and chemical timescales

## Qualitative findings

- CS 7-6 emission in HD 100546 is offset by ∼1" (∼100 au deprojected) to the west/southwest and has a narrow, single-peaked redshifted velocity profile (+1.5 km s-1), opposite to the SO emission which is brightest in the east and has a prominent blueshifted peak (-7.5 km s-1).  
  > "Fitting a Gaussian, we find the peak of the CS emission to be significantly offset from the host star by ∼ 1"." (1.1 Data & observational findings); "The SO line is broad ( FWZI ∼ 15 km s–1 ) and asymmetric, with a prominent blueshifted Keplerian peak at ∼ –7.5 km s–1 . In contrast, the CS emission is narrower (FWZI ∼ 10 km s–1 ) and sharply peaked in the red ( ∼ +1.5 km s–1 )." (1.1 Data & observational findings)
- The spatial and spectral asymmetries of both CS and SO are self-consistently reproduced by an azimuthal variation in the gas-phase C/O ratio: C/O = 0.5 in the bulk disk and C/O = 1.5 in a 60 deg wedge centered 10 deg north of west.  
  > "The model presented here incorporates a high-C/O wedge extending azimuthally 60 o, centered ten degrees north of west." (1.2 Modelling); "Using these values, we find that a wedge size of θ = 60o centred ten degrees north of west west ( φ = 10o ) accurately reproduces both the CS and SO emission morphology and line profiles." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries)
- Shadowing by an inner dust overdensity associated with a forming protoplanet at 13 au can cool the outer disk, freeze out H2O, and chemically convert O and S reservoirs to yield C/O > 1 within the ∼5 year shadow transit time inside r ≲ 200 au.  
  > "we take 5 years as our upper limit on the time available for changes in the chemistry to occur" (Supplementary Information 5)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The CS/SO molecular ratio varies by up to four orders of magnitude for small changes in C/O around unity, making combined observations of CS and SO in the same disk a sensitive diagnostic of asymmetric C/O variations.  
  > "Another probe is the CS/SO molecular ratio, which varies by up to four orders of magnitude for small variations in C/O (Semenov et al., 2018; Booth et al., 2021), and has led to claims of C/O > 1 in at least six disks (Dutrey et al., 2011; Bosman et al., 2021; Le Gal et al., 2021)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting was used in tCLEAN to maximize S/N; the elliptically-masked spectrum was used over the Keplerian-masked spectrum so as not to accidentally clip real emission from the peculiar CS profile; 2D DALI models were spliced into 3D because DALI assumes azimuthal symmetry.  
  > "Continuum and line imaging were performed with the tCLEAN algorithm using natural weighting in order to maximize the S/N ratio of the data." (3.1 Data reduction); "Due to the peculiar nature of the CS line profile, our analysis utilises only the elliptically-masked spectrum, in order to not mistakenly remove any real emission." (3.1 Data reduction); "DALI is a 2-dimensional code which relies upon azimuthal symmetry to produce 3-dimensional outputs, so in order to simulate azimuthal asymmetries it is necessary to run two separate models and splice together the outputs using the following procedure." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries)
- Alternative: Global disk-averaged oxygen depletion (C/O > 1 across the whole disk): rejected because HD 100546 is warm enough to prevent CO/CO2 freeze-out, water freeze-out is limited by the dust cavity and photodesorption, and global C2H upper limits require disk-averaged C/O < 1.  
  > "However, our modelling of HD 100546 finds no evidence of significant oxygen depletion relative to carbon in this disk, with a best-fit disk-averaged C/O ratio < 1." (2. Discussion)
- Alternative: Vortex dust trapping: rejected as a general explanation because molecular gas asymmetries often show no clear connection to millimeter dust vortices or across transitions.  
  > "The physical mechanisms responsible for gas asymmetries cannot therefore be easily attributed to vortices." (2. Discussion)
- Alternative: Obscuration by a warped/inclined inner dust disk (r < 1 au): disfavored because near-IR observations at ∼1 au scales find no evidence for an inclined inner dust disk.  
  > "However, near-IR observations at small spatial scales ( 1 au) find no evidence supporting an inclined inner dust disk ( r < 1 au) (Garufi et al., 2016; Follette et al., 2017; Lazareff et al., 2017; Bohn et al., 2022), which may indicate that the structure of the gas and dust is significantly different within the inner few au." (2. Discussion)
- Alternative: Circumplanetary disk (CPD) shocked gas alone: a CPD alone cannot account for the asymmetries observed in both SO and CS, and the SO kinematics and spatial distribution are consistent with a Keplerian protoplanetary disk.  
  > "While our model can be modified to account for an additional component of SO emission related to a CPD, a CPD alone is not able to account for the asymmetries observed in both the SO and CS." (2. Discussion)
- Alternative: Single constant sulfur abundance across the entire disk: rejected because it overpredicts SO emission from the outer dust cavity.  
  > "If a single sulfur abundance is used for the entire disk, our model overpredicts SO emission from the outer dust cavity." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The ACA CS 7-6 observation is essentially unresolved because the 4.78" beam is nearly equal to the radial extent of the gas disk.  
  > "The emission is essentially unresolved, since the beam size (4.78") is almost equal to the radial extent of the gas disk, as traced by CO (Walsh et al., 2017)." (1.1 Data & observational findings)
- Limitation (authors): The DALI model lacks a smooth transition between the inner cavity and dust ring (having a sharp boundary instead) and does not include the outer dust gap between r ∼ 40 - 150 au in ∆_g/d.  
  > "One possible explanation is that our model lacks a smooth transition between the cavity and dust ring, instead having a sharp boundary." (1.2 Modelling); "Our model allows for varitions in ∆ g/d within the inner dust cavity, but does not take into account other radial variations such as the observed dust gap in the outer disk between r ∼ 40 – 150 au." (3.3.4 Basic fitting procedure)
- Limitation (authors): Wedge splicing angles are measured in the image cube plane rather than projected onto the inclined 3D disk plane.  
  > "Angles are measured in the plane of the cube i.e. they are not projected on to the disk, and as such do not directly correlate to angles measured in the disk plane." (3.3.5 Modelling azimuthal asymmetries)
- Limitation (authors): Grain-surface conversion of CO to CO2 ice (reactions 14-16) is not included in the DALI chemical network and is approximated by manually depleting initial H2O, O, and CO abundances; reverse timescales and multi-transit cyclical shadowing are not modelled.  
  > "While reactions 14 - 16 are not included in our chemical network, we take them into account by manually adjusting the abundances as follows." (Supplementary Information 5); "A more sophisticated model could take into account the reverse timescale, and the cyclical nature of disk shadowing, by addressing the effects of many transits over the lifetime of the disk." (Supplementary Information 5)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing low-resolution ACA CS data (4.78" beam) with high-resolution 12m SO data (0.18" beam) means the radial and azimuthal distribution of CS is only constrained by a sub-beam centroid shift and velocity profile.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The depletion of gas-phase O and CO in the shadowed wedge is imposed manually as an initial condition rather than solved self-consistently through a coupled 3D radiative transfer and grain-surface chemical network.
