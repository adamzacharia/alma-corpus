## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate ALMA data and obtain calibrated VLA data**: Iterative phase self-calibration with decreasing solution interval, then one amplitude self-cal; calibrated VLA data taken from Disks@EVLA  
  > "We began applying phase self-calibration with an initial solution time interval equal to the total observation time." (Observations); "The calibrated VLA data were taken from the Disks@EVLA Collaboration" (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Iterate while SNR improves by at least 5%; solint decreases by a factor of 3 per iteration  
  > "If SNR improves by at least a factor of 5%, we continue applying successive phase self-calibration iterations with a smaller solution time interval (it decreases by a factor of 3 in consecutive iterations)." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SNR of imaged self-calibrated data used as the criterion for continuing  
  > "The new self-calibrated data were imaged and the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is computed." (Observations)
2. **Image continuum with multi-scale CLEAN**: CASA tclean, multi-scale CLEAN, mask on disk emission  
  > "For imaging, we employed the TCLEAN task with a multi-scale CLEAN algorithm" (Observations); "Additionally, we applied a mask covering the disk emission." (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Scales: point source, 1, 2, 3 times beam FWHM; robust 0.5 (ALMA) / 2.0 (VLA)  
  > "with scales corresponding to point sources, 1, 2, and 3 times the beam Full Width at Half Maximum (FWHM)" (Observations); "Ka | 34 | 80 - 11.1 | 115.1 | 2.0 | 401 $\times$ 293; 61 | 7.7 | AC982" (Observations table)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract visibilities, de-project, and fit disk center and geometry**: De-project visibilities by rotating baselines by the PA and stretching along the minor axis by cos(i); fit inclination/PA by minimizing visibility scatter in annuli; fit center by minimizing imaginary part or a 2D Gaussian on a tapered image  
  > "The de-projection consists of rotating the baseline coordinates using the disk’s position angle, and then stretching the baseline coordinates along the minor axis of the disk using the cosine of the disk inclination." (3 Visibility modeling); "In most of the cases, the offset is computed by minimizing the imaginary part of the visibilities (cite: Isella_2019)." (3.3 Phase shift and Geometry)
   - software: Frankenstein, uvplot, CASA
   - parameter: Geometry fixed for all wavelengths of a disk, taken from Band 6, 3, 6, 7, 6, 6 for CQTau, UXTau A, LkCa15, RXJ1615, SR24S, DMTau  
  > "Thus, our final decision on the disk geometry is based on Band 6, 3, 6, 7, 6, 6, for CQTau, UXTau A, LkCa15, RXJ1615, SR24S and DMTau, respectively." (3.3 Phase shift and Geometry)
   - assumption: Azimuthal emission symmetry  
  > "We assume azimuthal emission symmetry, which is a first order approximation of the disk morphologies in this work" (3 Visibility modeling)
   - fitting: Error bars from the 16th and 84th percentiles  
  > "Error bars are computed from the percentiles 16th and 84th." (3.3 Phase shift and Geometry)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit non-parametric radial brightness profiles to visibilities**: Frankenstein fit of the real part of the de-projected visibilities (Fourier-Bessel series) in logarithmic brightness space; hyper-parameters alpha and w_smooth chosen by visual inspection from a grid  
  > "where the disk brightness (represented as a Fourier-Bessel series) is reconstructed by fitting the real part of the visibilities as a function of the uv-distance (baselines)" (3.1 Non-parametric fit); "and we choose by visual inspection the pair of hyper-parameters that minimize the oscillatory artifacts." (3.1 Non-parametric fit)
   - software: Frankenstein
   - parameter: Grid of alpha = 1.05-1.30 and log10(w_smooth) = -4 to -1; chosen pairs per dataset in the visibility table  
  > "we fit all the observations using a grid of hyper-parameters varying between $\alpha = [1.05-1.30]$, and $\log_{10}(w_{\rm smooth}) = [-4, -1]$" (3.1 Non-parametric fit)
   - parameter: Fit in logarithmic brightness space; low-SNR VLA data fitted on visibilities binned at 10 klambda  
  > "We fit the data in the logarithmic brightness space, which helps to reduce the oscillatory artifacts in the reconstructed brightness profile" (3.1 Non-parametric fit)
   - assumption: Imaginary part of the visibilities not used because of the azimuthal symmetry assumption  
  > "is not used by FRANKENSTEIN given the azimuthal symmetry assumption" (3.1 Non-parametric fit)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Axisymmetric models judged a good representation of the visibilities; residual maps and image-plane profiles (GoFish) compared; no important difference when fitting in linear brightness space  
  > "In all cases, the axi-symmetric visibility model (from Frankenstein or parametric) is a good representation of the observed visibilities." (3.4 Results from the visibility modeling); "However, we found no important differences when fitting the data in a linear brightness space." (3.1 Non-parametric fit)
5. **Fit parametric models to VLA visibilities**: Sum of Gaussian rings at fixed radii plus a central point source (free-free), visibilities via Hankel transform, fitted to the non-binned VLA visibilities  
  > "we also directly fit the non-binned VLA visibilities using a parametric model with a given intensity profile" (3.2 Parametric fit); "The free-free emission is modeled by including a compact emission point source at the disk center" (3.2 Parametric fit)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 64 walkers and 10000 steps; ring radii fixed from ALMA profiles (RXJ1615 outer ring radius free)  
  > "and using 64 walkers and 10000 steps" (9.2 Parametric Models at VLA wavelengths); "The number of Gaussian functions for each disk is chosen based on the radial profile at ALMA wavelengths." (9.2 Parametric Models at VLA wavelengths)
   - assumption: Emission from spinning nanometer-size dust does not contribute  
  > "However, we assume that the emission from spinning nanometer-size dust (cite: Hoang_2018) does not contribute to the observed emission" (3.2 Parametric fit)
   - fitting: MCMC maximizing the log-likelihood, a weighted chi-square sum over the real and imaginary parts of the visibilities; priors not stated  
  > "The best fit parametric model is found by maximizing the logarithm of the likelihood function" (3.2 Parametric fit)
   - validation: Free-free fluxes compared with earlier centimeter estimates; fixed vs free ring radii tested  
  > "We also conducted tests allowing $r_i$ to be a free parameter, and the resulting posterior probability distributions aligned with those when the radial positions were fixed." (9.2 Parametric Models at VLA wavelengths)
6. **Build common-resolution radial profiles with uncertainties**: Image-plane profiles from GoFish for comparison; visibility-fit profiles convolved to the lowest resolution of each disk; azimuthal-asymmetry errors from the image plane added in quadrature to the visibility-fit errors; intrinsic resolution of each fit from delta-ring tests  
  > "the radial profiles for a specific disk are convolved to the same angular resolution, corresponding to the lowest angular resolution among the multi-wavelength observations of each disk." (3.4 Results from the visibility modeling); "We add the azimuthal asymmetries error bars computed in the image plane to the radial profile uncertainty computed from the visibility fit" (3.4 Results from the visibility modeling)
   - software: GoFish, Frankenstein
   - parameter: Band 9 data excluded from the multi-wavelength analysis, and low-resolution datasets excluded  
  > "We opted to exclude observations with low angular resolution (compared with the rest of the multi-wavelength data)" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - assumption: Filling factor of 1 everywhere for image-plane profiles  
  > "We assume a filling factor (cite: Thompson_2017) of 1 everywhere." (3.4 Results from the visibility modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Intrinsic resolution tested with a delta ring and a Gaussian disk sampled at the observed uv-coverage, with and without thermal noise  
  > "This FWHM represents the minimum spatial scale recoverable by the visibility fit, i.e., the intrinsic angular resolution of the visibility fit." (8.1 Delta Ring)
7. **Fit the multi-wavelength SED at each radius**: Fit intensity of a scattering-corrected emergent-intensity model at each radius for maximum grain size, dust surface density and dust temperature  
  > "The SED is fitted at each radius using the emergent intensity of the dust continuum emission at frequency" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 24 walkers and 10000 steps; log-spaced ranges 300 um <= a_max <= 30 cm, 1e-6 <= Sigma_d <= 10 g cm^-2, 3 <= T_d <= 300 K  
  > "using 24 walkers and 10000 steps to explore the space parameter of" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties); "The explored parameter space is log-spaced between 300 $\mu$m $\leq a_{\rm max} \leq 30$ cm, $10^{-6}$ g cm$^{-2}$ $\leq \Sigma_{\rm d}$ $\leq 10$ g cm$^{-2}$, and $3$ K $\leq T_{\rm d}$ $\leq 300$ K." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - parameter: Absolute flux uncertainty 5% (Bands 3, 4), 10% (Bands 6, 7), 20% (Bands 8, 9) included in the intensity errors  
  > "5% in Band 3 and 4, 10% in Band 6 and 7, and 20% in Bands 8 and 9." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - assumption: Particle size distribution n(a) ~ a^-p with p fixed to 3.0 and minimum grain size 0.05 um  
  > "The slope of the particle size distribution $p$ is fixed to 3.0." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties); "where the minimum grain size is fixed to 0.05 $\mu m$" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - assumption: Opacities and albedo from Birnstiel et al. 2018; spherical compact grains  
  > "are taken from (cite: Birnstiel_2018), and the dust grains are assumed to follow a particle size distribution" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties); "(e.g., particle size distribution slope, spherical and compact dust grains)" (5.1 Model limitations)
   - assumption: Temperature prior centered on a passively irradiated disk with width 50 K  
  > "The latter is computed from the temperature of an passively irradiated disk" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties); "to ensure that this prior does not dominate the posterior probability distribution." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - fitting: MCMC with chi-squared likelihood; log-uniform priors on a_max and Sigma_d, Gaussian prior on T_d; errors from the 32nd and 68th percentiles  
  > "are the maximum grain size and dust surface density priors, given by a log-uniform distribution within the explored parameter space" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties); "The error bars are computed from the percentile 32 and 68 respectively." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
   - validation: Tested p values in 2.5-3.5 and small maximum grain sizes; conclusions not sensitive  
  > "and the qualitative conclusions are not sensitive to this particular choice." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties); "but found that the walkers tend to the large maximum grain size regime after sufficient steps." (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
8. **Derive dust masses**: Integrate the fitted dust surface density profiles  
  > "are estimated by integrating the dust surface density profiles in Figure" (6 Does the origin of a large cavity depend on the disk mass?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Test radial smearing on the inferred dust properties**: Build a DMTau-like model profile, compute convolved intensities at the observed bands, refit the SED and compare to the input convolved profiles  
  > "We study the effects of radial smearing by creating a disk model with properties similar to those of DMTau, where large grain sizes are inferred within the disk cavity." (10 Radial smearing effects)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Model convolved at the DMTau resolution of 120 mas  
  > "using the resolution of the DMTau observations (120 mas)" (10 Radial smearing effects)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Max grain size around the ring peak is smeared into the cavity, so large grains inside cavities cannot be distinguished from smearing  
  > "we cannot rule out the possibility of small grain sizes in this region, which are hidden by radial smearing effects." (10 Radial smearing effects)
10. **Compare dust property profiles with cavity-origin signatures**: Qualitative comparison of Sigma_d, a_max and T_d profiles (peaks at ring, decreasing a_max with radius) with signatures of dust traps vs grain growth plus pressure bump, combined with literature gas cavities  
  > "Different observational signatures are expected depending on whether the large cavities result from a companion, dead zone, photo-evaporation, or enhanced grain growth." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- UXTau A, LkCa15 and RXJ1615 show surface density and maximum grain size peaks at the main ring, consistent with a dust trap that could be created by a companion  
  > "The observational signatures suggest that the cavities observed in the disks around UXTau A, LkCa15, and RXJ1615 could potentially originate from a dust trap created by a companion." (abstract)
- In CQTau, SR24S and DMTau the cavity origin is unclear; maximum grain size decreases with radius, compatible with a pressure bump plus grain growth  
  > "Conversely, in the disks around CQTau, SR24S, DMTau, the origin of the cavity remains unclear, although it is compatible with a pressure bump and grain growth within the cavity." (abstract)
- Visibility fits reveal sharper ring-gap contrast than CLEAN-image radial profiles  
  > "The radial profiles from the visibility fit reveal higher contrasts between rings and gaps compared to the azimuthal radial profiles computed from the CLEAN images" (3.4 Results from the visibility modeling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-wavelength data from optically thick to optically thin emission constrain dust properties at each radius  
  > "The dust continuum observations spans from optically thick emission (observed in ALMA Band 9 or Band 7) to optically thin emission (observed in VLA Band Q or Band Ka) in all disks" (Observations)
- Why this method: Fit de-projected visibilities to extract the highest angular resolution from the data  
  > "We fit the de-projected continuum visibilities of the dust continuum emission to extract the highest angular resolution from the observational data." (3 Visibility modeling)
- Alternative: Fitting in linear instead of logarithmic brightness space gave no important differences  
  > "However, we found no important differences when fitting the data in a linear brightness space." (3.1 Non-parametric fit)
- Alternative: Small maximum grain sizes (below 300 um) tested but walkers move to the large-grain regime, so the lower bound was set at 300 um  
  > "The smallest value for the maximum grain size is chosen to avoid degeneracy between a small-grain solution" (4 Constraints on Dust Properties)
- Alternative: Emission from spinning nanometer-size dust assumed not to contribute  
  > "However, we assume that the emission from spinning nanometer-size dust (cite: Hoang_2018) does not contribute to the observed emission" (3.2 Parametric fit)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Smearing within the cavities cannot be ruled out given the angular resolution  
  > "Given the angular resolution of the data, it is not possible to rule out the possibility of smearing effects within the cavities" (5.1 Model limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Grain sizes are biased by the spectral index analysis and differ from polarization-based sizes  
  > "Our results are biased by the spectral index analysis." (5.1 Model limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Long-wavelength sample biased toward massive disks  
  > "It is important to remark that the long wavelength data in this work (from DisksEVLA project, ID: AC982) are biased towards massive disks." (6 Does the origin of a large cavity depend on the disk mass?)
- Limitation (authors): Axisymmetry assumed; 2D analysis beyond scope  
  > "Additionally, we assume that the disks are axi-symmetric and compute their azimuthally averaged properties." (5.1 Model limitations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Maximum grain size inside cavities is largely set by the lowest-resolution dataset and the paper's own smearing test shows it can be artificial
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust opacity model, p = 3 and compact spherical grains fix the absolute grain sizes and masses; only qualitative tests of p given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ring-by-ring classification of cavity origin is qualitative with no quantitative model comparison
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Hyper-parameters of Frankenstein chosen by visual inspection, which introduces subjectivity in ring contrast
