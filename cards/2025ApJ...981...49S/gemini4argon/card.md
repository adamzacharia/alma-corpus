- Question: What is the physical and chemical nature of two peculiar isolated infrared objects with deep ice and dust absorption features discovered by AKARI?

## Qualitative findings

- Compact, spatially unresolved emission of CO(3-2) and SiO(8-7) is detected at the exact infrared positions of both Object 1 and Object 2, while no other dense-gas or hot-core lines (H12CO+, H13CN, SO) and no 853 um dust continuum emission are detected.  
  > "We detect compact emission of CO(3–2, $E_{u}$ = 33 K) and SiO(8–7, $E_{u}$ = 75 K) at the positions of Object 1 and 2 (Figures 2–3 c, d, f, g)." (Results and analysis); "No other emission line was detected although the present spectral setup covers transitions of dense gas tracers such as H$^{12}$CO$^+$(4-3, $E_u$= 43 K), H$^{13}$CN(4-3, $E_u$= 41 K), and SO($N_J$ = 8$_{8}$–7$_{7}$, $E_u$= 88 K)." (Results and analysis); "No continuum emission was detected in both objects despite the presence of deep ice and dust absorption bands." (Results and analysis)
- Both objects are spatially and kinematically isolated from surrounding line-of-sight CO clouds and from each other.  
  > "A clear velocity and spatial separation of the molecular gas associated with Object 1 and 2 from those of the surrounding line-of-sight CO gas would suggest that they are isolated and not associated with extended CO gas." (Systemic velocities and kinematic distances); "Furthermore, although Object 1 and 2 show similar infrared and submillimeter characteristics and they are located in the similar direction in the sky (3.3 arcminutes separation), their systemic velocities are different from each other, suggesting that they are kinematically unrelated." (Systemic velocities and kinematic distances)
- The combined infrared and submillimeter properties of Object 1 and 2 cannot be explained by any known class of interstellar ice-absorption sources (embedded YSOs, edge-on disks, background stars behind dense clouds, or OH/IR AGB stars), suggesting a previously unknown or rare type of isolated icy object.  
  > "These characteristics, (i) rich ice absorption features, (ii) large visual extinction, (iii) lack of mid-infrared and submillimeter excess emission, (iv) very compact source size, (v) SiO-dominated broad molecular line emission, and (vi) isolation, cannot easily be accounted for by any of known interstellar icy sources." (Summary); "They may represent a previously unknown or rare type of isolated icy objects." (Summary)

- Why this data: High-spatial-resolution submillimeter molecular line and continuum observations with ALMA can diagnose whether the icy objects harbor hot molecular cores around protostars or compact dense clouds in the line of sight.  
  > "High-spatial-resolution submillimeter observations are a powerful tool to diagnose their nature." (Introduction); "In this case, submillimeter observations will detect high-excitation transitions of hot-core tracers such as CH$_3$OH and SO$_2$." (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: tclean imaging with natural weighting (robust = 2.0) was tested instead of Briggs robust = 0.5, but rejected because target line SNR did not change significantly.  
  > "We also tried $\mathtt{tclean}$ with a robustness parameter of 2.0 (natural weighting), but the signal-to-noise ratio of emission lines in the target sources did not change significantly." (Data reduction)

  > "In the following discussion, we assume the far distance for Object 1, because it shares a common submillimeter and infrared characteristics with Object 2." (Luminosities)
