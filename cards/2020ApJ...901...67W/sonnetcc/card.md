## Analysis recipe

1. **Average and assemble visibility data per epoch**: Incoherent averaging of visibility amplitudes per scan, bispectral averaging for closure phases, incoherent averaging across bands and parallel-hand polarizations when both available; fit each year as one data set  
  > "We use incoherent averaging to estimate visibility amplitudes on each scan ($\sim$ few minutes of continuous observation) and bispectral averaging to estimate closure phases" (2 Observations and data); "Whenever both bands or both parallel-hand polarization components were available, we incoherently averaged all simultaneous visibility amplitudes." (2 Observations and data)
   - parameter: Multi-day data of one year fit simultaneously with a single source model  
  > "we simultaneously utilize the entire data set from each year, fitting to data from multiple days with a single source model, when available" (2 Observations and data)
   - assumption: No interday variability in proto-EHT data sets  
  > "lack of evidence for interday variability in the proto-EHT data sets" (2 Observations and data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Inspect visibility amplitudes against a thin-ring model and the 2017 source model**: Compare amplitudes versus projected baseline length with analytic thin-ring Fourier transform curves and with the mean 2017 eht-imaging model sampled at the earlier (u,v) points  
  > "Dashed lines represent $\widehat{R}$, the analytic Fourier transform of an infinitely thin ring with a total intensity $I_0$ and a diameter $d_0$" (2.5 M87* data properties)
   - software: eht-imaging
   - parameter: d_0 = 45.0 microarcsec; I_0 = 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 Jy  
  > "we use $d_0\,=\,45.0\,\mu$as and show $\widehat{R}$ curves corresponding to $I_0\,=\,$0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 to guide the eye" (2.5 M87* data properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Test the fitting procedure on synthetic data**: Generate synthetic observations of four emission models (GRMHD1, GRMHD2, MODEL1, MODEL2) with the (u,v) coverage and thermal errors of each epoch plus time-dependent station gain errors, then fit the RT model  
  > "by sampling four emission models (GRMHD1, GRMHD2, MODEL1, MODEL2) with the $(u,v)$-coverage and thermal error budget reported for past observations." (4 Modeling synthetic data); "Additionally, corruption from time-dependent station-based gain errors has been folded into the synthetic observations." (4 Modeling synthetic data)
   - software: eht-imaging, iharm, ipole, Themis
   - parameter: GRMHD snapshot: SANE, a*=0.5, R_high=40, R_low=1, inclination 22 deg  
  > "The camera is oriented with an inclination angle of $22^\circ$." (4.1 GRMHD snapshots)
   - assumption: Disk plane perpendicular to the black hole jet (no tilt); thermal electron distribution  
  > "We also assume that the accretion disk plane is perpendicular to the black hole jet (the disk is not tilted)." (4.1 GRMHD snapshots)
   - fitting: RT model fit with Themis as in Step 4  
  > "shows a summary of the maximum likelihood (ML) RT model fits to the synthetic data sets" (4.3 Results for the synthetic data sets)
   - validation: Recovered diameter and orientation compared with truth and with 2017 values; image-domain position angle eta used as a reference  
  > "We find that the diameter is well constrained in general" (4.3 Results for the synthetic data sets); "The orientation $\phi_{\rm B}$ is poorly constrained, with posterior distributions that depend strongly on the details of the $(u,v)$-coverage." (4.3 Results for the synthetic data sets)
4. **Fit RT and RG ring models to the 2009-2013 and 2017 data**: Bayesian fit of a slashed thick ring (RT) and a blurred thin slashed ring (RG), plus a large symmetric Gaussian for zero-baseline flux, with marginalization over station amplitude gains  
  > "In this model, the flux is contained within a circular annulus with the inner and outer radii $R_{\rm in}$ and $R_{\rm out}$, respectively." (3.1 Model specification); "we parameterize this large-scale flux using a large symmetric Gaussian component consisting of two parameters, flux and size." (3.1 Model specification)
   - software: Themis
   - parameter: Uniform priors: R_out [0,200] microarcsec, phi_B [0,2pi], psi [0,1], I_0 [0,2] Jy, beta [0,1]; RG: sigma uniform [0,40] microarcsec, psi fixed to [1e-7,1e-6]  
  > "Uniform priors are used for each of these parameters, with ranges of $[0,200]$ $\mu$as for $R_{\text{out}}$, $[0,2\pi]$ for $\phi_{\rm B}$, $[0,1]$ for $\psi$, $[0,2]$ Jy for $I_0$, and $[0,1]$ for $\beta$." (3.2 Fitting procedure and priors)
   - parameter: Secondary large-diameter mode excised, limiting d to less than ~80 microarcsec; equivalent to R_out prior [0,45] microarcsec for 2009-2012  
  > "effectively limits the diameter $d$ to be less than $\sim$ 80 $\mu$as" (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations); "We have verified numerically that this procedure produces the same results as restricting the priors on $R_{\rm out}$ to $[0,45]\, \mu$as for the analysis of the 2009–2012 data sets." (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations)
   - parameter: Brightness position angle chosen between phi_B and phi_B+180 deg using the jet position angle phi_jet = 288 deg (prior); 2012 reported as ML estimator  
  > "using the prior information about the position angle of the jet" (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations); "where $\phi_{\rm jet} = 288^\circ$" (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations)
   - parameter: 5 geometric parameters for 2009 and 5+2=7 for other epochs  
  > "Ultimately, the models that we use have 5 geometric parameters for the 2009 data set and 5+2=7 geometric parameters in all other cases." (3.1 Model specification)
   - assumption: Station amplitude gains: Gaussian priors, mean 0.0, sigma 0.15, one gain per (station, scan), uncorrelated across scans  
  > "we have adopted rather conservative 15% amplitude gain uncertainties for each station, represented by symmetric Gaussian priors with a mean value of 0.0 and standard deviation of 0.15." (3.2 Fitting procedure and priors)
   - fitting: Themis, differential evolution MCMC; gains marginalized at each step via a quadratic expansion of the log-likelihood; uniform priors as listed  
  > "Themis operates within a Bayesian formalism, employing a differential evolution Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler to explore the posterior space." (3.2 Fitting procedure and priors); "At each MCMC step, Themis marginalizes over the gain amplitude parameters (subject to Gaussian priors) using a quadratic expansion of the log-likelihood around its maximum given the current parameter vector" (3.2 Fitting procedure and priors)
   - validation: Consistency of RT and RG results; comparison with earlier EHT 2017 analyses  
  > "models considered in this work measure diameter and position angle consistently with more complex crescent models (GC) and with the image domain methods within the expected intermodel variation" (5.3 Consistency with the prior analysis)
5. **Assess fit quality**: Because chi-square per degree of freedom is unreliable, use mean absolute normalized residuals with and without fitted gains (e-bar, e-hat) and compare with thermal and gain-inflated error bars  
  > "we characterize the quality of the ML estimator fits to data using the two following metrics" (5.2 Quality of the visibility amplitude fits); "For all 2009–2013 data sets, the flexibility of the full model is sufficient to fit the sparse data to within the thermal uncertainty level with a best-fit ML estimator." (5.2 Quality of the visibility amplitude fits)
   - software: Themis
   - parameter: Inflated uncertainties s_i = sqrt(sigma_i^2 + 2 (0.15 V_i)^2)  
  > "approximately capturing the uncertainty related to the amplitude gains" (5.2 Quality of the visibility amplitude fits)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual plots with thermal and inflated error bars  
  > "In the bottom rows of Figures [10](#fig:fit_quality)-[11](#fig:fit_quality2) we indicate two error bars." (5.2 Quality of the visibility amplitude fits)
6. **Test the shadow (central flux depression) and its dependence on optical depth**: Use the fractional thickness f_w from RT/RG fits; ray-trace one SANE GRMHD snapshot (a*=0.5, R_high=20) with density scale adjusted to give total compact flux 0.1-1.1 Jy  
  > "The snapshot is then repeatedly ray-traced with its density scale (and hence opacities and emissivities) adjusted to give a total compact flux density equal to 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, and 1.1 Jy." (6.1 Presence of the shadow feature)
   - software: ipole, Themis
   - parameter: R_high = 20, R_low = 1, spin a* = 0.5, SANE  
  > "electron temperature parameter $R_{\rm high} = 20$" (6.1 Presence of the shadow feature)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Images normalized by brightness maximum and blurred to compare diameter and position angle across flux levels  
  > "variation in the total compact flux density between 0.1 and 1.1 Jy does not eliminate the central brightness depression" (6.1 Presence of the shadow feature)
7. **Quantify variability and compare with GRMHD position-angle distributions**: Compare circular standard deviation of ML position angles for real data versus static synthetic data; histogram image-domain position angle eta over 500 snapshots for four GRMHD simulations and compare with measured angles  
  > "For each snapshot we calculate the position angle of the bright component $\eta$" (6.3 Time variability of the source geometry); "the circular standard deviation is equal to 48$^\circ$" (6.3 Time variability of the source geometry)
   - software: ipole
   - parameter: Four simulations S1-S4 (MAD/SANE, a* = +/-0.5, R_high = 10 or 160), total compact flux 0.5 Jy  
  > "For our exploratory study, we utilize the following four simulations" (6.3 Time variability of the source geometry)
   - assumption: No tilt between accretion plane and black hole spin  
  > "In the current analysis we also make an assumption of no tilt between the plane of accretion and the black hole spin." (6.4 Limitations of the current approach)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Static synthetic spread (19, 19, 11, 4 deg) used as reference for intrinsic variability  
  > "is equal to 19$^\circ$, 19$^\circ$, 11$^\circ$, 4$^\circ$ for GRMHD1, GRMHD2, MODEL1, and MODEL2, respectively" (6.3 Time variability of the source geometry)

## Qualitative findings

- Ring diameter is stable across 2009-2017, consistent with a persistent asymmetric ring of ~40 microarcsec  
  > "we find the M87$^{\ast}$ morphology in 2009–2017 to be consistent with a persistent asymmetric ring of ${\sim}40\,\mu$as diameter." (abstract)
- Brightness position angle varies in time; significant difference between 2013 and 2017  
  > "In particular, we find a significant difference between the position angle measured in 2013 and 2017." (abstract)
- The GRMHD MAD model with a*=0.5 and R_high=160 is inconsistent with the measured position angles  
  > "is inconsistent with the presented position angle measurements." (7 Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Additional epochs are needed to test persistence of the ring and quantify variability beyond the 2017 week  
  > "Additional observations are required to investigate the persistence of the primary image feature – a ring with azimuthal brightness asymmetry – and to quantify the image variability on event horizon scales." (abstract)
- Why this method: Data lack baseline coverage for imaging but constrain simple geometric models, motivated by the 2017 results and GRMHD expectations of a persistent ring  
  > "they are sufficient to constrain simple geometrical models" (1 Introduction); "We employ asymmetric ring models that are motivated by both results obtained with the mature 2017 array and the expectation from GRMHD simulations that the ring feature is persistent." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Asymmetric Gaussian model fits the 2013 data about as well as the ring; rejected because ML Gaussian models are inconsistent in size, shape and orientation across years while ring models are similar in size  
  > "Both models attain similar fit qualities, as determined by Bayesian and Akaike information criteria" (3 Modeling Approach); "the maximum likelihood Gaussian models are very inconsistent in size, shape, and orientation across different years" (6.2 Black hole shadow or a transient feature)
- Alternative: RG model (blurred thin ring) used as a check against model-specific biases of the RT model  
  > "As a check against model-specific biases, we consider a second model consisting of an infinitesimally thin slashed ring, blurred with a Gaussian kernel" (3.1 Model specification)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Large-diameter secondary ring mode in 2009-2012 posteriors had to be excised using long-baseline detections from other epochs  
  > "Modeling tests revealed the presence of a large-diameter secondary ring mode in the posterior distributions for the 2009–2012 data sets" (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Visibility amplitudes alone cannot break the 180 degree degeneracy of phi_B, so only the axis of asymmetry is constrained  
  > "Hence, visibility amplitude data alone cannot break the degeneracy between the orientation of $\phi_{\rm B}$ and $\phi'_{\rm B} = \phi_{\rm B} + 180^\circ$" (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Geometric model parameters have no direct relation to physical source parameters  
  > "It is important to recognize that the parameters of a geometric model have no direct relation to the physical parameters of the source" (3.3 Degeneracies and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Chi-square goodness-of-fit cannot generally be used because effective degrees of freedom are unclear  
  > "the number of model parameters may be formally larger than the number of data points, and this complicates our estimation of the number of effective degrees of freedom" (4.3 Results for the synthetic data sets)
- Limitation (authors): GRMHD comparison omits electron heating physics, nonthermal electrons and disk tilt  
  > "The simulations that we consider do not capture the full extent of the physics relevant for M87$^{\ast}$." (6.4 Limitations of the current approach)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only visibility amplitudes (plus a few 2012 closure phases) constrain 2009-2013 fits, so position-angle differences rely on a jet-orientation prior
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gain priors of 15% and the per-scan gain model may absorb real amplitude variability
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Variability claim rests on comparing spreads of ML estimators with synthetic data without a formal significance test
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ML estimators do not coincide with marginal posterior maxima; results for 2009-2012 are weakly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA is used only as the phased anchor in 2017 data from earlier papers; no ALMA data are reduced here
