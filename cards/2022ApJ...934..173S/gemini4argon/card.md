## Analysis recipe

1. **Select galaxy sample and prepare data cubes**: Select 17 ALMaQUEST galaxies and 17 EDGE-CALIFA galaxies with inclinations between 40 and 75 deg, excluding barred, interacting, or morphologically distorted galaxies via visual inspection. Convolve CALIFA Halpha cubes to match EDGE CO spatial resolution (~4.5''). Apply S/N > 3 spaxel cut (or dilated mask for EDGE CO).  
  > "As adopted by (cite: 2008AJ....136.2648D), the practical upper and lower limits of inclination are suggested to be $40^\circ$ and $75^\circ$, respectively." (The subsample selected for this study); "We further exclude galaxies that show apparent bar structures, interacting galaxies, and galaxies with distorted morphology through visual inspection, as similarly done by (cite: 2020ApJ...900..109A)." (The subsample selected for this study); "Since CALIFA and EDGE have different spatial resolutions, with EDGE having the worse resolution, we convolved the CALIFA data to match the EDGE resolution." (The EDGE survey)
   - software: MaNGA reduction pipeline, MaNGA Data Analysis Pipeline, PIPE3D
   - parameter: Inclination range: 40 deg to 75 deg  
  > "As adopted by (cite: 2008AJ....136.2648D), the practical upper and lower limits of inclination are suggested to be $40^\circ$ and $75^\circ$, respectively." (The subsample selected for this study)
   - parameter: Flux threshold: S/N > 3 for MaNGA Halpha, CALIFA Halpha, and ALMaQUEST CO(1-0)  
  > "We only include the spaxels with S/N$>$3 for H$\alpha$ flux in this study." (The MaNGA survey); "In this study, we only include spaxels with S/N$>$3 for $^{12}$CO(1-0)." (The ALMaQUEST survey)
   - assumption: Gas motion in selected galaxies is dominated by rotation with constant inclination and PA  
  > "This selection makes sure that the gas motion is dominated by rotation and also validates the assumption of constant inclination and PA." (The subsample selected for this study)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit 3D tilted-ring models to derive CO and Halpha rotation curves and velocity dispersions**: Use 3D-Barolo in a two-step process on 3D data cubes. Step 1 fits geometric parameters, rotation velocity, and velocity dispersion simultaneously using annulus width of 1 spatial resolution unit. Step 2 fixes geometric parameters and fits V_rot and sigma_v with annulus width of 0.5 spatial resolution unit. Halpha cubes are fit using the same kinematic center, inclination, and PA derived from CO.  
  > "In the first step, we use one unit of spatial resolution as the annulus width and fit the geometric parameters, rotation curves, and velocity dispersion at the same time." (Fit rotation curves); "In the second step, we fix the geometric parameters and fit the rotation velocity and velocity dispersion with half of the unit of the spatial resolution as the annulus width." (Fit rotation curves); "The H$\alpha$ rotation curves for our sample are fit using the same method under the assumption that CO and H$\alpha$ are co-planar." (Fit rotation curves)
   - software: 3D-Barolo
   - parameter: Disk scale height z_d fixed to 100 pc  
  > "On the other hand, we fix the disk scale height to 100 pc." (3D-Barolo)
   - parameter: Initial V_rot = 100 km/s, initial sigma_v = 30 km/s  
  > "The initial values of rotation velocity and velocity dispersion are set to be 100 and 30 km/s, respectively." (Fit rotation curves)
   - parameter: Flux normalization: pixel-by-pixel, azimuthal weighting w(theta) = |cos(theta)|^2, residual Delta r = |M - D|  
  > "In this study, we apply the pixel-by-pixel approach since it allows us to accounts for the non-axisymmetric gas distribution." (3D-Barolo); "In this study, we apply the pixel-by-pixel approach since it allows us to accounts for the non-axisymmetric gas distribution." (3D-Barolo)
   - assumption: Thin disk, circular motion, constant inclination, PA, systemic velocity, and kinematic center across rings; CO and Halpha are co-planar  
  > "Finally, we assume a constant inclination, position angle, systematic velcity and kinematic center for each galaxy." (3D-Barolo); "In other words, CO and H$\alpha$ share the same kinematics center, inclination, and PA." (Fit rotation curves)
   - fitting: Minimization of pixel-by-pixel residual function F = (1/n) sum(|M - D| |cos(theta)|^2); uncertainties estimated by Gaussian distribution oversampling around the minimum where residual function < 1.05 of minimum.  
  > "In practice, 3DB fits the datacube ring by ring by building an artificial 3D model cube and finds the best fit through minimizing the residual calculated pixel by pixel between the datacube and the model cube." (3D-Barolo); "Errors for each parameter are determined by the range where the residual increases 5 percent with respect to the minimum. i.e., the range where the residual function is smaller than 1.05." (Fit rotation curves)
   - validation: Tested central turnover artifact and spectral resolution sensitivity by convolving ALMaQUEST CO cubes (sigma_inst ~ 11 km/s) to MaNGA (70 km/s) and CALIFA (116 km/s) spectral resolutions and re-fitting; tested thin disk assumption by re-fitting with scale height of 1 kpc.  
  > "To test if this feature is artificial, we degrade the spectral resolution of our CO datacubes, which have original spectral resolution $\sigma_{inst}\approx$11km/s, to the spectral resolution of MaNGA H$\alpha$ data ($\sigma_{inst}\approx$70km/s) by convolving them with a Gaussian kernel along the spectral axis." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "Hence, We convolve our ALMaQUEST KSS CO datacubes, which have original spectral resolution $\sigma_{inst} \approx$ 11 km/s, to the resolution of the CALIFA H$\alpha$ observation ($\sigma_{inst} \approx$ 116 km/s) with a Gaussian kernel and use 3DB to fit the CO datacube again." (The robustness of the rotation curves and the ADC corrections); "We re-fit the ALMaQUEST KSS and the EDGE-CALIFA KSS again with a scale height of 1 kpc, and then compare the fit result to the ones with a scale height of 100 pc." (Thin disk assumption)
3. **Quantify MaNGA-CALIFA systematic offset and calculate CO-Halpha velocity differences (Delta V)**: Linearly interpolate Halpha rotation curves to CO radial bins between 0.3 R_e and 1.5 R_e (excluding the central 1 beam area). Calculate variance-weighted mean velocity difference Delta V = V_rot,CO - V_rot,Halpha and standard deviation sigma_DeltaV. Compare 8 overlapping MaNGA-CALIFA galaxies via bootstrap to find an 8.5 km/s systematic offset, and subtract 8.5 km/s from Delta V in the EDGE-CALIFA KSS.  
  > "To quantify the velocity difference between CO and H$\alpha$ rotation curves for a given galaxy, we first linearly interpolate the H$\alpha$ rotation curve and resample the H$\alpha$ rotation velocity at the same radii where we sample the CO rotation velocity." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "We define $\Delta V$ for an individual galaxy as the variance weighted mean of the velocity differences $V_{rot,CO}-V_{rot,H\alpha}$." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "To compensate for this systematics, we subtracted the $\Delta V$ in the CALIFA KSS by 8.5 km/s." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation)
   - software: 3D-Barolo
   - parameter: Radial comparison range: 0.3 R_e to 1.5 R_e, excluding region within 1 beam from center (4 beams for 8952-12701)  
  > "the radial range in which we compare the CO and H$\alpha$ rotation curves are set to be between 0.3$R_e$ and 1.5$R_e$ for both the ALMaQUEST and the EDGE-CALIFA samples that are constrained by the coverage of CO." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "In this study, we exclude the rotation curves fit result within one resolution area from the center in the following analysis." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation)
   - parameter: CALIFA instrumental dispersion sigma_inst = 116 km/s; systematic correction = 8.5 km/s  
  > "In this study, we adopt the instrumental dispersion of all CALIFA H$\alpha$ data to be 116 km/s (cite: 2016A&A...594A..36S)." (The comparison of the velocity dispersion radial profiles); "Given that MaNGA data has the better spectral resolution, we correct the $\Delta V$s (i.e. the variance weighted mean of the velocity differences $V_{rot,CO}-V_{rot,H\alpha}$, as defined in Sec [5.1](#sec:sec5.1)) in the EDGE-CALIFA KSS vaule by subtracting 8.5 km/s." (The comparison of the rotation curves)
   - assumption: Rotation curves based on MaNGA data are accurate and MaNGA-CALIFA systematics are attributed to CALIFA data  
  > "We assume the rotation curves based on the MaNGA data are accurate and made the corresponding correction to the CALIFA data." (Abstract)
   - fitting: Bootstrap resampling of radial velocity differences in 8 MaNGA-CALIFA overlapping galaxies; Spearman correlation analysis between Delta V and radial mean of sqrt(sigma_Halpha^2 - sigma_CO^2).  
  > "Finally, we use bootstrap to test if this systematics is reliable, and a median value of $\Delta V = 8.5$ with a standard deviation of 1.3 km/s is found." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "We run the Spearman correlation analysis and find that the correlation coefficients give $r_s$ = 0.51 $\pm$ 0.19, 0.86 $\pm$ 0.22, and 0.71 $\pm$ 0.14 for the ALMaQUEST KSS, EDGE-CALIFA KSS, and the combined sample, respectively." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation)
   - validation: Tested opposite assumption where systematic offset is in MaNGA (+8.5 km/s to ALMaQUEST KSS); checked radial velocity dispersion profiles of the 8 overlapping galaxies between MaNGA and CALIFA.  
  > "For comparison, we discuss an opposite case, in which the offset is caused by the systematics in the MaNGA data only." (The MaNGA-CALIFA systematics); "To validate this choice, we compare the H$\alpha$ velocity dispersion radial profiles in these 8 overlapping galaxies measured between the MaNGA data and the CALIFA data." (The comparison of the velocity dispersion radial profiles)
4. **Apply asymmetric drift correction (ADC) to CO and Halpha rotation curves**: Fit radial profiles of velocity dispersion with polynomials (degree <= 3) and fit sigma_v^2 Sigma_obs cos(i) with an analytic profile f(R) to compute asymmetric drift velocity V_A and intrinsic circular velocity V_c = sqrt(V_rot^2 + V_A^2), then recalculate the velocity difference Delta V'.  
  > "The velocity dispersion is fitted by polynomials $\sigma_p(R,n_p)$ with the degree $n_p$ equal to or lower than 3." (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction); "On the other hand, we fit $\sigma_{V}^2 \Sigma_{obs}cos i$ by the the function ((cite: 2002AJ....123.1316B))" (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction); "Figure [5](#fig:kernel_DeltaV_asy_corr) shows the KDD of $\Delta V$ from both the ALMaQUEST KSS and the EDGE-CALIFA KSS after ADC, which is denoted as $\Delta V^{\prime}$ hereafter." (The CO-H\alpha velocity discrepancy after asymmetric drift correction)
   - parameter: Polynomial degree n_p <= 3 for velocity dispersion profile  
  > "The velocity dispersion is fitted by polynomials $\sigma_p(R,n_p)$ with the degree $n_p$ equal to or lower than 3." (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction)
   - assumption: Gas motion in equilibrium, turbulence pressure dominates (P = rho sigma_v^2), thin disk, isotropic velocity dispersion, constant scale height, exponential vertical distribution, constant filling factors, path length, temperature for Halpha, and constant CO X-factor  
  > "Under the assumption that turbulence pressure dominates the pressure term, the pressure term can be expressed as $\rho \sigma^{2}_{v}$ (cite: 2010ApJ...721..547D), where $\sigma_{v}$ is velocity dispersion." (The radial pressure support); "From Eq.[\[eq:3\]](#eq:3) to Eq.[\[eq:4\]](#eq:4), one needs to assume a constant scale height and exponential distribution in the vertical direction, which is adopted in this study." (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction); "In addition, we also assume constant filling factors, path length, and temperature for the H$\alpha$ and the X factor for the CO." (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction)
   - fitting: Polynomial fit to radial velocity dispersion and functional form f(R) = f_0 (R_c/arcsec + 1)(R_c/arcsec + exp(R/R_d))^-1 fit to sigma_v^2 Sigma_obs cos(i).  
  > "$f(R)=f_0(\frac{R_c}{arcsec}+1)(\frac{R_c}{arcsec}+e^{\frac{R}{R_d}})^{-1}$" (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction)
   - validation: Verified ADC stability against degraded spectral resolution by comparing ADC-corrected CO rotation curves before and after convolving to 116 km/s.  
  > "The comparison of the rotation curves after ADC correction is denoted as $\Delta V^{\prime}_{CO}$." (The robustness of the rotation curves and the ADC corrections)
5. **Evaluate the role of extra-planar diffuse ionized gas (eDIG) and compute star formation rate surface density**: Divide sample into high and low EW(Halpha) groups using a flux-weighted average cut of EW(Halpha) = 14 Angstrom within the Delta V region, and compare the resulting Delta V' kernel density distributions. Calculate deprojected Sigma_SFR in the Delta V region from Balmer-decrement corrected Halpha flux for BPT star-forming spaxels.  
  > "Following (cite: 2009RvMP...81..969H), we use the flux-weighted average of EW(H$\alpha$) in the region where we calculate the $\Delta V$ in each galaxy to divide our galaxies into two groups based on an EW(H$\alpha$)=14Å cut." (The presence of eDIG); "To take into account the effect of dust extinction on SFR, we use the Balmer decrement to correct the H$\alpha$ flux based on the equation which assumes a (cite: 1989ApJ...345..245C) extinction curve" (Star formation rate surface density); "Finally, we sum up the SFR spaxels within the region where we determine $\Delta V$ and divided it by the deprojected area the inclination correction applied." (Star formation rate surface density)
   - software: PIPE3D
   - parameter: EW(Halpha) = 14 Angstrom division cut  
  > "Following (cite: 2009RvMP...81..969H), we use the flux-weighted average of EW(H$\alpha$) in the region where we calculate the $\Delta V$ in each galaxy to divide our galaxies into two groups based on an EW(H$\alpha$)=14Å cut." (The presence of eDIG)
   - parameter: S/N > 3 in Halpha, Hbeta, [OIII]5007, and [SII]6717,31 for Sigma_SFR  
  > "To calculate the $\Sigma_{SFR}$ within the region where we determine $\Delta V$, for each galaxy, we first pick up the spaxel in which the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)$>$3 in the emission lines $H\alpha$, $H\beta$, \[OIII\]$\lambda$5007 and \[SII\]$\lambda \lambda 6717,31$." (Star formation rate surface density)
   - assumption: eDIG is negligible in high EW(Halpha) (>14 Angstrom) group and non-negligible in low EW(Halpha) group; Cardelli et al. (1989) extinction curve, intrinsic Halpha/Hbeta = 2.86, and Salpeter IMF for SFR  
  > "We simply assume the presence of the eDIG is negligible in the high EW(H$\alpha$) group, whereas it might be non-negligible in the low EW(H$\alpha$) group." (The presence of eDIG); "To take into account the effect of dust extinction on SFR, we use the Balmer decrement to correct the H$\alpha$ flux based on the equation which assumes a (cite: 1989ApJ...345..245C) extinction curve" (Star formation rate surface density); ", which assumes a Salpeter Initial Mass Function (IMF)." (Star formation rate surface density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked spaxel-level EW(Halpha) distribution in the high EW group, confirming 90% of spaxels have EW(Halpha) > 14 Angstrom.  
  > "As we can see, most of the spaxels (90%) in these regions have $EW(H{\alpha})$ larger than 14 Å, which are usually HII regions." (Thin disk assumption)

## Qualitative findings

- No correlation is found between the CO-Halpha velocity difference (Delta V) and galaxy inclination, SFR, M_*, sSFR, CO V_max, Sigma_SFR, or Sigma_M*, while Delta V tends to be smaller in late-type galaxies (low Sersic index).  
  > "Except for the Sersic index, we did not find any apparent trend between $\Delta V$ and other parameters." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "These plots suggest that (i) no correlation be found between the $\Delta V$ and the inclination; (ii) $\Delta V$ tends to be smaller in late-type galaxies (our galaxies are biased to the late-type, though)." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation)
- Convolving ALMaQUEST CO cubes to 116 km/s and re-fitting shows that neither the rotation curves nor the ADC corrections are sensitive to the lower spectral resolution of Halpha.  
  > "By combining this result and the fact that the velocity dispersion of H$\alpha$ is usually higher than that of CO, we conclude that: (i) The systematic velocity discrepancy between CO and H$\alpha$ we found is not sensitive to their different spectral resolutions within the range considered in this work; (ii) Statistically, the ADC applied to the rotation curves presented in this work are reliable." (The robustness of the rotation curves and the ADC corrections)
- Re-fitting the galaxies with a 1 kpc disk scale height instead of 100 pc has little impact on the derived rotation velocities or velocity dispersions.  
  > "We re-fit the ALMaQUEST KSS and the EDGE-CALIFA KSS again with a scale height of 1 kpc, and then compare the fit result to the ones with a scale height of 100 pc." (Thin disk assumption); "As we can see, there is little impact on the rotation velocity. The same result is found on the velocity dispersion." (Thin disk assumption)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The combined ALMaQUEST and EDGE-CALIFA surveys provide spatially matched high spatial and spectral resolution CO(1-0) and Halpha IFU observations across a wide radial extension for a larger combined sample of 34 nearby galaxies.  
  > "Besides the high spatial and spectral resolution of CO(1-0) and H$\alpha$ emission line provided by these datasets, the spatially matched resolution in CO(1-0) and H$\alpha$ observations make it more ideal to compare the molecular and ionized gas rotation curves." (Introduction)
- Why this method: 3D-Barolo fits the 3D data cube directly after convolving the model to the observational spatial and spectral resolution, providing better control of beam smearing than 2D velocity map fitting.  
  > "The most severe one is the beam smearing effect, which tends to flatten the gradient of the velocity field (cite: 2000AJ....119.1579V; 2009A&A...493..871S)." (Tilted-ring model); "Before comparing the datacube and the model cube, the former need to be convolved to the same spatial and spectral resolution as the latter. This ensures the full control of instrumental effects, such as the beam smearing effect, which can significantly influence the fitting result at the region where the velocity gradient is large." (3D-Barolo)
- Alternative: 2D velocity map tilted-ring fitting was rejected in favor of 3D cube fitting (3D-Barolo) due to severe beam smearing flattening the velocity gradient.  
  > "The standard approach to apply the tilted ring model was to fit the 2D-velocity maps, but some drawbacks of such a 2D fitting algorithm exist. The most severe one is the beam smearing effect, which tends to flatten the gradient of the velocity field (cite: 2000AJ....119.1579V; 2009A&A...493..871S)." (Tilted-ring model)
- Alternative: Azimuthally averaged flux normalization in 3D-Barolo was rejected in favor of pixel-by-pixel normalization to account for non-axisymmetric gas distributions.  
  > "Two different kinds of normalization are provided in 3DB: pixel-by-pixel and azimuthally averaged." (3D-Barolo); "In this study, we apply the pixel-by-pixel approach since it allows us to accounts for the non-axisymmetric gas distribution." (3D-Barolo)
- Alternative: Attributing the 8.5 km/s MaNGA-CALIFA systematic offset to MaNGA instead of CALIFA was tested as an alternative scenario, yielding a larger median Delta V (15.0 km/s before ADC, 10.8 km/s after ADC).  
  > "For comparison, we discuss an opposite case, in which the offset is caused by the systematics in the MaNGA data only." (The MaNGA-CALIFA systematics); "In this scenario, the median value of $\Delta$V and the $\Delta V^{\prime}$ in the ALMaQUEST+EDGE-CALIFA KSS become greater, 15.0 km/s and 10.8 km/s, respectively as shown in the Figure [7](#fig:ALMa+8.5)." (The MaNGA-CALIFA systematics)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties on the asymmetric drift correction (ADC) and the scatter in individual velocity dispersion measurements are not propagated into the ADC results.  
  > "No error of asymmetric drift correction is included in this study." (Notes about the asymmetric drift correction); "On the other hand, the scatter of the velocity dispersion offset simply means the asymmetric drift correction measurement is subject to the uncertainty in the individual velocity dispersion measurement, which is not considered in this study." (The comparison of the velocity dispersion radial profiles)
- Limitation (authors): The exact cause of the 8.5 km/s systematic offset between MaNGA and CALIFA Halpha rotation velocities is not well known.  
  > "Although the exact cause of the MaNGA-CALIFA systematics is not well known, this is a reasonable choice based on the fact that the MaNGA survey has higher spectral resolution, better control of instrumental dispersion and potentially lower systematics (cite: 2015ApJ...798....7B; 2021AJ....161...52L)." (The MaNGA-CALIFA systematics)
- Limitation (authors): In the central region (within one resolution element), low spectral resolution and fewer data points cause artificial turnover features in Halpha rotation curves, requiring exclusion of the central beam.  
  > "This phenomenon might be caused by the following two effects: (i) it is more difficult to derive reliable rotation velocity and velocity dispersion at the same time with a low spectral resolution; (ii) there are fewer data points in the most central region, which make it difficult to construct a reliable fit result." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation); "In this study, we exclude the rotation curves fit result within one resolution area from the center in the following analysis." (The systematic discrepancy between CO and H\alpha rotation)
- Limitation (authors): For low EW(Halpha) galaxies, eDIG contaminates mid-plane velocity dispersion and invalidates thin-disk/constant scale height assumptions, making ADC less accurate.  
  > "We note that for the low EW(H$\alpha$) group, the ADC might not be accurate because of the following reasons: (i) the presence of eDIG ”contaminates” the H$\alpha$ velocity dispersion on the mid-plane; (ii) the assumption we made in Sec.[5.2](#sec:sec5.2) (e.g. thin disk, constant scale height, etc) is no longer valid." (The presence of eDIG)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a constant 8.5 km/s additive velocity correction to all EDGE-CALIFA galaxies based on 8 overlapping MaNGA-CALIFA galaxies is a strong empirical adjustment that directly shifts the EDGE-CALIFA median Delta V from ~15.2 km/s to 6.7 km/s.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing the Halpha kinematic center, inclination, and position angle to the CO-derived values enforces co-planarity by construction, which could mask genuine kinematic warps or misalignments between molecular and ionized gas disks.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainties in the radial derivatives required for the asymmetric drift correction (which can be noisy and sensitive to the chosen polynomial order n_p <= 3) are not included in the error budget.
