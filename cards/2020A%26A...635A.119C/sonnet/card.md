## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce IFU data and extract H-alpha maps**: esorex/SPARK reduction; line fitting with AICc model selection per spectrum; adaptive binning up to 5x5 pixels  
  > "with an adaptive binning approach up to 5$\times$5 pixels depending on the signal-to-noise ratios" (3.1)
   - software: esorex, SPARK, IRAF
   - parameter: Delta chi2 > 16 for H-alpha-only, >9 per extra line  
  > "provides a $\chi^2$ improvement of $\Delta\chi^2 > 16$" (3.1)
   - fitting: AICc-based model selection; errors via Monte Carlo injections  
  > "The errors are derived using Monte Carlo simulations" (3.1)
   - validation: Sersic fits on narrow-band H-alpha images give consistent results  
  > "we also perform the Sérsic fits on the narrow-band H$\alpha$ imaging" (3.1)
2. **Measure sizes of H-alpha and 870 um via curve of growth**: Circular-aperture curve of growth; half of normalised flux via spline linear fit; PSF subtracted in quadrature  
  > "We use a spline linear fit to the measurements and derive the radius, along with the uncertainties, that corresponds to half of the normalized flux density." (3.1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with galfit Sersic major-axis sizes  
  > "they are consistent with those obtained from the curve-of-growth methods" (3.1)
3. **Fit Sersic profiles to H-alpha maps**: galfit single Sersic fit with Sersic index 0.1-4  
  > "We limit the Sérsic index range to between 0.1 and 4" (3.1)
   - software: galfit v3.0.5
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure 870 um flux and size in uv-plane**: Elliptical Gaussian fits in visibilities; uvmultifit for ALESS75.1  
  > "We measure their 870 $\mu$m continuum flux densities and sizes in the visibility domain using the casa package uvmodelfit assuming elliptical Gaussian profiles" (3.2)
   - software: CASA uvmodelfit, uvmultifit
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: imfit flux ratio to uv flux 1.1+-0.1, no missing flux  
  > "mean ratio of 1.1$\pm$0.1" (3.2)
5. **Compare H-alpha SFRs with IR SFRs**: hyperz UV-to-NIR fit for A_V; FIR template fitting for L_IR (8-1000 um); Kennicutt & Evans calibrations; attenuation relations from Calzetti and Kashino  
  > "we estimate the star-formation rates based on H$\alpha$ and infrared luminosities by adopting the calibration provided by (cite: Kennicutt:2012aa)" (4.2.1)
   - software: hyperz, magphys
   - parameter: A_V 0-5 in steps of 0.01; Calzetti law; BC03 templates  
  > "We follow the (cite: Calzetti:2000aa) law to allow total attenuation ($A_V$) between 0 to 5 in steps of 0.01." (4.2.1)
   - assumption: Solar metallicity; four SFHs (B, E, Sb, C)  
  > "assuming solar metallicities, with different SFHs" (4.2.1)
   - fitting: chi2 minimisation; A_V values from fits within Delta chi2<1  
  > "take all the $A_V$ values from fits that have $\Delta\chi^2<1$ from the lowest $\chi^2$" (4.2.1)
   - validation: Reproduced Simpson et al. 2014 hyperz results  
  > "We confirm that we are able to reproduce their results." (4.2.1)
6. **Build SFR density profiles**: Differentiate H-alpha curve of growth; IR SFR profile follows 870 um profile scaled to total SFR; PSF deconvolved in quadrature  
  > "the IR-based SFRs in each radial bin is the fraction of the total 870 $\mu$m flux in that bin multiplied by the total SFRs" (4.2.2)
   - assumption: Constant dust temperature and opacity; same attenuation across radial bins  
  > "This method effectively assumes a constant dust temperature and dust opacity" (4.2.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compare A_V with energy-balance SED fitting**: magphys high-z edition fits of UV-to-radio SEDs compared with hyperz A_V and L_IR  
  > "we also model our UV-to-radio SEDs using the magphys code. We adopt the high-$z$ edition" (4.3.1)
   - software: magphys
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Re-ran hyperz with tau-decay SFHs only; conclusions unchanged  
  > "We find that the output values vary within uncertainties and the conclusions remain unchanged." (4.3.1)

## Qualitative findings

- FIR continuum is more compact than H-alpha and optical continuum; proposes a three-component dust model with a central heavily obscured component  
  > "the heavily obscured central regions with low filling factor in which the bulk of attenuation cannot be reflected through H$\alpha$ or OIR continuum." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Needed ~0.5'' band 7 imaging matched to seeing-limited H-alpha for direct comparison  
  > "has a matched or better spatial resolution to the seeing-limited H$\alpha$ data" (2.1)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Disk models in uv fits rejected since Gaussians gave lower chi2  
  > "in all cases Gaussian models produce better fits with lower $\chi^2$" (3.2)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Small sample (six) with archival heterogeneous IFU data, hard to assess selection bias  
  > "it is difficult to assess the potential selection biases" (2.1)
- Limitation (authors): No Balmer decrement available to measure nebular attenuation  
  > "However H$\beta$ is not available in all but a marginal detection from one SMG" (4.2.1)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Six sources, one excluded from SFR analysis; Spearman p=0.2 not significant
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Seeing-limited IFU PSFs without dedicated PSF stars
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uniform attenuation across radial bins assumed
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Three-component model is speculative, not directly tested
