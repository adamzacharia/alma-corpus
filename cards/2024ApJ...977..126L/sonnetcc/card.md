## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the three Band 7 datasets**: Calibrated in CASA; images made with Briggs weighting robust 0.5 to produce CO, SiO and SO line cubes and a continuum image  
  > "The data were calibrated using the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA) package ((cite: CASAteam)) versions 4.5.0, 4.7.2, and 4.7.0 for the projects 2015.1.00024.S, 2015.1.00041.S, and 2016.1.01475.S, respectively." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "a Briggs weighting robust parameter of 0.5 was applied to balance sensitivity and angular resolution" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Make moment maps of CO, SiO and SO and compare structures**: Moment 0 maps of CO and SiO integrated over +/-11 km/s and SO low-velocity over +/-3 km/s relative to V_sys, overlaid on the 358 GHz continuum; CO moment 0 split into blueshifted and redshifted ranges; CO moment 1 map to find velocity gradients  
  > "$\pm11\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, while the SO (LV) was integrated over a range of $\pm 3\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$" (3.1 Comparison between Molecules); "displays the first moment, or intensity-weighted velocity map of the CO emission line" (3.3 Kinematic Properties)
   - parameter: Offset velocity defined relative to V_sys = 1.7 km/s  
  > "we define an offset velocity $V_\mathrm{off}= V_\mathrm{lsr}-V_\mathrm{sys}$" (3 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Locate and characterize CO jet knots**: Knot positions from emission peaks above 10 sigma identified from Gaussian fits; knot widths from Gaussian fits to spatial profiles across the jet, following Lee2017Nat; knot spacing converted to a timescale using the jet velocity  
  > "The positions of the knots were determined from the emission peaks with detections exceeding $>10\sigma$, as identified from the Gaussian fits." (3.2 CO Structure); "fitted these profiles with a Gaussian profile." (3.2 CO Structure)
   - parameter: Detection threshold >10 sigma for CO knots  
  > "The positions of the knots were determined from the emission peaks with detections exceeding $>10\sigma$" (3.2 CO Structure)
   - parameter: Jet velocity ~135 km/s  
  > "assuming a jet velocity of $\sim135\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ ((cite: Zinneckar1998; Lee2017Nat))" (3.2 CO Structure)
   - assumption: Jet velocity ~135 km/s from the literature  
  > "assuming a jet velocity of $\sim135\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ ((cite: Zinneckar1998; Lee2017Nat))" (3.2 CO Structure)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fits to spatial profiles; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "fitted these profiles with a Gaussian profile." (3.2 CO Structure)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Build position-velocity diagrams**: PV diagrams along the jet axis, and PV diagrams perpendicular to the jet axis at fixed heights, with CO in grayscale and SiO and SO as contours  
  > "The PV diagrams were made from $z=\pm300\,\mathrm{au}$ to $z=\pm400\,\mathrm{au}$ every 50 au." (3.3.1 Kinematic Properties Further at Larger than 200 au); "The diagrams were made from $z=\pm 50\,\mathrm{au}$ to $z=\pm200\,\mathrm{au}$ every 30 au." (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - parameter: Perpendicular cuts every 50 au for z = 300-400 au and every 30 au for z = 50-200 au  
  > "The diagrams were made from $z=\pm 50\,\mathrm{au}$ to $z=\pm200\,\mathrm{au}$ every 30 au." (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit ellipses to PV diagrams and derive outflow kinematics**: Spatial profiles extracted from cuts in each PV diagram, 2-sigma limit identified, ellipses fitted to these points (red set excluding high-velocity components; brown set for z<=110 au); R, V_exp, V_rot, V_z and specific angular momentum derived with the Louvet2018 outflow model  
  > "we extracted spatial profiles and identified the 2$\sigma$ limit, correlating these with positions in the PV diagram" (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source); "We then fitted ellipses to the points associated with the 2$\sigma$ limit following the CO and SO data in all PV diagrams for red ellipses" (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - parameter: Ellipse points at the 2 sigma limit  
  > "Our estimations were based on the emission at $2\sigma$." (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - assumption: Expansion, rotation and axial velocities corrected for inclination i ~ 86 deg  
  > "The expansion, rotation, and axial velocities are corrected for the inclination angle with respect to the line of sight $i\sim86^{\circ}$ ((cite: Claussen1998))." (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - assumption: Ellipse method strictly valid only for a narrow emitting ring  
  > "the ellipses method is only valid for a narrow emitting ring" (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - fitting: Ellipse fits to the 2-sigma points; algorithm not stated; uncertainties from propagating the ellipse-fit statistical errors through the Louvet2018 model  
  > "Error bars for these properties are estimated by propagating the statistical uncertainties derived from the ellipse fitting incorporated into the outflow model of (cite: Louvet2018)." (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
   - validation: Argument that contamination from CO and SiO knots does not affect the estimates because knots are brighter and faster than the 2-sigma emission used  
  > "this emission does not affect the estimation of the physical parameters of the outflow because the knots trace the emissions of higher intensity" (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
6. **Derive wind launching radius and magnetic lever arm parameter**: Solve the Anderson et al. (2003) free-wind equation for the launching radius using V_outflow, V_p, V_rot, R and M*; then use Blandford and Payne asymptotic relations between V_p, specific angular momentum, r_0 and lambda  
  > "The following equation (cite: Anderson2003) allows to find the launching point of a free magnetocentrifugal MHD wind under the conditions of application mentioned above" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios); "The asymptotic values of the poloidal velocity and the specific angular momentum at different heights $z$ are ((cite: Blandford1982))" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)
   - parameter: M* = 0.25 +/- 0.05 Msun  
  > "where the mass of the protostar is $M_*=0.25\pm0.05$ $\mathrm{M_\odot}$ ((cite: Codella2014; Lee2014))" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)
   - assumption: Free, dynamically cold wind (Anderson2003 conditions); justified by the disk wind outside the jet-perturbed cone being unperturbed (Tabone2018)  
  > "the wind is free, meaning that the wind is not interacting with other components of the protostellar system; (2) dynamically cold, such that the entropy effect is negligible" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios); "it is possible to construct a free-wind model that fits the known observations" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)
   - fitting: Graphical comparison of observed j and V_p with solutions for r_0 and lambda; no sampler or priors stated  
  > "for the various solutions of the launching point $r_0$ and magnetic lever arm parameter $\lambda$" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)
   - validation: Compared the derived launching radii and lambda with earlier SO results (Tabone2017, Tabone2020, Lee2021)  
  > "These values are consistent with those previously reported for SO observations." (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)

## Qualitative findings

- CO traces four regions: an outer outflow shell, a rotating wind between the SiO and CO shells, a shocked wide-angle wind inside the SiO shell, and the jet  
  > "the CO line traces four distinct regions: (1) an outer outflow shell, (2) a rotating wind region between the SiO and CO shells" (abstract)
- CO jet knots lie at |100| au <= z <= |700| au and their widths increase with distance from the central source  
  > "the width of the knots increases with the distance away from the central source" (5 Conclusions)
- SO is mixed with the CO emission within the thick rotating wind region  
  > "the SO is found to be mixed with the CO emission within the thick and extended rotating wind region" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier CO observations of HH 212 had only low angular resolution; high-resolution CO is needed near the central source  
  > "reported the emission of the CO molecular line of the HH 212 source at large scales with low angular resolution" (Introduction); "In this paper, we present high-angular resolution observations of the CO molecular line emission." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Quantities were measured at the 2-sigma emission limit to avoid contamination from the CO shocked wind inside the SiO shell and the SO shell  
  > "We choose to measure the physical quantities at 2$\sigma$ to avoid contamination from the CO shocked wind inside the SiO shell" (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)
- Alternative: Envelope entrainment of the outer shell by outflowing gas from the protostar-disk system, rather than an extended disk wind  
  > "Another possible explanation for the origin of the outflow shell is that it results from entrained material from the envelope by the outflowing gas from the protostar-disk system." (4.2 Origin of the CO Emission and the Molecular Outflow)
- Alternative: CO shocked wind inside the SiO shell as material ejected sideways from CO and SiO knots, instead of shocked wide-angle X-wind  
  > "Another explanation of the origin of the CO shocked wind inside of the SiO shell is material ejected sideways from the CO and SiO knots." (4.1 Onion-like Spatial and Kinematic Structures)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Rotation velocity and specific angular momentum may be overestimated because the ellipse method assumes a narrow emitting ring while the wind is radially extended  
  > "Both sets of ellipses are subject to possible overestimates of rotation velocity $V_\mathrm{rot}$, and therefore the specific angular momentum $j_\mathrm{obs}$" (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
- Limitation (authors): Poloidal and vertical velocities are highly uncertain because of the very high inclination of the jet axis to the line of sight  
  > "The poloidal and the vertical velocities present high uncertainties associated with a very high inclination angle of the jet axis to the line of sight." (3.3.2 Kinematic Properties Close to Central Source)
- Limitation (authors): The free-wind assumption of the launching-radius model does not strictly hold in HH 212  
  > "Thus, the wind is not free." (4.2.1 Free Ballistic Disk Wind Scenarios)
- Limitation (authors): CO emission at some velocities is absorbed by the parent cloud and a foreground cloud  
  > "The lack of CO emission around $V_\mathrm{off}=0$ is because the outflow emission at this velocity is absorbed by the parent cloud." (3.3 Kinematic Properties)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Launching radii and lambda rest on ellipse fits to 2-sigma contours with no stated fitting algorithm, and the red and brown ellipse sets differ by a factor of ~5-10 in launching radius, so the result depends on an arbitrary choice of velocity range
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainties on launching radius and lambda are not given; only ranges are quoted
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ellipse parameter table has incomplete headers and mixes columns, so the numbers cannot be cross-checked from the text
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Three datasets of different cycles and baselines enter the CO image but the combination method and uv-range matching are not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The unified-model versus disk-wind discrimination is argued qualitatively, not tested against simulated PV diagrams
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-dish or short-spacing recovery for extended CO (outer shell, cloud absorption) is not discussed
