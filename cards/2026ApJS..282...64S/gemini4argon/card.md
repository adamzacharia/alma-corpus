## Qualitative findings

- Nine hot cores and one hot-core candidate are identified among 36 compact continuum sources toward 30 LMC massive YSOs, including the newly discovered COM-rich hot core Lm10 and candidate Lm07.  
  > "Continuum imaging reveals 36 compact sources in total, among which line analyses identify 9 hot cores and 1 hot-core candidate, including two newly identified sources." (Abstract)
- CH3OCH3 is detected in Lm10, representing the first detection of a complex organic molecule larger than CH3OH in a hot core outside the LMC bar region.  
  > "This represents the first detection of COMs larger than CH$_3$OH in a hot core located outside the LMC bar region." (Lm10)
- Strong high-excitation SO (8_8-7_7) emission (>20 K km s^-1) clearly distinguishes LMC hot cores from non-hot-core sources and serves as a reliable hot core tracer.  
  > "All sources with SO integrated intensities greater than approximately 20 K km s$^{-1}$ are hot cores accompanied by high-excitation SO$_2$ and/or CH$_3$OH lines, whereas no such high-excitation lines are detected in sources below this SO threshold." (Molecular tracer of LMC hot cores)
- CH3OH abundances in LMC hot cores vary by more than two orders of magnitude and do not correlate with rotational temperature, whereas SO2 is detected in all hot cores and strongly correlates with rotational temperature.  
  > "Chemical analysis reveals a spread of more than two orders of magnitude in abundances, with some sources deficient in COMs. In contrast, is detected in all hot cores, and its abundance shows a good correlation with rotational temperature." (Abstract)
- COM-poor hot cores (lacking CH3OH detection) are all located outside the LMC bar region in environments with active star formation or high protostellar luminosity, suggesting a combination of low metallicity, strong local radiation fields, and high luminosity triggers COM-poor chemistry.  
  > "The hot cores without detections are all located outside the LMC bar region and are characterized by either high luminosity or active star formation in their surroundings. A combination of locally low metallicity, active star formation in the vicinity, and high protostellar luminosity may jointly trigger the COM-poor hot core chemistry observed in the LMC." (Abstract)
