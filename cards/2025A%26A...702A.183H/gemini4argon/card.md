## Analysis recipe

1. **Source identification and continuum photometry on Band 7 maps**: Custom-made Python script computing MAD and RMS (within +/-5*MAD), identifying peaks >5 sigma, extracting flux in a box aperture of width 2x beam major axis, computing light-weighted centroid (>3 sigma), fitting with CASA imfit in an elliptical aperture of 2x beam size, and performing forced photometry at phase centre for non-detections  
  > "We automated the source extraction procedure with a custom-made python script, that includes the identification of secondary counterparts, submm flux measurement and fitting on the ALMA Band 7 continuum maps." (Identification of submm sources); "Use of the CASA imfit task to fit the emission within a elliptical aperture twice the beam size (but with the same major to minor axis ratio and position angle), centred on the light-weighted centroid;" (Identification of submm sources); "For the quasars with no peak-fluxes above 5$\sigma$, photometry extraction at the phase centre was forced as described in point [\[ext:boxphot\]](#ext:boxphot), with an attempt to run imfit only when the peak-flux was above 3$\sigma$." (Identification of submm sources)
   - software: Python, CASA (imfit)
   - parameter: Detection threshold: 5 sigma peak flux; forced photometry threshold: 3 sigma peak flux  
  > "Identification of peak-fluxes above 5$\sigma$;" (Identification of submm sources); "For the quasars with no peak-fluxes above 5$\sigma$, photometry extraction at the phase centre was forced as described in point [\[ext:boxphot\]](#ext:boxphot), with an attempt to run imfit only when the peak-flux was above 3$\sigma$." (Identification of submm sources)
   - parameter: Box aperture width = 2x beam major axis; elliptical imfit aperture = 2x beam size  
  > "Extraction of the flux density within a box-shaped aperture centred on the peak pixel and with a width twice the beam major axis;" (Identification of submm sources); "Use of the CASA imfit task to fit the emission within a elliptical aperture twice the beam size (but with the same major to minor axis ratio and position angle), centred on the light-weighted centroid;" (Identification of submm sources)

3. ** Targeted and blind search for serendipitous emission lines (CO, H2O, HCN)**: Extract 1D spectra for all 209 continuum detections within an aperture matching the synthesised beam size and PA, visually inspect spectra and ALMA pipeline findcont plots for peaks >3 sigma per channel near expected transitions, fit detected lines with a Gaussian, and perform an independent blind search on continuum-subtracted smoothed data cubes using SoFiA  
  > "ALMA spectra were extracted for all 209 detections and were visually inspected. The extraction was performed within an aperture with the size and position angle of the synthesised beam." (Serendipitous line detections); "The observed frequency ($\nu_{obs}$), the velocity offset of the CO line with respect to $z_{\rm SDSS}$ ($\Delta_{vel}$), the significance of the detection peak ($\sigma_{peak}$), the FWHM of the Gaussian fit (FWHM$_{\rm CO}$) and the integrated line flux from the Gaussian fit (S$_{{\rm CO} fit}$) are reported." (Serendipitous line detections); "We also carried out an independent, blind search using the Source Finding Application (SoFiA; (cite: serra15))." (Serendipitous line detections)
   - software: SoFiA, ALMA pipeline
   - parameter: Line reliability threshold: >3 sigma per channel in binned spectrum within SDSS redshift uncertainty of 0.002  
  > "For the redshifts of the objects in the sample, with a redshift uncertainty of 0.002 (typical for SDSS quasars, as derived from Principal Component Analysis methods; (cite: lyke20)), and considering the Band 7 continuum spectral setup (which consists of four spectral windows, each 1.875 GHz wide, centred at 336.5 GHz, 338.5 GHz, 348.5 GHz, and 350.5 GHz), a total of 31 CO transitions fall within the spectral range covered by the ALMA observations." (Serendipitous line detections); "A detection was considered reliable if the peak of the binned spectrum exceeded the 3$\sigma$ per channel level and was supported by the existence of a nearby transition within a velocity shift compatible with the error on the redshift (see also the caption of Fig. 5)." (Serendipitous line detections)
   - parameter: SoFiA smoothing kernels: 3, 6, 9 pixels spatially (~0.14'' pixel scale) and 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 channels in velocity (~28 km/s channel width)

   - assumption: Typical quasar CO spectral line energy distribution (COSLED) from van der Werf et al. (2010) and Carilli & Walter (2013) to evaluate high-J sensitivity  
  > "Assuming a typical CO spectral line energy distribution (COSLED) for quasars, as reported by e.g. (cite: vanderwerf10) and (cite: carilli13), we estimate that the sensitivity of our ALMA observations is sufficient to detect all CO transitions with J$_{upp}$ between 6 and 10 at a significance level above 6$\sigma$, with the exception of one source that lacks a submm continuum detection." ## Qualitative findings

- In ~60% of the sample, the 870 um submm emission originates from a single counterpart centred on the optical coordinates of the quasar, implying secular or smooth-accretion processes must drive synchronous SMBH growth and star formation in isolated systems.  
  > "We find that in approximately 60% of all cases, the submm emission originates from a single counterpart within the SPIRE beam, centred on the optical coordinates of the quasar." (abstract); "The multiplicities observed in a fraction of the sample indicate that, while mergers are known to enhance gas inflow efficiency, there must be viable alternatives for driving synchronous SMBH growth and intense star formation in isolated systems." (abstract)
- Secondary submm counterparts within 6'' exceed blank-field expectations by a factor of ~10, strongly indicating physical associations rather than chance projections, and setting an upper limit of ~30% on merger-triggered activity.  
  > "This is a factor of ten lower than the total of 67 secondary counterparts detected at 870 ${\mu}$m. The excess is particularly striking up to the distance of 6$''$, beyond which counts around the quasars in our sample are compatible (within the errors) with the counts from the deep fields." (Multiplicities and associations); "Given the above discussion, we can interpret the findings of our study as an upper limit of $\sim$30% to the fraction of FIR- and optically-bright quasars in which a merger event could trigger concomitant accretion onto the central SMBH and intense star formation in their hosts, as well as star formation in the host of the merger partner." (Multiplicities and associations)
- The multiplicity rate increases by a factor of ~2.5 from z = 1 to z = 2.5, shows no dependence on Eddington ratio or 250 um flux, and does not differ between BAL and non-BAL quasars.  
  > "The rate of multiplicity increases with redshift, rising by a factor of $\sim$2.5 between redshifts 1 and 2.5. The incidence of multiplicities is consistent among broad absorption line (BAL) quasars and non-BAL quasars." (abstract); "Last but not least, no dependence of the multiplicity is found with ${\rm \lambda}_{Edd}$." (Multiplicities as a function of FIR properties, redshift and “balnicity”)
- Intermediate-J CO(6-5) and CO(7-6) transitions are detected in 5 out of 8 expected cases, whereas higher-J CO transitions (J_upp >= 8) are not detected despite sufficient sensitivity up to J_upp = 10, indicating the molecular gas is not strongly excited by the AGN or XDRs.  
  > "Additionally, we report the serendipitous detection of two CO(6-5) and three CO(7-6) transitions in five quasars at redshifts between 1 and 1.4, out of the eight such transitions expected based on the spectral setup and the redshifts of the objects in the sample. Higher transitions, although expected in a fraction of the sample, are not detected, indicating that the quasars are not exciting sufficiently the gas in their hosts." (abstract); "The lack of higher-J line detections suggests that XDRs do not significantly impact the molecular gas in the quasar hosts." (CO transitions in the low resolution spectra)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (0.8'') ALMA 12-m Band 7 observations of 152 quasars overcome the 18'' resolution limit of Herschel/SPIRE and the small sample size (28) and coarse resolution (~4'') of the previous ACA pilot study.  
  > "Furthermore, given SPIRE’s spatial resolution of 18$''$ at 250 ${\mu}$m (cite: griffin10), it is likely that in some cases multiple sources contribute to the observed FIR emission." (Introduction); "Given the small sample size, the coarse resolution of the ACA ($\sim$4$''$) and the relatively narrow redshift range, the results, while suggestive, were neither conclusive nor fully representative." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Self-calibration was omitted because WVR corrections kept phase-rms to 5-11 deg (decorrelation <=2%, well within the 10% absolute flux uncertainty) and most targets are too faint; both visual inspection and a blind SoFiA search with inverted-cube testing were used to ensure reliable line identification.  
  > "The expected signal decorrelation in such conditions is thus minimal ($\lesssim 2\,\%$), well within ALMA’s absolute flux uncertainty of 10% in Band 7. As a result, no self-calibration was pursued in the sample, especially because it would only be possible in a small fraction of the sample, given the fluxes of the sources (see Sect. 4.1)." (ALMA data); "We also carried out an independent, blind search using the Source Finding Application (SoFiA; (cite: serra15))." (Serendipitous line detections)
- Alternative: Self-calibration was considered and rejected because atmospheric phase decorrelation was <=2% after WVR correction and most sources lacked sufficient flux.  
  > "The expected signal decorrelation in such conditions is thus minimal ($\lesssim 2\,\%$), well within ALMA’s absolute flux uncertainty of 10% in Band 7. As a result, no self-calibration was pursued in the sample, especially because it would only be possible in a small fraction of the sample, given the fluxes of the sources (see Sect. 4.1)." (ALMA data)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Lacking spectroscopic redshifts and relative velocities for the secondary submm counterparts, it is impossible to confirm individual pairs as physical associations or true mergers.  
  > "Furthermore, lacking spectroscopic information of the secondary counterparts, it is not possible to assess whether these are physical associations at the redshift of the quasar or chance associations." (Discussion); "Since for the multiple objects in our sample we know neither the redshift of the secondary sources nor the relative velocities, we cannot assess the probability of these systems being real mergers." (Multiplicities and associations)
- Limitation (authors): SDSS optical imaging has insufficient spatial resolution and sensitivity to detect morphological signs of recent merger activity such as tidal tails, and post-coalescence mergers or line-of-sight companions cannot be resolved by the 0.8'' ALMA data.  
  > "At the limited SDSS resolution, none of the quasars in the sample bear signs of recent merger activity (i.e. they are point-like and have no features like tidal tails) but the spacial resolution and sensitivity of SDSS are too low for a proper assessment." (Discussion); "Excluding projection effects (i.e. the companion lying along the line of sight and, hence, undetectable) and a post-coalescence phase where the merger would no longer be detectable, and given the absence of disturbed morphologies or tidal features (within the limitations of the data in hand), these numbers highlight that, while mergers enhance gas inflow efficiency, there must be other viable alternatives for driving synchronous SMBH growth and extreme star formation." (Multiplicities and associations)
- Limitation (authors): FIR selection inherently biases the sample towards gas- and dust-rich systems that are more likely to be involved in mergers compared to optically selected quasars.  
  > "The FIR selection of the sample inherently biases it towards systems with more gas and dust, which are also more likely to be involved in mergers." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity for CO transitions with J_upp >= 11 is <= 5 sigma, preventing firm conclusions on those very high-J lines, and the small number of expected mid-J CO transitions limits statistical comparisons across redshift.  
  > "For transitions with J$_{upp} \geq 11$, our sensitivity would allow detections only at or below the 5$\sigma$ level, and we therefore refrain from drawing firm conclusions for these higher transitions." (CO transitions in the low resolution spectra); "The small number of detections involved in our work as well as the above works makes firm conclusions difficult." (CO transitions in the low resolution spectra)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Primary beam attenuation at 343.5 GHz (FWHM ~18 arcsec) is significant for secondary counterparts located up to ~10 arcsec from the phase centre; the paper does not state whether source detection (5 sigma threshold) was run on flat-noise (uncorrected) maps and flux measurements (and 1.1 mm number count comparisons) on primary-beam-corrected maps.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): PCA-derived optical redshifts from broad UV emission lines in SDSS quasars can exhibit systematic velocity offsets of hundreds to >1000 km/s relative to systemic host galaxy redshifts, which could shift expected CO lines outside the spectral windows or beyond the assumed dz = 0.002 window.
