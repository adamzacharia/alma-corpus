## Qualitative findings

- The central N2D+ filament in LBS 23 exhibits a clear east-west velocity gradient perpendicular to its major axis with no detectable velocity gradient along its major axis, indicating rotation in a filament lying nearly in the plane of the sky.  
  > "The velocity in the central filament gradient is purely along its minor axis with no detectable gradient along the length (major axis) of the filament." (3. RESULTS); "We interpret this gradient to be caused by rotation in the filament (see §.4.6 for a discussion on this), and use the velocity profile to estimate the rotation velocity." (4.1. Rotation and its effect on filament dynamics)
- The filament specific angular momentum profile follows a power law down to a few hundred AU without flattening, supporting simultaneous multi-scale formation of filaments and cores via turbulence rather than two-step gravitational fragmentation.  
  > "In contrast to this scenario, our observed filament shows a profile that is consistent with specific angular momentum profile set by turbulence all the way down to a few hundred au" (4.2. Specific angular momentum profile of filament); "Our observations are thus more consistent with the scenario in which filament and cores develop simultaneously due to the multi-scale growth by nonlinear perturbation generated by turbulence" (4.2. Specific angular momentum profile of filament)
- Turbulence in the central filament is transonic (M_s = 1.0), showing that star formation can proceed before turbulence decays to subsonic levels.  
  > "The existence of young protostars in a transonic filament, as it is the case in the central filament of LBS23, suggests that star formation can occur before the turbulence fully decay from supersonic to subsonic." (4.3. Transonic turbulence in the filament)
- The plane-of-sky magnetic field is roughly perpendicular to the filament's major/rotation axis, implying magnetic braking is negligible, and the filament is magnetically supercritical.  
  > "Therefore the gravitational energy is about two times larger than the magnetic field energy (see Table 1), indicating the filament is magnetic super-critical" (4.5. Magnetic fields in the LBS 23 filament); "magnetic braking should be negligible in LBS 23 as the magnetic field orientation is approximately perpendicular to the rotation axis of the filament." (4.5. Magnetic fields in the LBS 23 filament)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: LBS 23 is a very young star-forming filament (~50% Class 0 protostars), N2D+(3-2) traces cold high-density gas showing the complete narrow filament structure, and the central region has simple structure and low stellar feedback.  
  > "The large fraction of Class 0 protostars ( _∼_ 50 %) compared to the more evolved Class I, II and III sources in this region (see Megeath et al. 2012; Furlan et al. 2016) suggest that LBS23 is a very young filament undergoing its first major phase of star formation." (1. INTRODUCTION); "In this paper we concentrate on the N 2 D [+] (3-2) line (with a rest frequency of 231.32 GHz), the highest-density tracer in our spectral set-up as it clearly shows the most complete structure of the high-density narrow filament in the LBS23 region." (2. OBSERVATIONS); "In this paper, we will focus on the central region because of its comparative simplicity, significantly lower stellar feedback activity, and the relative young age of the filament and the sources in it." (3. RESULTS)
- Why this method: Calibrating ALMA N2D+ against Herschel-Planck 850 um dust optical depth avoids large uncertainties from variable N2D+/H2 abundance ratios; manual polynomial baseline subtraction on TP data was required due to a deep telluric absorption feature near N2D+.  
  > "The TP array data exhibit a deep telluric absorption feature near the N 2 D [+] line, which hampers the ability of the CASA pipeline to do a proper baseline subtraction." (2. OBSERVATIONS); "Therefore, mass estimates from measurements of the N 2 D [+] column density, assuming a constant N 2 D [+] to H 2 abundance ratio are highly uncertain." (3. RESULTS); "Here, instead, we use the 850 _µ_ m Hershal-Plank dust emission map (from Lombardi et al. 2014) in concert with our ALMA data to estimate the H 2 column density and N 2 D [+] abundance of the central filament (see Appendix A)." (3. RESULTS)
- Alternative: Estimating filament H2 mass directly from N2D+ column density assuming a constant N2D+/H2 abundance ratio (rejected due to orders-of-magnitude abundance variations).  
  > "However, vast variations in the deuterium to hydrogen abundance ratio in clouds and cores can yield N 2 D [+] abundance ratios that range a few orders of magnitude (Caselli et al. 2002; Linsky et al. 2006; Lique et al. 2015)." (3. RESULTS); "Therefore, mass estimates from measurements of the N 2 D [+] column density, assuming a constant N 2 D [+] to H 2 abundance ratio are highly uncertain." (3. RESULTS)
- Alternative: Using the default CASA pipeline baseline subtraction for the Total Power N2D+ data (rejected because of a deep telluric absorption feature near N2D+).  
  > "The TP array data exhibit a deep telluric absorption feature near the N 2 D [+] line, which hampers the ability of the CASA pipeline to do a proper baseline subtraction." (2. OBSERVATIONS)
- Alternative: Interpreting the minor-axis velocity gradient as multiple velocity components along the line of sight, converging flows / turbulent shock compression, or gravitational infall (all tested and rejected).  
  > "We thus can safely rule out that multiple velocity components is the origin of the velocity gradient seen in our filament." (4.6. Other interpretations of velocity gradients in filaments and the case for rotation); "We, therefore can rule out the scenario where the observed velocity gradient in LBS 23 is due to the convergence of large-scale flows or sheet-like structures created by turbulence compression." (4.6. Other interpretations of velocity gradients in filaments and the case for rotation); "It is therefore unlikely that the detected velocity gradient in the central filament is mainly due to gravitational infall." (4.6. Other interpretations of velocity gradients in filaments and the case for rotation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The filament mass estimate has an approximate 50% uncertainty from the empirical N(H2)-W(N2D+) fit and choice of S/N threshold.  
  > "We estimate an approximate 50% uncertainty in our calculation of the filament mass, which results from the error in the fit to the empirical relation between the N 2 D [+] integrated intensity and the H 2 column density (see Appendix A)" (3. RESULTS)
- Limitation (authors): The angular resolution of the observations is insufficient to directly measure the rotation axes of the protostellar envelopes, requiring outflow axes to be used as a proxy.  
  > "The resolution of our observations is not enough to obtain a reliable rotational axis from the envelope emission." (4.2.   > "Moreover, the Chandrasekhar-Fermi method is not optimal for dense structures where gravitational forces dominate over MHD turbulence." (4.5.
