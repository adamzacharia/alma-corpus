## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate the ALMA data**: Run the ALMA scriptforPI, flag +-20 km/s around 12CO, build a pseudo-continuum averaged to 125 MHz and 6 s, self-calibrate on the continuum (4 phase then amplitude), apply tables to the 12CO window  
  > "Using `CASA 5.6.2` we flagged the channels located at $\pm 20\,$km s$^{-1}$ from the line center to obtain the dust continuum emission." (2.3 ALMA observations); "A pseudo-continuum measurement set is created by combining the remaining channels with the continuum spectral windows." (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, CLEAN
   - parameter: phase solution intervals inf, 40s, 18s, 6s; amplitude 6s; spws and scans combined  
  > "We applied four phase calibrations, using solution intervals of length "inf", "40s", "18s", and "6s", while the amplitude calibration was done with a solution interval of "6s"" (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - parameter: imaging for self-calibration with Briggs robust 0.5  
  > "with a Briggs robust parameter of 0.5" (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image continuum and 12CO with JvM correction**: Continuum imaged with robust -0.5; 12CO with robust 0.0 and no taper; both cleaned to 4 sigma then JvM-corrected  
  > "After self-calibration, the continuum emission was imaged with a robust parameter of -0.5 to maximize the balance between angular resolution and sensitivity." (2.3 ALMA observations); "The gas emission was imaged with a robust parameter of 0.0" (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, CLEAN
   - parameter: JvM epsilon = 0.909 (continuum) and 0.694 (12CO)  
  > "We found $\epsilon$ values of $0.909$ for the dust continuum image and $0.694$ for the $^{12}$CO" (2.3 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Check optical depth and derive dust mass**: Compute tau at the continuum peak from I = B(Td)(1 - exp(-tau)); if optically thin, M_dust = d^2 F_nu / (kappa_nu B_nu(T))  
  > "To check if the emission is optically thin, we calculated the optical depth of the peak of the continuum by assuming (cite: dullemond2018):" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission); "If the emission is optically thin, the dust disk mass can be calculated as" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission)
   - parameter: kappa_nu = 2.3 cm2/g x (nu/230 GHz)^0.4  
  > "Taking for the mass absorption coefficient $\kappa_\nu=2.3\,$cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}\times(\nu/230\,\rm{GHz})^{0.4}$ (cite: beckwith1990; andrews2013)" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission)
   - assumption: dust temperature of 20 K for the optical depth  
  > "Assuming a dust temperature of 20 K, the optical depth at the peak is $\tau_{\rm{peak}}=0.27$" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission)
   - assumption: optically thin emission; distance to the source d  
  > "where $d$ is the distance to the source, $F_\nu$ is the total flux at 1.3 mm" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: tau compared with DSHARP values; paper notes tau may be underestimated  
  > "similar to the values found in the DSHARP sample (cite: huang2018; dullemond2018)" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission)
4. **Fit the continuum visibilities with a parametric model**: Four-component brightness model (central Gaussian, two ring Gaussians, azimuthal Gaussian asymmetry) Fourier transformed with galario and sampled with zeus MCMC  
  > "The dust continuum emission morphology was analyzed with a parametric brightness modeling using the packages `galario` (cite: galario)" (4.4 Disk structure); "We considered four components to describe the PDS 111 emission" (4.4 Disk structure)
   - software: galario, zeus
   - parameter: model images with 4 mas pixels and 2048 pixel image size  
  > "The model images have a pixel size of 4 mas and an image size of 2048 pixels." (4.4 Disk structure)
   - parameter: n_walk = 136 (eight times the number of parameters), 5000 burn-in steps and 6000 sampling steps per walker  
  > "We ran the MCMC with eight times the number of parameters as the number of walkers ($n_{\text{walk}}=136$). For each walker, the burn stage was 5000, and 6000 for the parameter space sampling." (4.4 Disk structure)
   - fitting: zeus MCMC with flat priors and physical bounds; free parameters include disk center, inclination and PA  
  > "Every parameter has a flat prior and boundaries representative of physical limitations, such as only allowing positive distances and brightness." (4.4 Disk structure); "We also include as free parameters the central position of the disk ($x_0$, $y_0$) and the geometry, given by inclination and $\rm PA$." (4.4 Disk structure)
   - validation: model and residual images compared with the data after convolving with the observed beam  
  > "The residual map demonstrates that our model recovers most of the morphology of the dust continuum emission." (4.4 Disk structure)
5. **Measure radial extent of dust, gas and scattered light**: Deproject and azimuthally average the ALMA images; take the radius enclosing 90% of the emission; SPHERE shown as radial cuts along the major axis  
  > "For the ALMA profiles, the images have been deprojected and then azimuthally averaged" (4.5 Radial extent of the disk); "The vertical lines show the radius that encloses 90% of the emission in each of the maps." (4.5 Radial extent of the disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: direct R90 (95 au) compared with visibility-fit R90 (55 au); difference attributed to the beam and inclination  
  > "This is a natural result of the large ALMA beam and the high inclination of the source." (4.5 Radial extent of the disk)
6. **Derive stellar mass from 12CO Keplerian rotation**: Fit the Keplerian rotation pattern of 12CO with eddy using a simple disk model  
  > "which gives 1.29$^{+0.82}_{-0.18}$ M$_{\odot}$ when assuming a simple disk model and fixing the inclination to be that of the continuum." (3.3.2 12CO emission)
   - software: eddy
   - parameter: inclination fixed to the continuum value  
  > "fixing the inclination to be that of the continuum" (3.3.2 12CO emission)
   - assumption: simple disk model  
  > "when assuming a simple disk model and fixing the inclination to be that of the continuum" (3.3.2 12CO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: consistent with the evolutionary mass from the HRD (stated as similar)  
  > "The mass is similar to the best fit mass value obtained in Sec. [3.3](#sec:ALMA)." (4.1 Age)
7. **Determine stellar parameters and age**: Fit Kurucz models to the 402-430 nm X-shooter spectrum for Teff, log g, v sin i; fit SED for luminosity and radius; place the star on Siess et al. tracks in the HRD  
  > "through the fitting of Kurucz models (cite: kurucz1993) in the spectral range of 402-430 nm" (3.1 Photospheric spectrum PDS 111); "Using the luminosity and effective temperature, we placed the star in the Hertzsprung Russell Diagram (HRD) on evolutionary tracks from (cite: siess2000)" (4.1 Age)
   - software: Molecfit, ESO X-shooter pipeline
   - parameter: Kurucz grid: Teff 5500-6500 K step 100 K, log g 4.0-4.5 step 0.1, v sin i 20-50 km/s step 1  
  > "with varying grid parameters based on the spectral classification" (3.1 Photospheric spectrum PDS 111)
   - parameter: R_V fixed to 3.1 in the SED fit; distance and Teff fixed  
  > "Throughout the SED fitting procedure, the total-to-selective visual extinction $R_{\rm V}$ is fixed to 3.1 (cite: Cardelli1989)." (4.1 Age)
   - assumption: Gaia DR3 distance (RUWE 2.6 discussed and judged not to invalidate it)  
  > "Therefore, we do not find any obvious indications that the *Gaia* measurements are inaccurate." (4.1 Age)
   - fitting: chi2 minimisation over the model grid  
  > "see Appendix [8](#p2:app:chi2) for the $\chi^2$ minimization results" (3.1 Photospheric spectrum PDS 111)
   - validation: sensitivity of age to R_V tested (R_V = 4.1 gives 11.3 Myr); radius cross-checked from v sin i, TESS period and inclination (1.53 Rsun)  
  > "we calculated a radius of 1.53 R$_{\odot}$, which is within errors similar to the derived radius from the SED fit" (3.1 Photospheric spectrum PDS 111); "the corresponding age in the HRD would be 11.3 Myr for the same temperature" (4.1 Age)
8. **Reduce SPHERE data and trace the disk surface**: IRDAP polarimetric differential imaging and ADI (PCA), then trace individual rings by eye as ellipses on polarized, high-pass filtered and AIDA-deconvolved images to get aspect ratios  
  > "We reduced the data using the IRDIS Data reduction for Accurate Polarimetry" (2.2 SPHERE/IRDIS near-infrared imaging); "we employed high pass filtering as well as deconvolution techniques using the Adaptive Image Deconvolution Algorithm (AIDA) code by (cite: Hom2007)" (4.2 Disk surface)
   - software: IRDAP, AIDA
   - parameter: ADI with 4 principal components; optimization annuli 10-30, 30-100, 100-512 pixels  
  > "For this image we specifically used 4 principal components and optimization annuli between 10 and 30 pixels (0.12" to 0.37")" (2.2 SPHERE/IRDIS near-infrared imaging)
   - parameter: ring inclination and PA fixed to the ALMA values  
  > "the inclination of the individual rings as well as their PA were fixed to the values extracted from the ALMA data" (4.2 Disk surface)
   - fitting: ellipses traced by eye (no fit)  
  > "we traced the individual rings by eye as was done by (cite: Avenhaus2018) for the case of IM Lup" (4.2 Disk surface)
   - validation: tracing done on original, high-pass filtered and deconvolved images to avoid structure mismatch  
  > "Tracing was done for all structures in the original polarized light images, in the high-pass filtered images and in the de-convolved images to ensure that there was no mismatch in structures." (4.2 Disk surface)
9. **Measure accretion rate and spectral variability**: EW of lines after Kurucz model subtraction converted to line luminosity and accretion rate with Alcala et al. relations; temporal variance spectrum for variability  
  > "The equivalent width (EW) is determined after subtracting the best fit Kurucz model from the observations." (4.6 Inner gaseous disk); "The temporal variance method (cite: Fullerton1996; dejong2001) is employed to study spectroscopic variability." (4.6 Inner gaseous disk)
   - parameter: 95% level used for a significant TVS detection  
  > "A 95% level is used for a significant detection." (4.6 Inner gaseous disk)
   - assumption: inner radius of 5 R_star for the accretion rate  
  > "assuming a typical inner radius of 5 R$_{\rm \star}$" (4.6 Inner gaseous disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- SPHERE shows a strongly flaring disk with an asymmetric substructure  
  > "The SPHERE observations show a strongly flaring disk with an asymmetric substructure." (abstract)
- 12CO shows no cavity and extends at least three times further than the dust  
  > "The $^{12}$CO observations do not show a cavity and the $^{12}$CO radial extension is at least three times larger than that of the dust emission." (abstract)
- Scattered-light brightness asymmetry flips around 0.3 arcsec (47 au) along the major axis  
  > "we find a flip in the asymmetric brightness distribution (the white line between the red and blue areas) around 0.3$''$ (47 au) along the major axis." (4.3 Disk asymmetry)
- PDS 111 is a weak-line T Tauri star with no forbidden emission lines  
  > "We do not detect any forbidden emission lines." (4.6 Inner gaseous disk); "which classifies PDS 111 as a weak-line T Tauri star" (4.6 Inner gaseous disk)
- No companion detected in SPHERE data down to ~0.25 Msun to the coronagraphic edge at 16 au  
  > "Specifically, we can rule out objects down to $\sim$0.25 M$_\odot$ and down to the coronagraphic edge at 0.1”, that is 16 au." (4.7 Binarity)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine SPHERE, ALMA and spectroscopy to build a detailed multi-wavelength picture of the star, inner disk and disk  
  > "In this paper, we combine SPHERE and ALMA observations with medium-resolution spectroscopy of the star and the hot inner disk in order to infer a detailed picture of the system PDS 111." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Continuum robust -0.5 chosen to balance resolution and sensitivity; 12CO robust 0.0 and no taper for best angular resolution  
  > "After self-calibration, the continuum emission was imaged with a robust parameter of -0.5 to maximize the balance between angular resolution and sensitivity." (2.3 ALMA observations); "and no visibility tapering to focus on best possible angular resolution" (2.3 ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Direct-image 90% dust radius (95 au) vs visibility-fit radius (55 au): the latter judged more representative  
  > "The $\sim$55 au estimate is probably more representative of the actual size, therefore of the distribution of mm-grains that dominate the emission at ALMA wavelengths." (4.5 Radial extent of the disk)
- Alternative: Manara et al. simultaneous age/extinction/accretion method not applicable because models only reach G4  
  > "However, their method cannot be applied to PDS 111 since their stellar models go up to spectral type G4." (4.1 Age)
- Alternative: Disk wind origin of the flared surface layer excluded  
  > "We exclude a disk wind from causing the flared surface layer, as seen in RY Tau (cite: valegard2022)." (5.1 A very flared, but old disk)
- How they decided it was enough: Both images cleaned to the 4 sigma noise level before JvM correction  
  > "Both images were cleaned until the $4\sigma$ noise level to apply the JvM correction (cite: jorsater1995)" (2.3 ALMA observations)
- Limitation (authors): Optical depth at the continuum peak may be underestimated (dust scattering neglected, beam size)  
  > "This value of the optical depth may be underestimated because dust scattering could be neglected" (3.3.1 Dust continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainty on the 12CO-derived stellar mass; higher resolution needed  
  > "The large uncertainty in the stellar mass may come from the unknown vertical structure and disk orientation." (3.3.2 12CO emission); "Higher angular resolution is needed to better resolve the surface height with ALMA." (3.3.2 12CO emission)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA beam and high inclination make the direct 90% radius differ from the visibility-fit radius  
  > "This is a natural result of the large ALMA beam and the high inclination of the source." (4.5 Radial extent of the disk)
- Limitation (authors): Accretion rate only indirectly constrained without UV photometry  
  > "Due to the lack of photometric observations in the UV, the mass-accretion rate could be constrained only indirectly." (4.6 Inner gaseous disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass depends on an unstated temperature and on the adopted flux and kappa; no uncertainty is given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA observation is very short (about 1.3 min per execution) and no image rms or sensitivity is quoted
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The eddy stellar mass has a very asymmetric, large error and a fixed inclination from the continuum
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Age rests on a single Gaia distance with RUWE 2.6 and on fixed R_V = 3.1; Teff step sizes are coarse
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ring ellipses were traced by eye, so aspect ratios have no formal uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Binary and planet explanations for the cavity are speculative and not tested with models
