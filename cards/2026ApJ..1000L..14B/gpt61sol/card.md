## Analysis recipe

1. **Select cubes and analysis regions**: Choose continuum-subtracted cubes by signal-to-noise ratio; exclude the central beam and contaminated regions.  
  > "Similar to (cite: Stadler2025), we consider cubes with different angular resolutions depending on the signal-to-noise ratio." (Data); "We exclude data within the central beam from the analysis." (Data); "We therefore exclude these outer regions (specifically, radii larger than 360 and 269 au, respectively)." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
   - parameter: Exclude SY Cha beyond 360 au and AA Tau beyond 269 au; exclude AA Tau from further analysis.  
  > "Surface height retrievals from the channel maps performed by (cite: Galloway2025) show that the emission in the outer regions of SY Cha and AA Tau is diffuse" (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.); "We therefore exclude these outer regions (specifically, radii larger than 360 and 269 au, respectively)." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.); "Given the combination of compact line emission and very high inclination, we exclude AA Tau from further analysis." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Earlier exoALMA analysis found that changing angular resolution did not significantly affect overall velocity extraction.  
  > "As shown in (cite: Stadler2025), using cubes at different angular resolution from the exoALMA images does not significantly affect the overall velocity extraction." (Data)
2. **Construct velocity residual maps**: Fit a Keplerian model to channel maps, estimate line-centroid velocities and subtract the Keplerian model.  
  > "fits a Keplerian disk model to the channel maps of a line intensity cube" (Velocity extraction); "the line-of-sight velocity is estimated by fitting the line profile with either a Gaussian, a Bell, or a double-Bell function" (Velocity extraction); "A Keplerian model is then subtracted from the data to produce a 2D residual" (Velocity extraction)
   - parameter: Radial parametric peak intensity, line width and emission surface height.  
  > "assuming radial parametric forms for the peak intensity, line width, and emission surface height of the disk (cite: Izquierdo_ea_2021)." (Velocity extraction)
   - parameter: Gaussian, Bell or double-Bell centroid fit, depending on back-side emission.  
  > "either a Gaussian, a Bell, or a double-Bell function, depending on whether there is significant contribution from the back side of the disk to the observed emission." (Velocity extraction)
   - assumption: Smooth disks with no warped regions.  
  > "In the analysis, we consider only smooth disks with no warped regions." (Velocity extraction)
   - assumption: Fixed geometrical parameters and systemic velocity.  
  > "As in all exoALMA papers, the geometrical parameters and the systemic velocity used in the models are fixed to those derived from the analysis." (Velocity extraction)
   - fitting: Keplerian channel-map fit and Gaussian/Bell/double-Bell line-profile fits; best-fit models in Izquierdo2025.  
  > "fits a Keplerian disk model to the channel maps of a line intensity cube" (Velocity extraction); "the line-of-sight velocity is estimated by fitting the line profile with either a Gaussian, a Bell, or a double-Bell function" (Velocity extraction); "The best fit models for all targets are presented in (cite: Izquierdo2025)." (Velocity extraction)
   - validation: Apply the observational retrieval procedure to synthetic cubes with known velocities.  
  > "we first test our retrieval methodology on synthetic cubes generated from hydrodynamical simulations for which we know the exact velocities." (Benchmarking against models)
3. **Assess asymmetry and select extraction approach**: Add residuals from opposite disk halves across the minor axis; compute folded-map mean and standard deviation.  
  > "we use folded residual maps, in which the line centroid velocity residuals from one half of the disk are added to those of the opposite half relative to the disk minor axis" (Folded residual maps.); "We compute the folded residual maps for all of our targets and order them by the mean and standard deviation of their folded residual maps as a very simple metrics to estimate their dynamical state." (Folded residual maps.)
   - parameter: MWC758, CQ Tau and SY Cha: spiral-spine extraction; remaining retained exoALMA targets: 1D axisymmetric extraction.  
  > "For targets displaying clear spiral residual patterns (MWC758, CQ Tau, and SY Cha), we extract velocities in the 2D residual maps directly along the spiral spines." (Summary and conclusions); "For all the remaining targets, except AA Tau (see below), we use the 1D axisymmetric extraction method as in previous studies." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
   - assumption: A coherent vertical flow appears as a single-colored semi-circle in folded residual maps.  
  > "In folded residual maps, any coherent vertical flow will appear as a single-colored semi-circle." (Folded residual maps.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Illustrated folded-map patterns for individual velocity components and their combinations.  
  > "illustrates the resulting folded maps for cases with only vertical, only azimuthal, and only radial velocity perturbations, and for combinations of those." (Folded residual maps.)
4. **Extract axisymmetric velocity profiles**: Azimuthally average residuals to recover vertical profiles; the text states division by -sec(i). Average remaining residuals for radial velocities.  
  > "the vertical velocity component is simply the azimuthal average of the residual $\upsilon_{\rm los}$ map divided by $-sec(i)$ (cite: Izquierdo_ea_2022)." (Velocity extraction); "The radial velocities are obtained through an azimuthal average of all remaining velocity residuals" (Velocity extraction)
   - parameter: Error bars: azimuthal standard deviation at each radius.  
  > "the plotted error bars represent the azimuthal standard deviation at each radius" (Radial profiles)
   - parameter: Significant motion: maximum velocity exceeds azimuthal standard deviation.  
  > "defined as cases where the maximum velocity exceeds the standard deviation along the azimuth." (Radial profiles)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric and orthogonal components; azimuthal symmetry of azimuthal and radial velocities.  
  > "Radial profiles of the azimuthal and vertical components are obtained by assuming that they are axisymmetric and orthogonal (cite: Izquierdo_ea_2023)." (Velocity extraction); "Assuming azimuthal symmetry of the azimuthal and radial velocity components" (Velocity extraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Planet–disk, VSI, MRI and GI synthetic observations recover model vertical velocities within a few tens m/s; approximately 10 m/s retrieval floor.  
  > "the true model vertical velocities are consistently recovered within a few tens m/s." (Benchmarking against models); "We validate our retrieval technique using hydrodynamical models of planet–disk interactions and three classes of disk instabilities (VSI, GI, and MRI)" (Summary and conclusions); "We therefore demonstrate that in these simulations vertical velocities can be reliably retrieved down to amplitudes of about 10 m/s even if the disk dynamical state is high." (Summary and conclusions)
5. **Measure spiral velocities**: Trace coherent residual filaments and extract peak velocities along their spines and velocity distributions across their widths.  
  > "we trace coherent sub- and super-Keplerian substructures (filaments) using (cite: Koch2015)." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.); "We then extract the peak velocity residual along the spine of the filaments as well as the distribution of velocities across the spiral width." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
   - parameter: Vertical-motion criterion: no velocity-sign change across either disk axis.  
  > "We consider a filament to trace vertical motions when the velocity does not change sign across neither the minor nor the major axis" (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
   - assumption: Filaments retaining velocity sign across both axes trace vertical motions.  
  > "We consider a filament to trace vertical motions when the velocity does not change sign across neither the minor nor the major axis" (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare spiral-spine extraction with azimuthal averaging for MWC758.  
  > "would underestimate the velocities in the spirals in MWC758." (Additional figures); "compares the two methods for MWC 758." (Spiral targets)
6. **Assess systemic-velocity offsets**: Fit another molecular line with systemic velocity free; compare systemic velocities between emission layers to estimate possible absolute vertical-velocity offsets.  
  > "To assess such a possible offset in the vertical velocity measurements, we examine the systemic velocities derived from another molecular line that traces a different vertical layer of the disk, providing an independent measurement." (Systemic velocities); "with the systemic velocity as a free parameter." (Systemic velocities)
   - assumption: Agreement between lines supports absolute gas motions; discrepancies may indicate an absolute offset.  
  > "If the systemic velocities retrieved from both lines agree, the inferred vertical velocities likely reflect the true absolute gas motions." (Systemic velocities); "However, any significant discrepancy between them would likely point toward an absolute offset affecting the retrieval." (Systemic velocities)
   - fitting: Keplerian channel-map fit with systemic velocity free.  
  > "fit a Keplerian model to the channel maps" (Systemic velocities); "with the systemic velocity as a free parameter." (Systemic velocities)
   - validation: Most offsets are marginal; the radial-profile discussion retains the uncorrected retrieval.  
  > "However, for most targets, the offset in is rather marginal and does not affect our interpretation of the direction of vertical motion." (Systemic velocities); "For the following discussion, we consider the radial profiles as retrieved from our analysis" (Radial profiles)
7. **Identify radial velocity structures**: Find profile peaks and valleys with scipy.signal.find_peaks; reject features whose error bars are consistent with zero.  
  > "To characterize this behavior, we identify peaks and valleys in the profiles using `scipy.signal.find_peaks` function." (Radial profiles); "We discard any peaks and valleys whose error bars make the feature consistent with zero." (Radial profiles)
   - software: scipy.signal.find_peaks
   - parameter: Reject peaks and valleys consistent with zero within their error bars.  
  > "We discard any peaks and valleys whose error bars make the feature consistent with zero." (Radial profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare radial profiles with folded residual maps as a visual diagnostic.  
  > "They provide a useful visual diagnostic as coherent vertical motions appear as azimuthally extended arcs of a single color" (Radial profiles)
8. **Compare velocities with disk structure and sound speed**: Compare velocity-profile perturbations with literature emission-surface and peak-intensity structures; express velocities in local sound-speed units.  
  > "to the molecular emission surface and temperature profile (traced through the peak intensity) structures identified in (cite: Galloway2025)." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles); "The arrows are given in units of the local sound speed, computed at the $^{12}$CO emission layer by using the peak intensity values." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles)
   - parameter: Spatial association within one beam; nine-disk comparison sample.  
  > "This leaves a sample of 9 disks." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles); "spatially correlate within a beam size with substructures in either the emission surfaces or peak intensity profiles in one of the two lines." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles)
   - parameter: Exclude spiral disks, nearly face-on HD135344B and J1604, and flat-surface PDS66.  
  > "and do not include disks with large-scale spirals." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles); "HD135344B and J1604 being nearly face-on, no surface could be retrieved and we exclude these two disks from our analysis" (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles); "We also exclude PDS66 that shows a flat surface." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles)
   - assumption: Peak intensity traces the temperature profile used for local sound speed.  
  > "temperature profile (traced through the peak intensity)" (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles); "The arrows are given in units of the local sound speed, computed at the $^{12}$CO emission layer by using the peak intensity values." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mirror-symmetric surface perturbations disappear under folding and averaging; previous AS209 comparison found no significant residual changes.  
  > "Such surface perturbations are mirror-symmetric and disappear when folding residuals maps and averaging, as in our methodology (cite: Izquierdo_ea_2023)." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles); "(cite: Galloway2023) found that there was no significant changes in the velocity residuals of AS209 when comparing a smooth surface to a structure surface." (Correlation with surface and intensity profiles)
9. **Assess thermal-wind launching**: Compare sound speed with approximately 0.3 times escape velocity at the CO emission surface.  
  > "Thermal winds are approximately launched when:" (Winds); "\approx 0.3 v_\mathrm{esc}" (Winds); "We can then consider how close to meeting this criterion the gas at the CO emission surface is for the disks in our sample." (Winds)
   - assumption: gamma = 5/3; escape velocity sqrt(2GM*/r).  
  > "$v_\mathrm{esc} = \sqrt{2G M_* /r}$ is the escape velocity and assuming $\gamma = 5/3$ (cite: Liffman_2003)." (Winds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Most disks exhibit vertical motions with diverse amplitudes and morphologies.  
  > "We find that the majority of the disks exhibit ubiquitous vertical motions, although their amplitudes and morphologies vary from one disk to another." (Summary and conclusions)
- Profiles show oscillatory up/down motions or a single downward-to-upward transition.  
  > "The first type of modulation is characterized by a single transition from downward to upward motion, with a broad downward plateau that reverses into an upward plateau at larger radii." (Radial profiles); "The second, observed in the majority of the disks such as LkCa15, J1615, and DM Tau, is alternating upward and downward motions." (Radial profiles)
- MWC758 and CQ Tau paired spirals are interpreted as disk eccentricity induced by massive companions.  
  > "In MWC758 and CQ Tau, we detect strong upward and downward motions, reaching up to approximately 300 m/s, which appear as red- and blue-shifted spiral structures." (Summary and conclusions); "We interpret these features as signatures of disk eccentricity induced by massive companions." (Summary and conclusions)
- Beam convolution and emission from multiple vertical layers reduce measured velocity amplitudes.  
  > "multiple contributions from velocities at different vertical layers around the $\tau = 1$ surface can smear out the observed signal." (Beam convolution effect); "beam convolution at the current exoALMA resolution can significantly reduce the amplitudes of the intrinsic velocities." (Beam convolution effect)
- Strong molecular winds appear relatively rare; measured motions may probe their bases.  
  > "and the measured vertical motions might be probing the base of such winds." (Summary and conclusions); "upward motions, possibly tracing winds, that span the entire radial range are not commonly observed" (Radial profiles)
- No single physical mechanism explains the full diversity.  
  > "Overall, the velocity structure traced in our discs is complex and highly diverse, suggesting that it is unlikely that a single physical mechanism is responsible for the vertical motions observed across all systems." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: exoALMA was specifically designed for detailed kinematic study.  
  > "exoALMA constitutes an unprecedented dataset specifically designed for detailed study of kinematics (cite: Teague2025)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Favor signal-to-noise for large-scale features; use spiral-spine extraction where azimuthal averaging underestimates velocities.  
  > "As we are mostly interested in following the large scale features in this paper, we favor high signal to noise ratio at the cost of angular resolution when selecting the imaging setup." (Data); "This approach ensures a more accurate characterization of the vertical velocities at a given radial separation and prevents the underestimation typically caused by azimuthal averaging." (Spiral targets)
- Alternative: Azimuthal averaging is unsuitable for prominent spiral residuals because it underestimates their velocities.  
  > "This approach ensures a more accurate characterization of the vertical velocities at a given radial separation and prevents the underestimation typically caused by azimuthal averaging." (Spiral targets)
- Alternative: HD135344B arcs are not treated as coherent vertical motions because they change sign across the disk axes.  
  > "HD135344B shows a variety of arcs similar to short spirals, but since the velocity spirals change sign when crossing major and minor axis, we do not consider those as coherent vertical motions." (Samples for 2D and 1D analysis.)
- Alternative: An outer planetary companion driving inner spirals produces insufficient vertical velocities in the cited models.  
  > "An alternative explanation for the observed spirals is that they are tracing the *inner* spirals driven by an outer companion." (Spirals); "the vertical motions in the inner spirals induced by massive planets do not show strong enough amplitudes to match our observations (cite: Rabago2021)" (Spirals)
- Alternative: Shadow-driven m=2 spirals are disfavored for the observed m=1 residual spirals.  
  > "However, MWC758 and CQ Tau residuals show $m=1$ spirals, hence this scenario does not appear to be likely." (Spirals); "Large non-Keplerian velocity $m=2$ spirals can also be induced by temperature variations in the disks, induced by shadows." (Spirals)
- Alternative: Large-scale infall could induce eccentricity and vertical motions, but no nearby large-scale streamer is indicated for CQ Tau or MWC758.  
  > "Large scale infall can also trigger spiral arms with large opening angles (cite: Hennebelle2017; Calcino2025) and result in disk eccentricity, naturally inducing vertical motions." (Spirals); "While there is so far no indication of large scale streamer in the vicinity of CQ Tau and MWC758" (Spirals)
- How they decided it was enough: Benchmark against known simulation velocities; assess azimuthal dispersion and reject radial extrema consistent with zero.  
  > "the true model vertical velocities are consistently recovered within a few tens m/s." (Benchmarking against models); "defined as cases where the maximum velocity exceeds the standard deviation along the azimuth." (Radial profiles); "We discard any peaks and valleys whose error bars make the feature consistent with zero." (Radial profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Strong non-axisymmetry can prevent correct velocity-component amplitudes from being recovered by decomposition.  
  > "when velocity perturbations strongly deviate from axisymmetry, such decomposition methods fail at properly retrieving the right amplitude for each velocity component." (Velocity extraction)
- Limitation (authors): The benchmark simulations may not represent the full exoALMA dynamical range.  
  > "While these simulations might not be fully representative of the dynamical states of the exoALMA sample" (Benchmarking against models)
- Limitation (authors): Systemic velocity is degenerate with a bulk vertical-motion offset.  
  > "actual vertical motions in the disk may be partially interpreted as a bulk velocity of the source relative to the observer" (Systemic velocities)
- Limitation (authors): Line-to-line systemic offsets can reflect real differences between vertical layers.  
  > "the measured offset traces the intrinsic difference in vertical motions between lines tracing different heights" (Systemic velocities)
- Limitation (authors): SY Cha interpretation is uncertain because no clear red-shifted counterpart exists and residuals are complex.  
  > "The velocity extraction for SY Cha should be interpreted with caution due to the lack of a clearly identifiable red-shifted spiral counterpart and the complex structure of the velocity residuals." (Spiral targets)
- Limitation (authors): Finite resolution and vertical-layer mixing suppress measured amplitudes.  
  > "multiple contributions from velocities at different vertical layers around the $\tau = 1$ surface can smear out the observed signal." (Beam convolution effect); "Overall, this suggests that the vertical motions measured in our data are likely lower limits on the true velocity amplitudes." (Beam convolution effect)
- Limitation (authors): A unique driving mechanism cannot be inferred; multiple processes may coexist.  
  > "preventing to infer a single, coherent, mechanism as the origin of the vertical motions for each disk." (Origin of the vertical motions); "In addition, the processes at play are not mutually exclusive and multiple processes can occur at different locations of the disk." (Origin of the vertical motions)
- Limitation (authors): CO observations may miss faster winds at higher altitudes.  
  > "any photoevaporative or MHD-driven wind might be active at higher altitudes above 4-5 scale heights (cite: Nakatani2018; Gressel2020) with much larger vertical velocities, however not contributing to the observed emission." (Winds)
