## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble disk fluxes and radii**: Use 2D Gaussian fits; use 5σ contours for non-Gaussian profiles or poor fits; prefer higher-resolution literature measurements at the same frequency.  
  > "Both disk sizes and fluxes were derived from a 2D Gaussian fit with the CASA *imfit* task (cite: 2007ASPC..376..127M)." (Disk fluxes and sizes); "we used the $5\sigma$ contour in the Briggs 0.5 weighted maps to measure the radius and flux (cite: 2024ApJ...973..138H)" (Disk fluxes and sizes); "if a CAMPOS source was also observed at the same frequency (ALMA Band 6) but with higher angular resolution by other surveys, we report the protostellar disk flux and size from the literature instead." (Disk fluxes and sizes)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: 2σ deconvolved major-axis size; approximately R90  
  > "Disk sizes are calculated as the 2$\sigma$ size of the deconvolved major axis, which corresponds to approximately the radius containing 90% of the flux R$_{90}$." (Disk fluxes and sizes)
   - parameter: 5σ contour in Briggs 0.5 weighted maps  
  > "we used the $5\sigma$ contour in the Briggs 0.5 weighted maps to measure the radius and flux (cite: 2024ApJ...973..138H)" (Disk fluxes and sizes)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit with CASA imfit  
  > "Both disk sizes and fluxes were derived from a 2D Gaussian fit with the CASA *imfit* task (cite: 2007ASPC..376..127M)." (Disk fluxes and sizes)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Assign evolutionary temperatures**: Cross-match with eHOPS and Spitzer c2d/GB; use literature temperatures when absent from both; compare eHOPS and extinction-corrected Dunham temperatures.  
  > "the Extension of HOPS Out to 500 ParSecs (eHOPS) catalog (Riwaj Pokhrel, private communication), to obtain the bolometric temperature" (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer); "For sources without a counterpart in both eHOPS and (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D), we adopted the $T_{\rm bol}$ from the literature." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer); "we adopt the extinction-corrected bolometric temperature from (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D)." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer)
   - parameter: Non-extinction-corrected Dunham temperatures for sources without eHOPS counterparts  
  > "we use the bolometric temperatures without extinction correction from (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D) for sources without a counterpart in eHOPS." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer)
   - assumption: Bolometric temperature is a proxy for relative evolutionary age  
  > "which serves as a proxy for relative evolutionary age for embedded protostellar systems" (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer)
   - assumption: Same bolometric temperature and class for all sources in close multiple systems  
  > "In the CAMPOS survey, we assume the same bolometric temperature and class for all sources in the close multiple systems." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Use both eHOPS and Dunham temperatures to quantify the onset range  
  > "We present the $T_{\rm bol}$ from both eHOPS and (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D) to quantify the range for the onset of protostellar disk substructures" (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer)
3. **Construct the resolved comparison sample**: Select ALMA 1.3 mm disks by effective resolution, inclination and bolometric temperature; detection-rate sample also has linear resolution below 20 au.  
  > "we only include protostellar disks observed at 1.3 mm wavelength by ALMA." (Observational data and the sample construction); "we only included samples with $T_{\rm bol} \le 1900$ K." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer); "have a disk diameter greater than 4 times the resolution ($\theta_D/\theta_{res} \ge 4$), with an inclination angle less than 75 $^\circ$, and linear resolution less than 20 au." (Disk substructure detection rates)
   - parameter: θD/θres ≥ 4; θD contains 90% of the continuum emission  
  > "where $\theta_D$ is the diameter of the disk containing 90% of the continuum emission and $\theta_{res}$ is the angular resolution of the observation." (Observational data and the sample construction); "we only analyze sources with $\theta_D/\theta_{res} \ge 4$" (Observational data and the sample construction)
   - parameter: θres = √(θmaj θmin), using synthesized-beam FWHM axes  
  > "$\theta_{res}$ is estimated by taking the geometric mean of the FWHM of the major ($\theta_{\rm maj}$) and minor ($\theta_{\rm min}$) axes of the synthesized beam" (Observational data and the sample construction)
   - assumption: Thin circular disks; printed inclination formula i = sin⁻¹(Rmin/Rmaj) × 180°/π  
  > "We assume thin circular disks and use the major-to-minor axis ratio of the dust continuum data to compute the inclination angle ($i$), that is $i = sin^{-1}(R_{\rm min}/R_{\rm maj})\times 180^\circ/\pi$." (Observational data and the sample construction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Classify visible substructures**: Count rings, gaps, central cavities and spirals visible in 1.3 mm images; reject asymmetry alone and image-smooth borderline detections.  
  > "In this paper, we labeled disks with substructures if the disk has rings, gaps, a central cavity, or spirals." (Substructure criteria); "all correspond to substructures that are immediately visible in the 1.3 mm image." (Substructure criteria); "Sources that show small variations as a function of distance in $uv-$space or small variation in the intensity radial profile but appear smooth in the image plane are counted here as disks without substructures" (Substructure criteria)
   - parameter: C-type: central cleared dust cavity >10 au; F-type: no central cavity  
  > "The cavity-type disk substructures exhibit large ($>$10 au) cleared central dust cavities." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities); "F-type disk substructures do not exhibit a central cavity." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
   - parameter: Multiple-ring systems: at least two rings with brightnesses within a factor of two  
  > "multiple-ring systems, which have at least two rings with similar brightness within a factor of two at different disk radii" (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: All accepted substructure disks were confirmed in separate published papers  
  > "all of the disks considered as with substructures in this work have already been confirmed as such in separate published papers" (Substructure criteria)
5. **Convert continuum flux to disk mass**: Convert 1.3 mm flux to dust mass with the ODISEA method; apply gas-to-dust ratio 100 for disk mass.  
  > "We adopted the method outlined in the ODISEA survey of Ophiuchus protoplanetary disks to convert the 1.3 mm disk flux into dust mass." (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks); "We then assumed a gas-to-dust ratio of 100 to obtain the disk mass (cite: 2023ASPC..534..423B)." (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission; constant temperature 20 K  
  > "This method assumes optically thin emission, a constant temperature of 20 K" (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks)
   - assumption: κν = (ν/100 GHz) cm² g⁻¹  
  > "an opacity coefficient of $\kappa_\nu= (\nu/100 \, {\rm GHz})$ cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: 2019ApJ...875L...9W)." (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "We then assumed a gas-to-dust ratio of 100 to obtain the disk mass (cite: 2023ASPC..534..423B)." (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Measure substructure detection rates**: Assign substructure flags 1/0; compute a moving mean over groups of eight disks ordered by bolometric temperature.  
  > "Each disk is assigned a value of 1 if it exhibits substructures and 0 if it does not." (Disk substructure detection rates); "We compute a moving average with a window size of 8, where the kernel slides through $T_{\rm bol}$ and calculates the mean for every group of 8 disks." (Disk substructure detection rates)
   - parameter: Window size n = 8  
  > "We adopt the moving average with a window size of 8 to ensure enough sampling resolution across $T_{\rm bol}$ and substructure detection rate space." (Disk substructure detection rates)
   - parameter: Binomial counting uncertainty σb = √[r(1−r)/n]  
  > "\sigma_b=\sqrt{\frac{r(1-r)}{n}}," (Disk substructure detection rates); "where $r$ is the substructure detection rate, and $n=8$ is the total number of sources in the window." (Disk substructure detection rates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare results using eHOPS and Dunham bolometric temperatures  
  > "The substructure detection rate adopting the (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D) $T_{\rm bol}$ in general agrees with the results adopting the eHOPS $T_{\rm bol}$" (Disk substructure detection rates)
7. **Compare cavity and filled populations**: For disks with substructures, assign C-type = 1 and F-type = 0; use eight-disk moving means versus temperature, radius and mass; count filled multiple-ring systems.  
  > "For disks with substructures, each disk is assigned a value of 1 if it exhibits a central cavity (C-type) and 0 if it does not (F-type)." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities); "We compute a moving average with a window size of 8, where the kernel slides through $T_{\rm bol}$, disk radius, and disk mass, and calculate the mean for every group of 8 disks." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities); "we found that the majority (10 out of 13 or $77\,\%$) are centrally filled (F-type)" (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
   - parameter: Large central cavity in multiple-ring comparison: ≥25% of disk diameter  
  > "Multiple-ring systems with a large central cavity ($\ge 25$ % of the disk diameter) are absent or very rare." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Assess optical-depth bias**: Calculate brightness temperature from average disk intensity with the full Planck equation; compare with luminosity-based dust temperature through Tbright,Planck/Tdust.  
  > "We estimated the brightness temperature of the disk from the average disk intensity using the full Planck equation." (The onset of protostellar disk substructures); "we plot the $T_{\rm bright,Planck} \, / \, T_{\rm dust}$ ratio as a function of evolutionary stage." (The onset of protostellar disk substructures)
   - parameter: Tdust = 43(Lbol/1 L⊙)^0.25  
  > "T_{\mathrm{dust}}=43\left(\frac{L_{\mathrm{bol}}}{1 L_{\odot}}\right)^{0.25}" (The onset of protostellar disk substructures)
   - parameter: Class I: 70 ≤ Tbol ≤ 650 K; Class II: Tbol ≥ 650 K  
  > "Class I sources ($70 \le T_{\rm bol} \le 650$ K), at 0.23, and Class II sources ($T_{\rm bol} \ge 650$ K), at 0.27." (The onset of protostellar disk substructures)
   - assumption: Tbright,Planck = Tdust(1−e⁻τν); optically thick ratio close to 1

## Qualitative findings

- Substructures are already common by the Class I stage  
  > "Our statistical analysis confirms that substructures similar to those in Class II disks are already common by the Class I stage." (Conclusion)
- The measured emergence is likely an upper age limit  
  > "We argue that this emergence is likely only an upper age limit, as our study suggests increasing optical depth toward the earliest stages." (Conclusion)
- Cavity disks are generally smaller and less massive than disks without cavities  
  > "in general, protostellar disks with a central cavity are smaller and less massive than those without a central cavity." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
- C-type and F-type populations coexist across evolutionary stages  
  > "we find that both populations coexist across evolutionary stages." (Conclusion)
- No clear, uniform evolutionary sequence in disk substructures  
  > "Our results suggest that there is no clear, uniform evolutionary sequence in disk substructures." (Central cavities in protostellar disks: are disk substructures evolutionarily linked?)
- C-type disks are candidates for gap-opening protoplanets or close protobinaries  
  > "Protostellar disks with C-type disk substructures are good candidates to search for gap-opening protoplanets or close protobinaries." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Compare the same wavelength, effective resolution and inclination range to reduce substructure-detection biases.  
  > "To take into account this resolution bias and have a comparable criteria for the different sets of observations, we only analyze sources with $\theta_D/\theta_{res} \ge 4$" (Observational data and the sample construction); "To ensure a consistent survey probing the substructures from similar solids, we only include protostellar disks observed at 1.3 mm wavelength by ALMA." (Observational data and the sample construction); "To take this into account, we exclude all disks in the sample with inclination angles larger than 75$^\circ$." (Observational data and the sample construction)
- Why this method: Conservative visible-image classification minimizes false positives; binary cavity classification is simple and robust.  
  > "Our conservative approach minimizes the chances of false positives, but we may miss lower-contrast (shallower) substructures in younger disks with borderline detection." (The onset of protostellar disk substructures); "This classification is advantageous due to its simplicity, robustness, and clarity." (Central cavities in protostellar disks: are disk substructures evolutionarily linked?)
- Alternative: Do not count image-smooth sources with only small uv-space or radial-profile variations as substructured.  
  > "Sources that show small variations as a function of distance in $uv-$space or small variation in the intensity radial profile but appear smooth in the image plane are counted here as disks without substructures" (Substructure criteria)
- Alternative: Compare eHOPS-based onset with extinction-corrected Dunham temperatures as an upper-age case.  
  > "to investigate the upper age limit for the onset of disk substructures, we adopt the extinction-corrected bolometric temperature from (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D)." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer)
- Alternative: Photoevaporative mass-loss does not explain large cavities in younger Class I and flat-spectrum sources.  
  > "The detection of large cavities in younger Class I and Flat-spectrum sources cannot be explained by photoevaporative mass-loss in winds (cite: 2001MNRAS.328..485C)." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
- Alternative: Radial drift and companion tidal interactions remain possible explanations for central cavities.  
  > "the central cavity in Class I can possibly be explained by radial drift (cite: 2010A&A...513A..79B; 2017MNRAS.465.3865C)." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities); "Another explanation for these central cavities is tidal interactions with companions." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
- Alternative: Rapid evolution, different triggering times and distinct formation mechanisms are alternatives to a uniform morphological sequence.  
  > "disk substructures evolve very rapidly and thus can be present at both Class I and Class II stages, and/or that they can be triggered at different times." (Conclusion); "Alternatively, the observed diversity could be explained by multiple distinct populations of disk substructures, shaped by different formation mechanisms." (Conclusion)
- Alternative: A non-steady-state lifetime calibration would imply earlier onset; conservative number counting is adopted.  
  > "If we adopt the duration from (cite: 2018A&A...618A.158K), the onset of disk substructure will occur much earlier." (Bolometric temperature as a proxy for relative evolutionary age); "In this paper, we adopt a more conservative number-counting method for the age estimate." (Bolometric temperature as a proxy for relative evolutionary age)
- How they decided it was enough: Eight-disk windows balance sampling across temperature and detection-rate space.  
  > "We adopt the moving average with a window size of 8 to ensure enough sampling resolution across $T_{\rm bol}$ and substructure detection rate space." (Disk substructure detection rates)
- Limitation (authors): Conservative classification can miss shallow substructures in younger disks.  
  > "we may miss some sources with borderline detections, introducing a bias against younger disks with smaller and lower-contrast (shallower) substructures" (Substructure criteria)
- Limitation (authors): Optically thin mass estimates can be lower limits.  
  > "We caution that disks might not be fully optically thin, particularly at the early stages" (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks); "In this case, the estimated masses can be conservatively considered a lower limit." (Deriving the mass of protostellar disks)
- Limitation (authors): Bolometric temperatures depend on extinction treatment and SED coverage.  
  > "However, the infrared portion of the SED is not corrected for extinction (cite: 2023ApJS..266...32P)." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer); "It depends both on the shape of the SED and on how well the SED was sampled by (cite: 2015ApJS..220...11D)." (Bolometric temperature as a disk evolutionary tracer)
- Limitation (authors): Tight companions may be unresolved or lack detectable dust disks.  
  > "we cannot exclude the presence of tight binary companions that either lack detectable dust disks or remain unresolved due to the linear resolution limit of 15 au." (The protostellar disk substructures with and without central cavities)
- Limitation (authors): Onset age is only a zeroth-order estimate.  
  > "the age estimate for the onset of protostellar disk substructures is only a zeroth-order estimate, and future work is needed to improve the $T_{\rm bol}$-age relation." (Bolometric temperature as a proxy for relative evolutionary age)
- Limitation (authors): Number counting ignores cloud-dependent star-formation histories and stage-duration distributions.  
  > "does not take into account different star-formation rates in different molecular clouds, assumes a steady state constant star formation rate, and treats the duration of each stage as a single value rather than a distribution" (Bolometric temperature as a proxy for relative evolutionary age)
- Limitation (authors): CFHTWIR-Oph 79 evolutionary classification is debated and its temperature is assigned through a close multiple system.  
  > "The classification of this source has been debated." (Table 1); "In the CAMPOS survey, we assume the same bolometric temperature and class for all sources in the close multiple systems." (Table 1)
