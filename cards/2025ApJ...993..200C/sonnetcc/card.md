## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image Stokes I, Q, U, V continuum in Band 3 and Band 6**: CASA calibration, phase-only self-calibration on Stokes I, then tclean with Briggs weighting at robust 0.5 and 2.0  
  > "We used CASA to calibrate the raw data." (Observations: ALMA Band 3); "We made images with the CASA $tclean$ task using a Briggs weighting with robust parameters of 0.5 and 2.0." (Observations: ALMA Band 3)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Band 3 self-cal solint 300 s; Band 6 solints 300, 100, 30 s  
  > "phase-only self-calibration using the Stokes $I$ map with solution intervals of 300 s to reduce the noise" (Observations: ALMA Band 3); "phase-only self-calibration using the Stokes $I$ map with solution intervals of 300 s, 100 s, and 30 s" (Observations: ALMA Band 6)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive polarized intensity, polarization fraction and magnetic field orientations**: Debiased polarized intensity; B-field orientation from 90 deg rotation of polarization angle; half-vectors above S/N cuts sampled at about half the beam; polarization fraction PI/I  
  > "Polarized intensity is debiased with $PI = \sqrt{Q^2 + U^2 - \sigma_{QU}^2}$, where $\sigma_{QU}$ is the rms noise of Stokes $Q$ and $U$." (Observations: ALMA Band 3); "The plane-of-sky magnetic field orientations are inferred from $90^{\circ}$ rotation of polarization directions." (Magnetic Field Morphology); "The polarization fraction is calculated with $PI/I$" (Polarized Intensity and Polarization Fraction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Band 3 vectors: I > 3 sigma_I and PI > 1.5 sigma_PI; Band 6: I > 3 sigma_I and PI > 3 sigma_PI  
  > "For Band 3 observations, we represent half-vectors satisfying $I > 3\sigma_{I}$ and $PI > 1.5\sigma_{PI}$, while for Band 6, the criteria are $I > 3\sigma_{I}$ and $PI > 3\sigma_{PI}$." (Magnetic Field Morphology)
   - parameter: Vector sampling 0.24 arcsec (robust 0.5) and 0.32 arcsec (robust 2.0)  
  > "Magnetic field vectors are sampled at intervals of 0.24$^{\prime\prime}$ and 0.32$^{\prime\prime}$ for robust parameters of 0.5 and 2.0, respectively" (Magnetic Field Morphology)
   - parameter: Band 3 image at robust 2.0 and Band 6 image at robust 0.5 selected for the main B-field figure  
  > "We selected the Band 3 image with a robust parameter of 2.0 and the Band 6 image with a robust parameter of 0.5" (Magnetic Field Morphology)
   - assumption: Polarization directions rotated by 90 deg trace the plane-of-sky magnetic field  
  > "The plane-of-sky magnetic field orientations are inferred from $90^{\circ}$ rotation of polarization directions." (Magnetic Field Morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with previous Band 7 (358 GHz) field orientations at the center and with JCMT core-scale fields  
  > "consistent with previous Band 7 (358 GHz) observations (cite: Lee_2019; Yen_2023)" (Magnetic Field Morphology)
3. **Make the Band 3 to Band 6 spectral index map**: Match uv-coverage with uvtaper and uvrange in tclean, then alpha = log(I1/I2)/log(nu1/nu2) with 233 and 97.5 GHz, shown where both bands exceed 3 sigma  
  > "we matched the $uv$-coverage of the Band 3 and Band 6 observations by adjusting `uvtaper` and `uvrange` parameters during the CASA `tclean` task." (Spectral Index); "The spectral index map is shown where both Band 3 and Band 6 intensities are larger than 3 times their rms noise levels." (Spectral Index)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: minimum uv-distance 9 klambda  
  > "In this process, the minimum $uv$-distance is set to 9 $k\lambda$." (Spectral Index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Solve for line-of-sight averaged temperature and H2 column density**: Solve the simplified radiative transfer I_nu = B_nu(T_los)(1 - exp(-tau)) using the uv-matched Band 3 and Band 6 images for T_los and column density simultaneously  
  > "and calculate the line-of-sight averaged temperature and the column density simultaneously." (Temperature and Density Distributions); "where $\overline{T}_{\mathrm{los}}$ is the line-of-sight averaged temperature, assuming uniform temperature along the line-of-sight." (Temperature and Density Distributions)
   - parameter: mu = 2.86, beta = 1.5, kappa = 0.01 at 233 GHz  
  > "we take $\mu = 2.86$, $\beta=1.5$, $\kappa_{\nu}=0.01$ at 233 GHz assuming the gas-to-dust ratio of 100 (cite: Ossenkopf_1994)" (Temperature and Density Distributions)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100; beta constant across the region; dust opacity from Ossenkopf_1994  
  > "We assume that $\beta$ is constant across the region." (Temperature and Density Distributions); "assuming the gas-to-dust ratio of 100 (cite: Ossenkopf_1994)" (Temperature and Density Distributions)
   - fitting: Pixel-wise two-parameter solution of the intensity equation for two bands; solver, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "and calculate the line-of-sight averaged temperature and the column density simultaneously." (Temperature and Density Distributions)
   - validation: Beta lowered to 1.2 changes boundary temperature; patterns independent of assumed opacity and beta; synthetic models in the appendix  
  > "If $\beta$ is as low as 1.2, the estimated line-of-sight temperature at the boundary would increase to about 6 K." (Temperature and Density Distributions); "Although different dust opacity values will affect the resulting line-of-sight temperature and column density values, their overall patterns do not change." (Temperature and Density Distributions)
5. **Test with synthetic RADMC-3D models whether filaments are dense or hot**: Build a flattened power-law envelope with a 20 au disk and two cylindrical filaments (density-enhanced Model 1, temperature-enhanced Model 2), synthesize Band 3 and Band 6 images with RADMC-3D, and repeat the spectral index, temperature and column density analysis  
  > "We run RADMC-3D to generate synthetic images in Band 3 (97.5 GHz) and Band 6 (233 GHz) frequencies (cite: Dullemond_2012)." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope); "we calculate the spectral index, the line-of-sight averaged temperature, and the column density using the same methods described in Sections" (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
   - software: RADMC-3D, Optool
   - parameter: Maximum dust grain size 200 um in disk and 10 um in envelope and filaments; thin icy mantles with MRN distributions  
  > "The maximum sizes of dust particles are assumed to be 200 $\mu$m in the disk and 10 $\mu$m in the envelope and the filaments." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope); "Dust particles have thin icy mantles with MRN distributions in our models." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
   - parameter: Filament width 200 au; density enhancement 6.0e-18 g cm-3 (Model 1) or temperature enhancement 6 K (Model 2); envelope flattening f = 0.7  
  > "We set $r_{\mathrm{fil}}$ to 200 au." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope); "We insert the parameter $f = 0.7$ in the $z$ direction to make a flattened envelope." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
   - assumption: Disk radius 20 au, height 10 au, PA 54 deg, inclination 81 deg, uniform density and temperature; envelope PA 10 deg  
  > "The radius of the disk in our model is 20 au and the height is 10 au with a position angle of $54^{\circ}$ and an inclination of $81^{\circ}$" (Modeling the HH 211 envelope); "The position angle of the envelope is assumed to be $10^{\circ}$." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
   - fitting: No fit: models compared qualitatively with observations; envelope slope q set to -0.65 instead of -0.4 to match observed features  
  > "The goal of our modeling is not to derive the best model parameters for the HH 211 envelope." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope); "but we set $q$ to be -0.65 to match the observational features." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
   - validation: Density-enhanced model resembles the observations more than the temperature-enhanced model  
  > "Overall, the general observed features are more similar to those predicted by the density-enhanced model than by the temperature-enhanced model." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
6. **Find CMU infall trajectories matching the southern filament and B-field lines**: Ballistic CMU (Cassen-Moosman-Ulrich) trajectories with M*, Rc, i, PA fixed from literature; search initial angles theta0 and phi0 for trajectories matching the dust filament and the field curvature  
  > "With these four parameters fixed, we search for CMU model trajectories that reproduce the southern filamentary structures traced by the dust continuum emission and aligned magnetic field lines." (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
   - parameter: M*+Mdisk = 0.08 Msun, Rc = 42 au, i = 98 deg, PA = 64 deg  
  > "a centrifugal radius of $R_c = 42$ au (cite: Lee_2019), and disk inclination and position angle of $i = 98^\circ$ and PA = $64^\circ$, respectively (cite: Lee_2023)" (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
   - parameter: Best trajectory theta0 = 90 deg, phi0 = 90 deg; others 80 < theta0 < 90 deg, 90 < phi0 < 95 deg  
  > "The red curve corresponds to the trajectory with initial angles $\theta_{0}=90^\circ$ and $\phi_{0}=90^\circ$" (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
   - assumption: Initial solid-body rotation, central point mass, pressure forces and magnetic fields neglected  
  > "The model assumes an initial solid-body rotation and a central point mass, and it neglects pressure forces and magnetic fields." (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
   - fitting: Manual search over initial angles; no sampler or likelihood stated  
  > "Upon careful inspection, we find no other sets of initial angles" (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
   - validation: Argues trajectories cannot be tuned to match arbitrary curved field lines given fixed M*, Rc, i, PA  
  > "We note that one cannot arbitrarily obtain infalling trajectories that reproduce any curved field lines simply by varying the initial angles" (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
7. **Estimate mass, length, velocity and mass infall rate of the southern stream**: Stream mass from the column-density map and independently from Band 6 flux (optically thin M = F D^2/(kappa B(T))); length and velocity from the CMU trajectory; Mdot = M/(L/vbar)  
  > "Using the column density map shown in the right panel of Figure" (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament); "The mass infall rate along the stream is calculated using $\dot{M} = M / (L/\bar{v})$, where $M$ is the mass of the stream." (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)
   - parameter: Central region with I > 50 sigma_I excluded; Band 6 integrated flux 10 mJy; T = 5-10 K; kappa = 0.01 cm2 g-1 at 233 GHz  
  > "We exclude the central part of the envelope ($I > 50\sigma_I$), where the mass is uncertain" (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament); "The integrated flux density within the defined region in Band 6 is 10 mJy." (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament); "Assuming a dust opacity of $\kappa_{\nu} = 0.01$ cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at 233 GHz with a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 100 (cite: Ossenkopf_1994) and a dust temperature in the range $T = 5$–10 K" (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)
   - assumption: Optically thin thermal dust emission; stream mass is an upper limit because of foreground and background contribution  
  > "Therefore, the derived mass should be considered an upper limit for the mass of the stream." (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Two mass estimates (column density map and flux-based) compared  
  > "Alternatively, the mass can be derived directly from the dust continuum emission under the assumption of optically thin thermal emission" (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)
8. **Compare stream infall rate with protostellar accretion rate**: Mdot_acc = L_acc R*/(G M*) with L_acc = 0.5-1 Lbol, R* = 1-3 Rsun, Lbol ~ 3.6 Lsun  
  > "Assuming an accretion luminosity of $0.5–1$ $L_{\text{bol}}$ and a stellar radius of $1–3$ $R_{\odot}$ (cite: Stahler_1988)" (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)
   - assumption: Accretion luminosity 0.5-1 Lbol; stellar radius 1-3 Rsun  
  > "Assuming an accretion luminosity of $0.5–1$ $L_{\text{bol}}$ and a stellar radius of $1–3$ $R_{\odot}$ (cite: Stahler_1988)" (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)

## Qualitative findings

- Southern and northwestern filaments are aligned with the core-scale JCMT magnetic field; the western filament is nearly perpendicular to it  
  > "Notably, the southern and northwestern filaments are well aligned with the core-scale magnetic fields." (Dust Continuum and Core-scale Magnetic Field); "In contrast, the western filament is almost perpendicular to the core-scale magnetic fields." (Dust Continuum and Core-scale Magnetic Field)
- Clear U-shaped pinched (hourglass) fields near the protostar and toroidal fields perpendicular to the outflow  
  > "Near the central protostar, we detect clear U-shaped pinched magnetic fields around the blue-shifted outflow axis" (Magnetic Field Morphology); "The magnetic field is perpendicular to the outflow directions in the outflow regions." (Magnetic Field Morphology)
- X-shaped depolarization pattern in Band 6  
  > "An X-shaped depolarization pattern appears in the Band 6 observations" (Polarized Intensity and Polarization Fraction)
- Dust filaments are high-density rather than high-temperature regions; temperature higher in the east, column density enhanced in the south and west  
  > "This suggests that the dust filaments observed in the dust continuum are likely regions of high density rather than high temperature." (Temperature and Density Distributions); "The temperature is higher in the eastern part of the envelope, while the column density is enhanced in the southern and western regions." (Conclusions)
- Magnetic flux must drop between core scale and inner envelope scale, via ambipolar diffusion or mass accumulation along field lines  
  > "Between the core scale ($\sim$10,000 au) and the inner envelope scale ($\sim$1000 au), magnetic flux must be significantly reduced." (Dynamical Role of Magnetic Fields in the HH 211 System)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Band 3 recovers larger-scale fields while Band 6 reveals smaller-scale fields  
  > "as larger-scale magnetic fields are more effectively detected in Band 3, whereas smaller-scale fields are more clearly revealed in Band 6." (Magnetic Field Morphology)
- Why this method: CMU model has been used to identify streamer trajectories in earlier studies  
  > "Infalling streamer trajectories in protostellar envelopes have been successfully identified using the CMU model in several previous studies" (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
- Alternative: Southern filament as outflow cavity wall versus dense infalling stream  
  > "One interpretation is that the southern filament traces the outflow cavity walls." (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?); "Alternatively, the southern filament might not be physically related to the outflow." (Outflow Cavity Walls or Infalling Streams?)
- Alternative: Temperature-enhanced filament model (Model 2) disfavored versus density-enhanced model  
  > "Overall, the general observed features are more similar to those predicted by the density-enhanced model than by the temperature-enhanced model." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
- Alternative: Binary companion at 0.3 arcsec southwest suggested by SMA not seen in ALMA data  
  > "However, high-resolution ALMA observations did not show any secondary source at the predicted position" (Dust Continuum and Core-scale Magnetic Field)
- How they decided it was enough: Synthetic-model comparison judged by similarity of the general observed features to the density-enhanced or temperature-enhanced model  
  > "Overall, the general observed features are more similar to those predicted by the density-enhanced model than by the temperature-enhanced model." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature and column density in the central region are uncertain because of embedded disk contamination and assumed beta  
  > "Estimates in the central region are uncertain due to contamination from the embedded disk emission, which increases the optical depth and affects the assumed $\beta$ value." (Temperature and Density Distributions)
- Limitation (authors): Very low boundary temperature (~4 K) may be due to resolved-out large-scale emission or assumed beta  
  > "This unexpectedly low temperature may be due to the fact that the continuum emission is resolved out on large scales" (Temperature and Density Distributions)
- Limitation (authors): Stream mass and infall rate are upper limits  
  > "Therefore, the derived mass should be considered an upper limit for the mass of the stream." (Mass Infalling Rate along the Southern Filament)
- Limitation (authors): Mass-to-flux ratios use plane-of-sky fields only and are upper limits  
  > "Since these estimates are based on plane-of-sky magnetic field measurements and do not account for the line-of-sight component, they should be considered upper limits." (Dynamical Role of Magnetic Fields in the HH 211 System)
- Limitation (authors): Infall in the filaments needs confirmation with high-resolution spectral line observations  
  > "though this should be confirmed with high-resolution spectral line observations." (Temperature and Density Distributions)
- Limitation (authors): Models were not aimed at best-fit parameters  
  > "The goal of our modeling is not to derive the best model parameters for the HH 211 envelope." (Modeling the HH 211 envelope)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties are given on the temperature, column density, stream mass or infall rate, and the beta, kappa and gas-to-dust assumptions dominate them
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single constant beta = 1.5 and optically thin plus line-of-sight averaging may bias the T and N maps, notably near the disk
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CMU model neglects magnetic fields yet is used to argue the fields are dragged by infall; trajectory selection is a visual match to a 2D projection
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Whether the Band 3 and Band 6 maps share the same resolved-out scales after uv matching is not demonstrated numerically; Band 3 field vectors use a lax PI > 1.5 sigma cut
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 90 deg rotation assumes B-aligned grains; Band 6 polarization may include self-scattering or radiative alignment effects not tested
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stream region boundary is hand-defined, so mass and infall rate depend on that choice
