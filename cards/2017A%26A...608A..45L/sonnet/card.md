## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure ALMA 233 GHz flux densities**: Point-source visibility fitting in each of four spectral windows, interpolated to 233 GHz, combined quadratically with calibrator scale uncertainty  
  > "The latter was quadratically combined with the uncertainty on the flux calibrator scale." (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pallas flux derived from J0006-0623 compared with thermal model (159±11 vs 153.7 mJy)  
  > "we derive a Pallas flux of 159$\pm$11 mJy for March 26, 2016, UT =14h40." (Absolute flux calibration)
2. **Derive calibrator fluxes at 233 GHz**: Combine ALMA/SMA monitoring and spectral-index interpolation from Bands 3 and 7  
  > "we combined this information with (i) $\sim$225 GHz SMA measurements and (ii) flux values in ALMA Band 3 and Band 7 and the associated spectral indices." (Absolute flux calibration)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Spherical NEATM fits to Spitzer/Herschel PACS/WISE data**: Fit diameter, albedo and beaming factor; apply to ALMA/SPIRE to get relative emissivity eps_lambda/eps_b  
  > "The flux measurements from ALMA and SPIRE were then fit to derive the relative spectral emissivity (i.e. $\epsilon_\lambda$ / $\epsilon_b$)" (Spherical NEATM fits)
   - software: NEATM
   - assumption: eps_b Gaussian mean 0.90, rms 0.06; q vs p_v of Brucker et al. with factor 2 variation; SPIRE excluded from fit  
  > "here implemented as a gaussian distribution of $\epsilon_b$ with mean and rms values of 0.90 and 0.06 respectively" (Spherical NEATM fits)
   - fitting: Synthetic flux datasets and input parameter draws  
  > "generating multiple datasets of synthetic fluxes to be fitted and of the input model parameters" (Spherical NEATM fits)
   - validation: Redetermined diameters of Brown & Butler objects consistent with their values  
  > "all diameter central values and uncertainties are fully consistent" (Spherical NEATM fits)
4. **Thermophysical and non-spherical (elliptical NEATM) models with rings for Chariklo, Chiron, Bienor**: TPM with four roughness levels; elliptical NEATM with scaling factor; ring thermal model subtracted from fluxes  
  > "we re-fitted the Chariklo thermal data with non-spherical models." (Chariklo: more detailed modelling)
   - parameter: pole orientation assumed equal to ring pole; Jacobi ellipsoid adopted for Chariklo  
  > "we prefer here the Jacobi ellipsoid as providing the best agreement to the (cite: jewitt98) flux; in what follows, this shape model is adopted." (Shape effects)
   - assumption: ring particle emissivity = 1, slow rotators f=2  
  > "we nominally assumed $\epsilon_{r,b}$ = $\epsilon_{r,\lambda}$ = 1." (Ring emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked against Jewitt & Kalas 20.3 um flux and H_v/lightcurve data  
  > "All of these values agree reasonably well with the measured flux (66$\pm$12 mJy)" (Shape effects)
5. **Makemake multi-component models and Pluto/Charon and other literature objects**: Makemake-alone, +moon, +dust NEATM with fixed occultation diameter; 1- and 2-component Pluto/Charon; Varuna and 1999 TC36 reanalysis  
  > "we considered three end-member models, termed “Makemake-alone", “Makemake + moon", and “Makemake + dust"." (Makemake)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Search for emissivity trends and recommend mean emissivity**: Spearman rank correlations of emissivity vs D, p_v, eta, T_ss, color, f_water; median/mean statistics  
  > "a simple Spearman rank correlation analysis (Table [2](#emissivity)), reveal no significant correlation of the emissivity with any of these five parameters" (Discussion and conclusions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- All objects except Makemake have radio emissivities significantly below unity; no significant correlation with diameter, albedo, beaming factor, color; emissivity possibly tied to grain size.  
  > "all the objects, except Makemake, have radio emissivities significantly lower than unity." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Combining mm flux with far-IR fits separates emissivity from size  
  > "The essential approach to determine the radio emissivity of the observed TNOs / Centaurs is to combine the modelling of their mm/submm flux with previous measurements from far-IR facilities" (Modelling)
- Alternative: Generic triaxial Chariklo shape (favored by Leiva et al.) rejected in favor of Jacobi ellipsoid  
  > "we prefer here the Jacobi ellipsoid as providing the best agreement to the (cite: jewitt98) flux" (Shape effects)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Ring conclusions sensitive to assumption of no water-ice scattering roll-off; Bienor pole unconfirmed; Varuna emissivities tentative  
  > "This conclusion is however sensitive to our assumption that the long wavelength emission of Chariklo’s rings is not affected by scattering due to water ice." (Ring emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Calibrator flux scale dominates errors for several targets and relies on interpolated quasar fluxes
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Shape/pole models uncertain for Chiron and Bienor
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small sample (18 values) for correlation tests
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Emissivity trend with grain size is speculative
