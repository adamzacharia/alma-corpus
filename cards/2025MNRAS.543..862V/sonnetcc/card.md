## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate NOEMA 100 GHz data**: CLIC standard pipeline in GILDAS with manual inspection and re-runs, per observing date; output as UV-tables per target per side band  
  > "We calibrated the data using the CONTINUUM AND LINE INTERFEROMETRIC CALIBRATION (CLIC) software within the GILDAS package (version Jan 2025)" (NOEMA observations); "The pipeline output was manually inspected and combined with observing log information to prepare and optimize re-runs of the pipeline, until satisfactory calibration was obtained." (NOEMA observations)
   - software: GILDAS (CLIC)
   - parameter: Calibration performed separately per observing date; baseline-based bandpass and amplitude for A configuration  
  > "Calibration was performed separately per observing date" (NOEMA observations); "acceptable calibration required switching from antenna to baseline-based configuration for bandpass and amplitude" (NOEMA observations)
   - parameter: Data dropped: 2024 March 19 segments 2 and 3 and all of 2024 March 14  
  > "Similarly, issues with the bandpass calibration prevented the use of data collected on 2024 March 14." (NOEMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image NOEMA data and measure 100 GHz flux densities**: Average UV-tables, clean, measure RMS, fit a point source in the UV plane at the phase centre, merge sidebands for final flux  
  > "we fitted a point source model in the UV plane with initial position estimated at the phase centre" (NOEMA observations); "we merge the side-band UV-tables using UV_MERGE for a final RMS measurement and flux density measurement for detected sources" (NOEMA observations)
   - software: GILDAS (MAPPING, UV_CONT, UV_MERGE, GO NOISE)
   - parameter: Clean region twice the primary beam FWHM, down to 2x expected RMS (2.5x with A configuration)  
  > "we frequency-averaged the UV-tables using UV_CONT and cleaned across a region with size twice the full-width-half-maximum of the primary beam" (NOEMA observations); "down to a flux density level of 2 times the expected RMS noise (2.5 times for data including A configuration)" (NOEMA observations)
   - parameter: UV-plane point-source fit is the formal detection criterion; 3 sigma upper limits for non-detections  
  > "we use the UV fit outcome as the formal assessment of detection" (NOEMA observations); "and the three sigma upper limit for the two non-detected targets" (NOEMA observations)
   - fitting: Point source in the UV plane, position initially at the phase centre (small offset for 4U 1907+09)  
  > "we fitted a point source model in the UV plane with initial position estimated at the phase centre" (NOEMA observations)
   - validation: Per-sideband detection checked before combining the sidebands  
  > "we first performed the analysis per side band, to assess whether the target was significantly detected in each band" (NOEMA observations)
3. **Measure ALMA flux densities from pipeline images**: Import pipeline FITS images into CASA, fit a 2D Gaussian with beam-fixed shape in the image plane, repeat on four sub-bands for detections  
  > "Those images were subsequently fitted with a 2D Gaussian point source in the image plane" (ALMA observations); "shape to the major and minor axes and position angle of the synthesized beam" (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA v.6.6.5.31 (importfits), ALMA pipeline
   - parameter: RMS in annulus of 15-30 pixels; detection if flux at least 3x local RMS  
  > "We measured the RMS noise of each image in an annulus with inner and outer radii of 15 and 30 pixels, respectively, around the phase centre, thereby avoiding target emission." (ALMA observations); "We regard any flux density at least three times the surrounding image RMS as a detection." (ALMA observations)
   - parameter: 4U 1907+09 (offset from phase centre) measured manually at the source position in the 40 and 150 GHz images  
  > "For the two ALMA images, we manually measure the flux density at the offset source position" (Millimeter and radio imaging)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fixed to the synthesized beam shape (point source)  
  > "Those images were subsequently fitted with a 2D Gaussian point source in the image plane" (ALMA observations)
   - validation: Full-band detections were also detected in each of the four sub-bands  
  > "In all cases, a significant source detection in the full band translated to detections in each of the four sub-bands." (ALMA observations)
4. **Calibrate, image and measure VLA 6 and 10 GHz flux densities**: Manual CASA calibration, tclean at each frequency, imfit of a beam-shaped Gaussian; 3 sigma limits otherwise  
  > "If a radio counterpart was detected at the known source position, we used IMFIT to measure its flux density by fitting a 2D Gaussian with full-width-half-maxima and orientation fixed to the synthesized beam" (Radio observations); "we calculate the RMS across the source position and report the $3\sigma$ upper limit on the flux density" (Radio observations)
   - software: CASA v.6.6.5.31 (tclean, imfit)
   - parameter: Robustness varied per target and band to optimise sensitivity vs sidelobes  
  > "varying the robustness parameter to optimize the trade-off between sensitivity and the effect of side-lobes and imaging artifacts" (Radio observations)
   - parameter: Flux error from RMS in a nearby source-free region  
  > "We calculated the flux density error as the RMS variability across a close-by region devoid of sources." (Radio observations)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian with fixed beam shape (imfit)  
  > "by fitting a 2D Gaussian with full-width-half-maxima and orientation fixed to the synthesized beam" (Radio observations)
   - validation: Two bad datasets (6 GHz IGR J18410-0535, 10 GHz 4U 1700-37) excluded from further analysis; cross-check against radio surveys  
  > "In two cases, observational issues strongly affect the quality of the data to the level where we do not use their results in the further analysis." (Radio observations)
5. **Compute specific luminosities**: L_nu = 4 pi F_nu D^2 with errors propagated from flux and distance  
  > "we use the observed flux density (limit) and distance to calculate the specific luminosity, defined as $L_\nu = 4\pi F_\nu D^2$" (Millimeter and radio imaging); "We calculate its error by propagating both flux density and distance errors" (Millimeter and radio imaging)
   - assumption: Distances from Table 1; 6.9 +/- 1.7 kpc adopted for IGR J18410-0535  
  > "we adopt the $6.9\pm1.7$ kpc out of the potential range" (footnote)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit power-law SEDs**: MCMC fit of S = xi S0 (nu/nu0)^alpha; three versions (ALMA full bands, ALMA sub-bands, ALMA+VLA); 4U 1907+09 uses ALMA 40+150 GHz and NOEMA 100 GHz  
  > "We attempt to fit the low-frequency millimeter or radio + millimeter SEDs of our targets with a power law model $S_\nu = \xi S_0 (\nu / \nu_0)^{\alpha}$." (Low-frequency SED fitting); "We perform three versions of the SED fit: firstly, we fit the ALMA SEDs using the three full ALMA bands, followed by a fit to the ALMA SEDs using the three times four sub-bands as a consistency check." (Low-frequency SED fitting)
   - software: emcee (cited in acknowledgements)
   - parameter: S0 = 1 mJy, nu0 = 121.6 GHz (log-mean of the three ALMA centroid frequencies)  
  > "we set $S_0 = 1$ mJy and $\nu_0 = 121.6$ GHz, equal to the mean of the three ALMA centroid frequencies in log space" (Low-frequency SED fitting)
   - parameter: Parameter estimates reported as 16/84th percentile intervals  
  > "we report the resulting parameter estimates and their $16/84^{\rm th}$ percentile confidence intervals" (Low-frequency SED fitting)
   - assumption: Single power law; 4U 1700-37 10 GHz tentative point (207 +/- 87 microJy) shown but excluded from fits; NOEMA two-sub-band indices not used  
  > "but do not use in further SED fitting or interpreting the broad-band spectrum" (Radio observations)
   - fitting: MCMC with flat priors on alpha and log xi; models above a 3 sigma upper limit get zero probability (-inf log probability). Sampler name not stated for the fit; EMCEE appears in the acknowledgements  
  > "We assume flat priors on the spectral index $\alpha$ and on the logarithm of the normalization parameter, $\log \xi$." (Low-frequency SED fitting); "We take non-detections into account by assigning a zero probability (or, in the fitting routine, a minus infinity log probability) to any model that predicts a flux above a $3\sigma$ upper limit." (Low-frequency SED fitting); "EMCEE (cite: emcee2013)" (Acknowledgments)
   - validation: Sub-band fit as consistency check; fits with and without VLA compared  
  > "using the three times four sub-bands as a consistency check" (Low-frequency SED fitting)
7. **Search for short-term variability and an RRL; assess long-term variability**: Light curves from uv-plane fits at 10 s and 1 min, constant-fit reduced chi-square, minimum detectable fractional variability; epoch-to-epoch comparison across campaigns  
  > "We searched the ALMA observations in each band where the target was detected at $>3\sigma$ significance (e.g., excluding 4U 1907+09) using the UVFIT tool in CASA and the calibrated measurement set." (Millimeter and radio imaging); "We measured the target light curves at both a 10 second and 1 minute time resolution, but did not find evidence for flaring or slower flux variations throughout the observations." (Millimeter and radio imaging)
   - software: CASA v.6.6.5.31 (uvfit)
   - parameter: Light curves in 1-minute bins assessed with reduced chi-square of a constant fit  
  > "we can calculate the reduced $\chi^2$ of a constant fit to the $1$-minute light curve as a first assessment of variability" (Variability in low-frequency stellar wind emission)
   - assumption: Constant source; per-bin uncertainty scales with sqrt(N)  
  > "Further assuming that the uncertainty in each time bin scales with $\sqrt{N}$" (Variability in low-frequency stellar wind emission)
   - fitting: Constant model per light curve  
  > "we can calculate the reduced $\chi^2$ of a constant fit to the $1$-minute light curve as a first assessment of variability" (Variability in low-frequency stellar wind emission)

## Qualitative findings

- No radio detection of any of the three VLA-observed SFXTs; SFXTs remain radio undetected  
  > "While our VLA observations are, to our knowledge, the deepest constraints on SFXT radio emission to date, this source class remains completely radio undetected." (Millimeter and radio imaging)
- No radio recombination line at 40.630 GHz in any target  
  > "we found no evidence for an RRL around 40.630 GHz in the ALMA Band 1 pipeline spectrum of any target" (Millimeter and radio imaging)
- IGR J17544-2619 full-band SED does not follow a single power law; possible turnover where the photosphere becomes smaller than the star  
  > "we may be observing the transition frequency, where the photosphere becomes smaller than the stellar radius, and the resulting flattening of the spectrum between the 40 and 100 GHz bands" (Low-frequency SED fitting)
- No short-timescale flaring in ALMA light curves  
  > "but did not find evidence for flaring or slower flux variations throughout the observations" (Millimeter and radio imaging)
- Factor of about 2 flux changes between epochs for IGR J18410-0535 and 4U 1700-37, taken as intrinsic  
  > "such a factor $\sim 2$ variation appears intrinsic to the source" (Variability in low-frequency stellar wind emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sample chosen mainly on NOEMA/ALMA observability and distance; ALMA exposures set to optimise sensitivity at the lowest frequencies where the wind spectrum is faintest  
  > "has primarily been constructed based on observability with NOEMA / ALMA and distance" (Targets); "The exposure times were set up to optimize sensitivity at the lower frequencies, where an inverted stellar-wind type spectrum is expected to be faintest." (ALMA observations)
- Why this method: Millimeter band is where the wind is more likely to dominate because of its strongly inverted spectrum  
  > "Millimeter observations, where the stellar wind is more likely to dominate due to its strongly inverted spectrum, are therefore crucial." (Introduction)
- Alternative: 6 GHz IGR J18410-0535 and 10 GHz 4U 1700-37 VLA data excluded from the analysis because of artifacts and phase-calibration problems  
  > "In two cases, observational issues strongly affect the quality of the data to the level where we do not use their results in the further analysis." (Radio observations)
- Alternative: NOEMA two-sub-band spectral indices not used because of large uncertainties  
  > "we do not use these measurements further in our interpretation of the SED shapes" (Low-frequency SED fitting)
- Alternative: NOEMA data of 2024 March 14 and part of 2024 March 19 dropped for calibration problems  
  > "Similarly, issues with the bandpass calibration prevented the use of data collected on 2024 March 14." (NOEMA observations)
- How they decided it was enough: Detection threshold of 3 times local RMS; 4U 1907+09 claimed on a faint point source at the right position in both ALMA images plus a NOEMA detection near 4 sigma  
  > "We regard any flux density at least three times the surrounding image RMS as a detection." (ALMA observations); "as sufficient evidence to claim a source detection" (Millimeter and radio imaging)
- Limitation (authors): Jet-vs-wind estimate is order-of-magnitude only  
  > "We emphasize that each of these steps in the calculation of the jet luminosity comes with statistical and systematic uncertainties, and therefore this comparison should be interpreted only at the order of magnitude level." (The origin of radio-millimeter emission)
- Limitation (authors): Extrapolated millimeter limits for non-detected SgXBs are not constraining relative to the SFXT range  
  > "the (extrapolated) millimeter upper limits for non-detected SgXBs are not constraining, currently, compared to the SFXT luminosity range" (Stellar wind properties in SgXBs and SFXTs)
- Limitation (authors): 4U 1907+09 was offset from the phase centre, raising the local RMS and making the ALMA detections formally insignificant  
  > "the offset from the phase centre increases the local RMS" (Millimeter and radio imaging)
- Limitation (authors): Small number of measurements prevents detailed variability analysis  
  > "The small number of measurements of the low-frequency SgXB emission in this work, prevents a more detailed analysis" (Variability in low-frequency stellar wind emission)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic uncertainties of the literature wind methods likely exceed the quoted errors  
  > "systematic uncertainties in the underlying method likely introduce more significant errors" (How do the millimeter properties compare with literature estimates?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several SFXT vs SgXB conclusions rest on four SFXTs and on distances with large Gaia errors (L_nu scales with D squared)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Variability claims compare different instruments and epochs with a 10 percent flux-scale systematic and no uncertainty model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): NOEMA and ALMA calibrators and ALMA pipeline versions are not stated, limiting independent checks
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Which sampler produced the SED fits is only implied through the acknowledgements
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Millimeter-to-100 GHz rescaling uses the fitted alpha, so the Mdot/v_inf proxy carries that uncertainty and a fixed temperature assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Names for some rows in the flux table are lost in this text conversion, so per-source values need to be checked against the published table
