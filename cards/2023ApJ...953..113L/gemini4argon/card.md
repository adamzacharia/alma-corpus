## Analysis recipe

1. **Resample sub-mm maps and apply polarimetric quality cuts**: Resample JCMT/POL-2 (to 7.5'') and ALMA (to 0.15'') Stokes maps to Nyquist sampling, rotate polarization angles by 90 deg to obtain B-field orientation, and filter pixels by SNR and polarization fraction thresholds.  
  > "We resampled the data at a pixel scale of $7.5\ensuremath{''}$ (i.e., Nyquist sampling)." (Galaxy sample and archival data); "We resampled the data at a pixel scale of $0.15\ensuremath{''}$ (i.e., Nyquist sampling)." (Galaxy sample and archival data); "Only polarization measurements with $PI/\sigma_{\rm{PI}} \ge 2$, $P\le 20$%, and $I/\sigma_{\rm{I}} \ge 60$ are displayed, where $\sigma_{\rm{PI}}$ and $\sigma_{\rm{I}}$ are the uncertainties of the polarized, $PI$, and total, $I$, intensities, respectively." (Galaxy sample and archival data)
   - software: astropy
   - parameter: Quality cuts: PI/sigma_PI >= 2, P <= 20%, I/sigma_I >= 60  
  > "1\. We select the polarization measurements using the following quality cuts: $PI/\sigma_{\rm{PI}} \ge 2$, $P\le 20$%, and $I/\sigma_{\rm{I}} \ge 60$." (3.2 Disentangle the disk and outflow components)
   - parameter: Resampled pixel scales: 7.5'' (JCMT 850 um), 0.15'' (ALMA 890 um)  
  > "We resampled the data at a pixel scale of $7.5\ensuremath{''}$ (i.e., Nyquist sampling)." (Galaxy sample and archival data); "We resampled the data at a pixel scale of $0.15\ensuremath{''}$ (i.e., Nyquist sampling)." (Galaxy sample and archival data)
   - assumption: Observed FIR/sub-mm polarization angle rotated by 90 deg traces the local B-field orientation  
  > "For polarized thermal emission observed at far-infrared (FIR) and sub-mm wavelengths, the observed position angle ($PA$) of polarization traces the orientation of the local B-field after a $90^{\circ}$ rotation" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Disentangle disk and outflow components geometrically**: Fit Gaussian profiles to Stokes Q and U distributions at 89 um, compute the B-field orientation range PA_B,+/- from the fitted means and standard deviations, compare angular differences with the galaxy tilt angle theta, and assign the larger angular difference to the outflow and smaller to the disk across all wavelengths.  
  > "2\. We fit the distribution of measurements of the Stokes $Q$ and $U$ of each galaxy using a Gaussian profile with two free parameters: the mean and standard deviation, $\sigma$." (3.2 Disentangle the disk and outflow components); "5\. Finally, we select the larger angular difference, $\Delta B_{1}$ or $\Delta B_{2}$, as the outflow region and the smaller as the disk region." (3.2 Disentangle the disk and outflow components); "We use the range of B-field orientation at $89$ $\mu$m to separate the outflow and disk range at all wavelengths." (Results)
   - parameter: Reference wavelength for separation: 89 um  
  > "Given the complexity of the B-field orientation at some wavelengths, we use the observations at the shorter wavelength common for all starbursts, i.e., $89$ $\mu$m." (Results)
   - parameter: Galaxy tilt angles theta: 64 +/- 1 deg (M82), 52 +/- 1 deg (NGC 253), 140 +/- 2 deg (NGC 2146)  
  > "| M 82 | $3.85$ | $18.49$ | $90$–$337$ | I0/Sbrst | $76\pm1$ | $64\pm1$ | $13$ | $^{1}$(cite: Vacca2015); $^{2}$(cite: Mayya2005); $^{3}$(cite: FS2003) |" (Table 1); "| NGC 253 | $3.50$ | $16.81$ | $5$–$229$ | SAB(s)c/Sbrst | $78.3\pm1.0$ | $52\pm1$ | $3$ | $^{1}$(cite: RS2011); $^{2}$(cite: Lucero2015); $^{3}$(cite: Bolatto2013) |" (Table 1); "| NGC 2146 | $17.20$ | $82.61$ | $401$–$1503$ | SB(s)ab/Sbrst | $63\pm2$ | $140\pm2$ | $20$ | $^{1}$(cite: Tully1988); $^{2}$(cite: Tarchi2004); $^{3}$(cite: Gorski2018) |" (Table 1)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile with two free parameters: mean and standard deviation sigma fitted to Stokes Q and U distributions  
  > "2\. We fit the distribution of measurements of the Stokes $Q$ and $U$ of each galaxy using a Gaussian profile with two free parameters: the mean and standard deviation, $\sigma$." (3.2 Disentangle the disk and outflow components)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compute resolved and integrated polarization fractions and polarization spectra**: Compute debiased mean polarization fraction <P^hist> (Eq. 1) and integrated polarization fraction P^int (Eq. 2) for full galaxy, disk, and outflow regions per band, and normalize polarization spectra to 154 um.  
  > "$$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Phist}
\langle P^{\rm{hist}} \rangle = \frac{\sqrt{\langle Q^2 + U^2 - \sigma_{Q}\sigma_{U}\rangle}}{\langle I \rangle}
\end{equation}$$" (3.1 Polarization measurements); "$$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pint}
P^{\rm{int}} = \frac{\sqrt{\langle Q \rangle^2 + \langle U \rangle^2 - \langle \sigma_{Q} \rangle \langle \sigma_{U} \rangle}}{\langle I \rangle}
\end{equation}$$" (3.1 Polarization measurements); "This figure also shows the normalized spectrum, $P^{\rm{int}}/P^{\rm{int}}_{\lambda_{0}}$, to the reference wavelength of $\lambda_{0}= 154$ $\mu$m for each region (full galaxy, disk, and outflow) of the starburst galaxies." (4.3 The polarization spectra of the disk, outflow, and full starburst galaxy)
   - parameter: Reference wavelength lambda_0 = 154 um for normalized polarization spectra  
  > "This figure also shows the normalized spectrum, $P^{\rm{int}}/P^{\rm{int}}_{\lambda_{0}}$, to the reference wavelength of $\lambda_{0}= 154$ $\mu$m for each region (full galaxy, disk, and outflow) of the starburst galaxies." (4.3 The polarization spectra of the disk, outflow, and full starburst galaxy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirmed that normalized polarization spectrum of <P^hist> is similar to that of P^int  
  > "We find that the normalized polarization spectrum of $\langle P^{\rm{hist}} \rangle$ is similar to that of the $P^{\rm{int}}$." (4.3 The polarization spectra of the disk, outflow, and full starburst galaxy)
4. **Fit total and polarized flux SEDs**: Sum Stokes I and PI over full galaxy, disk, and outflow regions and fit single-temperature modified blackbody functions in the 50-250 um wavelength range for M82 and NGC 2146 using MCMC.  
  > "The $50-250$ $\mu$m total and polarized SEDs are characterized using a single-temperature modified blackbody function expressed as" (3.3 Total and Polarized SED Fitting); "The sub-mm photometric measurements were not included in the fitting because the large-scale fluxes are missing in both JCMT and ALMA observations." (4.1 Total flux SEDs); "We do not perform the fitting procedure in NGC 253 because this galaxy only has two measurements in the $50-250$ $\mu$m wavelength range." (4.1 Total flux SEDs)
   - software: pymc3
   - parameter: Fitted wavelength range: 50-250 um  
  > "We have three free model parameters: $M_{\rm d}$, $\beta$, and $T_{\rm d}$ that we fit within the $50-250$ $\mu$m wavelength range." (3.3 Total and Polarized SED Fitting)
   - assumption: Single-temperature modified blackbody with dust mass absorption coefficient k_lambda0 = 0.29 m^2 kg^-1 at lambda_0 = 250 um  
  > "$k_{\lambda_{0}}$ is the dust mass absorption coefficient to be $0.29$ m$^{2}$ kg$^{-1}$ at a wavelength of $\lambda_{0} = 250$ $\mu$m (cite: Wiebe2009)" (3.3 Total and Polarized SED Fitting)
   - fitting: MCMC using No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS) in PYMC3 with 5 chains, 10,000 steps and 2,000 burn-in per chain (50,000 steps total); flat priors: log10(M_d [M_sun]) = [2, 9], beta = [1, 3], T_d = [10, 100] K  
  > "We compute a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach using the No-U-Turn Sampler (cite: NUTS) method in the PYTHON code PYMC3 (cite: pymc)." (3.3 Total and Polarized SED Fitting); "The prior distributions are set to flat within the range of $\log_{10}(M_{\rm{d}} [M_{\odot}]) = [2,9]$, $\beta = [1,3]$, and $T_{\rm d} = [10,100]$ K." (3.3 Total and Polarized SED Fitting); "We run the code using 5 chains with 10,000 steps and a 2,000 burn-in per chain, which provides 50,000 steps for the full MCMC code useful for data analysis." (3.3 Total and Polarized SED Fitting)
   - validation: Checked extrapolation of 50-250 um M82 polarized SED fit against the un-fitted 850 um JCMT/POL-2 polarized flux measurement  
  > "Note that the best fit to the polarized SED of M 82 also reproduces the $850$ $\mu$m polarized measurement of the outflow, although the $850$ $\mu$m was not used for the fitting procedure." (4.2 Polarized flux SEDs)
5. **Analyze vertical height dependence across the outflow and separate inner/outer outflow spectra**: Construct 2D vertical height maps rotated by galaxy tilt angle theta centered on the emission peak at each wavelength, extract Stokes IQU in 2-pixel-wide slots (1 beam), compute vertical profiles and power-law slopes of I, PI, and P^int, and compute separate polarization spectra for inner outflow (within h_peak^PI) and outer outflow (beyond h_peak^PI), comparing them with literature dust models via reduced chi-squared.  
  > "1\. We construct a two-dimensional map of vertical heights from the plane of the galaxy. We define the map to be centered at the peak emission at each wavelength and rotate the map on the plane of the sky using the tilt, $\theta$, of the galaxy (Table [1](#tab:table1))." (4.4 Outflow: vertical height dependence); "2\. We select the Stokes $IQU$ as a function of the vertical height in slots of two pixels wide." (4.4 Outflow: vertical height dependence); "Based on the results from Section [4.4](#subsec:RES_r), we now compute the polarization spectrum of the inner outflow as the integrated polarization within the maximum vertical height of the polarized flux (Table [\[tab:table1App\]](#tab:table1App))." (4.5 Polarization spectra across the outflow)
   - parameter: Slot width: 2 pixels (1 beam FWHM)  
  > "The width of two pixels corresponds to the beam size of the observations, which provides the minimum size at which each measurement is statistically independent as a function of the vertical height." (4.4 Outflow: vertical height dependence)
   - assumption: Galaxy disk lies on the x-y plane and is rotated by tilt angle theta along the z-axis  
  > "The map is rotated as $h^{\prime} = R_{z}[\theta]h$, where $h$ is the original vertical height map, $h^{\prime}$ is the new vertical height, and $R_{z}[\theta]$ is the rotation matrix for the tilt angle along the $z$-axis assuming that the galaxy’s disk is on the $x-y$ plane." (4.4 Outflow: vertical height dependence)
   - fitting: Reduced chi-squared comparison with dust models: chi_nu^2 = [(obs - model)^2 / model^2] / (N - 1)  
  > "We estimate the reduced $\chi_{\nu}^{2}$ to compare the measured polarization spectra with the dust models, where $\chi_{\nu}^{2} = [(obs-model)^{2}/model^{2}]/(N-1)$ with $N$ the number of measurements." (5.1 Polarization spectrum of starburst galaxies)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Estimate turbulent B-field strengths and energy budget in the outflow**: Scale turbulent B-field strength with SFR using B = B_0 * SFR^(0.34+/-0.04) calibrated on M82, then compute turbulent kinetic energy U_K = 0.5 * rho * sigma_v^2, turbulent magnetic energy U_B = B^2 / (8*pi), and plasma parameter beta' = U_K / U_B.  
  > "We estimate the expected turbulent B-field strength of the starburst galaxies in our sample using the $B\,=\,B_{\rm{0}}\,SFR^{0.34\pm0.04}$ relation." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "$$\begin{eqnarray}
    U_{\rm{K}} &=& \frac{1}{2} \rho \sigma_{\rm{v}}^{2} \\
    U_{\rm{B}} &=& \frac{B^{2}}{8\pi}
\end{eqnarray}$$" (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "The volume density was estimated as $\rho=\Sigma_{\rm{g}}/h_{\rm{c}}$, where $\Sigma_{\rm{g}}$ is the surface gas density and $h_{\rm{c}}$ is the depth of the starburst region." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)
   - parameter: B_0 = 128 uG M_sun^-0.34 yr^-0.34, calibrated from B_M82 = 305 +/- 5 uG and SFR_M82 = 13 M_sun yr^-1  
  > "Using M82’s result and $SFR_{M82}\,=\,13$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ (Table [1](#tab:table1)), the scaling factor is $B_{\rm{0}}\,=\,128\,\mu$G M$_{\odot}^{-0.34}$ yr$^{-0.34}$." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)
   - parameter: Core depth h_c: 500 pc (M82), 50 pc (NGC 253), 2000 pc (NGC 2146)  
  > "| M82 | 305$^{\dagger\dagger}$ | 500 | 0.69 | 66.6 | 3.70 | 9.92 | 2.68 | $^{(1)}$(cite: Leroy2015) |" (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "| NGC 253 | 186 | 50 | 0.47 | 50 | 1.35 | 38.1 | 28.2 | $^{(1)}$(cite: Krieger2019) |" (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "| NGC 2146 | 354 | 2000 | 0.12 | 250 | 4.93 | 7.08 | 1.43 | $^{(1)}$(cite: Kreckel2014) |" (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)
   - assumption: Turbulent B-field scales as B propto SFR^(0.34+/-0.04) and starburst region depth h_c equals the length of the polarized region along the major axis of the galaxy  
  > "We estimate the expected turbulent B-field strength of the starburst galaxies in our sample using the $B\,=\,B_{\rm{0}}\,SFR^{0.34\pm0.04}$ relation." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "we assume that the depth of the starburst region of each galaxy is isotropically distributed in a disk with a depth equal to the length of the polarized region along the major axis of the galaxy from our observations (Figure [3](#fig:fig2))." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared expected turbulent B-field strengths against revised cosmic-ray equipartition estimates from Lacki & Beck (2013) and resolved M82 analysis from Lopez-Rodriguez et al. (2021)  
  > "These expected B-field strengths are in good agreement with the revised equipartition between B-fields and cosmic rays taking into account energy losses due to strong B-fields in a dense medium put forward by (cite: LB2013)." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "This is evident in the estimated $\beta^{\prime} = 2.68$ in Table [\[tab:table3\]](#tab:table3) and the estimated $\beta^{\prime}=0.56\pm0.23$ using resolved observations and the more sophisticated approach to estimate the B-field strengths of M 82." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)

## Qualitative findings

- In the ALMA 890 um observations of NGC 253 at 5 pc resolution, the starburst region exhibits a filamentary B-field structure parallel to the galaxy disk connecting the star-forming clusters, while the B-field within the star-forming clusters is perpendicular to the disk.  
  > "The starburst region has a filamentary B-field structure parallel to the disk of the galaxy connecting the star-forming clusters, whereas the B-field in the star-forming clusters is perpendicular to the disk (Figure [14](#fig:fig13))." (5.3 Observational strategies)
- Polarized SEDs of the full galaxies are dominated by the outflow component, allowing disk- and outflow-dominated galaxies to be distinguished better with polarized SEDs than with total flux SEDs.  
  > "We find that the polarized spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of the full galaxies are dominated by the polarized SEDs of the outflows with dust temperatures of $T_{\rm{d,outflow}}^{\rm{PI}}\sim45$ K and emissive index of $\beta_{\rm{outflow}}^{\rm{PI}}\sim2.3$." (Abstract); "We show that disk- and outflow-dominated galaxies can be better distinguished by using polarized SEDs instead of total SEDs." (Abstract)
- The polarization spectrum of the inner outflow cannot be reproduced by existing single- or two-component dust models of the diffuse ISM or dense clouds, indicating large dust temperature gradients.  
  > "The polarization spectrum of the inner outflow cannot be reproduced by any of the dust models of the diffuse ISM or dense clouds." (5.1 Polarization spectrum of starburst galaxies); "The steep decrease of the polarization spectrum in the $50-154$ $\mu$m indicates large dust temperature gradients." (5.1 Polarization spectrum of starburst galaxies)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Optical/NIR polarimetry is contaminated by dust/electron scattering and radio polarimetry suffers from Faraday rotation and short cosmic-ray lifetimes, whereas 53-890 um polarimetry traces thermal emission from magnetically aligned dust grains in the cold, dense phase of galactic outflows.  
  > "Because dust scattering dominates at these wavelengths, B-fields along the galactic outflows in starburst galaxies are therefore very challenging to study using optical/NIR polarimetric observations." (Introduction); "Because radio polarimetric observations suffer from Faraday rotation and a short CR lifetime, the B-fields in the galactic outflows are very challenging to characterize." (Introduction); "The $50$–$850$ $\mu$m polarimetric observations presented here have shown to be excellent tracers of the B-fields in the outflows of starburst galaxies." (5.3 Observational strategies)
- Why this method: A geometric analysis of the 89 um Stokes Q and U distributions was chosen to separate disk and outflow components because 89 um is the shortest wavelength common to all three galaxies and clearly shows dominant outflow or disk B-field orientations; normalized polarization spectra at 154 um minimize opacity, LOS, and POS B-field variation effects.  
  > "Given the complexity of the B-field orientation at some wavelengths, we use the observations at the shorter wavelength common for all starbursts, i.e., $89$ $\mu$m." (Results); "The normalized polarization spectra minimize the effects of opacity, LOS, and POS variations of the measured B-field orientations (cite: H1999)." (4.3 The polarization spectra of the disk, outflow, and full starburst galaxy)
- Alternative: Including sub-mm (JCMT 850 um and ALMA 890 um) photometric points in the modified blackbody SED fits was rejected because missing large-scale flux and mismatched resolution/field-of-view cause the fit to yield unphysical beta > 3.  
  > "The sub-mm photometric measurements were not included in the fitting because the large-scale fluxes are missing in both JCMT and ALMA observations." (4.1 Total flux SEDs); "If the sub-mm observations are included the modified blackbody function tends to obtain $\beta>3$." (4.1 Total flux SEDs)
- Alternative: Fitting SEDs for NGC 253 or including the 214 um outflow point in NGC 2146 SED comparison was avoided/noted as biased because NGC 253 only has two 50-250 um bands and NGC 2146's outflow at 214 um is only detected in 2-3 independent beams.  
  > "We do not perform the fitting procedure in NGC 253 because this galaxy only has two measurements in the $50-250$ $\mu$m wavelength range." (4.1 Total flux SEDs); "The outflow region of NGC 2146 at $214$ $\mu$m is only detected by $2-3$ independent beams, which biases the polarized SED." (4.2 Polarized flux SEDs)
- Alternative: Including the ALMA 890 um measurement in the 50-850 um inner/outer outflow polarization spectrum characterization was excluded because the 0.3'' ALMA observations trace much smaller physical scales (5 pc) than the FIR/JCMT data.  
  > "As mentioned in Sections [4.1](#subsec:RES_I) and [4.3](#subsec:RES_P), the $890$ $\mu$m polarization measurement is not taken into account in this analysis because these observations trace different physical scales." (4.5 Polarization spectra across the outflow)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): JCMT/POL-2 and ALMA observations suffer from missing large-scale extended emission due to spatial filtering/interferometric mode, and ALMA covers a much smaller region (~150 pc at 0.3'' resolution) than SOFIA/HAWC+.  
  > "The sub-mm photometric measurements were not included in the fitting because the large-scale fluxes are missing in both JCMT and ALMA observations." (4.1 Total flux SEDs); "Although there may be an extended B-field in the ALMA polarimetric observations, this large-scale B-field component is lost due to the interferometric mode." (5.3 Observational strategies)
- Limitation (authors): Using the galaxy-integrated gas surface density concentrated within the outflow core overestimates the volume density and turbulent kinetic energy U_K.  
  > "Note that here we use the integrated surface density of the galaxy and assume that it is concentrated within the outflow region, which overestimates the volume density and therefore the turbulent kinetic energy." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)
- Limitation (authors): The energy budget and B-SFR scaling assume unscreened B-fields at large heights, apply a spiral-galaxy total-B vs SFR relation to turbulent B-fields in dense starbursts, and neglect that total B-field strength is 1.4-4.2 times larger than ordered B-field.  
  > "The B-field strength at large vertical heights is currently assumed to be unscreened. It is unknown how the geometry and strength of the B-fields propagate across the heterogenous distribution of gas and dust in the outflow." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "In addition, the total B-field strength is typically used in the relation between the SFR and the B-field strength, while here we used the turbulent B-field strength." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate); "Finally, the SFR and total B-field strength relation has been derived using spiral galaxies, which implies that this relationship has to be revisited due to energy losses in the dense ISM and strong B-fields found in starburst galaxies." (5.2 Polarized outflow and star formation rate)
- Limitation (authors): The 53-154 um polarization spectrum of the disk may be biased by strong radiation fields from the nearby base of the outflow and sparse independent beams (e.g., only three measurements at 53 um in NGC 2146).  
  > "The larger difference is found at $53$ $\mu$m by NGC 2146 because this galaxy only has three independent measurements associated with the disk." (4.3 The polarization spectra of the disk, outflow, and full starburst galaxy); "Based on these results, the $53$–$154$ $\mu$m polarization of the disk may be affected by the high polarization fraction from the strong radiation fields in the outflow producing a false-negative flat spectrum." (5.1 Polarization spectrum of starburst galaxies)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample size is only three galaxies (and only two, M82 and NGC 2146, have >= 3 bands for SED fitting), limiting statistical conclusions about SFR scaling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using a single 89 um PA_B threshold to classify pixels as disk or outflow across all wavelengths and resolutions (including 0.3'' ALMA data) may misclassify regions where field geometry twists or changes with scale.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Best-fit dust mass M_d values from the MCMC SED fits are not tabulated in the text or tables, only shown graphically on the SED plots.
