## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct the gamma-ray light curve**: Unbinned maximum likelihood; remove TS < 10 sources after an initial full-period fit, refit and generate weekly bins  
  > "following the standard unbinned likelihood procedure." (γ-ray data); "The second maximum-likelihood analysis was performed after removing the sources with TS $<$ 10." (γ-ray data); "Applying this result to the source model, we generated a weekly binned light curve for the entire period in this work." (γ-ray data)
   - software: SCIENCETOOLS, gtlike
   - parameter: SOURCE events: evclass = 128, evtype = 3; 100 MeV–300 GeV  
  > "We selected the SOURCE class events (`evclass = 128, evtype = 3`) at 100 MeV–300 GeV." (γ-ray data)
   - parameter: DATA_Qual>0 && LAT_CONFIG==1; maximum zenith angle 90 deg  
  > "we used the filter parameters ${\rm DATA\_Qual>0}~\&\&~{\rm LAT\_CONFIG==1}$." (γ-ray data); "We applied the maximum zenith angle of 90 $\deg$ to reduce contamination from Earth limb $\gamma$-rays." (γ-ray data)
   - parameter: ROI radius 15 deg; catalog sources within 30 deg; P8R3_SOURCE_V1  
  > "We defined a Region of Interest (ROI) of 15 $\deg$ radius centred at the location of CTA 102." (γ-ray data); "We used `P8R3_SOURCE_V1` as the instrument response function." (γ-ray data); "The source model included all sources from the fourth *Fermi* Large Area Telescope catalog (cite: Abdollahi+2020) that appeared within 30 deg of the centre of the ROI." (γ-ray data)
   - parameter: Exclude mid-March to early April 2018  
  > "We did not make use of the data from mid-March to early April 2018." (γ-ray data)
   - assumption: CTA 102 power-law spectrum; pivot energy 414 MeV  
  > "For CTA 102, the PL model was used" (γ-ray data); "We left the prefactor and the photon index free to vary and used the pivot energy $E_0 = 414$ MeV from 4FGL (cite: Abdollahi+2020)." (γ-ray data)
   - fitting: Maximum likelihood; CTA 102 prefactor and photon index free; nearby or significant source parameters free; other source parameters fixed  
  > "Model parameters for the sources within 10 $\deg$ of the centre of the ROI and for those whose detection significances are $\geq 15$ were set free." (γ-ray data); "We left the prefactor and the photon index free to vary" (γ-ray data); "For the other sources, the normalisation factor and the spectral shape parameters were fixed." (γ-ray data)
   - validation: TS = 10 detection limit; full-period TS = 340798  
  > "We evaluated the significances of the $\gamma$-ray signals from the sources using a maximum-likelihood analysis and setting a test statistic (TS) value of 10 as the detection limit." (γ-ray data); "The fit results for integrating over the whole period yield a TS value of 340798 in the 100 MeV–300 GeV energy range." (γ-ray data)
2. **Assemble lower-energy light curves**: Combine nearby radio frequencies; correct optical extinction and convert magnitudes to mJy; flag negative X-ray count rates  
  > "we used the 89 GHz (IRAM + ALMA), 230 GHz (IRAM + SMA + ALMA), and 343 GHz (SMA + ALMA) GHz data for the analyses discussed in this paper." (Radio data); "We converted the corrected magnitudes into flux densities (in units of mJy) using zero-magnitude flux densities (cite: Mead+1990)." (Optical data); "We flagged the data when the count rate is below zero." (Hard X-ray data)
   - software: NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database (NED)
   - parameter: X-ray light curve: monthly bins, 14–195 keV  
  > "We obtained the Crab-weighted monthly binned light curve at 14–195 keV." (Hard X-ray data)
   - assumption: Galactic extinction from NED; optical zero-magnitude flux densities from (cite: Mead+1990)  
  > "The magnitudes of the optical data have been corrected for Galactic extinction with values in the NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database (NED)." (Optical data); "We converted the corrected magnitudes into flux densities (in units of mJy) using zero-magnitude flux densities (cite: Mead+1990)." (Optical data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify flares and spectral trends**: Weighted-flux flare threshold, Bayesian blocks for local maxima, and Pearson correlation of photon index with flux  
  > "We regard a threshold as $\left<F_{\omega}\right> + 3\sigma_{\omega} = 3.4 \times 10^{-6}$ ${\rm ph~cm^{-2}~s^{-1}}$ to define a flaring state." (Multi-wavelength light curves); "We employed a Bayesian block analysis (cite: Scargle+2013) to identify the local maxima of multi-wavelength light curves." (Multi-wavelength light curves); "We performed a Pearson correlation analysis to evaluate the correlation between the $\gamma$-ray flux density and the $\gamma$-ray photon index." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
   - parameter: Bayesian block false positive rate 0.01 in all bands; local maximum exceeds both adjacent blocks  
  > "In this analysis, we set a false positive rate of 0.01 (the probability of falsely reporting the detection of a change point) for all energy bands." (Multi-wavelength light curves); "We identify a local maximum if the flux density of the block exceeds those of both previous and subsequent blocks." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
   - parameter: P1: MJD 57300–57550; P2: MJD 57550–57940; P3: MJD 57940–58400  
  > "This period extends from MJD 57550 to MJD 57940." (Multi-wavelength light curves); "The periods preceding and following P2 within the overall flaring period were determined as P1 (MJD 57300–57550) and P3 (MJD 57940–58400), respectively." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
   - assumption: Flux measurements within each Bayesian block are consistent with a constant  
  > "This method divides the light curves into blocks so that the flux measurements within each block are statistically consistent with a constant value." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
   - fitting: Seven-day bins for photon-index variations  
  > "We searched for variations in the $\gamma$-ray photon index over the flaring period divided into seven-day time bins." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
   - validation: Photon-index/flux anticorrelation confidence greater than 99.99%  
  > "we calculated the Pearson correlation coefficient, obtaining a value of $r_{\rm p} = -0.68$ for a confidence level greater than 99.99%." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
4. **Measure multi-wavelength correlations and lags**: DCF for uneven sampling over the entire period and P1–P3; no detrending  
  > "For this analysis, we calculated the discrete correlation function (DCF) in order to test for correlations between the various multi-wavelength light curves and to determine their time lags." (Method); "Thus, we did not detrend our light curves, following (cite: Max-Moerbeck+2014b)." (Method); "we investigated cross-correlations between the $\gamma$-ray and lower-energy light curves during three distinct periods (P1–3)." (Results)
   - software: PYTHON, public DCF code (cite: Robertson+2015), public simulation code (cite: Connolly+2015)
   - parameter: Lag-bin width between t_int and 2 t_int; t_int is the mean cadence of the less frequently sampled curve  
  > "For each frequency pair, we equated the bin width of the time lag ($\Delta t_{\rm bin}$) with the mean cadence ($t_{\rm int}$) of the less frequently sampled light curve." (Method); "The optimal bin width was determined in a range from $t_{\rm int}$ to $2 t_{\rm int}$." (Method)
   - parameter: 10,000 simulated DCFs for significance; 10,000 FRRSS light curves for peak and lag uncertainties  
  > "We then calculated the DCFs for a large sample of simulated light curves, obtaining 10,000 DCFs" (Method); "Using this method, we simulated 10,000 artificial light curves randomly sampled from each light curve." (Method)
   - assumption: Positive lag: higher frequency leads; negative lag: higher frequency lags  
  > "A positive (negative) time lag indicates that the higher-frequency time series leads (lags) the lower-frequency time series." (Method)
   - assumption: Power-law PSD; gamma PDFs for optical/gamma-rays and log-normal PDFs for radio/X-rays  
  > "we used a power-law spectrum, given by $P_f \propto {f}^{-\beta}$" (Method); "finding that the optical and $\gamma$-ray light curves follow gamma distributions, while the radio and X-ray light curves follow log-normal distributions (cite: Algaba+2018)." (Method)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo PSD slope estimation; Gaussian fits to FRRSS DCF-peak and lag distributions  
  > "We performed Monte Carlo simulations of light curves to find the best-fit slopes of the observed power spectra (cite: Park+2014; Park+2017)." (Method); "We fit the ${\rm DCF}_{\rm peak}$ and ${\tau}_{\rm DCF}$ distributions with Gaussian functions with means and standard deviations corresponding to the best-fit values for the parameters and their uncertainties, respectively." (Method)
   - validation: Simulated 68%, 95% and 99.7% confidence intervals  
  > "which we used to estimate 1$\sigma$, 2$\sigma$, and 3$\sigma$ (68$\%$, 95$\%$, and 99.7$\%$, respectively) confidence intervals." (Method)
5. **Measure VLBA jet components**: Fit circular two-dimensional Gaussians to 43 GHz CLEAN images  
  > "using the *MODELFIT* task in `DIFMAP`." (Jet kinematics); "We fit circular two-dimensional Gaussians to CLEAN images, finding multiple jet components for each epoch." (Jet kinematics)
   - software: DIFMAP, MODELFIT
   - parameter: Component flux density, relative core distance R, size and position angle PA; examine R < 0.5 mas  
  > "including the flux density, the relative distance from the core ($R$), the size, and the relative position angle with respect to the core (PA)." (Jet kinematics); "We examined the jet components close to the core, at $R < 0.5$ mas." (Jet kinematics)
   - assumption: Core C0 is the brightest region, presumed stationary and used as map origin  
  > "The core (labelled C0) is the brightest region presumed to be stationary at the upper end of the jet (i.e. the origin of each map)." (Jet kinematics)
   - fitting: Circular Gaussian model fitting; uncertainties from (cite: Jorstad+2017)  
  > "We fit circular two-dimensional Gaussians to CLEAN images, finding multiple jet components for each epoch." (Jet kinematics); "We calculate uncertainties of jet parameters using formulas given by (cite: Jorstad+2017)." (Jet kinematics)
   - validation: Example fitted components superimposed on a CLEAN image  
  > "which displays the fit of Gaussian components superimposed on the CLEAN image taken on 2016 January 31." (Jet kinematics)
6. **Infer component motions and ejection times**: Linear fits of C2/C3 core separation versus time; extrapolate to R = 0 for ejection  
  > "we fitted the measurements of $R$ for components C2 and C3 with a linear function and obtained the best-fit solution of the proper motion $\mu$ in mas/yr." (Jet kinematics); "We determined $t_0$ by extrapolating to $R=0$ from the fitted linear motion." (Jet kinematics)
   - parameter: 1 mas/yr corresponds to 55.2c  
  > "The apparent speed, $\beta_{\rm app}$, was obtained by using the relation that a proper motion of 1 mas/yr corresponds to 55.2c" (Jet kinematics)
   - assumption: Flat ΛCDM distance scale; ejection means component and core centroids coincide  
  > "Throughout this paper, we adopt a flat ${\rm \Lambda}$CDM cosmology with $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.315$, $\Omega_{\rm \Lambda} = 0.685$, and $H_0 = 67.4$ ${\rm km~s^{-1}~{Mpc}^{-1}}$ (cite: PlanckCollaboration+2020)." (Introduction); "The ejection time of a jet component, $t_0$, is defined as the time when the centroid of the jet component coincides with the centroid of the core." (Jet kinematics)
   - fitting: Linear motion; propagate fit errors and add minimum ejection-time uncertainty 0.005/μ yr  
  > "The uncertainty in $t_0$ was computed by propagating errors from the fit results." (Jet kinematics); "We added a minimum uncertainty of 0.005/$\mu$ yr to the uncertainty in $t_0$" (Jet kinematics)
   - validation: C2 ejection time roughly consistent with (cite: Casadio+2019) within uncertainty  
  > "Our estimate of $t_0$ is roughly consistent with that of (cite: Casadio+2019) within the uncertainty." (Jet kinematics)
7. **Derive physical jet parameters**: Fit exponential component-flux decays; derive variability timescale, Doppler factor, Lorentz factor and viewing angle  
  > "we followed (cite: Jorstad+2017) in using a model with an exponential decay in the form of $\ln{S(t)} \propto -t$." (Jet kinematics); "The variability timescale is computed to be $\tau_{\rm var} = |1/k|$." (Jet kinematics); "Using the apparent speed and the variability Doppler factor, the Lorentz factor ($\Gamma_{\rm var}$) and the viewing angle ($\theta_{\rm var}$) with respect to the line of sight are given by" (Jet kinematics)
   - software: EMCEE, PYTHON

## Qualitative findings

- Gamma-ray spectra show a harder-when-brighter trend  
  > "Therefore, we conclude that the harder-when-brighter trend is seen in the $\gamma$-ray band" (Multi-wavelength light curves)
- Nearly zero optical/X-ray versus gamma-ray lags suggest co-spatial emission  
  > "The analysis revealed that the time lags between the $\gamma$-ray light curve and the optical and X-ray light curves were nearly zero, implying a time coincidence and thus a co-spatial origin." (Summary)
- No significant correlations in P1; significant radio/gamma-ray correlations in P2 and P3  
  > "We found no significant correlations for P1." (Results); "However, significant correlations (above 99.7% confidence level) were observed between the $\gamma$-ray and radio light curves for P2 and P3." (Results)
- Opposite radio/gamma-ray lag signs indicate multiple gamma-ray emission regions  
  > "In the $\gamma$-radio correlation analysis, we observed both positive and negative time lags, indicating the existence of multiple $\gamma$-ray emission regions in the jet." (Results)
- Gamma-ray flares likely associated with shock-shock interactions  
  > "Our results suggest that the $\gamma$-ray flares originated from shock-shock interactions." (abstract)
- Seed photons may originate in the jet or dust torus  
  > "The $\gamma$-ray flaring regions are well beyond the BLR, which suggests that the sources of seed photons for $\gamma$-rays may come from either the jet itself or the dust torus." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Radio/gamma-ray correlations constrain gamma-ray emission-region locations  
  > "a correlation study between $\gamma$-ray and radio light curves during flaring activity is crucial to constrain the locations of the $\gamma$-ray regions" (Introduction)
- Why this method: DCF accommodates uneven sampling; separate periods address different radio/gamma-ray variability  
  > "We carried out a correlation analysis that is applicable to unevenly-sampled data sets." (Method); "the different variability behaviours between the $\gamma$-ray and radio emissions could affect the correlation results." (Results); "To further explore these correlations, we investigated cross-correlations between the $\gamma$-ray and lower-energy light curves during three distinct periods (P1–3)." (Results)
- Alternative: Detrending rejected because it can increase lag-estimation errors  
  > "(cite: Peterson+2004) found that detrending does not improve the accuracy of estimates of time lags in unevenly sampled time-series data; rather, detrending results in large errors." (Method); "Thus, we did not detrend our light curves, following (cite: Max-Moerbeck+2014b)." (Method)
- Alternative: SSC remains possible for a near-unity flux–flux slope if magnetic field or Doppler factor varies  
  > "a unity slope is possible within the SSC framework if variations in the magnetic field or the Doppler factor (resulting from changes in the jet viewing angle) are considered" (γ-ray/optical correlations)
- Alternative: Negative optical/gamma-ray lags may reflect mismatched local maxima  
  > "The negative time lags are likely due to the cross-match of two optical local maxima at MJD 58104 and MJD 58132 with two $\gamma$-ray local maxima at MJD 58121 (Flare 6) and MJD 58142." (γ-ray/optical correlations)
- Alternative: One-zone leptonic EC with different spatial energy-density gradients can explain negative optical/gamma-ray lags  
  > "The observed negative time lags could be explained in the framework of the one-zone leptonic EC scenario." (γ-ray/optical correlations); "If the magnetic field energy density decreases more rapidly than the external radiation field energy density, the EC ($\gamma$-rays) peak could be delayed relative to the synchrotron (optical) peak, resulting in a negative time lag." (γ-ray/optical correlations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low Swift-BAT sensitivity limits detected X-ray local maxima  
  > "The small number of local maxima in X-rays is mainly related to the relatively low sensitivity of *Swift*-BAT." (Multi-wavelength light curves)
- Limitation (authors): Missing VLBA data near C3 ejection limits core-flux/ejection comparison  
  > "The lack of 43 GHz VLBA data near the ejection time of C3 makes it challenging to study the relationship between the variation in the core flux density and the ejection of C3 from the core." (γ-ray flares in shock-shock interaction regions)
- Limitation (authors): Higher cadence and broadband SED modeling needed to clarify optical/gamma-ray correlation  
  > "High-cadence observations and modeling of the broadband SED are needed for a clear understanding of the $\gamma$-optical correlation." (γ-ray/optical correlations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA contributes to combined radio light curves; the reported lags are not ALMA-only measurements.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The text labels both the correlation results and jet-motion results as Table 2; table identity must be checked by content.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): P3 37 GHz and 230 GHz lag entries lack the table's 99.7% significance star despite broad prose about significant radio correlations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Temporal coincidence supports shock associations but does not uniquely establish the physical trigger.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distance estimates depend on applying C3 kinematics and earlier core-shift measurements to the relevant emission regions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The printed IR photon energy and wavelength pairing requires clarification before reproducing the electron-energy argument.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The variability-timescale table note calls the timescale a rise time, while the method fits exponential decay.
