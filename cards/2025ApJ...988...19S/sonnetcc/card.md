## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the visibilities**: Standard ALMA pipeline calibration in CASA, 30 s time averaging, natural-weighted tclean with automasking to 2 sigma  
  > "The ALMA data were reduced and calibrated following the standard ALMA pipeline procedures with the Common Astronomy Software Applications (v6.5.4-9)" (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA); "Imaging of the calibrated visibilities was performed with natural weighting using the TCLEAN task in CASA, cleaning to a depth of 2$\sigma$ using automasking" (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Time averaging bins of 30 seconds  
  > "The resulting calibrated measurement sets were time-averaged in bins of 30 seconds to reduce the data-size, after carefully verifying that time-average smearing does not impact our results" (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA)
   - parameter: Pixel scale 0.076 arcsec  
  > "We use a pixel scale of 0.076$''$ to properly sample the synthesized beam" (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Time-average smearing checked not to impact the results; no bad antennas or channels needed flagging  
  > "The data were well behaved, with no bad antennas or channels requiring flagging." (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA)
2. **Blind line search**: Collapse moment maps over 75-350 km/s widths at every channel, list >3 sigma peaks, prune duplicates, repeat on the negative moment map  
  > "The algorithm loops over all channels and collapses moment maps over a range of 75 to 350 km s$^{-1}$ in steps of 1 channel ($\sim$10 km s$^{-1}$)." (Line Search); "For each moment map, we identify the significant ($>$3$\sigma$) peaks and add the results to a large list of features." (Line Search)
   - parameter: Duplicates pruned within 2xFWHM and 1.5x the beamsize  
  > "This list is then pruned by removing duplicates within 2$\times$FWHM and 1.5$\times$ the beamsize." (Line Search)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Search repeated on the negative moment map to characterise the noise  
  > "We also perform the search on the negative moment map, which is useful to characterize the noise properties." (Line Search)
3. **Identify the line candidate and derive the redshift**: Take the most significant candidate within 0.5 arcsec of the JWST position; convert its frequency to a [OIII]88 redshift  
  > "is detected with a peak significance of 6.6$\sigma$ and separation of 0.12" (Line Search); "We have identified an emission line with a signal-to-noise of $6.6\sigma$ within $0.12''$ of GS-z14 at 223.528$\pm$0.009 GHz." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spatial coincidence with the JWST/NIRCam position and consistency with the Lyman-break and tentative CIII] redshifts  
  > "The candidate line is consistent with being spatially coincident with GS-z14 in the JWST/NIRCam observations" (Line Search); "greatly increases our confidence in the robustness of the current redshift determination given the availability of multiple line detections." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
4. **Measure line FWHM and integrated flux**: Iterative moment-zero map including channels within 2xFWHM; 1D spectrum from pixels >3 sigma; Gaussian fit for new FWHM  
  > "We measure the integrated line flux using the moment-zero map of the emission line, including all channels that fall within 2$\times$ the FWHM of the line." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14); "A new FWHM is then measured using this 1d spectrum by fitting a Gaussian." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - parameter: 1D spectrum extracted from all pixels with S/N > 3 sigma on the moment-zero map  
  > "which is extracted by including all pixels with S/N$>$3$\sigma$ on the moment-zero map." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to the 1D spectrum (sampler and priors not stated)  
  > "A new FWHM is then measured using this 1d spectrum by fitting a Gaussian." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - validation: Iteration converges in fewer than 10 steps  
  > "A stable FWHM is achieved in less than 10 steps" (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
5. **Derive [OIII]88 luminosity and compare with the local L-SFR relation**: Convert the integrated flux to a lensing-corrected luminosity and compare with the local relation for metal-poor dwarfs  
  > "This places GS-z14 a factor of $\sim2\times$ below the local relation between L$_\mathrm{[OIII]}$ and SFR for metal-poor dwarf galaxies from" (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - parameter: Lensing magnification 1.17x  
  > "All measured and derived physical quantities are corrected for gravitational lensing by a factor of 1.17$\times$" (Introduction)
   - assumption: H0=70, Omega_m=0.3, Omega_Lambda=0.7 cosmology  
  > "Throughout this paper we assume a standard $\mathrm{\Lambda}$CDM cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_m=0.3$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Estimate dynamical mass**: Dispersion-dominated estimate from the measured FWHM and r_[OIII] = r_e  
  > "Assuming that GS-z14 is dispersion-dominated and r$_\mathrm{[OIII]}$ = r$_e$ = 260 pc, this implies a dynamical mass of (1.0$\pm$0.5)$\times$10$^9$ M$_{\odot}(\mathrm{sin}\, i)^2$." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - parameter: r_e = 260 pc  
  > "Assuming that GS-z14 is dispersion-dominated and r$_\mathrm{[OIII]}$ = r$_e$ = 260 pc" (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - assumption: Dispersion-dominated; [OIII] radius equals the rest-UV effective radius  
  > "Assuming that GS-z14 is dispersion-dominated and r$_\mathrm{[OIII]}$ = r$_e$ = 260 pc" (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: No velocity gradient seen at the current resolution  
  > "At the current resolution we do not see evidence for a velocity gradient." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
7. **Upper limits on dust continuum, dust mass and IR luminosity**: Optically thin modified black body at fixed T_d and beta_IR with the Weingartner and Draine dust model, applied to the 3 sigma 90 um continuum limit  
  > "We provide an upper limit on its dust mass assuming an optically thin modified black body with a dust temperature of $T_\mathrm{d} = 60\,\mathrm{K}$ and $\beta_\mathrm{IR} = 2.03$ for the dust SED." (Results: Dust Continuum)
   - parameter: T_d = 60 K, beta_IR = 2.03  
  > "a dust temperature of $T_\mathrm{d} = 60\,\mathrm{K}$ and $\beta_\mathrm{IR} = 2.03$ for the dust SED." (Results: Dust Continuum)
   - parameter: kappa_88 = 34.15 cm^-2 g^-1; L_IR integrated over 8-1000 um  
  > "This yields a (lensing-corrected) upper limit on the dust mass and infrared luminosity (integrated across $8-1000\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) of $\log(M_\mathrm{d} / M_\odot) < 6.0$ and $\log(L_\mathrm{IR} / L_\odot) < 11.1$, respectively." (Results: Dust Continuum)
   - assumption: Dust temperature 60 K, justified by theory and observational trends extrapolated to z~14 and the CMB temperature  
  > "The assumed temperature is consistent with extrapolations from theoretical models" (Results: Dust Continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Dust-temperature sensitivity tested with T_d = 45 K and 100 K  
  > "a significantly higher temperature temperature of $T_\mathrm{d} = 100\,\mathrm{K}$ would result in a dust mass of $\log(M_\mathrm{d} / M_\odot) < 5.3$." (Results: Dust Continuum)
8. **Constrain ISM conditions from [OIII]88 versus MIRI [OIII]+Hbeta**: Monte Carlo draws of [OIII]/Hbeta from the MIRI SED-fitting distribution to compute [OIII]5007 luminosity and compare to ALMA [OIII]88 on a Cloudy grid  
  > "We repeat this calculation 10$^6\times$" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14); "In this process we also account for the uncertainty in the line luminosities from ALMA and JWST by drawing random values from a Gaussian error distribution." (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - software: Cloudy
   - parameter: Cloudy grids at fixed log U = -3.0 and -2.0, bracketing log U = -2.5 +/- 0.5  
  > "to be $\log(U_\mathrm{ion}) = -2.5 \pm 0.5$, and we therefore show two sets of Cloudy models bracketing this range" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - assumption: Flat underlying continuum for the F770W excess of 27.5 +/- 5.6 nJy  
  > "we assume that the flux excess in F770W is 27.5$\pm$5.6 nJy based on a flat underlying continuum emission." (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo sampling, not a fit  
  > "We repeat this calculation 10$^6\times$" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - validation: Equivalent widths compared with Cloudy predictions and distributions at z~0-4  
  > "The measured equivalent widths are in agreement with predictions from Cloudy at a metallicity of $Z\sim0.05-0.2\,Z_\odot$" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
9. **Estimate metallicity from the [OIII]88/SFR calibration**: Apply an empirical [OIII]88 + SFR oxygen-abundance calibration and compare with a Cloudy grid of [OIII]88/SFR versus ionisation parameter (1 Myr stellar population)  
  > "Using their calibration we derive 12 + $\log{\rm O^{++}/H^+}$=7.66$_{- 0.21}^{+0.19}$ ($\sim$6-14% solar metallicity, using the solar abundance from" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - software: Cloudy
   - parameter: Cloudy grid with a 1 Myr old input stellar population  
  > "we use a Cloudy grid with a 1Myr old input stellar population" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - assumption: Higher densities or older stellar ages would give higher metallicity (up to solar)  
  > "higher gas densities or older ages for the input stellar population will give higher metallicity estimates (up to solar metallicity)." (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
10. **Convert dust-to-stellar mass limit into a supernova dust yield limit and compare with the AFM**: Dust yield per supernova from the dust-to-stellar mass limit (Salpeter IMF); compare predicted AFM continuum flux with the ALMA limit  
  > "and adopting a Salpeter IMF, the limit of $\xi_d < 2 \times 10^{-3}$ suggests a yield of $y_d < 0.24\,M_\odot/$SN." (Discussion: Constraints on Dust Formation Processes); "the AFM predicts a continuum flux density for GS-z14 of $F_{88} = 14.9\,\mu\mathrm{Jy}$" (Discussion: Constraints on Dust Formation Processes)
   - assumption: Salpeter IMF for the yield conversion  
  > "and adopting a Salpeter IMF, the limit of $\xi_d < 2 \times 10^{-3}$ suggests a yield of $y_d < 0.24\,M_\odot/$SN." (Discussion: Constraints on Dust Formation Processes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with a commonly adopted yield of ~0.1 Msun/SN  
  > "This is consistent with a commonly adopted yield of $y_d\sim0.1\,M_\odot$/SN" (Discussion: Constraints on Dust Formation Processes)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GS-z14 is very luminous, making it a prime target for follow-up of its physical conditions; [OIII]88 is expected to be bright in high-z star-forming galaxies  
  > "The high luminosity of GS-z14 makes it an exceptional target for multi-wavelength follow-up observations capable of revealing its physical conditions." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Tunings were chosen to cover the redshift likelihood distribution from the Lyman break  
  > "An observing set-up using two tunings with three spectral windows each was utilized to maximize the coverage of the redshift likelihood distribution derived from the spectroscopic Lyman break." (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA)
- Alternative: Dust temperature of 45 K (no evolution from z~4) and 100 K tested as alternatives to 60 K  
  > "Assuming a lower temperature of $T_\mathrm{d} = 45\,\mathrm{K}$, which would imply no significant evolution of the dust temperature from $z\sim4$" (Results: Dust Continuum)
- Alternative: Dust distributed like the stellar component would make GS-z14 heavily obscured; an Attenuation Free Model with dust expelled by outflows is preferred  
  > "GS-z14 should be largely obscured (A$_{V}$$\sim$9.5, see also" (Discussion: Constraints on Dust Formation Processes)
- How they decided it was enough: Observations stopped when the requested sensitivity was reached in 2.8 h; the scan covers 98% of the P(z)  
  > "achieving the requested sensitivity in 2.8 hours" (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA); "that the observing strategy covers 98% of the P(z)." (Observations and Data-Reduction: ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass depends on an assumed dust temperature  
  > "The above estimates require the assumption of a dust temperature." (Results: Dust Continuum)
- Limitation (authors): ISM constraints rely on a photometric-only detection of [OIII]4959,5007  
  > "While the uncertainties are substantial, given the existence of only a photometric detection of the" (Discussion: Constraints on the ISM of GS-z14)
- Limitation (authors): Conclusive dust constraints need deeper, higher-resolution ALMA continuum data  
  > "requires deeper and high-resolution ALMA continuum observations." (Discussion: Constraints on Dust Formation Processes)
- Limitation (authors): Resolution too coarse to detect a velocity gradient  
  > "At the current resolution we do not see evidence for a velocity gradient." (Results: [OIII] in GS-z14)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 6.6 sigma significance comes from a blind search with no stated false-positive rate or trials correction
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust-mass limit is dominated by the assumed T_d and kappa; the limit varies by 1.5 dex across the tested temperatures
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Metallicity depends on one MIRI photometric point and an [OIII]/Hbeta distribution from SED fitting rather than direct spectroscopy
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dynamical mass assumes a dispersion-dominated geometry with the UV size, unresolved in [OIII]
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abstract quotes y_d < 0.3 while the main text and summary give y_d < 0.24 Msun/SN
