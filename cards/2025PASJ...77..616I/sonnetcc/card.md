## Analysis recipe

1. **build combined 12m+7m+TP CO(1-0) cube**: uvcontsub, concatenate 12 m and 7 m pointings, tclean mosaic with Briggs weighting, multiscale then single-scale CLEAN with automasking, feather with TP, primary-beam correct  
  > "we concatenated the visibility data sets of all the pointings of the 12 m and 7 m arrays into a single visibility data and conducted mosaicing and imaging using the `tclean` task" (2.1 12CO(1-0)); "Finally, we combined the TP cube using a feathering algorithm (cite: Cotton17) to create the final cube." (2.1 12CO(1-0))
   - software: CASA 6.5.2, tclean, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "applying a Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5" (2.1 12CO(1-0))
   - parameter: pixel 0.''13, ~5.1 km/s channels  
  > "We set a pixel scale of $0.''13$ and binned two velocity channels into one channel ($\sim5.1$kms$^{-1}$)." (2.1 12CO(1-0))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **derive SFR surface density map from H-alpha + 24 um**: H-alpha luminosity corrected with 0.031 L(24um), converted to Sigma_SFR with the Murphy+11 factor; all images matched to 24 um resolution (~6 arcsec = 600 pc); FUV+24um used as a cross-check  
  > "To derive SFR, we used H$\alpha$ and 24$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ data to compensate for the dust obscuration of H$\alpha$ emission," (3.1 SFR derived from Halpha, FUV and 24um flux); "In the following discussions, we use $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}}$ derived from H$\alpha$ and 24$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ flux." (3.3 SFR in the Antennae galaxies)
   - parameter: images convolved with Aniano+11 kernels to MIPS 24 um resolution and regridded  
  > "we convolved MUSE H$\alpha$ and GALEX FUV images using the kernel derived by (cite: Aniano+11) to match the angular resolution of MIPS 24$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ data" (3.1 SFR derived from Halpha, FUV and 24um flux)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Halpha+24 and FUV+24 SFR agree within a factor of 2 in low-SFR regions  
  > "agrees with scatter within a factor of 2 even in the region of lower value ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}} \lesssim 0.1$$M_\odot$yr$^{-1}$kpc$^{-2}$)" (3.3 SFR in the Antennae galaxies)
3. **remove IR cirrus from the 24 um flux**: Empirical: subtract stellar continuum with a 5000 K blackbody scaled by 2MASS Ks, scale cirrus 24/5.8 um ratio (mode in outer-region apertures) by 5.8 um dust intensity; model-based Draine & Li check  
  > "We employed two independent methods described below." (3.2 Estimation of IR cirrus contribution at 24um); "we assumed that stellar continuum at wavelength longer than $\sim2$$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ is approximated by the 5000 K black body" (3.2.1 Empirical method)
   - parameter: cirrus 24/5.8 um ratio taken as the mode of the ratio in outer-region apertures  
  > "as the mode value of the intensity ratio in the apertures." (3.2.1 Empirical method)
   - assumption: stellar continuum longer than ~2 um approximated by a 5000 K blackbody  
  > "we assumed that stellar continuum at wavelength longer than $\sim2$$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ is approximated by the 5000 K black body" (3.2.1 Empirical method)
   - assumption: PAH abundance fixed at 4.6% in the model-based method  
  > "We fixed this parameter as a typical value (4.6%) in the Milky Way." (3.2.2 Model based method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: empirical and model-based cirrus fractions agree within a factor of 2; empirical one adopted  
  > "They agree with each other within a factor of 2." (3.2.2 Model based method)
4. **identify GMCs in the CO cube**: Rosolowsky & Leroy (2006) significant-emission mask, then PYCPROPS decomposition (local maxima, watershed with compactness parameter)  
  > "Second, we decomposed the significant emissions into individual GMCs by using 3D clump-finding algorithm PYCPROPS (cite: Rosolowsky+21)." (4.1 GMC identification)
   - software: PYCPROPS, astrodendro
   - parameter: mask h = 5 sigma, l = 2 sigma, sigma = 0.14 K  
  > "We chose $h=5$ and $l=2$, and $\sigma=0.14$K." (4.1 GMC identification)
   - parameter: delta = 2 sigma  
  > "In this study, we used $\delta = 2\sigma$." (4.1 GMC identification)
   - parameter: compactness = 0.001  
  > "Therefore, we adopted a small compactness parameter of 0.001." (4.1 GMC identification)
   - parameter: sigdiscont = 0.0, specfriends = 0, friends = 0  
  > "each local maximum that satisfies the above conditions is assigned to an individual independent cloud (i.e., sigdiscont parameter is set to be 0.0)" (4.1 GMC identification); "i.e., specfriends = 0 and friends = 0" (4.1 GMC identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual check of GMC boundaries on channel map, PV diagram and spectra; parameter variations (h, l) change mask flux by <6%  
  > "Adopting $h$ of 4 or 6 and $l$ of 1.5 or 2.5 results in less than 6% change of the total flux in the mask." (4.1 GMC identification)
5. **measure GMC mass, radius and line width**: PYCPROPS extrapolated, beam- and channel-deconvolved moments; luminosity mass M_mol = alpha_CO times summed brightness  
  > "The luminosity mass of a GMC is derived by converting the integrated flux in the GMC as" (4.2 Derivation of physical properties of the GMCs)
   - software: PYCPROPS
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 4.36 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1, single value for the whole region  
  > "In this study, we adopt a single value of $4.36$$M_\odot$(Kkms$^{-1}$pc$^2$)$^{-1}$ as $\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}$ for the entire region" (4.2 Derivation of physical properties of the GMCs)
   - assumption: distance 22 Mpc  
  > "which corresponds to a physical scale of 76pc $\times$ 60pc at 22Mpc" (2.1 12CO(1-0))
   - assumption: 2D Gaussian surface brightness, eta ~1.18 in R = eta sqrt(sigma_maj sigma_min)  
  > "By assuming that the distribution of the surface brightness of a GMC is a two-dimensional Gaussian, $\eta$ is calculated to be $\sqrt{2\ln 2} \sim 1.18$" (4.2 Derivation of physical properties of the GMCs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Median properties compared with earlier GMC studies of the Antennae (massive GMCs, sigma_v 10-30 km/s)  
  > "They found a significant population of massive GMCs ($\sim10^8$$M_\odot$) in the active overlap and nuclear regions" (4.3 GMCs in the Antennae galaxies)
6. **estimate collision velocity and typical mass per aperture**: Hexagon apertures (300 pc side) centred every 300 pc; median GMC mass per aperture; v_col = sqrt(3 sum (v_los - mean)^2 / N) over GMCs in the aperture  
  > "we took hexagon apertures with 300pc side" (5 Aperture analysis); "we estimated the typical value of the collision velocity of the GMCs in each aperture as" (5 Aperture analysis)
   - parameter: 876 apertures in total, 1-27 GMCs each  
  > "The total number of apertures is 876 and the number of the GMCs in an aperture is 1–27 with a typical value of 6." (5 Aperture analysis)
   - parameter: apertures with a single GMC excluded; keep N_ap f_CCC > 0.1 per Myr (560 apertures)  
  > "Out of 876 apertures, 560 apertures meet this criterion." (5 Aperture analysis)
   - assumption: GMCs move randomly in 3-dimensional space within the aperture  
  > "assuming that GMCs are moving randomly in 3-dimension space in the aperture" (5 Aperture analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: v_col comparable to separation of most distant CO profile peaks; consistent with Tsuge+21 values of ~100 km/s; aperture size 200 and 400 pc tested  
  > "The estimated collision velocity is comparable to the difference of the velocity for the most separated peaks of the CO profile in the aperture." (5 Aperture analysis); "If we use 200 and 400pc as a side of the hexagon of the apertures, the final conclusions are not affected" (5 Aperture analysis)
7. **relate Sigma_SFR to v_col and GMC mass**: Plot Sigma_SFR of each aperture against <M_mol> and v_col, overall and by region; also <M_mol> divided into five bins  
  > "To explore this further, figure [11](#fig:CCC_SFRplot)b shows the $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}^{\rm ap}$ as a function of the $v_{\rm col}$, with the $\langle M_{\rm mol} \rangle$ divided into five bins (colored lines)." (6 Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative trend compared with NGC 1300 (Maeda+21)  
  > "This trend is qualitatively similar to that seen in the nearby barred spiral galaxies (cite: Maeda+21)." (6 Results)
8. **estimate star formation efficiency per collision**: SFR = eps_CCC <M_mol> N_ap f_CCC with f_CCC = sigma_c v_col n_ap, sigma_c = pi (2<R>)^2; solve for eps_CCC  
  > "Assuming that GMCs are in a random motion in 3-dimension, $f_{\mathrm{CCC}}$ within an aperture can be estimated as" (7 Star formation efficiency for a colliding GMC); "Here, $\sigma_{\mathrm{c}}$ is the collision cross-section, defined as $\pi (2 \langle R\rangle)^2$, where $\langle R\rangle$ is the median GMC radius in the aperture." (7 Star formation efficiency for a colliding GMC)
   - parameter: line-of-sight depth l = 600 pc  
  > "We adopted 600pc for $l$ to be similar to the size of the apertures." (7 Star formation efficiency for a colliding GMC)
   - assumption: eps_CCC is the product of the star-forming-collision fraction and the converted gas fraction; the two cannot be separated  
  > "However, since these two values cannot be measured separately, in this study we examine only $\epsilon_{\mathrm{CCC}}$." (7 Star formation efficiency for a colliding GMC)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the simulations of Takahira+18 (eps_CCC 1-10% at 10-30 km/s, falling with velocity)  
  > "the dependency of the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{CCC}}$ on the collision velocity and its value is similar to the results obtained in this study, supporting their suggestion." (7 Star formation efficiency for a colliding GMC)
9. **test robustness to delta, compactness and aperture size**: Repeat the main plots with delta = 1.5 and 2.5, compactness = 0.1 and 0.00001, and aperture sides of 200 and 400 pc  
  > "If we use $\delta=$1.5 and 2.5 instead of $\delta=2.0$, 2243 and 1415 GMCs are identified, respectively." (Effects of Parameter Choices)
   - software: PYCPROPS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Three characteristic parameter spaces and the eps_CCC trend persist under all variations  
  > "however, three characteristic parameter spaces discussed in section [6](#sec:results) remain." (Effects of Parameter Choices)

## Qualitative findings

- Highest Sigma_SFR occurs where high-speed (~100 km/s) collisions involve massive (~1e7-8 Msun) GMCs, mainly in the southern active overlap region  
  > "Those data points are located mostly in the southern part of the active overlap region, supporting high-speed collisions of massive GMCs cause active star formation." (6 Results)
- High-speed collisions of low-mass GMCs coincide with the lowest Sigma_SFR in the non-active overlap region  
  > "suggesting that the high-speed collision of low mass GMCs suppresses star formation" (6 Results)
- In the arm-like region Sigma_SFR decreases with increasing v_col  
  > "the trend that $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}}^{\mathrm{ap}}$ decreases with increasing $v_{\rm col}$ is seen" (6 Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Antennae show active massive star formation despite very high-speed collisions, so they extend CCC studies to ~100 km/s  
  > "Nevertheless, some systems exhibit active massive star formation, suggesting that even higher-speed collision can trigger the massive star formation." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Data cannot resolve individual colliding pairs, so a statistical approach based on velocity dispersion among GMCs per aperture is used  
  > "Unfortunately, the resolution and sensitivity of the data we use in this study are not good enough to identify individual colliding cloud pairs." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Sigma_SFR from FUV+24 um as an alternative to H-alpha+24 um; conclusions unchanged  
  > "Final conclusions of this paper do not change if we use $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}}$ derived from FUV and 24$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ flux." (3.3 SFR in the Antennae galaxies)
- Alternative: Model-based (Draine & Li) cirrus estimate used only as a comparison; empirical cirrus subtraction adopted  
  > "In the following discussion, we used the 24$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ image after subtracting the empirically estimated IR cirrus component." (3.2.2 Model based method)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Individual colliding cloud pairs cannot be identified at this resolution and sensitivity  
  > "Unfortunately, the resolution and sensitivity of the data we use in this study are not good enough to identify individual colliding cloud pairs." (Introduction)
- Limitation (authors): Only sub-kpc statistical properties examined; higher sensitivity and resolution data are desirable  
  > "However, we only focused on the properties in sub-kpc regions and it is desirable to identify individual colliding clouds using more sensitive and high-resolution data" (Summary)
- Limitation (authors): eps_CCC combines two factors that cannot be measured separately  
  > "However, since these two values cannot be measured separately, in this study we examine only $\epsilon_{\mathrm{CCC}}$." (7 Star formation efficiency for a colliding GMC)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): v_col is a line-of-sight velocity dispersion among GMCs in a 300 pc aperture; it also includes galactic and merger velocity gradients and projection effects, so it may not be a true collision velocity
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single alpha_CO of 4.36 is used though the reported range is 1.1-6.54 and varies with region; this changes absolute masses and eps_CCC
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 600 pc line-of-sight depth l is an assumed value that scales f_CCC and hence eps_CCC
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SFR map resolution (~600 pc) is coarser than the aperture grid (300 pc sides), so neighbouring apertures are not independent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Statistical association does not demonstrate that collisions cause the observed star formation
