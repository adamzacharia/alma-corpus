## Analysis recipe

1. **Merge ALMA uv data and measure continuum**: Convert calibrated data to uv tables, merge the two programmes with uv_merge, fit a point source to the continuum with uvfit in uv space  
  > "the calibrated data are converted to $uv$ table format" (2.2 ALMA); "The dust continuum is well-fitted by a point-source model using" (2.2 ALMA)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Upper limit on continuum size from Eq. (2) of Gomez-Guijarro et al. 2022  
  > "we place an upper limit on the continuum size using Eq. (2) from (cite: Gomez_Guijarro2022)" (2.2 ALMA)
   - fitting: Point-source model; an elliptical Gaussian gave no useful constraints  
  > "while fitting with an elliptical Gaussian does not yield useful constraints" (2.2 ALMA)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify lines and measure redshift**: Extract spectra in all SPWs, combine to a 1D spectrum, run line search, fit power-law continuum with fixed slope 3.7 and Gaussian lines  
  > "After extracting the ALMA spectra in all spectral windows (SPWs), we combine them in a single 1D spectrum" (3.1 Redshift identification); "The detected emission lines are fitted with a Gaussian profile in the continuum subtracted spectrum." (3.1 Redshift identification)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Continuum power law with fixed slope 3.7 in frequency  
  > "The continuum is fit with a power-law with fixed slope of 3.7 in frequency" (3.1 Redshift identification)
   - fitting: Gaussian line profiles on the continuum-subtracted spectrum  
  > "The detected emission lines are fitted with a Gaussian profile in the continuum subtracted spectrum." (3.1 Redshift identification)
   - validation: P_chance of the second line 0.4%; agreement with NIR photometric redshift PDF; z=3.66 (CO(4-3)) alternative rejected  
  > "yielding a low $P_{\rm chance}=0.4\%$" (3.1 Redshift identification); "and find they are in excellent agreement" (3.1 Redshift identification); "we tested the redshift solution of $z=3.66$ in the case of the bright line being CO(4-3)" (3.1 Redshift identification)
3. **Fit JWST morphology**: Galfit modelling of F277W and F444W with three components (compact, diffuse, companion); two cases tested (point source plus two Sersic vs three Sersic)  
  > "We adopt three separate components in both F277W and F444W: a compact central emission, a diffuse central emission, and a companion component at the south-east." (3.2 Morphology); "We consider two cases; one case using a point source (compact component) together with two Sérsic profiles" (3.2 Morphology)
   - software: Galfit, Webbpsf
   - parameter: Webbpsf PSFs with a pixel scale of 0.05 arcsec/pix  
  > "The NIRCam PSFs were obtained using Webbpsf (cite: Perrin2014_WebbPSF) with a pixel scale of" (3.2 Morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residuals inspected; three Sersic profiles leave no clear structure  
  > "In the case of three Sérsic profiles, there are no clear structures, as would be expected with random noise" (3.2 Morphology)
4. **Fit FIR SED for dust temperature and mass**: Modified blackbody fit to FIR and (sub)mm photometry with mercurius, in optically thin and optically thick cases  
  > "we fit the FIR and (sub)mm photometry of XS55 with a modified black-body (MBB) model (cite: Magdis2012SED) using the code" (3.3 FIR SED); "We explore two cases; one assuming optically thin dust" (3.3 FIR SED)
   - software: mercurius
   - parameter: Emitting-area upper limit from the half light radius in JWST/F444W (optically thick case)  
  > "based on the half light radius of the galaxy in JWST/F444W" (3.3 FIR SED)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: beta_IR consistent between the two cases, weighted average 2.02 +/- 0.12  
  > "In both cases, the IR spectral index $\beta_{\rm IR}$ is consistent with a weighted average of $\beta_{\rm IR}=2.02\,{\pm \,0.12}$." (3.3 FIR SED)
5. **Panchromatic SED for L_IR, SFR, stellar mass**: Fit stellar, AGN and dust components from optical to radio with STARDUST  
  > "To construct a panchromatic SED of XS55, we fit the stellar, AGN, and dust components using" (3.3 FIR SED)
   - software: STARDUST, Bagpipes, LePhare
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF; flat cosmology H0=70, Omega_M=0.27, Omega_Lambda=0.73  
  > "We adopt a flat cosmology with $H_0=70\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $\Omega_{\rm M}=0.27$, and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.73$" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stellar part refit with Bagpipes, and compared with LePhare catalog values; all agree  
  > "As an additional check, we also fit the stellar part of the spectrum (up to F444W) using" (3.3 FIR SED); "also agree with our results" (3.3 FIR SED)
6. **Derive molecular gas mass and CO size/kinematics**: Gas mass from dust mass with a gas-to-dust ratio, and from [CI](1-0) with alpha_CI; CO(5-4) size from elliptical Gaussian uvfit; moment-1 map in CARTA  
  > "using the standard gas-to-dust mass ratio of star-forming galaxies (cite: Magdis2012SED)" (3.4 Molecular gas mass and far-infrared lines); "We measure the size of the integrated CO(5-4) emission by fitting an elliptical Gaussian in $uv$-space with" (3.4 Molecular gas mass and far-infrared lines); "We constructed the CO(5-4) moment-1 map using Cube Analysis and Rendering Tool for Astronomy (cite: Comrie2021_CARTA)." (3.4 Molecular gas mass and far-infrared lines)
   - software: GILDAS, CARTA
   - assumption: delta_gdr = 100 and alpha_CI = 17 +/- 3  
  > "assuming $\delta_{\rm gdr}=100$ and $\alpha_{\rm [CI]}=17\pm3\,{\rm M_\odot\,K^{-1}\,km^{-1}\,s\,pc^{-2}}$" (4.1 Intrinsically cold or optically thick dust (or both)?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Thick-dust gas mass compared with the [CI]-derived gas mass  
  > "the lower gas mass from the thick dust model is in good agreement with the" (4.1 Intrinsically cold or optically thick dust (or both)?)
7. **Assess X-ray AGN**: Count Chandra photons in the 0.5-2 keV band, convert to flux, derive soft X-ray and bolometric luminosity  
  > "we detect 3 counts in the 0.5-2 keV band of Chandra" (3.5 Obscured AGN); "Using the soft X-ray to bolometric luminosity correction from (cite: Lusso2012_Lbol) we obtained a bolometric luminosity of" (3.5 Obscured AGN)
   - parameter: Flux conversion rate from Civano et al. 2016  
  > "adopting the conversion rate from (cite: Civano2016)" (3.5 Obscured AGN)
   - assumption: Flux treated as a lower limit because of dust  
  > "Since XS55 is extremely dusty, this flux should be considered a lower limit" (3.5 Obscured AGN)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note the detection could be spurious or from star formation  
  > "we cannot exclude that the detection could be spurious, or is originating from star-formation" (3.5 Obscured AGN)
8. **Test whether dust is optically thick**: Three diagnostics: gas mass comparison, dust opacity tau_100um and Sigma_SFR, and Sigma_IR vs T_dust  
  > "we apply three methods of diagnosing optically thick dust from (cite: Jin2022)" (4.1 Intrinsically cold or optically thick dust (or both)?)
   - assumption: Dust opacity kappa from Jones et al. 2013, spherical symmetry, R_e from the dust continuum  
  > "where $\kappa$ is adopted from (cite: Jones2013)" (4.1 Intrinsically cold or optically thick dust (or both)?); "assuming spherical symmetry" (4.1 Intrinsically cold or optically thick dust (or both)?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Thresholds tau_100um > 1 and Sigma_SFR > 20 from Jin et al. 2022  
  > "With both $\tau_{\rm 100\mu m}>1$ and $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}>20\,{\rm M_\odot\,yr^{-1}\,kpc^{-2}}$" (4.1 Intrinsically cold or optically thick dust (or both)?)

## Qualitative findings

- XS55 is a massive main-sequence galaxy with optically thick FIR dust and a surprisingly cold dust temperature  
  > "The dust of XS55 is optically thick in the far infrared (FIR) with a surprisingly cold dust temperature" (abstract)
- XS55 is tentatively detected in X-rays with Chandra and XMM-Newton, suggesting an AGN  
  > "XS55 is tentatively detected in X-rays with both Chandra and XMM-Newton, suggesting an active galactic nucleus (AGN) nature." (abstract)
- CO(5-4) is more extended than the dust continuum with a tentative velocity gradient

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Red SCUBA-2 colour suggested a FIR photo-z above 6, so ALMA 3 mm line scans were used to get a spectroscopic redshift  
  > "the red SCUBA-2 colour suggests a FIR photometric redshift of $z>6$" (2.1 Selection); "Consequently, XS55 was followed up by two ALMA 3 mm line scan projects in Cycle 9" (2.1 Selection)
- Why this method: CO and [CI] line detections with (sub)mm facilities are an efficient route to redshifts for optically faint DSFGs  
  > "(sub)mm facilities like ALMA and NOEMA, are more efficient in confirming their redshifts via detecting CO and/or" (Introduction)
- Alternative: z = 3.66 (bright line as CO(4-3)) rejected: [CI](1-0) not detected at 105.6 GHz and implausibly high M_dust/M*  
  > "we found $z=3.66$ is very unlikely" (3.1 Redshift identification); "the $z=3.66$ \[CI\](1-0) is not detected at the expected frequency 105.6 GHz" (3.1 Redshift identification)
- Alternative: Point source plus two Sersic profiles left a ring of residual in F444W, three Sersic profiles preferred  
  > "there is a ring of emission left in the residual" (3.2 Morphology)
- Alternative: Elliptical Gaussian fit to the dust continuum gave no useful constraints, point source adopted  
  > "while fitting with an elliptical Gaussian does not yield useful constraints" (2.2 ALMA)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low spatial resolution of the CO data prevents deciding whether the CO is a rotating disk  
  > "given the low spatial resolution of the CO data, high resolution data are needed to confirm whether it is a rotating disk" (4.2 Stable disk, outflow, or merger?)
- Limitation (authors): Low significance of X-ray detection; could be spurious or from star formation
