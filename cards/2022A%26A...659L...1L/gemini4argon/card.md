## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate visibilities and refine absolute flux scale**: Run ALMA pipeline in CASA, fit 5-degree polynomials to ALMA/SMA calibrator monitoring data to interpolate 233 GHz flux on observing date, fit calibrator visibilities, and rescale visibilities by 0.99 (SB1) and 0.995 (SB2).  
  > "Initial steps of the data reduction were performed in the CASA reduction package via the ALMA pipeline (cite: muders14), providing a set of visibilities as a function of baseline between each antenna pair." (Observations and data reduction); "Visibility fitting was performed for the two flux calibrators, and visibilities were rescaled (by factors 0.99 and 0.995 for SB1 and SB2, respectively, from the ALMA pipeline calibration)" (Observations and data reduction); "We fitted the 91.5, 103.5, and $\sim$ 340 GHz fluxes with 5-degree polynomials, from which the spectral index over this frequency range and the 233 GHz flux was determined as a function of time." (Absolute flux calibration)
   - software: CASA, ALMA pipeline
   - parameter: Rescaling factors: 0.99 (SB1) and 0.995 (SB2)  
  > "visibilities were rescaled (by factors 0.99 and 0.995 for SB1 and SB2, respectively, from the ALMA pipeline calibration)" (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Assumed flux calibrator fluxes: 1.22 Jy for J2258-2758 and 1.26 Jy for J0519-4546  
  > "| SB1 | 08-Aug-2021 | 1624 sec | J2258-2758 | 1.22 Jy | J0253-5411 | 0.374 Jy | 0.109$\pm$0.016 mJy | -0.091"   -0.249" |" (Table 1); "| SB2 | 08-Aug-2021 | 1613 sec | J0519-4546 | 1.26 Jy | J0253-5411 | 0.389 Jy | 0.147$\pm$0.016 mJy | -0.100"   -0.262" |" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Spectral index of -0.68 for J2258-2758 between 225.5 GHz and 233 GHz  
  > "Interpolating between 225.5 GHz measurements from July 6, July 9 and August 17, 2021, and applying a spectral index of -0.68 (see Fig. [2](#fig:calibration).1) leads to a 233 GHz flux of 1.24 Jy" (Absolute flux calibration)
   - fitting: 5-degree polynomial fit to calibrator flux monitoring data; visibility fitting for flux and phase calibrators  
  > "We fitted the 91.5, 103.5, and $\sim$ 340 GHz fluxes with 5-degree polynomials, from which the spectral index over this frequency range and the 233 GHz flux was determined as a function of time." (Absolute flux calibration)
   - validation: Phase calibrator J0253-5441 fluxes measured independently in SB1 (0.374 Jy) and SB2 (0.389 Jy) agreed within 4%.  
  > "Visibility fitting provided two independent values of the flux of J0253-5441, found to be consistent within 4 % (see Table [\[observations\]](#observations)), confirming the quality of the flux scale." (Observations and data reduction)
2. **Measure target continuum flux, astrometric offset, and constrain coma visibility contribution**: Weight-average real part of visibilities in 400-m wide uv-radius bins and fit visibility curve V(sigma) with point-like, uniform disk (J1 Bessel function), and point + 1/sigma coma models.  
  > "Fig. [1](#fig:fit_uv) shows the real part of the observed visibilities for , weight-averaged in 400-m wide $\sigma$ bins, the latter being plotted in terms of $\lambda$." (Estimate of coma contribution); "Fitting the V($\sigma$) curve with the sum of a constant and a 1 / $\sigma$ term provides no better fit than the previous point-like and $<$ 40 mas disk models" (Estimate of coma contribution)
   - parameter: uv-radius bin width: 400 m  
  > "Fig. [1](#fig:fit_uv) shows the real part of the observed visibilities for , weight-averaged in 400-m wide $\sigma$ bins, the latter being plotted in terms of $\lambda$." (Estimate of coma contribution)
   - assumption: Coma brightness distribution varies as 1/rho, leading to V(sigma) proportional to 1/sigma  
  > "Extended emission for a coma brightness distribution varying as 1/$\rho$, where $\rho$ is the distance to comet center, shows up as visibilities V($\sigma$) $\propto$ 1 / $\sigma$ (cite: bockelee10)." (Estimate of coma contribution)
   - fitting: Chi-squared analysis of visibility models  
  > "From $\chi^2$ analysis, visibility fitting cannot distinguish between disk sizes smaller than $\sim$40 mas, which is to be expected given the $\sim$65 mas resolution achieved and the measurements’ S/N." (Estimate of coma contribution)
   - validation: Point-like + 1/sigma coma model provides no better fit than point-like or <40 mas disk models; 3-sigma upper limit on coma visibility at 220 klambda is 0.1 mJy.  
  > "Fitting the V($\sigma$) curve with the sum of a constant and a 1 / $\sigma$ term provides no better fit than the previous point-like and $<$ 40 mas disk models" (Estimate of coma contribution); "A 3$\sigma$ upper limit to the contribution of the coma to the visibility at $\sigma$ = 270 m (220 k$\lambda$) can be set at the 0.1 mJy level (orange curves in Fig. [1](#fig:fit_uv))." (Estimate of coma contribution)
3. **Model theoretical dust production rate and 233 GHz coma thermal emission**: Derive dust production rate Q_d from optical Af-rho = 150 m using rescaled Mie scattering ratios, then compute dust thermal flux at 233 GHz in a 0.064" Gaussian beam using Mie and effective medium theory.  
  > "Our estimation of the coma flux density at 233 GHz is based on two steps: 1) the determination of the dust production rate $Q_{\rm d}$ from the measured $Af\rho$ using the Mie scattering calculations of (cite: 2012Icar..221..721F); 2) the computation of the dust thermal emission using the model of (cite: 2017MNRAS.469S.443B), also using Mie theory." (Dust signal estimation)
   - parameter: Af-rho = 150 m  
  > "In practice we used the $Af\rho$ value of $\sim$ 150 m deduced from the observations of C/2014 UN$_{271}$ on 29 June 2021 ($r_{\rm h}$ = 20.15 ua) (cite: Dekelver21) (cite: bernardinelli21b)." (Dust signal estimation)
   - parameter: Particle size index beta = 3, 3.5, 4; a_min = 0.01 um; a_max = 10 um and 1 cm  
  > "We considered a minimum size of 0.01 $\mu$m, and maximum sizes $a_{\rm max}$ of 10 $\mu$m and 1 cm." (Dust signal estimation); "the rescaled $Q_{\rm d}$/$Af\rho$ ratio and the derived dust production rate $Q_{\rm d}$ for C/2014 UN$_{271}$ are given in Table [1](#tab:dust) for size indexes of 3, 3.5, and 4" (Dust production rate)
   - parameter: Gaussian beam HPBW = 0.064"  
  > "The computed flux densities in a Gaussian beam of half-power beam width HPBW = 0.064" (corresponding to the ALMA synthesized beam) are given in the last column Table [1](#tab:dust)" (Thermal flux of the dust)
   - assumption: Phase function ratio p(3 deg)/p(40 deg) = 2.42; dust velocity v(a) = 0.083 a^-0.5 m/s; nucleus density 500 kg m^-3; dust density 1000 kg m^-3; CO production rate 4e28 mol s^-1 in 45 deg half-aperture cone  
  > "We rescaled $Q_{\rm d}$/$Af\rho$ to a phase angle of 3$^{\circ}$, assuming a phase function ratio $p$($\phi$=3$^{\circ}$)/$p$($\phi$=40$^{\circ}$) = 2.42." (Dust production rate); "we derived $v$ = 83 m/s for $a$ = 1 $\mu$m for , using a nucleus radius of 68 km, nucleus and dust densities of 500 kg m$^{-3}$ and 1000 kg m$^{-3}$, respectively, and a CO production rate of 4$\times$10$^{28}$ mol s$^{-1}$ emitted in cone of 45$^{\circ}$ half-aperture." (Dust production rate)
   - assumption: Dust composition: amorphous carbon matrix with amorphous olivine inclusions (Fe:Mg = 50:50), carbon/olivine mass ratio = 1  
  > "We considered a matrix of amorphous carbon with inclusions of amorphous olivine with a Fe:Mg composition of 50:50, and set the carbon/olivine mass ratio to unity." (Thermal flux of the dust)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared predicted visibility at 220 klambda (0.066 mJy for extreme beta=3, a_max=1 cm case) against the measured 3-sigma upper limit of 0.1 mJy.  
  > "Another way of seeing it is that this extreme case corresponds to a visibility of 0.015 $\times$ 4.4 = 0.066 mJy at 220 k$\lambda$, well within the 3-$\sigma$ measured upper limit of 0.1 mJy." (Visibility curve for dust thermal emission)
4. **Determine nucleus surface-equivalent diameter and geometric albedo via NEATM**: Apply Near-Earth Asteroid Thermal Model (NEATM) to the 0.128+-0.012 mJy nucleus flux combined with converted optical absolute magnitudes HV and HR, iterating once on albedo and running 40,000 Monte Carlo realizations for uncertainties.  
  > "From NEATM modelling combined with the $H_r$ magnitude, we determine a surface-equivalent diameter of 137$\pm$17 km and a red geometric albedo of 5.3$\pm$1.2 %." (abstract); "so iterating once on the albedo in NEATM was sufficient." (Nucleus diameter and albedo); "we randomly generated a large set (40,000) of synthetic data and model inputs, based on gaussian-added noise on each parameter at the appropriate level (cite: mueller11), solving for $D$ and $p_R$ in each case." (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
   - parameter: Beaming factor eta = 1.175+-0.42 (restricted to >0.6)  
  > "we adopted a beaming factor $\eta$ = 1.175$\pm$0.42, based on measurements of 85 Centaurs and TNOs (cite: lellouch13; lellouch17)." (Nucleus diameter and albedo); "In doing so, we restricted $\eta$ values to be $>$0.6 (cite: mommert12), as well as $\epsilon_b$ $<$ 1 and $\epsilon_r$ $<$ 1." (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
   - parameter: Bolometric emissivity epsilon_b = 0.90+-0.06 (<1) and relative radio emissivity epsilon_r = 0.70+-0.13 (<1)  
  > "We also specified a bolometric emissivity $\epsilon_b$ = 0.90$\pm$0.06 and a *relative* radio emissivity $\epsilon_r$ = $\epsilon_{mm}$ / $\epsilon_b$ = 0.70$\pm$0.13" (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
   - parameter: HV = 8.21+-0.05, HR = 7.75+-0.05, V-R = 0.46+-0.02, phase integral q ~ 0.4  
  > "yielding $H_V$ = 8.21$\pm$0.05, $H_R$ = 7.75$\pm$0.05, and V-R = 0.46$\pm$0.02, i.e. $p_R$ = 1.11$\pm$0.02 $p_V$." (Nucleus diameter and albedo); "(where a reasonable value of $q$, the phase integral, is $\sim$ 0.4)" (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
   - assumption: 100% of measured 0.128+-0.012 mJy thermal flux comes from the nucleus; V_sun = -26.76, (V-R)_sun = 0.35  
  > "In the rest of the paper, the measured thermal flux of 0.128$\pm$0.012 mJy is attributed to the nucleus only." (Estimate of coma contribution); "Using V$_{\odot}$ = -26.76 and (V–R)$_{\odot}$ = 0.35, one obtains $D$ = 1330 km /$\sqrt{p_V}$ 10$^{-0.2 H_V}$ from V band and $D$ = 1132 km /$\sqrt{p_R}$ 10$^{-0.2 H_R}$ from R band." (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo simulation with 40,000 realizations with Gaussian-added noise on flux and model parameters; error bars defined as central 68.3% of results  
  > "we randomly generated a large set (40,000) of synthetic data and model inputs, based on gaussian-added noise on each parameter at the appropriate level (cite: mueller11), solving for $D$ and $p_R$ in each case." (Nucleus diameter and albedo); "Those provided best fit values and error bars (defined to include the central 68.3 % of the results) for the diameter and albedo." (Results and discussion)
   - validation: Inferred apparent diameter of 9.6 mas is consistent with the point-like appearance (<40 mas) in the visibility curve.  
  > "This yields an apparent diameter of 9.6 mas at $\Delta$ = 19.68 au, consistent with the point-like appearance of the object in the visibility curve (Fig. [\[fig:fit_uv\]](#fig:fit_uv))." (Results and discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- C/2014 UN271 is by far the largest Oort-cloud object ever found (almost twice as large as Hale-Bopp) and the largest known standard comet in the Solar System.  
  > "This confirms that C/2014 UN$_{271}$ is by far the largest Oort-cloud object ever found (almost twice as large as comet C/1995 O1 Hale-Bopp), and except for the Centaur 95P/Chiron which shows outburst-like activity, the largest known comet in the Solar System." (abstract)
- The geometric albedo of C/2014 UN271 is typical of smaller comets, supporting a size-independent universal comet nucleus albedo.  
  > "On the other hand, the C/2014 UN$_{271}$ albedo is typical of comets, adding credence for a “universal" comet nucleus albedo." (abstract); "Our measurement thus adds evidence against a dependence of comet nucleus albedo on size." (Results and discussion)
- With its ~5% albedo and 5-10% / 100 nm spectral slope, C/2014 UN271 falls in the middle of the dark/neutral cluster identified in the Kuiper Belt.  
  > "We also note that with its $\sim$5 % albedo and 5-10 % /100 nm spectral slope,  falls in the middle of the “dark/neutral" cluster identified in the Kuiper Belt (cite: lacerda14)." (Results and discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA extended configuration C8 (~0.064" resolution) was chosen to filter out extended dust coma emission while measuring the compact nucleus thermal emission.  
  > "The high spatial resolution was chosen in order to filter out any dust contribution." (abstract); "This yielded baselines in the range 92m – 8282m, and a synthesized beam of $\sim$0.065" for robust weighting (0.5), larger than the expected nucleus size ($\sim$10 mas) but allowing to filter out dust emission." (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: NEATM was adopted instead of a thermophysical model because the nucleus shape and spin parameters (pole orientation and shape) are unknown.  
  > "In the lack of knowledge of nucleus shape and spin parameters (pole orientation and shape), a thermophysical model is pointless, and we instead adopted a NEATM model" (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
- Alternative: Thermophysical model (rejected due to lack of knowledge of nucleus shape and spin parameters)  
  > "In the lack of knowledge of nucleus shape and spin parameters (pole orientation and shape), a thermophysical model is pointless, and we instead adopted a NEATM model" (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
- Alternative: Submillimeter-sized coma particles emitted at low velocities as argued by Farnham et al. 2021 (considered inconsistent with maximum liftable size estimates, and shown to still produce negligible thermal flux)  
  > "We note finally that although (cite: farnham21) argued that the coma of  consists mostly of submillimeter sized particles emitted at low velocities, this does not appear consistent with our above estimate of the maximum liftable size." (Visibility curve for dust thermal emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Lack of knowledge of nucleus shape and spin parameters (pole orientation and shape) prevents thermophysical modeling.  
  > "In the lack of knowledge of nucleus shape and spin parameters (pole orientation and shape), a thermophysical model is pointless, and we instead adopted a NEATM model" (Nucleus diameter and albedo)
- Limitation (authors): Visibility fitting cannot distinguish between disk sizes smaller than ~40 mas given the ~65 mas resolution and measurement S/N.  
  > "From $\chi^2$ analysis, visibility fitting cannot distinguish between disk sizes smaller than $\sim$40 mas, which is to be expected given the $\sim$65 mas resolution achieved and the measurements’ S/N." (Estimate of coma contribution)
- Limitation (authors): Diameter and albedo uncertainties are dominated by model input errors, principally the uncertainty on the relative radio emissivity epsilon_r.  
  > "We find $D$ = 137$\pm$17 km [^1] and $p_R$ = 5.3$\pm$1.2 %, where error bars are dominated by model errors – and principally by the uncertainty on $\epsilon_r$." (Results and discussion)
- Limitation (authors): The assumed CO production rate of 4e28 mol s^-1 used in the dust velocity calculation is somewhat arbitrary.  
  > "The assumed CO production rate (1860 kg $^{-1}$) is reasonable but somewhat arbitrary." (Dust production rate)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The relative radio emissivity (epsilon_r = 0.70 +- 0.13) is adopted from TNOs/Centaurs rather than directly measured at multiple wavelengths for C/2014 UN271, dominating the final diameter uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The two scheduling blocks show a ~2-sigma difference in measured target flux density (0.109+-0.016 mJy in SB1 vs 0.147+-0.016 mJy in SB2), which is averaged together without detailed discussion of potential short-term rotational variability.
