## Analysis recipe

1. **Remove the optical stellar continuum**: Model and subtract the MUSE stellar continuum with STARLIGHT; fit emission lines in spaxels with AoN >3.  
  > "We used STARLIGHT (cite: Cid05) to model the stellar continuum." (Archival VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy); "After the continuum subtraction, we fit the emission lines in each spaxel with amplitude-over-noise" (Archival VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy); "ratio is larger than three with Gaussian functions to derive the 2-D maps of the brightest emission lines." (Archival VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy)
   - software: STARLIGHT
   - parameter: AoN >3; amplitude is the line peak.  
  > "amplitude-over-noise (AoN; note that the amplitude is the peak of the emission line) ratio is larger than three" (Archival VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy)
   - assumption: STELIB library spectra of various ages and metallicities reproduce the continuum.  
  > "This code combines library spectra (STELIB; (cite: Bruzual03)) of various ages and metallicities to reproduce the input spectrum." (Archival VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy)
   - fitting: Gaussian functions for emission-line maps.  
  > "with Gaussian functions to derive the 2-D maps of the brightest emission lines." (Archival VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure stellar rotation**: Fit MUSE Ca II triplet stellar kinematics using pPXF and Voronoi binning.  
  > "We derived the stellar kinematics from the Ca II triplet lines using MUSE data at $\lambda>$8000$\AA$ and the penalised pixel fitting (pPXF) method (cite: Cappellari04; Cappellari17)." (Stellar kinematics); "To achieve the best spatial resolution possible with good S/N, we used the Voronoi binning algorithm (cite: Cappellari03) for S/N$=$40." (Stellar kinematics)
   - software: pPXF, Voronoi binning algorithm
   - parameter: λ >8000 Å; S/N =40.  
  > "using MUSE data at $\lambda>$8000$\AA$" (Stellar kinematics); "we used the Voronoi binning algorithm (cite: Cappellari03) for S/N$=$40." (Stellar kinematics)
   - fitting: penalised pixel fitting  
  > "the penalised pixel fitting (pPXF) method (cite: Cappellari04; Cappellari17)." (Stellar kinematics)
   - validation: Stellar kinematics agree within errors with the CO(2-1) rotating-disk model.  
  > "the stellar kinematics is in fairly good agreement within the errors with the CO(2-1) rotating disk model" (Stellar kinematics)
3. **Model molecular disk rotation**: Fit a rotating-disk model to the CO(2-1) kinematics over a larger FoV than Herrero18.  
  > "Following (cite: Herrero18), we fitted a rotating disk model to the CO(2-1) kinematics using the $^{3D}$BAROLO code (cite: Teodoro15) but covering a larger FoV." (Rotating disk model)
   - software: 3D BAROLO
   - parameter: Fixed i =35°, PA =320°, v_sys =1994 km s^-1.  
  > "We fixed the following parameters: inclination i$=35^\circ$, the position angle of the major kinematic axis PA$=320^\circ$ and the systemic velocity v$_{sys}=1994$ km s$^{-1}$, as derived by (cite: Herrero18)." (Rotating disk model)
   - assumption: The main CO component traces galaxy rotation.  
  > "The main component traces galaxy rotation." (Rotating disk model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted disk velocity agrees with the brightest CO component where modelled.  
  > "In the region modelled by the rotating galaxy disk, there is a good agreement between the predicted velocity from the model and the brightest CO(2-1) component." (CO(2-1) data — Individual regions)
4. **Fit regional molecular and ionized spectra**: Extract 91 regions with 1.2″ × 1.2″ apertures; fit CO and [O iii] with Gaussian components using PySpecKit.  
  > "To study the kinematics of the ionized and molecular gas of NGC 5643, we selected 91 different regions." (Region selection); "we chose 1.2$\ensuremath{''}\times$1.2$\ensuremath{''}$ square apertures which are $\sim$2.5 times the MUSE FWHM" (Region selection); "To perform the emission line fitting we used the *PySpecKit* code as in Section [3.2](#molecular_fit)." (MUSE data — Individual regions)
   - software: PySpecKit
   - parameter: Add second/third components for residuals >3 times the standard deviation; retain multicomponent [O iii] fits when residuals improve considerably.  
  > "only when necessary (significant residuals of the fit; $>$3 times the standard deviation) we used two or three Gaussians for the fit." (CO(2-1) data — Individual regions); "Finally, we only considered for the analysis those fits using two/three components when the residuals improved considerably" (MUSE data — Individual regions)
   - parameter: [O iii] AoN >3; doublet shares velocity and widths; widths exceed instrumental FWHM ~2.5 Å.  
  > "we fit the \[O iii\] doublet emission lines (when the \[O iii\]$\lambda$5007$\AA$ emission line has AoN$>$3) simultaneously with the same velocity and widths" (MUSE data — Individual regions); "which are always larger than the instrumental one (i.e. MUSE nominal spectral resolution FWHM$\sim$2.5$\AA$)." (MUSE data — Individual regions)
   - assumption: Fixed [O iii] doublet ratio 1/3.  
  > "We also fixed the \[O iii\] doublet to its theoretical value (1/3; (cite: Osterbrock06))." (MUSE data — Individual regions)
   - fitting: PySpecKit χ² minimization; one to three Gaussians.  
  > "We used the *PySpecKit* code (cite: Ginsburg11), which produces the best fit using the minimization of $\chi^2$." (CO(2-1) data — Individual regions); "only when necessary (significant residuals of the fit; $>$3 times the standard deviation) we used two or three Gaussians for the fit." (CO(2-1) data — Individual regions)
   - validation: Inspect residuals and require substantial improvement for multicomponent [O iii] fits.  
  > "Finally, we only considered for the analysis those fits using two/three components when the residuals improved considerably" (MUSE data — Individual regions)
5. **Separate rotation, inflow and outflow**: Identify rotating components with the disk model, brightness and velocity continuity; subtract disk velocities; use bar and cone geometry to distinguish inflow/outflow.  
  > "When possible (high signal-to-noise ratio) we use the derived rotating disk model to identify the rotation component. Otherwise, we select the brightest line which we associated with the galaxy disk." (Stacking regions); "Once we identified the rotation disk components, we subtract the corresponding velocity of the galaxy disk component in each selected region from the other non-circular velocity components." (Spatially resolved kinematics); "we interpret the CO(2-1) blueshifted (reshifted) non-circular motions in the northern (southern) part of the spiral arms as local inflow." (Spatially resolved kinematics)

## Qualitative findings

- Narrow [O iii] traces disk rotation; broader components trace the ionized outflow.  
  > "The narrow component traces the rotation of the gas in the disk." (Conclusions); "The others, which have broader profiles (median $\sigma_{broad}^{[O\,III]}=$233$\pm$75 km s$^{-1}$), are related to the ionized gas outflow." (Conclusions)
- Molecular non-circular motions include bar-driven local inflow and outflow.  
  > "One is associated with the host galaxy rotating disk and the others with inflowing and outflowing motions. The streaming motions (local inflow) are due to the presence of a large-scale stellar bar." (Conclusions)
- Molecular gas in the disk is interpreted as entrained by the AGN wind.  
  > "the CO(2-1) non-circular motions in the remaining regions in the east-west \[OIII\] outflow region can be explained as molecular gas entrained by the AGN wind and outflowing in the plane of the galaxy (cite: Herrero18)." (Spatially resolved kinematics)
- Molecular gas dominates outflow mass and mass rate.  
  > "Thus, the molecular phase dominates the outflow mass and outflow mass rate, with ratios between 600 (110) and 360 (70) over the ionized one." (Conclusions)
- Tentative molecular-gas clearing coincides with wind deceleration on the eastern side.  
  > "We find tentative evidence of clearing of the molecular gas due to the interaction of the AGN wind with molecular gas in the galaxy" (Spatially resolved outflow properties); "including the outflowing component coinciding with the location where the AGN wind is decelerated." (Spatially resolved outflow properties)
- Radiative and kinetic feedback modes are suggested to coexist.  
  > "These results suggest that radiative and kinetic AGN feedback modes coexist in NGC 5643." (Integrated Outflow Properties)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Nearby Seyferts provide spatial resolution for multiphase outflow measurements within one galaxy.  
  > "Except for a few nearby AGN" (Introduction); "there is a lack of detailed multiphase studies using various regions within the same source." (Introduction); "afford the necessary physical resolution to study the multiphase outflow properties in different regions." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Stacking improves S/N and reveals weak kinematic components for uniform outflow-property measurements.  
  > "This stacking technique improves greatly the S/N of the extracted spectra and thus allows us to detect weak kinematic components which will be used to derive the outflow properties in a uniform way" (Region selection)
- Alternative: Bar streaming explains selected local inflows, but not all east–west non-circular CO components.  
  > "we interpret the CO(2-1) blueshifted (reshifted) non-circular motions in the northern (southern) part of the spiral arms as local inflow." (Spatially resolved kinematics); "the CO(2-1) non-circular motions in the remaining regions in the east-west \[OIII\] outflow region can be explained as molecular gas entrained by the AGN wind and outflowing in the plane of the galaxy (cite: Herrero18)." (Spatially resolved kinematics)
- Alternative: An extraplanar molecular component is disfavoured because non-circular velocities generally lie below escape velocity.  
  > "it is not expected an extra-planar outflow component since generally the non-circular velocity is below the escape velocity ($\sim$200 km/s at 5$''$), and any extra-planar component would fall back onto the disk." (Position-velocity diagrams)
- Alternative: Instrumental wings cannot explain the detected broad optical lines.  
  > "We find that the MUSE/VLT instrumental profile at $\lambda$5007$\AA$ is slightly asymmetry, but the detected broad lines in our work cannot be explained by an instrumental effect." (Stacking regions)
- Alternative: ULIRG α_CO tested alongside Galactic α_CO.  
  > "for comparison, we also estimate the molecular gas outflow properties using a lower CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor" (CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor); "We find that the main results of this work do not change significantly by using different CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factors." (CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor)
- Alternative: Ionized outflow properties also calculated without extinction correction.  
  > "Here we also derived the various outflow properties profiles (see Fig. [19](#c1)) without taking into account the extinction correction." (Observed outflow properties)
- How they decided it was enough: AoN >3; add components for >3σ residuals and retain multicomponent fits when residuals improve considerably.  
  > "For those fits with single Gaussians which show residuals in the \[O iii\] region above 3 times the standard deviation, we included a second kinematic component." (MUSE data — Individual regions); "If residuals above 3 times the standard deviation were still present, we included a third kinematic component." (MUSE data — Individual regions); "Finally, we only considered for the analysis those fits using two/three components when the residuals improved considerably" (MUSE data — Individual regions)
- Limitation (authors): Large-scale faint CO emission could not be modelled with BAROLO; velocity extrapolation and brightness-based identification were used.  
  > "Since we could not model the large-scale faint emission with BAROLO, we extrapolated the velocity field when possible to identify the galaxy disk emission line in the fitted kinematic components." (CO(2-1) data — Individual regions); "In those cases, we considered the brightest feature as the galaxy disk component." (CO(2-1) data — Individual regions)
- Limitation (authors): CO conversion factor poorly constrained; molecular masses depend on α_CO and line ratio.  
  > "the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor is poorly constrained for Seyfert galaxies." (CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor); "Note that the molecular gas mass depends on the assumed conversion factor, $\alpha_{\rm CO}$, and CO(1-0)/CO(2-1) ratio." (Spatially resolved outflow properties)
- Limitation (authors): Electron density is the largest ionized-mass uncertainty; [S II] can give lower densities than auroral/transauroral diagnostics.  
  > "The largest source of uncertainty in this measure is related to the electron density (see e.g. (cite: Harrison18))." (Spatially resolved outflow properties); "in (cite: Davies20) the authors found that this method estimate significantly lower electron densities in AGN photoionized gas than using auroral and transauroral lines." (MUSE data — Radial profile along the outflow)
- Limitation (authors): Intrinsic spiral-arm asymmetry could affect the inferred gas deficit.  
  > "However, it is possible that intrinsic variations in the surface brightness due to asymmetry could affect this measurement." (Destruction/Clearing of the Molecular Gas)
- Limitation (authors): Spiral-arm masses exclude nuclear regions containing much of the molecular mass.  
  > "for the spiral arms we did not include regions in the nuclear regions which accounts for the bulk of the molecular gas mass both in the disk and in the molecular outflow." (Destruction/Clearing of the Molecular Gas)
- Limitation (authors): Wind model assumes spherical symmetry and omits ISM resistance.  
  > "Note that this model assumed a spherical symmetry, for the sake of simplicity." (Outlfow Wind Analytic Model); "This approach takes into account the drag forces from gravity stopping and turning back the gas outflow but not the possible deceleration of the NLR outflow due to the resistance of the ISM." (Outlfow Wind Analytic Model)
