## Qualitative findings

- The compact millimeter sources are interpreted as thermal emitters in dense gas cores.  
  > "From the millimeter spectral index, we infer that the ALMA continuum signal of those compact objects traces thermal emission emanating from the center of dense gas cores." (Conclusions)
- The fragmenting continuum spiral has no Band 3 or Band 4 counterparts at the achieved sensitivities.  
  > "We do not find any band$\,$3 or band$\,$4 counterparts to these clumps." (ALMA continuum emission)
- E is deeply embedded and exhibits class-0 protostar characteristics, a CO outflow and a collimated SiO jet.  
  > "V960$\,$Mon$\,$E shows characteristics of a class-0 protostar and drives an outflow composed of a slower component traced by $^{12}$CO and a faster, more collimated jet identified through SiO emission." (Conclusions); "We conclude that V960$\,$Mon$\,$E is deeply embedded, causing its flux to be heavily attenuated at short wavelengths." (SPHERE total intensity)
- SE is elongated, bright in millimeter continuum and shows CO rotation around a compact central object.  
  > "It clearly shows that the emission is elongated along this axis, ruling out emission from a single point source." (Sources and Limits); "V960$\,$Mon$\,$SE is the most prominent in mm continuum emission and we detect rotation in $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO line data, indicating a compact object at its center." (Conclusions)
- C18O and DCN associate gas with the fragmented continuum spiral; their distributions are anti-correlated.  
  > "This comparison highlights a clear anti-correlation: DCN is more prominent in the outer regions of the spiral, whereas it is depleted toward the inner clumps, where C$^{18}$Ois strongest." (DCN); "The presence of DCN in the outer clumps implies a substantial gas reservoir at these locations, and the C$^{18}$O detection farther inward suggests that gas extends along the entire fragmented continuum spiral." (DCN)
- DCN with absent DCO+ and N2D+ in the spiral supports recent warming, potentially from the FUor event or clump contraction.  
  > "Therefore, the observed DCN emission, combined with the lack of DCO$^{+}$ and N$_2$D$^{+}$, supports a scenario in which the local chemistry is transitioning from a cold to a mildly warmer state." (DCN); "This is consistent with the recent FUor event, which is expected to heat the surrounding region through irradiation." (DCN); "However, a temperature increase could also be connected to contraction of the related clumps." (DCN)
- The SE connection likely represents infall onto V960 Mon.  
  > "We infer that the structure is situated behind V960$\,$Mon, and from counterparts in $^{13}$CO channels we find that it is blue-shifted – the material is moving towards V960$\,$Mon, likely representing infall onto the system." (Conclusions)
- N has no detected molecular tracers or connections; its physical association with V960 Mon is uncertain.  
  > "The northern companion, V960$\,$Mon$\,$N, shows no trace of any molecular tracer, nor does it show any connections to other objects in the field of view." (DCN); "This raises the question if V960$\,$Mon$\,$N is truly associated with V960$\,$Mon, or whether it is a fore- or background star." (DCN)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Probe the large-scale environment around an FUor with a previously reported fragmenting spiral using millimeter continuum, molecular lines and optical/NIR scattered light.  
  > "Here, we further study the case of V960$\,$Mon, turning our attention to the environment at an even larger scale ($\sim$10$^{4}$ au), presenting continuum and molecular line emission in the mm regime and scattered light in the optical and NIR." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Use reconstructed cubes to isolate line-emitting sources; select red MUSE channels for environmental signal; examine SPHERE total intensity to retain companion and multiply scattered light.  
  > "To achieve more robust detections and isolate emission from individual sources, we subsequently performed the analysis on the reconstructed data cubes for each line." (Molecular line emission); "Our analysis of the data revealed that the signals of interest, particularly those emanating from the large-scale environment of the FUor object, are predominantly found in the longer wavelength channels." (VLT/MUSE); "Consequently, we analyzed the total intensity in which photons from companions and multiple scattering events still contribute, even if at a contrast that is difficult to detect." (SPHERE total intensity)
- Alternative: Low N/E spectral indices may reflect dust scattering, temperature gradients or a partial optically thin free-free contribution.  
  > "(1) Dust emission is very optically thick at $>$93 GHz frequencies, and the value of $a_{\rm max}$ in the dominant (sub)millimeter emitters is a few times 100 µm (depending on the assumed dust composition and porosity)." (Source N, E); "(2) Dust emission is very optically thick, and there is a temperature gradient in the line of sight and the warmer emitters are obscured by the colder ones." (Source N, E); "The observed, lower $\alpha_{\rm 4/3}$ in these two sources may also be partly explained by the contribution of optically thin free-free emission." (Source N, E)
- Alternative: The SE connection could be a flyby bridge or colliding flows rather than infall when judged from optical/NIR morphology alone.  
  > "From the optical and NIR evidence alone, it is not discernible whether the feature connecting V960$\,$Mon$\,$SE and V960$\,$Mon represents infall onto the FUor." (Fragmentation and outburst induced by infall?); "A similar feature can also be observed during a flyby in which the gravitational interaction between the two objects creates an over-dense bridge (cite: Cuello2020), or from colliding flows in dense star formation regions (cite: Kuffmeier2019)." (Fragmentation and outburst induced by infall?)
- Alternative: Spirals may belong to a gravitationally unstable disk or be twisted infalling material.  
  > "The very extended structure around V960$\,$Mon raises the question of whether the spirals are part of a gravitationally unstable disk or if they arise from infalling material that becomes twisted as it moves inward." (Fragmentation and outburst induced by infall?)
- Alternative: The SiO velocity trend may be rotation rather than genuine acceleration.  
  > "However, this effect may not represent a genuine acceleration but could instead reflect jet rotation within $1''$." (SiO)
- Alternative: Double-peaked CO profiles may indicate rotation or self-absorption.  
  > "The double-peak feature for $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O probably indicates of rotation around the primary." (Line of sight Velocities); "However, we can not rule out that the diminution at the systematic velocity is due to self-absorption of the molecular line." (Line of sight Velocities)
- Alternative: A stellar intruder could eject or redirect fragments and promote outburst-producing inward migration.  
  > "Finally, we want to discuss the scenario that some of the observed mm-sources represent compact objects ejected from the circumstellar environment of V960$\,$Mon due to a stellar intruder, and the connection of this with the outbursting state of V960$\,$Mon." (Ejection from circumstellar environment?); "Thus, an interaction with an intruder could simultaneously explain the formation of the southeastern elongated feature that is connecting V960$\,$Mon$\,$SE and V960$\,$Mon, and V960$\,$Mon’s FUor outburst." (Ejection from circumstellar environment?)
- How they decided it was enough: No Band 3/4 self-calibration because low continuum SNR offered no expected significant improvement; supplement CO masks when faint emission persists across channels.  
  > "Because the continuum emission shows a low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in bands 3 and 4, and no significant improvement was expected, we did not perform any self-calibration."
