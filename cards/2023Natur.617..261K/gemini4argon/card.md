## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA mosaic datacubes and continuum**: Standard CASA pipeline calibration followed by CASA tclean imaging of the 7-pointing mosaic with natural weighting and a Gaussian uv-taper to circularize the beam; collapse 4 channels over 66 km s^-1 for moment-0 maps.  
  > "The ALMA data was processed using the CASA [34] pipeline for ALMA (version 5.4), using the standard calibration procedure. The final datacubes and the continuum images were obtained using CASA tclean task with natural weighting to maximize sensitivity." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "For the moment-0 [C ii] maps, the four channels (62.5 MHz; ≈ 66 km s − 1 ) that cover the emission line were collapsed" (ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting  
  > "using CASA tclean task with natural weighting to maximize sensitivity." (ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: uvtaper = 0 x 220 mas, PA = 119 deg  
  > "we adopted uvtaper (a Gaussian taper on the weights of the uv data) of 0 × 220 mas and a position angle (PA) 119 degree" (ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: moment-0 collapsed over 4 channels (62.5 MHz; ≈ 66 km s^-1)  
  > "For the moment-0 [C ii] maps, the four channels (62.5 MHz; ≈ 66 km s − 1 ) that cover the emission line were collapsed" (ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: survey radius ≈ 23" where primary beam response > 20%  
  > "survey field is defined as the area of ≈ 23 '' in radius where the relative response is above 20%." (Extended Data Fig. 1 caption)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Blind search for emission lines and fidelity assessment**: Run findclumps top-hat convolution over 4 to 17 channels on the datacubes for positive and negative peaks, crop duplicates within 1" and 280 MHz, fit an error function to positive/negative peak counts to compute detection fidelity, and verify detections by splitting data by polarization (XX vs YY), mosaic pointings, and exposure time halves.  
  > "We employed the findclumps algorithm [35], implemented in the interferopy package for Python [36], for our blind search for emission-line candidates." (Blind line search); "We ran the line search with frequency window sizes from 4 to 17 channels (corresponding to ≈ 66–282 km s − 1 ) and adopted the offset thresholds of 1 '' and 280 MHz (300 km s − 1 ) for cropping duplicates." (Blind line search); "We processed separately the two independent XX and YY polarization correlations." (Reliability check of the detections)
   - software: interferopy, findclumps
   - parameter: frequency window sizes = 4 to 17 channels (≈ 66–282 km s^-1)  
  > "We ran the line search with frequency window sizes from 4 to 17 channels (corresponding to ≈ 66–282 km s − 1 )" (Blind line search)
   - parameter: duplicate cropping offsets = 1" and 280 MHz (300 km s^-1)  
  > "and adopted the offset thresholds of 1 '' and 280 MHz (300 km s − 1 ) for cropping duplicates." (Blind line search)
   - parameter: S/N > 6.0 selection threshold  
  > "We selected the two claimed [C ii] sources ([C ii] 1030A and B), detected in four-channel collapsed images of two SPWs, as the only sources that have > 6 σ significance" (Blind line search)
   - assumption: Relevant velocity interval for absorber association is ±700 km s^-1 around each absorber (contiguous 2280 km s^-1 interval)  
  > "For each of the three absorption systems, a velocity interval of ± 700 km s − 1 was taken as the relevant velocity range within which a source could be considered to be associated with the absorber." (Blind line search)
   - fitting: Error function fit to fidelity as a function of S/N  
  > "Expressing the fidelity with an error function, we found that 95% fidelity is reached as S / N = 5 . 8–6 . 2, with only small variations between the SPWs." (Blind line search)
   - validation: Independent data splits: XX and YY polarizations (detected at 4.2–4.7σ), two overlapping mosaic pointings reduced separately (3.4–5.3σ), and first vs second temporal halves of exposure (3.9–4.9σ).  
  > "As shown in Extended Data Fig. 3, the line emission is always detected with consistent observed fluxes at 4 . 2–4 . 7 σ at the expected spatial position (within < 0 . 2 '' ) and at the expected frequency in each of the two independent data sets." (Reliability check of the detections); "The claimed sources were detected with consistent fluxes at 3 . 4–5 . 3 σ ." (Reliability check of the detections); "Again, the line emission is consistently detected at the same spatial positions and at the expected frequencies in both the first and second halves of the data, at signal to noise ratios of between 3 . 9–4 . 9 σ ." (Reliability check of the detections)
3. **Identify line transition and estimate low-z CO interloper probability**: Calculate expected number counts of foreground CO(3-2) to CO(8-7) interlopers brighter than [C II]1030A in the survey volume using published Schechter luminosity functions, and compute probability of two interlopers falling within the 2280 km s^-1 velocity window.  
  > "Using published CO luminosity functions for the redshift range between 0 and 6 [37], we estimated that the expected number of CO sources within the whole datacube should be approximately 0.4 sources." (Line identification); "The chance of finding two unrelated CO sources within the velocity range of interest (across 2280 km s − 1 around the absorption systems; see above) is therefore of order (0 . 4 × 2280 / 7380) 2 ≈ 1 . 5%" (Line identification)
   - parameter: Survey volume: circular radius 23" across 4 SPWs (7380 km s^-1 total velocity interval)  
  > "within a larger datacube that covers a circular survey area of 23 '' (138 physical kpc (pkpc) at z = 5 . 7) and a total velocity interval 7380 km s − 1 , corresponding to ≈ 76 comoving Mpc (cMpc)." (Main); "and within the circular survey volume of radius 23 '' and the frequency (redshift) interval corresponding to the whole ALMA datacube composed of four SPWs." (Line identification)
   - assumption: CO(6-5) luminosity function at z = 2 also applies to CO(7-6) and CO(8-7)  
  > "The luminosity function of CO(6–5) at z = 2 was adopted also for CO(7–6) and CO(8–7) as no measurement is provided for these highest transitions." (Line identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-checked against deep HST ~9000 Å images where low-z (z <= 2) stellar continuum would be detected, but is not.  
  > "Non-detection of both sources in available deep HST images at ∼ 9000 ˚ A (see below) also argues against any interpretation that places these sources" (Methods, Line identification)
4. **Measure [C II] line properties, FIR continuum upper limits, and derive SFRs**: Extract 1D spectra within 0.24" radius of peak position, fit a single Gaussian profile, correct for primary beam response and 20% aperture loss, and convert [C II] luminosities and 2σ continuum upper limits to SFRs.  
  > "We measured the line properties by fitting a simple Gaussian profile to the observed one-dimensional spectra extracted from the spaxels enclosed within 0 . 24 '' of the peak position of the [C ii] sources." (The properties of the [C ii] sources); "These fluxes are the values after correction for the primary beam response (67% and 47% at the positions of [C ii]1030A and B, respectively), and for the ≈ 20% loss falling outside the aperture of 0 . 24 '' in radius for extracting the spectra." (The properties of the [C ii] sources)
   - parameter: extraction aperture radius = 0.24"  
  > "These ALMA spectra are extracted within radius 0 . 24 '' of the peak position." (Main)
   - parameter: primary beam response = 67% ([C II]1030A), 47% ([C II]1030B)  
  > "These fluxes are the values after correction for the primary beam response (67% and 47% at the positions of [C ii]1030A and B, respectively)" (The properties of the [C ii] sources)
   - parameter: aperture loss correction ≈ 20%  
  > "and for the ≈ 20% loss falling outside the aperture of 0 . 24 '' in radius for extracting the spectra." (The properties of the [C ii] sources)
   - assumption: Flat ΛCDM cosmology with Ω_Λ = 0.69, Ω_M = 0.31, H_0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1  
  > "Throughout this paper, we adopted a flat Λ Cold Dark Matter cosmology with Ω Λ = 0 . 69, Ω M = 0 . 31 and H 0 = 67 . 7 km s − 1 Mpc − 1 [32] when calculating physical parameters." (Cosmology)
   - assumption: log10(SFR) = -7.06 + 1.0 log10(L_[C II] / L_sun)  
  > "An empirical conversion relation of SFR = − 7 . 06 + 1 . 0 log 10 (L [C ii] /L ⊙ ) yields a [C ii]-based SFR of SFR [C ii] = 14 M ⊙ yr − 1 ([C ii]1030A) and 19 M ⊙ yr − 1 (B)" (The properties of the [C ii] sources)
   - assumption: νL_ν / L_IR = 0.13 at ν = 1900 GHz (modified blackbody with β = 1.8, T_dust = 41 K)  
  > "To convert this continuum flux to a total infrared luminosity ( L IR ; 8–1000 µm ), we adopted a conversion factor of νL ν /L IR = 0 . 13 ( ν = 1900 GHz) that was inferred using a template of the infrared spectral energy distribution (SED) of z ∼ 4 galaxies [39]." (The properties of the [C ii] sources); "The template is consistent with a modified blackbody ( S ν ≈ ν β / (exp( hν/kT dust ) − 1)) with a fixed index β = 1 . 8 and dust temperature T dust = 41 K." (The properties of the [C ii] sources)

  > "Finally, we converted the L IR to obscured SFR by adopting the relation (SFR IR ( M ⊙ yr − 1 ) = 1 . 01 × 10 − 10 L IR ( L ⊙ )) [40] converted to a [41] initial mass function" (The properties of the [C ii] sources)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian profile fit to 1D extracted spectrum  
  > "We measured the line properties by fitting a simple Gaussian profile to the observed one-dimensional spectra extracted from the spaxels enclosed within" (Methods, The properties of the [C ii] sources); "The red solid line shows a single-Gaussian model fit to the data." (Figure 2 caption)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit C IV and Si IV Voigt profiles in QSO J1030+0524 spectra**: Reduce VLT/X-Shooter and Magellan/FIRE spectra, normalize continuum via iterative cubic spline fits, and simultaneously fit a hierarchical Voigt profile model of 3 absorption components convolved to instrument resolutions using MCMC.  
  > "The model vector is optimized to fit all instruments simultaneously using a Markov-Chain Monte Carlo walker as implemented by the emcee python package [48], to determine confidence intervals from the posterior distribution." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524); "Prior to fitting absorption models, each spectrum was normalized by a continuum iteratively determined by cubic spline fits with outlier rejection." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524)
   - software: PypeIt, FIREHOSE, xtellcor, emcee
   - parameter: X-Shooter spectral resolution λ/Δλ = 5300 (0.9" slit); FIRE spectral resolution λ/Δλ = 6000 (0.6" slit)  
  > "All exposures were obtained with a 0 . 9 '' slit, resulting in spectral resolution is λ/ ∆ λ = 5300." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524); "All data used a 0 . 6 '' slit for spectral resolution λ/ ∆ λ = 6000." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524)
   - assumption: Redshifts of C IV and Si IV components are constrained to align in velocity space  
  > "The adopted Voigt profile model constrains redshifts of the C iv and Si iv components to align in velocity." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524)
   - fitting: MCMC sampler (emcee) reporting median and [16%, 84%] confidence intervals  
  > "using a Markov-Chain Monte Carlo walker as implemented by the emcee python package [48], to determine confidence intervals from the posterior distribution." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524); "Table 2 lists the Voigt profile median and [16%, 84%] confidence intervals on the fit." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524)
   - validation: Tested that total column densities are robust as a sum of unresolved blends.  
  > "However, tests indicate that the total column densities of these fits are robust when interpreted as a sum of the unresolved blend." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524)
6. **Search for rest-frame UV and Lyα counterparts in HST and VLT/MUSE data**: Co-add HST images with DrizzlePac and perform 0.7"-diameter aperture photometry at [C II] positions and random sky positions; reduce MUSE cube with MUSE pipeline and CubEx, calibrate astrometry via HST F775W and Gaia, and search for Lyα within 3" and ±500 km s^-1.  
  > "No significant counterpart was found at the positions of the [C ii] sources, yielding 2 σ upper limits of 45 nJy (F775W), 59 nJy (F850LP), and 31 nJy (F160W) for the aperture photometry in 0 . 7 '' -diameter." (HST broadband imaging); "We used the MUSE datacube to search for counterparts of [C ii]1030A and B in the Ly α emission line within search radii of ≈ 3 '' , or 20 pkpc, in position and ± 500 km s − 1 ) in velocity." (MUSE observation)
   - software: DrizzlePac, MUSE pipeline, CubEx
   - parameter: HST aperture diameter = 0.7"  
  > "for the aperture photometry in 0 . 7 '' -diameter." (HST broadband imaging)

## Qualitative findings

- Two [C II] 158 μm emitters ([C II]1030A at z = 5.7354 and [C II]1030B at z = 5.7101) are detected near a three-component C IV absorption complex at z ≈ 5.7 toward QSO J1030+0524.  
  > "We interpret these two emission lines to be [C ii] 158 µ m at redshifts close to a complex C iv absorption system that is found with three distinct absorbers at z abs = 5 . 7246, 5.7411, and 5.7443" (Main)
- Both [C II] emitters are spatially unresolved (< 2.4 pkpc FWHM) and have narrow line widths (64 ± 11 and 62 ± 11 km s^-1 FWHM), which are smaller and narrower than typical high-redshift [C II] galaxies.  
  > "These two sources are both characterized by compact sizes (FWHM diameter < 2 . 4 pkpc) and narrow [C ii] emission line profiles (64 ± 11 km s − 1 and 62 ± 11 km s − 1 respectively for [C ii]1030A and B)." (Main)
- Together with four MUSE Lyα emitters, the six galaxies and the C IV absorbing gas likely belong to a common unvirialized large-scale structure or proto-cluster rather than the C IV gas arising from inflows/outflows of the individual [C II] sources.  
  > "All these arguments support a physical connection between the [C ii] sources and the C iv absorbing gas through a common membership of some larger structure(s) that may enclose all these systems" (Main); "However, the radial velocity dispersion of all six sources, σ ( v r ) = 830 ± 270 km s − 1 , is larger than expected for such halos ( ∼ 200 km s − 1 ). Therefore, the system is unlikely to be virialized." (Main)
- Neither [C II] emitter is detected in rest-frame UV continuum (HST) or Lyα (MUSE), indicating severe dust attenuation of the UV-emitting star-forming regions.  
  > "For our claimed [C ii] sources, no significant counterparts have so far been found either in the Ly α emission line or in the rest-frame FUV continuum, despite the availability of deep Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images and the MUSE datacube." (Main); "This therefore suggests that the FUV emission from SFR at the required levels must have been severely attenuated by dust, which would also explain the absence of Ly α ." (Main)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: A mosaiced ALMA observation centered on QSO J1030+0524 with two SPWs tuned to the [C II] 158 μm frequencies of the three z ≈ 5.7 C IV absorbers allows a blind search for absorber-associated galaxies across a 138 pkpc radius field.  
  > "Two line emitters are detected within a mosaiced submilimeter observation with Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) that covers a field of about 23 '' (138 physical kpc at z = 5 . 7) in radius centered on the position of QSO J1030+0524" (Main); "Of these, two SPWs are adjusted to cover the three absorbers at z abs = 5 . 7246 , 5 . 7411 and 5 . 7443 in the [C ii] 158 µ m emission line" (ALMA observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting maximizes sensitivity and a Gaussian uv-taper circularizes the synthesized beam; a blind search avoids bias toward UV-bright sources; data splits (polarization, pointings, time halves) verify that 6σ detections are not spurious noise.  
  > "The final datacubes and the continuum images were obtained using CASA tclean task with natural weighting to maximize sensitivity. In doing so, we adopted uvtaper (a Gaussian taper on the weights of the uv data) of 0 × 220 mas and a position angle (PA) 119 degree in order to circularize the synthesized beam as much as possible." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "We further examined the reliability of the two claimed sources by analysing independent sub-sets of the data." (Reliability check of the detections)
- Alternative: C IV absorption arising directly from gas inflowing into or outflowing from the individual [C II] sources (rejected in favor of common membership in a larger-scale structure).  
  > "All these arguments support a physical connection between the [C ii] sources and the C iv absorbing gas through a common membership of some larger structure(s) that may enclose all these systems, rather than the C iv absorption coming from gas falling into or flowing out of these individual sources." (Main)
- Alternative: Low-redshift CO emission-line interlopers (CO(3-2) to CO(8-7) at z ≈ 0.22–2.3; rejected due to 1.5% probability and non-detection of stellar continuum in deep HST images).  
  > "The chance of finding two unrelated CO sources within the velocity range of interest (across 2280 km s − 1 around the absorption systems; see above) is therefore of order (0 . 4 × 2280 / 7380) 2 ≈ 1 . 5%, which we consider to be very unlikely." (Line identification); "Non-detection of both sources in available deep HST images at ∼ 9000 ˚ A (see below) also argues against any interpretation that places these sources" (Methods, Line identification)
- Alternative: Low metallicity (Z < 0.1–0.2 Z_sun) for the [C II] emitters (considered unlikely because lower metallicity would imply an even higher [C II]-based SFR requiring even heavier dust obscuration).  
  > "For a lower metallicity, the [C ii]-based SFR would be even higher and thus requires even more dust obscuration, which is in turn at odds with the low metallicity." (The properties of the [C ii] sources)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cannot yet make a conclusive statement about the physical nature of the [C II]-emitting sources without rest-frame optical/NIR observations (e.g. JWST).  
  > "We cannot therefore yet make a conclusive statement about the nature of these [C ii]-emitting sources." (Main)
- Limitation (authors): X-Shooter and FIRE spectra likely do not resolve fine velocity structures in the absorbers, so Doppler parameters b may reflect velocity dispersion among blended components rather than intrinsic thermal/turbulent width.  
  > "It should be noted that XShooter and FIRE likely do not resolve fine velocity structures, so the Doppler parameters b in the fits may represent a velocity dispersion among components that would be separated at higher resolution, rather than the intrinsic width of a single component." (Measurements of the absorption systems toward QSO J1030+0524)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is an apparent discrepancy in the stated 1σ rms of the moment-0 maps between the Figure 1 caption (≈ 0.25 mJy km s^-1 per beam) and the Methods section (4.1 and 4.2 mJy km s^-1 per beam; likely a unit/factor typo in one of the two places since the peak flux is ~0.17–0.28 Jy km s^-1 at ~6σ).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Both ALMA detections have modest S/N (~6σ) and lack multi-wavelength counterparts in HST, MUSE, and FIR continuum, meaning their redshift identification relies on statistical arguments against low-z CO interlopers.
