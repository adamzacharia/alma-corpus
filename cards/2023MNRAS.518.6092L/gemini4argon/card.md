## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract radial intensity profiles and spectral index profile from ALMA images**: Convolve Band 3 and Band 6 ALMA images to a common beam of [0.13'', 0.09''], extract radial intensity profiles by azimuthal averaging on concentric ellipses defined by disk inclination and position angle, and compute the spectral index alpha_mm(R).  
  > "Figure 2 shows the ALMA images at Band 3 and Band 6, both of which are convolved with the same beam of [$0.13^{\prime \prime},\,0.09^{\prime\prime}$] in size for the measurement of the spectral index." (Observations); "The millimeter spectral index as a function of $R$ is obtained from $$\begin{equation} \label{alpha} \alpha_{\mathrm{mm}}(R)=\frac{\log [I_{\nu_{1}}(R) / I_{\nu_{2}}(R)]}{\log (\nu_{1} / \nu_{2})} \end{equation}$$" (Observations); "We extract the intensity profiles by applying an azimuthal average that is performed on a series of concentric ellipses with shapes determined by the disk inclination and position angle, see Table 2." (Observations)
   - parameter: Convolving beam = [0.13'', 0.09'']  
  > "Figure 2 shows the ALMA images at Band 3 and Band 6, both of which are convolved with the same beam of [$0.13^{\prime \prime},\,0.09^{\prime\prime}$] in size for the measurement of the spectral index." (Observations)
   - parameter: Inclination i = 67.58 deg, Position angle = 159.62 deg  
  > "$i$ [$\circ$] | 67.58 | 67.58 | 67.58 | 1 | fixed" (Table 2); "Position angle [$\circ$] | 159.62 | 159.62 | 159.62 | 1 | fixed" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Absolute flux calibration uncertainty (~10%) is neglected in main modeling  
  > "The absolute flux calibration accuracy has an impact on the uncertainty in the spectral index, especially when the spectral index is measured within one band (cite: Francis2020). In our modeling, we do not consider this impact." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested impact of ~10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty at both bands in Appendix A.  
  > "We rerun the modeling by assuming a ${\sim}\,10\%$ flux calibration accuracy at both ALMA bands." (More radiative transfer models)
2. **Fit the SED to determine representative disk geometry parameters**: Run a grid of self-consistent two-layer flared disk radiative transfer models in RADMC-3D with isotropic scattering and constant midplane maximum grain size, minimizing chi^2_SED over the two ALMA wavelengths (and checking against the full SED) to find best-fit H_100 and beta.  
  > "Given the stellar parameters, dust density distribution and dust opacities, the code self-consistently computes the dust temperature using the Monte Carlo approach, and then simulates the SED and millimeter images." (Modeling setup); "First, we fit the SED to find a parameter set that is representative of the disk geometry." (Fitting the SED); "In order to search for a good starting point as far as possible for the subsequent fitting to the ALMA images which is the key of this study, calculating $\chi^2_{\rm SED}$ only takes the data points at the two ALMA wavelengths into account." (Fitting the SED)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: H_100 in [5, 10] AU (6 grid points); beta in [1.00, 1.15] (4 grid points); gamma in [0.3, 1.5] (7 grid points); M_dust in [10^-7, 10^-2] M_odot (11 grid points); a_max^mid in [10^3, 10^5] um (5 grid points)  
  > "$H_{100}$ [AU] | 7 | 5 | 10 | 6 | free" (Table 2); "$\beta$ | 1.05 | 1.00 | 1.15 | 4 | free" (Table 2); "$\gamma$ | 0.5 | 0.3 | 1.5 | 7 | free" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Fixed parameters: M_* = 0.58 M_odot, T_eff = 3792 K, L_* = 0.7 L_odot, R_in = 0.1 AU, R_out = 80 AU, R_gap = 33 AU, sigma = 27 AU, eta = 0.99, H_ratio = 0.2, D = 158 pc  
  > "$M_{\star}\ [\mathrm M_{\odot}]$ | 0.58 | 0.58 | 0.58 | 1 | fixed" (Table 2); "The scale height of the interior layer is given by $H_{\mathrm {ratio}}$, and we fixed it to 0.2, which is a typical value found by previous modeling works of protoplanetary disks (cite: 2011ApJ...732...42A; Liuy2022)." (Disk model)
   - parameter: 160 wavelengths logarithmically distributed in [0.1, 10000 um]  
  > "The radiative transfer problem is solved self-consistently considering 160 wavelengths logarithmically distributed in the range of [0.1, 10000 $\mu$m]." (Stellar heating)
   - assumption: Passive stellar heating (accretion heating ignored)  
  > "We assume that the DS Tau disk is passively heated by absorbing energy from the central star (cite: 1997ApJ...490..368C)." (Stellar heating); "Therefore, we ignore the effect of accretion in the simulation." (Stellar heating)
   - assumption: 85% of total dust mass in midplane, 15% in surface layer; surface layer a_max = 1 um, a_min = 0.01 um, size distribution slope p = 3.5  
  > "Considering the settling model, we assume that $85\%$ of the total dust mass is concentrated in the midplane and $15\%$ in the surface layer." (Disk model); "The grain size distribution follows the power-law ${N(a)}\,{\propto}\,a^{-3.5}, a\,{\in}\,[a_{\mathrm {min}},\ a_{\mathrm {max}}]$, where $a$ is the grain radius and ${N(a)}$ is the number of dust particles whose size is in the interval $[{a,\ a\,{+}\,da}]$. We fix the minimum dust size to $a_{\mathrm {min}}\,{=}\,0.01\,\mu$m. The maximum grain size $a_{\rm max}$ in the disk surface layer is fixed to be $1\,\mu \mathrm m$." (Dust model)
   - assumption: DSHARP homogeneous sphere dust composition (20% water ice, 32% astronomical silicates, 8% troilite, 40% refractory organics; rho_dust = 1.675 g cm^-3) with Bruggeman and Mie theory and isotropic scattering  
  > "We assume that the dust grains are homogeneous spheres, composed of 20% water ice, 32% astronomical silicates, 8% troilite, and 40% refractory organics, with an average density of $\rho_{\mathrm {dust}}\,{=}\,1.675 \,\mathrm g/\mathrm {cm}^{-3}$." (Dust model); "Therefore, the option of isotropic scattering available in `RADMC-3D` is selected." (Modeling setup)
   - fitting: Grid search minimizing chi^2_SED  
  > "The best-fit model is identified as the one with the minimum $\chi^2_{\rm SED}$." (Fitting the SED)
   - validation: Checked model returned from fitting the entire SED (gave H_100 = 10 AU, all other parameters identical).  
  > "We also checked the model that is returned from fitting the entire part of the SED, and found that all of the parameter values, except for $H_{100}=10\,{\rm AU}$, are identical to those of the best-fit model." (Fitting the SED)
3. **Iteratively reconstruct the dust surface density profile Sigma(R) and fit the radial maximum grain size profile a_max^mid(R)**: Divide the disk midplane (out to 80 AU) into 32 radial bins of width 2.5 AU. Initialize a_max^mid(R) with a power law (a_max0 = 1000 um, b = 6 at R0 = 40 AU) and fix a_max^mid = 10 cm for R < 20 AU. Sequentially from inner to outer bins, perform a dense logarithmic grid search of a_max^mid to fit the observed spectral index alpha_mm(R). For every trial model, iteratively adjust Sigma(R) from scratch using the 2.9 mm observed-to-model radial intensity ratio until convergence (<5% change, ~12 iterations). Estimate uncertainties using 17 logarithmically spaced models in [log(a_best)-0.4, log(a_best)+0.4] per bin weighted by 1/chi^2.  
  > "We divide the observed surface brightness by the model brightness at each $R$ to obtain the scaling coefficient $\xi$(R): $$\begin{equation} \xi(R)\,{=}\,\frac{I_{\nu,\rm{obs}}(R)}{I_{\nu,\rm{mod}}(R)} \end{equation}$$" (Iteration for the dust surface density); "In order to build the radial profile of the maximum grain size, we divide the midplane into 32 equally spaced parts with a width of 2.5 AU in radius." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane); "After the best-fit solution is obtained, for each radial bin we simulated 17 new models with the maximum grain size $a_i$ centered at the best-fit value $a_{\mathrm {best-fit}}$ and logarithmically spaced from $\mathrm {log}(a_{\mathrm {best-fit}})\,{-}\,0.4$ to $\mathrm {log}(a_{\mathrm {best-fit}})\,{+}\,0.4$." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: 32 radial bins of width 2.5 AU; fixed H_100 = 7 AU, beta = 1.05; initial Sigma(R) with gamma = 0.5  
  > "set $\gamma\,{=}\,0.5$ as the starting profile." (Iteration for the dust surface density); "Fixing the flaring index $\beta\,{=}\,1.05$ and scale height $H_{100}\,{=}\,7\,\rm{AU}$, we simulate the continuum image at the wavelength of 2.9 mm." (Iteration for the dust surface density); "In order to build the radial profile of the maximum grain size, we divide the midplane into 32 equally spaced parts with a width of 2.5 AU in radius." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
   - parameter: Initial power-law search: R_0 = 40 AU, a_max0 in [100, 10000 um], b in [1, 8]; best initial power law a_max0 = 1000 um, b = 6; fixed a_max^mid = 10 cm for R < 20 AU  
  > "The best model ($a_{\max0}\,{=}\,1000\,\mu{\rm m}$, $b\,{=}\,6$) cannot simultaneously fit the two ALMA images" (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane); "The $a^{\mathrm {mid}}_{\mathrm {max}}$ for $R\,{<}\,20\,\mathrm{AU}$ are fixed to $10\,\mathrm {cm}$." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
   - parameter: Convergence threshold: < 5% surface density change at any radius (~ 12 iterations); 17 models per bin for uncertainty  
  > "According to our experiments, convergence can be achieved after about 12 iterations when the surface density of two adjacent iterations changes by less than $5\%$ at any radius." (Iteration for the dust surface density); "where the weighting factor equals to one over the chi-square of the model $\omega_{i}\,{=}\,\frac{1}{\chi_{i}^2}$, and $N\,{=}\,17$ is the number of models." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
   - assumption: Image-plane fitting is valid because uv sampling is similar between bands and MRS (~1.8'' and ~2.6'') is much larger than disk outer radius (~0.5'')  
  > "Fitting the ALMA observations is performed in the image plane, rather than in the interferometric (u, v) space, because the construction of the dust surface density is directly linked to the brightness profile, see Sect. 4.2.1." (Fitting the multi-band ALMA images); "The maximum recoverable scales for band 6 and band 3 observations are ${\sim}\,1.8^{\prime\prime}$ and ${\sim}2.6^{\prime\prime}$, respectively, sufficiently larger than the disk outer radius ($R_{\rm out}\,{=}\,80\,\rm{AU}$, or ${\sim}\,0.5^{\prime\prime}$)." (Fitting the multi-band ALMA images)
   - fitting: Sequential bin-by-bin grid search minimizing chi^2 on spectral index alpha_mm(R) coupled with iterative surface density scaling on 2.9 mm brightness  
  > "The spectral indices of these models are calculated from the simulated images at 1.3 and 2.9 mm, and fitted to the observation. The grain size in this radial bin is determined to be that of the best-fit model." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
   - validation: Reran the entire fitting procedure with an alternative dust opacity model (75% amorphous silicate, 25% carbon) and tested alternative smooth/inverted grain size profiles across the gap and ring.  
  > "We rerun the fitting procedure from scratch using the dust model chosen by (cite: Liuy2019)" (Effects of dust opacities); "We run two additional models to check how the fitting result would be when the radial profile of $a_{\rm max}^{\rm mid}$ is different from the best fit." (More radiative transfer models)
4. **Estimate depleted dust mass in the gap and evaluate Toomre Q gravitational stability**: Interpolate a smooth power-law dust surface density between R = 6 AU and R = 60 AU, integrate to find the pre-depletion dust mass, and subtract the best-fit dust mass. Compute the radial Toomre Q parameter from the midplane temperature and scaled gas surface density.  
  > "The surface densities for $6\,{\le}\,{R}\,{\le}\,60\,{\rm AU}$ are parameterized by a power law that connects the best-fit surface densities at $R\,{=}\,6\,\rm{AU}$ and $R\,{=}\,60\,\rm{AU}$, and the surface densities outside the radius range of [6 AU, 60 AU] are set to the best-fit values." (Potential for forming a ~ 3.5 M_Jup mass planet); "We check whether the disk is gravitationally stable by using the Toomre $Q$ criterion" (Gravitational instability)
   - parameter: Power-law interpolation range = [6 AU, 60 AU]  
  > "The surface densities for $6\,{\le}\,{R}\,{\le}\,60\,{\rm AU}$ are parameterized by a power law that connects the best-fit surface densities at $R\,{=}\,6\,\rm{AU}$ and $R\,{=}\,60\,\rm{AU}$, and the surface densities outside the radius range of [6 AU, 60 AU] are set to the best-fit values." (Potential for forming a ~ 3.5 M_Jup mass planet)
   - parameter: Mean molecular weight mu_m = 2.34  
  > "The sound speed $c_{\mathrm {s}}\,{=}\,\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{B}} T / \mu_{m} m_{\rm{H}}}$ is calculated from the midplane temperature $T$, with a mean molecular weight $\mu_{m}\,{=}\,2.34$." (Gravitational instability)
   - assumption: Uniform gas-to-dust mass ratio of 30 (and tested < 10 in the ring)  
  > "Taking the gas-to-dust mass ratio (typically ${\sim}\,30$ in protoplanetary disks) into account, the depleted mass is sufficient for forming a $3.5\,\mathrm M_{\mathrm {Jup}}$ mass planet." (Potential for forming a ~ 3.5 M_Jup mass planet); "To have the gas surface density $\Sigma_{\mathrm{g}}$, we simply scale the best-fit dust surface density by a gas-to-dust mass ratio of 30 that is assumed to be uniform in the entire disk." (Gravitational instability)

## Qualitative findings

- Maximum grain size in the DS Tau disk decreases from centimeters (10 cm) in the inner disk to ~30 um in the outer regions, consistent with radial drift and dust evolution theories.  
  > "The best-fit grain sizes range from centimeters in the inner disk down to ${\sim}\,30\,\mu{\rm m}$ in the outer regions. Such an inside-out decreasing tendency is consistent with theories of dust evolution." (Abstract)
- The inner disk (R < 20 AU) is optically thick even at 2.9 mm (3 mm), the gap is optically thin, and the boundary between the gap and ring (R ~ 50 AU) has optical depth > 1.  
  > "The inner disk ($R\,{<}\,20\,\rm{AU}$) is optically thick even at 3 mm. The gap is optically thin. However, the optically depth is higher than unity at the junction between the gap and the ring." (Summary)
- A discontinuity (jump of two orders of magnitude for DSHARP opacities, or a one-order-of-magnitude bump for amorphous silicate + carbon opacities) in maximum grain size occurs at the interface between the gap and the ring rather than at the ring center.  
  > "However, dust grains grow up by two orders of magnitude at the boundary between the gap and ring." (Overview of the modeling results); "Consequently, the originally claimed jump becomes a bump at the boundary between the gap and the ring, and the amplitude of the bump is roughly one order of magnitude." (Effects of dust opacities)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Self-consistent 3D radiative transfer modeling with isotropic scattering accounts for the coupling between dust temperature, vertical structure, surface density, and grain size, while image-plane fitting allows iterative scaling of the dust surface density directly from the optically thinner 2.9 mm brightness profile.  
  > "Given the stellar parameters, dust density distribution and dust opacities, the code self-consistently computes the dust temperature using the Monte Carlo approach, and then simulates the SED and millimeter images." (Modeling setup); "Fitting the ALMA observations is performed in the image plane, rather than in the interferometric (u, v) space, because the construction of the dust surface density is directly linked to the brightness profile, see Sect. 4.2.1." (Fitting the multi-band ALMA images); "Choosing 2.9 mm instead of 1.3 mm is due to the fact that the disk is optically thinner at longer wavelengths. Hence, the 2.9 mm ALMA image better traces the dust density distribution." (Iteration for the dust surface density)
- Alternative: Constant maximum grain size with radius and parametric Gaussian-depleted power-law surface density (rejected because it cannot simultaneously fit Band 6 and Band 3 surface brightness profiles and the radially varying spectral index)  
  > "However, within the whole parameter space composed of $\gamma$, $M_{\mathrm {dust}}$ and $a^{\mathrm {mid}}_{\mathrm {max}}$, it is challenging to simultaneously fit the surface brightnesses at both ALMA bands." (Fitting the multi-band ALMA images)
- Alternative: Single power-law radial profile for midplane maximum grain size a_max^mid(R) = a_max0 (R/R_0)^-b (rejected because the best model cannot simultaneously fit the two ALMA images)  
  > "The best model ($a_{\max0}\,{=}\,1000\,\mu{\rm m}$, $b\,{=}\,6$) cannot simultaneously fit the two ALMA images, implying that the maximum grain size in the midplane of DS Tau’s disk cannot be represented by a simple power-law function." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
- Alternative: Snow line condensation front as the cause of the grain size jump at the gap-ring boundary (rejected because the midplane temperature there is ~10 K, not associated with main volatile snow lines)  
  > "However, according to our model, the midplane temperature at the boundary between the gap and ring is about 10 K which is not associated with condensation fronts of main volatile species in protoplanetary disks." (Overview of the modeling results)
- Alternative: Smooth grain size profile across the gap-ring boundary or larger grains in the ring than in the gap (rejected because both setups fail to reproduce the observed spectral index profile)  
  > "One model features a relatively smooth profile of $a_{\rm max}^{\rm mid}$, i.e., there is no jump in $a_{\rm max}^{\rm mid}$ in the border between the gap and ring. The resulting spectral indices are shown with the yellow solid line in the top panel of Figure 2. In the other model, $a_{\rm max}^{\rm mid}(s)$ in the ring are devised to be larger than those in the gap. The result is indicated with the green solid line. It is clear that both setups cannot reproduce the observation." (More radiative transfer models)
- How they decided it was enough: Surface density iteration stopped after ~12 iterations when adjacent iterations changed by <5% at all radii; 32 radial bins of 2.5 AU (1/8 of the ALMA beam) were chosen because finer bins require more computation without significantly affecting results.  
  > "According to our experiments, convergence can be achieved after about 12 iterations when the surface density of two adjacent iterations changes by less than $5\%$ at any radius." (Iteration for the dust surface density); "The width of each part is only one-eighth of the size of the ALMA beam, meaning that our setup is sufficient for resolving the disk substructures. Using more radial bins will take more computational resources, and the effect on the results is not significant." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
- Limitation (authors): The inner disk (R < 20 AU) is optically thick even at 2.9 mm, so the grain size there cannot be constrained by the spectral index and is fixed to 10 cm.  
  > "After many simulation tests, we found that this inner region is optically thick even at 2.9 mm, indicating that the grain size can no longer be constrained by the observed spectral index." (Grid search for the maximum grain sizes in the midplane)
- Limitation (authors): Fluctuations in the retrieved grain size profile occur on spatial scales smaller than the beam size (~20 AU), requiring future higher-resolution observations to confirm.  
  > "The $a^{\mathrm {mid}}_{\mathrm {max}}\,{-}\,R$ profile exhibits some fluctuations on spatial scales smaller than the beam size. Future high-resolution observations are desired to confirm their reality." (Overview of the modeling results); "However, the measured spectral indices actually trace the mean dust properties over a radius range depending on the beam size, i.e., ${\sim}\,20\,\rm{AU}$ in our case." (Overview of the modeling results)
- Limitation (authors): The amplitude and shape of the grain size discontinuity at the gap-ring boundary depend on the assumed dust opacity model.  
  > "Moreover, our modeling also indicates that at the interface region between the gap and the ring, the grain size profile shows a discontinuity, with its amplitude dependent on the dust model adopted in the radiative transfer analysis." (Abstract)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitting 32 radial bins of 2.5 AU width to images with a ~16-20 AU beam without spatial regularization introduces strong bin-to-bin degeneracies and beam-smearing artifacts, especially at sharp intensity gradients like the gap-ring edge.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sequential inside-out bin-by-bin search does not guarantee a global minimum across all 32 coupled radial bins because beam convolution mixes emission from adjacent bins.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Isotropic scattering is used in RADMC-3D, whereas centimeter- and millimeter-sized grains have strongly forward-peaked phase functions that can alter effective albedo and spectral index.
