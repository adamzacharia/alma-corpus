## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate photo-dissociation column N_ph against DALI**: Compare emitting height from the direct problem (using DALI temperature and density) with DALI ray-traced heights; choose N_ph minimising mean squared deviation  
  > "we computed the mean squared deviation between our and DALI models and looked for the value of $N_{ph}$ that minimizes it" (3.2 Comparison between the emitting heights from DALI and from the direct problem)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: Temperature and total mass density taken directly from DALI so the comparison is fair  
  > "we read in the temperature computed by the DALI model and use it in our approach" (3.2 Comparison between the emitting heights from DALI and from the direct problem)
   - parameter: 13CO uses N_ph rescaled by the isotopic abundance  
  > "We find instead that for $^{13}$CO rescaling $N_{ph}$ with the isotopic abundance" (3.2 Comparison between the emitting heights from DALI and from the direct problem)
   - assumption: CO abundance parameterised in the direct model, computed chemically in DALI  
  > "since in our approach the CO abundance is parameterized, while in DALI it is computed self-consistently from the chemical network" (3.2 Comparison between the emitting heights from DALI and from the direct problem)
   - fitting: Mean squared deviation minimisation over N_ph; no sampler or priors  
  > "we computed the mean squared deviation between our and DALI models and looked for the value of $N_{ph}$ that minimizes it" (3.2 Comparison between the emitting heights from DALI and from the direct problem)
   - validation: Direct model matches DALI emitting heights well for suitable N_ph  
  > "in general our model matches well the results of the more accurate, but also significantly more computationally expensive, DALI model" (3.2 Comparison between the emitting heights from DALI and from the direct problem)
2. **Assemble inputs and select sample and radial ranges**: Adopt DISCMINER emitting heights and published temperature profiles; drop non-axisymmetric, low-inclination and problematic disks; apply radial cuts  
  > "Based on the results of the comparison with radiative transfer models, which show that DISCMINER typically reconstructs the emitting height with higher accuracy at high inclinations" (4 Application to observations); "We discarded in particular sources that show strong non-axisymmetric features (MWC 758, CQ Tau), since in our model we implicitly assume azimuthal symmetry" (4 Application to observations)
   - software: DISCMINER
   - parameter: Only radii larger than 0.3 arcsec (beam smearing)  
  > "we only consider radii larger than 0.3" since the inner part of the disk is affected by beam smearing" (4 Application to observations)
   - parameter: 13CO brightness temperature below 20 K: critical optical depth rescaled by Tb/20 K (about 10% effect on total mass)  
  > "by the ratio between the brightness temperature and 20 K" (4 Application to observations); "We find however this is only a small correction (a 10% factor on average on the total mass)." (4 Application to observations)
   - assumption: Azimuthal symmetry  
  > "since in our model we implicitly assume azimuthal symmetry" (4 Application to observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Solve the inverse problem for CO column and surface density (stratified model)**: Tabulate vertical integrals per radius, root-find the midplane CO number density n0 with a Brent solver, solving for z_ph at each trial n0 by linear interpolation  
  > "Operationally, we use a root finding algorithm (we use the Brent solver as for the isothermal case)" (2.3.2 The vertically stratified case); "Since we tabulate the integral appearing in" (2.3.2 The vertically stratified case)
   - software: scipy brentq
   - parameter: x_CO = 1e-4 (ISM value)  
  > "Unless otherwise specified, from now on we will use the standard value $x_\mathrm{CO}=10^{-4}$, as derived from the ISM." (2.4 The role of the unknown CO abundance)
   - parameter: mean molecular weight 2.35  
  > "where $\mu=2.35$ is the mean molecular weight" (2.2 The direct problem)
   - parameter: 12C/13C about 77  
  > "where we have assumed that $^{13}$CO is a factor $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C $\simeq 77$ (cite: isotopic_abundance) less abundant" (2.3.1 The isothermal case)
   - parameter: Brent search range a factor 1000 above and below the no-photodissociation estimate  
  > "We use values a factor 1000 above and below this initial estimate to provide the search range" (2.3.2 The vertically stratified case)
   - assumption: Eddington-Barbier: emission height at tau of about 2/3  
  > "to make the assumption that the CO emission height traces the location where $\tau \simeq 2/3$" (2 Model for emission height)
   - assumption: LTE, thermal broadening only, partition function expansion Z = 1/3 + kT/hB0  
  > "We estimate the partition function using the expansion $Z=1/3 + k_b T/hB_0$" (2.1 Line opacity and critical column probed by the emitting layer); "in what follows we only consider thermal broadening" (2.1 Line opacity and critical column probed by the emitting layer)
   - assumption: Fixed CO column N_ph needed to self-shield; no CO above z_ph, constant abundance below  
  > "we make the simplifying assumption that a fixed vertical column of the molecule $N_\mathrm{ph}$ is required to self-shield from UV radiation" (2 Model for emission height)
   - fitting: Brent root finding; no sampler, priors or likelihood  
  > "Operationally, we use a root finding algorithm (we use the Brent solver as for the isothermal case)" (2.3.2 The vertically stratified case)
   - validation: Photo-dissociation and direct problem calibrated against DALI; LkCa 15 compared with optically thin C18O/C17O and rotation-curve mass  
  > "their values are bracketed in most of the radial range by the other two constraints" (4.1 A worked example: LkCa 15)
4. **Propagate uncertainties by bootstrap**: Gaussian errors on emitting height (25 per cent) and on temperature fit parameters, 1000 bootstrap realisations, average and standard deviation in log space  
  > "Operationally, we assume the error is normally distributed and we bootstrap 1000 times to quantify uncertainties." (4 Application to observations); "the values we report in this paper are computed from the average and standard deviation in log space of the resulting 1000 samples" (4 Application to observations)
   - parameter: 25 per cent emitting-height uncertainty  
  > "We find that 25 per cent is a typical uncertainty and we therefore use this value in the rest of the paper." (4 Application to observations)
   - parameter: Temperature errors taken from the temperature-fit papers  
  > "For the temperatures, we simply use the uncertainties reported by (cite: exoALMA:Maria) and (cite: Law2021) for their temperature fits." (4 Application to observations)
   - assumption: Uncertainties are representative, not true statistical errors  
  > "we remark that the uncertainties we show should not be considered as true statistical uncertainties but should instead be considered as representative" (4 Application to observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Error budget dominated by emitting height rather than temperature (LkCa 15)  
  > "the uncertainty is entirely dominated by the uncertainty in the emitting height determination" (4.1 A worked example: LkCa 15)
5. **Integrate surface densities to disk masses and compare 12CO with 13CO**: Integrate surface density to get disk mass per isotopologue; fit 13CO mass versus 12CO mass with a linear-mixture power-law fit including errors on both axes  
  > "By integrating this value we also provide measurements of the disk mass." (6 Conclusions); "we fitted the values using a power-law relation with the method[^7] developed by (cite: Kelly2007), which allows us to include errors on both axis." (5.2 Difference between 12CO and 13CO estimates)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: 12CO mass computed only over the radial range where 13CO is defined  
  > "the $^{12}$CO mass reported in this plot has been computed only over radial range over which the $^{13}$CO estimate is defined" (5.2 Difference between 12CO and 13CO estimates)
   - assumption: 12C/13C = 77  
  > "We remark that a discrepancy could point to a isotopic ratio $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C different from the value of $\sim$77 we have assumed here." (5.2 Difference between 12CO and 13CO estimates)
   - fitting: Kelly (2007) linear regression with measurement errors in both variables (linmix); priors and sampler settings not stated  
  > "we fitted the values using a power-law relation with the method[^7] developed by (cite: Kelly2007), which allows us to include errors on both axis." (5.2 Difference between 12CO and 13CO estimates)
   - validation: 12CO and 13CO masses generally agree within uncertainties  
  > "Overall, the fact that the masses derived from the two isotopologues are in such good agreement is remarkable" (5.2 Difference between 12CO and 13CO estimates)
6. **Compare vertically isothermal model with stratified model**: Isothermal inversion using brightness temperature at the emission surface (and, for reference, midplane temperature) compared to the stratified masses  
  > "we show estimates from the isothermal model using the brightness temperature at the emission surface to set the scale-height." (5.1 Comparison with the isothermal model)
   - software: scipy brentq
   - parameter: Isothermal scale height H from brightness temperature, or from two-line system of equations (appendix)  
  > "The first consists in using the line brightness temperature under the assumption that the emission is optically thick" (2.3.1 The isothermal case)
   - assumption: Vertically isothermal disk with Gaussian density profile  
  > "In the commonly used vertically isothermal case, this is simply" (2.2 The direct problem)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stratified model treated as best estimate; isothermal brightness-temperature masses agree to ratio 0.8 with 0.2 dex spread  
  > "we compute that the average ratio between the two masses is 0.8 (the median has a similar value) with a spread of $\sim$0.2 dex" (5.1 Comparison with the isothermal model)
7. **Derive CO depletion from comparison with dynamical masses**: Ratio of dynamical mass (rotation curve) to 13CO-derived mass, giving a CO depletion factor relative to ISM abundance; upper limits excluded for the median  
  > "the ratio between the dynamical mass estimates and the $^{13}$CO masses" (5.3 Comparison with other mass estimates and CO depletion); "Excluding the disks that have only upper limits from the rotation curve" (5.3 Comparison with other mass estimates and CO depletion)
   - assumption: Standard ISM CO abundance 1e-4 in the surface-density derivation; dynamical masses assume self-similar profiles  
  > "Since our constraints are derived assuming the standard ISM CO abundance of $10^{-4}$" (4.1 A worked example: LkCa 15)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Disks with only upper-limit dynamical masses have the lowest masses with this method; 13CO mass agrees with C18O for MAPS to a factor 1.25  
  > "it is reassuring that the sources where (cite: exoALMA:Cristiano) reports only upper limits on the disk mass" (5.3 Comparison with other mass estimates and CO depletion)

## Software

- **DALI** : thermochemical models providing reference emitting heights to calibrate N_ph  
  > "The models have been run using the DALI thermochemical code (cite: Bruderer2013)" (3.1 DALI thermo-chemical models)
- **DISCMINER** : parametric emitting surfaces adopted as input (run in companion papers)  
  > "we opt to use the DISCMINER surfaces" (4 Application to observations)
- **disksurf** : alternative emitting-height extraction compared in the appendix  
  > "We show in [10](#fig:ds_dm) a comparison between the disk masses obtained using the emitting heights from `disksurf` and those from DISCMINER ." (Comparison between disksurf and DISCMINER emitting heights)
- **scipy brentq** : Brent root finder for the inverse problem  
  > "we use the Brent solver as implemented in the scipy function brentq" (2.3.1 The isothermal case)
- **linmix** : Kelly (2007) regression of 13CO mass on 12CO mass  
  > "An open source implementation is available at the following link: <https://github.com/jmeyers314/linmix>." (5.2 Difference between 12CO and 13CO estimates)
- **RADEX** : benchmark of the critical-column calculation (agrees except factor about 1.06)  
  > "We benchmarked our calculation against RADEX (cite: Radex) and confirm it yields the same results, except for a factor $\sim$1.06." (2.1 Line opacity and critical column probed by the emitting layer)

## Qualitative findings

- Derived surface densities decrease with radius and mostly follow the steeper r^-2 profile  
  > "Most of the surface densities we derive follow more closely the steeper profile" (4.2 Surface density profiles for the whole sample)
- Uncertainties are dominated by the emitting-height error, not the temperature error  
  > "we find that uncertainties are always dominated by the error we attribute to the emitting height determination, rather than the temperature uncertainty" (4.2 Surface density profiles for the whole sample)
- 12CO and 13CO surface densities generally agree within uncertainties but lie below the rotation-curve estimate, implying widespread CO depletion  
  > "The two estimates from $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO generally agree within the uncertainties, but they are always lower than the estimate from the rotation curve" (4.2 Surface density profiles for the whole sample)
- Using both emitting heights in a two-line isothermal system performs poorly and was abandoned  
  > "Because of these reasons, we will not consider further using both emitting heights at the same time." (Isothermal approach using 12CO and 13CO at the same time)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MAPS disks added to exoALMA because they also have high quality kinematic data  
  > "we consider also the disks included in the MAPS (cite: ObergMAPS) programme since they also have high quality kinematical data available" (4 Application to observations)
- Why this method: DISCMINER surfaces chosen over disksurf because they reconstruct heights more accurately at high inclination  
  > "which show that DISCMINER typically reconstructs the emitting height with higher accuracy at high inclinations due to less confusion with the backside, we opt to use the DISCMINER surfaces" (4 Application to observations)
- Alternative: disksurf emitting heights: masses agree on average with DISCMINER but vary significantly source by source  
  > "the estimates can vary significantly source by source" (Comparison between disksurf and DISCMINER emitting heights)
- Alternative: Isothermal model using 12CO and 13CO emitting heights together to solve for H: rejected, fails in inner disks and can be off by an order of magnitude  
  > "in some cases it yields disk masses that can differ up to one order of magnitude from either of the stratified estimates" (Isothermal approach using 12CO and 13CO at the same time)
- Alternative: Isothermal model with midplane temperature: strongly overestimates surface density  
  > "the mass estimated using the midplane temperature always overestimates the temperature of the stratified model" (5.1 Comparison with the isothermal model)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Estimates are extrapolations from the emitting height to the midplane and depend on the assumed temperature structure; parametrisation uncertainty not quantified  
  > "it is considerably more complex to estimate the uncertainty due to the chosen temperature parametrization" (5.4 Caveats and future prospects)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties are representative and not true statistical errors  
  > "we remark that the uncertainties we show should not be considered as true statistical uncertainties but should instead be considered as representative" (4 Application to observations)
- Limitation (authors): Emitting height accuracy limits the result; parametric surfaces and the two methods differ  
  > "our uncertainties are currently dominated by the emitting height determination" (5.4 Caveats and future prospects)
- Limitation (authors): Only CO is constrained; surface density depends inversely on assumed x_CO  
  > "the surface densities we derive in this work are inversely proportional to $x_\mathrm{CO}$" (2.4 The role of the unknown CO abundance)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 25 per cent emitting-height error is borrowed from a benchmark, so all quoted errors (dex-level) are not statistical; depletion factors inherit this
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Depletion factor depends on dynamical masses that themselves assume self-similar profiles
- Caveat (inferred, ours): N_ph calibrated against a single DALI grid with fiducial parameters; dependence on grain growth and UV not explored
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Isotope normalization of 2.5 dex versus text 'factor 2.5' is stated inconsistently
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample is those disks where heights are measurable, so biased toward large, bright, intermediate-inclination disks
