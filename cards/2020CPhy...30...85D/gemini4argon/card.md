## Qualitative findings

- High Doppler velocity wings up to ~20 km s^-1 are present in both blue-shifted and red-shifted hemispheres near the line of sight crossing R Doradus, particularly visible in CO, SiO, and SO, and accelerated at ~0.68 ± 0.10 km s^-1 au^-1.  
  > "They are present in both blue-shifted and red-shifted hemispheres but are not exactly back-to-back. They are accelerated at a typical rate of 0.7 km s−1 au−1 up to some 20 km s−1." (Abstract); "They are particularly visible in the CO, SiO and SO maps, both in the blue-shifted and red-shifted hemispheres, but more clearly in the former than in the latter." (III.1. Interpretation in terms of gas streams)
- Evidence for a stream-like morphology in HCN(4-3) is barely significant.  
  > "In particular, evidence for stream-like morphology is barely significant in the case of HCN as had first been noted by Decin et al. (2018) [5]." (III.1. Interpretation in terms of gas streams)
- The high-velocity wings are not artefacts of continuum subtraction, and they show remarkable symmetry in intensity between the blue-shifted and red-shifted wings in continuum-unsubtracted SiO spectra.  
  > "This result gives confidence in the reality of the high Doppler velocity components, seen to rise above continuum emission below |Vz| ∼ 20 km s−1. It displays a remarkable symmetry between blue-shifted and red-shifted wings, at variance with an earlier interpretation of the “blue blob” as causing one-sided ejection of material [28]." (III.2. Eliminating a possible effect related to continuum subtraction)
- No fully convincing physical interpretation (gas streams, rotation, or stellar pulsations) can currently explain the high-velocity wings without significant difficulties; turbulence on the Earth side of the stellar atmosphere is a possible unexplored alternative.  
  > "We conclude that, in the state of current knowledge, no fully convincing picture of the physics governing the production of such high velocities, typically twice as large as the terminal velocity, can be reliably drawn." (Abstract); "A possibility that has not been seriously explored would be that the large Doppler velocities, rather than revealing large radial wind velocities, would instead reveal large line widths caused by turbulence, which would then be dominantly detected on the Earth side of the star atmosphere and would provide a simple explanation for the nearly perfect blue-red symmetry displayed in Figure 6." (IV. CONCLUSION)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Archival ALMA observations of five molecular lines (CO, SiO, SO, SO2, HCN) probe distances between ~10 and ~100 au in the nascent wind of R Doradus with high angular resolution (~150 mas), where high-velocity wings had been noted by Decin et al. (2018) and where SO data had not yet been analysed for high velocities.  
  > "We probe distances from the star between ∼10 and ∼100 au using ALMA observations of the emission of five different molecular lines."
