2. **Construct and optimize 3D shape model and spin state of Psyche**: Use SHAPE software to fit 2015 Arecibo delay-Doppler images, 2015 Keck AO images, 2018-2019 VLT AO images, 22 ALMA images, and 2019 occultation silhouette sequentially from ellipsoids to spherical harmonics to a faceted vertex model (1652 vertices, 3300 facets).  
  > "We utilized the SHAPE modeling software and strategies described by Magri et al. [2007b] and Shepard et al. [2017]."    - software: SHAPE
   - parameter: 1652 vertices and 3300 facets  
  > "Based on a final model of 1652 vertices and 3300 facets." (Table 5)
   - parameter: 3 radar snapshots (~13 deg rotation apart) combined per 28-min radar integration to model rotational smearing  
  > "First, we had the SHAPE software break up the radar integration time into three snapshots of the model (~13º of rotation between them), synthesize the resulting radar images at each time, and combine them to effectively model the smearing due to rotation during the total integration time." (2.5 Methods)
   - parameter: Dataset weighting: ~75% for AO, ALMA, and occultation snapshots; ~25% for delay-Doppler images

## Qualitative findings

- Psyche's equatorial outline along the major and intermediate axes is well described by a rounded rectangular shape except between longitudes 200 and 290 degrees.  
  > "The view on the right shows an alternative rounded rectangular overlay which fits well everywhere except between longitudes 200° to 290°." (Figure 8 caption)
- Several major topographic features are confirmed as almost certain (Bravo, Charlie, Eros crater, Panthia crater) or likely (Alpha depression, Foxtrot north polar crater).  
  > "Bravo (B) 0, 340->50 Missing mass region, flat side Almost certain S17, V18, F20, this work" (Table 6); "Our model shows a significant topographical depression at the north pole of Psyche and we refer to it as Foxtrot (Figure 9)." (3. SHAPE AND TOPOGRAPHIC FEATURES)
- Psyche is not uniformly radar bright; it has a background radar albedo (~0.27) similar to enstatite or CH/CB chondrite analogs and four localized regions of enhanced or bifurcated radar echoes (Panthia, Golf, Hotel, India).  
  > "We find four regions of enhanced or complex radar albedo, one of which correlates well with a previously identified feature on Psyche, and all of which appear to correlate with patches of relatively high optical albedo." (abstract); "We find four regions of enhanced or complex radar albedo, one of which correlates well with a previously identified feature on Psyche" (abstract)
- Regions of high radar albedo positively correlate with regions of high optical albedo.  
  > "Based on this admittedly small sample, there appears to be a positive correlation between radar and optical albedos, i.e., the optically brighter regions are more radar reflective and darker regions less so." (4. RADAR PROPERTIES)
- The preferred interpretation is that Psyche is a differentiated silicate-metal body with a regolith analogous to enstatite or CH/CB chondrites, punctuated by localized high-metal concentrations emplaced via ferrovolcanism.  
  > "Based on these findings, we cannot rule out a model of Psyche as a remnant core, but our preferred interpretation is that Psyche is a differentiated world with a regolith composition analogous to enstatite or CH/CB chondrites and peppered with localized regions of high metal concentrations. The most credible formation mechanism for these regions is ferrovolcanism as proposed by Johnson et al. [Nature Astronomy vol 4, January 2020, 41-44.]." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA plane-of-sky thermal emission images, high-resolution Keck/VLT AO images, and the 2019 broadside stellar occultation provide direct plane-of-sky outlines and short-integration snapshots that tightly constrain the a-, b-, and especially c-axes while mitigating rotational smearing present in radar data.  
  > "In this paper, we use the observations primarily to constrain Psyche’s overall shape and size." (2.2 Atacama Large Millimeter Array); "In addition to tightly constraining the c-axis, the 2019 occultation was effectively broadside (shape model sub-observer longitude 95°) and helped to constrain the major (a-) axis." (2.4 2019 and 2010 Occultations); "Second, the AO, ALMA, and occultation datasets are snap-shots of Psyche with integration times of a few seconds to a few minutes and little or no smearing." (2.5 Methods)
- Why this method: Starting with a grid of ellipsoids before progressing to spherical harmonics and finally a faceted vertex model in SHAPE avoids local minima, while breaking radar integrations into sub-snapshots and weighting plane-of-sky snapshots at ~75% minimizes rotational smearing.  
  > "When beginning the modeling process, we start with ellipsoidal models of many different sizes, aspect ratios, and spin parameters, (i.e., a grid of parameters) and find those combinations that best fit the observations. This reduces the chance of falling into a local minimum solution space." (2.5 Methods); "By weighting these datasets more (~75%) than the reduced-smear delay-Doppler images (~25%) in the final model, we effectively reduced any smear to scales on the order of our image resolution." (2.5 Methods)
- Alternative: Stripped remnant metallic core model: largely disfavored due to bulk density (~4000 kg m^-3 vs ~7900 kg m^-3 for iron) requiring >50% porosity, though cannot be completely ruled out given recent hypervelocity impact experiments on iron substrates.  
  > "Based on the consensus bulk density measured for Psyche, the model of a remnant core is effectively ruled out unless it is a highly porous (~50% microporosity) object [Elkins-Tanton et al., 2020; Siltala and Granvik, 2021]." (5.3 Consequences for Psyche Model Interpretations); "Nevertheless, given the recent experiments of high velocity impacts on metal substrates [Libourel et al. 2020], we find that our results cannot rule it out." (6. SUMMARY AND FUTURE WORK)
- Alternative: Ferrovolcanism on a mostly metallic body (Abrahams and Nimmo 2019): rejected as incompatible with Psyche's overall bulk density.  
  > "However, the underlying assumption of this model is that Psyche is largely metallic throughout, possibly covered by a thin layer of silicate impact debris. This seems incompatible with Psyche’s overall bulk density." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Alternative: Impact-gardened or reaccumulated silicate-metal rubble pile (Davis et al. 1999): considered less compelling because random reaccumulation does not explain why metal concentrations are optically bright or how impacts concentrate metal without associated craters at all radar-bright sites.  
  > "If these concentrations are randomly distributed because of some reaccumulation process, it is not readily evident why they are optically brighter. If impacts are invoked, one must explain how they concentrate metal." (5.3 Consequences for Psyche Model Interpretations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Many subtle depressions in the shape model could be noise artifacts of data processing and inversion.  
  > "While there are many subtle depressions evident in our model, it is plausible that some are real, and equally plausible that some are noise artifacts." (3. SHAPE AND TOPOGRAPHIC FEATURES)
- Limitation (authors): Cross-hatched pattern in the ALMA images is an imaging artifact.  
  > "The cross-hatched pattern visible in the data is an artifact of the imaging process." (Figure 3 caption)
- Limitation (authors): Radar albedo is a whole-disk average (though weighted toward the sub-radar point within ~30-40 degrees), making the spatial map coarse and the correlation with optical albedo tentative.  
  > "As a caveat, we note that radar albedo is a whole-disk average; however, the echo will usually be most heavily weighted by the radar properties of features nearest (within ~30°-40°) the sub-radar point." (4. RADAR PROPERTIES); "Given the coarse nature of a radar albedo measurement, this conclusion is necessarily tentative, but appears credible." (5.2 Radar Albedo is Correlated with Optical Albedo)
- Limitation (authors): Arecibo radar is no longer available and Goldstone SNR (~15/day in 2025) is too low for future delay-Doppler imaging of Psyche.  
  > "Unfortunately, radar observations of Psyche at Arecibo are no longer possible. Goldstone radar can achieve SNRs of ~15/day in 2025, but these are too low for delay-Doppler imaging of Psyche." (6. SUMMARY AND FUTURE WORK)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA images are treated as simple opposition plane-of-sky silhouettes in SHAPE by artificially moving the Sun to opposition, neglecting millimeter thermal inertia/beaming phase effects across the disk.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The shape inversion relies on subjective penalty weights and visual inspection to select the final vertex model among chi-square-indistinguishable solutions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlation between radar and optical albedo is assessed qualitatively by visual inspection of 30 disk-integrated sub-radar points plotted over an optical map rather than a formal spatial deconvolution or statistical correlation test.

(Table 4); "On 2019 October 24, Psyche occulted a 10th magnitude star and a well-organized campaign led by members of the International Occultation and Timing Association (IOTA) generated 15 evenly spaced and highly accurate chords7 ." - Missing: Deconvolved 2015 and 2020 Keck AO images
