- Fields: nearby_galaxies, agn_and_quasars, astrochemistry
- Question: What are the physical and chemical properties (H2 volume density, kinetic temperature, and HCN-to-HCO+ abundance ratio) of nuclear dense molecular gas in the central 1–2 kpc of nearby (U)LIRGs with and without luminous AGN signatures?  
  > "their molecular line studies will provide important information of (1) the general properties of nuclear dense molecular gas in nearby (U)LIRGs at $\lesssim$1–2 kpc resolution and (2) possible difference of molecular gas properties between (U)LIRGs’ nuclei with and without luminous AGN signatures." (Targets); "Our ALMA 1–2 kpc resolution, multiple transition-line data of multiple molecules are a very powerful tool for scrutinizing the properties of molecular gas concentrated around luminous energy sources in nearby (U)LIRGs’ nuclei." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Match beam sizes of continuum-subtracted cubes and continuum images across transitions**: Convolve continuum-subtracted data cubes and continuum maps to a common circular beam size (1, 1.5, 1.6, or 2 kpc) for each target using CASA task imsmooth  
  > "first, the beam sizes of the multiple J-transition molecular line data were matched with the same value for each object using the CASA task “imsmooth” (cite: CASA)." (Observations and Data Analysis); "We obtained 1–2 kpc beam spectra by changing the beam size of continuum-subtracted data cube from the original values shown in the previous publications (cite: ima13a; ima13b; ima14; ima16a; ima16b; ima18; ima21; ima22), to the adopted ones (Table 2)." (Observations and Data Analysis)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Adopted circular beam sizes: 3.1'' (1 kpc) for NGC 1614; 1.1'' (1.6 kpc) for IRAS 06035-7102; 0.9'' (1 kpc) for IRAS 08572+3915; 1.1'' (1.5 kpc) for IRAS 12112+0305; 0.86'' (2 kpc) for IRAS 12127-1412; 0.84'' (2 kpc) for IRAS 13509+0442; 1.4'' (1.5 kpc) for IRAS 15250+3609; 0.85'' (1 kpc) for Superantennae; 1.2'' (1 kpc) for IRAS 20551-4250; 1.0'' (1.5 kpc) for IRAS 22491-1808

## Qualitative findings

- HCN and HCO+ line emission is significantly detected up to J=4-3, showing that dense (>=10^3-4 cm^-3) and warm (>=100 K) molecular gas is abundant in the central 1–2 kpc of nearby (U)LIRGs  
  > "HCN and HCO$^{+}$ line emission was significantly detected at up to J=4–3, suggesting that dense and warm molecular gas is abundant in the observed (U)LIRGs’ nuclei." (Summary); "Consequently, it was quantitatively derived that molecular gas in the observed (U)LIRGs’ nuclei was generally dense ($\gtrsim$10$^{3-4}$ cm$^{-3}$) and warm ($\gtrsim$100 K)." (Summary)
- The observed HCN-to-HCO+ flux ratios at J=2-1, J=3-2, and J=4-3 in the overall (U)LIRG sample are better reproduced by an enhanced HCN-to-HCO+ abundance ratio ([HCN]/[HCO+] ~ 3–7) than by equal abundances  
  > "The overall distribution of the observed HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios for the (U)LIRGs’ nuclei can be naturally reproduced with the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio of \[HCN\]/\[HCO$^{+}$\] = 3 rather than 1." (HCN-to-HCO+ Emission Line Flux Ratios)
- Nuclear molecular gas in the nine ULIRGs is significantly denser and warmer than in the LIRG NGC 1614, and all (U)LIRGs have denser and warmer nuclear gas than quiescently star-forming normal galaxies  
  > "The only one observed LIRG NGC 1614 showed molecular gas density and temperature distinctly lower than those of the remaining nine ULIRGs. ## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA 1–2 kpc resolution observations of HCN, HCO+, and HNC at J=2-1, J=3-2, and J=4-3 probe compact nuclear dense gas while minimizing contamination from extended (>a few kpc) host galaxy star formation, and suffer very small dust extinction  
  > "Probing only nuclear dense molecular gas through higher-spatial-resolution ($\lesssim$1–2 kpc) observations is highly desirable." (Introduction); "we aim to provide the clearest view of the nuclear dense molecular gas properties of nearby (U)LIRGs by minimizing possible contamination from spatially extended ($\gtrsim$a few kpc) star-forming regions in the host galaxies." (Observations and Data Analysis)
- Why this method: Non-LTE RADEX modeling of HCN and HCO+ line ratios constrains H2 density and kinetic temperature, while HNC is excluded due to its low abundance in warm gas near AGNs and non-simultaneous observations introducing extra flux calibration free parameters  
  > "To perform the RADEX non-LTE calculations, we use only the HCN and HCO$^{+}$ data, but exclude the HNC data for the following two reasons." (General Descriptions of Our RADEX Non-LTE Modeling); "Thus, possible absolute flux calibration uncertainty of three more ALMA observations (HNC J=2–1, J=3–2, and J=4–3) must be considered as additional free parameters for our RADEX non-LTE calculations" (General Descriptions of Our RADEX Non-LTE Modeling)
- Alternative: Double Gaussian fitting for sources with weak central dips was tested and rejected in favor of single Gaussian fits (except IRAS 12112+0305 NE) because flux estimates agreed within ~10%  
  > "however, we decide to adopt single Gaussian fitting results after confirming that the flux estimates between the single and double Gaussian fits agree within $\sim$10% for significantly detected emission lines ($\gtrsim$3$\sigma$)." (Results)
- Alternative: Including HNC data in RADEX modeling was rejected because HNC is depleted in warm gas near luminous sources and was observed separately from HCN/HCO+, adding three extra flux calibration scaling parameters  
  > "To perform the RADEX non-LTE calculations, we use only the HCN and HCO$^{+}$ data, but exclude the HNC data for the following two reasons." (General Descriptions of Our RADEX Non-LTE Modeling)
- Alternative: Correcting absolute flux calibration uncertainties by assuming a single power-law continuum SED across Bands 5, 6, and 7 was attempted and rejected because thermal dust emission in Band 7 makes the observed SED concave  
  > "Using the continuum data, we attempted to make some correction of the possible absolute flux calibration uncertainty for individual ALMA observations (maximum $\sim$10%; $\S$5.3) by assuming power law for an intrinsic continuum emission shape." (Continuum Flux Density Measured with 1–2 kpc Beam); "Thus, we decided not to apply this correction, because reliable correction turned out to be impossible." (Continuum Flux Density Measured with 1–2 kpc Beam)
- How they decided it was enough: In MCMC analysis, chains were lengthened while monitoring the integrated autocorrelation time tau and terminated when N/tau exceeded 100 (twice the emcee recommended minimum of 50)  
  > "Therefore, we lengthen the chain while monitoring $\tau$ and terminate the calculation when $N/\tau$ exceeds 100. This is a more conservative option than the minimum chain length recommended in the `emcee` documentation ($N/\tau=50$)." (Bayesian Analysis of Molecular Emission Line Flux Ratios)
- Limitation (authors): The sample of ten (U)LIRGs is neither statistically complete nor unbiased  
  > "Although the observed ten (U)LIRGs are neither statistically complete nor unbiased, their molecular line studies will provide important information" (Targets)
- Limitation (authors): For several transitions (e.g., IRAS 08572+3915 Cycle 0 J=4-3 and a few J=3-2/J=4-3 beams with major axis slightly larger than the adopted circular beam), the native beam is larger than the adopted beam, requiring the assumption that the bulk of the flux originates within the adopted beam  
  > "For IRAS 08572$+$3915, the beam size of J=4–3 data taken in Cycle 0 is significantly larger than the adopted value (Table 2). However, we assume that a dominant fraction of the observed J=4–3 emission originates from the adopted beam" (Observations and Data Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): When HCO+ column density and HCN-to-HCO+ abundance ratio are very high, HCN becomes optically thick and its flux saturates, so derived N_HCO+ and [HCN]/[HCO+] values can be lower limits  
  > "For very high HCO$^{+}$ column density and HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio, HCN emission line becomes very optically thick and its flux does not change. Thus, some of the derived HCO$^{+}$ column density and/or HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ abundance ratio could be lower limits." (Table 6)
- Limitation (authors): In the Bayesian MCMC analysis, the number of independent line ratios (5) is smaller than the total number of free parameters including flux scaling factors (7), and three ULIRGs (IRAS 15250+3609, Superantennae, IRAS 22491-1808) could not be well reproduced with small systematic discrepancy  
  > "There are five independent emission line flux ratios (HCN-to-HCO$^+$ flux ratios at J=2–1, J=3–2, and J=4–3, and HCO$^{+}$ J=3–2 to J=2–1 and J=4–3 to J=2–1 flux ratios), which are lower than the total number of parameters, including the absolute flux calibration scaling factors." (Bayesian Analysis of Molecular Emission Line Flux Ratios); "Results for IRAS 15250$+$3609, the Superantennae, and IRAS 22491$-$1808 are not shown because we are unable to provide model-calculated results which reproduce the observed results very well with small systematic discrepancy." (Bayesian Analysis of Molecular Emission Line Flux Ratios)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): One-zone static homogeneous RADEX modeling assumes identical beam filling factors and co-spatial emission for HCN and HCO+ across J=2-1, J=3-2, and J=4-3 within a 1–2 kpc beam
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Infrared radiative pumping of HCN (e.g., via 14 um vibrational transitions) in warm AGN-heated dust environments is not included in the collisional-only RADEX excitation model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For datasets where only the minor axis was smoothed or where the Cycle 0 beam was larger than the adopted beam, aperture/resolution mismatch could bias high-J to low-J line ratios
