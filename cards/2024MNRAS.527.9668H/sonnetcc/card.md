## Analysis recipe

1. **Align and flux-rescale executions**: Gaussian fits to each execution image to shift phase centres; fluxes rescaled with gencal using one execution per band as reference (Band 7 long-baseline data not rescaled)  
  > "we aligned the data by fitting a Gaussian to the image of each individual execution and shifting the phase center of each measurement set." (Observations and data reduction); "so in this band we only scaled the fluxes between the three short baseline executions." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, gencal
   - assumption: Thermal dust emission of V883 Ori does not vary over the up to 2 year spread in observing dates  
  > "we do not expect the thermal dust emission of FU Orionis objects like V883 Ori to vary on such short timescales" (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Self-calibrate and combine short and long baselines**: Iterative phase self-calibration on each dataset (tclean hogbom, briggs robust 0.5, nterms=1), then combine Bands 7 and 6, then another phase plus amplitude self-cal round with nterms=2  
  > "After combining the short and long baseline executions, we performed an additional cycle of phase self-calibration to ensure a good alignment of the data." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Self-calibration improved S/N by 10 to 220% on individual datasets  
  > "Overall, self-calibration on individual datasets provided improvements of the S/N ranging in between $10$ and $220\%$." (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: S/N improvement used as the criterion for solution interval and for rejecting amplitude self-cal  
  > "which did not provide any improvements of the S/N and was therefore not considered." (Observations and data reduction)
3. **Make final continuum images**: tclean with mtmfs, nterms=2, multiscale, Briggs weighting with robust 0 or 0.5 per band  
  > "We then built the final images with the task `tclean`, using the `mtmfs` deconvolver (cite: rau2011multi), $nterms = 2$" (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Robust 0 (Bands 7, 6) and 0.5 (Bands 4, 3)  
  > "6 | $1.33$ | $0$ | $26$ | $412$" (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive spectral index maps and radial profiles**: Pairs of bands combined in tclean (mtmfs, nterms=2) to give pixel-wise spectral index maps; azimuthally averaged in elliptical rings of 5 mas width; error of the mean per ring  
  > "we built spectral index maps by combining pairs of bands in the task `tclean`, which derives the spectral index at each pixel based on the observed visibilities, again using the `mtmfs` deconvolver and $nterms = 2$." (Observations and data reduction); "we average the spectral index in elliptical rings of width $5 \mathrm{~mas}$" (Spectral index radial profiles)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Inclination 38.3 deg, PA 32.4 deg (from Cieza et al. 2016) for the rings  
  > "with an eccentricity given by the disc inclination $i=38.3^\circ$ and position angle $\mathrm{P.A.} = 32.4^\circ$ (cite: cieza2016imaging)" (Spectral index radial profiles)
   - assumption: Nominal ALMA flux calibration errors 10% (Band 7) and 5% (Bands 6, 4, 3) shift profiles but not their relative shape  
  > "but it would not affect the relative shape of the individual profiles" (Spectral index radial profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Multi-band data argued to give a more robust spectral index than a single band; consistency among profiles suggests small flux calibration errors  
  > "Our multi-wavelength dataset also suggests that these systematic errors should not be significant" (Spectral index radial profiles)
5. **Simulate dust evolution through an outburst**: DustPy 1D coagulation/fragmentation/transport: 10^5 yr quiescent evolution, then step-like heating to outburst temperature profile for 10^4 yr; sublimation implemented as many-seeds (all particles reset to 0.5 micron monomers) or resilient (particles unchanged)  
  > "We let the dust population evolve in quiescent conditions until $t_\mathrm{otb} = 10^5 \mathrm{~yr}$, marking the onset of the accretion outburst." (Outburst event); "the many-seeds scenario, where we disrupt all dust particles in the sublimation region back to the monomer size ($0.5 \mathrm{~\mu m}$)" (Dust response to sublimation)
   - software: DustPy
   - parameter: 100 radial cells log-spaced between 1 and 200 au  
  > "We simulate the disc over a discretized grid of $100$ radial cells log-spaced between $1\mathrm{~au}$ and $200~\mathrm{au}$." (V883 Ori disc model)
   - parameter: Initial size distribution n(a) ~ a^-3.5 from 0.5 micron monomers to 100 micron aggregates  
  > "with a power-law $n(a) \propto a^{-3.5}$ from $0.5 \mathrm{~\mu m}$ monomers up to $100 \mathrm{~\mu m}$ aggregates" (Dust model)
   - parameter: v_frag = 1 m/s inside the water snowline, 10 m/s outside  
  > "We set the fragmentation velocity to $v_\mathrm{frag} = 1 \mathrm{~m~s^{-1}}$ inside the water snowline, and $10 \mathrm{~m~s^{-1}}$ outside" (Dust model)
   - parameter: Outburst active for 10^4 yr; outburst water snowline at about 80 au; sublimation temperature 150 K  
  > "we keep the outburst active for $10^4 \mathrm{~yr}$" (Outburst event); "the normalisation has been updated so that the water snowline position corresponds roughly to $80 \mathrm{~au}$" (Outburst event)
   - assumption: Disc mass about 0.3 Msun, tapered power-law gas surface density with gamma = 1.25 and r_c = 75 au; stellar mass 1.3 Msun; L* = 6 Lsun; disc opening angle 0.05  
  > "where $\phi=0.05$ is the disc opening angle and $L_\mathrm{*}=6~\mathrm{L_\odot}$ is the stellar luminosity (cite: cieza2016imaging)" (V883 Ori disc model)
   - assumption: Temperature rises instantaneously to the outburst profile and stays constant; water ice sublimates instantaneously at outburst onset; compact spherical homogeneously mixed aggregates; water ice 20% of grain mass  
  > "We assume the temperature to increase instantaneously to the outburst value" (Outburst event); "we assume dust grains to coagulate following a compact aggregation model, remaining spherical and homogeneously mixed in dust and ice at all times" (Dust model)
   - fitting: No fitting; forward simulations compared qualitatively with observed spectral indices  
  > "Note that obtaining a detailed fit of the observed spectral indices is beyond the scope of this paper" (Observational results)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Convert simulated dust distributions to spectral index**: Opacities from DSHARP-OPAC using Bruggeman mixed dielectric functions; 1D slab emergent intensity I_nu including scattering; spectral index from ln(I1/I2)/ln(nu1/nu2) at the central wavelengths of the images; not convolved with the ALMA beam  
  > "We then compute the mixed dielectric function using the Bruggeman effective medium theory (cite: bruggeman1935berechnung)" (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations); "As a result, the spectral index from our models is not convolved with the resolution of the ALMA images" (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations)
   - software: DSHARP-OPAC, DustPy
   - parameter: Inclination i = 38.3 deg used via mu = cos i  
  > "$\mu = \cos{i}$, and $i=38.3^\circ$ is the inclination angle of V883 Ori" (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations)
   - assumption: 1D slab is a good approximation because sub-mm emission traces large pebbles settled in the midplane; midplane dust temperature used  
  > "This 1D slab model is a good approximation for protoplanetary discs, given that, at sub-mm wavelengths, we mainly trace large pebbles highly settled into the disc midplane." (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compare models with observed profiles and test turbulence**: Qualitative comparison of simulated and observed spectral index profiles in the many-seeds and resilient scenarios; extra simulations with alpha_T = 10^-2 and 10^-4  
  > "We then qualitatively compared results of the dust evolution models with the observed spectral indices" (Summary and outlook); "we perform additional dust evolution simulations using $\alpha_\mathrm{T}=10^{-2}$ and $\alpha_\mathrm{T}=10^{-4}$." (Varying the turbulence parameter)
   - software: DustPy
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Conclusion robust against turbulence variation: many-seeds spectral index too high at all tested alpha_T  
  > "the spectral index in the many-seeds case is too high, and does not well represent the observed dust population in V883 Ori" (Varying the turbulence parameter)

## Qualitative findings

- The dark annulus near 42 au has no strong imprint in the spectral index profiles, so it is not tied to a local change in grain size  
  > "we notice that the dark annulus visible in the final images around $42 \mathrm{~au}$ (Fig. [1](#fig:fig1_final_images)) has no strong imprints in the spectral index profiles" (Spectral index radial profiles)
- Spectral indices are lower than the earlier Band 6 only estimate, implying considerably larger particles than previously assumed  
  > "Overall, our spectral index radial profiles suggest the presence of larger particles than previously assumed in V883 Ori" (Spectral index radial profiles)
- Only the resilient scenario reproduces the observed spectral indices; aggregates formed before the outburst and survive sublimation  
  > "the only way to explain the dust population in V883 Ori is that the resilient scenario applies" (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High resolution multi-wavelength data are needed to characterise the dust size distribution  
  > "To characterise the dust size distribution in V883 Ori, it is crucial to perform a multi-wavelength analysis with high resolution data." (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Spectral index maps from combined bands retain better uv coverage; the goal is to compare two end-member scenarios rather than to fit  
  > "while also benefiting from a better $uv$ coverage as compared to deriving the spectral index from the intensity measured in individual bands" (Observations and data reduction); "Rather, we seek to model how the two end-member cases (many seeds vs. resilient) impact the dust distribution and evolution of the spectral index" (Spectral index radial profiles)
- Alternative: Many-seeds scenario (all aggregates disrupt to micron grains) rejected because model spectral indices are too high (about 3.7)  
  > "Given that, in the many-seeds scenario, mm-sized aggregates require timescale significantly larger than the typical outburst duration to re-appear" (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations)
- Alternative: A hybrid scenario between many-seeds and resilient is envisioned but not modelled  
  > "a hybrid scenario can be envisioned" (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): No detailed fit of the observed spectral indices; unknown disc parameters and initial conditions  
  > "given the intricacies of all the different dust evolution models, and unknowns about disc parameters and initial conditions" (Spectral index radial profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Model spectral index not convolved with the ALMA beam; no vertical temperature structure; model does not reproduce alpha < 2 in the inner disc  
  > "The model does not manage to reproduce the values $\alpha<2$ in the inner disc" (Comparisons between observations and dust simulations)
- Limitation (authors): Constant outburst temperature profile assumed; luminosity decay not modelled; post-outburst phase not modelled  
  > "we assume a step-like function for the temporal evolution of the disc temperature" (Accretion luminosity variability)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA flux calibration systematics could shift spectral index at all radii  
  > "Such systematic uncertainty could increase or decrease the spectral index at all radii up to a maximum shift" (Spectral index radial profiles)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model-data comparison is purely qualitative, with no fitted parameters or likelihood, so the preference for the resilient scenario rests on visual agreement of alpha at one outburst baseline
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Many model choices (alpha_T, v_frag, t_otb, 80 au snowline, instantaneous heating) are fixed by hand; the 1D slab opacity treatment may differ from full radiative transfer
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux scale offsets between bands and the 10% Band 7 uncertainty could shift alpha by amounts comparable to the claimed difference from the earlier 3.5
