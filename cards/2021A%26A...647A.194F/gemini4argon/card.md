## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct 3D masks and zeroth-moment total-flux maps**: Sum emission across all channel maps after applying a 3D mask generated by smoothing and clipping followed by 3D source detection in 3DBarolo  
  > "The total-flux map (analogous to a zeroth moment) has been constructed by summing the emission across all channel maps after the application of a mask." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "The mask is calculated by [3D] Barolo through smoothing and clipping the data cube, followed by a source detection in the 3D space (Di Teodoro & Fraternali 2015)." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Experimented with only smoothing and clipping or only source detection; verified noise variations along outer contours are less than 20%  
  > "After several experiments with either only smoothing and clipping or only source detection, we found that a combination of the two produces the best result" (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "we have verified that the variations along the outer contours shown in Figs. 1 and 4 are small (less than 20%)." (Footnote 1)
2. **Fit 3D tilted-ring kinematic models to low spectral resolution data cubes**: Determine geometrical parameters (centre, systemic frequency, PA, inclination), fix them, and simultaneously fit rotation velocity and velocity dispersion in concentric rings using 3DBarolo with surface density derived from azimuthal average  
  > "We finally ran [3D] Barolo keeping all the geometrical parameters and the systemic frequency fixed and simultaneously fitting the rotation velocity and the velocity dispersion of the gas." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "The surface density of the model is derived directly from the data as an azimuthal average within the annuli." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
   - software: 3DBarolo, Galpak3D
   - parameter: AzTEC/C159: 2 rings of radial width 0.23'', fixed i = 40 ± 10 deg, PA = 170 ± 10 deg  
  > "Our AzTEC/C159 data have very few resolution elements across the galaxy, and thus we chose to use only two rings. These rings define two adjacent annuli, each with a radial width of 0.23 [′′] ." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "Disc inclination ( _i_ ) 40 ± 10 [◦] 1" (Table 1); "Disc position angle (PA) 170 ± 10 [◦] 1" (Table 1)
   - parameter: J1000+0234: 3 rings of radial width 0.2'', fixed i = 75 ± 10 deg, PA = 145 ± 5 deg  
  > "Given the size of the galaxy along the major axis and the orientation of the beam, we used three adjacent annuli that were each 0.2 [′′] wide." (3.3. 3D kinematic fit of J1000+0234); "Disc inclination ( _i_ ) 75 ± 10 [◦] 1" (Table 2); "Disc position angle (PA) 145 ± 5 [◦] 1" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Axi-symmetric rotating disc composed of concentric rings  
  > "In practice, this is a mock observation built as a kinematic model of concentric rings where the gas motion is characterised by 1) rotation and 2) random motions (that we ascribe to turbulence)." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
   - fitting: Minimise residuals between data and beam-convolved mock observation; uncertainties from Monte Carlo sampling summed in quadrature with errors from varying inclination by ±10 deg  
  > "After producing the mock observation, the algorithm convolves it with the beam before calculating and eventually minimising the residuals between data and model." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "Therefore, we calculated the errors by re-running [3D] Barolo and fixing the inclination first to 30 [◦] (the upper bound in rotation velocity) and then to 50 [◦] (the lower bound). The resulting errors were summed in quadrature with the nominal (Monte Carlo) errors of the fit" (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159)
   - validation: Inclination confirmed via minimum residuals between model and observed total-flux maps (42 deg for AzTEC/C159, 73 deg for J1000+0234); kinematic fits independently checked with Galpak3D  
  > "The residuals that we found display a clear minimum at 42 [◦], confirming the trial-and-error value." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "The calculation of the residuals between total-flux maps of [3D] Barolo models with varying inclinations and the observed total-flux map returns a minimum at 73 [◦], confirming the trial-and-error value." (3.3. 3D kinematic fit of J1000+0234); "We also ran kinematic fits of our two galaxies using Galpak [3D] (Bouché et al. 2015). We found values for the rotation velocities, the velocity dispersions, and the inclinations that are fully consistent, within the errors, with the [3D] Barolo runs." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159)
3. **Measure gas disc exponential scale-length and perform mass decomposition of rotation curves**: Fit [C II] zeroth-moment map with Galfit to derive gas exponential scale-length; calculate dynamical mass M(<R_out) = R_out * v_c^2 / G and illustrative rotation curve contributions from stellar and gas exponential discs plus NFW dark matter halo  
  > "We derived the exponential scale-length of the gas disc from the [C II] zeroth moment map using Galfit (Peng et al. 2010a) and obtained _R_ gas = 1.3 ± 0.4 kpc." (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159); "This is calculated with the standard formula _M_ (< _R_ out ) = _R_ out v [2] c [(] _[R]_ [out] [)][/] _[G]_ [(with] _[R]_ [out] [the] [radius] [of] [the] [outer] [ring),]" (Footnote 2); "As an illustration, in Fig. 3 (left panel) we show the contribution of two razor-thin exponential discs (Freeman 1970) to represent the contribution of stars and gas." (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159)
   - software: Galfit
   - parameter: NFW halo with c_200 = 3.4 and M_200 = 2 × 10^12 M_sun  
  > "For the dark matter halo, we assumed a Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) profile (Navarro et al. 1996) with a concentration _c_ 200 = 3.4, which is appropriate for halos of very different masses at these redshifts (Dutton & Macciò 2014), and a mass _M_ 200 = 2 × 10 [12] _M_ ⊙ (e.g. Rizzo et al. 2020)." (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159)
   - assumption: Planck Collaboration et al. (2018) cosmology (6.70 kpc/'' for AzTEC/C159, 6.72 kpc/'' for J1000+0234); v_rot = v_c; optical R_e = 1.4 * R_e,FIR; exponential scale-length R_d = R_e / 1.678; razor-thin exponential discs for stars and gas  
  > "Throughout the paper we use Planck cosmological parameters (Planck Collaboration et al. 2018)." (1. Introduction); "We converted this FIR radius to an approximate rest-frame optical effective radius using a multiplicative factor of 1.4 (Fujimoto et al. 2017). From this we derived the exponential scale-length _R_ d using the standard conversion _R_ d = _R_ e /1.678." (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159); "From this rotation velocity (assuming it is equal to the circular speed v c ), we can derive a dynamical mass" (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159)
   - fitting: Galfit 2D fit to [C II] zeroth-moment map; rotation curve decomposition is illustrative and not fitted to data points  
  > "This comparison is illustrative and not fitted to the data points." (Figure 3 caption); "For the gaseous disc, we used the [C II] 158-µm zeroth moment map to obtain an exponential scale-length _R_ gas = 1.5 ± 0.2 kpc from Galfit (Peng et al. 2010a)." (3.4. Rotation curve analysis of J1000+0234)
   - validation: Tested pseudo-isothermal halo profile as alternative to NFW; compared derived J1000+0234 gas mass and alpha_[CII] with literature starbursts and dust masses  
  > "Using alternative profiles for the dark matter halo (such as the pseudo-isothermal profile) also makes only a negligible difference." (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159); "This mass leads to a ratio between [C II] luminosity and gas mass (both in standard units), α [C II] ≈ 8 (using the luminosity _L_ [′] in Carilli & Walter 2013), in agreement with values determined for other high- _z_ starbursts (Gullberg et al. 2018)." (3.4. Rotation curve analysis of J1000+0234)
4. **Constrain intrinsic gas velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159 at high spectral resolution**: Fit the 10.3 km s^-1 channel separation data cube of AzTEC/C159 with 3DBarolo and map the 2D parameter space of (v_rot, sigma_gas) using the spacepar keyword  
  > "We thus produced a high spectral resolution data cube with a channel separation of 10.3 km s [−][1] ." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159); "To further test the reliability of the above fit, we ran 3D Barolo with the spacepar keyword turned on. This keyword allows the user to explore the full parameter space of two parameters by calculating the residual (|model−data|) in a grid with arbitrary spacing." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: Channel separation 10.3 km s^-1; initial guesses sigma_guess varied between 10 and 300 km s^-1; grid range ~200 km s^-1 in v_rot and sigma_gas  
  > "These low values of velocity dispersion are robust over any initial guesses for the fit, which we made to vary between σ guess = 10 km s [−][1] and σ guess = 300 km s [−][1] ." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159); "There we see the values of the residual for any pair of v rot and σ gas in a range of about 200 km s [−][1] in each direction." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159)
   - fitting: Minimise |model - data| across parameter space grid for each of the two rings separately  
  > "This exploration was done for the two rings separately, and the final result is shown in Fig. 8." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159)
   - validation: Verified robustness over initial guesses from 10 to 300 km s^-1 and confirmed a single well-defined absolute minimum in the 2D residual grid  
  > "It is clear that the exploration of both rings shows a well-defined absolute minimum, and there is no sign of a secondary minimum." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159)
5. **Estimate supernova-driven turbulent velocity dispersion and compare with z = 0 ETG Tully-Fisher relation**: Equate supernova kinetic energy input rate to turbulent dissipation rate to predict sigma_gas; place galaxies on the local ETG CO stellar-mass Tully-Fisher relation before and after converting all gas into stars  
  > "To maintain turbulence at the˙ observed value, supernovae must provide energy at the rate _E_ turb = _E_ turb /τ. By equating this energy input (see also Rizzo et al. 2020) with the one available in Eq. (1), we can estimate a gas velocity dispersion" (4.2. Understanding the low gas turbulence in AzTEC/C159); "For the stellar masses, we used the estimated stellar mass (empty symbols), and we estimated their ‘final’ mass by assuming that they will convert all their gas into stars (filled symbols)." (4.3. Evolution of high-z starbursts)
   - parameter: SNR ≈ 7 yr^-1, E_SN = 10^51 erg, efficiency eta = 0.1, disc scale-height h = 100-500 pc  
  > "Given the SFR of this galaxy of 740 _M_ ⊙ yr [−][1], one would expect a supernova rate (SNR) of SNR ≈ 7 yr [−][1], assuming a standard initial mass function." (4.2. Understanding the low gas turbulence in AzTEC/C159); "where we have taken the standard total energy per supernova of 10 [51] erg and an efficiency of transferring kinetic energy to the ISM of 10% (Chevalier 1974; Ohlin et al. 2019)." (4.2. Understanding the low gas turbulence in AzTEC/C159); "Turbulence is dissipated on timescales of τ ≈ _h_ /σ gas (Mac Low 1999), where _h_ is the scaleheight of the disc that unfortunately is unknown and we assume to be in the range _h_ = 100-500 pc." (4.2. Understanding the low gas turbulence in AzTEC/C159)
   - assumption: Standard IMF; 100% conversion of observed gas mass into stars for final stellar mass on Tully-Fisher relation; M/L = 0.5 M_sun/L_sun,K for local ETGs

Evolution of high-z starbursts); "The empty symbols show the positions of our galaxies based on the observed stellar mass, while the filled symbols show their positions assuming that all the observed gas mass is converted into stars." ## Qualitative findings

- The [C II] 158-µm emission of both AzTEC/C159 and J1000+0234 is very well reproduced by axi-symmetric gas discs in differential rotation.  
  > "1. The [C II] 158-µm emission of our two galaxies is perfectly reproduced by models of gas discs in regular (differential) rotation." (5. Conclusions)
- Both galaxies are strongly baryon-dominated in their inner few kiloparsecs, with dark matter playing a negligible role.  
  > "Both galaxies are strongly baryon-dominated in the probed regions (the inner few kiloparsecs) with a negligible dark matter contribution." (5. Conclusions)
- Once their gas is converted into stars, both galaxies overlap with local massive early-type galaxies on the CO stellar-mass Tully-Fisher relation, supporting an evolutionary connection.  
  > "We show that these high- _z_ galaxies overlap with _z_ = 0 massive ETGs in the ETG analogue of the stellar-mass Tully-Fisher relation once their gas is converted into stars." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected from the only seven spectroscopically confirmed z >~ 4 galaxies in the COSMOS field; [C II] 158-µm is the brightest atomic fine-structure line in the ISM and a good kinematic tracer of molecular and atomic gas at z >~ 4; the third observed galaxy (Vd-17871) lacked sufficient data quality.  
  > "The dynamics of high- _z_ galaxies and AGNs has also been studied with the [C II] 158-µm line, which is the brightest atomic fine-structure line in the interstellar medium (ISM) and is thought to be a good tracer of both molecular and atomic gas (Carilli & Walter 2013)." (1. Introduction); "Only three galaxies of the original sample were eventually observed, the third being Vd-17871, which we do not present here as the data are not of sufficient quality for a reliable kinematic fit." (2. Galaxy and data presentation)
- Why this method: 3D tilted-ring modelling directly on the data cube with 3DBarolo accounts for severe beam smearing at low angular resolution during the fitting process without extracting 2D velocity maps.  
  > "The best way to correct for this observational bias is to build artificial observations (3D tilted-ring models) and compared them directly with the emission in the data cube, without extracting 2D maps." (1. Introduction); "This approach allows us to account for the beam smearing effect in the best possible way, that is, during the fitting process (Di Teodoro et al. 2016; Iorio et al. 2017; Mancera Piña et al. 2020)." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
- Alternative: Using 3DBarolo with only smoothing and clipping or only 3D source detection for mask creation, rejected in favour of combining both.  
  > "After several experiments with either only smoothing and clipping or only source detection, we found that a combination of the two produces the best result of a mask that is not too tight around the emission but also not so large as to include too much noise." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
- Alternative: Fitting the 2D velocity field directly, rejected because the velocity field is heavily affected by beam smearing.  
  > "The morphology of the velocity field is largely dominated by the beam smearing. Therefore, the velocity field has not been used in the fit of the kinematics in any way, and it is only shown here as an illustration." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
- Alternative: Using a pseudo-isothermal dark matter halo profile instead of an NFW profile, found to make a negligible difference.  
  > "Using alternative profiles for the dark matter halo (such as the pseudo-isothermal profile) also makes only a negligible difference." (3.2. Rotation curve analysis of AzTEC/C159)
- Alternative: Interpreting the [C II] velocity gradients as outflows, mergers, or gas inflows, rejected due to the symmetric extreme-channel accumulation, butterfly pattern, and alignment of kinematic and morphological axes.  
  > "However, velocity gradients can also be evidence of outflows (Martini et al. 2018), mergers (Sweet et al. 2019), or, potentially, gas inflow." (4.1. Determining if velocity gradients can indicate anything other than rotation); "We conclude that there is no reason to doubt that the structures that we are observing in these two galaxies are rotating discs at _z_ ≈ 4.5." (4.1. Determining if velocity gradients can indicate anything other than rotation)
- How they decided it was enough: Checked that residual channel maps and p-v diagrams showed minimal deviations, tested inclination via total-flux map residual minimisation, explored the 2D (v_rot, sigma_gas) parameter space with spacepar, and confirmed results independently with Galpak3D.  
  > "To test this trial-and-error result, we also computed the residuals between the total-flux maps of the [3D] Barolo models with varying inclinations and the observed map." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "To further test the reliability of the above fit, we ran 3D Barolo with the spacepar keyword turned on." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159); "We also ran kinematic fits of our two galaxies using Galpak [3D] (Bouché et al. 2015)." (3.5. The [C II] velocity dispersion in AzTEC/C159)
- Limitation (authors): The rings used in the 3D kinematic fits (two for AzTEC/C159, three for J1000+0234) slightly oversample the beam and are not fully independent, preventing a full formal rotation curve decomposition.  
  > "Given the size of the beam (Table 3), this choice makes the properties extracted from the two rings not fully independent." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159); "As for AzTEC/C159, the presence of only three (and not fully independent) points does not allow for a full decomposition of the rotation curve" (Section 3.3)
- Limitation (authors): The relatively low inclination of AzTEC/C159 (40 ± 10 deg) introduces a large uncertainty in its rotation velocity.  
  > "Given the uncertainty of dealing with a disc at a relatively low inclination, we chose to adopt a conservative error of 10 [◦] in the final value (Table 1). This error in inclination is the main contributor to the uncertainty in the rotation velocity for AzTEC/C159." (3.1. 3D kinematic fit of AzTEC/C159)
- Limitation (authors): The high inclination of J1000+0234 (75 ± 10 deg) causes line-of-sight projection broadening that makes its velocity dispersion more uncertain and potentially overestimated, and its broad line reaches the edge of the ALMA band.  
  > "The systemic frequency is somewhat uncertain because the emission is so broad that it reaches the edge of the ALMA band." (3.3. 3D kinematic fit of J1000+0234); "However, being the galaxy at high inclination, these velocity dispersions should be considered to be more uncertain than those of AzTEC/C159. They could in fact be overestimated as some line broadening can occur, along the line of sight, because of projection effects." (3.4. Rotation curve analysis of J1000+0234)
- Limitation (authors): The stellar mass and stellar morphology of J1000+0234 are poorly constrained due to dust absorption in HST and low resolution in Spitzer.  
  > "As mentioned, the stellar mass of J1000+0234 is largely unconstrained as there are two very different determinations in the literature. The stellar size cannot be determined directly since the source is heavily absorbed in the _HST_ bands and is essentially unresolved by _Spitzer_ at near-infrared (NIR) wavelengths (Smolˇci´c et al. 2015)." (3.4. Rotation curve analysis of J1000+0234)
- Limitation (authors): The physical mechanism that transforms these fast-rotating, low-turbulence high-z discs into pressure-supported slow-rotator ETGs at z = 0 remains unclear.  
  > "This provides dynamical evidence of the connection between massive high- _z_ starbursts and ETGs, although the transformation mechanism from fast rotating to nearly pressure-supported systems remains unclear." (abstract)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The spatial resolution (~0.35'' compared to ~0.4''-0.6'' disc radius) means each galaxy is resolved by only ~2-3 beams across the major axis, making the flat rotation curve shape and ring-by-ring decompositions indicative rather than uniquely constrained.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For AzTEC/C159, the best-fit intrinsic velocity dispersion (16 ± 13 km s^-1) is comparable to a single channel width (10.3 km s^-1) and has nearly 100% fractional uncertainty, so the V/sigma >~ 20 result is effectively a lower limit sensitive to channelisation and low S/N per channel.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The role of ALMA project 2015.1.01564.S is acknowledged at the end of the paper but never explained in Section 2.
