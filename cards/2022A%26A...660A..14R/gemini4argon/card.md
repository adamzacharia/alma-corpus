## Analysis recipe

1. **Select sample of [CII] non-detections with robust UV spectroscopic redshifts**: Visually inspect optical/UV spectra of the 43 ALPINE [CII] non-detections to select sources with multiple high S/N spectral features and prominent Ly-alpha emission, excluding 9 sources with weak/no Ly-alpha and noisy spectra.  
  > "For this reason, we visually inspected the optical spectra of the non-detections. We found that 34 out of 43 sources present multiple high S/N spectral features, all of them showing a prominent Ly$\alpha$ line in emission" (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data); "We thus obtain a final sample of 34 [CII] non-detections." (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data)
   - parameter: 34 selected out of 43 [CII] non-detections (S/N < 3.5 in [CII])  
  > "We found that 34 out of 43 sources present multiple high S/N spectral features, all of them showing a prominent Ly$\alpha$ line in emission" (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data)
   - assumption: Redshift obtained from the peak of the Ly-alpha line can be corrected using the observed velocity offset distribution between Ly-alpha and [CII]  
  > "we make use of the redshift obtained from the peak of the Ly$\alpha$ line, and then correct it for the observed velocity offsets between Ly$\alpha$ and the systemic velocity traced by [CII]" (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract 1D ALMA spectra and perform Monte Carlo mean spectral stacking**: Extract 1D spectra from ALPINE DR1 continuum-subtracted data-cubes in a 1'' radius aperture centered at optical positions over ~3 GHz around the Ly-alpha expected frequency. Align to Ly-alpha redshift, apply random velocity offsets drawn from the Cassata et al. (2020) Delta v_Lya distribution, compute mean stack, fit a Gaussian to check FWHM <= 400 km s^-1 until 1000 realizations are collected, and average the realizations weighted by S/N.  
  > "we extract each spectrum from the original data-cubes of the ALPINE data release 1 (DR1; (cite: Bethermin20)), within a fixed aperture of 1” of radius" (Stacking of non-detections); "we apply to each source a spectral offset randomly extracted from the $\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}$ distribution obtained by (cite: Cassata20) from a sub-sample of the ALPINE detections, and then compute the mean stack on the shifted spectra." (Stacking of non-detections); "For each stacked spectrum, we then require that $\mathrm{FWHM}\leq400$ km s$^{-1}$, which defines the 84th percentile of the observed FWHM distribution of the ALPINE [CII] detections (see (cite: Bethermin20)), until reaching 1000 realizations." (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: aperture radius = 1''  
  > "within a fixed aperture of 1” of radius (defining the central regions of the ALPINE targets; see (cite: Bethermin20))" (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: spectral range = ~3 GHz  
  > "covering a spectral range of $\sim3$ GHz around the expected peak frequency of the emission line (as traced by the Ly$\alpha$ emission)." (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: FWHM <= 400 km s^-1 filter for 1000 realizations  
  > "For each stacked spectrum, we then require that $\mathrm{FWHM}\leq400$ km s$^{-1}$, which defines the 84th percentile of the observed FWHM distribution of the ALPINE [CII] detections (see (cite: Bethermin20)), until reaching 1000 realizations." (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: S/N weight w = S/N, with noise measured at |v| > 600 km s^-1 from peak  
  > "In the latter case, we estimate the S/N as the ratio between the peak flux of the stacked line and the standard deviation of the spectral channels at velocities greater and smaller than $\pm 600$ km s$^{-1}$ from the peak" (Stacking of non-detections)
   - assumption: 0 < Delta v_Lya < 400 km s^-1 distribution from ALPINE detections applies to non-detections  
  > "and then correct it for the observed velocity offsets between Ly$\alpha$ and the systemic velocity traced by [CII] ($0<\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}<400$ km s$^{-1}$) for the ALPINE detections (cite: Cassata20)." (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to the stacked line profile (FWHM = 2.355 sigma)  
  > "we compute each time a Gaussian fit on the stacked line, estimating its full width at half maximum as $\mathrm{FWHM}=2.355\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the Gaussian." (Stacking of non-detections); "By fitting the stacked line profile with a Gaussian function, we find the signal peaking at $\mathrm{x_{cent}}=-16\pm13$ km s$^{-1}$" (Stacking of non-detections)
   - validation: Tested weighting schemes (w=1, w=1/sigma_rms^2, w=S/N); tested random extraction method on a sub-sample of [CII] detections with known systemic and Ly-alpha redshifts; tested using constant mean offset (~183 km s^-1); tested removing the FWHM <= 400 km s^-1 constraint.  
  > "We select a sub-sample of [CII] detections having, in addition to the [CII]-traced systemic redshift, also the redshift measurements from Ly$\alpha$." (Stacking of non-detections); "As an additional check, we re-do the entire stacking procedure by considering, for each source, the mean value of the Ly$\alpha$-[CII] offset distribution found by (cite: Cassata20) (i.e., $\sim183~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}}$)" (Stacking of non-detections)
3. **Measure velocity-integrated flux, [CII] luminosity, and uncertainties, and repeat in SFR bins**: Integrate the Gaussian-fitted stacked line to get velocity-integrated flux, convert to L_[CII] using Solomon et al. (1992) equation at z_med = 5.52, and estimate uncertainty via delete-d jackknife resampling (500 realizations removing 20% of the 34 spectra). Repeat stacking by splitting the 34 sources into two bins of 17 sources each at median log(SFR_TOT) = 1.16.  
  > "Following (cite: Solomon92), we also compute $\mathrm{L_{[CII]}}$ as $$\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{L_{[CII]}} = 1.04 \times 10^{-3}~S_\mathrm{[CII]}~\Delta v~D_L^2~\nu_{obs}~[\mathrm{L_\odot}],
    \label{eq:line_lum}
\end{equation}$$" (Stacking of non-detections); "We produce 500 jackknife realizations following the same method described above, but randomly removing each time 20% of the 34 spectra in the parent sample" (Stacking of non-detections); "We use the median SFR of the sample ($\mathrm{log(SFR_{TOT,med}/M_{\odot}~yr^{-1}})=1.16$; as defined in Section [4]) to make two bins composed of 17 objects each, repeating for both of them the previously described stacking procedure." (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: z_med = 5.52  
  > "$D_L$ is the luminosity distance in Mpc at the median redshift of the sample (i.e., $z_\mathrm{med}=5.52$)" (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: 500 jackknife realizations removing 20% of spectra; 16th and 84th percentiles for error  
  > "We produce 500 jackknife realizations following the same method described above, but randomly removing each time 20% of the 34 spectra in the parent sample" (Stacking of non-detections); "The error on $S_\mathrm{[CII]}~\Delta v$ is then computed from the 16th and 84th percentiles of the fluxes distribution of the 500 jackknife realizations." (Stacking of non-detections)
   - parameter: 2 SFR bins of 17 objects each divided at log(SFR_TOT,med / M_sun yr^-1) = 1.16  
  > "We use the median SFR of the sample ($\mathrm{log(SFR_{TOT,med}/M_{\odot}~yr^{-1}})=1.16$; as defined in Section [4]) to make two bins composed of 17 objects each" (Stacking of non-detections)
   - assumption: Lambda-CDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7; Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "Throughout this work, we adopt a $\mathrm{\Lambda-CDM}$ cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\mathrm{\Omega_m}=0.3$ and $\mathrm{\Omega_\Lambda}=0.7$. Furthermore, we use a (cite: Chabrier03) initial mass function (IMF)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested removing the FWHM constraint on the stacked spectra, finding no significant difference in L_[CII].  
  > "We test that this result is not significantly different from the one obtained by removing the constraint on the FWHM of the stacked spectra, which thus affects only the width of the line." (Stacking of non-detections)
4. **Fit the L_[CII]-SFR linear relation**: Combine the stacked [CII] luminosity and mean total SFR of the non-detections with the individual ALPINE [CII] detections, and fit log(L_[CII]/L_sun) = a + b * log(SFR/M_sun yr^-1) using the Bayesian linear regression package linmix.  
  > "We fit the combined [CII] luminosity and SFR from the stacking of non-detections with the ALPINE [CII]-detected galaxies with a linear relation of the form $$\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{log(L_\mathrm{[CII]}/L_\odot)} = a + b \times \mathrm{log(SFR/M_{\odot}~yr^{-1}}),
    \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}$$" (Results); "For consistency with (cite: Schaerer20), we use the `linmix` package by (cite: Kelly07) which makes use of a Bayesian approach to account for measurement errors in both variables in linear regressions." (Results)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: Average uncertainty on total SFR ~ 0.2 dex  
  > "Regarding the individual uncertainties on the SFRs, we propagate the errors of SFR(UV) and SFR(IR) on the total SFR (obtaining on average $\sim0.2$ dex for both detections and non-detections)." (Results)
   - assumption: SFR_TOT = SFR(UV) + SFR(IR), where SFR(IR) for continuum-undetected galaxies comes from the IRX-beta relation of Fudamoto et al. (2020); Chabrier IMF (De Looze et al. 2014 relation scaled by dividing SFR by 1.06)  
  > "we use in this case the total SFRs obtained as $\mathrm{SFR_{TOT} = SFR(UV)+SFR(IR)}$, where SFR(UV) comes from the observed UV absolute magnitude at 1500 $\AA$, and SFR(IR) is obtained both through the ALMA continuum measurements and, for galaxies undetected in continuum, through predictions of the IRX-$\beta$ relation by (cite: Fudamoto20)" (Results); "As this relation is based on a (cite: Kroupa03) IMF, we scaled it to a (cite: Chabrier03) IMF by dividing the SFR by a factor 1.06" (Results)
   - fitting: Bayesian linear regression accounting for measurement errors in both variables (linmix)  
  > "For consistency with (cite: Schaerer20), we use the `linmix` package by (cite: Kelly07) which makes use of a Bayesian approach to account for measurement errors in both variables in linear regressions." (Results)
   - validation: Compared best-fit slope and intercept with Schaerer et al. (2020) and local/high-z literature relations.  
  > "This is consistent with the slope obtained from local galaxies ($b=1.00\pm0.04$; (cite: DeLooze14)), and in agreement with that previously found by (cite: Schaerer20) considering detections and $3\sigma$ upper limits on non-detections (i.e., $b=1.17 \pm 0.12$)." (Results)

## Qualitative findings

- The ALPINE [CII] non-detections are not drawn from a different population than the detections, but are normal galaxies lying at the lower-SFR and lower-stellar-mass end of the z ~ 5 main sequence with fainter [CII] emission.  
  > "This result suggests that the [CII] non-detections are not drawn from a different population of galaxies with respect to the ALPINE detections. Rather, these are galaxies lying on the bottom-left region of the $z\sim5$ main-sequence with lower SFRs and stellar masses" (Results)
- There is no evidence of a [CII]-deficit within the ALPINE sample; the galaxies that deviate most from the L_[CII]-SFR relation suffer from less precise UV spectroscopic redshift estimates.  
  > "Finally, we show that the galaxies of our sample that most deviate from the observed L$_\mathrm{[CII]}$-SFR relation could suffer from a less precise redshift estimation, perhaps artificially reducing their [CII] luminosity. In this respect, we claim that there is no evidence in favour of a deficit of [CII] content in high-z galaxies" (abstract); "In this scenario, we claim that there is no evidence of [CII]-deficit within the ALPINE sample, as also suggested by (cite: Schaerer20)." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The ALPINE survey provides the first statistically significant sample (118 SFGs, with 75 detections and 43 non-detections) of normal main-sequence galaxies at 4.4 < z < 5.9 with confirmed UV spectroscopic redshifts and rich multi-wavelength data.  
  > "the ALMA Large Program to INvestigate [CII] at Early times (ALPINE) survey (cite: Bethermin20; Faisst20; LeFevre20) has recently provided the first statistically significant sample of high-redshift normal galaxies" (Introduction); "However, to fully establish the connection between $\mathrm{L_{[CII]}}$ and SFR in distant galaxies a more in depth investigation of the ALPINE non-detections is needed." (Introduction)
- Why this method: 1D spectral stacking in the image plane was chosen over uv-plane or 2D/3D stacking because the exact systemic frequency and FWHM of individual non-detections are unknown due to Ly-alpha velocity offsets, and literature tests show image and uv stacking yield similar results; a 1'' radius aperture overcomes typical 0.15'' UV-[CII] astrometric offsets.  
  > "In the analysis of the ALPINE [CII] undetected galaxies, we do not know at which frequency the expected line is (because of the observed velocity offset between Ly$\alpha$ and [CII]), and we do not have any information on the FWHM of each source. For these reasons, and considering that both image and uv planes provide similar results, we decide to proceed with a 1D spectral stacking of the [CII] non-detections starting from the image plane." (Stacking of non-detections); "We overcome this problem by considering a fixed aperture of 1” of radius to extract the ALMA spectra of the [CII] non-detections" (Discussion)
- Alternative: Stacking in the uv plane or 2D/3D image-plane stacking (rejected because systemic redshifts and FWHMs of individual non-detections are unknown, and 1D image-plane stacking yields comparable results).  
  > "For these reasons, and considering that both image and uv planes provide similar results, we decide to proceed with a 1D spectral stacking of the [CII] non-detections starting from the image plane." (Stacking of non-detections)
- Alternative: Alternative stacking weights: unweighted (w = 1), inverse-variance of each stacked realization (w = 1/sigma_rms^2), and weighting individual spectra by their observed rms (tested; yielded similar profiles and S/N, with individual rms weighting giving ~0.1 dex lower L_[CII]; w = S/N was adopted for final presentation).  
  > "We assume $w=1$ in case of no weighting, $w=1/\sigma_{rms}^2$ for an inverse-variance weighting (where $\sigma_{rms}$ is the standard deviation of each stacked spectrum), and $w=\mathrm{S/N}$ to weight each stacked spectrum by its S/N." (Stacking of non-detections); "We check that the integrated flux and S/N of the final average stacked line do not change significantly if we weight the individual spectra by their observed rms in each realization." (Stacking of non-detections)
- Alternative: Using a fixed mean Ly-alpha to [CII] velocity offset (~183 km s^-1) or removing the FWHM <= 400 km s^-1 constraint (tested as checks; gave comparable integrated flux and luminosity).  
  > "As an additional check, we re-do the entire stacking procedure by considering, for each source, the mean value of the Ly$\alpha$-[CII] offset distribution found by (cite: Cassata20) (i.e., $\sim183~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}}$)" (Stacking of non-detections); "We test that this result is not significantly different from the one obtained by removing the constraint on the FWHM of the stacked spectra, which thus affects only the width of the line." (Stacking of non-detections)
- How they decided it was enough: 1000 Monte Carlo realizations satisfying FWHM <= 400 km s^-1 for the stack, and 500 delete-d jackknife realizations removing 20% of the sample as a compromise between error estimation and maintaining a reasonable sample size per stack.  
  > "For each stacked spectrum, we then require that $\mathrm{FWHM}\leq400$ km s$^{-1}$, which defines the 84th percentile of the observed FWHM distribution of the ALPINE [CII] detections (see (cite: Bethermin20)), until reaching 1000 realizations." (Stacking of non-detections); "This percentage represents a good compromise in using the jackknife method to estimate the error on our measurements while still having a reasonable number of sources to stack at each time (cite: Efron82)." (Stacking of non-detections)
- Limitation (authors): Systemic redshifts are unknown for the [CII] non-detections, requiring reliance on Ly-alpha redshifts and statistical velocity offset corrections that artificially broaden the stacked line FWHM by a factor of ~2.  
  > "We do not have the systemic redshift information for the 43 ALPINE non-detections, therefore we can just rely on their UV redshifts." (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data); "We find that the random extraction method produces a $\sim2$ times broader FWHM than the true value computed by stacking the spectra at their correct wavelengths." (Stacking of non-detections)
- Limitation (authors): 9 out of 43 non-detections had to be excluded from the stack and fit due to weak/no Ly-alpha emission and less precise UV spectroscopic redshifts.  
  > "Although it is likely that the redshift is generally accurate for these sources as well, we decide to exclude them from our stacking analysis, in order not to include additional uncertainties due to possible strong [CII] offsets" (Rest-frame UV spectroscopic data)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size causes statistical continuum fluctuations on either side of the stacked line and no significant detection in the low-SFR bin (log(SFR/M_sun yr^-1) < 1.16).  
  > "We do not find any significant emission in the low-SFR bin." (Stacking of non-detections); "However, we believe that this issue is only due to statistical fluctuations originated from the small size of the sample, rather than to calibration problems in the ALMA data reductions or physical processes in these sources." (Stacking of non-detections)
- Limitation (authors): None of the 34 [CII] non-detections is detected in FIR continuum, so their SFR(IR) relies entirely on the average IRX-beta relation prediction.  
  > "It is worth to specify that none of our 34 [CII] non-detections is detected in continuum. Therefore, the SFR(IR) for all of these galaxies comes from the IRX-$\beta$ relation by (cite: Fudamoto20)." (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Post-filtering the 1000 Monte Carlo random velocity-offset realizations by requiring FWHM <= 400 km s^-1 and weighting realizations by their resulting S/N could introduce a selection/confirmation bias toward realizations where noise peaks constructively align, although the authors test removing the FWHM threshold and using constant offsets.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the linear regression with linmix, combining 75 individual galaxy detections with a single stacked data point representing 34 galaxies gives the single stacked point the statistical weight of only one object unless weighted by sample size (which is not stated).
