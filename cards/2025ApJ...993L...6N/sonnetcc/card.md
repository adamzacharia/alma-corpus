## Analysis recipe

1. **measure ALMA continuum flux densities**: Downloaded archive pipeline images and estimated the source flux density with CASA  
  > "We downloaded the pipeline generated images from ALMA archive and estimated the flux density of the source using Common Astronomy Software Applications" (3.1 ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Band 3 (97.5 GHz) and Band 5 (203 GHz) at two epochs; 1 sigma uncertainties  
  > "The uncertainties on flux measurements are 1" (Table: ALMA observations of AT 2024wpp)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **fit single-epoch radio SEDs**: Smoothly broken power law with free F_pk and nu_pk; optically thin slope fixed at -1.5 and smoothness s=-1; optically thick slope free at 32 and 46 d, else fixed to nearest-epoch best fit  
  > "We model the single epoch SEDs of AT 2024wpp as a broken power-law of the form:" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters); "to the best fit values obtained in the nearest epoch SED." (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters)
   - assumption: Optically thin index -1.5 chosen from the 73 d index and cooling-frequency position  
  > "based on the optically thin spectral index at" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **derive shock radius, velocity, magnetic field, energy and density from equipartition**: Chevalier (1998) equipartition formulas; Ho et al. (2022) formulas where cooling frequency lies below the SED peak (17.5 and 32.4 d)  
  > "Following (cite: chevalier1998), we calculate the shock radius" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters); "which implies that (cite: chevalier1998) is not self-consistent at these epochs" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters)
   - parameter: Internal energy U computed from R, B, epsilon_B and filling factor  
  > "The shock internal energy" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters)
   - assumption: Equipartition (f_eB = 1); p = 3  
  > "We assume equipartition of energy" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters); "for synchrotron spectral modeling and parameter estimation" (5.1 General Considerations)
   - assumption: Volume filling factor f = 0.5  
  > "is the volume filling factor of the synchrotron emitting region" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked nu_m, nu_c,sync and nu_c,IC against nu_pk at each epoch to test model validity  
  > "at different epochs to check the validity of this model" (5.2 Spectral modeling and shock parameters)
4. **test whether electron cooling suppresses the early mm flux**: Extrapolate the 46 d SED back to 17.5 and 32.4 d and compute synchrotron and inverse-Compton cooling Lorentz factors  
  > "We explore the possibility of flux suppression via electron cooling to account for this." (6.1 Energy losses of synchrotron emitting electrons)
   - assumption: rho_CSM proportional to r^-3 and constant shock velocity 0.24c for the extrapolation  
  > "assuming a density profile" (6.1 Energy losses of synchrotron emitting electrons)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cooling predicted to lower the 100 and 200 GHz fluxes at 17-32 d, opposite to the observed rise  
  > "Thus, cooling effects cannot account for the observed sharp rise in millimeter fluxes." (6.1 Energy losses of synchrotron emitting electrons)
5. **test an off-axis relativistic jet explanation**: Top-hat jet deceleration radius and peak time (Beniamini et al. 2023) plus generalized equipartition analysis (Matsumoto and Piran 2023)  
  > "Consider a top-hat jet in which" (6.2 Off-axis models)
   - parameter: Viewing angles 30, 45, 60, 90 degrees  
  > "We consider four different observer viewing angles" (6.2 Off-axis models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted peak times far earlier than the observed mm peak; required Lorentz factors too high  
  > "This argues against the off-axis jet model for the observed increase in the millimeter emission" (6.2 Off-axis models)
6. **model the mm rise as free-free absorption by a dense CSM shell**: Use the flux ratio at 97.5 GHz between 17 and 32 d to get tau_FFA, then infer shell density and mass from the free-free opacity  
  > "If the mm-flux rise is due to the different optical depths" (6.3 Radio emitting outflow propagating through a dense CSM)
   - parameter: R_2 set to the equipartition radius at 32 d; R_1 lower limit from the optical photosphere at 17 d  
  > "Assuming the shock emergence to be happening at the edge of the dense shell" (6.3 Radio emitting outflow propagating through a dense CSM)
   - assumption: Constant density between R_1 and R_2  
  > "assuming constant density between" (6.3 Radio emitting outflow propagating through a dense CSM)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative consistency with AT 2018cow shell and with NIR-excess density from Paper I  
  > "A similar physical scenario was invoked to explain the early bright millimeter emission from AT 2018cow" (6.3 Radio emitting outflow propagating through a dense CSM)
7. **test a thermal plus non-thermal electron synchrotron model**: Fit SEDs at 32, 46 and 73 d with the Margalit and Quataert (2021) model and MCMC  
  > "and explore the parameter space using MCMC" (5.3 Synchrotron emission from thermal electrons)
   - parameter: Free: number density, shock radius, thermalization efficiency; fixed: epsilon_B = 0.1 and delta in 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1  
  > "We keep the number density, shock radius, and the electron thermalization efficiency" (5.3 Synchrotron emission from thermal electrons)
   - fitting: MCMC; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "and explore the parameter space using MCMC" (5.3 Synchrotron emission from thermal electrons)
   - validation: Model failed at 32 and 46 d, acceptable at 73 d; not needed to explain the SEDs  
  > "The model failed to reproduce the SEDs at" (5.3 Synchrotron emission from thermal electrons)
8. **derive time evolution of the shock and the CSM density profile**: Power-law fits to F_pk, nu_pk and R versus time over three phases, then CSM density versus radius compared with other LFBOTs  
  > "The corresponding evolution of the peak frequency is" (6 The origin and evolution of the radio emitting outflow); "The CSM density profile derived from later" (6.3 Radio emitting outflow propagating through a dense CSM)
   - assumption: Effective mass-loss rate computed for an arbitrary wind velocity of 1000 km/s  
  > "for an arbitrary wind velocity of" (8.1 A universal CSM density profile in LFBOTs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **fit broad-band X-ray spectra (non-ALMA part)**: Joint soft (Swift-XRT, XMM or CXO) and hard (NuSTAR) fits with absorbed simple and broken power laws in Xspec  
  > "We jointly fit the epochs with soft" (4.1 Joint soft and hard X-ray spectral modeling and X-ray flux calibration)
   - software: Xspec
   - parameter: W-statistics (and Cash statistics as a cross-check)  
  > "As a second approach, we use the W-statistics after having ensured that every bin in the background spectrum contains enough counts." (4.1 Joint soft and hard X-ray spectral modeling and X-ray flux calibration)
   - assumption: Solar abundances, tbabs, Galactic NH_MW = 2.6e20 cm^-2, no intrinsic absorption  
  > "We adopt Solar abundances" (4.1 Joint soft and hard X-ray spectral modeling and X-ray flux calibration)
   - fitting: Uncertainties derived with MCMC simulations  
  > "Uncertainties are self-consistently derived with MCMC simulations." (4.1 Joint soft and hard X-ray spectral modeling and X-ray flux calibration)
   - validation: Cash and W-statistic approaches consistent  
  > "We find that the two methods lead to statistically consistent results" (4.1 Joint soft and hard X-ray spectral modeling and X-ray flux calibration)

## Qualitative findings

- The mm flux rises about an order of magnitude between 17 and 32 d, unprecedented among LFBOTs  
  > "The evolution of millimeter band flux densities during" (7.1 LFBOTs at Radio and millimeter wavelengths)
- Radio spectral inversion at 5-9 GHz at 133 and 161 d may signal a new emission component  
  > "This kind of spectral inversion is unprecedented in FBOTs" (5.1 General Considerations)
- AT 2024wpp is the fourth millimeter-bright FBOT  
  > "marking AT 2024wpp as the fourth millimeter-bright FBOT" (9 Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Only a few LFBOTs have detailed X-ray and radio follow-up, so more events are needed  
  > "This highlights the need for detailed X-ray and radio observations of additional FBOTs." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Off-axis relativistic jet for the mm rise: disfavored  
  > "This argues against the off-axis jet model for the observed increase in the millimeter emission" (6.2 Off-axis models)
- Alternative: Electron cooling suppression of early mm flux: rejected  
  > "Thus, cooling effects cannot account for the observed sharp rise in millimeter fluxes." (6.1 Energy losses of synchrotron emitting electrons)
- Alternative: Thermal-electron synchrotron model: not necessary  
  > "we conclude that it is not necessary to invoke this model to interpret the observed SEDs" (5.3 Synchrotron emission from thermal electrons)
- Alternative: CSM-interaction-only progenitor models: ruled out  
  > "hence, CSM-interaction-only models can be ruled out" (8 Plausible Physical Models)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Equipartition-based energy is a lower limit  
  > "is a lower limit due to the equipartition assumption" (6.2 Off-axis models)
- Limitation (authors): Parameters at dt_rest ~ 17.5 d are not physical because the SED is free-free absorbed  
  > "are not physical as the SED is free-free absorbed due to the surrounding medium up to radius" (Table: Shock parameters of AT 2024wpp)
- Limitation (authors): Cooling estimates depend strongly on assumed CSM density profile and shock velocity  
  > "We caution that the above estimates based on cooling timescales depends strongly on the CSM density profile and shock velocities" (6.1 Energy losses of synchrotron emitting electrons)
- Limitation (authors): Small LFBOT sample  
  > "even though the sample is limited" (7.1 LFBOTs at Radio and millimeter wavelengths)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two ALMA epochs; the key mm rise rests on four flux densities with no stated image rms, beam, CASA version or flux-scale systematic
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Equipartition parameters at 17.5 d are admitted unphysical and the cooling-frequency regime changes between epochs, so velocity acceleration from 0.07c to 0.42c depends on mixed formulas (Chevalier vs Ho et al.)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Equipartition fraction quoted as 0.3 in text and 0.33 in table notes; filling factor and p=3 are assumed, not fitted
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SED fitting method and uncertainty derivation not described; peak parameters at 17.5 d are poorly constrained (two frequencies)
