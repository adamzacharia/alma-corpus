## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: Is the C2H emission (a C/O tracer) in protoplanetary disks related to the location of icy pebbles, i.e. to a CO-ice-covered dust reservoir outside the CO snowline set by dust rings/pressure bumps?  
  > "We combine all disks with spatially resolved ALMA C$_2$H observations with high-resolution continuum images and constraints on the CO snowline to determine if the C$_2$H emission is indeed related to the location of the icy pebbles." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Image archival Band 6 continuum of J1614-1906 and J1609-1908**: Calibrate with provided scripts in CASA 5.1.1 and image the three continuum windows with tclean using uniform weighting; measure integrated flux and radial extent with uvmodelfit  
  > "The dataset is reduced using CASA version 5.1.1 with the provided calibration scripts" (Appendix: ALMA archival continuum data 2017.1.01167.S); "Both disks are marginally resolved and the CASA" (Appendix: ALMA archival continuum data 2017.1.01167.S)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvmodelfit
   - parameter: uniform weighting; beam 0.22x0.20; rms 0.08 mJy/beam; average frequency 240 GHz, 6 GHz bandwidth  
  > "resulting in a beam size of 0.22$\times$0.20 and a rms noise of 0.08 mJy beam$^{-1}$" (Appendix: ALMA archival continuum data 2017.1.01167.S)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive 3 sigma upper limit on IRS 48 C2H (349.4 GHz)**: From the reported rms per channel, channel width and line width, compute a 3 sigma upper limit on the C2H flux density, then scale to T_b,peak at 100 pc  
  > "we compute a 3$\sigma$ upper limit on the C$_2$H flux density of 11 mJy bm$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$, or a scaled $T_{b,peak,100pc}$ limit of $<$1.1 K km s$^{-1}$" (Appendix: Data reduction IRS 48 C2H data)
   - parameter: rms 1.2 mJy/beam/channel, channel width 1.6 km/s, line width ~14 km/s, beam 0.63x0.50 arcsec  
  > "a line width of $\sim$14 km s$^{-1}$ and a beam size of 0.63$\times$0.50"" (Appendix: Data reduction IRS 48 C2H data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Line visually not detected on inspection of the dataset  
  > "Inspection of the dataset reveals that this C$_2$H line is not detected." (Appendix: Data reduction IRS 48 C2H data)
3. **Measure the C2H peak brightness from azimuthally averaged radial profiles**: Use the azimuthally averaged radial profile of the integrated (blended-doublet) C2H emission and take its peak value, or the 3 sigma upper limit for non-detections; profiles provided by earlier papers or computed from zero-moment maps  
  > "we use the azimuthally averaged radial profiles of the integrated C$_2$H emission, and use the peak value as our main measurement of the C$_2$H brightness" (4 Analysis); "or the 3$\sigma$ upper limit of the integrated emission in case of a non-detection" (4 Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Normalise C2H peak brightness across the sample**: Convert the integrated peak flux density to brightness temperature and scale to a distance of 100 pc  
  > "the integrated peak flux density is converted to the brightness temperature and scaled to a distance of 100 pc" (4 Analysis)
   - parameter: reference distance 100 pc  
  > "scaled to a distance of 100 pc" (4 Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Locate the CO snowline radius**: Solve the passively heated flared-disk midplane temperature T(r) = (phi L*/(8 pi sigma_B r^2))^(1/4) for the radius where T = 30 K and 22 K  
  > "we can deduce the radius $r_{\rm CO}$ where $T$=30/22 K in the midplane" (Icy dust mass calculation)
   - parameter: flaring angle phi = 0.02; stellar luminosity L*; snowline temperatures 22 K and 30 K  
  > "with $\sigma_B$ the Stefan-Boltzmann constant, $\phi$ the flaring angle (taken as 0.02) and $L_*$ the stellar luminosity" (Icy dust mass calculation); "we will adopt the 22 K and 30 K temperatures as the two extremes of the CO snowline location" (Icy dust mass calculation)
   - assumption: Passively heated, flared disk in radiative equilibrium; 22 K for pure CO ice, 30 K for CO mixed in H2O ice  
  > "is expected to be around 22 K for pure CO ice (cite: Bisschop2006) or as high as 30 K for CO mixed in H$_2$O ice (cite: Noble2012)" (Icy dust mass calculation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with directly measured snowlines where available; measured snowlines lie further in for some disks and further out for transition disks  
  > "The CO snowline as determined by a temperature profile is a reasonable approximation" (5 Results)
6. **Compute dust mass outside the CO snowline (icy dust mass)**: Integrate continuum flux density over all pixels above 3 sigma outside r_CO, then convert to mass with M_dust = F d^2 / (kappa_nu B_nu(T_dust))  
  > "The continuum flux is computed by integrating the flux density over all pixels$>3\sigma$ outside $r_{\rm CO}$, with $\sigma$ the rms noise." (Icy dust mass calculation)
   - parameter: T_dust = 20 K; evaluated for r_CO at 22 K and 30 K  
  > "the standard assumptions of isothermal, optically thin emission of 20 K" (Icy dust mass calculation)
   - assumption: Isothermal, optically thin dust emission at 20 K; kappa_nu value not stated  
  > "the standard assumptions of isothermal, optically thin emission of 20 K" (Icy dust mass calculation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Masses for the lower-resolution J1609-1908, J1612-1859, J1614-1906 flagged as upper limits due to beam dilution  
  > "Due to the lower spatial resolution of J1609-1908, J1612-1859 and J1614-1906, these icy dust masses should be considered upper limits." (Icy dust mass calculation)
7. **Test whether C2H detections and non-detections differ in icy dust mass and C2H peak**: Kaplan-Meier estimator and Gehan's Wilcoxon two-sample test with ASURV (upper limits treated), plus Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests against L*, M_dust and R68 to check for confounding correlations  
  > "For the scaled C$_2$H peak fluxes we compute a two sample test using ASURV (to properly treat the upper limits of the C$_2$H data points)." (5 Results); "we perform a Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic test for stellar luminosity $L_*$ and total dust mass $M_{\rm dust}$" (5 Results)
   - software: ASURV
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: KS tests on L*, M_dust and R68 used to check the result is not driven by another parameter  
  > "In order to check that this is not caused by an underlying correlation with another parameter" (5 Results)

## Qualitative findings

- All C2H non-detections have little or no dust emission outside the CO snowline, whereas all C2H detections have a significant cold dust reservoir  
  > "The disks with a C$_2$H non-detection have little or no dust emission outside the CO snowline" (5 Results); "whereas all C$_2$H detections still have a significant cold dust reservoir" (5 Results)
- 4 of 6 C2H non-detections are compact continuum disks without substructure; the exceptions are IRS 48 and J16070384  
  > "4 out of 6 of these disks are compact in continuum emission and do not show any signs of substructure" (6 Discussion and conclusions)
- Scaled C2H peak brightness shows no obvious bimodal distribution, unlike the integrated flux  
  > "there is no obvious bimodal distribution as seen in the integrated flux" (5 Results)
- Transition disks other than IRS 48 have dust rings outside the CO snowline, so behave like ring disks  
  > "the transition disks in this sample have their dust rings outside the CO snowline" (6 Discussion and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sample is every disk with deep, spatially resolved C2H observations from the literature, combined with the highest-resolution continuum images  
  > "Our sample consists of all protoplanetary disks with deep, spatially resolved C$_2$H observations from the literature" (2 Observations)
- Why this method: Radial profiles are used because disks differ in size and integrated flux may not be representative; 22 K and 30 K adopted as extreme CO snowline temperatures  
  > "the C$_2$H emission needs to be normalized spatially across the sample, as the sample consists of disks of a range of sizes" (4 Analysis); "Therefore, we will adopt the 22 K and 30 K temperatures as the two extremes of the CO snowline location." (Icy dust mass calculation)
- Alternative: For DM Tau two published C2H datasets exist; the Bergner et al. 2019 data were chosen over Bergin et al. 2016  
  > "For DM Tau, two published datasets exist (cite: Bergin2016; Bergner2019) and we choose to include the latter." (2 Observations)
- Alternative: Snowline tracers N2H+ and DCO+ not used to set snowlines because of complex emission; a temperature profile adopted instead  
  > "detailed 2D models have shown that their emission is more complex than a simple anti-correlation with CO" (Icy dust mass calculation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Beam dilution may affect lower resolution images so some icy dust masses may be upper limits  
  > "Beam dilution is possibly affecting the estimates for the lower resolution images so some of these values may be upper limits." (5 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass outside the CO snowline may be underestimated for ring disks and overestimated for transition disks, because CO snowline measurements are lacking for most disks  
  > "with the caveat that the dust mass may be somewhat underestimated for the ring disks and overestimated for the transition disks" (5 Results)
- Limitation (authors): Sample of C2H observations not well constructed to fully test the scenario; targeted high-resolution observations needed  
  > "The current sample of available C$_2$H observations is not well-constructed to fully test our proposed scenario." (6 Discussion and conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Centrally peaked C2H profiles may result from beam confusion  
  > "may just be the result of beam confusion and do not prove the presence of C$_2$H inside the CO snowline" (Appendix: C2H profiles)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heterogeneous C2H data (different lines, resolutions 0.2-0.8 arcsec, sensitivities) are normalised only via T_b scaled to 100 pc; beam and line-blending effects are not quantified
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small sample (26 disks, 6 non-detections) and several non-detections are compact or faint disks, confounding icy dust mass with disk size
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Icy dust mass relies on a single flared-disk temperature law with fixed flaring angle 0.02 and an unstated dust opacity
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlation, not causation: dust size (R68) KS probability 3.6% means an alternative explanation cannot be excluded
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No error bars on individual icy dust masses or scaled peak T_b; the 22-30 K range is the only uncertainty
