## Qualitative findings

- E-vectors are well organized in regions A and B (northern outflow and waist) and the inferred B-vectors form an hourglass centred on the central star  
  > "The ALMA data show well organized electric field orientations in most of the nebula" (abstract)
- Region C in the south shows a less organized vector distribution, possibly due to the shocked environment  
  > "One region in the southern part of OH 231.8+4.2 shows a less organized distribution probably due to the shocked environment." (abstract)
- The peak fractional polarization decreases from the outer to the central regions  
  > "we note that the peak fractional polarization decreases from the outer to the central regions" (3 ALMA dust polarization analysis)
- Combined with maser and dust results, the data suggest an overall magnetic hourglass inside a toroidal field, resembling a magnetic tower  
  > "suggest an overall magnetic hourglass located inside a toroidal field" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High resolution ALMA polarimetry probes the inner dusty region closer to the launch site and completes a multi-scale analysis of the field  
  > "probe the magnetic engine (assuming a dynamo mechanism) closer to its launch-site and complete a multi-scale analysis of the magnetic field" (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Radiative (radiation anisotropy) grain alignment rejected as the main mechanism  
  > "So we disregard radiative alignment as the main polarization process operating in OH 231.8+4.2." (4 Multi-scale dust polarization analysis)
- Alternative: Dust self-scattering rejected as the main mechanism  
  > "it is very unlikely that self-scattering is a main element in the polarization seen in the nebula" (4 Multi-scale dust polarization analysis)
- Alternative: Line polarization contamination of the earlier SMA maps was excluded  
  > "conducted independent ALMA data re-reductions" (4.1 Poloidal or hourglass magnetic distribution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stokes I dynamic range only about 300; flux not fully recovered and structure larger than the MRS of 3.9 arcsec is resolved out  
  > "indicating that all the flux is not recovered" (2 Observations and data reduction); "we have resolved out all the smooth structures larger than this size" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): The reason for structures seen in the SMA map but absent in the ALMA map is unclear  
  > "The reason for the absence of structures seen with the SMA (at lower resolution) in the ALMA map (having a higher resolution) is not clear." (4.1 Poloidal or hourglass magnetic distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Polarization process in the inner region is unclear  
  > "The polarization process occurring in the inner region is less clear though." (4 Multi-scale dust polarization analysis)
- Limitation (authors): The magnetic field strength at the stellar surface is not measured; the only value is an extrapolation from a single maser  
  > "it cannot be fully established as representative of the stellar surface" (5.1 Overall magnetic topology)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No quantitative test of grain alignment mechanisms: radiative alignment and self-scattering are dismissed by qualitative pattern comparison with disc models, not with models of this nebula.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Regions A, B, B1-B3 and C are drawn by eye and the mean PAs depend on this choice and on the 3 sigma polarized-intensity cut, with no debiasing stated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing short spacings (MRS 3.9 arcsec, dynamic range about 300) may bias the polarization fraction and the comparison with SMA and CARMA, which are sensitive to larger scales.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Tower-like interpretation relies on a qualitative sketch rather than a model fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Instrumental polarization calibration is described in one sentence, so spurious polarization in the faint outer regions cannot be assessed.
