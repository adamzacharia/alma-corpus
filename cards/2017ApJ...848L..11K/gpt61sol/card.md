## Qualitative findings

- Continuum emission contains an unresolved central peak, an asymmetric ring, a crescent split into C1/C2, and extended emission.  
  > "an unresolved, central emission peak near the location of the star," (Continuum imaging); "strong excess flux in the south-eastern direction (R1) and weaker excess in the north-western direction (R2)" (Continuum imaging); "but break down into two components (C1, C2)" (Continuum imaging)
- CO rotation has a blue-shifted North-West lobe and red-shifted South-East lobe.  
  > "show a rotation profile with the blue-shifted lobe to the North-West and the red-shifted lobe South-East of the star." (Line imaging)
- CO excess occurs near the crescent and south-east near R1; H12CO+ also shows a south-eastern excess near R1.  
  > "Interestingly, we see some excess of CO emission near the location of the crescent structure (C1-C2) and in the south-eastern part of the disk near feature R1 outside of the ring structure." (Line imaging); "As with CO, we see some excess emission in the south-eastern part of the disk near feature R1." (Line imaging)
- A dust-trapping vortex is the preferred explanation for the crescent; R1 might be another vortex.  
  > "However, we think that a dust-trapping vortex provides the most likely explanation for the observed co-radial structure of the crescent, its high azimuthal contrast and azimuthal extend." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps); "This leads us to suggest that the asymmetry R1 might also represent a vortex that formed at the inner edge of the gap." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps)
- The proposed protoplanet and its predicted circumplanetary sub-mm continuum emission are not detected.  
  > "This sub-mm flux is not detected" (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation); "The protoplanet itself has not been discovered so far, neither in SPHERE H$\alpha$ spectral differential imaging (cite: wil17) nor in our ALMA imaging, possibly due to confusion with the bright ring structure." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution resolves dust-trap substructure and permits vortex-elongation measurements.  
  > "The superb 0.04$''$-resolution of our ALMA image reveals intriguing substructure in the dust trap and provides for the first time the opportunity to confront competing theories of vortex formation with detailed observational evidence." (Conclusions); "which is an important quantity for comparison with theoretical models." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps)
- Why this method: Geometric modelling quantifies continuum components; dedicated hydrodynamics tests whether a spiral wake can produce the scattered-light signature.  
  > "In order we quantify these components we fitted a geometric model consisting of the following components" (Continuum imaging); "We conducted dedicated smooth particle hydrodynamics simulations with the specific parameters of V1247 Ori in order to test whether the scale-height variation of a planet-induced spiral wake could result in the detected scattered-light signature." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation)
- Alternative: Coplanar planet-induced spiral interpretation of the crescent is disfavoured by its pitch angle and co-radial morphology.  
  > "shows that the crescent does not follow the pitch angle of the spiral-arm model and seems to be co-radial, instead." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps)
- Alternative: A spiral misaligned by 17° gives a better crescent shape match and might explain the C1/C2 brightness drop through shadowing.  
  > "where we adopted a misalignment angle of $17^{\circ}$." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps); "The projection of the inclined spiral provides a better fit to the shape of the crescent, with a lower pitch angle than for a co-planar spiral." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps); "the shadow casted by the geometrically-thin, optically-thick inner disk might lower the temperature at this location in the spiral, potentially explaining the observed sub-millimeter brightness drop." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps)
- Alternative: An inner planet at ≲30 au could produce a dissipating primary vortex and an outer second-generation vortex; relative scattered-light/ALMA positions favour direct planet triggering instead.  
  > "we also considered an alternative scenario, where a planet orbits at $\lesssim30$ au and clears the region inside the ALMA ring." (Planet-triggered dust traps and a spiral wake); "The planet generates a primary vortex (which dissipates and evolves into the ring observed in the ALMA image) and triggers the formation of a second-generation vortex further out, corresponding to the ALMA crescent." (Planet-triggered dust traps and a spiral wake); "This is not the case for V1247 Ori, which is more consistent with the hypothesis that both are directly triggered by a planet." (Planet-triggered dust traps and a spiral wake)
- How they decided it was enough: Choose the lowest-RMS reconstruction; take simulation snapshots after quasi-steady state at 10 orbits; restrict model comparison to qualitative morphology.  
  > "We computed a grid with different reconstruction parameters and weighting schemes and selected the image with the lowest root-mean-square noise" (Observations); "Snapshots were taken once the simulation reached a quasi-steady state after 10 orbits (at the planet location)." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation); "Given the large number of free parameters involved, it is also out of the scope of this paper to model the images quantitatively." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation)
- Limitation (authors): Line data have relatively low signal-to-noise and permit only basic conclusions.  
  > "Despite the relatively low signal-to-noise, we are able to draw the following basic conclusions:" (Line imaging)
- Limitation (authors): Fixed gas-to-dust ratio and high viscosity make the simulation unsuitable for vortex formation itself.  
  > "Our simulation adopts a fixed gas-to-dust ratio and a relatively high viscosity ($\alpha\approx0.01$) and is therefore not suited to simulate the vortex formation itself, which will require further dedicated theoretical work." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation)
- Limitation (authors): Images are not modelled quantitatively because of the large number of free parameters.  
  > "Given the large number of free parameters involved, it is also out of the scope of this paper to model the images quantitatively." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation)
- Limitation (authors): Published stable-vortex aspect ratios refer to gas and cannot be compared directly with the observed values.  
  > "(cite: les09) predicted a stable regime for vortices with aspect-ratios between 4 and 5.9, although these numbers were computed for the gas and are thus not suitable for direct comparison with our observed values." (The crescent structure and ring asymmetry as dust traps)
- Limitation (authors): Quantitative circumplanetary constraints require simulations with decoupled gas/dust and grain growth; nearby R1 may confuse planet emission.  
  > "Sophisticated simulations with decoupled gas+dust populations and grain growth will be required to derive quantitative constraints." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation); "Also, the planet emission might be confused with the bright emission from the nearby ring feature R1." (Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulation)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): The simulation tests spiral visibility rather than formation of either proposed vortex.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3 MJup simulation planet is an assumed input, not a measured planet mass.

- Caveat (inferred, ours): The weak central component is labelled 2σ in Table 1; its quoted flux error alone should not be treated as its detection significance.
