## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image band 7 data**: Standard CASA calibration; CLEAN with natural weighting, 500 iterations, no mask; uv-plane continuum subtraction  
  > "The calibration of the data was done with CASA (McMullin et al. 2007; Petry & the CASA Development Team 2012) in standard manners." (2 Observation and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: pixel 0.3 arcsec; 20 channel binning to ~8.5 km/s  
  > "the pixel scale of all maps is set to 0$''$.3/pix" (2 Observation and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Astrometry checked via coincidence of 860 um peak with 6 cm peak  
  > "The precise match between 6 cm and 860 $\mu$m continuum peaks supports the accuracy of the astrometry of ALMA" (3 Continuum map)
2. **Measure continuum position and size**: 2D Gaussian fit to nuclear continuum using >5 sigma emission  
  > "We fit a 2-dimensional Gaussian to the nuclear 860 $\mu$m continuum source using only $>$ 5 $\sigma$ emission to derive the intrinsic size of the nucleus using the MIRIAD task `IMFIT`." (3 Continuum map)
   - software: MIRIAD IMFIT
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian  
  > "We fit a 2-dimensional Gaussian to the nuclear 860 $\mu$m continuum source using only $>$ 5 $\sigma$ emission to derive the intrinsic size of the nucleus using the MIRIAD task `IMFIT`." (Continuum map)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Make moment maps and extract line fluxes**: Zeroth moment using channels >3 sigma; first and second moments with 5 sigma clipping; integrated intensities from moment-0 at continuum peak; upper limits assuming Gaussian with FWHM like HCN  
  > "we computed these moment maps by using channels which contain $>$ 3 $\sigma$ emission only" (4 Channel maps and integrated intensities); "Upper limits to the integrated intensities were derived for these undetected transitions assuming a Gaussian profile with FWHM similar to the $\Delta$$v$ derived for HCN ($J$ = 4–3)." (5 Band 7 spectra)
   - software: MIRIAD MOMENT
   - parameter: 1 sigma = 2.27 mJy/beam (HCN), 2.39 mJy/beam (HCO+)  
  > "where 1 $\sigma$ = 2.27 mJy beam$^{-1}$ for HCN ($J$ = 4–3) and 2.39 mJy beam$^{-1}$ for HCO$^+$ ($J$ = 4–3)" (4 Channel maps and integrated intensities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate dynamical mass**: Keplerian thin-disk M_dyn = r v^2/G (sin i)^-2 at r~40 pc  
  > "Assuming a thin disk with Keplerian rotation, the dynamical mass within a radius $r$ is calculated as" (6 Dense molecular gas kinematics)
   - parameter: r ~ 40 pc; i = 35 deg  
  > "we set the radius to $r$ $\sim$ 40 pc, and we adopt an inclination angle of 35$^\circ$ (Fathi et al. 2006)" (6 Dense molecular gas kinematics)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation, thin disk; lower limit due to beam smearing  
  > "the dynamical mass estimated here is just an lower limit because the rotation velocity may be underestimated due to the beam smearing effect" (6 Dense molecular gas kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with previous dynamical masses (Davies 2007, Hicks 2009)  
  > "This value is within a factor of a few of previous measurements of the dynamical mass of 1.4 $\times$ 10$^8$ $M_{\rm \odot}$" (6 Dense molecular gas kinematics)
5. **LTE rotation diagram analysis**: Optically thin LTE excitation diagrams of HCN and HCO+ with J=1-0, 3-2, 4-3 convolved to common beam; single and two-component fits  
  > "we estimate the rotational temperatures ($T_{\rm{rot}}$) and column densities ($N_{\rm{mol}}$) of HCN and HCO$^+$ molecules in the nuclear region via excitation diagrams (rotation diagrams)." (8.1 Column density under LTE)
   - parameter: common beam 4.4 x 2.7 arcsec, P.A. -14.6 deg  
  > "We convolved all maps to 4$''$.4 $\times$ 2$''$.7 with P.A. = -14.6$^\circ$" (8.1 Column density under LTE)
   - assumption: Optically thin, LTE; T_rot lower limit to T_kin  
  > "the derived $T_{\rm{rot}}$ is thus only a lower limit to the kinetic temperature" (8.1 Column density under LTE)
   - fitting: linear fit of log(N_u/g_u) vs E_u  
  > "Therefore, the decimal logarithm of $N_u/g_u$ versus $E_u/k_{\rm B}$ will yield a straight line with a slope of $-\log{e}/T_{\rm{ex}}$." (Column density under LTE)
   - validation: Compared to ring, NGC 253, NGC 1068  
  > "we also made excitation diagrams of HCN and HCO$^+$ in one position in the circumnuclear starburst ring of NGC 1097" (8.1 Column density under LTE)
6. **Non-LTE RADEX chi2 grid**: RADEX grid in T_kin, n_H2, N_HCO+/dV, [HCN]/[HCO+]; chi2 on line-ratio constraints; final use of high-J ratios R43/43 and R32/32  
  > "we ran RADEX for each ($N_{\rm {HCO^+}}$/$dV$, \[HCN\]/\[HCO$^+$\]) case by changing $n_{\rm H_2}$ and $T_{\rm {kin}}$, and carried out a $\chi^2$ test" (8.2 Non-LTE excitation analysis)
   - software: RADEX, LAMDA
   - parameter: T_kin 10–600 K in 10 K steps; n_H2 10^2–10^7 cm^-3 in steps x10^0.25; dV = 250 km/s; T_bg = 2.73 K; N_H2 = 4.8e22 cm^-2 representative  
  > "we varied the gas kinetic temperature within a range of $T_{\rm{kin}}$ = 10 – 600 K using steps of $dT_{\rm{kin}}$ = +10 K" (8.2 Non-LTE excitation analysis)
   - assumption: Spherical geometry, homogeneous single-component medium; [HCO+]/[H2]=5e-9  
  > "RADEX uses an escape probability approximation to solve the non-LTE excitation in a homogeneous (single temperature, single density), single component medium" (8.2 Non-LTE excitation analysis)
   - fitting: chi2 grid search; well-fitted defined as chi2 <= 0.1  
  > "Within the range of well fitted cases (we define as $\chi^2$ $\leq$ 0.1)" (8.2 Non-LTE excitation analysis)
   - validation: Low-J vs high-J inconsistent fit (chi2 = 7.774) motivates using only high-J  
  > "$\chi^2$ = 7.774), from which we consider the $\chi^2$ value is too high" (8.2 Non-LTE excitation analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN and HCO+ J=4-3 emission is concentrated in the nucleus, whereas CO 3-2 and continuum also trace the starburst ring  
  > "whereas strong HCN ($J$ = 4–3) and HCO$^+$ ($J$ = 4–3) emissions originate primarily from the nucleus ($r \leq 100$ pc)" (4 Channel maps and integrated intensities)
- High temperature chemistry favoured over PDR/XDR gas-phase chemistry  
  > "Finally we propose that the $``$*high temperature chemistry*$"$ is more plausible to explain the observed enhanced HCN emission in NGC 1097 than the pure gas phase PDR/XDR chemistry." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Submm high-J lines trace denser/hotter gas with higher resolution than low-J lines  
  > "Compared to low-$J$ millimeter lines, these high-$J$ submillimeter lines can trace denser and/or hotter region with higher angular resolution in general" (Introduction)
- Alternative: UV (PDR), X-ray (XDR) chemistry and cosmic-ray heating rejected; mechanical heating by jet considered possible  
  > "Next we suggest that X-ray chemistry can not explain the observations in NGC 1097." (9.2 X-ray chemistry)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dynamical mass is simple Keplerian estimate and lower limit  
  > "Note that the estimation we present in this section is based on the simple assumptions of Keplerian rotation." (6 Dense molecular gas kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Submm-HCN diagram is tentative, few samples  
  > "this is a tentative view as we do not have clear theoretical evidence to support the use of this diagram, and it is based on few samples" (7.2 Molecular line diagnostics)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-zone RADEX with fixed linewidth and N_H2 assumptions; chi2 on two ratios gives poorly constrained solutions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Different uv coverages among transitions may bias ratios
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Continuum subtraction and line-free channels not documented
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Early Cycle 0 calibration with 14-15 antennas; no uncertainties propagated for chi2
