## Analysis recipe

1. **Select archival ALMA continuum maps and measure continuum flux densities and dust sizes**: Select public ALMA archival maps with highest spatial resolution (<= 1 arcsec), perform 2D Gaussian fit using CASA task imfit to measure flux densities and angular axes, and convert to circularized physical radii using Eq. 2.

## Qualitative findings

- The 11 DSFGs have very compact (sub-)millimeter continuum emission (always < 5 kpc), while rest-frame optical emission is more extended (r_H / r_ALMA > 1).  
  > "More than half of the sources (six out of eleven) are resolved and the median physical size is 1.15 kpc. We interpret this radius as a measure of the region where the bulk of dusty star formation is occurring. It results to be very compact and always $<5$ kpc." (Summary and conclusions); "We found a median ratio between HST and ALMA sizes of r$_{\rm H}/$r$_{\rm ALMA}=2.05$ and it is always $>1$" (Summary and conclusions)
- CO emission extends over an area greater than or equal to the dust continuum size and is equal to or more compact than the optical stellar emission.  
  > "For the 4 sources in our sample with detected CO lines (i.e., UDF1, UDF3, UDF8, ALESS067.1), and also for UDF2, the cold gas emission is equally or more extended then the ALMA B7 and B6 continuum (typically r$_{\rm CO}/$r$_{\rm ALMA}\gtrsim1$), while it is equal or more compact than the optical emission (see Tab. [\[tab:summary_resolved\]](#tab:summary_resolved))." (Discussion)
- Double-peaked CO line profiles and velocity/dispersion maps in UDF3, UDF8, and ALESS067.1 are consistent with rotating molecular gas discs or AGN-driven molecular outflows, while UDF1 is consistent with a face-on rotating disc.  
  > "The double-peaked spectral line profile of three CO lines, along with their velocity and velocity dispersion maps, are consistent with both a rotating disc of molecular gas and an AGN outflow." (Summary and conclusions); "The narrow single-peaked CO(3-2) line of UDF1 is consistent with a *faced-on* rotating molecular gas disc." (Summary and conclusions)
- Most of the 11 DSFGs are caught in the gas compaction phase and are expected to be quenched by AGN feedback in ~10^8 yr to become compact quiescent galaxies or massive ETGs.  
  > "We can state that most of the galaxies in our sample are caught in the *gas compaction* phase and models predict that they should be quenched by the AGN feedback in $\sim 10^{8}$ yr." (Summary and conclusions); "Ultimately, we expect our 11 DSFGs to become compact quiescent galaxies or massive ETGs." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected public ALMA archival maps with the highest spatial resolution (<= 1 arcsec) in Bands 6, 7, and 9 for a homogeneous sample of 11 spectroscopically confirmed z~2 DSFGs in GOODS-S that already have complete X-ray-to-radio SED modelling.  
  > "We analyse the public ALMA archival maps containing our 11 DSFGs and we select the ones that have the best spatial resolution ($\Delta\theta\lesssim1$ arcsec) in the wavelength range $\lambda_{\rm obs}\sim 500\mu$m $-$ 3 mm (corresponding to the frequency range $\nu_{\rm obs}\sim 100-600$ GHz)." (Continuum emission); "Almost all of the best spatially-resolved continuum maps are in ALMA B6 and B7 that, indeed, constitute a very good compromise between spatial resolution and the sampled wavelength." (Continuum emission)
- Why this method: Used image-plane 2D Gaussian fitting (CASA imfit) because visibility-domain fitting was precluded by the noise level; preferred CO-derived gas masses over dust-continuum gas masses because dust continuum can miss gas at larger radii; rebinned spectral channels to reduce noise and boost CO line signal.  
  > "The level of noise did not allow us to extract the sizes from a fit in the visibility domains." (Continuum emission); "Images native spectral resolution in most cases was too small to allow the CO line profile to be clearly detected. In such cases we perform a rebin (using the CASA task *imrebin*[^2]) of the line channels." (Spectroscopic emission); "While characterizing galaxy cold gas phase we preferentially use the gas mass measurements from CO spectral lines (when available; see Tab. [\[tab:summary_resolved\]](#tab:summary_resolved)) rather than the ones from dust continuum (Tab. [\[tab:summary_integral\]](#tab:summary_integral)), since the latter can miss the gas distributed over scales larger than the dust continuum size" (Individual source analysis)
- Alternative: Extracting continuum source sizes from a fit in the visibility (uv) domain was considered, but rejected because the noise level did not allow it.  
  > "The level of noise did not allow us to extract the sizes from a fit in the visibility domains." (Continuum emission)
- Alternative: 3D tilted-ring kinematic modelling of CO(3-2) with 3D-Barolo was considered for ALESS067.1, but not performed due to insufficient spatial and spectral resolution in the ALMA cube.  
  > "A clear interpretation requires higher spectral and spatial resolution in the ALMA cube, that would have allowed to model the CO(3-2) kinematics, exploiting e.g., 3D-Barolo (cite: DiTeodoro&Fraternali2015:2015MNRAS.451.3021D)" (ALESS067.1 (J033243.19-275514.3))
- Alternative: Alternative CO conversion factor alpha_CO = 1.8 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 (often preferred for compact SMGs) was considered alongside alpha_CO = 3.6; authors provide a 0.5 rescaling factor rather than favouring one.  
  > "However, due to the large uncertainties on $\alpha_{\rm CO}$, we do not favour one or the other value and we suggest just to re-scale the resulting molecular gas in Tab. [1](#tab:CO_gas_mol) by a factor $1.8/3.6=0.5$." (Molecular gas mass)
- How they decided it was enough: Selected the highest spatial resolution archival maps currently available and emphasized that higher spatial and spectral resolution observations are still needed to disentangle rotating discs from outflows and interactions.  
  > "Images at higher (spectral and spatial) resolution are needed to disentangle from the possible scenarios." (Abstract); "Finally, we stress the need of more sensitive multi-wavelength maps and higher spatial and spectral resolution images in the diverse spectral bands to definitely clarify the relative impact of in-situ processes, galaxy interactions and AGN feedback" (Summary and conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): 5 out of 11 sources in continuum maps (listed as 6 out of 11 in text, 5 marked with sb in Table 2) and all 4 CO-detected sources in mom0 maps are unresolved by 2D Gaussian fitting, yielding only upper limits or peak-separation estimates.  
  > "Some of the detections (6 out of 11) are not resolved in the corresponding ALMA map: in such a case we indicate the synthesized beam size (labelled with the apex *sb*) that provides an upper limit on the source size." (Continuum emission); "Since they all appear unresolved to a 2D Gaussian fit, we provide: an upper limit on UDF1 CO emission size, given by the synthesized beam; a measure of UDF3, UDF8 and ALESS067.1 CO emission equal to the angular distance between the peaks of the two spectral line components." (Spectroscopic emission)
- Limitation (authors): Current spatial and spectral resolution of the CO data cubes is insufficient to definitively disentangle rotating molecular gas discs from AGN-driven outflows or galaxy interactions.  
  > "CO lines reveal the presence of a rotating disc of molecular gas, but we can not exclude either the presence of interactions and/or molecular outflows. Images at higher (spectral and spatial) resolution are needed to disentangle from the possible scenarios." (Abstract)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainty in the CO-to-H2 conversion factor alpha_CO and assumed CO excitation ladder.  
  > "However, due to the large uncertainties on $\alpha_{\rm CO}$, we do not favour one or the other value and we suggest just to re-scale the resulting molecular gas in Tab. [1](#tab:CO_gas_mol) by a factor $1.8/3.6=0.5$." (Molecular gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity limits and low spatial resolution in the ALESS067.1 CO(6-5) map may miss extended CO emission and prevent spatial comparison with CO(3-2).  
  > "The low spatial resolution of the image does not allow to spatially compare the size of the two spectral emission, neither to understand the origin of the $J=6$ line" (Spectroscopic emission); "The sightly lower value we find is probably due to sensitivity limit that makes us miss the outskirts of the CO emission, coupled with low resolution that causes the flux to be distributed on a larger image area" (Molecular gas mass)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO sizes for UDF3, UDF8, and ALESS067.1 are estimated from the angular separation between double-peaked spectral components rather than deconvolved profile fits, which may systematically bias r_CO compared to Gaussian half-light radii.
