## Science

- Fields: star_formation, milky_way_ism
- Question: Does the hourglass-shaped magnetic field seen at core scales (1'' and 0.24'') in the hot molecular core G31.41+0.31 stay self-similar down to circumstellar scales (~0.068'', ~250 au), and is the polarization from aligned grains rather than self-scattering?  
  > "To study the magnetic field morphology toward the four (proto)stars A, B, C, and D contained in G31.41+0.31 and examine whether the self-similarity observed at core scales ($1''$ and $0.24\ensuremath{''}$ resolution) still holds at circumstellar scales" (abstract); "Observations at both wavelengths were carried out to investigate whether the polarization is due to the magnetic field or to dust self-scattering, which should introduce a dependence on the wavelength." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image Stokes I, Q, U at both bands**: Data calibrated and imaged in CASA with tclean; Stokes I, Q, U obtained from the four cross-correlations; final maps made with same uv-coverage at both bands  
  > "Maps were created using the `tclean` task with the `robust` parameter of Briggs ((cite: briggs95)) of 0.5." (Observations); "From the (XX, XY, YX, and YY) visibilities we obtained the Stokes $I$, $Q$, and $U$ in the image plane." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean, GILDAS
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "Maps were created using the `tclean` task with the `robust` parameter of Briggs ((cite: briggs95)) of 0.5." (Observations)
   - parameter: same uv-coverage at 1.3 mm and 3.1 mm  
  > "the final maps were produced with the same `uv`-coverage." (Observations)
   - assumption: Absolute flux uncertainty 6% (3.1 mm) and 10% (1.3 mm)  
  > "we conservatively assumed that the uncertainties on the absolute flux density calibration were $\sim$6% at 3.1 mm and $\sim$10% at 1.3 mm." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive polarized intensity, fraction and position angle**: P, p and psi computed from Stokes I, Q, U; segments rotated by 90 deg to show the magnetic field; angles computed where Q and U exceed 3 sigma  
  > "we have derived the linearly polarized intensity, $P=\sqrt{Q^2+U^2}$, the fractional linear polarization, $p=P/I$, and the polarization position angle, $\psi=\frac{1}{2}\arctan(U/Q)$." (Observations); "The polarization angles have been calculated for Stokes $Q$ and Stokes $U>3\sigma$." (Polarization angles)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Q and U > 3 sigma for angles  
  > "The polarization angles have been calculated for Stokes $Q$ and Stokes $U>3\sigma$." (Polarization angles)
   - assumption: Polarization produced by magnetically aligned dust grains, so segments are rotated by 90 deg  
  > "Assuming that the polarization is produced by magnetically aligned dust grains, in all the figures we show polarization segments rotated by $90^\circ$ to outline the orientation of the magnetic field." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Angle accuracy taken from Hull et al.: <~1 deg near beam center, ~1-5 deg near the primary-beam FWHM; p accuracy ~0.1%  
  > "The accuracy of the polarization position angle $\psi$ is $\lesssim 1^\circ$ near the beam center and $\sim$1–5$^\circ$ near the full-width at half-maximum area of the primary beam (Hull et al. (cite: hull20))" (Observations)
3. **Measure source peak flux densities and brightness temperatures**: Peak flux density of sources A-D read at the 3.1 mm emission peak position; brightness temperature from the Rayleigh-Jeans-type expression with beam FWHP and frequency  
  > "The peak brightness temperature has been estimated from the flux density at the peak of the emission following the expression $T_{\rm B} = 1.2221\times10^6/(\theta^2\,\nu^2)\,I^{\rm peak}_\lambda$" (Continuum emission); "The position of the sources has been measured at the peak of the emission at 3.1 mm, because the sources are better resolved at this wavelength" (Continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the Beltrán et al. 2021 positions and intensities  
  > "and coincides with that measured by Beltrán et al. ((cite: beltran21))." (Continuum emission)
4. **Spectral index maps of Stokes I and P**: Spectral index alpha (S_nu proportional to nu^alpha) computed between 3.1 and 1.3 mm for Stokes I and for P; optically thin alpha interpreted as beta + 2  
  > "computed using the 3.1 and 1.3 mm Stokes $I$ (*top panels*) and the polarized emission (*bottom panels*)." (Spectral index map); "$\alpha$ is related to the power-law index $\beta$ of the dust opacity coefficient $\kappa_\nu\propto \nu^\beta$, through $\alpha$ = $\beta$ + 2" (Spectral index map)
   - software: GILDAS
   - assumption: Dust emission in the Rayleigh-Jeans regime (T >~ 100 K)  
  > "Therefore, the dust continuum emission is in the Rayleigh-Jeans regime." (Spectral index map)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare polarization angles and fractions at the two wavelengths**: Histogram of angle residuals between 1.3 mm and 3.1 mm; p versus normalized Stokes I plot; zoom on the B-C region to test dichroic extinction versus different depth probed  
  > "we show the map of the polarization angle residuals, obtained from the difference between the polarization angles at 1.3 mm and 3.1 mm, as well as the corresponding histogram." (Polarization angles); "we have plotted the polarization fraction $p$ as a function of the normalized Stokes $I$, that is $I/I_{\rm max}$" (Dichroic extinction)
   - software: GILDAS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Agreement of angles at the two wavelengths taken as evidence against dust self-scattering  
  > "The fact that the polarization maps and the magnetic field orientations are basically the same at the two wavelengths indicates that the polarized observations are probably tracing the emission of magnetically aligned grains and are not affected by dust self-scattering." (Polarization angles)
6. **Fit the magnetic-field model to the 1.3 mm polarization**: DustPol Stokes I, Q, U images for each (i, phi) pair with lambda=2.66 and b0=0.1, run through simobserve/simanalyze with the real antenna configuration, compared to data with reduced chi-square  
  > "and used `DustPol` (Padovani et al. (cite: padovani12)) to create images of the Stokes parameters $I$, $Q$ and $U$ for each combination of $i$ and $\varphi$." (Modeling the magnetic field); "Finally, the convolved models have been compared to the two new sets of observations by computing the reduced chi squared, $\bar\chi^2$." (Modeling the magnetic field)
   - software: DustPol, CASA, simobserve, simanalyze
   - parameter: lambda = 2.66, b0 = 0.1 fixed; i and phi scanned (i in 0-90 deg by symmetry)  
  > "we fixed the values of $\lambda$ and $b_0$ at $\lambda=2.66$ and $b_0=0.1$, as done in Beltrán et al. ((cite: beltran19))" (Modeling the magnetic field); "we show results only for $i\in[0,90]^\circ$." (Modeling the magnetic field)
   - assumption: Axially symmetric singular toroid with poloidal field plus small force-free toroidal component  
  > "describes an axially symmetric singular toroid threaded by a poloidal magnetic field." (Modeling the magnetic field)
   - fitting: Grid search minimizing reduced chi-square over i and phi; only the 1.3 mm data modeled because the 3.1 mm chi-square map is flat and poorly sampled  
  > "For all these reasons, we decided to model only the 1.3 mm observations." (Modeling the magnetic field)
   - validation: Compared with the earlier 0.24'' result (phi = -44 deg, i = -45 deg); also tested 3.1 mm with and without a uv-taper  
  > "The minimum $\bar\chi^2$ is achieved for $\varphi=-63^\circ$, close to what we found with previous observations ($\varphi=-44^\circ$)." (Modeling the magnetic field)
7. **Magnetic field strength with the DCF method**: Angular dispersion of observed angles about the model inside the 5 sigma contour, restricted to |Delta psi| < 45 deg; B_pos = xi (sigma_los / delta psi_int) sqrt(4 pi rho); total B = B_pos / cos i  
  > "B_{\rm pos}=\xi\frac{\sigma_{\rm los}}{\delta\psi_{\rm int}}\sqrt{4\pi\rho}" (Magnetic field strength); "we decided to limit the range of $\Delta\psi$ between $-45^\circ$ and $+45^\circ$, and use the standard deviation on the polarization angle dispersion, $\sigma_\psi= 21.45^\circ$." (Magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: xi = 0.5; histogram bin 6 deg; sigma_QU = 15 uJy/beam; region inside the 5 sigma contour  
  > "therefore, we used a histogram bin of $6^\circ$." (Magnetic field strength); "We considered the region inside the $5\sigma$ contour level to focus only on the Main core." (Magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: sigma_los = Delta V / sqrt(8 ln 2) from CH3CN, DeltaV ~5.6 +/- 0.15 km/s; density 1.4e7-5e8 cm^-3  
  > "as $\sigma_{\rm los}=\Delta V/\sqrt{8\,\ln{2}}$." (Magnetic field strength); "which we assume to be an order of magnitude lower, $n\sim 5\times10^8$ cm$^{-3}$." (Magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Perturbations are Alfven waves; thermal velocity dispersion negligible at T >~ 100 K  
  > "the thermal contribution to the velocity dispersion is negligible for the temperatures $\ensuremath{\gtrsim} 100$ K estimated for the Main core" (Magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Inclination i = 50 deg from the model fit  
  > "which taking into account the inclination with respect to the plane of the sky, $i=50^\circ$, obtained from our modeling" (Magnetic field strength)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: DCF validity limit delta psi_int < 25 deg is met (21.45 deg); results compared with Crutcher (2012) and with the polarization-intensity gradient method  
  > "In our case, $\delta\psi_{\rm int}=21.45^\circ$ is large but still within the limit." (Magnetic field strength)
8. **Position-dependent field strength with the polarization-intensity gradient method**: Sigma_B = sin(Psi)/sin(alpha) from the angle between local gravity and intensity gradient and between polarization direction and intensity gradient; B_pos from Sigma_B, gravity and radius of curvature  
  > "\Sigma_{B} = \frac{\sin \Psi}{\sin \alpha}" (The polarization-intensity gradient method); "The local gravity direction is computed through the distribution of all surrounding mass, at any map position, under the assumption that the dust emission is proportional to the gas mass." (The polarization-intensity gradient method)
   - parameter: fewer than 3 pixels per beam; only polarization above 5 sigma; only Sigma_B < 2 used (threshold 5 raises B_pos by ~6%)  
  > "we have reduced the number of pixels contained in each beam surface to $<$3, and we have considered only polarization measurements above $5\sigma$." (The polarization-intensity gradient method); "only values of $\Sigma_B < 2$ have been considered to avoid artificially high values of the magnetic field strength" (The polarization-intensity gradient method)
   - assumption: Pressure gradient negligible compared with gravity; uniform density over 1.4e7-5e8 cm^-3  
  > "we assume that local changes in temperature and density are minor compared to gravity and therefore the pressure gradient $\nabla P$ to be negligible compared to the gravitational force $\rho\nabla\phi$." (The polarization-intensity gradient method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the DCF range (8.3-50 mG)  
  > "This result is consistent with the range obtained with the DCF method" (The polarization-intensity gradient method)
9. **Mass-to-flux ratio, Alfven velocity and turbulent-to-uniform field ratio**: Magnetic flux Phi = pi R^2 B inside 1'' (and 0.3''); lambda = 2 pi G^(1/2) M(Phi)/Phi with a 1.4 geometry correction; lambda_s from <Sigma_B>; v_A = B/sqrt(4 pi rho); delta B/B ~ sqrt(3) cos i delta psi_int / xi  
  > "\lambda=2\pi G^{1/2}\frac{M(\Phi)}{\Phi}" (Mass-to-flux ratio); "\varv_{\rm A} = \frac{B}{\sqrt{4\pi\rho}}" (Mass-to-flux ratio)
   - parameter: B ~13 mG within 1'' (core mass 70+20 = 90 Msun); B = 78 mG and R ~0.3'' for the inner part (57+20 = 77 Msun)  
  > "assuming spherical symmetry and a magnetic field strength of $\sim 13$ mG" (Mass-to-flux ratio); "and using a magnetic field strength of 78 mG" (Mass-to-flux ratio)
   - assumption: Spherical symmetry; mass in the flux tube equals core mass plus formed stars  
  > "we assumed that this is the mass of the core, 70 $M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ (Cesaroni (cite: cesa19)), plus the mass of the (proto)stars already formed in the core, $\sim$20 $M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ (Beltrán et al. (cite: beltran19))." (Mass-to-flux ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: lambda from the DCF route and from the gradient route agree; consistent with the earlier range 1.4-2.2  
  > "we obtain in this case $\lambda_s = 1.45$, in agreement with the value of $\lambda_s$ derived above." (Mass-to-flux ratio)

## Qualitative findings

- The orientation of the field at 1.3 mm matches that at 3.1 mm everywhere except between sources C and B, and also matches the 0.24'' and 1'' observations, indicating self-similarity and polarization from aligned grains rather than self-scattering  
  > "The orientations of the magnetic field lines at 1.3 mm coincide with those at 3.1 mm over the whole core" (Polarization angles); "This agreement on the global orientation of the magnetic field points to a self-similarity of the magnetic field from large to small scales." (Polarization angles)
- Between sources B and C the 1.3 mm and 3.1 mm angles differ by nearly 90 deg, which the authors attribute to possible dichroic extinction at 1.3 mm  
  > "The fact that the polarization directions at the two wavelengths are almost perpendicular, suggests that in this inner area of the Main core, the polarization mechanism could be affected by dichroic extinction" (Dichroic extinction)
- The 3.1 mm observations are too weak and unevenly sampled to constrain the axisymmetric model, and the 1.3 mm emission may be too optically thick to probe circumstellar scales  
  > "Hence the data are insufficient to properly constrain the models, which are axisymmetric" (Magnetic field at different spatial scales)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Two wavelengths at ~0.07'' resolution to separate magnetic alignment from self-scattering and resolve the envelope of the embedded protostars  
  > "Observations at both wavelengths were carried out to investigate whether the polarization is due to the magnetic field or to dust self-scattering, which should introduce a dependence on the wavelength." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Reuse of the earlier semi-analytical model to compare directly with 0.24'' results, after noticing a similar trend  
  > "we noted a trend similar to that found from previous observations at 1.3 mm by Beltrán et al." (Modeling the magnetic field)
- Alternative: uv-taper of 0.15'' on the 3.1 mm data to recover extended emission; the chi-square map stays flat but the minimum moves to a negative phi in agreement with 1.3 mm; 3.1 mm not used for modeling  
  > "After performing the `uv`-tapering, the distribution of $\bar\chi^2$ remains flat over the same range of inclinations" (Modeling the magnetic field)
- Alternative: Alternative to dichroic extinction: 1.3 mm traces outer layers and 3.1 mm deeper regions; tested with p versus I/Imax (slope ~ -1 at both wavelengths)  
  > "Another explanation could be that the polarized emission at 1.3 mm is tracing the outer layers of the core" (Dichroic extinction)
- Alternative: Alternative DCF correction factor xi ~0.25 from Liu et al. simulations versus 0.5 adopted  
  > "is $\sim$0.25 at clump and core scales if $\delta\psi_{\rm obs} < 25^\circ$, which is smaller than the value reported by Ostriker et al." (Magnetic field strength)
- How they decided it was enough: Only 1.3 mm modeled because the 3.1 mm chi-square distribution stays flat, even with a uv-taper  
  > "For all these reasons, we decided to model only the 1.3 mm observations." (Modeling the magnetic field)
- Limitation (authors): The 3.1 mm data do not sample the core uniformly and are less sensitive, so they cannot constrain the axisymmetric model  
  > "the observations are less sensitive than at 1.3 mm and the core at 3.1 mm is not properly sampled." (Magnetic field at different spatial scales)
- Limitation (authors): High opacity of the central region may hinder study of the field at circumstellar scales  
  > "might be hindered by the large opacity of the central region of the core" (Magnetic field at different spatial scales)
- Limitation (authors): The mass-to-flux ratio is a lower limit because the stellar mass content may be higher  
  > "This mass-to-flux ratio should be taken as a lower limit" (Mass-to-flux ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Stokes I rms is higher than Q/U rms because of imaging dynamic range  
  > "is due to a problem of imaging dynamic range" (Observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Calibration, polarization calibration and imaging parameters (cell, size, masks, deconvolver) are absent, so the maps cannot be rebuilt exactly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass, density and linewidth inputs come from earlier papers; the density range (a factor ~35) dominates the B_pos range
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DCF is applied with delta psi_int = 21.45 deg, close to the stated 25 deg validity limit, and the xi choice (0.5 vs 0.25) changes B by a factor of two
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reported angle accuracy is taken from other work, not measured here; no uncertainties on model parameters (phi, i)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The B-C 90 deg discrepancy is explained only qualitatively (dichroic extinction); no radiative transfer test
