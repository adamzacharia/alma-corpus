## Science

- Fields: stellar_physics, evolved_stars, milky_way_ism
- Question: Can the 340 GHz continuum of the red supergiant IRS 7 and the H30alpha ionized gas around it be detected, and what do they say about photosphere size, electron temperature and density, mass loss and kinematics  
  > "such observations are suitable to detect both the continuum emission from IRS 7 itself and the recombination line from the extended structure." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **image the 340 GHz continuum**: self-calibrate the 340 GHz data and image with clean using Briggs weighting on the Aug. and Sep. 2016 days  
  > "The imaging to obtain the maps was done using CASA 5.4 with `clean` task." (340 GHz Observation); "Using two days data of Aug. and Sep. 2016" (340 GHz Observation)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 0.2; beam 0.107 x 0.101 arcsec, PA=-78 deg; 1 sigma = 45 uJy/beam  
  > "briggs weighting (robust parameter = 0.2)" (340 GHz Observation); "The $1\sigma$ sensitivity in the emission-free area is $45~\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$." (340 GHz Observation)
   - parameter: phase calibrator J1717-3342; flux scale from Titan and J1733-1304  
  > "J1717-3342 was used as a phase calibrator in the 340 GHz observation." (340 GHz Observation); "The flux density scale was determined using Titan and J1733-1304." (340 GHz Observation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: dynamic range > 50000 in the area  
  > "The dynamic range reaches to $>50000$ in the area." (340 GHz Observation)
2. **make the H30alpha line cube**: subtract the continuum in the uv data, then tclean with auto-multithresh masking and Briggs weighting  
  > "After the subtraction of the continuum emission from the data using CASA task, `mstransform (fitorder=1)`" (H30alpha recombination line); "we made images using CASA 5.0 with `tclean` task with the multi-threshold auto-boxing algorithm" (H30alpha recombination line)
   - software: CASA, mstransform, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; beam 0.41 x 0.30 arcsec, PA=-77 deg; 0.2 mJy/beam in a line-free channel; phase calibrator J1744-3116  
  > "briggs weighting (robust parameter = 0.5)" (H30alpha recombination line); "J1744-3116 was used as a phase calibrator." (H30alpha recombination line)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: sensitivity compared with the ALMA sensitivity calculator  
  > "The sensitivity is close to the expected one by the ALMA sensitivity calculator." (H30alpha recombination line)
3. **measure IRS 7 position and 340 GHz flux density**: 2D Gaussian fitting of the point-like source in the 340 GHz map, primary-beam corrected; mean brightness temperature from the beam-averaged formula  
  > "Using 2D Gaussian fitting, the position of IRS 7 is derived to be" (Results); "The angular extent of IRS 7 itself is not detected, and thus the total flux density at 340 GHz of IRS 7 is estimated to be" (Results)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Tb[K] = 1.22e6 (theta_maj x theta_min / arcsec^2)^-1 (nu/GHz)^-2 S_nu[Jy]  
  > "This flux density corresponds to the beam averaged brightness temperature of $\bar{T_\mathrm{B}}=0.41\pm0.04$ K" (Results)
   - assumption: source is unresolved (point-like)  
  > "The angular extent of IRS 7 itself is not detected" (Results)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "Using 2D Gaussian fitting, the position of IRS 7 is derived to be" (Results)
   - validation: position compared with the IR position corrected to the observation epoch with the IR proper motion  
  > "using the IR proper motion ((cite: Schodel))" (Results)
4. **derive the photosphere radius**: treat the 340 GHz emission as optically thick photospheric emission; beam-dilution uniform-disk relation between mean Tb and Teff solved for R  
  > "Assuming that the distribution of the brightness temperature is like an uniform-disk, this is approximated as the following equation" (Photosphere at 340 GHz of IRS 7); "Therefore, the radius of the photosphere is estimated to" (Photosphere at 340 GHz of IRS 7)
   - parameter: Tb ~ Teff = 3600 K; mean Tb = 0.41 +/- 0.04 K; d ~ 8.2 kpc  
  > "The brightness temperature at 340 GHz is assumed to be $T_\mathrm{eff} = 3600$ K here." (Photosphere at 340 GHz of IRS 7)
   - assumption: optically thick thermal emission from the photosphere or its vicinity, uniform-disk brightness  
  > "This also suggests that the sub-millimeter continuum emission is emitted from the photosphere itself or the vicinity." (Photosphere at 340 GHz of IRS 7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: compared with the VLTI H-band radius, judged consistent because the star is variable in size  
  > "However, our radius would be consistent with the VLTI radius because IRS 7 is reported to be variable in size based on the observations in 2008 and 2013 ((cite: Paumard2014))." (Photosphere at 340 GHz of IRS 7)
5. **characterise the H30alpha kinematics**: channel maps, moment 0 and peak intensity maps, position-velocity diagram, Gaussian fit to the line profile, then sound speed, expansion velocity and spatial velocity from formulae  
  > "The central velocity and FWHM velocity width by Gaussian fitting is $V_\mathrm{c} = -124.4\pm0.63$ km s$^{-1}$ and $V_\mathrm{FWHM} =31.9\pm1.5$ km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - parameter: channel maps with dV = 10 km/s; moment 0 over V_LSR = -400 to 400 km/s  
  > "The integrated velocity range is $V_\mathrm{LSR} = -400$ to $400$ km s$^{-1}$." (Results); "The velocity width of each map is $\Delta V=10$ km s$^{-1}$." (Results)
   - assumption: hydrogen ion temperature equals the electron temperature, Te* = 4650 K  
  > "Assuming Hydrogen ion temperature is equal to the electron temperature" (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - assumption: observed velocity extent comes from gas expansion alone  
  > "If the observed velocity extent originates from the gas expansion alone" (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to the single-peak H30alpha profile; no sampler stated  
  > "The shell like structure around IRS 7 has a single-peak line profile." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - validation: SiO v=2 maser velocity (-120 km/s) compared with the ionized gas central velocity  
  > "There is a peak of the SiO maser emission line at $V_\mathrm{LSR} = -120$ km s$^{-1}$." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
6. **derive the LTE electron temperature**: iteratively solve the LTE formula using the H30alpha integrated line intensity and the 232 GHz continuum flux density, assuming both are optically thin  
  > "assuming that the line and continuum emissions are both optically thin and in local thermodynamic equilibrium." (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7); "The LTE electron temperature is obtained by iteratively solving the formula for $T^\ast_{\mathrm e}$." (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
   - parameter: S(232 GHz) = S(340 GHz) (340/232)^0.1 = 3.81 +/- 0.10 mJy  
  > "The continuum flux density is estimated to be $S_\nu(\mathrm{232GHz})=3.81\pm0.10$ mJy using $S_\nu(\mathrm{232GHz})=S_\nu(\mathrm{340GHz})(\frac{340}{232})^{0.1}$." (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
   - assumption: He+/H+ number ratio = 0.09; a = 0.678-0.892 for Te = 0.3-1.5e4 K  
  > "$a=0.678-0.892$ for $T^\ast_{\mathrm e}=0.3-1.5\times10^4$ K (cite: Mezger)" (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: compared with an earlier Te = 7000 K and with Teff of the photosphere  
  > "This is slightly higher than the effective temperature of the photosphere of IRS 7" (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
7. **derive electron density, gas mass and mass loss rate**: mean continuum brightness temperature of the shell gives ne with the Altenhoff formula; gas mass from a uniform sphere; expansion time from D/2 over Vexp; mass loss rate = M_gas / T_exp  
  > "The mean path length is given by" (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7); "If the gas mass is maintained mainly by the mass loss of IRS 7" (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
   - parameter: shell diameter D ~ 0.055 pc = 1.7e17 cm; path length L = 2D/3 ~ 1.1e17 cm; mass abundance ratio 1.4  
  > "The mean path length is $L\sim1.1\times10^{17}$ cm." (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
   - assumption: spherical ionized gas of diameter D; solar abundances  
  > "assuming that the ionized gas has a spherical shape with the diameter, $D$." (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7); "assuming the solar abundance of elements" (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: mass loss rate compared with expected values for a pulsating red supergiant of 20-25 Msun  
  > "consistent with the expected mass loss rate of a pulsating red supergiant star with $M=20-25 M_\odot$ (e.g. (cite: Yoon))" (Physical Properties of the Surrounding Structures of IRS7)
8. **test the bow shock interpretation**: compare the transverse velocity vector with the brightened southern half, and estimate the kinetic energy of a hydrogen atom against the 13.6 eV ionization energy  
  > "The kinetic energy reaches to $E_\mathrm{kin} \sim 22~\mathrm{eV}$" (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - parameter: V_air >~ 50 km/s, V_exp = 14 km/s; V_t = sqrt(V_perp^2 + V_LSR^2)  
  > "The 3D airspeed would be at least $V_\mathrm{air} \gtrsim 50$ km s$^{-1}$ or supersonic." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - assumption: IRS 7 SiO maser velocity and position trace the star itself  
  > "The radial velocity and position of the SiO maser emission should be nearly equal to the radial velocity and position of IRS 7 itself." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: positional shift of IRS 7 to the south judged consistent with earlier bow shock discussions  
  > "is consistent with the bow shock hypothesis, which have already been discussed" (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)

## Qualitative findings

## Reasoning

- Why this data: line to continuum ratio of recombination lines and photospheric emission favour millimeter and sub-millimeter wavelengths, so ALMA at 340 GHz was used  
  > "such observations are suitable to detect both the continuum emission from IRS 7 itself and the recombination line from the extended structure." (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Difference in appearance from the 34.5 GHz map attributed mainly to beam size; a Te distribution effect is not excluded  
  > "However, it cannot completely ruled out another cause, that is the distribution of the electron temperature because the line/continuum ratio is sensitive to the electron temperature." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): IRS 7 image is slightly distorted by unavoidable sidelobes of Sgr A*  
  > "the image is slightly distorted by unavoidable side lobes of Sgr A" (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Electron temperature distribution may contribute to the appearance difference from 34.5 GHz  
  > "However, it cannot completely ruled out another cause, that is the distribution of the electron temperature because the line/continuum ratio is sensitive to the electron temperature." (Kinematics of the Surrounding Structures of IRS 7)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Photosphere radius depends on assuming Tb = Teff = 3600 K and a uniform disk, and IRS 7 is only ~10 sigma at the beam size, so the R uncertainty of 60 Rsun looks small
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 34.5 GHz limit and the 340 GHz flux come from different epochs and a star variable in size, so the spectral index limit is loose
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Te and ne rely on optically thin LTE, a spherical shell geometry, the assumed continuum spectral index 0.1 and He+/H+ = 0.09
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Shell continuum at 232 GHz is scaled from 340 GHz rather than measured, and may include stellar photospheric flux
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass loss rate comes from a single expansion time scale with Vexp derived from the line width; non-thermal broadening and the dominance of ambient gas are not excluded
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Bow shock interpretation rests on a 2D proper motion plus line-of-sight velocity and a rough energy estimate
