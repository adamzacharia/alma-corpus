## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify CO cloud candidates**: Compute rms per spaxel; select ≥5 rms channels with at least two adjacent qualifying spaxels  
  > "we first compute the root-to-mean square (rms) for all the spaxels within the FoV of the CO(1-0) data." (Identification and significance of the CO(1-0) line detections in Leo T); "contain one or more channels with CO emission at least 5 times higher than the rms" (Identification and significance of the CO(1-0) line detections in Leo T); "have at least two adjacent spaxels that meet the criteria 1)" (Identification and significance of the CO(1-0) line detections in Leo T)
   - parameter: North, west, south: 9, 3, 4 validated spaxels  
  > "we identify three regions located in the north, west, and south of the FoV (see top panel of Fig. [2](#fig:peakSNR)), which contain 9, 3, and 4 validated spaxels, respectively" (Identification and significance of the CO(1-0) line detections in Leo T)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Distribution of positive and negative peak S/N per pixel  
  > "The bottom panel of Fig. [2](#fig:peakSNR) shows the distribution of positive and negative peak S/N values per pixel in the ACA-CO(1-0) data." (Identification and significance of the CO(1-0) line detections in Leo T)
2. **Define cloud sizes and apertures**: 2D Gaussian emission fit; circular aperture rCO≈FWHMCO/2  
  > "When fitting the emission by a 2D Gaussian function, we find full width at half maxima of FWHM$_{\rm CO}$$\sim{10}''$–${14}''$, which are similar to the beam major axis." (Results and discussion); "We adopted $r_{\rm CO}$ to set a circular aperture and extract the main molecular cloud physical properties (which should be considered as upper limits)" (Results and discussion)
   - parameter: North: 5.7±0.25; west: 6.2±0.8; south: 7.1±0.23 arcsec  
  > "| \(3\) $r_{\rm CO}(={\rm FWHM/2})$  \[arcsec\] | 5.7$\pm$0.25 | 6.2$\pm$0.8 | 7.1$\pm$0.23 |" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Spherical clouds; rCO≈FWHMCO/2  
  > "assuming that they have a spherical shape with a radius $r_{\rm CO} \approx \frac{1}{2}$FWHM$_{\rm CO}$." (Results and discussion)
   - assumption: 1 arcsec = 1.98 pc; Leo T distance 409 kpc  
  > "using the conversion of $1{\rm [arcsec]}=1.98\ensuremath{\,{\rm pc}}$ given the distance to Leo T of 409$\,{\rm kpc}$" (ACA-CO(1-0) and WSRT-HI  data products)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian; Table 1 describes fits to emission in MOM1 maps  
  > "derived from a 2D Gaussian fit of the emission in MOM1 maps" (Table 1)
   - validation: Fitted FWHM comparable to beam major axis; sources not fully resolved  
  > "The limited angular resolution of the CO(1-0) data does not allow us to fully resolve the sources." (Results and discussion)
3. **Fit spectra and construct moment maps**: Extract peak-centered aperture spectra; fit Gaussian; integrate vlos±FWHM; compute moment 1  
  > "centered at the peak of emission and adopting a circular aperture with radius $r_{\rm app} = r_{\rm CO} = {\rm FWHM/2}$" (ACA-CO(1-0) and WSRT-HI  data products); "by collapsing the data cube in the range $\rm [\upsilon^{\odot}_{\rm los, CO}-FWHM, \upsilon^{\odot}_{\rm los, CO}+FWHM]$" (ACA-CO(1-0) and WSRT-HI  data products); "Finally, we compute the moment 1 maps ($M_1$) using Eq. (2) in (cite: Villanueva2024a)" (ACA-CO(1-0) and WSRT-HI  data products)
   - parameter: FWHM=2√(2 ln(2))σ

## Qualitative findings

- First CO detection in Leo T; three molecular cloud candidates  
  > "We present the first detection of $^{12}$CO($J$=1-0) emission line data in Leo T" (Conclusions); "We identified three molecular cloud candidates located in the central region of Leo T." (Conclusions)
- Cloud properties agree with low-metallicity dwarf scaling relations  
  > "These results agree with the main properties and scaling relations of molecular (CO) clouds in other low-metallicity dwarf galaxies" (Conclusions)
- West and south clouds likely bound; north cloud may be undergoing ejection  
  > "Two clouds show low velocities and would remain gravitationally bound to Leo T, whereas the larger velocity of the north cloud could be direct evidence of ongoing molecular gas ejection in such low-mass dwarfs." (Conclusions)
- Ram pressure weakly affects modeled cloud orbits  
  > "ram pressure only weakly affects the clouds’ orbits due to the low CGM density, the slow clouds’ motions relative to the ISM, and the dense nature of the molecular clouds." (Results and discussion)
- Stellar winds may compress HI and form offset molecular structures  
  > "a plausible scenario is that stellar winds may compress HIto reach the critical column density" (Results and discussion); "and form H$_2$ structures with offset velocities." (Results and discussion)
- North-cloud quantities are more plausible at the distance of Leo T  
  > "the physical quantities of the north cloud are more plausible when we assume that it is located at the distance of Leo T." (The CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor in Leo T and in foreground MW material)
- No detectable cold dust continuum in ACA data  
  > "we report no detectable cold dust continuum emission in our ACA data for the targeted sensitivities." (Scaling and star formation relations in Leo T)

- Why this data: Use CO to trace H2 in a very low-mass, metal-poor dwarf

- Why this method: Briggs robust=0.5 balances synthesized beam and S/N  
  > "The cleaning routine considered a Briggs weighting (`robust`=$0.5$; (cite: Briggs1995)), with a good compromise between the synthesized beam and the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)." (Observational data and products)
- Alternative: North cloud as MW foreground material: considered unlikely  
  > "The cloud’s high velocity also suggests that this could belong to foreground MW material." (Results and discussion); "However, this seems unlikely, as we find that the factors $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ would be orders of magnitude higher than typical values for the MW gas" (Results and discussion)
