## Analysis recipe

1. **Averaging and coordinate system alignment**: Averaged visibility data across 32 channels (0.5 GHz) and 10.08 s, and relabeled J2000.0 coordinates of Cycle 1 and 2 archival data to ICRS using an ALMA project CASA script.  
  > "To reduce the data volume for easier handling in continuum imaging, we average the data in frequency and time directions with 32 channels ($\Delta 
u = 0.5$ GHz) and 10.08 sec, respectively." (2.2 Data Reduction); "we relabel the coordinates of Cycle 1 and 2 data from J2000.0 to the International Celestial Reference System (ICRS) by using a CASA script offered by the ALMA project" (2.4 Combined Map)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Channel averaging: 32 channels (0.5 GHz); Time averaging: 10.08 sec  
  > "we average the data in frequency and time directions with 32 channels ($\Delta 
u = 0.5$ GHz) and 10.08 sec, respectively." (2.2 Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Bandwidth and time smearing effects on peak flux density are negligible (<1%)  
  > "The effect of bandwidth smearing on the peak flux density of a source caused by the channel averaging is less than 1% even at the edge of the primary beam" (2.2 Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirmed negligible time averaging effect based on bandpass calibrator imaging.  
  > "We also confirm that the effect of the time averaging on the flux density is negligible based on the imaging of the bandpass calibrator." (2.2 Data Reduction)
2. **Continuum mosaic imaging**: Produced combined maps in CASA with uv-tapers of 250 klambda, 160 klambda, and untapered, representative frequency 243.047 GHz (1.23 mm).  
  > "The combined map was produced with CASA with the same parameters adopted in Sec [subsec:reduction]. The representative frequency of the map is 243.047 GHz (1.23 mm). We adopt an $uv$-taper of 250 k$\lambda$" (2.4 Combined Map)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: uv-taper: 250 klambda; representative frequency: 243.047 GHz  
  > "The representative frequency of the map is 243.047 GHz (1.23 mm). We adopt an $uv$-taper of 250 k$\lambda$ to weight extended components" (2.4 Combined Map)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Gaussian fit to pixel histogram yields 1 sigma = 34 uJy beam^-1.  
  > "The dashed curve represents the result of a Gaussian fit, which gives $1\sigma$ of 34 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$." (2.4 Combined Map)
3. **Source extraction and 2D Gaussian fitting**: Run BANE for background and noise estimation, then Aegean for source detection on non-primary-beam-corrected signal map; fit detected sources with 2D elliptical Gaussians.  
  > "Source detection is conducted on the signal map before correcting for the primary beam attenuation. We adopt the source-finding algorithm called Aegean (cite: hanc12; hanc18)" (3.1 Source Detection); "The detected sources are fitted with a 2D elliptical Gaussian to estimate the source size and integrated flux density." (3.1 Source Detection)
   - software: Aegean, BANE
   - parameter: SN thresholds >= 5.0 sigma (25 sources) and >= 4.5 sigma (45 sources)  
  > "We find 25 (45) sources with a peak SN of $\ge$5$\sigma$ ($\ge$4.5$\sigma$)." (3.1 Source Detection)
   - assumption: If Sint < Speak, Speak is adopted as the source flux density.  
  > "When $S_{m int} < S_{m peak}$, we adopt $S_{m peak}$, since it is possible that the source fitting failed due to the low SN." (3.1 Source Detection)
   - fitting: 2D elliptical Gaussian fitting to source components  
  > "The detected sources are fitted with a 2D elliptical Gaussian to estimate the source size and integrated flux density." (3.1 Source Detection)
   - validation: Checked negative peak statistics (1 at >=5 sigma, 8 at 4.5-5 sigma); cross-matched astrometry against VLA 5-cm radio sources.  
  > "The actual number of negative peaks in the combined map is 1 at $\ge$5$\sigma$ and 8 at 4.5–5$\sigma$." (3.1 Source Detection); "The radio counterparts are found for 20 out of the 25 ASAGAO 5$\sigma$ sources within a 0.5$''$ search radius" (3.2 Astrometry)
4. **Completeness and flux boosting simulations**: Injected 30,000 artificial Gaussian sources (convolving size 0.01"–0.5", flux 0.05–2 mJy) into the signal map one at a time and re-extracted them with Aegean.  
  > "In the completeness calculation, we take a convolving beam size to be uniformly distributed from 0.01$''$–0.5$''$. We input 30000 artificial sources into the signal map one at a time, each with an integrated flux density randomly selected from 0.05–2 mJy" (4.1 Completeness and Flux Boosting)
   - software: Aegean
   - parameter: Convolving FWHM 0.01"-0.5", flux 0.05-2 mJy, recovery threshold peak SN >= 4.5  
  > "When the input source is detected with a peak SN $\ge 4.5$, the source is considered to be recovered." (4.1 Completeness and Flux Boosting)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: Fitted functions to completeness, flux deboosting, and SN boosting curves  
  > "Then the counts are divided by the completeness by using the best-fit function as a function of SN" (4.2 1.2mm Number Counts)
   - validation: Verified completeness and flux boosting separately for coverage > 0.6 and < 0.6 regions.  
  > "The completeness calculated in regions with different coverage are consistent within errors and we do not find a significant difference." (4.1 Completeness and Flux Boosting)
5. **Constructing number counts and EBL integration**: Computed differential and cumulative number counts using the 45 sources >=4.5 sigma, correcting for effective area, spurious fraction, completeness, and flux boosting; fitted counts to a Schechter function and integrated.  
  > "By using the 4.5$\sigma$ sources, we create differential and cumulative number counts. To create number counts, we correct for the contamination of spurious sources, the effective area, and the completeness as follows" (4.2 1.2mm Number Counts); "The integration of the ASAGAO differential number counts yields $7.7^{+1.7}_{-1.2}$ Jy deg$^{-2}$ ($S_{m 1.2mm} > 135$ $\mu$Jy)." (4.4 Contribution to Extragalactic Background Light)
   - parameter: Schechter function fit to differential counts with Gehrels (1986) Poisson confidence limits  
  > "The uncertainties from Poisson fluctuations is estimated from Poisson confidence limits of 84.13% (cite: gehr86), which correspond to 1$\sigma$ for Gaussian statistics" (4.2 1.2mm Number Counts)
   - assumption: Modified black body with beta = 1.5, Tdust = 35 K, and z = 2 for scaling 870 um single-dish counts to 1.2 mm  
  > "Here we assume a modified black body with a dust emissivity index of $eta = 1.5$, dust temperature of 35 K, and $z = 2$." (4.2 1.2mm Number Counts)
   - fitting: Fit of differential number counts to a Schechter function  
  > "The differential number counts obtained in this study and previous studies are fitted to a Schechter function of the form" (4.2 1.2mm Number Counts)
   - validation: Compared counts with previous blank-field and serendipitous ALMA surveys.  
  > "We find that our number counts are consistent with those of the previous ALMA blank-field surveys." (4.3 Comparison with Previous ALMA Studies)
6. **Constructing infrared luminosity functions and cosmic SFRD**: Cross-matched 5 sigma sources with ZFOURGE Ks-band catalog, performed SED fitting with MAGPHYS to get L_IR, calculated LFs via Vmax method across z=1-2, 1.5-2.5, 2-3, fitted Schechter function, and integrated to derive SFRD.  
  > "By using ancillary multi-wavelength data (0.4–500 $\mu$m) and our ALMA photometry, SED fitting with the magphys model (cite: dacu08; dacu15) is performed." (5.1 IR Luminosity Function of ASAGAO Sources); "To construct the LFs, we adopt the $V_{m max}$ method (cite: schm68)." (5.1 IR Luminosity Function of ASAGAO Sources); "By integrating the best-fit IR LF and converting it to SFRD, we estimate the contribution of ASAGAO sources to the cosmic SFRD at $z \sim 2$." (5.3 Contribution to the Cosmic SFR Density)
   - software: MAGPHYS
   - parameter: Delta log(L) = 0.6; redshift bins 1.0 < z < 2.0, 1.5 < z < 2.5, 2.0 < z < 3.0  
  > "We adopt a luminosity bin width of $\Delta\log{(L)} = 0.6$." (5.1 IR Luminosity Function of ASAGAO Sources); "We derive IR LFs in the redshift ranges of $1.0 < z < 2.0$, $1.5 < z < 2.5$, and $2.0 < z < 3.0$ by using 6 (mean redshift of $z_{m mean} = 1.55$), 9 ($z_{m mean} = 2.12$), and 13 ($z_{m mean} = 2.49$) sources" (5.1 IR Luminosity Function of ASAGAO Sources)
   - assumption: Bruzual & Charlot (2003) templates, Charlot & Fall (2000) dust extinction, Chabrier (2003) IMF, Kennicutt (1998) SFR relation  
  > "The SED templates of (cite: bruz03) and the dust extinction model of (cite: char00) are adopted." (5.1 IR Luminosity Function of ASAGAO Sources); "SFR is converted from IR luminosity by using the relation of (cite: kenn98) and corrected to a (cite: chab03) IMF." (5.3 Contribution to the Cosmic SFR Density)
   - fitting: Schechter function fit to IR LFs at 1.5 < z < 2.5  
  > "We fit the IR LFs at $1.5 < z < 2.5$ obtained from the ASAGAO sources and the results of (cite: kopr17) with a Schechter function" (5.2 Comparison with Previous Studies)
   - validation: Compared derived SFRD with individual source summing and literature total SFRD.  
  > "This is consistent with the results of (cite: yama18), where they derived the SFRD by counting the contribution from individual ASAGAO sources." (5.3 Contribution to the Cosmic SFR Density)

## Qualitative findings

- Combining ASAGAO with archival ALMA data provides 25 sources at >=5.0 sigma and 45 sources at >=4.5 sigma, representing the largest blank-field sample at 1.2 mm at the time.  
  > "providing the largest sample of sources (25 sources at $\ge$5.0$\sigma$, 45 sources at $\ge$4.5$\sigma$) among ALMA blank-field surveys to date." (abstract)
- The majority of sources are spatially resolved by synthesized beams of ~0.3" to 0.6".  
  > "These suggest that sources are resolved by the synthesized beam in the 250 k$\lambda$-tapered and the untapered maps." (3.1 Source Detection)
- Obscured star formation from sources with log(L_IR / L_sun) >= 11.8 accounts for 60%–90% of the cosmic star-formation rate density at z ~ 2.  
  > "We find that obscured star-formation of sources with IR luminosities of $\log{(L_{m IR}/L_{\odot})} \gtrsim 11.8$ account for $\approx$60%–90% of the $z \sim 2$ cosmic star-formation rate density." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: An unbiased contiguous survey over 26 arcmin^2 avoids clustering and target sidelobe biases present in serendipitous field compilations and matches deep ancillary multiwavelength datasets in GOODS-S.  
  > "It is necessary to conduct “unbiased” surveys in a contiguous field rather than collecting discrete fields in order to obtain a census on the population of faint submm sources." (1 Introduction); "The observing area matches the deepest VLA C-band 5 cm (6 GHz) observations ((cite: rujo16); Rujopakarn et al. in prep.) and the ultra-deep VLT/HAWK-I $K_S$-band images." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Aegean and BANE achieve high reliability and completeness for radio maps, and 250 klambda tapering weights extended components of submillimeter sources.  
  > "We adopt the source-finding algorithm called Aegean (cite: hanc12; hanc18), which achieves high reliability and completeness performance for radio maps." (3.1 Source Detection); "We adopt an $uv$-taper of 250 k$\lambda$ to weight extended components" (2.4 Combined Map)
- Alternative: ASPECS archival dataset was excluded because its synthesized beam (1.68" x 0.92") is significantly larger than ASAGAO and other archival data (<=0.5").  
  > "We do not use the data set of ASPECS, where the synthesized beam size ($1.68'' 	imes 0.92''$) is largely different from those of the others ($\lesssim 0.5''$)." (2.3 ALMA Archival Data)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gaussian noise assumptions underestimate negative peak rates in interferometric ALMA maps.  
  > "The number of independent beams in the map is $2.7 	imes 10^5$, and the expected number of $\ge$$4.5\sigma$ sources in a Gaussian statistics is $\sim$1. However, it is reported that this estimation underestimates the negative peaks in previous studies based on ALMA images" (3.1 Source Detection)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainty remains when extrapolating Schechter function to the faintest flux regime (~20 uJy).  
  > "although we note that there is a large uncertainty to extend the function to the faint flux regime." (4.4 Contribution to Extragalactic Background Light)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size and limited luminosity coverage prevent definitive constraints on density versus luminosity evolution of submillimeter sources.  
  > "although the large uncertainties of our LFs due to the small sample size and the limited coverage of IR luminosity do not allow us to further discuss the density/luminosity evolution of submm sources." (5.2 Comparison with Previous Studies)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Source blending and resolution effects could lead to missing extended, low-surface-brightness flux, as evidenced by sources detected with tapering but undetected in untapered maps.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Counterpart identification relies on optical/NIR ZFOURGE positions, which might miss heavily dust-obscured high-redshift (z > 4) sources lacking optical counterparts.
