# N-bearing complex organics toward high-mass protostars. Constant ratios pointing to formation in similar pre-stellar conditions across a large mass range

`2022A&A...668A.109N` | Astronomy and Astrophysics 2022 | first author: Nazari, P. | arXiv: 2208.11128 | DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/202243788

Card schema 1.0 | model gemini-4-argon | depth full | type large_program_or_survey

## Science

- Fields: astrochemistry, star_formation
- Question: Study six N-bearing complex organic molecules (CH3CN, HNCO, NH2CHO, C2H5CN, C2H3CN, CH3NH2) and CH3OH in 37 line-rich high-mass protostars to determine excitation temperatures, column density ratios, and formation conditions across low- and high-mass protostars.  
  > "We aim to study six N-bearing species: methyl cyanide (CH$_3$CN), isocyanic acid (HNCO), formamide (NH$_2$CHO), ethyl cyanide (C$_2$H$_5$CN), vinyl cyanide (C$_2$H$_3$CN) and methylamine (CH$_3$NH$_2$) in a large sample of line-rich high-mass protostars." (abstract); "Next, we fit their spectra and find column densities and excitation temperatures of the N-bearing species mentioned above, in addition to methanol (CH$_3$OH) to be used as a reference species." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Select sample of 37 line-rich hot molecular cores and extract single-pixel spectra**: Select sources from ~200 publicly available ALMAGAL pipeline products (beam 0.5''-1.5'') where CH3CN 12_7-11_7 is detected at ~2.5-3 sigma, excluding sources with very blended lines; extract spectrum from the peak pixel of the CH3CN 12_4-11_4 moment zero map (1 beam off-source for 787212).  
  > "We consider the pipeline calibrated product data of the sources that were publicly available by 19$^{\rm th}$ of June 2021 and had beam sizes between 0.5$\ensuremath{''}$ and 1.5$\ensuremath{''}$ (${\sim} 1000-5000$ au for sources at a few kpc)." (The data); "The criterion for a line rich source in this work is the detection of the CH$_3$CN 12$_K$-11$_K$ $K=7$ line at ${\sim} 2.5-3\sigma$ level." (The data); "The spectrum for each source is extracted from the peak pixel of the CH$_3$CN 12$_4$-11$_4$ integrated intensity map." (The data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: beam size between 0.5'' and 1.5''  
  > "We consider the pipeline calibrated product data of the sources that were publicly available by 19$^{\rm th}$ of June 2021 and had beam sizes between 0.5$\ensuremath{''}$ and 1.5$\ensuremath{''}$ (${\sim} 1000-5000$ au for sources at a few kpc)." (The data)
   - parameter: CH3CN 12_7-11_7 detection threshold ~2.5-3 sigma  
  > "The criterion for a line rich source in this work is the detection of the CH$_3$CN 12$_K$-11$_K$ $K=7$ line at ${\sim} 2.5-3\sigma$ level." (The data)
   - assumption: CH3CN 12_4-11_4 traces the overall N-bearing COM emission  
  > "The reason for this choice is that here we focus on the N-bearing complex organics emission and CH$_3$CN is used as a proxy for overall COM emission." (The data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked peak position offset between CH3CN 12_4-11_4 moment zero and continuum (within half a beam ~0.5'' for all except 693050)  
  > "Most sources have similar peak positions for CH$_3$CN and continuum within half a beam size (${\sim} 0.5\ensuremath{''}$). Only source 693050 has more than half a beam difference between these two peaks." (The data)
2. **Fit LTE synthetic spectra to derive column densities, excitation temperatures, and FWHMs**: Use CASSIS assuming LTE with a single velocity component. ## Qualitative findings

- Excitation temperatures segregate into three groups (hot: NH2CHO; warm: C2H3CN, HN13CO, CH313CN; cold: CH318OH, CH3NH2) following their sublimation temperatures and binding energies, indicating an onion-like spatial stratification around the protostars.  
  > "We find three groups of species when comparing their excitation temperatures: hot (NH$_2$CHO; $T_{\rm ex} \gtrsim 250$ K), warm (C$_2$H$_3$CN, HN$^{13}$CO and CH$_3^{13}$CN; 100 K $\lesssim T_{\rm ex} \lesssim 250$ K) and cold species (CH$_3$OH and CH$_3$NH$_2$; $T_{\rm ex} \lesssim 100$ K). This temperature segregation reflects the trend seen in the sublimation temperature of these molecules and validates the idea that complex organic emission shows an onion-like structure around protostars." (abstract)
- Column density ratios of the N-bearing COMs and methanol are remarkably constant (scatter less than a factor of ~3) across 5-7 orders of magnitude in protostellar luminosity from low- to high-mass protostars, pointing to formation in pre-stellar ices.  
  > "Moreover, the molecules studied here show constant column density ratios across low- and high-mass protostars with scatter less than a factor ${\sim} 3$ around the mean." (abstract); "The constant column density ratios point to a common formation environment of complex organics or their precursors, most likely in the pre-stellar ices." (abstract)
- Ratios involving NH2CHO show larger scatter due to physical effects (dust optical depth and disk/envelope temperature structure) because NH2CHO traces the hottest, innermost regions.  
  > "Given that formamide is most probably tracing the closest regions to the protostars compared with the other species studied here, the column density ratios including NH$_2$CHO are most affected by source structure and dust optical depth." (Conclusions)
- The 'final' warm-up chemical model of Garrod et al. (2022) underpredicts C2H5CN/CH3CN by over an order of magnitude (unless gas-phase CH3CN routes are excluded) and overpredicts CH3NH2/CH3OH, while reproducing HNCO/CH3OH well.  
  > "However, given that the ‘final’ model does not agree well with the observational data (more than one order of magnitude difference) could mean that either not enough CH$_3$CN is produced in the cold stages, too much C$_2$H$_5$CN is produced, it is not as effectively destroyed at later hot stages or gas-phase production of CH$_3$CN is not observed." (Comparison with chemical models); "It is more clear from this figure that most models in (cite: Garrod2022) overproduce CH$_3$NH$_2$ assuming that their methanol abundances are more accurate." (Comparison with chemical models)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA provides high angular resolution (minimizing beam dilution) and high sensitivity (detecting optically thin isotopologues), and the ALMAGAL survey provides a large, homogeneous sample of high-mass protostars not previously analyzed statistically for N-bearing COMs.  
  > "It is important to have higher sensitivity to detect optically thin isotopologues of abundant and bright species such as CH$_3$OH and methyl cyanide (CH$_3$CN) and hence, put better constraints on the column densities of the main isotopologues. In addition, high spatial resolution minimizes the beam dilution effect." (Introduction); "In this work we use the data from the ALMA Evolutionary study of High Mass Protocluster Formation in the Galaxy (ALMAGAL) survey (2019.1.00195.L; PI: Sergio Molinari) to do such a chemistry analysis on a large sample of high-mass sources." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Spectra were extracted at the CH3CN 12_4-11_4 peak because CH3CN traces N-bearing COM emission and avoids continuum dust opacity peaks; fit-by-eye was used alongside grid fitting because severe line blending in line-rich hot cores makes blind chi^2 minimization unreliable; column density ratios were analyzed to cancel out unknown beam dilution factors.  
  > "The reason for this choice is that here we focus on the N-bearing complex organics emission and CH$_3$CN is used as a proxy for overall COM emission." (The data); "This is because these species mainly have blended lines and hence it is not reliable to blindly use a grid of column densities and excitation temperatures and assign the model with the lowest $\chi^{2}$ as the best fit model." (Spectral modeling); "However, since our results focus only on column density ratios, the precise source size does not matter as long as lines are optically thin ((cite: vanGelder2020); (cite: Nazari2021))." (Spectral modeling)
- Alternative: Extracting spectra at the continuum peak (as done in van Gelder et al. 2022b) was rejected in favor of the CH3CN 12_4-11_4 peak to focus on COM-bright cores and avoid optically thick continuum absorption on-source.  
  > "This is different from what is done in (cite: vanGelder2022) where the spectra are extracted from the peak pixel of the continuum." (The data); "Therefore, we only extract the spectrum from the brightest pixel in the moment zero map of CH$_3$CN 12$_4$-11$_4$ line." (The data)
- Alternative: Fitting a two-component velocity profile (as done in van Gelder et al. 2022a for methanol deuteration) was rejected because velocity components are not clearly separated for N-bearing COMs or CH3^18OH.  
  > "Although the lines are blended for many sources, the different velocity components are usually not clearly separated for the N-bearing COMs or $^{18}$O isotopologue of methanol. Hence here we fit the data with a single component, while (cite: vanGelder2022D_H) fit a two-component profile in their work on deuteration of methanol for three of the sources overlapping with this work." (Spectral modeling)
- Alternative: Fixing T_ex of NH2CHO to that of CH3^13CN and fitting its two lines separately (to avoid radiative excitation bias of the v12=1 line) was considered, found to shift column densities by an approximately constant factor without changing ratio scatter, and not adopted.  
  > "To deal with this potential issue one can find the column density of formamide by fixing the temperature to those of CH$_3^{13}$CN and fitting each line separately. However, the resulting column densities with this method will change by an approximately constant factor for all ALMAGAL sources which would not significantly change the scatter in column density ratios including formamide discussed in Sect. [4.3](#sec:low-to-high)." (Spectral modeling)
- Alternative: Including PEACHES low-mass protostar survey data in the ratio scatter calculation was rejected because PEACHES did not cover HNCO, lacked CH3^18OH detections, and derived CH3CN from optically thick main isotopologue lines.  
  > "The PEACHES survey that studies ${\sim} 50$ low-mass protostars does not include HNCO, and with their relatively short integration times $^{18}$O methanol isotopologue lines are unfortunately not detected for most of their sources. Moreover, the column density of CH$_3$CN is found from the main isotopologue lines which are likely optically thick. Hence, their column density values are not included in the quantification of the scatter." (Going from low- to high-mass protostars)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The sample of 37 protostars is biased toward the most line-rich cores (requiring CH3CN 12_7-11_7 detection at ~2.5-3 sigma) and excludes sources with very blended lines.  
  > "From the ${\sim 200}$ sources available at the time, the most line-rich cores are selected for this work. Therefore, the sample of protostars used here is biased." (The data)
- Limitation (authors): Emission is mostly spatially unresolved at ~1'' resolution, preventing direct measurement of emitting source sizes; a beam dilution factor of 1 is assumed.  
  > "A beam dilution factor of one is assumed given that the exact source sizes are not known." (Spectral modeling); "The emission is mostly unresolved, although it is clear that some lines show more extended emission." (Spatial extent)
- Limitation (authors): The NH2CHO v12=1 line (E_up = 476.5 K) may be radiatively pumped rather than collisionally excited, so derived NH2CHO excitation temperatures may mix kinetic and radiative temperatures.  
  > "The vibrationally excited line of formamide could be radiatively excited instead of collisionally excited. Hence, fitting the temperature for formamide could result in a mix of kinetic and radiative temperature." (Spectral modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperatures of CH3^18OH are likely biased toward lower temperatures because only two lines with E_up < 100 K and one line with E_up = 238.9 K are covered.  
  > "The excitation temperature of CH$_3^{18}$OH is likely biased toward lower temperatures given that it is mainly measured from two lines with $E_{\rm up} < 100$ K and one transition with $E_{\rm up} = 238.9$ K." (Excitation temperatures)
- Limitation (authors): The number of low-mass protostars with optically thin isotopologue COM measurements in the literature is very small (4-5 sources), and CH3NH2 is only (tentatively) detected in 12 ALMAGAL sources.  
  > "It is important to note that the calculated scatter for the low-mass protostars are mainly based on a handful (four or five) of measurements available from the literature. Therefore, one cannot make any conclusions solely based on the calculated scatter in low-mass protostars." (Going from low- to high-mass protostars); "It should be noted that the number of sources with CH$_3$NH$_2$ detection is lower than that of the other species studied here, and therefore, the conclusions made here should be confirmed with more data points in the future." (Going from low- to high-mass protostars)
- Limitation (authors): In the most line-rich sources, ^13CH3OH, CH3^13CN, and the low-E_up NH2CHO line can become marginally or partially optically thick.  
  > "The column density ratios of the three ALMAGAL sources with luminosities $>3 \times 10^4$ L$_{\odot}$ are on the higher end suggesting that the methanol column density is likely underestimated due to optical thickness of CH$_3^{13}$OH lines." (Optical depth); "Therefore, the column densities of NH$_2$CHO for most line-rich sources should be taken with care." (Optical depth)
- Limitation (authors): The chemical toy model is a simple approximation assuming constant pre-stellar temperature (10 K) and density evolution; variations in pre-stellar temperature and density could also drive the scatter.  
  > "Therefore, changes in temperatures and densities can also affect the scatter seen in column density ratios. Finally, we emphasize that these conclusions are based on a simple toy model and for more robust conclusions further study is needed." (Observed scatter)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Majority of the molecular fits (HN13CO, CH313CN, NH2CHO, CH3NH2, 13CH3OH, and most CH318OH) rely on subjective 'fit-by-eye' bounding rather than automated MCMC or chi^2 optimization, making exact column densities and error bars somewhat observer-dependent.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The NH2CHO excitation temperature (~250-450 K) is anchored by a single vibrationally excited transition (v12=1, E_up = 476.5 K) that is blended with C2H3CN and potentially infrared-pumped.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming equal beam filling factors when computing column density ratios between cold extended species (CH318OH) and hot compact species (NH2CHO) introduces systematic offsets that depend on unresolved source geometry.
