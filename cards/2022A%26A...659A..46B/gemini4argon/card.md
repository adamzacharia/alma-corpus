## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation, galaxy_clusters_and_sz
- Question: What are the ionisation sources, star formation efficiency, dust and gas properties, and evolutionary origins of the ionised gas discs and filaments in the inner regions of massive lenticular galaxies in the Virgo cluster?  
  > "In this paper, we study the properties of a sample of eight massive (10$^{10}$ $\lesssim$ $M_{star}$ $\lesssim$ 10$^{11}$ M$_{\odot}$) lenticular galaxies with interesting features in the ionised gas distribution such as discs or filaments." (Introduction); "Using our unique set of multifrequency data, we discuss the possible origin of the ionised gas in these objects, which suggests multiple and complex formation scenarios for massive lenticular galaxies in clusters." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and compute moment maps of archival ALMA CO data and derive H2 masses**: Recalibrate raw ALMA data in CASA 4.2.0, image with TCLEAN in CASA 5.8 using Briggs weighting (robust 0.5), compute moment maps with threshold masking in IMMOMENT, and convert integrated CO fluxes to molecular gas masses  
  > "The original raw data were recalibrated with the Common Astronomy Software Applications package (CASA, McMullin et al. 2007), version 4.2.0, and re-imaged with the 5.8 version. The image reconstruction was performed using the standard TCLEAN deconvolution using the Briggs weighting algorithm (robust parameter 0.5)." (ALMA); "The moments were computed in CASA using a mask to cover all pixels under a given threshold to shrink the noise contribution to the final maps, using the CASA task IMMOMENT." (ALMA); "Using the same procedure applied to the CARMA data, and assuming the ratios $^{12}$CO(2-1)/$^{12}$CO(1-0) = $0.65^{0.83}_{0.50}$ and $^{12}$CO(3-2)/$^{12}$CO(1-0) = $0.31^{0.42}_{0.20}$ from Leroy et al. (2021), quite consistent with previous values (e.g. Leroy et al. 2009, Wilson et al. 2012), we estimate total molecular hydrogen masses from the ALMA data." (Cold gas content)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN, IMMOMENT
   - parameter: robust parameter 0.5  
  > "The image reconstruction was performed using the standard TCLEAN deconvolution using the Briggs weighting algorithm (robust parameter 0.5)." (ALMA)
   - assumption: X_CO = 2.3 x 10^20 cm^-2/(K km s^-1) (Strong et al. 1988)  
  > "Notes: $M(H_2)$ are derived assuming a standard CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor $X_{CO}$ = 2.3 $\times$ 10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$/(K km s$^{-1}$) (Strong et al. 1988)" (Table 6)
   - assumption: CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = R_21 = 0.65 and CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) = R_31 = 0.31 (Leroy et al. 2021)  
  > "and an intensity ratio CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = $R_{21}$ = 0.65 and CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) = $R_{31}$ = 0.31 (Leroy et al. 2021)." (Table 6)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison between 12 m and 7 m ALMA data and comparison between ALMA and CARMA data (~0.1 and 0.2 dex typical uncertainty)  
  > "with a typical uncertainty of $\sim$ 0.1 and 0.2 dex, respectively, estimated from the comparison of the 12 m and 7 m ALMA data, or the comparison of the ALMA and CARMA data." (Cold gas content)
2. **Reduce VESTIGE narrow-band Halpha+[NII] images, subtract stellar continuum, and extract integrated fluxes**: Reduce MegaCam images with Elixir-LSB, subtract stellar continuum using r-band and NGVS g-r colour map, and extract fluxes and sky background in elliptical apertures using 1000 random sky displacements  
  > "As for all the VESTIGE data, the images were reduced using Elixir-LSB (Ferrarese et al. 2012), a data reduction pipeline designed to detect extended and low surface brightness features." (VESTIGE narrow-band imaging); "We thus followed the same procedure used for M87, where the stellar continuum was derived using the $r$-band frame combined with a $g-r$ colour map, the latter constructed using the NGVS $g$-band frame." (VESTIGE narrow-band imaging); "fluxes and uncertainties were derived by measuring both the galaxy emission and the sky background within the same elliptical aperture, randomly displaced on the sky after masking other contaminating sources. To minimise the uncertainty on the sky determination, this procedure was run 1000 times." (VESTIGE narrow-band imaging)
   - software: Elixir-LSB
   - parameter: 1000 random sky displacements; central saturated region masked in NGC 4262, 4459, 4526, 4552  
  > "To minimise the uncertainty on the sky determination, this procedure was run 1000 times. In a few galaxies (NGC 4262, 4459, 4526, 4552) the $r$-band images are slightly saturated in the nucleus. For these objects the saturated central region has been masked." (VESTIGE narrow-band imaging)
   - assumption: Poissonian distribution for source photo-electrons  
  > "The uncertainties on the flux counts are derived assuming a Poissonian distribution for the source photo-electrons." (VESTIGE narrow-band imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of VESTIGE Halpha+[NII] flux with MUSE extracted flux gives ratios of 1.05 (NGC 4469) and 1.18 (NGC 4526)  
  > "A comparison of the flux of the H$\alpha$+\[NII\] emission line extracted from the MUSE data cubes with the one obtained from the VESTIGE NB image gives $f(H\alpha +[NII])_{VESTIGE}/f(H\alpha +[NII])_{MUSE}$ = 1.05 for NGC 4469 and $f(H\alpha +[NII])_{VESTIGE}/f(H\alpha +[NII])_{MUSE}$ = 1.18 for NGC 4526." (MUSE spectroscopy)
3. **Extract emission line maps and ratios from MUSE and SOAR spectroscopy to determine gas density, metallicity, and ionisation mechanism**: Fit stellar continuum and nebular emission lines with GANDALF using MILES library; map Balmer decrement, [SII]6716/6731 electron density, O/H metallicity (Curti et al. 2017 calibration), and BPT diagnostic diagrams  
  > "the H$\alpha$ flux derived from the MUSE data has been determined after subtraction of the stellar continuum emission using the GANDALF code." (MUSE spectroscopy); "Each spectrum was fit with the GANDALF code (Sarzi et al. 2006) using the MILES stellar spectral library (Vazdekis et al. 2010) to model the stellar continuum and the nebular line emission." (SOAR spectroscopy); "The MUSE data can also be used to trace the oxygen abundance distribution within the ionised gas disc of NGC 4469 and NGC 4526 (Fig. 10). For this purpose, we use the calibration of Curti et al. (2017) based on the H$\beta$, \[OIII\], \[NII\], and H$\alpha$ lines with a $S/N$ $>$ 5." (Oxygen abundance)
   - software: GANDALF
   - parameter: S/N > 5 for oxygen abundance lines; radial extraction 3.3''-7.5'' (NGC 4429) and 3.3''-7.8'' (NGC 4476) for SOAR  
  > "a pair of 1D spectra were extracted on both sides of the spectral slit at radial distances from the galaxy centres of $3.3"-7.5"$ and $3.3"-7.8"$ for NGC4429 and NGC4476, respectively." (SOAR spectroscopy); "For this purpose, we use the calibration of Curti et al. (2017) based on the H$\beta$, \[OIII\], \[NII\], and H$\alpha$ lines with a $S/N$ $>$ 5." (Oxygen abundance)
   - assumption: Curti et al. (2017) O/H calibration; Osterbrock & Ferland (2006) and Proxauf et al. (2014) for ne from [SII] doublet  
  > "Figure 9 shows that in NGC 4469 the doublet ratio is \[SII\]$\lambda$6716Å/\[SII\]$\lambda$6731Å $\simeq$ 1.2-1.4 (corresponding to $n_e$ $\simeq$ 50-200 cm$^{-3}$, Osterbrock & Ferland 2006; Proxauf et al. 2014)" (Balmer decrement and gas density); "For this purpose, we use the calibration of Curti et al. (2017) based on the H$\beta$, \[OIII\], \[NII\], and H$\alpha$ lines with a $S/N$ $>$ 5." (Oxygen abundance)
   - fitting: GANDALF with MILES stellar spectral library  
  > "Each spectrum was fit with the GANDALF code (Sarzi et al. 2006) using the MILES stellar spectral library (Vazdekis et al. 2010) to model the stellar continuum and the nebular line emission." (SOAR spectroscopy)
   - validation: Comparison of [OIII]5007/Hbeta between ATLAS3D and MUSE in NGC 4526 (0.96+-0.57 vs 0.78+-0.42)  
  > "4526 & 0.96$\pm$0.57, 0.78$\pm$0.42 & 1,2" (Table 5)
4. **Correct Halpha luminosities for [NII] contamination and dust attenuation, derive SFRs, and characterise individual HII regions**: Correct Halpha+[NII] for [NII] and for dust attenuation via Balmer decrement (or A(g) scaling) and Spitzer 24 um emission, convert to SFR, and run HIIPHOT to detect and measure individual HII regions  
  > "H$\alpha$ fluxes can be corrected for dust attenuation using two different methods. The first one uses the Balmer decrement, while the second one uses the recipe of Calzetti et al. (2010) based on the 24$\mu$m $\textit{Spitzer}$/MIPS data" (Star formation activity); "Assuming the Kennicutt (1998a) calibration, a Chabrier (2003) IMF, and a mean value for the attenuation as estimated above, the total star formation rates of these galaxies are 0.02 $\lesssim$ $SFR$ $\lesssim$ 0.15 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$" (Conversion of Halpha luminosities into SFR); "Using the same procedures adopted in Boselli et al. (2020, 2021b) based on the HIIPHOT data reduction pipeline (Thilker et al. 2000), we identify the HII regions and derive their properties in the discs of the galaxies NGC 4429, 4459, 4476, 4477, and 4526." (Physical properties of individual HII regions)
   - software: HIIPHOT
   - parameter: L(Halpha) >= 10^37 erg s^-1, r_eq(Halpha) >= 50 pc, surface brightness limit Sigma(Halpha) = 3 x 10^-17 erg s^-1 cm^-2  
  > "we limit this analysis to the HII regions with $L(H\alpha)$ $\geq$ 10$^{37}$ erg s$^{-1}$ and equivalent radii $r_{eq}(H\alpha)$ $\gtrsim$ 50 pc, defined as the radii of the circles of surface equivalent to the area of the detected HII region down to a surface brightness limit of $\Sigma(H\alpha)$ = 3 $\times$ 10$^{-17}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Physical properties of individual HII regions)
   - assumption: [NII]/Halpha = 0.78 when spectroscopy unavailable; Halpha/Hbeta ~ 5 for NGC 4429 and NGC 4476; A(Halpha) = 3.01 x A(g) for NGC 4459 and 4477; Kennicutt (1998a) calibration and Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "For those galaxies without any available spectroscopic data we thus assume \[NII\]/H$\alpha$ = 0.78." (Star formation activity); "However, given their very similar spectroscopic properties, we assume for these two objects the mean Balmer decrement measured within the discs of NGC 4469 and NGC 4526 (H$\alpha$/H$\beta$ $\simeq$ 5)." (A(Halpha) from the Balmer decrement); "Assuming the Kennicutt (1998a) calibration, a Chabrier (2003) IMF, and a mean value for the attenuation as estimated above, the total star formation rates of these galaxies are 0.02 $\lesssim$ $SFR$ $\lesssim$ 0.15 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$" (Conversion of Halpha luminosities into SFR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency check between Halpha luminosities corrected via Balmer decrement and via 24 um Spitzer/MIPS emission (Fig. 13)  
  > "The H$\alpha$ luminosities derived using the two independent methods are fairly consistent, as indicated in Fig. 13." (A(Halpha) from the 24um Spitzer/MIPS emission)
5. **Measure dust masses from optical A(g) attenuation maps and from UV-to-far-IR CIGALE SED fitting, and compute gas-to-dust ratios**: Subtract 2D stellar models from NGVS g-band images to compute A(g) dust masses; fit stellar and IR SEDs with CIGALE to derive total dust masses; compare radial profiles of convolved Balmer decrement and Herschel PACS 100 um in NGC 4526; compute G/D ratios  
  > "For each object we construct a 2D model of the stellar distribution and subtract it from the NGVS $g$-band optical images (Ferrarese et al. 2012)." (Dust content and distribution); "For each object we construct a 2D model of the stellar distribution and subtract it from the NGVS $g$-band optical images" (Dust content and distribution); "The same set of data can be used to estimate the typical gas-to-dust ratio of these lenticular galaxies and compare it to that of late-type galaxies with similar metallicity." (Gas-to dust ratio)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: 10 photometric bands (FUV to K) for stellar SED; 8 IR bands (WISE 22 um, MIPS 24, 70 um, PACS/SPIRE 100, 160, 250, 350, 500 um) for dust SED; 0.4 kpc elliptical annuli for NGC 4526 radial profile  
  > "we determine the total dust mass by fitting only the spectral bands where the emission is dominated by dust, including WISE at 22 $\mu$m, *Spitzer*/MIPS at 24 and 70 $\mu$m, *Herschel*/PACS and SPIRE at 100, 160, 250, 350, and 500 $\mu$m (8 bands in total)." (Dust content and distribution); "We estimate this contamination by fitting the stellar SED (from the $FUV$ to the $K$-band, in ten photometric bands) of each galaxy again with CIGALE." (Dust content and distribution); "We then extract the radial profile of both the 100 $\mu$m PACS emission and of the convolved $E(B-V)_{BD}$ 2D map in concentric elliptical annuli centred on the nucleus of the galaxy and of size increasing by 0.4 kpc." (Dust content and distribution)
   - assumption: Milky Way extinction curve; Q_ext = 2, rho_dust = 3 g cm^-3, a = 0.1 um; Bruzual & Charlot (2003) models, Chabrier (2003) IMF, Draine & Li (2007) dust models; K_100 = 27.15 cm^2 g^-1, T = 22.6 K for NGC 4526; G/D = 80 for estimating H2 mass when CO unavailable  
  > "where $Q_{ext}$ is the extinction coefficient factor, $\rho_{dust}$ is the dust mass density, and $a$ is the grain radius, adopting the standard values given in the equation." (Dust content and distribution); "We then assume a dust grain opacity at 100 $\mu$m $K_{100}$=27.15 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (Darine 2003) and a dust temperature $T$ = 22.6 K" (Dust content and distribution); "This analysis indicates that we can get a rough estimate of the molecular gas mass of the galaxies NGC 4469 and NGC 4552 from their total dust masses assuming a typical gas-to-dust ratio of $G/D$ = 80" (Gas-to dust ratio)
   - fitting: CIGALE SED fitting  
  > "we compare the total dust masses derived in this way with those calculated by fitting the observed spectral energy distribution of each galaxy with the CIGALE SED fitting code" (Dust content and distribution)
   - validation: Comparison of PACS 100 um radial profile with convolved MUSE Balmer decrement profile in NGC 4526 (Fig. 17); comparison of H2 mass from G/D=80 with CO-derived H2 mass (Fig. 19)  
  > "We then compare this dust radial profile derived from the 100 $\mu$m PACS data to the one derived from the convolved MUSE Balmer decrement in Fig. 17" (Dust content and distribution); "as depicted in Fig. 19, where the molecular gas masses derived using a constant gas-to-dust ratio and those measured using CO data are confronted." (Gas-to dust ratio)
6. **Analyse the spatially resolved Kennicutt-Schmidt relation at ~100 pc scales using ALMA and VESTIGE data**: Convolve ALMA moment 0 and VESTIGE Halpha NB images to matching resolution, rebin to 1.2'' pixels, mask S/N < 5 and central <= 2'' pixels, and compare Sigma_SFR vs Sigma_gas with late-type galaxy relations  
  > "For this purpose, we convolved the ALMA moment 0 images of NGC 4429, NGC 4459, and NGC 4476 to the resolution of the H$\alpha$ NB images (0.70$''$, 0.86$''$, and 0.86$''$, respectively), and the VESTIGE image of NGC 4526 to the resolution of the ALMA moment 0 image (1.14$''$$\times$0.76$''$). We then rebinned the images to 1.2$''$ pixels to have an independent signal in each corresponding position, and removed the low signal-to-noise pixels ($S/N$ $<$ 5)." (Schmidt relation); "We also removed the central ($\lesssim$ 2$''$) pixels which might be contaminated by a nuclear activity or by artefacts due to the continuum subtraction procedure" (Schmidt relation)
   - parameter: matching resolutions: 0.70'' (NGC 4429), 0.86'' (NGC 4459), 0.86'' (NGC 4476), 1.14''x0.76'' (NGC 4526); pixel size 1.2''; S/N >= 5; central <= 2'' masked  
  > "For this purpose, we convolved the ALMA moment 0 images of NGC 4429, NGC 4459, and NGC 4476 to the resolution of the H$\alpha$ NB images (0.70$''$, 0.86$''$, and 0.86$''$, respectively), and the VESTIGE image of NGC 4526 to the resolution of the ALMA moment 0 image (1.14$''$$\times$0.76$''$). (Schmidt relation); "We also removed the central ($\lesssim$ 2$''$) pixels which might be contaminated by a nuclear activity or by artefacts due to the continuum subtraction procedure" (Schmidt relation)
   - assumption: X_CO = 2.3 x 10^20 cm^-2/(K km s^-1), R_21 = 0.65, R_31 = 0.31, and mean dust attenuation corrections  
  > "Notes: $M(H_2)$ are derived assuming a standard CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor $X_{CO}$ = 2.3 $\times$ 10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$/(K km s$^{-1}$) (Strong et al. 1988) and an intensity ratio CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = $R_{21}$ = 0.65 and CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) = $R_{31}$ = 0.31 (Leroy et al. 2021)." (Table 6)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- In six of the eight massive lenticular galaxies, the inner ionised gas is distributed in thin discs (0.7 <= R(Halpha) <= 2.0 kpc) photoionised by young stars, whereas in the two galaxies with filamentary structures (NGC 4262, NGC 4552) and extraplanar filaments of NGC 4469, the gas is shock-ionised.  
  > "we show that while the gas located within these inner discs is photoionised by young stars, signaling ongoing star formation, the gas in the filamentary structures is shock-ionised." (abstract)
- Dust masses derived from optical attenuation maps A(g) are underestimated by a factor of 14-380 compared to UV-to-far-IR SED fitting due to geometrical (sandwich vs screen) effects.  
  > "These dust masses are a factor of 14-380 higher than those derived from dust absorption." (Dust content and distribution); "we conclude that in these lenticular galaxies the observed difference between the dust content estimated from optical absorption features and SED fitting analysis is mainly due to geometrical effects related to the relative distribution of the absorbing dust and the emitting stars." (Dust content and distribution)
- No single mechanism explains the origin of the ionised gas across all eight lenticular galaxies; instead, a combination of outside-in ram pressure stripping and gravitational interactions/mergers is indicated.  
  > "Inspite of the exquisite quality of the multifrequency data in our hand and of the careful analysis carried oou in this work, we have not been able to identify a unique origin for the ionised gas in these eight lenticular galaxies located within the Virgo cluster." (Discussion and conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: At the Virgo cluster distance (16.5 Mpc, 1'' = 80 pc), deep blind VESTIGE Halpha imaging combined with high-resolution ALMA, MUSE, HST, UVIT, and Herschel multifrequency data dissects stars, gas, and dust down to ~100 pc scales.  
  > "At the distance of the cluster (16.5 Mpc, Gavazzi et al. 1999, Mei et al. 2007), 1 arcsec, corresponds to 80 pc, a sufficiently small value for dissecting the different galaxy components (stars, gas, dust) down to small scales unreachable elsewhere." (Introduction); "These molecular gas masses can be compared to those derived using different sets of ALMA data, which have the advantage of being at a much higher angular resolution than the CARMA data." (Cold gas content)
- Why this method: Subtracting stellar continuum via g-r colour maps in narrow-band imaging and via GANDALF in IFU spectra isolates faint nebular emission in bright early-type bulges; fitting only IR bands after subtracting stellar mid-IR contamination in CIGALE avoids bias from the much larger stellar body.  
  > "the subtraction of the stellar continuum is particularly critical in early-type galaxies where the emission of the ionised gas is marginal with respect to that of the stars. We thus followed the same procedure used for M87" (VESTIGE narrow-band imaging); "Given that the dusty disc is located only within the very inner regions compared to the much more extended stellar emission, we determine the total dust mass by fitting only the spectral bands where the emission is dominated by dust" (Dust content and distribution)
- Alternative: Nuclear AGN activity or evolved stars (post-AGB / ISRF) as the dominant ionisation source in the inner discs — rejected based on BPT diagrams and clumpy FUV/Halpha HII regions.  
  > "The analysis presented in this work has clearly shown that in most cases the gas is photoionised by young stars newly formed in the disc." (Discussion and conclusion); "The lack of strong AGNs makes us exclude nuclear activity as the major source of ionisation in all the targets." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Alternative: Diffuse unstructured dust heated by old stars as the main cause of the discrepancy between SED-fitted and optical absorption dust masses — rejected by showing PACS 100 um radial profile matches the convolved Balmer decrement profile of the inner disc in NGC 4526.  
  > "The radial variation of the two profiles are very similar, clearly indicating that the extended 100 $\mu$m emission of NGC 4526 is due to the dust frozen into the circumnuclear ionised gas disc." (Dust content and distribution); "This result thus excludes any major contribution of diffuse dust locked up in the atmosphere of evolved stars which might be missed in the visible 2D dust attenuation maps as the origin of this major discrepancy." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Alternative: Internal stellar mass loss from evolved stars as the sole origin of the ISM — questioned because the observed dust mass is too large compared to expectations from stellar mass loss alone.  
  > "The first hypothesis (gas produced by stellar mass loss), however, is questioned because the amount of associated dust is too low compared to that observed in these early-type systems (Patil et al. 2007)." (Discussion and conclusion)
