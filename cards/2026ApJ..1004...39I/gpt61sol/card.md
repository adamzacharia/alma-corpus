## Analysis recipe

1. **Reimage matched line cubes**: Reanalyze with tclean using the same velocity width, velocity range, and pixel scale for HCN and HCO+.  
  > "We therefore applied “tclean” (Briggs weighting; robust = 0.5, gain = 0.1)" (Targets and Data analysis); "the same velocity range of $\pm$1500 km s$^{-1}$ around each line, and the same pixel scale of 0.02$''$ pixel$^{-1}$, for both HCN and HCO$^{+}$." (Targets and Data analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs weighting; robust = 0.5; gain = 0.1  
  > "We therefore applied “tclean” (Briggs weighting; robust = 0.5, gain = 0.1)" (Targets and Data analysis)
   - parameter: 20 km s−1 velocity width; ±1500 km s−1 around each line; 0.02″ pixel−1  
  > "with the same velocity width (20 km s$^{-1}$ was finally adopted; see Section 3)" (Targets and Data analysis); "the same velocity range of $\pm$1500 km s$^{-1}$ around each line, and the same pixel scale of 0.02$''$ pixel$^{-1}$, for both HCN and HCO$^{+}$." (Targets and Data analysis)
   - assumption: Simultaneous same-J observations provide very accurate relative astrometry, unaffected by absolute pointing uncertainties.  
  > "Because the HCN and HCO$^{+}$ line data at the same J-transition were taken simultaneously, their relative astrometry is regarded as very accurate and is unaffected by possible absolute pointing uncertainties in individual ALMA observations." (Targets and Data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Select usable spaxels and targets**: Require >3σ detections in both lines; retain ULIRGs with more than 25 top-decile ratio spaxels in any available transition.  
  > "all spaxels with $>$3$\sigma$ detections for both HCN and HCO$^{+}$" (Targets and Data analysis); "the number of spaxels in the top 10% of the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios exceeds 25 in any of the available J=2–1, J=3–2, and/or J=4–3 data." (Targets and Data analysis)
   - parameter: >3σ in both HCN and HCO+  
  > "all spaxels with $>$3$\sigma$ detections for both HCN and HCO$^{+}$" (Targets and Data analysis)
   - parameter: Top 10% ratio spaxels >25 in at least one available transition  
  > "the number of spaxels in the top 10% of the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios exceeds 25 in any of the available J=2–1, J=3–2, and/or J=4–3 data." (Targets and Data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: The spaxel requirement is usually met when both moment-0 lines have ≳10σ significance.  
  > "if the detection significance in the integrated-intensity (moment 0) map is $\gtrsim$10$\sigma$ for both the HCN and HCO$^{+}$ emission lines (cite: ima19; ima23b), this numerical requirement for spaxels is usually met." (Targets and Data analysis)
3. **Construct ratio PPV plots**: Plot ratios for all detected spaxels, the top 10% ratios, and ratios >1.5.  
  > "We created position-position-velocity (PPV) plots of the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios" (Summary); "(a) all spaxels with $>$3$\sigma$ detections for both HCN and HCO$^{+}$, (b) spaxels with the top 10% flux ratios (relatively high) (with $>$3$\sigma$ detections)" (Summary); "(c) spaxels with flux ratios $>$1.5 (absolutely high) (with $>$3$\sigma$ detections)." (Summary)
   - parameter: Top 10%: relatively high; >1.5: absolutely high; >3σ in both lines  
  > "(b) spaxels with the top 10% flux ratios (relatively high) (with $>$3$\sigma$ detections)" (Summary); "(c) spaxels with flux ratios $>$1.5 (absolutely high) (with $>$3$\sigma$ detections)." (Summary)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare elevated-ratio geometry with all detected spaxels and compare multiple transitions where available.  
  > "We investigated how the geometry of spaxels with elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios differs from that of all the $>$3$\sigma$-detected spaxels in the PPV plots." (Summary); "The PPV plots at multiple J-transition lines for the same objects enable us to investigate the consistency and/or possible differences in the overall spectral and spatial properties of the elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios." (Targets and Data analysis)
4. **Check PPV pattern stability**: Test coarser and finer velocity resolutions and several viewing angles; adopt 20 km s−1 and a common viewing angle except IRAS 10190+1322.  
  > "we also tested coarser velocity resolutions (e.g., 30 km s$^{-1}$ and 40 km s$^{-1}$)." (Results); "we also tried finer velocity resolutions (e.g., 10 km s$^{-1}$ and even 5 km s$^{-1}$)." (Results); "We thus adopt the same viewing angle for all targets, except for IRAS 10190$+$1322, for which the angle is set to better visualize the north-south spaxel distribution" (Results)
   - parameter: 30 and 40 km s−1; 10 and 5 km s−1 for some bright targets; final 20 km s−1  
  > "we also tested coarser velocity resolutions (e.g., 30 km s$^{-1}$ and 40 km s$^{-1}$)." (Results); "we also tried finer velocity resolutions (e.g., 10 km s$^{-1}$ and even 5 km s$^{-1}$)." (Results); "We therefore display the PPV plots with a velocity resolution of 20 km s$^{-1}$ for all the observed ULIRGs" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Overall patterns do not change significantly with tested velocity resolutions or substantially with viewing angle.  
  > "However, the overall patterns in the PPV plots do not change significantly." (Results); "We also tested several different viewing angles of the PPV plots, but the overall patterns do not change substantially either." (Results)
5. **Classify morphology and possible origins**: Visually classify elevated-ratio PPV geometry as spherical shell, spectrally distinct and spatially compact, or filled; summarize alongside literature integrated ratios and AGN/outflow flags.  
  > "We visually classify these 14 ULIRGs mainly into (i) “spherical shell”, (ii) “spectrally distinct (and spatially compact)”, and (iii) “filled (spectrally filled and spatially compact)”" (ULIRGs with elevated velocity-integrated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios); "Classification of the spectrally and spatially resolved investigation of the elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios." ## Qualitative findings

- IRAS 16090-0139 has clear spherical-shell geometry in all three transitions.  
  > "In particular, for IRAS 16090$-$0139, this “spherical shell” geometry is clearly seen in all the J=2–1, J=3–2, and J=4–3 lines." (Summary)
- Higher-J lines may better probe higher-velocity outflow components in IRAS 16090-0139.  
  > "the spaxels with elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios are extended to higher velocity in the J=4–3 line than in the J=2–1 and J=3–2 lines" (Spherical shell geometry); "suggesting that higher J-transitions lines can better probe higher velocity outflow components than lower J-transition lines." (Spherical shell geometry)
- Some nuclei require possible contributions from both resolved outflows and AGN effects and/or unresolved outflows.  
  > "in addition to spatially resolved molecular outflow activity, AGN effects and/or spatially unresolved outflow activity also play a role" (Spherical shell geometry); "The plus (+) mark means that signatures of both features are seen." (Table 2)
- The origin of elevated ratios in IRAS 00091-0738 and 12112+0305 is unclear because self-absorption obscures velocity structure.  
  > "IRAS 00091$-$0738 and 12112$+$0305 display spectrally distinct (and spatially compact) geometry for all the $>$3$\sigma$-detected spaxels" (Other geometry); "strong absorption dips, due to self-absorption, in the beam-sized and/or nuclear area-integrated spectra hamper reliable investigation of the velocity structure of $>$3$\sigma$-detected spaxels in emission near the systemic velocity." (Summary)

  > "The spectrally and spatially resolved HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios can provide additional information on the physical origin of the elevated flux ratios in nearby ULIRG nuclei, compared to previously conducted spatially integrated and/or velocity-integrated analyses." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ≲0.2″ observations resolve ratio variations within compact ULIRG nuclei.  
  > "We limit the target ULIRGs for our PPV study to those with available $\lesssim$0.2$''$ resolution HCN and HCO$^{+}$ line data (cite: ima19; ima23b)" (Targets and Data analysis); "to investigate the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios in a spatially resolved manner at ULIRG nuclei." (Targets and Data analysis)
- Why this method: Matched velocity widths permit direct spaxel comparison; PPV morphology helps distinguish possible origins of elevated ratios.  
  > "The resulting velocity resolution slightly differs between HCN and HCO$^{+}$ at the same J-transition because of their slightly different rest frequencies." (Targets and Data analysis); "In this way, we can compare the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios on a spectrally and spatially resolved basis in a straightforward and reliable manner." (Targets and Data analysis); "whether AGN effects and/or outflow activity are responsible for elevating the ratios in compact ($\lesssim$1 kpc) nearby ULIRG nuclei" (Introduction)
- Alternative: Top-decile selection alone can select ratios near or below unity; an absolute ≳1.5 selection was added.  
  > "this top 10% criterion does not necessarily reflect truly elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios if the majority of the $>$3$\sigma$-detected spaxels show ratios below or around unity." (Targets and Data analysis); "We therefore investigate also the distribution of (b) spaxels with HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios $\gtrsim$1.5 (i.e., “absolutely” high)." (Targets and Data analysis)
- Alternative: Tested 30 and 40 km s−1 channels, and 10 and 5 km s−1 for some bright targets; retained 20 km s−1 for comparison with (cite: nis24).  
  > "we also tested coarser velocity resolutions (e.g., 30 km s$^{-1}$ and 40 km s$^{-1}$)." (Results); "we also tried finer velocity resolutions (e.g., 10 km s$^{-1}$ and even 5 km s$^{-1}$)." (Results); "We therefore display the PPV plots with a velocity resolution of 20 km s$^{-1}$ for all the observed ULIRGs, to enable straightforward comparison with those in (cite: nis24)." (Results)
- Alternative: AGN effects and compact outflows remain alternative explanations for filled geometry.  
  > "We interpret that AGN effects can explain the elevation." (Summary); "can also be reproduced by spatially confined molecular outflows with not yet clearly separated blueshifted and redshifted velocity components." (Summary)
- How they decided it was enough: Require >25 top-decile spaxels in any available transition; tested resolution and viewing-angle changes leave overall patterns stable.  
  > "the number of spaxels in the top 10% of the HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios exceeds 25 in any of the available J=2–1, J=3–2, and/or J=4–3 data." (Targets and Data analysis); "However, the overall patterns in the PPV plots do not change significantly." (Results); "We also tested several different viewing angles of the PPV plots, but the overall patterns do not change substantially either." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): The >3σ requirement in both lines can miss faint extended outflows.  
  > "spatially extended outflows with faint dense molecular line emission may be missed by our criterion of $>$3$\sigma$ detections for both HCN and HCO$^{+}$ emission lines." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Very compact (≲100 pc) outflows may remain unresolved; the resolved-outflow fraction can be a lower limit.  
  > "very compact outflows ($\lesssim$100 pc) may not be spatially resolved properly with our data at physical resolutions of $\sim$100–500 pc" (Results); "the detected fraction of spatially resolved outflows as the origin of elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios in our PPV analysis can be a lower limit." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): High-velocity outflow components may be too faint for the PPV selection.  
  > "High-velocity outflow components generally show smaller emission line flux than modest-velocity outflow components (cite: cic14; ima17; per18; flu19; lut20; lam22) and thus may not be sufficiently probed with our PPV analysis." (Spherical shell geometry)
- Limitation (authors): Self-absorption prevents reliable recovery of near-systemic emission geometry in some nuclei.  
  > "strong absorption dips, due to self-absorption, in the beam-sized and/or nuclear area-integrated spectra hamper reliable investigation of the velocity structure of $>$3$\sigma$-detected spaxels in emission near the systemic velocity." (Summary)
- Limitation (authors): IRAS 01004-2237, 13509+0442, and 23234+0946 have too few elevated-ratio spaxels to classify.  
  > "For IRAS 01004$-$2237, 13509$+$0442, and 23234$+$0946, the number of spaxels with elevated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios (top 10% and $>$1.5) is too small to discuss their geometry in the PPV plots" (ULIRGs with non-elevated ($\lesssim$1.0) velocity-integrated HCN-to-HCO$^{+}$ flux ratios)
- Limitation (authors): IRAS 09039+0503 SW and NE plots are partially contaminated by the other nucleus.  
  > "The PPV plots of both the IRAS 09039$+$0503 SW and NE nuclei are displayed, but each plot is partially contaminated by line emission from the other nucleus." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): Only the brighter primary nucleus is analyzed in the three systems marked β.  
  > "Only the brighter primary nucleus is investigated, because the secondary nucleus is too faint in dense molecular line emission to allow a meaningful PPV analysis." (Table 1)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Pixels within a synthesized beam are correlated; the >25-spaxel requirement does not specify an independent-resolution-element requirement.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Unstated beam matching could affect spatially resolved ratios.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Joint detection thresholds and ratio ranking can bias which low-surface-brightness structures survive.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Shell morphology supports an outflow interpretation but does not uniquely establish it.
