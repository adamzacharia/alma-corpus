## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare CO integrated-intensity maps**: Use AlFoCS moment-zero maps; cleaned CO cubes were converted to primary-beam-corrected maps with the masked moment method.  
  > "The molecular gas data used are $^{12}$CO(1-0) integrated intensity (moment zero) maps from AlFoCS." (CO data); "The cleaned data cubes were used to produce primary beam corrected moment maps of the CO(1-0) line emission using the masked moment method (cite: Dame2011)." (CO data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Interactive CLEAN; natural weighting  
  > "CLEANed (interactively, using a natural weighting scheme)" (CO data)
   - parameter: 10 km s−1 channels; FCC207: 2 km s−1  
  > "Channel widths are 10 ${\rm km}~{\rm s}^{-1}$ for most galaxies, and 2 ${\rm km}~{\rm s}^{-1}$ for the dwarf FCC207, because of its narrow linewidth." (CO data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract extinction-corrected Hα**: Reduce MUSE spectra; fit stellar and ionised-gas contributions simultaneously in each spaxel with GandALF and MILES templates.  
  > "Data reduction was performed using the MUSE pipeline (version 1.6.2, (cite: Weilbacher2012; Weilbacher2016)) under the ESOREFLEX environment (cite: Freudling2013)." (H$\alpha$ data); "the Gas and Absorption Line Fitting code (GandALF, (cite: Sarzi2006; Barroso2006)) was used to perform a spaxel-by-spaxel fit, simultaneously for both the stellar and ionised gas contributions." (H$\alpha$ data); "The full extent of the MILES library was used to decrease the impact of template-mismatch on the emission line measurement." (H$\alpha$ data)
   - software: MUSE pipeline, ESOREFLEX, GandALF
   - assumption: Two reddening components: entire spectrum and nebular emission; Calzetti extinction law for both  
  > "the GandALF fit included two reddening components, a first affecting the entire spectrum" (H$\alpha$ data); "a second which affects only the nebular emission" (H$\alpha$ data); "A Calzetti (cite: Calzetti1994) extinction law was used in both cases." (H$\alpha$ data)
   - fitting: Simultaneous spaxel-by-spaxel stellar and ionised-gas fit  
  > "was used to perform a spaxel-by-spaxel fit, simultaneously for both the stellar and ionised gas contributions." (H$\alpha$ data)
   - validation: Full MILES library used to reduce template-mismatch effects  
  > "The full extent of the MILES library was used to decrease the impact of template-mismatch on the emission line measurement." (H$\alpha$ data)
3. **Construct matched SFR surface-density maps**: Convert Balmer-decrement-corrected Hα flux to luminosity and SFR; divide by spaxel area; classify ionisation; convolve and regrid to ALMA.  
  > "SFR maps are obtained by calculating star formation rates from the Balmer decrement corrected H$\alpha$ maps" (Star formation rate surface densities); "The SFR is then divided by the spaxel area in kpc$^2$ to obtain the SFR surface density" (Star formation rate surface densities); "Finally, the image is convolved to match the ALMA beam and regridded to the resolution of the ALMA images." (Star formation rate surface densities)
   - parameter: L(Hα) = 4πD²F(Hα); SFR = L(Hα)/(1.86 × 10^41)  
  > "\text{SFR} \left( M_\odot\ \text{yr}^{-1} \right) = \frac{L \left( \text{H}_\alpha \right)}{1.86 \times 10^{41}}," (Star formation rate surface densities); "L \left( \text{H}_\alpha \right) = 4 \pi D^2 F \left( \text{H}_\alpha \right)," (Star formation rate surface densities)
   - parameter: Hα and BPT diagnostic lines require S/N > 3  
  > "Only spaxels with H$\alpha$ detections (S/N $>$ 3) are considered." (Star formation rate surface densities); "Only spaxels with sufficient S/N ($>$3) in these lines to reliably determine the nature of the H$\alpha$ emission were included in the maps)." (Star formation rate surface densities)
   - parameter: BPT: [OIII]λ5007/Hβ and [NII]λ6583/Hα; Kauffmann2003 star-forming boundary; Kewley2001 composite upper boundary  
  > "using the \[OIII\]$\lambda5007$/H$\beta$ and \[NII\]$\lambda 6583$/H$\alpha$ line ratios." (Star formation rate surface densities); "The boundary line from (cite: Kauffmann2003) was used as a cut-off to define which pixels contain star-forming H$\alpha$, and the one from (cite: Kewley2001) as an upper limit for composites." (Star formation rate surface densities)
   - assumption: 20 Mpc common distance  
  > "we adopt the distance to the Fornax cluster (20 Mpc, (cite: Blakeslee2009)) as a common distance to all galaxies." (Introduction)
   - assumption: No inclination correction  
  > "here we do not apply this correction, both due to the disturbed nature of some of the sources" (Star formation rate surface densities)
   - assumption: Pure star-forming Hα gives a lower SFR limit; adding composite regions gives an upper limit  
  > "Since emission from composite regions is only partly due to star formation, including these regions allows us to set an upper limit to the total star formation rate of each system." (Star formation rate surface densities); "A corresponding lower limit can be set by removing these spaxels entirely." (Star formation rate surface densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat analysis with star-forming-only and star-forming-plus-composite Hα  
  > "we consider two cases throughout this paper: one where only the spaxels strictly dominated by star formation are taken into account, and one where spaxels classified as “composite” are included also." (Star formation rate surface densities)
4. **Convert CO intensity to molecular-gas surface density**: Derive H2 column density from integrated CO flux and X_CO; multiply by 2m_H and convert cm−2 to pc−2.  
  > "N_{\text{H}_2} \left( \text{mol.\ cm}^{-2} \right) = X_{\text{CO}}\ \frac{\lambda^2}{2 k_{\text{B}}} \int S_\nu\  d\nu," (Molecular gas surface densities); "is multiplied by twice the mass of a hydrogen atom ($2\ m_\text{H}$, in $M_\odot$) and converted from cm$^{-2}$ to pc$^{-2}$." ## Qualitative findings

- Fornax relation is close to K98 and B08 but towards shorter B08 depletion times  
  > "The overall star formation relation in the Fornax cluster is close to those found by K98 and B08, but lies towards the short-depletion time end of the B08 relation." (Summary)
- Disturbed dwarf galaxies largely drive the short-depletion-time shift  
  > "This is mostly driven by dwarf galaxies with disturbed molecular gas reservoirs" (Summary)
- FCC184 composite-region Hα likely contains substantial star formation  
  > "This implies that star formation contributes significantly to the “composite” spaxels in this galaxy." (Combined $\Sigma_{{\text{H}_2}} - \Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$ relations)
- FCC290 cloud destruction is likely driven by photoionisation or stellar winds  
  > "cloud destruction is likely driven by early feedback mechanisms in FCC290, such as photoionisation or stellar winds." (Application to FCC290)
- Stellar mass appears a better predictor of cluster depletion times than SMSD  
  > "For this reason, galaxy stellar mass appears to be a better predictor of depletion times in the cluster than galaxy morphology as probed by their SMSD." (Relation with stellar mass surface density)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use the 9 galaxies with both AlFoCS CO and F3D Hα maps  
  > "Our sample consists of all Fornax cluster galaxies for which both CO and H$\alpha$ maps are available, from AlFoCS and F3D, respectively." (The sample); "There are 9 overlapping galaxies in these surveys." (The sample)
- Why this method: Beam-sized bins avoid interpreting unresolved structure; all grid origins address arbitrary bin placement  
  > "To avoid over-interpreting the data by studying these maps on smaller scales, both images are binned to the size of the ALMA beam." (Obtaining the $\Sigma_{{\text{H}_2}} - \Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$ relation and depletion times); "To account for this, we create a separate map for each possible starting point, effectively shifting the grid up/down and sideways until each possibility is covered." (Obtaining the $\Sigma_{{\text{H}_2}} - \Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$ relation and depletion times)
- Alternative: Analyse both pure star-forming and star-forming-plus-composite Hα maps  
  > "we consider two cases throughout this paper: one where only the spaxels strictly dominated by star formation are taken into account, and one where spaxels classified as “composite” are included also." (Star formation rate surface densities)
- Alternative: Do not apply inclination corrections to surface-density maps  
  > "here we do not apply this correction, both due to the disturbed nature of some of the sources" (Star formation rate surface densities); "we are only comparing surface density maps within individual galaxies, in which case this correction cancels out." (Star formation rate surface densities)
- Alternative: Alternative CO conversion factors change results only marginally  
  > "our results change only marginally when a different conversion factor is adopted, not affecting a comparison with their result." (Molecular gas surface densities)
- Alternative: FCC184 composite-inclusive relation considered more realistic  
  > "We therefore consider the situation where H$\alpha$ originating from composite regions is taken into account, in this case, more realistic." (Combined $\Sigma_{{\text{H}_2}} - \Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$ relations)
- Alternative: 13λ filter width balances diffuse-emission removal against compact-source artefacts  
  > "the best compromise between a selective filter to filter a maximum of the diffuse emission and a broader filter to limit the appearance of artefacts around compact structures" (Uncertainty principle for star formation)
- How they decided it was enough: Diffuse-filter convergence: λ within 5% for four iterations; peak-count and spatial-resolution checks for lifecycle fits  
  > "defined as obtaining the same value of $\lambda$ to within 5 per cent for four successive iterations" (Uncertainty principle for star formation); "a minimum of 35 peaks in each map ensures a precision better than 50%." (Application to FCC290); "This is sufficient to bring a strong constraint on $t_{\rm CO}$, but marginal for $t_{\rm fb}$ and $\lambda$" (Application to FCC290)
- Limitation (authors): Sample biased towards higher-mass, star-forming galaxies  
  > "As F3D targeted only the brightest galaxies ($m_B < 15$), and AlFoCS is mostly IR selected, the sample is likely biased towards higher-mass, star forming galaxies." (The sample)
- Limitation (authors): Six Paper I detections lack inclusion in this study  
  > "6 galaxies detected in Paper I are not included here: the large spiral FCC121 (NGC1365), the edge-on spiral FCC67 (NGC1351A), and four dwarf galaxies with disturbed molecular gas reservoirs." (The sample)
- Limitation (authors): FCC290 has only partial MUSE coverage  
  > "An exception is FCC290, which was only observed partially" (H$\alpha$ data)
- Limitation (authors): BPT boundaries and composite Hα contributions are uncertain  
  > "there is a level of uncertainty in the exact dividing line between spaxels dominated by ionisation as a result of star formation, and other ionising sources" (Star formation rate surface densities)
- Limitation (authors): FCC312 MUSE mosaic gap causes a local depletion-time overestimate  
  > "a small fraction of the galaxy was not covered by the mosaic, resulting in an overestimation of the depletion time here." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): FCC290 feedback lifetime and separation may be biased by insufficient resolution and blending  
  > "marginal for $t_{\rm fb}$ and $\lambda$, which therefore might be slightly biased because of the insufficient spatial resolution" (Application to FCC290); "The above indications of region blending imply that our measurement of $t_{\rm fb}$ may be overestimated" (Application to FCC290)
- Limitation (authors): Poor statistics and projected distances prevent isolating the cluster-location effect  
  > "However, it is difficult to draw conclusions with the poor statistics and lower limits on the distance from the BCG." (Relation with cluster-centric distance)
- Limitation (authors): Comparisons with B08 differ in CO transition, conversion factor, SFR tracer, sample and resolution  
  > "we use CO(1-0) as a tracer for the molecular gas rather than CO(2-1), used for most of the B08 sample." (The star formation relation in the Fornax cluster); "we use H$\alpha$ as a star formation tracer rather than UV + IR, used by B08." (The star formation relation in the Fornax cluster); "the resolution used by B08 is still a factor $\sim$2 worse than the resolution used here" (The star formation relation in the Fornax cluster)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 20 Mpc general distance and 17.7 Mpc HEISENBERG distance need reconciliation before comparing derived spatial scales.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): FCC167 is described as having depletion times mostly above 10 Gyr in the main results but mostly 1–2 Gyr in its appendix discussion; do not use either as an unambiguous answer key.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All shifted-grid points reuse the same data and are not independent resolution elements.
