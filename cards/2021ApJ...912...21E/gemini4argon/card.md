## Science

- Fields: transients, star_formation
- Question: Search for late-time non-thermal synchrotron radio and millimeter emission from superluminous supernovae (SLSNe) and long gamma-ray bursts (LGRBs) to constrain central engines (magnetar wind nebulae, off-axis relativistic jets), non-relativistic ejecta, fast radio bursts, and obscured star formation in host galaxies.  
  > "We present the largest and deepest late-time radio and millimeter survey to date of superluminous supernovae (SLSNe) and long duration gamma-ray bursts (LGRBs) to search for associated non-thermal synchrotron emission." (Abstract); "Here we present deep late-time radio and mm observations of 43 SLSNe and LGRBs to search for non-thermal synchrotron emission from these sources, which may shed light on their central engines, connection to FRBs, slow ejecta, and host galaxies." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract continuum flux densities and image rms values from VLA and ALMA images**: Use archival ALMA pipeline continuum images (and CASA-imaged VLA maps) and extract flux densities and rms noise at transient positions using pwkit imtool.  
  > "In all cases, we use the ALMA data products, which utilize standard imaging techniques within CASA." (ALMA Observations); "Flux densities and image rms values were obtained using the `imtool` program as part of the `pwkit` package." (ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA, pwkit (imtool)
   - parameter: 3sigma upper limits for non-detections; 5sigma detection threshold  
  > "No clear radio emission (SNR $> 5 \sigma$) is detected from the remainder of the SLSNe in our sample, or from the location of any of the LGRBs in our sample, with typical rms values of $5-10$ $\mu$Jy." (VLA Continuum Observations); "Table notes: Limits correspond to $3\sigma$." (Table 4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Relative astrometry between Liverpool Telescope and Pan-STARRS1 3pi images to test whether the 3sigma millimeter source near SN2007bi is associated with the SN or host galaxy.  
  > "We first perform relative astrometry between the PS1/$3\pi$ image and the Liverpool image and find an astrometric tie uncertainty of $\sigma_{\rm host}\approx 0.6''$." (ALMA Observations)
2. **Search Phased-VLA observations for fast radio bursts (FRBs)**: Perform RFI excision with rfifind, incoherently dedisperse filterbank time series across 1000 trial DMs, remove red noise, and search scans with matched-filtering single_pulse_search.py.  
  > "We performed a standard RFI search using the `rfifind` routine in `PRESTO` (cite: Ransom2001) with two second integration times." (Phased VLA Observations); "For each source, we incoherently dedispersed the data at $1000$ trial DMs ranging up to $\rm DM = 5000 \ \rm pc \ cm^{-3}$ with a step size of $5$" (Phased VLA Observations); "Individual scans are then searched for FRBs using the matched-filtering algorithm `single_pulse_search.py` (cite: Ransom2001)." (Phased VLA Observations)
   - software: PRESTO (rfifind, single_pulse_search.py)
   - parameter: DM up to 5000 pc cm^-3 with step size 5; 256 us time resolution; 2 MHz channels; 7sigma threshold  
  > "The phased-array data were recorded with 256 $\mu$s time resolution and 2 MHz channels with 2 GHz total bandwidth." (Phased VLA Observations); "For each source, we incoherently dedispersed the data at $1000$ trial DMs ranging up to $\rm DM = 5000 \ \rm pc \ cm^{-3}$ with a step size of $5$" (Phased VLA Observations)
   - assumption: Phasing efficiency factor of 0.9, nominal pulse width W = 10 ms, and beaming fraction f_b = 0.5  
  > "Assuming a phasing efficiency factor of $0.9$ and a nominal $10$ ms pulse width, we find a minimum detectable flux density of $S_{\rm min}\approx 16$ mJy ($7\sigma$) for our observations." (Phased VLA Observations); "where we assume a beaming fraction $f_b = 0.5$." (Phased VLA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Constrain obscured star formation rates in host galaxies and stack images**: Calculate obscured SFR limits from 6 GHz and 100 GHz flux density limits using the k-corrected radio-SFR relation, and median-combine images centered on transient positions to constrain mean population SFRs.  
  > "We place limits on the obscured SFRs in the host galaxies of our sample using the expression from (cite: Greiner2016), which extrapolates the 1.4 GHz derived SFR of (cite: Murphy2011b) assuming a power law $F_\nu \propto \nu^{\alpha}$ and accounting for proper $k$-corrections" (Obscured Star Formation); "We median-combine the images after centering on the transient position." (Obscured Star Formation)
   - parameter: Spectral index alpha = 0.75; median redshifts z approx 0.3 (LGRBs) and z approx 0.23 (SLSNe) for stacked limits  
  > "We adopt $\alpha = 0.75$ for the spectral index (cite: Condon1992)." (Obscured Star Formation); "For the SLSNe, the $3\sigma$ SFR limits are $\lesssim 1.7$ and $\lesssim 27$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ based on the 6 GHz ($z \approx 0.23$) and 100 GHz ($z \approx 0.23$) images, respectively." (Obscured Star Formation)
   - assumption: Planck cosmological parameters: H_0 = 67.8 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.308, Omega_Lambda = 0.692  
  > "Throughout the paper, we use the latest Planck cosmological parameters, $H_0 = \rm 67.8 \ km \ s^{-1} \ Mpc^{-1}$, $\Omega_m = 0.308$, and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.692$ (cite: Planck2016)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare radio SFR of PTF12dam (6.7 +/- 0.7 M_sun yr^-1) with previous 3 GHz and Halpha SFR measurements (4.8 M_sun yr^-1).  
  > "In the case of PTF12dam, our radio detection of the host galaxy corresponds to an SFR of $6.7\pm 0.7 \ \rm M_{\odot} \ yr^{-1}$, comparable to the value inferred from a previous 3 GHz detection ($4.8\pm 0.2 \ \rm M_{\odot} \ yr^{-1}$; (cite: Hatsukade2018)) and an H$\alpha$ measurement, $4.8\pm 1 \ \rm M_{\odot} \ yr^{-1}$ (cite: Perley2016a)." (Obscured Star Formation)
4. **Constrain non-relativistic SN ejecta outflows**: Place limits on shock velocity and progenitor mass-loss rate for SLSNe using Chevalier (1998) synchrotron self-absorption model, and model spherical SN outflow light curves for LGRBs using Barniol Duran & Giannios (2015) and Kathirgamaraju et al. (2016).  
  > "In the context of a shock interaction from the non-relativistic, quasi-spherical SN ejecta, we place limits on the shock velocity and mass-loss rate for each SLSN following the prescription of (cite: Chevalier1998) and assuming that the date of each observation corresponds to the peak luminosity" (Supernovae Ejecta); "For the LGRBs in our sample, we place constraints on emission from associated supernovae following the prescriptions of (cite: Barniol2015) and (cite: Kath2016)." (Supernovae Ejecta)
   - parameter: For LGRB SNe: epsilon_e = epsilon_B = 0.1, p = 2.5, E_SN = 10^52 erg, v_ej = 8 x 10^3 km s^-1, n = 10 and 100 cm^-3  
  > "We assume typical values of $\epsilon_e = \epsilon_B = 0.1$ and $p=2.5$." (Supernovae Ejecta); "In Figure [4](#fig:grb_sn), we plot representative light curves for external number densities of $n=10$ and $n=100 \ \rm cm^{-3}$ and a fiducial supernova energy of $10^{52}$ erg, typical of broad-line Ic SNe associated with GRBs (cite: Mazzali2014)." (Supernovae Ejecta); "We further fix the ejecta velocity to $8 \times 10^{3} \ \rm km \ s^{-1}$, consistent with the median observed nebular phase velocity for several broad-line Ic SNe" (Supernovae Ejecta)
   - assumption: Peak luminosity extrapolates back in time as L_nu,pk proportional to t^-1 for optically thin SLSN emission  
  > "We also consider the possibility that the emission from each event may have peaked at earlier times, by extrapolating the peak luminosity back in time assuming $L_{\nu,\rm pk} \propto t^{-1}$ (cite: Berger2002), and assuming that the emission is optically thin at the time of observations." (Supernovae Ejecta)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Constrain off-axis relativistic jets and minimum jet breakout energies**: Generate a grid of 6 and 100 GHz off-axis jet afterglow light curves using Boxfit v2 to determine ruled-out (E_iso, n) space, and compute minimum jet breakout energy E_min from optical light curve kinetic energies E_K derived with MOSFiT.  
  > "To constrain the presence of off-axis jets, we produce a grid of afterglow models for a range of jet energies and CSM densities using the two-dimensional relativistic hydrodynamical code `Boxfit v2` (cite: vanEerten2012)." (Off-axis Relativistic Jets); "For each SLSN in our sample, we therefore estimate the minimal energy for jet breakout to determine whether our non-detections can constrain the theoretical predictions." (Off-axis Relativistic Jets)
   - software: Boxfit v2, MOSFiT
   - parameter: theta_j = 10 deg; theta_obs = 30, 60, 90 deg; epsilon_e = 0.1; epsilon_B = 0.01; p = 2.5; f_j = 0.5  
  > "We model the light curves assuming a jet opening angle of $\theta_j = 10^\circ$ and viewing angles of $\theta_{\rm obs} = 30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$." (Off-axis Relativistic Jets); "We further assume a constant density CSM and jet microphysical parameters of $\epsilon_e = 0.1$, $\epsilon_B = 0.01$, and $p=2.5$" (Off-axis Relativistic Jets); "We assume $f_j = 0.5$ and adopt the inferred $E_K$ values for each source based on fits to the optical light curves." (Off-axis Relativistic Jets)
   - assumption: Constant density CSM; gamma_j = 1/(5 theta_j)  
  > "where $E_K$ is the initial kinetic energy of the SN, $\gamma_j = 1/5 \theta_j$ as per (cite: Mizuta2013), and $f_j$ is the fraction of energy contributing to a collimated jet (cite: Margalit2018a)." (Off-axis Relativistic Jets)
   - fitting: Markov chain Monte Carlo fits to multicolor optical light curves using MOSFiT for magnetar/ejecta parameters.  
  > "The parameters are derived via Markov chain Monte Carlo fits to the multicolor light curves using `MOSFiT`, an analytic Python-based modular code designed to model a variety of transients." (Magnetar Wind Nebulae)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Calculate free-free transparency timescales and model magnetar wind nebulae (ion-electron and electron-positron)**: Compute free-free optical depth evolution at 6 and 100 GHz using CLOUDY, then compare radio/mm limits against ion-electron wind models (Margalit & Metzger 2018) and electron-positron PWN Boltzmann-equation models (Omand et al. 2018; Murase et al. 2016, 2020).  
  > "Using the photoionization code `CLOUDY` (cite: Ferland2013), we compute the time-dependent evolution of the temperature and ionization structure of the ejecta assuming that some fraction of the spin-down power of the magnetar is deposited into ionizing radiation." (Magnetar Wind Nebulae); "Next, we constrain the presence of nascent magnetar nebulae in our sources following two unique prescriptions." (Magnetar Wind Nebulae); "The radio models solve the Boltzmann equation for photons and electron/positrons in the PWN over all photon frequencies and electron energies (cite: Murase2015)" (Electron-Positron Wind)
   - software: CLOUDY, MOSFiT
   - parameter: Broken power-law injection spectrum with q_1 = 1.5, q_2 = 2.5, and peak Lorentz factor gamma_b = 10^5 (and 10^4 for PTF10hgi)  
  > "The electron-positron injection spectrum is assumed to be a broken power law with injection spectral indices of $q_1=1.5$ and $q_2=2.5$ and a peak Lorentz factor of $\gamma_{b} = 10^{5}$" (Electron-Positron Wind); "In Figure [5](#fig:10hgi_gb1e4) we plot the same data and models, but with a peak Lorentz factor of $\gamma_b = 10^4$ instead of $10^5$; we find these lightcurves are more consistent with the data." (Electron-Positron Wind)
   - assumption: Energy injection rate L proportional to t^-2 in CLOUDY; singly-ionized oxygen ejecta for free-free absorption in electron-positron PWN model; no ejecta feedback  
  > "Assuming that the energy injection rate into the nebula is given by $L \propto t^{-2}$, we find $t_{\rm ff}\approx 1.3 - 16.7$ yr at 6 GHz and $\approx 1.2 - 4.6$ yr at 100 GHz." (Magnetar Wind Nebulae); "In this work, we modify the spin-down formula and neutron star masses, and ignore the effects of ejecta feedback for consistency with the `MOSFiT` models." (Electron-Positron Wind); "Free-free absorption in the ejecta is calculated assuming a singly-ionized oxygen ejecta, and we do not consider absorption outside the ejecta, as in (cite: Omand2018) and (cite: Law2019)." (Electron-Positron Wind)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested electron-positron PWN models against multi-frequency (0.6 - 100 GHz) observations of PTF10hgi for both gamma_b = 10^5 and gamma_b = 10^4.  
  > "For PTF10hgi, we plot the light curves over the range $0.6 - 100$ GHz in Figure [4](#fig:10hgi)." (Electron-Positron Wind); "In Figure [5](#fig:10hgi_gb1e4) we plot the same data and models, but with a peak Lorentz factor of $\gamma_b = 10^4$ instead of $10^5$; we find these lightcurves are more consistent with the data." (Electron-Positron Wind)

## Qualitative findings

- No late-time radio (6 GHz) or millimeter (100 GHz) emission is detected from the transient positions of 43 SLSNe and LGRBs, aside from the previously reported PTF10hgi and host-galaxy emission from PTF12dam.  
  > "We do not detect radio/mm emission from any of the sources, with the exception of a 6 GHz detection of PTF10hgi (cite: Eftekhari2019), as well as the detection of 6 GHz emission near the location of the SLSN PTF12dam, which we associate with its host galaxy." (Abstract)
- There is no evidence for significant dust-obscured star formation in the majority of SLSN and LGRB host galaxies.  
  > "We find no evidence for significant dust obscuration within the host galaxies." (Conclusions)
- Radio and mm upper limits rule out ion-electron magnetar wind nebulae with identical parameters to FRB 121102, but are mostly consistent with scaled-down models tuned to PTF10hgi (with the notable exception of SN2018bsz at 100 GHz).  
  > "Our limits are sufficient to rule out the presence of ion-electron magnetar nebulae with the same parameters invoked for FRB 121102, as they probe luminosities roughly an order of magnitude deeper." (Conclusions); "We find that the majority of our limits are consistent with the model light curves invoked for PTF10hgi, although several deep limits at 100 GHz, most notably SN2018bsz with $L_\nu = 7.6\times 10^{26} \rm \ erg \ s^{-1} \ Hz^{-1}$, strongly rule out the presence of such nebulae." (Ion-Electron Wind)
- In electron-positron PWN models, predicted peak radio luminosity is strongly anti-correlated with the magnetar magnetic field B, and the few sources ruled out by current limits are exclusively low-B (B <= 10^14 G) events.  
  > "We find a strong anti-correlation between the peak luminosity and magnetic field, i.e., the more luminous events correspond to lower magnetic fields." (Electron-Positron Wind); "Indeed, the five sources with predicted emission at or above the level of our $3\sigma$ limits correspond to exclusively low-$B$ ($B\lesssim 10^{14}$ G) events." (Electron-Positron Wind)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Volume-limited VLA sample (z <= 0.35, >= 5 yr post-explosion) and complete accessible ALMA samples (z <= 0.5, >= 3 yr post-explosion in 2017; Decl < 30 deg, z < 0.4 in 2019) to probe late-time synchrotron emission when ejecta become optically thin and off-axis jets decelerate.  
  > "The SLSNe observed with the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) represent a complete (as of February 2017) volume-limited sample to a distance twice that of FRB 121102 ($z\lesssim 0.35$), and with a timescale of $\gtrsim 5$ years post-explosion, leading to 15 SLSNe." (Sample Selection); "Events observed during the first campaign (2017.1.00280.S; PI: Berger) constitute all sources accessible to ALMA (as of March 2017) with a timescale of $\gtrsim 3$ years post-explosion and a distance of $z\lesssim 0.5$." (Sample Selection)
- Why this method: Combined 6 GHz and 100 GHz observations probe both GHz-peaking late-time afterglows/nebulae and earlier-peaking millimeter nebulae that experience lower free-free optical depth.  
  > "In each case, the emission is expected to peak in the millimeter on timescales of a few years, and in the GHz regime on timescales of $\gtrsim 10$ yr." (Ion-Electron Wind)
- Alternative: Association of the 3sigma 100 GHz source (55.3 +/- 18.8 uJy) with SN2007bi or its host galaxy was rejected based on astrometric offsets of 1 +/- 0.6 arcsec and 1.5 +/- 0.6 arcsec.  
  > "We find that the millimeter source is offset from the SN position by $1\pm 0.6''$ and is thus unlikely to be related to the SN." (ALMA Observations); "We thus conclude that the weak ALMA detection is unlikely to be related to the SN or the host galaxy." (ALMA Observations)
- Alternative: Association of the 6 GHz emission near PTF12dam with the SN itself was rejected in favor of host galaxy star formation because the radio emission is offset by ~1 arcsec, traces optical host emission, and is resolved out in A-configuration.  
  > "The radio emission is offset from the SN position by $\sim 1$ arcsecond and is instead centered on, and traces, the optical emission from the host galaxy. Thus the emission is most likely related to star formation in the host galaxy" (VLA Continuum Observations)
- Alternative: Marginal 6 GHz detections near SN2009jh (3.6sigma, offset 1.6 arcsec) and GRB 050826 (2.5sigma, offset 1.0 arcsec) were rejected as unlikely to be related to the transients.  
  > "In the case of SN2009jh, the source significance is $3.6\sigma$ ($F_\nu = 23.3\pm 6.4 \ \mu$Jy) but with an offset of $1.6\pm 0.3''$ from the SN position, and we therefore consider it unlikely to be related to the SN." (VLA Continuum Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Short phased-VLA observation duration (~40 min per source) is prohibitively small for detecting intermittent FRBs.  
  > "Thus, while we are sensitive to the low energy range of FRBs (from FRB 121102), given the intermittent nature of FRB 121102, the short duration of our observations ($\approx 40$ min per source) is prohibitively small." (Phased VLA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA 100 GHz observations are less constraining for obscured star formation rates and late-time off-axis jet afterglows because synchrotron emission peaks at lower (GHz) frequencies on these timescales.  
  > "For the remaining SLSNe in our sample, the radio-inferred SFR limits exceed the optically-inferred rates by factors of $\approx 2-5.2\times 10^3$ (Table [\[tab:master\]](#tab:master)), where the high end is driven primarily by the ALMA 100 GHz observations, which are less constraining in this context." (Obscured Star Formation); "Because the peak of the afterglow emission is generically at GHz frequencies on such long timescales, our mm limits can accommodate higher jet energies of $E_{\rm iso}\gtrsim 10^{54}$ erg." (Off-axis Relativistic Jets)
- Limitation (authors): CLOUDY models start at t > 1 yr in the source frame, so 100 GHz free-free transparency timescales shorter than 1 yr (1+z) rely on extrapolations.  
  > "Finally, we report upper limits on $t_{\rm ff, 100GHz}$ for eight sources where $t_{\rm ff, 100GHz} > 1 \rm{yr}$ $(1+z)$, as the `CLOUDY` models start from $t > 1$ yr in the source frame and these results are thus based on extrapolations." (Magnetar Wind Nebulae)
- Limitation (authors): Seven ALMA-observed SLSNe (LSQ14bdq, SN2015bn, SN2016ard, SN2017jan, SN2018ibb, SN2018hti, SN2018lfe) are expected to still be optically thick to free-free absorption at 100 GHz at the time of observation.  
  > "SN2011kg and LSQ12dlf are expected to be optically thick at the time of our 6 GHz observations, while LSQ14bdq, SN2015bn, SN2016ard, SN2017jan, SN2018ibb, SN2018hti, and SN2018lfe are expected to be optically thick in our 100 GHz observations." (Magnetar Wind Nebulae)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The obscured SFR relation used at 100 GHz extrapolates a 1.4 GHz synchrotron power-law (alpha = 0.75) without modeling thermal dust emission, which begins to contribute at 100 GHz.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA beam sizes vary by more than an order of magnitude between the 2017 campaign (~0.25-0.8 arcsec) and the 2019 campaign (~1.5-3.8 arcsec), affecting spatial filtering in host galaxy SFR comparisons and image stacking.
