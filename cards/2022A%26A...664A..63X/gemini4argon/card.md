## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure CO(3-2) and 3.2 mm dust continuum source sizes**: Fit an elliptical Gaussian to visibility CO(3-2) data in the UV plane using uvmodelfit, and perform 2D elliptical Gaussian fits on high-resolution Moment 0 maps of CO(3-2) and 3.2 mm continuum in the image plane  
  > "To avoid uncertainties from the imaging process, we derived the source sizes with the visibility CO(3$-$2) data in the $UV$ plane by fitting an elliptical Gaussian (task $uvmodelfit$)." (Structural properties of the molecular gas); "As a comparison, we also performed a two-dimensional (2D) elliptical Gaussian fit on the high-resolution $Moment~0$ map of CO(3$-$2)." (Structural properties of the molecular gas); "The dust size of the SB1 was measured by a 2D elliptical Gaussian fit on the high-resolution $Moment~0$ map at 3.2 mm." (Structural properties of the molecular gas)
   - software: CASA, uvmodelfit
   - assumption: Lack of significant size variation between CO and dust continuum when comparing with literature  
  > "Assuming a lack of significant size variation between the CO and dust continuum (cite: Puglisi2019), we compared the molecular gas size (and/or dust size) of our sources with the literature." (Structural properties of the molecular gas)
   - fitting: Elliptical Gaussian fitting in UV plane and 2D elliptical Gaussian fitting in image plane  
  > "we derived the source sizes with the visibility CO(3$-$2) data in the $UV$ plane by fitting an elliptical Gaussian (task $uvmodelfit$)." (Structural properties of the molecular gas)
   - validation: Compared deconvolved image-plane sizes with UV-plane sizes, finding consistency within errors for high-S/N sources  
  > "The deconvolved semi-major and semi-minor axis values were broadly consistent with our $UV$ analysis." (Structural properties of the molecular gas)
2. **Measure CO(3-2) flux, luminosity, and CO excitation R_31**: Perform 2D Gaussian fitting on the velocity-integrated intensity map (Moment 0) to get CO(3-2) flux, convert to L'_CO(3-2), and divide by JVLA L'_CO(1-0) to obtain R_31  
  > "The CO(3$-$2) line flux was measured from a 2D Gaussian fitting on the velocity-integrated intensity map ($Moment~0$) with the velocity range shown in Fig. [1](#fig1). Then we calculated its luminosity $L_{\mathrm{CO(3-2)}}^{\prime}$ (cite: Solomon2005)." (CO excitation); "Therefore, we can derive the CO excitation $R_{\rm 31}$ with the CO(3$-$2) to CO(1$-$0) line luminosity ratio ($R_{\rm 31}  = L_{\rm CO(3-2)}^{\prime}/L_{\rm CO(1-0)}^{\prime}$)." (CO excitation)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting on Moment 0 map  
  > "The CO(3$-$2) line flux was measured from a 2D Gaussian fitting on the velocity-integrated intensity map ($Moment~0$) with the velocity range shown in Fig. [1](#fig1)." (CO excitation)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive dust mass, total infrared luminosity, and SFR via IR SED fitting**: Fit multi-wavelength infrared photometry from 24 um to 3.2 mm using CIGALE with a two-component dust emission model and test for AGN contribution  
  > "To obtain the dust mass, $M_{\rm dust}$, we performed the infrared SED fitting with `CIGALE` (cite: Burgarella2005; Noll2009; Boquien2019). We fit data from 24 $\mu$m up to the millimeter wavelengths" (Dust mass); "$^{d}$SFR, derived from $L_{\rm IR}$ using (cite: kennicutt) (${\rm SFR}=\,1.49 \times 10^{-10} L_{\rm IR}$; (cite: Chabrier2003) IMF)." (ALMA observations)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: U_min = 0.1-50, alpha = 1-3, gamma = 0.0001-1, q_PAH = 0.47-7.32%  
  > "with a minimum radiation field $U_{\rm min}=0.1-50$ in the Habing unit (cite: Habing1968), and the dust tightly linked to star-forming regions illuminated by a variable radiation field ($U_{\rm min} < U < U_{\rm max}$) with a power-law distribution of a spectral index $\alpha=1-3$." (Dust mass); "The fraction of dust mass linked to the star-forming regions is $\gamma=0.0001-1$. Among the two dust components, the mass fraction of dust in the form of the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAH) is $q_{\rm PAH}=0.47-7.32 \%." (Dust mass)
   - assumption: Chabrier (2003) IMF, Draine (2014) dust model, Fritz (2006) AGN template  
  > "We adopt a (cite: Chabrier2003) initial mass function (IMF) to estimate the SFR and stellar mass." (Introduction); "The dust infrared emission model (cite: Draine2014) combines two components with an extensive grid of parameters" (Dust mass); "we also fit the infrared SED with an additional AGN template (cite: Fritz2006) using CIGALE." (Dust mass)
   - fitting: Grid-based SED model fitting in CIGALE  
  > "We note that a large grid of models was generated by `CIGALE` and then fitted to the observations to estimate physical properties; for more details, readers can refer to (cite: Boquien2019)." (Dust mass)
   - validation: Compared derived SFRs and AGN fractions with previous literature constraints, finding consistency within 1 sigma  
  > "The resulting SFRs of these four galaxies are consistent with those derived from the infrared luminosity of and within a 1$\sigma$ confidence level." (Starburst and main-sequence galaxies); "We did not find any sign of a significant contribution of an AGN ($f_{\rm AGN}<0.2 \%$) in the infrared SEDs of our two SBs and two MSs, in agreement with previous literature constraints ()." (Dust mass)
4. **Estimate molecular gas masses, gas fractions, and depletion times using three independent methods**: Calculate M_gas from (i) CO(1-0) luminosity with metallicity-dependent alpha_CO(Z), (ii) M_dust with metallicity-dependent gas-to-dust ratio delta_GDR, and (iii) 3.2 mm Rayleigh-Jeans dust continuum following Scoville et al. (2016)  
  > "The molecular gas masses were independently estimated based on the following: (i) the CO(1$-$0) line, (ii) the $M_{\rm dust}$ assuming a gas-to-dust mass ratio ($\delta_{\rm GDR}$), and (iii) the 3.2 mm dust continuum emission (see Table [\[tab3\]](#tab3))." (Molecular gas mass)
   - assumption: Helium correction factor of 1.36; PP04 metallicity scale with 0.15 dex uncertainty; RJ-tail parameters: beta = 1.8, T_d = 25 K, alpha_850 = 6.7e19 erg s^-1 Hz^-1 M_sun^-1 (corresponding to alpha_CO = 6.5)  
  > "The $M_{\rm gas,CO}$ was calculated by $M_{\rm gas,CO} = \alpha_{\rm CO}(Z) L_{\rm CO(1-0)}^{\prime}$, including a factor 1.36 correction for helium." (Molecular gas mass); "The dust emissivity power-law spectral index $\beta$ is assumed to be 1.8." (Molecular gas mass); "The mass-weighted dust temperature $T_d$ is assumed to be $25\ {\rm K}$, which is considered to be a representative value for both local star-forming galaxies and high-redshift galaxies (cite: Scoville2016)." (Molecular gas mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-checked the three independent gas mass estimates (consistent within 1 sigma for SBs and 2 sigma for MSs), tested local starburst alpha_CO = 0.8, and compared with literature CO(1-0) values  
  > "For our two SBs and two MSs, the $M_{\rm gas,CO}$, $M_{\rm gas,GDR}$, and $M_{\rm gas,3.2mm}$ derived from three different methods are consistent with each other (within one sigma for the two SBs and within two sigma for the two MSs)." (Molecular gas mass); "We found that using the CO(1$-$0) luminosity (and gas mass) from different papers did not change our final results." (Molecular gas mass)
5. **Model 3D molecular gas kinematics to derive V_max and sigma_0**: Fit the 0.3'' resolution CO(3-2) data cubes with a 3D Bayesian parametric disk model convolved with the 3D PSF and LSF using GalPAK^3D  
  > "We investigated the kinematic properties of the molecular gas of the two cluster SBs and two cluster MSs by applying a three-dimensional (3D) kinematic modeling technique (cite: Bouche2015) to the CO(3$-$2) line cube." (Molecular gas kinematics); "We adopted a thick exponential-disk light profile and an arctan rotational velocity curve to fit the 0.3$^{\prime\prime}$ resolution cube in order to determine the maximum circular velocity $V_{\rm max}$ and intrinsic velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\rm 0}$." (Molecular gas kinematics)
   - software: GalPAK^3D
   - parameter: 10 free parameters (x_c, y_c, z_c, Flux, r_1/2, r_v, Incl., PA, V_max, sigma_0); 20,000 MCMC iterations; last 30% chain fraction used  
  > "Table notes: Total ten free parameters and their 1$\sigma$ uncertainties were determined from the GalPAK$^{3D}$." (Dust mass); "Fig. [2](#fig2) shows the best-fit models and residuals, based on the last 30% chain fraction with 20,000 iterations." (Molecular gas kinematics)
   - assumption: Thick exponential-disk light profile and arctan rotational velocity curve V_rot propto V_max arctan(r/r_v)  
  > "We adopted a thick exponential-disk light profile and an arctan rotational velocity curve to fit the 0.3$^{\prime\prime}$ resolution cube in order to determine the maximum circular velocity $V_{\rm max}$ and intrinsic velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\rm 0}$." (Molecular gas kinematics)

## Qualitative findings

- The two cluster starburst galaxies (SB1 and SB2) host regular, rotation-dominated, dynamically cold gas-rich disks without clear evidence of major mergers.  
  > "In contrast to starburst galaxies in the field often associated with galaxy mergers or highly turbulent gaseous disks, our observations show that the two starbursts in the cluster exhibit dynamically cold (rotation-dominated) gas-rich disks." (abstract); "These four galaxies show regular rotating gas disks, without clear evidence of a past gas-rich major merger (Fig. [2](#fig2))." (Conclusions)
- The intrinsic velocity dispersions of the two cluster starbursts (20-30 km/s) are about three times lower than those of field counterparts and the two cluster main-sequence galaxies (90-110 km/s).  
  > "Their gas disks have extremely low velocity dispersion ($\sigma_{\mathrm{0}} \sim 20-30$ km s$^{-1}$), which is three times lower than their field counterparts at similar redshifts." (abstract); "Their intrinsic velocity dispersions are extremely low, with $\sigma_{\rm 0} \sim 20-30$ km s$^{-1}$, while the two MSs have $\sigma_{\rm 0} \sim 90-110$ km s$^{-1}$ (see Table [\[summary\]](#summary))." (Triggering mechanism of high-$z$ cluster starburst)
- The combination of high gas fractions (~0.7) and suppressed velocity dispersions makes the gas disks of the two cluster starbursts gravitationally unstable (Toomre Q << Q_crit), triggering intense star formation.  
  > "The high gas fraction and suppressed velocity dispersion yield gravitationally unstable gas disks, which enables highly efficient star formation." (abstract); "However, in our case, the two SBs have much lower Toomre $Q$ values than $Q_{\rm crit}$ and they have high gas fractions ($\sim$0.7; see Table [\[tab3\]](#tab3))." (Triggering mechanism of high-$z$ cluster starburst)
- The two cluster starbursts do not show compact gas disks according to the submillimeter compactness criterion, and show negligible AGN contribution (<0.2%) to their IR SEDs.  
  > "By further using the submillimeter compactness criterion (cite: Puglisi2021), which is used to select compact galaxies, defined as a ratio of the stellar size (cite: vanderWel2014) larger than the molecular gas size by a factor of 2.2, we conclude that the two SBs do not show compact gas disks." (Structural properties of the molecular gas); "We did not find any sign of a significant contribution of an AGN ($f_{\rm AGN}<0.2 \%$) in the infrared SEDs of our two SBs and two MSs, in agreement with previous literature constraints ()." (Dust mass)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CLJ1001 at z=2.51 is in rapid transformation from a protocluster to a mature cluster; combining compact (C40-4) and extended (C40-7) ALMA configurations provides both reliable total flux measurements and spatially/spectroscopically resolved (~2.5 kpc) CO(3-2) kinematics.  
  > "CLJ1001 is in the phase of rapid transformation from a protocluster to a mature cluster, which makes it an ideal laboratory to study the connection between the triggering mechanism of cluster starbursts and their dense environment." (Introduction); "We adopted two different array configurations to obtain reliable measurements of total flux and resolved kinematics information: a more compact array configuration (C40-4) observing low-resolution large spatial scales, while a more extended array configuration (C40-7) observing high-resolution small spatial scales" (ALMA observations)
- Why this method: UV-plane fitting (uvmodelfit) avoids imaging uncertainties for sizes; CO(1-0) + three independent gas mass estimators avoid CO excitation and conversion factor biases; 3D forward modeling with GalPAK^3D accounts for 3D PSF and LSF beam smearing.  
  > "To avoid uncertainties from the imaging process, we derived the source sizes with the visibility CO(3$-$2) data in the $UV$ plane by fitting an elliptical Gaussian (task $uvmodelfit$)." (Structural properties of the molecular gas); "To obtain the total molecular gas mass $M_{\rm gas,CO}$, we used the CO(1$-$0) luminosity () instead of CO(3$-$2) to avoid uncertainties caused by the CO excitation ladder." (Molecular gas mass); "The model is convolved with a 3D kernel to account for the instrument point spread function (PSF) and the line spread function (LSF) smearing effect. GalPAK$^{3D}$ can thus return intrinsic galaxy properties." (Molecular gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Local starburst CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO,SB = 0.8): rejected as primary choice because it yields unphysically low gas-to-dust ratios (delta_GDR ~ 20), though tested to confirm main conclusions and Q < Q_crit still hold.  
  > "However, using $\alpha_{\rm CO, SB}$ = 0.8 $M_{\odot}$(K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$)$^{-1}$ for the two SBs would result in very low gas-to-dust ratios ($\delta_{\rm GDR}$$\sim$20), making the two SBs extreme cases." (Molecular gas mass); "We however show that even if we had chosen a local SB conversion factor ($\alpha_{\rm CO, SB}$ = 0.8 $M_{\odot}$(K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$)$^{-1}$) for the two SBs, the main conclusions of the paper would remain unchanged." (Molecular gas mass)
- Alternative: Native ~12 km s^-1 channel width data cubes for final kinematic parameters: tested and found consistent with 30 km s^-1 cubes, but rejected in favor of 30 km s^-1 cubes which have better sensitivity to detect fainter galaxy edges.  
  > "We applied the same analysis to the data cubes under the original channel width of $\sim$12 km s$^{-1}$. The results were consistent with each other within errors. Therefore, considering that a better sensitivity can help detect fainter galaxy edges and better sample the flat part of the rotation curves, we have adopted the values from the data cubes with a channel width of 30 km s$^{-1}$ in this work." (Molecular gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Direct parameter uncertainties output by GalPAK^3D: rejected because injection-recovery simulations showed they were underestimated by a factor of 5; simulation-derived uncertainties were adopted instead.  
  > "In general, there is no global offset of the derived values from the GalPAK$^{3D}$, but the uncertainty is greatly underestimated by a factor of 5. Thus, we use the uncertainties of $\sigma_{\rm 0}$ and $V_{\rm max}$ given by this simulation in the main body of this paper" (Molecular gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Rapidly reformed rotating disks after final coalescence of gas-rich major mergers: considered as an alternative explanation for high gas fractions, but rejected because post-merger disks are predicted to be compact and highly turbulent.  
  > "However, simulations of gas-rich major mergers reveal enhanced gas turbulence and reduced sizes of disk components that survive or that one rebuilt after a merger (cite: Bournaud2011), which conflicts with the observed low $\sigma_{\mathrm{0}}$ and relatively extended disk sizes of the two SBs." (Drivers of low turbulent gas in the two cluster SBs)
- How they decided it was enough: Checked MCMC convergence over 20,000 iterations, verified consistency between 30 km/s and native ~12 km/s channel cubes, and ran 100 injection-recovery simulations per galaxy to calibrate parameter uncertainties.  
  > "We also plotted the full MCMC chain from GalPAK$^{3D}$ for the two SBs and two MSs with 20,000 iterations to confirm the convergence of rotational velocity and intrinsic velocity dispersion (Fig. [3](#ed1))." (Molecular gas kinematics); "We applied the same analysis to the data cubes under the original channel width of $\sim$12 km s$^{-1}$. The results were consistent with each other within errors." (Molecular gas kinematics); "Then, we ran the GalPAK$^{3D}$ to derive $\sigma_{\rm 0}$ and $V_{\rm max}$. These two steps were repeated 100 times for each galaxy." (Molecular gas kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): MS2 shows different CO(3-2) sizes between image-plane and UV-plane analyses due to its lower detection significance (10 sigma), and 3.2 mm dust continuum sizes could not be measured for SB2, MS1, and MS2 due to low S/N.  
  > "We note that MS2 shows different sizes in the imaging and $UV$ analyses, which could be caused by its lowest significance ($10\sigma$) of (3$-$2) detections among the four sources." (Structural properties of the molecular gas); "For the dust size, we only successfully measured the SB1 in the image plane, which has the highest signal-to-noise ratio ($S/N\sim8$) of 3.2 mm dust continuum emission among the four sources." (Structural properties of the molecular gas)
- Limitation (authors): Higher-J CO transitions (e.g., CO(5-4) and CO(6-5)) are lacking and needed to determine whether the warm/dense gas in the two SBs is highly excited or MS-like.  
  > "The higher-$J$ CO transition observations (e.g., CO(5$-$4) and CO(6$-$5) probing warm and dense molecular gas) are necessary to establish whether the gas in the two SBs is highly excited as expected in starburst galaxies, or less excited as in MS galaxies." (CO excitation)
- Limitation (authors): Some points in the 1D rotation velocity and velocity dispersion profiles deviate from the dynamical models because of flux contamination by neighboring sources.  
  > "Some points deviate from the models because of the flux contamination by neighboring sources." (Molecular gas kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Formal uncertainties from GalPAK^3D are underestimated by a factor of 5, and the possibility that the two SBs are caught in the short final coalescence stage of gas-rich mergers cannot be completely ruled out.  
  > "In general, there is no global offset of the derived values from the GalPAK$^{3D}$, but the uncertainty is greatly underestimated by a factor of 5." (Molecular gas kinematics); "While the possibility cannot be fully ruled out that the two SBs are in the final coalescence stage of gas-rich mergers, the chance that both SBs are caught in this short stage is small." (Drivers of low turbulent gas in the two cluster SBs)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The low inclination of SB2 (19.30 deg) introduces large degeneracy in V_max (497 +123/-252 km/s), which propagates into V_max/sigma_0 and the epicyclic frequency kappa in Toomre Q.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The best-fit intrinsic velocity dispersion of SB1 (25 km/s) is below the adopted 30 km/s channel width and only ~2 times the native ~12 km/s channel width, making it sensitive to line spread function assumptions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample size is small (two starbursts and two main-sequence galaxies in a single cluster), limiting statistical generalization to all high-z protoclusters.
