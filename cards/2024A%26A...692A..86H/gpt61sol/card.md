## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure the NOEMA continuum and register epochs**: Measure continuum flux and centre; re-centre 2024 data for proper motion.  
  > "We use coordinates centred on the continuum emission" (Observations and data calibration and reduction); "The 2024 data have been re-centred to take into account the proper motion of $\chi$ Cygni between 2023 and 2024." (Observations and data calibration and reduction); "The flux density of the continuum emission is measured to be 54 mJy, within $\pm$8 mJy depending on frequency." (Observations and data calibration and reduction)
   - assumption: Doppler velocity relative to systemic velocity 10 km s−1 in the LSR frame.  
  > "The Doppler velocity, $V_z$, is relative to the adopted systemic velocity of 10 km s$^{-1}$ in the local standard of rest (LSR) frame." (Observations and data calibration and reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum flux measurements in 2023 and 2024 agree within 0.8 mJy.  
  > "We checked that the 2023 and 2024 measurements agree within 0.8 mJy, well within uncertainty." (Observations and data calibration and reduction)
2. **Extract NOEMA inner-envelope spectra and line ratios**: Integrate line intensities, spectra and radial distributions in S1 and S2 and separate velocity intervals.  
  > "lists, for each line, the intensities measured in regions S$_1$ and S$_2$ in each Doppler velocity interval separately." (The inner CSE / General properties); "displays Doppler velocity spectra integrated over regions S$_1$ and S$_2$ separately" (The inner CSE / General properties); "displays radial distributions integrated over the low and high Doppler velocity intervals separately." (The inner CSE / General properties)
   - parameter: S1: ellipse with axes 0.5 and 0.3 arcsec at PA 32°; S2: complement within R<1 arcsec.  
  > "S$_1$ is defined as the interior of an ellipse having axes of 0.5 and 0.3 arcsec at a position angle of 32$^o$." (Table 3); "S$_2$ is the complement of S$_1$ in the circle $R$$<$1 arcsec" (Table 3)
   - parameter: |Vz|<5 km s−1 and 5<Vz<12 km s−1.  
  > "In each region, two Doppler velocity intervals are considered: $|V_z|$$<$5 km s$^{-1}$ and 5$<$$V_z$$<$12 km s$^{-1}$." (Table 3)
   - parameter: 2023 A-configuration measurements, except 12,13CO(2-1), which use merged 2023 A+B.  
  > "All values are for 2023 A-configuration observations, with the exception of $^{12,13}$CO(2-1) data, which are merged 2023 A+B data." (Table 3)
   - assumption: Isotopologue line ratios can be affected by opacity and need not equal abundance ratios.  
  > "opacity is expected to lower the measured emission of the $^{12}$C, $^{16}$O and $^{28}$Si isotopologues" ($^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, $^{16}$O/$^{17}$O and $^{28}$Si/$^{29}$Si abundance ratios from CO(2-1), HCN(3-2), SiO(5-4) and SiO(6-5) line emissions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat inner-CSE analysis with merged A+B data; Table 3 uncertainties are 3σ of the noise.  
  > "we have repeated the analysis presented in the present section using the merged A+B-configuration data and we have made sure that all our conclusions are robust in this respect." (The inner CSE / General properties); "Uncertainties are at 3$\sigma$ of the noise level" (Table 3)
3. **Separate HCN variability from beam smearing**: Compare epoch visibilities, S1/S2 intensities and radial profiles; smear the 2023 A-configuration profile to predict the B-configuration observation.  
  > "compares the dependence on baseline length of the absolute values of the visibility" (Variability of the H$^{12}$CN(3-2) and SiO($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) line emissions); "we smeared the radial distribution observed in 2023 using the A-configuration to predict what it would produce using the B-configuration." (Low Doppler velocities: the HCN singularity)
   - parameter: Compare S1 and S2 over |Vz|<5 km s−1.  
  > "Comparing the H$^{12}$CN(3-2) emissions observed in S$_1$ and S$_2$ in the $|V_z|$$<$5 km s$^{-1}$ interval" (Low Doppler velocities: the HCN singularity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare the smeared prediction with the actual 2024 observation.  
  > "Comparing the result with the actual 2024 observation, we estimate that the radial extension of the H$^{12}$CN(3-2) emission has increased by $\sim$0.1 arcsec between the two years." (Low Doppler velocities: the HCN singularity)
4. **Check the SiO ratio with ACA and LTE modelling**: Compare archived ACA and NOEMA SiO spectra; calculate LTE ratios and integrate radiative transfer through a spherical CSE.  
  > "In order to get an independent evaluation of the emission ratio, we analysed archived ALMA/ACA observations of the SiO(5-4) and SiO(6-5) line emissions of $\chi$ Cygni from Project 2021.2.00147.S (PI M. Saberi)." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE)); "producing a simple LTE model of the morpho-kinematics and integrating the radiative transfer equation over a spherical CSE." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - parameter: ACA covers the whole emission; NOEMA comparison uses R<1.5 arcsec.  
  > "The ACA measurement covers the whole emission while the NOEMA measurement is over $R$\<1.5 arcsec only." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - parameter: Reference model, as printed: mass-loss rate 2.4 M⊙ yr−1; terminal velocity 8 km s−1; SiO/H=10−5.  
  > "a mass-loss rate of 2.4 M$_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$, a terminal velocity of 8 km s$^{-1}$, an SiO/H abundance ratio of 10$^{-5}$" (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - parameter: SiO density 1.5 SiO/cm3 at 165 au, multiplied by a Gaussian with e-folding radius 215 au.  
  > "meaning a density of 1.5 SiO/cm$^3$ at a distance of 165 au from the centre of the star, multiplied by a Gaussian having a $e$-folding radius of 215 au" (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - parameter: Radial velocity: 2 km s−1 at 17 au, increasing to 2+6(1−0.1/r)^0.8.  
  > "a radial velocity increasing from 2 km s$^{-1}$ at 17 au to 2+6(1$-$0.1/$r$)$^{0.8}$ at a distance $r$" (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - parameter: T(K)=100(210/r[au])^0.78.  
  > "a radial dependence of the temperature of the form $T$(K)=100(210/$r$\[au\])$^{0.78}$." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - assumption: Initial LTE ratio calculation neglects differential opacity and assumes LTE level populations.  
  > "when neglecting opacity (more precisely when neglecting the difference between the effects of opacity on each of the two line emissions) and assuming level populations predicted by LTE" (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - fitting: Standard radial velocity, abundance and temperature distributions give good fits; fitting algorithm and statistic not stated.  
  > "We find that standard radial distributions of the velocity, molecular abundance and temperature give good fits to the data." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
   - validation: Independent ACA comparison and variation of LTE reference parameters within reasonable limits.  
  > "Indeed, it is $\sim$0.7 instead of 0.86, probably consistent within uncertainties." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE)); "when varying these within reasonable limits, we find that the emission ratio SiO(5-4)/SiO(6-5) remains between 0.5 and 0.6" (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
5. **Inspect mass-ejection and shell patterns**: Inspect NOEMA channel maps and Vz versus R projections for north-western enhancements, depressions and CO shell-cap patterns.  
  > "In order to better illustrate the morpho-kinematics at stake in the north-western quadrant, 270$^{\circ}$$<$$\omega$$<$360$^{\circ}$" (The red-shifted north-western octant: evidence for mass ejection); "for each line emission separately, $V_z$ vs $R$ maps." (The red-shifted north-western octant: evidence for mass ejection); "Together with the observation of arcs of enhanced emission in the channel maps, they suggest that mass loss proceeds via the emission of shell caps covering solid angles at steradian scale" (The $^{12}$CO circumstellar envelope)
   - parameter: North-western quadrant: 270°<ω<360°; high-velocity interval: 5<Vz<12 km s−1.  
  > "270$^{\circ}$$<$$\omega$$<$360$^{\circ}$" (The red-shifted north-western octant: evidence for mass ejection); "Intensity maps integrated over the high Doppler velocity interval, 5$<$$V_z$$<$12 km s$^{-1}$" (The red-shifted north-western octant: evidence for mass ejection)
   - parameter: Reference thin spherical shells: 250 years old, radial velocities 10 and 7 km s−1.  
  > "displays as reference thin spherical shells emitted 250 years ago with radial velocities of 10 and 7 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (The $^{12}$CO circumstellar envelope)
   - assumption: For radial expansion of an ejected sphere, Vz²+R²/t²=V²; at Vz=0, ejection time t=R/V.  
  > "a sphere ejected at time $t$ and expanding radially at velocity $V$ appears as an ellipse of equation $V_z^2+R^2/t^2$=$V^2$." (The $^{12}$CO circumstellar envelope); "for $V_z=$0, $R$=$Vt$ gives a measure, $t$=$R/V$, of the time of ejection." (The $^{12}$CO circumstellar envelope)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Test spatial filtering by imaging discs with different sizes and brightness profiles.  
  > "by imaging discs of different sizes and different brightness radial distributions, with the result that reliable imaging is obtained up to $\sim$2.0 arcsec." (The $^{12}$CO circumstellar envelope)
6. **Identify and compare the SiO maser**: Compare Si16O(ν=1,J=6-5) spectra and channel maps in February 2023 and February 2024.  
  > "The emission of the vibrationally-excited line of Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) was observed in the extended A-configuration in February 2023." (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser); "Observation of the same line emission using the B-configuration" (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser); "was made in February 2024." (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser)
   - parameter: Inspect the narrow peak near −1.5 km s−1 and enhanced emission at 3<Vz<8 km s−1.  
  > "The Doppler velocity spectrum displays a profile characteristic of maser emission, with a narrow peak at $\sim$$-$1.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser); "Another enhancement of emission is observed in the 3$<$$V_z$$<$8 km s$^{-1}$ interval of Doppler velocity." (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: A broader B-configuration beam cannot explain disappearance from the Doppler spectrum.  
  > "While the broader beam of the B-configuration would smear the image of point-like maser enhancements, this has no impact on the Doppler velocity spectrum and cannot account for the disappearance." (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN variability is established, but enhanced abundance versus enhanced excitation remains unresolved.  
  > "does the confined H$^{12}$CN(3-2) enhanced emission observed in 2023-2024 reveal the presence close to the photosphere of an enhanced molecular abundance or simply an enhanced excitation of the line, due to masing and/or shock dynamics?" (Low Doppler velocities: the HCN singularity)
- Strong absorption in the spherical LTE model does not explain the observed SiO ratio.  
  > "absorption, even when strong, does not much affect the emission ratio and cannot explain the large values that are observed." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
- Rare-isotopologue enhancements contribute to the low ratios close to the star.  
  > "the decrease of the ratios close to the star is in part the result of an increase of the rare isotope emissions" ($^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, $^{16}$O/$^{17}$O and $^{28}$Si/$^{29}$Si abundance ratios from CO(2-1), HCN(3-2), SiO(5-4) and SiO(6-5) line emissions)
- The SiO J=6-5 maser is reported as a first observation for χ Cygni.  
  > "To our knowledge the observation of a maser in the $J$=6-5 transition is presented here for the first time." (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Archived ACA observations provide an independent evaluation of the SiO emission ratio.  
  > "In order to get an independent evaluation of the emission ratio, we analysed archived ALMA/ACA observations of the SiO(5-4) and SiO(6-5) line emissions of $\chi$ Cygni from Project 2021.2.00147.S (PI M. Saberi)." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
- Why this method: Spherical LTE radiative transfer estimates SiO opacity and tests its effect on the emission ratio.  
  > "it is instructive to obtain an estimate of the opacity of the SiO lines" (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE)); "producing a simple LTE model of the morpho-kinematics and integrating the radiative transfer equation over a spherical CSE." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
- Alternative: Isotropic gas expansion cannot explain the HCN size change without conflicting with its narrow line profile.  
  > "it would mean an expansion velocity at the scale of $\sim$80 km s$^{-1}$, conflicting with the observed narrow width of the line profile, $\sim$$\pm$2 km s$^{-1}$." (Low Doppler velocities: the HCN singularity)
- Alternative: HCN masing is considered but does not straightforwardly explain changing location at approximately constant intensity.  
  > "would suggest variability in intensity at a same location, not variability in location at a same intensity as is observed." (The singularity of H$^{12}$CN line emission)
- Alternative: Opacity alone in the tested LTE models does not explain the large SiO ratio; detailed non-LTE comparison is deferred.  
  > "absorption, even when strong, does not much affect the emission ratio and cannot explain the large values that are observed." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE)); "a detailed comparison with currently available interferometer data would help with telling apart different contributions; this is, however, beyond the scope of the present article." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
- How they decided it was enough: NOEMA spatial reliability was tested with artificial discs; inner-CSE conclusions were checked using merged A+B data.  
  > "We studied carefully the impact of the lack of short spacing by imaging disc sources of different sizes and different radial dependence of the brightness." (Observations and data calibration and reduction); "we have repeated the analysis presented in the present section using the merged A+B-configuration data and we have made sure that all our conclusions are robust in this respect." (The inner CSE / General properties)
- Limitation (authors): ACA and NOEMA cover different spatial extents.  
  > "The ACA measurement covers the whole emission while the NOEMA measurement is over $R$\<1.5 arcsec only." (Si$^{16}$O(5-4)/Si$^{16}$O(6-5) emission ratio: comparison with Local Thermal Equilibrium (LTE))
- Limitation (authors): The north-western mass-ejection interpretation is limited by sensitivity and angular resolution.  
  > "the situation is not as clear as it was in the case of EP Aqr because the data are of lesser sensitivity and lower angular resolution." (The red-shifted north-western octant: evidence for mass ejection)
- Limitation (authors): The angular resolution cannot reliably map individual SiO maser spots.  
  > "the angular resolution is insufficient to map reliably the maser emission and possibly reveal multiple emitting spots simultaneously present in the close neighbourhood of the star" (Si$^{16}$O($\nu$=1,$J$=6-5) maser)
- Limitation (authors): High-velocity wings cannot be established confidently because of absorption and mass ejection.  
  > "absorption and mass ejection make it difficult to reveal high Doppler velocity wings in the spectrum of $\chi$ Cygni; if they are present, which one cannot assert with certitude, they are weak." (High Doppler velocity wings)
- Limitation (authors): No proposed physical interpretation is convincing enough; further measurements at different stellar phases are needed.  
  > "none has been found convincing enough and a clear understanding of the mechanisms at stake calls for new measurements of high resolution and sensitivity, in particular at different stellar phases." (Summary and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Do not transfer NOEMA calibration, robust-weighting or spectral-binning settings to ACA; ACA processing is unspecified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The wording assigning ∼0.7 and 0.86 in the ACA/NOEMA comparison is ambiguous relative to the earlier NOEMA ratios; verify the intended assignments before defining an answer key.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The LTE model prints 2.4 M⊙ yr−1, whereas the introduction quotes 2.4×10−7 M⊙ yr−1 from earlier work; resolve this discrepancy before implementing the model.
