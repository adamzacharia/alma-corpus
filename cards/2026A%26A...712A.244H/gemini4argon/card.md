## Science

- Fields: agn_and_quasars
- Question: Determine the polarization and Faraday rotation structure of the nucleus (multiple polarized components versus internal Faraday rotation) and the magnetic field structure of the kiloparsec-scale jet in the quasar 3C 273 using multiband ALMA polarimetry.  
  > "We used multiband Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) data to study the polarization structure of both the nucleus and the kiloparsec-scale jet of the archetypal quasar 3C 273." (abstract); "The broad wavelength coverage (145–345 GHz) allows us to revisit the extreme Faraday rotation previously detected in band 6 alone and to better test whether the behavior arises from multiple polarized components or from internal Faraday rotation within the nuclear region." (Results)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate visibilities and perform self-calibration**: Standard ALMA calibration and polarization calibration using 3C 279, with Spw-independent polarization modeling and self-bandpass calibration in Band 6, D-term copying from Nov 23 to Nov 15 in Band 7, and two rounds of phase-only and amplitude+phase self-calibration using 3C 273.  
  > "During calibration, we mostly followed the standard data reduction and polarization calibration steps (cite: nagai16), with some exceptions in bands 6 and 7, as we describe below." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "Given the short time between executions at the same band, we assumed similar D-terms for the two datasets, and copied the D-term solutions from the second execution (where the D-terms did not show any pathological behavior) into the first." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "We then improved the model and applied a round of phase and amplitude self-cal with the solution interval set to 10 s in bands 4 and 6 and 30 s in band 7. We repeated this procedure twice for each band." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Phase-only self-cal solint = int; phase and amplitude self-cal solint = 10 s (Bands 4 and 6) and 30 s (Band 7)  
  > "For this, we first obtained a model of the source using the CASA task `tclean`, and then applied a round of phase-only self-cal with solution interval set to *int*." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus); "We then improved the model and applied a round of phase and amplitude self-cal with the solution interval set to 10 s in bands 4 and 6 and 30 s in band 7." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
   - assumption: Stokes V vanishes at 232.86 GHz for Band 6 self-bandpass model (I = 4.35 Jy, alpha = -0.85, Q = 0.0238 Jy, U = -0.1212 Jy); similar D-terms between Nov 15 and Nov 23 in Band 7  
  > "We first obtained a model of the source using CASA task `tclean`, from which we found the total intensity of the bright point source in the nucleus to be 4.35 Jy with a spectral index of $-0.85$, Stokes Q of 0.0238 Jy, and Stokes U of $-0.1212$ Jy; for this, we assumed that Stokes V vanishes (or is negligible) at a reference frequency of 232.86 GHz." (ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked consistency of hotspot EVPAs between Band 7 epochs and eliminated nonphysical EVPA jumps between adjacent Spws in Band 6  
  > "This step ensures a correct amplitude ratio between polarizations at each frequency channel, resulting in EVPAs without abrupt jumps between adjacent Spws." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "After producing the images of the two executions separately, there were no changes in the EVPAs of the brightest hotspot, indicating a consistent calibration of the two epochs." (ALMA observations and data reduction)
2. **Extract channel-wise Stokes parameters and uncertainties of the nucleus**: Fit visibility data with a delta component at the phase center in each 31.25 MHz channel independently using UVMULTIFIT, and run 1000 Monte Carlo simulations to propagate thermal and systematic calibration uncertainties; fit power-law spectral index to total intensity.  
  > "Similar to , we extracted the Stokes parameters of the nucleus using the external CASA library `UVMULTIFIT` (cite: marti-vidal14)." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus); "We assumed that a single point source dominates the emission at the phase center of the observation and fit the visibility data with a delta component centered on each 31.25 MHz-wide Spw independently." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus); "Following , we ran Monte Carlo (MC) simulations to account for thermal and systematic uncertainties in the data, and obtained a distribution of 1000 Stokes I, Q, U, and V values." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
   - software: CASA, UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: 1000 Monte Carlo realizations; noise std devs: X-Y Phase = 2 deg, X-Y Bandpass amp = 0.1% (B4, B6) / 0.2% (B7), X-Y Bandpass phase = 0.5 deg, D-terms = 0.5% (B4, B7 Nov 23) / 2% (B6) / 1% (B7 Nov 15)  
  > "Following , we ran Monte Carlo (MC) simulations to account for thermal and systematic uncertainties in the data, and obtained a distribution of 1000 Stokes I, Q, U, and V values." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus); "| D-terms (real & imag) [%] | 0.5 | 2 | 1 | 0.5 |" (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
   - assumption: Single point source dominates emission at phase center  
  > "We assumed that a single point source dominates the emission at the phase center of the observation and fit the visibility data with a delta component centered on each 31.25 MHz-wide Spw independently." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
   - fitting: Visibility delta-component fitting via UVMULTIFIT; core spectral index power-law fit checked with reduced chi^2  
  > "The emission over all the bands is optically thin with a spectral index of $\alpha_{\rm core}=-0.7915\pm0.0003$ and a reduced $\chi^2=2.76$ of the fit when using the 15 November epoch in band 7." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
   - validation: Compared reduced chi^2 of core spectral index fit between Nov 15 (2.76) and Nov 23 (123.0) epochs, and cross-checked EVPA variability against AMAPOLA monitoring  
  > "If we use the 23 November epoch instead, the spectral index would be $-0.905\pm0.002$ with a significantly higher reduced $\chi^2=123.0$, further supporting our decision to use only the 15 November epoch in the modeling of the core polarization." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
3. **Model wavelength-dependent core polarization via QU-fitting**: Fit complex fractional polarization p(lambda^2) = q(lambda^2) + i u(lambda^2) across Bands 4, 6, and 7 (Nov 15) against four parametric Faraday rotation models (S, T_inv, S2, T2) using a nested-sampling backend.  
  > "We performed this QU-fitting in the complex plane using parametric models, $p(\lambda^2;\,\bm{\theta})$, and a nested-sampling backend that returns posterior samples for the parameter set $\bm{\theta}$ and the Bayesian evidence $\ln\mathcal{Z}$ for model comparison." (Polarization in the core); "We investigated four distinct models for the core polarization data from bands 4, 6, and 7 (15 November 2018 epoch)." (Polarization in the core)
   - parameter: Four models: S (3 params), T_inv (5 params), S2 (6 params), T2 (8 params)  
  > "| S (single thin) | 3 | [\[eq:m1_model\]](#eq:m1_model) |" (Polarization in the core); "| T2 (double ext. dispersion) | 8 | [\[eq:m4_model\]](#eq:m4_model) |" (Polarization in the core)
   - assumption: External Faraday dispersion or thin screens parameterized by mean RM, dispersion sigma_RM, intrinsic fractional polarization p_0, and intrinsic angle psi_0  
  > "Therefore, we consider this model as a parameterization of mean RM and dispersion, and the intrinsic polarization properties." (Polarization in the core)
   - fitting: Nested-sampling backend returning posterior samples, Bayesian evidence ln Z, BIC, and reduced chi^2  
  > "We performed this QU-fitting in the complex plane using parametric models, $p(\lambda^2;\,\bm{\theta})$, and a nested-sampling backend that returns posterior samples for the parameter set $\bm{\theta}$ and the Bayesian evidence $\ln\mathcal{Z}$ for model comparison." (Polarization in the core)
   - validation: Model comparison using Bayes factors (Delta ln Z >= 5 indicating very strong preference) and reduced chi^2  
  > "Table 2 shows that among the four tested models, the two–component external–dispersion case (T2) provides the highest Bayesian evidence and the lowest reduced $\chi^2$." (Polarization in the core)
4. **Image kiloparsec-scale jet polarization and map spectral index**: Image aggregated bandwidth and individual Spws with tclean, extract Stokes I, Q, U with immath, compute fractional polarization and EVPA along ridge lines and transverse slices, smooth single-Spw images to common beam with imsmooth, align using QA2 images, and fit pixel-by-pixel power-law spectral index across 12 Spws.  
  > "We used the task *immath* to extract the Stokes I, Q, and U images for further processing and plotting." (Imaging and alignment of the kiloparsec-scale jet); "In order to combine the images across the three bands, we also produced images of each Spw that we smoothed using the *imsmooth* task to have the same beam size (which corresponds to the beam size of the Spw 0 in band 4, $0.54\ensuremath{''}\times0.44\ensuremath{''}$)." (Imaging and alignment of the kiloparsec-scale jet); "The spectral index was calculated by fitting a linear function to the total intensity against the frequency in log-space using all the 12 Spws." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet)
   - software: CASA, tclean, immath, imsmooth
   - parameter: Polarization cutoff: I > 5*rms and polarized flux > 3*rms; Spectral index cutoff: I > 3*rms in each Spw, 10% calibration uncertainty added in quadrature, accepted fits with chi^2 < 18.307 (95% confidence for 10 dof); common beam 0.54'' x 0.44''; Band 6 declination shift -0.043''  
  > "In all bands, the fractional polarization was calculated for all pixels where the total intensity exceeded five times its rms uncertainty and the polarized flux density exceeded three times its rms uncertainty." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet); "We only used pixels where the total intensity in each pixel is higher than three times the rms uncertainty of the given image." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet); "We only accepted fits with $\chi^2<18.307$, corresponding to the 95% confidence limit for 10 degrees of freedom." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet)
   - assumption: Power-law spectrum I proportional to nu^+alpha across 138-350 GHz  
  > "We define the spectral index sign as $I \propto \nu^{+\alpha}$." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet)
   - fitting: Linear least-squares fit in log-space across 12 Spws, compared with log-parabola fit via reduced chi^2 and BIC  
  > "The spectral index was calculated by fitting a linear function to the total intensity against the frequency in log-space using all the 12 Spws." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet); "We found that a log-parabola shape results in a smaller reduced $\chi^2$; however, according to the Bayesian information criterion (BIC), we cannot disfavor the simpler power-law fit." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet)
   - validation: Verified image alignment against non-self-calibrated QA2 images and tested spectral curvature using BIC  
  > "Because the self-cal we applied changed the absolute position of the phase center in our images, we used the quality assurance level 2 (QA2) images delivered within the dataset (with no self-cal applied) to verify the image alignment between different bands." (Imaging and alignment of the kiloparsec-scale jet)

## Qualitative findings

- The core polarization across 138-350 GHz is decisively best fit by two polarized components with external Faraday dispersion rather than a single component or single internal Faraday rotation.  
  > "Table 2 shows that among the four tested models, the two–component external–dispersion case (T2) provides the highest Bayesian evidence and the lowest reduced $\chi^2$." (Polarization in the core); "The single–component models (T~inv~, S) cannot reproduce the observed fractional polarization or EVPA behavior and are also decisively disfavored." (Polarization in the core)
- The core polarization angle at Band 7 varied by 10 degrees over 8 days between 15 and 23 November 2018, and the Band 6 EVPA behavior changed significantly compared to 2017 epochs.  
  > "However, a $10\deg$ shift in the EVPAs of the nucleus was found, showing evidence of an intrinsic EVPA variability of the nucleus." (ALMA observations and data reduction); "Our new data taken over 1.5 years later show very different behavior with a nearly flat polarization angle over the 1.3 mm band, resulting in a much smaller nominal RM of $\sim-8.4\times10^4$ rad m$^−2$ if a simple linear fit as shown in Fig. 8 is performed." (Variability in the core RM)
- In the kiloparsec-scale jet, the magnetic field is predominantly poloidal along the jet until ~2'' from hotspot H2, where it turns perpendicular in the inner jet (transverse shock) and parallel/circumferential along the jet edges (shearing).  
  > "The magnetic field direction is predominantly poloidal along the jet direction until about $2\ensuremath{''}$ from the brightest hotspot H2. Closer to the hotspot, the magnetic field in the inner jet is perpendicular to the jet, while around the edges the magnetic field follows the jet direction." (Polarization of the kiloparsec-scale jet)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiband ALMA observations (Bands 4, 6, 7) provide broad wavelength-squared coverage to break model degeneracies from single-band observations, and separate pointings on the nucleus and hotspot H2 keep the kiloparsec-scale jet within the inner third of the primary beam where polarization calibration is reliable.  
  > "Due to the limited wavelength coverage of our observations, we were unable to discriminate between the two scenarios. Additionally, because the ALMA polarization calibration could be performed reliably only for the inner third of the primary beam of the telescopes, we were unable to study the polarization structure of the kiloparsec-scale jet." (Introduction); "Because we were interested in the polarization of both the nucleus and kiloparsec-scale jet, we performed two separate pointings in each band: one centered on the nucleus (3C 273) and another on the bright hotspot H2 (3C273_H2) about 21.3$''$ away." (ALMA observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: Complex-plane QU-fitting with nested sampling avoids EVPA n-pi ambiguity and allows rigorous Bayesian evidence comparison across multi-component models; Monte Carlo visibility fitting accounts for systematic D-term and bandpass uncertainties.  
  > "Following , we ran Monte Carlo (MC) simulations to account for thermal and systematic uncertainties in the data, and obtained a distribution of 1000 Stokes I, Q, U, and V values." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus); "We performed this QU-fitting in the complex plane using parametric models, $p(\lambda^2;\,\bm{\theta})$, and a nested-sampling backend that returns posterior samples for the parameter set $\bm{\theta}$ and the Bayesian evidence $\ln\mathcal{Z}$ for model comparison." (Polarization in the core)
- Alternative: Single Faraday-thin component (model S), single inverse-depolarizing component (model T_inv), and two Faraday-thin components (model S2) for the core polarization — all decisively ruled out by Bayesian evidence.  
  > "The simpler two–thin component model (S2) gives an inferior but acceptable fit based on the reduced $\chi^2$ but is decisively ruled out based on the Bayes Factor ($\Delta\ln\mathcal{Z}\simeq140$)." (Polarization in the core); "The single–component models (T~inv~, S) cannot reproduce the observed fractional polarization or EVPA behavior and are also decisively disfavored." (Polarization in the core)
- Alternative: Using the 23 November 2018 Band 7 execution for core modeling — rejected due to a 10 deg EVPA jump and poor spectral index continuity (reduced chi^2 = 123.0 vs 2.76 for 15 November).  
  > "If we use the 23 November epoch instead, the spectral index would be $-0.905\pm0.002$ with a significantly higher reduced $\chi^2=123.0$, further supporting our decision to use only the 15 November epoch in the modeling of the core polarization." (Extracting Stokes parameters of the nucleus)
- Alternative: Log-parabola curved spectral fit in the kiloparsec-scale jet — rejected because BIC does not disfavor the simpler power-law fit.  
  > "We found that a log-parabola shape results in a smaller reduced $\chi^2$; however, according to the Bayesian information criterion (BIC), we cannot disfavor the simpler power-law fit." (Polarization and spectral index of the kiloparsec-scale jet)
- How they decided it was enough: Stopped at the two-component external-dispersion model (T2) because it achieved reduced chi^2 = 0.35 and decisive Bayes factors over alternatives, while non-simultaneity across bands and lack of contemporaneous VLBI priors make more complex models unwarranted.  
  > "Therefore, we do not consider it useful to fit more complicated models (or equally complicated models with different underlying assumptions) in the absence of more robust prior information based on contemporaneous very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) imaging." (Polarization in the core)
- Limitation (authors): Core emission is spatially unresolved by ALMA (~2 kpc scale), making it impossible to distinguish external Faraday dispersion from internal Faraday rotation within the two components.  
  > "While the best-fit-model equation is nominally of external Faraday rotation, we note that in the case of unresolved emission we cannot reliably distinguish this from internal Faraday rotation effects that would cause similar spectro-polarimetric behavior." (Polarization in the core)
- Limitation (authors): Observations across Bands 4, 6, and 7 were spread over a week (11-18 November 2018), introducing systematic uncertainty in the multi-band core modeling due to potential source variability.  
  > "We also note that as the data in the different bands are not strictly simultaneous, and we see significant variability in the band 7 EVPA on a timescale of a week, there is additional uncertainty in the best-fit model due to potential variability." (Polarization in the core)
- Limitation (authors): Five antennas present on 15 November 2018 in Band 7 were absent on 23 November 2018 and therefore could not be corrected for instrumental polarization (D-terms).  
  > "There are, however, five antennas that were present on 15 November and were not available on 23 November. For those antennas, we did not correct for instrumental polarization." (ALMA observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity and wavelength-squared coverage in the kiloparsec-scale jet are insufficient to detect the small (~2 rad m^-2) Faraday rotation or to map transverse polarization in X-ray bright knots A and B.  
  > "We attempted to make an RM map of the kiloparsec-scale jet by using the data of the individual Spws similar to the spectral index map, but due to the relatively poor sensitivity and larger EVPA uncertainties, we do not detect any RM deviating from 0 rad m$^−2$ within three times the RM uncertainty." (Polarization of the kiloparsec-scale jet); "In this model, the very-high-energy gamma-ray emission would originate in the brightest X-ray knots, A and B, but unfortunately the sensitivity of our polarization maps is not high enough to detect polarization across the jet in this region to study if it supports the scenario of a sheared layer around the jet, as seen in the hotspot region." (Multiwavelength connection)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reduced chi^2 of 0.35 for the best-fit T2 model indicates that the channel-wise Monte Carlo error bars (which include inflated systematic D-term noise) are likely overestimated or correlated across channels within a spectral window.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because the two components in the T2 model are fit to total fractional polarization q and u without spectral index parameters for each polarized component, any intrinsic spectral index difference between the two emission regions is absorbed into the effective Faraday dispersion parameters sigma_RM.
