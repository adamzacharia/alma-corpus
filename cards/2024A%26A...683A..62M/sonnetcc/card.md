## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate visibilities and self-calibrate**: ALMA pipeline calibration followed by phase-only self-calibration of PKS 1830-211 visibilities  
  > "the visibilities of PKS 1830$-$211 were self-calibrated, phase only, at the shortest integration time interval of 6 sec." (2.1 Ortho-C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Self-calibration phase-only, solution interval = shortest integration time, 6 s  
  > "self-calibrated, phase only, at the shortest integration time interval of 6 sec." (2.1 Ortho-C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **extract spectra toward NE and SW images**: Visibility fitting with a two point-source model per channel, then normalization to continuum  
  > "The final spectra along the NE and SW images of PKS 1830$-$211 were extracted by visibility fitting using the Python-based tool `UVMultiFit` (cite: mar14)" (2.1 Ortho-C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - software: UVMultiFit
   - parameter: Source positions fixed at continuum locations; amplitude per spectral channel free  
  > "a model of two point sources, for which the positions were fixed at the continuum locations and the amplitude per spectral channel left as free parameters" (2.1 Ortho-C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - parameter: Continuum levels used for normalization: 1.3 Jy (NE) and 1.0 Jy (SW) at 250 GHz  
  > "i.e., 1.3 Jy and 1.0 Jy at 250 GHz for the NE and SW images, respectively" (2.1 Ortho-C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - assumption: Both lensed images are unresolved point sources  
  > "a model of two point sources" (2.1 Ortho-C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **fit Gaussian to the SW absorption feature**: Single Gaussian profile fit to estimate rest frequency, integrated opacity and FWHM  
  > "we adopt a simple Gaussian profile to obtain a first estimate of its rest frequency" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - assumption: Line centred at z=0.88582 in the heliocentric velocity frame  
  > "assuming that the line profile is centered at $z=0.88582$ in the heliocentric velocity frame" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile; fitting code, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "we adopt a simple Gaussian profile" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **identify the carrier in molecular line databases**: Search CDMS and JPL around the fitted rest frequency; only one low-energy candidate, the 2_12-1_01 transition of C2H3+; refit with that frequency to get velocity centroid  
  > "As far as we can find exploring a range of $\pm 10$ MHz around 494.032 GHz" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$); "By repeating the Gaussian fit with this new frequency, we find a velocity centroid" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - software: CDMS, JPL molecular database
   - parameter: Search window +/-10 MHz around 494.032 GHz; also searched around 261.961 GHz (z=0 case)  
  > "we also searched around 261.961 GHz (i.e., case where the absorption would occur at $z=0$), and could not find any convincing candidate" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Feature seen only toward SW, no counterpart in NE spectra at same velocities, excluding instrumental, atmospheric, and Milky Way origin  
  > "There is no counterpart in the NE spectra at the same velocities, removing the possibility of spurious instrumental artifacts or of atmospheric lines." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
5. **fit para-C2H3+ line in archival data**: Gaussian fit to the 1_11-0_00 line in the 2014 archival spectrum  
  > "The best fit values with a Gaussian profile are the following" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - assumption: Quasar illumination may have changed between the two epochs eight years apart, so centroids may differ  
  > "it is therefore possible that the quasar illumination changed between the two epochs" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile; fitting code not stated  
  > "The best fit values with a Gaussian profile" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **derive LTE column densities**: LTE with excitation at T_CMB using Gaussian-fit line parameters; total column from ortho column with assumed OPR of three  
  > "Assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$); "with an assumed ortho-to-para ratio (OPR) of three" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - parameter: Total column density 2e12 cm-2, OPR=3, Gaussian FWHM 20 km/s used for LTE opacities  
  > "a Gaussian profile with a FWHM linewidth of 20 km s$^{-1}$ to calculate the line peak opacity" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Excitation by 5.14 K CMB at z=0.89  
  > "radiative transitions in the 5.14 K black-body radiation field at $z=0.89$" (3.3 Excitation of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - assumption: Ortho and para columns cannot be summed because observed eight years apart  
  > "Since the lines were observed with a large time interval, we cannot directly sum the column densities of the two forms" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note LTE assumption may bias the column densities  
  > "We note, however, that those column densities could be affected by our simple assumption of LTE." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
7. **test formation-pumping non-LTE excitation**: Rate equations for rotational level populations with formation at Boltzmann temperature T_form and destruction by dissociative recombination  
  > "If we further assume that the formation process populates nascent states of C$_2$H$_3^+$ with a Boltzmann distribution at some formation temperature" (3.3 Excitation of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - parameter: T_k = 100 K, n(H2)=1000 cm-3, T_form=300 K, n(e-)=0.75 cm-3, total column 7.5e12 cm-2, FWHM 20 km/s, destruction rate 9.5e-7 s-1  
  > "destruction rate $9.5\times 10^{-7}$ s$^{-1}$, total column density $7.5\times 10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Table 1); "with guessed collision rates at $T_k = 100$ K" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Destruction rate the same for all states; electron collisions neglected; crude H2 collision rate coefficient 2e-10 cm3/s  
  > "provided that the destruction rate can be assumed to be the same for all states" (3.3 Excitation of C$_2$H$_3^+$); "Inelastic collisions with electrons should also play a role but are neglected for simplicity." (3.3 Excitation of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors state state-specific chemistry and collision rates are not known in detail  
  > "although we do not know in detail the state-specific chemical processes or state-to-state collisional rates for C$_2$H$_3^+$" (3.3 Excitation of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
8. **evaluate gas-phase formation routes**: Steady-state kinetics with parent species A+ and B; compare acetylene and methane routes for required abundance of B  
  > "Assuming steady-state kinetics and that the main formation route to C$_2$H$_3^+$ involves parent species A$^+$ and B" (3.4 Chemistry of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - software: KIDA
   - parameter: Formation rate coefficient ~1e-9 cm3/s; destruction k_e ~ (9-1)e-6 cm3/s at 10-100 K  
  > "with a typical formation rate $k_f \sim 10^{-9}$ cm$^3$ s$^{-1}$" (3.4 Chemistry of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
   - assumption: PDR conditions with n(C+) ~ n(e)  
  > "In PDR conditions where $n({\rm C}^+) \approx n({\rm e})$" (3.4.2 The route of methane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with empirical MH+/M versus proton affinity correlation of earlier work  
  > "found a good empirical correlation between the abundance ratio of a molecule M and its protonated form MH$^+$ and the proton affinity of M" (3.4.1 The route of acetylene)

## Qualitative findings

- The absorption feature is seen only toward the SW image, so it arises in the z=0.89 absorber, not the Milky Way  
  > "We thus have strong evidence that the lines arise in the $z=0.89$ molecular absorber." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Ortho and para C2H3+ lines overlap in velocity but are not aligned in centroid; time variability of the quasar may explain this  
  > "The spectra of the ortho and para lines are not exactly aligned in velocity centroids but clearly overlap in velocity." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Methane route is likely more efficient than acetylene route in PDR conditions  
  > "The methane route is shown to be likely more efficient in PDR conditions." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Absorber toward PKS 1830-211 has reduced line confusion and a quasar in a high flux state  
  > "our detection with ALMA could only be achieved because the quasar was in a phase of high flux density" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$); "the greatly reduced line confusion in the absorption spectrum" (Summary and conclusions)
- Why this method: Absorption favours low-energy transitions; CMB excitation dominates the spectrum  
  > "the absorption technique favors observations of low-energy level transitions" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Alternative: Atmospheric lines or instrumental artifacts rejected because no counterpart in NE spectra  
  > "There is no counterpart in the NE spectra at the same velocities, removing the possibility of spurious instrumental artifacts or of atmospheric lines." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Alternative: Milky Way origin and z=0 identification rejected  
  > "the non-detection of signal on the NE spectra also indicates that the SW absorption feature does not arise from interstellar medium in the Milky Way" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$); "could not find any convincing candidate" (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Alternative: Acetylene (C2H2) judged unlikely as the main parent of C2H3+ in the absorber  
  > "the abundance of C$_2$H$_2$ in PKS 1830$-$211 SW is likely too small for it to be the main parent of the observed C$_2$H$_3^+$" (3.4.1 The route of acetylene)
- How they decided it was enough: Judged C2H3+ most likely carrier after matching frequency, being the sole low-energy candidate, and plausibility in a hydrocarbon-rich absorber  
  > "At this stage, we already consider C$_2$H$_3^+$ as the most likely molecule responsible for the absorption feature observed in the SW line of sight of PKS 1830$-$211." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Limitation (authors): LTE assumption may affect column densities  
  > "We note, however, that those column densities could be affected by our simple assumption of LTE." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Limitation (authors): Ortho and para lines taken eight years apart, so OPR cannot be measured and quasar structure may have changed  
  > "Since the spectra have been taken eight years apart, it is therefore possible that the quasar illumination changed between the two epochs." (3.1 Identification of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation model uses crude collision rates  
  > "Crude estimates of inelastic collision rates have been made" (3.3 Excitation of C$_2$H$_3^+$)
- Limitation (authors): Limited S/N prevents determining the nature of the absorbing gas from the line profile  
  > "it is difficult to conclude on the nature of the C$_2$H$_3^+$ absorbing gas by comparing its line profile to previous observations" (3.5 Nature of the C$_2$H$_3^+$ absorbing gas toward PKS 1830$-$211)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Detection rests on a single ortho line at ~0.1% rms; no independent second line confirms it
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Para line from a different epoch with different quasar state limits consistency checks
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Column densities rely on a Gaussian fit and assumed OPR of three; non-LTE result differs by a factor of ~4
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Formation chemistry is qualitative with no chemical network model run
