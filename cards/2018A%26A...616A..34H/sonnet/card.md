## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure continuum and estimate dust mass**: Continuum image peak and 3 sigma flux; subtract blackbody stellar contribution; derive dust mass from remaining flux  
  > "The residual $\sim$1.30 Jy can thus be reasonably attributed to dust emission." (3.1 Continuum emission)
   - parameter: stellar blackbody bandwidth 1.6 GHz around 230.5 GHz  
  > "by assuming black-body emission in a bandwidth 1.6 GHz around the rest frequency of 230.5 GHz" (3.1 Continuum emission)
   - assumption: kappa=7 cm2/g, mean dust temperature ~700 K (T radial power law index -0.5), dust-to-gas 2e-2, mass-loss 1.2e-7 Msun/yr, optically thin  
  > "an estimated mean dust temperature of $\sim$700K (assuming a radial temperature power-law decay with index $-$0.5)" (3.1 Continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Determine position angle of the inclination axis**: Velocity-averaged stereograms of blue and red wings, outermost contours fitted by eye with circles; axis through circle centres  
  > "The outermost contours have been fitted (by eye) with the yellow and purple circles in the right panel." (3.2.1 CO emission)
   - parameter: integrate -14 to -2 and 2 to 14 km/s  
  > "we integrate from $-$14 to $-$2$\,\, {\rm km}\, {\rm s}^{-1}$" (3.2.1 CO emission)
   - assumption: radial outflow with minimal inclination  
  > "Assuming a radial outflow, this small offset between the positions of the virtual centres of the concentric shell-like patterns should originate from a minimal inclination" (3.2.1 CO emission)
   - fitting: by eye  
  > "The outermost contours have been fitted (by eye) with the yellow and purple circles in the right panel." (12CO J=3-2 emission)
   - validation: Agrees with SO2 rotation axis  
  > "The axis of rotation agrees well with the previously determined position angle of the system" (4.2 Inner gas rotation)
3. **Characterise CO spiral and outer cut-off**: Central channel image and radius-versus-angle intensity plot; synthetic observation of smooth outflow to test the 10" cut-off  
  > "We tested this by simulating a synthetic observation of a smooth outflow with the approximate properties of the wind of EP Aqr." (3.2.1 CO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Synthetic smooth outflow drops smoothly at ~8.5" rather than abruptly  
  > "it does so smoothly (at a radius of $\sim$8.5”) and not abruptly as we observe in the data" (3.2.1 CO emission)
4. **Derive inclination from spiral velocity width**: Project maximum outflow or Keplerian velocity onto half peak width of 1.5 km/s; with and without micro-turbulence  
  > "would yield a projected velocity component of $\sim$1.5$\,\, {\rm km}\, {\rm s}^{-1}$ (half the peak width of 3$\,\, {\rm km}\, {\rm s}^{-1}$) under an inclination angle of $\sim$7$^\circ$." (4.1 Origin and nature of the spiral)
   - assumption: M*~1.5 Msun, companion at ~65 AU, micro-turbulence ~0.5 km/s  
  > "For a companion at an angular separation of $\sim$0.5” or $\sim$65 AU (see below) and a mass of EP Aqr of $\sim$1.5$\,\,{\rm M}_\odot$" (4.1 Origin and nature of the spiral)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Detect SO2 rotation**: Wide-slit (1.5") PV diagrams at 5 degree intervals compared with orthogonal ones, plus first-moment map  
  > "we have constructed position–velocity (PV) diagrams with axes at $5^\circ$ intervals through the centre of the image. We used a slit width of 1.5” to maximise the signal-to-noise ratio" (3.2.3 SO2 emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Constrain companion from SiO void and continuum non-detection**: Measure void flux and position; estimate dust attenuation of companion continuum and compare to detection limit to bound mass  
  > "then it can at most be an M6 dwarf if it is to remain below the ALMA detection limit at 230.5 GHz." (4.3 Origin of the local void observed in SiO)
   - assumption: O-rich dust opacity 7 cm2/g at 230.5 GHz, density power law index -2  
  > "Assuming oxygen-rich dust with a mean opacity of 7 cm$^{2}$ gr$^{-1}$ at 230.5 GHz, and a density power law index of -2" (4.3 Origin of the local void observed in SiO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO shows a nearly face-on spiral of ~two windings; SiO shows no spiral but a local void west of the continuum peak; SO2 shows rotation  
  > "This is the first convincing detection of a spiral morphology in an O-rich wind." (abstract)
- SiO line wings reach almost 20 km/s, beyond CO's ~12 km/s  
  > "the SiO signal exhibits a signal up to almost 20$\,\, {\rm km}\, {\rm s}^{-1}$ from line centre." (4.4 High-resolution observations reveal enhanced expansion velocities)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Refine earlier PdB/30m analysis of the unusual CO line profile with spatially resolved data  
  > "To further refine the analysis by (cite: Winters2007), in the autumn of 2016 we obtained spatially resolved cycle 4 ALMA band 6 CO, SiO, and SO$_{\rm 2}$ observations" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: WRLOF may suit Mira but not EP Aqr, given faster wind and more distant companion  
  > "These differences may imply that WRLOF provides a plausible scenario for the mass-loss morphology of Mira, but not for EP Aqr." (4.1 Origin and nature of the spiral)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cut-off at ~10" only tentatively real; missing flux partly affects large radii  
  > "We therefore tentatively conclude that this cut-off may be real, and part of the observed morphology." (3.2.1 CO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination and companion constraints rest on assumed velocities, opacity and mass, with no formal uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Axis fitted by eye
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spiral vs disk interpretation not tested by modelling
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Companion hypothesis rests on a single void and a continuum non-detection
