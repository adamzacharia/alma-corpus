   - parameter: GAIA proper motions: μRA=4.018 ± 0.039; μDec=-24.027 ± 0.028 mas/yr.  
  > "Data were corrected for proper motions using coordinates from GAIA (ICRF, $RA$= 04:55:45.846 and $Dec$= +30:33:04.292, $\mu\mathrm{RA} = 4.018 \pm 0.039$ and $\mu\mathrm{Dec} = -24.027 \pm 0.028$ mas/yr, (cite: Gaia+2022))." (Observations)

(Images and profiles)
   - software: IMAGER, KEPLER
   - parameter: Continuum at 249 GHz.  
  > "continuum image at 249 GHz" (Images and profiles)
   - parameter: C17O 2-1 and C18O 2-1 observed frequencies: 224714.385 and 219560.354 MHz.

   - parameter: Average SO 6_5-5_4 and SO 6_7-5_6 emission for the SO 6-5 corrected profile.

3. **Fit the SO ring and rotation**: Fit all three continuum-subtracted SO lines simultaneously in the Fourier plane with DISKFIT.  
  > "We use the DISKFIT radiative transfer program (cite: Pietu+2007) to analyze the data in the Fourier plane." (Modeling); "The continuum opacity is low.    - software: DISKFIT
   - parameter: Fixed distance 155.9 pc, inclination 22° and orientation -36.5°.  
  > "155.9 pc" (Table 2); "22$^\circ$" (Table 2); "-36.5$^\circ$" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Fixed V_LSR=5.85 km s^{-1}.  
  > "5.85 km s$^{-1}$" (Table 2); "Parameters with no error bars were fixed at their specified values." (Table 2)
   - parameter: H(r)=11 (r/100 au)^{1.25} au.  
  > "$H(r)= 11\, (\frac{r}{100\,\textnormal{au}})^{1.25}$ au" (Table 2)
   - parameter: Temperature exponent fixed to 0.4 after a loosely constrained fit of 0.54±0.20.  
  > "The temperature exponent being loosely constrained ($0.54\pm0.20$) was assumed to be 0.4." (Table 2)
   - assumption: LTE and azimuthal symmetry.  
  > "The model assumes LTE conditions and azimuthal symmetry." (Modeling)
   - assumption: Radial power laws for column density and rotation temperature, truncated at inner and outer radii.  
  > "We assumed that the molecular column density and the rotation temperature follow power laws as a function of radius, with the molecule distribution truncated by inner and outer radii." (Modeling)
   - assumption: Gaussian vertical molecular distribution with a radial power-law scale height; radially constant local velocity dispersion.  
  > "The vertical distribution of the molecules is assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution, with a scale height being a power law of radius." (Modeling); "The local velocity dispersion (which includes thermal and turbulent motions on scales smaller than the beam size) is taken to be radially constant." (Modeling)
   - fitting: Simultaneous three-line fit; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated.  
  > "All three SO lines were fitted simultaneously, allowing us to derive the rotation temperature of SO." (Modeling)
   - validation: Best-fit model overlaid on the SO 5_6-4_5 integrated spectrum; geometrical parameters compared with C17O.  
  > "Fig [5](#fig:so-composite) shows this model superimposed to the integrated spectrum of the SO $5_6-4_5$ line." (Modeling); "Apart from a small shift in apparent PA of the rotation axis (by $2-3^\circ$), the C$^{17}$O and SO determinations of the disk “geometrical” parameters (centre of rotation, orientation, inclination, systemic and rotation velocities) are consistent among them." (Modeling)
4. **Fit the C17O ring**: Use DiskFit to derive C17O inner and outer radii, column-density law and temperature.  
  > "Using DiskFit, we derive inner and outer radii of 125 and 286 au (confirming that the C$^{17}$O emission mostly resides inside the SO emission)." (C$^{17}$O fit); "Assuming $q=0.4$, we obtain a reference column density of $4.2\pm0.3\,10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ and an exponent $p=2.00\pm0.04$, a temperature of $28\pm4$ K (at 200 au)." (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - software: DISKFIT
   - parameter: Reference radius 200 au; temperature exponent q=0.4.  
  > "Assuming $q=0.4$, we obtain a reference column density of $4.2\pm0.3\,10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ and an exponent $p=2.00\pm0.04$, a temperature of $28\pm4$ K (at 200 au)." (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - assumption: Azimuthal symmetry.  
  > "The average temperature derived using DiskFit from C$^{17}$O data is higher, 28 K, but this assumes azimuthal symmetry." (Gas temperature:)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Asymmetry investigated separately through azimuth-dependent hyperfine fits; global-fit error bars require caution.  
  > "The distribution being very asymmetric, the errorbars should be taken with caution." (C$^{17}$O fit); "To quantitatively evaluate the impact of the asymmetry, we extracted the spectra at a radius of 170 au as a function of azimuth." (C$^{17}$O fit)
5. **Measure azimuth-dependent gas temperatures**: Extract spectra at 170 au as a function of azimuth and fit the C17O 2-1 hyperfine structure to derive excitation temperature and opacity.  
  > "To quantitatively evaluate the impact of the asymmetry, we extracted the spectra at a radius of 170 au as a function of azimuth." (C$^{17}$O fit); "Each spectrum was fitted using the hyperfine structure of the 2-1 line." (C$^{17}$O fit); "the fit allows to derive both the excitation temperature and the opacity $\tau$ of the line" (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - parameter: Beam filling factor η=1.  
  > "Figure [7](#fig:tk-C17O) shows our results, using $\eta=1$." (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - parameter: Average over 0–180° to avoid fit-nonlinearity bias; compare with 240–300°.  
  > "The red line and light-red bar indicate the value derived by averaging over the $0-180^\circ$ azimuth range to avoid the bias due to the non linearity of the fit" (C$^{17}$O fit); "The corresponding blue line shows the average over the $240-300^\circ$ azimuth range." (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - parameter: Linear resolution 100 × 55 au.  
  > "these values are obtained using a linear resolution of $100 \times 55$ au" (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - assumption: T_ex ≈ T_K because the critical density of CO isotopologues is small.  
  > "Furthermore, the critical density of CO isotopologues being small, $T_\mathrm{ex} \approx T_K$." (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - fitting: Brightness model η(1-exp(-τ(v)))(Jν(T_ex)-Jν(T_bg)); hyperfine opacity is a sum of Gaussian components.  
  > "$$T_b(v) = \eta \left(1-\exp(-\tau(v))\right) \left(J_\nu(T_\mathrm{ex}) - J_\nu(T_\mathrm{bg})\right)$$" (C$^{17}$O fit); "$$\tau(v) = \tau \sum_i  x_i \exp\left(-((v-v_i)/\delta v)^2 \right)$$" (C$^{17}$O fit)
   - validation: Inspect hyperfine residuals; shifting the lowest-velocity CDMS component by +0.13 km s^{-1} improves agreement without significantly changing derived values.  
  > "The fit residuals show that the hyperfine structure parameters taken from the CDMS database are not optimal." (C$^{17}$O fit); "Shifting the lowest velocity component by $+0.13$ km s$^{-1}$ improves the agreement with the data, but does not significantly affect the derived values." (C$^{17}$O fit)
6. **Derive H2 column density and SO abundance**: Convert the averaged C17O column density to H2 using oxygen isotopic ratios and CO/H2, then derive SO/H2.  
  > "Using isotopic ratios $[^{16}$O$/^{18}$O$] = 557$ and $[^{18}$O$/^{17}$O$] = 3.6$ (cite: Wilson_1999)" (Column density & abundances:); "the averaged surface density derived using DiskFit modeling ($\sim 4\,10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$, see Appendix [8](#app:co))" (Column density & abundances:); "a normal \[CO/H$_2$\] abundance ratio of $10^{-4}$, this yields an H$_2$ surface density of $8\,10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$, and thus a SO abundance of $\sim 1.7\,10^{-10}$." (Column density & abundances:)
   - software: DISKFIT
   - parameter: [16O/18O]=557; [18O/17O]=3.6.  
  > "Using isotopic ratios $[^{16}$O$/^{18}$O$] = 557$ and $[^{18}$O$/^{17}$O$] = 3.6$ (cite: Wilson_1999)" (Column density & abundances:)
   - assumption: CO is not depleted at temperatures of 20 K or above.  
  > "The detection of C$^{17}$O at temperatures of 20 K or above allows for a direct estimate of the H$_2$ column density since at these temperatures, CO is not expected to be depleted." (Column density & abundances:)
   - assumption: CO/H2 = 10^{-4}.  
  > "a normal \[CO/H$_2$\] abundance ratio of $10^{-4}$" (Column density & abundances:)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SO column density compared with previous NOEMA determination.  
  > "For SO, we derive an average surface density of $\sim 1.5 \cdot 10^{13}$ cm$^{-2}$ at 200 au using DISKFIT, in correct agreement with the $2.5 \cdot 10^{13}$ cm$^{-2}$ value quoted by (cite: Riviere+2020)" (Column density & abundances:)

(SO emission inside the cavity); "The red wing emission peaks at about 5 sigma and is located close to the inner disk of AB Aur and the candidate proto-planet P1 (shown as a red point)." (SO emission inside the cavity)
   - parameter: Red-wing velocity range 7.4–8.5 km/s.  
  > "in the velocity range of 7.4-8.5 km/s" (SO emission inside the cavity)
   - parameter: For the P1 comparison, add systemic velocity 5.8 km/s to P1 velocity about 2.5 km/s, giving 8.3 km/s.  
  > "Based on (cite: Boccaletti+2020), the Keplerian velocity of P1 is about 2.5 km/s." (SO emission inside the cavity); "Adding the systemic velocity (5.8 km/s), we get a projected velocity of 8.3 km/s" (SO emission inside the cavity)
   - assumption: Possible streamer falling onto an undetected circumplanetary disk of P1.

## Qualitative findings

- SO does not peak at the dust trap and is more azimuthally uniform than continuum and CO isotopologues.  
  > "SO is distributed further away from AB Aur, peaking near 250 au and is much more uniform in azimuth, only peaking slightly in the North-East and not on the dust trap" (Images and profiles)
- SO extends beyond the continuum and C17O ring.  
  > "Our fit also confirms that the SO emission is essentially located beyond the continuum ring (cite: Riviere+2020) and extends up to 400 au" (Modeling); "Using DiskFit, we derive inner and outer radii of 125 and 286 au (confirming that the C$^{17}$O emission mostly resides inside the SO emission)." (C$^{17}$O fit)
- SO rotation is Keplerian.  
  > "The velocity index is found Keplerian ($0.50\pm0.01$)." (Modeling)
- Slow shocks at outer-streamer interfaces are a plausible origin of extended SO.  
  > "Such a scenario, originally developed to explain accretion at the interface envelope-disk in Class I proto-stars, seems plausible in explaining the origin of the extended SO emission in the ring around AB Aur." (Outer accretion streamers and shocks:)
- Cavity SO may trace accretion onto the circumplanetary disk of P1.  
  > "A possibility would be that SO traces an accretion shock between the circumplanetary disk of P1 and its surroundings." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Three detected SO lines constrain SO distribution and rotation temperature; C17O and C18O complement interpretation.  
  > "We also present C$^{17}$O and C$^{18}$O 2-1 data to complement the interpretation of the SO data." (abstract); "All three SO lines were fitted simultaneously, allowing us to derive the rotation temperature of SO." (Modeling)
- Why this method: Low continuum opacity motivates continuum-subtracted fitting; hyperfine fits assess C17O asymmetry; SO rotational symmetry motivates KEPLER profiles.  
  > "The continuum opacity is low. Hence, we use continuum-subtracted data for this purpose." (Modeling); "To quantitatively evaluate the impact of the asymmetry, we extracted the spectra at a radius of 170 au as a function of azimuth." (C$^{17}$O fit); "Since this process assumes rotational symmetry, it is appropriate for the SO lines, but non optimal for C$^{17}$O because it ignores the strong azimuthal dependency observed in Fig.[4](#fig:areas)." (Keplerian corrected profiles)
- Alternative: Inner-disk origin for cavity SO remains possible because insufficient sensitivity may hide the symmetric blue wing.  
  > "A first possibility would be that this SO emission mostly originates from the inner disk of AB Aurigae." (SO emission inside the cavity); "In this case, a symmetric blue-shifted wing would be expected, but the lack of sensitivity might preclude its detection." (SO emission inside the cavity)
