## Science

- Fields: evolved_stars, stellar_physics
- Question: What is the physical nature and evolutionary origin of water fountain (WF) sources, and are they binary systems that have recently undergone common-envelope evolution?  
  > "We present ALMA observations towards sources known as water fountains that reveal they had low initial masses ($<4~{\rm M}_\odot$) and ejected a significant fraction of it over less than a few hundred years." (abstract); "To investigate the nature of WFs, we have used the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) to observe the $J=2-1$ transitions of $^{12}$C$^{16}$O, $^{13}$C$^{16}$O, $^{12}$C$^{18}$O and the $J=1-0$ transitions of $^{12}$C$^{16}$O and $^{12}$C$^{17}$O towards all 15 known WF sources." ## Qualitative findings

- 12C16O(2-1) emission is spatially unresolved (~1'' beam) for all but three WFs (IRAS 15103-5754, IRAS 16342-3814, IRAS 18460-0151), showing no signs of enhanced mass loss over extended timescales (>1000 yr)  
  > "For all but three of the sources (IRAS 15103-5457, IRAS 16342-3814, and IRAS 18460-0151), we find the $^{12}$C$^{16}$O emission to be unresolved (Fig. [2](#fig:12CO_maps)), and there are thus no signs of enhanced mass loss over an extended period of time ($> 1000$ yrs)." (Results)
- Low observed 12C16O/13C16O line ratios (2.2-6.0) in WFs are strongly affected by high optical depths rather than intrinsically low 12C/13C ratios  
  > "Hence, the low observed values of the $^{12}$C$^{16}$O/$^{13}$C$^{16}$O line ratios in some sources are strongly affected by high optical depths rather than necessarily intrinsic low carbon isotopic ratios." (Results)
- The seven WF sources without 12C18O detections are consistent with having comparable rarer-isotopologue relative fluxes, with non-detections explained by lower signal-to-noise ratio  
  > "Hence, the non-detections are most likely due to the overall poorer signal-to-noise ratio rather than intrinsically low abundances of the rarer isotopologues." (Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Rarer CO isotopologues (12C17O and 12C18O) are needed because their lower abundances allow probing the dense, slowly expanding outflow optically thinly and measuring the 17O/18O ratio (an initial mass tracer), whereas 12C16O and 13C16O are optically thick  
  > "The observations allow us to estimate the stellar masses based on the $^{17}$O/$^{18}$O ratio. This ratio is a good tracer of the initial stellar mass because the surface abundance of $^{17}$O is modified by a mixing process that is strongly dependent on the initial stellar mass." (Results); "The lower abundance of $^{12}$C$^{18}$O allows us to probe the dense slow-expanding outflow that contains most of the mass, while we estimate the lines of the main isotopologues to be very optically thick in that region." (Results)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Intermediate-mass (M >= 5 M_odot) stars at the tip of the AGB experiencing hot-bottom burning: rejected because HBB destroys 18O by orders of magnitude, incompatible with observed 12C18O fluxes and 17O/18O ratios, and statistically unlikely given the 15 known WFs  
  > "The implied abundance of $^{18}$O in their circumstellar envelopes is incompatible with these stars being intermediate-mass stars ($M \gtrsim 5$ M$_\odot$) at the tip of the AGB because, in this case, theoretical models predict $^{18}$O abundances orders of magnitude lower than we find (cite: Boothroyd1995; Karakas2016)." (Results)
- Alternative: Massive young stellar objects or main-sequence stellar mergers (red novae): disfavored by high luminosities (10^3 - 10^4 L_odot), isotopic ratios, and WF phase lifetime  
  > "The high luminosity values, isotopic ratios, and the lifetime of the water fountain phase strongly disfavor interpretations that water fountains are (massive) young-stellar objects or mergers between main-sequence stars (as is expected to be the case of red novae)." (Distances, luminosities, and nature of water fountains)
- Alternative: Radiation-pressure driven wind acceleration: rejected because radiation momentum of a 5000 L_odot star requires >= 20,000 yr to match the outflow momentum, inconsistent with the compact envelope sizes and ages (<~ 200 yr)  
  > "The momentum carried by the radiation field of a star with 5000 L$_\odot$ only matches the linear momentum of a 0.1 solar mass of gas expanding at 20 km/s after 20 thousand years. Given that the crossing time to reach the observed envelope sizes (or the upper limits) is much lower than this even for very weak constant acceleration, we conclude that radiation pressure probably does not contribute significantly to the acceleration of the outflow." (Calculation of gas masses , average mass-loss rates and isotopic ratios)
- Alternative: Chemical contamination of oxygen isotopes from a binary companion: evaluated for both prior AGB/WD mass transfer and main-sequence companions; cannot explain the full sample and still requires binary interaction  
  > "While contamination from companions can potentially affect the derived circumstellar masses and isotopic ratios for WFs, it would obviously not change our main conclusion that the WF phase is the consequence of binary interactions." (Isotopic ratios derived for the water fountains in context)
