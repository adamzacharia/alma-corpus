## Science

- Fields: stellar_physics, protoplanetary_disks, star_formation
- Question: Derive revised stellar ages for 23 pre-main sequence K- and M-type stars in Upper Scorpius by using CO-derived stellar dynamical masses to constrain isochronal ages from five pre-main sequence stellar evolutionary models.  
  > "We present revised stellar ages for 23 pre-main sequence K- and M-type stars in the Upper Scorpius star-forming region, derived by using stellar dynamical masses to constrain isochronal ages from five pre-main sequence stellar evolutionary models." (Abstract); "In this work, we use these dynamical masses to constrain the stellar age predictions of five PMS model sets, and thus derive mass-constrained isochronal ages for each target." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Obtain stellar dynamical masses from ALMA CO J=3-2 disk kinematics**: Model Keplerian disk rotation in ALMA CO J=3-2 line data combined with Gaia distances using MCMC fitting (performed in Towner et al. 2025).  
  > "Using our CO line data, combined with *Gaia* distances (cite: Gaia_i; Gaia_DR2), we modeled the Keplerian motion of each disk to derive the enclosed stellar mass." (Sample & Dynamical Masses); "We used the open-source package `pdspy` (cite: Sheehan2019), which uses a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach to derive best-fit disk parameters." (Sample & Dynamical Masses)
   - software: pdspy
   - assumption: Keplerian motion of protoplanetary disk  
  > "Using our CO line data, combined with *Gaia* distances (cite: Gaia_i; Gaia_DR2), we modeled the Keplerian motion of each disk to derive the enclosed stellar mass." (Sample & Dynamical Masses)
   - fitting: Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach in pdspy; full details in Towner et al. (2025)  
  > "We used the open-source package `pdspy` (cite: Sheehan2019), which uses a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach to derive best-fit disk parameters." (Sample & Dynamical Masses); "Full details of the model and our fitting procedure are given in (cite: Towner2025)." (Sample & Dynamical Masses)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive unconstrained isochronal masses and ages**: Generate 10,000-point Gaussian distributions in T_eff and L_bol for each source, linearly interpolate onto five PMS evolutionary track grids (BHAC15, PARSEC v1.1, PARSEC v1.2S, Feiden 2016 non-magnetic and magnetic), and compute median and 1.4826*MAD of the resulting mass and age distributions.  
  > "for each source, we generate Gaussian distributions in stellar temperature and luminosity using the Python package `numpy.random.normal`." (Methods & Results); "We feed these $T_{\rm eff}$ and $L_{\rm bol}$ into each set of evolutionary tracks using linear interpolation between the grid points, and obtain corresponding distributions of isochronal $M_{\star}$ and $\tau$." (Methods & Results); "We take the median of the $M_{\star}$ and $\tau$ distributions as the isochronal masses and ages, respectively. We take as our uncertainties the scaled median absolute deviation (1.4826$\times$MAD)[^1] of the $M_{\star}$ and $\tau$ distributions." (Methods & Results)
   - software: numpy.random.normal
   - parameter: 10,000 points per Gaussian distribution in T_eff and L_bol  
  > "Each distribution has 10,000 points." (Methods & Results)
   - parameter: Track points restricted to within 3 sigma  
  > "Ages in columns 2-6 are the median of all ages returned by that track within 3$\sigma$." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Gaussian error distributions for T_eff and L_bol  
  > "for each source, we generate Gaussian distributions in stellar temperature and luminosity using the Python package `numpy.random.normal`." (Methods & Results)
   - assumption: For a normal distribution, 1.4826 x MAD is equivalent to 1 sigma  
  > "For a normal distribution, 1.4826$\times$MAD is equivalent to 1$\sigma$." (Methods & Results)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo sampling (10,000 draws) with linear grid interpolation  
  > "Each distribution has 10,000 points. We feed these $T_{\rm eff}$ and $L_{\rm bol}$ into each set of evolutionary tracks using linear interpolation between the grid points, and obtain corresponding distributions of isochronal $M_{\star}$ and $\tau$." (Methods & Results)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive dynamical-mass-constrained isochronal ages and summary statistics**: Filter the (M_*, tau) pairs from each PMS track to retain only those where isochronal M_* agrees with dynamical mass within uncertainties, then compute median tau, scaled MAD, age ratios (tau_all/tau_dyn), absolute age changes (Delta tau), and intra-source age spreads (sigma_tau).  
  > "Next, we discard all ($M_{\star}$$,\tau$) pairs for which the isochronal $M_{\star}$ does not agree with our dynamical mass within uncertainties (see Table [\[source_properties\]](#source_properties)). We then calculate the median $\tau$ and scaled MAD of all remaining ages." (Methods & Results); "This table also presents summary statistics for the ratio of unconstrained to mass-constrained ages ($\tau_{\rm all}$/$\tau_{\rm dyn}$), absolute changes in age ($\Delta\tau$), and intra-source age spreads ($\sigma_{\tau}$)." (Mass-Constrained Isochronal Ages)
   - parameter: Sample restricted to sources with both unconstrained and mass-constrained ages (automatically M_dyn <= 1 M_sun)  
  > "Because some sources do not have mass-constrained ages in some or all model sets, we restrict our statistical analysis in the following subsections to only those sources that have *both* an unconstrained and a mass-constrained age for a given model set." (Mass-Constrained Isochronal Ages); "Because the sources with M$_{\star,\,\rm dyn}$ $>$ 1 M$_{\odot}$ have no mass-constrained ages in Table [\[ages_table\]](#ages_table), our analysis here excludes those more massive sources automatically." (Mass-Constrained Isochronal Ages)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared restricted unconstrained sample statistics against full unconstrained sample statistics, finding they are identical within uncertainties; recalculated sigma_tau excluding outlier model sets.  
  > "However, we have calculated all statistics for the full unconstrained sample as well, and find that they are identical within uncertainties with the restricted unconstrained sample." (Mass-Constrained Isochronal Ages); "We recalculate $\sigma_\tau$ for these two sources excluding the relevant outlier model set under both constraint conditions." (Increasing Agreement Among Model Sets)
4. **Test for linear and non-linear correlations between stellar mass, age, and model accuracy**: Calculate Spearman's rho correlation coefficients and p-values between dynamical mass, mass-constrained age, isochronal-to-dynamical mass ratio, and unconstrained-to-constrained age ratio across all five model sets, and visually inspect scatterplots for non-linear trends.  
  > "We calculate Spearman’s $\rho$ correlation coefficients and $p$-values for each model set for the following quantities: isochronal-to-dynamical mass ratio versus dynamical mass, age ratio versus mass, mass ratio versus age, age ratio versus age, and mass ratio versus age ratio." (Linear Correlations with Mass and Age); "Therefore, in addition to the statistical analysis discussed above, we examine our distributions directly to search for more complex trends." (Linear Correlations with Mass and Age)
   - parameter: Statistical significance threshold of >= 4 sigma (p <= 6.3e-5)  
  > "We find that, in general, few model sets show statistically-significant correlations ($\geq$4$\sigma$) between dynamical mass or age and over- or underestimation of those values, but several do show $>$3$\sigma$ correlations." (Linear Correlations with Mass and Age); "4$\sigma$: p $\leq$ 6.3$\times$10$^{-5}$" (Table 4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeated correlation calculations excluding sources with confirmed or candidate companions.  
  > "We repeat these calculations excluding those sources with confirmed or candidate companions, in case any trends are significantly influenced by those few sources." (Linear Correlations with Mass and Age)
5. **Test for age bias in the 23-star sample against the broader Upper Sco K- and M-type population**: Select Upper Sco K- and M-type stars from Fang & Herczeg (2025) using Luhman (2022) and Ratzenböck et al. (2023a) kinematic group criteria, compute their Feiden (2016) magnetic isochronal ages and masses, and perform 2-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Anderson-Darling tests against the 23-star sample.  
  > "We generate isochronal masses and ages for these large samples using the (cite: Feiden2016) magnetic models." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample); "We perform a 2-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (KS test) to evaluate the statistical likelihood that our dynamical mass sample is drawn from a different parent population than Upper Sco as a whole." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample); "We also perform a 2-sample Anderson-Darling test." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample)
   - parameter: Luhman (2022) kinematic groups u, ul, ulc, uc (2059 K- and M-type sources)  
  > "Using the (cite: Luhman2022) group classifications, we consider all K- and M-type stars that are primarily associated with Upper Sco (kinematic groups ‘u,’ ‘ul,’ ‘ulc,’ and ‘uc’ in that system). We find a total of 2059 sources using these criteria." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample)
   - parameter: Ratzenböck et al. (2023a) SigMA groups 1 through 9 inclusive (2469 K- and M-type sources)  
  > "Using the (cite: Ratzenbock2023a) group classifications, we consider all K- and M-type stars in SigMA groups 1 through 9 inclusive (cite: Ratzenbock2023a). We find a total of 2469 sources using these criteria." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Calculated all population statistics and KS/Anderson-Darling tests both with and without low-mass grid-edge pile-up sources (0.08499 to 0.08501 M_sun), and using two independent kinematic group definitions.  
  > "We calculate all statistics in the following discussion both with and without these grid-edge sources." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample); "Regardless of which Upper Sco selection criteria are used, and regardless of the inclusion/exclusion of the grid-edge sources, we find no statistically-significant difference between our dynamical-mass sample and the broader Upper Sco population." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample)

## Qualitative findings

- Applying a dynamical-mass constraint to isochronal ages increases stellar ages for standard non-magnetic models (from 3-5 Myr to 6-9 Myr), bringing low-mass K- and M-type star ages into agreement with the 8-11 Myr age of Upper Sco derived from early-type stars.  
  > "We find that mass-constrained stellar ages for all model sets are more consistent with the older, $\sim$8-11 Myr age for Upper Sco derived using earlier-type stars." (Abstract); "Most standard (nonmagnetic) models produce younger ages (3-5 Myr) when unconstrained, but older ages (6-9 Myr) when constrained by dynamical mass." (Abstract)
- Applying a dynamical-mass constraint reduces the scatter in derived age between different PMS evolutionary model sets at both the population level and the individual source level (for 14 of 17 sources).  
  > "In contrast, the median mass-constrained ages span a narrower range (6 to 12 Myr), agree within uncertainties in all cases, and in fact are identical for three of the five model sets (9 Myr)." (Increasing Agreement Among Model Sets); "At the individual source level, we find a decrease in $\sigma_\tau$ for 14 out of the 17 sources that have at least two mass-constrained ages." (Increasing Agreement Among Model Sets)
- The Feiden (2016) magnetic models are the most reliable of the five tested PMS model sets, yielding 9-10 Myr median ages regardless of whether the dynamical mass constraint is applied.  
  > "Models that account for global magnetic fields consistently produce the best match to our observations: they change comparatively little when the mass constraint is applied, and produce 9-10 Myr ages under both unconstrained and mass-constrained conditions." (Abstract); "we conclude that the magnetic models of (cite: Feiden2016) are the most reliable isochronal method (of the five examined in this work) for determining stellar mass and age in the absence of independent mass constraints." (Overall Evaluation of the PMS Model Sets)
- Four of the five PMS model sets (all except PARSEC v1.2S) exhibit consistent non-linear mass-dependent inaccuracies: tending to overestimate mass and age at M_* <= 0.25 M_sun, most severely underestimating mass and age around 0.25-0.6 M_sun, and improving toward >= 0.75 M_sun.  
  > "Four of the five models (excepting PARSEC v1.2S) show the same mass-dependent behavior: the models are most likely to overestimate mass and age for $M_{\star}$ $\lesssim$ 0.25 M$_{\odot}$, they are most likely underestimate mass and age for $M_{\star}$ $\sim$0.5 M$_{\odot}$, and they show improved agreement with dynamical results as $M_{\star}$ rises to $\gtrsim$ 0.75 M$_{\odot}$." (Summary)
- The 23-star dynamical-mass sample shows no statistically significant age bias relative to the broader Upper Sco K- and M-type population (~2000-2500 stars).  
  > "Regardless of which Upper Sco selection criteria are used, and regardless of the inclusion/exclusion of the grid-edge sources, we find no statistically-significant difference between our dynamical-mass sample and the broader Upper Sco population." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample)
- The ~2x increase in low-mass stellar ages when constrained by dynamical masses supports literature findings that median protoplanetary disk lifetimes are ~2x longer than previously thought, providing >= 4 Myr of additional time for planet formation.  
  > "We find that the typical increase in median stellar age from standard models ($\sim$2$\times$ when the mass constraint is applied) is consistent with literature findings that suggest a median disk lifetime of $\sim$2$\times$ previous estimates (cite: Fang2025). Our results suggest that planet-formation timescales may thus be $\gtrsim$4 Myr longer than previously thought." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Upper Sco is the nearest massive star-forming region, exhibits a well-known age discrepancy between early- and late-type stars, and hosts tens of CO-detectable gas disks around K- and M-type stars suitable for Keplerian dynamical mass measurements.  
  > "Its overall well-studied nature, seemingly mass-dependent inferred ages, and large population of CO-detectable disks make Upper Sco an excellent candidate for a systematic calibration of isochronal ages by dynamical methods." (The Upper Scorpius OB Association)
- Why this method: Keplerian disk fitting yields dynamical masses but not ages directly; combining dynamical masses with HRD positions (T_eff, L_bol) across multiple PMS evolutionary tracks enables a direct comparison between unconstrained and mass-constrained isochronal ages.  
  > "A more direct comparison, however, would be between a purely isochronal age and a mass-constrained age for all PMS models." (Introduction); "Keplerian fitting of the protostellar disk does not directly yield a stellar age. Therefore, following methods established in the literature (cite: Soderblom2014; Rizzuto2016; David2019), we use five pre-main sequence evolutionary tracks to derive two stellar ages for each source:" (Methods & Results)
- Alternative: Lithium depletion boundary (LDB) and other age methods omitted from literature comparison because they are less accurate for the young (5-11 Myr) age range of Upper Sco.  
  > "However, some methods are known to be less accurate for young and/or low-mass sources (cite: Soderblom2014). As Upper Sco is generally considered to be younger than this (5-11 Myr), we restrict our comparison to two alternate methods: eclipsing binaries and dynamical traceback modeling." (Comparison with Existing Literature for Upper Sco)
- Alternative: Starspot evolutionary models (e.g., SP15 / Somers et al.) were discussed as a plausible physical explanation for non-linear mass-dependent trends, but were not tested in this work.  
  > "A model that combines (global) magnetic fields with the observational color and temperature effects of (localized) starspots, then, could potentially be a fruitful avenue of future research. However, as we do not test any starspot models in this work, such a possibility is highly speculative at this time." (On the Consistency of Trends Across Model Sets)
- Alternative: PARSEC v1.2S models rejected as reliable for this mass and age range due to severe mass overestimation at low masses, large population age scatter, and non-systematic behavior.  
  > "However, we do conclude that, for the mass and age range covered by our sample, the PARSEC v1.2S models are not as reliable as the other four model sets tested." (Overall Evaluation of the PMS Model Sets)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Five sources (all with M_dyn >= 1 M_sun) have no overlap between their CO dynamical masses and isochronal masses in any of the five PMS model sets, yielding no mass-constrained ages (attributed to binary companions for two sources and possible non-Keplerian CO components for three sources).  
  > "There are five sources with no mass-constrained ages in any model set, and four additional sources with no mass-constrained age in at least one model set." (Methods & Results); "The five sources with no mass-constrained ages at all are, without exception, those sources with $M_{\star}$ $\geq$ 1 M$_{\odot}$." (Methods & Results); "In that work, we raised the possibility that the CO data for these three sources contained some components that were insufficiently captured by the Keplerian-disk model;" (Methods & Results)
- Limitation (authors): Decreases in median relative age uncertainty when applying the dynamical mass constraint are not statistically significant.  
  > "However, we note that the decreases are not statistically significant in any case, i.e., the constrained and unconstrained medians agree within uncertainties for all model sets." (Increasing Stellar Ages)
- Limitation (authors): Even the best-performing Feiden (2016) magnetic models can have large deviations for individual sources and suffer from non-linear mass-dependent inaccuracies (such as overestimating mass by up to ~100% at <= 0.25 M_sun and underestimating by ~50% at ~0.3 M_sun).  
  > "indeed, they show a maximum overestimation of mass up to $\sim$100% in the $\leq$0.25 M$_{\odot}$ mass range, and maximum underestimation of $\sim$50% at $\sim$0.3 M$_{\odot}$ (excluding the confirmed or candidate binary sources marked in grey)." (Non-linear Trends with Mass and Age); "However, we caution that these magnetic models can have high deviations from the dynamical mass-constrained results for individual sources." (Overall Evaluation of the PMS Model Sets)
- Limitation (authors): The origin of the non-linear mass-dependent trends across models (systematic errors in input T_eff/L_bol vs missing stellar physics such as starspots) is not determined in this work.  
  > "It is beyond the scope of this paper to robustly determine the origin of this trend, but we briefly discuss two possibilities here." (On the Consistency of Trends Across Model Sets)
- Limitation (authors): The sample size of 23 sources (and <=18 with mass-constrained ages) is small compared to the overall Upper Sco population.  
  > "To the extent that there differences between our sample and the overall population of K- and M-type stars in Upper Sco, these may be attributable to our significantly smaller sample size, and in any case, the population medians all agree within their respective uncertainties." (Testing for Age Bias in Our Sample)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All ALMA data reduction, imaging, and dynamical mass MCMC fitting were performed in the companion paper (Towner et al. 2025); any systematic bias in the CO dynamical masses (e.g., disk inclination degeneracies, surface density/temperature parameterization, or unresolved companions) directly propagates into the mass-constrained ages here.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Five out of 23 targets (22% of the sample, including all stars with M_dyn >= 1 M_sun) fail to produce a dynamical-mass-constrained age in any model set because the CO dynamical mass does not overlap the HRD-derived isochronal mass.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sample consists exclusively of disk-bearing stars with surviving CO gas disks; although KS/AD tests against Fang & Herczeg (2025) HRD ages show no statistically significant difference, the p-values (0.002-0.09) are marginal and disk-bearing gas-rich stars could still represent a modestly younger or distinct subset.
