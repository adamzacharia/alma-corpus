## Analysis recipe

1. **image the 232 GHz continuum**: Combine ACA and 12m continuum visibilities, multi frequency synthesis, natural weighting, tclean hogbom without a mask  
  > "After calibration, the continuum ACA and 12m array visibilities were combined and Fourier transformed using the multi frequency synthesis mode and natural weighting for maximum sensitivity." (2 Observations); "The resulting image was cleaned using the `tclean` command with the `hogbom` (cite: hog74) option and no special mask because the emission was only detected toward the protostar." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting, MFS, hogbom, no mask  
  > "natural weighting for maximum sensitivity." (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **measure continuum flux and estimate disk mass**: 2D Gaussian fit to the 232 GHz peak with imfit; disk mass from the integrated flux  
  > "To characterize the 232 GHz emission peak we fit it with a 2D Gaussian using the CASA command `imfit`." (3.1 Continuum data); "the estimated flux of 76 mJy corresponds to a disk mass of 0.03 M$_\odot$." (3.1 Continuum data)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: dust temperature 20 K, optically thin emission, dust emissivity 0.9 cm2/g, gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "Assuming a typical dust temperature of 20 K, optically thin emission, an emissivity per dust mass of 0.9 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: oss94), and a standard gas to dust ratio of 100 (cite: boh78)" (3.1 Continuum data)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian (imfit)  
  > "we fit it with a 2D Gaussian using the CASA command `imfit`." (3.1 Continuum data)
   - validation: Compared with values at about the same frequency from san09 and eDisk  
  > "These values are in good agreement with those derived at approximately the same frequency by (cite: san09) and also by the eDisk ALMA project." (3.1 Continuum data)
3. **image the spectral-line cubes**: Resample calibrated ACA+12m visibilities to 0.5 km/s, subtract continuum from line-free channels, Fourier transform and clean with tclean multiscale and auto-multithresh  
  > "For the spectral-line emission, the calibrated ACA and 12m-array visibilities were resampled to a velocity resolution of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$, continuum-subtracted using line-free channels, and Fourier-transformed." (2 Observations); "The maps were cleaned with `tclean` using the `multiscale` (cite: cor08) option (scale sizes of 0, 5, 10, and 20 pixels) and `auto-multithresh` masking (cite: kep20)." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: multiscale scales 0, 5, 10, 20 pixels; 0.5 km/s channels  
  > "(scale sizes of 0, 5, 10, and 20 pixels) and `auto-multithresh` masking (cite: kep20)." (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Imaging tests with alternative weighting or cleaning options gave no significantly different maps  
  > "Several tests were carried out to optimize the choice of imaging parameters, although the use of alternative values for the weighting scheme or the cleaning options did not produce significantly different maps." (2 Observations)
4. **test flux recovery against single-dish data**: Convert cubes to GILDAS format, synthesize CO(2-1) spectra at 11 arcsec resolution and compare with IRAM 30m spectra  
  > "Once the images were cleaned using CASA, the data were converted to the GILDAS[^1] format for further processing." (2 Observations); "we synthesized a series of CO(2–1) spectra assuming an $11''$ angular resolution and compared them with the IRAM 30m single-dish observations of (cite: taf04)." (2 Observations)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: 11 arcsec angular resolution for synthesized spectra  
  > "we synthesized a series of CO(2–1) spectra assuming an $11''$ angular resolution" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: ALMA fully recovers the EHV regime but misses emission below about 10 km/s from the ambient cloud  
  > "shows that the ALMA observations fully recovered the emission of the EHV outflow regime, which is the main focus of this study." (2 Observations)
5. **map SHV and EHV velocity structure**: Integrated-intensity, rotated channel and centroid velocity maps; outflow rotated clockwise by 30.4 deg with y-axis reversed in the red lobe; lobes divided into six 8 km/s intervals; SHV/EHV boundary at 30 km/s  
  > "This has been done by rotating the outflow clockwise by 30.4 degrees, which is the outflow position angle determined by (cite: san09), and by reversing the y-axis in the red lobe." (3.3 Velocity structure of the IRAS 04166 outflow); "the emission range of each lobe has been divided into six velocity intervals of 8 km s$^{-1}$." (3.3 Velocity structure of the IRAS 04166 outflow)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: SHV/EHV boundary 30 km/s from ambient cloud, V_LSR = 6.7 km/s  
  > "we define the boundary between the SHV and EHV regimes at 30 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the ambient cloud ($V_\mathrm{LSR} = 6.7$ km s$^{-1}$)" (3.2 Overview of the line data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **build position-velocity diagrams along the outflow axis**: PV diagrams of CO(2-1) along the axis using a 4 arcsec wide slit, shown as absolute velocity displacement from the ambient cloud  
  > "These diagrams have been generated as if a $4''$-wide slit had been placed along the outflow axis, although the exact value of the slit width has very little effect on the result." (3.4 Connection between the SHV and EHV regimes)
   - parameter: slit width 4 arcsec  
  > "as if a $4''$-wide slit had been placed along the outflow axis" (3.4 Connection between the SHV and EHV regimes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Slit width stated to have very little effect on the result  
  > "although the exact value of the slit width has very little effect on the result." (3.4 Connection between the SHV and EHV regimes)
7. **build the LEAF ballistic model of lateral ejection**: Independent particles ejected from an internal working surface moving along the jet axis, with trajectories set by conservation of combined linear momentum of ejected and swept-up gas in a shear-flow environment; several planes combined to make a 3D model  
  > "The trajectory of each particle is determined at consecutive time steps by requiring conservation of the combined linear momentum of the ejected gas and the swept-up material." (4.2 A toy model of the lateral ejection from a jet); "we simulated the three-dimensional structure of the outflow by combining multiple LEAF solutions, each representing the ejection of gas in a different plane around the jet axis" (4.3 Different modes of lateral ejection and their signature in the PV diagram)
   - software: LEAF (authors' own model)
   - parameter: jet velocity 61 km/s; ejection followed for 650 yr; inclination 47 deg (and 90 deg); lateral velocity up to 15 km/s  
  > "In all models, we assumed a jet velocity of 61 km s$^{-1}$ for consistency with (cite: taf17), and we followed the ejection of jet material for 650 yr" (4.3 Different modes of lateral ejection and their signature in the PV diagram); "This instantaneous ejection was assumed to release gas that has a uniform mix of lateral velocities between zero and 15 km s$^{-1}$." (4.3 Different modes of lateral ejection and their signature in the PV diagram)
   - parameter: constant n0(r) adopted for the density profile  
  > "the radial dependence of the density profile plays a minimal role in the solution so, for simplicity, we take $n_0(r)$ as a constant value." (4.2 A toy model of the lateral ejection from a jet)
   - assumption: Ambient gas has a shear-flow velocity field with radial speed depending on angle from the jet axis, and a limb-brightened density profile  
  > "we model the gas around the jet as having a shear-flow pattern in which the material flows radially from the protostar with a speed that depends on the angle $\theta$ with respect to the jet direction" (4.2 A toy model of the lateral ejection from a jet)
   - fitting: No formal fit; four ejection modes compared qualitatively with the observed PV diagram  
  > "A single-event ejection containing multiple lateral velocities therefore provides the best fit to the the PV diagram of the individual EHV peaks in the IRAS 04166 outflow." (4.3 Different modes of lateral ejection and their signature in the PV diagram)
   - validation: Density-profile radial dependence tested with r^-2, r^-1 and constant; resulting PV diagrams changed little  
  > "we run models of the best-fit solution (described in the next section) using $n_0(r)$ factors with $r^{-2}$, $r^{-1}$, and no radial dependence." (4.2 A toy model of the lateral ejection from a jet)
8. **date the individual EHV ejections with multiple LEAF runs**: Run multiple LEAF instances with identical jet and shear-flow conditions, varying only the ejection time, and fit the positions of the EHV heads in the blue-lobe PV diagram; build a synthetic PV diagram assuming optically thin CO(2-1)  
  > "By fitting the position of their EHV heads, we found that the eight episodes need to have the following approximate ages: 120, 180, 310, 400, 500, 650, 850, and 1350 yr." (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram); "We can still estimate an approximate level of EHV emission by using the distribution of moving mass and assuming that the CO(2–1) emission is optically thin." (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram)
   - software: LEAF (authors' own model)
   - parameter: jet velocity 61 km/s, inclination 47 deg, eight ejections  
  > "As before, we assumed a constant jet velocity of 61 km $^{-1}$ and an inclination with the line of sight of $47^\circ$." (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram)
   - assumption: Optically thin CO(2-1) in the EHV regime  
  > "This assumption is consistent with our lack of detection of $^{13}$CO(2–1) in the EHV regime" (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram)
   - fitting: Fit by eye of EHV head positions; the model is not meant to fit the emission exactly  
  > "since we did not aim to fit exactly the emission but to show how its main features can be understood" (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram)
   - validation: Qualitative comparison of model and observed PV diagram (sawtooth, fingers, flattening)  
  > "a model with multiple instantaneous ejections reproduces most of the features seen in the PV diagram" (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram)

## Qualitative findings

- The slow (SHV) component occupies two opposed conical, limb-brightened lobes with a shear-flow velocity pattern  
  > "the slow outflow component is distributed in two opposed conical lobes and has a shear-flow pattern with velocity increasing toward the axis." (abstract)
- SiO(5-4) traces mainly the EHV regime; SO and 13CO show little or no EHV emission  
  > "The brightest of our additional lines is SiO(5–4), which traces predominantly the EHV regime" (3.2 Overview of the line data); "no evidence for EHV emission is seen in $^{13}$CO(2–1)" (3.2 Overview of the line data)
- No evidence in the data for an unperturbed jet component, except possibly unresolved SiO peaks near the protostar  
  > "The IRAS 04166 data, however, do not show any evidence for such an unperturbed jet component" (4.5 Evolution and detectability of the unperturbed jet)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: New ALMA mosaics to map the full length of the outflow and cover multiple EHV peaks  
  > "we carried out new ALMA observations to map the full length of the main flow and cover multiple EHV peaks." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Published jet simulations assume jet and cloud conditions that do not match IRAS 04166, so a simplified toy model was built  
  > "Unfortunately, these simulations cannot be easily compared to our ALMA data since they assume specific jet and cloud conditions that do not necessarily coincide with those of the IRAS 04166 outflow." (4.2 A toy model of the lateral ejection from a jet)
- Alternative: Hollow cone with constant velocity (projection effect) rejected as explanation of the opening-angle trend  
  > "An alternative interpretation in terms of a projection effect caused by gas moving in a hollow cone with constant velocity can be ruled out." (3.3 Velocity structure of the IRAS 04166 outflow)
- Alternative: Continuous lateral ejection with a single constant velocity rejected  
  > "We therefore conclude that a lateral ejection model having a constant single velocity fails to reproduce the observed PV diagram." (4.3 Different modes of lateral ejection and their signature in the PV diagram)
- Alternative: Continuous ejection with a mixture of lateral velocities rejected  
  > "A multiple-velocity model, therefore, also fails to reproduce the observations." (4.3 Different modes of lateral ejection and their signature in the PV diagram)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ALMA data miss significant emission below about 10 km/s from the ambient cloud  
  > "The ALMA observations, however, miss significant emission at velocities lower than about 10 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the ambient cloud despite including ACA data" (2 Observations)
- Limitation (authors): PV diagrams are limited in sensitivity by short integration time  
  > "Our PV diagrams of the IRAS 04166 outflow are significantly limited in sensitivity due to the short integration time of the observations" (3.4 Connection between the SHV and EHV regimes)
- Limitation (authors): LEAF model is highly simplified and cannot derive line intensities  
  > "Given its simplicity, we consider this model only as a first step toward a more realistic description of the interaction between a jet shock and its surrounding flow." (4.2 A toy model of the lateral ejection from a jet); "it does not solve the equation of radiative transfer, so it cannot be used to derive line intensities." (4.4 Simulating the multiple ejection events of the IRAS 04166 PV diagram)
- Limitation (authors): Additional lines (SiO, SO, 13CO) lack sensitivity because integration time was optimized for CO(2-1)  
  > "the emission from the additional lines lacks sensitivity and therefore provides only limited information about the outflow." (3.2 Overview of the line data)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LEAF model parameters are chosen by eye with no uncertainties; conclusions on ejection ages and instantaneous ejection are model dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Finger interpretation rests on weak emission and low sensitivity; alternative origins (e.g. wide-angle wind) are not tested quantitatively
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing short-spacing low-velocity emission may bias the SHV shear-flow analysis
