## Analysis recipe

1. **Search ALMA cubes for millimeter line emission, create moment maps, and measure continuum fluxes**: Blindly searched ALMA cubes for line emission using SOFIA v2.0 with residual continuum subtraction and the smooth+clip (S+C) algorithm; created moment 0, 1, and 2 maps; smoothed CO(7-6) to CO(3-2) resolution for line ratio map; measured continuum flux densities in Band 3 and Band 6 images.  
  > "The ALMA cubes were blindly searched for line emission using the H i Source Finding Application SOFIA v2.0. [46,47] We set SOFIA to subtract the residual continuum and use the smooth+clip (S+C) algorithm with a combination of spatial and spectral kernels matched to the expected signal size in a given band." (Millimetre line and continuum emission); "We set the threshold to 3.5𝜎 for detection. For the moment 1 and 2 maps, we used only those pixels where the signal was detected at 1.5 times the local rms value." (Millimetre line and continuum emission); "Based on measurements in SPWs 21, 23 and 25 of band 6, we adopt continuum flux densities at ≈ 226 GHz to be 330±30 𝜇Jy and < 180 uJy for companion and host galaxies, respectively." (Millimetre line and continuum emission)
   - software: SOFIA v2.0
   - parameter: Detection threshold = 3.5 sigma; moment 1 and 2 pixel threshold = 1.5 times local rms; non-detection 3 sigma upper limits assume line FWHM = 100 km/s.  
  > "We set the threshold to 3.5𝜎 for detection. For the moment 1 and 2 maps, we used only those pixels where the signal was detected at 1.5 times the local rms value." (Millimetre line and continuum emission); "𝑑 For non-detections 3𝜎 upper limits estimated assuming a line FWHM of 100 km s−1." (Extended Data Table 1)
   - assumption: Line FWHM of 100 km s^-1 for 3 sigma upper limits of non-detections.  
  > "𝑑 For non-detections 3𝜎 upper limits estimated assuming a line FWHM of 100 km s−1." (Extended Data Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked consistency of Band 6 continuum across individual SPWs (SPWs 21, 23, 25 vs multiband image).  
  > "However, the corresponding emission is not consistently detected in images from individual SPWs with rms noise of ∼25 𝜇Jy beam−1. Based on measurements in SPWs 21, 23 and 25 of band 6, we adopt continuum flux densities at ≈ 226 GHz to be 330±30 𝜇Jy and < 180 uJy for companion and host galaxies, respectively." (Millimetre line and continuum emission)
2. **Decompose optical broad-band images and extract 1D spectra of the quasar, companion galaxy, and quasar host galaxy**: Fitted Subaru HSC g and r band images with Galfit using a PSF + sky + two Sersic (n=1.0) components; extracted 1D quasar spectrum and disentangled companion galaxy spectrum from 2D X-shooter PA=90 deg spectra by fitting two Moffat profiles across rebinned wavelength bins; extracted quasar host galaxy residual emission from dark troughs of saturated absorption lines.  
  > "We fitted them with the Galfit package [43] using a model consisting of a Point Spread Function (PSF) source (representing the quasar), a sky background, and two additional Sérsic profiles with index = 1.0, with free position, effective radius, axis ratio, and orientation." (Broad-band photometry); "We then fitted the observed collapsed profiles using two Moffat functions (determined by peak position, width, and normalization), representing the quasar and companion galaxy (Extended Data Fig. 2)." (X-shooter spectrum of the companion galaxy); "We constrained the quasar host galaxy emission from residual light in the dark troughs of heavily saturated absorption lines (Ly𝛼, Si ii 1260 Å, O i 1302 Å, C ii 1334 Å, Al ii 1260 Å and series of H2 and Fe ii lines) in the quasar spectrum." (The quasar host galaxy as residual emission in saturated absorption lines)
   - software: Galfit, emcee
   - parameter: Sersic index = 1.0; X-shooter rebinning: 200 pixels (40 A) for UVB/VIS, 800 pixels (480 A) for NIR; slit widths: 1.0, 0.9, 1.2 arcsec for UVB, VIS, NIR.  
  > "two additional Sérsic profiles with index = 1.0, with free position, effective radius, axis ratio, and orientation." (Broad-band photometry); "we rebinned each exposure onto a coarser grid corresponding to 200 pixels (40 Å) for UVB and VIS arms, and 800 pixels (480 Å) for the NIR arm due to sky contamination and weaker fluxes in this region." (X-shooter spectrum of the companion galaxy); "The derived Moffat profiles were used to compute and correct for slit losses at each wavelength, considering the slit widths of 1.0, 0.9 and 1.2" for UVB, VIS and NIR arms, respectively." (X-shooter spectrum of the companion galaxy)
   - assumption: Companion galaxy spatial profile width is the quadratic sum of the unresolved quasar seeing and the 0.9'' width derived from Galfit, located 0.55'' from the quasar.  
  > "For the companion galaxy component, our priors are based on the results from the broad-band image fitting: the position is taken to be 0.55" from the quasar and the width is taken as the quadratic sum of the unresolved quasar component (i.e. the seeing measured at each wavelength) plus the 0.9"-width derived from Galfit." (X-shooter spectrum of the companion galaxy)
   - fitting: Bayesian inference using affine invariant MCMC sampler within emcee with flat priors for normalization and Gaussian priors for spatial position and width.  
  > "We followed Bayesian inference by sampling the model parameters using a Python implementation of an affine invariant Monte-Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) sampler [44] within the emcee package. [45] For normalization parameters, we used flat priors. For the position and width along the spatial axis, we used Gaussian priors centered at 0” and the seeing value for the quasar component." (X-shooter spectrum of the companion galaxy)
   - validation: Photometric points from HSC PSF extraction are consistent with the extracted X-shooter companion spectrum; residual flux in z=2.66 saturated troughs is absent in intervening Ly-alpha forest troughs, confirming it is not an artifact.  
  > "The photometric points obtained using PSF extraction from HSC Subaru imaging are consistent with this spectrum." (X-shooter spectrum of the companion galaxy); "However, this emission is absent in the troughs of strong Ly𝛼 forest lines, which fully absorb light from the background objects (quasar, host and companion galaxies) due to their origin in large-scale intergalactic structures. This confirms that the residual flux in the 𝑧 = 2.66 lines is real and not a data reduction artifact." (The quasar host galaxy as residual emission in saturated absorption lines)

   - parameter: Stellar birth cloud lifetime = 10 Myr; velocity dispersion = 500 km s^-1; free U_min and gamma for dust emission.  
  > "We assumed a stellar birth cloud lifetime of 10 Myr and a velocity dispersion of 500 km s−1." [51]" (Spectrophotometric modelling of the galaxies)
   - assumption: Double power law star formation history (SFH) model and standard Calzetti dust extinction law; standard LambdaCDM cosmology (H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, Omega_m = 0.3).

   - fitting: Nested sampling within bagpipes; Gaussian priors on redshift (from CO(7-6)) and metallicity (from absorption lines); uniform priors on SFH slope (decimal) and log-scale for other parameters.

5. **Analyse differential dust extinction and fit multi-component Voigt profiles to HI, H2, metal, CI, and CO absorption lines**: Compared quasar spectrum to X-shooter composite reddened with an SMC-like extinction curve (quasar pair method); fitted multi-component Voigt profiles to HI, H2 (2 components), low-ionization metals (18 components), CI fine-structure levels (6 components), and CO A-X bands (2 components) using the spectro package with a partial coverage factor C_f.  
  > "Following the quasar pair method, [29,63,64,65,66] the best matching is obtained by reddening the composite with an Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) like extinction curve [67] and visual extinction 𝐴V ≈ 0.20 ± 0.10." (Differential dust extinction); "We modelled the absorption lines using multi-component Voigt profile fitting using spectro package. The model parameters were constrained following a Bayesian approach to derive the posterior distribution function using the affine invariant MCMC sampler. [44]" (Line profile fitting method); "A partial coverage factor, 𝐶𝑓, was then included in the model. [70] To reduce the number of free parameters, a single 𝐶𝑓 value was assumed for all components at all wavelengths, as supported by the nearly constant residual emission across wavelengths." (Line profile fitting method)
   - software: spectro
   - parameter: 11th order Chebyshev polynomial adjustment for HI continuum; 2 components for H2; 18 components for metals (Gaussian prior on b: mean 6 km/s, sigma 2 km/s); 6 components for CI (T_CMB = 10 K at z = 2.662); 2 components for CO (b = 3 +/- 1 km/s, log T [K] = 2 +/- 0.3).  
  > "The total H i column density is determined by fitting the Ly-𝛼 absorption profile together with the unabsorbed continuum, initially estimated using the reddened quasar composite and adjusted with an 11th order Chebyshev polynomial." (Neutral hydrogen); "We fitted the data using an 18-component model, with Doppler parameters tied between different low-ionization metals for each component, i.e. implicitly assuming turbulence-dominated broadening." (Metal lines); "We used a two-component model with priors on the Doppler parameters 𝑏 = 3 ± 1 km s−1 and redshifts fixed to those from H2 absorption lines, obtaining log 𝑁(CO) < 13.6 and 13.99+0.15−0.09 in components A and B, respectively." (CO absorption)
   - assumption: SMC-like extinction curve; single partial coverage factor C_f across components and wavelengths; tied Doppler b for J=0,1 H2 levels; turbulence-dominated broadening for low-ionization metals; one-zone radiative + collisional excitation + CMB (10 K) for CI fine-structure levels; Boltzmann rotational distribution for CO.  
  > "We tied Doppler parameters (𝑏) for 𝐽 = 0 and 1 rotational levels, assuming that they trace the bulk of the cloud and are co-spatial." (Molecular hydrogen); "Consequently, we also tied the column densities of the C i levels using a one-zone model, assuming radiative pumping [27] and collisional excitation by H2 or H i, and Helium. [79,80,81]" (Neutral carbon); "Since lines from individual rotational levels within each band are not resolved, we fitted the band profiles assuming the rotational population follows a Boltzman distribution, setting the prior on the kinetic temperature to be log 𝑇 [K] = 2±0.3," (CO absorption)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC using affine invariant sampler in spectro; uniform priors on z, b, log N (unless specified); Gaussian priors on b for metals and CI (6 +/- 2 km/s) and CO (3 +/- 1 km/s); likelihood penalty for non-increasing H2 b with J and non-physical excitation temperatures.  
  > "The model parameters were constrained following a Bayesian approach to derive the posterior distribution function using the affine invariant MCMC sampler. [44] We report constraints on the model parameters using maximum a posteriori probability and 0.683 highest posterior density estimates of the 1D marginalized probability distribution function. ## Qualitative findings

- The quasar J012555.11-012925.00 and its companion galaxy form a major merging system at z ≈ 2.66 separated by ~5 kpc in projection and approaching each other at ~550 km s^-1.  
  > "Our high spatial resolution ALMA observations of the dense molecular component (d) reveal that the centroids of the two galaxies –quasar host and companion– are separated by only ∼0.7 arcsec on the plane of the sky, that is, a physical projected distance of about 5 kpc at redshift 𝑧 syst = 2.6618." (abstract); "The companion galaxy must therefore be situated in the foreground, showing that the objects are also approaching each other, indicating a merging scenario." (abstract)
- Lines from the first excited vibrational state of H2 (E >= 4000 cm^-3) are detected for the first time in absorption at high redshift, indicating extreme UV pumping ~1000 times stronger than the average Milky Way field.  
  > "Notably, we detect, for the first time in absorption at high redshift, lines from the first excited vibrational state, with energy levels at 𝐸 ≳ 4000 cm−3." (abstract); "Modelling the excitation of H2 with the Meudon PDR code, we infer the UV field to be about a thousand times stronger than the average Milky-Way field." (abstract)
- Diffuse molecular gas exposed to the quasar radiation is photo-dissociated, leaving behind only compact (< 0.02 pc), dense (10^5 - 10^6 cm^-3), low-mass (< 0.1 M_sun) cloudlets with a volume filling factor <= 10^-5 that are too small to form stars.  
  > "photo-dissociated (within timescales of several years for the constrained UV flux), and only the high density regions survived the intense radiation field." (main text); "We infer that the region exposed to the UV radiation from the accretion disk, i.e. a few kpc across at this distance from the quasar, is very clumpy with dense molecular gas surviving in no more than 10−5 of the volume." (abstract); "In our case, the surviving clumps have masses < 0.1 𝑀⊙ so that they cannot form stars, when the remaining of the companion galaxy, outside the anisotropic quasar radiation field, forms stars at a high pace." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High spatial resolution ALMA Band 3 and 6 observations resolve the dense molecular gas and continuum of the two galaxies separated by ~0.7 arcsec, while VLT/X-shooter spectroscopy at two slit PAs probes both the companion galaxy emission and the small-scale absorbing gas against the quasar.  
  > "We combine unique millimeter and optical observations of the quasar J 012555.11−012925.00 to investigate its galactic environment and directly assess the impact of its intense radiation on the physical state and small-scale structure of the molecular gas observed in absorption. [19]" (abstract); "Our high spatial resolution ALMA observations of the dense molecular component (d) reveal that the centroids of the two galaxies –quasar host and companion– are separated by only ∼0.7 arcsec on the plane of the sky," (abstract); "The use of different slit orientations during X-shooter observations allowed us to extract both the quasar (PA=0◦ and PA=90◦) and the companion galaxy (PA=90◦) optical spectra." (abstract)
- Why this method: Saturated absorption lines act as a natural coronagraph suppressing the quasar light to reveal the host galaxy SED, while ro-vibrational H2 absorption lines coupled with Meudon PDR modelling probe cloud structures ~10^5 times smaller than resolvable in emission.  
  > "This is also approximately 10^5 times smaller than currently resolvable through molecular-line emission at high redshifts." (abstract); "In addition, the absorption lines conveniently remove the otherwise outshining light from the quasar central engine, acting like a coronagraph which reveals emission from the host galaxy at various wavelengths." (abstract)
- Alternative: Uniform dust reddening of both the quasar continuum and emission lines was rejected because reddening the entire composite spectrum with A_V ≈ 0.20 significantly underpredicts the Si IV, C III], and C IV emission lines.  
  > "Following the quasar pair method, [29,63,64,65,66] the best matching is obtained by reddening the composite with an Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) like extinction curve [67] and visual extinction 𝐴V ≈ 0.20 ± 0.10. However, this significantly underpredicts the emission lines, Si IV, C III] and C IV, suggesting a differential reddening between the continuum and the emission lines." (Differential dust extinction)
- Alternative: Origin of the residual emission in saturated absorption troughs from the companion galaxy or a data reduction artifact was rejected because the residual emission is aligned with the quasar trace at both PA=0 deg and PA=90 deg and is absent in strong intervening Ly-alpha forest troughs.  
  > "The 2D spectrum (Extended Data Fig. 3) shows that this residual emission is aligned with the unresolved quasar trace and remains similar regardless of the position angle, indicating that it comes mainly from the quasar host galaxy, covered by both slit positions, not from the companion galaxy (covered by the slit at PA=90◦ but not at PA=0◦)." (The quasar host galaxy as residual emission in saturated absorption lines); "However, this emission is absent in the troughs of strong Ly𝛼 forest lines, which fully absorb light from the background objects (quasar, host and companion galaxies) due to their origin in large-scale intergalactic structures. This confirms that the residual flux in the 𝑧 = 2.66 lines is real and not a data reduction artifact." (The quasar host galaxy as residual emission in saturated absorption lines)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Band 6 continuum emission detected in the multiband image is not consistently detected in individual SPW images (rms ~ 25 uJy/beam), so continuum flux densities are adopted from SPWs 21, 23, and 25 with an upper limit for the host galaxy.  
  > "However, the corresponding emission is not consistently detected in images from individual SPWs with rms noise of ∼25 𝜇Jy beam−1. Based on measurements in SPWs 21, 23 and 25 of band 6, we adopt continuum flux densities at ≈ 226 GHz to be 330±30 𝜇Jy and < 180 uJy for companion and host galaxies, respectively." (Millimetre line and continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Nebular emission lines in the companion galaxy spectrum could be impacted by the large characteristic wavelength bin size and potential quasar light contribution, and some SED parameters are not well constrained due to limited observational data.  
  > "While we considered nebular emission to fit emission lines (Ly𝛼 and C III] lines are prominent in the companion galaxy spectrum), we acknowledge that these could be impacted by the large characteristic bin size and potential quasar contribution." (Spectrophotometric modelling of the galaxies); "While some parameters are not well constrained, due to limited observational data, others, like stellar mass and extinction, are precisely determined." (Spectrophotometric modelling of the galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): Individual rotational lines within the CO A-X absorption bands are not resolved, and the detection of CO absorption is tentative.  
  > "We tentatively detect CO absorption in A − X bands (1-0), (3-0), (4-0), (6-0) and (7-0) at 𝑧 = 2.66. Since lines from individual rotational levels within each band are not resolved, we fitted the band profiles assuming the rotational population follows a Boltzman distribution," (CO absorption)
- Limitation (authors): It is not feasible to determine the fraction of the total HI column density associated with the H2-bearing velocity components, so the HI/H2 transition theory provides only a limit in the n-UV plane.  
  > "Based on the analytical theory of the H i/H2 transition, [84,85] we can only obtain a limit in the 𝑛 − UV, [19] as it is not feasible to determine the fraction of the total H i column density associated with the H2 components." (Physical conditions in the absorbing gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Molecular gas masses rely on assumed high-J CO excitation ratios (r_71 = 0.2, r_31 = 0.8) and an assumed ULIRG/merger-like alpha_CO = 0.8 conversion factor, which carry significant systematic uncertainty, especially for the quasar host where only CO(7-6) is detected.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The PDR modelling assumes constant-density slabs illuminated on one side; the authors note that CO abundance hints at higher densities in the cloud cores, indicating internal density gradients.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Band 6 continuum flux of the companion galaxy differs between the all-SPW multiband image (420 uJy) and the SPW 21/23/25 measurement (330 +/- 30 uJy), likely due to line contamination in SPW 27.
