## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure Herschel PACS and SPIRE far-IR photometry and deblend confused sources**: Processed PACS images in HIPE 14.2 with ScanmapPhotProject using aperture photometry (10 or 5 arcsec radius); extracted SPIRE fluxes from level 2 archive products with sourceExtractorSussextractor PRF fitting; deblended confused sources using GetSources.  
  > "Aperture photometry was performed on the PACS maps inside the ScanmapPhotProject script with a fixed aperture of 10 arcseconds radius at both 100 and 160$\mu$m for most sources" (PACS data); "The photometry on the sources was performed with the sourceExtractorSussextractor task within the HIPE14 environment using the level 2 data products." (SPIRE data); "Therefore, we were able to use GetSources to extract the fluxes for PKS1934-63 from the PACS images, and the fluxes for PKS0915-11 and PKS2356-61 from the SPIRE images." (Confused sources)
   - software: HIPE, SUSSEXtractor, GetSources
   - parameter: PACS apertures: 10 arcsec radius (or 5 arcsec for faint <20 mJy / confused sources); 48 blank sky apertures for uncertainty  
  > "Aperture photometry was performed on the PACS maps inside the ScanmapPhotProject script with a fixed aperture of 10 arcseconds radius at both 100 and 160$\mu$m for most sources; however, for some sources that are faint at 160$\mu$m ($<$20 mJy), we used a smaller aperture of 5 arcseconds radius" (PACS data); "The apertures size used matched the photometry and a total of 48 apertures were used for the estimation." (PACS data)
   - assumption: Power-law continuum SED (F_nu proportional to nu^-alpha) iterated from 250 and 350 um fluxes for SPIRE colour/aperture corrections  
  > "The script queries the SPIRE Calibration Tree to obtain the appropriate values for the beams, colour corrections and aperture corrections (if used) for a given spectral index ($\alpha$) of the continuum SED, which was assumed to have a power-law shape (i.e. $F_{\nu} \propto \nu^{-\alpha}$)." (SPIRE data)
   - fitting: Point response function (PRF) fitting in SUSSEXtractor for SPIRE  
  > "The script was tailored to fit a point response function (PRF) to sources in the region of interest defined as the position of the target source within the SPIRE images." (SPIRE data)
   - validation: Tested GetSources on unblended source PKS1733-56 and confirmed agreement with PACS aperture photometry and SPIRE SUSSEXtractor  
  > "We tested the GetSources method using PKS1733-56, an object that did not need de-blending, in order to compare the results. The extracted fluxes agree well with the aperture photometry for PACS and with SUSSEXtractor used for the SPIRE images." (Confused sources)
2. **Measure APEX LABOCA 870 um sub-mm continuum fluxes**: Reduced chopped photometry scans with CRUSH and performed circular aperture photometry (49 arcsec diameter) on maps for two sources (PKS0023-26 and PKS2135-20).  
  > "We used CRUSH version 2.31-1, and followed the advice from the CRUSH LABOCA photometry guide in that we have not reported results from data with only one scan" (*APEX*/LABOCA data); "Aperture photometry was performed on the maps using a 49 arcseconds diameter circular aperture (2.5 beam FWHM), and taking the uncertainty as the standard deviation of the fluxes measured in eight blank sky apertures." (*APEX*/LABOCA data)
   - software: CRUSH
   - parameter: 49 arcsec diameter circular aperture (2.5 beam FWHM) and 8 blank sky apertures for map photometry  
  > "Aperture photometry was performed on the maps using a 49 arcseconds diameter circular aperture (2.5 beam FWHM), and taking the uncertainty as the standard deviation of the fluxes measured in eight blank sky apertures." (*APEX*/LABOCA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Scaled uncertainty by reduced-chi-squared when > 1 and visually rejected inconsistent scans  
  > "For multiple scan data we used the reduced-chi-squared value to estimate the significance of the flux detection, where for reduced-chi-squared values larger than unity we scaled the uncertainty by the reduced-chi-squared value. We also removed scans that by eye appeared inconsistent with the rest." (*APEX*/LABOCA data)
3. **Measure ALMA ~100 GHz non-thermal core continuum fluxes**: Measured peak mJy/beam flux of the point-like core from standard pipeline-processed average continuum images from the ALMA archive.  
  > "Non-thermal core continuum fluxes for the observed 2Jy sources were measured from the standard pipeline-processed average continuum images available from the *ALMA* archive." (ALMA data); "Since these point-like cores – with spatial FWHM comparable with those of the ALMA beams for the observations of individual sources – were clearly distinguishable from any extended jet/lobe emission in the maps, we took the peak mJy/beam measurement as the core flux." (ALMA data)
   - assumption: Cores are point-like within the 1.4-3.7 arcsec ALMA beam and absolute flux calibration uncertainty is ~5%  
  > "Since these point-like cores – with spatial FWHM comparable with those of the ALMA beams for the observations of individual sources – were clearly distinguishable from any extended jet/lobe emission in the maps, we took the peak mJy/beam measurement as the core flux." (ALMA data); "The uncertainties in these fluxes are dominated by the accuracy of the absolute flux calibration, which is estimated to be approximately 5% for the frequency of the observations (cite: warmels18)." (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked ATCA/VLA maps to verify kpc-scale jet/hotspot contamination in the ALMA beam is negligible  
  > "However, examination of the *ATCA/VLA* maps shows that such contamination is likely to be negligible in most cases" (Dependence of non-thermal contamination on radio/optical class)
4. **Construct radio-to-IR SEDs and classify/correct non-thermal synchrotron contamination**: Constructed radio-to-IR SEDs, classified sources as dominated (D), potentially contaminated (P), or correctable (C) for lobe/hotspot and core emission in <200 um and >200 um bands, and subtracted power-law extrapolations of radio/mm/sub-mm data for 6 C-class objects.  
  > "For each wavelength range, we mark objects in which the non-thermal emission dominates in a particular wavelength range with a “D”, those with a potential for non-thermal contamination with a “P”, and those with non-thermal contamination that can be corrected by a “C”." (Non-thermal contamination); "for four of the seven CSS/GPS objects (PKS0023-26, PKS1151-34, PKS1814-63 and PKS2135-20) it is possible to correct at least the shorter-wavelength far-IR fluxes for this contamination by using a simple power-law extrapolation of the radio-to-sub-mm SEDs" (Non-thermal contamination by extended jets, lobes or hotspots); "In these cases, a simple power-law extrapolation of the LABOCA to longer-wavelength SPIRE points allows us to estimate the contribution of the non-thermal contamination on the shorter-wavelength fluxes." (Non-thermal core contamination)
   - parameter: Criterion for potential core contamination (P): highest frequency radio-to-sub-mm core flux > 50% of the far-IR flux  
  > "To identify these objects, we adopt the criterion that any object for which the highest frequency radio to sub-mm core flux (usually the *ALMA* 100GHz core flux but in some cases the *APEX* LABOCA flux) is $>$50% the flux at a particular far-IR wavelength has a potential for non-thermal core contamination, and mark the object with a “P” in the table." (Non-thermal core contamination)
   - assumption: Non-thermal core SED remains flat up to far-IR wavelengths for P classification; power-law shape for C subtraction  
  > "In this case, we are making the assumption that the SED of the non-thermal core remains flat up to far-IR wavelengths" (Non-thermal core contamination)
   - fitting: Power-law fit to radio/mm/sub-mm/longer-SPIRE data  
  > "The power-law fits to the radio and sub-mm data used to calculate the results are plotted on the SEDs of each object." (Non-thermal contamination by extended jets, lobes or hotspots)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compute rest-frame 100 and 160 um luminosities and test correlations with AGN power indicators**: Calculated rest-frame L_100 and L_160 with modified black-body K-corrections, and computed Spearman rank and partial rank correlation coefficients against L_[OIII] and L_24 across sub-samples.  
  > "We have used the flux measurements from Tables 1 and 3 to calculate the rest-frame far-IR continuum luminosities at 100 and 160$\mu$m. As part of this, we made K corrections by assuming a modified black-body continuum shape with $\beta = 1.2$, and dust temperatures estimated using the 160/100$\mu$m flux ratios (cite: tadhunter14)." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators); "To put these correlations on a firm statistical footing, in the second column of Table [1](#tab:correlations) we present estimates of Spearman’s rank correlation statistic ($\rho$) for different combinations of luminosities, along with the probabilities that the luminosities are not correlated." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators)
   - parameter: Modified black-body emissivity index beta = 1.2; 100 Monte Carlo iterations for 160 um upper limits  
  > "As part of this, we made K corrections by assuming a modified black-body continuum shape with $\beta = 1.2$, and dust temperatures estimated using the 160/100$\mu$m flux ratios (cite: tadhunter14)." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators); "This process was repeated 100 times, with the correlation coefficients recalculated at each iteration." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators)
   - assumption: Cosmology: H_o = 71 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.27, Omega_lambda = 0.73; modified black-body with beta = 1.2  
  > "We assume a cosmology with $H_{o}=71$km s$^{-1}$Mpc$^{-1}, \Omega_{m}=0.27$ and $\Omega_{\lambda}=0.73$ throughout this paper." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Spearman rank and partial rank correlation statistics  
  > "so we also present results for the Spearman partial rank correlation coefficient that take into account the potential redshift dependence (third column of Table [1](#tab:correlations))." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators)

## Qualitative findings

- Non-thermal synchrotron contamination in the far-IR is strongest in CSS/GPS sources (from lobes/hotspots), WLRG/FRI sources (from cores), and BLRG/Q sources (from beamed cores).  
  > "Non-thermal contamination is strongest in objects with compact CSS/GPS or extended FRI radio morphologies, and in those with type 1 optical spectra." (Abstract)
- Selecting radio AGN on the basis of a steep low-frequency radio spectral index does not prevent significant non-thermal core or lobe contamination at far-IR wavelengths.  
  > "This emphasises that selection on the basis of a steep radio spectral index is not by itself sufficient to avoid non-thermal contamination in the far-IR." (Conclusions)
- Strong correlations between 100 and 160 um luminosities and AGN bolometric power indicators (L_[OIII] and L_24) persist after removing non-thermally contaminated and strongly star-forming galaxies, supporting AGN radiative heating of cool dust.  
  > "Finally, concentrating on the subset of objects that lack independent evidence for strong star formation activity, and for which the non-thermal contamination in the PACS bands is negligible or can be corrected, we find highly significant correlations between the 100 and 160$\mu$m monochromatic continuum luminosities ($L_{100}$ and $L_{160}$) and AGN bolometric power indicators ($L_{[OIII]}$ and $L_{24}$)." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Deep Herschel PACS/SPIRE observations achieve 89-100% PACS detection rates to avoid flux bias, while APEX 870 um and ALMA 100 GHz observations bridge the mm-to-sub-mm SED gap needed to constrain non-thermal synchrotron extrapolation into the far-IR.  
  > "We use these *Herschel* observations along with new *Atacama Pathfinder Experiment* (*APEX*) 870$\mu$m and *Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-Millimeter Array* (*ALMA*) 100 GHz measurements – which fill an important gap in the mm to sub-mm SEDs of the sources – to assess the incidence of non-thermal contamination at far-IR wavelengths." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used HIPE 14.2 for improved artefact removal and noise floor; used SUSSEXtractor PRF fitting for SPIRE to handle background confusion; used GetSources to deblend nearby companions; used partial rank correlation to remove mutual redshift dependence.  
  > "however, it is clear that the new processed images are superior in both the reduction of artefacts and the noise floor achieved in the imaging." (Herschel data); "The choice of using SUSSEXtractor to measure the flux was driven by the many background confusing sources at these wavelengths that can make aperture photometry difficult." (SPIRE data); "However, it is important to be cautious about spurious correlations that might arise through the mutual dependence of the luminosities on redshift, so we also present results for the Spearman partial rank correlation coefficient that take into account the potential redshift dependence" (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators)
- Alternative: Custom reprocessing of SPIRE maps in HIPE14 was rejected in favour of default archive level 2 products because custom reduction was no better.  
  > "however with HIPE14 and the updated calibration we choose to use the default pipeline processed point source and extended maps from the *Herschel* archive for the analysis, because our reduction was equivalent or no better than those products." (SPIRE data)
- Alternative: Driven by non-thermal or strong star-formation contamination as the sole cause of the far-IR vs AGN power correlations, rejected by removing D, P, and RSF sources.  
  > "To investigate the effect that this might have on the correlation statistics for the sample as a whole, we have removed the RSF objects, as well as those with any possibility of non-thermal contamination, and re-calculated the statistics." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneous observations from 2004 to 2019 mean radio/mm core variability could affect non-thermal extrapolation for individual sources.  
  > "Finally, it is important to add the caveat that non-thermal emission from the cores of the sources is potentially subject to variability. Therefore, there may be some level of variability between the high frequency radio core measurements taken in 2006 with *ATCA/VLA*, the *Spitzer*/MIPS data from 2004 – 2007, the *APEX* LABOCA data from 2011 – 2013, the *Herschel* data from 2012 – 2013, and the *ALMA* data from 2019." (Dependence of non-thermal contamination on radio/optical class)
- Limitation (authors): Incomplete SPIRE and LABOCA coverage across the sample makes non-thermal assessment uncertain for some sources.  
  > "The difficulty in determining the degree of non-thermal contribution from the core components is further exacerbated by the fact that some of the sources for which this form of non-thermal contamination might be important lack the constraints that would be provided by LABOCA or SPIRE data" (Non-thermal core contamination)
- Limitation (authors): APEX LABOCA dataset is non-uniform in completeness and depth due to wobbler mode instrument problems.  
  > "This has led to a data set that is not uniform across the sample in its completeness and depth" (*APEX*/LABOCA data)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot completely rule out that far-IR vs AGN power correlations arise indirectly from a mutual correlation between undetected moderate star formation and AGN accretion.  
  > "On the other hand, our observations do not entirely rule out some level of heating of the cool dust by regions of star formation, even in cases that currently lack strong independent evidence for recent star formation activity." (Correlations between far-IR luminosities and AGN power indicators)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Multi-epoch variability across a 15-year baseline (Spitzer 2004-2007, VLA/ATCA 2006, Herschel/LABOCA 2011-2013, ALMA 2016/2019) introduces unquantified systematic uncertainty into SED extrapolations of flat-spectrum radio cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA compact configuration resolution (1.4-3.7 arcsec) could include inner kpc-scale jet/lobe emission in addition to the nuclear core (explicitly noted for PKS 2314+03).

## Reproducibility

- Data public (paper): Herschel and ALMA data are public in their respective archives; APEX LABOCA data available on reasonable request to the authors.  
  > "The Herschel data underlying this article are available in the ESA Herschel Science Archive, at http://archives.esac.esa.int/hsa/whsa/. The ALMA data from this article are available in the ALMA Archive, at https://almascience.nrao.edu/alma-data. The APEX LABOCA data presented in this article can be shared on reasonable request to the authors." (Data Availability Statement)
- Products in archive: Standard pipeline-processed average continuum images in the ALMA archive and level 2 SPIRE maps in the Herschel archive were used directly.  
  > "Non-thermal core continuum fluxes for the observed 2Jy sources were measured from the standard pipeline-processed average continuum images available from the *ALMA* archive." (ALMA data); "however with HIPE14 and the updated calibration we choose to use the default pipeline processed point source and extended maps from the *Herschel* archive for the analysis" (SPIRE data)
- Missing: APEX LABOCA raw/reduced data (only available upon request to the authors).
- Missing: Online Appendix tables (Table 2 / tab:obs_param and Table A1 / tab:luminosities) containing observation logs, [OIII] luminosities, and derived 100/160 um luminosities.
- Missing: Which specific PACS targets used the 5 arcsec aperture instead of the default 10 arcsec aperture.
- Missing: Exact frequency ranges/points used to fit the non-thermal power-laws for the 6 C-class corrected objects.
- Data volume (inferred): ~10-50 GB (standard ALMA archive continuum images for 29 targets and Herschel PACS/SPIRE maps for 46 targets; raw ALMA CO(1-0) visibilities not needed)
- Score (inferred): 7/5, ALMA core fluxes are measured directly as peak mJy/beam from public pipeline-processed archive continuum images, and all photometric fluxes are tabulated in Table 1, though APEX LABOCA data are only available on request and some per-object fitting/aperture choices are not fully specified.
- Rediscovery candidate: yes, The ALMA part is trivial to reproduce from public archival pipeline continuum images (peak flux measurement of 29 targets), and the main photometric and correlation tables provide clear numeric benchmarks.
