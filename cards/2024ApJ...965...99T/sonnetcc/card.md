## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the 1.3 mm continuum**: tclean with multi-scale deconvolver, manual clean masks, Briggs weighting robust = -1.0 chosen after trying several weightings  
  > "We employed the `tclean` algorithm with the `multi-scale` deconvolver in the analysis. The clean mask regions were manually defined." (Observations and Data Reduction); "We experimented with several visibility weightings" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA 6.5.5, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = -1.0 gives beam 0.024'' x 0.017'', rms 20 uJy/beam  
  > "The resultant beam size and rms noise level are 0.024$''$ $\times$ 0.017$''$ with a position angle (P.A.) of 15.1$^\circ$" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Additional images with robust = 0.0 and -2.0 (Uniform) to check fidelity of faint disk and spike features  
  > "to ensure the fidelity of the faint features of the disk and excess, i.e., spike structures." (1.3 mm continuum imaging with different CLEAN weighting parameters)
2. **Measure disk size by 2D Gaussian fit**: 2D Gaussian fit to the central bright source in the robust = -1.0 image, giving observed and beam-deconvolved FWHM  
  > "yields the observed size in FWHM of 0.058$''$ $\times$ 0.025$''$" (Results); "with a beam-deconvolved size of 0.054$''$ $\times$ 0.018$''$" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Major axis roughly perpendicular to the disk rotation axis, taken to indicate a physical rather than beam-smeared elongation  
  > "suggesting that the north-south elongation is more likely to be physical, rather than a result of the beam-smearing effect" (Results)
3. **Measure disk and total continuum flux**: Disk flux from the Gaussian component compared with total flux of the detected emission over the displayed region  
  > "The disk flux is measured as $\sim$1.0 mJy, which corresponds to 75–80% of the total 1.3 mm flux, 1.4 mJy, of the detected emission" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive gas mass and H2 column density**: Optically thin dust emission: M_gas = F d^2 / (kappa B(T_d)); N_H2 from beam intensity with mu = 2.8  
  > "Assuming that the entire disk is optically thin, with $T_{\rm}$ = 20 K and $\kappa_{\rm 1.3\,mm}$ = 0.01 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Ossenkopf1994)" (Results); "$\mu_{\rm H_2}$ is molecular weight per hydrogen molecule ($\mu_{\rm H_2}$ = 2.8)" (Results)
   - parameter: d = 140 pc  
  > "$d$ is the distance to the source (140 pc)" (Results)
   - parameter: Peak and edge beam intensities ~0.28 and ~0.06 mJy/beam  
  > "at the disk edge and peak are $\sim$0.06 mJy beam$^{-1}$ and $\sim$0.28 mJy beam$^{-1}$, respectively" (Results)
   - assumption: Optically thin disk, T_d = 20 K, kappa(1.3 mm) = 0.01 cm^2 g^-1 per interstellar matter, mu_H2 = 2.8, d = 140 pc  
  > "Assuming that the entire disk is optically thin, with $T_{\rm}$ = 20 K and $\kappa_{\rm 1.3\,mm}$ = 0.01 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Ossenkopf1994)" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Same analysis repeated with lower-resolution data; uncertainty estimated as factor of ~2  
  > "the possible uncertainties for each quantity could be a factor of $\sim$2" (Results)
5. **Characterise the spike components from the residual image**: Subtract the Gaussian disk component from the 1.3 mm image and measure the excess flux, then convert with the same mass and column-density assumptions  
  > "shows the residual 1.3 mm image subtracting the Gaussian component described above" (Results); "Under the same assumptions as for the disk calculations, the measured flux corresponds to a mass of $\sim$1 $\times$10$^{-4}$ $M_{\odot}$" (Results)
   - assumption: Same as step 4  
  > "Under the same assumptions as for the disk calculations" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spike significance checked in images with different robust parameters (robust -1 and -2: three components each >~3 sigma)  
  > "In the case of the images with a Briggs weighting with robust parameters of $-$1 and $-$2, there are three components extending from the main source, each with emissions of $\gtrsim$3$\sigma$ significance." (Results)
6. **Test disk gravitational stability**: Toomre Q from a simplified formula of Kratter & Lodato, using the disk mass and stellar mass  
  > "Following a simplified formula of (cite: Kratter16), the Toomre Q value is estimated to be $>>$1, suggesting a substantially stable state of the disk." (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - parameter: Disk mass ~4e-4 Msun, stellar mass ~0.2 Msun  
  > "the mass of the disk is extremely light at $\sim$4 $\times$10$^{-4}$ $M_{\odot}$" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Estimate magnetic field needed for interchange instability at the disk edge**: Lubow & Spruit onset condition B_init^2 > 2 pi Sigma0 G M*/r^2 and development condition B_dev^2/8pi > rho v^2 with free-fall v  
  > "the required magnetic field strength $B_{\rm init}$ is on the order of $\sim$1 G" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?); "In this case, the required magnetic field strength $B_{\rm dev}$ is approximately 1 G." (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - parameter: Sigma0 ~5e24 cm^-2 at disk edge, r ~4 au, M* = 0.2 Msun  
  > "$\Sigma_{0}$ is approximately $\sim$5 $\times$10$^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$ at the edge of the disk, $r$ $\sim$4 au" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - parameter: Envelope density 1e-13 g cm^-3; free-fall velocity ~9 km/s at 4 au  
  > "A freefall velocity at $r$ = 4 au is $\sim$9 km s$^{-1}$, which is determined by the central stellar mass of 0.2 $M_{\odot}$." (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - assumption: Observed disk-edge densities not used for the infalling material because the disk is not smoothly connected to the envelope; envelope density adopted from numerical work  
  > "we adopt a value of 10$^{-13}$ g cm$^{-3}$ based on numerical work (cite: Masunaga2000)" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with ~<=1 G typical of non-ideal MHD simulations  
  > "The abovementioned field strength is slightly stronger than $\lesssim$1 G, which is typically seen in nonideal magnetohydrodynamics simulations" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
8. **Evaluate core-scale magnetic field and mass-to-flux ratio**: DCF field strength from literature polarization dispersion and N2H+ line width, then mu_c from the Crutcher formula  
  > "The two values provide a magnetic field strength of $\sim$510 $\mu$G using the same other assumptions (cite: Soam19; Fukaya23)." (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?); "is calculated to be $\sim$0.5 with a column density of 3 $\times$10$^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$ at a radius of 3000 au (cite: Tokuda_2016)" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note it is not a strong constraint  
  > "Note that it is not necessarily a strong constraint" (What is the origin of the spike-like structures around the disk?)
9. **Estimate timescales and accretion rate for the interchange-instability scenario**: Dynamical time = 2000 au / sound speed (0.2 km/s at 10 K); time-averaged accretion rate from 0.2 Msun over that time  
  > "the dynamical time for these structures would be $\sim$5 $\times$10$^5$ years (=2000 au/0.2 km s$^{-1}$)" (Summary of the MC 27/L1521F studies and a new star formation scenario); "the time-averaged accretion rate would be $\sim$4 $\times$10$^{-6}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$." (Summary of the MC 27/L1521F studies and a new star formation scenario)
   - assumption: Arcs and spikes expand at the sound speed c_s = 0.2 km/s at 10 K  
  > "Assuming their expansion speed as $c_{s}$, 0.2 km s$^{-1}$ at 10 K" (Summary of the MC 27/L1521F studies and a new star formation scenario)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with theoretical predictions just after protostar formation  
  > "This is consistent with theoretical predictions just after the protostar formation" (Summary of the MC 27/L1521F studies and a new star formation scenario)

## Qualitative findings

- The disk is resolved as a north-south elongated ~10 au source with spike-like extensions to the northeast; such protruding structures were identified for the first time in disks of young protostellar systems  
  > "such protruding structures have been identified for the first time in the disks of young protostellar systems" (Results)
- The spikes carry several tens of percent of the disk mass  
  > "it is quite evident that there are entities with masses amounting to several tens of percent of the protostellar disk" (Results)
- Spike direction agrees with the large-scale arc direction  
  > "The directions of the large-scale arc and the disk’s spike are consistent with each other" (Summary of the MC 27/L1521F studies and a new star formation scenario)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier ~0.1'' ALMA data revealed a ~10 au disk, so the highest-resolution configuration was used to resolve it  
  > "early ALMA observations with an angular resolution of $\sim$0.1$''$ revealed a compact protostellar disk with a size of $\sim$10 au (cite: Tokuda_2017)" (Introduction); "This paper presents an ALMA highest-resolution ($\sim$0.03$''$) 1.3 mm continuum study of the protostellar disk in MC 27." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Briggs robust = -1.0 chosen as the best balance of resolution and sensitivity; line data not used because they lack S/N at comparable resolution  
  > "a Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of $-$1.0 provided the best balance between resolution and sensitivity" (Observations and Data Reduction); "could not produce high-signal-to-noise-ratio maps at a comparable resolution with the continuum data" (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Uniform weighting gave a resolution comparable to robust = -1.0 with worse sensitivity, so robust = -1.0 was kept  
  > "Typically, Uniform weighting is expected to enhance angular resolution, but in this instance, it resulted in a resolution comparable to that obtained with a robust parameter of $-$1.0" (1.3 mm continuum imaging with different CLEAN weighting parameters)
