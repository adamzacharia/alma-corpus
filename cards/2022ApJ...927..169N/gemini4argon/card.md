## Analysis recipe

1. **Analyze SiO(5-4) line width and test for inner CSE rotation (5 to 15 au)**: Extract Doppler velocity spectra averaged in 30 mas broad annular rings centered on Mira A (from 25 to 215 mas in 10 mas steps) with a 3-sigma cut; measure full-width at 1/5 maximum; test velocity gradient impact by re-centering spectra in 30x30 mas^2 pixels; fit sine waves to mean Doppler velocity <V_z> vs position angle omega in ring 30<R<100 mas.  
  > "Figure 1 displays, in each of four quadrants, Doppler velocity spectra averaged in 30 mas broad annular rings centered on Mira A with mean radii increasing from 25 mas (blue) to 215 mas (red) in steps of 10 mas. A cut at 3$\sigma$ is applied to the data." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range); "In order to assess the importance of such a contribution, we repeat the analysis by re-centering, in each 30$\times$30 mas$^2$ pixel, the observed Doppler velocity spectrum on its mean value." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range); "Figure 2 right shows the dependence on $\omega$ of $<$$V_z$$>$ evaluated in intervals of $\pm$17 km s$^{-1}$ and $\pm$10 km s$^{-1}$, respectively. The best sine wave fits are $<V_z>=-0.53+0.54\sin(\omega-40$$^\circ$) and $-0.47+0.89\sin(\omega-41$$^\circ$) km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range)
   - parameter: 3-sigma cut; 30 mas annular rings from 25 to 215 mas in 10 mas steps; 30 < R < 100 mas ring; V_z intervals of +-10 and +-17 km s^-1  
  > "averaged in 30 mas broad annular rings centered on Mira A with mean radii increasing from 25 mas (blue) to 215 mas (red) in steps of 10 mas. A cut at 3$\sigma$ is applied to the data." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range); "We illustrate it using a ring covering 30 mas$<$$R$$<$100 mas" (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range); "Figure 2 right shows the dependence on $\omega$ of $<$$V_z$$>$ evaluated in intervals of $\pm$17 km s$^{-1}$ and $\pm$10 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range)
   - assumption: Distance of 100 pc (10 mas = 1 au); systemic velocity V_LSR = 47.7 km s^-1  
  > "In the present article we adopt a distance of 100 pc, 10 mas spanning 1 au (cite: Haniff1995; vanLeeuwen2007)." (Introduction); "As origin of velocity coordinate, we use a systemic velocity of 47.7 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the local standard of rest (LSR)" (OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - fitting: Sine wave fit of <V_z> vs position angle omega: <V_z> = V_0 + V_rot sin(omega - omega_0)  
  > "The best sine wave fits are $<V_z>=-0.53+0.54\sin(\omega-40$$^\circ$) and $-0.47+0.89\sin(\omega-41$$^\circ$) km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range)
   - validation: Evaluated systematic uncertainties by varying intervals of R and V_z, and compared with VLA SiO maser rotation measurements from literature.  
  > "Uncertainties accounting for the systematic effects of choosing different intervals of $R$ and/or $V_z$ are estimated as $\sim$$\pm$0.2 km s$^{-1}$ on the projected velocity and $\sim$$\pm$15$^\circ$ on the position angle of the projected rotation axis." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range); "It is in agreement with the values quoted by (cite: Cotton2004; Cotton2006): 1.5$\pm$0.9 km s$^{-1}$ and 17$\pm$36$^\circ$, respectively." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range)
2. **Map and compare 13CO(3-2), 12CO(3-2), and SiO(5-4) morpho-kinematics in the central region (region C)**: Select region C where |V_z| < 4 km s^-1 and 12CO(3-2) intensity exceeds 10 Jy arcsec^-2 km s^-1; extrapolate radial intensity in 1<R<2 arcsec to R=0 to measure central concentration; rotate coordinates (x,y) clockwise by 35 deg to (x',y'); construct integrated PV maps along x' and y' and measure integrated fluxes and ratios for 5 kinematic fragments (NEA, SWO, NEO, SWS, SEO).  
  > "we limit the present study (in addition to the restriction to the Doppler velocity interval of $\pm$4 km s$^{-1}$) to the central region where the intensity of the $^{12}$CO(3-2) emission exceeds 10 Jy arcsec$^{-2}$ km s$^{-1}$." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions); "we use coordinates ($x',y'$) rotated clockwise by 35$^\circ$ from ($x,y$) to explore the morpho-kinematics." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions); "For each of the fragments making up region C, we evaluate the $^{12}$CO(3-2) and $^{13}$CO(3-2) emissions and calculate their ratio." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: |V_z| < 4 km s^-1; 12CO intensity > 10 Jy arcsec^-2 km s^-1; coordinate rotation angle 35 deg; fragment boundaries in x', y', V_z from Table 3  
  > "we limit the present study (in addition to the restriction to the Doppler velocity interval of $\pm$4 km s$^{-1}$) to the central region where the intensity of the $^{12}$CO(3-2) emission exceeds 10 Jy arcsec$^{-2}$ km s$^{-1}$." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions); "The result is listed in Table 3, which defines which data cube elements are included in the definition of each fragment; in addition to be part of region C, their coordinates $x'$, $y'$ and $V_z$ must satisfy inequality relations listed in the table." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions)
   - assumption: Unresolved central continuum contribution of ~2.7 Jy km s^-1 excluded from central radial extrapolation  
  > "Extrapolation to $R$=0 of the intensity observed in the ring 1$<$$R$$<$2 arcsec gives excesses of 28.9 and 6.4 Jy km s$^{-1}$ for $^{12}$CO(3-2) and $^{13}$CO(3-2) respectively, excluding a contribution of $\sim$2.7 Jy km s$^{-1}$ of the unresolved continuum emission." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared mean 12CO/13CO emission ratio across fragments (7.7+-1.2) with the value quoted in HTN20 (7.3).  
  > "The $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO emission ratios vary between 6.6 and 10.0 with mean$\pm$rms values of 7.7$\pm$1.2, in good agreement with the mean value of 7.3 quoted by HTN20" (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions)
3. **Derive mass loss rates for the SEO and NEO fragments from 13CO(3-2) flux**: Calculate mass per unit radius M_R from optically thin 13CO(3-2) flux per unit radius F_R using level population and molecular constants, then multiply by projected radial wind velocity V_R: M_dot = M_R * V_R.  
  > "To do so we measure its mean mass $M_R$ per unit of $R$ and its radial velocity $V_R$ projected on the plane of the sky, the mass loss rate being the product of these two quantities: $\dot{M}=M_R \times V_R$." (Mass loss rate); "The relation between the total emission ($F$) and the number of molecules ($N$) is $F=hc/(4\pi d^2)NA_{ji}f_{pop}$ where $h$ is Planck constant, $c$ the velocity of light, $d$ the distance to the Earth, $A_{ji}$ the Einstein coefficients and $f_{pop}=(2J+1)\exp(-E_u/T)/(T/2.7)$ the population of $^{13}$CO at level $J$." (Mass loss rate)
   - parameter: A_ji = 2.18e-6 s^-1, E_u = 31.7 K for 13CO(3-2); V_R(SEO) = 3.0+-0.5 km s^-1; V_R(NEO) = 2.9+-0.8 km s^-1; radial extension 1.0+-0.2 arcsec (SEO) and 1.5+-0.3 arcsec (NEO)  
  > "Einstein coefficient, $A_{ji}$ (s$^{-1}$) | 2.50$\times$10$^{-6}$ | 2.18$\times$10$^{-6}$" (Table 2); "On average over the SEO, the mean projected radial velocity is therefore $\sim$3.0$\pm$0.5 km s$^{-1}$ or $\sim$(6.3$\pm$1.0)$\times$10$^{-3}$ arcsec yr$^{-1}$." (Mass loss rate); "We estimate the values of its projected radial velocity and of its radial extension (HTN20) to be 2.9$\pm$0.8 km s$^{-1}$ and 1.5$\pm$0.3 arcsec, respectively" (Mass loss rate)
   - assumption: Optically thin 13CO(3-2) emission; temperature profile T[K] = 109 / r[arcsec] (~50 to 200 K); 12C/13C isotopic ratio = 12; 12CO/H2 abundance = 4e-4; distance = 100 pc  
  > "We use the $^{13}$CO data to take advantage of the low line opacity: in the optically thin approximation, the total flux provides a direct measure of the number of molecules." (Mass loss rate); "To obtain the relation between $F_R$ and $M_R$ we assume a radial dependence of the temperature of the form $T$\[K\]$=$109/$r$\[arcsec\] (cite: Ryde2001)" (Mass loss rate); "Assuming a $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C isotopic ratio of 12 and an abundance relative to H$_2$ of 4$\times$10$^{-4}$ (cite: vanDishoeck1992; Khouri2018), this implies a total hydrogen mass of 12$\times$10$^{50}$ proton masses, or 1.0$\times$10$^{-6}$ solar masses for an emission of 1 Jy km s$^{-1}$." (Mass loss rate)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested two different selection criteria for the SEO fragment (giving 5.3 and 6.9 Jy km s^-1) and compared total derived mass loss rate with single dish estimates.  
  > "In order to illustrate the evaluation of $F_R$, we present here two sets of selection criteria, which give total fluxes of 5.3 and 6.9 Jy km s$^{-1}$, respectively, each covering a radial range of $\sim$1.0$\pm$0.2 arcsec." (Mass loss rate); "Adding together the contributions of the SEO and NEO, we obtain a mass loss rate of (6.6$\pm$2.0)$\times$10$^{-8}$ M$_\odot$yr$^{-1}$, accounting for $\sim$30% of the mass loss rate obtained from single dish observations (cite: Ryde2001; Heras2005)." (Mass loss rate)
4. **Determine SiO/CO abundance ratio outside the south-western quadrant and compare with RS Cnc and EP Aqr**: Measure SiO(5-4) to 13CO(3-2) intensity ratios in fragments outside the south-western quadrant; convert emissivity ratio to column density and abundance ratio in the optically thin limit; compare radial profiles of SiO(5-4) and CO(3-2) (scaling CO(2-1) to CO(3-2) for RS Cnc and EP Aqr) beyond 100 au.  
  > "In the optically thin approximation, the ratio between the SiO and $^{13}$CO column densities associated with a same emissivity is nearly temperature independent because of the similarity between the upper energy levels and is dominated by the ratio of the Einstein coefficients multiplied by the ($2J+1$) and partition function factors; its value is $\sim$7.5 10$^{-3}$." (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant); "Using a typical SiO(5-4)/$^{13}$CO(3-2) intensity ratio of $\sim$0.1 outside the south-western quadrant and a $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO abundance ratio of 12 gives an SiO/$^{12}$CO abundance ratio of 6.3 10$^{-5}$." (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant); "For the comparison with Mira to be meaningful, we have therefore multiplied the CO(2-1) brightness by ($A_{32}/A_{21}$)(7/5)\[$\exp(-E_{u32})/T$\]/\[$\exp(-E_{u21})/T$\]" (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant)
   - parameter: Excluded south-western quadrant: 200 deg < omega < 315 deg; A_21 = 6.91e-7 s^-1, A_32 = 2.50e-6 s^-1, E_u21 = 16.6 K, E_u32 = 33.2 K, A_SiO(5-4) = 5.2e-4 s^-1, E_u,SiO = 31.3 K  
  > "Figure 12 compares Mira, excluding the south-western quadrant (200$^\circ$$<\omega<$315$^\circ$), with RS Cnc and EP Aqr" (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant); "where $A_{21}=6.91\times10^{-7}$ s$^{-1}$ and $A_{32}=2.50\times10^{-6}$ s$^{-1}$ are the Einstein coefficients, $E_{u21}=16.6$ K and $E_{u32}=33.2$ K are the energies of the upper level" (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant); "Einstein coef. $A_{ji}$ (s$^{-1}$)$^a$ | 2.50$\times$10$^{-6}$ | 5.2$\times$10$^{-4}$" (Table 4)
   - parameter: SiO/13CO column density to emissivity ratio factor ~7.5e-3; SiO(5-4)/13CO(3-2) intensity ratio ~0.1  
  > "its value is $\sim$7.5 10$^{-3}$. (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant)

  > "Using a typical SiO(5-4)/$^{13}$CO(3-2) intensity ratio of $\sim$0.1 outside the south-western quadrant and a $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO abundance ratio of 12 gives an SiO/$^{12}$CO abundance ratio of 6.3 10$^{-5}$." ## Qualitative findings

- Dense gas is confined within ~50 au of Mira A, with broad SiO(5-4) line widths (>20 km s^-1) within 15 au driven by pulsation and convective cell shocks, and possible slow rotation within 15 au.  
  > "we give evidence for confinement and probably rotation of a dense gas volume within $\sim$50 au from the star and for a large SiO line-width within $\sim$15 au." (Abstract)
- Mass loss in Mira A is episodic and fragmented: SWO and NEA are detached fragments emitted decades ago, while current mass loss proceeds toward the blue hemisphere via SEO, NEO, and SWS.  
  > "the presence of two fragments, SWO and NEA, emitted a few decades ago and nearly detached from the central gas reservoir, giving additional evidence for the episodic nature of the mass loss" (Summary and conclusion); "the current mass loss proceeding toward the blue-shifted hemisphere along three favored directions: the SEO, dominated by the wind focused by Mira B, the NEO and the SWS" (Summary and conclusion)
- Outside the south-western quadrant, the SiO/CO abundance ratio is ~0.5-1x10^-4, at least two orders of magnitude lower than in similar O-rich AGB stars (RS Cnc, EP Aqr), likely due to dust grain depletion and UV photo-dissociation.  
  > "an evaluation of the SiO/CO abundance ratio at the level of 0.5-1$\times$10$^{-4}$ when excluding the south-western quadrant, at least two orders of magnitude smaller than other typical low mass-loss-rate oxygen-rich AGB stars, and a possible interpretation in terms of the combined effect of depletion on dust grains and photo-dissociation by interstellar UV radiation." (Summary and conclusion)
- The strong SiO emission in the south-western quadrant is anti-correlated with CO and is likely caused by the 2003 mass ejection / X-ray burst shock outgassing SiO from dust or recombining UV-dissociated SiO, rather than nascent wind generation.  
  > "We argue that the SiO emission observed in the south-western quadrant is not related to the mechanism of generation of the nascent wind but to a mass ejection that occurred eleven years before the observations." (Abstract); "the whole SiO emission is interpreted as due to SiO molecules, which recombined or outgassed, eleven years before the observation, from dust grains present in the pre-existing SWO." (Different emissions of the CO and SiO lines in the south-western quadrant)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 13CO(3-2) has much smaller optical depth than 12CO(3-2), allowing finer morphological definition and direct molecule counting for mass loss rates, while high-resolution SiO(5-4) probes the inner 5-15 au shock/rotation zone and outer SiO depletion.  
  > "In the present section, we take advantage of the smaller optical depth of the $^{13}$CO(3-2) observations to study the gas morphology in finer details than was possible using $^{12}$CO(3-2) observations." (13CO(3-2) emission); "We use the $^{13}$CO data to take advantage of the low line opacity: in the optically thin approximation, the total flux provides a direct measure of the number of molecules." (Mass loss rate)
- Why this method: Limiting Doppler velocity to |V_z| < 4 km s^-1 isolates recent mass loss from ancient (>1000 yr) shells; rotating coordinates by 35 deg aligns with the symmetry axis of region C; integrating PV maps over the third dimension captures the full flux of 3D fragments.  
  > "However, by limiting the range of Doppler velocities to $|V_z|$$<$4 km s$^{-1}$, one can reasonably well select most of the more recent emission." (Morpho-kinematics of the CSE beyond 20 au from Mira A); "To ease the comparison, we take advantage of the approximate symmetry displayed by region C about an axis $x'$ making an angle of $\sim$35$^\circ$ north of east: we use coordinates ($x',y'$) rotated clockwise by 35$^\circ$ from ($x,y$) to explore the morpho-kinematics." (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions); "Such PV maps, rather than being confined to a narrow slit as usually done in PV diagrams, are integrated over the third coordinate of the data cube not used in the map" (Comparing 13CO(3-2) and 12CO(3-2) emissions)
- Alternative: Interpreting the sine-wave dependence of <V_z> vs position angle within 15 au as radial outflows rather than rotation  
  > "In principle, the fits could also be interpreted in terms of radial outflows, but such a morphology would be very unusual for the inner layer of such an AGB star. Moreover, no trace of such outflows is seen at larger projected distances from the star in the emission of the CO lines (see Section 4)." (SiO(5-4) line width in the 5 to 15 au range)
- Alternative: Complete absence of SiO outside the south-western quadrant (rejected in favor of SiO being present in each CO fragment at a much lower level)  
  > "is SiO completely absent outside the south-western quadrant or is it present in each CO fragment, but at a much lower level? We explored this issue in considerable details and concluded in favor of the latter." (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant)
- Alternative: Pulsation-induced shock density contrast or a 2003 sudden mass-loss increase as the sole cause of the central gas confinement (rejected because neither explains the very low SiO/CO ratio)  
  > "They favor instead a scenario in which the mass-loss rate of Mira has suddenly increased in 2003, in association with the emission of the soft X-ray burst reported by (cite: Karovska2005). However, none of these two scenarios can explain the second specificity of Mira A: the very low SiO/CO ratio." (Confinement at short distances of the SiO emissions outside the south-western quadrant)

  > "However, as these data are prone to important opacity within some 100 au from the star, a reliable quantitative description would require a radiative transfer modeling accounting for the different morphologies and temperature gradients of the three stars." (Different emissions of the CO and SiO lines in the south-western quadrant)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison between SiO(5-4) and 12CO/13CO(3-2) relies on observations with very different angular resolutions (60x30 mas vs ~400x370 mas) and maximum recoverable scales (11.3 arcsec vs ~4 arcsec) without uv-tapering or smoothing to a common beam.
