## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image the visibilities**: ALMA pipeline calibration in CASA 6.4.1.12, uvcontsub, tclean cubes (Briggs 0.5), one round of phase self-calibration (SMM1 only), impbcor  
  > "The raw data were calibrated with the pipeline script provided by the observatory for the initial data flagging, the calibration of bandpass, complex gain, flux scaling, and imaging (pipeline version 2022.2.0.64) for all sources." (2.3 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA 6.4.1.12, ALMA pipeline 2022.2.0.64, uvcontsub, tclean, impbcor
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5 for each SPW  
  > "The line image cubes were obtained adopting Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5 for each SPW." (2.3 Data reduction)
   - parameter: phase self-calibration, one round, 60 s solution interval (Sh 2–283–1a SMM1)  
  > "One round of phase self-calibration was applied with the solution intervals of 60 seconds." (2.3 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **separate core, outflow and jet velocity components in CO(3-2)**: Core/outflow boundary from FWTM (1.8 x FWHM) of a Gaussian fit to HCO+ J=4-3 at the continuum peak; outflow/jet terminal velocities from emission morphology, line shape, channel-map bullets and a 3 sigma rms cut  
  > "The velocity criteria for separating the core and outflow components are based on the Full Width at Tenth Maximum (FWTM = 1.8 $\times$ FWHM) of the HCO$^+$($J$ = 4–3) line profile." (3.1 CO outflows and jets); "The terminal velocities of the jets are determined whether the line intensity is below 3$\sigma$ of the rms noise level." (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: HCO+ Gaussian fit spectrum from a 0.89'' x 0.70'' elliptical region at the 0.87-mm continuum peak  
  > "The FWHM of the HCO$^+$($J$ = 4–3) line was derived by performing a Gaussian fitting to the HCO$^+$ spectrum extracted from a 0.89$''$ × 0.70$''$ elliptical region centered at the peak of the 0.87-mm continuum emission." (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
   - parameter: CO spectra extracted from a 4.0'' x 10.0'' elliptical region around the continuum peak  
  > "spectra extracted from the 4.0$\ensuremath{''}$ $\times$ 10.0$\ensuremath{''}$" (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
   - assumption: Bullet structures appear near Vobs ~50 km/s, marking the outflow terminal velocity  
  > "bullet structures are appearing at the line of sight relative velocity of $V_{\rm{obs}}$ $\sim$50 km s$^{-1}$ for both red- and blue-shifted components in the channel maps" (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to HCO+ spectrum for FWHM  
  > "performing a Gaussian fitting to the HCO$^+$ spectrum" (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **map outflow and jet integrated intensity and measure the SiO jet**: Integrate CO channels over each velocity component for maps; for SiO, extract a spectrum from a 0.70'' diameter aperture at the SiO jet peak and fit a Gaussian to define the velocity range  
  > "Integrated intensity map is obtained by integrating these velocity components for CO($J$ = 3–2)" (3.1 CO outflows and jets); "We extracted the spectra from a 0.70$''$-diameter region centered on the emission peak of the SiO jet and fitted a Gaussian profile to the extracted spectra." (3.2 SiO jet)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fitted to SiO jet spectrum  
  > "fitted a Gaussian profile to the extracted spectra" (3.2 SiO jet)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **derive outflow and jet dynamical properties**: Dynamical timescale t_d = (L_obs/V_obs)(cos i / sin i); mass M_out = 2 mu_p m_p X_CO integral(T_CO dV); momentum, mass-loss rate, force, energy and kinetic luminosity derived for each lobe  
  > "the outflow mass ($M_{\rm{out}}$) is derived by using the following equation" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties); "$t_{\rm{d}}$ can be derived using the projected outflow length and velocity as follows" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - parameter: mean atomic mass per H atom 1.41  
  > "where $\mu_p$ is mean atomic mass per H atom (1.41)" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - parameter: X_CO = 5e20 cm^-2 (K km/s)^-1, metallicity corrected  
  > "Here, we use a metallicity-corrected $X_\mathrm{CO}$ of 5 $\times$ 10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$) from (cite: Bol13)." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - assumption: Inclination i = 45 deg to the line of sight  
  > "$i$ is the inclination angle with respect to the line of sight and we here assume 45$^{\circ}$." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - assumption: CO optically thin and in LTE for both outflow and jet  
  > "With assuming that CO emission in both the outflow and jet components are optically thin and under the condition of the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - assumption: Median velocity of each component taken as the representative flow velocity  
  > "with the assumption of the median flow velocity of each velocity component as the representative flow velocity of the CO($J$ = 3–2) outflows" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with massive protostellar outflows in the solar neighbourhood; flow-mass-related values flagged as lower limits (missing flux, edge sensitivity, LTE)  
  > "These dynamical properties are similar to those of massive protostellar sources detected in the solar neighborhood (cite: Beuther2002; Wu2004; Maud2015)." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
5. **estimate the protostar luminosity**: Scale the luminosity of the LMC hot core ST16 by Ks magnitude and distance; cross-check with an outflow mass-luminosity correlation  
  > "we estimate its luminosity by scaling the luminosity of the LMC hot core (ST16) with its" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties); "Using this correlation, the luminosity of Sh 2–283–1a SMM1 is estimated to be about 10$^3$ to 10$^5$ L$_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - assumption: Single-band scaling from ST16 (full SED of SMM1 unavailable), large uncertainty acknowledged  
  > "Note that this is an estimation from only one infrared data and the obtained value would have large uncertainty." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with AKARI FIS upper limit and with the outflow mass-luminosity correlation  
  > "The estimated luminosity by two different methods is consistent with each other" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
6. **measure episodic ejection intervals from the PV diagram**: Position-velocity diagram along the flow axis; time interval = inverse of the slope of each spine-like structure (S1-S3)  
  > "Since the inverse of the slope of the spine-like structures corresponds to their time scales, we can estimate the time intervals of the episodic mass-ejection events (jet episodicity) with the slope of each spine-like structure in Figure" (4.2 The episodic mass-ejection events)
   - parameter: PV cut along the flow axis at position angle 1.1 deg  
  > "shows the position–velocity diagram (PV diagram) of Sh 2–283–1a SMM1 along the flow axis (p.a = 1.1$^\circ$)" (4.2 The episodic mass-ejection events)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Similar PV structures in Takahashi et al. 2024 and Nony et al. 2020  
  > "Their PV diagram cut along the outflow axis (see their Figure 5) shows similar structures that are presented in Figure" (4.2 The episodic mass-ejection events)

## Qualitative findings

- CO(3-2) shows distinct outflow (about 5–50 km/s) and jet (about 50–100 km/s) components, with the jets collimated and showing multiple bullets  
  > "The outflows and jets are well-collimated, with the jets exhibiting multiple bullet structures." (abstract)
- PV diagram shows a Hubble-like linear velocity increase with offset and spine-like structures linked to jet bullets  
  > "S2 is connected to R3 and B1, as well as S3 is connected to R4 and B2, while S1 is not connected to any jet bullets." (4.2 The episodic mass-ejection events)
- SiO(8-7) jet coincides spatially and in velocity with the blue CO jet  
  > "The coincidence in both the spatial distribution and relative velocity of the SiO jet and the CO jet components would indicate a physical association between these two components." (3.2 SiO jet)
- HCO+(4-3) outflows trace the edges and denser parts of the CO outflows  
  > "The distribution of these outflow components shows fragmentary structures and they seem to trace the edge of the CO outflows" (3.3 HCO+ Outflow)
- Hot-core chemistry detected at the continuum peak of Sh 2–283–1a SMM1, the second such detection in the outer Galaxy  
  > "This is the second detection of a hot core in the outer Galaxy after WB89–789 SMM1 reported in (cite: Shimonishi2023)." (3.4 Various molecular lines in Sh 2–283–1a SMM1)
- NOMF05-63–1 shows no clear bipolar structure (monopolar-like), while NOMF05-16–1, 23–1 and 23–3 are bipolar; no jets or SiO in the other four sources  
  > "We do not detect any jet component and SiO emission in these 4 sources." (4.4 The other outflow sources)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA can spatially resolve about 0.1 pc protostellar cores in distant low-metallicity environments  
  > "Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) enables us to spatially resolve the protostellar cores ($\sim$0.1 pc scale) located in distant low-metallicity environments such as the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC), the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC), and the outer Galaxy." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: HCO+ core spectrum from a small region chosen to limit outflow-wing contamination when setting the core/outflow velocity boundary  
  > "This small region was chosen to minimize contamination from the outflow wing components." (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
- Alternative: Luminosity differences between SMM1 and the low-mass comparison sources may contribute to the SiO/CO difference, in addition to dust composition  
  > "The luminosity difference between Sh 2–283–1a SMM1 and its counterparts may also contribute to the observed variation in the $N\mathrm{(SiO)}$/$N\mathrm{(CO)}$ ratio." (4.3.2 Comparison of SiO abundance with nearby sources)
- Alternative: Non-LTE excitation as an alternative explanation of the low LTE SiO/CO ratio  
  > "Thus, the derived $N\mathrm{(SiO)}$/$N\mathrm{(CO)}$ ratio strongly depends on the assumed $n(\mathrm{H_2})$." (4.3.2 Comparison of SiO abundance with nearby sources)
- Alternative: Kinematic distance (7.2 kpc) not used; Gaia-derived distance adopted for dynamical properties  
  > "In this paper, we use the distance value derived from the Gaia data for determining dynamical properties." (2.1 Targets)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Flow-mass-related properties are lower limits because of missing flux and lower sensitivity at the field edge  
  > "Hence, the dynamical properties of the outflows and jets related to their flow mass (e.g., mass, momentum, energy) discussed below can be considered as lower limits." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
- Limitation (authors): Red jet extends to the field edge where sensitivity is about 3 times lower, so some flow may be missed  
  > "Consequently, some flow components may be missed in the outer regions of the observations." (3.1 CO outflows and jets)
- Limitation (authors): Inclination unknown (45 deg assumed)  
  > "Although the inclination angle of Sh 2–283–1a SMM1 is unknown" (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
- Limitation (authors): LTE CO(3-2) may underestimate masses by 1-2 orders of magnitude; lower-J CO needed  
  > "Thus, the derived dynamical properties related to the flow mass would be underestimated by the order of 1–2." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
- Limitation (authors): Luminosity from one infrared band with large uncertainty  
  > "Note that this is an estimation from only one infrared data and the obtained value would have large uncertainty." (4.1 Comparison of the morphological and dynamical properties)
- Limitation (authors): Other four sources: jets not detected, attributed to insufficient sensitivity  
  > "This would be due to the insufficient sensitivity to detect the relatively low intensity emission of their jet components compared with the outflow components" (4.4 The other outflow sources)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination of 45 deg is assumed with no constraint; t_d, momentum, force and luminosity scale strongly with it.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LTE and optically thin CO(3-2) is adopted for mass although CO jets and outflows are often optically thick or sub-thermal; mass lower limits are quantified only qualitatively.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The low SiO/CO result rests on assumed T_rot values from single-dish studies and is not robust to the non-LTE n(H2) assumption, so the chemistry/dust interpretation is weakly supported.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one SiO/CO bullet and one aperture are used; no uncertainties besides the single 1.7 +/- 0.6 e-4 figure.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Text and Table 4 give different dynamical timescales (e.g. 1.2e4 vs 1.1e4 yr red outflow) and the luminosity is quoted as 6.7e3 and 6.6e3 Lsun; the NOMF05-63 observation date differs between text and Table 1.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Episodic interval estimates rely on visually identified spine-like structures at about 5000 au resolution, with no objective fitting procedure or uncertainties.
