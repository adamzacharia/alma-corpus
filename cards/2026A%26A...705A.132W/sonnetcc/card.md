- Question: Is CH3(17)OH present in the ISM (Orion KL), and what are the CH3(18)OH/CH3(17)OH, 16O/17O and 16O/18O isotope ratios in methanol

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the archival cubes and continuum**: Calibrated visibilities (observatory script / SV data), continuum subtracted with a 0th-order polynomial fit to line-free channels, CLEAN with Briggs robust 0.5, primary-beam correction with impbcor  
  > "The visibility was imaged by using the CLEAN algorithm using Briggs weighting with a robustness parameter of 0.5" (Data and Reductions); "All the cube data and continuum images were corrected for the primary beam attenuation by applying task" (Data and Reductions)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5; 488 kHz frequency resolution; cube rms 7 mJy/beam (2013.1.00553.S) and 12 mJy/beam (SV)  
  > "The sensitivity and the synthesized beam of the cube data are 7 mJy beam$^{-1}$" (Data and Reductions); "The sensitivity and the synthesized beam of cube data are 12 mJy beam$^{-1}$" (Data and Reductions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum image reproduces structures reported by Pagani2017 from the same observation data  
  > "The image reproduces the structures reported by (cite: Pagani2017) which used the same observation data." (Data and Reductions)
2. **Locate CH3(18)OH emission peaks**: Integrated intensity map of CH3(18)OH 5_0-4_0 E over 4.5-11.5 km/s; two peaks MeOH1 and MeOH2 coincident with methyl formate peaks MF1 and MF2  
  > "We found two prominent CH$_3$$^{18}$OH peaks, MeOH1 and MeOH2" (Results); "which is made by integrating the velocity range from 4.5 km s$^{-1}$ to 11.5 km s$^{-1}$" (Results)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Velocity integration range 4.5-11.5 km/s  
  > "which is made by integrating the velocity range from 4.5 km s$^{-1}$ to 11.5 km s$^{-1}$" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Peaks coincide with methyl formate peaks MF1 and MF2 of Favre2011 within the beam  
  > "which coincide with the methyl formate peaks, MF1 and MF2 (cite: Favre2011), respectively" (Results)
3. **Identify CH3(17)OH lines**: Compare observed spectra at MeOH1 and MeOH2 to the laboratory CH3(17)OH spectrum and frequencies; assign other lines with CDMS and JPL catalogues; compare profiles and maps with CH3(18)OH  
  > "The red spectrum shows the CH$_3$$^{17}$OH spectrum obtained from spectroscopic measurements in our laboratory (cite: Tamanai2025)" (Results); "we assigned the other molecular lines in the spectra with the aid of spectral line databases" (Results)
   - software: CDMS, JPL catalog
   - parameter: Six CH3(17)OH lines identified at S/N more than 50 sigma; 5_2-4_2 A- blended with 5_3-4_3 E at 236.554 GHz  
  > "In MeOH1 and MeOH2, six emission lines are identified as CH$_3$$^{17}$OH with a S/N ratio of more than $50\sigma$." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Peak velocities and line profiles of CH3(17)OH similar to those of CH3(18)OH; maps peak at MeOH1 and MeOH2  
  > "The peak velocities and line profiles of CH$_3$$^{17}$OH lines are similar to those of CH$_3$$^{18}$OH lines" (Results)
4. **Match angular resolution for line comparison**: Convolve cubes with Gaussian kernels to a common 2.0" x 2.0" resolution  
  > "Here, the cube data was convolved with Gaussian kernels to compare the spectra at the same angular resolution of $2''.0 \times 2''.0$." (Results)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Common resolution 2.0" x 2.0"  
  > "the cube data was convolved with Gaussian kernels to compare the spectra at the same angular resolution of $2''.0 \times 2''.0$" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive CH3(18)OH and CH3(17)OH column densities and their ratio at MeOH1 and MeOH2**: LTE, optically thin, single-transition (5_0-4_0 E) integrated intensities from the 2.0" x 2.0" cubes with fixed rotation temperatures  
  > "The column densities of CH$_3$$^{17}$OH and CH$_3$$^{18}$OH were calculated in MeOH1 and MeOH2 under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) approximation with optically thin conditions" (Column densities); "We used the integrated intensities of CH$_3$$^{18}$OH ($5_0-4_0$ $E$) and CH$_3$$^{17}$OH ($5_0-4_0$ $E$) obtained from the convolved data cubes which have the common angular resolutions of $2''.0 \times 2''.0$." (Column densities)
   - software: CDMS, JPL catalog
   - parameter: Rotation temperature 58 K (MeOH1) and 130 K (MeOH2), fixed  
  > "In the calculation, we assumed the rotation temperature of 58 K and 130 K for MeOH1 and MeOH2, respectively, which are the rotation temperatures of CH$_3$OH in dM-3 and dM-1 (cite: Peng2012)." (Column densities)
   - assumption: LTE justified by dense gas (n_H2 > 1e8 cm^-3 vs n_crit ~1e5 cm^-3); optically thin lines  
  > "the assumption of LTE is justified because the emissions originate from sufficiently dense regions with the H$_2$ volume density of $n_{\rm H_2} > 10^8$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: Favre2011)" (Column densities)
   - fitting: LTE model spectra generated from the derived column densities and fixed rotation temperatures and overlaid on the observed spectra (no sampler or priors stated)  
  > "we generated model spectra under the LTE approximation and overlaid them on the observed spectra at MeOH1 and MeOH2" (Column densities)
   - validation: Model spectra agree with observations except contaminated lines; column densities from the less contaminated CH3(18)OH 5_1-4_1 E line are consistent; rotation diagram attempted but E_u range 40-60 K too narrow  
  > "The model spectra show good agreement with the observed spectra at the positions of CH$_3$$^{17}$OH and CH$_3$$^{18}$OH, except for the lines contaminated by emission lines from other molecules." (Column densities); "These are consistent, within the uncertainties, with the values obtained from CH$_3$$^{18}$OH ($5_0-4_0$ $E$)." (Column densities)
6. **Check the optical-depth assumption**: Estimate optical depths of CH3(18)OH and CH3(17)OH 5_0-4_0 E from Peng2012 CH3OH column densities and rotational temperatures with canonical 16O/18O = 560 and 16O/17O = 2000  
  > "the optical depths of CH$_3$$^{18}$OH ($5_0-4_0$ $E$) and CH$_3$$^{17}$OH ($5_0-4_0$ $E$) are expected to be $0.05-0.12$ and $0.01-0.04$, respectively." (Column densities)
   - parameter: Canonical 16O/18O = 560 and 16O/17O = 2000 (Wilson1999)  
  > "Assuming the canonical values of $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O $=560$ and $^{16}$O/$^{17}$O $=2000$ (cite: Wilson1999)" (Column densities)
   - assumption: Isotopologue ratios equal the canonical local-ISM values  
  > "Assuming the canonical values of $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O $=560$ and $^{16}$O/$^{17}$O $=2000$ (cite: Wilson1999)" (Column densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Optical depths 0.05-0.12 (CH3(18)OH) and 0.01-0.04 (CH3(17)OH), so optically thin is considered reasonable  
  > "Therefore, the assumption of the optically thin condition is considered reasonable." (Column densities)
7. **Derive 16O/17O and 16O/18O at dM-1 and dM-3 using Peng2012 CH3OH**: Smooth the ALMA data to 3.8" x 2.0" PA 22 deg, repeat the LTE column-density procedure with Trot 130 K (dM-1) and 58 K (dM-3), and combine with CH3OH column densities to form isotope ratios  
  > "We used the same procedures to calculate the column densities of the two CH$_3$OH isotopologues, after smoothing the angular resolution of the ALMA data to be $3''.8 \times 2''.0$ with PA of $22^{\circ}$" (Column densities)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Trot = 130 K (dM-1), 58 K (dM-3)  
  > "The rotation temperatures of 130 K and 58 K were adopted for dM-1 and dM-3, respectively, following (cite: Peng2012)." (Column densities)
   - assumption: CH3OH column densities from Peng2012 (optical depth below 0.2) are applicable at the ALMA positions, which coincide with the PdBI positions within the beam  
  > "The positions of dM-1 and dM-3 almost coincide with those of MeOH2 and MeOH1, respectively, within the beam size of the PdBI observation" (Column densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ratios compared with 16O/17O = 2000 +/- 200 and 16O/18O = 560 +/- 30 in the local ISM, and with CO-isotopologue and methyl-formate ratios from the literature  
  > "are consistent with the ratios of $^{16}$O/$^{17}$O $=2000\pm 200$ and $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O $=560\pm 30$ in the local ISM (cite: Wilson1999) within the uncertainty" (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CH3OH is abundant in the Orion KL compact ridge and archival ALMA data cover the CH3(17)OH and CH3(18)OH lines  
  > "As previous studies have demonstrated that CH$_3$OH shows a high column density and thus a high abundance in the compact ridge, we investigated the presence of CH$_3$$^{17}$OH lines in this region" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Fixed Trot from Peng2012 used because rotation diagram from narrow E_u range was too uncertain  
  > "the limited range of the upper state energies ($E_{\rm u} = 40-60$ K) resulted in significant uncertainties in the derived values" (Column densities); "Therefore, we employed the rotation temperatures of CH$_3$OH from (cite: Peng2012)." (Column densities)
- Alternative: Rotation diagram method for column density and temperature attempted but rejected for large uncertainties  
  > "we attempted to estimate column density and rotation temperature by using the rotation diagram method, however, the limited range of the upper state energies ($E_{\rm u} = 40-60$ K) resulted in significant uncertainties in the derived values." (Column densities)
- Alternative: Same J=5-4 lines of main CH3OH isotopologue not usable (high optical depth); column densities from optically thin lines of Peng2012 used  
  > "However, the same transition lines of the main isotopologue could not be used due to high optical depth." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Column densities from single transitions with fixed rotation temperatures carry uncertainties  
  > "Since we evaluated the column densities from the single transitions with the fixed rotation temperatures, the column densities are subject to uncertainties due to these factors." (Column densities)
- Limitation (authors): Missing flux from different antenna configurations or subthermal excitation might affect the 16O/18O ratio  
  > "Another possibility is the effect of missing flux caused by the different antenna configurations between the observations or the subthermal excitation condition of CH$_3$OH in this position" (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Several CH3(17)OH lines are contaminated by other molecular emission  
  > "although some of the CH$_3$$^{17}$OH lines are contaminated by other nearby molecular emission lines" (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CH3OH main-isotopologue columns come from a different instrument and beam (PdBI, Peng2012) and are not independently measured here, so 16O/17O and 16O/18O inherit that systematic error and any missing flux
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed Trot from Peng2012 (58 K, 130 K) applied to different tracers and beams, with no excitation analysis or non-LTE check; ratios are only insensitive to Trot because Eu are similar
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optical depth of CH3(18)OH was estimated from assumed canonical isotope ratios, which is circular for a paper measuring those ratios
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainties appear to be statistical only; no calibration or contamination uncertainty is propagated, and the CH3(17)OH 5_0-4_0 E column at MeOH1 has a large +/-0.6e15 error
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Internal inconsistencies: conclusions quote 3.4 +/- 0.2 and 2300 +/- 200 whereas the table and text give 3.3 +/- 0.2 and 2300 +/- 300
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two datasets with different phase centres, beams and calibration were combined without a stated flux-scale cross-check
