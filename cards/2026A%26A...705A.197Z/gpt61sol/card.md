## Analysis recipe

1. **Define disk density and temperature**: Combine radial and vertical density forms; assume blackbody equilibrium temperature and fit density.  
  > "The density structure $\rho(r,z)$ of the disk is defined by two components: a radial profile form and a vertical profile form." (Parametric modeling); "we assume a blackbody equilibrium temperature structure, allowing us to fit for the density structure." (Parametric modeling)
   - software: adapted parametric debris disk modeling code
   - parameter: Radial DPL, DG, Erf, AG, TPL or G; vertical Gaussian, exponential, double Gaussian or Lorentzian.  
  > "Most of our models use either a radial double power law or a radial double Gaussian" (Parametric modeling); "The best-fitting radial forms were a double power law, asymmetric Gaussian, and error function." (HD 76582); "we examined double power law, double Gaussian, Gaussian, and asymmetric Gaussian radial profiles." (HD 109573 (HR 4796A))
   - parameter: Radial DPL gamma=2.  
  > "For all radial DPL models, we fix $\gamma=2$." (Parametric modeling)
   - parameter: Scale heights reported as HWHM.  
  > "Therefore, we report scale heights in terms of the half width at half maximum (HWHM) of the vertical profile, $H_{\rm HWHM}$." (Definitions of scale height)
   - assumption: Optically thin, axisymmetric debris disks.  
  > "As debris disks are optically thin, their temperatures and densities are fully degenerate." (Parametric modeling); "optimized for modeling the continuum (dust) emission of axisymmetric debris disks" (Parametric modeling)
   - assumption: H(r)=hr; constant aspect ratio at all radii.  
  > "such that the vertical aspect ratio, $h$, is constant at all radii, $r$." (Definitions of scale height)
   - assumption: No hydrostatic equilibrium or velocity-field calculation; dust expected to dominate gas mass.  
  > "While some ARKS debris disks are known to contain gas, we do not calculate hydrostatic equilibrium or the velocity field as we expect the dust mass to dominate over the gas mass." (Parametric modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Radial forms informed by the independent ARKS radial structure analysis.  
  > "Thus, the set of radial functional forms applied for the vertical parametric modeling presented here was informed by the ARKS radial structure analysis (cite: Han_2026)." (Parametric modeling)
2. **Generate synthetic continuum visibilities**: Integrate radiative transfer with a Planck source function; apply primary beam; sample model visibilities at observed spatial frequencies.  
  > "We approximated the source function with the Planck function:" (Radiative transfer); "we used `galario` to obtain synthetic visibilities sampled at the same spatial frequencies as the data (cite: Galario_2018)." (Radiative transfer)
   - software: galario
   - parameter: Gaussian primary beam approximation; mosaic treatment for HD 39060.  
  > "After generating the model disk image and multiplying it by a Gaussian approximation of the primary beam," (Radiative transfer); "The archival Band 6 observations of HD 39060 are mosaicked. For this source, we apply a primary beam correction at each location in the mosaic field." (Radiative transfer)
   - parameter: Stellar flux added in visibility domain at disk center; dRA and dDec free.  
  > "The stellar flux is added to the image in the visibility domain at the disk center, which is set by free parameters that enable offsets in right ascension (dRA) and declination (dDec) from the phase center." (Radiative transfer)
   - assumption: Dust opacity 1.9 cm² g⁻¹ at 0.89 mm; 1.3 cm² g⁻¹ at 1.3 mm.  
  > "We adopted $\kappa_\nu=$1.9 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 0.89 mm and $\kappa_\nu=$1.3 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at 1.3 mm as in (cite: Marino_2026)" (Radiative transfer)
   - assumption: Grain-size slope -3.5; mass composition 70% astrosilicates, 15% crystalline water ice, 15% amorphous carbon.  
  > "who assumed a power law grain size distribution with a slope of -3.5, and a composition of astrosilicates (70%), crystalline water ice (15%), and amorphous carbon (15%) by mass (cite: Draine_2003; Li_1998)." (Radiative transfer)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit parametric disk models**: Fit observed visibilities using affine-invariant MCMC.  
  > "We used the affine-invariant MCMC implementation in `emcee` to obtain best-fit model parameters and their posterior distributions (cite: emcee_2013)." (Model parameters)
   - software: emcee, galario.double.chi2Image()
   - parameter: 64 walkers; at least 10^5 post-convergence samples, approximately 1500 steps per walker.  
  > "For each model, we initialized the MCMC with a 64 walker ensemble (more than double the number of free parameters for each model)." (Model parameters); "we required at least $10^{5}$ samples after convergence, which corresponds to approximately $1500$ steps for each of the 64 walkers." (Model parameters)
   - parameter: Fit inclination, PA, surface density normalization, stellar flux, dRA, dDec and radial/vertical form parameters.  
  > "the free parameters included the disk inclination ($i$), position angle (PA), surface density normalization ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{c}}$), stellar flux ($F_{\star}$) at the observing wavelength" (Model parameters); "and offsets from the phase center (dRA and dDec)." (Model parameters); "The additional free parameters for each model depend on the choice of radial and vertical density forms" (Model parameters)
   - parameter: AU Mic: three separate stellar flux values.  
  > "AU Mic exhibited significant variations in the stellar flux across the three observations used here. Thus, we fit three separate stellar flux values as in (cite: Daley_2019)." (Model parameters)
   - fitting: Log-likelihood proportional to -0.5 chi²; weighted squared real and imaginary visibility residuals. Priors not stated.  
  > "The log-likelihood is proportional to $-0.5 \chi^2$, where the model and data are compared using a $\chi^2$ metric" (Model parameters); "\chi^2=\sum_{j=1}^N w_j \left[\left(\operatorname{ReV}_{{\rm obs}\, j}-\operatorname{ReV}_{\bmod j}\right)^2+\left(\operatorname{ImV}_{{\rm obs}\, j}-\operatorname{ImV}_{\bmod j}\right)^2\right]," (Model parameters)
   - validation: Visual walker and likelihood inspection; discard burn-in and walkers at least 5 sigma below maximum log-likelihood.  
  > "Convergence was determined by visually inspecting the evolution of all walkers in both parameter space and log-likelihood space." (Model parameters); "We identified convergence once the log-likelihood stabilized and the walkers fluctuated around consistent parameter ranges, discarding earlier steps as burn-in." (Model parameters); "We further discarded walkers with log-likelihood values at least $5 \sigma$ lower than the maximum log-likelihood value in order to prevent individual unconverged walkers from affecting the results." (Model parameters)
4. **Select fiducial models and assess resolved heights**: Compare radial and vertical parameterizations with AIC and BIC; examine residuals, posteriors and nominal interferometer resolution.  
  > "We compared different model parameterizations using the Akaike and Bayesian Information Criteria (AIC and BIC, respectively)" (Model comparison); "We additionally examine how well-resolved $h_{\rm HWHM}$ is with respect to the resolution of the observations." (Analysis)
   - parameter: When criteria disagree, select model least strongly ruled out: AIC confidence <3 sigma and ΔBIC<10.  
  > "the model which is least strongly ruled out by the AIC and BIC (AIC confidence $<3 \sigma$ and $\Delta{\rm{BIC}}<10$) is selected as the fiducial model." (Model comparison)
   - parameter: Approximate 1 sigma from median minus 16th percentile; extrapolate to 3 sigma; lower bound at or below zero implies marginal resolution.  
  > "we take the difference between the median and 16th percentile $h$ values in order to approximate a $1\sigma$ confidence interval" (Analysis); "for sources with a $h_{\rm HWHM}\leq0$ at the $3\sigma$ lower bound, we consider $h_{\rm HWHM}$ to be marginally resolved, placing only an upper limit on $h_{\rm HWHM}$." (Analysis)
   - parameter: Nominal resolution theta_min=lambda_obs/b_max; 84th-percentile upper limits for HD 109573 and HD 131488.

## Qualitative findings

- Thick-tailed vertical profiles are common and may indicate multiple dynamical populations.  
  > "Lorentzian (or otherwise thick-tailed) vertical dust distributions are common, suggesting that systems may commonly host multiple dynamical populations within the disk." (Conclusions)
- Only HD 10647 has a fiducial vertical double Gaussian model.  
  > "only HD 10647 is identified as having a fiducial model with a vertical double Gaussian" (Non-Gaussian vertical distributions as the new norm)
- Vertically thicker and radially wider belts tend to have greater inferred mass.  
  > "We find that belts which are vertically thicker and radially wider tend to be more massive." (Constraints on the disk mass)
- HD 32297, HD 131488 and HD 197481 require alternatives to the adopted self-stirring scenario.  
  > "We find that all but three disks (HD 32297, HD 131488, and HD 197481) could have been self-stirred within the age of the system." (Constraints on the disk mass); "The disks that cannot be explained solely by self-stirring require an alternative mechanism" (Constraints on the disk mass)
- Scattered light scale heights tend to be similar to or larger than millimeter heights; no clear gas-bearing versus gas-poor trend.  
  > "Aspect ratios obtained from scattered light observations tend to be similar to or larger than the corresponding ALMA millimeter aspect ratios" (Conclusions); "We do not observe a clear trend when comparing the gas-bearing debris disks against those with no detected gas" (Conclusions)
- Possible increase of scale height with disk age remains tentative.  
  > "we may for the first time be observing the theoretically predicted increase in scale height with disk age." (Conclusions); "However, because the disks are so thin, follow-up observations at higher resolution could help us better understand this trend." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Highly inclined, high-resolution disks permit vertical recovery; millimeter grains trace large-body dynamics with little radiation-pressure influence.  
  > "In order to resolve the vertical distribution of dust, observations of highly inclined (i.e., close to edge-on) debris disks at high angular resolution are necessary." (Introduction); "At (sub)millimeter wavelengths, dust grains are largely unaffected by radiation pressure and allow direct probing of the mass distribution and level of dynamical excitation of large planetesimals in the system." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Visibility modeling retains small-scale spatial information; flexible radial profiles address radial–vertical degeneracy; constant aspect ratio limits free parameters.  
  > "Because our parametric modeling occurs in the visibility domain, we retain spatial information up to the nominal resolution of the interferometer" (Analysis); "Careful treatment of the radial structures is critical, as they can be degenerate with the vertical structure of the disk." (Parametric modeling); "the vertical aspect ratio $h$ is constant throughout the disk in order to minimize additional free parameters." (Parametric modeling)
- Alternative: AIC and BIC can select different models because BIC penalizes additional parameters more strongly.  
  > "four of the 13 sources (HD 9672, HD 10647, HD 39060, and HD 161868) have a vertical double Gaussian model which is most strongly favored by the AIC, while no sources have a vertical double Gaussian model which is most strongly favored by the BIC." (Non-Gaussian vertical distributions as the new norm)
- Alternative: Planet migration and secular planet–disk interactions are alternative explanations for thick-tailed profiles.  
  > "This could hint at Neptune-like migration histories in these systems or could be a remnant of long-term planet-disk interactions in general." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Wavelength-dependent vertical grain distributions can produce non-Gaussian emission even for Gaussian solids.  
  > "if the vertical distribution of solids changes with observing wavelength, then even a Gaussian vertical distribution of solids could produce a non-Gaussian emission profile (cite: Terrill_2023)." (Non-Gaussian vertical distributions as the new norm)
- Alternative: Alternative minimum cascade mass framework gives incompatible rescaled masses for three of seven compared disks.  
  > "and incompatible for HD 61005, HD 131488, and HD 197481." (Comparison of disk mass estimates)
- Alternative: Full planet-driven dynamical inference deferred to a future study.  
  > "A full exploration of planet-driven dynamical excitation scenarios is outside the scope of this study." (Constraints on planetary companions)
- How they decided it was enough: Observations designed for at least two vertical-FWHM resolution elements at h_sigma=0.05; MCMC required at least 10^5 post-convergence samples; most residuals clean at 5 sigma.  
  > "The new Band 7 observations were configured to resolve the vertical FWHM of the disks with at least two resolution elements, assuming a vertical aspect ratio" (Observations); "we required at least $10^{5}$ samples after convergence, which corresponds to approximately $1500$ steps for each of the 64 walkers." (Model parameters); "For most sources the fiducial models closely match the observations, with no positive or negative features in the residuals at the 5$\sigma$ level." (Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Axisymmetric, midplane-peaked models can obscure asymmetric structures and inflate heights in warped disks.  
  > "First, we consider only axisymmetric disks with vertical distributions that peak at the disk midplane." (Limitations); "the presence of a warp could bias the axisymmetric models to larger scale heights in order to account for the warped material sitting further from the midplane." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Constant aspect ratio excludes radial variation supported by some other studies.  
  > "Second, in this work we assume that aspect ratio is constant with radius." (Limitations); "The work presented here only considers the case of a radially constant $h$, but follow-up studies with nonconstant $h$ would be beneficial to fully understand how common scale height variability is in debris disks." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Chosen radial and vertical functions may bias heights or fail to capture source morphology.  
  > "A poor choice of radial profile can result in biased vertical results, (and vice versa)." (Limitations); "it is possible that the radial and/or vertical density distributions adopted in this work do not fully capture the morphology of certain sources." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): HD 109573 and HD 131488 heights are limited by resolution; AU Mic's smallest inferred height may also be inadequately resolved.  
  > "we may still be limited by the data resolution of the data ($\theta_{\rm min}=3.63$ au)." (HD 109573 (HR 4796A)); "the interpretation of our measured values of $h_{\rm HWHM}$ may be limited by the resolution of the data." (HD 131488); "it remains unclear whether the data resolution is sufficient to fully resolve such a small aspect ratio." (HD 197481 (AU Mic))
- Limitation (authors): Height–mass correlation is partly imposed by the model; gas effects are omitted.  
  > "the positive correlation occurs due to the model specification, as $h_{\rm HWHM}$ is used to obtain the typical grain velocity, which directly affects the derived disk mass" (Constraints on the disk mass); "gas is not considered in the collisional cascade model used to obtain our estimates on $M_{\rm{disk}}$." (Constraints on the disk mass)
- Limitation (authors): Inclination conversions are not strictly valid for non-Gaussian vertical profiles; quoted inclination errors omit these assumptions.  
  > "there is additional uncertainty stemming from the approximations and assumptions used to compute $\langle i_{\rm rms} \rangle$." (Theoretical implications); "However, the non-Gaussianity of most vertical profiles means that neither of these equations are strictly true for this application." (Theoretical implications)
- Limitation (authors): Mixed modeling methods and resolutions prevent firm multiwavelength conclusions.  
  > "The predictions come with the caveat that results are difficult to directly compare if the $h$ measurements were obtained with different methods or with different data resolutions." (Theoretical implications); "Unfortunately, this makes it impossible to draw firm conclusions with the current data and analyses." (Theoretical implications)
- Limitation (authors): Some scattered light disks are vertically unresolved and scale heights depend on dataset signal-to-noise.  
  > "First, not all disks are well resolved vertically in scattered light." (Comparison to other vertical measurements); "the scale height may be different, mostly because the signal-to-noise of the scattered light observations is not the same." (Comparison to other vertical measurements)
