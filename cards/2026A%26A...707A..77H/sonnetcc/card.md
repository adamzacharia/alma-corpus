   - parameter: target S/N = 50

(3.2 Outflow identification in the cold molecular phase)

## Qualitative findings

- Quiescent ionized gas is star formation-excited throughout; the outflow component in the SE shows shock or LINER-like ionization  
  > "For the quiescent component, the classification shows star formation is the dominant source of excitation throughout the system." - The NW nucleus shows no clear outflow; a molecular outflow there is uncertain and negligible in mass  
  > "The presence of an outflow in the NW is therefore uncertain" (4.1 Cold molecular gas)
- Outflow velocity does not decrease with distance out to ~5.5 kpc in any phase  
  > "There is no significant decrease of projected outflow velocities with distance from the nucleus out to the limit at which we detect outflowing gas ($\sim5.5\,$kpc) in any of the three phases." (5.1 Outflow velocity structure)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use multiwavelength data already necessary for outflow studies to improve outflow identification, with MUSE stellar kinematics at similar spatial resolution to CO(1-0)  
  > "In this work, we aim to leverage the already necessary multiwavelength data further to improve the outflow identification process." (1 Introduction); "the spectral decomposition of the CO(1-0) emission line is based on stellar kinematics derived from optical spectroscopy at similar spatial resolution." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Fixed velocity cuts developed for isolated rotating galaxies may not suit mergers; stellar kinematics trace gravitationally induced motions  
  > "This technique, developed for the analysis of isolated star-forming galaxies with gas dynamics dominated by regular rotating motion, might not be sufficient to capture the complexity of a merging system such as IRAS20100-4156." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Previous high-velocity-only outflow identification rejected as it can underestimate the outflow mass  
  > "This approach offers the potential to underestimate the total outflow mass by excluding outflowing gas at low velocities" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Same decomposition technique as CO(1-0) not applied to Halpha because of Halpha/[NII] blending  
  > "we were not able to apply the same technique for outflow identification here as for the CO(1-0) line." (3.3 Outflow identification in the ionized phase)
- Alternative: AGN-oriented electron density method for outflows not applied because the outflow region is shock-dominated  
  > "Since our outflow region appears dominated by LINER-like emission most likely powered by shocks we do not consider it applicable to our case." (Footnote 1)
- How they decided it was enough: Added Gaussian components until the lowest number sufficient to model the prominent line-profile features; best solution chosen by lowest AIC  
  > "We found this to be the lowest number of additional components sufficient to model prominent features in the complex line profiles throughout in the galaxy." (3.2 Outflow identification in the cold molecular phase)
- Limitation (authors): Ionized outflow masses are lower limits due to the electron density floor  
  > "As a result, the global outflow masses we obtained should be treated as the lower limits for the actual outflow masses." (4.2 Ionized gas tracers)
- Limitation (authors): alpha_CO in outflows is highly uncertain, so energetics are order-of-magnitude estimates  
  > "For cold molecular gas in outflows, $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ is highly uncertain" (5.3 Outflow energetics); "Due to this, the comparisons and derived efficiencies in this section should be considered as order-of-magnitude estimates." (5.3 Outflow energetics)
- Limitation (authors): No proper gravitational/non-gravitational decomposition possible for the ionized gas because of blending and low spectral resolution  
  > "We remind the reader that in contrast to the case of the molecular gas, for the ionized gas, we could not perform a proper decomposition into gravitational and non-gravitational features" (Footnote 2)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow presence in the NW nucleus is uncertain  
  > "The presence of an outflow in the NW is therefore uncertain" (4.1 Cold molecular gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Quiescent component assumes stars and gas share kinematics; real molecular gas in a merger can have kinematics offset from stars, so some quiescent gas may be classed as outflow
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single alpha_CO of 1.7 applied to both quiescent and outflowing gas; outflow mass and energetics scale directly with it
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass rate Mdot = M v / r per bin with minimum radii depends on beam size and the unspecified geometry
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO cube imaging parameters and S/N noise definition are not given, so bin-by-bin results are hard to reproduce exactly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass relies on a constant dust-to-gas ratio and a metallicity taken from the quiescent gas
