## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, continuum-subtract, and image uv datasets to matched spatial and spectral resolutions**: Calibrate raw uv data, subtract continuum with UVCONTSUB, image in TCLEAN with matched UVRANGE, WIDTH, CELL, and UVTAPER using AUTO-MULTITHRESH down to 2 sigma, and smooth to a common round beam with IMSMOOTH.  
  > "Continuum subtraction was performed on each *uv* dataset using line-free channels and CASA’s UVCONTSUB task." (Data and imaging); "Making use of TLCEAN’s CELL, UVTAPER, UVRANGE, and WIDTH parameters, the *uv* coverage, spectral channel width, and dirty beam sizes were compared and matched for each galaxy." (Data and imaging); "A final smoothing to a common round beam was completed for all lines in each galaxy with the CASA task IMSMOOTH." (Data and imaging)
   - software: CASA, UVCONTSUB, TCLEAN, IMSTAT, AUTO-MULTITHRESH, IMSMOOTH
   - parameter: UVRANGE > 15 klambda (NGC 3256), > 19.8 klambda (NGC 7469), > 15.9 klambda (IRAS 13120)  
  > "| UVRANGE cut-off (k$\lambda$)^*c*^ | $>15$ | $>19.8$ | $>15.9$ |" (Table 3)
   - parameter: Velocity resolution: 26.43 km s^-1 (NGC 3256), 20.68 km s^-1 (NGC 7469), 20.64 km s^-1 (IRAS 13120)  
  > "| Velocity resolution (km s$^{-1}$)^*b*^ | $26.43$ | $20.68$ | $20.64$ |" (Table 3)
   - parameter: Final smoothed round beam: 2.2'' (NGC 3256), 0.95'' (NGC 7469), 1.1'' (IRAS 13120)  
  > "| Beam size^*a*^ | $2.2''$ | $0.95''$ | $1.1''$ |" (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Masks produced by TCLEAN auto-masking algorithm were checked for consistency during major cleaning cycles.  
  > "Masks produced by TCLEAN’s auto-masking algorithm were checked for consistency during major cleaning cycles." (Data and imaging)
2. **Create integrated intensity (moment 0) maps, uncertainty maps, and regional line intensity ratios**: Extract cleaned smoothed cubes, trim to line emission channels (including all hyperfine components), mask at 3.5 sigma and require emission in all six lines, integrate to moment 0 in K km s^-1, apply primary beam correction, and compute regional and global line ratios.  
  > "An RMS cut-off of $3.5\sigma$ was used to mask the cleaned cubes to include strong emission while avoiding noisy emission in lines with lower S/N." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios); "The moment 0 maps were further masked to only include pixels with emission in all six spectral lines." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios); "The moment 0 maps were converted to physical units of K km s$^{-1}$ and corrected for the primary beam response." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios)
   - software: CASA, ASTROPY, PYTHON 3.7
   - parameter: RMS masking threshold = 3.5 sigma  
  > "An RMS cut-off of $3.5\sigma$ was used to mask the cleaned cubes to include strong emission while avoiding noisy emission in lines with lower S/N." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios)
   - parameter: Nuclear aperture size = FWHM of the beam centered on the 93 GHz continuum peak  
  > "The central nuclear pixels were identified using the 93 GHz radio continuum emission peak for NGC 3256 and NGC 7469. To isolate the nuclear regions, we used an aperture centred on the nuclear pixel. The size of the aperture is equal to the full-width at half-maximum of the beam in each galaxy." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios)
   - assumption: ALMA calibration uncertainties are 5% (Band 3) and 10% (Band 6 and 7)  
  > "ALMA calibration uncertainties are 5% (Band 3) and 10% (Band 6 and 7)." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Perform pixel-by-pixel linear regression of line intensities in log-log space**: Re-sample imaged cubes to half the beam FWHM (reducing pixel count across beam by factor of 3), recompute masked moment 0 maps, correct NGC 7469 for inclination, and fit log(y) = m log(x) + b using Linmix.  
  > "We first re-sampled the imaged data cubes to half the full-width at half-maximum of the beam. The number of pixels sampled across the beam was reduced by a factor of 3 for all galaxies, and the new pixel sizes can be found in Table 3." (Pixel-by-pixel comparisons); "We used the Linmix linear regression method to fit the integrated intensity pixels in log-log space and account for the higher uncertainties seen in the lower S/N pixels (cite: Kelly2007; Meyers2018)." (Pixel-by-pixel comparisons)
   - software: LINMIX, ASTROPY, PYTHON 3.7
   - parameter: Re-binned pixel sizes: 1.1'' (NGC 3256), 0.475'' (NGC 7469), 0.55'' (IRAS 13120)  
  > "| Re-binned pixel size^*b*^ | $1.1''$ | $0.475''$ | $0.55''$ |" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Inclination angle of 45 +/- 5 degrees applied to NGC 7469; no inclination correction applied to NGC 3256 or IRAS 13120  
  > "For NGC 7469, we corrected the measured intensities in the scatter plots for an inclination angle of $45\pm5$ degrees (cite: Davies2004). The intensities of the other two galaxies were not corrected for inclination angle[^3]." (Pixel-by-pixel comparisons)
   - fitting: Linmix Bayesian linear regression in log-log space of the form log(y) = m log(x) + b  
  > "*Notes:* Fit parameters determined from Linmix linear fitting of the form log(y) = m log(x) + b." (Table 5)
   - validation: Tested applying a conservative inclination angle of 60 degrees to NGC 3256 and IRAS 13120 and verified slopes and intercepts matched within one standard deviation.  
  > "We performed the same analysis applying a conservative estimate for inclination angles of $60^\circ$ to NGC 3256 and IRAS 1320 and found that the resulting slopes and intercepts match the results in Table 5 within one standard deviation." (Consistency in the CN ratios)
4. **Estimate CN/HCN column density ratios in NGC 3256 using LTE and 1D non-LTE radiative transfer**: Estimate column density range via simple LTE calculation, then run online RADEX non-LTE calculations to reproduce observed CN (1-0)/HCN (1-0) intensity ratios in NGC 3256.  
  > "A simple LTE calculation suggests that the CN and HCN column densities should be in the range of $10^{13}-10^{17}$ cm$^{-2}$. We used this column density range to perform a simple non-LTE calculation with the online version of RADEX[^2] (cite: vanderTak2007)." (The CN/HCN intensity ratio as a probe of radiation field)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Gas temperature = 40 K, H2 number density = 10^7 cm^-3, line width = 10 km s^-1, column density range = 10^13 - 10^17 cm^-2  
  > "Using a gas temperature of $40$ K, an H$_2$ number density of $10^7$ cm$^{-3}$, and a line width of $10$ km s$^{-1}$, we reproduce CN (1-0)/HCN (1-0) $\sim1$ in the non-nuclear pixels of NGC 3256 with a column density ratio of N~CN~/N~HCN~ $\sim10$." (The CN/HCN intensity ratio as a probe of radiation field)
   - assumption: T_gas = 40 K, n(H2) = 10^7 cm^-3, delta V = 10 km s^-1  
  > "Using a gas temperature of $40$ K, an H$_2$ number density of $10^7$ cm$^{-3}$, and a line width of $10$ km s$^{-1}$, we reproduce CN (1-0)/HCN (1-0) $\sim1$ in the non-nuclear pixels of NGC 3256 with a column density ratio of N~CN~/N~HCN~ $\sim10$." (The CN/HCN intensity ratio as a probe of radiation field)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- HCN (3-2)/HCN (1-0) varies spatially within NGC 3256 and NGC 7469 with superlinear trends, peaking in the nuclei, whereas IRAS 13120 shows higher overall ratios (~0.8) with little spatial variation at ~700 pc resolution.  
  > "The HCN (3-2)/HCN (1-0) intensity ratio varies in NGC 3256 and NGC 7469, with superlinear trends of $1.53\pm0.07$ and $1.55\pm0.05$, respectively." (Abstract); "At our resolution ($\sim700$ pc), IRAS 13120-5453 shows little variation in the HCN intensity ratios." (Abstract)
- Individual galaxies exhibit nearly constant CN (2-1)/CN (1-0) intensity ratios, with NGC 3256 offset to lower values (~0.45) compared to NGC 7469 and IRAS 13120 (~0.8).  
  > "We find relatively constant CN (2-1)/CN (1-0) ratios and subtle variations in the CN (3-2)/CN (2-1) ratio in our galaxy sample. We find an offset to lower ratios of $\sim0.45$ in the CN (2-1)/CN (1-0) ratio in NGC 3256 compared to $\sim0.8$ in NGC 7469 and IRAS 13120." (Conclusions)
- Enhanced CN (1-0)/HCN (1-0) ratios (~1.7) are found in strong starburst regions (IRAS 13120 and the northern nucleus of NGC 3256), whereas the AGN-dominated galaxy NGC 7469 shows a constant lower ratio (~1.1).  
  > "We find high ($\sim1.7$) CN (1-0)/HCN (1-0) intensity ratios in IRAS 13120-5453 and in the northern nucleus of NGC 3256, compared to a more constant ratio ($\sim1.1$) in NGC 7469 and non-starbursting regions of NGC 3256." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Three U/LIRGs hosting different combinations of starbursts and AGN observed in three transitions each of CN and HCN allow probing the effects of both UV (PDR) and X-ray (XDR) radiation fields on dense gas excitation at spatial scales isolating nuclear and disk regions.  
  > "In this paper, we present the results of an observational study of the intensity ratios of CN and HCN in three U/LIRGs. We use both new and archival ALMA observations of three lines each of CN and HCN. The higher energy lines allow us to explore the interaction between these molecules in a variety of radiation environments." (Introduction); "The different galaxy environments available in this sample allow us to probe the effects of both UV and X-ray radiation fields on the molecular gas." (U/LIRG sample)
- Why this method: Matching uv ranges, channel widths, and beams across all 6 lines per galaxy ensures recovery of similar spatial scales; masking at 3.5 sigma across all 6 lines avoids noisy emission; Linmix accounts for larger uncertainties in lower S/N pixels during log-log fitting.  
  > "The UVRANGE parameter was matched between all lines in each galaxy in order to recover similar flux scales for each line." (Data and imaging); "An RMS cut-off of $3.5\sigma$ was used to mask the cleaned cubes to include strong emission while avoiding noisy emission in lines with lower S/N." (Integrated intensities and measured ratios); "We used the Linmix linear regression method to fit the integrated intensity pixels in log-log space and account for the higher uncertainties seen in the lower S/N pixels (cite: Kelly2007; Meyers2018)." (Pixel-by-pixel comparisons)
- Alternative: Applying an inclination angle correction of 60 degrees to NGC 3256 and IRAS 13120 was tested, but omitted because NGC 3256 has two nuclei with different inclinations, IRAS 13120 has no literature inclination, and the resulting slopes and intercepts matched the uncorrected ones within 1 sigma.  
  > "We performed the same analysis applying a conservative estimate for inclination angles of $60^\circ$ to NGC 3256 and IRAS 1320 and found that the resulting slopes and intercepts match the results in Table 5 within one standard deviation." (Consistency in the CN ratios)
- Alternative: Requiring a 93 GHz radio continuum detection in each pixel (used by Wilson et al. in prep.) was not adopted so that the analysis could extend to lower line intensities.  
  > "Since we are not requiring 93 GHz continuum detections, we can extend our analysis to the lower line intensities where we find most of our variations in the CN (1-0)/HCN (1-0) ratios." (The CN/HCN intensity ratio as a probe of radiation field)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Applying UVRANGE cut-offs and UVTAPER in TCLEAN limits short baselines and can lead to missing flux on the largest angular scales.  
  > "We note that applying a *uv* cut-off and taper with the UVRANGE and UVTAPER parameters in TCLEAN will limit the number of short baselines in our data and thus could potentially lead to missing flux on the largest angular scales." (Data and imaging)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial resolutions (~300 pc in NGC 7469, ~470 pc in NGC 3256, ~700 pc in IRAS 13120) do not resolve individual XDRs (<50 pc) and blend AGN, starburst, and outflow shock contributions within the central beam.  
  > "Our observations of NGC 7469 have a resolution of $\sim300$ pc and thus blend the influence of both AGN and starburst activity in the circumnuclear disk." (Increased nuclear HCN (3-2)/HCN (1-0) ratio in NGC 7469); "Our resolution of $\sim700$ pc is insufficient to isolate the nuclear region and so the line ratio will have contributions from both the starburst and AGN components in this galaxy." (Higher HCN (3-2)/HCN (1-0) ratios in IRAS 13120 than NGC 3256)
- Limitation (authors): The CN (1-0) dataset for NGC 3256 is from ALMA Cycle 0 and has high calibration uncertainties, meaning the offset to lower CN (2-1)/CN (1-0) ratios in NGC 3256 could be an observational artifact.  
  > "The CN (1-0) data for NGC 3256 is our only data set from ALMA Cycle 0. As such, the flux measurements and intensity ratios using this line in this galaxy have the highest calibration uncertainties of our sample. Given the similarity of the slopes in the CN (2-1)/CN (1-0) ratio of all three galaxies, the offset in the ratio in NGC 3256 could be due to large observational uncertainties." (The CN (2-1)/CN (1-0) intensity ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Observed integrated intensity ratios are affected by gas temperature, density, and optical depth (opacity) effects, so full non-LTE radiative transfer modelling is required to determine true molecular abundance ratios.  
  > "We note that opacity effects could potentially be important and determining the CN/HCN abundance ratios is the next step in this analysis." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Physical spatial resolutions differ by more than a factor of 2 across the sample (304 pc in NGC 7469 vs 715 pc in IRAS 13120), making direct comparisons of nuclear versus global ratios sensitive to beam dilution.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 2.2.2 (NGC 7469 processing), CN (3-2) is listed under both robust = 0.5 and natural weighting while CN (2-1) is omitted, creating a minor ambiguity in the exact imaging recipe.
