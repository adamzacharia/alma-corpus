## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate and image Stokes I, Q, U continuum; derive polarization maps**: Self-calibration of Stokes I, then separate tclean imaging of each Stokes parameter; debiased polarized intensity, fraction and angle  
  > "We used CASA 6.5.2 to perform self-calibration and imaging." (Observations); "The debiased linear polarized intensity, polarization fraction and polarization angle images were constructed following (cite: 2006PASP..118.1340V)." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Self-cal: three phase iterations, final solint 10 s; Briggs robust = 1  
  > "was performed with three iterations in phase with a final solution interval of 10 seconds" (Observations); "using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 1" (Observations)
   - parameter: Polarized emission used within the inner one-third of the primary beam (24 arcsec)  
  > "Linearly polarized dust continuum emission is identified within the the inner one-third area of the primary beam (24$^{\prime\prime}$)." (Observations)
   - assumption: Dust grains are aligned with respect to the magnetic field, so B_pos is inferred from the polarization direction  
  > "is inferred by assuming the dust grains are aligned with respect to the magnetic fields" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stokes Q and U are argued not to be dynamic-range limited, unlike Stokes I  
  > "it is unlikely to be dynamic range limited, making them possible to reach the thermal noise" (Observations)
2. **Image H13CO+ (3-2) cube and moment maps**: tclean with yclean automatic masking; intensity-weighted velocity (moment 1) map relative to systemic velocity  
  > "was obtained by the automatic masking method *yclean* (cite: 2018ApJ...861...14C)" (Observations); "of the intensity-weighted velocity structure (moment 1 map) towards G333" (Results)
   - software: CASA, tclean, yclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 1; 0.56 km/s channels; noise 2.9 mJy/beam (0.61 K)  
  > "leading to a noise level of 2.9 mJy beam$^{-1}$ (0.61 K) per 0.56 km s$^{-1}$ channel" (Observations)
   - assumption: Systemic velocity v_lsr ~ -43 km/s  
  > "with respect to the systemic velocity ($v_\mathrm{lsr}\sim-43$ km s$^{-1}$)" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive gas temperature from CH3CN (14-13)**: Fit the average CH3CN (J=14-13) K=0-3 spectrum with XCLASS in a region avoiding MM1 and MM2  
  > "We detected primarily 4 brightest K components (K = 0, 1, 2, 3) of the transition towards this area." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion); "The blue dashed line represents the fitted spectrum that is obtained by fitting the observed spectrum with XCLASS (cite: 2017A&A...598A...7M) resulting in a temperature of 50 K." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - software: XCLASS
   - parameter: Region close to MM1 and MM2 avoided to limit line contamination  
  > "We avoided the region very close to MM1 and MM2 to refrain the line contamination by radiation from MM1 and MM2." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive gas mass, density and n(H2) of the analysis circle**: Optically thin dust emission, spherical geometry, M = Lambda F D^2 / (kappa B(T)) with T = 50 K; density from sphere of R = 1.5 arcsec  
  > "Using this temperature, and assuming optically thin dust emission and a spherical geometry, the total gas mass is estimated using" (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion); "We estimate the mass density $\rho$ as $3.9\times10^{-17}$ gm cm$^{-3}$, using a volume ($V=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}$, where, $R=1.5^{\prime\prime}$)." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - parameter: Flux density in the circle 493 mJy; T = 50 K  
  > "The flux density enclosed in the circular region is 493 mJy." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100:1  
  > "The gas-to-dust ratio is assumed to be 100:1." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - assumption: Dust opacity 1.03 cm^2/g (Ossenkopf & Henning 1994)  
  > "The dust opacity is assumed as 1.03 cm$^{2}$g$^{-1}$ (cite: 1994A&A...291..943O)." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Paper notes optically thick dust in the centre may cause the mass to be underestimated  
  > "The fact that the dust emission may be optically thick at the very central bright fragmented region, leads to underestimate the mass of the circular region can not be ignored." (Magnetic field strength)
5. **Identify MM1 and MM2 and measure their flux densities**: Dendrogram technique with astrodendro; flux density, FWHM and peak intensity of MM1 and MM2 in Table 1  
  > "the flux densities of MM1 and MM2 are estimated as 59.5 mJy and 25.8 mJy, respectively" (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: F_min = 5 sigma  
  > "$F_{min}=5\sigma$: the threshold above which the structures are defined." (Table 1)
   - parameter: delta = 1 sigma  
  > "$\delta=1\sigma$: the minimum significance for separation of the structures." (Table 1)
   - parameter: S_min = half of the beamsize  
  > "$S_{min}=$half of the beamsize: the minimum area to be allocated in the individual structure." (Table 1)
   - assumption: Similar temperature for MM1 and MM2 when comparing masses  
  > "Assuming the similar temperature, MM1 is 2.3 times more massive than MM2." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Measure turbulent velocity dispersion from H13CO+**: sigma_vlos = sqrt(sigma_obs^2 - sigma_th^2), with sigma_th = sqrt(kT/(mu m_H))  
  > "Using H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ line emission, we derive the dispersion in the turbulent velocity along the line of sight" (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - parameter: T = 50 K  
  > "Assuming a temperature of 50 K, $\sigma_{th}$ and $\sigma v_\mathrm{los}$ are estimated as 0.12 and 1.21 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Dust continuum emission and velocity dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Model the hourglass magnetic field and fit polarization angles**: DustPol (ARTIST, LIME) generates synthetic Stokes I, Q, U, run through simobserve/simanalyze with the same antenna configuration, compared with observed polarization angles for a toroid with poloidal plus toroidal field  
  > "we used the *DustPol* module included in the ARTIST package (cite: 2012A&A...543A..16P), which is based on the Line Modeling Engine (LIME) radiative transfer code (cite: 2010A&A...523A..25B)" (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field); "which are used as an input for the *simobserve* and *simanalyze* tasks of CASA, considering the same antenna configuration of the observing runs in ALMA." (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
   - software: DustPol, ARTIST, LIME, CASA, simobserve, simanalyze
   - parameter: Four free parameters: lambda, b0, magnetic-axis position angle phi, inclination i  
  > "The model used in this work has four free parameters" (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
   - parameter: chi-squared uses pixels above 3 sigma (sigma = 29 uJy/beam) in Stokes Q and U  
  > "The $\chi^{2}$ is estimated accounting for regions lying above the 3$\sigma$ level ($\sigma=29 \mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$) of Stokes $Q$ and $U$ images." (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
   - assumption: Constant temperature of 50 K in the model  
  > "A constant temperature (50 K) has been adopted in the modelling as no temperature map of this region is available currently." (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
   - fitting: chi-squared minimisation of polarization angle residuals over a grid of the four parameters; errors following Lampton et al. 1976; likelihood and priors not stated  
  > "enclosed within the circular area for each combination of the four free parameters" (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field); "The errors are estimated following (cite: 1976ApJ...208..177L)." (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
   - validation: Residual histogram of the polarization angle and visual comparison of observed and modeled field geometry  
  > "A Gaussian fit to the whole distribution of $\Delta\psi$ provides a mean value" (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
8. **Estimate field strength with the DCF method, mass-to-flux ratio and energy ratios**: B_pos = Q sqrt(4 pi rho) sigma_vlos / delta_psi_int; total B = B_pos / cos(i) with i = 45 deg from the model; Alfven speed and turbulent-to-magnetic energy ratio  
  > "We estimate B$_\mathrm{POS}$ towards G333 using the well-known Davis–Chandrasekhar–Fermi (DCF) method" (Magnetic field strength); "Adopting $i=45^{\circ}$ from our model, we also estimate the total magnetic field strength $B=B_\mathrm{pos}/\mathrm{cos}~(i)=5.7$ mG." (Magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: Q = 0.5; delta_psi_obs = 5.4 deg; delta_psi_int = 18.7 deg  
  > "The average uncertainty in the observed $\psi$ within the circle of analysis is $\delta\psi_{\rm obs}=5.4^{\circ}$." (Magnetic field strength); "The $\delta\psi_{int}$ obtained is $18.7^{\circ}$." (Magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Q = 0.5 from turbulent-cloud simulations; an alternative Q ~ 0.28 gives B_pos of 3.0-3.6 mG  
  > "where Q is a correction factor, 0.5,[^3] adopted from simulations in turbulent clouds (cite: 2001ApJ...546..980O)" (Magnetic field strength); "Adopting the later, the B$_{\mathrm{pos}}$ is estimated as $3.0-3.6$ mG" (Magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Gravity removed by using the residuals relative to the model, so delta_psi reflects turbulence versus magnetic field  
  > "by considering the $\Delta\psi$, we eliminate the gravitational effect" (Magnetic field strength)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency with the B-field strength versus density compilation of Liu et al. 2022; mass-to-flux ratio from the DCF angle (1.22) compared with the model value (1.43)  
  > "this strength is consistent as found in the compilation of B-field strength with density by (cite: 2022ApJ...925...30L) using the DCF method" (Magnetic field strength); "However, the estimated value of $\lambda$ is similar to the one obtained from the modelling (1.43)." (Magnetic field strength)
9. **Energy balance and virial parameter**: Virial parameter with kinetic and magnetic support for a centrally peaked density profile (rho proportional to r^-2), following Liu et al. 2020; rotational energy upper limit from the 0.56 km/s spectral resolution  
  > "We consider a spherical cloud having a radial density profile $\rho\propto r^{-\alpha}$, where $\alpha=2$ imply the density profile to be centrally peaked" (Virial parameter); "If we assume this resolution as the upper limit of the velocity due to rotation ($v_{\rm rot}$)" (Virial parameter)
   - assumption: Spherical cloud with centrally peaked density profile  
  > "We consider a spherical cloud having a radial density profile $\rho\propto r^{-\alpha}$, where $\alpha=2$ imply the density profile to be centrally peaked" (Virial parameter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rotational term found negligible and therefore dropped  
  > "As the contribution of the rotational energy is negligible compared to the other energies" (Virial parameter)
10. **Test whether MM1 and MM2 are gravitationally bound**: Compare kinetic energy E_i with gravitational potential V_i using masses from astrodendro flux densities, 3-D velocity difference and de-projected separation  
  > "we followed the methodology adopted in (cite: 2015Natur.518..213P) and (cite: 2024NatAs...8..472L)" (Stability analysis of the central protostars); "We measured the masses of MM1 and MM2 from the flux density obtained from the *astrodendro* task" (Stability analysis of the central protostars)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: Projected separation 1740 au times 4/pi = 2215 au  
  > "multiplying the measured projected separation (1740 au) with $4/\pi$, i.e., 2215 au, assuming a random orientation between them." (Stability analysis of the central protostars)
   - assumption: Measured velocity difference is 1-D, scaled to 3-D by sqrt(3)  
  > "Assuming that the measured velocity difference between MM1 and MM2 is in 1-D, the full velocity difference in 3-D is" (Stability analysis of the central protostars)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
11. **Search for outflows from MM1 and MM2 in archival 12CO (3-2)**: Regrid archival 12CO (3-2) data to the continuum phase centre with imregrid and integrate blue and red lobes  
  > "Using the CASA task *imregrid*, we match the phase-center of the" (Molecular outflows identified around G333); "to $0.7$ km s$^{-1}$ for the redshifted lobe" (Molecular outflows identified around G333)
   - software: CASA, imregrid
   - parameter: Integrated sensitivities 0.14 (blue) and 0.10 (red) Jy/beam  
  > "The sensitivities of the integrated images of the blueshifted and redshifted $^{12}$CO line emission are 0.14 and 0.10 Jy beam$^{-1}$, respectively." (Molecular outflows identified around G333)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The B_pos morphology is consistent with an hourglass, with more pinching towards the more massive MM1  
  > "The hourglass shape is found to be more pinched towards MM1 than MM2, which might be because of the larger mass of MM1 compared to MM2." (Summary and Conclusions)
- No large-scale velocity gradient in H13CO+, no rotation and no outflow from MM1/MM2, read as strong magnetic braking  
  > "No clear signature of large-scale velocity gradient is detected with a spectral resolution of 0.56 km s$^{-1}$, suggesting a quiescent environment" (Results); "none of the protostars show any clear signature of outflows, suggesting a strong magnetic braking" (Summary and Conclusions)
- Magnetic field is strong but does not prevent fragmentation  
  > "the magnetic field towards G333, in spite of being quite strong, could not prevent the fragmentation and eventually star formation" (Magnetic field strength)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G333 chosen because it shows the most extended hourglass morphology among the 30 MagMaR targets  
  > "shows the most extended hourglass morphology on a few 1000 au scale (cite: 2019A&A...631A..72L)" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Analysis circle chosen to avoid distortion of B_pos by surrounding cores  
  > "We chose this area to avoid the additional distortion of B$_{\mathrm{pos}}$ produced by other surrounding cores." (Results)
- Alternative: Using Q ~ 0.28 (Liu et al. 2021) instead of 0.5 gives B_pos of 3.0-3.6 mG, consistent with the adopted estimate  
  > "Adopting the later, the B$_{\mathrm{pos}}$ is estimated as $3.0-3.6$ mG, which is consistent with our estimated B$_{\mathrm{pos}}$." (Magnetic field strength)
- Alternative: Rotational energy term dropped from the virial parameter because it is negligible  
  > "we do not account the upper limit of rotational term in the estimation of virial parameter" (Virial parameter)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stokes I image is dynamic-range limited  
  > "making the image dynamic range limited" (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Constant 50 K temperature adopted because no temperature map is available  
  > "A constant temperature (50 K) has been adopted in the modelling as no temperature map of this region is available currently." (Modeling of the hourglass-shaped magnetic field)
- Limitation (authors): Mass may be underestimated because dust may be optically thick at the centre  
  > "The fact that the dust emission may be optically thick at the very central bright fragmented region, leads to underestimate the mass of the circular region can not be ignored." (Magnetic field strength)
- Limitation (authors): Lambda is a lower limit since protostellar mass is not included  
  > "Therefore, the estimated $\lambda$ should be considered as a lower limit." (Magnetic field strength)
- Limitation (authors): DCF method does not account for gravity  
  > "The DCF method assumes the deviation of uniform magnetic field by turbulent gas motions, without considering gravity, which is most likely responsible for the hourglass shape." (Magnetic field strength)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distance and hence all au scales, masses and densities are unspecified in the text
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Reduced chi-squared of 9.39 indicates a poor formal fit; no priors or posterior sampling, only a grid with Lampton errors
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DCF Q factor varies 0.28-0.5 and the 3.0-3.6 vs 4.0 mG spread is not propagated into B
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single temperature of 50 K for mass, sigma_th and model, taken from a region that avoids the cores
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Binary-boundness uses 1-D velocities from another paper and a random-orientation factor
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line-of-sight inclination from the model is a model-dependent quantity used to turn B_pos into B
