- Companion #1 (~10 arcsec SW of the HzRG, aligned with the radio jet) is confirmed in CO(3-2) as a single unresolved velocity component and is also detected in 100 GHz continuum and archival Spitzer IRAC 3.6 and 4.5 um images.  
  > "However, we only find one unresolved component at the position of companion #1." (CO(3–2) line emission in TXS 0828+193 and companion #1); "However, the ALMA detection of CO(3–2) line emission and 100 GHz continuum emission associated with this source strongly suggests that the IRAC counterpart of companion #1 is real." - Three additional candidate companions (#2, #3, #4) are detected in CO(3-2) at projected distances of ~170-250 kpc with no continuum or IRAC counterparts, and are interpreted as likely cold, dense gas clouds in the halo/cosmic web filaments.  
  > "In addition, we detected three new CO emitting regions in the field of TXS 0828+193 at a distance of $\sim170-250\,\rm kpc$." (Conclusions); "Since these sources are not aligned with the axis of the radio jet and are at higher distances from the HzRG than companion #1, they are likely cold, dense clouds in the environment of the central HzRG, potentially part of the large-scale filaments of the cosmic web, which provide the necessary gas for the growth of galaxies." - Why this data: ALMA CO(3-2) and 100 GHz continuum observations were obtained to reveal the origin of the luminous off-source CO emitting region discovered by IRAM PdBI and to search for additional companion galaxies in the known overdense environment around TXS 0828+193.  
  > "With the aim of revealing the origin of the luminous CO emitting region, we observed the HzRG with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), obtaining CO(3–2) and continuum measurements." (Introduction); "As HzRGs are known for residing in overdense environment, we also search for additional companion galaxies." (Introduction)
- Why this method: SOFIA was used because it is suitable for automated detection and reliability testing of sources in 3D spectral line data cubes; uv-tapering to the PdBI beam was performed to test whether extended emission in companion #1 was resolved out at ALMA's higher spatial resolution.  
  > "As the spatial resolution of our ALMA observations is higher than that of the IRAM observation, it is possible that we resolved out the CO emitting region of companion #1." (CO(3–2) line emission in TXS 0828+193 and companion #1); "For the companion search we used SOFIA, a source finder application suitable for detection and parametrisation of sources in 3D spectral line data cubes (cite: 2015MNRAS.448.1922S)." (CO(3–2) source finding)
- Alternative: Contamination of the 100 GHz continuum emission of companion #1 by synchrotron emission from the southern radio jet cannot be excluded due to its low flux density and alignment with the jet axis.  
  > "However, given the low flux density of companion #1 and its alignment with the radio axis, the contamination of the continuum emission by the southern radio jet cannot be excluded." (ALMA continuum emission)
- Alternative: Using a Milky Way / normal star-forming galaxy CO-to-H2 conversion factor (~4 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1) instead of the ULIRG value (0.8) would increase the molecular gas mass estimates of the companions by a factor of five.  
  > "We note that in the case of the companions by using a conversion factor of $\sim4\,\rm M_{\odot}\,(\rm K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{2})^{-1}$, found for molecular clouds in the Milky Way and for normal, high-$z$ star-forming galaxies, the molecular mass estimates are a factor of five higher than in Table \[tab:table1\] (cite: 2010ApJ...713..686D; 2010MNRAS.407.2091G)." (CO line luminosities and gas masses)
- Alternative: Identification of the line emission in companions #2, #3, and #4 as a transition other than CO(3-2) from galaxies at different redshifts was considered unlikely because their fitted CO redshifts are very close to that of the HzRG.  
  > "Alternatively, the detected line emission of these sources does not originate from the CO(3–2) transition but another line, thus the companions could be galaxies at different redshifts." (Off-source CO emission: real or spurious detections?); "However, given the fitted CO redshift of the companions are very close to that of the HzRG, this scenario is unlikely." (Off-source CO emission: real or spurious detections?)
- Alternative: Jet-induced gas cooling or jet-driven IGM enrichment for companions #2, #3, and #4 was ruled out because they are at larger distances from the HzRG than companion #1 and are not aligned with the radio axis.  
  > "Moreover, these sources are at greater distances from the HzRG than companion #1 and are not aligned with the radio axis, thus ruling out the possibility of jet-induced gas cooling or jet-driven enrichment of the intergalactic medium (IGM; see (cite: 2014MNRAS.438.2898E) and the references therein)." (Off-source CO emission: companion galaxies or cold gas in the halo?)

- Limitation (authors): The uv-tapered ALMA cube has worse sensitivity (~0.25 mJy beam^-1 per 72.6 km s^-1 channel) than the original IRAM PdBI observation (~0.3 mJy beam^-1 per 30 km s^-1 channel), so it cannot confirm the double-peaked CO(3-2) feature of companion #1.  
  > "As the resulted rms is worse than that of the IRAM PdBI observations ($\sim 0.3\,\rm mJy\,beam^{-1}$ per $30\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ channel), the tapered ALMA data cannot confirm the previously detected double-peak feature of the CO(3-2) emission." (CO(3–2) line emission in TXS 0828+193 and companion #1)
- Limitation (authors): Running SOFIA on the inverted cube yields one source at -5 sigma, and increasing the threshold to 5.5 sigma only detects the HzRG and companions #1 and #2, so the possibility that some of the newly detected companions are spurious cannot be completely ruled out.  
  > "This yielded one source at $-5\sigma$ threshold, which suggests that one of the three new sources could be spurious." (CO(3–2) source finding); "We note, that by increasing the threshold to $5.5\sigma$ SOFIA only detects the HzRG, companion #1 and #2." (CO(3–2) source finding); "However, the possibility of these newly found sources being spurious detections cannot be ruled out completly." (Off-source CO emission: real or spurious detections?)
- Limitation (authors): Companion #2 is very close to a bright foreground star, which impedes its detection in the Spitzer IRAC 3.6 um and 4.5 um maps.  
  > "Companion #2 is very close to a bright foreground star, which impends its detection in the $3.6\,\mu \rm m$ and $4.5\,\mu \rm m$ maps." (Multi-wavelength properties of the companion candidates)
- Limitation (authors): A more accurate stellar mass estimate for companion #1 would require higher quality multiband optical and near-IR photometry and SED fitting rather than a single-band rest-frame H-band mass-to-light ratio assumption.  
  > "However, we note that a more accurate way to estimate the stellar mass would require higher quality multiband optical and near-IR photometry and fitting its spectral energy distribution." (Multi-wavelength properties of the companion candidates)
- Limitation (authors): Molecular gas mass estimates heavily depend on the assumed CO-to-H2 conversion factor.  
  > "We note that the estimated molecular gas mass heavily depends on the conversion factor used in these studies." (Molecular gas in TXS 0828+193)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The statistical reliability of companions #3 and #4 is low: negative cube testing in SOFIA found one -5 sigma detection, companion #3 is not detected at 5.5 sigma, and companion #4 is only found visually at 3.5-4 sigma.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): None of companions #2, #3, or #4 have continuum or multi-wavelength counterparts, and they lie 21.6-30.5 arcsec from the phase centre (near the half-power radius of the ~60 arcsec ALMA Band 3 primary beam) where primary beam correction amplifies noise by ~2x.
