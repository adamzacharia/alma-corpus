- Question: How are molecular gas and dust distributed in the atmosphere and dust formation zone of the carbon AGB star IRC+10216 at one-stellar-radius resolution, and how is the material subsequently expelled

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum and line data**: Standard CASA pipeline calibration, polarisation flux correction, GILDAS self-calibration and imaging with uniform weighting  
  > "Calibration was performed with the standard pipeline of the Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA) software package" (ALMA Millimetre-wavelength interferometric observations, calibration, and reduction); "Imaging restoration was made with uniform weighting." (ALMA Millimetre-wavelength interferometric observations, calibration, and reduction)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Flux correction of 7-10 % for flux calibrator polarisation  
  > "Due to this, a later correction of 7-10 % was introduced in the data based on the polarisation knowledge of the flux calibrator" (ALMA Millimetre-wavelength interferometric observations, calibration, and reduction)
   - parameter: Hogbom and SDI clean; SDI only for lines  
  > "The SDI method was only used for line emission imaging, and its results were assessed by comparison with the more standard Hogbom ones." (ALMA Millimetre-wavelength interferometric observations, calibration, and reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SDI line images compared with Hogbom results; flux scale uncertainty <~ 5 %  
  > "The SDI method was only used for line emission imaging, and its results were assessed by comparison with the more standard Hogbom ones." (ALMA Millimetre-wavelength interferometric observations, calibration, and reduction); "make these flux values very reliable (with an uncertainty $\lesssim$ 5 %)" (ALMA Millimetre-wavelength interferometric observations, calibration, and reduction)
2. **Fit the main continuum component and measure flux and position**: Gaussian fit to the bulk of the 1.2 mm continuum emission; integrated flux in a 400 mas box; position uncertainty set to one fifth of the synthesised beam  
  > "The integrated flux, within a square box of 400 mas length centred at the fitted position, amounts to 496 $\pm$ 11 mJy" (Continuum main component fit and proper motion); "we adopted a more conservative value for the uncertainty as one fifth of the half power beamwidth (HPBW) synthesised beam" (Continuum main component fit and proper motion)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Position uncertainty = Gaussian-fit uncertainty added in quadrature with one fifth of the synthesised beam  
  > "The uncertainty of the position is the result of the addition in quadrature of the Gaussian fit uncertainty and one fifth of the synthesised beam." (Continuum main component fit and proper motion)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian fit to the continuum; fitting software and algorithm not stated  
  > "The fit to the continuum emission yields a major axis full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 56.0 $\pm$ 2.7 mas and a minor axis FWHM of 49.9 $\pm$ 2.3 mas" (Continuum main component fit and proper motion)
   - validation: Flux compared with previous measurements at similar wavelengths; position compared with VLA position of Menten et al.  
  > "This flux compares well, considering the source variability, with previous measurements at similar wavelengths" (Continuum main component fit and proper motion)
3. **Identify continuum extensions beyond the central source**: Inspect the continuum map and residuals after the central source for resolved extensions above 10 sigma, then measure offsets of the horseshoe structure and two outer clumps  
  > "shows resolved extensions (above 10 $\sigma$) that depart from spherical symmetry in addition to a bright central source" (main text); "A horseshoe shaped structure is seen approximately 25 mas from the centre of the bright central source" (main text)
   - assumption: Distance 123 +/- 14 pc used to convert to physical scales  
  > "the distance to IRC + 10$^{\circ}$216 has been measured as 123 $\pm$ 14 pc" (Continuum extended emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with previous infrared and optical clump detections in the literature  
  > "We discuss here previous observations of continuum extensions at different infrared (IR) and optical wavelengths connected to the NE and NW extensions seen in our continuum map" (Continuum extended emission)
4. **Map continuum-subtracted line emission of SiC2, SiS and HCN**: Continuum-subtracted channel/moment maps near the star; inspect symmetry, voids, arcs and clumps  
  > "The line emission arising near the star, which involves upper level energies up to some 6500 K, clearly deviates from the circular symmetry we would expect in an isotropic formation scenario." (main text)
   - software: GILDAS
   - assumption: The central void is due to absorption by foreground gas, not absence of gas  
  > "The void is not due to the absence of gas in front of the star but to absorption by foreground gas" (main text)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare HCN and SiS line intensity ratio versus position angle**: Build the HCN nu3=1 J=3-2 / SiS v=3 J=14-13 intensity ratio distribution and examine variation with angle at fixed radius; lines chosen for similar upper level energies  
  > "we have compared the intensity ratio distribution between the SiS $v$ = 3 $J$ = 14–13 and HCN $\nu_\mathrm{3}$ = 1 $J$ = 3–2 lines" (main text); "Both lines have similar upper level energies (3281 and 3042 K for SiS and HCN lines, respectively)." (main text)
   - software: GILDAS
   - assumption: Density fluctuations alone would change HCN and SiS abundances equally, so an angle-dependent ratio implies temperature anisotropy  
  > "in which case the fractional abundances of both HCN and SiS would change equally and the ratio would not vary with angle" (main text)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared against chemical equilibrium predictions (step 6)  
  > "Such behaviour is predicted by chemical equilibrium models" (main text)
6. **Chemical equilibrium modelling of abundance sensitivity to temperature**: Minimise the Gibbs free energy over a pressure-temperature grid for AGB-atmosphere conditions with 919 gaseous species  
  > "Chemical equilibrium is solved by minimisation of the Gibbs free energy of the system." (Chemical model); "We compute the chemical equilibrium composition of gas over a pressure-temperature parameter space relevant for the conditions of an AGB atmosphere (5 $\times$10$^{-4}\,$– 5 mbar and 2000–3000 K)." (Chemical model)
   - software: Chemical equilibrium code of Agúndez et al. 2020
   - parameter: 919 gaseous species; elemental composition typical of C-rich AGB stars  
  > "We consider 919 gaseous species, including atoms and molecules, with thermochemical data taken from different compilations, and an elemental composition typical of C-rich AGB stars" (Chemical model)
   - assumption: Chemical equilibrium (no kinetics or shock chemistry)  
  > "The chemical model is based on the code presented in Agúndez et al" (Chemical model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test the shock scenario with radiative transfer of a pulsating envelope**: MADEX molecular emission for a time-dependent envelope model with pulsation, compared with observed line profiles along strips through the atmosphere  
  > "We calculated the molecular emission of IRC + 10$^{\circ}$216 with the aid of the radiation transfer code MADEX" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity); "lack of any supporting evidence of shocks in any of the species analysed" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - software: MADEX
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted narrow features at specific velocities compared with observed profiles; authors state shocks cannot be ruled out  
  > "Nevertheless, we cannot rule out the existence of shocks that are not seen in our data due to effects such as excitation, gas turbulence, and opacity effects" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
8. **Model isolated, binary and random-blob envelopes and compare with the maps**: Evolving-envelope model with a modulated spherical wind plus randomly ejected spherical caps; LTE radiative transfer of CO J=2-1; maps convolved with a Gaussian beam; three cases: control, binary companion, binary plus blobs  
  > "The envelope is formed by a modulated ejection of matter in a spherical wind, which simulates the pulsation-driven outflow in AGB stars, that is the steady-state wind." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity); "Finally, the produced maps were convolved with a Gaussian beam to simulate the constraints imposed by interferometers to the observations." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - software: Evolving envelope model code (Cernicharo, Fonfria), simalma
   - parameter: Caps: random positions, formation probability typically lower than 10-30 %, angular width 60 deg  
  > "These caps were centred at random positions across the photosphere with a formation rate probability typically lower than 10–30%, and a typical angular width of 60$^{\circ}$." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - parameter: Each cap lasts about 2.5 months; one cap at a time  
  > "The formation of each cap lasts about 2.5 months and only one cap is formed at a time." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - parameter: Pulsation contrast up to 100 and periods up to 3 years tested  
  > "Different contrasts between minima and maxima in the pulsation stage, up to 100, and different periods, up to 3 years, were tested out." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - parameter: Binary case: 60 deg inclination, 25 AU separation, 4 Msun AGB, 1.1 Msun companion; blob case shortened to 10 AU  
  > "that is an inclination of 60$^{\circ}$, a separation of 25 AU and masses of 4 $M_{\odot}$ for the AGB and 1.1 $M_{\odot}$ for the companion" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity); "we have shortened the distance between the stars to 10 AU" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - assumption: Caps are temporary enhancements of wind density at the photosphere; all other physical and chemical quantities held constant  
  > "These spherical caps were modelled as temporary enhancements of the wind density at the photosphere, keeping constant any other physical or chemical quantity for the sake of simplicity." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - assumption: LTE for the CO J=2-1 line  
  > "the radiation transfer problem is solved for the CO $J$ = 2–1 line assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) to simplify calculations" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - fitting: No fit; the model aims at qualitative comparison with the maps  
  > "We note that the code aims to give qualitative information about the formation of structures in the envelope." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
   - validation: simalma check that the array configuration does not significantly change the region of the envelope explored  
  > "We checked with the CASA modelling tool simalma that the array configuration does not significantly modify the region of the envelope explored in the current work." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)

  > "As concerns the molecular gas, the different molecular species that form show varied and strikingly asymmetric distributions." (main text)

  > "After all this analysis we can conclude that the anisotropic features only appear if the ejection of blobs is included in the models." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Spatial resolution of previous data was insufficient to see molecular gas distribution in the atmosphere and dust formation zone  
  > "Because of the lack of sufficient spatial resolution, however, the distribution of molecular gas in the stellar atmosphere and the dust formation zone of AGB carbon stars is not known, nor is how it is subsequently expelled." (abstract)
- Why this method: Chemical equilibrium models predict enhanced abundance sensitivity to temperature at the photosphere, whereas shock chemistry models are uncertain  
  > "We note that chemical equilibrium models do effectively predict an enhanced sensitivity of the chemical composition to the temperature at the photosphere" (main text)
- Alternative: Binary companion and atmospheric shocks as alternative explanations of the asymmetries; considered and judged insufficient (shocks not ruled out)  
  > "Alternative scenarios considered to explain the asymmetries observed in IRC + 10$^{\circ}$216 are the effect of a binary companion and shocks in the atmosphere of the star." (main text)
- Alternative: Photochemistry from a close companion's UV emission rejected because several emission voids surround the star  
  > "cannot explain the asymmetries we observe at atmospheric level in the brightness distribution of SiS or HCN either, since several emission voids surround the star" (main text)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Shocks cannot be ruled out; they may be hidden by excitation, turbulence, opacity or insufficient resolution  
  > "Nevertheless, we cannot rule out the existence of shocks that are not seen in our data due to effects such as excitation, gas turbulence, and opacity effects" (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
- Limitation (authors): Blob model is qualitative only  
  > "We note that the code aims to give qualitative information about the formation of structures in the envelope." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity); "More accurate studies are out of the scope of the current work." (Shocks, outflows, and binarity)
- Limitation (authors): Mismatch of the measured position with the expected proper motion is not investigated  
  > "A deeper investigation on this issue is out of the scope of this paper" (Continuum main component fit and proper motion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The convective-cell interpretation rests on qualitative arguments; no quantitative model of HCN/SiS ratio versus angle is fitted to the data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Chemical equilibrium may not apply in a dynamic, shocked atmosphere, so abundance sensitivity to temperature is model dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Self-calibration, cleaning and masking choices are not given, so the clump significance and the structure of the 25 mas horseshoe cannot be checked
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Central absorption and optical depth are not modelled, which can imprint asymmetric structure on the line maps
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Blob models use CO J=2-1 in LTE, not the lines actually mapped (HCN, SiS, SiC2)
