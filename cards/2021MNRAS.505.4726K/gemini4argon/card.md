## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce VLA and ALMA radio/sub-mm observations and measure core fluxes**: Calibrate data with CASA / CASA pipeline, apply phase-only and amplitude+phase self-calibration, image with CLEAN (Briggs robust=0.5, nterms=2), and fit core flux with Gaussian fit in CASA.  
  > "We then produced images using the CLEAN algorithm with Briggs weighting, robust parameter of 0.5 and `nterms=2`. Imaging was improved by applying several rounds of phase-only self-calibration and a single round of amplitude and phase self-calibration to the measurement set. We then measured the core flux and beam size using the Gaussian fit feature in CASA." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5, nterms = 2  
  > "We then produced images using the CLEAN algorithm with Briggs weighting, robust parameter of 0.5 and `nterms=2`." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit feature in CASA  
  > "We then measured the core flux and beam size using the Gaussian fit feature in CASA." (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure optical nuclear jet fluxes in archival HST images of radio galaxies**: Mask the core region by hand, fit the host galaxy with a 2D Sersic model using astropy, subtract the model from the unmasked image, and measure excess core emission.  
  > "For each image, the host galaxy was fit with a 2D Sérsic model after masking the core region (selected by hand). The best-fit Sérsic model was then subtracted from the unmasked image and the core emission was measured, where we assume that the excess flux in the central core region is attributed to the jet." (Hubble Space Telescope Observations)
   - software: Python, astropy
   - assumption: Excess flux in the central core region after host subtraction is attributed to the jet  
  > "The best-fit Sérsic model was then subtracted from the unmasked image and the core emission was measured, where we assume that the excess flux in the central core region is attributed to the jet." (Hubble Space Telescope Observations)
   - fitting: 2D Sersic model fit  
  > "For each image, the host galaxy was fit with a 2D Sérsic model after masking the core region (selected by hand)." (Hubble Space Telescope Observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Decompose low-frequency radio spectra to measure extended 300-MHz luminosity, 1.4-GHz core luminosity, core dominance, and crossing frequency**: Compare single and added power-law fits to data below 10^13 Hz using a chi-squared test (2 dof, significance > 0.95), or fit/extrapolate direct extended flux measurements from radio maps to 300 MHz.  
  > "For each source, we initially took all data below $10^{13}$ Hz and compared single and added power-law fits to the data. As these are nested models, with the added power law having two additional degrees of freedom (the normalization and index of the second power law), a $\chi^2$ test with 2 degrees of freedom was used." (Jet Power); "The 300-MHz flux can then be estimated either through a fit to two or more extended flux measurements or by extrapolating from a single point to 300 MHz with a spectral index of 0.7 (e.g., M11)." (Jet Power)
   - parameter: Maximum frequency 10^13 Hz, chi-squared significance level alpha > 0.95  
  > "For each source, we initially took all data below $10^{13}$ Hz and compared single and added power-law fits to the data." (Jet Power); "We accepted the added power law for a significance level $\alpha > 0.95$, indicating that both components were reliably detected and measured." (Jet Power)
   - assumption: Lobe spectral index alpha = 0.7 for extrapolating single extended flux points or upper limits to 300 MHz; minimal contribution from extended jets or hotspots to L_ext  
  > "In this analysis we have assumed that contributions from extended jets or hotspots to $L_\textrm{ext}$ are minimal; this has been verified through careful checking of several dozen sources with these features." (Jet Power); "In sources where the extended radio emission was not detected, an upper limit on $L_\textrm{ext}$ was estimated by taking the lowest frequency measurement for the core and extrapolating that to 300 MHz, assuming a lobe spectral index of $\alpha = 0.7$." (Jet Power)
   - fitting: Single and added power-law fits compared via chi-squared test with 2 degrees of freedom  
  > "As these are nested models, with the added power law having two additional degrees of freedom (the normalization and index of the second power law), a $\chi^2$ test with 2 degrees of freedom was used." (Jet Power)
   - validation: Careful checking of several dozen sources with extended jets or hotspots  
  > "In this analysis we have assumed that contributions from extended jets or hotspots to $L_\textrm{ext}$ are minimal; this has been verified through careful checking of several dozen sources with these features." (Jet Power)
4. **Fit broadband jet SEDs to determine synchrotron peak frequency and peak luminosity**: Fit synchrotron SED above crossing frequency with the M11 parametric model (or power-law with exponential cutoff / two-sided parabolic fit for high-peaking sources), adding a thermal disk component if a big blue bump is visible; use a multivariate KDE and L_core-L_peak relation for sparse radio galaxies.  
  > "For sources identified as blazars (or otherwise clearly dominated by the non-thermal jet), the synchrotron portion of the spectrum was fit with the parametric SED model from M11 in order to estimate $\nu_\textrm{peak}$ and $L_\textrm{peak}$." (SED Fitting); "In cases with a high-peaking synchrotron spectrum ($\nu_\textrm{peak} > 10^{15}$ Hz), the SED was sometimes better represented with either a power law model with an exponential cutoff, shown in panel (c) of Figure [2](#sed_fig), or a two-sided parabolic fit." (SED Fitting); "In sources identified as radio galaxies where there was insufficient data density to fit the SED with the parametric model but published nuclear fluxes were available in the radio, optical/IR, and X-ray, we used a multivariate kernel density estimator (KDE) to estimate the peak frequency, as described in M11." (SED Fitting)
   - assumption: Standard Lambda-cosmology with Omega_M = 0.308, Omega_Lambda = 0.692, H_0 = 67.8 km s^-1 Mpc^-1; assumed z = 0.3 for sources without redshift  
  > "In this paper we have adopted a standard $\Lambda$-cosmology, with $\Omega_{M} = 0.308$, $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.692$, and $H_{0} = 67.8$ km s^-1^ Mpc^-1^(cite: planck16)." (Introduction); "In the TEX sample 103 sources (169 in the UEX sample) have no redshift information available on SIMBAD/NED. In these cases a value of $z=0.3$ is assumed ($\approx 10\%$ of the TEX sample, 16% of the UEX sample)." (The TEX and UEX Jet Samples)
   - fitting: Parametric SED model from M11, power law with exponential cutoff, two-sided parabolic fit, or multivariate KDE  
  > "For sources identified as blazars (or otherwise clearly dominated by the non-thermal jet), the synchrotron portion of the spectrum was fit with the parametric SED model from M11 in order to estimate $\nu_\textrm{peak}$ and $L_\textrm{peak}$." (SED Fitting)
   - validation: Visual assessment of all SEDs for goodness of fit without a priori knowledge of source identity or type  
  > "All SEDs were assessed visually for goodness of fit, without any a priori knowledge about their identity or type, simply based on being well-fit with an appropriate amount of spectral coverage, following the same procedure as in M11." (The TEX and UEX Jet Samples)
5. **Analyze jet population distributions, orientation indicators, jet speeds, and accretion efficiency in the peak frequency - peak luminosity plane**: Classify sources into type I/weak and type II/strong zones, compute Orientation Indicator (OI) from L_core and R_c, compute kinetic jet power L_kin and Eddington ratio L_kin/L_edd using compiled black hole masses, and compare distributions and theoretical de-beaming tracks.  
  > "The equation giving the OI value (before truncation) is $$\mathrm{OI} = 0.077 L_\mathrm{core} + 0.201 R_c - 2.615$$" (Orientation); "Here we use the Eddington ratio, log $L_\textrm{kin}/L_\textrm{edd}$, as a proxy for the accretion efficiency, where $L_\textrm{kin}$ is calculated from $L_\mathrm{ext}$ using the scaling presented in (cite: cavagnolo10): $$\log L_\mathrm{kin} = 0.64 (\log L_\mathrm{ext} - 40) + 43.54 \mathrm{ (ergs/s).}$$" (Accretion Mode in type I and II Jets)
   - parameter: Strong/type II zone: L_peak > 10^45 erg/s (or 10^45.5 erg/s) and nu_peak < 10^15 Hz; Weak/type I zone: nu_peak > 10^15 Hz  
  > "We propose to call these "type II" jets (quivalent to the "strong" jets of M11), and define the ‘strong zone’ as that bounded by $L_\mathrm{peak} > 10^{45}$ erg/s and $\nu_\mathrm{peak}< 10^{15}$ Hz." (Jet Power and the synchrotron $\nu_\mathrm{peak}-L_\mathrm{peak}$ plane); "As before, we identified sources as type II/strong ($L_\textrm{peak} > 10^{45.5}$ erg/s and $\nu_\textrm{peak} < 10^{15}$ Hz) and type I/weak ($\nu_\textrm{peak} > 10^{15}$ Hz)." (Accretion Mode in type I and II Jets)
   - assumption: Cavagnolo et al. (2010) scaling between L_ext and L_kin  
  > "Here we use the Eddington ratio, log $L_\textrm{kin}/L_\textrm{edd}$, as a proxy for the accretion efficiency, where $L_\textrm{kin}$ is calculated from $L_\mathrm{ext}$ using the scaling presented in (cite: cavagnolo10):" (Accretion Mode in type I and II Jets)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits to nu_peak histograms in L_ext bins; Kendall's tau correlation test between black hole mass and L_ext  
  > "As shown in Figure 2, we see that type II jets have a remarkably consistent distribution of synchroton peak frequencies over 4 orders of magnitude in jet power, as every bin has an average value of approximately $\nu_\mathrm{peak} = 10^{13.3}$ Hz (the average values and errors are obtained from a Gaussian fit, with exact values noted in the figure)." (The End of the Blazar Sequence); "Kendall’s $\tau$=0.13 with a p-value of 0.04" (Results and Discussion, Accretion Mode in type I and II Jets)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- There is little evidence for the blazar sequence anti-correlation; the 'forbidden zone' at high peak frequency and high peak luminosity is populated by highly aligned type I BL Lacs.  
  > "Here, based on the largest-ever sample of over 2000 well-sampled jet spectral energy distributions, we examine the synchrotron peak frequency – peak luminosity plane, and find little evidence for the anti-correlation known as the blazar sequence." (Abstract); "We find that there is no ‘forbidden zone’ at high-$\nu_\mathrm{peak}$ and high $L_\mathrm{peak}$, but rather that this area of the plane is populated by type I ("weak" or inefficiently accreting) BL Lacs." (Conclusions)
- Jetted AGN exhibit a dichotomy between type II (strong, efficient accretion) and type I (weak, inefficient accretion) jets, which is not a simple division in jet power.  
  > "Instead, we find strong evidence for a dichotomy in jets, between those associated with efficient or ‘quasar-mode’ accretion (strong/type II jets) and those associated with inefficient accretion (weak/type I jets)." (Abstract); "We have derived estimates of the total jet power for over 1000 of our sources from low-frequency radio observations, and find that the jet dichotomy does *not* correspond to a division in jet power." (Abstract)
- Low-synchrotron-peak BL Lacs (LBLs) in the strong zone have extended radio power distributions, big blue bump signatures, and hotspots consistent with FSRQs and should be classified as type II jets.  
  > "It is clear that the mean and distribution of $L_\mathrm{ext}$ for strong-zone BLLs is far more similar to FSRQ than to the high-$\nu_\mathrm{peak}$, weak-zone BLLs. Given these observations, as well as the fact that many LBLs have extended radio powers well above what are observed for FR I radio galaxies (cite: rector01), it seems that LBLs should be classified as type II jets along with FSRQ." (LBLs are type II Jets)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Collecting the largest possible sample of well-characterized jet SEDs (rather than small statistically complete samples) is best suited to revealing full population characteristics and determining which parts of parameter space are filled or empty.  
  > "We have opted for this approach because it is better suited to revealing the characteristics of the full population, and because looking at the largest possible sample is the best way to answer questions of which parts of the parameter space are filled or empty." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Low-frequency (300 MHz) extended radio luminosity (L_ext) is isotropic and correlates with lifetime-integrated jet power, avoiding the Doppler boosting degeneracies that affect radio core luminosity, and can be obtained for over a thousand sources.  
  > "In comparison, the radio core luminosity is not a good indicator of the true jet power, as it is enhanced by orders of magnitude from the intrinsic value due to Doppler boosting, and in general there is a strong degeneracy between angle, speed, and the intrinsic luminosity." (Jet Power); "However, it is the only estimate of jet power which we can easily obtain for a large sample (over a thousand sources as noted below), and with appropriate caution it should be suitable for broad characterization of large samples." (Jet Power)
- Alternative: Using only statistically complete samples was rejected because such samples are small and almost none have well-constrained broadband SEDs for the entire sample.  
  > "While there are advantages to using only statistically complete samples, there are also major disadvantages – namely, that such samples are small, and almost none have well-constrained broad-band SEDs for the entire sample as we require here." (Introduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Extended radio emission (L_ext) has considerable dispersion around jet power scaling relations and is not reliable for individual sources.  
  > "The extended radio emission is not a perfect measure of jet power, as there is considerable dispersion around the scaling relations referenced above, and it is almost certainly not reliable for individual sources (cite: 2013MNRAS.430..174H)." (Jet Power)
- Limitation (authors): About 10% of the TEX sample (103 sources) and 16% of the UEX sample (169 sources) lack redshift measurements and are assigned z=0.3 (though excluded from luminosity plots).  
  > "In the TEX sample 103 sources (169 in the UEX sample) have no redshift information available on SIMBAD/NED. In these cases a value of $z=0.3$ is assumed ($\approx 10\%$ of the TEX sample, 16% of the UEX sample)." (The TEX and UEX Jet Samples)
- Limitation (authors): The Orientation Indicator (OI) definition is not rigorous, could be affected by selection effects, and is meant to be illustrative rather than conclusive.  
  > "We also note that our definition of OI is far from rigorous and could be affected by selection effects, and the use of it here is meant to be more illustrative than conclusive." (Orientation)
- Limitation (authors): Small number of weak-zone sources with measured black hole masses and L_ext detections (as opposed to upper limits) gives low statistical power to detect an L_ext - M_BH correlation.  
  > "but the small number of measurements compared to upper limits gives us low statistical power to detect a correlation." (Results and Discussion, Accretion Mode in type I and II Jets)
- Limitation (authors): Misaligned jets (radio galaxies) and low-power jets with well-sampled jet SEDs remain rare, and excitation index was not explicitly accounted for in radio galaxy classifications.  
  > "While we have included as many misaligned jets (i.e., radio galaxies) as possible, those with well-sampled jet SEDs remain rare, as is also the case for low-power jets like those increasingly found in LLAGN (some of which are formally radio-quiet)." (Conclusions); "Such a study should also explicitly take into account the excitation index." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sample of 2124 sources is a heterogeneous compilation from 23 catalogs with visual SED selection, introducing complex, unmodeled selection biases.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Multi-wavelength SED data compiled from NED and various catalogs are non-simultaneous, which can significantly affect peak frequency and luminosity fits for highly variable blazars.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual inspection and hand-selection were used to filter outliers, add Big Blue Bump components, and reject poorly sampled SEDs, making the exact sample selection subjective.
