## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and register NaCo frames**: Flat-field, correct bad pixels, remove sky, extract subframes, recenter with Moffat fits, and reject bad frames.  
  > "As a first step, data reduction (flat-fielding, bad pixels, and sky removal) was performed on all the available cubes of each target." (Data processing); "These frames were then re-centered following a Moffat fitting on the non-saturated part of the stellar PSF wing." (Data processing); "The last step was to remove the bad frames (poorly saturated, overly extended PSF)" (Data processing)
   - software: IPAG-ADI pipeline
   - parameter: 128 × 128 pixels; 3.5" × 3.5" FoV.  
  > "Sub-frames of 128 $\times$ 128 pixels (FoV of $3.5"\times3.5"$) were extracted to reduce computation time." (Data processing)
   - fitting: Moffat fitting on the non-saturated stellar PSF wing.  
  > "These frames were then re-centered following a Moffat fitting on the non-saturated part of the stellar PSF wing." (Data processing)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Subtract the stellar PSF**: Apply cADI, sADI, rADI, LOCI, PCA and Angular Differential Optimal Exoplanet Detection Algorithm; PCA-based RDI for the Elias 24 second epoch.  
  > "namely classical ADI (cADI), smart ADI (sADI), radial ADI (rADI), all described in (cite: 2012A&A...542A..41C)" (Data processing); "as well as Locally Optimized Combination of Images (cite: 2007ApJ...660..770L), Principal Component Analysis (cite: 2012ApJ...755L..28S) and Angular Differential Optimal Exoplanet Detection Algorithm (cite: 2015A&A...582A..89C)." (Data processing); "For the RDI processing of Elias 24, data reduction was carried out using the PCA method as a reference used to model and substract the PSF from the images of Elias 24." (Data processing)
   - software: IPAG-ADI pipeline, cADI, sADI, rADI, Locally Optimized Combination of Images, Principal Component Analysis, Angular Differential Optimal Exoplanet Detection Algorithm
   - parameter: sADI/LOCI: FWHM=4.5 pixels; separation criterion 0.75 × FWHM.  
  > "For sADI and LOCI, we followed a similar configuration as (cite: 2012A&A...542A..41C), with a $FWHM=4.5$ pixels and a separation criteria of $0.75 \times FWHM$ at the companion separation." (Data processing)
   - parameter: PCA: k=1,5,20; optimization areas 5, 10, 40 and 60 pixels; no separation criterion.  
  > "For the PCA method, observations were reduced using three different number of modes ($k=1,5,20$) with optimization areas of 5, 10, 40 and 60 pixels." (Data processing); "No separation criteria was applied in this case." (Data processing)
   - assumption: RDI reference-star PSF models the Elias 24 PSF.  
  > "This allows us to use the PSF of the reference star as a model for the PSF of Elias 24 during the RDI reduction process." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare results across ADI algorithms.  
  > "We note these cADI images are consistent with the resulting images from all the reduction algorithms discussed above." (Results)
3. **Measure contrast limits**: Estimate local noise; inject and recover fake planets to correct throughput; normalize to the primary; correct small-number statistics.  
  > "Pixel-to-pixel noise maps of each ADI reduction were estimated within a sliding box of $1.5\times1.5$ *FWHM* in the NaCo field of view." (Detection Limits); "The final $5\sigma$ contrast maps were obtained using the pixel-to-pixel noise maps divided by the flux loss and normalized by the relative calibration with the primary star" (Detection Limits); "corrected from small number statistics following the prescription of (cite: 2014ApJ...792...97M) to adapt our $5\sigma$ confidence level at small angles." (Detection Limits)
   - software: IPAG-ADI pipeline
   - parameter: Fake planets every 10 pixels at 3 position angles; 100 ADU.  
  > "fake planets were regularly injected (every 10 pixels at 3 different position angles, with a flux corresponding to 100 ADU) on the original data cubes before PSF subtraction." (Detection Limits)
   - parameter: Azimuthal average of throughput losses at each radius.  
  > "by computing the azimuthal average of the flux losses for fake planets at the same radii(cite: 2010A&A...509A..52C; 2013A&A...553A..60R)." (Detection Limits)

## Qualitative findings

- Only RU Lup and Elias 24 exhibit point-like signals.  
  > "Of the ten analysed systems, only RU Lup and Elias 24 exhibit a point like signal in our ADI reduction." (Point source detections)
- RU Lup source is not comoving; its motion is more consistent with a background object.  
  > "indicate that the object is unambiguously not comoving with the primary star, with its astrometry being more consistent with that of a stationary background object." (RU Lup)
- Elias 24 candidate lies in a disk gap; second-epoch RDI does not confirm it.  
  > "This result locates the source within one of the observed gaps of the disk" (Elias 24); "In this case the previously reported signal was not recovered." (Elias 24); "Based on this, further follow-up is mandatory to clarify the status of this faint planet candidate." (Elias 24)
- Planetary origin of the observed disk structures remains unconfirmed.  
  > "Given our results, it is still too early to properly conclude if the observed structures on the disks are caused by the presence of planetary companions." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: DSHARP disks have ubiquitous annular substructures and young ages.  
  > "the ubiquity of annular substructures in the objects from the DSHARP sample (cite: 2018ApJ...869L..42H), and the relatively young age of the systems ($\sim1$ Myr on average)" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Direct imaging independently tests model-based companion interpretations; multiple ADI algorithms check consistency.  
  > "One solution for this problem is to directly observe and characterize the possible companions, to compare with the planetary and disks properties derived from these models." (Introduction); "The use of multiple ADI techniques allows for comparison and consistency on the results obtained." (Data processing)
- Alternative: Elias 24 signal could be a processing artifact, bound planet or background object.  
  > "We advocate future observations with JWST, ERIS to clarify its nature (artefact, bound planet or background object)." (Elias 24 second epoch observations)
- Alternative: CPD emission can alter the luminosity-based interpretation of the Elias 24 candidate.  
  > "Assuming it is a planetary companion, our analysis suggest a mass ranging from $0.5 M_J$ up to $5 M_J$, depending on the presence of a circumplanetary disk and its contribution to the luminosity of the system." (Abstract)
- Alternative: Gravitational instabilities are an alternative origin for WaOph 6 spiral arms.  
  > "other alternatives should be considered such as formation due to gravitational instabilities." (Planet-disk interaction: comparison with models)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ADI excludes the innermost disk regions.  
  > "In the majority of cases, the ADI reduction was limited to separations $\ge0.1-0.2"$, excluding the possibility of detections in the innermost regions of the systems (below $20-40$ AU)." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Elias 24 lies near residual artifacts and the RDI inner limit; the second epoch is inconclusive.  
  > "our ADI observations starts degrading at close separations in the inner region of the disk, as evidenced by the presence of artifacts that can be observed at separations similar to the alleged signal." (Elias 24); "the gap is located very close to the inner limit of the region that can be resolved with RDI" (Elias 24); "showing that the second epoch is not conclusive." (Elias 24)
- Limitation (authors): Detection probabilities omit possible CPD emission.  
  > "the derived detection probabilities for all the disks in the sample do not take into account the possible presence of a circumplanetary disk (CPD), which might alter these values, although to an unknown extent." (Planet-disk interaction: comparison with models)
- Limitation (authors): Disk extinction is omitted and may make sensitivity estimates optimistic.  
  > "the contrast values, and subsequently the detection probability maps, derived from our observations might be optimistic estimates, since we are not considering the effects of the disk material on the planets infrared emission." (Extinction from protoplanetary disk material)
- Limitation (authors): Direct-imaging mass estimates depend on uncalibrated formation-dependent luminosity relations.  
  > "the planet mass estimation is only accessible through the use of uncalibrated mass-luminosity relation that depends on the initial conditions of formation (cite: 2007ApJ...655..541M)." (Introduction)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The discussion and CPD table give an accretion-rate range of 1.2×10^-7–1.5×10^-5 M_J yr^-1, while the conclusions give 2×10^-8–1.5×10^-6; these cannot serve as one consistent answer key.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abstract/conclusions describe a CPD-dependent lower planet mass of 0.5 M_J, whereas the detailed calculation adopts 0.4 M_J.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Detection probabilities of roughly 50–70% do not establish definitive exclusions from individual non-detections.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Agreement with undetectably low model masses provides limited discrimination among planet and nonplanet explanations of disk gaps.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): RU Lup is classified as background, but the detailed discussion also notes a deviation from the stationary-background prediction.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA calibration and imaging cannot be reconstructed from this paper alone; its ALMA contribution is inherited comparison material.
