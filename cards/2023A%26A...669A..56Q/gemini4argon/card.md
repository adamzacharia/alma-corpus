## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify molecular transitions in the spectral windows**: Line identification across the four spectral windows using the MADEX spectroscopic catalog  
  > "The ALMA data covered a large variety of species (Table 1). The different species were identified using MADEX (cite: MADEX)." (Structure of the ejecta)
   - software: MADEX
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract position-velocity diagrams and fit expansion velocity law of localized outflows**: Construct position-velocity (PV) diagrams of NaCl v=0,1 and H2S emission along the main axes connecting VY to structures A and C, and fit a linear (Hubble-like) expansion velocity law V_exp proportional to r  
  > "We obtained position–velocity (PV) diagrams of these line emissions along the main axis of the two structures connecting them with the central star $VY$." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta); "In the case of NaCl and H$_2$S these diagrams showed that these outflows present a straight-line shape; in other words, they follow a Hubble-like expansion velocity field ($V_{exp} \propto r$; see Fig. 3)." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Inclination of outflow C assumed <= 15 deg  
  > "We thus can assume that the inclination of *C* is equal to or lower than that of blob *D*, which was $\sim 15^\circ$ (cite: Kaminski19)." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
   - assumption: Hubble-like linear velocity field v proportional to r  
  > "Assuming this inclination for the *C* jet, we found we could fit the expansion velocity to $v = 17 \times r/0\mbox{\rlap{.}$''$}1\, \mbox{km~s$^{-1}$}$." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
   - fitting: Fit expansion velocity law and systemic velocity V_LSR along PV diagrams accounting for inclination-velocity degeneracy  
  > "In order to fit the velocity field of these outflows, we took into account a twofold degeneracy. There is a series of pairs of values of the jet inclination with respect to the plane of the sky ($\theta$) and the value of the velocity constant ($V_0$), which would fit the velocity field of the outflow." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
   - validation: Compatibility with TiO2 line profile asymmetries and 3D back-tracing of outflow axes to the central star position  
  > "This is compatible with the asymmetries observed by de (cite: debeck15) for the TiO$_2$ line profiles." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta); "Tracing back the trajectory of the different outflows can be used to confirm the real position of the central star (see Fig. 4)." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
3. **Reconstruct 3D spatial structure of the fast outflows and determine stellar position and V_LSR**: Convert spectral cubes (R.A., Dec., velocity) of H2S, NaCl, and o-H2O into 3D spatial coordinates by cropping below 3 sigma and transforming velocity into line-of-sight distance using the fitted Hubble-like velocity law  
  > "Due to the complex structure of the ejecta we generated 3D structures imposing the expansion velocity field obtained from the PV diagrams using the `Astructures code`." (Three-dimensional reconstruction); "Briefly, `Astructures` reads the data from a spectral cube, estimates the Gaussian noise from the cube, and crops out the data below a given threshold (i.e., 3$\sigma)$." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
   - software: Astructures
   - parameter: Noise threshold = 3 sigma  
  > "Briefly, `Astructures` reads the data from a spectral cube, estimates the Gaussian noise from the cube, and crops out the data below a given threshold (i.e., 3$\sigma)$." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
   - assumption: Same Hubble-like velocity field applies to all fast outflows and inner o-H2O outflows  
  > "In order to obtain a first approach to the structure, we assumed a velocity field similar to that for the outer outflows." (Inner ejecta)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested higher exponential velocity orders to verify that outflow flatness is preserved; compared V_LSR = 12 km/s vs 22 km/s and maser vs continuum central position  
  > "In addition, we have modified the velocity law from constant expansion to higher exponential orders, and this flatness is maintained." (Sporadic outflows); "In the 3D videos labeled “lines” we plotted the axis and the two positions for the central source as a filled star corresponding to a $V_\mathrm{LSR} = 12\mbox{km~s$^{-1}$}$ and a filled circle for $V_\mathrm{LSR} = 22\mbox{km~s$^{-1}$}$." (Position and systemic velocity of the star)
4. **Compare fast outflows with cavities in the extended regular ejecta**: Overplot velocity-shifted H2S emission onto SO channel maps to test spatial correspondence between fast jets and SO shell cavities  
  > "In order to inspect the relation of the fast outflows with the bubbles observed in SO, we plotted the H$_2$S emission (overplotted the SO channel maps), for which we modified the velocities of H$_2$S so the shells and the outflows coincide (see Fig. 7, where the velocity of both emissions are shown)." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate linear momentum and required magnetic field for CME-like ejection of clump C**: Calculate linear momentum of clump C from adopted mass and Hubble velocity law, and use CME momentum transfer rate scaling from Welsch (2018) to derive required surface magnetic field strength  
  > "The total momentum driven by this ejection assuming the Hubble velocity field deduced above and a constant mass distribution along the ejecta is $P = 9.30 \times 10^{38}$ g cm s$^{-1}$." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.); "To estimate the momentum driven by a CME, we followed the discussion presented by (cite: Welsch18)." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
   - parameter: Outflow C total mass = 0.05 M_sun; cold spot area = 2.8 x 10^23 cm^2; formation time = 50 yr  
  > "The total mass of the outflow is therefore 0.05$M_{\odot}$." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.); "The size of the cold spots in the RSGs has been found (cite: kunstler15) to reach sizes of $2.8 \times10^{23}$ cm$^2$, and the time estimated for the formation of the *C*-outflow is $\sim$50 years." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio = 200; constant mass distribution along the ejecta; momentum transfer rate of 1 g cm s^-1 per cm^2 per s for B = 3 G proportional to B_perp  
  > "We adopt the higher value for the dust mass of *C* derived by (cite: ogorman15) with a gas-to-dust ratio of 200." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.); "The total momentum driven by this ejection assuming the Hubble velocity field deduced above and a constant mass distribution along the ejecta is $P = 9.30 \times 10^{38}$ g cm s$^{-1}$." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived ~5 kG field with literature magnetic field estimates of 1 kG to 50 kG  
  > "The magnetic field of VY CMa has been estimated to reach a strength of 1 kG at the surface of the star (cite: Vlemmings02), and more recently up to 50 kG (cite: Shinnaga17) assuming a toroidal magnetic configuration." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
6. **Model flattened outflows with a rotating stellar spot (Sprinkler model)**: Simulate gas ejection from active spots at latitude theta_i and initial rotation angle phi_i on a rotating star, with expansion velocity modulated by a sinusoid over activity duration t_e, and match to 3D H2S structures  
  > "The model consists of a position at the photosphere of the star from which a certain gas ejection takes place." (Sprinkler model); "We adopted the velocity law from the PV diagram, a rotation velocity of 1 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Zhang12), and adapted the lapse of activity of the ejection ($t_e$) with an expansion velocity moduled by a sinusoidal so it is at a minimum when the ejection starts and finishes (at $t_e$) and reaches a maximum at $t_e/2$." (Sprinkler model)
   - parameter: Rotation axis angle ~95 deg around R.A. axis; v_rot = 1 km s^-1 (also tested 15 km s^-1); R_* = 9.9 x 10^13 cm; V_16 = 94.4 and 85.9 km s^-1; t_e = 10 yr  
  > "In this sense, we have found a particular angle ($\sim$95$^\circ$) around the R.A. axis, which seems to be compatible with this scenario." (Sprinkler model); ": Parameters of the Sprinkler model outflows. The rotation velocity of the star is 1km s$^{-1}$. $V_{16}$ is the value for a Hubble-like expansion velocity at 10$^{16}$cm." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Distance = 1.2 kpc; stellar radius R_* = 9.9 x 10^13 cm; sinusoidal velocity modulation during spot activity  
  > "With a stellar radius of VY CMa of $R_{\star} = 9.9 \times 10^{13}$cm (cite: Wittkowski12) the effect of the rotation should be seen in a collimated outflow with the formation times derived." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
   - fitting: First modeled clump C and used it as starting point to fit outflows 1-5, reducing velocity by 10% for outflows 3-5  
  > "We first modeled the ejection corresponding to clump $C$ and used it as a starting point to fit the rest of the fast outflows. In particular we found that we had to reduce the velocity by 10% for outflows 3–5, although the ejection time could be maintained." (Sprinkler model)
   - validation: Visual comparison of 3D simulated sprinkler ejections with reconstructed H2S structures  
  > "The result of our fitting of the sprinkler ejections to the observed H$_2$S structures is presented in Fig. 10." (Sprinkler model)

## Qualitative findings

- The circumstellar ejecta of VY CMa is composed of two distinct mass loss types: an extended, irregular, slower ejecta traced by SiO, SiS, SO, SO2, and 13CO, and localized fast outflows traced by H2S, NaCl, o-H2O, and continuum.  
  > "Two types of ejecta are clearly observed: extended, irregular, and vast ejecta surrounding the star that are carved by localized fast outflows." (History of two mass loss processes in VY CMa); "The continuum emission (Fig. 1) presents relatively compact structures already identified in previous works (cite: Richards14; ogorman15; debeck15). These structures are also clearly traced by certain species such as H$_2$S or NaCl. On the other hand, the emission from SiO, SiS, SO or $^{13}$CO presents a more extended emission." (Structure of the ejecta)
- The fast localized outflows carve cavities and bubble-like shell structures into the previously ejected quiescent gas traced by SO and SiO.  
  > "SiO and SO emission present clear signs of this carving, as the maps present no emission of these lines in the regions where the narrow outflows arise (see Fig 2)." (Structure of the ejecta); "This comparison shows that the outflows traced by H$_2$S and the shells probed by SO are related. In particular it suggests that the outflows are carving previous ejecta and forming cavities." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
- The fast outflows exhibit a flattened morphology orthogonal to their outflow axes with a width-to-height ratio of ~6.  
  > "The extended outflows are well resolved and are found to have a flattened geometry (Fig. 9) in one of the directions orthogonal to the axis of the ejection when compared with pPNe bipolar outflows (cite: alcolea07). The ratio of width to height is $\sim\,6$." (Sporadic outflows)
- The systemic velocity and position of VY CMa are better represented by V_LSR = 12 km s^-1 and the 232 GHz o-H2O maser peak rather than the previously assumed 22 km s^-1 and continuum peak.  
  > "In addition, the fitting of the outflow connecting $VY$ and $C$ suggests the systemic velocity at $VY$ might not be 22 km s$^{-1}$ as previously thought (cite: Decin16; Richards14), but $V_{LSR}=$12 km s$^{-1}$." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta); "Since the pumping mechanism of the maser is the IR emission from the central star, and the velocity of the peak corresponds to what we found to be compatible with the origin of the fast outflows, we propose that the position of this peak might correspond with the real position of the central star." (Inner ejecta)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution (~25-35 mas) ALMA observations of molecular emission provide spatio-kinematical information in the inner ejecta to disentangle mass ejection mechanisms in VY CMa.  
  > "In particular, the molecular emission from these ejecta provides spatio-kinematical information which allows us to reconstruct the events generating the ejection." (Introduction); "In order to study the characteristics of the ejecta, and the processes driving these mass ejections we obtained ALMA molecular maps of VY CMa with an angular resolution of $\sim 25-35$ mas" (Introduction)
- Why this method: 3D reconstruction using the expansion velocity law from PV diagrams transforms the velocity axis into spatial depth, enabling identification of multiple overlapping outflows and cavities in a complex circumstellar structure.  
  > "Due to the complex structure of the ejecta we generated 3D structures imposing the expansion velocity field obtained from the PV diagrams using the `Astructures code`." (Three-dimensional reconstruction); "Assuming a given velocity field, this dimension can be transformed into a space dimension, allowing us to reconstruct a 3D spatial structure." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
- Alternative: Radiation pressure on dust grains formed at circular cold starspots rejected as the driver of the fast collimated outflows because it cannot produce a Hubble-like velocity law or a flattened cross-section.  
  > "Its important to note that this type of velocity field ($v\propto r$) cannot be created by the radiation pressure on dust grains, but a different mechanism might be the responsible for these ejections." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta); "The presence of cold starspots alone could not generate the structures observed as they usually show a roughly circular shape, and the produced outflows are expected to have a similar similar cross section orthogonal to their outflow axes." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
- Alternative: Cold-spot groupings considered less likely than helmet streamers / CMEs to produce the observed flattened outflow morphology.  
  > "On the other hand, a cold-spot grouping is unlikely to generate the shape of the ejections presented here, which in fact resembles the structure of the helmet streamers (cite: Glukhov_1997) and the final stages of the CMEs (cite: Gibson_1998)." (CME-like ejections. Linear momentum estimate.)
- Alternative: Higher exponential orders for the expansion velocity law were tested and rejected as the cause of the outflow flatness because flatness is maintained across velocity laws.  
  > "In addition, we have modified the velocity law from constant expansion to higher exponential orders, and this flatness is maintained." (Sporadic outflows)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Extended homogeneous emission on scales larger than ~0.4 arcsec (maximum recoverable scale 0.36 arcsec) is partially filtered out by the interferometer.  
  > "Baselines ranged from 41m to 16km, providing an angular resolution $\sim 0\mbox{\rlap{.}$''$}025$ and with maximum recoverable scale for the most extended emission of $0\mbox{\rlap{.}$''$}36$." (Observations); "The extended emission is partially filtered out." (Structure of the ejecta); "The flux filtering affects structures with scales larger than $\sim$0$''$4." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
- Limitation (authors): Some detected molecular lines are too weak to obtain usable maps.  
  > "Some of these lines were detected, but are too weak to obtain a usable map." (Structure of the ejecta)
- Limitation (authors): There is a twofold degeneracy between jet inclination (theta) and the velocity constant (V_0) when fitting PV diagrams.  
  > "In order to fit the velocity field of these outflows, we took into account a twofold degeneracy. There is a series of pairs of values of the jet inclination with respect to the plane of the sky ($\theta$) and the value of the velocity constant ($V_0$), which would fit the velocity field of the outflow." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot completely rule out that the northern VY-A jet arises from VY at V_LSR = 26 km s^-1 rather than 12 km s^-1.  
  > "Nevertheless, the possibility of the $VY$–$A$ jet arising from $VY$ at 26 km s$^{-1}$ cannot be completely ruled out, and future observations will shed light on this fact." (VY-A & VY-C ejecta)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot determine whether the bullet-like structure of the two rear outflows is real or due to low S/N.  
  > "For the last two outflows only bullet-like structures are visible. We cannot determine if the bullet-like structure of these outflows is real or due to a lack of S/N in the obtained maps." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
- Limitation (authors): Complex velocity field of SO prevents a 3D reconstruction of the SO emission.  
  > "As mentioned, the velocity field of SO is particularly complex, and different from that observed for the fast outflows. This prevented a 3D reconstruction and a direct comparison of the structures traced by SO and H$_2$S." (Three-dimensional reconstruction)
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution (~30 x 20 mas) is not high enough to resolve the structure of the inner o-H2O outflows.  
  > "This emission is constrained to the innermost regions of the envelope around the star and is barely resolved (half power beam width, HPBW, $\sim 30 \times 20$mas)." (Inner ejecta); "The resolution is not high enough to resolve the structure of the outflows." (Inner ejecta)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3D reconstruction relies on assuming the same single velocity gradient (17 km/s per 0.1 arcsec, derived from clump C assuming a 15 deg inclination) for all outflows, so any variation in inclination or velocity constant among the other outflows directly distorts their reconstructed 3D line-of-sight depths and shapes.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Sprinkler model and the CME momentum model are heuristic/analytical approximations rather than self-consistent magnetohydrodynamic or radiative transfer simulations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Lack of short-spacing data filters out >0.36 arcsec scales, which could affect the apparent morphology of the cavities in the extended SiO and SO emission.
