## Science

- Fields: astrochemistry, star_formation
- Question: Whether the prevalence of one isomer over the other for formic acid (HCOOH) and thioformic acid (HC(O)SH) in the interstellar medium is due to a cis-trans isomerization transformation via quantum tunneling, and how predicted ratios compare with ALMA observations toward G31.41+0.31.  
  > "In this study, we carried out a theoretical analysis of the isomerization reaction of the HCOOH and HC(O)SH acids at interstellar temperatures (T$_{kin}$=10-400 K) in vacuum, with the aim of understanding whether or not the prevalence of one isomer over the other in the ISM is due to an isomerization transformation." (Introduction); "We also searched for these molecules towards the hot core G31.41+0.31 to compare their abundances with the expected theoretical isomerization results." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Compute ab initio electronic structures and energies for cis, trans, and transition state geometries of HCOOH and HC(O)SH**: Unconstrained geometry optimization and frequency calculation at B2PLYP-D3(BJ)/aug-cc-pVTZ level, followed by single-point energy correction with CCSD(T)-F12 and anharmonic ZPE correction via second-order vibrational perturbative theory; orbital interactions studied via second-order perturbation theory and solvation test in water with SMD.  
  > "All the stationary points were optimized without constraints at the double-hybrid B2PLYP (cite: grimme06) with the dispersion-corrected D3(BJ) (cite: grimme10; grimme11) method in combination with the Dunning’s triple-zeta correlation-consistent basis set, augmented with diffuse functions for all atoms (aug-cc-pVTZ) (cite: dunning89; kendall92)." (Electronic structure calculations); "To improve the level of theory, we corrected the electronic energies of the optimized geometries at B2PLYP-D3(BJ)/aug-cc-pVTZ with the CCSD(T)-F12 method (cite: adler2007; knizia2009)" (Electronic structure calculations); "Finally, we corrected the electronic energies by computing the anharmonic zero-point energy (ZPE) of the stationary points within vibrational perturbative theory to second order (cite: Barone2004)." (Electronic structure calculations)
   - software: Gaussian 16, ORCA, Cylview, Chimera
   - parameter: Basis sets: aug-cc-pVTZ for B2PLYP-D3(BJ); cc-pVTZ-F12-CABS, cc-pVTZ-F12, and aug-cc-pVQZ for CCSD(T)-F12  
  > "As auxiliary and special orbital basis set, the cc-pVTZ-F12-CABS and cc-pVTZ-F12 were used in combination with the augmented quadruple zeta aug-cc-pVQZ for the correlation and coulomb fitting." (Electronic structure calculations)
   - assumption: Vacuum (gas-phase) conditions for ISM isomerization  
  > "In this study, we carried out a theoretical analysis of the isomerization reaction of the HCOOH and HC(O)SH acids at interstellar temperatures (T$_{kin}$=10-400 K) in vacuum" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Validated B2PLYP-D3(BJ) by optimizing all stationary points and computing frequencies for HCOOH directly with CCSD(T)-F12/cc-pVTZ-F12, confirming almost identical geometries and relative energies; checked imaginary frequencies (0 for minima, 1 for saddle points).  
  > "In order to validate the choice of the double-hybrid method we also optimized all the stationary points for HCOOH with the CCSD(T)-F12 method in conjunction with the cc-pVTZ-F12-CABS and cc-pVTZ-F12 basis set and the aug-cc-pVTZ for the correlation and coulomb fitting (see Appendix A)." (Electronic structure calculations); "The comparison between these two methods shows that the B2PLYP-D3(BJ) is suitable for quantitative investigations, because both the relative energies and the geometries of the stationary points are almost identical (see Tables A.1 and A.2 and Figure A.1)." (Electronic structure calculations)
2. **Calculate unimolecular cis-trans isomerization rate constants and equilibrium ratios across 10-400 K**: Canonical variational transition state theory (CVT) with multidimensional small curvature tunneling (SCT) approximation; minimum energy path (MEP) energies corrected with CCSD(T)-F12 using interpolated single-point energies (ISPE) algorithm.  
  > "We used the canonical variational transition state theory (CVT) (cite: bao) to compute rate constants with the small curvature tunneling approximation (SCT) in the temperature ($T$) range of 10-400 K." (Kinetics calculations); "The energies of the minimum energy path (MEP) obtained with the double-hybrid B2PLYP-D3(BJ)/aug-cc-pVTZ were corrected with the coupled cluster method mentioned in section 2.1 using the interpolated single-point energies (ISPE) algorithm (cite: chuang99)." (Kinetics calculations)
   - software: Pilgrim
   - parameter: Temperature range T = 10-400 K; ZPE scaling factors 0.948 for HCOOH and 0.949 for HC(O)SH  
  > "We used the canonical variational transition state theory (CVT) (cite: bao) to compute rate constants with the small curvature tunneling approximation (SCT) in the temperature ($T$) range of 10-400 K." (Kinetics calculations); "We take the high-frequency anharmonicities into account by multiplying the ZPE by a scaling factor for the energy minima and along the whole reaction path (0.948 for HCOOH and 0.949 for HC(O)SH)." (Kinetic results of the isomerizations)
   - assumption: High-frequency anharmonicities accounted for via constant ZPE scaling factor along reaction path  
  > "We take the high-frequency anharmonicities into account by multiplying the ZPE by a scaling factor for the energy minima and along the whole reaction path (0.948 for HCOOH and 0.949 for HC(O)SH)." (Kinetic results of the isomerizations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked variational effects along the MEP (K^CVT / K^TST = 1 over 10-400 K).  
  > "This effect is reflected in the relation $\textit{K}$$^{CVT}$/$\textit{K}$$^{TST}$, which is equal to 1 over the whole range of temperatures between 10 and 400 K." (Kinetic results of the isomerizations)
3. **Identify transitions and derive column densities, abundances, and trans/cis ratios of HCOOH and HC(O)SH toward G31.41+0.31**: Synthetic LTE spectrum generation and automated line fitting using SLIM and MADCUBA-AUTOFIT on the GUAPOS 3mm spectrum, accounting for line opacity and contamination from all previously modeled species in the survey.  
  > "The identification of the transitions of the cis and trans isomers of HCOOH and HC(O)SH in the GUAPOS spectrum was performed using version 01/12/2020 of the SLIM (Spectral Line Identification and Modeling) tool within the MADCUBA package ((cite: martin2019))." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey); "For each molecular species, we used the MADCUBA-AUTOFIT tool to compare the observed spectrum with the LTE synthetic one." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM, MADCUBA-AUTOFIT
   - parameter: Four LTE parameters: column density (N), excitation temperature (T_ex), peak radial velocity (v_LSR), and FWHM; for t-HCOOH, v_LSR fixed to 96.5 km s^-1; for c-HCOOH, t-HC(O)SH, and c-HC(O)SH, T_ex = 152 K, v_LSR = 96.5 km s^-1, and FWHM = 8.0 km s^-1 fixed to t-HCOOH values  
  > "To perform the fit we fixed the v$_{\rm LSR}$ to 96.5 km s$^{-1}$, which is the velocity of the source." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey); "We fixed $T_{\rm ex}$, v$_{\rm LSR}$, and $FWHM$ to those inferred for t-HCOOH" (Isomerization of thioformic acid, HC(O)SH, in the ISM)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), justified by high volume density n(H2) ~ 10^8 cm^-3; H2 column density N_H2 = (1.0 +/- 0.2) x 10^25 cm^-2; distance 3.75 kpc  
  > "The LTE assumption is well justified given the high volume density of the source $n$(H$_{2}$) $\sim$ 10$^{8}$ cm$^{-3}$ ((cite: mininni2020))." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey); "This implies an abundance of $X$ = (1.4$\pm$0.7)$\times$10$^{-8}$ for the H$_2$ column density of $N_{\rm H_{2}}$ = (1.0 $\pm$ 0.2) $\times$10$^{25}$ cm$^{-2}$ measured by (cite: mininni2020) at 3mm." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey)
   - fitting: MADCUBA-AUTOFIT non-linear least-squares LTE fitting on unblended/less-contaminated transitions; upper limit for c-HCOOH derived by increasing N to maximum value compatible with observed spectrum.  
  > "The parameters are left free to obtain the best-LTE fit. When convergence is not reached, the parameters $FWHM$, v$_{\rm LSR}$, and/or $T_{\rm ex}$ are fixed." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey); "Thus, we assumed $T_{\rm ex}$, v$_{\rm LSR}$ and $FWHM$ derived for t-HCOOH, and increased $N$ to the maximum value compatible with the observed spectrum." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity of HCOOH trans/cis lower limit to T_ex ranging from 50 to 300 K (yielding lower limits between >=70 and >=95).  
  > "We note that if we use other $T_{\rm ex}$ ranging from 50 up to 300$\,$K, we still get a trans/cis lower limit between $\geq$70 and $\geq$95." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey)

## Qualitative findings

- Cis-trans isomerization of HCOOH and HC(O)SH is viable at moderately low to high ISM temperatures due to ground-state quantum tunneling, enabling thermodynamic equilibrium.  
  > "Our results demonstrate that these isomerizations are viable in the conditions of the ISM due to ground-state tunneling effects, which allow the system to reach the thermodynamic equilibrium at moderately low temperatures." (abstract)
- At very low temperatures (~10 K), isomerization rate constants are too small to form cis isomers via tunneling, so observed c-HCOOH in cold cores (B5, L483) must arise from competitive chemical processes or secondary UV excitation.  
  > "Our kinetic results reveal that, at very low temperatures, the isomerization reactions cannot occur because their rate constants are very low. This is in disagreement with the observed trans/cis ratios of HCOOH acid in the cold molecular cores B5 and L483, which implies that chemical processes such as competitive chemical routes or excitations caused by secondary UV fields must be responsible." (Conclusions)
- Trans-HCOOH is detected and both trans- and cis-HC(O)SH are tentatively detected (cis for the first time in the ISM) toward G31.41+0.31, with abundances matching high-temperature thermodynamic equilibrium predictions.  
  > "We detect the trans form of HCOOH and tentatively detected trans and cis HC(O)SH, the latter for the first time in the ISM." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G31.41+0.31 is a massive, chemically rich hot molecular core where many complex organic molecules have been detected, and the unbiased ALMA Band 3 GUAPOS survey covers numerous transitions of HCOOH and HC(O)SH.  
  > "Towards G31, many molecules have been detected ((cite: beltran2005); (cite: beltran2009); (cite: rivilla2017b); (cite: beltran2018); (cite: mininni2020); (cite: gorai2021); (cite: laura21)), some of which are complex (>5 atoms). Therefore, G31 is a promising target to search for HCOOH and HC(O)SH." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey)
- Why this method: Multidimensional small curvature tunneling (SCT) was chosen because it accounts for all molecular degrees of freedom and yields transmission coefficients orders of magnitude more accurate than 1D tunneling treatments; LTE spectral modeling is justified by the high gas density n(H2) ~ 10^8 cm^-3 of G31.  
  > "To account for the quantum tunneling effect, which is very important especially at low temperatures, we selected the multidimensional small curvature tunneling (SCT) approximation, which has been demonstrated to give much higher transmission coefficients than other conventional monodimensional treatments (cite: juan)." (Introduction); "The LTE assumption is well justified given the high volume density of the source $n$(H$_{2}$) $\sim$ 10$^{8}$ cm$^{-3}$ ((cite: mininni2020))." (Comparison with observations from the GUAPOS spectral survey)
- Alternative: One-dimensional quantum tunneling treatments (rejected in favor of multidimensional SCT because 1D treatments underestimate transmission coefficients by orders of magnitude).  
  > "However, in previous works, the quantum tunneling corrections to the rate coefficients were carried out using a one-dimensional treatment (see section 2.3. in (cite: bao)). More recently, we demonstrated that multidimensional treatment of this quantum effect (e.g., where all the degrees of freedom of the molecule are considered in the tunneling process) increases the transmission coefficients by several orders of magnitude (cite: juan)." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Comparison with Orion Bar PDR HCOOH ratios (excluded because c-HCOOH in the Orion Bar is produced via UV photoisomerization through excited electronic states rather than ground-state tunneling).  
  > "We note that we do not compare our results with the trans/cis ratios measured toward the Orion Bar because the production of c-HCOOH in this source is due to the gas being irradiated by UV photons ((cite: cuadrado16))." (Isomerization of formic acid, HCOOH, in the ISM)
