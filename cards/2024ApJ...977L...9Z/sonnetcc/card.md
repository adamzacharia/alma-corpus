## Analysis recipe

1. **Create Band 6 and Band 8 data cubes**: Cubes made from calibrated uv-visibilities with natural weighting; Band 6 cubes built for several pixel sizes, channel widths, phase centers and first-channel velocity offsets  
  > "we created data cubes from the $uv$-visibilties using a Natural weighting to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio (at the cost of angular resolution)." (2.1 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Pixel sizes 0.05'', 0.075'', 0.10''; channel widths 35, 50, 75, 100 km/s  
  > "we tested several choices for the pixel size (namely $0.05\ensuremath{''}$, $0.075\ensuremath{''}$, and $0.10\ensuremath{''}$)" (2.1 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Variants of the imaging used to estimate line-parameter uncertainties  
  > "all of these datacubes are used to robustly estimate the uncertainties on the line parameters." (2.1 Observations and data reduction)
2. **Search for and fit the [OIII] 88 um line in each cube**: Extract spectrum at JWST position, Gaussian-fit brightest channel near expected frequency, build moment-0 over the fitted FWHM, re-extract at the peak pixel, refit  
  > "we create a moment-0 map by collapsing the datacube within the full-width half-maximum (FWHM) of the best-fit Gaussian function and re-extract the spectrum but now at the position of the peak pixel in this moment-0 map." (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
   - parameter: Gaussian with A, sigma, nu_cent free and flat zero continuum  
  > "perform a Gaussian fit with $A,\sigma, \nu_{\rm cent}$ as free parameters (with a flat zero continuum)" (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
   - assumption: Line expected within frequency range set by JWST NIRSpec/MIRI redshifts  
  > "within the expected frequency range of the redshifted" (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit with free amplitude, width, centre; no sampler or priors stated  
  > "perform a Gaussian fit with $A,\sigma, \nu_{\rm cent}$ as free parameters (with a flat zero continuum)" (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
   - validation: All spectra give the same feature with significance of about 4.5-5.2 sigma  
  > "all the extracted spectra (those extracted at the JWST position and at the peak ALMA pixel) identify the same feature as an emission line with a significance of $\approx4.5-5.2\sigma$" (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
3. **Adopt fiducial line properties and uncertainties**: Median of all best-fit functions; uncertainty from standard deviation across them  
  > "The fiducial line properties and associated uncertainties are assumed to be the median values of all the different best-fit functions and their standard deviation." (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Accounts for the impact of imaging parameters on the line shape  
  > "This approach allows us to properly take into account the impact of the imaging parameters in the shape of the line" (2.2 The [OIII]88 transition at z = 12.3: line properties)
4. **Derive the [OIII] 52 um upper limit and the 52/88 ratio limit**: 3 sigma upper limit from local noise r.m.s. in Band 8 cube, using the 88 um line width and assuming emission unresolved by the beam  
  > "using the measured local noise r.m.s." (2.3 An upper limit on the [OIII] 52 um line luminosity); "and assuming no spatially-extended emission beyond the beam-size." (2.3 An upper limit on the [OIII] 52 um line luminosity)
   - parameter: Line width 186 +/- 58 km/s adopted; search around 433.96 GHz  
  > "we search for the 52$\mu$m line around the expected frequency of 433.96 GHz" (2.3 An upper limit on the [OIII] 52 um line luminosity)
   - assumption: No spatially extended emission beyond the beam  
  > "and assuming no spatially-extended emission beyond the beam-size." (2.3 An upper limit on the [OIII] 52 um line luminosity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare L[OIII]88 with SFR relations**: Place GHZ2 (magnification-corrected luminosity, SFR 2-12 Msun/yr) on the De Looze et al. local relations along with other high-z detections  
  > "As can been seen in the figure, GHZ2 lies on a locus comparable to some $z\sim8$ LBGs/LAEs and in very good agreement with the local relationship for metal-poor dwarf galaxies." (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
   - parameter: magnification mu = 1.3  
  > "corrected by magnification ($\mu=1.3$;" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
   - assumption: Magnification-corrected SFR from SED fitting and Halpha  
  > "($2-12\,\rm M_\odot\,yr^{-1}$, after correcting for magnification)" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Broad agreement with serra zoom-in simulation predictions  
  > "the measured line luminosity is also in broad agreement with the predictions from the serra zoom-in simulations" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
6. **Test the L-sigma relation for HII regions**: Compare Hbeta luminosity with the [OIII] 88 um line width as proxy for velocity dispersion against the HII region relation  
  > "the \[OIII\] 88$\ensuremath{\mu}m$ line-width provided by the ALMA observations as a proxy for the \[OIII\]5007$\AA$ velocity dispersion" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
   - parameter: log L_Hbeta = 42.16 (+0.09/-0.13) erg/s  
  > "$\log\, L_{\rm H\beta} = 42.16^{+0.09}_{-0.13}\rm\,erg\,s^{-1}$" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
   - assumption: Hbeta luminosity inferred from Halpha assuming zero dust attenuation; relation does not evolve with redshift  
  > "(inferred from the H$\alpha$ line assuming zero dust attenuation)" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications); "assuming the relationship does not evolve significantly with redshift" (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Estimate dynamical mass**: Virial equation M_dyn = 5 sigma^2 R / G with sigma from [OIII] 88 um and R from JWST half-light radius; cross-checked with Ubler et al. approach  
  > "Assuming a spherical geometry (a reasonable assumption for this very compact source), we estimate the dynamical mass using the virial equation" (3.2 Dynamical mass)
   - parameter: sigma = 79 +/- 25 km/s; r_1/2 = 39 +/- 11 pc or 105 +/- 9 pc  
  > "($\sigma=79\pm25\rm\,km\,s^{-1}$)" (3.2 Dynamical mass); "$r_{1/2}=39\pm11\,$pc" (3.2 Dynamical mass)
   - assumption: Spherical geometry; virial radius equals the half-light radius  
  > "Assuming a spherical geometry (a reasonable assumption for this very compact source)" (3.2 Dynamical mass)
   - assumption: Sersic n = 1 and 1.5, axis ratios q = 0.3-0.7 in the alternative approach  
  > "Here, we assume Sersic indices of $n=1$ and $1.5$, with axis ratios in the range of $q=\{0.3:0.7\}$." (3.2 Dynamical mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative approach gives masses within the same range, with up to a 50% increase; virial value kept as fiducial  
  > "allow for up to a 50% increase" (3.2 Dynamical mass)
8. **Constrain the electron density from the 52/88 ratio**: Use the [OIII] 52/88 luminosity ratio limit to derive a 3 sigma upper limit on electron density  
  > "provides strong constraints on the electron density almost independently from metallicity, ionization field effects, or electron temperature" (3.3 Constraining the electron density); "we determine a $3\sigma$ upper limit on the ionized Oxygen electron density of $\log(n_e\,[\rm cm^{-3}])<3.62$" (3.3 Constraining the electron density)
   - parameter: L52/L88 < 5.6 (3 sigma)  
  > "$L_{\rm [OIII]52\mu m}/L_{\rm [OIII]88\mu m}<5.6$" (2.3 An upper limit on the [OIII] 52 um line luminosity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with measured electron densities at z>~5  
  > "this upper limit is totally consistent with the measured electron densities of high-redshift ($z\gtrsim5$) star-forming galaxies" (3.3 Constraining the electron density)

## Qualitative findings

- GHZ2 lies on the local L[OIII]88-SFR relation for metal-poor dwarf galaxies  
  > "GHZ2 lies on a locus comparable to some $z\sim8$ LBGs/LAEs and in very good agreement with the local relationship for metal-poor dwarf galaxies." (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
- GHZ2 follows the Hbeta luminosity - velocity dispersion relation of giant HII regions  
  > "GHZ2 follows the same relationship found for giant HII regions, suggesting that this very high-redshift galaxy is also powered by (extreme) star formation." (3.1 Detection of [OIII] 88 um: Expectations and implications)
- Dynamical-to-stellar mass ratio close to unity, like globular clusters  
  > "This implies a dynamical-to-stellar mass ratio of close to unity for GHZ2" (3.2 Dynamical mass)
- The ALMA continuum remains undetected at >3 sigma; a companion paper will give dust constraints  
  > "(which remains undetected at $>3\sigma$)" (1 Introduction)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier ALMA scan non-detections had uncertain redshifts; with a spectroscopic redshift, deeper single-setup observations were possible  
  > "Most of these observations, however, suffered from targets with highly uncertain redshifts (most of them limited to photometric redshifts), which require line-searches through multi-setup spectral scans" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting chosen to maximise S/N at the cost of resolution; Band 8 designed shallow to give a limit for extreme electron density  
  > "These observations are by design shallower than the Band 6 data since were only aimed to provide a limit on the" (2.1 Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Alternative dynamical mass from Ubler et al. approach (Sersic n=1, 1.5; q=0.3-0.7) gives up to 50% higher masses; virial equation kept as fiducial  
  > "we adopt the mass constraints from the virial equation described above as our fiducial value." (3.2 Dynamical mass)
- Alternative: AGN-dominated scenario judged unlikely but not fully excluded  
  > "This provides evidence against an AGN-dominated system, although it does not totally rule out some fractional contribution to its continuum luminosity." (4.1 A system dominated by star formation)
- How they decided it was enough: Matching Band 6 sensitivity in Band 8 would have needed more time than a DDT proposal allows  
  > "Matching the Band 6 sensitivity would have required a much larger time investment that is beyond the scope of a DDT proposal." (2.1 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Previous tentative line at 258.67 GHz not covered by the new data  
  > "Unfortunately, the new data do not cover the previous tentative line reported by" (2.1 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Band 8 data too shallow to detect [OIII] 52 um; only an upper limit  
  > "The line is undetected at the current depth of the observations" (Band 8 spectrum around the [OIII] 52 um transition)
- Limitation (authors): Interpretation is suggestive; further high spectral and angular resolution observations needed  
  > "further observations with both high spectral and high angular resolution are needed to test this hypothesis" (Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line significance is only ~4.5-5.2 sigma, so the detection and derived FWHM/sigma carry large errors that propagate into M_dyn
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dynamical mass assumes spherical virial geometry and an unresolved [OIII] width; dispersion from a marginal line is uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Hbeta luminosity assumes zero dust attenuation and the L-sigma relation uses the [OIII] 88 um width as proxy for [OIII] 5007
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Electron density comes from a single 3 sigma upper limit, so the lower bound relies on another paper
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cube-ensemble uncertainty (standard deviation of fits) is not a rigorous statistical error
