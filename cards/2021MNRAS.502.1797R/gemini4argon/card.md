## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce optical and near-IR imaging and extract multi-band source photometry**: Reduce Gemini GMOS data with Gemini-IRAF scripts, FLAMINGOS-2 Ks data with FATBOY pipeline, combine Spitzer IRAC mosaics, and extract photometry with SEXTRACTOR in single-image mode.  
  > "Data reduction followed Gemini-IRAF reduction scripts and standard parameters for the optical data." (Gemini-S imaging); "For the $K_{\rm s}$ observations, the data were reduced using the `python`-based FLAMINGOS-2 Data Pipeline, FATBOY, created by the Astronomy Department at the University of Florida." (Gemini-S imaging); "SEXTRACTOR (cite: Bertin) was used to extract catalogues of sources in all bands." (Gemini-S imaging)
   - software: Gemini-IRAF, FATBOY, SEXTRACTOR
   - parameter: Detection/analysis threshold 1.1-2.5 sigma per pixel, minimum detection area 3 pixels, 5x5 Gaussian filter with FWHM 3 pixels; apertures: 1.6 arcsec (g,r,i), m_auto (Ks), 4 arcsec with PSF curve-of-growth correction (IRAC)  
  > "The detection threshold and analysis threshold varied between bands from $1.1{-}2.5\sigma$ per pixel, while the minimum detection area was kept constant at 3 pixels." (Gemini-S imaging); "In the $g,r,$ and $i$ bands, the photometry was extracted within a 1.6 arcsec aperture. $K_{\rm s}$-band photometry was extracted using $m_{\rm{auto}}$." (Gemini-S imaging); "These are listed with 4 arcsec-aperture magnitudes (corrected to total flux using point spread function curve of growth) in Table" (IRAC imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual verification of cross-matching (<0.3 arcsec in GMOS, <0.5 arcsec between GMOS and FLAMINGOS-2/IRAC)  
  > "Any offsets between sources measured in different bands required cross-matching to within ${<}\,0.3$ arcsec in GMOS and ${<}\,0.5$ arcsec between GMOS and FLAMINGOS-2/IRAC. We verified visually that this did not result in any missed sources or erroneous associations." (Gemini-S imaging)
2. **Select Lyman-break galaxies (LBGs) and search for ALMA [CII] emission at LBG positions**: Apply g-band dropout colour selection criteria (standard and relaxed) to GMOS photometry, calculate UV SFRs, and check ALMA Band 7 maps at LBG positions for continuum and [CII] line emission.  
  > "We also use the optical imaging to search for LBGs using the $g$-band dropout technique of (cite: Toshikawa18), specifically $g-r>1,\ -1<r-i<1,\ {\rm and}\ 1.5(r-i)<g-r-0.8$." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "We have therefore also applied a selection with a relaxed upper limit of the $r-i$ colour to 2." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "For LBG3 there is a candidate $6.8\sigma$ $_{158\,\mu\text{m}}$ line detection at 359.4 GHz ($z=4.288$), a significance that places it just below the cutoff adopted in the catalogue of (cite: Hill20)." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
   - parameter: g - r > 1, -1 < r - i < 1 (or r - i < 2 relaxed), and 1.5(r - i) < g - r - 0.8; >5 sigma in i and >3 sigma in r  
  > "We also use the optical imaging to search for LBGs using the $g$-band dropout technique of (cite: Toshikawa18), specifically $g-r>1,\ -1<r-i<1,\ {\rm and}\ 1.5(r-i)<g-r-0.8$." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "We require a ${>}5\sigma$ and ${>}3\sigma$ detection in the $i$ and $r$ bands, respectively." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
   - assumption: SFR for LBG1-3 calculated as 1.4e-28 L_nu(1500 A) M_sun yr^-1 with extinction estimated from r - i colour  
  > "SFRs for LBG1–3 are calculated as $1.4\times10^{-28}$ L$_\nu$($1,500$ Å) M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, with extinction estimated from $r-i$ colour." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of UV SFR with [CII] line strength and spectroscopic confirmation from MUSE and ALMA  
  > "The rest-UV SFR estimate (calculated as described in Table 1) is 20 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, which agrees reasonably well with the  line strength for typical $z\sim4$ galaxies (cite: Schaerer)." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
3. **Spectroscopic analysis and gravitational lensing assessment for SMG A and SMG C**: Reduce VLT/X-shooter spectra using the ESO pipeline, measure foreground emission lines for source A, estimate dynamical mass and gravitational lensing magnification using a simple lens model.  
  > "We used the ESO pipeline (cite: Modigliani) to reduce our data." (VLT spectroscopy); "The linewidth suggests an upper limit to a dynamical mass enclosed within a 2 kpc-radius of $M_{\rm dyn}=1.56\times R_{1/2}\ \sigma^2<1.6\times10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ (cite: Erb06)." (Foreground source of SMG A); "Using a simple lens model (cite: Spilker) we set the Einstein lensing mass to $2.5\times M^*$ with $M^*=1.6\times10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ (a generous assumption) and adjust the background source offset until the apparent image is 0.5 arcsec from the lens position" (Foreground source of SMG A)
   - software: ESO pipeline
   - parameter: Slit width 1.2 arcsec, length 11 arcsec; total integration 5,400 sec per source; lens offset 0.5 arcsec, ellipticity e = 0.6  
  > "X-shooter is capable of near-continuous spectroscopy from 0.3 to $2.48\,\mu$m, with a slit width and length of 1.2 and 11 arcsec, respectively." (VLT spectroscopy); "We find a magnification factor of $\mu=1.15$ for a circular lens, or ranging from 1.09 to 1.24 for a highly elliptical lens ($e=0.6$), depending on the position angle." (Foreground source of SMG A)
   - assumption: R_1/2 = 2 kpc for dynamical mass; Einstein lensing mass = 2.5 x M* with M* = 1.6e9 M_sun  
  > "The linewidth suggests an upper limit to a dynamical mass enclosed within a 2 kpc-radius of $M_{\rm dyn}=1.56\times R_{1/2}\ \sigma^2<1.6\times10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ (cite: Erb06)." (Foreground source of SMG A); "Using a simple lens model (cite: Spilker) we set the Einstein lensing mass to $2.5\times M^*$ with $M^*=1.6\times10^{9}$ M$_{\odot}$ (a generous assumption)" (Foreground source of SMG A)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate stellar masses and physical properties of SMGs via SED fitting**: Model multi-wavelength SEDs using CIGALE for 9 detected SMGs, and use average 4.5 um mass-to-light ratio to place upper limits on the 5 undetected SMGs.  
  > "We estimate the stellar masses by modelling the multi-wavelength SEDs using the software *Code Investigating GALaxy Emission* (CIGALE; (cite: Noll09; Serra11; Boquien19))." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "We use the average mass-to-light ratio for the nine detected sources," (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: Delayed SFH with single exponential decrease; Inoue (2011) nebular templates; Calzetti (2000) attenuation curve with power-law slope 0; Draine (2014) dust templates and Fritz (2006) AGN templates (0.0 to 0.9) for source C; age restricted to 0.7-1.25 Gyrs for sources D, H, N  
  > "To model the sources we have assumed a delayed star-formation history with a single exponential decrease. The e-folding time ($\tau$) and age of the stellar population are kept as free parameters while a solar metallicity and a Chabrier initial mass function (IMF; (cite: Chabrier)) are assumed." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Nebular emission is included in the fitting using templates from (cite: Inoue11). The dust attenuation is modelled using a (cite: Calzetti00) attenuation curve with a power law slope of zero." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Here we constrain the SED fits, allowing the age of the stellar population to lie between $0.7{-}1.25$ Gyrs, consistent with that of the brighter sources in the proto-cluster." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
   - assumption: Solar metallicity, Chabrier IMF, H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, Omega_m = 0.3, alpha_CO = 0.8 with 1.36x helium factor for gas fractions  
  > "A Hubble constant $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ and density parameters $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$ and $\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3$ are assumed throughout." (Introduction); "The e-folding time ($\tau$) and age of the stellar population are kept as free parameters while a solar metallicity and a Chabrier initial mass function (IMF; (cite: Chabrier)) are assumed." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "gas masses were determined from the $^{12}$CO line luminosities ( when $^{12}$CO was not available) from (cite: Miller) using the standard conversion of (cite: Bothwell) with $\alpha_{\rm CO}=0.8$ and a $1.36\times$ correction factor to include helium." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
   - fitting: CIGALE pdf analysis module using reduced chi^2 grid and marginalization over probability distribution  
  > "The stellar masses for the sources are estimated using the ‘pdf analysis’ module in CIGALE." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "The array of reduced $\chi^2$ values for each model is then used to create a probability distribution for the stellar mass. This distribution is generally not a Gaussian and a value marginalized over the distribution is calculated." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
   - validation: Mock catalogue analysis generated by CIGALE and comparison of fits with and without ALMA photometry  
  > "We find the stellar mass values reported here to be consistent with mock catalogue results and are thus reliable within errors quoted." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Additionally, we found stellar mass estimates to be broadly consistent between fitting with and without ALMA data (adopted from (cite: Miller)), where deviations between the two are less than one tenth of the error of the estimates on a log scale." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
5. **Evaluate LBG overdensity, star-formation main sequence, BCG growth, and cluster baryon budget**: Compute LBG surface density and overdensity relative to Toshikawa et al. (2018) and Steidel et al. (1999), compare SMG stellar masses and ALMA SFRs against z~4.3 main sequence relations, and compare cumulative stellar and cold baryon masses against virial and dynamical masses.  
  > "Adopting the same metric to (cite: Toshikawa18), we find 16 LBGs to $i_{\rm AB}<25$ in a 1.8 arcmin-radius aperture for SPT2349-56, which would be a marginal $3\sigma$ overdensity detection" (Implications for optical proto-cluster identification); "Having estimated the stellar masses, we can assess the relation between the SFR and stellar mass for the SPT2349-56 SMGs, adopting SFRs based on ALMA photometry from (cite: Hill20), and compare them to the coeval field population." (Main sequence of star formation); "We can divide the total baryon mass by the virial mass estimated by (cite: Hill20), which yields a cluster baryon fraction of $f_{\rm b}=0.19$." (Baryon budget of SPT2349-56)
   - assumption: Average 2-kpc radius assumed in SMG dynamical mass calculations; virial mass M_vir = 1e13 M_sun from Hill et al. (2020)  
  > "Furthermore, in the absence of radial size estimates for each SMG, we assumed an average 2-kpc radius in the dynamical mass calculations." (Baryon budget of SPT2349-56); "(cite: Hill20) find a slightly lower $M_{\rm vir}\simeq1\times10^{13}$ M$_{\odot}$ from a larger number of galaxies within a somewhat larger radius, but still well consistent within the uncertainties." (Baryon budget of SPT2349-56)

## Qualitative findings

- Only nine of the 14 ALMA-identified SMGs in the SPT2349-56 core have optical/near-IR counterparts in Gemini-S and Spitzer-IRAC observations.  
  > "We detect optical/IR counterparts for only nine of the 14 submillimetre galaxies (SMGs) previously identified by ALMA in the core of SPT2349-56." (Abstract)
- The bright optical source near SMG A (offset by 0.4 arcsec) is a foreground z = 2.54 star-forming galaxy that provides negligible gravitational lensing magnification (mu < 1.24).  
  > "An X-shooter spectrum of source $A$ (see Fig. 4) does not show any emission features expected from a $z=4.3$ galaxy, but does reveal a foreground $z=2.54$, star-forming galaxy that likely dominates the $g$-band photometry" (Foreground source of SMG A); "We find a magnification factor of $\mu=1.15$ for a circular lens, or ranging from 1.09 to 1.24 for a highly elliptical lens ($e=0.6$), depending on the position angle." (Foreground source of SMG A)
- SPT2349-56 would be difficult to uncover in wide-field optical LBG surveys, showing only a marginal 3-sigma LBG overdensity within a 1.8 arcmin aperture.  
  > "Adopting the same metric to (cite: Toshikawa18), we find 16 LBGs to $i_{\rm AB}<25$ in a 1.8 arcmin-radius aperture for SPT2349-56, which would be a marginal $3\sigma$ overdensity detection and would therefore not meet the threshold set by (cite: Toshikawa18)." (Implications for optical proto-cluster identification)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Optical and near-IR (Gemini g,r,i,Ks and Spitzer-IRAC) imaging are needed to probe the rest-frame UV and optical properties, constrain stellar masses of the ALMA-identified SMGs, and search for optically-selected LBG members.  
  > "This paper presents optical-through-near-IR photometry of SPT2349-56, with the aim of searching for additional optically-selected cluster members and to study the bright SMGs in the SPT2349-56 proto-cluster core." (Introduction); "We consider next the observed $K_{\rm s}$, IRAC $3.6\,\mu$m, and IRAC $4.5\,\mu$m properties of the SPT2349-56 sources with an aim of constraining stellar mass." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- Why this method: CIGALE is used for SED modelling because it adopts an energy balance principle between UV-optical and far-IR-mm regimes and can incorporate upper limits in the reduced chi^2 calculation; single exponential decay SFH provides conservative stellar mass estimates.  
  > "CIGALE adopts an energy balance principle between the UV-optical and far-IR-mm regimes – the energy absorbed by dust in the UV-optical is proportional to the thermal radiation emitted by dust in the far-IR." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Typically the single exponential decay SFH provides the lowest stellar mass, our estimates are therefore relatively conservative." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Importantly, CIGALE can take upper limits as inputs in multiple bands and incorporate them in the reduced $\chi^2$ calculation (cite: Boquien19)." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- Alternative: Deconvolution of the crowded central region (sources B, C, G) in IRAC bands was not attempted; instead Ks-band limits were used to bound the IRAC contribution of B and G.  
  > "We did not attempt a deconvolution of the crowded field." (IRAC imaging); "The $K_{\rm s}$-band limit of sources $B$ and $G$ ($K_{\rm s}>24.5$) allows us to place limits on the IRAC flux that $B$ and $G$ may contribute." (IRAC imaging)
- Alternative: Varying the power-law slope of the Calzetti (2000) dust attenuation curve and including or excluding ALMA photometry in CIGALE were tested and yielded consistent stellar mass estimates.  
  > "The dust attenuation is modelled using a (cite: Calzetti00) attenuation curve with a power law slope of zero. The stellar mass estimates are consistent within the errors even when the slope is allowed to vary." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Additionally, we found stellar mass estimates to be broadly consistent between fitting with and without ALMA data (adopted from (cite: Miller)), where deviations between the two are less than one tenth of the error of the estimates on a log scale." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- Alternative: Type-I AGN contribution was ruled out by high extinction values, while Type-II AGN fraction (0.0 to 0.9) was tested for source C and set to zero for the remaining sources.  
  > "However, the extinction values for the SMGs in SPT2349-56 rule out large contribution to the luminosity by a type-I AGN." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "For source $C$, which does have good coverage in rest-frame optical, near-IR and far-IR, we estimated the stellar mass by fixing the AGN contribution to a type-II template, ranging from 0.0 to 0.9." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Relaxing the LBG r - i colour selection upper limit to 2 is incomplete for i_AB > 25.7.  
  > "However, we note that we are incomplete (potentially missing some sources) for $i_{\rm AB}>25.7$ when doing so." (Rest-frame ultraviolet properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- Limitation (authors): Apart from source C, the SMGs are slightly over-fitted in CIGALE due to the low number of photometric detections (reduced chi^2 < 1), and sparse/low-SNR photometry prevents detailed star-formation history constraints.  
  > "Apart from source $C$, we note that the sources are slightly over-fitted due to their low number of detections, which is indicated by their reduced $\chi^2$ values of less than 1 (see Appendix A)." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "Unfortunately, our photometric sampling is too sparse and our measurements often too low a SNR for precise SED fitting that would allow us to discuss in detail the star formation histories." (Main sequence of star formation)
- Limitation (authors): Mock catalogue analysis for source D showed its stellar mass estimate is less certain due to having only a single-band detection.  
  > "However, mock catalogue analysis for source $D$ showed the stellar mass estimate to not be as certain as for the others (as can be expected for fitting with a single-band detection) – the limits at our other wavelengths did not provide strong enough constraints." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- Limitation (authors): WISE 22 um and Herschel-SPIRE limits are not sufficiently deep to tightly constrain the fractional AGN contribution in source C.  
  > "However, the only mid-IR constraint is from WISE at $22\,\mu$m, with supplemental SPIRE limits, which were not sufficiently deep to quantify the fractional AGN contribution." (A large stellar mass for source C)
- Limitation (authors): Five of the 14 SMGs are undetected in all optical/near-IR bands, leaving an ambiguity between genuinely low stellar masses (high gas fractions) and extreme dust extinction.  
  > "The remaining five sources are undetected in all observed bands, and may actually have very low stellar masses." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core); "However, we do not discount the possibility that some of these five SMGs have extreme dust extinction levels, as seen in some field SMGs (cite: Simpson15) and that their stellar masses are sizeable, implying similar gas fractions to the other SMGs." (Rest-frame optical properties of the SPT2349-56 core)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): At z = 4.3, Spitzer IRAC 3.6 and 4.5 um bands probe rest-frame ~6800 A and ~8500 A (and 3.6 um is contaminated by H-alpha emission), missing the rest-frame 1.6 um stellar bump; stellar mass estimates therefore carry significant systematic uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The central trio (sources B, C, and G) is blended within 2 arcsec in the IRAC bands, so attributing all IRAC flux to source C relies on the assumed Ks - 3.6 um colour range of the other SMGs.
