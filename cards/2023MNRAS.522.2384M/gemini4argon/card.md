- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, star_formation
- Question: Investigate the gas in the disk of VLA 1623W and its dynamical interaction with the other protostellar sources in the VLA 1623 multiple protostellar system using ALMA 1.3 mm continuum and C18O (2-1) observations.  
  > "In this paper, we report ALMA observations of C$^{18}$O (2–1) and continuum emission at 1.3 mm used to investigate the gas in the disk of VLA 1623W, and the source dynamical interaction with the other protostellar sources in the VLA 1623 multiple protostellar system." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Fit 1.3 mm continuum emission to measure source positions, sizes, PAs, and fluxes**: Two-dimensional elliptical Gaussian fitting on the 12-m + 7-m 1.3 mm continuum image  
  > "We fit the continuum emission towards VLA 1623A, B, and W with the CASA task *imfit*, which perform a two-dimensional elliptical Gaussian fit." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - assumption: Elliptical Gaussian morphology for disk continuum; disk inclination derived from fitted aspect ratio (i ~ 80 deg for W)  
  > "For source W, we find that the disk is almost edge-on (inclination $i \simeq$ 80$^{\circ}$), and has a diameter of $\sim 93$ au." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - fitting: 2D elliptical Gaussian fit via CASA task imfit  
  > "We fit the continuum emission towards VLA 1623A, B, and W with the CASA task *imfit*, which perform a two-dimensional elliptical Gaussian fit." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - validation: Compared fitted parameters with previous 0.9 mm ALMA measurements by Harris et al. (2018)  
  > "The results of the fit are in agreement with those obtained by (cite: Harris2018) at 0.9 mm." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
2. **Estimate disk dust and total masses from 1.3 mm continuum flux**: Analytic optically thin isothermal dust mass calculation from integrated 1.3 mm continuum flux: M_dust = (F_1.3mm * d^2) / (kappa_nu * B_nu(T_dust))  
  > "From the integrated intensity at 1.3 mm we estimate the mass of dust in the disks, $M_{\rm dust}$, as (cite: Hildebrand1983; Beckwith1990): $$\begin{equation} M_{dust} = \frac{F_{1.3~mm} d^{2}}{\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}\left(T_{dust}\right)}. \label{eq:quadratic} \end{equation}$$" (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - assumption: Isothermal conditions and optically thin dust emission  
  > "We assume isothermal conditions and optically thin emission, dust opacity ($\kappa_{\nu}$) at 1.3 mm of 2.17 cm$^{2}$g$^{-1}$ (Zamponi et al. submitted), and dust temperature, $T_{\rm dust}$, between $20$ K, the typical value assumed for Class II disks (cite: Beckwith1990), and 50 K, to account for possible warmer dust in Class 0 and I disks (cite: Zamponi2021)." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - assumption: Dust opacity at 1.3 mm kappa_nu = 2.17 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "We assume isothermal conditions and optically thin emission, dust opacity ($\kappa_{\nu}$) at 1.3 mm of 2.17 cm$^{2}$g$^{-1}$ (Zamponi et al. submitted)," (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - assumption: Dust temperature T_dust between 20 K and 50 K  
  > "and dust temperature, $T_{\rm dust}$, between $20$ K, the typical value assumed for Class II disks (cite: Beckwith1990), and 50 K, to account for possible warmer dust in Class 0 and I disks (cite: Zamponi2021)." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - assumption: Distance d = 131 pc  
  > "VLA1623 is located in Ophiuchus A at a distance, $d$, of 131 pc (cite: Gagne2018)," (Introduction)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio of 100  
  > "For source W, assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100, we obtain a total disk mass of $6\pm3 \times 10^{-3}$ M$_{\odot}$." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived disk mass of source W with Sadavoy et al. (2019)  
  > "The difference with the estimate obtained by (cite: Sadavoy2019) in the same ALMA band ($1 \times 10^{-2}$ M$_{\odot}$) is due to the 10% uncertainty on the flux calibration and the different assumed distance, dust opacity and temperature." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
3. **Map spatial distribution and kinematics of C18O (2-1) emission in disk and streamers**: Generate channel maps, moment 0, and moment 1 maps for low velocities (+0.2 to +3.0 km/s using 12-m + 7-m data) and high velocities (-2.6 to +5.8 km/s using 12-m only data)  
  > "The data analysis was carried out using the IRAM-GILDAS software package." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Figure 1 (panels c & d) shows the moment 0 and moment 1 maps of C$^{18}$O (2–1) emission towards VLA 1623W integrated up to high-velocities (from –2.6 km s$^{-1}$ to +5.8 km s$^{-1}$). In this case, only the combined 12-m array is used to minimise contamination from the large scale emission." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W); "Both figures use the 12-m + 7-m dataset." (The streamers connecting VLA 1623W with the A+B system)
   - software: IRAM-GILDAS
   - parameter: Low velocity range: +0.2 to +3.0 km/s; High velocity range: -2.6 to -0.4 km/s and +3.6 to +5.8 km/s (overall -2.6 to +5.8 km/s)  
  > "At low velocities, i.e. between $+0.2$ km s$^{-1}$ and $+3.0$ km s$^{-1}$, C$^{18}$O emission extends on large scales ($>$ $2\ensuremath{''}$ from the continuum peak of VLA 1623W)" (C$^{18}$O (2–1) emission); "In contrast, the emission at high velocities, i.e. between $-2.6$ and $-0.4$ km s$^{-1}$, and $+3.6$ and $+5.8$ km s$^{-1}$, is compact ($<1$ $\ensuremath{''}$ from the VLA 1623W continuum peak)" (C$^{18}$O (2–1) emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate dynamical mass of VLA 1623W from high-velocity C18O disk kinematics**: Measure radial peak distances of symmetric high-velocity C18O channels and apply Keplerian rotation formula M_dyn = r * V^2 / G  
  > "We therefore use the emission in the channels at radial velocities of $\pm$ 2.8 and $\pm$ 3.0 km s$^{-1}$ to derive an estimate of the VLA 1623W dynamical mass, $M_{\rm dyn}$. The emission in these channels peak at a radial distance of 0.37$''$ and 0.33$''$. By assuming Keplerian motion we estimate a dynamical mass, $$\begin{equation} M_{dyn} = r \frac{V^{2}}{G} \end{equation}$$ where $r$ and $V$ are the distance and velocity of the blue- and red-shifted peaks, deprojected for the disk inclination of 80$\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W)
   - software: IRAM-GILDAS
   - parameter: Channels at radial velocities of +/- 2.8 and +/- 3.0 km/s with respect to V_sys = +1.6 km/s, peaking at 0.37'' and 0.33''  
  > "We therefore use the emission in the channels at radial velocities of $\pm$ 2.8 and $\pm$ 3.0 km s$^{-1}$ to derive an estimate of the VLA 1623W dynamical mass, $M_{\rm dyn}$. The emission in these channels peak at a radial distance of 0.37$''$ and 0.33$''$." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation, disk inclination of 80 deg, systemic velocity V_sys(W) = +1.6 km/s, distance d = 131 pc  
  > "The moment 1 map indicates that the systemic velocity of VLA 1623W is V$_{\rm sys}$(W) $\simeq$ +1.6 km s$^{-1}$," (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W); "By assuming Keplerian motion we estimate a dynamical mass, $$\begin{equation} M_{dyn} = r \frac{V^{2}}{G} \end{equation}$$ where $r$ and $V$ are the distance and velocity of the blue- and red-shifted peaks, deprojected for the disk inclination of 80$\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Selected only channels with velocities >= +/- 2.8 km/s where blue- and red-shifted emission is symmetric and uncontaminated by extended streamers  
  > "Indeed, we find that the blue-shifted disk side is brighter than the red-shifted one up to velocities of $\pm 2.6$ km s$^{-1}$ with respect to systemic, likely due to contamination from the extended streamers observed at larger scales (see Sect. 0.0.1). At higher velocities the emission from each disk side is symmetric." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W)
5. **Estimate relative proper motions between VLA 1623W and VLA 1623A/B**: Compare angular offsets and separations between source W and sources A and B across a 27.7-year baseline using 1991.8 VLA X-band (3.6 cm) and 2019.5 ALMA Band 6 (1.3 mm) continuum positions  
  > "In order to test the ejection scenario, we estimate the proper motions of VLA 1623W with respect to VLA 1623A and B by combining our FAUST ALMA Band 6 observations of the continuum at 1.3 mm, taken in 2019, with the data at 3.6 cm, taken with the VLA in the X-band in 1991 ((cite: Andre1993), project AB817). We measured the separation between A and W, and between B and W, in the VLA-X (1991.8) and ALMA-B6 (2019.5) images, which are separated by 27.7 years." (Proper motions between 1991.8 and 2019.5)
   - parameter: Time baseline of 27.7 years between 1991.8 and 2019.5  
  > "We measured the separation between A and W, and between B and W, in the VLA-X (1991.8) and ALMA-B6 (2019.5) images, which are separated by 27.7 years." (Proper motions between 1991.8 and 2019.5)
   - assumption: Distance d = 131 pc  
  > "VLA1623 is located in Ophiuchus A at a distance, $d$, of 131 pc (cite: Gagne2018)," (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived proper motions with estimates by Harris et al. (2018) from 2013 and 2016 ALMA epochs  
  > "These measurements are in agreement with the proper motions estimated by (cite: Harris2018) based on ALMA observations taken in 2013 (cite: Murillo2013) and 2016 but are affected by a lower uncertainty given the larger distance between the two epochs." (Proper motions between 1991.8 and 2019.5)

## Qualitative findings

- C18O (2-1) emission reveals for the first time the molecular gas in the edge-on disk of VLA 1623W, showing Keplerian rotation on 50-100 au scales.  
  > "The C$^{18}$O emission reveals, for the first time, the gas in the disk-envelope of VLA 1623W." (Abstract); "Specifically, C$^{18}$O (2-1) emission probes the following structures: (i) the molecular gas in the disk of VLA 1623W with Keplerian motion on $50-100$ au scales, constraining the protostellar mass (M$_{dyn}\sim0.45$ M$_{\odot}$);" (Discussion: On the origin of VLA 1623W)
- Three C18O streamers (N, S1, S2) extending over >1000 au spatially and kinematically connect the VLA 1623A+B envelope and outflow cavities with the disk of VLA 1623W.  
  > "Three streamers connecting VLA 1623A and B with VLA 1623W are observed: one in the northern VLA1623 region, labeled as N, the other two in the southern region labeled as S1 and S2 and detected on velocities of [$+1.2$, $+1.8$] km s$^{-1}$, and [$+2.0$, $+2.8$] km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (The streamers connecting VLA 1623W with the A+B system); "(ii) streamers which extend on scales $> 1000$ au and connect spatially and kinematically the two sides of the edge-on disk of VLA 1623W (PA$_{\rm disk} \sim 10^\circ$, $i\sim 80^\circ$, $M_{\rm disk} \sim 6\times10^{-3}$ M$_{\odot}$) with the envelope associated with VLA 1623A and B." (Discussion: On the origin of VLA 1623W)
- The formation of VLA 1623W in an adjacent Ophiuchus A ridge is unlikely because its systemic velocity (+1.6 km/s) differs from that of the ridges (+3 to +4 km/s) and VLA 1623AB (+3.8 km/s).  
  > "However, the maps by (cite: Chen2018) indicate that the systemic velocity of the two adjacent ridges is similar (both being between +3 and +4 km s$^{-1}$), while our observations indicate that VLA 1623W has a systemic velocity of $+1.6$ km s$^{-1}$, which differs from the systemic velocity of VLA 1623AB ($+3.8$ km s$^{-1}$) and the two ridges. Thus, this first scenario is unlikely." (Hypothesis 1: formation in a separate core)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 6 continuum and C18O (2-1) observations combining 12-m (C43-4, C43-1) and 7-m ACA arrays simultaneously resolve the ~50 au disk kinematics and recover extended streamer emission up to ~3000 au.  
  > "(2) combining the data taken with the 12-m and the 7-m arrays to recover emission extending up to $\sim 3000$ au" (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Why this method: Uniform weighting (robust = -2.0) was chosen for continuum to achieve highest angular resolution, whereas robust = 0.5 was chosen for C18O to optimize signal-to-noise; high-velocity channels (>= 2.8 km/s) on the 12-m-only cube were used for dynamical mass estimation to avoid contamination from large-scale streamers.  
  > "We adopted Briggs weighting with a robustness parameter of $-2.0$ (uniform weighting) for the continuum to obtain the highest angular resolution (beam: 0.42$''$ $\times$ 0.32$''$, PA=$-65^\circ$). On the other hand, a robustness parameter of $0.5$ was employed for the molecular emission to optimize the signal-to-noise, consistently with the previous FAUST paper on VLA 1623 (cite: Ohashi2022)." (Observations and Data Reduction); "In this case, only the combined 12-m array is used to minimise contamination from the large scale emission." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W); "Indeed, we find that the blue-shifted disk side is brighter than the red-shifted one up to velocities of $\pm 2.6$ km s$^{-1}$ with respect to systemic, likely due to contamination from the extended streamers observed at larger scales (see Sect. 0.0.1). At higher velocities the emission from each disk side is symmetric. We therefore use the emission in the channels at radial velocities of $\pm$ 2.8 and $\pm$ 3.0 km s$^{-1}$ to derive an estimate of the VLA 1623W dynamical mass, $M_{\rm dyn}$." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W)
- Alternative: Rejected that elongated C18O emission North and South of VLA 1623W is a jet or outflow, because it is aligned along the disk PA rather than perpendicular to it.  
  > "Moreover, C$^{18}$O shows emission towards the VLA 1623W disk continuum, as well as an elongated structure to the North and to the South of W, roughly along the disk position angle. This elongated emission cannot be due to a jet or an outflow as it is not perpendicular to the disk PA." (C$^{18}$O (2–1) emission)
- Alternative: Considered and deemed unlikely that VLA 1623W formed in a separate adjacent Ophiuchus A ridge interacting gravitationally with VLA 1623AB, because the systemic velocity of W (+1.6 km/s) differs from that of both adjacent ridges (+3 to +4 km/s) and VLA 1623AB (+3.8 km/s).  
  > "However, the maps by (cite: Chen2018) indicate that the systemic velocity of the two adjacent ridges is similar (both being between +3 and +4 km s$^{-1}$), while our observations indicate that VLA 1623W has a systemic velocity of $+1.6$ km s$^{-1}$, which differs from the systemic velocity of VLA 1623AB ($+3.8$ km s$^{-1}$) and the two ridges. Thus, this first scenario is unlikely." (Hypothesis 1: formation in a separate core)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution of the 1.3 mm continuum (~0.42'' x 0.32'') is too low to resolve the close binary components VLA 1623A1 and A2 (~30 au separation).  
  > "The protostellar sources A, B, and W are detected, as well as the circumbinary disk, but the angular resolution is too low to disentangle the close binary components, A1 and A2, resolved by (cite: Harris2018) at 0.9 mm (separation $\sim 30$ au)." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
- Limitation (authors): Derived disk mass is subject to large uncertainties from assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100 (upper limit if lower) and optically thin dust emission (lower limit if optically thick), plus 10% flux calibration uncertainty and temperature range (20-50 K).  
  > "Moreover, the derived disk mass is affected by large uncertainty due to: (i) the assumption on the gas-to-dust ratio. If this is lower than 100 (cite: Ansdell2016), the estimated disk mass should be regarded as an upper limit; (ii) the assumption that the dust continuum emission is optically thin. If the emission is optically thick, the estimated disk mass is a lower limit." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm)
- Limitation (authors): Low-velocity C18O emission towards the disk of VLA 1623W (up to +/- 2.6 km/s from systemic) is contaminated by extended emission from the streamers and/or residual envelope.  
  > "Indeed, we find that the blue-shifted disk side is brighter than the red-shifted one up to velocities of $\pm 2.6$ km s$^{-1}$ with respect to systemic, likely due to contamination from the extended streamers observed at larger scales (see Sect. 0.0.1)." (The gas towards the disk of VLA 1623W); "is affected by the extended emission from the streamers and/or the residual envelope at low velocities." (Appendix: Channel maps of C18O (2-1) towards source W at low velocities)
- Limitation (authors): Proper motion uncertainties (~1.3-1.5 km/s) are larger than the velocity required to eject VLA 1623W over ~10'' in 10^4 yr (0.65 km/s), preventing confirmation or rejection of the ejection scenario.  
  > "However, in the ejection scenario the velocity needed for VLA 1623W to move away from A+B up to a distance of $\sim10\ensuremath{''}$ in 10$^4$ years (i.e., the typical Class 0 age) is only 0.65 km s$^{-1}$, i.e. below the uncertainty associated with our proper motion measurements ($\sim 1.3-1.5$ km s$^{-1}$)." (Proper motions between 1991.8 and 2019.5)
- Limitation (authors): Line-of-sight geometry of the southern streamer with respect to VLA 1623W is unknown, leaving it ambiguous whether the 1 km/s velocity gradient traces infall onto W or outflow from W towards A+B.  
  > "In the ejection scenario, the velocity gradient of 1 km s$^{-1}$ detected along the southern streamer indicates either material falling on VLA 1623W if the streamer is located in front (i.e. between W and the observer) or alternatively gas moving away from VLA 1623W towards A+B if the streamer is located on the other side of W." (Hypothesis 2: ejection or flyby)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The dynamical mass of VLA 1623W is estimated from peak positions in just two pairs of velocity channels (+/- 2.8 and +/- 3.0 km/s) rather than full 3D forward modeling or a position-velocity diagram fit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The edge-on disk of VLA 1623W (i ~ 80 deg) is very likely optically thick at 1.3 mm, making the optically thin dust mass formula a strong lower limit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing 3.6 cm VLA free-free/gyrosynchrotron continuum peaks with 1.3 mm ALMA thermal dust continuum peaks for proper motions assumes both wavelengths trace the exact same physical center, and that the unresolved A1+A2 binary centroid has not shifted due to orbital motion or variability.
