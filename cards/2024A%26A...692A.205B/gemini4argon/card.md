(2.1. EHT observation); "The visibilities are consistent with a circular Gaussian model with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 43 µas, resulting in a brightness temperature estimate of T b = (4.7 ± 0.8) × 10 9 K and a circularGaussiansizeestimateof(43±6) µas" (2.1. EHT observation)
   - software: EHT-HOPS, CASA
   - parameter: Circular Gaussian model  
  > "The visibilities are consistent with a circular Gaussian model with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 43 µas" (2.1. EHT observation)
   - assumption: Uncertainties dominated by absolute flux density calibration uncertainties  
  > "whereuncertainties are dominated by the absolute flux density calibration uncertainties (see EHTC 2019b)." (2.1. EHT observation)
   - fitting: Circular Gaussian fit to visibility amplitudes  
  > "The visibilities are consistent with a circular Gaussian model with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 43 µas, resulting in a brightness temperature estimate of T b = (4.7 ± 0.8) × 10 9 K and a circularGaussiansizeestimateof(43±6) µas" (2.1. EHT observation)
   - validation: Parallel CASA-based pipeline used for independent verification of EHT-HOPS calibration  
  > "that relied on a custom-built EHT-HOPS fringe-fitting and flux density calibration pipeline (Blackburn et al. 2019), as well as a parallel CASA-based pipeline used for an independent verification (Janssen et al. 2019)." (2.1. EHT observation)
2. **Model GMVA 86 GHz and VLBA 1.6-43 GHz maps with 2D Gaussian components**: Fit circular 2D-Gaussian Modelfit components in Difmap to the GMVA (6 components) and VLBA maps  
  > "Lastly, we modeled the source using circular Gaussian Modelfit components in D ifmap with six components." (2.2. GMVA observation); "To study the continuum spectrum from 22 to 230 GHz, we modeled the VLBA maps with 2D-Gaussian functions (model components) for all VLBA maps." (2.3. VLBA observations)
   - software: Difmap
   - parameter: 6 circular Gaussian components for 86 GHz GMVA  
  > "Lastly, we modeled the source using circular Gaussian Modelfit components in D ifmap with six components." (2.2. GMVA observation)
   - assumption: Conservative uncertainties on model component width equal to 1/5th of the beam size and on component position equal to 1/10th of the beam size  
  > "As a consequence, we assumed conservative uncertainties on the model component width equal to 1/5th of the beam size and on component position equal to 1/10th of the beam size, following the approach in Lister et al. (2009)." (2.3. VLBA observations)
   - fitting: Modelfit in Difmap  
  > "Lastly, we modeled the source using circular Gaussian Modelfit components in D ifmap with six components." (2.2. GMVA observation)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Align multi-frequency images from 1.5 to 86 GHz and cross-identify components**: Apply 2D-cross correlation shifts from Baczko et al. (2022) to VLBA maps, then align 43 GHz and 86 GHz by identifying the central 86 GHz component with component A15 at 43 GHz, deriving an additional shift of (0.098, 0.176) mas in DEC and RA  
  > "The VLBA maps had been aligned using 2D-cross correlation according to Baczko et al. (2022) to shift clean maps and model components of the VLBA observations." (3.1. Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz.); "By comparing the 43 and 86 GHz clean images and model fits, we identified the central 86 GHz component with component" (3.1 Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz); "Based on this assumption, we derived the shift of (0.098 and 0.176) mas in DEC and RA between component A15 at 43 GHz and 86 GHz." (3.1. Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz.)
   - parameter: Shift of (0.098 and 0.176) mas in DEC and RA between 43 GHz and 86 GHz  
  > "Based on this assumption, we derived the shift of (0.098 and 0.176) mas in DEC and RA between component A15 at 43 GHz and 86 GHz." (3.1. Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz.)
   - assumption: The central brightest feature in 43 GHz maps (A15) coincides with the central engine and the 86 GHz and 230 GHz compact emission, and free-free absorption from the torus is negligible above 43 GHz  
  > "In agreement with previous interpretations, we assume this central feature to be located at the central engine. Furthermore, we assume that the effect of free-free absorption (FFA) from the surrounding torus is negligible above 43 GHz." (3.1. Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz.); "Thus, we conclude that the compact emission observed with the EHT (from visibility analysis only; see Sect. 2) also corresponds to the central engine location, namely, A15 ." (3.1. Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified plausibility of alignment by tracing model components over frequency and fitting continuum spectra of identified components with power-law and SSA models

Aligning images from 1.5 to 86 GHz.); "To further support our successful alignment, we also applied fits to the continuum spectrum of the identified components with a simple power-law and SSA spectrum, where appropriate." 4. **Fit synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) continuum spectrum of central component A15**: Fit a basic synchrotron self-absorption spectrum (Eq. 1 and 2, Türler et al. 1999) to flux densities of component A15 at 22, 43, 86, and 230 GHz  
  > "For the fit, we employed a basic synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) spectrum following Eq. (1) (Türler et al. 1999), but leaving the optically thin and optically thick spectral indices as well as the peak frequency and brightness as free parameters" (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15)
   - parameter: 4 free parameters: optically thin spectral index (alpha_thin), optically thick spectral index (alpha_thick), peak frequency (nu_m), and peak brightness (S_m)  
  > "leaving the optically thin and optically thick spectral indices as well as the peak frequency and brightness as free parameters" (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15)
   - assumption: At 22 GHz, the central component is a blend of A15 and B6 at 43 GHz, so A15 flux density at 22 GHz is 50% of the component flux density (17.5 mJy) with 50% uncertainty; 15% flux density uncertainty at 43, 86, and 230 GHz  
  > "Assuming this component at 22 GHz is a blend of A15 and B6 at 43 GHz we can estimate the flux density of component A15 to be equal to 50% of this components flux density." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15); "This provides us with a fourth point for the spectral fitting of 17.5 mJy for component A15 at 22 GHz." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15); "As many assumptions have gone into this estimate, we have assumed a large uncertainty on this data point of 50%. For 43, 86, and 230 GHz, we adopted a typical, conservative flux density uncertainty of 15%." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15)
   - fitting: Linear regression / SSA analytical fit with 4 data points and 4 free parameters  
  > "Given the limited number of data points, equal to the number of free parameters in the SSA model, the fit does not provide robust estimates of the uncertainties of the fitted parameters." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15); "In the case of the same number of data points and free fit parameters we are not providing uncertainties of the fit parameters as these cannot be derived statistically correctly from our linear regression fit." (Table 4)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to 22 GHz flux density assumption (higher flux vs 5 mJy) and tested alternative identification of EHT/GMVA core with component B6  
  > "If our assumption of the flux density at 22 GHz is wrong and the flux density for A15 is higher, then the best fit remains about the same, with ν m ≃ 130 GHz;" (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15); "it is only if the flux density is much lower (around 5 mJy) that spectral peak goes towards lower frequencies ν m ≃ 100 GHz (further α thick ≃ 4, α thin ≃−1, and S m ≃ 0.7)." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15); "However, a second possible identification is with component B6 . In this scenario, the turn-over around 100 GHz persists; however, the overall spectrum could be fitted successfully with either a power-law or a SSA-spectrum (compare the figures on Zenodo)." (4.2. Unambiguous identification of component A15)
5. **Estimate magnetic field strength at A15 and extrapolate to the event horizon**: Calculate brightness temperature and SSA magnetic field at A15 assuming equipartition, and extrapolate to 1 R_S assuming radial power-law dependencies (poloidal B ~ d^-2 or toroidal-to-poloidal transition)  
  > "Assuming that the spectral shape around the peak results indeed from SSA, we can estimate the magnetic field at the location of A15 , assuming equipartition between the magnetic field and the non-thermal electrons (as is shown, e.g., in Baczko et al. 2016a; Kadler et al. 2004a)." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15); "Assuming a turn-over at 130 GHz, a peak brightness of 1.75 Jy, and a size equivalent to the EHT size of 43 µas, we obtained T B = 6.9 × 10 10 K. On this basis, we deduced a magnetic field of ∼1.25 G within these inner 43 µas." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15); "Assuming a purely poloidal magnetic field with B ∝ d −2 , with d being the distance to the center, we obtained an upper limit on the magnetic field at the event horizon ( 1 R S distance to the central engine) of B SSA = 2.6 × 10 4 G." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)
   - parameter: nu_m = 130 GHz, S_m = 1.75 Jy, size = 43 microarcseconds (distance d = 21.5 or 22 microarcseconds)  
  > "Furthermore, we estimated the magnetic field to be 1.25 Gauss at a distance of 22 µas from the central engine by fitting a synchrotron-self absorption spectrum to the innermost emission feature, which shows a spectral turn-over at ∼130 GHz." (abstract); "Assuming a turn-over at 130 GHz, a peak brightness of 1.75 Jy, and a size equivalent to the EHT size of 43 µas, we obtained T B = 6.9 × 10 10 K." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)

  > "Assuming that the spectral shape around the peak results indeed from SSA, we can estimate the magnetic field at the location of A15 , assuming equipartition between the magnetic field and the non-thermal electrons" (4.1. (4.1. The spectral shape of A15); "Meanwhile, a change from a toroidal B-field distribution ( B ∝ d −1 ) to a poloidal at a distance of 2 R S to the central engine results in a lower estimate of B SSA = 392 G at 1 R S ." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived magnetic field with previous lower limit from synchrotron cooling (6.7 G at 15 microarcseconds) in Baczko et al. (2016a)  
  > "Baczko et al. (2016a) derived a lower limit for the magnetic field based on synchrotron cooling of 6.7 G at a distance of 15 µas (1.5 mpc) from the central engine." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15); "Both measurements provide comparable values for the magnetic field strength assuming a toroidal magnetic field between 15 µas and 21.5 µas." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)

(2022)

   - parameter: GMVA resolution limit of half the beam size (35 microarcseconds); upper limit on distance of A15 to center = 50 microarcseconds (half EHT resolution)

## Qualitative findings

- NGC 1052 was detected for the first time at 230 GHz with the EHT on the ALMA-SPT and JCMT-SMA baselines, resolving the central region transversely to the jet axis.  
  > "For the first time, NGC 1052 was detected at 230 GHz with the EHT on the two baselines ALMA-SPT and JCMT-SMA." (5. Summary and conclusions); "From this observation, we infer a size of the emission of the central feature perpendicular to the jet axis of 43 µas, resulting in T B = 4.7 × 10 9 K." (5. Summary and conclusions)
- The 230 GHz EHT size measurement supports previous jet expansion profile studies suggesting two breaks in the collimation profile at around 3 x 10^3 R_S and 1 x 10^4 R_S.  
  > "This size estimate supports previous studies of the jets expansion profile which suggest two breaks of the profile at around 3 × 10 3 R S and 1 × 10 4 R S distances to the core." (abstract)
- The continuum spectrum of the innermost component A15 shows a spectral turnover around 130 GHz, and its spectral shape could also be explained by a combination of jet and ADAF emission.  
  > "This results in a peak frequency at 126 GHz with 1.75 Jy peak brightness, a thin spectral index of α thin = −3.7, and a thick spectral index α thick = 3.3, which exceeds the limit of 2.5 for SSA 2 ." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: Millimeter VLBI (GMVA and EHT) peers through the dense free-free absorbing torus that blocks cm wavelengths and provides the highest angular resolution to resolve the jet launching and collimation region; observations were scheduled within one week to minimize flux density and structural variability.  
  > "Observations at millimeter (mm) wavelengths peer through the absorbing structure and reveal a central bright emission feature, which is isolated from the jets at 86 GHz (Baczko et al. 2016a)." (1. Introduction); "The GMVA and EHT observations were performed close in time (within one week) to minimize effects from flux density and structural variability." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Incoherent averaging of visibility amplitudes and Gaussian model fitting were used for EHT because only two baselines (JCMT-SMA and ALMA-SPT) had significant detections; CLCAL interpolation and manual flagging in Difmap were used for GMVA due to noisy data and low source brightness.  
  > "Only a very limited number of significant detections on JCMT-SMA (intra-site baseline of ∼0.1Mλ projected length detections at S/N ∼ 20) and ALMA-SPT (long baseline of ∼4.5Gλ projected length, detections at S/N ∼ 10) was found through incoherent averaging of the visibility amplitudes (Thompson et al. 2017)." (2.1. EHT observation); "Due to the small amount of finally calibrated data self-flagging was turned off and spurious data, outliers in the Visibility plot were flagged by hand after careful inspection." (2.2. GMVA observation)
- Alternative: Identifying the 86 GHz GMVA central component and 230 GHz EHT detection with component B6 instead of A15  
  > "However, a second possible identification is with component B6 . In this scenario, the turn-over around 100 GHz persists; however, the overall spectrum could be fitted successfully with either a power-law or a SSA-spectrum (compare the figures on Zenodo)." (4.2. Unambiguous identification of component A15)
- Alternative: Alternative 22 GHz flux density values for component A15 (higher flux density or much lower flux density around 5 mJy)  
  > "If our assumption of the flux density at 22 GHz is wrong and the flux density for A15 is higher, then the best fit remains about the same, with ν m ≃ 130 GHz;" (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15); "it is only if the flux density is much lower (around 5 mJy) that spectral peak goes towards lower frequencies ν m ≃ 100 GHz (further α thick ≃ 4, α thin ≃−1, and S m ≃ 0.7)." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15)
- Alternative: Explaining the core continuum spectral shape with a combination of jet and advection dominated accretion flow (ADAF) emission rather than pure jet SSA  
  > "The spectral shape might also be explained including nonthermal emission from an ADAF, which has been considered for other LLAGNs." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15); "A visual comparison of the obtained models from these studies with our high-resolution radio data of NGC 1052 suggests that the spectral shape could be explained by a combination of jet and ADAF emission." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Very limited number of EHT detections (only JCMT-SMA and ALMA-SPT baselines) due to low source brightness, poor conditions at ALMA in the late morning at low inclination, and poor performance of individual EHT stations.  
  > "Only a very limited number of significant detections on JCMT-SMA (intra-site baseline of ∼0.1Mλ projected length detections at S/N ∼ 20) and ALMA-SPT (long baseline of ∼4.5Gλ projected length, detections at S/N ∼ 10) was found" (2.1. EHT observation); "This is due to particularly low brightness of the source at the time of the EHT observations (0.4 Jy reported by the ALMA-array; Goddi et al. 2021), poor conditions at ALMA observing in the late morning and at a low inclination" (2.1. EHT observation)
- Limitation (authors): Insufficient data quality in the 86 GHz GMVA observation makes the properties of the extended structure modelfit components uncertain and leaves the innermost feature unresolved.  
  > "The exact properties of the modelfit components of the extended structure are uncertain due to the insufficient data quality." (2.2. GMVA observation); "When remaining conservative and assuming a resolution limit of half the beam size, this component is still not resolved in any direction at 86 GHz." (4.3. Combined size of the central region)
- Limitation (authors): The SSA spectral fit for component A15 has 4 data points and 4 free parameters, so statistical uncertainties on the fitted parameters cannot be robustly derived.  
  > "Given the limited number of data points, equal to the number of free parameters in the SSA model, the fit does not provide robust estimates of the uncertainties of the fitted parameters." (3.2. Continuum spectrum of A15)
- Limitation (authors): Free-free absorption below 43 GHz and blending at 22 GHz cause a large uncertainty on the 22 GHz flux density of A15 and a steep optically thick slope exceeding the SSA limit.  
  > "However, it results in a larger uncertainty of the flux density of the innermost component at 22 GHz and in a very steep slope between 22 and 86 GHz, exceeding the limit for synchrotron self-absorption (SSA)." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)
- Limitation (authors): Sparse frequency coverage between 86 GHz and 230 GHz limits the conclusions that can be drawn from the continuum spectrum.  
  > "The sparse frequency coverage limits the conclusions which can be drawn from the available data sets." (4.1. The spectral shape of A15)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 230 GHz EHT size measurement (43 microarcseconds) is derived from visibility amplitudes on only a single long baseline (ALMA-SPT) and a single short intra-site baseline (JCMT-SMA), assuming a circular Gaussian geometry without imaging.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SSA model fit to component A15 has zero degrees of freedom (4 data points, 4 free parameters) and yields an unphysical optically thick spectral index (3.3 > 2.5) and a very steep optically thin index (-3.7), indicating that a simple homogeneous SSA slab is not a fully self-consistent physical description.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The identification of the 230 GHz EHT compact emission with the 43 GHz component A15 relies on astrometric assumptions across frequencies without phase-referenced absolute astrometry at 86 or 230 GHz.
