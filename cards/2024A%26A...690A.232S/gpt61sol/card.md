## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure compact and extended mm emission**: Use continuum maps to separate the bright variable core from the faint eastern structure; measure the diffuse flux where distinguishable and neglect its contribution to core variability.  
  > "The exceptional resolution and sensitivity of ALMA allowed us to resolve a faint diffuse structure to the east of the central bright source in the continuum maps of IC 4329A" (Millimeter variability); "We measured the flux of this diffuse spot whenever possible" (Millimeter variability); "we ignored the contribution of this constant fainter elongated structure to the mm flux." (Millimeter variability)
   - parameter: 10 daily ALMA epochs at ~100 GHz.  
  > "Within this campaign, we contemporaneously obtained 10 epochs of high-resolution mm observations with daily cadence using ALMA at $\sim$100 GHz frequency." (Conclusions)
   - parameter: 5% ALMA flux uncertainty.  
  > "Therefore, we applied the 5% error to our analysis." (mm)
   - assumption: The constant diffuse structure is negligible compared with the variable core.  
  > "Given that this value is 10 times less than the flux emitted by the bright variable core even at its minimum" (Millimeter variability); "we ignored the contribution of this constant fainter elongated structure to the mm flux." (Millimeter variability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Diffuse structure observed throughout the campaign, clearly distinguishable in five epochs; measured flux consistent within uncertainties.  
  > "this structure is observed throughout the entire campaign, though due to beam size and shape, it is clearly distinguishable in only five of the 10 epochs." (Millimeter variability); "found that it is consistent, within the uncertainties in all epochs, with a flux density of $\sim$0.6 mJy." (Millimeter variability)
2. **Construct the X-ray light curve**: Extract EPIC spectra and responses, reduce NICER and Swift/XRT data, and combine 2–10 keV observations into a 40-day light curve.  
  > "Response matrices and auxiliary response files were generated using the SAS tools RMFGEN and ARFGEN, respectively." (XMM-Newton); "The X-ray light curve, obtained with *XMM-Newton*, *NICER* and *Swift*, spanning 40 days" (Results)
   - software: SAS, EPPROC, EMPROC, edetect-chain, RMFGEN, ARFGEN, EPATPLOT, XRTPIPELINE
   - parameter: EPIC small window and thin filter mode; latest calibration files available January 2023.  
  > "The European Photon Imaging Camera (EPIC hereafter) cameras were operated in the small window and thin filter mode." (XMM-Newton); "The latest calibration files, available at January 2023, were used." (XMM-Newton)
   - parameter: Background-flare time cuts from visual inspection of 10–12 keV EPIC-pn light curves, PATTERN=0.  
  > "The choice of optimal time cuts for the flaring particle background has been performed by visually inspecting light curves created in the energy range 10–12 keV (EPIC-pn) with PATTERN=0 (single events)." (XMM-Newton)
   - parameter: Source radius 30″; source-free background radii ~50″.  
  > "The resulting optimal source extraction radius was $30\ensuremath{''}$, and the background spectra were extracted from source-free circular regions with radii of $\sim$ $50\ensuremath{''}$ for each observation." (XMM-Newton)
   - parameter: EPIC-pn binning: resolution oversampled by a factor larger than three; at least 20 counts per background-subtracted channel.  
  > "EPIC-pn spectra were binned to oversample the instrumental energy resolution by a factor larger than three and to have no less than 20 counts in each background-subtracted spectral channel." (XMM-Newton)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: EPATPLOT indicated no significant EPIC pile-up; lost EPIC exposure 0862090401 excluded.  
  > "No significant pile-up affected the EPIC data, as indicated by the SAS task EPATPLOT." (XMM-Newton); "The observation 0862090401 is not included in the analysis since, due to a problem in the ground segment, the EPIC exposure was lost." (XMM-Newton)
3. **Compare variability amplitudes**: Calculate fractional variability from sample variance minus mean square measurement error, normalised by squared mean flux; compare full light curves and sliding 10-day X-ray windows.  
  > "F_{\rm var}=\sqrt{\frac{S^2-\overline{\sigma^2}}{\overline{x_i}^2}} \times 100\%" (X-ray variability); "Even scanning the X-ray light curve with a 10-day window for a conservative comparison" (Millimeter variability)
   - parameter: Include ALMA calibration uncertainties of ~5%.  
  > "including ALMA calibration uncertainties of $\sim$5%." (Millimeter variability)
   - assumption: Ignore intraday X-ray variations for the daily-timescale analysis.  
  > "Due to the focus of the work on the variability of the longer (daily) timescale, we ignored the intraday variations in the analysis." (X-ray variability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Sliding 10-day X-ray comparisons still give lower fractional variability than mm.  
  > "$F_{\rm var}^{\rm X-ray}$ was found to be significantly lower than in the mm band, from 6.5% to 18.5%" (Millimeter variability)

## Qualitative findings

- No statistically robust daily X-ray/mm correlated variability or reliable time lag.  
  > "However, we did not find statistically robust evidence of the correlated variability, and distinct variability patterns were observed in each band." (Conclusions); "The combined analysis of the 40-day X-ray (2–10 keV) and 10-day mm (100 GHz) light curves suggests no reliable time lag." (Conclusions)
- mm spectral-slope changes do not correlate with flux.  
  > "the spectral slope $\alpha$" (Millimeter variability); "did not show a correlation with the flux." (Millimeter variability)
- NICER hardness ratio is not significantly variable and does not correlate with mm flux or spectral-index changes.  
  > "We found that the HR does not vary significantly, and it does not show any correlation with the mm flux or mm spectral index changes." (Comparison with other mm/X-ray monitoring campaigns)
- Rapid mm variability rules out heated dust and thermal free–free emission as its origin.  
  > "This rules out the heated dust and thermal free–free emission origin, indicating a synchrotron origin for the mm radiation." (Conclusions)
- A compact X-ray corona is the preferred origin, but its identification remains uncertain.  
  > "While the exact source is not yet certain, an X-ray corona scenario emerges as the most plausible compared to a scaled-down jet or outflow-driven shocks." (abstract)
- Extended, non-variable emission likely comes from the narrow-line-region ionization cone.  
  > "The measured flux and the position of the non-variable component indicate that its emission likely originates in the ionization cone of the narrow-line region." (Conclusions)
- NGC 7469 reanalysis finds no X-ray periodicity and no unambiguous time lag.  
  > "Using the wavelet analysis, we do not find any evidence of a periodicity in the X-ray light curve of NGC 7469." (Comparison with other mm/X-ray monitoring campaigns); "we cannot clearly determine the presence and magnitude of the time delay between mm and X-rays." (Comparison with other mm/X-ray monitoring campaigns)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA isolates compact mm emission; IC 4329A is bright and variable in X-rays and mm.  
  > "All these characteristics, in both the X-ray and mm band, make IC 4329A the best target for the search for correlated X-ray/mm variability." (Introduction); "sub-arcsecond observations with ALMA can play a crucial role due to its sensitivity and angular resolution to localise the compact source of the mm emission." (Introduction)
- Why this method: TDM targets continuum; wavelets examine time-dependent periods; multiple lag methods address irregular sampling.  
  > "The spectral setup is in Time-Division Mode (TDM) around 100 GHz, as we aim to detect the continuum emission of IC 4329A." (mm); "Therefore, the wavelet analysis provides both variability frequency (or period) and its evolution during the observations." (X-ray variability); "It has been shown that CCF-based methods can be less stable and efficient when the observational cadence is irregular (cite: Li19)." (X-ray – mm correlation)
- Alternative: Thermal dust and free–free origins rejected for the compact variable emission.
