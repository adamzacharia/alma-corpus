## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST NIRCam imaging and extract resolved clump and halo photometry**: Reduce NIRCam data using modified STScI pipeline, align to HST ACS, drizzle to 0.04'' pixel scale, detect sources, and fit 3 point sources (clumps) + 1 Sérsic profile (halo) in 7''x7'' cutouts across 6 bands  
  > "We perform astrometric alignment of the different exposures of JWST/ NIRCam to HST Advanced Camera for Surveys images, sky subtraction, and drizzling to a common pixel scale of 0 ¢¢ . 04 using version 1.6.0 of the grism redshift and line analysis software for space-based slitless spectroscopy (G. Brammer & J. Matharu 2021, Grizli)." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry); "We fit the central coordinates of the four components using three point sources (for the clumps) and a Sérsic profile (for the halo) in the F277W image with GALFIT (C. Y. Peng et al. 2010)." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry)
   - software: calwebb_detector1, Grizli, Photutils, GALFIT
   - parameter: jwst_0916.pmap CRDS_CTX, 0.04'' pixel scale, 7'' x 7'' cutouts  
  > "In short, we use a modified version of the Detector1Pipeline (calwebb_detector1) stage of the official STScI pipeline and jwst_0916.pmap JWST Operational Pipeline (CRDS_CTX) to reduce the NIRCam data." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry); "We created 7″ × 7″ images centered on the galaxy in all six filters and visually determined the priors for the centers of the three clumps and the halo." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry)
   - assumption: Three clumps modeled as point sources (and later Sérsic profiles for radii of clumps 1 and 3), halo modeled as Sérsic profile; magnification μ = 1.60 +0.01/-0.02  
  > "Further, we fit Sérsic profiles for resolved clumps 1 and 3 with fixed positions and similar parameters to get the approximate radii of the clumps and the integrated galaxy." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry); "The magnification-corrected ( m = 1.60 -+ 0.020.01 ; refer to Section 2.4) radii of the four components and the galaxy are provided in Table 1." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry)
   - fitting: GALFIT with central coordinates fixed from F277W fit  
  > "These central coordinates were then used as fixed priors to repeat the process in all six filters and extract the photometry of the four components." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry)
   - validation: Injected model into 100 random positions in 7'' x 7'' images and refitted with same GALFIT settings; found no significant systematic offset  
  > "To derive the uncertainty in our flux estimation, we injected the model of MACS0416-Y1 into 100 random positions in our 7″ × 7″ images and refitted them with the same GALFIT settings." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry); "We found no significant systematic offset between the fitted and injected fluxes of any component, demonstrating the robustness of our photometric technique." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry)

   - fitting: Gaussian fit along spatial axis for 1D extraction; spline continuum model and emission line fits in msaexp

NIRSpec Spectroscopy); "We fit each 1D spectrum to a spline model using msaexp to create the continuum model and extract fluxes, velocity offsets, and widths of emission lines from the continuum-subtracted spectrum (Figure 2(b))." 5. **Fit cold dust SED to ALMA continuum detections and upper limits**: Fit a Modified Blackbody (MBB) function plus nonthermal radio contribution to detections at 465 and 353 GHz and forced to reach the 3σ upper limit at 200 GHz, at fixed dust temperatures (T_dust = 60 K and 80 K)  
  > "We perform a fit of the cold dust spectral energy distribution (SED) using the continuum emission detected at 465 and 353 GHz from Y. Tamura et al. (2019) and the stringent 3σ upper limit at 200 GHz from T. J. L. C. Bakx et al. (2020)." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting); "Given that the lack of data in the far-infrared prevents us from estimating the dust temperature (T dust ), we create models using two free parameters, the dust mass (M dust ) and the dust emissivity index (β), at different fixed T dust ." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting); "Furthermore, we force the model to reach the flux of the 200 GHz upper limit." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
   - software: EMCEE
   - parameter: T_dust fixed at 60 K and 80 K; MCMC with 20 chains, 2000 trials, burn-in phase of ~100  
  > "Figure 2(c) shows the best-fitting model with T dust = 60 K, resulting in β = 2.6 and μM dust = 2.8 × 10 5 M e, and μSFR = 78 M e yr −1" (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting); "The bestfitting model with T dust = 80 K, having β = 2.3 and μM dust = 3.4 × 10 5 M e, yields μSFR = 167 M e yr −1" (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting); "The best-fit models have been obtained from MCMC with 20 chains, 2000 trials, and a burn-in phase of ∼100." (Appendix B)
   - assumption: k_0 = 0.45 cm^2 g^-1 at 250 GHz (Beelen et al. 2006), T_0 = 2.73 K for CMB heating/background, Chabrier (2003) IMF, continuum mean size from Tamura et al. (2023), nonthermal radio contribution following Algera et al. (2021)  
  > "with β the emissivity index and k 0 = 0.45 cm 2 g −1 the mass absorption coefficient (A. Beelen et al. 2006). The solid angle is estimated using the continuum mean size from Y. Tamura et al. (2023)." (Appendix B); "Therefore, we assume a small radio contribution and model it as done in G. C. Jones et al. (2024b), adopting the nonthermal emission model of H. S. B. Algera et al. (2021)." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
   - fitting: MCMC via EMCEE with uniform priors: 3 < log(M_dust / M_sun) < 9, 1.0 < β < 5.0  
  > "We explore the 2D parameter space using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm implemented in the EMCEE package (D. Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013). We assume uniform priors for the fitting parameters: 3 < log( M dust / M  ) < 9, 1.0 < β < 5.0." (Appendix B)
   - validation: Compared implied SFR from dust model against SFR implied by [C II] and [O III] emission lines (~60-100 M_sun yr^-1) and rejected T_dust < 60 K due to nonphysical β > 2.6  
  > "We find that fixing T dust at values <60 K results in nonphysical values of β (β - 2.6) (β = 1.6 typically found in star-forming and QSO's host galaxies; see e.g., J. Witstok et al. 2023; R. Tripodi et al. 2024)." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting); "The bestfitting model with T dust = 80 K, having β = 2.3 and μM dust = 3.4 × 10 5 M e, yields μSFR = 167 M e yr −1, which is a factor of 1.7 higher than the upper bound for SFR from emission lines." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
6. **Perform optical-NIR SED and spectrophotometric fitting to infer stellar population properties and SFHs**: Fit resolved and integrated photometry with DENSE BASIS (nonparametric SFH), and fit integrated NIRCam photometry + scaled NIRSpec spectrum with Bagpipes (double power-law and Iyer et al. 2019 nonparametric SFHs)  
  > "First, we use DENSE BASIS (K. G. Iyer et al. 2019) to infer the stellar population properties of each of the resolved components by fitting the resolved photometry." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting); "We also perform a spectrophotometric fit to the integrated NIRCam photometry and NIRSpec spectroscopy using Bagpipes (A. C. Carnall et al. 2018, 2019). The spectrum is scaled to the photometry to account for slit losses." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting)
   - software: DENSE BASIS, Bagpipes
   - parameter: Fixed z = 8.31 in Bagpipes; parameter ranges and priors listed in Table A1  
  > "The Bagpipes fit is fixed to the spectroscopic redshift of 8.31 and assumes a double power-law star formation history (SFH), D. Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation, and G. Chabrier (2003) IMF." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting); "We list the parameters used for DENSE BASIS and Bagpipes SED fitting of resolved and integrated photometry in Table A1." (Appendix A)
   - assumption: Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation curve, Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "V. Markov et al. (2023, 2024) find that the dust attenuation curves of galaxies at z  8 are Calzetti like; thus, we use DENSE BASIS with the D. Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation and G. Chabrier (2003) IMF." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting)
   - fitting: Priors in Table A1: DENSE BASIS log M*/Msun (5, 11) flat, SFR (-2, 5) flat sSFR, Z (-1, 2) flat, z (8.29, 8.33) flat, Av (0, 2) exp; Bagpipes dblplaw tau (0, 15) flat, alpha (0.01, 10000) log10, beta (0.01, 1000) log10, log M*/Msun (6, 11) flat, Z (0.1, 2) flat, Av (0, 2) flat, log(U) = -2  
  > "Bagpipes and DENSE BASIS Model Parameters" (Table A1)
   - validation: Compared Bagpipes double power-law SFH results with nonparametric SFH from Iyer et al. (2019) and found similar results  
  > "We also performed a fit with the nonparametric SFH from K. G. Iyer et al. (2019) to enable a more direct comparison with DENSE BASIS and found similar results to the double power-law SFH." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting)
7. **Derive ISM physical properties and AGN diagnostics from optical and FIR emission line ratios**: Correct emission lines for dust using Hβ/Hδ and Calzetti law, deblend [O III]λ4363 from Hγ, compute T_e([O III]), ξ_ion, electron density (with PyNeb and ALMA [O III] 88 μm), oxygen abundance, ionization parameter, and compare line ratios on O32-R23, R2-R3, and [O III]λ4363 diagnostic diagrams  
  > "We follow the Calzetti extinction law (D. Calzetti et al. 2000) and use the ratio of Hβ/ Hδ to correct the observed line fluxes." (3.2. Physical Properties with Emission Line Ratios); "We calculate the expected Hγ flux based on the Hβ/ Hδ ratio and subtract the same from the combined [O III ]λ4363+Hγ flux to estimate the [O III ]λ4363 flux." (3.2. Physical Properties with Emission Line Ratios); "Next, we measure the electron density (≠) of MACS0416Y1 using the ratio [O III ]λ88 μm/[O III ]λ4960, 5008. We use the L [O III]λ88 μm = 0.66 ± 0.16 Jy km s −1 from Y. Tamura et al. (2019). Using PyNeb, assuming electron temperature T e = 18000 ± 4000 K" (3.2. Physical Properties with Emission Line Ratios)
   - software: PyNeb, MAPPINGS
   - assumption: Calzetti et al. (2000) extinction law, Case B recombination Hβ/Hδ = 3.78 for T_e = 1.6 x 10^4 K, T_e = 18000 ± 4000 K in PyNeb, solar oxygen abundance 12 + log(O/H) = 8.6

## Qualitative findings

- MACS0416-Y1 is resolved into three star-forming clumps and an underlying galaxy halo, all undergoing a simultaneous burst of star formation in the last 150 Myr while having formed 50% of their stars in the last 300 Myr, indicating a late-stage merger.  
  > "The galaxy is clearly resolved into three separate clumps and a smooth component (halo; Figures 1(b), (d))." (2.1. Imaging and Resolved Photometry); "All components have formed 50% of their stars in the last 300 Myr. All components are also undergoing a simultaneous burst of star formation in the last 150 Myr (bottom-right panels in Figure 1)." (3.1. Resolved SFRs and Histories); "The simultaneous burst of star formation in the galaxy components indicate that MACS0416-Y1 is a late-stage merger." (3.1. Resolved SFRs and Histories)
- UV emission is spatially offset from the ALMA dust continuum, indicating dust-obscured star formation.  
  > "The UV emission is offset from the dust continuum showing dust obscured star formation in the galaxy." (Figure 1 caption)
- Despite being considerably more gas-rich than cosmic noon and local galaxies, MACS0416-Y1 exhibits a star formation efficiency comparable to cosmic noon galaxies.  
  > "While MACS0416-Y1 is considerably more gas rich compared to the local and cosmic noon galaxies, it has comparable SFR surface density for a given gas mass surface density." (3.1. Resolved SFRs and Histories)
- Emission line diagnostics indicate an evolved, metal-rich ISM similar to z ~ 2 star-forming galaxies and tentative evidence for a narrow-line AGN or high-velocity shocks (>1000 km s^-1), with no broad-line component detected in Hβ.  
  > "The emission line ratios of the galaxy indicate an evolved interstellar medium similar to z ∼ 2 star-forming galaxies." (Abstract); "Further, we find possible presence of ionization from an active galactic nucleus (AGN) using emission line diagnostics; however, we do not detect a broad-line component in the Hβ emission line." (Abstract); "We find that the measured position of MACS0416-Y1 on the R2-R3 diagram can be described by a shock velocity of >1000 km s −1 ." (3.3. Emission Line Diagnostics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Deep CANUCS JWST NIRCam imaging and NIRSpec spectroscopy provide a resolved rest-frame UV and optical view of the stellar clumps and ISM, while unpublished ALMA Band 8 observations at 465 GHz add higher-frequency dust continuum constraints.  
  > "In this Letter, using the CANUCS NIRCam + NIRSpec rest-frame UV + optical observations along with existing observations from ALMA, we present the resolved ISM and stellar properties of MACS0416-Y1." (1. Introduction); "The availability of additional ALMA B8 observations allows us to better constrain the dust properties of MACS0416-Y1 compared to existing studies." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
- Why this method: Used Calzetti dust attenuation because Markov et al. (2023, 2024) found z >= 8 galaxies have Calzetti-like attenuation; used R2-R3 diagram due to absence of [N II], [S II], and [O I]; fixed T_dust in MBB fitting because lack of far-IR data prevents fitting T_dust directly.  
  > "Given that the lack of data in the far-infrared prevents us from estimating the dust temperature (T dust ), we create models using two free parameters, the dust mass (M dust ) and the dust emissivity index (β), at different fixed T dust ." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting); "V. Markov et al. (2023, 2024) find that the dust attenuation curves of galaxies at z  8 are Calzetti like; thus, we use DENSE BASIS with the D. Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation and G. Chabrier (2003) IMF." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting); "The R2-R3 diagram is also a useful diagnostic, especially in the absence of the typical diagnostic lines like [N II ], [S II ], and [O I ]." (3.3. Emission Line Diagnostics)
- Alternative: Excluded the 260 GHz upper limit from Tamura et al. (2019) in cold dust SED fitting because it is shallower than the other observations and cannot further constrain dust properties.  
  > "We do not use the upper limit at 260 GHz found in Y. Tamura et al. (2019) since it is shallower compared to the other observations (see the gray triangle in Figure 2(c)), and therefore, it cannot put further constraints on the dust properties." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
- Alternative: Rejected fixed dust temperatures T_dust < 60 K because they require nonphysical dust emissivity indices (β > 2.6).  
  > "We find that fixing T dust at values <60 K results in nonphysical values of β (β - 2.6) (β = 1.6 typically found in star-forming and QSO's host galaxies; see e.g., J. Witstok et al. 2023; R. Tripodi et al. 2024)." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
- Alternative: Tested both double power-law SFH and Iyer et al. (2019) nonparametric SFH in Bagpipes spectrophotometric fitting.  
  > "We also performed a fit with the nonparametric SFH from K. G. Iyer et al. (2019) to enable a more direct comparison with DENSE BASIS and found similar results to the double power-law SFH." (2.6. Optical–Near-infrared SED Fitting)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Lack of far-infrared data prevents directly fitting the dust temperature as a free parameter.  
  > "Given that the lack of data in the far-infrared prevents us from estimating the dust temperature (T dust ), we create models using two free parameters, the dust mass (M dust ) and the dust emissivity index (β), at different fixed T dust ." (2.5. Cold Dust Spectral Energy Distribution Fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Molecular gas mass estimated from L[C II] may be related to star formation and thus overestimated during an ongoing starburst.  
  > "We note that the molecular gas mass estimated from L[C II ] may be related to star formation and thus SFE (J. Kennicutt 1998). The ongoing star formation burst may lead to an overestimation of the molecular gas mass." (3.1. Resolved SFRs and Histories)
- Limitation (authors): [O III]λ4363 is blended with Hγ at NIRSpec prism resolution (R=100) and had to be estimated by subtracting the expected Hγ flux.  
  > "In the NIRSpec prism data, [O III ]λ4363 is blended with Hγ. We calculate the expected Hγ flux based on the Hβ/ Hδ ratio and subtract the same from the combined [O III ]λ4363+Hγ flux to estimate the [O III ]λ4363 flux." (3.2. Physical Properties with Emission Line Ratios)
- Limitation (authors): Low spectral resolution (R=100) of the NIRSpec prism cannot resolve broad-line AGN components or clump kinematics; higher-resolution and spatially resolved spectroscopy is required.  
  > "Further exploration of the gas kinematics for MACS0416-Y1 and the detection of a broad-line component using higher-resolution spectroscopy are required to make an inference in the existence of an AGN in MACS0416-Y1." (3.4. Is MACS0416-Y1 a Possible Active Galactic Nucleus?); "Spatially resolved spectroscopy of MACS0416-Y1 is required to study the kinematics of the three resolved clumps to further strengthen the evidence for merger." (4. Discussion and Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Forcing the Modified Blackbody curve to pass exactly through the 3σ upper limit at 200 GHz artificially drives the steep emissivity index (β = 2.3-2.6) and low dust mass; treating it as a probabilistic upper limit in the likelihood would yield broader posteriors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The [O III]λ4363 flux relies on deblending from Hγ in R=100 prism spectra using an assumed Case B Hγ/Hβ ratio and Hβ/Hδ dust correction, which introduces systematic uncertainty into Both T_e and the O3Hg AGN diagnostic.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Slight minor inconsistencies in reported numbers across text and tables (e.g., z = 8.312 vs 8.317 in Section 3.3/4; T_e = 18000 ± 4000 K in text vs 17634 ± 3900 K in Table 2; log U = -2.48 ± 0.03 in abstract vs ± 0.30 in Table 2; Hγ/Hβ = 1.92 in Table 2 which is likely Hβ/Hγ or Hβ/Hδ).
