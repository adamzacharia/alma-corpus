  > "The calibrated data were processed using the scriptForPI.py with the appropriate version of the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA, Bean et al. 2022) as outlined in the README files." ALMA OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION); "The continuum-subtracted visibilities were then imaged using the CASA task tclean with the hogbom algorithm for deconvolution of the point spread function (PSF), briggsbwtaper , and perchanweightdensity ."    - parameter: uvcontsub fit order = 1; tclean threshold = 2 sigma; velocity frame = BARY; continuum SPW channel width = 15.625 MHz; low-Band 3 nterms = 3; Band 3 split frequency = 110 GHz  
  > "The continuum subtraction was performed using the CASA task uvcontsub , which uses line-free channels and a linear fit of order 1 to estimate the continuum." (2.1. ## Qualitative findings

- The BH is detected in continuum at 99, 113, and 251 GHz, but not at 340 GHz where it sits between two nearby compact sources 0.217" (10 pc) and 0.526" (23 pc) away.  
  > "The two starburst regions are clearly visible in all the continuum maps; however, the central BH is not seen in the 340 GHz continuum map." (3.1. Continuum Emission and Radio SED of the Black Hole); "In the 340 GHz continuum image, the BH is located between two small continuum sources located 0 ′′ . 217 (10 pc) and 0 ′′ . 526 (23 pc) away." (3.1. Continuum Emission and Radio SED of the Black Hole)
- No sinusoidal velocity pattern is seen in the CO (3-2) or CO (1-0) position-velocity diagrams along the ionized outflow axis.  
  > "Unlike the ionized gas observed by Schutte & Reines (2022), no sinusoidal motions were discerned in either PVD." (3.2.2. Position-Velocity Diagram)
- The BH is spatially offset to the north of the molecular gas structure in its vicinity, likely due to stellar feedback from young clusters to the north, which may explain its low Eddington ratio.  
  > "The moment maps in Figure 6 clearly demonstrate that the BH (small black contour) is located north of the BH vicinity (black ellipse) in molecular gases such as CO and HCO + (3–2)." (3.2.3. Molecular Gas in the Vicinity of the BH); "This could help explain why the BH is accreting at such a low Eddington ratio ( ∼ 10 − 6 ; Reines et al. 2016)." (3.2.3. Molecular Gas in the Vicinity of the BH)
- Significant dense molecular gas (critical density >= 10^6 cm^-3) traced by HCO+ (1-0), HCO+ (3-2), and HCN (1-0) exists in the vicinity of the BH.  
  > "The detection of HCO + (1–0), HCO + (3–2), and HCN (1–0) above S/N > 5 indicate the existence of significant dense gas (with critical density ≳ 10 6 cm − 3 ) in the vicinity of the BH." (3.2.1. Moment Maps)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution multi-band ALMA archival observations trace both the sub-mm continuum and multiple molecular gas transitions (CO, HCN, HCO+) in the central ~130 pc around the BH.  
  > "Here we present the first study of the molecular gas in the immediate vicinity of the central BH using high-resolution observations with ALMA." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Used briggsbwtaper and perchanweightdensity in tclean for flat RMS and beam distribution across channels; used mtmfs (nterms=3) for low-Band 3 due to 23.7% fractional bandwidth; convolved continuum images to a common beam and aligned astrometry to 2MASS/LBA to avoid resolution and positional biases in BH photometry.  
  > "These options provide a flat root mean square (RMS) noise and beam size distribution over the channels, and the beam size comparable to that of the continuum image." (2.1. Spectral Line Image Cubes); "For the low-Band 3 data, we applied the multi-term MFS (Rau & Cornwell 2011), as its fractional bandwidth is about 23.7%" (2.2. Continuum Imaging); "Our measurements at 251 and 340 GHz had larger uncertainties, but it is important to match the beamsizes across the frequencies in order to accurately measure the spectral index." (3.1. Continuum Emission and Radio SED of the Black Hole)
- Alternative: Multi-component SED models including dust emission and/or free-free emission were tested and rejected because dust models yielded unphysical results and free-free + non-thermal models had large uncertainties and poorly constrained solutions.  
  > "The models that included dust emission did not yield physical results. The two-component model with non-thermal and freefree emission had large uncertainties and poorly constrained solutions." (3.1. Continuum Emission and Radio SED of the Black Hole)
- Alternative:  Considered that elevated R_31 at the edges could be an artifact of low CO (1-0) flux density, but rejected this as the sole cause because other peripheral regions show low R_31 and both cubes have similar sensitivities.  
  > "It is noteworthy that the elevated R 31 may be an edge effect limited by low flux densities of CO (1–0). Nevertheless, certain regions at the peripheries, particularly in the northeast and south, exhibit low R 31 values." (3.2.5. Shocked Molecular Clouds from the BH Outflow)
- How they decided it was enough: Stopped tclean when the maximum residual reached 2 sigma; restricted line ratio analysis to CO (1-0) and CO (3-2) because HCN/HCO+ (1-0) had large beams and HCO+ (3-2) had low S/N.  
  > "The cleaning process was repeated until the maximum of the residual map reached 2 σ , where σ is the RMS noise of the image cube per channel." (2.1. Spectral Line Image Cubes); "The HCN (1–0) and HCO + (1–0) line image cubes have large beam sizes, and the HCO + (3–2) line image cube has a relatively low signal-to-noise ratio, so we restricted our analysis to the line ratio between CO J=1–0 and J=3–2" (3.2.4. CO Line Ratios)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA angular resolution (>= 0.3" for lines) is at least 3x larger than the BH sphere of influence (~4 pc or 0.1") and HST/STIS resolution (~0.1"), which can cause beam smearing that obscures molecular outflow kinematics.  
  > "This may be attributed to the coarser angular resolution of our observations, leading to a beam smearing effect that obscures outflow signals amid adjacent background emissions." (3.2.2. Position-Velocity Diagram); "The ALMA observations do not have sufficient angular resolution to probe the BH sphere of influence. Given the BH mass of ∼ 10 6 M ⊙ (Reines et al. 2016; Riffel 2020), the sphere of influence is ∼ 4 pc (0 ′′ . 1), which is at least three times smaller than the angular resolution of our observations." (3.2.3. Molecular Gas in the Vicinity of the BH)
- Limitation (authors): Different interferometric spatial sensitivities and missing large-scale flux may affect marginally resolved emission (e.g., low 251 GHz measurement), and aperture flux uncertainties may be underestimated.  
  > "However, if the emission is marginally resolved, we may be missing flux density that could, for example, lead to the low measurement at 251 GHz seen in Figure 3." (3.1. Continuum Emission and Radio SED of the Black Hole); "Here, it is worth noting that the uncertainty of the flux density may be underestimated because we measured it as a mean of the RMS noise within an aperture."
