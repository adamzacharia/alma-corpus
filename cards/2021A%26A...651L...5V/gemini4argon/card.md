   - software: CASA, uvcontsub
   - parameter: first order polynomial for uvcontsub  
  > "The continuum is subtracted in the uv-plane using the CASA task uvcontsub with a first order polynomial." (2. Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Line imaging, spectral extraction, and moment map creation**: Image lines with tclean using natural weighting (and Briggs robust=0.5 for brightest lines). Extract integrated spectra and create zero- and first-moment maps using Keplerian masking; integrate spectra over velocity profiles (-2 to 12 km s^-1) to measure disk-integrated fluxes.  
  > "Each line was imaged using the tclean task at the channel resolution, using natural weighting." (2. Observations); "Spectra are extracted from the naturally weighted cubes using Keplerian masking (using d =135 pc, i =50 ◦ and M ∗ =2.0 M ⊙ ) and presented in Figure A.1." (2. Observations); "Zero-moment maps are created using Keplerian masking and presented in Figure A.2." (2. Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Keplerian mask parameters: d=135 pc, i=50 deg, M*=2.0 M_sun, v_source=4.55 km s^-1  
  > "Spectra are extracted from the naturally weighted cubes using Keplerian masking (using d =135 pc, i =50 ◦ and M ∗ =2.0 M ⊙ ) and presented in Figure A.1." (2. Observations); "The disk-integrated spectra are resolved even at our low spectral resolution, ranging from -2 to 12 km s −1 with v source =4.55 km s −1 ." (2. Observations)
   - parameter: Shared flux of blended p-H2CO 5_{4,2}-4_{4,1} and 5_{4,1}-4_{4,0} divided by 2  
  > "The H 2 CO 5 4,2 -4 4,1 and 5 4,1 -4 4,0 fluxes are computed by dividing their shared flux by 2." (2. Observations)
   - assumption: Equal flux contribution for blended p-H2CO 5_{4,2}-4_{4,1} and 5_{4,1}-4_{4,0} transitions  
  > "The H 2 CO 5 4,2 -4 4,1 and 5 4,1 -4 4,0 fluxes are computed by dividing their shared flux by 2." (2. Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: First-moment maps and channel cubes checked for Keplerian pattern along southern disk

  > "The profiles are extracted by deprojecting the zero-moment maps assuming a position angle of 100 ◦ and an inclination of 50 ◦ (Bruderer et al. 2014)." (3. Results); "The azimuthal profile is extracted at the dust peak radius of 62 au with a radial width of 60 au and the radial profile at the peak φ of 192 ◦ East-of-North with an azimuthal width of 100 ◦ ."    - parameter: PA = 100 deg, inclination = 50 deg  
  > "The profiles are extracted by deprojecting the zero-moment maps assuming a position angle of 100 ◦ and an inclination of 50 ◦ (Bruderer et al. 2014)." (3. Results)
   - parameter: Azimuthal profile at r = 62 au (width 60 au), radial profile at phi = 192 deg E of N (width 100 deg); for 13CO, r = 35 au and phi = 269 deg  
  > "The azimuthal profile is extracted at the dust peak radius of 62 au with a radial width of 60 au and the radial profile at the peak φ of 192 ◦ East-of-North with an azimuthal width of 100 ◦ ." (3. Results); "For 13 CO, the data are extracted around the peak radius of 35 au and the peak φ of 269 ◦ ." (3. Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Rotational diagram analysis to derive column densities, rotational temperatures, and abundances**: Estimate emitting area from high-resolution Band 7 continuum, calculate upper state column densities Nu and optical depths following Loomis et al. (2018), and fit rotational diagrams using emcee (both combined and separately for ortho/para H2CO).  
  > "Using this emitting area, the optical depth τ and the column densities of individual levels N u are estimated following the excitation equations in Loomis et al. (2018)." (4. Analysis); "The column density N T and temperature T rot are estimated by fitting the rotational diagrams using the emcee package to compute the posterior distributions (ForemanMackey et al. 2013), assuming optically thin emission." (4. Analysis); "Second, we estimate the ortho-to-para ratio of H 2 CO by fitting the ortho and para lines separately, without the ortho-to-para correction of 3 in the degeneracies as used in Figure 3." (Appendix B: Rotational diagram analysis)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Emitting area = 1.4e-11 sr (from 5 sigma contour of 0.18x0.14" continuum image)  
  > "The emitting area of the high-resolution continuum is 1.4·10 −11 sr with a 5σ threshold." (4. Analysis)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin emission (tau < 0.1 for T > 100 K)  
  > "With multiple line transitions it is possible to derive the column density and excitation temperature for both molecules under the assumption of LTE and optically thin emission (or a correction for optical depth)." (4. Analysis); "All lines are optically thin with τ < 0.1 for T >100 K." (4. Analysis)
   - assumption: H2CO and CH3OH emission follow the morphology of the dust crescent  
  > "with the underlying assumption that the H 2 CO and CH 3 OH emission follow the morphology of the dust crescent." (4. Analysis)
   - assumption: Ortho-to-para ratio of H2CO = 3, A/E ratio of CH3OH = 1, N_H2 = 1.6e22 cm^-2 at 60 au  
  > "The ortho-to-para ratio of H 2 CO in the degeneracies and partition functions is taken as 3, and the A/E ratio of CH 3 OH as 1." (4. Analysis); "The gas surface density derived by van der Marel et al. (2016) at 60 au radius corresponds to N H 2 ∼ 1.6 · 10 22 cm −2, so the relative abundances of H 2 CO and CH 3 OH are ∼ 10 −8 w.r.t. H 2, consistent with previous estimates by van der Marel et al. (2014)." (4. Analysis)
   - fitting: MCMC posterior sampling using emcee  
  > "The column density N T and temperature T rot are estimated by fitting the rotational diagrams using the emcee package to compute the posterior distributions (ForemanMackey et al. 2013), assuming optically thin emission." (4. Analysis)
   - validation: Corner plots of posterior distributions inspected for convergence; LTE assumption tested with RADEX  
  > "Figure B.1 shows the corner plots showing the posterior distributions and covariances of the fit containing all line transitions, confirming the fit has converged." (Appendix B: Rotational diagram analysis); "The LTE assumption and corresponding kinetic temperature can be tested using a calculation of the balance between excitation and de-excitation using RADEX (van der Tak et al. 2007)." (4. Analysis)
5. **Non-LTE excitation analysis of line ratios using RADEX**: Compute non-LTE line ratios across a grid of kinetic temperatures and H2 densities using RADEX and compare with observed line ratios.  
  > "The LTE assumption and corresponding kinetic temperature can be tested using a calculation of the balance between excitation and de-excitation using RADEX (van der Tak et al. 2007)." (4. Analysis); "We compute line ratios for a range of temperatures and H 2 densities and a molecular column density of 10 14 cm −2 shown in Figure C.3 following van Dishoeck et al. (1995)." (4. Analysis)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Molecular column density = 10^14 cm^-2, H2 density ~ 10^6 - 10^8 cm^-3  
  > "We compute line ratios for a range of temperatures and H 2 densities and a molecular column density of 10 14 cm −2 shown in Figure C.3 following van Dishoeck et al. (1995)." (4. Analysis); "The H 2 densities in the midplane and molecular layers of IRS 48 are ∼ 10 6−8 cm −3 (Bruderer et al. 2014)." (4. Analysis)
   - assumption: Collisional rate coefficients from LAMDA database  
  > "Collisional rate coefficients are taken from the LAMDA database for the individual molecules (Rabli & Flower 2010; Wiesenfeld & Faure 2013) as summarized in Schöier et al. (2005)." (4. Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with rotational diagram temperatures  
  > "In this regime, the line ratios are only sensitive to temperature, with typical inferred values of 200±50 K for H 2 CO and 100±20 K for CH 3 OH, confirming that the H 2 CO emission originates from a warmer layer (Fig. C.3)." (4. Analysis)
6. **Physical-chemical DALI modeling of disk temperature structure and line ratios**: Run 2D axisymmetric DALI models with varying dust surface densities between 60 and 80 au for settled and full dust columns, compute gas and dust temperatures, parametrize molecular abundance layers, and raytrace lines to compare line ratios with RADEX and observations.  
  > "We set up a series of physical-chemical models using DALI (Bruderer et al. 2012; Bruderer 2013) to compute the gas and dust temperature structure from the heatingcooling balance of gas and dust, following the parametrized model of the gas and dust surface density from van der Marel et al. (2016) that is consistent with the CO 6–5 isotopologue data presented in that work." (Appendix C: Temperature structure); "This leads to specific emitting layers in the disk where H 2 CO and CH 3 OH are located, which are used to raytrace the lines to compute line ratios which can be compared with the data." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: Sigma_60au,gas = 0.25 g cm^-2; Sigma_60au,dust = 0.005, 0.05, 0.5, 5.0 g cm^-2 between 60 and 80 au; settling parameter chi = 0.1 (settled) or 0.2 (full); h_c = 0.14, psi = 0.22  
  > "As Ohashi et al. (2020) derived a much higher dust surface density of Σ d ∼2-8 g cm −2, we explore the effect of the dust surface density on the radial and vertical temperature structure, using Σ d ∼0.05, 0.5 and 5.0 g cm −2 between 60 and 80 au." (Appendix C: Temperature structure); "Surface density Σ60au,gas (g cm−2) 0.25 0.25" (Table C.1)
   - parameter: Parametrized abundance = 10^-7 and 10^-9 where A_V > 1, r > 60 au, and T_dust > T_subl (66 K for H2CO, 100 K for CH3OH); 10^-12 elsewhere  
  > "This is parametrized by setting an abundance of 10 −7 and 10 −9 (optically thick and thin, respectively) for both molecules in the regions in the disk where the extinction A V >1 (shielding for photodissociation), radius r >60 au (in the dust trap) and the dust temperature T dust - T subl, with T subl =66 K for H 2 CO and 100 K for CH 3 OH." (Appendix C: Temperature structure); "In the rest of the disk, these molecular abundances are set to 10 −12 ." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric Gaussian vertical density structure; step-function molecular abundances governed by sublimation temperature and UV shielding (A_V > 1)  
  > "The vertical density structure in DALI is fixed to a Gaussian profile with different scale heights for the large and small grains to represent the settling, following Andrews et al. (2011):" (Appendix C: Temperature structure); "Also, as DALI is an axisymmetric model, the azimuthal structure is not constrained in this model." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Raytraced line fluxes and line ratios compared with observed values and RADEX grid  
  > "The line ratios are derived and compared with the line ratios in the RADEX plot in Figure C.3." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)

- **tclean** : Imaging the molecular line channels  
  > "Each line was imaged using the tclean task at the channel resolution, using natural weighting." (2. Observations)
- **emcee** : Fitting rotational diagrams to compute posterior distributions of column density and rotational temperature  
  > "The column density N T and temperature T rot are estimated by fitting the rotational diagrams using the emcee package to compute the posterior distributions (ForemanMackey et al. 2013), assuming optically thin emission." (4. Analysis)
- **RADEX** : Calculating non-LTE excitation and de-excitation balance and expected line ratios  
  > "The LTE assumption and corresponding kinetic temperature can be tested using a calculation of the balance between excitation and de-excitation using RADEX (van der Tak et al. 2007)." (4. Analysis)
- **DALI** : 2D physical-chemical modeling of the disk gas and dust temperature structure and raytracing molecular line ratios  
  > "This is well above the gas surface density of 0.07 g cm −2 derived from CO isotopologues and DALI modelling by van der Marel et al. (2016), implying a dust-to-gas ratio ≫1." (5. Discussion and conclusions); "We set up a series of physical-chemical models using DALI (Bruderer et al. 2012; Bruderer 2013) to compute the gas and dust temperature structure from the heatingcooling balance of gas and dust, following the parametrized model of the gas and dust surface density from van der Marel et al. (2016) that is consistent with the CO 6–5 isotopologue data presented in that work." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)

## Qualitative findings

- H2CO and CH3OH line emission show a crescent morphology following the millimeter dust trap, in contrast with 13CO which shows a full disk ring.  
  > "It is immediately clear that both molecules follow the dust trap morphology (Figure 1), in contrast with 13 CO which shows a full disk ring, just like the small grains (van der Marel et al. 2013)." (3. Results)
- H2CO is azimuthally more extended than CH3OH and trails the dust trap, whereas CH3OH is similar in azimuthal width to the continuum but slightly more extended radially.  
  > "The azimuthal profiles show that H 2 CO is azimuthally more extended than CH 3 OH and is trailing the dust trap, whereas CH 3 OH is similar in width as the continuum emission." (3. Results); "In contrast, the CH 3 OH profile appears somewhat further extended outwards and based on the line wings, also inwards." (3. Results)
- The low H2CO/CH3OH ratio (~0.16) and warm temperatures (>100 K) indicate ice-dominated chemistry where icy pebbles in the cold settled midplane dust trap are vertically mixed and thermally sublimated in warmer upper layers.  
  > "Rotational diagrams and line ratios indicate that both molecules originate from warm molecular regions in the disk with temperatures >100 K and column densities ∼ 10 14 cm −2 or a fractional abundance of ∼ 10 −8 and with H 2 CO/CH 3 OH∼0.2, indicative of ice chemistry." (abstract); "Based on arguments from a physical-chemical model with low gas-to-dust ratios, we propose a scenario where the dust trap provides a huge icy grain reservoir in the disk midplane or an ‘ice trap’, which can result in high gas-phase abundances of warm COMs through efficient vertical mixing." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Oph IRS 48 is a textbook example of an asymmetric dust trap with low gas-to-dust ratio and tentative prior H2CO detection, making deep ALMA Band 7 observations ideal to detect multiple H2CO and CH3OH transitions.  
  > "The Herbig disk Oph IRS 48 is a textbook example of such a dust trap showing an asymmetric dust concentration south of the star (van der Marel et al. 2013, 2015), and thus an ideal target for studying complex organic chemistry in a pebble-concentrated environment." (1. Introduction); "We use deep ALMA Band 7 line observations to search the IRS 48 disk for H 2 CO and CH 3 OH line emission, the first steps of complex organic chemistry." (abstract)
- Why this method: Multiple transitions spanning 17 to 260 K allow rotational diagram fitting and RADEX non-LTE modeling to constrain column densities and temperatures; high-resolution continuum is used for the emitting area because the line maps are marginally resolved.  
  > "With multiple line transitions it is possible to derive the column density and excitation temperature for both molecules under the assumption of LTE and optically thin emission (or a correction for optical depth)." (4. Analysis); "As the zero-moment maps of H 2 CO and CH 3 OH are marginally resolved, the emitting area cannot be reliably determined from these images." (4. Analysis)
- Alternative: In situ continuous formation of H2CO and CH3OH through CO ice hydrogenation in the dust trap is rejected because the midplane temperature (~40 K) does not drop below the CO freeze-out temperature of 22 K.  
  > "The temperature is unlikely to be below the CO freezeout temperature of 22 K, so continuous formation of H 2 CO and CH 3 OH through CO ice hydrogenation is not possible." (5. Discussion and conclusions)
- Alternative: Azimuthal gas overdensity as the cause of the crescent COM emission is rejected because an azimuthal contrast >10 would be required, which is ruled out by optically thin C18O 6-5 emission.  
  > "It is also important to point out that the COM emission is unlikely to trace an actual azimuthal gas overdensity in the disk: considering the high SNR, the azimuthal contrast would have to be more than a factor of 10, and such a contrast was not detected in the C 18 O 6-5 isotopologue emission (van der Marel et al. 2016), which is optically thin considering the ratio of 4.3±1.4 with the C 17 O flux measured by Bruderer et al. (2014)." (5. Discussion and conclusions)
- Alternative: Additional gas-phase chemistry production to explain the larger azimuthal extent of H2CO compared to CH3OH is rejected because gas-phase production would produce H2CO emission along the entire gas ring.  
  > "This discrepancy cannot be explained by additional gas phase chemistry production, as this would result in H 2 CO emission along the entire ring." (5. Discussion and conclusions)
- Alternative: Dust density increase throughout the full vertical column ('full models') is rejected because it only reaches 80 K in the emitting layer and underestimates line fluxes by 1 to 3 orders of magnitude.  
  > "The full models reach temperature of only 80 K, not high enough to explain the observed excitation temperatures, suggesting that the high dust concentration of large dust grains must be settled to the midplane." (5. Discussion and conclusions); "The models with 10 −9 abundance and high dust density full models underestimate all fluxes by 1 to 3 orders of magnitude." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Zero-moment maps of H2CO and CH3OH are only marginally resolved, so the emitting area cannot be reliably determined directly from the line images and is assumed to match the high-resolution continuum.  
  > "As the zero-moment maps of H 2 CO and CH 3 OH are marginally resolved, the emitting area cannot be reliably determined from these images." (4. Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Transitions may trace multiple regimes with different temperatures in the disk rather than a single temperature layer.  
  > "However, it is possible that the transitions trace multiple regimes with different temperatures in the disk." (4. Analysis); "Overall, the spread in temperatures for the different ratios implies that the various lines trace different temperature regions in the disk with potentially different abundances." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)
- Limitation (authors): The low H2CO ortho-to-para ratio (<3) could be an artifact of underestimated optical depth of the ortho lines if the true emitting area is smaller than assumed, or could be reset after ice desorption.  
  > "However, if the optical depth of the ortho lines is underestimated (if the emitting area is smaller than assumed) this could also explain the lower value." (Appendix B: Rotational diagram analysis); "for H 2 O it has been shown that the ortho-to-para ratio is reset after desorption from the ices (Hama et al. 2018) and if the same is true for H 2 CO, the ortho-to-para ratio does not provide information" (Appendix B: Rotational diagram analysis)
- Limitation (authors): DALI is a 2D axisymmetric model, so the azimuthal structure of the dust trap is not constrained, and raytraced CH3OH fluxes in the simple step-function abundance models are at least a factor of 10 too low.  
  > "Also, as DALI is an axisymmetric model, the azimuthal structure is not constrained in this model." (Appendix C: Temperature structure); "The raytraced line fluxes reproduce the H 2 CO fluxes reasonably well for the settled models for the 10 −7 abundance, the CH 3 OH fluxes are at least a factor 10 too low." (Appendix C: Temperature structure)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The column densities and optical depths depend directly on the assumed emitting area (1.4e-11 sr from high-resolution continuum); if the true line emitting area is smaller by a factor of >=5, the H2CO lines become optically thick, altering both rotational temperatures and the ortho-to-para ratio.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line wings of adjacent H2CO 5_{3,3}-4_{3,2} and 5_{3,2}-4_{3,1} transitions overlap by 2 channels at the modest ~1.6 km s^-1 spectral resolution, and p-H2CO 5_{4,2}-4_{4,1} / 5_{4,1}-4_{4,0} are fully blended.
