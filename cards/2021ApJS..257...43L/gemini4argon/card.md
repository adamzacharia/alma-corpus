## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: How can we map CO(2-1) emission at cloud scales (~1 arcsec ~ 100 pc) across a representative sample of 90 nearby star-forming galaxies to measure the demographics, life-cycle, and physical state of giant molecular clouds and connect them to galaxy-scale properties?  
  > "At the resolution achieved by PHANGS–ALMA, each beam reaches the size of a typical individual giant molecular cloud (GMC), so that these data can be used to measure the demographics, life-cycle, and physical state of molecular clouds" (Abstract); "the survey was designed with the broad goals of quantifying the physics of star formation and feedback at the scale of individual giant molecular clouds (GMCs) and connecting these measurements to galaxy-scale properties and processes." (Introduction)

### Imaging

- task: CASA tclean  
  > "We imaged the staged $u{-}v$ data using the `CASA` task `tclean`." (6.1 Summary)
- deconvolver: Two-stage: first multiscale clean (down to 4 sigma residual threshold), then classic Hogbom clean (down to 1 sigma residual threshold or convergence)  
  > "We use a two stage deconvolution process. In the first stage, we used the “multiscale clean” (cite: CORNWELL08) deconvolution algorithm with no clean mask or a very extended clean mask, and we imposed a residual threshold of 4 times the rms noise in the cube." (6.1 Summary); "Within this more restrictive clean mask, we continue deconvolving using a classic “Hogbom clean” (cite: HOGBOM74). We run this second stage of deconvolution, stopping when the residual, i.e., not deconvolved emission, within the clean mask has a peak $\leq 1\sigma$" (6.1 Summary)
- weighting robust: intermediate robustness parameter ( plus empirical uv reweighting)  
  > "This step involves continuum subtraction, spectral regridding and rebinning, and an empirical reweighting of the data." (6.1 Summary); "We also use a $u{-}v$ weighting scheme with intermediate “robustness parameter.”" (6.2 Validation, known issues, and limitations)

- cell size: _not stated_
- image size: _not stated_
- masking: Stage 1: no clean mask or a very extended clean mask; Stage 2: restrictive clean mask based on emission in Stage 1 deconvolved image  
  > "In the first stage, we used the “multiscale clean” (cite: CORNWELL08) deconvolution algorithm with no clean mask or a very extended clean mask" (6.1 Summary); "Then, we construct a more restrictive clean mask based on the location of emission in the current version of the deconvolved image." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Assess sample selection accuracy and completeness via Monte Carlo simulation**: Perturb catalog values of inclination, stellar mass, star formation rate, and distance for ~15,000 nearby galaxies over 10,000 realizations (with and without distance uncertainties) and check how many meet PHANGS-ALMA selection criteria.  
  > "To assess the uncertainty associated with our sample selection, we carry out a Monte Carlo exercise. We begin with estimates of stellar mass, star formation rate, inclination, and distance to ${\sim}15{,}000$ nearby galaxies drawn from (cite: LEROY19)." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection); "We repeat the exercise twice, each time using $10{,}000$ realizations. In the first case, we impose distance uncertainties. In the second case we skip step #3, and only consider uncertainties unrelated to distance." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection)
   - parameter: Inclination uncertainty +-5 deg (capped at 90 deg); M_star uncertainty ~0.1 dex; SFR uncertainty ~0.15 dex; distance uncertainty 0.03, 0.06, or 0.125 dex  
  > "We adopted a $\pm 5\ensuremath{^\circ}$ uncertainty for the inclination, consistent with (cite: LANG20), and we cap the value at $90^\circ$. For the SFR and stellar mass, we draw the uncertainties from (cite: LEROY19). These are typically $0.1$ dex for $M_\star$ and $0.15$ dex for the SFR" (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection); "Roughly, we adopt $0.03$ dex uncertainty for TRGB distances, $0.06$ dex for other quality distances, and $0.125$ dex for other distances." (Footnote 5)
   - parameter: Selection criteria: -75 deg < delta < +25 deg, i < 75 deg, d < 17 Mpc, log10 M_star > 9.75, log10(SFR/M_star) > -11 yr^-1  
  > "Selection criteria for main sample$^{a}$" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Catalog values from Leroy et al. (2019) and EDD represent the true underlying galaxy properties  
  > "We adopt the catalog values as the “true” values. We exclude the galaxies removed from the target list by hand from any calculations." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared Monte Carlo expected sample size (76 +- 6 and 82 +- 4) with actual selected main sample size (75 galaxies)  
  > "Specifically, as listed in Table 1, we select $76$ and $82$ galaxies on average, depending on whether we randomize the distance. We selected $75$ targets for PHANGS–ALMA." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection)
2. **Estimate stellar masses and stellar mass surface density maps**: Mask foreground stars and subtract background from S4G IRAC 3.6 um or WISE1 3.4 um images, interpolate masked pixels using radial median, convolve to 7.5 arcsec, and multiply by a radially varying mass-to-light ratio predicted from 15 arcsec radial profiles of SFR-to-WISE1 luminosity smoothed with a 2 kpc kernel.  
  > "We use near-infrared maps, combined with a radially varying mass-to-light ratio, $\Upsilon_\star^{3.4}$, to estimate stellar mass and stellar disk size in our targets" (4.3 Stellar Mass); "Then, we calculate radial profiles of WISE1 emission and SFR, either from FUV+WISE4, NUV+WISE4, or only WISE4 emission as available." (Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates)
   - parameter: Annulus for IRAC background: 1.5 to 3 r25; unmasked central region: 10 arcsec (IRAC) or 0.5 r25 (WISE); resolution: 7.5 arcsec for mass map, 15 arcsec and 2 kpc kernel for M/L profile; Equation 3 parameters: a = -11.0, b = -0.375, c = -10.2  
  > "This is usually calculated using the mode in a circular annulus $1.5{-}3~r_{25}$ in radius, but we adjusted the range based on visual inspection." (4.3 Stellar Mass); "where $Q = \log_{10} {\rm SFR}/( \nu_\textrm{ W1} L_{\nu ,\textrm{ W1}})$ and $\nu_\textrm{W1}$ refers to the frequency corresponding to the $3.4\mu$m central wavelength of the WISE1 band. Here $a=-11.0$, $b=-0.375$, and $c=-10.2$." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates)
   - assumption: Chabrier (2003) IMF, Bruzual & Charlot (2003) models, and same mass-to-light ratio Upsilon_star^3.4 for 3.4 um and 3.6 um with inclination factor cos(i)  
  > "These mass calculations assume a (cite: CHABRIER03) stellar initial mass function (cite: BRUZUAL03; SALIM16; SALIM18)." (4.3 Stellar Mass); "We assume that modulo the different pre-factors, the same mass-to-light ratio $\Upsilon_\star^{3.4}$ describes both $3.4$ $\mu$m to $3.6$ $\mu$m." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates)
   - fitting: Empirical piecewise linear relation predicting Upsilon_star^3.4 from Q = log10(SFR / nu_W1 L_nu,W1)  
  > "We predict $\Upsilon_\star^{3.4}$ from an empirical fit relating $\Upsilon_\star^{3.4}$ to the local SFR-to-WISE1 color from (cite: LEROY19)" (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates)
   - validation: Compared against PHANGS-MUSE spectral synthesis stellar mass surface densities across 19 galaxies (median offset 0.08 dex, scatter 0.11 dex) and against integrated photometry, WISE1 vs IRAC1, fixed M/L, and S4G ICA maps (Table 5).  
  > "Specifically, we find a median offset of ${\sim}0.08$ dex or a factor of ${\sim}1.2$, and ${\sim}0.11$ dex or $30\%$ point-to-point scatter." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates); "Comparison Among Stellar Mass Estimates" (Table 5)
3. **Measure stellar disk sizes (effective radius Re and exponential scale length l_star)**: Construct radial profiles of stellar mass surface density at 7.5 arcsec resolution (using mean within 30 arcsec and median within +-75 deg of major axis outside 30 arcsec), subtract background outside 2 r25, integrate out to 2 r25 to find half-mass radius Re, and fit an exponential over 0.4-1.0 r25 (adjusted by eye) to derive l_star.  
  > "We measure $R_e$ and $l_\star$ from the $7.5\ensuremath{''}$ resolution estimates of $\Sigma_\star$ described in §4.3. Using these data, we construct a radial profile using the orientations in Table 3." (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star); "The integral for calculating $R_e$ extends out to $2~r_{25}$, which we verified by eye in each case to represent an apparent convergence of the radial profile." (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star); "To fit $l_\star$, we identify a fitting range over which the radial profile appears exponential. By default, this range spans from $0.4$ to $1.0~r_{25}$" (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star)
   - parameter: Central aperture: 30 arcsec (all position angles, mean); outer rings: within +-75 deg of major axis (median); background: > 2 r25; integration limit: 2 r25; exponential fit range: 0.4 to 1.0 r25  
  > "In order to lower our sensitivity to the adopted inclination and resolution, we consider only points within $\pm 75^\circ$ of the major axis, except for the central $30\ensuremath{''}$. Within the central $30\ensuremath{''}$, we include all position angles" (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star); "Then, we measure the azimuthally averaged intensity using the mean within $30\ensuremath{''}$ and the median intensity in each ring at larger radii." (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star)
   - assumption: Azimuthal symmetry when integrating radial profiles  
  > "Finally, we integrate these profiles assuming azimuthal symmetry and use this integral to identify the half-mass radius, $R_e$, the radius encompassing $90\%$ of the mass, $R_{90}$, and the stellar scale length, $l_\star$." (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star)
   - fitting: Exponential profile fit over radial range ~0.4-1.0 r25  
  > "To fit $l_\star$, we identify a fitting range over which the radial profile appears exponential. By default, this range spans from $0.4$ to $1.0~r_{25}$" (4.4.1 Measurement of Re and l_star)
   - validation: Compared derived Re and l_star against RC3 r25, 2MASS LGA J/H/K Re, S4G profile Re (Munoz-Mateos et al. 2015), and S4G 2D Sersic/expdisk fits (Salo et al. 2015) in Table 6.  
  > "We compare our estimated sizes to literature size estimates for the same galaxies and show the results in Table 6 and Figure 10." (4.4.3 Checks on Stellar Disk Sizes)
4. **Estimate integrated and resolved star formation rates (SFR)**: Combine GALEX FUV (or NUV) and WISE4 22 um (or WISE3 12 um) luminosities using linear conversion factors C calibrated to the GSWLC.  
  > "We estimate the star formation rate (SFR) using GALEX FUV, GALEX NUV maps, WISE3 ($12$ $\mu$m), and WISE4 ($22$ $\mu$m) maps. We adopt the prescription suggested by (cite: LEROY19) and calibrated to match results from the population synthesis modeling in the GSWLC (cite: SALIM16; SALIM18)." (4.5 Star Formation Rate)
   - parameter: log10 C: -43.42 (FUV), -43.24 (NUV), -42.73 (WISE4 with FUV), -42.79 (WISE4 with NUV), -42.63 (WISE4 alone)  
  > "| FUV$^{d}$ | $-43.42$ | $0.1$ | $0.09$$^{d}$ |" (Table 7); "| WISE4 with FUV | $-42.73$ | $0.17$ | $0.06$ |" (Table 7)
   - assumption: Recent star formation history does not vary strongly across the sample; Chabrier (2003) IMF via GSWLC calibration  
  > "Physically, this reflects an assumption that the recent star formation history does not vary too much across our sample." (4.5 Star Formation Rate)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared across different UV+IR band combinations (Table 8) and against PHANGS-MUSE Balmer-decrement extinction-corrected H-alpha SFR surface densities at 15 arcsec resolution for 19 galaxies (Spearman r ~ 0.95, median H-alpha/FUV+W4 ratio ~0.73 to 0.84).  
  > "As the left panel shows, the two estimates correlate extremely well, showing a Spearman rank correlation coefficient of $\approx 0.95$ despite using completely different approaches to estimate $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$." (4.5.2 UV+IR and PHANGS–MUSE Balmer-Decrement); "Specifically, treating all points equally yields a median H$\alpha$-to-FUV+WISE4 ratio of $\approx 0.73$, or $-0.14$ dex, with $\approx 0.2$ dex, or $60\%$ rms scatter about the median." (4.5.2 UV+IR and PHANGS–MUSE Balmer-Decrement)
5. **Process ALMA interferometric and total power observations into science-ready data cubes and moment maps**: Apply ALMA pipeline calibration to 12-m and 7-m data; perform uv continuum subtraction, spectral regridding/rebinning to ~2.54 km s^-1, and empirical reweighting in CASA 5.6.1; run two-stage tclean (multiscale to 4 sigma, Hogbom to 1 sigma); apply primary beam correction and convolve to round Gaussian beam; linearly mosaic multi-part galaxies; feather with calibrated, baseline-subtracted TP cubes; convert to Kelvin; convolve to fixed angular and physical resolutions; generate 3D noise models, strict and broad masks, and moment maps (mom0, mom8, mom1, mom2, effective width) with uncertainty maps.  
  > "Next, we staged the calibrated $u{-}v$ data for imaging. This step involves continuum subtraction, spectral regridding and rebinning, and an empirical reweighting of the data. For the current release of PHANGS–ALMA, we carried out these steps in `CASA` version 5.6.1" (6.1 Summary); "Next, we combine the total power and interferometric data using the implementation of the “feather” algorithm (cite: COTTON17)." (6.1 Summary); "Finally, we apply the masks to the cubes and noise models and “collapse” the cubes to produce a series of two-dimensional maps and associated uncertainty maps." (6.1 Summary)
   - software: ALMA Calibration Pipeline, CASA, PHANGS-ALMA Pipeline, PHANGS-ALMA Total Power Pipeline, GILDAS, spectral-cube, radio-beam, cprops, astropy, numpy, scipy
   - parameter: Multiscale clean threshold: 4 sigma; Hogbom clean threshold: 1 sigma; strict mask: S/N > 4 in 2 channels expanded to S/N > 2 in 2 channels; moment 1 prior tolerance: +-30 km s^-1 and S/N >= 2 in mom0; peak intensity boxcar kernel: 12.5 km s^-1; target resolutions: 2, 7.5, 11, 15 arcsec and 60, 90, 120, 150, 500, 750, 1000 pc (10% tolerance)  
  > "Following (cite: ROSOLOWSKY06) and (cite: LEROY21a), these strict masks initially consider only locations in the cube where emission exceeds signal-to-noise of $4$ over two consecutive velocity channels. Then, we extend these masks to include any adjacent regions where the emission exceeds a signal-to-noise of $2$ over two consecutive velocity channels." (6.1 Summary); "We also create a version of the peak intensity map after convolving the spectral axis of the cube with a 12.5 km s$^{-1}$ boxcar kernel." (7.2 Description of high level data products); "Therefore, we blank all values from the map that either (1) have signal to noise less than two in the integrated intensity map or (2) deviate from a “prior expectation” velocity field by more than some tolerance, $\pm 30$ km s$^{-1}$ by default." (7.2 Description of high level data products)
   - assumption: Spatial and spectral variations of the noise in each cube are separable; conversion to molecular gas mass assumes Milky Way alpha_CO^(1-0) = 4.35 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 and R_21 = 0.65 (giving alpha_CO^(2-1) = 6.7 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1) and fiducial line width 5 km s^-1 for sensitivity metrics  
  > "For a standard Milky Way $\alpha_{\rm CO}^{1-0} = 4.35$ M$_\odot$ pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: BOLATTO13A), a CO($2\text{--}1$)-to-CO($1\text{--}0$) ratio of $R_{21} = 0.65$ (cite: DENBROK21; LEROY13)" (Target Sensitivity); "We construct the noise model by treating the spectral and spatial variations of the noise as separable and then using iterative outlier rejection to isolate the parts of the cube most likely to be signal free." (7.2 Description of high level data products)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Manual inspection by team experts, automated regression tests across pipeline versions, end-to-end tests using the CASA ALMA simulator, and checks of secondary calibrator fluxes and TP observation-to-observation stability (~3% rms).  
  > "For each version of the data after early 2019, we ran automated regression tests to quantify changes from version to version and to ensure that we understood the reason for all changes. Finally, to validate our pipeline, we carried out end-to-end tests using the ALMA simulator in `CASA`." (6.2 Validation, known issues, and limitations)
6. **Measure integrated CO(2-1) luminosities, derive WISE3 aperture corrections, and validate against IRAM 30-m HERACLES maps**: Integrate 17 arcsec resolution PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) cubes within a wide mask to obtain L_CO; compute aperture correction as the ratio of total galaxy WISE3 flux to WISE3 flux within the ALMA footprint; compare integrated fluxes and pixel-by-pixel intensities at 17 arcsec resolution with IRAM 30-m HERACLES CO(2-1) maps for 18 galaxies.  
  > "To do this, we integrate a $17\ensuremath{''}$ resolution version of the ALMA CO($2\text{--}1$) cube. Working with a low resolution version of the data makes signal identification easier among. We apply a wide mask to the data before integrating" (4.6 CO(2–1) Luminosity); "After considering several options, we chose to calculate this aperture correction by using the WISE3 map as a template for CO emission. We calculate the sum of WISE3 emission over the whole galaxy and the sum of WISE3 emission over just the region with CO coverage." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction); "The IRAM 30-m maps have native resolution of $13.3\ensuremath{''}$, so we must smooth the PHANGS–ALMA data make a direct comparison. For this exercise, we used a $17\ensuremath{''}$ version of both cubes." (4.6.2 Checks on CO(2–1) Luminosity)
   - parameter: Cube resolution for integration and HERACLES comparison: 17 arcsec; pixel-by-pixel comparison threshold: I_CO > 1 K km s^-1 (1,161 sightlines across 18 galaxies)  
  > "The bottom rows in Figure 15 explore the pixel-to-pixel correspondence between the PHANGS and HERA data at $17''$ resolution. Here we only consider where both maps have coverage and $I_{\rm CO} > 1$ K km s$^{-1}$ in both maps, a total of $1{,}161$ independent $17\ensuremath{''}$ lines of sight." (4.6.2 Checks on CO(2–1) Luminosity)
   - assumption: CO(2-1) intensity tracks WISE3 12 um intensity linearly within individual galaxies  
  > "Here a template refers to a quantity that might linearly trace CO intensity." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared WISE3 template correlation (r = 0.82) against WISE4 (0.77), SFR (0.79), stellar mass (0.53), and exponential disk models (0.18-0.43); compared ALMA CO fluxes with IRAM 30-m HERACLES fluxes (median ratio 0.97 integrated, 0.93 pixel-by-pixel).  
  > "| WISE3 (adopted) | $1.25~(1.10{-}1.57)$ | $0.82$ |" (Table 9); "We find a median PHANGS-to-HERA ratio of $0.97$, or $-0.01$ dex, with a robustly-estimated galaxy-to-galaxy scatter of $0.06$ dex, or about $15\%$." (4.6.2 Checks on CO(2–1) Luminosity)

## Qualitative findings

- Distance uncertainties are the dominant source of uncertainty in selecting volume- and mass-limited samples of nearby galaxies, leading to ~42% false positive and ~47% false negative rates, though the selected sample size matches expectations.  
  > "Distance represents the dominant source of uncertainty in sample selection:" (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection); "Based on the Monte Carlo exercise, we estimate a false positive rate of ${\sim}40\%$ and a false negative rate of ${\sim} 50\%$ (Table 1) when the distance uncertainty is fold into the model." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection)
- Near-IR stellar mass surface densities using a radially varying mass-to-light ratio agree well with independent PHANGS-MUSE optical spectral synthesis fits across 19 galaxies, showing a median offset of ~0.08 dex and 0.11 dex scatter.  
  > "The left panel shows overall excellent agreement between our near-IR based $\Sigma_\star$ and the MUSE-based values." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates); "Specifically, we find a median offset of ${\sim}0.08$ dex or a factor of ${\sim}1.2$, and ${\sim}0.11$ dex or $30\%$ point-to-point scatter." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates)
- FUV+WISE4 star formation rate surface densities correlate strongly (Spearman r ~ 0.95) with PHANGS-MUSE Balmer-decrement corrected H-alpha estimates, but systematically overpredict Sigma_SFR at low values (< 10^-2 M_sun yr^-1 kpc^-2) likely due to IR cirrus contamination in WISE4.  
  > "As the left panel shows, the two estimates correlate extremely well, showing a Spearman rank correlation coefficient of $\approx 0.95$ despite using completely different approaches to estimate $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$." (4.5.2 UV+IR and PHANGS–MUSE Balmer-Decrement); "The Figure shows that the offset and scatter between the two tracers does reflect an average offset and a systematic trend, such that the two tracers agree well at $\Sigma_{\rm SFR} \gtrsim 10^{-2}$ M$_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ kpc$^2$ but show increasing divergence below this value." (4.5.2 UV+IR and PHANGS–MUSE Balmer-Decrement)
- WISE3 (12 um) emission shows the strongest linear correlation (r = 0.82) with CO(2-1) intensity within galaxies, making it the best template for CO aperture corrections, with PHANGS-ALMA maps typically capturing 70-90% of total CO emission (median correction 1.25).  
  > "Table 9 shows that WISE3 exhibits the strongest linear correlation coefficient with CO intensity of any considered quantity." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction); "WISE3 offers an outstanding option to derive aperture corrections and we derive a median correction of ${\sim}1.25$ for PHANGS–ALMA, with most targets yielding corrections between $1.1$ and $1.5$." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction)
- PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) fluxes agree with IRAM 30-m HERACLES single-dish maps to within 3-7% on average (median ratio 0.97 integrated, 0.93 pixel-by-pixel) with ~15% galaxy-to-galaxy scatter.  
  > "We find a median PHANGS-to-HERA ratio of $0.97$, or $-0.01$ dex, with a robustly-estimated galaxy-to-galaxy scatter of $0.06$ dex, or about $15\%$." (4.6.2 Checks on CO(2–1) Luminosity); "There appears to be a ${\sim}3{-}7\%$ systematic offset in the flux scale between PHANGS and the HERA data, with the PHANGS data showing slightly lower flux." (4.6.2 Checks on CO(2–1) Luminosity)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO(2-1) at 230.538 GHz is 2-4 times faster to map than CO(1-0) at matched resolution and mass sensitivity, is well-excited in GMCs, and C43-2 + 7-m + TP achieves ~1 arcsec (~100 pc) GMC-scale resolution with full flux recovery across the WISE-defined active star-forming disk in ~1-2 hours of 12-m time per galaxy.  
  > "Although ALMA can achieve resolutions much better than $1\ensuremath{''}$ at the $\nu = 230$ GHz of CO($2\text{--}1$), such observations have poor surface brightness sensitivity and require a prohibitive amount of time to detect CO emission from molecular clouds." (2.2 PHANGS–ALMA); "Given typical observed CO($2\text{--}1$)-to-CO($1\text{--}0$) line ratios of $R_{21} \approx 0.5{-}1$ (cite: DENBROK21; LEROY13) and considering the array configurations and system temperatures at ALMA, mapping CO($2\text{--}1$) is a factor of ${\sim}2{-}4$ times faster than mapping CO($1\text{--}0$) to the same mass surface density sensitivity and angular resolution." (5.1.1 Spectral Setup and Choice to Target CO(2–1))
- Why this method: Calibrating stellar mass and SFR estimators to the GSWLC places PHANGS-ALMA on a self-consistent scale with >650,000 SDSS galaxies and ~15,000 local galaxies; radial profile-based mass-to-light ratios avoid biases from bright nuclear starbursts/AGN; WISE3 provides the tightest linear correlation with CO for aperture corrections.  
  > "In addition to matching the SDSS main galaxy sample via the GSWLC, this specifically places PHANGS–ALMA on a nearly-matched mass scale to the GASS and xCOLD GASS surveys (cite: SAINTONGE17; CATINELLA18)." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates); "Overall, we found that the azimuthal averages offered a good compromise between isolating distinct regions of a galaxy and achieving good signal to noise." (4.3.1 Mass-to-Light Ratio Estimates); "Table 9 shows that WISE3 exhibits the strongest linear correlation coefficient with CO intensity of any considered quantity." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction)
- Alternative: Higher angular resolution (~10 pc) ALMA mapping was rejected because surface brightness sensitivity integration time scales as theta^-4, which would limit the survey to only one or two galaxies in the same total time.  
  > "Specifically, the integration time ($t$) required to reach a fixed surface brightness sensitivity at a resolution ($\theta$) scales as $t \propto \theta^{-4}$. Thus, even targeting an order of magnitude poorer sensitivity, ALMA could only survey one or two nearby galaxies at ${\sim}10$ pc resolution during the time required to map all $90$ PHANGS–ALMA targets at ${\sim}100$ pc resolution." (2.2 PHANGS–ALMA)
- Alternative: Mapping CO(1-0) instead of CO(2-1) was rejected because CO(2-1) is 2-4 times faster to map to the same mass surface density sensitivity and angular resolution at ALMA.  
  > "mapping CO($2\text{--}1$) is a factor of ${\sim}2{-}4$ times faster than mapping CO($1\text{--}0$) to the same mass surface density sensitivity and angular resolution." (5.1.1 Spectral Setup and Choice to Target CO(2–1))
- Alternative: Nine candidate galaxies (ESO 138-010, ESO 494-026, IC 5201, NGC 1055, NGC 4802, NGC 6221, NGC 6875, PGC 18855, PGC 54411) were manually rejected after visual inspection due to edge-on inclination, compact nuclear AGN/starburst dominance, or bright foreground Milky Way stars.  
  > "The overall list of manually excluded galaxies is ESO 138-010, ESO 494-026, IC 5201, NGC 1055, NGC 4802, NGC 6221, NGC 6875, PGC 18855, and PGC 54411." (3.2 Implementation)
- Alternative: Using WISE3 (12 um) directly in the fiducial SFR estimator was rejected in favor of FUV+WISE4 because the WISE3-to-SFR calibration shows strong systematic bias across stellar mass and specific SFR due to PAH variations.  
  > "However, as Table 7 shows, the appropriate calibration to translate WISE3 to SFR shows strong trends as a function of stellar mass and specific star formation rate." (4.5.1 Checks on Star Formation Rates)
- Alternative: Using WISE4, FUV+WISE4 SFR, WISE1 stellar mass, or exponential disk models for CO aperture correction was considered, but WISE3 was adopted because it has the highest linear correlation with CO(2-1) intensity.  
  > "Inside the PHANGS–ALMA footprint, we construct several possible “templates” that could be used for the aperture correction." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction); "Table 9 shows that WISE3 exhibits the strongest linear correlation coefficient with CO intensity of any considered quantity." (4.6.1 Aperture Correction)
- Alternative: Additional manual flagging of the interferometric uv data was tested and rejected because inspection showed it did not appreciably improve the final data products over the ALMA pipeline calibration.  
  > "Based on inspection of the $u{-}v$ data, we found that, in general, additional by-hand flagging does not appreciably improve the quality of the final data products." (6.1 Summary)
- How they decided it was enough: Target sensitivity of 7.5 mJy beam^-1 per 5 km s^-1 channel was chosen to detect individual GMCs with characteristic mass M_mol >= 10^5 M_sun at >= 2-3 sigma out to 17 Mpc; mosaic field of view was set to enclose the WISE3 > 0.5 MJy sr^-1 contour (~70-90% of total CO emission); second-stage Hogbom clean stopped when peak residual inside mask reached <= 1 sigma.  
  > "Our chosen target sensitivity is motivated by the canonical value for the characteristic mass of GMCs in the inner Milky Way, $M_{\rm mol} \gtrsim 10^5$ M$_\odot$ (cite: BLITZ93), and thus, even in our most distant targets, we expect to detect individual moderately massive GMCs ($M_{\rm mol} \gtrsim 10^5$ M$_\odot$) at $2{-}3\sigma$ significance." (5.1.3 Target Sensitivity); "Specifically, when designing the field of view for the Large Program, we aimed to cover the full area of each galaxy that shows WISE3 intensity above $0.5$ MJy sr$^{-1}$ at $7.5\ensuremath{''}$ resolution." (5.1.4 Target Area); "We run this second stage of deconvolution, stopping when the residual, i.e., not deconvolved emission, within the clean mask has a peak $\leq 1\sigma$ or the amount of deconvolved emission changes by a negligible amount over successive calls to clean." (6.1 Summary)
- Limitation (authors): The sample lacks extreme starbursts, has few early-type galaxies, and does not extend into the low-mass dwarf galaxy regime below log10(M_star/M_sun) ~ 9.5.  
  > "We do not include many extreme starbursts due to the simple fact that these tend to be rare." (4.8 PHANGS–ALMA Sample Properties); "Finally, we do not push far into the regime of dwarf galaxies, with good coverage in mass down to only $\log_{10} M_\star\,[{\rm M}_\odot] \approx 9.5$." (4.8 PHANGS–ALMA Sample Properties)
- Limitation (authors): Distance uncertainties cause high false positive (~42%) and false negative (~47%) rates relative to the strict selection criteria, and several obvious candidates (e.g., NGC 3344, NGC 3368) were omitted.  
  > "Based on the Monte Carlo exercise, we estimate a false positive rate of ${\sim}40\%$ and a false negative rate of ${\sim} 50\%$ (Table 1) when the distance uncertainty is fold into the model." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection); "This exercise revealed two clear omissions that appear almost certain to meet our criteria, NGC 3344 and NGC 3368." (3.3 Accuracy and Uncertainty in Selection)
- Limitation (authors): FUV+WISE4 star formation rate estimates systematically overpredict Sigma_SFR at low star formation surface densities (< 10^-2 M_sun yr^-1 kpc^-2) compared to Balmer-decrement corrected H-alpha due to IR cirrus contamination.  
  > "Second, the clear systematic trends suggest that the FUV+WISE4 maps be treated with caution at low $\Sigma_{\rm SFR} \sim 10^{-3}$ M$_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ kpc$^2$." (4.5.2 UV+IR and PHANGS–MUSE Balmer-Decrement)
- Limitation (authors): Post-processing choices (round beam convolution, beam matching across mosaic parts, intermediate robust weighting, and 2.54 km s^-1 channel binning) slightly degrade the native spatial and spectral resolution.  
  > "Both the spectral and angular resolution of the data could be improved at the cost of signal-to-noise. The convolution to a round beam and matching beams between parts of multi-part mosaics both somewhat inflate the synthesized beam size." (6.2 Validation, known issues, and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Image reconstruction of 7-m-only data performs poorly at low or moderate signal-to-noise and is less reliable than 12-m+7-m+TP data.  
  > "However, the 7-m-only data for many galaxies appear unreliable. We also view the combined 7-m and total power data as less reliable than the full combined 12-m, 7-m, and total power data." (6.2 Validation, known issues, and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): A subset of Total Power datasets has a ~7% high calibration bias due to a lag in updating antenna surface accuracy gains, biasing overall cube fluxes high by 2-5%.  
  > "As a result, a subset of our total power data sets apply an observatory-provided calibration known to be high by ${\sim}7\%$. The net effect is that a subset of cubes has overall fluxes biased high by $2{-}5\%$." (6.2 Validation, known issues, and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral regridding and rebinning in CASA introduce a ~10% spectral variation in the noise across the cube bandwidth.  
  > "As discussed in (cite: LEROY21a), the regridding and rebinning parts of the $u{-}v$ staging procedure introduce a mild spectral variation into the noise of the final data cubes. The overall magnitude is ${\sim}10\%$ across the full bandwidth of the cubes." (6.2 Validation, known issues, and limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Delivered line width maps are not corrected for the spectral line spread function, and single-component moment maps do not capture multi-component velocity structures in galaxy centers or colliding flows.  
  > "For this delivery, we do not correct the measurements for the line spread function. This can be an important effect for narrow lines and should be addressed by the user (cite: LEROY16; KOCH18B; SUN20; ROSOLOWSKY21)." (7.2 Description of high level data products); "In the inner regions of galaxies and in regions of colliding flows (cite: BESLIC21), multi-component decomposition like that presented in (cite: HENSHAW20) represents a better treatment of the spectrum than single component methods." (7.2 Description of high level data products)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Detailed CASA imaging and post-processing parameters (e.g., exact robust value, multiscale scales, cell size, uv reweighting formula) are not given in this paper and require consulting the companion pipeline paper Leroy et al. (2021a).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Converting integrated CO(2-1) intensities to molecular gas masses or surface densities relies on a fixed Milky Way alpha_CO and R_21 = 0.65 in the summary tables, without accounting for metallicity or excitation variations across galaxies or galaxy centers.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Whether to apply a cos(i) inclination correction to molecular gas surface density is ambiguous at ~100 pc resolution because the beam size approaches the disk scale height.
