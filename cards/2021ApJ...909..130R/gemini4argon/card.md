## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, concatenate, and image ALMA Band 9 [C II] and 160-um continuum data**: Manually flag antennas with high Tsys, calibrate using DV07 as reference antenna, concatenate visibilities from both ALMA programmes while discarding C43-6 baselines > 1000 klambda, and create naturally-weighted dirty image cubes and continuum images at full resolution and with 0.5 and 1.0 arcsec Gaussian uv-tapers.  
  > "As the ALMA pipeline products suffered from calibration issues, we calibrated the data manually by completely flagging antennas with high system temperature: C43-1 configuration: DA62, DA44, DA50, DV09, DV24; C43-2: DA62." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging); "For the imaging, we concatenate the visibilities from both ALMA programmes to maximize the S/N; the total on-source time is 160 min." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging); "We produce dirty images of the concatenated dataset using natural weighting, at the full angular resolution and using a 0.5 and 1.0-arcsec Gaussian taper." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: uv cut: <= 1000 klambda for C43-6; weighting: natural; uv tapers: none, 0.5 arcsec, 1.0 arcsec; cube channel widths: 50-500 MHz (25-225 km/s)  
  > "For the C43-6 configuration, we discard all baselines longer than 1000 k _λ_, as adding the long baselines significantly reduces the surface brightness sensitivity of the combined dataset." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging); "For the [C II ] line, we create several dirty-image cubes using different (u,v)plane tapers (no taper, 0.5 and 1.0-arcsec taper) and channel width (∆ _f_ = 50-500 MHz, equivalent to 25-225 km s _[−]_ [1] )." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured rms levels in dirty images compared against expected ALMA sensitivity (matching within 10%).  
  > "The _σ_ rms levels in the resulting images match the expected ALMA sensitivity within 10%." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging)
2. **Extract image-plane spectra and derive source-plane [C II], 160-um continuum, and CO(2-1) upper limits**: Project circular source-plane apertures of radius R = 1, 2, and 3 kpc centered on the UV-continuum peak into the sky plane using the Cooray et al. (2011) lens model (retaining only the main arc), extract spectra from the dirty cubes, and integrate over 250 MHz (110 km/s) centered at 667.97 GHz to determine upper limits and de-lensed luminosities.  
  > "We consider three circular source-plane apertures centered on the UV-continuum peak, with a radius _R_ =1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 kpc; the latter is comparable to the largest [C II ] reservoirs observed in (much more massive) _z_ _∼_ 6 galaxies (Carniani et al. 2020)." (3.1.1. Searching for signal in the image plane); "We project these into the sky-plane using the Cooray et al. (2011) lens model for the lensing galaxy." (3.1.1. Searching for signal in the image plane); "Consequently, we adopt a [C II ] upper limit of _S_ [CII] _≤_ 21 mJy over 250 MHz (110 km s _[−]_ [1] ) bandwidth, measured for the 1.0-arcsec uv-taper and _R_ _≤_ 3 kpc source-plane aperture (3.2 _σ_ significance)." (3.1.2. Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane)
   - parameter: Aperture radius R <= 3 kpc, uv-taper = 1.0 arcsec, bandwidth = 250 MHz (110 km/s) centered at 667.97 GHz  
  > "The [C II ] emission is integrated over 250 MHz (110 km s _[−]_ [1] ) bandwidth centered at 667.97 GHz; this part of the spectrum shows the tentative 2-3 _σ_ extended emission." (Figure 1 caption); "Consequently, we adopt a [C II ] upper limit of _S_ [CII] _≤_ 21 mJy over 250 MHz (110 km s _[−]_ [1] ) bandwidth, measured for the 1.0-arcsec uv-taper and _R_ _≤_ 3 kpc source-plane aperture (3.2 _σ_ significance)." (3.1.2. Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane)
   - assumption: Magnification factors mu = 43, 25, and 16 for R = 1, 2, and 3 kpc apertures (main arc contains 95% of total flux); flat LambdaCDM cosmology with Omega_m = 0.315, H_0 = 67.4 km/s/Mpc (D_L = 14460 Mpc); line width 110 km/s.  
  > "We assume a flat ΛCDM cosmology, with Ω _m_ = 0 _._ 315 and _H_ 0 = 67 _._ 4 km s _[−]_ [1] Mpc _[−]_ [1] (Planck Collaboration et al. 2020)." (2.1. Target description); "The magnification factor is _µ_ = 43, 25 and 16 for the _R_ =1, 2 and 3-kpc apertures, respectively." (3.1.1. Searching for signal in the image plane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that the dirty beams are well-described by a central Gaussian with very small sidelobes (<= 5%) within the apertures.  
  > "However, the dirty beams corresponding to the Fig. 1 images are well-described by a central Gaussian with only very small sidelobes ( _≤_ 5%) within the apertures considered here." (3.1.1. Searching for signal in the image plane)
3. **Verify [C II] flux constraint in the (u,v) visibility plane**: Extract real and imaginary visibilities over a 250 MHz bandwidth (keeping XX and YY separate), estimate noise from per-scan baseline rms, forward-model sky brightness distributions (HST F606W arc and R <= 1, 2, 3 kpc apertures) multiplied by a Gaussian primary beam for total fluxes from 0 to 50 mJy, and compute the log-likelihood PDF of S_[CII].  
  > "The noise on the real/imaginary visibilities is estimated by taking the rms of visibilities for a given baseline for each individual scan." (3.1.2 Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane); "We consider the following skyplane [C II ] surface brightness distributions: HST F606W arc (see Fig. 1) and the _R_ _≤_ 1, 2 and 3 kpc apertures; the total flux varies between 0 and 50 mJy." (3.1.2. Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane)
   - parameter: Total flux grid: 0 to 50 mJy; primary beam FWHM: 9.0 arcsec at 680 GHz; bandwidth: 250 MHz  
  > "The primary beam FWHM was 9.0 arcsec at 680 GHz." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging); "We consider the following skyplane [C II ] surface brightness distributions: HST F606W arc (see Fig. 1) and the _R_ _≤_ 1, 2 and 3 kpc apertures; the total flux varies between 0 and 50 mJy." (3.1.2. Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane)
   - assumption: Primary beam response approximated by a Gaussian.  
  > "where _B_ is the primary beam response (approximated by a Gaussian), _I_ the input sky brightness distribution, and _m, l_ the sky-plane coordinates." (3.1.2. Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane)
   - fitting: Grid evaluation of Gaussian log-likelihood log L proportional to -sum (V_model - V_data)^2 / sigma^2 over S_[CII] = 0 to 50 mJy.  
  > "the total flux varies between 0 and 50 mJy. We calculate the log-likelihood value" (3.1.2 Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SL2S 0217 is strongly lensed (magnification ~17) at z = 1.844 with a low metallicity (0.05 Z_sun) and stellar mass ~10^8 M_sun, providing an accessible analogue of Epoch of Reionization galaxies; [C II] 158-um in ALMA Band 9 is the brightest probe of cool gas in metal-poor galaxies.  
  > "Our best bet for probing the molecular gas content of this metal-poor galaxies is the [C II ] 158- _µ_ m line, thanks to its low critical density and large intrinsic brightness." (1. INTRODUCTION); "Thanks to its large magnification, SL2S 0217 provides a unique opportunity to detect the neutral ISM in a high-redshift EELG with a stellar mass of just 10 [8] _M_ _⊙_ - 1 dex lower than any previous _z_ _∼_ 2 study." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Baselines > 1000 klambda were discarded and uv-tapering (1.0 arcsec) with a large (R <= 3 kpc) aperture was used to maximize surface-brightness sensitivity and account for potentially extended or spatially offset [C II] emission; (u,v)-plane fitting was performed to check for spatial filtering effects; C II] 2325 A was used in photoionization modelling to avoid systematic uncertainties from carbon abundance and ionization state.  
  > "For the C43-6 configuration, we discard all baselines longer than 1000 k _λ_, as adding the long baselines significantly reduces the surface brightness sensitivity of the combined dataset." (2.2. ALMA Band 9 observations and imaging); "The 1.0-arcsec taper maximizes the surface-brightness sensitivity, while large aperture size accounts for a potentially very extended [C II ] emission." (3.1.2. Searching for signal in the (u,v) plane); "Using C II ] instead of any of the other UV lines detected in the Keck spectrum by B18 avoids systematic uncertainties related to the carbon abundance and ionization state." (4.1. Photoionization modelling)
- Alternative: Modelling the neutral/molecular PDR phase directly was refrained from due to the lack of observational constraints (only [C II] 158-um and weak CO(2-1) upper limits).  
  > "Due to the lack of constraints on the PDR phase (just our [C II ] 158- _µ_ m and a very weak CO(2–1) upper limits, we refrain from modelling the neutral/molecular ISM." (4.1. Photoionization modelling)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Extracting flux measurements from dirty images over extended apertures can be biased because the area under a dirty beam integrates to zero for large apertures (though sidelobes are <= 5% here).  
  > "Consequently, flux measurements extracted from _dirty_ images over extended areas might be biased, particularly in presence of strong positive or negative sidelobes." (3.1.1. Searching for signal in the image plane)
- Limitation (authors): Photoionization modelling treats the ISM as uniform, whereas the UV spectral slope varies across the source and the C II], [C II], and C III] lines are likely not co-spatial on sub-kiloparsec scales.  
  > "Here, we treat the ISM as uniform. However, as shown by E19, the UV spectral slope varies considerably across the source; the C II ], [C II ] and C III ] lines are thus likely not co-spatial on sub-kiloparsec scales." (4.1. Photoionization modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of electron density constraints implies a ~1 dex uncertainty in the MAPPINGS-predicted [C II] 158-um / C II] 2325 A flux ratio.  
  > "This estimate is approximately 5 _×_ lower than our [C II ] 158 _µ_ m upper limit ( _L_ [sky] [CII]158 _µ_ m _[≤]_ [3] _[.]_ [4] _[×]_ [10] [8] _[ L]_ _[⊙]_ [)] although the lack of _n_ _e_ constraints implies a _∼_ 1 dex uncertainty in the predicted flux ratio." (4.1. Photoionization modelling)
- Limitation (authors): CO-based molecular gas mass upper limit is weakened by the large uncertainty in alpha_CO at low metallicity (up to 3 dex higher than the Galactic value), and extrapolating the Zanella et al. (2018) [C II]-to-H2 relation to low metallicity may not be straightforward.  
  > "However, studies of nearby low-metallicity dwarfs (e.g., Schruba et al. 2012; Hunt et al. 2015; Shi et al. 2016) found _α_ CO up to 3 dex higher than the Galactic value, significantly weakening our _M_ gas upper limit." (4.4. The fate of molecular gas in SL2S 0217); "we obtain _M_ H 2 _≤_ 0 _._ 8 _×_ 10 [9] _M_ _⊙_ ; although the extrapolation of the Zanella et al. (2018) relation to the low- _Z_ regime might not be straightforward." (4.4. The fate of molecular gas in SL2S 0217)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a minor inconsistency in the quoted source-plane L_[CII]/SFR upper limit between the Abstract/Table 3 (<= 1 x 10^6 L_sun/(M_sun yr^-1)) and Section 5 (2 x 10^6 L_sun/(M_sun yr^-1)), and in the assumed L_[CII] in Section 4.5 (2.6 x 10^7 L_sun vs 2.1 x 10^7 L_sun in Table 1).
