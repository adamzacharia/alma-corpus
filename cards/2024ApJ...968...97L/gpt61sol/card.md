- Question: Derive radial variations of the CO-to-H2 conversion factor and gas-to-dust ratio in M83; test metallicity and cloud population dependences.

## Analysis recipe

1. **Make calibrated HI cubes**: JVLA: subtract continuum in uv space, regrid narrow-band data and image with tclean. GBT: grid calibrated spectra with a GaussBessel kernel and correct relative map/polarization flux scales.  
  > "We subtracted the continuum emission in the *uv* space using line-free channels" (Data Reduction); "Imaging was performed with the CASA task “tclean"." (Data Reduction); "The calibrated spectra were gridded spatially to produce a data cube using the GaussBessel kernel function." (Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, GBTIDL
   - parameter: JVLA velocity grid: 200-800 km s^-1; 5 km s^-1 channels  
  > "covering the velocity range of 200-800$\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ with a channel width of 5$\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ for HI." (Data Reduction)
   - parameter: GBT pixels: 1.47 arcmin spatial; 0.75 km/s velocity  
  > "We adopted spatial and velocity pixel scales of $1.47\ensuremath{'}$ and 0.75 km/s." (Data Reduction)
   - parameter: GBT relative flux scaling factors: 0.98-1.02, median unity  
  > "Their range was small and was 0.98-1.02 (peak-to-peak) after setting the median to be unity." (Data Reduction)
   - fitting: GBT: measure ratios of 12 cubes and invert the ratio matrix for relative flux scaling factors  
  > "we made 12 data cubes for the two polarizations of the 6 OTF maps." (Data Reduction); "We measured their flux ratios and inverted the ratio matrix to derive relative flux scaling factors." (Data Reduction)
   - validation: JVLA: flag implausible amplitudes or phase scatter after calibration and repeat calibration  
  > "Some bad data with unrealistically high amplitudes or phase scatters became obvious after these calibrations." (Data Reduction); "We flagged those in the raw data and repeated all the calibrations from scratch." (Data Reduction)
2. **Combine HI and integrate emission**: Feather JVLA and GBT cubes with primary-beam handling; construct an expanded emission mask; integrate HI and propagate channel noise.  
  > "We regridded the GBT cube, applied the JVLA primary beam to it, combined it with the JVLA cube, and applied the JVLA primary beam correction to the combined cube." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data); "We applied a mask to the final cube for generating an integrated intensity map for a 11’$\times$11’ box around the galaxy center" (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data); "We used this mask also to calculate a noise map using the standard noise propagation based on the number of velocity pixels in each spatial pixel." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data)
   - software: CASA, feather
   - parameter: Initial mask: JVLA clean components smoothed with 60 arcsec Gaussian; >5 mJy/beam  
  > "smoothed it with a Gaussian kernel with a FWHM of $60\ensuremath{''}$ (about the width of the HI spiral arms), and took regions with fluxes $>5\,\rm mJy/beam$" (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data)
   - parameter: Expand binary mask with 90 arcsec Gaussian; threshold >0.1  
  > "Second, we expanded this mask by smoothing it with a Gaussian kernel with a FWHM of $90\ensuremath{''}$ and by taking the regions $>0.1$." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data)
   - assumption: Conservative mask may include blank sky but should retain all emission  
  > "the final mask may include blank sky but does not miss any emission." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare total flux with and without mask within 11 × 11 arcmin; ratio approximately 1.09  
  > "we calculated the total flux with and without applying the mask in the cube, and found that the ratio is $\sim$ 1.09." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data); "Thus, the mask likely encloses all of the emission." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data)
3. **Check input flux scales**: Compare HI cubes and distributed moment maps with THINGS/LVHIS after matching resolution and pixels; compare ALMA TP CO with Nobeyama CO.  
  > "For (1) and (2) we smoothed and regridded our data to the resolution and pixel scale of the archival data." (Comparisons with Previous HI Data); "For the flux comparisons, we consider the ratio of total fluxes in the 11’$\times$11’ region" (Comparisons with Previous HI Data); "As a consistency check, we compare this map to the CO(1-0) map obtained from the Nobeyama 45-m telescope (cite: Kuno:2007zr)" (CO(1-0) and Infrared Data)
   - parameter: CO comparison: intensity-weighted ratio within R=0-4 kpc  
  > "The intensity weighted average ratio between the Nobeyama CO(1-0) and ALMA TP maps within $R=$ 0-4 kpc is $\sim$ 0.8." (CO(1-0) and Infrared Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: THINGS has missing extended flux; LVHIS is relatively consistent with the new HI map  
  > "The THINGS data show the galactic structures in HI (e.g., spiral arms) clearly at the high resolution, but suffer from missing flux, due to the lack of single-dish data." (Comparisons with Previous HI Data); "On the other hand, the LVHIS data is relatively more consistent with our new map, though it has a much lower resolution." (Comparisons with Previous HI Data)
4. **Prepare surface-density maps**: Convert HI intensity to HI surface density; convert Herschel 500 μm luminosity surface density to dust surface density using Aniano; smooth dust to CO resolution.  
  > "\Sigma_{\rm HI} = \alpha_{\rm HI} I_{\rm HI}," (HI and H$_2$ Surface Densities); "Hence, we adopt this equation and convert the Herschel 500$\rm{\mu m}$ image into a $\Sigma_{\rm d}$ map." (Dust Surface Density); "We smooth this image with a Gaussian to the CO(1-0) resolution of $56.6\ensuremath{''}$." (Dust Surface Density)
   - parameter: alpha_HI = 1.98×10^-2 M_sun pc^-2 [K km s^-1]^-1  
  > "$\alpha_{\rm HI}= 1.98\times10^{-2} {\rm M_{\odot}}\,\, {\rm pc}^{-2} [{\rm K} \cdot  \, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}]^{-1}$" (HI and H$_2$ Surface Densities)
   - parameter: log(Sigma_d/[M_sun kpc^-2]) = -0.42 + 0.942 log(Sigma_nuLnu/[L_sun kpc^-2])  
  > "\log \left( \frac{\Sigma_{\rm d}}{{\rm M_{\odot}}\, \, {\rm kpc}^{-2}} \right) = -0.42 + 0.942 \log \left( \frac{\Sigma_{\nu L_{\nu}}}{{\rm L_{\odot}}\,\, {\rm kpc}^{-2}} \right)." (Dust Surface Density)
   - parameter: Overall dust surface-density uncertainty: 20%  
  > "Since this is larger than the uncertainties in the $500\mu$m data mentioned in Bendo et al. 2012, we assume that 20% is the overall uncertainty." (Dust Surface Density)
   - assumption: HI optically thin and in LTE; surface densities include helium/heavier elements by a factor of 1.36  
  > "(including the factor of 1.36 to account for helium) is derived theoretically in an optically-thin condition under local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (HI and H$_2$ Surface Densities); "Following the definition by (cite: Sandstrom:2013vn), ${\alpha_{\rm CO}}$ includes the mass of helium (a factor of 1.36)" (HI and H$_2$ Surface Densities)
   - assumption: Adopted 20% dust uncertainty includes sky fluctuations and background sources  
  > "We also assume this includes the uncertainty from sky fluctuation including background sources" (Dust Surface Density)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Accurate HI fixes the absolute gas-to-dust and conversion-factor scales; combine JVLA and GBT to recover extended flux.  
  > "The dust-based approach requires accurate $\Sigma_{\rm{HI}}$ measurements as $\Sigma_{\rm HI}$ determines the absolute scale of ${\delta_{\rm GDR}}$, which calibrates the scale for ${\alpha_{\rm CO}}$." (Summary and Conclusions); "This operation is to include any extended emission from the GBT data." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data)
- Why this method: Use both fitting methods, with slight preference for chi-square error estimates; use annuli wider than the beam and luminosity functions that avoid fixing alpha_CO.  
  > "Here, we use both methods with a slight preference to the $\chi^2$ method, as it gives a convenient estimation of errors, assuming that all errors in the analysis follow the normal distribution." (Finding Solutions); "The width is selected so that it is greater than the $56.6\ensuremath{''}$ resolution, which is 1.23 kpc at the distance of M83." (Radial Gradients); "We adopt a luminosity function instead of a mass function because a mass function would require assuming a fixed conversion factor." (Cloud Population Dependence)
- Alternative: Use Aniano's empirical 500 μm conversion rather than optically thin modified-blackbody SED fitting.  
  > "They found that their $\Sigma_{\rm d}$ can be estimated more reliably with this equation, than with the more widely-used SED-fitting with an optically thin modified blackbody approximation." (Dust Surface Density); "Hence, we adopt this equation and convert the Herschel 500$\rm{\mu m}$ image into a $\Sigma_{\rm d}$ map." (Dust Surface Density)
- Alternative: Compare whole-disk fits with annular averages; the single linear relation may fail across the whole disk.  
  > "These values are almost a factor of 2 larger than the annulus-based averages" (Galaxy-wide Averages); "may break down on the scales of the entire galactic disk in M83" (Galaxy-wide Averages)
- Alternative: Prefer peak brightness temperature to cloud radius for approximate resolution-based selection.  
  > "They adopted $T_{\rm peak}$ for this separation since it is among the direct observables in their data cube." (The Conversion Factor and Virial Parameter of Molecular Clouds in M83); "Cloud radius may be an alternative parameter for this separation; however, while it can be measured, it is uncertain for clouds with sizes close to or smaller than the beam size." (The Conversion Factor and Virial Parameter of Molecular Clouds in M83)
- Alternative: Explore several metallicity indices rather than adopting a single literature prescription.  
  > "Hence, instead of adopting a single relation directly, we explore a range of indices encompassing suggested ${\alpha_{\rm CO}}-Z$ relations in the literature." (Consideration of Metallicity Dependence)
- How they decided it was enough: Conservative mask flux check, S/N>3 selection retaining 95% of pixels, and consistency of the two fitting methods.  
  > "Thus, the mask likely encloses all of the emission." (Combination of JVLA and GBT Data); "95% of the pixels in the radial range of $R = 1$-$7$ kpc are used." (Finding Solutions); "Overall, the (${\alpha_{\rm CO}}$, ${\delta_{\rm GDR}}$) values obtained between the fitting methods for the same annulus are similar." (Radial Gradients)
- Limitation (authors): Adjacent annuli overlap and are not independent.  
  > "Adjacent annuli overlap and are not independent from each other, but are useful in finding a radial trend." (Radial Gradients)
- Limitation (authors): Central R<1 kpc excluded because continuum emission and HI absorption prevent accurate HI measurement.  
  > "The central 1 kpc in radius is excluded as $\Sigma_{\rm HI}$ cannot be measured accurately, due to the bright continuum emission at the galactic center, and HI absorption." (Radial Gradients)
- Limitation (authors): Gas-to-dust normalization depends on the dust model; dust systematics can affect the inferred nonlinearity.
