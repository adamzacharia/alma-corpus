## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, mosaic, and regrid Stokes I, Q, and U maps**: Calibrate and image individual fields in CASA using spw0-2, linearly mosaic the three pointings using Miriad linmos with primary beam weighting and taper option, and regrid onto a 0.25'' pixel grid.  
  > "The data were calibrated and imaged using CASA version 5.4.0, using imaging scripts supplied by the Observatory." (Data); "We linearly mosaicked our continuum Stokes $Q$, $U$ and $I$ maps using the Miriad task $linmos$ which performs a primary beam weighted mosaic of the three pointing centers to minimise the RMS error." (Data); "For the purpose of the analysis in Section 3 below, we regridded the Stokes $Q$, $U$ and $I$ maps to a 0.25$''$ pixel grid, approximately Nyquist-sampled on the major axis of the beam." (Data)
   - software: CASA, tclean, Miriad, linmos
   - parameter: Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5, cell size = 0.060'', regridded to 0.25'' pixel grid  
  > "The $tclean$ parameters used for imaging were Briggs weighting, with a robust parameter of 0.5, and a cell size of 0.060$^{\prime\prime}$." (Data); "For the purpose of the analysis in Section 3 below, we regridded the Stokes $Q$, $U$ and $I$ maps to a 0.25$''$ pixel grid, approximately Nyquist-sampled on the major axis of the beam." (Data)
   - assumption: Spectral lines in spw0-2 contribute negligible polarised signal  
  > "Fortunately for our purposes, most of these lines contribute little polarised signal, and so our Stokes $Q$ and $U$ maps made from spw0-2 are dominated by continuum emission." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of individual pointing images with the resulting mosaic  
  > "The individual images of the three pointings are in good agreement with the resulting mosaic." (Data)
2. **Calculate polarised intensity, polarisation angles, and sub-region statistics**: Compute polarised intensity PI = sqrt(Q^2 + U^2) (without debiasing at high SNR), apply a 6 mJy/beam threshold, compute polarisation angles theta = 0.5 arctan(U/Q) and uncertainties delta_theta = 0.5 * delta_Q / PI, and calculate mean and median angles in 5 defined sub-regions.  
  > "Polarised intensity is calculated as $$\begin{equation}
    PI = \sqrt{Q^{2}+U^{2}},
\end{equation}$$ and is shown in Figure 2. Polarised intensity is thresholded at 6 mJy/beam." (Data); "Polarisation angle is given by $$\begin{equation}
    \theta = \frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{U}{Q}\right).
\end{equation}$$" (Data); "The mean and median polarisation angles, as well as the number of independent measurements, in each region are listed in Table 1." (Results and Discussion)
   - parameter: PI threshold >= 6 mJy/beam; delta_Q = delta_U = 0.62 mJy/beam; delta_theta <= 3.0 deg  
  > "For $\delta Q = \delta U = 0.62$ mJy/beam and $PI\geq 6$ mJy/beam, $\delta \theta \leq 3.0^{\circ}$." (Data)
   - assumption: RMS uncertainties on Stokes Q and U emission are equal  
  > "When RMS uncertainties on Stokes $Q$ and $U$ emission are equal, uncertainty on polarisation angle is given in radians by $$\begin{equation}
    \delta\theta = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\delta Q}{PI}.
\end{equation}$$" (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative comparison with ALMA Band 3 and Band 6 results at comparable resolution  
  > "The polarisation geometry which we observe is in qualitative agreement with recent Band 3 and and Band 6 results at comparable resolution (cite: cortes2020)." (Data)
3. **Compare observed polarisation geometries in sub-regions against geometric models using Monte Carlo KS and Kuiper tests**: Predict pixel-by-pixel polarisation angles for geometric models (uniform 26 deg E of N, concentric around BN/KL, concentric around Source I, concentric around ring feature), perturb model angles with Gaussian noise of width sigma_theta (1 to 20 deg) 1000 times per sigma_theta, and run two-sided KS and Kuiper tests against observed angle distributions.  
  > "We quantified the similarity of these models to the data using two-sided Kolmorogov-Smirnov (KS) and Kuiper tests." (Comparison of data and models); "For each model, we determined polarisation angles predicted in each pixel by the model, and drew sets of perturbations on these angles from a Gaussian distribution of width $\sigma_{\theta}$." (Comparison of data and models); "We repeated this process 1000 times for each value of $\sigma_{\theta}$ considered. We tested $\sigma_{\theta}$ values in the range $1^{\circ} - 20^{\circ}$." (Comparison of data and models)
   - parameter: sigma_theta in range 1 deg - 20 deg; 1000 Monte Carlo realisations per sigma_theta; consistency criterion p > 0.05  
  > "We repeated this process 1000 times for each value of $\sigma_{\theta}$ considered. We tested $\sigma_{\theta}$ values in the range $1^{\circ} - 20^{\circ}$." (Comparison of data and models); "According to both tests, the BN/KL-concentric model is the only model that can be made consistent ($p > 0.05$) with the data in the eastern arm" (Comparison of data and models)
   - assumption: Large-scale magnetic field has orientation 116 deg E of N (polarisation angle 26 deg E of N); additional angular dispersion arises from Alfvenic distortion by non-thermal gas motions  
  > "Throughout the following discussion we principally compare the polarisation geometry in each region to three models: polarisation arising from grains aligned (1) with their major axes perpendicular to the large-scale 116$^{\circ}$ E of N magnetic field (polarisation angle $26^{\circ}$ E of N) observed on larger scales in the region" (Results and Discussion); "Additional dispersion in angle over our measured uncertainties ($\delta\theta < 3^{\circ}$) could result from Alfvénic distortion of the magnetic field by non-thermal gas motions (cite: davis1951a; chandrasekhar1953)." (Comparison of data and models)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo sampling (1000 realisations per sigma_theta) evaluated with two-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Kuiper non-parametric statistical tests  
  > "We then performed two-sided KS and Kuiper tests comparing the model, with added dispersion in angle, to the data. We repeated this process 1000 times for each value of $\sigma_{\theta}$ considered." (Comparison of data and models)
   - validation: Comparing KS test results with the cyclic-variable-appropriate Kuiper test  
  > "The two tests produce similar results, with the Kuiper test generally returning a more narrow range of angles over which the model and the data agree with a probability $p > 0.05$." (Comparison of data and models)
4. **Estimate characteristic grain alignment and damping timescales in the Anomalous Region/Fork**: Analytically evaluate and compare the Larmor precession timescale (tau_Lar), mechanical alignment timescale (tau_mech), radiative precession timescale (tau_rad,p), and gas damping timescale (tau_gas) as functions of grain size a.  
  > "We consider these hypotheses in turn below, and then as a check on our analysis confirm that the gas damping timescale is sufficiently long in the region to allow a preferential dust precession axis to exist." (Comparison of data and models); "$\tau_{gas}< \tau_{rad,p}$ holds only for unphysically small grains, with $a_{-5} < 10^{-10}$, at which size $\tau_{Lar} \ll \tau_{gas}$ would hold if such grains existed." (Gas damping timescale)
   - parameter: B ~ 10 mG (hat{B} ~ 2000), hat{chi} ~ 0.4 - 40, L > 10^4 L_sun, R ~ 3.5'' (~ 2 x 10^16 cm at 388 pc), T_eff ~ 1500 K (bar{lambda} ~ 1.9 um), T_d ~ T_gas ~ 135 K, n_H ~ 10^6 - 10^8 cm^-3  
  > "and so we adopt $\hat{B}\sim 2000$." (Larmor timescale); "The plane-of-sky separation between the Fork and Source I is $\sim 3.5$$''$, which at a distance of 388 pc corresponds to $\sim 2\times 10^{16}$ cm ($\sim 1400$ au)." (Radiative precession timescale); "$n_{\textsc{H}}\sim 10^{6} - 10^{8}$ cm$^{-3}$" (Gas damping timescale)
   - assumption: Paramagnetic grains with rho = 3 g cm^-3, axial ratio s = 0.5; unobscured radiation from Source I with anisotropy gamma ~ 1 and RAT efficiency |Q_Gamma| <= 0.4; thermal grain rotation (omega ~ omega_th); subsonic drift (Delta v < c_s) for v-MATs.  
  > "(cite: draine1996) gives values for magnetic susceptibility $\chi(0)$ in the range $4.2\times 10^{-5}-4.2\times10^{-3}$ for paramagnetic grains, and so we take $\hat{\chi}\sim 0.4-40$." (Larmor timescale); "As we are specifically considering the radiation field from Source I, which we expect to be strongly anisotropic, we take $\gamma\sim 1$. Note that this implies that radiation from Source I is effectively unobscured in the western arm of the Fork; a justifiable assumption if the polarised emission indeed arises from the Source I outflow cavity wall, which we discuss further below. We further take $|{Q_{\Gamma}}| \leq 0.4$ (cite: lazarian2007a; tazaki2017), and so $\gamma|{Q_{\Gamma}}|/0.01 \lesssim 40$." (Radiative precession timescale)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check that the gas damping timescale tau_gas is longer than precession timescales across physically plausible grain sizes  
  > "Thus grains in the Fork cannot have their alignments randomised by gas collisions faster than they can be induced to precess around either the magnetic field direction or the radiation anisotropy gradient by RATs." (Gas damping timescale)

## Qualitative findings

- In Orion Source I, unresolved Band 7 polarisation is parallel to the disc minor axis and likely arises from dust self-scattering in the optically thick, nearly edge-on disc, although the geometry is degenerate with concentric polarisation around BN/KL.  
  > "In Source I, we found an unresolved polarisation geometry parallel to the minor axis of the Source I disc, consistent with polarisation arising from dust self-scattering. The Source I disc is optically thick and viewed almost edge-on, supporting this interpretation; however, the predicted polarisation pattern is degenerate with that for a polarisation pattern concentric around the centre of the BN/KL explosion." (Summary)
- In the eastern arm of the Fork and in MF4/MF5, the polarisation geometry is concentric around the BN/KL explosion centre, tracing a magnetic field reordered into a radial configuration by the explosion.  
  > "In the eastern arm of the Anomalous Region/Fork, we found a polarisation geometry consistent with being concentric around the centre of the BN/KL explosion, consistent with the magnetic field having been reordered to be radial around the BN/KL explosion, as posited by (cite: tang2010) and (cite: cortes2020)." (Summary); "In MF4/MF5, we found a polarisation geometry consistent with being concentric around the BN/KL explosion centre, and so tracing a magnetic field that has been reordered to be radial around the BN/KL explosion." (Summary)
- In the western arm of the Fork, polarisation is concentric around Source I; moderately large grains (> 0.005-0.1 um) in unobscured outflow cavity walls may be aligned by k-RATs driven by Source I's radiation, or alternatively aligned by B-RATs tracing gas infall onto the Main Ridge.  
  > "While our estimates of both timescales are highly uncertain, we find $\tau_{rad,p}<\tau_{Lar}$ for moderately large grains ($>0.005-0.1\,\mu$m), suggesting that grains in this region may be aligned by radiative torques to precess around the radiation anisotropy gradient (k-RATs), i.e. to be perpendicular to the gradient of intensity from Source I." (Summary); "Alternatively, the grains may continue to precess around the magnetic field direction (B-RATs), and so could trace infall of material onto the Main Ridge/Hot Core region." (Summary)
- In the Main Ridge, grains remain aligned by B-RATs perpendicular to the large-scale magnetic field, likely because the dense gas in the Ridge has resisted disruption by the BN/KL explosion and high extinction shields the Ridge from Source I's radiation.  
  > "In the Main Ridge, we found a polarisation geometry consistent with that of the large-scale magnetic field in the region, and so determined that grains are aligned perpendicular to the magnetic field (B-RAT alignment)." (Summary); "The consistency with the large-scale magnetic field, despite recent findings that the magnetic field is energetically subdominant compared to the BN/KL outflow, may result from the field being well-coupled to gas in the Ridge that has not been disrupted by the BN/KL explosion." (Summary)
- In the Compact Ridge/MF1, the polarisation geometry is broadly similar to that of the large-scale magnetic field but shows ordered deviation (turning south at the periphery), possibly merging into the large-scale hourglass field of OMC-1.  
  > "In the Compact Ridge/MF1, likely located sufficiently far from the BN/KL explosion and Source I to remain uninfluenced by their effects, we found a polarisation geometry similar to, but showing ordered deviation from, being perpendicular to the large-scale magnetic field direction. The field may here be merging into the large-scale ‘hourglass’ field identified in single-dish observations." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 7 (881 um) polarimetry provides high spatial resolution and sensitivity in dense, high-mass star-forming gas around Source I to test whether a single grain alignment mechanism explains the complex polarisation morphology.  
  > "The polarimetric capabilities of the new generation of submillimetre telescopes, including the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), have made magnetic fields in dense, star-forming gas newly accessible (cite: cortes2016; kwon2019)." (Introduction); "In this paper we present ALMA Band 7 (0.88mm) observations of Source I and its surroundings, a subset of the area observed by (cite: cortes2020). We investigate whether a single model, or a single grain alignment mechanism, is sufficient to explain the complex polarisation morphology observed in the vicinity of Source I." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Two-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was chosen as the most widely-used distribution comparison statistic, and the Kuiper test was chosen as a more appropriate similarity measure for cyclic variables (angles).  
  > "We chose the KS test as the most widely-used statistic for comparing the similarity of two distributions, and the Kuiper test as a more appropriate measure of similarity for distributions of cyclic variables (cite: aizawa2020)." (Comparison of data and models)
- Alternative: Calculating polarisation fractions (P = PI / I) was rejected because the Stokes I map is much more dynamic-range-limited than Stokes Q and U, which would produce artificially large polarisation fractions.  
  > "We do not calculate polarisation fractions in this work, as the Stokes $I$ map is much more dynamic-range-limited than are the Stokes $Q$ and $U$ maps, as shown in Figure 2, and so any calculation of polarisation fraction is likely to produce artificially large values, particularly in low-surface-brightness regions." (Data)
- Alternative: Analysing the 12CO J=3->2 spectral window (spw3) was excluded because CO emission in outflows can be polarised by the Goldreich-Kylafis effect.  
  > "CO emission in outflows can be polarised up to a maximum of $\sim 3$% by the Goldreich-Kylafis effect (GK effect; (cite: goldreich1982; ching2016)), which has previously been observed in OMC-1 (cite: houde2013). Data from the spectral window dedicated to imaging this line (spw3) is therefore not considered in this work." (Data)
- Alternative: Rotating 'pseudo-disk' with a toroidal magnetic field in OMC-1 (proposed by Tang et al. 2010) was rejected based on ALMA studies of clump distances and velocities.  
  > "We consider this hypothesis to have been disfavoured by ALMA studies better determining the line-of-sight distances and velocities of the various OMC-1 clumps (cite: pagani2017)." (Distorted magnetic field)
- Alternative: Dust self-scattering in the western arm of the Fork was discounted because growing or entraining ~140 um grains into the outflow without destruction is unlikely.  
  > "However, transport of such large dust grains, as well as their avoiding destruction in the outflow in sufficient number to produce the observed polarisation pattern, does not seem likely (cite: giacalone2019). We thus discount this hypothesis, while noting that we cannot definitively rule it out." (Dust self-scattering)
- Alternative: Supersonic mechanical (Gold) alignment in the Fork was rejected because it predicts radial or outflow-parallel polarisation patterns inconsistent with the observations.  
  > "All of these geometries are inconsistent with the observed polarisation pattern shown in Figure 3, and so we do not consider Gold alignment further." (Supersonic mechanical (Gold) alignment)
- Alternative: Subsonic mechanical alignment torques (v-MATs) in the Fork were rejected because the mechanical alignment timescale is much longer than the Larmor timescale unless grains rotate subthermally.  
  > "The timescale for v-MAT alignment thus remains too long for this mechanism to be likely to be the main cause of grain alignment in the Fork." (Mechanical alignment timescale)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stokes I map is dynamic-range-limited compared to Stokes Q and U, preventing reliable calculation of polarisation fractions.  
  > "We do not calculate polarisation fractions in this work, as the Stokes $I$ map is much more dynamic-range-limited than are the Stokes $Q$ and $U$ maps, as shown in Figure 2, and so any calculation of polarisation fraction is likely to produce artificially large values, particularly in low-surface-brightness regions." (Data)
- Limitation (authors): Source I is unresolved (< 2 independent beams), and its minor-axis polarisation direction is degenerate with a BN/KL-concentric polarisation pattern.  
  > "Source I is effectively unresolved in our observations. We associate six Nyquist-sampled pixels (less than two independent measurements) with the source." (Source I); "The Source I disc is optically thick and viewed almost edge-on, supporting this interpretation; however, the predicted polarisation pattern is degenerate with that for a polarisation pattern concentric around the centre of the BN/KL explosion." (Summary)
- Limitation (authors): Estimates of Larmor and radiative precession timescales have large uncertainties (e.g., two orders of magnitude or more in magnetic susceptibility chi, unknown super-paramagnetic inclusions, and uncertain extinction/obscuration), preventing a definitive distinction between k-RAT and B-RAT alignment in the Fork.  
  > "The uncertainty on $\tau_{Lar}$ is thus difficult to quantify: (cite: cortes2020) give a formal uncertainty on $B$ of $\sim 20$%, but this is dwarfed by the two orders of magnitude (or larger) uncertainty on $\chi$ (cite: draine1996), and so we consider only the latter when estimating the range of plausible values of $\tau_{Lar}$." (Larmor timescale); "We emphasise that our estimates of both $\tau_{rad,p}$ and $\tau_{Lar}$ are highly uncertain. We do not have enough information to definitively identify grains in the Fork as being aligned either by k-RATs or by B-RATs." (Discussion of grain alignment in the Anomalous Region/Fork)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Continuum windows spw0-2 in the Orion Hot Core and OMC-1 contain rich molecular line forests; although the authors assume line polarisation is negligible in spw0-2, unsubtracted weakly polarised lines or line-wing contamination could bias Stokes Q and U in specific sub-regions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The KS and Kuiper tests are performed on 0.25'' pixels, which oversample the 0.54'' x 0.43'' beam by ~3-4 pixels per beam; pixel-to-pixel correlations in the noise and signal may affect the nominal p-values of the two-sample tests.
