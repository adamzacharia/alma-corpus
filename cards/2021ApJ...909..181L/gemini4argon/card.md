   - parameter: deconvolution threshold = 2 sigma, CLEAN mask = 1'' radius circular region

   - parameter: natural weighting and uvtaper = 0''.3, 0''.5, 0''.9; velocity resolution = 80 km s^-1 (default) or 50 km s^-1 (HAE 8)

## Qualitative findings

- Five out of sixteen targeted protocluster HAEs are detected in [C I] (1-0) (HAE 3, 4, 8, 9, and 16), all of which were previously detected in CO (3-2) and CO (4-3).  
  > "Five galaxies out of sixteen targeted galaxies are detected in the [C I ] line, and these are all previously detected in CO (3–2) and CO (4–3) and three in 1.1 mm dust continuum." (abstract); "We detect the [C I ] line from five galaxies previously detected in CO (3–2) and CO (4–3) lines, i.e., HAE 3, 4, 8, 9 and 16." (3.1. Detection of [C I ] and line properties compared to CO)
- The log L'_CO(4-3) - log L'_[CI](1-0) relation in the protocluster is super-linear, consistent with literature field galaxies, with a hint of a shallower slope and higher normalization that may indicate denser gas.  
  > "For log L ′ CO(4 − 3) − log L ′ [CI](1 − 0) relation, the slope is super-linear for the protocluster members, consistent with other best-fit using the literature values." (3.2. The L ′ [CI](1 − 0) – L ′ CO(J − J − 1) relation); "If we can apply a similar interpretation, a hint of a shallower slope (close to a linear relation) and a higher normalization factor in protocluster galaxies than other high- z main-sequence galaxies might hint at the existence of more denser gas in protocluster galaxies than high- z field main-sequence galaxies." (3.2. The L ′ [CI](1 − 0) – L ′ CO(J − J − 1) relation)
- Molecular gas masses from [C I], CO (3-2), and 1.1 mm dust agree within ~0.3 dex using field main-sequence calibrations, though CO-based estimates are ~40% (0.14 dex) higher on average, suggesting alpha_CO * R_13 may need to be lowered for some galaxies (HAE 3, 10, 16).  
  > "In summary, three gas tracers are broadly in good agreement for the inferred molecular masses within ≈ 0.3 dex, but CO-based gas measurements give ≈ 40% (0.14 dex) higher value than other gas tracers on average." (3.3. Molecular gas mass from [C I ], CO and dust); "Overall, there is a hint that the assumed α CO × R 13 needs to be lowered for HAE 3, 10, and 16 (thus reducing the CObased gas mass), though it is not conclusive for the remaining galaxies." (4.1. Self-consistent calibration from three tracers)
- The mean gas fraction of the protocluster galaxies for log(M_star/M_sun) = [10.6, 11.3] is comparable to field galaxies (~50%), while the gas depletion time scale decreases more rapidly with stellar mass than in field galaxies.  
  > "We generally reconfirm our previous findings that the mean gas fraction is comparable to field galaxies for a stellar mass range of log M star /M ⊙ = [10.6 , 11.3] ( f gas = M gas / ( M gas + M star ) ≈ 50%))." (5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION); "At least for these five [C I ] detected galaxies, the depletion time scale decreases more rapidly with stellar mass than field galaxies that might lead galaxies to quench earlier than field galaxies." (5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: [C I] (1-0) is an independent, optically thin molecular gas tracer less sensitive to excitation temperature and robust against low metallicity and cosmic rays, allowing an independent test of CO and dust gas mass calibrations in a protocluster.  
  > "Such a test is necessary by using an independent method, whether the calibration of field galaxies can be applied in the same manner. Fine structure lines of atomic carbon [C I ] is an alternative tracer of the molecular gas." (1. INTRODUCTION); "The lowest transition of [C I ] 3P1 − 3P0 (hereafter written simply as the [C I ] (1–0) transition) is optically thin in most cases, less sensitive to excitation temperature compared to [C I ] 3P2 − 3P1 (hereafter [C I ] (2–1)) when converting to a mass (Weiß et al. 2005)." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Subtracted continuum on 10'' x 10'' subcubes to improve subtraction for galaxies far from the phase center; reanalyzed CO (3-2), CO (4-3), and dust with the same imfit and aperture procedure to eliminate systematic errors from differing flux measurement methods.  
  > "We subtracted the continuum emission on the image domain using imcontsub (fitorder = 0) after we made smaller data cubes (10 ′′ × 10 ′′ ) centered on the target HAEs. This is to get improved results of continuum subtraction for galaxies not close to the phase center." (2.1. ALMA Band 4 observations); "We reanalyzed the CO (3–2), CO (4–3), and dust data for unified analyses to reduce the systematic errors coming from different flux measurement methods." (2.3. Reanalysis of previous CO and dust measurements)
- Alternative: Using a fixed 1''.0 circular aperture for all galaxies instead of S/N-optimized apertures (0''.7 for HAE 4); found to give consistent flux estimates.  
  > "However, we note that choosing 1 ′′ . 0 for all galaxies instead of different aperture sizes does not change our flux estimate." (2.3. Reanalysis of previous CO and dust measurements)
- Alternative: Using 50 km s^-1 vs 80 km s^-1 velocity binning for low S/N galaxies (HAE 3 and HAE 4); confirmed no change in line widths and fluxes within uncertainties.  
  > "For low S/N galaxies (HAE 4 and HAE 3), we also checked whether different velocity binnings (50 km s − 1 versus 80 km s − 1 ) change our results. We confirmed that the results did not change in terms of the line widths and fluxes within uncertainties between two different velocity binnings." (2.2. Detection criteria and flux measurements)
- Alternative: Metallicity-dependent alpha_CO correction; rejected because for log(M_star/M_sun) > 10.5 it only increases gas estimates by up to 0.07 dex for the lowest mass galaxies.  
  > "Here, we don’t correct for the metallicity-dependence of the α CO conversion factor. For the underlying stellar mass range that we observe (log ( M star /M ⊙ ) > 10.5), the metallicity-dependent α CO factor only increases the gas estimate the up to 0.07 dex for the lowest mass galaxies (HAE 16 and HAE 10) and does not affect higher mass regime." (3.3. Molecular gas mass from [C I ], CO and dust)
- Alternative: Recalibrating both X_CI (lowered by 0.35 dex) and dust temperature (T_d < 20 K) to match the CO-based gas mass in HAE 10; rejected as less likely than lowering alpha_CO * R_13.  
  > "To be consistent, α CO × R 13 should be lowered by a factor of ≈ 2.7, otherwise, both the X CI and dust temperature need to be recalibrated. The latter case is less likely, because X CI needs to be lowered by 0.35 dex, and the dust temperature should be lower than 20 K unless the difference is due to a higher gas-to-dust ratio." (C. GAS MASS CROSS CALIBRATION OF INDIVIDUAL GALAXIES)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size (low number statistics) and narrow dynamic range in line luminosities (less than an order of magnitude) prevent robust conclusions on systematic environmental differences in L'_CO - L'_[CI] relations.  
  > "We perform the same exercise for the protocluster galaxies only while keeping in mind that the dynamic range is much narrower i.e., less than an order of magnitude in luminosities." (3.2. The L ′ [CI](1 − 0) – L ′ CO(J − J − 1) relation); "The current data set is not sufficient to robustly conclude because of the the low number statistics and unknown systematic biases for currently available data sets in different environments." (3.2. The L ′ [CI](1 − 0) – L ′ CO(J − J − 1) relation)
- Limitation (authors): Without both [C I] (1-0) and [C I] (2-1) transitions, the excitation factor Q_10 cannot be measured directly and must be assumed.  
  > "Without measurements of both [C I ] (1–0) and [C I ] (2–1), we cannot measure Q 10 ." (3.3. Molecular gas mass from [C I ], CO and dust)
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution and sensitivity are insufficient to study spatially resolved gas excitation conditions within individual galaxies.  
  > "Discussing the resolved gas excitation conditions is beyond the scope of this paper, and this would need more sensitive and higher angular resolution observations to get more details." (3.1. Detection of [C I ] and line properties compared to CO)
- Limitation (authors): Potential selection biases from the flux-limited H alpha narrow-band selection and the specific halo properties of a single protocluster.  
  > "With limited number of galaxies detected in [C I ] (also CO and dust), it is difficult to verify whether there is any selection bias delivering such mass dependency. Such bias could originate not only from the flux-limited galaxy selection (H α ) but also from the halo property (mass, evolutionary stage) of the protocluster." (5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-line CO (4-3) detections (HAE 1, 2, 7, 12, 23) have lower significance upon reanalysis and are excluded from the main line ratio fits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Literature CO (3-2) vs [C I] comparison sample is heavily biased toward high-z DSFGs with only one main-sequence galaxy.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spatial and velocity offsets between [C I], CO (3-2), and CO (4-3) in merging/extended sources (HAE 3, 4, 9) introduce aperture and velocity-range sensitivity in flux ratios.
