## Analysis recipe

1. **Make channel maps and peak-intensity maps of SiO, CH3OH and CO**: Image lines with natural weighting and uv taper; compute peak intensity (moment 8) maps; smooth high-resolution maps with a 5 km/s boxcar  
  > "moment 8 in the CASA task" (3 Morphology of Jets and Outflows); "the maximum value of the spectrum in each pixel" (3 Morphology of Jets and Outflows)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Velocities relative to Vsys = 10.13 km/s; SiO slow |dV|<15, fast 15-40, EHV >40 km/s  
  > "slow ($|\Delta V|<15$ km s$^{-1}$), fast (15 km s$^{-1}$$<|\Delta V|<40$ km s$^{-1}$), and extremely high velocity (EHV, $|\Delta V|>40$ km s$^{-1}$)" (3 Morphology of Jets and Outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive ballistic ejection times of sub-knots from position-velocity gradients**: Measure velocity gradients versus distance in the PV diagram and convert to ejection times as a function of inclination  
  > "Therefore the sub-knots HR1a and HR1b would have been ejected 7.5 tan($i$) and 17 tan($i$) years ago, respectively, where $i$ is the inclination of jet from the plane of sky." (4 Position velocity diagrams of jets and outflows)
   - parameter: Velocity gradients 270 and 120 km/s/arcsec  
  > "velocity gradients along the distance from the origin with 270 km s$^{-1}$ arcsec$^{-1}$ and 120 km s$^{-1}$ arcsec$^{-1}$" (4 Position velocity diagrams of jets and outflows)
   - assumption: HR1 knots ballistically ejected  
  > "The HR1 knots could have been ballistically ejected." (4 Position velocity diagrams of jets and outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ejection times consistent with the periodic jet model times for HR1a and HR1b  
  > "The former two time scales are consistent with the dynamical time scales of HR1a (5.7 years) and HR1b (13 years)" (5 Jet Models)
3. **Derive jet inclination, velocity and period with a periodic-velocity jet model**: Solve analytic relations between observed (Vobs, dVobs, Dobs, dDobs) and intrinsic (Vj, dVj, P, i) quantities using red-shifted EHV knots HR2a and HR4  
  > "Therefore, the parameters of the jets ($V_j$, $\Delta V_j$, $P$, and $i$) can be derived from the observed parameters ($V_{\rm obs}$, $\Delta V_{\rm obs}$, $D_{\rm obs}$, and $\Delta D_{\rm obs}$)." (5 Jet Models); "The jet model is applied to the red-shifted EHV SiO knots HR2a and HR4." (5 Jet Models)
   - parameter: Dobs 2.8 +/- 0.3 arcsec; dDobs 1.8 +/- 0.3 arcsec; Vobs 55 +/- 5 km/s; dVobs 15 km/s  
  > "$D_{\rm obs}$ & 2.$''$8 $\pm$ 0.$''$3" (Table (jet model)); "$V_{\rm obs}$ & 55 $\pm$ 5 km s$^{-1}$" (Table (jet model))
   - assumption: No energy dissipation; sideways-ejection velocity equals dVj  
  > "when no energy dissipation is assumed and the sideways-ejection velocity is equal to $\Delta V_j$" (5 Jet Models)
   - assumption: HR3 excluded; HR1d not used as first knot  
  > "The knot HR3 is ignored because it does not show such a characteristic velocity pattern." (5 Jet Models); "Therefore, the knot HR1d is not used as the first knot in our jet model." (5 Jet Models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Including HR3 gives inclination 20 deg, period 13 yr, jet velocity 155 km/s; they prefer the model without HR3  
  > "Thus, we prefer the jet model without HR3." (5 Jet Models)
4. **Measure knot width and derive SiO and CO column densities, abundance ratio, knot mass and mass ejection rate**: imfit width of HR1a in high-resolution SiO; LVG model for CO and SiO; mass and ejection rate from column density, knot length, inclination and jet velocity  
  > "Using the CASA task" (6.1 SiO and CO); "We derive the SiO and CO column densities using a Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) model (cite: vanderTak2007; Lee2013)." (6.1 SiO and CO)
   - software: CASA, LVG model, LAMDA database
   - parameter: Gas density ~1e7 cm-3, gas temperature 100-500 K; HR1a length 123 +/- 13 mas, inclination 37 deg, jet velocity 91 km/s  
  > "Assuming dense ($\sim$10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$) and warm gas (100–500 K) conditions" (6.1 SiO and CO); "when the length of HR1a (123 $\pm$ 13 mas), the inclination (37$^\circ$), and the jet velocity (91 km s$^{-1}$) are adopted" (6.1 SiO and CO)
   - assumption: CO abundance 1e-4; line-of-sight length equals the knot width; CO 3-2 and SiO 8-7 trace the same gas  
  > "if the CO abundance is 10$^{-4}$ and the length of the line of sight is the same as the knot width (16.7 au)" (6.1 SiO and CO); "so we assumed that the CO 3–2 and SiO 8–7 lines trace the same gas" (6.1 SiO and CO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Derived gas density 0.4-1.2e8 cm-3 consistent with the initial assumption  
  > "then the derived gas density is 0.4–1.2 $\times$ 10$^8$ cm$^{-3}$, which is consistent with our initial assumption" (6.1 SiO and CO)
5. **Fit CH3OH line ratios with an LVG model at six emission peaks**: LVG fit of multiple CH3OH lines at CR1-3 and CB1-3 for column density, H2 density, temperature and emitting area  
  > "The multiple CH$_3$OH lines are fitted by the Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) model (cite: vanderTak2007; Lee2013)" (6.2 CH3OH)
   - software: LVG model, LAMDA database
   - assumption: A- and E-type CH3OH have the same abundance  
  > "We assume that the abundances of A- and E-type CH$_3$OH are the same." (6.2 CH3OH)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Estimate protostellar mass, jet-launching radius and dust sublimation radius**: Mass accretion rate from ejection rate via an assumed ratio, then stellar mass from luminosity, stellar radius and accretion rate; Rjet and Rs from analytic formulae  
  > "which results in a mass accretion rate of 0.6–1.7 $\times$10$^{-5} M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$" (7.1 Protostellar mass); "The stellar mass is then found to be between 0.025–0.07 $M_\odot$." (7.1 Protostellar mass)
   - parameter: Lbol 5.3 Lsun, Rstar 2 Rsun scaling; QR = 4, Tsub = 1500 K  
  > "Here, we adopt Q$_R$= 4 and T$_{\rm sub}$= 1500 K (cite: Dullemond2001)." (7.1 Protostellar mass)
   - assumption: Ejection-to-accretion rate ratio similar to other Class 0 protostars; Lbol of the whole HOPS 373 system attributed to SW  
  > "Class 0 protostars have a similar ratio of mass ejection rate to mass accretion rate" (7.1 Protostellar mass); "which is the total luminosity of HOPS 373 including the SW and NE sources" (7.1 Protostellar mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rjet (~0.03 au) compared with other jets and with Rs (0.16 au)  
  > "which is consistent with that in other jets (cite: Lee2020)" (7.1 Protostellar mass)
7. **Estimate inclination and dynamical times of Outflow2**: Use peak SiO knot intensity to infer a shock velocity (~30 km/s) from shock models, then derive inclination from observed peak velocities and ejection times  
  > "The peak temperature of 19–40 K is produced by a $\sim$30 km s$^{-1}$ shock if the pre-shock density is 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: Gusdorf2008)." (7.2 Dynamical time scales for the two outflows)
   - assumption: Pre-shock density 1e6 cm-3, shock velocity 30 km/s  
  > "For a shock velocity of 30 km s$^{-1}$" (7.2 Dynamical time scales for the two outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Test whether a close binary precession model reproduces the SiO wiggle**: Parametric precession model with beta, spatial period Vj*tau_p, phase angle and inclination, using Vj, P and i from the jet model  
  > "The wiggle motion is roughly reproduced by the precession model assuming $M_p/M_c$=1" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
   - parameter: beta = 8 deg, phi = 50 deg, Mp/Mc = 1, binary separation 4.4 au for ~50 yr, 1.3 au for ~8 yr, Mstar 0.035 Msun  
  > "$\beta$= 8$^\circ$, and $\phi$= 50$^\circ$" (7.3 Episodic accretions); "Adopting a protostellar mass of 0.035 $M_\odot$, the variation with $\sim$8 years and $\sim$50 years could be induced by events at 1.3 au and 4.4 au, respectively" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
   - assumption: Equal-mass protostar and companion  
  > "assuming $M_p/M_c$=1" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative match of curves to the SiO knots in Figure 1  
  > "This putative close binary could reproduce the wiggle pattern of the SiO EHV emission" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
9. **Infer past burst luminosity and timing from the extended CO snowline**: Measure C18O 2-1 FWHM with imfit on ACA data and compare with the value expected for the current luminosity; use N2D+ peak offset; bracket timing by CO and water freeze-out time scales  
  > "The C$^{18}$O emission has the Full Width Half Maximum (FWHM) of 4.$''$63 $\pm$ 0.$''$61" (7.3 Episodic accretions); "Thus, we expect that this recent burst occurred between the freeze-out time scales for the CO and the water" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: CO sublimation temperature 21 K; H2 density ~5e5 cm-3 at the CO snowline  
  > "when the CO sublimation temperature is 21 K (cite: Frimann2017)" (7.3 Episodic accretions); "The number density of molecular hydrogen around the observed CO snowline is $\sim$5 $\times$ 10$^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
   - assumption: Extended CO snowline set by a past accretion burst of HOPS 373SW  
  > "HOPS 373SW is very likely the dominant trigger for the extended CO snowline" (7.3 Episodic accretions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Slow SiO and CH3OH trace U-shaped bow shocks around two misaligned CO outflows (NE-SW Outflow1 and SE-NW Outflow2)  
  > "The slow moving SiO and CH$_3$OH show two distinct bow shocks which surround two separate CO outflows" (8 Summary)
- EHV SiO knots show a wiggle consistent with precession  
  > "These knots also show a wiggle motion previously noted in the CO and H$_2$ emissions by (cite: Yoon2022)." (8 Summary)
- EHV gas has a higher SiO/CO abundance ratio than slow components; slow SiO likely from grain sputtering in the bow shock  
  > "The EHV component has a higher abundance ratio of SiO to CO than the slow components" (6.1 SiO and CO); "Instead, the observed slow-moving SiO likely originated from grain sputtering within the bow shock." (6.1 SiO and CO)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA data show prominent jets and bow shocks ejected from HOPS 373SW  
  > "High spatial resolution ALMA observations show prominent jets and bow shocks ejected from HOPS 373SW." (Introduction)
- Why this method: SiO traces the fastest outflow gas and CH3OH low-velocity gas, so both are used as shock tracers  
  > "the CH$_3$OH emission favors a low-velocity gas, while the SiO emission is more abundant in the fastest gas (cite: Tafalla2010)" (3 Morphology of Jets and Outflows)
- Alternative: HR1d as the first knot of the jet model rejected because its distance (~0.8 arcsec) is shorter than the knot interval  
  > "Therefore, the knot HR1d is not used as the first knot in our jet model." (5 Jet Models)
- Alternative: Jet model including HR3 rejected in favor of the model without HR3  
  > "Thus, we prefer the jet model without HR3." (5 Jet Models)
- Alternative: Keplerian mass from 13CH3OH rotation rejected (<0.001 Msun) as the signal likely traces an infalling rotating envelope  
  > "Thus, the observed velocity features near HOPS 373SW might trace the infalling rotating envelope" (7.1 Protostellar mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Blue-shifted velocity coverage ends at -70 km/s so the fastest blue SiO may be missed  
  > "Thus, we cannot rule out that the highest velocity component in the blue-shifted SiO emission has been missed." (3 Morphology of Jets and Outflows)
- Limitation (authors): CO emission of Outflow2 may be resolved out  
  > "The CO outflow associated with Outflow2 might be resolved out" (3 Morphology of Jets and Outflows)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar mass, Rjet and Rs may be smaller if HOPS 373SW luminosity is below the total system 5.3 Lsun  
  > "could be smaller than the values estimated here if the bolometric luminosity of HOPS 373SW itself is lower than 5.3 $L_\odot$" (7.1 Protostellar mass)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Jet-model uncertainties are large (P = 50 +/- 26 yr) and rest on only two knots (HR2a, HR4).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass ejection and SiO abundances depend on assumed density, temperature range and CO abundance, with no fit uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Binary and reorientation scenarios are not distinguished by the data.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar mass depends on an assumed ejection/accretion ratio and stellar radius.
