## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure the 100 GHz continuum flux and identify hosts**: Continuum image over the full 30 GHz bandwidth with natural weighting; Aegean run with all default parameters for photometry  
  > "We ran Aegean with all defaults parameters to derive the photometry" (3.3.1 Continuum image and flux extraction)
   - software: CASA, Aegean
   - parameter: natural weighting; centred at 100 GHz  
  > "using natural weighting in order to maximise sensitivity for each source" (3.3.1 Continuum image and flux extraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum emission compared with the HAWK-I Ks positions to identify the host  
  > "The ALMA emission is associated with the eastern $K_s-$band source." (4.1 Multi-wavelength morphologies)
2. **Extract spectra at the host positions**: Taper cubes by a 3'' Gaussian, smooth to 80 MHz channels, extract spectra in a 0.8'' radius aperture on the Ks positions and subtract a sky-annulus background  
  > "we therefore extracted spectra over $0.8''$ radius centred on the $K_s$ positions" (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection); "From this we subtracted a background spectrum created from a sky annulus (radius of $3.5-5.5''$)." (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 3'' Gaussian taper; 20 to 80 MHz channel smoothing; annulus 3.5-5.5''  
  > "We also smoothed the data in frequency space from 20 to 80 MHz channels." (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Detect emission lines in the 1D spectra**: 1D line-finder with a low threshold (3 sigma, width larger than four channels; 2.5 sigma for 0917); detections checked at 20, 35, 40, 60 and 75 MHz channel width  
  > "The only exception is 0917, where we used a lower threshold" (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection); "we verified they were also successfully detected in higher resolution spectra with 20, 35, 40, 60 and 75 MHz-channel-width" (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection)
   - software: sslf
   - parameter: f_thres > 3 sigma, width larger than four channels (2.5 sigma for 0917)  
  > "f$_{\rm thres}>3\sigma$ and linewidth" (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Lines re-detected at finer channel widths; the 108.21 GHz line of 0913 rejected as likely spurious from line contours on the Ks image  
  > "Overlaying the contours of the two lines on the $K_s$-band image shows that the 108.21 GHz line is very likely spurious." (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
4. **Determine redshifts from the line frequencies**: Lines examined in decreasing S/N assuming CO, predicting other CO, [CI] and H2O lines at each trial redshift (splatalogue); then a simultaneous Gaussian fit with a redshift prior using equal amplitude and width for all lines plus a constant continuum  
  > "adding the corresponding number of Gaussians required to reproduce the spectra (note that we assume the exact same amplitude and width for all lines)" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra); "We also add a continuum component as a constant across the frequency range" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
   - software: splatalogue
   - parameter: Continuum modelled as a constant, except 0918 where a spectral index is added  
  > "We do not consider the HCN and HCO+ lines as they are expected to be much fainter." (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
   - assumption: All detected lines are CO (plus [CI], H2O predicted) and share one amplitude and width in the fit; HCN and HCO+ ignored  
  > "We do not consider the HCN and HCO+ lines as they are expected to be much fainter." (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency of candidate redshifts with the K-z relation, radio luminosity, predicted CO flux and photometric redshift  
  > "related radio luminosity (see Fig. [8](#fig:radio_lum)), $K_s$ luminosity (see Fig. [7](#fig:Kz)) and the predicted CO flux (see Fig. [9](#fig:CO_prediction)) are not compatible for such a low redshift source" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
5. **Test whether the 0917 lines are noise**: Run the line-finder on simulated spectra with the same Gaussian noise rms and no signal, counting cases with three lines above 2.5 sigma within a shift of less than 3 channels  
  > "we generated simulated spectra with the same Gaussian noise rms, but no true signal" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra); "out of $10^6$ simulated spectra only 626 cases (corresponding to a 0.06% chance)" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
   - software: sslf
   - parameter: 10^6 simulated spectra; threshold 2.5 sigma; line width larger than four channels  
  > "out of $10^6$ simulated spectra only 626 cases (corresponding to a 0.06% chance)" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
   - assumption: Gaussian noise; conservative shift of less than 3 channel bins between line centres  
  > "Assuming a conservative frequency difference between the central frequency of the different CO transition lines to be a shift of $<3$ channel bins" (4.3 Redshift determination from the ALMA spectra)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit the radio SED including the ALMA continuum point**: Fit simple, double and triple power-law models to the total integrated flux from 76 MHz to 100 GHz and compare with AICc  
  > "using three different models to fit the data: a simple power-law, a double power-law with a break frequency and a triple power-law with a double break frequency" (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs); "We compare the relative likelihood for each model with the Akaike Information Criteria" (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs)
   - software: MrMoose
   - parameter: Calibration uncertainties of 8% (GLEAM), 3% (NVSS), 10% (ALMA) added in quadrature  
  > "We assume 8% for GLEAM (cite: hurley-walker_galactic_2017), 3% for NVSS (cite: condon_nrao_1998) and 10% for ALMA." (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs)
   - assumption: ALMA continuum point of 0918 excluded from the fit  
  > "the ALMA continuum point is not included in the model fitting" (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs)
   - fitting: MrMoose fits; AICc used for model comparison; sampler and priors not stated  
  > "with $n$ the number of data points, $k$ the number of free parameters and ln$(L)$ the maximum of the likelihood function (calculated with" (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs)
   - validation: AICc comparison: double power-law preferred overall  
  > "The AICc criteria indicates that the model which best represents the sources overall is the double power-law model." (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs)
7. **Estimate CO luminosity and compare with predicted CO detectability**: Predict integrated CO line flux versus redshift for assumed SLEDs and intrinsic L'CO, compare with the 0.3 mJy/beam rms per 80 MHz channel; estimate L'CO for 0856  
  > "by assuming a spectral line energy distribution (SLED, the three panels, see insets) and an intrinsic L$^\prime_{\rm CO}$, we can predict the integrated flux in each of the CO lines" (4.4.3 Molecular gas properties and predictions)
   - parameter: line width range 200-1000 km/s; rms 0.3 mJy/beam per 80 MHz channel  
  > "We also report our rms sensitivity ($\sigma$=0.3 mJy/beam per 80 MHz channel-width)" (4.4.3 Molecular gas properties and predictions)
   - assumption: Assumed CO SLED (three SLED cases)  
  > "by assuming a spectral line energy distribution (SLED, the three panels, see insets)" (4.4.3 Molecular gas properties and predictions)

   - validation: Compared with other radio galaxies and quasars at similar redshift

## Qualitative findings

- Higher-resolution ALMA continuum resolves the host identification: for 0913 and 0918 the VIKING counterparts had been offset by about 6'' from the GLEAM position.  
  > "This demonstrates that higher resolution continuum data is essential to identify the host galaxy." (4.1 Multi-wavelength morphologies)
- Double power-law radio SEDs are preferred, with break frequency and a steeper high-frequency index.  
  > "The AICc criteria indicates that the model which best represents the sources overall is the double power-law model." (4.2.1 Radio broad-band SEDs)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Four sources close together in G09 shared overheads; a Band 3 scan gives a sub-arcsecond continuum image for host identification and lines for redshift  
  > "As our four sources were close together in G09 we could optimise our observing strategy by sharing overheads." (3.3 ALMA Band 3 follow-up observations); "to obtain simultaneously a deep, sub-arcsecond resolution continuum image to identify the host galaxy in the HAWK-I $K_s$ imaging" (2.4 Pilot project on the GAMA 09 field)
- Why this method: Taper and frequency smoothing to improve S/N for weak lines in poor weather  
  > "As the expected line strength was low and the atmospheric conditions non-optimal" (3.3.2 Spectral cubes, spectrum extraction and line detection)
