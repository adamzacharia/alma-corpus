## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive stellar parameters**: Bayesian inference on a PARSEC v1.2S model grid using Gaia absolute G magnitude, BP-RP colour and [Fe/H], with a pre-main-sequence prior  
  > "we applied the Bayesian inference code of (cite: delburgo2016; delburgo2018) on a grid of stellar evolution models constructed from the PARSEC v1.2S tracks (cite: bressan2012; chen2014; chen2015; tang2014)" (3.1 Fundamental stellar parameters); "Imposing the prior that it is on the pre-main sequence and using the aforementioned inputs to feed the Bayesian inference code, we inferred the fundamental parameters of HD 138965" (3.1 Fundamental stellar parameters)
   - software: Bayesian inference code of delburgo2016/2018, PARSEC v1.2S
   - parameter: Inputs: M_G, G_BP-G_RP, [Fe/H]  
  > "fed by the three following input parameters: the absolute $G$ magnitude $M_{G}$, the colour $G_{\rm BP}-G_{\rm RP}$, and the iron to hydrogen \[Fe/H\]" (3.1 Fundamental stellar parameters)
   - assumption: Null extinction  
  > "We reasonably assumed null extinction when deriving the colour" (3.1 Fundamental stellar parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Re-run without the evolutionary-stage prior gives age 805 ± 468 Ma, within 1 sigma of the Gaia-based 350 Ma  
  > "As a validation test, we fed our Bayesian code with the same three input parameters but without imposing the prior on the evolution stage, finding that the age significantly increases to 805$~\pm~$468 Ma." (3.1 Fundamental stellar parameters)
2. **Fit the ALMA continuum visibilities with a Gaussian belt**: Single Gaussian belt model, RADMC-3D images converted to model visibilities with Galario, sampled with emcee  
  > "We model the disc architecture as a single Gaussian belt." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission); "Each set of parameters is used to generate a disc model using the radiative transfer code RADMC-3D (cite: 2012Dullemond) which is then converted into synthetic visibilities using Galario (cite: 2018Tazzari)." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
   - software: emcee, RADMC-3D, Galario
   - parameter: 90 walkers, 1,000 steps (90,000 realisations); posterior from last 10,000 realisations  
  > "In total, we created 90,000 realisations of the model (90 walkers, 1,000 steps)." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission); "The final posterior probability distribution used the last 10,000 realisations" (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
   - parameter: Priors for radius, inclination, position angle normal with sd 3% of initial value, from Herschel  
  > "These priors are normally distributed with a standard deviation 3% of the initial value and bounds informed by the likely range of each parameter." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
   - assumption: Star at the phase centre; offsets of the disc centre fitted  
  > "between the star (assumed to be at the phase centre of the observations) and the disc centre" (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
   - fitting: emcee; walkers initialised at Herschel maximum-likelihood values; binned model visibilities compared to data by least-squares  
  > "Walkers are initialised at the maximum likelihood values for the disc parameters" (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission); "These model visibilities are binned and compared to the ALMA observations through least-squares fitting." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
   - validation: Posteriors monomodal and approximately normal; residuals shown after subtracting the maximum likelihood model  
  > "The posteriors are all monomodal and approximately normally distributed." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
3. **Search for CO (2-1) gas emission**: Mask the cube to the disc annulus, shift spectra by projected Keplerian velocity, interpolate to common velocities and sum  
  > "$R_{\rm peak} -$HWHM to $R_{\rm peak} +$HWHM and with a flux density $\geq$3-$\sigma$ being included in the summation." (3.3 Millimetre CO line emission); "For each pixel included in the mask we shift the spectrum’s frequency according to the projected Keplerian velocity, interpolate these individual spectra to a set of common velocities and sum these to produce a final CO spectrum." (3.3 Millimetre CO line emission)
   - parameter: Spectrum centred on stellar radial velocity of -9.38 km/s from Gaia DR3  
  > "centred at the stellar radial velocity of -9.38 km/s taken from" (3.3 Millimetre CO line emission)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation with the continuum-derived disc extent and orientation  
  > "We use the spatially resolved extent and orientation of the disc from the continuum image to identify the region of the spectral window covering the CO (2-1) line" (3.3 Millimetre CO line emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the CO level predicted by Kral et al.  
  > "which is around a factor of three above the predicted CO emission level of 1.1$\times10^{-23}$ W/m$^{2}$" (3.3 Millimetre CO line emission)
4. **Constrain the inner belt from the SED**: Stardust SED fit with inner and outer belts free, then re-run with fixed inner-belt s_min, q and fractional width, leaving only radius and mass free  
  > "we had the following components to be free parameters for both the inner and outer belts: belt radius ($r_m$) and width ($\sigma_r$), minimum grain size ($s_{\rm min}$), dust mass ($M_s$), and the size distribution component ($q$) to initially vary between 3 and 4" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution); "The mean radial distance of the inner belt and the mass were kept as free parameters." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - software: Stardust, emcee
   - parameter: Fixed for inner belt: s_min = 12 um, q = 3.5, Delta r/r = 0.3  
  > "minimum grain size of 12 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$, size distribution component to be 3.5 (cite: 1969Dohnanyi), and the disc fractional width ($\Delta r/r$) to be 0.3" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: Maximum grain size 3000 um; inner belt astronomical silicate  
  > "we assume the maximum size of the dust grains to be 3000 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ and the inner belt to have a grain composition of astronomical silicate (cite: 2003Draine)" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: Outer belt grains pure astronomical silicate when fixing the inner belt  
  > "we performed an initial analysis to estimate its characteristics under the assumption that the outer belt grains are composed of pure astronomical silicate" (5 Discussion)
   - fitting: emcee posterior (corner plot in Appendix B); solution did not converge  
  > "This scenario still did not converge to a solution" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - validation: Reduced minimum chi^2 below one  
  > "it did produce a reasonable goodness-of-fit to the data where our reduced minimum $\chi^2$ test was less than one" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
5. **Compare outer-belt dust compositions**: Stardust + emcee for each composition (astronomical silicate mixed with crystalline/amorphous water-ice, amorphous carbon or vacuum at volume fractions 0.5, 0.25, 0.1), outer belt radius and width fixed to ALMA values, ranked by BIC  
  > "The outer, cooler belt was assumed to have a fixed peak surface density at 150 au with a standard deviation of 49 au" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution); "we assumed the grains to be astronomical silicate combined with an inclusion material with a volume fraction of 0.5, 0.25, and 0.1 for each material" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - software: Stardust, emcee
   - parameter: s_min set to the blowout limit of each composition; q allowed 2 to 5  
  > "we set the minimum grain size in the outer belt to be the blowout limit of the corresponding grain composition" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution); "we allowed to vary between 2 and 5 to account for potential size-dependent velocity distributions" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: BIC with k = 3 or 4 and N = 15  
  > "In our scenarios, we have $k = 3$ (for outer belt composed of only astronomical silicate) or $k = 4$ (for outer belt composed of mixed grains), and $N = 15$." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: Inner belt fixed from the pure-silicate run; may bias toward astronomical silicate  
  > "This may introduce an inherent bias toward astronomical silicate being the best solution for the outer belt." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - fitting: emcee, 80 walkers, 4,000 steps (320,000 realisations), first 500 steps burn-in, final 280,000 realisations used  
  > "We ran a total of 320 000 realisations for each model with 80 walkers and 4 000 steps." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution); "We used the first 500 steps as burn-in for the MCMC chains and calculated probability distributions from the final 280 000 realisations." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
   - validation: Delta BIC thresholds (<2 indistinguishable); pure astronomical silicate best, 50:50 and carbon mixtures ruled out  
  > "If $\Delta BIC < 2$, the models are indistinguishable;" (3.4 Spectral energy distribution); "We can however rule out the 50:50 compositions for both amorphous and crystalline water ice, and all three of the carbon:astronomical silicate compositions tested here." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
6. **Constrain scattered-light brightness, albedo and companion masses from imaging**: Inject model discs into SHARDDS data and reduce with classical ADI; RSM contrast curve converted to mass with AMES-COND; HST/ACS surface-brightness limit converted to albedo limit  
  > "we inject model discs with an architecture consistent with the ALMA observation into the data cube. The cube was then reduced and stacked using the classical ADI algorithm." (3.5 Constraints from scattered light); "The masses were converted from the measured contrast using the AMES-COND grid of models (cite: 2001Allard)." (3.5 Constraints from scattered light)
   - software: Regime Switching Model
   - parameter: Ages 30 ± 10 Ma, 45 ± 5 Ma and 348 (+39/-54) Ma  
  > "The mass limit as a function of separation was calculated for ages of 30 $\pm$ 10 Ma (our inferred age), 45 $\pm$ 5 Ma (Argus association membership), and 348$^{+39}_{-54}$ Ma (from *Gaia*)." (3.5 Constraints from scattered light)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model-based albedo cross-check from Q_abs and Q_sca (omega = 0.56 over all angles; ~0.1 for restricted scattering angles)  
  > "We infer a scattering albedo of $\omega = 0.56$ over all angles at a wavelength of 1.6 $\mu$m" (3.5 Constraints from scattered light)
7. **Infer planetary companion mass limits from belt architecture**: Single-planet sculpting and self-stirring calculations following Pearce et al. using the belt inner edge and stellar parameters  
  > "We can therefore place constraints on the presence of a planetary companion stirring or sculpting the belt following the analysis presented in (cite: 2022Pearce)." (5 Discussion)
   - parameter: Disc inner edge at 101 au (R - sigma_R)  
  > "we assume the disc inner edge to lie at 101 au (i.e. $R$ - $\sigma_{\rm R}$)" (5 Discussion)
   - assumption: Single planet sculpting, or self-stirring by planetesimals up to 100 km  
  > "we can constrain the total mass of the debris disc to be at least $400~\pm~100~M_{\oplus}$ in bodies up to 100 km in size" (5 Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Matthews et al. high-contrast limit and the SHARDDS contrast limit  
  > "This is a factor of five improvement over the previous limit from (cite: 2018Matthews)" (5 Discussion)

- **Galario** : Converting models to synthetic visibilities

## Qualitative findings

- Pure astronomical silicate is the best-fitting outer-belt composition, but at least 10% water-ice inclusions cannot be rejected  
  > "We find astronomical silicate is the best fit for the dust composition. However, we could not reject possible scenarios where there are at least 10% water-ice inclusions." (abstract)
- A 2-sigma NW/SE ansa brightness asymmetry is seen but may not be real  
  > "A 2-$\sigma$ brightness asymmetry between the NW and SE ansae of the disc is seen in the imaging data." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
- No CO emission detected and no emission from the warm belt or star  
  > "We find no evidence for any CO emission associated with the disc" (3.3 Millimetre CO line emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Existing imaging had not resolved the outer belt width, leaving companion constraints weak  
  > "Existing imaging observations have not spatially resolved the width of the outer belt of HD 138965 (cite: 2021Marshall), so the constraints derived from such calculations remain weak." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Multi-wavelength resolved imaging with radiative transfer modelling weakens dust-property/architecture degeneracies  
  > "is necessary to weaken degeneracies inherent between the dust properties and architecture (cite: 2014aMarshall)" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: 50:50 water-ice and all carbon:silicate mixtures ruled out  
  > "We can however rule out the 50:50 compositions for both amorphous and crystalline water ice, and all three of the carbon:astronomical silicate compositions tested here." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): No constraint on vertical scale height because of limited resolution, S/N and inclination  
  > "Due to the limited angular resolution, signal-to-noise, and inclination we have no constraint on the disc’s vertical scale height." (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): Inner belt fixed from a pure-silicate run may bias the composition result  
  > "This may introduce an inherent bias toward astronomical silicate being the best solution for the outer belt." (3.4 Spectral energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Larger than expected flux calibration uncertainty from bad weather  
  > "This indicates a larger than expected flux uncertainty for the calibration, and therefore imaging, of HD 138965." (2.1 ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Brightness asymmetry may not be real given calibration and uncertainties  
  > "Given the large uncertainties and calibration of these observations it is not necessarily a real feature of the disc, but should motivate future observations." (3.2 Millimetre continuum emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disc fit is on a single 40 min low-resolution (1.4'') dataset with an acknowledged flux calibration problem, so the width and composition conclusions depend on the SED model assumptions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Composition ranking uses only N = 15 data points with k = 3-4 parameters, so the BIC differences are weakly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inner belt is fixed from a pure-silicate fit, which biases the outer-belt composition comparison
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abstract (a <= 78 au) and Discussion (a <= 74 au) disagree on the semi-major axis limit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Stardust code is not stated to be public
