## Analysis recipe

1. **Evolve the dust distribution**: Evolve dust surface density and the peak particle mass with coagulation and radial drift.  
  > "we follow how the peaks of dust surface density $\Sigma_{\rm d}$ and mass $m_{\rm p}$ change with coagulation and radial drift." (Disk Model)
   - parameter: Initial gas surface density: 1.7 × 10^3 (r / 1 AU)^(-3/2) g cm^-2  
  > "{\Sigma}_{\rm g} = 1.7\times10^3\left(\frac{r}{1\ {\rm AU}}\right)^{-3/2} {\rm g~cm^{-2}}" (Disk Model)
   - parameter: Initial particle size: 0.1 μm  
  > "The initial size of the dust particle is uniformly set to 0.1 $\mu$m." (Disk Model)
   - parameter: Internal grain density: 1.4 g cm^-3  
  > "The internal density of dust grains is fixed to be a typical value of $\rho_{\rm int}=1.4$ g cm$^{-3}$ for simplicity" (Disk Model)
   - parameter: Turbulence parameter: α_D = 10^-3  
  > "The turbulence parameter $\alpha_{\rm D}$ is set to be a typical value of $\alpha_{\rm D}=10^{-3}$ in this study." (Disk Model)
   - parameter: η v_K = 33 m s^-1  
  > "In our disk model, ${\eta}v_{\rm K} = 33~{\rm m~s^{-1}}$ is derived." (Disk Model)
   - assumption: Disk already formed before dust coagulation  
  > "However, this study assumes that the disk is already formed and then the dust coagulation occurs." (A picture of the Keplerian disk formation with dust evolution)
   - assumption: Single peak in particle mass at each radius; no fragmentation or bouncing  
  > "The mass distribution of dust particles is assumed to have a single peak in mass $m_{\rm p}(r)$ at each radial distance $r$." (Disk Model); "Note that we assume that the dust aggregates are so sticky that no fragmentation or bouncing occurs upon collision." (Disk Model)
   - assumption: Thermal-equilibrium temperature: T = 280 (r / 1 AU)^(-1/2) (L / L_sun)^(1/4) K  
  > "{T} = 280\left(\frac{r}{1\ {\rm AU}}\right)^{-1/2} \left(\frac{L}{L_\odot}\right)^{1/4}{\rm K}." (Disk Model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Vary initial surface densities, drift velocity and grain size by factors of ten; compare growth fronts and radial profiles.  
  > "the gas and dust surface densities are 10 times higher than the MMSN model." (Dependence on the Initial Conditions); "the initial grain size is 10 times larger than the previous MMSN model." (Dependence on the Initial Conditions); "we find that the growth front and radial profiles of dust, gas, and grain size are hardly changed with changing the initial conditions." (Dependence on the Initial Conditions)
2. **Generate synthetic continuum images**: Radiative transfer from the dust surface density, temperature and absorption/scattering opacity; opacity calculated using Mie theory.  
  > "The intensity is calculated by the radiative transfer equation using the given dust surface density, temperature, and dust absorption/scattering opacity." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations); "The dust opacity is calculated using Mie theory." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: 870 μm and 7 mm; assumed distance 100 pc  
  > "The observing wavelengths are set to $\lambda=870$ $\mu$m and 7 mm corresponding to ALMA Band 7 and VLA Q band observations, respectively." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations); "The distance is assumed to be 100 pc." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
   - assumption: Power-law grain-size distribution: q = -3.5  
  > "We calculate the dust evolution under the assumption of a power-law size distribution with an exponent of $q = -3.5$." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
   - assumption: Silicate (50%) and water ice (50%); Maxwell-Garnett effective medium theory  
  > "The dust composition was assumed to be a mixture of silicate (50%) and water ice (50%) (cite: pol94)." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations); "calculated the absorption and scattering opacity based on effective medium theory using the Maxwell-Garnett rule (cite: boh83; miy93)." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
   - assumption: Same scale height for all dust sizes; dust scale height an order of magnitude thinner than gas  
  > "The radiative transfer calculation is performed without changing the dust scale height for each dust size." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations); "Here, we assume that the dust scale height is an order of magnitude thinner than the gas scale height." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare synthetic images at both wavelengths and different times; rings appear at the growth front and move outward.  
  > "We find that the ring structure can be observed at the growth front with both wavelengths." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations); "The ring position moves outward over time." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
3. **Calculate model spectral indices**: α = (ln I_1 − ln I_2) / (ln ν_1 − ln ν_2) from the 870 μm and 7 mm intensity maps.  
  > "\alpha=\frac{\ln I_1 - \ln I_2}{\ln \nu_1 - \ln \nu_2}," (Spectral Index Distributions); "Here, we use the intensity maps of the 870 $\mu$m and 7 mm wavelengths." (Spectral Index Distributions)
   - assumption: α = β + 2 in the Rayleigh–Jeans limit  
  > "Note that the observed (sub)millimeter spectral index ($\alpha$) is related to $\beta$ by $\alpha=\beta+2$ in the Rayleigh–Jeans limit." (Spectral Index Distributions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare spectral indices with the grain sizes in the dust model and the grain-size dependence reported by (cite: ric10).  
  > "According to Figure 3 of (cite: ric10)" (Spectral Index Distributions); "Thus, these $\alpha$ values are consistent with the grain sizes of our dust model." (Spectral Index Distributions)
4. **Fit the growth-front location law**: Identify ring positions in synthetic images and fit R_c = A (M_star/M_sun)^(1/3) (ζ_d/0.01)^(2/3) (t_disk/1 yr)^(2/3) au.  
  > "R_{\rm c}=A\left(\frac{M_\star}{M_\odot}\right)^{1/3}\left(\frac{\zeta_{\rm d}}{0.01}\right)^{2/3}\left(\frac{t_{\rm disk}}{1\ {\rm yr}}\right)^{2/3}\ {\rm au}" (Growth Front Location); "The coefficient $A$ can be derived by fitting the various ring positions in time" (Growth Front Location)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Times: 6.4 × 10^3, 1.3 × 10^4, 2.6 × 10^4, 5.2 × 10^4 and 1.0 × 10^5 yr  
  > "we perform the radiative transfer calculations for the model at $t=6.4\times10^3$ yr, $1.3\times10^4$ yr, $2.6\times10^4$ yr, $5.2\times10^4$ yr, and $1.0\times10^5$ yr" (Growth Front Location)
   - parameter: λ = 870 μm; M_star = 1 M_sun; ζ_d = 0.01  
  > "by assuming $\lambda=870$ $\mu$m, $M_\star=1 M_{\rm \odot}$, and $\zeta_{\rm d}=0.01$." (Growth Front Location)
   - assumption: Growth timescale equals disk age at the growth front  
  > "if the timescale ($t_{\rm grow}$) is set to the disk age ($t_{\rm age}$), we can derive the critical radius ($R_{\rm c}$) where the growth front reaches" (Growth Front Location)
   - fitting: Fit ring positions to the growth-front law; ring-position errors represented by directly measured synthetic-image FWHM.  
  > "The error of the ring position indicates the full width half maximum (FWHM) derived by the direct measurements of the synthetic images." (Growth Front Location); "$A$ is derived to be $0.026$." (Growth Front Location)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare observed rings with predicted fronts**: Compare ring positions and R_p/R_c against stellar/disk age for 23 disks.  
  > "A total of 23 disks having ring structures are investigated." (Comparison with Observations); "we plot the ring location ($R_{\rm p}$) normalized by the growth front ($R_{\rm c}$) with respect to the stellar/disk age" (Comparison with Observations)
   - parameter: A = 0.026; factor-two stellar-age errors for all sources  
  > "with assuming $t_{\rm disk}=t_{\rm age}$ and $A=0.026$." (Comparison with Observations); "The errors of the stellar age are estimated by (cite: and18) in the DSHARP sample, and we use the average value of a factor two error in all source." (Comparison with Observations)
   - assumption: Disk age equals stellar age; growth-front error calculated from stellar-age error  
  > "with assuming $t_{\rm disk}=t_{\rm age}$ and $A=0.026$." (Comparison with Observations); "Note that the error of $R_{\rm c}$ is calculated from the error of the stellar age because the stellar age has the largest uncertainty." (Comparison with Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check whether normalized ring positions approach unity in young disks and diverge in older disks.  
  > "there are some rings having the ratio ($R_{\rm p}/R_{\rm c}$) of almost unity within the disk age of less than 1 Myr" (Comparison with Observations); "the ratio, $R_{\rm p}/R_{\rm c}$, decreases after 1 Myr, indicating that the growth front extends much larger than the observed ring positions." (Comparison with Observations)
6. **Compare L1527 morphology and spectral indices**: Compare ALMA Band 3 and VLA Q Band images and radial spectral-index profiles with edge-on synthetic continuum and spectral-index images.  
  > "We show the L1527 images of ALMA Band 3 and VLA Q Band continuum data" (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527); "we show the dust coagulation model images of the 7 mm continuum emission and spectral index maps" (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527); "The red squares indicate the spectral index along the north direction, while the blue squares indicate that along the south direction." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Edge-on model at t = 1.3 × 10^4 yr; smoothed with VLA beam 0.086″ × 0.067″  
  > "at $t=1.3\times10^4$ yr from edge-on view" (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527); "The model images are smoothed with the VLA beam size of $0.086\ensuremath{''}\times0.067\ensuremath{''}$." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
   - parameter: Spectral-index upper limits use 3σ VLA continuum emission  
  > "The upper limit of the spectral index is also derived by using 3$\sigma$ of the VLA continuum emission." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
   - assumption: Fiducial MMSN model with one solar mass protostar  
  > "The MMSN model with one solar mass protostar is applied for this simulation as the fiducial model." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Qualitative comparison only; observed spectral-index increase persists after the free–free extraction reported in (cite: nak20).  
  > "The comparison of models and observations on intensity and spectral index is only a qualitative discussion." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527); "This trend still remains even after extracting the free–free contamination (cite: nak20)." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
7. **Estimate growth-front observability**: Estimate the age interval between an unresolved small front and a front beyond a 100 au disk; compare with a PPD lifetime of a few Myr.  
  > "if a disk is younger than $10^3$ yr, the growth front cannot be observed with a spatial resolution of $\sim$ a few au such as ALMA observations." (Discussion and Summary); "if a disk is more evolved than $\sim3\times10^5$ yr, the growth front is beyond the disk size of 100 au and cannot be observed." (Discussion and Summary); "By taking into account the lifetime of PPDs of a few Myr (cite: hai01), the occurrence rate will be $\sim$ a few %." (Discussion and Summary)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Dust evolution proceeds inside out, producing a surface-density maximum at the growth front.  
  > "the dust evolution commences from inside out because the growth timescale is roughly proportional to the orbital period." (Dust Evolution); "As a result, the dust surface density is maximized in the growth front" (Dust Evolution)
- Synthetic rings appear at both wavelengths; the 7 mm ring lies a few au inward and is less enhanced than at 870 μm.  
  > "the ring structure is observed at a few au inner radius in the 7 mm image more than in the 870 $\mu$m image." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations); "We also find that the ring structure is more enhanced in the 870 $\mu$m image than the 7 mm image." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
- No correlation between stellar age and observed ring location.  
  > "we find no correlation between stellar age and ring location." (Comparison with Observations)
- Some young-disk rings agree with the growth front; older-disk rings require other mechanisms.  
  > "there are some rings having the ratio ($R_{\rm p}/R_{\rm c}$) of almost unity within the disk age of less than 1 Myr" (Comparison with Observations); "Thus, we suggest that the origin of the rings would be different from the growth front in the protoplanetary disks around Class II objects after 1 Myr." (Comparison with Observations)
- An edge-on ring can produce the observed double-clump morphology.  
  > "the 7 mm continuum image has double clumps at the growth front radius as similar to the observations because the ring structure has the longest line-of-sight at the edges." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: L1527 has a ring consistent with the predicted front and observations across a wide wavelength range.  
  > "the ring position ($R_{\rm p}=15$ au) is consistent with the growth front ($R_{\rm c}=22^{+15}_{-9}$ au) within the error" (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527); "observations over a wide range of wavelengths were performed toward this source." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
- Why this method: Spectral indices provide information on grain sizes; two synthetic wavelengths test wavelength dependence of the growth front.  
  > "Therefore, the spectral index, $\alpha$, provides us information on grain sizes." (Spectral Index Distributions); "We analyze the disk with two different observing wavelengths to investigate the dependence of the growth front on the observing wavelength." (Dust Ring Structure at the Growth Front by Radiative Transfer Calculations)
- Alternative: Pressure bumps from planets predict larger grains at ring positions, whereas a growth front predicts larger grains inside the ring.  
  > "If ring structures are formed by a pressure bump due to a presence of planets, dust grains become larger in the ring positions." (A Caveat on ring formation due to the growth front); "The grain size would be larger on the inside of the ring than on the outside of the ring and ring position." (A Caveat on ring formation due to the growth front)
- Alternative: High optical depth can explain L1527's low inner spectral index without establishing grain growth.  
  > "it is difficult to conclude that the dust grains have already grown inside the growth front because the low spectral index can also be explained by high optical depth." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
- Alternative: Free-free emission and dust scattering may affect the inner L1527 spectral index.  
  > "the VLA Q Band emission in the inner region will be affected by free-free emission from the protostar and may also be affected by the dust scattering." (A Case Study of the Growth Front: L1527)
