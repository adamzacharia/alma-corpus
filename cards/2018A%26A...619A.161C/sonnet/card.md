- Fields: protoplanetary_disks
- Question: What is the nature of the asymmetric mm-dust structure in the HD 135344B disk, and does it (a vortex) explain the scattered-light spirals without an outer planet?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate and image new Band 3/4 data**: ALMA pipeline calibration, Band 4 amplitude+phase self-calibration, tclean imaging with Briggs weighting  
  > "The visibilities were then Fourier inverted and deconvolved using the `TCLEAN` task in `CASA`, and imaged using Briggs weighting with a Robust parameter of 0.5." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "imaged using Briggs weighting with a Robust parameter of 0.5" (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit ring + crescent model to visibilities at four wavelengths**: Analytic model: radial Gaussian ring plus crescent with radial Gaussian and two-sided azimuthal Gaussian, Fourier transformed with GALARIO at observed uv-points and fit with emcee  
  > "by sampling it at the same uv-locations as the observations using the code `GALARIO` (cite: tazzari2018), and by then fitting them to the observed visibilities with the Markov-chain-Monte Carlo (MCMC) code `emcee`" (3.2 Model fit in the uv-plane)
   - software: GALARIO, emcee
   - parameter: 9 model parameters + center (x_c,y_c) + inclination; PA fixed at 62 deg  
  > "The final model $F(r,\theta)=F_1(r,\theta)+F_2(r,\theta)$ uses 9 parameters." (3.2 Model fit in the uv-plane); "the position angle $PA$, defined east-of-north, is fixed to $62^{\circ}$" (3.2 Model fit in the uv-plane)
   - parameter: 120 walkers, 24000 likelihoods  
  > "In the MCMC, 120 walkers were used and the parameters were extracted using 24000 likelihoods." (3.2 Model fit in the uv-plane)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; each band fitted independently; priors not stated  
  > "The Band 3 and Band 4 data, as well as the archival Band 7 and Band 9 data sets, are fitted independently with the model described above." (3.2 Model fit in the uv-plane)
   - validation: Model images and residuals made with same imaging parameters; residuals at most 25% at peak in Band 4; visibilities compared in appendix  
  > "residuals of at most 25% at the peak are found only in the Band 4 data set." (3.2 Model fit in the uv-plane)
3. **Estimate crescent and total dust mass and optical depth**: Optically thin M = F d^2/(kappa B(T)); brightness temperature gives tau; surface density from tau/kappa  
  > "Under the assumption of optically thin emission it is possible to convert the observed flux into dust mass." (4.1 Dust mass)
   - parameter: d = 136 pc, T_dust = 15 K, kappa = 10 cm2/g at 1000 GHz, beta = 1  
  > "where the distance $d=136\,\rm pc$, the dust temperature $T_{\rm dust}$ at the location of the asymmetry (i.e. $\sim80$ au) is assumed to be 15 K" (4.1 Dust mass)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission; T_dust = 15 K; kappa with beta=1  
  > "the dust grain opacity $\kappa_\nu$ is taken as $10\,\rm cm^2 g^{-1}$ at $1000\,\rm GHz$" (4.1 Dust mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested with T_dust higher by factor 1.5 (tau ~0.4)  
  > "The crescent remains only marginally optically thin even assuming $T_{\rm dust}$ higher by a factor of 1.5, with $\tau_\nu\sim 0.4$." (4.1 Dust mass)
4. **Map spectral index between Band 4 and Band 7**: Taper Band 4 to Band 7 beam, recenter on best-fit center, alpha = ln(F155/F335)/ln(155/335) for pixels with S/N>20 in both  
  > "The value of $\alpha$ is calculated using only those pixels with a signal-to-noise greater than 20 in both bands." (4.2 Spectral index distribution)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: S/N>20 in both bands  
  > "using only those pixels with a signal-to-noise greater than 20 in both bands" (4.2 Spectral index distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainty ±0.15 on absolute alpha from calibration; variation across the map not affected  
  > "The measured variation across the map is not affected by the absolute calibration uncertainty." (4.2 Spectral index distribution)
5. **Measure azimuthal peak shift and width versus wavelength; infer gas surface density and gas-to-dust ratio**: Compare fitted crescent theta_V and azimuthal widths across bands; invert Stokes number relation assuming St~1 to get Sigma_gas  
  > "we can therefore invert Eq. [\[eq:st\]](#eq:st) and obtain an estimate of the gas surface density at the vortex location of $\Sigma_{\rm gas}\approx1\,\rm g\,cm^{-2}$" (4.3 Peak shift)
   - parameter: grain size ~3 x lambda  
  > "grain size $\sim3\times\lambda$ (cite: 2006ApJ...636.1114D)" (4.3 Peak shift)
   - assumption: rho_pc = 1 g/cm3, St near unity  
  > "Assuming a typical value of $\rho_{\rm pc}=1\rm \, g\, cm^{-3}$" (4.3 Peak shift)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with Sigma_gas from CO isotopologue modelling  
  > "This gas surface density is consistent with that obtained by (cite: vdm2016_isot) by modelling the emission from $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O." (4.3 Peak shift)
6. **Estimate vortex total mass and compare with planet scenarios**: Dust mass ~50 Mearth times gas-to-dust ratio 1 or 10; gap-planet mass from gap width relation  
  > "Assuming a value of 10 (cite: 2017ApJ...835..118M; 2017ApJ...850..115S), the total mass for the vortex is $M_{\rm vortex}\approx1.7\,M_{\rm Jupiter}$." (5.2 Spirals launched by the vortex)
   - parameter: gap width ~20 au at ~68 au, alpha = 1e-3  
  > "a standard viscosity parameter $\alpha = 10^{-3}$" (5.1 Spirals launched by a planet)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Disk resolved into a symmetric ring plus asymmetric crescent; spectral index decreases in the crescent; azimuthal width decreases with wavelength  
  > "We resolve the asymmetric disk into a symmetric ring + asymmetric crescent" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long wavelengths at high resolution are expected to be optically thin, probing bulk dust mass  
  > "The dust emission is expected to be optically thin at these long wavelengths, and the bulk of the dust mass inside the observed structures can be determined." (Introduction)
- Why this method: uv-plane fitting is unaffected by the cleaning process  
  > "the analysis carried out in this work was performed in the uv-plane only, and is therefore not affected by the cleaning process" (2 Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Keplerian rotation ruled out as cause of peak shift (opposite direction)  
  > "Keplerian rotation can immediately be ruled-out as the cause of such a shift" (4.3 Peak shift)
- Alternative: External planet launching spirals considered unlikely given SPHERE limits  
  > "making this scenario unlikely" (5.1 Spirals launched by a planet)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Emission only marginally optically thin so masses are lower limits; large uncertainties in opacity and gas surface density  
  > "These numbers should be regarded as lower limits." (4.1 Dust mass)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral index decrease may be due to optical thickness rather than grain growth  
  > "but can also be due to high optical thickness, or to a combination of both effects" (4.2 Spectral index distribution)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed T=15 K and kappa dominate mass uncertainty
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas-to-dust ratio from rough St~1 argument is weakly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Bridge feature may be beam artifact
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gaussian crescent model is simplistic and posteriors may be underestimated
