## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and treat GMOS optical IFU data cube and subtract stellar/AGN continuum**: Reduce raw GMOS data in IRAF, correct for differential atmospheric refraction, combine via median, apply Butterworth spatial filtering, remove instrumental fingerprint, perform Richardson-Lucy deconvolution, and subtract continuum using STARLIGHT spectral synthesis.  
  > "The reduction of the data was performed using the Gemini package scripts in IRAF." (GMOS data); "Finally, a Richardson–Lucy deconvolution (cite: rich; lucy) was performed to improve the spatial resolution." (GMOS data); "To remove the continuum emission, we applied a spectral synthesis, with the STARLIGHT software (cite: cid05) to the spectrum corresponding to each spaxel of the data cube." (Creation of the gas data cube)
   - software: IRAF, Gemini IRAF package, IDL, STARLIGHT
   - parameter: spaxel size = 0.05 arcsec; Butterworth filter order n = 2, cut-off = 0.2 Ny; deconvolved PSF FWHM ~ 0.42 arcsec  
  > "The data cubes were reduced with spatial pixels (spaxels) of 0.05 arcsec." (GMOS data); "a Butterworth spatial filtering procedure (cite: gwoods), which was applied using a filter given by the product of two circular Butterworth filters, with order $n$ = 2 and with a cut-off frequency of 0.2 Ny." (GMOS data); "The resulting broad H$\alpha$ image provided a FWHM of the PSF of $\sim$ 0.42 arcsec." (GMOS data)
   - assumption: Galactic extinction AV = 0.058 with Cardelli et al. (1989) law; redshift z = 0.00476; MILES stellar library plus AGN power-law continuum with spectral index 1.5  
  > "Before this procedure, all the spectra of the data cube were corrected for Galactic extinction, using $A_V$ = 0.058 (taken from NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database -NED[^5]) and the extinction law of (cite: car89)." (Creation of the gas data cube); "We also corrected the spectra for redshift, using $z = 0.00476$ (cite: kor04)." (Creation of the gas data cube); "We also added a power-law with a spectral index of 1.5 (typical for AGNs) to this base to reproduce the AGN featureless continuum." (Creation of the gas data cube)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: PSF FWHM measured from spatially unresolved broad Halpha image before (0.61 arcsec) and after (0.42 arcsec) Richardson-Lucy deconvolution  
  > "The resulting broad H$\alpha$ image provided a FWHM of the PSF of $\sim$ 0.42 arcsec. The same procedure applied to the data cube before the Richardson–Lucy deconvolution resulted in a FWHM of the PSF of $\sim$ 0.61 arcsec." (GMOS data)
2. **Align multiwavelength datasets (GMOS, HST, ALMA, SPLUS) and map emitting regions and kinematics**: Remove cosmic rays from HST images using L.A.Cosmic, match HST filters on nuclear peak, align GMOS broad Halpha peak with HST F814W peak and ALMA CO(3-2) kinematic centre, and construct emission-line maps, channel maps, and Gaussian velocity/dispersion maps.  
  > "We applied a cosmic ray removal procedure to all *HST* images using the L.A.Cosmic routine (cite: van01)." (HST images); "We assumed that the molecular gas kinematic centre, obtained from the velocity map of the CO(3-2) emission line, corresponds to the AGN position (see Section [\[sec6\]](#sec6))" (ALMA data); "We fitted the [O III]$\lambda$5007 emission line in each spectrum of the data cube with a Gaussian function." (Gas kinematics)
   - software: L.A.Cosmic
   - parameter: HST FOV = 11.9 x 10.6 arcsec^2  
  > "The FOV of the images has 11.9 $	imes$ 10.6 arcsec$^2$." (HST images)
   - assumption: Emission peak in HST F814W, GMOS broad Halpha peak, and ALMA CO(3-2) kinematic centre all correspond to the AGN position  
  > "The matching between the *HST* data and the GMOS data, on the other hand, was made assuming that the emission peak in the I band (F814W) image, which is not significantly affected by dust extinction, corresponds to the AGN position" (HST images); "We assumed that the molecular gas kinematic centre, obtained from the velocity map of the CO(3-2) emission line, corresponds to the AGN position (see Section [\[sec6\]](#sec6))" (ALMA data)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian fit per spaxel for [O III]5007 velocity and dispersion maps  
  > "We fitted the [O III]$\lambda$5007 emission line in each spectrum of the data cube with a Gaussian function." (Gas kinematics)
   - validation: Verified superposition of stars across HST filters  
  > "At the end, we verified that the stars detected, in different filters, along the FOV were adequately superposed, indicating that our approach for the matching is reliable." (HST images)
3. **Decompose optical emission lines and measure fluxes and line ratios in Regions 1, 2, and 3**: Extract spectra from Regions 1, 2, and 3; decompose narrow lines into blueshifted and redshifted Gaussians using the [S II] doublet as an empirical width/redshift template, plus broad Gaussians for BLR emission; correct for interstellar extinction via Balmer decrement and repeat decomposition.  
  > "Each narrow emission line was fitted by a sum of two Gaussian functions a blueshifted and a redshifted component. The width and redshift of each component were estimated from the fitting of the [S II] doublet." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis); "We applied a correction of the interstellar extinction, using the H$\alpha$/H$eta$ ratio (Balmer decrement), based on the narrow components of these lines, and the extinction law of (cite: car89)." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis)
   - parameter: 100 Monte Carlo iterations for uncertainty estimation  
  > "The uncertainties were determined with a Monte Carlo procedure, with 100 iterations, taking into account the high-frequency spectral noise, the uncertainties of the Gaussian fits (in the case of emission lines that were decomposed), and also the residuals from the spectral continuum subtraction." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis)
   - assumption: [S II] and [N II] lines are emitted by the same partially ionized region with similar widths and radial velocities; Cardelli et al. (1989) extinction law  
  > "Since most of the [S II] and [N II] lines are probably emitted by the same partially ionized region, their widths and radial velocities should be similar." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm; reduced chi^2 comparison to select number of broad components  
  > "All Gaussian fittings were performed using the Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis); "For the spectrum of Region 1, we calculated the reduced $\chi^2$ ($\chi^2_{red}$) of the H$\alpha$ Gaussian decomposition with one and two broad components, the values being 27.6 and 11.02 respectively" (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis)
   - validation: Reduced chi^2 and >3sigma amplitude threshold for broad components in Regions 2 and 3  
  > "In Region 2, the value with the broad component was ${\chi^2}_{red}$ = 3.67 and the value without it was ${\chi^2}_{red}$ = 4.45, the broad component having an amplitude higher than 3$\sigma$ (A=0.32 and $\sigma$=0.09)." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis)
4. **Model photoionization and shock heating of narrow and coronal line regions**: Generate grids of photoionization models with CLOUDY v13.03 to reproduce observed emission-line ratios in Regions 1, 2, and 3, and compare asymmetric components in Regions 2 and 3 with pure shock-heating models from the Mappings III library.  
  > "we generated several grids of simple models with version 13.03 of the CLOUDY software, last described by (cite: fer13), aimed at reproducing the observed emission-line ratios of regions 1, 2 and 3." (Photoionization modelling); "In order to evaluate if such a scenario is plausible, we retrieved the values of the emission-line ratios resulting from pure shock-heating models from the Mappings III library (cite: all08)." (Shock heating)
   - software: CLOUDY
   - parameter: AGN continuum spectral index = 1.5; AGN bolometric luminosity = 2.5 x 10^43 erg s^-1; electron densities from [S II]6716/6731 at T = 10000 K; free parameters: filling factor, chemical abundances, low-energy cut-off, and (for Region 1) distance and CLR density  
  > "We assumed an AGN continuum in the form of a power-law, with a spectral index of 1.5, which is the usual value normally taken into account in the literature to reproduce an AGN featureless continuum." (Photoionization modelling); "The value of the AGN bolometric luminosity was taken as a fixed parameter and equal to $2.5 \times 10^{43}$ erg s$^{-1}$" (4.2 Photoionization modelling)
   - assumption: T = 10,000 K for [S II] density calculation; bolometric correction of 600 for [O III]5007; distance from AGN equals projected distance for Regions 2 (2.8 x 10^20 cm) and 3 (4.0 x 10^20 cm)  
  > "as determined from the corresponding [S II]$\lambda$6716/[S II]$\lambda$6731 ratios, assuming a temperature of 10 000 K (cite: ost06)." (Photoionization modelling); "We assumed that the distance between the AGN and the emitting cloud in Region 3 is equal to the observed projected distance between such regions ($4.0~	imes~10^{20}$ cm)." (Photoionization modelling)
   - fitting: Grid search matching observed emission-line ratios within 1sigma to 3sigma  
  > "In the case of the lower ionization emission lines, the simulated values were all compatible with the observed ones, at the 1$\sigma$ or 2$\sigma$ levels. In the case of the coronal emission lines, all the simulated emission-line ratios are compatible with the observed values up to the 3$\sigma$ level." (Photoionization modelling)
   - validation: Comparison of simulated vs observed line ratios at 1sigma to 3sigma levels  
  > "the predicted values of the ratios obtained with the blueshifted and redshifted components were all compatible with the observed values, at the 1$\sigma$ or 2$\sigma$ levels." (Photoionization modelling)
5. **Model the broad Halpha line profile in Region 1 to derive BLR geometry**: Fit a toy model of two circular rotating ring-shaped regions around the SMBH to the broad and very broad Halpha components.  
  > "In the model applied here, we assumed that each one of the two broad components is emitted by a circular rotating ring-shaped region, with a specific inclination relative to the line of sight and located at a specific distance from the central SMBH." (The broad Halpha and Hbeta components)
   - parameter: Free parameters: radius (r), inclination (i), and Gaussian width (sigma_Gauss) of each rotating ring  
  > "The radius ($r$), inclination ($i$) and the width of the Gaussian H$\alpha$ emission lines ($\sigma_{Gauss}$) emitted from each portion of the rotating rings were considered free parameters of the model." (The broad Halpha and Hbeta components)
   - assumption: SMBH mass = 1.74 +/- 0.26 x 10^6 M_sun; circular Keplerian rotating rings  
  > "The mass of the central SMBH was taken as $1.74 \pm 0.26~	imes~10^6$ M$_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ (cite: onori17)." (The broad Halpha and Hbeta components)
   - fitting: Minimum chi^2 estimation; uncertainties from standard deviation of Gaussian fit to parameter histograms for models with chi^2 < 1  
  > "The result of the modelling was determined based on minimum $\chi^2$ estimation." (The broad Halpha and Hbeta components); "we constructed a histogram for each parameter, taking into account only the cases with $\chi^2$ < 1 in the modelling. Then we fitted a Gaussian function to the histogram and the uncertainty of the parameter was taken as the standard deviation of the Gaussian." (The broad Halpha and Hbeta components)
   - validation: Compatibility of inclinations of the two rings at the 1sigma level  
  > "their values of $i$ are compatible, at the 1$\sigma$ level." (The broad Halpha and Hbeta components)
6. **Reduce X-ray data and perform spectral variability and broadband torus reflection modelling**: Process XMM-Newton data with SAS and NuSTAR data with NUSTARDAS/HEASOFT; perform individual and simultaneous multi-epoch XMM-Newton spectral fits and combined XMM-Newton+NuSTAR broadband spectral fits using pexrav and borus02 torus reflection models in XSPEC.  
  > "The observation data files (ODFs) from the European Photon Imaging Camera (EPIC) on the detector were processed using the Science Analysis System (SAS–version 17.0.0)." (XMM-Newton data); "The data were processed using NUSTARDAS v1.6.0, available in the *NuSTAR* Data Analysis Software." (NuSTAR data); "The spectral analysis of the *NuSTAR* and *XMM–Newton* data was performed using XSPEC version 12.12.0 (cite: arnaud96)." (X-ray Analysis)
   - software: SAS, EPATPLOT, NUSTARDAS, NUPIPELINE, NUPRODUCTS, CALDB, HEASOFT, FTOOLS, grppha, NH, XSPEC, BORUS
   - parameter: XMM source radius = 35 arcsec, background radius = 70 arcsec, min 25 counts/bin; NuSTAR source radius = 50 arcsec, min 20 counts/bin; Fe Kalpha E = 6.4 keV, sigma = 0.01 keV; pexrav E_cut = 100 keV; borus02 half-opening angle fixed to 25 deg  
  > "The source events were extracted using a circular region, with a radius of 35 arcsec, centred on the target, and the background events were extracted from a circular region, with a radius of 70 arcsec, on the same chip far from the source." (XMM-Newton data); "For both focal plane modules (FPMA and FPMB), we used a circular extraction region, with a radius of 50 arcsec, centred on the position of the source." (NuSTAR data); "Therefore, we fixed this parameter to the value obtained for the BLR (25 deg, see Sect. [\[sec43\]](#sec43))." (Broadband X-ray spectral analysis)
   - assumption: Galactic NH fixed to FTOOLS NH prediction; scattered power-law normalisation <= 1% of nuclear component; torus half-opening angle = 25 deg from BLR modelling  
  > "The Galactic absorption was fixed to the predicted value using the tool N$m H$ within FTOOLS (cite: 1990Dickey)." (Spectral variability); "In this work, we restrict the values of this parameter to a maximum value of 1 per cent of the normalisation of the nuclear component." (Spectral variability)
   - fitting: Chi-square minimisation in XSPEC with F-test model selection for untying variability parameters and adding broadband components  
  > "Allowing any additional parameter to vary freely did not improve significantly the quality of the fit (i.e. none gave an improvement with F-test values lower than 10$^{-5}$.)" (Spectral variability); "We checked that the addition of either component made a significant improvement compared to the simpler model, according to the F-test, and selected the model with the lowest $\chi^2$ (model 3)." (Broadband X-ray spectral analysis)
   - validation: Verified negligible photon pile-up with EPATPLOT; checked F-test significance and absence of structure in residuals  
  > "We verified that the photon pile-up is negligible in the filtered event list with the task EPATPLOT." (XMM-Newton data); "The best-fitting model is shown in Figure [3](#fig:xray_spectra) and it is statistically acceptable with $\chi^{2}$= 1616.37 for 1323 d.o.f. and no clear structure in the residuals." (Broadband X-ray spectral analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- The ALMA CO(3-2) molecular gas emission extends along the North-South direction, consistent with a rotating disc nearly perpendicular to the axis of the western [O III]5007 ionization cone, and shows blueshifted outflows and a kinematic connection to a south-eastern inner spiral feeding structure.  
  > "When we look at the CO(3-2) image in Fig. [5](#oiii_co)(b), obtained with ALMA, we notice that the molecular emission extends along the North-South direction and, therefore, it is approximately perpendicular to the main axis of the ionization cone" (Emitting regions); "The CO(3-2) channel maps (Fig. [\[channelmapCO\]](#channelmapCO)) show a possible rotation of molecular gas (also seen in the RGB composite image in Fig. [\[rgbCO\]](#rgbCO)), perpendicular to the ionization cone axis, and evidence of blueshifted outflows" (Gas kinematics); "This structure comes towards the nucleus with redshifted velocities at south and low velocities closer to the nucleus, as seen in the CO(3-2) RGB. That suggests a feeding process of the nucleus." (Conclusions)
- NGC 7314 is classified as a Seyfert 1 AGN based on broad Halpha and Hbeta components, and its historical classification changes may indicate it is a changing-look AGN.  
  > "The optical nuclear spectrum of NGC 7314 presents a Seyfert-like emission with broad components of H$\alpha$ and H$eta$. This characterises the source as a type 1 AGN." (Conclusions); "The fact that our classification for the nuclear emission-line spectrum of NGC 7314 (as a type 1 Seyfert) does not agree with those of some previous studies suggests that NGC 7314 may be a changing-look AGN." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiwavelength data (GMOS optical IFU, HST high-resolution imaging, ALMA CO(3-2) molecular gas, XMM-Newton/NuSTAR X-rays, SPLUS) were combined to study the complex nuclear and circumnuclear emission, ionization mechanisms, torus properties, variability, and gas kinematics.  
  > "we performed a multiwavelength analysis of the nuclear region of the nearby Seyfert galaxy NGC 7314, using an optical data cube obtained with the Integral Field Unit from the Gemini Multi-Object Spectrograph" (Abstract); "The ALMA data were used in this work to study the kinematics and morphology of the molecular gas in the central region of NGC 7314." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: The [S II] doublet was used as an empirical template to reduce free parameters and degeneracies when fitting blended [N II]+Halpha; [O III]5007 was chosen for kinematic maps because it is unblended and high A/N; simultaneous X-ray fitting with linked non-variable parameters was used to avoid degeneracies.  
  > "Therefore, the use of such an empirical template reduces the free parameters and, as a consequence, the degeneracies in the fit of the [N II]+H$\alpha$ spectral region." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis); "we chose the [O III]$\lambda$5007 emission line to do the channel maps (Fig. [4](#channelmapOIII)), as this emission line is not blended with others and has a high A/N." (Gas kinematics); "To avoid degeneracies, produced by small fluctuations in the less variable parameters, we performed a simultaneous fitting to all the epochs, allowing only the most variable parameters to vary freely (cite: her13)." (Spectral variability)
- Alternative: Fitting Halpha in Region 1 with a single broad component was rejected in favour of two broad components based on reduced chi^2 (27.6 vs 11.02); fitting Hbeta with two broad components was rejected because it did not converge.  
  > "For the spectrum of Region 1, we calculated the reduced $\chi^2$ ($\chi^2_{red}$) of the H$\alpha$ Gaussian decomposition with one and two broad components, the values being 27.6 and 11.02 respectively" (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis); "For the H$eta$ decomposition, the fitting did not converge using two broad components, therefore, we used only one." (Line decomposition and diagnostic diagram analysis)
- Alternative: Using [N II]+Halpha emission lines for gas kinematic maps was rejected due to degeneracy in multi-Gaussian fits including the broad Halpha component.  
  > "We also tried to use the [N II]+H$\alpha$ emission lines for the analysis of the gas kinematics. However, the results were not sufficiently reliable to be included in this work due to the degeneracy resulting from the fits of these lines with sums of Gaussian functions" (Gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Pure shock-heating models from Mappings III were rejected as the sole mechanism for Regions 2 and 3 because they could not reproduce the observed [O III]5007/Hbeta ratios.  
  > "On the other hand, the observed values of the [O III]$\lambda$5007/H$eta$ ratio are not consistent with those provided by the models. Considering this, we conclude that pure shock-heating models cannot reproduce all the observed properties" (Shock heating)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Using a low-energy cut-off in CLOUDY to approximate interstellar extinction does not account for extinction effects at energies higher than the cut-off.  
  > "One should note, however, that this simple approach has the disadvantage of not considering the effects of the interstellar extinction in spectral regions corresponding to energies higher than the cut-off." (Photoionization modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot confirm the detection of the inner spiral arm structure, the extended faint broad Halpha component in Regions 2 and 3, or the exact scattering agent (free electrons, dust, or molecules).  
  > "Although we cannot confirm the detection of this possible inner spiral arm in this galaxy, these results suggest that, if the structure is real, it may be associated with the feeding of the AGN." (The circumnuclear emission); "Although we cannot confirm the presence of this broad H$\alpha$ line, due to its very low amplitude (but still higher than 3$\sigma$), this topic deserves some discussion." (The circumnuclear emission); "In the specific case of NGC 7314, we cannot confirm whether the possible scattering of the BLR emission is being caused by free electrons, dust, or molecules." (The circumnuclear emission)
- Limitation (authors): Detailed combined photoionization+shock modelling is beyond the scope of the paper.  
  > "It is plausible, however, that these properties may be reproduced by models involving the combined effect of photoionization by a central source and shock heating from outflows, although such a detailed modelling is beyond the scope of this paper." (Shock heating)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Astrometric registration between ALMA, GMOS, and HST relies on assuming that the CO(3-2) kinematic centre, broad Halpha peak, and HST F814W peak are strictly coincident, without independent astrometric tie-points.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Noticeable inconsistency in Table 1 vs Conclusions for the projected distance of Region 2 from Region 1 (1.16 +/- 0.05 arcsec in Table 1 vs 1.03 +/- 0.05 arcsec in Conclusions).
