## Analysis recipe

1. **identify clouds in nearby galaxies**: astrodendro dendrogram on 2D Moment 0 maps; leaves are clouds; smallest structure must exceed one beam area  
  > "we only require that the smallest identified structure exceeds one beam area" (3.1.1 Nearby galaxies); "The number of identified molecular clouds (leaves) in each galaxy is shown in" (3.1.1 Nearby galaxies)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: min_npix = one beam area (21, 36, 20 pixels for NGC 5236, 628, 2835); no other constraints  
  > "No additional constraints are imposed within the algorithm to reduce the dependence of the identification results on parameter choices." (3.1.1 Nearby galaxies)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **identify clouds in the inner Milky Way lv maps**: dendrogram on the l-v map with several min_npix values, then duplicates removed by area-difference criterion; clouds then extracted from the lbv cube  
  > "500, 250, 100, 50, 25, 10, and 5 pixels, in order to cover continuous structures across different scales" (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way); "Because our goal is to eliminate duplicate identifications rather than nested structures, we adopt a value of 0.3." (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: min_npix = 500, 250, 100, 50, 25, 10, 5 pixels; duplicate threshold 0.3; 3 rms mask on the lbv cube  
  > "Before exporting the $lv$ map, we already applied a 3\*rms mask to the $lbv$ cube." (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **assign distances to Milky Way clouds**: match ATLASGAL clumps by position and velocity and adopt median clump distance; otherwise cross-match with SEDIGISM clouds (velocity difference < 10 km/s, separation smaller than cloud size)  
  > "we first match the ATLASGAL clumps (cite: Urquhart2022-510) to the identified clouds based on their spatial and velocity ranges, and then assign the median distance of all associated clumps as the distance of the molecular cloud." (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way); "We also require that the velocity difference of the pair is less than 10 km/s" (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: typical velocity difference between matched pairs ~1.2 km/s (clumps) and ~1.0 km/s (SEDIGISM)  
  > "A typical velocity difference between the median velocity of all associated clumps and the cloud velocity is $\sim$1.2 km/s." (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way)
4. **derive cloud physical parameters**: second-moment velocity dispersion (Gaussian fit to set velocity range in galaxies), column density from I_CO times X_CO, mass, radii r_min and r_max, virial parameter alpha_vir = 5 sigma^2 r_s/(G M)  
  > "the intensity-weighted velocity dispersion is calculated by the second moment method" (3.2 Physical parameters); "we perform Gaussian fitting on the CO line profile to determine the velocity range of the emission." (3.2 Physical parameters)
   - parameter: X_CO = 2e20 cm^-2 (K km/s)^-1  
  > "The $X_{\rm CO}$ is taken to be $2 \times 10^{20}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}~(\mathrm{K~km~s}^{-1})^{-1}$ (cite: Bolatto2013-51)." (3.2 Physical parameters)
   - assumption: mu_H2 = 2.8  
  > "where $\mu_{\rm H_{2}} = 2.8$ is the molecular weight per hydrogen molecule" (3.2 Physical parameters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **smooth Milky Way clouds to extragalactic resolution**: smooth each cloud to a common 53.5 pc resolution; adopt r_s by four cases based on r_max and filling factor f  
  > "we adjust the smoothing factor such that all clouds are brought to a common spatial resolution of 53.5 pc." (3.3 Radius after smoothing); "Here, we define the filling factor ($f$) as the ratio of pixels with emission to the total number of pixels in the image." (3.3 Radius after smoothing)
   - parameter: r_s = 26.75 pc if r_max < 26.75 pc; r_s = r_max for 26.75 < r_max < 53.5 pc, or r_max > 53.5 pc with f > 0.5; r_s = (r_max + 26.75 pc)/2 for r_max > 53.5 pc and f < 0.5  
  > "so its size is effectively the beam size after smoothing, i.e. $r_{\rm s}$ = 26.75 pc" (3.3 Radius after smoothing)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: only smoothed maps with one main emission structure are considered  
  > "for the smoothed map, we only consider the map with one main emission structure" (3.3 Radius after smoothing)
6. **estimate cloud star formation rates**: 21 um (JWST) or 24 um (Spitzer) intensity converted to SFR surface density; Milky Way 24 um flux scaled by CO fraction in the cloud velocity range to correct projection overlap  
  > "We then multiply the total 24 $\mu$m flux within the region by this factor to determine the 24 $\mu$m flux associated with the cloud." (3.4 Star formation rates)
   - parameter: Sigma_SFR = 3.8e-3 x I_21um (MJy/sr), in Msun/yr/kpc^2  
  > "3.8\times10^{-3} \left(\frac{I_{\rm 21 \mu m}}{\rm MJy~sr^{-1}}\right)" (3.4 Star formation rates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: the CO scaling method is said to be effective (Fig. 5)  
  > "As shown in Fig.[5](#sfr-m), this method proves to be effective." (3.4 Star formation rates)
7. **estimate true radii of unsmoothed Milky Way clouds and filter beam-affected clouds**: r_t from f and connectivity ratio c rules; compare column density with r_t versus r_s; define N0 and r_s = 26.75 pc boundaries to exclude beam-affected extragalactic clouds  
  > "we now recalculate their physical parameters using $r_{\rm t}$ instead of $r_{\rm s}$." (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds); "Here, we only consider the clouds of nearby galaxies located between the dashed and solid black lines in Fig.[7](#origin)(c)." (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds)
   - parameter: N0 ~ 10^20.9 cm^-2; 22 clouds (~2%) with c < 0.5 excluded  
  > "molecular clouds with column densities below the dashed black line ($N_{\rm 0} \sim 10^{20.9}$ cm$^{-2}$) are also significantly affected by the beam effect" (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds); "Note that 22 clouds ($\sim$2% of the total)" (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: after filtering, the extragalactic mass-radius relation becomes comparable to the Milky Way  
  > "after removing the molecular clouds affected by the beam effect, their mass-radius relation becomes comparable to that of molecular clouds in the inner Milky Way." (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds)
8. **relate clouds to internal ATLASGAL clumps**: associate clumps within r_t (lower limit) or 0.5 kpc (upper limit) of cloud distance; alternative association by 13CO (1-0) spectrum peak velocity; split clouds into with/without clumps and compare properties including mean 8 um brightness; fit mass relations  
  > "clumps within a deviation of $r_{\rm t}$ (lower limit) or 0.5 kpc (upper limit) from it are considered to be physically associated with the molecular cloud." (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps); "If the velocity corresponding to the peak of the spectral line falls within the velocity range of a molecular cloud, the clump is considered to be associated with that cloud." (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps)
   - fitting: relation form log10 M_clump,tot = 0.75 log10 M_cloud - 0.56; fitting method not stated  
  > "\mathrm{log}_{\rm 10} (M_{\mathrm{clump,tot}}) = 0.75 \times \mathrm{log}_{\rm 10} (M_{\mathrm{cloud}}) - 0.56" (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps)
   - validation: correlations checked against a 2-4 kpc distance-restricted sample and the lower-limit association case  
  > "If we restrict the cloud sample to those within 2–4 kpc to avoid possible distance bias" (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps)

## Qualitative findings

- Smoothed Milky Way clouds and extragalactic clouds in the same mass range have comparable physical properties and SFRs at matched resolution  
  > "As long as the observational resolution across these galaxies is consistent, the physical properties of their molecular clouds are overall comparable." (4.1 Comparability of physical properties)
- The strong column density-mass correlation after smoothing is an artifact of the beam effect  
  > "The strong correlation between column density and mass shown in Fig.[4](#para) is merely an artifact of image smoothing (the beam effect)." (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds)
- A small fraction of NGC 5236 clouds have higher velocity dispersions, possibly environmental  
  > "a small fraction of molecular clouds in NGC 5236 exhibit higher velocity dispersions" (4.2 Original and smoothed molecular clouds)
- Clump-associated Milky Way clouds are more massive, denser, with larger velocity dispersion, smaller virial parameter and stronger 8 um emission  
  > "molecular clouds in this group exhibit greater mass, higher density, larger velocity dispersion, and smaller virial parameters." (4.4 Molecular clouds in the inner Milky Way and NGC 628)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: PHANGS-ALMA galaxies chosen with ~50 pc linear resolution  
  > "we selected three nearby galaxies (NGC 5236, NGC 628 and NGC 2835) with a linear resolution $\sim$50 pc from the PHANGS-ALMA survey" (2.1 CO (2-1) cubes and mid-infrared images of nearby galaxies)
- Why this method: Milky Way clouds smoothed to the extragalactic resolution to enable like-for-like comparison  
  > "We emphasize the importance of matching angular resolution—i.e., smoothing the data to a common resolution—when comparing cloud properties across different observational datasets." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Alternative clump-cloud association via the 13CO (1-0) spectrum at each clump position; this strengthens the clump-cloud correlation  
  > "Comparing Fig.[13](#mtot2) and Fig.[9](#mtot), this method strengthens the correlation between clumps and molecular clouds." (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): A significant fraction of ThrUMMS and FUGIN clouds lack distances because SEDIGISM covers only |b| < 0.5 deg; only clouds with distances are analysed  
  > "In the following analysis, we focus only on molecular clouds with distance estimates." (3.1.2 The inner Milky Way)
- Limitation (authors): Physical mechanisms behind the clump-cloud correlations are not established  
  > "The specific physical mechanisms and how they give rise to the correlations shown in Fig.[9](#mtot) require further investigation." (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps)
- Limitation (authors): CMFs depend on consistent mass measurement of clouds and clumps; this sample identifies complexes and uses lower clump masses than earlier works  
  > "Overall, to compare CMFs from various works, the masses of molecular clouds and clumps must be measured consistently." (4.3 Molecular clouds and their internal clumps)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparability is partly built in by the smoothing recipe and the empirical r_s rules; the four-case radius assignment is ad hoc and untested against actual smoothed cubes
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Different CO lines (2-1 vs 1-0, 12CO vs 13CO) and a single constant X_CO are used across samples with no line-ratio or metallicity treatment
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cloud definitions differ between dendrogram on 2D maps (galaxies) and on l-v maps (Milky Way), so cloud complexes are not equivalent populations
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Galaxy sample is only three galaxies, one of which (NGC 2835) has 130 clouds
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties are given on the fitted mass relations
