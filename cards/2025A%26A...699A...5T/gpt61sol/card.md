## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the calibrated ALMA data**: Two-stage continuum self-calibration followed by CASA tclean imaging; use cubes without JvM correction.  
  > "We thus conducted standard continuum self-calibration in two stages, masking out the CRLE and HZ10 in the process." (ALMA); "Imaging was performed using the tclean task in CASA following the procedures described in Herrera-Camus et al. (in prep.)." (ALMA); "Therefore, all the figures throughout the paper are based on the ALMA cubes without JvM correction." (ALMA)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Natural weighting; beam 0.34'' × 0.27'', PA 54.9 degrees.  
  > "synthesized beam size of $0.34\ensuremath{''} \times0.27\ensuremath{''}$, positional angle of 54.9 degrees, natural weighting" (ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-check all results with and without JvM correction; consistent flux-density estimates; continuum rms decreased 21%.  
  > "by cross-checking all the results based on the data cubes with and without JvM correction applied." (ALMA); "We conclude that in both cases the flux density estimations are consistent." (ALMA); "The background rms decreased by $21\%$ from 19 to 15 $\mu{\rm Jy}\,{\rm beam}^{-1}$ for the continuum image." (ALMA)
2. **Measure integrated emission and moment maps**: Construct [C ii] moment maps; mask low-intensity channel pixels before moment-1 and moment-2; extract an integrated spectrum within an elliptical aperture.  
  > "we masked those pixels out before calculating moment-1 and moment-2." (Moment maps); "we show the \[C ii\] spectra of the HZ10 complex, extracted from the elliptical aperture" (Moment maps)
   - parameter: Exclude pixels below the respective channel's 2-σ noise level.  
  > "To do that we exclude pixels with intensity lower than the 2-$\sigma$ noise level of the respective channel." (Moment maps)
   - parameter: Zero velocity at the rest-frame [C ii] frequency at z = 5.6548.  
  > "Zero velocity corresponds to the rest-frame \[C ii\] frequency at $z=5.6548$." (Moment maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Masked and unmasked dispersion maps agree; integrated aperture flux agrees with Pavesi2016.  
  > "we compared the dispersion map calculated from the masked data with that derived from the unmasked data cube and found the values consistent." (Moment maps); "This flux is in agreement with the previously reported value estimated from the barely resolved \[C ii\] observations of (cite: Pavesi2016)." (Moment maps)
3. **Fit the spatial morphology**: Fit the [C ii] moment-0 map with two Sérsic components using PyAutoGalaxy, following Villanueva2024arXiv.  
  > "We perform parametric 2D modeling of the \[C ii\] moment-0 map with a two-component Sérsic profile using PyAutoGalaxy software." (Morphological 2D parametric modeling); "The procedure follows the steps as the 2D modeling of the HZ10 system presented in (cite: Villanueva2024arXiv)." (Morphological 2D parametric modeling)
   - software: PyAutoGalaxy, PyAutoFit
   - assumption: Two-component Sérsic representation.  
  > "We perform parametric 2D modeling of the \[C ii\] moment-0 map with a two-component Sérsic profile using PyAutoGalaxy software." (Morphological 2D parametric modeling)
   - assumption: Flat ΛCDM: Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7, h = 0.7; 5.899 kpc/'' at z = 5.65.  
  > "Throughout the paper, we adopt a standard flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with the matter and dark energy density parameters $\Omega_m = 0.3$, and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$" (Introduction); "with the reduced Hubble constant $h=0.7$, which gives a conversion scale of 5.899 kpc/$''$ at redshift $z=5.65$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect model residuals; no distinctive [C ii] bridge component in the two-Sérsic residual map.  
  > "The result of the modeling, along with the residuals between the observations and the model, are shown in Fig." (Morphological 2D parametric modeling); "we do not see such a distinctive component in the residuals of \[C ii\] line emission 2D parametric fit with two Sérsic profiles" (Kinematic analysis)
4. **Separate kinematic components with PV diagrams**: Extract PV diagrams along the whole-system main axis and the HZ10-C+HZ10-E major/minor axes; construct velocity-selected moment-0 maps.  
  > "calculate the PV diagram along the “main” axis connecting HZ10-C and HZ10-W components" (Kinematic analysis); "To calculate PV diagrams for the HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E complex, we placed a pseudo slit of 10-pixel width along its major and minor axes." (Kinematic analysis); "we show moment-0 maps, calculated within the specified velocity ranges" (Kinematic analysis)
   - parameter: 10-pixel-wide pseudo slit; cube pixel size 0.0372''.  
  > "We placed a pseudo slit of 10-pixel width, which is comparable with the beam size of the observations (pixel size of the data cube is 0.0372$''$)" (Kinematic analysis)
   - parameter: Major axis follows the higher velocity gradient excluding HZ10-W and is centered on the moment-0 intensity peak.  
  > "The major axis corresponds to the direction of the higher velocity gradient on the moment-1 map for these two components (excluding the HZ10-W component)." (Kinematic analysis); "It is centered on the intensity peak of the moment-0 map." (Kinematic analysis)
   - parameter: Minor-axis position defined using Vsys = −133 km s−1 from rotation-curve symmetry.  
  > "found it to be symmetrical about the systemic velocity of $V_{\rm sys}=-133$ km s$^{-1}$." (Kinematic analysis); "We use this systemic velocity to define the position of the minor kinematic axis." (Kinematic analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: The faint kinematically detected HZ10-E component aligns with a rest-frame optical component.  
  > "The latter provides independent confirmation of the reliability of the kinematically detected dim HZ10-E component in \[C ii\] emission." (Comparison with JWST/NIRSpec)
5. **Extract rotation and dispersion curves**: Extract major-axis aperture spectra and fit single/double Gaussians for velocity centroids and dispersions; focus on single-Gaussian curves for DysmalPy.  
  > "We fit the extracted spectra with single and double Gaussian profiles to calculate velocity centroids and velocity dispersion in each aperture." (Aperture spectra fit); "Although we performed both single and double Gaussian profile fits, our focus is on the single Gaussian fit, as we aim to model the rotation curves using DysmalPy." (Aperture spectra fit)
   - parameter: Circular apertures of diameter 0.26'' along the major axis.  
  > "We extract spectra along the major axis using circular apertures with a diameter of 0.26$''$" (Kinematic analysis)
   - assumption: Aperture-profile asymmetry partly results from beam-smearing blends.  
  > "The aperture spectra show distinct non-symmetric profiles, partly due to blending velocity components within the apertures from the beam-smearing effect." (Kinematic analysis)
   - fitting: Use a single Gaussian when a double-component fit leaves one component poorly constrained; analyze mock-cube spectra with single Gaussians too.  
  > "In cases where the double-component profile fitting effectively results in a single-component solution (i.e. one of the components is poorly constrained), we use only a single-Gaussian model fit." (Aperture spectra fit); "the aperture spectra extracted from the mock cube also exhibit asymmetrical profiles, which are further analyzed using single Gaussian fits." (Aperture spectra fit)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Test a rotating-disk mass model**: DysmalPy forward-models a 3D mock cube and compares observed/model rotation curves extracted in the same way.  
  > "generating a 3D mock cube capturing composite kinematics and accounting for observational effects such as beam smearing and instrumental line broadening." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "This allows us to compare the observed rotation curves with those extracted from the simulated data in the same way." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
   - software: DysmalPy, emcee
   - parameter: Free parameters: total baryon mass, disk effective radius, Sérsic index, dark-matter fraction within effective radius, intrinsic dispersion and inclination.  
  > "During the fitting, we treat total baryon mass, $M_{\rm bar}$, disk effective radius, $r_{\rm eff}$, disk Sérsic index, $n$, dark matter fraction within the effective radius, $f_{\rm DM}(r_{\rm eff})$, intrinsic velocity dispersion, $\sigma_0$, and galaxy inclination, $i$, as free parameters." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
   - assumption: HZ10-C+HZ10-E approximated by a rotating Sérsic baryon disk plus Navarro–Frenk–White dark-matter halo.  
  > "We test the feasibility of this scenario by approximating the HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E system with a disk model." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "We use a model consisting of a baryon disk parametrized by a Sérsic profile and a dark-matter halo with a Navarro–Frenk–White profile (cite: Navarro1996)." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
   - fitting: Bayesian affine MCMC with emcee; Gaussian priors on effective radius and Sérsic index from the 2D fit, and inclination from axis ratio; report posterior medians and 68 percent credible intervals.  
  > "we use the Bayesian framework with the affine Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler emcee (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013) implemented into DysmalPy." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "As prior Gaussian distributions of the HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E disk effective radius and Sérsic index, we use the results of the 2D parametric modeling" (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "The point estimations correspond to the median of the posterior distributions and the uncertainties are 68 percent credible intervals, respectively." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
   - validation: Compare modeled and observed velocity/dispersion curves and compare baryon mass with the literature CO-derived gas mass.  
  > "we overplot modeled rotation velocity and dispersion curves based on the best-fit results with the black curves." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "The estimated baryon mass of HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E is in agreement with the gas mass of the HZ10 estimated from the CO luminosity in (cite: Pavesi2019)." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "In addition, we use an estimate from the axis ratio derived during the 2D parametric modeling as a Gaussian prior distribution of the system inclination." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
7. **Measure rotation support**: Average the absolute minimum and maximum observed major-axis velocities, divide by sin(i), then divide by modeled beam-smearing-corrected intrinsic dispersion.  
  > "We calculated $V_{\rm rot}$ as an average between the absolute values of minimum and maximum observed velocities along the major kinematic axis" (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "divided by $\sin(i)$, inferred from the modeling." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "we estimated the ratio between inclination-corrected rotation velocity and derived from modeling intrinsic dispersion corrected by beam smearing." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
   - assumption: Inclination and intrinsic dispersion from the disk model.  
  > "divided by $\sin(i)$, inferred from the modeling." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "derived from modeling intrinsic dispersion corrected by beam smearing." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare the ratio with dynamically warm high-redshift disks and the dispersion with observations and simulations.  
  > "ratio is consistent with that observed in dynamically warm disk galaxies at $z\sim4-8$ (cite: Kohandel2024)" (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "significantly exceeds the values predicted by simulations (cite: Kohandel2024)" (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
8. **Check the effect of HZ10-E blending**: Model HZ10-E with a Gaussian in the first three aperture spectra, extract HZ10-C rotation curves and repeat DysmalPy modeling with the same model parameters.

## Qualitative findings

- At least three [C ii] components agree with the components identified in JWST/NIRSpec optical lines.  
  > "We found evidence for at least three \[C ii\] emission components of the HZ10 system, which are consistent with those recently identified with JWST/NIRSpec rest-frame optical emission line observations" (Discussion)
- HZ10-W is treated as a separate galaxy merging with HZ10-C.  
  > "we further regard HZ10-W as a separate galaxy merging with HZ10-C." (Kinematic analysis)
- [C ii] and broad [O iii] emission kinematically trace each other.  
  > "The joint analysis of the \[C ii\] and \[O iii\] spectral profiles and velocity maps indicates that \[C ii\] and broad \[O iii\] emission kinematically trace each other." (Discussion)
- HZ10-E is bright in [O iii] but very dim in [C ii].  
  > "Despite being the brightest component in the HZ10 system in \[O iii\], HZ10-E is very dim in \[C ii\]." (Discussion)
- HZ10-C remains highly dispersed after removing the modeled HZ10-E contribution.  
  > "We conclude that even when analyzes independently, HZ10-C exhibit a high velocity dispersion, similar to what we obtained for HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular and spectral resolution [C ii] data are needed to address HZ10 kinematics beyond previous barely resolved observations.  
  > "the kinematic properties of this galaxy could only be addressed with higher angular resolution data." (Early studies); "In this work, we address the kinematic properties of HZ10 based on the high angular and spectral resolution \[C ii\] 158$\mu$m emission." (Early studies)
- Why this method: DysmalPy accounts for beam smearing and instrumental broadening; observed and mock rotation curves are extracted consistently.  
  > "generating a 3D mock cube capturing composite kinematics and accounting for observational effects such as beam smearing and instrumental line broadening." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario); "This allows us to compare the observed rotation curves with those extracted from the simulated data in the same way." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
- Alternative: Retained alternatives: disturbed disk with HZ10-E as a clump; HZ10-E satellite merging with disk-like HZ10-C; triple merger with HZ10-C itself a close double merger.  
  > "(i) HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E is a disturbed galaxy disk, where HZ10-E is a fainter clump within the extended disk" (Discussion); "(ii) HZ10-E is a satellite galaxy merging with the disk-like galaxy HZ10-C" (Discussion); "(iii) HZ10-C$+$HZ10-E is triple merger where HZ10-C is itself a close double merger showing ongoing merging with the faint companion galaxy HZ10-E." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Connecting [C ii] PV emission may be a dusty bridge or beam smearing.  
  > "we see \[C ii\] emission connecting HZ10-C and HZ10-W, which could be associated with the dusty “bridge” component or potentially attributed to beam smearing." (Kinematic analysis)
- Alternative: JvM-corrected and uncorrected cubes give consistent flux-density estimates; figures use uncorrected cubes.  
  > "We conclude that in both cases the flux density estimations are consistent. Therefore, all the figures throughout the paper are based on the ALMA cubes without JvM correction." (ALMA)
- Alternative: HZ10-E's weak [C ii] may reflect lower metallicity, but component metallicities agree within uncertainties.  
  > "the decreased \[C ii\] flux for the HZ10-E component can be explained by its relatively low metallicity among the entire HZ10 complex" (Discussion); "However, metallicity measurements based on JWST observations for all three components agree with each other within the provided uncertainties." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Photoevaporation is only tentatively applicable to HZ10-E's optical [O iii]/[C ii] ratio.  
  > "not necessarily attributed to the increased \[O iii\] 5007$\AA$/\[C ii\] 158$\mu$m flux ratio, which means that this scenario may only be tentatively applicable to HZ10-E." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: About five independent beams across HZ10-C permit detailed kinematic analysis; the observations still cannot distinguish all proposed scenarios.  
  > "HZ10-C is resolved along its major kinematic axis in $\approx5$ independent beams." (Kinematic analysis); "Since we cannot currently discard any of the three possible scenarios, ALMA observations with a higher angular resolution that matches that of JWST data but with a superior spectral resolution are necessary to distinguish between them." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Line self-calibration was prevented by insufficient signal-to-noise.  
  > "The signal-to-noise level achieved was not enough to perform line self-calibration." (ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Beam smearing dominates multicomponent [C ii] profiles and prevents a comparable spaxel-by-spaxel broad-component search.  
  > "since the multi-component Gaussian profile of \[C ii\] emission is dominated by the beam smearing." (Comparison with JWST/NIRSpec); "The spatial resolution of ALMA \[C ii\] observations does not allow us to perform the same spaxel-by-spaxel \[C ii\] spectral fitting with a double Gaussian profile to search for the presence of a broad emission component" (Comparison with JWST/NIRSpec)
- Limitation (authors): No statistically significant broad [C ii] spectral component can be claimed.  
  > "the available data do not allow us to claim the presence of a statistically significant broad \[C ii\] spectral component." (Kinematic modeling: testing the disk scenario)
- Limitation (authors): All three central-system scenarios remain possible; higher-resolution ALMA observations are needed.  
  > "Since we cannot currently discard any of the three possible scenarios, ALMA observations with a higher angular resolution that matches that of JWST data but with a superior spectral resolution are necessary to distinguish between them." (Discussion)
