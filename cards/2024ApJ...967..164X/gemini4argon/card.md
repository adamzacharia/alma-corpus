## Analysis recipe

1. **Image VLA and ALMA continuum and spectral line cubes**: Subtract continuum in uv-plane (for VLA) and image spectral windows using CASA tclean with auto-masking and natural weighting.  
  > "For each spectral window, the continuum emission was subtracted in the uv -plane before imaging the spectral cubes. We used the tclean task with the auto-masking algorithm for imaging with the natural weighting scheme and a cell size of 0 . 27 ′′ ." (2. OBSERVATIONS); "Different from Xue et al. (2019), we used the tclean task with the auto-masking algorithm for the imaging analysis, consistent with the VLA imaging process." (2. OBSERVATIONS)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: VLA cell size = 0.27'', natural weighting, auto-masking  
  > "We used the tclean task with the auto-masking algorithm for imaging with the natural weighting scheme and a cell size of 0 . 27 ′′ ." (2. OBSERVATIONS)
   - parameter: ALMA synthesized beam = 2.23'' x 1.67'' (84 GHz) and 1.94'' x 1.49'' (95 GHz), median RMS = 4 mJy beam^-1  
  > "The resultant synthesized beam size is 2 . 23 ′′ × 1 . 67 ′′ at 84 GHz and 1 . 94 ′′ × 1 . 49 ′′ at 95 GHz with a median RMS value of 4 mJy beam − 1 ." (2. OBSERVATIONS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Compute extended collisional rate coefficients for CH2NH**: Calculate rotational transitions among the first 49 levels of CH2NH colliding with para-H2 and ortho-H2 using the coupled-states approximation in MOLSCAT, and derive hyperfine-resolved rates via the statistical approximation.  
  > "Extended from the calculations in Faure et al. (2018), we calculated the collisional data for rotational transitions of CH 2 NH among the first 49 rotational levels, i.e. up to the state 8 2 , 6 at 99.5 cm − 1, and for kinetic temperatures in the range 10 – 150 K." (4.2. CH 2 NH Masers); "Because close-coupling calculations are computationally very demanding when the temperature increases, we had recourse to the coupled-states approximation." (C. COLLISIONAL RATE COEFFICIENT CALCULATIONS FOR CH 2 NH)
   - software: MOLSCAT
   - parameter: EMAX = 1200 cm^-1 (p-H2) and 1318.64 cm^-1 (o-H2); total energies 2-800 cm^-1 (p-H2) and 120-918 cm^-1 (o-H2); Tk = 10-150 K  
  > "The basis for o-H 2 was restricted to j 2 = 1 and the MOLSCAT parameter EMAX (used to limit basis functions to CH 2 NH plus H 2 energies less than EMAX ) was set to 1200 cm − 1 for p-H 2 and 1318.64 cm − 1 for o-H 2 ." (C. COLLISIONAL RATE COEFFICIENT CALCULATIONS FOR CH 2 NH); "The coupled-states calculations were performed for total energies between 2 and 800 cm − 1 for p-H 2 and between 120 and 918 cm − 1 for o-H 2 ." (C. COLLISIONAL RATE COEFFICIENT CALCULATIONS FOR CH 2 NH)
   - assumption: Coupled-states approximation and statistical approximation for hyperfine splitting  
  > "Hyperfine-resolved rate coefficients were derived using the statistical approximation, as in Faure et al. (2018)." (C. COLLISIONAL RATE COEFFICIENT CALCULATIONS FOR CH 2 NH)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract spectra and model E-type CH3OH maser and non-maser transitions toward MS1 and MS2**: Extract spectra of four uncontaminated E-type CH3OH transitions toward MS1 and MS2 and fit with a modified multi-component non-LTE RADEX radiative transfer model in molsim using MCMC.  
  > "We implemented the RADEX non-LTE radiative transfer code (van der Tak et al. 2007) into molsim written in Python." (3.1. Non-LTE Radiative Transfer Modeling); "In our implementation, we construct a modification and extend it to support a continuum temperature ( T cont ) different from the local radiation field temperature ( T rad 1 ) and allow multiple components aligned perfectly along the line of sight (LOS)." (3.1. Non-LTE Radiative Transfer Modeling); "Four uncontaminated E -type CH 3 OH transitions covered in the collision data were used to constrain the physical parameters quantitatively using a non-LTE model and MCMC fitting algorithm." (4.1.1. Masers with Dark Continuum Background – MS1)
   - software: molsim, RADEX, Numba, emcee
   - parameter: 11 free parameters (Tk, nc, Ncol, dV, Vlsr for each of 2 components, plus Tcont); 100 walkers with 50,000 samples each (MS1) and 10,000 samples each (MS2); Ncol capped at 10^17.5 cm^-2 for MS1  
  > "There are 11 free parameters in total to be adjusted in the MCMC analyses for E -type CH 3 OH." (B. MCMC FITTING DETAILS); "Posterior probability distributions for each parameter and their covariances were generated with 100 walkers with 50000 samples each 5 ." (4.1.1. Masers with Dark Continuum Background – MS1); "Following 100 walkers with 10,000 samples each, all parameters converge to primarily Gaussian-like distributions in our model." (4.1.2. Masers with Bright Continuum Background – MS2)
   - assumption: Local radiation field is CMB only (Trad = 2.725 K); uniform spherical geometry; H2 is sole collision partner with thermal ortho-to-para ratio; continuum source is spatially distant from gas clumps; 10% systematic uncertainty added to baseline RMS  
  > "In this work, we assume that the local radiation is contributed by the cosmic microwave background only, i.e., T rad = 2 . 725 K (Mather et al. 1999) and a uniform spherical geometry." (3.1. Non-LTE Radiative Transfer Modeling); "We only consider H 2 as the collision partner, assuming a kinetic temperature-dependent thermal ortho-to-para ratio (OPR)," (3.1. Non-LTE Radiative Transfer Modeling); "and σ is the uncertainty of the observations including an estimated 10% systematic uncertainty with the standard baseline deviation." (3.2. MCMC Fitting)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC sampling with logarithmic Gaussian likelihood; for MS1, Gaussian priors on Vlsr (58.3 +/- 2 km s^-1), uniform prior on Tcont (2.725-15 K), uniform priors on remaining parameters; for MS2, Gaussian priors on Vlsr (centered at 83 and 63 km s^-1) and Tcont (6.67 +/- 0.52 K), uniform priors on remaining parameters.  
  > "The priors for V lsr of both components are chosen to be a Gaussian distribution centered at 58 . 3 km s − 1 with a standard deviation of 2 km s − 1 . Since the continuum is weak as evident by the observations, we opted a uniform prior distribution in the range of 2.725 − 15 K for T cont ." (B. MCMC FITTING DETAILS); "The priors for V lsr were chosen to be Gaussian distributions centering on 83 km s − 1 and 63 km s − 1 respectively, based on the difference between the observed sky frequency and the rest frequency. Meanwhile, the T cont prior also has a Gaussian distribution, based on the measured continuum level of 6 . 67 ± 0 . 52 K." (4.1.2. Masers with Bright Continuum Background – MS2)
   - validation: Verified that molsim reproduces RADEX down to machine precision when converged; compared synthetic spectra against observed profiles and noise levels; inspected corner plots for convergence and covariance.  
  > "The calculations in molsim reproduces the results returned by RADEX down to machine precision when the results are converged." (3.1. Non-LTE Radiative Transfer Modeling); "If we take the noise level measured in each passband into account, the synthetic spectral profiles fit satisfactorily with the observed spectra for both the maser and optically thick absorption features." (4.1.2. Masers with Bright Continuum Background – MS2)
4. **Model CH2NH maser hyperfine spectrum toward MS2**: Fit the 5.29 GHz CH2NH 1_1,0 - 1_1,1 hyperfine spectrum toward MS2 with a one-component non-LTE model using MCMC, adopting CH3OH posterior distributions for Tk and nH2 as priors.  
  > "We performed the MCMC analysis with a onecomponent model using the prior probability distributions depicted in Figure B4." (4.2 CH2NH Masers); "The posteriors on T k and n H 2 obtained from CH 3 OH (Table 4; Figure 5; Figure B3) are adopted as MCMC priors for the CH 2 NH model." (4.2. CH 2 NH Masers)
   - software: molsim, RADEX, Numba, emcee
   - parameter: 100 walkers with 10,000 samples each  
  > "As shown in the posterior distributions in Figure B4, following 10,000 samples with each of the 100 walkers, the samplers converge at a N col of 4 . 76 +1 − 0 . . 09 64 × 10 14 cm − 2 ." (4.2. CH 2 NH Masers)
   - assumption: 83 km s^-1 CH2NH and CH3OH maser components toward MS2 are spatially coherent and share physical conditions (Tk and nH2)  
  > "We opted to use E -type CH 3 OH discussed in Section 4.1.2 for physical constraints, where the 83 km s − 1 components between CH 3 OH and CH 2 NH are spatially coherent and are both associated with the K6 H-II region." (4.2. CH 2 NH Masers)
   - fitting: MCMC with CH3OH posteriors as priors for Tk and nH2, Gaussian priors for Vlsr, dV, and Tcont (based on measured 5 GHz continuum of 2687 +/- 58 K), and uniform prior for Ncol.  
  > "The priors for V lsr and dV are chosen to be Gaussian distributions, informed by the strongest emission feature (12 σ ) detected in the data. The Gaussian prior for T cont is based on the continuum level of 2687 ± 58 K measured by the VLA. Meanwhile, the N col of CH 2 NH has a non-informative uniformly distributed prior." (4.2. CH 2 NH Masers)
   - validation: Synthetic line profile reproduces all six hyperfine components of the 1_1,0 - 1_1,1 transition within the noise level.  
  > "The synthetic line profile is shown in Figure 8, reproducing six hyperfine components of the 1 1 , 0 − 1 1 , 1 transition within the noise level." (4.2. CH 2 NH Masers)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of the 84 GHz Class I CH3OH maser toward Sgr B2 and first interferometric map of the 5.29 GHz CH2NH maser toward Sgr B2(N).  
  > "We report the first detection of the Class I methanol (CH 3 OH) maser at 84 GHz and the first interferometric map of the methanimine (CH 2 NH) maser at 5.29 GHz toward this region." (ABSTRACT)
- The 5.29 GHz CH2NH maser at 83 km s^-1 is spatially and kinematically coherent with the 84 GHz Class I CH3OH maser toward the K6 H II region (MS2), indicating a collisional pumping mechanism for CH2NH.  
  > "We found that MS2, superimposed on the K6 H II region, harbors maser emission of both Class I CH 3 OH at 84 GHz and CH 2 NH at 5 . 29 GHz (Figure 11)." (5.2. Spatial Origin and Pumping Mechanism); "Their consistent morphology serves as strong evidence to support a similar collisional pumping mechanism for the CH 2 NH maser with the Class I CH 3 OH maser as well as a common physical condition for triggering these masers." (5.2. Spatial Origin and Pumping Mechanism)
- The 95 GHz CH3OH 8_0 - 7_1 A-type transition shows weaker emission toward the 84 GHz maser spots and is concentrated toward the N1 hot core, similar to thermal molecular lines.  
  > "In contrast, we found that the 95 GHz line exhibits weaker emission toward the identified maser spots in Sgr B2(N) compared to the 84 GHz line. The spatial distribution for the 95 GHz emission is similar to that for other thermal molecular lines, concentrating toward the N1 mm-wave source." (5.2. Spatial Origin and Pumping Mechanism)
- Masing clumps of CH3OH and CH2NH reside in the colder, lower-density external envelope of Sgr B2(N) (Tk ~ 29-37 K, nH2 ~ (5-8) x 10^4 cm^-3) rather than in the hot cores.  
  > "The relatively low values of temperatures and densities indicate that the masing clumps are situated in the colder and low-density external envelope." (5.3. Physical Conditions of Masing Clumps)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Single-dish PRIMOS observations had a large ~2.5' x 2.5' field of view at C band that blended Sgr B2 North and Main, so interferometric VLA and ALMA observations were needed to spatially disentangle the maser components.  
  > "However, the large ∼ 2 . 5 ′ × 2 . 5 ′ field of view of PRIMOS at C band ( ∼ 5 GHz) includes both the North and Main components of Sgr B2, complicating the interpretation of the spectral profiles. Therefore, it is crucial to have interferometric observations to disentangle in detail the various possible spatial components of maser emission." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: MCMC sampling with a multi-component modified RADEX code explores a larger parameter space more efficiently than least-squares fitting and yields full posterior probability distributions to assess parameter covariances.  
  > "One of the advantages of the MCMC technique over a traditional least-squares fit approach is that a larger parameter space can be explored more efficiently." (3.2 MCMC Fitting)
- Alternative: Single-clump LTE (thermal) model: rejected because observed brightness temperatures (>120 K) far exceed kinetic temperatures (~29-37 K) and spectral profiles deviate substantially from a thermal model.  
  > "Furthermore, we also performed a spectral analysis assuming a single excitation temperature to equal T k for comparison and found that the observed spectral profiles deviate substantially from the thermal model." (4. RESULTS)
- Alternative: Single-clump non-LTE maser model for MS1: rejected because strong background continuum needed to stimulate the 84.5 GHz maser introduces unphysical absorption features at 85.5 and 108.9 GHz and no compact continuum source is observed toward MS1.  
  > "In order to match the bright maser emission at 84.5 GHz, strong background radiation is needed to stimulate the maser gain in the single-clump model" (4.1.1 Masers with Dark Continuum Background - MS1)
- How they decided it was enough: Used 100 walkers with 10,000 samples each for MS2 (and 50,000 samples each for MS1 where RADEX convergence slowed down at high Ncol) until parameters converged to well-defined peaks.  
  > "We found that the convergence rate of the RADEX algorithm slows down tremendously at high N col > 10 17 cm − 2 . Therefore, we capped N col at 10 17 . 5 cm − 2 and used 50,000 samples per walker (five times the value used for MS2)." (B. MCMC FITTING DETAILS)
- Limitation (authors): The two-chain-clump model for MS1 does not match the low-frequency side of the 108.87 GHz emission plateau (70-90 km s^-1), and the MS2 model does not match the 64 km s^-1 absorption feature at 85.55 GHz; thus MCMC uncertainties are lower limits.  
  > "Our model, unfortunately, falls short in matching the low-frequency side of the emission plateau at 108.87 GHz, corresponding to the 0 0 − 1 − 1 transition at a velocity range of 70 − 90 km s − 1 ." (4.1.1. Masers with Dark Continuum Background – MS1); "from the MCMC results should be considered as a lower limit of the uncertainties" (4.1.1 Masers with Dark Continuum Background - MS1); "However, the low-velocity component (64 km s − 1 ) of the 6 − 2 − 7 − 1 transition fails to match the observed absorption feature at 85.55 GHz." (4.1.2. Masers with Bright Continuum Background – MS2)
- Limitation (authors): Measured brightness temperature of CH2NH at 5.29 GHz is a lower limit due to the limited angular resolution of the VLA observations.  
  > "The measured T B of CH 2 NH represents a lower limit to the T B , peak because of the limited angular resolution of the VLA observations." (Table 2)
- Limitation (authors): One-zone escape probability approximation in RADEX/molsim neglects maser beaming, inhomogeneities, and non-local radiative transfer effects, which become significant when optical depth tau < -1 (as in the 84 GHz CH3OH maser where |tau| ~ 2.5).  
  > "Geometric effects related to the propagation of maser radiation become increasingly significant as the optical depth becomes lower than -1. Although RADEX and MOLSIM support various geometries through escape probability approximation, other effects such as maser beaming are not treated." (5.4. Caveat on Geometric Effects); "The models also assume homogeneous physical conditions (i.e. one-zone models), which is inappropriate for very negative optical depths. For precisely modeling maser lines with strong amplification, a non-local treatment of radiation and level population is required." (5.4. Caveat on Geometric Effects)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For CH3OH at MS1 and MS2, the derived optical depth is tau ~ -2.44 to -2.52, where maser saturation, beaming, and non-local radiative transfer effects can substantially alter level populations compared to the local escape probability approximation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The VLA beam (~3.4'' x 1.3'') and ALMA beam (~2.2'' x 1.7'') are not matched in uv-coverage or convolved to a common resolution before extracting spectra toward MS2.
