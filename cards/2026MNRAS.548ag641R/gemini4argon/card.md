## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image new VLA 6.8 mm and 3 cm observations and measure fluxes, plus extract archival ALMA 3 mm flux**: Calibrate VLA Q and X band data with CASA and the VLA pipeline, perform one round of phase-only self-calibration, image with tclean (natural weighting at 6.8 mm, briggs robust=0.5 at 3 cm, hobgom deconvolver), measure 3 cm fluxes with imfit, and fit a Gaussian to unresolved archival ALMA 3 mm emission.  
  > "The data were calibrated using CASA version 6.6.6 (cite: CASA) and the VLA pipeline (cite: VLApipeline) version 2025.1.0.32." (New VLA data); "We estimated a 3 mm flux of $10.6\pm0.5$ mJy by fitting a Gaussian to the emission, as the source appears unresolved." (Ancillary observations and spectral energy distributions); "The new VLA observations were imaged using the tclean task in CASA version 6.6.6." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - software: CASA, VLA pipeline, tclean, imfit
   - parameter: Q band: natural weighting, hobgom deconvolver; X band: briggs weighting with robust = 0.5, hobgom deconvolver  
  > "In the case of the Q band, we used natural weighting to maximize sensitivity, while for the X band we used briggs weighting (cite: Briggs1995) with a robust parameter of 0.5" (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass); "For both wavelengths, the hobgom deconvolver was used." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - assumption: Absolute flux calibration uncertainty is 5% at ALMA 3 mm and VLA 3 cm, and 10% at VLA 6.8 mm  
  > "The uncertainty includes a 5 % absolute flux calibration error." (Ancillary observations and spectral energy distributions); "where the uncertainty includes the image root mean square (rms) of 19 $\mu$Jy/beam as well as the 10% absolute flux calibration error at Q band." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass); "where the uncertainties combine those from imfit and a 5% absolute flux calibration uncertainty at X band." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit for ALMA 3 mm flux; two point sources fitted with CASA imfit for blended 3 cm VLA emission  
  > "We estimated a 3 mm flux of $10.6\pm0.5$ mJy by fitting a Gaussian to the emission, as the source appears unresolved." (Ancillary observations and spectral energy distributions); "We therefore calculated the flux of the A and B components by fitting two point sources with the imfit task in CASA." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - validation: Phase-only self-calibration increased peak SNR from 9 to 13 at 6.8 mm and from 42 to 49 at 3 cm  
  > "This process increased the peak signal to noise from 9 to 13 at 6.8 mm, and from 42 to 49 for the 3 cm data." (New VLA data)
2. **Estimate disc dust mass of HD 98800B from 6.8 mm flux**: Calculate optically thin dust mass from the 6.8 mm VLA flux using standard blackbody relation and bootstrap 1000 realizations to propagate parameter uncertainties  
  > "Under the assumption of optically thin emission, the dust mass of protoplanetary discs can be estimated from their millimetre fluxes (cite: Hildebrand1983; Beckwith1990)" (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass); "To account for these uncertainties, we bootstrapped 1000 estimates by randomly varying the 6.8 mm VLA flux within its uncertainty following a Gaussian distribution" (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - parameter: 1000 bootstrap realizations  
  > "To account for these uncertainties, we bootstrapped 1000 estimates" (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission at 6.8 mm, distance d = 45 pc, dust opacity at 6.8 mm varied log-uniformly between 0.03 and 0.3 g cm^-2, dust temperature varied uniformly between 50 and 100 K  
  > "where $M_{\rm dust}$ is the total dust mass, $F_\nu$ is the flux density observed at the frequency $\nu$, $d$ is the distance to the source (which we set to 45 pc)" (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass); "the dust opacity at 6.8 mm between 0.03 and 0.3 g cm$^{-2}$ uniformly in logarithmic space (cite: Painter2025), and the dust temperature uniformly between 50 and 100 K." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Temperature range is consistent with radiative transfer models  
  > "as compact discs such as the one around HD 98800B will be hotter, and it is also consistent with the results of the radiative transfer models in Sec." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
3. **Measure azimuthal asymmetry significance in VLA 6.8 mm and 8.8 mm images**: Compare peak brightness on the east and west sides of the disc relative to the image rms noise: (I_max,East - I_max,West) / sqrt(2 * sigma_rms^2)  
  > "We calculate the statistical significance of the asymmetry as $(I_{\rm max, East} - I_{\rm max, West})/\sqrt{2 \sigma_{\rm rms}^2}$, where $I_{\rm max, East}$ and $I_{\rm max, West}$ are the emission peaks on the east and west side of the disc, and $\sigma_{\rm rms}$ is the corresponding image noise." (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Asymmetry appears in two independent VLA datasets (6.8 mm and 8.8 mm)  
  > "the significance is further increased if we consider that the asymmetry appears on two independent observations" (VLA images, fluxes, and dust mass)
4. **Derive pairwise mm/cm spectral indices and fit multi-wavelength SED model for dust and non-thermal contamination**: Compute pairwise spectral indices alpha = log(F_nu1/F_nu2)/log(nu1/nu2) and fit all photometry at lambda >= 500 um with a 7-parameter model combining a sigmoid-transitioning double power-law dust component and a single power-law contamination component using MCMC  
  > "We used the measured fluxes of A and B to determine the corresponding spectral indices (calculated simply as $\alpha = \log(F_{\nu_1}/F_{\nu_2}) / \log(\nu_1/\nu_2)$, where $F_{\nu_1}$ and $F_{\nu_2}$ are the fluxes at frequencies $\nu_1$ and $\nu_2$)" (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths); "The total model is then defined as $F_{\rm total} (\nu)= F_{\rm dust}(\nu) + F_{\rm cont}(\nu)$, and contains seven free parameters: $F_{0,\rm dust}$, $\eta_-$, $\eta_+$, $\nu_{\rm dust}$, $\gamma$, $F_{0,\rm cont}$, and $\alpha_{\rm cont}$." (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths); "With this model, we fit all existing photometric data at wavelengths $\geq$500 $\mu$m with a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach using the emcee package (cite: emcee)." (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 7 free parameters: F_0,dust, eta_-, eta_+, nu_dust, gamma, F_0,cont, alpha_cont  
  > "and contains seven free parameters: $F_{0,\rm dust}$, $\eta_-$, $\eta_+$, $\nu_{\rm dust}$, $\gamma$, $F_{0,\rm cont}$, and $\alpha_{\rm cont}$." (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths)
   - assumption: Non-thermal contamination is represented by a single power law; alpha_cont <= eta_+ <= eta_- enforced  
  > "and a single power law to account for all other emission mechanisms (hereafter referred to as contamination)." (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths); "We also enforced $\alpha_{\rm cont} \leq \eta_+ \leq \eta_-$ to prevent swapping between contributions." (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; uniform log priors on F_0,dust in [-4, -2] and F_0,cont in [-7, -4], uniform priors on eta_- in [1.5, 4], eta_+ in [1.5, 3], log(nu_dust) in [10, 1000] GHz, Gaussian prior on gamma with mean -2 and std 0.5  
  > "We used broad priors for all parameters: uniform positive priors for $F_{0,\rm dust}$ and $F_{0,\rm cont}$ explored in logarithmic space (between -4 and -2, and -7 and -4, respectively), uniform priors for $\eta_-$ (between 1.5 and 4) and $\eta_+$ (between 1.5 and 3), a uniform prior for $\log(\nu_{\rm dust})$ between 10 and 1000 GHz (i.e., 0.3 mm to 3 cm), and a broad Gaussian for $\gamma$ centered at -2 with a standard deviation of 0.5 following (cite: Painter2025)." (Spectral indices at millimetre/centimetre wavelengths)
   - validation: Compared with a simpler two power-law fit to SMA, ALMA 1.3 mm, and four VLA bands which yielded alpha_dust = 2.09 +/- 0.05  
  > "A simpler modeling approach using two power laws (one for the dust contribution and one for the contamination) yielded $\alpha_{\rm dust}=2.09\pm0.05$ when fitting the fluxes from SMA (880 $\mu$m), ALMA (1.3 mm), and the four VLA wavelengths." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800)
5. **Test illumination and optically thick inner wall explanations for the azimuthal asymmetry**: Calculate binary orbital phases at the epochs of ALMA 1.3 mm and VLA 6.8 and 8.8 mm observations, and generate 3D radiative transfer simulated images at 1.3 mm, 6.8 mm, and 8.8 mm with MCFOST convolved with the observed beams  
  > "We calculated the orbital phase $\phi$ at the time of the VLA 6.8 mm, ALMA 1.3 mm, and VLA 8.8 mm observations using a period of 314.8 days (cite: Zuniga2021)" (Illumination changes during the binary orbit); "To show this effect, we produced a radiative transfer model of HD 98800B using the MCFOST code (cite: MCFOST; MCFOST2)." (Emission from an optically thick inner wall); "show a comparison of the observations at 1.3 mm, 6.8 mm, and 8.8 mm with the simulated images at similar wavelengths and using the corresponding beams." (Emission from an optically thick inner wall)
   - software: MCFOST
   - parameter: Disc radial extent 2.5 to 4.6 au, inclination 26 deg, PA 16 deg, dust mass 5e-5 M_sun, stars placed 0.5 au above/below disc plane  
  > "update the radial extent (2.5 to 4.6 au), inclination (26 $\deg$), and position angle (16 $\deg$) to match those from the ALMA high-resolution observations in (cite: Kennedy2019)." (Emission from an optically thick inner wall); "We note that the model is quite massive for such a small size ($5\times10^{-5}~M_\odot$ in dust)" (Emission from an optically thick inner wall); "We also place the stars 0.5 au above/below the disc plane to better produce a stellar illumination pattern representative of the one experienced by the disc during the binary orbit." (Emission from an optically thick inner wall)
   - assumption: Far side of the disc is located on the east side in the test model; dust density distribution is azimuthally symmetric  
  > "In our configuration, the far side of the disc is located on the east side." (Emission from an optically thick inner wall); "Since dust density structure used in the models is azimuthally symmetric, this is solely due to the different beam orientations in both VLA observations." (Emission from an optically thick inner wall)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared simulated images at 1.3 mm, 6.8 mm, and 8.8 mm against ALMA and VLA observations, ruling out the inner wall hypothesis because the 1.3 mm ALMA image lacks the predicted east-side asymmetry  
  > "given that the emission at 1.3 mm is optically thicker than at the VLA wavelengths, the asymmetry should be clearly visible at 1.3 mm on the east side too (see Figure 1). This is not the case in the ALMA observations" (Emission from an optically thick inner wall)

## Qualitative findings

- The dust emission in the HD 98800B disc is optically thick at wavelengths <= 2-3 mm (with alpha_dust <= 2.15 and < 2 up to 3 mm) and becomes at least partially optically thin at VLA wavelengths (6.8 and 8.8 mm).  
  > "yields low $\alpha_{\rm dust}$ values ($\leq 2.15$) for wavelengths $\lesssim$3 mm." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800); "The derived dust spectral index indicates that the disc is optically thick up to 2-3 mm, especially given the $\alpha_{\rm dust} < 2$ value." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800); "The increase in the dust spectral index at longer wavelengths imply that the emission is becoming optically thinner." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800)
- Large dust grains (maximum grain sizes of 0.1-1 mm) are present in the polar circumbinary disc of HD 98800B.  
  > "If the VLA emission is indeed optically thin, the values of $\alpha_{\rm dust}$ at long millimetre wavelengths indicates the presence of large grains in the disc, corresponding to maximum grain sizes of 0.1-1 mm (cite: Testi2014; Birnstiel2018; Guidi2022; Birnstiel2024)." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800)
- Centimetre emission (>= 3 cm) from both HD 98800A and HD 98800B is dominated by non-thermal gyro-synchrotron emission from stellar magnetic activity (with possible free-free contribution in B).  
  > "In the case of HD 98800B, the emission at 3-5 cm likely arises from gyro-synchrotron emission, although a contribution from free-free cannot be ruled out. For the discless HD 98800A component, the fluxes at 8.8 mm, 3 cm, and 5 cm can be more robustly attributed to gyro-synchrotron from stellar activity." (Conclusions)
- The eastern side of the HD 98800B disc is 15-25% brighter at 6.8 and 8.8 mm but symmetric at 1.3 mm (ALMA), indicating a physical local dust overdensity (such as a vortex or flyby relic) rather than variable binary illumination or a hot inner wall.  
  > "Both VLA observations of the disc at 6.8 and 8.8 mm show an azimuthal asymmetry, with its east side appearing 15-25% brighter. In contrast, the ALMA 1.3 mm data do not show signs of this asymmetry." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: VLA observations at 6.8 mm and 3 cm in the most extended A configuration were obtained to sample the mm/cm SED, separate non-thermal contamination from thermal dust emission, and spatially resolve the compact HD 98800B disc.  
  > "a robust characterization of their contribution to the observed fluxes is necessary to accurately constrain the dust millimetre spectral index and quantify the level of grain growth in the disc." (Introduction); "Both wavelengths were observed in the A configuration (baselines ranging from 0.8 to 36 km) to achieve the highest angular resolution possible." (New VLA data)
- Why this method: Used the Painter et al. (2025) sigmoid-transitioning double power-law dust prescription plus a single power-law contamination term because the sparse >= 5 mm SED cannot break the degeneracy of a three-component (dust, free-free, synchrotron) fit.  
  > "given the scarcity of observations at $\gtrsim$5 mm for the HD 98800 system, fitting three components (dust, free-free, and synchrotron) would be highly degenerate with the existing data." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800)
- Alternative: Simpler two power-law SED model (one power law for dust and one for non-thermal contamination) considered as a baseline check.  
  > "A simpler modeling approach using two power laws (one for the dust contribution and one for the contamination) yielded $\alpha_{\rm dust}=2.09\pm0.05$ when fitting the fluxes from SMA (880 $\mu$m), ALMA (1.3 mm), and the four VLA wavelengths." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800)
- Alternative: Three-component SED model (dust + free-free + synchrotron) rejected due to degeneracy from sparse long-wavelength coverage.  
  > "given the scarcity of observations at $\gtrsim$5 mm for the HD 98800 system, fitting three components (dust, free-free, and synchrotron) would be highly degenerate with the existing data." (Grain growth and non-thermal emission in HD 98800)
- Alternative: Illumination variations during the eccentric polar orbit of Ba-Bb rejected as the cause of the VLA azimuthal asymmetry.
