## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum and spectral images**: Image with TCLEAN; subtract a first-order uv-plane continuum fit; form spectral cubes.  
  > "To image the data, we used the TCLEAN algorithm." (Observations); "For the lines of interest, we then subtracted the continuum emission by fitting a first-order polynomial to the continuum in the $uv$-plane" (Observations)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN
   - parameter: Briggs R = 0.5  
  > "the Briggs weighting parameter $R$ was set to 0.5 for the continuum map and spectral line image cubes." (Observations)
   - parameter: 0.06 km s^-1; 256 × 256 pixels; 0.1'' pixels  
  > "produced spectral data cubes with 0.06 km s$^{-1}$ velocity resolution." (Observations); "The image cubes were constructed on a 256 $\times$ 256 pixel grid with 0.1$''$ pixel size." (Observations)
   - assumption: Weak continuum subtraction should not remove significant line flux  
  > "Given the relatively weak continuum emission, continuum subtraction should not remove significant line flux (cite: Weaver2018)." (Observations)
   - fitting: First-order polynomial continuum fit in the uv-plane  
  > "fitting a first-order polynomial to the continuum in the $uv$-plane" (Observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure integrated emission**: Construct moment 0 maps, merging CN A–E and clipping below 1 sigma; measure continuum and line fluxes.  
  > "including a map merging CN components A–E. by clipping the signal at $<$1$\sigma_{rms}$." (Observations); "The integrated emission line intensities over the entire emitting region are 1.60 $\pm$ 0.05 Jy km s$^{-1}$, 3.90 $\pm$ 0.12 Jy km s$^{-1}$, and 1.80 $\pm$ 0.16 Jy km s$^{-1}$ for CS, CN, and CO, respectively." (Observations)
   - parameter: Clip signal below 1 sigma_rms  
  > "by clipping the signal at $<$1$\sigma_{rms}$." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum agrees with (cite: Dutrey2017); CN flux agrees with IRAM measurement from (cite: Reboussin2015).  
  > "The total continuum flux is 37.4 $\pm$ 0.88 mJy at 242 GHz, which is in good agreement with 35 mJy" (Observations); "The CN line flux is consistent with that previously obtained by (cite: Reboussin2015), 3.4 $\pm$ 0.5 Jy km s$^{-1}$, with the IRAM 30 m telescope." (Observations)
3. **Measure radial structure and characteristic emission heights**: Fit vertical Gaussian profiles and deconvolve the beam; use double-Gaussian peak offsets for CO; compare heights with radius.  
  > "For the continuum, CS, and CN, which are marginally resolved we use the FWHMs of Gaussian fits to the observed vertical profiles to quantify $H_{r}$." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "To obtain $H_{r}$, we then deconvolve the beam width ($\theta_{FWHM}$) from the resulting FWHM" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "We then adopt the average of these (upper and lower) $H_{r}$ values." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
   - parameter: Vertical cuts at PA ~3.6 degrees east from north; radii 60 to ~300 au  
  > "we estimate the characteristic vertical disk height of CS, CN, and CO emission ($H_{r}$) at various radii from 60 au to $\sim$300 au." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "These vertical profiles were extracted along a position angle (PA) of $\sim$3.6$^\circ$ measured east from north (cite: Dutrey2017)." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
   - parameter: Only signal >3 sigma; CO profiles only on the east side  
  > "we only analyze vertical profiles from the least contaminated region, i.e. the east side of the disk." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "Overall, $H_{r}$ values are only measured over the area where the signal is $> 3\sigma$." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
   - parameter: Hr = sqrt(FWHM^2 - theta_FWHM^2)/(2 sqrt(2 ln 2)); CO additionally includes |mu|  
  > "\rm H_{r} = \frac{\left ( \sqrt{ \rm FWHM^{2} - \theta_{ \rm FWHM}^{2}} \right )}{2\sqrt{ \rm 2\: \textup{ln}2}}." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "\rm H_{r} = \frac{\left ( \sqrt{ \rm FWHM^{2} - \theta_{FWHM}^{2}} \right )}{2\sqrt{2\: \textup{ln}2}}  +  \left |  \mu \right | ," (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
   - assumption: 120 pc distance  
  > "Its distance is estimated at $\sim$ 120 pc (cite: Loinard2008)." (Introduction); "we adopt this distance for all calculations in this paper." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Similar characteristic heights on west and east; average their FWHM values  
  > "Assuming that the characteristic emission height at a given radius $r$ is similar on the western and eastern sides of the disk, the estimated FWHM values are averaged." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
   - fitting: Gaussian profiles; multi-Gaussian CO profiles; uncertainties are formal Gaussian-fit errors  
  > "For the continuum, CS, and CN, which are marginally resolved we use the FWHMs of Gaussian fits to the observed vertical profiles to quantify $H_{r}$." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "Multi-gaussian fitting performed; see text." (Table 2); "The tabulated uncertainties only reflect the formal errors on the Gaussian fits to the intensity profiles" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
   - validation: West/east profiles checked at 170 au; double-Gaussian treatment gives similar CS and CN heights.  
  > "illustrate representative profiles along vertical cuts at a radius of 170 au on each side, verifying their similarity." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "We find very similar values of $H_{r}$ for CS and CN if we adopt the double-Gaussian fitting approach used for CO" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
4. **Constrain disk kinematics**: Extract a major-axis PV diagram and compare with geometrically thin Keplerian rotation using CN B as reference.  
  > "obtained from a cut along the major axis of the disk (i.e. P.A $\sim$ 93.5 deg.)." (Position-Velocity Diagram); "Taking the brightest HF line (B component) as the reference frame, the PV diagram is well reproduced by a Keplerian rotation profile in a geometrically thin disk" (Position-Velocity Diagram)
   - parameter: Major-axis P.A. ~93.5 degrees; CN B reference  
  > "obtained from a cut along the major axis of the disk (i.e. P.A $\sim$ 93.5 deg.)." (Position-Velocity Diagram); "Taking the brightest HF line (B component) as the reference frame" (Position-Velocity Diagram)
   - assumption: Geometrically thin Keplerian disk; distance 120 pc  
  > "a Keplerian rotation profile in a geometrically thin disk" (Position-Velocity Diagram); "assuming a distance of 120 pc." (Position-Velocity Diagram)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Agreement with the previous CS-based mass; fainter HF components also show Keplerian rotation.  
  > "These values are in good agreement with the previous estimate of 0.58$\pm$ 0.01 M$_{\odot}$ obtained from a model of the disk CS emission by (cite: Dutrey2017)." (Position-Velocity Diagram); "These satellite components also present a typical Keplerian rotation in a disk." (CN HF Components)
5. **Measure CN hyperfine profiles and opacity indicators**: Extract ellipse-aperture spectra where HF components are resolved; fit A–E with Gaussians; compare ratios to optically thin LTE predictions.  
  > "presents the spectra extracted from ellipse-shaped apertures" (CN Line Profiles and Parameters); "We used Gaussian fits to these line profiles to determine line widths and relative integrated intensities of the five HF components A–E." (CN Line Profiles and Parameters)
   - parameter: Radii >210 au on both east and west sides; ratios A/B, C/B, D/B and E/B  
  > "The CN HF transitions are well resolved at radii larger than 210 au, on both the east and west sides of the disk." (CN Line Profiles and Parameters); "the ratio of the intensity of the HF components relative to the brightest component (A/B, C/B, D/B, and E/B) would range between 0.1 and 0.7" (CN Line Profiles and Parameters)
   - assumption: Optically thin LTE ratios as reference; uniform excitation temperature for thick-line saturation  
  > "Under optically thin local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions" (CN Line Profiles and Parameters); "Under optically thick conditions and for a uniform excitation temperature, the lines would saturate, reaching equal intensities in the limit of very large optical depth." (CN Line Profiles and Parameters)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits for line widths and relative integrated intensities  
  > "We used Gaussian fits to these line profiles to determine line widths and relative integrated intensities of the five HF components A–E." (CN Line Profiles and Parameters)
   - validation: Compare measured HF ratios with theoretical ratios adapted from (cite: Punzi2015)  
  > "as determined by the measured HF transition ratios shown in Table" (CN Line Profiles and Parameters); "The relative intensities of HF transitions of CN are adapted from Table 2 of (cite: Punzi2015)." (CN Line Profiles and Parameters)
6. **Generate non-LTE CN spectral models**: Build and interpolate a 3-D RADEX LVG grid; use predicted Tex and tau to synthesize and sum A, B and C profiles.  
  > "we have modeled the CN HF line structure using a grid-based parameter exploration approach that exploits the non-LTE radiative transfer code RADEX under the so-called Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) framework (cite: vanderTak2007)." (CN Column Densities); "we upsampled these RADEX 3D-grid models by interpolating between grid points to increase the resolution of the parameter space explored." (CN Column Densities); "The complete synthetic spectrum of the CN 2–1 lines for a given parameter set is obtained as the sum of intensities of the individual HF components" (CN Column Densities)
   - software: RADEX, PySpecKit
   - parameter: 21 H2 densities: 10^4–10^9 cm^-3  
  > "21 values of H$_{2}$ density in the range $\rm n_{H_{2}}$ = 10$^{4}$ - 10$^{9}$ cm$^{-3}$" (CN Column Densities)
   - parameter: 21 CN columns: 10^11–10^17 cm^-2  
  > "21 values of CN column density in the range $\rm N_{CN}$ = 10$^{11}$ - 10$^{17}$ cm$^{-2}$" (CN Column Densities)
   - parameter: 51 kinetic temperatures: 5–60 K  
  > "51 values of $\rm T_{kin}$ from 5 to 60 K." (CN Column Densities)
   - assumption: Fixed FWHM 0.4 km s^-1 for all three HF components  
  > "The FWHM line width of all three HF components was held fixed at 0.4 km s$^{-1}$, which is the average of the individual line-widths derived" (CN Column Densities)
   - assumption: Background temperature 2.73 K; collision rates from (cite: HilyBlant2013)  
  > "For the background temperature, we adopt 2.73 K." (CN Column Densities); "using collision rates from (cite: HilyBlant2013)." (CN Column Densities)
   - assumption: Restrict modeling to spectrally resolved regions because RADEX does not treat line overlaps  
  > "Since RADEX does not treat line overlaps, we restricted the spectral modeling to disk regions where these CN HF structures are well resolved" (CN Column Densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: A posteriori similar component excitation temperatures and modest A/B overlap  
  > "This procedure is validated a posteriori because the excitation temperatures of each component are very similar and line overlap for the A and B components is modest" (CN Column Densities)

## Qualitative findings

- CO, CN and CS emission is vertically stratified, successively closer to the dust midplane.  
  > "the peak emission from the three molecular species is stratified, with CO, CN, and CS lying increasingly close to the disk midplane" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- CN is radially more extended and elevated relative to CS.  
  > "The CN originates from a layer that is radially more extended than and elevated with respect to CS." (Vertical Molecular Emission Structure)
- CN has a midplane deficit at ~80–200 au and extends toward the midplane in the outer disk.  
  > "there appears to be a dearth of CN emission at the midplane at radii between $\sim$80 and 200 au" (Vertical Molecular Emission Structure); "At the outer regions of the disk, the CN emission arises from the upper and lower intermediate layers and extends down to the midplane" (Vertical Molecular Emission Structure)
- CN PV structure is consistent with Keplerian rotation.  
  > "the PV diagram reveals a disk consistent with Keplerian rotation" (Position-Velocity Diagram)
- CN approaches thermalization near the midplane and is subthermally excited in intermediate layers.  
  > "Our results characterized a disk with thermal emission from the midplane and sub-thermal conditions in intermediate layers." (Summary)
- Cold CN suggests FUV alone may be insufficient; deeper-penetrating radiation such as X-rays may contribute.  
  > "This suggests that FUV radiation alone may be insufficient to explain the observed CN abundances, and one must invoke more deeply penetrating sources of high energy radiation, such as X-rays" (CN: Location and Formation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Nearly edge-on geometry enables a direct investigation of CN vertical distribution.  
  > "To more directly investigate the vertical distribution of CN molecules within protoplanetary disks, we have exploited archival ALMA data obtained for the nearly edge-on Flying Saucer disk" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Briggs R = 0.5 balances sensitivity and resolution; non-LTE modeling constrains CN conditions; resolved HF regions avoid RADEX line-overlap limitations.  
  > "To achieve a good balance between sensitivity and angular resolution the Briggs weighting parameter $R$ was set to 0.5 for the continuum map and spectral line image cubes." (Observations); "we have modeled the CN HF line structure using a grid-based parameter exploration approach that exploits the non-LTE radiative transfer code RADEX" (CN Column Densities); "Since RADEX does not treat line overlaps, we restricted the spectral modeling to disk regions where these CN HF structures are well resolved" (CN Column Densities)
- Alternative: Single-Gaussian height approximation is unsuitable for double-peaked CO profiles; use fitted peak offsets and widths.  
  > "These effects are manifested in the form of a lack of CO emission from the midplane, generating double-peaked vertical intensity profiles" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "Hence, the disk scale height cannot be characterized by the above approximation" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- Alternative: Double-Gaussian fitting was also tried for CS and CN, yielding similar heights.  
  > "We find very similar values of $H_{r}$ for CS and CN if we adopt the double-Gaussian fitting approach used for CO" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- Alternative: CN models with Tkin >25 K underestimate component C.  
  > "For $\rm T_{kin}$ $>$ 25 K, we find the models fail to reproduce (underestimate) the intensity of the C component of the CN HF structure." (CN Column Densities)
- Alternative: Dense cold and lower-density warm models can both reproduce HF lines.  
  > "there is an inherent degeneracy, wherein the HF lines are well reproduced either by very dense and cold gas or by lower density, warmer gas." (CN Column Densities)
- Alternative: FUV-only formation is questioned in favor of deeper-penetrating radiation or combined X-ray/FUV irradiation.  
  > "This suggests that FUV radiation alone may be insufficient to explain the observed CN abundances, and one must invoke more deeply penetrating sources of high energy radiation, such as X-rays" (CN: Location and Formation)
- How they decided it was enough: Measure heights only above 3 sigma; model only resolved outer-disk HF spectra; select lowest chi-square model.  
  > "Overall, $H_{r}$ values are only measured over the area where the signal is $> 3\sigma$." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "eight specific outer disk regions (r $>$ 210 au) where the HF components are spectrally well resolved" (CN Column Densities); "the model with the lowest $\chi^{2}$ value." (CN Column Densities)
- Limitation (authors): Characteristic emission heights should not be equated with physical disk scale heights; opacity affects interpretation.  
  > "the emission from a given species only directly traces the disk density structure and abundance gradients if optically thin" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights); "it is important not to equate the results for $H_{r}$ to disk scale height measurements." (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- Limitation (authors): Continuum, CS and CN vertical profiles are marginally resolved.  
  > "For the continuum, CS, and CN, which are marginally resolved" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- Limitation (authors): CO is contaminated by background clouds near ~1.8 and 6–7 km s^-1.  
  > "CO is contaminated by background emission from extended molecular clouds at velocities around $\sim$1.8 km s$^{-1}$ and in the range 6-7 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Guilloteau2016)" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- Limitation (authors): Height uncertainties include only formal Gaussian-fit errors.  
  > "The tabulated uncertainties only reflect the formal errors on the Gaussian fits to the intensity profiles" (Integrated Intensity Maps and Scale Heights)
- Limitation (authors): RADEX does not treat line overlaps; modeling is restricted to resolved HF regions.  
  > "Since RADEX does not treat line overlaps, we restricted the spectral modeling to disk regions where these CN HF structures are well resolved" (CN Column Densities)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature-density degeneracy and difficult-to-quantify temperature systematics.  
  > "there is an inherent degeneracy, wherein the HF lines are well reproduced either by very dense and cold gas or by lower density, warmer gas." (CN Column Densities); "the results for $\rm T_{kin}$ are subject to systematic uncertainties" (CN Column Densities); "that are difficult to quantify." (CN Column Densities)
- Limitation (authors): East-west thermalization differences may not reflect a real physical asymmetry.  
  > "it remains to be determined whether the apparent differences in the CN thermalization inferred for the two sides of the disk reflect a real asymmetry in disk physical conditions." (CN Physical Conditions)
- Limitation (authors): Very low inferred temperatures pose a gas-phase depletion problem for CN and CS.  
  > "one would expect CN and CS to be severely depleted from the gas phase in disk regions in which $\rm T_{kin}$ drops well below the freeze-out temperature of CO" (CN Physical Conditions)
- Limitation (authors): No X-ray detection; edge-on geometry prevents a firm inference of intrinsic stellar X-ray luminosity.  
  > "Neither observation yield a detection at the position of the object." (Vertical Molecular Emission Structure); "Given the disk’s edge-on orientation, however, it is difficult to draw conclusions as to the intrinsic X-ray luminosity of the central star." (Vertical Molecular Emission Structure)
- Limitation (authors): Higher-resolution, multiple-transition CN observations are needed to confirm cold temperatures and distinct production layers.  
  > "Higher-resolution ALMA imaging spectroscopy of multiple transitions of CN is required to confirm the cold outer disk temperatures we infer from our radiative transfer modeling." (Summary); "Such followup observations would also help establish whether there exist multiple, distinct disk layers involved in efficient CN production." (Summary)
