## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce VLT/NACO images and detect visual companion candidates**: Background subtract, flat-field, re-centre and average pairs of frames; astrometrically calibrate using ALMA coordinates and pixel scale; identify >5sigma signals using Daophot via photutils followed by visual inspection; compute azimuthal rms contrast curves.  
  > "the two exposures per star are background subtracted (one from the other), flat-fielded, and then re-centred to have the target in the center of the detector. Each final image is the mean of the two re-centred frames." (VLT/NACO); "The companion detection procedure starts with the automatic identification of $>$5$\sigma$ signals using the *Daophot* algorithm via the `photutils` package in the `astropy` library (cite: astropy:2018)." (VLT/NACO)
   - software: astropy, photutils
   - parameter: detection threshold > 5 sigma; pixel scale = 0.02719 arcsec/pixel; FOV ~ 32 arcsec  
  > "For the astrometric calibration, we adopt the ALMA coordinates for the center of the star, the true North direction as given by the position angle of the exposure on sky, and the pixel scale of 0.02719 arcsec/pixel." (VLT/NACO); "The companion detection procedure starts with the automatic identification of $>$5$\sigma$ signals using the *Daophot* algorithm" (VLT/NACO)
   - assumption: ALMA coordinates correspond to the center of the star  
  > "For the astrometric calibration, we adopt the ALMA coordinates for the center of the star" (VLT/NACO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of all automatic detections in individual images  
  > "All of these automatic detections are inspected visually for confirmation in the individual images (in very few cases some artifacts were selected by the algorithm)." (VLT/NACO)
2. **Assess physical boundness and background probability of detected companions**: Compare Gaia DR2 distances of components where available; for unconfirmed candidates, model background stars as a homogeneous spatial Poisson point process within a circle of radius equal to the companion separation using TRILEGAL galaxy model number densities; estimate substellar mass using AMES-COND models.  
  > "Systems for which *Gaia* detected the secondary component are listed in Table [1](#t:gaia); the systems that present discrepancies in the distances of the components are assumed as not physically bound." (Results); "For each companion candidate, we set $B$ to the area of a circle with a radius equal to the candidate separation, and estimate the number density of background stars that are of equal or greater brightness in that area of the sky using the TRILEGAL model of the Galaxy (cite: Girardi2012)." (Results)
   - software: TRILEGAL
   - parameter: Circle radius = candidate separation  
  > "For each companion candidate, we set $B$ to the area of a circle with a radius equal to the candidate separation" (Results)
   - assumption: Homogeneous spatial Poisson point process for background stars; discordant Gaia distances imply unbound systems  
  > "We first consider a homogeneous spatial Poisson point process to calculate the probability $\mathcal{P}$($n$=$0|\lambda, B$) of not having any background object" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compile disc dust masses and sizes and compare cumulative distributions across multiplicity categories**: Take Class II/III disc dust masses and sizes (90% flux curve-of-growth radius) from Ansdell et al. (2018) and Lovell et al. (2020); compute companion dust masses from Ansdell et al. (2018) mm fluxes; construct Kaplan-Meier cumulative distribution functions accounting for non-detections using lifelines; perform two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for Lupus and combined Lupus+Ophiuchus samples.  
  > "In Figure [5](#f:cum_single), we show cumulative distribution functions of disc mass, accounting for non-detections using the python package lifelines (cite: 2019JOSS....4.1317D), a Kaplan-Meier (KM) product estimator, as in (cite: Zurlo2020)" (Results); "The dust radii are measured from the 1.33 mm continuum images using a curve-of-growth method, in which successively larger photometric elliptical apertures are applied until the measured flux is 90% of the total flux." (Results); "A Kolmogorov-Smirnov test comparing the distributions of singles versus multiples gives a p-value of 40% for Lupus and 51% for Lupus and Ophiuchus combined." (Discussion)
   - software: lifelines
   - parameter: Disc radius defined as aperture containing 90% of total 1.33 mm continuum flux  
  > "The dust radii are measured from the 1.33 mm continuum images using a curve-of-growth method, in which successively larger photometric elliptical apertures are applied until the measured flux is 90% of the total flux." (Results)
   - fitting: Kaplan-Meier product estimator for survival analysis of censored data  
  > "accounting for non-detections using the python package lifelines (cite: 2019JOSS....4.1317D), a Kaplan-Meier (KM) product estimator" (Results)
   - validation: Kolmogorov-Smirnov test comparing single and multiple distributions  
  > "A Kolmogorov-Smirnov test comparing the distributions of singles versus multiples gives a p-value of 40% for Lupus and 51% for Lupus and Ophiuchus combined." (Discussion)

(Discussion); "Using the data from both star-forming regions, we obtain the radial profiles for sources where the peak SNR is $>$ 10$\times$rms and the imfit gaussian coincides with the signal peak, thereby excluding transition discs as well as poorly resolved sources." (Discussion)

   - parameter: peak SNR > 10 x rms; imfit Gaussian coincides with signal peak; 147 radial profiles total  
  > "we obtain the radial profiles for sources where the peak SNR is $>$ 10$\times$rms and the imfit gaussian coincides with the signal peak, thereby excluding transition discs as well as poorly resolved sources. The figure includes 147 radial profiles between single and binary stars for both samples." (Discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- The most massive (M_dust > 50 M_earth) and largest (R_dust > 70 au) discs are only seen around stars lacking visual companions (20-4800 au), while for M_dust < 50 M_earth and R_dust < 70 au (~80-90% of the sample) the mass and size distributions of single and multiple systems are nearly identical.  
  > "we find that the most massive (M$_{\rm dust}$ $>$ 50 M$_{\oplus}$) and largest ($R_{\rm dust}>$ 70 au) discs are only seen around stars lacking visual companions (with separations of 20–4800 au)" (Abstract); "the distribution of disc masses and sizes are similar for single and multiple systems for M$_{\rm dust} < 50$ M$_{\oplus}$ and radii $R_{\rm dust}<$ 70 au. Such discs correspond to $\sim$80–90% of the sample." (Abstract)
- Discs around primary stars are systematically more massive than discs around secondary stars.  
  > "Discs around the primary stars are indeed systematically more massive than the ones around the secondary stars." (Discussion)
- Normalized 1.3 mm brightness profiles show that discs in multiple systems are much more compact than discs around single stars, suggesting different surface density profiles.  
  > "Discs in multiple systems are clearly more compact than single star discs." (Discussion); "Discs around stars in multiple systems have flux distributions that tend to be much more compact than those seen for single stars. This suggests significant differences in their surface density profiles." (Summary and conclusions)
- Sz 68 is discovered to be a triple system rather than a binary, with a close companion Sz 68B at 17 au separation.  
  > "Two new binaries are found, while Sz 68 is discovered to be a triple system, not a binary as presented in (cite: Reipurth1993)." (Archival data)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Lupus Class II and Class III YSOs already had complete ALMA millimetre continuum coverage constraining disc masses and sizes; VLT/NACO L' star-hopping AO observations provided a fast way to complete the visual multiplicity census down to ~20 au and combine with the identical NIR-ODISEA Ophiuchus survey.  
  > "All 105 targets have millimetre ALMA data available, which provide constraints on disc masses and sizes." (Abstract); "In short, we used very quick (0.16–0.17 s) $L^{\prime}$ band filter exposures with the “star-hopping” technique to minimize overheads and maximize the number of observed objects." (VLT/NACO)
- Why this method: Kaplan-Meier estimator via lifelines accounts for ALMA continuum non-detections (censored data); deprojected 1.3 mm brightness profiles serve as a proxy for radial mass distribution without requiring full radiative transfer modeling.  
  > "we show cumulative distribution functions of disc mass, accounting for non-detections using the python package lifelines (cite: 2019JOSS....4.1317D), a Kaplan-Meier (KM) product estimator" (Results); "Deriving accurate surface density profiles requires radiative transfer modeling, which is outside the scope of this paper. However, at 1.3 mm the brightness profile is well correlated with the distribution of mass as a function of radius." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Radiative transfer modeling to derive accurate surface density profiles was considered outside the scope of the paper, using 1.3 mm brightness profiles instead.  
  > "Deriving accurate surface density profiles requires radiative transfer modeling, which is outside the scope of this paper. However, at 1.3 mm the brightness profile is well correlated with the distribution of mass as a function of radius." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Candidate secondary sources detected only in ALMA continuum (Sz 88B?, J16070384-3911113B?, J16073773-3921388B?) were excluded from the multiplicity statistics because they lack NIR counterparts.  
  > "The candidate secondary sources reported in (cite: Ansdell2018) but detected only in the ALMA data are not considered as multiple systems in this analysis, as they do not have NIR counterparts." (ALMA data)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Binaries with projected separations < 20 au (0.1 arcsec AO diffraction limit in L' band at 150 pc) and spectroscopic binaries are not resolved, and companions wider than ~4800 au fall outside the NACO field of view.  
  > "The minimum separation probed by our observations is related to the AO diffraction limit of the instrument in $L^{\prime}$ band. This is 0.1$''$, i.e. $\sim$18 au at 150 pc. Binaries with separations closer than 20 au would not be resolved in this study." (VLT/NACO); "Our study is sensitive to visual binaries with projected separations between 20–4800 au —spectroscopic, tight, and large separation binaries are not included in our statistics." (Summary and conclusions)

- Limitation (authors): The 12 M_Jup planetary-mass companion candidate around 2MASS J16095628-3859518 has a 16.4% (~17%) background probability and requires a second epoch to confirm common proper motion.  
  > "We estimate the probability of this object being a background star to be $\sim$17%, hence a second-epoch observation is needed to rule out this possibility." (Results)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Combining Class II (Band 6, 0.25 arcsec) and Class III (Band 7, 0.7 arcsec) ALMA surveys mixes different wavelengths and angular resolutions.
