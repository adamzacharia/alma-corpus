## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct continuum-subtracted line cubes**: Combine three configurations with mosaic gridder in tclean; identify and subtract line-free continuum channels; self-calibrate, image and clean.  
  > "The observations taken in the three configurations were combined using the ‘mosaic’ gridder in *tclean* within the Common Astronomy Software Applications package (CASA)." (Observations and data reduction); "The line-free continuum channels have been identified and subtracted and the data have been self-calibrated, imaged and cleaned applying robust weighting (robust 0.5), obtaining a clean beam of $\sim 0.45\ensuremath{''}$ for the lines." (Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust 0.5  
  > "applying robust weighting (robust 0.5)" (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Final channel widths: H2CO 218222.192 MHz 0.17 km s^-1; D2CO 231410.234 MHz 0.16 km s^-1; D2CO 233650.441 MHz 0.63 km s^-1  
  > "| p-H$_2$CO | $3_{0,3}-2_{0,2}$ | 218222.192 | 21           | $0.52\times0.42$ ($-87.3\ensuremath{^\circ}$)                    | 0.17          | 2.5" (Table 1); "| o-D$_2$CO | $4_{0,4}-3_{0,3}$ | 231410.234 | 28           | $0.49\times0.40$ ($-86.6\ensuremath{^\circ}$)                    | 0.16          | 2.5" (Table 1); "| o-D$_2$CO | $4_{2,3}-3_{2,2}$ | 233650.441 | 50           | $0.48\times0.39$ ($-86.1\ensuremath{^\circ}$)                    | 0.63          | 1.0" (Table 1)
   - parameter: Final channel widths: HDCO 246924.600 MHz 1.19 km s^-1; HDCO 259034.910 MHz 0.14 km s^-1  
  > "| HDCO      | $4_{1,4}-3_{1,3}$ | 246924.600 | 38           | $0.53\times0.38$ ($-84.9\ensuremath{^\circ}$)                    | 1.19          | 1.0" (Table 1); "| HDCO      | $4_{2,2}-3_{2,1}$ | 259034.910 | 63           | $0.50\times0.37$ ($-84.5\ensuremath{^\circ}$)                    | 0.14          | 3.5" (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum disk properties**: 2D Gaussian fit to the 1.3 mm continuum for peak coordinates, peak intensity, integrated flux density, position angle and inclination.  
  > "A 2D Gaussian fit is performed to estimate the disk parameters:" (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm: the dusty disk); "peak intensity ($F_{\rm peak} = 112.7 \pm 0.4$ mJy/beam), and integrated flux density at 1.3 mm ($293 \pm 1$ mJy), disk position angle and inclination (PA$_{\rm disk} = 149 \pm 1 \ensuremath{^\circ}$, $i=47\pm1 \ensuremath{^\circ}$)." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm: the dusty disk)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit  
  > "A 2D Gaussian fit is performed to estimate the disk parameters:" (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm: the dusty disk)
   - validation: Agreement with parameters from higher resolution continuum maps (cite: segura-cox20).  
  > "The values are in agreement with those estimated by (cite: segura-cox20) from their higher resolution continuum maps." (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm: the dusty disk)
3. **Map disk, streamer and outflow emission**: Make integrated intensity (moment 0) and intensity-weighted velocity (moment 1) maps; inspect channel maps and compare molecular distributions.  
  > "Finally, integrated intensity maps (moment 0) and intensity-weighted velocity maps (moment 1) are obtained by" (Observations and data reduction); "applying a $2\sigma$ clipping" (Observations and data reduction); "The channel maps in Fig. [5](#fig:h2co_streamer_channels) show that the spatial distribution of the H$_2$CO emission in the disk, in each velocity channel, is not symmetric with respect to the disk major axis," (The streamer feeding the IRS 63 disk)
   - parameter: 2σ clipping for moment maps  
  > "applying a $2\sigma$ clipping" (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Blue-shifted streamer integration: V_LSR = [+2.0, +2.6] km s^-1  
  > "line emission integrated on the blue-shifted velocity range where the streamer emits, i.e. $V_{\rm LSR} =[+2.0, +2.6]$ km s$^{-1}$." (The streamer feeding the IRS 63 disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: HDCO and D2CO outflow velocity gradient consistent with the previously identified 12CO outflow.  
  > "The moment 1 map shows that the velocity gradient is consistent with that of the $^{12}$CO outflow, with blue-shifted emission towards the South-West and red-shifted towards the North-East." (H2CO, HDCO and D2CO in the disk and outflow)
4. **Extract spectra and assess line emission**: Integrate line cubes over the entire disk and separately over SE and NW regions where HDCO is detected; recover faint lines and assess common emitting layers and optical depth.  
  > "the spectra of H$_2$CO, HDCO, and D$_2$CO transitions integrated on the entire disk (out to a radius of $\sim 1.3\ensuremath{''}$, i.e. 187 au), on the SE and NW disk side." (Integrated spectra); "integrating the line cubes on the SE and NW disk sides, on a $0.35\ensuremath{''}$ area (corresponding to $\sim 1.4$ beams) where HDCO emission is detected." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation); "The low $T_{\rm mb}$ values in the spectra ($<2$ K) indicates that the lines are optically thin." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - parameter: Entire-disk radius ~1.3″, 187 au  
  > "out to a radius of $\sim 1.3\ensuremath{''}$, i.e. 187 au" (Integrated spectra)
   - parameter: SE and NW extraction: 0.35″ area, ~1.4 beams  
  > "on a $0.35\ensuremath{''}$ area (corresponding to $\sim 1.4$ beams) where HDCO emission is detected." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - assumption: LTE: gas density larger than the line critical density  
  > "If we assume that the lines are in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), i.e. the gas density is larger than the lines critical density, which is very likely in the disk," (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: HDCO 259.0 GHz detected at >2σ in both disk sides; D2CO detected only in the lower excitation line.  
  > "the fainter, higher excitation, HDCO line at 259.0 GHz is detected at $>2\sigma$ both in the SE and in the NW disk side," (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation); "while D$_2$CO is detected only in the lower excitation line at $E_{\rm up}=28$ K." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
5. **Estimate excitation temperatures**: Use the ratio of integrated intensities of the two HDCO lines to estimate excitation temperature separately in the SE and NW disk.  
  > "First, the ratio of the integrated intensities of the two HDCO lines is used to estimate the excitation temperature." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - parameter: HDCO upper level energies: 38 K and 63 K  
  > "the range of upper level energies covered by the lines is small ($E_{\rm up}=38$ K and $63$ K)" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin emission  
  > "The molecular column densities and gas temperature are obtained in two steps, under the assumption of LTE and optically thin emission." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive molecular column-density maps**: Obtain H2CO, HDCO and D2CO column densities pixel by pixel from integrated-intensity maps.  
  > "obtain their column density, $N$, pixel by pixel from the line integrated intensities (moment 0) maps" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - software: CDMS
   - parameter: Average excitation temperature 45 K  
  > "at an average excitation temperature of $45$ K" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - parameter: CDMS partition functions; statistical ortho-to-para ratios 3 for H2CO and 2 for D2CO  
  > "Partition functions are taken from the CDMS (cite: Mueller2005) and takes into account a statistical ortho-to-para ratio of 3 for H$_2$CO, and 2 for D$_2$CO." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin emission  
  > "The molecular column densities and gas temperature are obtained in two steps, under the assumption of LTE and optically thin emission." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - assumption: H2CO, HDCO and D2CO emission originates from the same disk layer  
  > "Second, we assume that the H$_2$CO, HDCO, and D$_2$CO emission originates from the same disk layer" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - assumption: Distance 144 pc  
  > "the Class I source IRS 63, located in the nearby Ophiuchus star forming region at a distance of 144 pc (cite: ortiz-leon18a)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Common emitting layer supported by co-spatial moment 0 emission and the same velocity range in integrated spectra.  
  > "as indicated by the fact that they are co-spatial in the moment 0 maps and emit on the same velocity range in the integrated spectra" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
7. **Calculate abundance ratios and deuterium fractionation**: Divide column-density maps to obtain [HDCO]/[H2CO], [D2CO]/[H2CO] and [D2CO]/[HDCO]; convert isotopologue ratios to [D]/[H] using deuterium arrangements.  
  > "From the obtained column densities maps, we estimate the abundance ratios maps" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation); "by taking into account the number of arrangements of the deuterium atoms (cite: manigand19)," (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - parameter: [D]/[H] = 0.5 × [HDCO]/[H2CO]; [D]/[H] = sqrt([D2CO]/[H2CO])  
  > "Therefore, \[D\]/\[H\] = 0.5 $\times$ \[HDCO\]/\[H$_2$CO\], and \[D\]/\[H\] $= \sqrt{\rm [D_2CO]/[H_2CO]}$." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare abundance ratios with prestellar cores, Class 0 and I protostars, a disk upper limit and the L1157-B1 shock.  
  > "prestellar cores (cite: chacon-tanarro19; bacmann03), in Class 0 (cite: parise06; watanabe12; persson18; manigand20; evans23) and Class I (cite: bianchi17a; mercimek22) protostars," (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation); "the only available upper limit obtained in a disk (Oph IRS48, (cite: vandermarel21b; brunken22))." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation); "that estimated in the L1157-B1 protostellar shock ($\sim 0.1$, (cite: fontani14b))." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
8. **Interpret chemical formation routes**: Compare spatial distributions and abundance ratios with gas-phase and grain-surface formation routes.  
  > "we examine the different routes for the formation of HDCO and D$_2$CO, either in gas-phase or on the grains," (D2CO in the accretion and outflow shocks); "use the different spatial distribution of these molecules to put constraints on their chemical origin and to verify the above proposed scenario of D$_2$CO-rich ices released in shocked regions." (D2CO in the accretion and outflow shocks)
   - assumption: D2CO-rich grain mantles released in accretion and outflow shocks  
  > "The observed spatial distribution and abundance of D$_2$CO could be due to the release of D$_2$CO from the icy mantles of grains in the accretion shock" (D2CO in the accretion and outflow shocks); "as well as in the shocks along the outflow." (D2CO in the accretion and outflow shocks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Gas-phase model [HDCO]/[H2CO] up to 0.2 comparable to observations; gas-phase reactions unable to reproduce [D2CO]/[HDCO] up to ~0.7.  
  > "Theoretical models predict \[HDCO\]/\[H$_2$CO\] ratios up to 0.2 (cite: Bergman2011-d2co; Roueff2013-CH2D+), comparable to the values observed in the disk." (HDCO and D2CO chemistry); "On the other hand, gas-phase reactions are unable to reproduce the large \[D$_2$CO\]/\[HDCO\] ratio inferred in the region where the streamer falls onto the disk (up to $\sim 0.7$)." (HDCO and D2CO chemistry)

## Qualitative findings

- HDCO follows H2CO in the disk and streamer, whereas D2CO emission is concentrated in the SE disk and outflow spots.  
  > "The spatial distribution of H$_2$CO and HDCO in the disk and in the streamer is similar," (Conclusions); "while D$_2$CO is detected only: (i) in the South-East disk side, where the streamer impacts on the disk, as well as (ii) in two spots along the outflow." (Conclusions)
- H2CO disk emission is asymmetric with respect to the major axis; the streamer perturbation is the proposed cause.  
  > "the spatial distribution of the H$_2$CO emission in the disk, in each velocity channel, is not symmetric with respect to the disk major axis," (The streamer feeding the IRS 63 disk); "This asymmetry is likely produced by the perturbation induced by the streamer as it approaches the disk." (The streamer feeding the IRS 63 disk)
- H2CO and HDCO trace material at least partially reprocessed in the disk and streamer; D2CO traces low-temperature grain-surface chemistry.  
  > "Therefore, both H$_2$CO and HDCO probe material which is (at least partially) reprocessed in the disk and in the streamer;" (HDCO and D2CO chemistry); "probes the composition of the ices, which is set by grain surface chemistry occurring at low temperatures, i.e. at the prestellar stage and in the disk midplane." (HDCO and D2CO chemistry)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: FAUST multiple configurations probe chemistry from thousands of au to 50 au; deuterated molecules probe the streamer's impact on disk chemistry.  
  > "using multiple antennas configurations which allows us to probe the chemistry from large (thousands of au) to small (50 au) scales (cite: codella21)." (Introduction); "Hence, they are ideal to investigate what is the impact, if any, of the large scale streamer" (Introduction); "on the disk chemical content at the time of planet formation." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Integrate SE and NW spectra to check a common emitting layer and recover faint higher-excitation lines.  
  > "This allows us to verify that the detected H$_2$CO 218.2 GHz, HDCO 246.9 GHz, and D$_2$CO 231.4 GHz lines originate from the same disk layer," (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation); "and to recover the emission in the fainter HDCO 259.0 GHz and D$_2$CO 233.7 GHz lines (undetected in the channel and moment 0 maps)." (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
- Alternative: Gas-phase reactions rejected as sufficient explanation for [D2CO]/[HDCO] up to ~0.7 at the streamer-disk interface.  
  > "On the other hand, gas-phase reactions are unable to reproduce the large \[D$_2$CO\]/\[HDCO\] ratio inferred in the region where the streamer falls onto the disk (up to $\sim 0.7$)." (HDCO and D2CO chemistry)
- Alternative: D2CO-rich ices may originate in the cold prestellar phase or form in the cold disk midplane.  
  > "The above described enhancement of D$_2$CO on the ices, may have occurred in the cold prestellar phase," (HDCO and D2CO chemistry); "On the other hand, the same grain chemistry may also occur in the cold disk midplane." (HDCO and D2CO chemistry)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Detailed physical, kinematical and chemical analysis of the streamer deferred to Pineda et al. (in prep).  
  > "A detailed analysis of the physical, kinematical, and chemical properties of the streamer is beyond the scope of this letter and will be presented in a paper by Pineda et al. (in prep)." (The streamer feeding the IRS 63 disk)
- Limitation (authors): Small upper-level-energy range for the two HDCO temperature diagnostics.  
  > "the range of upper level energies covered by the lines is small ($E_{\rm up}=38$ K and $63$ K)" (Molecular column density and deuterium fractionation)
- Limitation (authors): Higher-excitation HDCO and D2CO lines absent from 2σ-clipped moment maps; higher-excitation D2CO also absent from integrated spectra.  
  > "The higher excitation HDCO 259.0 GHz and D$_2$CO 233.6 GHz lines ($E_{\rm up} \sim 63$ K and $50$ K, respectively) are fainter and no emission is detected in the moment 0 maps obtained applying a $2\sigma$ threshold." (H2CO, HDCO and D2CO in the disk and outflow); "while no emission is seen in the higher excitation D$_2$CO line at 233.7 GHz" (H2CO, HDCO and D2CO in the disk and outflow)
- Limitation (authors): H2CO absorption near systemic velocity and negative inner-disk fluxes complicate interpretation.  
  > "A narrow stripe with no emission is observed along the minor axis due to absorption of the line emission by H$_2$CO molecules in the surrounding envelope at velocities close to systemic" (H2CO, HDCO and D2CO in the disk and outflow); "negative fluxes in the inner $\sim 20$ au disk region, in the channels close to systemic velocity (between 2.15 and 3.15 km s$^{-1}$), due to optically thick continuum emission and absorption by molecules along the line of sight." (H2CO, HDCO and D2CO in the disk and outflow)
- Limitation (authors): FAUST continuum resolution does not resolve the previously identified rings and gaps.  
  > "The continuum emission shows a smooth distribution, with no signatures of the rings and gaps first identified by (cite: segura-cox20), due to the $\sim 10$ times lower angular resolution" (Continuum emission at 1.3 mm: the dusty disk)
- Limitation (authors): Quantitative grain-surface reaction scheme remains incomplete.  
  > "leaving the whole quantitatively reaction scheme incomplete." (HDCO and D2CO chemistry)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Low brightness alone does not independently exclude beam dilution or establish optical thinness.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single 45 K excitation temperature is applied despite measured SE/NW temperature differences and emission extending into the streamer and outflow.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Different line beams and spectral resolutions could affect ratios unless matched or explicitly accounted for.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Clipping and faint-line significance may influence apparent asymmetry; NW D2CO is absent from clipped maps but contributes to integrated-spectrum estimates.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The gas-phase versus grain-surface interpretation is suggestive; no fitted chemical or shock model is presented.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The observations do not distinguish prestellar inheritance from D2CO formation in the disk midplane.
