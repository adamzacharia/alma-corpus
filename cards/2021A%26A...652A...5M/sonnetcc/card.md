## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and extract spectra toward SW and NE images**: CASA standard calibration, phase-only self-calibration on continuum, conversion topocentric to LSRK then heliocentric, spectra extracted with UVMultiFit using a two-point-source model  
  > "After the standard gain calibration using the quasar J 1832$-$2039, the phase solutions were further self-calibrated on the continuum of PKS 1830$-$211, applying phase-only gain corrections to every 6 s integration." (2 Observations); "The final spectra were extracted using the CASA-Python task UVMultiFit (cite: mar14), by fitting a model of two point sources (corresponding to the northeast and southwest core images of PKS 1830$-$211) to the interferometric visibilities." (2 Observations)
   - software: CASA, UVMultiFit
   - parameter: v_Helio = v_LSRK - 12.4 km/s  
  > "is $v_{\rm Helio} = v_{\rm LSRK} - 12.4$ km s$^{-1}$" (2 Observations)
   - parameter: Phase-only gain corrections every 6 s  
  > "applying phase-only gain corrections to every 6 s integration" (2 Observations)
   - assumption: Two point sources adequately describe the data; third image and weak extensions have negligible effect on the spectra  
  > "these have a negligible effect on the absorption spectra extracted with a two point-source model and have hence been ignored here" (2 Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Assess quasar continuum variability and flux ratios**: Continuum flux densities and ratios of the NE and SW images tracked across tunings; light curve reconstructed using a time delay and spectral index  
  > "We used the continuum data to infer the quasar activity during our ALMA campaign." (3.2.1 Quasar activity); "we used a time delay of $27$ days to reconstruct the intrinsic light curve of the quasar" (3.2.1 Quasar activity)
   - parameter: time delay 27 days; spectral index alpha close to -0.7  
  > "indicates that the spectral index $\alpha$ (defined by the relationship $F=F_0 \times (\nu/\nu_0)^{\alpha}$) is close to $-0.7$" (3.2.1 Quasar activity)
   - assumption: Light curve is smooth after the start of the observations  
  > "we thus assume that the light curve is smooth after the start of our observations" (3.2.1 Quasar activity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit a common two-Gaussian opacity profile to all methanol lines**: Joint fit of all transitions with a sum of two Gaussian velocity components shared by all lines, with per-tuning covering factor f_c, per-line opacity scaling gamma_i and per-line bulk velocity v_i; 13CH3OH opacities tied to 12CH3OH  
  > "We chose to model the common line profile as a sum of Gaussian velocity components $G_{ki}$ with velocity centroids $v_k$, FWHM $\Delta v_k$, and integrated opacities $\int \tau dv_k$" (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile); "However, we allowed $f_c$ to vary between the different tunings to account for potential variations with the observing epoch and frequency." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
   - software: Python scipy.optimize
   - parameter: Reference line: ground-state A-CH3OH (350.905 GHz) with gamma = 1  
  > "We chose the ground-state transition of A-CH$_3$OH (rest frequency 350.905 GHz, redshifted into band 5) as a reference for the normalization for the line profile ($\gamma=1$)" (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
   - assumption: Constant f_c as a function of velocity within a tuning  
  > "we adopted a constant $f_c$ as function of velocity because the temporal variations of the absorption profile have a small amplitude" (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
   - assumption: Errors Gaussian, parameters uncorrelated, model a good representation of the data  
  > "Hence, we assume that errors follow a Gaussian distribution, that the parameters are not correlated, and that the model is a good representation of the data." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
   - fitting: Least-squares (Levenberg-Marquardt); uncertainties from covariance matrix diagonal scaled by reduced chi2  
  > "For the fit, we used the Python scipy.optimize least-squares routine based on the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile); "The uncertainties are derived as the square root of the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix multiplied by the reduced $\chi^2$." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
   - validation: Residuals below 1% of continuum; reduced chi2 of 2.0 for 50 free parameters; changing the reference line did not change f_c and profile significantly  
  > "(reduced $\chi^2$ of 2.0 for 50 free parameters)" (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile); "We checked that changing the reference line to another with comparable S/N did not change significantly the resulting covering factors and characteristics of the line profile." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
4. **Constrain methanol excitation with a RADEX non-LTE grid**: RADEX models on a grid of n_H2 and Tkin (also G1 column density and A/E), compared to the ALMA spectra via chi2 summed over tunings; two Gaussian components treated as independent  
  > "We adopted a uniform sphere geometry and took the two-Gaussian line profile (i.e., fixing their velocity centroids, line widths, and integrated opacity ratios)" (5 Excitation of methanol lines); "For each model, we constructed the RADEX methanol spectrum and calculated the resulting $\chi^2$, summing over the different tunings $i$ as" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Grid: n_H2 1e3-1e6 cm^-3, Tkin 6-100 K  
  > "We explored a density range between $10^3$ and $10^6$ cm$^{-3}$ (in logarithmic steps) and kinetic temperatures between 6 and 100 K" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
   - parameter: Two B4 visits averaged, average f_c adopted; 13CH3OH modeled with ratio 62 but not in the chi2  
  > "For this exercise, the two visits of the B4 tuning were averaged, and we took their average $f_c$." (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
   - assumption: Para-H2 is the collisional partner; no additional heating by ambient radiation field or cosmic rays; T_CMB = 5.14 K  
  > "Para-H$_2$ is assumed to be the main collisional partner, since it is expected to be the dominant form at low temperatures ($T < 100$ K)." (5 Excitation of methanol lines); "we did not consider additional heating, either by the ambient radiative field or cosmic rays" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
   - fitting: Grid chi2 minimization; 1-sigma contour defined at Min(chi2_r)+2.30  
  > "The equivalent $1\sigma$ contour, defined at a value Min($\chi^2_r)+2.30$" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
   - validation: Good match for A-CH3OH; E-CH3OH lines (except ground state) underestimated; removing 3_0-2_-1 and 4_0-3_-1 E lowers chi2_r from 13 to 9  
  > "for most lines of E-CH$_3$OH (except the ground state transition), the RADEX spectra underestimate the absorption depths" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
5. **Derive A/E ratio, methanol abundance and 12C/13C ratio**: A/E from the RADEX grid; total column from summing A and E over G1 and G2 divided by an H2 proxy; 12CH3OH/13CH3OH from the tied opacity ratio in the joint profile fit  
  > "By adding the column densities of A- and E-CH$_3$OH over the two velocity components $G_1$ and $G_2$ , we estimate a total methanol column density of $\sim 5 \times 10^{14}$ cm$^{-2}$ along the southwest line of sight." (5.2 Methanol relative abundance); "we tied the opacities of the $^{13}$CH$_3$OH lines to those corresponding to the $^{12}$C- main isotopologue, considering only the two brightest ones" (5.3 The 12CH3OH/13CH3OH ratio)
   - software: RADEX
   - assumption: H2 column density 2e22 cm^-2 from 2012 CH spectra used as a proxy, despite profile variations between 2012 and 2019  
  > "We further take the H$_2$ column density of $2 \times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$, estimated by (cite: mul14a) using CH spectra (obtained in 2012), as an H$_2$ proxy." (5.2 Methanol relative abundance)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit multivariate regression of line velocities for delta mu / mu**: Weighted linear regression v_i/c = (dmu/mu)*K_mu + Gamma_t*t + Gamma_E*Elow + Gamma_0, with several variable combinations; rest-frequency uncertainties added quadratically  
  > "weighted by the inverse of their squared uncertainties (to which we quadratically added the rest frequency uncertainties)" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics); "with different variable combinations for a multivariate linear regression analysis of the following type" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
   - parameter: K_mu values from Jansen et al. 2011a; frequency and K_mu not used as simultaneous variables (Pearson r ~ 0.8)  
  > "We did not consider the frequency and $K_\mu$ as simultaneous variables, because there is a clear correlation between them, with a Pearson coefficient $r\sim0.8$" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
   - assumption: Gaussian errors; 3-sigma upper limit taken as three times the 1-sigma uncertainty  
  > "Assuming a Gaussian distribution of errors" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
   - fitting: Weighted linear regression (inverse squared uncertainties); lowest chi2_r for the K_mu + t + Elow model  
  > "The final multivariate regression with $K_\mu$, observing epoch, and $E_{low}$ yields the lowest $\chi^2_r$ value" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
   - validation: Fit quality improves drastically once a time term is allowed (chi2_r from >24 to <6); other time functions tested without drastic improvement  
  > "Our first obvious result is that the fit quality drastically improves when a linear dependence with observing epoch is allowed" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics); "We have further tested different time functions, such as quadratic polynomial and step functions" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
7. **Test chromatic and A/E systematics**: Replace K_mu with frequency in the regression; estimate f_c vs frequency and an upper limit on core shift; repeat regression separating A- and E-CH3OH  
  > "we did not find a significant correlation between $v_i$ and frequencies, showing that the chromatic effects from the background blazar have little impact on our constraint on $\Delta \mu$/$\mu$" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics); "we did not see any significant differences in the resulting $\Gamma$ coefficients between the two types" (6.5 Is there a difference between A/E methanol types?)
   - parameter: Core shift Omega ~0.8 mas GHz (from Marti-Vidal 2013) gives max core shift ~2 microarcsec across the campaign  
  > "we obtain a maximum core shift of $\sim 2$ $\mu$as" (6.3 Chromatic structure of the quasar)
   - assumption: Absorbing gas has constant solid angle so 1/f_c traces continuum solid angle  
  > "if we assume that the absorbing gas has a constant solid angle, then the inverse of $f_c$ gives the solid angle subtended by the continuum emission" (6.3 Chromatic structure of the quasar)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Upper limit on methanol column density toward the northeast image**: Stack A- and E-CH3OH lines with Elow < 30 K (weights = inverse squared rms), derive integrated opacity upper limit, convert to column density under LTE with T_ex = T_CMB  
  > "First, we determined the upper limit on the integrated opacity as $3\sigma_{\tau}\sqrt{\delta v \Delta v}$, where we assumed a line width $\Delta v = 20$ km s$^{-1}$ for the absorption near $v=-150$ km s$^{-1}$" (3.3 Non-detection of methanol on the northeast line of sight); "we used the squared inverse of the rms noise levels" (3.3 Non-detection of methanol on the northeast line of sight)
   - parameter: alpha = 2.2e13 cm^-2 km^-1 s for the ground-state A-CH3OH line; A/E = 1  
  > "we calculated a conversion factor $\alpha = 2.2 \times 10^{13}$ cm$^{-2}$ km$^{-1}$ s between integrated opacity and column density" (3.3 Non-detection of methanol on the northeast line of sight)
   - assumption: LTE with Tex = TCMB; line width 20 km/s  
  > "under conditions of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)" (3.3 Non-detection of methanol on the northeast line of sight)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Methanol absorption detected only toward the southwest image; undetected toward the northeast  
  > "Methanol remains undetected along this line of sight" (3.3 Non-detection of methanol on the northeast line of sight)
- Temporal variation of the absorption profile (quasar morphology changes) is the dominant systematic for mu tests  
  > "Therefore, temporal variations of the absorption profile are the dominant systematic effect in our data." (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
- No lines detected in emission, so no inversion or masing in the measured transitions  
  > "None of the methanol lines were detected in emission, implying an absence of inversion and maser effects in all measured transitions." (3.1 Description of the spectra)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: All observations requested within the shortest possible time interval, with a repeated B4 tuning, to address time variability of PKS 1830-211 as a systematic  
  > "For this reason, we requested all observations be performed within the shortest possible time interval, depending on the dynamical scheduling constraints of ALMA." (3.2 Time variability of the quasar and of the absorption lines)
- Why this method: Velocity centroids and cross-correlation are unsuitable because of asymmetric profiles and optically thick lines, so a common optical-depth profile is fit to all lines  
  > "This is why we turned to a fit of a single common absorption profile, in terms of optical depths, to all methanol transitions toward the southwest image of PKS 1830$-$211." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
- Alternative: Covering factor fixed to 1 for all spectra was tried first and rejected because the deepest absorption was underestimated  
  > "We started our fitting exercise by forcing the background source covering factor (see Appendix [9](#app:fc)) $\hbox{$f_c$}= 1$ for all spectra" (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
- Alternative: Not combined with previously published low-frequency methanol data (12.2 GHz line) to remain conservative  
  > "chose not to combine our results with previously published low-frequency methanol data" (6 Invariance of mu and systematics)
- Alternative: Rabli and Flower collisional rates tried but not adopted because of inconsistencies in the A-CH3OH LAMDA file  
  > "we continued to use the (cite: pot04) rates for both A- and E-CH$_3$OH" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
- How they decided it was enough: Two-Gaussian model kept because residuals were small; no additional components added  
  > "We therefore refrained from invoking a higher degree of complexity for the model by adding more velocity components." (4 Construction of a global line opacity profile)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in the four-dimensional RADEX parameter space are not straightforward to define  
  > "It is not straightforward to define uncertainties in the four-dimensional parameter space." (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
- Limitation (authors): RADEX underestimates absorption depths for most E-CH3OH lines  
  > "for most lines of E-CH$_3$OH (except the ground state transition), the RADEX spectra underestimate the absorption depths" (5 Excitation of methanol lines)
- Limitation (authors): Methanol abundance is a rough estimate because H2 column is from 2012 and profile varied  
  > "We note that this is a rough estimate, because there were some variations in the line profile between 2012 and 2019" (5.2 Methanol relative abundance)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only three observing epochs for a time-dependent regression term; linear time dependence is assumed and cannot be well tested
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Covering factor held constant across velocity although the paper itself sees evidence it varies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Delta mu / mu uncertainty from a Gaussian-error 3x multiple; chi2_r of 3.5 means errors are likely underestimated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-component RADEX model applied to a multiphase absorber; the 2012 H2 proxy is not simultaneous
