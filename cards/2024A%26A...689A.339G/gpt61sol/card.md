## Analysis recipe

1. **Detect and measure continuum sources**: Select beam-sized or larger structures above 5σ; fit 2D Gaussians for positions, sizes, intensities, fluxes, and uncertainties.  
  > "We consider structures with sizes equal to or larger than the beam size and flux densities greater than 5$\sigma\sim$0.3 mJy beam$^{-1}$ as criteria for identifying continuum sources" (Millimeter Continuum Sources); "These parameters, and their uncertainties were estimated fitting 2D-Gaussians by using the CASA task `imfit`." (Millimeter Continuum Sources)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: 5σ ∼0.3 mJy beam−1; source sizes at least the beam size  
  > "We consider structures with sizes equal to or larger than the beam size and flux densities greater than 5$\sigma\sim$0.3 mJy beam$^{-1}$ as criteria for identifying continuum sources" (Millimeter Continuum Sources)
   - fitting: 2D-Gaussian fitting with imfit  
  > "These parameters, and their uncertainties were estimated fitting 2D-Gaussians by using the CASA task `imfit`." (Millimeter Continuum Sources)
   - validation: Estimate σ from empty background emission  
  > "here $\sigma$ is estimated analyzing the empty background emission" (Millimeter Continuum Sources)
2. **Catalog CO condensations**: Inspect the cube manually channel by channel; select compact CO condensations and record positions and radial velocities with cgcurs.  
  > "Condensations were identified after careful manual inspection of the velocity cube on a channel-by-channel basis." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics); "The position and the radial velocity of each condensations, were obtained using the Miriad’s task `cgcurs` (cite: 1995_Sault) and added to a list of CO condensations." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
   - software: Miriad, cgcurs
   - parameter: CO emission above 20 mJy beam−1 (4σ); sizes similar to the beam minor axis  
  > "we use structures with CO emission above 20 mJy beam$^{-1}$(emission over 4$\sigma$ threshold), and sizes similar to beam’s minor axis." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
   - parameter: Avoid cloud channels between −26 and +21 km s−1  
  > "In that specific velocity range (i.e., from $-26$ to $+21$ km s$^{-1}$) the cloud contamination hampers the identification of filaments." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Manual inspection; exclude channels affected by cloud emission and missing-flux artefacts  
  > "Condensations were identified after careful manual inspection of the velocity cube on a channel-by-channel basis."    - validation: Some streamers agree with earlier SMA detections

  > "We further prepared a 5-channel binned version of the CO cube with 6.3 km s$^{-1}$ channels, which we use to estimate the outflow mass and energetics" (ALMA observations)
   - parameter: Exclude central channels contaminated by cloud emission or missing extended emission  
  > "Again, we avoid the central channels with cloud contamination and/or missing extended emission." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium; optically thin CO  
  > "Considering local thermodynamic equilibrium and an optically thin CO emission, we estimate the mass, momentum and energy of the explosive outflow." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
   - assumption: 70 K and 140 K  
  > "The derived total mass ranges between 120 and 210 $M_{\odot}$ for 70 K and 140 K, respectively." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
   - assumption: Mean molecular weight 2.76; CO abundance XCO=10−4

  > "The derived total mass ranges between 120 and 210 $M_{\odot}$ for 70 K and 140 K, respectively." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics); "with values correct within a factor of 2-3." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
7. **Estimate the dynamical age**: Combine a literature outflow extent of 120″ with a speed of 100 km s−1 inferred from the maximum observed CO velocity.

(Explosive’s outflow dynamical time and rate of events in the Milky Way)
   - parameter: 100 km s−1; 120″ outflow size  
  > "We set a new dynamical time scale for the explosive outflow of 8600 years using an expansion velocity of 100 km s$^{-1}$ and a outflow diameter of 120$''$." (CONCLUSIONS)
   - assumption: All streamers have the same constant speed since ejection; maximum radial speed comes from a streamer closer to the line of sight  
  > "For this estimate we assume all streamers move at the same constant speed since their ejection." (Explosive’s outflow dynamical time and rate of events in the Milky Way); "We assume this would be produced in a streamer ejected in a direction closer to the line-of-sight." (Explosive’s outflow dynamical time and rate of events in the Milky Way)
   - assumption: Plasmon speed approximately preserved for large density contrast and/or limited mass loss  
  > "if the density contrast between the ambient and the bullet is large enough ($\rho_{ambient}/\rho_{bullet}\leq10^{-2}$), and/or the mass detached from the plasmon is not reduced in a large percentage, the plasmon preserves almost the same velocity since the ejection." (Explosive’s outflow dynamical time and rate of events in the Milky Way)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Agreement with the earlier kinematic age; interpret the new age as an upper limit

## Qualitative findings

- Streamers are quasi-radial, have roughly linear velocity gradients, and lack bipolar counterparts.  
  > "we found 18 quasi-radial collimated streamers that seem to emerge from a common center." (CONCLUSIONS); "The radial velocities along each streamer roughly follow linear gradients." (CONCLUSIONS); "Blue-shifted and red-shifted streamers are interspersed along similar directions and do not posses bipolar counterparts." (CONCLUSIONS)
- No detected continuum source coincides with the streamer origin.  
  > "The position of the center does not coincide with any of the continuum sources." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
- The new center lies inside the cometary HII region containing A, B and C, ∼1″ north of source C's edge.  
  > "This position lies within the expanding cometary HII region that contains the continuum sources A, B and C, just $\sim1\ensuremath{''}$ north of the edge of source C." (Estimation of the center of the explosive outflow)
- The main bipolar outflow may comprise multiple ejections in slightly different directions.  
  > "which suggest the presence of an outflow with multiple ejections in slightly different directions." (CONCLUSIONS)
- A newly identified outflow is associated with MM5; the five new compact cores probably trace disks and envelopes.  
  > "Finally, the CO ALMA emission revealed still a new outflow associated with one of the five newly detected millimeter cores, MM5." (CONCLUSIONS); "These five compact cores are probably associated with disks and envelopes of young stars." (CONCLUSIONS)
- The Galactic explosive-outflow rate is estimated to be of the same order as supernova and massive-star formation rates.  
  > "our conclusion should be that the rate of explosive is of the same order of both, the supernova rate and the rate of formation of massive stars in the Milky Way." (Explosive’s outflow dynamical time and rate of events in the Milky Way)

- Why this data: High angular resolution and sensitivity to study DR21 CO outflow emission

- Why this method: Moment-8, moment-9 and moment-2 maps emphasize filament morphology and velocity gradients  
  > "We choose these moment maps over the more classical moment-0 (integrated intensity) and moment-1 (intensity weighted velocity), because they better stress the filament morphology and velocity gradients of these structures." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)

  > "We choose these moment maps over the more classical moment-0 (integrated intensity) and moment-1 (intensity weighted velocity), because they better stress the filament morphology and velocity gradients of these structures." (Outflow Identification, Kinematics and Energetics)
