## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and combine ACA and 12m visibilities**: Standard ALMA pipeline in CASA 5.4, then ACA weights modified and ACA plus 12m data concatenated  
  > "The data were calibrated by the standard pipeline (CASA, version 5.4;" (2.2 Observations and data reduction); "The ACA and 12m-array observations were then concatenated to proceed with the imaging." (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: ACA visibility weight factor 2.4  
  > "We therefore applied a modified weight of $2.4$ to the ACA observations" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pipeline quality assessment outputs checked  
  > "The quality assessment outputs were checked, and the automatic calibration results are satisfactory." (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
2. **image continuum and o-H2D+ cubes**: tclean interactively with natural weighting and multiscale deconvolver; continuum from dedicated SPW plus line-free channels; 100-channel line cube; regrid to 3 pixels per beam; convert to brightness temperature  
  > "using a natural weighting and the multiscale deconvolver algorithm (cite: Cornwell08)" (2.2 Observations and data reduction); "The data-cubes hence present 100 channels in the frequency axis" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Continuum total bandwidth about 4.2 GHz  
  > "obtaining a total bandwidth of $\approx 4.2 \, \rm GHz$" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Cube spectral resolution 0.22 km/s  
  > "The data-cubes hence present 100 channels in the frequency axis" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: 3 pixels per beam minor axis  
  > "have been re-gridded in order to ensure 3 pixel per beam minor axis, in agreement with the Nyquist theorem" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Jy/beam to brightness temperature gain G = 11 mK/(mJy/beam) at beam 1x0.8 arcsec  
  > "The molecular line data have been converted into the brightness temperature" (2.2 Observations and data reduction); "computing the corresponding gain, $G= 11 \, \rm mK/ (mJy\, beam^{-1})$" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **identify cores in PPV space**: SCIMES (dendrogram leaves) on the o-H2D+ cubes before primary-beam correction; leaves treated as prestellar cores; 7 cores in AG351 and 9 in AG354  
  > "we use the Spectral Clustering for Interstellar Molecular Emission Segmentation package (SCIMES, (cite: Colombo15))" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES); "We therefore focus on the leaves, which in the context of this paper correspond to prestellar cores." (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
   - software: SCIMES
   - parameter: S/N_peak = 2; S/N_lim = 1.5 (AG351), 1.3 (AG354); min_val = 0  
  > "We therefore set $\rm S/N_{peak} = 2$, and $\rm S/N_{lim} = 1.5$ for AG351, and $\rm S/N_{peak} = 2$, and $\rm S/N_{lim} = 1.3$ for AG354." (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES); "we set the minimum value ($min_\mathrm{val}$) of the dendrogram algorithm to zero" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
   - parameter: Delta_min = 2.0 x rms with rms = 150 mK (before primary-beam correction)  
  > "In our case, we set $\Delta_{min} = 2.0 \times rms$." (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES); "Their average noise is hence $rms = 150\, \rm mK$." (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
   - parameter: Minimum channels per leaf = 2; leaves smaller than twice the beam area excluded  
  > "$N \rm^{min} _{chan} = 2$" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES); "Finally, we exclude leaves smaller than two times the beam area." (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Parameter choice from multiple tests with visual inspection  
  > "We performed multiple tests, followed by visual inspection of the results, to find the best choice for our" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
4. **fit o-H2D+ spectra pixel by pixel**: MCWEEDS: LTE synthetic spectra (WEEDS/GILDAS) with Bayesian MCMC (PyMC); free N_col, V_lsr, FWHM; T_ex fixed at 10 K; results masked where FWHM exceeds a per-core threshold  
  > "we perform a spectral fitting of the lines pixel-per-pixel using MCWEEDS (cite: Giannetti17)" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS); "The remaining free parameters in the fit are hence the molecular column density" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - software: MCWEEDS, WEEDS, GILDAS, PyMC, multiprocessing
   - parameter: 100000 iterations per spectrum, first 1000 steps burn-in  
  > "For each spectrum, the code performs 100000 iterations, and excludes the first 1000 steps (burn-in)" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - parameter: Noise rms 180 mK (AG351), 170 mK (AG354)  
  > "We adopt $rms = 180 \, \rm mK$ (AG351) and $rms = 170 \, \rm mK$ (AG354)" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - parameter: Fits with FWHM above a per-core threshold of 1-1.2 km/s discarded (<=3% of pixels)  
  > "we find that a successful masking strategy is to discard fits with" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS); "This threshold, which is chosen for each core individually, is in the range" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - assumption: T_ex = 10 K (single transition cannot constrain N_col and T_ex independently); LTE  
  > "they do not allow to constrain independently the molecular column density" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS); "we have assumed an excitation temperature of $T_\mathrm{ex}$$=10\, \rm K$" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - fitting: MCMC with uninformative flat priors, 95% HPD intervals; likelihood form not stated beyond a specified noise rms  
  > "We use a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm to sample the parameter space, using uninformative flat priors" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS); "In order to estimate correctly the parameters distributions, the noise level of the spectral line data has to be indicated." (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - validation: Visual inspection of best-fit models and check of chain stability and convergence  
  > "The best-fit models have been visually inspected, in order to assess their quality." (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS); "The stability and convergence of the chains was in any case checked." (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
5. **derive optical depth maps**: Optical depth computed from fitted N_col maps with the LTE formula using CDMS spectroscopic parameters  
  > "From the column density maps we have estimated the optical depth" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - software: CDMS
   - parameter: A_ul = 1.20e-8 s^-1, g_u = 9, E_u = 17.9 K, Q(10 K) = 10.48  
  > "where $A_\mathrm{ul} = 1.20\times 10^{-8} \, \rm s^{-1}$ is the Einstein coefficient for spontaneous emission" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS); "$E_\mathrm{u} = 17.9 \, \rm K$ is the upper level energy" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - assumption: T_ex = 10 K  
  > "we have assumed an excitation temperature of $T_\mathrm{ex}$$=10\, \rm K$" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **average spectra per core and decompose linewidth**: Spectra in each core aligned to the mean centroid velocity, averaged, refitted with MCWEEDS (T_ex = 10 K); non-thermal and thermal dispersion separated in quadrature to give 1D turbulent Mach number  
  > "we first aligned the spectra pixel-per-pixel to the mean centroid velocity obtained from the spectral fitting" (4.3 Average cores parameters); "The averaged spectra have then been re-analysed using MCWEEDS, as previously described" (4.3 Average cores parameters)
   - software: MCWEEDS
   - parameter: Mach number computed at T_gas = 10 K (sigma_th = 0.14 km/s); c_s = 0.19 km/s with mu = 2.33  
  > "assuming $T_\mathrm{gas}= 10 \, \rm K$" (4.3 Average cores parameters); "for a gas composition of H$_2$ and 10% of helium" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
   - assumption: Thermal and non-thermal components independent and sum in quadrature  
  > "In the assumption that the two components are independent, they sum in quadrature" (4.3 Average cores parameters)
   - fitting: Same MCWEEDS MCMC fit; 95% HPD intervals; rms from line-free channels  
  > "We have computed the $rms$ values of the average spectra considering line-free channels." (4.3 Average cores parameters)
   - validation: Average spectra overlaid with best-fit models; all show Gaussian line shapes  
  > "It is worth noting that all the spectra present Gaussian line shapes." (4.3 Average cores parameters)
7. **derive gas temperature upper limit from linewidth**: Set observed sigma_V equal to the thermal dispersion of H2D+ to get T_H2D+ per pixel; bounded to 5-20 K  
  > "This quantity represents an upper limit on the real gas temperature" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+); "are not predicted even at very high volume densities" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - parameter: Lower limit 5 K, upper limit 20 K (H2D+ abundance expected to drop above 20 K)  
  > "The upper limit is instead due to the fact that the" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - assumption: All observed linewidth is thermal (so T is an upper limit)  
  > "This quantity represents an upper limit on the real gas temperature" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **compute virial masses**: M_vir = 5 R sigma_dyn^2 / G with R_core = R_eff and sigma_dyn from the fitted sigma_V; done at constant 10 K and at T_min of each core; distributions propagated to median and 95% HPD  
  > "it can be expressed in observational units as (cite: Bertoldi92)" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+); "For each core we compute the distribution of the virial mass from the corresponding distribution of" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - parameter: Constant gas temperature 10 K, and T_min from step 7  
  > "using a constant gas temperature of $10\, \rm K$" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - assumption: Spherical uniform-density core; non-thermal component equal for all gas components  
  > "holds in the assumption of a spherical core of radius" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+); "assuming that the non-thermal component of the latter is the same for all the gas components" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **dust column density, core masses and densities from continuum**: Pixel-by-pixel N(H2) and total core mass from 0.8 mm dust emission, computed at T_dust = 10 K and at T_min; M_core only for cores with peak flux above 5 sigma; volume density from M_core and R_eff for a uniform sphere  
  > "We compute pixel-by-pixel the quantity" (5.2 Correlation between molecular and continuum emission); "for cores where the peak flux is detected above the $5\sigma$ level" (5.2 Correlation between molecular and continuum emission); "From the dust masses and effective radii we can estimate the average volume density of the cores, in the assumption of constant distribution and spherical geometry" (5.2 Correlation between molecular and continuum emission)
   - parameter: Dust opacity kappa = 1.71 cm2/g at the observed frequency (kappa_0 = 10 cm2/g at 250 um, beta = 1.5)

## Qualitative findings

- o-H2D+ is detected in both clumps with an interferometer for the first time in high-mass star-forming regions, with extended emission and narrow, often subsonic lines  
  > "The line was successfully detected in both sources, and its emission appears extended." (1 Introduction)
- o-H2D+ integrated intensity generally does not follow the dust continuum  
  > "Figure [2](#ResultsFig) shows that the" (3 Results)
- Most H2D+-identified cores are low-mass and subvirial; one core in AG354 is a high-mass prestellar candidate at low dust temperature  
  > "Furthermore, most of them are subvirial and larger than their Jeans masses." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Light deuterated species with high critical densities stay in the gas phase at high density, so o-H2D+ is a reliable prestellar tracer  
  > "Transitions of lighter, deuterated molecules with high critical densities then represent the only reliable tracers of prestellar gas, since they can persist in the gas phase at high densities." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: SCIMES chosen because it is built for PPV molecular emission data  
  > "it is developed specifically to analyse molecular emission data in the form of position-position-velocity cubes" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
- Alternative: Dust temperature of 5 K versus 10 K versus 20 K considered because constant 10 K is questionable  
  > "Only in the assumption of very low dust temperatures" (5.2 Correlation between molecular and continuum emission)
- Alternative: Magnetic support considered as an alternative to kinetic virialisation  
  > "We cannot exclude however than the cores are virialised by sources of pressure other than the kinetic one, and in particular by the presence of magnetic fields." (5.2 Correlation between molecular and continuum emission)
- How they decided it was enough: Dendrogram parameters chosen after multiple tests and visual inspection  
  > "We performed multiple tests, followed by visual inspection of the results, to find the best choice for our" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
- Limitation (authors): No total-power data; large-scale emission filtered out and core masses may be underestimated  
  > "the lack of total-power observations, which should recover the most extended emission surrounding the cores" (4.1 Core identification with SCIMES)
- Limitation (authors): Single transition prevents independent determination of gas temperature and density  
  > "having access to only one molecular transition prevents us to determine the gas temperature and density independently at the same time" (5.1 The gas traced by o-H2D+)
- Limitation (authors): Limited spectral resolution may overestimate linewidths  
  > "the limited spectral resolution of our observations may cause an overestimation of the linewidths" (4.2 Spectral fitting with MCWEEDS)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): T_ex = 10 K and T_dust = 10 K are assumed; results (masses, alpha_vir) change by large factors at 5 K
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral resolution 0.22 km/s is comparable to thermal widths, so subsonic claims depend on unresolved instrumental broadening
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 50-60% of continuum flux and up to 4/5 of line peak flux are missing; masses and kinematics of cores are biased
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass from continuum compared with virial mass from line-core volumes that differ in extent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 4 contains an obvious typo (tau uncertainty 0.49 +/- 150 for AG354 core 4)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two low-mass clumps (150-170 Msun) are studied, so the HMPC conclusion is weakly tested
