## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the continuum**: Band 7 manual calibration by ALMA staff; Band 6 ALMA pipeline; natural-weighted continuum from line-free channels with uv-range cuts  
  > "The Band 6 data were calibrated with the ALMA pipeline version 2021.2.0.128 using CASA version 6.2.1.7." (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging); "We imaged the continuum using natural weighting to maximize sensitivity, averaging over all spectral windows after including only line-free regions." (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging)
   - software: CASA, ALMA pipeline
   - parameter: Short baselines excluded in some cases (Band 6 uv > 50 klambda; B331 fields > 500 klambda in both bands)  
  > "in some cases we had to exclude the data from shorter baselines that are sensitive to extended emission" (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging); "we removed baselines \< 500 k$\lambda$ for images in both bands" (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Detect sources and measure continuum fluxes**: 2D Gaussian fit with imfit; detection at >5x noise rms in one or both bands; noise from emission-free region  
  > "Fluxes were determined using the imfit task in CASA, which fits a two-dimensional Gaussian to the selected image component" (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes); "A source is considered a detection if its flux is higher than five times the noise $rms$ in one or both bands." (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: Flux error = max(10% calibration error, noise rms, fit statistical error); peak fluxes reported  
  > "The errors on the flux are taken to be the maximum of the standard flux calibration error of 10%, the noise $rms$ and statistical errors from the fit." (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian least squares via imfit; no priors stated  
  > "Fluxes were determined using the imfit task in CASA, which fits a two-dimensional Gaussian to the selected image component" (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes)
   - validation: Fitted FWHM at most beam size so sources unresolved; peak equals integrated flux within errors; NIR counterparts checked with LBT  
  > "All resulting major-axis FWHM were at most on the order of the beam size, so we concluded that none of the detected sources is resolved." (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes)
3. **Derive spectral indices to identify emission mechanism**: alpha = log(F1/F2)/log(nu1/nu2) between Band 7 and Band 6; for B331 also Band 6 to VLA 10 GHz  
  > "following, e.g., (cite: beckwith1990; tychoniec2020), is defined as:" (3.1 Spectral index and origin of detected fluxes); "For all detected sources we calculate the spectral index between ALMA Bands 7 and 6." (3.1 Spectral index and origin of detected fluxes)
   - parameter: Adopted frequencies 341.4183 GHz (Band 7), 225.4311 GHz (Band 6), 10 GHz (VLA X)  
  > "The adopted frequencies for these bands are $\nu_7 = 341.4183$ GHz, $\nu_6 = 225.4311$ GHz, and $\nu_{\rm X} = 10$ GHz for Band 7, Band 6, and VLA 10 GHz respectively." (3.1 Spectral index and origin of detected fluxes)
   - assumption: Alpha=2 for optically thick dust, ~0.6 for ionized winds, -0.1 for optically thin free-free  
  > "If all the ionized gas close to the star is optically thin, the emission will have an index $\alpha \simeq -0.1$ in the observed ALMA frequencies." (4.1.1 Origin of radio emission and relation to spectral index)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: B331 ALMA index compared with VLA index of Rodriguez et al. 2012  
  > "is consistent with our derived index between the two ALMA flux points ($0.52 \pm 0.48$)" (4.1.2 Spectral indices for detected sources)
4. **Upper limit on gas mass from CO non-detection**: 3 sigma integrated 12CO 2-1 limit converted with RADEX to column density, then to gas mass with an assumed CO abundance, for optically thin and compact optically thick cases  
  > "We used the non-LTE molecular radiative transfer code RADEX [^22] (cite: vandertak2007) to estimate line radiation temperatures" (3.2 Upper limit on the gas mass)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: T_kin=100 K, n(H2)=1e10 cm-3, line width 10 km/s, beam 0.07x0.04 arcsec, limit ~24.0 mJy/beam km/s  
  > "using a kinetic temperature $T_{\rm kin}=100~\rm K$, an $\rm H_2$-density of $10^{10}~\rm cm^{-3}$ leading to LTE conditions, and a line width of $10~\rm km~s^{-1}$" (3.2 Upper limit on the gas mass)
   - assumption: CO abundance n(CO)/n(H2)=1e-4  
  > "assuming a CO abundance of $n({\rm CO})/n({\rm H_2}) = 10^{-4}$" (3.2 Upper limit on the gas mass)
   - assumption: Compact optically thick case: constant N_CO ~5e20 cm-2 over a 30 au disk  
  > "we adopt a constant, average CO column density of $N_{\rm CO}\sim 5 \times 10^{20}~\rm cm^{-2}$ and a CO abundance of $n({\rm CO})/n({\rm H_2}) = 10^{-4}$ throughout a disk of 30 au." (3.2 Upper limit on the gas mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Dust masses from optically thin continuum flux**: M_dust = F d^2 / (kappa B(T_dust)) per band, with power-law opacity  
  > "Under this approximation the dust mass in the disk is given by (cite: beckwith1990):" (3.3 Dust mass estimation: optically thin approach)
   - parameter: kappa = kappa0 (nu/nu0)^beta with kappa0=10 cm2/g, nu0=1000 GHz, beta=1; T_dust = 25 (L/Lsun)^1/4 K (110-246 K); T_dust=150 K for serendipitous sources; d = 1.7 kpc  
  > "resulting in dust temperatures between $110~\rm K$ and $246~\rm K$ for the objects in" (3.3 Dust mass estimation: optically thin approach); "For the serendipitous detections, for which we do not know the stellar luminosities, we adopt $T_{\rm dust} = 150~\rm K$." (3.3 Dust mass estimation: optically thin approach)
   - assumption: Optically thin, isothermal dust emission; flux purely dust (not valid for free-free dominated B331, B268)  
  > "assuming optically thin dust emission from an isothermal disk" (3.3 Dust mass estimation: optically thin approach)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with ProDiMo and thin-disk estimates  
  > "lead to similar dust masses, that are at most an order of magnitude higher than the previous two estimates" (4.2 Fit results and disk mass estimates)
6. **Compare with a ProDiMo model-grid of SEDs**: Fit grid SEDs to photometry plus ALMA points for each target after swapping the stellar continuum  
  > "We fitted the model SEDs to the available photometry and ALMA points for each of the four targets in the sample." (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
   - software: ProDiMo
   - parameter: Grid 10x10x5: total mass 5e-4 to 5e-1 Msun, outer radius 20-500 au, inclination 15-75 deg; inner radius 11.2 au  
  > "The grid consists of $10 \times 10 \times 5$ models with, respectively, total disk masses ranging from $5\times10^{-4}$ to $5\times10^{-1}$ $M_{\odot}$," (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid); "outer radii from $20~\rm au$ to $500~\rm au$, and inclinations from $15^{\circ}$ to $75^{\circ}$" (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid); "The inner radius was set to $11.2~\rm au$, the dust sublimation radius for this object." (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
   - assumption: DIANA-standard setup, gas-to-dust 100, central object B331  
  > "In these models we used the DIANA-standard model setup with a gas to dust mass ratio of 100 (cite: woitke2016)." (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
   - fitting: Grid SEDs fitted to data (fit metric not stated)  
  > "We fitted the model SEDs to the available photometry and ALMA points for each of the four targets in the sample." (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
   - validation: Grid cannot fit NIR/MIR and ALMA simultaneously, so used only as a reference  
  > "Because the grid models are unable to fit the near- to mid-infrared photometry simultaneously with the ALMA points, and also provide little constraint on the disk mass, we use another disk model" (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
7. **Gas/dust masses from extrapolation of CO bandhead results and thin-disk SED fits**: (a) Extrapolate inner-disk CO column density with q=-1.5 out to R_out = half beam to get gas and dust mass; (b) fit SEDs with adapted P23 model with logistic dust-to-gas ratio  
  > "using the power law slope, $q = -1.5$, from the same fits" (3.5.2 Extrapolation and fitting); "we fit an SED to the photometry and ALMA flux points using the P23 model with the described adaptations" (3.5.2 Extrapolation and fitting)

## Software

- **CASA** 6.1.1 (Band 7 calibration); 6.2.1.7 (Band 6 calibration, imaging, flux determination): Calibration, imaging and flux determination  
  > "For all subsequent image deconvolution (imaging) and flux determination we used CASA version 6.2.1.7." (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging)
- **ALMA pipeline** 2021.2.0.128: Calibration of Band 6 data  
  > "The Band 6 data were calibrated with the ALMA pipeline version 2021.2.0.128 using CASA version 6.2.1.7." (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging)
- **imfit** : 2D Gaussian flux fits  
  > "Fluxes were determined using the imfit task in CASA, which fits a two-dimensional Gaussian to the selected image component" (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes)
- **RADEX** : Non-LTE radiative transfer to convert the 12CO limit to column density  
  > "We used the non-LTE molecular radiative transfer code RADEX [^22] (cite: vandertak2007) to estimate line radiation temperatures" (3.2 Upper limit on the gas mass)
- **ProDiMo** : Model grid of SEDs for disks around hot stars  
  > "we computed a grid of models with an extended version of the radiation thermo-chemical code ProDiMo" (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
- **SciPy (curve_fit)** : Non-linear least-squares SED fitting of the thin-disk model  
  > "from the SciPy module (cite: virtanen2020) is used to fit the data." (3.5.2 Extrapolation and fitting)
- **Kurucz model** : Stellar component of the SEDs  
  > "The SED has a stellar component that is represented by a Kurucz model matching the stellar parameters" (3.5.2 Extrapolation and fitting)
- **Fastwind / Kiwi-GA** : Stellar parameter fits in earlier work (Backs et al.), not in this paper's analysis  
  > "by fitting the normalized photospheric spectra using the non-LTE stellar atmosphere code Fastwind (cite: santolaya-rey1997; puls2005) and a genetic algorithm based fitting approach Kiwi-GA (cite: brands2022)" (1 Introduction)

## Qualitative findings

- Line emission in the CO cubes is dominated by large scales from surrounding cloud; the continuum-detected sources are not detected in 12CO or 13CO  
  > "We found that all line emission was dominated by large scales, likely from the surrounding cloud." (2.2 ALMA observations and imaging)
- All serendipitous detections other than B331 NE are seen in both bands and have optical/NIR counterparts  
  > "All other detections are in both bands and have optical and/or NIR counterparts." (2.3 Detections and determination of fluxes)
- B331 flux is dominated by free-free emission  
  > "These indices clearly indicate that in B331 free-free emission dominates the measured flux, though a thermal dust contribution from a disk cannot be excluded." (4.1.2 Spectral indices for detected sources)
- Fits suggest dust-depleted inner disks, and a dust gap in B331  
  > "suggest that the inner disks are dust depleted, or, in the case of B331, that there is a dust gap." (4.2 Fit results and disk mass estimates)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Few disk studies exist for intermediate- to high-mass stars and in massive SFRs; M17 targets have visible photospheres and known inner-disk signatures  
  > "In the context of the dearth of disk studies in the higher stellar mass regime, and in massive SFRs in general" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Constant dust-to-gas disk models could not fit all data, motivating a logistic dust-to-gas ratio  
  > "The motivation for introducing this function is that the P23 disk models with a constant dust-to-gas ratio cannot account for all the data points at the same time." (3.5.1 Model description)
- Alternative: ProDiMo grid rejected as primary model because it cannot fit NIR/MIR and ALMA points together and constrains mass poorly  
  > "Because the grid models are unable to fit the near- to mid-infrared photometry simultaneously with the ALMA points, and also provide little constraint on the disk mass, we use another disk model" (3.4 Disk mass estimation: ProDiMo model grid)
- Alternative: Non-thermal emission judged unlikely since no negative spectral indices observed  
  > "Non-thermal emission is most commonly, though not exclusively, associated with a negative spectral index, which we do not observe for any of the sources." (4.1.1 Origin of radio emission and relation to spectral index)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Sample is small  
  > "Compared to the other samples, our sample is small." (5.2.1 Low-mass star forming regions and Herbig stars)
- Limitation (authors): Gas mass estimates depend heavily on the earlier CO bandhead fits  
  > "Of course, this does not change the fact that the estimation heavily relies on the adopted results from the CO bandhead fitting." (4.2 Fit results and disk mass estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Number of serendipitous sources too small to be conclusive  
  > "The number of sources is too small to be conclusive" (5.3 Serendipitous discovery of sources)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust masses rely on a fixed opacity (kappa0=10 cm2/g, beta=1) and an assumed T_dust, both strongly changing results (paper itself notes ~10x change for 20 K)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): B331 and B268 fluxes are free-free dominated, so their ALMA-based masses are not meaningful beyond upper limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): B268 Band 6 detection is only ~3 sigma and was imaged with a taper; detection threshold of 5 sigma is relaxed for it
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SED fits with several free parameters against few data points, with many parameters pinned at bounds, are poorly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas masses depend on the assumed CO abundance and temperature and on extrapolating the inner-disk CO column density by orders of magnitude in radius
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Different uv-range cuts per source and band may bias flux comparisons and spectral indices
