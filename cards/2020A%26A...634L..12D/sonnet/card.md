- Fields: star_formation, protoplanetary_disks
- Question: What are the structure and kinematics of the DG Tau B disk and of its CO(2-1) outflow, and do the rotating conical outflow walls trace the interface with infalling material or material ejected from the disk?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate and combine configurations**: CASA calibration per cycle, phase self-calibration on continuum applied to continuum and 12CO, combine configurations with default weights  
  > "The three configurations were entirely reprocessed and combined both in continuum and $^{12}$CO" (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that average weights of each data set were similar  
  > "Before combination, we checked that the average weights of each data set were similar." (2 Observations and data reduction)
2. **Image continuum and CO**: tclean Hogbom with Briggs weighting  
  > "Joint deconvolution was achieved by the CASA tclean procedure using the Hogbom algorithm" (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA tclean
   - parameter: robust -1 continuum, 0.5 CO  
  > "with a robust parameter of -1 for the continuum data and 0.5 for the CO." (2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Disk geometry and radial continuum profile**: IMFIT elliptical Gaussian for PA; inclination from axisymmetric midplane assumption; fit major-axis intensity profile with tapered power law plus two Gaussians  
  > "We fit an elliptical Gaussian with the CASA function IMFIT to derive a position axis (PA) of $25.7 \pm 0.3 ^\circ$ for the disk major axis" (3.1)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - assumption: Disk axisymmetric and mm emission mainly in the midplane  
  > "Assuming that the disk is axisymetric and that the millimetric continuum emission is mainly located in the midplane" (3.1)
   - fitting: Nonlinear least squares, model convolved with beam FWHM; priors not applicable  
  > "The fitting is achieved using a nonlinear least squares method." (Appendix 5)
   - validation: Compared PA and inclination with Guilloteau et al. 2011 and Zapata et al. 2015  
  > "which is consistent with the estimate of 64$^\circ$ $\pm 2^\circ$ by (cite: guilloteau_dual-frequency_2011)." (3.1)
4. **Stellar mass from Keplerian PV diagram**: Thin Keplerian disk model fit to 3 sigma contour of 13CO PV blue wing  
  > "the best fit to the 3$\sigma$ contour of the $^{13}$CO PV diagram is found for a systemic local standard of rest (LSR) velocity" (3.1)
   - parameter: i = 63 deg  
  > "With an inclination $i$ = 63$^\circ$" (3.1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Outflow cone morphology and rotation**: Channel maps and transverse PV cuts; cone fit by eye; rotation from velocity shift between symmetric positions  
  > "By extrapolating the conical morphology fit by the naked eye to the channel maps" (3.2.1)
   - assumption: Axisymmetry, so velocity difference is rotation  
  > "Assuming axi-symmetry, this difference can only be due to rotation." (3.2.1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Zapata et al. 2015 rotation finding  
  > "We thus confirm the finding of (cite: zapata_kinematics_2015)" (3.2.1)
6. **Outflow mass flux**: LTE CO mass integrated over V-Vsys 2.15-8 km/s in 0.7 arcsec slices with 12CO optical depth correction using 13CO  
  > "We integrated $T_{\rm mb}$ from $(V-V_{\rm sys}) = 2.15$ to 8 km s$^{-1}$ in slices of $\delta z =$ 0.7$^{\prime\prime}$" (Appendix 7)
   - parameter: Tex = 50 K; 12CO/13CO = 65  
  > "We set the CO excitation temperature to $T_{\rm ex}=50$K, corresponding to the peak $T_{\rm mb}$ in this region." (Appendix 7)
   - assumption: Steady flow; LTE  
  > "Assuming a steady flow, the mass flux along the conical structure is estimated" (3.2.1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Integrated flux compared with SMA data (64 Jy km/s)  
  > "A similar integrated flux was found on the SMA data of (cite: zapata_kinematics_2015) (64 Jy km s$^{-1}$)." (Appendix 7)
7. **Compare wide outflow with infall model**: Ulrich rotating infall shell projected into synthetic channel maps  
  > "for a centrifugal radius $R_d$=700 au and an initial polar angle from the disk axis $\theta_0$=70$^{\circ}$" (3.2.2)
   - parameter: Rd=700 au, theta0=70 deg, M=1.1 Msun  
  > "around a central point mass of 1.1 M$_{\odot}$" (3.2.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Varied Rd and theta0 (Fig. 10)  
  > "No combination of infall parameters $R_d$ and $\theta_0$ can reproduce both the large extent and the apparent acceleration of the wide redshifted component." (3.2.2)

## Qualitative findings

- Inner conical outflow shows hollow cone with shear-like velocity structure, coincident with scattered light cavity  
  > "These walls coincide with the scattered light cavity, and they appear to be rooted within $<$ 60 au in the disk." (abstract)
- A wider slower outflow component surrounds the cone and also rotates with the disk  
  > "We also detect a wider and slower outflow component surrounding this inner conical flow, which also rotates in the same direction as the disk." (abstract)
