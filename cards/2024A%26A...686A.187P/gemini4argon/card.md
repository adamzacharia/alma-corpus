## Analysis recipe

1. **Sample selection of ALPINE continuum sources**: Select continuum-detected ALPINE galaxies at ~158 um, exclude 3 multi-component objects, and apply a continuum signal-to-noise cut of S/N > 4.5 (resulting in 12 galaxies).  
  > "Over the 23 ALPINE continuum sources, 3 galaxies are classified in the continuum images as multi-components objects (VC_5101209780,VE_530029038, DC_881725, see Béthermin et al. 2020 )." (2. Sample, observations and data analysis); "We applied the described procedure to sources detected in the continuum at S/N > 4.5 to obtain reliable measurements. Given this cut in S/N, our final sample is composed of 12 galaxies." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - parameter: S/N > 4.5  
  > "We applied the described procedure to sources detected in the continuum at S/N > 4.5 to obtain reliable measurements." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Continuum imaging and initial image-plane fitting**: Remove channels containing [CII] emission from the visibility data, produce continuum maps with CASA TCLEAN, and run CASA IMFIT in the image plane to obtain initial guesses for positions, fluxes, and sizes.  
  > "First of all, we remove from the visibility data the channels containing the [CII] emission." (2.1. Size measurement); "task TCLEAN and we run in CASA the task IMFIT to achieve a first guess of the target’s properties (positions, fluxes and sizes)." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN, IMFIT
   - fitting: Image-plane fitting with CASA IMFIT  
  > "and we run in CASA the task IMFIT to achieve a first guess of the target’s properties (positions, fluxes and sizes)." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **uv-plane dust continuum size measurement**: Fit a 2D Gaussian model in the uv-plane using CASA UVMODELFIT initialized with IMFIT outputs, circularize the deconvolved major and minor axis FWHMs, and compute effective radius r_e,FIR = FWHM_circ / 2. For unresolved sources, compute 3-sigma upper limits using the Marti-Vidal et al. (2012) formula.  
  > "are used as first guess for the proper size measurements in the uv-plane, where we assume a 2-dimensional Gaussian model for the intensity profile and we measure the sizes with the CASA task UVMODELFIT." (2.1. Size measurement); "The effective radius r_e, which represents the radius that encloses half of the total light, is then derived as r_e=FWHM_circ/2" (2.1. Size measurement); "we estimate the size upper limit from the formula by Martí-Vidal et al. (2012) considering for the likelihood λ_c a value equal to 9 corresponding to a 3σ cut-off." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - software: CASA, UVMODELFIT
   - parameter: lambda_c = 9 (3 sigma cut-off for upper limits)  
  > "considering for the likelihood λ_c a value equal to 9 corresponding to a 3σ cut-off." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - assumption: 2D Gaussian intensity profile (Sersic index n = 0.5), which returns r_eff consistent at a 5% level with an exponential profile (n = 1)  
  > "where we assume a 2-dimensional Gaussian model for the intensity profile" (2.1. Size measurement); "Fujimoto et al. (2020) describe how a Gaussian profile return an effective radius r_eff consistent at a 5% level with a more reliable exponential shape (corresponding to a Sérsic index n=1)." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - assumption: Flat LambdaCDM cosmology with Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_lambda = 0.7, h_0 = 0.7  
  > "Throughout the paper, we assume a flat ΛCDM cosmology with Ω_m = 0.3, Ω_λ = 0.7 and h_0 = 0.7." (1. Introduction)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit in uv-plane via CASA UVMODELFIT  
  > "where we assume a 2-dimensional Gaussian model for the intensity profile and we measure the sizes with the CASA task UVMODELFIT." (2.1. Size measurement)
   - validation: Visual check of observed, model, and residual maps in the image plane to exclude sub-beam multi-components; comparison of 5 overlapping sources with CRISTAL survey measurements showing agreement within 1 sigma.  
  > "We also visually check the observed, model and residual maps in the image plane to exclude multi-components below the scale of the beam (see Fig. 2)." (2.1. Size measurement); "Among the 5 unresolved sources, 2 sources (DC_683613, VC_5100822662) have a measured size in the CRISTAL sample consistent with our upper limits." (2.1. Size measurement)
4. **Multi-wavelength size comparison and scaling relation analysis**: Compare measured r_e,FIR with [CII] sizes (r_e,[CII]), rest-frame UV sizes (r_e,UV), and empirical JWST rest-frame optical sizes (r_e,opt), and analyze the r_e,FIR - L_IR and r_e,FIR - M_* scaling relations against literature samples at 4 < z < 6 and cosmological simulations.  
  > "In the left panel r_e,FIR is presented as a function of M_⋆, in the middle panel as a function of the rest-frame UV sizes r_e,UV and of the optical radius r_e,opt as derived by the JWST stellar mass − size relation (Ward et al. 2023), and in the right panel as a function of the [CII] sizes r_e,[CII]." (3.1. FIR, [CII] and UV sizes); "Together with our measurements, we report all the available rest-frame FIR size measurements from the literature at similar redshifts (4 < z < 6)." (3.2. FIR sizes versus IR luminosity and stellar masses)
   - assumption: L_IR computed from rest-frame 158 um emission assuming the Bethermin et al. (2017) SED  
  > "Here, L_IR has been computed from the rest-frame 158 µm emission assuming the Béthermin et al. (2017) SED." (3.2. FIR sizes versus IR luminosity and stellar masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-check interpretation using gas depletion times and gas fractions from Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. (2020)  
  > "measured dust size, derived by Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. (2020) from the [CII] luminosities" (4. Discussion and Summary)

## Software

- **CASA** : Continuum imaging (TCLEAN), initial image-plane fitting (IMFIT), and uv-plane size fitting (UVMODELFIT)  
  > "task TCLEAN and we run in CASA the task IMFIT to achieve a first guess of the target’s properties (positions, fluxes and sizes)." (2.1. Size measurement); "and we measure the sizes with the CASA task UVMODELFIT." (2.1. Size measurement)
- **TCLEAN** : Producing continuum maps after removing [CII] emission channels  
  > "First of all, we remove from the visibility data the channels containing the [CII] emission. We then produce the continuum maps with the CASA" (2.1. Size measurement)
- **IMFIT** : Obtaining initial guesses of target positions, fluxes, and sizes in the image plane  
  > "and we run in CASA the task IMFIT to achieve a first guess of the target’s properties (positions, fluxes and sizes)." (2.1. Size measurement)
- **UVMODELFIT** : Fitting a 2D Gaussian model in the uv-plane to measure deconvolved major and minor axis FWHMs  
  > "where we assume a 2-dimensional Gaussian model for the intensity profile and we measure the sizes with the CASA task UVMODELFIT." (2.1. Size measurement)
- **GALFIT** : Measuring rest-frame UV sizes from HST maps in Fujimoto et al. (2020)  
  > "the rest-frame UV emission from the F814W HST map (Koekemoer et al. 2007) and the F160W HST maps (Koekemoer et al. 2011) using the GALFIT task (Peng et al. 2010)." (2.1. Size measurement)

## Qualitative findings

- Dust continuum emission in the 7 resolved ALPINE galaxies at z ~ 5 is extended (r_e,FIR ~ 1.5 - 3 kpc), similar to [CII] 158 um and rest-frame optical sizes, but ~2.5 times larger than rest-frame UV sizes.  
  > "The dust emission in the selected ALPINE galaxies is rather extended (r_e,FIR ∼ 1.5 − 3 kpc), similar to [CII]158 µm but a factor of ∼ 2 larger than the rest-frame UV emission." (abstract); "They are slightly smaller than the [CII] sizes, tracing both the atomic and the molecular gas, and significantly larger (a factor of 2.5) than the rest-frame UV sizes" (3.1. FIR, [CII] and UV sizes)
- Across 10^11 L_sun to 10^13 L_sun at z ~ 4-6, the r_e,FIR - L_IR relation shows a steeply increasing trend at L_IR < 10^12 L_sun, a downturn around 10^12 L_sun, and a decreasing trend at brighter luminosities.  
  > "Putting together all the measurements at z ∼ 5, spanning 2 decades in luminosity from L_IR ∼ 10^11 L_⊙ to L_IR ∼ 10^13 L_⊙, the data highlight a steeply increasing trend of the r_e,FIR − L_IR relation at L_IR < 10^12 L_⊙, followed by a downturn and a decreasing trend at brighter luminosities." (abstract)
- Cosmological zoom-in simulations from Cochrane et al. (2023) predict a subset (~25% at 10^10 M_sun < M_* < 10^11 M_sun) of galaxies with dust sizes as large as those measured in ALPINE, whereas Popping et al. (2022) TNG50 models predict smaller sizes.  
  > "Finally, simulations that extend up to the stellar masses of the ALPINE galaxies considered in the present work predict a sub-set of galaxies (∼ 25 % at 10^10 M_⊙ < M_⋆ < 10^11 M_⊙) with sizes as large as those measured." (abstract); "This suggests that the model from Popping et al. (2022) could predict too compact FIR dust emission extension." (3.1. FIR, [CII] and UV sizes)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The completed ALPINE survey observes normal main-sequence galaxies at z ~ 4.4 - 5.9, increasing the number of high-z sources with dust continuum size measurements and probing lower IR luminosities than previously studied IR-bright galaxies.  
  > "The present study increases the number of sources with measured dust continuum sizes at very high z and, given the lower IR luminosity range spanned by the ALPINE galaxies, better constrains the r_e,FIR-L_IR scaling relation." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Adopted the uv-plane fitting procedure of Fujimoto et al. (2020) for consistency with the ALPINE [CII] size measurements, and restricted literature comparisons to 0.8-1 mm observations to sample the same rest-frame frequencies and avoid bias from radial dust temperature gradients.  
  > "In order to measure the continuum sizes of the targets, we adopted the procedure outlined in Fujimoto et al. (2020) to measure the sizes of the [CII] emission of the ALPINE targets." (2.1. Size measurement); "To avoid bias introduced by the radial gradient in dust temperature (e.g. Calistro Rivera et al. 2018; Cochrane et al. 2019), we consider galaxies observed at nearly the same wavelengths of the ALPINE galaxies (0.8 − 1 mm)" (3.2. FIR sizes versus IR luminosity and stellar masses)
- Alternative: Multi-component objects (VC_5101209780, VE_530029038, DC_881725) were rejected from the analysis because mergers or patchy disturbed morphologies make constraining the system center and size difficult.  
  > "Over the 23 ALPINE continuum sources, 3 galaxies are classified in the continuum images as multi-components objects (VC_5101209780,VE_530029038, DC_881725, see Béthermin et al. 2020 )." (2. Sample, observations and data analysis); "For this reason, we choose to remove these galaxies from our analysis." (2. Sample, observations and data analysis)
- Alternative: Exponential intensity profile (Sersic index n = 1) noted as an alternative to the adopted 2D Gaussian profile (n = 0.5); Gaussian was retained because Fujimoto et al. (2020) showed it returns r_eff consistent at the 5% level.  
  > "Fujimoto et al. (2020) describe how a Gaussian profile return an effective radius r_eff consistent at a 5% level with a more reliable exponential shape (corresponding to a Sérsic index n=1)." (2.1. Size measurement)
- How they decided it was enough: Applied a continuum threshold of S/N > 4.5 (leaving 12 galaxies) to ensure meaningful size measurements, excluded 3 multi-component sources, visually checked residual maps, and verified 1-sigma consistency for 5 sources overlapping with the higher-resolution CRISTAL survey.  
  > "We applied the described procedure to sources detected in the continuum at S/N > 4.5 to obtain reliable measurements." (2.1. Size measurement); "We note that 3 resolved sources (DC_818760, DC_848185, DC_873756) are in common with the recent results from the CRISTAL survey" (2.1. Size measurement)
- Limitation (authors): Small number of galaxies with a measured dust continuum size at high redshift (z > 4) and high IR luminosity (L_IR > 10^11.5 L_sun).  
  > "of galaxies with a measured dust continuum size at" (3.2. FIR sizes versus IR luminosity and stellar masses)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming only SFR_UV for continuum non-detected ALPINE galaxies can underestimate the total SFR by a factor of 2.  
  > "For the galaxies not detected in continuum, following Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. (2020), we consider only SFR_UV. As found by Schaerer et al. (2020) by studying the UV-continuum slope, this assumption can underestimate the total SFR by a factor of 2." (2. Sample, observations and data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): The stellar mass range of the analyzed ALPINE galaxies is not sampled in the volume of the Popping et al. (2022) TNG50 simulation, requiring extrapolation.  
  > "Moreover, our r_e,UV/r_e,FIR are significant smaller (up to a factor of 5-10) than the values predicted by the hydrodynamical cosmological simulations from Popping et al. (2022) and extrapolated at the stellar masses of our galaxies." (3.1. FIR, [CII] and UV sizes); "The stellar mass range of our galaxies is not sampled in the volume of the Popping et al. (2022) simulation." (3.2. FIR sizes versus IR luminosity and stellar masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Selection bias towards larger or brighter galaxies due to restricting the analysis to continuum detections with S/N > 4.5, where 5 of 12 targets remain unresolved.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Moderate spatial resolution of the ALPINE Band 7 observations compared to follow-up surveys like CRISTAL (~0.3''), resulting in large fractional uncertainties on several deconvolved sizes.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-band continuum observations at rest-frame 158 um require assuming a fixed SED template (Bethermin et al. 2017) to infer L_IR, without direct constraints on dust temperature or optical depth.
