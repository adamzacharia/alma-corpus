- Fields: agn_and_quasars, nearby_galaxies
- Question: What is the relative contribution of far-ultraviolet radiation from star formation (PDRs) and X-ray radiation from active galactic nuclei (XDRs) to the molecular gas heating and CO line excitation in local active galaxies?  
  > "Our goal is to infer the contribution of far-ultraviolet (FUV) radiation from star formation (SF), and X-ray radiation from the active galactic nuclei (AGN), respectively producing photodissociation regions (PDRs) and X-ray dominated regions (XDRs), to the molecular gas heating." (Abstract); "The purpose of this work is to investigate the possible relation between the AGN activity and the conditions of molecular gas in a sample of local active galaxies with well-sampled CO SLED." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure the size and flux of nuclear CO(6-5) emission from ALMA archival cubes**: Retrieve calibrated and cleaned CO(6-5) data cubes from the ALMA Archive for 7 sample galaxies, generate moment 0 maps using CASA task immoments, fit a 2D Gaussian with task imfit (for NGC 34 high-res data), and measure the radial extent of the CO(6-5) emitting region.  
  > "Using CASA 5.6 (cite: mcmullin07), we produced the moment 0 map from the data cube with the task `immoments`. To estimate the flux, we performed a 2D Gaussian fit with the task `imfit`, which returned $62 \pm 3$ Jy km s$^{-1}$" (ALMA ancillary data); "All these sources are characterized by spatially resolved CO(6–5) emission arising from the galaxy center and extending up to $150-1000$ pc, with median $r=250$ pc." (ALMA ancillary data)
   - software: CASA, immoments, imfit
   - parameter: median nuclear radius r = 250 pc adopted as upper limit for J >= 6 emitting region  
  > "We therefore assume that the bulk of higher-$J$ CO line luminosity – for which we have only *Herschel* at low resolution – arise from a comparable region of radius $r=250$ pc." (ALMA ancillary data)
   - assumption: Higher-J CO lines (J >= 6) originate from a region at most as extended as CO(6-5) (r = 250 pc)  
  > "As the critical density of the CO transitions increases with $J$ ($n_{\text{crit}} \propto (J+1)^3$), and given that the gas density increases as we get closer to the galaxy center, we expect the higher-$J$ lines to originate from an area extended at most like CO(6–5) (cite: mingozzi18)." (ALMA ancillary data)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit with CASA task imfit on moment 0 map of NGC 34 (2016.1.01223.S)  
  > "To estimate the flux, we performed a 2D Gaussian fit with the task `imfit`, which returned $62 \pm 3$ Jy km s$^{-1}$" (ALMA ancillary data)
   - validation: Compared ALMA CO(6-5) flux of NGC 34 against single-beam Herschel/SPIRE-FTS flux  
  > "This flux, which is shown with the green contours in Figure [1](#fig:N34) (cite: xu14; mingozzi18), matches that recovered by *Herschel*/SPIRE within a much larger beam of 31.2$''$." (ALMA ancillary data)
2. **Derive galactic-scale CO(1-0) fluxes, molecular gas masses, SFRs, and Schmidt-Kennicutt relation**: Extrapolate single-dish CO(1-0) beam fluxes to the CO scale radius r_CO using a 3D exponential disk profile, compute molecular gas mass M_mol, integrate Herschel/PACS 70 um radial profiles up to r_CO to obtain SFR, and compare Sigma_mol and Sigma_SFR on the Schmidt-Kennicutt plane.  
  > "We calculate the surface densities $\Sigma_{\text{mol}}$ and $\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$ within the CO radius r$_{\text{CO}}$, defined as a fraction of the optical radius $r_{25}$ (see Section [3.5](#sec:lowj))." (CO emission on global galactic scales); "The SFRs are estimated from the radial profile $F_{70} (r)$ of the 70 $\mu$m photometry maps: $\log \text{SFR} = \log L_{70} - 43.23$ (cite: calzetti10; ke12), where $L_{70}$ is in units of erg s$^{-1}$ and comes from the integration of $F_{70} (r)$ up to r$_{\text{CO}}$." (CO emission on global galactic scales)
   - parameter: r_CO = 0.17 r_25, z_CO = 0.01 r_25  
  > "where $r_{\text{CO}} = 0.17 \, r_{25}$ and $z_{\text{CO}} = 0.01 \, r_{25}$, as in (cite: casasola17) and (cite: boselli14)." (Low-J CO data)
   - parameter: X_CO = 2 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 (alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^-2)^-1)  
  > "To line up with the other samples included in our comparison, we adopt a Milky Way value of $X_{\text{CO}} = 2 \times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$, corresponding to $\alpha_{\text{CO}} = 4.3$ M$_{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{-2}$)$^{-1}$" (CO emission on global galactic scales)
   - assumption: CO(1-0) emission follows a 3D exponential profile in r and z  
  > "assuming that the CO(1–0) emission is well described by an exponential decline both along the radius $r$ and above the galactic plane on the $z$ direction (3D method)" (Low-J CO data)
   - assumption: Milky Way CO-to-H2 conversion factor applies, and 70 um emission traces star formation with negligible AGN contamination  
  > "For this reason we adopt the 70 $\mu$m emission maps from the *Herschel* Photoconductor Array Camera and Spectrometer (cite: pacs) as a proxy for SF in our sample galaxies. In this regime the AGN contamination, if any, accounts for a few percent" (Dust continuum emission as a proxy for star formation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived surface densities and depletion times against literature samples (K21, L20)  
  > "As shown in Figure [2](#fig:SK), our sample of AGN fit well in between the starburst galaxies of K21 and the mixed (AGN/SFGs) sources from L20." (CO emission on global galactic scales)

## Qualitative findings

- On kpc scales, active galaxies lie in the same region of the Schmidt-Kennicutt plane as non-active star-forming and starburst galaxies, showing no measurable AGN impact on global star formation or cold molecular gas.  
  > "By comparing our results to the Schmidt-Kennicutt relation, we find no evidence for AGN influence on the cold and low-density gas on kpc-scales." (Abstract); "Overall, we find that, on the kpc-scale, an AGN effect on the SF is not evident, thus confirming earlier findings from (cite: lamperti20), and from (cite: casasola15)" (CO emission on global galactic scales)
- At nuclear scales (r = 250 pc), CO line ratios correlate only weakly (sub-linearly) with both FUV flux G_0 and X-ray flux F_X, indicating neither mechanism alone dominates molecular gas excitation at this scale.  
  > "On nuclear ($r = 250$ pc) scales, we find weak correlations between the CO line ratios and either the FUV or X-ray fluxes: this may indicate that neither SF nor AGN radiation dominates the gas excitation, at least at $r = 250$ pc." (Abstract)
- PDR models can reproduce the observed high-J CO line ratios only at extremely high gas densities (n > 10^5 cm^-3), whereas XDR models reproduce the observations at moderate densities (n ~ 10^2 - 10^4 cm^-3).  
  > "From a comparison of the CO line ratios with PDR and XDR models, we find that PDRs can reproduce observations only in presence of extremely high gas densities ($n > 10^5$ cm$^{-3}$). In the XDR case, instead, the models suggest moderate densities ($n \approx 10^{2-4}$ cm$^{-3}$)." (Abstract)
- A combination of PDR heating (driving low/mid-J CO lines) and XDR or shock heating (driving high-J CO lines) is required to explain the observed CO SLEDs in active galactic nuclei.  
  > "We conclude that a mix of the two mechanisms (PDR for the mid-$J$, XDR or possibly shocks for the high-$J$) is necessary to explain the observed CO excitation in active galaxies." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected local galaxies with intrinsic L_X >= 10^42 erg s^-1 to ensure unambiguous AGN activity, Herschel/SPIRE observations to sample the high-J CO SLED where XDR imprints appear, Herschel/PACS 70 um maps to trace star formation with minimal AGN contamination, and archival ALMA CO(6-5) maps to resolve the spatial extent of the warm/dense nuclear gas.  
  > "Selecting sources with intrinsic L$_{\text{X}} \geq 10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$ is the standard criterion for identifying AGN, since stellar processes alone (e.g. X-ray binaries, hot ionized ISM) rarely reach this X-ray luminosity (cite: hickox18)." (Sample selection); "We use these maps to infer the size of the high-density molecular gas region that cannot be estimated from the *Herschel* data given their poor spatial resolution." (ALMA ancillary data); "For this reason we adopt the 70 $\mu$m emission maps from the *Herschel* Photoconductor Array Camera and Spectrometer (cite: pacs) as a proxy for SF in our sample galaxies. In this regime the AGN contamination, if any, accounts for a few percent" (Dust continuum emission as a proxy for star formation)
- Why this method: Used a 3D exponential CO(1-0) disk profile to account for highly inclined galaxies (~30% of the sample have i >= 60 deg), used Linmix to properly include upper limits (20-50% of high-J fluxes) in linear regressions, and compared CO line ratios rather than absolute fluxes to 1-D CLOUDY slab models to cancel out emitting area differences.  
  > "Since the $\sim \! 30 \%$ of our sample contains highly inclined galaxies ($i \geq 60^{\circ}$), we follow (cite: boselli14) and (cite: casasola20) assuming that the CO(1–0) emission is well described by an exponential decline both along the radius $r$ and above the galactic plane on the $z$ direction (3D method)" (Low-J CO data); "Since an important fraction (between 20 and 50 $\%$, depending on the transition) of the high-$J$ CO fluxes are actually upper limits (see Table [\[tab:cosled\]](#tab:cosled)), we plot the Linmix results" (PDR); "The results of these simulations mainly apply for a single cloud, while we are dealing with entire galaxies (or at least their inner regions); it is therefore especially convenient to study the effect on the line ratios, rather than line fluxes or luminosities, assuming that both numerators and denominators originate from the same area." (Comparison with models)
- Alternative: 2D exponential radial profile for CO(1-0) instead of 3D profile (3D chosen because ~30% of the sample is highly inclined with i >= 60 deg)  
  > "Since the $\sim \! 30 \%$ of our sample contains highly inclined galaxies ($i \geq 60^{\circ}$), we follow (cite: boselli14) and (cite: casasola20) assuming that the CO(1–0) emission is well described by an exponential decline both along the radius $r$ and above the galactic plane on the $z$ direction (3D method)" (Low-J CO data)
- Alternative: Ordinary least squares (OLS) and bootstrapped OLS regression on detections only instead of Linmix (rejected because 20-50% of high-J CO fluxes are upper limits)  
  > "We also tried to fit only the detections with an ordinary least squares regression and with a bootstrapped version of the same algorithm, finding limited differences with respect to the Linmix regression, which includes the censored data." (PDR)
- Alternative: Correlating CO line ratios against local X-ray energy deposition rate H_X and effective ionization parameter xi_eff instead of F_X (omitted from main plots because they showed less defined trends than F_X)  
  > "In Figure [6](#fig:FXCOratios) we plot the same luminosity line ratios (CO(6–5)/CO(1–0), CO(8–7)/CO(1–0) and CO(13–12)/CO(1–0) on the left panel, CO(9–8)/CO(6–5), CO(11–10)/CO(6–5) and CO(13–12)/CO(6–5) on the right panel) analysed in Figure [3](#fig:G0COratios), as a function of $F_{\text{X}}$ only, since both $H_{\text{X}}$ and $\xi_{\text{eff}}$ were showing, compared to $F_{\text{X}}$, less defined trends." (XDR)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): High-resolution (35 mas) ALMA CO(6-5) observation of NGC 34 recovers less than 10% of the total SPIRE-FTS flux due to its small maximum recoverable scale (0.5 arcsec).  
  > "To estimate the flux, we performed a 2D Gaussian fit with the task `imfit`, which returned $62 \pm 3$ Jy km s$^{-1}$, less than $10 \%$ of the total flux measured by SPIRE-FTS ($920 \pm 56$ Jy km s$^{-1}$). The reason for this discrepancy is that this observation is limited by a much smaller maximum recoverable scale, compared to the 200-mas data." (ALMA ancillary data)
- Limitation (authors): Adopting a Milky Way CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO = 4.3) may overestimate molecular gas masses in dusty (U)LIRGs and starburst galaxies, where alpha_CO ~ 0.8 is often thought to apply.  
  > "We note that these masses could be upper limits, since we are adopting a Milky Way value of $\alpha_{\text{CO}}$, while it is thought that dusty (U)LIRGs and starburst galaxies have a lower $\alpha_{\text{CO}} \approx 0.8$ M$_{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{-2}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: downes98; bolatto13)." (CO emission on global galactic scales)
- Limitation (authors): Low-J transitions CO(2-1) to CO(5-4) are not corrected for aperture/radial profile in the nuclear ratios because no theoretical radial profile is available for them.  
  > "Since these low-$J$ CO transitions are not the focus of the present work, and since we do not have a theoretical radial profile to correct them, we leave them unaltered, and put the relative plots only in the Appendix [\[sec:COratiofull\]](#sec:COratiofull)." (CO emission in the galaxy centers)
- Limitation (authors): The adopted nuclear radius r = 250 pc (set by CO(6-5) maps) may still be larger than the true AGN XDR sphere of influence (r ~ 100 pc), diluting F_X and high-J CO ratios with stellar FUV heating due to Herschel's coarse spatial resolution.  
  > "A plausible explanation is that at $r=250$ pc we are still outside of the actual AGN sphere of influence of the molecular gas: several studies on Seyfert galaxies (cite: davies07; kawakatu08; esquej14; motter21) indeed place it within the central $r=100$ pc. At larger radii, we cannot isolate the contribution of X-rays due to dilution with stellar FUV photons." (XDR)
- Limitation (authors): Mechanical heating from AGN/starburst outflows and shocks can also enhance high-J CO lines, but is not modelled in this work.  
  > "Disentangling the contribution of shock heating from that produced in XDRs is a challenging task (cite: hollenbach89; meijerink13; mingozzi18). However, the study of mechanical heating is beyond the scope of this paper." (Comparison with models)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming 100% of J >= 6 CO emission observed within a 17-43 arcsec Herschel/SPIRE beam originates inside r = 250 pc (based on CO(6-5) maps of only 7/35 galaxies) may overestimate nuclear mid/high-J surface brightnesses if extended starburst regions contribute.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating single-dish CO(1-0) fluxes down to r = 250 pc using a smooth exponential disk profile ignores central molecular rings, bars, or nuclear concentrations common in Seyferts and mergers.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing single-density 1-D slab CLOUDY models directly to galaxy-integrated line ratios ignores multi-phase gas structure and differential beam/volume filling factors between CO(1-0), CO(6-5), and high-J transitions.
