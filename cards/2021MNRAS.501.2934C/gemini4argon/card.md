## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive stellar properties and classify SEDs**: Classify YSOs using 2-20 um IR slope alpha_IR and mid-IR morphology; fit extinction A_V and luminosity using J-band bolometric corrections and BT-Settl model photospheres; infer stellar masses and ages from BT-Settl evolutionary tracks (or statistical durations for embedded sources).  
  > "We derive the luminosities by applying the bolometric corrections from (cite: allard2012), appropriate for the given effective temperature, to the extinction-corrected values in the J-band." (Stellar properties); "For Class II sources, we estimate stellar masses and ages by comparing the effective temperatures and luminosities to the predictions of the BT-Settl evolutionary models from (cite: allard2012)." (Stellar properties)
   - parameter: Class I: alpha_IR > +0.3; FS: +0.3 > alpha_IR > -0.3; Class II: -0.3 > alpha_IR > -1.6; Class III: -1.6 > alpha_IR; TD/PTD threshold: [3.6]-[4.5] = 0.25  
  > "- Class I : $\alpha_{IR}$ $>$ +0.3 - Flat Spectrum (FS): +0.3 $>\alpha_{IR} >$ -0.3 - Class II: -0.3 $>\alpha_{IR} >$ -1.6 - Class III: -1.6 $>\alpha_{IR}$" (Spectral Energy Distributions); "- Transition discs (TD): Class II sources without significant IR excess at $\lambda$ $\lesssim$ 4.5 $\mu$m (i.e. \[3.6\]-\[4.5\] $<$ 0.25, (cite: cieza2010)), indicating depleted inner dust discs." (Spectral Energy Distributions)
   - assumption: Temperature scale from Pecaut & Mamajek (2013); extinction law from Fitzpatrick (1999) with NIR corrections by Indebetouw et al. (2005); statistical ages <= 0.5 Myr for Class I and <= 1 Myr for Flat Spectrum sources  
  > "For Class II sources, we adopt the spectral types listed in Table [\[t:basic_prop\]](#t:basic_prop) and the temperature scale from (cite: pecautmamajek2013)." (Stellar properties); "The A$_V$ values were calculated using the extinction law from (cite: fitzpatrick1999), including the NIR empirical corrections by (cite: indebetouw2005)." (Stellar properties); "we adopt ages for them based on the statistical duration of each stage provided by (cite: evans2009): $\lesssim$ 1 Myr for Flat Spectrum sources and $\lesssim$ 0.5 Myr for Class I sources." (Stellar properties)
   - fitting: Best match between extinction-corrected optical SED (or near-IR colors for embedded targets) and BT-Settl model photospheres  
  > "We adopt the A$_{V}$ that provides the best match between the extinction-corrected optical SED and the corresponding model photospheres from (cite: allard2012)." (Stellar properties)
   - validation: Compared derived stellar properties against DSHARP values from Andrews et al. (2018), finding good agreement within uncertainties  
  > "For these DSHARP objects, we adopt the stellar properties (measured and derived) listed by (cite: Andrews2018). The derived properties (A$_V$, L$_{\star}$, M$_{\star}$, and age) are in good agreement with the values obtained by our own procedures, within the uncertainties discussed above." (Stellar properties)
2. **Fit disc geometry, measure disc radii R_90%, and calculate dust masses**: Find disc center (Ra_disc, Dec_disc) by minimizing the imaginary part of continuum visibilities; fix center and calculate PA and inclination by minimizing dispersion of deprojected visibilities in circular annuli; measure 90% flux radius R_90% in concentric ellipses; compute dust mass from 1.3 mm flux.  
  > "Following (cite: isella2019), the Ra$_{disc}$ and Dec$_{disc}$ values were obtained by minimizing the imaginary part of the continuum visibilities, which in turn minimizes the asymmetry of the continuum emission relative to the phase center." (Basic disc properties); "After fixing the Ra$_{disc}$ and Dec$_{disc}$ values, the $PA$ and $i$ were calculated by minimizing the dispersion of the deprojected visibilities on circular annuli in the Fourier space." (Basic disc properties); "In Table [\[t:basic_disc_prop\]](#t:basic_disc_prop), we also list the radii of the discs containing 90$\%$ of the flux which we call R$_{90\%}$. These values were calculated by measuring the flux in concentric ellipses with aspect ratios corresponding to the inclination of each disc." (Basic disc properties)
   - assumption: Dust opacity kappa_1.3mm = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1, dust temperature = 20 K, distance = 140 pc for targets without Gaia parallax, and low-resolution 1.3 mm fluxes used for total dust mass  
  > "This equation assumes a 1.3 mm dust opacity, $\kappa_{1.3mm}$, of 2.3 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$, and a dust temperature of 20 K." (Basic disc properties); "We adopt the 1.3 mm fluxes from the low-resolution ODISEA data listed in Table [\[t:sample\]](#t:sample)" (Basic disc properties); "For objects without distances from Gaia, we adopt 140 pc, which is very close to the mean Gaia distance obtained by (cite: canovas2019) for Ophiuchus members (139 pc)." (Basic disc properties)
   - fitting: Minimization of imaginary part of visibilities for center and minimization of dispersion of deprojected visibilities in Fourier annuli for PA and inclination  
  > "After fixing the Ra$_{disc}$ and Dec$_{disc}$ values, the $PA$ and $i$ were calculated by minimizing the dispersion of the deprojected visibilities on circular annuli in the Fourier space." (Basic disc properties)
   - validation: Disc centers checked for consistency with Gaia positions and proper motions  
  > "The Ra$_{disc}$ and Dec$_{disc}$ values are consistent with the positions and proper motions from Gaia listed in Table [\[t:sample\]](#t:sample)." (Basic disc properties)
3. **Extract deprojected radial brightness profiles and characterize substructures**: Deproject CLEAN images to 0 deg inclination using fitted centers, PA, and inclinations; azimuthally average into radial brightness profiles; identify local minima (gaps), local maxima (rings), inner cavities (R_Cav, R_Cav.10%, 50%, 90%), central beam inner disc fluxes/masses, small cavities, and inflection points.  
  > "Using the disc centers and inclinations listed in Table [\[t:basic_disc_prop\]](#t:basic_disc_prop), we deproject the discs to an inclination of 0.0 deg." (Substructures from radial profiles); "also following (cite: huang2018), the depths of the gaps are defined from the ratio of the local maxima and local minima in gap-ring pairs, $I_{min}$/$I_{max}$. Similarly, the widths of the gaps and the rings are measured at the mean intensity between these adjacent pairs of gaps and rings, 0.5 $\times$ ($I_{min}$ + $I_{max}$)." (Gaps and rings); "in Table [\[t:cav\]](#t:cav) we also report the radii at which the profiles reach 10%, 50$\%$ and 90$\%$ of the peak flux ($R_{Cav.,10\%}$, $R_{Cav.,50\%}$, and $R_{Cav.,90\%}$, respectively)." (Discs with large (r > 20 au) dust inner cavities)
   - parameter: Azimuthal angles restricted to within 20 deg of the semi-major axis for highly inclined systems (ISO-Oph 37 and WSB 82); combined 0.03'' + 0.2'' image used beyond 1.0'' for WSB 82  
  > "Following (cite: huang2018), for highly inclined systems (ISO-Oph 37 and WSB 82), we only use the azimuthal angles within 20 deg of the semi-major axis orientation in the reprojected coordinates" (Substructures from radial profiles); "For WSB 82, we derive the radial profile beyond 1.0$''$ from the image that combines the long-baseline data with the observations at 0.2$''$ resolution." (Gaps and rings)
   - assumption: All features in a given disc share the same center, position angle, and inclination; inner disc dust mass uses T = 20 K and kappa_1.3mm = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "Namely, we assume that all features in a given disc have the same center, position, angle and inclination." (Caveats and uncertainties); "These masses have been calculated using the same temperature (20 K) and opacities (2.3 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$) as in (cite: cieza2019)" (Discs with large (r > 20 au) dust inner cavities)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Gaps and rings are the most common substructures, with 26 narrow rings and gaps identified across 8 of the 10 ODISEA long-baseline sources.  
  > "By number, gaps and rings are the most common types of substructures. We identify a total of 26 narrow rings and gaps (width/radius $\lesssim$ 1) distributed in 8 sources" (Summary and conclusions)
- Two embedded Flat Spectrum sources (WLY 2-63 and ISO-Oph 37) lack clear gaps and rings, whereas the Class I object ISO-Oph 54 and all 12 Class II discs in the combined sample show rich substructures, suggesting substructures develop during the embedded stage.  
  > "Two discs (WLY 2-63 and ISO-Oph 37) around embedded protostars with Flat Spectrum SEDs lack the clear gaps and rings that are ubiquitous in more evolved sources with Class II SEDs." (Summary and conclusions); "Since other embedded sources in the literature also lack clear gaps and rings, this suggests that these substructures typically appear in the embedded stage of YSOs (age $\lesssim$ 1.0 Myr), but are not necessarily present from the formation of the disc." (Summary and conclusions)
- Five discs with large (> 20 au) dust cavities show radial profiles consistent with dust trapping at planet-induced pressure bumps; three (DoAr 44, WSB 82, EM* SR 24S) have unresolved inner dust discs and outer plateaus, while two (ISO-Oph 2A, RX J1633.9-2442) lack detected inner discs and have narrow outer rings.  
  > "The five Objects with large ($>$ 20 au) dust cavities (ISO-Oph 2A; EM\* SR 24S, DoAr 44, RXJ1633.9-2442, and WSB 82) exhibit different degrees of dust accumulation at the edges of their cavities, in agreement with numerical simulations of pressure bumps produced by giant planets." (Summary and conclusions); "The other 2 objects with large dust cavities (ISO-Oph 2A and RXJ1633.9-2442) have outer discs with ring-like morphologies and lack ALMA-detected inner dust discs."
