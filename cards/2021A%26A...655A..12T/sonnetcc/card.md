## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image the para-H2CO cubes**: Pipeline calibration with CASA 4.2.2, imaging with CASA 5.6.1 using a modified script from the data release; primary beam correction applied for analysis  
  > "version 4.2.2 pipeline and were then imaged using CASA version 5.6.1" (2.2 Observations and data reduction); "We imaged the calibrated data using a modified script associated with the data release." (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: channel width 1.27-1.35 km/s; rms ~2.0 mJy/beam per channel  
  > "A typical rms noise level is $\sim$2.0 mJy beam$^{-1}$ for a 1.34 km s$^{-1}$ wide channel" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: ALMA fluxes compared with APEX 12 m single-dish fluxes: ~75-80% recovered in N113, ~45-50% in N159W  
  > "We integrate all the emission within our ALMA data and compare it to the single-dish APEX 12 m data" (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
2. **extract line spectra and fit Gaussians at clump positions**: Gaussian fits to the three para-H2CO lines at 7 clump positions per region (selected by eye) to get integrated flux, V_LSR, FWHM and peak flux  
  > "were obtained from Gaussian fits using CLASS as part of the GILDAS software." (Table 2 notes); "The location of the seven dense H$_2$CO clumps in each of the two massive star forming regions, selected by eye" (3.1 Overview)
   - software: GILDAS, CLASS
   - fitting: Gaussian line profile fits in CLASS; priors/likelihood not stated  
  > "were obtained from Gaussian fits using CLASS as part of the GILDAS software." (Table 2 notes)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **build para-H2CO line-ratio maps**: Ratio of velocity-integrated intensities 0.5 x [(3_22-2_21 + 3_21-2_20)/3_03-2_02], only where the two weaker lines are detected at S/N >= 3 sigma  
  > "The line ratios are calculated by velocity-integrated intensities where the para-H$_2$CO 3$_{22}$–2$_{21}$ and 3$_{21}$–2$_{20}$ lines are detected with signal-to-noise ratios (S/N) $\gtrsim$ 3$\sigma$." (3.3 H2CO line ratios)
   - software: GILDAS
   - assumption: 3_22-2_21 and 3_21-2_20 have almost the same upper-level energy (~68 K) and similar profiles, so their average is used  
  > "The upper levels of the para-H$_2$CO 3$_{22}$–2$_{21}$ and 3$_{21}$–2$_{20}$ transitions have almost the same energy above the ground state" (3.3 H2CO line ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **convert ratio maps to kinetic temperature maps**: RADEX non-LTE ratio-versus-Tkin relation at n(H2) = 1e5 cm-3 applied pixel by pixel to the ratio maps; also compared with an LTE relation  
  > "to convert ratio maps to temperature maps in Figs." (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios); "Therefore, here we adopt 10$^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$ as an averaged spatial gas density in the N113 and N159W regions." (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: T_bg = 2.73 K, linewidth 4.0 km/s, N(para-H2CO) = 2.7e12 (N113) and 3.7e12 cm-2 (N159W)  
  > "adopting a 2.73 K background temperature, an average observational linewidth of 4.0 km s$^{-1}$, and column densities $N$(para-H$_2$CO) = 2.7$\times$10$^{12}$ and 3.7$\times$10$^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$ for N113 and N159W, respectively" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
   - assumption: Average density 1e5 cm-3 for both regions (Tkin at 1e5 is lower than at 1e4 and 1e6 by <~23% and <~34% for Tkin <~ 100 K); lines optically thin  
  > "Different column densities of para-H$_2$CO only weakly affect derived kinetic temperatures" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Beam-averaged Tkin (48 K N113, 36 K N159W) agrees with APEX values 54 +/- 7 and 35 +/- 4 K  
  > "The averaged temperatures are 48 and 36 K in N113 and N159W, respectively" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
5. **test optical-depth bias of Tkin and the NH3 discrepancy**: RADEX models reproducing a peak Tmb(3_03-2_02) ~2.5 K at Tkin 16 K versus ~50 K, checking the resulting line ratio and opacity  
  > "we used again the RADEX non-LTE program (cite: van2007)" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios); "we obtain peak temperatures of only $\sim$2.5 K" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Tkin set to 16 K (NH3 value) with a 3 km/s linewidth; a second case at Tkin ~50 K  
  > "Setting $T_{\rm kin}$ to 16 K, the low value derived by (cite: Ott2010) from ammonia" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 16 K model gives line ratio ~0.04, inconsistent with the data; ~50 K model gives 0.23-0.24  
  > "far below and thus inconsistent with the obtained data" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
6. **derive thermal and non-thermal velocity dispersions and Mach numbers**: sigma_T from Tkin and H2CO mass; sigma_NT from the fitted 3_03-2_02 FWHM after removing thermal width; Mach = sigma_NT / c_s  
  > "we can derive thermal and non-thermal linewidths" (3.5 Thermal and non-thermal motions)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: mean molecular weight mu = 2.37 in the sound speed  
  > "is the sound speed, $\mu$ = 2.37 is the mean molecular weight for molecular clouds" (3.5 Thermal and non-thermal motions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Narrow linewidths (<3 km/s, 2-3 channels) and two-component regions are flagged as overestimating sigma_NT and Mach numbers; two-component locations are intensity weighted  
  > "Therefore, the non-thermal linewidths and the Mach numbers are likely overestimated in these regions." (3.5 Thermal and non-thermal motions)
7. **test radiative heating by YSOs**: Compute Tkin from YSO luminosity and distance with a modified Stefan-Boltzmann law at R = 0.2, 0.4, 1.0 pc and compare with measured H2CO temperatures around four YSOs  
  > "We determine the gas temperature in regions heated by YSOs following the modified Stefan-Boltzmann blackbody radiation law" (4.2 Radiative heating); "the derived gas temperatures at radii of 0.2, 0.4, and 1.0 pc from Eq." (4.2 Radiative heating)
   - assumption: Each YSO (or candidate) is the dominant heating source at its location  
  > "Assuming these YSOs (or YSO candidates) are the dominant sources at their locations" (4.2 Radiative heating)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured H2CO temperatures agree well with the radiation law at 0.2 pc radius  
  > "which agrees well with the results obtained from the radiation law at a radius of 0.2 pc" (4.2 Radiative heating)
8. **test turbulent heating and the Tkin-sigma_NT relation**: Fit Tkin versus sigma_NT for strongly non-thermal positions near dense clumps in N113 (power law); solve the turbulent heating balance equation for T_turb at the clumps and compare with Tkin(H2CO). N159W not tested because channel width affects its narrow lines  
  > "We select positions with strong non-thermal motions ($\mathcal{M}$ $\gtrsim$ 3.5) located near the dense clumps" (4.3 Turbulent heating); "Therefore, we did not examine this relationship in N159W." (4.3 Turbulent heating)
   - parameter: velocity gradient 1 km/s/pc, cloud size ~1 pc, n(H2) = 1e5 cm-3  
  > "a typical velocity gradient of 1 km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{-1}$" (4.3 Turbulent heating)
   - assumption: Dust temperature set equal to the H2CO gas temperature  
  > "so here we adopt the gas temperatures obtained from our H$_2$CO line ratios as the dust temperatures" (4.3 Turbulent heating)
   - fitting: Power-law fit of Tkin to sigma_NT in N113; fitting method not stated  
  > "positively correlated with the gas kinetic temperatures, by a power-law of the form" (4.3 Turbulent heating)
   - validation: T_turb values reasonably consistent with Tkin(H2CO); slope consistent with Galactic regions  
  > "values are reasonably well consistent with the gas kinetic temperatures" (4.3 Turbulent heating)

## Qualitative findings

- Para-H2CO 3_03-2_02 emission agrees best with the 1.3 mm dust continuum and well with Spitzer 8 micron emission, but not with H-alpha  
  > "The para-H$_2$CO (3$_{03}$–2$_{02}$) integrated intensity distribution agrees best with the 1.3 mm continuum emission in N113 and N159W" (4.2 Radiative heating)
- Thermal linewidths are much smaller than non-thermal ones, so dense gas is dominated by non-thermal (supersonic) motions  
  > "The thermal linewidth is significantly smaller than the non-thermal linewidth in N113 and N159W" (3.5 Thermal and non-thermal motions)
- No evidence in H2CO for the ~16 K NH3 temperature; the NH3 gas is interpreted as a highly embedded component not seen in H2CO  
  > "We did not find any evidence for the low $T_{\rm kin}$ value ($\sim$16 K)" (4.1 Comparison of temperatures obtained from H2CO, CO, and NH3)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets were chosen because they were detected in the three para-H2CO (3-2) lines with APEX  
  > "were selected from previous single dish observations with the Atacama Pathfinder EXperiment telescope (APEX)" (2.1 Targets)
- Why this method: H2CO line ratios between different Ka ladders trace kinetic temperature because level populations are governed by collisions  
  > "line ratios involving different $K_{\rm a}$ ladders are good tracers of the kinetic temperature" (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Highly subthermal or saturated 3_03-2_02 emission with a low Tkin (~16 K, as from NH3) was rejected because the predicted line ratio (~0.04) is inconsistent with the data  
  > "indicates either highly subthermal excitation with potentially saturated para-H$_2$CO 3$_{03}$–2$_{02}$ line emission or a purely optically thin scenario" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios); "far below and thus inconsistent with the obtained data" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Opacities of the para-H2CO (3-2) lines are poorly known; saturation could overestimate Tkin  
  > "a major caveat in our discussion are the poorly known opacities of para-H$_2$CO ($J$=3–2) lines in N113 and N159W." (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
- Limitation (authors): Tkin toward cores might be overestimated, though not grossly  
  > "values toward the cloud cores might be overestimated, they are not grossly overestimated" (3.4 Kinetic temperatures from H2CO line ratios)
- Limitation (authors): Missing flux relative to APEX, partly due to field centres offset from the emission  
  > "so that part of the missing flux in our ALMA data may not be due to a lack of short baselines but due to the chosen field centers." (2.2 Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Coarse channel width prevents resolving narrow velocity components, so linewidths and Mach numbers are likely overestimated in some places  
  > "Our observational set can not well identify the velocity components with narrow linewidths using Gaussian profiles in these regions." (3.5 Thermal and non-thermal motions)
- Limitation (authors): Limited resolution and 2D view prevent a clean Tkin versus YSO-distance relation  
  > "and our limited 2-dimensional perspective, the relation between gas temperature and distance from YSOs is not perfectly revealed" (4.2 Radiative heating)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single assumed density (1e5 cm-3) and fixed column densities taken from 30 arcsec APEX data are applied to all pixels; Tkin changes by up to 23-34% with density
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainty maps are propagated from line-ratio noise or RADEX assumptions into Tkin
- Caveat (inferred, ours): T_turb uses T_dust = T_gas, which makes the turbulent-heating consistency check partly circular
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Tkin-sigma_NT correlation uses pixels selected by Mach number > 3.5 and only in N113; selection effects and fit method are not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 3_03 optical depth is probed with one-zone RADEX tests; ALMA missing flux (up to 50-55% in N159W) may bias ratios
