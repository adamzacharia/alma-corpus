## Science

- Fields: cosmology, agn_and_quasars, astrochemistry
- Question: What is the cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation temperature at z = 0.68 derived from rotational molecular absorption lines toward the lensed quasar B0218+357, and is it consistent with the standard Big Bang cosmological model?  
  > "In this paper, we accurately evaluate the excitation temperatures and their uncertainties from the absorption strengths of these components, and use them to determine the CMB temperature." (Abstract); "In this work, we report the first measurement of $T_\mathrm{CMB}(z\!=\!0.68)$ using quasar absorption lines, derived from millimeter data obtained with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) toward the A component of B0218+357." (Introduction)

  > "B0218+357 is a quasar at $z\!=\!0.944$ (cite: Cohen_2003), and a nearly face-on spiral galaxy (cite: York_2005) is known to lie along its line of sight at $z=0.68466$ (cite: Browne_1993; Carilli_1993; WiklindCombes_1995)." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Extractspectral windows and regrid velocity axis**: Extract spectral windows containing target absorption lines and regrid spectral axis to minimum velocity resolution in barycentric frame using relativistic Doppler conversion  
  > "Subsequently, using the CASA task `mstransform`, we extracted the spectral windows containing the HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC absorption lines" (Data Processing); "The spectral axis was then regridded using the `mstransform` task to achieve the minimum velocity resolution specified in the ALMA archive, and the velocity reference frame was adopted as barycentric." (Data Processing)
   - software: CASA, mstransform
   - parameter: bandwidths: 0.23 GHz (0.47 GHz for Band 4 HNC) for HCN, HCO+, HNC; 0.12 GHz (Band 3) and 0.94 GHz (Band 4) for H13CN and H13CO+  
  > "with bandwidths of 0.23 GHz (0.47 GHz for the Band 4 data covering HNC), and the spectral windows containing the H$^{13}$CN and H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ absorption lines with bandwidths of 0.12 GHz (Band 3) and 0.94 GHz (Band 4), respectively." (Data Processing)
   - parameter: z = 0.68466, V_sys = 1.4357e5 km/s  
  > "The redshift of the absorption system ($z\!=\!0.68466$) corresponds to a systemic velocity of $V_\mathrm{sys}\!=\!1.4357\times10^{5}$ $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$." (Data Processing)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract absorption spectra toward lensed quasar component A**: Direct visibility fitting in the Fourier plane using a two-point-source model: (1) determine peak positions from continuum visibilities, (2) fit visibilities in each spectral channel at fixed positions  
  > "The spectra toward components A and B of B0218+357 were extracted using the Python-based package $\texttt{UVMULTIFIT}$ (cite: MartiVidal_2014) by directly fitting the visibilities for each component." (Data Processing); "We adopted a two-point-source model and obtained the spectra through a two-step procedure: (1) determining the peak positions using only the continuum visibilities, and (2) fitting the visibilities in each spectral channel at those positions." (Data Processing)
   - software: UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: two-point-source model with separation ~0.3 arcsec  
  > "the simple and well-known two-point-source geometry of this system, with a separation of $\!\sim\!0{\overset{\prime\prime}{.}}3$ on the sky, enables spectral extraction via visibility fitting in the Fourier plane (cite: MartiVidal_2014; Wallstrom_2019)." (Data Processing)
   - assumption: Two compact lensed components can be modeled as two point sources; no significant time variability over the 9-month observation interval  
  > "We adopted a two-point-source model and obtained the spectra through a two-step procedure" (Data Processing); "On the basis of these considerations, we assume that no significant variability in the absorption strength occurred over this short period." (Time Variability)
   - fitting: Visibility-plane fitting per spectral channel  
  > "(1) determining the peak positions using only the continuum visibilities, and (2) fitting the visibilities in each spectral channel at those positions." (Data Processing)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compute optical depth profiles and propagate covering factor uncertainties**: Monte Carlo sampling of continuum covering factor f_c and continuum-normalized absorption depth to compute optical depth probability distribution, followed by correction for lognormal column-density nonuniformity  
  > "For each velocity bin and each transition, we drew random and independent samples of $f_\mathrm{c}$ and $\Delta I/I_\mathrm{c}$ from their respective distributions and computed the optical depth using Equation (\[eq:taudef\])." (Continuum Covering Factor); "The resulting relation between $\tau_{\rm obs}$ and $\tau_{\rm real}$ is shown in Figure 1 of (cite: Kotani_2025). We applied this nonuniformity correction to all data points in the absorption spectra." (Correction for Nonuniformity)
   - software: NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: 100,000 Monte Carlo realizations  
  > "Repeating this procedure 100,000 times yielded the probability distribution of the optical depth." (Continuum Covering Factor)
   - parameter: f_c uniform distribution in [0.77, 1.00]  
  > "We assumed that $f_\mathrm{c}$ follows a uniform distribution between 0.77 and 1.00." (Continuum Covering Factor)
   - assumption: Lognormal column density distribution with parameters from Orion A GMC (mean N(H2) ~ 1.5e22 cm^-2)  
  > "In this study, we adopted the N-PDF parameters derived by (cite: Ma_2020) for the Orion A giant molecular cloud, which has a comparable mean column density of $\sim 1.5\times10^{22}\,{\rm cm}^{-2}$ (cite: Ma_2020), to compute the correction." (Correction for Nonuniformity)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo sampling (100,000 iterations); uniform prior on f_c in [0.77, 1.00], normal distribution on ΔI/I_c  
  > "We assumed that $f_\mathrm{c}$ follows a uniform distribution between 0.77 and 1.00." (Continuum Covering Factor); "The absorption depth normalized by the continuum level, ${\Delta I/I_\mathrm{c}} = 1 - I_\mathrm{obs}$, is assumed to follow a normal distribution, with its mean and standard deviation given by the observed value and its uncertainty, respectively." (Continuum Covering Factor)
   - validation: Checked saturation bottom of HCN J=2-1 at +5 km/s yielding lower limit f_c ~ 0.8, consistent with f_c >= 0.77  
  > "If we assume that the optical depth becomes infinite at the absorption minimum, Equation (\[eq:taudef\]) yields a lower limit of $f_{\rm c} \sim 0.8$, which is consistent with the constraint $f_{\rm c} \ge 0.77$." (Optical Depth)
4. **Derive excitation temperatures T_ex from J=2-1 and J=3-2 optical depths**: Resample HCN, HCO+, HNC spectra to ΔV_bar = 0.909 km/s and fit Boltzmann optical depth relation (Eq. 3) per velocity bin where tau < 1, then take weighted mean over +5 km/s component; for H13CN and H13CO+, fit single Gaussian to optical depth, integrate over [-5, +15] km/s, and fit Eq. 3  
  > "All spectra of these molecules were resampled to match the velocity spacing of the *J*=3–2 data, which have the lowest spectral resolution ($\Delta V_\mathrm{bar}=0.909\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$)." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC); "$T_\mathrm{ex}$ and $N$ were determined by performing a weighted least-squares fit of Equation (\[eq:tobesolved\]) to the integrated optical depth calculated in each velocity bin." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC); "We therefore fitted each optical depth profile with a single Gaussian component and computed the respective velocity-integrated optical depths over the range $-5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ to $+15\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ for each transition of each molecule." (13C isotopologues)
   - software: SciPy, RADEX
   - parameter: ΔV_bar = 0.909 km/s; tau < 1 cutoff; initial T_ex = 4.59 K; initial N = 7.8e13, 4.5e13, 2.6e13, 2.9e12, 2.0e12 cm^-2 for HCN, HCO+, HNC, H13CN, H13CO+  
  > "The initial value of $T_\mathrm{ex}$ was set to the standard Big Bang model prediction, $T_\mathrm{CMB}(z=0.68)=4.59\,\mathrm{K}$." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC); "To ensure that the derived excitation temperature reliably reflects the CMB temperature, the analysis was restricted to the velocity ranges where the optical depth is less than unity." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC); "The initial values of $N$ were adopted from (cite: Wallstrom_2016), namely $2.9\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ for H$^{13}$CN and $2.0\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ for H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$." (13C isotopologues)
   - assumption: Rotational levels follow a Boltzmann distribution characterized by a single excitation temperature T_ex  
  > "Under the assumption that the molecular rotational transitions are characterized by a single excitation temperature ($T_{\rm ex}$) but are not necessarily thermalized (cite: WiklindCombes_1995), $N_{J+1}$ and $N_J$ follow a Boltzmann distribution determined by the $T_{\rm ex}$." (Fundamentals)
   - fitting: Weighted least-squares fit of Eq. 3 with uncertainties from 68% confidence interval of Monte Carlo distribution  
  > "$T_\mathrm{ex}$ and $N$ were determined by performing a weighted least-squares fit of Equation (\[eq:tobesolved\]) to the integrated optical depth calculated in each velocity bin." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC); "The uncertainty in $T_\mathrm{ex}$ corresponds to the 68% confidence interval of the Monte Carlo-derived distribution." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC)
   - validation: RADEX non-LTE calculations (at T_kin = 55 K, n(H2) = 5e3 cm^-3) performed to assess collisional excitation, leading to exclusion of HCO+  
  > "To quantitatively assess the impact of collisional excitation, we performed RADEX (cite: vanderTak_2007) calculations adopting the physical conditions of the $z\!=\!0.68$ absorbing cloud ($T_\mathrm{kin}\!=\!55 \,\mathrm{K}$ and $n(\mathrm{H_2})\!=\!5\times10^3\, \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$) reported by (cite: Henkel_2005)." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC)
5. **Determine T_CMB(z=0.68) and constrain cosmological deviation parameter beta**: Compute weighted mean of HCN and HNC excitation temperatures assuming T_CMB = T_ex, and fit Lima et al. (2000) scaling relation T_CMB(z) = T_0(1+z)^(1-beta) to combined literature compilation  
  > "For this reason, we regard the HCN and HNC results as the most reliable tracers of the CMB temperature in the $z\!=\!0.68$ absorber toward the A image of B0218+357. We then applied the weighted-mean procedure described by Equation (\[meanTex\]) to the excitation temperatures of these two species." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC); "We fitted these data using the phenomenological model proposed by (cite: Lima_2000), $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:btcmb}
  T_\mathrm{CMB}(z)=T_0\,(1+z)^{1-\beta}, 
\end{equation}$$ and obtained ${\beta=(3.9^{+7.4}_{-8.2})\times10^{-3}}$." (Redshift Dependence of T_CMB)
   - software: SciPy, Matplotlib
   - parameter: T_0 = 2.72548 +/- 0.00057 K  
  > "The cosmic microwave background (CMB; (cite: Penzias_1965; Dicke_1965)) is a nearly uniform, isotropic blackbody radiation field with a current temperature of $2.72548\pm0.00057\,\mathrm{K}$ (cite: Fixsen_2009)." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Radiative equilibrium with the CMB (T_CMB = T_ex), negligible local dust emission and negligible collisional excitation for HCN and HNC in optically thin regime  
  > "Under these conditions, the rotational level populations of the highly polar molecules detected in this absorber, namely HCN, HCO$^+$, HNC, and their isotopologues, are in radiative equilibrium with the CMB." (T_CMB Calculations)
   - fitting: Weighted mean for T_CMB(z=0.68); fit of T_CMB(z) = T_0(1+z)^(1-beta) across ~80 literature points plus new measurement  
  > "We fitted these data using the phenomenological model proposed by (cite: Lima_2000), $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:btcmb}
  T_\mathrm{CMB}(z)=T_0\,(1+z)^{1-\beta}, 
\end{equation}$$ and obtained ${\beta=(3.9^{+7.4}_{-8.2})\times10^{-3}}$." (Redshift Dependence of T_CMB)
   - validation: Compared derived T_CMB with standard Big Bang prediction (4.59 K) and SZ measurements at z ~ 0.68  
  > "This value is in good agreement, within the uncertainties, with the prediction of the standard Big Bang model ($4.59~\mathrm{K}$)." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC)

## Qualitative findings

- Two distinct velocity components at V_bar = +5 km/s and -6 km/s are detected in HCN, HCO+, and HNC, while only the +5 km/s component is detected in H13CN and H13CO+, and an additional 0 km/s component is seen in HCO+ (both transitions) and HCN J=3-2.  
  > "In the absorption profiles of HCN, HCO$^{+}$, and HNC, at least two components are commonly observed, centered at $V_{\rm bar}\!=\!+5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ and $-6\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, respectively." (Optical Depth); "The $+5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ component is also visible in the profiles of H$^{13}$CN and H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$, whereas the $-6\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ component is not detected in either species." (Optical Depth)
- HC15N J=2-1 and J=3-2 transitions are not detected within the observed spectral windows.  
  > "The HC$^{15}$N *J*=2–1 and *J*=3–2 transitions also fall within the observed spectral windows, but neither line is detected." (Optical Depth)
- Thermal dust emission in the z=0.68 absorber is negligible compared to the CMB (intensity ratio ~10^-6 to 10^-5).  
  > "Thus, the dust radiation field is negligible compared to the CMB, and its contribution to the rotational excitation of the molecules considered in this study can be safely neglected in the $z\!=\!0.68$ absorption system." (T_CMB Calculations)
- HCO+ excitation temperature is biased high by partial collisional excitation (due to lower critical density) and/or contamination from the 0 km/s velocity component.  
  > "Because the excitation of HCO$^+$ is biased by an additional velocity component and partial collisional excitation, this species was excluded from the final determination of the CMB temperature." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: B0218+357 A hosts a rich intervening molecular absorption system at z = 0.68466 with diffuse molecular gas where rotational transitions of highly polar molecules (HCN, HCO+, HNC) are expected to be radiatively coupled to the CMB.  
  > "Along the line of sight toward the A component at $z=0.68466$, a rich molecular absorption system arising in the spiral galaxy has been detected, first discovered by (cite: WiklindCombes_1995)." (Introduction); "They pointed out that, under optically thin conditions, the excitation temperatures of the rotational transitions of these molecules are expected to provide a reliable probe of $T_\mathrm{CMB}(z\!=\!0.68)$." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Direct visibility fitting with UVMULTIFIT resolves the ~0.3 arcsec lensed components A and B that are not clearly resolved in the image plane; Monte Carlo sampling propagates covering factor uncertainty; lognormal N-PDF correction removes saturation bias from column-density nonuniformity.  
  > "Although the two lensed images were not clearly resolved in the image plane, the simple and well-known two-point-source geometry of this system, with a separation of $\!\sim\!0{\overset{\prime\prime}{.}}3$ on the sky, enables spectral extraction via visibility fitting in the Fourier plane (cite: MartiVidal_2014; Wallstrom_2019)." (Data Processing); "To rigorously account for both the indeterminacy and the possible frequency dependence of $f_\mathrm{c}$, we performed Monte Carlo calculations." (Continuum Covering Factor); "(cite: Kotani_2025) corrected this bias by assuming that the column density follows a lognormal probability distribution function (N-PDF) and by computing a correction factor relating the observed optical depth ($\tau_{\rm obs}$) to the averaged actual optical depth ($\tau_{\rm real}$)." (Correction for Nonuniformity)
- Alternative: Using the -6 km/s absorption component for T_CMB determination: rejected because it shows no evidence of saturation, preventing a meaningful constraint on f_c.  
  > "In contrast, the $-6\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ component shows no evidence of saturation, preventing a meaningful constraint on $f_\mathrm{c}$ in that velocity range; consequently, the associated $T_\mathrm{ex}$ remains uncertain." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC)
- Alternative: Including HCO+ in the final weighted mean of T_CMB: rejected because its excitation temperature is biased high by partial collisional excitation and/or blending with the 0 km/s component.  
  > "Because the excitation of HCO$^+$ is biased by an additional velocity component and partial collisional excitation, this species was excluded from the final determination of the CMB temperature." (Abstract)
- Alternative: Including H13CN and H13CO+ in the final weighted mean of T_CMB: rejected due to weak absorption and low signal-to-noise ratio (S/N ~ 3).  
  > "However, because the absorption is weak and the S/N is low for these isotopologues, we did not use them in the final estimate of the CMB temperature." (13C isotopologues)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The Band 3 and Band 4 observations were separated by ~9 months (October 2016 to July 2017) without simultaneous millimeter monitoring, though a few percent variability would shift T_CMB by < 0.1 K.  
  > "The Band 3 and Band 4 observations used in this study are separated by approximately nine months (from October 2016 to July 2017)" (Time Variability); "Even if variability at the level of a few percent is assumed toward the A image alone in this analysis, the resulting change in the inferred CMB temperature is less than 0.1 K, which is well within the final statistical uncertainty." (Time Variability)
- Limitation (authors): The -6 km/s component cannot constrain f_c due to lack of saturation, leaving its T_ex uncertain.  
  > "In contrast, the $-6\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ component shows no evidence of saturation, preventing a meaningful constraint on $f_\mathrm{c}$ in that velocity range; consequently, the associated $T_\mathrm{ex}$ remains uncertain." (HCN, HCO+, and HNC)
- Limitation (authors): H13CN and H13CO+ transitions have low S/N (~3), limiting their statistical significance.  
  > "The excitation temperatures derived from H$^{13}$CN and H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ are also consistent with the prediction of the standard Big Bang model, although their statistical significance is limited by the lower S/N of the data." (Summary and Future Prospects)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Independent sampling of f_c across transitions and velocity bins in the Monte Carlo procedure assumes no correlation between J=2-1 and J=3-2 covering factors; if f_c is strongly correlated or systematically frequency-dependent between Band 3 and Band 4, the true bias or uncertainty on the J=3-2/J=2-1 optical depth ratio could differ.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a lognormal N-PDF calibrated on the Galactic Orion A GMC (Ma et al. 2020) to a pencil-beam extragalactic absorption line of sight assumes identical turbulence/clumping statistics.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Excluding bins with tau >= 1 leaves only the line wings of the +5 km/s component (6 bins for HCN, 9 for HNC), where S/N is lower and velocity-wing blending with the 0 km/s component could still subtly affect HCN/HNC.
