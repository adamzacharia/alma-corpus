## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, star_formation
- Question: To test tidal truncation models by measuring the gas size of protoplanetary discs in multiple stellar systems in the Taurus region and comparing them with dust disc sizes and analytical truncation models.  
  > "Our aim is to test tidal truncation models by measuring for the first time the gas size of discs in multiple stellar systems in a uniform way, with the same instrument and a similar noise level at high spatial resolution." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct velocity maps and zeroth/first moment maps of CO emission**: Create zeroth and first moment maps of CO isotopologues, and compute velocity maps and relative uncertainties using the quadratic method with 4-sigma clipping  
  > "In particular, the “quadratic” method to image the velocity maps, created with *bettermoments* tools (cite: 2018Teague_Foreman-Mackey) and applying a 4 sigma clipping process, is shown." (Observations and data reduction); "fitting a Keplerian rotation pattern to the velocity map of the observations, taking into account the relative uncertainties calculated with the *bettermoments* tool (cite: 2018Teague_Foreman-Mackey)" (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
   - software: bettermoments, CASA
   - parameter: 4 sigma clipping for quadratic velocity maps  
  > "In particular, the “quadratic” method to image the velocity maps, created with *bettermoments* tools (cite: 2018Teague_Foreman-Mackey) and applying a 4 sigma clipping process, is shown." (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: Quadratic fit to line profiles per pixel via bettermoments  
  > "In particular, the “quadratic” method to image the velocity maps, created with *bettermoments* tools (cite: 2018Teague_Foreman-Mackey) and applying a 4 sigma clipping process, is shown." (Observations and data reduction)
   - validation: Visual inspection of channel maps and spectra to identify Keplerian rotation and foreground cloud absorption  
  > "The channel maps and the spectra of the $^{12}$CO emission for all targets in the sample are reported in Appendix 7 and are useful to check for absorption due to foreground cloud material in the data." (Observations and data reduction)
2. **Derive disc geometrical properties (centre, inclination, position angle, systemic velocity)**: Fit a geometrically thin Keplerian rotation pattern to the 12CO velocity map (13CO for DH Tau A) using eddy, excluding cloud-absorbed velocity channels; fit two elliptical Gaussians with CASA imfit for circumsecondary disc centres and RW Aur A; adopt literature PA and inclination for edge-on HK Tau B  
  > "To estimate these properties, we used the *eddy* tool (cite: 2019Teague), that aims at reconstructing the rotational profile of a gas emission line by fitting a Keplerian rotation pattern to the velocity map of the observations" (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling); "We account for cloud absorption in discs excluding from the fit the velocities absorbed by the cloud (see Table 3). Since the disc around DH Tau A is heavily absorbed in the red-shifted velocities, we only fit the $^{13}$CO velocity map of DH Tau." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling); "To avoid issues with the convergence of the *eddy* fit, we estimated the source centre of the circumsecondary discs fitting two elliptical Gaussian components to the $^{12}$CO image with the CASA task *imfit*." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
   - software: eddy, CASA imfit
   - parameter: 5 free parameters (ra, dec, PA, i, v_LSR) and 2 fixed parameters (M_star, d); three fits per target at M_star and +/-1 sigma M_star  
  > "In this way, *eddy* would fit five free parameters (ra, dec, PA, $i$ and the systemic velocity $v_\mathrm{LSR}$), given two fixed parameter ($M_\star$ and the distance $d$ of the disc from the observer)." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling); "we performed for each target three fits, one adopting the assumed value of $M_\star$, and one each for the $\pm$1$\sigma$ values of $M_\star$ given the uncertainty" (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
   - assumption: Geometrically thin disc in Keplerian rotation with fixed stellar mass and Gaia DR2 distance  
  > "fitting a Keplerian rotation pattern to the velocity map of the observations, taking into account the relative uncertainties calculated with the *bettermoments* tool (cite: 2018Teague_Foreman-Mackey) and assuming a geometrically thin disc." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
   - fitting: MCMC in eddy with flat priors on PA, i, v_LSR and Gaussian priors on target centre; initialized at moment 0 centre and Manara et al. (2019) PA and i; typically 200 walkers, 1000-2000 steps, last 800-1000 steps sampled  
  > "Flat priors for all parameters were chosen, with Gaussian priors only for the target center. The number of walkers and steps needed to achieve the convergence of the chain were typically $200$ walkers and $\sim1000$-$2000$ steps, with the last $\sim800$-$1000$ steps used to sample the posterior distribution." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
   - validation: Compared source centres, PA, and inclinations with uv-plane continuum fits from Manara et al. (2019), finding agreement within 3 sigma except for compact/uncertain discs  
  > "We also compared the position angle and inclination obtained fitting the gas emission with *eddy* with the values obtained by (cite: 2019Manara+) fitting the continuum data in the uv-plane. This comparison is shown in Figure 4. The values obtained with the two methods are in agreement within the (3$\sigma$) uncertainties" (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
3. **Measure total fluxes and gas and dust disc radii (R68 and R95)**: Apply the cumulative flux (curve of growth) aperture photometry technique on 12CO, 13CO, C18O zeroth moment maps and continuum images using concentric elliptical annuli (or rectangular apertures for HK Tau B, half-disc masks for DH Tau A and UZ Tau Wa/Wb, and imfit-constrained total flux for RW Aur A/B)  
  > "To estimate the disc radii, we perform an image plane analysis using the cumulative flux technique (cite: 2018Ansdell+), computing aperture photometry on the zeroth moment images of the $^{12}$CO emission and on the continuum images using the CASA tool *imstat*." (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique); "Once the maximum flux is calculated, we estimate the disc radius $R_\mathrm{disc}$ (or $R_{95}$) as the radius containing $95\%$ of the total flux and the effective disc radius $R_\mathrm{eff}$ (or $R_{68}$) as that containing $68\%$ of the total flux." (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique)
   - software: CASA imstat, CASA imfit
   - parameter: Radial step dr = 0.05'', 100 random background annuli for noise, 0.075'' added in quadrature to radius uncertainty; fixed height ~0.25'' for HK Tau B rectangular apertures  
  > "The annuli are increased in step of $\mathrm{d}r=$ 0.05$''$ (one-third of the angular resolution of the data), and the flux uncertainty in each annulus is estimated as the standard deviation of the fluxes in 100 random annuli selected well outside the disc." (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique); "The uncertainty on the radius is calculated considering the statistical error on the maximum flux and, since the error in the radius estimate must be at least half of the resolution, we summed in quadrature this error with 0.075$''$." (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique)
   - assumption: Elliptical apertures projected according to PA and inclination from Table 5  
  > "for each target the flux is calculated summing over concentric annuli centred in the source centre and corrected for the projected position of the disc" (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique)
   - fitting: Two-Gaussian fit with CASA imfit used to constrain total 12CO flux of RW Aur A and B  
  > "the $^{12}$CO total flux of the RW Aur A and B discs was assumed to be the enclosed flux closest to the one estimated through the CASA tool *imfit* (two-Gaussian fit) – $3.661 \pm 0.094$ Jy km/s and $0.949 \pm 0.076$ Jy km/s, respectively." (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique)
   - validation: Compared RW Aur effective gas radii with Rodriguez et al. (2018); compared new continuum fluxes and radii with Manara et al. (2019) continuum data and uv-plane Galario fits, modeling 1D Gaussian beam convolution effects  
  > "The effective disc radii estimated for the RW Aur system are in very good agreement with the ones reported in (cite: 2018Rodriguez+)" (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique); "We then compare the measured sizes of the discs on the old and new data, accounting for the differences in resolution and method." (Comparison between new continuum data and older continuum data)
4. **Compare gas-to-dust disc size ratios with isolated systems**: Compute R68,gas/R68,dust and R95,gas/R95,dust using Galario dust radii from Manara et al. (2019) (excluding edge-on HK Tau B), and perform two-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests between primary and secondary discs and against Lupus single discs from Sanchis et al. (2021)

   - parameter: HK Tau B excluded due to edge-on optical depth effects  
  > "We thus decided to exclude HK Tau B disc from the statistical analysis discussed below." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust size ratios are uncorrelated with stellar mass, allowing unbiased comparison with Lupus  
  > "For the sake of this work, the lack of any correlation between these two quantities assures that we can carry out a comparison between our results with those by (cite: 2021Sanchis+) with no biases due to different stellar mass distributions." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Two-sided K-S tests evaluating statistical compatibility of the distributions  
  > "The K-S test performed on these two samples confirms that the gas-to-dust size ratios in Taurus multiples are likely to be drawn by the same distribution of Lupus single discs (p-value $\sim 0.13$)." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii)
5. **Compare gas disc radii with tidal truncation models and constrain minimum orbital eccentricities and disc misalignment**: Compare observed R95,gas/a_p with analytical tidal truncation models of Artymowicz & Lubow (1994) to derive minimum orbital eccentricities e_min, and compute relative disc inclinations using the spherical law of cosines  
  > "To better quantify this result, we compare $R_{95,\mathrm{gas}}/a_\mathrm{p}$ with theoretical models by (cite: 1994Artymowicz_Lubow), using the equation derived by (cite: 2019Manara+) under the assumption that the discs and the binary orbit are co-planar:" (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models); "However, the degree of alignment should be explored using the relative inclination of the discs, computed using the spherical law of cosines: $\cos(\Delta i)=\cos(i_1)\cdot \cos(i_2)+\sin(i_1) \sin(i_2) \cos(\Omega_1 - \Omega_2)$" (Disc misalignment)

  > "The dominant uncertainty on the eccentricity estimated in this way comes from the lack of information on the local Reynolds number, and hence different Reynolds numbers are assumed – $\mathcal{R} = 10^4, 10^5, 10^6$." (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models)
   - assumption: Co-planar discs and binary orbit, zero orbital inclination (i = 0 deg) with secondary at apoastron for minimum eccentricity; UZ Tau treated as two binaries (UZ Tau E - UZ Tau Wab with a_p = 3.52'', and UZ Tau Wa - UZ Tau Wb with a_p = 0.375'')  
  > "Since the UZ Tau system is a quadruple system (with UZ Tau E being a spectroscopic binary), when comparing observations with analytical models, we consider the UZ Tau system as two binaries." (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models); "The intersection between the observed $R_\mathrm{t}/a_\mathrm{p}$ (red bands in Figure 14) and the theoretical model assuming that the secondary is observed at apoastron (lower black curves in the figure) represents the minimum possible value of the eccentricity for the system." (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models)

## Qualitative findings

- Gas disc radii in Taurus multiple systems are ~2-4 times larger than dust disc radii, with average ratios R68,gas/R68,dust = 3.2 +/- 1.1 and R95,gas/R95,dust = 4.2 +/- 1.6.  
  > "Our sample of multiple system reveals that the gas disc radii are typically $\sim2-4$ times larger than the dust disc radii, for both the effective gas-to-dust radii $R_{68, \mathrm{gas}}/R_{68, \mathrm{dust}}$ and the gas-to-dust disc radii $R_{95, \mathrm{gas}}/R_{95, \mathrm{dust}}$" (Comparing dust and gas disc radii); "The average value (and relative standard deviation) of $R_{68, \mathrm{gas}}/R_{68, \mathrm{dust}}$ is $3.2\pm1.1$ ($3.1\pm1.1$ and $3.4\pm0.9$ for circumprimary and circumsecondary discs, respectively), and $R_{95, \mathrm{gas}}/R_{95, \mathrm{dust}}=4.2\pm1.6$" (Comparing dust and gas disc radii)
- The 68% gas-to-dust size ratio distribution in Taurus multiple systems is statistically compatible with single discs in Lupus (K-S p-value ~0.13), whereas the 95% gas-to-dust size ratio is significantly larger (K-S p-value ~0.012).  
  > "The K-S test performed on these two samples confirms that the gas-to-dust size ratios in Taurus multiples are likely to be drawn by the same distribution of Lupus single discs (p-value $\sim 0.13$)." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii); "On the contrary, the $R_{95, \mathrm{gas}}/R_{95, \mathrm{dust}}$ average ratio in Taurus multiple systems is larger than the ratio estimated by (cite: 2021Sanchis+), with the K-S test confirming the two samples to be statistically different (p-value $\sim 0.012$)." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii)
- Observed 95% gas disc radii are typically 0.15-0.35 times the projected separation, yielding minimum orbital eccentricities of ~0.15-0.5 (average ~0.3), consistent with main-sequence binary eccentricities without requiring highly eccentric (e > 0.7) orbits.  
  > "In general, the 95% gas disc radii are $\sim 0.15-0.35$ times the projected separation of the binaries, suggesting that the systems are not in circular orbits." (Conclusions); "Excluding these systems, the estimated minimum eccentricities show typical values of $\sim 0.15-0.5$ and an average value of $\sim 0.3$, in very good agreement with the observed distribution of eccentricities for main-sequence binary systems of low-mass (cite: 2013Duchene_Kraus)." (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models)
- Most binaries where both components are resolved (RW Aur, DK Tau, HK Tau) are misaligned (relative inclinations ~25 deg to ~50 deg), while UZ Tau discs are better aligned (<= 0-20 deg), suggesting formation via turbulent fragmentation.  
  > "The discs in the UZ Tau system show relative inclination differences $\lesssim 0-20 \si{\ensuremath{^\circ}}$, while the RW Aur, DK Tau and HK Tau systems show a larger relative inclination ($\sim 25 \si{\ensuremath{^\circ}}$, $\sim 40 \si{\ensuremath{^\circ}}$, and $\sim 50 \si{\ensuremath{^\circ}}$, respectively)." (Disc misalignment); "We conclude that most of the observed binaries appear not to be coplanar, possibly a result of turbulent fragmentation, rather than disc fragmentation." (Disc misalignment)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Longer integration time (~40 min/source) in ALMA Band 6 at ~0.15'' resolution was needed on the Manara et al. (2019) multiple-system sample to detect CO molecular emission lines with high SNR and measure gas disc sizes.  
  > "Our aim is to test tidal truncation models by measuring for the first time the gas size of discs in multiple stellar systems in a uniform way, with the same instrument and a similar noise level at high spatial resolution." (Introduction); "In fact, the aim of the new observations was to detect different molecular emission lines with a higher signal-to-noise (SNR)." (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: The cumulative flux technique on zeroth moment maps captures the outer gas disc extent where emission is faint without assuming a parametric profile, while Galario uv-plane dust radii from Manara et al. (2019) achieve sub-beam resolution and enable direct comparison with Sanchis et al. (2021).  
  > "As shown by the red solid ellipses, the radii estimated with the cumulative flux technique get the really outer extent of the discs, where little emission is detected, giving the possibility to test tidal truncation models." (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique); "For the following discussion, we use the values of $R_\mathrm{dust}$ obtained by (cite: 2019Manara+) by modelling the continuum data in the visibility plane using the *Galario* library (cite: 2018Tazzari+) because this makes the results directly comparable with (cite: 2021Sanchis+), who performed a similar analysis." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii); "Since in the visibility plane analysis a resolution under the beam size is achievable, the dust radii estimated using *Galario* are more reliable, especially for the more compact discs." (Comparison between new continuum data and older continuum data)
- Alternative: Using cumulative flux dust radii from the image plane instead of Galario uv-plane dust radii for the main gas-to-dust comparison; rejected because uv-plane fitting achieves sub-beam resolution and matches Sanchis et al. (2021).  
  > "For the following discussion, we use the values of $R_\mathrm{dust}$ obtained by (cite: 2019Manara+) by modelling the continuum data in the visibility plane using the *Galario* library (cite: 2018Tazzari+) because this makes the results directly comparable with (cite: 2021Sanchis+), who performed a similar analysis." (Comparing dust and gas disc radii); "Since in the visibility plane analysis a resolution under the beam size is achievable, the dust radii estimated using *Galario* are more reliable, especially for the more compact discs." (Comparison between new continuum data and older continuum data)
- Alternative: Fitting HK Tau B with eddy and measuring its gas radius with elliptical annuli; rejected because HK Tau B is edge-on and vertically extended, so rectangular apertures along the major axis and literature PA/inclination were used instead.  
  > "We did not fit the disc around the secondary star of the HK Tau system. This disc is almost edge-on, so that the assumption of geometrically thin disc emission cannot be applied and the vertical height of the disc must be considered." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling); "To correct for this effect, the disc radius is measured along the major axis, centering on the source rectangles with increasing width and fixed height" (Disc radii estimate: the cumulative flux technique)
- How they decided it was enough: Stopped phase-only self-calibration rounds when no significant SNR improvement occurred from one step to the next; ran eddy MCMC chains for ~1000-2000 steps with 200 walkers until convergence.  
  > "In each EB we applied from one to four rounds of phase-only self-calibration, stopping the calibration when no significant improvement was measured in the SNR from one step to the next one." (Observations and data reduction); "The number of walkers and steps needed to achieve the convergence of the chain were typically $200$ walkers and $\sim1000$-$2000$ steps, with the last $\sim800$-$1000$ steps used to sample the posterior distribution." (Geometrical properties of discs from Keplerian modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Only the C43-6 configuration was executed (C43-3 was not executed), limiting the maximum recoverable scale to ~1.8''.  
  > "Two configurations were requested, C43-6 and C43-3, but only observations in the former configuration were executed. Therefore, the maximum recoverable scale of the observations was only $\sim$1.8$^{\prime\prime}$." (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Foreground cloud absorption affects 12CO emission near systemic or redshifted velocities in almost all discs, particularly DH Tau A where the southern half is totally absorbed.  
  > "Signatures of partial absorption around the systemic velocity is observed in almost all discs; absorption in the red-shifted velocities is observed in the disc around CIDA 9 A, while the red-shifted velocities around DH Tau A are totally absorbed by the cloud below the continuum level." (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): T Tau and UY Aur do not show Keplerian rotation patterns and could not be analysed for individual disc radii.  
  > "Finally, the observation of UY Aur and T Tau do not show a velocity pattern compatible with Keplerian rotation (see Section 3), making it difficult to determine if the gas emission is reflecting the disc dynamics, and therefore to estimate the radius of individual discs." (Sample properties)
- Limitation (authors): Analytical co-planar face-on tidal truncation models cannot explain the large R95,gas/a_p values in CIDA 9 A, UZ Tau, and RW Aur without high orbital inclination or additional physical processes (substructures, tidal interactions).  
  > "The tidal truncation models assuming face-on inclination of the orbit cannot explain the observed large values of $R_\mathrm{t}/a_\mathrm{p}$ in the CIDA 9, UZ Tau, and RW Aur systems, except assuming unrealistic parameters ($e\sim 1$ and targets observed at the periastron)" (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size with measured gas-to-dust size ratios and eccentricities is too small to definitively test a correlation between size ratio and orbital eccentricity.  
  > "However, to definitively refute a dependence of the gas-dust size ratio on the eccentricity, it is necessary to significantly increase the sample of systems with measured size ratio and eccentricity." (Comparison of gas disc radii with tidal truncation models)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The maximum recoverable scale of ~1.8'' is smaller than the derived R95,gas of UZ Tau E (2.40'') and comparable to CIDA 9 A (1.70''), meaning extended outer gas emission could be affected by spatial filtering.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing non-parametric image-plane cumulative flux gas radii with parametric/deconvolved uv-plane Galario dust radii mixes two different resolution regimes, and also differs from the parametric Gaussian/Nuker gas profile fitting used in the Lupus comparison sample (Sanchis et al. 2021).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The analytical tidal truncation comparison assumes coplanarity between the discs and the binary orbit, whereas Section 5.3 shows that most of the resolved binaries have significant mutual disc misalignments (25-50 deg).
