## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and export ALMA continuum visibilities**: Obtain raw data from the ALMA Science Archive, process to calibrated visibility sets, split and bin in time and channels, and export as a single continuum UVFITS table per galaxy.  
  > "We obtained the raw data from the ALMA Science Archive and processed them to produce calibrated visibility sets, which were then split and binned in both time and channels, and finally exported as a single continuum UVFITS table per QG with GILDAS[^1]." (Sample and data)
   - software: GILDAS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Construct extended stellar distribution models from HST F160W imaging**: Extract cutouts centered on QGs from HST/WFC3 F160W images, model and subtract nearby objects using GALFIT, and isolate the QG with SExtractor while zeroing out pixels below 3 sigma of the background.  
  > "Cutouts centered on the QGs were extracted from the F160W images and the light of nearby objects was modeled and subtracted using GALFIT (cite: Peng10)." (Analysis); "All pixels below 3$\sigma$ of the background value were then zeroed out, using SEXTRACTOR (cite: BA96) on the residual image to isolate the QG." (Analysis)
   - software: GALFIT, SEXTRACTOR
   - parameter: Background threshold = 3 sigma  
  > "All pixels below 3$\sigma$ of the background value were then zeroed out, using SEXTRACTOR (cite: BA96) on the residual image to isolate the QG." (Analysis)
   - assumption: Dust emission is coterminous with the rest-frame optical stellar distribution traced by F160W.  
  > "Namely, we allowed for extended dust emission coterminous with the stellar distribution, with or without an additional compact component, and we converted the measured intensities to dust masses using an empirical FIR SED derived from larger statistical QG samples." (Conclusions)
   - fitting: GALFIT modeling of nearby objects  
  > "Cutouts centered on the QGs were extracted from the F160W images and the light of nearby objects was modeled and subtracted using GALFIT (cite: Peng10)." (Analysis)
   - validation: Examined VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopy of MRG-0138 and MRG-1341 and found no appreciable difference between inner and outer spectral regions.  
  > "As a test, we examined resolved, integral field spectroscopy of two of the most extended galaxies in the sample, MRG-0138 and MRG-1341 (see Appendix [\[appendix:muse\]](#appendix:muse)). Within the wavelength range of the spectra, we found no appreciable difference between the bright central regions of these galaxies and their extended outer regions, which would for example suggest that one contains a larger fraction of younger stars than the former." (Analysis)
3. **Extract 1.3 mm continuum fluxes by uv-plane visibility modeling**: Fit the complex visibilities in the uv-plane using the primary-beam-attenuated F160W stellar light model (plus optional central point source when residuals > 3 sigma, and simultaneous modeling of bright field sources) as well as pure point-source fits. Estimate uncertainties from the r.m.s. dispersion of 1000 random off-center extractions.  
  > "Given an intensity distribution on the sky of $M(x,y)$, where $x$ and $y$ are spatial coordinates from the phase center, we modeled the complex visibility as" (uv plane modeling); "In cases where the residuals of the extended model fit contained noticeable ($>$3$\sigma$) leftover emission, we also allowed for excess central emission in the form of an additional point source with free amplitude for a total of two components per fit in this case." (Analysis); "Uncertainties (and thus upper limits) for model and point source fluxes were estimated from the r.m.s. dispersion of a thousand extractions performed with each component at random positions with large offsets from phase center (that is, staying well clear of the target galaxy and other sources within the field of view)." (Analysis)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Free astrometric offset (< 0.15 arcsec for detections)  
  > "To account for astrometry differences between the F160W and ALMA data, the fit allowed for a free offset. For detected objects, this yielded offsets of $<$0.15", which is significantly smaller than the spatial resolution of the ALMA data." (Analysis)
   - parameter: 1000 random off-center extractions for r.m.s. error estimation; 3 sigma upper limits for non-detections  
  > "Uncertainties (and thus upper limits) for model and point source fluxes were estimated from the r.m.s. dispersion of a thousand extractions performed with each component at random positions with large offsets from phase center (that is, staying well clear of the target galaxy and other sources within the field of view). The resulting magnified fluxes and errors, or 3$\sigma$ upper limits, are shown in Table [\[tab:results\]](#tab:results)." (Analysis)
   - assumption: Primary beam response is B(x,y) = (2 J1(theta)/theta)^2  
  > "where $B(x,y) = (2J_1(\theta)/\theta)^2$ is the primary beam response of the antennas, with $J_1$ the first order Bessel J function, $\theta=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}/\text{HWBN}$, and HWBN is the half-beam width at first null." (uv plane modeling)
   - assumption: For MRG-0138, two point-source components have relative amplitudes fixed by the ratio of mean magnifications between the two lensed images from N18, and results are corrected to the magnification of the first image.  
  > "One exception was MRG-0138, where the lensed arc targeted by the ALMA observation is a combination of two images bisected by a critical line. We did not attempt to separate them, but rather counted them as a single image, thus requiring the use of two point-source components, with relative amplitudes fixed by the ratio of mean magnifications between the two images given in N18." (Analysis)
   - fitting: Direct uv-plane complex visibility modeling of extended stellar light models and/or point sources  
  > "Given an intensity distribution on the sky of $M(x,y)$, where $x$ and $y$ are spatial coordinates from the phase center, we modeled the complex visibility as" (uv plane modeling)
   - validation: Sanity check by extracting 1.3 mm fluxes with a point-source fit without the extended model for direct comparison with W21.  
  > "As a sanity check and to allow for a direct comparison with W21, we also extracted 1.3 mm fluxes by fitting a point source without the total light model." (Analysis)
4. **Convert 1.3 mm continuum fluxes into dust masses**: Convert magnified 1.3 mm fluxes (both extended model and point-source) to magnified dust masses using the average FIR SED template for z > 0 QGs from M21 sampled at 1.3 mm, and compare against the S16 calibration.  
  > "We then converted these fluxes into (magnified) dust masses using the average FIR SED template for $z>0$ QGs of M21, sampled at 1.3 mm." (Dust masses); "In order of complexity, they are as follows: point-source extraction converted to a dust mass using S16, point-source extraction converted to dust mass using the M21 SED, and an extended model fit converted to dust using M21." (Dust masses)
   - parameter: Dust temperature Td = 21 K (M21 template)  
  > "This model has a dust temperature of $T_d=21$ K, which is a few Kelvin lower than the mass-weighted $T_d$ typically used for SF galaxies (cite: Sco16; Sco17)." (Dust masses)
   - assumption: Concordance cosmology with H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "We assumed a concordance cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\text{M}}=0.3$, and $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$, as well as a (cite: Sal55) initial mass function (IMF), with quantities from the literature having been converted to this IMF." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared three combinations of flux extraction and conversion methods (point+S16, point+M21, extended+M21).  
  > "In order of complexity, they are as follows: point-source extraction converted to a dust mass using S16, point-source extraction converted to dust mass using the M21 SED, and an extended model fit converted to dust using M21." (Dust masses)
5. **Recompute stellar masses and derive dust-to-stellar mass ratios**: Extract HST photometry with SExtractor in dual-image mode using the F160W model as reference, deblend Spitzer/IRAC photometry via multicomponent decomposition with MPFIT using PSF-convolved F160W and GALFIT neighbor models, fit SEDs with BC03 templates, and compute Md/M* ratios.  
  > "That is, we extracted photometry from HST images using SEXTRACTOR in dual-image mode, with the F160W model as a reference. For *Spitzer* data, which have significantly lower resolution and are thus often blended, we instead performed a multicomponent decomposition[^2] of the image, using the F160W model and GALFIT models of nearby objects convolved with the point response functions of *Spitzer*/IRAC." (Stellar masses); "We then modeled these SEDs with (cite: BC03) stellar population templates, assuming star formation histories (SFHs) with exponential cutoffs, as already used for high-redshift QGs (cite: Sch18; Val20)." (Stellar masses)
   - software: SEXTRACTOR, GALFIT, MPFIT
   - parameter: SFH start at z = 10; tau in (0, 3] Gyr; t_q in [1, 3] Gyr; tau_q in [0.1, 1.] Gyr  
  > "Here we fixed the start of the SFH at $z=10$ and assumed parameter ranges of $(0,3]$ Gyr for $\tau$, $[1,3]$ Gyr for $t_q$, and $[0.1,1.]$ Gyr for $\tau_q$." (Comparison of stellar masses)
   - assumption: Salpeter (1955) IMF  
  > "We assumed a concordance cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\text{M}}=0.3$, and $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$, as well as a (cite: Sal55) initial mass function (IMF), with quantities from the literature having been converted to this IMF." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Comparable source-plane distributions of dust and stars so that Md/M* is independent of lensing magnification mu  
  > "Under the assumption that interstellar dust and stars have comparable distributions in the source plane, the dust-to-stellar mass ratio $M_d/M_{\star}$ does not depend on the lensing magnification $\mu$." (Results)
   - assumption: Spectroscopic metallicity priors from N18 and Man21  
  > "We assumed metallicity priors from spectroscopy (cite: New18; Man21) and delayed exponentially declining SFHs with an exponential cutoff of the form" (Comparison of stellar masses)
   - fitting: SED template fitting with BC03 models and delayed exponentially declining SFHs with exponential cutoff; Spitzer deblending with MPFIT.  
  > "We assumed metallicity priors from spectroscopy (cite: New18; Man21) and delayed exponentially declining SFHs with an exponential cutoff of the form" (Comparison of stellar masses); "[^2]: with MPFIT (cite: MPF)" (Comparison of stellar masses)
   - validation: Compared derived stellar masses with previously published values from the literature (Fig. D.1).  
  > "We checked that the stellar masses thusly obtained are consistent with those from the literature." (Stellar masses); "The stellar masses thusly derived from the SEDs are consistent with those published in previous works and used in W21, except for MRG-1341, where our estimate is almost a factor 2 higher." (Comparison of stellar masses)

## Software

- **GILDAS** : Splitting, binning in time and channels, and exporting calibrated ALMA visibility sets as continuum UVFITS tables  
  > "We obtained the raw data from the ALMA Science Archive and processed them to produce calibrated visibility sets, which were then split and binned in both time and channels, and finally exported as a single continuum UVFITS table per QG with GILDAS[^1]." (Sample and data)
- **GALFIT** : Modeling and subtracting the light of nearby objects in HST F160W cutouts and for Spitzer multicomponent decomposition  
  > "Cutouts centered on the QGs were extracted from the F160W images and the light of nearby objects was modeled and subtracted using GALFIT (cite: Peng10)." (Analysis); "For *Spitzer* data, which have significantly lower resolution and are thus often blended, we instead performed a multicomponent decomposition[^2] of the image, using the F160W model and GALFIT models of nearby objects convolved with the point response functions of *Spitzer*/IRAC." (Stellar masses)
- **SEXTRACTOR** : Isolating target galaxies in residual F160W images and extracting multi-band HST photometry in dual-image mode  
  > "All pixels below 3$\sigma$ of the background value were then zeroed out, using SEXTRACTOR (cite: BA96) on the residual image to isolate the QG." (Analysis); "That is, we extracted photometry from HST images using SEXTRACTOR in dual-image mode, with the F160W model as a reference." (Stellar masses)
- **MPFIT** : Multicomponent decomposition of blended Spitzer/IRAC images  
  > "For *Spitzer* data, which have significantly lower resolution and are thus often blended, we instead performed a multicomponent decomposition[^2] of the image, using the F160W model and GALFIT models of nearby objects convolved with the point response functions of *Spitzer*/IRAC." (Stellar masses); "[^2]: with MPFIT (cite: MPF)" (Comparison of stellar masses)
- **MUSE pipeline** : Reducing raw VLT/MUSE integral field spectroscopic data into calibrated data cubes  
  > "The raw data were reduced using the standard MUSE pipeline (cite: MUSE) to produce corrected and calibrated data cubes." (Spatially resolved spectroscopy)
- **Zurich Atmosphere Purge (ZAP)** : Improving sky subtraction on VLT/MUSE data cubes  
  > "We then applied the `Zurich Atmosphere Purge` tool (cite: ZAP) to improve the sky subtraction and, for each cluster, matched the world coordinate system (WCS) of the data cube (based on the white image) to that of the HST/WFC3 F160W image." (Spatially resolved spectroscopy)

## Qualitative findings

- Both the FIR flux extraction method (point source vs. extended stellar model) and the light-to-mass conversion factor (S16 vs. M21 SED) strongly impact derived dust fractions, combining to produce up to an order of magnitude variation.  
  > "We found that, while some tension with SED-derived results might still exist, the method used for FIR flux extraction and the light-to-mass conversion factor both have a significant impact on the estimate of their dust fractions, with both adding up to an order of magnitude variation." (Conclusions)
- Using extended stellar light models and the M21 SED makes the Md/M* constraints of the REQUIEM-ALMA sample broadly consistent with the 0.08-0.09% values derived from FIR stacks of unlensed QGs.  
  > "Under our physically motivated assumptions, the constraints on the dust fraction of this sample are broadly consistent with the $M_d/M_{\star}\sim0.08-0.09$% values derived by G18 and M21 for coeval QGs from lower-resolution FIR stacks." (Conclusions)
- MRG-0138 remains almost an order of magnitude more dust-poor than the other five QGs even under the extended model and M21 SED analysis, which may reflect an anticorrelation between ISM fraction and stellar mass.  
  > "The notable exception is MRG-0138 which, even in this new analysis, remains almost an order of magnitude more dust-poor than the other five QGs." (Results); "Its tension with the stacked results of G18 and M21 might then be explained by an anticorrelation between ISM mass and stellar mass being already present at this epoch, which would be largely diluted with them being averaged over large samples with relatively broad stellar mass ranges." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The REQUIEM-ALMA sample of six strongly lensed QGs was previously used by W21 to infer very low dust/gas fractions in tension with FIR SED stacks, making it the direct benchmark needed to test whether methodology drives the discrepancy.  
  > "To remedy this doubt, we revisited the lensed QG sample of W21 using a methodology consistent with that of G18 and M21." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Using HST F160W stellar light models for uv-plane flux extraction reproduces the effective aperture of low-resolution FIR SED stacks (minus neighbor contamination), and the M21 FIR SED template (Td = 21 K) reflects the softer radiation field expected in quiescent galaxies.  
  > "The use of these stellar light models should therefore come closest to reproducing the effective aperture of the SED stacks in M21, minus the potential residual contamination by nearby sources." (Analysis); "In addition to being consistent with the methodology of M21, with which we compare in this study, we consider it a more likely description of the ISM of QGs which, given their quiescence, should be subjected to a softer radiation field than in SF galaxies." (Dust masses)
- Alternative: Extended model fit combined with the Scoville et al. (2016) star-forming calibration was omitted for clarity because it falls between the other three tested combinations and can be easily inferred from them.  
  > "For clarity, we do not include the fourth possible combination here, that is, the extended model fit with the S16 calibration, as it falls between the first and third method and can be easily inferred from the first three (see Sect. [\[results\]](#results))." (Dust masses)
- Alternative: Separating the two lensed images of MRG-0138 bisected by a critical line was rejected in favor of modeling them as a single combined image with two point-source components of fixed relative amplitude.  
  > "One exception was MRG-0138, where the lensed arc targeted by the ALMA observation is a combination of two images bisected by a critical line. We did not attempt to separate them, but rather counted them as a single image, thus requiring the use of two point-source components, with relative amplitudes fixed by the ratio of mean magnifications between the two images given in N18." (Analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Zeroing out pixels below 3 sigma in the F160W cutouts to avoid image noise might cut out some of the galaxies' extended stellar profiles.  
  > "We note that, while this threshold was chosen to avoid including image noise in the model, it might cut out some of the galaxies’ extended profiles." (Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): If dust and stars do not have comparable distributions in the source plane, differential lensing magnification across images would affect Md/M* and require a full lensing model.  
  > "We note that, in any other case, correctly estimating $M_d/M_{\star}$ would require the usage of a full lensing model to account for differential magnification across images." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Four of the six galaxies remain undetected at 3 sigma, leaving upper limits that are compatible with much lower ISM fractions and preventing firm conclusions on quenching scenarios.  
  > "However, we caution that, while at least one object (MRG-2129) has 0.13% dust, implying $f_{\text{gas}}\sim10$%, the upper limits derived here remain compatible with much lower ISM fractions, which makes it difficult to draw firm conclusions on quenching scenarios from these data." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): VLT/MUSE spectra only cover the rest-frame near-ultraviolet, which is suboptimal for reconstructing star formation histories and insensitive to completely dust-shrouded stellar populations.  
  > "However, we caution that, although the absorption features covered by the MUSE spectra correlate with a combination of age and metallicity (cite: Fan92; Mar09), being limited to the near-ultraviolet, which is most sensitive to young massive stars, makes them suboptimal for reconstituting SFHs. In addition, some stellar populations could be invisible if, for example, they are entirely shrouded by dust." (Spatially resolved spectroscopy)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The preference for the extended stellar model over a point-source model in MRG-2129 is not statistically significant in the residuals, meaning the higher flux and dust mass depend on an unverified prior that dust follows the optical stellar light.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-band continuum photometry on the Rayleigh-Jeans tail (1.3 mm) cannot independently constrain the dust temperature Td; the factor of 3-3.5 increase in dust mass is entirely driven by adopting Td = 21 K from M21 instead of the S16 calibration.
