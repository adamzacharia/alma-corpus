- In core #2, the blue- and red-shifted velocity components of O-bearing molecules (CH3OCHO, CH3OH, OCS) do not peak at the continuum core center but coincide with the bipolar outflow lobes.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: W43-MM1 hosts a large number of massive cores at 2000 au scales, making it an ideal laboratory to study massive star formation; Band 6 spw7 (~2 GHz around 233 GHz) is rich in COM lines without strong contamination from simple molecules, while Band 3 covers the K-ladders of thermometer molecules CH3CN and CH3CCH at matched ~0.46'' resolution.  
  > "The large number of massive cores detected in W43-MM1 make it an ideal laboratory for understanding the physical processes and chemical evolution involved in the formation of massive stars." (Introduction); "To highlight the presence of hot cores, we focus on the analysis of the “continuum” band (spw7) at 233 GHz, because this band offers a large band width ($\sim$ 2 GHz) not contaminated by strong emission lines coming from the simplest molecules (like H$_2$CO, CO or SiO) but mainly by COMs." (Hot core identification from the spatial distribution of COMs); "Methyl cyanide (CH$_3$CN) and methyl acetylene (CH$_3$CCH) are considered as two good thermometers, as long as the lines are optically thin, because their emission K-ladder lines are close in frequency and cover a large enough range of upper level energies $E_{\rm u}$ (cite: giannetti17)." (Core temperatures)
- Why this method: Pixel-based and core-integrated COM line richness methods identify hot cores without requiring prior line identification or core velocity determinations; scaling by CH3OCHO doublets and channel-by-channel correlation plots allow quantitative comparison of chemical composition without lengthy full physical/chemical modelling of every line.  
  > "To identify hot cores, we used two different approaches, one which uses molecules known to trace hot cores (see Sect. 3.5) and another one which does not need any line identification and which relies on the richness of the hot core spectra in COM lines." (Identification of hot cores); "The MF doublets transitions have similar $E_{\rm u}$ levels (99 to 109 K) and are therefore most probably tracing the same volume of gas." (Similarity of the normalised spectra); "A full modelling of each source in terms of physical structure and chemical composition would be the ideal approach but it is a lengthy process.
