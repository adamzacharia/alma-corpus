## Analysis recipe

1. **Baseline subtraction and noise estimate**: Baselines fitted by regression to line-free spectral intervals and subtracted; mean rms assigned to whole spectrum  
  > "Before processing, we subtracted baselines from each spectrum." (Data and method); "The baseline was defined from the regression analysis of the mean signals from spectral intervals without emission lines and/or noise spikes." (Data and method)
   - assumption: Individual rms uncertainties of the same order, so their mean is used for the whole spectrum  
  > "Since individual *rms* uncertainties were of the same order of magnitude, their mean value was assigned to the whole spectrum." (Data and method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit line profiles of J0439+1634**: Single-component Gaussian fit to each of [CII], [CI], CO(6-5), CO(7-6), CO(9-8); CO(10-9) excluded because blended  
  > "Each individual line was fitted by a single-component Gaussian model (shown by red)." (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519); "We do not use the last one since it is blended with the H" (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519)
   - assumption: Alpha is kept fixed, so Delta F/F = -Delta mu/mu  
  > "Therefore, in this paper we will assume that $\alpha$ is kept fixed." (Data and method)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per line; centroid and FWHM free  
  > "Each individual line was fitted by a single-component Gaussian model (shown by red)." (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519)
   - validation: Chi-squared per degree of freedom of about 1  
  > "with the minimum values of $\chi^2$ per degrees of freedom of about 1" (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519)
3. **Delta mu/mu from J0439+1634 at z=6.519**: Average redshifts separately of fine-structure lines and of CO lines, then Delta mu/mu = (<z>_rot - <z>_fs)/(1 + zbar)  
  > "To estimate an upper limit on $\Delta\mu/\mu$, at first we average separately the redshifts of the fine structure lines" (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519); "It gives $\langle z \rangle_{\rm fs} = 6.51885(18)$ and $\langle z \rangle_{\rm rot} = 6.51885(9)$." (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519)
   - assumption: Doppler noise is normally distributed with zero mean and finite variance, so averaging over lines gives the signal  
  > "The Doppler noise is supposed to be normally distributed with a zero mean and a finite variance" (Data and method)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Delta mu/mu from J2310+1855 at z=6.003**: Average five CO redshifts from L20 and compare with [CII] redshift  
  > "With $z_{\rm fs} = z_{\scriptscriptstyle \rm [CII]} = 6.0031(2)$ the updated result at $z = 6.003$ yields $\Delta\mu/\mu$ = $(2.3\pm3.4)\times10^{-5}$ ($1\sigma$)." (3.2 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.003); "From this dataset we selected CO lines obtained with the highest angular resolution and with redshifts measured with errors" (3.2 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.003)
   - assumption: [CII] and CO lines co-spatially distributed (similar widths)  
  > "The width of [CII] is in good agreement with CO lines, i.e., they all are, most probably, co-spatially distributed." (3.2 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.003)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Combine the three z~6 estimates**: Weighted mean of Delta mu/mu at z=6.519, 6.003 and 6.419 (J1148+5251)  
  > "Being averaged, these three estimates provide a weighted mean value of" (Description of the data points)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit redshift dependence of Delta mu/mu**: Regression of power law Delta mu/mu = k_mu (1+z)^p to all compiled points from z=0 to z=1100; exponential alternative also tried  
  > "To approximate the $z$-dependence of $\mu$ we employ a simple power law" (Possible z-dependence of mu); "We also tried an exponential function depending on two parameters" (Possible z-dependence of mu)
   - assumption: Electron mass shift at recombination from Hart & Chluba (2020) is real  
  > "If we assume that the result of Hart & Chluba (2020), $m_{{\rm e},z} = (1.0190\pm0.0055)m_{\rm e,0}$, is real at the recombination epoch" (Possible z-dependence of mu)
   - fitting: Regression analysis (method not specified further)  
  > "The regression analysis yields $k_\mu = (1.6 \pm 0.3)\times10^{-8}$ and $p = 2.00 \pm 0.03$ ($1\sigma$)" (Possible z-dependence of mu)
   - validation: Power law and exponential cannot be distinguished by the available points  
  > "It is clearly seen that the points available do not allow to decide between the power law and exponential functions." (Possible z-dependence of mu)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Objects at z>6 probe hidden-field couplings; published spectra of two quasars with [CI]/[CII]/CO lines  
  > "in the present paper we probe the non-standard coupling in two objects at $z > 6$ by means of radio spectroscopy of atomic and molecular transitions." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Fine Structure Method is at least 30 times more sensitive than UV/optical resonance lines  
  > "the FSM provides, at least, a factor of 30 more sensitive probe of the variability of physical constants" (Data and method)
- Alternative: CO lines other than CO(6-5), (7-6), (9-8) excluded for J0439+1634 (asymmetric or different resolution); CO(10-9) excluded for blending  
  > "Among them, only [CI], [CII], CO(6-5), CO(7-6) and CO(9-8) demonstrate simple symmetric profiles" (Data and method)
- Alternative: Exponential function of z tried instead of the power law  
  > "We also tried an exponential function depending on two parameters" (Possible z-dependence of mu)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Unexplained 20.3 km/s offset of CO(7-6), probably systematic  
  > "The reason for such a large velocity offset is unclear and probably due to some systematic effects" (3.1 Constraints on mu-variation at z=6.519)
- Limitation (authors): Differential lensing in compact lensed J0439+1634 may distort line profiles and shift line positions  
  > "This may modify the offset between the relative positions of spectral lines." (Data and method)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Regression uses points of very different quality and systematics, and the recombination point rests on one CMB analysis
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Error propagation and weighting of the weighted mean are not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Channel width (~50 km/s) is large compared with the offsets sought, so centroid errors dominate
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Delta alpha assumed zero without test
