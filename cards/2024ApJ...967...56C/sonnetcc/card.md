## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image the 1.3 mm continuum**: Pipeline calibration, line-free continuum visibilities, tclean multiscale mosaic imaging  
  > "The raw data were calibrated with the data reduction pipeline using CASA 5.4.0 (cite: Mcmullin07)." (3.1 ALMA band 6 observations); "We constructed the continuum visibility with all line-free channels." (3.1 ALMA band 6 observations)
   - software: CASA 5.4.0, CASA 6.5.0, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "with Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 0.5" (3.1 ALMA band 6 observations)
   - parameter: multiscale scales [0, 5, 15, 50], pixel size 0.15''  
  > "a multiscale algorithm with scales of \[0, 5, 15, 50\] and a pixel size of 0$''$15" (3.1 ALMA band 6 observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **identify cores and measure sizes and flux densities with getsf**: getsf extraction on the non-primary-beam-corrected 1.3 mm images; fluxes then primary-beam corrected; catalogue cut with the recommended optimal selection criteria  
  > "is a method for extracting sources and filaments in astronomical images based on separation of multiscale structural components" (4.2 Core Identification); "The final catalogue includes 183 cores that pass the recommended optimal selection criteria based on benchmark tests" (4.2 Core Identification)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: maximum source size 10 arcsec  
  > "the maximum size of the sources to be extracted, for which we adopt 10$''$" (4.2 Core Identification)
   - parameter: selection includes aspect ratio below 2, peak and integrated flux S/N above 2  
  > "including an aspect ratio lower than 2, peak and integrated flux signal-to-noise ratios above 2, etc." (4.2 Core Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Artificial core insertion tests (step 3) and comparison with astrodendro (step 7)  
  > "we run experiments of artificial core insertion by generating artificial cores of certain flux densities, randomly putting them in the original image" (4.2 Core Identification)
3. **estimate 90% completeness by artificial core insertion**: Insert synthetic beam-shaped cores into the image, rerun getsf, count recoveries  
  > "In each experiment, we insert 10 cores of a given total flux, and repeat this for 10 times." (4.2 Core Identification)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: 10 cores per experiment, 10 repetitions per flux  
  > "In each experiment, we insert 10 cores of a given total flux, and repeat this for 10 times." (4.2 Core Identification)
   - assumption: inserted cores have the same shape as the synthesized beam (unresolved limit)  
  > "the artificial cores are assumed to have the same shape as the synthesized beam" (4.2 Core Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **measure core gas temperatures from VLA NH3**: Average NH3 (1,1) and (2,2) spectra within the getsf FWHM of each core, fitted with an LTE model  
  > "model the spectra assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - software: CASA (VLA data)
   - parameter: fit only if (1,1) detected above 4 sigma and (2,2) above 2.5 sigma; 125 of 183 cores qualify  
  > "and 125 cores out of 183 satisfy this criterion" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - parameter: free parameters: velocity, rotational temperature, NH3 column density, velocity dispersion  
  > "The free parameters in the fit are the core velocity, rotational temperature" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - assumption: uniform filling factor of 1 for all transitions and hyperfine components  
  > "During the fitting we assume a uniform filling factor of 1 for all transitions and hyperfine components." (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - fitting: Spectral line fit following Lu18; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "following the routine in (cite: Lu18)" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **convert 1.3 mm flux densities to core gas masses**: Optically thin isothermal dust emission, M = d^2 F / (kappa B(T)), multiplied by 100 for gas  
  > "The core mass is then estimated assuming that the emission purely comes from optically thin isothermal dust emission" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - parameter: kappa(1.3 mm) = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "We adopt $\kappa_{\rm 1.3 mm}$ = 0.899" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - assumption: dust-to-gas mass ratio 1:100  
  > "assuming a dust-to-gas mass ratio of 1:100 (cite: Bohlin78)" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - assumption: dust temperature equal to the NH3 gas temperature; region median used where NH3 temperature is unavailable  
  > "In the absence of high resolution dust temperature measurements, we adopt the gas temperature derived with" (4.3 Properties of dense cores); "due to insufficient signal to noise ratio, we adopt the median" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - assumption: target distances from Lu18 (3.8-5.4 kpc)  
  > "We take the distance and systematic velocity from (cite: Lu18)." (2 Sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **classify cores as prestellar or protostellar**: Protostellar if associated with a molecular outflow or compact warm-core emission (H2CO 3(2,2)-2(2,1), 3(2,1)-2(2,0), CH3OH 4(2,2)-3(1,2)); otherwise prestellar candidate  
  > "Cores that are absent in both molecular outflows and warm core tracers are then classified as prestellar candidates." (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - parameter: warm core: peak above 4 sigma in any of three lines, with centrally peaked compact morphology  
  > "If a peak above 4 $\sigma$ is found in any of the three spectral lines, and the emission has a centrally peaked compact morphology associated with dense cores" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - assumption: outflow-based count is a lower limit because outflows near the plane of sky are missed  
  > "This is most likely a lower limit of protostellar cores since we will miss outflows orientated close to the plane of sky" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **compare getsf and astrodendro**: Run both on the same maps, repeat the artificial-core insertion for detection rate and flux recovery  
  > "To quantitatively evaluate the performance of the two algorithms, we run experiments of artificial core insertion." (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
   - software: getsf, astrodendro
   - parameter: astrodendro: base flux density threshold 4 sigma, minimum significance 1 sigma, minimum area half the synthesized beam  
  > "we set the base flux density threshold to 4$\sigma$, the minimum significance for structures to 1$\sigma$, and the minimum area to half the synthesized beam" (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
   - assumption: comparison concerns detecting point-like sources in a 2-D map  
  > "Note that the comparison here only concerns the performance of algorithms in detecting point-like sources in a 2-D map." (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Artificial core insertion in random and crowded-region placements  
  > "We also test the case with artificial cores restricted to the spatial range defined by a flux density threshold" (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
8. **test whether protostellar cores are more massive than prestellar cores**: Single-sided Mann-Whitney U test on 1.3 mm flux density and mass, for all clouds and for young and evolved groups  
  > "We carry out a Mann-Whitney U test (cite: Mann47) to statistically examine the result" (6.2 Absence of high-mass prestellar core and mass growth of cores)
   - parameter: null hypothesis: protostellar flux or mass is equal or smaller than prestellar  
  > "the null hypothesis is that the protostellar flux/mass is equal or smaller than that of the prestellar population." (Table 5)
   - assumption: sample is flux limited; temperature needed to convert flux to mass; prestellar cores probably under-counted at low mass  
  > "Therefore, it is likely that we are missing more prestellar cores in the lower mass regime." (6.2 Absence of high-mass prestellar core and mass growth of cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeated for gravitationally bound cores (virial parameter below 2): p = 0.0028  
  > "can be rejected with a confidence greater than 99% ($p$ = 0.0028)" (10 Virial states of cores)
9. **fit the high-mass slope of the core mass function**: Maximum likelihood power-law fit of dN/dlogM above 1.5 Msun for all cores, prestellar cores, and young and evolved cloud groups  
  > "using the the maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) method in (cite: Clauset09)" (6.3 Core mass function)
   - software: plfit
   - parameter: fit range M > 1.5 Msun  
  > "We fit the high-mass end of the CMFs (M $>$ 1.5" (6.3 Core mass function)
   - parameter: young group: I18308, I18337, I18460; evolved group: I18530, I19074, I19220 (L/M plus other indicators)  
  > "i.e., the young group includes I18308, I18337 and I18460; and the evolved group includes I18530, I19074 and I19220" (6.1 Evolutionary status of the protocluster)
   - assumption: masses use NH3 temperatures (see step 5); completeness 0.53-0.89 Msun at 15 K, 1.0-1.5 Msun at 10 K  
  > "But considering cores with a lower temperature of 10 K, the 90% completeness limit can be higher, i.e., 1.0 – 1.5" (6.3 Core mass function)
   - fitting: Maximum likelihood (Clauset et al. 2009) via plfit; priors not applicable  
  > "using the the maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) method in (cite: Clauset09)" (6.3 Core mass function)
   - validation: Alternative evolution classifications (cases 2-4) and astrodendro CMF compared  
  > "In all cases the power-law index $\alpha$ for the evolved group is slightly steeper." (9 Slopes of CMFs and cloud evolution)
10. **estimate virial parameters of cores**: alpha = 5 sigma^2 R / (G M) with NH3 line width (channel width subtracted, thermal part removed) and deconvolved radius  
  > "We use the velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\rm NH_3}$ of $\rm NH_3$ lines to estimate $\sigma_{\rm in}$." (10 Virial states of cores)
   - parameter: minimum deconvolved size of half the beam; critical alpha = 2  
  > "We set a minimum deconvolved size of half the beam" (10 Virial states of cores)
   - assumption: mean molecular weight 2.33  
  > "where $\mu_p$ = 2.33 is the mean molecular weight" (10 Virial states of cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- getsf gives more robust core identification and flux measurements than astrodendro, which picks up extra weak cores on strong background and returns inflated fluxes for massive cores  
  > "has a better treatment of the varying background levels in identification of dense cores, and gives more robust flux measurements." (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
- No high-mass prestellar cores are found in the survey  
  > "Thus, there are no high-mass prestellar cores in this survey." (6.2 Absence of high-mass prestellar core and mass growth of cores)
- Protostellar cores are statistically more massive than prestellar cores, read as continued accretion onto cores after protostar formation  
  > "Therefore the protostellar cores are statistically more massive." (6.2 Absence of high-mass prestellar core and mass growth of cores)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: To study the relationship between filaments and star formation across a range of evolutionary stages with high resolution and sensitivity covering whole filamentary clouds  
  > "aimed at investigating the relationship between filaments and star formation across a range of evolutionary stages" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: getsf chosen as the fiducial core finder: widely used for multi-scale source extraction, including ALMA-IMF  
  > "is the fiducial method in this paper and also commonly used in other works for multi-scale multi-wavelength source-extraction" (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
- Alternative: astrodendro core extraction, found to give less robust fluxes and many weak irregular cores; getsf kept as fiducial  
  > "Cautions should be made when directly interpreting the leaf structures given by" (5 Comparison of core identification algorithms)
- Alternative: Three alternative young/evolved cloud classifications tested for the CMF slope trend  
  > "In all cases the power-law index $\alpha$ for the evolved group is slightly steeper." (9 Slopes of CMFs and cloud evolution)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Outflow-based protostellar count is a lower limit  
  > "This is most likely a lower limit of protostellar cores since we will miss outflows orientated close to the plane of sky" (4.3 Properties of dense cores)
- Limitation (authors): Flux-limited sample with temperature-dependent mass limit biases against low-mass prestellar cores  
  > "Therefore, it is likely that we are missing more prestellar cores in the lower mass regime." (6.2 Absence of high-mass prestellar core and mass growth of cores)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot tell whether the CMF turnover is real or due to incompleteness  
  > "thus we can not say with certainty whether the turnover seen in the CMF is physically real or due to incompleteness" (6.3 Core mass function)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial filtering by the interferometer removes filamentary emission in some clouds  
  > "likely due to spatial filtering effect" (4.1 Dust Continuum Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Evolutionary grouping of clouds is ambiguous  
  > "A caveat in this survey is concerning the classification of two groups with different evolutionary phases." (6.3 Core mass function)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core masses depend on NH3 temperatures that come from separate VLA data not described in this paper, and the median regional temperature is used for 58 cores without a fit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The same temperature is used for dust as for NH3 gas, while prestellar and protostellar cores have systematically different temperatures, which could affect the mass comparison
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Prestellar versus protostellar labels depend on outflow and warm-line detection limits, so misclassified protostellar cores would feed into the mass comparison and the prestellar CMF
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CMF slopes use a single fit range (M > 1.5 Msun) with only three clouds per evolution group, so the evolutionary steepening trend is of limited significance
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The completeness limit is estimated for beam-sized point sources, not for resolved cores in crowded regions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The comparison of getsf and astrodendro only tests point-like sources, and astrodendro parameters were not tuned
