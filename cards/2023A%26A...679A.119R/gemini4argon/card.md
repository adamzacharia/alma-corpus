4. **Extract line fluxes, upper limits, and sky-plane line luminosities**: Extract flux from a manually drawn aperture over the main lens arc (~6 beams for HCN and HCO+, 1.5 beams for HNC), adopt 3 sigma upper limits for undetected HCN and HNC, and multiply fluxes by 1.31 to account for total line flux vs flux within FWHM.

(HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission); "To calculate the line luminosities, we multiplied the measured fluxes by a factor of 1.31 (a ratio between the total line flux and the flux contained within the line FWHM)." (HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission)
   - parameter: aperture area ~ 6 beams (HCN, HCO+) and 1.5 beams (HNC)

## Qualitative findings

- HCO+(4-3) is clearly detected and resolved in SDP.81 (third detection in a high-z DSFG), while HCN(4-3) and HNC(4-3) remain undetected.  
  > "As shown in Fig. 1, the HCN(4–3) and HNC(4–3) lines are undetected in the dirty-image maps." (HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission); "On the contrary, the HCO$^+$(4–3) line is clearly detected and resolved: the peak signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) is 4.3$\sigma$ and we see $\geq3\sigma$ excess emission over 4–5 beams." (HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission)
- The high HCO+(4-3)/HCN(4-3) ratio (>= 2.2) in SDP.81 is best explained by a sub-solar metallicity (Z = 0.5 Z_odot) and low or negligible mechanical heating (alpha = 0-10%).  
  > "We therefore conclude that the most direct explanation of the high HCO$^+$/HCN ratio in SDP.81 is a combination of a lower-than-solar metallicity and low (or outright negligible) mechanical heating." (Photon-dissociation region modelling)
- A gas-rich companion galaxy at z = 0.301 (M_gas ~ 2x10^10 M_odot) is serendipitously detected in CO(1-0) 12 arcsec south-east of SDP.81, along with a tentative foreground CO(0-1) absorption feature at 87.9 GHz.  
  > "In particular, we find an indication of a foreground CO(0–1) absorption and a nearby, CO(1–0) luminous galaxy." (Environment of the lensing galaxy)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Mid-J (4-3) transitions of HCN, HCO+, and HNC are intrinsically brighter than (1-0), fall into the accessible 3-mm (Band 3) window at z~3, and are much more sensitive to dense gas and mechanical heating than CO lines.  
  > "The mid-$J$ lines are both intrinsically brighter and, at high redshift, conveniently fall into the easily accessible 3-mm atmospheric window (cite: Wagg2005)." (Introduction); "The sensitivity of the HCN/HCO$^+$/HNC lines to mechanical heating makes them potentially powerful tracers of turbulence in star-forming galaxies (cite: Loenen2008; Kazandjian2015)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Dirty images with uv-tapering were used for the lines because the lines are faint and combining discrepant array configurations produced strong dirty-beam sidelobes; PDR modelling was used to interpret the high HCO+/HCN ratio in terms of metallicity and mechanical heating.  
  > "Due to the faintness of the lines, we produced dirty images only (i.e. without any deconvolution)." (Imaging); "For the HCN(4–3) and HCO$^+$(4–3) lines, the combination of discrepant array configurations produces a dirty beam with very strong sidelobes; we mitigate this effect by using different $uv$-plane tapers with a final beam FWHM of $\sim$0.85”$\times$0.94”." (Imaging); "To investigate which physical conditions cause the high HCO$^+$/HCN(4–3) ratio, we used photon-dissociation region (PDR) modelling." (Photon-dissociation region modelling)
- Alternative: Differential magnification as the cause of the high HCO+/HCN ratio (rejected because HCN and HCO+ are expected to be co-spatial on kpc scales and magnification variations across tracers in SDP.81 are <= 15%).  
  > "While different spatial distributions of the tracers might result in differential magnification, we consider this effect to be limited: HCO$^+$ and HCN are expected to be co-spatial on kpc-scales and previous modelling of dust continuum and CO and [C ii] emission in SDP.81 found magnification factors varying by $\leq$15% (cite: Rybak2015a; Rybak2015b; Swinbank2015; Rybak2020b), which are too small to explain the observed high HCO$^+$/HCN ratio." (HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission)
- Alternative: Increased cosmic-ray ionisation rate in PDR models (rejected because models with extra CR contribution overpredict the HCO+(4-3)/HCN(1-0) ratio).  
  > "We also explore the option of increasing the CR ionisation rate above the default value; models with extra CR contribution are ruled out as they overpredict the HCO$^+$(4–3)/HCN(1–0) ratio." (Footnotes)
- Alternative: Solar (1.0 Z_odot) or extreme (0.1, 0.2, 2.0 Z_odot) metallicities in PDR models (rejected because 0.1, 0.2, and 2.0 Z_odot yield no solutions and 1.0 Z_odot implies unrealistically low gas densities <= 10^2 cm^-3 ruled out by CO detections).  
  > "The models with $Z=0.1$ $Z_\odot$ and $2.0$ $Z_\odot$ are effectively excluded by the observations." (Photon-dissociation region modelling); "Models with $Z=1$ $Z_\odot$ are consistent with the data for $\alpha=$5 – 10%, but imply unrealistically low gas densities ($\leq10^2$ cm$^{-3}$) that are ruled out by the existing mid- and high-$J$ CO detections." (Photon-dissociation region modelling)

  > "Compared to the dust continuum, the HCO$^+$(4–3) emission seems to be concentrated to the north; however, given the low S/N of our data, the HCO$^+$(4–3) morphology can not be reliably assessed." (HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission); "The low S/N also precludes a reliable reconstruction of the HCO$^+$(4–3) emission in the source plane." (HCN, HCO$^+$, and HNC emission)

  > "Finally, we note that in our PDR models, changing metallicity values merely ends up scaling the C, N, O abundances with respect to the solar values." - Caveat (inferred, ours): The PDR metallicity constraint relies on discrete grid steps (0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 Z_odot) and fixed solar abundance ratios, where N/O scaling directly affects HCN/HCO+ chemistry.
