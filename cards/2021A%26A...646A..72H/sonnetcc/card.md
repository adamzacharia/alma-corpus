## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, star_formation, astrochemistry
- Question: Determine the molecular abundance structure of the young Class 0/I-type (Stage I) disk around TMC1A on au scales, to understand its chemical structure, whether planet-forming material enters the disk unaltered, and what drives accretion.  
  > "We aim to determine the molecular abundance structure of the young disk around the TMC1A protostar on au scales in order to understand its chemical structure and any possible implications for disk formation." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Combine and self-calibrate the three ALMA data sets**: Concatenate the three projects, self-calibrate (phase and amplitude) using line-free channels, fix phase center via UVFIT  
  > "We combined these ALMA projects into a single measurement set using the CASA task CONCAT to improve the $S/N$ of the dust continuum model." (2.3 Self-calibration and combined ALMA data); "This self-calibration is based on the line-free channels in each spectral window" (2.3 Self-calibration and combined ALMA data)
   - software: CASA 5.4.1, CONCAT, UVFIT
   - parameter: Phase center J2000 04h39m35.203s +25d41m44.21s from fitting an elliptical Gaussian to continuum visibilities  
  > "The phase center of J2000 04h39m35.203s +25d41m44.21s is determined by fitting an elliptical Gaussian to the continuum visibilities with CASA task UVFIT." (2.3 Self-calibration and combined ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image continuum and line cubes**: TCLEAN with Briggs weighting for continuum; UVCONTSUB for line windows; line cubes at 0.3 km/s with 0.08 arcsec taper  
  > "The imaging of the continuum is performed with TCLEAN using Briggs weighting (cite: briggs95) to minimize the side lobes." (2.3 Self-calibration and combined ALMA data); "The spectral lines are imaged at 0.3 km s$^{-1}$ velocity resolution including a spatial tapering at 0.08$''$" (2.3 Self-calibration and combined ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN, UVCONTSUB
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit Gaussian intensity profiles to continuum visibilities**: GALARIO synthetic visibilities of a Gaussian intensity profile fitted to observed visibilities at each frequency  
  > "GALARIO calculates the synthetic (u,v) visibilities given an intensity profile and makes it possible to fit Gaussian intensity profiles to the observed visibilities" (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum)
   - software: GALARIO, emcee
   - parameter: Free parameters: peak intensity, size, inclination, position angle, position offset  
  > "The free parameters are the peak intensity $I$ in Jy sr$^{-1}$, size of the emitting region, inclination $i$, position angle $PA$, and position offset." (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum)
   - parameter: First run 60 walkers and 1000 steps; second run 120 walkers, 10000 steps; best fit = mean of last 1000 steps of walkers with acceptance ratio >20%  
  > "a first run is performed with 60 walkers ($10 \times$ free parameters) and 1000 steps." (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum); "This second run is performed with 10000 steps to obtain the final set of best-fit parameters." (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum); "taking the mean of the last 1000 steps of the walkers with acceptance ratios greater than 20%" (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC with chi^2-based exploration; priors and likelihood form not stated  
  > "Each walker explores the parameter space by gradually stepping into a region with the lowest $\chi^2$." (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum)
   - validation: Compared integrated flux density of NOEMA and ALMA at 220 GHz; flux scale uncertainty within 10%  
  > "the uncertainty in the absolute flux density is within 10%" (3.1 Spatially resolved dust continuum)
4. **Measure line integrated flux densities, upper limits and proxy-masked spectra**: Sum pixels above 3 sigma for flux densities; upper limits from a spatial mask over the continuum assuming 1 km/s Gaussian linewidth; DCN/HCN spectra extracted with proxy mask from a line cube  
  > "The tabulated integrated flux densities are calculated considering pixels whose intensities $> 3 \sigma$ over the entire image." (3.2 Molecular gas observations); "a proxy mask is created from the  spectral cube by taking pixels ($x,y,v$) that are $>3 \sigma$" (3.2 Molecular gas observations)
   - parameter: Gaussian linewidth of 1 km/s for upper limits  
  > "assuming a Gaussian linewidth of 1 km s$^{-1}$" (3.2 Molecular gas observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive molecular column densities (optically thin LTE and non-LTE slab)**: Optically thin thermalized emission with fixed Tex, checked with RADEX non-LTE slab models using LAMDA collisional rates  
  > "the molecular column density of the emitting gas can be determined by assuming a thermalized, optically thin layer of emission" (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk); "A non-LTE radiative transfer calculation of a slab using RADEX (cite: radex) is also performed as a check on the column density and excitation temperature." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
   - software: RADEX, LAMDA
   - parameter: Excitation temperature 30 K adopted in the optically thin case  
  > "In the optically thin approximation, adopting an excitation temperature of 30 K" (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
   - assumption: T_gas = T_dust for the emitting region of the optically thin CO isotopolog, justifying 30 K  
  > "A temperature of 30 K is usually adopted to convert the dust thermal emission to dust mass" (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
   - assumption: Single collisional partner H2, ortho-to-para ratio ignored for HCN, HCO+ and SO rates  
  > "All of these rates consider a single collisional partner, H$_2$, without taking into account its ortho-to-para ratio." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Optically thin limit compared with non-LTE slab results for the optically thin CO isotopolog and the other molecules  
  > "Therefore, the optically thin limit provides a reasonable estimate for the  emitting layer." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
6. **Constrain DCN/HCN ratio**: Disk-averaged HCN column density and upper limit for DCN from spectra summed over the Keplerian disk, for Tex 15-50 K  
  > "For a range of excitation temperatures between 15 to 50 K, an average value of $N_{\ce{HCN}} \sim 1.1 \times 10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$ is derived for the TMC1A disk." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Disk-averaged HCN spectrum checked against azimuthally averaged values  
  > "As a check, we determined that the physical conditions derived from the averaged HCN spectrum are similar to the azimuthally-averaged values in the previous section." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
7. **Estimate the disk temperature structure**: Continuum and line peak brightness temperatures vs deprojected radius in high-velocity channels; by-eye power-law fit  
  > "Using the high-velocity channels ($1 < v < 4$ km s$^{-1}$ and $9< v <12$ km s$^{-1}$; to avoid resolved-out emission near the systemic velocity)" (4.2 Temperature structure of the disk); "We find that, by eye, a temperature profile given by" (4.2 Temperature structure of the disk)
   - assumption: Dust emission optically thick within the inner 30 au so continuum brightness temperature traces the temperature profile  
  > "If the dust emission is optically thick within the inner 30 au, however, the peak continuum brightness temperature gives indications on the disk’s temperature profile." (4.2 Temperature structure of the disk)
   - fitting: By-eye comparison, no formal fit  
  > "We find that, by eye, a temperature profile given by" (4.2 Temperature structure of the disk)
   - validation: Profile checked against dust brightness temperature, CO peak and Lbol-based inner temperature  
  > "It reproduces the dust brightness temperature profile in the inner 30 au and intersects the peak of the  emission." (4.2 Temperature structure of the disk)
8. **Derive molecular abundances relative to CO and H2**: Column density ratios to 13CO or C18O converted with ISM isotopic ratios, then scaled with X_CO = 1e-4  
  > "Adopting the ISM isotopic ratios of $\ce{^{12}C}:\ce{^{13}C} = 70$ and $\ce{^{16}O}:\ce{^{18}O} = 540$ (cite: wilson94)" (5.2 Molecular abundances in the TMC1A disk.); "$10^{-4}$ relative to (cite: harsono14; vanthoff18b; kzhang20)" (5.2 Molecular abundances in the TMC1A disk.)
   - assumption: CO gas abundance close to canonical ISM value 1e-4 relative to H2  
  > "$10^{-4}$ relative to (cite: harsono14; vanthoff18b; kzhang20)" (5.2 Molecular abundances in the TMC1A disk.)
   - assumption: Abundances valid only for inner warm envelope and disk layers above the dust photosphere  
  > "these abundance fractions are valid in the emitting regions of the inner warm envelope and the layers of the young disk above the dust photosphere" (5.2 Molecular abundances in the TMC1A disk.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with protostellar envelope, Class II disks and Solar System objects  
  > "Remarkably, the gas abundance ratios in the disk around TMC1A are similar to its protostellar envelope" (5.2 Molecular abundances in the TMC1A disk.)
9. **Estimate ionization fraction and test MRI activity**: Reduced CO-HCO+ chemical network solved for equilibrium abundance with cosmic-ray ionization rate; magnetic Reynolds number and Am evaluated  
  > "We compute the electron number density from the cosmic ray (CR) ionization rate" (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk); "we estimate that $R_{\rm e} > 10^{6}$ and $Am < 0.1$" (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)
   - parameter: Fixed CO abundance 1e-4 and a second species abundance 3e-6 relative to H2; range of densities, temperatures and zeta explored  
  > "We adopt a fixed CO abundance of $10^{-4}$ and a abundance of $3 \times 10^{-6}$ relative to" (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk); "A range of densities, temperature, and $\zeta$ values are explored to investigate the effect on the resulting  abundance." (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)
   - assumption: HCO+ taken as the dominant ion  
  > "Here, we assume that  is the dominant ion." (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Dependence on temperature tested (variance less than a factor of 2 between 20 and 100 K)  
  > "the variance in  abundance is less than a factor of 2 for temperatures between 20 and 100 K" (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)
10. **Infer current stellar accretion rate from water snow surface**: Water snow surface location from the brightness temperature profile combined with the snowline-accretion relation and Lbol  
  > "We use the brightness temperature profile in Fig. [10](#fig:TMC1Atemp) to estimate that the water snow surface in TMC1A is located at $\simeq$10 au" (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)
   - assumption: Observed brightness temperature profile traces the irradiated disk surface  
  > "likely traces the irradiated disk surface" (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Noted as higher than Lbol-based and large-scale values; 2D physical structure needed  
  > "A more detailed 2D physical structure (cite: cleeves13; cleeves17) of the disk+envelope is, however, required to more accurately assess these values." (5.3 Accretion processes in a young disk)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN emission peaks toward the outflow cavity region and toward the red-shifted part of the Keplerian disk; the narrow HCN line traces the disk-envelope transition region  
  > "HCN emission peaks toward the outflow cavity region connected with the CO disk wind and toward the red-shifted part of the Keplerian disk." (abstract); "Therefore, we conclude that the HCN line traces the disk-envelope transition region." (5.1 Physical components of embedded protostars traced by molecular lines)
- SO traces the infalling, warm inner envelope along the outflow cavity wall, not an accretion shock or disk wind  
  > "indicates that it originates from the infalling, warm inner envelope along the line of sight" (5.1 Physical components of embedded protostars traced by molecular lines)
- The warm disk shows no CO freeze-out region in the inner 100 au  
  > "does not have a CO freeze-out region ($T_{\rm dust} = 30$ K) in the inner 100 au of the disk" (4.2 Temperature structure of the disk)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: TMC1A has a favorable inclination and simple geometry with a well-defined disk, making it a laboratory for the abundances of simple molecules on ~15 au scales  
  > "The relatively simple geometry of TMC1A with its well-defined disk, makes it an ideal laboratory for determining the distribution and abundances of common, simple molecules" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Optically thin LTE column densities as a zeroth-order estimate, checked with non-LTE radiative transfer  
  > "To zeroth order, the molecular column density of the emitting gas can be determined by assuming a thermalized, optically thin layer of emission" (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
- Alternative: Accretion shock origin of SO rejected because of narrow line profile and low peak temperatures  
  > "rule out an accretion shock origin (cite: sakai14nat)" (5.1 Physical components of embedded protostars traced by molecular lines)
- Alternative: Disk wind origin of SO excluded because SO and CO emission are not co-spatial  
  > "it excludes a disk wind origin for SO (cite: tabone17) for TMC1A" (5.1 Physical components of embedded protostars traced by molecular lines)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Much of the large-scale emission is filtered out; one line cannot be imaged  
  > "emission cannot be imaged because it is mostly filtered out" (6 Summary and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Optically thick lines give lower limits to the total column density  
  > "This implies that the derived column densities are lower limits to the total column density of the gas that is present in the system." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
- Limitation (authors): Envelope material contaminates some lines of sight, so disk abundances are approximate  
  > "Although some envelope material is contaminating the detected emission, it is still useful to calculate the relative abundances for the disk" (5.2 Molecular abundances in the TMC1A disk.)
- Limitation (authors): A detailed 2D physical and chemical model is needed to place the emission exactly; beyond scope  
  > "A detailed 2D physical and chemical model is required to quantify the exact location of the molecular emission, but this is beyond the scope of the current work." (5.1 Physical components of embedded protostars traced by molecular lines)
- Limitation (authors): Non-LTE collisional rates use a single H2 partner without ortho-to-para ratio  
  > "All of these rates consider a single collisional partner, H$_2$, without taking into account its ortho-to-para ratio." (4.1 Molecular column density of the disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abundances rest on one assumed Tex (30 K) and a CO abundance of 1e-4 with ISM isotopic ratios; uncertainties are quoted only as ranges spanning orders of magnitude
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No formal error budget for column densities; by-eye temperature profile
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Strong spatial filtering (about 90% of CO-isotopolog flux lost vs lower resolution) biases abundance ratios, especially SO/CO
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disk/envelope separation is qualitative (spectra and moment maps), not from a physical model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): PA quoted as 75 in Sect. 3.1 and 76 in Summary; HCO+ frequency in text (267.8527) differs from the table (267.5576)
