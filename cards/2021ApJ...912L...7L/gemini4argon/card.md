## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify compact NH2D cores and extract morphological and flux parameters**: Preselect compact structures from the NH2D velocity-integrated intensity map (-11.5 to 5.6 km s^-1) using astrodendro, filter out structures associated with continuum cores, YSOs, multiple peaks, or extreme elongation (major/minor >= 3), and fit 2D Gaussians with CASA-imfit.  
  > "The `astrodendro` algorithm was used to preselect the compact NH$_{2}$D emission structures (cite: 2008ApJ...679.1338R) from the NH$_{2}$D velocity-integrated intensity image (integrated between -11.5 and 5.6 km s$^{-1}$)." (NH$_{2}$D core identification); "From the identified leaves, we further search for the compact structures that are no associated with continuum cores, YSOs, and showing a single peak and no extreme elongations (major axis smaller than 3 times minor axis)." ## Qualitative findings

- All 17 NH2D cores show no signs of protostellar outflow or H2O maser activity and are dark from 3.6 to 70 um, indicating they are cold and quiescent starless core candidates.  
  > "All the NH$_{2}$D cores show no signs of protostellar activity." (Cold and Quiescent Cores); "In addition, the NH$_{2}$D cores do not show any appreciable ($< 3\sigma$) continuum emission in the infrared wavelengths from 3.6 up to 70 $\mu{}$m, and therefore they are completely dark in these infrared images." (Cold and Quiescent Cores)
- Turbulence is significantly dissipated toward the centers of the NH2D cores, and gas kinematics are dominated by thermal motions.  
  > "Turbulence is significantly dissipated and the gas kinematics are dominated by thermal motions toward these candidates." (Abstract); "This increasing trend of $\sigma_{\rm obs}$ along R$_{\rm dist}$ indicates that the turbulence is significantly dissipated toward the center of the core." (Small Velocity Dispersions)
- The embedded cores in NGC 6334S span a wide range of masses and evolutionary stages, consistent with continuous star formation and the competitive accretion scenario.  
  > "In summary, NGC 6334S is forming a stellar protocluster, its embedded cores show a wide diversity in masses (0.13 – 14.03 $M_{\odot}$) and evolutionary stages (pre-stellar – Class  phases)" (Discussion); "This seems to be consistent with the competitive accretion massive star formation scenario (cite: 2006MNRAS.370..488B)" (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 6334S is a nearby massive IRDC with sufficient mass (1.3e3 M_sun) to form a cluster of both low- and high-mass stars, and NH2D (1_11-1_01) has a high critical density (~10^5 cm^-3) that survives in the gas phase in the cold, dense interiors of pre-stellar cores.  
  > "NGC 6334S provides an ideal laboratory to search for and study the early stages (e.g., pre-stellar phase) of star formation in the high-mass regime." (Introduction); "NH$_{2}$D ($1_{11}-1_{01}$, critical density $n_{\rm cr} \sim$ 10$^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$) is a good tracer of cold and dense molecular gas (cite: 2007A&A...470..221C; 2013ApJ...773..123S), which can survive in the gas phase in the dense interior region of pre-stellar cores (cite: 2017A&A...600A..61H)." (Cold and Quiescent Cores)
- Why this method: Used M1 as a detailed showcase because it has high S/N and is relatively isolated from continuum core outflows; tapered JVLA visibilities to increase S/N for NH3.  
  > "Among the NH$_{2}$D cores, M1 is the most prominent pre-stellar candidate with high signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio and a relatively isolated environment that eliminates the contamination from the outflows driven by the continuum cores." (Cold and Quiescent Cores); "An elliptical Gaussian with a FWHM of 6$^{\prime\prime}$$\times$ 3$^{\prime\prime}$ (P.A. = 0$^{\circ}$) was used to taper the visibilities, in order to increase the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)." (JVLA Observations)
- Alternative: Larger-scale collapse scenario where infall speed decreases toward the center, producing smaller central line widths instead of turbulence dissipation; not ruled out and requires higher spectral resolution data.  
  > "Alternatively, in the larger-scale collapse scenario, if the pre-stellar cores undergo accretion, the infall speed decreases towards the center, and thus the line width will be smaller for more central regions (cite: 2021MNRAS.tmp..412G)." (Small Velocity Dispersions)
- Alternative: Lower central dust temperature of 5.5 K instead of 10 K, which would increase derived gas masses by a factor of 2.25 and decrease virial parameters by 2.25.  
  > "The derived gas mass can increase by a factor of 2.25 if a temperature of 5.5 K is assumed, so that the resulting virial parameter will decrease by a factor of 2.25." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Ambient pressure confinement from parental clumps and filaments could bind the remaining 8 starless cores that have alpha_vir > 2.  
  > "In addition, the ambient pressure from the parental clumps and filaments could also provide additional confinement to make these NH$_{2}$D cores bound (cite: 2019ApJ...877...93C; 2020ApJ...896..110L)." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cannot rule out unresolved or weak outflows or very faint embedded low-mass protostars due to coarse spectral resolution (3.0-3.3 km s^-1) in the broad-band windows and lack of better outflow tracers like CO or SiO.  
  > "Although we currently cannot rule out the existence of unresolved or weak outflows due to the coarse spectral resolution and the lack of better low-mass outflow tracers such as CO, these NH$_{2}$D cores appear to be starless with the available evidence." (Cold and Quiescent Cores); "though some undetected very faint embedded low-mass protostars cannot be fully ruled out (cite: 2008ApJS..179..249D; 2009ApJS..181..321E; 2011ApJ...736...53O; 2017ApJ...840...69F)." (Cold and Quiescent Cores)
- Limitation (authors): Measured velocity dispersions may be upper limits due to limited spectral resolution (0.21 km s^-1), blending of velocity components along the line of sight, and opacity broadening.  
  > "The observed velocity dispersions may be regarded as upper limits, given the limited spectral resolution, the blending of velocity components along the line of sight and opacity broadening effects." (Small Velocity Dispersions); "For the extremely narrow line emission, a higher spectral resolution data is required to accurately characterize the actual intrinsic velocity dispersion." (Small Velocity Dispersions)
- Limitation (authors): Excitation temperature and optical depth from hyperfine fits have relatively large uncertainties, and annularly averaged velocity dispersions suffer from low S/N at core outer edges.  
  > "In the hyperfine fits, the uncertainties of the LSR velocities and line widths are small, while those of the excitation temperature and optical depth are relatively large (Figure 3)." (Small Velocity Dispersions); "although the annularly averaged $\sigma_{\rm obs}$ starts to suffer from low S/N toward the outer edges of the cores." (Small Velocity Dispersions)
- Limitation (authors): Levels of missing flux are unknown due to the lack of single-dish data.  
  > "The levels of the missing flux are unknown due to the lack of single dish data." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): NH2D core identification is likely incomplete because compact structures blended with clumpy or filamentary emission could be missed.  
  > "We stress that this identification is likely to be incomplete. Potential NH$_{2}$D compact structures could have been missed if they cannot be distinguished from the clumpy or filamentary structures." (NH$_{2}$D core identification)
