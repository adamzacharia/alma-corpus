1. **Identify continuum sources and measure their sizes and integrated fluxes**: Compute hierarchical dendrograms on the 230 GHz continuum image (robust = 0.5) using astrodendro to identify leaves as compact continuum cores, and perform a 2D Gaussian fit on the double-peaked central source #1 to decompose it into #1a and #1b.  
  > "To quantify this substructure, we compute dendrograms using the astrodendro Python package." (Continuum Data); "A 2D Gaussian fit to the central objects yields deconvolved mean radii of $\sim$ 1300 AU for both sources, and integrated fluxes of 12.1 mJy and 14.3 mJy, respectively for #1a and #1b (16.2 M$_{\odot}$and 18.0 M$_{\odot}$ at 22 K)." (Continuum Data)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: threshold = 3 sigma, increment = 1 sigma, minimum number of pixels = 100 (where sigma ~ 50 uJy beam^-1)  
  > "To compute the dendrogram, a threshold of 3$\sigma$, an increment between structures of 1$\sigma$, and a minimum number of pixels in a source of 100 are specified (which is $\sim$ 50% of the synthesised beam), where $\sigma$ $\sim$ 50 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$." (Continuum Data)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to central sources #1a and #1b  
  > "A 2D Gaussian fit to the central objects yields deconvolved mean radii of $\sim$ 1300 AU for both sources, and integrated fluxes of 12.1 mJy and 14.3 mJy, respectively for #1a and #1b (16.2 M$_{\odot}$and 18.0 M$_{\odot}$ at 22 K)." (Continuum Data)

   - software: CASA, XCLASS
   - parameter: Single-component fit to beam-averaged spectrum  
  > "We opt to fit a single average spectrum rather than a pixel-by-pixel fit, as the source is not well sampled by the synthesised beam." (Gas temperature modelling)
   - assumption: Beam filling factor = 1, and thermalisation between gas and dust (T_dust = T_gas) at high densities (~10^7-8 cm^-3)  
  > "We assume a filling factor of unity." (Gas temperature modelling); "If we take this estimated gas temperature of 167 K and assume that the gas and dust are thermalised at these densities, we can use this to better constrain the lower limit of the dust mass." (Gas temperature modelling)

  > "The best-fitting parameters for the gas temperature, column density, and line-width are 167 K, 8.9$\times$10$^{16}$ cm$^{-2}$, and 3.6 km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Gas temperature modelling)
   - validation: Compared resulting thermalised dust masses (2-3 M_sun) with dynamical mass estimates of the central binary (1.5-5.6 M_sun).  
  > "While this is a simple argument with several caveats, and one that requires that the sources are actually in a binary system, it demonstrates that the dynamical masses in such a scenario are consistent with the dust masses that have been estimated assuming that T$_{\textrm{dust}}$ = T$_{\textrm{gas}}$."   > "If we assume that the sources are of equal mass, and that their measured velocities are the maximum line-of-sight velocities (i.e. that we are viewing the system edge-on), then we can use a simple gravitational-kinetic energy balance argument to estimate their masses." ## Qualitative findings

- Active star formation is unambiguously occurring in 'the Brick' (G0.253+0.016), evidenced by fragmentation into at least 18 continuum cores, 9 bipolar SiO (5-4) outflows, and internal heating in the central binary (#1a, #1b) seen in CH3CN.  
  > "We detect a population of eighteen continuum sources (median mass $\sim$ 2 M$_{\odot}$), nine of which are driving bi-polar molecular outflows as seen via SiO (5-4) emission." (Abstract); "These observations reveal the presence of fragmentation, bipolar outflows and internal heating – unambiguous confirmation of active star formation in G0.253$+$0.016." (Introduction)
- Significant compact molecular line emission in the spectral setup is only detected towards the central binary cores #1a and #1b; no single molecular line reliably traces all continuum sources.  
  > "Manual inspection of all spectral windows towards the continuum sources reveals that significant compact line emission is only detected towards the central sources, 1a and 1b." (Molecular Line Data); "We do not find any single emission line in our spectral setup that can reliably trace all continuum sources." (Molecular Line Data)
- The detected SiO outflows do not have sufficient kinetic energy (~8x10^45 erg) to drive the local turbulence (4x10^48 - 3x10^49 erg) or gravitationally disrupt the clump (5x10^48 erg).  
  > "These results suggest that the detected population of SiO outflows are not energetic enough to drive the local turbulence or to significantly disrupt the local material." (Outflow energetics)
- 13CO (2-1) emission does not show clear-cut bipolar outflows due to complex widespread emission and spatial filtering, but reveals potential outflow cavities, an hourglass structure around source #8, a braided linear feature from source #1, and 'bear-claw' filaments coinciding with large-scale HCO+ absorption filaments.  
  > "Unlike with the SiO emission, we do not detect clear-cut outflow emission in $^{13}$CO." (13CO (2-1) emission); "Inspection of the HCO$^{+}$ cube reveals that the two prominent broad-line absorption filaments shown in Figure 1 of (cite: Bally14) directly cross the centre of our ALMA field and coincide with the arcuate $^{13}$CO structures, both in position and velocity." (13CO (2-1) emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G0.253+0.016 is one of the most massive and dense clouds in the CMZ with no prior unambiguous signs of star formation except a water maser coinciding with a compact mm continuum source; high-resolution (~1000 AU) ALMA Band 6 continuum and outflow/temperature line tracers were chosen to search for fragmentation, outflows, and internal heating.  
  > "In this paper, we present high angular resolution Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observations of this ‘maser core’ in G0.253$+$0.016." (Introduction); "Lines that were specifically targeted are SiO (5-4) and $^{13}$CO (2-1) as these are traditionally good outflow tracers (cite: Bally_16), 3 para-H$_{2}$CO transitions, which can be used to measure gas temperatures in the range $\sim$ 50 – 150 K, and the J=12-11 k-ladder of CH$_{3}$CN, which can be used to measure higher gas temperatures and is often found in the vicinity of protostars." (Molecular Line Data)
- Why this method: Cleaned spectral cubes prior to continuum subtraction and used STATCONT in the image plane because uvcontsub prior to cleaning did not perform a satisfactory continuum subtraction for the line-rich spectral windows; fitted a single beam-averaged spectrum in XCLASS because the central source is not well sampled by the synthesised beam.  
  > "This was done as we found that using the `uvcontsub` task prior to cleaning did not perform a satisfactory continuum subtraction for the more line-rich spectral windows." (Imaging); "We opt to fit a single average spectrum rather than a pixel-by-pixel fit, as the source is not well sampled by the synthesised beam." (Gas temperature modelling)
- Alternative: Visibility-plane continuum subtraction with uvcontsub prior to cleaning was tested and rejected in favour of image-plane continuum subtraction with STATCONT after cleaning.  
  > "In contrast to the continuum, we opted to perform the cleaning prior to continuum subtraction." (Imaging); "This was done as we found that using the `uvcontsub` task prior to cleaning did not perform a satisfactory continuum subtraction for the more line-rich spectral windows." (Imaging)
- Alternative: Continuum imaging with Briggs robust = -2.0 (uniform weighting) was generated to inspect the central binary/multiple system, but was not used for quantitative analyses.  
  > "To more clearly resolve this region, we also imaged the continuum using the Briggs weighting scheme with a robust parameter of -2.0 (i.e. uniform weighting), which prioritises resolution over sensitivity." (Continuum Data); "Note that this image is not used for any analyses – all results reported use the image generated with a robust parameter of 0.5." (Continuum Data)
- Alternative: Alternative shorter distance of 7.2 kpc from Zoccali et al. (2021) was considered (which would reduce masses by ~20%), but the standard 8.1 kpc distance was retained.  
  > "We note that (cite: Zoccali21) recently reported a distance of 7.2 kpc based on near-infrared star counts towards the cloud." (Continuum Data); "Thus, we use the commonly assumed distance of 8.1 kpc for all analyses presented in this paper." (Continuum Data)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dust temperatures and opacities on 1000 AU scales are observationally unconstrained for the majority of cores, and gas and dust may still be weakly decoupled.  
  > "The dust temperature on these spatial scales is observationally unconstrained towards this source." (Continuum Data); "Thus, in the following we estimate gas temperatures (where possible) and use these as a proxy for the dust temperatures, while acknowledging the caveat that they may still be weakly decoupled." (Gas temperature modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Missing short-spacing data (12 m array only, no 7 m or Total Power arrays) causes spatial filtering of large-scale continuum and diffuse 13CO emission, making core and outflow masses potential lower limits.  
  > "In addition to our assumptions, these masses are potentially lower limits due to the fact that the large scale emission, some of which may be associated with the cores, is filtered out by the interferometer." (Continuum Data); "As our observations do not utilise the 7 m or Total Power arrays of ALMA, we are missing short spacing data and so much of the larger-scale diffuse emission will be filtered out by the long baselines of the interferometer." (13CO (2-1) emission)
- Limitation (authors): Fractional SiO abundance is uncertain by several orders of magnitude, and outflow inclination angles and individual core systemic velocities are unknown.  
  > "We also assume that the outflow emission is parallel to the plane of the sky, since we do not have any knowledge of possible inclinations." (Outflow properties); "The SiO abundance is subject to high uncertainty, with several orders of magnitude of spread reported in the literature." (Outflow properties); "In the majority of cases, the source velocity is not known due to the lack of line emission associated with most of the continuum sources (see section 3.2)." (Outflow properties)
- Limitation (authors): Outflows from sources #14 and #16 are near the edge of the field where primary beam response degrades sensitivity and increases noise, so their properties are not quantified.  
  > "Though we clearly detect outflows associated with sources #14 and #16, we do not report their properties as they are very close to the edge of the field where the sensitivity is worse and the noise is higher due to the primary beam response." (Outflow properties)
- Limitation (authors): Central binary sources #1a and #1b are separated by ~1000 AU, approximately equal to the synthesised beam size, so they are not well resolved and individual outflow contributions cannot be separated.  
  > "As this separation is approximately equivalent to the size of the synthesised beam, the sources are not well resolved." (Continuum Data); "With the data presented in this paper, we are not able to resolve the central sources well, and it is not possible to determine the contribution that either of the sources is making to the outflowing material." (There are no high-mass protostars in G0.253+0.016’s ‘maser core’ ... yet)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core radii in Table 3 for dendrogram leaves appear to be undeconvolved effective radii (other than 1a/1b which were 2D Gaussian fitted), which can bias volume density estimates for marginally resolved cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Nearest-neighbour separation analysis does not account for projection effects or primary-beam sensitivity roll-off towards the field edges.
