## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure 3 mm continuum core properties**: Two-dimensional Gaussian fitting to the 12-m array and 12-m+ACA combined 3 mm continuum maps to obtain positions, sizes, peak flux densities, and total flux densities of the dense cores.  
  > "The positions, sizes, peak flux density ($I_{\rm peak}$) and total flux density ($S_{\nu}$) of six dense cores obtained by two dimensional Gaussian fitting are listed in Table" (ALMA 3 mm continuum emission)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fitting  
  > "of six dense cores obtained by two dimensional Gaussian fitting are listed in Table" (ALMA 3 mm continuum emission)
   - validation: Comparison between 12-m array and 12-m+ACA combined continuum fluxes shows 12-m array missed ~5% of extended emission.  
  > "The observations of 12-m array missed $\sim$ 5% of extended emissions compared to the observations of 12-m + ACA combined data." (Results / ALMA 3 mm continuum emission)
2. **Extract spectra and identify molecular line transitions**: Extract full-band beam-averaged spectra at the continuum peaks from 12-m array observations and identify transitions above 3 sigma using XCLASS with CDMS and JPL databases, requiring consistent rest frequencies, LSR velocities, and spatial distributions.  
  > "We identified spectral line transitions using the eXtended CASA Line Analysis Software Suite (XCLASS; (cite: Moller17))." (Results / Molecular lines identification); "Furthermore, all lines above the 3$\sigma$ "detection level" are available in our identification and follow-up analyses." (Results / Molecular lines identification); "display the full-band beam-averaged spectra at the six continuum peaks." (Molecular lines from different cores)
   - software: XCLASS, CDMS, JPL
   - parameter: Detection threshold: > 3 sigma (1 sigma RMS = 0.12 - 0.14 K computed in line-free channels)  
  > "Estimated 1$\sigma$ RMS is 0.13 K, 0.12 K, 0.13 K, 0.14 K, 0.12 K, and 0.12 K for MM1, MM6, MM7, MM8, MM4, and MM11, respectively." (Results / Molecular lines identification / Molecular lines from different cores); "The noise level $\sigma$ in each spectrum is computed in line-free channels." (Results / Molecular lines identification / Molecular lines from different cores)
   - assumption: Transitions of a specific species are excited by similar conditions and share similar LSR velocity and spatial distribution  
  > "The rest frequency must be consistent with the value from laboratory measurement; for a certain species, all detected transitions from the same source must have a similar LSR velocity; and transitions for a specific species should have similar spatial distribution owing to that they are excited by similar conditions (cite: Peng17)." (Results / Molecular lines identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of 2'' and 10'' averaged spectra between 12-m array and 12-m+ACA combined data in Appendix A shows no difference for compact hot core chemistry.  
  > "In the Appendix A, we make a comparison between 12-m array and combined data, suggesting there is no difference when using them to study the hot core chemistry." (Results / Molecular lines identification)
3. **Map spatial distributions of molecular emission and outflows**: Generate integrated intensity maps of main transitions of O-, N-, and S-bearing species and outflow tracers (SiO, HCO+, CS).  
  > "The integrated intensity maps of main transitions of Oxygen-, Nitrogen- and Sulphur-bearing species are shown in Figure 5$-$7." (Results / Spatial distributions); "For spectral lines, combined SiO (2-1), HCO$^{+}$ (1-0) and CS (2-1) are utilized to probe the kinematics of this source." (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive rotational temperatures, column densities, and line optical depths via LTE spectral modeling**: Model observed 12-m spectra using myXCLASS and MAGIX under LTE assumptions, fitting unblended transitions (>3 transitions with wide Eu range) for T_rot and N_T, or fixing T_rot to core average gas temperature when fewer than 3 transitions are detected.  
  > "The software includes myXCLASS program, which is used to model data by solving the radiative transfer equation." (Results / Molecular lines identification); "We model the observed spectra using the XCLASS suite. The software contains an interface for the model optimizer package MAGIX (Modeling and Analysis Generic Interface for eXternal numerical codes; (cite: Moller13)), which helps to optimize the fit, finds the best solutions of parameters, and provides corresponding error estimates by using different optimization algorithms or algorithm chain." (LTE Analyses / LTE calculation); "Assuming the gas temperature of each core is equal to the average value of rotational temperatures derived from spectral fits, we use 99 K, 134 K, 137 K, and 100 K as the gas temperatures toward MM7, MM8, MM4, and MM11." (LTE Analyses / Rotational Temperatures and Column Densities)
   - software: XCLASS, myXCLASS, MAGIX
   - parameter: Model parameters: source size, rotational temperature, column density, FWHM line width, velocity offset  
  > "The main modeling parameters of XCLASS for each molecule are the source size, rotational temperature, column density, full width at half maximum of the observed line, and the velocity offset of the line with respect to the systemic velocity of the object." (LTE Analyses / LTE calculation)
   - parameter: Fixed T_rot for species with <3 transitions: 99 K (MM7), 134 K (MM8), 137 K (MM4), 100 K (MM11)

## Qualitative findings

- About 172 transitions attributed to 16 molecular species (including O-, N-, and S-bearing molecules and isotopologues) are identified toward six dense cores in G9.62+0.19, with MM7, MM8, MM4, and MM11 being line-rich.  
  > "Toward these cores, about 172 transitions have been identified and attributed to 16 species including organic Oxygen-, Nitrogen-, Sulfur-bearing molecules and their isotopologues. Four dense cores MM7, MM8, MM4, and MM11 are line rich sources." (Abstract)
- The evolutionary sequence of the six dense cores is classified as MM6 (HMPO) -> MM7 (early HMC) -> MM8 (HMC) -> MM4 (evolved HMC) -> MM11 (HMC/UC HII) -> MM1 (evolved HII), while MM3 and MM9 are massive starless core candidates.  
  > "MM3 and MM9 are considered as massive starless core candidates." (Discussions / Evolution of dense cores); "In short, their evolutionary sequence is MM6$\rightarrow$MM7$\rightarrow$MM8$\rightarrow$MM4$\rightarrow$MM11$\rightarrow$MM1." (Conclusions)
- MM4 and MM8 exhibit strong N/O chemical differentiation: MM4 dominates in O-bearing COMs while MM8 dominates in N-bearing (especially C2H5CN and vibrationally excited HC3N) and S-bearing molecules.  
  > "The cores MM8 and MM4 show chemical difference between Oxygen- and Nitrogen-bearing species, i.e., MM4 is rich in oxygen-bearing molecules while nitrogen-bearing molecules especially vibrationally excited HC$_{3}$N lines are mainly observed in MM8." (Abstract); "The emission of N-bearing and S-bearing molecules is strongest toward MM8, while emission of O-bearings is dominated by core MM4." (Conclusions)
- Spatial distributions and column density correlations indicate that C2H5OH and CH3OCH3 likely share CH3OH as a common precursor, and CH3OCHO and CH3OCH3 are chemically linked, whereas chemical links within the C2HnO family (H2CCO, CH3CHO, C2H5OH) remain uncertain.  
  > "Through analyzing column densities and spatial distributions of O-bearing Complex Organic Molecules (COMs), we found that C$_{2}$H$_{5}$OH and CH$_{3}$OCH$_{3}$ might have a common precursor, CH$_{3}$OH. CH$_{3}$OCHO and CH$_{3}$OCH$_{3}$ are likely chemically linked." (Abstract); "It seems likely that the chemical link of O-bearing COMs in the formula C$_{2}$H$_{\rm n}$O is uncertain at present work." (Conclusions)
- HC5N spatial distribution differs from HC3N and its isotopologues/vibrationally excited states, suggesting different formation mechanisms in hot and cold regions, while C2H5CN is spatially correlated with HC3N.  
  > "is almost identical to that of HC$_{3}$N, predicting that C$_{2}$H$_{5}$CN is correlated with HC$_{3}$N in formation mechanism" (Chemical differentiation); "The comparison of HC$_{3}$N and HC$_{5}$N suggests that they trace different gas structures, and the formation mechanisms of HC$_{3}$N and HC$_{5}$N may differ in hot and cold regions." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G9.62+0.19 contains dense cores spanning multiple evolutionary stages and rich chemistry, making ALMA Band 3 observations from the ATOMS survey ideal for exploring chemical differentiation; 12-m array data alone are used for line modeling because they provide higher angular resolution for compact cores without suffering missing flux on <10'' scales, while 1.3 mm archival continuum is used for H2 column densities to avoid 3 mm free-free contamination.  
  > "In general, G9.62+0.19 contains different evolutionary types of dense cores, and harbours rich chemistry indicating that it is an ideal object to explore the distinct chemical properties of cores at different evolutionary stages." (Introduction); "However, at 3 mm observations, free-free emission from ionized gas could contribute to the continuum emission (detailed analyses are provided in (cite: Liu20a; Liu2021)), which will overestimate the column density of H$_{2}$. Instead, we use 1.3 mm continuum data from (cite: Liu17), and smooth it to the same spatial resolution as that of 3 mm continuum data." (LTE Analyses / Molecular abundance); "Therefore, 12-m array and combined data have no difference when using them to study the hot core chemistry, but 12-m array data have higher angular resolution for better investigating the compact cores." (Spectra from 12-m array and 12-m + ACA combined data.)
- Why this method: XCLASS with MAGIX solves the radiative transfer equation under LTE while accounting for beam dilution, dust attenuation, line opacity, and line blending; abundances relative to CH3OH are used when comparing with literature sources to mitigate differences in spatial resolution and spectral setup across telescopes.  
  > "Under the assumption of local thermodynamical equilibrium (LTE), XCLASS takes the beam dilution, dust attenuation, the line opacity, and line blending into account (cite: Moller17)." (Results / Molecular lines identification); "Since column densities of these O-bearing COMs have been derived from various observational equipments with different spatial resolution and spectral setup, comparison of column density is not very helpful to derive sound conclusions for chemical links among O-bearing COMs. It is more reliable to compare the abundance of COMs relative to CH$_{3}$OH." (Discussions / Chemical differentiation / Chemical relationships of Oxygen-bearing molecules)
- Alternative: Using 3 mm continuum emission to derive H2 column densities was rejected because free-free emission from ionized gas at 3 mm would overestimate N_H2; 1.3 mm continuum from Liu et al. (2017) was used instead.  
  > "However, at 3 mm observations, free-free emission from ionized gas could contribute to the continuum emission (detailed analyses are provided in (cite: Liu20a; Liu2021)), which will overestimate the column density of H$_{2}$. Instead, we use 1.3 mm continuum data from (cite: Liu17), and smooth it to the same spatial resolution as that of 3 mm continuum data." (LTE Analyses / Molecular abundance)
- Alternative: Using 12-m+ACA combined spectra for hot core LTE modeling was tested and rejected in favor of 12-m array alone to retain higher angular resolution for the compact cores.  
  > "Therefore, 12-m array and combined data have no difference when using them to study the hot core chemistry, but 12-m array data have higher angular resolution for better investigating the compact cores." (Spectra from 12-m array and 12-m + ACA combined data.)

  > "The observations of 12-m array missed $\sim$ 5% of extended emissions compared to the observations of 12-m + ACA combined data." (Results / ALMA 3 mm continuum emission); "The ratios between HC$_{3}$N v=0 and its isotopologues are underestimated by a factor of six, which may due to the large optical depth and possible missing-flux of HC$_{3}$N v=0." - Limitation (authors): Cores MM2, MM3, MM5, MM9, and MM10 are not resolved in the 3 mm continuum map due to the relatively lower spatial resolution, and 2D Gaussian fitting toward MM7 failed to converge because it is not well resolved.

- Limitation (authors): Four dense cores in G9.62+0.19 provide a very limited sample size for testing chemical correlations among COMs.  
  > "Moreover, comparisons of the very limited sample can not testify the potential chemical link among species well." (Discussions / Chemical differentiation / Chemical relationships of Oxygen-bearing molecules)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adopting a single core-averaged rotational temperature for species with <3 transitions (including ground-state CH3OH in MM7, MM8, and MM11) ignores temperature gradients and the fact that different species trace different spatial layers.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deriving H2 column densities from 1.3 mm continuum smoothed to 3 mm resolution (~1.5 arcsec) while fitting molecular source sizes independently in XCLASS can introduce beam-dilution and spatial-scale mismatches in fractional abundances.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Linear/correlation analyses on 4 cores in G9.62+0.19 have very low statistical degrees of freedom.
