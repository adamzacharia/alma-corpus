## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate MIRIM and MRS data and align astrometry**: JWST calibration pipeline plus custom steps (striping, background gradient, 1/f noise); astrometry aligned to Gaia DR3 stars  
  > "The MRS observations were processed with version 1.11.2 of the JWST calibration pipeline and context 1100 of the CRDS." (2 MIRI observations and data calibration); "The F560W image was realigned by measuring the centroid of three field stars with available GAIA DR3" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
   - software: JWST calibration pipeline
   - parameter: MIRIM F560W: pipeline 1.12.3, CRDS context 1188; drizzled pixel size 0.06 arcsec  
  > "The MIRIM F560W observations were calibrated with version 1.12.3 of the JWST pipeline" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration); "and context 1188 of the Calibration Reference Data System (CRDS)" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
   - parameter: MRS 1LONG cube: pipeline 1.11.2, CRDS context 1100; outlier_detection step on in stage 3  
  > "we turned on the `outlier`$\_$`detection` step in stage three of the JWST pipeline" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
   - parameter: MRS cube sampling 0.13 x 0.13 arcsec x 0.8 nm, resolving power ~3500 (85 km/s)  
  > "a resolving power of $\sim$3500 (85 km s$^{-1}$," (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Final alignment uncertainty below one MIRIM drizzle pixel (<60 mas)  
  > "The final uncertainty in the data set alignment is less than a pixel in the MIRIM drizzle images" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
2. **Build the Halpha line map**: Integrate the MRS 1LONG cube over -150 to +150 km/s around the Gaussian-fit Halpha peak  
  > "The H$\alpha$ line map was generated by integrating the MRS 1LONG cube in the velocity range" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration); "taking the H$\alpha$ emission line peak derived by the Gaussian line fit as a reference" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
   - parameter: velocity range -150 < v < 150 km/s  
  > "in the velocity range, -150 \< $v$ \[km s$^{-1}$\] \< 150" (2 MIRI observations and data calibration)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract and fit 1D Halpha spectra for the galaxy and clumps S and N**: Elliptical-aperture extraction from the MRS cube; local background median from nine emission-free apertures subtracted; aperture-loss and companion-clump corrections  
  > "We also extracted nine 1D spectra using the same aperture at different positions of the MRS FoV clean of emission from the MACS1149-JD1 galaxy." (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes); "The median, which is compatible with zero, was subtracted from the H$\alpha$ spectrum" (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
   - parameter: Integrated aperture: ellipse, semi-major 0.5 arcsec, semi-minor 0.325 arcsec, PA 115 deg  
  > "an elliptical aperture with a semi-major axis of 0.5$\ensuremath{''}$, a semi-minor axis of 0.325$\ensuremath{''}$, a position angle of 115$^\circ$" (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
   - parameter: Clump N and S apertures: semi-major 0.225 and 0.275 arcsec, PA 130 and 30 deg, semi-minor 0.175 and 0.225 arcsec  
  > "used elliptical apertures of semi-major axis of 0.225$\ensuremath{''}$ and 0.275$\ensuremath{''}$, a position angle of 130 and 30 deg" (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
   - assumption: The two clumps are unresolved sources for the MRS angular resolution when correcting aperture losses  
  > "The H$\alpha$ fluxes were corrected for aperture losses, assuming that the two clumps identified in the H$\alpha$ line map are unresolved sources for the MRS angular resolution." (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
   - fitting: One-component Gaussian plus second-order polynomial for the residual background gradient  
  > "The H$\alpha$ spectrum was modeled by a one-component Gaussian function and a second-order polynomial to fit the emission line and any residual background gradient, respectively." (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
   - validation: Monte Carlo with 1000 noisy realisations; uncertainty is the standard deviation of the refits; sum of clump fluxes agrees with total  
  > "This noise was used to generate new spectra (n = 1000), where a random Gaussian noise with a sigma equal to the rms was added to the original spectrum before the lines were fitted again." (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes); "The total H$\alpha$ flux and the sum of the individual clump fluxes are in agreement within the uncertainties." (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
4. **Measure F560W and F160W photometry**: Aperture photometry with the same ellipses, annulus background and aperture-loss corrections; GALFIT decomposition of F560W (two point sources plus Sersic)  
  > "The MIRIM F560W total photometry was generated using the same aperture as for the 1D spectral extraction of H$\alpha$." (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry); "we used two unresolved sources to fit the central emission of the galaxy (corresponding to C2 and C3) and a Sérsic profile to fit the diffuse emission (corresponding to G)." (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
   - software: GALFIT, Photutils
   - parameter: Background annulus inner radius 0.7 arcsec, outer radius 1.5 arcsec  
  > "with an inner radius of 0.7$\ensuremath{''}$ and outer radius of 1.5$\ensuremath{''}$" (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
   - parameter: F560W: 37% of flux outside aperture; F160W: 28% outside aperture  
  > "The percentage of flux outside the selected elliptical aperture is 37% of the total" (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry); "The percentage of flux outside the selected elliptical aperture is 28%." (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
   - assumption: Emission treated as unresolved for aperture corrections; F160W light dominated by the bright NIRCam clumps because the GALFIT model for F160W did not converge  
  > "The GALFIT model for the HST F160W image does not converge to a solution, so we assumed that the UV light is dominated by the bright clumps identified in the NIRCam images" (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
   - fitting: GALFIT surface-brightness fit of F560W  
  > "we modeled the surface brightness distribution in F560W using GALFIT" (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
   - validation: GALFIT total (0.33 +/- 0.03 uJy) agrees with the total-aperture flux; F160W agrees with NIRCam/NIRISS at 1.5 um  
  > "The total flux given by the sum of all individual components is 0.33 $\pm$ 0.03 $\mu$Jy, which is in perfect agreement with the one obtained in the total aperture." (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
5. **Derive Halpha-based instantaneous SFR**: Halpha luminosity to SFR conversion (Chabrier IMF) with f_esc = 0; lower conversion factor for 0.28 Zsun  
  > "we derived the instantaneous SFR directly from the H$\alpha$ flux following" (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate); "a slightly lower H$\alpha$ luminosity to SFR conversion factor (3.236 $\times$ 10$^{-42}$ for 0.28 $Z_{\odot}$," (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate)
   - parameter: f_esc = 0  
  > "we assumed $f_\mathrm{esc}$ = 0 for comparison with other works" (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate)
   - assumption: No significant internal extinction  
  > "Considering no significant internal extinction is present" (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF  
  > "for a Chabrier IMF and a given escape fraction ($f_\mathrm{esc}$) for the ionizing photons" (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with UV-continuum SFR from F160W and the [CII]158um SFR  
  > "These SFR values agree with those derived from the UV continuum using the HST F160W image" (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate)
6. **Derive ionizing photon production efficiency zeta_ion**: zeta_ion = N_LyC / L_UV, with N_LyC from the Halpha luminosity assuming recombination and L_UV from the HST F160W UV imaging  
  > "The ionizing photon production efficiency is given as the ratio of the ionizing to the non-ionizing UV flux." (4.3 Ionizing photon production efficiency); "the latter were obtained by also using available UV imaging" (Summary)
   - parameter: Electron temperature 1.5e4 K for the Halpha-to-N_LyC factor; f_esc = 0  
  > "We chose the factor for a temperature of 1.5 $\times$ 10$^4$ K, measured in low-metallicity low-$z$ galaxies" (4.3 Ionizing photon production efficiency); "Assuming an escaping fraction of zero" (4.3 Ionizing photon production efficiency)
   - assumption: Case B recombination with low density ISM  
  > "and low densities (e.g.," (4.3 Ionizing photon production efficiency)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with canonical value and with other z>6 JWST samples (EIGER, JADES, MIDIS, CEERS)  
  > "A comparison of the photon production efficiency of MACS1149-JD1 and its clumps with galaxies at higher redshifts" (4.3 Ionizing photon production efficiency)
7. **Derive rest-frame EW0(Halpha)**: Subtract the [OIII]4960,5008 contribution from the F560W flux to get the continuum, extrapolate flat in F_nu to Halpha, divide the Halpha flux by that continuum  
  > "First, we measured the rest-frame optical ($\sim$0.55 $\mu$m) continuum by subtracting the contribution of \[OIII\]4960,5008$\AA$ emission lines from the F560W flux." (4.4 EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) and redshift evolution); "Finally, the EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) was derived by the ratio between the H$\alpha$ flux and the continuum flux under the H$\alpha$." (4.4 EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) and redshift evolution)
   - parameter: [OIII]5008/Halpha = 2 (range 1 to 3.5 for uncertainties)  
  > "We considered a \[OIII\]5008$\AA$/H$\alpha$ flux ratio equal to two" (4.4 EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) and redshift evolution); "We used a range in the \[OIII\]5008$\AA$/H$\alpha$ flux ratio between 1 to 3.5 to determine the uncertainties." (4.4 EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) and redshift evolution)
   - assumption: Flat optical continuum in F_nu extrapolated to the Halpha wavelength  
  > "we assumed a flat optical continuum spectrum in $F_\mathrm{\nu}$ in order to extrapolate the derived optical ($\sim$0.55 $\mu$m) continuum fluxes to the H$\alpha$ wavelength" (4.4 EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) and redshift evolution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the (1+z)^2.1 extrapolation and JWST z=6-8 measurements  
  > "The EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) of MACS1149-JD1 is a factor of two lower than the extrapolated value at a redshift of 9.11" (4.4 EW$_{0}$ (H$\alpha$) and redshift evolution)
8. **Estimate dynamical mass from Halpha size and velocity dispersion**: Virial expression M_dyn = K R_hm sigma_V^2 / G with K = 6, sigma_V from the deconvolved Halpha profile, R_hm from the PSF-deconvolved Halpha half-light radius corrected for magnification  
  > "an estimate of the dynamical mass can be given assuming the virial expression" (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas); "The velocity dispersion ($\sigma_\mathrm{V}$ = 69 $\pm$ 5 km s$^{-1}$) is derived from the H$\alpha$ line profile after deconvolution with the instrumental response." (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas)
   - parameter: K = 6; G = 4.3e-3 pc Msun^-1 km^2 s^-2  
  > "where $G$ is the gravitational constant with a value of 4.3 $\times$ 10$^{-3}$ pc M$_{\odot}^{-1}$ km$^2$ s$^{-2}$ and $K$ is set to a value of six" (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas)
   - parameter: R_e = 4.522 (a b)^0.5 mu^-0.5 pc with a, b in mas (4.522 kpc/arcsec at z = 9.1092)  
  > "1 arcsec corresponds to 4.522 kpc at $z$ = 9.1092" (Introduction)
   - assumption: Kinematics compatible with a dispersion-dominated or slow-rotation system (Vrot/sigma = 0.69); magnification 11.5  
  > "Under the assumption that the spatially resolved \[OIII\]88$\mu$m kinematics are compatible with a dispersion-dominated or a slow rotational system" (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the dynamical mass range from the ALMA [OIII]88um velocity field  
  > "This value is within the range derived from the spatially resolved \[OIII\]88$\mu$m velocity field (0.7 $-$ 3.7 $\times$ 10$^9$ $M_{\odot}$," (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas)

## Qualitative findings

- Halpha emission splits into two clumps S and N carrying ~60% and ~40% of the total flux, matching the rest-frame UV morphology  
  > "The structure of the H$\alpha$ emitting gas consists of two clumps, S and N, carrying about $60\%$ and $40\%$ of the total flux, respectively." (abstract)
- Internal dust extinction is not relevant and star formation is unobscured  
  > "These results support the hypothesis that internal dust extinction is not relevant and that the star formation is unobscured in MACS1149-JD1." (Summary)
- [NII] and [SII] lines are not detected  
  > "Fainter \[NII\]6550,6565$\AA$ and \[SII\]6718,6733$\AA$ emission lines were not detected with an observed 3$\sigma$ upper limit of 2.1 $\times$ 10$^{-18}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$" (3.1 H$\alpha$ spectra and line fluxes)
- Halpha redshift and velocity dispersion agree with ALMA [OIII]88um  
  > "agree with that of the \[OIII\]88$\mu$m line previously measured with ALMA" (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MIRI is the only instrument able to observe Halpha at z above ~7  
  > "is the only instrument that can provide spectroscopic observations of the H$\alpha$ emission line of galaxies at redshift above approximately seven" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Rest-frame UV imaging plus Balmer-line spectroscopy gives a direct measure of the ionizing flux  
  > "Deep rest-frame ultraviolet imaging combined with the spectroscopy of hydrogen Balmer emission lines provides an opportunity to directly measure the ionizing flux" (Introduction)
- Alternative: GALFIT decomposition of the HST F160W image was tried but did not converge, so aperture corrections for unresolved clumps were used instead  
  > "The GALFIT model for the HST F160W image does not converge to a solution" (3.2 MIRIM F560W and HST F160W photometry)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): f_esc cannot be derived, so zero was assumed  
  > "As we are not in the position to derive $f_\mathrm{esc}$ for this source, we assumed $f_\mathrm{esc}$ = 0 for comparison with other works." (4.2 Instantaneous star-formation rate)
- Limitation (authors): Only an upper limit on the emitting region size is obtained, so the dynamical mass is uncertain  
  > "Our measurements give only an upper size for the emitting regions." (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas)
- Limitation (authors): Higher resolution velocity maps and longer-wavelength imaging are required  
  > "Higher angular resolution velocity maps with NIRSpec/JWST and ALMA and imaging at wavelengths longer than 4.4 $\mu$m with MIRI/JWST are required" (4.6 Kinematics of the ionized gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data are used only through previously published [OIII]88um results and a shared spectrum, so the ALMA comparison is not independently derived
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sub-solar SFR uncertainty is 0.3 in the abstract and Table but 0.2 in Sect. 4.2
- Caveat (inferred, ours): EW0(Halpha) depends strongly on the assumed [OIII]5008/Halpha ratio (17% to 67% of F560W), giving large asymmetric errors and a lower limit for clump N
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Aperture corrections assume unresolved clumps while the dynamical mass uses a resolved size; the size is only an upper limit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Clump N dispersion (113 +/- 33 km/s) comes from a faint, low-S/N line, so the outflow interpretation is tentative
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA code 2017.1.01493.S printed in the paper differs from the archive-linked 2017.1.01423.S
