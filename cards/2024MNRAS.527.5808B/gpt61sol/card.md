## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble total optical/NIR photometry**: Use neighbour-subtracted, aperture-corrected REBELS photometry and COSMOS2020 Classic photometry for ALPINE; exclude ECDFS sources.  
  > "The aperture flux was corrected to total according to the MOPHONGO model for the galaxy." (Optical and near-infrared imaging); "We used the ‘Classic’ catalogue that provides photometry measured in $2''$ diameter apertures." (Optical and near-infrared imaging); "we excluded these ten sources from further analysis, leaving a final ALPINE sample of 108 galaxies." (Optical and near-infrared imaging)
   - software: MOPHONGO, Source Extractor
   - parameter: REBELS aperture diameters: 0.6 arcsec; IRAC: 0.9 arcsec.  
  > "Photometry was extracted using $0.6''$ diameter apertures ($0.9''$ for IRAC)" (Optical and near-infrared imaging)
   - parameter: COSMOS2020 aperture-to-total correction from Source Extractor MAG_AUTO.  
  > "In the COSMOS2020 catalogue this aperture photometry is corrected to a total flux by a constant factor determined from the Source Extractor MAG_AUTO." (Optical and near-infrared imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Empty-aperture photometric errors; visual isolation checks for ALPINE.  
  > "Errors on the photometry were derived from empty aperture measurements on the data." (Optical and near-infrared imaging); "We visually checked the ALPINE sources in the COSMOS optical and NIR imaging, but found that they are all sufficiently isolated for the catalogue fluxes to be robust." (Stacking analysis)
2. **Bin galaxies and stack measurements**: Weighted-mean image-plane ALMA stacking at rest-frame UV positions; average catalogue optical/NIR fluxes; bootstrap with replacement.  
  > "We performed weighted mean stacking of the ALMA continuum data in the specified bins in the image plane." (Stacking analysis); "we stack at the position of the observed rest-frame UV." (Stacking analysis); "To determine the mean optical and NIR photometric measurements we averaged the fluxes from the REBELS and COSMOS2020 catalogues" (Stacking analysis)
   - software: PyBDSF
   - parameter: REBELS stacks: z = 6.5–6.9 and 6.9–7.7; MUV splits at −22.0 and −22.5 respectively.  
  > "The first bin included galaxies at $z = 6.5$–$6.9$ (20 galaxies), with the second bin included galaxies in the range $z = 6.9$–$7.7$ (20 galaxies)." (Stacking analysis); "We split the sub-samples by ${M_{\rm UV}}$$\,= -22.0$ (${M_{\rm UV}}$$\, = -22.5$) at $z \simeq 6.7$ ($z \simeq 7.2$)" (Stacking analysis)
   - parameter: Exclude REBELS-16 and REBELS-40; do not stack the seven z > 7.7 galaxies.  
  > "We excluded the *HST* selected sources REBELS-16 and REBELS-40 from the stacks" (Stacking analysis); "Due to the wide range of photometric redshifts in this bin, and the differing ALMA measurement bands, we do not create a stack from this sub-sample." (Stacking analysis)
   - parameter: ALPINE: MUV split at −22.0 and additional stellar-mass bins; final stacks contain 54 and 32 galaxies.  
  > "For ALPINE we split by ${M_{\rm UV}}$$\,= -22.0$ for both redshift bins, and in addition we separate into two stellar mass bins" (Stacking analysis); "a final ALPINE sample of 54 galaxies within the $z \simeq 4.5$ bin and 32 within the $z \simeq 5.5$ bin." (Stacking analysis)
   - parameter: Mask foreground objects using PyBDSF maps, excluding pixels within 1.5 arcsec of the UV centroid.  
  > "Foreground sources were masked based on a map of objects derived from PyBDSF (cite: Mohan15), excluding pixels within a radius of $1.5$$''$ from the rest-frame UV centroid of the galaxy." (Stacking analysis)
   - assumption: UV–FIR offsets expected to have a small effect given the large beams.  
  > "Given the large beam of the REBELS and ALPINE observations, and the relatively small absolute offsets found for these samples" (Stacking analysis); "we expect the effect to be small." (Stacking analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Median stacking gives unchanged results; bootstrap resampling determines stacked-flux errors.  
  > "Our results are unchanged if we instead use a median stacking procedure." (Stacking analysis); "Errors on the stacked flux measurements were determined by remaking each stack using bootstrap resampling with replacement." (Stacking analysis)
3. **Fit stellar SEDs and derive physical properties**: BAGPIPES fits to individual and stacked photometry, using bands above the Lyman break through 4.5 μm.  
  > "We fit the photometric data for the individual REBELS and ALPINE sources (and the derived stacks) using BAGPIPES (cite: Carnall18)" (SED fitting); "We include bands above the Lyman-break reaching to the $[4.5\mu{\rm m}]$ filter" (SED fitting)
   - software: BAGPIPES
   - parameter: Fix spectroscopic redshift when available; fix stacked photometry to average redshift; exclude break-containing bands from stacks.  
  > "We fix the redshift to the spectroscopic redshift when available (28 sources in REBELS, all of the sources in ALPINE)" (SED fitting); "for the stacked photometry we fix the redshift to the average redshift." (SED fitting); "We also exclude bands that contain the Lyman-break in the fitting of the stacked photometry" (SED fitting)
   - parameter: REBELS stack filters: YJHKs + IRAC 3.6/4.5 μm at z = 6.5–6.9; JHKs + IRAC above z = 6.9.  
  > "we fit to the $YJHK_{s}$$[3.6\mu{\rm m}]$$[4.5\mu{\rm m}]$ bands for the $z = 6.5$–$6.9$ sub-sample stack" (SED fitting); "to the $JHK_{s}$$[3.6\mu{\rm m}]$$[4.5\mu{\rm m}]$ bands for the $z > 6.9$ stack." (SED fitting)
   - parameter: ALPINE stack filters: IzYJHKs + IRAC at z = 4.5; zYJHKs + IRAC at z = 5.5.  
  > "we fit to the $IzYJHK_{s}$$[3.6\mu{\rm m}]$$[4.5\mu{\rm m}]$bands for the $z = 4.5$ stack" (SED fitting); "to the $zYJHK_{s}$$[3.6\mu{\rm m}]$$[4.5\mu{\rm m}]$bands for the $z = 5.5$ stack." (SED fitting)
   - assumption: Delayed-τ SFH; τ = [0.3, 10.0] Gyr; age 1 Myr to age of Universe.  
  > "A delayed-$\tau$ model was assumed ($\Phi(t) \propto t e^{-(t/\tau)}$)" (SED fitting); "$\tau = [0.3, 10.0]\,{\rm Gyr}$ and the age from $1\,{\rm Myr}$ up to the age of the Universe at that redshift." (SED fitting)
   - assumption: Metallicity 0.2 Z⊙; Calzetti00 dust law; AV = [0, 2]; log10(U) = [−2, −4].  
  > "The metallicity was fixed to $0.2\,{\rm Z}_{\odot}$" (SED fitting); "the (cite: Calzetti00) dust law was assumed with the attenuation in the $V$-band constrained to the range $A_{\rm V} = [0, 2]$." (SED fitting); "We allowed the nebular ionization parameter to vary in the range ${\rm log}_{10}(U) = [-2, -4]$." (SED fitting)
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1; Ωm = 0.3; ΩΛ = 0.7.  
  > "$H_{0} = 70 \, {\rm km}\,{\rm s}^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.3$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.7$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Uniform priors for all fitted parameters.  
  > "Uniform priors were assumed for all of the fitted parameters." (SED fitting)
   - validation: Acceptable fits and untruncated corner plots; alternate parametric SFH/metallicity changes M⋆ by at most 0.1 dex and β by <0.05.  
  > "with no evidence for truncation of the resulting corner plots." (SED fitting); "Assuming a different SFH (e.g. constant or $\tau$) or metallicity only marginally affected the $M_{\star}$ by at most $0.1\,{\rm dex}$ and the derived $\beta$ values by $< 0.05$." (SED fitting)
4. **Measure UV luminosity and slope**: Measure 1500 Å luminosity and β from best-fitting BAGPIPES SEDs; restrict β fitting to Calzetti windows.  
  > "The monochromatic rest-frame UV luminosity was derived at $1500\,$Å using a top-hat filter of width $100\,$Å" (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination); "In this work we measure $\beta$ from the best-fitting SED model derived from BAGPIPES" (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination); "excluding regions that are outside of the Calzetti windows, to avoid strong absorption or emission features." (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination)
   - software: BAGPIPES
   - parameter: 1500 Å top-hat width 100 Å; β continuum windows span 1268–2580 Å.  
  > "at $1500\,$Å using a top-hat filter of width $100\,$Å" (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination); "from $\lambda_{\rm rest} = 1268$–$2580\,$Å (cite: Calzetti94)." (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination)
   - assumption: Aperture-corrected MUV represents a total absolute magnitude.  
  > "the derived $M_{\rm UV}$ can be considered a total absolute magnitude." (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination)
   - fitting: Power-law fit to the SED within continuum windows.  
  > "computed the rest-frame UV slope using an identical method to that used on the ALPINE and REBELS stacks, by fitting a power law to the resulting predicted SED." (Comparison to models)
   - validation: Bootstrap restacking and BAGPIPES refitting; compare with original ALPINE β estimates.  
  > "Errors were derived using a bootstrap analysis, where we restacked the photometry and re-fit using BAGPIPES." (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination); "with find on average a very mild bias to bluer slopes by $0.05$ in our analysis" (Rest-frame UV luminosity and slope determination)
5. **Measure ALMA continuum flux**: PyBDSF peak and Gaussian flux measurements; use peak for unresolved stacks and Gaussian flux for resolved high-mass ALPINE stacks.  
  > "Using PyBDSF (cite: Mohan15) we measured both the peak flux and flux derived from a Gaussian fit for the individual sources and the stacked ALMA data." (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation); "Hence we used the peak flux measure in these cases." (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation); "For the ALPINE sample at ${\rm log}_{10}(M_{\star}/{\rm M}_{\odot})$$> 10$ we instead used the Gaussian flux measurement" (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)
   - software: PyBDSF
   - parameter: Unresolved if major and minor axes agree with beam size within 1σ.  
  > "We define an unresolved source if the measured major and minor axes from PyBDSF are consistent with the beam size within the $1\sigma$ error" (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)
   - fitting: Gaussian source fit with PyBDSF.  
  > "flux derived from a Gaussian fit for the individual sources and the stacked ALMA data." (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)

- **MOPHONGO** : Neighbour subtraction and aperture-to-total corrections for REBELS photometry.  
  > "on images where the neighbouring sources had been subtracted using MOPHONGO (cite: Labbe15)." (Optical and near-infrared imaging); "The aperture flux was corrected to total according to the MOPHONGO model for the galaxy." (Optical and near-infrared imaging)
- **Source Extractor** : MAG_AUTO determines COSMOS2020 aperture-to-total correction.  
  > "In the COSMOS2020 catalogue this aperture photometry is corrected to a total flux by a constant factor determined from the Source Extractor MAG_AUTO." (Optical and near-infrared imaging)
- **PyBDSF** : Foreground-object maps, peak/Gaussian continuum fluxes and source-size errors.  
  > "Foreground sources were masked based on a map of objects derived from PyBDSF (cite: Mohan15)" (Stacking analysis); "Using PyBDSF (cite: Mohan15) we measured both the peak flux and flux derived from a Gaussian fit for the individual sources and the stacked ALMA data." (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)
- **BAGPIPES** : Individual/stacked photometric SED fits, stellar properties and models for UV-slope measurements.  
  > "using BAGPIPES (cite: Carnall18) to provide a best-fitting model with which to measure the rest-frame UV slope." (SED fitting); "The fitting also provides physical properties for the stacks" (SED fitting)
- **DELPHI** : Semi-analytic model comparison; output from Mauerhofer23 reanalysed.  
  > "we present the most recent analysis of dust emission from the DELPHI SAM in comparison to our derived ${\rm IRX}$–$\beta$ results from REBELS and ALPINE." (Comparison to models); "We took the output of the model presented in (cite: Mauerhofer23)" (Comparison to models)
- **MAFUSA** : Literature comparison of attenuation-law predictions.  
  > "(cite: Narayanan18) used the MAFUSA model to predict the attenuation law at $z = 6$." (Comparison to models)
- **FLARES** : Literature comparison of attenuation curves and star-dust geometry.  
  > "(cite: Vijayan23) used the FLARES simulation to investigate the expected dust attenuation curve for galaxies at $z = 5$–$10$" (Comparison to models)
- **SERRA** : Literature comparison of high-redshift simulated galaxies.  
  > "The zoom-in cosmological SERRA simulations presented in (cite: Pallottini22) produces galaxies at $z \simeq 7.7$" (Comparison to models)
- **MassiveFIRE** : Literature comparison of IRX–β predictions.

## Qualitative findings

- Bright REBELS galaxies favour a flattening or possible turnover of the colour-magnitude relation.  
  > "Our results support a flattening, and potentially even a turn-over, of the colour-magnitude relationship" (Colour-magnitude relation at z ≃ 7)
- With the common FIR SED, stacked ALPINE and REBELS IRX–β measurements agree with a Calzetti-like relation.  
  > "consistent with a Calzetti-like relationship as found at $z = 0$, with our assumed rest-frame FIR SED for both samples." (Implications for the dust attenuation curve at z > 4)
- Little evolution in IRX–β from z ≃ 4 to z ≃ 7.2 under approximately constant FIR SED and intrinsic UV slope.  
  > "We instead find little evolution in the ${\rm IRX}$–$\beta$ relation from $z \simeq 4$ to $z \simeq 7.2$ from our analysis." (Implications for the dust attenuation curve at z > 4); "This relies on our assumption that the FIR SED (and intrinsic rest-frame UV slope) remains approximately constant between the samples and redshift ranges" (Implications for the dust attenuation curve at z > 4)
- Higher-mass ALPINE galaxies are redder and have higher IRX.  
  > "the galaxies at ${\rm log}_{10}(M_{\star}/{\rm M}_{\odot})$$> 10$ showing considerably redder colours with $\beta \gtrsim -1.75$ and a higher IRX by $\simeq 0.75$." (IRX–β from z = 4-8 from ALPINE and REBELS)
- DELPHI predictions agree with the observed mass dependence of IRX and β.  
  > "The predicted change in IRX and $\beta$ with increasing stellar mass matches very well with what we find in the data" (Comparison to models)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Large homogeneous high-redshift samples with direct dust detections or strong limits, extending consistently from z ≃ 4 to 8.  
  > "REBELS provides a unique sample of galaxies with direct dust detections (or strong upper limits)" (Introduction); "Where possible we used identical approaches for the REBELS and ALPINE analysis to provide a direct comparison between galaxies spanning $z \simeq 4$ to $z \simeq 8$." (Methods)
- Why this method: Stacking addresses low continuum detection fractions; MUV binning provides dynamic range while reducing scatter-induced biases.  
  > "Due to the relatively low fraction of sources that are directly detected in the dust continuum (0.43 for REBELS, 0.19 for ALPINE), stacking is a key tool" (Methods); "We further choose to split the sample using $M_{\rm UV}$ as this provided the greatest dynamic range" (Stacking analysis); "as the $\beta$ and $M_{\star}$ values have significant statistical errors, scatter between bins can lead to biases" (Stacking analysis)
- Alternative: Median stacking instead of weighted mean: unchanged results.  
  > "Our results are unchanged if we instead use a median stacking procedure." (Stacking analysis)
- Alternative: Optical/NIR image stacking did not improve on catalogue-flux stacking because of neighbour contamination.  
  > "We also experimented with stacking the images themselves, however we concluded that it was not possible to improve on the flux stacking results, due to close neighbours contaminating the flux measurements." (Stacking analysis)
- Alternative: Luminosity-weighted instead of average stack redshift: no effect.  
  > "We found that using a luminosity-weighted redshift instead of an average had no effect on our results, as the difference was $\delta z \le 0.03$." (SED fitting)
- Alternative: Alternate parametric SFHs or metallicity have small effects; standard parametric masses retained for literature comparison.  
  > "Assuming a different SFH (e.g. constant or $\tau$) or metallicity only marginally affected the $M_{\star}$ by at most $0.1\,{\rm dex}$ and the derived $\beta$ values by $< 0.05$." (SED fitting); "we primarily consider the $M_{\star}$ values derived with standard parametric SFHs" (SED fitting)
- Alternative: SMC dust law in optical SED fits produces β slopes redder by around 0.1.  
  > "the dervied $\beta$-slopes being redder by around $0.1$ when fitting with an SMC dust law in comparison to a Calzetti law." (SED fitting)
- Alternative: Lower ALPINE dust temperature would lower its IRX–β relation by 0.25 dex.  
  > "then we would recover a lower ${\rm IRX}$–$\beta$ relation for the ALPINE dataset by $0.25\,{\rm dex}$." (Comparison to previous results from the ALPINE survey)
- Alternative: Uniform single-band FIR estimates retained despite multiband observations for some sources.  
  > "we choose here to provide a uniform measure of $L_{\rm IR}$ from the single main band that is available for the full REBELS + ALPINE samples." (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)
- How they decided it was enough: Mean/median stack agreement, SED-fit checks and a 1σ beam-size criterion for choosing peak versus Gaussian flux.  
  > "Our results are unchanged if we instead use a median stacking procedure." (Stacking analysis); "with no evidence for truncation of the resulting corner plots." (SED fitting); "We define an unresolved source if the measured major and minor axes from PyBDSF are consistent with the beam size within the $1\sigma$ error" (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)
- Limitation (authors): Individual dust temperatures are unavailable for most sources; a single FIR SED is assumed.  
  > "Due to the lack of $T_{\rm d}$ measurements for the vast majority of our sample, we are unable to account for this in our analysis and leave it to a future work." (Rest-frame FIR luminosity derivation)
- Limitation (authors): ALPINE and REBELS have different selection functions, including differing Lyα-emitter fractions.  
  > "Another important caveat is that the ALPINE and REBELS samples were selected in different ways" (Caveats); "this difference could cause a bias to lower IRX values in the ALPINE sample." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): UV/Lyα selection misses highly obscured galaxies.  
  > "Due to the rest-frame UV based Lyman-break selection (or Lyman-$\alpha$ selection for some ALPINE sources), our samples will be incomplete to any highly obscured sources." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Parametric SFHs may underestimate stellar mass.  
  > "(cite: Topping22) has shown that these measurement may underestimate the mass by on average $0.5\,{\rm dex}$." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): IRX–M⋆ slope is weakly constrained by the limited stellar-mass range.  
  > "the derived slope is only weakly constrained due to the small dynamic range in stellar mass" (The IRX–M⋆ relation at z = 4–8)
- Limitation (authors): UV–FIR offsets may suppress stacked peak fluxes.  
  > "this could cause an underestimate of the peak ALMA flux if there exists significant offsets between the rest-frame UV and FIR emission" (Stacking analysis)
- Limitation (authors): High-redshift individual β values are uncertain because of few bands and photometric-redshift degeneracy.  
  > "due to the few bands ($H, K_s$) available for fitting, and the broader uncertainty in photometric redshift leading to a degeneracy between redshift and slope." (Individual REBELS galaxies at z > 7.7)
- Limitation (authors): IRX–β alone does not uniquely establish the dust attenuation curve.  
  > "taking the observed ${\rm IRX}$–$\beta$ relation as evidence for a particular dust attenuation curve can be problematic due to the myriad of other factors that can influence the observed relation" (Implications for the dust attenuation curve at z > 4)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stated ALPINE stacking intervals z = 4.0–4.5 and 4.8–5.4 conflict with tabulated mean redshifts near 4.52 and 5.57.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): IRX is defined logarithmically in the introduction, while the IRX–β equation uses a ratio-like expression and the mass relation applies log10 to IRX; resolve conventions before implementation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The supplied IRX–β equation has unmatched parentheses; do not silently repair its algebra.
