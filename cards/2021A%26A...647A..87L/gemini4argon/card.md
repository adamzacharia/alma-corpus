## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum subtraction and spectrum extraction for SMM1-a and IRAS 16293B**: Image spectral windows without continuum subtraction, run STATCONT corrected sigma-clipping to locate line-free channels, run CASA uvcontsub in the uv-plane, re-image, and extract spectra at SMM1-a continuum peak and IRAS 16293B offset positions; also compute moment 0 maps integrated over 8 velocity bins.  
  > "Therefore, the STATCONT outputs were used to identify the line-free channels in the spectra and the continuum was subtracted in the uv-plane with the uvcontsub task in CASA." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1); "From the resulting datacube, the SMM1a hot core spectrum was extracted towards the peak continuum position α J2000 = 18:29:49.793, δ J2000 = +1.15.20.200." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1); "Both lines are integrated over 8 velocity bins, centered on the peak frequency of each line as determined towards SMM1-a." (Figure 2 caption)
   - software: CASA, STATCONT
   - parameter: SMM1-a extraction position: α_J2000 = 18:29:49.793, δ_J2000 = +1.15.20.200  
  > "From the resulting datacube, the SMM1a hot core spectrum was extracted towards the peak continuum position α J2000 = 18:29:49.793, δ J2000 = +1.15.20.200." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1)
   - parameter: Line-free bandwidth fraction: ≥20% (10% in two spectral windows)  
  > "Line-free channels are sparse, but for most spectral windows, at least 20% of the bandwidth was given as input to the uvcontsub task, with the exception of two spectral windows, where only 10% of the bandwidth was line-free." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1)
   - assumption: SMM1-a background temperature T_bg = 5.2 K from average continuum flux density (0.41 mJy beam^-1); IRAS 16293B T_BG = 21 K (full-beam offset) and 52 K (half-beam offset)  
  > "From the average continuum flux density (0.41 mJy beam −1 ), the background temperature was determined to be ∼5.2 K." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1); "At the full-beam offset position T BG = 21 K, while at the half-beam offset position it is T BG = 52 K." (2.2. PILS observations and spectra of IRAS 16293B)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Synthetic spectrum fitting and column density determination**: Initial by-eye LTE synthetic spectrum fit in CASSIS using CDMS/JPL line lists, followed by MCMC chi-squared minimisation on optically thin unblended transitions to derive N_T, T_ex, V_LSR, and ΔV; scale optically thick main isotopologues from minor isotopologues.  
  > "Given a spectroscopic line list as input, CASSIS can produce synthetic spectra of a molecule based on parameters such as column density ( N T ), excitation temperature ( T ex ), peak gas velocity ( V LSR ), line width at half maximum (∆ V ), and source size (θ source )." (2.3. Analysis method); "The by-eye fit results were given as starting parameters for the MCMC χ 2 minimisation routine." (2.3. Analysis method); "The main isotopologues of CH 3 OH and HNCO are optically thick and their column densities are therefore determined from minor isotopologues." (3.1. SMM1)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: T_ex free range: 50 – 350 K; N_T free range: two orders of magnitude centered on by-eye fit  
  > "The column density was given as a free parameter over two orders of magnitude centered on the by-eye fit column density and the excitation temperature was a free parameter" (2.3 Analysis method)
   - parameter: SMM1-a: ΔV = 1.0 – 4.0 km s^-1, V_LSR = 6.0 – 9.0 km s^-1, θ_source = 1''.2, T_bg = 5.2 K  
  > "For SMM1-a, ∆ V was a free parameter from 1.0 – 4.0 km s −1, and the source velocity was a free parameter between 6.0 – 9.0 km s −1 ." (2.3. Analysis method); "The source size was assumed to be equal to the beam size and taken to be 1 ′′ . 2, resulting in a beam filling factor of 0.5." (2.3. Analysis method)
   - parameter: IRAS 16293B: ΔV = 1.0 km s^-1 (fixed), V_LSR = 2.7 km s^-1 (fixed), θ_source = 0''.5, T_bg = 21 K  
  > "For the analysis of IRAS 16293B, ∆ V and V LSR were fixed to 1.0 km s −1 and 2.7 km s −1, respectively. The source size was assumed to be equal to the beam size at 0 ′′ . 5, while a background continuum temperature of T bg = 21 K was used." (2.3. Analysis method)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and optically thin lines (τ ≪ 1.0)  
  > "For the analysis, optically thin lines (τ ≪ 1.0) were used." (2.3. Analysis method); "The molecules were assumed to be in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (2.3. Analysis method)
   - assumption: Source size equal to beam size (beam filling factor = 0.5)  
  > "The source size was assumed to be equal to the beam size and taken to be 1 ′′ . 2, resulting in a beam filling factor of 0.5." (2.3. Analysis method)
   - assumption: Isotopic ratios 16O/18O = 560 and 12C/13C = 52.5±15.4; T_ex = 200 K for SMM1-a upper limits  
  > "Main isotopologue column densities are determined by applying the ratios 16 O/ 18 O = 560 and 12 C/ 13 C = 52.5 to the minor isotopologue column densities." (Table 2); "For undetected species, upper limit column densities are determined by assuming T ex = 200 K, which is chosen as a representative excitation temperature from the molecules that are detected." (3.1. SMM1)
   - fitting: Monte-Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) algorithm and χ^2 minimisation routine in CASSIS; errors calculated from spread in χ^2 around minimum to 3σ confidence level including flux uncertainty.  
  > "These parameters were given as free parameters to a Monte-Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) algorithm and χ 2 minimisation routine." (2.3. Analysis method); "Errors on physical parameters take the uncertainty of the fit and the flux uncertainty as input and are calculated from the spread in χ 2 values around the minimum to a 3σ confidence level." (2.3. Analysis method)
   - validation: Check line positions for blending species with A_ij > 1×10^-6 and E_up of 0–1000 K; compare against full-inventory synthetic spectrum in IRAS 16293B.  
  > "Blending species were identified by checking the line position for other lines of known hot core / corino species with A ij - 1×10 −6 and E up of 0–1000 K." (2.3. Analysis method); "position is added in green. Note that this synthetic spectrum only includes molecules listed in previous publications and does not include HOCH 2 CN." (3.2 IRAS 16293B)
3. **Abundance ratio comparison and statistical distance calculation**: Compute abundance ratios relative to CH3OH and HNCO and calculate statistical distances S_(X/Y) in sigma between molecular ratios in SMM1-a and IRAS 16293B.  
  > "To gain further insight in how the chemical compositions of SMM1-a and IRAS 16293B differ, the statistical distance of molecular ratios are plotted in Fig. 9." (4.1. SMM1-a: A HOCH 2 CN-rich source); "The statistical distance of molecular ratios between SMM1-a and IRAS 16293B is calculated according to the following equation:" (Appendix E: Statistical distance)

   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CH3NCO and HOCH2CN are simultaneously detected towards Serpens SMM1-a with equal abundances within uncertainties, indicating kinetic rather than thermodynamic control.  
  > "The equal abundances of these molecules in SMM1-a indicate that their formation is driven by kinetic processes instead of thermodynamic equilibrium, which would drive the chemistry to one product." (abstract)
- HOCH2CN is identified in IRAS 16293B PILS data at the half-beam offset position (compact emission), but only tentatively at the full-beam offset position.  
  > "At the half-beam offset position (see Fig. 3), which is closer to the continuum peak of IRAS 16293B, HOCH 2 CN can be identified." (3.2. IRAS 16293B); "Because the chemical inventory at the full-beam offset position of the PILS data is best characterised, the tentative detection of HOCH 2 CN towards this position, with a column density" (3.2 IRAS 16293B)
- HOCH2CN and HNCO are significantly enhanced in SMM1-a compared to IRAS 16293B (by 3-6 sigma in statistical distance), possibly due to prolonged warm grain mantle processing of HCN.  
  > "The relatively high abundances of HOCH 2 CN and HNCO in SMM1-a may be explained by a prolonged stage of relatively warm ice mantles, where thermal and energetic processing of HCN in the ice results in the efficient formation of both species." (abstract); "Fig. 9 highlights that HOCH 2 CN and HNCO are more abundant in SMM1-a (σ ranging from 3 – 6) and CH 3 NCO is moderately more abundant in SMM1-a (σ ≥ 2)." (4.1. SMM1-a: A HOCH 2 CN-rich source)
- The [CH3NCO]/[HNCO] ratio is uniform at ~10% across low-, intermediate-, and high-mass star-forming regions, suggesting a chemical link and early formation in ice mantles.  
  > "These ratios are found to be uniform throughout all sources at [CH 3 NCO]/[HNCO] = ∼10 %. This indicates that there is a chemical link between both species," (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Simultaneous detections of CH3NCO and HOCH2CN in sources beyond IRAS 16293B were needed to overcome a sample size of one and constrain C2H3NO isomer chemistry.  
  > "Due to this limited sample size, it is difficult to derive correlations or variations in the C 2 H 3 NO isomer chemistry and therefore simultaneous identifications in other sources are required." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: STATCONT followed by CASA uvcontsub in the uv-plane was chosen because STATCONT only subtracts zeroth-order polynomials while non-zeroth-order baselines are present, and uv-plane subtraction avoids deconvolution errors from the brighter continuum.  
  > "STATCONT can only subtract zeroth-order polynomials, while in this dataset non-zeroth-order baselines are visible. Furthermore, continuum subtraction in the uv-plane is more desirable since the deconvolution of the line emission is more robust when it is not subjected" (2.1 Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1)
- Alternative: Image-plane continuum subtraction with STATCONT alone was rejected for final cubes because it only subtracts zeroth-order polynomials and is less robust to continuum deconvolution errors than uvcontsub.  
  > "STATCONT can only subtract zeroth-order polynomials, while in this dataset non-zeroth-order baselines are visible." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1); "Therefore, the STATCONT outputs were used to identify the line-free channels in the spectra and the continuum was subtracted in the uv-plane with the uvcontsub task in CASA." (2.1. Observations and spectra of Serpens SMM1)
- Alternative: Thermodynamic equilibrium formation of C2H3NO isomers was rejected because CH3NCO and HOCH2CN are equally abundant rather than showing the >10^9 CH3NCO excess predicted by thermodynamics.  
  > "temperature of 300 K). The fact that CH 3 NCO and HOCH 2 CN are found to be equally abundant is, therefore, evidence that the formation of these molecules is rather driven by kinetics." (4 Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Observational spatial scales and extraction positions differ between SMM1-a (~500 au, continuum peak) and IRAS 16293B (~70 au, full-beam offset), which may affect molecular ratio comparisons.  
  > "Therefore, the chemical inventory of SMM1-a and the involved chemical processes are investigated on a much larger spatial scale of roughly 500 au, compared to about 70 au for IRAS 16293B." (4. Discussion); "Therefore, when comparing molecular ratios between the two sources, they may not only be affected by different physical conditions but also due to the way the sources were observed." (4. Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Baseline subtraction in IRAS 16293B is challenging due to line-richness and can be over-subtracted, causing dips at some HOCH2CN line positions; two temperature components may also be blended.  
  > "We note that due to the line-richness of the source the baseline subtraction is challenging and in some cases, it can be oversubtracted." (3.2. IRAS 16293B); "This could indicate that two gas components are traced, as also shown by Zeng et al. (2019), but at this position these components are hard to distinguish." (3.2. IRAS 16293B)
- Limitation (authors): D2CO lines in SMM1-a are blended and the D/H ratio of H2CO in SMM1-a is unknown, preventing a firm conclusion on the H2CO correlation with HOCH2CN.  
  > "However, care needs to be taken with this interpretation, since the D 2 CO spectral lines in the SMM1-a spectrum are blended and thus there is a large uncertainty on its column density. At the same time, the D/H ratio of H 2 CO is not known in SMM1-a, which introduces another source of uncertainty." (4.1. SMM1-a: A HOCH 2 CN-rich source)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming source size equals the 1''.2 beam size for SMM1-a may underestimate column densities if the hot corino emission is more compact than the beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using the tentative/upper-limit column density of HOCH2CN at the full-beam offset position of IRAS 16293B (where compact emission is missed) may exaggerate the HOCH2CN enhancement in SMM1-a relative to IRAS 16293B.
