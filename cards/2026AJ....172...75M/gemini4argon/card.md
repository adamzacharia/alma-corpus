## Analysis recipe

1. **Compute debiased polarized intensity, polarization fraction, and magnetic field position angles**: Compute polarized intensity from quadrature sum of Stokes Q and U, debias following Vaillancourt (2006), apply 3 sigma_P cutoff, compute polarization percentage p = P/I and position angle, and rotate by 90 degrees to get magnetic field orientation.  
  > "To address this issue, we followed the debiasing method proposed by Vaillancourt (2006)." (2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS); "Since this debiasing method provides better estimations for high signal-to-noise measurements (Vaillancourt 2006), we limited our analysis to a threshold of 3 σ P ." (2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS); "Figure 1(b) shows the magnetic field orientations derived by rotating the polarization angles by 90 [◦], revealing a clear hourglass-shaped magnetic field around the central bright source," (3.1. The Dust Continuum Emission and the Magnetic Field Morphology)
   - parameter: Threshold = 3 sigma_P  
  > "Since this debiasing method provides better estimations for high signal-to-noise measurements (Vaillancourt 2006), we limited our analysis to a threshold of 3 σ P ." (2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS)
   - assumption: sigma_P approx sigma_Q approx sigma_U  
  > "For simplicity, we assume σ P ≈ σ Q ≈ σ U in the following calculations." (2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared magnetic field orientation with previous SMA 1.3 mm polarization observations  
  > "The average orientation of the hourglass field is aligned with the elongation of the central source, and is consistent with previous SMA polarization observations with a lower angular resolution (1 . 7 [′′] × 1 . 6 [′′] )" (3.1. The Dust Continuum Emission and the Magnetic Field Morphology)
2. **Estimate magnetic field angular dispersion**: Fit analytical hourglass models (parabolic model of Girart et al. 2006 and Green's function model of Ewertowski & Basu 2013) by minimizing chi^2 of position angle residuals, and independently apply the structure function method (Falceta-Goncalves et al. 2008; Hildebrand et al. 2009) and angle dispersion function (ADF; Houde et al. 2016).  
  > "To provide a simple representation of the field geometry, we adopted a parabolic function model as proposed by Girart et al. (2006), with the equation of y = g (1+ Cx [2] )." (A.1. The angular dispersion); "Moreover, we further adopted a meticulous fitting based on a more realistic model from the theoretical solutions of Green’s function (Ewertowski & Basu 2013)." (A.1. The angular dispersion); "The structure function method is another possible approach to separate the turbulent component from the large-scale ordered field, initially proposed by Falceta-Gon¸calves et al. (2008) and Hildebrand et al. (2009)." (A.1. The angular dispersion)
   - parameter: Pixel size = 0.15'', angle measurement uncertainty sigma_phi = 1.4 deg; structure function fit range 1.1'' < l < 2.2''  
  > "Therefore, the corrected dispersion comes to δϕ cor,parab = 5 . 6 [◦], considering the angle measurement uncertainty of σ ¯ ϕ = 1 . 4 [◦] based on S/N of the polarized emission." (A.1. The angular dispersion); "The fit to the structure function was carried out for 1 . 1 [′′] < l < 2 . 2 [′′] to avoid the influence below the synthesized beam." (A.1. The angular dispersion)
   - assumption: B0 = 1 in Ewertowski & Basu (2013) model; only first three terms in series considered; symmetry center and axis fixed to parabolic fit values  
  > "We set B 0 = 1, representing the unit strength of the background magnetic field." (A.1. The angular dispersion); "To reduce the free parameters, we fixed the symmetry center and axis orientation to be the same as in the parabolic model.    - parameter: Protostar mass = 60 M_sun, envelope mass = 80 M_sun, inner radius r0 = 730 AU, outer radius R = 12600 AU, density profile rho prop r^-2.5

  > "Because the thermal velocity is negligible, we decompose the dispersion into components of turbulence and bulk motions, that is σ v [2] = σ turb [2] + σ bm [2] ." (A.3. The turbulence velocity)
   - fitting: Best-fit envelope-disk radiative transfer model from Mo & Qiu (2023)  
  > "The best-fitted parameters for this model are a protostar mass of 60 M ⊙ and a turbulence velocity of 1.4 km/s (see details, Mo & Qiu 2023)." (A.3. The turbulence velocity)

Comparison of the magnetic field, gravity, and turbulence)

## Qualitative findings

- The 1.3 mm polarized dust emission reveals an ordered hourglass-shaped magnetic field on ~10,000 AU scales whose mean orientation (PA = 127 deg, NW-SE) is aligned with the core elongation and consistent from 0.01 pc to 0.5 pc scales, but nearly perpendicular to the NE-SW bipolar outflow and core rotation axes.  
  > "The polarized dust emission at 1.3 mm reveals a clear hourglass-shaped morphology of the magnetic field. Interestingly, the magnetic field orientation is nearly perpendicular to both the outflow and core rotation axes, while it aligns with the elongation of the core." (abstract); "Therefore, the magnetic field has a mean orientation all the way aligned from the 0.01 pc (revealed by the ALMA C43-4/5 data) to 0.04 pc (revealed by the ALMA C43-1 data and the SMA data) and 0.5 pc scales." (3.1. The Dust Continuum Emission and the Magnetic Field Morphology)
- The hourglass magnetic field morphology in IRAS 18360-0537 is not a result of classical magnetically-dominated collapse, but is heavily shaped and dragged by gravity and gas bulk motions, including the bipolar outflow cavity, fast core rotation, and gas accretion/infall.  
  > "Overall, our observations of a high-mass star-forming region showcase a well-shaped, but misaligned (with respect to the rotation axis) hourglass magnetic field that is not a consequence of magnetically-dominated collapse, but instead heavily influenced by bulk gas motions, including outflow, rotation, and accretion." (5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS)
- The ~90 degree misalignment between the initial magnetic field and angular momentum reduces magnetic braking efficiency, allowing retention of angular momentum, formation of a large rotating structure, and envelope fragmentation.  
  > "These simulations predict that due to reduced magnetic braking efficiency, more angular momentum can be retained at the central region, enabling the formation of a larger rotation-supported disk." (4.3. Magnetic Braking Efficiency); "The misalignment could lead to reduced magnetic braking and the formation of large disks." (5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution ALMA 1.3 mm full polarization observations are needed to probe the magnetic field structure at 10^3 to 10^4 AU scales in the high-mass star-forming region IRAS 18360-0537.  
  > "Here, we aim to reveal the magnetic field structure at high angular resolutions and investigate the importance of the magnetic field, gravity, and turbulence in the formation of high-mass stars in IRAS18360-0537." (1. INTRODUCTION); "This study is part of the ALMA project aimed at imaging magnetic fields on scales of 10 [3] au envelopes." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Both analytical hourglass model fitting and the model-independent structure function method were used to separate the ordered field from turbulent angular dispersion in the DCF analysis, and the polarization-intensity gradient technique and beta_r-G ratio were used because they do not rely on strong-field DCF assumptions.  
  > "The advantage of this method is that it allows us to obtain the magnetic field angle dispersion without relying on any specific model." (A.1. The angular dispersion); "It is significant to note that this assumption does not rely on a strong magnetic field environment." (A.4. Comparison of the magnetic field, gravity, and turbulence); "An alternative approach to assessing rotational significance is to evaluate the rotation-to-gravity energy ratio, β r−G = (5 − 2 n ) / (5 −n )( v rot [2] R/ 3 GM ) (Sanhueza et al. 2021). This ratio avoids complications from magnetic field measurements." (4.2. Quantitative Comparisons)
- Alternative: Classical strongly magnetized collapse with decoupling at disk scales (< few hundred AU): considered as a hypothetical way to reconcile the hourglass field with the perpendicular outflow, but set aside as requiring higher-resolution observations.  
  > "Of course, conversely, if we insist on adopting such a classical strongly magnetized collapse model, we might assume that at smaller scales, the disk scale magnetic field on scales of a few hundred AU, where the outflow originates, is aligned with the outflow direction." (4.1. Does hourglass shape morphology represent a strong field ?); "Nevertheless, this predictive interpretation requires higher-resolution observations and is beyond the scope of this paper." (4.1. Does hourglass shape morphology represent a strong field ?)
- Alternative: Angle dispersion function (ADF, Houde et al. 2016): applied to estimate turbulent-to-total magnetic field ratio, but rejected because the ratio diverges with scale.  
  > "We have also applied the angle dispersion function approach (ADF, Houde et al. 2016), while the turbulent-to-total field ratio diverges with the scale." (A.1. The angular dispersion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Targeted FDM molecular lines CO(2-1), OCS(19-18), 13CS(5-4), and N2D+(3-2) failed to be detected due to an incorrect configuration of the systematic cloud velocity.  
  > "However, all of these molecular lines failed to be detected due to an incorrect configuration of the systematic cloud velocity; therefore, the analysis of these lines is not included in this paper." (2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow mass and kinetic energy could not be directly derived from the ALMA dataset due to the lack of CO line data and poorly constrained SiO abundance.  
  > "Because our dataset lacks CO line data, we could not directly derive the outflow mass and kinetic energy." (4.2. Quantitative Comparisons); "Given the poorly constrained SiO abundance, we adopted two independent approaches to estimate the outflow mass." (4.2. Quantitative Comparisons)
- Limitation (authors): Basic assumptions of the DCF method (turbulent energy equipartition and purely turbulent field distortion) may not be satisfied because gravity and bulk gas motions shape the field, making DCF field strength estimates likely overestimated upper limits.  
  > "The perpendicular orientation of the magnetic field relative to the outflow direction indicates that the magnetic field might be influenced by the gas. As a result, the basic assumptions required for the DCF method may not be satisfied." (A. THE ESTIMATION OF MAGNETIC FIELD STRENGTH USING DCF METHOD); "However, it is crucial to note that the magnetic field strength estimated via the Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi (DCF) method, as shown in the appendix A, is likely significantly overestimated." - Limitation (authors): Values of beta_rot in the sample comparison are lower limits because they are not corrected for inclination.  
  > "We note that the values of β rot in this plot are lower limits, as they are not corrected for inclination." (Figure 5 caption)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA observations did not provide precise dust temperature measurements, requiring upper and lower limits from CH3OH and NH3 lines.  
  > "Although our ALMA observations did not give precise dust temperature measurements, we can estimate the upper and lower limits of dust temperature using data from two typical molecules." (A.2. Temperature and volume density)
