## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JCMT POL-2 data to Stokes I, Q, U maps and a polarization catalogue**: Starlink SMURF pol2map run twice (skyloop, mapvar), instrumental polarization correction, binning to 12'' pixels  
  > "In this second run, we used the $skyloop$ and $mapvar$ parameters in $pol2map$." (Data reduction procedures); "We binned the polarization segments to a 12$''$ pixel grid using the $binsize$ parameter in the Starlink software" (2.1 JCMT Observations)
   - software: Starlink, SMURF, pol2map
   - parameter: August 2019 instrumental polarization model  
  > "We used the August 2019 instrumental polarization model to correct the Stokes $Q$ and $U$ maps." (Data reduction procedures)
   - parameter: Final conversion factor 668 Jy/beam/pW  
  > "(which results in a final conversion factor of 668 Jy beam$^{-1}$ pW$^{-1}$)" (2.1 JCMT Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Select polarization segments and compute angles and debiased fractions**: Compute theta, PI and p from Stokes parameters; apply S/N cuts on I and p and a 20% maximum polarization fraction  
  > "We selected polarization segments using the following criteria: $I$/$\Delta I \geq$ 10, $p$/$\Delta p \geq$ 3 and $p$ $<$ 20%" (2.1 JCMT Observations)
   - parameter: Maximum polarization fraction 20% justified by Planck  
  > "The maximum dust polarization fraction is 20% in *Planck* observations (cite: Planck2015)." (2.1 JCMT Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compare JCMT and ALMA field orientations**: Rotate polarization segments by 90 degrees to get field orientation; ALMA segments selected with PI/sigma_PI > 3; compare mean angles in a polar histogram  
  > "with selection criteria of $\mathrm{PI}/\sigma_{\mathrm{PI}}$ $>$ 3, where $\mathrm{PI}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{PI}}$ are the polarized intensity and its uncertainty" (3.1 Magnetic field orientations); "In the right panel, these segments are rotated by 90 degrees to show magnetic field orientations" (3.1 Magnetic field orientations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Planck 350 GHz large-scale orientation shown for context  
  > "large-scale magnetic field orientations at 350 GHz" (3.1 Magnetic field orientations)
4. **Map polarization angle dispersion and define the P2 clump boundary**: Moving 3x3 pixel (36'' x 36'') box over the 12'' grid; clump boundary from a dendrogram  
  > "we assume that the mean magnetic field orientation in a box of 36$''$ $\times$ 36$''$ (3 $\times$ 3 pixels) is uniform" (3.2 Polarization angle dispersion); "The red contour marks the boundary of the P2 clump, which we identified using a dendrogram algorithm." (3.2 Polarization angle dispersion)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: Dendrogram: minimum level 27 mJy/beam (3 sigma_I), minimum structure size 14.1'' (JCMT beam)  
  > "A minimum intensity level of 27 mJy beam$^{-1}$ (3 $\sigma_I$) and a minimum size of hierarchical structures of 14.1$''$ (the JCMT beam size) were used when applying the algorithm." (footnote 2)
   - assumption: Field is uniform inside the box; radii of curvature all larger than 36''  
  > "The derived radii of curvatures are larger than 36$''$" (3.2 Polarization angle dispersion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Per-pixel uncertainties propagated from segment angle errors  
  > "The uncertainties in each pixel are estimated from the measurement errors on the polarization segments" (3.2 Polarization angle dispersion)
5. **Derive column density, volume density and clump mass from 850 um intensity**: N(H2) = I_nu / (mu m_H kappa B_nu(T)) per pixel; n(H2) = N(H2)/R with R the equivalent radius of the P2 clump  
  > "By dividing our column densities by this radius, we obtained volume densities of molecular hydrogen in each pixel" (3.3 Volume density)
   - parameter: Equivalent radius ~1 pc  
  > "The estimated radius was $\sim$1 pc." (3.3 Volume density)
   - assumption: mu = 2.8 (10% helium)  
  > "$\mu$ = 2.8 is the mean molecular weight per particle, assuming 10% of the total number of gas particles is helium (cite: Kauffmann2008)" (3.3 Volume density)
   - assumption: kappa = 0.1 cm2/g at 1 THz, beta = 2  
  > "where $\kappa_{\nu_0}$ is 0.1 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at $\nu_0 = 1$ THz, taking the dust emissivity index $\beta$ to be 2" (3.3 Volume density)
   - assumption: Dust-to-gas 1:100  
  > "assuming a dust-to-gas mass ratio of 1:100 (cite: Motte2001; Andre2010)" (3.3 Volume density)
   - assumption: Dust temperature 29 K from NH3  
  > "We assumed that the dust temperature is similar to the rotation temperature of 29 K" (3.3 Volume density)
   - assumption: Depth equals equivalent radius (oblate clump); using 2R would lower density by 2 and field strength by 1.4  
  > "We note that we have assumed that P2 clump is an oblate structure." (3.3 Volume density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Carey et al. 2000 (factor ~2) and with PPMAP and SED-fit maps (factor of a few)  
  > "which are consistent within a factor of a few with our value" (3.3 Volume density)
6. **Measure non-thermal velocity dispersion from TRAO N2H+ (1-0)**: Hyperfine-structure fit of each spectrum, then subtract the thermal component at T_k = 29 K  
  > "We fit each N$_2$H$^+$ spectrum with a hyperfine line model using the Python package of *pyspeckit*" (3.4 Velocity dispersion); "is the kinetic temperature which is taken here to be the rotational temperature of NH$_3$ spectral line, 29 K" (3.4 Velocity dispersion)
   - software: pyspeckit, CLASS
   - parameter: TRAO map grid 22'', spectral resolution 0.3 km/s  
  > "The map was reconstructed with a pixel size of 22$''$ and a spectral resolution of 0.3 km s$^{-1}$." (2.2 TRAO Observations)
   - assumption: TRAO 54'' beam does not bias line broadening  
  > "Therefore, it seems likely that the large beam of the TRAO does not affect the line broadening" (3.4 Velocity dispersion)
   - fitting: Hyperfine-structure line model in pyspeckit; priors and sampler not stated  
  > "We estimated the velocity dispersion from the hyperfine structure fitting." (3.4 Velocity dispersion)
   - validation: Compared with VLA NH3 non-thermal dispersion of about 1.8 km/s (Wang et al. 2008)  
  > "is about 1.8 km s$^{-1}$ in the P2 clump, which is comparable to our results (cite: Wang2008)" (3.4 Velocity dispersion)
7. **Estimate plane-of-sky magnetic field strength map with the DCF method**: B_pos = Q sqrt(4 pi rho) sigma_v_nth / sigma_theta, evaluated per pixel on the 12'' grid  
  > "We obtained the map of plane-of-sky magnetic field strengths using the maps of polarization angle dispersion, volume density, and velocity dispersion." (3.5 Magnetic field strengths)
   - parameter: Q = 0.28 adopted for the analysis (Q = 0.5 gives about 2x higher)  
  > "We will use the magnetic field strengths determined using $Q$ = 0.28 in our analysis in the upcoming sections." (3.5 Magnetic field strengths)
   - assumption: No 3D correction applied to B_pos (B = 1.27 B_pos noted)  
  > "so we do not apply it to our value of $B_{\text{pos}}$" (3.5 Magnetic field strengths)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors state DCF is correct only to within a factor of a few; error bars are statistical only  
  > "suggest that the DCF method produces magnetic field strength values that are correct to within a factor of a few" (3.5 Magnetic field strengths)
8. **Compute mass-to-flux ratio map and radial profile**: mu_Phi = 7.6e-21 N(H2)/B in units of the critical value, using plane-of-sky B; mean values in circles of growing radius about the Stokes I peak  
  > "Therefore, the estimated mass-to-flux ratios may be upper limits." (4.1 Mass-to-flux ratio); "centered on the peak intensity position in our 850 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ Stokes $I$ map" (4.1 Mass-to-flux ratio)
   - assumption: Critical value of a magnetized disk, 1/(2 pi G^1/2)  
  > "usually taken to be the value of a magnetized disk, $1/2\pi G^{1/2}$ where $G$ is the gravitational constant" (4.1 Mass-to-flux ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: DCF overestimation (factor ~3-5) argued to make a supercritical result robust  
  > "Despite our large uncertainties, we can have some confidence that G28 is magnetically supercritical." (4.1 Mass-to-flux ratio)
9. **Derive clump mass and energy budget**: Mass from summed 850 um flux density; E_K = 3/2 M sigma_tot^2, E_G = -3/5 G M^2/R, E_B = 1/2 M v_A^2  
  > "We calculate a mass for the P2 clump of 2037 M$_\odot$." (4.2 Energy Balance); "The mean velocity dispersion of 1.2 km s$^{-1}$ and the magnetic field strength of 185 $\mu$G in the P2 clump are used when calculating $E_{\rm K}$ and $E_{\rm B}$." (4.2 Energy Balance)
   - assumption: Thermal dispersion uses mu = 2.37  
  > "is the thermal velocity dispersion for free particle with the mean molecular weight, $\mu$=2.37" (4.2 Energy Balance)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Values flagged as order-of-magnitude only  
  > "We emphasize that these values are order-of-magnitude estimates only" (4.2 Energy Balance)

- The magnetic field is strongest where the ALMA core is embedded, and the clump is magnetically supercritical in the surrounding region  
  > "The region forming the core shows the highest magnetic field strength." (abstract)
- Gravitational energy is the dominant term in the P2 energy budget  
  > "The gravitational potential energy is the dominant term in the energy budget of the P2 clump." (4.2 Energy Balance)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: P2 and P1 show different core fragmentation modes, so G28 is a good test of the role of the field  
  > "G28 is a good target to examine this role due to the two distinctive core fragmentation types in clumps P1 and P2." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Moving-box dispersion handles curved fields and yields a field-strength map; N2H+ chosen as dense-gas tracer for non-thermal motions  
  > "Using this moving box method, a map of the magnetic field strength can be obtained." (1 Introduction); "it is one of the best tracers with which to measure non-thermal velocity dispersion within the P2 clump" (3.4 Velocity dispersion)
- Alternative: Q = 0.5 versus Q = 0.28; the latter adopted (field about 2x lower)  
  > "We will use the magnetic field strengths determined using $Q$ = 0.28 in our analysis in the upcoming sections." (3.5 Magnetic field strengths)
- Alternative: Outflow-driven field geometry considered as second scenario but not confirmed  
  > "it is hard to constrain the effect of the outflow on the magnetic field orientations" (4.3 Scenarios changing magnetic field orientations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): DCF has large statistical and systematic uncertainties; error bars are statistical only
