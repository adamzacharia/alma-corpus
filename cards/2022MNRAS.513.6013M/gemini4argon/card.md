## Analysis recipe

1. **Select initial known blazars and define sub-mm/radio colour selection criteria**: Cross-match H-ATLAS catalogues with BZCAT (5th edition) and 1.4 GHz radio surveys (FIRST and NVSS), then compute SPIRE and 1.4 GHz flux density ratios to define selection boundaries.  
  > "To define improved selection criteria we first cross-matched the H-ATLAS catalogues with the latest (5th) edition of the BZCAT (cite: Massaro2015b), considering only sources with $S_{500\mu\rm m}> 35\,$mJy and using a search radius of $10\,$arcsec." (Sample selection); "Based on these data, we have adopted the following selection criteria for blazar candidates, that avoid the risk of missing genuine blazars, at the cost of more work to clean the sample to remove non-blazars: $S_{500\mu\rm m}> 35\,$mJy; $0.5 < S_{250\mu\rm m}/S_{350\mu\rm m}< 1.2$, $0.4< S_{350\mu\rm m}/S_{500\mu\rm m}< 1.2$ and $S_{500\mu\rm m}/S_{1.4\rm GHz}< 1.7$." (Sample selection)
   - software: TOPCAT
   - parameter: S_500um > 35 mJy; 0.5 < S_250um/S_350um < 1.2; 0.4 < S_350um/S_500um < 1.2; S_500um/S_1.4GHz < 1.7; search radius = 10 to 15 arcsec  
  > "On account of the weakness of the blazar flux density at $250\,\mu$m, pointed out above, we extended the search radius for counterparts in $1.4\,$GHz catalogues to $15\,$arcsec, carefully checking the reliability of associations beyond $10\,$arcsec." (Sample selection); "$S_{500\mu\rm m}> 35\,$mJy; $0.5 < S_{250\mu\rm m}/S_{350\mu\rm m}< 1.2$, $0.4< S_{350\mu\rm m}/S_{500\mu\rm m}< 1.2$ and $S_{500\mu\rm m}/S_{1.4\rm GHz}< 1.7$." (Sample selection)
   - fitting: Double power law representation of the mean smoothed blazar SED  
  > "The mean smoothed shape can be represented by a double power law:" (Sample selection)
   - validation: Control sample of 16 candidates slightly outside the final criteria tested via radio follow-up, finding zero blazars among them.  
  > "Actually, none of the 16 objects turned out to be a blazar, implying that the new criteria increase the selection efficiency without losing genuine blazars." (Sample selection)
2. **Follow up candidate blazars in radio and cross-match with radio/mm catalogues to classify genuine blazars**: Observe candidates with ATCA and VLA, cross-match with AT20G, PACO, ALMA calibrator catalogue, and NED, and use radio-to-sub-mm colour-colour plots and SEDs to separate synchrotron-dominated blazars from dusty galaxies.  
  > "Finally, we cross-matched our selected sources with the ALMA calibrator catalogue to recover flux densities at frequencies above 100 GHz." (Radio and mm cross-matches); "The plot clearly separates candidates in two groups with blazars located in the bottom right-hand corner, at $|\alpha_{2.1}^{39}|< 0.5$ and $-0.5<\alpha_{39}^{600}<0$." (Radio to sub-mm SEDs: how to identify blazars)
   - software: MIRIAD, CASA
   - parameter: |alpha_2.1^39| < 0.5 and -0.5 < alpha_39^600 < 0  
  > "with blazars located in the bottom right-hand corner, at $|\alpha_{2.1}^{39}|< 0.5$ and $-0.5<\alpha_{39}^{600}<0$." (Radio to sub-mm SEDs: how to identify blazars)
   - assumption: S_nu proportional to nu^alpha  
  > "As for radio spectral indices, $\alpha$, we adopt the convention $S_\nu \propto \nu^\alpha$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-matching H-ATLAS with WIBRaLS2, KDEBLLACS, BROS, and 4FGL catalogues to verify 100% sample completeness.  
  > "In conclusion, a large fraction of our blazars were recovered by the multiwavelength cross-matches we performed and no additional sub-mm blazars were found, indicating that our sample is 100% complete." (Gamma-ray selection)
3. **Estimate synchrotron peak frequencies and derive 500 um blazar number counts**: Fit rest-frame SEDs with a parabola in nu*L_nu to find synchrotron peak frequencies, and compute differential and integral number counts at 500 um with Poisson errors.  
  > "Such frequencies were derived by fitting with a parabola, as usual, the part of the rest-frame SED, in terms of $\nu L_{\nu}$, likely dominated by the Doppler boosted synchrotron emission. In practice we fitted data in the rest-frame range $5\le [\nu_{\rm rest}/\rm GHz] \le 10^5$." (Blazar SEDs); "The differential counts per dex estimated from our sample are listed in Table 5 together with their Poisson errors, computed using the tables by (cite: Gehrels1986)." (Number counts of sub-mm selected Blazars)
   - parameter: rest-frame fitting range: 5 <= [nu_rest / GHz] <= 10^5  
  > "In practice we fitted data in the rest-frame range $5\le [\nu_{\rm rest}/\rm GHz] \le 10^5$." (Blazar SEDs)
   - assumption: Flat LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 67.37 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 and Omega_m = 0.315  
  > "Throughout the paper we use the “combined” values of cosmological parameters for the base (flat) $\Lambda$CDM model (cite: Planck2018parameters): $H_0=67.37\,\hbox{km}\,\hbox{s}^{-1}\,\hbox{Mpc}^{-1}$ and $\Omega_m = 0.315$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Parabolic fit to rest-frame nu*L_nu SED; power-law fit to differential number counts dN/dS  
  > "Such frequencies were derived by fitting with a parabola, as usual, the part of the rest-frame SED, in terms of $\nu L_{\nu}$, likely dominated by the Doppler boosted synchrotron emission." (Blazar SEDs); "In the flux density range covered by our data the counts can be described by ${\rm d}N/{\rm d}S\simeq 5.6\,S^{-2.1}\,\hbox{Jy}^{-1}\,\hbox{sr}^{-1}$, with $S$ in Jy." (Number counts of sub-mm selected Blazars)
   - validation: Comparison of derived counts with Tucci et al. (2011) models and Bonato et al. (2019) ALMA-based estimates.  
  > "The derived counts are compared with model predictions finding good consistency with the C2Ex model by (cite: Tucci2011) and with estimates by (cite: Bonato2019) based on ALMA data." (Conclusions)

## Qualitative findings

- Blazar spectra are flat from 1 to 600 GHz, with an indication of a slight hump between 40 and 100 GHz and a progressive steepening above 100 GHz and 250 GHz.  
  > "ATCA observations and counterparts in the ALMA calibrator catalogue allowed us to confirm that the blazar spectra are relatively flat from 1 to 600 GHz, with an indication of a slight hump between 40 and 100 GHz and a progressive steepening at higher frequencies." (Conclusions)
- Photometry at tens of GHz is crucial to distinguish genuine synchrotron-dominated sub-mm blazars from steep-spectrum radio sources hosted by dusty star-forming galaxies.  
  > "This example clearly indicates that, while the selection must include the $\sim 2\,$GHz frequency point to be comprehensive, photometry at tens of GHz is crucial for the identification of genuine sub-mm blazars." (Radio to sub-mm SEDs: how to identify blazars)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Herschel H-ATLAS provides higher sensitivity and resolution than Planck, bypassing dust contamination; ATCA/VLA and ALMA calibrator data at tens to hundreds of GHz bridge the gap between 1.4 GHz and sub-mm, where dusty radio galaxies show a deep spectral minimum.  
  > "They also stressed that the spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of blazars strongly differ from those of steep-spectrum dusty sources at frequencies of a few tens of GHz, where the second group show a deep minimum." (Introduction); "Finally, we cross-matched our selected sources with the ALMA calibrator catalogue to recover flux densities at frequencies above 100 GHz." (Radio and mm cross-matches)
- Why this method: For ATCA Epoch B (2015), visibility triple product amplitudes were used instead of imaging because the non-hybrid 6A configuration and single 3-minute cuts did not allow image reconstruction.  
  > "However, because of the observing conditions, for most of the targets only one 3-minutes cut was efficiently observed at any of the frequencies. This fact, together with the non-hybrid array, did not allow us to recover an image suitable to extract flux densities. Therefore, the flux densities were extracted from the triple product amplitude." (ATCA observations)
- Alternative: Earlier selection criterion S_500um/S_1.4GHz <= 2.4 without sub-mm colour cuts was rejected due to low efficiency (contaminated by steep-spectrum radio sources in dusty star-forming galaxies).  
  > "The first observations made clear the reason for the low selection efficiency: the criterion $S_{500\mu{\rm m}}/S_{1.4\rm GHz}\le 2.4$ selects, in addition to blazars with roughly flat spectra from radio to sub-mm wavelengths, also steep-spectrum radio sources hosted by star-forming galaxies whose dust emission dominates at sub-mm wavelengths." (Introduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Multi-frequency SED data points are non-simultaneous, so source variability broadens the observed flux density ratio distributions.  
  > "The differences of individual SEDs from the average one are limited, implying that flux density ratios, such as $S_{500\mu\rm m}/S_{1.4\rm GHz}$ and *Herschel* colours, span a limited range of values in spite of the broadening of the ranges by variability in the case of non-simultaneous measurements." (Sample selection)
- Limitation (authors): In ATCA Epoch B, lack of images prevented direct position and structure determination, and triple product amplitudes can underestimate flux densities for extended sources or be uncertain when multiple sources are in the field of view.  
  > "Due to the lack of images we also lack a straightforward determination of the source positions and of their structure." (ATCA observations); "In the case of extended sources the ratio between the flux densities estimated on the longest visibilities and on the shorter ones is smaller than 1 (cite: mm2008MNRAS384; chhetri2013MNRAS434) and in these cases the triple product may underestimate the real flux density." (ATCA observations)
- Limitation (authors): VLA time was not granted for the NGP field candidates, requiring classification of NGP candidates solely from literature photometry.  
  > "Our VLA proposal did not get time to observe candidates in the NGP H-ATLAS field. Our analysis of literature photometric data found only 2 of them with a SED clearly consistent with the typical blazar SED" (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): NGP blazar candidates were not followed up with new high-frequency radio observations (30-40 GHz), so their classification relies on sparser literature data.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-simultaneity of radio, ALMA, and Herschel observations could affect individual spectral index measurements near the synchrotron peak due to blazar variability.
