## Analysis recipe

1. **Map CO emission and kinematics**: Generate smoothed-cube 3D masks; calculate unmasked M0, M1, and M2; collapse the mask spectrally for other analyses  
  > "To calculate the moments, we used only pixels that were not masked." (Moments and mask); "The 3D mask was only used to obtain the moments, but a spectrally collapsed 2D version was used in the other analysis as the limit of the CO emission." (Moments and mask)
   - parameter: Default mask smoothing: beam ×2; threshold 2 sigma_rms  
  > "first, the original data cubes were smoothed, channel by channel, by convolution with their beam multiplied by a factor of $k_{\rm{mult}}$ = 2" (Moments and mask); "then, any resulting values below a $k_{\rm{cut}}$ = 2 $\sigma_{rms}$ cut were masked." (Moments and mask)
   - parameter: Mrk 915: k_mult = 3 and k_mult = 1  
  > "For Mrk 915, we used values of $k_{\rm{mult}}$ = 3 and $k_{\rm{mult}}$ = 1." (Moments and mask)
   - parameter: Additional M1/M2 mask: non-convolved flux density below 3 sigma  
  > "we masked pixels with flux density below 3 $\sigma$ in the non-convolved data." (Moments and mask)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Additional non-convolved threshold limits low-S/N effects on M1 and M2  
  > "This was necessary since these $M_1$ and $M_2$ are more affected by pixels with low S/N." (Moments and mask)
2. **Determine disk near and far sides**: Inspect DECam g/z attenuation maps and compare the inferred orientation with trailing arms and CO M1 rotation  
  > "We used the ratio between the DECam g and z-band images as a proxy for dust attenuation in the host galaxy" (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk); "We also checked if the resulting orientation agrees with the galaxy having trailing spiral arms." (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk)
   - parameter: Consider both orientations for Mrk 915  
  > "Both orientations for the Mrk 915’s disk were considered in the analysis" (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk)
   - assumption: The more dust-attenuated optical-continuum side is the near side  
  > "For this, we assumed that the side where the optical stellar continuum is more attenuated by dust is the near side." (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk)
   - assumption: Molecular gas and stars co-rotate when inferring arm rotation from M1  
  > "If the molecular gas and stars co-rotate, the CO(2-1) first moment maps ($M_1$) can be used to gauge the sense of rotation of the spiral arms" (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Trailing-arm consistency check; Mrk 915 remains ambiguous  
  > "These orientations agree with the galaxies having trailing arms." (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk); "For Mrk 915, the orientation is ambiguous" (Dust attenuation and orientation of the disk)
3. **Decompose CO line profiles**: Fit each spaxel with single- and double-Gaussian models; retain double components satisfying S/N and spatial-contiguity criteria  
  > "To account for the presence of double peaks, each spectrum was fitted with two models: a single Gaussian (`1g`), and two Gaussian curves (`2g`)." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting); "We modeled the CO profiles with multiple Gaussian components using the python package ifscube (cite: ruschel-dutra+21)." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting)
   - software: ifscube, scipy.minimize
   - parameter: Fit amplitude, LoS velocity, and velocity dispersion for each Gaussian  
  > "Each Gaussian component is characterized by its amplitude ($S_{\nu,\rm{CO}}$), line of sight velocity (${\rm{v}}_{\rm{CO}}$) and velocity dispersion ($\sigma_{\rm{CO}}$)." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting)
   - parameter: Discard S/N <3 components; select 2g when both components exceed the criterion; discard isolated 2g spaxels  
  > "Gaussian components with a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) below 3 were discarded." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting); "Therefore, `2g` models were selected over `1g` ones when both Gaussian components (of the `2g` model) exceeded the above S/N criterion." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting); "Since neighbors’ spaxels are non-independent inside the beam area, isolated spaxels with `2g` were also discarded." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting)
   - assumption: Double peaks tentatively trace clouds with distinct global kinematics  
  > "We tentatively attribute the origin of these two peaks as coming from different molecular gas clouds with distinct global kinematics." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting)
   - fitting: Residual minimization using Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP) from scipy.minimize  
  > "the program returns the Gaussian parameters from the model that minimizes the residuals." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting); "The optimization is done by using the Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP) method from scipy.minimize." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting)
   - validation: Inspect example profile fits, parameter maps, and a spatial grid of spectra  
  > "Examples of CO profile fits are shown in Fig. [11](#fig:fitgrid)." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting); "which allows a comparison of CO profiles inside and outside the identified double-peak regions." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting)
4. **Fit global disk rotation**: Fit a Bertola 2D rotating disk to single-component LoS velocities; exclude double-peak regions and the NGC 6860 bar  
  > "To verify this, we fitted a 2D disk rotation model (cite: bertola+91) to the data." (Kinematic modeling); "the LoS velocity map of the `c1` component (${\rm{v}}_{\tt{c1}}$), excluding the region with double-peak profiles" (Kinematic modeling); "For NGC 6860, the CO data in the bar were also masked during the fit, with only data in the ring being used for the global rotating disk modeling." (Kinematic modeling)
   - parameter: V_c(r) = r V_max / (r^2 + c_0^2)^(p/2); free disk inclination, PA, and systemic velocity; 1 <= p <= 3/2  
  > "V_{\rm{c}} (r) = \frac{r\, V_{\rm{max}} }{(r^2 + c_0^2)^{p/2}}" (Disk model fit); "the observed LoS velocity depends on the inclination ($i$) of the disk, the position angle (PA) of the kinematic major axis, and the systemic velocity of the host galaxy ${\rm{v}}_{\rm{sys}}$." (Disk model fit); "Following (cite: bertola+91), we let $p$ range between 1 (asymptotically flat) and 3/2 (finite total mass)." (Disk model fit)
   - parameter: Center fixed at ALMA millimeter continuum peak  
  > "The kinematic model center was fixed at the ALMA millimeter continuum peak" (Disk model fit)
   - assumption: Circular orbits; velocity function used as an analytical model for residual analysis  
  > "using a disk model with circular orbits" (Disk model fit); "it should ultimately be considered only an analytical model." (Disk model fit)
   - assumption: Velocities in LSRK unless specified otherwise  
  > "Unless otherwise specified, all velocities are in the Kinematic Local Standard of Rest (LSRK)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Minimize residuals in 100 noise-perturbed velocity-field fits; report parameter means and standard deviations; Gaussian noise scaled by cube channel width  
  > "The values and uncertainty are the mean and standard deviation, resulting from 100 fits done on the original velocity field" (Disk model fit); "added by a Gaussian random noise." (Disk model fit); "This noise map was scaled by the cube channel width" (Disk model fit)
   - validation: Inspect model/residual maps and PV overlays; compare residuals with velocity errors; test NGC 6860 ring+bar fit  
  > "The mean values of residuals in the single-peak CO region are $\sim$ 20 – 30 $\rm{km\,s^{-1}}$" (Results: global rotation); "Therefore, the maximum residuals are a factor of $\sim$ 2 – 3 above the uncertainties." (Results: global rotation); "including the data from the bar in a global fit would not significantly change the results" (Kinematic modeling)
5. **Identify disturbed components and compare gas phases**: Assign c1 by smaller absolute disk-model residual and map continuity; inspect major/minor/third-axis PV diagrams and optical/radio comparisons  
  > "we named the component with lower absolute residuals relative to the disk model as “`c1`”." (Identification of the components in the double-peak region); "we visually selected it to be the one that minimizes the discontinuity in the `c1` maps." (Identification of the components in the double-peak region); "Figure [12](#fig:pv) shows the CO(2-1) position-velocity (PV) diagrams for three orientations" (Position velocity diagrams)

## Qualitative findings

- Most cold molecular gas follows disk rotation, especially outside the nucleus  
  > "The bulk of the cold molecular gas is rotating in a disk, particularly in the outer regions where the profiles are narrower." (Results: global rotation)
- NGC 6860 bar kinematics admit inflow or decoupled CND rotation  
  > "Since we cannot fully discard any of the above scenarios, we considered both explanations for gas in the bar traced by the component `c1`: inflows or decoupled rotation." (NGC 6860: alternative scenario for the CO in the bar: decoupled disk rotation?)
- NGC 6860 molecular outflow partly surrounds the ionized outflow  
  > "the ionized gas outflow seems to be partly surrounded by a molecular one, traced by the `c2` component in our CO(2-1) data" (NGC 6860: a molecular outflow encasing the ionized one)
- Mrk 915 inflow and outflow interpretations are mutually exclusive and depend on disk orientation  
  > "For Mrk 915, the inflow and outflow scenarios are mutually exclusive, making a comparison between them not possible." (Comparing different flow rates); "for Mrk 915, the inflow interpretation is only true if we assume a leading spiral arm scenario. Otherwise, it would be consistent with outflows." (Inflows)
- MCG -01-24-012 has a possible rotating CND disturbed sideways by an ionized outflow  
  > "the CO-emitting gas in the double-peak region was disturbed and partly pushed to the side by the passage of the \[O iii\] ionized outflow, while keeping its bulk rotation motion." (MCG -01-24-012: CO in a circumnuclear disk being disturbed by an ionized outflow?)
- Measured outflow properties imply relatively weak feedback on the cold molecular phase  
  > "values seems to indicate relatively weak AGN feedback on the cold molecular gas." (Outflows)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO(2-1) traces cold molecular gas; ALMA resolves ~150–400 pc scales  
  > "using emission lines that trace the total cold H$_2$ amount, such as the CO(2-1) molecular emission line (cite: bolatto+13)." (Introduction); "The high angular resolution of the ALMA observations allowed us to resolve the gas on scales of $\sim$ 0.5 – 0.8$''$ ($\sim$ 150 – 400 pc)." (Conclusions)
- Why this method: Double peaks motivate Gaussian decomposition; narrow single-component regions define rotation for residual analysis  
  > "To account for the presence of double peaks, each spectrum was fitted with two models: a single Gaussian (`1g`), and two Gaussian curves (`2g`)." (CO(2-1) emission-line fitting); "most of the CO profiles far from the nucleus show narrow single-component profiles." (Kinematic modeling); "the above velocity function was used to model the velocity field, in order to analyze the velocity residuals" (Disk model fit)
- Alternative: NGC 6860 bar inflow and decoupled disk rotation both retained  
  > "Since we cannot fully discard any of the above scenarios, we considered both explanations for gas in the bar traced by the component `c1`: inflows or decoupled rotation." (NGC 6860: alternative scenario for the CO in the bar: decoupled disk rotation?)
- Alternative: NGC 6860 simultaneous molecular/ionized ejection favored; different launch times and untraced molecular phases remain possible
