## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble the new galaxy sample and photometry**: Select 14 ASPECS CO emitters at z=1–3; exclude foreground-obscured 3mm.08 and z=3.6 source 3mm.13; combine HST/JWST photometry with FIR continuum.  
  > "The sample in this work consists of all but one (14 out of 15) of the CO emitters in the flux-limited sample from ASPECS at $z=1$–3 (cite: boogaard19)." (This work); "source (3mm.08) was excluded because it is almost fully behind a foreground spiral galaxy prohibiting an analysis of its SED." (This work); "The redshift criterion was set to ensure sufficient coverage of the PAH MIR emission by MIRI and only one galaxy (3mm.13 at $z=3.6$) from the full parent CO sample (16 sources) was excluded by this criterion." (This work)
   - parameter: PACS 100 and 160 μm; ALMA continuum 1.2 and 3.0 mm  
  > "The FIR photometry is detailed in (cite: boogaard20) and includes the PACS photometry at 100 and 160  $\mu$m" (This work); "as well as ALMA continuum at 1.2 and 3.0 mm." (This work)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Unambiguous Herschel counterparts  
  > "and unambiguous far-IR *Herschel* counterparts (cite: elbaz11)." (This work)
2. **Fit SEDs and identify AGN**: Fit SEDs with PROSPECTOR; identify AGN using deep Chandra observations and SED fitting.  
  > "In brief, the SED fitting was done using the PROSPECTOR code (cite: johnson21)" (This work); "AGN are identified through X-ray emission from deep Chandra observations (cite: luo17; boogaard19) and SED fitting." (This work)
   - software: PROSPECTOR
   - parameter: Spectroscopic redshifts from CO observations  
  > "All galaxies have spectroscopic redshifts from CO observations." (This work)
   - parameter: Significant AGN IR contribution: AGN fraction >0.05; 3mm.01 and 3mm.09  
  > "From the SED fitting, we identified two galaxies where the AGN contributed significantly to the IR SED (AGN fraction $> 0.05$; 3mm.01 and 3mm.09)." (This work)
   - assumption: Delayed-tau star formation history; flexible Calzetti attenuation curve; DL07 IR models; nebular and AGN emission  
  > "assuming delayed-tau star formation history, a flexible Calzetti attenuation curve, (cite: DL07) IR models, and including nebular and AGN emission." (This work)
   - fitting: SED fitting follows (cite: shivaei24), adding Herschel and ALMA FIR continuum; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated.  
  > "For details about the *HST* and *JWST* photometry and SED fitting, we refer to (cite: shivaei24)." (This work); "The only difference between the SED fitting in (cite: shivaei24) and this work is the inclusion of FIR continuum data from *Herschel* and ALMA in this work." (This work)
   - validation: Excluding AGN does not change the results  
  > "All AGN are marked in the following figures, however, they do not significantly differ from the rest of the sample and excluding them do not change the results." (This work)
3. **Measure PAH 7.7 μm luminosities**: Measure from best-fit SEDs using Equation 19 of draine21; integrate 6.9–9.7 μm and correct the continuum-subtracted 7.7+8.6 μm complex for 8.6 μm contamination.  
  > "The PAH emission adopted in this work was measured from the best-fit SEDs." (This work); "We measured the PAH 7.7 $\mu$m luminosity for individual fits using Equation 19 of (cite: draine21)." ## Qualitative findings

- New z>1 data bridge the PAH/CO luminosity gap between nearby galaxies and previous high-redshift samples.  
  > "The newly added data from this work extends the PAH and CO luminosity of previous studies at $z>1$ to about an order of magnitude lower values" (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships); "clearly bridging the gap between the $z\sim 0$ and the $z>1$ samples before *JWST*." (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships)
- PAH-to-CO is the only explored luminosity ratio that remains constant across the full population.  
  > "the L(PAH)-L$^{\prime}$(CO) ratio is the only one that remains constant across the entire population." (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships)
- PAH–IR and CO–IR relations are sub-linear.  
  > "Both the L(PAH)-L(IR) and L$^{\prime}$(CO)-L(IR) relations are sub-linear, indicating a different coupling of the PAH and cold gas emission to the total dust luminosity in the ULIRG and HyLIRG regimes." (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships)
- Slightly super-linear PAH–CO slope is primarily driven by 70 μm-selected sources.  
  > "The best-fit slope being slightly super-linear is primarily driven by the 70 $\mu$m-selected sources that all have high PAH-to-CO ratios with small error bars." (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ASPECS CO measurements and SMILES photometry probe typical z=1–3 galaxies; the redshift cut ensures MIRI PAH coverage.  
  > "to investigate the coupling between PAH and molecular gas emission in typical galaxies at $z=1-3$." (Introduction); "The redshift criterion was set to ensure sufficient coverage of the PAH MIR emission by MIRI" (This work)
- Why this method: 7.7 μm is the strongest and best photometrically constrained PAH feature; HYPERFIT handles intrinsic scatter and heteroscedastic errors.  
  > "We limit our analysis to the 7.7 $\mu$m PAH feature, as it is the strongest PAH feature and best constrained by photometry." (This work); "we fit the data using HYPERFIT[^1], which implements a two-dimensional model that takes into account intrinsic scatter in the relationship as well as heteroscedastic errors on both variables (cite: robotham2015)." (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships)
- Alternative: SPIRE photometry is typically strongly blended; the adopted FIR photometry includes PACS.  
  > "includes the PACS photometry at 100 and 160  $\mu$m (as the SPIRE photometry is typically strongly blended)" (This work)
- Alternative: Cubic-spline PAH estimates can differ by a factor of 3.5 and underestimate PAH fluxes.  
  > "We note, however, it can differ by a factor of 3.5 from estimates derived using cubic spline methods, which are known to underestimate PAH fluxes (cite: smith07; pope08b)." (This work)
- Alternative: Prefer direct high-S/N VLA CO(1–0) where available over converted higher-J measurements.  
  > "or, where available at high-S/N, direct CO(1–0) measurements from the VLA (cite: boogaard20; riechers20)." (This work)
- Alternative: Use a fitted PAH–CO relation instead of the median ratio for the gas conversion.  
  > "Alternatively, the preferred $L({\rm PAH}_{7.7})$–$L'{\rm (CO)}$ relation from Table [\[tab:fit\]](#tab:fit) can be inserted in Equation [\[eq:alphaPAH\]](#eq:alphaPAH)." (Implications: PAH luminosity as a tracer of molecular gas mass)
- How they decided it was enough: No significant 9.7 μm residuals support omitting silicate correction; removing AGN does not change the results.  
  > "our fits using the (cite: DL07) PAH models show no significant residuals around the 9.7 $\mu$m silicate absorption." (This work); "All AGN are marked in the following figures, however, they do not significantly differ from the rest of the sample and excluding them do not change the results." (This work)
- Limitation (authors): High-redshift literature samples are limited to SMGs, ULIRGs, starbursts and massive galaxies.  
  > "However, owing to the sensitivity limitations, the high-redshift sample ($z>1$) is inevitably limited to SMGs, ULIRGs, starbursts, and massive galaxies." (Literature)
- Limitation (authors): Direct photometric/spectroscopic PAH comparison for normal high-redshift star-forming galaxies requires future studies.  
  > "Future studies with both photometric and spectroscopic MIR data for normal star-forming galaxies at high redshifts will be needed to thoroughly compare the photometrically and spectroscopically derived PAH luminosities." (This work)
- Limitation (authors): αCO varies across galaxy types; nuclear starbursts may require lower values and low-metallicity environments higher values.  
  > "The $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ conversion factor is known to vary among galaxies and galaxy types" (Implications: PAH luminosity as a tracer of molecular gas mass); "in extreme (nuclear) starburst conditions, such as ULIRGS and SMGs, a lower value of $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ may apply (cite: downes98; papadopoulos12)" (Implications: PAH luminosity as a tracer of molecular gas mass); "while in low-metallicity environments the conversion factor increases (cite: maloney88; israel97; wolfire10; sandstrom13)" (Implications: PAH luminosity as a tracer of molecular gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): 70 μm-selected sources with high PAH-to-CO ratios and small errors drive the slightly super-linear slope.  
  > "The best-fit slope being slightly super-linear is primarily driven by the 70 $\mu$m-selected sources that all have high PAH-to-CO ratios with small error bars." (Results: PAH, CO and IR luminosity relationships)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The printed mass recipe scales as 4.3/αCO, whereas the preceding definition scales proportionally to αCO. Resolve this algebraic inconsistency before varying αCO.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The prose calls 0.49 a mean absolute deviation, while the table labels it MAD without defining mean versus median. It is a population scatter, not a stated uncertainty on the median.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A fitted PAH–CO slope of 1.07±0.04 is slightly super-linear; a flat moving median and an exactly constant ratio are different statistical statements.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Shared luminosity-distance factors, heterogeneous selection and correlated SED errors warrant checks beyond luminosity–luminosity correlations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The molecular-gas calibration is tied to CO and an assumed αCO; an independent gas-mass validation is not provided.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several tabulated IR-luminosity uncertainties have negative signs after ±; retain and flag these rather than silently correcting them.
