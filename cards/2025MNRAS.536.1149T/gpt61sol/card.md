- Exact numerical CO(4–3)-to-CO(3–2), R_3,2, and R_6,5 conversion factors.

## Analysis recipe

1. **Clean calibrated visibility cubes**: Calibrate raw data to measurement sets; clean with tclean, natural weighting, a fixed circular mask, and a common restoring beam  
  > "We calibrated the raw data to obtain the measurement sets from which the data-cubes were cleaned using the tclean algorithm" (Observations and Data Reduction); "We also adopt a natural baseline weighting" (Observations and Data Reduction); "The size of the restoring beam, which is convolved with the model and added to the residuals, was set as the common size appropriate for all channels (`restoringbeam` = “common”)." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Cell size = 1/5 beam FWHM; imsize covers full field of view  
  > "for a given source and SPW, we adopt a spatial sampling (cell) size equal to $1/5$ of the FWHM of the synthesised beam" (Observations and Data Reduction); "and set the `imsize` based on the cell size, so that the number of cells covers the spatial extent of the full field of view." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: Circular mask radius = 1.5 times major-axis beam FWHM  
  > "We apply a circular mask of radius $1.5$ times the FWHM of the synthesised beam (major-axis diameter) in the pipeline-reduced data-cube from the image centre." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: threshold = 3σ_RMS,dirty  
  > "three times the root-mean-square of the background measured from the cubes pre-cleaning (`threshold` $= 3\sigma_{\rm RMS, dirty}$)." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mask checked with pipeline-product curves of growth; residual maps show no dirty-beam remnants  
  > "based on inspection of the curves of growth for the pipeline-reduced products." (Observations and Data Reduction); "demonstrated by the absence of remnants of the dirty beam in the residual map." (Observations and Data Reduction)
2. **Extract total-flux spectra**: Extract spectra at published 870 μm positions using beam-shaped elliptical apertures three times the beam size; apply aperture corrections  
  > "we adopt apertures centred on the $870$-$\upmu$m ALMA positions of emission determined by (cite: Stach_2019) and (cite: Simpson_2020) for the AS2UDS and AS2COSMOS sources, respectively." (Spectral Analysis); "we employ an elliptical aperture with the same axis ratio as the synthesised beam, but with three times its size." (Spectral Analysis); "We calculate and apply aperture corrections to each spectrum using restored images of the calibrators." (Spectral Analysis)
   - parameter: Calibrator-derived correction factors 1.01–1.16; median 1.06  
  > "Given the apertures we adopt, the correction factors range from 1.01–1.16 with a median of 1.06." (Spectral Analysis)
   - parameter: AS2COSMOS mid-J correction 1.2; AS2UDS mid-J corrections already applied  
  > "applying a correction of a factor of 1.2 (median of range 1.1–1.3) to the corresponding spectra." (Spectral Analysis); "For the CO(3–2) or CO(4–3) spectra of AS2UDS sources, aperture corrections have already been applied (cite: Birkin_2021)." (Spectral Analysis)
   - assumption: Maximum recoverable scales contain the expected extended emission  
  > "the maximum recoverable scales of each of the observations using JVLA, NOEMA, or ALMA are sufficiently large enough ($\gtrsim 7$ arcsec) to contain the most extended emission which is expected." (Spectral Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure continuum and CO line fluxes**: Fit continuum linearly outside |v| = 800 km s−1; simultaneously fit mid-J and high-J spectra with single or double Gaussians; remeasure existing mid-J spectra consistently  
  > "We measured the continuum using a linear fit to each of the spectra, considering only channels $|v| > 800$ km s$^{-1}$" (Spectral Analysis); "we also remeasure the fluxes of the CO(3–2) and CO(4–3) lines from (cite: Birkin_2021) and (cite: Chen_2022) in the same way." (Spectral Analysis); "We fit the mid-$J_{\rm up}$ and high-$J_{\rm up}$ lines simultaneously." (Spectral Analysis)
   - parameter: Seven single-Gaussian sources and five double-Gaussian sources  
  > "single Gaussian profiles were fitted to the spectra of seven sources in our sample, while double Gaussians were fitted to the spectra of the remaining five sources" (Spectral Analysis)
   - parameter: High-J errors from 5000 background-noise Monte Carlo refits; mid-J errors estimated from spectral S/N  
  > "refitting the CO lines after randomly resampling and adding noise from apertures in the background of the maps, a total of 5000 times." (Spectral Analysis); "Uncertainties on the measured $J_{\rm up} =$ 3 or 4 FWHMs and fluxes were estimated using the S/N of their associated spectra." (Spectral Analysis)
   - fitting: Tie redshift and FWHM across transitions; for double Gaussians tie corresponding components across transitions  
  > "For single Gaussian profiles, the redshift and FWHM were required to agree between both transitions." (Spectral Analysis); "For double Gaussian profiles, the redshift and FWHM of each component were allowed to vary, but were required to match the same component in the other transition." (Spectral Analysis)
   - validation: Remeasured mid-J fluxes agree within 1σ except AS2UDS014.0 within 2σ  
  > "The fluxes we measure agree within 1$\sigma$ of their previously reported values in all cases except for AS2UDS014.0 which agrees within $2\sigma$." (Spectral Analysis)
4. **Calculate CO luminosities and compare infrared scaling**: Calculate L′_CO following Solomon_2005; compare L_IR with L′_CO(6–5), calculate population median ratios, and fit the logarithmic relation  
  > "we calculate line luminosities $L^{\prime}_{\rm CO,J}$ following (cite: Solomon_2005) in units of K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$" (Spectral Analysis); "We calculated the $L_{\rm IR}/L^{\prime}_{\rm CO(6-5)}$ ratio" (Spectral Analysis); "An orthogonal distance regression (ODR) of the form $\log_{10}(L_{\rm IR}) = N \times \log_{10}(L^{\prime}_{\rm CO(6-5)}) + A$ was applied to the literature values" (Spectral Analysis)
   - parameter: Bootstrap errors on median L_IR/L′_CO(6–5) ratios  
  > "for each population, finding median and bootstrap errors" (Spectral Analysis)
   - assumption: Flat Λ-CDM cosmology: Ω_m = 0.32, Ω_Λ = 0.68, H_0 = 67 km s−1 Mpc−1  
  > "We adopt a flat $\Lambda$-CDM cosmology with $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.32$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.68$, and H$_0 = 67$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ (cite: Planck_2018)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: ODR on literature; include sample with literature slope fixed and refit zero-point  
  > "An orthogonal distance regression (ODR) of the form $\log_{10}(L_{\rm IR}) = N \times \log_{10}(L^{\prime}_{\rm CO(6-5)}) + A$ was applied to the literature values" (Spectral Analysis); "When we include the results for our SMGs, fixing the slope to the optimum value measured for the literature, we find a zero-point of $A^\prime = 1.77 \pm 0.01$, consistent within $2\sigma$." (Spectral Analysis)
   - validation: Sample consistent with local slope and zero-point within 2σ  
  > "The results of our sample are consistent with the slope and zero-point of local systems to within $2\sigma$" (Spectral Analysis)
5. **Estimate the dust emissivity index**: Combine Band 5/6 continuum measurements with published 870 μm fluxes to measure β  
  > "using the continuum observations from our Band 5/6 spectra combined with the published $870\upmu$m flux derivatives from (cite: Simpson_2020) and (cite: Stach_2019)" (Dust emissivity)
   - parameter: Median and bootstrap error  
  > "found a median and bootstrap error of $\beta = 1.4 \pm 0.5$" (Dust emissivity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Milky Way, other galaxies, and daCunha_2021 measurements  
  > "Our measurement is consistent with the emissivity index of dust in the Milky Way and other local and high-redshift galaxies" (Dust emissivity); "also consistent with (cite: daCunha_2021) from 2mm observations of 27 SMGs who measured $\beta = 1.9 \pm 0.4$." (Dust emissivity)
6. **Derive candidate excitation drivers**: Derive Σ_SFR from half the SED-derived SFR and dust half-light radius; derive SFE from CO(1–0) gas masses; derive ⟨U⟩ from L_IR/M_dust  
  > "To derive the star-formation rate surface density, we adopt the half light radii of the dust (and correspondingly half of the total star-formation rate)." (Sample Selection); "star-formation efficiency (SFE = SFR / M$_{\rm gas}$)." (Introduction); "We adopt the same definition for the average intensity of the radiation field $\langle U\rangle = 1/P_0 \times L_{{\rm IR}[8-1000\upmu {\rm m}]} / M_{\rm dust}$ as (cite: Valentino_2020)" (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
   - software: magphys
   - parameter: P_0 = 125 L⊙/M⊙  
  > "where $P_0 = 125$ $L_\odot/M_\odot$ so that $\langle U\rangle$ is dimensionless (cite: Draine_2007)" (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
   - assumption: α_CO = 1; helium factor 1.36  
  > "assuming a CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 1$" (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field); "with a 1.36 factor to account for Helium abundance (cite: Bolatto_2013)." (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
   - assumption: Dust half-light radii enclose half the total SFR  
  > "To derive the star-formation rate surface density, we adopt the half light radii of the dust (and correspondingly half of the total star-formation rate)." (Sample Selection)
   - fitting: Adopt magphys SED-derived SFRs using CO-derived redshifts and multi-wavelength photometry  
  > "we adopt the star-formation rates derived from a magphys (cite: daCunha_2008; daCunha_2015)" (Sample Selection); "analysis of the source spectral energy distributions (SEDs) using the CO-derived redshift and the multi-wavelength photometry" (Sample Selection)
   - validation: Compare dust temperatures and Σ_SFR with parent SMG samples  
  > "representative of the parent SMG population with respect to both their dust temperatures and $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$." (Sample Selection)
7. **Construct individual and composite CO SLEDs**: Calculate line flux and luminosity ratios; normalise individual SLEDs by CO(1–0) and CO(3–2); take median Σ_SFR-normalised line ratios for the composite  
  > "We show the SLEDs of all the sources, normalised to the CO(1–0)." (CO SLEDs); "We also show the line intensities normalised by the CO(3–2)" (CO SLEDs); "We determined the composite SLED of the sample by computing the median of line ratios normalised by the star-formation rate surface density of each source" (CO SLEDs)
   - parameter: Exclude J_up = 8 from the composite because only two sources are available  
  > "$J_{\rm up} = 8$ measurements are omitted, however, since there is only a maximum of two available sources." (CO SLEDs)
   - assumption: Use SMM J2135-0102's ratio to predict missing CO(3–2) from CO(4–3)  
  > "we predict them from CO(4–3) using the line/brightness temperature ratio measured for the SMG SMM J2135-0102 (cite: Danielson_2011)." (CO SLEDs)
   - assumption: Σ_SFR correlates strongly with CO excitation when constructing the composite  
  > "under the assumption that it correlates strongly with CO excitation." (CO SLEDs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Infrared-luminosity normalisation gives the same composite result with larger scatter at J_up = 3  
  > "We also test how the combined SLED of the sample changes if we normalise by infrared luminosity and we recover the same result albeit with larger scatter at $J_{\rm up} = 3$." (CO SLEDs)
8. **Constrain gas temperature, density, and velocity gradients**: Fit the composite SLED with the interpolated RADEX temperature-density grid; use optimal temperature and density to determine column density and velocity gradient  
  > "We also show the best-fit temperature-density `RADEX` (cite: vanderTak_2007) model to the composite SLED, computed from the interpolated temperature-density grid taken from (cite: Liu_2021b)" (CO SLEDs); "we use the model to determine the column density, and consequently $\langle dv/dr \rangle$, which could describe the SLED, using the optimal grid $T_{\rm kin}$ and $n_{\rm H_2}$ values as inputs" (CO SLEDs)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: CO/H2 = 5 × 10−5  
  > "and CO abundance $\bigl[{\rm CO}/{\rm H}_2\bigr] = 5 \times" (CO SLEDs); "10^{-5}$ consistent with (cite: Liu_2021b)." (CO SLEDs)
   - assumption: Clouds likely virial or super-virial; use Davies_2012 virial velocity gradient as a physical lower limit  
  > "However, on average, the clouds are likely to be virial (or super-virial)." (CO SLEDs); "We therefore use the approximation by (cite: Davies_2012) to provide an estimate of the likely velocity gradient for the virialised case" (CO SLEDs)
   - fitting: Best-fit family from interpolated temperature-density grid; strong temperature-density covariance  
  > "There is strong covariance between temperature and density" (CO SLEDs); "computed from the interpolated temperature-density grid taken from (cite: Liu_2021b)" (CO SLEDs)
   - validation: Inferred ranges compared with M82 and Arp 220 gas conditions  
  > "these ranges are similar to the physical conditions of the gas in the cores of local starburst galaxies such as M82 and Arp 220 (cite: Weiss_2005; Rangwala_2011)." (CO SLEDs)
9. **Place literature samples on the same excitation scale**: Use R_6,3 as excitation proxy; convert literature r_5,2 measurements with M82 ratios; compare against SFE, ⟨U⟩, and Σ_SFR  
  > "first, we use the $I_{\rm CO(6-5)}/I_{\rm CO(3-2)}$ ($R_{6,3}$) line ratio" (Drivers of CO excitation); "We converted their $r_{5,2}$ line luminosity ratios to equivalent $R_{6,3}$ line fluxes" (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field); "using $R_{3,2}$ and $R_{6,5}$ line fluxes taken from (cite: Weiss_2005) for the local starburst galaxy M82." (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
   - assumption: Adopt M82 line ratios for cross-transition conversions  
  > "using $R_{3,2}$ and $R_{6,5}$ line fluxes taken from (cite: Weiss_2005) for the local starburst galaxy M82." (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative SMM J2135-0102 conversion decreases ratios by 3%  
  > "using line fluxes from (cite: Danielson_2011) for SMM J2135-0102 only decreases the line ratios by 3%" (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
10. **Rank excitation-driver correlations**: Compute MIC with minepy using SMGs plus Valentino_2020 detections; evaluate significance by Jackknife resampling  
  > "We computed the maximal information coefficient (MIC) using minepy (cite: Albanese_2012)" (Star-formation rate surface density); "we combine our measurements with those of low-redshift galaxies from (cite: Valentino_2020)." (Star-formation rate surface density); "we also computed the probability that the measured strength of the correlation could be found in the available data, via Jackknife resampling." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - software: minepy
   - parameter: Detections only; discard R_6,3 upper/lower limits; exclude ASPECS  
  > "we only use detections, discarding lower and upper limits on $R_{6,3}$." (Star-formation rate surface density); "we do not include the ASPECS galaxies from (cite: Boogaard_2020) since they only apply to star-formation rate surface density." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - fitting: Maximal information-based nonparametric exploration  
  > "to carry out a maximal information-based nonparametric exploration (MINE) of the correlations between CO SLED shape and the possible physical drivers" (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - validation: Jackknife resampling; Σ_SFR correlation reported significant to 2σ  
  > "with an ${\rm MIC} = 0.81$ and $p = 0.96$, significant to $2\sigma$." (Star-formation rate surface density)
11. **Test the excitation–surface-density relation and model**: Attenuate intrinsic model R_6,3 with dust extinction; fit logarithmic linear and power-law relations; compare fits with BIC  
  > "To make comparisons between the simulation results and our observational measurements, we apply a correction to the simulation." (Star-formation rate surface density); "We applied a linear orthogonal distance regression to the data to account for uncertainties in both $R_{6,3}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$" (Star-formation rate surface density); "We tested the form of the relationship by applying the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) to the linear and power-law fits" (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - parameter: Extinction β = 2; ν_0 = 353 GHz, λ_0 = 850 μm  
  > "a spectral index of $\beta = 2$ (cite: Tacconi_2018; daCunha_2021; Liao_2024) at a reference frequency $\nu_0 = 353$ GHz (i.e., $\uplambda_0 = 850$ $\upmu$m)." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - parameter: Power-law offset χ = -1.85  
  > "where $\chi = -1.85$" (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - assumption: Gas and dust well mixed in the ISM  
  > "The corrections required use a dust extinction model which assumes that the gas and dust are well mixed in the ISM." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - fitting: log10(R_6,3) = m log10(Σ_SFR) + c; alternative A[log10(Σ_SFR) − χ]^B + C; bootstrap parameter errors and 68% confidence level  
  > "of the form $\log_{10}(R_{6,3}) = m \times \log_{10}(\Sigma_{\rm SFR}) + c$" (Star-formation rate surface density); "\log_{10}(R_{6,3}) = A \times \left[\log_{10}\left(\Sigma_{\rm SFR}\right)-\chi\right]^B + C" (Star-formation rate surface density); "determined the 68% confidence level via bootstrapping." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - validation: BIC favours the linear logarithmic relation; extinction-corrected model describes data within approximately 3σ  
  > "The linear fit provides a better parameterisation of the relationship between excitation and star-formation rate surface density" (Star-formation rate surface density); "The CO SLED shape in SMGs is consistent with the unresolved empirical model (with added extinction) to within $3\sigma$." (Conclusions)
12. **Test geometry and investigate model residuals**: Test extreme scale heights; compute MIC between model offsets and other source properties  
  > "we consider how the extreme cases change the resulting observed relationship between CO excitation and star-formation rate density." (Star-formation rate surface density); "We computed the maximal information coefficient (MIC) to carry out a maximal information-based nonparametric exploration of the other observable properties of the sample" (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - parameter: Test dust temperature, flux, luminosity, mass, size, stellar mass, and extinction  
  > "the properties we investigated include the dust temperature ($T_{\rm d}$), dust continuum flux ($S_{870}$), dust luminosity ($L_{\rm d}$), dust mass ($M_{\rm d}$), dust continuum size ($R_{\rm d}$), stellar mass ($M_\ast$), and dust extinction ($A_{\rm V}$)." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - assumption: Minimum scale height 0.6 kpc; maximum 1.3 kpc with spherical geometry  
  > "$h_{\rm min} \approx 0.6$ kpc (cite: Hawthorn_2016)" (Star-formation rate surface density); "the largest scale height would be equal to the dust continuum radius under the assumption of a spherical geometry, $h_{\rm max} \approx 1.3$ kpc." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - fitting: MIC correlations of observed-minus-model high-J/mid-J ratios  
  > "that may correlate with the offsets we find between the observations and the model predictions of the line ratios." (Star-formation rate surface density); "for high-$J_{\rm up}/$mid-$J_{\rm up}$ line ratios, eight sources differed by more than $1\sigma$ and one (AS2COS0065.1) by more than $3\sigma$." (Star-formation rate surface density)
   - validation: Jackknife suggests at least approximately 8% chance of correlations this strong  
  > "Jackknife resampling of the data shows that there is at least an $\approx 8\%$ chance of finding a correlation of this strength by chance in our sample" (Star-formation rate surface density)

## Qualitative findings

- SMG SLEDs resemble NGC 253, M82, and local ULIRGs and are more excited than the Milky Way inner disc  
  > "The spectral line energy distribution (SLED) has a similar shape to NGC 253, M82, and local ULIRGs, with much stronger excitation than the Milky Way inner disc." (Abstract)
- No source has significant excitation above J_up² scaling  
  > "None of our sources have significant excitation above $J_{\rm up}^2$ scaling." (CO SLEDs)
- High r_3,1 and low r_6,3 indicate broad two-phase differentiation of the molecular gas  
  > "Since we measure high $r_{3,1}$ and low $r_{6,3}$ line ratios, a broad two-phase differentiation of the molecular gas is certainly apparent in SMGs." (Discussion)
- Cosmic-ray ionization and turbulence dissipation are possible contributors to intrinsic excitation scatter  
  > "may represent intrinsic variations in the ISM of SMGs, such as cosmic ray ionization rates and mechanical heating effects through turbulence dissipation." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Expand excitation-driver tests to higher redshifts, higher-J transitions, and a wider Σ_SFR range  
  > "This study aims to expand the parameter space explored in the investigation of the correlation between SLED shape and the possible physical drivers, with a particular focus on $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$, to higher $J_{\rm up}$ and higher redshifts." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Measure global ISM properties with total fluxes; use R_6,3 because CO(3–2) is measured for most sources  
  > "We measure the total flux from the sources for each $J_{\rm up}$ transition so that the resulting CO SLEDs reflect the global properties of the ISM in the galaxies." (Spectral Analysis); "since we have measurements of the CO(3-2) line flux for the majority of (7 out of 12) sources in the sample." (Drivers of CO excitation)
- Alternative: User-defined cleaning mask instead of auto-mask  
  > "different to the auto-mask approach which would automatically determine a mask shape and size." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Single Gaussian replaces the earlier profile choice for AS2COS0139.1 because it fitted better  
  > "This was done for all sources, with the exception of AS2COS0139.1, where the fit performed better with a single Gaussian profile and which was subsequently adopted." (Spectral Analysis)
- Alternative: Infrared-luminosity normalisation instead of Σ_SFR normalisation yields the same composite with larger J_up = 3 scatter  
  > "We also test how the combined SLED of the sample changes if we normalise by infrared luminosity and we recover the same result albeit with larger scatter at $J_{\rm up} = 3$." (CO SLEDs)
- Alternative: SMM J2135-0102 instead of M82 for comparison-sample conversions changes ratios by 3%  
  > "using line fluxes from (cite: Danielson_2011) for SMM J2135-0102 only decreases the line ratios by 3%" (The star-formation efficiency and the average intensity of the interstellar radiation field)
- Alternative: Linear logarithmic relation preferred over the alternative power law  
  > "We test the form of the relationship between $R_{6,3}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ by comparing a linear fit and power-law fit to the quantities on logarithmic scales, finding that a linear fit is preferred." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Test extreme disc scale heights rather than assume a distribution of individual heights  
  > "we would need to assume some distribution of their values." (Star-formation rate surface density); "Instead, we consider how the extreme cases change the resulting observed relationship between CO excitation and star-formation rate density." (Star-formation rate surface density)
- How they decided it was enough: Stop cleaning at three times dirty-cube background RMS; inspect curves of growth and residual dirty-beam structure  
  > "three times the root-mean-square of the background measured from the cubes pre-cleaning (`threshold` $= 3\sigma_{\rm RMS, dirty}$)." (Observations and Data Reduction); "based on inspection of the curves of growth for the pipeline-reduced products." (Observations and Data Reduction); "demonstrated by the absence of remnants of the dirty beam in the residual map." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Sparse transition coverage prevents an unambiguous SLED peak measurement  
  > "Unfortunately, the lack of $J_{\rm up} = 5$ and $J_{\rm up} \geq 7$ lines means that we are unable to unambiguously identify the peak of the SLED." (CO SLEDs)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature-density degeneracies remain without optical-depth constraints  
  > "Without constraints on the average line optical depths, which would be potentially obtainable using $^{13}$CO observations, temperature and density degeneracies remain at $J_{\rm up} \geq 3$." (Spectral Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): CO(1–0) measurement errors dominate some normalised-SLED uncertainties  
  > "given the errors dominated by the low S/N CO(1–0) measurements" (CO SLEDs)
- Limitation (authors): Different comparison-sample sizes are used for different excitation drivers  
  > "the number of sources supplementing our data from their work differs for each driver of CO excitation" (Star-formation rate surface density)
- Limitation (authors): Surface density as a proxy for volume density depends on disc scale height  
  > "the treatment of an observed star-formation rate surface density as a proxy for the volume density should not be made without considering how it is affected by the scale height of the disc." (Star-formation rate surface density)
- Limitation (authors): Alternative turbulence prescriptions can change CO optical depths and line ratios  
  > "different models for turbulence might yield different line ratios (cite: Bournaud_2015; Kamenetzky_2018; Bisbas_2021)." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Spatially resolved observations are required to test proposed causes of the excitation scatter  
  > "Spatially resolved observations, and their comparison to the model are needed to test this." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The strongest-driver result combines populations separated in surface density; this does not establish causation within the twelve SMGs.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Different detection-only subsets for each driver complicate direct comparison of MIC values.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Clarify the definition and null interpretation of the reported Jackknife p values before using them as significance measures.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several CO(3–2) values and comparison-sample excitation ratios depend on external template conversions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The measured median β differs from the fixed β used for model extinction; assess sensitivity to that choice.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ASPECS conversion attributes M82 ratios to Danielson_2011, whereas the preceding comparison uses Weiss_2005; resolve this provenance inconsistency.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stated spectral-window description is ambiguous, and some tabulated CO(1–0) entries do not match the blanket statement that every source has a measurement or robust limit.
