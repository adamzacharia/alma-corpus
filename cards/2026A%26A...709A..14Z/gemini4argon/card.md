## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate raw ALMA visibilities and convert to uvfits for GILDAS analysis**: Standard ALMA calibration pipeline followed by conversion to uvfits format and 3x spectral binning  
  > "The data were reduced with the standard ALMA pipeline, based on the CASA software (cite: McMullin2007). The calibrated data cubes were then converted to *uvfits* format and analyzed with the software GILDAS (cite: Guilloteau2000)." (ALMA CO(5-4) data); "The native spectral resolution of the observations is 6.5 - 13.5 km s$^{-1}$, later binned to three times lower velocity resolution for our purposes." (ALMA CO(5-4) data)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: 3x velocity binning from native 6.5 - 13.5 km s^-1 resolution  
  > "The native spectral resolution of the observations is 6.5 - 13.5 km s$^{-1}$, later binned to three times lower velocity resolution for our purposes." (ALMA CO(5-4) data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract 1D spectra and measure CO(5-4) and archival low-J CO line fluxes and redshifts in the uv plane**: Iterative channel-by-channel uv-plane fitting of an elliptical Gaussian at fixed CO(2-1) position to extract 1D spectra, followed by 1D Gaussian fitting on optimal S/N spectrum  
  > "We modeled the emission in the *uv* plane[^1] as an elliptical Gaussian, fitting channel by channel across all four sidebands with the GILDAS task `uv_fit`." (CO(5-4) emission); "The spatial positions of the models were fixed to those derived from the CO(2–1) emission, which are consistent with the positions measured from optical imaging." (CO(5-4) emission); "We estimated the total line flux by fitting a 1D Gaussian to the extracted optimal S/N spectrum." (CO(5-4) emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: 3 sigma detection threshold; 3 sigma upper limits using CO(2-1) channel range for non-detections  
  > "We considered a galaxy to be detected if its peak flux reached a significance of at least $3\sigma$." (CO(5-4) emission); "When a line was not detected, we instead created two-dimensional maps using the channel range identified for the CO(2–1) emission and adopted a $3\sigma$ flux upper limit." (CO(5-4) emission)
   - assumption: Elliptical Gaussian source model with fixed aperture across all channels and transitions  
  > "In our analysis, we adopt fixed elliptical apertures that are held constant across all velocity channels and across all datasets." (Low-$J$ CO emission)
   - fitting: GILDAS uv_fit in uv-plane; 1D Gaussian fit to extracted spectra  
  > "We modeled the emission in the *uv* plane[^1] as an elliptical Gaussian, fitting channel by channel across all four sidebands with the GILDAS task `uv_fit`." (CO(5-4) emission); "We estimated the total line flux by fitting a 1D Gaussian to the extracted optimal S/N spectrum." (CO(5-4) emission)
   - validation: Compared point-source vs extended models, circular vs elliptical Gaussian models, two redshift estimation methods (signal-weighted average vs 1D Gaussian fit), and checked agreement with optical/CO(2-1) redshifts and positions  
  > "We verified that using a point-source model yields CO(5–4) integrated fluxes and upper limits that are, on average, 1.5 times fainter." (CO(5-4) emission); "We estimated the redshift of the 3 detected CO(5-4) lines in two ways, both giving consistent results ($\delta z< 0.001$): by computing the signal-weighted average frequency within the line channels and by fitting the one-dimensional spectrum with a Gaussian function." (CO(5-4) emission); "We verified that using elliptical instead of circular Gaussian models yields negligible differences." (Low-$J$ CO emission)
3. **Stack CO(5-4) non-detections in the uv plane**: Align individual CO(5-4) maps to CO(2-1) positions, coadd visibilities, and fit stacked uv data with a 2D Gaussian  
  > "To increase the signal-to-noise ratio for non detections we aligned the individual CO(5-4) maps to the CO(2-1) positions and stacked them (coadding visibilities)." (CO(5-4) emission); "We estimated a CO(5-4) flux upper limit by fitting the stacked data in the $uv$ plane with the Fourier transform of a 2D Gaussian with parameters fixed to those obtained by stacking the CO(2-1) stacking." (CO(5-4) emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - assumption: 2D Gaussian parameters fixed to those obtained from the CO(2-1) stack  
  > "We estimated a CO(5-4) flux upper limit by fitting the stacked data in the $uv$ plane with the Fourier transform of a 2D Gaussian with parameters fixed to those obtained by stacking the CO(2-1) stacking." (CO(5-4) emission)
   - fitting: uv-plane fit of the Fourier transform of a 2D Gaussian  
  > "We estimated a CO(5-4) flux upper limit by fitting the stacked data in the $uv$ plane with the Fourier transform of a 2D Gaussian with parameters fixed to those obtained by stacking the CO(2-1) stacking." (CO(5-4) emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure dust continuum upper limits and search for serendipitous continuum sources**: Integrate visibilities over line-free spectral ranges (both 260-360 GHz and combined with lower-J continuum bands)  
  > "We generated averaged continuum maps by integrating over the spectral range. For the CO(5–4) detected galaxies, we excluded the channels dominated by line emission, while for the non-detections we excluded the channels where emission is expected based on the low-$J$ CO transitions" (Continuum emission); "We also produced continuum maps by averaging over a broader spectral range, including not only the continuum underlying the CO(5–4) line but also that associated with the lower-$J$ transitions." (Continuum emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: 3 sigma upper limits for continuum flux  
  > "(8) Flux of the continuum underlying the line. Upper limits are $3\sigma$" (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive molecular gas masses and excitation ratios (R52, R53) and compute sample averages**: Calculate brightness temperature luminosity ratios R52 and R53, convert low-J CO to M_H2, and compute sample mean R52 using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and stacking  
  > "By combining CO(5–4) and lower-$J$ CO transitions, we investigated the ratios $R_{52} = L'\mathrm{CO(5-4)}/L'\mathrm{CO(2-1)}$ and $R_{53} = L'_\mathrm{CO(5-4)}/L'_\mathrm{CO(3-2)}$, which serve as proxies for the excitation of the molecular gas." (Molecular gas excitation); "Finally, we computed the average $R_{52}$ ratio of the entire sample by employing the Kaplan–Meier estimator from survival analysis to properly account for both detections and upper limits[^4]." (Molecular gas excitation)
   - software: lifelines
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.4 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 for post-SBs and 0.8 for starbursting companions  
  > "We used $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 4.4\, \mathrm{M_\odot pc^{-2} (K\, km\, s^{-1})^{-1}}$ for the post-starbursts and $\alpha_\mathrm{CO} = 0.8\, \mathrm{M_\odot pc^{-2} (K\, km\, s^{-1})^{-1}}$ for the starbursting companions" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Excitation ratios R31 = 0.32, R21 = 0.52, R32 = 0.62 for post-SBs; R31 = 0.66, R21 = 0.85, R32 = 0.78 for starbursting companions  
  > "We re-estimated the molecular gas masses considering the average excitation factors obtained for local post-SBs, namely $R_{31} = 0.32$, $R_{21} = 0.52$, and $R_{32} = 0.62$ (cite: French2021)." (Physical properties); "For the two starbursting companions of ID83492 instead we considered a CO-to-H2 conversion factor and excitation ratios of typical submillimeter galaxies, namely $R_{31} = 0.66$, $R_{21} = 0.85$, $R_{32} = 0.78$" (Physical properties)
   - fitting: KaplanMeierFitter in lifelines with bootstrap resampling for uncertainties  
  > "We used the python package `lifelines` and the task `KaplanMeierFitter`" (Footnote 4); "To estimate the associated uncertainties we employed a bootstrap resampling approach." (Molecular gas excitation)
   - validation: Compared gas masses derived with local post-SB excitation ratios against literature values using R31=0.5 and R21=1; computed Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients with galaxy properties  
  > "We obtain on average 0.2 dex larger gas masses and gas fractions in the range $2\% - 40\%$ (Table [\[tab:properties\]](#tab:properties)). However, since gas masses and fractions play only a minor role in this study, our conclusions are not affected by this choice." (Physical properties); "To quantify any trends, we computed both the Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients." (Molecular gas excitation)
6. **Construct CO SLEDs and compare with theoretical models and test for residual obscured star formation**: Normalize CO SLEDs to CO(2-1), compare with Narayanan et al. (2014) models, and compare L_IR predicted from CO(5-4) against maximum L_IR allowed by continuum non-detections  
  > "In Figure [3](#fig:sleds), we show the individual SLEDs of our sample galaxies, all normalized to the CO(2-1) transition." (Spectral line energy distribution); "Finally, we compared our observations with the model proposed by (cite: Narayanan2014), which links a galaxy’s mean SFR surface density ($\Sigma_\mathrm{SFR}$), a proxy for gas density and temperature, to the shape of its SLED." (Spectral line energy distribution); "We converted the CO(5-4) luminosity into total IR luminosity ($\mathrm{L_{IR}}$) following the relation of (cite: Valentino2020b), and then converted $\mathrm{L_{IR}}$ into SFR using the relation from (cite: Kennicutt2021)." (Residual star formation)
   - parameter: Sigma_SFR = 0.05 to > 500 M_sun yr^-1 kpc^-2 for SLED models; T_d = 20 K and 30 K for dust SED templates

## Qualitative findings

- Non-interacting post-starburst galaxies are undetected in CO(5-4) with stacked R52 < 0.10, indicating cold, diffuse molecular gas and low dense-gas fractions.  
  > "However, when considering only the CO(5-4) non-detections, which also coincide with post-starbusts that do not show signs of interaction, we obtain $R_{52} < 0.10$, twice lower than local star-forming galaxies and more than 2.5 times lower than high-redshift sources." (abstract)
- Only the three interacting/merging post-starbursts are detected in CO(5-4), showing elevated excitation likely driven by AGN activity, shocks, or merger-driven turbulence rather than star formation.  
  > "Three out of the eight galaxies are detected in CO(5-4). All three show signs of ongoing mergers or interactions" (Summary and conclusions); "For the merging post-starbursts, however, the higher CO excitation indicates elevated kinetic temperatures, likely induced by shocks and/or AGN activity, which act to suppress star formation." (Summary and conclusions)
- Molecular gas and dust appear decoupled in post-starburst galaxies, as none are detected in the dust continuum despite bright CO(5-4) detections in the merging systems.  
  > "Additionally, the fact that our post-starbursts do not agree with the CO(5-4) - L$_\mathrm{IR}$ relation and that none of them is detected in dust continuum indicates that molecular gas and dust in quiescent galaxies might be decoupled, as recently suggested by (cite: Spilker2025)." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected all publicly available and ALMA-observable z >= 0.6 post-SB galaxies with detected low-J CO lines at the time of proposal to probe denser/warmer gas via CO(5-4).  
  > "They were all the publicly available (to our best knowledge) and observable by ALMA $z \gtrsim 0.6$ post-SBs with detected low-$J$ CO ($J=2,3$) lines at the time when the observations were proposed (cite: Suess2017; Bezanson2021; Zanella2023)." (Data); "Probing denser and/or warmer molecular gas through CO(5–4) emission provides a key diagnostic of the excitation state of the molecular medium" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Performed flux measurements in the uv plane with fixed elliptical Gaussian apertures across all channels and transitions to avoid imaging/deconvolution biases, minimize channel noise fluctuations, and ensure identical spatial regions for line ratios.  
  > "Being based on the raw interferometric visibilities, it does not require image reconstruction or deconvolution, avoiding biases and artefacts introduced during imaging and cleaning" (Footnote 1); "We prefer the fixed-aperture approach because it minimizes the impact of noise fluctuations in individual channels, and it ensures that exactly the same spatial region is used when measuring fluxes in different transitions" (Low-$J$ CO emission)
- Alternative: Point-source uv model instead of elliptical Gaussian model (tested; yielded CO(5-4) fluxes 1.5x fainter on average, did not affect conclusions)  
  > "Nevertheless, we chose to adopt an extended model rather than a point source, to ensure consistency with the analysis of the low-$J$ CO emission, which in several cases is spatially resolved (Section [3.2](#subsec:co21))." (CO(5-4) emission); "We verified that using a point-source model yields CO(5–4) integrated fluxes and upper limits that are, on average, 1.5 times fainter." (CO(5-4) emission)
- Alternative: Circular Gaussian models with channel-varying radii as in Suess et al. (2017) and Bezanson et al. (2021) (rejected in favor of fixed elliptical apertures)  
  > "In contrast, (cite: Suess2017) and (cite: Bezanson2021) adopt circular Gaussian models with radii that vary on a channel-by-channel basis. We prefer the fixed-aperture approach because it minimizes the impact of noise fluctuations in individual channels" (Low-$J$ CO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: Did not refine continuum stacking (e.g., assuming a spectral slope across frequencies) because neither single-band nor multi-band continuum averaging yielded detections.  
  > "Since no detections were found, we did not refine this analysis further, for example by assuming a continuum slope when stacking across different frequency ranges." (Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size (8 galaxies) and few CO(5-4) detections (3 detections) limit statistical correlation analyses.  
  > "We do not find any clear correlation with $D_n4000$Åor $t_\mathrm{q}$, and a mild trend with $f_\mathrm{H2}$ (Fig. [5](#fig:co_ratio_properties)). However, a larger sample with more detections is needed to confirm it." (Molecular gas excitation)
- Limitation (authors): Incomplete CO ladder coverage for individual galaxies (e.g., missing CO(2-1) for ID83492 and ID97148; missing intermediate J=3,4 lines for J2258).  
  > "For the remaining merger, only two data points are available (CO(3-2) and CO(5-4)), so additional observations are required to fully constrain the shape of its SLED." (Spectral line energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is biased toward the gas-rich tail of the post-starburst population with pre-existing low-J CO detections.  
  > "We note, however, that our analysis is limited to post-starbursts with existing low-$J$ CO detections, which may represent the gas-rich tail of the population." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Observations lack the spatial resolution and sensitivity to map spatial variations in excitation, resolve tidal tails in CO(5-4), or perform dynamical stability studies.  
  > "Spatially resolved observations would provide stronger constraints by mapping the extent of both high-$J$ and low-$J$ CO emission and identifying potential spatial variations in excitation across the galaxies." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistent conversion ratio R32 assumed across sections: Section 4.1 and Table 3 use R32 = 0.62 to estimate pseudo-CO(2-1) for ID83492 and ID97148, whereas Section 4.2 states R32 = 0.50 was used for the SLED normalization.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistent reporting of the mean R52 for merging post-starbursts: Abstract, Section 4.1, and Table 4 state R52 = 0.40, while Section 6 (Summary and conclusions) states R52 ~ 0.49.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using an extended elliptical Gaussian model on unresolved CO(5-4) data inflates fluxes and upper limits by a factor of ~1.5 compared to a point-source model, which could bias R52 upward if the CO(5-4) emission is more compact than the low-J CO aperture.
