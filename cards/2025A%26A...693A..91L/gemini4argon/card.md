## Qualitative findings

- The [C I] (2-1) emission traces a dynamically cold, rotation-supported disk with V_rot/sigma_v = 6 +- 3 and a flat rotation curve out to 3.3 kpc.  
  > "We find that (1) the molecular gas forms a rotation-supported disk with Vvrot/σv = 6 ± 3 and displays a flat rotation curve out to 3.3 kpc;" (abstract)
- Three non-circular kinematic components are present: a South-West gas tail (SW-tail), a weaker Eastern gas tail (E-tail), and a blueshifted kinematically anomalous structure near the center, together accounting for at least 12% of the total [C I] (2-1) flux.  
  > "(2) there are several non-circular components including a kinematically anomalous structure near the galaxy center, a gas tail to the South-West, and possibly a second weaker tail to the East;" (abstract); "The non-circular motions are responsible for at least 12% of the total flux in the gas disk (2.8 ± 0.3 Jy km s−1)." (4.4. Non-circular motions)
- A gas-only mass model cannot reproduce the high inner rotation velocities, requiring a centrally concentrated stellar component (or supermassive black hole).  
  > "The left panel of Fig. 10 shows that it is impossible to reproduce the inner parts of the rotation curve using only the gas disk component. The high rotation velocities in the innermost two rings require the existence of a central mass concentration, such as a stellar spheroid and/or a supermassive black hole." (5.2.1. Gas only)
- Multi-component mass models exhibit strong disk-halo degeneracy: both baryons-only and baryons+DM models fit the rotation curve equally well, leaving the dark matter content unconstrained but consistent with LambdaCDM scaling relations.  
  > "Even though the DM contribution is not constrained, the rotation curve of PKS 0529-549 is consistent with the expectations from the ΛCDM cosmology." (5.3.2. Baryons plus dark matter); "4. Mass models with multiple components display a strong disk-halo degeneracy: models with or without a DM halo can explain equally well the observed circular velocities, so the DM content is virtually unconstrained;" (7. Conclusions)
- Dynamical upper limits on the stellar and gas masses are significantly smaller than published photometric SED stellar mass estimates and standard [C I]/dust gas mass estimates.  
  > "5. The dynamical upper limit on M⋆ is exceeded by the stellar masses from available SED fitting, while the dynamical upper limit on Mgas is exceeded by gas masses from usual recipes. The origin of these discrepancies remain unclear." (7. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous [C I] (2-1) observations had insufficient spatial resolution and sensitivity to measure the gas velocity dispersion, investigate non-circular motions, or construct multi-component mass models; [C I] lines are efficient H2 tracers in ALMA Band 4/6 at z ~ 2 where CO(J>=3) is weak and [C II] requires difficult high-frequency weather.  
  > "For the same reasons, it was not possible to measure the gas velocity dispersion and to investigate possible non-circular motions in the molecular disk." (1 Introduction); "The [C i] lines are among the most efficient H2-tracers for galaxies at cosmic noon because they are accessible through ALMA band 4 and band 6." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: 3D kinematic modeling with 3DBarolo fits a tilted-ring model convolved with the synthesized beam directly to the 3D data cube to overcome beam smearing effects.  
  > "To overcome the beam smearing effect, various tools have been developed to fit a rotating disk model directly to the 3-dimensional (3D) emission-line cubes (e.g., Bouché et al. 2015; Di Teodoro & Fraternali 2015; Kamphuis et al. 2015)." (1. Introduction); "The tilted-ring model is convolved with the telescope beam and then is iteratively compared with the observations to obtain the best-fit parameters." (4. Gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Letting the kinematic center vary freely in 3DBarolo was rejected because disturbed kinematics on the approaching side shifted the best-fit center away from the kinematic minor axis.  
  > "After several tests, we find that the kinematic center is difficult to measure because the best-fit value does not coincide with the kinematic minor axis (defined by the iso-velocity contour equal to Vsys) as expected for a rotating disk. This is likely due to the disturbed gas kinematics on the approaching side of the disk." (4.1. Disk geometry)
- Alternative: Using a radially varying velocity dispersion profile sigma_v(R) for the asymmetric drift correction was tested against a constant sigma_v = 47 km s^-1, and the constant value was adopted because both yielded consistent V_c(R) within errors.  
  > "Fig. 6 shows the resulting Vc(R) assuming a constant σv = 47 km s−1 or a radially varying σv(R) (taken from the two-sides fitting). We find that the values of Vrot and the two versions of Vc are consistent within the errors, confirming that the rotation support is dominant while pressure support is nearly negligible. Hereafter, we use Vc from a radially constant σv for simplicity." (4.3. Asymmetric drift correction)
- Alternative: A two-component stellar model (exponential disk + De Vaucouleurs bulge) was tested as an alternative to the single spherical Sersic stellar model, yielding a slightly smaller total stellar mass (~8 x 10^10 M_sun) with strongly degenerate parameters.  
  > "We have also explored a mass model (Fig. A.3) where the stellar component is given by the sum of an exponential disk and a De Vaucouleurs’ bulge (with n⋆,bul = 4). This multi-component mass model has four strongly degenerate parameters: the stellar masses and the effective radii of each component." (5.2.2. Stars only)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Current [C I] (2-1) data cannot properly constrain the radial profile of the gas velocity dispersion.  
  > "The current data are unable to properly constrain the radial profile of the gas velocity dispersion" (4.2 Rotation velocity and velocity dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): Fluxes of the non-circular components measured from the residual moment-0 map are lower limits because the axisymmetric disk model azimuthally averages over all emission including non-circular features and low-S/N tail pixels are excluded.  
  > "we stress that these values are lower limits. In fact, given that the rotating disk model assumes axissymmetry, the flux in the observed moment-0 map is azimuthally averaged over rings" (4.4 Non-circular motions)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of high-resolution optical/NIR imaging prevents directly measuring the stellar surface brightness profile, leaving the stellar effective radius R_e,* poorly constrained.  
  > "Given the lack of high-resolution optical/NIR imaging for PKS 0529-549, we cannot directly compute V⋆ using the observed stellar surface brightness profile (as for Vgas)." (5.2.2. Stars only); "The only exception is represented by the effective radius of the stellar spheroid (Re) which is poorly constrained in all models, so it should be interpreted as a fiducial upper limit." (Appendix A: Posterior probability distribution from Markov-Chain Monte-Carlo fits)
- Limitation (authors): Strong disk-halo degeneracy prevents constraining the dark matter contribution without independent, high-precision (<25% uncertainty) baryonic mass measurements.  
  > "In Section 5.3.1, we have shown that the rotation curve of PKS 0529-549 is well fitted by a baryon-only model with sensible baryonic masses. Therefore, it is immediately clear that the DM contribution is unconstrained due to the disk-halo degeneracy (van Albada et al. 1985; Lelli 2023)." (5.3.2. Baryons plus dark matter)
- Limitation (authors): Preliminary 22-point SED fits with CIGALE are not fully satisfactory in the AGN-dominated far-infrared regime.  
  > "The SED fittings, however, are not fully satisfactory, especially in the AGN-dominated far-infrared portion of the spectrum, so we will investigate this issue in more detail in a future paper, in which we will test different SED fitting codes and AGN models." (6.2.2. Discrepancies in stellar masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The rotation curve is sampled by only 5 radial rings (width 0.09 arcsec, half the 0.18 arcsec beam), so adjacent rings are correlated and inner velocity gradients are heavily dependent on beam-smearing deconvolution.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Manual fixing of the kinematic center to lie along the kinematic minor axis could introduce systematic biases in the derived rotation curve asymmetry and residual moment-0 map.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): If the gas disk is out of dynamical equilibrium following a recent merger (< 20 Myr vs orbital time ~ 80 Myr), circular velocities systematically underestimate the true dynamical mass.
