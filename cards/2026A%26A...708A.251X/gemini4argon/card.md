## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct multi-resolution dust temperature and column density maps**: Fit single-component modified blackbody SEDs pixel-by-pixel at 36.3'' (Herschel 160-500 um) and 18'' (Herschel 160, 250 um + APEX/ArTéMiS 350, 450 um), extrapolate the 18'' SED to 1.3 mm, combine with ACA 7m 1.3 mm continuum in the uv domain using J-comb, convert combined 1.3 mm image to 6.2'' column density using 18'' dust temperature, and merge 6'', 18'', and 36'' maps in the image domain via weighted edge fusion.  
  > "We used the *Herschel* 160 and 250 $\mu$m bands together with the APEX/ArTéMiS 350 and 450 $\mu$m data to produce $18\ensuremath{''}$-resolution images in the canonical way." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "The resulting $18\ensuremath{''}$-resolution 1.3 mm map was then combined with the ACA 7m continuum image at $6\ensuremath{''}$ resolution in the $uv$ domain, as described in Appendix 8.2." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "With a medium-resolution ($18\ensuremath{''}$) dust temperature ($T^{\rm midres}_{\rm dust}$), we converted the combined 1.3 mm image $I^{\rm comb}_{\rm 1.3~mm}$ into a high-resolution column density map" (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images)
   - software: J-comb
   - parameter: 36.3'' coarse resolution with 14.0'' pixel size; 18'' medium resolution; 6.2'' high resolution  
  > "Canonical and conservative SED fitting smooths all images to the coarsest resolution of 36.3$''$ and regrids them to the same pixel size of 14.0$''$." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "The obtained high-resolution column density map $N^{\rm hires}_{\rm H_2}$ has a resolution of 6.2$''$, or 0.08 pc in physical scale." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images)
   - parameter: uv overlap between 18.2'' and 28.0''  
  > "The uv range of Interf and SD data overlaps between 18.2$''$ (SD resolution) and 28.0$''$ (Interf MRS), so the combination is more robust." (The combination in th uv domain)
   - assumption: Single-component graybody emission; Hildebrand (1983) dust opacity kappa_0 = 0.1 cm^2 g^-1 at 300 um (gas+dust), spectral index beta = 2.0, mean molecular weight mu = 2.8  
  > "where $\mu$ is the mean molecular weight relative to the mass of hydrogen atom $m_\mathrm{H}$ and is taken as 2.8 assuming $n_{\rm He} = 0.1\,n_{\rm H}$ with ignoring other heavier elements." (Spectral energy distribution fitting); "where we used the reference dust opacity per unit gas and dust mass $\kappa_0=0.1$ cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at the reference wavelength of 300 $\mu$m and dust opacity spectral index $\beta=2.0$ (cite: Hildebrand1983)." (Spectral energy distribution fitting)
   - assumption: Dust temperature changes slowly and 1.3 mm emission is purely thermal dust emission  
  > "The assumption here is that dust temperature changes slowly and that the emission at this wavelength originates purely from thermal dust emission." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images)
   - fitting: Pixel-wise modified blackbody SED fitting (REweis)  
  > "Under the assumption of single-component graybody emission and the dust opacity model for diffuse interstellar clouds (cite: Hildebrand1983), pixel-wise temperature and column density were fit" (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "With the Reweis model, we extrapolate the 18.2$''$ image to 1.3 mm for further image combination with interferometric data." (Spectral energy distribution fitting)
   - validation: Assessed free-free contamination using archival ATCA 24 GHz data (<=14% in 8'' beam), dust temperature smoothing bias (~25% for 33 K vs 40 K), and OH94 vs H83 opacity prescription (27% lower N_H2, 9% higher T_dust).  
  > "But the corresponding 1.3 mm emission is as bright as 0.53 Jy beam$^{-1}$, so the free-free contamination is at most 14% in an 8$''$ beam." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "Relative to H83, the OH94 assumption yields lower column densities of $27\pm1$% and higher dust temperatures higher of $9\pm1$%." (Potential bias and caveats)
2. **Measure global subregion properties and extract hierarchical dense fragments**: Define subregion ROI where N_H2 > 3e22 cm^-2 on 18'' map to sum cloud mass and FIR luminosity; run astrodendro on the ACA 7m 1.3 mm continuum map to identify hierarchical structures (leaves/fragments) and compute deconvolved sizes and isothermal masses.  
  > "Using the 18$''$-resolution column density images in Sect. 4.1, we defined the region of interest (`roi`) as those with column density higher than $3\times10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$ or $\sim0.14$ g cm$^{-2}$." (Hierarchical structures and physical properties); "To better characterize these differences, we used the *astrodendro* algorithm (cite: Rosolowsky2008) to extract dense structures in the 1.3 mm continuum map." (Hierarchical structures and physical properties)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: astrodendro min_value = 3 sigma, min_delta = 1 sigma, min_npix = pixels in half a synthesized beam  
  > "We adopted a intensity threshold of $3\sigma$, a step of $1\sigma$, and a minimum number of pixels equal to those contained in half of each synthesized beam." (Hierarchical structures and physical properties)
   - assumption: Distance d = 2.8 kpc, deconvolution factor eta = 2.4, leaf dust temperature taken from mean of 18'' temperature map within leaf mask  
  > "We adopted $\eta=2.4$, which is the median value derived in numerical simulations (cite: Rosolowsky2010). The angular size was then converted to physical size by $R_{p,\rm dec} = R_{\theta,\rm dec}\, d$, where $d=2.8$ kpc is the distance of G316.8." (Hierarchical structures and physical properties); "where $\langle T^{\rm midres}_{\rm dust}\rangle$ is the mean dust temperature within the leaf mask from the 18$''$-resolution temperature map." (Hierarchical structures and physical properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared monochromatic leaf mass M_leaf with enclosed mass M_enc and checked mass sensitivity bias between cold (10 K) and warm (30 K) regions.  
  > "Throughout our work, we used $M_{\rm leaf}$ rather than $M_{\rm enc}$, as it measures the dense part of the hierarchical structures without including fluxes from the trunks." (Hierarchical structures and physical properties); "For instance, adopting characteristic dust temperatures of 10 K in the young region and 30 K in the evolved region yields mass sensitivities of $\sim\!0.9$ and $\sim\!0.2$ $M_\odot$, respectively." (Dense fragments and structural hierarchy)
3. **Compute and fit Column Density Probability Distribution Functions (N-PDFs)**: Subtract uniform foreground/background column density measured in a 5'x5' box, bin normalized logarithmic column density eta = ln(N_H2 / <N_H2>) with 0.1 dex bins for quiet and active regions, and fit a piecewise lognormal + 1 or 2 power-law tail (PLT) model using MCMC.  
  > "We removed a uniform $N^{\rm bkg}_{\rm H_2} = 7 \times 10^{21}$ cm$^{-2}$ throughout the image." (Column density probability distribution function (N-PDF)); "To understand the physical nature of N-PDFs, we performed model fitting of a lognormal part and one or two PLTs following the formula" (Column density probability distribution function (N-PDF))
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Background N_H2 = 7e21 cm^-2 in 5' x 5' box; bin size = 0.1 dex; intrinsic scatter s_int = 0  
  > "We removed a uniform $N^{\rm bkg}_{\rm H_2} = 7 \times 10^{21}$ cm$^{-2}$ throughout the image." (Column density probability distribution function (N-PDF)); "As suggested by (cite: Schneider2015a), the bin size of 0.1 dex provides the best compromise between resolving small features in the N-PDF and avoiding low pixel statistics." (Column density probability distribution function (N-PDF)); "Besides, we include an intrinsic scatter $s_{\mathrm{int}}$ to avoid sharp likelihood function, though in our final fits we set $s_{\mathrm{int}} = 0$." (MCMC fitting of the N-PDF)
   - assumption: Conversion N_H2 = A_v * 0.94e21 cm^-2 mag^-1  
  > "we transformed H$_2$ column density into visual extinction, using the conversion formula $N_{\rm H_2} = A_v \times 0.94 \times 10^{21} \mathrm{cm}^{-2} \mathrm{mag}^{-1}$ (cite: Bohlin1978; Guver2009)." (Column density probability distribution function (N-PDF))
   - fitting: MCMC ensemble sampler (emcee) with Gaussian log-likelihood and broad uniform priors; median and 16th/84th percentiles reported  
  > "We adopt broad, uniform priors to ensure flexibility while avoiding nonphysical solutions." (MCMC fitting of the N-PDF); "We employ the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) ensemble sampler implemented in the `emcee` Python package (cite: Foreman2013)." (MCMC fitting of the N-PDF)
   - validation: Compared N-PDFs before and after foreground/background subtraction  
  > "As shown in the comparison between black and gray N-PDFs, foreground and/or background contamination can have a strong narrowing effects on the lognormal part, which requires deduction (cite: Lim2016; Ossenkopf2016)." (Column density probability distribution function (N-PDF))
4. **Perform Delta-variance structural analysis across subregions**: Convolve the multi-resolution H2 column density map with a circularly symmetric wavelet over 50 spatial lags from 0.04 to 2.04 pc, estimate uncertainties via 500 block-bootstrap realizations, and fit a double power-law model over lags 0.1 to 0.9 pc.  
  > "We applied the $\Delta$-variance technique to the multi-resolution H$_2$ column-density map." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance); "Point-wise uncertainties on $\sigma_{\Delta}^{2}(\ell)$ were obtained via block bootstrap. The region was partitioned into $n_{\rm tile}$ equal tiles, resampled with replacement, and the full pipeline was rerun over 500 realizations to capture sampling variance and edge effects." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance); "Accordingly, we fit a double power-law (DPL) model over $\ell\in[0.1,\,0.9]$ pc with a fixed transition at 0.3 pc, excluding the beam-dominated and map-size–affected ends of the spectrum." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance)
   - software: turbstat
   - parameter: Annulus-to-core-diameter ratio = 1.5; 50 lags from 0.04 to 2.04 pc; 500 bootstrap realizations; DPL fit range [0.1, 0.9] pc with fixed break at 0.3 pc  
  > "We used a filter function with an annulus-to-core-diameter ratio of about 1.5 since it provides the best results for a clear detection of pronounced scales (cite: Ossenkopf2008a; Ossenkopf2008b)." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance); "Fifty lags were sampled from 0.04 and 2.04 pc." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance); "Accordingly, we fit a double power-law (DPL) model over $\ell\in[0.1,\,0.9]$ pc with a fixed transition at 0.3 pc, excluding the beam-dominated and map-size–affected ends of the spectrum." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance)
   - fitting: Linear regression weighted by 1-sigma errors from the 500 block-bootstrap realizations  
  > "We determined $\alpha$ through linear regression weighted by $1\sigma$ errors from the bootstrap distribution." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance)
   - validation: Excluded smallest lags (<0.1 pc) affected by beam over-sampling and largest lags (>0.9 pc) affected by finite-map window effects  
  > "Accordingly, we fit a double power-law (DPL) model over $\ell\in[0.1,\,0.9]$ pc with a fixed transition at 0.3 pc, excluding the beam-dominated and map-size–affected ends of the spectrum." (Structural analyses $\Delta$-variance)

## Qualitative findings

- Dendrogram trees with multilevel branching occur only in the intermediate and evolved subregions, whereas the young subregion contains only isolated, nonhierarchical leaf structures.  
  > "Trees that exhibit multilevel branching host leaves exclusively from the intermediate and evolved subregions, whereas leaves from the young subregion occur only in isolated, nonhierarchical trees." (Dense fragments and structural hierarchy)
- Across the evolutionary sequence from young to evolved, gas is progressively concentrated into sub-parsec dense structures, reflected by increasing maximum fragment mass, broadening fragment mass distributions, and rising dense-gas fraction.  
  > "The more physically meaningful evolutionary signature is therefore the increasing dynamic range of fragment masses, as reflected by the progressively broader mass distributions in Fig. 8." (Dense fragments and structural hierarchy); "Along the sequence young to intermediate to evolved, the maximum fragment mass and the regional dense-gas mass fraction both increase, indicating progressive concentration of mass into compact (sub-parsec) structures." (Conclusions and perspectives)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G316.8 is a single 14-pc linear filament at uniform distance (2.8 kpc) containing three contiguous subregions of ~10,000 M_sun each that span young, intermediate, and evolved stages, eliminating distance and environmental biases present in Galactic-wide surveys.  
  > "The G316.8 filament provides an excellent controlled case: a 14-parsec, nearly linear structure comprising three contiguous subregions with comparable molecular gas reservoirs (each $\sim\!10{,}000$ $M_\odot$), yet spanning a clear evolutionary sequence" (Linear filament and nested cluster evolution tomography (LANCET))
- Why this method: Combining Herschel and APEX/ArTéMiS with ACA 7m data via multi-resolution SED fitting and J-comb uv combination preserves 6'' resolution to pinpoint dense fragments while recovering large-scale flux and eliminating interferometric negative bowls.  
  > "To this end, we did not simply smooth all images to the coarsest resolution but instead made full use of their intrinsic resolution information to construct so-called multi-resolution images." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "More importantly, the interferometric ‘negative bowl’ around the bright source in the original Interf image also disappears, increasing the image fidelity." (The combination in th uv domain)
- Alternative: Canonical SED fitting smoothing all bands to 36.3'' resolution was found insufficient to resolve dense cores at 2.8 kpc, motivating the multi-resolution approach.  
  > "While sufficient for nearby star-forming regions, the method cannot pinpoint dense cores in distant regions (cite: Jiao2025b)." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images)
- Alternative: Herschel 70 um data and ATLASGAL 870 um data were excluded from the SED fitting (REweis vs EQweis) because 70 um is contaminated by warm protostellar dust and 870 um is contaminated by CO (3-2) line emission.  
  > "The 70 $\mu$m data were not used in our fitting due to their potential contamination by hot dust components from protostars." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images); "Besides, the 870 $\mu$m point deviates significantly from the model which is supposed to be contaminated by the CO (3-2) line emission." (Spectral energy distribution fitting)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Using an 18''-resolution dust temperature to derive 6.2'' column density smooths temperature gradients and can overestimate column density toward hot evolved sources by ~25% (e.g., 33 K vs 40 K).  
  > "To give an estimate, at 1.3 mm, adopting the assumed 33 K instead of the true 40 K overestimates the inferred column density by $N_{\rm assu}/N_{\rm true}\sim B_\nu(40\,\mathrm{K})/B_\nu(33\,\mathrm{K})\simeq1.25$, i.e., $\sim25\%$ in the optically thin limit." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images)
- Limitation (authors): Free-free emission from ionized gas in the evolved southern region can contaminate the 1.3 mm continuum by up to 14% in an 8'' beam.  
  > "But the corresponding 1.3 mm emission is as bright as 0.53 Jy beam$^{-1}$, so the free-free contamination is at most 14% in an 8$''$ beam." (Multi-resolution temperature and column density images)
- Limitation (authors): Dust opacity model choice (Hildebrand 1983 vs Ossenkopf & Henning 1994) introduces a ~30% systematic uncertainty on absolute column densities and ~9% on dust temperatures.  
  > "Relative to H83, the OH94 assumption yields lower column densities of $27\pm1$% and higher dust temperatures higher of $9\pm1$%. This implies that the absolute values of $N_{\rm H_2}$ and $T_{\rm dust}$ are uncertain at the $\sim$30% level or higher." (Potential bias and caveats)
- Limitation (authors): Apparent decrease in minimum fragment mass from young (2.2 M_sun) to evolved (0.6 M_sun) is an observational mass-sensitivity bias caused by warmer dust temperatures in the evolved region.  
  > "In contrast, the apparent decrease in the minimum fragment mass from 2.2 to $0.6~M_\odot$ is unlikely to reflect intrinsic physical evolution but rather arises from observational biases." (Dense fragments and structural hierarchy)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The multi-resolution column density map has spatially varying resolution (6.2'', 18'', 36.3''), which introduces an artificial break near 18'' (~0.25 pc) in spatial power spectra and Delta-variance and may affect the high-column-density tail of the N-PDF relative to the low-column-density lognormal component.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dividing the 3 subregions (Young, Intermediate, Evolved) into 2 N-PDF zones (Quiet and Active) splits the Intermediate region in half rather than comparing the three stages directly.
