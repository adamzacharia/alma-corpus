## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract pixel-by-pixel spectra and measure flow velocities**: Extract spectra in beam-sized regions centered on each pixel in the entire emission region, fit with a 1-Gaussian model, and compute flux-weighted average speed along spatially extended filaments or clumps within flow regions (also compare with 1- or 2-Gaussian fits to total emission region following Fluetsch et al.).  
  > "Spectra from data cubes were extracted in beam-sized regions centered on each pixel in the entire emission region. They were fitted with a model consisting of one Gaussian component using the LMFIT Python package" (Flow velocities); "Gas speeds were estimated as flux weighted average speed along the spatially extended filaments or clumps of gas from their velocity maps within regions we considered as flows." (Flow velocities)
   - software: LMFIT, python
   - parameter: > 3 sigma detection threshold evaluated with 1000 Monte Carlo iterations  
  > "A significance of greater than $3\sigma$ was imposed for the detection of line emission, evaluated by performing 1000 Monte Carlo iterations." (Flow velocities)
   - assumption: 0.3 dex uncertainty in velocity  
  > "Although these effects are difficult to quantify, we adopt 0.3 dex uncertainty in velocity." (Measurement uncertainties)
   - fitting: One Gaussian component per pixel spectrum using LMFIT; one or two Gaussian components for total emission region spectrum  
  > "They were fitted with a model consisting of one Gaussian component using the LMFIT Python package" (Flow velocities); "To allow for a comparison to their method, we extracted spectra in the entire emission region including all emission structures and fit them with one or two Gaussian components." (Flow velocities)
   - validation: Compared spatially-resolved flux-weighted flow speeds with the Fluetsch spectroscopic method (Pearson r = 0.77, p = 0.001)  
  > "The flow speeds estimated using the two methods are correlated with a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.77 and a p-value of 0.001." (Flow velocities)
2. **Measure projected flow sizes**: Image the data cube over the velocity ranges observed in the filament and measure projected length from the BCG nucleus to the most distant part of the filament, averaging when multiple filaments are present.  
  > "To examine the filaments we imaged the data cube over the velocity ranges observed in the filament. We then measured the filament sizes from the BCG nucleus to the most distant part of the filament. When multiple filaments are present their sizes are averaged." (Flow sizes)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: Off-nuclear filaments represent molecular gas flows  
  > "These filaments are considered off-nuclear flows." (Flow sizes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Calculate total and flow molecular gas masses**: Extract spectra from flow regions and from a region enclosing all detected CO emission, convert CO(2-1) or CO(3-2) integrated flux densities to CO(1-0) using empirical line ratios, and convert integrated CO(1-0) flux density to molecular gas mass.  
  > "Flow masses were estimated from the CO intensity of spectra extracted from flow regions. The total molecular gas mass was estimated using the spectrum extracted from a region enclosing all detected CO emission." (Flow masses); "The integrated line flux ratios of $I_{\rm CO(2-1)}/I_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ $\approx$ 3.2 and $I_{\rm CO(3-2)}/I_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ $\approx$ 7.2 are used to convert integrated CO(2-1) and CO(3-2) flux densities to CO(1-0), respectively." (Flow masses)
   - parameter: I_CO(2-1)/I_CO(1-0) = 3.2; I_CO(3-2)/I_CO(1-0) = 7.2  
  > "The integrated line flux ratios of $I_{\rm CO(2-1)}/I_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ $\approx$ 3.2 and $I_{\rm CO(3-2)}/I_{\rm CO(1-0)}$ $\approx$ 7.2 are used to convert integrated CO(2-1) and CO(3-2) flux densities to CO(1-0), respectively." (Flow masses)
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_lambda = 0.7  
  > "Throughout this paper, we adopt $H_{0} =$ 70 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{M} =$ 0.3 and $\Omega_{\lambda} =$ 0.7." (Introduction)
   - assumption: X_CO,Gal = 2 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 for all BCGs except Phoenix, where X_CO = 0.4 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 is adopted  
  > "where $S_{\rm CO} \Delta v$ is the integrated flux density of the CO(1-0) line, $D_{\rm L}$ is the luminosity distance, $z$ is the redshift of the galaxy and $X_{\rm CO}$ is the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor, with $X_{\rm CO,Gal}$ = 2$\times$10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$." (Flow masses); "Therefore, we have adopted $X_{\rm CO}$ = 0.4$\times$10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ for Phoenix following (cite: russell17a)." (Flow masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared ALMA molecular gas masses with single-dish observations from Edge (2001) using the same X_CO factor to check for missing flux  
  > "Therefore, we compared our molecular gas mass measurements with molecular gas masses obtained from single-dish observations (cite: edge01) using the same CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor as ours to quantify the missing flux in our observations." (Flow masses)
4. **Compute mass flow rates, momenta, momentum fluxes, kinetic energy fluxes, and lifting factors**: Compute mass flow rate as M_flow / (R / v), flow momentum as M_flow * v, momentum flux as M_dot * v, kinetic energy flux as 0.5 * M_dot * v^2, and lifting factor as M_flow * R_flow / P_drive; compare with AGN radiative luminosity (L_AGN = 10 * L_nuc) and X-ray cavity mechanical power.  
  > "The flow momentum flux is calculated as $\dot{M} v$, where $\dot{M}$ is the mass flow rate in gm s$^{-1}$ and $v$ is the flow speed in cm s$^{-1}$. $\dot{M}$ is estimated by dividing the mass of the flow by the time it takes the flow to reach its projected size ($R/v$)." (Flow momentum flux vs $L_{\rm AGN}/c$); "We define the molecular flow kinetic energy flux as 0.5$\dot{M} v^2$, where $\dot{M}$ is the mass flow rate and $v$ is the average speed of the flow." (Lifting mechanism); "This phenomenon is explored further in Figure [2](#thrust), where we compare the product of the mass and flow size per unit power. We refer to this as the lifting factor." (Lifting Factor)
   - parameter: X-ray bolometric correction factor kappa = 10  
  > "Therefore, we adopt $\kappa = 10$ for our calculations." (Flow momentum flux vs $L_{\rm AGN}/c$)
   - assumption: Mechanical power from X-ray cavities E = 4PV divided by buoyancy time represents driving power for BCGs; P_SFR = 2.5 x 10^41 SFR for starbursts  
  > "The energy required to inflate a bubble is given by $E=4PV$, where $P$ and $V$ are pressure and volume of the bubble, respectively, where the bubble is approximately in pressure equilibrium with the surrounding ICM (cite: churazov00)." (Gas Flows and Mechanical Power); "For starburst galaxies, we adopted the star formation power estimated from star formation rates using the relation $P_{\rm SFR}$ (erg s$^{-1}$) $= 2.5 \times 10^{41}$ SFR ($M_\odot$) from (cite: veilleux05)." (Lifting Factor)
   - fitting: Linear regression between P_drive and M_dot  
  > "Using the mechanical powers as $P_{\rm drive}$ for BCGs in the relationship between $P_{\rm drive}$ and $\dot{M}$ using linear regression gives a slope and intercept of 0.42$\pm$0.25 and 0.97$\pm$0.40 $M_\odot$, respectively." (Gas Flows and Mechanical Power)
   - validation: Pearson correlation tests on flow momentum flux vs L_AGN/c and P_drive vs M_dot  
  > "As expected, BCGs show no correlation between the flow momentum flux and the AGN radiative momentum flux (Pearson r-value of 0.24, the 50 percent confidence level)." (Flow momentum flux vs $L_{\rm AGN}/c$); "The Pearson correlation coefficient 0.61 with a p-value of 0.02 indicates, again, a weak correlation." (Gas Flows and Mechanical Power)
5. **Analyze star formation laws, mass loading factors, and Kennicutt-Schmidt relation**: Calculate mass loading factor eta = M_dot / SFR, fit linear regressions in log(SFR) vs log(M_tot / 10^9 M_sun) for BCGs and Fluetsch galaxies, and compute surface densities Sigma_SFR and Sigma_H2 to fit the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation.  
  > "The mass loading factor $\eta$ is defined as the ratio of the molecular gas flow rate to the star formation rate ($\eta = \dot{M}/SFR$)." (Mass loading factor); "Linear regressions of the form log SFR ($M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$) = $a$ log $\frac{M_{\rm tot}}{10^9 M_\odot}$ $+$ $b$ were fitted separately to the data for the Fluetsch galaxies and BCGs." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?); "Figure [5](#fig:ks) shows the star formation law in BCGs and Fluetsch galaxies when only molecular gas surface density is used for all galaxies." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?)
   - parameter: Star formation region areas: HST UV-band area for 8 BCGs, inner 3 kpc radius for remaining BCGs, 1 kpc radius for Fluetsch galaxies with SFR > 20 M_sun/yr, RC3 blue-band elliptical areas for remaining Fluetsch galaxies  
  > "Therefore, we have adopted the area of a 1 kpc radius region around the nucleus to estimate gas and star formation surface densities for Fluetsch galaxies with star formation rates greater than 20 $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?); "In eight BCGs, the areas of the star formation regions were estimated using UV-band images from the Hubble Telescope." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?); "For the remaining BCGs, we adopted a conservative area of the inner 3 kpc region centered on the nucleus." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?)
   - assumption: Cold gas surface density is approximated by molecular gas surface density Sigma_H2 alone  
  > "We consider here only the molecular gas mass because H I emission is rarely observed in BCGs, whose cold gas reservoirs are likely dominated by molecular gas with H$_2$/H I ratio unity or above (cite: babyk21)." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?)
   - fitting: Linear regressions in log space  
  > "Linear regressions of the form log SFR ($M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$) = $a$ log $\frac{M_{\rm tot}}{10^9 M_\odot}$ $+$ $b$ were fitted separately to the data for the Fluetsch galaxies and BCGs." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?)
   - validation: Two-sample t-test comparing regression slopes and intercepts of BCGs and Fluetsch galaxies  
  > "To test the statistical significance of the difference observed between the two best-fit lines, we conducted a two-sample t-test with a null hypothesis that the coefficients of the two linear regressions are equal." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?)
6. **Estimate escape velocities and escaped gas fractions**: Model galaxy gravitational potential with a Hernquist profile to calculate escape velocity v_esc, and integrate CO spectrum above positive and below negative v_esc relative to total flow integrated intensity.  
  > "To compare the molecular gas speeds to the escape speeds, we adopt the Hernquist profile to model a galaxy’s gravitational potential." ## Qualitative findings

- Thirty to seventy percent of the molecular gas in BCGs lies outside the nucleus in extended filamentary flows, compared to 3-10 percent in AGN, composite, and starburst galaxies.  
  > "Thirty to seventy percent of the gas lies outside of the nucleus in extended, filamentary structures that appear to be moving relative to the BCG." (Summary); "In AGN hosting galaxies the amount of molecular gas in the outflow is only $\sim$10 percent. It is even lower in composite and starburst galaxies at $\sim$3 percent." (Flow masses)
- Radio-mode feedback in BCGs yields lifting factors (M_flow * R_flow / P_drive) that exceed starbursts and quasar nuclei by two to three orders of magnitude.  
  > "The product of the molecular gas mass and lifting altitude divided by the AGN or starburst power — a parameter referred to as the lifting factor—exceeds starbursts and quasar nuclei by two to three orders of magnitude, respectively." (Abstract)
- The kinetic energy flux of molecular gas flows generally lies near or below a few percent of the AGN or starburst driving power across all galaxy types.  
  > "Figure [1](#power) shows that the kinetic energy fluxes of molecular gas in most systems lie near or below a few percent of the AGN or starburst power." (Lifting mechanism)
- Escaped molecular gas fractions in BCGs are negligible, indicating a molecular fountain where lifted gas eventually returns to the galaxy.  
  > "We found that for all BCGs the escaped gas fraction is negligible." (Fate of the Outflowing Gas); "Radio-mechanical feedback is likely driving a fountain of hot and cold gas that eventually returns to the central galaxy." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA provides unprecedented spatial and velocity resolution to resolve extended filamentary molecular gas flows in BCGs, which can be compared to the local active galaxy sample of Fluetsch et al. (2019) that omitted radio-mode feedback in central cluster galaxies.  
  > "In recent years, the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) has observed molecular gas in central galaxies with unprecedented spatial and velocity resolution." (Introduction); "Here we analyze molecular flows in central galaxies and compare them to those in active galaxies from the (cite: fluetsch18) (Fluetsch). Fluetsch did not include outflows driven by radio-mode feedback in central galaxies." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Because BCG molecular flows are spatially resolved into off-nuclear filaments whose summed spectra may not deviate strongly from Gaussianity, flow properties are measured from spatially extended emission components rather than relying solely on broad spectral wings.  
  > "Applying this method to Abell 2597, Abell 1835, AS1101 and NGC4696, the summed spectra deviate only marginally from gaussianity. Their flows would therefore have been missed were they too distant to be spatially resolved." (Flow velocities); "We identify gas flows in BCGs and determine their velocities and sizes individually from spatially extended emission components." (Flow velocities)
- Alternative: Spectroscopic 2-Gaussian broad-component method of Fluetsch et al. (2019) for flow speeds: tested for comparison, found to overestimate flow velocities by a factor of two and miss spatially resolved flows with nearly Gaussian summed spectra.  
  > "Speeds estimated using the Fluetsch method are two times higher compared to our method, except for RXCJ0821." (Flow velocities); "The Fluetsch method captures a small fraction of the gas that is flowing at high speeds and overestimates the flow velocity. In what follows, we adopt speeds estimated using our method." (Flow velocities)
- Alternative: Including displaced hot X-ray atmospheric gas masses in flow rates and lifting factors: omitted in order to maintain a direct comparison of molecular gas phases with the Fluetsch sample.  
  > "Therefore, in order to compare our systems with Fluetsch, we have not included the hot gas masses. But were we to do so the flow rates and masses would increase by a factor of two or more." (Lifting Factor)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The BCG sample is biased and incomplete because targets were selected for ALMA observations based on having large molecular gas reservoirs.  
  > "Most ALMA archival targets were observed based on their large molecular gas reservoirs (cite: edge02; pulido18). Therefore, our sample is biased, incomplete and does not represent all central cluster galaxies." (Sample selection)
- Limitation (authors): True flow sizes and velocities are systematically underestimated due to projection and orientation effects.  
  > "The true sizes of molecular flows and their velocities are systematically underestimated due to projection." (Measurement uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): In emission, it is unknown whether individual filaments are flowing outward or inward, and simultaneous inflows and outflows along the line of sight can lower observed flux-weighted velocities.  
  > "Therefore, whether the filaments in an individual object are flowing in or out is unknown. Both inflows and outflows can be present at the same time." (Measurement uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): The adopted CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO introduces a factor of two or more (0.3 dex) uncertainty in molecular gas mass estimates.  
  > "The adopted value of $X_{\rm CO}$ lends to a factor of two or more uncertainty in the gas mass estimates." (Flow masses); "The primary mass uncertainty comes from the application of $X_{\rm CO}$, which contributes 0.3 dex (cite: bolatto13)." (Measurement uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Interferometric filtering by ALMA may resolve out 25 to 40 percent of extended molecular gas in some systems (A1664, A1835, NGC5044) compared to single-dish measurements.  
  > "ALMA detected 60 percent more molecular gas in RXCJ0821, while in A1664, A1835 and NGC5044 the ALMA masses lie 25 to 40 percent below the single dish mass measurements. In the latter systems, ALMA may have resolved away some extended molecular gas." (Flow masses)
- Limitation (authors): Molecular gas surface densities may be overestimated by a factor of ~2 in some BCGs where the molecular gas is more extended than the UV star-forming region.  
  > "In some BCGs (e.g., Abell S1101, RXCJ1539.5, PKS0745), molecular gas is more extended than the star forming region. Therefore, the molecular gas surface densities may be overestimated by a factor of $\sim$2 in these systems." (Does Radio-mode Feedback Suppress Star Formation?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The definition of 'flow' in BCGs treats all off-nuclear filamentary gas as a flow (and uses R_flow / v_flow as the flow timescale), whereas in the comparison sample of Fluetsch et al. (2019) outflows are defined kinematically from broad spectral wings; this methodological difference could systematically inflate BCG flow masses and lifting factors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Appendices 1 and B (containing the exact flow region maps, velocity maps, and velocity adjustment tests) are referenced in the text but omitted from the main article body, making exact verification of the spatial masks difficult.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample count discrepancy between the Abstract/Summary/Table 1 (14 BCGs total, including NGC 1275 from IRAM and 13 ALMA targets) and Section 2 ('Archival ALMA observations of 12 BCGs were analyzed').
