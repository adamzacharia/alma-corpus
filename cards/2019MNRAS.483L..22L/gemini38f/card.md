(3.2.1 Thermal SZE Model); "We thus consider a model in which the observed SZE decrement is due to significant contributions from both the tSZE and kSZE." (3.2.2 Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
   - software: not stated
   - parameter: Cone half angle theta = 30 deg; axis inclination 45 deg to line of sight; f_Omega = 0.067  
  > "We estimate the half angle of the cone in our system to be $\theta = 30^{\circ}$ (Figure 1; upper right). We further assume that the axis of the outflow is at an angle of 45$^{\circ}$ to the outward extension of our line of sight." (3.2.1 Thermal SZE Model); "$f_\Omega = (1 - \cos \theta )/2 \approx 0.067$" (3.2.1 Thermal SZE Model)
   - assumption: Equipartition of ions and electrons (T = Te); negligible cooling; conical expansion behaves as a portion of a spherically symmetric outflow  
  > "we assume equipartition of ions and electrons, so that $T = T_e$, and $V$ is the volume of the bubble." (3.2.1 Thermal SZE Model); "The first is that cooling of the hot phase gas is negligible."   > "Equations (6) and (7) are two relations with two unknowns. ## Qualitative findings

- Direct detection of an SZE decrement located to the southwest of the hyperluminous quasar HE 0515-4414, indicating a hot thermal wind bubble.  
  > "In this letter, we present evidence that we have made the first detection of such a bubble, associated with the hyperluminous quasar HE 0515-4414." (abstract)
- The SZE signal is one-sided, which is naturally explained by a combination of thermal SZE (decrement) and kinetic SZE (decrement for receding outflow, increment/cancellation for approaching outflow).  
  > "Our detection appears on only one side of the quasar, consistent with the SZE signal arising from a combination of thermal and kinetic contributions." (abstract); "In the receding half of the outflow (relative to the observer) the kZSE and tSZE contributions to the SZE are both of the same sign, but in the approaching half the two partially cancel." (3.2.2 Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- Extended optical/NIR emission line gas ([OIII]) shows high velocity gradient (+1100 km/s over 0.24'') along the same position angle as the SZE bubble.  
  > "The SZE detection is corroborated by the presence of extended emission line gas at the same position angle as the wind." (abstract); "we find [Oiii] emission and faint continuum in the C1 component, which appears to be both extended and characterized by a significant velocity gradient ($\approx +1100$ km s$^{-1}$ over 0.24$''$). This is consistent with a warm component to the wind." (3.1 The quasar host galaxy and its environment)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 4 (~130 GHz) covers the peak of the thermal SZE decrement, and compact configurations match the 1-10 arcsec angular scale of quasar wind bubbles.  
  > "The peak intensity of the thermal SZE decrement is seen at $\approx 130$ GHz (Sunyaev & Zeldovich 1972), which lies in ALMA band-4." (2.1 ALMA); "The predicted size scale of the signal, $\sim 10-100$ kpc, corresponding to $\sim 1-10$ arcsec at $z\stackrel{>}{_{\sim}}1$, is well matched to the angular resolution of ALMA in the most compact configurations." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Observing in both compact and extended configurations allows subtraction of bright compact point sources in the uv-plane before tapering to maximize surface brightness sensitivity to extended SZE.  
  > "Two scheduling blocks were made: one executed once in a relatively large configuration (delivering $\theta_{\rm FWHM}$ $\approx$0.7$''$) to measure the point source contribution, and one executed 14 times in a compact configuration (delivering $\theta_{\rm FWHM}$ $\approx$2.7$''$) to maximize our response to the SZE." (2.1 ALMA); "subtraction of point source models in the $uv$-plane was adequate to remove them" (2.1 ALMA)
- Alternative: Contamination by intracluster or intragroup medium: Bielby et al. (2017) poor cluster at z=0.28 (6e12 M_sun) is too low-mass to produce the signal, and no other cluster/group is found in Spitzer/HST/Gemini data.  
  > "The estimated mass of this cluster, $6\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}$ is much too small for it to contribute to the observed SZE though. The lack of any other obvious group or cluster in the field argues against significant contamination" (3.1 The quasar host galaxy and its environment)
- Alternative: Starburst wind origin: A star formation rate of ~500 M_sun/yr over 1.3e8 yr could produce the observed signal; cannot be definitively ruled out without better SFR constraints.  
  > "In the absence of constraints on the star formation rate in this object, we cannot currently rule out a starburst origin." (3.2.2 Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- Alternative: Intrinsically one-sided outflow or counter-flow blocked by dense ISM.  
  > "There are a number of possible explanations, including an intrinsically one-sided outflow, or the counter-flow being blocked by a higher ISM density in its path. However, a combined tSZE and kSZE model provides a natural explanation" (3.2.2 Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Detection significance is modest at 3.5 sigma.  
  > "recovers an apparent dip of flux density of $-23.1\,\mu$Jy, significant at the 3.5$\sigma$ level, to the SW of the quasar position." (2.1 ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Analysis is based on a single object; population confirmation is required.  
  > "We emphasize though that this is only a single object, and that studies of further objects are needed to confirm this in the general quasar population." (4 Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Host star formation rate is unconstrained, leaving starburst feedback as an unruled-out possibility.  
  > "In the absence of constraints on the star formation rate in this object, we cannot currently rule out a starburst origin." (3.2.2 Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 3.5 sigma detection is marginally significant and could be sensitive to uv-subtraction residuals of nearby bright continuum sources, especially A5.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The geometric assumptions (conical bubble, 30 deg half-angle, 45 deg inclination) strongly determine the derived wind parameters and kSZE cancellation fraction.
