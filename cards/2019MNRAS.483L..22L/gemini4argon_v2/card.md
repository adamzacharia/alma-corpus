## Science

- Fields: agn_and_quasars, high_z_galaxies, galaxy_clusters_and_sz
- Question: Can the Sunyaev-Zeldovich Effect (SZE) from a thermal wind bubble around a hyperluminous quasar be directly detected with ALMA to constrain the nature and energetics of quasar feedback?  
  > "The nature and energetics of feedback from thermal winds in quasars can be constrained via observations of the Sunyaev-Zeldovich Effect (SZE) induced by the bubble of thermal plasma blown into the intergalactic medium by the quasar wind." (Abstract); "In this paper, we describe our attempt to use ALMA to directly detect the SZE from a quasar wind." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Subtract continuum point sources in the uv-plane and image the SZE decrement**: Fit/define point source models for the quasar and three field sources (A1, A3, A5), subtract them in the uv-plane, make a naturally-weighted image and a uv-tapered image, and apply primary beam correction to the decrement peak  
  > "In order to establish the presence or otherwise of the SZE from the quasar host it was first necessary to subtract the emission from the quasar itself, and also the three other bright ($>20\, \mu$Jy) sources in the field (A1, A3, and A5;" (ALMA); "Fortunately, all sources were unresolved in the smaller configuration data, and the subtraction of point source models in the $uv$-plane was adequate to remove them," (ALMA); "so tapering was applied to improve the surface brightness sensitivity of the image." (ALMA)
   - parameter: Subtracted flux densities: A1 = 41.7 uJy, A3 = 21.8 uJy, A5 = 188.2 uJy, QSO = 66.2 uJy  
  > "| A1 | 05:17:07.767 | -44:10:43.87 | 41.7 |" (Table 1); "| A3 | 05:17:08.483 | -44:10:51.22 | 21.8 |" (Table 1); "| A5 | 05:17:09.455 | -44:10:52.39 | 188.2 |" (Table 1)
   - parameter: Tapered beam: 6.7'' x 6.4'' at PA = 110 deg; Primary beam correction: 12%  
  > "produced an image with $\sigma =$ 6.6 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ and $\theta_{\rm FWHM} =$ 6.7$''$$\times$6.4$''$ at PA $= 110^{\circ}$" (ALMA); "(After a 12% correction for the response of the primary beam, this peak value of the decrement becomes $-25.9 \, \mu$Jy.)" (ALMA)
   - assumption: Sources A1, A3, A5, and QSO are unresolved point sources  
  > "Fortunately, all sources were unresolved in the smaller configuration data, and the subtraction of point source models in the $uv$-plane was adequate to remove them," (ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested tapering the beam to 20'' to check for a larger halo SZE signal  
  > "However, we detect no SZE from the quasar (even after tapering the beam to 20$''$), suggesting that no such halo is present." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
2. **Process multi-wavelength optical/IR imaging and spectroscopy and estimate black hole mass and outflow kinematics**: Use standard Spitzer pipeline products, reduce Gemini GMOS and FLAMINGOS-2 images with IRAF, combine HST WFC3 images with astrodrizzle, extract G141 grism spectra with aXe, measure [OIII] velocity gradient in C1 and quasar H-beta width to estimate black hole mass  
  > "The broad-band images were combined using astrodrizzle, and the aXe software (Kümmel et al. 2009) was used to extract the grism spectra." (Optical and Infrared); "We used the *HST* grism spectrum of the quasar to estimate the mass of the black hole, $M_{\bullet}\approx 4.3\times 10^{10}\,M_{\odot}$, based on the width of the H$\beta$ line (9440 km s$^{-1}$) and the continuum flux from 2MASS $J$-band data (Skrutskie et al. 2006) using the formulation of Bennert et al. (2015)." (Optical and Infrared); "However, using the *HST* grism spectroscopy, we find \[Oiii\] emission and faint continuum in the C1 component, which appears to be both extended and characterized by a significant velocity gradient ($\approx +1100$ km s$^{-1}$ over 0.24$''$)." (The quasar host galaxy and its environment)
   - software: IRAF, astrodrizzle, aXe
   - parameter: H-beta line width = 9440 km s^-1  
  > "based on the width of the H$\beta$ line (9440 km s$^{-1}$) and the continuum flux from 2MASS $J$-band data (Skrutskie et al. 2006) using the formulation of Bennert et al. (2015)." (Optical and Infrared)
   - assumption: Lambda CDM cosmology with H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "We assume a $\Lambda$ CDM cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm M}=0.3$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Bennert et al. (2015) black hole mass scaling relation  
  > "using the formulation of Bennert et al. (2015)." (Optical and Infrared)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked field environment with Spitzer, HST, and Gemini to rule out cluster or group SZE contamination  
  > "The lack of any other obvious group or cluster in the field argues against significant contamination of the SZE signal by the intracluster medium of a compact cluster or intragroup medium of a group of galaxies either associated with the quasar, or along the line of sight to the SZE signal." (The quasar host galaxy and its environment)
3. **Measure SZE bubble geometry and calculate minimum bubble enthalpy**: Convert peak SZE flux decrement to brightness temperature change and Compton y parameter, measure beam-corrected bubble radii, and compute electron pressure and thermal enthalpy (5/2 n k_B T V)  
  > "The peak brightness of the SZE decrement of $-$25.9 $\mu$Jy (Section 2.1) corresponds to a brightness temperature change of $\Delta T_{\rm b,peak} = -37.7$ $\mu$K." (Interpretation of the SZE Signal); "For the observed peak decrement, this gives a Compton parameter $y=\tau k_{\rm B} T_{\rm e}/(m_{\rm e}c^2) \approx 2.08 \times 10^{-5}$." (Thermal SZE Model); "The minimum amount of energy injected into the bubble by the wind is given by the enthalpy $(3/2) n k_{\rm B} T V + p V = (5/2) n k_{\rm B} T V$." (Thermal SZE Model)
   - parameter: Beam-corrected average projected radius r = 3.9'' = 33 kpc; peak distance r_max = 10.1'' = 85 kpc; outer edge distance R = 12.8'' = 108 kpc  
  > "The average projected radius of the bubble is $r \approx 3.9\ensuremath{''} = 33$ kpc. The peak is located at a distance of $r_{\rm max} \approx 10.1\ensuremath{''} = 85$ kpc from the QSO. The distance from the QSO to the outer edge of the bubble is about $R \approx 12.8\ensuremath{''} = 108$ kpc." (Interpretation of the SZE Signal)
   - assumption: Pure thermal SZE (v_r/c << (k_B T_e)/(m_e c^2) f_1(x)), n = 1.92 n_e, equipartition of ions and electrons (T = T_e)  
  > "We first consider a pure thermal SZE model \[$v_{\rm r}/c << (k_{\rm B} T_{\rm e})/( m_{\rm e }c^2) f_1(x)$\]." (Thermal SZE Model); "Here, $n \approx 1.92 n_e$ is the total number density of thermal particles, $T$ is an average temperature, and we assume equipartition of ions and electrons, so that $T = T_e$, and $V$ is the volume of the bubble." (Thermal SZE Model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Equipartition assumption validated by short proton-electron equipartition time (~4000 yr)  
  > "The equipartition time between protons and electrons is short, $\approx 4000$ yr (e.g., Wong & Sarazin 2009). Thus, the assumption of equipartition is justified." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
4. **Solve conical wind bubble models (pure tSZE and combined tSZE+kSZE) for wind power, age, energy, and shock properties**: Adapt the RS11 self-similar wind bubble model to a conical outflow geometry and solve the two simultaneous equations for outer radius R and peak Compton parameter y_max (with and without the kSZE velocity term) to derive wind kinetic power L_W, age t, total energy E_W, shock speed v_s, and shock temperature T_s  
  > "To remove the assumption of spherical symmetry, we assume that our observed SZE signal comes from a region which is a spherical cone, with the cone apex and the center of curvature located at the QSO." (Thermal SZE Model); "Since we have observed values for both $R \approx 112$ kpc (corrected for projection) and $y_{\rm max} \approx 2.08 \times 10^{-5}$, we can estimate both $L_W$ and $t$, and the total energy of the event, $E_W = L_W t$." (Thermal SZE Model); "We thus consider a model in which the observed SZE decrement is due to significant contributions from both the tSZE and kSZE." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
   - parameter: Cone half-angle theta = 30 deg (solid angle fraction f_Omega = 0.067), outflow axis angle = 45 deg to line of sight, deprojected outer radius R = 112 kpc, 8% pressure profile correction  
  > "We estimate the half angle of the cone in our system to be $\theta = 30^{\circ}$ (Figure 1; upper right). We further assume that the axis of the outflow is at an angle of 45$^{\circ}$ to the outward extension of our line of sight." (Thermal SZE Model); "where $L_W$ is the wind kinetic luminosity of the observed SZE bubble, and $f_\Omega = (1 - \cos \theta )/2 \approx 0.067$ is the fraction of the total 4$\pi$ ster of the outflow." (Thermal SZE Model); "The projected outer radius of the observed bubble is 108 kpc, so that the actual radius is about 112 kpc." (Thermal SZE Model)
   - assumption: Negligible cooling of hot phase gas, IGM density from cosmological bias and halo overdensity, strong shock jump conditions, radiative efficiency eta = 0.1 for black hole comparison  
  > "The first is that cooling of the hot phase gas is negligible. The second is that the density of the IGM can be estimated from the typical cosmological bias parameter for quasars combined with an estimate of the over-density of the halo." (Thermal SZE Model); "The temperature behind the shock front, $T_s$, is given by the strong shock jump condition, $T_s = 3 \mu m_p v_s^2 / (16 k_{\rm B})$." (Thermal SZE Model); "As an alternative to comparing the wind luminosity to the current power of the quasar, we can compare $E_{\rm W}$ to the total radiative energy of the black hole (assuming a radiative efficiency of $\eta=0.1$)." (Discussion)
   - fitting: analytic solution of two equations with two unknowns  
  > "Equations ([eqn:radius]) and ([eqn:ymax]) are two relations with two unknowns. Since we have observed values for both $R \approx 112$ kpc (corrected for projection) and $y_{\rm max} \approx 2.08 \times 10^{-5}$, we can estimate both $L_W$ and $t$, and the total energy of the event, $E_W = L_W t$." (Thermal SZE Model)
   - validation: Compared total energy across three methods (enthalpy, tSZE only, tSZE+kSZE) and checked bremsstrahlung cooling time (~0.6 Gyr vs age ~0.1 Gyr)  
  > "Furthermore, the fair agreement in the total energy estimate between the three different methods (total enthalpy, tSZE only and kSZE+tSZE) suggests that our estimate of that quantity at least is fairly robust." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model); "This corresponds to a cooling time of $\sim$0.6 Gyr, suggesting that cooling effects can be ignored." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)

## Qualitative findings

- First direct interferometric detection of an SZE decrement associated with a quasar wind bubble around HE 0515-4414, aligned with extended [OIII] emission line gas  
  > "In this letter, we present evidence that we have made the first detection of such a bubble, associated with the hyperluminous quasar HE 0515-4414." (Abstract); "The SZE detection is corroborated by the presence of extended emission line gas at the same position angle as the wind." (Abstract)
- The one-sided nature of the SZE decrement is naturally explained by a combination of thermal SZE and kinetic SZE, which add on the receding side and partially cancel on the approaching side  
  > "In the receding half of the outflow (relative to the observer) the kZSE and tSZE contributions to the SZE are both of the same sign, but in the approaching half the two partially cancel." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- The wind kinetic luminosity (~0.003%-0.01% of the quasar luminosity) is much lower than typical theoretical predictions (1-10%), but the long cooling time (~0.6 Gyr) allows the bubble to provide long-term feedback  
  > "Estimates of the energy in the wind allow us to constrain the wind luminosity to the lower end of theoretical predictions, $\sim 0.01$% of the bolometric luminosity of the quasar." (Abstract); "However, the age we estimate for the bubble, $\sim 0.1$ Gyr, and the long cooling time, $\sim 0.6$ Gyr, means that such bubbles may be effective at providing feedback between bursts of quasar activity." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected the most luminous radio-quiet quasar visible to ALMA (HE 0515-4414 at z=1.71) and observed in Band 4 (~130 GHz) where the thermal SZE decrement peaks, combining a compact configuration matched to the 1-10'' wind scale with a larger configuration to subtract point sources.  
  > "The predicted size scale of the signal, $\sim 10-100$ kpc, corresponding to $\sim 1-10$ arcsec at $z\stackrel{>}{_{\sim}}1$, is well matched to the angular resolution of ALMA in the most compact configurations." (Introduction); "We selected the most luminous radio-quiet quasar we could find in the literature that had good visibility to ALMA, HE 0515-4414 ($z=1.71$; Reimers et al. 1998)." (Introduction); "The peak intensity of the thermal SZE decrement is seen at $\approx 130$ GHz (Sunyaev & Zeldovich 1972), which lies in ALMA band-4." (ALMA)
- Why this method: Subtracted point sources in the uv-plane and applied uv-tapering to optimize surface brightness sensitivity on the scale of the wind bubble; modified the RS11 model to a cone with kSZE because the observed outflow is non-spherical and one-sided.  
  > "No clear signal was readily apparent in the naturally-weighted map (which has the highest point source sensitivity, with an RMS of $\sigma =$ 3.5 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ and a synthesized beam of $\theta_{\rm FWHM} =$ 3.2$''$$\times$2.3$''$ at a position angle of PA = 106$^{\circ}$), so tapering was applied to improve the surface brightness sensitivity of the image." (ALMA); "To remove the assumption of spherical symmetry, we assume that our observed SZE signal comes from a region which is a spherical cone, with the cone apex and the center of curvature located at the QSO." (Thermal SZE Model)
- Alternative: Contamination of the SZE signal by a foreground or host galaxy cluster/group (rejected based on Spitzer, HST, and Gemini data showing no massive cluster/group, and the z=0.28 poor cluster of mass 6e12 M_sun being too small)  
  > "The estimated mass of this cluster, $6\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}$ is much too small for it to contribute to the observed SZE though." (The quasar host galaxy and its environment); "The lack of any other obvious group or cluster in the field argues against significant contamination of the SZE signal by the intracluster medium of a compact cluster or intragroup medium of a group of galaxies either associated with the quasar, or along the line of sight to the SZE signal." (The quasar host galaxy and its environment)
- Alternative: Intrinsically one-sided outflow or counter-flow blocked by higher ISM density (considered as alternative explanations for the one-sided SZE decrement)  
  > "There are a number of possible explanations, including an intrinsically one-sided outflow, or the counter-flow being blocked by a higher ISM density in its path." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- Alternative: Starburst-driven wind (~500 M_sun/yr sustained for 1.3e8 yr; cannot be ruled out in the absence of star formation rate constraints)  
  > "For the conical outflow model above, assuming a mass loading factor of unity and a wind velocity of 1000 km s$^{-1}$, a star formation rate of approximately $500 \, M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ sustained for $1.3 \times 10^8$ yr could produce our observed signal." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model); "In the absence of constraints on the star formation rate in this object, we cannot currently rule out a starburst origin." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)
- Alternative: Weak radio AGN jet feedback below the SUMSS detection limit (<6 mJy at 843 MHz; cannot be ruled out without deeper radio continuum observations)  
  > "HE 0515$-$4414 is undetected in the cm radio ($<$6 mJy at 843 MHz in the SUMSS survey, Mauch et al. 2003, corresponding to a radio luminosity at 1.4 GHz, $L_{1.4} < 6 \times 10^{25}$ W Hz$^{-1}$ assuming a spectral index of -0.8), but even a relatively weak radio source can have significant feedback effects (e.g., McNamara et al. 2014)." (Thermal Plus Kinetic SZE Model)

- Limitation (authors): Possible residual diffuse extended emission associated with the brightest subtracted field source A5
