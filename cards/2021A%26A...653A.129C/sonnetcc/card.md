## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract the Band 3 spectrum and subtract the continuum**: Extract a spectrum over a beam-sized area at the continuum peak; split into 32 windows of 1 GHz, apply STATCONT c-SMC to each, average the continuum-subtracted windows with MADCUBA; fit a power law to the STATCONT continuum levels  
  > "we have analysed a spectrum extracted inside an area equal to the beam size towards the continuum peak position" (2.1 ALMA data); "Thus, we have applied the corrected sigma clipping method (c-SMC) approach of the Python-based tool STATCONT" (2.1 ALMA data); "we have divided the final spectrum obtained by (cite: mininni2020) in 32 spectral windows of 1 GHz each." (Appendix: Continuum determination)
   - software: STATCONT, MADCUBA
   - parameter: 32 spectral windows of 1 GHz each  
  > "we have divided the final spectrum obtained by (cite: mininni2020) in 32 spectral windows of 1 GHz each." (Appendix: Continuum determination)
   - parameter: Continuum uncertainty +/-1.2 K, 11% of the continuum level at the reference frequency 84.579 GHz, added to the fit errors  
  > "which corresponds to the 11% of the continuum level at a reference frequency of 84.579 GHz" (2.1 ALMA data)
   - assumption: Continuum brightness temperature follows a power law in frequency with alpha = 2 + beta for optically thin dust emission  
  > "for optically thin dust emission (e.g. (cite: miyake1993))" (Appendix: Continuum determination)
   - fitting: Linear regression of log T_SB versus log(nu/nu0) over the 32 continuum levels gives beta = 0.71+/-0.06  
  > "We applied the linear regression fit to the latter equation" (Appendix: Continuum determination)
   - validation: The STATCONT-based continuum error of 1 K agrees with the 1.2 K from the combination of the spectra in the GUAPOS paper  
  > "The error of 1 K obtained with this procedure is consistent with the uncertainty of 1.2 K obtained from the combination of the spectra" (Appendix: Continuum determination)
2. **Identify lines and check contamination**: Build LTE synthetic spectra in MADCUBA-SLIM from CDMS/JPL entries (plus added entries); identify other species preliminarily to evaluate line contamination  
  > "SLIM uses the spectroscopic entries from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy" (2.3 Spectral analysis); "a preliminary identification of other molecular species has been done to evaluate the effect of possible line contaminations" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM, CDMS, JPL
   - parameter: A line is non-contaminated if the peak of another species lies farther than FWHM from it; in a blend with separation below FWHM the contamination must be below 15%  
  > "non-contamination from a line of another species if its peak is at a distance larger than FWHM from the peak of the considered line" (2.3 Spectral analysis); "the contamination of the line of the other species should be" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - assumption: LTE with line opacity included, justified by n(H2) ~ 1e8 cm-3  
  > "assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions and taking into account the line opacity" (2.3 Spectral analysis); "the LTE assumption is well justified because of the high volume density" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit LTE models to get N, Tex, vLSR and FWHM**: MADCUBA-AUTOFIT non-linear least-squares fit of the LTE synthetic spectrum to the non-contaminated transitions of each species; vibrational states and 13C isotopologues fitted separately for HNCO, HC(O)NH2 and CH3NCO; CH3C(O)NH2 v=0 and vt=1,2 fitted together  
  > "This fitting tool provides the best non-linear least-squared fit to all the transitions taken into account by using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm." (2.3 Spectral analysis); "we have limited the fit to non-contaminated or slightly contaminated transitions" (2.3 Spectral analysis); "we have been able to fit them simultaneously with a single LTE fit" (3.1.4 Acetamide (CH3C(O)NH2))
   - software: MADCUBA, AUTOFIT
   - parameter: Free parameters: column density N, excitation temperature Tex, peak velocity vLSR, FWHM; FWHM, vLSR and/or Tex fixed when the fit does not converge (e.g. HNCO FWHM fixed to 8 km/s; 13C isotopologue parameters fixed to the main species)  
  > "which best reproduce the observed line profiles" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO)); "because the fit did not converge leaving it free" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO))
   - parameter: Total partition function Qvibrot = Qrot x Qvib when vibrational spectroscopy exists  
  > "has been derived taking into account the contribution of both the rotational partition function" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - assumption: Emission fills the beam, so no beam dilution correction  
  > "we did not consider beam-dilution in the fitting procedure" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt least squares in AUTOFIT (no sampler or priors); final relative error on N is the quadrature sum of the fit relative error and the 11% continuum error  
  > "The final relative error on the total column densities has been derived as the square root of the quadratic sum of the relative error given by the fit algorithm and the error on the continuum level" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Correct for optical thickness to get final column densities**: Where the main-isotopologue ground state is optically thick (HNCO, HC(O)NH2), take N from the optically thinner 13C isotopologue multiplied by the Galactic 12C/13C ratio; for CH3NCO adopt the vb=1 result; compare with the vibrationally excited states  
  > "Since HNCO is optically thick, we have derived the column density using the optically thinner" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO)); "following the galactocentric trend recently obtained by (cite: yan2019)" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO)); "the derived line opacities are up to 0.4 confirming that" (3.1.3 Methyl isocyanate (CH3NCO))
   - software: MADCUBA
   - parameter: 12C/13C = 37+/-12 at the galactocentric distance of G31 (5.02 kpc)  
  > "At the galactocentric distance of G31" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO))
   - assumption: 13C isotopologues and vibrationally excited states are optically thin; 12C/13C follows the Galactic trend  
  > "which suggests that both isotopologues are reasonably optically thin" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Simulating the ground-state spectrum with the corrected column density gives opacities 1.3-3.4 for HNCO v=0, confirming optical thickness; corrected values agree with vibrationally excited states  
  > "the derived line opacities are in the range 1.3–3.4 confirming that HNCO" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO))
5. **Derive upper limits for non-detections and undetected states**: Assume Tex, vLSR and FWHM from a related species or fixed values, then raise N to the maximum value compatible with the observed spectrum; MADCUBA upper limits on integrated intensity use 3 x rms x dv / sqrt(n_chan)  
  > "where rms is the root-mean-square measured over a line-free spectral range" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO)); "to the maximum value compatible with the observed spectrum" (3.1.6 Non detections)
   - software: MADCUBA
   - parameter: For NH2C(O)NH2, NH2C(O)CN, HOCH2C(O)NH2: Tex = 150 K, vLSR = 96.5 km/s, FWHM = 7 km/s; CH3CH2NCO uses the CH3NCO vb=1 values  
  > "we have assumed $T_{\rm ex}$ = 150 K, $\varv_{\rm LSR}$=96.5 km s$^{-1}$, and FWHM = 7 km s$^{-1}$" (3.1.6 Non detections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CH3NCO/CH3CH2NCO lower limit compared with the ratio >10 found in Orion KL and Sgr B2 by Kolesnikova et al.  
  > "which is consistent with the CH" (3.1.6 Non detections)
6. **Convert column densities to abundances**: X = N / N(H2), with N(H2) from the continuum inside the extraction area; abundances relative to CH3OH also derived  
  > "obtained from the continuum emission inside the area from which we extracted the spectrum" (2.3 Spectral analysis); "is the abundance inside a beam calculated as" (Table 2 notes)
   - parameter: N(H2) = (1.0+/-0.2)e25 cm-2  
  > "obtained from the continuum emission inside the area from which we extracted the spectrum" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Map the emission at 1.2'' and 0.2'' and test spatial consistency**: Crop each cube around the rest frequency, subtract the continuum pixel by pixel, integrate four channels per transition; compare emission regions across Eup and molecules, and compare 1.4 mm column densities from the Band 6 data with the 3 mm ones  
  > "for each transitions we have cropped the cube to frequencies" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps); "we obtained the integrated intensity maps from the four channels around the rest frequencies" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
   - software: CASA, MADCUBA
   - parameter: Velocity range for the maps 93 to 100 km/s; four channels correspond to 6.5 km/s at ~90 GHz  
  > "The velocity range used for the integrated intensity maps goes from 93 to 100 km" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps); "corresponds to 6.5 km" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
   - fitting: At 1.4 mm Tex, vLSR and FWHM are fixed to the 3 mm values  
  > "are consistent to those derived from the GUAPOS data" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
   - validation: 1.4 mm column densities agree with 3 mm within a factor of 2  
  > "the column densities of the three molecules obtained at 1.4 mm are consistent within a factor of 2 with those obtained at 3 mm" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
8. **Compare with other sources and fit abundance correlations**: Compare abundances relative to H2 and to CH3OH, and column density ratios relative to HC(O)NH2, across hot cores, hot corinos, a GC cloud and comet 67P; fit power laws between pairs of abundances and report Pearson coefficients  
  > "Figure 15 shows the molecular abundances derived in G31 and in the other regions." (4.1 Molecular abundances); "with a Pearson coefficient of 0.99 and a P-value" (4.1 Molecular abundances)
   - parameter: A 25% error is assumed where literature errors are not given  
  > "For abundances whose errors were not provided we assumed a value of 25%." (4.1 Molecular abundances)
   - assumption: CH3OH column densities from 13CH3OH or CH3 18OH are corrected with 12C/13C and 16O/18O ratios as a function of galactocentric distance  
  > "ratios corrections as a function of the galactocentric distance" (4.2 Abundances with respect to CH3OH)
   - fitting: Power-law fits X_B = a X_A^b with Pearson coefficient and P-value; weighting not stated  
  > "with a Pearson coefficient of 0.99 and a P-value" (4.1 Molecular abundances)
   - validation: Fit compared with earlier power-law fits for HC(O)NH2 versus HNCO  
  > "the best power-law fits derived by (cite: lopez-sepulcre2015)" (4.1 Molecular abundances)

## Qualitative findings

- Ground vibrational states of HNCO, HC(O)NH2 and CH3NCO are optically thicker than the vibrationally excited states  
  > "the ground vibrational states of these molecules are optically thicker than the vibrationally excited ones" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
- The low 14N/15N ratio of 23 is the lowest ever estimated toward massive star-forming regions, taken as a sign of opacity in the main isotopologue  
  > "This is by far the lowest ratio ever estimated towards massive star-forming regions" (3.1.1 Isocyanic acid (HNCO))
- High-resolution maps show a ring-like morphology from an infalling and rotating toroid around four massive protostars, with higher Eup transitions more compact  
  > "The explanation for this morphology is that most of the emission comes from a rotating and infalling toroid surrounding a small protocluster of 4 massive protostars" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps); "present a more compact structure than the lower energy ones" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
- Emission of all studied species arises from the same region regardless of molecule and upper energy level  
  > "From these figures it is clear that the emission of different transitions arises from the same region regardless of the molecule and of the upper energy level." (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The GUAPOS 3 mm survey gives a single consistent spectrum of the line-rich hot core G31; the Band 6 data add higher angular resolution to test spatial differences  
  > "The aim of this work is to study these species together to allow a consistent study among them." (abstract); "We refer to (cite: cesaroni2017) and (cite: beltran2018) for detailed information on the observations." (2.2 Additional high-angular resolution data); "Therefore, to test this and eventually unveil spatial differences unresolved at" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
- Why this method: Vibrational states and isotopologues are fitted separately because a single Tex cannot fit all transitions of a molecule in a core with a temperature gradient; STATCONT is used because the spectrum is too line rich for line-free-channel continuum subtraction  
  > "because of the difficulty to fit all of the transitions of a molecule with a single" (2.3 Spectral analysis); "The spectrum of G31 is very line rich, which prevents to subtract the continuum by simply using line-free channels." (2.1 ALMA data)
- Alternative: Subtracting the Band 3 continuum with line-free channels was rejected because the spectrum is too line rich  
  > "The spectrum of G31 is very line rich, which prevents to subtract the continuum by simply using line-free channels." (2.1 ALMA data)
- Alternative: Beam dilution is not modeled because the hot core emission fills the beam  
  > "we did not consider beam-dilution in the fitting procedure" (2.3 Spectral analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The similar spatial distributions at 1.2'' could partly be due to the emission being only partially resolved  
  > "could be in part due to the fact that the emission is only partially resolved at an angular resolution of" (3.2 Integrated intensity maps)
- Limitation (authors): All transitions of the undetected species are contaminated, making upper limits difficult  
  > "All of the transitions of these molecules are contaminated with those of other species, making it difficult to derive upper limits for the column densities." (3.1.6 Non detections)
- Limitation (authors): Source comparisons may be affected by opacity effects in other works and should be taken with caution  
  > "All these similarities are within a factor of 4, and should be taken with caution since possible opacity effects of" (4.1 Molecular abundances)
- Limitation (authors): Whether correlations reflect a direct chemical link cannot yet be concluded  
  > "cannot be firmly concluded yet" (4.1 Molecular abundances)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Final column densities for HNCO, HC(O)NH2 and CH3NCO rest on an assumed Galactic 12C/13C and on tentative 13C or vibrationally excited detections
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Tex is fixed for several species (CH3NHCHO, 13C isotopologues, non-detections), so the dependence of N on these choices is only partly explored
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Power-law fits use few, heterogeneous literature sources with assumed 25% errors and mixed optical-depth treatment
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-pixel (beam-sized) spectrum from a source with a strong temperature gradient and absorption toward the center at 0.2''; the 1.4 mm values are reported without full analysis details
