## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum**: CASA pipeline calibration, self-calibration of 12m data, tclean multi-scale, combination with ACA via model-assisted imaging and FEATHER  
  > "In the first step, the ACA-only continuum image was used as a model in the `CLEAN`ing process of the 12m-ALMA data, following the so-called model-assisted imaging method." (2 ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, feather
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; 1.0" circular super-resolution beam  
  > "setting the robust parameter of Briggs equal to 0.5 as compromise between resolution and sensitivity" (2 ALMA observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract compact source catalogue**: SExtractor via sewpy with 6 sigma detection and 24 sigma deblending thresholds, min 5 pixels; visual removal of artifacts  
  > "we used a detection threshold of $6\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the rms noise level, and a deblending threshold of $24\sigma$." (3.1 Continuum source identification)
   - software: SExtractor, sewpy
   - parameter: 185 automatic detections, 142 after manual removal  
  > "A total of 185 sources were automatically identified by `SExtractor`." (3.1 Continuum source identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection to remove sources in noisy regions and clean artifacts  
  > "After visual inspection, we manually removed those located in regions of the map with a higher rms noise level" (3.1 Continuum source identification)
3. **Derive core masses and densities**: Optically thin dust emission mass equation with assumed temperatures; spherical density and column density  
  > "We assume optically thin emission, a gas to dust mass ratio of 100" (3.3 Physical properties of the continuum sources)
   - parameter: Td = 20 K (no counterpart), 50 K (IR or radio counterpart), 100 K (hot core)  
  > "is assumed to be 20 K when the source is only detected in the ALMA image and has no counterparts, 50 K if it has an infrared or radio counterpart, and 100 K" (3.3 Physical properties of the continuum sources)
   - assumption: kappa = 0.131 cm2/g at 87.6 GHz (Ossenkopf & Henning, beta = 2), gas-to-dust 100, D = 1.3 kpc, mu = 2.3  
  > "which we extrapolate to 0.131 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at 87.6 GHz assuming a power-law dependence with a $\beta$ power-law index of 2" (3.3 Physical properties of the continuum sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Free-free contamination estimated for radio counterparts with spectral indices -0.1 and +2  
  > "we assume two different spectral indices[^3] for the thermal free-free emission: $-0.1$ representing the case of optically thin emission, and $+2$ for the optically thick case" (3.3 Physical properties of the continuum sources)
4. **Identify clusters and measure spatial structure**: Seven scikit-learn clustering algorithms with K=4 from elbow and silhouette analysis; membership by vote (>=60%); MST, Q parameter, nearest-neighbour KDEs, two-point correlation function  
  > "If this value is lower than 60%, we consider that we can not assign the corresponding core to one of the clusters." (4.1 Cluster identification)
   - software: scikit-learn, NetworkX, FragMent
   - parameter: TPCF with 10000 random instances per cluster  
  > "We compute the random separation distributions ($DR(r)$ and $RR(r)$) by producing 10 000 instances of randomly placed points for each cluster" (4.2 Spatial structure of the clusters in NGC 6334)
   - fitting: Q uncertainty from 100 random 20% subsamples with Gaussian fit  
  > "Repeating this process 100 times, we obtained a distribution of the measured values of $Q$." (4 Clusters in NGC 6334)
   - validation: Cross-check of number of clusters with elbow and silhouette  
  > "both methods suggest that the number of clusters in our region is between 2 and 6, with $K=4$ the most probable value." (4.1 Cluster identification)
5. **CMF slope, completeness and bias tests**: Power-law fit of dN/dlnM above 2 Msun; Monte Carlo completeness; random-temperature test; Jeans-fragmentation resolution test  
  > "We fitted a power-law function given by $dN/d\ln(M) \propto M^{\alpha}$ to the CMFs for the range of masses above the completenes level." (5.1 Core mass function)
   - parameter: Completeness 2 Msun; temperature test 20-100 K, 100000 trials; epsilon0 = 0.4, p = 1.3  
  > "We find that $\epsilon_0=0.4$ and $p=1.3$ reproduce a slope of $-1.35$ in the CMF" (5.1.2 Resolution bias in the determination of the CMF)
   - assumption: IMF slope -1.35 (Salpeter/Kroupa) for comparison  
  > "The measured slope for the high-mass end of CMF in NGC 6334 clusters is not consistent with the $-1.35$ value of the IMF" (5.1 Core mass function)
   - fitting: Power-law fit; no sampler stated  
  > "We fitted a power-law function given by $dN/d\ln(M) \propto M^{\alpha}$ to the CMFs for the range of masses above the completenes level." (5.1 Core mass function)
   - validation: Predicted fragments compared to higher-resolution observations (source 43: 2, source 44: 9 vs 7)  
  > "For source 44 we estimate 9 fragments, in agreement with the 7 components that have been resolved with a resolution of $0.2\ensuremath{''}$." (5.1.2 Resolution bias in the determination of the CMF)
6. **Mass segregation**: Three approaches: MST of low/high mass (20 Msun), radial mass distribution, and mass segregation ratio with 500 random clusters  
  > "We used 500 randomly generated uniform clusters for a good determination of $\langle l_\mathrm{random}\rangle$." (5.2 Mass segregation)
   - software: NetworkX
   - assumption: Star formation efficiency 30%, so 20 Msun core forms B-type star  
  > "Assuming a star formation efficiency of 30%, a compact source with a mass of $\sim$20 $M_\odot$ ends up with forming a B-type star" (5.2 Mass segregation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Gaussian-cluster test of Lambda_MSR vs concentration  
  > "For each value of $\sigma$, we generate 50 Gaussian clusters containing 70 cores each." (10 Correlation between the mass segregation parameter)
7. **Fragmentation analysis (Jeans, filament)**: Thermal and turbulent Jeans mass per cluster; critical line mass and fragmentation spacing for southern filament of NGC 6334-I(N)  
  > "Integrating the flux of the ALMA combined map over the extent of the filament yields a total flux of 0.51 Jy." (5.3.2 Filament fragmentation)
   - parameter: sigma_nth ~0.7 km/s; filament T = 26 K  
  > "($\sigma_{\mathrm{nth}}\sim$0.7 km/s)" (5.3.1 Clump fragmentation)
   - assumption: Filament inclination neglected; line mass is an upper limit  
  > "As we neglect the inclination of the filament, the derived line-mass is an upper limit." (5.3.2 Filament fragmentation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Four clusters identified; NGC 6334-E most evolved, I(N) youngest with most cores, I(NW) low-mass cores only  
  > "NGC 6334-E appears as the most evolved cluster, already harboring a well-developed H ii region." (abstract)
- Hints of mass segregation in NGC 6334-I and I(N)  
  > "clusters NGC 6334-I and NGC 6334-I(N) show hints of segregation with the most massive cores located in the centre of the clusters." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Robust 0.5 as compromise between resolution and sensitivity; super-resolution image better for crowded sources  
  > "The super-resolution image is better suited to identify and distinguish continuum compact sources in crowded environments." (2 ALMA observations and data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Temperatures assumed rather than measured; unresolved multiplicity biases CMF  
  > "the measured slope can be biased due to the uncertainty in temperature determination and the unresolved multiplicity in the massive cores." (5.1 Core mass function)
- Limitation (authors): Low number statistics limit Q-parameter conclusions  
  > "the total number of sources in that cluster (10) limits the conclusions that can be derived from this analysis." (4.2 Spatial structure of the clusters in NGC 6334)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Temperatures assigned by category rather than measured, so masses are uncertain and CMF slope tests partly circular
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Bias tuning parameters (gamma, epsilon0, p) are fit to reproduce the IMF, not independent evidence
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Catalogue depends on SExtractor thresholds and manual removal
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Free-free and line contamination of 3 mm continuum only partly treated
