## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract knot spectra and measure radial velocities**: Extract 5-pixel-wide spectra, identify emission peaks with image knots, and average the two K I velocities relative to the star.  
  > "The same 5 pixel wide extractions along the slit were used for all of the spectra." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "The emission peaks were cross identified with the diffuse knots and condensations visible in the image by comparing their measured positions along the slit with the positions of the emission peaks knowing the scale of the spectra and images." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "The radial velocity is the average velocity of the K I lines relative to the velocity of the K I lines in the star’s spectrum measured in the 0.1$''$ slit (Table 1)." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - parameter: Processed sampling 0.0253 arcsec per pixel.  
  > "Consequently, the ”sampling”scale in the processed spectra is 0.0253$''$ per pixel, rather than the CCD’s 0.05$''$ scale." (*HST/STIS* Observations)
   - parameter: Stellar K I reference velocity 55 km s−1.  
  > "and the change in the adopted velocity for the central star, 55 km s$^{-1}$, see Paper 1." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - assumption: K I emission defines the reference frame.  
  > "As in previous papers, we adopt VY CMa’s K I emission velocity as our reference frame." (*HST/STIS* Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: W1 knot C was extracted and measured in both S1 and W1.  
  > "The profile in Figure 6 is from S1, but spectra were extracted from both slits and their measurements are included in Table 2." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
2. **Measure transverse motions**: Align and blink two-epoch images; measure x and y positions three times per usable filter combination and combine with a weighted mean.  
  > "The images are tightly aligned in pixel space and blinked to identify any offset." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "We measure the x and y positions of the blinked images three times in each filter combination in which the knot or filament is identified." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "The measurements are then combined for a weighted mean." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - parameter: HST/WFPC2 epochs 1999 and 2005.  
  > "We determine the transverse velocity from the proper motions of the knots measured in the two epochs of HST/WFPC2 images from 1999 and 2005 as decribed in (cite: RMH2007)." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - parameter: F410M, F547M, F656N and F1042M.  
  > "VY CMa was imaged in four filters, F410M, F547M, F656N, and F1042M, with a range of exposure times." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - assumption: Distance 1.2 kpc.  
  > "at the 1.2 kpc distance of VY CMa (cite: Zhang)." (The Central Star)
   - fitting: Weighted mean of position measurements.  
  > "The measurements are then combined for a weighted mean." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - validation: Previous measurements checked and some repeated to resolve discrepancies or multiple filter entries.  
  > "For this paper we checked all of our previous measurements, and in some cases repeated them to resolve discepancies or multiple entries from the different filters." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
3. **Derive knot geometry and ejection ages**: Combine transverse and stellar-relative radial velocities to derive orientation, motion direction, total velocity and age.  
  > "Combining the resulting transverse velocity with the radial velocity from the K I lines relative to the star, we then determine each knot’s orientation ($\theta$) with respect to the plane of the sky," (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "the direction of motion ($\phi$) and total space velocity and then estimate its age or time since ejection assuming a constant velocity." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - assumption: Constant velocity.  
  > "the direction of motion ($\phi$) and total space velocity and then estimate its age or time since ejection assuming a constant velocity." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - assumption: Inner-knot total velocity assumed to be 20–30 km s−1, like nearby Paper I knots.  
  > "If we assume that its total velocity is similar to those for the knots just to the west of the star in Paper I (20 – 30 km s$^{-1}$)," (The Central Star)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Illustrative deceleration and gradual-acceleration models give ages about 10% smaller and 5% longer, respectively.  
  > "At radial distances of 300–800 AU, the true age since ejection is then about 10% smaller than the time estimated by our simplified constant-velocity estimate." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "Alternatively, suppose that the ejection process was quite gradual, with a net outward acceleration proportional to $1/r^2$. In that case, the true age is about 5% longer than the constant-velocity estimate." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
4. **Test expansion of the SW Clump**: Fit an ellipse to five spatial points in each of two epochs and compare center motion and major-axis expansion.  
  > "To test this possibility, we fit an ellipse to the positions of the knots in the two epochs, 6.23 years apart." (The SW Clump); "Thus, in order to have five points necessary to define an ellipse, we used a point on the south rim, part of a luminous arc on the apparent south side of the "SW clump."" (The SW Clump)
   - parameter: Exclude S-E1 because its position is relatively uncertain.  
  > "Faint knot S-E1 was excluded because its position is relatively uncertain." (The SW Clump)
   - assumption: Intrinsic deviations from an ellipse are similar at both epochs.  
  > "Since the intrinsic deviations from a true ellipse are presumably similar for both epochs, we can meaningfully compare these two ellipses." (The SW Clump)
   - fitting: Best-fit ellipses; sampler, likelihood and priors not stated.  
  > "The best-fit ellipse is shown superposed on the F656N image from 1999 in Figure 8." (The SW Clump)
   - validation: Expansion-adjusted ellipse agreement: 16 mas rms and radial disagreement of order 5 mas; S-D and SW-A motions challenge the bubble model.  
  > "The two ellipses coincide with a r.m.s. difference of 16 mas when we allow for expansion, and the disagreement in the radial direction is of the order of 5 mas;" (The SW Clump); "Both have a direction of motion consistent with radial motion outward from the star as opposed to an expanding outer shell of a bubble moving away from the ellipse center." (The SW Clump)
5. **Revise ages of major ejecta**: Redetermine earlier large-feature ages using the revised distance and stellar radial velocity.  
  > "For comparison with the large arcs and features visible in the more distant ejecta (cite: RMH2007), we’ve redetermined their ejection ages (Table 3)" (The Mass Loss History); "with the improved distance to VY CMa (cite: Zhang) and our radial velocity for the central star from Paper I and see Table 1." (The Mass Loss History)
   - assumption: Distance 1.2 kpc and adopted central-star velocity 55 km s−1.  
  > "In all cases, the previous measurements were adjusted to the distance change from 1.5 kpc used in (cite: RMH2007) to 1.2 kpc (cite: Zhang)" (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "and the change in the adopted velocity for the central star, 55 km s$^{-1}$, see Paper 1." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Revised ages average slightly less than the 2007 values.  
  > "The revised ages average slightly less than reported in our 2007 paper." (The Mass Loss History)
6. **Reinterpret ALMA features**: Compare LSR-corrected inner-knot velocity and slit position with published maser images; revise Clump C age with the nearby optical-knot outflow velocity.  
  > "For a direct comparison, we show the distribution of the maser emission at 30.5 km s$^{-1}$ with the 0.1$''$ slit and the position of the inner knot superposed in Figure 3." (The Central Star); "With this adopted velocity we estimate an age of about 70 years for Clump C, for example, corresponding with the extended period of large light variations in the first half of the 20th century, Figure 9." (The Mass Loss History)
   - parameter: Adopted Clump C outflow velocity 27.5 km s−1.  
  > "The average outflow velocity for the small knots close to the star (Paper I) and at comparable projected distances as the ALMA clumps, is 27.5 km s$^{-1}$." (The Mass Loss History)
   - assumption: Apply the outflow velocity of nearby optical knots to ALMA Clump C.  
  > "The average outflow velocity for the small knots close to the star (Paper I) and at comparable projected distances as the ALMA clumps, is 27.5 km s$^{-1}$." (The Mass Loss History); "With this adopted velocity we estimate an age of about 70 years for Clump C," (The Mass Loss History)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Possible positional and velocity agreement with published maser peaks; association remains tentative.  
  > "Two of the published frames show maser peaks within 50 mas to the south and southwest of the star with velocities of 29.8 and 37.8 km s$^{-1}$ which may be coincident with inner the knot." (The Central Star)
7. **Compare ejections with historic variability**: Assemble the historic light curve and mark knot ejection ages to compare with extended dimming and variability.  
  > "In Figure 9, we show VY CMa’s light curve from 1800 to 2000." (The Mass Loss History); "The ejection ages of several of the knots are marked on the light curves. We emphasize that they agree with these extended periods of variability and change." (The Mass Loss History)
   - parameter: Robinson71 column 4 magnitudes reduced to Harvard photometry.  
  > "The upper panel for the 19th century is from the table in (cite: Robinson71)." (The Mass Loss History); "We use the magnitudes from his column 4 which are reduced to the Harvard photometry and are approximately visual magnitudes." (The Mass Loss History)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Overlapping 19th- and 20th-century light curves confirm the persistent decline.  
  > "Fortunately the 20th century light curve, (middle panel) overlaps the 1890 - 1910 period with no additional deep minima and confirms the decline by 1 to 1.5 magnitudes to 7.5 – 8 magnitudes in the visual" (The Mass Loss History)

## Qualitative findings

- Knot ejection ages correspond with extended periods of variability and dimming.  
  > "The ejection ages of several of the knots are marked on the light curves. We emphasize that they agree with these extended periods of variability and change." (The Mass Loss History)
- The inner optical K I knot may coincide with H2O masers.  
  > "We therefore suggest that the optical K I emission inner knot may coincide with the H$_{2}$O masers at similar velocities." (The Central Star)
- The SW Clump may be a coherent object or a chance alignment.  
  > "Thus it is not clear if the SW clump is a coherent object, or chance alignment of unrelated clumps of dust and gas." (The SW Clump)
- Surface activity and accompanying outflows may contribute substantially to red-supergiant mass loss.  
  > "Surface activity and the accompanying outflows may thus be a major contributor to mass loss in these red supergiants." (Comments on Betelgeuse)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: STIS resolves inner ejecta inaccessible to the earlier 0.8 arcsec ground-based spectroscopy.  
  > "Ground-based optical spectroscopy of the central star and ejecta has been limited to 0.8$''$ spatial resolution (cite: RMH2005)." (Introduction); "We therefore obtained *HST/STIS* observations to probe VY CMa’s innermost ejecta and numerous small, diffuse condensations to the south and southwest of the star." (Introduction)
- Why this method: K I emission traces gas; ellipse fitting avoids cross-correlation dominated by bright inner knots.  
  > "The K I lines, formed by resonant scattering, are the strongest emission features in VY CMa and are the best tracers of the gas." (*HST/STIS* Observations); "Comparing these loops or arcs via cross-correlations would be difficult because their inner knots are much brighter than their outer parts." (The SW Clump)
- Alternative: SW Clump as an arc or loop with S-D and SW-A superposed from later eruptions.  
  > "An alternative possibility would place knots S-E2 and SW-B and the south rim on an expanding arc or loop like several small arcs seen throughout the ejecta as well as the giant arcs/loops in the outer ejecta." (The SW Clump); "If that is the case, this arc or loop would have been ejected about 300 years ago, and the S-D and SW-A knots would then be chance superpositions from later eruptions." (The SW Clump)
- Alternative: The dusty VY feature may be a knot nearly in the line of sight rather than material surrounding the star in all directions.  
  > "Alternatively, the very dusty ‘VY’ may not completely surround the star and is not obscuring it in all directions, but is instead a large knot," (The Mass Loss History)
- Alternative: Acceleration and gravitational deceleration as alternatives to constant-velocity ages.  
  > "In one model, suppose that the ejecta were accelerated instantaneously in that region, and later were gravitationally decelerated as they moved outward." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star); "Alternatively, suppose that the ejection process was quite gradual, with a net outward acceleration proportional to $1/r^2$." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
- Alternative: Non-radial pulsation remains a possibility.  
  > "Non-radial pulsation is a possibility," (Outflows and Ejections: Pulsation and/or Surface Activity?)
- How they decided it was enough: Five suitable points defined each ellipse; the residual agreement was judged satisfactory given fuzzy positions.  
  > "Obviously it would be better to use more than five spatial points, but no other features were suitable." (The SW Clump); "this is satisfactory considering the fuzziness of each data point." (The SW Clump)
- Limitation (authors): The inner knot has no measured proper motion or transverse velocity.  
  > "We therefore do not have a measured proper motion for it and its tranverse velocity is not known." (The Central Star)
- Limitation (authors): Acceleration or slowing of the discrete ejecta is unknown.  
  > "Although the winds of evolved cool stars are known to accelerate, these knots and arcs are discrete ejections, and it is not known if the plasma is accelerated or slowed by interaction with surrounding material." (The Knots and Clumps South and Southwest of the Star)
- Limitation (authors): The fifth ellipse point is less well defined and has no velocity measurement.  
  > "Its position is less well defined than the other knots and it has no velocity measurement." (The SW Clump)
- Limitation (authors): Historic light-curve gaps leave recovery and additional minima unknown.  
  > "The decline to about 8th magnitude in 1880 is followed by a gap of 10 years. We do not know if the star recovered or had additional minima." (The Mass Loss History)
- Limitation (authors): Solar-motion choices affect the LSR correction; radio observations use LSRK.  
  > "The correction to the LSR depends on the adopted Solar motion." (The Central Star); "LSRK (Local Standard of Rest (Kinematic) used for the radio observations is based on the average velocity of stars near the Sun." (The Central Star)
