## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure CO(3-2) flux, errors and line widths**: Integrate CO(3-2) emission in circular apertures on continuum-subtracted, primary-beam-corrected maps; flux error following Lamperti2020; FWHM from single Gaussian fits; upper limits as 3 x error with DeltaV = 220 km/s  
  > "were extracted by integrating the CO(3-2) emission over the full spatial extent of the source using circular apertures with radii between 0.8$''$ and 1.4$''$ depending on the size of the galaxy" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass); "The full widths at half maximum (FWHM) were derived from single Gaussian fits of the emission lines." (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: circular apertures with radii 0.8-1.4 arcsec, chosen per galaxy size  
  > "using circular apertures with radii between 0.8$''$ and 1.4$''$ depending on the size of the galaxy" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: spectral resolution Delta w_ch = 50.7 km/s in the flux error formula  
  > "$\Delta w_{ch} = 50.7$ km s$^{-1}$" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: sigma_CO is the rms noise in Jy per spectral resolution element; DeltaV is the width of the window used for the line flux  
  > "where $\sigma_{\rm CO}$ is the rms noise (in Jy) calculated in units of spectral resolution $\Delta w_{ch}$" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: upper limits: 3 x epsilon_CO with DeltaV = 220 km/s  
  > "The flux for our upper-limit detections are defined as $3\times\epsilon_{\rm CO}$, with $\Delta V=220$ km s$^{-1}$, the median value of our sample." (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - fitting: single Gaussian line fits for FWHM (no sampler or priors stated)  
  > "The full widths at half maximum (FWHM) were derived from single Gaussian fits of the emission lines." (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Convert CO(3-2) flux to line luminosity**: L'CO = 3.25e7 S_CO DeltaV nu_obs^-2 D_L^2 (1+z)^-3 in K km/s pc^2  
  > "3.25 \times10^{7}" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass); "is the luminosity distance in Mpc" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: S DeltaV in Jy km/s, nu_obs in GHz, D_L in Mpc, z of the galaxy  
  > "is the observed frequency in GHz" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - assumption: flat LCDM with Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc for D_L  
  > "In the following, we assume a flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) cosmology with $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.3$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$, and $H_{0} = 70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive cold molecular gas mass and gas fraction**: M_H2 = alpha_CO L'CO(3-2) / r31, with 36% helium correction in alpha_CO; mu_H2 = M_H2 / Mstar  
  > "where $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ is the CO(1-0) luminosity-to-molecular-gas-mass conversion factor, considering a 36% correction to account for interstellar helium" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 4.36 +/- 0.9 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1 (includes helium)  
  > "we have considered $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}=4.36 \pm 0.9\,M_\odot\,{\rm (K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^2)^{-1}}$, which includes the correction for helium" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: r31 = 0.5 +/- 0.05  
  > "we assume $r_{31}=0.5\pm0.05$ similarly to other intermediate to high-$z$ studies" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - assumption: Milky-Way-like alpha_CO appropriate for normal star-forming galaxies  
  > "as a good estimate for normal star-forming galaxies" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive stellar masses and SFRs from SED fitting**: MAGPHYS SED fits to u, g, r, i, z, Ks total magnitudes; peak PDF values with 68% intervals  
  > "Stellar masses and SFRs were derived with MAGPHYS[^3] (cite: daCunha2008), based on the $u$, $g$, $r$, $i$, $z$, and $K_s$ total magnitudes." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs); "Their values in this work correspond to the peak values of the PDFs. The uncertainties are the 68% confidence interval of the PDFs." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - software: MAGPHYS
   - assumption: Bruzual & Charlot stellar populations and Charlot & Fall dust attenuation models  
  > "The stellar populations and dust extinction models are those of (cite: Bruzual2003) and (cite: Charlot2000)." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF  
  > "we use a Chabrier initial mass function (IMF) (cite: Chabrier2003)" (Introduction)
   - fitting: MAGPHYS probability density functions for each parameter (library-based; no sampler or priors detailed)  
  > "MAGPHYS provides probability density functions (PDFs) for each parameter, such as the SFR, $M_{\rm star}$, dust mass, and dust temperature." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - validation: AGN contamination judged unlikely from [O II]/Hbeta line ratio in galaxy spectra; photometry cannot identify AGN  
  > "the analysis of the galaxy spectra, in particular, the \[O II\] to H$\beta$ line ratio suggests that they are not typical AGNs" (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
5. **Place targets on the star-forming main sequence and compare sSFR and gas fractions with literature samples**: Compare galaxies with the Speagle2014 MS (+/-0.3 dex dispersion); normalise sSFR to the MS (Genzel2015 approach) and compare distributions of sSFR/sSFR(MS) and mu_H2 for field and cluster CO samples in redshift slices  
  > "Almost two-thirds (65%) of our ALMA targets fall within the $\pm0.3$ dex dispersion of the MS." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs); "Galaxies are grouped in redshift slices and we distinguish between field and clusters samples." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - assumption: literature stellar masses converted to Chabrier IMF with M_C = 0.94 M_K  
  > "$M_{{\rm star},\,C}=0.94 \times M_{{\rm star},\,K}$" (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Identify low gas fraction star-forming galaxies relative to the field Mstar-mu_H2 relation**: Adopt field MS Mstar-mu_H2 relation with fixed slope and a redshift zero-point shift, with +/-1 sigma_H2 band; galaxies below the -1 sigma_H2 line are low-mu_H2; passive galaxies excluded  
  > "with a constant slope of $-0.82$ and a shift of $-0.08$ dex, induced by the transition from $z=0.55$ to $z=0.45$" (4.1 Gas fractions); "three star-forming galaxies fall below the $-1\times \sigma_{\rm H_2}$ line of the $M_{\rm star}$–$\mu_\mathrm{H_2}$ relation" (4.1 Gas fractions)
   - parameter: slope -0.82, zero-point shift -0.08 dex from z=0.55 to z=0.45  
  > "with a constant slope of $-0.82$ and a shift of $-0.08$ dex" (4.1 Gas fractions)
   - parameter: sigma_H2 = 0.37  
  > "with $\sigma_{\rm H_2}=0.37$ the variance of the" (4.1 Gas fractions)
   - assumption: slope of the relation does not change with redshift (Tacconi2018)  
  > "does not vary with redshift and that only a zero point shift should be taken into account" (4.1 Gas fractions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test whether gas fraction distributions differ from the PHIBSS2 field**: Anderson-Darling k-sample test on mu_H2 distributions: PHIBSS2 MS vs CL1411.1-1148, and PHIBSS2 vs combined CL1301.7-1139 + CL1411.1-1148  
  > "We performed a Anderson–Darling (A-D) test (cite: Scholz1987) between the $\mu_\mathrm{H_2}$ distributions of the MS PHIBSS2 and CL1411.1$-$1148 galaxies." (4.1 Gas fractions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Examine projected phase space and infall regions of both clusters**: Projected phase-space diagram of star-forming spectroscopic members within 4 sigma_cl and 8 R200 relative to virialised, infall and escape boundaries; infalling = 1 to ~5 R200  
  > "The spectroscopic members, including the ALMA targets, within 4 $\sigma_{cl}$ and $8 \times R_{200}$, are seen relatively to the virialised, infall, and escape region boundaries (cite: Mahajan2011)." (4.2 Comparison with CL1411.1-1148); "Galaxies are considered infalling onto the clusters when they are located between 1 and $\sim 5$ virial radii" (4.2 Comparison with CL1411.1-1148)
   - parameter: NFW halo with concentration c = 6 for escape velocity  
  > "we assumed a NFW halo profile with a concentration of ${\rm c} = 6$" (4.2 Comparison with CL1411.1-1148)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Characterise local density and photometric membership of the targets (sample context)**: Photometric redshifts with EAZY; nearest-neighbour surface density rho_N = N / (pi r_N^2) in a photometric redshift slice around the cluster, N = 10  
  > "Photometric redshifts, $z_{\rm phot}$ were obtained with EAZY (cite: Brammer2008)" (2.1 Sample selection); "We chose $N=10$, which corresponds to an average scale of about 0.8 Mpc" (2.1 Sample selection)

   - parameter: photometric slice +/- 1 delta_cl = (1+z_cl) x sigma_NMAD = 0.0533

## Qualitative findings

- Samples without FIR/SFR selection lack galaxies above the main sequence, unlike most earlier CO surveys  
  > "Very noticeably, our CL1301.7$-$1139 and CL1411.1$-$1148 samples, without any prior on previous FIR detection and in the vicinity of high density environments, are devoid of galaxies above the MS." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- Differences between CO surveys' gas fractions depend on target selection criteria rather than on field versus cluster environment  
  > "It does not depend on field or cluster environments, but rather on the target selection criteria" (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- Three passive galaxies have only CO upper limits, while similar early-type-looking galaxies were detected, so morphology does not explain the CO deficiency  
  > "The lack of star formation activity and their CO deficiency does not seem to be driven by morphology (internal quenching)." (Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CL1301.7-1139 was chosen from EDisCS because at its redshift environmental galaxy transformation was at its peak and its medium mass makes it representative of progenitors of nearby clusters; targets selected without SFR or FIR prior for an unbiased view  
  > "CL1301.7$-$1139 was chosen from EDisCS (cite: White2005) because, at its intermediate redshift, environmentally induced galaxy transformation was in its heyday" (2.1 Sample selection); "Unlike previous works, our sample selection does not impose a minimum star formation rate or detection in the far-infrared." (abstract)
- Why this method: alpha_CO, r31 and the low-gas-fraction criterion are kept the same as in the first SEEDisCS paper so results can be compared reliably  
  > "Just as in and for the sake of reliable comparison, we have considered" (3.1 CO flux and molecular gas mass)
- Alternative: Attributing lack of star formation and CO deficiency of the three passive galaxies to early-type morphology (rejected because disk-less galaxies are detected in CO)  
  > "It would be tempting to attribute their lack of star formation activity and their CO deficiency to their early-type morphologies." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- Alternative: Using Milky Way alpha_CO for samples that used metallicity or MS-dependent alpha_CO changes distribution positions slightly but not shapes  
  > "Considering $\alpha_{\rm MW}$ for these two samples slightly redistributes the position of the galaxies within the distributions, but does not modify their global shapes and mean positions." (4.1 Gas fractions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low number statistics: only three star-forming CL1301.7-1139 galaxies between 1 and 4 R200  
  > "given that we only observed three CL1301.7$-$1139 star-forming galaxies between 1 and $4 \times R_{200}$" (4.2 Comparison with CL1411.1-1148)
- Limitation (authors): Photometry cannot identify AGN, which could bias SFRs  
  > "The photometric wavelength coverage does not allow for the identification of active galactic nuclei (AGNs), which could affect the SFRs in particular." (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- Limitation (authors): Low gas fraction is defined relative to field surveys, not in absolute terms  
  > "It does not necessarily imply that these star-forming galaxies have a low cold gas content in absolute terms" (4.1 Gas fractions)
- Limitation (authors): SFR estimates in comparison samples rely on a larger set of methods  
  > "the estimates of the SFRs rely on a much larger set of methods" (3.2 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas masses depend on a single assumed Milky Way alpha_CO and r31 = 0.5 with no metallicity or MS-dependent alpha_CO test for the target galaxies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small samples (22 galaxies, 3 low-mu_H2) make the infall-region inference statistically weak; the A-D test compares different pairings and the CL1301.7-1139 infall subsample is only three galaxies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No per-galaxy aperture/velocity-window details; flux errors rely on a single-channel-based formula
