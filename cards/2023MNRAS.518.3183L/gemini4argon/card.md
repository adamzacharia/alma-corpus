## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, concatenate, and image ALMA visibility data with JvM correction**: Perform initial pipeline calibration, apply manual flagging of bad time periods and channels, concatenate with compact-configuration data from Capak et al. (2015), interactively CLEAN with natural weighting and manual masking, and apply the JvM residual flux scaling correction.  
  > "The calibrated visibility data were then re-analyzed, performing additional flagging of bad time periods in the data and bad channels. We concatenated the new ALMA observations with that obtained by (cite: Capak2015)." (ALMA Observations); "The data were re-imaged using a natural weighting of the u-v visibility data to create continuum, moment zero, and channel maps with an average beam size of 0.27"." (ALMA Observations); "Since the residual map which results from the CLEAN algorithm can have incorrect default flux scaling when combining different array configurations (cite: JVM1995), we apply a correction as suggested by (cite: JvMModern2021)." (ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: natural weighting  
  > "The data were re-imaged using a natural weighting of the u-v visibility data to create continuum, moment zero, and channel maps with an average beam size of 0.27"." (ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: average beam size of 0.27"  
  > "The data were re-imaged using a natural weighting of the u-v visibility data to create continuum, moment zero, and channel maps with an average beam size of 0.27"." (ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that main results remain the same when using only the new ALMA data and that no artificial structures are added when combining with Capak et al. (2015) data.  
  > "We remark that the main results presented in this paper remain the same when using only the new ALMA data and no artificial structures are added when combining with data from Capak et al. (2015)." (ALMA Observations)
2. **Astrometrically calibrate archival HST/WFC3 images**: Query GAIA DR2 using astroquery.gaia, manually associate GAIA stars with stars in the HST F105W, F125W, and F160W images, compute positional offset, and apply average offset to HST images.  
  > "These data products were corrected astrometrically by calibrating them to the GAIA DR2 release (cite: GAIADR2). We queried the DR2 release using the python package `astroquery.gaia` [^2]." (HST Observations); "The difference in position between the DR2 release and the actual stars in the HST images renders the offset which is then applied to the HST images. The average offset was $\sim$ 0.300 $\pm$ 0.008" ($\Delta \alpha = -0.128"$; $\Delta \delta = 0.283"$) [^3]." (HST Observations)
   - software: astroquery.gaia, Align.py
   - parameter: Average astrometric offset ~ 0.300 +/- 0.008" (Delta alpha = -0.128"; Delta delta = 0.283")  
  > "The average offset was $\sim$ 0.300 $\pm$ 0.008" ($\Delta \alpha = -0.128"$; $\Delta \delta = 0.283"$) [^3]." (HST Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract spatially integrated [CII] spectrum and measure line properties**: Iterative aperture and masking procedure following Bethermin et al. (2020): place beam-area aperture at source center, extract spectrum, fit Gaussian to get FWHM, create moment-0 map over FWHM, mask cube at >3 sigma, and repeat until spectrum converges.  
  > "This is an iterative process where a small aperture equal to the area of the beam width is centred on the main source of emission ($\alpha$ = 09:59:30.459, $\delta$ = 2.08.02.586); using this aperture, an integrated spectrum is extracted and a Gaussian profile can be fitted to the emission; the full width at half maximum (FWHM) from this fit is used to create a simple moment-0 map which in turn provides a mask—by taking all emission greater than 3$\sigma$—and then masks the entire cube." (Spatially integrated emission line); "This masked cube is used to extract another integrated spectrum, and the process is repeated, until the extracted integrated spectrum remains unchanged." (Spatially integrated emission line)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Initial aperture center: alpha = 09:59:30.459, delta = 2.08.02.586; area equal to beam width  
  > "This is an iterative process where a small aperture equal to the area of the beam width is centred on the main source of emission ($\alpha$ = 09:59:30.459, $\delta$ = 2.08.02.586);" (Spatially integrated emission line)
   - parameter: 3 sigma moment-0 threshold mask  
  > "the full width at half maximum (FWHM) from this fit is used to create a simple moment-0 map which in turn provides a mask—by taking all emission greater than 3$\sigma$—and then masks the entire cube." (Spatially integrated emission line)
   - assumption: Vanilla LambdaCDM cosmology with Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1  
  > "Throughout this paper we adopt a vanilla $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $\Omega_{m} = 0.3$, $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.7$, and $H_0 = 70~\rm km~\rm s^{-1}~\rm Mpc^{-1}$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: 1D Gaussian profile fit to integrated spectrum using CASA viewer and associated fit tools  
  > "Gaussian fitting of the spectral and spatial data was performed using the Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA) software viewer and associated fit tools (cite: CASAPAPER)." (ALMA Observations)
   - validation: Compared FWHM, integrated flux density, and [CII] luminosity with previous measurements by Capak et al. (2015).  
  > "The shape and integrated flux of the spectrum is similar with that obtained by (cite: Capak2015) with a FWHM of $298 \pm 27$ km s$^{-1}$ and an integrated flux density ($S_{\rm [CII]}$) of $0.86~\pm~0.11$ Jy km s$^{-1}$ (as compared to $380 \pm 42$ km s$^{-1}$ and 0.71 $\pm$ 0.07 Jy km s$^{-1}$)." (Spatially integrated emission line)
4. **Generate [CII] moment-0, velocity field (moment-1), and velocity dispersion (moment-2) maps**: Collapse channels within the fitted [CII] FWHM (18 channels on either side of central channel, 540 km s^-1 range) to form moment-0 map; mask velocity field and dispersion maps using >3 sigma region of the moment-0 map.  
  > "Specifically, we chose all channels within the FWHM, which resulted in 18 channels on either side of the central emission channel—a velocity range of 540 km s$^{-1}$." (Spatial and velocity distribution of [CII]); "We used the integrated [CII] emission to build masks for the velocity field and dispersion map, by taking all emission greater than 3$\sigma$ within the moment-0 map." (Spatial and velocity distribution of [CII])
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 18 channels on either side of central emission channel (velocity range of 540 km s^-1)  
  > "Specifically, we chose all channels within the FWHM, which resulted in 18 channels on either side of the central emission channel—a velocity range of 540 km s$^{-1}$." (Spatial and velocity distribution of [CII])
   - parameter: 3 sigma moment-0 mask for moment-1 and moment-2 maps  
  > "We used the integrated [CII] emission to build masks for the velocity field and dispersion map, by taking all emission greater than 3$\sigma$ within the moment-0 map." (Spatial and velocity distribution of [CII])
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Extract surface brightness radial profiles and fit Sersic models to [CII] and UV maps**: Convolve HST images with the ALMA beam. Determine emission peaks via independent 1D Gaussian fits to RA- and Dec-summed profiles. Place concentric annuli in 0.1" bins around the peak, compute area-normalized flux and uncertainties (Eqs. 1 and 2), and fit Sersic profiles (both free n and fixed n=1) using emcee.  
  > "A radial profile was generated by placing a series of increasingly larger annuli, in bins of 0.1", around the peak [CII] emission of the moment-0 map. This peak was determined by summing up the emission along the RA and Dec axis independently and then determining the central RA and Dec values which were calculated from the resulting two, independent, 1-D Gaussian fits." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV); "A Sérsic profile was adopted in order to fit the radial profiles using a Monte-Carlo Markov-chain fitting routine, with the python package `emcee` (cite: emcee)." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV); "To appropriately compare morphologies between the HST and ALMA data, the high resolution HST data was convolved with the ALMA beam." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
   - software: CASA, emcee
   - parameter: Annulus bin width = 0.1"  
  > "A radial profile was generated by placing a series of increasingly larger annuli, in bins of 0.1", around the peak [CII] emission of the moment-0 map." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
   - parameter: Sersic index n free, and fixed n = 1.0  
  > "To compare all the fitted profiles in the same scale, and to be comparable to what is usually adopted in the literature, we also used a fixed Sérsic index of 1.0." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
   - assumption: Independent centering of HST and [CII] profiles removes residual systematic astrometric offsets  
  > "The centers of the individual HST filter images were determined in the same manner as the [CII] profile (explained earlier); therefore, any residual systematic offset between the [CII] and rest-frame UV emission do not affect the final results." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
   - fitting: Monte-Carlo Markov-chain (MCMC) fitting of Sersic profiles using emcee  
  > "A Sérsic profile was adopted in order to fit the radial profiles using a Monte-Carlo Markov-chain fitting routine, with the python package `emcee` (cite: emcee)." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
   - validation: Qualitative comparison with 2D surface brightness distributions and testing both free Sersic index and fixed n=1.  
  > "We once again see that the the [CII] emission is far more extended than that of the rest-frame UV emission. This is qualitative confirmation of this extension found for the radial profiles." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
6. **Test [CII] halo detection criteria and search for outflow signatures in multi-aperture spectra**: Apply Fujimoto et al. (2020) and Herrera-Camus et al. (2021) >4 sigma halo tests. Place seven small circular apertures (radii 0.05" and 0.02") around the source and inspect [CII] line profiles for velocity offsets or broad secondary components.  
  > "(cite: Fujimoto2020) provides a test to determine if a system has a [CII] halo: masking out a beam-sized area of central [CII] emission, and all emission greater than 10 kpc, which results in an annulus of [CII]; if the remaining emission is greater than 4$\sigma$, then the system can be considered to have a [CII] halo." ([CII] Halo); "Therefore, (cite: Herrera-Camus2021) suggest a more physical, but similar, definition: measuring if the extended [CII] emission—the emission which extends further than the rest-frame UV emission—is greater than $4\sigma$. In both definitions, HZ7 is considered a system with a [CII] halo." ([CII] Halo); "This involved placing seven small apertures (once with radii of 0.05" and once with 0.02") around the source" (Physical Interpretations)
   - parameter: Halo detection threshold: > 4 sigma outside central beam (up to 10 kpc) or beyond rest-frame UV emission  
  > "if the remaining emission is greater than 4$\sigma$, then the system can be considered to have a [CII] halo." ([CII] Halo); "measuring if the extended [CII] emission—the emission which extends further than the rest-frame UV emission—is greater than $4\sigma$." ([CII] Halo)
   - parameter: Seven circular apertures with radii of 0.05" and 0.02"  
  > "This involved placing seven small apertures (once with radii of 0.05" and once with 0.02") around the source (Fig. 5)." (Physical Interpretations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- HZ7 hosts an extended [CII] halo whose effective radius is 2 to 3 times larger than its rest-frame UV effective radius, passing both the Fujimoto et al. (2020) and Herrera-Camus et al. (2021) >4 sigma halo definitions.  
  > "The comparison between the [CII] and rest-frame UV radial profiles show that the effective radius of the [CII] emission is 2 - 3 times larger than that of the UV emission." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV); "In both definitions, HZ7 is considered a system with a [CII] halo." ([CII] Halo)
- The [CII] velocity field (moment-1) and velocity dispersion map (moment-2) show disordered kinematics with no velocity gradient, indicating that HZ7 is not a simple rotating disk and may be a merging system.  
  > "Both higher-order moment maps are disordered with the velocity field in the middle panel of Figure 2 showing no indication of a gradient and therefore no clear indication of a rotating disk." (Spatial and velocity distribution of [CII]); "HZ7 is unequivocally not a simple rotating disk galaxy and this has been shown by the [CII] velocity field and the [CII] dispersion map (middle and bottom panels of Figure 2)." (Summary and Conclusions)
- No spectral evidence of outflows (velocity offsets or broad secondary line components) is detected in small apertures across HZ7 at the sensitivity of the observations.  
  > "However, we found that there was no evidence for an outflow, which would be identified as an offset or wider secondary line components in the spectra, at the sensitivity of our observations." (Physical Interpretations)
- The dust continuum emission is clumpy and features an eastern component overlapping [CII] without UV emission, as well as a southern extension extending beyond both the UV and [CII] emission.  
  > "The dust continuum emission appears to be more extended than the rest-frame UV and contains a southern extension which surpasses even the [CII] emission." (Spatial distribution of [CII], rest-frame UV, and dust continuum)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HZ7 is one of the brightest normal main-sequence (near L*) galaxies in the Capak et al. (2015) z~5 sample with good multiwavelength coverage, and 0.3" ALMA observations match HST angular resolution to directly resolve [CII] vs UV morphology without stacking.  
  > "In this paper we study the observations of HZ7 in particular, one of the brightest objects in the (cite: Capak2015) sample, with a rest-frame UV spectroscopic redshift of z = 5.2532±0.0004 (cite: Capak2015)." (Introduction); "Here we present high signal-to-noise ALMA [CII] follow-up observations of HZ7 at an angular resolution comparable to that of HST and JWST." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Concatenating compact-configuration data increases sensitivity to extended emission, JvM correction fixes multi-configuration CLEAN flux scaling, HST convolution matches the ALMA PSF, and independent profile centering removes residual astrometric offsets.  
  > "The previous data was taken in a more compact configuration providing extra sensitivity to extended emission." (ALMA Observations); "Since the residual map which results from the CLEAN algorithm can have incorrect default flux scaling when combining different array configurations (cite: JVM1995), we apply a correction as suggested by (cite: JvMModern2021)." (ALMA Observations); "To appropriately compare morphologies between the HST and ALMA data, the high resolution HST data was convolved with the ALMA beam." ([CII] compared to rest-frame UV)
- Alternative: Outflows as the origin of the [CII] halo: tested via multi-aperture spectra but no velocity offsets or secondary broad components were found at current sensitivity.  
  > "However, we found that there was no evidence for an outflow, which would be identified as an offset or wider secondary line components in the spectra, at the sensitivity of our observations." (Physical Interpretations)
- Alternative: Central dust obscuration suppressing UV emission to mimic a [CII] halo: considered unlikely because HZ7 would have to be dust-poor in the center and dust-rich in the outskirts, contrary to observations and theory.  
  > "But based on these results of HZ7, the galaxy would be dust poor towards the center, which seems unlikely. There would also be an expectation for the [CII] and dust emission to be aligned, and dust emission towards the outskirts of the system. Both of these criteria are not observed in this data." (Physical Interpretations)
- Alternative: Bright in-falling satellite clumps producing the [CII] halo: no bright clumps are seen in the diffuse halo at 0.3" (~2 kpc) resolution, so any clumps would have to be numerous and much smaller than 2 kpc.  
  > "However, we only find an extended diffuse emission, with no signs of bright clumps, at the resolution of our observations (0.3" $\sim$ 2 kpc). This implies that if indeed the extended emission is produced by clumps, they would have to be numerous and significantly smaller than 2 kpc." (Physical Interpretations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): It is not possible to determine the exact physical process behind the observed [CII] halo from these ALMA observations alone.  
  > "In the case of HZ7, it is not possible to determine the exact physical process or processes behind the observed [CII] halo from these ALMA observations alone." (Physical Interpretations)
- Limitation (authors): Current sensitivity/SNR of the observations may be insufficient to detect faint outflow components or to draw firm conclusions from the dust continuum morphology.  
  > "This could imply that either deeper, or higher signal-to-noise, observations might be necessary in order to detect evidence of outflows." (Physical Interpretations); "It is entirely possible that the continuum data does not have a high enough SNR in order to make any firm deductions. Higher S/N observations of the continuum would be needed in order to fully investigate its contribution" (Physical Interpretations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1D radial profile fitting uses circular annuli rather than elliptical annuli despite the elongated NE morphology of the [CII] emission.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The uncertainty formula for the HST annular flux, u(F) = sqrt(n*sigma^2 + F), sums background variance and source flux in raw units without explicitly stating the gain/exposure conversion to electron counts or accounting for correlated noise after convolving the HST image with the ALMA beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 4.2.1, the lower limit on log(L_[CII]/L_FIR) is written as >1.5 based on log(L_[CII]/L_sun) = 8.8 and log(L_FIR/L_sun) < 10.35, which is actually log(L_FIR/L_[CII]) < 1.55 (or log(L_[CII]/L_FIR) > -1.55).
