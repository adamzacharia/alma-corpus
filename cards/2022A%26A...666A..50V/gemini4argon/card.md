## Analysis recipe

1. **Quantum-chemical calculations of vinyl isocyanate**: Coupled-cluster CCSD/cc-pVTZ harmonic and anharmonic force field calculations  
  > "We used coupled-cluster approximation on the level of coupled-cluster-single-double (CCSD) model (cite: ccsd) as it is implemented in CFOUR program package (cite: cfour), in conjunction with Dunning’s correlation consistent triple-$\zeta$ (cc-pVTZ) basis set (cite: Dunning1989)." (Quantum-chemical calculations)
   - software: CFOUR
   - parameter: convergence criteria set to 10^-8 atomic units  
  > "The convergence criteria for the HF-SCF equations, the CC amplitude equations and the linear equations were set to 10$^{-8}$ atomic units." (Quantum-chemical calculations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with experimental rotational and centrifugal distortion constants  
  > "Furthermore, the physical meaning of those newly determined quantities is corroborated by the agreement between the fitted values and their quantum-chemical counterparts in Table [\[constants\]](#constants)." (Laboratory spectroscopy of vinyl isocyanate)
2. **Laboratory rotational spectrum assignment and Hamiltonian fitting**: Loomis-Wood-type plot assignment and fitting to Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian in I^r-representation  
  > "The predicted transitions were searched for with the help of the Loomis-Wood-type plot technique (cite: LW) implemented in our own program (cite: Vavra2020)." (Trans isomer); "analyzed using Watson’s $A$-reduced Hamiltonian in I$^{\text{r}}$-representation (cite: Watson1977) with terms up to the sixth power in the angular momentum." (Trans isomer)
   - software: MATLAB, SPFIT/SPCAT
   - parameter: trans isomer: J_min/J_max = 4/80, K_a^min/K_a^max = 0/7, N = 464 lines  
  > "$J_{\text{min}}/J_{\text{max}}$ | 4 / 80 | 4 / 19 | ... | | 1 / 61 | 4 / 6 | ..." (Table 1); "$N$$^{e}$ | 464 | 30 | ... | | 608 | 18 | ..." (Table 1)
   - parameter: cis isomer: J_min/J_max = 1/61, K_a^min/K_a^max = 0/21, N = 608 lines  
  > "It was possible to identify and iteratively fit the rotational transitions up to $J=61$ and $K_a=21$." (Cis isomer)
   - assumption: For trans isomer, Phi_K = -471.8 Hz and phi_K = 21.58 Hz fixed to quantum-chemical values  
  > "The only exceptions were $\Phi_K$ and $\phi_K$ which were fixed at the values estimated from quantum-chemical calculations." (Trans isomer)
   - fitting: Voigt profile line frequency determination and least-squares fit in SPFIT  
  > "precise determination of the line frequencies employing the Voight profile function, and saving the data in a line list among other features. It generates input files for the SPFIT/SPCAT program package (cite: Pickett1991)" (Trans isomer)
   - validation: Checked consistency with Watson's S-reduced Hamiltonian and previous microwave transitions from Kirby & Kroto (1978)  
  > "We note that all transitions from (cite: Kirby1978) were found to be fully consistent with our measurements and could be perfectly fitted." (Trans isomer); "Watson’s $S$-reduced Hamiltonian led to results of similar quality." (Trans isomer)
3. **Compute rotational and vibrational partition functions and line catalogs**: Numerical summation over ground state energy levels in SPCAT and harmonic/anharmonic vibrational mode summation  
  > "We used the SPCAT program (cite: Pickett1991) to undertake the numerical summation over the ground state energy levels up to $J=240$ and $K_{a}=46$ for the *trans* isomer and $J=210$ and $K_{a}=72$ for the *cis* isomer." (Partition functions); "The vibrational partition functions ($Q_{\text{vib}}$) were estimated using Eq. 3.60 of (cite: Gordy1970) by taking into account the anharmonic frequencies of the eighteen normal vibrational modes" (Partition functions)
   - software: SPFIT/SPCAT
   - parameter: J up to 240, K_a up to 46 (trans); J up to 210, K_a up to 72 (cis)  
  > "up to $J=240$ and $K_{a}=46$ for the *trans* isomer and $J=210$ and $K_{a}=72$ for the *cis* isomer." (Partition functions)
   - assumption: Energy difference between cis and trans isomers Delta E_ZPE = 301 cm^-1  
  > "We used $\Delta E_{\text{ZPE}}=301$ cm$^{-1}$ from our quantum-chemical calculations." (Partition functions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified that Delta E_ZPE = 301 cm^-1 reproduces experimental relative intensities of cis and trans lines  
  > "This value reproduces quite well the experimental intensities of the *cis* isomer lines with respect to neighboring lines of the *trans* isomer in our spectrum." (Partition functions)

  > "We assumed local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and produced synthetic spectra with the astronomical software Weeds (cite: Maret11) in order to analyze the observed spectrum." (Observations); "For an undetected species, the first four parameters are fixed to values obtained for a related species and the column density is varied until discrepancies at the $\sim$3$\sigma$ level appear between the synthetic and observed spectra."   > "C$_2$H$_5$NCO, $\varv=0$ | n | 0 | 2.0 | 200 | $<$ 8.1 (16) | 10.1 | 5.0 | 0.0 | $>$ 3.1" (Table 3); "*trans*-C$_2$H$_3$NCO, $\varv=0$ | n | 0 | 2.0 | 200 | $<$ 2.4 (16) | 4.00 | 5.0 | 0.0 | $>$ 11" (Table 3)

## Qualitative findings

- Neither trans nor cis vinyl isocyanate (C2H3NCO) nor ethyl isocyanate (C2H5NCO) is detected toward Sgr B2(N1S) in the ALMA ReMoCA survey.  
  > "We report nondetections of vinyl and ethyl isocyanate toward the main hot core of Sgr B2(N)." (abstract)
- The observational upper limit on CH3NCO:C2H5NCO is consistent with ethyl isocyanate forming on dust grains via H-abstraction or photodissociation of methyl isocyanate followed by methyl radical addition, while the lack of an analogous pathway for vinyl isocyanate implies C2H3NCO:C2H5NCO < 1.  
  > "our observational upper limit for the CH$_3$NCO:C$_2$H$_5$NCO ratio in Sgr B2(N) is consistent with ethyl isocyanate being formed on dust grains via the abstraction or photodissociation of an H atom from methyl isocyanate, followed by the addition of a methyl radical." (abstract); "The dominance of such a process for ethyl isocyanate production, combined with the absence of an analogous mechanism for vinyl isocyanate, would indicate that the ratio C$_2$H$_3$NCO:C$_2$H$_5$NCO should be rather less than unity." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sgr B2(N) reveals a rich collection of complex organic molecules including isocyanates, and the position Sgr B2(N1S) offset by ~1'' south of the main hot core has a lower continuum opacity than the peak.  
  > "We target the high-mass star-forming region Sgr B2(N) which reveals an astonishingly rich collection of molecular species, including isocyanates." (Introduction); "This position is offset by about 1$\ensuremath{''}$ to the south of the main hot core Sgr B2(N1) and has a lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core." (Observations)
- Why this method: The LTE assumption in Weeds is justified by the high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N).  
  > "The LTE assumption is justified by the high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (cite: Bonfand19)." (Observations)
- Alternative: Experimental value of cis-trans energy difference Delta E_ZPE = 417(20) cm^-1 from Kirby & Kroto (1978) was rejected in favor of the quantum-chemical value 301 cm^-1 because 417 cm^-1 underestimates the relative intensities of cis isomer lines.  
  > "On the other hand, the experimental value 417(20) cm$^{-1}$ from (cite: Kirby1978) underestimates these *cis* isomer lines relative intensities. For this reason we consider our calculated value for the energy difference as more reliable." (Partition functions)
