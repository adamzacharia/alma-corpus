## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure 2 mm continuum flux densities and noise from ALMA images**: Extract peak flux density and rms from primary-beam-corrected images within the SCUBA-2 beam (7.5 arcsec radius) using CASA imstat, adopting a 3 sigma detection threshold; sum components for resolved pair SSA.0007  
  > "We derive flux density and noise measurements from primary-beam-corrected images using the `CASA` task `imstat`." (ALMA Observations); "On these scales ($\sim12 \times 14.5$\,kpc at $z = 2$), it is expected that all detected sources are unresolved, therefore we adopt the peak flux density from `CASA imstat` within the SCUBA-2 beam (7.5 arcsec radius) as the measured 2\,mm flux density of the source." (ALMA Observations); "Given that all of our targets had prior SCUBA-2 detections, we invoked a $3\sigma$ detection threshold for our ALMA observations." (ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA, imstat, viewer
   - parameter: search radius = 7.5 arcsec (SCUBA-2 beam); detection threshold = 3 sigma  
  > "therefore we adopt the peak flux density from `CASA imstat` within the SCUBA-2 beam (7.5 arcsec radius) as the measured 2\,mm flux density of the source." (ALMA Observations); "Given that all of our targets had prior SCUBA-2 detections, we invoked a $3\sigma$ detection threshold for our ALMA observations." (ALMA Observations)
   - assumption: Detected sources are unresolved at 1.7'' x 1.4'' resolution (except SSA.0007 which is resolved into two physically associated components)  
  > "On these scales ($\sim12 \times 14.5$\,kpc at $z = 2$), it is expected that all detected sources are unresolved, therefore we adopt the peak flux density from `CASA imstat` within the SCUBA-2 beam (7.5 arcsec radius) as the measured 2\,mm flux density of the source." (ALMA Observations); "Therefore, we adopt the sum of the 2\,mm flux densities for SSA.0007.1 and SSA.0007.2 reported in Table [\[tab:flux\]](#tab:flux) for our SED characterization described in Section §3." (Redshifts from the Literature)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared peak flux density from CASA imstat to extended aperture flux measurements using CASA viewer and found good agreement  
  > "We confirmed the robustness of this method by comparing to measurements of the source flux using `CASA` `viewer` across an extended aperture, and found good agreement between the two measurement methods." (ALMA Observations)
2. **Crossmatch and extract multiwavelength ancillary photometry and literature redshifts**: Crossmatch ALMA 2 mm positions (or S2CLS positions for <3 sigma sources) to literature redshifts, X-ray catalog, AzTEC 1.1 mm catalog/map, and Herschel/SPIRE 250, 350, 500 um maps/catalogs; combine statistical and absolute calibration uncertainties in quadrature  
  > "As noted in Section §2.4, for sources detected at $>3\sigma$ in 2\,mm, positions derived from 2\,mm measurements are adopted, otherwise we take the S2CLS positions." (Ancillary Data); "For our photometric redshift and SED fitting, we add absolute flux scale calibration uncertainty in quadrature with the statistical photometric uncertainties, taking that calibration error to be 4% for *Herschel* (cite: 2013bendo), 5% for SCUBA-2 (cite: 2020simpson), 10% for AzTEC (cite: 2008wilson), and 5% for ALMA (cite: 2021braatz)." (Ancillary Data)

## Qualitative findings

- A loose positive correlation exists between 2 mm flux density and redshift, and sources with lower S_850um / S_2mm flux ratios tend to lie at higher redshifts.  
  > "We find a positive correlation between 2\,mm flux density and redshift, and sources with lower flux ratios $S_{850\rm\,\mu m}/S_{\rm2\,mm}$ tend to have higher redshifts (see Figure [5](#fig:z_2mm) and Figure [6](#fig:btrax))." (Redshift Distribution of $S_{850\rm\,\mu m} > 5.55\,$mJy Sources)
- 850 um and 2 mm photometry alone are insufficient to constrain redshifts without 250-500 um data to bracket the dust SED peak.  
  > "(cite: mmpz) notes, and we find using the same process described therein, that using only 850$\,\rm{\mu m}$ and 2\,mm photometry as in (b) – two points that should exclusively probe the Rayleigh-Jeans tail for most redshifts and temperature – is insufficient." (Utility of 2 mm as a Redshift Filter)
- Adding 2 mm photometry improves redshift accuracy when Herschel data exist, but does not significantly change the precision (PDF breadth remains delta_z = 1.1 - 1.2).  
  > "While 2\,mm data does improve the accuracy of redshift constraints, they do not significantly change the precision of those redshift solutions." (Utility of 2 mm as a Redshift Filter); "Quantitatively, the average breadth of redshift PDFs for our sample is $\delta z = 1.1 - 1.2$ with or without the 2\,mm data folded into the photometry." (Utility of 2 mm as a Redshift Filter)
- The bright S_850um > 5.55 mJy DSFG population in SSA22 is consistent with the cosmic average field density and is not significantly biased by the z = 3.09 protocluster.  
  > "We find that this sample of DSFGs in SSA22 is representative of the cosmic average density, within uncertainties, and not impacted by the known protocluster structure in the field." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected brightest (S_850um > 5 mJy, >5 sigma) S2CLS DSFGs because brighter DSFGs are expected to have higher average redshifts; chose ALMA Band 4 (2 mm) because it filters for high-z sources and is ~4-5x brighter than 3 mm, requiring <1 min on-source per target vs >100 hours for ALMA spectral scans.  
  > "We selected the brightest 850$\,\rm{\mu m}$-selected DSFGs for 2\,mm follow up due to the observation that brighter DSFGs have a higher average redshift than galaxies at fainter submillimeter flux densities (cite: 2015bethermin; casey2018b; casey2018a)." (Sample of DSFGs); "For example, following up these sources with spectral scans in the millimeter would take over 100 hours with ALMA (at least $\sim 3$ hours per source with overheads), versus the total time spent in this paper, with a total on-source time of just 34.12\,min for 39 sources ($<1$\,min per source)." (Utility of 2 mm as a Redshift Filter); "Note that while similar long wavelength observations (for example at 3\,mm) are also informative, we opt for 2\,mm observations since sources are $\sim4-5\times$ brighter at 2\,mm and therefore 2\,mm follow-up is more observationally efficient." (Utility of 2 mm as a Redshift Filter)
- Why this method: Used natural weighting (robust = 2) and peak flux from CASA imstat because sources are unresolved at ~1.5'' resolution; invoked a 3 sigma threshold because targets have prior SCUBA-2 detections; used MMpz because it accounts for intrinsic SED breadth and temperature-redshift degeneracy when spectroscopy/OIR photo-z are absent.  
  > "We adopted robust $=2$ natural weighting to optimize imaging depth given the sources were unresolved at this resolution." (ALMA Observations); "While a $3\sigma$ detection threshold is below the nominal $5\sigma$ threshold for blank field detection, other works have demonstrated that prior-based measurements are far less burdened by contaminants (cite: 2013hodge; 2017dunlop)." (ALMA Observations); "The technique accounts for instrinsic SED breadth as it probes a wide range of dust temperatures at fixed IR luminosity." (Photometric Redshifts)
- Alternative: Using MIPS 24 um photometry directly in SED fitting was rejected because depth varies across the field, coverage is incomplete, and at z ~ 2-6 it probes rest-frame mid-IR PAH emission and silicon absorption rather than FIR dust emission.  
  > "Note that we do not use the MIPS 24$\,\rm{\mu m}$ data directly in our analysis because the depth varies significantly across the field and the coverage is incomplete." (Ancillary Data); "Furthermore, at the expected redshifts of our sources ($z \approx 2 - 6$), observed 24$\,\rm{\mu m}$ does not probe rest-frame FIR emission, but instead measures the mid-IR, rich with complex Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon (PAH) emission and silicon absorption that we are not attempting to constrain in this work." (Ancillary Data)
- Alternative: Correcting for gravitational lensing by the foreground z = 3.09 SSA22 protocluster was rejected because the protocluster is unvirialized and diffuse (~10 Mpc), causing only weak lensing consistent with any extragalactic sightline.  
  > "Given that the magnitude of lensing caused by this foreground overdensity is likely small and consistent with what is likely present in any extragalactic field, we do not attempt to correct for it here." (The SSA22 Field)
- Alternative: Literature OIR photometric redshifts for SSA.0028 (z = 0) and SSA.0006 (z = 6) were excluded from comparison/fitting because z = 0 is inconsistent with FIR and SDSS imaging and z = 6 is precluded by a 24 um detection.  
  > "Note that our comparison to OIR photo-$z$’s excludes SSA.0028, which has a reported OIR photo-$z$ of $z = 0$, inconsistent with both our FIR results and publicly available shallow SDSS optical imaging, which shows no obvious local universe source within 12” (cite: sdss_dr16)." (Photometric Redshifts); "Additionally, we exclude source SSA.0006 which has an OIR photo-$z$ of $z = 6$; its detection at 24$\,\rm{\mu m}$ likely precludes such a high redshift solution." (Photometric Redshifts)
- Alternative: Using luminosity-weighted T_dust from SED fitting to compute gas masses was rejected in favor of a single mass-weighted T_dust = 25 K because hot dust dominates L_IR but represents a very small fraction of total dust mass.  
  > "We adopt a single mass-weighted dust temperature of 25\,K (cite: 2016scoville; 2019casey) for this calculation rather than our SED measurements of $T_{\rm dust}$, which are luminosity-weighted." (IR SED Characterization)
- How they decided it was enough: Achieved median rms of 0.08 mJy beam^-1 (better than requested 0.1 mJy) detecting 35/39 sources; SSA22 subsample of 39 targets (~9% of full S2CLS bright sample) is large enough to generalize conclusions while COSMOS and UDS targets await ALMA Cycle 8 observations.  
  > "Though the SSA22 subsample constitutes $\sim 9\%$ of the full sample to be observed, the subsample is sufficiently large to generalize conclusions on the nature of the brightest $\gtrsim5$\,mJy DSFGs." (Sample of DSFGs); "The median rms achieved was $0.08\,$mJy\,beam$^{-1}$ (range $[0.07 - 0.11]$\,mJy\,beam$^{-1}$), better than the requested rms of 0.1\,mJy." (ALMA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Extremely obscured AGN could heat dust and cannot be ruled out without follow-up data (e.g., JWST).  
  > "This suggests the sample as a whole is not dominated by X-ray bright AGN (extremely obscured AGN could produce hotter dust temperatures and cannot be ruled out without follow-up data from e.g. *JWST*), and that the presence of AGN is unlikely to impact the millimeter photometric analysis presented later in this paper as both SSA.0001 and SSA.0007 have secure spectroscopic redshifts." (Ancillary Data)
- Limitation (authors): Fixing redshift to the best available photo-z solution during IR SED fitting introduces degeneracy and may lead to underestimated parameter uncertainties.  
  > "We then fix the best available redshift solution to infer basic characteristics about the galaxies’ SEDs. Note this degeneracy limits the scope of our conclusions as it may lead to underestimated uncertainties." (IR SED Characterization)
- Limitation (authors): Combining interferometric ALMA 2 mm data with single-dish SCUBA-2 850 um data (which may suffer from residual confusion boosting or source multiplicity) limits the precision of individual beta measurements and could bias beta high.  
  > "The use of ALMA data with single-dish SCUBA-2 data, possibly suffering from confusion boosting, could impact individual derivations of $\beta$ in this sample." (IR SED Characterization); "It is possible the bias from single dish data could be responsible for the difference between a $\beta \approx 2$ result and our higher $\beta \approx 2.4$ measurement." (Emissivity Spectral Index)
- Limitation (authors): Physical interpretation of galaxy-integrated beta is not possible for spatially unresolved high-z galaxies due to ISM heterogeneity and limited photometry/redshift constraints.  
  > "Still, drawing a physical interpretation for a measured $\beta$ is not possible for spatially unresolved high redshift galaxies due to the complexity and heterogenity of the ISM." (Emissivity Spectral Index)
- Limitation (authors): The positive correlation between 2 mm flux density and redshift is partially circular because 2 mm photometry is used as an input to MMpz for sources lacking independent redshifts, and spectroscopic follow-up is needed to definitively verify 2 mm redshift filtering.  
  > "We caution that this observation is partially due to the use of 2\,mm flux densities in measuring redshifts for sources lacking other independent redshift constraints; however, it should be noted that removing the 2\,mm flux densities from `MMpz` redshift determinations still results in a trend between redshift and 2\,mm flux density, though it introduces more scatter." (Redshift Distribution of $S_{850\rm\,\mu m} > 5.55\,$mJy Sources); "While we do find that sources with higher 2\,mm flux densities tend to sit at higher redshifts, spectroscopic follow up is needed to verify the efficacy of the 2\,mm redshift filtering technique." (Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-dish SCUBA-2 850 um flux densities are combined with interferometric ALMA 2 mm peak fluxes; any unflagged multiplicity (beyond SSA.0007) or under-deboosted 850 um flux will artificially steepen the 850 um / 2 mm ratio and bias beta upward.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Redshift and SED parameters are fit sequentially (first MMpz with fixed beta = 1.8 to get z, then fixing z to fit beta and T_dust) rather than jointly marginalizing over redshift uncertainty, which underestimates error bars on L_IR, T_dust, and beta.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The extracted Herschel/SPIRE (250, 350, 500 um) and AzTEC (1.1 mm) flux densities for the 39 sources are plotted in Figure 4 but not tabulated in the paper, hindering independent SED re-fitting.

  > "The authors also thank Mark Swinbank for providing SPIRE imaging, Hideki Umehata for sharing AzTEC 1.1\,mm imaging and catalog data, and Claudia Lagos for providing SHARK model lightcone results." (Acknowledgements); "This paper makes use of the following ALMA data: ADS/JAO.ALMA#2019.1.00313.S." (Acknowledgements)

- Missing: The Bayesian MCMC SED fitting software (Drew & Casey, submitted) and the exact prior distributions used for beta, T_dust, and L_IR
- Missing: Detailed CASA tclean imaging parameters (cell size, image size, stopping criteria) for the ALMA Band 4 continuum maps
- Data volume (inferred): Small (~5-15 GB for 39 short <1 min Band 4 continuum pointings on the 12-m array)
- Score (inferred): 68/5, The ALMA 2 mm data reduction and peak flux measurement recipe (CASA 5.6.0, robust=2, PB correction, imstat within 7.5'' radius) is simple and fully reproducible, and Table 1 provides all 850 um and 2 mm fluxes. However, reproducing the full 6-band MMpz and Bayesian SED fits is blocked by the absence of a published table for the extracted Herschel/SPIRE and AzTEC fluxes (some extracted from privately shared maps) and reliance on an unpublished SED fitting code (Drew & Casey, submitted).
- Rediscovery candidate: yes, The ALMA project 2019.1.00313.S is public, the 39 continuum pointings are small and fast to image, and all 39 ALMA 2 mm flux densities, uncertainties, and astrometric offsets are explicitly tabulated in Table 1 for direct numerical verification.
