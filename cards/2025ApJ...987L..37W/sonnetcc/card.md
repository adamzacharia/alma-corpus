## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the ALMA Band 8 data into [C II] cubes and continuum maps**: tclean with Briggs weighting; [C II] cubes from two combined spectral windows at 14 km/s per channel; continuum maps from single spectral windows  
  > "We cleaned the ALMA data via the task `tclean` using the Common Astronomy Software Applications for Radio Astronomy v6.6.0" (Sample and data); "The final \[C II\] cubes, constructed with two spectral windows combined, have $\Delta v=14\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ per channel." (Sample and data)
   - software: CASA v6.6.0, tclean
   - parameter: robust = +0.5 (1.7 h fields) and +2.0 (0.8 h fields)  
  > "Briggs weighting of robust$=+0.5$ for the two targets with 1.7 hours of exposure each (TN J0121+1320 and 4C+19.71) and robust$=+2.0$ for the other two with 0.8 hours of exposure each (TN J0205+2242 and 4C+03.24)" (Sample and data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Select the [C II] companions**: Visual check over the Band 8 primary beam and spectral bandwidth, then S/N >~ 3 over 125 km/s and spectral validation (summary of the companion-paper selection)  
  > "The \[C II\] companions are selected first based on visual check in the field of ALMA Band 8 primary beam ($\sim10\ensuremath{''}\times10\ensuremath{''}$) over the spectral bandwidth of $\sim5300\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$." (Analysis); "The companions are then checked to have a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $\gtrsim3$ collapsed over $125\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ and are validated spectrally." (Analysis)
   - parameter: S/N >~ 3 collapsed over 125 km/s  
  > "The companions are then checked to have a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $\gtrsim3$ collapsed over $125\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ and are validated spectrally." (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spectral validation of each companion  
  > "The companions are then checked to have a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $\gtrsim3$ collapsed over $125\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ and are validated spectrally." (Analysis)
3. **Extract and fit the [C II] spectra of each companion**: Custom elliptical extraction apertures drawn from the moment 0 morphology, followed by single-Gaussian line fits  
  > "To maximize the S/N of the \[C II\] detections, we customize the extraction apertures based on their morphology in the moment 0 maps (color dotted ellipses)." (Analysis); "We then perform single-Gaussian fitting to \[C II\] line." (Analysis)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per companion; velocity shift, width and intensity fitted  
  > "We then perform single-Gaussian fitting to \[C II\] line." (Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate [C II] upper limits at the radio core (AGN host)**: Assume a 5 kpc disk and take the 3 sigma intensity upper limit over [-125, 125] km/s (shifted by the [O III] offset for 4C+19.71)  
  > "we estimate the \[C II\] upper limits at the radio core by assuming a disk size of 5 kpc (cite: Venemans_2020)" (Analysis); "we take the $3\sigma$ \[C II\] intensity upper limit from a velocity range of \[$-125$, 125\]$\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$" (Analysis)
   - parameter: Velocity window equals the median FWHM of all fitted [C II] lines  
  > "This range equals to the median full width at half maximum (FWHM) of all fitted \[C II\] lines" (Analysis)
   - assumption: Host disk size of 5 kpc  
  > "we estimate the \[C II\] upper limits at the radio core by assuming a disk size of 5 kpc (cite: Venemans_2020)" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Doubling the velocity range would raise the L_[CII] upper limit by ~30%  
  > "If we double the velocity range, i.e., two times of FWHM, the estimated $L_{\rm [\textup{C\,\textsc{\lowercase{ii}}}]}$ upper limit at radio core would be $\sim30\%$ higher." (Analysis)
5. **Re-extract the archival [C I] spectra at the [C II] companion positions**: Apertures of the Band 3 beam size at the [C II] positions, single-Gaussian fit when detected, 3 sigma upper limit over [-125, 125] km/s otherwise, with an overlap correction for blended apertures  
  > "we re-extract the \[C I\] spectra at the same position as \[C II\] companions using apertures corresponding to the Band 3 synthesized beam size" (Analysis); "We then perform single-Gaussian fitting to the \[C I\] line when detected." (Analysis); "In case of a non-detection, we calculate the $3\sigma$ \[C I\] intensity upper limit from a velocity range of \[$-125$, 125\]$\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ following the estimate of \[C II\] upper limits." (Analysis)
   - parameter: Overlap correction f = (1 - P_overlap) f_fit applied at J0121 [C II]-C and radio core  
  > "we correct \[C I\] at \[C II\]-C and radio core for overlap, $f_{\rm [\textup{C\,\textsc{\lowercase{i}}}]} = (1-P_{\rm overlap})f_{\rm [\textup{C\,\textsc{\lowercase{i}}}],fit}$, where $P_{\rm overlap}$ is the percentage of overlap" (Analysis)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian when detected  
  > "We then perform single-Gaussian fitting to the \[C I\] line when detected." (Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Convert line intensities to line luminosities**: L_line/Lsun = 1.04e-3 x I_line x D_L^2 x nu_obs (equation from the cited literature)  
  > "We compute the emission line luminosity, $L_{\rm line}$, with the equation (cite: Carilli_2013; Bethermin_2020; Falkendal_2021)" (Analysis); "where $I_{\rm line}$ is the line intensity, $D_{\rm L}$ is the luminosity distance in Mpc, and $\nu_{\rm obs, line}$ is the observed line frequency." (Analysis)
   - assumption: Flat Lambda cosmology, H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Omega_m = 0.3  
  > "we assume a flat $\Lambda$ cosmology with $H_{0} = 70\, \rm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_{m}=0.3$" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive the molecular gas mass from [C I](1-0)**: M_H2 from the [C I](1-0) luminosity following Kolwa et al., with a 1375.8/(Q10(1+z)) prefactor  
  > "we calculate $M_{\rm H_{2}}$ solely based on \[C I\](1-0) following (cite: kolwa2023)" (Analysis); "\frac{1375.8}{Q_{10}(1+z)} \times" (Analysis)
   - parameter: Q10 = 0.48  
  > "where the excitation factor $Q_{10}$ is assumed as $Q_{10}=0.48$" (Analysis)
   - parameter: A10 = 7.93e-8 s^-1  
  > "$A_{10}$ is the Einstein A-coefficient with $A_{10}=7.93\times10^{-8}\,\rm s^{-1}$" (Analysis)
   - assumption: [C I]-to-H2 abundance ratio X_[CI] = 3e-5  
  > "the \[C I\]-to-$\rm H_{2}$ ratio is assumed as $X_{[\textup{C\,\textsc{\lowercase{i}}}]}=3\times10^{-5}$" (Analysis)
   - assumption: [C I] is a good H2 tracer in the radiation fields of bright quasars, where the [C II]-H2 relation may not hold  
  > "The neutral carbon fine structure transitions are found to be a good tracer of cold molecular gas in these extreme fields" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the Zanella et al. 2018 M_H2-L_[CII] relation  
  > "Three of our detections generally follow the relation while the majority have a tendency to fall below it especially for brighter \[C II\] emitters ($>10^{9}\,L_{\odot}$)." (Discussion and conclusion)
8. **Estimate dispersion-dominated dynamical masses of the companions**: M_dyn^dis = K(n) K(q) sigma_v^2 r_e / G, with r_e from a 2D Gaussian fit to the [C II] moment 0 map  
  > "we can estimate the dynamical masses of these systems assuming that they are dispersion-dominated" (Analysis); "We perform a 2D Gaussian fit to the \[C II\] moment 0 maps using `lmfit` task in CASA and take $r_{e}=\sqrt{\rm FWHM_{x}\times FWHM_{y}}/2$" (Analysis)
   - software: CASA (lmfit task)
   - parameter: K(n) = 8.87 - 0.831n + 0.0241n^2 and K(q) = [0.87 + 0.38 exp(-3.71(1-q))]^2  
  > "$K(n)=8.87-0.831n+0.0241n^{2}$ is a function of Sérsic index, $n$, and $K(q)=[0.87+0.38e^{-3.71(1-q)}]^{2}$ is a function of axis ratio, $q$" (Analysis)
   - parameter: For J0205-[C II], r_e = 2 sqrt(theta_400GHz)  
  > "For J0205-\[C II\], we use $r_{e}=2\sqrt{\theta_{\rm 400\,GHz}}$." (Analysis)
   - assumption: q = 1 and n = 1, which gives an upper limit on M_dyn^dis  
  > "we take $q=1$ and $n=1$ in this calculation which returns the upper limit of $M_{\rm dyn}^{\rm dis}$" (Analysis)
   - assumption: Clouds are virialized and dispersion-dominated  
  > "If the \[C II\] emitting gas clouds are virialized, we can estimate the dynamical masses of these systems assuming that they are dispersion-dominated" (Analysis)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to the moment 0 map (CASA lmfit)  
  > "We perform a 2D Gaussian fit to the \[C II\] moment 0 maps using `lmfit` task in CASA and take $r_{e}=\sqrt{\rm FWHM_{x}\times FWHM_{y}}/2$" (Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Derive L_IR and SFR_IR from the Band 8 continuum**: Continuum flux extracted at the same position and aperture as [C II]; L_158um = L_IR/0.13 and SFR_IR = 2.64e-44 L_IR; 3 sigma upper limits for non-detections  
  > "We extract the continuum flux at the same position and from the same aperture as the \[C II\] emissions." (Analysis); "using $L_{\rm 158\,\mu m}=L_{\rm IR}/0.13$ and then to SFR with ${\rm SFR}_{\rm IR}=2.64\times10^{-44}L_{\rm IR} [\frac{M_{\odot}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}}{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}]$ (cite: MandD_2014)" (Analysis); "For the non-detections, we calculate $3\sigma$ Band 8 continuum upper limits." (Analysis)
   - parameter: L_IR integrated over 8-1000 um  
  > "we convert the monochromatic luminosity into the total IR luminosity (integrated in $8-1000\,\rm \mu m$)" (Analysis)
   - assumption: Contamination from hotter dust (T >~ 60 K) is negligible at the cold-dust peak  
  > "We note that we are observing at the peak frequency of cold dust emission where the contamination of hotter dust, $T\gtrsim60\,\rm K$, in HzRGs is known to be negligible" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SFR_IR consistent with SFR_SED of Falkendal et al. 2019 for TN J0121+1320, and for the two shallow fields; 4C+19.71 higher by ~2  
  > "Our SFR$_{\rm IR}$ estimate in this work is consistent with (cite: Falkendal_2019) SED fitting measurements of TN J0121+1320" (Analysis)
10. **Place companions in scaling relations and estimate zeroth-order stellar masses**: Assume gas fraction ~1 so M_star = M_dyn/2, then compare M_H2-L_[CII], L_[CII]/L_IR-L_IR and SFR-M_star with literature samples  
  > "Adopting a gas mass fraction of $\sim1$ (cite: Geach_2011; Lehnert_2016), we estimate zeroth-order stellar mass estimates, $M_{\star}=M_{\rm dyn}/2$" (Analysis)
   - assumption: No large dark matter fraction because only the inner ~3-8 kpc is probed

## Qualitative findings

- Cold gas exists around the z~3.5 radio AGN but most of it is not at the AGN position  
  > "The immediate conclusion from our \[C II\] detections is that there are cold gas systems in the surroundings of $z\approx$3.5 radio AGN, but the majority of the gas is not at the AGN position." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Companions are more gas-rich than the AGN hosts  
  > "Compared to the cold gas content at the radio core position, we find that our companions are more gas-rich than the AGN hosts." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Most companions fall below the Zanella et al. M_H2-L_[CII] relation, especially the brighter ones  
  > "Three of our detections generally follow the relation while the majority have a tendency to fall below it especially for brighter \[C II\] emitters ($>10^{9}\,L_{\odot}$)." (Discussion and conclusion)
- The sample follows the same inverse L_[CII]/L_IR-L_IR trend as comparison samples despite five of eight L_IR upper limits  
  > "Even though five out of eight of our companions only have $L_{\rm IR}$ upper limits, we find that our sample is on the same inversely correlated trend as the comparison samples" (Discussion and conclusion)
- The 4C+03.24 [C II] clouds are the only ones with a morphological connection to the jet  
  > "The \[C II\] companions around 4C+03.24 is the only example in our sample that shows a morphological connection with the jet." (Discussion and conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Higher resolution and sensitivity are needed to locate gas and star formation relative to the AGN, since earlier low-resolution [C I] could not say where the H2 sits  
  > "Therefore, observations at higher resolution and higher sensitivity will help reveal the locations of gas and star formation activity, leading to a better understanding of the role of AGN" (Introduction)
- Why this method: [C I](1-0) is used for H2 mass because the [C II]-H2 relation may fail near bright quasars and no higher-order [C I] data exist  
  > "Without additional data on higher order \[C I\] transitions, we calculate $M_{\rm H_{2}}$ solely based on \[C I\](1-0) following (cite: kolwa2023)" (Analysis)
- Alternative: Doubling the velocity range of the core upper limit (two times FWHM) was tested and raises the L_[CII] upper limit by ~30%  
  > "If we double the velocity range, i.e., two times of FWHM, the estimated $L_{\rm [\textup{C\,\textsc{\lowercase{ii}}}]}$ upper limit at radio core would be $\sim30\%$ higher." (Analysis)
- Alternative: The earlier [C I] detection in 4C+03.24 at +1123 km/s is judged possibly false (S/N ~2) because no [C II] is seen at that velocity  
  > "This may be a false detection as no \[C II\] has been detected at the same $\Delta v$" (Discussion and conclusion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Deeper and higher-resolution [C I] data are needed to determine M_H2 accurately  
  > "Deeper and higher spatial resolution \[C I\] are required to more accurately determine the $M_{\rm H_{2}, [\textup{C\,\textsc{\lowercase{i}}}]}$" (Discussion and conclusion)
- Limitation (authors): [C I] extraction apertures overlap in fields with multiple [C II] companions  
  > "We caution that for fields of TN J0121+1320 and 4C+03.24 where multiple \[C II\] companions are detected, the extraction apertures of \[C I\] may be overlapped." (Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Positions on the SFR-M_star plane are uncertain because most measurements are upper limits  
  > "We caution that, given most of our measurements are upper limits, the positions of our companions on the SFR-$M_{\star}$ plane are uncertain." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Most ALMA processing, calibration and the companion selection are deferred to Wang_companion, so this Letter alone cannot be reproduced from raw data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas-poor host claim rests on upper limits from an assumed 5 kpc disk and a fixed 250 km/s window, and on ~2 arcsec [C I] data with overlapping apertures
- Caveat (inferred, ours): M_H2 uses a single Q10 and X_[CI] with no stated uncertainty on those assumptions; [C II] is not an H2 tracer near AGN, which limits the gas-rich interpretation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar mass M_star = M_dyn/2 with q = n = 1 gives only upper limits, so SFR-M_star placement is weakly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Merger-stripping and jet-shock scenarios are not tested against alternatives such as simple CGM or satellite gas
