## Science

- Fields: evolved_stars, stellar_physics
- Question: Are two lines detected at 232 and 354 GHz towards HD 101584 Rydberg transitions (X30alpha, X26alpha) of neutral atoms heavier than carbon, and what gas conditions explain them?  
  > "We report the detection of two lines at millimetre wavelengths towards the immediate surroundings of the post-giant and most likely post-common-envelope star HD 101584 using high-angular-resolution ALMA observations." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image the ALMA data**: CASA calibration (Tsys, WVR, bandpass, gain), Band 6 self-calibration, continuum subtraction, tclean imaging  
  > "After corrections for the time and frequency dependence of the system temperatures, and rapid atmospheric variations at each antenna using water vapour radiometer data, bandpass and gain calibration were performed." (Observations / ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Bandpass: J1107-4449 (TA1-TA3), J1037-2934 and J0635-7516 (TA4); gain: J1131-5818 (TA1), J1132-5606 (TA2-TA4)  
  > "Bandpass calibration was performed on J1107-4449 (TA1, TA2, TA3) and J1037-2934 and J0635-7516 (TA4), and gain calibration on J1131-5818 (TA1), J1132-5606 (TA2, TA3, TA4)." (Observations / ALMA data)
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5 for final line images  
  > "The final line images were created using Briggs robust weighting (0.5)." (Observations / ALMA data)
   - assumption: absolute flux calibration accurate to within 5%  
  > "Based on the calibrator fluxes, we estimate the absolute flux calibration to be accurate to within 5%." (Observations / ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **measure line parameters**: Gaussian fits to the line profiles and to the line brightness distribution in the systemic-velocity channel  
  > "FWHM of a Gaussian fit to the line profile (deconvolved with the spectral resolution)." (Table 1 notes); "Upper limit to the source size (FWHM of a Gaussian source after deconvolving with the beam) using a Gaussian fit to the line brightness distribution in the systemic velocity channel." (Table 1 notes)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: rest frequencies computed assuming systemic velocity 41.5 +/- 0.15 km/s  
  > "Assuming emission at the systemic velocity of 41.5 $\pm$ 0.15 km s$^{-1}$." (Table 1 notes)
   - fitting: single Gaussian per line  
  > "The lines are perfectly Gaussian, within the noise levels, and there is no evidence of low-intensity, broad line wings." (Line identification)
   - validation: upper limits for H, He and C lines of the same transitions derived  
  > "Upper limits to the corresponding H, He, and C lines are 1 and 2 mJy in the two bands, respectively." (Line identification)
3. **measure continuum flux density and size**: Gaussian fit to the continuum brightness distribution; uv-tapering at three beam sizes to test dust contamination at 232 GHz  
  > "Estimated source size (FWHM of a Gaussian source after deconvolving with the beam) using a Gaussian fit to the continuum brightness distribution." (Table 1 notes); "using different uv-tapering we estimate flux densities of 7.5, 10.1, and 13.2 mJy for beam sizes of about 0.025$''$, 0.083$''$, and 0.15$''$, respectively." (Line identification)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: line and continuum emission come from the same region  
  > "We make the reasonable assumption that the line and continuum emissions come from the same region." (A simple model in strict LTE / Geometry)
   - assumption: Band 7 continuum likely has a significant dust contribution  
  > "Even if the dust contribution increases only slowly with beam area, the contribution from dust continuum is likely to be significant in this case." (Line identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: slow growth of flux with beam area taken to show the 232 GHz point-like continuum is relatively free of dust  
  > "This gives a flux density that increases only slowly with beam area, which suggests that the 7.5 mJy within an estimated source size of 0.011$''$" (Line identification)
4. **test line identification against molecules and atomic Rydberg lines**: search for molecular matches; compute RTL rest frequencies with the Rydberg formula and compare with the observed frequencies  
  > "On the other hand, these rest frequencies are consistent with an identification in terms of the X30$\alpha$ and X26$\alpha$ RTLs from an element X heavier than carbon." (Line identification); "is given by the Rydberg formula of (cite: towletal96)," (Line identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: H2(13)CO(5-4) at 353.8119 GHz ruled out because that species is not detected at the extreme velocity spots  
  > "However, this can be ruled out as an identification because we know from previous data that the H$_2$CO lines are strongest at the so-called extreme velocity spots" (Footnote 1)
5. **build a strict-LTE model of line and continuum emission**: uniform isothermal parcel of gas in strict LTE; Saha/Boltzmann ionisation and excitation balance, molecular equilibrium chemistry, free-free and related continuum opacities, line opacity, flux S = B(T)[1-exp(-tau)] pi (R/D)^2 for a cylinder seen face-on  
  > "This parcel of gas is characterised by two parameters: a number density of hydrogen in all forms, $n_{\rm H}$, and a temperature, $T$." (A simple model in strict LTE); "The ionisation balance is governed by the Saha equation" (A simple model in strict LTE / Ionisation balance)
   - software: NIST Atomic Spectra Database, ExoMol, LAMDA, Storey and Hummer (1991) program
   - parameter: geometry parameter rho = dz/R = 0.1 (thin disc), source size 11 mas, R = 5.5 au at 1 kpc  
  > "We adopt $\rho$ = 0.1, that is, a geometrically thin disc, as the most likely geometry for describing the RTL region." (A simple model in strict LTE / Geometry); "We will nevertheless adopt 11 mas as the size of the RTL region" (A simple model in strict LTE / Geometry)
   - parameter: abundance Set 1 solar photosphere; Set 2 depleted by factor d  
  > "The reference abundances are those of the solar photosphere ($Y$; Set 1), and Set 2 consists of abundances depleted by a factor of $d$." (A simple model in strict LTE / Chemistry)
   - assumption: strict LTE applies  
  > "that is, we assume that strict local LTE applies." (A simple model in strict LTE)
   - assumption: distance 1 kpc, solar abundances for the reference model  
  > "undepleted solar abundances, a nominal distance of $D$ = 1.0 kpc" (Results from modelling / The reference model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: line flux ratio 26alpha/30alpha close to blackbody ratio taken as support for thermal equilibrium; molecular formation and timescale arguments for LTE  
  > "Simple estimates of chemical, photodissociation, and ionisation timescales, as well as comparisons of collisional and radiative strengths, suggest that the LTE assumption is, if not perfect, at least acceptable." (A simple model in strict LTE)
5. **search the (nH, T) grid for the best-fit model**: for each (nH, T) on a grid, compute line fluxes and iterate the source radius R until the model matches the line flux; then check H line upper limit, adjacent continuum and angular size  
  > "Iterate over values of the source radius $R$ until the model matches the line flux." (Results from modelling / Procedure); "Examine the resulting grid of solutions to see which of them also satisfies the upper limit on the hydrogen line flux, the flux of the adjacent continuum, and the observed constraints on angular size of the source." (Results from modelling / Procedure)
   - parameter: reference model taken as the midpoint of the polygonic area of acceptable solutions in the (nH, T) plane  
  > "We pick the midpoint of the polygonic area in the left panel of Fig." (Results from modelling / The reference model)
   - fitting: grid search with iteration on R; no sampler, priors or likelihood used  
  > "Iterate over values of the source radius $R$ until the model matches the line flux." (Results from modelling / Procedure)
   - validation: secondary high-density solution rejected; no solutions for depleted abundances; solution insensitive to geometry, size and distance (only mass scales with distance)  
  > "However, this solution is implausible for two reasons." (Results from modelling / The reference model); "There are no reasonable solutions for the X30$\alpha$ line with depleted interstellar abundances." (Results from modelling / The reference model); "The primary model is not very sensitive to the source size either." (Results from modelling / Dependence on geometry and distance)
6. **model the blended line profiles and set density and magnetic-field limits**: weighted sum of Gaussian-broadened fine-structure components of Na, Mg isotopes, Al, Ca, Si and Fe; add Lorentzian collisional and radiation damping and Zeeman splitting; fit single Gaussians to the model profiles  
  > "We computed theoretical line profiles for the 26$\alpha$ and 30$\alpha$ transitions for the blend of these eight atomic species (including isotopic variants) in the reference model." (Results from modelling / Identification of the line emitters)
   - parameter: thermal and turbulent widths added in quadrature; single micro-turbulent width for all species  
  > "The Gaussian widths add in quadrature $\Delta \upsilon^2$ = $\Delta \upsilon_{\rm th}^2$ + $\Delta \upsilon_{\rm turb}^2$." (A simple model in strict LTE / Line broadening)
   - parameter: uniform magnetic field assumed for Zeeman models  
  > "Finally, models with Zeeman splitting have been computed assuming a uniform magnetic field throughout the RTL region." (Results from modelling / Line broadening)
   - assumption: Mg isotopes in terrestrial relative amounts  
  > "Magnesium isotopes are assumed to be present in the same relative amounts as in standard terrestrial material" (Additional line broadening / blended line profile)
   - fitting: least-squares fit of single Gaussians to computed profiles  
  > "The small shift of the blended profile relative to Mg is quantified in Fig." (Results from modelling / Identification of the line emitters)
   - validation: model blend rest frequencies agree with observed rest frequencies at 41.5 km/s  
  > "These values agree very well with the rest frequencies of the observed lines estimated assuming a systemic velocity of 41.5 km s$^{-1}$" (Results from modelling / Identification of the line emitters)
7. **compare model with molecular lines and optical emission lines**: re-analysis of CCS molecular lines in the most extended Band 6 configuration; Gaussian fits; Gaussian plus low-order polynomial fits to UVES optical lines; comparison with model-predicted fluxes  
  > "In order to compare with our ALMA data, we re-analysed the molecular line data for the CCS component" (Predictions for radio lines); "The amplitude, centre, and width of each line was determined from a simultaneous least-squares fit of a low-order polynomial continuum and several Gaussian functions to a small wavelength interval, typically 50 Å." (Constraints from optical emission lines)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: molecular lines analysed with the most extended Band 6 configuration, about 0.03 arcsec resolution  
  > "using only the data obtained with the most extended configuration in Band 6, which therefore has the highest angular resolution" (Predictions for radio lines)
   - assumption: predicted molecular fluxes use line width 8.5 km/s and angular diameter 11 mas for all lines  
  > "with the assumption that the same line width (8.5 km s$^{-1}$) and angular diameter (11 mas) apply to all." (Table 3 notes)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits  
  > "The amplitude, centre, and width of each line was determined from a simultaneous least-squares fit of a low-order polynomial continuum and several Gaussian functions to a small wavelength interval, typically 50 Å." (Constraints from optical emission lines)
   - validation: model reproduces the Ca II forbidden lines but under-predicts the O I and C I lines  
  > "The model nicely reproduces the integrated fluxes of the two Ca II forbidden lines." (Optical lines)

## Qualitative findings

- line emission in the model is dominated by Rydberg transitions of the stable Mg isotopes; Na, Al and Ca contribute 5-10 % each  
  > "With these conditions, the line emissions are dominated by Rydberg transitions within the stable isotopes of Mg." (abstract); "The contributions from Na, Al, and Ca lie in the range of 5–10 % each." (Conclusions)
- emitting gas lies within about 5 au of the star (at 1 kpc)  
  > "The gas lies within a distance of about 5 au from the star (assuming a distance of 1 kpc)." (abstract)
- the gas appears stationary and the heating and turbulence mechanism is unidentified  
  > "The gas appears to be stationary, and the process responsible for keeping the gas heated and turbulent over a region of about 10 au remains to be identified" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: a simple strict-LTE model has the fewest free parameters and allows predictions, since a full non-LTE model is beyond the scope  
  > "Instead, we have devised a model in strict LTE that will explain the presence of heavy-element RTLs and a free-free continuum, at their observed intensities, and the simultaneous absence of the corresponding C and H RTLs." (A simple model in strict LTE)
- Alternative: secondary higher-density, lower-temperature solution (2.2e13 cm^-3, 2050 K) rejected as implausible  
  > "However, this solution is implausible for two reasons." (Results from modelling / The reference model)
- Alternative: RTL region at a distance from the star with a cavity (photoionised) judged less likely  
  > "The alternative, that the RTL region is located at a distance from the star with an absorption-free cavity in between, appears less likely." (A simple model in strict LTE)
- Alternative: H2(13)CO(5-4) identification ruled out  
  > "However, this can be ruled out as an identification because we know from previous data that the H$_2$CO lines are strongest at the so-called extreme velocity spots" (Footnote 1)
- How they decided it was enough: reference model chosen as the midpoint of the polygon of acceptable solutions  
  > "We pick the midpoint of the polygonic area in the left panel of Fig." (Results from modelling / The reference model)
- Limitation (authors): a non-LTE model with chemistry, ionisation, excitation and radiative transfer is beyond the scope  
  > "A complete analysis of the data requires a non-LTE model that takes into account chemistry, ionisation, excitation, and radiative transfer in a source of arbitrary geometry." (A simple model in strict LTE)
- Limitation (authors): the two lines were observed at different times so variability could affect the flux ratio  
  > "The observations of the two lines were made at different times; therefore, it is possible that variability has affected the ratio of their fluxes." (Footnote 3)
- Limitation (authors): continuum from the line-emitting source is contaminated by dust  
  > "The continuum flux from the line-emitting source is difficult to estimate due to contamination with dust continuum emission." (Line identification)
- Limitation (authors): collisional broadening coefficients are uncertain  
  > "Unfortunately, there is some uncertainty about the collisional broadening coefficients" (Results from modelling / Line broadening)
- Limitation (authors): distance is uncertain (Gaia parallax suspected unreliable)  
  > "However, as argued by (cite: olofetal19) there are reasons to suspect that this distance is not correct" (HD 101584)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line identification rests on a single-zone strict-LTE model with free parameters tuned to two lines, so the identification is not unique and Na/Fe/Mg blends cannot be separated observationally
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Band 7 continuum is dust contaminated and the two lines come from different epochs and different array resolutions, so the 26alpha/30alpha ratio is uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No formal uncertainties or parameter posteriors on T, nH, R or mass; only a hand-picked reference model within a solution polygon
