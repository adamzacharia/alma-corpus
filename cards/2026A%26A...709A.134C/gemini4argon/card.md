## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare and astrometrically align archival JWST NIRSpec IFU data cubes**: Retrieve stage 3 NIRSpec IFU data cubes from MAST and apply astrometric offsets to align the 4.5 um continuum peak with the VLBI position of the S AGN, without performing wiggle corrections.  
  > "We gathered the combined science (stage 3) data products from the MAST web portal" (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations); "The correction offsets applied to the data are $\mathrm{\delta R.A.=-0.22''}$ and $\mathrm{\delta Dec.}=0.17''$." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations); "For all these reasons, we decided not to perform a wiggle correction on our data." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations)
   - software: JWST pipeline v1.14.0
   - parameter: delta R.A. = -0.22'', delta Dec. = 0.17''  
  > "The correction offsets applied to the data are $\mathrm{\delta R.A.=-0.22''}$ and $\mathrm{\delta Dec.}=0.17''$." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations)
   - parameter: spaxel size = 0.1'' x 0.1'', FoV = 5.1'' x 4.5''  
  > "In 1, we show a composite image of the NIRSpec field-of-view (FoV) ($5.1''\times4.5''$, i.e. 2.5 kpc $\times$ 2.3 kpc)" (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations); "The spaxel size is $0.1''\times0.1''$." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations)
   - assumption: The 4.5 um continuum peak coincides with the S AGN VLBI position  
  > "We assume that the $4.5~\mu$m continuum peak coincides with the S AGN, the brighter one." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Both nuclei are consistent with VLBI AGN positions after correction; tested impact of wiggles by masking the 15 affected spaxels around S AGN and found line fits and flux maps unchanged within uncertainties.  
  > "After the correction, both nuclei are consistent with the VLBI AGN positions." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations); "We have also repeated the whole analysis by masking the 15 spaxels that are affected by wiggles, which are all around the S AGN. The resulting line fits and flux maps remain unchanged within the uncertainties" (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations)

  > "We finally conservatively select the line-free channels using the `CASA` task `split`, i.e.: $\nu_{obs}\in (349.5, 350.4)$ GHz in spw 0, $\nu_{obs}\in (350.3, 351.9)$ GHz in spw 1, and $\nu_{obs}\in (339.5, 340.2)$ GHz in spw 3." (Band 7 (870\mu m) continuum data); "We then image the corresponding uv visibilities using the task `tclean` with `specmode = mfs`, using a hogbom deconvolver, with briggs weighting (robust = 0.5)." (Band 7 (870\mu m) continuum data); "We use the task `uvcontsub` to fit the continuum emission with a first-order polynomial and subtract it from the uv visibilities." (Band 3 CO(1-0) line data)
   - software: CASA, split, tclean, impbcor, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Band 7: specmode = mfs, hogbom, Briggs robust = 0.5, cell = 0.05'', mask radius = 1.3''  
  > "We then image the corresponding uv visibilities using the task `tclean` with `specmode = mfs`, using a hogbom deconvolver, with briggs weighting (robust = 0.5). We use a cleaning mask centred at RA=16h52m58.91s, Dec=02d24m03.89s with a radius of $1.3~''$. The cell size is $0.05''$." (Band 7 (870\mu m) continuum data)
   - parameter: Band 3: specmode = cube, channel width re-binning = 5 (13 km s^-1), hogbom, Briggs robust = 0.5, cell = 0.08''  
  > "We image the continuum-subtracted visibilities using `tclean`, `specmode=cube`, re-binning the data using a channel width of 5. We select the hogbom deconvolver and use Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5." (Band 3 CO(1-0) line data); "The cell size is $0.08''$, and the resulting synthesized beam is $0.55''\times 0.52''$, PA=-52.8 deg." (Band 3 CO(1-0) line data)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: 1st-order polynomial continuum fit in uvcontsub for Band 3 over nu_obs in (111.6, 112.1) GHz and (113, 113.4) GHz  
  > "We use the task `uvcontsub` to fit the continuum emission with a first-order polynomial and subtract it from the uv visibilities. The frequency ranges selected to estimate the continuum level are $\nu_{obs}\in (111.6, 112.1)$ GHz and $\nu_{obs}\in (113, 113.4)$ GHz." (Band 3 CO(1-0) line data)
   - validation: Band 7 continuum map is consistent with the map produced from the same data by Fyhrie+21.  
  > "We note that the same data are used by (cite: Fyhrie+21) and their Band 7 continuum map is consistent with the one produced by us." (Band 7 (870\mu m) continuum data)
3. **Fit stellar continuum and derive stellar kinematics**: Fit stellar continuum and absorption features spaxel-by-spaxel in the NIRSpec bands using pPXF with E-MILES templates, and visualize the orientation of the rotating stellar structures with kinemetry.  
  > "We fit a stellar continuum to the data using the penalized pixel-fitting routine (pPXF) (cite: Cappellari+Emsellem2004; cappellari17). We use the E-MILES stellar population synthesis templates (cite: vazdekis_uv-extended_2016)" (Stellar kinematics); "The rightmost panel of 3 shows the result of a fit to the 1.7 $\mu$m band stellar maps with the `kinemetry` tool (cite: krajnovic_kinemetry_2006), obtained by selecting the VLBI positions of the two AGN as kinematic centres." (Stellar kinematics)
   - software: pPXF, kinemetry
   - parameter: 1.0 um band: 5th-order additive and 2nd-order multiplicative Legendre polynomials; 1.7 um band: 5th-order additive and 3rd-order multiplicative Legendre polynomials; 2.9 um band: 6th-order additive and 3rd-order multiplicative polynomials  
  > "We use a fifth-order additive and a second-order multiplicative Legendre polynomial for the 1.0 $\mu$m band. We also use a fifth-order additive polynomial for the 1.7 $\mu$m band, with a third-order multiplicative polynomial to account for the shape variations." (Stellar kinematics); "We attempt a fit with a sixth-order additive and a third-order multiplicative polynomial, but the comparison with the results obtained in the other two bands indicates that the stellar fit results are unreliable at these longer wavelengths." (Stellar kinematics)
   - assumption: Single kinematic stellar component per spaxel; VLBI positions of the two AGN selected as kinematic centres for kinemetry visualization.  
  > "The rightmost panel of 3 shows the result of a fit to the 1.7 $\mu$m band stellar maps with the `kinemetry` tool (cite: krajnovic_kinemetry_2006), obtained by selecting the VLBI positions of the two AGN as kinematic centres." (Stellar kinematics); "These authors demonstrated that the $\sigma_v>330~$ km s$^{-1}$ values can be the consequence of a single-kinematic component fit performed on data where absorption features from two stellar populations at different relative line-of-sight velocities are blended." (Stellar kinematics)
   - fitting: Penalized pixel-fitting (pPXF)  
  > "We fit a stellar continuum to the data using the penalized pixel-fitting routine (pPXF) (cite: Cappellari+Emsellem2004; cappellari17)." (Stellar kinematics)
   - validation: Comparison between bands (showing 1.7 um band is most reliable and 2.9 um band is unreliable) and consistency with previous studies (Ceci+24, Engel+10, Ilha+16, Kollatschny+20).  
  > "The resulting stellar moment maps are consistent with previous studies (cite: ceci_jwstnirspec_2024; Engel+10; Ilha+16; Kollatschny+20), especially those obtained from the 1.7 $\mu$m band data which we deem to be more reliable thanks to the availability of numerous stellar features." (Stellar kinematics)
4. **Decompose emission lines into rotating and non-rotating components and compute moment maps**: Fit six NIR emission lines (Pa alpha, Pa beta, [FeII] 1.257 um, [FeII] 1.644 um, H2 1-0 S(1), H2 0-0 S(8)) spaxel-by-spaxel for spaxels with S/N > 10 using up to three Gaussians plus a linear baseline, tying one Gaussian to the pPXF stellar velocity and dispersion within 3 sigma, then compute 0th, 1st, and 2nd moment maps for total, rotating, and non-rotating components.  
  > "Under this assumption, we tie one spectral component of the gaseous emission lines to the stellar kinematics, forcing it to share the same velocity and velocity dispersion as the stars (on a spaxel-by-spaxel basis) within their $3\sigma$ uncertainty derived from `pPXF`." (Spectral line fitting); "We perform an $\mathrm{S/N}$-cut by taking the median of the intensity at each spaxel and only choosing the spaxels with $\mathrm{S/N>10}$. Then, each spectrum is fitted using a compound model consisting of up to three Gaussian functions and one linear function." (Spectral line fitting); "Our line fitting procedure, described in Sect. 3.3, allowed us to de-blend the non-rotating from the rotating components of the gas emission." (Moment maps of non-rotating gas components)
   - software: lmfit

## Qualitative findings

- NIR emission lines in the central ~2 kpc of NGC 6240 are dominated (~64-66% of flux) by gas components kinematically decoupled from the stellar rotating structures.  
  > "The NIR emission lines in NGC 6240 are dominated by gas components whose kinematics is considerably decoupled from that of the stellar component. These contribute to as much as $64\%$ of the Pa$\alpha$ and \[FeII\]1.644$\mu$m fluxes, and to $66\%$ of the H$_2$ 1-0 S(1) line flux." (Summary and conclusions)
- PAH 3.3 um emission and ALMA 870 um cold dust continuum both peak at the two AGN nuclei, differing markedly from the internuclear peak of H2 emission, which rules out PDRs as the origin of the bulk H2 emission.  
  > "The PAH 3.3 $\mu$m feature and the cold dust emission at 870$\mu$m peak at the two nuclei; hence, they present a very different morphology from the H$_2$ gas." (Summary and conclusions)
- A bi-conical AGN-driven wind (sigma_v ~ 400 km s^-1, extending to r ~ 500 pc) is launched from the northern nucleus along the minor axis of stellar rotation, predominantly in ionised gas (Pa alpha, [FeII]) with a low H2/PAH ratio.  
  > "Because of its characteristic geometry, its high-velocity dispersion of 400 km s$^{-1}$, and because it is clearly decoupled from the stars, we suggest that this structure traces an AGN-driven wind entraining ionised gas and warm molecular gas" (Moment maps of non-rotating gas components); "This bi-conical wind mainly involves ionised gas and extends up to $r\sim500$ pc on both sides of the nucleus." (A bi-conical AGN wind from the northern nucleus)
- The warm H2 gas kinematics and morphology correlate closely with the cold molecular outflow traced by ALMA CO(1-0) and [CI](1-0), and the redshifted outflow wing (up to ~900 km s^-1) coincides with the peak H2/PAH ratio (>0.75), showing that shocked outflowing gas at T~2000 K dominates the NIR H2 emission.  
  > "This hypothesis is supported by the striking correlation between the warm H$_2$ gas kinematics and that of the cold molecular outflow traced by \[CI\] and CO ALMA data." (Summary and conclusions); "In conclusion, we showed that the bulk of the NIR H$_2$ emission from the central $\sim2$ kpc of NGC 6240 arises from $T\sim2000$ K shocked gas entrained in a massive molecular outflow, which is cooling down and coexisting with a colder phase probed by low-J CO and atomic carbon (\[CI\](1-0)) emission." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA Band 7 continuum data provide angular resolution comparable to JWST NIRSpec to map cold dust; ALMA Band 8 [CI](1-0) and Band 3 CO(1-0) trace the cold molecular outflow for direct comparison with the warm H2 phase observed by JWST NIRSpec.  
  > "From this dataset, we produce a map of the 870$\mu$m dust continuum in the nuclear region of NGC 6240, with an angular resolution comparable to that of JWST NIRSpec." (Band 7 (870\mu m) continuum data); "The ALMA Band 8 data from project ID 2015.1.00717 (PI: Cicone) enable us to compare the morphology of the cold molecular outflow as imaged using the atomic carbon \[CI\]$^3P_1-^3P_0$ line (hereafter, \[CI\](1-0)) with that of the warm molecular hydrogen observed by JWST." (Band 8 [CI]^3P_1-^3P_0 line data); "To strengthen our comparison between the cold phase of the molecular gas and the warm phase traced by the NIR H$_2$ lines, we also use the ALMA Band 3 CO(1-0) line data from project ID 2015.1.00003.S (PI: Saito)." (Band 3 CO(1-0) line data)
- Why this method: Standard disc modelling or arbitrary narrow/broad Gaussian decomposition fails in NGC 6240 because outflowing gas dominates even the narrow/low-velocity line emission; tying one component to the stellar kinematics provides a physically motivated separation of rotating and non-rotating gas. Powell's optimizer was chosen over Levenberg-Marquardt to handle multiple local minima.  
  > "We thus devised a new fitting approach to disentangle rotating from non-rotating motion that relies on the results of the stellar kinematics fit." (Spectral line fitting); "We set the minimization optimizer to Powell’s method, a conjugate direction method, which we find to be better suited for multi-component spectral fitting with several local minima than the default optimizer (the Levenberg-Marquardt method)." (Spectral line fitting)
- Alternative: Empirical wiggle correction on JWST NIRSpec spectra: rejected because extended gas and stellar features overlap with the point source, making empirical modelling risky, and masking affected spaxels did not change results.  
  > "It is especially risky in our case, where extended emission from gas and stars overlaps with the point sources" (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations); "For all these reasons, we decided not to perform a wiggle correction on our data." (Archival JWST NIRSpec observations)
- Alternative: Standard disc modelling followed by residual analysis: rejected because disc-like rotation does not dominate the gas emission in NGC 6240.  
  > "To do so, we cannot rely on standard disc modelling procedures followed by a study of residual emission: such approach is valid for sources where disc-like rotation dominates the gas emission, but this is not NGC 6240’s case." (Spectral line fitting)
- Alternative: Narrow/broad spectral line decomposition (as in Ceci+24): rejected because the outflow dominates even the narrow component flux in NGC 6240, so narrow/broad decomposition fails to de-blend the outflow.  
  > "Another approach could be a narrow/broad spectral line decomposition such as the one adopted by (cite: ceci_jwstnirspec_2024)." (Spectral line fitting); "clearly proving that the outflow is not just contaminating, but it is even dominating the flux in the so-called “narrow” components identified by (cite: ceci_jwstnirspec_2024)." (Spectral line fitting)
- Alternative: Using the 2.9 um band pPXF stellar kinematics fit: rejected as unreliable due to the scarcity of stellar features at those wavelengths.  
  > "We attempt a fit with a sixth-order additive and a third-order multiplicative polynomial, but the comparison with the results obtained in the other two bands indicates that the stellar fit results are unreliable at these longer wavelengths." (Stellar kinematics)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Limited spectral resolution of JWST NIRSpec data compared to VLT/SINFONI and ALMA.  
  > "Although the limited spectral resolution of the JWST data demands some caution" (Stellar kinematics); "The latter may be a consequence of the lower spectral resolution of NIRSpec compared to ALMA." (A powerful outflow from the southern nucleus)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming the same velocity dispersion (sigma_v) for rotating gas and stars is a limitation, and assuming every spaxel has a gas component associated with the stellar component may overestimate the rotating gas fraction and underestimate the non-rotating fraction.  
  > "Assuming the same $\sigma_v$ of rotation for gas and stars is a limitation of this approach" (Spectral line fitting); "Furthermore, assuming that in each spaxel each stellar component has an associated gas component may lead to overestimating the fraction of gas coupled with the stars, and consequently underestimating the fraction of gas not involved in stellar motions." (Spectral line fitting)
- Limitation (authors): The 'rotating' gas component by construction includes any gas motion with a stellar counterpart in the NIRSpec bands, such as stellar tidal tails and blended merger-related stellar populations.  
  > "We note that this is an oversimplification, because by construction these components account for any motion with a stellar counterpart in the NIRSpec bands. For example, gas-rich stellar tidal tails and similar merger-related features are included among such “rotating” components." (Spectral line fitting)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Forcing the rotating gas component to share the stellar velocity dispersion (which exceeds 350 km/s in the internuclear region due to blended stellar populations) likely absorbs a substantial fraction of turbulent or outflowing gas into the 'rotating' component.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No dust extinction correction was applied to the integrated Pa alpha, Pa beta, [Fe II], and H2 fluxes before computing component fractions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Band 7 continuum observation has a very short on-source integration time (~3 min) and an elongated beam (0.315'' x 0.14''), limiting sensitivity to diffuse dust structures.
