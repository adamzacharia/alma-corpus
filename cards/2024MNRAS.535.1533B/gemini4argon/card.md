## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate visibility data, subtract continuum, and image continuum and spectral cubes**: Restore pipeline calibration in CASA, bin channels by 16 to locate lines, subtract continuum from visibilities using line-free channels, and image continuum and cubes with tclean using Hogbom deconvolver and 0.05 arcsec uv-taper (plus 0.5 arcsec taper for HerBS-159A/B).  
  > "The pipeline calibration of the visibility data were first restored using the standard scripts from the ALMA Science Archive and using casa version 6.2.1. After this, the data were imaged using tclean." (2.3 Data reduction); "To further optimize the images for the detection of faint line or continuum emission, we used the Hogbom deconvolver. Additionally, a uv taper of 0.05 arcsec was applied to remove noise with very high spatial frequencies." (2.3 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Channel binning factor = 16 for initial line search; line exclusion window = 0.4 GHz for undetected expected lines  
  > "We initially created image cubes binning the channels by a factor of 16 to identify detectable spectral lines from each target within each spectral window" (2.3 Data reduction); "we identified all channels that lied within a 0.4 GHz region centred on the expected frequency of the spectral line for each target as potentially containing line emission" (2.3 Data reduction)
   - parameter: Natural weighting for continuum; briggsbwtaper with robust = 2 for cubes; uv-taper = 0.05 arcsec  
  > "Natural weighting was used to create the continuum images, but when creating the image cubes, we set the weighting to briggsbwtaper, which is a variant of Briggs weighting that makes adjustments for variations in the beam size with frequency, and we set the robust factor to 2" (2.3 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectral line profiles and measure integrated line fluxes, widths, and velocity offsets**: Define apertures by matching Band 7 2-sigma continuum contours (and 2-sigma moment-0 contours for large sources) to the cube resolution and extending by up to 3 beams; extract spectra and fit single Gaussian profiles.  
  > "we combine these methods into an algorithm that matches the highest-fidelity (band 7) observation to the cube resolution, and extending the 2𝜎 continuum contour by up to 3 beams of the cube." (3.2 Line measurements); "The subsequent spectrum is then fitted with a single Gaussian profile to provide a line estimate." (3.2 Line measurements)
   - parameter: 2-sigma continuum and moment-0 contour thresholds, extended by up to 3 beams (no spatial smoothing for absorption lines)  
  > "For sources where the apertures cover a large number of beams, an additional aperture selection criterion is used, based on the 2𝜎 contours of the moment-0 map before extending the aperture by up to 3 beams." (3.2 Line measurements)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian profile fit to extracted spectrum  
  > "The subsequent spectrum is then fitted with a single Gaussian profile to provide a line estimate." (3.2 Line measurements)
   - validation: Compared extracted CO fluxes with BEARS curve-of-growth 5-sigma method on resolved and unresolved data (finding 4 +/- 8% systemic difference on resolved data).  
  > "finds only a 4 ± 8 per

For unlensed sources, fit Sersic profile in image plane using CASA IMFIT.  
  > "We perform this lens modelling (lens mass model optimization and background source reconstructions) using the open-source software PyAutoLens" (4.1 Lensing reconstruction); "In the first phase we fit a parametric model for the source (i.e. a Sersic profile) while in the second phase we switch to pixelated reconstructions. In both phases the mass model for the lensing galaxy is a Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid (SIE)." (4.1 Lensing reconstruction); "The non-lensed sources are fitted using the
`casa` task `IMFIT`, which assumes a Sersic index of 0.5" (6.2 Star-formation surface densities of ANGELS sources)

   - parameter: Sersic index n free between 0.5 and 5 for lensed sources; n = 0.5 fixed in IMFIT for unlensed sources  
  > "We use the Sersic profiles for the sources when available from the lensing reconstruction (seen by crosses; Table 3), where the Sersic indices are free to vary between 0.5 and 5. The non-lensed sources are fitted using the casa task IMFIT, which assumes a Sersic index of 0.5" (6.2 Star-formation surface densities of ANGELS sources)

## Qualitative findings

- Optimizing local oscillator tunings for clustered sources with known redshifts triples the number of observable spectral lines in a snapshot survey and detects one line every six minutes.  
  > "The simple act of optimizing the tuning towards the known spectroscopic redshifts can triple the number lines within any snapshot program." (2.2 The ANGELS method); "At only 6.5 hours of observing time, the ANGELS survey targeted one line every four minutes, and detected a line every six minutes." (8.2 Efficiency compared to overhead-limited surveys)
- CO SLEDs show diverse excitation conditions, with ~36% of sources exhibiting strong high-J CO emission indicative of warm, dense gas reservoirs.  
  > "The prevalence of dense, warm gas thus appears diverse across distant dusty galaxies, with ∼ 36 ± 13 per cent (using a binomial distribution; Gehrels 1986) of sources showing such evidence" (5.1 CO Spectral Line Energy Distributions)
- Multiple absorption species (CH+ and OH+) within the same galaxy show similar velocity offsets relative to CO, indicating shocked gas and diffuse molecular gas reside in similar regions.  
  > "The sources with multiple transitions (HerBS-21 and -22) find similar systematic velocity offsets for both their tracers, suggesting that the shocked gas traced by CH+ and the more diffuse molecular gas traced by OH+ originate from similar regions." (Figure 9 caption)
- HerBS-170 is the brightest [NII] 205 um emitter observed to date, and the detected sources show no evidence for a far-infrared line deficit.  
  > "Our sources lie on the bright end of the scaling relations, and HerBS-170 qualifies as the brightest Nitrogen emitter observed to date. There is no evidence for a far-infrared line deficit, which appears to affect high star-formation surface-density galaxies (e.g., Rybak et al. 2019)." (5.3 Atomic lines)
- A bimodal picture of DSFGs emerges: strongly lensed sources are typically compact (0.3-2 kpc) central dusty starbursts, while weakly- or unlensed HyLIRGs are more extended (0.3-3 kpc) and clumpy.  
  > "A preliminary bimodal picture of DSFGs is appearing, where lensed sources are indicative of typically central dusty star-bursts, found in other surveys to be rotationally stable, while the weakly- or unlensed HyLIRGs could represent turbulent massive star-forming events driven by unstable galaxy kinematics and clumpy gas flows." (9 CONCLUSIONS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sources within 10 degrees on the sky with known spectroscopic redshifts from BEARS allow shared calibration in configurations C43-6 and lower, enabling 2-minute snapshots across Bands 3-8 to sample rest-frame 200-2000 GHz at 0.5-0.1 arcsec resolution.  
  > "The number density of sources allowed the use of short snapshots (≈ 2 min) with only a single ∼ 20 - 30 min. calibration step to reduce the overheads typically associated with line surveys (∼one calibration per targeted line)." (2.2 The ANGELS method); "This shared calibration step is only enabled for sources within 10 deg. on the sky for configurations below ∼ 3 km (C43-6 or lower)" (2.2 The ANGELS method)
- Why this method: Tuning optimization triples targeted lines; visibility-plane two-phase lens modelling avoids parametric bias on complex resolved structures; extended continuum+moment-0 apertures recover total line flux in resolved sources.  
  > "The simple act of optimizing the tuning towards the known spectroscopic redshifts can triple the number lines within any snapshot program." (2.2 The ANGELS method); "Effectively, we test the best aperture for each line to include as much flux as possible, favouring larger apertures to measure the total flux as accurately as possible at the potential cost of signal-to-noise ratio." (3.2 Line measurements); "In such cases, relying on a parametric model could introduce biases in the lens parameters and, by extension, in the derived magnification factors" (4.1 Lensing reconstruction)
- Alternative: Pure 2-sigma moment-0 apertures or pure dust continuum apertures for line flux extraction, rejected in favor of a combined extended aperture to avoid false positives and missing edge flux.  
  > "In an effort to extract the total line fluxes, we tried several flux measurement techniques, including 2𝜎 moment-0 apertures (biased to including false positive 2𝜎 features and missing the 0−2𝜎 regions on the edges of the contours; Stanley et al. 2023) and dust continuum apertures (presuming dust and star-formation are coincident with line emission)." (3.2 Line measurements)
- Alternative: Curve-of-growth aperture stopping at 5-sigma SNR (used in BEARS), which biases resolved line fluxes slightly low compared to the ANGELS matched-aperture method.  
  > "in this paper we match the apertures to extract flux despite the additional noise that arises from increased apertures, while previous studies use a curve-of-growth approach that stops velocity-integrated flux density measurements at a 5𝜎 threshold, which appears to bias the observed fluxes low." (5.1 CO Spectral Line Energy Distributions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Snapshot observations are inhomogeneous in line coverage per source and not deep enough to guarantee detections of all targeted lines, introducing potential detection bias.  
  > "Firstly, the ANGELS method is efficient, but does not provide the homogeneity of targeted observations with ALMA. In fact, these observations are not deep enough to guarantee a detection" (8.3 Caveats with the ANGELS method)
- Limitation (authors): Line stacking only probes average population-wide behavior and is dominated by the brightest sources due to variance weighting.  
  > "As discussed in detail by Hagimoto et al. (2023) and Reuter et al. (2023), this means that the brightest sources are still predominantly determining the spectrum." (5.4 Stacked spectrum); "Since DSFGs are found to be a diverse population, line stacking alone cannot reveal more than population-wide behaviour" (8.3 Caveats with the ANGELS method)
- Limitation (authors): Tuning optimization did not optimize the variable intermediate frequency of Band 6, did not account for lines already observed in BEARS, and did not account for smaller primary beams at higher frequencies missing wide companions.  
  > "Band 6 has a variable intermediate frequency which has not been further optimized during these ANGELS observations, and would require higher-order optimization steps to fully explore." (8.3 Caveats with the ANGELS method); "these graphs do not account for the change in primary beam sensitivity with increasing frequency. As several of our sources were known multiples, we are unable to resolve some sources across all bands." (8.3 Caveats with the ANGELS method)
- Limitation (authors): Some lines sit near the edge of spectral windows (e.g., [OI] 145 um in HerBS-41A only probes delta V < 150 km/s; [NII] in HerBS-81B only covers V > 50 km/s; CH+(2-1) in HerBS-36 may miss delta V < 0 km/s).  
  > "Meanwhile, this is only the second time that CH+(2–1) is seen in the z > 0.1 Universe (Bakx et al. 2024), although we note that we might miss the ΔV < 0 km/s component in the ANGELS observations." (5.2 Absorption lines); "The [O i] emission line was detected in HerBS-41A, although we note that the total flux estimate might be underestimated since we probe only the ΔV < 150 km/s region." (5.3 Atomic lines)
- Limitation (authors): Resolved Schmidt-Kennicutt analysis uses mid-J CO transitions (J=3-2 to 6-5) and a constant alpha_CO = 4.0, which introduces conversion uncertainties, and the >1-sigma pixel cut truncates outskirts.  
  > "Additionally, CO line tracers between CO(3–2) and CO(6–5) are used for the molecular gas mass estimates, which add additional uncertainties in the conversion to the ground-state, i.e., to the luminosity of CO(1–0)." (6.1 The resolved star-formation law in ANGELS sources); "Note that the sharp lines in resolved Schmidt-Kennicutt relations in Figure G2, particularly for HerBS-25 and -106, come from the > 1𝜎 criterion." (6.1 The resolved star-formation law in ANGELS sources)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Differential lensing can distort high-J CO SLEDs and line ratios since compact warm regions may be magnified differently from extended cold gas, and spectral lines were not individually lens-modeled.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Converting Band 7 continuum and mid-J CO (up to CO 6-5) pixel-by-pixel into Sigma_SFR and Sigma_H2 assuming a fixed T_d = 35 K and alpha_CO = 4.0 across all pixels ignores spatial gradients in dust temperature, optical depth, and excitation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several reported line detections in Table C1 have integrated flux SNR below 2-3 sigma (e.g., OH+ in HerBS-42A at -0.40 +/- 0.32 Jy km/s) or hit the upper linewidth boundary of 941 km/s.
