## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: Do spiral arms enhance star formation efficiency, or only gather gas? Measured via arm/interarm contrasts in stellar mass, molecular gas, SFR and SFE at ~100 pc in 28 PHANGS spiral galaxies  
  > "Here, we aim to examine this problem in greater detail by assembling a comprehensive set of homogeneous measurements of spiral arm-to-interarm contrasts in stellar mass, gas, SFR, and SFE" (Introduction); "it is still unclear if this results from the mere accumulation of large amounts of gas or if it is due to a more efficient conversion of gas into stars in spiral arms than in interarm regions." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Convert CO(2–1) moment-0 maps to molecular gas surface density**: Use PHANGS-ALMA broad-mask moment-0 maps; apply alpha_CO and a constant R21 to get Sigma_mol  
  > "To determine the CO integrated intensity, we employ the zeroth-order moment maps based on the ‘broad’ masks presented in" (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
   - parameter: R21 = 0.65  
  > "For simplicity, we adopted a constant line ratio of $R_{21}=0.65$" (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
   - parameter: alpha_CO with radial metallicity-gradient trend (PHANGS prescription)  
  > "which includes a radial trend according to the metallicity gradient in galaxies" (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
   - assumption: Purely radial alpha_CO; azimuthal changes give 0.1 dex uncertainty on contrasts  
  > "we conservatively assume a 0.1 dex uncertainty on the molecular surface density contrasts as a consequence of neglecting azimuthal changes in" (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
   - assumption: Global 0.14 dex uncertainty on molecular contrast (alpha_CO and R21 in quadrature)  
  > "we consider a global uncertainty of 0.14 dex in our estimate of molecular gas surface density contrast." (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative Teng et al. velocity-dispersion alpha_CO changes Sigma_mol contrasts by ~10%  
  > "which has an impact of only ${\sim}10$% on $\alpha_{\rm CO}$." (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
2. **Derive SFR contrasts from narrow-band Halpha with an empirical extinction calibration**: Use continuum-subtracted narrow-band Halpha contrasts and map them to extinction-corrected SFR contrasts with a power law fitted to PHANGS-MUSE Balmer-decrement contrasts in 13 galaxies  
  > "We use narrow-band H$\alpha$ observations to trace star formation, and calibrate the impact of extinction using Balmer-corrected H$\alpha$ maps." (2.3 Star formation rate measurements); "Both broad-band and narrow-band images were obtained, which allowed us to derive H$\alpha$ continuum-subtracted images." (2.3 Star formation rate measurements)
   - parameter: log(SFR contrast, ext-corr) = -0.0013 + 1.1799 log(Halpha contrast, no ext)  
  > "$\log\left({\rm SFR_{contrast}^{ext-corr}}\right)=-0.0013 + 1.1799\, \log\left({\rm H\alpha_{contrast}^{no-ext}}\right)$" (Appendix: Calibration of SFR arm/interarm contrasts)
   - assumption: Final uncertainty on SFR contrasts of 0.122 dex (extinction-calibration scatter and narrow-band calibration scatter 0.059 dex added in quadrature)  
  > "Like this, we obtain a final uncertainty of 0.122 dex on the SFR surface density contrasts." (Appendix: Calibration of SFR arm/interarm contrasts)
   - fitting: Power-law fit of MUSE extinction-corrected SFR contrast against MUSE no-extinction Halpha contrast; fitting method not named  
  > "We find that the following power-law captures this systematic amplification very well:" (2.3 Star formation rate measurements)
   - validation: Spearman 0.97 for the calibration relation; narrow-band vs MUSE Halpha contrasts correlate at 0.98 with 0.059 dex scatter  
  > "there is a very good correlation between both variables (Spearman rank coefficient 0.98), with a vertical scatter of 0.059 dex around the 1:1 relation" (Appendix: Calibration of SFR arm/interarm contrasts)
3. **Derive stellar mass surface density contrasts from Spitzer 3.6 micron**: ICA-corrected IRAC 3.6 micron maps converted with a constant M/L; original IRAC maps where S4G is not available  
  > "For the galaxies which are not in S$^4$G, we use the original IRAC $3.6$ $\mu$m maps" (2.5 Stellar mass surface densities)
   - parameter: Spitzer PSF of 1.7 arcsec  
  > "with a PSF of $1.7''$" (2.5 Stellar mass surface densities)
   - assumption: Constant M/L; global 0.11 dex uncertainty on stellar contrasts (0.1 dex M/L plus 0.04 dex ICA, in quadrature)  
  > "Adding both in quadrature, we obtain a global uncertainty of 0.11 dex for the stellar arm/interarm contrasts." (2.5 Stellar mass surface densities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with MUSE stellar-population-fitting contrasts for 13 galaxies; agreement judged excellent  
  > "for 13 galaxies we compare the IRAC contrasts with contrasts measured from the MUSE stellar mass map and we find excellent agreement" (2.5 Stellar mass surface densities)
4. **Define spiral and interarm regions**: Use the broad log-spiral masks of the earlier PHANGS environmental-mask paper (width ~1-2 kpc); interarm is the ring outside the spiral mask at matched radius  
  > "Throughout this paper, we use the spiral component of the environmental masks defined in" (2.6.1 Original spiral masks); "divide it by the mean surface density within the ring outside the spiral mask (interarm value)" (2.7 Strategies to measure arm/interarm contrasts)
   - parameter: typical mask width ~1-2 kpc  
  > "with a typical width of ${\sim}1{-}2$ kpc in order to accommodate most $3.6$ $\mu$m, CO, and H$\alpha$ emission along the arm." (2.6.1 Original spiral masks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Narrow-mask alternative tested in appendix  
  > "we show that contrasts are not strongly affected by the width of the spiral masks" (2.6.1 Original spiral masks)
5. **Measure arm/interarm contrasts in 500 pc radial bins**: Split each galaxy into elliptical annuli of 500 pc width in the galaxy plane; contrast = mean surface density in each spiral segment at that radius divided by mean in the interarm ring; compute for Sigma_star, Sigma_mol, Sigma_SFR and SFE = Sigma_SFR/Sigma_mol  
  > "our nominal method splits each galaxy into a number of radial bins in the plane of the galaxy" (2.7 Strategies to measure arm/interarm contrasts); "which we define as the inverse of the depletion time" (3.1 Range of arm/interarm contrasts)
   - parameter: radial bin width 500 pc; at least 50% of spiral and interarm pixels within the PHANGS-ALMA field of view  
  > "The width of the radial bins is chosen to be 500 pc in the plane of the galaxy to mitigate stochastic sampling effects" (2.7 Strategies to measure arm/interarm contrasts); "At the outermost radial bins, we require at least 50% of the spiral and interarm pixels in the ring to lie within the PHANGS–ALMA field of view for the measurement to be considered." (2.7 Strategies to measure arm/interarm contrasts)
   - assumption: No clipping of intensity maps, so non-detections do not bias the means  
  > "We considered the intensity maps without clipping and, therefore, we should not be biased by non-detections." (2.7 Strategies to measure arm/interarm contrasts)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternatives: 250 and 1000 pc bins, perpendicular boxes along arms, adjacent interarm boxes; results remain qualitatively the same  
  > "Even though the sampling method affects the actual values of the contrasts, our results remain qualitatively the same independently of these different choices." (2.7 Strategies to measure arm/interarm contrasts)
6. **Compare contrast distributions and correlations, and test SFE enhancement**: Medians and 16-84 percentiles over bins; KS test grand-design vs rest, jackknife by galaxy; Spearman correlations and bisector fits of gas/SFR/SFE contrasts versus stellar contrast; bootstrap and half-sample jackknife for SFE median  
  > "We ran a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for the two populations" (3.2 Grand-design spirals show larger contrasts); "In the case of CO, the slope from a bisector fit is $m=1.8$, while for SFR it is even steeper, $m=2.9$." (3.3 Correlations among contrasts); "With a Jackknife approach, if we randomly remove half of the datapoints and repeat the process" (3.4 Star formation efficiency in spiral arms)
   - parameter: SFE bootstrap within error bars, 10 000 realisations  
  > "If we bootstrap the SFE values within their error bars" (3.4 Star formation efficiency in spiral arms)
   - assumption: SFE defined for molecular gas only, SFE = Sigma_SFR/Sigma_mol = 1/tau_dep  
  > "We emphasise that by default we consider the efficiency associated with molecular gas only" (3.4 Star formation efficiency in spiral arms)
   - fitting: Bisector fits; priors and likelihood not applicable  
  > "In the case of CO, the slope from a bisector fit is $m=1.8$, while for SFR it is even steeper, $m=2.9$." (3.3 Correlations among contrasts)
   - validation: Jackknife and bootstrap confirm SFE median above unity at about 4 sigma  
  > "we confirm that the enhancement of the median with respect to unity is statistically significant" (3.4 Star formation efficiency in spiral arms)
7. **Add atomic gas to the contrasts**: Convert HI intensity to Sigma_atom (optically thin 21 cm, with 1.36 helium factor, cos i correction); for 15 galaxies with HI better than 2.5 kpc, match CO to the HI resolution and compute total-gas and SFE contrasts  
  > "We apply the following equation to transform H I observations to atomic gas surface density, which assumes an optically thin 21 cm emission:" (2.4 H I data); "We limit our analysis to the cases where H I achieves a physical resolution of 2.5 kpc or better" (3.5 Atomic gas contrast and its effect on SFE)
   - parameter: Sigma_atom = 2.0e-2 x I_HI cos i (M_sun pc^-2, I_HI in K km/s), includes 1.36 factor  
  > "where $\Sigma_\mathrm{atom}$ includes the 1.36 factor to account for helium and heavier elements." (2.4 H I data)
   - assumption: Optically thin 21 cm emission; inclinations from the PHANGS sample table  
  > "which assumes an optically thin 21 cm emission:" (2.4 H I data)
   - fitting: Power-law fit of HI+H2 contrast versus H2 contrast  
  > "The relation between H I+H$_2$ and H$_2$ contrast can be fitted by a power-law with slope $0.67$" (3.5 Atomic gas contrast and its effect on SFE)
   - validation: Authors caution that coarse HI resolution may wash out effects  
  > "We caution that the limited resolution of the H I observations might wash out some of these effects." (3.5 Atomic gas contrast and its effect on SFE)
8. **Test robustness to resolution, mask width, DIG and binning**: Repeat contrast measurement after convolving to 1.5 kpc; with narrow masks (ridge of peak CO or Halpha dilated with 7.5 arcsec FWHM Gaussian, threshold 0.01, union of CO and Halpha masks); with different bins; test DIG with PHANGS-MUSE HII region masks  
  > "on which we impose a threshold of 0.01" (2.6.2 A set of thinner spiral masks); "We take the union of the two masks to yield a final narrow mask." (2.6.2 A set of thinner spiral masks); "we performed a test using the PHANGS–MUSE data, for which H II region masks are available" (Appendix: DIG)
   - parameter: Narrow-mask kernel FWHM = 7.5 arcsec, threshold 0.01  
  > "on which we impose a threshold of 0.01" (2.6.2 A set of thinner spiral masks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Narrow masks change molecular and SFR contrasts by ~10-20% and SFE by ~4%; resolution is the dominant effect  
  > "The resulting SFE is just $4$% higher for the narrow masks" (4.4 Methodology and measurement caveats)

## Qualitative findings

- Stellar contrasts are modest, but gas and SFR contrasts are much larger  
  > "The arm/interarm enhancement in stellar mass surface density" (Summary and conclusions)
- Grand-design spirals show larger contrasts in stellar mass, molecular gas and SFR, but not in SFE  
  > "This is not the case for SFE contrasts, though ($p=0.49$), which do not show a significant difference for grand-design spirals." (3.2 Grand-design spirals show larger contrasts)
- SFE of molecular gas is higher in arms in only about 6 of 10 bins; spirals gather gas but the triggering effect on molecular SFE is very limited except for the largest stellar contrasts  
  > "Our observations do not support the simple, naive picture where spiral arms always increase the efficiency by which molecular gas forms stars." (4.3 Considering whether the SFE is enhanced in spiral arms)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: PHANGS provides ALMA CO at cloud scale plus matching MUSE, Halpha, HST and JWST data for nearby galaxies  
  > "PHANGS provides a unique view on the distribution of molecular clouds in galaxies, their collapse to form stars, and the feedback associated with the star formation process." (Introduction)
- Why this method: A single consistent arm definition and homogeneous calibrations are needed to compare SFE fairly across galaxies  
  > "To unambiguously determine the efficiency of star formation in spiral arms, it is crucial to first control for as many independent factors as possible." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Narrow spiral masks (about half the area) tested; not used for main results because contrasts barely change  
  > "These masks are not used for the main measurements in this paper" (2.6.2 A set of thinner spiral masks)
- Alternative: Median rather than mean surface density in bins; mean preferred as more robust, median does not change conclusions  
  > "Using the median instead of the mean does not qualitatively affect our conclusions." (Footnote 5)
- Alternative: Treating Halpha as proportional to SFR (no extinction calibration) removes the SFE enhancement  
  > "If we were to take SFR as directly proportional to H$\alpha$, then the SFE enhancement would completely vanish" (3.4 Star formation efficiency in spiral arms)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): HI maps have coarse resolution (10-60 arcsec), so atomic-gas results are only at kpc scales  
  > "We caution that the limited resolution of the H I observations might wash out some of these effects." (3.5 Atomic gas contrast and its effect on SFE)
- Limitation (authors): Sample limited to galaxies with long continuous spiral segments, mostly grand-design  
  > "This choice limits our study to galaxies with long and continuous spiral segments" (2 Data and methods)
- Limitation (authors): Azimuthal variations of alpha_CO are not negligible; a 0.1 dex uncertainty is assumed  
  > "However, azimuthal variations in the real $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ are not completely negligible" (2.2 Molecular gas surface densities)
- Limitation (authors): Gas flows, shear and magnetic fields were not examined  
  > "Here we have not examined effects such as detailed gas flows, shear patterns, or magnetic fields within spirals" (4.3 Considering whether the SFE is enhanced in spiral arms)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SFE conclusion depends on an empirical Halpha extinction calibration derived on 13 galaxies and applied to all, plus a fixed alpha_CO and R21.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Arm/interarm definition rests on pre-existing masks whose interarm ring includes inner rings and bar ends, which biases SFR contrasts low at small radii.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Radial bins within a galaxy are not independent, so the 4 sigma significance on the median SFE contrast probably overstates the evidence.

- Products in archive: _not stated_

- Data volume (inferred): Small if only public PHANGS-ALMA moment-0 products (a few GB for 28 galaxies) plus masks; far larger (TB scale) if reducing raw ALMA data, which the paper does not do
- Score (inferred): 3/5, Public CO products and published masks allow a partial redo, but Halpha maps, SFR calibration details and several choices are inherited or in prep., and no code is released.
- Rediscovery candidate: no, The ALMA CO products and masks are public and results are numeric, but the Halpha SFR maps are unpublished and recipe details are missing, so a full redo is not possible from the paper alone.
