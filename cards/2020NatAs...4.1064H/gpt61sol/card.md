## Qualitative findings

- Velocity fluctuations occur throughout the selected molecular ISM environments.  
  > "First, we identify ubiquitous velocity fluctuations throughout the molecular interstellar medium." (Summary of the results and physical interpretation)
- Filamentary regions have characteristic velocity wavelengths matching density-enhancement spacing within uncertainties.  
  > "In these regions, the separation between regularly-spaced density enhancements agrees (within the uncertainties) with the characteristic wavelength of the oscillations detected in velocity." (Summary of the results and physical interpretation)
- Selected GMC density and velocity fluctuations lack a characteristic scale.  
  > "the density and velocity fluctuations observed throughout both of our selected GMCs do not show any characteristic scale." (Origins of scale-free density and velocity fluctuations)
- Periodic velocity oscillations likely trace longitudinal convergence in CMZ and GMC filament, and radial convergence in the spiral arm.  
  > "the periodic velocity oscillations are likely the result of convergent motion, either longitudinal convergence as in the case of the CMZ and the GMC filament, or radial convergence as in the case of the spiral arm of NGC 4321." (Origins of the periodic velocity fluctuations)
- Gravitational instabilities are a plausible source of periodic density structure.  
  > "gravitational instabilities represent a plausible source of the periodic structure identified in this study" (Origins of the periodic density fluctuations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Span four orders of magnitude and compare filamentary regions with quasi-periodic peaks against disordered GMCs.  
  > "Our observations cover a spatial dynamic range of about four orders of magnitude, from kpc scales in the nearby galaxy NGC 4321 down to 0.1 pc scales in the Milky Way." (Environment selection); "they are highly filamentary and (qualitatively) interspersed with quasi-periodic intensity peaks along their crests" (Data selection for statistical analysis); "We further select two GMCs, one in the Galactic disc and one in the Milky Way’s CMZ, the intensity profiles of which are complex and disordered." (Data selection for statistical analysis)
- Why this method: Spectral decomposition separates individual emission features; structure-function minima reveal periodicity.  
  > "moment analysis, which computes intensity-weighted average quantities, but does not describe the properties of individual emission features where multiple components are evident." (An introduction to spectral decomposition); "The structure function of a periodic quantity will show minima located at the wavelength of the function, as well as at integer multiples of that wavelength" (Structure functions and their application to toy models)
- Alternative: Moment analysis does not distinguish individual overlapping emission features.  
  > "moment analysis, which computes intensity-weighted average quantities, but does not describe the properties of individual emission features where multiple components are evident." (An introduction to spectral decomposition)
- Alternative: Use GaussPy+ instead of the ScousePy approach for the large, complex GRS data set.  
  > "The size and complexity of the GRS data set means that a different approach is required." (An introduction to spectral decomposition); "Here, we employ the bottom-up methodology of GaussPy+(cite: riener19), which is specifically designed for the fitting of survey data." (An introduction to spectral decomposition)
- Alternative: Exclude Herschel 70 μm from modified blackbody fitting because of possible small warm-grain contamination.  
  > "The 70 $\mu$m band was excluded from the fit due to possible contamination from very small, warm dust grains." (Observations and data — Central Molecular Zone (CMZ))
- Alternative: Subtract the structure function of noise to account more accurately for spatially correlated observational noise.  
  > "Observational data will usually have spatial correlations from the beam and scanning effects that will more accurately be accounted for by subtracting the structure function of the noise" (The impact of noise)
- Alternative: GMC scaling can also be interpreted as global virialisation or global collapse.  
  > "It has been cited as evidence for the global virialisation of molecular clouds(cite: heyer09) as well as the global collapse of clouds(cite: ballesteros-paredes11), in addition to the universality of supersonic turbulence(cite: larson81)." (Origins of scale-free density and velocity fluctuations)
- How they decided it was enough: Limit 1-D lags to half crest length; restrict GMC fits between beam and turnover; require cross-correlation significance >3.  
  > "the maximum, fully sampled lag that fits within these 1-D data sets is half the total length of each crest." (Analysis of filamentary structures); "the approximate scale above which the structure functions begin to turn over" (Analysis of multi-dimensional structures); "all significant ($XC(\ensuremath{\boldsymbol\ell})/\sigma_{XC}(\ensuremath{\boldsymbol\ell}))>3$) peaks in the cross-correlation function" (Analysis of filamentary structures)
- Limitation (authors): Observational density and velocity estimators can alter inferred structure functions.  
  > "since the velocity centroid is an emission weighted estimate of the velocity field, features in the structure function may be altered by the interaction between the velocity and the density fields." (Structure functions and their application to toy models)
- Limitation (authors): Mock-field tests lack physical excitation and optical-depth treatment.  
  > "We note that these are simple mock data models without underlying physical basis and no treatment of excitation conditions or optical depth effects." (2-D models)
- Limitation (authors): Randomly generated periodicity does not establish a physical origin.  
  > "If periodicity exists within data, even if it is generated randomly, it will be seen as an oscillation in the structure function." (1-D models); "care should be taken when interpreting the physical origins of periodicity in real data" (1-D models)
- Limitation (authors): Simple cylindrical fragmentation omits shear and magnetic fields.
