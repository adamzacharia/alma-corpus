## Analysis recipe

1. **Compare molecular gas and dust morphology**: Overlay ALMA CO(1-0) contours on the HST image; shift the HST–ALMA registration to align dust lanes and molecular gas.  
  > "The green contours in the right panel of Fig. [1](#Fig.1) correspond to the CO(1-0) emission detected with the Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA)." (DESI, *HST*, and ALMA data); "Since the astrometry of HST is not accurate, we applied a shift between the HST image and the ALMA CO map so that the dust lanes coincide with the molecular gas" (DESI, *HST*, and ALMA data)
   - assumption: Dust lanes normally coincide with molecular gas in spiral galaxies.  
  > "so that the dust lanes coincide with the molecular gas, which is normally the case in spiral galaxies (cite: bolatto17; ramos2022)." (DESI, *HST*, and ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Prepare and bin the MUSE spectra**: Process the reduced MUSE cube with GIST; mask spaxels and make separate stellar and emission-line Voronoi binnings.  
  > "We used the galaxy IFU spectroscopic tool (`GIST`), version 3.1.0 to process the data cube." (MUSE data processing); "Spatial masking was performed to remove defunct spaxels and those with a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) below the given threshold before we binned the data." (MUSE data processing); "we therefore used two binnings produced with the Voronoi binning code written by (cite: cappellari2003adaptive)." (MUSE data processing)
   - software: GIST, Voronoi binning code
   - parameter: 4750–7000 Å; ignore 5780–6050 Å affected by laser guide stars.  
  > "The input data cube was read using `GIST` and the wavelength range is shortened to $4750\,\r{A}- 7000\,\r{A}$." (MUSE data processing); "Above $7000\,\r{A}$, there is contamination due to strong sky lines, and the range $5780\,\r{A} - 6050\,\r{A}$, which is affected by the laser guide stars was,ignored." (MUSE data processing)
   - parameter: Stellar S/N = 50: 426 bins; emission-line S/N = 100: 762 bins.  
  > "We required an ${\rm S/N}=50$ per stellar bin and a total of 426 Voronoi bins were generated." (MUSE data processing); "For the emission line binning, we required an ${\rm S/N}=100$ and in this case, a total of 762 Voronoi bins were generated." (MUSE data processing)
   - parameter: Stellar S/N window: 5580–5680 Å; emission binning based on continuum-subtracted [O III] λ5007.  
  > "For the stellar component we computed the median S/N from the rest-frame wavelength range 5580 – 5680 Å, which does not include any strong emission lines." (MUSE data processing); "The emission line or ionised gas binning was made based on a \[O III\] $\lambda$5007 continuum-subtracted image." (MUSE data processing)
   - parameter: [O III] image: 10 Å line window minus an equal-width window 50 Å redwards.  
  > "We built the image by integrating the data cube flux in a $10\,\AA$ window centred on the \[O III\] $\lambda$5007 rest-frame wavelength of NGC 4593, and by subtracting the integrated flux in a window of equal width but $50\,\AA$ redwards." (MUSE data processing)
   - parameter: Spaxel S/N > 0.1 for emission and > 1.0 for stars; minimum 50 contiguous spaxels.  
  > "We considered spaxels with an S/N greater than 0.1 and 1.0 for the emission line and stellar binning, respectively." (MUSE data processing); "To avoid these spaxels from being accreted into Voronoi bins we modified GIST to consider clusters with a minimum of 50 contiguous spaxels for the binning." (MUSE data processing)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure stellar rotation and dispersion**: pPXF full spectral fitting to extract stellar velocity and velocity dispersion.  
  > "We used the penalized pixel-fitting (`pPXF`) method to extract the stellar velocity field and velocity dispersion." (MUSE data processing)
   - software: pPXF, GIST
   - parameter: Initial dispersion 50 km s^-1; eighth-degree additive Legendre polynomial.  
  > "The initial guess for the velocity dispersion was $\mathrm{50\,km~s^{-1}}$ and the stellar continuum was modelled with an eighth-degree additive Legendre polynomial." (MUSE data processing)
   - assumption: E-MILES templates, BaSTI isochrones, Kroupa universal IMF and solar abundances.  
  > "We used the E-MILES library ((cite: vazdekis2016uv)) templates together with BaSTI isochrones ((cite: pietrinferni2004large)), a Kroupa universal stellar initial mass function ((cite: kroupa2001variation)), and solar abundances." (MUSE data processing)
   - fitting: Linear combination of template spectral energy distributions broadened to match the LOSVD.  
  > "The code performs a full spectral fitting of each stellar emission bin with a linear combination of template spectral energy distributions widened to match the line-of-sight velocity distribution (LOSVD) of the spectral lines." (MUSE data processing)
   - validation: Rotation amplitude matches Barbosa et al.; central approximately 0.7″ cannot be fitted accurately.  
  > "with a velocity amplitude of $\pm110\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}$ (see Fig. [2](#Fig.2)), which matches the findings by (cite: barbosa2006gemini)." (Stellar kinematics); "We were unable to accurately fit the stellar kinematics in the central $\sim 0.7\ensuremath{''}$ (120 pc) of the galaxy" (Stellar kinematics)
4. **Separate disc and outflow kinematics**: Fit narrow and broad Gaussian components to [O III] λ5007 at bin level with PyGandALF.  
  > "the Gas and Absorption Line Fitting`PyGandALF` module (cite: sarzi2006sauron; falcon2006sauron) from the `GIST` pipeline, which was run on the bin level to perform the emission line analysis." (MUSE data processing); "Therefore, we performed two Gaussian fits, narrow and broad, which correspond to the narrow and outflow components, respectively." (Emission lines)
   - software: PyGandALF, GIST
   - parameter: Initial velocity/dispersion: narrow 0/100 km s^-1; broad outflow and BLR −500/500 km s^-1.  
  > "The initial guess for the velocity and velocity dispersion is 0 and $\mathrm{100\,km~s^{-1}}$ for the narrow-component, while for the broad outflow and BLR components, the initial guess is -500 and $\mathrm{500\,km~s^{-1}}$ respectively." (MUSE data processing)
   - parameter: Use pPXF stellar fit multiplied by a second-order Legendre polynomial for the stellar and AGN continuum.  
  > "In any case, we used the stellar fit from `pPXF` as input to `PyGandALF`, multiplied by a second-order Legendre polynomial." (MUSE data processing); "This component accounts for the underlying stellar and AGN continuum." (MUSE data processing)
   - assumption: [O III] broadening traces kinematically disrupted gas rather than the BLR.  
  > "Hence, any broadening of the \[O III\] line profiles will be associated with kinematically disrupted gas, and not with the BLR." (MUSE data processing)
   - fitting: Fixed theoretical doublet flux ratios; line kinematics tied to [O III].  
  > "We reduced the number of degrees of freedom in the fits by fixing the flux ratios of several doublets to their theoretically estimated values ((cite: storey2000theoretical)), which are listed in Table [1](#table 1)." (MUSE data processing); "We also tied the kinematics of all the lines considered here to those of \[O III\], since otherwise it would be impossible to successfully fit the H$\alpha$+\[N II\] blend." (MUSE data processing)
   - validation: Blueshifted broad emission coincides with high velocity dispersion, confirming the previously tentative outflow.  
  > "This coincides with a region of high velocity dispersion (up to 250 km s$^{-1}$), confirming the presence of outflowing gas, as first tentatively reported by (cite: ruschel2021agnifs)." (Emission lines)
5. **Map ionisation mechanisms**: Resolved BPT diagrams from narrow [N II]/Hα and [O III]/Hβ flux ratios; map η.  
  > "The maps used the \[N II\]$\,\lambda$6583/H$\alpha$ and \[O III\]$\,\lambda$5007/H$\beta$ narrow line flux ratios." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms); "We used the parameter $\eta$ defined by (cite: erroz2019muse) as an indicator of the strength of the ionisation mechanism, given by the distance of a given point to the curves." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)
   - software: PyGandALF, GIST
   - parameter: Single narrow Gaussian for [O III] and [N II]; narrow and broad Gaussians for Hα and Hβ.  
  > "we fitted the \[O III\] $\lambda$5007 and \[N II\] $\lambda$6583 lines with a narrow Gaussian component, and H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$ with two (narrow and broad) Gaussians." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)
   - parameter: Star formation: η < −0.5; AGN: η > 0.5; intermediate region composite.  
  > "The region below the dashed line is considered to be ionised by star formation ($\eta < -0.5$), and the region above the solid line is ionised by the AGN ($\eta > 0.5$)." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms); "The region between the two lines is considered to be composite and is ionised by a mix of the two mechanisms." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)
   - assumption: Broad Balmer component is BLR emission; narrow Balmer component combines disc and outflow.  
  > "The broad-component fitted to H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$ corresponds to emission from the BLR, and the narrow Gaussian includes the disc and outflow components." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)
   - fitting: Do not separate disc and outflow in the Balmer narrow component because BLR emission prevents a good three-component separation.  
  > "We did not consider the latter as two separate kinematic components as we did in previous sections because the BLR component makes it impossible to achieve a good separation into three components for the H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$ lines." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Estimate ionised outflow mass**: Fit outflow Hβ intensity with [O III] kinematics fixed; use integrated broad [S II] emission to estimate density; apply the Rose et al. Hβ mass estimator.  
  > "To measure the H$\beta$ line flux and hence obtain its luminosity, we first tied its kinematics to those of \[O III\]$\,\lambda$5007 to reduce the uncertainties." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "Using this spectrum and the flux of the broad-components that we fitted, we obtained an electron density of $n_{\rm e}= 400\,{\rm cm}^3$." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "To estimate the total mass of the outflow $M_{\rm out}$, we used Eq. [\[Eq.1\]](#Eq.1) in (cite: rose2018quantifying)," (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
   - parameter: Integrated [S II] spectrum in a nuclear aperture of diameter 2″.  
  > "We thus constructed an integrated \[S II\] spectrum within a diameter of $2\ensuremath{''}$, centred on the galaxy nucleus (see Fig. [7](#fig:7)) as shown in (cite: sanders2015mosdef)." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
   - assumption: Case B coefficient 3.03 × 10^-14 cm^3 s^-1; Te = 10^4 K.  
  > "is the effective Case B coefficient (we adopted $\mathrm {3.03 \times 10 ^{-14}\,cm^{3}\,s^{-1}}$), which corresponds to an electron temperature $T_{\rm e} = 10^4\,{\rm K}$ (cite: osterbrock2006astrophysics)" (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
   - assumption: No extinction correction can be determined; Hβ luminosity and derived quantities are lower limits.  
  > "As a result, the H$\beta$ luminosity that we measure, $4.7\times10^{39}$ erg s$^{-1}$, and all the derived quantities are lower limits." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
   - fitting: Hβ intensity is the only free parameter after tying its kinematics to [O III].  
  > "We then fitted the emission line profile leaving the intensity as the only free parameter" (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "To measure the H$\beta$ line flux and hence obtain its luminosity, we first tied its kinematics to those of \[O III\]$\,\lambda$5007 to reduce the uncertainties." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Calculate outflow rate and energetics**: Use Ṁ = Mout Vout / Rout; kinetic power = Ṁout(Vout² + 3σ²)/2; divide kinetic power by AGN bolometric luminosity for coupling efficiency.  
  > "\dot{M} = \frac{M_{\rm out} {V_{\rm out}}}{R_{\rm out}}~," (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "\dot{E}_{\rm kin} = \frac{\dot{M}_{\rm out}}{2} (V_{\rm out}^2 + 3\sigma^2)," (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "To estimate the coupling efficiency, we divided the outflow kinetic energy by the bolometric luminosity of the AGN" (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
   - parameter: Vout approximately 200 km s^-1: maximum measured blueshifted outflow velocity.  
  > "where $V_{\rm out}$ is the outflow velocity, which we estimated as the maximum velocity that we measured for the outflowing gas." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "In this case it is $\sim200$ km s$^{-1}$, derived from the blue-shifted broad-components shown in the middle panel of Fig. [4](#Fig.4)." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA CO(1-0) traces dusty spiral arms.  
  > "The ALMA CO(1-0) contours shown in the right panel of Fig. [1](#Fig.1), which probe cold molecular gas with an angular resolution similar to that of the MUSE data, trace the dusty spiral arms of the galaxy" (Morphology)
- An inner spiral reaches the nucleus; central gas accumulation may fuel future nuclear activity or star formation.  
  > "We can clearly distinguish the innermost spiral arm originating in the south, which surrounds the nucleus in anti-clockwise direction, until it reaches the nucleus through the north." (Discussion); "The central region of the galaxy is most likely accumulating gas that will eventually fuel future episodes of nuclear activity and/or star formation." (Discussion)
- The circumnuclear stellar population is supported by rotation.  
  > "show that the circumnuclear stellar population is supported by rotation with a velocity amplitude of $\pm110\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}$" (Stellar kinematics)
- The detected outflow is probably the approaching side; the disc may obscure its receding counterpart.  
  > "Considering the almost face-on orientation of NGC 4593, we most likely see the approaching side of the outflow, and its potentially red-shifted counterpart is hidden by the galaxy disc." (Emission lines)
- AGN photoionisation dominates; composite spaxels occur beyond approximately 2″.  
  > "indicates that the bulk of the narrow-line emission measured from the MUSE data is gas that is photoionised by the AGN, with a few spaxels lying in the composite region of the diagram." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms); "The spaxels lying in the composite region of the diagram ($-0.5<\eta<0.5$) are found at radii larger than $\sim$2$''$(340 pc)." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High spatial resolution is required to study gas transport and feedback near the nucleus; AO IFS resolves central tens of parsecs.  
  > "This study requires observations with a very high spatial resolution (e.g.- (cite: comeron2021complex; bessiere2022; ramos2022))." (Introduction); "With the advent of AO-supported integral field spectroscopy (IFS), the detailed study of the central tens of parsecs of many galaxies becomes possible." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Use [O III] to separate disrupted gas from BLR emission; tie other line kinematics to reduce fitting freedom and handle the Hα+[N II] blend.  
  > "Hence, any broadening of the \[O III\] line profiles will be associated with kinematically disrupted gas, and not with the BLR." (MUSE data processing); "We also tied the kinematics of all the lines considered here to those of \[O III\], since otherwise it would be impossible to successfully fit the H$\alpha$+\[N II\] blend." (MUSE data processing)
- Alternative: Spatially resolved [S II] density map rejected because S/N was insufficient; use an integrated nuclear spectrum.  
  > "To estimate the electron density, we first attempted to create a map of the ratio \[S II\] $\lambda$6716/\[S II\] $\lambda$6731, but the S/N of the data was not high enough." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas); "We thus constructed an integrated \[S II\] spectrum within a diameter of $2\ensuremath{''}$, centred on the galaxy nucleus (see Fig. [7](#fig:7)) as shown in (cite: sanders2015mosdef)." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
- Alternative: Separate disc, outflow and BLR Balmer components rejected for BPT analysis because a good three-component separation was impossible.  
  > "We did not consider the latter as two separate kinematic components as we did in previous sections because the BLR component makes it impossible to achieve a good separation into three components for the H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$ lines." (Resolved BPT diagrams: Ionisation mechanisms)
- Alternative: Hα/Hβ extinction determination was not possible because BLR emission is present throughout the field through PSF smearing.  
  > "from the data we could not determine the extinction using the H$\beta$ and H$\alpha$ lines because the emission from the BLR is present everywhere in the field due to point-spread function (PSF) smearing." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Central approximately 0.7″ stellar kinematics cannot be fitted accurately because AGN emission dilutes stellar absorption.  
  > "We were unable to accurately fit the stellar kinematics in the central $\sim 0.7\ensuremath{''}$ (120 pc) of the galaxy" (Stellar kinematics); "because the line and continuum emission from the AGN are so strong that the stellar absorption lines are completely diluted." (Stellar kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Unknown extinction makes outflow mass, mass-loss rate, kinetic energy and coupling efficiency lower limits.  
  > "Since we cannot constrain the outflow extinction, we derived lower limits to the outflow mass, mass-loss rate, kinetic energy, and coupling efficiency." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): The minimum cluster size of 50 spaxels is arbitrary.  
  > "The number of 50 spaxels is arbitrary." (MUSE data processing)
- Limitation (authors): The Hβ outflow component is buried beneath a broad component with FWHM approximately 1600 km s^-1.  
  > "where the blue-shifted component representing the outflow is buried below the broad-component of ${\rm FWHM}\sim$1600 km s$^{-1}$." (Physical properties of the outflowing gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust–CO coincidence is partly imposed by the astrometric alignment; it is not an independent registration check.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA morphology alone does not measure molecular inflow or establish an inflow rate.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The methods section prints electron-density units as cm^3, while the discussion gives cm^-3.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The coupling-efficiency lower limit depends on which of the two quoted AGN luminosities is used.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The kinetic-power calculation calls approximately 250 km s^-1 an average dispersion, whereas the kinematic discussion describes it as a maximum.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties are reported for density, outflow radius, mass-loss rate or kinetic power.
