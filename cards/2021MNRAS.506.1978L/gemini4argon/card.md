## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, average, realign, and image ALMA Band 6 and Band 7 continuum data**: Calibrate visibilities with ALMA pipeline, flag outlying points in Band 6, time-average (10s) and channel-average (4 channels/spw) Band 7 data, reweight with statwt, realign phase centres to Gaia DR2 positions using fixvis and fixplanets, concatenate 2016 and 2018 Band 7 datasets with concat, and image using tclean (natural for Band 6; Briggs robust=1.0 and 0.5'' uv-taper for Band 7).  
  > "Continuum imaging was conducted using the CASA $\rm{tclean}$ algorithm with natural weighting (to enhance S/N), shown in Figure 1 (bottom) in which the disc is clearly detected." (ALMA Band 6 Observations); "Both Band 7 data sets were time averaged to 10-second widths, channel averaged into 4 channels per spectral window, and the CASA $\rm{statwt}$ task was run to estimate the visibility weights based on the measured variance." (ALMA Band 7 Observations); "Since the *ALMA* observation phase centres were not perfectly aligned with the expected stellar position from *Gaia* DR2, we realigned to these coordinates using the CASA task $\rm{fixvis}$. We then used the CASA task $\rm{fixplanets}$ to shift the coordinates of the 2018 observations to coincide with the 2016 measurement set phase centre (see Table 2 for these expected DR2 coordinates). The two resulting measurement set epochs were then combined with the CASA task $\rm{concat}$." (ALMA Band 7 Observations)
   - software: CASA, plotms, flagdata, statwt, fixvis, fixplanets, concat, tclean
   - parameter: Time averaging = 10 s, channel averaging = 4 channels per spw (Band 7)

## Qualitative findings

- The q1 Eri outer debris belt is broad (extending from at least 34 to 134 au, FWHM width ~43 au), highly inclined (~77-79 deg), and peaks in brightness at ~81.6 au.  
  > "**O1 - Broad Structure**: q$^1$ Eri has a bright, broad disc, with a position angle $\rm{PA}{=}57.0{\pm}1.0^{\circ}$, and an inclination $i{=}76.7{\pm}1.0^{\circ}$. This emission peaks at a radius of $81.6{\pm}0.5$ au, and has a half-maximum width of $W_{\rm{HM}}{=}43{\pm}4$ au, although likely extends from at least $34{-}134$ au." (Summary of Observational Analysis)
- The millimetre continuum emission is asymmetric along the major axis, with the SW inner edge brighter and closer to the star than the NE inner edge, and ~20% more integrated flux in the SW half.  
  > "**O2 - Major Axis Brightness Asymmetry**: the major axis emission brightness profile is asymmetric. At the same projected radii, the SW inner edge is brighter than the NE, but the outer edge is comparable on both sides." (Summary of Observational Analysis); "**O3 - Major Axis Flux Asymmetry**: the total Band 7 fluxes between $30{-}82$ au and $30{-}200$ au are greater on the SW side of the disc than on the NE side by fractional amounts $f_{\rm{82au}}{=}1.21{\pm}0.06$ and $f_{\rm{200au}}{=}1.23{\pm}0.04$ respectively." (Summary of Observational Analysis)
- HST scattered light emission is radially extended further to the NE than the SW and is brighter on the SE side of the minor axis, suggesting the SE side is the near side of the disc.  
  > "**O4 - Scattered Light**: the scattered light data shows the disc brightness to be radially and azimuthally asymmetric. We showed that dust emission has a significant radial offset towards the NE of the star for the 22 mag arcsec$^{-2}$ emission contour lines, $R_{\rm{scat}}{=}0.081{\pm}0.011$, at an average projected radius of ${\sim}\rm{108\,au}$. We also found the SE side of the disc to be brighter than the NW, suggestive that the SE edge is on the disc’s near-side to us." (Summary of Observational Analysis)
- The known radial-velocity planet q1 Eri b at ~2 au is too close to the star to sculpt the outer belt at ~57 au; instead, an unseen planet of 8 M_earth to 11 M_Jup near ~60 au that migrated outwards and trapped planetesimals in resonance can simultaneously explain the sharp inner edge, the SW mm clump, and the NE scattered light extension.  
  > "At just ${\sim}2$ au, the known planet q$^1$ Eri b is too close in to affect the observed outer main belt, and we discuss how the broad disc may be connected to other aspects of this planetary system, such as other potential planets and planetesimal collisions. One interpretation of this system is that the belt of planetesimals had its inner edge carved by a planet which formed a clump on the inner edge whilst migrating, similar to the inferred evolution of Neptune. This scenario may simultaneously explain the inner edge sharpness, the SW ansa sub-mm clump and the scattered light asymmetry on the outer edge in the NE." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sub-arcsecond ALMA Band 6 and Band 7 observations provide five times better resolution (~0.8'') than Herschel (~4'') and avoid the HST coronagraphic inner working angle obscuration (~2.25''), enabling direct constraints on the debris belt's inner edge, asymmetries, and the inner regions near the star.  
  > "Combining these data provides a resolution of ${\sim}0.8''$, five times better than that achieved by *Herschel*, sufficient to constrain the inner edge of the debris disc and observe the previously unresolved inner regions." (Introduction); "Until now the debris disc architecture has been interpreted from low resolution thermal imaging (${\sim}4 \ensuremath{''}$) and scattered light HST imaging with a poor inner working angle (${\sim}2.25\ensuremath{''}$)." (Conclusions)
- Why this method: Combined 2016 and 2018 Band 7 visibilities after Gaia DR2 proper-motion correction to improve S/N and resolution; applied a 0.5'' uv-taper to raise S/N per beam by ~50% for image-plane analysis; fitted parametric 3D models directly in visibility space with rescaled statwt weights (f_sigma) to avoid deconvolution biases; used spectro-spatial Keplerian filtering to maximise sensitivity to faint CO emission.  
  > "To improve the S/N and resolution of the Band 7 data, we combined the two Band 7 data sets." (ALMA Band 7 Observations); "Prior to conducting any further image analysis we raised the S/N per beam (by ${\sim}50\%$) of the Band 7 data by applying a uv-taper of $0.5''$, effectively increasing the synthesised beam to ${\sim}0.82\times0.75''$, shown in Fig. 4." (Continuum Analysis); "To measure the line profiles, we applied the spectro-spatial filtering method of (cite: Matra15; Matra17)." (CO J=3-2 Spectral Line Analysis)
- Alternative: Constant-eccentricity debris disc model without a clump (model ECC): tested via MCMC visibility fitting and rejected because it leaves >3 sigma residuals in the image (chi^2_nu,Im = 1.72) and poorly reproduces the SW/NE flux ratios.  
  > "We therefore assess the CL and CL+ECC residual maps as being consistent with a map comprised entirely of noise (i.e., given their $\chi_{\rm{Im}}^2$ values are both 1.13), but that both the ECC and SYM residual maps are inconsistent." (Model Results)
- Alternative: Recent massive planetesimal collision as the origin of the SW clump: considered and deemed unlikely because producing 0.0029 M_earth of mm dust requires a parent body >>2300 km and a total disc mass >=300-3000 M_earth to achieve a reasonable detection probability.  
  > "Therefore, whilst a collisional origin for this asymmetry could explain the observed asymmetry in both the sub-mm and scattered light, it is perhaps unlikely." (A recent collision?)
- Alternative: Background sub-millimetre galaxy (SMG) causing the SW clump: considered and deemed unlikely due to a ~0.2% chance alignment probability, the extended (>0.7'') nature of the clump, and its inability to explain the NE scattered light asymmetry.  
  > "Whilst we cannot exclude the possibility that the clump is due to extra-galactic emission, this interpretation seems less likely than a planet-driven asymmetry (see $\S$5.2.1) since this cannot simultaneously explain the scattered light asymmetry and such an SMG would have a very low occurrence rate." (Clumpiness from extra-galactic emission?)
- Alternative: ISM interaction causing the scattered light asymmetry independently of the sub-mm asymmetry: noted as a possibility that cannot be ruled out, though a single planet-driven mechanism is preferred on probabilistic grounds.  
  > "Whilst a single mechanism may still however be preferable for probabilistic reasons, we cannot rule out the possibility that the asymmetries observed in the scattered light and the sub-mm are independent, i.e., with both ISM interactions and planet-disc interactions affecting these respectively." (Interactions with the ISM?)
- Alternative: Sub-mm stellar flares or noise causing the central 0.86 mm excess: considered alongside an inner warm planetesimal belt and kept open because sub-mm flares on solar-type stars or noise/minor-axis contamination cannot be excluded with current data.  
  > "Therefore, we equally cannot rule out the possibility that this warm excess is due to stellar variability from sub-mm flares." (What is the Inner Warm Component?)
- How they decided it was enough: Used 120-180 MCMC walkers and 600-2000 steps (with 250-1200 burn-in steps) to ensure parameter convergence, and generated N = 3000 simulated beam-convolved noise maps to test whether residual map reduced chi^2 values were consistent with pure noise.  
  > "For these MCMC routines, between 140-200 walkers and 600-2000 steps were used to ensure parameter solutions were converged on, with burn in lengths varying between 250 and 1200." (Model Results); "By producing $N{=}3000$ maps of noise convolved with a beam equal to that in our residual maps (with a resulting rms equal to $\rm{15.9\,\mu Jy\,beam^{-1}}$), we found $\chi_{\rm{Im}}^2$ exceeded 1.13 in ${\sim}4\%$ of all iterations" (Model Results)
- Limitation (authors): The modified blackbody parameters (lambda_0 and beta_0) for the inner warm component are poorly constrained because the sub-mm slope relies only on low-S/N Band 6 and Band 7 inner excesses.  
  > "However, the parameters defining the blackbody modification are not well constrained (i.e., due to uncertainty on the stellar photosphere and given that the two Band 6 and Band 7 data points these model have only low signal-to-noise excesses) and thus there are a broad range of values for which $\lambda_0$ and $\beta_0$ are consistent with our data." (Flux Density Distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Lower sensitivity in the Band 6 image prevents an independent detection of the ~20% SW/NE flux asymmetry seen in Band 7.  
  > "Despite these Band 6 flux ratios and flux difference not showing evidence for an asymmetry, the lower S/N of this image means that an asymmetry could still be present at broadly the same ${\sim}20$ percent level seen in Band 7. Deeper observations of q$^1$ Eri in Band 6 would be required to determine this." (Flux Profile Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): The HST coronagraph occults emission within ~2.25'', limiting the ability to quantify the significance of the minor-axis scattered light asymmetry.  
  > "However, since emission within ${\sim}2.25\ensuremath{''}$ cannot be probed due to coronagraph obscuration, this limits our ability to estimate the significance of this minor axis asymmetry." (Scattered Light Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Visibility-space BIC penalises additional model parameters disproportionately because a large fraction of the 19.4 million visibility points are dominated by noise and unaffected by the disc asymmetry, causing BIC_Vis to favour model SYM despite poor image residuals.  
  > "However, our data also contain a high fraction of data points that are negligibly affected by the model or the asymmetry, resulting in the BIC applying a disproportionate penalty to additional model parameters." (Model Results)
- Limitation (authors): The parametric models assume a simple 2D Gaussian clump or a radially constant eccentricity and pericentre orientation, which may be too simplistic for a broad belt with a radially decaying forced eccentricity or an azimuthally extended resonant clump.  
  > "Thus, whilst our single clump model provides a reasonable model of our data, we leave open the possibility that more complicated clump profiles or multi-component eccentric distributions may better and consistently interpret our observations." (Modelling Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The visibility-space modelling is performed only on the Band 7 data and not jointly on Band 6, leaving the clump spectral index essentially unconstrained (alpha_mm ~ 9 +/- 4).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO J=2-1 spectral window observed in the Band 6 dataset is described in the observations section but never analysed or used to check the CO gas mass upper limit.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the eccentric disc model (ECC), the fitted 2018 phase-centre offset shifts significantly away from the origin (x_off,18 = -0.11'', y_off,18 = -0.109''), indicating a strong degeneracy between astrometric alignment of the two epochs and disc eccentricity.
