## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, average, reweight, and image ALMA Band 6 continuum visibilities**: Calibrate visibilities with standard ALMA pipeline, inspect with plotms, purge calibrators, time average into 10-second bins, channel average into 4 channels per spectral window, run statwt to estimate visibility weights from variance, and image continuum with tclean using natural weighting.  
  > "The measurement set was purged of calibrator data and time averaged into 10-second bins, channel averaged into 4 channels per spectral window, and the *CASA* $\it{statwt}$ task was run to estimate the visibility weights based on the measured variance." (ALMA data reduction); "Continuum imaging was conducted using the *CASA* $\it{tclean}$ algorithm with natural weighting (providing a good trade-off between resolution and signal-to-noise)" (ALMA data reduction)
   - software: CASA, plotms, statwt, tclean
   - parameter: time averaging = 10-second bins; channel averaging = 4 channels per spectral window; weighting = natural  
  > "The measurement set was purged of calibrator data and time averaged into 10-second bins, channel averaged into 4 channels per spectral window, and the *CASA* $\it{statwt}$ task was run to estimate the visibility weights based on the measured variance." (ALMA data reduction); "Continuum imaging was conducted using the *CASA* $\it{tclean}$ algorithm with natural weighting (providing a good trade-off between resolution and signal-to-noise)" (ALMA data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspected visibility amplitudes vs time and uv-distance in plotms and determined no flagging was necessary.  
  > "The $\it{plotms}$ task in $\it{CASA}$ was used to examine the visibility amplitudes as functions of both time and uv-distance, and as a result it was deemed that no data flagging would be necessary." (ALMA data reduction)
2. **Extract empirical disc geometry, radial profile, and fluxes from the ALMA Band 6 continuum image**: Measure PA by maximising total flux in 5 deg wedges within 250 au; subtract unresolved stellar emission with a 2D Gaussian matched to the beam and fit elliptical rings within 3-sigma contours to measure inclination; compute deprojected azimuthally averaged radial surface brightness and integrated flux profiles.  
  > "We start by measuring the $\rm{PA}$ measured anti-clockwise from North centred on the star by plotting the total flux within $5^\circ$ wedges within a distance of 250 au" (ALMA analysis); "we therefore subtracted the unresolved stellar emission using a 2D Gaussian, defined by the beam major and minor axes, beam position angle, and peak pixel intensity, from the centre of the Band 6 image" (ALMA analysis); "Fig. 3 shows the azimuthally-averaged radial profile centred on the star, using our derived inclination of $61^\circ$ and position angle of 146$^\circ$." (ALMA analysis)
   - parameter: PA = 146 +/- 5 deg; inclination = 61 +/- 5 deg  
  > "we have observationally constrained its position angle on the sky as ${\rm{PA}}{=}146{\pm}5^\circ$, its inclination as $i{=}61{\pm}5^\circ$, the radial peak location of its disc emission at ${\sim}$110 au, starting at ${\sim}50\,$au, and extending outwards to at least ${\sim}$180 au." (Summary of ALMA analysis)
   - assumption: 10% ALMA flux calibration uncertainty  
  > "Combined in quadrature with an assumed $10\%$ ALMA calibration uncertainty, this results in a value of $F_{\rm{tot,\,B6}}{=}0.96\pm0.10\,$mJy" (ALMA analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared integrated flux profiles in NW and SE halves of the disc to check for asymmetry; later confirmed empirical PA and inclination against visibility modelling.  
  > "there is no detectable asymmetry in the total flux in the two disc ansae, given that the integrated flux profiles in the NW and SE halves of the disc are consistent over all radii." (ALMA analysis); "In $\S$4 with more detailed modelling we show that these are consistent with best fits of the interferometric visibilities." (ALMA analysis)
3. **Reduce HST/STIS coronagraphic images and extract scattered light radial profiles and albedo**: Register and median-combine STIS WEDGEA0.6 and WEDGEA2.0 images, subtract sky background and reference PSF star HD 139323, remove horizontal band artifact, derotate to North up, and extract radial surface brightness and r^2-scaled profiles in 10 deg wedges.  
  > "Each final $\kappa$ CrB image for each wedge position in each orbit was then subtracted by the final image for each wedge position in the orbit that targeted HD 139323." (HST data reduction); "we produced a radial profile of the scattered light by averaging emission within 10$^\circ$ wedges either side of the star, assuming a position angle of 146$^\circ$ and an inclination of 40$^\circ$" (HST analysis)
   - parameter: WEDGEA2.0 IWA >= 1.7'' (51 au); conversion 1 cts sec^-1 pix^-1 = 177 uJy arcsec^-2  
  > "with a gain of 4 and the pixel size stated above yields 1 cts sec$^{-1}$ pix$^{-1}{=}177\,\mu$Jy arcsec$^{-2}$." (HST data reduction); "Our analysis will therefore focus on the WEDGEA2.0 data which are not vignetted, and are deeper, but have a larger inner working angle (IWA; $\geq$1.7$''$ or 51 au) than the WEDGEA0.6 data." (HST analysis)
   - assumption: PA = 146 deg, i = 40 deg for radial profile extraction; F = 2923 mJy, f = 5e-5, d_phi = 2-8'' for albedo estimation  
  > "we produced a radial profile of the scattered light by averaging emission within 10$^\circ$ wedges either side of the star, assuming a position angle of 146$^\circ$ and an inclination of 40$^\circ$" (HST analysis); "We find $A{\sim}10\%$, based on $F{=}2923$ mJy, $f{=}5\times10^{-5}$ (see $\S$1), $d\phi{=}$2-8$''$, and $i{=}40^\circ$" (HST analysis)
   - fitting: Over-plotted power-law profiles I(r) = I_0 (r/R_in)^-gamma with I_0 = 100 uJy arcsec^-2 and gamma ~ 2.2 (or 0.2 for surface density).  
  > "by over-plotting an intensity profile $I(r) = I_0 \times (r/R_{\rm{in,\,HST}})^{-\gamma}$ (where $I_0$ is set to the inner-edge intensity, i.e., $I_0{=}100\,\mu$Jy arcsec$^{-2}$) and $I(r)\times r^2$, we show that the brightness and surface density fall-offs are consistent with simple power-laws." (HST analysis)
   - validation: Checked that profiles extracted in 10 deg wedges are consistent across a wide range of assumed inclinations and position angles.  
  > "We note that the derived profiles with 10$^\circ$ wedge angles are consistent with a wide range of assumed inclinations and position angles, including those derived from the ALMA image." (HST analysis)
4. **Fit the spectral energy distribution (SED) of star and disc**: Fit multi-wavelength photometry (including ALMA Band 6 total flux) with a stellar photosphere plus a single-component modified blackbody following Kennedy et al. (2014) and Yelverton et al. (2019).  
  > "To investigate where the emission in this system originates from, we fitted the complete flux distribution with a stellar photosphere plus a ‘modified’ blackbody, applying the methodology of (cite: Kennedy14) and (cite: Yelverton19)." (Flux distribution)
   - parameter: WISE1 and WISE2 excluded due to error flags  
  > "Error flags on the WISE1 and WISE2 data points meant that these were excluded from the fitting procedure (though these are still plotted on Fig. 6)." (Flux distribution)
   - assumption: 2% calibration uncertainties on stellar luminosity and radius; modified blackbody modified by (lambda/lambda_0)^-beta_0 beyond lambda_0  
  > "and a star with a stellar effective temperature of $T_{\rm{eff}}{=}4850{\pm}100\,\rm{K}$, and a stellar luminosity and radius of $L{=}12.0\,L_{\odot}$, and $R{=}4.9\,R_{\odot}$ respectively (with 2% calibration uncertainties)." (Flux distribution); "We note that this modified blackbody model is simply a normal blackbody over all wavelengths, until a specific wavelength, $\lambda_0$, whereby the flux density is then modified by a term $(\lambda/\lambda_0)^{-\beta_0}$" (Flux distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared fitted stellar parameters with Gaia DR2 Priam and FLAME estimates, and compared the ratio of resolved mm radius to blackbody radius with the empirical relation of Pawellek & Krivov (2015).  
  > "We further note that (cite: Gaia18) estimated these stellar parameters (using the “Priam" and “FLAME" algorithms) as $4750\,\rm{K}$, $12.7\,L_{\odot}$, and $5.1\,R_{\odot}$ respectively, which are all broadly in agreement with these model-fitted values." (Flux distribution); "Since there is good consistency between $\Gamma {\times} r_{\rm{bb}}{=}107{-}117$ au and the ${\sim}110$ au peak millimetre-emission radius, this demonstrates that our measurements align well with those of discs around similarly luminous stars." (Flux distribution)
5. **Parametric visibility modelling of the ALMA Band 6 continuum emission**: Compute dust temperatures and 1.3 mm model images with RADMC-3D for two radial surface density parameterisations (power-law 'SPL' with 9 free parameters and Gaussian 'SGR' with 8 free parameters), transform to model visibilities at the observed uv-baselines, and fit using MCMC with emcee.  
  > "We modelled the system with two belt distributions, ‘model SPL’, defined by an inner edge ($r_1$), outer edge ($r_2$), and power-law profile, with an index ($p_{\rm{SPL}}$) for a total of 9 free parameters, and ‘model SGR’, defined by a peak emission radius ($r_1$) and Gaussian width ($\sigma_{\rm{SGR}}$; the standard deviation of the Gaussian distribution) for a total of 8 free parameters." (Millimetre model setup); "We used the *RADMC-3D* package (cite: Dullemond12) with the mcmc parameter estimator, $\it{emcee}$ (cite: Goodman10; FM13) to compute the dust temperature of our disc models." (Millimetre modelling results); "These dust temperatures were used to create 2D images at $1.3\,$mm and subsequently used to compute model visibilities at identical uv-baselines to our ALMA observations." (Millimetre modelling results)
   - software: RADMC-3D, emcee
   - parameter: Free parameters: M_dust, r_1, i, PA, F_*_B6, x_off, y_off, plus (p_SPL, r_2) for SPL or (sigma_SGR) for SGR; N_Dat = 12,104,328  
  > "The models described in $\S$3.1.1 share a common set of 7 free parameters: dust mass ($M_{\rm{dust}}$), main belt radius ($r_{\rm{1}}$), inclination ($i$), position angle ($\rm{PA}$), photospheric Band 6 flux ($F_{\star,\,\rm{B6}}$), and the Band 6 measurement set phase centre offsets in RA and Dec ($x_{\rm{off}}$, $y_{\rm{off}}$ respectively)." (Millimetre model setup); "For our Band 6 data, there are $2\times N_{\rm{Vis}} = N_{\rm{Dat}}=12,104,328$ independent data points, accounting for the real and imaginary components of the interferometric visibilities." (Millimetre model statistical comparison)
   - assumption: Vertical aspect ratio h = H/r = 0.05 (fixed); circular orbits; grain sizes a_min = 1.0 um to a_max = 1 cm with power-law index alpha = 3.5; grain density rho = 2.7 g cm^-3; weighted mean dust opacity kappa = 1.88 cm^2 g^-1 (15% water-ice, 70% astrosilicates, 15% amorphous carbon)  
  > "Given our observations only have a low signal-to-noise and the disc is only moderately inclined, we fix $h{=}0.05$ (cite: Hughes18)" (Millimetre model setup); "Additionally, all these models have identical (and fixed) minimum and maximum grain radii of $a_{\rm{min}}{=}1.0\,\mu \rm{m}$ and $a_{\rm{max}}{=}1$ $\rm{cm}$." (Millimetre model setup); "We further assume a dust grain density of $\rho=2.7$ $\rm{g}$ $\rm{cm}^{-3}$, a size distribution with power-law exponent $\alpha{=}3.5$ (cite: Dohnanyi69), and a weighted mean dust opacity of $\kappa{=}1.88\,\rm{cm}^2\,\rm{g}^{-1}$" (Millimetre model setup)

## Qualitative findings

- The debris disc around kappa CrB is a single broad planetesimal belt (with a >50 au radial extent and inner edge at 93 au in the millimetre) rather than a narrow ring.  
  > "By modelling the Band 6 ALMA data, we have shown that the continuum emission is consistent with being due to a single broad planetesimal belt with an extent ${>}50\,$au and inner edge starting at 93 au." (Conclusions)
- The disc dust mass, total planetesimal belt mass, and belt radius are consistent with collisional evolution models of main-sequence A-type star debris discs despite the star's post-main-sequence sub-giant status.  
  > "We show that the debris disc of $\kappa\,$CrB is consistent with the population of discs around main sequence A-type stars (which this star evolved from) and has a mass consistent with expectations from collisional evolution models" (Conclusions)
- The inclination inferred from HST scattered light modelling (30-40 deg) is significantly lower than the inclination measured in the millimetre with ALMA and far-IR with Herschel (~61 deg), despite consistent position angles.  
  > "Our scattered light modelling shows that the dust must have a high anisotropic scattering factor ($g{\sim}0.8{-}0.9$) but an inclination ($i{\sim}30{-}40^\circ$) that is inferred to be significantly lower than the $i{\sim}61\ensuremath{^\circ}$ millimetre inclination." (Abstract)
- No CO J=2-1 emission is detected, consistent with low CO production from either steady-state planetesimal collisions or CO-ice sublimation at the current 70 K dust temperature.  
  > "We place an upper limit on the CO gas mass of $M_{\rm{CO}}{<}(4.2{-}13) \times10^{-7}\,M_\oplus$, and show this to be consistent with levels expected from planetesimal collisions, or from CO-ice sublimation as $\kappa\,$CrB begins its giant branch ascent." (Abstract)
- Without planetary migration, carving the wide inner cavity out to 93 au requires a string of lower-mass planets in addition to kappa CrB b and the putative outer RV companion.  
  > "There is a large inner cavity seen in the millimetre emission, which we show is consistent with carving by such an outer massive companion with a string of lower mass planets." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: kappa CrB is the closest (by a factor 2-4x) and brightest known sub-giant hosting both a giant planet and a debris disc, and ALMA + HST provide >=3x higher resolution than previous Herschel PACS images to resolve the belt structure and test planet-disc interactions.  
  > "At a distance of 30.1 pc (cite: Gaia18), of the known sub-giants that host at least one planet and debris disc, $\kappa\,$CrB is the closest (by a factor $2{-}4\times$), and hosts the brightest disc." (Introduction); "We present here the first ALMA and HST images of $\kappa\,$CrB, providing strict constraints on this planetary system, with observational resolution ${\gtrsim}3\times$ higher than previous Herschel images in an attempt to unravel the evolution of this planetary system." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting in tclean was chosen as a good trade-off between resolution and signal-to-noise; spectro-spatial Keplerian masking was used for CO because gas co-orbiting in a broad inclined disc spreads emission across multiple velocity channels.  
  > "Continuum imaging was conducted using the *CASA* $\it{tclean}$ algorithm with natural weighting (providing a good trade-off between resolution and signal-to-noise)" (ALMA data reduction); "Given the disc is broad, any gas that is co-orbiting with the disc will span a range of velocities and thus fall over a range of channels. To measure the line profiles from our CO data cube, we therefore applied the spectro-spatial filtering method of (cite: Matra15; Matra17)" (CO J=2-1 Analysis)
- Alternative: Two narrow planetesimal belts: tested against the ALMA Band 6 visibilities but yielded poorer chi^2, BIC, and visibly poorer residuals than a single broad belt.  
  > "Albeit with less significant fits in additional tests, i.e., poorer $\chi^2$ and $\rm{BIC}$ values, and visibly poorer residuals, we found that the data can nevertheless be modelled by *two* narrow belts." (Alternative models?)
- Alternative: Leaving the vertical scale height h as a free parameter in the ALMA visibility modelling: yielded unconstrained values due to low S/N and moderate inclination, so h was fixed to 0.05.  
  > "Given our observations only have a low signal-to-noise and the disc is only moderately inclined, we fix $h{=}0.05$ (cite: Hughes18), and further note that by investigating model fits with $h$ left as a free parameter we always yielded unconstrained values." (Millimetre model setup)
- Alternative: HST STIS WEDGEA0.6 data: rejected for main disc analysis due to a negative inner halo artifact out to 1.7'' and detector edge vignetting at 2.7''.  
  > "because the WEDGEA0.6 position is near the edge of the detector, the $\kappa$ CrB image is vignetted at 2.7$''$ radius in the PA range $45\ensuremath{^\circ}{-}90\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (HST data reduction); "Our analysis will therefore focus on the WEDGEA2.0 data which are not vignetted, and are deeper, but have a larger inner working angle (IWA; $\geq$1.7$''$ or 51 au) than the WEDGEA0.6 data." (HST data reduction)
- Alternative: N=2 closed-orbit RV planet fit in RadVel: statistically favoured by BIC over N=1, but rejected because the outer planet's periastron (~1.3 au) crosses inside the 2.8 au orbit of kappa CrB b and is dynamically unstable.  
  > "Such an eccentricity and orbital radius result in this body having a periastron at ${\sim}1.3\,$au, i.e., inside the 2.8 au orbit of the exo-Jupiter, $\kappa$ CrB b, which we deem an unstable configuration over the 2.5 Gyr age of the system." (The $\kappa\,$CrB Planetary System)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): With only one photometric point beyond ~160 um, the modified blackbody parameters lambda_0 and beta_0 in the SED fit are poorly constrained and degenerate.  
  > "The profile turn-over at 400 $\mu$m can be seen clearly in Fig. 6, though we note here that with only one data point beyond ${\sim}160\,\mu$m, $\lambda_0$ and $\beta_0$ are poorly constrained and degenerate." (Flux distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Low signal-to-noise and spatial resolution of the ALMA Band 6 data prevent ruling out unresolved radial substructure or gaps (multiple narrow belts) and leave the outer edge r_2 bounded only by a lower limit.  
  > "Whilst the data can be fitted with a smooth wide disc, we cannot rule out the possibility that there is a gap or narrow radial sub-structure in the disc that gives rise to the observed emission structure." (Alternative models?)
- Limitation (authors): The inner edge of the dust in scattered light lies inside the HST WEDGEA2.0 inner working angle (51 au) and imaging artifacts, making the scattered light inclination model-dependent.  
  > "In our analysis of the scattered light emission, the greatest uncertainty that remains is the location of the inner edge of the dust. Since this lies within the HST imaging artefacts, the scattered light dust inclination derived in this work is model dependent" (The inclination of $\kappa$ CrB’s planetesimal belt)
- Limitation (authors): The Gaia proper motion anomaly mass upper limit assumes circular orbits and the long-period limit.  
  > "this equation yields a new upper limit on the putative RV planet mass of ${\sim}120\,M_{\rm{Jup}}$, though we note that this equation is only valid for outer bodies on circular orbits and in the ‘long period limit’" (The $\kappa\,$CrB Planetary System)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The continuum rms noise per beam of the ALMA Band 6 map is not explicitly stated in the text, making direct S/N verification of the continuum map and the 30-40 uJy southern clump dependent on re-imaging.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The scattered light modelling fixes the inner edge at 73 au (the observed peak) while the WEDGEA2.0 IWA is 51 au, which may bias the inferred inclination when combined with a single Henyey-Greenstein phase function.
