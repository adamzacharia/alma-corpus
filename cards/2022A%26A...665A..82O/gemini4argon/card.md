- bands: Band 6, Band 7  
  > "The ALMA dataset consists of three different tunings to cover the $^{12}$CO $J$ = 2–1 line in Band 6 ($\approx$ 230 GHz) and the $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO $J$ = 3–2 lines in Band 7 ($\approx$ 345 and 330 GHz, respectively)." (ALMA data)
- frequencies and spectral setup: one 1.875 GHz SPW with 1920 channels (0.977 MHz spacing, 0.8-1.3 km/s native after Hann smoothing, 5 km/s final) on CO lines (~230, ~330, ~345 GHz); three 2 GHz SPWs with 128 channels (15.625 MHz spacing, 27-40 km/s) for continuum at 222, 324, 339 GHz  
  > "For all tunings, the correlator was set up with one 1.875 GHz spectral window centred on the relevant CO line, with 1920 channels separated by 0.977 MHz, and three additional 2 GHz spectral windows with 128 channels and a coarser spacing of 15.625 MHz for continuum measurements" (ALMA data); "After Hann smoothing[^2], the native velocity resolution for the CO spectral windows is between 0.8 and 1.3 km s$^{-1}$. For the continuum windows, the velocity resolution is between 27 and 40 km s$^{-1}$. For analysis, the final CO line velocity resolution was set to 5 km s$^{-1}$." (ALMA data)
- continuum bandwidth: 6 GHz at each effective frequency (222, 324, and 339 GHz)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Suppress interstellar CO contamination and image line and continuum emission**: Image visibilities using CASA tclean with Briggs weighting (robust = 0.5). Where interstellar CO emission contaminates the line of sight, reject visibilities from shortest baselines (<25 m or <50 m) before imaging.  
  > "Imaging was done using the CASA `tclean` algorithm. The final line images were created using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5." (ALMA data); "The presence of interstellar CO line emission along the line of sight occasionally makes it difficult to isolate the circumstellar emission. In such cases, we rejected visibility data from the shortest baselines, up to 50 m." (ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; velocity resolution = 5 km s^-1; baseline cutoffs >25 m or >50 m for affected sources  
  > "The final line images were created using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5." (ALMA data); "For analysis, the final CO line velocity resolution was set to 5 km s$^{-1}$." (ALMA data); "| 4 - | 1.5 | 31 | –30 | 25 | –0.5 | 0.70 | $B$ $>$ 25 m data used |" (Table 6)
   - assumption: Eliminating baselines <= 50 m suppresses extended emission on scales > 5-10'' without significant loss of circumstellar flux  
  > "Eliminating the shortest baselines, $\le$ 50 m (and those are in the far minority of all the baselines) will effectively suppress any extended emission on scales larger than 5$-$10$''$ ." (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Isolation of 12CO circumstellar emission was guided by the less-contaminated 13CO(J=3-2) results  
  > "In the case of the $^{12}$CO lines this method was used for nine sources, but here the isolation of the circumstellar emission was guided by the $^{13}$CO results." (ALMA data)
2. **Measure source positions, sizes, and continuum flux densities, and extract line profiles**: Fit 2D Gaussians in the image plane to velocity-integrated line brightness distributions and continuum maps to determine positions, deconvolved FWHMs, and continuum fluxes. Extract line spectra over apertures 2-3 times the brightness distribution FWHM and fit an analytical line profile function.  
  > "Hence, the sizes of the molecular envelopes cannot be determined from our data, and we report the FWHM (deconvolved with the synthesised beam) of a two-dimensional Gaussian fitted to the velocity-integrated brightness distribution." (ALMA data); "The line profiles were extracted over an aperture, centred on the peak of the brightness distribution, larger than the estimated FWHM of the brightness distribution, normally 2–3 times larger." (ALMA data); "The continuum brightness distributions are sharply peaked and we determine their sizes, positions, and flux densities (and their associated uncertainties) by fitting two-dimensional Gaussians to the data in the image plane." (ALMA data)
   - parameter: Aperture size = 2-3 times FWHM of brightness distribution; Line shape function S(v) = S(v_c) [1 - ((v - v_c)/v_inf)^2]^beta  
  > "The line profiles were extracted over an aperture, centred on the peak of the brightness distribution, larger than the estimated FWHM of the brightness distribution, normally 2–3 times larger." (ALMA data); "Flux densities, centre velocities (in the local standard of rest frame), and expansion velocities were determined through fitting the line shape function $$\begin{equation} S(\varv) = S(\varv_{\rm c})\, \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{\varv - \varv_{\rm c}}{\varv_\infty} \right)^2 \right]^\beta \end{equation}$$ to the data" (ALMA data)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting in image plane; analytical line shape profile fitting for S(v_c), v_c, v_inf, and beta  
  > "Flux densities, centre velocities (in the local standard of rest frame), and expansion velocities were determined through fitting the line shape function $$\begin{equation} S(\varv) = S(\varv_{\rm c})\, \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{\varv - \varv_{\rm c}}{\varv_\infty} \right)^2 \right]^\beta \end{equation}$$ to the data" (ALMA data)
   - validation: Cross-matched ALMA positions against VVV near-IR counterparts and inter-compared 13CO, 12CO, and 324 GHz continuum positions (median offsets ~0.02''-0.11'')  
  > "The median and maximum offsets between the $^{13}$CO($J$ = 3–2) and near-IR positions are 0.11$''$ and 0.23$''$, respectively." (The $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO lines); "The median and maximum offsets between the $^{13}$CO($J$ = 3–2) and 324 GHz continuum positions are 0.02$''$ and 0.12$''$, respectively" (ALMA continuum measurements)
3. **Construct extinction-corrected SEDs and determine pulsation periods**: Gather archival photometry (1-30+ um), correct for interstellar extinction up to 30 um using reddening maps and extinction laws, and analyse VVV Ks and WISE W1/W2 light curves to determine single pulsation periods.  
  > "The reddening is converted into extinction in the $K_{\rm s}$ band, $A_{K_{\rm s}}$ given in Table 2, and extrapolated up to 8 $\mu$m using the relationships provided by (cite: nishetal09). In the range 8 to 30 $\mu$m we use the results of (cite: wangetal15) and (cite: xueetal16)." (Photometry and variability); "The $K_{\rm s}$-band light curves have been analysed using the codes and methodology outlined in (cite: groeetal20) assuming a single period. For the WISE data a very similar approach was adopted to analyse the light curves in the W1 (3.4 $\mu$m) and W2 (4.6 $\mu$m) bands, as outlined in (cite: groe22)." (Photometry and variability)
   - parameter: Wavelengths corrected for extinction: <= 30 um  
  > "No corrections were applied for data at wavelengths longer than 30 $\mu$m because $A_{\lambda}/A_{K_{\rm s}}$ is expected to be lower than 0.2, which would imply relatively small corrections even for the higher-extinction sources." (Photometry and variability)
   - assumption: Single pulsation period assumed in light curve fitting  
  > "The $K_{\rm s}$-band light curves have been analysed using the codes and methodology outlined in (cite: groeetal20) assuming a single period." (Photometry and variability)
   - fitting: Light-curve period fitting following (cite: groeetal20) and (cite: groe22)  
  > "The $K_{\rm s}$-band light curves have been analysed using the codes and methodology outlined in (cite: groeetal20) assuming a single period." (Photometry and variability)
   - validation: Compared derived periods across Ks, W1, and W2 bands and against literature periods where available  
  > "The results, including the adopted period are collected in Table \[t:summary_periods\] that also cites periods found in the literature." (Photometry and variability)
4. **Model dust continuum SEDs to derive luminosities, dust mass-loss rates, and CSE inner radii**: Fit dust radiative transfer models using MCMax to extinction-corrected photometry at wavelengths > 1 um, and integrate the fitted SED over wavelength using the adopted distance to derive stellar luminosity and dust mass-loss rate.  
  > "To reproduce the SED of each source, we calculated radiative transfer models using the code MCMax (cite: minetal09) assuming spherical symmetry of the dust CSEs." (Dust continuum radiation); "In the fitting procedure, we only include photometric points for wavelengths larger than 1 $\mu$m because of the large extinction towards some of the sources and the relatively small contribution to the total luminosity of the emission at optical wavelengths." (Dust continuum radiation); "The stellar luminosity is obtained by integrating the fit to the SED over wavelength and using the distance to the given source." (Dust continuum radiation)
   - software: MCMax
   - parameter: Grain size distribution power-law index = -3.5, a_min = 0.001 um, a_max = 1.0 um; R_out = 10000 au; initial T_* = 2500 K; T_d(R_in) = 1000 K (for standard envelopes)  
  > "We consider astronomical silicate grains (cite: osseetal92) with a number size distribution given by a power law with index $-$3.5 and minimum and maximum grains sizes equal to 0.001 $\mu$m and 1.0 $\mu$m, respectively." (Dust continuum radiation); "The spherical dust CSEs were assumed to extend from an inner radius $R_{\rm in}$ to 10000 au. The former is either determined by the radius at which the dust temperature is 1000 K (as calculated by the code), or as the required inner radius to give a good fit to the data." (Dust continuum radiation); "The stellar temperature, $T_\ast$, was set to 2500 K initially." (Dust continuum radiation)
   - assumption: Spherical symmetry; r^-2 density distribution (constant mass-loss rate and expansion velocity); astronomical silicate grains (cite: osseetal92); dust expansion velocity equals CO gas expansion velocity; GC distance = 8.2 kpc for inner-GB sources  
  > "The density distribution is proportional to $r^{-2}$, where $r$ is the radial distance from the central star (corresponding to constant mass-loss rate and expansion velocity)." (Dust continuum radiation); "The dust-mass-loss rate is obtained assuming that the expansion velocity of the dust equals that estimated from the observed CO lines." (Dust continuum radiation)
   - fitting: Fitted by eye by varying tau_9.7, and where required R_in and T_*  
  > "The free parameters in the fit are $R_{\rm in}$ (where required), $T_\ast$ (where required), and the dust optical depth at 9.7 $\mu$m ($\tau_{9.7}$)." (Dust continuum radiation); "The SED was fitted by eye by varying mainly $\tau_{9.7}$. When an acceptable fit could not be obtained, we varied $R_{\rm in}$. If necessary, we also increased the stellar temperature to decrease the stellar emission in the near-infrared." (Dust continuum radiation)
   - validation: Checked long-wavelength (>70 um) and ALMA continuum points against SED predictions, testing a 10x larger outer radius (10^5 au)  
  > "Increasing the outer radius by a factor of ten (i.e. 10$^5$ au, corresponding to a high-mass-loss-rate epoch of about 30 000 yr) leads to significantly improved, but not perfect, fits." (Spectral energy distributions)
5. **Model CO line radiative transfer to derive gas mass-loss rates, terminal expansion velocities, gas-to-dust ratios, and 12CO/13CO abundance ratios**: Run non-LTE Monte Carlo radiative transfer models for a spherically expanding CSE to fit velocity-integrated 12CO(2-1) and 12CO(3-2) flux densities for gas mass-loss rate and expansion velocity, then match the 12CO/13CO(J=3-2) integrated flux ratio to derive the 13CO abundance.  
  > "For the line emission analysis, we used a well-tested, non-local-thermodynamic-equilibrium radiative transfer code based on the Monte Carlo method (cite: bern79; schoolof01)." (CO line radiation); "The best-fit circumstellar model for the $^{12}$CO line emission is obtained by minimising the difference between the model and observational results in terms of the velocity-integrated flux densities by adjusting the only free parameters, the gas expansion velocity and the mass-loss rate." (CO line radiation); "The estimate of the $^{13}$CO fractional abundance is based on this model and the requirement that the model and the observational $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO $J$ = 3–2 velocity-integrated flux density ratios are the same." (CO line radiation)
   - software: Monte Carlo non-LTE radiative transfer code (Bernes 1979; Schoier & Olofsson 2001)
   - parameter: R_in = 4e14 cm; R_out = 3 * R_1/2; v_turb = 1 km s^-1; T_min = 7.5 K; 82 rotational levels (up to J=40, v=0 and v=1); ortho-to-para-H2 = 3  
  > "In addition to thermal motion of the molecules, we assume a micro-turbulent velocity field of 1 km s$^{-1}$." (CO line radiation); "The inner radius of the CO envelope is chosen to be 4$\times$10$^{14}$ cm." (CO line radiation); "The CO excitation analysis includes 82 rotational energy levels, up to $J$ = 40 within the ground ($\nu$ = 0) and first vibrationally excited ($\nu$ = 1) states." (CO line radiation)
   - assumption: Constant isotropic expansion and mass-loss rate; kinetic and dust temperature profiles from IK Tau (cite: ramsolof14); initial 12CO/H2 abundance f_i = 4e-4; photodissociation profile from (cite: sabeetal19); central star blackbody at 2500 K  
  > "We assume a CSE expanding with constant velocity (the terminal gas expansion velocity $\varv_\infty$), which is formed by a constant and isotropic gas-mass-loss rate ($\dot{M}_{\rm g}$)." (CO line radiation); "We assume an initial fractional abundance of $^{12}$CO/H$_2$, $f_{\rm i}$, of 4$\times$10$^{-4}$, which corresponds to about 80 % complete formation of CO assuming solar abundances of C and O (cite: agunetal20)." (CO line radiation)
   - fitting: Minimise difference between model and observed velocity-integrated flux densities by adjusting v_inf and Mdot_g (for 12CO) and 13CO abundance (for 12CO/13CO 3-2 ratio)  
  > "The best-fit circumstellar model for the $^{12}$CO line emission is obtained by minimising the difference between the model and observational results in terms of the velocity-integrated flux densities by adjusting the only free parameters, the gas expansion velocity and the mass-loss rate." (CO line radiation)
   - validation: Sensitivity tests on dust optical depth, kinetic temperature law (+/-20%), photodissociation radius R_1/2 (factor of 3), resolved-out flux vs MRS, and comparison of model vs observed azimuthally averaged radial brightness profiles and 12CO 3-2/2-1 ratios  
  > "For example, changing the optical depth by a factor of 10 up or down leads to non-significant changes ($\ensuremath{\lesssim}$ 1 %) in the CO line intensities for a $\dot{M}_{\rm g}$ = 2$\times$10$^{-5}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ and $\varv_\infty$ = 18 km s$^{-1}$ model (our median values, see below)." (CO line radiation); "Decreasing $R_{1/2}$ by a factor of three for a model with our median values, 2$\times$10$^{-5}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ and 18 km s$^{-1}$, results in flux densities lowered by 34 % and 6 % for the $^{12}$CO $J$ = 2–1 and 3–2 lines, respectively." (CO photodissociation and brightness distributions); "In Fig. 9, we compare the azimuthally averaged observed brightness distributions of the $^{12}$CO(2–1) line towards four of our sources (these are inner-GB objects having the largest $\theta_{\rm s}$ and the highest S/N data), with the brightness distributions of the best-fit radiative transfer models for each source" (CO photodissociation and brightness distributions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: CO rotational lines provide mass-loss rates and 12C/13C isotopic ratios that OH masers cannot, and ALMA's high sensitivity and interferometric spatial filtering suppress ubiquitous extended interstellar CO contamination along lines of sight toward the inner Galactic Bulge.  
  > "The OH maser emission provides interesting insights for studying OH/IR stars, but only brings (at best) order-of-magnitude estimates of the mass-loss rate, and no information on elemental isotopic ratios, such as $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C, which are key signposts of stellar evolution. Observations of rotational lines from carbon monoxide (CO) have proven to be essential in this context (cite: schoolof01; debeetal10; ramsolof14)." (Introduction); "Observing OH/IR stars in CO line radiation towards regions of high extinction is a difficult task due to the contamination of ubiquitous interstellar CO along the line of sight. At the distance of the GC this is further exacerbated by the weakness of the circumstellar line and continuum radiation. Nevertheless, through the use of the ALMA interferometer both of these problems have been, at least partly, circumvented" (Conclusions)
- Why this method: Because only two 12CO lines were observed, radial kinetic temperature profiles could not be constrained individually, motivating the adoption of a single well-tested high-mass-loss-rate circumstellar model (IK Tau) across the sample; short baselines (<50 m) were omitted where needed to filter out extended interstellar CO.  
  > "We have observational data for only two rotational lines of $^{12}$CO, and this is not enough to put constraints on the radial distribution of the kinetic temperature for each individual source. We have therefore opted for using the same circumstellar model for all sources, the one obtained for the high-mass-loss-rate (10$^{-5}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$) M-type AGB star by (cite: ramsolof14)." (CO line radiation); "We have observational data for only two rotational lines of $^{12}$CO, and this is not enough to put constraints on the radial distribution of the kinetic temperature for each individual source." (CO line radiation)
- Alternative: Excluding baselines longer than 50 m to remove interstellar CO contamination was rejected because it leads to flux loss and poorer S/N.  
  > "Excluding data from baselines longer than this leads to a loss of flux density and poorer S/N." (ALMA data)
- Alternative: Raising the minimum kinetic temperature T_min in the outer CSE to fit the extended low-surface-brightness CO(2-1) emission was tested and rejected because maintaining the integrated intensity requires lowering the mass-loss rate, leaving the outer brightness underestimated.  
  > "Within the adopted circumstellar model, the only way to selectively increase the model brightness in the external parts is to raise the minimum kinetic temperature, $T_{\rm min}$ (it should be noted here that the physical properties of the external parts of AGB CSEs are particularly poorly constrained). However, in order to still fit the observed velocity-integrated intensities, this requires a corresponding decrease in the mass-loss rate, to the extent that the under-estimate of the brightness in the external parts persists." (CO photodissociation and brightness distributions)
- Alternative: Non-isotropic dust density distributions (e.g., equatorial density enhancements) for DE sources and modified grain/geometry models for >70 um SED excesses were discussed as plausible alternatives but not modelled in detail.  
  > "Alternative ways of improving the fits are a mass-loss rate varying with time, a different dust CSE geometry, and/or different grain properties. However, the aim of this paper is not to provide the best possible fits to the long-wavelength regime of the SEDs by individually selecting the dust envelope geometry or dust composition for each source" (Spectral energy distributions); "This opens up the question of an alternative, non-isotropic, dust density distribution for the DE sources as, for instance, suggested by (cite: decietal19) for evolved OH/IR stars." (Objects with detached dust CSEs)
- How they decided it was enough: Fast snapshot observations (~1 hour total on-source time for 22 stars) were sufficient to detect all 22 targets in at least one CO line, though the authors note more CO transitions and larger samples are needed to refine the models.  
  > "The total amount of on-source observing time was only about an hour for the whole project." (ALMA data); "Given that our data on a sample of 22 stars were obtained with a short observing time, it is certainly feasible to achieve such an improvement within a reasonable amount of observing time." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Extended dust continuum emission is resolved out by the interferometer due to small MRS (1-2.5''), making measured ALMA continuum fluxes conservative lower limits (at least a factor of two below true fluxes).  
  > "Using the dust radiative transfer modelling (described in Sect. 5.1) we estimate that the measured ALMA flux densities are in general, at least, a factor of two below the true flux densities, that is the reported ALMA flux density is a conservative lower limit to the total flux density for the detected sources." (ALMA continuum measurements)
- Limitation (authors): Gas mass-loss rates are uncertain by at least a factor of five, gas-to-dust ratios by a factor of seven, and 12CO/13CO abundance ratios by a factor of four due to having only two 12CO lines and one 13CO line with limited S/N and using a single circumstellar model for all stars.  
  > "The availability of only two $^{12}$CO lines (and their generally low S/N) and the use of the same circumstellar model for all stars make our mass-loss rate estimates particularly uncertain, at least by a factor of five." (CO line radiation); "In total, the uncertainty of the $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO abundance ratio is about a factor of four." (CO line radiation); "$^{(d)}$ The gas-to-dust mass ratio estimated as $\dot{M}_{\rm g}/\dot{M}_{\rm d}$. The uncertainty is estimated to be a factor of seven." (Table 4)
- Limitation (authors): The CO radiative transfer models fail to reproduce the observed 12CO J=3-2/2-1 line intensity ratios (off by factors of 1.5-3 in 11 inner-GB sources) and underestimate the extended low-surface-brightness emission.  
  > "We find a significant discrepancy, by factors of 1.5–3, between the observational and model ratios (for the derived mass-loss rate) in eleven of our inner-GB sources, and under-estimates are as common as over-estimates." (Mass-loss rates and gas kinematics); "Our circumstellar CO line radiative transfer modelling fails in two respects. The observed $^{12}$CO $J$ = 3$-$2/2$-$1 line intensity ratios are not reproduced in several cases. The observed presence of extended, low-level, brightness emission in many cases is also not reproduced in the models." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): SED fits underestimate flux densities at wavelengths longer than ~70 um in 10 of 13 objects with far-IR data.  
  > "It is apparent that in the 13 objects where data at wavelengths longer than about 70 $\mu$m are available, the SED fits are often not very good. In eight objects (5 are SE objects, 3 are DE objects), the longer wavelength points are underestimated by a factor of a few, while they are severely underestimated in two objects (both are SE objects)" (Spectral energy distributions); "It is also apparent that, when data at longer wavelengths ($\ensuremath{\gtrsim}$ 70 $\mu$m) are available, the SED fits are often not very good, the flux densities are underestimated. This applies to ten objects" (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Sample selection is biased towards the highest-mass-loss-rate OH/IR objects in directions of lower interstellar CO emission and may also be biased towards detached-envelope (DE) objects.  
  > "In this way, the sample is biased towards the highest-mass-loss-rate OH/IR objects in the inner GB that are not severely affected by interstellar extinction (as a consequence only five of our objects lie within 0.3$^\circ$ of the GC, corresponding to $\ensuremath{\lesssim}$ 40 pc)." (The sample); "It may be that our way of selecting the sample has lead to a bias towards objects with DE characteristics, but it is difficult to quantify the magnitude of any bias, if present." (Objects with detached dust CSEs)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size is too small to statistically compare the physical distributions (L, Mdot, v_inf, 12CO/13CO) between SE and DE classes.  
  > "Unfortunately, our sample size is not large enough to allow a comparison between the luminosities, mass-loss rates, gas expansion velocities, and circumstellar $^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO abundance ratios of the SE and DE sources." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SED fitting was performed 'by eye' rather than through formal statistical minimization, which introduces subjective degeneracy between tau_9.7, R_in, and T_*.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the markdown/text conversion of the paper, the specific OH/IR source names in Table 1 and throughout the text are missing/blanked out (leaving only source numbers 1-22 and coordinates), though coordinates and CDS tables allow unambiguous identification.
