## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image CO line cubes**: Calibration with CASA standard procedures; cleaning in GILDAS (Hogbom, Briggs robust 1 for compact 3-2; natural weighting for 6-5)  
  > "The calibration of the visibilities was performed using standard procedures with the CASA software." (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data); "The GILDAS software was used for the subsequent cleaning, for which we used the Hogbom method and the Briggs weighting scheme with a robust value of 1." (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Velocity channels resampled to 0.8 km/s; continuum subtracted in the uv plane except for J=6-5  
  > "the data were resampled to the same velocity resolution, 0.8 km s$^{-1}$." (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data); "the continuum was subtracted from the line maps (at the stage of uv data), to better show the emission in the weak wings of the line." (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux loss in high-resolution 3-2 maps checked against compact-configuration maps and against single-dish J=2-1 flux  
  > "this moderate flux loss is confirmed by our $J$=2$-$1 maps, see below, whose integrated profile can be compared with single-dish data and shows very similar fluxes." (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data)
2. **Recentre maps on continuum centroids and compare lines and configurations**: Each map centred on the continuum centroid measured independently per frequency and epoch; brightness distributions and fluxes compared across lines and configurations  
  > "we decided to center all our maps on the respective continuum centroids, which were measured independently for each frequency and epoch." (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data); "We conclude that the flux lost in our high-resolution $J$=3$-$2 maps is moderate, less than about one-third" (2 New observations and reanalysis of previous data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit a heuristic 3D LTE radiative-transfer model of the CO emission**: Free-form 3D density/velocity model (central condensation plus spiral arcs A, B, C, D, W) with LTE radiative transfer convolved with the beam and compared to the 12CO J=3-2 maps (and less closely the other lines)  
  > "The code we used for our heuristic model allows general 3D distributions of the physical conditions and kinematics, whose descriptions are quite free." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components); "This distribution is numerically convolved with the observational beam, yielding maps that are directly comparable to the ALMA maps" (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
   - software: in-house heuristic 3D radiative-transfer code
   - parameter: Equatorial plane at 15 deg from the line of sight; rotation axis projected at PA 5 deg  
  > "In this best-fit model we also take an angle between the equatorial plane and the line of sight of 15$^\circ$ and a position angle of the rotation axis projection on the plane of the sky of 5$^\circ$." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
   - assumption: Symmetry plane (equator) coincident with the orbital plane; LTE excitation  
  > "that there is a symmetry plane (the equator), which is the same as that of the orbit" (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components); "The line excitation is described in the code by LTE." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
   - fitting: Manual comparison of predicted and observed maps; no sampler, priors or likelihood stated  
  > "The model predictions were compared with the ALMA maps." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
   - validation: Visual agreement with the 12CO J=3-2 maps; LTE assumption tested in Appendix with statistical equilibrium calculations  
  > "The comparison is very satisfactory." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components); "The deviation from thermalized populations is always negligible, smaller than 10%" (Limits of our LTE treatment of the CO excitation)
4. **Run hydrodynamical simulations of the WD-AGB wind interaction**: FLASH 4.5 simulations with the R Aqr stellar and orbital parameters; model cube rotated to the observed viewing geometry and compared with the heuristic model and ALMA channel maps  
  > "We performed hydrodynamical simulations with the stellar and orbital parameters of R Aqr binary stars using the code FLASH 4.5 (cite: fry00)." (4.2 Hydrodynamical simulations)
   - software: FLASH 4.5
   - parameter: Average orbital separation 15.7 AU, orbital period 42 yr, eccentricity 0.57  
  > "the average orbital separation (sum of the semimajor axes) of 15.7 AU, yielding an orbital period of 42 yr" (4.2 Hydrodynamical simulations); "An orbital eccentricity of 0.57 was applied, causing the orbital separation to vary from 24.6 AU at the apocenter down to 6.8 AU at the pericenter." (4.2 Hydrodynamical simulations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: A circular-orbit test with the same parameters did not reproduce the observed density discontinuity  
  > "A circular-orbit simulation, which was tested with the same orbital parameters except for the orbital shape, does not show this density discontinuity." (4.2 Hydrodynamical simulations)
5. **Estimate WD UV temperature and luminosity**: Blackbody fit of the R-J tail of the UV continuum plus HeII 1640 emission from coadded IUE spectra  
  > "We used two long-exposure IUE spectra of the central source of R Aqr taken on 14 July 1991 (SWP 42069 and SWP 42070)." (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region); "Assuming a blackbody distribution of the hot WD continuum, we fit the R-J tail to selected continuum windows shortward of" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
   - assumption: Reddening E(B-V) ~0.23 mag toward the WD; distance 265 pc  
  > "which, assuming a distance of 265 pc, gives" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Model CO photodissociation and chemistry with a PDR code**: Meudon PDR code with geometrical dilution of the WD UV field, steady-state chemistry along four directions in the orbital plane, extended heuristic density model and density reduced by factors of 2 and 3  
  > "by using the Meudon PDR code (cite: lepetit06), in which we implemented a correction to take the geometrical dilution of the UV field" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region); "We also explored models in which we decreased the density by factors of two and three" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
   - software: Meudon PDR code
   - parameter: Turbulent velocity 15 km/s  
  > "we adopted a relatively high turbulent velocity of 15 km s$^{-1}$ to reduce the optical depth of the UV lines of CO and the extent of selfshielding." (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
   - assumption: Solar elemental abundances; ISM-type dust extinction with dust-to-gas ratio half the interstellar value  
  > "We adopted solar elemental abundances and the gas-phase chemical network used by (cite: agundez18) to model protoplanetary disks" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region); "but with a dust-to-gas mass ratio half of the interstellar value to account for the probable depletion of dust" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted CO behaviour compared qualitatively with the observed maps and with observed extinction  
  > "This behavior is consistent with our observations (Sect. 4.1)." (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)

## Qualitative findings

- CO emission comes from a central condensation plus discontinuous spiral arcs strongly focused on the equatorial plane, seen nearly edge-on  
  > "CO is emitted from a central intense clump, about 0$''$2 wide, plus an elongated region formed by various arcs, which look like shreds of spiral arms." (Conclusions)
- Central condensation shows no sign of rotation in the data  
  > "It is remarkable that the observations do not show any sign of rotation in these inner regions." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
- The high eccentricity of the orbit explains the abrupt density drop beyond the B-C arm  
  > "is probably due to the high eccentricity of the orbit." (4.2 Hydrodynamical simulations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Hydrodynamical predictions alone are too extended to explain the small emitting region, so a descriptive model was built  
  > "we readily found that the predictions from hydrodynamical models by themselves are not able to provide an adequate density distribution, as they are in general too extended to explain the small emitting region and the very weak total CO emission." (4 Modeling the ALMA maps of R Aqr)
- Alternative: Circular-orbit simulation rejected because it lacks the observed density discontinuity and is too isotropic  
  > "A circular-orbit simulation, which was tested with the same orbital parameters except for the orbital shape, does not show this density discontinuity." (4.2 Hydrodynamical simulations)
- Alternative: CO displacement by CH4 near the AGB star considered unlikely over wide regions  
  > "We therefore consider that the displacement of CO by CH$_4$ is unlikely to occur in wide regions around R Aqr." (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Extension of the heuristic model used for the PDR calculation is very tentative  
  > "because of the lack of empirical information and the complex structure suggested in the hydrodynamical models, this extension is very tentative" (4.3 Effects of photodissociation on the extent and shape of the CO-rich region)
- Limitation (authors): Comparison between heuristic and hydrodynamical models is only semiquantitative  
  > "Although our comparison of the two models can only be semiquantitative" (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Maps from different epochs are hard to compare in this rapidly evolving system  
  > "comparing data from different epochs in such a rapidly evolving system is not straightforward" (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
- Limitation (authors): Some kinematic parameters are degenerate  
  > "It is important to note that some of the kinematical items of our description show a certain degree of degeneracy." (4.1 Main model characteristics and nebular components)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heuristic model parameters are tuned by eye with no stated uncertainties, sampler or goodness-of-fit metric
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model compared mainly to a single line and epoch (12CO J=3-2) while the system evolves quickly near periastron
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distance of 265 pc is an intermediate guess; derived linear sizes and the WD luminosity scale with it
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Hydrodynamics details are deferred to a paper in preparation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Flux loss of up to ~25-35% in the high-resolution maps could bias contrasts of faint arcs
