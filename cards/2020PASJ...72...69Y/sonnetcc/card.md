## Analysis recipe

1. **Build the combined 1.2 mm continuum map**: Calibrate and image ASAGAO plus two archival projects with CLEAN via tclean; details in Hatsukade et al. 2018  
  > "The maps were processed with the `CLEAN` algorithm (cite: hogdom1974) with the task `tclean`." (2.1 ALMA Band-6 data); "Details of the data analysis are given in (cite: hatsukade2018)." (2.1 ALMA Band-6 data)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: CASA 5.1.1 for imaging, CASA 4.7.2 for calibration  
  > "version 5.1.1, but calibration was done with the version 4.7.2." (2.1 ALMA Band-6 data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify ALMA counterparts of ZFOURGE sources**: Shift ZFOURGE coordinates to the Gaia frame, then match to ALMA sources with S/N > 4.5 within positional offset < 0.5 arcsec (larger, up to the K-band half-light radius, for extended counterparts), excluding ZFOURGE use flag = 0 and removing p > 0.05 sources  
  > "the ZFOURGE source coordinates were corrected by $-$0$^{\prime\prime}$.086 in right ascension and $+$0$^{\prime\prime}$.282 in declination" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification); "we allow the positional offsets between ZFOURGE and ALMA positions of less than $0^{\prime\prime}.5$" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification); "We remove these ASAGAO sources with $p>0.05$ (i.e., ID1 and ID7)" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
   - parameter: S/N threshold = 4.5 (more conservative than the 2.0 used by Bouwens et al. 2016)  
  > "we adopt a more conservative threshold of S/N $=$ 4.5" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
   - parameter: astrometric shift of -0.086 arcsec in RA and +0.282 arcsec in Dec  
  > "the ZFOURGE source coordinates were corrected by $-$0$^{\prime\prime}$.086 in right ascension and $+$0$^{\prime\prime}$.282 in declination" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Random-coincidence p-value of 0.03 from ~3,000 ZFOURGE sources; negative-peak test finds no chance coincidences at S/N <= -4.5; 45 positive and 9 negative sources at S/N > 4.5  
  > "the likelihood of random coincidence is estimated to be 0.03" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification); "We extracted 45 positive sources and 9 negative sources (i.e., false detections) with S/N $>$ 4.5." (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
3. **Measure ALMA flux densities and correct for flux boosting**: Measure flux densities at ZFOURGE positions allowing positional offsets; de-boost using the ratio of input to output integrated flux of 30,000 injected artificial sources  
  > "We consider the flux-boosting effect by calculating the ratio between input and output integrated flux densities of inserted 30,000 artificial sources into the signal map" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
   - parameter: 30,000 artificial sources injected  
  > "inserted 30,000 artificial sources into the signal map" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux boosting for S/N > 4.5 is <~ 15%, comparable with previous studies  
  > "The effect of flux boosting for the sources with S/N $>$ 4.5 is $\lesssim$ 15%" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
4. **Assign redshifts and derive the median redshift**: Use ZFOURGE EAZY photometric redshifts (40 photometric points), spectroscopic redshifts where available (Skelton et al. 2014; MUSE), and the Luo et al. 2017 value for ID26; compute the median  
  > "We use the spectroscopic redshifts of (cite: inami2017) for ASAGAO sources that are detected by MUSE." (2.4 Redshift distribution of ASAGAO sources); "present its photometric redshift as $z$ = 2.14" (2.4 Redshift distribution of ASAGAO sources)
   - software: EAZY
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Median absolute difference between photo-z with and without K-luminosity priors for the 15 sources without spectroscopic redshift is 0.03  
  > "The median value of the absolute differences is only 0.03." (2.4 Redshift distribution of ASAGAO sources)
5. **Assemble mid-IR to millimeter photometry**: Use ZFOURGE, Spitzer MIPS 24 um, Herschel PACS, and de-blended SPIRE photometry; SPIRE de-blending uses 24 um and radio sources as priors with iterative flux prediction, freezing faint predicted fluxes  
  > "we have used all 24 $\mu$m and radio continuum sources as priors to extract fluxes in Herschel bands." (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - parameter: sources with predicted flux below the detection depth (typically 2-3 times the instrumental noise) are frozen at the predicted flux  
  > "for sources with predicted fluxes below the detection depth (typically 2-3 times the instrumental noise), we have frozen their fluxes to be the best predicted flux" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Monte Carlo injection of sources into real maps and re-extraction to estimate flux accuracy and uncertainties  
  > "We then run Monte Carlo simulations by injecting sources into real maps and re-do the source extraction together with true priors to estimate the accuracy for flux and flux uncertainties." (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
6. **Fit optical-to-millimeter SEDs**: Bayesian SED fitting with the MAGPHYS high-z extension, redshift fixed, BC03 templates and Charlot & Fall dust model; sources with flag = 0 are removed  
  > "we perform bayesian-based SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths using `MAGPHYS` (see (cite: dacunha2008; dacunha2015), for details)" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths); "For ASAGAO sources, we use the `MAGPHYS` high-$z$ extension version." (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths); "We manually remove 4 sources" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - software: MAGPHYS
   - parameter: redshift fixed to ZFOURGE photometric or spectroscopic redshift  
  > "we fixed the redshift of the ASAGAO sources to the best-fit photometric redshift presented by (cite: straatman2016) or spectroscopic redshift if available" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - assumption: AGN contribution (if any) is negligible for the derived physical properties  
  > "we assume that the contribution from an AGN (if any) will have negligible impact on the physical properties derived from the SED analysis" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - assumption: only photometric errors are used; absolute flux calibration systematics are not included  
  > "Although we consider photometry errors in each band, we do not consider systematic uncertainties" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - fitting: MAGPHYS Bayesian library fit; priors optimized for IR-luminous dusty star-forming galaxies at high redshift  
  > "This code uses priors which are optimized for IR luminous dusty star-forming galaxies at high redshift (cite: dacunha2015)." (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
   - validation: Changes in physical parameters from redshift uncertainties are <~ 0.3 dex; L_IR from MAGPHYS agrees with the Casey 2012 templates with no significant systematic offset  
  > "the changes in the estimated physical parameters are within $\lesssim$ 0.3 dex" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths); "there is no significant systematic offset between the two methods." (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
7. **Derive stellar masses, UV+IR SFRs, and cross-check L_IR**: Stellar masses from MAGPHYS (and ZFOURGE FAST masses used for comparison); SFR(UV+IR) = 1.09e-10 (L_IR + 2.2 L_UV); L_IR cross-checked with Casey 2012 templates  
  > "we compute SFRs by summing the ultraviolet (UV) SFRs and IR SFRs based on the work of (cite: bell2005) scaled to a Chabrier IMF" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs); "1.09\times10^{-10}(L_\mathrm{IR}+2.2L_\mathrm{UV})" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - software: MAGPHYS, FAST
   - parameter: L_UV = 1.5 nu L_nu(2800)  
  > "is defined as $L_\mathrm{UV} = 1.5\nu L_{\nu2800}$ as described in (cite: straatman2016)" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF  
  > "We adopt the Chabrier IMF (cite: chabrier2003) in this paper." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: MAGPHYS SFRs consistent with ZFOURGE; MAGPHYS stellar masses higher than FAST by >~ 0.2-0.5 dex, so FAST masses are used for comparison  
  > "the stellar masses obtained by using `MAGPHYS` are systematically higher than that of `FAST` by $\gtrsim$ 0.2–0.5 dex" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
8. **Place sources on the M*-SFR plane relative to the main sequence**: Median stellar mass and SFR in two redshift bins (1<z<=2, 2<z<=3) for flag=1 sources, compared with ALMA non-detected ZFOURGE UVJ star-forming galaxies and the Schreiber et al. 2015 main sequence; starburst = more than a factor 4 above the main sequence  
  > "In each redshift bin, there are 7 and 15 ASAGAO sources, respectively." (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs); "where an SFR increased by more than a factor 4 (or 0.6 dex) compared to the main sequence" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Compare IRX (L_IR/L_UV) of detected and non-detected sources**: Plot IRX versus M*, SFR (three mass bins) and beta_UV; beta_UV from a power-law fit over rest-frame 1500-2500 A of ZFOURGE photometry; compare with the Bouwens et al. 2016 consensus relation and the Meurer 1999 / Takeuchi 2012 local relations  
  > "over the rest-frame wavelength range of 1500–2500 Å using ZFOURGE photometies" (4.2.2 The IRX-beta_UV relation); "we focus on IRX (i.e., $L_\mathrm{IR}/L_\mathrm{UV}$) as a key parameter to distinguish between ALMA-detected sources and non-detected sources" (4.2 The infrared excess (IRX))
   - assumption: L_UV from rest-frame 2800 A is approximately equivalent to the 1600 A luminosity used in most IRX studies  
  > "which are supposed to be approximately equivalent" (4.2 The infrared excess (IRX))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
10. **Estimate the contribution to the cosmic SFR density**: Sum the SFRs of ASAGAO sources with K-band counterparts and divide by comoving volume, corrected with the survey completeness of Hatsukade et al. 2018  
  > "we simply sum up the SFRs of the ASAGAO sources with the $K$-band counterparts and divide them by the co-moving volumes" (5 Contribution to the cosmic SFRD)
   - assumption: LCDM with Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc  
  > "$\Omega_\mathrm{M}$ = 0.3, $\Omega_\mathrm{\Lambda}$ = 0.7, and $H_0$ = 70 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Values consistent with Hatsukade et al. 2018  
  > "These values are consistent with results by (cite: hatsukade2018)." (5 Contribution to the cosmic SFRD)

  > "The maps were processed with the `CLEAN` algorithm (cite: hogdom1974) with the task `tclean`." (2.1 ALMA Band-6 data)
- **EAZY** : ZFOURGE optical-to-near-IR photometric redshifts (run by Straatman et al. 2016)  
  > "We also have to note that `EAZY` adopts $K$-luminosity priors, but it does not affect our results significantly." (2.4 Redshift distribution of ASAGAO sources)
- **MAGPHYS (high-z extension)** : optical-to-millimeter SED fitting for stellar mass, L_IR and SFR  
  > "For ASAGAO sources, we use the `MAGPHYS` high-$z$ extension version." (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
- **FAST** : ZFOURGE stellar masses (run by Straatman et al. 2016), adopted for comparison  
  > "Here, we use stellar masses obtained with `FAST` to compare our results with the ZFOURGE results (estimated by `FAST`) directly." (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)

## Qualitative findings

- ASAGAO sources primarily lie on the main sequence of star-forming galaxies (high-mass end), with a few starburst-like sources  
  > "the ASAGAO sources primarily lie on the main sequence of star-forming galaxies, although some ASAGAO sources shows starburst-like features" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- ASAGAO sources have systematically lower SFRs than ALESS sources at similar stellar mass  
  > "ASAGAO sources tend to have systematically lower SFRs for a similar stellar mass range" (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- ASAGAO sources lie above the local IRX-beta_UV relations and above ALMA non-detected ZFOURGE sources  
  > "We find that ASAGAO sources tend to have larger IRX values compared to the ALMA-non-detected ZFOURGE sources, as well as the local starburst relations" (4.2.2 The IRX-beta_UV relation)
- ASAGAO contribution to the IR SFRD is >~ 1 dex below the Burgarella et al. 2013 value at 1<z<2 but consistent at 2<z<3  
  > "our results are $\gtrsim$ 1-dex smaller than the cosmic IR SFRD obtained by (cite: burgarella2013) at 1 $<z<$ 2" (5 Contribution to the cosmic SFRD); "our results are consistent with the cosmic IR SFRD by (cite: burgarella2013)" (5 Contribution to the cosmic SFRD)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Higher angular resolution and sensitivity than single-dish (sub)mm surveys are needed to resolve faint dust-obscured sources  
  > "Therefore, more sensitive observations with higher angular resolution are needed." (Introduction)
- Why this method: A conservative S/N = 4.5 threshold is adopted for counterpart identification  
  > "we adopt a more conservative threshold of S/N $=$ 4.5" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
- Alternative: Use of MAGPHYS stellar masses rejected for the comparison; FAST masses adopted to compare directly with ZFOURGE  
  > "Here, we use stellar masses obtained with `FAST` to compare our results with the ZFOURGE results (estimated by `FAST`) directly." (4.1 Stellar masses and SFRs)
- Alternative: ID1 and ID7 removed from conclusions because p > 0.05 (traditional threshold)  
  > "We remove these ASAGAO sources with $p>0.05$ (i.e., ID1 and ID7)" (2.2 ALMA counterparts identification)
- Alternative: ZFOURGE photometric redshift of ID26 (z = 9.354) rejected as an incorrect SED fit; Luo et al. 2017 value used  
  > "has an extremely large photometric redshift ($z$ = 9.354), which is apparently caused by a incorrect SED fitting" (2.4 Redshift distribution of ASAGAO sources)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): MAGPHYS ignores any AGN contribution  
  > "We have to note that `MAGPHYS` ignores any contribution by an AGN." (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic uncertainties such as absolute flux calibration are not included in the SED fitting  
  > "we do not consider systematic uncertainties" (3 SED fitting from optical to millimeter wavelengths)
- Limitation (authors): SFRD estimate may suffer from small statistics and field-to-field variance  
  > "may be suffered from small statistics and/or field-to-field variance" (5 Contribution to the cosmic SFRD)
- Limitation (authors): Redshift difference from classical SMGs may be a selection effect  
  > "although we have to note that this can be caused by selection effect." (6 Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Median redshift is given as 2.38 in the abstract and conclusions but 2.39 in Section 2.4, with no explanation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample is small (22 galaxies after removals, 7 and 15 per redshift bin) and drawn from one field, so medians and the SFRD are sensitive to cosmic variance
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison samples use different stellar-mass methods (MAGPHYS, FAST, BC03 fits) and the paper itself finds 0.2-0.5 dex offsets, which affects the IRX-M* and M*-SFR comparisons
- Caveat (inferred, ours): IRX uses L_UV from rest-frame 2800 A while the literature relations use 1600 A; only approximate equivalence is asserted
- Caveat (inferred, ours): K-band selection and ZFOURGE completeness at high z bias the redshift distribution and the non-detected comparison sample
- Caveat (inferred, ours): AGN contribution is assumed negligible although many sources have Chandra counterparts
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust-property explanations for elevated IRX (starburst age, dust geometry, composition) are not tested with data in this paper
