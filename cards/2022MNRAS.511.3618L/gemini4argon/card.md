## Analysis recipe

1. **Generate Moment maps and visually identify SiO clumps**: Generate Moment 0, Moment 1, and Moment 2 maps of SiO (2-1) using channels with signals > 3 sigma; visually identify SiO clumps in Moment 0 maps and inspect spectra for weak (~2 sigma) clumps.  
  > "For the identification of SiO clumps, we firstly generate the integrated intensity (Moment 0) maps of SiO emission using channels with signals higher than 3${\sigma}$." (Results); "In Moment-0 maps, we have identified 171 SiO clumps in 128 sources by eye." (Results); "Because of the complicated and non-Gaussian SiO line profiles, we instead use their Moment-0 maps, the intensity-weighted velocity (Moment 1) maps, and the intensity-weighted velocity dispersion (Moment 2) maps to derive their velocity ($\nu$), and velocity dispersion ($\sigma_\textup{v}$)." (Detection rates of SiO emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: threshold > 3 sigma for Moment 0 maps  
  > "For the identification of SiO clumps, we firstly generate the integrated intensity (Moment 0) maps of SiO emission using channels with signals higher than 3${\sigma}$." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual identification used to avoid fake detections close to the low-S/N image edges; clumps in marginal regions excluded.  
  > "Therefore, we did not apply any algorithm in clump identification in order to avoid fake detection close to the edges of images where the signal-to-noise levels are low." (Results); "SiO clumps in the marginal region are excluded." (Results)
2. **Measure SiO clump sizes, positions, integrated intensities, and radii**: Fit 2D Gaussians to SiO clumps in Moment-0 maps using CASA imfit; calculate linear radius as geometric mean of major and minor axis FWHM (or use beam size if unresolved).  
  > "Then we used the two-dimensional fitting tools ($imfit$) in CASA to fit these SiO clumps one by one. We get deconvolved sizes (the full width at half maximum (FWHM) and position angle (PA)), center positions and integrated intensity for these SiO clumps." (Results); "We calculated the geometric mean of the major axis FWHM and the minor axis FWHM and got the SiO clumps linear radius." (SiO clumps properties); "we failed to fit 116 clumps because the shape of SiO clumps is irregular and the size of these SiO clumps is smaller than or close to the beam size. Instead, we give the beam size as these clump sizes" (SiO clumps properties)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - assumption: Source distances from Paper I-III  
  > "The basic parameters of the sample were taken from (cite: liu2020atoms), (cite: liu2020atomsII), and (cite: liu2021atomsIII) ( paper I, paper II, and paper III of the series)." (Sample)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional fitting in CASA (imfit)  
  > "Then we used the two-dimensional fitting tools ($imfit$) in CASA to fit these SiO clumps one by one." (Results)

## Qualitative findings

- Shocks traced by SiO (2-1) emission are ubiquitous in massive star-forming regions, with a detection rate of 87.7%.  
  > "Toward the 146 sources, SiO (2-1) emission was detected in 128 (87.7$\%$) sources, indicating that the presence of shocks is very common in high-mass star forming regions." (Detection rates of SiO emission)
- Outflow activities are strongly connected to shocked gas, as clumps with outflow wings have significantly larger SiO velocity dispersions than those without outflows.  
  > "The median velocity dispersion of the SiO line for outflow sources and non-outflow sources is 1.91 km s$^{-1}$ and 0.99 km s$^{-1}$, respectively. These results indicate that outflow activities could be connected to strongly shocked gas." (Abstract)
- SiO velocity dispersion and [SiO]/[H13CO+] ratio do not correlate with dust temperature or particle number density in most sources, indicating SiO emission is governed by shock activities rather than thermal conditions.  
  > "In most sources, the velocity dispersion of SiO emission and \[SiO\]/\[H$^{13}$CO$^+$\] show no obvious correlations with dust temperature ($T_\textup{d}$) and particle number density ($n$). These results indicated that the SiO emission is not likely affected thermal conditions but is more likely affected by shock activities." (Summary)
- SiO line luminosity positively correlates with bolometric luminosity, and sources with HII regions show lower L_SiO/L_bol ratios and a steeper slope, suggesting potential negative feedback from HII regions.  
  > "Sources containing Hii regions show relatively lower $L_\textup{sio}$/$L_\textup{bol}$ ratios, indicating that Hii regions may have negative feedback on surrounding gas and may suppress further star formation, leading to less energetic outflows and thus weaker shocks." (The excitation condition for SiO emission)
- Neither L_SiO nor [SiO]/[H13CO+] correlates with L_bol/M, indicating the fraction of shocked gas does not vary significantly across evolutionary stages.  
  > "In addition, we do not see clear correlations between the SiO line luminosity and $L_\textup{bol}/M$. There is also no robust trend in the \[SiO\]/\[H$^{13}$CO$^+$\] and $L_\textup{bol}/M$ relation. This implies that the fraction of shocked gas in dense gas does not change obviously at various evolutionary stages." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ACA 7m observations provide a large field of view across a large sample of 146 massive star-forming regions, enabling a complete catalog of SiO emission clumps.  
  > "We focus the latter in this work with an aim at compiling a complete catalog of SiO emission clumps owing to the benefit from the large field of view of the ACA observations." (ALMA Observations)
- Why this method: Visual clump identification avoids fake detections near low-S/N image edges; Moment 0, 1, 2 maps handle complicated non-Gaussian SiO profiles; combining SiO, HCO+, and CS line wings overcomes low SiO abundance and HCO+ self-absorption when identifying outflows.  
  > "We identified SiO clumps by eye because there are not so many SiO clumps within individual sources and the emission peaks of most SiO clumps are clearly separated from each other in these low resolution ACA images. Therefore, we did not apply any algorithm in clump identification in order to avoid fake detection close to the edges of images where the signal-to-noise levels are low." (Results); "Because of the complicated and non-Gaussian SiO line profiles, we instead use their Moment-0 maps, the intensity-weighted velocity (Moment 1) maps, and the intensity-weighted velocity dispersion (Moment 2) maps to derive their velocity ($\nu$), and velocity dispersion ($\sigma_\textup{v}$)." (Detection rates of SiO emission); "However, in some sources the line profile of HCO$^{+}$ emission is self-absorbed. We inspect the spectra of CS and find its spectra to be less self-absorbed. As a consequence, we use the line wings of SiO, HCO$^{+}$, and CS together to identify strong outflows." (The velocity dispersion of SiO and the line wings of SiO, HCO$^{+}$, and CS)
- Alternative: Automated clump-finding algorithms were rejected in favor of visual identification to avoid fake detections near low-S/N image edges.  
  > "Therefore, we did not apply any algorithm in clump identification in order to avoid fake detection close to the edges of images where the signal-to-noise levels are low." (Results)
- Alternative: Gaussian profile fitting for the main sample of 171 clumps was rejected in favor of Moment 0, 1, and 2 maps because 60% of the SiO line profiles are complicated and non-Gaussian.  
  > "Because of the complicated and non-Gaussian SiO line profiles, we instead use their Moment-0 maps, the intensity-weighted velocity (Moment 1) maps, and the intensity-weighted velocity dispersion (Moment 2) maps to derive their velocity ($\nu$), and velocity dispersion ($\sigma_\textup{v}$)." (Detection rates of SiO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 116 of the 171 SiO clumps could not be deconvolved by CASA imfit due to irregular shapes and sizes smaller than or close to the ACA beam size (~13''), requiring the beam size to be adopted as an upper limit.  
  > "we failed to fit 116 clumps because the shape of SiO clumps is irregular and the size of these SiO clumps is smaller than or close to the beam size. Instead, we give the beam size as these clump sizes" (SiO clumps properties)
- Limitation (authors): Clumps classified as without strong outflows cannot be ruled out from hosting weak or unresolved low-mass outflows.  
  > "However, we cannot exclude these clumps without outflows that are not associated with weak outflows." (The velocity dispersion of SiO and the line wings of SiO, HCO$^{+}$, and CS)
- Limitation (authors): 12 sources show absorption features in H13CO+ spectra and had to be excluded from the [SiO]/[H13CO+] analysis, and I17441-2822 had to be excluded due to complicated Galactic center line profiles.  
  > "We exclude I17441-2822 spectra in further analysis because this source is close to the galactic center and shows very complicated line profiles." (The velocity dispersion of SiO and the line wings of SiO, HCO$^{+}$, and CS); "We inspected H$^{13}$CO$^+$ spectra and found that 12 sources have absorption features in their spectra. As a result, in the latter analysis, we ignore these 12 sources." (The fraction of shocked gas)
- Limitation (authors): H13CO+ abundance may not be strictly constant with time, which could affect the interpretation of [SiO]/[H13CO+] intensity ratios as shocked gas fraction.  
  > "However, H$^{13}$CO$^+$ abundance may not be constant with time (cite: sanhueza2012chemistry), which may affect the interpretation of \[SiO\]/\[H$^{13}$CO$^+$\] ratios." (The excitation condition for SiO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual ('by eye') identification of clumps and subjective visual classification of line wings introduce human bias and reduce strict reproducibility.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): More than two-thirds (116/171) of the SiO clumps are unresolved by the ~13'' ACA beam, which affects the derived linear radii and aperture-extracted quantities.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using the [SiO]/[H13CO+] integrated intensity ratio as a proxy for shocked gas fraction assumes optical thinness and constant excitation temperatures without multi-transition excitation analysis.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Clump physical properties (L_bol, M, T_d, n) are taken from single-dish/continuum literature compilations at the parent clump scale rather than measured at the individual SiO clump scale.
