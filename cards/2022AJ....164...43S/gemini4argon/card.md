## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: How do molecular cloud populations depend on local and global properties of the host galaxy, and what are the characteristic timescales relevant to molecular cloud evolution and star formation across 80 PHANGS-ALMA galaxies?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive individual molecular cloud and pixel-level physical properties**: Extract object-by-object properties from PHANGS-ALMA CPROPS catalogs and pixel-by-pixel properties from strict CO(2-1) moment maps at 60, 90, 120, and 150 pc resolutions, applying inclination corrections.  
  > "In this study, we measure properties of molecular cloud populations from the PHANGS–ALMA CO data using two different approaches." (PHANGS–ALMA CO Data); "These catalogs are constructed by running CPROPS (cite: Rosolowsky_Leroy_2006) on data cubes at a set of common spatial resolutions (60, 90, 120, and 150 pc whenever available)." (Object-by-object Measurements); "For our pixel-by-pixel analysis, we primarily use the “strict” moment maps so that only pixels with reliable measurements are included in our calculation." (Pixel-by-pixel Measurements)
   - software: CPROPS
   - parameter: Spatial resolutions: 60, 90, 120, 150 pc  
  > "As an alternative to the object-by-object approach, we also derive molecular gas properties at fixed spatial resolutions (60, 90, 120, and 150 pc whenever available)" (Pixel-by-pixel Measurements)
   - assumption: CO(2-1)-to-CO(1-0) line ratio R_21 = 0.65  
  > "Here $R_\mathrm{21}=0.65$ is the adopted $\mathrm{CO}\,(2\text{--}1)$ to $\mathrm{CO}\,(1\text{--}0)$ line ratio (cite: denBrok_etal_2021; Leroy_etal_2021c)" (Object-by-object Measurements)
   - assumption: Fiducial metallicity-dependent conversion factor alpha_CO(1-0) = 4.35 * Z'^-1.6 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1  
  > "By default, we use a metallicity-dependent $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ prescription as described in (cite: Sun_etal_2020b): $$\begin{equation}
  \alpha_\mathrm{CO(1\text{--}0)} = 4.35~Z^\mathrm{\prime-1.6}~\mathrm{M_\odot~(\mbox{$\rm K~km~s^{-1}$}~pc^2)^{-1}}~.
  \label{eq:alphaCO}
  \end{equation}$$" (Object-by-object Measurements)
   - assumption: Molecular gas disk thickness H = 100 pc  
  > "Here $H=100$ pc is an assumed molecular gas disk thickness perpendicular to the galaxy plane (cite: Heyer_Dame_2015)" (Object-by-object Measurements)
   - assumption: Inclination correction of cos(i) for surface density and (cos(i))^0.5 for velocity dispersion  
  > "to eliminate the apparent $\cos{i}$ dependence in these models (which should not be present were these models reflecting purely physical trends), one would have to multiply $\cos{i}$ to the measured surface densities and $(\cos{i})^{0.5}$ to the velocity dispersions." (Inclination Corrections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared surface density-velocity dispersion relation before and after inclination corrections (Appendix 10) and compared object-based vs pixel-based approaches  
  > "Figure [9](#fig:incl) compares the surface density–velocity dispersion relation before and after applying the inclination corrections." (Inclination Corrections)
2. **Aggregate cloud and environmental properties in kpc-scale apertures and correct for completeness**: Divide each galaxy into 1.5 kpc hexagonal apertures (and 500 pc radial bins). Compute mass-weighted ensemble averages of cloud properties, clumping factor, and area-weighted local environmental metrics. Apply lognormal PDF completeness corrections.  
  > "We divide the sky footprint of each galaxy into a set of hexagonal apertures, as illustrated in Figure [2](#fig:image)." (Defining Averaging Apertures); "Within each aperture, we calculate the ensemble average of molecular cloud measurements using a molecular gas mass-weighted averaging scheme." (Aggregating Molecular Cloud Measurements); "To account for this systematic bias, we introduce a correction factor for our measurements in each aperture based on the $f_\mathrm{flux}$ and $f_\mathrm{area}$ values in that aperture" (CO Flux Completeness and Corrections)
   - software: MegaTable
   - parameter: Hexagonal aperture spacing = 1.5 kpc (projected area 1.95 kpc^2), out to r_gal = 1.5 r_25  
  > "Adjacent apertures are separated by a linear distance of 1.5 kpc, which implies that each aperture has a projected area of 1.95 kpc$^2$ on the sky." (Defining Averaging Apertures); "For a complete coverage of the galaxy footprint, we include all apertures covering out to $r_\mathrm{gal}= 1.5r_\mathrm{25}$ in each galaxy" (Defining Averaging Apertures)
   - parameter: Radial bin width = 500 pc  
  > "Specifically, we define a series of radial bins that are 500 pc in width and again cover out to $r_\mathrm{gal}= 1.5\,r_\mathrm{25}$ in each galaxy." (Defining Averaging Apertures)
   - assumption: Intrinsic CO intensity distribution within each aperture follows a lognormal distribution above a sharp intensity threshold  
  > "We assume that the CO intensity distribution (or equivalently, molecular gas surface density distribution) has a lognormal shape within each averaging aperture, and that the aforementioned selection bias prevents us from detecting CO emission below an intensity threshold." (CO Flux Completeness and Corrections)
   - assumption: Optically thin HI 21 cm emission with 35% helium/heavy elements contribution  
  > "Assuming optically thin 21 cm emission, we convert 21 cm line intensity $I_\mathrm{21cm}$ to atomic gas surface density $\Sigma_\mathrm{atom}$" (H I Data); "Here $\Sigma_\mathrm{atom}$ includes the (extra 35%) mass of helium and heavier elements." (H I Data)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF for UV+MIR SFR and global stellar mass; Kroupa IMF for Halpha+MIR SFR  
  > "These prescriptions assume a Chabrier initial mass function (cite: Chabrier_2003) via their calibration against (cite: Salim_etal_2016)" (Mid-IR and UV Data); "This prescription assumes constant star formation over 100 Myr and a Kroupa IMF (cite: Kroupa_2001)." (Other Data)
   - assumption: Global mass-metallicity relation with a fixed radial metallicity gradient of -0.1 dex/r_e  
  > "we first infer the metallicity at $r_\mathrm{gal}= 1.0\,r_\mathrm{e}$ in each galaxy based on a galaxy global mass–metallicity relationship (cite: Sanchez_etal_2019), and then extrapolate to all $r_\mathrm{gal}$ assuming a fixed radial metallicity gradient of $-0.1\,\mathrm{dex}/r_\mathrm{e}$" (Aggregating Local Environmental Metrics)
   - fitting: Analytical inverse error function solution for lognormal PDF width and threshold from f_flux and f_area  
  > "Notice that these two relations allow us to inversely solve for $\sigma_\mathrm{int}$ and $\ln(I_\mathrm{CO,\,th}/\bar{I}_\mathrm{CO,\,int})$ and express their combinations in terms of the measurable quantities $f_\mathrm{area}$ and $f_\mathrm{flux}$" (Completeness Corrections)
   - validation: Compared predicted metallicities against PHANGS-MUSE observations in 18 galaxies; compared UV/IR SFR against H-alpha SFR  
  > "To evaluate these concerns, we compare the UV/IR-based $\Sigma_\mathrm{SFR}$ measurements with H$\alpha$-based measurements" (Aggregating Local Environmental Metrics); "As a sanity check, we compare our predictions to the observed two-dimensional metallicity distributions in 18 galaxies in the PHANGS–MUSE survey (cite: Williams_etal_2021a)." (Metallicity)
3. **Characterize pairwise correlations and perform variable selection for predictive models**: Calculate Spearman's rank correlation coefficients and variance inflation factors (VIFs) across 871 high-completeness apertures, then perform multivariable linear regression in log space using Lasso regression and BIC model selection.  
  > "we calculate Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients for each pair of population-averaged molecular cloud property and host galaxy environmental metric within the sub-selected $871$ apertures." (Pairwise Correlation); "With this regression setup, we perform a *lasso* model fit (cite: Tibshirani_1996) and use the Bayesian Information Criterion (cite: Schwarz_1978) for model selection. This is implemented with the `LassoLarsIC` function in the `scikit-learn` Python library." (Variable Selection Methodology)
   - software: scikit-learn
   - parameter: Subsample selection: 871 apertures from 42 galaxies with f_flux > 0.5 and f_area > 0.2  
  > "this part of the analysis works with a subsample of $871$ apertures from 42 galaxies." (Environmental Dependence of Molecular Cloud Populations in PHANGS-ALMA); "the PHANGS–ALMA CO data have reasonably high flux completeness and area coverage fraction inside these apertures ($f_\mathrm{flux}>0.5$ and $f_\mathrm{area}>0.2$, see Appendix [11](#apdx:completeness))" (Environmental Dependence of Molecular Cloud Populations in PHANGS-ALMA)
   - parameter: Noise variance sigma^2 = 0.1 dex^2 for M, R, Sigma, sigma; sigma^2 = 0.25 dex^2 for P_turb and alpha_vir; model selection threshold Delta BIC <= 10  
  > "Considering these uncertainties, we conservatively use a noise variance of $\sigma^2=0.1\;\mathrm{dex}^2$ (i.e., about a factor of 2) for the cloud masses, sizes, surface densities, and velocity dispersions. We use a larger variance of $\sigma^2=0.25\;\mathrm{dex}^2$" (Variable Selection Methodology); "identify a subset of candidate models that satisfy $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}\equiv\mathrm{BIC}-\mathrm{BIC_{min}}\leq10$ (cite: Kass_Raftery_1995). Among this subset, we select the one model that corresponds to the largest $\alpha$ value" (Variable Selection Methodology)
   - assumption: Gaussian error in logarithmic space for BIC calculation  
  > "This expression of BIC is derived from its formal definition of $d\ln(n)-2\ln(\hat{L})$ assuming Gaussian error, with $\hat{L}$ being the maximum of the likelihood function." (Variable Selection Methodology)
   - fitting: Lasso regression minimizing squared residuals plus L1 penalty, with BIC model selection via LassoLarsIC  
  > "With this regression setup, we perform a *lasso* model fit (cite: Tibshirani_1996) and use the Bayesian Information Criterion (cite: Schwarz_1978) for model selection. This is implemented with the `LassoLarsIC` function in the `scikit-learn` Python library." (Variable Selection Methodology)
   - validation: Evaluated R^2, residual scatter in dex, and Delta BIC relative to a null model  
  > "We also report in Table [3](#tab:models) the model residual scatter, the model coefficient of determination ($R^2$, which quantifies the model explanatory power), and the BIC difference between the preferred model and a null model" (Variable Selection Outcomes)
4. **Estimate characteristic timescales of molecular cloud evolution and star formation**: Calculate mass-weighted harmonic mean free-fall time, turbulence crossing time, and cloud-cloud collision time, alongside orbital time, shearing time, and molecular gas depletion time across apertures, and compute Spearman rank correlations with depletion time.  
  > "In this section, we estimate six different characteristic timescales as a use case demonstration for our rich multiwavelength measurements." (Characteristic Timescales of Molecular Cloud Evolution in PHANGS–ALMA); "Beyond the typical values of the estimated timescales and their ratios, we also examine which timescales correlate the best with $t_\mathrm{dep,\,mol}$." (Timescale Comparisons)
   - assumption: Spherical symmetry for free-fall time calculation  
  > "We estimate this timescale from the mean volume density of molecular clouds under the assumption of spherical symmetry." (Timescale Definitions)
   - assumption: Shear-induced cloud-cloud collision model with random spatial distribution of clouds on adjacent circular orbits  
  > "We estimate this timescale following a simplified model of shear-induced collision (cite: Tan_2000). The key assumptions are that molecular clouds are randomly distributed in each aperture, and that cloud–cloud collision happens *only* when clouds catch up with other clouds on adjacent circular orbits due to orbital shear." (Timescale Definitions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived cloud internal timescales against independent cloud lifetime estimates from Chevance et al. and Kim et al.  
  > "For reference, we also mark the range of molecular cloud lifetimes, $t_\mathrm{life}$, measured from the spatial (de-)correlation of molecular gas and young star tracers in a subset of PHANGS–ALMA targets (cite: Chevance_etal_2020a; Chevance_etal_2020b; KimJY_etal_2020)." (Timescale Comparisons)

## Qualitative findings

- When weighting all kpc-scale apertures equally, the typical environment probed by PHANGS-ALMA resembles the Solar Neighborhood, whereas most molecular gas mass resides in higher surface density environments not matched by kpc-scale regions in the Milky Way.  
  > "When weighting all apertures equally, the most representative local environment in our sample closely resembles the Solar Neighborhood in many aspects. Comparatively, most molecular gas mass is hosted in regions that are closer to the galaxy center" (Sub-galactic Environments Probed by PHANGS–ALMA)
- Population-averaged cloud properties correlate strongly with both local kpc-scale environmental metrics and global host galaxy properties, with clouds in galaxy centers and bars having systematically higher surface densities, velocity dispersions, and turbulent pressures.  
  > "The population-averaged cloud properties in each aperture correlate strongly with both local environmental properties and host galaxy global properties." (Abstract); "The sense of these correlations indicate that cloud populations with higher average mass, surface density, and turbulence strength prefer galactic environments at smaller galactocentric radii, higher gas, star, and SFR surface densities, shorter orbital period, and stronger shear." (Summary)
- Kpc-scale molecular gas surface density and SFR surface density carry the most predictive power for cloud properties; once controlled for, global galaxy properties and galactocentric radius add little explanatory power.  
  > "Leveraging a variable selection analysis, we find that the kpc-scale surface densities of molecular gas and SFR tend to possess the most predictive power for the population-averaged cloud properties. Once their variations are controlled for, galaxy global properties contain little additional information" (Abstract)
- Cloud internal timescales (free-fall and crossing times, ~5-20 Myr) are comparable to cloud lifetimes, galactic dynamical timescales (orbital, shear, collision, ~100 Myr) are an order of magnitude longer, and molecular gas depletion time (1-3 Gyr) shows weak to no correlation with the other timescales.  
  > "The cloud-scale free-fall time and turbulence crossing time are ${\sim}5{-}20$ Myr, comparable to previous cloud lifetime estimates. The timescales for orbital motion, shearing, and cloud–cloud collisions are longer, ${\sim}100$ Myr. The molecular gas depletion time is $1{-}3$ Gyr" (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: PHANGS-ALMA provides sensitive, high-resolution (<=150 pc), wide field-of-view CO(2-1) imaging across 80 moderately inclined (i <= 75 deg) main-sequence galaxies with rich multiwavelength ancillary coverage, enabling representative GMC population statistics across diverse environments.  
  > "This survey provides sensitive, high resolution, wide field-of-view $\mathrm{CO}\,(2\text{--}1)$ imaging data for ${\sim}90$ nearby, high-mass, star-forming galaxies." (Introduction); "We select these galaxies according to two criteria: (1) their PHANGS–ALMA $\mathrm{CO}\,(2\text{--}1)$ observations have beam full-width-half-maximum (FWHM) sizes corresponding to physical scales of $150$ pc or smaller" (Data); "and (2) they are not too heavily inclined ($i \lesssim 75^\circ$), so that we can unambiguously determine the locations of molecular clouds in the host galaxy." (Data)
- Why this method: Both object-by-object (CPROPS) and pixel-by-pixel approaches are used to complement each other and test methodological sensitivity; sharp-boundary hexagonal apertures avoid built-in correlations between adjacent regions; Lasso regression with BIC handles strong multicollinearity among environmental metrics.  
  > "While the two approaches access similar physical properties and often lead to similar results (cite: Sun_etal_2020a; Rosolowsky_etal_2021), they complement each other in important ways." (PHANGS–ALMA CO Data); "Since one of the main goals of this work is to derive reliable molecular cloud population statistics, we deem the “sharp boundary” scheme more appropriate here" (Aggregating Molecular Cloud Measurements); "This approach has the advantage of removing irrelevant feature variables while optimizing prediction accuracy in the face of multicollinearity." (Variable Selection)
- Alternative: Noise-homogenized CPROPS catalogs were rejected in favor of native-noise catalogs to allow direct comparison with pixel-by-pixel measurements and to recover as much CO emission above the noise floor as possible.  
  > "Note that A. Hughes et al. (in preparation) present two versions of CPROPS catalogs for the PHANGS-ALMA sample: one is constructed from data cubes whose noise levels are homogenized among all galaxies, and the other from data cubes with the native noise. We use the latter version" (Object-by-object Measurements)
- Alternative: Gaussian kernel convolution for aperture averaging (as in Leroy et al. 2016 and Sun et al. 2020b) was rejected in favor of sharp hexagonal aperture boundaries to avoid correlated adjacent measurements and biases near bright galaxy centers.  
  > "In that work, the averaging is performed via a Gaussian kernel convolution, in which case the averaging result at any given location has a nonzero response to molecular clouds far away from that location." (Aggregating Molecular Cloud Measurements); "We note that our methodology for calculating this kpc-scale aperture averaged $\Sigma_\mathrm{mol}$ is different from the one used in (cite: Sun_etal_2020b)." (Aggregating Local Environmental Metrics)
- Alternative: Four alternative CO-to-H2 conversion factor prescriptions (constant Galactic, Narayanan+12, Bolatto+13, Gong+20) were calculated and included in the database alongside the fiducial metallicity-dependent prescription.  
  > "Beyond this fiducial $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ prescription, we calculate four alternative prescriptions, following and expanding on (cite: Sun_etal_2020b)." (CO-to-H2 Conversion Factor)
- Alternative: Radial 500 pc bins were computed in parallel to hexagonal apertures and included in the data release.  
  > "In addition to the hexagonal tiling method described above, we also run a parallel line of analysis with a different binning scheme. Specifically, we define a series of radial bins that are 500 pc in width" (Defining Averaging Apertures)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Assumed constant molecular gas disk scale height H = 100 pc is uncertain by a factor of ~2 and does not capture radial or galaxy-to-galaxy variations.  
  > "Our adopted value for $H$ is likely uncertain by a factor of ${\sim}\,2$ due to variations within a galaxy and among galaxies (cite: Yim_etal_2014; Bacchini_etal_2019a)." (Object-by-object Measurements)
- Limitation (authors): Finite resolution (60-150 pc) and sensitivity limit the ability to identify clouds below ~10^5 M_sun, causing blending of moderate-sized clouds in CPROPS and beam dilution in pixel-based surface densities.  
  > "As pointed out by (cite: Rosolowsky_etal_2021), the finite resolution and sensitivity of the PHANGS–ALMA CO data limit our ability to identify molecular clouds with mass $\lesssim10^5~\mbox{$\rm M_\odot$}$." (Molecular Cloud Populations in PHANGS-ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Completeness corrections are not applied to cloud size, velocity dispersion, or virial parameter because their appropriate functional forms remain uncertain, and the lognormal PDF assumption breaks down in apertures with low area coverage or f_flux < f_area.  
  > "but we leave molecular cloud size, velocity dispersion, and virial parameter uncorrected. Though these latter quantities likely do suffer sensitivity-induced selection biases (cite: Sun_etal_2018), the appropriate functional forms of their completeness corrections remain uncertain at present." (CO Flux Completeness and Corrections); "These apertures tend to have low $f_\mathrm{area}$, possibly suggesting that these apertures include true “empty” areas devoid of CO emission, in which case the lognormal PDF assumption is no longer appropriate." (Completeness Corrections)
- Limitation (authors): Fiducial metallicity-dependent alpha_CO prescription only mildly depresses alpha_CO in galaxy centers and does not account for excitation temperature variations in R_21.  
  > "Several lines of evidence suggest lower $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ in galaxy centers (cite: Bolatto_etal_2013; Sandstrom_etal_2013; Israel_2020; Teng_etal_2021) and our fiducial prescription only mildly depresses $\alpha_\mathrm{CO}$ near galaxy centers." (Molecular Cloud Populations in PHANGS-ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Line-of-sight blending of multiple velocity components in crowded galaxy centers/bars biases pixel-based effective line widths and virial parameters high, while beam smearing from galactic rotation may affect inferred dynamical states.  
  > "in places where molecular clouds are spatially crowded (such as galaxy centers), the $ppv$ space segmentation in CPROPS helps to demarcate clouds that fall along the same line of sight but are separable in velocity space, whereas the pixel-based analysis simply measures the line effective width" (Molecular Cloud Populations in PHANGS-ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Cloud-cloud collision timescale estimates are likely biased high because they neglect random non-circular cloud motions, spatial clustering, and sub-beam cloud blending.  
  > "We caution that the simplifying assumptions involved in Equations [\[eq:t_coll\]](#eq:t_coll) and [\[eq:t_coll_pix\]](#eq:t_coll_pix) likely bias our $\bar{t}_\mathrm{coll}$ estimates high." (Timescale Definitions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The strong correlation between cloud-scale surface density (<Sigma_pix> or <Sigma_obj>) and kpc-scale molecular gas surface density (Sigma_mol) is partly built-in by construction because both derive from the same PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) dataset and share the exact same aperture-level alpha_CO and R_21 conversion factors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Some input datasets cited in the workflow (e.g., Hughes et al. in prep CPROPS v4 catalogs, Nofech et al. in prep analytical rotation curve fits, Sardone et al. in prep PHANGS-VLA HI maps, Razza et al. in prep PHANGS-Halpha v2.3 maps) were not yet published as standalone refereed releases at the time of publication, though their aggregated aperture values are released in the paper's MegaTable.
