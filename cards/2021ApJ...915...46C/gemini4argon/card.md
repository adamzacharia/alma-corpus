## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA continuum and CO(1-0) observations**: Run ALMA Science Pipeline calibration, merge execution blocks where needed (GRB 050709), and image continuum and a 180 km s^-1 CO(1-0) velocity-integrated map using TCLEAN with Briggs weighting  
  > "Data calibration was performed using the ALMA Science Pipeline Software of CASA version 4.7.0-1. Maps were processed using the TCLEAN algorithm with the Briggs weighting (with a robust parameter of 0.5)." (XRF 020903); "Furthermore, a CO ($J=1-0$) velocity-integrated map with a velocity width of 180 km $^{-1}$ was created using the TCLEAN algorithm." (XRF 020903); "Two execution blocks for GRB 050709 observations (Project code: 2016.1.01333.S) at the identical band (Band 6) were reduced separately. We reproduced the calibrated data, merged them, and then imaged them using TCLEAN." (GRB Host Galaxy Samples)
   - software: CASA, ALMA Science Pipeline Software, TCLEAN
   - parameter: Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5  
  > "Maps were processed using the TCLEAN algorithm with the Briggs weighting (with a robust parameter of 0.5)." (XRF 020903)
   - parameter: CO(1-0) velocity integration width = 180 km s^-1  
  > "Furthermore, a CO ($J=1-0$) velocity-integrated map with a velocity width of 180 km $^{-1}$ was created using the TCLEAN algorithm." (XRF 020903)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum fluxes, upper limits, and extract CO spectrum**: Measure total flux for detections using CASA imfit, estimate RMS noise for nondetections using CASA imstat, and extract CO(1-0) spectrum within a 0".8 radius aperture centered on the host galaxy  
  > "The spectra around the CO($J=1-0$) line for the host region was centered at R.A. = $22^{h}48^{m}42^{s}.37$, decl. = $-20^{\circ}46^{'}08".72$ with a radius of $0".8$(i.e., including all four optical components) indicates a null detection (Figure [2](#spec))." (XRF 020903); "The total flux measurements and the RMS noise estimations for the nondetection cases were performed using function "imfit" and "imstat", respectively." (GRB Host Galaxy Samples)
   - software: CASA, imfit, imstat
   - parameter: Spectral extraction aperture centered at R.A. = 22h48m42s.37, decl. = -20d46'08".72 with radius 0".8  
  > "The spectra around the CO($J=1-0$) line for the host region was centered at R.A. = $22^{h}48^{m}42^{s}.37$, decl. = $-20^{\circ}46^{'}08".72$ with a radius of $0".8$(i.e., including all four optical components) indicates a null detection (Figure [2](#spec))." (XRF 020903)
   - fitting: imfit used for total flux measurements  
  > "The total flux measurements and the RMS noise estimations for the nondetection cases were performed using function "imfit" and "imstat", respectively." (GRB Host Galaxy Samples)
   - validation: Confirmed continuum measurements of reference GRB samples were consistent with previous literature results  
  > "We confirmed that our continuum measurements were consistent with previous results (cite: wang12; huang17; berger14; hatsukade20)." (GRB Host Galaxy Samples)
3. **Derive IR luminosity and star formation rate from dust continuum**: Apply target redshift to luminosity-dependent IR SED templates from Chary & Elbaz (2001) to constrain L_IR from continuum flux/limits, then convert L_IR to SFR using Kennicutt (1998)  
  > "Based on (cite: wang12), a redshift to a template IR SED was applied to these results. We referred to the SED library provided by (cite: chary01), which is luminosity-dependent ($2\times10^8$ to $4\times10^{13} L_\ensuremath{\odot}$) based on a locally calibrated luminosity-dust temperature relation and does not enable SED scaling." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies); "Using the SFR conversion of star-forming galaxies, SFR($M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ yr$^{-1})=1.7\times10^{-10} L_{\rm IR}/L_\ensuremath{\odot}$ (cite: kennicutt98), we obtained the 3$\sigma$ upper limit of the SFR as $<2.2$ ($M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$)." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
   - assumption: Cosmological parameters H0 = 70 km s^-1, Omega_0 = 0.3, Omega_lambda = 0.7  
  > "In Sections 4 and 5, we present the results of the continuum and CO line observations and discuss their properties, respectively, based on using the following cosmological parameters: $H_{0}$=70 $\rm {km\ s^{-1}}$, $\Omega_{0}=0.3$, and $\Omega_{\lambda}=0.7$." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Chary & Elbaz (2001) luminosity-dependent IR SED templates without scaling  
  > "We referred to the SED library provided by (cite: chary01), which is luminosity-dependent ($2\times10^8$ to $4\times10^{13} L_\ensuremath{\odot}$) based on a locally calibrated luminosity-dust temperature relation and does not enable SED scaling." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
   - assumption: Kennicutt (1998) conversion SFR(M_sun yr^-1) = 1.7 x 10^-10 L_IR / L_sun  
  > "Using the SFR conversion of star-forming galaxies, SFR($M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ yr$^{-1})=1.7\times10^{-10} L_{\rm IR}/L_\ensuremath{\odot}$ (cite: kennicutt98), we obtained the 3$\sigma$ upper limit of the SFR as $<2.2$ ($M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$)." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
   - fitting: Template matching without SED scaling  
  > "We referred to the SED library provided by (cite: chary01), which is luminosity-dependent ($2\times10^8$ to $4\times10^{13} L_\ensuremath{\odot}$) based on a locally calibrated luminosity-dust temperature relation and does not enable SED scaling." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
   - validation: Checked consistency of IR SED templates with HST optical flux measurements and compared IR-derived SFR with H-alpha SFR  
  > "Moreover, the templates with $L_{\rm IR} <1.3\times10^{10} L_\ensuremath{\odot}$ are also consistent with optical flux measurements (the blue line in the Figure [3](#020903temp))." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies); "This estimation is consistent with the measurement (2.65$M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ yr$^{-1}$) using $H_{\alpha}$ or rather small (cite: ghost)." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
4. **Derive CO luminosity, molecular gas mass, gas fraction, and gas depletion timescale**: Calculate L'_CO upper limit using Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005) equation, convert to M_gas using metallicity-dependent and Galactic alpha_CO factors, and compute gas depletion timescale t_depl = M_gas / SFR  
  > "The luminosity of CO was estimated based on the equation ($L'_{\rm CO} = 3.25 \times 10^{7}S_{\rm CO}\Delta\nu\nu_{obs}^{-2}D_{L}^{2}(1+z)^{-3}$) described by (cite: solomon05), where $L'_{\rm CO}$ is in K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}, S_{\rm CO}\Delta\nu$ is the velocity-integrated intensity in Jy km s$^{-1}$, $\nu_{\rm obs}$ is the observed line frequency in GHz, and $D_{L}$ is the luminosity distance in Mpc." (Molecular Gas Mass); "The upper limit of the molecular gas mass of the XRF 020903 host galaxy was derived from $M_{gas}=\alpha_{\rm CO}L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}$, where $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ is the CO-to-H$_{2}$ conversion factor, including the contribution of the helium mass." (Molecular Gas Mass); "The majority of the GRB host galaxies were located on the molecular gas depletion timescale ($t_{\rm depl}$=$M_{\rm gas}$/SFR) of $\sim$1 Gyr, whereas some hosts with lower redshift had a shorter gas depletion timescale (Figure [6](#sfrmgas))." (Molecular Gas Mass-SFR, Gas Fraction, and Depletion Timescale)
   - assumption: CO velocity width = 180 km s^-1  
  > "Because the CO(1-0) line was not detected from the ALMA observation of the XRF 020903 host galaxy, we derived the upper limit of the CO luminosity by assuming a velocity width of 180 km s$^{-1}$ (Table [1](#tab))." (Molecular Gas Mass)
   - assumption: Metallicity-dependent alpha_CO from harmonic mean of Genzel et al. (2012) and Bolatto et al. (2013): alpha_CO = 15 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1 for region A (12+log(O/H) = 8.1) and alpha_CO = 9.6 for regions B, C, D (12+log(O/H) ~ 8.2); Galactic alpha_CO = 4.4  
  > "Therefore, we adopted the harmonic mean of the recipes of (cite: genzel12) and Bolatto et al. (2013), similar to (cite: hatsukade20). The converted upper limits of the gas mass were $M_{\rm gas}<3.9\times10^{9}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ with $\alpha_{\rm CO}=15$ $M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$) for region A and $M_{\rm gas}<2.5\times10^{9}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ with $\alpha_{\rm CO}=9.6$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$) for regions B, C, and D." (Molecular Gas Mass); "The smaller upper limit of $M_{\rm gas}<1.1\times10^{9}$ was estimated by applying the galactic conversion factor of $\alpha_{\rm CO}$=4.4 (cite: bolatto13b)." (Molecular Gas Mass)
   - assumption: H-alpha SFR = 2.65 M_sun yr^-1 used for M_gas-SFR and depletion timescale comparison  
  > "The SFR of XRF 020903 estimated using the $H_{\alpha}$ line as 2.65 $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ yr$^{-1}$ (cite: ghost), was used." (Molecular Gas Mass-SFR, Gas Fraction, and Depletion Timescale)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived M_gas, gas fraction, and depletion timescale against GRB/SLSN host samples and star-forming galaxy scaling relations  
  > "We compared the molecular gas mass of the XRF 020903 with other GRB and SLSN samples reported by (cite: hatsukade20) as a function of redshift (Figure [5](#mgas2mstar), top) and stellar mass (Figure [5](#mgas2mstar), bottom)." (Molecular Gas Mass)

## Qualitative findings

- Neither dust continuum (Band 3 and Band 6) nor CO(1-0) line emission was detected from the XRF 020903 host galaxy.  
  > "By resolving the four optical host galaxy components, the 3$\sigma$ upper limits of 0.032 mJy at 113 GHz and 0.061 mJy at 224 GHz were estimated." (XRF 020903); "The spectra around the CO($J=1-0$) line for the host region was centered at R.A. = $22^{h}48^{m}42^{s}.37$, decl. = $-20^{\circ}46^{'}08".72$ with a radius of $0".8$(i.e., including all four optical components) indicates a null detection (Figure [2](#spec))." (XRF 020903)
- The SFR upper limit derived from L_IR (< 2.2 M_sun yr^-1) is slightly smaller than the optical H-alpha SFR (2.65 M_sun yr^-1), indicating low dust content similar to several other GRB host galaxies.  
  > "The SFR estimation for the XRF 020903 host obtained using the $L_{\rm IR}$ method was one of the notable events that exhibited smaller values than the optical measurements, similar to GRB 031203 ($z=0.105$), GRB 050826 ($z=0.296$), GRB 051006 ($z=1.059$), and GRB 060814 ($z=1.923$). This result indicates that the low dust content of the GRB host galaxies." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
- The molecular gas mass upper limit and gas depletion timescale of the XRF 020903 host galaxy match the trends of GRB and SLSN host galaxies, supporting the common origin of XRF 020903 and classical GRBs.  
  > "The gas mass matched with the global trend of the GRB host galaxies, in addition to the redshift and stellar mass." (Summary); "As one of the potential common properties of GRB host galaxies, we confirmed that the gas depletion timescale is equivalent to those of the GRBs and SLSNs at the same redshift range (i.e., GRB 031203, GRB 050826, and PTF10tpz). These results indicate the similar properties of the XRF 020903 host galaxy to those of GRB host galaxies and supports the identical origins of XRF 020903 and GRBs." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: XRF 020903 is the best-characterized off-axis GRB sample (with redshift, low E_peak, SN association, and off-axis afterglow rebrightening), and ALMA dust continuum and CO observations allow testing whether its host environment matches classical GRB hosts.  
  > "We investigated the radio properties of the host galaxy of X-ray flash, XRF 020903, which is the best example for investigating of the off-axis origin of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs)." (Abstract); "Among all the XRF samples, XRF 020903 was the only sample suitable for investigating its origin." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used HST optical photometry rather than ground-based multicolor photometry for SED comparison because ground-based data were contaminated by nearby galaxies; used harmonic mean of Genzel et al. (2012) and Bolatto et al. (2013) for alpha_CO because low-metallicity conversion recipes differ and are not well established.  
  > "In terms of the SED for describing optical measurements, we primarily used the HST measurement (cite: soderberg2004), because the ground-based multicolor measurements were affected by the nearby galaxies, as reported by (cite: bersier06)." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies); "In the low-metallicity range, the conversion factor estimation becomes difficult, because the estimation methods have not yet been established. For example, the empirical relations between metallicity and $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ of Genzel et al. (2012) and Bolatto et al. (2013) differed. Therefore, we adopted the harmonic mean of the recipes of (cite: genzel12) and Bolatto et al. (2013), similar to (cite: hatsukade20)." (Molecular Gas Mass)
- Alternative: Ground-based multicolor optical measurements of the XRF 020903 host galaxy were rejected in favor of HST measurements due to contamination from nearby galaxies.  
  > "In terms of the SED for describing optical measurements, we primarily used the HST measurement (cite: soderberg2004), because the ground-based multicolor measurements were affected by the nearby galaxies, as reported by (cite: bersier06)." (IR Luminosity and Star Formation Rate (SFR) with GRB Host Galaxies)
- Alternative: Evaluated both metallicity-dependent alpha_CO (15 and 9.6) and Galactic alpha_CO (4.4) conversion factors for molecular gas mass estimation.  
  > "The converted upper limits of the gas mass were $M_{\rm gas}<3.9\times10^{9}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ with $\alpha_{\rm CO}=15$ $M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$) for region A and $M_{\rm gas}<2.5\times10^{9}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ with $\alpha_{\rm CO}=9.6$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$) for regions B, C, and D. The smaller upper limit of $M_{\rm gas}<1.1\times10^{9}$ was estimated by applying the galactic conversion factor of $\alpha_{\rm CO}$=4.4 (cite: bolatto13b)." (Molecular Gas Mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The CO-to-H2 conversion factor is difficult to estimate and not well established in the low-metallicity regime.  
  > "In the low-metallicity range, the conversion factor estimation becomes difficult, because the estimation methods have not yet been established." (Molecular Gas Mass)
- Limitation (authors): A comparable galaxy sample in the same stellar mass range did not exist for comparing M_gas.  
  > "A comparable sample in the same stellar mass range did not exist." (Molecular Gas Mass)
- Limitation (authors): The upper limit on the molecular gas mass fraction (mu_gas) could not constrain the redshift trend effectively.  
  > "Although the upper limit of $\mu_{\rm gas}$ could not constrain the trend effectively, the limit of $t_{\rm depl}$ was consistent with the results of most of GRB samples (e.g., GRB 980703, GRB 021211, GRB 031203, GRB 050826, GRB 060814, GRB 070306, GRB 071021, GRB 081109A, GRB 110918A), and one of SLSN, PTF10tpz." (Molecular Gas Mass-SFR, Gas Fraction, and Depletion Timescale)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All ALMA measurements of the primary target XRF 020903 are non-detections (upper limits), which can only show consistency with rather than tight confirmation of GRB host scaling relations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is an unexplained discrepancy in the reported continuum frequencies for XRF 020903 between Section 3.1 (113 GHz and 224 GHz) and the Abstract/Table 1 (97.2 GHz and 233.0 GHz).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3-sigma limit of the CO velocity-integrated map is stated as 0.57 mJy rather than in velocity-integrated flux units (mJy km s^-1 or Jy km s^-1), though calculating L'_CO = 2.6e8 K km s^-1 pc^2 at z=0.251 requires S_CO * Delta_v ~ 0.103 Jy km s^-1 (i.e., 0.57 mJy * 180 km s^-1).
