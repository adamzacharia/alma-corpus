## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, agn_and_quasars
- Question: Does the concentration and normalized radial distribution of cold (CO) and hot (H2 2.12 um) molecular gas on nuclear (r<=50 pc) and CND (r<=200 pc) scales change with X-ray luminosity across 45 AGN and 19 non-AGN galaxies, as a signature of AGN feedback?  
  > "We explore whether the concentration and normalized radial distribution of cold molecular gas change as function of the X-ray luminosity to quantify the imprint left by AGN feedback on our sources." (Aims of this paper)

## Analysis recipe

1. **assemble the CO sample and define AGN vs non-AGN**: Select spiral galaxies with high-resolution CO(3-2), CO(2-1) and/or CO(1-0) ALMA/PdBI data from several surveys; AGN = well detected in hard X-rays with a Seyfert/LINER classification in NED; non-AGN comparison drawn from LLAMA and PHANGS  
  > "that have high-resolution CO(3-2), CO(2-1), and/or CO(1-0) observations obtained with ALMA and/or the PdBI arrays from different surveys." (3 The sample); "We consider a galaxy to be an AGN if it is well detected in hard X-rays and have a corresponding Seyfert/LINER classification according to NED." (3 The sample)
   - parameter: AGN at D_L = 7-45 Mpc; non-AGN at D_L = 7-41 Mpc  
  > "We also used a comparison sample of non-AGN drawn from the LLAMA and PHANGS surveys, with a range of distances similar to the AGN sources" (3 The sample)
   - parameter: 57 CO images of 45 AGN plus maps of 19 non-AGN galaxies  
  > "The CO sample comprises 57 different images of 45 AGN as well as maps of 19 non-AGN galaxies." (3 The sample)
   - assumption: L_X (2-10 keV) used as proxy for AGN luminosity  
  > "are adopted as a proxy for the AGN luminosity." (3 The sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **convert CO fluxes to cold molecular gas masses**: Mass formula from CO(i-j) flux scaled to CO(1-0) with line ratio R_ij, alpha_CO and distance, including a Helium factor of 1.36  
  > "which includes the mass of Helium ($M_{\rm gas}^{\rm CO}=1.36\times M({\rm H_2})$)" (4.1 CO observations); "$R_{\rm ij}$ is the assumed CO(i-j)-to-CO(1-0) line ratio (in $T_{\rm mb}$-units)" (4.1 CO observations)
   - parameter: R31 = 2.9, R21 = 2.5, alpha_CO = 2e20 mol cm^-2 (K km/s)^-1, same for both spatial scales  
  > "We assume common values of $R_{\rm ij}$ and $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ for the two spatial scales used in this work" (Footnote 3); "$R_{\rm 31}$=2.9 and $R_{\rm 21}$=2.5 measured by (cite: Vit14) in NGC 1068" (Footnote 3)
   - assumption: Milky-Way alpha_CO for all galaxies; R31 and R21 from NGC 1068  
  > "$\alpha_{\rm CO}=2\times10^{20}$mol cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: Bol13)" (Footnote 3)
   - assumption: Mass uncertainty up to +/-0.20 dex if R_ij and alpha_CO errors are uncorrelated  
  > "Under these extreme hypotheses the total uncertainty would amount to $\pm$0.20 dex." (4.1 CO observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Argues the concentration index is insensitive to galaxy-to-galaxy changes in the conversion factors because it is a ratio  
  > "Furthermore, the normalized radial distribution is by definition insensitive to any potential galaxy-to-galaxy change in the global conversion factors." (5.1 Concentration indices)
3. **measure nuclear and CND surface densities and the cold concentration index CCI**: Deproject CO images with PA and i, average surface densities within r<=50 pc and r<=200 pc, CCI = log10(Sigma50/Sigma200)  
  > "are derived by deprojecting the CO images using the $PA$ and $i$ values listed in Table" (4.1 CO observations); "we first derived the average molecular gas surface densities measured from the different CO lines using two spatial scales representative of the nuclear ($r\leq50$ pc) and CND environments ($r\leq200$ pc)" (5.1 Concentration indices)
   - parameter: Normalise nuclear-scale surface density by CND-scale surface density  
  > "We normalize the nuclear scale gas surface densities ($\Sigma^{\rm gas}_{\rm 50}$) by the corresponding densities measured on CND scales ($\Sigma^{\rm gas}_{\rm 200}$)." (5.1 Concentration indices)
   - assumption: Same conversion factors (R31, R21, alpha_CO) at both spatial scales; adopted choice judged conservative  
  > "our adopted choice of common values of the conversion factors for the nuclear and CND scales would be therefore conservative" (5.2 Dependence of trends on CO transitions and conversion factors)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Alternative definition normalising by the outer corona (50 pc<=r<=200 pc); comparison across CO transitions  
  > "an alternative definition of the concentration of cold molecular gas, which adopts the region defined by the outer corona of the CND ($50$ pc$\leq r \leq 200$ pc)" (Appendix: Other trends)
4. **bin by X-ray luminosity and compute median CCI**: Group data sets into L_X bins (7 for CO) with similar numbers per bin and compute median values and median absolute deviations of CCI  
  > "Column (3) lists the median values and the median absolute deviations of the concentration indices ($CCI$ and $HCI$)" (Table 1 notes); "The binning used along the $L_{\rm X}$ axis is designed to have a similar number of 8-9 independent CO data sets per luminosity bin." (Footnote 4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **locate the turnover in CCI vs L_X**: MARS piecewise linear regression to find the break point and slopes of the two branches, on 100 Monte Carlo realisations that include uncertainties on both axes  
  > "we derived the `MARS` fit on 100 Monte Carlo realisations of the $CCI$ versus $L_{\rm X}$ relation" (5.1 Concentration indices); "due to the assumed conversion factors ($\pm0.2$ dex), and on the AGN luminosities ($\pm0.15$ dex)" (5.1 Concentration indices)
   - software: MARS, Rstudio
   - parameter: Uncertainties of +/-0.2 dex (conversion factors) on CCI and +/-0.15 dex on L_X  
  > "due to the assumed conversion factors ($\pm0.2$ dex), and on the AGN luminosities ($\pm0.15$ dex)" (5.1 Concentration indices)
   - fitting: MARS (Multivariate Adaptive Regression Splines) from the Rstudio package; priors and likelihood not applicable or not stated  
  > "we used the Multivariate Adaptive Regression Splines (`MARS`) fit routine available from the `Rstudio` package (cite: Rcorteam)" (5.1 Concentration indices)
   - validation: Repeated with the corona-normalised concentration, which gives a similar turnover  
  > "finds similar values for the turnover at log$_{\rm 10}L_{\rm X}=41.54$$\pm0.25$ erg s$^{-1}$" (5.1 Concentration indices)
6. **derive normalised radial profiles of cold gas and compare bins**: Radial profiles out to 210 pc in 20 pc bins, each normalised by CO flux within 210 pc, median per L_X bin; KS tests between bins  
  > "we derived the radial profiles of cold molecular gas out to $r=210$ pc for each target using a radial binning $\Delta r=20$ pc" (5.3 Radial distributions); "Individual profiles are further normalized by the CO flux integrated out to $r=210$ pc for each target ($I_{\rm norm}(r)$)." (5.3 Radial distributions)
   - parameter: Radial bin width 20 pc, about half the median spatial resolution  
  > "which corresponds to about half of the median value of the spatial resolutions" (5.3 Radial distributions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests rejecting that profiles of bins 1 and 5, and bins 1 and 7, are drawn from the same distribution  
  > "yield consistently low $p$-values in either case ($p$-values$\leq$0.001)" (5.3 Radial distributions)
7. **non-parametric check of profile shape with the Gini coefficient**: Compute the Gini coefficient of the radius^2 distribution weighted by CO flux over r=0-200 pc, so that a flat profile gives G ~ 1/3; 99% confidence intervals from bootstrap percentiles  
  > "we measure the inequality in the distribution of radius$^2$, so that a flat radial profile corresponds to $G\approx1/3$" (5.4 Non-parametric analysis); "We calculate $G$ for each galaxy in our sample over the $r$=0–200 pc range with the `DescTools R` package." (5.4 Non-parametric analysis)
   - software: DescTools R
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compares G with CCI and with the flux-weighted mean radius via Spearman coefficients  
  > "tight correlation between the two statistics (Spearman coefficient, $R_s$ of $\sim0.85$)" (5.4 Non-parametric analysis)
8. **derive hot H2 concentration index HCI from NIR data**: Fit the H2 2.12 um line per spaxel with LINEFIT, convert flux to hot H2 mass under LTE, same r<=50 pc and r<=200 pc surface density ratio, HCI = log10(Sigma_hot50/Sigma_hot200); MARS fit as for CCI  
  > "The emission-line flux distribution was extracted by fitting the H$_{\rm 2}$ 2.12 $\mu$m line profile using our custom code `LINEFIT` (cite: Dav11)." (4.2 NIR observations); "Following the same approach of Sect" (6.1 Concentration indices)
   - software: LINEFIT, MARS, SINFONI pipeline, SPRED
   - parameter: LTE with excitation temperature 2000 K  
  > "Under the assumptions of local thermal equilibrium and an excitation temperature of 2000 K" (4.2 NIR observations)
   - parameter: Aperture correction when at least 70% of the 200 pc aperture is covered  
  > "an aperture correction was applied assuming an azimuthally symmetric flux distribution" (3 The sample)
   - assumption: Helium correction M_gas = 1.36 M(H2); same conversion factor at both scales  
  > "correct for the mass of Helium using the expression $M_{\rm gas}^{\rm NIR}=1.36\times M({\rm H_2})$" (4.2 NIR observations)
   - fitting: MARS fit on HCI vs L_X  
  > "we used the `MARS` algorithm to find the position of the breaking point and the slopes of the two AGN branches" (6.1 Concentration indices)
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **measure hot-to-cold molecular gas ratios**: Ratio of hot to cold gas mass surface density from the 2.1 um and CO emission at r<=50 pc and r<=200 pc, per L_X bin, for 42 independent map pairs  
  > "Taking advantage of the significant overlap between the CO and NIR samples, which span 42 independent map pairs" (7 Hot-to-cold molecular gas ratios)
   - assumption: Ratio insensitive to galaxy-to-galaxy variation of conversion factors  
  > "By definition, this ratio is insensitive to any galaxy-to-galaxy variation of conversion factors for the cold and hot molecular gas." (7 Hot-to-cold molecular gas ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
10. **estimate transit and exhaustion timescales**: Analytic estimates of transit time (concentration change times nuclear mass over outflow rate) for NGC 1068, NGC 4388, NGC 7172, NGC 7582, and of torus gas exhaustion time from torus mass, efficiency and L_X  
  > "We can make a rough estimate of these typical transition timescales for four of the galaxies belonging to the late-phase AGN feedback branch" (8.3 An estimate of timescales)
   - parameter: r = 50 pc; accretion efficiency 0.1; bolometric correction 10  
  > "assuming a canonical accretion efficiency $\epsilon\simeq0.1$, and a bolometric correction $k_{\rm bol}\simeq10$" (8.3 An estimate of timescales)
   - assumption: Initial CCI typical of the late AGN build-up branch (NGC 613, CCI=+0.82); Sigma_200 and outflow rate constant during transition  
  > "such as NGC 613 ($CCI=+0.82$)" (8.3 An estimate of timescales)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with AGN flickering timescales from the literature  
  > "AGN activity could still continue in these Seyfert galaxies for a time span $\simeq10-100$ longer than the expected *flickering* timescale." (8.3 An estimate of timescales)
11. **derive L_X for five low-luminosity galaxies from archival X-ray spectra**: Process Chandra and XMM-Newton data with CIAO and SAS, extract nuclear spectra, group with grppha, fit absorbed power-law models (with apec and pexmon where needed) in XSPEC  
  > "We then used the latest version of the `XSPEC` software to perform spectral fitting." (Appendix: Estimate of L_X); "We grouped the spectra with a minimum of 20 counts per bin using the command line grppha within `Heasoft` software." (Appendix: Estimate of L_X)
   - software: CIAO, SAS, Heasoft, XSPEC
   - parameter: Photon index fixed to 1.8 in three sources where poorly constrained  
  > "In these cases, we fixed it to the standard value of $\rm{\Gamma = 1.8}$." (Appendix: Estimate of L_X)
   - fitting: Spectral fits with models M1-M3 (pcfabs x powerlaw, optional apec and pexmon); statistic not stated  
  > "This new baseline model is written in the nomenclature of the software as *apec + pcfabs $\times$ powerlaw* (M2 in Table" (Appendix: Estimate of L_X)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Galaxies on the AGN feedback branch with the lowest nuclear concentrations lie near the region of N_H-Eddington ratio space favourable to IR-driven polar dusty outflows  
  > "galaxies in the AGN feedback branch with the lowest nuclear-scale molecular gas concentrations tend to be close to the region where IR-driven polar dusty outflows would be more favorably launched." (8.2 The AGN feedback phase)
- The link between CCI and the fraction of large-scale bars is weak  
  > "suggests that the link between $CCI$ and the fraction of large-scale bars is weak." (8.1 The AGN build-up phase)
- CCI trends are consistent across the CO(3-2), CO(2-1) and CO(1-0) lines  
  > "The picture drawn from the different CO lines is overall consistent within the uncertainties regarding the existence of the two branches described above." (5.2 Dependence of trends on CO transitions and conversion factors)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: A larger sample (45 AGN, 19 non-AGN) and more CO transitions to test the earlier GATOS result on firmer statistics  
  > "To verify the results of (cite: GB21) on a more robust statistical basis, we have selected for this paper a larger sample" (Aims of this paper)
- Why this method: Normalising nuclear to CND surface density removes bias from differing overall gas content and conversion factors  
  > "This normalization counterbalances any potential bias associated with the different overall molecular gas content of the galaxies of the sample" (5.1 Concentration indices)
- Alternative: Alternative concentration definition normalising the nuclear region by the outer corona (50 pc<=r<=200 pc); gives a larger 0.72 dex range and a similar turnover  
  > "an alternative definition of the concentration of cold molecular gas, which adopts the region defined by the outer corona of the CND ($50$ pc$\leq r \leq 200$ pc)" (Appendix: Other trends)
- Alternative: Non-parametric Gini coefficient as an alternative to the CCI  
  > "We confirm these results with an alternative, non-parametric method that builds on the Gini coefficient, $G$." (5.4 Non-parametric analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: L_X bins sized to hold a similar number of independent data sets per bin  
  > "The binning used along the $L_{\rm X}$ axis is designed to have a similar number of 8-9 independent CO data sets per luminosity bin." (Footnote 4)
- Limitation (authors): R31, R21 and alpha_CO are not constrained for the sample; combined mass uncertainty up to 0.20 dex  
  > "While the values of $R_{\rm 31}$, $R_{\rm 21}$ for the galaxies in our sample are not constrained" (5.2 Dependence of trends on CO transitions and conversion factors); "Under these extreme hypotheses the total uncertainty would amount to $\pm$0.20 dex." (4.1 CO observations)
- Limitation (authors): The increase of CCI along the AGN build-up branch is not statistically significant  
  > "However, we note that given the associated error bars, the reported increase in $CCI$ among the galaxies of the AGN build-up branch is not statistically significant." (5.1 Concentration indices)
- Limitation (authors): Small statistics per L_X bin  
  > "Within the limits of our admittedly small statistics" (8.1 The AGN build-up phase)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO images have heterogeneous resolution, sensitivity and recoverable scale (median 36 pc beam versus a 50 pc nuclear aperture), so CCI may be resolution-dependent; no common-resolution test is described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single set of R31, R21 and alpha_CO from NGC 1068 and the Milky Way is applied to all galaxies, so any real nuclear excitation or conversion-factor gradient with L_X would bias the trend
- Caveat (inferred, ours): L_X (2-10 keV) is a snapshot proxy for a variable AGN; Eddington ratios are only available for 27 galaxies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Bins hold only 8-9 data sets, some galaxies appear several times (e.g. NGC 7172, NGC 5728), so points are not fully independent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The evolutionary scenario is inferred from a cross-section of different galaxies, not tracked in time
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Selection from several heterogeneous surveys may introduce bias; early-type galaxies from WISDOM are excluded by choice
