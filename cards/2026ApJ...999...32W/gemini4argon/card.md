## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA Band 6 continuum and CO spectral line data, and measure central source flux and extended dust upper limits**: Calibrate raw data with ALMA pipeline, concatenate three nights, image continuum with uniform and natural+taper weightings, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, and image CO isotopologue cubes with natural weighting and multi-scale tclean.  
  > "We calibrated the data using the standard ALMA pipeline. The data from the three nights were then concatenated into a single dataset. The combined dataset was then imaged using the `tclean` task in CASA." (ALMA Observations and Data Reduction); "The spectral cubes of the molecular transitions of the three CO isotopologues were imaged using natural weighting after continuum subtraction through `uvcontsub`." (ALMA Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub, auto-multithresh
   - parameter: robust = -2 (uniform) and robust = 2 with 2" uv-taper (natural) for continuum  
  > "one with uniform weighting, or Briggs weighting with a robustness of -2, to achieve the highest resolution possible, and the other with natural weighting, or Briggs weighting with a robustness of 2, and a $u-v$ taper of 2 ″" (ALMA Observations and Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Distance of 3.39 kpc to WR 112  
  > "This suggests any substructure, if it exists, would have a size less than 1700 au, assuming a distance of 3.39   to WR 112 (cite: Lau2020b)." (Continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared CO radial velocity (~35 km s^-1) and morphology with large-scale ISM surveys (Dame 2001; HI4PI 2016) peaking at 37.5 km s^-1 to identify CO as interstellar.  
  > "Given the close match of the radial velocity between the CO detected by ALMA and that in the interstellar medium, and the lack of a correspondence between the CO morphology that we observe and the dust structures" (CO emission)
2. **Constrain stellar spectra and properties of WR 112 and its OB companion**: Fit sum of PoWR model spectra for WC ('MW WC') and OB ('GAL-OB-Vd3') stars to archival unresolved photometry at lambda < 0.9 um and wind momentum ratio log(eta).  
  > "To constrain the stellar spectra of the stars, we only use observations with wavelength $\lambda <$ 0.9 μm, where stellar light should dominate over dust thermal emissions." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system); "We then optimized $L_{\text{WC}}$ for every point in the five-dimensional parameter grid that satisfies the physical constraint, and computed the $\chi^2$." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system)
   - software: PoWR
   - parameter: 6 free parameters: R_V (3.1 to 4.1 in steps of 0.1), g_OB, T_OB, T_WC, R_t, and continuous L_WC (1e5 to 1e6 L_sun)  
  > "Therefore, $R_V$ is set to be a free parameter when we fit the stellar models to the observed stellar SED, with possible values between $3.1$ and $4.1$, in increments of $0.1$." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system); "In our fits, we limit the range of $L_{\text{WC}}$ to be between $1\times 10^5$ and $1\times 10^6$ $L_{\odot}$, which is the observed range of luminosities for WC stars (cite: Sander2019)." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system)
   - assumption: Milky Way extinction curve G23 with A_V = 11.0 +- 0.3 mag; Galactic metallicity; WC mass loss rate > 1.0e-5 M_sun yr^-1; wind momentum ratio eta ~ 0.13 with 1 dex uncertainty; helium mass fraction 55%  
  > "We used the Milky Way extinction curve `G23` from (cite: Gordon2024)." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system); "This allows extinction of WR 112 to be estimated, with $A_V \approx 11.0$. To account for potential uncertainties in extinction, we assume an uncertainty in $A_V$ of $0.3$." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system); "Based on the observed range of mass loss rates of WC8 and WC9 stars (cite: Sander2019), we only included WC stars whose mass loss rate is greater than $1.0 \times 10^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}~\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system)
   - fitting: Grid search over 5D grid optimizing continuous L_WC via chi^2; models with chi^2 < 6.25 (dof = 3, 90% confidence interval) accepted to compute median, 16th, and 84th percentile spectra.  
  > "With a degree of freedom of 3 (8 photometric data points plus one constraint on $\log \eta$ and 6 free parameters), models with $\chi^2 < 6.25$ fall within the 90$\%$ confidence interval, and are considered acceptable." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system)
   - validation: Compared derived wind momentum ratio and WC mass with literature values and WC8/WC9 observations.  
  > "Our estimate for the wind momentum ratio ($\eta=0.049^{+0.182}_{-0.041}$) is consistent with the estimate of (cite: Lau2020b) at $1\sigma$ level." (Stellar SED of the WR 112 system)
3. **Extract spatially resolved dust SEDs for 23 extended dust arc apertures**: Define 23 annulus-sector apertures in two clean azimuthal sectors (13 in Sector I, 10 in Sector II) by locating radial profile troughs (cavities) in 21 um JWST image, mask 3-sigma background sources, integrate JWST fluxes, and compute ALMA 1.3 mm 3-sigma upper limits.  
  > "The sectors are defined by position angles: $216^{\circ} \leq \mathrm{PA} \leq 256^{\circ}$ for the first and $94^{\circ} \leq \mathrm{PA} \leq 134^{\circ}$ for the second." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs); "The masks were computed by repeatedly applying the $3\sigma$ level mask estimated from `photutils.background`." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs); "The upper limit at 1.3 mm for each aperture is given by the $3\sigma$ level of emission of the ALMA continuum image multiplied by the square root of the number of pixels in the aperture." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs)
   - software: photutils.background
   - parameter: Sector I: 216 deg <= PA <= 256 deg (13 apertures); Sector II: 94 deg <= PA <= 134 deg (10 apertures); 20% uncertainty adopted for JWST fluxes  
  > "There are 13 apertures in the first sector and 10 apertures in the second." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs); "To account for these uncertainties conservatively, we assume a $20 \%$ uncertainty for every JWST photometric data point." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs)
   - assumption: Deprojected distance estimated as sky-plane distance at PA = 216 deg for Sector I and PA = 134 deg for Sector II  
  > "The geometric model of WR 112 suggests the deprojected distance from the star to each aperture can be estimated as the distance (to the midpoint of the aperture along the radial direction) in the plane of sky at $\mathrm{PA} = 216^{\circ}$" (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs)
   - fitting: Linear interpolation of cavity locations across position angles  
  > "The locations of the cavities at other position angles are computed by interpolating the predicted locations of the cavities at the three selected position angles." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs)
   - validation: Verified that read/Poisson noise and background/masking uncertainties are <1%.  
  > "The uncertainties of the JWST photometric points due to read and Poisson noise of the instrument are estimated to be much less than $1\%$. The uncertainties caused by background subtraction and masking of background stars is also found to be less than $1\%$." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs)
4. **Fit spatially resolved dust SEDs with free dust temperatures and a single grain radius**: Perform a joint MCMC fit of modified blackbody emission across all 23 apertures treating each aperture temperature as a free parameter and sharing effective solid angles and a single grain radius.  
  > "To constrain the parameters that are shared across apertures in a robust way, we performed a joint Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) fit to the SEDs from the apertures using the `emcee` package (cite: ForemanMackey2013)." (Temperatures as Free Parameters)
   - software: emcee, Mie scattering code (Dullemond 2012)
   - parameter: 26 free parameters: Omega_eff^I, Omega_eff^II, grain radius a (-9.0 <= log[a/m] <= -4.0), and 23 temperatures T_d^(1)...T_d^(23)  
  > "There are 26 free parameters in total, including three parameters shared over apertures $\Omega_{\text{eff}}^{\text{I}}, \Omega_{\text{eff}}^{\text{II}}, a$ as well as temperatures that are specific to each aperture, $T_d^{(1)}, \cdots ,T_d^{(23)}$." (Temperatures as Free Parameters); "For the grain radius $a$, the bounds are chosen to be $-9.0 \leq \log [a/\textrm{meter}] \leq -4.0$, since it is unrealistic for grains to be smaller than 1 nanometer." (Temperatures as Free Parameters)
   - assumption: Radiative equilibrium, optically thin uniformly expanding dust, ACAR-type amorphous carbon optical constants (Zubko 1996)  
  > "We adopted optical constants of ACAR-type amorphous carbon prepared by (cite: Colangeli1995) in laboratory and measured by (cite: Zubko1996)." (Temperatures as Free Parameters); "Since the optically thin dust structure is expanding uniformly and the apertures are drawn between fixed position angles, the dust in one aperture will expand into the dust in the next aperture." (Temperatures as Free Parameters)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee; power-law fit T_d(r) = T_0 (r/r_0)^gamma to derived aperture temperatures  
  > "The best-fit power law for the temperature profile from all the apertures has power law index $\gamma = -0.384 \pm 0.021$." (Temperatures as Free Parameters)
   - validation: Evaluated reduced chi^2 (chi_nu^2 = 5.28) and compared temperature power-law index with Marchenko (2002).  
  > "The $\chi^2$ for the median posterior model is 227.2. With 26 free parameters and 69 data points from 23 apertures, the reduced $\chi^2$ is $\chi_\nu^2 = 5.28$." (Temperatures as Free Parameters)
5. **Fit spatially resolved dust SEDs using an equilibrium temperature grid and four grain size distributions**: Compute radiative equilibrium temperature grid T_d(a, r) by balancing stellar photon absorption and thermal emission, then run MCMC fits to the 23 aperture SEDs for four grain size distribution parameterizations.  
  > "$T_d(a,r)$ is the equilibrium temperature of dust grains derived numerically from Equation [\[eq:Tgrid\]](#eq:Tgrid) for every grain radius $a$ at every distance $r$ away from the stars." (Equilibrium Temperature Grid); "We conducted MCMC fitting to the observed dust SEDs using the model-predicted temperatures with four parameterizations of the grain size distributions: a single grain radius, a (base-10) log-normal distribution, two grain radii (or two delta functions), and a continuous power law." (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
   - software: emcee, Mie scattering code (Dullemond 2012)
   - parameter: Grain radius parameters sampled in log space between 1 nm and 100 um; 4 distributions (single radius a_e; log-normal a_c, sigma_log_a; two radii a_1, a_2, f_2; power law a_l, a_u, beta)  
  > "All free parameters except $\sigma_{\log a}$ and $\beta$ are sampled from the $\log$ space during the MCMC fit. The sampling of all parameters related to the grain radius is restricted to the range 1 nm to 100 μm." (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
   - assumption: Radiative equilibrium, no scattering, median stellar spectrum S_*(lambda), constant grain size distribution across outer apertures, ACAR optical constants  
  > "$S_{\ast}(\lambda)$ is the spectral luminosity of the stellar photon flux, for which we adopt the median stellar spectrum of WR 112 and its companion derived in Section [3.2](#sec:stellarsed)" (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling with emcee  
  > "We conducted MCMC fitting to the observed dust SEDs using the model-predicted temperatures with four parameterizations of the grain size distributions" (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
   - validation: Compared equilibrium temperature power-law index (~-0.38 for a <= 30 nm) with free-temperature fit (-0.384) and compared reduced chi^2 across the four distributions.  
  > "For $a \lesssim 3 \times 10^{-8}~\mathrm{m}$, the power law index is approximately $-0.38$, which agrees with similar computation using the optical constants by (cite: Williams2009). This value is also consistent with the $\gamma = -0.384$ derived in Section [3.3.2](#sec:Tfree)" (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
6. **Fit spatially resolved dust SEDs accounting for stochastic heating of small grains**: Compute grain temperature probability distributions P(T) on a 200-radius grid (1 nm to 10 um) for each aperture using DustEM, interpolate P(T) via quantile function linear interpolation, and fit the 23 aperture SEDs with MCMC for the four grain size distributions.  
  > "In this work, we used `DustEM` (cite: Compigne2011) to compute the probability density $P(T;~a, S_{\ast}, r^{(i)}, \cdots)$, which is based on the formalism of (cite: Desert1986)." (Stochastic heating); "To speed up computation, we computed the probability density of temperature $P(T;~a, S_{\ast}, r^{(i)}, \cdots)$ for each aperture and a grid of 200 grain radii $a$ between 1 nm and 10 μm." (Stochastic heating); "Then, the smooth transformation between PDFs is given by the linear interpolation of the quantile function over the external parameter space" (Interpolation of probability density function)
   - software: DustEM, emcee, Mie scattering code (Dullemond 2012)
   - parameter: Grid of 200 grain radii from 1 nm to 10 um; four grain size distributions (single radius, log-normal, two radii, power law)  
  > "Using the probability density of temperatures and Equation [\[eq:stoch_heating2\]](#eq:stoch_heating2), we conducted MCMC fits to the observed SEDs. We adopted the four parameterizations of grain distributions used in Section [3.3.3](#sec:tempgrid): one radius, the base-10 log-normal, two radii and a power law." (Stochastic heating)
   - assumption: Graphite heat capacity (Draine 2001), dust bulk density rho = 1.81 g cm^-3, ACAR optical constants (also tested ACH2 and BE types)  
  > "Thus, we adopted the heat capacity of graphite from (cite: Draine2001). We also set the density of dust to be $\rho = 1.81~\mathrm{g~cm^{-3}}$, which is in agreement with (cite: Compigne2011)." (Stochastic heating)
   - fitting: MCMC fitting with emcee  
  > "Using the probability density of temperatures and Equation [\[eq:stoch_heating2\]](#eq:stoch_heating2), we conducted MCMC fits to the observed SEDs." (Stochastic heating)

(Dust SED at the location of the star); "We conducted MCMC fits of the three models to the unresolved SED, and the results are summarized in table [\[tbl:grain_stellar_sed\]](#tbl:grain_stellar_sed)." (Dust SED at the location of the star)

  > "Since each aperture spans $40^{\circ}$ in the azimuthal direction, the mass of dust formed in an orbital period of WR 112 is approximately $9 M^{\text{ap}}_d$, where $M^{\text{ap}}_d$ is the average of the mass of dust in an aperture in the first and the second sector. The dust production rate is then approximately $9 M^{\text{ap}}_d / P$" (Dust production rate); "To understand this quantitatively, we can compute the temperature distributions of grains with different sizes using DustEM, just like in Section [3.3.4](#sec:stochastic). We can calculate the probability of the dust to be below the sublimation temperature $T_{\rm sub}$" (Radiation-driven sublimation)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA Band 6 continuum observations detect only an unresolved central point source at WR 112 dominated by free-free emission, with no extended dust emission detected above 3-sigma.  
  > "The continuum image with the higher spatial resolution, created using uniform weighting, only shows one unresolved central emission at the location of WR 112" (Continuum emission); "This implies that the observed ALMA flux should be almost entirely from free-free emissions." (Sources of continuum emission observed by ALMA)
- Detected 12CO(2-1), 13CO(2-1), and C18O(2-1) emissions originate from interstellar gas along the line of sight or in the environment rather than the stellar winds of WR 112.  
  > "the CO emissions we observe are likely from interstellar gas rather than stellar winds of WR 112." (CO emission)
- The spatially resolved dust SEDs and radial temperature profile of WR 112 are consistent with emission from hydrogen-poor amorphous carbon grains, predominantly smaller than 0.5 um and dominated by nanometer-sized (<30 nm) grains.  
  > "We found that the SEDs are consistent with emissions from hydrogen-poor amorphous carbon grains. Notably, our results also suggest that the majority of grains in the system have radii below one micrometer, and the extended dust structures are dominated by nanometer-sized grains." (Abstract); "Most grains in the outer dust structures are smaller than 0.5 μm, likely dominated by very small grains with radius $\lesssim 30~\mathrm{nm}$." (Conclusions)
- Stochastic heating is essential to account for the 7.7 um emission in the outer dust arcs, and a bimodal grain size distribution (~1.25 nm and ~0.134 um) best reproduces both the spatially resolved and unresolved SEDs.  
  > "As a result, the 7.7 μm emission can be well accounted for by the presence of nanometer-size grains. This improves the fit for the model with two grain radii and a power law as the grain distribution." (Stochastic heating); "Particularly, the two grain radii model almost perfectly reproduces the observed SED, with a reduced chi-squared $\chi^2_\nu = 0.470$." (Stochastic heating)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: WR 112 is a continuous dust producer with a relatively uniform spiral structure, and combining resolved mid-IR JWST images with millimeter ALMA Band 6 observations provides sensitivity to both small and large grains.  
  > "WR 112 is an ideal system for constructing models to constrain the dust properties: it is a continuous dust producer that hosts a relatively uniform dust structure and lacks excessive geometric complexity (cite: Lau2020b)." (Introduction); "The infrared-to-millimeter observations combined are sensitive to the emissions of both small and large grains. This enables us to more accurately constrain the grain size distribution of dust in WR 112" (Introduction)
- Why this method: A 4-pointing mosaic and 2" uv-tapered natural weighting were used to maximize sensitivity to extended dust emission; two clean azimuthal sectors were chosen on JWST images to avoid MIRI diffraction spikes; DustEM stochastic heating was used because radiative equilibrium cannot heat >=1 nm grains enough to match outer 7.7 um fluxes.  
  > "We constructed a mosaic with four pointings around the star with a total field of view of over 49 ″ to capture any extended emission." (ALMA Observations and Data Reduction); "To avoid contamination, we chose two sector regions on the image where there is no diffraction spike or obvious PSF artifact." (Dust SED for the extended dust arcs); "Even grains as small as 1 nm are not warm enough under RE conditions in outer apertures to produce enough emission at 7.7 μm that is consistent with the observations." (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
- Alternative: Single grain radius and log-normal grain size distributions (rejected due to poor fits to the 21 um inner and 7.7 um outer aperture fluxes)  
  > "The single grain radius distribution and the log-normal distribution fit the observed SEDs the worst, and should be unlikely." (Stochastic heating)
- Alternative: Power-law grain size distribution, both static and time-varying across apertures (disfavored relative to the bimodal distribution due to higher chi^2 and lack of physical mechanisms to destroy large grains at r > 25,000 au)  
  > "By contrast, the two-grain-radii model has only 5 free parameters and yields $\chi^2$ of $30.1$. It performs considerably better than both cases of the power law size distribution, no matter what the uncertainties in the SED are." (Time-varying power law distribution)
- Alternative: Radiative equilibrium temperature assumption for small grains (rejected because even 1 nm grains in radiative equilibrium cannot reproduce the observed 7.7 um emission in outer apertures)  
  > "This suggests that simply modifying the grain size distribution cannot account for the observed SEDs under the RE assumption." (Equilibrium Temperature Grid)
- Alternative: Collisional fragmentation barrier, thermal/non-thermal sputtering, and shock processing as the origin of the bimodal grain size distribution (rejected because they either destroy larger grains faster or destroy nanometer grains faster rather than creating an intermediate-size valley)  
  > "Similarly, thermal and non-thermal sputtering can be ruled out as sputtering leads to the destruction of grains of all sizes and quicker destruction of the nanometer-sized dust (cite: Tielens1994)." (Theoretical challenge: origin of bimodality); "Shock processing of dust, which include the collective effects of sputtering, shattering and vaporization, also destroy larger grains more quickly and thus cannot establish such kind of barrier (cite: Jones1996)." (Theoretical challenge: origin of bimodality)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Simplified parameterizations of the grain size distribution due to having only three resolved mid-IR filters and one mm upper limit per aperture.  
  > "Our parameterizations of the grain size distribution are necessarily simplified, and additional photometric data points would allow more complex distributions to be tested." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): JWST saturation at r < 25,000 au prevents directly resolving whether the grain size distribution evolves shortly after formation.  
  > "The saturation of JWST at $r < 25{,}000$ au also limits our ability to confirm with certainty whether the grain size distribution evolves shortly after dust formation." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Degeneracy between grain chemistry (ACAR, ACH2, BE) and grain size prevents determining the hydrogen content of carbon grains from continuum SED fitting alone.  
  > "With the grain size distribution largely unknown, models of the continuum emissions from dust cannot place constraints on the hydrogen content of the grains." (Types of carbon grains)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in applying bulk laboratory optical constants and bulk graphite heat capacities to ~1 nm grains with discrete energy states.  
  > "While it is generally assumed that optical constants depend only on the material, we are not entirely certain if they extend to smaller grains (e.g., 1 nm)." (Robustness and sources of errors); "We followed (cite: Michelsen2008) and assumed that the heat capacity of graphite is a good estimate for the heat capacity of amorphous carbon. The accuracy of this approximation, however, remains uncertain, especially for the nanometer-sized grains." (Robustness and sources of errors)
- Limitation (authors): DustEM computes steady-state temperature distributions under a constant radiation field without an absorbing sublimation boundary, which is an oversimplification for grains moving outward in a time-varying radiation field.  
  > "However, our calculation is subject to a significant caveat: the temperature distribution computed by DustEM is the steady-state distribution under a constant radiation field when no sublimation is considered (cite: Desert1986; Compigne2011)." (Radiation-driven sublimation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA 1.3 mm data only provide non-detection upper limits in the extended dust shells, meaning the 0.134 um grain population in the outer shells is constrained primarily by the 15-to-21 um JWST slope and the 1.3 mm upper limit rather than a direct millimeter detection.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adopting a uniform 20% photometric uncertainty across all JWST apertures artificially lowers the reduced chi-squared (chi_nu^2 = 0.470) and prevents formal model comparison via information criteria like BIC.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The posterior distribution for the small grain radius a_1 piles up near the imposed 1 nm lower boundary, indicating sensitivity to the prior cutoff.
