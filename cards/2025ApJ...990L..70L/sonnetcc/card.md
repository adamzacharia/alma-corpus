## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, exoplanets
- Question: Is there an accreting protoplanet (H-alpha excess point source) inside the large dust gap of the 2MJ1612 disk, as seen with MagAO-X, and can it explain the gap?  
  > "Thus our observations can be best explained by the discovery of an accreting protoplanet, 2MJ1612 b" (abstract); "It is still unclear if just one planet can clear this large gap" (3 Results and Discussion)

## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image ALMA Band 6 continuum**: pipeline calibration, self-calibration, concatenation of all executions and tclean imaging  
  > "Data reduction started with the standard ALMA pipelines, and the subsequent self-calibration and imaging were performed with CASA 6.5" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations); "The final continuum image at 1.3 mm was created by concatenating all these datasets" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "concatenating all these datasets using `robust=0.5` in the Briggs weighting scheme" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
   - parameter: beam 85x67 mas, 1 sigma = 9.6 microJy/beam  
  > "which results in a beam of $85\times67$ mas" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the earlier image: factor of 2 better angular resolution and sensitivity  
  > "our final image has a factor of 2 better angular resolution and sensitivity" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
2. **fit radial visibility profile with frank to find rings and point-source residuals**: frank fit of azimuthally symmetric disk to the real component of the visibilities  
  > "that assumes an azimuthally symmetric disk model and fits the real component of the ALMA visibilities" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
   - software: frank, tclean
   - parameter: alpha = 1.4, omega_smooth = 0.01  
  > "The `frank` parameters are set as $\alpha=1.4$ and $\omega_{smooth}=0.01$" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
   - assumption: inclination 37 deg and PA 45 deg fixed; outer ring center used as phase center offsets  
  > "We fixed the disk inclination of 37" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations); "and adopted the outer ring center as the phase center offsets" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual images made with the same tclean parameters; earlier 3 sigma compact source not recovered  
  > "is not recovered in our deeper data" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)
3. **select, align and combine MagAO-X frames**: Strehl thresholding, cross-correlation alignment, frame combination by PARANG (Apr 13) or hybrid time/PARANG (Apr 16), Gaussian high-pass filter  
  > "All frames that meet the selection criteria described above are aligned via cross-correlation with bi-cubic interpolation using the OpenCV Python package" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction); "Thus, we applied this method to the Apr 13 observation." (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction); "we opted to use a hybrid method" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - software: OpenCV
   - parameter: rejected frames: roughly 70% (Apr 13) and 25% (Apr 16)  
  > "We rejected data with lower Strehl, which are roughly 70$\%$ and 25$\%$ of the total data for the April 13 and 16 data." (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - parameter: Gaussian high-pass filter, standard deviation 5 pixels  
  > "The Gaussian HP filter applied has a standard deviation value of 5 pixels" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **subtract starlight with KLIP-ADI and form the ASDI image**: pyKLIP KLIP-ADI on H-alpha and continuum cubes with identical parameters; scaled continuum subtracted from H-alpha  
  > "We used the Python package PyKLIP (cite: 2015ascl.soft06001W) to perform KLIP-ADI for starlight subtraction." (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction); "both reduced with the same KLIP parameters to minimize PSF differences from starlight subtraction" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - software: pyKLIP
   - parameter: 4 subsections, 10 annuli, 1 to 50 KL modes  
  > "We reduced our data with 4 subsections, 10 annuli, and 1 to 50 KL modes subtracted." (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - parameter: movement 0-5 pixels  
  > "was set to values ranging from 0-5 pixels" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - parameter: continuum image also scaled spatially by 656.3/668 = 0.98  
  > "We also scaled the continuum image spatially by the ratio of the two wavelengths (656.3/668 = 0.98)" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Source recovered independently in two epochs (Apr 13 and Apr 16)  
  > "we conclude that the H$\alpha$ excess detected in both epochs are certainly coming from the same source" (3 Results and Discussion)
5. **measure companion astrometry and contrast by forward modeling**: negative fake planet injection grid with pyKLIP forward modeling; position minimizes flux in aperture, contrast minimizes RMS  
  > "we obtained companion astrometry and photometry with the forward modeling feature in PyKLIP" (2.3 Astrometry and Photometry of Planet Candidate 2MJ1612 b); "we minimized the root-mean-square (RMS) of the individual pixel values in the aperture centered on the optimized position" (2.3 Astrometry and Photometry of Planet Candidate 2MJ1612 b)
   - software: pyKLIP
   - parameter: optimal position is median over aperture radii 0.75 to 1.5 FWHM  
  > "the optimal location is taken to be the median value of positions with minimized total flux with aperture radii ranging from 0.75 FWHM to 1.5 FWHM" (2.3 Astrometry and Photometry of Planet Candidate 2MJ1612 b)
   - parameter: true north 2.0 +/- 0.6 deg; pixel scale 5.93 +/- 0.03 mas  
  > "For the true-north rotation correction, we adopted the value of 2.0$\pm$0.6$^\circ$." (2.3 Astrometry and Photometry of Planet Candidate 2MJ1612 b)
   - assumption: injected PSFs are Gaussian while the observed stellar PSFs are elliptical  
  > "the injected PSFs are assumed to be Gaussian while the stellar PSFs observed are elliptical" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux)
   - fitting: grid of injected negative planets, no sampler  
  > "A grid of negative planets is injected into the datacube of combined frames around the initial position" (2.3 Astrometry and Photometry of Planet Candidate 2MJ1612 b)
   - validation: Error budget adds retrieval, plate scale and true-north terms in quadrature  
  > "Adding the uncertainty from retrieval, plate scale, and true-north offset in quadrature" (2.3 Astrometry and Photometry of Planet Candidate 2MJ1612 b)
6. **estimate detection significance**: small-sample two-sample t-test SNR with a ring of FWHM-wide apertures  
  > "we calculate the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) considering small sample statistics rather than the standard Gaussian approach (cite: 2014ApJ...792...97M)" (2.4 Signal to Noise Calculations)
   - parameter: aperture diameters 6-8 pixels  
  > "Thus, the diameter of apertures ranged from 6-8 pixels." (2.4 Signal to Noise Calculations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **fit an orbit to the K band and H-alpha astrometry**: OFTI rejection sampling in orbitize!  
  > "we use the Orbits For The Impatient (OFTI) algorithm (cite: 2017AJ....153..229B) implemented in the `orbitize!` python package" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
   - software: orbitize!
   - parameter: astrometric error bars of 1 FWHM for K and H-alpha  
  > "We adopt a larger error bar of 1 FWHM for both the K" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
   - parameter: 50 highest-likelihood orbits shown from 10,000 accepted solutions  
  > "The 50 highest-likelihood orbits out of the 10,000 accepted solutions have orbital periods of approximately 100 years" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
   - assumption: coplanar orbit with the disk  
  > "as co-planar orbit is commonly expected for embedded or recently formed planets" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
   - assumption: system mass 0.70 +/- 0.05 Msun  
  > "The system mass is set to be $M_\text{tot}$=0.70$\pm$0.05$M_\odot$" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
   - fitting: OFTI; priors: semi-major axis uniform 1-60 au, inclination Gaussian (37 deg, sd 5 deg), eccentricity Gaussian (0.1, sd 0.1); likelihood form not stated  
  > "The semi-major axis is assigned a uniform prior from 1 to 60 au" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations); "The eccentricity prior is chosen to be a Guassian centered at 0.1 with standard deviation of 0.1" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations); "A Gaussian prior is placed on inclination centered at the disk inclination of 37$^\circ$ with a standard deviation of 5$^\circ$" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
   - validation: Authors caution the fit has too little orbital phase coverage; fitted separation not used downstream  
  > "The current astrometric data lack sufficient orbital phase coverage, and additional epochs are required to constrain the orbit meaningfully" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
8. **convert ASDI contrast to H-alpha line flux and accretion rate**: line flux from ASDI contrast and stellar r' magnitude following Close et al. 2025; accretion rate with extinction range 0-3 mag and 5 Myr DUSTY radius  
  > "Following Section 6 from (cite: 2025AJ....169...35C), we estimated the" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux); "by adopting an extinction range of 0–3 magnitudes" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux)
   - parameter: stellar r' = 12.64 +/- 0.01  
  > "magnitude of 12.64$\pm$0.01 (cite: 2012yCat.1322....0Z)" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux)
   - assumption: zero extinction for line flux (A_R = A_p = 0)  
  > "we adopt values of 0 for both the common extinction to both the star and the planet (A$_{R}$) and the extra extinction toward the planet (A$_{p}$)" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux)
   - assumption: planet mass 4 MJup with radius 1.5 RJup  
  > "the 4$M_\text{Jup}$ planet would have radius = 1.5$R_\text{Jup}$(cite: 2000ApJ...542..464C)" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with PDS 70 protoplanet line fluxes  
  > "The H$\alpha$ line flux and $\dot M$ of 2MJ1612 b are similar to that of PDS 70 protoplanets" (3.2 H-alpha Variability and Line Flux)

## Qualitative findings

- No continuum counterpart at 668 nm, so the companion is likely substellar  
  > "the non-detection at continuum wavelengths suggests the companion is a substellar object, possibly a protoplanet" (3 Results and Discussion)
- The earlier 3 sigma compact sub-mm source in the gap is not recovered in the deeper ALMA data  
  > "is not recovered in our deeper data" (ALMA Band 6 Continuum Observations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Disk shows a large dust and gas depleted gap like PDS 70, so a prime target for accreting planets  
  > "All of these features observed in the sub-mm and NIR make 2MJ1612 a prime target to search for accreting planets" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: OFTI chosen over MCMC for faster convergence with limited orbital coverage  
  > "for a faster convergence in fitting an orbit with limited coverage of orbital fraction" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
- Alternative: Used OFTI rather than MCMC for the orbit fit  
  > "Rather than the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm, we use the Orbits For The Impatient (OFTI) algorithm" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
- Alternative: The 2024 March 25 MagAO-X dataset was excluded because of readout stripe contamination  
  > "Therefore, we chose to include only the 25A measurements in our analysis." (MagAO-X 2024 March 25 SDI Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: Number of combined frames chosen at 50 to 200 for efficiency and PARANG diversity  
  > "We have found that having 50 to 200 average combined frames leads to higher efficiency" (2.2 MagAO-X Data Selection and Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Orbit fit lacks phase coverage; more epochs needed  
  > "additional epochs are required to constrain the orbit meaningfully" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot rule out that the H-alpha source differs from the K band source  
  > "we cannot dismiss the possibility that the H$\alpha$ source has a different origin than the K band source" (3.1 Comparison to SPHERE/IRDIS Observations)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA non-detection of the compact source may be due to resolving out with a smaller beam  
  > "The non-detection in our ALMA maps may be due to the emission being resolved out with a smaller beam." (3.4 Search for Outer Companions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Detection SNR is about 5 with a variable line flux (factor ~3.6 between nights) and unmodelled PSF eccentricity; false positive from scattered inner-disk light is not excluded.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deprojected separation is quoted as 23.45 +/- 0.29 au in the abstract but 23.8 +/- 1.7 au in the results text; the separation uncertainty is written in arcsec-like units (141.96 +/- 2.10) in one place.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass of ~4 MJup depends on the K band source being the same object and on a DUSTY model.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data play only a supporting role and the planet claim rests on MagAO-X data available only on request.
