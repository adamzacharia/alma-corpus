  > "The raw spectra produced by the APEX standard pipeline was reduced using the GILDAS software package." (APEX observations); "This process involved the removal of bad scans, a linear baseline fitting and a velocity smoothing to a final resolution of 1 km s$^{-1}$ to improve rms." (APEX observations); "Conversion from the original antenna temperatures ($T_A^*$) to main-beam brightness temperature (T$_\mathrm{mb}$) was done by correcting for the forward-hemisphere efficiency and the beam efficiency of the antenna, so that" (APEX observations)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: F_eff = 0.97, B_eff = 0.73 at 345 GHz; velocity resolution = 1 km s^-1  
  > "For the APEX telescope, $F_\mathrm{eff}$ = 0.97 and $B_\mathrm{eff}$ = 0.73 at 345 GHz." (APEX observations)
   - fitting: Linear baseline fitting  
  > "This process involved the removal of bad scans, a linear baseline fitting and a velocity smoothing to a final resolution of 1 km s$^{-1}$ to improve rms." (APEX observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Calibrate, image, and feather ALMA ACA and Total Power data**: Calibrate ACA and TP data with the ALMA pipeline, subtract continuum and CLEAN ACA line visibilities, combine ACA and TP cubes using CASA feather, and rescale flux density to T_mb.  
  > "Cubes of line emission, with a characteristic beam of $7\times5$ arcsec, were constructed from the calibrated visibilities using the CLEAN algorithm after continuum-subtraction." (ALMA observations); "Finally, to recover all the spatial scales and avoid missing flux, we combined the ACA and the Total Power (hereafter ALMA TP) cubes with the CASA `feather` task, which involved regridding the single-dish data and correcting it for the primary beam response." (ALMA observations); "The resulting products were then re-scaled from flux densities to brightness temperature using the standard equation" (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, ALMA pipeline, feather
   - parameter: K-to-Jy factor ~41.5 K/Jy for TP; channel width ~1.3 km s^-1  
  > "At our frequencies the factors had a value of about 41.5 K/Jy." (ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_

  > "The feathered cubes recover 99.7% of the single-dish flux." (ALMA observations)
3. **Measure 225 GHz radio continuum fluxes and derive current mass-loss rate and ionised gas mass**: Fit a 2D Gaussian to the central compact source, subtract the point source to measure the extended nebular flux, and apply the Panagia & Felli (1975) free-free wind formula and VN15 approach to derive current mass-loss rate and nebular ionised mass.  
  > "We fitted a two-dimensional gaussian to the compact source, measuring a flux density of $30.2\pm2.2$ mJy." (Radio continuum emission); "Under the assumption that the flux density of the point-like source measured at 225 GHz is due to stellar wind, we can approximate the current mass-loss rate of AG Car using the empirical formula by (cite: 1975A&A....39....1P) for expanding envelopes around hot stars, in which we assume full ionization and standard cosmic abundances, so that" (Mass loss history of AG Car)
   - parameter: nu = 225 GHz; calibration error = 5%  
  > "Fig. 4 shows the ACA radio continuum map of AG Car at 225 GHz." (Radio continuum emission); "The uncertainty is the quadratic sum of the rms noise in the map and a calibration error of 5%." (Radio continuum emission)
   - assumption: Full ionization, standard cosmic abundances, terminal wind velocity v_inf = 100 km s^-1, wind plasma temperature T = 10^4 K, distance d = 6 kpc  
  > "Therefore, since we are now on the way to a new visual maximum, we adopt a conservative value of 100 km s$^{-1}$, compatible with the wind velocity measured near the 1995 maximum." (Mass loss history of AG Car); "For a wind temperature of $10^4$ K and a distance of 6 kpc, we obtain a mass loss rate of $\dot M = (1.55\pm0.21)\times10^{-5}\, \mathrm{M}_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ yr$^{-1}$."    - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fit to the central compact continuum source  
  > "We fitted a two-dimensional gaussian to the compact source, measuring a flux density of $30.2\pm2.2$ mJy." (Radio continuum emission)
   - validation: Compared derived mass-loss rate with Groh et al. (2009) quiescent estimate (1.5x10^-5 M_sun yr^-1) and ionised gas mass with VN15 (6.6 +/- 1.9 M_sun).  
  > "This mass-loss rate is in very good agreement with (cite: 2009ApJ...698.1698G) estimates of the quiescent $\dot M$ of AG Car, of $1.5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{M}_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ yr$^{-1}$." (Mass loss history of AG Car)
4. **Characterize ring morphology, inclination, expansion velocity, and kinematic timescales**: Integrate CO J=2-1 emission over (+29, +36) km s^-1, fit an ellipse to the lobes to determine angular size, position angle, and inclination (from axial ratio), and compute deprojected radius, expansion velocity, kinematic age, and outflow duration.  
  > "By fitting an ellipse to the lobes, we derive an approximate angular size of 35$\times$15 arcsec, with a position angle of 135 degrees east of north." (CO and 13CO emission); "Assuming that such structure is perfectly axisymmetric, we can make an elliptical fit and use the axial ratio $R_\mathrm{ab}$ to estimate the viewing angle, such that $\theta_i = \arctan(R_\mathrm{ab})$." (Morphology and kinematics); "Correcting for the inclination, we estimate an expansion velocity of $v_\mathrm{exp}=3.5\pm0.5$ km s$^{-1}$." (Morphology and kinematics)
   - parameter: Velocity integration range (+29, +36) km s^-1; systemic velocity = 32.5 km s^-1; ring reference width = 7 arcsec (ACA beam)  
  > "Fig. 3 shows the integrated intensity map of the CO $J=2\rightarrow1$ line in the range ($+$29, $+$36 km s$^{-1}$)." (CO and 13CO emission); "Therefore, in our analysis, we consider a systemic velocity of 32.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Morphology and kinematics); "However, we warn that the reliability of this method is limited, in the sense that the emitting region may be smaller than the beam; therefore, we decided to conservatively adopt the size of the ACA beam, of about 7$''$, as the reference ’width’ of the ring." (Mass loss history of AG Car)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric ring geometry at distance d = 6 kpc, constant expansion velocity with no velocity dispersion across the ring  
  > "Assuming that such structure is perfectly axisymmetric, we can make an elliptical fit and use the axial ratio $R_\mathrm{ab}$ to estimate the viewing angle, such that $\theta_i = \arctan(R_\mathrm{ab})$." (Morphology and kinematics); "In addition, we work under the assumption that the expansion velocity does not change and there is no velocity dispersion across the ring, which is probably unrealistic." (Mass loss history of AG Car)
   - fitting: Elliptical fit to the ring lobes  
  > "By fitting an ellipse to the lobes, we derive an approximate angular size of 35$\times$15 arcsec, with a position angle of 135 degrees east of north." (CO and 13CO emission)
   - validation: Compared ring orientation and deprojected radius with the infrared shell of Voors et al. (2000) and VN15, and inclination with stellar rotation viewing geometry of Groh et al. (2006, 2009).  
  > "This physical size is in good agreement with the inner radius of the infrared dusty shell derived by VN15." (Morphology and kinematics); "This is in excellent agreement with the viewing geometry proposed by (cite: 2006ApJ...638L..33G; 2009ApJ...705L..25G), who measured a remarkably high rotation velocity for the star ($v\sin{i}=220$ km s$^{-1}$) and concluded that we necessarily see AG Car from the equator." (Origin of the molecular ring)
5. **Extract single-dish line intensities, line ratios, and excitation conditions via non-LTE RADEX modelling**: Compute beam-averaged single-dish spectra at the stellar position, smooth to 1.3 km s^-1, fit Gaussians to components C1-C4 in CLASS, compute beam-dilution-corrected line ratios R_32 and R_12/13, and minimize reduced chi^2 over a RADEX non-LTE model grid.  
  > "Then, we smoothed the resulting averaged spectra to a common velocity resolution of 1.3 km s$^{-1}$, and we attempted a gaussian fitting on components C1...C4 with CLASS, as shown in Fig. \[fig:spec\]." (Physical parameters of the gas); "To model the excitation of the CO and $^{13}$CO lines, we employed the non-LTE radiative transfer code RADEX (cite: 2007A&A...468..627V)." (Physical parameters of the gas); "Once we constrained the parameter space, we followed a reduced $\chi^2$ minimisation technique to find the model that better reproduces the observed line intensities, such that" (Physical parameters of the gas)
   - software: CLASS, RADEX
   - parameter: Grid: T_k = 10 to 200 K, N(CO) = 10^13 to 10^19 cm^-2, n(H2) = 10^2 to 10^5 cm^-3; CMB = 2.73 K; representative FWHM = 6 km s^-1  
  > "The grid covered $T_\mathrm{k}$ from 10 to 200 K, $N\mathrm{(CO)}$ from 10$^{13}$ to 10$^{19}$ cm$^{-2}$ and $n(\mathrm{H_2})$ from 10$^2$ to 10$^5$ cm$^{-3}$." (Physical parameters of the gas); "We used H$_2$ as the only collision partner, with a cosmic background temperature of 2.73 K." (Physical parameters of the gas); "We also adopted a representative FWHM of 6 km s$^{-1}$." (Physical parameters of the gas)
   - assumption: Each component C_i has the same FWHM across all three transitions; homogeneous medium; beam-filling factors F <= 0.2 at 230.538 GHz and F <= 0.38 at 345.799 GHz for C4 (no beam dilution correction for C1-C3)  
  > "Therefore, we imposed the condition that every velocity component C$_i$ should have a same FWHM in each of the three lines, so that the derived line ratios were consistent." (Physical parameters of the gas); "We adopt very conservative estimates of $F\leq$0.2 and $F\leq$0.38 at 230.538 and 345.799 GHz respectively, assuming an emitting area comparable to the ACA beam or smaller." (Physical parameters of the gas); "In this case, as the emission is widespread –in fact, much larger than the beam– we did not apply a correction for beam dilution." (Physical parameters of the gas)
   - fitting: Multi-Gaussian spectral line fitting in CLASS; reduced chi^2 minimization over RADEX grid  
  > "Then, we smoothed the resulting averaged spectra to a common velocity resolution of 1.3 km s$^{-1}$, and we attempted a gaussian fitting on components C1...C4 with CLASS, as shown in Fig. \[fig:spec\]." (Physical parameters of the gas); "Once we constrained the parameter space, we followed a reduced $\chi^2$ minimisation technique to find the model that better reproduces the observed line intensities, such that" (Physical parameters of the gas)
   - validation: Cross-checked best-fit RADEX parameters by predicting 13CO J=2-1 intensity to derive N(13CO) = 7.5x10^14 cm^-2 and an isotopic ratio of ~30, matching observed R_12/13.

## Qualitative findings

- The broad CO component at 26.5 km s^-1 previously linked to AG Car by Nota et al. (2002) is resolved out by ACA and corresponds to extended ambient gas, whereas a newly identified component at 32.5 km s^-1 (29 to 36 km s^-1) forms a compact circumstellar ring.  
  > "The interferometer filtered out most of the emission at $v_\mathrm{LSR} <$ 30 km s$^{-1}$, including the broad line at 26.5 km s$^{-1}$." (CO and 13CO emission); "Such improvement allows us to resolve the emission, revealing an elliptical structure enclosing AG Car and apparently detached from the star." (CO and 13CO emission)
- The molecular ring is aligned with the SE-NW elongation of the infrared shell and perpendicular in projection to the optical continuum jet, placing it near the stellar equatorial plane.  
  > "Considering this, we can safely assume that the ring lies in –or at least close to– the equatorial plane of the star." (Origin of the molecular ring); "The ring and the jet appear perfectly perpendicular in projection, with P.A. of 135 and 225$^\circ$ respectively." (Origin of the molecular ring)
- Pure radial expansion or pure rotation cannot explain the connecting arcs in intermediate velocity channels; adding either differential rotation or macro-turbulence to radial expansion reproduces the observed morpho-kinematic features.  
  > "Still, we note that the arc-like features that connect the lobes at intermediate velocities (from 29.5 to 30.77 and 34.58 to 35.85 km s$^{-1}$) can only be reproduced by models 3 and 4, i.e. if we add rotation or turbulence to the expanding ring." (A closer view to CO kinematics)
- An equatorially enhanced mass outflow (possibly driven by rotationally-induced bistability in a pre-LBV or LBV phase) is the most promising formation scenario for the molecular ring, which subsequently shaped the younger bipolar nebula via wind-wind interaction.  
  > "We regard the mass outflow as the most promising, taking into account the evolutionary stage of the star and the evidence available." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous SEST single-dish CO observations had coarse angular resolution (45 and 23 arcsec), so higher-resolution ALMA/ACA interferometric mapping combined with Total Power and APEX single-dish data was needed to resolve the circumstellar structure while recovering all spatial scales.  
  > "Despite this promising result, the region was never properly imaged at higher resolution to confirm the existence of such a structure." (Introduction); "Finally, to recover all the spatial scales and avoid missing flux, we combined the ACA and the Total Power (hereafter ALMA TP) cubes with the CASA `feather` task, which involved regridding the single-dish data and correcting it for the primary beam response." (ALMA observations)
- Why this method: RADEX non-LTE modelling of single-dish data was used because interferometric CO J=3-2 data were unavailable; qualitative LIME 3D modelling was chosen over formal fitting to avoid overinterpreting maps with modest spatial (~7 arcsec) and spectral (1.3 km s^-1) resolution.  
  > "However, the analysis is restricted to the single-dish data, i.e. APEX and ALMA TP, since we lack interferometric CO $J=3\rightarrow2$ observations." (Physical parameters of the gas); "To avoid overinterpreting our maps, instead of fitting the observed data, we qualitatively compared the overall morpho-kinematic features in the structure with those obtained with different kinematic models." (A closer view to CO kinematics)
- Alternative: Isolating the 32.5 km s^-1 component in the APEX cube by subtracting neighbouring Gaussian components was attempted but rejected because it introduced artefacts that altered the line shape.  
  > "We attempted to isolate the line by removing the neighbouring components through a gaussian fitting, but this resulted in the introduction of multiple artefacts that altered the line shape." (APEX sigle-dish data)
- Alternative: Accretion disk of infalling gas rejected as the kinematic explanation for the radial velocity gradient because infalling disks are only expected in pre-main-sequence stages, whereas AG Car is an evolved star.  
  > "Two physical scenarios are possible: (1) an accretion disc of gas falling onto the star; or (2), an expanding ring, composed of stellar ejecta or compressed interstellar gas." (Morphology and kinematics); "From an evolutionary perspective, the accretion disk scenario is highly improbable." (Morphology and kinematics)
- Alternative: RADEX solutions with kinetic temperatures T_k > 120 K rejected because they require diffuse-cloud volume densities (~100 cm^-3) well below the CO J=2-1 critical density.  
  > "Regarding the temperature, though, solutions for $T_\mathrm{k}>120$ K are unlikely: the required volume densities are comparable to those of diffuse clouds ($\sim$ 100 cm$^{-3}$), falling well below the critical density of the $J=2\rightarrow1$ transition." (Physical parameters of the gas)
- Alternative: Purely rotating ring (LIME model 1) rejected because it concentrates emission around the systemic velocity and fails to reproduce the shape and velocity extent of the ring.  
  > "Not surprisingly, the rotating ring (model 1) fails to reproduce the shape and velocity extent of the ring, with all the emission significantly concentrated around the systemic velocity." (A closer view to CO kinematics)
- Alternative: Swept-up pre-existing natal cloud material and binary interaction/merger scenarios considered less likely than an equatorially enhanced mass outflow, since AG Car sits inside a 9 pc evacuated cavity and lacks detected binary companions.  
  > "Should the CO arise from compressed ISM material, one would expect to find it farther from the star, toward the edges of a wind-blown bubble." (Swept-up pre-existing material); "We regard the mass outflow as the most promising, taking into account the evolutionary stage of the star and the evidence available." (Conclusions)

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Severe blending between the ring component C4 (32.5-33.7 km s^-1) and the narrow ambient component C3 (29.6 km s^-1) introduces systematic uncertainty in the Gaussian decomposition and integrated flux of the ring.
