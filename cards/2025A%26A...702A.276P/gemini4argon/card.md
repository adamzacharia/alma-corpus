## Analysis recipe

1. **Search for spectral line emission and derive line upper limits**: Per-day, per-SPW continuum-subtracted full spectral resolution cubes were searched using the ALMA QA Pipeline line search algorithm and visual inspection; a 2-sigma upper limit on HCN v=0 J=4-3 in combined Band 7+7b SPW 4 was derived from the peak channel.  
  > "Each spectral window (SPW) underwent the sophisticated line search of the ALMA QA Pipeline (cite: 2023PASP..135g4501H) and was in addition scrutinized by visual inspection." (Observations); "On a per-day, per-SPW basis (after continuum subtraction) with full spectral resolution to search for line emission." (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements); "We thus obtain a $2 \sigma$ upper limit on the line surface brightness (assuming a 10 km/s line width) for any potential line in spectral window 4 from the peak channel of that spectral window with a value of 3.1 mJy/beam." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - software: ALMA Pipeline, CASA
   - parameter: velocity resolution 0.82 km/s in Band 7 SPW 4  
  > "The line emission in this spectral and spatial region is best constrained in band 7 (7 and 7b combined), spectral window 4 (velocity resolution 0.82 km/s, see also Tab. [1](#table:speclimits))." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - assumption: 10 km/s line width assumed for the HCN J=4-3 upper limit  
  > "We thus obtain a $2 \sigma$ upper limit on the line surface brightness (assuming a 10 km/s line width) for any potential line in spectral window 4 from the peak channel of that spectral window with a value of 3.1 mJy/beam." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of all SPWs and peak channel images confirming noise-like appearance  
  > "Each spectral window (SPW) underwent the sophisticated line search of the ALMA QA Pipeline (cite: 2023PASP..135g4501H) and was in addition scrutinized by visual inspection." (Observations); "The image of the peak and all other channels look like noise (Fig. [4](#fig:trc-b7-peak))." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
2. **Measure per-band/per-visit continuum flux densities and noise RMS**: Image each 12m MOUS over aggregate bandwidth with CASA tclean (mtmfs, robust=0.5), estimate RMS in an annulus between 30% and 60% of the primary beam radius in the non-PB-corrected image, and fit a 2D Gaussian to an elliptical region of synthesized beam size around the peak to take the peak of the fitted Gaussian as flux density.  
  > "In order to obtain per-band/per visit images of the target, we produce MTMFS images from the combination of all target data from one MOUS using the CASA tclean task with standard ALMA QA parameters" (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements); "We obtain the final estimate of the noise RMS from an annulus in the non-primary-beam-corrected image between ca. 30% and 60% of the primary beam radius around the image center." (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements); "The flux density of the target is obtained by fitting a Gaussian to an elliptical region of the size of the synthesized beam around the peak emission (which we find to be within a synthesized beam radius of the image center, the nominal position of T CrB[^1])." (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: deconvolver='mtmfs' (nterms=2 where fractional bandwidth > 10%), Briggs robust=0.5, pixel width = 10% of synthesized beam minor axis  
  > "in particular using deconvolver "mtmfs" (with nterms=2 where the fractional bandwidth is $>$10%), and Briggs weighting with robust=0.5." (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements); "The pixel width was chosen to be 10% of the minor axis of the synthesized beam size (see column "beam" in Table [\[table:obs\]](#table:obs))." (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements)
   - assumption: Source is an unresolved point source; systematic absolute flux calibration uncertainty is 2.5% in Bands 1-5 and 5% in Bands 6-8  
  > "All images show a central emission compatible with a point-source after deconvolution ("clean") using a tight elliptical mask around the image center" (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements); "The second errors are the additional systematic uncertainty of the ALMA absolute flux calibration of 2.5% in bands 1 - 5 and 5% in bands 6 - 8 (away from strong atmospheric absorption as in our case)(cite: almathbcy11)." (Table 2)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to an elliptical region of the size of the synthesized beam around peak emission  
  > "The flux density of the target is obtained by fitting a Gaussian to an elliptical region of the size of the synthesized beam around the peak emission" (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements)
   - validation: Peak position verified to be within one synthesized beam radius of the nominal phase center in all per-band images  
  > "Also in all individual per-band images, the peak position was less than a beam radius away from the image center." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
3. **Fit power-law SED to ALMA continuum flux densities and cross-check with combined MTMFS imaging**: Fit power law F(nu) = F0 * nu^alpha to the five concurrent phase 0.43 points and to all nine points using scipy.optimize.curve_fit in Python 3; cross-check by imaging all 12m data combined with tclean MTMFS (nterms=2) and reading the central pixel of the PB-corrected alpha and alpha.error images.  
  > "Figure [1](#fig:alma-spec) shows a power-law fit (solid line) to these five flux density measurements we have at near-equal phase (see also Table [2](#table:fluxes))." (Spectral and spatial analysis); "All fits were performed under Python 3 using the "curve_fit" function from the scipy.optimize package (see https://scipy.org)." (Spectral and spatial analysis); "The primary-beam-corrected "alpha" and "alpha.error" images (spectral index $\alpha$ and corresponding error estimate) produced by the MTMFS deconvolution contain in their central pixels (where also the peak flux density is measured) the value $\alpha=0.57\pm0.23$" (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - software: Python 3, scipy.optimize, CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Power law F(nu) = F0 * nu^alpha with nu in GHz; tclean MTMFS with nterms=2  
  > "Results of fits of a power law ($F(\nu) = F_0 \nu^{\alpha}$, $\nu$ (GHz)) to different subsets of our data points from Table [2](#table:fluxes)." (Table 3); "we combine all our ALMA 12 m array data and image it using CASA tclean with deconvolver MTMFS with two Taylor terms (nterms=2)" (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - assumption: Power-law spectral energy distribution across 42 to 400 GHz  
  > "the quiescent T CrB was a faint mm source with a spectral energy distribution well described by a powerlaw with index $\alpha=$0.56$\pm$0.11" (abstract)
   - fitting: Least-squares curve fitting via scipy.optimize.curve_fit reporting reduced chi-squared (chi^2/ndf = 0.27 for phase 0.43, 1.11 for all phases)  
  > "All fits were performed under Python 3 using the "curve_fit" function from the scipy.optimize package (see https://scipy.org)." (Spectral and spatial analysis); "| only 0.43 | 0.014$\pm$0.0072 | 0.56$\pm$0.11 | 0.27 |" (Table 3)
   - validation: Independent MTMFS nterms=2 spectral index measurement on the combined visibility dataset (alpha = 0.57 +/- 0.23) agrees with the per-band power-law fit (alpha = 0.56 +/- 0.11)  
  > "contain in their central pixels (where also the peak flux density is measured) the value $\alpha=0.57\pm0.23$ which is in very good agreement with our measurement in Table [3](#tab:specfit) albeit with a larger uncertainty." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
4. **Constrain extended continuum emission and circum-binary brightness temperature**: Combine all Band 7 and 8 data to maximize angular resolution and measure the mean surface brightness and RMS in an annulus of inner radius 0.8 arcsec and outer radius 1.6 arcsec centered on T CrB to derive a 2-sigma continuum surface brightness and brightness temperature upper limit.  
  > "In the combined image from all our band 7 and 8 data, the beam FWHM major and minor axes are 0.48 arcsec and 0.35 arcsec, 0.42 arcsec on average (Fig. [3](#fig:trc-b78-cont))." (Spectral and spatial analysis); "In an annulus of 0.8 arcsec (= twice the angular resolution to avoid any residual sidelobes of the point spread function) inner radius and 1.6 arcsec outer radius centered on T CrB (equivalent to 720 AU - 1440 AU at the distance of T CrB based on Gaia DR3 data (cite: 2021AJ....161..147B)), we obtain a mean surface brightness of 4.2 $\mu$Jy/beam and an RMS of 43.7 $\mu$Jy/beam." (Spectral and spatial analysis); "With the given beam size, this corresponds to a brightness temperature upper limit in the annulus of $T_b < 4.7$ K." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Annulus inner radius = 0.8 arcsec, outer radius = 1.6 arcsec; beam FWHM = 0.48 x 0.35 arcsec; confidence level = 2 sigma  
  > "In an annulus of 0.8 arcsec (= twice the angular resolution to avoid any residual sidelobes of the point spread function) inner radius and 1.6 arcsec outer radius centered on T CrB" (Spectral and spatial analysis); "This means there is no significant continuum emission between ca. 344 GHz and 407 GHz down to a surface brightness of 0.09 mJy/beam with a confidence level of 2 $\sigma$." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - assumption: Gaia DR3 distance of ~895 pc used to convert 0.8-1.6 arcsec to 720-1440 AU  
  > "(equivalent to 720 AU - 1440 AU at the distance of T CrB based on Gaia DR3 data (cite: 2021AJ....161..147B))" (Spectral and spatial analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inner radius set to twice the angular resolution (0.8 arcsec) to avoid PSF sidelobes  
  > "In an annulus of 0.8 arcsec (= twice the angular resolution to avoid any residual sidelobes of the point spread function) inner radius and 1.6 arcsec outer radius centered on T CrB" (Spectral and spatial analysis)
5. **Re-analyze 2016/17 VLA high-state spectra from Linford et al. ## Qualitative findings

- In August-November 2024, quiescent T CrB was an unresolved faint mm point source (~0.1 mJy at 44 GHz to ~0.4 mJy at 400 GHz) with no significant spectral line emission or extended circum-binary structure.  
  > "In the second half of 2024, after the end of its latest high state, the quiescent T CrB was a faint mm source with a spectral energy distribution well described by a powerlaw with index $\alpha=$0.56$\pm$0.11 and a flux density of ca. 0.1 mJy at 44 GHz and 0.4 mJy at 400 GHz." (abstract); "There is no significant line emission." (abstract); "Our per-band and aggregate bandwidth images of T CrB show an unresolved point source with no evidence for extended structure." (abstract)
- Extrapolated to 35 GHz, T CrB in late 2024 was a factor of ~5 fainter and had a softer spectral index than during the 2016/17 high state observed with the VLA.  
  > "This is in agreement with expectations for free-free emission from the partially ionized wind of the red giant donor star and, in extrapolation to 35 GHz, a factor 5 fainter than the emission observed in 2016/17 during the latest high state." (abstract); "Comparing the spectra from that high-state between 13.5 GHz and 35 GHz with our spectrum from 2024, our spectrum is softer." (abstract)
- An isotropic, isothermal, fully-ionized free-free wind model explains the 2016/17 VLA high state, but fails to reproduce the low flux and >350 GHz turnover frequency of the 2024 ALMA data, implying the wind was only partially ionized in 2024.  
  > "A simple model of a free-free emitting, fully-ionized stellar wind seems to describe well the 2016/17 high state of T CrB but not our 2024 ALMA measurements with their low flux and high turnover frequency suggesting that in 2024, the wind was far from fully ionized." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Bands 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 8 were chosen in a near-concurrent setup to obtain a wide-band snapshot spectrum (42-407 GHz) at a single binary orbital phase and to search for bright spectral lines and high-resolution circum-binary continuum structure before the imminent nova eruption.  
  > "in frequency bands 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 8 in a near-concurrent fashion such that a wide-band snap-shot at a single binary orbital phase could be constructed." (Observations); "The spectral setup of each MOUS tried to enable the detection of various potential bright spectral lines while at the same time also achieving good continuum sensitivity." (Observations)
- Why this method: MTMFS deconvolution with two Taylor terms (nterms=2) on the combined 12m visibilities was used both to search for resolved spatial features at high sensitivity and to obtain an independent check on the power-law spectral index.  
  > "All MOUSs and all bands combined with full aggregate bandwidth using Multi-Term Multi-Frequency Synthesis (cite: 2011A&A...532A..71R) in order to verify the spectral parameters of T CrB obtained from the per-band measurements with an alternative method and search for potential spatial features in the image (Fig. [2](#fig:image))." (Calibration, imaging, and flux density measurements)
- Alternative: Band 9 observations were originally planned as the highest frequency band, but were switched to Band 8 due to inadequate weather conditions.  
  > "For reasons of inadequate weather conditions, the highest band observations (originally planned as band 9 but later switched to band 8) could only be obtained 26 days later." (Observations)
- Alternative: Two test observations with the 7 m ACA array in Bands 3 and 6 were obtained, but rejected from the analysis due to inferior sensitivity and non-detection of T CrB.  
  > "As the table shows, these observations have much inferior sensitivity and thus did not result in a detection of T CrB. They are shown here only for completeness and are not further included in the analysis." (Observations)
- Alternative: Fitting a single power law to all nine ALMA measurements across all orbital phases was considered, but only the orbital phase 0.43 spectrum was regarded as well-defined due to expected orbital modulation and lack of full multi-band coverage at other phases.  
  > "While the reduced $\chi^2$ of 1.1 for the power law fit to all data points is perfectly acceptable, and the spectral index is formally better constrained when all data are used, we note that due to the well motivated expectation of spectral variability, only our spectrum for phase 0.43 can be regarded as well defined because our dataset lacks spectral coverage at other phases." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
- Alternative: Two observing nights (2016-08-25 and 2016-09-22) from the Linford et al. (2019) VLA dataset were excluded from the spectral index re-analysis due to documented VLA atmospheric delay model and high-frequency decorrelation issues.  
  > "We interpret this as a justification to exclude the two observing nights from the sample. They obviously are affected by systematic errors which make them not useful for our investigation of the variability of the spectral index." (Spectral re-analysis of the 2016/17 VLA data)
- How they decided it was enough: Supplemental observations in Bands 1 and 7 were requested after the 10 August 2024 analysis revealed the unexpected faintness of T CrB, and a Band 1 re-observation was granted due to disabled Walsh switching.  
  > "Meanwhile the analysis of the data from 10 August had revealed the unexpected faintness of the object and supplemental observations were requested in bands 1 and 7 to increase sensitivity." (Observations); "And because of the disabled Walsh switching in our first band 1 observation, we were granted a re-observation 104 days after 10 August (marked "1" in "ALMA Band")." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Incomplete orbital phase coverage across all ALMA bands: only orbital phase 0.43 has near-complete multi-band coverage (Bands 1, 3, 4, 6, 7), while the full pre-burst dataset spans 104 days (46% of the orbit).  
  > "Our pre-burst observations of T CrB span nearly half a rotation of the system (46% from phase 0.4328 to 0.8889) but we are far from having a complete coverage of all bands at all orbital phases." (Spectral and spatial analysis)
- Limitation (authors): In Band 1a, 180-degree Walsh switching was turned off, and in Band 1b one continuum spectral window was accidentally centered at 40 GHz instead of 46 GHz.  
  > "Among these, the band 1 observation (marked "1a" in column "ALMA Band") was taken with 180$^\circ$ Walsh switching turned off" (Observations); "In the case of the Band "1b" observations, one of the two continuum spectral windows was accidentally placed at a central frequency of 40 GHz instead of 46 GHz which led to an overall shift of the centroid frequency of all combined band "1b" data to 42.089 GHz instead of 44.014 GHz observed for the band "1" data." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): The difference between the softest individual 2016/17 VLA spectral index and the 2024 ALMA spectral index is only 0.26 +/- 0.13, a 2-sigma effect, requiring deeper observations over a full orbit for firmer conclusions.  
  > "However, the difference between the softest VLA index and ours is only 0.26$\pm$0.13, a 2 sigma effect. Deeper observations covering the entire orbital period would be needed to draw firmer conclusions." (Spectral re-analysis of the 2016/17 VLA data)
- Limitation (authors): The analytical stellar wind model neglects geometrical effects, assumes an isotropic, isothermal, fully ionized power-law wind, and has inherent inconsistencies when estimating mass-loss rate at a fixed launch speed for beta > 2.  
  > "Yet, we assume the wind temperature $T_0$ to be uniform and discard geometrical effects such as the ones considered by (cite: 1984ApJ...286..263T)." (Model); "The derived uncertainties of the $\beta$ values do not include systematics arising from the simplifying assumptions of the model (e.g. assuming a power-law for the wind density profile)." (Data interpretation); "In the top $x$-axis, we provide an estimate of the stellar mass-loss rate assuming a wind launched at $10$ km/s from the stellar surface (and neglecting the inherent inconsistencies due to $\beta>2$)." (Data interpretation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing the 2016/17 VLA spectral index (13.5-35 GHz) with the 2024 ALMA spectral index (44-350 GHz) conflates temporal state changes with potential intrinsic spectral curvature across different frequency regimes.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The combined Band 1-8 MTMFS image mixes visibilities taken across 104 days (nearly half an orbital period) during which the flux density at 42-44 GHz varied from 111 to 182 uJy, violating the time-invariant source assumption of MTMFS.
