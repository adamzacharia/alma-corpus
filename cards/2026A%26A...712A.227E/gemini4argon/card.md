## Analysis recipe

1. **Combine multi-configuration ALMA data and image CO(1-0) data cubes**: Use QA2 pipeline products directly for 15 single-configuration (C-3) galaxies; for 26 two-configuration galaxies, subtract continuum with uvcontsub, concatenate MS files with concat, and clean with tclean using auto-multithresh and primary beam correction.  
  > "For those galaxies observed with a single array configuration (i.e. C$-3$), we directly used primary beam corrected clean maps at native resolution after inspecting the reliability through Quality Assurance Level 2 (QA2) reports and other data products." (4.1 ALMA Observations and Data reduction); "During this process, we applied a continuum subtraction of each MS with the `uvcontsub` task and we used the task `concat` to combine the MS files with a frequency tolerance of 30 MHz." (4.1.1 Data combination); "We obtained the clean data cubes using `tclean` with `ROBUST` parameter 0.5, a sensitivity threshold of $\rm 2\times \sigma$, a channel width of 10 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$, and a pixel size of 0.1$''$." (4.1.1 Data combination)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, concat, tclean
   - parameter: freqtol = 30 MHz in concat  
  > "During this process, we applied a continuum subtraction of each MS with the `uvcontsub` task and we used the task `concat` to combine the MS files with a frequency tolerance of 30 MHz." (4.1.1 Data combination)
   - parameter: robust = 0.5, threshold = 2 sigma, channel width = 10 km s^-1, pixel size = 0.1'', auto-multithresh  
  > "We obtained the clean data cubes using `tclean` with `ROBUST` parameter 0.5, a sensitivity threshold of $\rm 2\times \sigma$, a channel width of 10 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$, and a pixel size of 0.1$''$." (4.1.1 Data combination); "We used the auto-masking `auto-multithresh` option and applied the primary beam correction to obtain the final clean data cube." (4.1.1 Data combination)

## Qualitative findings

- Void galaxies contain significant amounts of molecular gas exhibiting diverse morphologies (centrally concentrated, spiral arms, rings, bars) that correlate well with stellar mass and SFR surface densities.  
  > "The molecular gas exhibits a range of morphologies: while the majority of galaxies show centrally concentrated distributions, several display structures such as spiral arms, ring features, and bars. In most cases, the molecular gas distribution correlates well with both $\Sigma_\star$ and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}}$." (6 Discussion)
- Galaxies in ALMA CO-CAVITY have lower average stellar and molecular gas masses (~0.5 dex lower M_H2) and higher isolation (65% isolated, 83% singlets) than those in previous resolved CO surveys like EDGE-CALIFA and ALMaQUEST.  
  > "An 83% (34 objects) of the ALMA CO-CAVITY sample are singlets, and 65% (27) are isolated." (3 Sample); "However, we observe generally smaller log($M_{\rm H_2}$) by about 0.5 dex in the ALMA CO-CAVITY distribution compared to those of the EDGE-CALIFA and ALMaQUEST samples." (5.1 Molecular gas masses)
- Global scaling relations (SK, MGMS, SFMS) for star-forming void galaxies are compatible with those in denser environments, with the MGMS showing the smallest scatter.  
  > "We present global scaling relations for the ALMA CO-CAVITY sample and find that the molecular gas main sequence exhibits the smallest scatter (0.21 dex), followed by the Schmidt–Kennicutt relation and the star-forming main sequence. From integrated properties alone, we find that these scaling relations for VGs are compatible with those for denser environments." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 3 in C-3 or C-1+C-4 configurations provides 1.0''-1.5'' resolution (superior to the 2.5'' CAVITY optical IFU data) and a 15'' largest angular scale, while a single 54'' pointing covers the optical disks (d25 < 60'') of the void galaxies.  
  > "The angular resolution range requested was $1.0\ensuremath{''}-1.5\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim0.64-0.96$ kpc). This ensures a resolution superior to that of optical IFU data from CAVITY, which is of $2.5\ensuremath{''}$. The requested largest angular scale (LAS) was $15\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim10\, \rm kpc$)." (4.1 ALMA Observations and Data reduction); "The optical sizes of the galaxies are in most cases $d_{25}$ $<$ 60$''$, so the CO(1–0) emission can be well observed in a single pointing with ALMA." (4.1 ALMA Observations and Data reduction)
- Why this method: 3D masking with SoFiA 2 avoids noise outside the emission region, and degrading ALMA cubes to the 2.5'' PSF and pixel scale of PMAS/PPak and MaNGA enables direct pixel-to-pixel comparison with optical IFU products.  
  > "This downgrade in resolution was performed to allow direct comparison between ALMA CO-CAVITY and the IFU data products." (4.1.2 Rebinning, smoothing and regridding); "We used the `Source Finding Application, SoFiA 2` pipeline (cite: Westmeier2021) to obtain the 3D masks. We first cropped the datacube in a range of $\pm 300$ $\rm km\, s^{-1}$ around the optical velocity to avoid false detections outside the emission region." (4.1.3 Masks and detections)
- Alternative: Using a fixed Galactic CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1) instead of the metallicity-dependent Accurso et al. (2017) prescription was tested and found not to change the results.  
  > "We note that using a fixed value of $\alpha_{\rm CO}=4.3\ \mathrm{M_{\odot}\ (K\ km\ s^{-1}\ pc^{2})^{-1}}$ as in EDGE-CALIFA and ALMaQUEST does not substantially change the offset." (5.1 Molecular gas masses); "Again, considering a fixed $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ in our case does not change the results." (5.4 Global scaling relations: SK, MGMS, SFMS)
- Alternative: Excluding galaxy 53609 due to potential AGN contamination in single-fibre BPT classification was rejected after resolved IFU BPT maps showed it is composite and not AGN-dominated.  
  > "For galaxy `53609`, using the BPT map (as in 5.3) obtained from IFU data, we confirmed that although potentially contaminated as a composite, it is not AGN dominated and therefore decided not to exclude it." (3 Sample)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Galaxies with SFR < 0.1 M_sun yr^-1 and M_star < 10^9 M_sun / 12+log(O/H) < 8.4 are excluded by the sample selection criteria.  
  > "From the CAVITY sample, the selection criteria that yields the 41 galaxies in ALMA CO-CAVITY are: 1) Declination $\rm Dec < 30$$^{\circ}$, so that they are easily observable from the ALMA site, 2) $M_{\star}>10^9\ {\rm M_\odot}$, 3) metallicity $\rm 12+\log(O/H) > 8.4$" (3 Sample); "However, objects with SFR below $0.1\ \mathrm{M_{\odot}\ yr^{-1}}$ are missing." (3.1 Comparison with other samples)
- Limitation (authors): In the low-flux regime, some ALMA and IRAM 30m integrated fluxes disagree due to poor S/N and baseline subtraction uncertainties in the single-dish spectra.  
  > "However, in the low-flux regime, in a few cases the integrated fluxes disagree even if taking absolute flux scale uncertainties in both instruments. These differences are probably due to poor S/N and uncertain baseline subtraction in the single dish data" (4.1.4 Potential flux loss due to lack of short-spacings)
- Limitation (authors): Quantitative analysis of resolved kpc-scale scaling relations, radial profiles, and rotation curves is beyond the scope of this introductory paper and deferred to future work.  
  > "However, a more quantitative comparison beyond the scope of this work is required to assess potential differences. In particular, analyses of radial distributions (cite: Bolatto2017; 2024ApJ...964..120P) and the derivation of rotation curves (cite: 2022ApJ...934..173S) would provide further insight." (6 Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The masking resolution in SoFiA 2 is inhomogeneous across the sample (12 galaxies masked at native resolution, 17 spectrally rebinned to 10 km/s, 6 spatially smoothed to 2.5'', and 2 manually edited), which could introduce systematic biases in recovered low-surface-brightness flux.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Global scaling relation slopes for ALMA CO-CAVITY are systematically steeper (1.08-1.25) than those of the parent IRAM 30m CO-CAVITY SF sample (0.77-0.89), likely because the ALMA fit includes starburst and transition-zone galaxies whereas the comparison fits are restricted to SFMS galaxies.
