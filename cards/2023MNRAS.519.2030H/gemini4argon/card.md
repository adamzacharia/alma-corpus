## Analysis recipe

1. **Image CO(3-2) data cube and generate moment maps**: Image calibrated visibility data using CASA tclean with Briggs robust=2.0 at ~9 km s^-1 resolution, convert to rest-frame using z=0.3214 and CO(3-2) rest frequency 345.796 GHz, and create moment 0 and moment 1 maps in CARTA  
  > "The data cube is obtained with the `CASA` task `tclean()` by setting the robust parameter of $2.0$ for Briggs weighting with a velocity resolution of $\sim$9 km s$^{-1}$." (Observation and Data analysis); "The velocity-integrated intensity (moment 0) map and the velocity structure (moment 1) map are obtained by Cube Analysis and Rendering Tool for Astronomy (CARTA) (cite: Comrie2021)." (Observation and Data analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean, CARTA
   - parameter: robust = 2.0, velocity resolution ~9 km s^-1  
  > "The data cube is obtained with the `CASA` task `tclean()` by setting the robust parameter of $2.0$ for Briggs weighting with a velocity resolution of $\sim$9 km s$^{-1}$." (Observation and Data analysis)
   - parameter: moment 0 integration range ~ -180 km s^-1 to ~ 110 km s^-1  
  > "Figure [1](#fig.1) (left) shows the velocity-integrated intensity map with the integration range of $\sim -180$ km s$^{-1}$ to $\sim 110$ km s$^{-1}$." (CO emission line detection)
   - parameter: moment 1 detection threshold > 4 sigma  
  > "Furthermore, figure [4](#fig.4) shows the velocity field of the region with CO (3-2) emission-line detection higher than $4 \sigma$." (Asymmetric CO spectrum)
   - assumption: Spectroscopic redshift z = 0.3214, CO(3-2) rest frequency = 345.796 GHz  
  > "The observed frequency of the data cube is converted to the rest-frame frequency using the spectroscopic redshift of $z=0.3214$ (cite: Bannister2019)." (Observation and Data analysis); "The Doppler velocity is calculated using the rest-frame frequency of the CO (3-2) emission line, i.e., 345.796 GHz." (Observation and Data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract CO(3-2) spectrum and fit velocity components**: Extract CO spectrum using a circular aperture centred on the host galaxy and fit double Gaussian functions to the two detected velocity components  
  > "We utilize an aperture with a radius of 1.032 arcsec (5 kpc) centred at the host galaxy (RA, Dec)$=$ (21h44m25.25s, $-40^{\circ} 54\ensuremath{'} 00.81\ensuremath{''}$) (cite: Bhandari2020) to extract a CO spectrum of the host galaxy." (CO emission line detection); "We fit double Gaussian functions to these two components in the spectrum." (CO emission line detection)
   - parameter: aperture radius = 1.032 arcsec (5 kpc), centre = (21h44m25.25s, -40d54m00.81s)  
  > "We utilize an aperture with a radius of 1.032 arcsec (5 kpc) centred at the host galaxy (RA, Dec)$=$ (21h44m25.25s, $-40^{\circ} 54\ensuremath{'} 00.81\ensuremath{''}$) (cite: Bhandari2020) to extract a CO spectrum of the host galaxy." (CO emission line detection)
   - fitting: Double Gaussian function fit  
  > "We fit double Gaussian functions to these two components in the spectrum." (CO emission line detection)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure peak positions and spatial offset of the two velocity components**: Integrate each velocity component over +/- 2 sigma_V from V_sys, fit central peak positions with CASA task imfit, and compute positional uncertainty from beam major axis and S/N  
  > "Using the `CASA` task `imfit`, we obtain the central position of each peak of the two velocity components." (Positional offset between two peaks); "Each velocity component is integrated over $\pm$ 2 $\sigma_{V}$ from $V_{\rm sys}$." (Positional offset between two peaks); "We calculate a quadrature sum of the positional errors of the two velocity components to derive $\sim 0.\ensuremath{''}10$ positional uncertainty." (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: integration range = +/- 2 sigma_V from V_sys  
  > "Each velocity component is integrated over $\pm$ 2 $\sigma_{V}$ from $V_{\rm sys}$." (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - parameter: beam major axis a ~ 0.68 arcsec  
  > "Both $a$ $\sim 0.\ensuremath{''}68$ and S/N $= 9.60$ and $8.76$ (table [\[table1\]](#table1)) are obtained from the velocity-integrated intensity map in figure [2](#fig.2)." (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - fitting: CASA task imfit on velocity-integrated intensity maps of each component  
  > "Using the `CASA` task `imfit`, we obtain the central position of each peak of the two velocity components." (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive CO luminosities and molecular gas masses**: Calculate L'_CO(3-2) from integrated CO(3-2) line flux using Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005) equation, convert to L'_CO(1-0) via luminosity ratio, and multiply by alpha_CO to obtain M_gas  
  > "The CO luminosity and molecular gas mass are derived from the CO(3-2) emission-line flux measurement for each component." (Positional offset between two peaks); "The CO Luminosity is calculated using equation (3) in (cite: Solomon2005) as follows:" (Positional offset between two peaks); "The Molecular gas mass is derived as follows: $$\begin{equation}
\label{molecular gas equ}
M_{\rm gas}=\alpha_{\rm CO} L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}.
\end{equation}$$" (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - assumption: L'_CO(3-2)/L'_CO(1-0) = 0.55  
  > "The CO (1-0) luminosity ($L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}$) is derived by applying the CO luminosity ratio of $L'_{\rm CO(3-2)}/L'_{\rm CO(1-0)}=0.55$ for star-forming galaxies (cite: Lamperti2021; Hatsukade2022)." (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun (K km^-1 s^-1 pc^2)^-1  
  > "We adopted $\alpha_{\rm CO}=4.3$ $M_\odot$ (K km$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$)$^{-1}$ since the gas-phase metallicity is $12 + \log ({\rm O/H})=8.93 \pm 0.02$ (cite: Heintz2020), which is comparable to the Milky way gas-phase metallicity." (Positional offset between two peaks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared total L'_CO and M_gas with Hatsukade et al. (2022) and confirmed consistency within errors  
  > "This value is consistent with ($3.1 \pm 0.3) \times 10^{8}$ K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$(($2.4 \pm 0.2) \times 10^{9}$ M$_\odot$) reported by (cite: Hatsukade2022) within the errors." (Positional offset between two peaks)
5. **Quantify CO spectral asymmetry**: Calculate integrated flux ratio A_flux and peak flux density ratio A_peak from the best-fit Gaussian functions following Reynolds et al. (2020)  
  > "To test the asymmetry of the CO spectrum, we apply the diagnostic (cite: Reynolds2020) to define the spectrum asymmetry, deriving the ratio of the integrated fluxes between the left and right halve of the spectrum, A$_{\rm flux}$ (cite: Reynolds2020)" (Asymmetric CO spectrum); "We integrate the best-fit Gaussian function from $V_{\rm low}$ to $V_{\rm mid}$ for the left halve and $V_{\rm mid}$ to $V_{\rm high}$ for the right halve." (Asymmetric CO spectrum)
   - parameter: 20% of maximum peak height used to define V_low, V_mid, and V_high; asymmetry threshold A_flux > 1.05  
  > "To derive A$_{\rm flux}$, the boundary between the left and right halve of the spectrum is defined by the mid-point of the full width at 20 % of the maximum peak height as $V_{\rm mid}$." (Asymmetric CO spectrum); "Following (cite: Reynolds2020), we define the spectrum as asymmetry with A$_{\rm flux}>1.05$, which is commonly used in the early studies (cite: Reynolds2020)." (Asymmetric CO spectrum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Stars form directly from molecular gas, making CO observations a more direct probe of kinematic features and recent star formation enhancement than HI gas; re-imaging the Hatsukade et al. (2022) ALMA data at 9 km s^-1 (instead of 50 km s^-1) enables resolving the molecular gas kinematics.  
  > "Since stars are born from molecular gases, they provide us with a more direct probe of kinetic features and a recent enhancement of star formation than HI gas." (Introduction); "In this work, we use the same data as used by (cite: Hatsukade2022) with a better velocity resolution of 9 km s$^{-1}$ to focus on the kinematics of molecular gas." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Applied the spectral asymmetry diagnostic (A_flux and A_peak) from Reynolds et al. (2020) and threshold A_flux > 1.05 to allow a fair comparison with the repeating FRB host galaxy sample of Michalowski (2021).  
  > "To test the asymmetry of the CO spectrum, we apply the diagnostic (cite: Reynolds2020) to define the spectrum asymmetry, deriving the ratio of the integrated fluxes between the left and right halve of the spectrum, A$_{\rm flux}$ (cite: Reynolds2020)" (Asymmetric CO spectrum); "The comparison sample by (cite: Michalowski2021) also uses 1.05, allowing a fair comparison with previous studies." (Asymmetric CO spectrum)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): HI and H2 gas do not necessarily trace each other or share physical association, so comparing molecular gas (CO) kinematics in the non-repeating FRB host with atomic gas (HI) kinematics in repeating FRB hosts is not strictly direct.  
  > "However, we note that the HI and H$_{\rm 2}$ gas do not necessarily trace each other or are not necessarily physically associated." (Discussion and conclusion); "Therefore, the following comparison between the HI kinematics of the repeating FRB host galaxy and molecular gas kinematics is not strictly direct." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Limitation (authors): A_flux for the FRB 180924B host (1.05 +/- 0.16) lies on the boundary of the asymmetry definition because kinetic information can be lost during the flux integration process.  
  > "A$_{\rm flux}$ of the host galaxy of FRB 180924B is on the boundary of the asymmetry, whereas A$_{\rm peak}$ indicates the clear asymmetric kinematics." (Asymmetric CO spectrum); "This might be because the kinetic information could be lost by the integration process (cite: Reynolds2020), making A$_{\rm flux}$ less sensitive to the spectral shape of FRB 180924B host." (Asymmetric CO spectrum)
- Limitation (authors): Observations of molecular gas in FRB host galaxies are still limited to a very small sample.  
  > "However, the observation of molecular gas in FRB host galaxies is still limited. Therefore, more observations are essential to understand the relationship between the gas kinematics and FRB progenitor." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistent association of the two velocity components with physical locations across sections: the Abstract states the -155.40 km s^-1 component is near the host centre and the -7.76 km s^-1 component is near the FRB position, whereas Section 3.2 states the blue contour (-155.40 km s^-1) is close to the FRB position and the red contour (-7.76 km s^-1) is close to the host galaxy centre.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistent host redshift values stated in the text (z = 0.3216 in Abstract vs. z = 0.3214 in Section 2).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using natural-like weighting (Briggs robust = 2.0) maximizes surface brightness sensitivity but degrades spatial resolution (~0.51 x 0.68 arcsec), meaning the two components separated by ~0.51 arcsec are marginally resolved by approximately one beamwidth.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample size of one non-repeating FRB host galaxy is insufficient to establish a statistically robust population-level link between molecular gas kinematics and FRB progenitors.
