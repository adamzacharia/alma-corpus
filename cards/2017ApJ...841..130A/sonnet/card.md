## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image ALMA Band 7 data**: CASA calibration with WVR continuum subtraction, field-phase-offset removal, Tsys extrapolation; natural-weighted imaging of full band and each sideband  
  > "A simple solution of subtracting the field-averaged phase correction from the calibration table produced by `wvrgcal` was applied." (Appendix)
   - software: CASA, wvrgcal, TelCal
   - parameter: Tatm=270 K for Tsys extrapolation  
  > "A nominal atmosphere and blocking temperature $T_{\rm atm}=270\,{\rm K}$ was used" (Appendix)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: calibrator source structure of the gain calibrator checked via clean-component self-calibration; sideband images as cross-check  
  > "The maximum in the residual self-calibration phases of QSO$\,$J0635-7516 was around $3^\circ$, and there was no significant effect on the image of RMC$\,$127" (Appendix)
2. **measure central-object flux densities and size**: peak flux at central position; 9 GHz diagonal-arm contribution subtracted via Gaussian slice; 2-D Gaussian imfit at 23 GHz  
  > "We subtract this value from the peak flux density at the position of the central object and derive a brightness of $0.08\pm0.05\,\rm mJy$ at 9 GHz." (3.2 Spectral index of the central object)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - fitting: 2-D Gaussian in image plane  
  > "we used the task `imfit` of CASA to fit a 2-D Gaussian to the central object in the image-plane." (3.1 The ATCA and ALMA maps)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **fit radio-sub-mm spectral index**: weighted power-law fit S~nu^alpha, 9 to 349.5 GHz  
  > "We derive a weighted fit of the power-law ($S_{\nu}\sim \nu^{\alpha}$) between the centimeter and sub-millimeter flux densities of the central object" (3.2 Spectral index of the central object)
   - fitting: weighted power-law fit  
  > "We derive a weighted fit of the power-law ($S_{\nu}\sim \nu^{\alpha}$) between the centimeter and sub-millimeter flux densities of the central object" (3.2 Spectral index of the central object)
   - validation: large frequency coverage deemed robust against systematics  
  > "given the large frequency coverage (9 to 349.5 GHz) we are confident of the derived spectral index." (3.2 Spectral index of the central object)
4. **nebula spectral index and index map**: integrated flux densities at 5.5, 9, 17 GHz; spectral index map from 5.5 and 9 GHz maps regridded, pixels >3 sigma  
  > "we computed the spectral index map and its associated error map (left and right panels of Fig. [4](#fig:spix_lbv), respectively) in each common pixel $>3 \sigma$." (3.3 Spectral index of the outer nebula)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 17 GHz excluded from index maps due to artifacts  
  > "We prefer not to use the map at 17 GHz to derive spectral index maps" (3.3 Spectral index of the outer nebula)
5. **fit IR SED with grey-body**: single-temperature grey-body with power-law opacity index beta fit to mid- to far-IR photometry  
  > "We fit the SED from the mid- to the far-IR with a single-temperature gray-body with power-law opacity index $\beta$." (3.4 SED)
   - software: VizieR
   - parameter: beta = 1.5, 2, 2.5; best beta=2.0  
  > "We found a range of characteristic temperatures between $71$ to $90\,\rm K$ by varying $\beta$ between 1.5, 2 and 2.5 (extreme case)." (3.4 SED)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **collimated ionized wind models and mass-loss rate**: Reynolds (1986) models 1 and 2 solved for jet width index epsilon from alpha, then Mdot from Reynolds equation  
  > "The mass-loss rate can be written in a general form that takes into account all these parameters (cite: 1986Reynolds)" (4.1 Collimated stellar wind models)
   - parameter: S at 349.5 GHz, D=48.5 kpc, T=6420 K, v=148 km/s, varphi=75 deg, x0=1, mu=1, theta0=0.5 rad, numax=1e13 Hz  
  > "Here we assume $\varphi=75\,\rm \deg$ (thus $i=15\,\rm \deg$)." (4.1 Collimated stellar wind models); "A reasonable value for $\nu_{max}$ seems to be $10^{13}$ Hz." (4.1 Collimated stellar wind models)
   - assumption: isothermal, constant-velocity outflow; model 1 fully ionized conical, model 2 with recombinations qx=-0.2  
  > "model 1 (isothermal, constant velocity, fully ionized conical outflow) and for model 2 (isothermal, constant velocity, well-collimated outflow with recombinations)" (Table 2)
   - fitting: analytic solution  
  > "Knowing the spectral index $\alpha$ of the wind, it is possible to determine how the jet width varies with distance (parameter $\varepsilon$)" (4.1 Collimated stellar wind models)
   - validation: compared with spherical-wind Panagia & Felli Mdot  
  > "The mass-loss rate can be a factor of two or more smaller than in the spherical case" (4.1 Collimated stellar wind models)
7. **dust mass of the nebula**: Md = S D^2/(B(T) kappa) using grey-body flux at 343 GHz  
  > "considering that $M_{d}=S_{\nu}\,D^{2}/(B_{\nu}(T)\,\kappa_{\nu})$" (4 outer nebula)
   - assumption: kappa(343 GHz)=1.7 cm2/g; T=80+-10 K  
  > "average temperature of $\rm T=80\pm10\, K$" (4 outer nebula)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **precessing-jet nebula simulation and binary toy model**: RHOCUBE 3-D double conical helix simulation; precession period from diagonal arm size; companion separation from torque equation  
  > "we used our new public code RHOCUBE (cite: 2016N&A; 2017AgliozzoA) to simulate a 3-D double conical helix nebula." (5 precessing jet model)
   - software: RHOCUBE
   - parameter: M2=12 Msun, R*=50 Rsun, M1=60 Msun, i=15 deg, theta=45 deg  
  > "If we assume as stellar radius $R_{*}$ = 50 R$_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ and mass $M_{1}$ = 60 M$_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$" (5 binary toy model)
   - assumption: constant-velocity jet; solid sphere moment of inertia  
  > "For the moment of inertia we approximate a solid sphere, given the small dependence of $a$ on $I$." (5 binary toy model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: qualitative similarity to 17 GHz morphology; X-ray archival limits  
  > "We find a remarkable similarity of panel (2) in the figure with the 17-GHz morphology of RMC$\,$127." (5 precessing jet model)

## Qualitative findings

- Radio maps reveal a Z-pattern asymmetric nebula core, also hinted in HST Halpha.  
  > "The radio maps show for the first time the core of the nebula and evidence that the nebula is strongly asymmetric with a Z-pattern shape." (abstract)
- Nebula was not detected with ALMA; only the central point source.  
  > "Therefore, the nebula was not detected. The central object was instead detected at $15 \sigma$." (2.1 ALMA observation and data reduction)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Spherical and clumpy wind models cannot reproduce the observed SED, so Reynolds collimated wind used  
  > "None of the clumpy stellar wind models can reproduce the observed radio SED." (4 central object)
- Alternative: Spherical wind and clumpy/porous wind models rejected for the central-object SED  
  > "These results make unsuitable all the models based on spherical symmetry." (4 central object)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Planned ALMA sensitivity not reached, so nebula undetected  
  > "The proposed sensitivity of $40\,\mu{\rm Jy}\,{\rm beam^{-1}}$ could not be achieved" (2.1 ALMA observation and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Central-object size is an upper limit; flux systematics from differing beams and nebular contamination  
  > "Hence, the size provided above must be considered an upper limit." (3.1 The ATCA and ALMA maps)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spectral index fit mixes beams from 1.5 arcsec to 0.6 arcsec and observations separated by 3 years of a variable star
- Caveat (inferred, ours): nu_max and inclination are assumed, driving Mdot
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only a single ALMA band so little leverage within ALMA alone
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass depends on assumed kappa and grey-body fit with sparse far-IR points
