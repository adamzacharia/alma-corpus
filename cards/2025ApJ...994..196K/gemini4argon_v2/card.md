## Analysis recipe

1. **Collect and calibrate multi-frequency total flux and polarimetric time-series data for 3C 286**: Collect ALMA grid monitoring data reduced with AMAPOLA, process IRAM 30-m XPOL data with fixed Kelvin-to-Jansky conversion factors, and calibrate SMA SMAPOL monitoring data using MIR and MIRIAD.  
  > "For polarization analysis, we collected data reduced using the Analytic Matrix for ALMA POLArimetry (AMAPOLA)." (Data / ALMA); "Consequently, the conversion factor was determined to be ($6.1\pm0.1$) Jy K$^{-1}$ and ($8.6\pm0.4$) Jy K$^{-1}$ for 86 and 229 GHz, respectively (cite: agudo18_1). These factors were applied to the entire data obtained from 2006 to 2024" (Data / The IRAM 30-m Telescope); "The initial data calibration such as flagging, bandpass, cross-receiver delays, complex gains, flux calibration, and RL-phase offset was performed using the MIR software package , and the instrumental polarization was calibrated with the MIRIAD (cite: sault95)." (Data / SMA)
   - software: AMAPOLA, CASA, MIR, MIRIAD
   - parameter: IRAM conversion factors: (6.1 +/- 0.1) Jy K^-1 at 86 GHz and (8.6 +/- 0.4) Jy K^-1 at 229 GHz  
  > "Consequently, the conversion factor was determined to be ($6.1\pm0.1$) Jy K$^{-1}$ and ($8.6\pm0.4$) Jy K$^{-1}$ for 86 and 229 GHz, respectively (cite: agudo18_1)." (Data / The IRAM 30-m Telescope)
   - assumption: ESA4 brightness temperature model for Uranus and Lellouch & Amri model for Mars  
  > "Specifically, the model for Uranus is based on the ESA4 model" (Data / ALMA); "The Mars model is based on the Lellouch & Amri model, which provides the brightness temperature and flux of Mars for any frequency and date between 30-5000 GHz and year 1990-2036." (Data / The IRAM 30-m Telescope)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-check IRAM polarization calibration with the Crab Nebula; AMAPOLA dataset validated in previous AGN studies.  
  > "The dataset has been validated and used in previous studies of AGN jets such as M87 (cite: goddi21; goddi25) and OJ 287 (cite: jormanainen25), as well as in studies of radio-quiet AGN (cite: shablovinskaia25), supporting the reliability of the polarization measurements." (Data / ALMA); "To cross-check the results of the polarization calibration, the Crab Nebula was observed (cite: agudo18_1)" (Data / The IRAM 30-m Telescope)
2. **Evaluate long-term stability of total flux, EVPA, and fractional polarization and derive reference values**: Divide ALMA data into three time periods (Period I: MJD 58230-59000, Period II: MJD 59200-59900, Period III: after MJD 60100) to compare period averages, fit linear secular trend models to ALMA and IRAM time series (including IRAM Uranus residuals), and compute weighted means across epochs as reference values.  
  > "To investigate this in further detail, we divided the ALMA data into three groups: the first, obtained between MJD 58230 and 59000, referred to as Period I, the second, obtained between MJD 59200 and 59900, referred to as Period II, and the third, after MJD 60100, referred to as Period III." (Results / Total flux); "To further investigate the long-term trend in the ALMA data, we fitted all the total flux measurements at each frequency with the following function: $S_{\nu} = c_0 \times (t-58000) + c_1$" (Results / Total flux); "Therefore, we provide the weighted mean of the EVPA and fractional polarization, as well as the total flux, as the reference values for 3C 286 (Table 2)." (Results / Polarization)
   - parameter: Period I: MJD 58230-59000; Period II: MJD 59200-59900; Period III: MJD > 60100  
  > "the first, obtained between MJD 58230 and 59000, referred to as Period I, the second, obtained between MJD 59200 and 59900, referred to as Period II, and the third, after MJD 60100, referred to as Period III." (Results / Total flux)
   - fitting: Linear function fit: S_nu = c_0 * (t - 58000) + c_1 (and equivalent for EVPA, fractional polarization, and Uranus residual flux)  
  > "To further investigate the long-term trend in the ALMA data, we fitted all the total flux measurements at each frequency with the following function: $S_{\nu} = c_0 \times (t-58000) + c_1$" (Results / Total flux)
   - validation: Compare secular variations against standard deviations and measurement uncertainties; examine IRAM 86 GHz Uranus residual flux to test calibrator brightening.  
  > "A fit using Equation [eq:fit] to the entire residual flux indicates a systematic increase of 6.6%$\pm$1.5% from 2006 to 2024" (Results / Total flux); "Furthermore, the best-fit parameters derived using Equation [eq:fit] indicate that any potential secular changes in both EVPA and fractional polarization during the monitoring period are smaller than or comparable to their standard deviations and/or uncertainties at all frequencies" (Results / Polarization)
3. **Measure spectral indices and spectral break frequency**: Fit power-law spectra (S_nu proportional to nu^-alpha) separately to historical VLA measurements (3-50 GHz) and IRAM reference measurements (86-229 GHz) to determine spectral indices and their intersection (break frequency).  
  > "For the frequencies higher than those covered by VLA, we use only the IRAM measurements to determine the spectral index, as the ALMA flux appears to vary with different rates in different frequency bands." (Discussion / Synchrotron cooling); "Specifically, the spectral index between 3 and 50 GHz, where the flux nearly follows a simple power-law, yields a spectral index of $\alpha=0.712\pm0.003$. In contrast, the spectral index between 86 and 229 GHz is $\alpha=1.039\pm0.008$ (Table 3)." (Discussion / Synchrotron cooling)
   - parameter: Frequency ranges: 3-50 GHz (VLA) and 86-229 GHz (IRAM)  
  > "3–50 | $0.712\pm0.003$ | $67.2\pm1.2$ | $1.2\pm0.1$" (Table 3); "86–229 | $1.039\pm0.008$" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Power-law spectrum S_nu proportional to nu^-alpha in each frequency regime  
  > "The spectrum steepens at higher frequencies, resulting in a spectral break where the spectral index $\alpha$ (defined by $S_{\nu}\propto \nu^{-\alpha}$) changes between lower and higher frequency ranges" (Discussion / Synchrotron cooling)
   - fitting: Power-law spectral fit S_nu proportional to nu^-alpha  
  > "where the spectral index $\alpha$ (defined by $S_{\nu}\propto \nu^{-\alpha}$) changes between lower and higher frequency ranges" (Discussion / Synchrotron cooling)
   - validation: Assess sensitivity of break frequency to a potential 2% underestimation in IRAM 86 GHz flux (which shifts break to 71.4 GHz).  
  > "As described in Section 3.1, if the IRAM flux measurements were underestimated by $\sim2\%$, the break frequency is shifted to 71.4 GHz." (Footnotes)
4. **Estimate equipartition magnetic field strength, synchrotron age, and jet apparent speed**: Calculate equipartition magnetic field strength B_eq using the Govoni & Feretti (2004) formula, then compute synchrotron age tau_syn from the break frequency using the continuous injection model (Equation 4), and derive the corresponding apparent jet speed.  
  > "We estimate the average magnetic field strength in the emitting region by assuming the equipartition condition, in which the total energy is minimized and the energy densities of relativistic particles and magnetic fields are approximately equal (cite: govoni04)." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength); "Substituting the magnetic field strength of 4.4 mG into Equation [eq:tau_syn], we derive the age of 3C 286 to be $\tau_{\rm syn}$=450 years. Given that the source size of 50 mas corresponds to 362 pc, this age implies an average apparent speed $\beta_{\rm app}=2.6$." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength)
   - parameter: xi = 1.23 x 10^-12, I_0 = 7.297 Jy at nu_0 = 4.885 GHz, source size = 50 mas (d = 0.36 kpc), k = 1  
  > "We adopt $\xi(\alpha, \nu_1, \nu_2)=1.23\times10^{-12}$, which is given for $\alpha\sim0.7$ between the turnover and break frequencies (cite: govoni04), and the total flux of $I_{0}$=7.297 Jy at $\nu_0$=4.885 GHz (cite: perley13_flux)." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength); "we adopt a source size of 50 mas. This corresponds to a depth of $d=3.6\times10^{-1}$ kpc, assuming a spherical geometry for simplicity." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength); "Assuming an energy ratio between electrons and protons of $k=1$, as commonly adopted in the literature, these parameters yield an equipartition magnetic field strength of $B_{\rm eq}$$\sim$4.4 mG." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength)
   - assumption: Continuous injection model, no source expansion, constant magnetic field strength, energy equipartition, spherical geometry, proton-to-electron energy ratio k = 1, cosmology H_0 = 73.30 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.327, Omega_Lambda = 0.673  
  > "We adopt cosmological parameters $H_{\rm 0}= \rm 73.30\ km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}$ and $q_{0}=-0.51\pm0.024$ obtained from the Type Ia supernovae in the local universe (cite: riess22). These parameters correspond to $\Omega_{\rm m}=\rm 0.327$ and $\Omega_{\rm \Lambda}=\rm 0.673$ in a flat universe." (Introduction); "Assuming (i) a continuous injection model, where new relativistic electrons with a power-law energy distribution are continuously injected to the synchrotron-emitting source, (ii) no expansion of the emitting source, and (iii) constant magnetic field strength" (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength)
   - fitting: Analytical calculation from Equations 4 and 5  
  > "Substituting the magnetic field strength of 4.4 mG into Equation [eq:tau_syn], we derive the age of 3C 286 to be $\tau_{\rm syn}$=450 years." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength)
   - validation: Test sensitivity to choice of I_0 across 1-40 GHz (<6% change in B_eq) and proton-to-electron ratio k = 1836 (B_eq = 31 mG), and compare derived apparent speed with VLBI measurements.  
  > "However, these apparent speeds are significantly faster than the apparent speed $\beta_{\rm app}=0.6\pm0.5$ observed in the inner jet at 5 mas from the core (cite: an17)." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength); "$B_{\rm eq}$ varies by less than $\sim$6% when using other values of $I_0$ at frequencies between 1–40 GHz in Table 9 in (cite: perley13_flux)." (Footnotes); "However, adopting $k=1836$ changes $B_{\rm eq}$ by only several factors ($B_{\rm eq}=31$ mG), indicating that $B_{\rm eq}$ is not highly sensitive to the choice of $k$." (Footnotes)
5. **Model frequency-dependent EVPA, rotation measure (RM), and fractional polarization**: Perform weighted power-law fits to EVPA and fractional polarization as functions of wavelength squared (Equations 6 and 8), sample model EVPAs at representative frequencies to compute pairwise observing-frame and rest-frame RMs, and fit a power law to rest-frame RM versus frequency.  
  > "To investigate the frequency dependency of the RM in more detail, we fit all the EVPA values with a single power-law function of the form $\mathrm{log(\chi)} = k\ \mathrm{log(\lambda^2) + C}$" (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "Using these coefficients, we sample the EVPA values along the best-fit curve at ten representative frequencies and calculate the best-fit RM values between adjacent pairs." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "Motivated by the EVPA distribution, we fit the fractional polarization values with a single power-law function of the form: $\mathrm{log}(m) = k'\ \mathrm{log(\lambda^2) + C'}$" (Discussion / Depolarization mechanism)
   - parameter: 10 representative frequencies between 5 and 343.4 GHz for RM sampling  
  > "Using these coefficients, we sample the EVPA values along the best-fit curve at ten representative frequencies and calculate the best-fit RM values between adjacent pairs." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency)
   - assumption: Blandford & Konigl (1979) conical jet model with core-shift and Faraday rotation (|RM_rest| = (1+z)^2 |RM_obs|)  
  > "with $\rm \left|RM_{\rm rest}\right|$ and $\rm \left|RM_{\rm obs}\right|$ denoting the rest frame and the observing frame RMs, respectively" (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "The best-fit line represents $\mid$RM~core,$\nu$~$\mid\propto \nu^{a}$ with the power-law index $a=2.05\pm0.06$, which is consistent with the theoretically expected value of $a=2$ for the BK conical jet model." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency)
   - fitting: Weighted log-linear power-law fits: log(chi) = k log(lambda^2) + C; |RM| proportional to nu^a; log(m) = k' log(lambda^2) + C'  
  > "The resulting coefficients from the weighted fit are $k=-0.016\pm0.002$ and $\rm C=1.547\pm0.003$." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "The best-fit line represents $\mid$RM~core,$\nu$~$\mid\propto \nu^{a}$ with the power-law index $a=2.05\pm0.06$" (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "Notably, the fractional polarization, spanning four orders of magnitude in wavelength squared, is well-described by a single power-law function with coefficients $k'=-0.047\pm0.001$ and $\rm C'=1.108\pm0.001$" (Discussion / Depolarization mechanism)
   - validation: Compare fitted RM frequency power-law index (a = 2.05 +/- 0.06) with the theoretical prediction (a = 2) of the Blandford & Konigl conical jet model.  
  > "The best-fit line represents $\mid$RM~core,$\nu$~$\mid\propto \nu^{a}$ with the power-law index $a=2.05\pm0.06$, which is consistent with the theoretically expected value of $a=2$ for the BK conical jet model." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency)

## Qualitative findings

- The intrinsic total flux of 3C 286 has remained stable up to 229 GHz from 2006 to 2024; the apparent decrease in ALMA flux (2%-15%) is partially attributable (up to 4%) to the brightening of Uranus as its north pole rotates into view while the ESA4 model assumes constant brightness.  
  > "The IRAM measurements from 2006 to 2024 show that the total flux of 3C 286 is stable within measurement uncertainties, indicating long-term stability up to 229 GHz, when applying a fixed Kelvin-to-Jansky conversion factor throughout its dataset." (Abstract); "ALMA data from 2018 to 2024 exhibit a decrease in flux, which up to 4% could be attributed to an apparent increase in the absolute brightness of Uranus, the primary flux calibrator for ALMA with the ESA4 model." (Abstract)
- Both fractional polarization and EVPA of 3C 286 have remained stable over the ~20-year monitoring period across 86 to 343.4 GHz.  
  > "Likewise, long-term monitoring observations with ALMA and IRAM suggest that both EVPA and fractional polarization of 3C 286 have remained nearly constant over the last $\sim$20 years." (Results / Polarization)
- The EVPA rotates and fractional polarization increases with frequency up to 343.4 GHz, both following single power-law relationships with wavelength squared.  
  > "Given that the observed EVPA values are not only well-described by a single power-law function over three orders of magnitude in wavelength squared" (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "Notably, the fractional polarization, spanning four orders of magnitude in wavelength squared, is well-described by a single power-law function with coefficients $k'=-0.047\pm0.001$ and $\rm C'=1.108\pm0.001$ (Figure 10)." (Discussion / Depolarization mechanism)
- Theoretical EVPA values from the fitted power-law model curve are proposed as a reference for absolute EVPA calibration between 5 and 343.4 GHz.  
  > "We therefore propose using the theoretical EVPA values from this model curve for absolute EVPA calibration between 5 and 343.4 GHz." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining long-term multi-frequency monitoring from ALMA, IRAM, and SMA overcomes the low brightness and large uncertainties of 3C 286 at millimeter/submillimeter wavelengths that previously hindered stability and trend analyses. Pre-2022 SMA archival data and 1.4 GHz MeerKAT EVPA data were excluded to avoid systematic calibration offsets and large frequency-dependent uncertainties.  
  > "The combined data from these three monitoring programs allows for a precise investigation of the stability and properties of 3C 286 at millimeter and submillimeter wavelengths." (Introduction); "However, we do not include these measurements in our analysis due to inconsistencies in the flux calibration process and transitions from older solar system models to the CASA-standard models during the monitoring." (Data / SMA); "We did not include $\chi=29.2^\circ$ at 1.4 GHz obtained with MeerKAT in the RM calculation, due to its frequency-dependent uncertainties of up to 3$^\circ$ (see Figure 2 in (cite: taylor24))." (Footnotes)
- Why this method: Only IRAM flux measurements were used for the high-frequency spectral index because ALMA fluxes showed band-dependent secular declines; stacking all stable polarization epochs into weighted means and fitting a smooth power-law curve reduces statistical uncertainties in pairwise RM estimates.  
  > "This stability allows us to obtain the fractional polarization and EVPA values with unprecedented accuracy at millimeter/submillimeter wavelengths by stacking all the data." (Results / Polarization); "For the frequencies higher than those covered by VLA, we use only the IRAM measurements to determine the spectral index, as the ALMA flux appears to vary with different rates in different frequency bands." (Discussion / Synchrotron cooling); "Since these model EVPAs along the best-fit curve are derived from a fit to multiple data points, their statistical uncertainties are smaller than those of individual EVPA measurements by a factor of the square root of the number of EVPA measurements." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency)
- Alternative: Gradual change in magnetic field direction with optical depth as the cause of EVPA rotation, considered alongside Faraday rotation with core-shift.  
  > "If the direction of the magnetic field gradually changes with optical depth, it could explain the observed EVPA rotation with frequency (cite: agudo12; nagai16)." (Discussion / EVPA rotation as a function of frequency); "Alternatively, it can also be explained with Faraday rotation, without requiring magnetic fields that gradually change direction with optical depth." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: One-zone synchrotron cooling model rejected in favor of a multi-zone model (with inner jet break at 67 GHz and outer jet break at ~3.6 GHz) due to implausibly high apparent jet speed (beta_app = 2.6).  
  > "This, in turn, implies that the overall spectrum may not be fully explained by a one-zone synchrotron model and may instead require a more complex, multi-zone interpretation." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength); "Specifically, if the 67 GHz break traces synchrotron aging in the inner jet while the 3.6 GHz break corresponds to the outer jet at 50 mas, the inferred synchrotron age of the outer jet becomes 1950 years." (Discussion / Synchrotron age and magnetic field strength)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): 3C 286 is observed at low elevations from ALMA (maximum elevation 37 deg), is weak at high frequencies, and is opposite in RA to Uranus, making its flux calibration dependent on secondary quasar transfer.  
  > "However, 3C 286 is at low elevations for ALMA (with the maximum elevation of 37$^\circ$) and its flux is relatively weak at higher frequencies. In addition, its flux calibration primarily relies on flux transfer from quasars, as its right ascension is opposite to the main planetary primary calibrators." (Footnotes)
- Limitation (authors): The SMA SMAPOL monitoring period (June 2022 to February 2025) is too short to test the long-term total flux stability of 3C 286.  
  > "We do not use the SMA data to test the flux stability of 3C 286, as its monitoring period from June 2022 to February 2025 is too short for a reliable investigation." (Footnotes)
- Limitation (authors): Uranus brightening (up to 4%) cannot fully account for the >10% total flux decreases observed in ALMA Bands 6 and 7.  
  > "Nevertheless, the change in the brightness of Uranus alone cannot fully account for the decreasing trend observed in the ALMA measurements, as the magnitudes of the decrease in Band 6 and 7 exceed 10%." (Results / Total flux)
- Limitation (authors): IRAM EVPA uncertainties at 229 GHz (average 5.1 deg) appear to be underestimated compared to the standard deviation of the measurements (9.6 deg).  
  > "Moreover, the average of the EVPA uncertainties for IRAM at 229 GHz is 5.1$^\circ$, while the standard deviation of those measurements is 9.6$^\circ$. This discrepancy suggests that the EVPA uncertainties for IRAM may have been underestimated." (Results / Polarization)
- Limitation (authors):  IRAM flux measurements at 86 GHz could be underestimated by ~2% because the conversion factor was derived from 2006-2014 data while the ESA4 model best matches Uranus around 2017.  
  > "Given that the ESA4 model appears to agree best with the data around 2017 (Figure 5), the IRAM flux measurements could have been underestimated by $\sim2\%$ at 86 GHz, although their stability remains intact." (Footnotes)
- Limitation (authors): Standard theoretical depolarization models assume optically thin emission and are not directly applicable to 3C 286 because its emitting regions are partially optically thick and exhibit core-shift.  
  > "However, those models are primarily suited for optically thin cases, whereas the emitting regions in 3C 286 are partially optically thick, exhibiting the core-shift effect." (Discussion / Depolarization mechanism)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The pairwise rotation measure (RM) values in Table 4 and the power-law fit |RM| proportional to nu^a are not derived from independent frequency pairs, but are sampled from a single smooth power-law fit to EVPA vs lambda^2; therefore, the reported uncertainties and power-law slope a = 2.05 +/- 0.06 are mathematically coupled to the assumed functional form log(chi) = k log(lambda^2) + C.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 4, the rest-frame RM for 103-233 GHz is printed as (8.6 +/- 1.7) x 10^4 rad m^-2, which is a factor of 10 higher than (1+z)^2 times the observing-frame RM of (2.5 +/- 0.4) x 10^3 rad m^-2 (~8.5 x 10^3 rad m^-2), indicating a likely typographical error in the exponent.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-dish and connected-element interferometers (VLA, IRAM 30-m, SMA, ALMA 7-m) integrate emission over the entire CSS source (core plus steep-spectrum jet components C1, C2, and outer lobes), so attributing the integrated EVPA rotation and depolarization purely to core-shift in an optically thick conical core ignores frequency-dependent spectral blending between the core and steeper-spectrum jet knots.
