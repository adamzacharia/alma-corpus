## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare the [C II] comparison cube**: Reduce with the FIFI-LS pipeline, correct atmospheric transmission with ATRAN, and project reduced data into a spectral cube.  
  > "The data were reduced using the FIFI-LS pipeline (cite: 2020ASPC..Vacca)." (SOFIA Observations); "the data were corrected for atmospheric transmission using the ATRAN model (cite: 1992nstc.rept.....L)" (SOFIA Observations); "The reduced data were projected into spectral cubes with a fine grid of 3$''$ sampling" (SOFIA Observations)
   - software: FIFI-LS pipeline, ATRAN
   - parameter: 3″ sampling; Gaussian spectral dispersion 1/4 spectral resolution; Gaussian spatial dispersion 1/2 spatial resolution  
  > "with a fine grid of 3$''$ sampling using a Gaussian spectral kernel with a dispersion equal to 1/4 of the spectral resolution" (SOFIA Observations); "a Gaussian spatial kernel with a dispersion equal to 1/2 the spatial resolution." (SOFIA Observations)
   - parameter: Spatial resolution 15.6 arcseconds; resolving power 1167; unresolved FWHM 257 km s^-1  
  > "The spectral resolving power of FIFI-LS is 1167, meaning that an unresolved line has a FWHM of 257 km s$^{-1}$." (SOFIA Observations); "The spatial resolution of the instrument is 15.6 arcseconds" (SOFIA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Projection parameters conserve instrumental spatial and spectral resolutions.  
  > "These parameters produced a data cube that conserves the instrumental spectral and spatial resolutions." (SOFIA Observations)
2. **Remove MIPS artifacts and nuclear PSF**: Correct jail bars, subtract latency ghosts, subtract fitted synthetic nuclear point-source realizations, and mosaic the corrected BCDs.  
  > "To remove these artefacts, we coadded every fourth column in each BCD and fitted a third-degree Chebyshev polynomial." (Spitzer Observations); "To remove these spurious sources we subtracted the previous four BCDs from each BCD, scaled as in (cite: Fadda2006)." (Spitzer Observations); "After subtracting these PSRs from all the BCDs, we made a new mosaic using the MOPEX software (cite: 2005PASP..117.1113M)." (Spitzer Observations)
   - software: STinyTim, MOPEX
   - parameter: PSF oversampling factor ten; CSM_SKY header positions for focal-plane displacement  
  > "For each BCD, we generated a PSF at the position of the nucleus in the BCD with an oversampling factor of ten." (Spitzer Observations); "we used the predicted positions on the sky recorded in the header as CSM_SKY as inputs for STinyTim for the displacement of the focal plane along the scan direction." (Spitzer Observations)
   - parameter: 100 point source realizations at different subpixel positions  
  > "We computed a stack of 100 point source realizations (PSR) by integrating the synthetic PSF in MIPS pixels centered at 100 different positions in the brightest central pixel of the galactic nucleus." (Spitzer Observations)
   - fitting: Maximize BCD–PSR cross-correlation; determine normalization by minimizing squared differences.  
  > "To find the best approximation, we maximized the cross-correlation between each BCD and the PSRs." (Spitzer Observations); "Finally, we minimized the sum of the squares of the differences between each BCD and the optimal PSR to compute the normalization factor." (Spitzer Observations)
   - validation: Inspect residual Airy rings; empirical PSFs from other observations did not improve subtraction.  
  > "there are parts of the first and second Airy rings which are slightly over-subtracted." (Spitzer Observations); "Experiments with empirical PSFs from other MIPS observations did not yield a better subtraction." (Spitzer Observations)
3. **Measure matched-resolution aperture photometry**: Define 12 apertures, degrade images and the CO cube to [C II] resolution, and measure multiband flux densities.  
  > "we defined 12 different apertures with a diameter of 15.6 arcseconds, equivalent to the spatial resolution of our \[C II\] map." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "To compare the emission at these bands with the \[C II\] emission, we degraded the spatial resolution of each image to that of the \[C II\] spectral cube." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "The spatial resolution of the CO spectral cube has been degraded to the resolution of the \[C II\] cube to have meaningful comparisons." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
   - parameter: Circular 15.6″ diameter apertures; centers in Table 2  
  > "Each region consists of a circular aperture with a 15.6$''$ diameter and the reported center." (Table 2)
   - parameter: Centers on far-IR peaks or 20 cm continuum regions  
  > "Each aperture covers a different part of the bar and is centered either on a peak of the far-IR emission, or on a region with 20 cm radio continuum emission." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
   - parameter: Two GALEX, five SDSS, four IRAC bands; MIPS 24 μm; PACS 70, 100, 160 μm; SPIRE 250 μm  
  > "we measured the flux densities in the two GALEX bands, five SDSS bands, four IRAC bands, the MIPS 24 $\mu$m image, the PACS 70, 100, and 160 $\mu$m images and the SPIRE 250 $\mu$m image." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit SEDs and derive infrared and stellar properties**: Fit aperture SEDs with Magphys; integrate the fitted infrared SED; subtract synthetic stellar emission from IRAC 8 μm to estimate PAH luminosity.  
  > "Each SED has been fitted with the Magphys code (cite: daCunha2008)." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "For each aperture, we computed the FIR emission by integrating the spectral energy distribution of the best fitting MagPhys model" (Infrared Diagnostics); "The 7.7 $\mu$m PAH luminosity is estimated by subtracting the stellar emission from the IRAC 8 $\mu$m image." (Infrared Diagnostics)
   - software: Magphys
   - parameter: FIR integration from 8 to 1000 μm  
  > "The FIR luminosity was computed by integrating the best-fitting model between 8 and 1000 $\mu$m." (Table 2)
   - parameter: Stellar subtraction from synthetic photometry of unattenuated best-fitting MagPhys flux  
  > "The stellar emission estimate was computed through synthetic photometry of the unattenuated flux from the best-fitting MagPhys model." (Infrared Diagnostics)
   - parameter: ISM temperature, stellar mass, and sSFR uncertainties are 95% confidence intervals  
  > "Temperature of the ISM, stellar mass, and specific star formation rate are MagPhys outputs with 95% confidence interval uncertainties." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Metallicity is the best-fitting MagPhys model value  
  > "The metallicity Z is the value of the MagPhys model that best fits the photometric data." (Table 2)
   - fitting: Magphys best-fitting attenuated and unattenuated SEDs; sampler, priors, and likelihood not stated.  
  > "Each SED has been fitted with the Magphys code (cite: daCunha2008)." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "In the figure, the two lines represent the best fit with the attenuated and unattenuated distributions in red and blue respectively." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Measure aperture line luminosities**: Fit aperture [C II] and CO profiles with pseudo-Voigt functions and derive line luminosities.  
  > "the middle panel shows the \[C II\] line from the apertures fitted with one or two pseudo-Voigt functions (red lines)." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "The right panel, finally, shows the CO lines fitted again with a combination of pseudo-Voigt functions." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "\[C II\] and CO luminosities were computed by fitting the line inside the aperture" (Table 2)
   - parameter: [C II]: one or two pseudo-Voigt functions; CO: combination of pseudo-Voigt functions  
  > "the middle panel shows the \[C II\] line from the apertures fitted with one or two pseudo-Voigt functions (red lines)." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "The right panel, finally, shows the CO lines fitted again with a combination of pseudo-Voigt functions." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
   - fitting: Pseudo-Voigt profile fitting; optimizer, likelihood, bounds, and component-selection criterion not stated.  
  > "The right panel, finally, shows the CO lines fitted again with a combination of pseudo-Voigt functions." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
   - validation: Compare profiles with rescaled Hα profiles.  
  > "The profile of the H$\alpha$ line, rescaled to the \[C II\] and CO lines, is shown with a green shade." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission)
6. **Diagnose gas-heating mechanisms**: Compare [C II], CO, FIR, and PAH diagnostics with PDR models and galaxy samples.  
  > "shows the relationship between the \[C II\] and CO emission in the different apertures, normalized to the far-IR emission." (Relative Strength of CO and \[C II\] Emissions); "We show, for reference, a grid of PDR models from (cite: Kaufman1999)." (Relative Strength of CO and \[C II\] Emissions); "By comparing the values in different regions of normal star-forming galaxies (cite: Croxall2012; Bigiel2020), we see that region I and K have anomalously high ratios." (Infrared Diagnostics)
   - parameter: [C II]/FIR comparison with GOALS  
  > "we compare the values of the \[C II\]/FIR ratios in the different apertures on NGC 7479 with values from the GOALS sample." (Infrared Diagnostics)
   - assumption: FIR and 7.7 μm PAH emission are star-formation diagnostics  
  > "The far-IR emission (between 8 and 1000 $\mu$m) has been shown to be a good estimator of star formation (cite: Kennicutt1998)." (Infrared Diagnostics); "Another quantity that correlates very well with the star formation rate is the emission at 7.7 $\mu$m from the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (cite: Peters2004)." (Infrared Diagnostics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check multiple infrared diagnostics; note possible dilution between the two [C II] components in E.  
  > "the corresponding region E (at the tip of the northern jet) does not seem to have anomalous ratios in these two diagnostics." (Infrared Diagnostics); "any excess in this ratio in one component may be diluted by normal PDR emission from the other." (Infrared Diagnostics)
7. **Classify the optical nucleus**: Use the SDSS nuclear spectrum, BPT diagrams, Seyfert subtype schema, and Balmer decrement.  
  > "the galaxy can be classified as a Seyfert according to the BPT diagrams by (cite: Kewley2006)." (The Counter-arm Structure); "the galaxy can be classified as Sy 1.8, according to the schema of (cite: Winkler1992)." (The Counter-arm Structure); "The SDSS spectrum shows a high extinction ($A_V = 8.4$ mag, from the Balmer ratio decrement)" (The Counter-arm Structure)
   - parameter: SDSS nuclear spectrum observed March 2012  
  > "A recent SDSS spectrum of the nucleus (observed in March 2012" (The Counter-arm Structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Measure X-ray emission and test a jet origin**: Reprocess Chandra data; adaptively smooth the image; extract and background-subtract spectra separately, combine them, and fit spectral models.  
  > "We reprocessed the data using the latest Chandra calibration (CALDB 4.9.3) with CIAO, version 4.12.1." (Chandra X-Ray Data); "Because of the low photon statistics, we extracted spectra from the two observations and studied the combined spectrum after a separate background subtraction." (Chandra X-Ray Data); "We used the specextract tool to extract X-ray spectra in the 0.3–8.0 keV range for the region centered on the nucleus and the regions on the counter-arms." (Chandra X-Ray Data)

## Qualitative findings

- Most [C II] emission is consistent with cooling of photoelectrically heated gas in PDRs.  
  > "As in most normal galaxies, the \[C II\] emission is generally consistent with emission from cooling gas excited by photoelectric heating in photo-dissociation regions." (Abstract)
- Both counter-arm ends have enhanced [C II]/CO; the southern end also has infrared-diagnostic excesses attributed to shocked molecular gas.  
  > "However, anomalously high \[C II\]/CO ratios are seen at the two ends of the counter-arms." (Abstract); "the southern end (region I) has an excess of \[C II\] emission unrelated to star formation." (Summary and Conclusions); "We attribute this excess to the cooling of molecular gas shocked by the jet." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Regions C and K are possible CO-dark molecular clouds; K is the best candidate.  
  > "Another location along the bar (region C) and one location external to the bar (region K) have very low CO/\[C II\] ratios." (Summary and Conclusions); "These could be locations of CO-dark molecular clouds." (Summary and Conclusions); "Region K appears to have a low metallicity, and is the best candidate for CO-dark molecular gas." (Summary and Conclusions)
- The CO components distinguish strong bar emission from weaker trailing emission.  
  > "Each velocity component consists of strong emission along the bar on one side with respect to the nucleus and of weak emission on the other side that trails the bar in the sense of the rotation of the galaxy." (Summary and Conclusions)
- X-ray emission follows the radio counter-arms.  
  > "Our reprocessing of Chandra archival data shows that the X-ray emission follows the same pattern as the counter-arms visible in the 20 cm radio continuum." (The Counter-arm Structure)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA covers the SOFIA region and resolves CO velocity components much better than the previous CO data.  
  > "cover the whole region observed with SOFIA" (ALMA CO Data); "The spectral resolution is 1.27 km/s, roughly ten times better than that of the data used by (cite: 1999ApJ...511..709L)." (ALMA CO Data)
- Why this method: Match spatial resolutions for meaningful comparisons; use resolved CO velocity differences to separate components.  
  > "The spatial resolution of the CO spectral cube has been degraded to the resolution of the \[C II\] cube to have meaningful comparisons." (Origin And Distribution of Gas Emission); "Because of the substantial difference in velocity of these two components with respect to the spectral resolution of the ALMA observations" (An Interpretation of the Molecular Gas Flow)
- Alternative: Other SPIRE images rejected because of insufficient spatial resolution and limited usefulness for total infrared emission.  
  > "We do not consider the other SPIRE images, since their spatial resolution is too low in comparison to our SOFIA data" (Herschel Observations); "the emission at those wavelengths is well beyond the peak of the infrared emission and not useful to constrain the total infrared emission." (Herschel Observations)
- Alternative: Empirical MIPS PSFs from other observations did not improve subtraction.  
  > "Experiments with empirical PSFs from other MIPS observations did not yield a better subtraction." (Spitzer Observations)
- Alternative: Non-nuclear X-ray spectra fitted with either apec or power law plus apec, with similar results.  
  > "We fitted the other two spectra either with apec thermal models or a combination of power-law and apec thermal models." (The Counter-arm Structure); "In both cases we also assumed a Galactic intrinsic absorption, obtaining similar results." (The Counter-arm Structure)
- Alternative: Trailing gas could arise partly during bar formation rather than exclusively from disrupted companion gas.  
  > "some of this trailing gas forms naturally in a bar forming process (that may have been triggered by the minor merger)" (An Interpretation of the Molecular Gas Flow)
- How they decided it was enough: Archived Herschel image quality was adequate; selected SOFIA cube-projection parameters preserve instrumental resolution.  
  > "We did not reprocess the images for our work since the quality of the archived products in the *Herschel* Science Archive is adequate for our analysis." (Herschel Observations); "These parameters produced a data cube that conserves the instrumental spectral and spatial resolutions." (SOFIA Observations)
