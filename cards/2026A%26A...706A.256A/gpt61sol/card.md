## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure both continuum components**: Measure integrated and peak fluxes with two-dimensional Gaussian fits in the image plane.  
  > "The integrated and peak flux were calculated using two-dimensional (2D) Gaussian fits in the image plane." (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
   - parameter: 233.0, 303.0, and 328.2 GHz.  
  > "The continuum emission is shown in Fig. [1](#fig:cont), for three different frequencies (233.0, 303.0, and 328.2 GHz)." (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fits; optimizer and fit constraints not stated.  
  > "The integrated and peak flux were calculated using two-dimensional (2D) Gaussian fits in the image plane." (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
   - validation: Source A S/N around 60 at band 6, 14 at 303.0 GHz, and 6 at 328.2 GHz.  
  > "Source A is clearly detected in ALMA band 6, with a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) of around 60, while the S/N decreases for shorter wavelengths (S/N = 14 and 6 for 303.0 and 328.2 GHz, respectively)." (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
2. **Derive disk masses**: M_disk = Sν d² / [κν Bν(T)].  
  > "M_\mathrm{disk} = \frac{S_{\nu}d^{2}}{\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}(T)}" (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
   - parameter: OH5 κν = 0.0092, 0.0141, and 0.0172 cm² g−1 at 233.0, 303.0, and 328.2 GHz.  
  > "| 233.0 | 1.287 | 0.0092 | 5.6 $\pm$ 0.2 | 0.5 $\pm$ 0.3 |" (Table 2); "| 303.0 | 0.989 | 0.0141 | 4.4 $\pm$ 0.2 | 0.5 $\pm$ 0.3 |
| 328.2 | 0.913 | 0.0172 | 5.2 $\pm$ 0.4 | 0.6 $\pm$ 0.3 |" (Table 2); "From (cite: Ossenkopf1994) using OH5." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Distance 139 pc (cite: Canovas2019).  
  > "IRS 44, also known as YLW 16 (cite: Young1986), is a Class I source located in the Ophiuchus molecular cloud, at a distance of 139 pc (cite: Canovas2019)." (Introduction)
   - assumption: Single dust temperature 15 K; gas-to-dust ratio 100; masses are gas + dust.  
  > "The adopted dust temperature (*T$_\mathrm{dust}$*) is 15 K (cite: Dunham2014b), and the derived masses (gas + dust, assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100)" (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare masses at different wavelengths; discrepancy suggests optical depth, so interpret masses as lower limits.  
  > "This discrepancy hints at optically thick emission, mainly at 303.0 and 328.2 GHz, but the continuum emission at 233.0 GHz might also suffer from optical depth effects (to a lesser degree)." (IRS 44: A proto-binary); "Therefore, *M$_\mathrm{disk}$* should be taken as a lower limit." (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
3. **Measure line emission and identify detections**: Integrate spectra in a circular aperture; classify detections and upper limits using 3σ.  
  > "Detections are defined when the integrated emission in a circular area with radius of 0.5$''$ is above a 3$\sigma$ value." (Molecular transitions); "Upper limits represent a 3$\sigma$ value." (Molecular transitions)
   - parameter: Aperture radius 0.5″.  
  > "Detections are defined when the integrated emission in a circular area with radius of 0.5$''$ is above a 3$\sigma$ value." (Molecular transitions)
   - parameter: Δv = 50 km s−1 for detections and 20 km s−1 for non-detections.  
  > "$\Delta$v is 50 km s$^{-1}$ and 20 km s$^{-1}$ for detections and non-detections, respectively." (Molecular transitions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Integrated-emission threshold above 3σ.  
  > "Detections are defined when the integrated emission in a circular area with radius of 0.5$''$ is above a 3$\sigma$ value." (Molecular transitions)
4. **Assess infall and streamer candidates**: Inspect CO 2–1 moment 8, 0, and 1 maps, channel maps, and PV diagrams; compare CO structures with SO and SO2 emission.  
  > "Figure [4](#fig:CO_moms) shows moment 8, 0, and 1 maps for the CO 2–1 transition in a zoomed-in-on area." (Streamer candidates); "Position-velocity (PV) diagrams of CO 2–1 are shown in Fig. [6](#fig:CO_PV), for different position angles (PAs)." (Streamer candidates); "The extents of the proposed *r$_\mathrm{CB}$* and *r$_\mathrm{CR}$* are shown in Fig. [8](#fig:SO_moms_comb), where SO and SO$_{2}$ emission is superimposed with the CO emission." (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks)
   - parameter: CO channel maps in ranges of 2 km s−1.  
  > "Figure [5](#fig:CO_chans) shows channel maps for the CO 2–1 emission, for ranges of 2 km s$^{-1}$." (Streamer candidates)
   - parameter: Archival CO cube for spatial scales 1.0″–5.0″; excluded from quantitative analysis.  
  > "The main purpose of retrieving this cube was to assess the CO intermediate spatial scales (between 1.0$''$ and 5.0$''$) that are filtered out in our data" (Archival ALMA data); "but this dataset was not used in the analysis." (Archival ALMA data)
   - assumption: Higher velocities closer to the protostar interpreted as infalling gas.  
  > "This velocity gradient is likely due to infalling gas, as higher velocities are seen closer to the protostar;" (Streamer candidates)
   - assumption: Prior centrifugal barrier r_CB = 0.08″; r_CB is half r_CR; infalling-rotation between the radii.  
  > "They proposed that the radius of the centrifugal barrier (*r$_\mathrm{CB}$*) is 0.08$''$," (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "The *r$_\mathrm{CB}$* is half of the centrifugal radius (*r$_\mathrm{CR}$*), and infalling-rotation motions are present between both radii." (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check inward increase in velocity and spatial agreement between the SE candidate and SO emission; detailed kinematic modeling deferred.  
  > "Maps with mean velocities between -7 and -11 km s$^{-1}$ show that the blueshifted emission extends to $\sim$1.0$''$ and approaches source B as the velocity increases, in agreement with infalling signatures." (Streamer candidates); "Additionally, it matches the direction of the SE streamer candidate, seen in CO in Fig. [4](#fig:CO_moms)." (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "A more detailed theoretical and kinematic analysis of the streamer candidates will be presented in a future work." (Streamer candidates)
5. **Constrain SO excitation and column density**: Compare a RADEX non-LTE model grid with observed intensity ratios between four clearly detected SO transitions.  
  > "we employed the non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) radiative transfer code RADEX and compared the models with the observed intensity ratios between the four SO transitions clearly detected" (SO)
   - software: RADEX, Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS)
   - parameter: H2 number densities 10^6, 10^7, and 10^8 cm−3.  
  > "The models are run for three different H$_{2}$ number densities: 10$^{6}$, 10$^{7}$, and 10$^{8}$ cm$^{-3}$." (Non-LTE radiative transfer models)
   - parameter: Energy levels, transition frequencies, and Einstein A coefficients from CDMS; collisional rates from (cite: Lique2006).  
  > "Energy levels, transitions frequencies, and Einstein A coefficients were taken from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS), while collisional rates were taken from (cite: Lique2006)." (SO)
   - parameter: Displayed temperature upper limit 220 K chosen because gas-phase SO2 formation is more efficient below 200 K.  
  > "temperatures above 220 K are not shown in Fig. [16](#fig:radex), but this upper limit was chosen given that gas-phase SO$_{2}$ formation is more efficient at temperatures below 200 K" (SO)
   - fitting: Grid comparison with observed SO intensity ratios; sampler, likelihood, and priors not stated.  
  > "Thus, the SO physical parameters were estimated by comparing a grid of models with observed line ratios." (Excitation temperatures and molecular column densities)
   - validation: Reject n_H = 10^6 cm−3 because no model range contains the three observed line ratios.  
  > "The *n$_\mathrm{H}$* = 10$^{6}$ cm$^{-3}$ scenario is discarded, given that there is no range of temperatures and densities that contain the three observed line ratios." (Non-LTE radiative transfer models)
6. **Derive CO, CS, and H2CO column densities**: Under LTE, derive upper-level column density from integrated flux and convert using level degeneracy, partition function, and excitation temperature.  
  > "Assuming LTE conditions, the following equation was employed:" (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO); "N = \frac{N_\mathrm{u}}{g_\mathrm{u}} Q(T_\mathrm{ex}) \ \mathrm{exp}\left( \frac{E_\mathrm{u}}{T_\mathrm{ex}} \right)" (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO); "N_\mathrm{u} = \frac{4 \pi S_{\nu} \Delta v}{A_{ij} \Omega h c}" (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO)
   - parameter: Circular region r = 0.5″; excitation temperatures 50–200 K.  
  > "over a circular region with *r* = 0.5$''$, and assuming a range of *T$_\mathrm{ex}$* between 50 and 200 K." (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO)
   - parameter: Use 13CO 3–2, CS 7–6, and H2CO 4_1,3–3_1,2.  
  > "| CO                 | 50 – 200          | (2.9 – 7.6) $\times$ 10$^{18}$  | Using $^{13}$CO 3–2 and $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C = 69 $^{(b)}$ |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "| CS                 | 50 – 200          | (3.1 – 4.9) $\times$ 10$^{13}$  | Using CS 7–6                                            |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "| H$_{2}$CO          | 50 – 200          | (0.6 – 2.6) $\times$ 10$^{13}$  | Using H$_{2}$CO 4$_{1,3}$–3$_{1,2}$                     |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks)
   - assumption: LTE; excitation temperatures 50–200 K.  
  > "Assuming LTE conditions, the following equation was employed:" (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO); "over a circular region with *r* = 0.5$''$, and assuming a range of *T$_\mathrm{ex}$* between 50 and 200 K." (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO)
   - assumption: 12C/13C = 69 (cite: Wilson1999).  
  > "| CO                 | 50 – 200          | (2.9 – 7.6) $\times$ 10$^{18}$  | Using $^{13}$CO 3–2 and $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C = 69 $^{(b)}$ |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "From (cite: Wilson1999)." (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 13CS and C34S non-detections used to argue CS 7–6 is not optically thick; CO spectra show absorption and optical-depth concerns.  
  > "$^{13}$CS and C$^{34}$S are not detected, ruling out the possibility that the CS 7–6 transition is optically thick." (Very low CS/SO); "CO isotopologs are usually optically thick and absorption features are seen in their spectra" (CO, CS, and H$_{2}$CO)
7. **Set molecular column-density upper limits**: Apply the integrated-flux column-density conversion to 3σ non-detections; select the highest-Aij transition when multiple transitions were targeted.  
  > "For non-detections, an integrated flux equivalent to 3$\sigma$ was employed" (OCS, H$_{2}$S, and H$_{2}$CS); "In cases in which more that one transition was targeted (such as OCS and H$_{2}$S), the line with the highest *A$_{ij}$* was selected to estimate the total molecular column density." (OCS, H$_{2}$S, and H$_{2}$CS)
   - parameter: OCS 28–27, H2S 3_3,0–3_2,1, H2CS 10_0,10–9_0,9; 50–200 K.

   - parameter: Non-detection flux integration width 20 km s−1.  
  > "$\Delta$v is 50 km s$^{-1}$ and 20 km s$^{-1}$ for detections and non-detections, respectively." (Molecular transitions)
   - assumption: Excitation temperatures 50–200 K.  
  > "| OCS $^{(d)}$       | 50 – 200          | $\leq$ 1.4 $\times$ 10$^{14}$   | Using OCS 28–27                                         |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "| H$_{2}$S $^{(d)}$  | 50 – 200          | $\leq$ 3.6 $\times$ 10$^{13}$   | Using H$_{2}$S 3$_{3,0}$–3$_{2,1}$                      |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "| H$_{2}$CS $^{(d)}$ | 50 – 200          | $\leq$ 3.2 $\times$ 10$^{13}$   | Using H$_{2}$CS 10$_{0,10}$–9$_{0,9}$                   |" (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Interpret the calculated column densities as upper limits.  
  > "calculated column densities should be interpreted as upper limits." (OCS, H$_{2}$S, and H$_{2}$CS)
8. **Interpret sulfur chemistry and accretion shocks**: Compare column densities with Perseus sources, assess CS/SO, and interpret spatial and spectral evidence using thermal desorption and gas-phase chemistry.  
  > "Figure [9](#fig:Nall) shows that the SO and SO$_{2}$ column densities toward IRS 44 are higher than the average values from the Perseus sources (represented by the horizontal gray bars)." (Bright SO and SO$_{2}$); "In IRS 44, the low CS/SO ratio ($\leq$ 0.008) is a consequence of the weak CS emission." (Very low CS/SO); "The high column densities of SO and SO$_{2}$ might therefore be a consequence of both processes: direct thermal desorption from dust grains and gas-phase formation due to the availability of O- and S-bearing species." (Very low CS/SO)
   - parameter: SO and SO2 spectra extend from −15 to 15 km s−1.  
  > "The broad spectra seen for SO and SO$_{2}$ (from -15 to 15 km s$^{-1}$) with multiple peaks at different velocities" (Bright SO and SO$_{2}$)
   - assumption: Most atomic carbon might be locked into CO, leaving an oxygen-rich environment.  
  > "Most of the atomic carbon might be locked into CO, so there is no available C to form CS and H$_{2}$CS, which leaves an oxygen-rich environment." (Very low CS/SO)
   - assumption: Adopt literature SO2 and CH3OH column densities for the chemical comparison.  
  > "values for SO$_{2}$ and CH$_{3}$OH were taken from the literature (cite: Artur2022; Artur2019a)." (Excitation temperatures and molecular column densities)

## Qualitative findings

- IRS 44 is resolved as a binary for the first time at sub-millimeter wavelengths.  
  > "The binarity of IRS 44 was proposed before from infrared observations; nevertheless, this is the first time that the binary components have been resolved in the sub-millimeter regime." (Summary)
- Non-coeval formation or rejuvenation of source B by late infall are favored explanations of the component differences.  
  > "a non-coeval scenario or a rejuvenation of source B due to late infall are the more plausible scenarios for the IRS 44 system." (IRS 44: A proto-binary)
- CO traces outflow, envelope infall, and two disk-scale streamer candidates.  
  > "CO 2–1 emission is tracing the outflow component at large scales, infalling envelope material at intermediate scales, and two infalling streamer candidates are identified at disk scales." (abstract)
- SO and SO2 emission is associated with the SE streamer direction and the proposed infalling-rotating region.  
  > "Additionally, it matches the direction of the SE streamer candidate, seen in CO in Fig. [4](#fig:CO_moms)." (SO and SO$_{2}$ as tracers of accretion shocks); "The peak of the SO and SO$_{2}$ emission is seen toward the edges of the *r$_\mathrm{CB}$*, and most of the emission arises from the region where rotating-infalling motions were proposed."
