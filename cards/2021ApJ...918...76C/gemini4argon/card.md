## Analysis recipe

1. **Convolve radio/mm/submm maps to a common beam, stack them to define source apertures, and extract multiwavelength continuum and Pa-alpha photometry**: Convolve all VLA and ALMA maps to a 0.3'' circular beam, stack the 8 convolved maps, define source footprints using the 20-sigma (73 uJy/bm) contour of the stacked map with manual masking in crowded regions, sum intensity divided by beam area, subtract median masked background; for HST/NICMOS Pa-alpha, shift by 1.33'', scale F187N by 3% and subtract F190N, and extract using the 33 GHz half-light radius contour (~2-sigma) with local background annuli.  
  > "We overlaid contours of the stacked image and selected the contour of 20$\times$ the rms (73 $\mu$Jy bm$^{-1}$) of the stacked map." (Source Identification and Photometry for Sources Extracted from the Convolved Maps); "To briefly summarize, we sum the intensity across the pixels associated with the source and divide it by the beam area." (Source Identification and Photometry for Sources Extracted from the Convolved Maps); "To avoid unphysical extinction values, we extracted the flux density for a region that corresponds to the half light radius of each source in the 33 GHz map, which roughly corresponds to a 2$\sigma$ contour." (Photometry in Pa\alpha Map)
   - software: CASA, Photutils
   - parameter: Common synthesized beam = 0.300''  
  > "We convolved the maps to a common synthesized beam of 0.3$''$, which is roughly the synthesized beam at a robust of 0.5, excluding the two historical VLA programs." (Source Extraction and Photometry)
   - parameter: Stacked map contour level = 20 x rms (73 uJy bm^-1)  
  > "We overlaid contours of the stacked image and selected the contour of 20$\times$ the rms (73 $\mu$Jy bm$^{-1}$) of the stacked map." (Source Identification and Photometry for Sources Extracted from the Convolved Maps)
   - parameter: Pa-alpha background annulus = 1.5 to 2 times source size (2 to 3 times for Knot 4b)  
  > "Regardless of the method used, we place an annulus around the source with an inner and outer radii of 1.5 and 2 times the size of the source for the background estimate. ## Qualitative findings

- High angular resolution (~0.3'') resolves the five previously identified radio knots in He 2-10 into seven sources: Knots 1a, 1b, 2, 3, 4a, 4b, and 5.  
  > "With the higher resolution of these maps compared to previous studies, we extract individual clusters from aggregate complexes as identified at lower resolution. In total, we identify seven knots: Knots 1a, 1b, 2, 3, 4a, 4b, and 5." (Summary)
- Knots 1a, 1b, 2, and 5 are consistent with thermal+dust SEDs and retain their classification as UD HII regions, whereas Knots 4a and 4b have a non-negligible non-thermal component and are likely a mix of HII regions and SNRs (or contaminated by nearby evolved SSCs).  
  > "Knots 1a, 1b, 2, and 5 are consistent with being UD H$\,$ii regions, as initially concluded by (cite: Johnson:2003) and (cite: Cabanac:2005). The SEDs for Knots 4a and 4b appear to have a non-negligible non-thermal component." (Summary)
- The central region of He 2-10 (traced by Knots 4a, 4b, and optical SSCs) is in a more evolved state than the eastern and western edges (Knots 1a and 5), supporting the correlation between HCN/HCO+ and HCO+/CO ratios and cluster evolutionary state.  
  > "Generally, the central region of He 2-10, as traced by Knots 4a and 4b and the optical SSCs, appears to be more evolved than the eastern and western regions." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution (~0.3'') observations from 1.87 um to 6 cm (VLA, ALMA, HST/NICMOS) are required to disentangle individual natal SSCs from aggregate complexes and separate non-thermal, free-free, and dust emission.  
  > "Here, we discuss radio, submillimeter, and infrared observations of He 2-10 from 1.87 $\mu$m to 6 cm in high angular resolution ($\sim$0.3$''$), which allows us to disentangle individual clusters from aggregate complexes as identified at lower resolution." (Abstract); "The ALMA data will probe the dust emission associated with the sources, so we include a dust component as well to disentangle the non-thermal, thermal, and dust emission, as only the thermal emission will provide an estimate of the rate of ionizing photons" (Spectral Energy Distributions from 0.9 mm to 6 cm)
- Why this method: Extracting photometry from a single 20-sigma contour footprint on the stacked 0.3'' image proved least dependent on subjective user input compared to CASA imfit and Photutils automated deblending.  
  > "We compared the results of these routines, and we determined that the extraction based on a contour level was the least dependent on user input, once the initial level was specified. This method best mitigated the subjectivity in the uncertainties inherent to extracting extended structures." (Remarks on Photometry)
- Alternative: Extracting sources using CASA task imfit on manually drawn polygonal regions or using Photutils (detect_sources and deblend_sources) with 2D Gaussian fitting; rejected in favor of stacked 20-sigma contour extraction.  
  > "In addition to the contour method, we used the CASA task imfit to extract sources from manually drawn polygonal regions. We also utilized an automated source identification program in the Python package *Photoutils* v0.7.2 (cite: Bradley:2019) to identify sources." (Remarks on Photometry); "We compared the results of these routines, and we determined that the extraction based on a contour level was the least dependent on user input, once the initial level was specified." (Remarks on Photometry)
- Alternative: Using the 20-sigma stacked radio contour footprint for HST/NICMOS Pa-alpha photometry; rejected because it included an offset optically bright feature near Knot 4b that boosted flux by ~2.6x and yielded unphysical negative extinction.  
  > "Inclusion of this compact feature alone significantly boosts the flux density by a factor of $\sim$2.6 for Knot 4b, which has undesirable consequences in our calculation of the extinction in Section [\[sec:extinction\]](#sec:extinction): namely, it produces a negative extinction value for this line of sight." (Photometry in Pa\alpha Map); "To avoid unphysical extinction values, we extracted the flux density for a region that corresponds to the half light radius of each source in the 33 GHz map, which roughly corresponds to a 2$\sigma$ contour." (Photometry in Pa\alpha Map)
- Alternative: Fitting SED models with a non-thermal spectral index of alpha_nth = -0.5 for all knots; rejected in favor of alpha_nth = -0.7 (except for Knot 3) because alpha_nth = -0.7 gave slightly better reduced chi-squared values.  
  > "We compared the models with $\alpha_{nth}$ = –0.7, which is a typical synchrotron spectral index, and $\alpha_{nth}$ = –0.5 and found that the reduced chi-squared, $\chi_{\nu}^2$, of $\alpha_{nth}$ = –0.7 models are slightly better (i.e., closer to 1) than the models with –0.5" (Spectral Energy Distributions from 0.9 mm to 6 cm)

- Limitation (authors): The new 33 GHz VLA observations suffer from significant decorrelation due to weather and low declination (-26 deg), so the 33 GHz flux densities must be treated as lower limits and excluded from SED fitting.
