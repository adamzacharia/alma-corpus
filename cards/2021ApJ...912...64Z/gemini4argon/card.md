## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce VLT/NACO NIR data and subtract stellar PSF**: Background subtraction (one frame from median of others), flat-fielding, recentering, median combination; 2D elliptical Gaussian fit to center, azimuthal average over angles devoid of emission (PA 230 to 350 deg) expanded to 2D PSF and subtracted  
  > "the exposures were background subtracted (one from the median of the others), flat-fielded, and then recentered to have the target in the center of the detector. Each final image is the median of all the exposures combined together." (Observations and data reduction); "A radial PSF profile is then constructed by performing an azimuthal average over angles devoid of extended emission (between PAs 230$^\circ$ and 350$^\circ$ eastwards from the North direction)." (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Azimuthal average angles: PA 230 deg to 350 deg  
  > "performing an azimuthal average over angles devoid of extended emission (between PAs 230$^\circ$ and 350$^\circ$ eastwards from the North direction)." (Observations and data reduction)
   - parameter: Astrometric center: RA=17:11:03.9234, Dec=-27:22:55.4725  
  > "For the astrometric calibration, we adopted the ALMA coordinates for the center of the star, RA=17:11:03.9234 and Dec=-27:22:55.4725, as measured in (cite: Alves2020)." (Observations and data reduction)
   - fitting: 2D elliptical Gaussian fit to the central region to determine the stellar centroid  
  > "First, a 2D elliptical Gaussian is fit to the central region to determine the stellar centroid." (Observations and data reduction)
   - validation: Compared against subtracting a 2D Gaussian or scaled field star PSF; removes Airy rings in L' band  
  > "This procedure produced qualitatively better results than subtracting an 2D Gaussian or a scaled version of the PSF given by another star in the field. In $L^\prime$ band, the PSF subtraction allows to remove the Airy rings." (Observations and data reduction)
2. **Assess astrometry, distance, and physical association of sources in field**: Query Gaia EDR3 astrometric fidelity classifier and compare relative astrometry across two NACO epochs (~4 years apart)  
  > "We checked for the fidelity of the EDR3 astrometric solutions for BHB2007-1 (sources ID 4108624199978985984) and BHB2007-2 (source ID 4108624195634062464) by querying the neural network classifier recently made available by (cite: 2021arXiv210111641R)." (Astrometry and photometry); "To assess whether VLT 171104.07-272258.8 (for which no Gaia parallax is available) is physically bound to the system, we exploited the NACO images from two different epochs ($\sim$4 years apart)." (Astrometry and photometry)
   - assumption: Distance of 166 pc adopted for [BHB2007]-1 from [BHB2007]-2 Gaia EDR3 parallax  
  > "Therefore, we are inclined to consider the Gaia EDR3 parallax erroneous and assume a distance of 166 pc, like [BHB2007]-2." (Astrometry and photometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Systemic velocity from CO millimeter lines (3.6 km/s) matches Barnard 59 region; disk size at 232 pc would be unusually large  
  > "Furthermore, the systemic velocity inferred for [BHB2007]-1 by (cite: Alves2020) from CO millimeter lines (3.6 km s$^{-1}$) matches the systemic velocity of the other members of the Barnard 59 region." (Astrometry and photometry)
3. **Derive stellar properties (visual extinction, luminosity, mass, and age)**: Fit B-to-W4 photometry with Phoenix stellar photosphere models to obtain A_V and L_*, then compare with pre-main-sequence evolutionary tracks  
  > "We adopted a Phoenix model of the stellar photosphere (cite: Hauschildt1999) with the effective temperature constrained by (cite: Covey2010) ($T_{\rm eff}=4060$ K and $T_{\rm eff}=3346$ K, for -1 and -2, respectively, with typical uncertainties of $\pm$300 K) and visual extinction $A_{\rm V}$ calculated from the observed colors between $R$, $I$, and $J$ bands, and stellar luminosity $L_*$ from the integrated photospheric model." (Stellar properties); "Finally, we constrained stellar mass $M_*$ and age $t$ of the two sources through multiple sets of pre-main-sequence tracks (cite: Baraffe2015; Dotter2016; Bressan2012)." (Stellar properties)
   - assumption: T_eff = 4060 +/- 300 K for [BHB2007]-1 and T_eff = 3346 +/- 300 K for [BHB2007]-2A  
  > "with the effective temperature constrained by (cite: Covey2010) ($T_{\rm eff}=4060$ K and $T_{\rm eff}=3346$ K, for -1 and -2, respectively, with typical uncertainties of $\pm$300 K)" (Stellar properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure NIR disk brightness contrast and vertical scattering surface height profile**: Compute disk-to-stellar light contrast phi = F_disk * 4*pi*r^2 / F_*; trace disk surface by fitting a Gaussian as a function of height along disk PA, deproject by sin(i), and fit power-law h(R) ~ R^(1+alpha)  
  > "To obtain a meaningful measurement of the disk brightness, we computed the disk-to-stellar light contrast $\phi=F_{\rm disk}\times 4\pi r^2 / F_*$" (Disk); "We trace the disk surface by fitting a Gaussian as a function of height, for each pixel along the disk PA." (Disk); "The height profile is then deprojected by the disk inclination by dividing by $\sin(i)$, where $i=75^\circ$ as reported in (cite: Alves2020)." (Disk)
   - assumption: Disk midplane PA = 12.5 deg and inclination i = 75 deg from ALMA continuum  
  > "Here, we assume that the midplane is given by the disk PA of 12.5$^\circ$, as determined from the ALMA continuum by (cite: Alves2020)." (Disk); "The height profile is then deprojected by the disk inclination by dividing by $\sin(i)$, where $i=75^\circ$ as reported in (cite: Alves2020)." (Disk)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit as a function of height for each pixel along disk PA; power-law fit h(R) ~ R^(1+alpha)  
  > "We trace the disk surface by fitting a Gaussian as a function of height, for each pixel along the disk PA." (Disk); "The height profiles can be well approximated by a power-law in radius $h(R) \propto R^{1+\alpha}$." (Disk)
   - validation: Southern filament seen in H-band confirmed by Gaia DR3 detection (G=20.77) and ALMA 12CO overlap  
  > "The confirmation that the filament seen in the NIR is not a NACO artifact came with the Gaia DR3, where signal is detected at the same position with a magnitude of $G$=20.77." (Disk)
5. **Characterize position and mass of embedded substellar companion candidate**: Measure position and L' flux ratio on PSF-subtracted image and compare with COND, DUSTY, and BEX evolutionary models

  > "For that assumption we used the range of values of 0.9–5.7 Myr for the age of the system as presented in Sec. 3.3, a value of 7.93 (derived from the WISE W1 data) for the $L^\prime$ magnitude of the star, and 166 pc as the distance of the system" (Embedded sub-stellar object)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that non-detections in shorter wavelength bands (J, H, K_S) are consistent with the upper mass limit; spatial coincidence with VLA 14-mm emission

## Qualitative findings

- [BHB2007]-2 is resolved as a physically bound binary system ([BHB2007]-2A and B) for the first time, while nearby source VLT 171104.07-272258.8 is not co-moving.  
  > "On the other hand, the known object [BHB2007]-2 is resolved here as a binary system, to our knowledge, for the first time." (Overview of the NACO images); "Given the differences in RA and Dec we can say that VLT 171104.07-272258.8 is not co-moving with either of the two other systems visible in the detector." (Astrometry and photometry)
- A faint southern filament is detected in the NACO H-band image that overlaps with the southern 12CO filament detected by ALMA.  
  > "In the South of the object $\sim 4$$''$ below the NACO brightness peak a faint filament is seen in the $H$ band filter with higher SNR with respect to the other filters, which can be compatible to the signal seen in the $^{12}$CO by ALMA" (Disk)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: VLT/NACO high-contrast NIR imaging in J, H, K_S, and L' probes scattered light from micron-sized dust and thermal emission from embedded companions inside the millimeter gap.  
  > "We present VLT/NACO high-contrast images in $J$, $H$, $K_S$, and $L^{\prime}$ band of the young star [BHB2007]-1 probing the inclined disk in scattered light and revealing the probable presence of a companion."
