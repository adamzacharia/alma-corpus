## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct continuum maps**: Image with natural weighting, clean to 2 × RMS and exclude frequencies within 2 × line FWHM.  
  > "using natural weighting to optimize the continuum sensitivity, adopting a cleaning threshold of $2\times$ the root-mean-square (RMS) noise." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "within $2\times$ the full-width-half-maximum (FWHM) of the \[C ii\] 158 $\mu{\rm m}$ (cite: hygate2023), \[O iii\] 88 $\mu{\rm m}$ (cite: algera2024) and CO(7-6) (A. Hygate et al. in prep) lines were excluded." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - parameter: Cleaning threshold: 2 × RMS  
  > "adopting a cleaning threshold of $2\times$ the root-mean-square (RMS) noise." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: No other emission lines detected.  
  > "No other emission lines are detected in the data." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
2. **Measure integrated continuum emission**: Re-image over a range of uv-tapers; fit 2D Gaussians; adopt the peak flux at the lowest taper where it converges.  
  > "measure the peak and integrated flux densities in each of the tapered images through 2D Gaussian fitting with (cite: mohanrafferty2015)." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "We choose to adopt the peak flux density at the lowest tapering (that is, in the highest resolution image) where the flux measurement converges to a constant value" (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - parameter: Band 3: naturally weighted map  
  > "In Band 3, the measured peak (and integrated) flux density does not change appreciably depending on the applied $uv$-taper, and as such we adopt the peak flux density measured in the naturally-weighted map." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - parameter: Bands 4, 5 and 8: uv-taper 1.4″–1.6″; Band 4 taper only along minor beam axis  
  > "In Bands 4, 5 and 8, we adopt a $uv$-taper ranging from $1.4\ensuremath{''} - 1.6\ensuremath{''}$." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "the final $uv$-tapering we adopt is only applied along the minor beam axis." (Grain Growth in the ISM)
   - parameter: Band 6: uv-taper 2.5″  
  > "For Band 6, where REBELS-25 is detected at high S/N and was previously found to be resolved at the native $\sim1.0\ensuremath{''}$ resolution (cite: inami2022), we adopt a coarser tapering of $2.5\ensuremath{''}$." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - parameter: Band 9: upper limit 3 × RMS in the 0.8″ tapered map  
  > "For Band 9, where REBELS-25 is not detected, we conservatively quote the upper limit as $3$ times the RMS noise in the $0.8\ensuremath{''}$ tapered map." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - assumption: At sufficiently coarse resolution, unresolved-source peak flux represents total flux.  
  > "once tapered to a sufficiently coarse resolution, the galaxy will become unresolved and its total flux density is well-represented by the peak flux density." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fits to peak and integrated flux densities; peak uncertainty approximately equals tapered-map RMS.  
  > "measure the peak and integrated flux densities in each of the tapered images through 2D Gaussian fitting with (cite: mohanrafferty2015)." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "approximately equals the RMS in the corresponding tapered map." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
   - validation: Peak and integrated fluxes agree; common taper ≳1.5″ and aperture photometry give consistent results.  
  > "We verify that, at sufficiently coarse $uv$-tapers, the peak and integrated flux densities agree within the uncertainties." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "We note that our results do not change when we adopt a common UV-taper $\gtrsim1.5\ensuremath{''}$ across all bands." ## Qualitative findings

- REBELS-25 is detected in Bands 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8, with only a Band 9 upper limit.  
  > "The dust continuum is clearly detected in Bands 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8, while it remains undetected in Band 9 at the $3\sigma$ level." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
- Moderately optically thick dust does not appreciably alter the inferred dust parameters.  
  > "we find that moderately optically thick dust does not appreciably alter the inferred dust parameters for REBELS-25." (A fixed transition wavelength $\lambda_\mathrm{thick}$)
- The steep emissivity index is robust to optical-depth assumptions.  
  > "the steep dust emissivity index we infer for REBELS-25 is robust against assumptions regarding the optical depth." (Dust Emissivity Indices across Cosmic Time)
- High dust mass and steep β_IR may indicate grain growth through accretion and coagulation.  
  > "We argue that both the high dust mass and steep $\beta_\mathrm{IR}$ inferred for REBELS-25 may be indicative of grain growth through metal accretion and coagulation taking place in its ISM" (Conclusions)
- A high-S/N [4,6,8,9] setup balances peak and Rayleigh-Jeans coverage for z ∼ 7 galaxies.  
  > "With a four-band setup, including either Bands 3 or 4, in addition to Bands 6, 8 and 9, we can accurately recover the dust mass, temperature and emissivity index provided the S/N is sufficiently high." (A Comparison of Multi-band ALMA setups); "We here recommend a setup of Bands \[4, 6, 8, 9\] given the balance between the decreasing flux towards longer wavelengths and the sensitivity of the ALMA bands." (A Comparison of Multi-band ALMA setups)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Six-band coverage samples the SED peak and Rayleigh-Jeans tail to constrain mass, temperature and emissivity together.  
  > "spanning rest-frame $50-350\,\mu$m – enabling simultaneous constraints on its dust mass ($M_\mathrm{dust}$), temperature ($T_\mathrm{dust}$) and emissivity index ($\beta_\mathrm{IR}$) via modified blackbody fitting." (Abstract); "two ingredients are crucial for an accurate measurement of the emissivity index: 1) a measurement sampling the Rayleigh-Jeans tail of the dust emission, and 2) a measurement sampling the peak of the dust SED." (The Need for Probing the Rayleigh-Jeans Tail)
- Why this method: Natural weighting maximizes sensitivity; tapered peak photometry avoids resolution-dependent flux loss and noise-sensitive integrated measurements; broad β_IR priors preserve discovery space.  
  > "using natural weighting to optimize the continuum sensitivity" (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "peak flux densities tend to be more robust against the inclusion of noise peaks when the signal-to-noise ratio of the data is modest" (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "it will have the side effect of limiting one’s discovery space to dust emissivity indices more in line with the canonical $\beta_\mathrm{IR} \sim 1.5 - 2$." (Constraining Dust SEDs with Mock ALMA Observations)
- Alternative: Fixed λ_thick = 65 μm and freely varying λ_thick compared with optically thin dust.  
  > "we re-fit the dust SED of REBELS-25 with either a fixed transition wavelength $\lambda_\mathrm{thick}$ where $\tau_\nu = 1$" (Optically Thick Dust); "or by freely varying this wavelength during the fitting process" (Optically Thick Dust)
- Alternative: Optically thin model retained as fiducial because opacity remains uncertain and for consistency with high-redshift literature.  
  > "In light of these prevailing unknowns, as well as for consistency with the general high-redshift literature, we proceed with our optically thin model as the fiducial one." (Optically Thickness of REBELS-25)
- Alternative: Top-heavy IMF modestly lowers the required supernova yield.  
  > "this yield only modestly reduces the need for efficient dust production in SNe compared to a fiducial Chabrier IMF." (Supernova Dust Production)
- Alternative: A common taper and aperture photometry give consistent results.  
  > "We note that our results do not change when we adopt a common UV-taper $\gtrsim1.5\ensuremath{''}$ across all bands." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "Furthermore, we find consistent results when performing aperture photometry on either the naturally-weighted or tapered images." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements)
- Alternative: Band 3 offers marginally better leverage at fixed S/N, but Band 4 is preferred for observing efficiency.  
  > "the longer lever arm provided by Band 3 results in marginally better constraints when employing a setup consisting of Bands \[3, 6, 8\] compared to \[4, 6, 8\], assuming a fixed S/N." (A Comparison of Multi-band ALMA setups); "detecting dust continuum emission is much easier in Band 4, and as such it is likely to be the more efficient choice" (A Comparison of Multi-band ALMA setups)
- Alternative: Informative β_IR priors cannot substitute for high-S/N multi-band observations.  
  > "a more informative prior should not be regarded as an adequate substitute for high-S/N, multi-band observations." (Constraining Dust SEDs with Mock ALMA Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: Select the first taper with converged peak flux; check peak/integrated agreement and alternative photometry; mock S/N = 8 adopted because S/N = 4 is insufficient.  
  > "We choose to adopt the peak flux density at the lowest tapering (that is, in the highest resolution image) where the flux measurement converges to a constant value" (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "We verify that, at sufficiently coarse $uv$-tapers, the peak and integrated flux densities agree within the uncertainties." (Target, Observations and Flux Density Measurements); "a lower S/N = 4 per band is generally not sufficient in order to obtain tight constraints on the dust parameters" (Constraining Dust SEDs with Mock ALMA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Band 9 provides only an upper limit, leaving the free-opacity fit unconstrained at short wavelengths.  
  > "the fit is unconstrained at the shortest wavelengths where we rely on just an upper limit in ALMA Band 9." (A varying transition wavelength $\lambda_\mathrm{thick}$)
- Limitation (authors): The size-based optical-depth estimate assumes spherical, homogeneous dust.  
  > "this estimate relies on the assumption of a spherical and homogeneous dust distribution." (A varying transition wavelength $\lambda_\mathrm{thick}$)
- Limitation (authors): Dense optically thick clumps cannot be definitively excluded.  
  > "However, we can not definitively rule out the presence of dense, optically thick clumps within REBELS-25." (Optically Thickness of REBELS-25)
- Limitation (authors): MBB temperature need not equal a physical dust temperature in the galaxy.  
  > "dust temperatures obtained from MBB-fitting do not necessarily constitute an accurate representation of any physical dust temperature within the galaxy" (Methods)
- Limitation (authors): True dust opacity depends on unknown high-redshift dust composition.  
  > "the true dust opacity depends on its chemical composition – which is inherently unknown at high redshift" (Methods)
- Limitation (authors): Grain coagulation could increase emissivity and cause an overestimated dust mass.  
  > "such coagulated grains will thus be more luminous than standard dust grains given a fixed total dust mass, and may hence cause the observationally inferred dust mass to be overestimated." (The Dust Mass and Emissivity Index of REBELS-25)
- Limitation (authors): The link between emissivity index and grain properties remains uncertain.  
  > "the relation between the dust emissivity index and physical properties of dust grains remains nebulous." (The Dust Mass and Emissivity Index of REBELS-25)
- Limitation (authors): Cross-study comparisons are affected by fitting differences; high-redshift samples are small and biased to bright objects.  
  > "comparing fitted dust emissivity indices among different studies is complicated by the variety of fitting techniques utilized in the literature." (Dust Emissivity Indices across Cosmic Time); "studies of dust properties at $z\gtrsim6$ remain limited to small numbers of the brightest objects." (Dust Emissivity Indices across Cosmic Time)
- Limitation (authors): Equal-S/N mocks are a general case; forecasting a particular target requires its existing S/N and approximate temperature.  
  > "the simulations presented below show only a general case." (Constraining Dust SEDs with Mock ALMA Observations); "Accurately forecasting the expected constraints on $\beta_\mathrm{IR}$ for any particular galaxy requires considering the S/N of previously taken observations, as well as obtaining a rough estimate of its dust temperature" (Constraining Dust SEDs with Mock ALMA Observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Five detections and one upper limit constrain an effective single-temperature SED; they do not uniquely establish grain composition or a grain-growth mechanism.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The optical-depth argument depends on unresolved geometry and the assumption that gas and dust are well-mixed.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Metallicity, density and co-spatiality support partly relies on studies still in preparation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Absolute flux-scale errors and cross-band covariance need explicit treatment before interpreting the precision of β_IR.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The mock tests generate and fit optically thin MBBs, so they do not test recovery under more complex dust-temperature or opacity structures.
