## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate stellar properties**: Construct SEDs; scale PHOENIX photospheres to Gaia distance and dereddened R photometry; infer mass and age using pre-main-sequence tracks.

(Stellar properties); "through a set of pre-main-sequence tracks (cite: Siess2000; Bressan2012; Baraffe2015; Choi2016)" (Stellar properties)
   - software: VizieR, PHOENIX
   - parameter: Luminosity errors propagated from distance, A_V (20%), and T_eff (±100 K)  
  > "Error bars on the stellar luminosity are propagated from $d$, $A_{\rm V}$ (20%), and $T_{\rm eff}$ ($\pm$100 K)." (Stellar properties)
   - parameter: Age intervals reflect different pre-main-sequence tracks  
  > "reflects the variety of values suggested by the different tracks." (Stellar properties)
   - assumption: PHOENIX stellar photosphere; nonmagnetic and magnetic evolutionary tracks

## Qualitative findings

- Most Taurus disks are faint in scattered light; small size and self-shadowing provide different explanations.  
  > "One of the key results of this work is that the prototypical disk in Taurus (1–3 Myr) appears faint in scattered light." (Discussion); "This is partly due to the small physical extent of some disks and partly to the low incidence of an inner cavity in these disks that would allow the efficient illumination of the outer disk regions." (Discussion)
- IP Tau shows spirals co-spatial with the ALMA disk inner edge.  
  > "This exercise clearly reveals the presence of spirals in the disk of IP Tau that are co-spatial with the inner edge of the ALMA disk." (The peculiar cases of IP Tau and DM Tau)
- DM Tau shows a southeastern asymmetry; an unresolved spiral is possible.  
  > "In DM Tau, a clear asymmetry is visible to the SE" (The peculiar cases of IP Tau and DM Tau); "In principle, this asymmetry may also point to an unresolved feature such as a spiral." (The peculiar cases of IP Tau and DM Tau)
- ALMA comparisons support extended self-shadowed disks whose full extent is not seen by SPHERE.  
  > "Generally speaking, the comparison reveals that only a fraction of the disk is probed by the SPHERE observations." (The cases of the self-shadowed disks); "This configuration is the most direct proof that these disks are self-shadowed, like formulated by (cite: Dullemond2004a) and shown observationally by (cite: Garufi2022b)." (The cases of the self-shadowed disks)
- Taurus appears to be at an early evolutionary stage, with individual advanced or exceptional systems.  
  > "several findings indicate an early evolutionary stage for the overall Taurus population, although some individual objects may represent advanced or exceptional evolutionary stages and some others may reveal intermediate phases of the disk lifetime." (Summary)

- Why this method: High-pass filtering highlights elusive substructures in the SPHERE images.  
  > "We show a high-pass-filtered version of the SPHERE image obtained by convolving the image with a Gaussian profile and then removing the convolved image from the original image."
