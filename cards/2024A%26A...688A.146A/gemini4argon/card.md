## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST/NIRSpec IFU cubes, register astrometry, and image ALMA data**: Reduce NIRSpec R100 and R2700 cubes with modified JWST pipeline v1.8.2 (drizzle, 0.05 arcsec spaxels), align astrometry to HST F125W and Gaia DR3, subtract median sky from R100, and image/regrid ALMA Band 6 continuum and [C II] cubes to 0.05 arcsec spaxels with moment maps.  
  > "The data reduction was performed with the JWST calibration pipeline (Bushouse et al. 2023) version 1.8.2, using the context file jwst_1068.pmap ." (2.2. NIRSpec IFU data reduction); "The [C ii] moment 0 map (intensity map) was generated by integrating the continuum subtracted data-cube in the spectral channels from -1000 km s −1 to 1200 km s −1 with respect to z = 6.900." (2.5. Ancillary data); "Finally, the continuum image and [C ii] datacube were resampled to a spaxel size of 0.05" to match the size of the NIRSpec spaxels using the CASA task imregrid ." (2.5. Ancillary data)
   - software: JWST calibration pipeline v1.8.2, CASA v6.2.1, imregrid
   - parameter: spaxel scale = 0.05 arcsec; [C II] integration range = -1000 to +1200 km s^-1 (w.r.t. z=6.900); S/N threshold for [C II] moment 1 and 2 = 2.3  
  > "we generated the final datacubes with the drizzle weighting method and a spaxel scale of 0.05 ′′ in the cube_build step." (2.2. NIRSpec IFU data reduction); "Before producing the moment 1 and moment 2 maps, the spaxels with low signal-to-noise ratio (S/N< 2.3) and those" (2.5. Ancillary data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked R100 and R2700 cube alignment via 2D Gaussian fit to lens galaxy peak (< 10 mas offset).  
  > "In fact, a 2D Gaussian fit to the brightest region of the lens galaxy in a common wavelength range led to differences smaller than a fraction of a spaxel (i.e. < 10 mas)." (2.3. Astrometric registration)
2. **Identify sources, extract spectra, fit emission lines, and model gravitational lensing**: Collapse R100 cube in continuum and line bands to identify sources, extract aperture spectra, fit Gaussian emission line profiles to R100 and R2700 spectra (including 2-Gaussian kinematic decomposition for E and L7), fit low-z galaxy photo-z with EAZY, and model lens magnification with PyAutoLens.  
  > "The emission lines in the R100 and R2700 integrated spectra were fit to single Gaussian profiles linking their relative fluxes and wavelengths according to their atomic parameters, and imposing the same line width for all the lines." (Appendix A.4: Line fitting); "Hence we performed a two-Gaussian model decomposition of these two kinematically distinct components by comparing the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) of fits performed with one and two components (Perna et al. 2022)." (3.3.3. Identification of L7 at z ∼ 6.9 via 2D kinematic decomposition); "To this aim we use the public lens modeling software PyAutoLens (Nightingale & Dye 2015, Nightingale et al. 2018, 2021), and follow the methodology described in Jones et al. (2024)." (Appendix B: Magnification map)
   - software: EAZY, PyAutoLens
   - parameter: 10% flux reduction applied to R100 fluxes; circular Gaussian PSF FWHM = 0.1 arcsec for lens modelling  
  > "As the R100 data are subject to larger uncertainties in the absolute calibration (Charles Profitt, priv. comm.), we applied this 10 percent correction to the R100 data." (Appendix A.5: Line fluxes); "The light profile for the lens galaxy in this image was fitted with a Sérsic profile, and we model the PSF as a circular Gaussian with FWHM= 0.1 ′′ ." (Appendix B: Magnification map)
   - assumption: Isothermal ellipsoid mass profile for lens with same centroid, axis ratio, and PA as light profile; mu = 2.2 for galaxy W from Marrone et al. (2018)  
  > "We assume that the mass profile can be represented by an isothermal ellipsoid with the same" (Appendix B); "Since source W is multiply imaged, it was not included in this simple analysis, and we adopt the magnification factor of Marrone et al. (2018) instead." (Appendix B: Magnification map)
   - fitting: Single and double Gaussian line fitting with BIC selection; Sersic profile fitting for lens  
  > "Hence we performed a two-Gaussian model decomposition of these two kinematically distinct components by comparing the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) of fits performed with one and two components (Perna et al. 2022)." (3.3.3. Identification of L7 at z ∼ 6.9 via 2D kinematic decomposition)
   - validation: Compared line fluxes between R100 and R2700 spectra across two orders of magnitude (Fig. A.3).  
  > "As the main lines used in this work appear in both the R100 and R2700 datacubes, this give us the opportunity to compare the derived fluxes with the two spectral configurations (see Fig. A.3)." (Appendix A.5: Line fluxes)
3. **Derive stellar masses, dust attenuation, metallicities, and star formation rates**: Fit R100 spectra with Bruzual & Charlot (2003) models and CIGALE to derive stellar masses; compute A_V from H-alpha/H-beta and Pa-alpha/H-alpha; infer gas-phase metallicities via MCMC using Curti et al. (2017, 2020b) calibrations; and compute H-alpha SFRs.  
  > "In short, the NIRSpec R100 integrated spectra were compared to stellar population models from the Bruzual & Charlot (2003) library, assuming a delayed exponential star formation history" (4.1. Stellar masses); "In Fig. 10, we present the metallicity map of SPT0311-058 according to the calibration of Curti et al. (2017, 2020b)." (4.4. Metallicity); "We estimate the nearly instantaneous (i.e. last 3-10 Myrs) star formation rate (SFR) from the Hα luminosity following the commonly used expression provided by Kennicutt & Evans (2012)" (4.5. H α -based star formation)
   - software: CIGALE, CLOUDY v17.02
   - parameter: Intrinsic (H-alpha/H-beta)_int = 2.79; SFR(H-alpha) = 5.37e-42 L(H-alpha) with 0.5 factor for metal-poor galaxies C2, L1-L5  
  > "To convert recombination line flux ratios into visual attenuation values (i.e. A V ) we take the intrinsic (dust-free) line ratio (Hα/Hβ) int = 2.79 given by Reddy et al. (2023)" (4.2. Nebular dust attenuation from the recombination lines); "SFR(Hα)/(M ⊙ yr −1 ) = 5.37 × 10 −42 L(Hα)/(erg s −1 )." (4.5. H α -based star formation); "To take into account this effect, and based on the results of the previous section, the SFRs obtained with the expression quoted above for galaxies C2, L1-L5 have been corrected accordingly by a factor 0.5." (4.5. H α -based star formation)
   - assumption: Kroupa (2001) IMF; Calzetti et al. (2000) attenuation law; Case B recombination (T_e = 10000 K, n_e = 100 cm^-3); H0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.307, Omega_Lambda = 0.691  
  > "In this work, we adopt the cosmological parameters from Planck Collaboration et al. (2016): H 0 = 67.7 km s −1 Mpc −1 , Ω m = 0.307, and Ω Λ = 0.691, which provide a scale of 5.41 kpc/ ′′ at z =6.90." (1. Introduction); "We adopt a Kroupa initial mass function (IMF; Kroupa 2001)" (1. Introduction); "We also use the Calzetti et al. (2000) law as, e.g. in Domínguez et al. (2013)." (4.2. Nebular dust attenuation from the recombination lines)
   - fitting: SED fitting with delayed exponential SFH (tau = 1 Myr to 1 Gyr, Z = 0.02 to 2.5 Z_sun, A_V = 0 to 3 mag); MCMC maximum likelihood for log(O/H)  
  > "assuming a delayed exponential star formation history characterised by a timescale τ (taking values from 1 Myr to 1 Gyr in 0.1 dex steps) and age t 0 (ranging from 1 Myr to the age of the Universe at the redshift of the galaxy)." (4.1. Stellar masses); "We followed the procedure developed by Curti et al. (2024), which explores the log(O/H) parameter space with a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm" (4.4. Metallicity)
   - validation: Compared E galaxy stellar mass and predicted 10 um flux with Marrone et al. (2018) and MIRI measurement from Álvarez-Márquez et al. (2023); checked metallicities against Sanders et al. (2024) calibration.  
  > "The stellar mass obtained for E (3.5±0.4 × 10 10 M ⊙ ) is in excellent agreement with the one derived by Marrone et al. (2018) (3.5±1.5 × 10 10 M ⊙ ). The predicted value from the fit at 10 µm (1.8 µJy) is also in good agreement with the actual MIRI measurement by Álvarez-Márquez et al. (2023) (1.6±0.3 µJy)." (4.1. Stellar masses)

## Qualitative findings

- Ten new galaxies at z ~ 6.9 (and three low-z galaxies at z ~ 1.68-2.58) are discovered in the 17 x 17 kpc^2 field around the E and W galaxies of SPT0311-58.  
  > "These observations reveal ten new galaxies at z∼ 6.9 characterised by dynamical masses spanning from ∼10 9 to 10 10 M ⊙ and a range in radial velocity of ∼ 1500 km s −1 , in addition to the already known E and W galaxies." (abstract); "Our observations reveal ten new sources at a redshift of ∼ 6.9 (and three additional foreground galaxies at z ∼ 1-3) in the field around the two main, previously identified massive galaxies of the system" (7. Summary and conclusions)
- The core of the SPT0311-58 protocluster (r < 0.2 R_200) is likely not yet in virial equilibrium, though most member galaxies appear gravitationally bound to the dark-matter halo.  
  > "A basic analysis indicates that the innermost regions of the protocluster (r < 0.2 R 200 ) have likely not yet reached virial equilibrium. We also located the galaxies in the projected phase space (PPS) diagram, and find that most are gravitationally bound to the halo." (7. Summary and conclusions)
- The galaxies in SPT0311-58 exhibit diverse ISM ionisation and metallicity conditions, ranging from low-metallicity high-excitation conditions typical of z > 6 field galaxies in the external sources to nearly solar metallicity and lower log([O III]/H-beta) near the massive E and W galaxies.  
  > "Their ionisation conditions range from those typical of the field galaxies at similar redshift recently studied with JWST to those found in more evolved objects at lower redshift, with log([OIII]/Hβ) varying from ∼ 0.25 to 1. The metallicity spans more than 0.8 dex across the FoV, reaching nearly solar values in some cases." (abstract)
- The E galaxy exhibits a metallicity gradient of ~0.1 dex/kpc (0.7 dex peak-to-peak), an unsettled turbulent ionised gas disc (v/sigma_0 ~ 1), velocity offsets and higher velocity dispersions in [O III] compared to ALMA [C II] in the south/east, and a likely pre-collision minor merger with L7.  
  > "The detailed spatially resolved spectroscopy of the E galaxy reveals that it is actively assembling its stellar mass, showing inhomogeneities in the ISM properties at subkiloparsec scales, and a metallicity gradient (∼ 0.1 dex/kpc) that can be explained by accretion of low metallicity gas from the intergalactic medium." (abstract); "The kinematic maps also depict an unsettled disc characterised by deviations from regular rotation, elevated turbulence, and indications of a possible precollision minor merger." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST/NIRSpec IFU covers rest-frame UV and main optical emission lines (including H-alpha at z = 6.9, the highest redshift accessible to NIRSpec) with ~0.5 kpc spatial resolution over a ~17 x 17 kpc^2 field around SPT0311-58.  
  > "The JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations presented here cover the rest-frame UV wavelengths and main optical emission lines of SPT0311–58 (i.e. ∼ 0.13 – 0.67 µm). This allows us to characterise the physical and kinematic properties of the warm ionised gas component of its ISM at subkpc scales (i.e. ∼ 0.5 kpc) over a field of view of ∼ 17 × 17 kpc 2 ." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Used the Curti et al. (2017, 2020b) metallicity calibration because it covers the observed metallicity range well with low scatter; used bootstrap for cluster velocity dispersion because it is more robust for small samples.  
  > "This calibration is based on local samples, but it has the advantage of covering well the metallicity range observed in the system, and it shows relatively low scatter." (4.4. Metallicity); "The bootstrap estimate is preferred over the standard deviation (507 km s −1 ), as it is more robust for small samples (Beers et al. 1990)." (Footnote 8)
- Alternative: Outflow origin for component L7 or for the E galaxy metallicity gradient: rejected because BIC did not support a third outflow component, L7 is redshifted while lying in front of dusty E, and no spectral outflow signatures or minor-axis gradients are seen.  
  > "The fact the L7 is redshifted with respect to E excludes the possibility that the detected emission is due to an outflow." (3.3.3. Identification of L7 at z ∼ 6.9 via 2D kinematic decomposition); "However, we do not see any clear evidence for outflows in our spectra. Moreover, if an outflow were present (or had been active in recent times), its most likely orientation would be along the minor axis, which is perpendicular to the direction of the metallicity gradient." (6.2.1. Metallicity gradient and evidence of gas accretion in the E galaxy)
- Alternative: Major merger of ES and EN as the cause of the E galaxy metallicity gradient: disfavoured compared to gas accretion because [O III] and [C II] velocity fields are smooth on large scales without double velocity dispersion peaks or separate nuclear rotation.  
  > "However, the kinematic properties in the main structure of E do not show evidence for two kinematically distinct objects, such as peaks in the velocity dispersion map, or rotation associated with their nuclear regions." (6.2.1. Metallicity gradient and evidence of gas accretion in the E galaxy)
- Alternative: Post-starburst interpretation for the discrepancy between SFR(FIR) and SFR(H-alpha) in the W galaxy: rejected in favour of heavy dust obscuration because W is extremely dusty with high nebular attenuation.  
  > "we are dealing with an extremely dusty galaxy, we favour the star-forming interpretation, and explain the discrepancy between the SFR(Hα) and SFR(FIR) as due to heavy dust attenuation." (4.5. Hα-based star formation)
- Alternative: Disc fragmentation origin for L7: rejected because L7 is kinematically decoupled (+250 km s^-1) from E.  
  > "First, this significant kinematic decoupling rules out that L7 has been formed by disc fragmentation in contrast to the clumps identified by Spilker et al. (2022) at submm wavelengths." (6.2.2. Kinematic evidence for an unsettled disc structure at redshift ∼ 7)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Stellar masses for the newly discovered z ~ 6.9 galaxies lack MIRI photometry and may be systematically overestimated by ~0.4 dex; systematic model uncertainties on stellar masses are factors of 1.5-2.  
  > "The quoted stellar mass errors correspond to the uncertainties in the fit due to flux errors, but they do not include systematic uncertainties associated with the model assumptions, which are estimated to be about factors of 1.5-2." (4.1. Stellar masses); "We also note that recent work has shown that stellar masses inferred by SED fitting without MIRI photometry may be overestimated by ∼ 0.4 dex for sources at the high-redshift of SPT0311–58 (Papovich et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024)." (4.1. Stellar masses)
- Limitation (authors): Absolute fluxes from NIRSpec apertures are subject to uncertainties from irregular source morphologies, detector artefacts, edge effects (for C3, L3, L5), and unconsolidated instrument calibration.  
  > "As a consequence, the absolute fluxes inferred from the spectra should be taken with caution, also considering that the calibration of the instrument is not consolidated yet." (Appendix A.1: Definition of extraction apertures for regional integrated spectra); "3 These sources are close the edge of the FoV, and subject to larger calibration uncertainties." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): Virialisation and halo mass conclusions are subject to large uncertainties from small sample size (12 galaxies), uncertain systemic redshift of W, and poorly constrained M_star/M_h ratio at z ~ 7.  
  > "The M ⋆ / M h ratio is not well constrained for the redshift and stellar mass of SPT0311–58" (6.1.1. Main properties of the dark-matter halo); "However, as mentioned above, the uncertainties are large and this conclusion lacks robustness." (6.1.2. Probing the dynamical state of the protocluster core)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA tclean imaging parameters (weighting/robustness, clean threshold, uv-coverage) are not specified in the paper, relying on previously published reductions in Marrone et al. (2018) and Spilker et al. (2022).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The K-S test for virialisation is highly sensitive to the adopted systemic velocity of galaxy W, which differs by up to 700 km s^-1 between [C II], optical lines, and Pa-alpha.
