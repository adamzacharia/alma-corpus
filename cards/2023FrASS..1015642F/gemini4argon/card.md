## Analysis recipe

1. **Image calibrated archival visibilities and measure channel map noise**: Manual imaging using the clean task in CASA with Briggs weighting (robust = 0.5), and calculating channel rms using imstat near the field center excluding emission.  
  > "The imaging process of the calibrated data was performed using CASA 4.3.1[^2] software manually." (Observations); "The “clean" task was employed, and to achieve a balance between spatial resolution and noise gain in the resulting image, the Briggs weighting function was applied along with adjustments to the “robust" parameter of 0.5." (Observations); "The rms values for all channel maps were obtained using the task “imstat” in CASA software." (Observations)
   - software: CASA 4.3.1, clean, imstat
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "The “clean" task was employed, and to achieve a balance between spatial resolution and noise gain in the resulting image, the Briggs weighting function was applied along with adjustments to the “robust" parameter of 0.5." (Observations)
   - parameter: pixel size = 0.2 arcseconds  
  > "Each pixel on the image plane corresponded to a size of 0.2 arcseconds." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Analyze spatial distribution of SiC2 via channel maps and moment 0 maps**: Generate radial velocity channel maps and zeroth-order moment maps using Python to examine the shell morphology and compare SiC2 with parent species SiO.  
  > "Then we used Python software to map and analyze ALMA product data." (Observations); "In Figure [3](#fig:3), we present channel maps which provide images of vertical slices through the expanding envelope while Figure [4](#fig:4) shows the line intensity integrated over all velocities." (4.1.1 AI Vol)
   - software: Python, astropy, matplotlib
   - parameter: Velocity channels around V_LSR (from -32.3 to -46.5 km s^-1 for AI Vol Fig 3)  
  > "Brightness distribution of the $4_{0,\,4} -3_ {0,\,3}$ transition of SiC$_2$ and the 2 - 1 transition of SiO around $V\rm_{LSR}$ channels (from - 32.3 to - 46.5 km s$^{-1}$) is shown in Figure [3](#fig:3)." (4.1.1 AI Vol)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of spatial extent with parent molecule SiO and theoretical CSE zones  
  > "The SiC$_2$ and SiO shown in Figure [3](#fig:3) represent the spatial distribution characteristics of the daughter species and parent species, respectively, exhibiting distinct hollow and compact structures." (4.1.1 AI Vol)
3. **Extract spectral line parameters via shell fitting**: Shell profile fitting in GILDAS software to obtain integrated intensity (int T_R dv), peak temperature (T_peak), and S/N for each observed SiC2 transition.  
  > "To calculate the molecular column density, we employed the shell fitting in the GILDAS software[^3], along with the splatalogue [^4] and the CDMS databases[^5] (cite: Muller+2005)." (Observations); ": Spectral line parameters of different transitions in SiC$_2$ obtained via shell fitting using GILDAS." (Table 2)
   - software: GILDAS
   - fitting: Shell fitting in GILDAS  
  > ": Spectral line parameters of different transitions in SiC$_2$ obtained via shell fitting using GILDAS." (Table 2)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive excitation temperature, column density, and fractional abundance of SiC2**: Linear fitting of rotational diagrams to derive T_ex and N(SiC2), calculation of average H2 column density within the SiC2 radius from mass-loss rate and expansion velocity, and computing fractional abundance f = N(SiC2)/N(H2).  
  > "In the practical calculations, firstly, the excited temperature and column density of SiC$_2$ are fitted by using the rotation diagram method that described in equation [\[equ:1\]](#equ:1), and plotted in Figure [7](#fig:7); secondly, the fractional abundance of SiC$_2$ is calculated by $f$ = $N/N_{\rm H_2}$." (4.2 Abundance)
   - parameter: R = 3'' (for AI Vol)  
  > "R is the radius of the peak of the highest brightness point in Figure [4](#fig:4), which is 3$^{\prime \prime}$;" (4.2 Abundance)
   - parameter: E_u and S*mu^2 from Splatalogue  
  > "The values of $S \mu^2$ and $E\rm_u/k$ are taken from the splatalogue databases[^6]." (4.2 Abundance)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Using the parameters in Table [2](#table:2), the molecular excitation temperature and column density are calculated under the assumption of LTE, and the equation is as follows (cite: zhang2009molecular; wangke2014)," (4.2 Abundance)
   - assumption: Mean molecular weight mu = 2.8  
  > "$\mu$ the mean molecular weight of 2.8 of (cite: gong20151)." (4.2 Abundance)
   - fitting: Linear regression of ln(3kW / (8 pi^3 nu S mu^2)) vs E_u/k with 1-sigma error propagation  
  > "In the rotational analysis using Equation [\[equ:1\]](#equ:1), we added error propagating and obtained the error of excitation temperature ($\delta$$T \rm _{ex}$) and column density ($\delta$$N$), with the integral intensity of 1$\sigma$-error." (4.2 Abundance)
   - validation: Comparison with single-dish literature values in Table 3 and IRC+10216  
  > ": The deduced excitation temperature ($T\rm_{ex}$), column density ($N$), fractional abundance ($f$) of SiC$_2$ relative to H$_2$ in the target sources by the rotational diagram method, along with the previous observational results." (Table 3)

## Qualitative findings

- SiC2 exhibits an annular, hollow shell-like distribution in AI Vol, RAFGL 4211, and II Lup, indicating it is a daughter species formed in the outer CSE.  
  > "Here, we analyze the ALMA observations of four SiC$_2$ transitions in the CSEs of three C-rich AGB stars (AI Vol, II Lup, and RAFGL 4211), and found that SiC$_2$ exhibits an annular, shell-like distribution in these targets, suggesting that SiC$_2$ can be a daughter species in the CSEs of carbon-rich AGB stars." (Abstract); "The distribution in hollow rings of the three sources indicates that SiC$_2$ is formed in the star’s outer layer through chemical reactions." (4.1.3 Summary of morphology)
- Higher rotational quantum number transitions of SiC2 are distributed closer to the star than lower rotational quantum number transitions.  
  > "Compared to transitions with low rotational quantum numbers, transitions with high rotational quantum numbers are distributed closer to the star." (4.1.1 AI Vol)
- The fractional abundance of SiC2 increases with increasing wind density in the three observed C-rich AGB stars.  
  > "We found that the fractional abundance of SiC$_2$ increases with the increasing wind density in the three observed C-rich AGB stars." (4.2 Abundance)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: A 3 mm survey revealed abundant molecules in these three carbon-rich stars, and high-resolution interferometric observations from ALMA are required to sensitively detect their spatial distributions.  
  > "A survey conducted by (cite: smith2015molecular) at the 3 mm band revealed that the three carbon-rich stars possess abundant molecules, and the sensitive detection of molecular spatial distributions relies on more advanced telescopes such as ALMA." (Observations)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Treating SiC2 solely as a parent species in C-rich AGB stars, as done in previous single-dish studies and astrochemical models.  
  > "In the previous astrochemical models of C-rich CSEs of AGB stars, SiC$_2$ is always treated as a parent species with an initial abundance of $\thicksim$ $10^{-5}$ (cite: li2014photodissociation; 2018A&A...611A..29M; 2022MNRAS.510.1204V). However, this study clearly shows that this species is a daughter species and therefore the chemistry of SiC$_2$ in the chemical models needs to be reinvestigated." (4.2 Abundance)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Because the upper energy levels of the observed transitions are close in temperature (11.23 to 19.22 K), the errors on the derived excitation temperatures and column densities are rather large.  
  > "As the upper energy levels are close in temperature, the errors on the derived quantities are rather large." (4.2 Abundance)
- Limitation (authors): The 4_2,2 - 3_2,1 transition was not detected in II Lup (possibly due to an anomalous 300x300 pixel map size in the archive data), and the rotational diagram fitting of II Lup was performed using only two data points, resulting in lower precision and no error estimation.  
  > "In contrast, the data without signal detection only covered a map size of 300 $\times$ 300 pixels. We speculate that there may have been some unknown special circumstances during the observations that led to the absence of signal." (4.1 Spatial distribution of SiC2); "The fitting of II Lup was performed using only two data points, which resulted in a lower precision and the absence of error estimation." (Table 3)
- Limitation (authors): More sensitive observations may detect SiC2 emission at low levels in the inner CSE.  
  > "More sensitive observations may, of course, detect SiC$_2$ emission at low levels in the inner CSE." (Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 12 m array observations with baselines 21-783 m lack short-spacing (ACA or total power) data, which could affect the recovery of extended emission in the CSEs, though a compact central parent component would not be resolved out.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using LTE rotational diagrams over a very narrow upper-state energy range (11.2-19.2 K) yields poorly constrained excitation temperatures (e.g., 17.25 +/- 13.07 K in RAFGL 4211) and sub-thermal values (4.12 K and 6.66 K) that may indicate non-LTE excitation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A minor numerical discrepancy exists between the text (f(SiC2) = 1.55e-8 for AI Vol) and Table 3 (1.64e-8, which is 0.90e13 / 5.52e20 = 1.63e-8).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The conclusion that f(SiC2) increases with wind density is based on only three sources, one of which (II Lup) was fit with just two transitions and has no uncertainty estimate.
