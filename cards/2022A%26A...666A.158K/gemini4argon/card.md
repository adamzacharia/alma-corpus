## Science

- Fields: astrochemistry, star_formation
- Question: Determine the millimetre-wave rotational spectrum of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal in the laboratory and search for this molecule (and related aldehydes) towards the solar-type protostar IRAS16293-2422 and the high-mass star-forming region Sgr B2(N).  
  > "The aim of this work is to deepen knowledge about the millimetre-wave spectrum of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal in the region enabling its detailed search towards astronomical objects. In particular, we target the solar-type protostar IRAS16293–2422 and star-forming region Sagittarius (Sgr) B2(N)." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Quantum-chemical calculations and laboratory rotational spectroscopy of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal**: Record millimetre-wave spectrum (128-166 GHz and 285-329 GHz), perform DFT B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) calculations, assign transitions using Loomis-Wood plots, fit spectroscopic constants using Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian in I^r representation, and compute rotational and vibrational partition functions.  
  > "we have performed quantum-chemical calculations employing the density functional theory (DFT) combined with the B3LYP functional and Pople’s basis set with added polarization and diffusion functions (6-311++G(d,p))." (Quantum-chemical calculations); "The Pickett’s program package SPFIT/SPCAT (cite: PICKETT1991) with Watson’s $A$-reduced Hamiltonian in I$^{\text{r}}$ representation were employed throughout the analysis." (Ground state); "The rotational partition functions for different temperatures were obtained from SPCAT covering all rotational states up to $J$ = 100." (Ground state)
   - software: Gaussian 16, AABS, SPFIT/SPCAT
   - parameter: Frequency ranges: 128-166 GHz and 285-329 GHz; J up to 100 for Q_rot  
  > "The spectrum of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal was recorded with the upgraded Prague semiconductor millimetre-wave spectrometer described elsewhere (cite: Kania2006) in two spectral ranges (128–166 GHz and 285–329 GHz)." (Experiment); "The rotational partition functions for different temperatures were obtained from SPCAT covering all rotational states up to $J$ = 100." (Ground state)
   - assumption: Electric dipole moments |mu_a| = 1.146(10) D, |mu_b| = 1.560(12) D, |mu_c| = 0 D from Lovas2003  
  > "The previously determined values of electric dipole moments were $|\mu_{a}|=$ 1.146(10) D, $|\mu_{b}| =$ 1.560(12) D, and $|\mu_{c}| =$ 0 D from symmetry (cite: Lovas2003)." (Ground state)
   - fitting: Least-squares fit of rotational and centrifugal distortion constants in SPFIT (669 distinct lines / 968 transitions for ground state, sigma_fit = 0.024 MHz; 295 lines / 487 transitions for v21=1, sigma_fit = 0.032 MHz)  
  > "A total number of 669 and 295 distinct frequency lines covering 968 and 487 rotational transitions was used in the assignment of the ground and excited vibrational states, respectively." (Conclusions)
   - validation: Comparison of experimental spectroscopic constants and vibrational-rotational changes with DFT calculations; Loomis-Wood diagrams  
  > "The analysis of the ground state provides an extended set of spectroscopic constants, which is in an excellent agreement with quantum-chemical calculations as depicted in Table [\[T1\]](#T1)." (Ground state); "Nevertheless, the Loomis-Wood type plots corroborate our assignment of the rotational lines." (Excited vibrational state v_21=1)
2. **Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal and derive column density upper limits towards IRAS16293 B**: Model the PILS ALMA spectrum at the IRAS16293 B offset position under LTE using CASSIS and determine 3-sigma upper limits on column density incorporating vibrational partition function correction.  
  > "We modelled the astronomical spectra under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium." (abstract); "We used for that the CASSIS[^2] software. We assumed a FWHM of 1 km s$^{-1}$ and a source size of 0.5$\ensuremath{''}$ to determine the upper limit." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: FWHM = 1 km s^-1, source size = 0.5 arcsec, T_ex = 125 K and 300 K  
  > "We assumed a FWHM of 1 km s$^{-1}$ and a source size of 0.5$\ensuremath{''}$ to determine the upper limit. Complex organic molecules in this object show excitation temperatures, $T_{\rm ex}$, between 125 and 300 K (cite: Jorgensen2018)." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), vibrational correction from Table 2  
  > "We modelled the astronomical spectra under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium." (abstract); "The 3$\sigma$ upper limit is $\sim$2.4 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ based on the undetected lines and taking into account the vibrational correction (see Table [\[T2\]](#T2))." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
   - fitting: 3-sigma upper limit determination based on undetected lines  
  > "The 3$\sigma$ upper limit is $\sim$2.4 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ based on the undetected lines and taking into account the vibrational correction (see Table [\[T2\]](#T2))." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
   - validation: Compared derived abundance upper limits and isomer ratios against chemical network predictions from Coutens2022  
  > "The upper limit derived for the 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal/3-hydroxypropenal abundance ratio ($\lesssim$ 0.9 for $T_{\rm ex}$= 125 K and $\lesssim$ 1.3 for $T_{\rm ex}$= 300 K) is consistent with the value predicted by the chemical model ($\sim$ 1.4), taking into account the uncertainties described above." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
3. **Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal, 2-hydroxypropanal, and 3-hydroxypropenal towards Sgr B2(N1S)**: Produce LTE synthetic spectra using Weeds with fixed physical parameters adopted from acetaldehyde (CH3CHO) and derive column density upper limits from the ReMoCA spectrum.  
  > "We assumed local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and produced synthetic spectra with the astronomical software Weeds (cite: Maret11) in order to analyse the observed spectrum." (Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal and related aldehydes toward Sgr B2(N1) - Observations); "Assuming that the more complex molecule 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal traces the same region as acetaldehyde, we produced LTE synthetic spectra for the former species adopting the same parameters as for the latter with only the column density left as a free parameter." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: Source size = 2.0 arcsec, T_rot = 250 K, Delta V = 5.0 km s^-1, V_off = 0.0 km s^-1, V_sys = 62 km s^-1  
  > "We used a set of five parameters to model the contribution of each species: size of the emitting region ($\theta_{\rm s}$), column density ($N$), temperature ($T_{\rm rot}$), linewidth ($\Delta V$), and velocity offset ($V_{\rm off}$) with respect to the assumed systemic velocity of the source, $V_{\rm sys}=62$ km s$^{-1}$." (Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal and related aldehydes toward Sgr B2(N1) - Observations); "CH$_3$CHO$^{j}$$^\star$ | d | 31 | 2.0 | 250 | 6.7 (17) | 1.09 | 5.0 | 0.0 | 1" (Table 4)
   - assumption: LTE; target aldehydes trace the same region with identical physical parameters as acetaldehyde; vibrational correction factors F_vib = 2.41 (2-hydroxyprop-2-enal), 5.68 (2-hydroxypropanal), 2.00 (3-hydroxypropenal) at 250 K  
  > "Assuming that the more complex molecule 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal traces the same region as acetaldehyde, we produced LTE synthetic spectra for the former species adopting the same parameters as for the latter with only the column density left as a free parameter." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal); "CH$_2$C(OH)CHO, $\varv=0$ | n | 0 | 2.0 | 250 | $<$ 7.2 (16) | 2.41 | 5.0 | 0.0 | $>$ 9.3" (Table 4)
   - fitting: Visual comparison of LTE synthetic spectra overlaid on the observed ReMoCA spectrum and baseline-subtracted all-species model  
  > "We derived a best-fit synthetic spectrum for each molecule separately, and then added the contributions of all identified molecules together." (Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal and related aldehydes toward Sgr B2(N1) - Observations); "The molecule is not detected toward Sgr B2(N1S), as illustrated in Figs. [4](#f:spec_ch2cohcho_ve0) and [5](#f:spec_ch2cohcho_v21e1)." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)

  > "The Pickett’s program package SPFIT/SPCAT (cite: PICKETT1991) with Watson’s $A$-reduced Hamiltonian in I$^{\text{r}}$ representation were employed throughout the analysis." ## Qualitative findings

- 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal is not detected towards IRAS16293 B, but its upper limit is consistent within uncertainties with chemical model predictions.  
  > "We report a nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal toward the protostar IRAS16293 B. The derived upper limit is in agreement within the uncertainties with the predictions of the chemical model developed for 3-hydroxypropenal in (cite: Coutens2022)." (Conclusions)
- 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal, 2-hydroxypropanal, and 3-hydroxypropenal are not detected towards Sgr B2(N1S).  
  > "We also report the nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal toward the hot molecular core Sgr B2(N1). We did not detect the related aldehydes 2-hydroxypropanal and 3-hydroxypropenal either." (abstract)
- Abundance upper limits relative to acetaldehyde in Sgr B2(N1S) are less constraining by more than an order of magnitude compared to IRAS16293 B.  
  > "The limits obtained for both molecules in Sgr B2(N1S) are thus less constraining by more than one order of magnitude compared to IRAS16293 B." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous microwave predictions below 25 GHz were unreliable when extrapolated to ALMA frequencies; PILS and ReMoCA provide high-sensitivity, high-angular-resolution unbiased spectral surveys of prime astrochemical targets where related isomers (like 3-hydroxypropenal in PILS) were studied.  
  > "The predictions of the rotational transitions to the frequency range of common surveys, such as surveys performed with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), like the Re-Exploring Molecular Complexity with ALMA survey (ReMoCA) that covers the frequency range 84–114 GHz (cite: Belloche19) or the Protostellar Interferometric Line Survey (PILS) covering 329–363 GHz (cite: Jorgensen2016), are rather unreliable if they are based on low-frequency data." (Introduction); "Thanks to its high sensitivity (rms $\sim$ 4-5 mJy in a bin of 1 km s$^{-1}$), a large variety of species were detected for the first time in low-mass protostars through this survey" (Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal towards IRAS16293-2422 - Observations)
- Why this method: Selected the offset position Sgr B2(N1S) because it has lower continuum opacity than the hot core peak, and used LTE synthetic modeling justified by the high gas densities of the hot core.  
  > "It has a lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core which facilitates the detection of spectral lines of molecules." (Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal and related aldehydes toward Sgr B2(N1) - Observations); "The high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (cite: Bonfand19) justify our assumption of LTE." (Search for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal and related aldehydes toward Sgr B2(N1) - Observations)
- Alternative: Using glyceraldehyde instead of dihydroxyacetone as the chemical precursor for in situ synthesis of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal (dihydroxyacetone was chosen as more cost-effective).  
  > "Either of them proved to be a good source for producing the desired species in our experiment. Dihydroxyacetone as a precursor turned out to be a cost-effective solution, and therefore, all further measurements were carried out with it." (Species preparation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Insufficient line intensities prevented the analysis of other low-lying excited vibrational states of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal, hampering assessment of possible Coriolis or Fermi interactions with the v15 state at 277.4 cm^-1.  
  > "However, the possible interaction with the nearby excited vibrational state at 277.4 cm$^{-1}$, or even a combination of both effects cannot be excluded either. Unfortunately, the insufficient intensities for other low-lying vibrational states made their analysis impossible, thus hampering further assessment of this issue." (Excited vibrational state v_21=1)
- Limitation (authors): Lines of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal in IRAS16293 B at 125 K are mostly blended with transitions of other species.  
  > "For 125 K, which was slightly favoured for 3-hydroxypropenal (cite: Coutens2022), the lines of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal are usually blended with other species." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainties (factor of 20) in the chemical network predictions for 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal production and destruction.  
  > "The derived abundance is still within the uncertainties of the chemical model which is estimated to be a factor 20 for this species." (Nondetection of 2-hydroxyprop-2-enal)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits in Sgr B2(N1S) and IRAS16293 B rely on subjective visual inspection or unspecified single-line criteria in crowded spectra rather than automated statistical fitting across all transitions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming identical source size, temperature, and linewidth as acetaldehyde in Sgr B2(N1S) may introduce systematic bias if C3 hydroxy-aldehydes trace a more compact or cooler sub-region.
