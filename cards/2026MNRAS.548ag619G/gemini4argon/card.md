  > "The median spectrum, shown in Fig. [2](#fig:medspec), can be characterised by a simple power-law with exponent $\alpha_{\rm MIRI}^{\rm obs}$ = +0.39 $\pm$ 0.07 (with flux density $F_{\rm \nu} \propto \nu^{\alpha_{\rm MIRI}^{\rm obs}}$)." ## Qualitative findings

- MIR compact jet emission in the soft state of GX 339-4 is quenched by a factor of ~300 or more relative to previous hard-state detections.  
  > "The source is significantly detected between 5–10 μm, albeit at a faint flux level requiring MIR compact jet emission to be quenched by a factor of $\sim$ 300 or more relative to previous hard-state detections." (Abstract)
- Radio and sub-mm detections (ATCA and ALMA) are offset by ~1.2 arcsec west of the optical core position, arising from a downstream discrete jet ejection rather than a compact core jet.  
  > "However, detailed imaging analysis of these data indicate that the radio and sub-mm counterparts do not arise from the core position of GX 339–4; instead the detections at these wavelengths are shifted from the optical *Gaia* DR3 position of GX 339–4 (cite: gaiamission; gaiadr3summarycontents) to the West by $\approx$ 1.2$''$, along its known jet axis (cite: 2004MNRAS.347L..52G; mastroserio25)." (On the origin of the radio and sub-mm emission: a discrete jet ejection)
- Synchrotron radiation from an extended coronal hot flow (B ~ 3e4 G, r_C ~ 170 R_G) self-consistently explains both the MIRI spectrum and stochastic timing behaviour.  
  > "The most plausible scenario for powering the observed MIR emission is synchrotron radiation from a coronal hot flow (§ [\[sec:hotflow\]](#sec:hotflow))." (Summary); "With a magnetic field $B$ $\approx$ 3 $\times$ 10$^4$ G and plasma extended out to $r_{\rm C}$ $\sim$ 170 $R_{\rm G}$, the same medium could also Comptonise disc photons to account for the high-energy power-law tail, while self-consistently accounting for the MIRI timing properties." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST/MIRI provides the sensitivity and spatial resolution needed to detect faint soft-state MIR emission and perform spectral-timing without neighbour contamination, while coordinated radio/sub-mm/optical/X-ray observations constrain the full broadband SED.  
  > "Here, we present one of the most sensitive MIR detections of a black hole XRB in a disc-dominated accretion state." (Introduction); "*JWST*’s excellent spatial resolution rules out any contamination to our MIRI extraction." (Archival Mid-IR detections of GX 339-4)
- Why this method: Spectral-timing observations disentangle emission components based on their differing spatial scales and characteristic variability timescales.  
  > "Spectral-timing observations offer the possibility to disentangle these components based on their differing scales and characteristic variability timescales (cite: uttleycasella14)." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Compact jet emission as the origin of the soft-state MIRI continuum (rejected because the MIRI slope matches neither the optical/NIR nor the radio/sub-mm slopes, and no core radio jet is detected)  
  > "The MIR slope $\alpha_{\rm MIRI}$ matches neither $\alpha_{\rm ONIR}$ nor $\alpha_{\rm RS}$, so the MIRI continuum cannot be connected seamlessly at either end without invoking the presence of a spectral break." (Discussion); "So the origin of the MIR emission cannot be the compact jet, as in the hard state (cite: Gandhi-2011)." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Circumbinary dust disc (CBD) (rejected because its dynamical timescale >= 12.3 days cannot explain the observed MIRI variability on timescales of a few thousand seconds)  
  > "Thus, we do not expect substantial CBD variability on timespans of a few thousand seconds, as observed in our MIRI light curves." (Circumbinary Disc?)
- Alternative: Warm wind reprocessing / bremsstrahlung (rejected because the MIRI spectral slope matches neither optically-thin nor optically-thick free-free emission, and predicted Pf-alpha recombination lines are absent)  
  > "Our inferred MIRI slope of $\alpha_{\rm MIRI}$ = +0.36 $\pm$ 0.07 fits neither of these." (Wind reprocessing?); "This predicted flux is about a factor of 5 larger than the actual detection limit, arguing against recombination." (Wind reprocessing?)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): JWST/MIRI pipeline systematic uncertainties at longer wavelengths (>10 um) underestimate long-wavelength noise and restrict analysis to 5-10 um.  
  > "The signal weakens towards longer wavelengths where calibrations remain uncertain, and background artefacts become more difficult to account for." (JWST/MIRI); "Though there appears to be a slight rise towards the red end, we caution that systematic uncertainties in the MIRI pipeline may be underestimating the long-wavelength noise, so this should be treated with caution (see more extensive discussion and Appendix in (cite: gandhi25))." (MIRI spectral-timing)
- Limitation (authors): Unannounced 2-hour shift in the JWST observing window resulted in imperfect temporal overlap with some multiwavelength facilities.  
  > "Due to a failure in unrelated observations scheduled just prior to ours, the *JWST* schedule changed dynamically and the GX 339–4 observing window slid forward by approximately 2 hours without our knowledge, resulting in imperfect overlap with some facilities." (Multiwavelength Campaign)
- Limitation (authors): Presence of the bright downstream jet knot prevents placing deep constraints on the core radio/sub-mm compact jet quenching factor.  
  > "Our imaging requires that the compact jet emission at the core has been quenched (cite: fender04; corbel13b; russell20), however, we are unable to place deep constraints on the quenching factor due to the presence of the jet knot." (On the origin of the radio and sub-mm emission: a discrete jet ejection)
- Limitation (authors): Coronal hot flow synchrotron model requires the self-absorption break frequency to coincidentally remain near the MIRI band across both hard and soft states.  
  > "Another apparent coincidence for this scenario to work is the requirement for the SSA break to lie in the MIRI band, straddling frequencies very similar to those inferred for the optically-thick–to–thin break associated with the hard-state jet base (cite: Gandhi-2011; russell13), despite dramatic changes in flux and timing properties between the two states (Fig. [\[fig:wisecomparison\]](#fig:wisecomparison); (cite: Gandhi-2008; Casella-2010))." (Synchrotron from a hot flow overlying the disc?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Detailed spatial and temporal analysis of the ALMA and ATCA jet ejection is deferred to a companion paper (Russell et al. in prep.), leaving exact astrometric fits and beam parameters unreported here.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Simultaneous NICER and fast HAWK-I timing datasets are excluded from the present analysis and deferred to future work.
