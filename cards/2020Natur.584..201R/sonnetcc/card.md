## Science

- Fields: high_z_galaxies
- Question: Is the lensed dusty starburst SPT0418–47 at z = 4.2 dynamically hot and chaotic, or a cold rotating disk? Measured via 3D lens-kinematic modelling of the [CII] cube (rotation curve, V/sigma, Toomre Q, mass decomposition).  
  > "Interferometric imaging at a spatial resolution of about 60 pc reveals a ratio of rotational-to-random motions of $V/\sigma = 9.7\pm 0.4$" (abstract); "We derive a rotation curve with the typical shape of nearby massive spiral galaxies, which demonstrates that at least some young galaxies are dynamically akin to those observed in the local Universe" (abstract)
- Target **SPT0418–47 (SPT–S J041839–4751.9)**: coordinates _not stated_; z/distance z = 4.2248 (source); lens at z = 0.263  
  > "Galaxy SPT–S J041839–4751.9 is strongly gravitationally lensed by a foreground galaxy at $z = 0.263$" (abstract); "confirmed SPT0418–47 to be a dusty starburst galaxy at redshift ($z$) 4.2248(cite: weis)" (Observations); distance from Weiss et al. (cite: weis)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and prepare the [CII] visibilities**: ALMA pipeline calibration in CASA, phase-only self-calibration on the continuum applied to the line window, uv continuum subtraction, averaging of four channels  
  > "We calibrated the raw visibility data using the ALMA pipeline in the Common Astronomy Software Applications CASA package(cite: mcmullin)." (Observations); "Phase-only self-calibration was performed on the continuum with a solution interval of 100 s to correct for residual phase errors." (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: self-calibration solint 100 s; 28 channels of 25.7 km/s  
  > "resulting in twenty-eight independent channels each with a width of 25.7 km s$^{-1}$." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: visual inspection of the pipeline calibration quality  
  > "The data were then inspected to confirm the quality of the pipeline calibration" (Observations)
2. **Make CLEAN images and moment maps for visualisation only**: CLEAN with natural weighting at 0.03 arcsec/pixel; zeroth, first and second moment maps; modelling itself is done on visibilities  
  > "SPT0418-47 was imaged on a pixel scale of 0.03 arcsec per pixel with natural weighting of the visibilities and deconvolved using CLEAN(cite: hog)." (Observations); "we note that these images are intended only for visualisation, as all the modelling and analysis is performed on the visibility data directly" (Observations)
   - software: CLEAN
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit the lens mass model and source kinematics to the [CII] visibilities**: 3D hierarchical Bayesian lens-kinematic modelling in visibility space: pixellated source on an adaptive grid regularised by a 3D rotating-disk kinematic model; NUFFT and preconditioned conjugate gradient solver  
  > "using a 3D Bayesian lens-kinematic modelling technique that fits the data directly in visibility space" (Lens and kinematic model); "is solved using a preconditioned conjugate gradient solver" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - software: in-house lens-kinematic code, MULTINEST
   - parameter: conjugate gradient tolerance 1e-12 (typical source-inversion accuracy 1e-6)  
  > "we use a tolerance of 10$^{-12}$, which results in typical accuracies of 10$^{-6}$ for the source inversions" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - parameter: 163190 visibilities per channel  
  > "Because of the large number of visibilities per channel, n$_{\rm{vis},i}=163190$" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - parameter: rotation curve function V_t, R_t, beta, xi; exponential velocity dispersion profile; no radial variation of inclination and position angle; gas surface density pixel-by-pixel from the zeroth-moment lens reconstruction  
  > "we impose a pixel-by-pixel normalization, which is given by the surface brightness distribution obtained from the lens modelling of the zeroth-moment map" (Lens and kinematic model); "The velocity dispersion profile, $\sigma(R)$ is described by an exponential function" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - parameter: four-step optimisation scheme for lens mass model and source kinematics  
  > "The derivation of the lens mass model and the source kinematics is done using a four-step optimisation scheme" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - assumption: LCDM cosmology, H0 = 67.8 km/s/Mpc, Omega_m = 0.308, Omega_Lambda = 0.691  
  > "we assume a $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, with Hubble constant H$_0$ = 67.8 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, matter density $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ = 0.308, and vacuum energy density $\Omega_\mathrm{\Lambda}$ = 0.691(cite: planck)" (Observations)
   - assumption: lens is a cored elliptical power-law profile plus external shear  
  > "the lens is described by a projected mass density profile with a cored elliptical power-law distribution plus the contribution of an external shear component" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - fitting: MULTINEST with tolerance 0.5, sampling efficiency 0.8, 200 live points; flat priors within +/-20% of best-fit values from non-linear optimisation; reported errors are posterior 1 s.d. and the MAP-vs-optimiser difference added in quadrature  
  > "by adopting the user-defined tolerance, sampling efficiency and live points of 0.5, 0.8 and 200" (Lens and kinematic model); "To be conservative, we report as errors in the parameters the sum in quadrature of the following two contributions" (Lens and kinematic model)
   - validation: evidence flattens with prior volume; direct forward modelling with a parametric source agrees within 1 s.d.; lens parameters agree within 2 s.d. with dust-continuum lens model; exponential dispersion preferred over linear (Bayes factor 1.8)  
  > "We then verified that the evidences estimated by MULTINEST flatten as a function of the prior volume." (Lens and kinematic model); "The parameters obtained with the forward modelling method are within the 1 s.d. uncertainties with respect to those obtained with our fiducial model" (Direct Forward modelling); "The lens mass model parameters are in agreement within 2 s.d. with those derived from modelling the dust continuum(cite: spilker)." (Lens and kinematic model)
4. **Measure V/sigma and test beam-smearing-corrected rotational support**: ratio of Vmax to mean dispersion sigma_m, and of V_flat to outer dispersion sigma_ext (R > 1 kpc); recompute after adding 15 km/s to sigma to mimic ionized-tracer offsets  
  > "We obtain $V/\sigma = 9.7\pm0.4$ when we calculate $V/\sigma$ as the ratio between the maximum rotation velocity $V_{\mathrm{max}}$ and the mean velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$" (Beam smearing correction); "If we add this 15 km s$^{-1}$ to our inferred" (V/sigma for the comparison samples)
   - parameter: outer dispersion defined at R > 1 kpc  
  > "the velocity dispersion at outer radii ($R > 1$ kpc) $\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}$" (Beam smearing correction)
   - assumption: ionized and cold gas kinematics assumed comparable when comparing with literature tracers  
  > "is based on the assumption that the ionized gas kinematics reflects the cold gas motions of galaxies" (V/sigma for the comparison samples)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: V/sigma remains much higher than observed and simulated values after adding 15 km/s to sigma (6.6 and 7.8)  
  > "we find $V_{\mathrm{max}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{m}} = 6.6$ and $V_{\mathrm{flat}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}} = 7.8$" (V/sigma for the comparison samples)
5. **Decompose the rotation curve into stars, gas and dark matter**: circular velocity (rotation plus asymmetric-drift correction) modelled as quadrature sum of Sersic stellar, exponential gas (scaled by alpha_[CII]) and NFW halo components  
  > "we model the circular velocity as $V_{\mathrm{c}} = \sqrt{V^2_{\mathrm{star}} +V^2_{\mathrm{gas}}+ V^2_{\mathrm{DM}}}$" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter); "gives a small contribution ($\lesssim 1\%$) with respect to $V_{\mathrm{rot}}(R)$." (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - software: DYNESTY
   - parameter: R_gas = 0.9 kpc from exponential fit to azimuthally averaged zeroth-moment rings, fixed in V_gas  
  > "The surface density profile obtained in this way is then fitted using the exponential profile, resulting in a value of $R_{\mathrm{gas}} = 0.9$ kpc." (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - parameter: [CII] luminosity 1.8e9 Lsun used with alpha_[CII] to get gas mass  
  > "luminosity is 1.8 $\times 10^{9}$ L$_{\odot}$" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - assumption: NFW halo with fixed concentration c = 3.06 (mean at z = 4.22 for halo masses 1e10-1e13 Msun)  
  > "with a concentration parameter of $c = 3.06$" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - assumption: gas disk thickness independent of radius, thin, exponential surface density  
  > "Under the assumptions that the gas of the rotating disk has a thickness that is independent of the radius(cite: iorio), it is thin" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - assumption: alpha_[CII] uniform prior within +/-3 s.d. around mean 30 Msun/Lsun (range 3.8-238)  
  > "we employ a uniform prior in the range corresponding to $\pm3$ standard deviations around the mean value of 30" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - fitting: DYNESTY dynamic nested sampling; log-uniform priors on mass parameters, uniform priors on scale radii; percentiles 16/50/84 from posterior samples  
  > "We compute the Bayesian posterior distribution of these parameters using DYNESTY, a python implementation of the Dynamic Nested Sampling algorithm(cite: dyn)" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter); "We use log-uniform priors for the mass parameters and uniform priors for the scale radii" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - validation: fitting c free (uniform or Gaussian prior) left parameters essentially unchanged; M_star agrees with independent SED value of (9.5±3.0)e9 Msun  
  > "and found that all inferred dynamical parameters do not change significantly with $c$." (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter); "is in excellent agreement with the value of ($9.5 \pm 3.0) \times 10^{9}$ M$_\odot$ found in a recent independent study(cite: deb)" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
6. **Compute the Toomre Q profile**: Q(R) = sigma*kappa/(pi G Sigma_gas) with epicycle frequency from the fitted rotation curve  
  > "using the general definition $Q(R) = (\sigma \kappa )/ (\pi G \Sigma_{\mathrm{gas}})$" (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive magnification, intrinsic L_IR, SFR and gas depletion time from the dust continuum**: pixellated lens reconstruction of the 160 micron rest-frame continuum gives magnification; L_IR,obs/mu gives intrinsic L_IR; SFR from L_IR conversion factor; t_dep = M_gas/SFR  
  > "we perform a pixellated source reconstruction of the dust continuum at 160 $\mu$m (rest frame), which allows us to calculate a total magnification factor" (Dust continuum and SFR); "The gas-depletion timescale is $M_{\mathrm{gas}}$/SFR $= 38\pm9$ Myr." (Dust continuum and SFR)
   - parameter: SFR conversion 1.48e-10 Msun/(yr Lsun), Kroupa IMF  
  > "by applying a conversion factor of $1.48 \times 10^{-10}$ M$_{\odot}$ (yr L$_{\odot}$)$^{-1}$, which is valid for a Kroupa Initial Mass Function(cite: kennicutt)" (Dust continuum and SFR)
   - assumption: source morphology the same at all infrared photometric points, so the 160 micron magnification applies to L_IR; infrared emission is thermal dust heated by young stars  
  > "Under the assumption that the morphology of the source is the same for all photometric points in the infrared band" (Dust continuum and SFR); "By assuming that the infrared emission comes from the thermal emission of dust, heated by the radiation field coming from young stars" (Dust continuum and SFR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Compare with galaxy evolution models, local ETGs and a supernova turbulence budget**: compare V/sigma with TNG50, analytic disk-instability and feedback models; compare mass, size and DM fraction with ATLAS3D early-type galaxies; compare gas kinetic energy with supernova energy injection  
  > "we derived a value of $\sigma = 183_{-42}^{+53}$ km s$^{-1}$, which is a factor of $\sim 6$ higher than our measured value of 32 km s$^{-1}$" (V/sigma in galaxy evolution models); "we determine whether the turbulence driven by supernova explosions can explain the velocity dispersion measured for SPT0418–47" (Velocity dispersion in starburst galaxies)
   - parameter: gas scale height h ~ 500 pc; t_D = h/sigma_m  
  > "where $h$ is the scale height of the gas, assumed to be $\sim 500$ pc." (Velocity dispersion in starburst galaxies)
   - assumption: all observed gas converted into stars for the baryonic (upper-limit) ETG comparison  
  > "calculated under the assumption that all gas is converted into stars" (Comparison with local ETGs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- The rotation curve has a bump near 0.2 kpc and then declines before flattening beyond 1.5 kpc, like a bulge-dominated local spiral  
  > "it has a bump at 0.2 kpc from the galaxy centre and then declines before flattening at radii larger than 1.5 kpc" (Main text)
- The velocity dispersion is well described by an exponential profile, higher in the inner regions than the outer disk  
  > "is well described by an exponential profile, with average values of $\sim$ 45 km s$^{-1}$ in the inner regions" (Main text)
- Central regions are baryon dominated, consistent with local ETGs  
  > "the central regions of SPT0418–47 are dominated by baryons" (Main text)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: flexible rotation-curve function and kinematic-prior source reconstruction chosen to avoid imposing a parametric source  
  > "We choose the above multi-parameter function because it is flexible enough to reproduce the large variety of observed rotation curves" (Lens and kinematic model); "The main advantage of the fiducial approach is that it does not directly impose a parametric model which may not be a good fit to the data." (Direct Forward modelling)
- Alternative: Linear velocity dispersion profile tested and disfavoured (Bayes factor 1.8) relative to exponential  
  > "We also tested a linear function but found it to be significantly less favoured by the data, by a Bayes factor of 1.8 relative to the exponential model." (Lens and kinematic model)
- Alternative: Direct forward modelling with a parametric source as a consistency check  
  > "For a consistency check, we also derived the lens" (Direct Forward modelling)
- Alternative: Free NFW concentration with uniform or Gaussian prior tested; results insensitive  
  > "and found that all inferred dynamical parameters do not change significantly with $c$." (Dynamical model and Toomre parameter)
- How they decided it was enough: checked that the MULTINEST evidences flatten with prior volume  
  > "We then verified that the evidences estimated by MULTINEST flatten as a function of the prior volume." (Lens and kinematic model)
- Limitation (authors): A single system; larger samples are needed to confirm trends in V/sigma with time  
  > "However, larger samples will be needed to confirm this trend." (Beam smearing correction)
- Limitation (authors): The baryonic upper limit ignores gas accretion if no baryons are expelled  
  > "However, we caution that this upper limit does not take into account the accretion of gas if no baryons are expelled by outflows." (Comparison with local ETGs)
- Limitation (authors): Comparison with ionized-gas literature assumes ionized and cold gas kinematics are similar; ionized dispersions can be 10-15 km/s higher  
  > "velocity dispersions measured from ionized tracers tend to be $\sim$ 10 - 15 km s$^{-1}$ higher than those measured from molecular tracers" (V/sigma for the comparison samples)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single archival 21-min execution block; no independent dataset to check the very high V/sigma
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The [CII] velocity dispersion is the smoothed kinematic-model value and depends on the assumed exponential sigma(R) and smooth rotation-curve parametrisation; the 25.7 km/s channel width is comparable to the quoted sigma_ext
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rotation-curve decomposition uses a fixed NFW concentration and a prior-bounded alpha_[CII]; dark matter mass is weakly constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Main code is not public, so the headline numbers cannot be independently reproduced
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Printed numbers are inconsistent in places (V_flat/sigma_ext 13.7 vs 13.5; f_DM 0.095 text vs 0.018 stellar in table)
