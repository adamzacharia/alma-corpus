## Analysis recipe

1. **Select ALMA main-sequence star sample from the archive**: Search ALMA archive science categories for main-sequence stars, remove unknown types and spectroscopic binaries, then keep stars with clean significant detections in JVO first-look images  
  > "In total, publicly available data sets for 104 main sequence stars were identified." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band); "From these, 8 stars with unknown spectral type or luminosity class and 4 stars in spectroscopic binaries were removed." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band); "The final sample of stars with a significant detection in at least one ALMA band consists of 12 sources." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
   - software: Python, VizieR, SIMBAD, ALMA Science Archive, JVO
   - parameter: Detection: flux beyond 3x the RMS of the background sky  
  > "the flux density of the target star was beyond at least three times the root mean square (RMS) flux of the background sky in the image" (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
   - parameter: No nearby or background source within the synthesised beam  
  > "there was no nearby or background source within the synthesised beam or close enough to contaminate the observed stellar flux" (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
   - parameter: Science categories: Debris disks, Exoplanets, Disks around low-mass stars, Disks around high-mass stars, Main-sequence stars  
  > "‘Debris disks’, ‘Exoplanets’, ‘Disks around low-mass stars’, ‘Disks around high-mass stars’, and ‘Main-sequence stars’" (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Contaminated sources identified by beam shape differing from the synthesised beam  
  > "data for many were found to have contamination from nearby sources, which was visible from the shape of the stars, which was significantly different from that of the synthesised beam." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
2. **Measure mm-cm flux densities**: 2D Gaussian fit to each JVO ALMA image; VLA archive data imaged with an automated CASA-based pipeline; published ALMA fluxes preferred where available  
  > "The flux densities of different ALMA bands for each star were determined by fitting a two-dimensional Gaussian function to each image downloaded from the JVO." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band); "ALMA flux values from publications were preferred over values derived from JVO images" (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: two-dimensional Gaussian function fit to each image  
  > "fitting a two-dimensional Gaussian function to each image downloaded from the JVO" (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
   - validation: Published VLA stellar fluxes compared with the pipeline values  
  > "However, the published fluxes from stellar observations with the VLA match our pipeline values quite well." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
3. **Compile IR-EUV SED from catalogues**: Query the CDS SED service by RA/DEC and search radius; for gamma Vir A&B use a smaller radius and split combined fluxes by Teff ratio  
  > "When provided the RA and DEC of a target source and a search radius, the CDS server looks for sources detected within the radius across the different astronomical catalogues and reports their fluxes as a function of frequency." (2.1.2 Archival data at higher frequencies)
   - software: CDS SED service (VizieR)
   - parameter: Default CDS search radius of 5 arcsec  
  > "CDS by default sets a radius of 5" (2.1.2 Archival data at higher frequencies)
   - parameter: For gamma Vir A&B, both stars assumed to contribute to measured fluxes between 10^3 and 5x10^5 GHz; separate fluxes by Teff ratio  
  > "For this frequency range, it is assumed that both stars contribute to the measured fluxes." (2.1.2 Archival data at higher frequencies)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Compute photospheric model SED**: PHOENIX 1D LTE model atmosphere and synthetic spectrum per star using the Husser et al. grid as starting point and rounded literature stellar parameters  
  > "A one-dimensional local thermal equilibrium (LTE) model atmosphere and the corresponding synthetic spectrum were calculated for each star in the sample using the PHOENIX code" (2.2 PHOENIX model atmospheres)
   - software: PHOENIX
   - parameter: PHOENIX version 18.05  
  > "using the current production version of PHOENIX (version 18.05)." (2.2 PHOENIX model atmospheres)
   - parameter: Synthetic spectrum covers 0.01 nm to 3.0 cm; Rayleigh-Jeans tail at low frequencies  
  > "The synthetic spectrum covers a wavelength range from 0.01 nm to 3.0 cm" (2.2 PHOENIX model atmospheres)
   - assumption: No chromospheric temperature rise in the models  
  > "Consequently, none of the models includes a chromospheric temperature rise." (2.2 PHOENIX model atmospheres)
   - assumption: Rounded literature values for stellar parameters  
  > "For the stellar parameters, rounded literature values were used" (2.2 PHOENIX model atmospheres)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Model compared with observed SEDs at IR-UV frequencies, e.g. Delta S/S_mod near 0 at high frequencies for the Sun  
  > "while it remains relatively close to 0 at high frequencies between 10$^3$ GHz and 10$^6$ GHz, where the emission primarily originates from the photosphere." (3.1 Sun as a star)
5. **Quantify excess and brightness temperature**: Define spectral flux excess Delta S/S_mod = (S_obs - S_mod)/S_mod; convert S_nu to T_B with the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation; compute spectral index beta assuming S_obs proportional to nu^beta  
  > "as the ratio of the difference between the observed and the PHOENIX model spectral flux density to the respective model values." (Conclusions and outlook); "Using the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation, the spectral flux density," (Introduction)
   - parameter: T_B conversion uses solid angle Omega_s = pi R*^2 / D^2 (stellar radius and distance)  
  > "The latter is given as $\Omega_s = \pi R_*^2/D^2$" (Introduction)
   - assumption: Mm-cm emission originates from optically thick layers so T_B traces atmospheric temperature stratification  
  > "Because the emission primarily originates from optically thick layers in the mm range" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Results compared with earlier T_B analyses of alpha Cen A/B and Sirius  
  > "confirm the results of" (3.2 Stellar sample)
6. **Compare excess across spectral types**: Examine Delta S/S_mod spectra and Delta S/S_mod versus Teff in ALMA bands 6 and 7 for the sample  
  > "for the stars in the sample for ALMA bands 7 and 6." (4.2 Dependence on spectral type)
   - assumption: Sample chosen without companion or disk contamination, so excess is attributed to the star's own atmosphere  
  > "Because the stellar sample was chosen to have no contamination from any companion or disk in the mm–cm band range, the excess flux can be attributed to the excess thermal and/or non-thermal energy content that is connected to the presence of a stellar chromosphere." (4.2 Dependence on spectral type)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Estimate ALMA detection distance for Sun-like stars**: Scale the solar band 6 brightness temperature to a 3 sigma ALMA sensitivity threshold to find the maximum detection distance  
  > "with the aforementioned detection threshold ALMA will detect a Sun-like star located within $10$ pc." (4.3 Possible extension of the sample size)
   - parameter: Band 6 (240 GHz), all antennas, 1 hour integration gives 3 sigma threshold of about 50 microJy  
  > "An observation in this band using all available antennas of ALMA with full spectral averaging and an integration time of 1 hour can provide a $3 \sigma$ source detection threshold of around $50\,\mu$Jy." (4.3 Possible extension of the sample size)
   - assumption: Solar T_B of 5900 K in band 6  
  > "Assuming a typical temperature of $5900$ K for the Sun in band 6" (4.3 Possible extension of the sample size)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- F-M type stars (Teff 3000-7000 K) show systematically higher S_obs than the photospheric model in the mm-cm range, with Delta S/S_mod rising monotonically toward lower frequency  
  > "exhibits a monotonic rise with decreasing frequency." (abstract)
- A-type stars agree with the photospheric model without a chromosphere  
  > "This demonstrates that the spectral flux densities of A-type stars can be matched with models without a chromosphere, as expected for this stellar type." (Conclusions and outlook)
- The single fully convective star (Proxima Cen) deviates from the trend with a lower excess than expected  
  > "although the value was significantly lower than what would be expected from the apparent trend for the FGKM stars." (Conclusions and outlook)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA is the most sensitive mm array and the only telescope meeting the angular resolution and sensitivity needs in the mm range  
  > "is currently the only telescope that meets the requirements in the mm range." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Interferometric resolution is needed to separate the star from companions and disks, so only clean detections were kept  
  > "Consequently, in order to measure the stellar flux without contamination from a companion or a disk, the angular resolution of an observation must be high enough to separate the star from these potential components." (Introduction)
- Alternative: JVO-image-derived ALMA fluxes were not preferred over published fluxes because the latter come from more refined processing  
  > "ALMA flux values from publications were preferred over values derived from JVO images" (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
- Alternative: Stars with contamination from nearby sources or no/poor detection were excluded from the sample  
  > "In many other cases, there was either no detection of the star itself or data were poor." (2.1.1 Archival data in the mm-cm band)
- How they decided it was enough: The sample is explicitly an initial starting point, complete only for public data to 31 December 2020  
  > "The sample presented in this study is intended to be a starting point to illustrate the potential scientific value of a larger sample." (4.3 Possible extension of the sample size)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar radius, Teff and distance uncertainties cause notable flux uncertainty in the model comparison (gamma Vir)  
  > "uncertainties for the stellar radii, effective temperatures, and to a lesser extent, for their distance, result in a notable flux uncertainty" (3.2 Stellar sample)
- Limitation (authors): Remaining calibration issues in the alpha Cen 230 GHz C2 data point  
  > "The C2 data point at 230 GHz seems to still have some calibration issues" (3.2 Stellar sample)
- Limitation (authors): A single T_B point (GJ 2006 A) cannot support robust conclusions  
  > "it is hard to draw any robust conclusion." (4.2 Dependence on spectral type)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is small and an initial version  
  > "The sample presented in this study is intended to be a starting point to illustrate the potential scientific value of a larger sample." (4.3 Possible extension of the sample size)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 12 stars, so the trend with Teff rests on a few objects with large error bars and mixed epochs/instruments
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fluxes mix ALMA pipeline images, published values and VLA/ATCA data from different epochs; stellar variability and flares are not controlled
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model comparison depends on uncertain literature stellar radii, Teff and distances; no formal error propagation described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): GJ 2006 A excess is quoted as 12000% in the Discussion but about 1100% in the Conclusions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Selection by significant detection biases the sample toward bright, nearby, active stars
