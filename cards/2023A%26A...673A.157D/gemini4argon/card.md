## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract single-pixel spectra and fit emission lines and continuum**: Single-pixel spectral extraction at the quasar position, followed by MCMC fitting of a flat continuum plus a fixed-width Gaussian profile for each targeted line.  
  > "Therefore, we extracted the spectra of the dust continuum and targeted lines in PJ183+05 on a single-pixel basis." (Observations and data reduction); "We model the observed spectra as a flat continuum plus a Gaussian profile for each line." (Spectral fits)
   - software: custom Monte Carlo Markov Chain tool
   - parameter: Fixed Gaussian FWHM = 375 km/s  
  > "Because of the limited S/N of some of the lines in our new data, we opt to assume a fixed Gaussian profile for each transition, with a line full width at half maximum of 375 km s$^{-1}$ as observed for the \[C ii\] line (cite: decarli18)." (Spectral fits)
   - parameter: 3-sigma detection threshold and upper limits  
  > "We consider lines detected if the best fit of the line flux (as inferred from the median value of the posterior distribution) exceeds its 3-$\sigma$ confidence level." (Spectral fits)
   - assumption: All lines have the same Gaussian width of 375 km/s as [C II]  
  > "Because of the limited S/N of some of the lines in our new data, we opt to assume a fixed Gaussian profile for each transition, with a line full width at half maximum of 375 km s$^{-1}$ as observed for the \[C ii\] line (cite: decarli18)." (Spectral fits)
   - fitting: Custom MCMC tool; free parameters: redshift, line normalization, continuum flux density; priors: Gaussian (sigma=0.002) around [C II] redshift, Gaussian for line normalization, Maxwellian for continuum flux density.  
  > "For the fitting, we used our custom Monte Carlo Markov Chain tool to sample the posterior probability of each fitted parameter." (Spectral fits); "The redshift of the line, its normalization, and the underlying continuum flux density are left free in the fit." (Spectral fits); "As priors, we used a Gaussian with a standard deviation of 0.002 around the \[C ii\] redshift, corresponding to $\sim 80$ km s$^{-1}$; a Gaussian distribution for the line normalization (centered on a rough estimate of the line flux based on the line peak and assumed width); and a Maxwellian distribution for the continuum flux density, with a scale width based on the median value of the spectrum in each frequency tuning." (Spectral fits)
   - validation: Refit [C II] line with the same approach for internal consistency  
  > "We note that, for the sake of internal consistency, we refit the \[C ii\] line with the same approach as all of the other lines analyzed in this paper, rather than referring to values reported in the literature." (Spectral fits)
2. **Model the dust continuum SED and derive dust properties, IR luminosity, and optical depth**: Fit a modified black body observed against the CMB and corrected for radiative transfer (optical depth) using MCMC, then integrate over 8-1000 um for L_IR.  
  > "Under the assumption of a single dust temperature, $T_{\rm dust}$, the flux density at a given observed frequency $\nu=\nu_0/(1+z)$ results from the black body emissivity, $B(\nu_0,T_{\rm dust})$ observed against the cosmic microwave background (CMB) at the quasar’s redshift, integrated over the source apparent area, $\Omega_{\rm s}$, and corrected for the effects of radiative transfer:" (Dust continuum modeling); "The IR luminosity is computed by integrating the dust emission model in the range IR luminosity in the 8–1000 $\mu$m wavelength range." (Dust continuum modeling)
   - software: Monte Carlo Markov Chain
   - parameter: T_CMB = 20.27 K at z=6.4386  
  > "The temperature of the CMB is $T_{\rm CMB}$ = 2.725 (1+$z$) K = 20.27 K at the quasar’s redshift." (Dust continuum modeling)
   - parameter: Four free parameters: T_dust, Omega_s, M_dust, beta  
  > "In this framework, the overall dust continuum is uniquely determined by a combination of four parameters: $T_{\rm dust}$, $\Omega_{\rm s}$, $M_{\rm dust}$, and $\beta$, which we determine via a Monte Carlo Markov Chain fit." (Dust continuum modeling)
   - assumption: Single dust temperature T_dust  
  > "Under the assumption of a single dust temperature, $T_{\rm dust}$, the flux density at a given observed frequency $\nu=\nu_0/(1+z)$ results from the black body emissivity, $B(\nu_0,T_{\rm dust})$ observed against the cosmic microwave background (CMB)" (Dust continuum modeling)
   - assumption: Dust emissivity law from Draine (2003) at nu_0 > 1500 GHz and power-law extrapolation kappa(nu_0) = 6.37 (nu_0/1500 GHz)^beta cm^2 g^-1 at lower frequencies  
  > "and $\kappa(\nu_0)$ is the dust emissivity law, for which we adopt an interpolation of the values reported in Table 5 of (cite: draine03) at $\nu_0>1500$ GHz, and a power-law extrapolation $\kappa(\nu_0)=6.37\,(\nu_0/1500\,$GHz$)^\beta \,{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}$ at lower frequencies." (Dust continuum modeling)
   - fitting: MCMC fit with priors: log-normal on T_dust - T_CMB (scale 100 K - T_CMB), Gaussian on Omega_s around 0.45'' x 0.35'' (30% width), log-normal on M_dust centered at 10^8 M_sun (1 dex width), log-normal on beta centered at 1.5 (0.1 dex width).  
  > "As priors, we adopt a log-normal distribution in $\tilde{T}_{\rm dust}=T_{\rm dust}-T_{{\rm CMB}}$ with scale parameter $\tilde{T}_{\rm ref}=$100 K $-T_{{\rm CMB}}$ for the dust temperature" (Dust continuum modeling); "For the angular size, $\Omega_{\rm s}$, we adopt a Gaussian distribution around the spatial extent reported in (cite: venemans20) (deconvolved dust sizes: $0.45''\times0.35'' \approx 2.4\times 1.9$ kpc$^2$). The width of the prior distribution is set to 30$\%$ of the central value." (Dust continuum modeling); "We also adopt a log-normal distribution for the prior on the dust mass, centered around $M_{\rm dust}=10^8$ M$_\odot$ (cite: venemans18) and with a 1-$\sigma$ width of 1 dex; and a log-normal distribution centered on $\beta=1.5$ with a 1-$\sigma$ width of $0.1$ dex (cite: beelen06)." (Dust continuum modeling)
   - validation: Posterior distributions inspected (Fig. 3)  
  > "Fig. [3](#fig_sed_pars) shows the posterior distributions of the fitted parameters." (Dust continuum modeling)
3. **Model emission lines using analytical first-principles prescriptions and Cloudy simulations**: Solve linear statistical equilibrium equations for level populations in ionized gas and run Cloudy photoionization, PDR, and XDR slab models across grids of density, metallicity, and radiation fields.  
  > "In order to put our observations in the context of the physical properties of the interstellar medium in PJ183+05, we derive analytical predictions based on first principles, and complement them with grids of predicted line ratios using the photoionization code Cloudy (cite: ferland98; ferland13; ferland17)." (Emission line modeling); "Namely, we model the ISM as a homogeneous plane-parallel slab of gas exposed to a radiation field." (Cloudy modeling)
   - software: Cloudy
   - parameter: Ionized grid: fixed log U = -2, log n [cm^-3] = [0, +3] (step 0.25 dex), log Z [Z_sun] = [-1, +0.4] (step 0.2 dex), turbulence RMS = 100 km/s, stopping at x_HI = 0.05  
  > "We thus use a fixed log $U$=$-2$ in our analysis." (Cloudy modeling); "We assume that the gas volume density is constant within the cloud, and we sample the line emissivity over a range of $\log n$ \[cm$^{-3}$\] $=[0, +3]$ in steps of $0.25$ dex. We assume ISM and PAH grains. The metallicity[^3] in our grids ranges between $\log Z$ \[Z$_\odot$\] $=[-1, +0.4]$ in steps of 0.2 dex." (Cloudy modeling); "We also include a CMB background computed at the source’s redshift, and a turbulence with an RMS of 100 km s$^{-1}$. We stop the integration when we reach a ionized fraction of $x_{\rm HI}=0.05$." (Cloudy modeling)
   - parameter: PDR/XDR grid: log n [cm^-3] = [2, 6] (0.29 dex spacing), log G_0 = [1, 6] (0.29 dex spacing) or log F_X = [-2.0, 2.0] (0.24 dex spacing), log N_H [cm^-2] = [22, 24] (1 dex spacing)  
  > "For the former, we adopt $\log n$ \[cm$^{-3}$\] = \[2, 6\] (0.29 dex spacing). For the latter, we adopt a range of $\log G_0$ = \[1, 6\] (0.29 dex spacing) for PDRs, and $\log F_{\rm X}$ \[erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$\] = \[-2.0, 2.0\] (0.24 dex spacing) for XDRs." (Cloudy modeling); "The total cloud column density lies in the range $\log N_{\rm H}$ \[cm$^{-2}$\]=\[22, 24\] (1 dex spacing)." (Cloudy modeling)
   - assumption: Constant density throughout each cloud; three distinct components (ionized, neutral, molecular)  
  > "We treat all the emission lines as arising from three distinct components (ionized, neutral, molecular), each described with a simple parametrization (e.g., constant density throughout each cloud)." (Emission line modeling)
   - assumption: Abundance scaling from Nicholls et al. (2017)  
  > "Here, following (cite: nicholls17), for the abundance of carbon, nitrogen, and helium we adopt:" (Cloudy modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-comparison between analytical models and Cloudy predictions (Appendix A)  
  > "Fig. [13](#fig_cii_nii_predict) compares the predicted \[C ii\]$_{158\,\mu{\rm m}}$/\[N ii\]$_{205\,\mu{\rm m}}$ luminosity ratio for the ionized medium based on the analytical model described in sec. [3.3.1](#sec_model) and on the Cloudy models described in sec. [3.3.2](#sec_cloudy), for various assumptions on the gas metallicity Z and temperature $T_{\rm gas}$." (Analytical vs. Cloudy modeling)
4. **Infer [N II] 205 um luminosity, PDR fraction of [C II], electron density, H II volume, and radiation field hardness**: Estimate [N II] 205 um from dust color C(60/100) via Lu et al. (2015), compare with [C II] and [N II] 122 um to derive f([C II]^PDR), electron density n, H II region volume, and compare [O III]/[N II] with Cloudy grids for radiation hardness.  
  > "We estimate the luminosity of the \[N ii\]$_{\rm 205\,\mu m}$ line following the empirical trend presented in (cite: lu15) for a sample of local luminous IR galaxies:" (Inferred [N ii]205 um and the origin of [C ii]); "Using the measured \[N ii\]$_{122\,\mu{\rm m}}$ luminosity from Tab. \[\[tab_lines\]\](#tab_lines), and the inferred \[N ii\]$_{205\,\mu{\rm m}}$ from the previous section, we find a ratio of $\log$\[N ii\]$_{122\,\mu{\rm m}}$/\[N ii\]$_{205\,\mu{\rm m}}$=$0.56_{-0.16}^{+0.23}$, corresponding to a gas density of $\log n$ \[cm$^{-3}$\] $=2.26\pm 0.35$." (Electron density and size of the Hii regions); "Assuming the best-fit value for $n$ from sec. [4.3](#sec_e_dens), we can use the Cloudy models and analytical prescriptions described in sec. [3.3.2](#sec_cloudy) to predict the \[O iii\]$_{88\,\mu{\rm m}}$/\[N ii\]$_{122\,\mu{\rm m}}$ ratio as a function of the hardness of the radiation field, parameterized as the effective temperature of a black body, $T_*$, and of metallicity $Z$." (Hardness of the radiation field and metallicity)
   - software: Cloudy
   - parameter: C(60/100) = 0.84  
  > "In the case of PJ183+05, $C(60/100)=0.84$, yielding $\log L_{\rm [NII]}$ \[L$_\odot$\]$\approx 8.51\pm0.23$, where the uncertainty is dominated by the scatter in the relation from (cite: lu15)." (Inferred [N ii]205 um and the origin of [C ii])
   - assumption: No dust opacity correction applied to fiducial line luminosities; solar metallicity Z = Z_sun and T_gas = 10,000 K for H II volume  
  > "Because of this uncertainty, and due to the modest impact that this correction would have on our final results, we opt not to apply the opacity correction to our fiducial measurements." (Electron density and size of the Hii regions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked sensitivity to alternative abundance ratios (Savage & Sembach 1996 vs Nicholls et al. 2017) and dust opacity corrections

5. **Estimate ionized and molecular gas masses and star formation rates from multiple tracers**: Calculate ionized and molecular gas masses from level populations, dust mass, CO(7-6), [C I], and [C II]^PDR, and compute SFR from [O III], [C II], and IR luminosity using empirical scaling relations.  
  > "Having constrained the excitation conditions of the ionized species examined in our work (Fig. [7](#fig_fsl_ratios)), we can use the information on the relative population of the energy levels to infer the total mass in each ion, and thus the total gas mass via our abundance assumptions." (Star formation rate estimates)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection beyond the local Universe of the H2O 3(3,1)-3(2,2) and 3(0,3)-2(1,2) transitions.  
  > "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that the 3(3,1)–3(2,2) and 3(0,3)–2(1,2) lines have been detected beyond the local Universe." (Water and hydroxyl emission)
- Line velocity profiles across all detected transitions (ionized, neutral, molecular) are consistent at the observed S/N with negligible velocity shifts relative to [C II].  
  > "The velocity profiles of all the detected transitions appear in good agreement at the S/N levels of our observations." (Line profiles and relative velocities); "All the detected lines show negligible velocity shifts along the line of sight compared to the rest frame of the galaxy as defined by the \[C ii\] line, with all the velocity shifts, $\Delta v=c\,\delta z / (1+z)$, significantly smaller than the adopted channel width (90 km s$^{-1}$)." (Line profiles and relative velocities)
- Tentative absorption feature shifted by about -750 km/s in the H2O 3(3,1)-3(2,2), 3(0,3)-2(1,2), and possibly 4(3,2)-4(2,3) transitions.  
  > "We note the presence of a tentative absorption feature shifted by about -750 km s$^{-1}$ in the H$_2$O 3(3,1)-3(2,2), 3(0,3)-2(1,2), and possibly 4(3,2)-4(2,3) lines." (Line profiles and relative velocities)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: PJ183+05 has the highest [C II] luminosity among 27 z>6 quasars surveyed in Decarli et al. (2018) and sits at a redshift (z ~ 6.4) where multiple key FIR lines fall into transparent atmospheric windows.  
  > "The \[C ii\] luminosity in this source is the highest among 27 quasars at $z>6$ surveyed in (cite: decarli18), and one of the highest from non-lensed sources currently known at any redshift (cite: pavesi16; tadaki19; andika20; mitsuhashi21)." (Introduction); "The quasar resides at a redshift that is convenient for observations of various far-infrared emission lines that fall in transparent windows of the atmosphere at $z\approx 6.4$." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting and 90 km/s channel binning were chosen to maximize the S/N of line detections while sampling the ~375 km/s line width in ~4 channels; single-pixel extraction was used because the source is spatially unresolved or marginally resolved at >0.7'' resolution.  
  > "We imaged the measurement sets using natural weighting, in order to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio of line detections." (Observations and data reduction); "The expected line width ($\sim 375$ km s$^{-1}$ from the analysis of the \[C ii\] data) is thus sampled in $\sim4$ independent channels, while at the same time maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio in the lines." (Observations and data reduction); "Two-dimensional channel-by-channel fits of the quasar emission at 88 $\mu$m in the rest-frame revealed that the emission is spatially unresolved or only marginally resolved at $>0.7''$ resolution (cite: venemans20). Therefore, we extracted the spectra of the dust continuum and targeted lines in PJ183+05 on a single-pixel basis." (Observations and data reduction)
- Alternative: Simultaneous radiative transfer modeling of dust and molecular gas (Harrington et al. 2021) was rejected because the CO SLED constraints are too sparse.  
  > "While the method by (cite: harrington21) can in principle be applied here, our observational constraints (in particular concerning the CO spectral line energy distribution) are too sparse to lead to informative results." (Analytical prescriptions from first principles)
- Alternative: Applying dust opacity corrections to fiducial line luminosities was rejected due to unknown line-of-sight geometry between gas and dust and its modest impact on final results.  
  > "However, the actual values of such corrections strongly depend on how the line–emitting gas clouds and the dust are distributed along the line of sight, which is unknown. Because of this uncertainty, and due to the modest impact that this correction would have on our final results, we opt not to apply the opacity correction to our fiducial measurements." (Opacity)
- Alternative: Using [O III]/[C II]^ion instead of [O III]/[N II] to constrain radiation hardness was rejected due to large uncertainties on f([C II]^PDR).  
  > "We opt to focus on the \[O iii\]/\[N ii\] ratio rather than on the \[O iii\]/\[C ii\]$^{\rm ion}$ ratio because of the uncertainties on $f($\[C ii\]$^{\rm PDR})$ discussed in sec. [4.2](#sec_fpdr)." (Hardness of the radiation field and metallicity)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The [O I] 63 um line was scheduled but not observed due to unfavorable weather in Cycle 4.  
  > "The \[O i\] 63 $\mu$m line was also scheduled for observations, but unfavorable weather conditions prevented the completion of the program within Cycle 4." (Observations and data reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Modeling the ISM as three distinct homogeneous constant-density components is a crude approximation that ignores spatial and velocity substructure.  
  > "We treat all the emission lines as arising from three distinct components (ionized, neutral, molecular), each described with a simple parametrization (e.g., constant density throughout each cloud). This is clearly a crude approximation that does not capture the complex structure of the ISM in galaxies." (Emission line modeling)
- Limitation (authors): The PDR fraction of [C II] is highly sensitive to assumed elemental abundances, changing from 0.44 to 0.81 depending on the calibration adopted.  
  > "We point out that this value is highly sensitive to the assumed relative abundances. If we adopt the traditional values from (cite: savage96), we find a much higher $f({\rm [CII]^{PDR}})=0.81$." (Inferred [N ii]205 um and the origin of [C ii])
- Limitation (authors): Sparsity of water and hydroxyl transitions prevents an extensive radiative transfer analysis of H2O and OH.  
  > "However, because of the sparsity of the water and hydroxyl transitions studied here, an extensive analysis of the radiative properties of these molecules is beyond the scope of this work." (Cloudy modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Gas mass and SFR estimates rely on uncertain conversion factors, abundance assumptions, and star-formation timescales.  
  > "While all the mass estimates rely on uncertain conversion factors (cite: dunne21; dunne22; decarli22), all the methods independently converge towards a massive gaseous reservoir in both molecular \[$(0.5-1)\times10^{11}$ M$_\odot$\] and ionized \[$(2-3)\times 10^{10}$ M$_\odot$\] phase." (Masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-pixel spectral extraction across beams ranging from 0.8''x0.7'' (Band 6/8) to 4.0''x3.3'' (Band 3) assumes point-source behavior across all bands; any extended emission beyond ~0.7'' could introduce resolution-dependent aperture bias in line ratios and continuum SED fitting.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The electron density diagnostic relies on [N II] 205 um, which was not actually observed in PJ183+05 but inferred from the empirical LIRG relation of Lu et al. (2015) using the modeled dust color C(60/100).
