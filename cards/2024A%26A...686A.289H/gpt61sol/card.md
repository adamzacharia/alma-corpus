## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract and identify NOEMA lines**: Extract single-point continuum-peak spectra; identify broadband lines with XCLASS lineID.  
  > "The single-point spectra toward the continuum peak" (Observations); "Line identification is first conducted using the lineID function of the eXtended CASA Line Analysis Software Suite (XCLASS) package" (Line identification)
   - software: XCLASS, lineID, ListDatabase
   - parameter: Full broadband windows covering 16 GHz.  
  > "with the full broadband windows covering 16 GHz." (Line identification)
   - parameter: Transition information from CDMS through ListDatabase.  
  > "Column 1-5: The transition information obtained from CDMS using the XCLASS task `ListDatabase`." (Line fitting)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Expose multiple spectral peaks**: Manually select less-contaminated transitions, interpolate to a common grid, normalize by peak flux, and average with inverse-square-noise weights.  
  > "we manually select several transitions that are less contaminated by others." (Stacking of lines); "The spectra are first interpolated using SciPy package (cite: scipy) to the same grid." (Stacking of lines); "Then, the spectra are normalized by the peak flux and averaged with a weight of inverse of square noise" (Stacking of lines)
   - software: SciPy, NumPy
   - assumption: Selected transitions have similar normalized profiles.  
  > "These transitions are selected as they share similar normalized spectrum profiles" (Line stacking)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare candidate transitions with averaged profiles and stacked profiles with individual spectra.  
  > "we compare the averaged profiles with the candidate transition to see if they look similar." (Line stacking); "The stacked profile are broadly similar to the individual spectral profile" (Stacking of lines)
3. **Measure velocities and emitting centers**: Fit single Gaussians to selected narrowband line profiles; fit 2D Gaussians to resampled visibilities channel by channel.  
  > "we conduct Gaussian fitting to the line profiles (narrowband) of each selected transition from the six O-bearing COMs." (Gaussian widths and velocities); "the visibility data is first resampled to a width of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$, and `uv_fit` task in `MAPPING` is used to conduct the 2D Gaussian fit for each channel." (Spatial distribution of COM emission)
   - software: MAPPING, uv_fit
   - parameter: Visibility channel width: 0.5 km s−1.  
  > "the visibility data is first resampled to a width of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$" (Spatial distribution of COM emission)
   - parameter: Accept uv-plane points with S/N>7 and position uncertainty <0.5 arcsec.  
  > "we only consider those data points with S/N$>$7 and position uncertainty $<0.5\ensuremath{''}$." (Spatial distribution of COM emission)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian spectral profiles and channel-by-channel 2D Gaussian visibility fits.  
  > "the best-fit line widths and the central velocities of these spectral-line profiles with a single Gaussian." (Gaussian widths and velocities); "`uv_fit` task in `MAPPING` is used to conduct the 2D Gaussian fit for each channel." (Spatial distribution of COM emission)
   - validation: Compare emitting-center distributions across transitions of different energies and opacities.  
  > "although the transitions have different upper energy levels and opacities" (Spatial distribution of COM emission); "the spatial distributions are similar." (Spatial distribution of COM emission)
4. **Estimate single-component physical conditions**: Fit selected high-resolution transitions with a five-parameter LTE radiative-transfer model; remove contaminated transitions or mask overlapping velocities.  
  > "We construct the LTE-radiative transfer model to fit the line profiles of the selected transitions." (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "The model includes five free parameters which are column density ($N_{\rm tot}$), excitation temperature ($T_{\rm ex}$), central velocity ($V_{\rm LSR}$), linewidth ($\Delta V$), and source size ($\Omega_S$)." (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "Line contamination can also be treated by masking the specific, overlapping velocity ranges so that more transitions can be included" (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
   - software: SciPy, curve_fit, emcee
   - parameter: Observed intensities scaled to a 1.2 arcsec × 0.7 arcsec beam.  
  > "$\Omega_B=1.2\ensuremath{''}\times0.7\ensuremath{''}$ as we first scale the intensity of the observed spectra into this beam size for convenience." (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
   - parameter: One intrinsic opacity linewidth shared by all transitions.  
  > "We note here the linewidth is set to a free parameter as one value for all transitions." (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
   - assumption: LTE.  
  > "We use the LTE approach as the collisional rate coefficients of COMs are only available in a few COMs" (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
   - assumption: Tbg=2.7 K.  
  > "$T_{\rm bg}=2.7$K." (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
   - fitting: SciPy curve_fit initialization; emcee MCMC with uniform priors; 16%, 50%, and 84% quantiles.  
  > "We conduct the fitting using the Scipy `curve_fit` method" (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "`emcee` (cite: fo13) is used to perform MCMC sampling using the parameters from `curve_fit` estimates with uniform distribution for priors." (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "The fitted parameters and their uncertainties are taken with relevant 16%, 50%, and 84% quantiles (cite: ho18)." (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
   - validation: Check agreement with broadband XCLASS modeling and evaluate optical-depth coverage versus upper-state energy.  
  > "the final (one-velocity-component) model of the high-spectral resolution data is broadly consistent with the XCLASS model considering more channels but fewer transitions." (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "Figure [17](#fig:trans) shows the $\tau$ of the best-fit model as a function of E$_{\rm u}$ for the transitions in use." (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "We use this figure to evaluate the the fitting results." (One-component LTE Gaussian model)
5. **Decompose kinematic components**: Add Gaussian LTE components to the same selected spectra and masks; select component count using AIC and inspect unreasonable components.  
  > "The multiple-velocity-component model setup is the same as that of the one-component model" (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model); "To decide how many velocity components are needed to fit the line emission, we employ the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC)" (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model); "We stop adding additional components when the $\chi^2$ does not significantly improve comparing with the previous iteration and/or the fitting found an unreasonable component." (Line fitting)
   - software: SciPy, curve_fit, emcee
   - parameter: Five additional free parameters per component: Tex, log Ntot, ΔV, VLSR, size.  
  > "one adds five additional free parameters ( $T_{\rm ex}$, $\log N_{\rm tot}$, $\Delta V$, $V_{\rm LSR}$, size) for each additional velocity component added to the model." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model)
   - parameter: AIC=2k+χ²+C; adding one component requires χ² to decrease by 10 to compensate AIC.  
  > "$AIC=2k+\chi^2+C$" (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model); "Thus, by adding one velocity component, $\chi^2$ needs to decrease by 10 to compensate AIC." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model)
   - assumption: Components are spatially separated and do not interact.  
  > "We assume these components are spatially separated and do not interact." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model)
   - fitting: curve_fit for component-count selection; emcee Stretch Moves with uniform priors, tens of walkers, and initialization near the best fit.  
  > "This progress to decide the number of velocity components is done using gradient based optimization (Scipy `curve_fit`) with less computational expense than MCMC method." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model); "with uniform priors; tens of walkers (depending on the $k$) were running simultaneously using the “Stretch Moves” in `emcee`(cite: go10; fo13)." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model); "The initial values are set to the nearby the best-fit model from `curve_fit`." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model)
   - validation: Inspect selected high-S/N transitions with different upper energies and reject components contributing only at the noise level with extreme parameters.  
  > "two selected transitions which have different upper energy levels and relatively high S/N among the transitions in use for fitting." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model); "We stop adding a new component at the 2-component model because the 3rd component contributes emission at about the noise level with an extreme high optical depth" (Line fitting)
6. **Check NOEMA components against ALMA**: Compare fitted velocities, linewidths, and sizes with ALMA PV diagrams along an east-west cut through VLA4A and VLA4B.  
  > "the PV cut is taken along the east-west direction through VLA4A and VLA4B" (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data); "Here we compare the best-fit $V_{\rm LSR}$, $\Delta V$, and sizes with the PV diagrams." (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data)
   - parameter: Ellipse width, height, and x-axis location represent ΔV, source size, and VLSR; y offsets adjusted by eye.  
  > "The width, height, and x-axis location correspond to the $\Delta V$, source sizes, and $V_{\rm LSR}$." (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data); "The y-axis offsets of ellipses are manually adjusted by eyes." (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data)
   - parameter: Ellipse height: (size²+beamEW²)^(1/2).  
  > "The height of the ellipse is given by $({\rm size}^2+{\rm beam_{EW}}^2)^{1/2}$" (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Relatively good match between NOEMA components and ALMA structures; unresolved broad components provide only size upper limits.  
  > "each component from the lower-spatial-resolution NOEMA data is a relatively good match of the components seen in the high-spatial-resolution ALMA data" (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data); "the ALMA data do not resolve these components and only provide an upper limit to their source size." (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA data independently evaluate NOEMA fitted velocities, linewidths, and source sizes.  
  > "here we further evaluate the fitting results with high-angular-resolution data." (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data); "We compare the central velocity, linewidth, and source size from the best-fit with the high-angular-resolution data." (Comparison with high-angular-resolution data)
- Why this method: LTE addresses limited collision coefficients; multiple velocity components separate complex spectral structures.  
  > "We use the LTE approach as the collisional rate coefficients of COMs are only available in a few COMs" (One-component LTE Gaussian model); "Therefore, we perform multiple velocity components fitting to disentangle the physical conditions traced by the line profiles." (Multi-velocity component LTE Gaussian model)
- Alternative: Extended outflow cavity walls are disfavored by compact emitting sizes.  
  > "The small source sizes show that the emission unlikely arise from outflow cavity walls." (Conclusion)
- Alternative: Heating only by the central protostar is disfavored.  
  > "This complex structure is unlikely caused only by heating of the central protostar." (Conclusion)
- Alternative: Disk self-gravity or tidal forces could also produce shocks.  
  > "Other possibilities are shocks produced by the self-gravity or tidal forces within the disks themselves." (Origin of COM emission in SVS13A)
- Alternative: Optical-depth effects alone are disfavored for the different molecular distributions.  
  > "This suggests that the discrepancy seen in the COM distribution most likely originate from chemical, rather than optical depth effects." (Kinematics)
- How they decided it was enough: Stop adding components when χ²/AIC improvement becomes insignificant or an unreasonable component appears.  
  > "We stop adding additional components when the $\chi^2$ does not significantly improve comparing with the previous iteration and/or the fitting found an unreasonable component." (Line fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Ignoring interactions between overlapping components can make compact broad-component physical conditions inaccurate.
