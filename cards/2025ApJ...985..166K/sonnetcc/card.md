## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce the JCMT C18O (2-1) map**: Starlink reduction, conversion of antenna temperature to main beam temperature, spectral binning  
  > "The obtained data were reduced with `Starlink` version 2023A." (2.1 JCMT); "The antenna temperature was converted to the main beam temperature ($T_\mathrm{mb}$) with the main beam efficiency of 0.66." (2.1 JCMT)
   - software: Starlink 2023A
   - parameter: Main beam efficiency 0.66  
  > "with the main beam efficiency of 0.66" (2.1 JCMT)
   - parameter: Cube binned to 91.6 kHz (0.125 km/s) channels; rms 0.31 K per channel in Tmb  
  > "The image cubes were binned to a channel width of 91.6 kHz, corresponding to 0.125 km s$^{-1}$." (2.1 JCMT); "The rms noise level of 0.31 K per channel" (2.1 JCMT)
   - parameter: Raster scan of a 120'' x 120'' region  
  > "We mapped a square region of 120$\ensuremath{''}$$\times$120$\ensuremath{''}$ around the protostar with the raster scan mode." (2.1 JCMT)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Calibrate and image the ALMA 12-m + 7-m C18O data**: Pipeline calibration, mstransform extraction of C18O, uvcontsub continuum subtraction, joint tclean of all data sets with mosaic gridder  
  > "All the data were calibrated with the pipeline of the Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA) package" (2.2 ALMA); "All the C$^{18}$O data were imaged together using the task `tclean` of CASA version 6.6" (2.2 ALMA)
   - software: CASA 6.6, mstransform, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust 2.0, mosaic gridder  
  > "with the Briggs robust parameter of 2.0 and the mosaic gridder" (2.2 ALMA)
   - parameter: Velocity resolution 0.167 km/s  
  > "The velocity resolution was set to 0.167 km s$^{-1}$." (2.2 ALMA)
   - parameter: Auto-masking with sidelobe threshold 1.5, noise threshold 4.25, low noise threshold 1.0, minimum beam fraction 0.3, negative threshold 0.0  
  > "The auto-masking function of clean was adopted with the sidelobe threshold of 1.5, noise threshold of 4.25, low noise threshold of 1.0, minimum beam fraction of 0.3, and negative threshold of 0.0." (2.2 ALMA)
   - parameter: Primary beam correction applied; final beam 0.30''x0.22'', rms 1.35 mJy/beam  
  > "A primary beam correction was applied." (2.2 ALMA); "The rms noise level of the final image cube was measured to be 1.35 mJy beam$^{-1}$ ($=$ 0.52 K)" (2.2 ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 12-m-only images made with different auto-mask parameters to test for artifacts and missing flux  
  > "Thus, there is a caution of potential artifacts, when discussing streamer-like structures using the 12-m array only data." (Channel maps of the 12-m array data)
3. **Combine JCMT and ALMA by feathering**: Regrid JCMT cube to the ALMA axes, multiply by the ALMA primary beam, feather, then divide by the primary beam  
  > "The JCMT cube was first regridded with `imregrid` to have the same spatial and velocity axes as the ALMA cube and multiplied by the primary beam pattern of the ALMA map created in Section" (2.3 JCMT and ALMA combined map); "Then the ALMA and JCMT maps were combined using the `feather` task in CASA (cite: 2017Cotton; 2023Plunkett)." (2.3 JCMT and ALMA combined map)
   - software: CASA imregrid, CASA immath, CASA feather
   - parameter: Feathered cube beam 0.30''x0.22'', rms 1.46 mJy/beam (0.56 K)  
  > "The rms noise level in the combined map was measured to be 1.46 mJy beam$^{-1}$ ($=$ 0.56 K)" (2.3 JCMT and ALMA combined map)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Integrated C18O spectrum over the ALMA FOV from the feathered cube matches the JCMT-only spectrum  
  > "The spectrum of the feathered image coincides with that of the JCMT-only image, showing that the flux is properly recovered with the feathered procedure." (3.4 Influence of the Missing Flux)
4. **Estimate the dense core mass from JCMT C18O**: LTE C18O column density and mass from the JCMT moment 0 map (v = 4.0-6.0 km/s) within a box of about 9000 x 9000 au  
  > "We estimate the C$^{18}$O column density under the assumption of the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) condition." (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass); "integrated intensity (moment 0) map of the C$^{18}$O emission over the velocity range of $V_\mathrm{LSR}$ $=$ 4.0–6.0 km s$^{-1}$" (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass)
   - parameter: Excitation temperature 15 K; mean molecular weight 2.8; C18O/H2 = 1.7e-7  
  > "The excitation temperature is assumed to be 15 K, which is the average dust temperature in the envelope on a 60$\ensuremath{''}$ scale" (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass); "The mean molecular weight of 2.8 (cite: 2008Kauffmann)" (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass)
   - assumption: C18O emission optically thin  
  > "the C$^{18}$O emission is most likely optically thin." (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass)
   - assumption: LTE with Tex equal to the 15 K dust temperature; Tex of 10 or 30 K raises the mass by 15%  
  > "If the excitation temperature is assumed to be 10 or 30 K, the estimated core mass becomes 15% higher." (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with earlier dust-continuum masses of 0.6-1.9 Msun  
  > "Our estimate is comparable to the previous estimates of 0.6–1.9 $M_{\odot}$ with radii of 1000-5000 au" (3.1.1 Morphology and Mass)
5. **Measure core velocity gradient, line widths and non-thermal motion**: Goodman-style velocity gradient from the moment 1 map; single-Gaussian fits to JCMT spectra with S/N > 3; thermal width subtracted to get non-thermal FWHM and Mach number  
  > "We fit these spectra with a single Gaussian function." (3.1.2 Velocity Structures); "The fitting is performed for those spectra with a peak signal-to-noise ratio greater than 3." (3.1.2 Velocity Structures); "We estimate the non-thermal gas motion by subtracting the thermal components from the measured C$^{18}$O linewidth" (3.1.2 Velocity Structures)
   - parameter: Moment 1 integrates emission above 3 sigma  
  > "integrating the emission above 3$\sigma$" (3.1.2 Velocity Structures)
   - parameter: North/south regions split at declination offset 0''  
  > "based on the declination offset of 0$''$" (3.1.2 Velocity Structures)
   - assumption: Uniform gas kinetic temperature of 15 K, range 10-30 K for the uncertainty  
  > "We assume a uniform gas kinetic temperature of 15 K to derive the non-thermal gas motion" (3.1.2 Velocity Structures)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per spectrum; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "We fit these spectra with a single Gaussian function." (3.1.2 Velocity Structures)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Estimate B-field strength and mass-to-flux ratio**: DCF method and the Skalidis formulation using the non-thermal line width, the POL-2 angular dispersion and mean density; mass-to-flux ratio from mean surface density  
  > "We adopt the Davis-Chandrasekhar-Fermi (DCF) method (cite: 1951Davis; 1953Chandrasekhar)" (3.1.3 B-field strength); "The dimensionless mass-to-flux ratio ($\mu_\mathrm{pos}$) of the dense core on the plane of sky is derived as" (3.1.3 B-field strength)
   - parameter: sigma_NT 0.18-0.20 km/s; sigma_theta 7-13 deg; xi = 0.5; mean density (6.1-7.1)e-20 g cm-3  
  > "and it is 0.18–0.20 km s$^{-1}$" (3.1.3 B-field strength); "(6.1–7.1)$\times$10$^{-20}$ g cm$^{-3}$" (3.1.3 B-field strength)
   - assumption: Core depth equals its 850 um continuum diameter of 140''  
  > "we assume the depth of the dense core to also be 140$\ensuremath{''}$" (3.1.3 B-field strength)
   - assumption: Surface density from core plus central protostellar mass within a 60'' box  
  > "divided by the core area within a box of 60$\ensuremath{''}$ from the protostar" (3.1.3 B-field strength)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Two formulations (DCF and Skalidis) compared  
  > "the magnetic field strength in the dense core is estimated to be 23–37 $\mu$G" (3.1.3 B-field strength)
7. **Identify streamers and compare with UCM infall trajectories**: Inspect ALMA-only channel maps and moment 0 maps, overplot UCM free-fall trajectories, and check position-velocity diagrams cut along the trajectories  
  > "Blue and red lines in Figure [4](#fig_almamom0) represent the free-fall trajectories coincident with the elongated structures." (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers); "Figure [5](#fig_streamerpv) presents the Position–Velocity (PV) diagrams of the elongated structures cut along the model infalling trajectories." (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers)
   - software: CASA, PVextractor
   - parameter: NW and NNW: i_s = 107 deg, theta_s = -45 deg, r_d = 55 au, M* = 0.14 Msun fixed; theta_0, phi_0 free  
  > "we fix *i*$_\mathrm{s}$ $=$ 107$\ensuremath{^\circ}$, $\theta_\mathrm{s}$ $=$ $-$45$\ensuremath{^\circ}$, $r_\mathrm{d}$ $=$ 55 au, and $M_*$ $=$ 0.14 $M_\odot$" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers)
   - parameter: Streamer edges set at 3 sigma in moment 0 maps  
  > "We set the 3$\sigma$ emissions in the moment 0 maps (Figure [4](#fig_almamom0)) as the edge of the streamers." (3.2.2 Mass infalling rates of streamers)
   - assumption: NW and NNW streamers share the disk rotation axis and have centrifugal radius equal to the gas disk radius  
  > "we assume that their rotational axes are aligned with the disk and that their centrifugal radii are the same as the gas disk radius" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers)
   - fitting: Visual comparison of all combinations of theta_0 and phi_0, no sampler or likelihood  
  > "We visually inspect and compare the observed structures with the model trajectories of all combinations of the parameters" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers)
   - validation: PV diagrams follow the UCM velocities; alternative parameter sets also reproduce the data (degeneracy)  
  > "These velocity structures are consistent with the UCM model." (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers); "We note that these solutions are not unique because of the projection effect." (4.1 Degeneracy of the UCM model)
8. **Derive streamer masses, lengths, free-fall times and mass infall rates**: Moment 0 integrated over each streamer velocity range; LTE C18O mass as for the core; 3D length from the UCM trajectory; infall rate = mass / free-fall time  
  > "Then we calculate the integrated C$^{18}$O flux of the streamer in its moment 0 map and estimated its mass with the same method" (3.2.2 Mass infalling rates of streamers); "the mass infalling rates are estimated to be" (3.2.2 Mass infalling rates of streamers)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Velocity ranges NE 3.6-4.7, NW -0.1-4.9, NNW 5.2-6.8 km/s  
  > "The NE, NW, and NNW streamers are detected at velocities of $V_\mathrm{LSR}$ $=$ 3.6–4.7 km s$^{-1}$, $-$0.1–4.9 km s$^{-1}$, and 5.2–6.8 km s$^{-1}$, respectively" (3.2.2 Mass infalling rates of streamers)
   - parameter: Free-fall time t_ff = sqrt(R^3 / 2GM*)  
  > "($t_\mathrm{ff}$ $=$ $\sqrt{{R^3} / {2GM_*}}$)" (3.2.2 Mass infalling rates of streamers)
   - assumption: C18O abundance (1-2)e-7 and excitation temperature 10-30 K give the mass uncertainty  
  > "The uncertainties originate from the possible ranges of the C$^{18}$O abundance of (1–2)$\times$10$^{-7}$ (cite: 2022GOng) and the excitation temperature of 10–30 K." (3.2.2 Mass infalling rates of streamers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Conservative upper limit on the infall rate from the plane-of-sky length (~1300 au); mass recomputed from feathered data is 20-50% higher  
  > "This approach yields mass infalling rates of 1 to 2$\times$10$^{-7}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$" (4.1 Degeneracy of the UCM model); "the estimated mass becomes 20% to 50% higher than the ALMA-only data" (3.4 Influence of the Missing Flux)
9. **Test infall of the extended envelope with a kinematical model**: UCM infalling-rotating envelope with embedded flared Keplerian disk, RADMC-3D C18O LTE image cube, convolved to the feathered beam, PV cuts along disk major and minor axes compared to the feathered data  
  > "We then perform radiative transfer calculations using RADMC-3D (cite: 2012Dullemond) and generate a model C$^{18}$O image cube of our kinematical model." (3.3 Infalling protostellar envelope observed with ALMA and JCMT); "The model images are convolved with a Gaussian beam of the same size as that of our feathered map of the C$^{18}$O emission." ## Qualitative findings

- Three prominent elongated structures (NE, NW, NNW) lie on the northern side and are consistent with infalling streamers rather than outflows  
  > "These elongated structures are unlikely to be associated with outflows because their velocity structures are different from those of typical outflows" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers); "Therefore, these elongated structures most likely trace infalling streamers toward the protostar." (4.2 Mass accretion via streamers)
- The feathered cube shows extended emission over the field of view; PV diagrams are dominated by the extended infalling envelope with only a minor streamer contribution  
  > "There is only a minor contribution from the streamers in the PV diagrams." (3.3 Infalling protostellar envelope observed with ALMA and JCMT); "This suggests that the streamers comprise only a portion of the entire protostellar envelope." (3.3 Infalling protostellar envelope observed with ALMA and JCMT)
- The dense core is supercritical  
  > "suggesting that the dense core of IRAS 16544 is supercritical" (3.1.3 B-field strength)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JCMT, FAUST and eDisk data have overlapping uv coverage and give a large spatial dynamic range  
  > "we utilize the unique combination of the JCMT, FAUST, and eDisk data that have overlapping *uv* coverages and result in a large spatial dynamical range" (Introduction)
- Why this method: UCM model is commonly used to describe infalling material kinematics, so it is used for streamer trajectories and the envelope model  
  > "which is often called the UCM model and adopted to describe the kinematics of infalling material" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers)
- Alternative: Outflow origin of the elongated structures rejected (velocity structure differs from typical outflows), though the redshifted NW structure may partly trace outflow  
  > "These elongated structures are unlikely to be associated with outflows because their velocity structures are different from those of typical outflows" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers); "it is possible that the redshifted elongated structure traces parts of the molecular outflow" (3.2.1 Spatial and velocity structures of streamers)
- Alternative: Alternative UCM parameter sets reproduce the observed streamers equally well  
  > "We note that these solutions are not unique because of the projection effect." (4.1 Degeneracy of the UCM model)
- Alternative: Stellar flyby as streamer origin judged unlikely  
  > "Therefore, it is unlikely that a flyby encounter has occurred in IRAS 16544." (4.3 Formation mechanisms of streamers)
- Alternative: Cloudlet capture as streamer origin judged unlikely  
  > "Therefore, the streamers in IRAS 16544 are unlikely to be caused by the accretion from passing cloudlets." (4.3 Formation mechanisms of streamers)
- Alternative: Core-core or core-filament collision judged unlikely  
  > "Hence, there is no sign of collision between the dense core of IRAS 16544 and another component." (4.3 Formation mechanisms of streamers)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): UCM trajectories are degenerate, so 3D lengths and infall rates are uncertain by up to a factor of four  
  > "the mass infalling rates estimated with these different trajectories only vary within a factor of four" (4.1 Degeneracy of the UCM model)
- Limitation (authors): More, unidentified streamers may exist  
  > "We note that there could be more, unidentified streamers existing in the dense core of IRAS 16544." (4.2 Mass accretion via streamers)
- Limitation (authors): Axisymmetric envelope model does not fully explain the intensity distribution  
  > "the observed intensity distribution cannot be fully explained by our axisymmetric envelope model" (3.3 Infalling protostellar envelope observed with ALMA and JCMT)
- Limitation (authors): JCMT resolution could smooth out 1000 au cloudlets  
  > "could smooth out 1000 au sized cloudlets, if any" (4.3 Formation mechanisms of streamers)
- Limitation (authors): 12-m-only data may contain artifacts at low velocities  
  > "there is a caution of potential artifacts, when discussing streamer-like structures using the 12-m array only data." (Channel maps of the 12-m array data)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Streamer-to-envelope comparison mixes a short free-fall time estimate (mass / t_ff) with a core-scale infall estimate from the JCMT mass at 4000 au; the two are not derived consistently
- Caveat (inferred, ours): UCM trajectory parameters were picked by eye with no goodness-of-fit metric or uncertainty on theta_0 and phi_0
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-line optically thin LTE C18O mass with fixed Tex = 15 K and abundance 1.7e-7; CO depletion and temperature gradients are not treated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Feathered-data masses are 20-50% higher than adopted ALMA-only masses, which shifts the 2% mass fraction
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The JCMT beam (21'') averages over the core, so the 2% and 6% fractions depend on the box chosen
