## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate the visibilities**: ALMA pipeline calibration with observatory scripts in CASA; no extra calibration; uv data exported to MIRIAD  
  > "The calibration was made in CASA (v6.4.1.12; (cite: McMullin07)) using the reduction scripts provided by the ALMA observatory." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2)); "The calibrated $uv$ data produced by the pipeline were exported to MIRIAD (cite: Sault95) for the further analysis." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - software: CASA, MIRIAD
   - parameter: Flux/bandpass calibrator J2253+1608  
  > "The source J2253+1608 was used as bandpass, flux, and atmospheric calibrator for the observations of both transitions." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - assumption: Flux error taken as 10% because the flux calibrator varied  
  > "To be on the safe side, we therefore assumed that the error on the derived fluxes is 10%." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Pipeline products judged of sufficient quality  
  > "The visibility products resulting from the ALMA pipeline were found to be of sufficient quality" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
2. **model and remove CO absorption against the N lobe from the uv data**: Make high-resolution uniform-weighted cubes at native velocity resolution, run clean only where absorption is seen, and subtract the negative clean components from the uv data before making the low-velocity-resolution cubes  
  > "we therefore only used the deconvolution task clean on these high-resolution cubes at the location where absorption was seen and subtracted these negative clean components from the original $uv$ data sets" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - software: MIRIAD
   - parameter: Native-resolution cubes of 3.4 km/s (CO(1-0)) and 1.7 km/s (CO(2-1)), uniform weighting  
  > "we also made CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) data cubes at the original velocity resolution of the observations (3.4 $\,$km$\,$s$^{-1}$ and 1.7 $\,$km$\,$s$^{-1}$ respectively" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Absorption detected at high significance in the high-resolution cubes, while most CO emission falls below the noise there  
  > "most of the CO emission is below the noise level, while the absorption is detected at high significance" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
3. **make continuum-subtracted line cubes**: Bin channels to 16.8 km/s, Hanning smooth to 33.6 km/s, natural weighting, subtract a per-pixel straight-line continuum fit in the image domain, clean to half the noise with masks from a 2 sigma threshold on a cube smoothed to twice the resolution, then de-redshift with z = 0.32188  
  > "The continuum was subtracted in the image domain by fitting for every position a straight line through the line-free channels and subtracting this fit from the observed spectrum." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2)); "After cleaning, the CO(1-0) and CO(3-2) data cubes were de-redshifted using $z = 0.32188$." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - software: MIRIAD
   - parameter: Velocity resolution 33.6 km/s to match the CO(2-1) cube; natural weighting  
  > "We made them using natural weighting." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - assumption: Narrowest spectra have velocity dispersion of about 25 km/s, so the binning still resolves them  
  > "The binning improves the S/N of the emission in the channel maps while still resolving the individual spectra" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **match resolution and grids across the three transitions**: Convolve CO(1-0) and CO(3-2) cubes to the CO(2-1) beam and regrid to the CO(2-1) spatial and velocity grids  
  > "we convolved the CO(1-0) and CO(3-2) cubes to the same spatial resolution as the CO(2-1) cube (0.45$''$ $\times$ 0.34$''$ $\equiv 2.1 \times 1.6$ kpc)" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - software: MIRIAD
   - parameter: Common beam 0.45 x 0.34 arcsec  
  > "(0.45$''$ $\times$ 0.34$''$ $\equiv 2.1 \times 1.6$ kpc)" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **build moment images with a common mask**: Run SoFiA-2 on the CO(2-1) cube and apply the same mask to the CO(1-0) and CO(3-2) cubes to make moment images  
  > "The moment-0 images were obtained by first running the SoFia-2 source-finding software (cite: Westmeier21) on the CO(2-1) data cube" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2)); "we subsequently applied the same mask generated by Sofia-2 on the cubes of the two other transitions to make the moment images" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - software: SoFiA-2
   - assumption: CO(2-1) cube used as benchmark because its typical S/N is highest; separate masks would integrate different velocity ranges and bias line ratios  
  > "We chose the CO(2-1) cube as the benchmark because the typical S/N of the emission is highest in this data cube." (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **derive line-ratio images and the central CO(1-0) limit**: Construct brightness-temperature ratio maps R32 = I(3-2)/I(2-1); for the undetected centre, take a 3 sigma CO(1-0) upper limit assuming the CO(3-2) line width, giving a lower limit on R21  
  > "the distribution of the brightness temperature ratio $R_{\rm 32}\equiv I_{\rm 3-2}/I_{\rm 2-1}$" (3.3 Line ratios); "We estimated an upper limit to the CO(1-0) flux by assuming the same line width as for CO(3-2)" (3.3 Line ratios)
   - parameter: 3 sigma CO(1-0) flux integral limit 39 mJy km/s for the central region  
  > "this gives a 3$\sigma$ upper limit to the CO(1-0) flux integral of the central region of 39 mJy $\,$km$\,$s$^{-1}$" (3.3 Line ratios)
   - assumption: Same line width for CO(1-0) as CO(3-2) in the centre  
  > "We estimated an upper limit to the CO(1-0) flux by assuming the same line width as for CO(3-2)" (3.3 Line ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with typical ratios of star-forming galaxies from the literature  
  > "ranges between 0.2 and 0.6 (16% – 84% range; (cite: Leroy22))" (3.3 Line ratios)
7. **estimate the molecular gas mass**: Convert CO luminosity to H2 mass with an alpha_CO and R21 chosen to be consistent with the observed line ratio  
  > "gives an H$_2$ mass of $M_{\rm mol} = 6.2 \times 10^{9}$ $M_\odot$ for a conversion factor $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ typical of ULIRGs" (3.3 Line ratios)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 0.89 (ULIRG-like) with R21 = 1; alternative Galactic alpha_CO = 4.3 gives upper limit  
  > "typical of ULIRGs, $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 0.89" (3.3 Line ratios); "all the gas had a typical Galactic $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 4.3" (3.3 Line ratios)
   - assumption: R21 = 1 as a compromise between large radii and the central part  
  > "and $R_{21}=1$. The latter represents a compromise between the value measured at large radii and the value in the central part." (3.3 Line ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **estimate outflow mass, rate and kinetic power**: Upper limit on outflow mass from the CO(1-0) limit; Mdot ~ M_out x v_out / r_out; kinetic power Ekin = 0.5 Mdot (v_out^2 + 3 sigma^2)  
  > "The mass outflow rate can be estimated using $\dot{M} \sim M_{\rm out} \times v_{\rm out} /r_{\rm out}$" (4.2 The energetics of the near-nuclear outflow); "$\dot{E}_{\rm kin} = \frac{1}{2}\dot{M}(v^2_{\rm out}+3\sigma^2)$" (4.2 The energetics of the near-nuclear outflow)
   - parameter: v_out between 200 and 300 km/s (300 for the limit); r_out about 1 kpc (one beam); sigma = 85 km/s  
  > "We took $v_{\rm out}$ to be in the range between 200 and 300 $\,$km$\,$s$^{-1}$" (4.2 The energetics of the near-nuclear outflow); "A conservative estimate for the radial extent $r_{\rm out}$ is about 1 kpc, that is, about one beam." (4.2 The energetics of the near-nuclear outflow)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 0.89 for the outflow mass; true outflow mass assumed not much lower than the upper limit  
  > "we suspect that the true mass of the molecular outflow is not much lower than our upper limit" (4.2 The energetics of the near-nuclear outflow)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the warm ionised outflow rate of about 1 Msun/yr from the literature and with the jet power  
  > "the mass outflow rate for the molecular gas is much higher than the $\dot{M} \sim 1$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ measured for the warm ionised gas component" (4.2 The energetics of the near-nuclear outflow)
9. **characterise the CO absorption against the N lobe**: Extract optical-depth profiles from the high-resolution CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) cubes, fit Gaussians, derive excitation temperature from the optical-depth ratio and H2 column density  
  > "The profiles are extracted from the high spatial- and velocity resolution CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) cubes, given the small velocity width of the absorption." (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe); "The results from Gaussian fits to the CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) profiles are given in Table [1](#tab:absFit)." (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe)
   - parameter: CO abundance relative to H2 of 1e-4; Tex = 5 K  
  > "Using $T_{\rm ex}$ = 5 K and a CO abundance relative to H$_2$ of $10^{-4}$" (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe)
   - assumption: Optical-depth ratios are very uncertain because of different continuum sizes at different frequencies and cloud structure  
  > "make the interpretation of optical depths ratios very uncertain" (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits to the optical-depth profiles (priors/sampler not stated)  
  > "The results from Gaussian fits to the CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) profiles are given in Table [1](#tab:absFit)." (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe)
   - validation: Compared with column densities seen in other radio galaxies  
  > "This is in the range of column densities observed by (cite: Rose24) in a number of radio galaxies." (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe)
10. **measure continuum fluxes and spectral indices**: Integrated flux of core, N lobe and SE lobe at 87, 174 and 262 GHz from the continuum images; spectral index alpha (S proportional to nu^alpha) from these fluxes  
  > "give the integrated flux for the three components and their spectral indices $\alpha$ (defined through $S\propto \nu^\alpha$) as derived from these fluxes" (7 Continuum properties)
   - parameter: Flux error assumed to be 10%  
  > "The error in the fluxes is assumed to be 10%." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Lobe indices steeper than at lower frequencies, consistent with spectral breaks in young compact steep spectrum sources  
  > "The spectral indices of the lobes are quite steep and steeper than seen at lower frequencies" (7 Continuum properties)

## Qualitative findings

- CO emission avoids the locations with the strongest continuum emission  
  > "The CO emission clearly avoids the locations with the strongest continuum emission." (3.1 Distribution of molecular gas in the three CO transitions)
- CO(3-2) absorption not detected against the northern lobe, unlike CO(1-0) and CO(2-1)  
  > "In contrast to CO(1-0) and CO(2-1), no CO(3-2) absorption is detected against the northern lobe" (3.1 Distribution of molecular gas in the three CO transitions)
- Narrow CO(1-0) absorption (FWHM ~10 km/s) near the systemic velocity, likely from a single giant molecular cloud  
  > "the narrow width of the absorption suggests that it is likely produced by a single giant molecular cloud" (6 Absorption against the N radio lobe)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: New transitions observed with parameters matched to the earlier CO(2-1) data so gas is traced on similar scales as the radio emission  
  > "This allowed us to study the distribution of the molecular gas on similar spatial scales as the extended radio emission" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
- Why this method: Common SoFiA-2 mask from the CO(2-1) cube for all transitions to avoid S/N-dependent bias in line ratios  
  > "which would lead to S/N-dependent biases in the line ratio" (2 ALMA observations of CO(1-0) and CO(3-2))
- Alternative: Radiation-pressure-driven wind versus jet driving of the compact outflow could not be separated  
  > "Thus, it is not possible to determine whether the radio jet or a radiation-pressure-driven wind accelerates this compact outflow." (4.1 Jet-ISM interactions)
- Alternative: X-ray excitation of CO considered as an alternative to the cocoon for the high ratios perpendicular to the jet  
  > "An alternative explanation could be that the X-ray emission that is observed in this region excites the CO gas to higher excitation levels" (4.1 Jet-ISM interactions)
- Alternative: Merger-induced turbulence and dissipation proposed as an alternative for the high line ratios  
  > "This suggests an alternative explanation for the high line ratios in terms of the turbulence and dissipation induced as the accreted gas settles following a galaxy merger or interaction." (Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Near-nuclear outflow is spatially unresolved  
  > "the near-nuclear outflow is spatially unresolved in our observations" (4.1 Jet-ISM interactions)
- Limitation (authors): Residual absorption effects against the northern lobe could not be fully excluded in CO(2-1)  
  > "we were unable to completely exclude some effect of the CO absorption seen against the northern lobe" (3.1 Distribution of molecular gas in the three CO transitions)
- Limitation (authors): Galaxy interaction may also affect gas conditions where ratios are elevated  
  > "we cannot exclude the possibility that other mechanisms that are connected to the on-going galaxy interaction also affect the gas conditions" (3.3 Line ratios)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Line-ratio uncertainties and beam-matching effects are not quantified; only 10% flux error is given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow energetics are upper limits that depend on the assumed alpha_CO = 0.89 and a CO(3-2) line width for the CO(1-0) limit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mask from CO(2-1) only may exclude CO(1-0) emission at other velocities, biasing the central R21 limit
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Merger origin of the excitation is not ruled out, so attribution to the jet is partly inferred
