## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce SOFIA FIFI-LS [CII] data and measure line fluxes and kinematics**: Use FIFI-LS pipeline Level 4 data for NGC 4203 (detecting >3-sigma pixels via median absolute deviation, clustering with DBSCAN in scikit-learn, fitting 2D spatial Gaussian and 1D spectral Gaussian to brightest pixel) and Level 3 data for the other 5 galaxies (removing background with Ridge linear regression, computing median spectrum per pixel, and fitting 1D or 2D Gaussian line profiles).  
  > "Following recommendation from SOFIA staff, NGC 4203 data were analyzed using Level 4 products, whereas the rest of the data was analyzed using Level 3" ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction); "The selected pixels are clustered spatially and spectrally simultaneously using the Density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise (DBSCAN) algorithm implemented in the scikit-learn python library" ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction); "A 1D Gaussian function is fitted on the median spectrum of each of the 25 pixels." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
   - software: FIFI-LS pipeline, scikit-learn
   - parameter: DBSCAN min_samples = 3 pixels, max distance = 4; pixel selection threshold = 4.45 * MAD (~3-sigma)  
  > "pixels with a flux in excess of the median flux by at least 4.45 times the median absolute deviation (equivalent to 3-$\sigma$ but with outlier removal) are selected." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction); "3 pixels are required in order to define a cluster and the maximum distance is set to 4, otherwise all other parameters are set to default." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
   - parameter: Line candidate selection: |v| < 400 km/s and SNR > 3  
  > "Fitted Gaussians with an absolute velocity below 400 km/s and a signal-to-noise detection greater than 3 are selected as emission line candidate." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
   - assumption: For Level 3 data, the source is assumed to be smaller than the FIFI-LS beam  
  > "Given the sparsity of the spatial sampling (5x5 pixel image), the source is assumed to be smaller than the FIFI-LS beam (the FIFI-LS beam size is used for the flux density normalization)." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
   - fitting: 1D and 2D Gaussian fitting; 2-Gaussian model selected for NGC 4261 via chi^2 and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)  
  > "For NGC 4261, given the width and the shape of the detected \[CII\] feature, we performed a fit with 2 Gaussians (Figure 5). The resulting $\chi^2$ decreases significantly (by almost a factor 3), and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is reduced by 13.9" ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
   - validation: BIC reduction of 13.9 for two-Gaussian fit in NGC 4261  
  > "the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is reduced by 13.9, which strongly indicates that the two line model is favored." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
2. **Measure CO line fluxes, upper limits, and molecular gas masses from ALMA and IRAM data**: Reduce ALMA data with CASA, measure CO fluxes or 3-sigma upper limits (in a 500 km/s window per beam), convert higher-J CO transitions to CO(1-0) using empirical line ratios, and compute molecular gas masses M_mol.  
  > "The data were reduced using the CASA software (cite: mcmullin07)." (CO Observations); "The CO upper limit fluxes are given for the ALMA beam size (generally sub-arcsec) and they correspond to 3-$\sigma$ in a 500 km/s window." (Results); "Table [\[tab:CII_CO_gal\]](#tab:CII_CO_gal) reports the molecular gas mass based on the conversion from the CO flux." (Origin of the Cold Gas)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 3-sigma upper limit window = 500 km/s  
  > "The CO upper limit fluxes are given for the ALMA beam size (generally sub-arcsec) and they correspond to 3-$\sigma$ in a 500 km/s window." (Results)
   - assumption: CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = 0.7 +/- 0.2 (2.8 +/- 0.9 in flux) and CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) = 0.5 +/- 0.3 (4.5 +/- 3.0 in flux) from Vila-Vilaro et al. (2003)  
  > "(cite: vila03) found line ratios for elliptical galaxies on the scale of tens of arcseconds to be about 0.7$\pm$0.2 (2.8$\pm$0.9) for CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) in brightness temperature (flux) and 0.5$\pm$0.3 (4.5$\pm$3.0) for CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) in brightness temperature (flux)." (Results)
   - assumption: Reference CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO = 2 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "We use the reference conversion factor $X_{CO}=2\times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ to evaluate the molecular gas mass in each galaxy." (Origin of the Cold Gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Map nuclear dust absorption and Halpha+[NII] emission from HST and MUSE, and derive FIR dust masses**: Subtract stellar emission models (photutils or linear function of red filter) from HST blue filters for dust maps; subtract wide-band continuum from narrow-band HST images using RANSAC regression for Halpha+[NII] fluxes; fit MUSE emission lines with KUBEVIZ; evaluate FIR dust mass and luminosity with CIGALEMC, MAGPHYS14, and modified blackbody fits.  
  > "In the end, we chose a photutils model for NGC 4406 and NGC 4552, and a model estimated from the red filter for the other galaxies." (Dust absorption from HST); "The RANSAC Regressor algorithm from the Python library scikit-learn was chosen to perform the regression in order to reject outliers in the data." (Halpha+[NII] emission from HST); "Dust mass and luminosity have been evaluated using the spectral energy distribution (SED) modeling software CIGALEMC (cite: serra2011) and MAGPHYS14 (cite: dacunha2008), and by fitting some modified black-body spectra to the FIR portion of our data" (Results)
   - software: GALFIT, photutils, scikit-learn, ESO MUSE pipeline, ZAP, KUBEVIZ, CIGALEMC, MAGPHYS14
   - parameter: Ring aperture for HST continuum regression typically ~5 to 8 arcsec  
  > "(typical values for the inner and outer radius are about 5 and 8 arcsecs)." (Halpha+[NII] emission from HST)
   - assumption: Galaxy SED does not change much across the galaxy when using red filter as stellar template  
  > "or with a linear function of the “red” filter assuming that the galaxy SED does not change much across the galaxy." (Dust absorption from HST)
   - fitting: RANSAC linear regression for HST continuum; 1D Gaussian groups with fixed relative velocities in KUBEVIZ; UV-to-mm SED fitting in CIGALEMC and MAGPHYS14  
  > "Groups of lines were fitted simultaneously using a combination of 1D Gaussian functions with fixed relative velocities." (Halpha+[NII] emission from SOAR & VMOS)
   - validation: Outlier fraction in RANSAC regression verified to remain below 15%  
  > "The percentage of outliers for each regression remains below 15%, an acceptable level" (Halpha+[NII] emission from HST)
4. **Derive X-ray hot gas thermodynamic profiles and 2D maps, VLA 1.5 GHz radio images, and CCA condensation diagnostics**: Fit deprojected radial X-ray spectra in concentric annuli with XSPEC (PROJCT+APEC) and 2D contour-binned spectra with SPEX; image VLA 1-2 GHz radio data in CASA using MultiTerm MultiFrequency synthesis clean with robust=0 and self-calibration; compute k-plot and C-ratio = t_cool / t_eddy to compare with Chaotic Cold Accretion (CCA) predictions.  
  > "We extracted spectra from concentric annuli with a signal to noise ratio of at least 18 ($\sim 320$ counts per region) and fitted them simultaneously in the 0.5–2 keV band using the PROJCT model implemented in XSPEC (cite: arnaud96)." (X-ray emission, hot gas and entropy); "The final total intensity images, for both new Karl Jansky VLA and historical VLA observations (Figure 15, 18), are created using the casa MultiTerm MultiFrequency synthesis `clean` algorithm (cite: rau11) with the `briggs (robust=0)` weighting scheme" (Radio emission); "Another diagnostics of the top-down multiphase condensation is the $C$-ratio, i.e., the cooling time divided by the turbulence eddy time, $t_{\rm eddy} = 2\pi r^{2/3} L^{1/3}/\sigma_{v,L}$." (Top-down condensation)
   - software: XSPEC, SPEX, CASA
   - parameter: X-ray spectral extraction SNR >= 18 (~320 counts per region), energy band 0.5-2.0 keV  
  > "We extracted spectra from concentric annuli with a signal to noise ratio of at least 18 ($\sim 320$ counts per region) and fitted them simultaneously in the 0.5–2 keV band" (X-ray emission, hot gas and entropy)
   - parameter: VLA imaging: Briggs robust = 0; self-calibration (3 phase cycles + 1 amplitude and phase cycle) when SNR reaches 100  
  > "When the dynamic range of the radio map (signal to noise ratio) reaches values of 100, the self-calibration method consisting of three cycles of phase and one cycle of amplitude and phase calibration, is performed." (Radio emission)

## Qualitative findings

- SOFIA FIFI-LS detects [CII] 157.74 um emission in 3 of the 6 observed massive elliptical galaxies (NGC 4203, NGC 4261, and NGC 4406) and sets upper limits in NGC 4374, NGC 4552, and NGC 4649.  
  > "SOFIA FIFI-LS observations reveal \[CII\] line emission in 3 (NGC 4203, NGC 4261 and NGC 4406) out of 6 observed galaxies." (Discussion)
- In the non-central galaxies of the sample, the detected cool gas is centrally concentrated and compact (often in rotating sub-kpc disks), in contrast to the extended filamentary structures seen in group-centered ellipticals.  
  > "The distribution of the detected cool gas phase in our six (non-central) galaxies is often centrally concentrated and compact, at variance with the cold gas-rich galaxies studied in (cite: werner14)." (Conclusions)
- Kinematical and morphological misalignments between the multiphase gas/dust and the stars, along with a two-order-of-magnitude spread in molecular gas mass at similar stellar luminosity, indicate that stellar mass loss is not the primary driver of the cold gas.  
  > "Misalignment between the multiphase gas and stars suggest that stellar mass loss is not the primary driver." (Abstract); "Thus, these additional data confirm the lack of dependence of the molecular gas content on the stellar mass, with the implication that stellar mass loss is not the primary source of molecular gas in these galaxies." (Stellar mass loss)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SOFIA FIFI-LS [CII] observations were obtained to expand the Herschel PACS sample to the remaining nearby X-ray bright massive early-type galaxies from Dunn et al. (2010), complemented by ALMA, IRAM, VLA, HST, MUSE, and Chandra to probe all gas phases and AGN feedback.  
  > "The goal of the SOFIA FIFI-LS observations was to expand the sample of early type galaxies observed in the \[CII\] line with Herschal PACS by obtaining a complete coverage of the sample of 18 nearby ($d<100$ Mpc), massive, X-ray bright early-type galaxies" (Sample description); "The recently acquired SOFIA data are complemented with a large suite of multiwavelength observations available in literature which include, radio (ALMA, IRAM, VLA), optical (HST, SOAR and VMOS), IR (Spitzer, Herschel), and X-ray (Chandra) data." (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: GALFIT single and multi-component (Sersic, Nuker, Ferrer) models were tried for HST stellar emission subtraction, but rejected in favor of photutils or red-filter linear models which matched the true stellar emission better.  
  > "We tried several types of profile (Sersic, Nuker, Ferrer, etc.) with GALFIT, but our models from the photutils or the "red" filter seem to be closer to the true stellar emission for these galaxies." (Dust absorption from HST)
- Alternative: Single-Gaussian fit to the NGC 4261 SOFIA [CII] line was rejected in favor of a two-Gaussian fit because chi^2 decreased by almost a factor of 3 and BIC decreased by 13.9.  
  > "For NGC 4261, given the width and the shape of the detected \[CII\] feature, we performed a fit with 2 Gaussians (Figure 5). The resulting $\chi^2$ decreases significantly (by almost a factor 3), and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is reduced by 13.9, which strongly indicates that the two line model is favored." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): High angular resolution ALMA 12 m array observations can resolve out large-scale diffuse CO emission (as seen in NGC 5044 where ALMA recovered only 20% of the IRAM 30m flux).  
  > "Given the high angular resolution of the 12m array data, some diffuse CO component might have been filtered out by the interferometer such as was the case for NGC 5044 observations, where the ALMA CO(2-1) flux was only 20% of the IRAM-30m flux (cite: david14)." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): SOFIA FIFI-LS observations have coarse spatial resolution and low SNR, leaving the cool gas spatially under-resolved and requiring bright-pixel fitting or point-source assumptions.  
  > "We chose to fit the brightest pixel instead of integrating over the source extent because of the poor signal-to-noise of the data." ([CII] SOFIA FIFI-LS data reduction); "Moreover, our cool gas is often under-resolved (especially with SOFIA), meaning that we are only able to capture larger associations of clouds, rather than single elements" (Top-down condensation)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic errors on HST Halpha+[NII] fluxes from imperfect continuum subtraction are likely underestimated due to residual point sources.  
  > "Due to residual point sources affecting the H$\alpha+\rm [NII]$ images, it is likely that we have underestimated this error to some degree." (Halpha+[NII] emission from HST)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size prevents a robust statistical correlation analysis between AGN jet power P_jet and Halpha luminosity or cold gas mass.  
  > "The limited data available does not allow for a robust analysis of correlations between the $P_{\rm jet}$ and H$\alpha$ luminosity or the amount of cold gas." (Top-down condensation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing SOFIA FIFI-LS [CII] fluxes (12 arcsec pixel scale) with high-resolution ALMA CO interferometric fluxes (0.12-0.3 arcsec beam, MRS 4-5.6 arcsec) introduces severe aperture and spatial-filtering mismatches in the [CII]/CO ratios.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA imaging and calibration parameters for the newly analyzed CO datasets (NGC 4636, NGC 4261, NGC 4649) are largely omitted.
