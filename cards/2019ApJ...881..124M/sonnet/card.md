## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA cubes and continuum**: CASA clean with natural weighting + 650 kλ taper for detectability; Briggs robust 0.5 for resolved analysis  
  > "we first reduced the data with natural weighting and a UV tapering of 650 k$\lambda$" (2.1 ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 2-channel averaging, ~18 km/s  
  > "by averaging over two velocity channels" (2.1 ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Non-detections tested with different weighting/tapering  
  > "we test the robustness of not-detecting the line/continuum by changing the reduction method" (2.1 ALMA)
2. **HST photometry, UV slope and sizes**: 1.2'' aperture photometry in F110W/F160W with Lyα correction; imfit exponential profile fits on F160W  
  > "Measurements are performed with 1.2$''$ diameter apertures to account for the objects sizes" (3.2); "modelling the light distribution with an exponential profile using the imfit software package" (3.3)
   - software: imfit, Swarp
   - fitting: Single vs two-component exponential fits compared by reduced chi2 (2.83 vs 1.10 for VR7)  
  > "(with reduced $\chi^2_r=1.10$ versus $\chi^2_r=2.83$, detailed in Appendix $\ref{app:size}$)" (5.2)
   - validation: PSF from four stars; flux consistent within 5% with previous reduction  
  > "We have verified that the integrated flux and sensitivity are consistent within $5$ %." (2.2 HST/WFC3)
3. **Continuum limits and IR luminosity/obscured SFR**: 1σ limits from tapered continuum; optically thin greybody 8-1000 µm, CMB-corrected, Kennicutt SFR_IR  
  > "integrating an optically thin grey body between 8-1000$\mu$m" (5.2)
   - assumption: Td=45 K, β=1.5, κ850=0.077 m^2/kg  
  > "We assume a dust temperature 45 K and power law exponent $\beta=1.5$" (5.2); "which we assume is 0.077 m$^2$ kg$^{-1}$ similar to (cite: Ota2014)" (Appendix compilation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Td=35 K alternative also reported  
  > "Assuming a dust temperature of 35 K would result in L$_{\rm IR, T=35K} < 1.3\times10^{10}$ L$_{\odot}$" (5.2)
4. **Integrated [CII] properties of VR7 and MASOSA limit**: Bootstrap (1000 perturbations) of 1D spectrum for z and FWHM; CASA imfit elliptical Gaussian on collapsed PB-corrected cube; MASOSA limit from rms and fake-source injection (500 positions)  
  > "For 1000 iterations, we perturb each data-point in the 1D extraction with its associated uncertainty" (4.1); "We place these sources in 500 random positions in our collapsed images and measure the median peak S/N." (4.2)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: VR7 collapse 252.285-252.519 GHz (-20 to -320 km/s wrt Lyα)  
  > "(252.285 GHz - 252.519 GHz; corresponding to -20 to -320 km s$^{-1}$ w.r.t. Ly$\alpha$" (4.1)
   - assumption: MASOSA conservative limit assumes 200 km/s width and ~0.7'' beam  
  > "assuming a line width of 200 km s$^{-1}$ and using a reduction with beam FWHM $\approx0.7''$" (4.2)
   - fitting: elliptical Gaussian (imfit); two-Gaussian 1D line fit  
  > "slightly better described by a two-component gaussian ($\chi^2_{\rm red}=0.59$) than a single gaussian component" (5.1)
   - validation: Inspected collapsed images of different widths and tapers  
  > "We have also changed the reduction strategy including no UV tapering to a large UV taper of 200k$\lambda$" (4.2)
5. **Resolved [CII] morphology, moments, PV, resolved UV/[CII] ratio**: High-res Briggs cube; moment maps 0-3; PV diagrams with CASA impv, 1.5'' slit; SFR_UV/SFR_[CII] maps using Kennicutt and De Looze  
  > "We construct PV diagrams by averaging over a slit-width 1.5$''$ around the center in the NS direction (PA=$90\degree$) using the CASA task impv." (5.5.2)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Astrometry checked with one foreground object (0.1-0.2'')  
  > "meaning uncertainties on the order $\approx0.1-0.2''$ exist" (footnote 3)
6. **Place on [CII]-SFR relation and infer metallicity**: Compile and homogenise literature sample; compare with De Looze relation; Vallini2015 for metallicity  
  > "we homogenise the measurements by converting all UV luminosities to SFR$_{\rm UV}$ consistently and we revise upper limits following the method applied to MASOSA" (6.3)
   - software: TOPCAT
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Diffuse, redshifted [CII] 'shell' at ~+100 km/s possibly outflowing  
  > "The shell is redshifted by $\approx100$ km s$^{-1}$ compared to the central component" (5.5.2)
- VR7 is dispersion dominated (Δv/2σ≈0.3)  
  > "results in a kinematic ratio $\Delta v/2\sigma \approx 0.3$" (5.5.1)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Tapered natural-weighted reduction chosen to optimise extended emission detection while resolving the line  
  > "The tapering was used to optimise detectability of extended emission" (2.1 ALMA)
- Alternative: Strong CIII] emission to explain red β in MASOSA judged insufficient  
  > "meaning that exceptionally strong lines would be required" (6.1)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): MASOSA red colour needs deeper observations; HST cannot distinguish one vs two components  
  > "While MASOSA’s red UV color needs to be verified with deeper observations" (6.1)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA-HST astrometry uncertain at 0.1-0.2''  
  > "meaning uncertainties on the order $\approx0.1-0.2''$ exist" (footnote 3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperature unconstrained so obscured SFR limits are model dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Low S/N of shell and second [CII] component (3-4σ)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heterogeneous literature compilation for [CII]-SFR relation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): MASOSA [CII] limit depends on assumed width and size
