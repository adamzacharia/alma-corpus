[5](#fig5)(a) and [10](#fig10)(a)." (Data analysis)
   - software: STATCONT
   - parameter: Spatial resolution 1.6″; spectral resolution Δv∼10 km s−1  
  > "The data cubes from the ALCHEMI survey are imaged to a spatial resolution of 1.6$^{\prime\prime}$ ($\sim27$ pc) and a spectral resolution of $\Delta$v$\sim$10 km s$^{-1}$." (Data analysis)
   - parameter: Extract 1000 km s−1 around each transition  
  > "We extract spectral channels covering $1000\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$ velocity range around each transition from the continuum-subtracted cubes" (Molecular emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Check missing flux**: Smooth ALCHEMI cubes to IRAM 30m beams and compare integrated intensities with single-dish measurements.  
  > "We unify the beam size of the ALCHEMI data cubes to that of the IRAM 30m observations using the CASA command `imsmooth` (cite: CASATeam2022PASP) and compare the integrated intensities between them." (Data analysis)
   - software: CASA, imsmooth
   - fitting: _not stated_

  > "All the missing fluxes of the molecular lines in this work are less than a few percent." (Data analysis)
3. **Make masked moments and position-velocity diagrams**: Use uncorrected cubes to define channel masks; generate moments 0, 1, and 2 and PVDs along the major axis and SW slice.  
  > "We take the data cubes before primary beam correction as references, which share the same dimensions as those after primary beam correction." (Data analysis); "The integrated intensity, velocity, and velocity dispersion maps, i.e., moments 0, 1, and 2 maps, in the CMZ (inner $\sim$500 pc) of NGC 253 are generated by the CASA command `immoments` (cite: CASATeam2022PASP)." (Data analysis); "The PVDs are generated by the Python-based tool `pvextractor` (cite: 2016ascl.soft08010G)." (Data analysis)
   - software: CASA, immoments, pvextractor
   - parameter: Per-channel 3σ detection threshold  
  > "For each targeted molecular line, we mask the region without 3$\sigma$-detection for each channel in the corresponding data cube." (Data analysis)

   - validation: 3σ detection limits applied before map and PVD analyses  
  > "Given the 3$\sigma$-detection limits prior to analyses, all the features presented in the following maps and PVDs will be robust." (Data analysis)
4. **Separate streamer and disk kinematics**: Extract averaged CO(1-0) spectra from a beam-area circle and a high-σ ellipse; separate blueshifted streamer and redshifted disk components and derive local outflow velocity.  
  > "From a circular region in the SW streamer region having the same area as the beam size (the black empty circle in Fig. [4](#fig4)b), we extract the averaged CO(1-0) spectrum" (Gas kinematics); "We also extract the averaged CO(1-0) spectrum from a region with high-velocity-dispersion (high-$\sigma$) in the SW streamer region (the black empty ellipse in Fig. [4](#fig4)b)" (Gas kinematics); "Combining the velocity field of CO(1-0) in Fig. [4](#fig4)(a), the blueshifted component relative to the reference velocity is emitted by the SW streamer, and the redshifted component is emitted by the gas disk." (Gas kinematics)
   - software: curve_fit
   - parameter: Reference velocity ∼300 km s−1  
  > "The averaged CO(1-0) spectrum shows a double-peak structure, where we take the velocity $\sim$300 km s$^{-1}$ as a reference velocity for the emission near the base of the SW streamer." (Gas kinematics)
   - assumption: Use high-σ blueshifted center ∼219.7 km s−1 as the averaged streamer velocity  
  > "If we take the velocity center of the blueshifted component from the high-$\sigma$ region ($\sim$219.7 km s$^{-1}$) as the averaged velocity for the SW streamer" (Gas kinematics)
   - assumption: Gas-disk inclination ∼78.5°; outflow perpendicular to the disk  
  > "Considering the inclination angle of the gas disk ($\sim$78.5$^{\circ}$) and the outflow being perpendicular to the disk, the deprojected local velocity of the SW streamer approaches $\sim$400 km s $^{-1}$" (Gas kinematics)
   - fitting: Double Gaussian fits with curve_fit; priors, bounds, likelihood, and weights not stated  
  > "Using Python-based tool `curve_fit`, we fit the CO(1-0) spectra in Figs. [4](#fig4)(c) and [4](#fig4)(d) with double Gaussian function." (Gas kinematics)
   - validation: Compare fitted components with the velocity field and the streamer velocity gradient in the PVD  
  > "Combining the velocity field of CO(1-0) in Fig. [4](#fig4)(a), the blueshifted component relative to the reference velocity is emitted by the SW streamer, and the redshifted component is emitted by the gas disk." (Gas kinematics); "Moreover, there is a gradual velocity shift along the SW streamer in the CO(1-0) PVD outlined by white dashed profiles in Fig. [3](#fig3)(a)." (Gas kinematics)
5. **Diagnose optical depth**: Map self-calibrated CO/13CO(1-0) integrated ratios and inspect ratio PVDs; compare with the literature C/13C abundance ratio.  
  > "Fig. [5](#fig5)(a) shows the integrated intensity ratio map of CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) based on self-calibration, where we masked the regions with ratio S/N being less than $3\sigma$." (Optical depth); "To decompose different velocity components, we plot in Figs. [6](#fig6)(a) and [6](#fig6)(b) the intensity ratio of CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) in the PVDs along the major axis and the SW slice" (Optical depth)
   - parameter: CO/13CO ratio 15 defines the disk/outflow boundary; ratio 21 outlines higher-ratio outflow regions  
  > "The black contour with CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) ratio of 15 defines the boundary between the outflow streamers and the disk." (Optical depth); "The CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) ratio further increases in part of the outflow region and is outlined by the blue contour at 21, where the CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) ratio is highest in the SW streamer region." (Optical depth)
   - assumption: CO/13CO ratio approaches C/13C in the optically thin limit and decreases with increasing optical depth

## Qualitative findings

- CO and HCN emission extend farther towards the SW streamer than the weaker molecular tracers.  
  > "The CO(1-0) and HCN(1-0) emission is the most extended, which can be attributed to the optically thick environments in the gas disk and decreased optical depths towards the SW streamer." (Summary)
- Dense gas fraction is high in GMC 3 and at the streamer base, but moderate in the extended streamer.  
  > "In summary, we find that the dense gas fraction is high inside GMC 3 (with reference velocity, region A) and at the base of the SW streamer (with outflow velocity, region B)." (Dense gas fraction); "The dense gas fraction in the extended streamer region (outlined by the black dashed profiles) is moderate in Fig. [7](#fig7)(d) and doesn’t show visible signs of accumulation of dense gas traced with HCN/CO(1-0)." (Dense gas fraction)
- The central region is dominated by fast shocks; fast and slow shocks coexist at the SW streamer base.  
  > "where the central region turns out to be dominated by the fast shocks." (Shock strength); "while fast and slow shocks co-exist at the base of the SW streamer and can be related to the formation of molecular outflow." (Summary)
- Dense gas tracers have larger streamer-to-disk peak ratios than the other lines.  
  > "It is interesting to find that all the dense gas tracers (marked by the star symbols in the first column of Table [\[tab2\]](#tab2)) have higher streamer-to-disk ratios than the others." (Correlation between the dense gas fraction and the strength of fast shocks)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Band 3 covers ground-state molecular transitions accessible even in ∼10 K gas; the survey supplies multiple physical diagnostics.  
  > "In this paper, we only make use of the Band 3 data, which covers the ground-state transitions of common molecular lines that can be emitted even from the coldest ($\sim$10 K) medium." (Data analysis); "Taking advantage of the numerous molecular species in the ALCHEMI survey, we study the properties through various integrated intensity ratios." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Self-calibration improves S/N for complicated structure; isotopologue ratios provide an optical-depth diagnostic; lower-opacity tracers check dense gas.  
  > "The use of self-calibration allows for a higher signal-to-noise ratio and is better for images with complicated spatial structures" (Data analysis); "However, the $^{13}$C-bearing molecular species being optically thinner than the $^{12}$C-bearing ones implies that the CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) ratio is a more reliable indicator of CO optical depth." (Optical depth); "To confirm the distribution of the dense gas in the CMZ of NGC 253, we turn to tracers of dense gas with lower opacity." (Dense gas fraction)
- Alternative: A velocity gradient may represent acceleration or faster ejecta reaching farther than slower ejecta.  
  > "The velocity gradient inside the SW streamer can be evidence for an inside-out acceleration on the gas velocity, i.e., the gas inside the SW streamer is accelerated as it outflows." (Gas kinematics); "It can also be attributed to the ability for the fast ejecta to reach farther away than the slow ejecta." (Gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Optically thick outflow interpretation from CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) contrasted with the isotopologue-ratio inference.  
  > "(cite: 2018ApJ...867..111Z) derived the optical depth with the integrated intensity ratio of CO(2-1)/CO(1-0), and suggested that the majority of the CO emission is optically thick in the outflow region of NGC 253." (Optical depth); "However, the $^{13}$C-bearing molecular species being optically thinner than the $^{12}$C-bearing ones implies that the CO/$^{13}$CO(1-0) ratio is a more reliable indicator of CO optical depth." (Optical depth)
- Alternative: Weak central methanol may reflect infrequent slow shocks, methanol depletion by fast shocks, or photo-dissociation.  
  > "Meanwhile, the weak methanol emission in the center could be a result of the infrequent slow shocks, or the depletion of methanol molecules (cite: 1995MNRAS.272..184H; 2017MNRAS.472..604E) by fast shocks or photo-dissociation due to intense star formation (cite: 2022A&A...663A..33H)." (Shock strength)
- Alternative: A common denominator was checked as an explanation for the ratio correlation.  
  > "Therefore, the correlation between H$^{13}$CN/$^{13}$CO(1-0) and SiO(2-1)/$^{13}$CO(1-0) ratios is real, it is not a result from the common denominator." (Correlation between the dense gas fraction and the strength of fast shocks)
- Alternative: In situ formation is rejected; direct radiation/pressure driving versus hot-wind entrainment remains unresolved.

- How they decided it was enough: Require per-channel 3σ detections; check missing flux against single-dish measurements.

- Limitation (authors): Interferometric cubes lack short spacings, although the reported missing flux is less than a few percent.  
  > "The data cubes from the ALCHEMI survey are subject to the missing flux problem due to the lack of short spacing in interferometric observations." (Data analysis); "All the missing fluxes of the molecular lines in this work are less than a few percent." (Data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Other outflow streamers are fainter or behind the disk and indistinguishable in the CO velocity field.

  > "Given that N$_{2}$H$^{+}$(1-0) is not safely optically thin and the optical depths of HCN(1-0) and CO(1-0) can be different in the central region, the N$_{2}$H$^{+}$/$^{13}$CO(1-0) and HCN/CO(1-0) ratios inside GMC 3 may be affected by optical depths." (Dense gas fraction)
- Limitation (authors): Shared high critical densities may contribute to the dense-gas/shock correlation.

- Limitation (authors): More evidence is needed to explain different streamer-to-disk behavior of dense-gas and shock tracers.  
  > "It seems that the enhancement of dense gas fraction is stronger in the SW streamer than in the gas disk, while the shock strength is equivalently enhanced in the SW streamer and the gas disk." - Limitation (authors): Direct molecular driving and hot-wind entrainment are difficult to distinguish.  
  > "However, it is still difficult to distinguish whether the outflowing molecular gas is directly driven by radiation/pressure or is entrained by the hot wind." (Formation of the molecular outflow)
