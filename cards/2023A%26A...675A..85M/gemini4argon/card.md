- Question: Derive lensing-corrected 1.1 mm number counts down to flux densities fainter than 0.1 mJy towards five Hubble Frontier Fields galaxy clusters and determine their contribution to the extragalactic background light (EBL).  
  > "We aim to derive number counts at $1.1\,\textmd{mm}$ down to flux densities fainter than $0.1\,\textmd{mJy}$, based on ALMA observations towards five *Hubble* Frontier Fields (FF) galaxy clusters, following a statistical approach to correct for lensing effects." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Source detection and extraction in ALMA 1.1 mm mosaics**: Search for pixels with S/N >= 4.5 within the PB > 0.3 region and group pixels into individual sources using the DBSCAN algorithm.  
  > "We detect sources by searching for pixels with signal-to-noise ratio $(\textmd{S/N})$ larger or equal than a given threshold, grouping pixels as individual sources with the DBSCAN python algorithm (cite: Pedregosa2012)." (Observations with ALMA); "Therefore, in this study we use $\textmd{PB}>0.3$ for all cluster fields." (Observations with ALMA)
   - software: DBSCAN
   - parameter: S/N >= 4.5 threshold, PB > 0.3 region  
  > "We choose a threshold of $\textmd{S/N}=4.5$, as this is found to be an appropriate tradeoff between the purity and completeness correction factors that will be applied to our number counts (see $\S$[3](#sect_method))." (Observations with ALMA); "Therefore, in this study we use $\textmd{PB}>0.3$ for all cluster fields." (Observations with ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked spurious detections in negative mosaics and 300 simulated noise maps  
  > "This is consistent within $1\sigma$ with both the amount of spurious sources from the negative mosaics (zero and one, respectively) and the number of sources without reliable counterparts at other wavelengths (two and one, respectively)." (Methodology)
2. **Measure integrated flux densities and sizes of detected sources**: For high-significance (S/N >= 5) sources, fit elliptical Gaussians in the uv-plane using UVMCMCFIT. For low-significance (4.5 <= S/N < 5) sources, scale peak intensities using median size parameters from high-S/N sources and source injection simulations.  
  > "We measure integrated flux densities in the *uv*-plane for our new high-significance detections as in Paper I, using the UVMCMCFIT python algorithm (cite: Bussmann2016)." (Observations with ALMA); "As in Paper IV, we estimate the integrated flux densities of low-significance detections in A370 and AS1063 using source peak intensities; this choice is motivated by the large uncertainties in integrated source flux densities that are given at low S/N by two-dimensional Gaussian fits in the $uv$-plane." (Observations with ALMA)
   - software: UVMCMCFIT
   - parameter: Peak-to-integrated flux ratios of 0.80 (A370) and 0.71 (AS1063) for 4.5 <= S/N < 5 sources  
  > "We conduct source injection simulations (see $\S$[3](#sect_method)), finding a typical ratio between the peak and integrated flux density for these size parameters of 0.80 and 0.71 in A370 and AS1063, respectively." (Observations with ALMA)
   - assumption: Low-significance detections have observed effective radius r_eff,obs = 0.23'' and axial ratio q_obs = 0.58 (median of high-S/N sources)  
  > "We adopt as their observed effective radius and axial ratio the median values found for the high-significance sources. Including the five FFs, these are $r_{\textmd{eff,obs}}=0.23\ensuremath{''}$ and $q_{\textmd{obs}}=0.58$, respectively." (Observations with ALMA)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fits in the uv-plane with UVMCMCFIT  
  > "We measure integrated flux densities in the *uv*-plane for our new high-significance detections as in Paper I, using the UVMCMCFIT python algorithm (cite: Bussmann2016)." (Observations with ALMA)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to assumed source sizes for low-S/N sources using point source and r_eff,obs = 0.5'' extreme cases  
  > "Since we have assumed a given image-plane source size for low-significance detections (11/29 of our sample), we explore the effect of varying this source size by testing two extreme cases." (Comparison to previous works)
3. **Assign source redshifts**: Cross-match ALMA detections with spectroscopic (ALMA linescan, GLASS, VLT/MUSE, Gemini/GMOS) and photometric (HFF-DeepSpace EAZY posteriors, Paper II) catalogs; assign Gaussian redshift prior to unassociated sources.  
  > "When available, we use spectroscopic redshifts and (if not) photometric redshift probability distributions." (Source redshifts); "Nine detections have no reliable counterparts in the aforementioned catalogs. As in Paper IV, for these sources we assume a Gaussian redshift distribution centered at $z=2$ with $\sigma=0.5$, which is consistent within $1\sigma$ with average redshift values found for faint dusty galaxies from the literature (e.g. (cite: Aravena2016; Dunlop2017; Aravena2020))." (Source redshifts)
   - parameter: Matching radius <= 0.5'' for photometric counterparts (up to ~1'' for spectroscopic), redshift quality parameter p_Delta_z ~ 1  
  > "Among the sources that lack spectroscopic redshifts, one in A370 and two in AS1063 have counterparts with reliable photometric redshifts within $0.5\ensuremath{''}$. We use the redshift quality parameter $p_{\Delta z}$ provided by the catalog as a measure of the $z_{\textmd{phot}}$ reliability, with $p_{\Delta z}\approx1$ indicating that most of the total integrated probability lies within $\Delta z=0.2$ of the $z_{\textmd{phot}}$ estimate." (Source redshifts)
   - assumption: Gaussian redshift distribution z = 2 +/- 0.5 for sources without counterparts  
  > "As in Paper IV, for these sources we assume a Gaussian redshift distribution centered at $z=2$ with $\sigma=0.5$, which is consistent within $1\sigma$ with average redshift values found for faint dusty galaxies from the literature (e.g. (cite: Aravena2016; Dunlop2017; Aravena2020))." (Source redshifts)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested four alternative redshift distribution assumptions across all detections (Gaussian z=2+/-0.5, fixed z=2, uniform from cluster z to 4, Gaussian z=3+/-0.5)  
  > "As in Paper IV, we explore the effect of adopting different source redshifts in the predicted counts, considering the following cases for all detections: a) assuming a Gaussian redshift distribution centered at $z=2\pm0.5$, b) adopting exactly $z=2$, c) assuming a uniform redshift distribution between the cluster redshift and $z=4$, and d) assuming a Gaussian redshift distribution centered at $z=3\pm0.5$." (Comparison to previous works)
4. **Estimate image-plane completeness, flux deboosting, and spurious source fractions**: Perform image-plane source injection simulations to derive completeness as a function of flux and size, and flux deboosting as a function of S/N. Extract sources from 300 simulated noise maps per cluster to determine spurious fraction p_false(S/N).  
  > "We use source injection simulations, in the image-plane, to estimate the completeness and deboosting corrections (see Paper IV $\S$3.1 for details)." (Methodology); "We compute the fraction of spurious sources as a function of S/N for each galaxy cluster, $p_{\textmd{false}}$, as the average ratio between the number of detections over a peak S/N in a set of 300 simulated noise maps and in the actual ALMA mosaic (see Paper IV and Paper IV Corrigendum)." (Methodology)
   - parameter: 300 simulated noise maps per cluster; 10^4 simulated SIDES point sources for Eddington bias check  
  > "We compute the fraction of spurious sources as a function of S/N for each galaxy cluster, $p_{\textmd{false}}$, as the average ratio between the number of detections over a peak S/N in a set of 300 simulated noise maps and in the actual ALMA mosaic (see Paper IV and Paper IV Corrigendum)." (Methodology); "We create sets of $10^4$ simulated point sources that follow the SIDES galaxy formation model (cite: Bethermin2017) distributions of both redshift and $1.1\,\textmd{mm}$ flux density, lens these sources using the “best” CATS v4 model for each cluster, inject and extract them in our ALMA mosaics down to $\textmd{S/N}=4.5$, and obtain the ratio between output and input demagnified flux density as a function of S/N (see Paper IV $\S$3.1 for details)." (Methodology)
   - assumption: Skip separate Eddington bias corrections since SIDES simulation flux enhancements match deboosting corrections  
  > "These ratios are consistent with the deboosting corrections obtained in Fig. [3](#fig_debcorr). We thus consider safe to skip Eddington bias corrections in this work." (Methodology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared simulated noise map spurious counts with negative mosaic detections and unassociated source counts  
  > "Based on the source extraction on the simulated noise maps, the average number of spurious sources at $\textmd{S/N}\geq4.5$ is $0.93\pm1.33$ for A370 and $1.45\pm1.79$ for AS1063. This is consistent within $1\sigma$ with both the amount of spurious sources from the negative mosaics (zero and one, respectively) and the number of sources without reliable counterparts at other wavelengths (two and one, respectively)." (Methodology)
5. **Compute source-plane magnification maps and effective areas from lens models**: Reproject surface mass density and shear maps from public v4/v4.1 lens models to ALMA resolution, deflect magnification and PB-corrected rms maps to the source plane over a redshift grid, and integrate pixels where S_demag,peak / sigma_demag >= 4.5.  
  > "We use the full set of individual mass reconstructions released by the teams (which sample the range of uncertainties), reprojected to the size and resolution of the ALMA maps." (Lensing models); "At each demagnified peak intensity $S_{\textmd{demag,peak}}$, the effective area $A_{\textmd{eff}}$ is obtained from the source-plane pixels that satisfy $S_{\textmd{demag,peak}}/\sigma_{\textmd{demag}}\geq4.5$, with $\sigma_{\textmd{demag}}$ the PB-corrected rms corrected for magnification." (Methodology); "Since we need to create several source-plane maps for each lens model (sampling the source redshift distributions) but also want our Monte Carlo simulations to run fast, we precompute source-plane maps for all mass reconstructions and lens models, over the redshift range $z_{\textmd{min}}=0.4$ to $z_{\textmd{max}}=4$, using steps of $\Delta z=0.2$." (Methodology)
   - parameter: Redshift grid z_min = 0.4 to z_max = 4 with step Delta z = 0.2; 8, 9, 7, 9, and 9 lens models for A2744, MACSJ0416, MACSJ1149, A370, and AS1063  
  > "Table [\[tab_models\]](#tab_models) lists these models, together with the models considered for the two FF clusters introduced in this work; we adopt for use eight, nine, seven, nine, and nine lens models for A2744, MACSJ0416, MACSJ1149, A370, and AS1063, respectively." (Lensing models); "Since we need to create several source-plane maps for each lens model (sampling the source redshift distributions) but also want our Monte Carlo simulations to run fast, we precompute source-plane maps for all mass reconstructions and lens models, over the redshift range $z_{\textmd{min}}=0.4$ to $z_{\textmd{max}}=4$, using steps of $\Delta z=0.2$." (Methodology)
   - assumption: No magnification (mu = 1) for pixels missing in lens model maps; neglect differential magnification; flat LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "We adopt a flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with parameters $H_0=70\,\textmd{km}\,\textmd{s}^{-1}\textmd{Mpc}^{-1}$, $\Omega_m=0.3$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7$." (Introduction); "We consider no magnification effects ($\mu=1$) for the missing pixels in magnification maps." (Lensing models); "We neglect the effects of differential magnification, as all our detections lie far enough (i.e. more than four synthesized beams away) from critical lines in most of the adopted redshifts and lens models[^5]." (Methodology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared results across all independent v4/v4.1 lens models  
  > "We find variations across lens models in the median differential counts per flux bin up to $\approx0.6\,\textmd{dex}$ (although consistent within the error bars)." (Number counts)
6. **Derive lensing-corrected differential and cumulative number counts via Monte Carlo simulations**: Run 1000 Monte Carlo realizations per lens model per cluster drawing observed fluxes, peak intensities, redshifts, and lens mass reconstructions, computing demagnified fluxes and summing weights X_i = (1 - p_false,i) / (C_i * A_eff,i). Combine by taking medians and adding 16th/84th percentile errors in quadrature with Gehrels Poisson uncertainties.  
  > "We run 1000 realizations of the number counts per lens model per cluster field." (Methodology); "X_i=\frac{1-p_{\textmd{false},i}}{C_i\,A_{\textmd{eff},i}}." (Methodology); "We finally obtain combined differential (cumulative) counts, both in each separate cluster and including the five FFs, as the median values per flux density bin (limit). The associated uncertainties are given by the 16th and 84th percentiles, added in quadrature with scaled Poisson confidence levels for $1\sigma$ lower and upper limits (cite: Gehrels1986)." (Methodology)
   - parameter: 1000 realizations per lens model per cluster field  
  > "We run 1000 realizations of the number counts per lens model per cluster field." (Methodology)
   - assumption: Modified black body with beta = 1.5, T_dust = 35 K, z = 2.5 when scaling literature counts from 850 um, 1.2 mm, and 1.3 mm to 1.1 mm  
  > "Since we include in our comparison counts derived at wavelengths other than $1.1\,\textmd{mm}$, we scale their estimates as $S_{1.1\,\textmd{mm}}=1.59\times S_{1.3\,\textmd{mm}}$, $S_{1.1\,\textmd{mm}}=1.27\times S_{1.2\,\textmd{mm}}$, and $S_{1.1\,\textmd{mm}}=0.51\times S_{850\,\mu\textmd{m}}$. These factors are obtained assuming a modified black body model with a dust emissivity index $\beta=1.5$, dust temperature $T_{\textmd{dust}}=35\,\textmd{K}$, and $z=2.5$, which are values typically found for dusty galaxies (e.g. (cite: Swinbank2014))." (Comparison to previous works)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo sampling with Gaussian priors on fluxes and spectroscopic/photometric priors on redshift  
  > "Simulated catalogs have, for each source $i$, integrated flux densities $S_{\textmd{obs},i}$ and peak intensities $S_{\textmd{obs,peak},i}$ drawn from Gaussian distributions centered in the measured values (see Table [\[tab_snr45gals\]](#tab_snr45gals)), and redshifts $z_i$ drawn from the values/distributions selected in $\S$[2.2](#sect_z)." (Methodology)
   - validation: Compared against Paper IV counts and evaluated cosmic variance via three methods (empirical field-to-field variance, Driver & Robotham 2010 Eq. 4, and Cosmic Variance Calculator v1.03)  
  > "We obtain $1\sigma$ cosmic variance estimates using three different approaches." (Cosmic variance); "Finally, we obtain another estimate using the Cosmic Variance Calculator v1.03[^6] by (cite: Trenti2008), where cosmic variance is computed via the two point correlation function in extended Press-Schechter theory." (Cosmic variance)
7. **Compute resolved and extrapolated Extragalactic Background Light (EBL) at 1.1 mm**: Sum the integrated flux contribution across differential count bins in each Monte Carlo realization, and fit a double power-law function to non-zero bins of each realization to integrate down to S_lim = 0, 0.013, and 0.133 mJy.  
  > "Using the Monte Carlo realizations of our differential number counts, we compute the contribution to the extragalactic background light (EBL) provided by each of them, by adding up the contribution contained in each flux bin." (Extragalactic background light); "In order to estimate the EBL derived from our counts at $1.1\,\textmd{mm}$ for any flux density limit, we fit a double power-law function (e.g., (cite: Scott2002)) to our differential number counts." (Extragalactic background light)
   - parameter: Parameter bounds: N_0 = 10^3 - 10^6 mJy deg^-2, S_0 = 0.01 - 1 mJy, alpha = 0 - 5, beta = -1 to 1  
  > "We consider the ranges $N_0=10^3-10^6\,\textmd{mJy}\,\textmd{deg}^{-2}$, $S_0=0.01-1\,\textmd{mJy}$, $\alpha=0-5$, and $\beta=-1$ to 1." (Extragalactic background light)
   - fitting: Fit logarithmic double power-law function to non-zero flux density bins of each Monte Carlo realization  
  > "We fit Eq. [\[eq_dpl_dndlogs\]](#eq_dpl_dndlogs) to each realization of our differential number counts, including only flux density bins that have non-zero values (thus allowing to extrapolate our counts to flux densities outside the regime covered by our ALMA sources)." (Extragalactic background light)
   - validation: Compared direct summation EBL values with double power-law integrated EBL and Planck/COBE estimates  
  > "We compare our estimates to that by the Planck collaboration (using their best-fit extended halo model, see (cite: Ade2014)) and a recent determination based on COBE/FIRAS and Planck observations (cite: Odegard2019)." (Extragalactic background light)

## Qualitative findings

- Cumulative 1.1 mm number counts flatten below ~0.1 mJy and extend down to ~0.01 mJy, consistent within 1 sigma with Paper IV and the ASPECS-LP survey.  
  > "Importantly, the flattening found for our cumulative counts at $\lesssim0.1\,\textmd{mJy}$ also extends further to $\approx0.01\,\textmd{mJy}$, i.e., $\approx0.4\,\textmd{dex}$ fainter than ASPECS-LP, and remains in agreement with extrapolations of their number counts down to this flux limit." (The ALMA Frontier Fields Survey)
- Cosmic variance estimates (9-31%) are exceeded by Monte Carlo simulation uncertainties (75-281%) and Poisson uncertainties (48-79%) at the faintest flux limit.  
  > "At our faintest flux limit, uncertainties in the median combined cumulative counts coming from our Monte Carlo simulations are in the range 75-281%, while those from Poisson statistics are in the range 48-79%. From these we note that cosmic variance estimates from the three approaches mentioned before are all exceeded by these uncertainties." (Cosmic variance)
- Most (75-86% compared to Planck/COBE, or ~94% of the double power-law total) of the 1.1 mm extragalactic background light is resolved by demagnified sources down to ~0.013 mJy.  
  > "The contribution provided by our demagnified sources at $1.1\,\textmd{mm}$ down to $0.013$ ($0.133$) $\textmd{mJy}$ represents $74.5_{-38.4}^{+64.0}\%$ ($38.9_{-20.1}^{+33.9}\%$) of the EBL by (cite: Ade2014) and $86.4_{-50.0}^{+120.4}\%$ ($45.6_{-26.2}^{+57.3}\%$) of that by (cite: Odegard2019)." (Extragalactic background light); "For $S_{\textmd{lim}}=0.013$ ($0.133$) $\textmd{mJy}$, we obtain a median contribution of $18.8_{-8.8}^{+40.4}$ ($5.0_{-3.1}^{+42.1}$) $\textmd{Jy}\,\textmd{deg}^{-2}$, corresponding to $94.2_{-14.8}^{+5.3}\%$ ($27.3_{-17.0}^{+63.0}\%$) of our estimate of the total EBL." (Extragalactic background light)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Strong gravitational lensing by the Frontier Fields massive galaxy clusters magnifies background sources beyond the confusion limit, and the fields have rich public multiwavelength data and multiple independent mass models. MACSJ0717.5+3745 was excluded because it was only partially observed to much lower sensitivity.  
  > "This ALMA survey benefits from the unique ALMA capabilities and the strong gravitational lensing power of massive galaxy clusters, allowing to detect (if magnified) sources beyond the confusion limit (e.g., rms depths in the order of tens of $\mu\textmd{Jy}\,\textmd{beam}^{-1}$ for current deep ALMA maps at $1.1\,\textmd{mm}$)." (Introduction); "Since the sixth FF cluster, MACSJ0717.5+3745, was only partially observed in cycle 3 to much lower sensitivity, source extraction was not performed on it, and thus we do not include it in this study." (Observations with ALMA)
- Why this method: Monte Carlo simulations fold uncertainties from observed flux densities, redshift distributions, and multiple lens models into the number counts. S/N = 4.5 was chosen as a tradeoff between purity and completeness, PB > 0.3 was chosen to include a high-S/N detection (A370-ID02 at PB = 0.34), and peak intensity scaling was used for 4.5 <= S/N < 5 sources because uv-plane Gaussian fits have large uncertainties at low S/N.  
  > "We choose a threshold of $\textmd{S/N}=4.5$, as this is found to be an appropriate tradeoff between the purity and completeness correction factors that will be applied to our number counts (see $\S$[3](#sect_method))." (Observations with ALMA); "However, in one of the last two FFs we find a high $\textmd{S/N}$ source at $\textmd{PB}=0.34$, A370-ID02. Therefore, in this study we use $\textmd{PB}>0.3$ for all cluster fields." (Observations with ALMA); "As in Paper IV, we estimate the integrated flux densities of low-significance detections in A370 and AS1063 using source peak intensities; this choice is motivated by the large uncertainties in integrated source flux densities that are given at low S/N by two-dimensional Gaussian fits in the $uv$-plane." (Observations with ALMA)
- Alternative: Zitrin & Merten v3 lens models were rejected because only v4 or newer models (which use the best data to date as constraints) were considered; GLAFIC v4 was excluded for A370 because it did not cover A370-ID03.  
  > "We consider only v4 or newer models, as these use the best data to date as constraints[^4]. We also restrict our set only to models that cover the whole region where our ALMA sources lie. A370-ID03 is not covered by GLAFIC v4 model, and therefore we do not include it for A370." (Lensing models)
- Alternative: Photometric redshift for AS1063-ID05 (z_phot = 2.256) was rejected because its quality parameter p_Delta_z ~ 0.2 indicated a broad/multi-modal distribution; Herschel IR redshift (~0.9) for A370-ID03 was rejected in favor of its spectroscopic redshift.  
  > "AS1063-ID05 has a counterpart within $\approx0.3\ensuremath{''}$ having $z_{\textmd{phot}}=2.256_{-1.302}^{+1.766}$. However, it has $p_{\Delta z}\approx0.2$, indicating a broad and/or multi-modal probability distribution. Therefore, we do not include this redshift estimate in this work." (Source redshifts); "A370-ID03 has a IR-estimated redshift of $\sim0.9$ from SED fitting using five *Herschel* bands (see (cite: Rawle2016)); we do not use this estimate, as we prioritize the spectroscopic redshift found for A370-ID03." (Source redshifts)
- Alternative: Separate Eddington bias corrections were skipped because flux enhancements from SIDES simulations were consistent with image-plane deboosting corrections.  
  > "These ratios are consistent with the deboosting corrections obtained in Fig. [3](#fig_debcorr). We thus consider safe to skip Eddington bias corrections in this work." (Methodology)
- How they decided it was enough: Counts are presented down to 0.024 mJy because it is the faintest bin where at least one cluster has non-zero combined differential counts at the 84th percentile; redshift grids were precomputed with Delta z = 0.2 to keep the 1000 Monte Carlo realizations per model fast.  
  > "Since we need to create several source-plane maps for each lens model (sampling the source redshift distributions) but also want our Monte Carlo simulations to run fast, we precompute source-plane maps for all mass reconstructions and lens models, over the redshift range $z_{\textmd{min}}=0.4$ to $z_{\textmd{max}}=4$, using steps of $\Delta z=0.2$." (Methodology); "We present counts down to the flux density bin centered on $0.024\,\textmd{mJy}$, as this is the faintest flux density bin where at least one cluster field has non-zero combined differential counts at the 84th percentile." (Number counts)
- Limitation (authors): Nine out of 29 ALMA detections (and all but one of the faint-end contributors) lack reliable multiwavelength counterparts, requiring an assumed Gaussian redshift distribution (z = 2 +/- 0.5) and preventing detailed physical comparison with models.  
  > "Nine detections have no reliable counterparts in the aforementioned catalogs. As in Paper IV, for these sources we assume a Gaussian redshift distribution centered at $z=2$ with $\sigma=0.5$, which is consistent within $1\sigma$ with average redshift values found for faint dusty galaxies from the literature (e.g. (cite: Aravena2016; Dunlop2017; Aravena2020))." (Source redshifts); "Moreover, only one of the sources that contribute to our faint-end number counts has a reliable counterpart (AS1063-ID04, see Table [\[tab_snr45gals\]](#tab_snr45gals) and Fig. [6](#fig_flux_intr_flux_obs)), preventing a detailed comparison with (cite: Popping2020) results." (Number counts flattening)
- Limitation (authors): Some lens models (GLAFIC v4, Sharon v4, Williams v4/v4.1) do not fully cover the PB > 0.3 region (missing 0.7% to 19% of the area), where no magnification (mu = 1) had to be assumed.  
  > "A fraction of the region where the ALMA maps have $\textmd{PB}>0.3$ is not fully covered by the models GLAFIC v4 ($\sim2\%$ and $3\%$ for A2744 and AS1063, respectively), Sharon v4 ($\sim0.7\%$ for A370), Williams v4 ($\sim3\%$, $19\%$, $10\%$ and $14\%$ for A2744, MACSJ0416, MACSJ1149 and A370, respectively) and Williams v4.1 ($\sim14\%$ for A370). We consider no magnification effects ($\mu=1$) for the missing pixels in magnification maps." (Lensing models)
- Limitation (authors): Systematic discrepancies across different lens models reach up to ~0.6 dex in median differential counts, and for most sources model-to-model magnification discrepancies exceed individual model uncertainties.  
  > "For most sources, the discrepancies in the median values predicted by different models exceed their individual uncertainties, as Fig. [5](#fig_magnif_gal) shows." (Methodology); "We find variations across lens models in the median differential counts per flux bin up to $\approx0.6\,\textmd{dex}$ (although consistent within the error bars)." (Number counts)
- Limitation (authors): The bright end of the number counts (> 1 mJy) is expected to be affected by cosmic variance due to the small survey area, missing massive (> 10^11 M_sun) bright galaxies.  
  > "Therefore, from their estimates we expect the bright end of our number counts ($>1\,\textmd{mJy}$) to be more affected by cosmic variance, missing a fraction of the objects with stellar mass in the range $5\times10^{10}-10^{11}\,\textmd{M}_{\odot}$ and most of the objects at $>10^{11}\,\textmd{M}_{\odot}$ that a wider-area survey may detect." (Cosmic variance)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Low-significance detections (4.5 <= S/N < 5) make up 11/29 of the catalog (including almost all of the faintest demagnified sources), have a high expected spurious fraction (18-28% at S/N >= 4.5), lack multiwavelength counterparts (9/11), and rely on assumed source sizes and redshifts.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cycle 5 ALMA spectroscopic redshifts for 5 sources in A2744 are taken from an unpublished work in preparation (Bauer et al., in prep.) without showing spectra.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Differential magnification effects are neglected even though dust continuum sizes (r_eff ~ 0.23'') are extended.
