## Analysis recipe

1. **Evolve the planet–disk models**: Multi-fluid FARGO3D hydrodynamics with a simplified MHD wind torque  
  > "we use the hydrodynamic grid-basics code `FARGO3D` (cite: FARGO3D) with its multi-fluid version (cite: FARGO3D-multifluid)." (Numerical Setup); "our hydrodynamic simulations are limited by the 2D geometry, with the MHD wind only added as a torque term driving radial gas inflow." (Caveats and Outlook)
   - software: FARGO3D
   - parameter: Domain: 0.3–5.0 r_0; r_0 = 20 au; planet fixed at r_0  
  > "The simulation spans a computational domain from 0.3 $r_0$ to 5.0 $r_0$, with $r_0 =20$ au denoting the unit length in code, the planet is also fixed at this radial position." (Numerical Parameters)
   - parameter: 512 radial cells; 656 azimuthal cells  
  > "The domain resolution includes 512 radial cells and 656 azimuthal cells." (Numerical Parameters)
   - parameter: q = 0.0003; approximately 53 Earth masses  
  > "In this exploratory work, we use the same planet-to-stellar mass ratio $q=0.0003$ as in that parameter space." (Results); "Which corresponds to approximately 53 times Earth mass in DM Tau." (Results)
   - parameter: 1500 orbital periods at r_0, approximately 0.2 Myr  
  > "All of our simulations were run for 1500 orbital periods at $r_0$ (corresponding to $\sim 0.2$ Myr for physical units)" (Results)
   - parameter: Initial gas surface density: Sigma_g = Sigma_0 (r/r_0)^-1; Sigma_0 = 4 × 10^-4 M_star/r_0^2  
  > "\Sigma_{\rm g}(r) = \Sigma_0 ({r}/{r_0})^{-1}" (Numerical Parameters); "$\Sigma_0 = 4 \times 10^{-4} M_{\star}/r_0^2$ in code units" (Numerical Parameters)
   - parameter: h(r) = h_0 (r/r_0)^0.25; h_0 = 0.03  
  > "h(r) = c_s/V_{\rm K} = H/r = h_{0}(r/r_0)^{0.25}" (Numerical Parameters); "here we set $h_{0}=0.03$, which means a flatter disk compared to some other disks is considered in our simulations." (Numerical Parameters)
   - parameter: Five dust populations, logarithmically uniform from 30 micrometres to 3 mm; dust feedback included  
  > "In our model, we introduce five distinct dust populations, with particle sizes ranging logarithmically and uniformly from 30 $\mu$m to 3 mm." (Numerical Parameters); "The dust feedback is included in our simulations." (Numerical Parameters)
   - parameter: Initial size distribution s^-3.5; dust-to-gas mass ratio 1%; grain internal density 2.3 g/cm^3  
  > "The size distribution at the onset follows a power law of $s^{-3.5}$, as suggested by (cite: MRN)." (Numerical Parameters); "We set the initial dust-to-gas mass ratio to $\epsilon=1\%$, and assume a uniform internal density of $2.3~g/cm^{3}$ for the dust grains." (Numerical Parameters)
   - parameter: Wave-damping gas boundaries; open inner dust radial-velocity boundary  
  > "we implement wave-damping boundary conditions for the gas component, where the gas density and velocity are gradually adjusted back to their original radial profiles" (Numerical Parameters); "For the dust components, we employ open boundary conditions at the inner edge of the grid, specifically for their radial velocities." (Numerical Parameters)
   - assumption: 2D cylindrical polar, locally isothermal gas-and-dust disk  
  > "Our work consider a 2D cylindrical polar coordinate ($r$, $\varphi$), locally isothermal protoplanetary disk system with an embedded planet, composed of gas and dust." (Hydrodynamic Model)
   - assumption: Neglect disk self-gravity, planet migration and accretion  
  > "We neglect the disk self-gravity, planet migration and accretion in all of our simulations." (Hydrodynamic Model)
   - assumption: M_star = 0.53 M_sun  
  > "For DM Tau, we set $M_{\star} = 0.53~M_{\odot}$ of the host star in our simulations (cite: Gaia_2023)" (Hydrodynamic Model)
   - assumption: alpha_acc = alpha_dw + alpha_v  
  > "Therefore, the total accretion level in the disk is $\alpha_{\rm acc} = \alpha_{\rm dw} + \alpha_{\rm v}$, see (cite: Tabone22) for more details." (Hydrodynamic Model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

  > "Then to process our `FARGO3D` simulation results for use as inputs in `RADMC-3D`, we used the publicly available code `fargo2radmc3d` (cite: fargo2radmc3d-dust)." (Simobserve); "we first model the vertical expansion of the 2D dust density map into 3D by adopting follow Gaussian distribution for the dust densities along the vertical axis with 40 grids" (Radiative Transfer Calculations); "To calculate the dust absorption and scattering opacities, we apply Mie theory, utilizing `OpTool` (cite: optool-2021) for the computations." (Simobserve)
   - software: RADMC-3D, fargo2radmc3d, OpTool
   - parameter: 40 vertical grids; Gaussian dust density distribution  
  > "we first model the vertical expansion of the 2D dust density map into 3D by adopting follow Gaussian distribution for the dust densities along the vertical axis with 40 grids" (Radiative Transfer Calculations)
   - parameter: Ray tracing with 10^8 photons for scattering  
  > "For the determination of the dust continuum emission’s specific intensity, we deploy ray tracing with $10^8$ photons for scattering." (Simobserve)
   - parameter: 1.3 mm and 7 mm  
  > "our radiative transfer calculations at 1.3 mm and 7 mm wavelengths considered only dust thermal emission." (Observation at 1.3 mm and 7 mm)
   - assumption: Distance 145 parsecs  
  > "Based on the known observation data of DM Tau, we assume the disk distance is 145 parsecs far from us (cite: DMTau145pc_2007; Gaia2021; Gaia_2023)." (Simobserve)
   - assumption: Stellar radius 0.60 solar radii; effective temperature 3705 K  
  > "And the star radius is set to 0.60 solar radii with a stellar effective temperature of 3705 K (cite: Gaia_2023)." (Simobserve)
   - assumption: Inclination 35.2 degrees; position angle 158 degrees  
  > "For the geometry of the disk, we set the disk inclination of DM Tau is $35.2^{\circ}$ and the disk position angle is $158^{\circ}$ (cite: Kudo2018)." (Simobserve)

   - assumption: Dust thermal emission only  
  > "our radiative transfer calculations at 1.3 mm and 7 mm wavelengths considered only dust thermal emission." (Observation at 1.3 mm and 7 mm)
   - fitting: _not stated_

  > "Combining the parameter space study from (cite: WU_Chen_jiang_2023) with the morphological characteristics of DM Tau in dust continuum emission" (Results); "by adjusting $\alpha_{\rm dw}$ and $\alpha_{\rm v}$." (Results)

## Qualitative findings

- Combined wind and viscosity reproduce some observed substructures  
  > "Through planet-disk interaction and wind-viscosity interaction, we successfully fit some observational characteristics of DM Tau in different wavelength (ALMA Band 6 and JVLA Q Band)" (Conclusions)
- Appropriate viscosity breaks large clumps into smaller clumps while retaining a dust ring
