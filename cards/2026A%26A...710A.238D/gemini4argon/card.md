## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, align, and image multiwavelength ALMA continuum data**: Flag +/- 25 km s^-1 around lines, average to 125 MHz channels, align phase centers via Gaia DR3 and fixplanets, perform iterative phase and amplitude self-calibration, and image with tclean (mtmfs or multiscale) followed by uv-tapering and imsmooth to common beam sizes.  
  > "The continuum was extracted by flagging channels within $\pm$ 25 km s$^{-1}$ around the targeted emission lines, and the data were subsequently averaged over 125 MHz channel widths, following the same procedure as (cite: andrews2018)." (Calibration); "The final imaging was performed with the `tclean` task in CASA v6.6.5." (Imaging); "The final common beam size was achieved with the `imsmooth` task on the uv-tapered images, ensuring that all images of the same source have the same angular resolution." (Imaging)
   - software: CASA, astroquery, phaseshift, fixplanets, split, tclean, imsmooth
   - parameter: channel width = 125 MHz, line flagging = +/- 25 km s^-1  
  > "The continuum was extracted by flagging channels within $\pm$ 25 km s$^{-1}$ around the targeted emission lines, and the data were subsequently averaged over 125 MHz channel widths, following the same procedure as (cite: andrews2018)." (Calibration)
   - parameter: image size = 10", pixel size = 1/10 of highest-resolution beam, multiscale scales = 0, 2, 6, 12 times beam  
  > "For all sources, the pixel size was set to one-tenth of the synthesized beam of the highest-resolution dataset, and the image size was fixed to 10”." (Imaging); "Multiscale clean components with scales of zero, two, six, and 12 times the beam size were used, with a slight bias toward smaller scales to better recover compact emission." (Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-calibration repeated until no further improvement in S/N was achieved  
  > "Several rounds of phase self-calibration were performed, starting with a solution interval of infinite and progressively decreasing (to multiples of the integration time and finally to `solint=‘int’`) until no further improvement in S/N was achieved." (Calibration)
2. **Extract radial intensity, brightness temperature, and spectral index profiles, and measure disk sizes and halo fluxes**: Deproject images using literature inclination and PA, azimuthally average in radial annuli, compute brightness temperature using the full Planck function, compute spectral index profiles, and integrate flux densities to determine R_68, R_90, R_99 and halo flux.  
  > "After imaging, we generate azimuthally averaged intensity profiles by deprojecting the images using the disk’s inclination and position angle derived from literature." (Radial profiles); "The disk sizes $R_{68}$, $R_{90}$, and $R_{99}$, estimated as the radius enclosing 68%, 90%, and 99% of the total flux density, are computed for the most sensitive observation and are listed in Table 1." (Radial profiles); "From these profiles, we derive the brightness temperature profiles using the full Planck function, since we employ 0.45 mm data (ALMA Band 9) where the assumptions of low temperature and low frequency inherent to the Rayleigh–Jeans approximation no longer hold." (Radial profiles)
   - parameter: Halo starting radii: 86 au (Elias 2-24), 150 au (IM Lup), 130 au (DM Tau); outer boundary at 3 sigma_I of least sensitive observation (150, 300, 300 au)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected disks with smoothly declining outer continuum profiles beyond their outermost rings, archival coverage in at least three ALMA bands separated by > 20 GHz, angular resolution < 0.3" to separate inner features from the faint halo, and short-baseline data for large spatial scales.  
  > "For the dust characterization, it was essential that each source had archival data in at least three ALMA bands." (Sample selection); "In addition, we only included observations with angular resolutions < 0.3”, which ensured that the compact inner disk features could be spatially separated from the faint extended emission." (Sample selection); "All observations contained short baseline data to be sensitive to larger spatial scales." (Sample selection)
- Why this method: Used a 1D vertically isothermal slab model including anisotropic scattering because continuum emission traces the settled midplane and shorter wavelengths (e.g. 0.45 mm) require full Planck function and scattering; favored Bayes factor over chi^2 to naturally incorporate prior influence.  
  > "From these profiles, we derive the brightness temperature profiles using the full Planck function, since we employ 0.45 mm data (ALMA Band 9) where the assumptions of low temperature and low frequency inherent to the Rayleigh–Jeans approximation no longer hold." (Radial profiles); "These assumptions are well justified at (sub)millimeter wavelengths, where the continuum emission primarily traces the midplane layer in which the largest dust grains have settled (cite: pinte2016)." (Dust characterization); "We favor the Bayes factor over a traditional $\chi^{2}$ comparison because it naturally incorporates the influence of the prior." (SED modeling results)
- Alternative: Envelope contamination was ruled out based on axisymmetry, vertically thin geometry, and Keplerian rotation of the 12CO gas disk.  
  > "We ruled out contamination of the extended emission by envelope emission based on its axisymmetry, vertically thin geometry, and Keplerian rotation of the $^{12}$CO gas disk well beyond the continuum emission (Elias 2-24: (cite: pinte2023); IM Lup: (cite: martire2024); DM Tau: (cite: longarini2025))." (Sample selection)
- Alternative: Four dust compositions (DSHARP compact, DSHARP 70% porosity, ZUBKO compact, ZUBKO 70% porosity) were tested and three rejected per target based on Bayes factors and physical consistency.  
  > "In this work, we fit our free parameters using four dust compositions: DSHARP with no porosity, DSHARP with 70% porosity, ZUBKO with no porosity, and ZUBKO with 70% porosity." (Dust characterization); "By evaluating the Bayes factors relative to DSHARP (compact), we identify the preferred compositions for Elias 2-24, IM Lup, and DM Tau as ZUBKO (70% porosity), ZUBKO (compact), and DSHARP (compact), respectively." (Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: Stopped self-calibration rounds when S/N no longer improved; verified MCMC convergence via integrated autocorrelation times of 60-80 steps over 5000 steps.  
  > "Several rounds of phase self-calibration were performed, starting with a solution interval of infinite and progressively decreasing (to multiples of the integration time and finally to `solint=‘int’`) until no further improvement in S/N was achieved." (Calibration); "Across all radii and disks, the integrated autocorrelation times typically lie in the range of 60 - 80 steps, indicating that the number of steps satisfies standard convergence criteria." (Dust characterization)
- Limitation (authors): Substructures in smooth outer disks may remain undetected due to limited spatial resolution or high optical depth, making halo definition ambiguous.  
  > "Recent studies suggest that substructures in smooth disks may remain undetected due to limited resolution (cite: alvarado2025; quincy2026) or high optical depth (e.g., MP Mus; (cite: ribas2025)), in which case defining a halo would be ambiguous." (Radial profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Smoothing images to a common beam introduces beam dilution, which suppresses brightness temperatures and affects inner disk modeling when each radius is fitted independently without beam convolution.  
  > "Furthermore, smoothing the images introduces beam dilution, which can suppress the brightness temperature. This effect is an inherent limitation of our modeling approach." (Radial profiles); "The model is not convolved with the beam, since each radius is fitted independently." (Dust characterization)
- Limitation (authors): The 0.45 mm (Band 9) observation of DM Tau may trace a different vertical layer of the disk than longer wavelengths due to higher optical depth.  
  > "These substructures are not evident in the 0.9 mm and 1.3 mm profiles, despite their higher resolution, indicating that the 0.45 mm observations might trace a different vertical layer of the disk due to its higher optical depth (see Fig. [\[fig:optical_depth\]](#fig:optical_depth) in Appendix [\[appendix_opacity\]](#appendix_opacity) where $\tau \sim 0.3$ near B140)." (DM Tau.)
- Limitation (authors): In the halos of IM Lup and DM Tau, a_max and q are largely unconstrained (uncertainties of ~1 dex in a_max and ~1.2 in q), which propagates into large uncertainties in Stokes numbers.  
  > "At r < 90 au, $a_\mathrm{max}$ is $\sim$ 10 cm with an uncertainty of 0.7 dex, whereas at larger radii $a_\mathrm{max}$ remains mostly unconstrained with median values at 400 $\mu$m and uncertainties of 1 dex." (IM Lup); "Due to the larger uncertainties in $a_\mathrm{max}$, the inferred St carries significant uncertainties; however, throughout most of the halo we find St $\leq 1$, consistent with marginal dust–gas coupling." (IM Lup.)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For Elias 2-24, the gas surface density is not measured dynamically in the same way as IM Lup and DM Tau, but instead assumes a global gas-to-dust ratio of 100, making its halo gas-to-dust ratio (~196) dependent on that prior assumption.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Total dust masses rely on extrapolating a constant dust surface density from r = 11 au inward to r = 0 au, though the inner disk contribution to total area is small.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ad-hoc modifications were required in the SED fitting for Elias 2-24 (penalizing T_d outside 5th-95th percentiles) and DM Tau (restricting Sigma_d upper bound around B24 to 10 g cm^-2).
