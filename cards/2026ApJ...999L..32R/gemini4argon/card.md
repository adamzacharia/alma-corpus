## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA ACA visibility data and transform to cometocentric coordinates**: Flag, calibrate, and image in CASA using Högbom deconvolution with natural weighting and a 10'' mask; convolve with synthesized beam, apply Gaussian ACA primary beam correction, and transform to projected cometocentric distances centered on the peak gas flux.  
  > "The data were flagged, calibrated, and imaged using standard routines in Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA) package version 6.4.1 (cite: CASA2022)." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging); "We deconvolved the point-spread function with the Högbom algorithm, using natural visibility weighting, a 10$\ensuremath{''}$ diameter mask centered on the peak continuum position, and a flux threshold of twice the rms noise." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging); "We transformed the images from astrometric coordinates to projected cometocentric distances, with the location of the peak gas flux chosen as the origin" (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 10'' diameter mask, threshold = 2 x rms noise, natural weighting  
  > "We deconvolved the point-spread function with the Högbom algorithm, using natural visibility weighting, a 10$\ensuremath{''}$ diameter mask centered on the peak continuum position, and a flux threshold of twice the rms noise." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging)
   - parameter: ACA primary beam FWHM = 29.3'' at 338 GHz and 27.9'' at 354 GHz  
  > "corrected for the (Gaussian) response of the ACA primary beam (FWHM $29.3\ensuremath{''}$ at 338 GHz and $27.9\ensuremath{''}$ at 354 GHz, respectively)." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging)
   - assumption: Gaussian ACA primary beam response; origin at peak gas flux (<0.75'' offset from phasecenter)  
  > "with the location of the peak gas flux chosen as the origin, which was in good agreement with the comet’s predicted ephemeris position ($<0.75\ensuremath{''}$ offset between the peak position and the array pointing phasecenter)." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked ALMA Calibrator Catalog for quasar flux variability and verified peak gas flux position was within 0.75'' of the JPL Horizons ephemeris phasecenter  
  > "We examined the ALMA Calibrator Catalog for both quasars and found no evidence for significant variability in their Band 7 (345 GHz) fluxes during measurements taken within several days of our observations." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging); "which was in good agreement with the comet’s predicted ephemeris position ($<0.75\ensuremath{''}$ offset between the peak position and the array pointing phasecenter)." (Observing Log, Calibration, and Imaging)
2. **Construct non-LTE radiative transfer models and sample visibilities in the Fourier domain**: Generate 1D and 3D non-LTE radiative transfer model cubes using SUBLIME with Haser radial density profiles, time-average observed visibilities, Fourier-transform model cubes onto the observed uv coverage with vis_sample, and optimize parameters with lmfit.  
  > "We modeled molecular line emission using the SUBLIME radiative transfer code for cometary atmospheres (cite: Cordiner2022)." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "We then used the `vis_sample` program (cite: Loomis2018) to take the Fourier transform of our radiative transfer model cubes using the same $uv$ coverage as our (time averaged) ALMA observations" (Fourier Domain Modeling); "We used the `lmfit` application of the Levenberg-Marquardt minimization technique and retrieved uncertainties on our optimized parameters from the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix, minimizing the residual for the real and imaginary parts of the observed and modeled interferometric visibilities." (Fourier Domain Modeling)
   - software: SUBLIME, vis-sample, lmfit
   - parameter: CH3OH-H2O average collisional cross-section = 5 x 10^-14 cm^-2  
  > "using an average collisional cross-section with H$_2$O of $5\times10^{-14}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - parameter: Q(OH) = (5.7 +/- 0.6) x 10^28 s^-1 at r_H = 1.43 au; branching ratio = 85.5%  
  > "we scaled the $Q$(OH)=$(5.7\pm0.6)\times10^{28}$ s$^{-1}$ measured by (cite: Crovisier2025) at *r*~*H*~ = 1.43 au to the *r*~*H*~ of the ACA observations assuming insolation-driven (*r*~*H*~$^{-2}$) dependence and an branching ratio of 85.5% (cite: Huebner2015)." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
   - parameter: 3D cone geometry: phi = phi_STO, psi = psi_sun, gamma = 90 deg  
  > "We assumed hemispheric asymmetry along the projected Sun-comet vector ($\phi=\phi_\mathrm{STO}$, $\psi=\psi_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$, $\gamma=90\ensuremath{^\circ}$)." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Outgassing Geometry)
   - assumption: Insolation-driven r_H^-2 scaling of Q(H2O) from Q(OH); thermalization approximation for CH3OH-H2O collisions; Haser density distribution; constant expansion velocity in each region  
  > "There are no published state-to-state CH$_3$OH-H$_2$O collisional rates, so we calculated them using the thermalization approximation (cite: Crovisier1987; Biver1999; Bockelee-Morvan2012)" (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "We determined the CH$_3$OH distribution in $R_1, R_2$ using a Haser formalism (cite: Haser1957)" (Determination of CH$_3$OH Outgassing Geometry)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares minimization in lmfit on real and imaginary visibilities normalized by per-baseline noise; uncertainties from diagonal elements of the covariance matrix  
  > "We used the `lmfit` application of the Levenberg-Marquardt minimization technique and retrieved uncertainties on our optimized parameters from the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix, minimizing the residual for the real and imaginary parts of the observed and modeled interferometric visibilities." (Fourier Domain Modeling); "We normalized the residuals by the measured per-baseline noise at each iteration of the fitting process." (Fourier Domain Modeling)
   - validation: Compared 1D and 3D models via F-test on residual sum of squares (F = 1.45 vs critical value 2.99 at 95% confidence)  
  > "We performed an F-test calculating the residual sum of squares (RSS) between each model and the data and determining the F value as $F = \frac{RSS_{\mathrm{1D}}-RSS_{\mathrm{3D}}}{RSS_{\mathrm{3D}}}\frac{n-p_{\mathrm{3D}}}{p_{\mathrm{3D}}-p_{\mathrm{1D}}}$" (Determination of CH$_3$OH Production Mechanisms); "For $\alpha=0.05$ (95% confidence), the critical value for an F-distribution with $(p_{\mathrm{3D}}-p_{\mathrm{1D}},n-p_{\mathrm{3D}})$ degrees of freedom is 2.99, whereas our F-value is 1.45." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Production Mechanisms)
3. **Retrieve CH3OH kinematics, coma kinetic temperature profiles, parent scale length, and production rates across epochs**: First fit parent 1D and 3D models to Sept 18 high-res spectra (with T_kin = 60 K) to get initial kinematics; fix 1D v and Delta v to fit isothermal T_kin = 49 +/- 12 K on Sept 18 low-res J_K = 7_K-6_K lines; adopt T_kin = 49 K to run Delta-chi^2 grid over L_p on Sept 18 high-res spectra; apply 1D models to low-res epochs (scaling v as r_H^-0.5) and retrieve segmented T(r) profiles on Sept 22 and Oct 1.  
  > "We took $v$ and $\Delta v$ found from the 1D model of the high resolution spectra and fixed those values while running a 1D parent model of the low resolution spectra." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Outgassing Geometry); "We iterated over a range of $L_p$ values, performing least-squares fitting where the production rate and expansion speed were allowed to vary while holding all other parameters fixed." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Production Mechanisms); "Following (cite: Cordiner2023), we used a smoothed, segmented, linear $T(r)$ function (in $\log(r)-T$ space where $r\in[\theta_\mathrm{min},\theta_\mathrm{MRS}])$ using a variable number of segments $(n)$ of equal length $(l_s)$ in $\log(r)$ space." (Determination of Kinetic Tempature)
   - software: SUBLIME, vis-sample, lmfit
   - parameter: Initial T_kin = 60 K; retrieved Sept 18 T_kin = 49 +/- 12 K; assumed Aug 28 T_kin = 40 K  
  > "We assumed a gas kinetic temperature $T_{\mathrm{kin}}$ = 60 K as a first pass" (Determination of CH$_3$OH Outgassing Geometry); "we assumed an isothermal profile and let the production rate and kinetic temperature vary as free parameters, finding *T*~kin~=$49\pm12$ K." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Outgassing Geometry); "For August 28 (when only the $J_K=7_{-1}-6_{-1} E$ transition was detected) we assumed *T*~kin~ = 40 K based on our retrievals at smaller *r*~*H*~." (Determination of Kinetic Tempature)
   - parameter: Segmented T(r) radial points n = 2 at r = [3500 km, 12,870 km]  
  > "A good fit was found for $n=2$ radial points on both dates (being $r=[3500$ km, 12,870 km\])." (Determination of Kinetic Tempature)
   - parameter: Delta-chi^2 thresholds = 1 (1 sigma, 68% confidence) and 6.63 (2.6 sigma, 99% confidence)  
  > "so we obtained the 1$\sigma$ and 2.6$\sigma$ uncertainties from the values for $\Delta\chi^2$ = 1 (68% confidence) and 6.63 (99% confidence) thresholds." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Production Mechanisms)
   - assumption: v scales as r_H^-0.5 on low-resolution dates; parent model (L_p = 0 km) assumed for Aug 28, Sept 22, and Oct 1; constant temperature interior to theta_min/2 and exterior to MRS/2  
  > "we used a 1D model and assumed that $v$ varied as *r*~*H*~$^{-0.5}$ from the value retrieved from the high-resolution September 18 spectra" (Application to Low Resolution CH$_3$OH Spectra); "Thus, we used a parent model when applying 1D models to the low resolution CH$_3$OH spectra on all dates except September 18." (Application to Low Resolution CH$_3$OH Spectra); "We set the temperature at a constant value interior to a radius equal to half the minor axis of the synthesized beam $(r=\theta_\mathrm{min}/2)$ and exterior to a radius equal to half the maximum recoverable scale (MRS)." (Determination of Kinetic Tempature)

## Qualitative findings

- HCN and CH3OH showed distinct outgassing patterns: HCN was depleted in the sunward hemisphere (redward Doppler shift), whereas CH3OH was enhanced in the sunward hemisphere (blueward shift).  
  > "The molecules showed outgassing patterns distinct from one another, with HCN production being depleted in the sunward hemisphere of the coma, whereas CH$_3$OH was enhanced in that direction." (Abstract); "There was a blueward offset to the line center in contrast to HCN" (Production Mechanisms and Kinematics of CH$_3$OH)
- HCN production was indistinguishable from direct nucleus sublimation at 99% confidence, while CH3OH showed evidence of coma production (likely icy grain sublimation) at L_p > 258 km at 99% confidence, though direct nucleus release cannot be completely ruled out due to low S/N on the longest baselines.  
  > "Statistical analysis of molecular scale lengths in 3I/ATLAS indicated that CH$_3$OH included production from coma sources at $L_p>258$ km at 99% confidence. However low signal-to-noise on long baselines, which sample emission on small spatial scales closest to the nucleus, prevented definitively ruling out CH$_3$OH as purely a parent species." (Abstract); "In contrast, HCN production was indistinguishable from direct nucleus sublimation." (Abstract)
- The HCN line FWHM increased with angular scale on September 15, consistent with coma gas acceleration.  
  > "This is qualitatively consistent with the signatures of coma gas acceleration as seen in other comets (cite: Lammerzahl1987; Tseng2007; Cordiner2025b)." (HCN Modeling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Modeling was performed in the Fourier domain to avoid imaging artifacts from incomplete uv sampling and the CLEAN algorithm; 2-region 3D SUBLIME model was chosen as the simplest model capable of fitting the asymmetric line profiles.  
  > "We chose to perform modeling of the interferometric spectra in the Fourier domain to avoid the introduction of imaging artifacts arising from incomplete $uv$ sampling inherent to interferometric observations and the CLEAN algorithm itself (cite: Boissier2007; Bockelee2010; Cordiner2025b)." (Fourier Domain Modeling); "This is the simplest model capable of fitting the observed asymmetric CH$_3$OH line profile." (Radiative Transfer Modeling)
- Alternative: Gas-phase photolysis precursor for CH3OH was rejected because the retrieved L_p (and beta_p) on Sept 18 is inconsistent with any known cometary molecule with photolysis pathways to CH3OH, favoring sublimation from icy grains instead.  
  > "However, the $L_p$ (and in turn, $\beta_p$) retrieved from the high resolution spectra on September 18 is inconsistent with that for any known cometary molecule with photolysis pathways to CH$_3$OH (cite: Hrodmarsson2023), making it likely that the precursor was sublimation from icy grains (see § [4.2](#subsec:methanol-kinematics))." (Application to Low Resolution CH$_3$OH Spectra)
- Alternative: 3D vs 1D SUBLIME model for CH3OH: F-test showed the 3D model does not provide a statistically significant improvement over the 1D model at 95% confidence (F = 1.45 < 2.99).  
  > "Despite appearing to better reproduce the asymmetry of the red and blue peaks on short baselines (Figure [3](#fig:ch3oh-models)C), the 3D model does not provide a significantly better fit than the 1D model at the 95% confidence level (Appendix [8](#sec:fourier))." (Production Mechanisms and Kinematics of CH$_3$OH); "For $\alpha=0.05$ (95% confidence), the critical value for an F-distribution with $(p_{\mathrm{3D}}-p_{\mathrm{1D}},n-p_{\mathrm{3D}})$ degrees of freedom is 2.99, whereas our F-value is 1.45. Thus, we cannot reject the null hypothesis." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Production Mechanisms)
- Alternative: Strong inner-coma temperature gradient vs coma gas acceleration to explain HCN model underfitting on longest baselines and shortest-baseline widths; line FWHM increasing with angular scale favored coma gas acceleration.  
  > "On the one hand, this could be indicative of a strong temperature gradient in the coma, with our isothermal model underestimating the strength of the HCN (*J* = $4-3$) line in the warmer, inner coma." (Complex Line Profile and Production Mechanisms of HCN); "Such behavior could be indicative of coma gas acceleration (cite: Lammerzahl1987; Cordiner2025b), with smaller velocities near the nucleus resulting in increased line strengths on long baselines compared to the predictions of our constant velocity model." (Complex Line Profile and Production Mechanisms of HCN)
- How they decided it was enough: For radial temperature profiles, model complexity (number of segments n) was increased incrementally from a constant temperature until an acceptable fit was obtained without large-amplitude ripples; 1D vs 3D models were evaluated using an F-test at 95% confidence.  
  > "We incrementally increased the model complexity from the simplest assumption of a constant temperature until we obtained an acceptable fit to the data." (Determination of Kinetic Tempature); "We avoided ill-conditioned models (with too many segments) by testing for large-amplitude ripples in $T(r)$." (Determination of Kinetic Tempature); "An F-test is an appropriate comparison of the relative goodness of fit for nested models, where the null hypothesis is that the simpler (1D) model provided a better fit to the data." (Determination of CH$_3$OH Production Mechanisms)
- Limitation (authors): Low signal-to-noise on the longest ACA baselines prevented definitively ruling out CH3OH as purely a parent species.  
  > "However low signal-to-noise on long baselines, which sample emission on small spatial scales closest to the nucleus, prevented definitively ruling out CH$_3$OH as purely a parent species." (Abstract); "However, Figure [3](#fig:ch3oh-models) shows that CH$_3$OH was not firmly detected on the longest baselines, and we cannot completely rule out CH$_3$OH as a purely parent species." (Production Mechanisms and Kinematics of CH$_3$OH)
- Limitation (authors): Low spectral resolution (0.87 km s^-1) on Aug 28, Sept 18 (low-res), Sept 22, and Oct 1 was comparable to the line FWHM, preventing direct retrieval of kinematics or L_p on those dates.  
  > "For the low spectral resolution observations, the line full width at half maximum (FWHM) was comparable to the instrumental resolution; therefore, we could not retrieve robust kinematics directly from fits to the lines." (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "Unfortunately, the necessity to use an assumed expansion speed precludes a retrieval of $L_p$ on these dates due to its dependence on $v$." (Application to Low Resolution CH$_3$OH Spectra)
- Limitation (authors): Absence of contemporaneous Q(H2O) measurements required scaling Q(OH) measured at r_H = 1.43 au assuming r_H^-2 insolation-driven dependence, and CH3OH/HCN and CH3OH/H2O ratios relied on interpolating/extrapolating non-simultaneous CH3OH measurements.  
  > "In the absence of contemporaneously measured $Q$(H$_2$O) values, we scaled the $Q$(OH)=$(5.7\pm0.6)\times10^{28}$ s$^{-1}$ measured by (cite: Crovisier2025) at *r*~*H*~ = 1.43 au to the *r*~*H*~ of the ACA observations assuming insolation-driven (*r*~*H*~$^{-2}$) dependence" (Radiative Transfer Modeling); "affirming that 3I is a CH$_3$OH-enriched object compared to solar system comets while keeping in mind the caveats of comparing non-simultaneous measurements." (Comparison to Solar System Comets)
- Limitation (authors): Constant expansion velocity models underestimate HCN flux on long baselines and line width on short baselines; determining the v(r) acceleration law is left to future work.  
  > "The 1D and 3D models underestimate the HCN line strength on the longest baselines (smallest angular scales) on both dates, and underestimate the width of the line on the shortest baselines on September 15." (Complex Line Profile and Production Mechanisms of HCN); "Determining the $v(r)$ law for 3I’s coma is beyond the scope of this work and the subject of a future publication." (Complex Line Profile and Production Mechanisms of HCN)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CH3OH and HCN were never observed on the same date; the CH3OH/HCN ratios on Sept 12 and 15 rely on interpolating a power-law fit to Q(CH3OH) across non-simultaneous epochs, which is vulnerable to rotational or day-to-day outgassing variability.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): H2O collision partner densities in SUBLIME were estimated by scaling a single-epoch OH observation from r_H = 1.43 au as r_H^-2 across the 1.69-2.60 au transition zone where H2O sublimation was turning on.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming L_p = 0 km (parent model) for the Aug 28, Sept 22, and Oct 1 low-resolution CH3OH data while using L_p = 2884 km on Sept 18 introduces a systematic difference in how Q(CH3OH) is derived across the power-law fit.
