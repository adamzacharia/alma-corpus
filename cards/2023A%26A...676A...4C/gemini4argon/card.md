## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare, align, rescale, self-calibrate, and concatenate multi-epoch ALMA/ACA continuum visibilities**: Split Band 3 into two frequency groups (88 and 100 GHz) to avoid frequency smearing; visually inspect cubes and flag spectral lines; channel-average spectral windows; fix visibility weights with statwt; image with tclean and fit Gaussian peak with imfit; shift phase center to peak with fixvis and align metadata coordinates with fixplanets; rescale fluxes across execution blocks in overlapping uv-ranges using gencal/applycal; perform 3 rounds of phase-only self-calibration (solint=inf, 60s, 10s); combine datasets with concat and cut at shortest common baseline of 9.8 klambda.  
  > "First, we ran the statwt CASA routine in order to fix the visibility weights." (Data reduction and self-calibration); "We then imaged the averaged-channel visibilities with the tclean task and fitted a Gaussian with imfit to pinpoint the maximum of the emission. Next, we used the fixvis function to shift the phase center to the position of the peak of L1527" (Data reduction and self-calibration); "We cut the ALMA datasets at the shortest common baseline, set by B7 at 9.8 k$\lambda$, hence a Maximum Recoverable Scale (MRS) of roughly 26.0$''$, or 3500 au at the distance of the source." (L1527 as seen in the visibility space)
   - software: CASA, statwt, tclean, imfit, fixvis, fixplanets, gencal, gaincal, applycal, concat
   - parameter: Five final frequencies: 88, 100, 141, 249, and 348 GHz  
  > "This way, after calibration, we work with five final frequencies: 88, 100, 141, 249, and 348 GHz." (Data reduction and self-calibration)
   - parameter: Shortest common baseline cut: 9.8 klambda  
  > "We cut the ALMA datasets at the shortest common baseline, set by B7 at 9.8 k$\lambda$" (L1527 as seen in the visibility space)
   - parameter: Self-calibration solution intervals: inf (gaintype='G'), 60s (gaintype='T'), 10s (gaintype='T')  
  > "In the first round, we set the solution interval of gaincal to inf, combined the scans within each execution block and set gaintype $=$ "G"" (Data reduction and self-calibration); "In the second and third rounds, we combined the spectral windows of each block, shortened the solution intervals to 60 and 10 seconds, respectively, and set gaintype $=$ "T"" (Data reduction and self-calibration)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit in image plane with imfit to locate emission peak in each execution block  
  > "We then imaged the averaged-channel visibilities with the tclean task and fitted a Gaussian with imfit to pinpoint the maximum of the emission." (Data reduction and self-calibration)
   - validation: Evaluated peak S/N improvements (10-500%) on combined images; tested shorter self-calibration time intervals and found no appreciable improvement  
  > "The self-calibration yielded peak S/N improvements in the range 10-500 % when evaluated on the images obtained with the combined datasets." (Data reduction and self-calibration); "We did not find any appreciable improvement in the noise and signal-to-noise properties for phase-only self-calibration steps with even smaller time intervals." (Data reduction and self-calibration)
2. **Model visibilities to disentangle compact disk and extended envelope emission**: Fit a 10-parameter composite geometrical model (Gaussian for the compact disk plus a Plummer-like broken power law for the envelope) to the visibilities at each of the 5 frequencies using galario.  
  > "Thus, we consider a geometrical model in which a Gaussian, that will trace the compact emission, is summed to a power law (Plummer profile)" (Disentangling disk and envelope emission); "In our framework, `galario` would compute a model image given our total profile, then it would Fourier-transform it into synthetic visibilities and sample them at the uv-points covered by the antenna configurations with which the observations were performed." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission)
   - software: galario
   - parameter: 10 free parameters: I_0 (f_0), r, sigma, inc, PA, R_in, R_out, p, dRA, dDec  
  > "The 10 fit parameters were the flux amplitude $I_0$ and the $r$ factor; the width $\sigma$, the inclination (inc) and position angle (PA) of the Gaussian; the inner and outer envelope radii, $R_{in}$ and $R_{out}$, and the power law exponent $p$. Finally two parameters, dRA and dDec, fit the offset of the peak from the phase center." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission)
   - assumption: Envelope emission is constant within R_i, follows Plummer power law between R_i and R_out, and is zero beyond R_out; compact disk is represented by a 2D Gaussian  
  > "These are such that the envelope emission is constant within a radius $R_i$, it follows Eq. [\[eq:total_profile\]](#eq:total_profile) between $R_i$ and $R_{out}$ and it is null beyond $R_{out}$." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission)
   - fitting: Minimum-chi^2 MCMC fit using 80 walkers, 3,000 burn-in steps, and 7,000 post-burn-in steps (Figures 18-20 captions also note 50 or 60 walkers and 2000/5000 steps for some bands)  
  > "Then, it would run a minimum-$\chi^2$ fit between the data and the model visibilities." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission); "For each fit, 80 walkers were set to run for 3,000 burn-in steps and 7,000 more steps after the burn-in stage." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission)
   - validation: Compared derived disk radius (~75 au in B7), inclination (~80 deg), and Plummer power-law exponent (p = 2.6-3.1) with literature values (Aso et al. 2017, Ladd et al. 1991); checked dRA and dDec are near zero  
  > "If we define the radius of the disk to be the $2\sigma$ contour of the Gaussian component, then $R_{disk} \sim 75$ au in B7, consistent with what was found in the kinematical analysis of (cite: Aso2017)." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission); "Finally, the power law exponent of the Plummer profile ($p$) is found to be in the range 2.6-3.1, roughly consistent with what was found by (cite: Ladd1991)." (Fit results)
3. **Construct and fit the circumstellar disk SED and estimate free-free contamination**: Combine the ALMA disk flux densities derived from the Gaussian model component at 5 frequencies with literature VLA measurements from Melis et al. (2011) up to 6 cm, and fit power-law spectral indices across 0.88-7 mm and 35-60 mm.  
  > "To offer a more comprehensive view to the reader, we extended the range of wavelengths by including literature Very Large Array (VLA) measurements from (cite: Melis2011), up to 6 cm." (The circumstellar disk); "Fitting the SED in the 0.88 to 7-millimeter range using our measurements along with the values of (cite: Melis2011), we find an optically thick disk with $\alpha = 2.1 \pm 0.1$." (The circumstellar disk)
   - parameter: Wavelength ranges fitted: 0.88-7 mm for dust disk SED, 35-60 mm for free-free slope  
  > "Fitting the SED in the 0.88 to 7-millimeter range using our measurements along with the values of (cite: Melis2011), we find an optically thick disk with $\alpha = 2.1 \pm 0.1$." (The circumstellar disk); "The slope at these longer wavelengths is $\alpha_{35-60} = 0.33 \pm 0.17$, as reported in Fig. 5 and is compatible with what is expected for free-free emission (cite: Panagia1975)." (The circumstellar disk)
   - assumption: Distance d = 140 pc  
  > "In this paper we focus on a single source in Taurus, L1527 IRS, (140 pc, e.g., (cite: Torres2007), (cite: Zucker2019))" (Introduction)
   - fitting: Power-law SED slope fit  
  > "Fitting the SED in the 0.88 to 7-millimeter range using our measurements along with the values of (cite: Melis2011), we find an optically thick disk with $\alpha = 2.1 \pm 0.1$." (The circumstellar disk)
   - validation: Checked free-free emission contribution at millimeter wavelengths (<10% in B3 and <1% in B7)  
  > "The contribution of the free-free emission is lower than 10% in B3 and lower than 1% in B7." (The circumstellar disk)
4. **Derive the envelope spectral index as a function of spatial scale and position angle**: Subtract the best-fit galario Gaussian disk component from the visibilities, log-uniformly bin the residual envelope visibilities across uv-distance (starting from ~9 klambda), and perform a weighted linear regression to log10(F) vs log10(nu) across all 5 frequencies in each uv-bin, as well as in three position angle sectors (envelope, outflow, cavity walls) and between adjacent band pairs.  
  > "Starting from the common shortest baseline of $\sim$ 9 k$\lambda$, we log-uniformly bin the visibilities across the available uv-distances and then measure the spectral index in each bin." (The envelope); "Since we are interested in studying the spectral index of the envelope, we have subtracted the galario best-fit Gaussian component of our model (see Section 3) from the data." (The envelope); "To make the best use of our multiwavelength data, we fitted a line to the fluxes along all wavelengths in each uv-distance bin and we more robustly determined the spectral index." (The envelope)
   - parameter: Flux calibration uncertainty: 10% for B7, 5% for B3, B4, B6  
  > "We set the latter to 10% for B7 and 5% for bands 3, 4 and 6, following the prescriptions of the ALMA Handbook and on-sky analysis (cite: Remijan2019; francis20)." (The envelope)
   - parameter: Angle sectors: envelope (-20 deg < PA < 20 deg), outflow (70 deg < PA < 110 deg), cavity walls (remaining zones)  
  > "We divided the uv-plane into three different regions: the envelope ($-20^\circ< PA <20^\circ$), the outflow ($70^\circ< PA <110^\circ$) and the cavity walls (the remaining zones)." (The envelope)
   - fitting: Weighted linear regression across all 5 frequencies in each uv-distance bin  
  > "The uncertainty on the spectral index obtained by fitting all bands is the error on the slope obtained with the weighted linear regression." (The envelope)
   - validation: Compared multi-frequency fit with two-frequency adjacent-band spectral indices and across three PA sectors  
  > "For completeness, we show the spectral indices computed between adjacent bands in Appendix 8." (The envelope); "We do not observe any significant difference among the three spectral indices at scales larger than 200 au (Fig. 7)." (The envelope)
5. **Compute temperature-corrected dust emissivity index beta(R) and fit its radial gradient**: Check optical depth by comparing specific intensity F_0.88mm/Omega to Planck function B(T_dust) using a centrally illuminated envelope temperature profile; correct for departures from the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation by fitting log10[F_nu / B_nu(T)] = beta * log10(nu) + A in each uv-bin; fit a linear relation between beta and log10(R_au) using Bayesian linear regression in linmix.  
  > "We find that, even at the shortest of our wavelengths, the envelope emission is optically thin at all scales as $F_{0.88mm}/\Omega < B(T_{dust})$ by a factor of 10 (Fig. 8)." (Dust emissivity index); "Thus, using the same profile, we can obtain a temperature-corrected $\beta$ profile as: $$\begin{equation} \log_{10} \Bigg[\frac{F_{\nu}}{B_{\nu}(T)}\Bigg] = \beta \cdot \log_{10}({\nu}) + A , \label{eq:beta} \end{equation}$$" (Dust emissivity index); "We have run the fit by means of the Bayesian method described in (cite: Kelly2007) and implemented in linmix. The priors were uniformly distributed." (Dust emissivity index)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: Radial scale range fitted: 50 to 2000 au  
  > "We find evidence for an outward increasing $\beta$, rising from 0.8 to 1.6 between 50 and 2000 au radial scales" (Dust emissivity index)
   - assumption: Dust temperature profile T(R) = 38 L^0.2 (R / 100 au)^-0.4 with L_bol = 1.6 L_sun and d = 140 pc  
  > "To evaluate the optical thickness, we consider a typical dust temperature prescription for a centrally illuminated protostellar envelope ((cite: Shu1977), (cite: Butner1990), (cite: Terebey1993), (cite: Motte2001)): $$\begin{equation} T(R) = 38 L^{0.2} \Bigg(\frac{R}{100 au}\Bigg)^{-0.4}. \label{eq:temp} \end{equation}$$" (Dust emissivity index)
   - assumption: Envelope continuum emission is optically thin at all studied wavelengths and scales, and temperature profile is exact so Delta_beta = Delta_alpha  
  > "The error on the dust emissivity index has been considered to be the same one that affects the spectral index since we are assuming an exact temperature profile, thus $\Delta\alpha = \Delta\beta$." (Dust emissivity index)
   - fitting: Bayesian linear regression (Kelly 2007) with uniform priors via linmix  
  > "We have run the fit by means of the Bayesian method described in (cite: Kelly2007) and implemented in linmix. The priors were uniformly distributed." (Dust emissivity index)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity of beta to varying the temperature profile radial exponent between -0.3 and -0.6 and compared results with Galametz et al. (2019)  
  > "We also investigated the effects on $\beta$ of changing the temperature profile’s radial power law exponent. We found that exponents in the interval [-0.3,-0.6] do not affect our conclusions." (Dust emissivity index)

## Qualitative findings

- The compact circumstellar disk (within ~75 au) is optically thick across 0.88-7 mm, preventing direct constraints on disk grain sizes from the spectral index alone.  
  > "The high optical depth of the circumstellar disk of L1527 prevents us from concluding anything about its dust grain properties based on the spectral index alone." (The circumstellar disk)
- Free-free emission contributes less than 10% to the flux in Band 3 and less than 1% in Band 7.  
  > "The contribution of the free-free emission is lower than 10% in B3 and lower than 1% in B7." (The circumstellar disk)
- The disk-subtracted envelope spectral index is roughly constant (alpha ~ 3) from 50 to 2000 au and shows no significant azimuthal variations between the envelope, outflow, and cavity walls beyond 200 au.  
  > "Figure 6 shows that the fitted spectral index of the envelope of L1527 appears roughly flat in the studied range that spans from $\sim 2000$ down to 50 au, $\alpha \sim 3$." (The envelope); "We do not observe any significant difference among the three spectral indices at scales larger than 200 au (Fig. 7)." (The envelope)
- The envelope continuum emission is optically thin at all scales and wavelengths, but violates the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation in the cold outer regions.  
  > "Thus, while the envelope of L1527 is in the optically thin emission regime, it does not satisfy the RJ approximation and $\beta \neq \alpha - 2$." (Dust emissivity index)
- After correcting for departures from the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation, the envelope dust emissivity index beta shows a tentative positive outward gradient, increasing from ~0.8 at 50 au to ~1.6 at 2000 au, consistent with submillimeter grains within ~300 au.  
  > "We find evidence for an outward increasing $\beta$, rising from 0.8 to 1.6 between 50 and 2000 au radial scales" (Dust emissivity index); "Furthermore, low $\beta$ at scales lower than $\sim$ 300 au are consistent with the presence of submillimeter sized grains in the inner envelope." (Dust emissivity index)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: L1527 is nearby (140 pc), bright, and extensively observed across four ALMA bands (B3, B4, B6, B7) with both the 12m and 7m arrays, allowing a deep continuum combination covering 9.8 to 1000 klambda to simultaneously resolve the compact disk and recover the faint extended envelope.  
  > "Due to its distance and brightness, L1527 is one of the best studied class 0/I YSOs." (Introduction); "We collect every available ALMA and ACA dataset suitable for our study of L1527, aiming to combine them in the deepest continuum analysis of the envelope so far." (ALMA observations); "On top of enriching the studied frequency range, we used a combination of ALMA and ACA data to study the dust continuum emission with high S/N at scales 2 times larger than G19, toward both shorter and longer baselines." (L1527 as seen in the visibility space)
- Why this method: Working directly in the uv-plane avoids image reconstruction artifacts and non-linearities, and fitting a composite Gaussian + Plummer model across 9.8-1000 klambda removes the resolved disk more accurately than subtracting a constant long-baseline amplitude.  
  > "Considering the wider uv-coverage of the data we used (9.8-1000 k$\lambda$), we can more precisely model and subtract the compact emission by fitting a Gaussian component within a model that separately accounts for envelope and disk (see Section 3)." (L1527 as seen in the visibility space); "To investigate dust properties in the envelope of L1527, we worked in the visibility ($u, v$) plane. This approach has the main advantage of keeping the analysis clear of the artifacts and non-linearity from which image reconstruction algorithms suffer." (Disentangling disk and envelope emission)
- Alternative: Subtracting a constant visibility amplitude (F_200klambda) to remove the disk, as done in Galametz et al. (2019) and Miotello et al. (2014), was rejected in favor of fitting a 2D Gaussian + Plummer profile because the disk is resolved at long baselines.  
  > "G19 subtracted a constant amplitude (F$_{200 k\lambda}$) from each visibility at the two frequencies, assuming an unresolved disk that would contribute a constant flux at baselines shorter than 200 k$\lambda$ ($\sim$1.0$''$, or 140 au)." (L1527 as seen in the visibility space); "Considering the wider uv-coverage of the data we used (9.8-1000 k$\lambda$), we can more precisely model and subtract the compact emission by fitting a Gaussian component within a model that separately accounts for envelope and disk (see Section 3)." (L1527 as seen in the visibility space)
- Alternative: Computing spectral indices from only two adjacent frequency bands was shown to suffer from large uncertainties and systematic differences compared to fitting all five frequencies simultaneously.  
  > "Moreover, the spectral index $\alpha$ shows systematic differences among different combination of adjacent bands. Only using multiple bands (>2), one can robustly constrain the spectral index, damping the uncertainties that arise using only two frequencies (see Section 4)." (Adjacent bands spectral index)
- Alternative: Explaining low inner-envelope beta (<1) solely via dust composition/porosity variations or a much colder envelope temperature (<5 K at 300 au) was rejected as physically unreasonable.  
  > "However, to produce $\beta \sim$ 1.7, the temperature of the envelope at 300 au would need to be lower than 5 K, which is far below a reasonable value for a protostellar envelope." (Grain size, composition and temperature effects on $\beta$); "None of these possibilities alone are able to justify $\beta$ values as low as 1 at hundreds of au, thus strengthening the hypothesis of submillimeter grains at these scales." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: In situ coagulation of millimeter grains in the low-density envelope is disfavored by theoretical models, so differential collapse of prestellar core grains or outflow transport of grown disk grains are considered as viable alternatives.  
  > "While in situ formation seems disfavored by most theoretical studies, differential core dust collapse or outflow dust transport from the disk could explain our observations." (Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: Collected almost every available archival ALMA/ACA dataset to maximize S/N; stopped self-calibration at 10s intervals when shorter intervals gave no further S/N improvement; tested temperature profile exponents from -0.3 to -0.6 to verify robustness of beta.  
  > "We did not find any appreciable improvement in the noise and signal-to-noise properties for phase-only self-calibration steps with even smaller time intervals." (Data reduction and self-calibration); "We also investigated the effects on $\beta$ of changing the temperature profile’s radial power law exponent. We found that exponents in the interval [-0.3,-0.6] do not affect our conclusions." (Dust emissivity index)
- Limitation (authors): A given interferometric baseline B probes all structures within scale theta ~ lambda/B rather than an isolated annulus, so the derived alpha and beta at a given uv-distance represent flux-weighted averages that can be dominated by the brighter inner envelope.  
  > "First, while we present a study of the spectral- and dust emissivity indices as a function of uv-distance and physical source scales, we have to keep in mind that a particular baseline does not only probe an annular region at some distance from the center, rather it probes any physical structure within a scale given by $\theta \sim \lambda/B$." (Caveats); "If the inner envelope is much brighter than the outer one, as expected, the flux at even the shorter baselines will be dominated by the inner envelope." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): The dust emissivity index derivation relies on a simple 1D analytical power-law temperature profile T proportional to R^-0.4 rather than 3D radiative transfer modeling with temperature uncertainties.  
  > "a more thorough treatment that accounts for the temperature structure and its associated uncertainties can be achieved by post-processing 3D envelope models with radiative transfer in order to." (Caveats)
- Limitation (authors): High optical depth of the edge-on disk prevents extracting dust grain properties within the disk without radiative transfer modeling.  
  > "No dust grain properties information can be extracted in such a condition without thorough radiative transfer modelling, outside of the scope of this work." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Deprojecting and mapping uv-distance directly to a physical radius R_au (via R ~ lambda/B) treats the visibility amplitude at baseline B as local to radius R, whereas the Fourier transform of a power-law envelope mixes emission across scales.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Gaussian + Plummer visibility fit is performed independently in each frequency band, resulting in band-dependent envelope outer radii (R_out = 1.35'' in B7 vs 12.8'' in B4) and disk widths (sigma = 0.147'' to 0.330''), so subtracting the fitted Gaussian removes slightly different spatial structures at each frequency.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Primary beam attenuation at large scales (19'' FWHM in B7 for the 12m array) is estimated to be ~10% at 20 klambda, but visibilities from 12m and 7m arrays are concatenated directly in the uv-plane without joint primary-beam deconvolution.
