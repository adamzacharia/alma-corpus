## Analysis recipe

1. **Image line cubes and combine 12 m, 7 m and TP data**: Continuum-subtract, tclean 12 m+7 m together with multiscale and auto-multithresh, then feather with TP  
  > "After subtracting continuum emission, we combined the 12 m array data with the 7 m array data" (Observations and Data Reduction); "we combined TP data with the interferometric images using the CASA task, feather" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, feather
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5; multiscale scales 0, 5, 15, 25, 50 x pixel; pixel 0.2''  
  > "We used TCLEAN with Briggs’ robust weighting of 0.5 to the visibilities and an imaging option of MULTISCALE with scales of 0, 5, 15, 25, and 50 times the pixel size (0.2$''$)" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: auto-multithresh: sidelobethreshold 1.75, noisethreshold 2, lownoisethreshold 1.5, negativethreshold 0, minbeamfrac 0.3  
  > "are set to 1.75, 2, 1.5, 0, and 0.3, respectively" (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared channel maps and spectra before and after TP combination to show TP matters for line shape  
  > "it is clear that the combination of the 7 m and TP matters and can alter the conclusion." (Total–power Data Combination Effects)
2. **Extract core-averaged HCO+ and HNC spectra and test detection**: Average spectra within elliptical core masks from the continuum dendrogram (major/minor axes, position angle); detection at >5 sigma  
  > "Line profiles of HCO$^{+}$ and HNC toward the 304 identified cores were extracted by averaging within the cores." (Line Detection); "HCO$^+$ emission was detected in 282 cores, and HNC in 285 cores, at the $>5\sigma$ noise level." (Line Detection)
   - parameter: ellipse mask from dendrogram major/minor axes and position angle of the 1.3 mm continuum cores  
  > "This was done by creating an ellipse mask from the major and minor axes and position angle information obtained from dendrogram analysis applied to continuum emission (cite: Morii23)." (Line Detection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Determine systemic velocity V_thin of each core from optically thin lines**: Use DCO+ / N2D+ 3-2 (Morii24 Gaussian fits), brightest DCO+ peak for multi-component cores, N2H+ 1-0 isolated hyperfine component for the rest, region-average V_thin for cores with no thin detection; flag 121 cores  
  > "we first utilized the optically thin DCO$^{+}$ and N$_2$D$^{+}$ ($J$=3–2) lines." (Systemic Velocity Determination); "we fit an isolated hyperfine component of N$_2$H$^+$ ($J$=1–0, $F_1, F$ = 0,1–1,2 $\nu$= 93.176265 GHz)" (Systemic Velocity Determination); "we assigned the average $V_{\rm thin}$ of their respective regions." (Systemic Velocity Determination)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian fits to core-averaged DCO+/N2D+ spectra (Morii24); N2H+ hyperfine component fit (details not stated)  
  > "applied a single Gaussian fit to the core-averaged spectra" (Systemic Velocity Determination)
   - validation: Spectra of 222 cores checked by eye along with DCN, 39 found with multiple velocity peaks  
  > "By carefully checking the spectra of these 222 cores, along with DCN($J$=3–2), we found that 39 cores host multiple velocity peaks." (Systemic Velocity Determination)
4. **Check HCO+ optical depth using HC18O+**: LTE column density from HC18O+ 3-2 Gaussian fit, scaled by the 16O/18O Galactocentric ratio to HCO+, then inverted for peak tau(HCO+) in 30 cores in G022.69, G023.47, G030.91; 3 sigma upper limits for non-detections  
  > "we utilized the rarer isotopologue HC$^{18}$O$^+$ ($J=3-2$) as an optically thin reference." (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$)); "For the remaining 16 cores where HC$^{18}$O$^+$ was not detected, we used the $3\sigma$ rms noise level of the spectra to derive upper limits" (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$))
   - parameter: T_ex = 18.0, 13.9, 12.4 K for G022.69, G023.47, G030.91 (Herschel SED, Morii23); Q(T_ex)=0.4671 T_ex + 0.3445  
  > "We adopted the excitation temperature $T_{\rm ex} =$18.0, 13.9, and 12.4 K for G022.69, G023.47, and G030.91, derived from Herschel SED fitting (cite: Morii23)" (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$)); "the partition function is approximated as $Q(T_{\rm ex}) = 0.4671\,T_{\rm ex} + 0.3445$." (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$))
   - assumption: LTE, both isotopologues share the same T_ex; T_ex possibly overestimated would underestimate tau  
  > "Assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)" (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$)); "and assumed that both isotopologues share the same $T_{\rm ex}$." (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$))
   - assumption: 16O/18O = 58.8 R_GC + 37.1 (Wilson94), ratios ~295, ~284, ~379  
  > "corresponding to ratios of $\sim$295, $\sim$284, and $\sim$379, respectively" (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$))
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to HC18O+ line for integrated intensity (details not stated)  
  > "is the integrated intensity estimated from a Gaussian fit to the HC$^{18}$O$^+$ line" (Optical Depth Estimation of HCO$^{+}$ ($J=3-2$))
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Quantify blue asymmetry with delta_v and A**: delta_v = (V_thick - V_thin)/dV_thin (Mardones97) and A = (I_blue - I_red)/(I_blue + I_red) (Jackson19), integrated within +/-2 FWHM_thin of V_thin  
  > "Here $V_{\rm thick}$ is the brightest peak velocity of the optically thick line, $V_{\rm thin}$ is the peak velocity of the optically thin line tracing the systemic velocity of the dense core" (Infall Statistics); "The integration range for $I_{\rm blue}$ extended from $V_{\rm thin} - 2\Delta V_{\rm thin}$ to $V_{\rm thin}$ and that for $I_{\rm red}$ was from $V_{\rm thin}$ to $V_{\rm thin} + 2\Delta V_{\rm thin}$" (Infall Statistics)
   - parameter: delta_v < -0.25 counts as significant blue asymmetry; cores with uncertainty >100% shown in gray  
  > "Restrictively, $\delta_v<-0.25$ is considered to be a significant blue asymmetric profile." (Infall Statistics); "Cores with a large uncertainty ($>$100%) are colored in gray." (Infall Statistics)
   - assumption: V_thin traces the core systemic velocity  
  > "is the peak velocity of the optically thin line tracing the systemic velocity of the dense core" (Infall Statistics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Both metrics and both lines compared for consistency  
  > "we found almost consistent results across the two metrics and also across the lines." (Infall Statistics)
6. **Classify line profile shapes**: Find S/N>3 peaks within +/-4 sigma_thin of V_sys, fit single or double Gaussian (upgrade if residual > 5 sigma), then classify as single / skew / double peak / complex using centroid separation and widths  
  > "First, all local intensity maxima with S/N $>$3 were identified within a velocity range of $\pm$ 4$\times \sigma_{\rm thin}$ around the systemic velocity ($V_{\text{sys}}$)." (Line Profile Classification); "If the resulting residuals exceeded $5\sigma_{\rm rms}$ noise level, the model was upgraded to a double-Gaussian to account for hidden sub-structures." (Line Profile Classification)
   - parameter: Delta v < 0.2 km/s: single; Delta v > sigma1+sigma2: double peak; either sigma > 2.0 km/s: complex; otherwise skew  
  > "If $\Delta v < 0.2\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$" (Line Profile Classification); "If $\Delta v > \sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}$" (Line Profile Classification); "If either Gaussian width satisfied $\sigma>2.0\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$" (Line Profile Classification)
   - fitting: One- or two-Gaussian fits; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "We measured the peak velocity and performed one- or two-Gaussian fits" (Line Profile)
   - validation: Classification compared against delta_v and A, and between HCO+ and HNC  
  > "These results confirm that the quantitative asymmetry parameters and the morphological classification are in good agreement." (Line Profile)
7. **Relate infall signatures to core properties and evolutionary stage**: Compare delta_v, A and profile classes versus core mass, surface density, virial parameter, bound/unbound status and evolutionary stage (prestellar, outflow, warm) using scatter plots, boxplots and binned fractions  
  > "Single and red skew profiles are seen in cores with low-mass, low-density, and relatively high virial parameters." (Line Profile Classification and Core Properties); "Blue skew, blue double-peak, and red double-peak profiles are found in relatively more massive and dense cores with a small virial parameter ($\lesssim$2)." (Line Profile Classification and Core Properties)
   - software: seaborn, Matplotlib
   - fitting: Regression lines overlaid on scatter plots (details not stated)  
  > "The lines overlaid are regression plots." (Infall Parameters and Physical Parameters)
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Measure clump-scale asymmetry from TP data and correlate with core scale**: Compute delta_v and A on clump-scale total-power spectra and compare with clump density, protostellar fraction, number of cores and core-scale blue fraction f_blue,all  
  > "Using the total-power data, we measured $\delta_v$ and $A$ parameters at the clump-scale." (Hierarchical Inward Motion); "$f_{blue, all}$ is the fraction of blue-skew or blue-double peak profiles against the total number of cores in the region." (Hierarchical Inward Motion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note TP data may partly reflect summed emission from multiple cores  
  > "However, we cannot fully rule out the possibility that the TP data partially reflect the cumulative emission from multiple cores rather than purely tracing the diffuse clump gas." (Hierarchical Inward Motion)
9. **Rough infall velocity indicators**: Compare dip velocity minus systemic velocity and double-peak separation across evolutionary stage and mass as proxies for infall velocity and velocity dispersion  
  > "which roughly trace the infall velocity and the velocity dispersion, respectively." (Implications for Infall Velocity)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors state careful radiative-transfer fitting is required for real infall parameters  
  > "more careful fitting with parameters of optical depth, temperature, and density, as well as infall velocity, is required to estimate the infall parameters." (Implications for Infall Velocity)

## Qualitative findings

- Blue asymmetry is detected from the prestellar stage and becomes more dominant with evolution  
  > "This implies that the blue asymmetry profile emerges from the prestellar stage and becomes increasingly dominant as evolution proceeds." (Line Profile Classification and Core Properties)
- HNC shows a higher blue-asymmetry fraction than HCO+, attributed to outflow contamination of HCO+  
  > "with HNC showing slightly higher detection rates of blue asymmetry (58% vs. 50% for $\delta_v < 0$)" (Conclusions)
- Moderate correlation between clump-scale and core-scale inward motion, interpreted as hierarchical collapse  
  > "The correlation between clump-scale and core-scale inward motion implies multiscale, hierarchical collapse (cite: Smith09; VazquezSemadeni24)" (Hierarchical Inward Motion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Core-scale infall in high-mass regions had been reported only in a few case studies, so a large ALMA sample of 70 um-dark clumps was needed  
  > "direct kinematic evidence of gravitational collapse via blue asymmetry at the core scale has been reported in only a few individual case studies (cite: Csengeri11; Contreras18; Morii25)" (Introduction)
- Why this method: An optically thin line is used to confirm blue asymmetry and avoid multi-velocity-component contamination  
  > "To avoid contamination from the multi-velocity components in a line of sight, the blue asymmetry profile is confirmed with an optically thin line." (Infall Statistics)
- Alternative: Blue asymmetry in unbound cores may reflect external pressure-driven collapse, or an overestimated non-thermal velocity dispersion caused by infall broadening  
  > "Or, as implied by (cite: Morii25), this may be due to the overestimation of the non-thermal velocity dispersion caused by the broadening produced by local infall motions." (Line Profile Classification and Core Properties)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Clump-scale TP data may include cumulative emission from multiple cores  
  > "However, we cannot fully rule out the possibility that the TP data partially reflect the cumulative emission from multiple cores rather than purely tracing the diffuse clump gas." (Hierarchical Inward Motion)
- Limitation (authors): Optical depth is a lower limit and depends on assumed T_ex  
  > "These values serve as lower limits due to the uncertainties in excitation temperature" (Line Detection)
- Limitation (authors): Blue fraction is not significantly higher than the red fraction  
  > "However, the fraction of blue asymmetry profiles is still not significantly higher than that of red asymmetry." (Infall Statistics)
- Limitation (authors): Quantitative infall rates deferred to a later paper  
  > "Detailed radiative transfer modeling to derive quantitative infall rates and other physical parameters will be presented in a subsequent paper." (Introduction)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Blue asymmetry fractions of 50-58% are close to the red-asymmetry fraction, so a null of random asymmetry is not clearly excluded
- Caveat (inferred, ours): V_thin is uncertain for 121 flagged cores (82 without DCO+/N2D+ detection, 39 multi-component) yet they are included in headline fractions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Blue asymmetry can arise from outflow, multiple components or geometry, not just infall; no radiative-transfer model is run here
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core-averaged spectra smooth over sub-core structure; Tex is assumed for tau estimates from only 3 of 24 regions
