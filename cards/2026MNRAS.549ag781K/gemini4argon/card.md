## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA CO(2-1) cube and match resolution to JWST/MIRI-MRS**: Recreate calibrated MSs via CASA pipeline, isolate CO(2-1) spectral window, subtract 0th-order continuum with uvcontsub, image both configurations jointly in 4.5 km/s channels with tclean using Briggs robust=2.0, multi-scale then hogbom cleaning, apply primary beam correction, smooth to 0.8 arcsec Gaussian beam and re-grid to MRS.  
  > "Each MS was trimmed down to contain only the UV visibilities from the spectral window containing the CO(2-1) line, afterwards the 0th order polynomial fit to the continuum emission in the spectral window was then subtracted using the `uvcontsub` task." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations); "We perform imaging of the CO(2-1) line in 4.5 km s$^{-1}$ channels combining data from both MSs with `tclean`, adopting Briggs weighting (cite: briggs) with robust of 2.0" (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations); "Lastly, we smooth the primary beam-corrected CO cube to a Gaussian beam with FWHM of $0.8$ arcsec and re-grid the cube to match the worst spatial resolution of the MRS data in Channel-4." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: channel width = 4.5 km s^-1, robust = 2.0, multiscale scales = [0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 3.0] arcsec, smoothed beam = 0.8 arcsec  
  > "We perform imaging of the CO(2-1) line in 4.5 km s$^{-1}$ channels combining data from both MSs with `tclean`, adopting Briggs weighting (cite: briggs) with robust of 2.0, `auto-multithresh` masking (cite: automasking), and the `multi-scale` deconvolver (cite: mulitscale) to model emission on scales of 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, and 3.0 arcseconds." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
   - fitting: 0th order polynomial fit in uvcontsub  
  > "afterwards the 0th order polynomial fit to the continuum emission in the spectral window was then subtracted using the `uvcontsub` task." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
   - validation: Visual inspection and manual editing of cleaning masks in interactive mode down to 3 sigma_RMS, then deep hogbom clean to 1 sigma_RMS  
  > "We use the interactive mode to visually inspect and manually edit the cleaning mask generated at each cycle as needed, until the cycle threshold reaches $\sim 3\sigma_{\rm RMS}$" (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
2. **Reduce JWST/MIRI-MRS IFU data and extract nuclear and integrated spectra**: Run 3-stage JWST pipeline with CRDS context jwst_1256.pmap, cosmic ray shower finding, sigma-clipping of warm pixels on rate files, residual fringe correction, dedicated background subtraction, and pixel_replace; extract R = 0.5 and 1.5 arcsec aperture spectra and stitch continuum across channels.  
  > "We used the standard MRS data reduction procedure using the JWST pipeline (cite: labiano16) using the Calibration Reference Data System (CRDS) context `jwst_1256.pmap`." (JWST/MIRI-MRS); "We first extract the spectra from each MRS cube (Channels 1-4: Short, medium and long) using two circular apertures with radii 0.5 and 1.5 arcsec (corresponding to physical scales of $\approx$0.4 and 1.3 kpc, respectively), which we call nuclear and integrated spectra." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
   - software: JWST pipeline
   - parameter: CRDS = jwst_1256.pmap, find_showers = True, skip_residual_fringe = FALSE, outlier threshold = 99%, pixel_replace = True, aperture radii = 0.5 and 1.5 arcsec  
  > "The first stage performs detector-level corrections, where we turned on the `find_showers` keyword and kept the rest of the default pipeline parameters unchanged." (JWST/MIRI-MRS); "This stage also performs additional corrections to residual fringes in the individual channels by setting the parameter `skip_residual_fringe` to `FALSE`." (JWST/MIRI-MRS); "The parameters for the third stage of the pipeline were mostly left unchanged, except changing the outlier threshold at 99%." (JWST/MIRI-MRS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared nuclear and integrated MRS spectra with archival Spitzer/IRS spectra  
  > "We also compare the nuclear and integrated spectra with archival Spitzer/IRS spectra and demonstrate the incredible improvement in depth and spectral resolution of MRS compared to these archival spectra." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
3. **Model mid-infrared continuum and warm H2 rotational lines S(1)-S(8)**: Model overall mid-IR continuum, PAH emission, and silicate absorption with CAFE; subtract continuum and fit H2 0-0 S(1)-S(8) lines with single Gaussians within +/-0.03 um windows in aperture spectra and spaxel-by-spaxel; apply CAFE extinction corrections and instrumental LSF corrections.  
  > "Therefore, we only use the overall continuum models from `CAFE`, and use a custom script to model the S(1) – S(8) H$_{2}$ lines after subtracting the overall continuum. Each H$_{2}$ line is fitted with a single Gaussian function around spectral windows within $\pm 0.03\mu$m of the respective line centres." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data); "The extinction correction factors were obtained from `CAFE`." (Warm molecular gas morphology and kinematics)
   - software: CAFE
   - parameter: spectral window = +/-0.03 um around line centre, 100 Monte Carlo mock spectra for uncertainties  
  > "Each H$_{2}$ line is fitted with a single Gaussian function around spectral windows within $\pm 0.03\mu$m of the respective line centres." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data); "To estimate the uncertainties on the derived parameters, we generated 100 mock spectra by adding rms noise to the modeled spectrum and repeated the line-fitting procedure for each realization." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per H2 line selected via Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC); integrated fit parameters used as prior for spaxel-by-spaxel fits; uncertainties from 100 noise realizations.  
  > "We did not add additional Gaussians, based on minimal changes in the Bayesian Information Criterion (cite: gideon78) values between single and multiple Gaussian models." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data); "We also fit each H$_{2}$ line spaxel-by-spaxel with a single Gaussian function and using the parameters from the integrated line fitting as a prior." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
   - validation: BIC comparison between single and multiple Gaussian models  
  > "We did not add additional Gaussians, based on minimal changes in the Bayesian Information Criterion (cite: gideon78) values between single and multiple Gaussian models." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
4. **Fit ALMA CO(2-1) emission line profiles and derive non-parametric kinematics**: Extract R = 1.5 arcsec integrated spectrum and spaxel-by-spaxel spectra from ALMA CO(2-1) cube, fit up to two Gaussians selected by BIC, and compute non-parametric v_50, w_80, and total flux.  
  > "To model the cold molecular gas using the CO(2-1) emission line, we follow the same procedure as modelling the H$_{2}$ emission lines in the mid-infrared spectra i.e., use multiple Gaussian functions based on the BIC criterion." (Resolved CO(2-1) characterisation with ALMA); "We derive the following parameters with CO(2-1): $v_{50}$, $w_{80}$ ($v_{90} - v_{10}$) and the total flux of the two Gaussian profiles." (Resolved CO(2-1) characterisation with ALMA)
   - parameter: aperture radius = 1.5 arcsec, maximum Gaussians = 2  
  > "At most two Gaussians were required to reproduce the CO(2-1) line profile. The CO(2-1) spectra extracted from a circular aperture of radius 1.5 arcsec and the multi-Gaussian model is shown in Fig. 6." (Resolved CO(2-1) characterisation with ALMA)
   - fitting: Multi-Gaussian fitting (up to 2 Gaussians) selected via BIC  
  > "To model the cold molecular gas using the CO(2-1) emission line, we follow the same procedure as modelling the H$_{2}$ emission lines in the mid-infrared spectra i.e., use multiple Gaussian functions based on the BIC criterion." (Resolved CO(2-1) characterisation with ALMA)
   - validation: BIC criterion for number of Gaussian components  
  > "use multiple Gaussian functions based on the BIC criterion." (Resolved CO(2-1) characterisation with ALMA)
5. **Model VLT/MUSE optical spectra, construct BPT maps, and measure ionised outflow rate**: Subtract stellar continuum using ppxf within LZIFU, fit optical emission lines (Hbeta, [O III]5007, Halpha, [N II]6585, [O I]6300, [S II]6716,6731) with up to 2 Gaussians plus linear continuum, construct [N II] and [O I] BPT maps, compute [O III] v_50 and w_80, and derive ionised gas mass outflow rate.  
  > "To model these spectra, we first subtract the contribution from the stellar continuum in the optical spectra using penalised pixel fitting routine (cite: cappellari04; cappellari17), implemented within the `LZIFU` software (cite: ho16; kreckel18)." (Resolved BPT maps with VLT/MUSE); "We also calculate mass outflow rates in the centre of IR20551 (using the MUSE aperture spectrum shown in Fig. [8](#fig:muse_fluxmaps)) following the method described in (cite: kakkad22), assuming solar metallicity" (Warm molecular gas morphology and kinematics)
   - software: LZIFU, ppxf
   - parameter: aperture radius = 1.5 arcsec, maximum Gaussians = 2, [O III]5007:[O III]4959 = 3:1  
  > "A maximum of two Gaussians was required to reproduce the emission lines. We employ additional constraints while performing these Gaussian fits, such as setting the emission line ratio [O iii]$\lambda$5007:[O iii]$\lambda$4959 = 3:1 and coupling the centroid locations of all emission lines based on their expected locations in the spectra." (Resolved BPT maps with VLT/MUSE)
   - assumption: Solar metallicity of the gas; broad Gaussian component represents outflowing gas  
  > "assuming solar metallicity of the gas and calculating the electron density from the [S ii]$\lambda\lambda$6716, 6731 doublet. Assuming that the broad Gaussian component represents the outflowing gas, we find an ionised gas mass outflow rate of $<$0.01 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$." (Warm molecular gas morphology and kinematics)
   - fitting: ppxf stellar continuum fit followed by up to 2 Gaussians + linear residual continuum selected via BIC  
  > "After subtracting the stellar continuum emission, we fit the emission lines using Gaussian functions along with a linear function that takes into account any residual underlying continuum emission in the spectra. Similar to the procedure adopted for the mid-infrared H$_{2}$ and sub-mm CO lines, the number of Gaussian functions used was decided based on the BIC values." (Resolved BPT maps with VLT/MUSE)
   - validation: BIC criterion for number of Gaussian components  
  > "Similar to the procedure adopted for the mid-infrared H$_{2}$ and sub-mm CO lines, the number of Gaussian functions used was decided based on the BIC values." (Resolved BPT maps with VLT/MUSE)
6. **Derive warm H2 excitation temperatures and warm molecular gas mass**: Construct H2 rotational excitation diagrams from extinction-corrected S(1)-S(8) fluxes in nuclear and integrated apertures and spaxel-by-spaxel; fit two-temperature linear models to excitation diagrams and a continuous power-law temperature distribution (Togi & Smith 2016) to derive warm H2 mass at T >= 200 K.  
  > "We created the rotational diagrams from the line fluxes in the nuclear and integrated spectra, following the methodology in (cite: rigopoulou02) (cite: zakamska10; togi16; petric18)." (H2 excitation in IR20551); "We fit the rotational H$_{2}$ line fluxes with a power-law, temperature dependent excitation function (cite: togi16)." (H2 excitation in IR20551)
   - parameter: upper temperature bound = 2000 K, lower integration temperature = 200 K, aperture R = 1.5 arcsec  
  > "The (cite: togi16) model assumes an upper bound on the temperature distribution at 2000 K, as the mass contribution from rotational transitions beyond this temperature is negligible." (H2 excitation in IR20551); "However, for the warm gas temperatures considered here (T$\geq$200 K), this assumption provides a useful conservative upper limit." (H2 excitation in IR20551)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) and ortho-para ratio of 3:1  
  > "In making these rotational diagrams, we assume the gas to be in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) and an ortho-para ratio of 3:1." (H2 excitation in IR20551)
   - fitting: Two-temperature linear fits in excitation diagram and power-law temperature distribution fit  
  > "The nuclear and integrated MRS spectra of IR20551 broadly fall into two temperature distributions, shown by the blue and orange lines in the top panels in Fig. 13" (H2 excitation in IR20551); "We fit the rotational H$_{2}$ line fluxes with a power-law, temperature dependent excitation function (cite: togi16)." (H2 excitation in IR20551)
   - validation: Power-law index compared against ULIRG literature range  
  > "We find a power-law index, $n = 4.76\pm0.16$, consistent with the range observed in ULIRGs (cite: togi16)" (H2 excitation in IR20551)
7. **Calculate cold molecular gas mass from ALMA CO(2-1) and compare cold-to-warm gas ratios**: Calculate CO(2-1) luminosity L'_CO(2-1) within R = 1.5 arcsec using Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005) equation, convert to cold H2 mass using excitation correction r_21 and alpha_CO, and compute integrated and spatially resolved (convolved/rebinned) CO(2-1)/H2 S(2) ratios.  
  > "To calculate the cold molecular gas mass from CO(2-1) line, we use the following equation from (cite: solomon05):" (Cold gas to warm gas ratios); "Assuming an excitation correction, $r_{21} = 0.4$ (cite: iono09; papadopoulos12; bolatto13), we get a cold molecular gas mass of $M_{\rm H_{2}}^{\rm cold} = 1.3 \times 10^{10}$ M$_{\odot}$, using an $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ conversion factor of 1.8 M$_{\odot}$ K$^{-1}$ km$^{-1}$ s pc$^{-2}$" (Cold gas to warm gas ratios); "The bottom panel in Fig. 14 shows the ratio between the flux of the CO(2-1) and S(2) line (normalised to an arbitrary scale)." (Cold gas to warm gas ratios)

- **CASA** 6.5.4-9-pipeline-2023.1.0.124: Recreating calibrated ALMA Measurement Sets, continuum subtraction, and CO(2-1) imaging.

  > "afterwards the 0th order polynomial fit to the continuum emission in the spectral window was then subtracted using the `uvcontsub` task." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
- **tclean** : Imaging the ALMA CO(2-1) line cube from combined TM1 and TM2 Measurement Sets.  
  > "We perform imaging of the CO(2-1) line in 4.5 km s$^{-1}$ channels combining data from both MSs with `tclean`" (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)

  > "We used the standard MRS data reduction procedure using the JWST pipeline (cite: labiano16) using the Calibration Reference Data System (CRDS) context `jwst_1256.pmap`." (JWST/MIRI-MRS)
- **CAFE** modified version for JWST: Modelling the mid-infrared continuum, PAH features, silicate absorption, and extinction correction factors in JWST/MIRI-MRS spectra.  
  > "To model the continuum emission in each spectra, we use a modified version of the `CAFE` software (cite: marshall07; lai22; armus23; cafe_software) that was originally adopted for Spitzer/IRS data and is currently being updated for JWST." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data); "The extinction correction factors were obtained from `CAFE`." (Warm molecular gas morphology and kinematics)
- **LZIFU** : Wrapper implementing ppxf stellar continuum subtraction for VLT/MUSE optical spectra.  
  > "To model these spectra, we first subtract the contribution from the stellar continuum in the optical spectra using penalised pixel fitting routine (cite: cappellari04; cappellari17), implemented within the `LZIFU` software (cite: ho16; kreckel18)." (Resolved BPT maps with VLT/MUSE)
- **ppxf** : Penalised pixel fitting to subtract stellar continuum from VLT/MUSE optical spectra.  
  > "During the `ppxf` fitting procedure, we masked the emission lines, regions contaminated by sky lines, and telluric absorption features." (Resolved BPT maps with VLT/MUSE)

## Qualitative findings

- Mid-infrared spectral and synthetic colour-colour diagnostics place IR20551 between pure AGN and starburst regimes and do not rule out an obscured AGN, while optical BPT diagrams show star-forming H II regions in the nucleus and SE clump surrounded by extended composite/LINER emission.  
  > "The nuclear and integrated synthetic photometric colours of IR20551 lie approximately between the regions characteristic of pure AGN and pure starburst systems, again suggesting that the contribution from an AGN cannot be excluded and that IR20551 is consistent with being a composite system." (Ionisation Source in the Central Region of IR20551); "The strong [O i] line emission shows that most of the observed center of the galaxy has a LINER spectrum–not that of a Seyfert AGN." (Ionisation Source in the Central Region of IR20551)
- Warm H2 (S(1)-S(8)) and cold CO(2-1) molecular gas are co-spatial, show two tidal tails and a smooth NE-to-SW velocity gradient consistent with a settling disk in a late-stage merger, and show no central molecular gas deficit.  
  > "The figure clearly demonstrates that the cold and warm molecular gas are co-spatial. Unlike some resolved multi-phase studies reported in the literature, we do not find regions in IR20551 devoid of cold molecular gas (cite: rosario19; davies24)." (Cold gas to warm gas ratios)
- Warm H2 excitation temperature maps peak at the putative AGN location, indicating central UV heating by the central source, with possible shock contribution in extended regions.  
  > "These maps clearly show a peak in the temperature distribution corresponding to the location of the putative AGN, with a slightly higher temperature in the NE tidal tail." (H2 excitation in IR20551)
- High-velocity outflows are present only in the warm ionised gas ([O III]), whereas neither the warm H2 nor the cold CO(2-1) molecular phase shows fast outflows.  
  > "[O iii]$\lambda$5007 in VLT/MUSE data reveal ionised gas outflows with $w_{80}^{\rm [OIII]} \sim 790$ km s$^{-1}$ and $\dot{M}_{\rm out}^{\rm[OIII]}<0.01$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$. No outflows are observed in either molecular phases." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST/MIRI-MRS resolves mid-IR rotational warm H2 transitions (100-1000 K), ALMA CO(2-1) resolves the cold (<100 K) molecular phase at sub-arcsec scales, and VLT/MUSE provides optical BPT ionisation diagnostics and ionised gas outflow kinematics.  
  > "Therefore, we complemented our JWST/MIRI-MRS observations with resolved sub-mm observations from ALMA and archival optical IFS observations from VLT/MUSE." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
- Why this method: Used synthetic MIRI photometry from MRS spectra because the MIRI imaging was saturated in the nucleus; used custom Gaussian fits for H2 lines because CAFE left significant line residuals; used continuous power-law temperature distribution (Togi & Smith 2016) to capture multi-temperature warm H2 mass.  
  > "Although the `CAFE` software models these individual H$_{2}$ and other ionised emission lines in the spectra, there are significant residuals at the location of these emission lines. Therefore, we only use the overall continuum models from `CAFE`, and use a custom script to model the S(1) – S(8) H$_{2}$ lines after subtracting the overall continuum." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data); "As the MIRI image in Fig. 1 is saturated, we cannot use it to calculate these colours directly. Instead, we use the available MRS spectra to construct synthetic F560W, F770W, and F1500W colours by convolving the spectra with the relevant MIRI filter throughputs." (Ionisation Source in the Central Region of IR20551)
- Alternative: Detailed multi-component AGN/starburst/dust continuum decomposition in CAFE was rejected due to component degeneracies.  
  > "As the main focus of our paper is to analyse the warm molecular gas transitions, we model the overall continuum emission, without a detailed decomposition into individual contributions from the AGN, starburst or different dust components. As there are many components, such continuum decompositions are also prone to degeneracies." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
- Alternative: Using CAFE's built-in emission-line models for H2 transitions was rejected due to significant residuals at the emission line locations.  
  > "Although the `CAFE` software models these individual H$_{2}$ and other ionised emission lines in the spectra, there are significant residuals at the location of these emission lines. Therefore, we only use the overall continuum models from `CAFE`, and use a custom script to model the S(1) – S(8) H$_{2}$ lines after subtracting the overall continuum." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
- Alternative: Multiple Gaussian components for the MRS H2 lines were rejected based on minimal change in BIC compared to single Gaussian fits.  
  > "We did not add additional Gaussians, based on minimal changes in the Bayesian Information Criterion (cite: gideon78) values between single and multiple Gaussian models." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data)
- How they decided it was enough: Used the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) to decide whether to add Gaussian components for H2, CO(2-1), and optical emission lines; cleaned ALMA cube to 3 sigma_RMS with multi-scale and 1 sigma_RMS with hogbom.  
  > "We did not add additional Gaussians, based on minimal changes in the Bayesian Information Criterion (cite: gideon78) values between single and multiple Gaussian models." (Modelling warm molecular gas transitions in MRS data); "We then repeat the above process but with the `hogbom` deconvolver (cite: hogbom) to clean more deeply until the residual level reaches $\sigma_{\rm RMS}$ (cite: leroy21)." (Archival ALMA and VLT/MUSE Observations)
- Limitation (authors): MIRI F770W imaging is saturated at the central source, preventing direct photometric colour measurements from the images.  
  > "As the MIRI image in Fig. 1 is saturated, we cannot use it to calculate these colours directly." (Ionisation Source in the Central Region of IR20551)
- Limitation (authors): Coarser spectral resolution and sampling of JWST/MIRI-MRS compared to ALMA could hide mild line asymmetries or weak wings in the warm H2 profiles.  
  > "Visually, the S(2) line in the MRS spectra has a width comparable to CO(2–1) but lacks its asymmetry, likely due to the superior velocity resolution and spectral sampling of the ALMA data." (Warm molecular gas morphology and kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Cold molecular gas mass and spatial CO/H2 ratio estimates are subject to systematic uncertainties from assuming a uniform alpha_CO conversion factor and CO excitation ratio r_21.  
  > "However, we note that the cold gas mass estimate is subject to systematic uncertainties from assumed $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ value, which relies on the physical conditions of the ISM such as shocks, photo-dissociated regions, metallicity etc (cite: narayanan12; bolatto13)." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): The SE Halpha star-forming clump where the tidal tails split lies outside the JWST/MIRI-MRS field-of-view, preventing warm H2 analysis there.  
  > "However, this is outside the field-of-view of MRS observations and we cannot conclude whether this star forming clump is indeed triggered due to warm or cold gas flows." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Hot molecular gas (T > 1000 K) traced by near-IR ro-vibrational transitions is excluded from the total molecular gas budget.  
  > "We note that additional phases, such as hot molecular gas traced by ro-vibrational transitions in the near-infrared ($T > 1000$ K), also contribute to the molecular reservoir. However, this component generally represents only a small fraction compared to the warm and cold phases (cite: kakkad25; zanchettin25)." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Slight internal text inconsistency in the reported integrated CO(2-1) w_80 value (200 km s^-1 in Section 3.2 vs ~210 km s^-1 in Section 5) and native vs smoothed ALMA resolution (0.12x0.10 arcsec^2 in Section 2.2 vs ~0.3 arcsec in Section 3.2).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cold molecular gas mass depends linearly on the assumed r_21 = 0.4 and alpha_CO = 1.8 M_sun (K km s^-1 pc^2)^-1; adopting a standard ULIRG alpha_CO ~ 0.8 or higher r_21 ~ 0.8 would reduce the inferred cold gas mass and cold-to-warm ratio by a factor of ~2-4.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Interactive manual mask editing during tclean introduces a minor subjective element to the ALMA deconvolution.
