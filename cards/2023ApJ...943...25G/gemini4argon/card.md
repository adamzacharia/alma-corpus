## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure ALMA sub-mm and JVLA radio positions and flux densities of lensed QSO images**: Image continuum visibilities and fit 2D Gaussian / PSF profiles to each lensed QSO component using CASA imfit; predict radio star-formation flux from ALMA sub-mm flux using the far-IR-radio correlation  
  > "Finally, a 2D Gaussian fit using the task imfit to the second epoch CLEANed image (because of the more robust detection) found that both lensed images are consistent with a point source." (JVLA Radio follow-up); "The task imfit within CASA was used to fit a PSF to each lensed image." (ALMA mm imaging); "The integrated emission that we measure from the two epochs of VLA data is $\sim60-100\mu$Jy, $6-10$ times larger compared to the prediction of $\sim10\mu$Jy based on the ALMA data, assuming a typical effective dust temperature of 40 K (cite: stacey18; stacey19) and using the known far-IR $-$ radio correlation of star-forming galaxies" (Clues from the radio data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Synthesised beam 0.64'' x 0.57'', rms 66 uJy (ALMA)  
  > "resulting in a synthesised beam of $0.64\ensuremath{''}\times0.57\ensuremath{''}$ and an rms noise of 66 $\mu$Jy." (ALMA mm imaging)
   - assumption: Effective dust temperature of 40 K and far-IR-radio correlation of star-forming galaxies  
  > "assuming a typical effective dust temperature of 40 K (cite: stacey18; stacey19) and using the known far-IR $-$ radio correlation of star-forming galaxies (cite: kruit71; yun01; ivison10a; ivison10b)." (Clues from the radio data)
   - fitting: PSF / 2D Gaussian fitting in image plane with CASA task imfit  
  > "The task imfit within CASA was used to fit a PSF to each lensed image." (ALMA mm imaging)
   - validation: Inspected post-fit residuals to confirm point-source consistency  
  > "No significant residuals remain after fitting, suggesting that the lensed images are not resolved." (ALMA mm imaging)
2. **Coupled morphological and gravitational lens mass modeling of HST WFC3/IR images**: Iteratively fit QSO point sources, Sérsic profiles for lens G, galaxy G1, and lensed QSO host Einstein ring with hostlens, reconstruct the PSF in 30 steps, and fit shared lens mass models (SIE+gamma, free SIE+gamma, SIE+gamma+GX) with glafic; also perform exploratory pixelated source reconstruction with glee  
  > "To tackle the issues above, we wrote a custom wrapper code around `hostlens`, which uses an iterative approach:" (Coupled light and lens modeling); "We now proceed in an iterative fashion, where we first refit the parameters of the shared lens mass model with `glafic` using the improved relative astrometry from step 3, and repeating steps 3 and 4. We do this in 30 steps, where the PSF correction box size is increased from 7 to 35 pixels on a side" (Coupled light and lens modeling); "To identify such counterimages, we conduct an exploratory analysis where we model the first visit in the F125W filter by reconstructing the source on a grid of pixels, with the code `glee` (cite: Suyu10; Suyu12)." (The QSO host galaxy, structure along the Einstein ring and pixelated modeling)
   - software: hostlens, glafic, glee, DrizzlePac
   - parameter: 16'' x 19'' cutout, pixel scale 0.06''/pixel, circular masks of ~0.3'' radius around QSO images, PSF correction box 7 to 35 pixels over 30 steps  
  > "We reduced the images using the `DrizzlePac` software package to a final pixel scale of 0.06$''$ pixel$^{-1}$." (Hubble Imaging); "We focused on a $16\ensuremath{''} \times 19\ensuremath{''}$ cutout around the system starting with a newly constructed PSF (cite: Glikman15) for each filter." (Modeling of the system); "We therefore hold fix the best-fit models of the QSO images and mask them using circular masks of $\sim0.3\ensuremath{''}$ in radius" (Coupled light and lens modeling)
   - parameter: z_l = 0.599 for G and GX, z_s = 2.517 for source  
  > "Both G and GX are assumed to be at $z_l=0.599$ (see §[2.4.1](#sec:lensz)), and the source is fixed at $z_s=2.517$." (Table 5)
   - assumption: Circular Sérsic profile with fixed n = 4 (de Vaucouleurs) for the QSO host galaxy  
  > "We fixed the Sérsic index to the fiducial value for an early-type galaxy, $n=4$ (cite: devaucouleurs48)" (Coupled light and lens modeling)
   - assumption: Singular isothermal ellipsoid plus external shear (SIE+gamma) for lens G, plus singular isothermal sphere (SIS) for perturber GX  
  > "A commonly used model to fit gravitationally lensed QSOs, when the main constraints are astrometric, is the singular isothermal ellipsoid with external shear (SIE$+\gamma$)." (Lensing analysis); "If we include it in the fit as a SIS at the observed location and at the redshift of the lens, with a velocity dispersion free to vary, we obtain a perfect fit for zero degrees of freedom" (Lensing analysis)
   - fitting: Nelder-Mead chi^2 minimization followed by 5 independent MCMC chains of 15,000-30,000 steps for light profiles and 5 MCMC runs with 10^5-10^6 steps for glafic mass models  
  > "During the fitting, our wrapper performs the parameter optimization by minimizing the sum of the quality-of-fit $\chi^2$ reported by `hostlens` for the 4 cutouts, using the Nelder-Mead algorithm (cite: Gao12)." (Coupled light and lens modeling); "Once we have inferred the best-fit profile parameters as described above, we determine the corresponding uncertainties by combining 5 independent MCMC chains of 15,000 - 30,000 steps." (Coupled light and lens modeling); "Based on fitting with `glafic`. Uncertainties are determined from 5 MCMC runs with $10^5$-$10^6$ steps each, for each mass model." (Table 5)
   - validation: Monitored MCMC convergence, checked chi^2 improvement and residuals, verified alignment of mass and light axes and Faber-Jackson scaling for GX  
  > "We ensure their convergence by monitoring the change in the parameter values over time and removing the “burn-in“ steps." (Coupled light and lens modeling); "While the velocity dispersion of GX was a free parameter during the fit, its best-fit value of $\sim 46.5$ km/s is in good agreement with the predicted value of $\sim50$ km/s based on its relative luminosity compared to G, and the Faber-Jackson law." (The Fiducial Model)
3. **Calculate total magnification and test microlensing, extinction, and substructure explanations for the flux ratio anomaly**: Anchor lens model magnification predictions by intersecting the C/D model distribution with a Gaussian fit to observed F160W fluxes; evaluate differential extinction; run GPU-D microlensing simulations; model substructure perturber near image A  
  > "Based on the arguments we presented in §[3](#sec:morphmodeling), we construct an “anchor” by using the fiducial lens model, in spite of the fact that the SIE$+\gamma$ model shows a better match, and intersecting the C/D model prediction distribution with a Gaussian fit to the observed fluxes in filter F160W only" (Flux ratio analysis and total magnification); "We conducted microlensing simulations in order to investigate the plausibility of microlensing as the reason for the large flux anomaly of image A." (Microlensing analysis, intrinsic variability and time delays); "In Figure [11](#fig:substruct) we demonstrate with a toy model that a relatively small-mass perturber, placed on the order of milli-arcseconds away from image A, can produce enough magnification to cause the highly anomalous flux observed in this image, while leaving all other observables unchanged" (Substructure in the lensing galaxy)
   - software: GPU-D, extinction
   - parameter: Cardelli et al. (1989) extinction curve with R_V = 3.1; accretion disk inclination i = 60 deg, PA = 90 deg, relative microlens speed ~340 km/s  
  > "The median differential extinction was found to be $E(B-V)=0.04$, the median total extinction $E(B-V)=0.08$, and the median $R_V$ in particular, for the early-type sample, was consistent with the Galactic value of 3.1." (Extinction); "For the size of the accretion disk, $R_0$, in the observed F160W filter we use Equation 2 from (cite: morgan10), and assume inclination $i=60$ deg and PA$=90$ deg. To compute the microlensing time scale represented in the horizontal axis of the left plot in Figure [9](#fig:microlensing), we follow the methodology in (cite: kochanek04), and compute a typical microlens relative speed of $\sim340$ km/s." (Details of the microlensing calculation)
   - assumption: Salpeter IMF with mean stellar mass 0.3 M_sun and stellar mass fraction 0.2 in De Vaucouleurs profile for kappa_*  
  > "We then reduced the mass in the best-fit De Vaucouleurs profile to a stellar fraction of 0.2 (cite: dai10), and computed the resulting convergence at the location of the QSO images." (Table 6); "For the population of lenses we assume a Salpeter (cite: salpeter55) IMF and a mean solar mass of $0.3 M_\ensuremath{\odot}$." (Details of the microlensing calculation)
   - fitting: 5 MCMC chains of 10,000 points each for the toy substructure model  
  > "We used 5 MCMC chains of 10000 points each. The fluxes of the images predicted by the SIE$+\gamma$+GX model were used as constraints (the flux of image A being boosted), with 5% uncertainty." (Footnote 21)
   - validation: Checked that C/D intersection samples correspond to typical chi^2 values of the full MCMC chain and verified accretion disk size is much smaller than source position uncertainty  
  > "We checked that, although this intersection samples from the tail of the C/D model prediction distribution, the resulting samples correspond overall to the distribution of $\chi^2$ values of the entire MCMC chain we used in Table [\[tab:lens\]](#tab:lens), and therefore not to particularly poor fits." (Flux ratio analysis and total magnification)
4. **Fit optical/NIR spectra to measure QSO reddening, UV leakage, outflow velocities, and virial black hole mass**: Fit reddened composite QSO template and two-component power-law continuum to LBT/IRTF/Keck spectra; fit Gaussian profiles to Mg II, C IV, Ly-alpha absorption features and broad H-alpha emission; compute virial black hole mass and Eddington ratio from de-reddened, de-magnified F160W and WISE W4 luminosities  
  > "We then fit the line-free portions of the spectrum with a two-component power-law, one reddened and one pure, both with the same power-law index" (Spectral characteristics); "Figure [13](#fig:mbh), left, shows that the H$\alpha$ line in the near-infrared spectrum is well fit by a single Gaussian, with a FWHM in velocity space of $v_{\rm FWHM} = 8479$ km s$^{-1}$, which we use to estimate the black hole mass of W2M J1042+1641." (Black hole mass); "For comparison, recompute these physical parameters, using the observed *WISE* $W4$ ($22 \mu$m) instead of the F160W flux" (Black hole mass)
   - software: IRAF, Spextool, modsCCDred
   - parameter: Power-law spectral index alpha_nu = -0.5; virial mass coefficients a = 0.774, b = 0.520, c = 2.06; magnification mu = 117; bolometric corrections BC_5100 = 9.5 and BC_W4 = 7.5  
  > "Following our interpretation that W2M J1042+1641 is a reddened QSO with some leakage of the intrinsic spectrum, we model the QSO spectrum as a power law, with spectral index $\alpha_\nu = -0.5$ ($F_{\lambda0} \propto \lambda^{-(\alpha_\nu+2)}$)." (Spectral characteristics); "adopting the values $a=0.774$, $b=0.520$, $c=2.06$ for single-epoch measurements of FWHM$_{H\alpha}$ and $L_{5100}$, based on the calibration of (cite: Assef11)." (Black hole mass); "To estimate $L/L_{Edd}$, we use a bolometric correction (BC) at 5100Å of 9.5 from (cite: Richards06)." (Black hole mass)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.30, Omega_Lambda = 0.70  
  > "When computing luminosities and any other cosmology-dependent quantities, we use the $\Lambda$CDM concordance cosmology: $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_M=0.30$, and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.70$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Template fitting, two-component power-law continuum fitting, and single/double Gaussian line profile fitting  
  > "While the template-based fit results in $E(B-V)=0.73$ mag, the two-component power-law fit yields $E(B-V) = 0.89$ mag." (Spectral characteristics); "We see two distinct absorption systems that are well fit by a double Gaussian" (Spectral characteristics)
   - validation: Compared bolometric luminosity estimates derived independently from de-reddened HST F160W and unreddened WISE W4  
  > "we compute $\log (L_{\rm bol} ~{\rm [erg/s]}) = 46.47$ – consistent with the estimate based on the de-reddened and F160W flux." (Black hole mass)

## Qualitative findings

- W2M J1042+1641 is a quadruply lensed radio-quiet dust-reddened QSO at z = 2.517 in a cusp configuration with an Einstein ring of radius ~0.9'' and a lens galaxy at tentative redshift z = 0.5985.  
  > "The image reveals four point sources surrounding an extended-appearing source at the center, in a geometry suggestive of quadruply lensed system with a cusp configuration." (Hubble Imaging); "We identify absorption consistent with Mg II $\lambda\lambda$2796,2803 at $z=0.5985$, Figure [4](#fig:mg2), which is in excellent agreement with the photometric redshift expected from the color of the lensing galaxy, and we thus adopt this as a tentative redshift for the lensing galaxy." (Lens Redshift)
- The reddening of the QSO is intrinsic to the QSO environment rather than caused by the lensing galaxy, evidenced by ~0.8% leaked intrinsic UV continuum emission.  
  > "We plot in Figure [1](#fig:spec1) the QSO template scaled to 0.8% of the intrinsic spectrum (with a dashed blue line) and find that it fits well the spectral shape." (Initial Spectroscopy); "These arguments lead us to conclude that the dust is local to the lensed QSO and that the QSO is not reddened by the lens." (Initial Spectroscopy)
- The QSO spectrum exhibits blueshifted broad absorption features in Mg II, C IV, and Ly-alpha indicative of outflowing gas, and pixelated modeling of the Einstein ring reveals an extended companion or tidal feature (source X) consistent with a merger-driven transitional phase.  
  > "These features are indicative of outflowing gas." (Spectral characteristics); "In the bottom row of Figure [18](#fig:extendsrcmodel), rightmost panel, the source of X itself may even be resolved and possibly connected to the host galaxy by a tidal tail." (The QSO host galaxy, structure along the Einstein ring and pixelated modeling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JVLA radio and ALMA sub-mm observations are unaffected by dust extinction and stellar microlensing, enabling a test of whether the extreme optical/near-IR flux ratio anomaly is caused by microlensing or by a low-mass substructure perturber.  
  > "Radio and far-IR photometry of lensed QSOs is considered to be unaffected by microlensing (cite: koopmans03), as well as dust extinction." (Clues from the radio data); "If the emission in radio and far-IR is due to the AGN, we would expect to measure a similarly anomalous flux ratio to what we measure with *HST* if it is the case that the anomaly is due to substructure (we use here the term to refer to a low-mass perturber), but we should measure something comparatively closer to the non-anomalous prediction of the best-fit lens model, if it is due to microlensing." (Clues from the radio data)
- Why this method: Used a custom iterative wrapper around hostlens and glafic with data-driven PSF reconstruction because direct hostlens modeling cannot decouple anomalous image fluxes from a smooth mass model, initial PSFs left strong residuals, and joint fitting across four cutouts was needed; used glee pixelated modeling to capture asymmetric source structure X and its counterimage.  
  > "There are three reasons why a naive, direct modeling of this system with `hostlens` would be sub-optimal: (1) employing `hostlens` with a single smooth lens mass model (and without multiple mass substructures whose parameters are highly degenerate) cannot account for the flux ratio anomalies found in this system" (Coupled light and lens modeling); "To identify such counterimages, we conduct an exploratory analysis where we model the first visit in the F125W filter by reconstructing the source on a grid of pixels, with the code `glee` (cite: Suyu10; Suyu12). The advantage of using a pixellated grid is that it does not need to assume a particular morphology of the source object." (The QSO host galaxy, structure along the Einstein ring and pixelated modeling)
- Alternative: Smooth SIE+gamma and free SIE+gamma lens mass models without perturber GX were tested and rejected as fiducial models due to poor astrometric fit (chi^2 = 62.5) or unphysical mass-light centroid offsets and position angle misalignments.  
  > "However, this model results in a statistically very poor fit with $\chi^2\sim62.5$ for a single degree of freedom." (Lensing analysis); "We therefore adopt the SIE$+\gamma$+GX model as our fiducial model" (The Fiducial Model)
- Alternative: Including nearby galaxy G1 as a second singular isothermal sphere (SIS) was tested and rejected because it provided negligible improvement to the lens model.  
  > "Including G1 in the fit as a second singular isothermal sphere (SIS) does not result in a significant improvement." (Lensing analysis)
- Alternative: Using the ALMA C/D flux ratio as the magnification anchor was rejected because the sub-mm dust emission is extended and associated with the host galaxy rather than the compact AGN.  
  > "We have a direct measurement of C/D $=1.05\pm0.34$ from ALMA imaging of host galaxy thermal dust emission in §[2.6](#sec:alma), which is lower by a factor of $\sim2$ than the *HST*-derived value, as well as the lens model predictions. However, this sub-mm emission is extended (see Figure [5](#fig:radio)), and expected to be primarily associated with dust in the QSO host galaxy (cite: stacey18; stacey21). These characteristics make the ALMA-based measurement unsuitable to use as an anchor." (Footnote 16)
- Alternative: Differential dust extinction and stellar microlensing were considered and rejected as the primary cause of the large image A flux ratio anomaly.  
  > "The differential extinction is too small to significantly impact our measured flux ratios" (Extinction); "We therefore conclude that, while microlensing can explain part of the observed anomaly, it is highly unlikely to explain all of it." (Microlensing analysis, intrinsic variability and time delays)
