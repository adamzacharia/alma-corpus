## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure raw source positions and apply astrometric corrections**: Fit a 2D Gaussian function to each component using CASA task IMFIT (fitting Aa and Ab separately in 11 high-resolution epochs, and a single Gaussian to A in others), then apply corrections for changes in cataloged gain calibrator positions and for trigonometric parallax to transform from geocentric to barycentric coordinates.  
  > "The position of each VLA 1623–2417 component was determined by fitting a two-dimensional Gaussian function using the CASA task IMFIT." (Determination of absolute and relative positions); "The first one is a correction to account for changes over the years in the cataloged positions of the gain calibrators. The second correction accounts for the trigonometric parallax of the source ($\varpi=7.28\pm0.06$ mas, as appropriate for Lynds 1688; (cite: 2017ApJ...834..141O))" (Determination of absolute and relative positions)
   - software: CASA, IMFIT
   - parameter: 11 resolved epochs for Aa and Ab; single Gaussian for A in remaining epochs  
  > "In the eleven observations indicated with a grey background in Table [\[tab:obs\]](#tab:obs), components Aa and Ab were resolved. In the others, we fitted a single Gaussian to the entire A component." (Determination of absolute and relative positions)
   - assumption: Trigonometric parallax of 7.28 +/- 0.06 mas (distance 137.3 +/- 1.2 pc)  
  > "The second correction accounts for the trigonometric parallax of the source ($\varpi=7.28\pm0.06$ mas, as appropriate for Lynds 1688; (cite: 2017ApJ...834..141O))" (Determination of absolute and relative positions)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian fit in image plane via CASA IMFIT  
  > "The position of each VLA 1623–2417 component was determined by fitting a two-dimensional Gaussian function using the CASA task IMFIT." (Determination of absolute and relative positions)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit absolute proper motions and peculiar velocities**: Fit linear and uniform motion to the gain-calibrator and parallax-corrected positions over time using scipy.optimize.curve_fit, adding source- and frequency-dependent systematic errors in quadrature to achieve reduced chi-squared ~ 1, and subtract the reflex Solar motion to obtain peculiar velocities.  
  > "The absolute proper motions of each component were calculated from the measured positions, after gain calibrator and parallax corrections, using the Python function `curve_fit` from the `scipy.optimize` module, assuming linear motion." (Absolute proper motions); "To account for these additional sources of error, we have quadratically added source- and frequency-dependent uncertainties to those delivered by IMFIT. The exact values were obtained by requiring the final astrometric fits to have reduced $\chi^2$ on the order of one." (Absolute proper motions); "Indeed, subtracting the reflex Solar motion from the measured proper motion, we find that the remaining (peculiar) proper motions of sources A, B, and W are only 7.3, 8.2, and 9.2 mas yr$^{-1}$, respectively." (Absolute proper motions)
   - software: Python, scipy.optimize, curve_fit
   - parameter: Systematic errors typically 10 to 50 mas added in quadrature; for A, exclude VLA C, X, Ku bands from fit; for W, exclude X-band observations with RA resolution worse than 3 times the 0.33 arcsec shock separation  
  > "These systematic errors are typically between 10 and 50 mas depending on the source and band; the proper motions errors quoted in Table [2](#table:abs) account for these additional errors." (Absolute proper motions); "For completeness, the positions derived from the VLA Ku, X and C band observations are also shown in the upper right panel of Figure [2](#fig:Abs-all2), although they were not included in the linear fit." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 A); "We then incorporated the C-, Ku-, and X-band datasets, excluding observations whose resolution in right ascension was worse than three times the separation between W and the possible stationary shock discussed above." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 W)
   - assumption: Linear and uniform proper motion; Solar motion relative to LSR of (U_sun = 11.1, V_sun = 12.24, W_sun = 7.25) km s^-1  
  > "The absolute proper motions of each component were calculated from the measured positions, after gain calibrator and parallax corrections, using the Python function `curve_fit` from the `scipy.optimize` module, assuming linear motion." (Absolute proper motions); "Here, we adopt the values of (cite: 2010MNRAS.403.1829S) for the latter: $(U_\odot = 11.1, V_\odot = 12.24, W_\odot = 7.25)$ km s$^{-1}$." (Absolute proper motions)
   - fitting: Least-squares linear fit via scipy.optimize.curve_fit calibrated to reduced chi^2 ~ 1  
  > "The absolute proper motions of each component were calculated from the measured positions, after gain calibrator and parallax corrections, using the Python function `curve_fit` from the `scipy.optimize` module, assuming linear motion." (Absolute proper motions); "The exact values were obtained by requiring the final astrometric fits to have reduced $\chi^2$ on the order of one." (Absolute proper motions)
   - validation: Comparison of initial (ALMA + VLA K/Q only) and extended (including C, Ku, and 6 X-band epochs plus BIMA/SMA) fits for W, and comparison with literature proper motions  
  > "This second fit extends the time baseline to $\sim$32.5 years and improves the accuracy of the results by 35–40%; the results are shown in Table [2](#table:abs) and a visual comparison between our initial and final fits is provided in Figure [7](#fig:comp_Sada)." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 W)
3. **Compute relative proper motions between system components**: Compute relative proper motions using two methods: (1) fitting linear motion to epoch-by-epoch angular offsets between source pairs (Delta theta), and (2) subtracting the fitted absolute proper motions (Delta mu).  
  > "The first one is to measure their absolute proper motions, as we did earlier for the sources in VLA 1623–2417, and simply subtract their values. The alternative is to calculate, for each epoch, the angular offsets between the sources of interest, and then fit the resulting value with the appropriate function (e.g. a linear and uniform motion)." (Relative proper motions); "For completeness, we have applied both methods to our observations; the results are listed in Table [\[table:rel\]](#table:rel)." (Relative proper motions)
   - parameter: Time baselines: 11.12 yr (Aa-Ab), 11.35 yr (B-A and A-W), 31.84 yr (B-W)  
  > "| **Aa – Ab** | 11.12 | $\Delta \theta$ | $-$1.93 $\pm$ 0.13 | 0.94 | $-$3.94 $\pm$ 0.18 | 0.99 | $+$ 2.94 $\pm$ 0.16 | 1.31 | $-$0.90 $\pm$ 0.03 | 0.44 | $+$1.92 $\pm$ 0.11 |" (Relative proper motions); "| **B – W** | 31.84 | $\Delta \theta$ | $+$2.51 $\pm$ 0.60 | 0.99 | $+$1.00 $\pm$ 0.79 | 0.94 | $-$2.40 $\pm$ 0.58 | 0.93 | $+$0.008 $\pm$ 0.005 | 0.97 | –0.73 $\pm$ 0.48 |" (Relative proper motions)
   - assumption: Linear and uniform relative motion over the observed time span  
  > "The alternative is to calculate, for each epoch, the angular offsets between the sources of interest, and then fit the resulting value with the appropriate function (e.g. a linear and uniform motion)." (Relative proper motions)
   - fitting: Linear fit to angular offsets (Delta alpha, Delta delta, rho, theta)  
  > "The alternative is to calculate, for each epoch, the angular offsets between the sources of interest, and then fit the resulting value with the appropriate function (e.g. a linear and uniform motion)." (Relative proper motions)
   - validation: Consistency check between the angular offset method (Delta theta) and absolute proper motion subtraction method (Delta mu), which agree within one sigma  
  > "The two methods yield results that are consistent within one sigma, but the latter (based on first calculating angular offsets) results in better accuracy." (Relative proper motions)
4. **Fit orbital parameters of the compact binary Aa/Ab**: Run MCMC orbit fitting on the relative astrometry of Aa and Ab using the orbitize! package under both agnostic orbital phase priors (near apoastron) and restricted orbital phase priors (near periastron).  
  > "To attempt to constrain orbital parameters, we used the MCMC orbit fitting algorithm implemented in the `orbitize!` package (cite: Blunt2017; 2020AJ....159...89B), which is optimized for long-period stellar systems constrained only over a short time span (cite: Blunt2017)." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system); "To explore this issue, we also searched for fits where the system would currently be close to periastron rather than apoastron. This was achieved placing priors on the orbit phase (between 0.9 and 0.1) in `orbitize!`" (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
   - software: orbitize!
   - parameter: 10,000 walkers, 500 burn-in steps  
  > "We employed 10,000 walkers and 500 burn-in steps, and initially remained very agnostic on priors: all angles were allowed to vary over their entire natural range; the prior on the mass was 0–2 M$_\odot$, while the prior on the semi major axis was 20–75 au." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
   - assumption: Keplerian two-body orbit  
  > "To attempt to constrain orbital parameters, we used the MCMC orbit fitting algorithm implemented in the `orbitize!` package (cite: Blunt2017; 2020AJ....159...89B), which is optimized for long-period stellar systems constrained only over a short time span (cite: Blunt2017)." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
   - fitting: MCMC sampler in orbitize! with 10,000 walkers and 500 burn-in steps; priors: angles over full natural range, total mass 0-2 M_sun, semi-major axis 20-75 au; second run adds orbital phase prior of 0.9-0.1  
  > "We employed 10,000 walkers and 500 burn-in steps, and initially remained very agnostic on priors: all angles were allowed to vary over their entire natural range; the prior on the mass was 0–2 M$_\odot$, while the prior on the semi major axis was 20–75 au." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system); "To explore this issue, we also searched for fits where the system would currently be close to periastron rather than apoastron. This was achieved placing priors on the orbit phase (between 0.9 and 0.1) in `orbitize!`" (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
   - validation: Comparison of fitted orbital orientation, inclination, and total mass with the geometry and dynamical mass (0.2-0.3 M_sun) of the circumbinary disk around Aa/Ab  
  > "Interestingly, the orientation ($\theta = 40^\circ\pm10^\circ$) and inclination ($i = 73^\circ \pm 2^\circ$ when measured in the first quadrant) of the best-fit orbit are roughly similar to that of the circumbinary disk measured from molecular line emission" (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system); "However, the mass derived from the best orbit, about $1.0\pm0.2$ M$_\odot$, is a factor of 3-5 larger than previously estimated for the A component." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)

## Qualitative findings

- The absolute proper motions of all components in VLA 1623-2417 are dominated by the reflex Solar motion.  
  > "As for other nearby young stars (e.g., (cite: 2024MNRAS.535.2948H)), the absolute proper motions of all sources in VLA 1623–2417 are dominated by the reflex Solar motion." (Conclusions and perspectives)
- Component W is moving toward components A and B at 1 to 2 km s^-1 on the plane of the sky, ruling out the scenario that W was dynamically ejected from the A/B system; W is either unbound to A/B or on a highly inclined (nearly edge-on) orbit.  
  > "The relative proper motions of W with respect to components A and B indicate that their projected separations are decreasing at a rate of 1 to 2 km s$^{-1}$. These inward motions are inconsistent with scenarios in which component W has been dynamically ejected from the VLA 1623–2417 system. Instead, we argue that W is either not bound to the A/B components or is moving on a highly inclined orbit." (Abstract); "Our astrometric analysis definitely rules out the ejection scenario since we find that W is moving **toward** the A/B system at 1 to 2 km s$^{-1}$ (see Table [\[table:rel\]](#table:rel))." (Dynamics of source W)
- Components A and B are approaching each other and their relative position angle change (~0.5 deg over 15 years, indicating an orbital period of ~1,000-1,200 yr) is consistent with a gravitationally bound system of total mass ~2 M_sun.  
  > "Their relative proper motions indicate that components A and B are approaching each other (Table [\[table:rel\]](#table:rel)). Their relative position angle has changed by about half a degree over the last 15 years. This indicates an orbital period of order 1,000 yr." (Dynamics of the A/B system); "The good agreement with the estimation above based on the relative proper motions confirms that the A/B system is gravitationally bound." (Dynamics of the A/B system)
- Low-frequency centimeter emission offset to the west of source W by ~0.33 arcsec suggests that W drives a one-sided jet with a stationary shock feature.  
  > "This suggests that source W currently drives a one-sided jet. In this scenario, the secondary peak seen in two images would correspond to a shock feature along that jet." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 W); "Within the errors, the right ascension separation between the eastern and western peaks remains constant around $\sim$0.024 s ($=0.33$ arcsec)." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 W)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Radio and millimeter interferometry (VLA and ALMA) penetrates the severe extinction of Class 0/I protostars, and combining 37 archival epochs with angular resolution better than ~1.1 arcsec (plus BIMA/SMA data) provides a >30-year time baseline for B and W and an ~11-year resolved baseline for Aa/Ab.  
  > "For the youngest and most deeply embedded objects, however, such measurements cannot be obtained at optical or near-infrared wavelengths owing to severe extinction. In these cases, radio interferometric observations offer a unique means of probing their kinematics." (Introduction); "There are, however, dozens of high angular resolution observations of VLA 1623–2417 in the VLA and ALMA archives, providing more than 30 years of astrometric coverage. In addition, resolved observations of the Aa/Ab binary are now available over a span of more than a decade." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Calculating epoch-by-epoch angular offsets cancels out common-mode systematic errors (such as residual phase calibration errors), while subtracting absolute proper motions maximizes the number of usable epochs when one component is undetected or contaminated; orbitize! is specifically optimized for long-period systems observed over a short fraction of an orbit.  
  > "On the other hand, systematic observational errors in interferometric observations (for instance residual phase errors after calibration) typically affect equally the sources in a given field, so their contributions will cancel out when angular offsets are calculated. Thus, the second approach mentioned above tends to be more immune to systematic observational errors." (Relative proper motions); "To attempt to constrain orbital parameters, we used the MCMC orbit fitting algorithm implemented in the `orbitize!` package (cite: Blunt2017; 2020AJ....159...89B), which is optimized for long-period stellar systems constrained only over a short time span (cite: Blunt2017)." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
- Alternative: Dynamical ejection of component W from the A/B system, rejected because W is moving toward A and B rather than away from them.  
  > "Our astrometric analysis definitely rules out the ejection scenario since we find that W is moving **toward** the A/B system at 1 to 2 km s$^{-1}$ (see Table [\[table:rel\]](#table:rel))." (Dynamics of source W)
- Alternative: A highly eccentric bound orbit for component W around A/B, rejected as unlikely because periastron passages would bring A, B, and W into an unstable close configuration every <4,000 yr, much shorter than the 10^4-10^5 yr lifetime of a Class 0/I source.  
  > "We argue that a highly eccentric orbit is unlikely because it would bring A, B, and W in close proximity at each periastron passage resulting in a highly unstable configuration." (Dynamics of source W); "This time is much shorter than the lifetime of a class 0/I source (10$^4$–10$^5$ yr), so the system would have passed many times by this highly unstable configuration and it is unlikely that it would have survived in that configuration to this day." (Dynamics of source W)
- Alternative: Including low-frequency VLA (C, X, Ku band) positions in the proper motion fit for component A and low-resolution X-band positions for component W, rejected due to astrometric contamination from free-free jet emission.  
  > "For completeness, the positions derived from the VLA Ku, X and C band observations are also shown in the upper right panel of Figure [2](#fig:Abs-all2), although they were not included in the linear fit. These lower frequency measurements exhibit a noticeably larger scatter relative to the fitted trend than the millimeter data." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 A); "We then incorporated the C-, Ku-, and X-band datasets, excluding observations whose resolution in right ascension was worse than three times the separation between W and the possible stationary shock discussed above." (Absolute proper motions of VLA 1623–2417 W)
- How they decided it was enough: Selected all archival VLA and ALMA observations with angular resolution sufficient to resolve A and B (<1.1 arcsec for ALMA), and adjusted systematic astrometric uncertainties so that linear proper motion fits achieved reduced chi-squared ~ 1.  
  > "For the ALMA data, we required the angular resolution to be better than $1\mbox{$''\mskip-7.6mu.\,$}1$ (again, to ensure that A and B are well resolved)." (Data selection and calibration); "The exact values were obtained by requiring the final astrometric fits to have reduced $\chi^2$ on the order of one." (Absolute proper motions)
- Limitation (authors): The ~11-year astrometric baseline for Aa and Ab covers too small a fraction of their ~300-500 year orbit to yield a unique orbital fit or reliable dynamical mass estimate from astrometry alone.  
  > "The relative proper motions of Aa/Ab indicate significant orbital motions, but cover too small a fraction of the orbit to provide a reliable mass estimate." (Abstract); "Further astrometric monitoring of the system over the next decades will provide stronger constraints on the orbit of the system but, given the long orbital period, the possibility of obtaining a unique orbital fit from the astrometry alone appears remote." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
- Limitation (authors): Relative astrometry alone leaves a 180-degree degeneracy in the orbital angles (Omega, omega) because the ascending and descending nodes cannot be distinguished without time-resolved radial velocity measurements.  
  > "This results in a bimodal distribution for the $(\Omega, \omega)$ pair, reflecting the well-known degeneracy affecting these angles when only relative astrometric data are available (cite: Heintz1978; Pourbaix2000)." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system); "This degeneracy can be lifted if time-resolved spectroscopic observations are also available, but this is not the case here." (Orbital motions in the compact Aa/Ab system)
- Limitation (authors): The orbital orientation and properties of the wider A/B system cannot be constrained due to the very limited orbital coverage (~15 years of a ~1,000-1,200 year period).  
  > "Little can be said about the orientation and properties of the orbit given the very limited coverage provided by the observations." (Dynamics of the A/B system)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ad-hoc source- and frequency-dependent systematic uncertainties (10-50 mas) were tuned to force reduced chi-squared ~ 1, which directly governs the formal uncertainties on the fitted proper motions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In epochs where Aa and Ab are unresolved, fitting a single Gaussian to A measures the flux-weighted centroid, which can shift with wavelength if Aa and Ab or their circumstellar/circumbinary structures have different spectral indices.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Primary beam phase-center offsets for component W (~10 arcsec from A/B) at the highest ALMA frequencies (Band 7, where the primary beam FWHM is ~18 arcsec) could introduce minor wide-field astrometric or bandwidth/time-averaging smearing effects if not carefully handled.
