## Analysis recipe

1. **Rotate Stokes maps and isolate rings**: Q′ = Q cos[2(PA−90°)] + U sin[2(PA−90°)]; U′ = U cos[2(PA−90°)] − Q sin[2(PA−90°)]; mask rings and focus on Q′.  
  > "Q'&= Q\cos[2(\mathrm{PA}-90^\circ)]+U\sin[2(\mathrm{PA}-90^\circ)]" (Observation); "U'&= U\cos[2(\mathrm{PA}-90^\circ)]-Q\sin[2(\mathrm{PA}-90^\circ)]" (Observation); "we mask the data so that only rings are shown, as they are the focus of this investigation." (Observation)
   - parameter: Disk PA = 138.02°; positive Q′ along minor axis; negative Q′ along major axis  
  > "where $\mathrm{PA}=138.02^\circ$ is the position angle of the disk." (Observation); "A positive (negative) $Q'$ means polarization along the minor (major) axis." (Observation)
   - assumption: Inner disk and rings are dominated by dust self-scattering; model Q′  
  > "In this work, we aim to study the parts of the high-resolution polarization image that are dominated by self-scattering of dust grains, both in the inner disk and in the rings." (Observation); "In this work, we will focus on modeling the $Q'$ maps." (Observation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect transformed maps: Q′ dominates in rings and inner disk  
  > "We can see that in the rings and the inner disk, the polarization is dominated by $Q'$" (Observation)
2. **Construct dust and disk models**: Fixed DSHARP dust model; inner disk plus eight radial rings; tune ring scale heights and join gap profiles with a hyperbolic tangent.  
  > "We fix the dust model, as the aim of this paper is to study the effect of dust vertical distributions on the detailed structures of scattering-induced polarization." (Model); "In addition, we also add 8 rings, prescribed as Gaussian profiles in radial direction" (Model); "In the gap, we allow the scale height to change from one profile to another smoothly using a hyperbolic tangent function profile." (Model)
   - software: bhmie
   - parameter: Minimum grain size 0.1 μm; maximum grain size 140 μm  
  > "We assume a minimum grain size of $0.1\rm\, \mu m$ and a maximum grain size of $140\rm\, \mu m$." (Model)
   - parameter: τ_ID(R) = τ_c,0 (R/R_c)^−0.5 exp[−(R/R_c)^−1.5]; τ_c,0 = 2.3  
  > "\tau_{ID}(R)=\tau_{c,0}\left(\frac{R}{R_c}\right)^{-0.5}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{R}{R_c}\right)^{-1.5}\right]," (Model); "where $\tau_{c,0}=2.3$ is a characteristic optical depth." (Model)
   - parameter: Printed ring profile: τ_i(R) = τ_c,i exp[−0.5((R−C_i)/W_i)^−2]; parameters in Table 1  
  > "\tau_i(R) = \tau_{c,i} \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R-C_i}{W_i}\right)^{-2}\right]," (Model); "Their values are tabulated in Table" (Model)
   - parameter: H_i = H_c,i(R/C_i)^p; p = 1 in most cases  
  > "We allow each ring to have different scale height profiles, parameterized as $H_{i}=H_{c,i}(R/C_i)^{p}$." (Model); "In most cases, we let $p=1$, describing a disk with a constant aspect ratio, $H/R$." (Model)
   - parameter: H_gap(R) = H_1(R) + 0.5[H_2(R)−H_1(R)] × [tanh((R−C)/(W/2))+1]  
  > "H_{gap}(R) =& H_1(R) + 0.5[H_2(R)-H_1(R)]\times" (Model); "&\left[\tanh\left(\frac{R-C}{W/2}\right)+1\right]." (Model)
   - assumption: DSHARP composition  
  > "For the dust model, we adopt the DSHARP composition (cite: Birnstiel2018)" (Model)
   - assumption: T = T_10(R/10 au)^−0.65; T_10 = 137.5 K  
  > "The temperature profile is assumed as a power law profile, $T=T_{10}(R/10\rm\, au)^{-0.65}$, where $T_{10}=137.5\rm\, K$" (Model)
   - assumption: Distance 147.3 pc; inclination 46.7°  
  > "This corresponds to a resolution of $5.2$ au at a distance of $147.3$ pc (cite: GaiaDR3)." (Observation); "$i=46.7^\circ$ is the inclination angle (cite: ALMA2015)." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
   - fitting: Modify the disk model to reproduce Stokes I visually  
  > "We introduce a few modifications to this model in order to better reproduce the Stokes I image with our dust model visually." (Model)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Generate observable polarization images**: Calculate Mie dust opacities, run Monte Carlo radiation transfer and smooth model images with Gaussian beams.  
  > "The dust opacities are calculated using the Mie theory code, `bhmie` (cite: BH83)." (Model); "We set up our models using the Monte Carlo Radiation Transfer code, RADMC-3D" (Model); "The results are smoothed by Gaussian beams before being compared with observations." (Model)
   - software: bhmie, RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Spherical polar grid: 300 × 256 × 256 cells  
  > "The model is set in a spherical polar grid with $300\times 256\times 256$ cells in total." (Model)
   - parameter: 1.6 × 10^9 photons per simulation  
  > "For each radiation transfer simulation, we use $1.6\times 10^9$ photons to obtain a polarization image with a high signal-to-noise ratio." (Model)
   - parameter: Convolve with observational synthesized Gaussian beam  
  > "The models are convolved with the observational synthesized Gaussian beam before comparing with the data." (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare beam-smoothed model images with observations  
  > "The results are smoothed by Gaussian beams before being compared with observations." (Model)
4. **Constrain inner-disk thickness**: Compare near-far polarization asymmetry across H/R models; add a negative Gaussian to mimic contamination from the first gap.  
  > "The left columns in Figure" (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry); "shows the polarization profiles for models with $H/R=0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2$." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry); "To mimic the transition of polarization patterns in the observed $Q'$ radial profile, we introduce a Gaussian profile" (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: H/R = 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2  
  > "shows the polarization profiles for models with $H/R=0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2$." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
   - parameter: Gap Gaussian: peak −0.16 mJy/beam; center 14 au; width 5.1/(2 cos i) au  
  > "with a peak flux density of $-0.16\rm\, mJy/beam$, centered at $14$ au, with a width of $5.1/(2\cos i)$ au" (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
   - assumption: Southwest near side; northeast far side  
  > "the polarized intensity has a significant asymmetry between the near side (southwest) and the far side (northeast)" (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
   - assumption: Non-negligible first-gap contamination near the inner-disk edge  
  > "the contamination from the first gap is non-negligible near the edge of the inner disk." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Maximum model asymmetry comparable to, but slightly smaller than, observed asymmetry  
  > "The maximum difference between the near side and the far side is comparable to the asymmetry in the observed data, although it is still slightly smaller than the observed one." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
5. **Constrain first-ring thickness**: Compare Q′ averaged in four quarters of the first ring with models of varying H/R.  
  > "shows the averaged $Q'$ in the four quarters of the ring for the data and for models with $H/R=0.01,0.05,0.1$." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: H/R = 0.01, 0.05, 0.1; additional models 0.15 and 0.2  
  > "shows the averaged $Q'$ in the four quarters of the ring for the data and for models with $H/R=0.01,0.05,0.1$." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast); "We also experiment with models with $H/R=0.15,$ and 0.2." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast)
   - assumption: Interplay of inclination-induced polarization and radiation-anisotropy-induced polarization  
  > "the observed pattern is a result of the interplay between the inclination-induced polarization and the radiation-anisotropy-induced polarization" (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: H/R = 0.1 best reproduces the azimuthal Q′ profile; 0.15 and 0.2 give similar results  
  > "We can see that the model with $H/R=0.1$ best reproduces the azimuthal profile of $Q'$ in the first ring." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast); "We also experiment with models with $H/R=0.15,$ and 0.2. The results are similar to $H/R=0.1$." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast)
6. **Limit outer-disk thickness**: Average Q′ over a 30-degree wedge and each ring's width; compute ΔQ′ = near-side Q′ minus far-side Q′ and compare with models.  
  > "To compare the model with data more quantitatively, we average the $Q'$ over a 30-degree wedge and over the width of each ring." (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry); "We then subtract the far side $Q'$ from the near side value to obtain $\Delta Q'$ for each ring" (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: Outer disk R > 30 au; wedge width 30 degrees  
  > "the polarization flux is relatively symmetric with respect to the major axis on large scales, $R>30\rm\, au$." (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry); "we average the $Q'$ over a 30-degree wedge and over the width of each ring." (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: H/R = 0.1 predicts significant near-far contrast; H/R < 0.05 does not  
  > "We can see that in the models with $H/R=0.1$, the near side is significantly brighter than the far side in $Q'$." (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry); "For models with $H/R<0.05$, the contrast is not significant." (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry)
7. **Translate thickness into turbulent stirring**: Calculate gas scale height and Stokes number; use the Youdin2007 dust-height relation; compare constant and radially varying α with the thickness constraints.  
  > "The gas scale height profile, $H_g(R)$, can then be calculated using the central stellar mass $1.3\rm\, M_\odot$ (cite: ALMA2015), the temperature profile in our model, and a mean molecular weight of $2.33$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring); "The dust scale height can be calculated as (cite: Youdin2007):" (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring); "we plot two dashed curves representing models with constant turbulence $\alpha$ at levels of $10^{-5}$, and $10^{-3}$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - parameter: St = ρ_s a/Σ_g; ρ_s = 3 g/cm^3; a = 140 μm  
  > "The degree of coupling of the dust grains with the gas is determined by the Stokes number $\mathrm{St}=\rho_s a /\Sigma_g$ and the turbulence $\alpha$, where we adopt $\rho_s=3\rm\, g/cm^3$ and $a=140\rm\, \mu m$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - parameter: H_d = H_g(1+St/α)^−1/2[(1+2St)/(1+St)]^−1/2  
  > "H_d = H_g \left(1+\frac{\mathrm{St}}{\alpha}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\frac{1+2\mathrm{St}}{1+\mathrm{St}}\right)^{-1/2}," (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - parameter: Constant α = 10^−5 and 10^−3; varying α = 10^−5 × R_100^−3.5, R_100 = R/100 au  
  > "we plot two dashed curves representing models with constant turbulence $\alpha$ at levels of $10^{-5}$, and $10^{-3}$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring); "with $\alpha=10^{-5}\times R_{100}^{-3.5}$, where $R_{100}\equiv (R/100\,\mathrm{au})$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - assumption: Σ(R) = Σ_0(R/R_c)^−γ exp[−(R/R_c)^(2−γ)]; R_c = 79 au; γ = 0.2; disk mass 0.13 M_⊙  
  > "\Sigma(R) = \Sigma_0 \left(\frac{R}{R_c}\right)^{-\gamma} e^{-(R/R_c)^{(2-\gamma)}}," (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring); "where $R_c=79$ au and $\gamma=0.2$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring); "The total disk mass is $0.13\rm\, M_\odot$ (cite: Kwon2011)." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - assumption: Stellar mass 1.3 M_⊙; mean molecular weight 2.33; model temperature profile  
  > "The gas scale height profile, $H_g(R)$, can then be calculated using the central stellar mass $1.3\rm\, M_\odot$ (cite: ALMA2015), the temperature profile in our model, and a mean molecular weight of $2.33$." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - fitting: Draw a varying-α curve to mimic different turbulence levels  
  > "To mimic the effect of different levels of turbulence, we draw one curve with varying $\alpha$" (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
   - validation: Varying-α model reproduces the constraints reasonably well  
  > "This model reproduces the constraints reasonably well, with $\alpha=10^{-5}$ at $100$ au and $\alpha=3\times 10^{-3}$ at the first ring." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
8. **Assess final images and residuals**: Combine the three radial thickness constraints into a scale height profile; compare convolved model Stokes images with observations and inspect Q′ and U′ residuals.  
  > "we put together scale height constraints from three different parts of the disk and define a scale height profile that roughly agrees with these constraints" (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment); "The models are convolved with the observational synthesized Gaussian beam before comparing with the data." (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment); "We can also see that there are some systematic residuals in both $Q'$ and $U'$ in the outer disk." (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rough agreement over the whole domain; excess model polarization in the inner disk; systematic outer residuals  
  > "We can see that it roughly agrees with the observations in the whole domain." (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment); "In the inner disk, the polarization is higher than in the observation" (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment); "Specifically, the $Q'$ in the data is larger along the major axis than the minor axis, and the $U'$ has some quadruple modes." (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment)

## Qualitative findings

- Inner disk: southwest near side brighter in polarized intensity than northeast far side  
  > "the polarized intensity has a significant asymmetry between the near side (southwest) and the far side (northeast)" (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry); "The near side is much brighter than the far side" (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
- First ring: polarization enhanced along the minor axis relative to the major axis  
  > "Specifically, continuum emission is more polarized in regions along the minor axis than in those along the major axis" (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast)
- Outer disk: no significant near-far polarization asymmetry  
  > "the polarization flux is relatively symmetric with respect to the major axis on large scales, $R>30\rm\, au$." (Outer disk: lack of near-far asymmetry)
- Constant turbulence cannot reproduce the inferred radial scale height profile  
  > "Models with constant turbulence $\alpha$ cannot reproduce the scale height profile" (Summary)
- Small systematic residuals may arise from toroidally oriented prolate grains  
  > "There are some small yet systematic residuals that may arise from toroidally oriented prolate grains (cite: Yang2019; Stephens2023)." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution polarization probes dust properties and radial scale height at previously unresolved small scales  
  > "the high-resolution polarization observations also provide a unique opportunity to probe the dust grain properties and their radial scale height profile, especially at small scales that are not resolved in earlier polarization observations." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Compare polarization at the same radius to constrain vertical structure while fixing the dust model  
  > "We fix the dust model, as the aim of this paper is to study the effect of dust vertical distributions on the detailed structures of scattering-induced polarization." (Model); "This paper focuses on comparing polarization at the same radius (for both the near-far side asymmetry and the azimuthal contrast), so the exact choice of radial profiles does not affect our conclusions." (Model)
- Alternative: Thin first-ring model H/R = 0.01 gives slightly stronger major-axis polarization  
  > "This is also what we see in the model with $H/R=0.01$" (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast); "In this model, we also observe a slightly larger polarization flux along the major axis than along the minor axis." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast)
- Alternative: Constant turbulence model rejected for the radial thickness profile  
  > "A model with constant turbulence cannot reproduce the high-resolution polarization observations in the HL Tau system." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
- Alternative: Smaller grains or large porous grains proposed for excess inner polarization; deferred  
  > "This may be solved with different grain models, using either a smaller grain size or large and porous grain models. We will leave these explorations to future studies." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry)
- Alternative: Disk winds may produce the large scale height instead of midplane turbulence  
  > "It is also possible that the large scale height is not due to a large turbulence in the disk midplane, but rather a result of disk winds, magnetically driven winds or photoevaporation winds (cite: Pascucci2023)." (Scale height profile and turbulent stirring)
- Alternative: Residual alignment patterns may involve toroidally aligned prolate or radially aligned oblate grains  
  > "They can be either toroidally aligned prolate grains or radially aligned oblate grains." (The polarization image in the final model and residual signals from grain alignment)
- How they decided it was enough: Inner-disk asymmetry saturates at H/R ≥ 0.15; first-ring models at 0.15 and 0.2 resemble 0.1; use 1.6 × 10^9 photons for high signal-to-noise  
  > "For $H/R\ge 0.15$, the near-far side asymmetry is roughly saturated and does not increase any more." (Inner disk: Near-far asymmetry); "We also experiment with models with $H/R=0.15,$ and 0.2. The results are similar to $H/R=0.1$." (Ring 1: azimuthal contrast); "For each radiation transfer simulation, we use $1.6\times 10^9$ photons to obtain a polarization image with a high signal-to-noise ratio." (Model)
- Limitation (authors): Inner-disk model polarized intensity exceeds observations and U′ is not reproduced
