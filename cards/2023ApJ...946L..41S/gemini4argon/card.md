## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and spectral cubes and extract 1D spectra**: Image continuum from line-free channels and subtract continuum in uv-plane via uvcontsub; image spectral cubes with tclean using Briggs robust=0.5 and auto-multithresh masking; apply primary beam correction (impbcor); convolve cubes to 0.43'' circular beam and extract spectra from 0.43'' diameter circular apertures at continuum/line peaks.  
  > "For the spectral analysis in this work, all the images are convolved to have a common circular beam size of 0.43$''$, which corresponds to 0.13 pc at the distance of the SMC." (Data reduction); "The CASA task tclean is used for imaging and the masking is done using the auto-multithresh algorithm." (Data reduction); "The spectra and continuum flux are extracted from the 0.43$''$ diameter circular region centered at RA = 0$^\mathrm{h}$54$^\mathrm{m}$3.443s and Dec = -73$\ensuremath{^\circ}$19$\ensuremath{'}$38.51$''$ (ICRS) for S07 and RA = 0$^\mathrm{h}$44$^\mathrm{m}$56.421s and Dec = -73$\ensuremath{^\circ}$10$\ensuremath{'}$11.20$''$ (ICRS) for S09, respectively." (Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, impbcor, uvcontsub
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; common circular beam = 0.43''; extraction aperture diameter = 0.43''  
  > "The original synthesized beam size is 0.37$''$–0.43$''$ $\times$ 0.30$''$–0.33$''$ with the Briggs weighting and a robustness parameter of 0.5." (Data reduction); "The spectra and continuum flux are extracted from the 0.43$''$ diameter circular region centered at RA = 0$^\mathrm{h}$54$^\mathrm{m}$3.443s and Dec = -73$\ensuremath{^\circ}$19$\ensuremath{'}$38.51$''$ (ICRS) for S07 and RA = 0$^\mathrm{h}$44$^\mathrm{m}$56.421s and Dec = -73$\ensuremath{^\circ}$10$\ensuremath{'}$11.20$''$ (ICRS) for S09, respectively." (Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify spectral lines and measure line parameters**: Identify lines using CDMS and JPL databases based on 3sigma significance and velocity coincidence with systemic velocity; bin 2-3 channels for weak lines; fit Gaussian profiles to measure peak brightness temperature, FWHM, LSR velocity, and integrated intensity (direct integration for non-Gaussian CO 3-2).  
  > "The detection criterion adopted here is 3$\sigma$ significance level and the velocity coincidence with the systemic velocities of S07 (163.1 km s$^{-1}$) and S09 (132.0 km s$^{-1}$), which are measured using the SO($N_J$ = 8$_{9}$–7$_{8}$) line." (Spectra); "Line parameters are measured by fitting a Gaussian." (Spectra); "For CO(3–2), since it shows a clear non-Gaussian component, the integrated intensity is calculated by directly integrating the spectrum over the frequency region of emission." (Measured line parameters)
   - parameter: 3sigma detection threshold; systemic velocities 163.1 km s^-1 (S07) and 132.0 km s^-1 (S09); Delta V = 4 km s^-1 for non-detection upper limits  
  > "The detection criterion adopted here is 3$\sigma$ significance level and the velocity coincidence with the systemic velocities of S07 (163.1 km s$^{-1}$) and S09 (132.0 km s$^{-1}$), which are measured using the SO($N_J$ = 8$_{9}$–7$_{8}$) line." (Spectra); "Upper limits on integrated intensities are estimated assuming $\Delta$$V$ = 4 km s$^{-1}$." (Measured line parameters)
   - assumption: Delta V = 4 km s^-1 assumed for upper limits on non-detected lines  
  > "Upper limits on integrated intensities are estimated assuming $\Delta$$V$ = 4 km s$^{-1}$." (Measured line parameters)
   - fitting: 1D Gaussian profile fitting to spectral lines  
  > "Line parameters are measured by fitting a Gaussian." (Spectra)
   - validation: 3sigma significance and velocity coincidence with systemic velocity  
  > "The detection criterion adopted here is 3$\sigma$ significance level and the velocity coincidence with the systemic velocities of S07 (163.1 km s$^{-1}$) and S09 (132.0 km s$^{-1}$), which are measured using the SO($N_J$ = 8$_{9}$–7$_{8}$) line." (Spectra)
3. **Measure spatial distributions and deconvolved source sizes**: Construct integrated intensity images over velocity ranges of detected emission (stacking lines with similar E_u for SO2 and CH3OH); fit 2D Gaussian to emission peak and compute deconvolved source size by subtracting beam size in quadrature.  
  > "The images are constructed by integrating spectral data in the velocity range where the emission is detected." (Images); "For SO$_2$ and CH$_3$OH, several emission lines with similar upper state energies are stacked to reduce the noise level." (Images); "We also estimated the deconvolved source sizes as $\sqrt{\mathrm{FWHM_{maj}} \times \mathrm{FWHM_{min}} - \Theta_{\mathrm{beam}}^2 }$, where $\mathrm{FWHM_{maj/min}}$ is the major/minor FWHM size as estimated from the two-dimensional Gaussian fit, and $\Theta_{\mathrm{beam}}^2$ is the geometric mean of the beam major and minor axes." (Images)
   - parameter: Minimum deconvolved size set to half the beam size for unresolved/marginally resolved emission  
  > "$^{d}$ We set a minimum deconvolved size to be half the beam size to avoid unrealistically small source sizes." (Table 1)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to emission peak

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of hot molecular cores in the SMC (S07 and S09), demonstrating that hot-core formation is a common phenomenon during high-mass star formation across 0.2-1 Z_sun.  
  > "We report the first detection of hot molecular cores in the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC), a nearby dwarf galaxy with 0.2 solar metallicity." (Abstract); "This work, in conjunction with previous hot-core studies in the LMC and outer/inner Galaxy, suggests that the formation of a hot core would be a common phenomenon during high-mass star formation across the metallicity range of $0.2$–$1{\rm\:}Z_\odot$." (Abstract)
- High-excitation SO2 lines trace compact (~0.1 pc), hot (>100 K) gas in the SMC hot cores and serve as a reliable low-metallicity hot-core tracer, whereas CH3OH is extended (~0.2-0.3 pc) and cool (~39 K).  
  > "Compact hot-core regions are traced by SO$_2$, whose spatial extent is about $0.1{\rm\:pc}$, and the gas temperature is higher than 100 K based on the rotation diagram analysis. In contrast, CH$_3$OH, a classical hot-core tracer, is dominated by extended ($\sim$0.2–0.3${\rm\:pc}$) components in both sources, and the gas temperature is estimated to be 39$^{+8}_{-6}$ K for one source." (Abstract); "High-excitation SO$_2$ lines will be a useful hot-core tracer in the low-metallicity environments of the SMC and LMC." (Abstract)
- Protostellar outflows are detected as high-velocity CO(3-2) components in both S07 and S09, with S09 being the second CO-traced protostellar outflow found in the SMC.  
  > "Protostellar outflows are also detected from both sources as high-velocity components of CO." (Abstract); "The outflows detected in S09 is the second example of CO-traced protostellar outflows found in the SMC." (Molecular outflows traced by CO)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The SMC has the lowest metallicity (0.2 Z_sun) among Local-Group star-forming galaxies where ALMA can detect and spatially resolve dense gas tracers at 0.1 pc scales; S07 and S09 are embedded high-mass YSOs with ice absorption bands.  
  > "The SMC harbors the lowest metallicity environment (cite: Hun05; Cho18) among the Local-Group star-forming galaxies where we can detect and spatially resolve dense gas tracers." (Introduction); "The absorption bands of silicate dust, H$_2$O ice, and CO$_2$ ice are detected in both sources, suggesting that they are still embedded in a dense core (cite: Oli13)." (Targets)
- Why this method: Rotation diagram analysis allows simultaneous estimation of gas temperature and column density where multiple lines spanning a range of upper-state energies are detected (SO2 in both sources, CH3OH in S07); for S09 SO2, a two-component fit is used because two temperature regimes are clearly seen.  
  > "We use the rotation diagram method to estimate column densities and temperatures of SO$_2$ in both sources and CH$_3$OH in S07 (Figure 2), while the number of detected CH$_3$OH lines in S09 is not sufficient for this purpose." (Column densities and gas temperatures); "For SO$_2$ in S09, a straight-line fit is separated into low- and high-temperature regimes ($E_{u}$ $<$60 K and $E_{u}$ $>$60 K), because different temperature components are clearly seen in the diagram." (Column densities and gas temperatures)
- Alternative: Formation of SO2 via strong-shock sputtering was considered and rejected in favor of thermal sublimation of sulfur-bearing ices, because SO2 column density correlates tightly with SO (r=0.98) but not with the shock tracer SiO (r=0.32).  
  > "The SO$_2$ column densities show a tight correlation with SO (correlation coefficient: 0.98), while they do not correlate with the strong-shock tracer SiO (correlation coefficient: 0.32). This would suggest that the formation of SO$_2$ in the current sample is mainly induced by the sublimation of sulfur-bearing ices, rather than sputtering by strong shocks." (SO$_2$ as a tracer of low-metallicity hot cores)
- Alternative: UV photodesorption in an outflow cavity wall as the origin of gaseous CH3OH was considered alongside shock sputtering, with the caveat that laboratory experiments show solid CH3OH dissociates upon UV irradiation.  
  > "Considering the presence of protostellar outflows (Appendix 9), sputtering of ice mantles by shock (cite: Aot15) or photodesorption by UV photons in an outflow cavity wall of the central protostar may be responsible for the desorption mechanism. Note that the latter mechanism is still under debate, since laboratory experiments have reported that solid CH$_3$OH readily dissociates upon photodesorption by the UV irradiation (cite: Ber16; Cru16)." (SO$_2$ as a tracer of low-metallicity hot cores)
- Alternative: Assuming CH3OH in S09 has the same rotation temperature as the high-temperature SO2 component was tested, yielding a twice higher CH3OH column density.  
  > "If the rotation temperature of CH$_3$OH in S09 is assumed to be the same as that of the high-temperature component of SO$_2$, then we obtain a twice higher CH$_3$OH column density." (Column densities and gas temperatures)
