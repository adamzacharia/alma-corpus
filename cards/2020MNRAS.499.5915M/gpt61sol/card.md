## Analysis recipe

1. **Constrain the millimetre flux**: Determine an upper limit from the ALMA non-detection using pipeline reduced imaging data from JVO.  
  > "We also determine an upper limit to the millimetre flux of the disc based on a non-detection of the disc (and star) in an archival ALMA Band 6 imaging observation" (Ancilliary data); "using the pipeline reduced imaging data obtained from the Japanese Virtual Observatory" (Ancilliary data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Determine interstellar wavelength dependence**: Joint full-bandpass fits of Serkowski-Wilking and Serkowski laws to HD 162521 and HD 167425.  
  > "Multi-band data was taken for HD 167425 and HD 162521." (Wavelength dependence); "Using a full bandpass model we use PYTHON’S CURVE_FIT program to fit both the Serkowski-Wilking Law ($K$ not fit) and the Serkowski Law ($K$ fit)." (Wavelength dependence)
   - software: Python, CURVE_FIT
   - parameter: Adopt λmax = 472.6 nm and K = 0.79.  
  > "values of $\lambda_{\rm max}$ equal to 472.6 nm and $K$ equal to 0.79 are adopted." (Wavelength dependence)
   - assumption: HD 162521 and HD 167425 are proxies for the target's interstellar polarisation.  
  > "As stars with similar $p/d$, $\theta$, $d$ and small separations from HD 172555, they were chosen as proxies to investigate its interstellar polarisation wavelength dependence." (Wavelength dependence)
   - fitting: Fit pmax for both stars and shared λmax; additionally fit K for the Serkowski Law.  
  > "The method fits $p_{\rm max}$ for both control stars, along with $\lambda_{\rm max}$, and in the latter case $K$ as well." (Wavelength dependence)
   - validation: Compare reduced χ² and parameter agreement; prefer the fit aligned with earlier nearby-ISM studies.  
  > "The Serkowski and Serkwoski-Wilking Law fits are similarly good, as judged by the reduced $\chi^2$ statistic, and the values of each fit parameter agree within the stated uncertainties." (Wavelength dependence); "The past studies of the interstellar medium (cite: 2016Marshall; 2019bCotton; 2020bBailey) have found values of $\lambda_{\rm max}$ more closely aligned with the Serkowski-Wilking Law fit" (Wavelength dependence)
3. **Subtract interstellar polarisation**: Subtract model interstellar q and u, rotate the remaining polarisation, and constrain u′ to zero and 650LP q′ to the SPHERE measurement.  
  > "Rotates the remaining (intrinsic) polarisation by $\theta_{\star}$ into a new frame ($q^{\prime}$, $u^{\prime}$)." (Interstellar subtraction); "Returns $u^{\prime}$ for each observation, and for the 650LP observations also $62-q^{\prime}$, for comparison with model values of 0" (Interstellar subtraction)
   - software: Python, CURVE_FIT
   - parameter: Fixed λmax = 472.6 nm and K = 0.79.  
  > "the Serkowski-Wilking Law described interstellar polarisation with $\lambda_{\rm max}$ of 472.6 nm, $K$ of 0.79" (Interstellar subtraction)
   - parameter: SPHERE anchor: 62 ± 8 ppm; include its uncertainty in q′ errors.  
  > "the measured integrated disc$+$star polarisation reported by (cite: 2018Engler) of 62 $\pm$ 8 ppm." (Interstellar subtraction); "the $q^{\prime}$ errors are modified to incorporate the uncertainty in the (cite: 2018Engler) measurement" (Interstellar subtraction)
   - assumption: Serkowski-Wilking interstellar polarisation; interstellar and intrinsic orientations each constant with wavelength.  
  > "the interstellar polarisation is described by the Serkowski-Wilking Law, has a consistent orientation with wavelength, and that the intrinsic polarisation of the star-disc system has a consistent orientation with wavelength too." (Interstellar subtraction)
   - assumption: 650LP and SPHERE VBB are equivalent because of similar effective wavelengths.  
  > "We consider the 650LP observations to be equivalent to the SPHERE VBB band observations given their similar $\lambda_{\rm eff}$, 725 nm and 735 nm respectively" (Interstellar subtraction)
   - fitting: CURVE_FIT minimisation over θi, pmax and θstar.  
  > "To carry out the subtraction $\theta_i$, $p_{\rm max}$ and $\theta_{\star}$ – where the subscripts $i$ and $\star$ represent respectively interstellar and intrinsic – need to be determined." (Interstellar subtraction); "This is done using a PYTHON CURVE_FIT-based program which tries to minimise the difference between a set of model values and the data as modified by a function." (Interstellar subtraction)
   - validation: Check control-star p/d, agreement with the imaging orientation and near-zero u′ in each band; reject a free-λmax fit.  
  > "a $p/d$ similar to that of HD 167425, a value of $\theta_{\star}$ consistent with that of (cite: 2018Engler), and values of $u^{\prime}$ consistent with zero to close to 1-$\sigma$ or better in each band" (Interstellar subtraction); "Consequently, we favour the fit with fixed $\lambda_{\rm max}$." (Interstellar subtraction)
4. **Construct reference dust emission models**: Generate individual material SEDs with Hyperion, fixed disc geometry and grain distribution; include a scaled NEXTGEN photosphere.  
  > "We use the 3D Monte Carlo radiative transfer code Hyperion (cite: 2011Robitaille) to model the dust continuum emission." (Spectral energy distribution); "Using these extrapolated optical constants we run individual disc models through Hyperion, assuming the previously determined disc spatial extent and dust size distribution for all materials" (Spectral energy distribution); "The stellar photospheric contribution to the total emission was represented by a stellar atmosphere model from the NEXTGEN grid" (Spectral energy distribution)
   - software: Hyperion
   - parameter: R0 = 11.3 au; αin = -3.5; αout = 6.8; z0 = 0.6 au; β = 0.40.  
  > "$R_{\rm 0} = 11.3~$au, $\alpha_{\rm in} = -3.5$, $\alpha_{\rm out} = 6.8$, $z_{0} = 0.6$ au, and $\beta = 0.40$." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: amin = 0.01 μm; amax = 1 mm; q = 3.95.  
  > "We fix the minimum and maximum grain sizes for the dust as $a_{\rm min} = 0.01~\mu$m and $a_{\rm max} = 1~$mm." (Spectral energy distribution); "The exponent of the size distribution $q$ was also fixed to be 3.95." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: Extrapolate optical constants to 0.1 μm–2 mm using the first or last two points.  
  > "extrapolation down to 0.1 $\mu$m and up to 2 mm, using the linear slope of the first (or last) two data points for the measured optical constants to extend the range." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: Reference mass for each material SED: 3.45×10^23 g.  
  > "each individual SED represented emission from the same mass of dust, in this case 3.45$\times10^{23}$ g." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: NEXTGEN: 7800 K; log(g) = 4.2 interpolated from 4.0 and 4.5; Solar metallicity; luminosity 8.093 L☉.  
  > "an effective temperature of 7800 K, surface gravity $\log (g)$ of 4.2 (interpolated from $\log (g)$ 4.0 and 4.5), and Solar metallicity" (Spectral energy distribution); "The photosphere model was scaled to provide a total luminosity of 8.093 $L_{\odot}$." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: The same dust grains produce continuum emission, scattering and polarisation.  
  > "We have therefore tacitly assumed that the same dust grains that produce the dust continuum emission are responsible for the scattering and polarisation." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: Hard compact spherical grains.  
  > "an appropriate material density assuming the grains are hard compact spheres" (Spectral energy distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit the dust composition**: Fit a weighted combination of 11 reference material SEDs to photometry and mid-infrared spectral samples.  
  > "Summary of the material weights determined by fitting of a composite SED of 11 materials to the SED and mid-infrared spectrum." (Table 6)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 14 IRS samples evenly distributed between 6 and 34 μm.  
  > "taking 14 values evenly distributed between 6 and 34 $\mu$m." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: Weights 10^-3–10^3; initial log weights 0 with walker scatter 0.05.  
  > "assigning each individual SED a weighting factor between 10$^{-3}$ and 10$^{3}$ (i.e. between -3 and 3 in log space)" (Spectral energy distribution); "The weighting factors for each species were initialised at 1 (0 in log space), with the individual walkers given a small scatter of 0.05 about this value." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - parameter: 200 walkers; 1 000 steps; discard first 400 steps; report 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles.  
  > "We used 200 walkers and 1 000 steps (200 000 realisations) to deduce the maximum probability weightings for the materials" (Spectral energy distribution); "discarding the first 400 steps of each chain before calculating the probability distribution to extract the 16$^{\rm th}$, 50$^{\rm th}$, and 84$^{\rm th}$ percentile values." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - assumption: Fixed spatial extent and dust size distribution; composition is fitted.  
  > "given that the disc spatial extent and dust size distribution are fixed, the only constraint left is to determine the dust composition that best replicates the SED." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - fitting: Affine-invariate MCMC with emcee; least squares for the weighted sum of component SEDs.  
  > "we use the affine-invariate Markov Chain Monte Carlo code *emcee* (cite: 2013ForemanMackey)" (Spectral energy distribution); "calculating the least squares fit of the weighted combination of individual SEDs to the observations." (Spectral energy distribution)
   - validation: Model agrees with the mid-infrared spectrum and far-infrared photometry and remains below the ALMA upper limit.  
  > "The model is consistent, within uncertainties, with the mid-infrared spectrum across its full extent, reproduces the far-infrared photometry, and falls below the millimetre-wavelength upper limit." (Spectral energy distribution)
6. **Fit the polarising grain size**: Calculate Mie albedo and Müller matrices, generate 3D disc polarisation spectra and bandpass synthetic measurements, then fit minimum grain size.  
  > "The albedo and scattering Müller matrix for each composition was calculated using a parallelized Fortran95 implementation of BHMie" (Polarimetry); "We calculate the disc polarisation using a 3D model of the disc density distribution combined with the dust optical constants, following the model presented in (cite: 2015Scicluna)." (Polarimetry); "A bandpass model was used to calculate synthetic HIPPI/-2 polarimetric measurements across the model spectra at the relevant wavelengths for the fitting process." (Polarimetry)
   - software: BHMie
   - parameter: 200 wavelengths over 0.3–0.9 μm; minimum sizes 0.01–6.5 μm.  
  > "We proceed by generating model polarisation spectra for each dust composition spanning 0.3 to 0.9 $\mu$m, sampling 200 wavelengths within this range, for minimum dust grains sizes between 0.01 and 6.5 $\mu$m." (Polarimetry)
   - parameter: Maximum size 1 mm; size-distribution exponent -3.95.  
  > "We fixed the maximum grain size as 1 mm, with a power law size distribution exponent of -3.95, as per the radiative transfer modelling." (Polarimetry)
   - parameter: Compare the SED-derived mixture with astronomical silicate.  
  > "We calculate a single model polarisation spectrum for each grain size using the weightings calculated in the dust grain radiative transfer modelling." (Polarimetry); "We also calculate polarisation spectra for astronomical silicate dust grains (cite: 2003Draine) as a point of comparison to the dust composition determined from this work." (Polarimetry)
   - assumption: Single scattering of unpolarised starlight in an optically thin disc.  
  > "We assume the measured polarisation is produced in single scattering events for dust grains in the optically thin disc redirecting unpolarised starlight toward the observer." (Polarimetry)
   - assumption: Orient the model so U is zero; retain grains between 0.1 and 1.0 μm.  
  > "The orientation of the disc in the model is chosen such that the degree of polarisation in the U vector is zero by default." (Polarimetry); "We did not omit grains with sizes 0.1 to 1.0 $\mu$m from the scattering polarisation calculation despite these grains being swiftly removed by radiation forces." (Polarimetry)
   - fitting: Least-squares fit of each grain-size model spectrum to multi-wavelength polarimetry.  
  > "The best-fit grain size was then determined using a least-squares fit of the model polarisation spectrum for each grain size to the multi-wavelength polarimetry." (Polarimetry)
   - validation: Assess agreement within uncertainties; the shortest-wavelength rise is not reproduced.  
  > "The model polarisation spectra do not replicate the steep rise in polarimetric signal toward shorter wavelengths" (Polarimetry); "are consistent within the substantial uncertainties attached to the magnitude and shape of the model polarisation spectra at those shortest wavelengths." (Polarimetry)

## Qualitative findings

- Aperture polarimetry detects debris-disc polarisation consistent with imaging polarimetry.  
  > "We have convincingly detected polarisation from a debris disc using aperture polarimetry for the first time, demonstrating the consistency of our measurements with those of imaging polarisation at comparable wavelengths." (Conclusions)
- The composite emission model fits the infrared observations and stays below the millimetre upper limit.  
  > "The model is consistent, within uncertainties, with the mid-infrared spectrum across its full extent, reproduces the far-infrared photometry, and falls below the millimetre-wavelength upper limit." (Spectral energy distribution)
- The smallest grains producing mid-infrared features are not responsible for the observed polarisation.  
  > "The smallest grains in the disc, i.e. those responsible for the mid-infrared spectral features and which dominate the disc surface area, are not the ones responsible for the observed polarisation from the disc." (Conclusions)
- Dust-composition fitting is degenerate and depends on spectral sampling.  
  > "The composition analysis is somewhat degenerate, and the relative weightings of different species tied to the sampling of different regions of the mid-infrared spectrum" (Spectral energy distribution)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine polarimetry with infrared and millimetre photometry to constrain dust grains through scattered light and continuum emission.  
  > "We seek to refine constraints on the disc’s constituent dust grains by combining our polarimetric measurements with available infrared and millimetre photometry to model the scattered light and continuum emission from the disc." (Abstract)
- Why this method: Use continuum fitting followed by scattering modelling to extract additional grain information from polarimetry.  
  > "Through this two step approach we demonstrate that the polarimetric measurements can provide additional, unique information to assist in the interpretation of debris dust." (Modelling)
- Alternative: Compare free-K Serkowski and constrained-K Serkowski-Wilking fits; adopt the latter's λmax and K.  
  > "Using a full bandpass model we use PYTHON’S CURVE_FIT program to fit both the Serkowski-Wilking Law ($K$ not fit) and the Serkowski Law ($K$ fit)." (Wavelength dependence); "values of $\lambda_{\rm max}$ equal to 472.6 nm and $K$ equal to 0.79 are adopted." (Wavelength dependence)
- Alternative: Reject free λmax in target interstellar subtraction because it drives less realistic pmax and an inconsistent intrinsic orientation.  
  > "A fit allowing $\lambda_{\rm max}$ to be free was also tried" (Interstellar subtraction); "the value of $p_{\rm max}$ is pushed up to less realistic levels and $\theta_{\star}$ is reduced such that it is no longer consistent with the value obtained by (cite: 2018Engler)." (Interstellar subtraction)
- Alternative: Retain a single power law instead of removing radiation-pressure-sensitive 0.1–1.0 μm grains.  
  > "a more physically motivated size distribution would omit grains in the range $0.1 < a < 1.0$ $\mu$m from the radiative transfer modelling" (Spectral energy distribution); "we adopt a single power law size distribution as this is still consistent with the observations as presented in (cite: 2012Johnson) and simplifies the modelling process." (Spectral energy distribution)
- Alternative: Astronomical silicate used as a comparison to the SED-derived mixture.  
  > "We also calculate polarisation spectra for astronomical silicate dust grains (cite: 2003Draine) as a point of comparison to the dust composition determined from this work." (Polarimetry)
- How they decided it was enough: Accept continuum agreement within uncertainties, consistency with the millimetre limit and an adequate polarisation match.  
  > "The model is consistent, within uncertainties, with the mid-infrared spectrum across its full extent, reproduces the far-infrared photometry, and falls below the millimetre-wavelength upper limit." (Spectral energy distribution); "The best-fit polarisation model using this dust composition has a minimum grain size $a_{\rm min} = 3.89~\mu$m and is an adequate match to the observations" (Polarimetry)
- Limitation (authors): Patchy ISM prevents control-star polarisation versus distance alone from determining the target's interstellar component.  
  > "the ISM in this region is patchy which prevents firm conclusions being drawn on the interstellar polarisation of HD 172555 from this data alone." (Polarisation vs. distance)
- Limitation (authors): A composite 425SP observation may have underestimated error.  
  > "one of the 425SP observations is a composite of two measurements, and the associated error may be under-estimated." (Interstellar subtraction)
- Limitation (authors): Unavailable optical constants prevent a direct composition comparison with the earlier spectral fit.  
  > "Not all the materials utilized in the spectrum fitting presented in table 2 of (cite: 2009Lisse) had ($n$,$k$) values available through accessible online databases." (Spectral energy distribution); "The dust composition fit undertaken here should not therefore be directly compared to that work for consistency." (Spectral energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Component weightings are degenerate and depend on spectral sampling.  
  > "The composition analysis is somewhat degenerate, and the relative weightings of different species tied to the sampling of different regions of the mid-infrared spectrum" (Spectral energy distribution)
- Limitation (authors): The model does not reproduce the steep short-wavelength polarisation rise.  
  > "The model polarisation spectra do not replicate the steep rise in polarimetric signal toward shorter wavelengths" (Polarimetry)
- Limitation (authors): Optical-constant extrapolation and assumed grain shapes prevent tight constraints on exact polarising grain sizes.

(Dust properties:)
