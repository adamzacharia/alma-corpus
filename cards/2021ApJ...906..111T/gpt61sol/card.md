## Qualitative findings

- Emission follows Keplerian rotation.  
  > "The channel maps clearly show that the emission follows the velocity pattern of a disk in Keplerian rotation." (Observational Results)
- A partially filled inner ring is followed by a smooth radial decline; no secondary outer-disk enhancement.  
  > "We detect emission across the entire disk out to 180 au, with a partially filled emission ring at 20 au and a smooth decrease beyond this radius." (Summary); "no secondary increase in the emission or column density is seen in the outer disk." (Summary)
- The radial OPR decrease survives temperature and missing-flux tests.  
  > "Repeating the analysis at fixed rotational temperatures ranging from 30 to 40 K, and omitting the 3$_{12}$–2$_{11}$ transition that may miss extended emission, we recover the same downward OPR trend." (Radially resolved rotational diagram)
- Predominantly gas-phase formation is proposed, rather than established conclusively.  
  > "does not reflect direct ice-formation, as is commonly assumed, but instead hints at predominantly gas-phase formation." (Summary); "This scenario will be tested in a follow-up study with forward models that include chemistry, spin-states, and radiative transfer" (Summary)
- Gas-phase production of observed emission does not exclude solid-state formation in the midplane.  
  > "although gas-phase chemistry is sufficient to explain observed *gas-phase* column densities it does not imply that *solid-state* formation does not occur in the disk midplane." (Gas-phase vs grain-surface formation of)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiple ortho and para transitions spanning 21–141 K constrain excitation, column density, and spatial structure.  
  > "In the current paper, we use a comprehensive multiline data set, including a wider range of upper state energies, 21–141 K, and now in both ortho- and para-spin-isomers, taken with ALMA toward the TW Hya disk." (Introduction); "without having to rely on parametric models like those used by (cite: 2017_Oberg_H2CO_ApJ...839...43O)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Multiscale imaging reduces sidelobes and improves signal-to-noise; weighting balances angular resolution, flux recovery, and sensitivity; separate partition functions enable independent spin-isomer columns.  
  > "to reduce side lobes and increase the signal-to-noise ratio." (Observations and Reduction); "a good balance between the angular resolution and recovery of flux on all scales." (Observations and Reduction); "These separate partition functions allow independent determination of the column densities of each spin isomer." (Rotational diagram analysis)
- Alternative: Imaging masks rejected because they did not significantly change images and can suppress multiscale components.  
  > "No masking is applied as no significant difference was observed between the images with and without masking." (Observations and Reduction); "Furthermore, masking creates a bias as scales used by the multiscale deconvoler larger then the masks are ignored by CLEAN." (Observations and Reduction)
- Alternative: Temperature bias or missing extended 3_12–2_11 flux does not explain the tested radial OPR decline.  
  > "Repeating the analysis at fixed rotational temperatures ranging from 30 to 40 K, and omitting the 3$_{12}$–2$_{11}$ transition that may miss extended emission, we recover the same downward OPR trend." (Radially resolved rotational diagram)
- Alternative: An inherited ice reservoir with wholly preserved OPR would predict a constant value, contrary to the observed gradient.  
  > "If, however, the observed has an inherited origin and we assume the OPR is preserved we would expect a constant value." (Constraints from the OPR on the formation); "This inherently implies that the OPR of the inherited ice is not wholly preserved." (Constraints from the OPR on the formation)
- Alternative: Multiple components hidden by beam smearing remain possible but are considered unlikely.  
  > "It is possible that due to beam smearing multiple components are hidden in what now seems to be a single component." (Constraints from the OPR on the formation); "However, the OPR drops across three beam sizes making this scenario unlikely." (Constraints from the OPR on the formation)
- Alternative: Low-temperature gas-phase formation and spin-state chemistry are alternative explanations for low OPR.  
  > "To explain the low OPR requires either low temperature formation or a *chemical* explanation." (Constraints from the OPR on the formation); "The latter requires detailed modeling including the spin state chemistry, and the role of H$_2$ spin." (Constraints from the OPR on the formation)
- How they decided it was enough: Stop CLEAN at three times line-free noise; accept the OPR trend after fixed-temperature and omitted-line tests; do not claim meaningful temperature constraints beyond 120 au.
