- **tclean** : Continuum and spectral-line imaging

- **uvsub** : Subtracting continuum from calibrated spectral-line visibilities  
  > "The continuum is subtracted from the calibrated spectral-line visibilities using the routine uvsub." (Observations and Data reduction)
- **imcontsub** : Subtracting residual continuum emission from spectral cubes using a first-order polynomial fit to line-free channels  
  > "Any residual continuum emission is subtracted from the spectral cubes by fitting a first-order polynomial to line-free channels, using the routine imcontsub." (Observations and Data reduction)
- **SOFIA** : Automatic source finding on spectral windows covering the targeted CO lines  
  > "We perform an automatic source finding on the spectral windows covering the targeted CO line using the SOFIA source finding application (cite: Serra2015) developed for H i interferometric observations." (Analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- Absorption-selected galaxies exhibit a wide variety of ISM CO excitation conditions, ranging from Milky Way inner disk-like excitation to thermalised mid-J (J=4) excitation.  
  > "Our results show that the excitation conditions in the ISM of absorption-selected galaxies can range from low excitation similar to the inner disk of the Milky Way up to thermal excitation of the mid-J levels." (Conclusions)
- No additional galaxies associated with the absorbers were found in the ALMA spectral cubes, including no CO(4-3) detection of the low-impact-parameter (b approx 16 kpc) galaxy in the J0918+1636 field.  
  > "Apart from these previously reported galaxies, we do not find any additional galaxies." (Analysis); "However, we do not detect CO(4–3) emission from this galaxy." (Analysis)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets were previously detected in a single lower-J CO transition and selected to have high metallicities (>10 per cent solar), where higher stellar masses, larger molecular gas masses, and lower alpha_CO values are expected; observing higher-J CO transitions with ALMA allows characterising their CO SLEDs.  
  > "The goal of this study is to characterise the CO SLEDs of absorption-selected galaxies that have been detected in CO emission in previous studies, using higher-order CO rotational transitions that are observable with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)." (Introduction); "This is because high-metallicity absorbers are expected to be associated with more massive galaxies (cite: Moller2013; Christensen2014), which are consequently expected to have both larger molecular gas masses and lower \alpha_{\rm CO} values." (Observations and Data reduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting was chosen for spectral cubes to maximize sensitivity for unresolved line detection; MBB rescaling was used in addition to MAGPHYS to avoid overfitting the unprobed far-IR peak; Fundamental Mass Metallicity relation was used instead of pencil-beam DLA absorption metallicities to account for metallicity gradients.  
  > "Natural weighting is used, to obtain the maximum sensitivity, appropriate for a line detection experiment for unresolved sources." (Observations and Data reduction); "To avoid possible overfitting of the far-IR regime, we derived an alternative estimate of M_{\rm dust} by simply rescaling the observed photometry in the mm regime — probing the Rayleigh-Jeans tail of the dust emission — with a modified black body curve (MBB; (cite: Berta2016))." (SED Fitting); "Due to metallicity gradients (cite: Christensen2014), the metallicity of the host galaxy in which the molecular gas is located may be different from that measured in absorption." (Dust-Based CO Luminosities and Molecular Gas Masses)
- Alternative: Using MAGPHYS energy-balance dust masses alone (supplemented with MBB rescaling because lack of far-IR peak photometry leaves dust temperature and total IR luminosity poorly constrained)  
  > "However, we currently do not have any measurements probing the peak of the far IR emission and, thus, the dust temperature and total IR luminosity are not well-constrained." (SED Fitting); "To avoid possible overfitting of the far-IR regime, we derived an alternative estimate of M_{\rm dust} by simply rescaling the observed photometry in the mm regime — probing the Rayleigh-Jeans tail of the dust emission — with a modified black body curve (MBB; (cite: Berta2016))." (SED Fitting)
- Alternative: Using pencil-beam DLA absorption metallicities or the absorption-based mass-metallicity relation to estimate host galaxy gas-to-dust ratio (rejected in favour of the Fundamental Mass Metallicity relation due to metallicity gradients and because J0918+1636 has a closer candidate DLA galaxy)  
  > "Due to metallicity gradients (cite: Christensen2014), the metallicity of the host galaxy in which the molecular gas is located may be different from that measured in absorption." (Dust-Based CO Luminosities and Molecular Gas Masses); "The latter is not applicable for DLA J0918+1636, where the more likely DLA galaxy is at a closer projected separation to the quasar (cite: Fynbo2013)." (Dust-Based CO Luminosities and Molecular Gas Masses)
- Alternative: Sub-thermal low-to-mid-J CO excitation ratio r_31 = 0.55 for B1228-113 and J0918+1636 (rejected in favour of r_31 = 1 based on measured CO(4-3)/CO(3-2) thermal excitation and dust-based L'_CO(1-0) agreement)  
  > "This suggests that, both mid-J levels would also show thermal excitation relative to the J=1 level (i.e. that r_{41}\approx 1 and r_{31} \approx 1, rather than r_{31} \approx 0.55, as assumed by (cite: Kanekar2020))." (Dust-Based CO Luminosities and Molecular Gas Masses)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): CO(1–0) observations are not available for any of the targets, and only a few CO transitions are covered per galaxy, making it impossible to determine the full slope and peak of the CO SLED in all targets and potentially causing ambiguities when anchoring ratios to higher-J transitions.  
  > "The CO(1–0) transition that is traditionally used for this comparison is not available for any of our targets." (CO Excitation and Line Energy Distribution); "Therefore we caution that differences in the CO SLEDs as well as line luminosity ratios r_{ul} anchored on higher J rotational transitions (above the CO(1–0) transition) can appear less pronounced." (CO Excitation and Line Energy Distribution); "It is important to emphasize that our study is limited by the coverage of only a few observed CO transitions for each galaxy, and especially does not include the J(1–0) transition." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): No photometry probes the peak of the far-IR dust emission, leaving dust temperature and total IR luminosity poorly constrained.  
  > "However, we currently do not have any measurements probing the peak of the far IR emission and, thus, the dust temperature and total IR luminosity are not well-constrained." (SED Fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties in dust-based molecular gas masses from assuming the Fundamental Mass Metallicity relation, assuming M_HI approx M_mol, assuming no redshift evolution in delta_GDR, and uncertainties in the dust mass absorption coefficient.  
  > "The main uncertainties of this method include the assumption that the galaxies follow the Fundamental Mass Metallicity relation mentioned, the fraction of molecular gas in the total gas mass, and the dust mass absorption coefficient." (Dust-Based CO Luminosities and Molecular Gas Masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The appendix figures and tables (Fig. A1, Fig. A2, Table A1, Table A2) referenced in Section 3.2 for the continuum flux densities and MAGPHYS inputs/outputs are omitted from the main text.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In J0918+1636, the CO(5-4) line width (FWHM = 119 +/- 40 km s^-1) is much narrower than the CO(4-3) (320 +/- 40 km s^-1) and CO(3-2) (350 +/- 25 km s^-1) widths, despite the text stating that FWHMs agree within uncertainties, which could affect the integrated flux ratio if part of the line is missed at low SNR.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DLA J0918+1636 is at an impact parameter of 117 kpc while another galaxy exists at b approx 16 kpc, so DLA J0918+1636 may be a companion galaxy in the same group rather than the primary absorber host.
