## Qualitative findings

- Evidence for disk-orbit alignment within measurement uncertainties  
  > "We find evidence for alignment among the binary and protoplanetary orbital planes within the uncertainty of our measurements." (Disk-Orbit Alignment)
- Compact circumstellar disks; dust unresolved and CO only marginally resolved  
  > "The disks are compact and likely truncated by the binary orbit." (Summary & Conclusions); "Circumstellar dust disks are not resolved in the image plane ($\sim$5 AU resolution) and the CO gas disks are only marginally resolved." (Summary & Conclusions)
- No detected circumbinary dust or CO emission beyond the binary orbit  
  > "We do not find evidence for a circumbinary disk or extended emission detected beyond the binary orbit in dust or CO above an RMS of 0.02 and 0.9 mJy/beam, respectively." (Summary & Conclusions)
- Keplerian disk models leave an extended CO arc east of the primary  
  > "In the residual image of the CO visibilities fit (Figure [9](#fig:chan_map), bottom panel set), we find an arc of extended emission to the east of the primary disk" (Extended CO Emission)
- Stellar rotation and stellar-spin alignment cannot be established from TESS  
  > "Given the large amplitude variability and lack of spatial information, it seems most likely that the true stellar rotation periods are unrecoverable from the *TESS* light curves." (Photometric Variability); "As such we are not able to comment on the alignment of the stellar angular momenta with that of the binary orbit or the protoplanetary disks." (Photometric Variability)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Compact and extended configurations probe both circumstellar and circumbinary scales; CO spectral resolution resolves Keplerian rotation while preserving continuum sensitivity  
  > "To achieve high angular resolution while maintaining sensitivity to extended emission, observations were made in compact and extended array configurations." (Observations); "This setting was chosen to yield a velocity resolution required to resolve a Keplerian rotation profile while maintaining a broad bandwidth for continuum sensitivity." (Observations); "This sensitivity range covers the physical scales of interest for our analysis, specifically probing both circumstellar and circumbinary emission." (Observations)
- Why this method: Visibility fitting accesses dust properties below image-plane resolution; simplified CO models target inclinations and velocities under resolution and sensitivity limits  
  > "To measure continuum disk properties below the image-plane resolution, we forward model the continuum visibilities with an exponentially tapered power-law intensity profile," (1.3 mm Continuum); "In the present work, we are primarily interested in the individual disk inclinations and their systemic velocities." (Modeling the CO Gas Disk); "the sensitivity and angular resolution of our data limit our ability to constrain a full suite of disk parameters" (Modeling the CO Gas Disk)
- Alternative: Combined amplitude-only self-calibration rejected because it did not significantly improve data quality  
  > "An iteration of amplitude-only self-calibration did not significantly improve the data quality, leaving the last iteration of phase-only self-calibration as our fiducial continuum data set." (Calibration and Imaging)
- Alternative: CO radius priors capped at 8 and 5.5 AU tested; parameters agree within 68% intervals, but constrained fits miss outer emission  
  > "we perform two additional MCMC fits that limit the upper bound of the radius priors to 8 and 5.5 AU." (Modeling the CO Gas Disk); "The disk parameters show some variation, but all have agreement within the 68% confidence interval to the fiducial fit." (Modeling the CO Gas Disk); "The radius-constrained fits are unable to describe the emission at larger radii from the continuum peaks" (Modeling the CO Gas Disk)
- Alternative: Retrograde misaligned disk solution retained as an unresolved alternative  
  > "Although we cannot strictly rule out the pathological misaligned scenario," (Disk-Orbit Alignment)
- Alternative: Magnetic and nonmagnetic stellar evolution models compared; broad total-mass prior adopted  
  > "the models that include prescriptions for the effect of magnetic fields (DSEP-magnetic, SPOTS $f_{spot} >$ 0) predict masses up to 65% higher." (Modeling the Spectral Energy Distribution); "we adopt the total-mass mean from the DSEP model above and a 1$\sigma$ width of 20% (i.e., $\mathcal{N}(0.58,0.12)$) as our prior in the orbit fit." (Modeling the Spectral Energy Distribution)
- Alternative: Formation alignment and subsequent viscous evolution cannot be distinguished  
  > "The present analysis does not distinguish between formation or subsequent evolution as the source of FO Tau’s disk-orbit alignment." (Disk-Orbit Alignment)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration stops at SNR plateau, >20% flagged solutions or 6 s integration; expensive visibility fits use visual chain convergence and final chain segments  
  > "the SNR does not improve from the previous iteration, the number of flagged solutions for any time interval exceeds 20%, or we reach the native integration step (6 s)." (Calibration and Imaging); "inspection of the parameter chains shows clear convergence." (1.3 mm Continuum); "we assess convergence by visually inspecting the parameter chains and confirming that parameter values are constant in 100 step increments moving backward from the last step." (Modeling the CO Gas Disk)
- Limitation (authors): Dust image-plane radii and projections are not robust  
  > "we cannot robustly measure disk radii or their projections (e.g., inclination, position angle) in the image plane." (1.3 mm Continuum)
- Limitation (authors): Cloud absorption excludes CO channels between 5 and 8 km s^{-1}  
  > "ignoring channels between 5 and 8 km s$^{-1}$ that have foreground cloud absorption." (Modeling the CO Gas Disk)
- Limitation (authors): Simple isolated-disk models may bias radii and other parameters when fitting extended emission  
  > "the disk radii and other parameters may be affected by our simple disk model attempting to fit extended emission." (Modeling the CO Gas Disk)
- Limitation (authors): Near/far disk sides and rotation direction are degenerate  
  > "our observation does not reveal the near and far sides of the disks." (Disk-Orbit Alignment)
- Limitation (authors): Compact disks may be optically thick; dust masses are lower limits and no total disk mass is attempted  
  > "These values are taken to be lower limits on the total dust mass considering how compact the disks are," (1.3 mm Continuum); "We do not attempt to compute a total disk mass for this reason." (1.3 mm Continuum)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar parameter uncertainties have model-dependent and astrophysical systematics  
  > "The primary errors quoted in Table [3](#tab:char) are internal fitting uncertainties and do not include systematic or additional astrophysical sources of error." (Modeling High-Resolution NIR Spectra); "The uncertainties of this fit are likely underestimated." (Modeling the Spectral Energy Distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Only approximately one quarter of the orbit is traced, requiring informed mass priors  
  > "With only $\sim$one quarter of the orbit traced with astrometry, the current observational baseline does not precisely constrain the stellar masses on its own." (Modeling the Spectral Energy Distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Only one NIR RV epoch prevents direct jitter estimation  
  > "With only one RV epoch we cannot measure a jitter value for either star, nor can we fit for it within the orbital solution." (Binary Orbital Solution)
