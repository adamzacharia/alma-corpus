## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA 12CO/13CO J=2-1 cubes**: CASA pipeline calibration, phase self-cal on compact-config continuum applied to line spws, uvcontsub, tclean at 0.22 km/s with robust 0.5; extra uvtaper for one smoothed 12CO channel  
  > "We extracted the emission lines from the calibrated visibilities after subtracting continuum emission using the `uvcontsub` function in CASA." (2.3 ALMA); "We used the `tclean` task, with a spectral resolution of 0.22 km s$^{-1}$ and a Briggs robust parameter of 0.5, to produce two sets of line emission maps." (2.3 ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 0.5; 0.22 km/s channels; beams 0.19" x 0.37" and 0.74" x 0.67"  
  > "For the maps combining both extended and compact configuration observations, we obtain an angular resolution of 0.19$''$$\times$0.37$''$." (2.3 ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Moment maps, systemic velocity and CO radial/vertical extent**: Compare blue- and red-shifted channels for Vsys; make moment maps; inspect individual 12CO channels (V-Vsys = -0.3 km/s) for faint extended emission  
  > "By comparing the blue- and red-shifted channels, we estimate that $V_{sys} = 7.4$ km s$^{-1}$" (3.4 The gaseous disk); "faint $^{12}$CO emission is detected out to much larger distances and is best mapped in a few individual channels." (3.4 The gaseous disk)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: distance 130 pc to convert 8 arcsec to about 1000 au  
  > "at a distance of 130 pc (cite: galli2019)" (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Vsys compared with L1495 cloud range 7.0+/-0.5 km/s; 12CO suppression at 7-8 km/s attributed to filtered-out cloud emission  
  > "consistent with cloud emission (cite: goldsmith2008) that is filtered by the interferometer" (3.4 The gaseous disk)
3. **Estimate stellar dynamical mass from PV diagrams**: 12CO and 13CO position-velocity diagrams overlaid with Keplerian curves; coarse exploration of mass  
  > "We then constructed the $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO position-velocity diagrams and superimposed representative Keplerian curves (Figure [10](#fig:PVdiag))." (3.4 The gaseous disk); "A coarse exploration suggests a stellar mass in the 0.3–0.4 $M_\odot$ range." (3.4 The gaseous disk)
   - assumption: near Keplerian rotation  
  > "estimate the dynamical mass of the central star via its (near) Keplerian motion" (3.4 The gaseous disk)
   - fitting: not stated (no formal fit; precise estimate declared out of scope)  
  > "While obtaining a precise estimate of the stellar mass is beyond the scope of this study since it does not directly affect our analysis" (3.4 The gaseous disk)
   - validation: compared with spectral-type range of Luhman et al. 2009 and the 0.25 Msun of Simon et al. 2019  
  > "Conversely, the 0.25 $M_\odot$ estimated by (cite: Simon2019) is too low." (3.4 The gaseous disk)
4. **Measure dark-lane thickness (nebula separation) vs wavelength in HST and JWST images**: vertical brightness profiles in 0.4-0.5" windows, polynomial fits to nebula peaks, second-order fit to each spine, minimum distance between parabolas  
  > "We first generated vertical brightness profiles, averaging within 0.4$''$–0.5$''$ windows to improve SNR, and fitted sixth to eighth order polynomials to the peak of each nebulae." (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light); "fitted second order polynomials to the spine of each nebulae to evaluate the closest distance between the resulting parabolas" (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
   - parameter: profiles traced out to 1.5-3 arcsec on either side of the symmetry axis depending on filter  
  > "We repeated the process out to 1.5$''$–3$''$ (depending on filter) on either side of the symmetry axis" (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
   - fitting: polynomial fits (6th-8th order to peaks, 2nd order to spines)  
  > "fitted sixth to eighth order polynomials to the peak of each nebulae" (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
   - validation: uncertainty from varying polynomial degrees, about 0.02 arcsec on d_neb  
  > "Varying the polynomial degrees used in the method, we estimate uncertainties of about 0.02$''$ on $d_{neb}$." (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
5. **Measure vertical separation of the two surfaces in ALMA continuum**: extract profile within 0.5 arcsec of the symmetry axis and fit a double Gaussian  
  > "we extracted the profile within 0.5$''$ of the symmetry axis and noticed that it presents a shoulder characteristic of two distinct components separated by a distance comparable to the projected beam size" (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light); "We thus fitted a double Gaussian model to this profile and estimated a distance between the two surfaces of 0.11$''$$\pm$0.02$''$" (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
   - assumption: disk still optically thick at 890 micron but surfaces not fully separated  
  > "We interpret this as an indication that the disk is still optically thick at 890 $\mu$m, but with the two disk surfaces not fully separated." (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
   - fitting: double Gaussian (priors/sampler not stated)  
  > "We thus fitted a double Gaussian model to this profile" (3.1 The dust disk in scattered light)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Radiative transfer modelling of grain size distribution and settling**: MCFOST models with fixed stellar and disk structure; vary size distribution (p, amin, amax, pristine dust) and settling (a_mix, eta_settl); adjust disk mass to match dark-lane thickness at 2 micron  
  > "All model calculations are performed with the MCFOST radiative transfer code (cite: pinte2006; pinte2009)." (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup); "we then adjusted the disk mass to match the observed dark lane thickness at 2 $\mu$m" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - software: MCFOST
   - parameter: Mstar = 0.4 Msun, Teff = 3700 K  
  > "we adopt a stellar mass of 0.4 $M_\odot$ (Section [3.4](#subsec:gasdisk)) and an effective temperature of 3700 K" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - parameter: L* = 0.85 Lsun  
  > "We further set the stellar luminosity to $L_\star = 0.85\,L_\odot$ based on the median luminosity of Taurus members with the same spectral type" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - parameter: gamma = gamma_exp = 1, r_c = 300 au, r_out = 400 au  
  > "where we set $\gamma = \gamma_{\exp} = 1$ and $r_c = 300$ au, coupled with a hard outer cutoff at $r_{out} = 400$ au" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - parameter: h0 = 11 au at r0 = 100 au, beta = 1.125  
  > "characterized by a scale height of $h_0 =11$ au at $r_0 = 100$ au and a flaring exponent of $\beta = 1.125$" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - parameter: inclination 88 deg  
  > "we selected an inclination of $i=88$$^\circ$" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - parameter: standard size distribution p = 3.5, amin = 0.03 micron, amax = 1 mm  
  > "power law with $p=3.5$ ranging from $a_\mathrm{min} = 0.03\,\mu$m to $a_\mathrm{max} = 1$ mm" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - parameter: settling models a_mix = 100, 10, 1 micron with eta_settl = -0.5  
  > "we considered three settling models in which grains up to $a_\mathrm{mix}\!=\!100\,\mu$m, $10\,\mu$m or $1\,\mu$m are fully mixed, with large grains increasingly settled with $\eta_\mathrm{settl} = -0.5$" (4.2 Modeling results)
   - assumption: DIANA dust composition with Distribution of Hollow Spheres  
  > "we adopted the DIANA mixture, namely a 60/15/25 (volume fraction) mixture of silicates, amorphous carbon and vacuum (cite: woitke2016)" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - assumption: Gaussian vertical density profile (vertically isothermal), temperature computed during radiative transfer  
  > "We assumed a Gaussian vertical density profile, appropriate for a vertically isothermal disk" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup); "the temperature is subsequently computed locally during the radiative transfer calculation" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - assumption: settling prescription h(a) proportional to (a/a_mix)^eta_settl for a > a_mix  
  > "grains with $a > a_\mathrm{mix}$ have a reduced scale height: $h(a) \propto (a/a_\mathrm{mix})^{\eta_\mathrm{settl}}$, with $\eta_\mathrm{settl} < 0$" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - fitting: manual exploration of discrete models; only disk mass tuned to match 2 micron dark lane (no sampler or likelihood)  
  > "These values were selected based on an initial manual exploration of the parameter space" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
   - validation: model images and SED computed at other wavelengths (optical to sub-mm) and judged acceptable or not against the observations  
  > "we then compute images at other wavelengths from the optical to the sub-millimeter as well as the complete SED, and determine whether it is an acceptable fit to the observations" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
7. **Compare models to observations and infer grain mixing and settling**: Compare dark-lane thickness chromaticity, 890 micron map and SED among models  
  > "Furthermore, Figure [14](#fig:model_geometry_nosettling) presents the wavelength dependence of the distance between the top and bottom nebulae" (4.2 Modeling results)
   - software: MCFOST
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Scattered-light morphology changes very little from 0.6 to 21 micron, implying near-gray opacity of dust in the disk surface layers  
  > "indicating that dust in the disk surface layers is characterized by an almost gray opacity law." (abstract)
- 12CO emission is most extended at very low velocity relative to systemic, suggesting non-Keplerian motion  
  > "The fact that the CO emission is most extended at such a low velocity (relative to the systemic velocity) is highly unusual among EODs" (3.4 The gaseous disk)
- The warm molecular layer traced by 12CO lies between the 5-8 micron scattered-light surface and the X-shaped feature  
  > "It appears that the warm molecular layer is located between the 5–8 $\mu$m scattered light disk “surface" and the X-shaped feature itself." (3.3 A new X-shaped feature)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA CO maps used to locate the warm molecular layer and estimate the dynamical stellar mass  
  > "CO ALMA maps to locate the warm molecular layer of the disk and to estimate the dynamical mass of the central star" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Dark-lane thickness used as a simple observable tracer of dust opacity and vertical structure, allowing an exploratory model rather than a full fit  
  > "we consider the thickness of the dark lane that separate the two scattered light surfaces ($d_{neb}$) as a direct, albeit reductive, observable tracer of dust opacity and disk vertical structure" (4.1 Objectives and modeling setup)
- Alternative: Pristine molecular-cloud dust model rejected: strongly chromatic dark lane, underpredicts 2-20 micron SED and gives too concentrated sub-mm image  
  > "In addition, this model underpredicts the SED by more than an order of magnitude in the 2–20 $\mu$m range, for lack of effective scatterers in that wavelength regime." (4.2 Modeling results)
- Alternative: Fully mixed models (standard, p = 3.25, amin = 0.3 micron) reproduce HST/JWST but not the submillimeter map  
  > "However, they still produce submillimeter maps that do not match the observed one." (4.2 Modeling results)

  > "indicating that this it produces an insufficient degree of settling for mm-emitting grains" (4.2 Modeling results)
