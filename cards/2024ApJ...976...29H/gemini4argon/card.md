## Qualitative findings

- Two localized, chemically rich regions containing H2CO, CH3OH, OCS, 13CS, and H2S are discovered near the bases of the bipolar molecular outflow in HOPS 87.  
  > "In the protostellar core HOPS 87, we discovered two chemically rich and localized regions containing CH 3 OH, H 2 CO, OCS, [13] CS, and H 2 S near the bases of the molecular bipolar outflow." (5. CONCLUSIONS)
- The localized chemically rich regions are driven by jet shocks rather than wide-angle winds or being part of the central hot corino.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Cycle 6 ALMASOP data provide wide spectral coverage of multiple COMs and outflow tracers with combined 12-m and 7-m arrays, while Cycle 3 archival data provide higher spatial (0.16'') and velocity (0.32 km s^-1) resolution to separate the lobes from the hot corino.  
  > "This study also retrieved ALMA Band 6 data obtained from a Cycle 3 program (#2015.1.00341.S, PI: Satoko Takahashi) for better-visualizing the intensity maps of the CO 2 − 1 and CH 3 OH 5 1 , 4 – 4 2 , 4 transitions as it had a much better spatial resolution (FWHM beam size ∼ 0 ′′ . 16)." (2.2. Archival Data: ALMA Cycle 3 #2015.1.00341.S)
- Why this method: PV diagrams along and across the outflow axis distinguish between central hot corino emission, wide-angle winds, and jet-driven bow shocks based on kinematic spur structures.  
  > "To have a closer look at the kinematics, we made the position-velocity (PV) diagrams (Figure 4) cut along the outflow axis" (3.2. Kinematics); "To compare the chemical compositions of the hot corino and chemically rich shocked regions near the outflow origins, we extracted the PV diagrams with cuts along and across the outflow axis" (4.1.2. Comparisons with the counterpart hot corino)
- Alternative: The two chemically rich lobes are the outer extended parts of the hot corino (rejected based on high-resolution channel maps and kinematic offsets).  
  > "We first checked whether the two regions are the outer extended parts of the hot corino in HOPS 87." (3.1.5. Molecules Exhibiting Two Localized Lobes); "These observations suggest that the two lobes and the hot corino are tracing different regions." (3.1.5. Molecules Exhibiting Two Localized Lobes)
- Alternative: The hole in C18O emission at the continuum peak is due to a complete absence of C18O molecules (rejected; attributed instead to comparable dust continuum and C18O excitation temperatures / continuum absorption).  
  > "Given that the C [18] O emission is significant around the hole, it is unlikely that it results from a complete absence of C [18] O molecules. This hole could be due to the comparable dust (continuum) temperature and the C [18] O gas (excitation) temperature." (3.1.2. C18O: Extended Warm Gas)
- Alternative: The molecules in the localized lobes trace wide-angle winds rather than jet-driven outflows (rejected based on localized velocity broadening in PV diagrams).  
  > "These characteristics suggest that these molecules (i.e., SiO, CH 3 OH, H 2 CO, OCS, H 2 S, and 13 CS) are more likely tracing jet-driven outflows rather than wide-angle winds (Lee et al. 2001)." (3.2. Kinematics)
- Alternative: Pure thermal desorption instead of shock-induced grain sputtering for the localized lobes and bow shock components (rejected in favor of grain sputtering).  
  > "Among the above two mechanisms (i.e., ice sublimation and grain sputtering) able to lead the richness of molecules, in particular those form within ice mantle such as CH 3 OH and OCS, our findings favor the shock-induced grain sputtering picture" (4.1.1. The Origin of the Localized Chemically Rich Feature)
