## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA Band 6 continuum and CO(2-1) line datacube**: Calibrate uv data with CASA pipeline, manually flag bad antennas/baselines with flagdata, image continuum with tclean, subtract continuum in uv plane with uvcontsub (1st-order polynomial), bin to 15 km/s, clean cube with CASA tclean and GIPSY clean, and extract moment maps with 3DBAROLO  
  > "The $uv$ data were flagged and calibrated using the standard CASA pipeline. After preliminary imaging of the combined dataset, additional bad antennas and baselines were visually identified and flagged using the CASA task `flagdata` in the `manual` mode." (ALMA observations); "The CO datacube was obtained after subtracting the continuum in the $uv$ plane using the CASA task `uvcontsub`, fitting a first-order polynomial to the line-free channels. The spectral channels were binned to 15 $\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$to increase the signal-to-noise ratio." (ALMA observations); "Moment maps were constructed using the $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO package (cite: Barolo)." (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, GIPSY, 3DBAROLO
   - parameter: Högbom deconvolver, Briggs robust = 0.5, cleaned down to ~3 sigma, 15 km/s channel binning, GIPSY Boolean mask smoothed to 0.4'' clipped at 3 sigma_s  
  > "Both the line and continuum data were imaged using the `tclean` task with a Högbom deconvolver and Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5. The images were interactively cleaned down to $\sim$3$\sigma$." (ALMA observations); "(2) using the task `clean` in the Groningen Imaging Processing System (GIPSY) package (cite: Gipsy), adopting a Boolean mask that was constructed by smoothing the dirty cube to a resolution of 0.4$''$ and clipping at 3$\sigma_{\rm s}$ (where $\sigma_{\rm s}$ is the rms noise of the smoothed cube)." (ALMA observations)
   - fitting: First-order polynomial fitted to line-free channels in uvcontsub  
  > "The CO datacube was obtained after subtracting the continuum in the $uv$ plane using the CASA task `uvcontsub`, fitting a first-order polynomial to the line-free channels." (ALMA observations)
   - validation: Compared dynamical modeling results from CASA tclean cube and GIPSY clean cube; checked spatial rms noise map is Gaussian  
  > "The results of our dynamical modeling (Sect. 4) do not strongly depend on the adopted cleaning procedure." (ALMA observations); "We also built a map of the mean rms noise at each spatial position and checked that it is Gaussian with a mean value of 0.7 mJy/beam per channel." (ALMA observations)
2. **Extract HST F606W surface photometry and calculate the stellar gravitational potential**: Fit ellipses to HST WFPC2 F606W isophotes using ARCHANGEL to derive the 1D surface brightness profile; non-parametrically subtract the inner HSB (AGN/starburst) component within R_HSB ~ 200 pc; compute stellar circular velocity contribution using both 1D MGE (MGEFIT) and thick-disk (GIPSY Rotmod) methods  
  > "We performed surface photometry on the Planetary Camera (PC) frame (Fig. 1) using the ARCHANGEL software (cite: Archangel)." (HST surface photometry); "The HSB component is decomposed in a non-parametric fashion, following a similar strategy as (cite: Lelli2016) for bulge-disk decompositions." (Stellar gravitational potential); "We explored two different approaches to compute the stellar gravitational contribution from the 1D luminosity profile (with or without the HSB component)." (Stellar gravitational potential)
   - software: ARCHANGEL, MGEFIT, GIPSY
   - parameter: 1D MGE axial ratio q = 0.01; thick-disk exponential vertical scale height h_z = 253 pc; baseline HSB subtraction of ~10^9 L_odot within ~200 pc  
  > "For our baseline model, the luminosity of the HSB component is about $10^9$ L$_\odot$ within $\sim$200 pc ($\sim$0.5$''$), corresponding to 8$\%$ of the total luminosity of Fairall 49." (Stellar gravitational potential); "In the 1D MGE approach, we adopt a constant axial ratios $q=0.01$ for all the MGE Gaussian components, so the density is stratified on oblate spheroids." (Stellar gravitational potential); "In the thick-disk approach, we assume an exponential vertical density distribution with fixed scale height $h_{\rm z} = 253$ pc at each radius." (Stellar gravitational potential)
   - assumption: Inner HSB component within ~200 pc is dominated by AGN and starburst emission rather than tracing stellar mass (assigned M/L = 0 in baseline model)  
  > "This suggests that the inner HSB component is dominated by AGN and starburst emission, rather than tracing stellar mass." (Stellar gravitational potential); "In our baseline mass model, this inner HSB component is subtracted (see inset in Fig. 2) and neglected in the calculation of $V_{\star}$. Essentially it is assigned $\Upsilon_\star=0$." (Mass models & rotation-curve fits)
   - assumption: Thick-disk vertical scale height h_z = 253 pc from empirical relation h_z = 0.196 h_R^0.633 with h_R ~ 1.5 kpc  
  > "This scale height is motivated by the empirical relation $h_{\rm z} = 0.196 h_{\rm R}^{0.633}$ from edge-on disk galaxies (cite: Bershady2010), where $h_{\rm R}$ is the observed scale length in the outer regions ($\sim$1.5 kpc for Fairall 49)." (Stellar gravitational potential)
   - fitting: Automated ellipse fitting to isophotes, asymptotic fit to curve-of-growth, and 1D multi-Gaussian expansion fit  
  > "Next, ellipses were automatically fitted to the galaxy isophotes and the azimuthally-averaged surface brightness profile was determined. We also built the luminosity curve-of-growth, replacing empty pixels with the mean value over the isophote, and determined the total magnitude with an asymptotic fit." (HST surface photometry); "The first approach (1D MGE) fits the surface brightness profiles with MGEFIT (cite: Cappellari2002) approximating the profile with multiple Gaussians" (Stellar gravitational potential)
   - validation: Compared 1D MGE and thick-disk circular velocity profiles across four levels of HSB subtraction (none, minimal, baseline, maximal) and checked consistency of HSB luminosity with AGN torus templates  
  > "If we use the AGN templates of (cite: Stalevski2012; Stalevski2016) scaled to the bolometric luminosity of Fairall 49 ($\sim$10$^{11}$ from X-ray observations) and assume that the AGN torus is co-aligned with the disk, we find that the F606W luminosity of the AGN is in the range 10$^{8}-10^{9}$ L$_\odot$, consistent with the luminosity of the HSB component." (Stellar gravitational potential); "For our adopted subtraction of the inner HSB component, both the thick-disk and the 1D MGE models generate similar circular velocities and can reproduce both kinematic and photometric data." (Stellar gravitational potential)
3. **Fit 3D tilted-ring kinematic model to the CO(2-1) datacube**: Divide the CO disk into 19 independent concentric rings and perform a two-stage 3D tilted-ring fit with 3DBAROLO to derive rotation velocity, radial velocity, and velocity dispersion profiles  
  > "We adopt 19 rings with a width of 0.186$''$, corresponding to the geometric resolution element $\sqrt{a\times b}$ where $a$ and $b$ are the major and minor axes of the synthesized beam, thus the rings are fully independent." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions); "We set the options “twostage=true” and “polyn=0”: in a first run all seven parameters are left free, then the geometric parameters (center, $V_{\rm sys}$, PA, and inc) are fixed to the mean values across the rings and a second fit is performed with only three free parameters ($V_{\rm rot}$, $V_{\rm rad}$, $\sigma_{\rm V}$)." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions)
   - software: 3DBAROLO
   - parameter: 19 rings of width 0.186'' (out to ~3.5''), cos(theta) azimuthal weighting, exponential vertical profile with z_0 = 100 pc (0.25''), twostage=true, polyn=0  
  > "We assume an exponential vertical distribution with a fixed scale height of 100 pc (0.25$''$): the precise value of $z_0$ has virtually no effects on our results." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions); "We adopt 19 rings with a width of 0.186$''$, corresponding to the geometric resolution element $\sqrt{a\times b}$ where $a$ and $b$ are the major and minor axes of the synthesized beam, thus the rings are fully independent." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions); "Within each ring, the 3D pixels are weighted according to cos($\theta$) where $\theta$ is the azimuthal angle in the disk plane measured from the projected major axis; this is a standard approach in tilted-ring modeling because the kinematic information lies mainly along the disk major axis." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions)
   - assumption: Fully axisymmetric flat gas disk with exponential vertical distribution (scale height 100 pc); western side is near side based on HST dust lanes  
  > "For simplicity, we adopt a fully axisymmetric disk, so the surface density of each ring is directly computed from the observed CO intensity map using azimuthal averages." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions); "The dust lanes visible in the HST image (Fig. 1, left panel) strongly suggest that the western side is the near one, so the disk is rotating in anti-clockwise direction and the radial motions are an inflow." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions)
   - fitting: Two-stage 3D tilted-ring residual minimization in 3DBAROLO; asymmetric errors correspond to 5% residual variation from global minimum  
  > "We set the options “twostage=true” and “polyn=0”: in a first run all seven parameters are left free, then the geometric parameters (center, $V_{\rm sys}$, PA, and inc) are fixed to the mean values across the rings and a second fit is performed with only three free parameters ($V_{\rm rot}$, $V_{\rm rad}$, $\sigma_{\rm V}$)." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions); "$^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO provide asymmetric errors ($\delta_{+}$, $\delta_{-}$) on $V_{\rm rot}$ corresponding to a variation of 5$\%$ of the residuals from the global minimum: we compute symmetric 1$\sigma$ errorbars as $\sqrt{\delta_{+}\delta_{-}}$." (Mass models & rotation-curve fits)
   - validation: Inspected velocity residual map and major/minor axis PV diagrams  
  > "To further investigate non-circular motions, Figure 5 shows the observed, model, and residual velocity fields." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions); "Figure 6 shows position-velocity (PV) diagrams along the major and minor axis of the disk. Overall, the observed PV diagrams are well reproduced by our model." (Tilted-ring modeling: rotation and non-circular motions)
4. **Fit dynamical mass models to the rotation curve and derive gas inflow rate**: Fit a 4-component mass model (stellar disk, molecular gas disk, central SMBH, NFW dark matter halo) to the 3DBAROLO rotation curve using MCMC (emcee), and compute radial gas inflow rate Mdot_gas(R) = M_gas(R) * V_rad(R) / R  
  > "We build a mass model with four components: a stellar disk, a cold gas disk, a central SMBH, and a dark matter (DM) halo. The model has six parameters: disk inclination ($i$), stellar mass ($M_{\star}$), gas mass ($M_{\rm gas}$), SMBH mass ($M_{\bullet}$), halo mass ($M_{200}$), and halo concentration ($C_{200}$)." (Mass models & rotation-curve fits); "The posterior probability distributions of the parameters are mapped using `emcee` (cite: Foreman2013)." (Mass models & rotation-curve fits); "To a first order approximation, $\dot{M}_{\rm gas}$ can be estimated as $$\begin{equation} \label{eq:inflow} \dot{M}_{\rm gas}(R) = M_{\rm gas}(R) \dfrac{V_{\rm rad}(R)}{R}, \end{equation}$$" (Gas mass & gas inflow rate)
   - software: GIPSY, emcee, corner.py
   - parameter: 200 walkers, 1000 burn-in iterations, 2000 sampling iterations, stretch move parameter a = 2, gas vertical scale height h_z = 100 pc  
  > "The MCMC chains are initialized with 200 walkers." (Mass models & rotation-curve fits); "The MCMC chains are initialized with 200 walkers." (Mass models & rotation-curve fits); "We run 1000 burn-in iterations, then the sampler is run for another 2000 iterations. ## Qualitative findings

- The CO(2-1) kinematics in Fairall 49 are well described by a regularly rotating disk of diameter ~3 kpc with radial inflow motions at R < 400 pc, indicating that AGN and starburst feedback have weak/moderate effects on the global cold gas disk.  
  > "The CO velocity map reveals a rotating disk with a diameter of $\sim$6$''$ ($\sim$3 kpc). The presence of a regular CO disk is not trivial given the LIRG and Seyfert nature of Fairall 49: the combined feedback from AGN and starburst activity appears to not strongly affect the overall kinematics of the molecular gas." (Gas distribution & kinematics); "The CO kinematics are well described by a rotating disk with a radial inflow at $R<400$ pc. The overall regularity of the molecular gas suggests weak feedback from both the starburst and AGN activity." (Conclusions)
- Fairall 49 lies on the same M_bullet - M_star scaling relation as early-type galaxies within 2 sigma of its intrinsic scatter, while its SMBH mass is ~50 times higher than previous X-ray variability estimates.  
  > "(cite: Iwasawa2016) found $2\times10^6$ M$_\odot$ from X-ray variability; this value is in strong disagreement with our value being 50 times smaller." (Implications for the SMBH and host galaxy); "Figure 3 shows that Fairall 49 lies on the same $M_\bullet-M_\star$ relation as ETGs within 2$\sigma$ of its intrinsic scatter, giving further support to the reliability of our SMBH mass measurement." (Implications for the SMBH and host galaxy)
- The dynamically inferred molecular gas mass is ~30 times lower than predicted using a Galactic X_CO and thermalized R_21 = 1, implying a starburst-like X_CO,20 ~ 0.2 and a Seyfert-like R_21 ~ 2.5.  
  > "(4) The dynamically inferred molecular gas mass is 30 times smaller than that inferred from adopting the Galactic CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor ($\ensuremath{X_\mathrm{CO}}$) for thermalised gas, suggesting low values of $X_\mathrm{CO}$;" (Abstract); "Thus, for a starburst-like value of $X_{\rm CO, 20}\simeq0.2$ and a Seyfert-like value of $R_{21}\simeq2.5$, the luminosity-based and dynamical-based gas masses of Fairall 49 would be entirely consistent." (Implications for the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor)
- No strong evidence for a molecular gas outflow is found; tentative anomalous central CO emission at -100 to -200 km/s has velocities well below the ~700 km/s galaxy escape velocity.  
  > "We do not find strong evidence for a molecular gas outflow" (Implications for the AGN baryon cycle); "The velocities of the putative outflow, therefore, would be significantly smaller than the galaxy escape velocity ($\sim$700 $\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at $R\lesssim 100$ pc) and the gas would eventually fall back on the system in a galactic-fountain fashion." (Implications for the AGN baryon cycle)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (~80 pc) ALMA CO(2-1) observations resolve the SMBH sphere of influence (~150 pc) in a dynamically cold gas disk where pressure support is negligible; HST F606W is the reddest unsaturated high-resolution optical image available to trace stellar mass.  
  > "Over the past decade, another method has become available thanks to the substantial increase in angular resolution and sensitivity of sub-millimeter interferometry: probing the rotation of dynamically cold gas disks using CO emission lines (cite: Davis2013)." (Introduction); "Since we are interested in modeling the stellar mass distribution and calculating the stellar gravitational field, we analysed the reddest optical image available, taken with the F606W filter of the Wide Field and Planetary Camera 2 (WFPC2; Project ID 5479)." (HST surface photometry); "For the baseline mass model in Figure 1, the SOI of the SMBH occurs at $R\simeq150$ pc (within which $V_\bullet > V_\star$), so it is well resolved by the angular resolution of the ALMA data ($\sim$80 pc)." (Supermassive black hole mass)
- Why this method: 1D non-parametric HSB subtraction gives direct control over resolved AGN/starburst light that 2D MGEFIT cannot handle; combining non-parametric 3DBAROLO tilted-ring fits with parametric KINMS forward-modeling cross-checks kinematic and dynamical degeneracies.  
  > "Thus, we prefer to compute the stellar gravitational contribution using the azimuthally-averaged surface brightness profile (Fig. 2), performing a classic 1-dimensional (1D) subtraction of the HSB component to have more direct control on the inner light profile." (Stellar gravitational potential); "Approach (i) is computationally fast and allows modeling the gas dynamics in a non-parametric fashion, but it is not straightforward to investigate possible degeneracies between the kinematic parameters. Approach (ii) is more computationally expensive and requires defying a parametric model (including the gravitational potential) but explores the full parameter space with a Markov-Chain-Monte-Carlo (MCMC)." (Gas distribution & kinematics)
- Alternative: Two additional ALMA C43-8 execution blocks from November 2017 were rejected because they were flagged as 'semi-pass' by QA.  
  > "Two additional execution blocks were obtained in configuration C43-8 on 23 and 26 November 2017, but they were flagged as “semi-pass” by the ALMA quality-assurance team and are not analysed here." (ALMA observations)
- Alternative: HST NICMOS F160W near-IR image was rejected because it is heavily saturated, and ground-based VISTA Hemisphere Survey J-band image was rejected due to insufficient angular resolution.  
  > "A near-IR image in the F160W filter of the Near Infrared Camera and Multi-Object Spectrometer (NICMOS) exists but is heavily saturated, so is not useful for our purposes." (HST surface photometry); "We also analysed a ground-based $J$-band image from the VISTA Hemisphere Survey (cite: VHS) but its angular resolution is too low to trace the inner light distribution" (HST surface photometry)
- Alternative: 2D MGEFIT on the HST image and models with weak or no inner HSB light subtraction were rejected because they produce unphysical stellar rotation curve shapes or prolate nuclei with 90-degree twisted isophotes.  
  > "In the case of Fairall 49, the 2D MGEFIT gives a stellar velocity curve that completely differs in shape from the observed rotation curve derived in Sect. 4.2, unless we remove the two innermost Gaussian components from the calculation." (Stellar gravitational potential); "Overall, both approaches indicate that models with a substantial HSB component are unable to fit the data, so they must be discarded." (Stellar gravitational potential)
- Alternative: Assigning the inner mass to a point-like unresolved nuclear star cluster instead of a SMBH was considered and rejected as unlikely given the resolved HST HSB profile and Seyfert-1h spectrum.  
  > "The only way to remove the need of a SMBH is to assign a substantial fraction ($\sim$15$\%$) of the luminosity of the HSB component to a point-like (unresolved) nuclear star cluster with $\Upsilon_\star\simeq1$" (Supermassive black hole mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The innermost pixels of the HST WFPC2 F606W image are saturated, and the inner light concentration is slightly offset (~100 pc) from outer isophotes, possibly due to dust obscuration.  
  > "The light concentration is also slightly offset ($\sim$100 pc) from the outer isophotes, possibly due to dust obscuration." (The target: a LIRG with a Seyfert nucleus); "The weak horizontal stripe in the middle of Fig. 1 shows that the innermost pixels are saturated, but their contribution to the total luminosity is negligible (a few percent)." (HST surface photometry)
- Limitation (authors): The dynamically inferred molecular gas mass and DM halo mass have large uncertainties because both components are strongly subdominant to the stellar potential at all fitted radii.  
  > "The molecular gas mass and the DM halo mass are less well determined than the SMBH mass because these two components are strongly subdominant at each radius." (Gas mass & gas inflow rate)
- Limitation (authors): The gas inflow rate equation provides only an upper limit, and radial inflow velocities differ by a factor of ~5 between 3DBAROLO (~75 km/s) and KINMS (~15 km/s) due to degeneracy with gas velocity dispersion.  
  > "provides an upper limit on $\dot{M_{H_2}}$ because we cannot tell how much gas in each ring is directly involved in the radial flow." (Gas mass & gas inflow rate); "Moreover, in the next section, we will show that the forward modeling with KINMS provide significantly lower values of $V_{\rm rad}$ ($\sim$15 $\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$rather than $\sim$75 $\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$), so the gas inflow rates in Fig. 1 may be up to a factor 5 smaller." (Gas mass & gas inflow rate); "It is likely that KINMS is compensating a lower $V_{\rm rad}$ with a higher $\sigma_{\rm gas}$, or alternatively $^{\rm3D}$Barolo is compensating a higher $V_{\rm rad}$ with a lower $\sigma_{\rm gas}$." (Forward modeling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust attenuation in the optical HST F606W band could significantly distort the inner stellar surface brightness profile in a LIRG nucleus, introducing additional systematic uncertainty into the stellar mass-to-light ratio and SMBH mass beyond the parametric HSB subtraction tests.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a spatially constant stellar mass-to-light ratio across a merging starburst system with young spiral arms and older shells may bias the relative radial weighting of the stellar and gas gravitational potentials.
