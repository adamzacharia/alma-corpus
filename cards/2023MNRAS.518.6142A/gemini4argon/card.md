## Science

- Fields: high_z_galaxies
- Question: What is the typical fraction of dust-obscured star formation in UV-selected galaxies at z~7 and what is the dust-obscured cosmic star formation rate density at z~7?  
  > "Here we use Atacama Large Millimeter Array observations of 45 galaxies from the Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey (REBELS) and its pilot programs, designed to target \[CII\] and dust emission in UV-selected galaxies at $z\sim7$, to investigate the dust content of high-redshift galaxies through a stacking analysis."   > "We utilize mean stacking in the image plane, at the native resolution of the REBELS images." (Stacking)

4. **Convert stacked flux densities to infrared luminosities, IR SFRs, and obscured fractions**: Apply CMB correction at median stack redshift, convert rest-frame 158 um luminosity to L_IR and SFR_IR, compute f_obs = SFR_IR / (SFR_IR + SFR_UV), and fit linear f_obs - M_star relation  
  > "We correct the stacked flux densities for the cosmic microwave background following (cite: dacunha2013) (see also (cite: inami2022)), thereby adopting the median redshift of the stack." (Infrared Luminosities and SFRs); "The infrared luminosities are calculated as $L_\mathrm{IR} = 11.7_{-2.3}^{+4.1} \times \nu_0 L_{\nu_0}$, where $\nu_0 \approx 1900\,$GHz is the rest-frame frequency of the \[CII\] emission line and $L_{\nu_0}$ the specific continuum luminosity at this frequency (cite: sommovigo2022)." (Infrared Luminosities and SFRs); "For each of the stacks we determine the fraction of obscured star formation as $f_\mathrm{obs} = \mathrm{SFR}_\mathrm{IR} / \left( \mathrm{SFR}_\mathrm{IR} + \mathrm{SFR}_\mathrm{UV} \right)$, where $\mathrm{SFR}_\mathrm{UV}$ represents the mean UV-based star formation rate of the galaxies within the corresponding bin." (Obscuration across the full REBELS sample)
   - software: Survival analysis package from (cite: davidson-pilon2019)
   - parameter: nu_0 approx 1900 GHz  
  > "The infrared luminosities are calculated as $L_\mathrm{IR} = 11.7_{-2.3}^{+4.1} \times \nu_0 L_{\nu_0}$, where $\nu_0 \approx 1900\,$GHz is the rest-frame frequency of the \[CII\] emission line and $L_{\nu_0}$ the specific continuum luminosity at this frequency (cite: sommovigo2022)." (Infrared Luminosities and SFRs)
   - assumption: L_IR = 11.7 (+4.1 / -2.3) * nu_0 * L_nu_0 (corresponding to modified blackbody with <T_dust> = 45 +/- 4 K and beta = 2.0)  
  > "The infrared luminosities are calculated as $L_\mathrm{IR} = 11.7_{-2.3}^{+4.1} \times \nu_0 L_{\nu_0}$, where $\nu_0 \approx 1900\,$GHz is the rest-frame frequency of the \[CII\] emission line and $L_{\nu_0}$ the specific continuum luminosity at this frequency (cite: sommovigo2022)." (Infrared Luminosities and SFRs); "As such, the infrared luminosities and limits determined for the REBELS sample effectively assume a modified blackbody SED with a dust temperature of $\langle T_\mathrm{dust} \rangle = 45\pm4\,$K and $\beta=2.0$." (Flux Density to Infrared Luminosity Conversion)
   - assumption: SFR_IR / (M_odot yr^-1) = 1.2e-10 * (L_IR / L_odot) assuming constant SFR for 100 Myr, Z = 0.002, and Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "In addition, when determining dust-obscured star formation rates from $L_\mathrm{IR}$, we adopt the conversion factor used throughout the REBELS papers given in (cite: bouwens2021) of $\mathrm{SFR}_\mathrm{IR} / (M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}) = 1.2 \times 10^{-10} \times \left(L_\mathrm{IR} / L_\odot\right)$." (Infrared Luminosities and SFRs); "This value – as well as the conversion for UV-based star formation rates quoted in Section [3.3](#sec:starburst) – is obtained from (cite: madau2014) under the assumption of a constant star formation rate for a duration of 100 Myr and a fixed metallicity of $Z=0.002$ (see also Figure 3 in (cite: madau2014))." (Infrared Luminosities and SFRs)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.30, Omega_Lambda = 0.70, and Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "Throughout this work, we assume a standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, with $H_0=70\,\text{km\,s}^{-1}\text{\,Mpc}^{-1}$, $\Omega_m=0.30$ and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.70$ and adopt a (cite: chabrier2003) initial mass function." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Survival analysis for upper limits in bins; linear fit to f_obs - M_star across N_MC samples with flat prior on slope > 0  
  > "In the case of upper limits among the MC samples, we determine the median and uncertainties through a standard survival analysis, making use of the package (cite: davidson-pilon2019)." (Obscuration across the full REBELS sample); "We determine $M_\star^\mathrm{lower}$ as the mass where our $f_\mathrm{obs} - M_\star$ relation predicts that dust-obscured star formation is negligible: we first fit a linear function to the individual $N_\mathrm{MC}$ samples of the $f_\mathrm{obs} - M_\star$ relation. We adopt a flat prior on the slope, requiring it to be larger than zero." (The Full $z\sim7$ Obscured SFRD from REBELS)
   - validation: Tested T_dust = 35 K and 55 K in Section 5.4.5  
  > "As such, we here assume a simple systematic under/overprediction of the typical dust temperature of the REBELS sample by $\pm10\,$K." (Flux Density to Infrared Luminosity Conversion)
5. **Fit L_IR - M_star relation and integrate over z=7 SMF to derive obscured cosmic SFRD**: Fit linear relation to log(L_IR) vs log(M_star) for each of the N_MC stacks using MCMC, then integrate Phi(M_star) * L_IR(M_star) over stellar mass and convert rho_IR to SFRD  
  > "We stack the REBELS sample in distinct mass bins in order to determine a relation between the stacked infrared luminosity $L_\mathrm{IR}$ and stellar mass $M_\star$." (The Obscured Cosmic Star Formation Rate Density); "We fit the data using a standard Monte Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) analysis, making use of the library (cite: foreman-mackey2013)." (A Lower Limit on the $z\sim7$ Obscured SFRD); "We integrate the $L_\mathrm{IR} - M_\star$ relations over the (cite: stefanon2021) stellar mass function at $z=7$, incorporating the covariance between their fitted parameters into our error propagation." (A Lower Limit on the $z\sim7$ Obscured SFRD)
   - software: MCMC library from (cite: foreman-mackey2013)
   - parameter: Lower limit integration range: 8.8 <= log10(M_star / M_odot) <= 10.8  
  > "We first provide a direct measurement of the SFRD by considering only the mass range spanned within the REBELS sample of $8.8 \lesssim \log_{10}\left(M_\star / M_\odot\right) \lesssim 10.8$ and adopting this as our integration limit." (A Lower Limit on the $z\sim7$ Obscured SFRD)
   - parameter: Full SFRD integration range: log10(M_star^lower / M_odot) = 8.2 (+0.7 / -2.1) (where f_obs = 0, floored at 5.0) to log10(M_star^upper / M_odot) = 12.4

## Qualitative findings

- The fraction of obscured star formation in z~7 UV-selected galaxies increases with stellar mass, but is lower at fixed stellar mass than at z <= 2.5.  
  > "We find that $f_\mathrm{obs}$ increases with stellar mass, similar to what is observed at $z\lesssim2.5$ (cite: whitaker2017). However, at fixed $M_\star$, the REBELS sources are less obscured than their lower redshift counterparts, with the obscuration ranging from $f_\mathrm{obs} = 0.3 - 0.6$ across $\log_{10}\left(M_\star / M_\odot\right) = 9.4 - 10.4$ (Figure [\[fig:fobs_Mstar\]](#fig:fobs_Mstar))." (Summary)
- Continuum-detected REBELS sources follow the z <= 2.5 f_obs - M_star relation (f_obs ~ 0.6-0.7), whereas continuum non-detections have much lower obscuration (f_obs ~ 0.25), indicating a large diversity in dust properties at z~7.  
  > "As such, the continuum-detected sources show obscured fractions that are in close agreement with the $0 \lesssim z \lesssim 2.5$ results from (cite: whitaker2017), while the non-detections contain significantly less obscured star formation. This indicates a large variety in the dust properties of $z\sim7$ galaxies, even across a sample uniformly selected based on rest-frame UV emission." (Summary)
- The dust-obscured cosmic SFRD declines only modestly from z~3 to z~7 and contributes ~30% of the total cosmic SFRD at z~7.  
  > "Our results suggest only a modest decrease in the SFRD between $3\lesssim z \lesssim 7$, with dust-obscured star formation still contributing $\sim30\%$ at $z\sim7$." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: REBELS is the first systematic ALMA survey of [CII] and rest-frame 158 um dust continuum emission in UV-selected galaxies at z >= 6.5, providing 45 galaxies (with pilots) to probe dust in the epoch of reionization.  
  > "REBELS is being executed as a Cycle-7 ALMA Large Program and represents the first systematic survey of the ISM and dust properties of $z\gtrsim6.5$ galaxies." (Introduction); "While a significant fraction ($\sim40\%$) of REBELS galaxies are individually detected in the dust continuum (cite: inami2022), in this work we also include the continuum-undetected sources through a stacking analysis in order to provide a complete census of dust in UV-selected galaxies at $z\sim7$." (Stacking)
- Why this method: Stacking includes the ~60% continuum-undetected galaxies; Monte Carlo sampling propagates the ~0.3 dex stellar mass uncertainties across bins; Peak flux densities avoid upward bias in low-S/N integrated fluxes; Integrating L_IR(M_star) over the SMF overcomes the targeted (non-volume-limited) selection of REBELS.  
  > "In this work, we adopt the stacked peak flux densities, as integrated flux measurements may be biased upwards for low signal-to-noise stacks (cite: algera2020a; leslie2020)." (Stacking); "Given the complexities of accurately measuring stellar masses at $z\sim7$, we adopt a Monte Carlo approach sampling across the $M_\star$ posterior probability distributions for the REBELS targets in order to robustly incorporate the stellar mass uncertainties into our analysis." (Summary); "However, given that REBELS is a targeted survey and hence does not constitute a volume-limited sample, we follow the approach taken by (cite: khusanova2021) in order to estimate the infrared SFRD." (Summary)
- Alternative: Parametric (constant SFH) stellar masses vs. non-parametric stellar masses (adopted non-parametric for greater flexibility; tested shifting SMF by +0.45 dex)  
  > "Given the greater flexibility offered by the non-parametric modelling, we adopt the (cite: topping2022) stellar masses for the REBELS galaxies throughout this work." (Stellar Masses); "To investigate the effect of integrating our non-parametric $L_\mathrm{IR} - M_\star$ relation across a parametric stellar mass function on the obscured $z=7$ SFRD, we therefore shift the (cite: stefanon2021) SMF to higher masses by a constant factor of $0.45\,$dex to mimic the assumption a non-parametric star formation history." (Parametric vs. Non-parametric Stellar Masses)
- Alternative: Fixed stellar masses without Monte Carlo sampling ('no-MC' default stacks provided alongside MC stacks)  
  > "For a more direct comparison with the literature, where stellar mass uncertainties are generally not taken into account when stacking, we also provide “default” stacks throughout this work." (Stacking)
- Alternative: Integrated flux measurements on stacks rejected in favor of peak flux densities  
  > "In this work, we adopt the stacked peak flux densities, as integrated flux measurements may be biased upwards for low signal-to-noise stacks (cite: algera2020a; leslie2020)." (Stacking)
- Alternative: Speagle et al. (2014) main sequence parametrization tested as alternative to Schreiber et al. (2015) for flagging starbursts  
  > "We verified that adopting the main sequence parametrization from (cite: speagle2014) results in a similar set of galaxies being identified as starbursts, and hence the precise functional form of the main sequence does not affect our results (see also Figure 8 in (cite: topping2022))." (Starburst Identification)
- Alternative: Including starbursts in the stacking and SFRD derivation tested as an alternative to excluding them  
  > "As such, it is worth investigating quantitatively how incorporating starbursts into our analysis may affect the inferred SFRD." (The Effect of Excluding Starbursts)
- Alternative: Alternative SFRD parameters tested: steeper L_IR - M_star slope (gamma = 1), fixed lower mass integration limit (M_star^lower = 10^6 M_odot), and alternative dust temperatures (T_dust = 35 K and 55 K)  
  > "The magnitudes of these biases, and their effect on the fraction of the SFRD inferred to be dust-obscured, are summarized in Table [\[tab:sfrd\]](#tab:sfrd)." (Caveats)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): REBELS is a targeted, UV-selected survey and therefore biased against highly dust-obscured / optically dark galaxies, making f_obs a formal lower limit and missing ~15-20% of the obscured SFRD.  
  > "However, we must also consider that REBELS, by its very nature, is a UV-selected survey. REBELS is therefore biased against selecting highly dust-obscured galaxies, and the resulting $f_\mathrm{obs}$ can hence be regarded as a formal lower limit on the true typical obscuration of $z\sim7$ galaxies." (Obscuration across the full REBELS sample); "As such, while optically dark sources are a highly interesting population, their contribution to the obscured SFRD at $z\approx7$ might be only up to $\sim15 - 20\%$." (Comparison to the Literature)
- Limitation (authors): At low stellar masses (log10(M_star/M_odot) <= 9.5), REBELS is mostly sensitive to starbursts because of its UV-brightness selection and nearly constant continuum sensitivity.  
  > "As a result of this approximately constant sensitivity to dust-obscured star formation, REBELS is mostly sensitive to starbursts at low masses ($\log_{10} (M_\star / M_\odot) \lesssim 9.5$)." (Starburst Identification)
- Limitation (authors): Existence and functional form of the star-forming main sequence at z=7 rely on extrapolation from lower redshifts and have not been robustly established observationally.  
  > "However, we acknowledge the inherent uncertainties associated with extrapolating these main sequences to $z=7$, and therefore we discuss the effect of omitting the starburst exclusion in detail in Section [\[sec:results_sfrd_starbursts\]](#sec:results_sfrd_starbursts)." (Starburst Identification); "However, the existence of the main sequence at $z=7$ has not been robustly established observationally, although simulations predict it to already be in place (e.g., (cite: ma2018))." (The Effect of Excluding Starbursts)
- Limitation (authors): Mismatch between non-parametric stellar masses used for the L_IR - M_star relation and the parametric stellar mass function from Stefanon et al. (2021) used for integration.  
  > "In this work, we adopt the non-parametric stellar masses from (cite: topping2022), which offer greater modelling flexibility and do not require the a priori assumption of a star formation history. However, in order to determine the cosmic SFRD, we integrate across the (cite: stefanon2021) $z=7$ stellar mass function, which was constructed using parametric masses via SED modelling code (cite: kriek2009)." (Parametric vs. Non-parametric Stellar Masses)
- Limitation (authors): Single-band (rest-frame 158 um) dust continuum observations require assuming an effective dust temperature (<T_dust> = 45 +/- 4 K) and emissivity (beta = 2.0) from models to infer L_IR.  
  > "As such, the infrared luminosities and limits determined for the REBELS sample effectively assume a modified blackbody SED with a dust temperature of $\langle T_\mathrm{dust} \rangle = 45\pm4\,$K and $\beta=2.0$." (Flux Density to Infrared Luminosity Conversion); "Future multi-band ALMA observations of the REBELS sources will be crucial to better constrain their dust SEDs, and to hence provide more accurate infrared luminosities for individual sources." (Flux Density to Infrared Luminosity Conversion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating a shallow linear L_IR - M_star relation (gamma ~ 0.52) down to log10(M_star/M_odot) ~ 8.2 (or 5.0 in flat draws) below the mass range directly probed by REBELS (log10(M_star/M_odot) >= 9.4 after starburst removal) drives the large upper uncertainty (+1.18 dex) on the obscured SFRD.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying the Sommovigo et al. (2022) 158 um-to-L_IR conversion—calibrated only on the 13 [CII]+continuum detected galaxies—to stacks dominated by continuum non-detections may systematically bias L_IR if non-detections have systematically different dust temperatures or optical depths.
