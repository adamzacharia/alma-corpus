## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST NIRSpec IFU data to spectral cubes**: JWST pipeline Detector1, Spec2 and Spec3 with custom bias and bad-pixel handling; drizzle cube build with 0.1 arcsec spaxels  
  > "We used the Spec3 module to build the two 3D spectral sub-cubes (NIRSpec detectors 1 and 2) from the fully calibrated Spec2 products." (2.1 JWST NIRSpec integral field spectroscopy)
   - software: JWST Calibration pipeline
   - parameter: Pipeline version 1.9.4, CRDS context 1063  
  > "Calibration pipeline (version 1.9.4) together with the calibration context 1063 from the Calibration References Data System (CRDS)." (2.1 JWST NIRSpec integral field spectroscopy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure ALMA 12CO(1-0) and (2-1) fluxes in the apertures and r21**: Extract spectra of each region from the ALMA cubes, integrate the line profile, and compute r21 in K km/s  
  > "We extracted the spectra of the each region from the ALMA data cubes and integrated the line profile." (3.4 ALMA rotational 12CO emission); "ratio between the fluxes of the CO(2–1) and CO(1–0) transitions" (3.4 ALMA rotational 12CO emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Eight circular apertures of 0.5 arcsec diameter (~100 pc)  
  > "We extracted the spectra from eight circular apertures" (3.3 Spectra from regions with CO v=1-0 in emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flux uncertainties dominated by calibration uncertainties (10% and 20%)  
  > "The flux uncertainties are dominated by the calibration uncertainties which are 10% for CO(1–0) and 20% for CO(2–1)." (Table 4)
3. **Fit spaxel spectra to make continuum, ionized, H2 and CO maps**: Per-spaxel fit with linear local continuum and Gaussian profiles, with velocity and dispersion tied within gas phases, over 4.59-4.75 micron  
  > "For the spectral features we used Gaussian profiles." (3.1 Mid-IR continuum, ionized, and molecular gas maps); "We tied the velocity and dispersion for groups of lines tracing the same phase of the gas" (3.1 Mid-IR continuum, ionized, and molecular gas maps)
   - parameter: HI 7-5/11-6 tied to case B 6.43; [KIII] 4.62 tied to HI 7-5 with ratio 0.104  
  > "We estimated an average $[$K iii$]$4.62 $\upmu$m/H i 7–5 4.65 $\upmu$m ratio of 0.104 in this object" (3.1 Mid-IR continuum, ionized, and molecular gas maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Extract aperture spectra, subtract nuclear PSF, baseline and fit CO, 13CO, C18O, H2, HI and D-PAH**: Scaled WebbPSF nuclear contribution subtracted; spline baseline subtracted; 13CO and C18O bands modelled from P-branch fluxes assuming optically thin emission and subtracted; then 12CO band fitted together with H2, HI and D_n-PAH Gaussians  
  > "We calculated this contribution at each wavelength using point-spread functions (PSFs) created with WebbPSF (v1.1.1; (cite: Perrin2014))." (3.3 Spectra from regions with CO v=1-0 in emission); "we defined a spline-interpolated baseline." (3.3.1 Continuum baseline); "we fitted the $^{12}$CO band together with the H$_2$ and H I transitions" (3.3.5 12CO v=1-0 band)
   - software: WebbPSF
   - parameter: Spline pivot points at 4.395, 4.530, 4.586, 4.667, 4.845, 4.980 micron  
  > "The pivot points (4.395, 4.530, 4.586, 4.667, 4.845, and 4.980" (3.3.1 Continuum baseline)
   - parameter: 13CO/C18O abundance ratio ~7.4 from 13CO(2-1)/C18O(2-1) (ALMA, r=1 arcsec aperture)  
  > "abundance ratio of $\sim$7.4" (3.3.2 Optically thin 13CO and C18O v=1-0 bands)
   - assumption: 13CO and C18O v=1-0 emission optically thin; same excitation temperatures for both  
  > "share the same excitation temperatures and have abundance ratio of" (3.3.2 Optically thin 13CO and C18O v=1-0 bands)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per CO transition; two Gaussians for N1 and N2 where a broader second component appears in the residuals  
  > "Two regions (N1 and N2) show a second broader kinematic component in the residuals." (3.3.5 12CO v=1-0 band)
   - validation: Predicted 13CO R-branch fluxes from P-branch agree with observed spectra in all regions  
  > "agree with the observed spectra for all the regions including region S3" (3.3.2 Optically thin 13CO and C18O v=1-0 bands)
5. **Fit P(J+2)/R(J) ratios with a single non-LTE component and derive alpha_CO**: RADEX grid of v=0 populations, v=1 populations assumed proportional (N1,J = k N0,J), line fluxes from radiative transfer equations, fit to low-J ratios, then convert N(H2)/W(CO 1-0) to alpha_CO  
  > "First, we used RADEX (cite: vanderTak2007) to determine the CO" (4.1.3 Non-LTE models); "Then, the ratio $N({\rm H_2})$/$W$(CO(1–0)), known as $X_{\rm CO}$, is converted to $\alpha_{\rm CO}$" (5 CO-to-H2 conversion factor)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Grid: 50 log steps per parameter, 125000 models  
  > "50 log steps for each parameter (125 000 models in total)" (4.1.3 Non-LTE models)
   - assumption: [CO/H2] = 3.2e-4  
  > "We assumed a standard CO abundance of 3.2$\times$10$^{-4}$ (e.g., (cite: Bolatto2013))" (6 Discussion)
   - assumption: Mass multiplied by 1.36 for helium  
  > "to account for helium and other elements (cite: Bolatto2013)" (5 CO-to-H2 conversion factor)
   - fitting: chi2 fit to the low-J ratios; the highest J included is where chi2 does not significantly increase; 4-6 ratios fitted per region  
  > "This single component model can fit between four and six ratios." (5.1 Single component model)
   - validation: Compared model r21 and r31 with measured ALMA r21  
  > "with a mean ratio of 0.88 $\pm$ 0.15, that is in good agreement with the models." (5.1 Single component model)
6. **Fit two non-LTE components to all ratios**: chi2 over all pairs of grid models with only the solid-angle ratio free; probability exp(-chi2/2) marginalized for each parameter; alpha_CO weighted by CO(1-0) intensity of each component  
  > "In total, we explored 56 million combinations of models for each region." (5.2 Two component model); "To determine the likelihood function of the parameters, we assigned a probability to each model of" (5.2 Two component model)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: Only nH2 = 1e3, 1e4, 1e5 cm^-3 considered  
  > "To limit the number of combinations, we only considered three number densities" (5.2 Two component model)
   - fitting: Grid search chi2 with likelihood exp(-chi2/2) and marginalization; no sampler  
  > "To determine the likelihood function of the parameters, we assigned a probability to each model of" (5.2 Two component model)
   - validation: With two components all observed ratios can be fitted  
  > "With two components, it is now possible to fit all the observed ratios." (5.2 Two component model)
7. **Derive alpha_CO from the optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band**: Use 13CO (optically thin) and 12CO (optically thick) R(0) transitions to get N(12CO, v=0, J=0)/dv, extrapolate to J=1 and other levels with the single-component excitation temperatures, convert FWHM to sigma_v with extra opacity factor, then compute alpha_CO as above  
  > "we first divided $\Delta {\rm v}$ by $\sim$2.355 to account for the ratio between FWHM and $\sigma$." (5.3 Optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band); "by an extra factor between 1.2 and 2.6 (valid for" (5.3 Optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band)
   - parameter: Excitation temperature of low-J levels 15-30 K from the single-component fits  
  > "based on the excitation temperature of the low-$J$ levels, 15–30 K, of these regions" (5.3 Optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band)
   - assumption: Same excitation temperature of v=1 and v=0 levels and same solid angle for 12CO and 13CO; 12CO/13CO abundance uncertain  
  > "the excitation temperature of the $v$=1 and $v$=0 levels is the same for $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO" (5.3 Optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with single and two component results (1.1-2.1 and 1.7-3.2 times lower)  
  > "The median $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ are 1.1–2.1 times lower than those derived using the single component non-LTE model" (5.3 Optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band)

## Qualitative findings

- The 12CO v=1-0 emission in the outflow has similar morphology and kinematics to ALMA CO(2-1), so it traces the cold molecular outflow  
  > "we conclude that the rovibrational CO emission is related to the cold molecular outflow identified in the ALMA data." (3.1.2 Molecular outflow. H2 and CO emissions)
- 12CO v=1-0 emission is optically thick at low J while 13CO emission is optically thin  
  > "while the $^{13}$CO $v$=1–0 emission remains optically thin." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution ALMA CO shows the emission is not part of the northern galaxy spiral arms, so it is outflow gas  
  > "Thanks to the high angular resolution of the ALMA data, it is clear that this emission is not part of the spiral arms of the northern galaxy" (3.1.2 Molecular outflow. H2 and CO emissions)
- Why this method: alpha_CO is usually not constrained by observations and a fixed value is assumed, so the rovibrational CO band offers a way to constrain it  
  > "which in most cases is not constrained by the observations and a fixed value is assumed." (Introduction)
- Alternative: A single non-LTE component cannot reproduce both low and high J ratios, so a second component was added  
  > "A single component is not able to simultaneously reproduce the low- and high-$J$ P($J$+2)/R($J$) ratios." (5.1 Single component model)
- Alternative: Optically thin CO explanation of low alpha_CO (as in IC 5063) rejected for this outflow  
  > "However, this explanation does not work for NGC 3256-S since the CO emission from the outflow is optically thick." (6 Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: Highest J in the single-component fit is chosen where chi2 stops rising when a ratio is added  
  > "(i.e., when $\chi^2$ does not significantly increases when adding that ratio)" (5.1 Single component model)
- Limitation (authors): The CO abundance in molecular outflows is not established and is a major uncertainty for alpha_CO  
  > "The CO abundance in molecular outflows has not been firmly established yet, so this is a major uncertainty for these results." (6 Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): 13CO-based alpha_CO is more uncertain because it depends on 12CO and 13CO abundances in the outflow  
  > "are more uncertain than those derived using the single and two component methods" (5.3 Optically thin 13CO v=1-0 band)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA is ancillary; alpha_CO rests on JWST line ratios and a non-LTE model that assumes v=1 populations scale with v=0
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO is quoted scaled by the assumed CO abundance, so absolute values are tied to [CO/H2]=3.2e-4
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only eight 0.5 arcsec apertures in one galaxy; no independent check of alpha_CO (e.g. dust or [CI])
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA flux uncertainties of 10-20% were not propagated into the alpha_CO error budget as far as stated
