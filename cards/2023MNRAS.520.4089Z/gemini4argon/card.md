## Analysis recipe

1. **Perform quantum chemical calculations of 2-propanimine structure, dipole moments, vibrational frequencies, and internal rotation barriers**: Geometry optimization in Cs symmetry, harmonic/anharmonic vibrational frequency calculations, and 1D/2D potential energy surface (PES) scans using DFT (B3LYP, M062X, MN15) and ab initio MP2 methods  
  > "Molecular structure of 2-propanimine, fixed to Cs symmetry, was optimized, and harmonics vibrational frequencies were calculated based on the optimized geometry." (A3 Quantum chemical calculation); "To estimate the barrier to internal rotation of the two CH3 groups, 1-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) scan was conducted by twisting the dihedral angle of one of the C−H bond in the CH3 group" (A3 Quantum chemical calculation)
   - software: Gaussian16
   - parameter: DFT methods B3LYP, M062X, MN15 with 6-311++G(3df,3pd) basis set; MP2 with aug-cc-pVTZ basis set; 2D PES with B3LYP/6-311+g(2d,2p) in 2° steps from 0° to 120°  
  > "Several density functional theory (DFT) methods, B3LYP (Lee et al. 1988), M062X (Zhao & Truhlar 2008), MN15 (Yu et al. 2016), were used with the 6-311++G(3df,3pd) basis set, which include polarization and diffuse functions." (A3 Quantum chemical calculation); "Second order Møller–Plesset perturbation (MP2) calculation was used with aug-cc-pVTZ basis set." (A3 Quantum chemical calculation); "The PES scan was performed from 0° to 120° with a step of 2°, and extrapolated to 360° based on the symmetry of the PES. The basis set is reduced to 6-311+g(2d,2p) to speed up the calculation." (B2 Molecular Structure)
   - assumption: Cs molecular symmetry for equilibrium geometry optimization (relaxed during PES scans)  
  > "Molecular structure of 2-propanimine, fixed to Cs symmetry, was optimized, and harmonics vibrational frequencies were calculated based on the optimized geometry." (A3 Quantum chemical calculation); "During the PES scans, the molecular symmetry was relaxed to allow re-optimization of the molecular structure at each scanned point." (A3 Quantum chemical calculation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 1D N-H bond twist PES scan and 2D CH3 internal rotation PES scan confirmed Cs global minimum and absence of other conformers; experimental absence of c-type transitions confirmed µc = 0  
  > "Supplementary 2-dimentional PES scan (see Appendix B2) also excludes other local minimum structure than the global equilibrium structure." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "We also show evidence that the normal c- type transitions, which correspond to a non-zero µc dipole moment component, do not exist in the experimental spectra." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
2. **Synthesize 2-propanimine, measure laboratory millimeter-wave spectrum, and fit spectroscopic constants and internal rotation parameters**: Synthesize sample from KOH and 2-amino-2-methylpropanenitrile, record absorption spectrum from 50 to 500 GHz, remove standing-wave baseline via Fourier transform, fit line peaks with second-derivative Voigt profiles, and iteratively assign and fit torsional-rotational transitions using Watson's A reduction Hamiltonian in Ir representation  
  > "The 2-propanimine was synthesized from the reaction of KOH and precursor 2-amino-2-methylpropanenitrile." (2 EXPERIMENTAL METHODS); "After baseline removal, peaks were identified and fitted with a second-derivative Voigt line profile to obtain their peak frequencies." (A4 Spectral analysis); "The spectral fit was performed using both the XIAM (Hartwig & Dreizler 1996; Herbers & Nguyen 2020) and ERHAM (Groner 1997, 2012) programs with the standard Watson’s A reduction Hamiltonian under the Ir representation." (A4 Spectral analysis)
   - software: custom spectral assignment software, XIAM, ERHAM, CALPGM/SPFIT, BARRIER, GNU Fortran 11.1.0
   - parameter: Frequency ranges: 50–110 GHz, 150–330 GHz, 360–500 GHz; cell pressure 8–20 µBar at −60 °C; frequency uncertainties 25–200 kHz; 9,349 line frequencies (27,259 transitions) fitted with 44 parameters in ERHAM  
  > "Throughout the measurement, the sample was submerged in an ethanol cold bath at −60 °C, and a minimum flow of the sample vapor was maintained between 8–20 µBar." (2 EXPERIMENTAL METHODS); "We associated various uncertainties, ranging from 25 kHz to 200 kHz, to each fitted peak according to the frequency resolution at each frequency band" (A4 Spectral analysis); "A fit residual of 53.5 kHz and dimensionless standard error of 0.69 were obtained by ERHAM using a total of 44 parameters" (5 CONCLUSIONS)
   - assumption: Partially resolved 14N quadrupole hyperfine components (~1% of lines) are replaced by their intensity-weighted average frequencies; two methyl tops treated as independent in BARRIER calculation  
  > "For each set of hyperfine components, we calculated the weighted averaged line frequency using the theoretical hyperfine intensity ratio as the weighting factor." (A4 Spectral analysis); "In this case, the two methyl tops are almost independent (for the ground state), and we can approximately use the “BARRIER” program to calculate their V3 barriers individually." (A4 Spectral analysis)
   - fitting: Weighted least-squares fit in XIAM and ERHAM with weights proportional to relative line intensities for blended transitions  
  > "Weight used in fitting blended transitions. Weight is proportional to the line intensity and normalized to unity for all blended transitions associated with a single frequency." (Table 1)
   - validation: Evaluated microwave rms (78.0 kHz for XIAM, 53.5 kHz for ERHAM), dimensionless standard deviation (1.38 for XIAM, 0.69 for ERHAM), and fraction of transitions within 3σ (99.91% for ERHAM)  
  > "The XIAM fit produces a microwave root-mean-square (r.m.s.) of 78.0 kHz and a dimensionless standard deviation of 1.38. The ERHAM fit produces a lower microwave r.m.s. of 53.5 kHz and a dimensionless standard deviation of 0.69." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "In the ERHAM fit, 99.91 % of the fitted transitions" (B4 Fit statistics)
3. **Generate rotational line catalog and partition functions for astronomical searches**: Predict transition frequencies and intensities up to J'' = 80 and 1 THz from the best-fit ERHAM parameters and MP2 dipole moments, and compute the torsional-rotational partition function by direct summation of Boltzmann factors up to J'' = 120  
  > "Using the ERHAM fit, we generated a prediction of the transition frequencies of 2-propanimine up to J′′ = 80 and 1 THz. We used the dipole moments from the MP2 calculation to calculate the line strength." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "The torsional-rotational partition function Q(T) was calculated by direct summation of Boltzmann factors up to J′′ = 120 (maximum J allowed in ERHAM) and listed in Table 5." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
   - software: ERHAM, CALPGM/SPCAT
   - parameter: J'' up to 80 and frequency up to 1 THz for catalog; J'' up to 120 for partition function; µa = 1.3711 D, µb = −2.0643 D; reduced spin weight 2:2:2:1:1 (total 8) vs full spin weight 16:16:16:8:8 (total 64)  
  > "For this practical reason, we used a reduced spin weight of 2:2:2:1:1 for the five torsional states. The total spin weight reduces from 64 to 8, and therefore the partition function also reduces to 1/8 of the full spin weight case." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "µa (1) Debye 1.3216 1.3142 1.2797 1.3711" (Table 3); "µb (1) Debye −2.1122 −2.1066 −2.1242 −2.0643" (Table 3)
   - assumption: Vibrational partition function estimated from harmonic/anharmonic vibrational energies in Table B5; no conformational correction needed (Fconf = 1.00)  
  > "Furthermore, vibrational partition function can be estimated using the vibrational energies listed in Table B5, and combined with the torsional-rotational partition function to provide more accurate line intensity estimation under elevated temperature." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "2-propanimine does not have conformers, which means that no conformational correction to the column density is needed." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Search for 2-propanimine and derive column density upper limit toward Sgr B2(N1S) using ALMA ReMoCA data**: Compute LTE synthetic spectra with Weeds using the ERHAM spectroscopic predictions and LTE parameters adopted from methanimine, compare with the observed ReMoCA spectrum and complete multi-molecule model toward Sgr B2(N1S), and derive a column density upper limit  
  > "The observed spectrum was compared to synthetic spectra computed under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with the astronomical software Weeds (Maret et al. 2011)." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1)); "In order to search for 2-propanimine, CH3C(NH)CH3, toward Sgr B2(N1S), we relied on the LTE parameters that we previously derived for methanimine, CH2NH, toward this position with the ReMoCA survey (Margulès et al. 2022)." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: θs = 2.0'', Trot = 230 K, ∆V = 5.0 km s−1, Voff = 0.0 km s−1 (Vsys = 62 km s−1), Fvib = 3.33, Fconf = 1.00  
  > "Each species was modeled with a set of five parameters: size of the emitting region (θs), column density (N), temperature (Trot), linewidth (∆V), and velocity offset (Voff) with respect to the assumed systemic velocity of the source, Vsys = 62 km s−1." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1)); "CH3C(NH)CH3 n 0 2.0 230 < 5.0 (16) 3.33 1.00 5.0 0.0 > 18" (Table 6)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) justified by high density (> 1×10^7 cm^-3); resolved emission size fixed to 2''; identical LTE parameters (θs, Trot, ∆V, Voff) to methanimine  
  > "This assumption is justified by the high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (> 1×107 cm−3, see Bonfand et al. 2019)." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1)); "As explained in Belloche et al. (2019), the molecular emission toward Sgr B2(N1S) is resolved and we fixed its size to 2′′ for the LTE modeling." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
   - fitting: Manual adjustment of column density by hand until synthetic line peak temperatures match or bound observed spectrum  
  > "We derived by hand a best-fit synthetic spectrum for each molecule separately, and then added together the contributions of all identified molecules." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1)); "In the end, for a given molecule, the only really free parameter is the column density, which we adjust until the peak temperatures of the synthetic lines match the detected ones." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
   - validation: Checked line blending against the complete multi-molecule synthetic spectrum of Sgr B2(N1S) and 3σ noise threshold  
  > "However, all other transitions of 2-propanimine that are expected to be stronger than 3σ are heavily blended with emission from other molecules and cannot be identified." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
5. **Search for 2-propanimine and derive column density upper limit toward IRAS 16293B using ALMA PILS data**: Compute LTE synthetic spectra assuming optically thin emission using custom PILS routines and compare predicted bright transitions against line-free regions of the observed ALMA Band 7 spectrum down to the RMS noise level  
  > "To perform the search, we calculated synthetic spectra for 2propanimine assuming optically thin emission and LTE using custom routines" (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422); "The upper limit to column density can be derived by comparing to the parts of the spectra where no lines are seen down to the RMS noise level of the data (4–5 mJy beam−1 km s−1)" (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422)
   - software: custom PILS LTE routines
   - parameter: Trot = 100 K, RMS noise level 4–5 mJy beam−1 km s−1, key frequency windows around 341.96, 346.86, and 361.34 GHz  
  > "We adopted a temperature of 100 K and systemic velocities consistent with other species detected in PILS." (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422); "where no lines are seen down to the RMS noise level of the data (4–5 mJy beam−1 km s−1) but where 2-propanimine is predicted to show emission, e.g., around 341.96, 346.86 and 361.34 GHz." (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and LTE at excitation temperature of 100 K  
  > "To perform the search, we calculated synthetic spectra for 2propanimine assuming optically thin emission and LTE using custom routines" (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422); "We adopted a temperature of 100 K and systemic velocities consistent with other species detected in PILS." (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422)
   - fitting: Comparison of synthetic spectrum with RMS noise level in line-free regions  
  > "The upper limit to column density can be derived by comparing to the parts of the spectra where no lines are seen down to the RMS noise level of the data (4–5 mJy beam−1 km s−1)" (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422)

## Software

- **Gaussian16** Revision C.01: Quantum chemical calculations of molecular structure, dipole moments, vibrational frequencies, and internal rotation barriers  
  > "Quantum chemical calculation using Gaussian16 (Frisch et al. 2016) was also performed to guide the spectral analysis" (2 EXPERIMENTAL METHODS); "Frisch M. J., et al., 2016, Gaussian 16 Revision C.01" (REFERENCES)
- **XIAM (XIAM_mod)** modified (DIMLIN=99999): Predicting and fitting the rotational-torsional spectrum and internal rotation barriers of 2-propanimine  
  > "The experimental spectrum was processed using a custom spectral assignment software, and fitted using XIAM (Hartwig & Dreizler 1996; Herbers & Nguyen 2020) and ERHAM (Groner 1997, 2012) programs" (2 EXPERIMENTAL METHODS); "For XIAM, we obtained the source code of the official “XIAM_mod” program 10, and modified the “DIMLIN” parameter, which controls the limit of input lines, to 99999." (A4 Spectral analysis)
- **ERHAM** erham_r3a.exe / erhamz_R3a.exe: Fitting the rotational-torsional spectrum of 2-propanimine, generating the line frequency catalog up to 1 THz, and calculating the partition function  
  > "Using the ERHAM fit, we generated a prediction of the transition frequencies of 2-propanimine up to J′′ = 80 and 1 THz." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "the modified ERHAM code, named erham_r3a.exe, can be also found in Kisiel’s website 11." (A4 Spectral analysis)
- **CALPGM/SPFIT and SPCAT** : Fitting averaged center frequencies fc to an asymmetric rotor model (SPFIT) and formatting the predicted line catalog (SPCAT)  
  > "We generated a line list from fc and fitted it to a simple Watson’s A reduction Hamiltonian in Ir representation using the CALPGM/SPFIT program (Pickett 1991)." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "Prediction uses dipole moment values from the MP2/aug-cc-pVTZ calculation, and the table follows the CALPGM/SPCAT format." (Table 4)
- **BARRIER** : Deriving V3 internal rotation barriers from torsional energy differences in the ERHAM fit  
  > "Approximate values derived from program “BARRIER” using torsional energy differences." (Table 2); "In this case, the two methyl tops are almost independent (for the ground state), and we can approximately use the “BARRIER” program to calculate their V3 barriers individually." (A4 Spectral analysis)
- **GNU Fortran** 11.1.0: Compiling the modified XIAM source code

## Qualitative findings

- The equilibrium geometry of 2-propanimine has Cs symmetry with no other local minimum conformers, and has zero c-axis dipole moment (µc = 0), showing that previously published dipole moments by Sil et al. (2018) are erroneous.  
  > "Supplementary 2-dimentional PES scan (see Appendix B2) also excludes other local minimum structure than the global equilibrium structure." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "We note that the dipole moment values reported by Sil et al. (2018) were erroneously taken from the initial structure of the molecule before geometry optimization, and therefore should not be used." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
- 2-propanimine is not detected toward Sgr B2(N1S) or IRAS 16293B; in Sgr B2(N1S), the upper limit is a factor of three more stringent than that previously obtained for 1-propanimine.  
  > "We report the nondetection of 2-propanimine toward the offset position Sgr B2(N1S) of the hot molecular core Sgr B(N1) and toward the protostar IRAS 16293B." (5 CONCLUSIONS); "The upper limit is a factor three more stringent than the one obtained previously for 1-propanimine." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))

## Reasoning

- Why this data: A pure synthesized sample avoids contamination from pyrolysis/flame side products; the 50–110 GHz laboratory band accesses high-J and high-Ka Q-branch transitions; Sgr B2(N1S) and IRAS 16293B offset positions have lower continuum opacity and narrower lines.  
  > "The pure sample, which is free from the inevitable contamination of side products in pyrolysis and flame reactions, allows us to obtain high quality rotational spectrum of 2-propanimine between 50 and 500 GHz." (1 INTRODUCTION); "This position is offset by about 1′′ to the south of the main hot core Sgr B2(N1) and has a lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1)); "We focus on a position offset by one beam (0.5′′ or 70 au) from the B component where the lines are narrow (≈ 1 km s−1) and absorption due to optical thickness (of continuum and line) is less" (4.2 Search toward IRAS 16293-2422)
- Why this method: Used both XIAM (which directly fits V3 barriers) and ERHAM (which includes more higher-order tunneling terms to reach experimental accuracy); used LTE modeling justified by high gas densities (> 10^7 cm−3) in Sgr B2(N).  
  > "whereas the XIAM model cannot reach experimental accuracy because of the limited number of high order correction terms available in the model." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION); "This assumption is justified by the high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (> 1×107 cm−3, see Bonfand et al. 2019)." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
- Alternative: Rejected dipole moment values from Sil et al. (2018) because they were erroneously taken from the unoptimized initial geometry and violated Cs symmetry.  
  > "We note that the dipole moment values reported by Sil et al. (2018) were erroneously taken from the initial structure of the molecule before geometry optimization, and therefore should not be used." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
- Alternative: Chose not to include 14N nuclear quadrupole hyperfine coupling directly in the spectroscopic Hamiltonian, instead fitting intensity-weighted average frequencies because hyperfine splitting is only partially resolved in ~1% of the lines.  
  > "Therefore, instead of including the hyperfine effect into the spectroscopy model, we chose to treat these partially blended hyperfine lines separately before the final spectral fit." (A4 Spectral analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): XIAM cannot reach experimental accuracy at high J and Ka because of the limited number of high-order correction terms available in the model.  
  > "whereas the XIAM model cannot reach experimental accuracy because of the limited number of high order correction terms available in the model." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
- Limitation (authors): Predicted frequencies for transitions not accessed experimentally should be viewed with caution because high-order terms in the ERHAM fit may be correlated and fluctuate when extrapolated.  
  > "predicted frequencies of transitions not accessed by this experiment should still be viewed with caution because the high order terms in the ERHAM fit may be highly correlated and subject to large fluctuation if more spectral data were accessible." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
- Limitation (authors): Two low-lying torsional excited states (around 120 and 183 cm−1) were observed in the laboratory spectrum but could not be analyzed due to Coriolis coupling requiring a global analysis.  
  > "The analysis of these excited states, however, is complicated due to their Coriolis coupling. To correctly model the lines from these excited states, it is necessary to perform a global analysis of them together with the ground state lines." (3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION)
- Limitation (authors): Without higher-resolution microwave-cavity spectra, the millimeter-wave data alone cannot fully determine the coupling between the 14N nuclear spin and the internal rotors.  
  > "Without the access to microwave-cavity spectrum at higher resolution, our millimeter-wave data alone were not sufficient to fully determine this interaction." (A4 Spectral analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Severe spectral line blending in Sgr B2(N1S) prevents identification of almost all 2-propanimine transitions stronger than 3σ.  
  > "However, all other transitions of 2-propanimine that are expected to be stronger than 3σ are heavily blended with emission from other molecules and cannot be identified." (4.1 Search toward Sgr B2(N1))
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Noticeable discrepancy in the Conclusion text where XIAM internal rotation barriers are quoted as 533.764(63) cm^-1 and 466.657(26) cm^-1, whereas Abstract, Section 3, Table 2, and Table 3 consistently report 531.956(64) cm^-1 and 465.013(26) cm^-1.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limit in Sgr B2(N1S) relies heavily on the completeness and accuracy of the background LTE model of all other interfering species.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LTE parameters for 2-propanimine in Sgr B2(N1S) (especially T_rot = 230 K) are assumed to be identical to methanimine without direct constraint.
