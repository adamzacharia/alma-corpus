- Question: What is the relation between nuclear 100 GHz millimeter continuum emission and X-ray emission in nearby hard X-ray selected radio-quiet AGN at <100 mas resolution, and can 100 GHz emission be used to probe AGN accretion and column density?  
  > "With the goal of probing smaller scales in a homogeneous way, we study here the relation between X-ray and 100 GHz emission using the results obtained by a dedicated very high-resolution ($<100$ mas) ALMA campaign of a volume limited ($D<50$ Mpc) sample" (Introduction); "With the goal of constraining the relation between mm and X-ray continuum emission in AGN, we have studied here a sample of 26 hard X-ray selected, radio-quiet AGN at distances $<50$ Mpc with ALMA at $<100$ milliarcsecond resolution" (Summary and conclusions)

## Qualitative findings

- Nuclear 100 GHz continuum emission is detected in 25/26 sources and is dominated by an unresolved nuclear component, making it nearly ubiquitous in radio-quiet accreting SMBHs.  
  > "The 100 GHz emission in all other objects is dominated by an unresolved nuclear component." (ALMA data reduction); "Our sensitive 100 GHz ALMA observations detect a very high fraction (25/26 or $94^{+3}_{-6}\%$) of the radio-quiet AGN of our sample, showing that an unresolved core at mm-wavelengths is almost ubiquitous in accreting SMBHs." (The relation between X-ray and 100 GHz emission)
- The ratio of 100 GHz to X-ray emission shows no statistically significant correlation with column density, Eddington ratio, black hole mass, or host galaxy star formation rate.  
  > "The ratio between the 100 GHz continuum and the X-ray emission does not show any correlation with column density, black hole mass, Eddington ratio or star formation rate" (Abstract); "In all cases we found no statistically significant trend in the X-ray–to–100 GHz ratio." (The relation between X-ray and 100 GHz emission)
- Two galaxies (NGC 3281 and NGC 4941) show dual nuclear 100 GHz sources separated by ~0.3 arcsec with nearly equal fluxes.  
  > "In two cases (NGC 3281 and NGC 4941) ALMA detected two nuclear sources (per galaxy), separated by $\sim 0.3\ensuremath{''}$. In both cases, the fluxes of the two nuclear sources were almost identical, with flux ratios of $1.07$ and $1.11$ for NGC 4941 and NGC 3281, respectively." (ALMA data reduction)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (<100 mas) 100 GHz ALMA observations of a volume-limited (<50 Mpc) hard X-ray selected sample probe small physical scales (1.5-23 pc) homogeneously, avoiding host contamination seen at lower resolution or 230 GHz, while 100 GHz is near the expected self-absorbed synchrotron peak of the corona.  
  > "The size ($R$) of a self-absorbed synchrotron source decreases with the frequency following $R\propto \nu^{-7/4}$, implying that the synchrotron emission from an X-ray corona sized source would peak at $\sim 100$ GHz." (Introduction); "With the goal of probing smaller scales in a homogeneous way, we study here the relation between X-ray and 100 GHz emission using the results obtained by a dedicated very high-resolution ($<100$ mas) ALMA campaign of a volume limited ($D<50$ Mpc) sample" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5 was chosen to retain almost the highest spatial resolution with only a small loss in sensitivity compared to robust = 2.0.  
  > "Briggs weighting with robust parameter equals to 0.5 is used for the imaging of all the sources, with a little loss in sensitivity from robust = 2.0, which yields the possible lowest noise level, allowing us to retain almost the highest resolution." (ALMA data reduction)
- Alternative: Thermal dust emission as the origin of the nuclear mm continuum is considered unlikely based on flat spectral slopes found by Kawamuro et al. (2022).  
  > "While the recent work of (cite: Kawamuro:2022eg) (focused on 230 GHz emission) showed that a dust origin for the mm emission appears unlikely, one cannot rule out that it is associated with shocks produced by outflows or to free-free emission" (Summary and conclusions)
- Alternative: Outflow-driven shocks as the origin of the 100 GHz emission are argued against due to the lack of correlation between the mm-to-X-ray ratio and Eddington ratio.  
  > "As discussed in (cite: Kawamuro:2022eg), if the 100 GHz emission was due to outflow-driven shocks, then one might expect an increase in $L_{100\rm\,GHz}/L_{14-150\rm\,keV}$ with increasing $\lambda_{\rm Edd}$, which is not observed here (top right panel of Fig. [2](#fig:Ratios))." (The relation between X-ray and 100 GHz emission)
- Alternative: Using both or the fainter nuclear source in the dual systems NGC 3281 and NGC 4941 was considered and found not to significantly change the results.  
  > "For the present analysis, we used only the brighter source in each of these two systems, but we stress that this choice does not significantly change our results." (ALMA data reduction)
- Alternative: Variability as the cause of the ALMA non-detection in MCG-05-14-012 was tested and ruled out using a NuSTAR observation taken ~3 weeks prior.  
  > "We checked whether the ALMA non-detection could be ascribed to variability, and found that a *NuSTAR* observation carried out $\sim3$ weeks before the ALMA observation (ID 60160243002) points towards the same 14–150 keV flux reported by (cite: Ricci:2017if)" (ALMA data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 100 GHz ALMA and X-ray observations are not simultaneous, so AGN variability (~0.2 dex in X-rays) may contribute to the observed scatter.  
  > "It should be noticed that, at least part of the scatter could be due to variability, since the 100 GHz and X-ray observations are not simultaneous." (The relation between X-ray and 100 GHz emission)
- Limitation (authors): At high column densities (log N_H >= 24.5), the observed 2-10 keV to 100 GHz flux ratio flattens and depends strongly on the torus covering factor and Thomson scattered fraction.  
  > "It should be stressed that the behaviour of the curve for high column densities (and in particular its flattening) is significantly influenced by the choice of covering factor and scattered fraction values." (Estimating column densities from the X-ray/100 GHz relation)
- Limitation (authors): The exact physical mechanism producing the mm continuum remains uncertain; outflow shocks or free-free emission in the inner AGN regions cannot be completely ruled out.  
  > "However, it is still unclear what physical mechanisms produces the mm-continuum in AGN." (Summary and conclusions); "one cannot rule out that it is associated with shocks produced by outflows or to free-free emission in the inner regions of the AGN." (Summary and conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainties on individual 100 GHz flux measurements are not listed in Table 1, and it is not specified whether measurement errors or intrinsic scatter weights were included in the ordinary least-squares fits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The treatment of the single non-detection (MCG-05-14-012) in the linear regression is not explicitly stated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 2-10 keV intrinsic luminosities and fluxes are derived from 14-150 keV values using a fixed photon index (Gamma = 1.8) rather than individual source photon indices, making the 2-10 keV fit a deterministic rescaling of the 14-150 keV fit.
