## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure 0.87 mm continuum size, flux, column density, and disk mass of MMS-1**: Deconvolve MMS-1 in the 0.87 mm continuum image plane using the synthesized beam PSF, measure peak and total flux, and convert to peak H2 column density and total gas+dust mass.  
  > "We deconvolved the source in the image plane by using the point spread function (PSF) of the synthesized beam." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "The peak flux and the total flux are 2.8 mJy beam$^{-1}$ and 3.6 mJy, respectively, corresponding to the peak H$_2$ column density and the total mass of 3.1 $\times$ 10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$ and 8.3 $\times$ 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$" (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission, uniform dust temperature of 100 K, dust opacity kappa_345GHz = 1.2 x 10^-2 cm^2 g^-1 per unit (gas + dust) mass, and gas-to-dust ratio of 100  
  > "with the assumptions of the optically thin emission, the uniform dust temperature of 100 K, and the dust opacity per unit (gas + dust) mass column density, $\kappa_{345 GHz}$ = 1.2 $\times$ 10$^{-2}$ cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ ((cite: Hildebrand83; Oss94; Kauffmann08); Paper I)" (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "which means that gas-to-dust ratio is assumed to be 100." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - fitting: Image-plane deconvolution using the synthesized beam PSF  
  > "We deconvolved the source in the image plane by using the point spread function (PSF) of the synthesized beam." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure disk position angle and velocity structure from 12CO (J=3-2) channel maps**: Fit 2D Gaussians to the 12CO (J=3-2) emission peaks in each velocity channel to determine the disk position angle and projected peak offsets as a function of relative velocity.  
  > "A position angle of the line passing through the emission peaks in each velocity channel is $-$17$^\circ$, which is measured counterclockwise relative to the north celestial pole." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "(1) The peak positions of the $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) in each channel (Figure 2) with both the ALMA observations and the synthetic observations of Keplerian models are derived by 2D Gaussian fitting." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: V_sys = 6.75 km s^-1, PA = -17 deg  
  > "We adopted the $V_{\rm sys}$ = 6.75 km s$^{-1}$ because the high-velocity components are symmetric around this velocity (see Figure 1 (b) for the mirrored spectrum)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "and the position angle = $-$17$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ as described in Sec. [subsec:MMS-1_obs]." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting to emission peaks in each velocity channel  
  > "(1) The peak positions of the $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) in each channel (Figure 2) with both the ALMA observations and the synthetic observations of Keplerian models are derived by 2D Gaussian fitting." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - validation: Position angle is consistent with being perpendicular to the Spitzer bipolar nebula; systemic velocity is consistent with single-dish core systemic velocity and mirrored high-velocity spectrum symmetry  
  > "The angle is nearly perpendicular to that of the bipolar nebula seen in the $Spitzer$ observations (cite: Bourke06; Terebey09)." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "This velocity is also consistent with the systemic velocity of the dense core observed with the single-dish observations (cite: Onishi99)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
3. **Model Keplerian disk and central protostellar mass via LTE radiative transfer and CASA synthetic observations**: Calculate LTE radiative transfer emission for a Keplerian disk model, generate synthetic ALMA C40-6 observations with CASA simobserve and simanalyze, and iteratively tune M_*, R_disk, T_1au, and M_disk in three steps to match high-velocity channel peak offsets, 12CO line intensity, and 0.87 mm continuum flux.  
  > "Expected line emissions along lines of sight were calculated with radiative transfer equations, assuming the local thermal equilibrium (cite: Ohashi14), and then we simulated the ALMA observations in $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) and 0.87 mm continuum with the C40-6 configuration" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "We initially assumed $M_{\rm disk}$ of 8 $\times$ 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$ and $T_{\rm 1au}$ of 200 K for the simulation, and iterate the simulation after steps (2) and (3)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "(2) We then fitted the $T_{\rm 1au}$ to reproduce the intensity of the spectrum at high-velocity parts. $M_{\rm disk}$ does not affect the intensity, because the disk is optically thick in $^{12}$CO." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Fixed: V_sys = 6.75 km s^-1, i = 70 deg, PA = -17 deg; Tuned: M_*, R_disk, T_1au, M_disk  
  > "We adopted the $V_{\rm sys}$ = 6.75 km s$^{-1}$ because the high-velocity components are symmetric around this velocity (see Figure 1 (b) for the mirrored spectrum)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "We also adopted the inclination angle = 70$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ derived from the scattered light seen in the $Spitzer$ (cite: Terebey09), and the position angle = $-$17$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ as described in Sec. [subsec:MMS-1_obs]." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "Thus, the remaining free parameters are the $M_{*}$, $R_{\rm disk}$, $M_{\rm disk}$, and $T_{\rm 1au}$." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation, temperature profile proportional to r^-0.5, density profile proportional to r^-1, local thermal equilibrium (LTE), symmetric gas distribution  
  > "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach, to estimate the mass of the protostar and the disk properties." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "The temperature and density profiles of the simulated models are assumed to be $\propto$ $r^{-0.5}$ and $\propto$ $r^{-1}$." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "Our simulation also assumed the symmetric gas distribution of the disk." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - fitting: Iterative three-step manual parameter tuning against 2D Gaussian channel peak positions (step 1), high-velocity 12CO spectrum intensity (step 2), and 0.87 mm continuum flux (step 3)  
  > "We derived the optimum values with the following three steps." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "(3) The remaining parameter, $M_{\rm disk}$, was calculated to reproduce the observed 0.87 mm continuum flux by assuming the temperature distribution derived in step (2)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - validation: Visual comparison of synthetic and observed channel maps, P-V diagram, high-velocity spectrum, and radial-velocity plots for M_* = 0.13, 0.18, and 0.23 M_odot  
  > "Our observation and model agree reasonably well (see also the channel maps in Figure 2 and the P-V diagram in Figure 4)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "The inner slopes of models with the $M_{*}$ = 0.13, 0.23 $M_{\odot}$, which have the same other parameters as shown above, are also plotted in Figure 3 for the comparison." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
4. **Model pre-main-sequence stellar luminosity evolution with cold accretion**: Compute stellar luminosity evolution using the Hosokawa numerical stellar evolution code under cold accretion until the star reaches 0.2 M_odot, then halt accretion and track post-accretion luminosity evolution.  
  > "We calculated the evolution of the stellar luminosity with the cold accretion by using the numerical code described by (cite: Hosokawa09) and (cite: Hosokawa10; Hosokawa11)." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion); "We used a fixed accretion rate of 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ until the star has reached the mass of 0.2 $M_{\odot}$, and then we stopped the accretion and tracked the evolution of pre-main-sequence star." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
   - software: Hosokawa stellar evolution code (cite: Hosokawa09; Hosokawa10; Hosokawa11)
   - parameter: Fixed accretion rate = 10^-5 M_odot yr^-1 up to M_* = 0.2 M_odot, initial stellar mass = 0.01 M_odot, initial stellar radius = 0.65 R_odot  
  > "We used a fixed accretion rate of 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ until the star has reached the mass of 0.2 $M_{\odot}$, and then we stopped the accretion and tracked the evolution of pre-main-sequence star." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion); "Our calculation starting with a 0.01 $M_{\odot}$ star with the initial radius of 0.65 $R_{\odot}$ reproduces the observed luminosity ($\sim$0.07 $L_{\odot}$) in $<$10$^5$ years after we stop the mass accretion." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
   - assumption: Cold accretion (accreting gas provides very low entropy to the protostar)  
  > "The stellar luminosity can be very low even just after the accretion ceases when the accreting gas provides very low entropy (cold accretion; (cite: Hartmann97; Hosokawa11; Baraffe12; Vorobyov17; Kunitomo17) and references therein)." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Blueshifted and redshifted 12CO (J=3-2) emission is clearly detected toward MMS-1 at ~20 au resolution, revealing a compact rotating disk perpendicular to the Spitzer bipolar nebula.  
  > "We have clearly detected the blueshifted and redshifted emission of the $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) at MMS-1 (Figure 1 (a,b)) for the first time at the present high-angular resolution." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "These gas/dust distributions strongly indicate that the rotating disk-like envelope exists around the very low-luminosity protostar, and the disk orientation is consistent with the direction of the outflow and the bipolar nebula (Paper I)." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
- High-density tracers H13CO+ and C17O (J=3-2) are not detected toward MMS-1, and there is no extended high-density envelope connecting to the disk, indicating the disk is detached from the surrounding dense core.  
  > "It is to be noted that we cannot detect high-density tracers, H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ ($J$ = 3–2) and C$^{17}$O ($J$ = 3–2), toward MMS-1 even with the higher angular resolution compared with Paper II." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "These results indicate that the disk may be detached from the surrounding environment although it is located at the center of the dense core whose density is $\sim$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
- High-temperature (~50 K) 12CO (J=3-2) gas is detected several hundred au away from MMS-1, indicating shock heating from turbulent gas interactions.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous Cycle 0/1 ALMA observations (0.74'' x 0.34'') did not spatially resolve MMS-1 or detect associated gas, requiring higher spatial resolution (~0.18'' x 0.1'') in Band 7 continuum and 12CO (J=3-2) to resolve the disk and determine the evolutionary status of the VeLLO.  
  > "A mm-dust source (MMS-1), which is associated with the protostar, has not been spatially resolved even at the resolution of $\sim$0.74$''$ $\times$ 0.34$''$ and confirmed no gas association" (Introduction); "In particular, the evolutionally status of the very low-luminosity protostar is still unclear, possibly due to insufficient spatial resolutions." - Why this method: Because the ALMA beam (~20 au) only marginally resolves the ~10 au disk, forward modeling with LTE radiative transfer and CASA synthetic observations (simobserve/simanalyze) of a Keplerian disk is required to remove beam dilution effects.  
  > "However, since the present ALMA observations do not have a sufficient resolution to spatially resolve the disk structure, the comparisons with the simulation of rotating disks are needed to remove the effect of the beam dilution." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach, to estimate the mass of the protostar and the disk properties."
