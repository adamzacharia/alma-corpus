2. **Model disk kinematics and constrain central stellar mass and disk properties**: Synthetic observation of Keplerian rotating disk models using radiative transfer under LTE; iterative three-step fitting matching channel peak positions vs. projected distance, high-velocity spectral intensity, and 0.87 mm continuum flux  
  > "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach, to estimate the mass of the protostar and the disk properties. The temperature and density profiles of the simulated models are assumed to be ∝ r^-0.5 and ∝ r^-1." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "We derived the optimum values with the following three steps. (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "(3) The remaining parameter, Mdisk, was calculated to reproduce the observed 0.87 mm continuum flux by assuming the temperature distribution derived in step (2)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)

  > "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach, to estimate the mass of the protostar and the disk properties. ## Qualitative findings

- 12CO (3-2) emission shows rotating gas kinematics consistent with a Keplerian disk detached from the surrounding dense envelope.  
  > "These gas/dust distributions strongly indicate that the rotating disk-like envelope exists around the very low-luminosity protostar, and the disk orientation is consistent with the direction of the outflow and the bipolar nebula (Paper I)." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "These results indicate that the disk may be detached from the surrounding environment although it is located at the center of the dense core whose density is ~10^6 cm-3." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
- High-density tracers H13CO+ (4-3) and C17O (3-2) were not detected toward MMS-1.  
  > "It is to be noted that we cannot detect high-density tracers, H13CO+ (J = 3–2) and C17O (J = 3–2), toward MMS-1 even with the higher angular resolution compared with Paper II." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-angular resolution (~20 au) ALMA Band 7 observations were required to overcome beam dilution and probe the compact kinematics of the embedded protostellar disk in MC27/L1521F  
  > "In particular, the evolutionally status of the very low-luminosity protostar is still unclear, possibly due to insufficient spatial resolutions." (Introduction); "However, since the present ALMA observations do not have a sufficient resolution to spatially resolve the disk structure, the comparisons with the simulation of rotating disks are needed to remove the effect of the beam dilution." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Why this method: Keplerian synthetic disk modeling with radiative transfer LTE calculation was used as the simplest approach to disentangle beam dilution and determine central stellar mass and disk size/temperature/mass  
  > "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach, to estimate the mass of the protostar and the disk properties." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Alternative: Inside-out collapse model with high accretion rate (1 x 10^-5 M_sun/yr): rejected because the expansion wave could not have reached the 3000 au density slope break within the required shorter timescale  
  > "If we assume the higher accretion rate to be 1 × 10-5 M⊙ yr-1 (cite: Larson69; Hunter77), the time scale becomes much shorter to achieve the present observed stellar mass, and the expansion wave cannot reach the slope breaking radius of 3000 au." (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
- Alternative: Standard pre-main-sequence evolutionary tracks: rejected because reaching <0.1 L_sun takes >1 Myr, inconsistent with the dense embedded core age  
  > "According to the classical evolutionary tracks of pre-main-sequence stars (cite: Baraffe98), it takes >1 Myr for a ~0.2 M⊙ protostar to reach a luminosity of <0.1 L⊙. The timescale is much longer than that expected by the present observations" (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The disk structure is not fully spatially resolved by the synthesized beam (~25 au x 14 au), requiring synthetic observation modeling to overcome beam dilution  
  > "However, since the present ALMA observations do not have a sufficient resolution to spatially resolve the disk structure, the comparisons with the simulation of rotating disks are needed to remove the effect of the beam dilution." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): Dense foreground core gas obscures the disk kinematics near systemic velocity (4.45-9.55 km/s) and at redshifted velocities (>12.10 km/s)  
  > "We note that we simulated only the disk component, although the source is heavily embedded in a dense core. This difference is seen in the spectrum mainly around the systemic velocity (4.45–9.55 km s-1); the dense core is observed in this velocity range." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "It is likely that the redshifted (>12.10 km s-1) gas components of the disk are not seen for the same reason." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): Physical mechanisms producing a detached disk or enabling cold accretion with a small initial radius (~0.65 R_sun) remain theoretically uncertain  
  > "Although we cannot clearly describe the mechanisms to create the detached disk without the significant mass accretion at an early stage of star formation so far, a highly dynamical (turbulent) environment seen in this system (Paper I, II) may be a hint" (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core); "Mechanisms to create such small stellar core and environments that enable the cold accretion are currently unknown." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Keplerian disk model is marginally resolved, and parameters like M_* and R_disk rely on synthetic modeling rather than directly resolved rotation curves.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Foreground core absorption hides a significant portion of the velocity spectrum around the systemic velocity.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The cold accretion scenario requires an exceptionally small initial protostellar radius (0.65 R_sun) that lacks established theoretical mechanisms.
