## Analysis recipe

1. **measure continuum size, flux and disk mass**: Image-plane deconvolution using the synthesized-beam PSF; peak/total flux converted to H2 column density and mass  
  > "We deconvolved the source in the image plane by using the point spread function (PSF) of the synthesized beam." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: Optically thin, Tdust = 100 K, κ345GHz = 1.2e-2 cm^2 g^-1 per gas+dust mass, gas-to-dust 100  
  > "with the assumptions of the optically thin emission, the uniform dust temperature of 100 K" (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1); "which means that gas-to-dust ratio is assumed to be 100" (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **identify rotation and disk orientation in 12CO**: Blue/redshifted 12CO maps and channel maps; position angle of line through channel emission peaks  
  > "A position angle of the line passing through the emission peaks in each velocity channel is $-$17$^\circ$, which is measured counterclockwise relative to the north celestial pole." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: PA compared with Spitzer bipolar nebula orientation  
  > "The angle is nearly perpendicular to that of the bipolar nebula seen in the $Spitzer$ observations (cite: Bourke06; Terebey09)." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
3. **Keplerian disk modeling to derive stellar mass and disk properties**: LTE radiative-transfer Keplerian disk models, synthetic ALMA observations with simobserve/simanalyze, comparison of 2D-Gaussian channel peak positions (velocity vs projected distance), then T1au from high-velocity spectrum intensity, then Mdisk from continuum flux; iterated  
  > "we simulated the ALMA observations in $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) and 0.87 mm continuum with the C40-6 configuration, which is the same as in our present observations, using tasks $``$simobserve$"$ and $``$simanalyze$"$ in CASA." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "The peak positions of the $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) in each channel (Figure [2](#fig:chanmap)) with both the ALMA observations and the synthetic observations of Keplerian models are derived by 2D Gaussian fitting." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "(3) The remaining parameter, $M_{\rm disk}$, was calculated to reproduce the observed 0.87 mm continuum flux by assuming the temperature distribution derived in step (2)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - software: CASA simobserve, CASA simanalyze
   - parameter: Fixed Vsys = 6.75 km/s, i = 70°, PA = -17°; free M*, Rdisk, Mdisk, T1au; initial Mdisk 8e-5 Msun, T1au 200 K  
  > "We adopted the $V_{\rm sys}$ = 6.75 km s$^{-1}$ because the high-velocity components are symmetric around this velocity" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "We initially assumed $M_{\rm disk}$ of 8 $\times$ 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$ and $T_{\rm 1au}$ of 200 K for the simulation" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation; T ∝ r^-0.5, density ∝ r^-1; LTE; only disk component simulated; symmetric gas distribution  
  > "The temperature and density profiles of the simulated models are assumed to be $\propto$ $r^{-0.5}$ and $\propto$ $r^{-1}$." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "We note that we simulated only the disk component, although the source is heavily embedded in a dense core." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - fitting: Manual parameter tuning to match high-velocity 12CO parts; no sampler stated  
  > "We tuned the parameters to reproduce the $^{12}$CO profile at the high-velocity parts because the spectrum is affected by the surrounding dense core toward the source." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - validation: Channel maps and P-V diagram compared with model; models with M* = 0.13, 0.23 Msun plotted for comparison  
  > "Our observation and model agree reasonably well (see also the channel maps in Figure [2](#fig:chanmap) and the P-V diagram in Figure [4](#fig:PV))." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
4. **estimate mass accretion rate**: Upper limit assuming luminosity is accretion luminosity  
  > "Even if the luminosity of MMS-1 is dominated by the accretion luminosity, the mass accretion rate is estimated to be quite low as $<$ 2 $\times$ 10$^{-8}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ with the assumption of a stellar radius of 1.5 $R_{\odot}$." (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
   - assumption: Stellar radius 1.5 Rsun  
  > "with the assumption of a stellar radius of 1.5 $R_{\odot}$" (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **cold accretion stellar evolution comparison**: Hosokawa code with fixed accretion rate 1e-5 Msun/yr to 0.2 Msun, then accretion stopped; initial 0.01 Msun star  
  > "We used a fixed accretion rate of 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ until the star has reached the mass of 0.2 $M_{\odot}$, and then we stopped the accretion and tracked the evolution of pre-main-sequence star." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
   - software: Hosokawa stellar evolution code
   - parameter: Initial radius 0.65 Rsun  
  > "Our calculation starting with a 0.01 $M_{\odot}$ star with the initial radius of 0.65 $R_{\odot}$ reproduces the observed luminosity ($\sim$0.07 $L_{\odot}$) in $<$10$^5$ years after we stop the mass accretion." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)

## Qualitative findings

- MMS-1 hosts a rotating disk detached from the surrounding dense gas; high-density tracers not detected toward it  
  > "These results indicate that the disk may be detached from the surrounding environment although it is located at the center of the dense core whose density is $\sim$10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
- Disk mass more than an order of magnitude smaller than other Class 0/I Keplerian disks  
  > "the disk mass is more than an order of magnitude smaller than those of the Keplerian disks around them" (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
- Hot (~50 K) gas found several hundred au from MMS-1, indicating shock heating  
  > "found very high-temperature ($\sim$50 K) gas component at several hundred au away from MMS-1, indicating that there was shock heating" (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Earlier ALMA resolution could not resolve MMS-1; higher resolution needed to understand the protostar's status  
  > "In particular, the evolutionally status of the very low-luminosity protostar is still unclear, possibly due to insufficient spatial resolutions." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Disk unresolved, so models simulated with the same configuration to account for beam dilution; Keplerian rotation as the simplest model  
  > "the comparisons with the simulation of rotating disks are needed to remove the effect of the beam dilution." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Alternative: Standard pre-main-sequence tracks (Baraffe98) need >1 Myr to reach <0.1 Lsun, inconsistent with the young age  
  > "it takes $>$1 Myr for a $\sim$0.2 $M_{\odot}$ protostar to reach a luminosity of $<$0.1 $L_{\odot}$." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- Alternative: Higher accretion rate 1e-5 Msun/yr would prevent the expansion wave reaching the 3000 au break radius, unlike the 1.6e-6 inside-out rate  
  > "the time scale becomes much shorter to achieve the present observed stellar mass, and the expansion wave cannot reach the slope breaking radius of 3000 au." (A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
- Alternative: Underestimated intrinsic luminosity considered  
  > "The possible scenario to overcome this issue is thus either the accretion mechanism is different from that or the intrinsic luminosity is underestimated." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Disk not spatially resolved  
  > "since the present ALMA observations do not have a sufficient resolution to spatially resolve the disk structure" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): Only disk component simulated; symmetric distribution assumed; redshifted >12.10 km/s components likely absorbed; excess at -0.65 km/s  
  > "Our simulation also assumed the symmetric gas distribution of the disk." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "It is likely that the redshifted ($>$12.10 km s$^{-1}$) gas components of the disk are not seen for the same reason." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): Emission near systemic velocity not seen due to optical depth  
  > "The observed gas components around the systemic velocity of this core (6.15–7.85 km s) are not seen due to the optical thickness of the line." (Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): Mechanisms for the detached disk and for cold accretion unknown  
  > "Mechanisms to create such small stellar core and environments that enable the cold accretion are currently unknown." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar mass from by-eye tuning, no formal fit or posterior; ±0.05 Msun uncertainty is rough
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination fixed at 70° from Spitzer; M* scales with sin^-2 i and the uncertainty is not propagated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only blueshifted high-velocity side constrains the rotation curve because redshifted emission is absorbed
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Velocity resolution of 0.85 km/s is coarse for a ~10 au disk
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Optically thin, 100 K dust mass may be biased for a compact optically thick disk
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Luminosity-based accretion limit depends on an assumed 1.5 Rsun radius, while the cold accretion model needs 0.65 Rsun
