## Analysis recipe

1. **image the 0.87 mm continuum and 12CO (J = 3-2) data**: Calibrate and image the ALMA 12 m array data in CASA with Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "We used the briggs weighting with the robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations)
   - software: CASA 4.7.2
   - parameter: Briggs weighting, robust = 0.5  
  > "We used the briggs weighting with the robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Reported rms noise of ~1.6 mJy/beam (CO) and ~35 μJy/beam (continuum)  
  > "$\sim$35 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$, respectively." (Observations)
2. **measure the continuum size, flux and dust mass of MMS-1**: Deconvolve the continuum source in the image plane using the synthesized-beam PSF; convert peak and total flux to H2 column density and mass  
  > "We deconvolved the source in the image plane by using the point spread function (PSF) of the synthesized beam." (Results: Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - parameter: Peak flux 2.8 mJy beam-1; total flux 3.6 mJy  
  > "The peak flux and the total flux are 2.8 mJy beam$^{-1}$ and 3.6 mJy, respectively" (Results: Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission, uniform dust temperature of 100 K, kappa_345GHz = 1.2e-2 cm2/g, gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "with the assumptions of the optically thin emission, the uniform dust temperature of 100 K" (Results: Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **map the kinematics of 12CO around MMS-1**: Inspect velocity channel maps, identify the line of emission peaks in each channel with 2D Gaussian fits, and plot relative velocity against projected distance from the continuum peak (P-V style diagram)  
  > "The peak positions of the $^{12}$CO ($J$ = 3–2) in each channel" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - parameter: Position angle of emission line -17 deg (counterclockwise from north celestial pole)  
  > "A position angle of the line passing through the emission peaks in each velocity channel is" (Results: Gas/dust distributions toward MMS-1)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting of per-channel peak positions  
  > "are derived by 2D Gaussian fitting." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **fit a Keplerian disk model to derive M*, R_disk, M_disk and T_1au**: Simulate Keplerian disk models with CASA simobserve/simanalyze using the C40-6 configuration and compare with observed spatial/velocity structure; tune parameters in three steps  
  > "then we simulated the ALMA observations in" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - software: CASA 4.7.2
   - parameter: Inclination 70 deg; position angle -17 deg; Vsys 6.75 km/s; initial M_disk 8e-5 Msun and T_1au 200 K before iteration  
  > "We adopted the $V_{\rm sys}$ = 6.75 km s$^{-1}$ because the high-velocity components are symmetric around this velocity" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "We also adopted the inclination angle = 70$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ derived from the scattered light seen in the $Spitzer$ (cite: Terebey09)" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation; temperature and density profiles proportional to r^-0.5 and r^-1; symmetric gas distribution of disk; LTE line radiative transfer  
  > "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - fitting: Manual, iterative parameter tuning in three steps: M* and R_disk from highest velocity and breaking radius; T_1au fitted to high-velocity intensity; M_disk fitted to 0.87 mm continuum flux  
  > "We derived the optimum values with the following three steps." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1); "The remaining parameter, $M_{\rm disk}$, was calculated to reproduce the observed 0.87 mm continuum flux by assuming the temperature distribution derived in step (2)." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
   - validation: Model and observations agree reasonably well in channel maps and P-V diagram; M* uncertainty roughly +/-0.05 Msun  
  > "Our observation and model agree reasonably well" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
5. **estimate the present mass accretion rate**: Assume accretion dominates the luminosity and adopt a stellar radius of 1.5 Rsun to derive an upper limit on the accretion rate  
  > "the mass accretion rate is estimated to be quite low as" (Discussions: A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
   - parameter: Stellar radius 1.5 Rsun  
  > "with the assumption of a stellar radius of 1.5 $R_{\odot}$" (Discussions: A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
   - assumption: Luminosity dominated by accretion luminosity  
  > "Even if the luminosity of MMS-1 is dominated by the accretion luminosity" (Discussions: A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **test whether cold accretion can produce the low luminosity**: Evolve a pre-main-sequence star with cold accretion: fixed accretion until 0.2 Msun, then stop and track luminosity, for different initial radii  
  > "For the cold accretion case, the luminosity depends on the initial stellar radius." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
   - parameter: Accretion rate 1e-5 Msun/yr until 0.2 Msun; start 0.01 Msun with initial radius 0.65 Rsun  
  > "We used a fixed accretion rate of 10$^{-5}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ until the star has reached the mass of 0.2 $M_{\odot}$" (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
   - assumption: Cold accretion (very low entropy accreting gas)  
  > "The stellar luminosity can be very low even just after the accretion ceases when the accreting gas provides very low entropy" (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)

   - validation: Calculated luminosity compared to the observed ~0.07 Lsun

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Higher angular resolution than earlier ALMA observations was needed to resolve the evolutionary status of the very low-luminosity protostar  
  > "In particular, the evolutionally status of the very low-luminosity protostar is still unclear, possibly due to insufficient spatial resolutions." (Introduction)
- Why this method: A Keplerian rotation model was adopted as the simplest approach to estimate stellar mass and disk properties  
  > "In the present study, we assume a Keplerian rotation, as the simplest modeling approach" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Alternative: Cold accretion versus a different accretion mechanism or underestimated intrinsic luminosity  
  > "The possible scenario to overcome this issue is thus either the accretion mechanism is different from that or the intrinsic luminosity is underestimated." (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- Alternative: Higher accretion rate of 1e-5 Msun/yr in the inside-out collapse model, rejected because the expansion wave cannot reach the breaking radius  
  > "the expansion wave cannot reach the slope breaking radius of 3000 au." (Discussions: A detached disk at the center of an isolated protostellar core)
- How they decided it was enough: Parameter sensitivity was used to decide which parameters are constrained: M* from highest velocity, R_disk from breaking radius, and M_disk and T_1au from flux and intensity  
  > "$M_*$ is basically constrained by the highest velocity and $R_{\rm disk}$ is by the breaking distance." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): The disk structure is not spatially resolved, so simulations are needed to remove beam dilution  
  > "However, since the present ALMA observations do not have a sufficient resolution to spatially resolve the disk structure" (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): The model assumes only the disk component and a symmetric gas distribution  
  > "Our simulation also assumed the symmetric gas distribution of the disk." (Physical quantities of disk of MMS-1)
- Limitation (authors): Luminosity uncertainty may come from incomplete modeling of ISRF and anisotropic radiation  
  > "The uncertainty of the luminosity may come from the incomplete modeling" (A very low-luminosity object as a candidate evolved by cold accretion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disk mass and radius are model-based at 20 au beam resolution, with a single parameter-tuning path and no formal fit or uncertainty on M_disk and R_disk
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Redshifted CO is absorbed by the foreground core, so the model fits only the blueshifted side and the systemic velocity range is excluded
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several key assumptions (100 K dust temperature, kappa, gas-to-dust 100) are inherited and affect the disk mass directly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cold-accretion explanation depends on a single chosen accretion rate and initial radius

  - axes: colour (a): 0.87 mm continuum flux [mJy beam-1]; contours (a): 12CO (J=3-2) integrated emission, blue and red components [Jy beam-1 km s-1]; x (a): Right Ascension (J2000) [not labelled in time units; hms seconds]; y (a): Declination (J2000) [arcsec]; x (b): velocity [km s-1]; y (b): brightness temperature [K]
  - supports step 3
  - validation: Compares the observed averaged 12CO spectrum with the simulated Keplerian disk model (M*=0.18 Msun) and mirrored profile to show where the model fails to match the observed intensity.
  - read off the plot: approx. continuum peak about 2.5 mJy beam-1; approx. systemic velocity 6.75 km/s; approx. observed blue peak about 5 K near 3.5 km/s; approx. observed red peak about 5 K near 10 km/s; approx. beam 0.18 x 0.10 arcsec

- **Figure 3** (scatter_or_relation, 1 panel(s), data, model): Projected distance from the continuum peak versus velocity offset of 12CO (J=3-2) peaks, observed (blueshifted and redshifted points) compared with Keplerian models for three stellar masses.
