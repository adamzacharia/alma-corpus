## Analysis recipe

1. **Image the continuum at 0.87, 2.2 and 6.8 mm and reuse the NOEMA 1.1 mm map**: tclean imaging in CASA: Band 4 two configurations combined with mtmfs and natural weighting; Band 7 with Hogbom and natural weighting; VLA phase self-calibrated (two rounds) and imaged; NOEMA 1.1 mm map taken from earlier work  
  > "We combined the visibilities from the two configurations using the *tclean* method in CASA to produce high angular resolution maps." (ALMA images); "the map was built using tclean with a Hogbom deconvolver and natural weighting" (ALMA images); "we then performed phase self-calibration on the combined dataset." (VLA images)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: VLA self-calibration imaging: scale=0, nterms=2, natural weighting; gaintype T in round 1  
  > "The mtmfs algorithm in tclean (CASA 6.5.1) was used during the self-calibration, with scale=0, nterms=2, and natural weighting." (VLA images)
   - assumption: VLA executions aligned using Gaia DR3 proper motions of AB Aur  
  > "the data were aligned by correcting for the proper motions of AB Aur as reported by Gaia DR3" (VLA images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure integrated fluxes and separate the compact central source from the outer disk**: Sum flux inside 5 sigma contours; flux inside a 5 sigma contour around the centre gives the inner source, subtracted from the total to get the outer disk; 2D Gaussian fit to test whether the central source is resolved; Gaussian fit of the 6.8 mm central source in the uv-plane and subtraction from the visibilities  
  > "This flux was later subtracted from the total flux to get the flux of the outer disk." (Spectral energy distribution at mm wavelengths); "an initial step involved fitting it directly in the uv-plane using a Gaussian profile." (Continuum maps)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Fluxes within 5 sigma contours at the four frequencies  
  > "We also computed the integrated continuum fluxes at the four frequencies by adding the fluxes inside 5$\sigma$ contours." (Continuum maps)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to the 5 sigma contour of the central source at 2.2 mm; uv-plane Gaussian fit at 6.8 mm (sampler/likelihood not stated)  
  > "an initial step involved fitting it directly in the uv-plane using a Gaussian profile." (Continuum maps)
   - validation: Compared the fitted Gaussian axes with the beam to conclude the source is not resolved  
  > "we cannot claim that the source is resolved with our 2 mm ALMA observations" (Continuum maps)
3. **Compute radial and azimuthal profiles and ring azimuthal contrast ratios**: Azimuthally averaged radial profiles of deprojected maps; radially averaged azimuthal profiles; contrast ratio as max/min of ring flux in maps convolved with the NOEMA 1.1 mm beam  
  > "Azimuthal contrast ratios were then computed as the ratio of the maximum to the minimum of the continuum flux along the ring in the maps convolved with the NOEMA beam." (Continuum maps)
   - parameter: Azimuthal profiles averaged between r=50 au and r=140 au  
  > "The profiles were computed by averaging the intensity at each azimuth between $r=50$ au and $r=140$ au." (Azimuthal profiles)
   - assumption: PA=-37 deg and i=24.9 deg for deprojection  
  > "The maps were deprojected assuming $PA=-37^{\circ}$ and $i=24.9^{\circ}$ (cite: Riviere2020)." (Radial profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Second method with radially averaged intensity profiles gave contrast ratios in agreement with the first  
  > "we obtain contrast ratios of 2.9$\rm \pm$0.7 at 0.87 mm, 3.1$\rm \pm$0.2 at 1.1 mm and 2.3$\rm \pm$ 0.7 at 2.2 mm, in agreement with the first method." (Continuum maps)
4. **Build the mm SED and fit spectral indices of the inner source and outer disk**: SED computed separately for the inner compact source and the outer disk from the four bands, with a power-law spectral index fit  
  > "we computed the SED for each region separately." (Spectral energy distribution at mm wavelengths)
   - parameter: Fluxes from Table of outer and inner disk at 0.87, 1.1, 2.2 and 6.8 mm  
  > "Table [\[Tab:disk_masses\]](#Tab:disk_masses) summarizes the continuum fluxes employed." (Spectral energy distribution at mm wavelengths)
   - fitting: Power-law fit to the SED (fitting method not stated)  
  > "We measure a spectral index of 1.1$\rm \pm$0.1 for the compact inner source, and 2.3$\rm \pm$0.1 for the outer disk." (Spectral energy distribution at mm wavelengths)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compute spectral index maps and their radial and azimuthal profiles**: Pairwise maps (0.87-1.1, 0.87-2.2, 1.1-2.2 mm) after convolving to the lower resolution; a combined map from a pixel-by-pixel power-law fit to 0.87, 1.1 and 2.2 mm convolved to the 1.1 mm beam; 6.8 mm excluded for lack of emission  
  > "Taking advantage of the frequency coverage of the NOEMA and ALMA observations, we produced a spectral index map computed by fitting a power law to individual pixels." (Spectral index maps); "To compute each spectral index map, we convolved the map with the highest angular resolution to reach the resolution of the one with the lowest angular resolution." (Spectral index maps)
   - parameter: Pixels with s/n<3 in any band masked out for the statistics  
  > "Pixels with s/n$<$3 in any of the bands employed in each map were masked out to compute these statistics." (Spectral index maps)
   - fitting: Power law fit to each pixel (details not stated)  
  > "we produced a spectral index map computed by fitting a power law to individual pixels." (Spectral index maps)
   - validation: The bump near 0.5 arcsec is flagged as possibly caused by beam extension  
  > "However, one should be aware that it could also be due to the beam extension." (Spectral index maps)
6. **Derive dust masses from the mm fluxes**: Optically thin dust mass from M_d = F_nu d^2 / (kappa_nu B_nu(T_d)) with kappa = kappa_0 (nu/nu_0)^beta and beta = alpha - 2  
  > "Assuming that the continuum emission at millimeter wavelengths is optically thin, we can use the measured millimeter fluxes to derive the dust mass using:" (Dust mass)
   - parameter: nu_0 = 230 GHz and kappa_0 = 2.3 cm2/g; T_dust = 39 K; beta = 0.2 +/- 0.1 from four bands  
  > "with $\nu_0$ = 230 GHz and $\kappa_0 = \kappa(\nu_0)= 2.3$ cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$." (Dust mass); "Using the four bands available, we get $\beta$=0.2$\rm \pm$0.1." (Dust mass)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission; T_dust = 39 K; kappa_0 = 2.3 cm2/g at 230 GHz; distance 162.9 pc; gas-to-dust ratio 40 for total mass  
  > "we assume a value of 39 K based on previous work (cite: Riviere2020)" (Dust mass); "In (cite: Riviere2020) we derived a mean gas-to-dust ratio of 40" (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Result compared with earlier dust masses; optical depth caveat noted (estimates are lower limits)  
  > "and thus our estimates are lower limits to the actual disk mass." (Dust mass)
7. **Fit a 1D isothermal slab model to the azimuthal profile of the ring**: Radially averaged azimuthal profile of the 0.87, 1.1 and 2.2 mm maps (convolved to the NOEMA 1.1 mm beam) fit with a 1D isothermal slab with scattering; parameters T_d, Sigma_d and a_max  
  > "However, given the large cavity present in AB Aur, we are more interested in modeling the radially averaged azimuthal profile to compare the distribution of grain sizes between the crescent and the rest of the ring." (A 1D isothermal dust slab model); "the intensity at a given azimuth and a given wavelength can be computed as a function of the dust temperature" (Assumptions)
   - parameter: a_min fixed to 0.01 um; size distribution slope p = 3.5; inclination 24.9 deg; fit only 0.87, 1.1, 2.2 mm  
  > "thus we fixed $a_{\rm min}$=$0.01$ $\mu$m in our model." (Assumptions); "we assume the standard ISM value of 3.5 (cite: Mathis1977)" (Assumptions); "Given the limited sensitivity of our 6.8 mm VLA maps, we performed the model fitting using only the 0.87, 1.1, and 2.2 mm bands." (Assumptions)
   - assumption: DSHARP dust composition; 10% flux calibration error at all bands  
  > "we use the dust composition assumed by the DSHARP Large Programme (cite: Birnstiel2018)" (Assumptions); "We assumed a 10% error at all bands." (Assumptions)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC with a log-normal likelihood; Gaussian prior on T_d (mean 39 K, sigma 4); flat priors on Sigma_d (1e-4 to 10 g/cm2) and a_max (1e-2 to 10 cm)  
  > "To retrieve the model parameters we followed a Bayesian approach, and performed a Monte-Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) exploration of the parameter space." (Assumptions); "we assumed a Gaussian prior for $T_{\rm d}$ with a mean value of 39 K and $\sigma$=4" (Assumptions)
   - validation: The T_d posterior follows the prior; trends in Sigma_d and a_max are within the 16-84% uncertainties and called tentative  
  > "the posterior strongly follows the prior for the dust temperature, and thus the model fitting does not add new information regarding this parameter." (Results); "In both cases, the variations are within the computed uncertainties, and thus the following trends are only tentative" (Results)
8. **Model the ring emission with dust radiative transfer on the disk-planet simulation**: RADMC-3D radiative transfer on the earlier hydrodynamical simulation (post-processed with fargo2radmc3d), producing synthetic maps at 1.1, 2.2 and 6.8 mm compared with the observed azimuthal contrast ratios and with the VLA uv-plane data  
  > "The new radiative transfer calculations presented here use the same parameters as in (cite: Fuente2017) except for" (Dust radiative transfer calculations); "Synthetic maps of continuum emission were computed at 1.1, 2.2, and 6.8 mm." (Dust radiative transfer calculations)
   - software: RADMC-3D, fargo2radmc3d, Dusty FARGO-ADSG
   - parameter: Dust mixture 70% water ice and 30% astro-silicates; size distribution slope changed from -3.5 to -3.0; distance 162.9 pc; max dust size 1 cm  
  > "which is now assumed to be a mixture comprised of 70% water ices and 30% astro-silicates" (Dust radiative transfer calculations); "the slope of the dust size distribution is changed from -3.5 to -3.0" (Dust radiative transfer calculations); "the maximum dust size along the ring is taken to be 1 cm instead of" (Dust radiative transfer calculations)
   - assumption: 2 Jupiter-mass planet at about 96 au; Lagrangian dust particles entering the planet Hill radius reinjected elsewhere (no circumplanetary emission predicted)  
  > "where a 2 Jupiter-mass planet at about 96 au was proposed to shape the mm cavity and the non-axisymmetric ring of emission outside it." (Dust radiative transfer calculations)

## Qualitative findings

- The compact central source is not resolved at 2.2 mm (beam is an upper limit) and at 6.8 mm  
  > "we cannot claim that the source is resolved with our 2 mm ALMA observations" (Continuum maps)
- Spectral index rises with radius to about 0.5-1.0 arcsec and then plateaus; a bump near 0.5 arcsec may indicate sub-micron to sub-mm grains inside the cavity (or beam extension)  
  > "The profiles show a trend for increasing spectral index with increasing radius until r$\sim$0.5-1.0$\ensuremath{''}$ beyond which it plateaus." (Spectral index maps)
- The VLA 6.8 mm ring emission is roughly ring-shaped, consistent with a nearly axisymmetric ring in the RT model  
  > "the resulting map shows that the emission is roughly consistent with being ring-shaped." (Continuum maps)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: New ALMA 2.2 mm map used instead of the earlier NOEMA 2.2 mm map for its better resolution; only the NOEMA 1.1 mm map is reused  
  > "In the present paper, we are only interested in the continuum emission at 1.1 mm, since our new ALMA map at 2.2 mm have a better resolution than the ones from (cite: Fuente2017)" (NOEMA images)
- Why this method: A slab model of the azimuthal profile was chosen because of the large cavity, to compare grain sizes in the crescent and the rest of the ring  
  > "However, given the large cavity present in AB Aur, we are more interested in modeling the radially averaged azimuthal profile to compare the distribution of grain sizes between the crescent and the rest of the ring." (A 1D isothermal dust slab model)
- Alternative: A single power law over the whole sub-mm to cm SED was rejected as a poor fit; a two-segment fit was adopted  
  > "We fitted a single power law to the whole SED, but this resulted in a poor fit at most wavelengths." (Discussion)
- Alternative: 6.8 mm map excluded from the slab-model fit, spectral index maps and azimuthal profiles because of limited sensitivity  
  > "Given the limited sensitivity of our 6.8 mm VLA maps, we performed the model fitting using only the 0.87, 1.1, and 2.2 mm bands." (Assumptions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Limited sensitivity of the VLA data prevents the 6.8 mm azimuthal contrast ratio  
  > "We could not compute the azimuthal contrast ratio of the 6.8 mm map due to the limited sensitivity of our VLA observations." (Continuum maps)
- Limitation (authors): The 6.8 mm ring map was not cleaned because of very limited SNR  
  > "Cleaning of the map was not attempted due to the very limited SNR" (Continuum maps)
- Limitation (authors): Dust masses are uncertain because the opacity depends on size distribution, composition and porosity  
  > "thus have to be considered with caution." (Dust mass)
- Limitation (authors): Slab-model trends in surface density and grain size are within the uncertainties and need more bands and sensitivity  
  > "Adding more bands to our model comparison would help reduce the fitting uncertainty" (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The free-free fraction at 0.87 mm is 37% in the abstract but 31% in the Discussion
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 1.1 mm integrated flux is 108 mJy in Table 2 and Section 3.1 but 92 mJy in the conclusions; central-source spectral index is 1.1 in Section 3 but 1.0 in the conclusions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Contrast ratios from the ring maximum/minimum depend on beam convolution and noise; the claimed wavelength trend is not significant
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Slab-model temperature is prior-dominated, so grain size and surface density depend on the fixed 39 K prior
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust composition and opacity are assumed (DSHARP composition in the slab model, water ice and silicate in the RT), so dust masses and grain sizes are model-dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): RT model uses a_max of 1 cm and different parameters than the slab fit, and matches contrast only qualitatively (synthetic 4.3 vs observed 3.1)
