## Analysis recipe

1. **Subtract continuum, image continuum and fit its size and flux**: uvcontsub with first-order baseline fit to separate continuum and line data; two-dimensional Gaussian fit to the dust continuum  
  > "Implementing the first-order baseline fit and by using ‘uvcontsub’ command available in the CASA program, we separated each spectral window into two data: continuum and line emission." (ALMA archival data); "Using two-dimensional Gaussian fitting over the dust continuum emission, we obtained the deconvolved size of G31 $\sim 0.90 '' \times 0.68''$" (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: peak 158.4 mJy/beam, integrated flux 242.4 mJy, rms 5 mJy/beam  
  > "Two dimensional Gaussian fitting of the continuum yields a peak intensity (S$_\nu^{beam}$) of dust continuum of 158.4 mJy/beam and integrated flux (F$_\nu$) of 242.4 mJy." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - assumption: Source treated as unresolved or at best marginally resolved; beam dilution correction applied to brightness temperature  
  > "It should be noted that in our observation, the source was not resolved or at best marginally resolved" (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive H2 column density and dust optical depth from the continuum**: Optically thin dust continuum equation N_H2 = (S_beam/Omega_beam)/(2 kappa B_nu(T_d) Z mu_H m_H), with brightness-temperature check of optical depth  
  > "Assuming the dust continuum to be optically thin, flux density can be written as," (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation); "By using equation 4, total hydrogen column density of G31 source is $\sim{\rm1.53\times10^{25}}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - parameter: kappa = 0.176 cm2/g at 94 GHz  
  > "By considering a thin ice condition, here, we obtained the mass absorption coefficient at 94 GHz ($3187.90$ micron) $\sim 0.176$ cm$^2$/g." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - assumption: dust-to-gas ratio Z = 0.01, mu_H = 1.41, dust temperature 150 K  
  > "Here, we used $Z=0.01$, $\mu_H=1.41$ (cite: cox00), and dust temperature 150 K." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - assumption: Dust continuum optically thin  
  > "After adding the beam dilution correction for the unresolved source, the brightness temperature is estimated to be $\sim$36 K." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation); "Considering this value of brightness temperature; dust emission remains optically thin ($\tau_\nu \sim 0.24$)." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with higher-resolution continuum of earlier work, which suggested optically thick emission, and notes that this lower-resolution data cannot probe the inner region  
  > "However, due to the lower angular resolution, our present data would not provide further details about the dust opacity of the whole region of G31." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
3. **Extract spectra and measure line parameters**: Spectra extracted from a 1.1 arcsec diameter circular region at the phase centre; single Gaussian fits for FWHM, V_LSR and integrated intensity; multi-Gaussian fits for blended CH3SH profiles  
  > "Observed spectra are obtained within the circular region having a diameter of 1.1$''$ centered at RA (J2000) = 18$^h$47$^m$34.309$^s$, Dec (J2000) =-1$^{o}$12$'$46.00$''$." (Line analysis, molecular emission and spectral fitting); "Line parameters of all the observed transitions are obtained using a single Gaussian fit." (Line analysis, molecular emission and spectral fitting)
   - software: CASSIS, CASA
   - parameter: CH3SH multi-Gaussian: fixed velocity separation and line-intensity ratio, FWHM free  
  > "For multiple Gaussian fitting, we have used fixed values of velocity separation and expected line intensity ratio. Only FWHM is kept as a free parameter." (Complex Organic Molecules)
   - assumption: Line identification with CDMS and JPL spectroscopic databases  
  > "The identification of lines of all the observed species presented in this paper is carried out using CASSIS" (ALMA archival data)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fits (single, or fixed-ratio multi-component for CH3SH); no sampler or priors stated  
  > "Line parameters of all the observed transitions are obtained using a single Gaussian fit." (Line analysis, molecular emission and spectral fitting)
   - validation: Optical thinness of CH3NCO, CH3OH and CH3OCHO lines checked by comparing observed and LTE-expected line ratios  
  > "The similarity between the expected and observed line ratios of these transitions suggest that they are optically thin." (Complex Organic Molecules)
4. **Derive rotational temperatures with rotation diagrams**: Linear fit of log(N_u/g_u) versus E_u for CH3OH, CH3OCHO, CH3SH and CH3NCO; two temperature components fitted for methanol  
  > "Two parameters, column density and rotational temperature can be obtained by fitting a straight line to the values of $\rm{log(N_u/g_u)}$ plotted as a function of E$_u$" (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - parameter: T_rot: CH3OCHO ~162 K, CH3SH 76 K, CH3NCO 48 K, CH3OH cold 33 K and hot 181 K  
  > "We have obtained a rotational temperature of 76 K for CH$_3$SH and 48 K for CH$_3$NCO." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature); "Hot component is obtained by CH$_3$SH, CH$_3$OH and CH$_3$OCHO where the temperature ranges between 143 and 181 K." (Conclusions)
   - assumption: Optically thin lines in LTE  
  > "We performed the rotation diagram analysis by assuming the observed transitions to be optically thin and are in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - fitting: Straight-line fit; error bars from Gaussian-fit integrated intensity uncertainties; no sampler or priors  
  > "this arises from the error of the observed integrated intensity that we measured using a single Gaussian fitting to the observed profile of each transition." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - validation: Methyl formate temperature compared with earlier measurement in this source, though the fit is described as not very good  
  > "Though we did not find a very good fitting, the obtained rotational temperature of methyl formate $\sim 162$ K is consistent with the previously measured value in this source for the same molecule" (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
5. **Derive molecular column densities and abundances with LTE modeling**: CASSIS LTE model spectra compared with observed spectra; only column density varied, with Tex, source size, FWHM and N_H2 fixed; beam-averaged column density; abundances relative to H2  
  > "To obtain the molecular column density of the observed chemical species, we used LTE modeling with CASSIS." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature); "only column density was varied by keeping all other parameters (Tex, source size, FWHM, and hydrogen column density) fixed" (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Source size set to the deconvolved continuum size; FWHM from Gaussian fits; Tex assumed ~150 K  
  > "For LTE fitting, we used the source sizes for molecular emission similar to the deconvolved size as we obtained from 2D Gaussian fitting." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature); "excitation temperature (Tex) is assumed to be $\sim$ 150 K, a typical hot core temperature." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - assumption: Beam dilution not taken into account in the beam-averaged column density; abundances relative to N_H2 = 1.53e25 cm-2  
  > "We calculated beam average column density (N$_b$), where the effect of beam dilution was not taken into consideration." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - fitting: Best fit chosen when LTE model spectra match the observed spectra (visual match; no statistic, sampler or priors stated)  
  > "we choose the best fitted-results when LTE model spectra are the same as the observed spectra" (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
   - validation: LTE column densities compared with rotation-diagram column densities; opacities >=1 for some CH3OH and CH3NCO lines, so those LTE values are lower limits  
  > "We have also compared the column densities of CH$_3$OH, CH$_3$SH, CH$_3$NCO, and CH$_3$OCHO between rotation diagram analysis and LTE model" (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature); "Thus, the column density estimated for CH$_3$OH and CH$_3$NCO by using LTE should be considered as lower limit." (Table of column densities and abundances (notes))
6. **Constrain density and temperature from absorption lines with non-LTE radiative transfer**: RADEX grid over n_H = 1e3-1e7 cm-3 and T_kin = 1-100 K for HCN (1-0), H13CO+ (1-0), SiO (2-1) with a 36 K continuum background  
  > "For the non-LTE modeling, we use RADEX program (cite: vand07)." (Radiative transfer modeling); "In RADEX, we used the continuum background temperature (T$_c$) of 36 K, which corresponds to the peak brightness temperature of the 3.2 mm continuum by considering the beam dilution." (Radiative transfer modeling)
   - software: RADEX, LAMDA, JPL, CDMS
   - parameter: column densities 3.55e15 (H13CO+), 8.92e15 (HCN), 1.18e15 (SiO) cm-2  
  > "For this modeling, we have used colum density $\rm{3.55 \times 10^{15} \ cm^{-2}}$, $\rm{8.92 \times 10^{15} \ cm^{-2}}$, and $\rm{1.18 \times 10^{15} \times cm^{-2}}$ for H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$, HCN, and SiO respectively." (Radiative transfer modeling)
   - assumption: Collisional rates from LAMDA; spectroscopic constants from JPL/CDMS  
  > "The collisional rates were taken from LAMDA database and the spectroscopic constants were taken from JPL/CDMS catalog." (Radiative transfer modeling)
   - fitting: Parameter-space grid of radiation temperature; no fitting statistic stated  
  > "Our parameter space consists of the number density of hydrogen (varies in between $\rm{10^3-10^7\ cm^{-3})}$ and kinetic temperature (varies in between 1-100 K)." (Radiative transfer modeling)
   - validation: Predicted SiO emission/absorption behaviour consistent with observed SiO profile  
  > "The observed spectral profile of SiO is in line with this results." (Radiative transfer modeling)
7. **Characterise infall and outflows**: Infall velocity from the offset of the H13CO+ red-shifted absorption from the systemic velocity; SiO and HCN blue/red lobes imaged and outflow velocity, dynamical time, mass, momentum and energy derived  
  > "The infall velocity can be estimated as the difference between systematic velocity and the velocity at the observed inverse P-Cygni profile’s absorption feature." (Infalling envelope); "To estimate the other outflow parameters such as mass, momentum, energy, mass loss, momentum loss, and energy loss per year, we used the formula mentioned in (cite: cabr90)." (Molecular outflow)
   - parameter: Outflow velocity taken as maximum outflow velocity relative to 97 km/s LSR; SiO blue/red lobe velocities 23 and 33 km/s  
  > "For the blue lobe and red lobe of SiO outflow, velocities are found to be $23$ km s$^{-1}$ and $33$ km s$^{-1}$." (Molecular outflow)
   - assumption: Same abundance 1.0e-8 and excitation temperature 20 K for SiO and HCN; distance 7.9 kpc, with values for 3.7 kpc given in parentheses  
  > "To estimate the outflow parameters, we assumed same abundance ($\rm{1.0\times10^{-8}}$) and excitation temperature ($20$ K) of SiO (cite: code13) and HCN." (Molecular outflow); "In parenthesis, we have noted the outflows parameters by considering the new distance 3.7 kpc of G31." (Table of outflow parameters (note))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Outflow parameters and dynamical times compared with earlier published values  
  > "All these outflow parameters are in good agreement with the observed values of (cite: arya08)." (Molecular outflow)
8. **Compare abundances with other hot cores and a chemical model**: Compare G31 column densities, abundances and molecular ratios with Sgr B2(N), Orion KL, G10.47+0.03 and the hot-core model peak gas-phase abundances at 100-200 K (fast warm-up)  
  > "We used peak gas-phase abundances of all the species around 100-200 K of fast warm-up timescale model to compare with the observed values." (Discussions)
   - assumption: Fast warm-up timescale model judged most suitable for high-mass star-forming regions  
  > "The timescale of the fast warm-up model is more suitable for studying the chemical evolution in high-mass star-forming regions." (Discussions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- H13CO+ shows red-shifted absorption (inverse P-Cygni profile), indicating infall  
  > "This is a signature of inverse P-Cygni profile. This suggests that the source has an infall nature." (Simple Molecules)
- SiO and HCN trace outflows, with a clear East-West outflow and a North-South component  
  > "Integrated emission of SiO and HCN transition (Figures [7](#outflow-sio) and [8](#outflow-hcn)) shows that there is a clear outflow direction along the East-West direction." (Molecular outflow)
- Emitting regions of CH3NCO and CH3SH are slightly smaller than the beam (marginally resolved); different COMs may have different emitting regions but this is not conclusive  
  > "Molecular distributions show that CH$_3$NCO and CH$_3$SH have a slightly lower-emitting region as compared to beam size (marginally resolved)." (Molecular distributions and abundances)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Archival ALMA Band 3 data were used to search for COMs and study kinematics, giving first detections of CH3NCO and CH3SH in G31  
  > "In this work, we present the interferometric (ALMA archival data) observation towards G31." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Rotation diagram and LTE modelling used because multiple transitions with different E_u were detected for several COMs; RADEX used because absorption features trace excitation conditions  
  > "Thus, we employed the rotation diagram analysis to obtain the rotational temperatures." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature); "Here, we used non-LTE modeling to derive excitation conditions and physical properties from these absorption features." (Radiative transfer modeling)
- Alternative: Dust opacity also checked with beam-dilution correction versus uncorrected brightness temperature (18 K, tau ~0.12 versus 36 K, tau ~0.24); both found optically thin  
  > "After adding the beam dilution correction for the unresolved source, the brightness temperature is estimated to be $\sim$36 K." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
- Alternative: Outflow and mass results given for both 7.9 kpc and 3.7 kpc distances  
  > "However, by considering the new distance measurement of $\sim 3.7$ kpc of this source, we obtained the dynamical time scale of outflow around 1-2$\rm{\times10^{3}}$ years." (Molecular outflow)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Emission not fully resolved; poor angular resolution prevents spatial separation of temperature components and emitting regions  
  > "Though the spectral emissions towards G31.41+0.31 are not fully resolved" (abstract); "However, due to poor angular resolution, we could not provide further details about these two temperature components’ spatial distribution." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
- Limitation (authors): LTE column densities of CH3OH and CH3NCO are lower limits because some lines have opacity >=1  
  > "Thus, the column density estimated for CH$_3$OH and CH$_3$NCO by using LTE should be considered as lower limit." (Table of column densities and abundances (notes))
- Limitation (authors): Beam-averaged column density with no beam dilution correction  
  > "We calculated beam average column density (N$_b$), where the effect of beam dilution was not taken into consideration." (Rotation diagram analysis: estimation of gas temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Dust opacity of the inner region cannot be probed with the present lower-resolution data  
  > "However, due to the lower angular resolution, our present data would not provide further details about the dust opacity of the whole region of G31." (Continuum emission and H2 column density estimation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Internal inconsistencies: CH3SH rotation temperature given as 76 K in Section 3.3 but the Conclusions place CH3SH in a 143-181 K hot component; text gives CH3SH column density 4.13e-17 versus 4.13(17) in the Table; text says rotation-diagram CH3NCO column density is one order higher than LTE, but the Table lists a lower rotation-diagram value
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Tex = 150 K is assumed in the CASSIS models even though the CH3NCO and CH3SH rotation temperatures are 48 K and 76 K; the choice of Tex strongly changes column densities
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LTE best fit judged by eye; no uncertainties on column densities or abundances and no detection significance for the new species
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Many CH3NCO and CH3SH lines are blended (including with ethylene glycol) and the beam is comparable to the source size, so the identification and emitting sizes are uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Large distance ambiguity (7.9 versus 3.7 kpc) changes source size, mass and outflow results
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Calibration and imaging recipe is not described, so continuum and line flux scale cannot be verified
