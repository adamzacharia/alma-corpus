- Question: Investigate the chemical composition of four FUor-like Class I YSOs and compare their COM abundances with other YSOs.  
  > "Our aim is to investigate the chemical composition of four FUor-like Class I YSOs: L1551 IRS 5, Haro 5a IRS, V346 Nor, and OO Ser, and to compare their abundances of COMs with other YSOs in the literature." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct matched line cubes**: Apply phase and amplitude self-calibration solutions to continuum-subtracted measurement sets, concatenate configurations, and image with tclean.  
  > "We carried out self-calibration for both the visibility phases and amplitudes to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and we applied these solutions to the continuum-subtracted measurement sets." (Observations); "The common baselines between the three different observing configurations were consistent so we concatenated them into a single measurement set." (Observations); "The continuum-subtracted data cubes were created using *tclean* in CASA with a Briggs scheme and a robust value of 0.5, thus generating maps with a compromise between sensitivity and angular resolution." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "with a Briggs scheme and a robust value of 0.5" (Observations)
   - parameter: Both windows use the image size, pixel size and beam shape obtained from the lower-frequency window  
  > "Both data cubes were made using the same image size, pixel size, and beam shape that was obtained using the spectral window with the shortest frequencies." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Common baselines consistent between configurations  
  > "The common baselines between the three different observing configurations were consistent" (Observations)
2. **Extract spectra and identify molecules**: Search individual pixels for strongest line emission in both windows; extract off-center spectra; identify lines with CASSIS and molecular databases.  
  > "We chose these positions by extracting spectra using a pixel-size aperture placed at multiple positions throughout the disks, and finding the pixels where the line emission was stronger in both spectral windows." (Spectra); "For the line identification we used the CASSIS[^1] software" (Spectra)
   - software: CASSIS, CDMS, JPL molecular database, Lille Spectroscopic Database
   - parameter: Spectral extraction coordinates: N 04:31:34.1575 +18:08:04.793; S 04:31:34.1669 +18:08:04.253; Haro 05:35:26.5616 -05:03:55.064; V346 16:32:32.1951 -44:55:30.769; OO 18:29:49.1535 +01:16:19.646.  
  > "04:31:34.1575 $+$18:08:04.793" (Spectra); "04:31:34.1669 $+$18:08:04.253" (Spectra); "For all targets, the separation between the peak of the continuum and where we extracted the spectra is less than the beam size." (Spectra)
   - parameter: CDMS for most species; JPL for CHD2OH, CH3COCH3 and CH3OCHO as written; LSD for CH3CHO.  
  > "We used CDMS for most of the species." (Spectra); "For CHD$_2$OH, CH$_3$COCH$_3$, and CH$_3$OCHO, we utilized the JPL database, and for CH$_3$CHO we used LSD." (Spectra)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare continuum-peak and displaced spectra; continuum-peak lines are weaker.  
  > "We found weaker line emission in the spectra extracted on the pixel closest to the protostar." (Spectra)
3. **Measure molecular morphology and methanol extent**: Inspect moment maps for rotation and offsets; fit a 2D Gaussian to the 232.783 GHz CH3OH integrated emission; convert major-axis FWHM to Gaussian σ as a disk-radius proxy.  
  > "We estimated the size of the CH$_3$OH emission from the disk by using the *imfit* tool in CASA to fit a 2D Gaussian to the Moment 0 maps of the 232.78 GHz transition of each target." (Spatial distribution of COMs); "we converted to a Gaussian $\sigma$ by dividing the FWHM by $2\sqrt{2 \ln{2}}$, and we used this $\sigma$ as a proxy for the CH$_3$OH disk radius." (Spatial distribution of COMs); "We measured the separations between the peaks in the Moment 0 maps of the COMs and the peaks of the continuum, and compared them to the semi-major axis of the beam of each map." (Spatial distribution of COMs)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: CH3OH moment 1 cutoff: 3σ; smaller-molecule moment 1 cutoff: 4σ  
  > "the Moment 1 maps of CH$_3$OH for each of our targets, which were created with a 3$\sigma$ cutoff" (Spatial distribution of COMs); "These maps were constructed with a 4$\sigma$ cutoff." (Spatial distribution of smaller molecules)
   - parameter: Extent tracer: 232.783 GHz CH3OH, Eup = 446.5 K  
  > "we chose to only use the transition at 232.783 GHz ($E_\mathrm{up}$ = 446.5 K) to measure the extent of CH$_3$OH in all protostars." (Spatial distribution of COMs)
   - assumption: Gaussian σ represents the CH3OH disk radius  
  > "we used this $\sigma$ as a proxy for the CH$_3$OH disk radius." (Spatial distribution of COMs)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian with CASA imfit  
  > "using the *imfit* tool in CASA to fit a 2D Gaussian" (Spatial distribution of COMs)
   - validation: Visual confirmation of off-center COM emission; compare gas geometry with literature dust geometry.  
  > "We consider these separations to be an indication of the COM emission being off-center only for L1551 IRS 5 N and Haro 5a IRS, and we corroborated this via a visual inspection." 4. **Fit LTE synthetic spectra**: CASSIS MCMC synthetic spectra fit N, Tex, vLSR, FWHM and source size using selected unblended lines with sufficient SNR.  
  > "We used the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach provided in CASSIS for our analysis." (Analysis); "Each synthetic spectrum is created using a combination of column density ($N$), excitation temperature ($T_\mathrm{ex}$), systemic velocity ($v_\mathrm{LSR}$), FWHM of the line, and source size ($s$)." (Analysis); "A $\chi^2$ is computed for all the transitions selected for the comparison (meaning lines that are not blended and with sufficient SNR)." (Analysis)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: N: 10^12–10^19 cm−2; Tex: 10–300 K; FWHM: 1–10 km s−1; s: 0.01–1.0 arcsec  
  > "10$^{12}$ cm$^{-2}\leq N \leq 10^{19}$ cm$^{-2}$, 10 K $\leq T_\mathrm{ex} \leq$ 300 K" (L1551 IRS 5 North); "1 km s$^{-1}$$\leq \mathrm{FWHM} \leq$ 10 km s$^{-1}$, and 0.01$''$$\leq s \leq$ 1.0$''$." (L1551 IRS 5 North)
   - parameter: N, Tex and s logarithmic; vLSR and FWHM linear

   - parameter: 50 000 iterations; first 45 000 discarded; uncertainties from 0.16, 0.50 and 0.84 percentiles as written  
  > "We ran the MCMC for 50 000 iterations and dropped the first 45 000 as a burn-in phase." (L1551 IRS 5 North); "Afterwards we determined the best-fit parameters and their uncertainties using the 0.16, 0.50, and 0.84 percentiles." (L1551 IRS 5 North)
   - assumption: LTE; other targets have densities comparable to L1551 IRS 5

   - fitting: Minimum χ²; errors include spectral-window rms and nominal ALMA flux calibration uncertainty; explicit priors not stated.

(Analysis)
   - validation: Check transitions across the full windows; shorter burn-in did not alter best-fit values.  
  > "Using this methodology instead of a rotational diagram approach allows us to verify that all the transitions in the full spectral window (not just those used in the fit) match the observational data." ## Qualitative findings

- All four FUor-like systems show compact warm COM emission consistent with hot corinos.  
  > "The emission of the COMs is generally compact and comparable to the size of the dust disk." (Conclusions); "Our best-fit resulted in most COMs having $T_\mathrm{ex}$ values above 100 K, indicating warm emission. Therefore, we propose that these four FUor-type protostars are hot corinos." (Conclusions)
- Both disks in L1551 IRS 5 have hot corino-like properties.  
  > "Here we confirm that both disks have hot corino-like properties." (L1551 IRS 5)
- Haro 5a IRS weak lines are attributed to high dust optical depths.  
  > "Haro 5a IRS has the weakest line emission despite having comparable luminosities and at a comparable distance to OO Ser. We expect this to be due to high dust optical depths." (Conclusions)
- Main-isotopologue methanol normalization can overestimate COM/methanol ratios; rarer isotopologues are preferred.  
  > "Studies based on the main isotopologue only consequently leads to overestimates of the column density ratios w.r.t. CH$_3$OH. Comparison using lower abundant isotopologues of CH$_3$OH should be favored when possible." (Comparison with other YSOs)
- No abundance-ratio trend distinguishes eruptive from quiescent sources; sample size limits interpretation.  
  > "Regardless of the COM used to calculate the abundance ratios, no trend is seen between the outbursting and quiescent sources but the sample is too small for any firm conclusion." (Comparison with other YSOs)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Elevated luminosities warm disks, sublimate frozen molecules and ease COM detection in evolved protostars.  
  > "The elevated luminosities of eruptive YSOs increase disk temperatures sublimating frozen molecules and easing their detection." (abstract)
- Why this method: Single-pixel extraction reduces velocity blending; synthetic spectra check all transitions and allow optically thick lines to constrain size.  
  > "Our decision to use an individual pixel to extract the spectra, instead of a larger aperture to boost the SNR, was to minimize the blending caused by the distribution of velocities found in a larger aperture." (Spectra); "Using this methodology instead of a rotational diagram approach allows us to verify that all the transitions in the full spectral window (not just those used in the fit) match the observational data." (Analysis); "the use of moderately optically thick lines can help constrain the source size of the analyzed species." (Analysis)
- Alternative: Larger extraction apertures rejected to minimize velocity blending  
  > "Our decision to use an individual pixel to extract the spectra, instead of a larger aperture to boost the SNR, was to minimize the blending caused by the distribution of velocities found in a larger aperture." (Spectra)
- Alternative: Rotational diagrams replaced by full synthetic-spectrum comparison  
  > "Using this methodology instead of a rotational diagram approach allows us to verify that all the transitions in the full spectral window (not just those used in the fit) match the observational data." (Analysis)
- Alternative: Median-constrained Haro 5a IRS models replaced by methanol-constrained models  
  > "We ran a third set of MCMC fits using the $T_\mathrm{ex}$ and $v_\mathrm{LSR}$ from the CH$_3$OH fit, and found that the resulting models were a slightly better fit than the ones using the medians." (Haro 5a IRS)
- Alternative: Two-component OO Ser fitting abandoned because weak secondary emission prevented consistent results  
  > "We attempted to add a second component to the fitting procedure but, because some of the intensities of this possible secondary component are close to the noise level, we could not find consistent results during the runs." (OO Ser)
- Alternative: Single-dish comparison sources excluded because of beam dilution and envelope/shock contamination  
  > "We limited this comparison only to sources that have been observed with interferometers, as the single-dish observations can cause significant beam-dilution of the emission line fluxes and are more susceptible to envelope or large scale shock contamination." (Comparison with other YSOs)
- How they decided it was enough: Check full-window models, residuals near 3σ, burn-in sensitivity and isotope consistency; choose constrained models by visual comparison.  
  > "We found that shortening the burn-in phase did not cause changes in the values of the best-fit parameters." (L1551 IRS 5 North); "We examined the residuals between the data and the best-fit model and found that the residual of all lines used for the fit fall within $\pm$3$\sigma$." (Haro 5a IRS); "We carried out a visual comparison between the models obtained using the medians and the models obtained with the values found for CH$_3$OH." (V346 Nor)
- Limitation (authors): Fit uncertainties are statistical and underestimated  
  > "We emphasize that these uncertainties are purely statistical and should be considered as underestimated." (L1551 IRS 5 North)
- Limitation (authors): Baseline placement changes column densities by factors 1.2–1.8 in L1551 IRS 5 North  
  > "found that the values of $N$ were higher by a factor between 1.2 to 1.8, depending on the COM." (L1551 IRS 5 North)
- Limitation (authors): Haro 5a IRS source size and column density are degenerate  
  > "There is a degeneracy between the column densities and the source size." (Haro 5a IRS)
- Limitation (authors): L1551 IRS 5 South methanol velocity differs from other COMs, making abundance ratios uncertain  
  > "It is unclear whether the discrepancy between $v_\mathrm{LSR}$ values is real or an effect caused by absorption at redshifted velocities." (L1551 IRS 5 South); "These abundance ratios could then be quite uncertain." (L1551 IRS 5 South)
- Limitation (authors): V346 Nor profile discrepancies cannot be diagnosed at the available resolution  
  > "the angular and spectral resolutions of the observations are not sufficient to identify what causes these different line profiles." (V346 Nor)
- Limitation (authors): V346 Nor D/H is highly uncertain because of methanol opacity and poor CH2DOH modeling  
  > "the possible underestimation of the column density of CH$_3$OH and the low quality of the CH$_2$DOH model make this value highly uncertain." (V346 Nor)
