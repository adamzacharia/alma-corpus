## Science

- Fields: star_formation
- Question: Can a shock-forming model of semi-periodical jet velocity variations derive the inclination angle and velocity of protostellar jets from single-epoch ALMA observations of SiO knots?  
  > "Here we employ a novel method to constrain both the inclination angle and the jet velocity, requiring only one epoch of observation." (Introduction); "Therefore, we adopt the shock-forming model (cite: 1990ApJ...364..601R; 2004ApJ...606..483L), which uses such variations, to derive the inclination angle and jet velocity from the knots in the jets." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Determine systemic velocity of each target**: Identify the velocity at the peak emission of the C18O position-velocity structure cut perpendicular to the jet axis through the source center  
  > "In each source, the systemic velocity $V_{sys}$ is assumed to be the velocity at the peak emission of the C$^{18}$O position-velocity (PV) structure cut perpendicular to the jet axis going through the sources’ center." (Results and models)
   - assumption: Peak emission in C18O PV cut perpendicular to the jet axis represents the systemic velocity  
  > "In each source, the systemic velocity $V_{sys}$ is assumed to be the velocity at the peak emission of the C$^{18}$O position-velocity (PV) structure cut perpendicular to the jet axis going through the sources’ center." (Results and models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure SiO jet knot spatial and kinematic properties**: Measure distance to first knot (D_obs) and average knot interval (Delta D_obs) from total intensity SiO maps, and measure radial sideways-ejection velocity (Delta V_obs) and radial jet velocity (V_obs) from SiO PV diagrams  
  > "Table 1 shows the values of $D_{\rm obs}$ and $\Delta D_{\rm obs}$ measured from the total intensity SiO maps (Figure [\[fig:SiOjet\]](#fig:SiOjet)), the values of $\Delta V_{\rm obs}$ and $V_{\rm obs}$ measured from the SiO PV diagrams (cite: 2022ApJ...925...11D)," (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
   - parameter: Error on D_obs assumed to be -0.5 Delta D_obs and +0.5 beam size  
  > "Here the distance where the shock first forms $D_{\rm obs}$ is uncertain and is thus assumed to have an error of $0.5 \Delta D_{\rm obs}$ on the negative side and a half of the beam size on the positive side," (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
   - assumption: Knots very close to the central source (blueshifted side of G205S3, both sides of G206, BK0/RK0 in HH 211, R1-a/BI-a in L1448C(N)) have a different origin and are excluded; downstream knots are adopted as the first shock-formed knots  
  > "Notice that the first knots on the blueshifted side of G205S3 and on the both sides of G206 are too close to the sources to form shocks, and thus should have a different origin. Therefore, we adopted the knots downstream to be the first knots formed by shocks." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model); "In the case of HH 211, the first pair of knots (knots BK0 and RK0) are too close ( $<$ 25AU) to the source, so they are unlikely formed by shocks and thus excluded from our analysis." (Application to other sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive jet inclination angle, velocity, and velocity variation period using the shock-forming model**: Apply analytical shock-forming model equations (Equations 3, 6, 7, 8) to measured D_obs, Delta D_obs, Delta V_obs, and V_obs to compute inclination angle i, jet velocity V_j, and period P  
  > "Then, from Equations 6 and 7, we can derive the inclination angle with the following $$\begin{equation}
i = \tan^{-1} \left(\frac{V_{\rm j} \sin i}{V_{ \rm j} \cos i}\right) = \tan^{-1} \left(\frac{V_{\rm obs}}{\pi \frac{D_{\rm obs}}{\Delta D_{\rm obs}} \Delta V_{\rm obs}}\right).
\end{equation}$$ using the values measured from the observations, and then $V_{j}$ with Equation 6." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model); "Therefore, the interval of the knots ($\Delta D$) is given as: $$\begin{equation}
\Delta D = PV_{\rm j}.
\end{equation}$$" (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
   - assumption: Jet velocity varies sinusoidally with period P and amplitude Delta V: V = V_j - (Delta V / 2) sin(2 pi t / P)  
  > "In the model, the jet is assumed to have the following velocity, $$\begin{equation}
V = V_{\rm j} - \frac{\Delta V}{2} \sin \left(\frac{2\pi t}{P}\right),
\end{equation}$$" (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
   - assumption: No energy dissipation in shocks; thermal energy is totally transferred sideways so sideways-ejection velocity equals Delta V  
  > "In addition, we assume no energy dissipation so that the thermal energy produced by the shocks would be totally transferred sideways into the sideways ejection. Thus, the sideways-ejection velocity is equal to $\Delta V$." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
   - fitting: Direct analytical calculation from Equations 3, 6, and 8  
  > "Then, from Equations 6 and 7, we can derive the inclination angle with the following $$\begin{equation}
i = \tan^{-1} \left(\frac{V_{\rm j} \sin i}{V_{ \rm j} \cos i}\right) = \tan^{-1} \left(\frac{V_{\rm obs}}{\pi \frac{D_{\rm obs}}{\Delta D_{\rm obs}} \Delta V_{\rm obs}}\right).
\end{equation}$$ using the values measured from the observations, and then $V_{j}$ with Equation 6." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
   - validation: Compared derived inclination angles with CO outflow shell inclinations, and tested model on three literature jets (HH 211, HH 212, L1448C(N)) with independent proper motion measurements  
  > "As can be seen from Table [2](#tab:CO), the inclination angles derived for the jets, $i(\rm SiO)$, using the shock-forming model are broadly consistent (within the errors) with those derived for the associated outflows, $i(\rm CO)$, using the wind-driven shell model." (Discussion); "As can be seen in Table 1, the derived inclination angle and velocity of the jet in these three jets are also broadly consistent with those previously measured from proper motion and radial velocity." (Application to other sources)
4. **Fit CO outflow shells with the wide-angle wind-driven shell model**: Fit projected parabolic shell equation (Eq. 11) to outflow boundaries in low-velocity CO channel maps and tilted parabolic PV equation (Eq. 12) to CO PV diagrams along the jet axis to obtain i, c, and t_0  
  > "Therefore, for each outflow, we have obtained the values of $i$, $c$, and $t_0$ (see Table [2](#tab:CO)), by fitting Equation 11 to the boundary of the outflow in the low-velocity channel maps (Figure [2](#outflow) (a) to (d)), and by searching the tilted parabolic PV structure and then fitting Equation 12 to its tilt and opening angle in the PV diagrams (Figure [2](#outflow) (e) to (h))." (CO outflows and the wide-angle wind-driven shell model)
   - assumption: Outflow shell is a radially expanding parabolic shell with z = c R^2 and v_R = R / t_0  
  > "In this model, the outflow shell is assumed to be a radially expanding parabolic shell with its structure and velocity given in the cylindrical coordinate system $(z,R)$ by $$\begin{equation}
 z = c R^{2} 
\end{equation}$$ $$\begin{equation}
 v_{R} =
\frac{R}{t_{0}}, 
\end{equation}$$" (CO outflows and the wide-angle wind-driven shell model)
   - fitting: Fitting Equations 11 and 12 to channel map boundaries and PV diagrams; errors estimated from uncertainty of intensity peak values  
  > "The errors of these parameters are estimated from the uncertainty of intensity peak values in the observed channel maps and PV diagrams (Figure [2](#outflow))." (CO outflows and the wide-angle wind-driven shell model)
   - validation: Compared derived CO outflow inclination angles with SiO jet inclination angles  
  > "As can be seen from Table [2](#tab:CO), the inclination angles derived for the jets, $i(\rm SiO)$, using the shock-forming model are broadly consistent (within the errors) with those derived for the associated outflows, $i(\rm CO)$, using the wind-driven shell model." (Discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- Four of the six jets (G209, G205, G203, and G191) are monopolar in both SiO and high-velocity CO on the redshifted side, while their low-velocity CO outflows are bipolar.  
  > "Notice that four of the jets (G209, G205, G203, and G191) happen to be monopolar jets in both SiO and CO and are seen only on the redshifted side, and the other two jets (G205S3 and G206) are bipolar." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model); "In our sample, four of the jets detected in SiO are monopolar and are seen only on the redshifted side, although their associated CO outflows are bipolar." (Jets and Outflows)
- There is a gap without SiO emission between the central source and the first knot in four of the jets, explained by the catch-up distance required to form internal shocks.  
  > "Interestingly, there is no SiO emission between the central sources and their first (closest) knots in four of the jets." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model); "However, an internal shock does not form immediately near the source because it takes time (or distance) for the fast material to catch up with the slow material, as shown in Figure [1](#jet) (g)." (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
- A clear majority of the analyzed jet knot sources have velocity variation periods on the order of decades and show evidence of multi-year mid-IR or sub-mm accretion variability.  
  > "It is striking that the majority of jets analyzed in this paper have knot timescales on the order of decades (Table [1](#tab-params)), suggesting that there is some recurrent form of disk or accretion instability operating at this frequency." (Jet Knot Timescales and Mid-IR/Sub-mm Light Curve Variability); "In summary, a clear majority of this paper’s jet knot sources show evidence of many-year accretion variability." (Jet Knot Timescales and Mid-IR/Sub-mm Light Curve Variability)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The six sources were selected from the ALMASOP survey because they were the only ones showing clear periodical knotty structures in their SiO jets.  
  > "The data are part of the ALMA Survey of Orion Planck Galactic Cold Clumps (ALMASOP) project (cite: 2020ApJ...898..107H; 2020ApJS..251...20D; 2021ApJ...907L..15S), in which only these six targeted sources were seen with clear periodical knotty structures." (Observations)
- Why this method: Direct measurement of jet velocity via proper motion requires multi-epoch observations over several years, whereas the shock-forming model constrains both inclination angle and jet velocity from a single epoch of observation.  
  > "However, it requires multi-epoch observations with enough spatial and velocity resolution over several years to measure the jet proper motion in order to derive the jet velocity. In addition, inclination angle of the jet, which is also needed to derive the jet velocity, can not be measured directly. Here we employ a novel method to constrain both the inclination angle and the jet velocity, requiring only one epoch of observation." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Periodical enhanced mass ejections as the origin of the knots (considered unlikely because it is unclear how such ejections can form shocks to produce SiO emission)  
  > "Alternatively, the knots could be due to periodical enhanced mass ejections. However, it is unclear how such ejections can form shocks (cite: 2014prpl.conf..451F) to produce the observed SiO knotty shocks." (Jets and Outflows)
- Alternative: Asymmetry of ambient material as the cause of monopolar SiO/CO jets (rejected because ambient material can only affect low-velocity CO outflow material)  
  > "This asymmetry of the jets is unlikely to be caused by an asymmetry of the ambient material, which can only affect material at low velocity in the CO outflow (cite: 2014A&A...563L...3C)." (Jets and Outflows)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The distance where the shock first forms (D_obs) is uncertain.  
  > "Here the distance where the shock first forms $D_{\rm obs}$ is uncertain and is thus assumed to have an error of $0.5 \Delta D_{\rm obs}$ on the negative side and a half of the beam size on the positive side," (SiO jets and the shock-forming model)
- Limitation (authors): For the shock-forming model to work, jets must have at least a few roughly equally spaced knots produced by internal shocks, shock speed around 10 to 40 km s^-1, and no significant energy dissipation in the shocks.  
  > "Notice that, in order for this model to work, the jets must have at least a few roughly equally spaced knots produced by internal shocks. In addition, the shock speed ($\Delta V$) should be around 10 to 40 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: 1997A&A...321..293S), so that the SiO emission is indeed produced by shocks. Moreover, no significant energy is dissipated in the shocks so that the thermal energy produced by the shocks can be totally transferred sideways into the sideways ejection." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Further observations at a larger scale are needed to check whether asymmetry in the ambient material causes the opening angle asymmetry of the CO outflows.  
  > "Some asymmetry is seen in the ambient material in the C$^{18}$O total intensity maps around the sources (Figure [2](#outflow) (a) to (d)), however, further observations at a larger scale are needed to check it." (Jets and Outflows)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The assumption of zero radiative/thermal energy dissipation in internal shocks (so that sideways expansion velocity strictly equals the velocity variation amplitude Delta V) is an idealized approximation for radiative protostellar shocks.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Subjective exclusion of the innermost knots in several sources (G205S3 blue, G206, HH 211, L1448C(N)) as having a 'different origin' directly alters D_obs and therefore the derived inclination and velocity.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The values of Delta V_obs in Table 1 are rounded to 20, 30, or 40 km s^-1 without quoted measurement uncertainties, so uncertainty in Delta V_obs is not propagated into i and V_j.
