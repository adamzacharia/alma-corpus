## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and continuum-subtract millimeter interferometric datasets**: For NOEMA (IRAS 21078+5211), calibrate and image in GILDAS (Clic/Mapping) with phase self-calibration on continuum (3 loops: 300, 150, 45 s) and uniform weighting, keeping non-self-calibrated cubes for lines at 0.5 km/s resolution. For ALMA (G035.02+0.35), restore calibration via CASA pipeline, image each SPW manually with TCLEAN (Hogbom, Briggs robust=0.5), and separate continuum and line emission in the image plane using STATCONT.  
  > "Calibration and imaging of the data was conducted within the GILDAS framework with Clic and Mapping." (NOEMA observations); "The image of each SPW (continuum plus line emission) was produced manually using the TCLEAN task, using Hogbom deconvolution (cite: Hog74) and Briggs weighting (cite: Bri95), with the robust parameter set to 0.5" (ALMA archive data); "For each SPW, to determine the continuum level of the spectra and subtract it from the line emission, we used STATCONT (cite: Sanc18), a statistical method of estimating the continuum level at each position of the map from the spectral distribution of the intensity at that position." (ALMA archive data)
   - software: GILDAS, Clic, Mapping, CASA, TCLEAN, STATCONT
   - parameter: NOEMA spectral resolution: 0.5 km s^-1; self-cal integration times: 300, 150, and 45 s; weighting: uniform  
  > "In our data reduction we employed a uniform spectral resolution of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$." (NOEMA observations); "The continuum data were self-calibrated within Mapping in phase-only in three loops with decreasing integration times of 300, 150 and 45 s. Imaging was then conducted in uniform weighting to achieve the highest spatial resolution." (NOEMA observations)
   - parameter: ALMA TCLEAN weighting: Briggs robust = 0.5; deconvolver: Hogbom  
  > "using the TCLEAN task, using Hogbom deconvolution (cite: Hog74) and Briggs weighting (cite: Bri95), with the robust parameter set to 0.5" (ALMA archive data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked whether applying continuum phase self-calibration solutions improved NOEMA spectral line cubes; found no clear improvement and retained non-self-calibrated line cubes.  
  > "While the self-calibration improved the continuum data significantly with an increase in the signal-to-noise ratio of the maps from 19 to 136, no clear improvement of the line data was recognizable after applying the gain solutions from the continuum phase self-calibration. Therefore, for the spectral lines, we used the non-self-calibrated data products." (NOEMA observations)

  > "To study the kinematics, we surveyed the spectral images and selected the molecular lines that satisfy two conditions: 1) being sufficiently intense to map an extended velocity range; 2) being representative of well-defined kinematic structures." (Structure and kinematics); "The gas distribution is traced with bright lines of CH$_3$CN, CH$_3$OH, HC$_3$N, and SO$_2$." (Structure and kinematics)

3. **Extract spectra and fit LTE synthetic spectra to derive gas physical conditions**: Integrate spectra over the lowest 1.3 mm continuum contour toward both YSOs (and two 0.2'' square outflow apertures in IRAS 21078+5211), identify unblended optically thin lines with MADCUBA SLIM, and fit LTE synthetic spectra using MADCUBA AUTOFIT.  
  > "For both IRAS 21078$+$5211 and G035.02$+$0.35, we derived the spectral emission in the direction of the YSO by integrating the signal over the area inside the lowest 1.3 mm continuum contour" (Physical conditions); "Using the SLIM (Spectral Line Identification and Modeling) tool of MADCUBA, we surveyed the spectra to search for molecular species with a relatively large number of unblended and optically thin lines" (Physical conditions); "To derive the physical parameters of molecular gas, we used the tool AUTOFIT of MADCUBA, which compares the observed spectra with the LTE synthetic spectra, taking into account all transitions and the line opacities." (Physical conditions)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM, AUTOFIT
   - parameter: Outflow extraction boxes in IRAS 21078+5211: 0.2'' square along axis at RA=21h09m21.691s, Dec=+52d22m36.76s and off-axis at RA=21h09m21.735s, Dec=+52d22m36.77s  
  > "1) along the outflow axis (inside the thick green square in Fig. 2 centered at RA(J2000) = $21^{\rm h} \, 09^{\rm m}$ 21$\rlap{.}^{\rm s}$691 and Dec(J2000) = $+52$$^{\circ}$ $22^{\prime}$ 36$\rlap{.}^{\prime\prime}$76, with size of 0.2$''$); 2) at a wide separation from the axis (inside the thick blue square in Fig. 2 centered at RA(J2000) = $21^{\rm h} \, 09^{\rm m}$ 21$\rlap{.}^{\rm s}$735 and Dec(J2000) = $+52$$^{\circ}$ $22^{\prime}$ 36$\rlap{.}^{\prime\prime}$77, with a size of 0.2$''$)." (Physical conditions)
   - parameter: 4 free parameters per species: column density (N_col), excitation temperature (T_ex), velocity (v_0), and line width (FWHM)  
  > "Leaving four parameters free to vary, that is, the column density ($N_{\rm col}$), excitation temperature ($T_{\rm ex}$), line $V_{\rm LSR}$ ($\varv_0$), and line width (FWHM), AUTOFIT provides the best nonlinear least-squares fit" (Physical conditions)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), single velocity component per molecular species, and negligible density and temperature gradients  
  > "To derive the physical parameters of molecular gas, we used the tool AUTOFIT of MADCUBA, which compares the observed spectra with the LTE synthetic spectra, taking into account all transitions and the line opacities. We warn that a single component was fit for each molecular species, under the assumption of negligible density and temperature gradients." (Physical conditions)
   - fitting: Nonlinear least-squares fit using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm; errors derived from the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix at the chi^2 minimum.

## Qualitative findings

- In IRAS 21078+5211, SO, SO2, HC3N, CH3CN, and CH3OH emissions are elongated along the radio jet axis with a transversal V_LSR gradient and radially nested widths (SO2 and HC3N narrowest near the axis, SO intermediate, CH3CN and CH3OH widest in a funnel/ring pattern).  
  > "In IRAS 21078$+$5211, the emissions of several molecules, particularly SO, SO$_2$, CH$_3$CN, and CH$_3$OH, are distributed along the axis of the previously identified radio jet, and present a local standard of rest (LSR) velocity ($V_{\rm LSR}$) gradient transversal to the jet axis." (Protostellar Outflows at the EarliesT Stages (POETS)); "The wind in IRAS 21078$+$5211 harbors many molecules with a clear radial distribution, going from SO$_2$ and HC$_3$N, found close to the jet axis, through SO, covering larger radii, to CH$_3$CN and CH$_3$OH, with the maximum width." (Conclusions)
- In G035.02+0.35, CH3CN, CH3OH, HC3N, and SO2 are confined to the rotating disk midplane, whereas SO is the only molecule extending well outside the disk into the wind.  
  > "In G035.02$+$0.35, the same molecules are instead distributed along the (sky-projected) major axis of the rotating disk reported previously, and their $V_{\rm LSR}$ gradients consistently trace the disk rotation. The corresponding PV plots present Keplerian profiles. SO is the only molecular species whose emission extends well outside the disk." (Protostellar Outflows at the EarliesT Stages (POETS))
- In both YSOs, SO shows a Keplerian-like rotation profile both at the disk midplane and at off-disk positions along the rotation axis, while SO2 is compact and shows the steepest velocity-position profile.  
  > "In both sources, the SO emission is one of the most radially extended and presents the clearest Keplerian-like velocity profile in the PV plots, while SO$_2$ is spatially compact and yields the steepest velocity-position profile." (Conclusions); "As in IRAS 21078$+$5211, also in G035.02$+$0.35 the PV plots of SO across the disk midplane and offset of the disk (see Fig. 10) are fully consistent with each other, which ensures that SO is tracing the rotation profile of the gas in the wind." (G035.02+0.35)
- SiO 5-4 emission is resolved out at the ~250 au scales of the interferometric observations.  
  > "For completeness, we note that the SiO 5–4 emission, another potential outflow tracer, is resolved out at these small scales." (Structure and kinematics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The NOEMA long-baseline dataset for IRAS 21078+5211 and the archival ALMA Cycle 4 Band 6 dataset for G035.02+0.35 overlap in frequency and provide matched linear (~250 au) and velocity (~0.5 km/s) resolutions, enabling a direct comparison of two YSOs of different mass (5.6 vs ~20 M_sun) representing the two POETS water maser kinematic configurations.  
  > "IRAS 21078$+$5211 has been recently reobserved with NOEMA at 1.3 mm using long baselines within the large program CORE (cite: Beu18; Gie21; Ahm23); for G035.02$+$0.35, ALMA Cycle 4 Band 6 archival observations are available. These two interferometric datasets overlap in frequency, and provide comparable linear ($\approx$250 au at the target distance) and velocity ($\approx$0.5 km s$^{-1}$) resolutions, which is optimal for a comparison of the gas kinematics between the two sources." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5 was chosen as a compromise between angular resolution and sensitivity to extended emission; STATCONT was used to statistically determine continuum levels per pixel; MADCUBA AUTOFIT was used to fit LTE synthetic spectra across multiple optically thin transitions spanning a wide range of excitation energies.  
  > "with the robust parameter set to 0.5 as a compromise between resolution and sensitivity to extended emission." (ALMA archive data); "to determine the continuum level of the spectra and subtract it from the line emission, we used STATCONT (cite: Sanc18), a statistical method of estimating the continuum level at each position of the map from the spectral distribution of the intensity at that position." (ALMA archive data); "We selected the CH$_3$CN, CH$_3$OH, and SO$_2$ molecules (see Table 3) that in both targets have optical depths of less (or much less) than 0.25, and cover a sufficiently wide range in excitation energy to allow for a reliable estimate of the excitation temperature." (Physical conditions)
- Alternative: Gravitationally bound rotation of the ambient YSO envelope as the cause of the transversal V_LSR gradient in IRAS 21078+5211 (rejected because it requires a central mass of ~44 M_sun, far exceeding the measured 5.6 +/- 2 M_sun).  
  > "We can reasonably rule out that the observed $V_{\rm LSR}$ gradient transversal to the jet axis is due to the rotation of the ambient gas induced by the gravitational force of the YSO." (IRAS 21078+5211); "Therefore, we can conclude that the observed $V_{\rm LSR}$ gradient is by far too large to correspond to the rotation of the YSO envelope, and has to be linked instead with the rotation of the wind." (IRAS 21078+5211)
- Alternative: Compact X-wind entraining ambient gas (rejected because it cannot explain the nested radial chemical differentiation in IRAS 21078+5211 or the absence of all observed disk molecules except SO in the wind of G035.02+0.35).  
  > "In G035.02$+$0.35 the protostellar wind is traced by SO emission only. That already rules out the possibility that we are observing ambient gas entrained in a fast wide-angle X-wind as there is no reasonable explanation why all the other observed molecular emissions would not be present in the wind." (G035.02+0.35)
- Alternative: Photoevaporated disk wind in G035.02+0.35 (rejected because water maser velocities >= 20 km/s exceed typical photoevaporative wind speeds of <= 10 km/s, and radio continuum emission is orders of magnitude below the ZAMS Lyman continuum level).  
  > "The high maser velocities, $\ge$20 km s$^{-1}$, favor a MHD DW rather than a photoevaporated DW, whose typical speeds are $\le$10 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: Hol94; Ale14)." (G035.02+0.35)
