  > "using Hogbom deconvolution (cite: Hog74) and Briggs weighting (cite: Bri95), with the robust parameter set to 0.5" (2.2 ALMA archive data)

   - assumption: Spectral distribution of intensity allows statistical determination of line-free continuum level  
  > "STATCONT (cite: Sanc18), a statistical method of estimating the continuum level at each position of the map from the spectral distribution of the intensity at that position." (2.2 ALMA archive data)

3. **Extract spectra and fit physical parameters of molecular gas**: Extract spectra over the area inside the lowest 1.3 mm continuum contour; identify unblended lines using SLIM; perform LTE synthetic spectrum fitting using AUTOFIT with the Levenberg-Marquardt nonlinear least-squares algorithm.  
  > "we derived the spectral emission in the direction of the YSO by integrating the signal over the area inside the lowest 1.3 mm continuum contour" (3.2 Physical conditions); "Using the SLIM (Spectral Line Identification and Modeling) tool of MADCUBA, we surveyed the spectra to search for molecular species with a relatively large number of unblended and optically thin lines" (3.2 Physical conditions); "To derive the physical parameters of molecular gas, we used the tool AUTOFIT of MADCUBA, which compares the observed spectra with the LTE synthetic spectra, taking into account all transitions and the line opacities." (3.2 Physical conditions); "Leaving four parameters free to vary, that is, the column density (Ncol), excitation temperature (Tex), line VLSR (v0), and line width (FWHM), AUTOFIT provides the best nonlinear least-squares fit using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm" (3.2 Physical conditions)
   - software: MADCUBA
   - parameter: 4 free parameters: Ncol, Tex, v0, FWHM  
  > "Leaving four parameters free to vary, that is, the column density (Ncol), excitation temperature (Tex), line VLSR (v0), and line width (FWHM)" (3.2 Physical conditions)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and single-component emission with negligible density and temperature gradients  
  > "We warn that a single component was fit for each molecular species, under the assumption of negligible density and temperature gradients." (3.2 Physical conditions); "AUTOFIT of MADCUBA, which compares the observed spectra with the LTE synthetic spectra" (3.2 Physical conditions)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt nonlinear least-squares fit; nominal errors derived from diagonal elements of the covariance matrix at chi^2 minimum  
  > "AUTOFIT provides the best nonlinear least-squares fit using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm (cite: Lev44; Mar63). Nominal errors of the fit parameters were derived from the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix calculated at the χ2 minimum." (3.2 Physical conditions)
   - validation: Inspection of LTE synthetic spectrum overlays on observed spectra (Figs. 6, 7, 8) and checking line optical depths (tau < 0.25)  
  > "We selected the CH3CN, CH3OH, and SO2 molecules (see Table 3) that in both targets have optical depths of less (or much less) than 0.25" (3.2 Physical conditions); "The emission profile of each transition is reasonably well fit with MADCUBA, determining the values of the column density, temperature, velocity, and line width listed in Table 3."   > "at distances of ∼100 au from the disk midplane of YSOs of ∼10 M⊙, we can expect that a magneto-centrifugal wind displays a Keplerian rotation profile comparable to that of the gas rotating in the disk." (4.1 Magneto-centrifugal winds)

## Qualitative findings

- In G035.02+0.35, CH3CN, CH3OH, and SO2 emissions are distributed along the disk major axis tracing Keplerian rotation, while SO extends outside the disk into spurs parallel to the rotation axis.  
  > "In G035.02+0.35, the same molecules are instead distributed along the (sky-projected) major axis of the rotating disk reported previously, and their VLSR gradients consistently trace the disk rotation. The corresponding PV plots present Keplerian profiles. SO is the only molecular species whose emission extends well outside the disk." (abstract)
- In IRAS 21078+5211, molecular emissions (SO, SO2, CH3CN, CH3OH) are elongated along the jet axis and show a transverse velocity gradient consistent with a rotating magneto-centrifugal wind rather than envelope rotation.  
  > "In IRAS 21078+5211, the emissions of several molecules, particularly SO, SO2, CH3CN, and CH3OH, are distributed along the axis of the previously identified radio jet, and present a local standard of rest (LSR) velocity (VLSR) gradient transversal to the jet axis." (abstract); "Therefore, we can conclude that the observed VLSR gradient is by far too large to correspond to the rotation of the YSO envelope, and has to be linked instead with the rotation of the wind." (4.1.1 IRAS 21078+5211)
- The lack of molecular wind emission besides SO in G035.02+0.35 rules out ambient entrainment by an X-wind and indicates that wind material stems from a warm, shock-ionized disk atmosphere where molecules other than SO are dissociated.  
  > "In G035.02+0.35 the protostellar wind is traced by SO emission only. That already rules out the possibility that we are observing ambient gas entrained in a fast wide-angle X-wind as there is no reasonable explanation why all the other observed molecular emissions would not be present in the wind." (4.2.2 G035.02+0.35); "can be well explained in terms of a radially extended MHD DW, rather than a compact X-wind." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Archival ALMA Cycle 4 Band 6 data and NOEMA long-baseline data overlap in frequency and provide matched linear resolution (~250 au) and velocity resolution (~0.5 km s^-1) for direct comparison of two prototypes from the POETS survey.  
  > "These two interferometric datasets overlap in frequency, and provide comparable linear (≈250 au at the target distance) and velocity (≈0.5 km s−1) resolutions, which is optimal for a comparison of the gas kinematics between the two sources." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Briggs weighting (robust = 0.5) balances spatial resolution with sensitivity to extended emission; STATCONT separates continuum from complex line emission statistically; MADCUBA fits multi-transition LTE spectra with opacity corrections.  
  > "with the robust parameter set to 0.5 as a compromise between resolution and sensitivity to extended emission." (2.2 ALMA archive data); "STATCONT (cite: Sanc18), a statistical method of estimating the continuum level at each position of the map from the spectral distribution of the intensity at that position." (2.2 ALMA archive data); "AUTOFIT of MADCUBA, which compares the observed spectra with the LTE synthetic spectra, taking into account all transitions and the line opacities." (3.2 Physical conditions)
- Alternative: Rotation of ambient gas envelope: rejected for IRAS 21078+5211 because the observed transverse velocity gradient would require an unphysical central mass of ~44 M_sun, vastly exceeding the known 5.6 M_sun disk mass.  
  > "We can reasonably rule out that the observed VLSR gradient transversal to the jet axis is due to the rotation of the ambient gas induced by the gravitational force of the YSO." (4.1.1 IRAS 21078+5211); "Therefore, we can conclude that the observed VLSR gradient is by far too large to correspond to the rotation of the YSO envelope, and has to be linked instead with the rotation of the wind." (4.1.1 IRAS 21078+5211)
- Alternative: X-wind scenario: rejected because ambient material entrained in a wide-angle X-wind would entrain all disk molecules, which contradicts the observation that only SO is found in G035.02+0.35's wind.  
  > "In G035.02+0.35 the protostellar wind is traced by SO emission only. That already rules out the possibility that we are observing ambient gas entrained in a fast wide-angle X-wind as there is no reasonable explanation why all the other observed molecular emissions would not be present in the wind." (4.2.2 G035.02+0.35)
- Alternative: Photoevaporated disk wind: rejected for G035.02+0.35 because water maser velocities (>20 km s^-1) greatly exceed typical photoevaporation wind speeds (<=10 km s^-1).  
  > "The high maser velocities, ≥20 km s−1, favor a MHD DW rather than a photoevaporated DW, whose typical speeds are ≤10 km s−1 (cite: Hol94; Ale14)." (4.2.2 G035.02+0.35)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single-component LTE assumption in MADCUBA fitting, assuming negligible temperature and density gradients along the line of sight.  
  > "We warn that a single component was fit for each molecular species, under the assumption of negligible density and temperature gradients." (3.2 Physical conditions)
- Limitation (authors): Insufficient angular resolution to resolve the vertical structure of the disk and separate neutral disk midplane from ionized atmospheric layers.  
  > "Figure 3 shows that the emissions of the molecular lines, dust, and free-free continuum are basically overlapping in the disk. That is certainly due to insufficient angular resolution to resolve the disk structure vertically, since ionized and neutral gas cannot be cospatial." (4.1.2 G035.02+0.35)
- Limitation (authors): SiO 5-4 line is resolved out at the observed high angular resolution.  
  > "For completeness, we note that the SiO 5–4 emission, another potential outflow tracer, is resolved out at these small scales." (3.1 Structure and kinematics)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) for multiple molecular species in shocked wind environments could bias derived excitation temperatures and column densities.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The physical separation between wind and disk kinematics in PV cuts relies on spatial averaging perpendicular to the cut, which might carry minor contamination from disk rotation.
