  > "Moment 8 (maximum intensity) and moment 1 (peak velocity) maps were produced using the velocity range of ±10 km s−1 with respect to the systemic velocity of 5.5 km s−1. 3. **Derive excitation temperatures, column densities, and fractional abundances under LTE**: Use two SO transitions to determine T_ex and N(SO) under LTE and optically thin assumptions, then use T_ex(SO) to calculate N(SiO) and N(C18O), and convert N(C18O) to N(H2) for fractional abundances  
  > "First, we analyzed the two SO transitions to determine the excitation temperatures, Tex, and column densities, N, assuming LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium) and optically thin conditions."    - assumption: LTE and optically thin conditions  
  > "First, we analyzed the two SO transitions to determine the excitation temperatures, Tex, and column densities, N, assuming LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium) and optically thin conditions." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
   - assumption: SiO and C18O share the same excitation temperature as SO and are emitted from the same gas  
  > "The derived molecular abundances rely on the hypothesis that the excitation temperatures of the different molecules are similar, and adopting the excitation temperature of SO is a simplifying hypothesis." (4.3. Molecular abundances); "As was mentioned above, we simply assume that SiO, SO, and C18O are emitted from the same gas." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
   - assumption: N[C18O]/N[H2] = 1.7 x 10^-7  
  > "C18O was used to estimate the H2 column densities, assuming the C18O/H2 ratio of 1.7×10−7 (Frerking et al. 1982)." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
   - fitting: Best fit analysis under LTE approximation  
  > "the excitation temperature were derived from the best fit analysis." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity of N(SiO) to assumed T_ex = 50 K and 100 K (changes by less than a factor of 2), and validated against non-LTE LVG analysis (consistent within 10-20% except for W-blob)  
  > "Thus, the possible uncertainties in the SiO column densities caused by the assumed excitation temperatures would be smaller than a factor of 2." (4.3. Molecular abundances); "To check the validity of the derived column densities and excitation temperatures, we also conducted a non-LTE analysis, which is discussed in Appendix D. Except for the results of W-blob, the derived column densities and excitation temperatures are consistent within 10%–20% (Tables 3 and D.1)." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
4. **Perform non-LTE LVG radiative transfer analysis of SO lines**: Large-velocity gradient (LVG) modeling to estimate SO excitation temperatures, optical depths, column density, and H2 density by least-squares analysis  
  > "We employed the LVG (large-velocity gradient) model (Goldreich & Kwan 1974) to estimate SO excitation temperatures Tex, optical depths τ, column density N[SO], and H2 density n[H2] for each position by a least-squares analysis." (Appendix D: Non-LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Kinetic temperatures T_kin = 20, 30, 40, and 50 K; SO energy levels up to N_J = 10_10 (E_u = 127 K)

   - assumption: Large-velocity gradient (LVG) approximation with para-H2 collision rates  
  > "We employed the LVG (large-velocity gradient) model (Goldreich & Kwan 1974) to estimate SO excitation temperatures Tex, optical depths τ, column density N[SO], and H2 density n[H2] for each position by a least-squares analysis." (Appendix D: Non-LTE analysis)

  > "We employed the LVG (large-velocity gradient) model (Goldreich & Kwan 1974) to estimate SO excitation temperatures Tex, optical depths τ, column density N[SO], and H2 density n[H2] for each position by a least-squares analysis." (Appendix D: Non-LTE analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- Two central SiO peaks are identified: SiO-peak (~100 au NE of continuum peak, blueshifted with broad linewidth ~5.5 km s^-1) and SiO-N (~200 au N of continuum peak, near systemic velocity with narrow linewidth ~1.4 km s^-1).  
  > "Two SiO emission peaks are identified in the central part of L483, which have offsets of 100 au and 200 au toward the northeast (SiO-peak) and north (SiO-N), respectively, from the continuum peak." (abstract); "The SiO-peak shows only blueshifted emission with a broad linewidth of 5 km s−1, while that of SiO-N corresponds to the systemic velocity." (abstract)
- Weak, compact SiO emission components with narrow linewidths (~1 km s^-1) are distributed up to 2400 au from the continuum peak, including the isolated blueshifted NE-cloud outside the E-W outflow lobes.  
  > "Furthermore, weak and compact SiO emission components are distributed up to 2400 au away from the continuum position. They have narrow linewidths of ∼1 km s−1." (abstract); "One of these components is a blueshifted isolated emission feature, 2400 au northeast of the continuum peak, NE-cloud, located outside the east-west outflow lobes." (abstract)
- SiO in the central region shows asymmetric spatial and velocity structures distinct from SO, CS, and C18O, which trace the rotating-infalling envelope.  
  > "In contrast, the PV diagram of the SiO line shows an asymmetric pattern with emission only toward the northern side." (4.1. SiO emission close to the continuum peak); "The difference of PV diagrams between SiO and the other molecules suggests that SiO is probably tracing a shocked gas region not associated with the rotating envelope." (4.1. SiO emission close to the continuum peak)
- Central SiO emission can be explained by either two outflows driven by both binary protostars or a single outflow from one circumstellar disk (via cavity wall, rotating base, reorientation, misalignment, or precession).  
  > "The central SiO emission could be interpreted as either two different outflows driven by both protostars or as an outflow ejected from one of the circumstellar disks in the binary system." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: FAUST multiple-array-configuration observations provide both ~50 au high spatial resolution and high sensitivity to extended structures up to ~1000 au scales, recovering weak and compact SiO components missed by previous observations.  
  > "Thanks to high-sensitivity observations using multiple array configurations, FAUST data can reveal not only envelopes and disks on scales of 50 au, but also more extended structures on ∼1000 au scales covering wide spatial ranges of outflows." (1. Introduction); "present observations could recover weak and compact SiO emission components widely distributed at a scale of 1000 au thanks to the higher-sensitivity images with multiple array configurations." (2. The protobinary system L483)
- Why this method: SiO and SO are used as shock tracers, CS as a known outflow tracer in L483, and C18O as a standard dense-gas column density tracer; moment 8 (maximum intensity) maps are used alongside moment 0 because weak narrow-line components are much clearer in peak intensity.  
  > "The SO lines were selected as good shock tracers which are similar to SiO (Podio et al. 2021; Codella et al. 2024), and CS is known to trace the outflow in L483 (Oya et al. 2017, 2018). C18O, on the other hand, is generally used as a standard molecule that traces the gas column density." (1. Introduction); "These weak SiO emission components are marginally seen in the integrated intensity (moment 0) map, while they are significant in the maximum intensity (moment 8) image (right panel of Fig. 2)." (4.2. Localized SiO distribution in and around the outflow)
- Alternative: Different shock dissipation levels explaining the linewidth difference between SiO-peak and SiO-N was considered and ruled out because the SiO-N dynamical timescale (<2000 yr) is shorter than the shock dissipation timescale (3000 yr).  
  > "It may also be possible that the different linewidths could be attributed to different levels of shock dissipation (Sect. 5.2). However, given the shorter dynamical timescales of <2000 yr for SiO-N than that of the shock dissipation of 3000 yr, this possibility may be ruled out." (5.1.6. (f) Twin outflows)
- Alternative: Pole-on outflow geometry (i < 45 deg) for SiO-peak/SiO-N and NE-cloud was rejected based on the lack of spatial overlap between SiO-peak and SiO-N and the small radial velocity of NE-cloud.  
  > "Because one can see no overlap of the SiO-peak and SiO-N positions with the separation of 100 au, it is unlikely that the outflow orientation would be close to a pole-on geometry (i.e., i < 45 degrees)." (5.1.3. (c) Reorientation of outflow); "As was discussed for the central SiO-peak and SiO-N emission (Sect. 5.1), the small radial velocities of the NE-cloud region could rule out the possibility of a pole-on geometry (i.e., i < 45 degrees)." (5.2. Possible origins of NE-cloud)

- Limitation (authors): Some SiO emission components are comparable to the synthesized beam and may be unresolved, which can lower brightness temperatures and underestimate excitation temperatures.  
  > "Thus, some of the SiO components could be unresolved with the present observations." (4.1. SiO emission close to the continuum peak); "This would suggest that the SO emission components of the continuum, SiO-peak, and SiO-N positions could be unresolved with our observations (Sect. 4.1) that yields lower brightness temperatures, and hence underestimate the excitation temperatures." (5.1.8. Summary and caveats of interpretations)
- Limitation (authors): Single-Gaussian fitting is not valid for the double-peaked C18O profile at the continuum peak.  
  > "At the continuum peak, a single Gaussian fitting is not valid for C18O, as its line profile is double-peaked. Hence, the fit for C18O does not provide accurate line parameters." (4.1. SiO emission close to the continuum peak)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming SiO, SO, and C18O share the same excitation temperature and trace the same gas is a simplifying hypothesis, as they show different spatial distributions and line profiles.  
  > "However, only one line was observed for SiO and C18O, and therefore we have no information about their excitation conditions." (4.3. Molecular abundances); "Furthermore, the assumption that all of SiO, SO, and C18O are emitted from the same gas is not always valid as they show different spatial distributions in the moment maps (Figs. 2, 4 –7) and different line profiles even toward the same positions (Fig. 9)." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
- Limitation (authors): Extended C18O emission at the NE-cloud may be resolved out by the interferometer, causing the upper limit on C18O column density to be underestimated and the lower limits on SiO/H2 and SO/H2 to be artificially high.  
  > "Because the CO distribution could be more extended than the maximum recoverable scale, the derived C18O abundance (upper limit) could be significantly affected by missing flux of the C18O line, which is possibly resolved out with the present ALMA observations." (4.3. Molecular abundances)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow launching radii (<20 au) and circumstellar/circumbinary disks are unresolved at the current ~50 au angular resolution, and the true 3D binary separation and orbital/outflow inclinations are unknown.  
  > "Even the largest estimated launching radius of 20 au (0.1′′) could not be resolved with the present spectral line images." (5.1.8. Summary and caveats of interpretations); "It should also be noted that the projected separation of 34 au would give a lower limit of the current separation and the actual separation and inclination of the binary orbit are unknown, and hence the estimated quantities such as the sizes and timescales of this protobinary system would have large uncertainties." (5.1.8. Summary and caveats of interpretations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Setup 1 (containing SiO 5-4, SO 5_6-4_5, and C18O 2-1) lacks ACA 7m data (MRS = 9.4''), whereas Setup 2 (containing SO 6_6-5_5 and CS 5-4) includes ACA 7m data (MRS = 27''), and the two SO cubes have slightly different beam sizes (0.39'' x 0.31'' vs 0.46'' x 0.33'') without stated beam matching prior to line ratio and excitation temperature calculations.
