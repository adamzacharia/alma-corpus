## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and extract 2.1 GHz radio continuum flux densities from ATCA observations**: Automatic flagging with PGFLAG, standard 16 cm calibration, uv-combination with UVAVER, self-calibration, Briggs-weighted (robust=0.5) continuum imaging in Miriad, and conversion from 2.1 GHz to 1.4 GHz using a power-law spectral index  
  > "Data editing, calibration and imaging is performed using Miriad ((cite: Sault1995))." (ATCA follow-up); "Flux densities ($S_{\rm image}$) and image noise ($\sigma_{\rm image}$) are then extracted from the Briggs-weighted continuum images with robust factor of 0.5." (ATCA follow-up); "For later use we convert the ATCA flux densities from $2.1$ GHz to $1.4$ GHz assuming a power law spectrum $S_{\nu}=\nu ^{\alpha}$ with average radio spectral index $\alpha=-0.7\pm 0.14$, consistently with (cite: Stacey2018)." (ATCA follow-up)
   - software: Miriad
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "Flux densities ($S_{\rm image}$) and image noise ($\sigma_{\rm image}$) are then extracted from the Briggs-weighted continuum images with robust factor of 0.5." (ATCA follow-up)
   - parameter: 5% calibration error added in quadrature to image noise  
  > "\delta S_{\rm image} = \sqrt{(\sigma_{\rm image})^2 + (0.05 \times S_{\rm image})^2}" (2.2 ATCA follow-up)
   - assumption: Power-law radio spectrum S_nu = nu^alpha with alpha = -0.7 +/- 0.14  
  > "For later use we convert the ATCA flux densities from $2.1$ GHz to $1.4$ GHz assuming a power law spectrum $S_{\nu}=\nu ^{\alpha}$ with average radio spectral index $\alpha=-0.7\pm 0.14$, consistently with (cite: Stacey2018)." (ATCA follow-up)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Manual checking of RFI removal  
  > "Manual checking confirms that all the major RFI features are removed from the data, flagging more than 30% of the data, but still recovering a 0.06 mJy beam$^{-1}$ theoretical noise level (1$\sigma$)." (ATCA follow-up)
2. **Cross-match H-ATLAS candidates with FIRST, ATCA, and literature radio data using NIR and archival ALMA high-resolution imaging**: Cross-match within 10 arcsec of H-ATLAS position in FIRST catalogue/maps and ATCA maps for >3 sigma detections, selecting the component nearest to the SPIRE peak that overlaps with NIR (HST, UKIDSS, VIKING) or archival ALMA Band 4/6/7 continuum emission  
  > "The cross-match with the FIRST catalogue within 10 arcsec of the Herschel-ATLAS position yields 8 potential counterparts, while for 12 objects we find a clear $\gtrsim 3\,\sigma$ signal in the FIRST maps, within the H-ATLAS beamsize." (FIRST catalogue counterparts); "we selected radio detections with at least $> 3\sigma_{\rm image}$ confidence level and then consider their position with respect to the optical/IR and the mm high angular resolution imaging." (Counterparts selection); "We consider as a counterpart only the component nearest to the SPIRE peak position, whose radio detected emission overlaps with sources detected the in NIR or mm" (Counterparts selection)
   - parameter: Detection threshold > 3 sigma (and 3 sigma upper limits for non-detections)  
  > "For the remaining objects we define an upper limit for detection at 3$\sigma$ from the noise at the position of the sources in the FIRST images and as 3$\sigma_{\rm image}$ for ATCA images." (Counterparts selection)
   - assumption: Observed SPIRE flux density is mostly originated by the lensed object with negligible contamination from nearby sources in the Herschel beam  
  > "Here we assume that the observed SPIRE flux density is mostly originated by the same object and that no strong FIR contamination from possible nearby sources is present." (Counterparts selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked consistency of unconfirmed lensed sources with confirmed lensed galaxies and Stacey et al. (2018, 2019) in the q_FIR-L_1.4GHz relation  
  > "Finally, our assumption that the FIR luminosity is not strongly affected by nearby objects entering the Herschel beam is reasonable. If not, the trend in Figure 5 should show a clear offset with respect to the confirmed lensed galaxies and/or with the points from (cite: Stacey2018; Stacey2019)." ## Qualitative findings

- Weak yet appreciable decline of the FIR-to-radio luminosity ratio q_FIR with increasing redshift out to z <= 4  
  > "By taking advantage of source brightness possibly enhanced by lensing magnification, we identify a weak evolution with redshift out to $z\lesssim 4$ of the FIR-to-radio luminosity ratio $q_{\rm FIR}$." (Abstract); "We find a weak yet appreciable decline of $q_{\rm FIR}$ with increasing $z$." (The FIR-radio correlation for (candidate) lensed galaxies)
- Clear decreasing trend of q_FIR with increasing 1.4 GHz radio luminosity, covering a star-formation-dominated region (~90% with q_FIR > 1.8) complementary to optically/radio-selected lensed quasars  
  > "We find a clear tendency of the $q_{\rm FIR}$ parameter to decrease with increasing radio power." (The FIR-radio correlation for (candidate) lensed galaxies); "Most relevantly, the majority of our sources lie ($\sim 90 \%$) in the region where radio emission is dominated by star formation, thus complementing the radio/optical selection adopted in (cite: Stacey2018) and actually extending to lower radio luminosities a consistent $q_{\rm FIR}$ vs. radio luminosity relation." (The FIR-radio correlation for (candidate) lensed galaxies)
- The observed q_FIR vs L_1.4GHz behaviour agrees with an in-situ galaxy and BH co-evolution scenario transitioning from an early dust-obscured star-forming phase to a late radio-loud quasar phase  
  > "We interpret such a behavior in the framework of an in-situ galaxy formation scenario, as a result of the transition from an early dust-obscured star-forming phase (mainly pinpointed by our FIR selection) to a late radio-loud quasar phase (preferentially sampled by the optical/radio selection)." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: FIR/submm selection with strong gravitational lensing magnification allows probing high-z dusty star-forming galaxies with minimal dust obscuration or foreground lens contamination, and high-resolution NIR/ALMA images enable reliable radio counterpart identification.  
  > "In FIR/submm bands, high-z lensed dusty galaxies are particularly bright, while a negligible signal comes from the foreground lens, which is often a massive evolved elliptical at $z\lesssim 1$." (Introduction); "in order to establish whether a radio source is correctly assigned as a counterpart of our selected Herschel sample, we selected radio detections with at least $> 3\sigma_{\rm image}$ confidence level and then consider their position with respect to the optical/IR and the mm high angular resolution imaging." (Counterparts selection)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Excluded H-ATLASJ090311.6+003907 (SDP.81) a priori because its radio emission likely originates from the AGN hosted by the foreground lens rather than the background lensed galaxy  
  > "However, its radio emission is likely to be originated from the AGN hosted by the foreground lens ((cite: Tamura2015a), (cite: Rybak2020)); for this reason it has been excluded a priori as a possible counterpart." (FIRST catalogue counterparts)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution of ATCA and FIRST radio observations (~5-10 arcsec) is insufficient to resolve lensed arcs  
  > "The detected sources display a variety of radio morphologies: some of them show a compact radio emission, others instead feature a more extended structure or even multiple components, but the angular resolution of the observations is not enough to resolve any of the arcs possibly associated to lensing effects." (Counterparts selection)
- Limitation (authors): Selecting only the radio component nearest to the SPIRE peak may underestimate radio luminosity for multi-component radio sources  
  > "We consider as a counterpart only the component nearest to the SPIRE peak position, whose radio detected emission overlaps with sources detected the in NIR or mm, even though this can result in a underestimation of the radio luminosity in case of multiple component radio sources." (Counterparts selection)
- Limitation (authors): Known positional offset between ALMA and HST/WFC3 counterparts is not corrected in the cutout maps  
  > "Please note that the HST/WFC3 maps reported here do not take into account the well known positional offset between ALMA and HST counterparts ((cite: Dunlop2016), (cite: Rujopakarn2016))." (Counterparts selection)
- Limitation (authors): For unconfirmed lensing candidates lacking lens models or high-resolution radio data, the fraction of radio and SPIRE emission associated with the lensed object or multiple ALMA sources in the Herschel beam cannot be confirmed, and luminosities are not corrected for differential magnification  
  > "for the remaining unconfirmed ones in absence of an accurate lens modelling and/or higher angular resolution observations, it is not possible to confirm whether or in which fraction the radio emission is associated to the lensed object." (Counterparts selection); "Indeed, as showed in Figures 1 and 2, multiple sources entering the Herschel beam are detected in the ALMA (sub-)mm maps, leading to a possible overestimation of the effective FIR luminosity of the actual counterpart." (Counterparts selection)
