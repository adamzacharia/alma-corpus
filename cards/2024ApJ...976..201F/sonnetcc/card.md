## Analysis recipe

1. **Fit Sersic profile to CO intensity maps**: Moment maps from a dilated mask, then a 2D Sersic model convolved with the synthesized beam fitted to the CO intensity map  
  > "The mask was initiated at $\geq$5$\sigma$ peaks in the data cube and expanded to include the surrounding 2$\sigma$ contour." (Distribution of the Molecular Gas); "and convolved with the synthesized beam to produce the line-intensity map." (Distribution of the Molecular Gas)
   - software: maskmoment, Astropy, emcee
   - parameter: Six free parameters: I_e, n, R_e, photometric PA, b/a and the centre (x0, y0)  
  > "This two-dimensional model includes six free parameters" (Distribution of the Molecular Gas)
   - assumption: Sersic profile adequate for the CO distribution despite clumpy or ring-like structure; dynamical masses considered insensitive to this  
  > "However, we note that our dynamical model, in particular, the derived stellar mass, is not sensitive to this issue." (Distribution of the Molecular Gas)
   - fitting: emcee affine-invariant ensemble MCMC; log-likelihood compares observed surface brightness per pixel with the model, using the pixel noise  
  > "which employs the affine-invariant ensemble sampler for Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling." (Distribution of the Molecular Gas); "comparing the observed surface brightness at each pixel $z_i$ (with associated noise $\sigma_i$) to the model value $z_i^m$." (Distribution of the Molecular Gas)
   - validation: Residual images shown in Figure 2; authors note the Sersic model hardly fully describes some maps  
  > "the Sérsic model hardly fully describes the CO intensity map" (Distribution of the Molecular Gas)
2. **Fit disk kinematics of CO and Halpha cubes**: 3DBarolo tilted-ring fit in two stages, with regularisation in the second stage  
  > "which regards rotating disk as tilted rings and fit each ring independently" (Gas kinematics); "We employed regularization with a constant function for the kinematic center, the systemic velocity, the inclination, and the position angle" (Gas kinematics)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: Ring width set to the beam FWHM (seeing limit for optical IFU data)  
  > "The ring width was set to the FWHM of the beam size (for optical IFU data, we adopted the seeing limit as the beam size) in the fitting process." (Gas kinematics)
   - parameter: First step: kinematic centre, systemic velocity, rotation velocity, dispersion, PA and inclination free; second stage fits only rotation velocity and dispersion  
  > "In the first step of fitting, we allowed the kinematic center, systematic velocity, rotation velocity, velocity dispersion, position angle, and inclination angle to be free parameters." (Gas kinematics); "which results in only two free parameters, the rotation velocity and velocity dispersion, being fitted in this second stage" (Gas kinematics)
   - parameter: Initial PA and inclination from the literature  
  > "The initial estimates for the position angles and inclination angles were adopted from the literature (cite: Bolatto+2017; Shangguan+2020a)." (Gas kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual velocity maps small compared with observed velocities  
  > "The residuals are small compared to the total observed velocities (on average about 10%)." (Non-circular motions)
3. **Correct rotation velocities for asymmetric drift**: Simultaneous fit of gas surface density and velocity dispersion profiles to estimate the gas pressure, following Iorio+2017  
  > "To precisely calculate the asymmetric drift correction, we utilized the methodology proposed by (cite: Iorio+2017), which involves simultaneously fitting the gas surface density and velocity dispersion profiles to estimate the gas pressure." (Asymmetric Drift Correction)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - assumption: Isotropic velocity dispersion  
  > "denotes the velocity dispersion, assumed to be isotropic" (Asymmetric Drift Correction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Circular velocities from cold and hot gas agree within uncertainties  
  > "we found that the circular velocities derived from cold and hot gas are the same, taking into account the uncertainties." (Gas kinematics)
4. **Fit multicomponent mass model to the rotation curve**: Circular velocity from stellar bulge, stellar disk, molecular gas disk and NFW halo computed with AGAMA and fitted to the 3DBarolo rotation curve  
  > "we consider four main components contributing to the circular motion of gas within the galaxies: the stellar bulge, the stellar disk, the molecular gas disk, and the dark matter halo." (Dynamical modeling); "We derive the circular velocities by employing the action-based galaxy modelling architecture (AGAMA; (cite: Vasiliev2019))." (Circular velocity derivations)
   - software: AGAMA, emcee
   - parameter: At least 8 free parameters; Gaussian priors from HST fits on bulge/disk radii and Sersic indices; stellar-to-halo mass ratio uniform from -2 to -1.5; halo concentration scatter 0.2 dex; alpha_CO Gaussian prior at 3.1 with 0.3 dex  
  > "At least 8 parameters are used to fit the rotation curves." (Best-fit Dynamical modeling and Results); "we applied Gaussian priors to the effective radius of the stellar bulges and stellar disks, as well as to the Sérsic index of the bulge and disks." (Best-fit Dynamical modeling and Results); "we implemented a Gaussian prior centered at $3.1\,M_\odot\,\left(\rm K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{-2}\right)^{-1}$ with an uncertainty of 0.3 dex" (Best-fit Dynamical modeling and Results)
   - parameter: Innermost and outermost rotation-curve points excluded  
  > "we omit the innermost data points, where complex gas kinematics may be present" (Dynamical modeling)
   - assumption: HI and ionized gas ignored; SMBH contribution neglected; bulge treated as spheroidal  
  > "We disregard the HI gas component due to its typically much larger spatial extent compared to stars and molecular gas" (Dynamical modeling); "Similarly, the contribution from the SMBH is not taken into account." (Dynamical modeling); "we treat the stellar bulge component as spheroidal, thus neglecting its asymmetry" (Circular velocity derivations)
   - fitting: emcee: 400 walkers, 1000 steps after 500-step burn-in, MAP values restart a second identical round, first 500 steps discarded; best fit = 50th percentile, errors from 16th and 84th percentiles  
  > "We initiate the MCMC sampling process with 400 walkers, each taking 1000 steps, following a burn-in phase of 500 steps." (Best-fit Dynamical modeling and Results); "We utilize the 50th percentile of the MCMC samples as the best-fit results and assess the uncertainties of the parameters by calculating the 16th and 84th percentiles of the MCMC samples." (Best-fit Dynamical modeling and Results)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare dynamical and literature stellar masses**: Linear fit with slope fixed to 1 and a linmix fit between dynamical and literature stellar masses of the EDGE-CALIFA sample; Salpeter to Kroupa conversion by a factor 1.5  
  > "we fit the linear relationship between the stellar masses derived from the two methods of the EDGE-CALIFA sample while fixing the slope to 1." (Mass comparison); "we apply a scaling factor of 1.5 to recalibrate the stellar mass determinations for inactive galaxies" (Mass comparison)
   - software: linmix
   - assumption: IMF difference handled by a factor of 1.5 (Kennicutt+2012)  
  > "effecting a transition from results predicated upon the Salpeter IMF to the Kroupa IMF (cite: Kennicutt+2012)" (Mass comparison)
   - fitting: linmix Bayesian linear regression  
  > "Python package to model their relationship" (Mass comparison)
   - validation: Derived masses consistent with literature within 0.3 dex  
  > "Our derived stellar masses are consistent with those provided by the literature after applying the IMF calibration, within the confines of a 0.3 dex uncertainty." (Mass comparison)
6. **Derive pixel-wise intrinsic velocity dispersion and gas scale height**: Moment 2 map minus 3DBarolo-simulated beam-smearing dispersion in quadrature, then thermal broadening added back; hg per pixel from equating weight and kinetic pressure  
  > "The simulated velocity dispersion is then subtracted in quadrature from the observed moment 2 map to obtain the beam-smearing-corrected (bsc) velocity dispersion" (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies); "estimated $h_{\rm g}$ for each pixel by assuming the equality between $\mathcal{W}$ and $P_{\rm ISM,\,mod}$" (The kinetic pressure, scale height, and the weight of the cold ISM)
   - software: 3DBarolo, CASA
   - parameter: Thermal broadening sigma_th ~ 0.3 km/s in the simulated model  
  > "albeit with a velocity dispersion that is only contributed by the thermal broadening, $\sigma_{\rm th}\sim 0.3\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$" (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
   - assumption: Atomic gas contribution negligible; molecular gas only  
  > "we assume the atomic gas contribution negligible, permitting us to replace $\Sigma_{\rm g}$ with $\Sigma_{\rm mol}$." (The kinetic pressure, scale height, and the weight of the cold ISM)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Beam-smearing correction tested on a GALMOD mock disk; input dispersions recovered for different inclinations and dispersions  
  > "By testing different input inclination angles and velocity dispersion values, we consistently reproduced the input values, demonstrating the reliability of our method." (Recover the pixel-wise velocity dispersion)
7. **Compare kinetic pressure with ISM weight**: Pixel-wise P_ISM and weight W from the mass model, with uncertainties from posterior and dispersion realizations; binned and fitted in log space for quasar hosts and controls  
  > "we have created $N$ realizations of each pixel by randomly selecting parameters from the posterior distribution of parameters" (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies); "The white circles with error bars represent the intensity-weighted mean and standard deviation error of $P_{\rm ISM}$ within each 0.3 dex $\mathcal{W}$ bins." (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: N = 1000 realizations; R21 = 0.53, 0.90, 0.62 for the three quasars; pixels with SNR < 3 or dispersion below the channel-width sigma removed  
  > "We have found that $N=1000$ is sufficient to obtain a good description of the error." (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies); "or those pixels with SNR$<3$" (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
   - assumption: Magnetic field and cosmic-ray contribution alpha ~ 0.3  
  > "represents the contribution of magnetic field and the cosmic ray" (The kinetic pressure, scale height, and the weight of the cold ISM)
   - fitting: linmix regression in log space of P_ISM against W  
  > "we fit the relation between these two parameters in logarithmic space using the Python package" (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
   - validation: Compared with theoretical 1:1 relation of Ostriker and Kim 2022  
  > "which is similar to the theoretical 1:1 relation proposed by (cite: Ostriker&Kim2022)" (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
8. **Estimate molecular outflow rates and compare with jet power**: Subtract the 3DBarolo model from the cube, treat residual as outflow, derive velocity and mass, and compute rates for cone and shell geometries; compare kinetic outflow rate with jet power from 5 GHz luminosity  
  > "we distinguish the non-circular motion, which is regarded as the outflow component, by subtracting the $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO model from the actual observed data cubes." (The molecular gas outflow); "We compute the outflow velocity as $V_{\rm out} = V_{\rm shift} + 2\sigma_{\rm out}$" (The molecular gas outflow)
   - software: 3DBarolo, CASA
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 0.8 for outflow mass; rates computed with R_out = 1 kpc and alpha_CO = 3.1 (cone) or ULIRG-like alpha_CO and Delta R = 200 pc (shell)  
  > "where $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ is assumed to be $0.8\,M_\odot\,\rm (K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{-2})^{-1}$, a commonly employed value for molecular gas outflows" (The molecular gas outflow); "with $R_{\rm out}=1\,$kpc and $\alpha_{\rm CO}=3.1\,M_\odot\,\rm (K\,km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{-2})^{-1}$" (The molecular gas outflow)
   - assumption: Non-circular motion in PG 2130+099 treated as outflow to bound the maximum AGN feedback; PG 0923+129 excluded as spiral-driven  
  > "For simplicity, we regard the non-circular motion as an outflow in PG 2130$+$099 in the following analysis to constrain the maximum contribution from possible AGN feedback." (The molecular gas outflow); "Therefore we remove this target in the following analysis." (The molecular gas outflow)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Outflow rates compared with literature empirical relation and upper limits

## Qualitative findings

- Kinetic pressure and ISM weight follow the 1:1 hydrostatic relation in the quasar hosts as in inactive galaxies, with some deviations at small radii and above the line in places  
  > "We note that the relationships within the host galaxies of quasars are consistent with those in actively star-forming galaxies and the theoretical 1:1 relation, accounting for the uncertainties." (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
- Residual CO motions in PG 0923+129 are spiral-arm driven, not AGN outflow; in PG 1126-041 and PG 2130+099 they are treated as possible outflow  
  > "suggesting that the non-circular motion is caused by the gravitational perturbation rather than the AGN feedback" (The molecular gas outflow)
- Outflow orientation and extent resemble the radio jet, suggesting jet-driven outflow, but outflow velocities lie below the escape velocity  
  > "The outflow extension is similar to the size of the radio jet, supporting the scenario that the radio jet contributes to such outflow." (The molecular gas outflow)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets chosen for extended CO and Halpha emission (at least 4 beams along the half-major axis), no bars or close companions, so dynamical analysis is reliable  
  > "We focused on galaxies with extended molecular and ionized gas distributions, requiring at least 4 beams along the half-major axis." (Quasar sample); "The above-mentioned selection criteria allow the reliable application of dynamical analysis." (Quasar sample)
- Why this method: Dynamical stellar masses avoid assumptions on IMF and star formation history  
  > "This methodology obviates the need for making assumptions about fundamental factors such as the initial mass function (IMF) or the star formation history (SFH)" (Mass comparison)
- Alternative: Outflow rate computed for both a filled-cone geometry and an expanding shell geometry  
  > "For an expanding shell-like shock front outflow scenario (cite: Husemann+2019), the outflow rate is then estimated by" (The molecular gas outflow)
- Alternative: PG 0923+129 removed from the outflow analysis because its residuals follow spiral arms  
  > "Therefore we remove this target in the following analysis." (The molecular gas outflow)
- Alternative: Galaxies with poor Sersic fits to CO not excluded  
  > "Therefore, we do not exclude these galaxies from our analysis." (Distribution of the Molecular Gas)
- How they decided it was enough: N = 1000 realizations judged sufficient for the error  
  > "We have found that $N=1000$ is sufficient to obtain a good description of the error." (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA resolution is too coarse to separate causes of the pressure deficit at small radii  
  > "Due to the limited resolution of ALMA data, it is hard to investigate which reason dominates the phenomenon." (The cold gas equilibrium in quasar hosts and inactive galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): Inflow versus outflow classification depends on the poorly known inclination  
  > "We caution that the divergency of inflow and outflow depends on the inclination angle of galaxies, which is hard to determine." (The molecular gas outflow)
- Limitation (authors): Halpha dispersions underestimated for several targets, making the asymmetric drift correction unreliable  
  > "The velocity dispersion of H$\alpha$ of those targets should be underestimated, and the asymmetric drift correction is unreliable." (Gas kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar-mass comparison limited to moderate masses and a small sample  
  > "it is necessary to study a more extensive sample size that spans a wider parameter space." (Mass comparison)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only three quasars with extended CO were analysed, selected for regular disks, so the result may not generalise to the PG quasar population
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Strong priors on alpha_CO, halo mass ratio and stellar structure mean several parameters are effectively fixed, so P_ISM and W partly reflect the priors
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO posteriors are broad (about 3.5 with large asymmetric errors) so weight and surface density are highly uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Control CO(1-0) data come from a different telescope and resolution (CARMA, 4.5 arcsec) and the CALIFA dispersions are poorly resolved
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow rates depend on the assumed alpha_CO, cone or shell geometry and a 1 kpc radius, and the residual cube may include inflow or inclination errors
