## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate, continuum-subtract and image the new (and unpublished archival) data**: CASA pipeline calibration, alignment of execution blocks, short-baseline then combined self-calibration, uvcontsub, tclean with multiscale deconvolver and Keplerian mask  
  > "Self-calibration was then carried out first on just the short baseline observations, followed by a second self-calibration using the combined self-calibrated short baseline and non-self-calibrated long baseline observations." (2.2.1 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5, multiscale scales [0, 5, 15], gain 0.08, small scale bias 0.45  
  > "a gain of 0.08 and a small scale bias of 0.45" (2.2.2 Imaging)
   - parameter: Continuum channels averaged to a maximum width of 125 MHz  
  > "The resulting line-free continuum channels were averaged together to channels with a maximum width of 125 MHz." (2.2.1 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Execution-block flux scales checked by comparing deprojected continuum visibility amplitude profiles  
  > "we checked relative flux scaling between execution blocks by deprojecting the continuum visibilities using disk geometry from the literature" (2.2.1 Data reduction)
2. **Measure disk-integrated line and continuum fluxes**: JvM-corrected cubes, integrated intensity maps with the Keplerian mask, curve-of-growth flux in increasingly larger elliptical apertures until the flux plateaus  
  > "Here the flux is measured using increasingly larger elliptical apertures with the same aspect ratio and orientation as the disk until integrated flux plateaus." (2.4 A first look at the observations)
   - software: curve-of-growth code (Zenodo)
   - parameter: Elliptical apertures with the disk aspect ratio and orientation  
  > "increasingly larger elliptical apertures with the same aspect ratio and orientation as the disk" (2.4 A first look at the observations)
   - assumption: Published fluxes are adopted where available for consistency; C18O (3-2) fluxes scaled down by 2.3 to compare with (2-1)  
  > "we have scaled down the" (2.4 A first look at the observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit T Tauri disk gas masses with CO isotopologue + N2H+ fluxes**: MCMC chi2 fit of the 225 GHz continuum, 13CO, C18O and N2H+ (3-2) integrated fluxes to the DALI grid of AGE-PRO V, with extra L* = 3 Lsun models  
  > "Using synthetic observations from this model grid they used a MCMC to carry out a $\chi^2$ fit of the observed 225 GHz continuum," (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
   - software: DALI (grid from AGE-PRO V)
   - parameter: Peak CO abundance in the CO emitting layer is a free parameter, 3e-7 <= x_CO <= 1e-4  
  > "the peak CO abundance in the CO emitting layer is included as a free parameter" (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
   - assumption: Prior on disk mass uniform in logspace between 1e-4 and 0.5 Msun  
  > "This prior is assumed to be uniform in logspace as the gas mass is also fit in logspace." (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
   - fitting: MCMC chi2 fit; sampler name not stated; median and 16th/84th percentiles of posterior reported  
  > "with the uncertainties being the 16$^{\rm th}$ and 84$^{\rm th}$ quantile of the distribution" (Table 1 notes)
   - validation: Herbig masses compared with Stapper et al. 2024 values, agreeing within a factor ~3  
  > "with gas masses being within a factor $\sim3$ ($1\sigma$) from each other" (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
4. **Fit Herbig disk gas masses**: MCMC fit with the Stapper et al. 2024 thermochemical grid to the 13CO and C18O fluxes (no N2H+ in the main fit)  
  > "using a similar MCMC as was used for the T-Tauri disks" (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
   - software: Stapper et al. 2024 model grid
   - assumption: Peak CO abundance not a free parameter in the Stapper grid  
  > "do not include the peak CO abundances as a free parameter" (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Compare line-based and kinematic gas masses**: Median ratio from a Kaplan-Meier estimator (to handle kinematic upper limits), repeated 10000 times drawing masses from posteriors  
  > "To quantify this factor we compute the median ratio using a Kaplan-Meier estimator." (4.2 Comparing with gas disk masses from kinematics); "we repeat this process 10000 times, each time drawing random line-emission and kinematically measured gas masses from their respective posterior distributions" (4.2 Comparing with gas disk masses from kinematics)

## Qualitative findings

- Herbig disks have N2H+ (3-2) fluxes about an order of magnitude fainter than T Tauri disks of the same continuum flux, attributed to higher stellar luminosity warming the disk and reducing CO freeze-out  
  > "For Herbig disks of a given continuum flux the" (2.4 A first look at the observations); "A likely cause for this are the higher stellar luminosities of these sources warming up the disk." (2.4 A first look at the observations)
- Line-based gas masses are consistently lower than kinematic masses  
  > "we also see that the line based gas disk masses are consistently lower than the kinematically measured gas masses" (4.2 Comparing with gas disk masses from kinematics)
- RXJ1842.9-3532 and AA Tau line-based masses ~5x lower than kinematic masses; RXJ1615.3-3255 line-based mass >~4x larger than kinematic mass  
  > "The difference for RXJ1615.3-3255 is harder to explain." (4.2 Comparing with gas disk masses from kinematics)
- Herbig disks have ISM-level peak CO abundances while T Tauri disks are typically lower  
  > "This suggests different chemical paths for the T-Tauri and Herbig disks" (5.4 The role of N2H+ in constraining Herbig gas disk masses)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: N2H+ traces CO-poor gas and breaks the disk mass vs CO abundance degeneracy  
  > "is a molecule sensitive to CO freeze-out and has recently been shown to significantly improve the accuracy of gas masses estimated from CO line emission" (abstract)
- Why this method: Including N2H+ constrains the peak CO abundance that is degenerate with disk mass when using only CO lines  
  > "Constraining this parameter is the main reason for including $\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{H}^+$ in addition to the CO lines" (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
- Alternative: Elias 2-27 J=2-1 data rejected for J=3-2 because of cloud contamination  
  > "show significantly more cloud contamination than the" (2.3 Archival observations)
- Alternative: Rerunning the full model grid with dust cavities rejected as computationally impracticable  
  > "As it is computationally impracticable to rerun the full model grid" (5.2 The effect of an inner dust cavity)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): N2H+ alone only moderately constrains the ionization rate  
  > "suggesting that $\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{H}^+$ alone only provides a moderate constrain on the ionization rate" (5.3 Constraining midplane ionization rates)
- Limitation (authors): Dust cavities and substructures are absent from the thermochemical models  
  > "which are not present in thermochemical models" (3 Measuring gas disk masses from line emission)
- Limitation (authors): Kinematic mass is an upper limit (Mdisk/M* = 5%) for five disks  
  > "the kinematically measured gas mass is an upper limit" (4.2 Comparing with gas disk masses from kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): CQ Tau x_CO fit is bimodal because of a lack of models and should not be used  
  > "Due to this incomplete fit we caution against using the" (5.4 The role of N2H+ in constraining Herbig gas disk masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas-to-dust ratios depend on an assumed 20 K and a single opacity while the continuum may be optically thick
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Result depends on a model grid published in a companion paper (in press), with no sampler or convergence details given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Kinematic masses are treated as truth though they carry their own systematics and several are upper limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Midplane ionization rate and N2 abundance are degenerate and N2 is held fixed
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample is mixed between archival data of different resolutions and aperture choices
