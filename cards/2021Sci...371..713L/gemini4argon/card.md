## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, reweight, and image ALMA continuum and dirty [C II] data cube, then continuum-subtract and clean the cube in GIPSY**: Calibrate Fourier-plane data with standard CASA pipeline, harmonize visibility weights on line-free channels with oldstatwt, regrid line data to 15.626 MHz channels, image continuum and dirty line cube with Hogbom deconvolver and Briggs robust=0.5, subtract first-order polynomial continuum in image plane in GIPSY, clean within a 3 sigma smoothed Boolean mask down to 3 sigma_d, and apply Hanning spectral smoothing  
  > "We first split the science target from the Fourier-plane data and used the CASA task `oldstatwt` on the line-free channels to set consistent visibility weights among the different datasets." (Observations and data reduction.); "The combined dataset was imaged using a Högbom deconvolver with Briggs' robust parameter of 0.5 (cite: Briggs1995)." (Observations and data reduction.); "The continuum was subtracted from the \[C II\] cube in the image plane, after fitting a first-order polynomial to the line-free channels." (Observations and data reduction.)
   - software: CASA, oldstatwt, GIPSY
   - parameter: 102 channels of 15.626 MHz (~14 km s^-1) width; Hanning smoothed to ~28 km s^-1  
  > "Next, we regridded the line-data to a common bandwidth of about 1.6 GHz using 102 channels with a spectral width of 15.626 MHz, corresponding to a velocity width of $\sim$14 km s$^{-1}$." (Observations and data reduction.); "The cleaned cube was spectrally smoothed using the Hanning scheme, giving a velocity resolution of $\sim$28 km s$^{-1}$ (twice the channel width)." (Observations and data reduction.)
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5; Boolean mask smoothed to 0.4'' clipped at 3 sigma_s; cleaned to 3 sigma_d  
  > "The combined dataset was imaged using a Högbom deconvolver with Briggs' robust parameter of 0.5 (cite: Briggs1995)." (Observations and data reduction.); "The Boolean mask was constructed by smoothing the continuum-subtracted \[C II\] cube to a resolution of 0.4$''$ and clipping at 3$\sigma_{\rm s}$, where $\sigma_{\rm s}$ is the rms noise of the smoothed cube." (Observations and data reduction.); "The cleaning was done down to 3$\sigma_{\rm d}$, where $\sigma_{\rm d}$ is the rms noise of the continuum-subtracted dirty cube." (Observations and data reduction.)
   - fitting: First-order polynomial fitted to line-free channels for continuum subtraction  
  > "The continuum was subtracted from the \[C II\] cube in the image plane, after fitting a first-order polynomial to the line-free channels." (Observations and data reduction.)
   - validation: Flagged two semi-pass execution blocks due to time-dependent phase jumps; excluded 2015 data from [C II] cube due to filtering by long baselines  
  > "We attempted to calibrate these additional data but concluded that they were unavoidably damaged due to time-dependent phase jumps." (Observations and data reduction.); "We used the 2015 data for the continuum imaging only, because the \[C II\] emission was filtered out by the very long baselines (up to 16.2 km) of those observations (cite: Gullberg2018)." (Observations and data reduction.)
2. **Construct moment maps and model 3D [C II] gas kinematics to derive surface brightness profile, rotation curve, and velocity dispersion profile**: Construct moment maps using smooth & search option in 3DBAROLO; fit 3D tilted-ring rotating disk model to the [C II] cube in three runs: (1) estimate center (x0, y0), PA, and V_sys as ring averages; (2) fix PA=40 deg and estimate average inclination i; (3) fit V_rot and sigma_V with geometric parameters fixed  
  > "The \[C II\] cube was analysed using the $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO package (cite: DiTeodoro2015). Moment maps were constructed using the "smooth & search" option, setting the `threshVelocity` parameter to 1." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "A first run of $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO is used to estimate ($x_0, y_0$), PA, and $V_{\rm sys}$, considering the arithmetic average of the best-fitting values across the rings." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "A second run of $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO is used to estimate an average inclination of $25^{\circ}\pm3^{\circ}$; the uncertainty is taken to be the standard deviation across the rings." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "The final run of $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO uses only $V_{\rm rot}$ and $\sigma_{\rm V}$ as free parameters." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
   - software: 3DBAROLO
   - parameter: threshVelocity = 1; 9 rings of width 0.06''  
  > "Moment maps were constructed using the "smooth & search" option, setting the `threshVelocity` parameter to 1." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "We use nine rings with a width of 0.06$''$, corresponding to half of the resolution element $\sqrt{\theta_{\rm x}\times \theta_{\rm y}}$, where $\theta_{\rm x}$ and $\theta_{\rm y}$ are the major and minor axes of the synthesized beam." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
   - parameter: z_0 = 300 pc (fixed); V_rad = 0 (fixed); PA = 40 deg (fixed after run 1); i = 25 +/- 3 deg (from run 2)  
  > "We fix the vertical thickness of the gas disk to 300 pc; the value of $z_0$ has little effect on our results because the disk is nearly face-on." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "The PA is then fixed to 40$^{\circ}$ because the velocity field does not show any strong indication for a warped disk within $R\simeq0.45''$." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "A second run of $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO is used to estimate an average inclination of $25^{\circ}\pm3^{\circ}$; the uncertainty is taken to be the standard deviation across the rings." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
   - assumption: Planck 2018 LambdaCDM cosmology (Omega_m = 0.315, Omega_Lambda = 0.685, H_0 = 67.4 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, 0.1'' = 657.7 pc at z = 4.7555); fully axisymmetric disk model with azimuthal averages for surface density; negligible radial motions (V_rad = 0)  
  > "We assume a $\Lambda$CDM cosmology adopting the Planck 2018 cosmological parameters (cite: Planck2018): matter density $\Omega_{\rm m} = 0.315$, cosmological constant density $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.685$, and Hubble constant $H_0 = 67.4$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "For simplicity, we adopt a fully axisymmetric disk model, so the surface density of each ring is directly computed from the observed \[C II\] intensity map using azimuthal averages." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "The radial velocity is negligible because the kinematic major axis of the disk is perpendicular to the kinematic minor axis (Fig. S1D), so we fix it to zero." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
   - fitting: 3D tilted-ring residual minimization in 3DBAROLO; asymmetric uncertainties (delta_+, delta_-) corresponding to 5% residual variation treated as 2 sigma, converted to symmetric 1 sigma error sqrt(delta_+ * delta_-)/2  
  > "For each parameter, $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO provides asymmetric uncertainties ($\delta_{+}$, $\delta_{-}$) corresponding to a variation of 5$\%$ of the residuals from the global minimum. We consider these errors as 2$\sigma$ deviations from the best-fitting value and compute symmetric 1$\sigma$ uncertainties as $\sqrt{\delta_{+}\delta_{-}}/2$, which are used in our Bayesian mass models (see Eq. 1)." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
   - validation: Visual inspection of channel maps, major/minor axis PV diagrams, and moment/residual maps; tested leaving V_rad free and confirmed consistency with zero  
  > "We verified that the best-fitting values of ($x_0, y_0$), $V_{\rm sys}$, and PA are plausible by visually inspecting \[C II\] channel maps, position-velocity (PV) diagrams, and moment maps." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "If we leave $V_{\rm rad}$ as a free parameter, we find values consistent with zero within the uncertainties, so we conclude that large-scale radial motions in the disk must be smaller than $\sim$30 km s$^{-1}$ (approximately the velocity resolution)." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "To assess the reliability of our kinematic model, Figure S2 shows PV diagrams along the major and minor axis of the disk." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
3. **Model 2D dust continuum morphology and HST rest-frame UV bulge size with GALFIT**: Fit 2D exponential disk (Sersic n=1) to ALMA dust continuum map in GALFIT with iterative 5 sigma residual masking; fit HST F160W image in GALFIT using a 7-star PSF to obtain upper limit on bulge effective radius R_e  
  > "We use the GALFIT package (cite: Peng2010) to fit 2D analytic functions to the dust distribution taking the point-spread function (PSF) into account." (Dust distribution.); "Because the dust distribution has substructures, we use an iterative approach masking residual pixels above 5$\sigma$ until the fitting results converge (cite: Hodge2019)." (Dust distribution.); "To constrain the bulge effective radius ($R_{\rm e}$), we analyze an image of ALESS 073.1 from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) in the F160W filter (cite: Grogin2011). We use GALFIT (cite: Peng2010) and construct a PSF using seven nearby stars." (Mass Models.)
   - software: GALFIT
   - parameter: Gaussian PSF = 0.123'' x 0.095'' at PA = -63.9 deg for ALMA continuum; Sersic index n = 1, projected axis ratio = 0.9, center fixed to [C II] disk; 5 iterations of >5 sigma residual masking  
  > "Because the ALMA continuum map was cleaned down to the noise level, we consider a Gaussian PSF with a full-width half-maximum equal to the synthesized beam ($0.123'' \times 0.095''$ with PA of $-63.9^{\circ}$)." (Dust distribution.); "Thus, we fix several GALFIT parameters, adopting an exponential disk (Sérsic index $n=1$) with the same center and inclination as the \[C II\] disk (projected axis ratio of $0.9$)." (Dust distribution.); "GALFIT returns $\rm{PA}=41^{\circ}\pm5^{\circ}$ at the first iteration and $\rm{PA}=36^{\circ}\pm6^{\circ}$ at the fifth, final iteration (Fig. S4B)." (Dust distribution.)
   - fitting: 2D analytic profile fitting with GALFIT; free parameters for dust continuum are intrinsic PA, effective radius, and integrated flux  
  > "The free parameters are the intrinsic PA, the effective radius, and the integrated flux." (Dust distribution.)
   - validation: Tested leaving all GALFIT parameters free (axis ratio and Sersic index) and examined residual maps against [C II] residual maps  
  > "However, if we allow the axis ratio and/or the Sérsic index to vary, a wide range of PA values are found, indicating that the intrinsic geometry cannot be constrained without any prior information." (Dust distribution.); "Similar structures are also visible in the residual \[C II\] intensity map (Fig. S4D)." (Dust distribution.)
4. **Compute gravitational circular velocity contributions of individual mass components and fit dynamical mass models to the rotation curve via MCMC**: Compute circular velocity profiles for cold gas disk, stellar disk, stellar bulge, and NFW DM halo using GIPSY task Rotmod; fit 4-component fiducial mass model (and 3-component alternatives) to the rotation curve using EMCEE MCMC sampler  
  > "We build a mass model with four components: a cold gas disk, a stellar disk, a stellar bulge, and a DM halo. For each mass component, we compute the expected circular velocity of a test particle using the `Rotmod` task in GIPSY." (Mass Models.); "The posterior probability distributions of the parameter set are mapped using the affine-invariant ensemble sampler EMCEE (cite: Foreman2013)." (MCMC fitting.)

## Qualitative findings

- The [C II] emission in ALESS 073.1 is about twice as extended as the dust continuum emission and forms a regularly rotating disk with negligible noncircular motions in the inner parts.  
  > "This galaxy's cold gas forms a regularly rotating disk with negligible noncircular motions." (abstract); "The \[C II\] emission is about twice as extended as the dust emission and shows an asymmetry to the northeast." (main text)
- The [C II] disk is rotationally supported (V_rot / sigma_V ~ 10) rather than turbulence supported, suggesting starburst and AGN feedback have only a gentle effect on the cold ISM.  
  > "The \[C II\] disk of ALESS 073.1 is supported by rotation, not turbulence." (main text); "This suggests that both starburst and AGN feedback have only a gentle effect on the cold interstellar medium of ALESS 073.1 because the \[C II\] disk is regular and unperturbed, contrary to galaxy formation models with violent feedback." (main text)
- Non-axisymmetric residual structures in both dust and [C II] emission resemble spiral arms embedded in the rotating disk.  
  > "The residual map (Fig. S4C) includes a prominent arm-like feature to the southwest which may connect to a broader feature to the southeast. Similar structures are also visible in the residual \[C II\] intensity map (Fig. S4D)." (Dust distribution.); "We interpret these features as a spiral arm embedded in a rotating disk." (Dust distribution.)
- Baryons dominate the gravitational potential within 3.5 kpc, and a central massive bulge component is required to reproduce the steeply rising inner rotation curve.  
  > "The bulge component of the model is necessary to reproduce the observed high rotation speeds at small radii." (main text); "Baryons dominate the gravitational potential within 3.5 kpc, similar to massive galaxies at $z=0$ (cite: Lelli2016) and $z=1$ to 3 (cite: Lelli2018; Genzel2017)." (main text)
- Massive early-type galaxies at z = 0 hosting molecular gas disks with similar rotational speeds (300-400 km s^-1) are the likely descendants of galaxies like ALESS 073.1.  
  > "Similar rotational speeds are observed in the most massive early-type galaxies at $z=0$ that host molecular gas disks (cite: Davis2016), so we conclude that they are the likely descendants of galaxies similar to ALESS 073.1." (main text)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous ALMA [C II] observations at 0.5'' (~3 kpc) resolution only marginally resolved the galaxy; 0.11'' (~700 pc) resolution covers the [C II] disk with ~45 resolution elements, providing >6 independent rotation curve points needed to distinguish rotating disks from mergers/outflows and fit dynamical mass models.  
  > "Limited spatial resolution has prevented the measurement of well-sampled rotation curves (with more than six independent elements), which can be used to measure different mass components by fitting dynamical models." (main text); "Previous \[C II\] observations with ALMA indicated a rotating disk (cite: DeBreuck2014), but the spatial resolution (0.5$''$ corresponding to $\sim$3 kpc) was insufficient to determine its detailed properties or identify gas inflows and outflows." (main text); "This is not the case for ALESS 073.1 because the area with detectable \[C II\] emission is covered by $\sim$45 resolution elements." (main text)
- Why this method: 3DBAROLO directly models the 3D data cube to account for beam smearing rather than relying on 2D moment maps; an axisymmetric model is chosen because pixel-by-pixel renormalization is insensitive to inclination; image-plane cleaning in GIPSY accounts for channel-dependent rms noise from combining different ALMA cycles; Gaussian/lognormal priors in MCMC prevent parameter degeneracies.  
  > "The root-mean square (rms) noise of the dirty \[C II\] cube depends on frequency because a different number of visibilities from different ALMA cycles are combined in each rebinned channel. This is taken into account during the cleaning process with GIPSY." (Observations and data reduction.); "$^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO directly models the 3D cube taking beam-smearing effects into account, so does not rely on 2D moment maps." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "The non-axisymmetric approach would be nearly insensitive to the inclination angle because the observed intensity map (moment zero) is always reproduced by construction." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
- Alternative: Two additional 2018 ALMA execution blocks flagged as 'semi-pass' were calibrated and rejected because they were damaged by time-dependent phase jumps.  
  > "Two additional execution blocks of 45 minutes were flagged as "semi-pass" by the ALMA quality-assurance team. We attempted to calibrate these additional data but concluded that they were unavoidably damaged due to time-dependent phase jumps." (Observations and data reduction.)
- Alternative: 2015 ALMA data were excluded from the [C II] line cube imaging because the extended [C II] emission was filtered out by the very long baselines (up to 16.2 km).  
  > "We used the 2015 data for the continuum imaging only, because the \[C II\] emission was filtered out by the very long baselines (up to 16.2 km) of those observations (cite: Gullberg2018)." (Observations and data reduction.)
- Alternative: Non-axisymmetric 3DBAROLO modeling (pixel-by-pixel flux renormalization) was rejected because it is unnecessary for ALESS 073.1 and nearly insensitive to disk inclination.  
  > "This technique was introduced to account for rotating disks with massive gas clumps and/or feedback-driven cavities (cite: Lelli2012a; Lelli2012b) but it appears unnecessary for ALESS 073.1. The non-axisymmetric approach would be nearly insensitive to the inclination angle because the observed intensity map (moment zero) is always reproduced by construction." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
- Alternative: Modeling a warped outer disk (varying PA at large radii) was rejected because it is uncertain and would only affect the outermost 2-3 points of the rotation curve.  
  > "At larger radii, the asymmetric \[C II\] extension to the north may potentially be due to a warp: this would only affect the outermost 2-3 points of the rotation curve, so we keep the PA constant at all radii rather than modeling an uncertain, asymmetric warp." (Gas distribution and kinematics.)
- Alternative: Three-component mass models (without bulge, without stellar disk, without gas disk, or without DM halo) were tested and rejected as either unable to fit the inner rotation curve (no bulge) or physically/cosmologically less plausible.  
  > "We also investigate mass models with three components, which we find either cannot fit the observations or are less plausible in the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm (cite: Supp)." (main text); "The model without the bulge (Fig. S5A) is unsatisfactory because the inner rotation velocities cannot be reproduced." (Alternative Mass Models.)
- How they decided it was enough: In GALFIT, iterative 5 sigma residual masking was repeated until PA converged (5th iteration); in EMCEE, 200 walkers with 1000 burn-in and 2000 production steps and a=2 gave ~50% acceptance fractions and fast convergence.  
  > "Because the dust distribution has substructures, we use an iterative approach masking residual pixels above 5$\sigma$ until the fitting results converge (cite: Hodge2019)." (Dust distribution.); "We run 1000 burn-in iterations, then the sampler is reset and run for another 2000 iterations. The EMCEE parameter `a`, which controls the size of the stretch move, is set equal to 2. This gives acceptance fractions around 50%." (MCMC fitting.)
- Limitation (authors): Low signal-to-noise ratio in the outer regions prevents distinguishing whether the northeastern/northwestern kinematic asymmetry is due to recently accreted gas, a warped outer disk, or streaming motions along a spiral arm.  
  > "The gas kinematics are less regular in the northeastern extension: This may be due to recently accreted gas, a warped outer disk, or streaming motions along a spiral arm (cite: Supp). The limited signal-to-noise ratio in the outer regions does not allow us to distinguish between these possibilities." (main text)
- Limitation (authors): The [C II] disk is close to face-on (i ~ 22-25 deg), so the formal inclination uncertainty should be taken with caution and causes degeneracy with absolute baryonic mass normalizations.  
  > "This value of $i$ is confirmed by comparing the model \[C II\] intensity map with the observed one, but its formal uncertainty should be taken with caution because the \[C II\] disk is close to face-on." (Gas distribution and kinematics.); "As expected, both $M_{\rm disk}$ and $M_{\rm bul}$ are degenerate with $i$. This occurs because the overall normalization of the rotation curve is set by $1/\sin(i)$." (MCMC fitting.)
- Limitation (authors): The stellar component is spatially unresolved in HST images, and the HST F160W rest-frame near-UV image may be affected by AGN emission, making the bulge effective radius R_e uncertain.  
  > "ALESS 073.1 has been detected at multiple wavelengths but the stellar component has not been spatially resolved." (Mass Models.); "The HST image may be affected by the AGN contribution because it measures the rest-frame near-ultraviolet emission, so the value of $R_{\rm e}$ is uncertain." (Mass Models.)
- Limitation (authors): The mass of the central super-massive black hole cannot be independently measured with the available data and is implicitly included in the bulge mass.  
  > "Formally, $\Upsilon_{\rm bul}$ includes the mass of the central super-massive black hole, which cannot be independently measured with the available data." (MCMC fitting.)
- Limitation (authors): Conclusions are based on observations of a single galaxy.  
  > "Although we observe only a single object, we conclude that the Universe produced regularly rotating galaxy disks with prominent bulges at $<$10$\%$ of its current age." (main text)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stellar disk and bulge are not directly resolved in rest-frame optical/near-IR starlight; the dynamical decomposition relies on the assumption that 160 um dust continuum traces the stellar disk while an unresolved central component is a stellar bulge.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because the disk is nearly face-on (i ~ 22 deg), small systematic errors in inclination derived from the [C II] morphology/kinematics translate into large changes in V_rot and total dynamical mass.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A compact, centrally concentrated molecular gas component (if [C II]/CO radial ratios vary strongly with radius) or a more overmassive central SMBH than assumed (<10%) could contribute to the central dynamical mass attributed to the stellar bulge.
