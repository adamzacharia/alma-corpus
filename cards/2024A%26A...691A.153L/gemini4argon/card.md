## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST/NIRSpec IFS data cubes and register astrometry**: Reduce raw NIRSpec R2700 and R100 data with modified JWST pipeline v1.8.2, drizzle to 0.05 arcsec spaxels, rescale noise vectors, and align astrometry to HST/WFC3 F125W and Gaia DR3.  
  > "The raw data were reduced with the JWST calibration pipeline version 1.8.2, using the CRDS context file jwst_1068.pmap." (2.2. Data reduction); "We build the final cubes with a spaxel size of 0.05" using the ‘drizzle’ method." (2.2. Data reduction); "We register the astrometry of the NIRSpec data by matching the central position of the target GS5001 in the NIRSpec data cube and in the HST/WFC3 F125W band image, after aligning the HST image to the Gaia co-ordinates frame." (2.4. Astrometry)
   - software: JWST calibration pipeline
   - parameter: spaxel size 0.05 arcsec; error rescaling median factor 1.6  
  > "We build the final cubes with a spaxel size of 0.05" using the ‘drizzle’ method." (2.2. Data reduction); "Therefore, we re-scale the ‘error’ vector in each spaxel by a factor of (median 1.6) to match the noise estimated from the standard deviation of the continuum in spectral regions free of line emission" (2.2. Data reduction)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to peak emission for astrometric alignment  
  > "Then, we align the position of the peak emission of our target (GS5001) in the HST/WFC3 125W band, derived with a 2D Gaussian fit, and in an image created by collapsing the NIRSpec R100 data cube over a similar wavelength range" (2.4. Astrometry)
   - validation: Checked alignment of other sources in the FoV between HST and NIRSpec images  
  > "We check that the positions of the other sources in the FoV are well aligned in the HST and NIRSpec image, so no rotation term is considered." (2.4. Astrometry)
2. **Image ALMA CO(4-3) data cube and create moment 0 map**: Image calibrated ALMA MS in CASA tclean with natural weighting, bin spectral channels to ~10 km s^-1, and integrate continuum-subtracted cube within ±800 km s^-1 of CO(4-3) at z=3.47.  
  > "We use the CASA task tclean applying natural weighting to generate a data cube with synthesised beam FWHM 1.9 × 1.3 arcsec2." (2.3. Ancillary data); "The CO(4–3) intensity map (moment 0) is produced by integrating the continuum subtracted data cube in the spectral channels within ±800 km s−1 from the CO(4–3) emission frequency at z = 3.47." (2.3. Ancillary data)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting; spectral binning to ~10 km s^-1; integration window ±800 km s^-1  
  > "The original spectral resolution of the observations is ∼ 2.5 km s−1 and we binned the spectral channels to reach a resolution of ∼ 10 km s−1." (2.3. Ancillary data); "The CO(4–3) intensity map (moment 0) is produced by integrating the continuum subtracted data cube in the spectral channels within ±800 km s−1 from the CO(4–3) emission frequency at z = 3.47." (2.3. Ancillary data)
   - assumption: z = 3.47 for CO(4–3) rest-frequency 461.04 GHz  
  > "The CO(4–3) intensity map (moment 0) is produced by integrating the continuum subtracted data cube in the spectral channels within ±800 km s−1 from the CO(4–3) emission frequency at z = 3.47." (2.3. Ancillary data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit optical emission lines spaxel-by-spaxel and in integrated apertures**: Subtract continuum with Savitzky-Golay filter and simultaneously fit optical emission lines in R2700 and R100 cubes using 1 or 2 Gaussian components.  
  > "We interpolate the flux using a Savitzky-Golay filter (Savitzky & Golay 1964) and first-order polynomials." (4.1. R2700 data); "After subtracting the continuum, we fit the following emission lines: Hα, Hβ, Hγ, [O iii]λλ4959,5007, [N ii]λλ6548,6583, [S ii]λλ6716,6731, [O ii]λλ3726,3729, [O i]λ6300. We fit all these lines simultaneously using a combination of Gaussian profiles." (4.1. R2700 data)
   - software: scipy.optimize.curve_fit, emcee
   - parameter: 1-comp: 20 < σ < 300 km s^-1; 2-comp: 20 < σ_n < 120 km s^-1, 140 < σ_b < 300 km s^-1; 3x3 spaxel averaging  
  > "For the one-component model, we constrain the width of the line to be 20 km s−1 < σ < 300 km s−1." (4.1. R2700 data); "For the two-component model, we constrain the width of the narrow component to be 20 km s−1 < σn < 120 km s−1 and the width of the broad component to be 140 km s−1 < σb < 300 km s−1." (4.1. R2700 data)
   - assumption: [N II]λ6583/[N II]λ6548 and [O III]λ5007/[O III]λ4959 fixed to 2.99; [O II] and [S II] doublet ratios bounded by theoretical limits for 1-10^5 cm^-3  
  > "We fix the flux ratio of the [N ii]λ6583/[N ii]λ6548 and [O iii]λ5007/[O iii]λ4959 lines to the theoretical value 2.99 (Osterbrock & Ferland 2006)." (4.1. R2700 data)
   - fitting: Non-linear least-squares (scipy.optimize.curve_fit) for spaxels; MCMC (emcee) with Gaussian likelihood and uniform priors for integrated spectra; BIC (>20% improvement) for component selection  
  > "We fit the emission lines using the Python routine ‘scipy.optimize.curve_fit’, which uses non-linear least-squares to fit a function to data." (4.1. R2700 data); "In this case, we fitted the spectra using the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) programme emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013), which allows us to robustly estimate the uncertainties." (4.1. R2700 data); "We select the two-component model if it causes an improvement in the BIC larger than 20%." (4.1. R2700 data)
   - validation: Compared 1-component and 1-or-2-component kinematic maps  
  > "For comparison, we also report in Fig. Fig. C.1 the appendix the velocity maps obtained with the one-component Gaussian fit, showing no significant differences from those in Fig. 5." (4.1. R2700 data)
4. **Derive ISM properties (attenuation, excitation, metallicity, SFR, ionisation parameter, electron density) and outflow energetics**: Calculate A_V from Hα/Hβ Balmer decrement, BPT ratios, metallicity from R3/O3O2/R23, SFR from dust-corrected Hα, log U from S2S3, n_e from simultaneous [S II] and [O II] fit, and outflow mass/rate from broad Hα.  
  > "We measure the dust attenuation in each spaxel using the Balmer decrement Hα/Hβ." (5.2.1. Dust attenuation); "We fit simultaneously the R3, O3O2 and R23 parameters to reduce the uncertainties, following the procedure described in Curti et al. (2024)." (5.2.3. Gas-phase metallicity); "In order to better constrain the electron density, we fit simultaneously the [S ii] and [O ii] lines, forcing the line ratios to agree on the same ne parameter, following the same approach used by Rodríguez Del Pino et al. (2024)." (5.2.6. Electron density)
   - software: emcee
   - assumption: Intrinsic Hα/Hβ = 2.86 (Case B, T_e = 10^4 K, n_e = 10^2-10^3 cm^-3); Cardelli et al. (1989) attenuation law; Chabrier (2003) IMF; Kennicutt & Evans (2012) SFR relation; Curti et al. (2020b) metallicity calibrations; Sanders et al. (2016) density formulas  
  > "We assume a theoretical value of Hα/Hβ= 2.86, estimated for Case B recombination assuming a temperature of 104 K and an electron density ne = 102 − 103 cm−3 (Osterbrock & Ferland 2006). We adopt the Cardelli et al. (1989) attenuation law." (5.2.1. Dust attenuation); "(6) SFR derived from the dust-corrected Hα luminosity using the relation from Kennicutt & Evans (2012), assuming a Chabrier (2003) IMF." (Table 1)
   - fitting: MCMC simultaneous fit of [O II] and [S II] doublets for electron density  
  > "Simultaneous spectral fit of the [O ii]λλ3726, 29 and [S ii]λλ6716, 31 doublets to derive the electron density from the integrated spectrum of the main target." (Figure 13 caption)
   - validation: Compared ionisation parameter maps across S2S3, O3O2, S2Hβ, and O2Hβ calibrations  
  > "We calculated the ionisation parameter maps also using the O3O2, S2Hβ and O2Hβ calibrations from Díaz et al. (2000). These maps show similar spatial variations, even though the normalisation is different." (5.2.5. Ionisation parameter)

- GS5001 is surrounded by a southern companion with three sub-structures (blueshifted by ~-150 km s^-1) and a northern companion with three sub-structures and a tidal tail (redshifted by ~200 km s^-1), forming a pre-coalescence merger system aligned with the large-scale CO(4-3) filament.  
  > "In addition to the main galaxy (GS5001), the NIRSpec FoV covers a close companion in the south, with three sub-structures with velocities blueshifted by ∼−150 km s−1 with respect to GS5001, and another source in the north redshifted by ∼ 200 km s−1." (abstract); "The north and south companions, which are broadly oriented along the same direction (see Fig. 14), could have formed in this gas filament and be in the process of moving towards the central galaxy and merging with it." - Spatially resolved optical line ratios across the system are consistent with star formation ionisation with no evidence of AGN excitation.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST/NIRSpec IFS enables spatially resolved mapping of rest-frame optical emission lines (including Hα, Hβ, [O III], [S III]) at z > 3 at ~0.1 arcsec resolution, which is inaccessible from the ground.  
  > "However, ground-based observations are limited in their ability to probe beyond a redshift of z ∼ 2.5 for emission lines like Hα, and up to z ∼ 3.5 for Hβ and [O iii]." (1. Introduction); "enable one for the first time to map the optical lines at z - 3 with a spatial resolution of ∼ 0.1" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Simultaneous fitting of [S II] and [O II] doublets constrains electron density better than individual doublets because [O II] is not fully resolved while [S II] has lower S/N; S2S3 is used for ionisation parameter because it is less metallicity-dependent than O3O2.  
  > "We note that the two [O ii] lines are not fully spectroscopically resolved in our data. On the other hand, the [S ii] lines are well resolved, but have lower S/N. In order to better constrain the electron density, we fit simultaneously the [S ii] and [O ii] lines" (5.2.6. Electron density); "The S2S3 line ratio is less dependent on metallicity than O3O2, thus it is considered to be a more accurate estimator of the ionisation parameter" (5.2.5. Ionisation parameter)
- Alternative: Gravitational interaction turbulence as the primary cause of the off-nuclear high-W80 regions in the main galaxy (disfavoured relative to a biconical outflow, though contribution from interactions is not excluded).  
  > "Thus, we favour the outflow scenario to explain the enhanced line widths in the off-nuclear regions of the main galaxy, even though we acknowledge the possibility that galaxy interactions may also contribute to the broadening of the lines." (5.1.2. High-velocity dispersion regions: Outflow or gravitational interaction)
- Alternative: AGN excitation as the source of X-ray and MIR emission in GS5001 (rejected as a strong component, though a weak obscured AGN cannot be ruled out).  
  > "In summary, we do not find evidence for an AGN in GS5001, even though we cannot rule out that a weak and possibly obscured AGN is present." (6.3. No evidence of an active galactic nucleus in GS5001)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N in the broad component prevents measuring outflow electron density or spatially resolved broad-component dust attenuation.  
  > "We derive the attenuation for the global emission inferred from the one-component fits, as the S/N of the broad component is not high enough to derive reliable line ratios separately for the broad and narrow components in individual spaxels, nor binning 3 × 3 adjacent spaxels." (5.2.1. Dust attenuation); "We cannot estimate the electron density in the outflow component as the broad component of [S ii] and [O ii] are too faint, so we use the electron density estimated for the total line profile" (6.2.1. Outflow properties)
- Limitation (authors): Only the ionised phase of the outflow is traced; neutral atomic and molecular phases are not measured.  
  > "We note however that we are not tracing the neutral and molecular phases of the outflow." (7. Summary and conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Only projected velocities and distances are measured, leaving true 3D relative velocities and clump 'c' line-of-sight geometry uncertain.  
  > "We note however that we can only measure projected velocities, thus, the ‘true’ relative velocities are uncertain." (6.1. Dynamical and physical status of the system: A pre-coalescence merger)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data are only used qualitatively in Fig. 14 to overlay CO(4-3) contours on the NIRSpec [O III] map; no new quantitative molecular gas measurements are derived.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Metallicity calibrations (Curti et al. 2020b) are calibrated on local galaxies and applied at z = 3.47, where shock contamination in the northern region may also bias R3/O3O2/R23.
