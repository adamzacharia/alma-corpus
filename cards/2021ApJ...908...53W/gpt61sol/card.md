## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure individual X-ray luminosities**: Extract source/background spectra; infer fluxes and rest-frame 2–10 keV luminosities with a fixed power law  
  > "We extract the spectrum for each object within a $3.0\ensuremath{''}$ radius circular region centered at the optical position using the `specextract` script." (Chandra X-ray Observations); "The rest-frame 2-10 keV luminosites were estimated by assuming $\rm \Gamma=2.0$ as listed in Table [2](#tab_cxo)." (Measurements and Results)
   - software: CIAO, specextract, srcflux
   - parameter: Source radius 3.0 arcsec; background annulus 10.0–30.0 arcsec  
  > "We extract the spectrum for each object within a $3.0\ensuremath{''}$ radius circular region centered at the optical position using the `specextract` script." (Chandra X-ray Observations); "We choose a background annulus centered at the optical positions with an inner radius of $10.0\ensuremath{''}$ and an outer radius of $30.0\ensuremath{''}$." (Chandra X-ray Observations)
   - assumption: Γ=2.0; Galactic absorption from Kalberla05; target-specific response matrices and ancillary files  
  > "We measure the X-ray flux by assuming a power-law spectrum with $\rm \Gamma=2.0$ (cite: Nanni17; Vito19), accounting for the Galactic absorption (cite: Kalberla05)" (Measurements and Results); "using the response matrices and ancillary files extracted at the position of each target." (Measurements and Results)
   - fitting: No individual X-ray spectral fitting; fewer than 20 full-band net counts per quasar  
  > "Since all quasars have less than 20 net counts in the full 0.5-7.0 keV band, we do not attempt spectral fitting for individual quasars." (Measurements and Results)
   - validation: Using Γ=2.3 would increase L2–10keV by ∼20%  
  > "the $L_{\rm 2-10keV}$ would be $\sim$20% higher if we use $\rm \Gamma=2.3$" (Measurements and Results)
2. **Fit NIR continua and broad lines**: Iterative pseudo-continuum fitting, subtraction and two-Gaussian Mg ii/C iv fitting  
  > "we first fit a pseudo-continuum model to the emission line (except for iron emission) free regions." (Measurements and Results); "The Mg ii and C iv lines are then fitted with two Gaussian functions for each line after subtracting the pseudo-continuum model." (Measurements and Results)
   - parameter: Power-law, Balmer continuum and iron emission; iron template broadened to Mg ii width  
  > "The pseudo-continuum model includes three components, a power-law continuum ($f_\lambda \propto \lambda^{\alpha_\lambda}$), Balmer continuum (cite: Derosa14), and iron emission (cite: Vestergaard01; Tsuzuki06)." (Measurements and Results); "We perform the whole fitting process iteratively and broaden the iron template by convolving it with a Gaussian kernel to match the line width of the Mg ii line." (Measurements and Results)
   - fitting: 100 Gaussian-noise mock spectra; errors from average 16% and 84% percentile deviations  
  > "We created 100 mock spectra by randomly adding Gaussian noise to each pixel with standard deviation equal to the spectral error at that pixel." (Measurements and Results); "The uncertainties of measured spectral properties are then estimated as the average of the 16% and 84% percentile deviation from the median." (Measurements and Results)
   - validation: Mg ii redshifts generally agree with [C ii] within <800 km s−1  
  > "The redshifts based on the Mg ii line are in generally consistent with ($\rm<800~km~s^{-1}$) the \[C ii\] redshifts listed in Table [1](#tab_basic)." (Measurements and Results)
3. **Derive black-hole and accretion properties**: Convert 3000Å luminosity to Lbol; apply Mg ii virial estimator; calculate λEdd=Lbol/LEdd  
  > "The bolometric luminosities are estimated by assuming a bolometric correction of $L_{\rm bol}$=5.15$\times$ $\lambda L_{\rm3000 \text{\normalfont\AA}}$ (cite: Shen11)." (Measurements and Results); "The black hole masses, $M_{\rm BH}$, are then estimated using the single virial estimator proposed by (cite: Vestergaard09)" (Measurements and Results); "The Eddington ratio of each quasar is then calculated as $\lambda_{\rm Edd}= L_{\rm bol}/L_{\rm Edd}$" (Measurements and Results)
   - assumption: LEdd=1.26×10^38 MBH  
  > "where the $L_{\rm Edd}=1.26\times10^{38} ~M_{\rm BH}$ is the Eddington luminosity." (Measurements and Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure optical-to-X-ray ratios**: αox=log(f2keV/f2500Å)/log(ν2keV/ν2500Å)

- LIR and SFR do not correlate with X-ray luminosity in the luminous samples  
  > "The $L_{\rm IR}$ and SFR do not correlate with the $L_{\rm 2-10 keV}$ in these luminous distant quasars" (Summary)

  > "we conclude that the steeper $\Gamma$ of $z > 6.5$ quasars is mainly driven by their higher Eddington ratios rather than by their higher redshifts." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use 1mm ALMA measurements to estimate host infrared luminosities  
  > "In order to measure the infrared luminosities of these quasars we used the 1mm (in the observed frame) ALMA observations collected by (cite: Venemans18) and (cite: Decarli18)." (Measurements and Results)
- Why this method: Haro 11 is suggested as a high-z host analog; modified blackbody misses mid-infrared flux  
  > "Since Haro 11, a low metallicity dwarf galaxy, has been suggested as the best candidate analog for high-$z$ quasar host galaxies (cite: Lyu16)" (Measurements and Results); "because the modified blackbody misses flux in the mid-infrared." (Measurements and Results)
- Alternative: Modified blackbody with T=47 K, β=1.6 gives about half the Haro 11 LIR  
  > "the $L_{\rm IR}$ estimated using the Haro 11 template is usually about two times higher than that estimated from a modified blackbody with $T=47$ K and $\beta=1.6$ (cite: Beelen06)" (Measurements and Results)
- Alternative: Retain Γ=2 rather than Γ=2.3 for consistency with previous high-redshift work  
  > "Considering that most previous work has used $\rm \Gamma\sim2$ when measuring $L_{\rm 2-10keV}$ at high redshifts (cite: Nanni17; Vito19)" (Measurements and Results); "we will only use the $L_{\rm 2-10keV}$ values derived by assuming $\rm \Gamma=2$ in what follows." (Measurements and Results)

  > "we do not attempt to fit the individual quasar X-ray spectrum due to the limitation of small number of photons detected."
