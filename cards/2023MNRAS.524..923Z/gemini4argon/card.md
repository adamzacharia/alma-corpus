## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract 1D CO(3-2) spectra, optimize spatial position, and measure line flux and redshift in the uv plane**: Iterative uv-plane point-source modelling per channel across all four sidebands, averaging over channels maximizing S/N to fit spatial position, and fitting average line maps in uv plane with a 2D PSF model; Gaussian fit to 1D spectrum allowing for negative continuum for ID97148  
  > "When a signal was present, we averaged the data over the channels maximizing the detection signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)" (3.1 CO emission); "We finally estimated their CO (3-2) flux by fitting their average emission line maps in the uv plane adopting the Fourier Transform of a two-dimensional PSF model with the GILDAS task uv_fit." (3.1 CO emission); "To account for the effects of the negative noise peak on the line emission flux, we fitted the 1D spectrum (Figure 1) with a Gaussian model, allowing for a negative continuum, and estimated the integrated flux of the emission." (3.1 CO emission)
   - software: GILDAS, CASA
   - parameter: 2D PSF (point-source) model in uv plane  
  > "We finally estimated their CO (3-2) flux by fitting their average emission line maps in the uv plane adopting the Fourier Transform of a two-dimensional PSF model with the GILDAS task uv_fit." (3.1 CO emission)
   - assumption: Targets are spatially unresolved point sources at the angular resolution of the observations  
  > "Since our targets are expected to be unresolved at the angular resolution of the observations" (3.1 CO emission)
   - fitting: Fourier Transform of a 2D PSF model in GILDAS uv_fit and CASA uvmodelfit; 1D Gaussian fit for redshift and negative-continuum correction  
  > "We finally estimated their CO (3-2) flux by fitting their average emission line maps in the uv plane adopting the Fourier Transform of a two-dimensional PSF model with the GILDAS task uv_fit." (3.1 CO emission); "Fluxes and uncertainties determined with the uvmodelfit task provided by CASA give consistent results." (3.1 CO emission)
   - validation: Cross-checked GILDAS uv_fit fluxes with CASA uvmodelfit; compared signal-weighted average frequency redshift with 1D Gaussian fit redshift (agree within delta_z < 0.0004) and optical spectroscopic redshift (agree within 1 sigma)  
  > "We estimated the redshift from the CO (3-2) lines in two ways, both giving consistent results (𝛿𝑧< 0.0004): by computing the signal-weighted average frequency within the line channels and by fitting the one-dimensional spectrum with a Gaussian function." (3.1 CO emission); "We compared these redshift estimates with those obtained from optical spectra (Maltby et al. 2016) and found that they agree within 1𝜎, increasing the reliability of the CO detection (Table 2)." (3.1 CO emission); "Fluxes and uncertainties determined with the uvmodelfit task provided by CASA give consistent results." (3.1 CO emission)

(3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - software: GILDAS, CASA
   - parameter: 5 sigma upper limit at fixed CO position  
  > "In Table 2, we report the total flux density upper limits that we estimated as the 5𝜎 uncertainty obtained when fitting, in the uv plane, the Fourier Transform of a two-dimensional PSF model with center fixed at the position of the CO detection." (3.2 Continuum emission)
   - assumption: Point-source profile centered at the CO detection position  
  > "the Fourier Transform of a two-dimensional PSF model with center fixed at the position of the CO detection." (3.2 Continuum emission)

(3.3 Molecular gas mass); "We then converted the estimated CO(1-0) luminosity into molecular gas mass by adopting the standard Milky-Way like conversion factor 𝛼CO = 4.4 M⊙ K km s−1 pc2 (Bolatto et al. 2013)." (3.3 Molecular gas mass); "By considering the continuum flux upper limits estimated as in Section 3.2 (Table 2), a spectral index 𝛽 = 1.8 (Magdis et al. 2021; Cochrane et al. 2022), and a specific luminosity at 850 GHz restframe (L𝜈850) to gas mass conversion factor" (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: r_32 = 0.5  
  > "We considered a brightness temperature ratio 𝑟32 = 0.5 (Carilli & Walter 2013) to account for the excitation of the CO(3-2) transition with respect to the thermalized case." (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: alpha_CO = 4.4 M_sun K km s^-1 pc^2  
  > "We then converted the estimated CO(1-0) luminosity into molecular gas mass by adopting the standard Milky-Way like conversion factor 𝛼CO = 4.4 M⊙ K km s−1 pc2 (Bolatto et al. 2013)." (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
   - parameter: beta = 1.8, alpha_850 = 1 x 10^20 erg s^-1 Hz^-1 M_sun^-1  
  > "a spectral index 𝛽 = 1.8 (Magdis et al. 2021; Cochrane et al. 2022), and a specific luminosity at 850 GHz restframe (L𝜈850) to gas mass conversion factor 𝛼850 = L𝜈850/Mgas = 1 × 1020 erg s−1 Hz−1 M−⊙1 (Scoville et al. 2015)" (3.3 Molecular gas mass)

4. **Estimate stellar masses and star formation rates via spectro-photometric SED fitting and [OII] emission**: Simultaneously fit multi-wavelength SXDS photometry and VLT/VIMOS optical spectra at fixed spectroscopic redshift using Fast++; also compute dust-corrected [OII] luminosity SFR using Kewley et al. (2004)  
  > "We fixed the redshifts to the spectroscopic values and modelled the photometry and the spectra simultaneously." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate); "We convert the dust-corrected [OII] luminosity into SFR by using the calibration by Kewley et al. (2004)." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate)
   - software: Fast++
   - parameter: log(tau/yr^-1) in [6.5, 10] in log-steps of 0.1; ages in log-steps of 0.1 from 100 Myr to age of Universe; A_V in [0, 6] mag; Z = 0.008, 0.02, 0.05  
  > "the Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation curve (𝐴V ∈[0, 6] mag), and free metallicities including sub- and supersolar estimates (𝑍 = 0.008, 0.02, 0.05)." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate)
   - assumption: Bruzual & Charlot (2003) models, Chabrier (2003) IMF, delayed SFHs, Calzetti et al. (2000) dust attenuation curve, E(B-V)_star = 0.58 E(B-V)_nebular  
  > "We fit the SEDs with Fast++4 (Schreiber et al. 2018) using Bruzual & Charlot (2003) models, the Chabrier (2003) IMF, delayed SFHs" (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate); "accounting for the fact that emission lines are more attenuated than the stellar continuum (E(B − V)star = 0.58E(B − V)nebular, Calzetti et al. 2000)." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate)
   - fitting: Fast++ SED fitting with uncertainties estimated analytically following Avni (1976) and via Monte Carlo bootstrapping  
  > "The latter were estimated both analytically following Avni (1976) and via Monte Carlo bootstrapping (see Schreiber et al. 2018 for details about the exact implementation in Fast++)." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate)
   - validation: Compared M_* for ID83492 with BAGPIPES fits by Wild et al. (2020) and Carnall et al. (2019), 3D-HST (Skelton et al. 2014), and Mehta et al. (2018); compared SED SFR with dust-corrected [OII] SFR  
  > "We also checked for consistency with SED fitting results reported in the literature (Carnall et al. 2019; Wild et al. 2020)." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate); "We find SFR = 0.2 ± 0.1 M⊙ yr−1 for ID97148 and SFR = 0.3 ± 0.1 M⊙ yr−1 for ID83492, which are consistent, within the uncertainties, with those estimated from the SED fit." (3.4 Stellar mass and star formation rate)
5. **Measure spectral absorption indices and derive stellar population ages**: Measure Lick indices H_delta_F, H_gamma_F, and D_n4000 from VIMOS optical spectra and compare with a Monte Carlo library of 500,000 synthetic spectra to construct Bayesian PDFs of R-band light-weighted and mass-weighted mean stellar ages  
  > "From the optical spectra of our galaxies we measure the Lick indices of the high-order Balmer lines H𝛿F, H𝛾F5, and the D𝑛4000 index following Gallazzi et al. (2014)." (3.5 Stellar age); "Specifically, we compare the observed D𝑛4000, H𝛿F, H𝛾F absorption index strengths with those predicted by a large Monte Carlo library of model spectra." (3.5 Stellar age); "we construct the posterior probability function (PDF) of the 𝑅-band light weighted mean stellar age and of the mass-weighted mean stellar age." (3.5 Stellar age)
   - parameter: 10% error added to D_n4000 error budget; median of PDF as fiducial value, 16th and 84th percentiles as uncertainties  
  > "We add a 10% error to the error budget of D𝑛4000 to account for uncertainties in spectrophotometric calibration (Figure 3)." (3.5 Stellar age); "We take the median of the PDF as our fiducial value and the 16th and 84th percentile range as the uncertainties accounting for model degeneracies." (3.5 Stellar age)
   - assumption: Zibetti et al. (2017) library of 500,000 synthetic spectra with Sandage (1986) SFHs plus random bursts, varying metal enrichment histories, and 2016 Bruzual & Charlot (2003) / Chevallard & Charlot (2016) SSPs  
  > "We adopted the model library described in Zibetti et al. (2017), which comprises 500000 synthetic spectra assuming randomly generated star formation histories (Sandage 1986, allowing both rising and declining phases, with the addition of random bursts)" (3.5 Stellar age); "The base models are Bruzual & Charlot (2003) Simple Stellar Populations in the 2016 version (Chevallard & Charlot 2016)." (3.5 Stellar age)
   - fitting: Bayesian posterior probability distribution over 500,000 Monte Carlo synthetic spectra  
  > "We estimated the mean age of the stellar populations of our sample galaxies by adopting the Bayesian approach described in Gallazzi et al. (2005, 2014)." (3.5 Stellar age)

## Qualitative findings

- Massive (M_* ~ 5 x 10^10 M_sun) post-starburst galaxies at z > 1 retain a substantial molecular gas fraction (f_H2 ~ 8% - 16%), comparable to lower-redshift post-starburst galaxies.  
  > "We measure their molecular gas fraction to be 𝑓H2 = MH2/M★ ∼ 8% - 16%, consistent with 𝑧< 1 post-starburst galaxies from the literature." (abstract)
- The star formation efficiency of z > 1 post-starburst galaxies is ~10x lower than that of star-forming galaxies at similar redshift, and they have higher f_H2 than expected for their sSFR.  
  > "The star formation efficiency of our targets is ∼ 10× lower than that of star-forming galaxies at similar redshift, and they are outliers of the 𝑓H2 - specific star formation rate (sSFR) relation of star-forming galaxies," (abstract)
- The molecular gas fraction of post-starburst galaxies anti-correlates with the D_n4000 spectral index and stellar population age, indicating gas declines after the last major burst of star formation.  
  > "The gas fraction of post-starbursts from our sample and the literature correlates with the D𝑛4000 spectral index, a proxy of the stellar population age. This suggests that their gas content decreases after the last major burst of star formation." (abstract)
- ID83492 is undergoing an early major merger phase with two gas-rich, starbursting companions, supporting quenching mechanisms that suppress star formation without completely removing the molecular gas.  
  > "Finally, one of our targets is undergoing a major merger phase with two highly star-forming companions. This hints at a picture where a perturber event (e.g., major merger) quenches star formation without completely removing the molecular gas." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targeting CO(3-2) at z > 1 probes the dense molecular gas phase during the main quenching epoch of lower-mass (M_* ~ 5 x 10^10 M_sun) post-starburst galaxies with secure spectroscopic redshifts and stringent SFR limits.  
  > "In this study we expand upon those works, including two postSB galaxies at 𝑧> 1, with lower stellar masses (M★ ∼ 5 × 1010M⊙) than other high-redshift samples from the literature, allowing us to start widening the explored parameter space." (1 INTRODUCTION); "we selected two objects with stringent star formation rate limits" (2.1 Sample selection and ancillary data)
- Why this method: Since targets are unresolved at ~1.5'' resolution, modelling point sources directly in the uv plane per channel and iterating spatial positions maximizes S/N; excluding dust-based literature gas estimates avoids systematic discrepancies.  
  > "To have a sample with homogeneously-derived molecular gas masses, we only considered galaxies with CO observations. We excluded from the literature comparison the galaxies with molecular gas mass estimates based on SED fitting and/or dust continuum observations." (2.3 Literature samples); "Since our targets are expected to be unresolved at the angular resolution of the observations" (3.1 CO emission)
- Alternative: CO emission of ID97148 originating from a nearby dust-obscured satellite not detected in near-IR imaging rather than the post-SB itself (in which case M_H2 is an upper limit)  
  > "Alternatively the CO emission might originate from a nearby dust-obscured satellite which is not detected in the near-infrared imaging (e.g., Schreiber et al. 2018). In this case the molecular gas mass that we estimate for ID97148 should be considered as an upper limit." (3.1 CO emission)
- Alternative: Continuum source near ID97148 rejected as a physical companion and considered a spurious detection or lower-redshift, low-mass interloper  
  > "Given the angular distance from ID97148, the redshift difference of Δ𝑧> 0.15 and the highly uncertain values retrieved from the SED fit, we do not consider such detection as a companion galaxy, but rather as a spurious detection or lower-redshift and low-mass interloper." (3.2 Continuum emission)
- Alternative: Post-SB ID83492 being a quiescent galaxy replenished with molecular gas stripped from its star-forming companions during the merger  
  > "Another possible scenario is the fact that the post-SB is a quiescent galaxy which was replenished with molecular gas, stripped" (5.1 The merger scenario)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size of two post-starburst galaxies at z > 1  
  > "Having in the future larger samples of high-redshift post-SBs with CO observations will allow us to confirm the results reported in this study." (6 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties do not account for systematic differences in unconstrained CO excitation (r_32) and CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO) in post-starburst galaxies  
  > "These uncertainties do not account for systematic differences in the (unconstrained) excitation and CO-to-H2 factors in post-SB galaxies, but allow to directly compare our results with the literature on star-forming and quiescent galaxies." (3.3 Molecular gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): Coarse angular resolution (~1.5'') leaves targets spatially unresolved, preventing detection of close pairs (< 1.5'') or morphological asymmetries and contributing to positional offset uncertainty in ID97148  
  > "Observations with better angular resolution are needed to understand whether ID97148 is undergoing a late merger phase with two nuclei which are not resolved with the current angular resolution (pair closer than ∼ 1′′.5 ∼ 12.5 kpc at 𝑧 ∼ 1.2)." (5.1 The merger scenario)
- Limitation (authors): Negative noise peak next to ID97148 in the continuum map affects the measured CO(3-2) flux and line width by a factor of 2-3 depending on whether a negative continuum is fitted  
  > "We also highlight that next to the CO(3-2) detected line there is a negative noise peak which is evident in the continuum map (Figure 1) and that could hide part of the emission and artificially offset its peak." (3.1 CO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CO(3-2) detection of ID97148 is low S/N (4 sigma), spatially offset by ~1.6'' from the H-band peak, and sits on a negative continuum trough that changes the derived gas mass by a factor of >3 depending on baseline treatment.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming fixed r_32 = 0.5 and Milky Way alpha_CO = 4.4 for both post-starburst targets and starbursting companions (sSFR > 10 Gyr^-1) may introduce systematic biases in M_H2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Note that in Table 3, log(f_H2) for '97148 corrected' is printed identically as -1.0 ± 0.1 despite M_H2 increasing from 4.1 to 13.8 x 10^9 M_sun.
