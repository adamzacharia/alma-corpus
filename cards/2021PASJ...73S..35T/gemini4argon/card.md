- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: Whether cloud-cloud collisions trigger the formation of super star clusters (SSCs) in the overlap region of the Antennae galaxies (NGC 4038/NGC 4039), and how compressed gas pressure relates to SSC stellar mass.

## Analysis recipe

1. **Re-image ALMA Cycle 0 and Cycle 4+0 12CO (3-2) visibility data and evaluate missing flux**: Re-image archival visibilities using multiscale CLEAN with natural weighting in CASA, and compare smoothed 14 arcsec peak brightness temperature to JCMT single-dish data  
  > "We re-performed the imaging process from the visibility data by using the CASA (Common Astronomy Software Application) package (McMullin et al. 2007) version 5.0.0. We used the multiscale CLEAN algorithm (Cornwell 2008) implemented in the CASA with natural weighting." (2.1 Cycle 0 data toward the overlap region: GMC scale); "we redid the imaging process from the visibility data by using the CASA package version 5.4.1. and the multiscale CLEAN algorithm same as for the Cycle 0 data (Section 2.1.)." (2.2 Cycle 4 data toward the Firecracker: cluster scale)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: natural weighting; velocity resolution 5.0 km s^-1  
  > "The resultant synthesized beam size is 0′′. 70×0′′. 46, and the 1σ RMS noise is ∼4.8×10−3 Jy beam−1 at a velocity resolution of 5.0 km s−1, which corresponds to a surface brightness sensitivity of ∼0.15 K." (2.1 Cycle 0 data toward the overlap region: GMC scale)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared pixel intensity histograms and channel maps with Whitmore et al. (2014) (Appendix 1), and compared smoothed data at 14 arcsec with JCMT single-dish data from Zhu et al. (2003)  
  > "To investigate the missing flux, we compared the peak brightness temperature of the single-dish (JCMT) data (Zhu et al. 2003) and our re-processed data smoothed to the single-dish resolution of 14′′." (2.1 Cycle 0 data toward the overlap region: GMC scale); "We made count histograms of integrated intensities from the data of Whitmore et al. (2014) and the data of this paper as shown in Figure 18." (Appendix 1 Results of multiscale CLEAN algorithm)
2. **Compute moment maps, position-velocity diagrams, and define cloud velocity components and bridge features**: Compute 0th, 1st, and 2nd moment maps using MIRIAD with a 1.5 K (1 sigma) clipping threshold; construct Declination-velocity and R.A.-velocity diagrams to separate blue, green, red, and intermediate-velocity bridge components  
  > "1st moment map of 12CO (3–2) toward south part of the overlap region derived using miriad task." (Figure 5 caption); "Values larger than 1.5 K (1σ) are used for making 1st and 2nd moment maps." (Figure 5 caption); "By using the 1st and 2nd moments in Figure 5 and channel maps of declination–velocity diagrams in Figures 20, 21, and 22, we defined the two velocity ranges in each SGMC as follows;" (3.1 Intensity Distribution)
   - software: MIRIAD
   - parameter: Clipping threshold > 1.5 K (1 sigma) for 1st and 2nd moments; V_LSR integration range 1300-1795 km s^-1 for total intensity  
  > "The integration velocity range is VLSR = 1300–1795 km s−1." (Figure 2 caption); "Values larger than 1.5 K (1σ) are used for making 1st and 2nd moment maps." (Figure 5 caption)
   - parameter: Velocity ranges: SGMC1 [1350-1460, 1505-1550 km s^-1, bridge 1460-1505 km s^-1]; SGMC2 [1400-1530, 1580-1700 km s^-1, bridge 1530-1580 km s^-1]; SGMC4-5 [1450-1535, 1560-1700 km s^-1, bridge 1535-1560 km s^-1]; Firecracker [1370-1510, 1595-1745 km s^-1, bridge 1510-1595 km s^-1]  
  > "the ranges are 1350–1460 km s−1 and 1505 –1550 km s−1 for SGMC1, 1400–1530 km s−1 and 1580–1700 km s−1 for SGMC2, and 1450–1535 km s−1 and 1560–1700 km s−1 for SGMC4–5." (3.1 Intensity Distribution); "In Figure 15a we redefined the two velocity ranges from [1400–1530 km s−1 and 1580–1700 km s−1] to [1370–1510 km s−1 and 1595–1745 km s−1] by using high-resolution 1st moment map." (4.2 High resolution spatial- and velocity-distributions toward the proto-cluster cloud the Firecracker)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive physical parameters and masses (virial and X_CO) of the cloud components**: Fit Gaussian profiles to spectra at the maximum integrated intensity position of each cloud, measure effective radius enclosed by the 20% peak contour, and calculate M_vir and X_CO mass  
  > "first, we performed spectral line fitting to the Gaussian profile at the position where the integrated intensity is maximum for each cloud, and derived peak velocity, peak intensity, and velocity dispersion." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs); "We calculated the cloud mass of the region enclosed by a contour of 20% of the peak integrated intensity (∼30 σ)." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs)
   - parameter: 20% of peak integrated intensity contour (~30 sigma) for cloud boundary and effective radius r = (A/pi)^0.5  
  > "We define the cloud size as an effective radius of (A/π)0.5, where A is the region enclosed by a contour of 20% of peak integrated intensity." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs)
   - assumption: Uniform density sphere for virial mass: M_vir / M_sun = 210 r (dV)^2  
  > "We use the equation by assuming a uniform density sphere of radius r as follows: Mvir/M⊙ = 210r(dV )2 M⊙ (1)" (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs)
   - assumption: CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO = 0.6 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "XCO mass was estimated by using the CO-to-H2 conversion factor XCO = 0.6×1020 cm−2 (K km s−1)−1 (Zhu et al. 2003; Kamenetzky et al. 2014)." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs)
   - assumption: 12CO (3-2) to 12CO (1-0) ratios: 0.4 +/- 0.1 (SGMC 1), 0.3 +/- 0.1 (SGMC 2), 0.5 +/- 0.1 (SGMC 4-5), 0.3 +/- 0.1 (The Firecracker)  
  > "we adopted the 12CO (3–2) to 12CO (1–0) ratios of SGMC 1, SGMC 2, SGMC 4–5, and the Firecracker are 0.4±0.1, 0.3±0.1, 0.5±0.1, and 0.3±0.1, respectively." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs)
   - assumption: Distance of 22 Mpc  
  > "The Antennae galaxies, the closest major merger at a distance of 22 Mpc (Schweizer et al. 2008)," (1.1 Background)
   - fitting: Gaussian spectral line fitting  
  > "first, we performed spectral line fitting to the Gaussian profile at the position where the integrated intensity is maximum for each cloud, and derived peak velocity, peak intensity, and velocity dispersion." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Quantify spatial displacement between complementary colliding clouds and estimate collision timescales**: Shift one velocity component relative to the other in 1.8 pc pixel steps up to +/- 240 pc, compute Spearman's rank correlation coefficient of integrated intensities to find the minimum, and divide displacement by relative velocity  
  > "Specifically, we moved the red-cloud from the original position to ±240 pc with a 1.8 pc (size of pixel) step, and calculated the correlation coefficient with the integrated intensity of the green-cloud for each pixel" (4.1 SGMC1 and SGMC4-5); "we moved the red-cloud from the original position to ±240 pc with a 1.8 pc (size of pixel) step" (4.1 SGMC1 and SGMC4-5)
   - parameter: Shift range +/- 240 pc, step size 1.8 pc (1 pixel)  
  > "Specifically, we moved the red-cloud from the original position to ±240 pc with a 1.8 pc (size of pixel) step, and calculated the correlation coefficient with the integrated intensity of the green-cloud for each pixel" (4.1 SGMC1 and SGMC4-5)
   - assumption: Collision angle to the line of sight theta = 45 deg (range 30 to 60 deg)  
  > "We thus can assume θ is 30–60 deg. The ideal case of θ= 45 deg. collision gives a collision time scale of 0.6 Myrs for SGMC 4–5." (5.1 Collisions in the three complexes SGMC1, SGMC2, and SGMC4–5)
   - fitting: Minimization of Spearman's correlation coefficient over 2D spatial shifts  
  > "we calculated Spearman’s correlation coefficient between the integrated intensity of the two complementary velocity components, and searched for a position where the correlation coefficient becomes minimum" (4.1 SGMC1 and SGMC4-5)
   - validation: Compared derived collision timescales with literature cluster ages and evolutionary stages  
  > "Moreover, the collision time scale of 0.3–1.2 Myr is roughly consistent with the age of SSC B1 1–3.5 Myr as shown in Table 3." (5.2 Details of the collisional triggering in B1 and the Firecracker)
5. **Evaluate gravitational binding and stellar feedback energy budgets**: Calculate gravitational binding mass M = l sigma^2 / (2G) for SGMC 4-5, and compare cloud kinetic energy to supernova and stellar wind energy injection  
  > "The total mass required to gravitationally bind these two clouds can be calculated as M =l σ2/2G∼3×108 M⊙, where l is separation of the two clouds, and σ is velocity separation of the two clouds." (5.2 Details of the collisional triggering in B1 and the Firecracker); "For the total cloud mass ∼108 M⊙, the total kinetic energy required for the gas motion is estimated to be 1.3×1054 erg." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion)
   - parameter: Expansion velocity ~50 km s^-1 (half of velocity separation)  
  > "If we assume that the expansion velocity is about a half of the velocity separation, the expansion velocity is estimated to be ∼50 km s−1." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion)
   - assumption: Supernova kinetic energy conversion efficiency of 5% (Kruijssen 2012); stellar wind energy conversion efficiency of 20% (Weaver et al. 1977)  
  > "If we adopt an efficiency of conversion of a supernova explosion (SNe) into the gas kinetic motion to be 5% (Kruijssen 2012), the observed kinetic energy requires 26000 SNe’s." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion); "Assuming ideal adiabatic wind bubbles, 20% of the wind luminosity is transferred to the expanding gas shell (Weaver et al. 1977)." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Calculate external compressed gas pressures and fit correlation with SSC total stellar mass**: Calculate external pressure at cloud edge P_e = (3 Pi M v^2) / (4 pi R^3) = rho_e v^2 for Antennae SSCs, R136, and Westerlund 2, and perform linear regression in log-log space using MPFITEXY  
  > "We calculated the external pressure at the cloud edge (P e ) from the cloud’s mass, size, and colliding velocity following the equation (Elmegreen 1989)" (5.5 Comparison with the other clusters); "We performed linear fittings using the MPFITEXY routine, which provides the slope and intercept values (Williams et al. 2010)." (5.5 Comparison with the other clusters)
   - software: MPFITEXY
   - parameter: R = 150 pc extraction radius around C, D, D1, and D2

## Qualitative findings

- SGMC 1, SGMC 2, and SGMC 4-5 in the Antennae overlap region exhibit two velocity components separated by ~100 km s^-1, intermediate-velocity bridge features, and complementary spatial distributions characteristic of cloud-cloud collisions.  
  > "found three typical observational signatures of a cloud-cloud collision toward SSC B1 and other SSCs in the overlap region; i. two velocity components with ∼100 km s−1 velocity separation, ii. bridge features connecting the two components, and iii. a complementary spatial distribution between them" (abstract)
- Towards both SSC B1 and the proto-cluster candidate the Firecracker, a smaller cloud is associated with a matching cavity in a larger cloud, indicating a head-on collision.  
  > "In both of these two regions we find complementary distributions between the two clouds at 50 pc resolution for B1 and 15 pc resolution for the Firecracker. In both, a small cloud is associated with a cavity in a larger cloud." (6 Conclusions)
- Stellar feedback (supernovae and stellar winds) is insufficient by an order of magnitude to explain the ~100 km s^-1 velocity separation and ~10^54 erg kinetic energy of the molecular clouds.  
  > "Then, 2×1053 erg of stellar wind energy is converted into the expansion energy, which is an order of magnitude smaller to explain the gas motion." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion); "We present estimates that the gas motion cannot be explained by the stellar feedback if we employ the typical supernova kinetic energy as the energy source." (6 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The Antennae is the closest major merger actively forming thousands of SSCs, and archival ALMA Cycle 0 and Cycle 4 CO (3-2) data resolve the molecular gas distribution and kinematics down to ~50 pc and ~15 pc scales.  
  > "The ALMA CO data offer an ideal opportunity to investigate the molecular gas distribution and kinematics which are likely connected to the cluster formation and feedback at resolution better than 10 pc." (1.1 Background); "In order to resolve the complementary spatial distribution toward the Firecracker, we analyzed the spatial distribution of the CO cloud in SGMC2 by using the Cycle 4 data (Finn et al. 2019) whose resolution is ∼3 times higher than Cycle 0 data" (4.2 High resolution spatial- and velocity-distributions toward the proto-cluster cloud the Firecracker)
- Why this method: Multiscale CLEAN with natural weighting recovers extended CO emission and reduces negative bowls compared to Briggs weighting; synthetic observations of numerical simulations (Fukui et al. 2018a) validate complementary spatial distributions and velocity bridges as cloud-cloud collision signatures.  
  > "Fukui et al. (2018a) made synthetic observations of cloud-cloud collision by employing the numerical simulations of collision of Takahira et al. (2014), and confirmed these two observable signatures as viable." (1.2 The triggered formation of SSCs); "We recovered extended emission and reduced negative levels by using multiscale CLEAN algorithm (see Figure 2). Total flux is recovered ∼54%, and hence the extended components are important." (2.1 Cycle 0 data toward the overlap region: GMC scale)
- Alternative: Stellar feedback (radiation pressure, stellar winds, supernovae) as the driver of the ~100 km s^-1 velocity separation in SGMCs was considered and rejected based on kinetic energy requirements and ring-like cloud morphology.  
  > "Herrera & Boulanger (2017) proposed a possible scenario that the spatial distribution and velocity separation in SGMC4–5 (Figure 9) originated by radiation pressure as an outflowing gas with a velocity separation of ∼100 km s−1." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion); "In summary, the stellar feedback energy is unlikely to cause expansion of the clouds even if the energy is totally converted to expansion." (5.4 Possible role of stellar feedback in accelerating the gas motion)
- Alternative: Gravitationally bound system for the two velocity components in SGMC 4-5 was tested and rejected because the required binding mass (~3 x 10^8 M_odot) is three times larger than the observed molecular mass (8.5 x 10^7 M_odot).  
  > "This value is a factor of three larger than the total molecular mass of 8.5×107 M⊙ calculated in section 3.2. This indicates that the coexistence of the two velocity clouds are not in a gravitationally bound system." (5.2 Details of the collisional triggering in B1 and the Firecracker)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO can vary by a factor of three due to CO abundance variations, and CO(3-2)/(1-0) ratios have 20-30% uncertainties, leading to 20-50% relative errors in cloud mass and density.  
  > "The value of XCO possibly changes by a factor of three because of the difference of the relative abundance of CO to H2." (3.2 Physical properties of SGMCs); "From these uncertainties, the relative errors of density of colliding clouds are estimated to be 20–50%, and average value is ∼30 %." (5.5 Comparison with the other clusters)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA Cycle 0 data miss ~20% of the flux compared to single-dish JCMT observations.  
  > "Although the ALMA flux is ∼20% smaller than the single-dish data, the relative error in mass estimation is 20–50% with an average of 30%, as shown in Table 2." (2.1 Cycle 0 data toward the overlap region: GMC scale)
- Limitation (authors): Collision timescales have a factor of two uncertainty due to the unknown inclination angle (theta) of the collision path relative to the line of sight.  
  > "we roughly estimate θ is in a range from 30 deg. to 60 deg. corresponding to the time scale in a range of 0.3-1.2 Myrs. This gives an uncertainty of 0.6+0.6 −0.3 Myrs." (5.1 Collisions in the three complexes SGMC1, SGMC2, and SGMC4–5)
- Limitation (authors): Current MHD simulations of colliding molecular flows are limited to collision velocities < 30 km s^-1, lower than the ~100 km s^-1 velocities observed in the Antennae.  
  > "at present the MHD simulations by Inoue & Fukui (2013) of colliding molecular flows is limited to a low velocity less than 30 km s−1." (5.5 Comparison with the other clusters)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Velocity ranges for the blue, green, red, and bridge components are adjusted somewhat subjectively across different figures and SGMCs (e.g., SGMC4-5 green cloud is 1450-1535 km/s in Section 3.1, 1470-1530 km/s in Fig. 9, and misprinted as 1530-1580 km/s in Fig. 12 caption; SGMC2 green cloud is misprinted as 1580-1700 km/s in Fig. 11 caption).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Spearman correlation minimization boxes (Figs. 13 and 14) are chosen around specific sub-regions; sensitivity of the displacement vector to box size and boundary selection is not quantified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the P_e vs. M_cluster correlation (Fig. 17), five of the seven data points come from the Antennae at virtually identical collision velocities (~100-120 km/s) and pressures (~10^8 K cm^-3), so the dynamic range of the fit is entirely anchored by two external systems (R136 in HI and Westerlund 2 in CO).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For older clusters (D1, D2, C with ages 4.8-6.1 Myr), associating currently observed molecular gas with the parent collision event assumes gas survival and coherence over several dynamical times (~0.3-1.2 Myr).
