# On dust evolution in planet-forming discs in binary systems - II. Comparison with Taurus and ρ Ophiuchus (sub-)millimetre observations: discs in binaries have small dust sizes

## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks
- Question: Whether theoretical models of dust evolution (grain growth and radial drift) in binary protoplanetary discs agree with ALMA (sub-)millimetre observations of disc dust sizes, tidal truncation radii, and the flux-radius correlation in Taurus and rho Ophiuchus.  
  > "In this paper we confront our theoretical and numerical results with observations: disc dust fluxes and sizes from our models are computed at ALMA wavelengths and compared with Taurus and $\rho$ Ophiuchus data." (Abstract); "In comparing our models with the observations, there are two issues in particular we would like to focus on: the relationship between disc dust sizes and the tidal truncation radius, as well as the flux-radius correlation."   > "In Fig. [1](#fig.2.1a) the $0.85\text{ mm}$ flux of each binary pair, $F_\text{pair}$, re-scaled to a distance $d=140\text{ pc}$, is plotted as a function of the binary projected separation, $a_\text{p}$." (A logbook of binary disc observations); "We use the `linmix` package[^5] to perform a three-Gaussian hierarchical Bayesian linear regression of the data (cite: Kelly07_2007ApJ...665.1489K) in the log space." (A logbook of binary disc observations)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: 10 chains and 2.5x10^4 steps; non-detection uncertainty assumed as 1/3 of quoted upper limit; pairs closer than 0.15 arcsec in Harris et al. (2012) excluded as circumbinary  
  > "In the latter case, uncertainties on the non-detections have been assumed as a third of the quoted upper limit." (A logbook of binary disc observations); "We employed 10 chains and $2.5\times10^4$ steps." (A logbook of binary disc observations)
   - assumption: F_nu proportional to nu^2 between 0.85 mm and 1.33 mm; reference distance d = 140 pc  
  > "To convert the fluxes to $0.85\text{ mm}$, in this paper we assume that $F_\nu\propto\nu^2$." (A logbook of binary disc observations)

  > "We use the `linmix` package[^5] to perform a three-Gaussian hierarchical Bayesian linear regression of the data (cite: Kelly07_2007ApJ...665.1489K) in the log space." (A logbook of binary disc observations)

\end{equation}$$" (Model description and numerical methods); "Once the surface brightness profile has been determined, we compute the dust flux of a model disc as: $$\begin{equation}

\end{equation}$$" (Model description and numerical methods); "we define the 68-per-cent-flux radius, $R_\mathrm{68,mod}$, and the 95-per-cent-flux radius, $R_\mathrm{95,mod}$, as the disc sizes enclosing at a given time 68 per cent and 95 per cent of the model dust flux, respectively." ## Qualitative findings

- Measured dust disc sizes in binary systems are smaller than the tidal truncation radius because of grain growth and radial drift, meaning small observed dust sizes are consistent with zero-eccentricity orbits within 1 sigma.  
  > "In particular, we show that the dust disc sizes are generally smaller than the binary truncation radius due to the combined effect of grain growth and radial drift: therefore, small disc sizes do not require implausibly high eccentricities to be explained." (Abstract); "Fig. [4](#fig.2.6) shows that the *measured* dust radii are compatible with our zero-eccentricity models within their $1\sigma$ spread." (Do models and observations agree?)
- Binary pair continuum fluxes increase continuously with projected separation across Taurus, rho Ophiuchus, and Lupus, rather than in discrete jumps.  
  > "Moreover, $F_\text{pair}$ appears to increase continuously with $a_\text{p}$, rather than in discrete jumps as stated in (cite: Harris+12_2012ApJ...751..115H)." (A logbook of binary disc observations)
- Observed binary discs in Taurus and rho Ophiuchus show a tentative flux-radius correlation; Taurus binaries are compatible within 1 sigma with the single-star quadratic relation of Tripathi et al. (2017).  
  > "Taurus best-fit parameters (slope and intercept) in Tab. [2](#tab:5.1) are compatible with the (cite: Tripathi+17_2017ApJ...845...44T) ones ($\alpha= 2.12\pm0.05$, $\beta=0.50\pm0.07$) within $1\sigma$" (Flux-radius correlation in binary disc observations)
- Binary disc models in the drift-dominated regime follow the single-star flux-radius correlation at almost all times for wide binaries (R_trunc >= 100 au), and after t ~ 1 Myr for closer binaries.  
  > "To summarise, our models follow the flux-radius correlation in (cite: Tripathi+17_2017ApJ...845...44T) in wide binaries ($R_\text{trunc}\gtrsim100\text{ au}$) at almost all times. However, for a smaller $R_\text{trunc}$ this is true only after $t\sim1\text{ Myr}$." (Flux-radius correlation in binary disc models)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Taurus (Manara et al. 2019, ~0.12 arcsec) and rho Ophiuchus (Cox et al. 2017, ~0.20 arcsec) ALMA surveys provide high enough angular resolution to spatially resolve primary and secondary binary disc components.  
  > "Recently, (cite: Manara+19_2019A&A...628A..95M) provided the first homogeneous survey of discs in multiple stellar systems in the unbiased sample of Taurus sources in (cite: Long+18_2018ApJ...869...17L; Long+19_2019ApJ...882...49L) whose resolution ($\sim0.12$ arcsec) was high enough to spatially resolve both the circumstellar binary discs." (Introduction); "The angular resolution of the latter survey ($\sim0.20$ arcsec) is high-enough to resolve the primary component of all binaries and, at least marginally, several secondaries." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Fitting the rho Ophiuchus continuum emission in the visibility plane with galario and emcee using the exact same functional profile as Manara et al. (2019) ensures a homogeneous sample of disc fluxes and sizes across both star-forming regions.  
  > "For this reason, to be consistent with the analysis in (cite: Manara+19_2019A&A...628A..95M), we fit the dust continuum emission in (cite: Cox+17_2017ApJ...851...83C) discs in multiple stellar systems in the visibility plane employing the state-of-the-art techniques used in Taurus (cite: Tazzari+18_2018MNRAS.476.4527T). This is needed for a homogeneous treatment of the data in the two samples." (Introduction)
