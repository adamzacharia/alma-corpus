## Science

- Fields: stellar_physics, transients
- Question: Can X-ray or radio/(sub-)mm disk-jet coupling signatures act as indicators for whether an outburst of GX 339-4 will remain in the hard X-ray spectral state ('hard-only') rather than completing a full outburst?  
  > "Finally, we compare our results to other outbursts of GX 339$-$4, both hard-only and successful, searching for any X-ray or radio signatures that may act as indicators for an outburst only remaining in the hard or intermediate states" (Introduction); "This result is suggestive that for GX 339$-$4 a flatter radio – X-ray correlation, implying a more inefficient coupling between the jet and accretion flow, could act as an indicator for a hard-only outburst." (Abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and image ALMA sub-mm continuum and measure integrated and sub-band flux densities**: Multi-frequency synthesis continuum imaging in CASA using tclean with natural weighting (both full 8 GHz bands and 2 GHz sub-bands), followed by point source fitting in the image plane with imfit  
  > "To image the continuum emission, we performed multi-frequency synthesis imaging on the data using the tclean task within CASA, with natural weighting to maximize sensitivity." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data); "Flux densities of the source were then measured by fitting a point source in the image plane (using the imfit task)." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data); "Imaging the ALMA data on finer frequency scales (in 2 GHz sub-bands for all bands) shows that the in-band ALMA spectral indices were flat/inverted throughout the observation (Table 3)." (Intra-observational variability)
   - software: CASA, tclean, imfit
   - parameter: natural weighting; central frequencies 97.5, 145, 233 GHz (8 GHz full bandwidth and 2 GHz sub-bands)  
  > "Data were taken sequentially in Bands 3, 4, and 6, at central frequencies of 97.5, 145, and 233 GHz, respectively." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data); "Imaging the ALMA data on finer frequency scales (in 2 GHz sub-bands for all bands) shows that the in-band ALMA spectral indices were flat/inverted throughout the observation (Table 3)." (Intra-observational variability)
   - assumption: 5% systematic uncertainty on absolute flux density calibration for ALMA bands <350 GHz  
  > "Systematic errors were applied to the absolute flux density calibration of the ALMA data, where an uncertainty of 5% is expected for ALMA bands $<$350 GHz." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data)
   - fitting: Point source fit in the image plane using imfit  
  > "Flux densities of the source were then measured by fitting a point source in the image plane (using the imfit task)." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data)
   - validation: Check source J1631-5256 observed and checked for flux stability  
  > "We used J1617–5848/J1427–4206 as bandpass & flux calibrators, J1650–5044 as a phase calibrator, and J1631–5256 as a check source, for all the observations." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data)
2. **Reduce and image ATCA radio continuum and measure flux densities, luminosities, and spectral indices**: Standard calibration and natural-weighted imaging in CASA, image-plane Gaussian fitting with FWHM fixed to the synthesised beam, and Monte Carlo fitting for spectral index alpha  
  > "Each frequency band was imaged with natural weighting to maximize sensitivity." (ATCA radio data); "To determine the radio flux density, $S_{\nu}$, of the source for each epoch, we fit for a point source in the image plane, where we use a Gaussian with a full width half maximum (FWHM) equal to the synthesised beam of the observation." (ATCA radio data); "Radio spectral indices ($\alpha$, where $S_{\nu} \propto \nu^{\alpha}$; (cite: 2001MNRAS.322...31F)) have been determined by Monte Carlo fitting using all the radio bands that were available for each date." (ATCA radio data)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 5.5, 9, 17, 19 GHz; 2 GHz bandwidth per band  
  > "Observations were carried out at central frequencies of 5.5 and 9 GHz on all dates and additionally at 17 and 19 GHz on 5 dates, see Table 2." (ATCA radio data)
   - assumption: Source distance D = 8 kpc; systematic flux scale uncertainty of 2% at 5.5/9 GHz and 4% at 17/19 GHz  
  > "In this work, we adopt a source distance of 8 kpc (cite: 2004MNRAS.351..791Z; 2019MNRAS.488.1026Z; infogx3394)." (Swift-XRT); "Errors on the absolute flux density scale include systematic uncertainties of 2% for the 5.5/9 GHz data (e.g., (cite: 2011MNRAS.415.1597M); (cite: 2012MNRAS.422.1527M)) and 4% for the 17/19 GHz data (cite: 2010MNRAS.402.2403M; 2016ApJ...821...61P)." (ATCA radio data)
   - fitting: Point-source Gaussian fit in image plane; Monte Carlo fitting for power-law spectral index  
  > "Radio spectral indices ($\alpha$, where $S_{\nu} \propto \nu^{\alpha}$; (cite: 2001MNRAS.322...31F)) have been determined by Monte Carlo fitting using all the radio bands that were available for each date." (ATCA radio data)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract intra-observational radio and sub-mm light curves and quantify short-timescale variability**: Extract 5-min time-resolved ATCA light curves at 5.5 and 9 GHz using UVMULTIFIT in the uv-plane and 30-sec time-resolved ALMA sub-mm light curves across Bands 3, 4, and 6, then compute fractional RMS variability statistic F_var  
  > "For each time interval, we used UVMULTIFIT to fit for a point source (which is a delta function in the uv-plane) at the target position (as well as all other sources in the field when detectable)." (ATCA radio data); "Figure 10 displays intra-observation light curves from the ATCA radio data sampled on 5-min timescales, while Figure 11 displays intra-observation light curves from the ALMA sub-mm data sampled on 30-sec timescales." (Intra-observational variability); "To characterize the amplitude of any variability and compare between frequency bands, we use the fractional root mean square (RMS) statistic" (Intra-observational variability)
   - software: UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: 5-min time intervals for ATCA; 30-sec time intervals for ALMA  
  > "Figure 10 displays intra-observation light curves from the ATCA radio data sampled on 5-min timescales, while Figure 11 displays intra-observation light curves from the ALMA sub-mm data sampled on 30-sec timescales." (Intra-observational variability)
   - assumption: Point source (delta function in uv-plane for ATCA)  
  > "For each time interval, we used UVMULTIFIT to fit for a point source (which is a delta function in the uv-plane) at the target position (as well as all other sources in the field when detectable)." (ATCA radio data)
   - fitting: uv-plane delta-function fitting with UVMULTIFIT for ATCA  
  > "For each time interval, we used UVMULTIFIT to fit for a point source (which is a delta function in the uv-plane) at the target position (as well as all other sources in the field when detectable)." (ATCA radio data)
   - validation: Comparison against intra-observation light curves of check sources in ALMA (J1631-5256, <10% variation) and ATCA fields  
  > "To ensure that the sub-mm variations we observe from GX 339$-$4 are intrinsic to the source, and not due to atmospheric or instrumental effects, we also extracted intra-observation light curves for our check source." (Intra-observational variability); "We find that the check source shows relatively constant flux densities throughout our observations in all the bands (with any variations present being $<10$% of the average flux density)" (Intra-observational variability)
4. **Fit X-ray spectra and measure X-ray fluxes, photon indices, hardness ratios, and luminosities**: Fit 0.5-10 keV Swift-XRT spectra in XSPEC with an absorbed power-law (tbabs*powerlaw) and extract unabsorbed fluxes with cflux; convert MAXI 2-20 keV count rates during Sun constraint to 1-10 keV fluxes using WebPIMMS  
  > "The data were analysed using XSPEC (version 12.8; (cite: 1996ASPC..101...17A)) from HEASOFT software package (version 6.21)." (Swift-XRT); "The 0.5–10 keV X-ray spectra were modelled with an absorbed power-law (TBABS$\times$POWERLAW) within XSPEC." (Swift-XRT); "During times when Swift-XRT was sun constrained but radio observations were taken (MJDs 58080, 58090, 58103 and 58123), we used the MAXI count rates to estimate the X-ray flux and luminosity (using WebPIMMS)." (MAXI)
   - software: HEASOFT, XSPEC, WebPIMMS
   - parameter: Energy bands: 1–10 keV, 0.5–10 keV, 0.5–1.5 keV, and 1.5–10 keV; tied N_H = (0.56 +/- 0.02) x 10^22 cm^-2  
  > "Tying the line of sight equivalent hydrogen absorption, $N_{\rm H}$, across all epochs provided $N_{\rm H}$ = $(0.56 \pm 0.02) \times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Swift-XRT); "The X-ray fluxes were measured for four different energy bands, namely 1–10 keV, 0.5–10 keV, 0.5–1.5 keV and 1.5–10 keV." (Swift-XRT)
   - assumption: Distance D = 8 kpc; M_BH = 7.8 M_sun for Eddington luminosity; Gamma = 1.5 - 2.5 for MAXI flux conversion  
  > "To do this, we used the $N_{\rm H}$ found from our Swift-XRT analysis ($N_{\rm H} = (0.56 \pm 0.02) \times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$), and assumed $\Gamma$ to be 1.5 – 2.5, conservatively estimating the 1–10 keV X-ray flux from the 2 – 20 keV MAXI count rate (Table 1)." (MAXI); "assuming a distance of 8 kpc and $M_{BH} = 7.8\,M_{\odot}$, which is the peak of the best-fit mass distribution of the BH LMXB population; (cite: 2010ApJ...725.1918O)" (Source brightness)
   - fitting: Joint spectral fitting across epochs in XSPEC with N_H tied  
  > "Tying the line of sight equivalent hydrogen absorption, $N_{\rm H}$, across all epochs provided $N_{\rm H}$ = $(0.56 \pm 0.02) \times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Swift-XRT)
   - validation: Tested fixing N_H to literature value (0.851 x 10^22 cm^-2) or leaving N_H free across epochs; conclusions unchanged  
  > "We also compared all results with $N_{\rm H}$ either fixed to literature values ($N_{\rm H}$$_{\rm ,fixed} = 0.851 \times 10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$; (cite: 2015ApJ...808..122F)), or left free (untied) for all epochs, finding our conclusions were not altered by the method that was used." (Swift-XRT)
5. **Fit the radio-X-ray luminosity correlation and compare hard-only vs successful outbursts**: Perform linear regression in logarithmic space on quasi-simultaneous radio and X-ray luminosities using Python LINMIX_ERR (10,000 iterations), and compare hard-only and successful outburst distributions with a Monte-Carlo 2D 2-sample K-S test  
  > "We used the PYTHON implementation of the linear regression algorithm LINMIX_ERR (cite: 2007ApJ...665.1489K) to perform a (10,000 iteration) linear fit to the radio and X-ray luminosities in logarithmic space, finding a flatter than usual radio – X-ray coupling for GX 339$-$4, where $L_{\rm r} \propto L_{\rm X}^{0.39 \pm 0.06}$ for this hard-only outburst (Figure 8)." (The radio – X-ray correlation); "Performing a Monte-Carlo 2-dimensional 2-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (K-S) test (cite: 1983MNRAS.202..615P; 1987MNRAS.225..155F) on the successful and hard-only data sets yields a $p$-value of 0.031" (The radio – X-ray correlation)
   - software: LINMIX_ERR, ndtest
   - parameter: 10,000 iterations in LINMIX_ERR; quasi-simultaneous data within 1 day (MJD 58058 interpolated assuming exponential rise)  
  > "We used the PYTHON implementation of the linear regression algorithm LINMIX_ERR (cite: 2007ApJ...665.1489K) to perform a (10,000 iteration) linear fit to the radio and X-ray luminosities in logarithmic space" (The radio – X-ray correlation); "Therefore, to best estimate the simultaneous radio luminosity, we fit the radio light curve assuming an exponential rise to estimate the 5.5 GHz flux density at the time of the X-ray observation." (Footnote 14)
   - assumption: Power-law relation between L_r and L_X (linear in logarithmic space)  
  > "We used the PYTHON implementation of the linear regression algorithm LINMIX_ERR (cite: 2007ApJ...665.1489K) to perform a (10,000 iteration) linear fit to the radio and X-ray luminosities in logarithmic space" (The radio – X-ray correlation)
   - fitting: Bayesian linear regression via LINMIX_ERR with 10,000 iterations  
  > "We used the PYTHON implementation of the linear regression algorithm LINMIX_ERR (cite: 2007ApJ...665.1489K) to perform a (10,000 iteration) linear fit to the radio and X-ray luminosities in logarithmic space" (The radio – X-ray correlation)
   - validation: Repeated fits excluding MAXI data points and confining successful outburst data to the 2017-2018 luminosity range  
  > "As such, excluding the MAXI points did not change our results, providing a best-fit relation of $L_{\rm r} \propto L_{\rm X}^{0.3 \pm 0.1}$." (The radio – X-ray correlation); "Confining the successful outburst data from (cite: corbel) so that the data are within our observed 2017–2018 radio and X-ray luminosities, we find a best fit of $L_{\rm r} \propto L_{\rm X}^{0.61 \pm 0.04}$ (Figure 9, right panel), showing that there isn’t a consistent deviation at these X-ray luminosities." (The radio – X-ray correlation)

## Qualitative findings

- The radio and sub-mm emission during the 2017–2018 outburst had a predominantly flat-to-inverted spectrum consistent with a steady, partially self-absorbed compact jet that remained active and unquenched throughout the outburst.  
  > "Therefore, the radio and sub-mm results are consistent with emission from a compact, partially self-absorbed synchrotron jet. This emission remained on for the entire outburst, with no indication of the jet emission being quenched in our observations." (Radio/sub-mm lightcurves)
- Clear short-timescale (minute-scale) structured flaring is present in the ALMA sub-mm bands, with higher fractional variability amplitude at higher frequencies, consistent with emission originating closer to the base of a variable compact jet.  
  > "In the ALMA sub-mm data, we detect clear short-timescale variability, in the form of structured, small-scale flaring episodes across all three bands sampled." (Intra-observational variability); "Comparing the variability properties between the radio and sub-mm bands, we see a pattern of higher variability amplitudes at higher electromagnetic frequencies." (Intra-observational variability)
- The X-ray spectral evolution (photon index Gamma and hardness ratio) of the 2017–2018 hard-only outburst follows a standard 'V'-shaped evolution with flux and shows no preliminary X-ray signature that distinguishes hard-only from successful outbursts.  
  > "Therefore, the 2017–2018 X-ray outburst of GX 339$-$4 seems to follow a relatively typical evolution when compared to the X-ray behaviour of both hard-only and successful outbursts, with no preliminary indicators that may identify whether an outburst is successful or not." (Comparison with other outbursts)
- Hard-only outbursts of GX 339-4 exhibit a significantly flatter radio–X-ray luminosity correlation than successful outbursts, which may indicate a more inefficient coupling between the accretion flow and jet.  
  > "Combining our 2017–2018 outburst data with those from other hard-only outbursts from this system also yielded a flatter than typical correlation, where $L_{\rm r} \propto L_{\rm X}^{0.46 \pm 0.04}$, which may arise from a more inefficient coupling between the accretion flow and the jets" (Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GX 339-4 is one of the best-studied BH LMXBs with numerous successful and hard-only outbursts, and early optical discovery by XB-NEWS triggered quasi-simultaneous radio, sub-mm, and X-ray monitoring from low luminosities during the rise phase.  
  > "Undergoing numerous successful and hard-only outbursts over the past few decades (cite: watchdog), GX 339$-$4 is one of the best studied BH LMXBs (cite: 2004MNRAS.351..791Z; 2012AJ....143..130B), particularly at both radio and X-ray wavelengths (cite: corbel)." (GX 339$-$4); "The 2017–2018 hard-only outburst of GX 339$-$4 was well monitored in the radio and X-ray bands at low X-ray luminosities during the rise phase due to the early discovery of the outburst from regular optical monitoring (cite: 2017ATel10797....1R)." (Source brightness)
- Why this method: Natural weighting was used in radio and sub-mm imaging to maximize sensitivity; UVMULTIFIT was used on ATCA data to measure short-timescale variability despite sparse uv-coverage; LINMIX_ERR and a 2D K-S test were used to quantify and compare L_r-L_X slopes and distributions.  
  > "To image the continuum emission, we performed multi-frequency synthesis imaging on the data using the tclean task within CASA, with natural weighting to maximize sensitivity." (ALMA (sub)-millimetre data); "We used the PYTHON implementation of the linear regression algorithm LINMIX_ERR (cite: 2007ApJ...665.1489K) to perform a (10,000 iteration) linear fit to the radio and X-ray luminosities in logarithmic space" (The radio – X-ray correlation)
- Alternative: Transient jet ejecta as the cause of the steep ATCA spectral index (alpha = -0.44 +/- 0.13) on MJD 58080, rejected in favour of compact jet intra-observational variability because transient ejecta are not expected at low X-ray luminosity and hard X-ray spectrum without sudden X-ray changes.  
  > "This is suggestive that the steep spectral index from this epoch could be the result of compact jet variability (where we might expect a delay between variability features of up to tens of minutes between these radio bands, towards the lower frequency band; (cite: 2019MNRAS.484.2987T))." (Radio/sub-mm lightcurves); "In addition, we do not expect transient ejecta (which tend to show a steep spectrum) to be launched at such a low X-ray luminosity and hard X-ray spectrum, where there does not appear to be any sudden X-ray changes." (Radio/sub-mm lightcurves)
- Alternative: Synchrotron emission from the jet (and shifting of the cooling break) dominating the low-luminosity X-ray spectrum during the rise phase, rejected because NuSTAR detected strong X-ray reprocessing signatures and because synchrotron-cooled X-rays would produce a steeper rather than shallower radio-X-ray correlation.  
  > "using broadband NuSTAR X-ray observations, (cite: 2019ApJ...885...48G) detected strong signatures of X-ray reprocessing on 2017-10-02 (see also (cite: 2020ApJ...899...44W)), which are not expected if the X-ray emission is synchrotron in origin (cite: 2004ApJ...609..972M)." (X-ray spectral evolution); "In addition, a further argument against synchrotron cooled X-rays is that they are expected to yield a steeper correlation between the radio and X-ray luminosities (cite: 2005ApJ...629..408Y), which we did not observe, instead detecting a shallower correlation" (X-ray spectral evolution)
- How they decided it was enough: Because the 2017-2018 outburst alone spanned less than the >2 orders of magnitude in L_X needed for a reliable correlation slope, archival hard-only outbursts from 2008, 2009, and 2012 were added to extend the dynamic range to ~2.8 orders of magnitude in L_X.  
  > "While our 2017–2018 outburst data only span a limited radio and X-ray luminosity range ($\sim$1 order of magnitude in both, where more than 2-orders of magnitude in $L_{\rm X}$ is desired), including data from other hard-only outbursts from GX 339$-$4 extends the luminosity range to $\sim$1.2 and $\sim$2.8 orders of magnitude, respectively" (Abstract)
- Limitation (authors): Sun constraints prevented Swift-XRT observations from early November 2017 to mid-January 2018 during the outburst peak, requiring reliance on poor-quality MAXI images and an assumed photon index (Gamma = 1.5–2.5) to estimate X-ray fluxes.  
  > "However, from the beginning of November 2017 until mid-January 2018, constraints due to the proximity of the Sun prevented Swift-XRT from obtaining data of GX 339$-$4." (Swift-XRT); "We do note that the unknown values of $\Gamma$ may make these fluxes unreliable, however, the range taken for $\Gamma$ provides a conservative estimate" (MAXI)
- Limitation (authors): The 2017–2018 outburst data alone span a limited luminosity range (~1 order of magnitude in L_r and ~1.5 in L_X), over which individual outbursts can deviate significantly from the global L_r-L_X correlation.  
  > "(cite: corbel) suggested that over luminosity ranges less than two orders of magnitude, the $L_{\rm r} - L_{\rm X}$ correlation index may deviate substantially (where indices of between 0 and 2 have been observed)." (The radio – X-ray correlation); "Here, our radio and X-ray data only span a range of $\lesssim$1 order of magnitude in $L_{\rm r}$ and $\gtrsim$1.5 orders of magnitude in $L_{\rm X}$ (Figure 8)." (The radio – X-ray correlation)
- Limitation (authors): ATCA radio observations had sparse uv-coverage and were too faint for self-calibration, so short-timescale radio variability could only be probed down to 5-min bins and may be affected by phase decorrelation or gain drifts.  
  > "Additionally, ATCA is a 6-element linear array meaning that the limited instantaneous uv-coverage did not allow variability shorter than 5-min intervals to be tested." (ATCA radio data); "While this may indicate a mildly variable source at times, the source was too faint for self-calibration and, therefore, the results may suffer from phase decorrelation or gain drifts." (Intra-observational variability)
- Limitation (authors): Radio and X-ray observations were only quasi-simultaneous (within 1 day) rather than strictly simultaneous, so intra-observational jet variability may impact the measured L_r-L_X correlation.  
  > "However, our radio and sub-mm monitoring indicate that the compact jet was variable within single observations (Section 3.2.1). Therefore, without strictly simultaneous radio and X-ray observations, such variability may impact our findings." (The radio – X-ray correlation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA observation covers only a single epoch (2017 Oct 05), so sub-mm behaviour and fast variability are not tracked across the outburst evolution.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 2D K-S test p-value of 0.031 between hard-only and successful outbursts is only marginally significant (~2.2 sigma) and combines heterogeneous archival data.
