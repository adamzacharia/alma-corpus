## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble the ALMA-selected sample**: Use the ASPECS 1.2 mm continuum sample; discard three possible false positives; retain two CO-only sources for completeness.  
  > "The flux-limited ASPECS sample consists of all galaxies that are detected in the deep 1.2 mm dust continuum image from ASPECS" (Sample); "From this sample, we discard three sources (1mm.C27, 1mm.C29, and 1mm.C34) that remain without counterparts in the optical-FIR and also the new JWST imaging and are potentially false-positives" (Sample); "apart from two sources detected in $^{12}$CO only" (Sample)
   - parameter: 35 galaxies; 28 inside the MIRI/F560W footprint; z = 0.45–3.71  
  > "The complete sample consists of 35 galaxies, 28 of which are inside of the MIRI/F560W footprint in the HUDF" (Sample); "and span a redshift range of $z=0.45$–3.71." (Sample)
   - assumption: 1.2 mm selection is essentially a cold dust mass selection and effectively a cold molecular gas mass selection.  
  > "the 1.2 mm selection is essentially a cold dust mass selection (the phase which contains most of the dust mass) and effectively also a cold, molecular gas mass selection" (Sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Rejected sources lack optical–FIR and JWST counterparts; rejection is consistent with sample fidelity estimates.  
  > "that remain without counterparts in the optical-FIR and also the new JWST imaging and are potentially false-positives, consistent with the fidelity estimates of the sample (cite: Gonzalez-Lopez2020)." (Sample)
2. **Complete spectroscopic redshifts**: Extract FRESCO spectra, remove continuum with a median filter and search around known redshifts; use NGDEEP for 1mm.C2.  
  > "We extract FRESCO spectra for all ALMA sources, removing the continuum using a median filter, and search for bright emission lines in a $\Delta z/(1+z) = 0.1$ window around the known redshift." (Redshifts); "in the NGDEEP slitless spectroscopy (cite: Bagley2023b; Pirzkal2023)." (Redshifts)
   - parameter: Δz/(1+z) = 0.1 search window  
  > "search for bright emission lines in a $\Delta z/(1+z) = 0.1$ window around the known redshift." (Redshifts)
   - parameter: FRESCO F444W; GrismR; R ∼ 1600  
  > "These observations were taken in the F444W filter" (JWST Observations); "and a single grism orientation (GrismR) with a resolution of $R\sim1600$." (JWST Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirm earlier photometric redshifts; all sample sources now have spectroscopic redshifts.  
  > "Notably, we confirm the earlier photometric redshifts for all sources (cite: Aravena2020)." (Redshifts); "Notably, all sources in the sample now have a spectroscopic redshift." (Redshifts)
3. **Construct position-dependent F560W PSFs**: Stack LMC stars in three detector-position bins; insert empirical PSFs into MIDIS calibrated exposures and difference resampled runs with and without inserted stars.  
  > "Instead, we create the empirical PSFs by stacking stars in the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) images from the commissioning program ID 1473 and two calibration programs (IDs: 1040 and 1024)." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "The images are resampled keeping the native detector orientation, which enables us to separately stack three bins of stars, situated in upper, middle, and lower detector positions." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "We then run the Stage 3 Imaging Processing twice, once with the inserted stars and once without, and recover the varying PSF model by taking the difference." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
   - software: JWST pipeline, PHOTUTILS, photutils.detection, resample
   - parameter: LMC frames resampled to 55 mas; 64 stars; detector bins of 31, 21 and 12 stars  
  > "The F560W frames in each program are reduced and resampled to a pixel scale of 55 mas (i.e., oversampled by a factor of 2) using the standard JWST pipeline." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "We then select a total of 64 stars in the stacked frames." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "The total number of stars in these bins is 31, 21, and 12, respectively." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
   - parameter: ∼16 arcsec stamps; local background subtraction; mask faint sources  
  > "A stamp of size $\sim16$ arcsec is cut out for each star and a local background is subtracted." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "Faint sources are masked in each stamp using standard source detection techniques (`photutils.detection`)." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
   - parameter: Normalize within 2 pixels (0.11''); median stack, dropping the 2 brightest and faintest data points  
  > "We scale the star stamps to have the same flux within a radius of 2 pixels (0.11$''$, measured using `photutils`)" (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "and stack the stars in each detector position bin by using median stacking (dropping the 2 brightest and faintest data points)." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
   - parameter: 96 calibrated exposures; only resample enabled; 9 effective MIDIS PSFs  
  > "into each of the 96 individual exposures (i.e., WCS-aligned `cal` files)." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "To ensure the pipeline produces identical results in the different runs, we turn off all steps except the `resample` step." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "This results in 9 effective PSFs" (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
   - assumption: Three detector-position bins capture the strongest variation along the detector's longest axis.  
  > "By doing so we take into account the strongest PSF variation in the field that occurs over the longest axis of the detector." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect signal-to-noise maps and compare radial profiles with the isolated HUDF North star.  
  > "We inspect signal-to-noise maps (using the standard deviation of the stacked star stamps as noise), to make sure that the final PSF model is valid." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "The radial profile of the MIDIS PSF is very close to the profile of the only sufficiently bright and isolated real star in the MIDIS field (HUDF North star)." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
4. **Measure multi-band photometry**: Detect and model sources in F560W, then perform forced photometry in other bands with fixed structural parameters and variable flux.  
  > "In brief, we first detect and model the galaxies in F560W MIRI band, and then perform forced photometry in the other multi-wavelength bands" (Spectral energy distributions); "allowing the flux to vary, whilst keeping the structural parameters fixed" (Spectral energy distributions)
   - software: THE FARMER, WEBBPSF
   - parameter: MIRI, NIRCam and HST bands; empirical F560W PSF and WEBBPSF NIRCam models  
  > "Photometry is performed for all sources in the F560W image in the MIRI, NIRCam and HST bands with THE FARMER (cite: Weaver2022)" (Spectral energy distributions); "using our F560W PSF model for MIRI" (Spectral energy distributions); "WEBBPSF models for NIRCam (cite: Perrin2012; Perrin2014)." (Spectral energy distributions)
   - assumption: Keep structural parameters fixed across bands during forced photometry.  
  > "allowing the flux to vary, whilst keeping the structural parameters fixed" (Spectral energy distributions)
   - fitting: Flux varies; structural parameters fixed.  
  > "allowing the flux to vary, whilst keeping the structural parameters fixed" (Spectral energy distributions)
   - validation: Compare F560W with CANDELS IRAC/Channel 3 fluxes, mask field edges and visually inspect outliers for blending.  
  > "to the Spitzer IRAC/Channel 3 (5.8$\mu$m) fluxes from CANDELS (cite: Guo2013)" (Spectral energy distributions); "masking sources near the edge of the MIDIS image." (Spectral energy distributions); "Visual inspection shows that the strong outliers above the Spitzer limit are largely due to neighbouring sources that are poorly (or not) deblended in the Spitzer photometry." (Spectral energy distributions)
5. **Revise stellar masses**: Fit high-z MAGPHYS SED models to new JWST/HST photometry plus existing longer-wavelength total fluxes.  
  > "We model the spectral energy distributions using the high-z version of MAGPHYS (cite: daCunha2008; daCunha2015)." (Spectral energy distributions); "We join the new JWST and HST photometry with the total fluxes from existing longer-wavelength photometry" (Spectral energy distributions)
   - software: MAGPHYS
   - parameter: Spitzer 24 μm; PACS 100 and 160 μm; ALMA 1 and 3 mm; 5σ mm upper limits  
  > "Spitzer/IRAC 24 $\mu$m (cite: Whitaker2014), Herschel/PACS 100 and 160 $\mu$m (cite: Elbaz2011)" (Spectral energy distributions); "ALMA at 1 mm and 3 mm (cite: Dunlop2017; Gonzalez-Lopez2019; Gonzalez-Lopez2020), including $5\sigma$ upper limits on the mm flux." (Spectral energy distributions)
   - assumption: Chabrier initial mass function  
  > "We adopt a (cite: Chabrier2003) initial mass function" (Introduction)
   - assumption: Flat ΛCDM: H0 = 67.7 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.31, ΩΛ = 0.69  
  > "a (cite: PlanckCollaboration2018a) cosmology (flat $\Lambda$CDM with $H_{0} = 67.7$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{m} = 0.31$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.69$)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: High-z MAGPHYS; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated.  
  > "We model the spectral energy distributions using the high-z version of MAGPHYS (cite: daCunha2008; daCunha2015)." (Spectral energy distributions)
   - validation: Compare with pre-JWST masses derived using the same models.  
  > "The total stellar masses are on average very consistent (within 0.04 dex) with the pre-JWST determinations using the same models (cite: Aravena2020; Boogaard2020)" (Spectral energy distributions)
6. **Fit F560W galaxy structure**: Fit single Sérsic profiles with GALFIT; initialize from THE FARMER; simultaneously fit nearby sources and mask fainter sources.  
  > "We measure the global structural parameters observed in the MIRI/F560W filter by modeling the galaxies with a single Sérsic profile (cite: Sersic1963) using GALFIT (cite: Peng2002)." (Structural parameters); "We adopt initial guesses on the magnitude, half light radius ($R_e$), Sérsic index ($n$), axis ratio ($b/a$), and position angle from THE FARMER catalog." (Structural parameters); "We simultaneously model all sources in the cutout up to 2.5 magnitudes fainter and within 3$''$ of the target source and mask fainter sources using the segmentation map." (Structural parameters)
   - software: GALFIT, THE FARMER
   - parameter: 200 × 200 pixel cutouts; 0.06'' pixels; background-subtracted F560W science image and error map  
  > "We create $200\times200$ pixel cutouts around each source from the background-subtracted F560W science image and error map at a pixel scale of $0.06\ensuremath{''}$." (Structural parameters)
   - parameter: Empirical, position-dependent PSF model  
  > "For the PSF, we use our empirical, position-dependent PSF model" (Structural parameters)
   - parameter: Minimum 5% relative uncertainty on effective radius and Sérsic index, folded in quadratically  
  > "we quadratically fold in a minimum relative uncertainty on the effective radius and Sérsic index of 5%, following (cite: vanderWel2012)." (Structural parameters)
   - parameter: Exclude point-source AGN 3mm.09 and 1mm.C08 from structural analysis  
  > "We discard the two AGN that are best-fit with a point-source template from the structural analysis (3mm.09 and 1mm.C08" (Structural parameters)
   - assumption: Single Sérsic profile  
  > "by modeling the galaxies with a single Sérsic profile (cite: Sersic1963) using GALFIT (cite: Peng2002)." (Structural parameters)
   - fitting: GALFIT; initial magnitude, Re, n, b/a and position angle from THE FARMER; individual adjustments to background-source parameters.  
  > "We adopt initial guesses on the magnitude, half light radius ($R_e$), Sérsic index ($n$), axis ratio ($b/a$), and position angle from THE FARMER catalog." (Structural parameters); "We slightly tweak these parameters for the background sources in a few individual cases to improve the overall fit." (Structural parameters)
   - validation: Inspect models/residuals; reject reference-sample fits with bad flags or boundary parameters.  
  > "The GALFIT models and residuals for the galaxies are shown" (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$); "we remove all galaxies for which GALFIT returned bad flags or parameter values at the edge of the parameter space (indicative of bad fits)." (Structural parameters)
7. **Compare structure across wavelengths and populations**: Compare MIRI with HST morphology and structural parameters; compare with a flux-limited HUDF reference sample; examine size ratios versus stellar and gas properties.  
  > "Next, we compare the structural parameters measured at $\lambda_{\rm obs} = 5.6$ $\mu$m by MIRI to the measurements at $\lambda_{\rm obs} = 1.6$ $\mu$m." (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST); "We also investigate correlations between the JWST/HST size ratio and quantities related to the cold gas mass" (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST); "Overall the mass-weighted stellar structure of the ALMA-selected galaxies is indistinguishable from a reference sample of other galaxies in the HUDF." (Summary and Conclusions)
   - parameter: Reference sample: HUDF galaxies covered by MIRI and ALMA; z = 1–4; F560W flux density ≥ 1.0 μJy  
  > "consisting of all galaxies at $z=1$–4 in the HUDF galaxy population covered by MIRI and ALMA that have a flux density $\fnumiri \geq 1.0$ $\mu$Jy." (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST)
   - parameter: Use HST/F160W catalog measurements; exclude 1mm.C12 and 1mm.C22 from HST comparison  
  > "but instead refer to the established measurements from HST/F160W by (cite: vanderWel2012)." (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST); "This excludes 1mm.C12 (AGN) and 1mm.C22 as they are not properly deblended in the HST catalog." (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST)
   - assumption: Rest-frame near-infrared light traces the bulk of older stellar light and is less affected by dust attenuation.  
  > "These wavelengths trace the bulk of the (older) stellar light, that remained inaccessible with HST, and are less affected by dust attenuation than observations at shorter wavelengths." (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Use comparable HST/MIRI PSF sizes to assess smoothing; inspect F560W residual structure; check AGN versus non-AGN size ratios.  
  > "This is not a resolution effect as both observations have a similar PSF." (Summary and Conclusions); "However, in all cases, the galaxies show significantly more substructure in the F160W filter than in F560W." (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$); "between the ALMA galaxies that are identified as AGN and those that are not (note this already excludes the two AGN best-fit with a point-source model)." (HST vs. JWST sizes and color gradients)

## Qualitative findings

- Rest-frame near-infrared light is centrally concentrated and smoother than rest-frame optical light.  
  > "The MIRI observations reveal that all $z\approx2.5$ sources have a centrally concentrated and relatively smooth light distribution at rest-frame 1.6 $\mu$m." (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$); "This is in contrast to the significantly more substructured or clumpy appearance in HST and NIRCam at rest-frame 450 nm." (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$)
- The smoother appearance is intrinsic rather than primarily a MIRI PSF effect.  
  > "We stress the smoother morphology is intrinsic and not primarily due to the MIRI/F560W PSF" (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$)
- More massive galaxies have relatively more compact longer-wavelength light profiles.  
  > "We find that the ratio decreases as a function of stellar mass, such that more massive galaxies have relatively more compact light profiles at longer wavelengths." (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST)
- ALMA-selected galaxies have stellar structures indistinguishable from the HUDF reference sample.  
  > "Overall the mass-weighted stellar structure of the ALMA-selected galaxies is indistinguishable from a reference sample of other galaxies in the HUDF." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Size ratio weakly anti-correlates with cold gas/dust mass and correlates more strongly with gas-to-stellar mass ratio.  
  > "We find that the size-ratio weakly anti-correlates with cold gas (or cold dust) mass, but more strongly correlates with the cold gas-to-stellar mass ratio" (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST)
- Revised stellar masses imply no fundamental change to previously reported gas and dust-mass fractions.  
  > "implies that there are no fundamental changes to the previously reported gas and dust-mass fractions (for these bright and relatively massive systems)" (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA 1.2 mm selection traces cold dust and molecular gas; MIRI resolves rest-frame near-infrared light tracing older stars.  
  > "the 1.2 mm selection is essentially a cold dust mass selection (the phase which contains most of the dust mass) and effectively also a cold, molecular gas mass selection" (Sample); "These wavelengths trace the bulk of the (older) stellar light, that remained inaccessible with HST, and are less affected by dust attenuation than observations at shorter wavelengths." (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$)
- Why this method: Empirical position-dependent PSFs address MIRI detector diffraction; established HST fits avoid uncertain NIRCam PSFs.  
  > "The shape of the cruciform artefact is dependent on the angle of incidence and varies with position across the detector, with the spikes effectively bending inwards towards the center of the detector." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "For these reasons, we construct a varying PSF model for the MIDIS field making use of empirical PSFs at different positions on the MIRI detector." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "As measuring the structural parameters requires accurate knowledge of the PSF, we do not measure the structural parameters from the NIRCam imaging" (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST)
- Alternative: A single MIDIS star cannot provide an empirical PSF for the full field.  
  > "Hence, we cannot use this single star as an empirical PSF for the entire field." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
- Alternative: MIRI PSF smoothing is rejected as the primary explanation for smoother morphology.  
  > "We stress the smoother morphology is intrinsic and not primarily due to the MIRI/F560W PSF" (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$)
- Alternative: Use established HST/F160W structural measurements instead of fitting NIRCam structure.  
  > "As measuring the structural parameters requires accurate knowledge of the PSF, we do not measure the structural parameters from the NIRCam imaging" (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST); "but instead refer to the established measurements from HST/F160W by (cite: vanderWel2012)." (Structural parameters at $z=1$–$4$ with MIRI and HST)
- Alternative: Patchy extinction and intrinsically clumpy star formation remain alternative explanations for optical substructure.  
  > "the structure at 450 nm could be due to a patchy distribution of attenuating dust, leading to the more clumpy morphology." (HST vs. JWST structure and morphology); "On the other hand, star formation is expected to occur in spatially-separated clumps, i.e., the observed HST morphology may reflect the actual underlying distribution of young stars." (HST vs. JWST structure and morphology)
- Alternative: Older central populations and stronger central extinction both explain smaller near-infrared sizes.  
  > "The results can thus be attributed to the presence or formation of an older, more centrally concentrated stellar population (such as a bulge) and/or stronger dust extinction in the center" (HST vs. JWST sizes and color gradients)
- How they decided it was enough: Validate empirical PSFs with signal-to-noise maps and the isolated field star; finer spatial PSF modeling exceeds available star counts.  
  > "We inspect signal-to-noise maps (using the standard deviation of the stacked star stamps as noise), to make sure that the final PSF model is valid." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "There is an insufficient number of stars for a more detailed spatially varying model using this technique" (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function); "The radial profile of the MIDIS PSF is very close to the profile of the only sufficiently bright and isolated real star in the MIDIS field (HUDF North star)." (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
- Limitation (authors): The bright-end Spitzer/MIRI flux offset remains unexplained.  
  > "though the exact nature remains unclear at the time of writing." (Spectral energy distributions)
- Limitation (authors): Insufficient stars for a more detailed spatially varying empirical PSF.  
  > "There is an insufficient number of stars for a more detailed spatially varying model using this technique" (MIRI/F560W Point Spread Function)
- Limitation (authors): F560W images retain structure after single-Sérsic subtraction.  
  > "The F560W images are on the other hand not perfectly smooth" (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$); "(after subtracting a single Sérsic component)" (Rest-frame Near-Infrared Morphology at $z\approx2.5$)
