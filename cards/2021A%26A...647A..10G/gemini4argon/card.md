  > "We reduced and analyzed the data using the GILDAS software as described in Cuadrado et al. (2015)." (2.2. Observations of H 2 S isotopologues and H 3 S +); "We rotated this image 37.5 [o] clockwise to bring the FUV illumination in the horizontal direction." (2.3. ALMA imaging of Orion Bar edge in SH + emission); "To determine their beam coupling factors ( f b ), we smoothed the bigger 4 [′′] -resolution ACA + TP SH [+] image shown in Goicoechea et al. (2017) to the different HIFI’s HPBWs." (2.3. ALMA imaging of Orion Bar edge in SH + emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: η_mb = 0.74 at ∼ 168 GHz (IRAM 30m); η_mb = 0.71 (SOFIA/GREAT)  
  > "(η mb = 0.74 at ∼ 168 GHz)." (2.2. Observations of H 2 S isotopologues and H 3 S +); "and calibration to T mb intensity scale (η mb = 0.71) ." (2.4. SOFIA/GREAT search for SH emission)
   - parameter: 5'' aperture toward (+10'', -10'') for ALMA SH+ line profiles  
  > "[a] Integrated over a 5 [′′] aperture to increase the S/N of the line profiles." (Table E.2)
   - parameter: Beam coupling factors for Herschel/HIFI SH+: f_b ≃ 0.4 at ∼ 526 GHz and f_b ≃ 0.6 at ∼ 683 GHz  
  > "We obtain f b ≃ 0.4 at ∼ 526 GHz and f b ≃ 0.6 at ∼ 683 GHz. The corrected intensities are computed as W corr = W HIFI / f b ." (2.3. ALMA imaging of Orion Bar edge in SH + emission)
   - assumption: T_ex = 10-30 K and extended emission for H3S+ upper limit  
  > "This results in N (H 3 S [+] ) =(5.5-7.5) × 10 [10] cm [−][2] (5 σ ) assuming an excitation temperature range T ex = 10-30 K and extended emission." (3.3. Search for SH, H 3 S + , and H 2 S ν 2 = 1 emission)
   - fitting: Gaussian fitting programme for spectral line profiles  
  > "Parentheses indicate the uncertainty obtained by the Gaussian fitting programme." (Table E.1)
   - validation: Compared observed isotopic line ratios o-H2S/o-H2^34S and o-H2^34S/o-H2^33S against solar isotopic ratios (32S/34S = 23, 34S/33S = 5.5) to assess optical depth.  
  > "The observed o -H 2 S/ o -H [34] 2 [S 1] [1][,][0] [-1] [0][,][1] [line] [intensity] [ratio] toward the DF is 15 ± 2, below the solar isotopic ratio of 32 34 S/ S = 23 (e.g., Anders & Grevesse 1989)." (3.1. H 2 32 S, H 2 34 S, and H 2 33 S across the PDR); "However, the observed o -H [34] 2 [S][/] [o] [-H] [33] 2 [S 1] [1][,][0] [-1] [0][,][1] [ intensity ratio is] 6 ± 1, thus compatible with the solar isotopic ratio ( [34] S/ [33] S = 5.5 ) and with H [34] 2 [S and H] [33] 2 [S optically thin emission.]" (3.1. H 2 32 S, H 2 34 S, and H 2 33 S across the PDR)
2. **Calculate ab initio state-dependent reaction rates for SH+ + H2(v) <-> H2S+ + H and S + H2(v) <-> SH + H**: Compute >150,000 ab initio points with RCCSD(T)-F12a in MOLPRO to fit a 6D PES for H3S+(3A), then run ZPE-corrected quasi-classical trajectories (QCT) with miQCT to get SH+ + H2(v) and H2S+ + H rates; perform quantum wave packet calculations with MADWAVE3 on the Maiti et al. (2004) PES for S(3P) + H2(v) and SH + H.  
  > "We first built a full dimensional potential energy surface (PES) of the triplet H 3 S [+] ( [3] A ) system by fitting more than 150,000 ab initio points" (Appendix A: H 2 S + formation and destruction); "We studied the reaction dynamics using a quasi-classical trajectory (QCT) method with the code miQCT (Zanchet et al. 2018; Roncero et al. 2018)." (Appendix A.2: Determination of reactive collision rates); "We performed quantum wave packet calculations for the reactions S + H 2 (v = 2, 3, j =0) and SH (v = 0, j =0) + H . We used MADWAVE3 (Gómez-Carrasco & Roncero 2006; Zanchet et al. 2009)" (Appendix B: Reaction S ( 3 P) + H 2 ( v ) ⇄ SH + H)
   - software: MOLPRO, miQCT, MADWAVE3
   - parameter: Initial reactant distance = 85 bohr; minimum 10^5 trajectories per temperature in miQCT  
  > "The initial distance between the center-of-mass of the reactants ( H 2 + SH [+] or H 2 S [+] + H ) is set to 85 bohr" (Appendix A.2: Determination of reactive collision rates); "The reaction thermal rate in specific initial vibrational state of reactants are calculated running a minimum of 10 [5] trajectories per temperature" (Appendix A.2: Determination of reactive collision rates)
   - parameter: Total angular momenta J = 0, 10, 20 with J-shifting interpolation in MADWAVE3  
  > "We calculated only few total angular momenta of the triatomic system, J = 0, 10 and 20 . The other J needed in the partial wave expansion were obtained using the J -shifting-interpolation method" (Appendix B: Reaction S ( 3 P) + H 2 ( v ) ⇄ SH + H)
   - assumption: Boltzmann distribution over translational and rotational degrees of freedom  
  > "assuming a Boltzmann distribution over translational and rotational degrees of freedom" (Appendix A.2: Determination of reactive collision rates)
   - fitting: Arrhenius-like form k(T) = α (T/300 K)^β exp(-γ/T) fitted to calculated reaction rates  
  > "Relevant rate coefficients from a fit of the Arrhenius-like form k ( T ) = α ( T /300 K) [β] exp(−γ/ T ) to the calculated reaction rates." (Table 1)
   - validation: Checked RCCSD(T)-F12a against ic-MRCI-F12+Q (t1 diagnostic < 0.03, overall PES fit rms error ≃ 0.01 eV); applied ZPE-leakage correction to QCT trajectories.  
  > "The analytical fit has a overall rms error of ≃ 0.01 eV (Fig. A.2)." (Appendix A: H 2 S + formation and destruction); "They are in good agreement with the ic-MRCI-F12 results and the t 1 diagnostic is always below 0.03." (Appendix A.1: Ab initio calculations and PES)
3. **Derive single-slab non-LTE excitation and column densities of SH+, SH, and H2S**: Solve statistical equilibrium and nonlocal radiative transfer with a Monte Carlo code including chemical formation pumping and destruction rates, dust continuum radiation, and inelastic collisions with H2, H, and e-.  
  > "In these cases, a proper treatment of the molecule excitation requires including chemical formation and destruction rates in the statistical equilibrium" (4 Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry); "Here we implemented this formalism in a Monte Carlo code that explicitly models the nonlocal behavior of the excitation and radiative transfer problem (see Appendix of Goicoechea et al. 2006)." (4. Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry)
   - parameter: Modified blackbody dust continuum at T_d ∼ 50 K with τ_λ = 0.03 (150/λ[µm])^1.6  
  > "for completeness we also included radiative excitation by a modified blackbody at a dust temperature of ∼ 50 K and a dust opacity τ λ = 0.03 (150/λ[µm]) [1][.][6]" (4. Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry)
   - parameter: T_form ≈ 2000 K for SH+; T_form = T_k for SH  
  > "Since the formation of SH [+] is driven by reaction (1) when H 2 molecules are in v ≥ 2, here we adopted T form ≃ E (v = 2, J = 0) / k − 9860 K ≈ 2000 K ." (4.1. SH + excitation and column density); "Models in Fig. 7 include these chemical rates for T form = T k (a lower limit to the unknown formation temperature)." (4.2. SH excitation and column density)
   - assumption: f(H2) = 2n(H2)/n_H = 2/3; n_e = 1.4x10^-4 n_H; H2 OTP ratio thermalized to T_k  
  > "The molecular gas fraction, f (H 2 ) = 2 n (H 2 )/ n H, is set to 2/3, where n H = n (H) + 2 n (H 2 ) is the total density of H nuclei ." (4. Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry); "the electron density n e is set to n e ≃ n (C [+] ) = 1.4×10 [−][4] n H (e.g., Cuadrado et al. 2019). For the inelastic collisions with o -H 2 and p -H 2, we assumed that the H 2 ortho-to-para (OTP) ratio is thermalized to the gas temperature." (4. Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry)
   - assumption: SH-H and SH-H2 collisional rates scaled from SH-He rates by the square root of reduced mass ratios  
  > "Lacking specific inelastic collision rate coefficients, we scaled the available SH– He rates of Kłos et al. (2009) by the square root of the reduced mass ratios and estimated the SH– H and SH– H 2 collisional rates." (4.2. SH excitation and column density)
   - fitting: Grid search / comparison of single-slab models against observed line intensities and ratios  
  > "we search for the combination of single T k, n H, and N that better reproduces the observed line intensities (so-called “single-slab” approach)." (4. Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry)
   - validation: Compared models with and without formation pumping (F=D=0 vs F,D vs F,Dx10) and verified consistency with the 683 GHz SH+ upper limit.  
  > "Figure 6 shows results of several models: without formation pumping (dotted curves for model “ F = D = 0” ), adding formation pumping with SH [+] destruction by H and e [−] (continuous curves for model “ F, D ” )" (4.1. SH + excitation and column density); "This model is consistent with the upper limit intensity of the 683 GHz line (Nagy et al. 2013) ." (4.1. SH + excitation and column density)
4. **Model depth-dependent PDR chemistry and multi-slab radiative transfer for pure gas-phase and gas-grain networks**: Run stationary isobaric PDR models of the Orion Bar (and constant-density models for general G0 and n_H) in the Meudon PDR code (v1.5.4) with updated gas-phase rates, wavelength-dependent photoreaction cross sections, and gas-grain / surface reactions (low E_b and high E_b); feed the depth-dependent PDR outputs into the multi-slab Monte Carlo radiative transfer code to predict emergent line intensities.

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA 1'' images show SH+ emission is confined to a narrow ~10'' filament following the PDR edge, with small-scale peaks matching or close to vibrationally excited H2(v=1-0) emission.  
  > "Compared to CO, the SH [+] emission follows the edge of the molecular PDR, akin to a filament of ∼ 10 [′′] width" (3.2. SH + emission from the PDR edge); "The SH [+] emission shows localized small-scale emission peaks (density or column density enhancements) that match, or are very close to, the vibrationally excited H 2 (v = 1-0) emission (Fig. 3)." (3.2. SH + emission from the PDR edge)
- Rare isotopologue H2^33S is detected in a PDR (Orion Bar DF) for the first time outside hot cores.  
  > "The presence of moderately large H 2 S column densities in the PDR is also demonstrated by the unexpected detection of the rare isotopologue H [33] 2 [S] [toward] [the] [DF]" (3.1. H 2 32 S, H 2 34 S, and H 2 33 S across the PDR); "To our knowledge, H [33] 2 [S lines had only been reported toward the hot] cores in Sgr B2 and Orion KL before (Crockett et al. 2014)." (3.1. H 2 32 S, H 2 34 S, and H 2 33 S across the PDR)
- Radiative association reactions S+ + H2 -> H2S+ + hν and SH+ + H2 -> H3S+ + hν via spin-flip are negligible because the triplet and singlet wells have very different geometries.  
  > "This probability is very small because the two wells, [3] W 1 and [1] W 1, correspond to very different geometries. In consequence, we conclude that this radiative association mechanism must be negligible" (Appendix A.3: On the radiative associations of H n S +)
- No or very little depletion of elemental sulfur from the gas phase is required in the Orion Bar PDR layers up to A_V < 8 mag.  
  > "we conclude that the SH [+] -emitting layers at the edge of the Orion Bar ( A V < 2 mag) are charaterized by no or very little depletion of sulfur from the gas-phase. At intermediate PDR depths ( A V < 8 mag) the observed H 2 S column densities do not require depletion of elemental (cosmic) sulfur abundances either." (7. Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The Orion Bar is seen nearly edge-on, allowing spatial resolution of the ionization and dissociation fronts and chemical stratification; ALMA 1'' imaging resolves the narrow SH+ filament, while SOFIA/GREAT accesses the 1383 GHz SH ground-state rotational lines that lie in a frequency gap inaccessible to Herschel/HIFI.  
  > "This favorable orientation allows observers to spatially resolve the H [+] -to-H transition (the ionization front or IF; see, e.g., Walmsley et al. 2000; Pellegrini et al. 2009) from the H-to-H 2 transition" (2.1. The Orion Bar); "These lines lie in a frequency gap that Herschel/HIFI could not observe from space." (2.4. SOFIA/GREAT search for SH emission)
- Why this method: Reactive molecular ions like SH+ have short lifetimes comparable to nonreactive collision timescales, requiring chemical formation and destruction rates in the statistical equilibrium equations within a nonlocal Monte Carlo radiative transfer code; state-specific ab initio quantum rates are required because FUV-pumped H2(v >= 1) drives endoergic reactions out of thermal equilibrium.  
  > "In this case, state-specific reaction rates for H 2 (ν, J ) are needed to make realistic predictions of the abundance of the product XH (Agúndez et al. 2010; Zanchet et al. 2013b; Faure et al. 2017)." (1. Introduction); "The lifetime of reactive molecular ions observed in PDRs (e.g., Fuente et al. 2003; Nagy et al. 2013; van der Tak et al. 2013; Goicoechea et al. 2017, 2019) can be so short that they do not get thermalized by nonreactive collisions" (4. Coupled nonlocal excitation and chemistry)
- Alternative: Pure gas-phase sulfur chemistry including state-specific H2(v) reactions and radiative associations S+ + H2 -> H2S+ + hν and SH+ + H2 -> H3S+ + hν (rejected as insufficient to produce observed H2S and physically unlikely for spin-flip radiative association).  
  > "In particular, pure gas-phase models underestimate the H 2 S column density observed in the Orion Bar by more than two orders of magnitude. This implies that these models miss the main H 2 S formation route." (7. Summary and conclusions); "In consequence, we conclude that this radiative association mechanism must be negligible, especially at the high gas temperatures of PDR edges where the H 3 S [+] ( [3] A ) [∗] complex is not formed." (Appendix A.3: On the radiative associations of H n S +)
- Alternative: Low adsorption binding energies for S (1100 K) and SH (1500 K) on grains (produces H2S line intensities up to a factor of ten below observed levels in the Orion Bar).  
  > "Model low E b produces higher H 2 S columns and brighter H 2 S lines, but still below the observed levels (by up to a factor of ten)." (6.4. Line intensity comparison and H 2 S ortho-to-para ratio)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Bad weather limited SOFIA/GREAT observations to ~70 min on a single flight, so the achieved rms (50 mK) is insufficient for an unambiguous detection of SH, providing only upper limits.  
  > "The original plan was to observe during two flights in November 2019 but due to bad weather conditions, only ∼70 min of observations were carried out in a single flight." (2.4. SOFIA/GREAT search for SH emission); "Unfortunately, the achieved rms is not enough to assure the unambiguous detection of each component of the doublet." (2.4. SOFIA/GREAT search for SH emission)
- Limitation (authors): Specific inelastic collision rate coefficients for SH with H and H2 are not available, requiring mass-scaling from SH-He rates without hyperfine resolution.  
  > "Lacking specific inelastic collision rate coefficients, we scaled the available SH– He rates of Kłos et al. (2009) by the square root of the reduced mass ratios and estimated the SH– H and SH– H 2 collisional rates." (4.2. SH excitation and column density)
- Limitation (authors): Gas-grain models do not include formation of more complex S-bearing ices (s-OCS, s-H2S2, s-S_n, s-SO2, s-HSO) or gas-phase reactions of S+ with desorbed organic molecules like H2CO, and assume steady state, making predictions deep inside the cloud (A_V >= 8 mag) less precise.  
  > "However, we stress that here we do not consider the formation of more complex S-bearing ices such as s-OCS, s-H 2 S 2, s-S n, s-SO 2 or s-HSO (Jiménez-Escobar & Muñoz Caro 2011; Vidal et al. 2017; Laas & Caselli 2019)." (6.3. Gas-grain PDR model results); "Similarly, reactions of S [+] with abundant organic molecules desorbed from grains (such as s-H 2 CO, not considered in our study) may contribute to enhance the H 2 S [+] abundance through gas-phase reactions" (7. Summary and conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Low-temperature (< 1000 K) radiative and dielectronic recombination rates of S+ used in PDR models may not be sufficiently accurate.  
  > "For example, the low-temperature (T k < 1000 K) rates of the radiative and dielectronic recombination of S [+] used in PDR models may still be not accurate enough (Badnell 1991)." (7. Summary and conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-dish observations of H2S (IRAM 30m, 15''-8'' beam; Herschel/HIFI, 42''-20'' beam) and SH (SOFIA, 20'' beam) have much coarser angular resolution than the 1'' ALMA SH+ image, introducing beam-dilution and spatial-coincidence uncertainties when jointly modeling the DF.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Surface hydrogenation reactions on grains are treated as instantaneous whenever H-atom sticking timescales are shorter than desorption timescales, rather than solving a full rate-equation or microscopic Monte Carlo grain-surface network.
