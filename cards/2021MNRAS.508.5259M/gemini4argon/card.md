## Science

- Fields: high_z_galaxies, agn_and_quasars, galaxy_clusters_and_sz
- Question: Characterize the 1.3 mm continuum (thermal dust vs non-thermal synchrotron) and serendipitous molecular gas line properties of high-redshift radio galaxy (HzRG) candidates selected in the ELAIS-S1 field, and evaluate the efficiency of IR-radio selection criteria.

(Abstract); "In order to contribute to the general understanding of HzRGs and in an effort to address the issues raised above, we present the first results of a survey targeting HzRG candidates selected within a sky-area of 3.9\,deg$^2$." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Select HzRG candidate sample from multi-frequency radio and MIR catalogues**: Cross-match ATLAS 2.3 GHz, MOST 843 MHz, and GMRT 610 MHz catalogues to ATLAS 1.4 GHz (M08) positions using 9'', 25'', and 6'' radii; cross-match 1.4 GHz positions with Spitzer SEIP MIR catalogue using 7'' radius; apply USS, LRS, and IFRS flux and colour cuts.  
  > "The ATLAS 2.3\,GHz, MOST 843\,MHz and GMRT 610\,MHz catalogues were matched with the 1.4\,GHz positions with a 9$''$, 25$''$\, and 6$''$ radius, respectively." (Radio photometry); "The cross-match between the radio and NIR catalogues was done adopting a 7$''$ radius nearest-neighbour cone search between the 1.4\,GHz coordinates reported in M08 and those in SEIP." (HzRGs selection criteria)
   - software: Topcat
   - parameter: Matching radii: 9'' (2.3 GHz), 25'' (843 MHz), 6'' (610 MHz), 7'' (SEIP IR), 2'' (redshift catalogues)  
  > "The ATLAS 2.3\,GHz, MOST 843\,MHz and GMRT 610\,MHz catalogues were matched with the 1.4\,GHz positions with a 9$''$, 25$''$\, and 6$''$ radius, respectively." (Radio photometry); "The reported values were obtained by cross-matching with a 2$''$ matching radius the radio and the redshift catalogues reported in column 9 ($z_{ref}$)." (The HzRG candidates sample)
   - parameter: USS: S_5.8/S_3.6 > 1.3 and alpha < -1.3; LRS: S_5.8/S_3.6 > 1.3 and S_1.4GHz > 1 mJy; IFRS: S_5.8/S_3.6 > 0.79 and S_1.4GHz/S_(3.6 or 4.5) > 100  
  > "| USS | $>1.3$ | $\alpha_{\rm LF}^{\rm HF}<-1.3$ |
| LRS | $>1.3$ | $S_{\rm 1.4\,GHz}>1\,$mJy |
| IFRS | $>0.79$ | $S_{\rm 1.4\,GHz} / S_{\rm (3.6~or~4.5\mu m)} > 100$ |" (Table 1)
   - assumption: S_1.4GHz > 1 mJy ensures an AGN-like radio emitter (L_1.4GHz > 10^24.5 W Hz^-1) at z >= 1.5  
  > "The cut in radio flux ensures an AGN-like radio emitter ($L_{\rm 1.4\,GHz}>10^{24.5}\,$W Hz$^{-1}$) at $z\gtrsim1.5$ (cite: afonso05)." (HzRGs selection criteria)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Assessed false-positive matching probability (2%) using deeper SERVS 3.6-4.5 um imaging in overlap region; compared M08 1.4 GHz fluxes with H14 and F15 releases.

## Qualitative findings

- Combining an IR colour cut (S_5.8/S_3.6 > 1.3) and a radio flux cut (S_1.4GHz > 1 mJy; LRS criterion) yields the highest selection efficiency of z > 0.8 sources (86%) and the largest number of candidates.  
  > "The criterion providing the highest selection rate of high-redshift sources (86% at $z>0.8$) is one combining an IR colour cut and radio flux cut ($S_{\rm 5.8\mu m} / S_{\rm 3.6\mu m}>1.3$ and $S_{\rm 1.4\,GHz}>1\,$mJy)." (Abstract)
- For half of the 1.3 mm continuum detections (8 out of 16), non-thermal synchrotron emission dominates over thermal dust emission, preventing direct study of the host galaxy dust at z < 2.  
  > "Out of the 16 central detections, 8 are of non-thermal origin (Sec. [2.2](#sec:radphot), Tab. [3](#tab:radiosed)), leaving only 8 where ACA is detecting the dust emission from the host galaxy." (Millimetre continuum detections); "We finally caution against the use of the mm spectral range to study the host galaxies of the most luminous radio-galaxies at least at $z<2$ (where most of our sample lies)." (Millimetre continuum detections)
- In source 32 (z_sp = 1.5805), CO(5-4) emission aligns with the IR host while the 1.3 mm continuum is spatially offset by 1.5 +- 0.8 arcsec, consistent with jet-dominated mm continuum.  
  > "We note that there is a significant offset between the centroid of the line and the continuum (1.5$\pm$0.8 arcsec), with the former being more aligned with the IR detection. This is compatible with the 233\,GHz detection being emission from the jet" (Serendipitous line emitters)
- In source 21 (z_sp = 0.225), serendipitous CS(6-5) emission extends ~20 kpc outside the host galaxy in a narrow jet-like or outflow structure.  
  > "Among the objects with serendipitous line detections there is a source with a narrow jet-like region, as revealed by CS(6-5) emission stretching 20\,kpc out of the host galaxy." (Abstract)
- Four fields show > 4 sigma negative continuum decrements (~ -0.6 to -1.1 mJy); at least one (ap5) is statistically unlikely to be a calibration artefact, but explaining it via the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect alone requires extreme gas masses (10^11 - 10^13 M_sun), densities, or relativistic velocities.  
  > "Purely based on location while comparing with the simulations results (Fig. [12](#fig:sims)) it is shown that at least one source should be considered as highly reliable." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: ELAIS-S1 has low foreground contamination, deep multi-wavelength (X-ray to radio) coverage, and fits the under-subscribed LST range required for an ACA Observatory Filler Programme; sensitivity (0.2 mJy requested) was chosen to match SCUBA2 850 um depth (~1.2 mJy).  
  > "As OFPs are meant to fill the ACA observing queue in under-subscribed Local Sidereal Time (LST) ranges, the targets had to be selected in a field in the adequate RA range with, at the same time, available multi-wavelength ancillary data." (The choice of the ELAIS-S1 field); "The requested sensitivity of 0.2\,mJy was set to match that of $\sim$1.2\,mJy at 850\,$\mu$m provided by SCUBA2 maps (cite: geach17) assuming a thermal-like spectral slope of $\alpha=3.9$ (with $S_\nu\propto\nu^\alpha$)." (ACA observations)
- Why this method: Adopted Middelberg et al. (2008) 1.4 GHz catalogue over Franzen et al. (2015) because M08 covers the full radio area and groups multi-lobe radio sources; used Briggs robust = 1.5 instead of 0.5 to improve rms from ~0.23 mJy to ~0.15 mJy; used SoFiA on multi-resolution cubes with inverted-cube validation for unbiased line finding.  
  > "Given the consistency between the two catalogues, we chose not to use F15 as their analysis does not consider the full radio-coverage and does not group multiple radio lobes into single sources, as it is done in M08" (Radio photometry); "The achieved sensitivity reached $\sim$0.15\,mJy by imaging with a Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 1.5 (a robust parameter of 0.5 would have resulted in an rms$\sim$0.23\,mJy)." (ACA observations)
- Alternative: Rejected using the deeper Franzen et al. (2015) or flux-deboosted Hales et al. (2014) 1.4 GHz catalogues in favour of Middelberg et al. (2008) because F15 covers a smaller area and does not group radio lobes.  
  > "Given the consistency between the two catalogues, we chose not to use F15 as their analysis does not consider the full radio-coverage and does not group multiple radio lobes into single sources, as it is done in M08, increasing the possibility for erroneous cross-matchings." (Radio photometry)
- Alternative: Rejected Briggs robust = 0.5 weighting in favour of robust = 1.5 because robust = 0.5 yielded a higher rms (~0.23 mJy vs ~0.15 mJy).  
  > "The achieved sensitivity reached $\sim$0.15\,mJy by imaging with a Briggs weighting and a robust parameter of 1.5 (a robust parameter of 0.5 would have resulted in an rms$\sim$0.23\,mJy)." (ACA observations)
- Alternative: Discarded large-scale cluster thermal or kinetic SZE as the origin of the negative continuum peaks because ACT CMB maps show no large-scale tSZE imprints toward the four sources.  
  > "We have checked in Atacama Cosmology Telescope (cite: Naess2020; Hilton2021) maps for evidence of large scale tSZE imprints on the CMB. However, we find no significant features toward the four mentioned galaxies. As a result, we discard the possibility that these features are due to bulk motion of the inter-cluster gas" (The Sunyaev-Zel’dovich effect as a physical explanation)
- How they decided it was enough: Used N=2000 visibility bootstrap simulations and counts of negative 3-4.2 sigma features to establish that > 4.2 sigma continuum detections are reliable and that 3-4.2 sigma detections are reliable when matched within 2'' of an IRAC source; required positive line candidates in SoFiA to exceed the most significant negative feature across all inverted cubes.  
  > "We also note that we find 27 negative features with significances between $3\sigma$ and $4.2\sigma$. None of them matches in position with an IRAC source. This implies that of order 27 of the positive detections in this signal-to-noise range would be expected to be spurious, consistent with the 26 we discard." (Continuum fluxes); "The reported lines are those which, in a given spectral smoothing, showed a velocity integrated signal more significant than any of the negative spurious ones identified among all the inverted cubes at a given spectral smoothing." (Serendipitous line emitters)
- Limitation (authors): Six out of 36 sample sources lack any spectroscopic or photometric redshift measurements, and available redshifts come from heterogeneous catalogues.  
  > "Six out of the 36 objects do not have any distance measurements. In a forthcoming work, we will revise the redshift estimates by using a common tool." (The HzRG candidates sample)
- Limitation (authors): Only a single mm frequency (233 GHz) is available, preventing separation of thermal and kinetic SZE components or full dust SED characterisation.  
  > "Ideally, in order to separate the contribution of both effects, one would need multi-frequency coverage. However, in this work, only a single frequency is available." (Significance and possible implications of negative peaks)
- Limitation (authors): All selection criteria require a 5.8 um detection from shallow SWIRE data, biasing the sample toward lower redshifts (median z = 1.4) and the most luminous sources.  
  > "Finally, it is worth noting that all the adopted criteria require a detection at 5.8\,$\mu$m. The shallow nature of the underlying *Spitzer* Wide-area IR Extragalactic survey (cite: lonsdale03) in ELAIS-S1 and the lower sensitivity of the band with respect to the shorter-wavelength IRAC bands result in a bias towards the most luminous and rare sources, but also in an overall lower median redshift of the selected sample" (The selection criteria)
- Limitation (authors): A coordinate entry error in the ALMA Observing Tool caused pointing offsets up to ~1 beam (up to 8.7 arcsec) between the input phase centres and the target IRAC positions.  
  > "Due to a mistake in the coordinates inserted in the ALMA Observing Tool, there is a noticeable offset (up to $\sim$1\,beam away from phase centre) between some of the targets and the corresponding observed phase centres (Tab. [\[tab:offset\]](#tab:offset))." (Input coordinates in ACA observations)
- Limitation (authors): Six observed sources (ap1-ap6) no longer satisfy the selection criteria after updating from SEIP DR3 to DR4 and adopting M08 1.4 GHz fluxes.  
  > "six additional sources (Tab. [\[atab:samp\]](#atab:samp), Fig. [14](#afig:aca3)) were observed by ACA as part of the initial candidate selection which, based on the above considerations, they no longer comply with any of the original selection criteria" (Other observed sources)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-power-law extrapolation over two orders of magnitude in frequency (from ~1 GHz to 233 GHz) ignores potential spectral ageing, steepening, or high-frequency turnovers in radio AGN lobes/cores, which could lead to overestimating the synchrotron contribution at 233 GHz.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): For 10 sources lacking >2 radio bands, a fixed median spectral index of alpha = -0.68 is assumed to extrapolate to 233 GHz, introducing substantial classification uncertainty.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All four >4 sigma negative continuum features fall in the north-east quadrant relative to the phase centre, which strongly suggests a residual phase/amplitude calibration or uv-coverage artefact despite the bootstrap tests.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Molecular gas masses derived from CO(5-4) rely on highly uncertain CO(5-4)/CO(1-0) excitation ratios (0.3-0.7) and alpha_CO (0.5-1.0), spanning nearly an order of magnitude in systematic uncertainty.
