## Science

- Fields: agn_and_quasars, nearby_galaxies
- Question: Whether a tight correlation (fundamental plane) exists at millimetre wavelengths between nuclear 1 mm luminosity, intrinsic 2-10 keV X-ray luminosity, and supermassive black hole mass across low- and high-luminosity AGN, and what physical accretion/ejection mechanism drives it.  
  > "In this Letter, we report the discovery of a FP at millimetre wavelengths, namely the existence of a tight correlation between the nuclear (i.e. ≪ 100 pc) mm luminosities ( 𝐿 𝜈, mm ), 𝑀 BH and intrinsic 𝐿 X , 2−10" (1 INTRODUCTION); "We also present the analysis of the physics underlying such correlation, and discuss how our results may have profound implications for our understanding of BH accretion in different AGN types." (1 INTRODUCTION)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA 1 mm continuum and measure nuclear 1 mm luminosities**: Combine continuum SPWs and line-free channels of line SPWs in CASA tclean (MFS mode), measure continuum flux density from the innermost synthesised beam coincident with the galaxy core, and compute L_nu,mm = 4 * pi * D_L^2 * f_mm * nu_obs.  
  > "For each dataset, continuum images were produced by combining the continuum spectral windows (SPWs) and the line-free channels of the line SPW (when included) using the CASA task tclean in multi-frequency synthesis (MFS) mode." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA); "For each source, we measured the continuum flux density 𝑓 mm from the innermost synthesised beam, coincident with the galaxy core." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA); "The mm luminosities were then estimated as 𝐿 𝜈, mm = 4 𝜋𝐷 2 L 𝑓 mm 𝜈 obs , where 𝐷 L is the luminosity distance and 𝜈 obs the observed frequency (between 231 and 239 GHz)." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: multi-frequency synthesis (MFS) mode  
  > "using the CASA task tclean in multi-frequency synthesis (MFS) mode." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
   - parameter: nu_obs between 231 and 239 GHz  
  > "where 𝐷 L is the luminosity distance and 𝜈 obs the observed frequency (between 231 and 239 GHz)." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
   - assumption: Extended dust emission is resolved out by long-baseline configurations and central point-like emission arises from the unresolved AGN core  
  > "As all the data were obtained with long-baseline configurations, extended dust emission is resolved out. We indeed typically detect only a point-like source at each galaxy centre, arising from unresolved core emission." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
   - assumption: 10% flux calibration uncertainty for Band 6  
  > "We note that the error budget of the derived 𝐿 𝜈, mm is dominated by the ALMA flux calibration uncertainties (≈10% for Band 6 data)." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that removing the 3 slightly resolved galaxies does not affect the results  
  > "In 3 (out of 48) galaxies, the emission is slightly resolved, making our measurements more uncertain. Removing these 3 objects, however, does not affect our results in any way." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
2. **Compile X-ray luminosities and SMBH masses for primary and BASS samples**: Retrieve intrinsic 2-10 keV X-ray luminosities and dynamical SMBH masses from literature; for galaxies without dynamical masses, estimate M_BH from stellar velocity dispersion using the M_BH-sigma_* relation of van den Bosch (2016), and recalibrate BASS M_BH-sigma_* masses to van den Bosch (2016).  
  > "The intrinsic (absorption-corrected) 2–10 keV luminosities ( 𝐿 X , 2−10 ) of the primary sample sources were retrieved from the literature, as detailed in Elford et al. (2023)." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA); "For the remaining 17 galaxies, we estimated 𝑀 BH using the 𝑀 BH – 𝜎 ★ relation of van den Bosch (2016), where 𝜎 ★ is the stellar velocity dispersion within one effective radius." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA); "We re-calibrated these measurements using the 𝑀 BH – 𝜎 ★ relation of van den Bosch (2016), for consistency with the 17 primary sample sources without a dynamical SMBH mass estimate." (3.1 BASS galaxies)
   - assumption: M_BH-sigma_* relation of van den Bosch (2016) applies to non-dynamical mass targets  
  > "For the remaining 17 galaxies, we estimated 𝑀 BH using the 𝑀 BH – 𝜎 ★ relation of van den Bosch (2016), where 𝜎 ★ is the stellar velocity dispersion within one effective radius." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified minimal X-ray contamination from hot gas/XRBs in 5 non-Chandra sources using scaling laws, and checked that removing non-Chandra sources or non-dynamical M_BH sources does not change best-fitting parameters  
  > "While we verified that any such contamination should be minimal (based on the scaling laws of Grimm et al. 2003, Kim & Fabbiano 2004 and Boroson et al. 2011), we cannot rule it out entirely." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE); "In any case, removing these five sources does not make any relevant change in the best-fitting parameters of the mmFP. The same applies when removing the sources without a robust, dynamical 𝑀 BH estimate" (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE)
3. **Fit 1D M_BH-L_nu,mm relation and 2D mm Fundamental Plane (M_BH-L_X,2-10-L_nu,mm)**: Perform robust linear and plane regressions using lts_linefit and lts_planefit, and quantify statistical significance via Spearman rank correlation analyses.  
  > "A power law was fitted to the observed trend, using the lts_linefit routine (Cappellari et al. 2013)." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE); "In this case, we used the lts_planefit routine (Cappellari et al. 2013) to find the best-fitting plane in the (log 𝑀 BH, log 𝐿 X , 2−10, log 𝐿 𝜈, mm ) space:" (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE); "For both correlations, we also performed Spearman rank analyses to quantify their statistical significance, and show the resulting correlation coefficients in the top-left corner of each panel of Fig. 1." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE)

## Qualitative findings

- A tight correlation exists between nuclear 1 mm luminosity, intrinsic 2-10 keV X-ray luminosity, and SMBH mass (the mm fundamental plane of BH accretion), holding for both low-luminosity and high-luminosity AGN.  
  > "We report the discovery of the “mm fundamental plane of black-hole accretion”, which is a tight correlation between the nuclear 1 mm luminosity ( 𝐿 𝜈, mm ), the intrinsic 2 – 10 keV X-ray luminosity ( 𝐿 X , 2−10 ) and the supermassive black hole (SMBH) mass ( 𝑀 BH ) with an intrinsic scatter ( 𝜎 int ) of 0 . 40 dex." (abstract); "Combining these sources with a sample of high-luminosity (quasar-like) nearby AGN, we show that the plane still holds." (abstract)
- Classic geometrically-thin accretion disc plus dusty torus models (SKIRTOR) fail to reproduce the observed mm luminosities by 2 to 4 orders of magnitude.  
  > "The luminosities extracted from the torus models reasonably reproduces the slope of the 𝐿 X , 2−10 – 𝐿 𝜈, mm relation of the BASS sources, but with an offset of about two orders of magnitude at a given L bol ." (4.1 Torus model); "On the other hand, to explain 𝐿 𝜈, mm of the lower accretion rate galaxies, the mm luminosities in the SKIRTOR models would need to be at least four orders of magnitude larger at a given accretion rate (and thus X-ray luminosity)." (4.1 Torus model)
- ADAF models naturally reproduce the slope and nearly edge-on projection of the mmFP (with a 0.5 dex offset in M_BH using default parameters), while compact jet models encompass most LLAGN but exhibit 3D curvature and require extra X-ray components for high-luminosity AGN.  
  > "In Fig. 3b, we show the projection of the ADAF model grid onto the best-fitting mmFP. This latter seems to arise naturally from these models, as an (almost) edge-on view" (4.2 ADAF model); "The model grids encompass the majority of the LLAGN and some BASS sources, but they have significant curvature in the 3D 𝑀 BH - 𝐿 X , 2−10 𝐿 𝜈, mm space" (4.3 Compact jet model)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-angular-resolution long-baseline ALMA Band 6 observations resolve out extended host galaxy dust emission, isolating the unresolved nuclear (<100 pc, average ~25 pc) core emission.  
  > "In this Letter, we report the discovery of a FP at millimetre wavelengths, namely the existence of a tight correlation between the nuclear (i.e. ≪ 100 pc) mm luminosities ( 𝐿 𝜈, mm ), 𝑀 BH and intrinsic 𝐿 X , 2−10" (1 INTRODUCTION); "As all the data were obtained with long-baseline configurations, extended dust emission is resolved out. We indeed typically detect only a point-like source at each galaxy centre, arising from unresolved core emission." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
- Why this method: lts_linefit and lts_planefit combine least-trimmed-squares robust regression with least-squares fitting to account for intrinsic scatter and uncertainties in all coordinates.  
  > "This combines the least-trimmed-squares (LTS) robust regression technique (Rousseeuw 1984) with a least-squares fitting algorithm, and allows for intrinsic scatter and uncertainties in all coordinates." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE)
- Alternative: Classic dusty torus and thin accretion disc model (SKIRTOR) considered and rejected as the physical driver of the mmFP  
  > "We thus conclude that, while “classic” torus models seem to be ruled out, either ADAF-like or compact jet emission have the potential to explain the observed trend." (5 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Alternative: Spurious correlation induced by galaxy distance uncertainties considered and rejected via Monte Carlo simulation and redshift-independent distance subsample test  
  > "To test that this is not affecting our results, we performed a simple Monte Carlo simulation, drawing 𝑀 BH and the luminosities from independent Gaussian distributions that are truly uncorrelated, forcing a correlation to arise due to distance errors alone." (4.4 Distance uncertainties); "We thus conclude that our results are not biased due to distance uncertainties." (4.4 Distance uncertainties)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Primary sample is constructed based on data availability only and is not statistically complete.  
  > "Crucially, although constructed based on data availability only (and thus not meant to be complete in any statistical sense), our primary sample spans four orders of magnitude in SMBH mass." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
- Limitation (authors): In 3 out of 48 galaxies, the central mm emission is slightly resolved, making their flux measurements more uncertain.  
  > "In 3 (out of 48) galaxies, the emission is slightly resolved, making our measurements more uncertain." (2 PRIMARY SAMPLE AND DATA)
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneous nuclear mm and X-ray observations are subject to AGN time variability (factor of 2-3 over year timescales), which likely dominates the observed scatter.  
  > "Since the nuclear mm and X-ray emission from AGN is known to be time variable (typically by a factor of 2 – 3 over year timescales; Prieto et al. 2016; Fernández-Ontiveros et al. 2019; Behar et al. 2020), variability likely dominates the observed scatters (and thus the underlying correlations may be tighter)." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE)
- Limitation (authors): Five X-ray observed sources lack Chandra data and could have L_X,2-10 slightly overestimated due to diffuse hot gas or X-ray binaries.  
  > "We also note that the 𝐿 X , 2−10 of the five X-ray observed sources without available Chandra data could be slightly overestimated, due to contamination from diffuse hot gas in the galactic and circum-galactic medium (CGM; although this mainly emits in the 0 . 3 – 2 keV range) and/or X-ray binaries." (3 THE MM FUNDAMENTAL PLANE)
- Limitation (authors): Default ADAF model parameters overpredict L_nu,mm at a given SMBH mass by ~0.5 dex, and plasma physics parameters in both ADAF and compact jet models are poorly constrained.  
  > "We note, however, that keeping the default model parameters from Pesce et al. (2021), the model well predicts the gradient of the mmFP, but is offset by a small amount (i.e. the model overpredicts 𝐿 𝜈, mm at a given SMBH mass by ≈ 0 . 5 dex)." (4.2 ADAF model); "We caution, however, that the plasma physics underlying both the ADAF and compact jet models is not well constrained, and significant uncertainties are present in all the model parameters and how they interact." (5 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spatial resolution varies by nearly two orders of magnitude across the primary sample (6 to 330 pc), which could introduce aperture/resolution bias if sub-kpc dust or star formation contributes in the more distant or lower-resolution targets.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 5 non-detections (upper limits) in L_nu,mm are listed in Table A1 (FRL1146, NGC3351, NGC3368, NGC5806, NGC6753), and the paper does not explicitly state how upper limits were treated in lts_linefit and lts_planefit (which standardly do not model censored data).
