## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and [CII] cubes**: Calibrate with ALMA pipeline, subtract continuum, image with multi-scale WSClean natural weighting  
  > "We perform synthesis imaging of the visibility data using the Ws-Clean algorithm introduced by (cite: Offringa2014), specifically its multi-scale version (cite: Offringa2017)." (3.1.1 Imaging)
   - software: CASA, WSClean
   - parameter: 5 mas pixels, 1024x1024, auto-mask SNR 2  
  > "We use the automatic SNR-based masking, with `auto-mask` SNR threshold of 2." (3.1.1 Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CO(3-2) re-imaged identically; changes <=10% in rms and luminosity  
  > "these result in minor ($\leq$10%) changes in the rms noise and the inferred CO(3–2) luminosity" (3.1.1 Imaging)
2. **Measure source sizes by uv-plane fitting**: Fit radially binned visibilities with 1 Gaussian, 2 Gaussians, or Gaussian+constant; F-test for model selection  
  > "we compare the two models using the F-test (cite: Bevington1969)." (3.2 uv-plane analysis)
   - parameter: uv-bin size varied 5 to 50 klambda  
  > "we vary the $uv$-bin size from 5 to 50 k$\lambda$." (3.2 uv-plane analysis)
   - assumption: circularly symmetric Gaussian profile  
  > "we assume the surface brightness distribution to follow a circularly symmetric Gaussian profile." (3.2 uv-plane analysis)
   - fitting: least-squares visibility fits; two-component preferred at p>0.95 for [CII] in 49.1 and continuum in 57.1  
  > "The two-component model is strongly ($p>0.95$) preferred for the \[C II\] emission in ALESS 49.1 and the continuum emission in ALESS 57.1." (3.2 uv-plane analysis)
   - validation: Mock ALMA observations: 1000 noise realizations per injected source  
  > "generating 1000 datasets with different noise realizations for each source" (3.2.1 Robustness)
3. **Kinematic modelling of [CII]**: 3D rotating exponential disc model fit to cubes  
  > "We model the velocity-fields using the GalPak3D software (cite: Bouche2015)." (3.3)
   - software: GalPak3D
   - parameter: 25 mas pixel cubes, natural weighting  
  > "we re-sampled our data onto cubes with a pixel size of 25 mas, using natural weighting" (3.3)
   - assumption: exponential disc (Sersic n=1)  
  > "we assume an exponential disc profile" (3.3)
   - fitting: Monte-Carlo approach (GalPak3D)  
  > "GalPak3D uses a Monte-Carlo approach to extract the kinematic and morphological parameters" (3.3)
   - validation: inclinations agree with CO(3-2) values within 1-2 sigma  
  > "agree with those derived from CO(3–2) imaging by (cite: CR18) within 1–2$\sigma$ uncertainty" (3.3)
4. **SED modelling**: MagPhys high-z fits with spectroscopic z and ALMA Band 3/4/8 photometry  
  > "using the MagPhys package (cite: daCunha2008), specifically its high-redshift extension (cite: daCunha2015)" (3.4)
   - software: MagPhys
   - assumption: constant Tdust and optical depth across source  
  > "assuming constant $T_\mathrm{dust}$ and optical depth across the source" (3.4)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: predicted 160-um flux without that point matches observed within <10%  
  > "the observed flux densities match the predicted ones within $<$10%" (3.4)
5. **Resolved [CII]/FIR vs Sigma_SFR**: Bin maps into 1-beam pixels (SNR>=2), SFR from SED fits linearly mapped to 160-um; compare with Smith 2017 and fit power law  
  > "binning the \[C II\] and 160-$\mu$m continuum maps (Fig. 1) into pixels 1 beamwidth large; only pixels for which both the \[C II\] and 160-$\mu$m continuum have SNR$\geq$2 are considered." (4.3)
   - assumption: linear mapping 160-um to SFR; no AGN contribution; universal IMF  
  > "(1) a linear mapping between the rest-frame 160-$\mu$m emission and SFR; (2) no AGN contribution to the rest-frame FIR luminosity; (3) universal IMF" (4.3)
   - fitting: power-law fit gives slope -0.53±0.12  
  > "we obtain a best-fitting slope of $\gamma = -0.53\pm0.12$" (4.5.1)
   - validation: compared with thermal-saturation slope -0.5  
  > "This is in agreement with the thermal-saturation model slope of $\gamma = -0.5$" (4.5.1)
6. **PDR modelling for G0, n, T_PDR**: Compare [CII]/CO(3-2) and [CII]/FIR in central R<=2 kpc with PDRToolbox models  
  > "We now use our resolved \[C II\]/CO(3–2)/FIR observations to infer the $G_0$ and $n$ using the PDR models from the PDRToolbox library" (4.4)
   - software: PDRToolbox
   - parameter: f_[CII]^PDR=0.8; factor 2 correction for optically thin [CII]  
  > "we adopt a conservative (i.e., low) estimate of $f_\mathrm{[CII]}^\mathrm{PDR} = 0.8$." (4.4)
   - assumption: CO(3-2) optically thick, FIR continuum thin; [CII] optically thin adopted  
  > "we adopt the values derived for the optically-thin \]C II\] for the rest of this paper." (4.4)
   - fitting: contour fits in G0-n space  
  > "Figure 9 shows the contours in the $G_0$/$n$ space" (4.4)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Needed kpc-scale [CII], FIR and low-J CO in unlensed z~3 sources  
  > "high-resolution (kpc-scale) observations of the \[C II\] emission, alongside the rest-frame FIR continuum (tracing the obscured star formation) and the low-$J$ CO emission tracing the molecular gas are necessary." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: AGN, dust-bounded HII regions and grain charging disfavoured as main drivers  
  > "Consequently, we do not expect a significant AGN contribution to the observed \[C II\] deficit." (4.5.3)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only two galaxies; extended [CII] likely resolved out  
  > "With only two galaxies in our sample, it is difficult to generalize our conclusions to the entire population of submillimeter galaxies." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Low [CII] SNR (5 and 8 sigma) and short on-source time limit kinematics
- Caveat (inferred, ours): uv fits assume circular Gaussians
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Linear FIR-SFR mapping and constant Tdust assumed in resolved Sigma_SFR
- Caveat (inferred, ours): PDR model is 1-D slab with correction factors
