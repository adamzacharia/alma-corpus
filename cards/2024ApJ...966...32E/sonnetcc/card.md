## Analysis recipe

1. **Combine execution blocks and self-calibrate the continuum**: Combine all six EBs, rescale fluxes to the 2021-08-18 EB, run four phase-only self-cal iterations (inf-EB, inf, 18.14 s, int) then amplitude+phase self-cal at inf; self-cal also tested on short baselines alone and compared  
  > "We performed continuum self-calibration on the short baseline observations alone, then separately on the combined short and long baseline observations" (2 Observations and Data Reduction); "All execution blocks had their flux rescaled to be in line with the execution block from 2021 August 18th." (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA 6.2.1.7
   - parameter: Phase-only solution intervals: inf-EB, inf, 18.14 sec, int; then amplitude and phase at inf  
  > "We did four iterations of phase-only self-calibration at solution intervals of" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-cal solutions from short baselines alone and from combined data were compared to check the short-baseline results were similar  
  > "and then compared the corrected data to verify that the results were similar for the short baselines" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
2. **Image the 1.3 mm continuum**: Briggs weighting, robust -0.5, mtmfs deconvolver, primary-beam corrected, on combined data; low-resolution map from short-baseline data only  
  > "We used Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of -0.5 for all the final images since it slightly favors uniform weighting over natural weighting." (2 Observations and Data Reduction); "when using only the short baseline data of the project, we can make a low resolution map" (3.1 Continuum data)
   - software: CASA 6.2.1.7
   - parameter: robust = -0.5; deconvolver mtmfs  
  > "or Multi-Term Multi-Frequency Synthesis (cite: rau2011)" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit Gaussians to the continuum to get flux density, sizes and PA**: Simultaneous two-Gaussian fit to the high-resolution map (one per disk); simultaneous three-Gaussian fit to the low-resolution map (two disks plus circumbinary structure)  
  > "We can derive disk properties directly from the images and from simple Gaussian fits to the two disks and the circumbinary structure." (3.1 Continuum data); "We do another simultaneous three Gaussian fit to the low resolution map" (3.1 Continuum data)
   - parameter: Gaussian fit peak intensity not used in the analysis because it is less robust than total flux  
  > "The Gaussian fit peak intensity is also given but not used anywhere in the analysis since the fit peak is less robust than the total flux." (3.1 Continuum data)
   - parameter: Circumbinary-structure quantities have errors of order ~10%; main purpose was the PA  
  > "The other circumbinary structure quantities are also given but the errors should be assumed to be of order $\sim$10%." (Table 1 note)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Radial intensity profiles checked how well the Gaussian assumption holds  
  > "The first feature we notice is the non-Gaussian nature of the major axis for both sources on both sides of the center." (3.1.1 Radial profile)
4. **Derive inclination, disk radius and dust mass**: Inclination from arccos(b/a) of the fitted Gaussian; radius as 2-sigma value of deconvolved major axis; dust mass from optically thin isothermal Equation with T = 20 K and T = 40.7 K  
  > "The inclination, $i$, is estimated by using $\arccos{\frac{b}{a}}$, where a and b are the major and minor axes of the fitted Gaussian." (3.1 Continuum data); "The dust mass estimate assumes isothermal and optically thin emission and is derived from" (3.1 Continuum data)
   - parameter: d = 150 pc; kappa = 2.3 cm2 g-1 at 225 GHz; T0 = 43 K; L_bol = 1.6 Lsun split 50% each giving T_dust = 40.7 K  
  > "we assign 50% of the luminosity to each of them" (3.1 Continuum data); "T$_{dust}$=40.7 K" (3.1 Continuum data)
   - assumption: Dust mass opacity 2.3 cm2 g-1 at 225 GHz (Beckwith et al. 1990)  
  > "We adopt a dust mass opacity, $\kappa_{\nu}$ = 2.3 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ at $\nu=225$ GHz (cite: beckwith1990)." (3.1 Continuum data)
   - assumption: Isothermal, optically thin emission; masses are lower limits since disks likely not optically thin  
  > "In addition, since the disks are likely not optically thin, the estimates here are disk mass lower limits." (3.1 Continuum data)
   - assumption: Temperature of 20 K as the first case  
  > "First, we adopt a temperature of 20 K." (3.1 Continuum data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Check continuum radial profiles and near/far-side asymmetry**: Intensity profiles along the major and minor axes of each source; near/far side assigned using the CO outflow; brightness asymmetry along minor axis interpreted as optically thick, geometrically thick, unsettled dust  
  > "We can use the outflows to determine the near/far side of the disks." (4.1 Continuum Emission); "Thus, this brightness asymmetry along the minor axis is evidence that the dust disk is optically thick and has a significant geometrical thickness" (4.1 Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with other eDisk sources showing the same asymmetry (IRAS 04302+2247, R CrA IRS7B-a)  
  > "similar to other eDisk sources (e.g., IRAS 04302+2247, (cite: lin2023); R CrA IRS7B-a, (cite: takakuwa2024))" (4.1 Continuum Emission)
6. **Make line cubes and moment maps**: Continuum-subtracted cubes imaged like the continuum but with auto-multithresh masks and briggsbwtaper, robust 0.5; moment 0 and 1 with bettermoments using >3 sigma cuts; adopted systemic velocity 5.86 km/s  
  > "with a robust parameter of 0.5 for all the final molecular line images." (2 Observations and Data Reduction); "Threshold cuts were implemented to only include signal above 3$\sigma$." (3.2 Molecular line data); "The systemic velocity adopted was 5.86 km s$^{-1}$" (3.2.1 12CO emission)
   - software: CASA 6.2.1.7, bettermoments
   - parameter: Moment maps clipped at 3 sigma; auto-multithresh default parameters  
  > "Threshold cuts were implemented to only include signal above 3$\sigma$." (3.2 Molecular line data)
   - assumption: Systemic velocity 5.86 km/s adopted by visual inspection of the cube and used for all molecules  
  > "The adopted systemic velocity was based on a visual inspection of the cube" (3.2.1 12CO emission)
   - fitting: bettermoments determines the central velocity by fitting a quadratic around the brightest pixel and its 2 closest neighbors  
  > "determining the central velocity emission by fitting a quadratic around the brightest pixel and its 2 closest neighbors" (3.2 Molecular line data)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Fit rotation curves in PV diagrams and derive enclosed masses**: PV diagrams made with CASA impv; SLAM pvanalysis fits ridge and edge of the 13CO PV diagrams with a power law v = V_b (r/R_b)^-p; mass from M = V_b^2 R_b / (G sin^2 i); central velocity left free  
  > "The PV diagrams were created using the CASA task" (4.2 Gas Kinematics); "SLAM uses the gas kinematics to fit the gas rotational profile as a simple power-law with radius described as" (4.2 Gas Kinematics); "we allowed the central velocity to be a fit parameter in SLAM and did not force the adopted 5.86 km s$^{-1}$ used in our figures" (4.2.1 Circumbinary structure)
   - software: CASA impv, SLAM pvanalysis
   - parameter: PV cut angle = Gaussian-fit position angles; PV line width ~1 beam  
  > "For the angle of the PV cuts, we used the Gaussian-fit position angles." (4.2 Gas Kinematics); "The PV line widths used are $\sim$1 beam for all images and any subsequent analysis." (4.2 Gas Kinematics)
   - parameter: Source A PV extended to 2 arcsec, source B to 1.5 arcsec; only the opposite side of each disk fitted to limit contamination  
  > "we fit the IRAS 32 A PV diagram extended to 2 arcseconds around the center, while the IRAS 32 B fit extended out to 1.5 arcseconds around the center" (4.2.2 Circumstellar Disks)
   - assumption: p ~ 0.5 consistent with Keplerian rotation; p ~ 1 with rotation plus infall conserving angular momentum; ridge and edge fits bracket the Keplerian mass; inclination from the continuum fits  
  > "A power-law index of $p\approx0.5$ would be consistent with Keplerian rotation of a disk, whereas $p\approx1$ would be consistent with rotating and infalling material with conserved angular momentum." (4.2 Gas Kinematics); "These two cases should bracket the Keplerian central protostellar mass, assuming a Keplerian profile power-law." (4.2 Gas Kinematics)
   - fitting: SLAM pvanalysis ridge and edge fits (priors and likelihood not stated)  
  > "SLAM will fit the velocities along the emission edge and ridge of the contours" (4.2 Gas Kinematics)
   - validation: Choice of 13CO as best compromise between S/N and outflow/other-disk contamination; ridge fit for B judged likely not representative  
  > "as the best compromise between signal to noise and contamination from the outflow and other disk." (4.2.2 Circumstellar Disks); "the ridge fit is likely not a good representation of the kinematics as it falls much more steeply than the edge fit" (4.2.2 Circumstellar Disks)
8. **Estimate outflow dynamical age**: Deprojected dynamical age from deprojected maximum 12CO outflow velocity and projected outflow length  
  > "We calculate a deprojected dynamical age of the observed outflow of $\sim$280 years by using the deprojected maximum velocity of the $^{12}$CO outflow" (4.4 Outflows)
   - parameter: Projected length ~20 arcsec (3000 au); maximum velocity ~17 km/s from visual inspection of the cube; inclination 70 deg  
  > "putting the projected length of any one particular side at about 20 arcsec (3000 au)" (4.4 Outflows); "adopting an inclination of 70$^\circ$" (4.4 Outflows)
   - assumption: Inclination of 70 deg is the average of the disk inclination fits  
  > "the average of the disk inclination fits" (4.4 Outflows)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Software

- **CASA** 6.2.1.7: Imaging of continuum and line cubes, self-calibration, and PV diagram creation (impv task)  
  > "They were imaged within CASA, Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: casa2022), version 6.2.1.7." (2 Observations and Data Reduction); "The PV diagrams were created using the CASA task" (4.2 Gas Kinematics)

  > "or Multi-Term Multi-Frequency Synthesis (cite: rau2011)" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)

  > "with the default parameters over the ranges of spectral windows containing specific molecular lines instead of over the full window" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
- **bettermoments** : Moment 0 and moment 1 maps with error maps, RMS from empty channels, quadratic peak velocity  
  > "Briefly, it integrates along the spectral dimension for each pixel and returns both a moment map and an error map." (3.2 Molecular line data)
- **SLAM (pvanalysis)** : Fitting power-law rotation profiles to PV diagram ridge and edge  
  > "SLAM uses the gas kinematics to fit the gas rotational profile as a simple power-law with radius described as" (4.2 Gas Kinematics)

## Qualitative findings

- Both circumstellar disks and the circumbinary structure have well-aligned position angles and inclinations, interpreted as ordered formation such as disk fragmentation  
  > "indicating formation in a smooth, ordered process such as disk fragmentation" (abstract)
- No clear substructures are detected in the circumstellar disks; radial profiles are non-Gaussian and need more careful modeling  
  > "No clear substructures are detected in the circumstellar disks." (6 Conclusion)
- SO traces the circumbinary structure, circumstellar disks and inner envelope with little outflow emission; SiO, CH3OH and one c-C3H2 line not detected  
  > "SO emission mainly shows the inner envelope/circumbinary disk and the circumstellar disks structures." (6 Conclusion); "We detected all the molecular lines in Table" (6 Conclusion)
- 13CO shows inner-disk absorption attributed to continuum over-subtraction of optically thick line emission plus resolved-out cold envelope  
  > "This absorption is likely from an over subtraction of the continuum due to the optically thick line emission" (3.2.2 13CO emission)
- Outflow interaction regions suggested between the A and B outflows, with a possible third outflow from the circumbinary structure left inconclusive  
  > "so visually the existence of three outflows is inconclusive" (4.4 Outflows)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Molecular lines were chosen to characterize disk properties in young systems; C18O is the main workhorse, 13CO tracks the disk and inner envelope, 12CO the outflow  
  > "Given that one of the goals of eDisk is to characterize the properties of disks in young systems, the molecular line data were carefully chosen." (3.2 Molecular line data)
- Why this method: 13CO PV fits chosen for the mass as best compromise between S/N and contamination; Briggs robust -0.5 chosen because it slightly favors uniform over natural weighting  
  > "as the best compromise between signal to noise and contamination from the outflow and other disk." (4.2.2 Circumstellar Disks); "since it slightly favors uniform weighting over natural weighting" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
- Alternative: Two dust temperatures used for the mass: fixed 20 K versus luminosity-derived 40.7 K, giving about twice the mass at 20 K  
  > "As the luminosity prescription gives nearly twice the temperature, it estimates a lower disk mass." (3.1 Continuum data)
- Alternative: Ridge fit of disk B judged likely not a good representation of the kinematics  
  > "the ridge fit is likely not a good representation of the kinematics as it falls much more steeply than the edge fit" (4.2.2 Circumstellar Disks)
- Alternative: Self-calibration run on short baselines alone and on the combined data with different thresholds and intervals, then compared  
  > "with different thresholds and solution intervals" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration stopped when further iterations gave no improvement  
  > "further iterations showed no improvements" (2 Observations and Data Reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Dust masses are lower limits because the disks are likely not optically thin; true disk temperature probably warmer than the 40.7 K estimate  
  > "we would expect our disks to actually be even warmer on average to this estimate" (3.1 Continuum data); "since the disks are likely not optically thin, the estimates here are disk mass lower limits" (3.1 Continuum data)
- Limitation (authors): Binary kinematic interaction and circumbinary, envelope and outflow contamination complicate the PV fits  
  > "Note that IRAS 32, with the combination of two close disks and a large circumbinary disk/envelope, complicates the fitting process." (4.2 Gas Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties are statistical only; circumbinary quantities carry ~10% errors  
  > "For the remainder of this paper any uncertainties are only statistical in nature." (2 Observations and Data Reduction); "the errors should be assumed to be of order $\sim$10%" (Table 1 note)
- Limitation (authors): Individual protostellar luminosities cannot be estimated accurately  
  > "It is not possible to accurately estimate the individual protostellar contributions to the total luminosity" (3.1 Continuum data)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust mass depends on assumed kappa (2.3 cm2/g, from a 1990 work) and on a luminosity-based temperature with an arbitrary 50/50 split; the optically thick asymmetry the authors argue for undermines the optically thin mass assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination from arccos(b/a) of a Gaussian ignores the geometric thickness the authors claim, so inclinations and SLAM masses (sin^2 i) are biased
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SLAM masses differ strongly between ridge and edge (A: 0.5 vs 0.91 Msun) with errors up to 100%, and fitted p of 0.35 for disk A is far from Keplerian, so Keplerian rotation is not firmly shown
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitted systemic velocities (5.05 and 4.87 vs 5.48 and 5.46 km/s) differ from the adopted 5.86 km/s, suggesting contamination or an offset the paper does not resolve
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Numbers differ between abstract/text and conclusions (dust masses 22.5/12.4 vs 21.9/12.7; 53.0 vs 52.0; disk radius quoted 180 mas versus 26.9 au; disk B p ridge 0.43 vs 0.4)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow dynamical age (~280 yr) uses a visually chosen maximum velocity and a single inclination
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No radiative-transfer model supports the near/far-side asymmetry or over-subtraction explanation; no substructure search beyond Gaussian residual inspection
