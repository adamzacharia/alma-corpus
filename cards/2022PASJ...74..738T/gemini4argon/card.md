## Analysis recipe

1. **Sub-select observation epochs to minimize time-variability artifacts of Sgr A* and perform iterative self-calibration and continuum/line imaging**: Select first 7 days of Oct 2017 data and last 2 days of Jun 2019 data; perform iterative phase and amplitude self-calibration with gaincal, applycal, and tclean; subtract continuum with uvcontsub (fitorder=1) and image H30alpha line with tclean.  
  > "Accordingly, we used the first seven days of the data. The observation epoch is $t_\mathrm{obs}=2017.773$ year." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "The imaging to obtain the maps was done using `gaincal`, `applycal`, and `tclean` tasks sequentially." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "Before imaging in the recombination line, the continuum emissions of Sgr A$^\ast$ and the GCMS were subtracted from the combined data observed in the first seven days using CASA task, `uvcontsub (fitorder=1)`." (Observation in Oct. 2017)
   - software: CASA, tclean, gaincal, applycal, uvcontsub
   - parameter: solint='inf' (first 2 self-cal iterations, phase only), solint='int' (successive iterations, then phase+amplitude), 7-8 iterations  
  > "In the first 2 self-calibration iterations, only the phase correction was applied using the solution interval of ‘solint=inf’ in `gaincal`, which means that the solution interval is the length of a scan." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "In the successive iterations, we used the solution interval of ‘solint=int’, which means that the solution interval is the length of data sampling." (Observation in Oct. 2017)
   - parameter: natural weight and Briggs weight (robust parameter = 0.5)  
  > "The angular resolutions using natural weight and Briggs weight (robust parameter = 0.5) as *u-v* sampling are $\theta_{{\mathrm maj}}\times\theta_{{\mathrm min}}=0.037\ensuremath{''} \times 0.025\ensuremath{''}, PA=85^\circ$ and $\theta_{{\mathrm maj}}\times\theta_{{\mathrm min}}=0.029\ensuremath{''} \times 0.020\ensuremath{''}, PA=86^\circ$, respectively." (Observation in Oct. 2017)
   - assumption: Sources in FOV are assumed non-variable during synthesis imaging  
  > "Because such intensity change is contrary to the basic assumption of synthesis imaging that sources seen in the FOV are not variable, it makes specific artifacts in the resultant map (e.g. (cite: Cotton), (cite: Rau))." (Observation in Oct. 2017)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked disappearance of side lobe features around weak objects (< 1 mJy/beam) and evaluated map dynamic range (>= 200,000 for Oct 2017, ~90,000 for Jun 2019).  
  > "After 7 iterations, side lobe features were disappeared even around weak objects with peak intensity of $< 1$ mJy beam$^{-1}$. Then we concluded that the imaging reached to the goal." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "Although some image quality degradation mentioned above is still identified around Sgr A$^\ast$ even after applying the “self-calibration" method, the dynamic range[^1] reaches to $\frac{\mathrm{Flux~density~of~Sgr A^\ast}}{\mathrm{image~noise~level}} = \frac{\sim3 \mathrm{Jy}}{12~\mathrm{or}~15 \mu\mathrm{Jy}} \gtrsim200000$ using both of these weight schemes." (Observation in Oct. 2017)
2. **Measure astrometric positions, peak flux densities, and angular sizes of Sgr A* and compact objects in the continuum maps**: 2-D Gaussian fitting and deconvolution on the continuum maps at 343 GHz (2016.693), 226 GHz (2017.773, Briggs weight chosen for positions), and 230 GHz (2019.471, natural weight) using CASA task imfit.  
  > "The position of Sgr A$^\ast$ at 226 GHz was $\alpha_{\rm ICRS}$ = $17^{\rm h}45^{\rm m}40^{\rm s}.40.03338820\pm0^{\rm s}.00000025$, $\delta_{\rm ICRS}$= $-29^{\circ}00'28.23047008\ensuremath{''}\pm0.00000147\ensuremath{''}$ at $t_\mathrm{obs}=2017.773$ year using 2-D Gaussian fitting for the continuum map using Briggs weight (CASA task, `imfit`[^2])." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "The positions and flux densities of these objects are derived by 2-D Gaussian fitting to the continuum map and summarized in Table 2. The angular extent of these objects is also estimated by 2-D Gaussian deconvolution." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: Point source threshold: deconvolved size much smaller than synthesized beam (30 mas at 230 GHz, 40 mas at 226 GHz, 100 mas at 343 GHz)  
  > "$^4$ When the deconvolved image size is much smaller than the synthesized beam ($30$ mas at 230 GHz, $40$ mas at 226 GHz and $100$ mas at 343 GHz), we define that it is a point source, P." (Table 2)
   - assumption: WR stars are assumed not to vary significantly in flux density within the elapsed time between 2017.773 and 2019.471  
  > "We assumed here that WR stars are not variable significantly within the elapse time." (Flux densities of Member Stars of the Nuclear Star Cluster)
   - fitting: 2-D Gaussian fit via CASA imfit; errors estimated by the standard procedure of CASA (Condon 1997)  
  > "The errors of central position, peak flux density, and angular size in this paper are estimated by the standard procedure of CASA ((cite: Condon))." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects)
   - validation: Compared positions and flux densities derived from Briggs vs. natural weight maps at 2017.773, checked positional error vs. flux density, and compared flux reproducibility between 226 and 230 GHz.  
  > "We checked the relation between the flux density and the fitting positional error." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "Figure 3a shows the angular offsets between the positions relative to Sgr A$^\ast$ derived using Briggs and natural weights in the epochs of 2017.773 year." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "We checked the reproducibility in the observed flux densities. Figure 5 shows the relation between the flux densities at 226 and 230 GHz, which have been observed at 2017.773 and 2019.471 year, respectively." (Flux densities of Member Stars of the Nuclear Star Cluster)
3. **Derive proper motions relative to Sgr A* and evaluate orbital kinematics**: Subtract Sgr A* position from star positions at 2017.773 and 2019.471, compute angular shifts (Delta Ra, Delta Dec) over Delta t = 1.698 yr, convert to linear velocities at d = 8.0 kpc, and compare with Keplerian orbital velocity limits around Sgr A*.  
  > "First, the position at 2017.773 year of Sgr A$^\ast$, which is mentioned in the previous subsection, is subtracted from the star positions of the epoch in order to calculate the relative positions referenced to Sgr A$^\ast$." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster); "In order to obtain the proper motion, the angular shifts are divided by the elapsed time, and times the Galactic center distance, which are given by $V_\mathrm{Ra}=d\frac{\Delta Ra}{\Delta t}$ and $V_\mathrm{Dec}=d\frac{\Delta Dec}{\Delta t}$." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster); "The solid and broken line curves in the figure show the upper limit of the rotation velocity for a circular orbit around Sgr A$^\ast$, $V=\sqrt{\frac{GM_\mathrm{GC}}{r_\mathrm{peri}}}$, and the upper limit of the orbit velocity of a Kepler orbit with high eccentricity when the object is at the periastron, $V=\sqrt{\frac{2GM_\mathrm{GC}}{r_\mathrm{peri}}}$, respectively." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster)
   - parameter: Elapsed time Delta t = 1.698 year  
  > "The elapsed time between these observation epochs is $\Delta t=1.698$ year." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster)
   - assumption: Galactic center distance d = 8.0 kpc; GCBH mass M_GCBH ~ 4 x 10^6 M_sun  
  > "This region harbors the GC super massive black hole (GCBH) with $M_\mathrm{GCBH}\sim4\times10^6 M_\odot$ (e.g. (cite: Ghez), (cite: Boehle), (cite: Abuter))." (Introduction); "Throughout this paper, we adopt $d=8.0$ kpc as the distance to the Galactic center: $1\ensuremath{''}$ corresponds to 0.039 pc or 8000 au at the distance." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived ALMA proper motions against published infrared proper motions (Paumard et al. 2006, Muzic et al. 2008, Schodel et al. 2009, Fritz et al. 2010, Eckart et al. 2013).  
  > "The proper motions by IR observations are also shown for comparison in Table 3 ((cite: Paumard), (cite: Muzic2008), (cite: Schodel2009)). The derived proper motions are mostly consistent with those by IR observations except a few cases." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster)
4. **Measure radial velocities and line widths from H30alpha recombination line profiles**: Extract H30alpha spectra toward compact objects and fit 1-D Gaussian profiles to determine central velocity and deconvolved FWHM velocity width.  
  > "Using Gaussian fit, the central velocity and FWHM velocity width of IRS13E5 are estimated to be $V_\mathrm{C}=-169.8\pm4.9$ km s$^{-1}$ and $V_\mathrm{FWHM}=\sqrt{\Delta V_\mathrm{obs}^2-(2sampling)^2}=67\pm12$ km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters); "Using Gaussian fit, the central velocity and FWHM velocity width of IRS13N$\delta$ are estimated to be $V_\mathrm{C}=-43.1\pm2.3$ km s$^{-1}$ and $V_\mathrm{FWHM}=\sqrt{\Delta V_\mathrm{obs}^2-(2sampling)^2}=59\pm16$ km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters)
   - parameter: Velocity channel width Delta V ~ 20 km s^-1; instrumental broadening correction sqrt(Delta V_obs^2 - (2*sampling)^2)  
  > "The sensitivities of the original velocity width channels, $\Delta V\simeq 20$ km s$^{-1}$, are $1\sigma=110~ \mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ or 3.1 K in $T_\mathrm{B}$ in the emission-free areas." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "Using Gaussian fit, the central velocity and FWHM velocity width of IRS13E5 are estimated to be $V_\mathrm{C}=-169.8\pm4.9$ km s$^{-1}$ and $V_\mathrm{FWHM}=\sqrt{\Delta V_\mathrm{obs}^2-(2sampling)^2}=67\pm12$ km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to spectral line profile  
  > "Using Gaussian fit, the central velocity and FWHM velocity width of IRS13E5 are estimated to be $V_\mathrm{C}=-169.8\pm4.9$ km s$^{-1}$ and $V_\mathrm{FWHM}=\sqrt{\Delta V_\mathrm{obs}^2-(2sampling)^2}=67\pm12$ km s$^{-1}$, respectively." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters)
   - validation: _not stated_

- **CASA** 5.5, 5.6, 6.0: Data reduction, self-calibration, continuum subtraction, imaging, and 2-D Gaussian source fitting

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA achieves ~0.001'' astrometric accuracy relative to Sgr A* at ~0.03'' resolution for 65 compact objects within 0.5 pc of Sgr A*.  
  > "The angular resolution is $\sim0.03\ensuremath{''}$. We determined the relative positions to Sgr A$^\ast$ of 65 compact objects in the area with the accuracy of $\sim0.001\ensuremath{''}$." (Abstract)
- Derived proper motions of 64 objects show clockwise and counterclockwise rotations bounded by Keplerian orbits around Sgr A*, as well as co-moving motions in the IRS13E and IRS13N clusters, consistent with previous IR observations.  
  > "The derived proper motions are roughly described with both clockwise and counterclockwise rotations around Sgr A$^\ast$. The rotation velocities are reproduced by Kepler orbits bounded around Sgr A$^\ast$. Moreover, the proper motions include co-moving clusters for example IRS13E and IRS13N." (Abstract)
- Sgr A* and nearby WR stars IRS16NW, IRS16C, and IRS16SW are unresolved point sources (< 25 mas ~ 0.001 pc), while S-stars are not detected at 226/230 GHz.  
  > "Accordingly, the physical extent of the emitting region of Sgr A$^\ast$ is thought to be much less than that of the beam size, $25~\mathrm{mas}\sim0.001\ensuremath{''}$ pc. Similarly, the extended components larger than the beam size are not detected around IRS16NW, IRS16C, and IRS16SW." (Region within 0.1 pc of Sgr A*); "It is considered that the S stars are too faint to be detected in these observations because they have the spectral type of B." (Region within 0.1 pc of Sgr A*)
- Ionized gas outflows from most WR stars (including IRS16NW, IRS16C, IRS16SW) are not detected in the H30alpha recombination line, whereas IRS13E3, IRS13Ec, IRS13E5, and IRS13N delta are detected.  
  > "However, such ionized gas outflows were not detected in the H$30\alpha$ recombination line data of the Cy.5 observation." (H30alpha Recombination Line Map); "Although IRS13E1, IRS13E2, IRS13E4, and IRS13E6 are not detected in the H$30\alpha$ recombination line, IRS13E3, IRS13Ec and IRS13E5 are detected in the line (see (cite: Tsuboi2019b))." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters); "IRS13N$\delta$ is also detected as a single peak line profile object in the H$30\alpha$ recombination line (see Figure 9)." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters)
- The proper motions of gas blobs IRS13E3 and IRS13Ec differ significantly from the other IRS13E objects, suggesting they are not genuine members of the co-moving IRS13E cluster.  
  > "The dissimilarity of the proper motions of IRS13E3 and IRS13Ec from the rest of IRS13 cluster member objects suggests that they are not member objects of the cluster." (Proper Motion of the Member Stars of the IRS13E and IRS13N Clusters)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA at ~230 GHz provides ~0.03'' resolution, high sensitivity (~12-39 uJy/beam), a wide ~26'' FOV covering r ~ 0.5 pc, and continuous detection of Sgr A* as a direct astrometric reference; only the first 7 days of Oct 2017 and last 2 days of Jun 2019 were used to minimize artifacts from Sgr A* flux variability.  
  > "The position of the stars can be measured directly by referencing the position of Sgr A$^\ast$ by ALMA because ALMA can always detect Sgr A$^\ast$." (Introduction); "The flux density of Sgr A$^\ast$ were significantly changed between the first seven days (from 5 Oct. 2017 to 14 Oct. 2017) and the last three days (from 17 Oct. 2017 to 20 Oct. 2017). Accordingly, we used the first seven days of the data." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "As mentioned in the previous subsection, such short-time scale variation of Sgr A$^\ast$ induces some image quality degradation in the resultant map. Accordingly, we used only the last two days data to minimize the effect." (Archival Data from Jun. 2019)
- Why this method: Iterative phase and amplitude self-calibration was used to achieve high dynamic range (~90,000 to >=200,000) in the presence of bright Sgr A*; Briggs weighting (robust=0.5) was chosen for 2017.773 positions to reduce contamination by non-uniform extended emission, while natural weighting was required for 2019.471 to detect weak stars.  
  > "We used the “self-calibration" method to obtain a high dynamic range in the continuum map." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "Because these stars are, for the most part, located in the IRS13E and IRS13N clusters, the large offsets would be caused by complex extended emission around stars in the clusters (see Figure 9). Therefore, it is better to use the star positions with Briggs weight for the data of 2017.773 year." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "On the other hand, we used unavoidably the star positions with natural weight for the data of 2019.471 year because the stars with weak intensity are undetectable because of the insufficient $SNR$ if we use Briggs weight." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects)
- Alternative: Using all 10 days of Oct 2017 data or all 5 days of Jun 2019 data was rejected because significant flux variability of Sgr A* creates concentric ring artifacts in synthesis imaging.  
  > "The flux density of Sgr A$^\ast$ were significantly changed between the first seven days (from 5 Oct. 2017 to 14 Oct. 2017) and the last three days (from 17 Oct. 2017 to 20 Oct. 2017). Accordingly, we used the first seven days of the data." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "As mentioned in the previous subsection, such short-time scale variation of Sgr A$^\ast$ induces some image quality degradation in the resultant map. Accordingly, we used only the last two days data to minimize the effect." (Archival Data from Jun. 2019)
- Alternative: Using natural weighting for the 2017.773 astrometry was rejected in favor of Briggs weighting (robust=0.5) because the 60% larger beam solid angle in natural weighting shifts positions of stars embedded in complex extended emission.  
  > "Because the synthesized beam size (beam solid angle) using natural weight, is $60 \%$ larger than that using Briggs weight, the former is easily affected by the extended emission around the object." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "Therefore, it is better to use the star positions with Briggs weight for the data of 2017.773 year." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects)
- Alternative: Using Briggs weighting for the 2019.471 data was rejected because weak stars become undetectable due to lower SNR.  
  > "On the other hand, we used unavoidably the star positions with natural weight for the data of 2019.471 year because the stars with weak intensity are undetectable because of the insufficient $SNR$ if we use Briggs weight." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration was stopped after 7-8 iterations when sidelobe features disappeared even around weak objects (< 1 mJy/beam) and dynamic range reached >= 200,000.  
  > "After 7 iterations, side lobe features were disappeared even around weak objects with peak intensity of $< 1$ mJy beam$^{-1}$. Then we concluded that the imaging reached to the goal." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "After 8 iterations which is the same as that of the Cy.5 observation, the resultant map is obtained." (Archival Data from Jun. 2019)
- Limitation (authors): Short-timescale intensity variations and flaring of Sgr A* violate the non-variability assumption of synthesis imaging, producing concentric circular artifacts that degrade SNR and can shift positions and flux densities (notably in 2019.471 declinations and for IRS13E1).  
  > "When the observed source is on the artificial concentric circle, the position and intensity may shift from the original values besides the degradation of SN." (Observation in Oct. 2017); "Although the error in right ascension is almost less than 1 mas, the error in declination often reaches to several mas. This situation may be caused especially by the artifact around Sgr A$^\ast$ mentioned above in the observation data at 2019.471 year." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "It is not ruled out that the observed position of IRS13E1 suffers from an unavoidable artifact as mentioned previously (see Figure 9)." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster)
- Limitation (authors): Reported positional and proper motion errors include only statistical 2-D Gaussian fitting errors and do not include calibration or systematic errors.  
  > "These positional errors include only fitting errors not calibration errors." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "The errors include only statistical errors ($1\sigma$)." (Proper Motion Referenced to Sgr A* of the Detected Objects in the Nuclear Star Cluster)
- Limitation (authors): Extended structures of the Galactic Center Mini-Spiral (GCMS) larger than the maximum recoverable scale (~0.4'') are resolved out, and complex extended emission in IRS13E and IRS13N can bias Gaussian fits.  
  > "Because these stars are, for the most part, located in the IRS13E and IRS13N clusters, the large offsets would be caused by complex extended emission around stars in the clusters (see Figure 9)." (Continuum Maps and Positions of the Detected Objects); "The disappearance of the GCMS is considered to be caused by that the structure is mainly consist of extended components over the maximum detectable angular scale of the Cy.5 observation and they are resolved-out." (H30alpha Recombination Line Map)
- Limitation (authors): Faint patches (~0.2 mJy/beam) within <=0.25'' of Sgr A* are likely residual sidelobes rather than B-type S-stars, which are too faint to detect.  
  > "There are some faint patches, $S_\nu\sim0.2$ mJy beam$^{-1}$, in the immediate vicinity ($\lesssim0.25\ensuremath{''}\sim0.01$ pc) of Sgr A$^\ast$ in Figures 4b and 4c. These patches are probably residuals of the side lobes after applying the “self-calibration" method." (Region within 0.1 pc of Sgr A*)
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneous observations (~2.8 yr apart) prevent disentangling intrinsic mm/submm spectral indices of WR stars from temporal flux variability.  
  > "However, it is impossible to determine if this is due to that they have such scattered intrinsic spectral indexes or the flux densities are variable in the observation duration ($\sim2.8$ years)." (Flux densities of Member Stars of the Nuclear Star Cluster)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Time-variable central point source (Sgr A*) is not subtracted or modeled with time-dependent visibilities prior to imaging, leaving concentric ring artifacts that systematically bias astrometry in declination in the 2019 epoch.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing positions measured on a Briggs-weighted map (2017.773) with positions measured on a natural-weighted map (2019.471) introduces differential sensitivity to surrounding extended free-free/dust emission in crowded regions like IRS13E and IRS13N.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Proper motions are derived from only two epochs separated by 1.698 years, making the velocities highly susceptible to single-epoch systematic distortions near the FOV edge (e.g., IRS5) or on sidelobe rings (e.g., IRS13E1).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Primary beam attenuation corrections are not explicitly mentioned for flux density comparisons out to radii of ~13'' (at the FWHM edge of the 26'' FOV).
