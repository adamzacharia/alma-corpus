## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate data**: ALMA pipeline in CASA 4.7.0-1 with manual inspection  
  > "We performed data-reduction using the ALMA calibration pipeline in CASA version 4.7.0-1." (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspection of diagnostic plots and calibrated data  
  > "After the inspection of both the diagnostic plots and calibrated data, the calibration resulted to be satisfactory." (Observations)
2. **Image CO line and dust continuum**: Continuum-subtract line spw, image with velocity-mode and mfs, robust weighting chosen as tradeoff of sensitivity and resolution  
  > "The imaging parameters were chosen as best trade–off between sensitivity and angular resolution" (4 Data analysis)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: channel width 40/120/40 km/s for XID 42/539/666  
  > "a channel width of 40, 120 and 40 km/s, respectively" (4.1 CO-line imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: S/N from brightest pixel over rms in four regions ~10" away  
  > "The S/N is defined as the ratio between the value of the brightest pixel and the root mean square (rms) derived averaging the rms measured in four regions placed at $\sim$10" from any source." (4.1 CO-line imaging)
3. **Spectra and moment maps**: Gaussian fits of line spectrum; moments 0,1,2 over v0±FWHM with 3 sigma threshold  
  > "to calculate the moments we considered a velocity range given by $\mathrm{v_0} \pm \mathrm{FWHM}$" (4.2 Moments)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: moment threshold 3 sigma  
  > "We extracted the moments using as a threshold a value of $3\sigma$ calculated by the measured rms on the image." (4.2 Moments)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian for XID 42, 539; two Gaussians for XID 666  
  > "As for XID 666, we fit two Gaussian components on the whole source region" (4.2 Moments)
   - validation: Single-component fits on separate peaks consistent within 1 sigma  
  > "Both the line width and central velocity of the single blue and red shifted components are fully consistent (within $1\sigma$)" (4.2 Moments)
4. **Fit sizes and flux densities**: 2-D elliptical Gaussian fit to images, then to visibilities with image fit as starting values; position fixed in second fit  
  > "we rerun a second fit keeping the position fixed in order to reduce the uncertainties related to the flux densities and the sizes." (4.4 Visibility fitting)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: elliptical 2D Gaussian (Condon 1997 algorithm) in image plane; uv-plane Gaussian  
  > "which relies on the algorithm developed by (cite: condon_1997) using as a model a spatial elliptical Gaussian 2–D function" (4.4 Visibility fitting)
   - validation: Position offset between image and uv fit within 1 sigma  
  > "Since the offset was within $1\sigma$" (4.4 Visibility fitting)
5. **Dust mass**: Optically thin dust emission with Td from single MBB SED fit  
  > "We derive the mass of the dust $M_d$ from its emission in the optically thin regime" (5.2 Dust mass)
   - assumption: k_nu=4[nu/1.2THz]^beta, beta=2.0; Td error ~5 K; upper limit 3 rms  
  > "The index $\beta$ is set equal to 2.0 (cite: magnelli_2012; gilli_2014)." (5.2 Dust mass)
   - fitting: Single MBB SED fit (Feltre et al. 2012 code)  
  > "derived from the SED fit with a single modified black body (MBB) component" (5.2 Dust mass)
   - validation: Compared with Rybak 2019 and Gilli 2014 dust masses  
  > "our results are in agreement with (cite: rybak_2019)" (5.2 Dust mass)
6. **Gas mass from CO and continuum**: L'CO from Solomon 1992; convert to L'1-0 with SMG/QSO CO SLED; M_H2=alpha_CO L'1-0 (0.8 and 6.5), add HI = H2/5; continuum via Scoville L850 relation with RJ MBB scaling  
  > "We estimate the CO luminosity $L'_{CO}$ in $\mathrm{K ~km ~s^{-1} ~pc^2}$ from $F_{CO}$, given the redshift, by means of the well known relation reported in (cite: solomon_1992)" (5.3 Gas mass)
   - parameter: CO(7-6)/CO(1-0)=0.16 SMG, 0.48 QSO; CO(6-5)/CO(1-0)=0.34 SMG, 0.64 QSO  
  > "They are equal to 0.16 and 0.48 (for SMG and QSO, respectively) for the CO(7–6)/CO(1–0) ratio, and equal to 0.34 and 0.64 for the CO(6–5)/CO(1–0) ratio." (5.3 Gas mass)
   - assumption: alpha_CO=0.8 (and 6.5), M_H2/M_HI=5, beta=2, nu0=1.5 THz, undetected line width 400 km/s  
  > "We assume a typical ratio $M_{H_2}/M_{HI}\approx5$ as found by (cite: calura_2014)" (5.3 Gas mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CO-based vs continuum-based vs SED-based masses compared  
  > "the gas masses obtained from the dust emission are fully compatible (within 1$\sigma$) with those derived from both the SMG CO–SLED conversion factor and IR-SED fitting" (5.3 Gas mass)
7. **Column density**: Uniform sphere: density=Mgas/V, divide by proton mass, path length = radius, diameter = mean of major and minor axes  
  > "As the source diameter we considered the mean value between the major and minor axis of the Gaussian ellipse fit" (5.5 Column density associated with the ISM)
   - assumption: Undetected sources assumed mean sizes of detected ones  
  > "for the undetected sources (XID 337, XID 551 and XID 746) we assume as “typical” major and minor axes the mean of those of the detected ones" (5.1 Source size)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with X-ray NH from Circosta 2019  
  > "All the column densities derived through the different methods show values similar to $N_{H_X}$" (5.5 Column density associated with the ISM)
8. **Dynamical mass**: Thin disk relation from Wang 2013 using FWHM (XID 42, 539) or vc,proj (XID 666)  
  > "we derive the dynamical masses in the idealized case of a simple uniform thin disk geometry" (5.6 Dynamical mass)
   - parameter: i = 32, 37, 27 deg for XID 42, 539, 666  
  > "We assume $i$ = $32^\circ$, $37^\circ$, $27^\circ$ for XID 42, XID 539 and XID 666, respectively." (5.6 Dynamical mass)
   - assumption: v_c,proj = 0.75 Δv  
  > "which assumes a ratio of 0.75 between the maximum projected circular velocity $v_{c, proj}$ and $\Delta$v" (5.6 Dynamical mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with De Breuck 2014 and Coppin 2010 for XID 539  
  > "for XID 539, (cite: debreuck_2014) and (cite: coppin_2010) found $M_{dyn}$ in agreement with the value derived in this work." (5.6 Dynamical mass)

## Qualitative findings

- Dust is more compact than molecular gas (~70-90% radius)  
  > "We also found that the dust component is confined in a smaller region, ($\sim$ 70% radius for XID 539 and XID 666, $\sim$ 90% for XID 42) than the molecular gas component." (5.1 Source size)
- All detected sources show velocity gradients; XID 42 and XID 666 show possible merger features  
  > "all the detected sources show a velocity gradient reminding one rotating system, even though two of them show peculiar features in their morphology" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Visibility fitting avoids dependence on deconvolution and can give sizes below the beam  
  > "if the sources are not or barely spatially resolved in the images, the visibility fitting provides an estimate of their angular size even on scales smaller than the synthesized beam." (4.4 Visibility fitting)
- Alternative: Double-component MBB not possible due to SED coverage  
  > "the data coverage of the SED does not allow us to properly constrain a double-component MBB." (5.2 Dust mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Large size uncertainties and sensitivity limits; spherical geometry underestimates density  
  > "The spherical model likely underestimates the source density" (5.5 Column density associated with the ISM)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 3 of 6 sources detected; low S/N (4-6) line detections
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas mass depends strongly on alpha_CO and assumed CO SLEDs
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Undetected sources use assumed sizes and line widths
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperatures from single MBB are luminosity-weighted
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dynamical masses conflict with baryonic masses by ~10x
