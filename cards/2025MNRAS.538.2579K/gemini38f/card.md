## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract spectra towards hot core positions**: Extract spectra from an area corresponding to the synthesized beam size centred on the continuum peak  
  > "The spectra of our sample were extracted from an area corresponding to the size of the beam, centred on the peak of the continuum." (2.1 Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Extraction area equal to beam size centred on continuum peak  
  > "The spectra of our sample were extracted from an area corresponding to the size of the beam, centred on the peak of the continuum." (2.1 Observations)
   - assumption: Continuum contribution and dust attenuation can be neglected  
  > "We have estimated the continuum contribution to the line emission using Eq. (5) from the paper of (cite: 2020A&A...635A..48Manigand). The correction factors range from 0.99-1.1. And then the continuum contribution to the line emission can be neglected." (3.1 Line identification); "The dust optical depths in our sample are less than 0.08. The hydrogen column densities were obtained from (cite: 2025A&A...694A.166C) (in their Tables C1-C4). Thus, we neglect continuum emission and dust attenuation in our spectral line fitting." (3.1 Line identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Calculated continuum correction factor (0.99-1.1) and dust optical depth (< 0.08) to justify neglecting continuum and dust attenuation  
  > "The correction factors range from 0.99-1.1. And then the continuum contribution to the line emission can be neglected. To evaluate the dust attenuation, we used the dust opacity equation (see Eq.(4) of (cite: 2021A&A...651A...9Moller) to calculate the dust optical depth. The dust optical depths in our sample are less than 0.08." (3.1 Line identification)
2. **Initial spectral line modeling and parameter estimation**: LTE radiative transfer modeling in XCLASS with source size fixed to deconvolved continuum size, velocity offset referenced to CH3OH 100.6389 GHz line, and manual adjustment of rotation temperature, column density, and line width  
  > "- In the first step, we take deconvolved angular sizes of the continuum sources as source sizes which are given by (cite: 2022MNRAS.511.3463Qin) in their Table 1. The velocity offsets are determined with reference to the rest frequency of the CH3OH transition line, 13(-2,12)-12(-3,10) E, vt=0, at 100.6389 GHz. Then, three parameters: rotation temperature, column density, and line width are set to be free parameters for fitting the observed spectra using the XCLASS software. The rotation temperature, column density, and line width were manually adjusted to achieve a reasonable match between the synthetic and observed spectra." (3.1 Line identification)
   - software: XCLASS
   - parameter: Source size fixed from continuum; velocity offset referenced to 100.6389 GHz CH3OH transition  
  > "- In the first step, we take deconvolved angular sizes of the continuum sources as source sizes which are given by (cite: 2022MNRAS.511.3463Qin) in their Table 1. The velocity offsets are determined with reference to the rest frequency of the CH3OH transition line, 13(-2,12)-12(-3,10) E, vt=0, at 100.6389 GHz." (3.1 Line identification)
   - assumption: Molecular gas is in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "Assuming molecular gas is in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and solving the radiative transfer equation, XCLASS produces theoretical spectra using spectroscopic data from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy (CDMS; (cite: 2005JMoSt.742..215M)) and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory molecular databases (JPL; (cite: 1998JQSRT..60..883Pickett))." (3.1 Line identification)
   - fitting: Manual adjustment of free parameters to achieve match  
  > "The rotation temperature, column density, and line width were manually adjusted to achieve a reasonable match between the synthetic and observed spectra." (3.1 Line identification)
   - validation: Visual comparison with observed spectra and accounting for line blending from CH3OH, C2H5CN, and CH3OCHO  
  > "We also plot modelled spectra of three molecules (CH3OH, C2H5CN, and CH3OCHO) from (cite: 2022MNRAS.511.3463Qin) in order to assess the potential blending issue." (3.1 Line identification)
3. **Optimization and error estimation of rotation temperatures and column densities**: Fix line width from Step 1, run MAGIX with Levenberg-Marquardt and MCMC algorithms to optimize Trot and N and derive uncertainties. For sources with only 1-2 lines, assume Trot = 150 K  
  > "- In the second step, the line width is set as a fixed parameter based on the first step. We then employ the Modeling and Analysis Generic Interface for eXternal numerical codes (MAGIX; (cite: 2013A&A...549A..21Moller)) to obtain optimized rotation temperatures and column densities. In the calculation, the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm (LM) and the Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm (MCMC) are employed to estimate the errors in the derived rotation temperatures and column densities." (3.1 Line identification); "For sources where only one or two molecular lines of DE and EA were detected, the rotation temperature could not be constrained. We calculated the column densities using three different temperatures (100 K, 150 K, and 200 K), which showed a slight increase in column density, with the variation remaining within 20%. We chose a median value of 150 K to derive their column densities." (3.1 Line identification)
   - software: XCLASS, MAGIX
   - parameter: Fixed line width; fixed Trot = 150 K when only 1-2 lines detected  
  > "- In the second step, the line width is set as a fixed parameter based on the first step." (3.1 Line identification); "We chose a median value of 150 K to derive their column densities." (3.1 Line identification)
   - assumption: 150 K is representative of typical hot core rotation temperature  
  > "We calculated the column densities using three different temperatures (100 K, 150 K, and 200 K), which showed a slight increase in column density, with the variation remaining within 20%. We chose a median value of 150 K to derive their column densities." (3.1 Line identification)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) and Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms within MAGIX  
  > "In the calculation, the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm (LM) and the Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm (MCMC) are employed to estimate the errors in the derived rotation temperatures and column densities." (3.1 Line identification)
   - validation: For sources where MAGIX calculations do not converge, results from Step 1 are retained  
  > "In some sources, the MAGIX calculations do not converge, so we show the results from the first step." (3.1 Line identification)
4. **Statistical correlation and abundance analysis**: Compute relative abundances relative to CH3OH, calculate Pearson correlation coefficients between column densities and temperatures, and compare with literature and chemical models  
  > "Pearson’s co-efficient for EA between rotation temperatures and column densities is 0.4, indicating that column densities of EA are not correlated with rotation temperatures." (3.2 Statistical analysis); "We computed the molecular abundances relative to methanol by fCH3OH = N(EA or DE)/N(CH3OH), which are listed in Table [tab:1] and Table [tab:A.4]." (4.1 Comparison with other sources and parameter correlations)
   - parameter: Pearson correlation coefficient rho; f_CH3OH = N(COM)/N(CH3OH)  
  > "We computed the molecular abundances relative to methanol by fCH3OH = N(EA or DE)/N(CH3OH), which are listed in Table [tab:1] and Table [tab:A.4]." (4.1 Comparison with other sources and parameter correlations); "Combined with previous studies of hot cores and hot corinos, we find strong column density correlations among EA and DE (ρ = 0.92), EA and CH3OH (ρ = 0.82), as well as DE and CH3OH (ρ = 0.80)." (abstract)
   - assumption: Literature column densities are comparable due to consistent source size definitions and excitation temperatures  
  > "The comparable rotation temperatures between ours and those in the literature indicate that the measured column densities are consistent." (4.1 Comparison with other sources and parameter correlations)
   - fitting: Linear correlation analysis (Pearson rho)  
  > "Rotation temperatures between EA and DE show no correlation, with Pearson’s co-efficient of 0.44." (3.2 Statistical analysis)
   - validation: Comparison against chemical models and literature hot core/hot corino samples  
  > "Compared with chemical models, the three different warm-up timescale models result in the systematic overproduction of EA and the systematic underproduction of DE." (abstract)

## Qualitative findings

- Ethanol is detected in 40 hot cores, dimethyl ether is detected in 59 hot cores, and both molecules are simultaneously detected in 39 hot cores.  
  > "1. EA is detected in 40 hot cores and DE is detected in 59 hot cores among 60 hot cores. Both EA and DE are detected in 39 hot cores." (5 Conclusions)
- Average rotation temperature of EA is systematically higher than DE, while column density of DE is slightly higher than EA.  
  > "2. The average rotation temperature of EA is higher than DE, with EA having a temperature of 167±14 K compared to 136±12 K of DE. The average column density of DE is slightly higher than EA" (5 Conclusions)
- Strong linear column density correlations exist among EA, DE, and CH3OH, and EA/DE ratios remain nearly constant within ~1 order of magnitude across hot cores and hot corinos.  
  > "The column density ratios of EA/DE versus the column densities of CH3OH remain nearly constant with values within ~1 order of magnitude. These strong correlations and the stable ratios, suggest that EA, DE, and CH3OH could be chemically linked" (abstract)
- Comparison with chemical models shows systematic overproduction of EA and underproduction of DE across warm-up timescale models.  
  > "5. Compared with chemical models, the abundance of EA is systematically overproduced while the abundance of DE is systematically underproduced in chemical models." (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 3 wideband observations from the ATOMS survey provide high sensitivity and spatial resolution to detect multiple COM transitions with uniform observational setup across a large sample of 60 hot cores.  
  > "To the best of our knowledge, large sample observations of EA and DE molecules with similar spatial resolutions and the same spectral coverage are still lacking." (1 Introduction); "The study is based on high-sensitivity, high-resolution ALMA observations as part of the ATOMS project." (2.2 Hot core sample)
- Why this method: 12-m array data are used because hot cores have compact angular sizes and COM lines are less affected by missing flux issues, and XCLASS provides full LTE synthetic spectrum fitting taking line blending into account.  
  > "In this study, we only employ 12 m array data as hot cores typically have smaller source sizes and the COM lines are less affected by missing flux issues, focusing on two broad bandwidth windows (SPWs 7-8) to identify EA and DE." (2.1 Observations); "Assuming molecular gas is in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and solving the radiative transfer equation, XCLASS produces theoretical spectra using spectroscopic data from the Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy" (3.1 Line identification)
- Alternative: Inclusion of ACA 7-m data was considered but rejected because hot cores are compact and COM lines are less affected by missing flux  
  > "In this study, we only employ 12 m array data as hot cores typically have smaller source sizes and the COM lines are less affected by missing flux issues, focusing on two broad bandwidth windows (SPWs 7-8) to identify EA and DE." (2.1 Observations)
- Alternative: Including continuum emission and dust optical depth correction in spectral line fitting was evaluated and determined to be negligible (correction factor 0.99-1.1, tau_dust < 0.08)  
  > "The correction factors range from 0.99-1.1. And then the continuum contribution to the line emission can be neglected. To evaluate the dust attenuation, we used the dust opacity equation (see Eq.(4) of (cite: 2021A&A...651A...9Moller) to calculate the dust optical depth. The dust optical depths in our sample are less than 0.08. The hydrogen column densities were obtained from (cite: 2025A&A...694A.166C) (in their Tables C1-C4). Thus, we neglect continuum emission and dust attenuation in our spectral line fitting." (3.1 Line identification)
- How they decided it was enough: For sources with only 1-2 detected lines where Trot cannot be constrained, testing column densities at 100, 150, and 200 K showed variations within 20%, justifying fixing Trot to 150 K.  
  > "We calculated the column densities using three different temperatures (100 K, 150 K, and 200 K), which showed a slight increase in column density, with the variation remaining within 20%. We chose a median value of 150 K to derive their column densities." (3.1 Line identification)
- Limitation (authors): Adopting source sizes from continuum emission may underestimate or overestimate column densities if molecular line emission extent differs from continuum  
  > "It should be noted that in some sources, the molecular line emissions of EA and DE are not well associated with the continuum emissions. In such case, the adopted source sizes from the continuum may lead to an underestimation of molecular column densities if the molecular emission more compact than the continuum emissions and vice versa." (3.1 Line identification)
- Limitation (authors): Band 3 spectral setup lacks low upper level energy (< 35 K) EA transitions with high Einstein A coefficients, requiring observations at other wavelengths to confirm non-detection in Group D sources  
  > "Because we do not cover the transition line of EA with lower upper level energy (<35 K) and higher Einstein A coefficient, observations at other wavelengths are needed to confirm the absence of EA in these sources, where only two emission lines of DE are detected with upper level energies ranging from 10 to 25 K." (3.1 Line identification)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) may not hold uniformly across all hot core sub-regions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing rotation temperature to 150 K for sources with only 1-2 detected lines introduces systematic uncertainty, although bounded to ~20%.
