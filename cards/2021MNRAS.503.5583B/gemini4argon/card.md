## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract 1D spectra, create moment maps, and measure source sizes and line/continuum properties**: Identify 3D signal mask using 3DBarolo SEARCH utility, collapse significant line channels into moment 0, 1, and 2 maps using CASA immoments, fit 2D Gaussians to moment 0 maps with CASA imfit and in the uv-plane with uvmodelfit, extract spectra in 1-2'' apertures, and fit 1D Gaussian components to spectral line profiles.  
  > "We infer initial source sizes by fitting a 2-dimensional Gaussian to the zeroth moment image using CASA. The spectra have been extracted using apertures of variable sizes (diameter 1-2$\ensuremath{''}$) centred on the positions of each galaxy stated in Table [\[tab:properties\]](#tab:properties)." (OBSERVED MOLECULAR GAS AND DUST CONTINUUM PROPERTIES); "Next, we use the CASA task *immoments* to create moment 0 (integrated intensity) maps using all channels containing significant line emission." (3DBarolo); "Using these constraints as an initial guess, the fitting was then re-done in the u-v plane using *uvmodelfit*." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
   - software: CASA, imfit, immoments, uvmodelfit, 3DBarolo, DUCHAMP
   - parameter: Aperture diameter 1-2''; SEARCH thresholds S/N_upper = 3.5 and S/N_lower = 3.0  
  > "The spectra have been extracted using apertures of variable sizes (diameter 1-2$\ensuremath{''}$) centred on the positions of each galaxy stated in Table [\[tab:properties\]](#tab:properties)." (OBSERVED MOLECULAR GAS AND DUST CONTINUUM PROPERTIES); "This utility automatically finds the RMS noise level of an input data cube and identifies signal peaks above a user-provided signal-to-noise threshold (S/N$_{\rm{upper}}=3.5$). A search is then conducted around these peaks for emission above a second user-provided S/N$_{\rm{lower}}=3.0$, creating a three-dimensional mask of significant emission." (3DBarolo)
   - assumption: r_1/2 = 0.5 x FWHM for a Gaussian profile  
  > "The FWHM values can be converted to a half-light radius using the equation, R$_{1/2}$=0.5$\times$FWHM for a Gaussian profile, and correspond to physical scales of $\sim$2 kpc at the redshifts of these galaxies." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting in image plane (imfit) and uv-plane (uvmodelfit); two-component 1D Gaussian fitting for J1234 system spectra  
  > "Initial source sizes for the gas emission were derived by fitting a single 2-dimensional Gaussian to the CO images using the CASA task *imfit*. Using these constraints as an initial guess, the fitting was then re-done in the u-v plane using *uvmodelfit*." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions); "Two Gaussian components are required to adequately model the 1-dimensional spectral line profiles and these best spectral-fit models are over-plotted on the observed spectra in Fig. [1](#fig:CO_J1234)." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
   - validation: Checked consistency between image-plane imfit and uv-plane uvmodelfit sizes, and verified radial profiles are more extended than a point source.  
  > "We have also checked that the radial profiles of all three galaxies are more extended than would be expected for a point source." (OBSERVED MOLECULAR GAS AND DUST CONTINUUM PROPERTIES); "The results from the two fitting approaches are consistent for all the galaxies" (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
2. **Model 3D gas kinematics and derive inner dynamical masses using tilted-ring fitting**: Fit 3D tilted-ring models directly to the CO(3-2) data cubes using 3DBarolo, convolving models with the restoring beam channel-by-channel, and compute dynamical mass from M_dyn = v_rot,max^2 * r_max / G.  
  > "We model the CO(3-2) traced gas dynamics in our quasars and their companion galaxies using the tilted ring model fitting code $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo - 3D-Based Analysis of Rotating Objects from Line Observations (cite: DiTeodoro:15)." (3DBarolo); "M_{\rm{dyn}}=\frac{v^2_{\rm{rot, max}} r_{\rm{max}}}{G}" (3DBarolo)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: 3 rings; max model radius = 1.0 x deconvolved major axis FWHM; min ring width = (beam minor axis FWHM)/2.5; initial inclination = 45 deg (allowed range 10-80 deg); scale height Z_o = 0.01'' (~100 pc); central position fixed to 2D Gaussian centroid  
  > "We base the maximum model radius on the 2-D Gaussian fit to the moment zero map: $1.0\times$(the deconvolved FWHM of the major axis). In order to not over-resolve the data, we adopt a minimum ring width of (FWHM of the minor axis of the restoring beam)/2.5 - see also (cite: Talia:18; Fan:19)." (3DBarolo); "For all galaxies modelled in this work, this process returns models with three rings." (3DBarolo); "The inclination estimate is set to $45^{\circ}$, and allowed to vary between $10-80^{\circ}$, while the position angle is provided by the user. We assume a thin disk with a scale height of $0.01''\sim100$ pc." (3DBarolo)
   - assumption: Rotating thin disk geometry with scale height 0.01'' (~100 pc)  
  > "We assume a thin disk with a scale height of $0.01''\sim100$ pc." (3DBarolo)
   - fitting: Two-stage residual minimization across rings (first fitting all parameters and averaging i, PA, v_sys across rings, then refitting remaining parameters)  
  > "When the residual is minimized, the fitting stops, and the spatial parameters (*i, PA, $v_{\rm{sys}}$*) are averaged across all rings. The fitting is then repeated for the remaining parameters." (3DBarolo)
   - validation: Visual inspection of moment 0, 1, and 2 model and residual maps; comparison with exponential disk dynamical masses.  
  > "The best-fit $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo models together with the fit residuals can also be seen in the same figures. In general the fit residuals are small, at least in the central portions of these galaxies, suggesting that a smooth velocity gradient provides a reasonable approximation to these data." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
3. **Model kinematics with a thin rotating exponential disk to derive extended dynamical masses and half-light radii**: Fit intensity and velocity maps with a thin rotating exponential disk model convolved with the synthesized beam, using emcee MCMC to sample free parameters and derive the dynamical mass enclosed within 10 kpc.  
  > "We therefore use an independent method to check the mass estimates by approximating the kinematics of the galaxies as a thin rotating disk (cite: Carniani:13; Pensabene:20)." (Exponential Disk Modelling); "The dynamical mass is the disk mass enclosed within a radius of 10 kpc." (Exponential Disk Modelling); "The full parameter space is explored by using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm *emcee* (cite: Foreman-Mackey:13)), which allows one to obtain a probability distribution of all free-parameters along with marginalised uncertainties." (Exponential Disk Modelling)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Free parameters: disk centre coordinates, half-light radius R_1/2 = 1.67 * R_d, inclination, position angle, dynamical mass (enclosed within 10 kpc), systemic velocity  
  > "The free parameters of this dynamical model are the spatial coordinates of the disk centre, the half-light radius (or scale radius), inclination, position angle, dynamical mass, and systemic velocity. The dynamical mass is the disk mass enclosed within a radius of 10 kpc." (Exponential Disk Modelling)
   - assumption: Exponential surface brightness profile Sigma proportional to e^(-r/R_d); mass surface density follows gas emission profile; circular velocity from Eq. 2.157 of Binney & Tremaine (2008); mass within 10 kpc represents total baryonic mass in disk  
  > "Such a model assumes that the surface brightness of the disk has an exponential profile $\Sigma \propto e^{-r/R_d}$, where $R_d$ is the scale radius that corresponds to a half-light radius of $R_{1/2}=1.67 \times R_d$. By assuming that the mass surface density has the same profile as the gas emission, the circular velocity associated with the exponential profile can be calculated following equation 2.157 of (cite: Binney:08)." (Exponential Disk Modelling)
   - fitting: MCMC exploration with emcee; disk centre and half-light radius derived from intensity maps, other parameters from velocity maps  
  > "The full parameter space is explored by using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm *emcee* (cite: Foreman-Mackey:13)), which allows one to obtain a probability distribution of all free-parameters along with marginalised uncertainties. The disk centre coordinates and half-light radius are derived from modelling the intensity maps, while the probability distributions of the other parameters are driven from the fitting of the velocity maps." (Exponential Disk Modelling)
   - validation: Comparison against 3DBarolo dynamical masses and Gaussian half-light radii  
  > "We also derive dynamical masses for all three galaxies using the exponential disk modelling. The resulting dynamical masses summarised in Table [\[tab:dynmod\]](#tab:dynmod) are larger than those obtained from $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo by 0.9 dex, 0.1 dex and 0.3 dex for the three galaxies." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)

## Software

- **CASA** v4.7.0: Calibration, uv-plane continuum subtraction, imaging (clean), primary beam correction (pbcor), moment map creation (immoments), and 2D Gaussian source fitting (imfit, uvmodelfit)  
  > "The raw data were processed using CASA (v4.7.0), by executing the appropriate calibration scripts for each set of observations as provided by the ALMA Observatory." (ALMA Band 3 Observations); "All images have had a primary beam correction applied to them using the CASA task *pbcor*." (ALMA Band 3 Observations); "Initial source sizes for the gas emission were derived by fitting a single 2-dimensional Gaussian to the CO images using the CASA task *imfit*. Using these constraints as an initial guess, the fitting was then re-done in the u-v plane using *uvmodelfit*." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
- **clean** : Deconvolving and imaging line cubes and continuum (mfs mode) with natural weighting  
  > "We then used the *clean* algorithm with a *natural* weighting of the visibilities to transform to the image plane. Continuum images were also produced by running *clean* in *mfs* mode on the line-free spectral windows, and once again using a *natural* weighting." (ALMA Band 3 Observations)
- **pbcor** : Primary beam correction on all images  
  > "All images have had a primary beam correction applied to them using the CASA task *pbcor*." (ALMA Band 3 Observations)
- **imfit** : Fitting 2D Gaussians to CO moment 0 maps to measure beam-deconvolved source sizes, position angles, and centroids  
  > "$^{\ast}$FWHM of major axis from a Gaussian fit to the CO moment 0 image using CASA *imfit* and after deconvolving from the beam." (Table 2); "By fitting this map with a two-dimensional Gaussian (CASA task *imfit*), we find a best-fit beam-deconvolved FWHM of the major and minor axes, position angle, and morphological central position." (3DBarolo)
- **immoments** : Creating moment 0 (integrated intensity), moment 1 (line of sight velocity), and moment 2 (velocity dispersion) maps  
  > "Next, we use the CASA task *immoments* to create moment 0 (integrated intensity) maps using all channels containing significant line emission." (3DBarolo); "We also apply *immoments* to all pixels identified as signal to create moment 1 (line of sight velocity) and moment 2 (velocity dispersion) maps." (3DBarolo)
- **uvmodelfit** : Fitting 2D Gaussian source sizes in the uv-plane  
  > "Using these constraints as an initial guess, the fitting was then re-done in the u-v plane using *uvmodelfit*." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
- **3DBarolo** : 3D tilted-ring dynamical modelling of CO(3-2) data cubes and 3D signal masking via its SEARCH utility  
  > "We model the CO(3-2) traced gas dynamics in our quasars and their companion galaxies using the tilted ring model fitting code $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo - 3D-Based Analysis of Rotating Objects from Line Observations (cite: DiTeodoro:15)." (3DBarolo); "We first utilize the $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo *SEARCH* utility (based on DUCHAMP; (cite: Whiting:12)) to create a signal mask." (3DBarolo)
- **DUCHAMP** : Basis of the 3DBarolo SEARCH utility used for 3D signal masking  
  > "We first utilize the $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo *SEARCH* utility (based on DUCHAMP; (cite: Whiting:12)) to create a signal mask." (3DBarolo)
- **emcee** : Markov Chain Monte Carlo parameter space exploration for exponential disk dynamical modelling  
  > "The full parameter space is explored by using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm *emcee* (cite: Foreman-Mackey:13)), which allows one to obtain a probability distribution of all free-parameters along with marginalised uncertainties." (Exponential Disk Modelling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ULASJ1234+0907 and ULASJ2315+0143 were marginally resolved in compact Cycle 3 ALMA observations (~2.5-3''), motivating Cycle 4 re-observations at ~3x better angular resolution (~0.8-1.0'') to spatially resolve the CO(3-2) gas emission and constrain gas dynamics.  
  > "Two of the quasars from the sample - ULASJ1234+0907 ($z\sim2.50$; J1234 hereafter) and ULASJ2315+0143 ($z\sim2.56$; J2315 hereafter) - were marginally resolved in the Cycle 3 data and were therefore re-observed during ALMA Cycle 4 at $\sim$3$\times$ better angular resolution with the aim of spatially resolving the gas emission in both sources and constraining the gas dynamics." (ALMA Band 3 Observations)
- Why this method: 3DBarolo fits models to the entire 3D data cube rather than just the 2D velocity field, reducing beam smearing; thin rotating exponential disk modelling provides an independent check of total baryonic mass out to 10 kpc and enables direct comparison with z ~ 4-6 quasars analysed with the same method; hard X-ray luminosities are used for M_BH because they suffer much less extinction than the 5100 A continuum.  
  > "The approach is similar to our previous work (B17) employing the GIPSY *rotcur* task, but with the key improvement that $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo fits models to the entire data cube rather than just the velocity field. By convolving the model with the restoring beam and comparing data and model on a channel-by-channel basis, the effects of beam smearing are reduced" (3DBarolo); "We therefore use an independent method to check the mass estimates by approximating the kinematics of the galaxies as a thin rotating disk (cite: Carniani:13; Pensabene:20)." (Exponential Disk Modelling); "We also make use of X-ray observations of both our quasars from (cite: Banerji:14) and (cite: Lansbury:20) to derive hard X-ray luminosities, which suffer significantly less from the effects of extinction compared to the optical continuum." (The M_BH-M_gal relation)
- Alternative: 2D velocity field fitting (such as GIPSY rotcur used in B17) was superseded by 3DBarolo 3D data cube fitting to mitigate beam smearing.  
  > "The approach is similar to our previous work (B17) employing the GIPSY *rotcur* task, but with the key improvement that $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo fits models to the entire data cube rather than just the velocity field." (3DBarolo)
- Alternative: 3DBarolo tilted-ring disk modelling was attempted for the J2315 system but rejected because the system is a major merger and the fits are poor.  
  > "The fits are poor as expected as the system cannot be well modelled as a rotating disk and we do not consider the $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo fitting results in detail in the paper as we do not expect them to be robust." (Dynamical Mass & Gas Fraction)
- Alternative: Optical 5100 A continuum luminosity for black hole mass estimation (used in Banerji et al. 2012, 2015) was replaced with 2-10 keV hard X-ray luminosity due to large dust-extinction corrections and potential host galaxy contamination at 5100 A.  
  > "As noted in those papers, there are large systematic uncertainties associated with the black-hole mass derivations, not least because the use of a continuum luminosity at 5100Å in the black-hole mass estimate requires significant dust-corrections given the typical extinction levels in the HRQs." (The M_BH-M_gal relation); "We also make use of X-ray observations of both our quasars from (cite: Banerji:14) and (cite: Lansbury:20) to derive hard X-ray luminosities, which suffer significantly less from the effects of extinction compared to the optical continuum." (The M_BH-M_gal relation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Formal uncertainties on 3DBarolo dynamical masses do not include the significant uncertainty on galaxy inclination and are therefore likely under-estimated.  
  > "The uncertainties on the dynamical mass only include the output rotational model uncertainties from $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo ($\delta v_{\rm{rot, max}}$) and the width of the model ring ($\delta r_{\rm{max}}$) and the significant uncertainty on the inclination is not included." (3DBarolo); "we simply note that the formal uncertainties on the dynamical masses from $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo are likely to be under-estimated." (3DBarolo)
- Limitation (authors): Considerable uncertainties exist in both dynamical and gas masses, as shown by discrepancies between 3DBarolo and exponential disk models and between CO and CI gas mass tracers.  
  > "We caution however that there are considerable uncertainties in both the dynamical and gas masses as illustrated by the discrepancies in these estimates using different dynamical modelling methods and different gas mass tracers." (Dynamical Masses, Sizes & Gas Fractions)
- Limitation (authors): Modest angular resolution of the ALMA observations (~0.8-1.0'') means that the smooth velocity gradient in J1234 could still be a merging system close to or soon after final coalescence, and individual velocity structures within the J2315 merging galaxies are not resolved.  
  > "No velocity structure is evident in the individual galaxies at the current resolution, although we clearly see that the northern companion galaxy is blueshifted relative to the quasar host." (Dynamical Mass & Gas Fraction); "While the velocity fields of the CO(3-2) molecular gas observed in this work are consistent with rotational dynamics, the relatively modest resolution of the observations do allow the possibility of a merging system seen close to or soon after final coalescence when the gas is perhaps settling into a rotation pattern." (MULTI-WAVELENGTH PROPERTIES OF THE HRQS)
- Limitation (authors): The blue side of the broad CO(7-6) wing in G1234S cannot be traced because of contamination by the adjacent CI(2-1) emission line, and no X-ray/mid-IR data yet confirm an AGN in G1234S.  
  > "Note - the blue side of the broad-wing is not traced by our observations as the CI(2-1) emission line affects this portion of the spectrum." (CO Line Profiles); "We therefore speculate that G1234S with its broad wings seen in CO(7-6) emission and high excitation CO SLED (Paper I) may well host an obscured AGN although high-resolution, deep X-ray or mid infra-red data would be needed to confirm or refute this claim." (CO Line Profiles)
- Limitation (authors): The Illustris-1 simulation volume ((106.5 cMpc)^3) is too small to contain >10^8 M_sun black holes at z ~ 6.  
  > "While the simulation volume is not large enough to contain $>10^8$M$_\odot$ black holes at the highest redshifts of $z\sim6$, the simulations can nevertheless be used to track back the $z \sim 2.5$ population to understand the progenitors of these massive black holes at higher redshifts." (Comparison to Illustris)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size of only two heavily reddened quasars and lack of a matched control sample of unobscured UV-luminous quasars with resolved gas observations limit firm conclusions.  
  > "With a sample of only two obscured quasars analysed in the present work, it is difficult to draw any firm conclusions regarding how they fit into a standard, merger-driven paradigm of massive galaxy formation. Crucially, matched control samples of unobscured, UV-luminous, high-redshift quasars with similar resolved observations of the gas emission are still missing." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): With ~0.8-1.0'' beam and source FWHM of ~0.48-0.57'' in the J1234 system, the galaxies are resolved by fewer than 2-3 beams across the major axis, making 3-ring tilted-ring models and inclination constraints highly degenerate.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating an exponential disk mass out to 10 kpc when CO(3-2) emission is only detected within r_1/2 ~ 2-4 kpc relies heavily on the assumed profile shape and increases M_dyn by up to 0.9 dex for J1234.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inferring stellar mass via M_* = M_dyn - M_gas - M_BH is vulnerable to systematic offsets between M_dyn and M_gas estimators (e.g., if M_dyn is underestimated or inclination-biased, gas fractions become unphysical).
