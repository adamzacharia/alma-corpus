## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: What is the chemistry of protoplanetary disks around low-mass M4-M5 stars at 10-100 AU scales, and how does it compare to disks around solar-type stars?  
  > "We present an ALMA survey of molecular line emission toward a sample of five protoplanetary disks around M4-M5 stars (FP Tau, J0432+1827, J1100-7619, J1545-3417, and Sz 69)." (Abstract); "Overall, most of the observable chemistry at 10-100AU appears similar for solar-type and M4-M5 disks, but hydrocarbons may be more abundant around the cooler stars." (Abstract); "To date, disk chemistry around low-mass M-stars (stellar masses $<$0.5M$_\Sun$, spectral types typically from $\sim$M4-M9) has barely been explored beyond the inner $\sim$10 AU." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Estimate disk centers and extract line/continuum emission with Keplerian masks**: Fit 2D Gaussians to the dust continuum to find disk centers; apply Keplerian masks across emission velocity channels to extract spectra, moment 0 maps, radial profiles, and integrated fluxes; estimate uncertainties via 1000 random/bootstrapped samples.  
  > "We estimated the disk centers using two-dimensional Gaussian fits to the dust continuum emission. We used Keplerian masks (cite: cite_rosenfeldetal2013a; cite_yenetal2016; cite_salinasetal2017) to extract emission for each disk (cite: cite_kepmask) across all emission channels for each molecular line." (Image Analysis); "We used the Keplerian-masked channels to then generate spectra, velocity-integrated emission maps, radial profiles, and integrated fluxes." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: Five M4-M5 disks from multiple star-forming regions (Taurus, Lupus, Chamaeleon I) were selected for bright 12CO and 13CO detections in existing ALMA surveys to avoid single-cloud bias and match selection criteria of early solar-type disk chemistry surveys.  
  > "The disks were chosen from multiple star-forming regions, avoiding bias toward any one molecular cloud." (Disk Sample); "We note that the five disks in the M4-M5 disk sample were selected specifically for their bright $^{12}$CO (J=3–2) and $^{13}$CO (J=3–2) detections in existing ALMA disk surveys (cite: cite_ansdelletal2016; cite_longetal2017). These selection criteria are similar to those used by early exploratory chemistry surveys of solar-type and Herbig Ae disks" (Disk Sample)
- Why this method: Keplerian masks reduce noise in integrated emission; pixel-by-pixel hyperfine fitting reduces line blending and avoids systematic errors from radial variations in line width, center, and peak.  
  > "Keplerian masks decrease the amount of noise that is incorporated into the integrated emission." (Image Analysis); "By fitting to the spectrum through each pixel, we reduce line blending of the hyperfine components of the spectra, which allows for tighter constraints on the fit. We also avoid systematic errors due to changes in line width, line center, and line peak as a function of disk location." (Pixel-by-Pixel Hyperfine Fits)
- Alternative: Optical depth as the primary driver of the C2H vs. HCN flux correlation was investigated by comparing against optically thick 13CO 2-1 fluxes and rejected.  
  > "We thus conclude that optically thick emission is not the primary reason for the C$_2$H 3–2 vs. HCN 3–2 correlation we find in Section 4.3." (Investigation of Optical Depth as the Primary Contributor to Flux Correlations)
- Alternative: Planck-correcting the background continuum temperature prior to calculating excitation temperature (used in previous studies) was rejected as an over-correction.  
  > "Note that unlike the previous studies, we do not calculate the Planck-corrected temperature of the background continuum prior to Equation [\[eq_Texset_Tex1\]](#eq_Texset_Tex1), as this is an over-correction of the background brightness temperature." (Pixel-by-Pixel Hyperfine Fits (Appendix))
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Sample size is small (5 disks) and biased toward bright, gas-rich M4-M5 disks.  
  > "We stress that our sample is biased toward bright, gas-rich M4-M5 disks, and that these findings serve as an *initial* window into $>$10AU disk chemistry for low-mass M-stars." (Summary)
- Limitation (authors): Significant cloud contamination affects CO emission in 3/5 disks (FP Tau, J1545-3417, and Sz 69).  
  > "The disks FP Tau, J1545-3417, and Sz 69 are significantly affected by cloud contamination, which is evident in the CO spectra and the CO channel maps (Appendix 10)." (Emission Morphologies)
- Limitation (authors): Pixel-by-pixel hyperfine fits have significant intrinsic uncertainties (up to ~40% at high optical depths) due to narrow line widths (0.2-0.5 km/s) vs. coarse velocity resolution (0.14 km/s), unmodeled pixel-to-pixel correlations, reliance on faint HCN satellite lines, and breakdown of Gaussian line shape assumptions at high optical depth.  
  > "First, the C$_2$H and HCN pixel-by-pixel spectra toward J1100-7619 have relatively small line widths ($\sim$0.2-0.5km s$^{-1}$) because of the disk’s low inclination angle. This fact, coupled with the relatively coarse velocity resolution of the data (0.14km s$^{-1}$), means that the hyperfine procedure has a limited number of data points to fit for each hyperfine component." (Intrinsic Sources of Error); "Second, the pixel-by-pixel fitting procedure does not account for uncertainties due to correlations between neighboring pixels. Third, the error in the fits is intrinsically large." (Intrinsic Sources of Error); "At the optical depths we are encountering for C$_2$H and HCN, uncertainties intrinsic to the model may become as large as $\sim$40%." (Intrinsic Sources of Error)
- Limitation (authors): Literature distances were used instead of updated Gaia DR2 distances to maintain consistency with literature stellar characteristics (which changes J1100-7619 distance from 160 pc to 191.5 pc).  
  > "The distances in parentheses are the distances assumed in the literature for these disks. We use the values in parentheses throughout this paper to be consistent with the derived stellar characteristics." (Table 1)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using literature distance (160 pc) rather than the Gaia distance (191.5 pc) for J1100-7619 underestimates its physical radii by ~20% and luminosity/distance-normalized fluxes by ~43%.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two disks (J0432+1827 and J1100-7619) could not be self-calibrated while the other three were self-calibrated, introducing inhomogeneous phase/amplitude calibration across the sample.
