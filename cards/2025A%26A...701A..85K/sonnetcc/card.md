## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA Bands 3, 6, 8; subtract continuum**: ALMA pipeline (Band 6) or observatory-delivered calibration (Bands 8 and 3); imaging with Briggs robust 0.5 (Band 6) or natural weighting with taper (Band 3); uvcontsub on [CII] and [OIII] cubes  
  > "Data were calibrated using the ALMA pipeline" (2.1 ALMA data); "A continuum subtraction was performed for both the \[C ii\] and \[O iii\] data cubes using the CASA task uvcontsub where the continuum was estimated using the line-free channels." (2.1 ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, ALMA pipeline
   - parameter: Band 6 robust = 0.5, 5 km/s channels  
  > "when imaging using a Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5" (2.1 ALMA data)
   - parameter: Band 8 channels of 15.625 MHz (~11 km/s)  
  > "an line cube with an rms of 0.39–0.44 mJy/beam in $\sim$11 km s$^{-1}$ (15.625 MHz) channels" (2.1 ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Band 6 flux scale redone for three of ten executions; absolute flux uncertainty about 5% (Band 6) and 10% (Band 8)  
  > "the uncertainty of the absolute flux calibration is estimated to be about 5%" (2.1 ALMA data); "A conservative estimate on the absolute flux calibration is 10%." (2.1 ALMA data)
2. **Derive systemic redshift from line centroids**: Single Gaussian profile fit to each line; mean of the two redshifts as systemic redshift, difference as uncertainty  
  > "based on a single Gaussian profile fit. Taking the mean of these as the systemic redshift and the difference as the uncertainty" (3.2 Redshift)
   - parameter: Line redshifts 7.1329 ([CII]) and 7.1334 ([OIII])  
  > "suggest central redshifts of 7.1329 and 7.1334, respectively" (3.2 Redshift)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per line  
  > "based on a single Gaussian profile fit." (3.2 Redshift)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Estimate lensing magnification**: Parametric Lenstool mass model of Abell 1689 evaluated at the source position; statistical error from MCMC samples, systematic error from alternative mass models  
  > "this model predicts a single image at this location with a total magnification of 9.6" (3.4 Gravitational lensing); "we used the best-fit mass model and the range of Markov chain Monte Carlo samples from the optimisation performed in" (3.4 Gravitational lensing)
   - software: Lenstool
   - parameter: Statistical relative error 19%; systematic relative error 10%  
  > "We obtain a statistical relative error of 19%" (3.4 Gravitational lensing); "We estimate a systematic relative error of 10%." (3.4 Gravitational lensing)
   - assumption: No significant cluster substructure or cluster member near the source, so magnification varies by less than 10% across the source  
  > "This is due to the fact that there is no significant cluster substructure or cluster member identified in the vicinity of A1689-zD1" (3.4 Gravitational lensing)
   - fitting: MCMC optimisation of the mass model by Limousin et al.  
  > "the range of Markov chain Monte Carlo samples from the optimisation performed in" (3.4 Gravitational lensing)
   - validation: Compared with other mass models of the cluster with different cluster-scale and galaxy-scale mass contributions  
  > "we make use of additional existing mass models of the same cluster" (3.4 Gravitational lensing)
4. **Decompose line cubes into spatial-spectral components**: Iterative per-component procedure on the continuum-subtracted cubes: narrowband image, 2D elliptical Gaussian fit, 1D Gaussian spectrum fit, subtraction of a 3D model; repeated three times, with an arctan velocity-gradient model for extended components  
  > "we produce a narrowband image from the line data comprising the main channels of emission in each component; (2) we fit this image with a 2D elliptical Gaussian model for the line flux distribution" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components); "we subtracted a 3D model of line emission for this component combining the spatial and spectral model" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components); "this fit is performed three times iteratively, subtracting all other components from the datacube before a new fit" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
   - parameter: Five components (violet, blue, green, yellow, red), blue and red each with two sub-components; components first identified in [CII] and recovered in [OIII]  
  > "We note that we originally identified the components from the" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components); "In addition to the three main components mentioned above, two fainter components peaking at" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
   - parameter: Velocity gradients modelled with an empirical arctan model (truncation velocity and radius)  
  > "an empirical velocity gradient model (arctan model with a truncation velocity" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
   - assumption: Choice of decomposition is somewhat arbitrary and driven by data complexity and S/N; inclination fixed to 30 deg for all components (bracketed values are fixed)  
  > "the choice of decomposition (number of components and their parametrization), while somewhat arbitrary, is driven by the complexity and the high signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) of the data" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components); "Values in square brackets are fixed parameters." (Table 4 notes)
   - fitting: 2D elliptical Gaussian for flux distribution; 1D Gaussian for velocity and dispersion; no sampler, priors or likelihood stated  
  > "we extracted the corresponding spectrum of this component and fit line parameters with a 1D Gaussian" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
   - validation: Components recover 95% and 97% of total [CII] and [OIII] emission; model and observed moment-0/1 maps and residuals compared  
  > "we recovered 95 and 97% of the total emission measured in the \[C ii\] and \[O iii\] line data, respectively" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components); "The residual maps indicate that the models effectively reproduce the observations." (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
5. **Decompose the dust continuum and derive dust temperature and L_IR per component**: Forced-aperture photometry following the [OIII] component model, plus CASA imfit modelling with four components; modified blackbody with beta=1.7 to get T_dust and L_IR; T_dust fixed to 41 K for violet and red  
  > "we optimized spatial positions and shapes of the apertures following the five components of the" (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum); "Next, we modeled and decomposed each contribution by using CASA imfit. From the observed morphology, we assumed four components." (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: Violet (red) component not fit in Band 6 (Band 8) maps; photometry taken in a 0.2'' radius aperture on the residual map; positions of faint components fixed, flux density free  
  > "We fixed the spatial positions of the faint components of red and violet when they are included in the model." (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum)
   - assumption: Single modified blackbody with spectral index beta = 1.7; T_dust = 41 K for violet and red  
  > "By assuming a single modified blackbody and a spectral index of $\beta=1.7$ in a spatially averaged manner (cite: akins22)" (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum); "$T_{\rm dust}=41$ K taken from the spatially integrated best-fit value presented in (cite: akins22)" (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Sum of components compared with total continuum: Band 8 comparable, Band 6 model underestimates total flux by ~50%  
  > "while the band 6 continuum model underestimates the total flux by $\sim50\%$" (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum)
6. **Derive total and per-component SFR**: Sum of IR-based (obscured) and UV-based (unobscured) SFR using the ALMA and HST observations; UV luminosity measured in 0.2'' radius apertures, 3 sigma upper limits for components without HST counterparts  
  > "We calculated the total SFR by summing the obscured (SFR$_{\rm IR}$) and unobscured SFR (SFR$_{\rm UV}$) based on the ALMA and HST observations." (4.1.1 Total SFR); "we obtained a 3$\sigma$ upper limit with the same size aperture for the other components" (4.1.1 Total SFR)
   - assumption: Same IR and UV to SFR conversions as Akins et al., based on Murphy et al.  
  > "We used the same conversions from IR and UV luminosity to SFR as (cite: akins22) based on the calibration presented in (cite: murphy11)" (4.1.1 Total SFR)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistent with previous measurements; calibration uncertainty not included in the error  
  > "We did not account for the calibration uncertainty in the error estimate." (4.1.1 Total SFR)
7. **Compare line luminosities, ratios and sizes with literature relations**: Per-component and total L[CII], L[OIII], their ratio and SFR placed on SFR-L_line, SFR-L[OIII]/L[CII], Sigma_SFR-Sigma_[CII] and size-velocity dispersion diagrams alongside local and z>6 literature samples  
  > "We also show for comparison results obtained in local galaxies and $z>6$ galaxies without active galactic nuclei (AGNs) from the literature." (4.1.2 SFR-L_line relation); "By assuming virialized clouds in the same manner as (cite: spilker22), we also calculated the expected size and velocity dispersion at given mass surface density" (4.1.4 Size-velocity relation)
   - parameter: Component sizes from 2D Gaussian FWHM of the model; effective radius taken as HWHM  
  > "assuming for the Gaussian profiles that the half-width-at-half-maximum is comparable with the effective radius" (4.1.4 Size-velocity relation)
   - assumption: Plotted errors do not include size uncertainties  
  > "We note that the plotted errors do not include the uncertainties of the sizes." (4.1.3 SFR-L_[OIII]/L_[CII] relation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Estimate dynamical masses of components**: Disk formula (Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. 2020 Eq. 4) for the blue component, virial dispersion-dominated formula (Bothwell et al. 2013 Eq. 10) for the others, separately for [CII] and [OIII]; v_circ = 1.763 sigma_0 / sin(i) with i = arccos(FWHM2/FWHM1)  
  > "For blue, we assumed a disk-like gas distribution, given the potential rotation" (4.1.6 Dynamical masses); "we assume dispersion-dominated systems and virialized bodies, following Equation 10 of (cite: bothwell13)" (4.1.6 Dynamical masses)
   - assumption: Components may not be virialized, but estimates considered meaningful  
  > "We note that given the complicated morphology and kinematics of the system, each component might not be virialized." (4.1.6 Dynamical masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Both lines show complex profiles and surface brightness; the continuum shows similar complex structure  
  > "Both lines show a complex line profile as well as complex surface brightness distribution." (3.1 [CII] and [OIII])
- CO(3-2), CO(6-5), CO(7-6) and [CI](1-0) are not detected; 3 sigma upper limits given  
  > "The CO(3-2) line was not detected, and the $3\sigma$ limit is given in Table [1](#tab:fluxresults) assuming a linewidth of 300 km s$^{-1}$." (3.3 Molecular gas)
- Violet and yellow components have no HST counterpart but show dust continuum, indicating completely dust-obscured regions  
  > "the violet and yellow components do not show HST counterparts, while dust continuum emission is detected from these components" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
- Only the blue component shows a clear velocity gradient in [CII]; other components regarded as dispersion-dominated  
  > "We thus regarded blue as a potential rotator and the other components as dispersion-dominated systems." (4.1.5 Kinematics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The Band 6 observations were designed to find the [CII] line and determine the redshift; Band 8 then followed up [OIII] using that redshift  
  > "Using this redshift ALMA band 8 observations were obtained for the" (2.1 ALMA data); "While the original goal of the \[C ii\] observations was to determine the redshift" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Components do not follow a regular rotating-disk structure, so an iterative component decomposition was used  
  > "Therefore, we identified and model each component from the datacubes using an iterative process" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
- Alternative: Three physical interpretations discussed: early assembly of five small clumps, a two- or three-galaxy merger, and a combination of the two; no single one is selected  
  > "A third plausible interpretation could be a combination of the two." (4.3.3 Combined scenario); "one interpretation could be interaction and merger" (4.3.2 Galaxy merger interpretation)
- Alternative: A simple ordered rotation is ruled out, in contrast to the clear NW-SE velocity gradient argued by Wong et al.  
  > "which allows us to clearly rule out a simple ordered rotation for A1689-zD1" (4.3 Considering the physical origin of A1689-zD1)
- How they decided it was enough: Number of components chosen by data complexity and S/N; judged adequate from the recovered emission fraction and residual maps  
  > "is driven by the complexity and the high signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) of the data" (3.5 Decomposition into galaxy components)
- Limitation (authors): Aperture photometry may be contaminated by neighbouring components  
  > "we cannot rule out the possibility that some measurements within the aperture are affected by contributions from other components" (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum)
- Limitation (authors): Band 6 continuum model misses about half the total flux because diffuse emission is not recovered  
  > "while the band 6 continuum model underestimates the total flux by $\sim50\%$" (3.6 Disentangling the dust continuum)
- Limitation (authors): Line-of-sight (radial) structure cannot be measured, so merger scenarios are hard to constrain  
  > "However, without the spatial information from the radial direction, such merger scenarios are difficult to constrain." (4.3.2 Galaxy merger interpretation)
- Limitation (authors): Calibration uncertainty is not included in the SFR error  
  > "We did not account for the calibration uncertainty in the error estimate." (4.1.1 Total SFR)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Decomposition into five components is non-unique and has no stated fit statistics, priors or uncertainty treatment for the shape parameters.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Magnification uncertainty is stated as 0.19 in the abstract but as a 19% statistical error plus 10% systematic in the text; propagation into luminosities and masses is not detailed.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dynamical masses rest on virial or disk formulas with fixed inclination 30 deg for most components, and per-component [CII]-[OIII] differences are large.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 1 line rows are unlabeled in the provided text, so mapping of intensities to lines relies on the conclusions.
