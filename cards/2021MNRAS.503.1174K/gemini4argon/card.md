## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image JVLA 3 GHz and GMRT 325 MHz radio continuum data**: Calibrate, flag RFI, and image JVLA S-band (A and C configurations) and GMRT P-band data using TCLEAN in CASA, with self-calibration applied to GMRT data  
  > "All data were calibrated and imaged following the standard procedures with the Common Astronomy Software Application (cite: mcmullin07)." (JVLA S-band); "For cleaning the A-configuration calibrated data, we used the TCLEAN task with a Briggs weighting scheme featuring a robust parameter of 2.0." (JVLA S-band); "We executed manual calibration and RFI flagging before initiating the cleaning procedure with the TCLEAN task." (GMRT P-band)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN
   - parameter: JVLA A-config: Briggs robust = 2.0, nterms = 2, wprojplanes = 128, multiscale scales = [2, 5, 10] pixels; JVLA C-config and GMRT: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "Frequency dependent clean components (with two Taylor terms; nterms=2) were also used in imaging to mitigate large-bandwidth effects (cite: rau11)." (JVLA S-band); "Additionally, we implemented widefield imaging with 128 projection planes (wprojplanes=128) and image deconvolution was carried out with the multi-scale multi-frequency synthesis algorithm on scales of [2,5,10] pixels (cite: rau11) for improved handling of the extended sources present in the field." (JVLA S-band); "A similar procedure was used for C-configuration, however with a robust parameter of 0.5 in order to achieve a balance between the resolution and sensitivity." (JVLA S-band)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Examined higher resolution images for compact sources; measured RMS noise in final images  
  > "However, we also created images with lower sensitivities and therefore higher resolutions, in order to examine any undetected compact sources." (JVLA S-band)
2. **Measure 3 GHz radio continuum fluxes and point-source sensitivity via PSF fitting**: Inject ~200 artificial point sources with GALFIT to determine 1-sigma sensitivity, then iteratively fit and subtract point sources at HST/WFC3 and ALMA 870 um prior positions followed by free-position point sources for residual jet components  
  > "We use the software GALFIT (cite: peng10) to artificially place point sources (about 200 in number) in an area of \sim 80^{\prime\prime} in radius centered at the location of the cluster centre." (Radio continuum flux measurement); "Hence we execute another round of fitting with additional point sources having both position and flux free, besides the ones for the galaxies." (Radio continuum flux measurement); "Hence we execute another round of fitting with additional point sources having both position and flux free, besides the ones for the galaxies." (Radio continuum flux measurement)
   - software: GALFIT
   - parameter: Artificial source flux range: 4.5 - 7.5 \mu Jy within 80'' radius; exclusion radius 1'' around >4\sigma pixels  
  > "The flux of these sources is randomly chosen within the range 4.5 \mu Jy – 7.5 \mu Jy which is where most of the radio-detected cluster galaxies lie (cite: coogan18)." (Radio continuum flux measurement); "Regions that are within a distance of 1^{\prime\prime} from any > 4\sigma pixel have been ignored during this exercise in order to prevent source confusion." (Radio continuum flux measurement)
   - assumption: Sources are unresolved point sources smaller than the synthesized beam HPBW  
  > "We choose to use point sources since the size of the galaxies are smaller than the synthesised beam half-power beam width." (Radio continuum flux measurement)
   - fitting: Simultaneous point-source fitting in GALFIT with fixed positional priors for known galaxies and free position and flux for additional radio sources  
  > "We use the results from the final round of fitting where we incorporate the complete list of sources simultaneously." (Radio continuum flux measurement)
   - validation: Checked residuals after final subtraction; constrained fluxes with C-configuration integrated flux; checked astrometric offsets against HST F140W and ALMA 870 \mu m images  
  > "Following the final subtraction, we check for any residual emission around the fits." (Radio continuum flux measurement); "We also repeat the same exercise with respect to the ALMA 870 \mu m image and draw the same conclusion, although with only 4 sources that are detected at \gtrsim 6 \sigma at 3 GHz." (Origin of the radio emissions)
3. **Compute TIR-radio luminosity ratio (q_TIR) and compare with the main-sequence relation**: Convert 3 GHz flux to rest-frame 1.4 GHz luminosity and combine with L_IR (derived from ALMA 870 \mu m fluxes) to calculate q_TIR for cluster galaxies and 3\sigma upper limits for jet sources  
  > "Using the revised 3 GHz flux values of the cluster core galaxies along with the total-infrared luminosity (L_{IR}; rest-frame 8–1000 \mu m) that was derived from the 870 \mu m flux presented in (cite: coogan18) using SEDs from (cite: bethermin15), we measure the total-infrared (TIR) and radio luminosity ratio (q_{TIR}) using the following relation (cite: helou88):" (TIR-radio correlation); "Furthermore, we measure the q_{TIR} 3\sigma upper limits (due to a lack of sub-mm detection) for the additional radio sources reported in the previous section and show their range on the y-axis of Fig. 4." (TIR-radio correlation)
   - assumption: Radio spectral index \alpha = -0.7 for star formation; cosmology \Omega_m = 0.3, \Omega_\Lambda = 0.7, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1; Chabrier IMF  
  > "Throughout, we adopt the concordance \Lambda CDM cosmology, characterized by \Omega_{m}=0.3, \Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7, and H_{0}=70 km s^{-1} Mpc^{-1}." (Introduction); "Since in this section we are targeting emission due to star formation, we assume an \alpha=-0.7 (cite: delhaize17)." (TIR-radio correlation)
   - fitting: Fit normalisation of the Delvecchio et al. (2020) q_TIR(M_*, z) relation to the 10 cluster galaxies  
  > "Since we just have 10 galaxies to work with, we only fit for the normalisation of the equation for a comparison of our sample with their results." (TIR-radio correlation)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Test physical origin of the counterpart-less radio sources via SED prediction and multi-wavelength image stacking**: Calculate noise fluctuation probabilities, predict expected 870 \mu m fluxes assuming star formation, and median stack 1.5'' cutouts of the ALMA 870 \mu m continuum image and HST F140W image at the radio source positions  
  > "To examine if these are simply undetected galaxies, which would mean that the radio flux could be traced back to star formation. We estimate an expected 870 \mu m flux for each of them." (Origin of the radio emissions); "We also median stack 1.5^{\prime\prime} cutouts of the 870 \mu m continuum image from the regions of the sources and do not detect any emission even with the resulting sensitivity of 28 \mu Jy/beam." (Origin of the radio emissions)
   - parameter: 1.5'' cutout size for ALMA 870 \mu m median stacking; 20'' radius (170 kpc) region for noise probability calculation  
  > "where P_{o} is the probability of finding a point source of a certain sigma within a Gaussian distribution while n is the number of synthesised beams that make up a region with radius 20^{\prime\prime} (or \sim 170 kpc) surrounding the centre of CL J1449+0856." (Origin of the radio emissions); "We also median stack 1.5^{\prime\prime} cutouts of the 870 \mu m continuum image from the regions of the sources and do not detect any emission even with the resulting sensitivity of 28 \mu Jy/beam." (Origin of the radio emissions)
   - assumption: Spectral index \alpha = -0.7 and Bethermin et al. (2012) main-sequence SED templates  
  > "This is done by first converting the measured 3 GHz fluxes (S_{3GHz}; W Hz^{-1} m^{-2}) into observed frame 1.4 GHz (S_{1.4GHz}; W Hz^{-1} m^{-2}), using a spectral index of –0.7 (cite: delhaize17)." (Origin of the radio emissions); "This is then converted to the expected flux at 870 \mu m using SEDs from (cite: bethermin12)." (Origin of the radio emissions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Calculate integrated radio-jet luminosity, mechanical cavity power, SZ residual correlation, and Inverse-Compton X-ray contribution**: Integrate rest-frame 10 MHz - 10 GHz radio luminosity from the 6 jets, convert to cavity power P_cav via the O'Sullivan et al. (2011) scaling relation, compare jet locations with the ALMA+ACA 92 GHz SZ residual map, and estimate IC X-ray flux limits from GMRT 325 MHz and Chandra stacking  
  > "To make a comparison, we add up the contributions of all the detected jets in place of the jets from a central AGN, which gives us a flux of 30.6\pm3.3 \, \mu Jy and a corresponding integrated radio luminosity (10 MHz–10 GHz rest-frame) of 2.1 ~(\pm 0.2)~ \times 10^{41} ~ergs~ s^{-1}." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?); "We can hence use the integrated radio luminosity from all these jets" (Quantifying the feedback); "In Fig. 1 we superimpose this map with the location of the radio-jets." (The SZ residual); "Furthermore, we stack the Chandra X-ray data at the locations of the jets to observationally confirm a lack of IC X-ray emission." (Inverse-Compton contribution)
   - assumption: Jet spectral index \alpha = -1.04 \pm 0.03 (explored range -0.7 to -1.5, and -1.2); cavity energy E_cav = 4pV; Lorentz factor 10^3 - 10^5 and photon index 2 for IC calculation  
  > "With a robust sample of 190 extragalactic radio-jets, they find a mean spectral index of -1.04 \pm 0.03 that we use in our calculation." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?); "This comes out to be 4pV under the assumption that the cavity is dominated by relativistic plasma." (Quantifying the feedback); "This is measured using a Lorentz factor of 10^{3}–10^{5} and a photon index of 2, the reasons for which has been provided in (cite: fabian09) who estimate the IC contributions for HDF130 at z=1.99: a case very similar to ours." (Inverse-Compton contribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Assessed flux boosting upper limit (~40% assuming 1.5\sigma noise contribution per source) and tested single central jet R1 alone  
  > "We can also have flux boosting contributions and we estimate a generous upper limit of the resulting overestimation of the jet flux to be \sim40\%, by assuming an additional noise contribution of 1.5\sigma for each source." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?); "But repeating the same analysis with the central jet (R1) only brings down the luminosity by a factor of 4, keeping it well within the scatter and still slightly above the average." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?)

## Qualitative findings

- Six faint 3 GHz radio sources without HST NIR or ALMA 870 \mu m counterparts are detected in the core of CL J1449+0856 and identified as AGN radio-jets.  
  > "We report the detection of multiple faint radio sources, that we identify as AGN-jets, within CLJ1449+0856 at z=2 using 3 GHz VLA observations." (Abstract); "Finally, the possible association with known galaxies due to physical proximity for 4/6 of these objects without any overlap led us to conclude them to be AGN radio-jets." (Summary and Conclusion)
- AGN kinetic feedback in CL J1449+0856 is distributed over multiple sites rather than a single central BCG, pointing toward residual cold-gas accretion and a non-cool-core steady state.  
  > "In line with the already detected abundance of star formation, this mode of feedback being distributed over multiple sites, contrary to a single central source observed at low redshifts, points to accretion of gas into the cluster centre." (Abstract); "This also suggests a ‘steady state’ of the cluster featuring non cool-core like behaviour." (Abstract)
- A marginal 2\sigma positive signal in the ALMA+ACA 92 GHz SZ residual map is located between the brightest jets R1 and R7_W, hinting at an ICM electron under-density caused by jet feedback.  
  > "Interestingly, there seems to be a hint of an under-density (in the form of a positive signal), albeit of a 2\sigma significance, between sources R1 and R7_W which are two of the brightest sources and therefore strongest jets in our list." (The SZ residual)
- Cluster galaxies in the dense core of CL J1449+0856 follow the same TIR-radio correlation (within 0.2 dex) as field main-sequence star-forming galaxies.  
  > "We find agreement within 0.2 dex with the results of (cite: delvecchio20) for star-forming main sequence galaxies." (Summary and Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining 3 GHz VLA continuum with ALMA sub-mm and HST/WFC3 NIR observations provides a complete picture of galaxies regardless of dust obscuration, while revised flagging and Briggs robust=2.0 weighting of the 3 GHz data maximizes sensitivity for faint source detection.  
  > "We combine 3 GHz Very Large Array (VLA) continuum data with ALMA sub-mm and HST/WFC3 NIR observations to extract a complete picture of galaxies irrespective of their levels of dust obscuration." (Data observation and reduction); "For cleaning the A-configuration calibrated data, we used the TCLEAN task with a Briggs weighting scheme featuring a robust parameter of 2.0. This was aimed at facilitating the highest sensitivity possible for source detection." (JVLA S-band)
- Why this method: Point-source PSF fitting in GALFIT was chosen because the galaxies are smaller than the synthesized beam HPBW, and the empirical L_radio - P_cav correlation was used because the 4\sigma diffuse X-ray detection is too shallow to detect X-ray cavities directly.  
  > "We choose to use point sources since the size of the galaxies are smaller than the synthesised beam half-power beam width." (Radio continuum flux measurement); "But such a direct calculation would not be possible in this work as no cavities were detected in the 4\sigma X-ray diffuse emission (cite: valentino16)." (Quantifying the feedback); "Moreover, the use of such a relationship allows us to make measurements directly on the radio data (cite: best07), thereby avoiding the issue of cavity detectability in shallow X-ray images (which is the case in our work)." (Quantifying the feedback)
- Alternative: Noise fluctuations as the origin of the additional radio sources (rejected due to low random probabilities of 5e-5 to 0.03 and spatial clustering near known galaxies/AGNs)  
  > "The probabilities for the three primary sources (R1, R2 and R3) are found to vary from 5 \times 10^{-5} for R1 to 0.03 for R3." (Origin of the radio emissions); "Hence the possibility of these radio sources being generated due to noise is rather small." (Origin of the radio emissions)
- Alternative: Undetected dusty star-forming galaxies as the origin of the radio sources (rejected because expected 870 \mu m fluxes would be detected at >4\sigma and stacked 870 \mu m cutouts show no detection)  
  > "it is evident that these sources would have been comfortably detected at $>4\sigma$ had their emission been due to star formation" (Origin of the radio emissions)
- Alternative: Galaxy merger-driven tidal features (rejected due to lack of stellar emission in HST F140W stacking and lack of star formation in ALMA 870 \mu m stacking)  
  > "This eliminates a likelihood of these being galaxy merger driven tidal features, since had this been the case, there would have been structures with either detectable star formation or unobscured stellar emission." (Origin of the radio emissions)
- Alternative: Cluster merger-driven diffuse radio emission such as radio halos or relics (rejected due to the concentrated, unresolved nature of the emission sites)  
  > "This only leaves two likely candidates: AGN radio-jets or cluster merger driven diffuse emission (radio halos and relics), both resulting from synchrotron emission. Given the concentrated nature of the emission sites, we can reject the latter." (Origin of the radio emissions)
- How they decided it was enough: In GALFIT point-source extraction, the list of fitted sources was incrementally expanded until no detectable (>=3.5\sigma) residual emission remained in the cluster core.  
  > "The list of sources being fit is incrementally expanded until there are no remaining detectable sources present within the core of CL J1449+0856." (Radio continuum flux measurement)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size of 10 cluster galaxies prevents detection of subtle deviations in the q_TIR vs M_* relation  
  > "As shown in Fig. 4, we see an agreement within 1\sigma (0.2 dex), although acknowledging the inability of our limited dataset to detect a more subtle digression in the behaviour of q_{TIR}, if it exists." (TIR-radio correlation)
- Limitation (authors): Radio luminosity and cavity power estimates depend strongly on the unknown radio spectral index, which cannot be tightly constrained without deeper low-frequency observations  
  > "It is worth noting however that this luminosity calculation is strongly dependent on the radio spectral index which can be anywhere between –0.7 (cite: delhaize17) to –1.5 (the upper limit we calculate based on our GMRT 325 MHz measurements)." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?); "Only deeper low-frequency observations can settle this uncertainty." (Summary and Conclusion)
- Limitation (authors): Low S/N (4\sigma) of the diffuse X-ray ICM emission prevents direct detection of X-ray cavities, measurement of cluster binding energy, or precise localisation of the X-ray peak  
  > "However, we can only estimate a rough peak of the X-ray emission with the limited S/N, which still seems to be within this region." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?); "But such a direct calculation would not be possible in this work as no cavities were detected in the 4\sigma X-ray diffuse emission (cite: valentino16)." (Quantifying the feedback); "Although the measurement of the binding energy is not possible in CL J1449+0856 due to a low significance detection of the ICM X-ray emission, its galaxy group-like mass could suggest that we are underestimating the consequences of the detected radio-jets on the overall evolution of CL J1449+0856." (Effect of low cluster mass)
- Limitation (authors): Possible flux boosting of up to ~40% in the faint radio-jet flux measurements  
  > "We can also have flux boosting contributions and we estimate a generous upper limit of the resulting overestimation of the jet flux to be \sim40\%, by assuming an additional noise contribution of 1.5\sigma for each source." (Radio emission: How much do we really have?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two of the six claimed radio-jet components (R7_N and R7_E) are detected at <= 3.5 sigma (2.6 sigma based on 3.4 / 1.3 uJy) and are included primarily due to spatial proximity to R7_W and AGN S7.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying the O'Sullivan et al. (2011) cavity power scaling relation (calibrated on low-z massive systems with single central AGNs) to the sum of six unresolved radio sources in a z=2 group-mass cluster involves substantial extrapolation.
