## Analysis recipe

1. **Select millimeter sources**: SExtractor detection in native and tapered natural-weighted maps; blind MAIN and IRAC-prior SECONDARY selections  
  > "The source detection was performed with the `SExtractor`" (Observations and source detection with ALMA); "141 sources were detected with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) over 5.0 in the natural maps or over 4.5 in the tapered maps" (Observations and source detection with ALMA); "39 sources are detected with $5.0\geq \mathrm{SNR}\geq 4.0$" (Observations and source detection with ALMA)
   - software: SExtractor
   - parameter: MAIN: natural-map SNR>5.0 or tapered-map SNR>4.5  
  > "141 sources were detected with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) over 5.0 in the natural maps or over 4.5 in the tapered maps" (Observations and source detection with ALMA)
   - parameter: SECONDARY: natural-map 5.0≥SNR≥4.0 and IRAC channel 2 SNR≥5.0  
  > "39 sources are detected with $5.0\geq \mathrm{SNR}\geq 4.0$" (Observations and source detection with ALMA); "in the natural maps associated with Spitzer/IRAC channel 2 ($4.5\,\mu \mathrm{m}$) detection ($\mathrm{SNR}\geq 5.0$)." (Observations and source detection with ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: False-detection rates <1% for MAIN and <50% for SECONDARY  
  > "which corresponds to a false-detection rate of ${<}$1%." (Observations and source detection with ALMA); "(a false-detection rate of ${<}$50%)" (Observations and source detection with ALMA)
2. **Prepare multiwavelength photometry**: Combine literature photometry, cross-match SEIP, screen blending, and classify sources by counterpart detections  
  > "Those sources are cross-matched with the ALMA source positions within 1.5$''$." (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts); "To evaluate this effect, we compare the flux densities of IRAC channel 2 extracted from the SEIP" (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts); "We classify the ALCS sources into eight groups based on the quality of the multi-wavelength photometry." (Data quality, sample selection, and magnification)
   - software: Spitzer Enhanced Imaging Products (SEIP)
   - parameter: Use SEIP for 0.5<fALCS/fSEIP<2.0; upper limits for ratio<0.5; omit for ratio>2.0  
  > "If $0.5 < f_{\mathrm{ALCS}}/f_{\mathrm{SEIP}} < 2.0$, we use the photometric data from the SEIP for our SED modeling." (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts); "If $f_{\mathrm{ALCS}}/f_{\mathrm{SEIP}} < 0.5$, we treat the SEIP data as upper limits." (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts); "In the case of $f_{\mathrm{ALCS}}/f_{\mathrm{SEIP}} > 2.0$, we conservatively do not utilize the data from the SEIP" (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts)
   - parameter: Tier counterpart detection: above 2σ in at least one band  
  > "If a source is detected above 2$\sigma$ in at least one band, the corresponding column is checked by the symbol" (Table 1)
   - parameter: Exclude BCGs, possible mismatched/contaminated sources, and the confirmed foreground-cluster member  
  > "In this classification, BCGs (11 sources), a possible mismatched source, a possible contaminated source, and a spectroscopically-confirmed member galaxy of a cluster are excluded." (Table 1)
   - parameter: Omit IRAC channel 2 for M0416-ID120, M1206-ID60, ACT0102-ID11, M1115-ID34, ACT0102-ID224  
  > "the photometric data of Spitzer/IRAC channel 2 in M0416-ID120, M1206-ID60, ACT0102-ID11, M1115-ID34, and ACT0102-ID224 are not used in the SED modeling." (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare SEIP IRAC channel 2 fluxes against the HST-position-deblended catalog  
  > "who de-blended the photometric data utilizing the HST source positions." (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts); "To evaluate this effect, we compare the flux densities of IRAC channel 2 extracted from the SEIP" (HST, Spitzer, and Herschel counterparts)
3. **Assign redshifts and magnifications**: Adopt catalog redshifts and fiducial GLAFIC magnifications; fit multiple images individually  
  > "In the SED modeling, the multiply-imaged sources are treated individually." (SED modeling with `CIGALE`); "The redshifts are fixed at the values reported in (cite: 2023arXiv230301658F) (69/180 sources have spectroscopic redshifts)." (SED modeling with `CIGALE`); "The magnification factors are extracted from (cite: 2023arXiv230301658F), where the lens model of `GLAFIC` (cite: 2010PASJ...62.1017O) is adopted as a fiducial." (Data quality, sample selection, and magnification)
   - software: GLAFIC
   - assumption: Main analysis neglects photometric-redshift and lens-model/magnification uncertainties  
  > "The uncertainties of the photometric redshifts are not considered in the main part of this paper." (SED modeling with `CIGALE`); "Here we note that the uncertainties in the lens models and magnification factors are not considered in this paper." (Data quality, sample selection, and magnification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spectroscopic confirmation for six multiply-imaged sources; additional associations use positions, lens models, and SED similarities  
  > "We spectroscopically confirm six multiply-imaged sources" (Data quality, sample selection, and magnification); "based on the source positions, the lens models, and the similarities in their SEDs." (Data quality, sample selection, and magnification)
4. **Extend the dust-emission library**: Generate EThemis by extending Themis with DustEM  
  > "We use the DustEM code (cite: 2011A&A...525A.103C) to extend the Themis model." (Dust emission from host galaxy); "our new model, which we hereafter call EThemis (Extended Themis)." (Dust emission from host galaxy)
   - software: DustEM, Themis, EThemis
   - parameter: Umin=0.1–6309; logarithmic step 0.2  
  > "We extend it to $U_{\mathrm{min}}=0.1\text{--}6309$ ($10^{-1}\text{--}10^{3.8}$) with a step size of 0.2 in logarithmic space." (Dust emission from host galaxy)
   - parameter: Tdust=12–83 K; Tdustpeak=20–141 K  
  > "This parameter range corresponds to $T_{\mathrm{dust}}=12\text{--}83\,\mathrm{K}$ ($T_{\mathrm{dust}}^{\mathrm{peak}}=20\text{--}141\,\mathrm{K}$" (Dust emission from host galaxy)
   - assumption: Dust temperature characterized by the minimum illuminating radiation field  
  > "Dust temperature is characterized by the minimum radiation field illuminating the interstellar dust ($U_{\mathrm{min}}$)." (Dust emission from host galaxy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Reproduce the original Themis model while covering high dust temperatures  
  > "We confirm that the extended model successfully reproduces the original Themis model by covering high dust temperature cases." (Dust emission from host galaxy)
5. **Fit galaxy and AGN SEDs**: CIGALE multi-component UV-to-millimeter SED fitting; compare fits with and without an AGN  
  > "We conduct multi-component SED modeling to the UV to millimeter photometry of the ALCS tier-1/2/3/4/5/6 samples" (SED modeling with `CIGALE`); "To identify whether a galaxy hosts an AGN or not, we utilize the Bayesian Information Criterion" (AGN emission)
   - software: CIGALE, EThemis, Skirtor
   - parameter: AGN required for ΔBIC>10; AGN always excluded for tier-2/3/4/5/6  
  > "If the addition of an AGN component reduces BIC by more than 10" (AGN emission); "For the tier-2/3/4/5/6 samples, the AGN component is always excluded" (AGN emission)
   - parameter: Basic grid for tier-1/2/5/6; limited dust-emission grid for tier-3/4; extended AGN grid for A2744-ID227  
  > "First, the ALCS tier-1/2/5/6 samples were analyzed with a basic parameter set" (Detailed description of SED modeling); "Hence, we reanalyzed the SED with an extended parameter set of the AGN module" (Detailed description of SED modeling); "Second, the ALCS tier-3/4 sources were analyzed with a limited parameter set for the dust emission model to avoid unreasonable fitting results." (Detailed description of SED modeling)
   - parameter: Polar dust: emissivity index 1.6; temperature 150 K; opacity unity at 200 μm  
  > "We also add a polar dust emission modeled by a single graybody, where we assume an emissivity index of 1.6, temperature of 150 K, and opacity of unity at 200 $\mu$m." (AGN emission)
   - assumption: Energy balance between UV/optical absorption and infrared re-emission  
  > "`CIGALE` assumes energy balance between UV/optical absorption and infrared re-emission" (SED modeling with `CIGALE`)
   - assumption: Delayed SFH plus exponentially decaying burst  
  > "we employ a delayed star-formation history (SFH), assuming an additional burst with exponential decay." (Stellar emission, dust attenuation, and nebular emission)
   - assumption: Bruzual–Charlot stellar templates; Chabrier IMF; modified Calzetti attenuation; Inoue nebular emission  
  > "The simple stellar population (SSP) is modeled with the stellar templates of (cite: 2003MNRAS.344.1000B), assuming the (cite: 2003PASP..115..763C) initial mass function." (Stellar emission, dust attenuation, and nebular emission); "The dust attenuation is modeled with the Calzetti starburst attenuation law (cite: 2000ApJ...533..682C), where we allow slightly steeper or flatter curves than the original one" (Stellar emission, dust attenuation, and nebular emission); "We also add nebulae emission according to (cite: 2011MNRAS.415.2920I)." (Stellar emission, dust attenuation, and nebular emission)
   - fitting: Model grid; BIC=χ²+k ln(n), with non-reduced χ², k degrees of freedom, n photometric points  
  > "we calculate the likelihood-weighted mean of all the models on the grid as a Bayesian estimation" (Estimation of physical properties); "The BIC is calculated as $\mathrm{BIC}=\chi^2+k\times\ln(n)$" (AGN emission); "where $\chi^2$ is the non-reduced chi-square, $k$ is the number of degrees of freedom (dof), and $n$ is the number of the photometric data points." (AGN emission)
   - validation: χ²/dof<5; inspect SED residuals; reject R0032-ID57 AGN classification using other multiple images and lack of obvious AGN signs  
  > "We successfully reproduce the SEDs of the ALCS tier-1/2/3/4/5/6 samples with a fit quality of $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}<5$." (Results); "However, the other multiple images (R0032-ID32/53/55/58) are not classified as AGNs, and no obvious signs of AGN are found in the SED of R0032-ID57." (Overall properties of ALCS sample); "Therefore, we do not consider R0032-ID57 as a host of an AGN." (Overall properties of ALCS sample)
6. **Estimate intrinsic physical properties**: Likelihood-weighted marginalized-PDF means; derive sSFR; divide quantitative variables by magnification factors  
  > "we calculate the likelihood-weighted mean of the marginalized PDF with a log-uniform prior for the former parameters and with a uniform prior for the latter, respectively." (Estimation of physical properties); "The specific star-formation rates (sSFR = SFR / stellar mass) are derived from the stellar masses and the SFRs" (Estimation of physical properties); "We correct all the quantitative variables for the lensing effect by dividing the observed values by the magnification factors." (Estimation of physical properties)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: Logarithmic: SFR, stellar mass, dust luminosity, dust mass  
  > "where we treat the following parameters in a logarithmic space:" (Estimation of physical properties); "Star-formation rate (SFR)" (Estimation of physical properties); "Dust luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{dust}}$) and mass ($M_{\mathrm{dust}}$)" (Estimation of physical properties)
   - parameter: Linear: dust temperature, E(B−V), IRX, β, δ, bolometric AGN luminosity  
  > "while the following parameters are treated in a linear space:" (Estimation of physical properties); "Infrared excess ($\mathrm{IRX}=\log (L_{\mathrm{dust}}/L_{\mathrm{UV}})$)" (Estimation of physical properties); "Bolometric AGN luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{AGN,\,bol}}$)" (Estimation of physical properties)
   - assumption: SFR averaged over the last 10 Myr; dust luminosity/mass exclude AGN-torus dust  
  > "refers to the average star-formation rate in the last 10 Myr." (Estimation of physical properties); "to describe those of interstellar dust (i.e., not including those in the AGN torus)." (Estimation of physical properties)
   - assumption: Flat universe; H0=70.4 km s−1 Mpc−1; ΩM=0.272; errors 1σ unless specified  
  > "Throughout the paper, we assume a flat universe with $H_0=70.4\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_M=0.272$ (cite: 2011ApJS..192...18K)." (Introduction); "If not specifically mentioned, errors correspond to $1\sigma$ confidence." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Log-uniform priors for logarithmic quantities; uniform priors for linear quantities  
  > "we calculate the likelihood-weighted mean of the marginalized PDF with a log-uniform prior for the former parameters and with a uniform prior for the latter, respectively." (Estimation of physical properties)
   - validation: sSFR confidence intervals propagate stellar-mass and SFR uncertainties  
  > "the confidence intervals of the sSFRs are calculated by propagating the uncertainties associated with the stellar masses and the SFRs." (Estimation of physical properties)
7. **Assess redshift and SED-model systematics**: MAGPHYS photo-z and fixed-EAZY-redshift fits; compare CIGALE and MAGPHYS physical properties with Passing-Bablok regression  
  > "First, we calculate the photometric redshifts of tier-1/2/5/6 sources by optical to millimeter SED analysis with `MAGPHYS photo-z extension`" (Validity of photometric redshift and Impact of redshift uncertainty on physical properties); "where the redshifts are fixed at the photometric ones derived by `EAZY`." (Validity of photometric redshift and Impact of redshift uncertainty on physical properties); "We perform Passing-Bablok regression analysis for each parameter" (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`)
   - software: CIGALE, MAGPHYS photo-z extension, MAGPHYS high-z extension (v2), EAZY
   - parameter: Exclude ALCS-XAGNs and ALCS-nonXAGNs from MAGPHYS tests; code comparison uses tier-1  
  > "For a conservative discussion, we only discuss the tier-1 samples" (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`); "since the current version of `MAGPHYS` cannot treat an AGN component, ALCS-XAGNs and ALCS-nonXAGNs are excluded." (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`)
   - fitting: Passing-Bablok regression for inter-code property conversions  
  > "We perform Passing-Bablok regression analysis for each parameter, where the conversion relation is shown at the bottom of each panel." (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`)
   - validation: Compare photo-z against spectroscopy and EAZY; compare variable/fixed-redshift properties; rerun CIGALE with the MAGPHYS attenuation model  
  > "The left panel plots photometric redshifts estimated by `MAGPHYS photo-z extension` versus spectroscopic ones." (Validity of photometric redshift and Impact of redshift uncertainty on physical properties); "plots the physical properties estimated with variable redshifts versus those estimated at fixed redshifts." (Validity of photometric redshift and Impact of redshift uncertainty on physical properties); "In this case, we confirm good agreement between stellar mass estimated with `CIGALE` and that with `MAGPHYS`" (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`)
8. **Compare star formation and dust properties**: Compare SFR–mass, IRX–mass, IRX–β, attenuation slope, dust temperature, and dust mass with literature samples  
  > "Figure [8](#figure:Ms_SFR) plots the relation between stellar mass and SFR." (SFR versus stellar mass); "The left panel of Figure [9](#figure:beta_IRX) shows the relation between stellar mass and IRX." (IRX versus stellar mass); "The left panel of Figure [13](#figure:dustmass) compares the dust temperature, dust mass, and dust luminosity in the ALCS and the AS2UDS samples." (Dust mass)
   - parameter: Apply −0.37 dex to stellar masses of AS2UDS, ASAGAO, ALESS, ASPECS  
  > "Accordingly, we impose a systematic offset of –0.37 dex in the stellar mass of the AS2UDS, ASAGAO, ALESS, and ASPECS samples." (Comparison samples)
   - parameter: Temperature comparison: Herschel detections over 2σ for ALCS, ASPECS, AS2UDS; well-sampled ALESS subset  
  > "For a conservative discussion, we only plot the sources that are detected with Herschel over 2$\sigma$ for the ALCS, ASPECS, and AS2UDS samples" (Dust temperature); "For the same reason, the ALESS sample is limited to the well-sampled subset reported in (cite: 2021ApJ...919...30D)." (Dust temperature)
   - parameter: Characteristic temperature Tdustpeak[K]=5099/λpeak[μm]; ALCS conversion approximately 1.7×Tdust  
  > "T_{\mathrm{dust}}^{\mathrm{peak}}\,[\mathrm{K}]=5099/\lambda^{\mathrm{peak}}\,[\mu\mathrm{m}]" (Characteristic dust temperature); "T_{\mathrm{dust}}^{\mathrm{peak}}\simeq 1.7\times T_{\mathrm{dust}}" (Conversion method of dust temperature); "Therefore, we convert the value by applying Equation" (Conversion method of dust temperature)
   - assumption: Characteristic temperature uses the dust-SED peak in Jy and is not intrinsic dust temperature  
  > "we utilize the peak wavelength in the dimension of “Jy”" (Characteristic dust temperature); "the characteristic dust temperature does not denote the intrinsic temperature of dust" (Characteristic dust temperature)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inter-code comparison found no obvious systematic differences requiring corrections to SFR, dust luminosity, or dust mass  
  > "The SFRs, dust luminosities, and dust masses are not corrected, because we did not find obvious systematic differences for these parameters." (Comparison samples)
9. **Derive X-ray luminosity upperbounds**: Reprocess Chandra data, merge observations, extract 3σ count-rate upperbounds, and convert with assumed AGN spectra  
  > "Next, we combined the products by using `merge_obs`." (Chandra observations); "Then, we ran `srcflux` to the combined data and obtained the $3\sigma$ upperbounds of the 0.5–7 keV count rates for each source." (Chandra observations); "Then, we converted the count rates to the intrinsic X-ray luminosities, assuming typical X-ray spectra of AGNs." (Chandra observations)
   - software: CIAO, CALDB, merge_obs, srcflux, XSPEC, XCLUMPY
   - parameter: Source radius 1.5 arcsec; background annulus 2–4 arcsec; mask obvious X-ray point sources  
  > "we employed circular regions with radii of 1.5$''$ and annuli with inner and outer radii of 2$''$ and 4$''$, respectively, after masking obvious X-ray point sources." (Chandra observations)
   - parameter: Model 1: log NHLOS=21, 23, 24.5; Γ=1.9; Ecut=370 keV; scattered fraction 0.01  
  > "The line-of-sight hydrogen column density is tested at $\log N_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{LOS}}=21,\,23,\,24.5$." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds); "The photon index and cutoff energy are fixed at $\Gamma=1.9$ and $E_{\mathrm{cut}}=370\,\mathrm{keV}$" (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds); "We fix the scattered fraction (`const`) to 0.01" (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
   - parameter: Torus angular width 20°; inclination 30° at log NHLOS=21, 70° at 23 and 24.5  
  > "We fixed the torus angular width at $\sigma=20\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds); "The inclination angle is fixed at $i=30\ensuremath{^\circ}$ when $\log N_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{LOS}}/\mathrm{cm}^{-2}=21$, and $i=70\ensuremath{^\circ}$ when $\log N_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{LOS}}/\mathrm{cm}^{-2}=23$ and 24.5." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
   - parameter: Model 2: log NHLOS/cm−2=22; Γ=1.9; Ecut=370 keV  
  > "The line-of-sight hydrogen column density, photon index, and cutoff energy are fixed to $\log N_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{LOS}}/\mathrm{cm}^{-2}=22$, $\Gamma=1.9$, and $E_{\mathrm{cut}}=370\,\mathrm{keV}$, respectively." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
   - assumption: Model 1 contains direct nuclear, torus reflection, and scattered components  
  > "Hence, in this study, we only consider a direct component, a reflection component from a torus, and a scattered component." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Also estimate upperbounds with a simpler absorbed power-law model  
  > "X-ray spectra of distant AGNs are often analyzed with simpler models, such as an absorbed power law model. Accordingly, we also estimate the X-ray luminosity upperbounds with a simple model" (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
10. **Assess AGN obscuration**: Compare SED bolometric AGN luminosities against obscuration-dependent X-ray upperbounds  
  > "plots the X-ray luminosity upperbounds with various assumptions on line-of-sight obscuration, against bolometric AGN luminosity derived by `CIGALE`." (Comparison with X-ray luminosity upperbounds)
   - software: CIGALE
   - assumption: Empirical local-AGN Lbol/L2–10 keV=20  
  > "the empirical bolometric-to-X-ray luminosity relation of local AGNs ($L_{\mathrm{bol}}/L_{\text{2--10 keV}}=20$; (cite: 2007MNRAS.381.1235V))" (Comparison with X-ray luminosity upperbounds)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consider X-ray-weak AGNs as an alternative allowing less obscured cases  
  > "If this is applicable in our sample, the ALCS-nonXAGNs can be consistent with their X-ray luminosity upperbounds even in the less obscured cases" (Comparison with X-ray luminosity upperbounds)
11. **Estimate AGN luminosity density**: Multiply summed best-fit AGN/infrared luminosity ratios by the integrated U/LIRG infrared luminosity density  
  > "These values are calculated by comparing the summed best-fit AGN luminosities and infrared luminosities of the U/LIRGs in the tier-1 sample in each redshift bin." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density); "Then, we apply the luminosity ratio to the infrared luminosity density of high-redshift U/LIRGs and obtain the AGN luminosity density." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)
   - parameter: Redshift bins z=1–2, 2–3, 3–4  
  > "First, we estimate the average $L_{\mathrm{AGN}}$-to-$L_{\mathrm{IR}}$ ratio in redshift bins of $z=1\text{--}2$, $z=2\text{--}3$, and $z=3\text{--}4$." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)
   - parameter: Integrate the literature infrared luminosity function over 10^11 L⊙<LIR<10^13 L⊙  
  > "integrating the infrared luminosity function derived by (cite: 2023arXiv230301658F) over $10^{11}L_{\odot}<L_{\mathrm{IR}}<10^{13}L_{\odot}$." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)
   - assumption: Infrared luminosity excludes the AGN component; include previously analyzed ALCS-XAGNs  
  > "the infrared luminosity mentioned in this subsection refers to the one that does not contain the AGN component" (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density); "For the ALCS-XAGNs, we employ the values derived by the SED analysis performed by (cite: 2023ApJ...945..121U)." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with luminosity density estimated by X-ray surveys below 10 keV  
  > "We also plot the AGN luminosity density estimated by X-ray surveys below 10 keV (cite: 2014ApJ...786..104U)." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)

## Qualitative findings

- ALCS sources mostly lie on the star-forming main sequence; a smaller fraction are starbursts  
  > "We confirm that the majority of the ALCS sources lie on the star-forming main sequence, while a smaller fraction shows intense starburst activities." (Summary)
- ALCS has higher IRX than optical/UV-selected galaxies  
  > "We see that the ALCS sample has higher IRXs than the conventional relationship, implying that these galaxies are more deeply enshrouded by dust than optical- or UV-selected galaxies." (IRX versus stellar mass)
- Higher stellar masses weakly correlate with flatter attenuation curves  
  > "A weak correlation is noticed between the steepness of attenuation curve and stellar mass, i.e., galaxies with high stellar masses tend to have flatter attenuation curves." (Dust Attenuation Law)
- ALCS spans wider dust temperatures and lower dust masses than conventional SMG samples  
  > "We confirm that the ALCS sample exhibits a wider range in dust temperatures than the conventional SMG samples (ALESS, and AS2UDS) in the same redshift range." (Summary); "We also confirm that the ALCS sample shows a large scatter in a lower dust mass range than the conventional SMG samples after lensing correction." (Summary)
- No obvious evolution in mean ALCS dust mass  
  > "there is no obvious evolution in the mean dust mass of the ALCS sample" (Dust mass)
- Possible AGN luminosity-density excess at z=2–3 relative to X-ray surveys  
  > "We confirm a possible excess at $z=2$–3." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density); "We also plot the AGN luminosity density estimated by X-ray surveys below 10 keV (cite: 2014ApJ...786..104U)." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use lensing to detect faint millimeter galaxies in fields covered by major HST treasury programs  
  > "in order to detect faint sources by utilizing the lensing effect." (Introduction); "The ALMA observations were designed to target areas which were covered by the major HST treasury programs" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Energy-balanced galaxy/AGN SED modeling; extend dust-temperature coverage to avoid biased AGN contributions or infrared luminosities  
  > "`CIGALE` assumes energy balance between UV/optical absorption and infrared re-emission, which enables us to self-consistently model the SED of each galaxy." (SED modeling with `CIGALE`); "This limitation in the higher temperature regimes may result in an overestimation of AGN contribution" (Dust emission from host galaxy); "or an underestimation of infrared luminosity from star-formation activity." (Dust emission from host galaxy)
- Alternative: Exclude tier-7/8 from SED analysis because photometry is insufficient  
  > "The tier-7/8 samples are not analyzed in this paper because it is challenging to derive the physical properties from their limited photometric data." (SED modeling with `CIGALE`)
- Alternative: Exclude AGN components for tier-2/3/4/5/6 because limited photometry cannot identify them reliably  
  > "For the tier-2/3/4/5/6 samples, the AGN component is always excluded, because it is challenging to identify the presence of an AGN from the limited photometry of those samples." (AGN emission)
- Alternative: Reject the BIC-selected AGN interpretation of R0032-ID57  
  > "However, the other multiple images (R0032-ID32/53/55/58) are not classified as AGNs, and no obvious signs of AGN are found in the SED of R0032-ID57." (Overall properties of ALCS sample); "Therefore, we do not consider R0032-ID57 as a host of an AGN." (Overall properties of ALCS sample)
- Alternative: Use a simpler absorbed power-law X-ray model as an alternative  
  > "X-ray spectra of distant AGNs are often analyzed with simpler models, such as an absorbed power law model. Accordingly, we also estimate the X-ray luminosity upperbounds with a simple model" (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
- Alternative: Omit photoionized plasma, accretion-disk reflection, and star-formation X-ray emission from the adopted model  
  > "Since the emission from photoionized plasma is not dominant above rest-frame 2 keV, we exclude this component from our model." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds); "We disregard the reflection component from the accretion disk, whose interpretation is under debate" (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds); "we do not consider the X-ray emission from star-formation activity, which is much fainter than the AGN component above rest-frame 2 keV." (Detailed Description of X-ray Analysis and X-ray Luminosity Upperbounds)
- Alternative: X-ray weakness can allow less obscured interpretations of the AGN candidates  
  > "some AGNs in local ULIRGs show “X-ray weak” features" (Comparison with X-ray luminosity upperbounds); "If this is applicable in our sample, the ALCS-nonXAGNs can be consistent with their X-ray luminosity upperbounds even in the less obscured cases" (Comparison with X-ray luminosity upperbounds)
- Alternative: Test Charlot–Fall attenuation in CIGALE; stellar masses then agree with MAGPHYS  
  > "In `CIGALE`, we can also choose the (cite: 2000ApJ...539..718C) model for the dust attenuation model." (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`); "In this case, we confirm good agreement between stellar mass estimated with `CIGALE` and that with `MAGPHYS`" (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`)
- How they decided it was enough: SED fit quality χ²/dof<5; require ΔBIC>10 for an AGN component  
  > "We successfully reproduce the SEDs of the ALCS tier-1/2/3/4/5/6 samples with a fit quality of $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}<5$." (Results); "If the addition of an AGN component reduces BIC by more than 10" (AGN emission)
- Limitation (authors): Lens-model and magnification uncertainties are ignored  
  > "Here we note that the uncertainties in the lens models and magnification factors are not considered in this paper." (Data quality, sample selection, and magnification)
- Limitation (authors): Photometric-redshift uncertainties are ignored in the main analysis  
  > "The uncertainties of the photometric redshifts are not considered in the main part of this paper." (SED modeling with `CIGALE`)
- Limitation (authors): Model uncertainties make reported physical-property errors underestimated  
  > "This indicates that the reported errors in the physical properties are fairly underestimated, considering the uncertainties in the SED models." (Comparison between `CIGALE` and `MAGPHYS`)
- Limitation (authors): Herschel-selected comparison subsamples may be biased  
  > "One should be aware that these selections can cause biases and the subsamples might not reflect the averaged properties of the whole samples." (Dust temperature)
- Limitation (authors): M0416-ID156 high temperature is uncertain because its far-infrared SED is poorly fitted  
  > "the far-infrared SED of M0416-ID156 is not well-fitted by the EThemis model." (Dust temperature); "This may be attributed to the oversimplification of the model, and can cause large uncertainty in dust temperature and dust luminosity." (Dust temperature)
- Limitation (authors): A2537-ID24/66 high temperatures may reflect residual blending  
  > "Nevertheless, there remains a possibility that their high dust temperatures are actually caused by the remaining blending effect of low-redshift sources." (Dust temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Best-fit SEDs show substantial ALMA or Herschel discrepancies for some sources  
  > "the best-fit SEDs of A383-ID50 and M0416-ID156 look inconsistent with the observed ALMA fluxes" (Detailed description of SED modeling); "the best-fit SED of ACT0102-ID50 looks inconsistent with the photometric data of Herschel" (Detailed description of SED modeling)
- Limitation (authors): AGN identifications and luminosity-density conclusions depend strongly on SED-model assumptions  
  > "the AGN detection by SED analysis should have large uncertainties, and the results should highly depend on the model assumptions." (Cosmological evolution of AGN luminosity density)
- Limitation (authors): No Chandra upperbounds for RXC0032/RXC0600; BCGs excluded because cluster diffuse emission complicates extraction  
  > "Since there are no Chandra observations in RXC0032 and RXC0600, the X-ray luminosity upperbounds in those fields are not given." (Chandra observations); "We excluded the brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) from the catalog because of the difficulty in separating the X-ray point sources from the diffuse X-ray emission of the galaxy clusters." (Chandra observations)
- Limitation (authors): Bolometric-to-X-ray conversion has large scatter and possible Eddington-ratio dependence  
  > "this relationship has a large scatter ($L_{\mathrm{bol}}/L_{\mathrm{2\text{--}10\,keV}}=6\text{--}400$) and shows potential strong dependence on the Eddington ratio" (Comparison with X-ray luminosity upperbounds)
