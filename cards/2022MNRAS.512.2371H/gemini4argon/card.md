## Analysis recipe

1. **Create CO(3-2) and continuum intensity (0th moment) maps and measure total CO fluxes**: Apply a 3sigma cut in each channel of the naturally weighted CO(3-2) cube and integrate across the total line width; apply a 3sigma cut to the continuum map.  
  > "To make the CO(3-2) intensity maps we performed a 3$\sigma$ cut in each channel of the data cube and then integrated the remaining flux across the total width of the emission line. For the continuum 0th moment maps we made a 3$\sigma$ cut on the continuum map." (CO(3-2) and Continuum Emission)
   - parameter: 3sigma threshold per channel for CO(3-2) and 3sigma cut on continuum map  
  > "To make the CO(3-2) intensity maps we performed a 3$\sigma$ cut in each channel of the data cube and then integrated the remaining flux across the total width of the emission line." (CO(3-2) and Continuum Emission)
   - assumption: Rest ~0.9 mm continuum arises from Rayleigh-Jeans dust emission and targets are star-formation dominated.  
  > "In this analysis we assume that the main source of the continuum emission, observed at a rest wavelength $\sim$ 0.9 mm, arises from the Rayleigh-Jeans tail of the dust emission." (CO(3-2) and Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare CO(3-2) line widths with previous single-dish measurements in M14.  
  > "Line widths are in agreement with previous measurements using a single dish observation in M14." (CO(3-2) and Continuum Emission)
2. **Compute CO(3-2) line luminosities and molecular gas masses**: Convert integrated CO(3-2) flux to line luminosity L'_CO(3-2) using Solomon et al. (1997) formula, then multiply by metallicity-dependent conversion factor alpha_CO(3-2)(Z) = alpha_0 * chi(Z) * R_1-3.  
  > "Firstly, we must convert the CO(3-2) flux density from our intensity maps to the integrated line luminosity using the following equation from (cite: solommon_molecular_1997)," (Molecular Gas Mass); "To account for metallicity we adopt a metallicity dependent conversion factor described in (cite: genzel_metallicity_2012) and (cite: genzel_combined_2015):" (Molecular Gas Mass)
   - parameter: alpha_0 = alpha_MW = 4.36 +/- 0.9 M_sun / (K km s^-1 pc^2), R_1-3 = 1.9, chi(Z) = 10^(-1.27 * (12 + log(O/H) - 8.67))  
  > "We assume $\alpha_{\rm 0}$ equals the Milky Way conversion factor $\alpha_{\rm MW}$ = 4.36 $\pm$ 0.9 M$_{\odot}$ / (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$), including the correction for Helium, and R$_{1-3}$ = 1.9 ((cite: lamperti_co32co10_2020)), which is a typical value seen in star forming galaxies." (Molecular Gas Mass); "$\chi (Z) = 10^{-1.27 \times (\rm{12 + log(O/H)} - 8.67)}$" (Molecular Gas Mass)
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7; Chabrier (2003) IMF; chi(Z) = 1 for SWIRE7.  
  > "When required, values of H$_{0}$ = 70 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{m}$ = 0.3 and $\Omega_{\Lambda}$ = 0.7 were used in this paper. When required the initial mass function (IMF) was assumed to be a (cite: chabrier_galactic_2003) form." (Introduction); "For SWIRE7 we set $\chi$(Z) = 1 as it had no \[NII\] 6583 Å detection in PS19." (Molecular Gas Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with M14 dust-to-gas ratio alpha_CO estimates and evaluate effect of using local ULIRG alpha_CO = 0.8 or geometric-mean chi(Z).  
  > "If we chose the geometric mean, as in (cite: genzel_combined_2015), then our $\alpha_{\rm{CO (3-2)}}(Z)$ would be $\sim$ 15% higher on average, with a corresponding increase in M$_{\rm mol}$." (Molecular Gas Mass); "Finally, M14 estimated the M$_{\rm mol}$ using the dust to gas mass ratio technique (see M14 and references therein) and inferred an $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ consistent with local star forming galaxies for our sample (with the exception of FLS02), further corroborating our choice." (Molecular Gas Mass)
3. **Calculate star formation surface densities, molecular gas surface densities, SFE, and depletion times**: Measure star-forming area A_cont from pixels with SNR > 3sigma in continuum map, mask CO(3-2) cube to A_cont and integrate over velocity channels with no threshold, then compute Sigma_mol = M_mol(masked)/A_cont, Sigma_SFR = SFR_IR/A_cont, and tau_dep = 1/SFE.  
  > "we measured this area directly from the moment maps by multiplying the area of each pixel by the number of pixels with SNR" (Star Formation Efficiency); "To do this we placed a mask on the emission line cube that ensured only CO(3-2) flux that arises within A$_{\rm cont}$ is extracted." (Star Formation Efficiency); "The surface density for the molecular gas was then calculated using $\Sigma_{\rm mol}$ = M$_{\rm mol}$ / A$_{\rm cont}$ and the SFR surface density was found via $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ = SFR$_{\rm IR}$ / A$_{\rm cont}$, where A$_{\rm cont}$ is the area of the star forming region as defined at the start of this section." (Star Formation Efficiency)
   - parameter: 3sigma continuum contour mask A_cont; 300 km s^-1 assumed linewidth for SWIRE7 3sigma upper limit  
  > "We assumed a linewidth of 300 km s$^{-1}$ and multiplied the 3$\sigma$/beam by the square root of the number of beams within A$_{\rm cont}$ of SWIRE7 to get the upper limit." (Star Formation Efficiency)
   - assumption: SFR_IR approximates total SFR; for FLS02-S A_cont equals the CO(3-2) emitting region; for FLS02 system SFR_IR is apportioned between N and S by relative continuum flux; all CDFS1 SFR_IR arises from CDFS1-W.  
  > "For FLS02-S there is a marginal ($\approx$ 4$\sigma$) unresolved continuum detection so we assumed A$_{\rm cont}$ is equal in size to the CO(3-2) emitting region." (Star Formation Efficiency); "Since CDFS1-E is not observed in either continuum or CO(3-2), it is not included in this analysis, and we assume that all the SFR$_{\rm IR}$ arises from CDFS1-W." (Star Formation Efficiency); "For FLS02 we measure the total continuum flux from both sources and find each galaxy’s relative contribution to the total flux. This apportions the SFR$_{\rm IR}$ = 380 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ for the system into 353 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ for FLS02-N and 27 M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ for FLS02-S." (Star Formation Efficiency)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Test sensitivity of average tau_dep to using 3sigma/5sigma CO area cuts or total unmasked CO flux within A_cont (changes average tau_dep by < 40 Myr).  
  > "Depending on how we select this larger region, e.g. a 3$\sigma$ or 5$\sigma$ cut on the CO(3-2) maps (which is equivalent to choosing a lower $\sigma$ threshold on the continuum maps), the average $\tau_{\rm dep}$ of our sample only increases by at most 40 Myr and therefore our conclusions remain unchanged." (Star Formation Efficiency); "As a test for this we assumed that the total integrated galaxy flux (see column 3 in Table \[tab:co\]) arises from within A$_{\rm cont}$ and found that the increase in $\Sigma_{\rm mol}$ changes the average $\tau_{\rm dep}$ by less than 40 Myr, demonstrating the robustness of our findings." (Star Formation Efficiency)
4. **Fit 3D tilted-ring kinematic models to the CO(3-2) data cubes**: Use 3DBAROLO SEARCH algorithm to mask emission and fit a 3D tilted-ring thin-disk model convolved with the synthesized beam to derive v_rot and sigma_gas.  
  > "To fit a kinematic model to the CO(3-2) emission line we used the $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO code, which fits a 3D tilted ring model to emission line data cubes ((cite: di_teodoro_3d_2015))." (Kinematics); "We used the SEARCH algorithm within $^{\rm 3D}$BAROLO to mask the galaxy from the background." (Kinematics)
   - software: 3DBAROLO
   - parameter: SEARCH 5sigma initial cut with 3sigma adjoining threshold; ring width 0.3'' - 0.4''; centre fixed to continuum peak; v_rad = 0; v_sys = 0 at centre; PA and inclination fixed after initial guess; v_rot and sigma_gas free  
  > "The algorithm is a source finder that works by first making a 5$\sigma$ cut across all channels and then assumes that all 3$\sigma$ emission in adjoining channels/pixels are part of the same source ((cite: whitling_duchamp_2012))." (Kinematics); "We chose the peak of the continuum as the centre point of each galaxy, set the radial velocity to zero, assumed a thin disk for the scale height, fixed the systematic velocity to zero at the centre of the emission and left the gas surface density to be estimated by the algorithm." (Kinematics); "For our ring size we choose 0.3$^{\prime \prime}$ - 0.4$^{\prime \prime}$ to maximize the number of rings when fitting our source, while also ensuring an adequate number of spaxels within each ring to enable an adequate fit." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Thin disk scale height and zero radial velocity.  
  > "We chose the peak of the continuum as the centre point of each galaxy, set the radial velocity to zero, assumed a thin disk for the scale height, fixed the systematic velocity to zero at the centre of the emission and left the gas surface density to be estimated by the algorithm." (Kinematics)
   - fitting: 3D tilted-ring fitting in 3DBAROLO with PA and inclination fixed and v_rot and sigma_gas free.  
  > "We initially let the code make initial guesses for the PA and inclination, and then left them fixed at that value. The v$_{\rm rot}$ and $\sigma_{\rm gas}$ were left as free parameters for the code to fit." (Kinematics)
   - validation: Compare output model 0th, 1st, and 2nd moment maps and PV diagrams to observed maps and manually adjust PA/inclination if needed.  
  > "To determine the best fit model we compared the output model’s 0th, 1st and 2nd moment maps to the observed 0th, 1st and 2nd moment maps." (Kinematics)
5. **Compare sample M_mol, SFE, and L_IR with intermediate-z cold LIRGs and warm ULIRGs**: Combine sample with Combes et al. (2011) warm ULIRGs and Lee et al. (2017) cold LIRGs and compute Pearson correlation between log(SFE) and log(L_IR).  
  > "Molecular gas masses and SFEs have been determined in both the cold LIRG and warm ULIRG samples ((cite: combes_galaxy_2011), (cite: lee_fine_2017)), and these results along with our sample are plotted in Figure 7." (Molecular Gas and SFR at 0.2 < z < 0.6); "There is a statistically significant strong positive correlation for log(SFE) - log(L$_{\rm IR}$) of the three samples, with a Pearson’s $\rho$ = 0.91 (p-value < 0.001)." (Molecular Gas and SFR at 0.2 < z < 0.6)
   - fitting: Pearson correlation test on log(SFE) vs log(L_IR).

## Qualitative findings

- Transitioning intermediate-z U/LIRGs fall between the loci of main sequence galaxies and starbursts on the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation.  
  > "We detect CO(3-2) emission in four targets and these galaxies fall between the loci of regular star forming galaxies and starbursts on the Kennicutt-Schmidtt relation." (Abstract)
- Intermediate-z cold LIRGs, transitioning U/LIRGs, and warm ULIRGs have similar molecular gas masses, and the increase in L_IR (and SFR) across them is driven by increasing star formation efficiency.  
  > "All three samples have similar M$_{\rm mol}$ so the amount of molecular gas available to form stars is not causing the differing L$_{\rm IR}$. When comparing SFE ($\tau_{\rm dep}$) of the three samples it is likely the increase in L$_{\rm IR}$ is being driven by the SFE of the molecular gas." (Molecular Gas and SFR at 0.2 < z < 0.6)
- All four CO(3-2)-detected targets show signatures of rotating disks and are either in interacting pairs (CDFS1-W, FLS02-N/S) or show morphological/kinematic disturbances (SWIRE5).  
  > "All four galaxies for which we detected a CO(3-2) emission line show signatures of rotating disks. Two of these show ordered rotation (CDFS1-W and SWIRE5) that could be adequately fit with a model rotating gas disk using $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo." (Conclusion); "As both FLS02 and CDFS1 are interacting systems and SWIRE5 shows a disturbance that deviates from the disk model, which may be a hidden minor merger or a disk instability, it seems that morphology and kinematics play a significant role in elevating the SFE of a galaxy above the MS." (Conclusion)
- SWIRE7 has a strong (~20sigma) continuum detection but no CO(3-2) detection, suggesting either a CO-dark ULIRG, metal-poor ISM with free-free continuum contribution, or an incorrect redshift.  
  > "SWIRE7 poses more of a challenge as it has a strong ($\approx$ 20$\sigma$) detection in the continuum despite having no CO(3-2) detection." (CDFS1-E & SWIRE7 Non-Detections); "If SWIRE7 is a true CO-dark ULIRG, and not an incorrect redshift, then it poses a challenge to the understanding of the ISM within dusty star forming galaxies galaxies, so followup observations are required." (CDFS1-E & SWIRE7 Non-Detections)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets straddle the transition region between MS LIRGs and starbursting ULIRGs at 0.28 < z < 0.44 and already have Herschel IR, single-dish CO, and optical IFS data; spatially resolved CO(3-2) and ~0.9 mm continuum map the star-forming molecular clouds and obscured star formation.  
  > "In addition, the sample straddles the region between the MS LIRGs and starbursting ULIRGs at this epoch, so it is ideal for investigating the transition between MS and starburst galaxies and its link to interaction stage, and also enabling a better insight into the U/LIRG population at an epoch when the universe is experiencing a decrease in SFRD." (The Sample); "The CO(3-2) transition is 33 K above the ground state and has a critical density of $>$ 10$^{4}$ cm$^{-3}$ at temperatures $<$ 20 K, so it traces the molecular clouds within the ISM from which stars are formed (e.g. (cite: rigby_chimps_2016)), hence these observations allow us to explore the relationship between molecular gas and ongoing star formation." (CO(3-2) and Continuum Emission)
- Why this method: Metallicity-dependent alpha_CO(Z) is chosen because these intermediate-z U/LIRGs have softer radiation fields, different ISM conditions, and earlier interaction stages than local ULIRGs; masking to A_cont ensures maximum spatial overlap between molecular gas and obscured star formation.  
  > "Our sample of U/LIRGs have different ISM conditions compared to those of local ULIRGs (as discussed in M14) and are in an earlier stage of interaction (PS19). Therefore the metallicity dependent $\alpha_{\rm{CO (3-2)}}$(Z) is the most appropriate conversion factor for our sample." (Molecular Gas Mass); "Our motivation for using the A$_{\rm cont}$, i.e. the 3$\sigma$ cut on the continuum, to calculate the surface densities, is to ensure maximum overlap between star forming regions and molecular gas." (Star Formation Efficiency)
- Alternative: Using local ULIRG conversion factor alpha_CO = 0.8 M_sun / (K km s^-1 pc^2) or the geometric mean of the two Genzel et al. (2015) metallicity scaling functions.  
  > "If we chose the geometric mean, as in (cite: genzel_combined_2015), then our $\alpha_{\rm{CO (3-2)}}(Z)$ would be $\sim$ 15% higher on average, with a corresponding increase in M$_{\rm mol}$." (Molecular Gas Mass); "If we were to use the low-z ULIRG value ($\approx$ 0.8 M$_{\odot}$ / (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$) for CO(1-0), (cite: downes_rotating_1998)) our $\tau_{\rm dep}$ would be between 2 - 84 Myr (21 - 84 Myr if we remove SWIRE7)." (Molecular Gas and SFR at 0.2 < z < 0.6)
- Alternative: Using larger areas based on 3sigma or 5sigma CO(3-2) cuts or using total unmasked CO flux within A_cont when computing surface densities and depletion times.  
  > "Depending on how we select this larger region, e.g. a 3$\sigma$ or 5$\sigma$ cut on the CO(3-2) maps (which is equivalent to choosing a lower $\sigma$ threshold on the continuum maps), the average $\tau_{\rm dep}$ of our sample only increases by at most 40 Myr and therefore our conclusions remain unchanged." (Star Formation Efficiency); "As a test for this we assumed that the total integrated galaxy flux (see column 3 in Table \[tab:co\]) arises from within A$_{\rm cont}$ and found that the increase in $\Sigma_{\rm mol}$ changes the average $\tau_{\rm dep}$ by less than 40 Myr, demonstrating the robustness of our findings." (Star Formation Efficiency)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): AGN contribution to the IR and sub-mm continuum cannot be completely ruled out.  
  > "It is difficult to rule out AGN contribution, but as our sample do not show broad components in the H$\alpha$ emission line (PS19) we assume they are star formation dominated." (CO(3-2) and Continuum Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Herschel/SPIRE resolution (~20 arcsec) cannot resolve individual galaxies in the interacting pairs FLS02 (separated by ~4 arcsec) and CDFS1 (~7 arcsec), so L_IR is measured for the combined systems.  
  > "We are, therefore, constrained by the resolution afforded by the Herschel$/$SPIRE bands, which is $\approx$ 20 arcseconds ((cite: griffin_herschel-spire_2010)). Hence, the L$_{\rm IR}$ reported for the two interacting systems, FLS02 (centres separated by $\approx$ 4$^{\prime \prime}$) and CDFS1 (separated by $\approx$ 7$^{\prime \prime}$), corresponds to the entire system and, cannot be calculated for each of the two nuclei independently." (Star Formation Efficiency)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size prevents drawing strong conclusions on whether the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation is bimodal or continuous.  
  > "As our sample is small we cannot draw any strong conclusions, and expanding the available samples of transitioning objects with future observations is necessary to fully explore this open issue." (Molecular Gas and SFR at 0.2 < z < 0.6)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial resolution of the NOEMA observations (~3.5 - 5.7 kpc) is insufficient to resolve velocity dispersion profiles in FLS02 or disentangle clumpy disk instabilities from a hidden minor merger in SWIRE5.  
  > "Higher spatial resolution observations are needed to properly probe the velocity structure and determine what is triggering the high rates of SFR." (FLS02); "As the major axis of the synthesized beam is $\approx$ 3.5 kpc in width we require high spatial resolution observations to disentangle the possible scenarios." (SWIRE5)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one of the four systems (CDFS1) was observed with ALMA; the other three systems were observed with NOEMA at ~3x coarser angular resolution (~0.8''-1.0'' vs 0.31''), which may systematically affect the measured 3sigma continuum areas A_cont and surface densities.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The strong log(SFE) vs log(L_IR) correlation (rho = 0.91) across the combined literature samples is partly driven by the different alpha_CO assumptions adopted for the different sub-samples (0.8 for Combes et al. warm ULIRGs vs 2.5-6.3 for this work and 4.36 for Lee et al. cold LIRGs).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Note a typo in Section 3.4 where the text states 'CDFS1-W and SWIRE7 show ordered rotation and were fit with a model thin disk' whereas Table 3, Figure 5, and the Conclusion show the fitted target is SWIRE5 (SWIRE7 is undetected in CO).
