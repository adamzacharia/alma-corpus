## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: Determine the high-mass IMF from O and B stellar ratios and test variations across arms, spurs, and inter-arm regions.  
  > "This paper presents a new method to determine the slope $\alpha$ of the high mass end of the IMF using observations of individual star-forming complexes (SFCs)." (Introduction); "We investigate whether $\alpha$ varies with regions, i.e., spiral arms, spurs, and inter-arm regions, as the mechanisms leading to star formation in these regions are different." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract and measure star-forming complexes**: Reduce UVIT images, subtract backgrounds, match the MUSE footprint and UV resolution, extract SFCs, mask foreground sources, measure counts, and match detections across bands.  
  > "We reduced the raw UVIT data using the graphical user interface, CCDLAB (cite: 2017PASP..129k5002P)." (Source extraction and classification); "We extracted SFCs in all three images using the command line program Source Extractor (SExtractor; (cite: 1996A&AS..117..393B))." (Source extraction and classification); "We determined the total counts from the extracted SFCs using the Photutils module (cite: 2018AJ....156..123A)." (Source extraction and classification)
   - software: CCDLAB, IRAF, Source Extractor, TOPCAT, Photutils
   - parameter: detection threshold five times global background noise  
  > "We set the detection threshold to five times the estimated global background noise for confident detection." (Source extraction and classification)
   - parameter: Halpha, Hbeta, and JWST maps convolved to UVIT resolution; 1.2 arcsec  
  > "We convolved the H$\alpha$ map to the UV resolution of 1.2$^{\prime\prime}$." (Source extraction and classification); "We also convolved the H$\beta$ and JWST map to the UVIT resolution." (Source extraction and classification)
   - parameter: IMF sample detected in all three bands; largest SFC area across the three bands  
  > "taking SFCs that are detected in all three bands and taking the same area for all SFCs (the largest area of SFCs of the three bands) for IMF estimation" (Distribution of SFCs in NGC 628 in Halpha, FUV and NUV emission.)
   - assumption: UV resolution 1.2 arcsec from approximately 5–6 field stars  
  > "Using around 5 to 6 stars in the FUV and NUV image fields, we found that the mean PSF of the field stars is around 1.2$^{\prime\prime}$." (Source extraction and classification)
   - assumption: distance 9.84 Mpc  
  > "and is at a distance of 9.84 Mpc (cite: 10.1093/mnras/staa3668)." (NGC 628 and Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Foreground masking using Gaia parallax and proper motion; matched detections and common areas for IMF estimation.  
  > "Then, we masked the foreground sources (with high parallax and high proper motion) by comparing the extracted SFCs with the bright sources in the Gaia catalog using TOPCAT (cite: 2005ASPC..347...29T)." (Source extraction and classification); "taking SFCs that are detected in all three bands and taking the same area for all SFCs (the largest area of SFCs of the three bands) for IMF estimation" (Distribution of SFCs in NGC 628 in Halpha, FUV and NUV emission.)
2. **Correct extinction and compare luminosities**: Compare Balmer decrement, Beta slope, and JWST 21 micrometre corrections; mainly adopt Balmer decrement; fit corrected UV and Halpha luminosities.  
  > "we corrected SFC luminosities for internal extinction using the Balmer decrement, Beta slope, and JWST 21$\mu m$ methods to see which method gives a better correlation for H$\alpha$ and UV luminosities." (Internal extinction correction); "Hence, we mainly used the Balmer decrement method ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.21 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.25) for the extinction correction for SFCs with H$\alpha$ luminosity;" (Internal extinction correction)
   - parameter: E(B−V)=1.97 log10[(Halpha/Hbeta)obs/2.86]  
  > "E(B-V)=1.97log_{10}[\frac{(\frac{H\alpha}{H\beta})_{obs}}{2.86}]" (Internal extinction correction)
   - parameter: AHalpha=(3.33 ± 0.80)E(B−V); AFUV=3.6AHalpha; ANUV=0.8AFUV  
  > "$A_{H\alpha}=(3.33 \pm 0.80) \times E(B-V)$." (Internal extinction correction); "$A_{FUV}=3.6A_{H\alpha}$" (Internal extinction correction); "$A_{NUV}=0.8A_{FUV}$" (Internal extinction correction)
   - parameter: JWST correction: Lcorr=Lobs+cL(24 micrometres); c=3.89, 2.26, 0.0024 for FUV, NUV, Halpha; L(24)=4.54L(21)  
  > "$L(\lambda)_{corr}=L(\lambda)_{obs}+cL(24 \mu m)$." (Internal extinction correction); "The parameter c is 3.89, 2.26, and 0.0024 when we correct the FUV, NUV, and H$\alpha$ luminosities, respectively." (Internal extinction correction); "We find that the ratio is approximately equal to 4.54. We multiplied this value with $L(21 \mu m)$ to obtain the $L(24 \mu m)$." (Internal extinction correction)
   - assumption: Case B; intrinsic Halpha/Hbeta=2.86; temperature 10^4 K; electron density 10^2 cm^-3  
  > "$(\frac{H\alpha}{H\beta})_{int}=2.86$, at the temperature $10^{4}$ K and an electron density $n_{e}=10^{2}cm^{-3}$ for Case B recombination (cite: 1989agna.book.....O)." (Internal extinction correction)
   - fitting: least square fit for Balmer-corrected UV and Halpha luminosities  
  > "Hence, we obtained the least square fit for UV (FUV and NUV) and H$\alpha$ luminosities, which is corrected for extinction using the Balmer decrement method." (UV and Halpha luminosity correlation)
   - validation: Compare luminosity-correlation scatter; Beta slope scatters more; JWST coverage limits the sample.  
  > "The SFC luminosities corrected with the Beta slope method show more scatter ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.38 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.37), making it less preferable." (Internal extinction correction); "Although the JWST 21$\mu m$ method showed a tight H$\alpha$ and UV luminosity correlation ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.17 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.17), due to its limited field of view, very few SFCs had 21$\mu m$ emission." (Internal extinction correction)
3. **Estimate SFRs and stellar masses**: Convert corrected luminosities to SFRs, divide by SFC areas, and interpolate UV color and FUV magnitude on starburst99 SSP predictions for stellar mass.  
  > "We calculated the SFR density $\Sigma(SFR)$ by dividing the SFRs by their respective areas." (Characterization of the SFCs); "We plotted the observed extinction-corrected UV color and FUV magnitudes of the SFCs on the theoretically predicted values from Starburst99. We then interpolated the values for each SFC to find the mass." (Characterization of the SFCs)
   - software: starburst99
   - parameter: SFRFUV=0.68 × 10^-28 L(FUV)corr; SFRHalpha=5.3 × 10^-42 L(Halpha)corr  
  > "$SFR_{FUV}=0.68 \times 10^{-28} \times L(FUV)_{corr}$." (Characterization of the SFCs); "$SFR_{H\alpha}=5.3 \times 10^{-42} \times L(H\alpha)_{corr}$." (Characterization of the SFCs)
   - parameter: model age range 1–200 Myrs  
  > "We evolved the model from 1 Myrs to 200 Myrs, as UV can trace up to 200 Myrs." (Characterization of the SFCs)
   - assumption: Padova tracks with asymptotic giant branch stars; Salpeter IMF; 0.1–100 Msun; solar metallicity Z=0.02  
  > "We ran starburst99 assuming Padova tracks with asymptotic giant branch stars and standard Salpeter IMF (cite: 1955ApJ...121..161S) with the lower cutoff mass set to $m_{l} = 0.1M_{\odot}$ and the upper cutoff mass to be $m_{u} = 100M_{\odot}$." (Characterization of the SFCs); "We adopted a solar metallicity (Z=0.02; (cite: 1999ApJS..123....3L))," (Characterization of the SFCs)
   - fitting: interpolation on starburst99 predicted color and magnitude values  
  > "We then interpolated the values for each SFC to find the mass." (Characterization of the SFCs)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure molecular gas and classify environments**: Use the ALMA CO(J=2-1) map to estimate H2 masses and identify spur-associated SFCs after NUV arm/interarm classification.  
  > "We estimated the molecular hydrogen mass using the ALMA CO(J=2-1) map." (Characterization of the SFCs); "Hence, we first classified the SFCs as arms and interarm sources based on their location in the NUV image" (Source extraction and classification); "Then, we again checked which SFCs lie in and around the spur regions of the ALMA observations" (Source extraction and classification)
   - parameter: alphaCO=4.35 Msun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1; R21=0.62  
  > "We converted the CO flux to $H_{2}$ mass using the conversion factor $\alpha_{CO(}=4.35M_{\odot} pc^{-2}(Kkms^{-1})^{-1}$ and $R_{21}=0.62$ (cite: 2013ApJ...777....5S; Kreckel_2018)." (Characterization of the SFCs)
   - assumption: single conversion factor for all SFCs  
  > "The reason for the scatter in the correlation could be that a single conversion factor was considered to obtain the molecular hydrogen mass for all the SFCs." (Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate O, B0, and B1 star counts**: Assign Halpha luminosity fractions to O-star types; infer O counts; subtract their FUV contribution for B0 counts and O/B0 NUV contributions for B1 counts.  
  > "we randomly populate O stars in the SFCs by assigning a fraction of the $H\alpha$ luminosity for each type of O star we are considering." (The number of high-mass stars and their ratios in the SFCs); "N(B0)= \frac{L_{FUV} - \sum_{n=3}^{9}(N(On) \times L_{FUV}(On))}{L_{FUV}(B0)}" (The number of high-mass stars and their ratios in the SFCs); "N(B1)= \frac{L_{NUV}-(N(B0)\times L_{NUV}(B0))}{L_{NUV}(B1)} \\ 
- \frac{\sum_{n=3}^{9}(N(On)\times L_{NUV}(On))}{L_{NUV}(B1)}" (The number of high-mass stars and their ratios in the SFCs)
   - parameter: three cases: O8V only; O7V/O8V/O9V; O3V–O9V  
  > "(i) O8V star (whose luminosity corresponds to the median luminosity of the SFCs in NGC 628; (ii) a combination of O7V, O8V, and O9V stars" (Discussion); "(iii) a combination of OV stars with a mass limit of 100 $M_{\odot}$ (O3V to O9V stars)." (Discussion)
   - parameter: 10,000 iterations per SFC; uniform draws between 0 and 1; sum P(On)=1  
  > "Initially, the algorithm carried out 10,000 iterations for each SFC, drawing a random $H\alpha$ luminosity probability for each type of O star from a uniform distribution between 0 and 1." (The number of high-mass stars and their ratios in the SFCs); "P(On) is the randomly generated probability for an On-type star, such that $\sum_{n=3}^{9}P(On)=1$." ## Qualitative findings

- Alpha2 is less sensitive to the adopted O-star population than alpha1.  
  > "Nonetheless, the reduced variation and smaller uncertainties associated with it make $\alpha_{2}$ a more reliable indicator of the IMF index." (Determining the IMF of the SFCs)
- No statistically significant alpha1 difference between arms and spurs.  
  > "There is no statistically significant difference between $\alpha_{1}$ in the spurs and the arms for any three cases." (Discussion)
- Interarm IMF estimates are statistically insignificant.  
  > "Hence, the IMF index values we estimated for the interarm region in this study are statistically insignificant." (Discussion)
- Redder SFCs have steeper inferred IMF indices; blue selection moves alpha2 toward the canonical value.  
  > "The IMF gets steeper as the SFCs become redder, again showing the bottom heaviness." (Discussion); "However, $\alpha_{2}$ values became less steep, making $\alpha_{2}$ closer to the canonical value." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Halpha and UV trace different stellar lifetimes; ALMA supplies molecular gas distribution and spur locations.  
  > "It depends crucially on the difference in time scales over which H$\alpha$ and UV emission trace star formation." (The number of high-mass stars and their ratios in the SFCs); "used ALMA observations to define the molecular gas distribution." (Abstract); "Then, we again checked which SFCs lie in and around the spur regions of the ALMA observations" (Source extraction and classification)
- Why this method: Balmer decrement balances tighter UV/Halpha correlations against JWST's limited coverage; alpha2 is less sensitive to O-star population choices.  
  > "The SFC luminosities corrected with the Beta slope method show more scatter ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.38 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.37), making it less preferable." (Internal extinction correction); "Although the JWST 21$\mu m$ method showed a tight H$\alpha$ and UV luminosity correlation ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.17 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.17), due to its limited field of view, very few SFCs had 21$\mu m$ emission." (Internal extinction correction); "Nonetheless, the reduced variation and smaller uncertainties associated with it make $\alpha_{2}$ a more reliable indicator of the IMF index." (Determining the IMF of the SFCs)
- Alternative: Beta slope correction was less preferable because of greater luminosity-correlation scatter.  
  > "The SFC luminosities corrected with the Beta slope method show more scatter ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.38 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.37), making it less preferable." (Internal extinction correction)
- Alternative: JWST correction gave a tight correlation but covered few SFCs.  
  > "Although the JWST 21$\mu m$ method showed a tight H$\alpha$ and UV luminosity correlation ($\sigma_{FUV}$=0.17 and $\sigma_{NUV}$=0.17), due to its limited field of view, very few SFCs had 21$\mu m$ emission." (Internal extinction correction)
- Alternative: Tested O8-only, O7/O8/O9, and O3–O9 populations rather than a single fixed O-star mixture.  
  > "(i) O8V star (whose luminosity corresponds to the median luminosity of the SFCs in NGC 628; (ii) a combination of O7V, O8V, and O9V stars" (Discussion); "(iii) a combination of OV stars with a mass limit of 100 $M_{\odot}$ (O3V to O9V stars)." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: Five-sigma detections and common three-band apertures support the selected IMF sample.  
  > "We set the detection threshold to five times the estimated global background noise for confident detection." (Source extraction and classification); "taking SFCs that are detected in all three bands and taking the same area for all SFCs (the largest area of SFCs of the three bands) for IMF estimation makes us confident of our analysis." (Distribution of SFCs in NGC 628 in Halpha, FUV and NUV emission.)
- Limitation (authors): SFCs contain unresolved clusters; smoothing can blend sources and increase diffuse ionized gas contributions.  
  > "the SFCs may be hosting multiple clusters that cannot be individually resolved." (Discussion); "This increase in luminosity could be because we have convolved the H$\alpha$ image to a lower resolution, which can cause some clusters to blend into larger ones and potentially increase the contribution of DIGs." (UV and Halpha luminosity correlation)
- Limitation (authors): Unknown O-star populations and possible massive-B-star Halpha contributions create degeneracy.  
  > "Apart from O stars, there can also be some contribution from very massive B stars in $H\alpha$, and we are aware of the degeneracy present in considering the type of the population." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Requiring every O-star type in an SFC may be unrealistic.  
  > "When we populated the SFCs with different types of O stars, we ensured that all types were present in each SFC. But in reality, this may not be the case." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Only the inner region is covered; three usable interarm SFCs give statistically insignificant estimates.  
  > "The MUSE observations of NGC 628 cover only the inner region." (Discussion); "Unfortunately, only three interarm SFCs have FUV, NUV, and the $H\alpha$ emission, which is necessary to obtain the IMF index." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Single CO conversion factor may contribute to gas-correlation scatter.  
  > "The reason for the scatter in the correlation could be that a single conversion factor was considered to obtain the molecular hydrogen mass for all the SFCs." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): NUV can contain evolved stellar emission; stellar mass estimates need improvement.  
  > "However, some emissions from NUV might come from the evolved stellar populations." (Discussion); "However, a more accurate estimation of stellar mass might help us understand it better." (Discussion)
