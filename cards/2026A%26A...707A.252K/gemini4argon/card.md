## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure image-plane continuum flux, extract line spectra, fit line profiles, and compute moment maps and line luminosities**: Extract continuum flux and molecular line spectra from the expected emission region of Yang19; fit asymmetric line profiles with double Gaussian profiles; compute moment-0 and moment-1 maps (clipping <3 sigma in moment-1); calculate line luminosities using Solomon97 formula.  
  > "We extracted the continuum from the region of expected emission from (cite: Yang19), shown in Fig. [1](#fig:continuum)." (Continuum emission); "We created moment-0 and moment-1 maps from the same region used to extract the spectra. For clarity, we removed flux at lower than $3\sigma$ levels in the moment-1 maps." (Line emission); "The spectrum was fit with a double Gaussian profile to account for the asymmetric line shape." (CO(6–5) emission)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 3 sigma cut for moment-1 maps  
  > "For clarity, we removed flux at lower than $3\sigma$ levels in the moment-1 maps." (Line emission)
   - assumption: Flat LambdaCDM cosmology with H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_Lambda = 0.7, Omega_m = 0.3  
  > "Throughout this paper, we adopt a flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with ${H_{0}}$ = 70 km $\mathrm{s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $\mathrm{\Omega_{\Lambda}}$ = 0.7, and $\mathrm{\Omega_{m}}$ = 0.3." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Double Gaussian profile fitting to extracted spectra  
  > "The spectrum was fit with a double Gaussian profile to account for the asymmetric line shape." (CO(6–5) emission)
   - validation: Compared CO(6-5) line flux between high-resolution dataset and combined dataset to check for resolved-out flux  
  > "The flux of the CO(6–5) emission line was compared between the high-resolution ALMA dataset and the combined dataset, with no obvious difference observed (see Fig. [\[fig:co_combined_highres_spec_comparison\]](#fig:co_combined_highres_spec_comparison))." (CO(6–5) emission)
2. **Perform parametric gravitational lens modeling on visibilities**: Fit two foreground lens galaxies as single isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) mass profiles and the background source as a single linear Sérsic profile directly on the interferometric visibilities in PyAutoLens; optimize the lens mass model on dust continuum first, then fix the lens mass model to fit CO(6-5), H2O, and H2O+ emission.  
  > "PyAutoLens is capable of handling interferometric data and performing reconstructions directly on the visibilities, thereby minimizing the effect of correlated noise in interferometric images." (Lens modeling); "We modeled these two galaxies as single isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) mass distributions." (Parametric source modeling); "We first model the dust continuum emission to obtain an optimized lens model and proceed to use this to reconstruct the molecular line emission." (Parametric source modeling)
   - software: PyAutoLens
   - parameter: Lens redshifts fixed at z = 0.626 (or 0.636 in text) and z = 1.002; 1000 posterior samples for magnification error  
  > "| Lens SE | 0.626 | $-0.21^{+0.02}_{-0.02}$ | $-0.08^{+0.02}_{-0.02}$ | $0.51 ^{+0.04} _{-0.04}$ | $18.97 ^{+4.03} _{-3.78}$ | $0.44^{+0.01}_{-0.01}$ |" (Lens modeling); "We performed the sampling 1000 times for each emission type, obtained magnification factors for each of these samples, and calculated the standard deviation of the magnification factors from each sample, thus obtaining the magnification factor error." (Parametric source modeling)
   - assumption: Two SIE lens mass profiles and a single Sérsic source profile  
  > "We modeled these two galaxies as single isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) mass distributions." (Parametric source modeling); "We modeled the source as a single Sérsic profile using a linear light profile, allowing the intensity parameters to be solved via linear algebra." (Parametric source modeling)
   - fitting: Uniform priors on lens and source parameters; linear algebra solve for intensity; 1000 posterior samples from maximum likelihood solution for errors  
  > "During lens modeling, the redshifts of the lens galaxies were fixed while we allowed all other parameters to vary, assuming uniform priors." (Parametric source modeling); "We modeled the source as a single Sérsic profile using a linear light profile, allowing the intensity parameters to be solved via linear algebra." (Parametric source modeling)
   - validation: Tested adding a second Sérsic source profile for CO(6-5) as in Yang19 and verified it did not improve residuals  
  > "We attempted to improve the model by including a second source, also described by a Sérsic profile and as motivated by (cite: Yang19). However, the inclusion of this second source did not improve the residuals, and we discarded this model." (CO(6–5) emission)
3. **Perform non-parametric pixelated source-plane reconstructions and reconstruct the de-magnified CO(6-5) spectral cube**: Using the continuum-optimized lens mass model, reconstruct source-plane emission on a magnification-weighted adaptive Voronoi pixel mesh with constant regularization in PyAutoLens; bin CO(6-5) into 50 km/s channels, reconstruct each bin non-parametrically, and interpolate onto a square pixel grid to form a de-magnified spectral cube; compute source-plane error maps from lens posterior sampling and visibility covariance matrix (via CASA STATWT).  
  > "In this work, we use the optimized lens mass model derived from the dust continuum emission (described in Section [3.3.1.1](#subsubsec:parametric_dust_lens_model)) to create a pixelized reconstruction using a magnification weighted adaptive Voronoi pixel mesh." (Non-parametric source modeling); "To create de-magnified source-plane emission cubes, we divided the emission line into bins of equivalent size and non-parametrically modeled the emission in each bin. We used a spectral resolution of $\approx 50$ km/s" (Spectral cube reconstructions); "In our case, this matrix is computed directly from the interferometric visibilities in the uv-plane and corresponding visibility errors extracted using CASA’s STATWT task, as described in Equation 8 of (cite: Enia18)." (Non-parametric source modeling)
   - software: PyAutoLens, CASA
   - parameter: Circular region of radius 1.5 arcsec (11 kpc); CO(6-5) cube spectral resolution ~50 km/s; static source-plane beam of 0.035'' x 0.027'' (5 pixels per beam); 1000 samples for sigma_l; 100 samples for pixelized magnification error  
  > "We used this methodology to create pixelized reconstructions of the dust continuum emission, CO(6–5), , and $^+$ emission using a Voronoi mesh covering a circular region of radius 1.5 arcseconds (11 kpc) in the image plane around G09v1.97." (Non-parametric source modeling); "Thus, the static angular resolution achieved in the source plane was approximately $0''.035 \times 0''.027$ ($\approx 260 \times 200$ pc) for the reconstructed CO(6–5) spectral cube." (Spectral cube reconstructions); "The pixelization magnification factor error comes from sampling the maximum-likelihood lens model 100 times, and creating pixelized image and source plane emission maps for each sample where the magnification error is the standard deviation of the magnification factor across these 100 samples." (Non-parametric source modeling)
   - assumption: Static source-plane beam defined as theta_maj/sqrt(mu) x theta_min/sqrt(mu) with 5 pixels per beam; Gaussian distribution of lens model parameters over 1000 samples  
  > "We assumed a standard beam size of five pixels per source-plane beam, where the source-plane beam was defined as $\theta_{\rm maj}/\sqrt{\mu} \times \theta_{\rm min}/\sqrt{\mu}$, with $\mu$ given by the parametrically modeled CO(6–5) emission (see Table [\[tab:lensing_bestfit_sourceparams\]](#tab:lensing_bestfit_sourceparams))." (Spectral cube reconstructions); "This approach is valid only if the lens model parameters form Gaussian distributions over the 1000 samples. We confirmed that this assumption holds." (Non-parametric source modeling)
   - fitting: Magnification-weighted adaptive Voronoi pixel mesh with constant regularization scheme  
  > "We used a constant regularization scheme parameterized by the regularization coefficient." (Non-parametric source modeling)
   - validation: Compared moment-0 and moment-1 maps on Voronoi pixels versus interpolated square pixels; also tested doubling the number of Voronoi pixels and using a rectangular mesh  
  > "We computed moment-0 and moment-1 maps for the Voronoi pixels and visually verified that the interpolation to square pixels did not significantly affect the moment-0 or moment-1 maps." (Spectral cube reconstructions); "In the first, we used twice the number of pixels in the Voronoi mesh prior to interpolation to a square grid, and in the second, we used a rectangular mesh instead of a Voronoi mesh." (G09v1.97 - merger or disk?)
4. **Model source-plane CO(6-5) kinematics and classify disk vs. merger**: Fit tilted rings to the de-magnified source-plane CO(6-5) spectral cube using 3DBarolo (first with PA and inclination free, then fixing them to their averages and refitting); calculate PVsplit parameters from the major-axis PV diagram to test disk vs. merger classification.  
  > "We use $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo to model the kinematics of the CO(6–5) emission in the source plane." (Modeling with $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo); "We first ran the fitting procedure with both position angle and inclination free (together with the rotation velocity and dispersion). We then took the averages of the position angle and the inclination, respectively, and fixed those values to refit the disk." (Modeling with $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo); "Nonetheless, we calculate the PVsplit parameters for G09v1.97 and find: $P_{\rm major} = -1.56$, $P_{\mathrm V} = -0.33$, and $P_{\mathrm R} = 0.50$." (G09v1.97 - merger or disk?)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: Smooth & Search masking with 3 sigma cut; 11 rings with radial separation 0.0138'' (theta_maj / 2.5); center fixed via 2D-Gaussian fit to moment-0 map; fixed i = 50 deg and PA = 6.7 deg  
  > "We use $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo ’s Smooth & Search algorithm to mask the data. This algorithm first smooths the data, performs a $3\sigma$ cut to the data to mask values at $<3\sigma$, and then searches for sources in the smoothed and masked data." (Modeling with $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo); "We use a radial separation of $0.0138''$ for the individual rings, calculated by $\theta_{\rm maj}/2.5$ where $\theta_{\rm maj}$ is the major axis of the source-plane static beam, resulting in 11 rings." (Modeling with $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo); "The inclination ($i$), at 50 degrees, did not vary at all, and the position angle varied from $\approx 1$ to $\approx 19$ degrees, resulting in an average of $(6.7 \pm 1.7)^{\circ}$." (Modeling with $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo)
   - assumption: Static source-plane beam of 0.035'' x 0.027''; V_rot in the outermost ring approximates V_max  
  > "We perform all kinematic modeling on the CO(6–5) source plane de-magnified cube (see Section [3.3.3](#subsec:cube_and_spec_reconstructions)), assuming a static beam in the source plane." ## Qualitative findings

- In the source plane, the spatial distributions of CO(6–5) and H2O(2_11-2_02) match closely, whereas the dust continuum emission is more compact.  
  > "We find that the regions of CO(6–5) and H$_2$O($2_{11} - 2_{02}$) emission match closely in the source plane but that the dust continuum emission is more compact." (abstract)
- Both parametric and non-parametric lens models require only a single background source to fit G09v1.97, contrary to previous work that required two merging sources.  
  > "We find that our lens modeling results do not require more than one source, contrary to what has been found in previous studies." (abstract)
- G09v1.97 is best described as a warm, turbulent rotating disk (V_max / sigma_bar = 2.8 ± 0.4) with ~6 sigma velocity residuals indicative of non-circular motions (such as a bi-conical outflow, tidal tail, or companion galaxy).  
  > "Instead, we find that G09v1.97 resembles a rotating disk with $\rm V_{max}/\bar{\sigma} = 2.8 \pm 0.4$ with evidence for residual emission indicative of non-circular motions such as outflows, tidal tails, or an additional background galaxy." (abstract); "These residuals cannot be explained by the lensing analysis and source plane cube reconstruction, and are statistically significant ($\sim 6\sigma$)." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High angular resolution (~0.1'') ALMA observations combined with gravitational lensing magnification (mu ~ 6-10) probe physical scales of a few hundred parsecs in the source plane to resolve the ISM and kinematics; lower-resolution archival data are combined to detect the faint H2O+ line.  
  > "The current paper utilizes very high angular-resolution ALMA observations ($\approx 0''.1$) of G09v1.97, achieving scales of a few hundred parsecs in the source plane, to study the ISM using the emission from the dust, CO(6–5), ($2_{11} - 2_{02}$), hereafter H$_2$O and $^+$ ($2_{02}\text{--}1_{11}$), hereafter H$_2$O$^{+}$." (Introduction); "To be able to study the fainter $^+$ emission line, we combined the data presented above with lower-spatial-resolution observations (C36-5 configuration with baseline range 0.02–1.5 km) used in (cite: Yang19) (project ID: 2015.1.01320.S, P.I.: Omont)." (Observations and data reduction)
- Why this method: PyAutoLens fits directly in the visibility plane to minimize correlated noise and uses a magnification-weighted adaptive Voronoi mesh to avoid a priori morphological assumptions (disk vs. merger); 3DBarolo fits tilted rings with PA and inclination fixed to their averages so residuals reveal non-axisymmetric structures.  
  > "PyAutoLens is capable of handling interferometric data and performing reconstructions directly on the visibilities, thereby minimizing the effect of correlated noise in interferometric images. Additionally, PyAutoLens can create source plane pixelizations from interferometric data, which eliminates the need for assumptions about the background galaxy’s morphology." (Lens modeling); "By fixing the position angle and inclination, we ensure that the final fit most closely resembles a disk, and the residuals retain the ability to reveal the presence of non-axisymmetric structures such as outflows, tidal tails, etc." (Modeling with $^{\rm 3D}$Barolo)
- Alternative: Two-source parametric Sérsic lens model for CO(6-5): tested as motivated by Yang19, but discarded because it did not improve the residuals.  
  > "We attempted to improve the model by including a second source, also described by a Sérsic profile and as motivated by (cite: Yang19). However, the inclusion of this second source did not improve the residuals, and we discarded this model." (CO(6–5) emission)
- Alternative: Voronoi mesh interpolation artifacts as the cause of kinematic residuals: tested by doubling the number of Voronoi pixels and by using a rectangular mesh instead of a Voronoi mesh, and rejected because very similar residuals remained.  
  > "In the first, we used twice the number of pixels in the Voronoi mesh prior to interpolation to a square grid, and in the second, we used a rectangular mesh instead of a Voronoi mesh. In both cases, we found very similar residuals, suggesting that the interpolation from the Voronoi mesh to the square pixel grid was not the cause of the residuals." (G09v1.97 - merger or disk?)
- Alternative: Obscured AGN contamination of L_IR and SFR: considered and rejected because no compact central excess is seen in CO(6-5), H2O, or continuum maps, consistent with line-to-IR ratios and q-parameter.  
  > "In addition, we do not observe any excess emission appearing as a bright central source in either the or continuum maps. Hence, we find no evidence of an AGN in the observed frequencies." (Physical properties of the warm rotating disk G09v1.97)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): About 20 ± 6% of the 1.940 mm continuum emission may be resolved out in the high-resolution observations compared to Yang19.  
  > "This value is lower than that found by (cite: Yang19) of $8.8 \pm 0.5$ mJy, suggesting the possibility that some continuum emission has been resolved out in the high-resolution observations utilized in this paper." (Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Intensity values from PyAutoLens visibility fits are in arbitrary units.  
  > "In its current form, intensity values from PyAutoLens fits performed on interferometric visibilities are in arbitrary units." (Lens modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Simple SIE lens mass models have small formal statistical uncertainties and are prone to model misspecification.  
  > "Such small formal uncertainties are common in group-scale strong lenses like G09v1.97, particularly when adopting simple mass models such as the Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid (SIE), which are prone to model misspecification." (Non-parametric source modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Interpolating the source-plane CO(6-5) cube onto a square grid requires assuming a static source-plane beam, whereas the true lensing magnification and effective resolution vary across the source.  
  > "We note that although we assumed a static beam across the image (see Section [3.3.3](#subsec:cube_and_spec_reconstructions)), as has been done in previous kinematic studies of lensed galaxies (cite: Amvrosiadis24), the magnification across the image is not, in fact, static" (Modeling results)
