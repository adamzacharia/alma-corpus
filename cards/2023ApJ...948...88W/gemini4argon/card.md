## Analysis recipe

1. **Define overlapping IRS apertures and extract spectra and CO/IR photometry**: Define 154 polygonal apertures from the spatial overlap of Spitzer-IRS SL, SH, and LH modules; convolve IRAC, WISE, MIPS, and PACS photometry to 15" FWHM to match the CO J=(2-1) maps; perform background subtraction outside R_25 with a 3σ mask; extract average IRS spectra, photometry, and CO J=(2-1) intensities and errors in the apertures; exclude AGN nuclei and regions lacking neon detections.  
  > "Our sample is therefore composed of the overlapping areas between the SL, SH, and LH data for each nuclear and extranuclear target." (2.1. IRS Spectroscopy); "We convolve all photometric data to 15″ to match the resolution of our CO data, except SPIRE 250 μm which has 18″ resolution (see Section 2.4)." (2.2. MIR and FIR Photometry); "We remove these AGN from our sample, leaving a total of 112 SF regions and of these there are 75 that are covered by CO mapping and 67 that have CO detections at S/N >3." (2.5. Additional Measurements)
   - software: Astropy, NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: 15" FWHM Gaussian PSF; S/N > 3 for CO detections; 154 total apertures (112 SF regions, 67 with CO S/N > 3)  
  > "These CO maps have been convolved to a Gaussian point-spread function (PSF) with FWHM of 15″." (2.3. CO Mapping); "We use the full set of 112 regions for SFR correlations and CO correlations only use the subset of 67 regions." (2.5. Additional Measurements)
   - assumption: Constant 10% uncertainty on measured surface brightness in each aperture for all photometric bands; no extended source or IRS aperture corrections applied  
  > "We assume a constant 10% of the measured surface brightness in each aperture for all photometric bands." (2.2. MIR and FIR Photometry); "We find our final results are not altered if we use this aperture correction on the IRS data, so we proceed without applying any correction." (2.4. Convolution and Aperture Corrections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared native resolution Spitzer photometric maps to 15" convolved images at 5.8, 8, and 24 μm; found native-to-convolved ratio is 1.1 ± 0.1 and does not alter final results  
  > "The native-to-convolved ratio is found to vary little with wavelength: 1.1 ± 0.1 for each photometric band." (2.4. Convolution and Aperture Corrections)
2. **Fit MIR spectral features in SL, SH, and LH spectra**: Fit SL spectra using PAHFIT with no attenuation fitting. Fit SH spectra using custom FARAMIR routine (linear continuum subtraction between 10–15 μm and 15–18.2 μm, Drude profiles for PAHs including 4 Drude components for the 11.3 μm complex, and Gaussian profiles for lines). Fit LH spectra by cropping ±1 μm around H2 S(0), [S III] 33.5 μm, and [Si II] 34.8 μm, subtracting a linear continuum, and fitting Gaussians. Estimate SH and LH uncertainties with 500 Monte Carlo trials and apply a 3σ residual detection threshold.  
  > "The extracted SL spectra were fit with the IDL program PAHFIT (Smith et al. 2007)." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting); "We then take the mean and standard deviation of 500 trial fits as the value and uncertainty of the feature. For convenience, we refer to this method and the program used to implement it as the Fitter for Aromatic, Atomic, and Molecular features in the Infrared (FARAMIR)." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting); "We define our non-detection threshold as thrice the standard deviation of the residuals, such that any Drude or Gaussian with a fitted amplitude below this threshold is considered a non-detection." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
   - software: PAHFIT, FARAMIR
   - parameter: 500 Monte Carlo trials; 3σ residual amplitude detection threshold; initial feature wavelengths and FWHMs in Table 2  
  > "We then take the mean and standard deviation of 500 trial fits as the value and uncertainty of the feature." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
   - assumption: MIR dust attenuation is minimal and not fit in PAHFIT; linear dust continuum in SH and LH spectral windows  
  > "the attenuation in the MIR is generally minimal (Roussel et al. 2007; Smith et al. 2007), so we do not fit the attenuation." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting); "We subtracted two linear estimates of the continuum between three points with no nearby emission features: one between 10 and 15 μm, and another between 15 and 18.2 μm." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
   - fitting: Linear combination of Gaussian and Drude profiles with 500 Monte Carlo trials perturbing spectrum points within Gaussian uncertainties  
  > "In each trial, we perturb each point in the SH spectrum within its uncertainty assuming a Gaussian distribution, then run our fitting routine to find the integrated intensity of each emission feature." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
   - validation: Compared FARAMIR SH fits against PAHFIT SL fits for overlapping features (11.3 μm, 12.6 μm, H2 S(2), [Ne II] 12.8 μm); FARAMIR agrees well, overestimating by ~20% or less on average  
  > "FARAMIR reproduces the relevant PAH feature and emission line intensities but overestimates by ∼20% or less on average relative to PAHFIT." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
3. **Decompose IRAC4 8 μm and WISE3 12 μm bands via synthetic photometry**: Stitch SL (5.2–14.7 μm) and continuum-adjusted SH (14.7–16.4 μm) spectra, decompose SL spectra into PAH, dust continuum, starlight, and line components using PAHFIT, and perform synthetic photometry to measure fractional contributions to IRAC4 8 μm and WISE3 12 μm.  
  > "In order to study the contributions of PAH features, emission lines, and dust continuum to the IRAC4 8 μm and WISE3 12 μm photometric bands, we perform synthetic photometry on decomposed IRS spectra." (2.8. Synthetic Photometry); "The final stitched spectra are composed of the full SL spectrum from 5.2–14.7 μm and the continuum-adjusted SH spectrum from 14.7–16.4 μm." (2.8. Synthetic Photometry)
   - software: PAHFIT, synphot
   - parameter: 120 non-AGN spectra; SL 5.2–14.7 μm stitched with SH 14.7–16.4 μm  
  > "We use PAHFIT to decompose the SL1 and SL2 spectra into PAH, starlight continuum, dust continuum, and line emission contributions in the 120 non-AGN spectra." (2.8. Synthetic Photometry)
   - assumption: Appended 14.7–16 μm SH spectral segment is treated as continuum  
  > "For the wavelengths longer than the SL coverage (greater than 14.7 μm) we consider the appended 14.7–16 μm portion of the SH spectrum to be continuum." (2.8. Synthetic Photometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive reference Σ_SFR and TIR and compute correlations with CO and Σ_SFR**: Calculate TIR using Eq. (1) and reference Σ_SFR from [Ne II] 12.8 μm, [Ne III] 15.6 μm, and KK04 metallicity using Eq. (2). Compute Spearman rank correlation coefficients (ρ_s) and fit logarithmic power laws log10(Y) = m log10(X) + b via ordinary least squares with 500 Monte Carlo perturbation and bootstrap resampling trials.  
  > "We also derive SFR for each of our apertures using the calibration presented in Whitcomb et al. (2020)." (2.6. Derived Quantities); "We quantify the correlation between two emission features with the Spearman rank correlation coefficient. We also fit a power law to the data by ordinary least-squares using the logarithmic form: log 10 (Y) = m ´ log 10 (X) + b (3)" (2.9. Correlation Methods); "We complete 500 such trials and take the mean and standard deviation as the value of each quantity and its uncertainty." (2.9. Correlation Methods)
   - software: SciPy, NumPy
   - parameter: 500 Monte Carlo + bootstrap trials; subsample split at physical area 0.4 kpc^2  
  > "We compare the behavior of correlations involving the half of regions greater than 0.4 kpc 2 to those with smaller areas in Section 3.1." (2.5. Additional Measurements); "We complete 500 such trials and take the mean and standard deviation as the value of each quantity and its uncertainty." (2.9. Correlation Methods)
   - assumption: CO J=(2-1) surface brightness used directly as reference molecular gas tracer without applying a CO-to-H2 conversion factor  
  > "We use CO emission as our reference molecular gas tracer and we do not convert to H 2 gas masses with a CO-to-H 2 conversion factor." (2.3. CO Mapping)
   - fitting: Ordinary least-squares fit to log10(Y) = m * log10(X) + b with 500 Monte Carlo Gaussian perturbation and bootstrap resampling trials  
  > "We also fit a power law to the data by ordinary least-squares using the logarithmic form: log 10 (Y) = m ´ log 10 (X) + b (3)" (2.9. Correlation Methods); "In each of the trials we randomly offset each point according to its uncertainty assuming a Gaussian distribution. We simultaneously perform a bootstrap that randomly resamples the whole data set with replacement." (2.9. Correlation Methods)
   - validation: Tested PT05 vs KK04 metallicities, doubled photometric uncertainty assumption, and restricted SFR and CO correlations to identical region subsets; found no significant changes in results  
  > "We find our results do not vary significantly if this assumption is doubled." (2.2. MIR and FIR Photometry); "We find no significant difference in our observed trends in Section 3 if the Pilyugin & Thuan (2005; PT05) calibrated metallicities are used instead of the KK04 values." (2.5. Additional Measurements); "We find that restricting the set to include the same points in both correlations does not significantly alter our results." (2.9. Correlation Methods)

## Qualitative findings

- MIR emission features (PAHs and H2 rotational lines) trace both CO and SFR better than CO and SFR trace one another, with H2 rotational lines and PAH features correlating best with CO (ρ_CO > 0.8).  
  > "In general, we find MIR emission features (i.e., PAHs and H 2 rotational lines) trace both CO and SFR better than CO and SFR trace one another. H 2 lines and PAH features correlate best with CO." (Abstract); "All major PAH features and H 2 rotational lines are strongly correlated with CO (ρ CO - 0.8)." (3.2. Molecular Gas Correlations)
- Fine structure lines from ions correlate best with SFR, and the [S III] lines at 18.7 and 33.5 μm provide single-parameter MIR SFR tracers with negligible metallicity dependence in the sample.  
  > "Fine structure lines from ions correlate best with SFR. The [S III ] lines at 18.7 and 33.5 μm, in particular, have a very tight correlation with SFR, and we use them to calibrate new single-parameter MIR tracers of SFR that have negligible metallicity dependence in our sample." (Abstract)
- The 17 μm/7.7 μm PAH feature ratio increases with CO emission (with power-law slope increasing from 0.8 at 7.7 μm to 1.2 at 17 μm), which may indicate PAH growth or neutralization in molecular gas.

(Abstract); "In contrast with the H 2 :CO correlations, the power-law slope increases for CO correlations with longer wavelength PAH features, ranging from 0.8 at 7.7 μm, 0.9 at 11.3 μm, and 1.2 at 17 μm (see Table 4)." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: SINGS archival Spitzer-IRS spectra cover 5–35 μm (capturing major PAH bands, H2 rotational lines, and fine structure lines including [Ne II] and [Ne III] for dust-independent SFR) at sub-kpc scales in nearby galaxies that also have matched 15" CO J=(2-1) maps and 3.4–250 μm photometry.  
  > "Finally, the 15.6 μm [Ne III ] and 12.8 μm [Ne II ] lines are derived from the same MIR spectra as the dust and other features in this study. This removes concerns related to calibration differences and allows us to use all SINGS spectroscopy regions in our study." (1.1.1. Tracing SFR); "The resulting spectral range includes all the major PAH features (except 3.3 μm, which contributes ∼2% of the total PAH power; Lai et al. 2020) and the 12.8 μm [Ne II ] and 15.6 μm [Ne III ] lines that are needed for our SFR tracer (see Section 2.6)." (2.1. IRS Spectroscopy)
- Why this method: Used a dust-independent neon fine-structure line SFR tracer calibrated to free-free emission to avoid circular correlations with dust, exploited the small-scale (<0.4 kpc^2) breakdown of the K-S relation to separate SF and gas tracers, and built FARAMIR because PAHFIT only operates on low-resolution spectra.  
  > "Because our aim is to distinguish between correlations with SFR and cold gas (and therefore dust) it is critical to use an SFR tracer that is minimally influenced by dust, both in attenuation and by any hybrid SFRtype correction." (1.1.1. Tracing SFR); "At present PAHFIT only operates on low-resolution spectra. To separate the underlying hot dust continuum from PAH emission in high-resolution SH spectra, we constructed a program similar to PAHFIT following Smith et al. (2007)." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
- Alternative: Using Spitzer-IRS Long-Low (LL) instead of Long-High (LH) for long-wavelength coverage; rejected because LL spatial coverage of extranuclear regions in SINGS is sparse compared to SH and LH.  
  > "The LL spatial coverage of extranuclear regions in SINGS is sparse compared to SH and LH, so we make use of LH for the long wavelength coverage." (2.1. IRS Spectroscopy)
- Alternative: Applying aperture corrections to native-resolution IRS spectra to match 15" photometry; rejected because testing native-to-convolved ratios at 5.8, 8, and 24 μm showed a constant ratio of 1.1 ± 0.1 that does not alter final results.  
  > "We find our final results are not altered if we use this aperture correction on the IRS data, so we proceed without applying any correction." (2.4. Convolution and Aperture Corrections)
- Alternative: Using Pilyugin & Thuan (2005; PT05) metallicities instead of KK04 metallicities; tested and found no significant difference in observed trends.  
  > "We find no significant difference in our observed trends in Section 3 if the Pilyugin & Thuan (2005; PT05) calibrated metallicities are used instead of the KK04 values." (2.5. Additional Measurements)
- Alternative: Including CO detections with S/N < 3 as upper limits; rejected because they do not significantly constrain the slope and intercept of the power-law fits.  
  > "Including the weaker detections as upper limits does not significantly constrain the slope and intercept of our power-law fits, so we exclude them from the analysis." (2.5. Additional Measurements)

- Limitation (authors): The SINGS sample focuses on optically bright H II regions in local spiral galaxies, biasing the sample toward more massive and evolved H II regions and limiting low-metallicity coverage (only 12 regions between 1/5 and 4/5 Z_sun).  
  > "We note that this data set is focused on optically bright HII regions in local spiral galaxies. This biases our sample toward more massive and evolved H II regions." (2.1. IRS Spectroscopy); "Since we have few low-metallicity regions (only 12 between 1 5 and 4 5 Z ☉ ), we only make inferences based on the presence or absence of a metallicity trend in the scatter of each correlation." (4.5. Implications for High-redshift IR Studies)
- Limitation (authors): Dividing the sample by physical aperture size (<0.4 kpc^2) biases the small-area subsample toward closer galaxies, which are more likely to be dwarf and/or lower-metallicity galaxies.  
  > "In particular, since the SINGS apertures are all of similar angular size, the smallest regions will be from the closest sources, which are more likely to be dwarf and/or lower metallicity galaxies." (3.1. Breakdown in the K-S Relation Reveals SF-dominant and CO-dominant Tracers)
- Limitation (authors): FARAMIR uses a simple linear continuum model for SH spectra, which overestimates feature intensities by ~20% or less relative to PAHFIT and yields few detections for weak features near continuum anchor points (e.g., 13.5, 14.2, 15.9, and 17.9 μm PAHs).  
  > "FARAMIR reproduces the relevant PAH feature and emission line intensities but overestimates by ∼20% or less on average relative to PAHFIT." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting); "We find very few detections for the 15.9 and 17.9 μm PAH features, likely because these are intrinsically weak and immediately near our assumed continuum points at 15 and 18.2 μm." (2.7. MIR Spectral Fitting)
- Limitation (authors): Using a short-timescale (<5 Myr) reference SFR tracer means features responding to SF on longer timescales cannot be easily distinguished from molecular gas tracers by correlation alone.  
  > "Since our reference tracer responds to SF events on short timescales, an emission feature could be less correlated with our SFR if it responds directly to SF on longer timescales." (2.6. Derived Quantities); "It is also due to our use of a short timescale reference SFR tracer, with which we cannot easily distinguish between a tracer of a varying SF history and a tracer of molecular gas from correlation alone." (3. Results)
- Limitation (authors): CO J=(2-1) emission is used without converting to H2 mass via R_21 and α_CO, which can vary with excitation, optical depth, and metallicity.  
  > "CO J = (2 − 1) can be converted to CO J = (1 − 0) with a ratio R 21 that is ∼0.65 on average and can vary between ∼0.4 and 1.0 (Leroy et al. 2022). This introduces some uncertainty in translating our correlations with CO J = (2 − 1) emission into correlations with M H2 ." (4.2. CO-to-H 2 Conversion Factor)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The reference SFR tracer is derived from the same Spitzer-IRS spectra ([Ne II] 12.8 μm and [Ne III] 15.6 μm) as the MIR features being tested, which could introduce correlated aperture/flux-calibration systematics relative to the external 15" CO maps.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): IRS spectra are not convolved to the 15" PSF of the CO and photometric maps, so spatial resolution mismatch may affect small apertures.
