## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce MEGARA and SITELLE optical IFU observations, subtract stellar continuum, and fit emission lines**: Reduce MEGARA blocks with MEGARA DRP and custom sky/wavelength corrections; reduce SITELLE with ORBS/ORCS and Gaia DR3 flux calibration. Subtract stellar continuum using pPXF with MILES templates. Fit optical emission lines with 1 or 2 Gaussian components using LMFIT (for MEGARA) and ORCS (for SITELLE).  
  > "Each observing block was reduced independently using the MEGARA Data Reduction Pipeline `MEGARA DRP` (version 0.15)." (MEGARA); "The median spectrum was fitted using pPXF algorithm (Penalized Pixel-Fitting; (cite: Cappellari2004))." (Stellar Continuum Modeling and Subtraction); "For each spectrum, the H$\alpha$, \[N II\]$\lambda$6548, 6583 and \[S II\]$\lambda$6716, 6731 lines were fitted simultaneously with Gaussian profiles using the `LMFIT` routine, based on the Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares algorithm (cite: Newville2016)." (Emission Line Fitting)
   - software: MEGARA DRP, ORBS, ORCS, pPXF, LMFIT
   - parameter: 0.31'' square spaxels for MEGARA; 0.32'' spaxels binned 2x2 for SITELLE  
  > "Finally, each observing block was resampled onto a regularized grid with 0.31$\ensuremath{''}$ square spaxels, producing data cubes with dimension of 43 $\times$ 40 $\times$ 4300, corresponding to a total of 1720 spectra per cube." (MEGARA); "To enhance the SNR, maps were spatially binned (2 $\times$ 2)." (SITELLE)
   - assumption: Galactic extinction A_V = 0.063 mag, R_V = 3.1 following Cardelli et al. (1989); systemic velocity v = 1307 km s^-1  
  > "Fluxes were corrected for Galactic extinction assuming $A_V = 0.063$ mag (NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database) and $R_V = 3.1$, following the (cite: Cardelli1989) extinction law, with a wavelength-dependent correction applied to each line." (Map Generation); "For consistency with previous studies (cite: Werner2013; Simionescu2018), we adopt a systemic velocity of $v = 1307$ km s$^{-1}$ for M87 ($z = 0.00436$, $D_L = 16.1$ Mpc; (cite: Blakeslee2009)), where 1$'$ = 4.68 kpc." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares via LMFIT; 3-sigma residual criterion and AIC difference (Delta AIC > 10) for 2-component selection  
  > "If $\epsilon_\text{line} > 3 \times \epsilon_c$ (following a $3\sigma$ criterion), the addition of a second component is considered." (Emission Line Fitting); "In most cases, the two-component fits in the northwestern region are justified when the AIC difference, defined as $\Delta \text{AIC} = \text{AIC}_{N + 1} - \text{AIC}_N$, exceeds 10 (cite: Wei2016)." (Emission Line Fitting)
   - validation: Visual inspection of spectra and AIC statistical validation  
  > "A visual inspection of the spectra was also performed to validate the statistical selection of components." (Emission Line Fitting)
2. **Align WCS, convolve MEGARA maps to ALMA beam, and resample ALMA CO(2-1) maps for multi-phase comparison**: Cross-correlate MEGARA H\alpha with SITELLE using correlate2d to align WCS; convolve MEGARA maps with a Gaussian kernel to match the ALMA beam (1.4'' x 0.7'', PA 75 deg); resample archival ALMA CO(2-1) moment maps onto MEGARA's spaxel grid using astropy.reproject; compute flux ratio, LOS velocity difference, and velocity dispersion ratio maps.  
  > "To enable direct spaxel-by-spaxel comparisons with ALMA, MEGARA maps were convolved with a Gaussian kernel to match the ALMA beam." (Archival Data, WCS Alignment and Spatial Resampling); "The ALMA data were resampled onto MEGARA’s spaxel grid using `astropy.reproject` function. This enabled the construction of three key comparative maps: (1) the peak-normalized (\[N II\] + H$\alpha$)/CO flux ratio, (2) the line-of-sight (LOS) velocity difference, and (3) the velocity dispersion ratio (see Section 3.2.2)." (Archival Data, WCS Alignment and Spatial Resampling)
   - software: correlate2d, astropy.reproject
   - parameter: ALMA beam 1.4'' x 0.7'' (PA 75 deg), MEGARA grid 0.31'' spaxels  
  > "The ALMA data reach an rms sensitivity of 0.2 mJy beam$^{-1}$ per 50 km s$^{-1}$ channel, with a synthesized beam of 1.4$\ensuremath{''}$ $\times$ 0.7$\ensuremath{''}$ (position angle of 75$\ensuremath{^\circ}$; (cite: Simionescu2018))." (Archival Data, WCS Alignment and Spatial Resampling)
   - assumption: ALMA CO(2-1) corresponds kinematically to MEGARA's first velocity component in the two-component region  
  > "The ALMA observations indicate a LOS velocity of $\sim$130 km s$^{-1}$, which falls within the range of MEGARA’s first component. We therefore use the first component in this region and the single component map elsewhere." (Comparison Between SE Filaments and CO(2-1))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Spaxel-by-spaxel scatter plots comparing velocity and velocity dispersion between H\alpha and CO(2-1)  
  > "We complemented this analysis with spaxel-by-spaxel scatter plots comparing velocity and velocity dispersion between the two tracers, following the approach of (cite: Tremblay2018)." (Comparison Between SE Filaments and CO(2-1))
3. **Model analytical thin-disk rotation and compute velocity structure functions (VSFs) and turbulent heating rate**: Compare SITELLE velocities to an analytical thin-disk rotation model. Compute first-order VSFs across MEGARA, SITELLE, ALMA CO(2-1), and segmented MUSE subregions to extract slope, flattening scale, and amplitude. Estimate turbulent heating rate Q_turb and radiative cooling rate Q_cool in the FE filament.  
  > "Here, we extend this approach by analyzing the nature of filament motions using first-order VSFs ($n~=~1$)." (Velocity Structure Functions); "Given this consistency, we further compare turbulent properties using VSFs constructed from both velocity maps (i.e., MEGARA, ALMA)." (Comparison Between SE Filaments and CO(2-1)); "We estimate the turbulent heating rate, $Q_{\text{turb}}$, at the seeing scale (1$''$ $\approx$ 78 pc), following the formalism of (cite: Ganguly2023) for VSFs steeper than the Kolmogorov slope" (Turbulent Heating in the FE filament)
   - software: astropy
   - parameter: Velocity uncertainty threshold <= 20 km s^-1; localized VSF aperture radius ~0.39 kpc spaced by ~100 pc  
  > "To ensure the reliability of the VSFs, spaxels with velocity uncertainties greater than 20 km s$^{-1}$ were excluded, a threshold above the mean uncertainty that does not significantly affect the results." (Velocity Structure Functions); "Building on this segmentation, we defined a series of central positions along the isolated structures, spaced by $\sim100$ pc. Around each center, a localized VSF was computed from spaxels within a $\sim$0.39 kpc radius aperture – chosen to match the widest filament widths in projection and to align with the MEGARA FOV." (Across the Central Filaments)
   - assumption: SMBH mass M = 6.0 x 10^9 M_sun, disk inclination i = 25 deg, PA = 45 deg; for FE heating rate: n_e = 0.015 cm^-3, T = 1.9 keV, mu = 0.61, xi = 1.912  
  > "where $G$ is the gravitational constant, $M_\bullet = 6.0\times10^9$ M$_\odot$ is the mass of the SMBH" (Velocity Field Across the Filament Network); "where $i = 25\ensuremath{^\circ}$ is the disk inclination, and $\theta$ is the azimuthal angle in the disk plane between the major axis (assumed at PA = $45\ensuremath{^\circ}$), adopting the geometric parameters derived by ." (Velocity Field Across the Filament Network); "Adopting $n_e \approx 0.015$ cm$^{-3}$ and $T \approx 1.9$ keV at 15 kpc (cite: Zhuravleva2014), we estimate a turbulent heating rate of $Q_\text{turb}\approx4.9~\times~10^{-28}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-3}$ and a radiative cooling rate of $Q_\text{cool}\approx~4.1~\times 10^{-27}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-3}$ for the FE filament." (Turbulent Heating in the FE filament)
   - fitting: Error propagation and bootstrapping for VSF uncertainties  
  > "For the uncertainties of the mean velocity differences, they were estimated using a combined approach that accounts for both error propagation from individual velocity measurements and statistical variations via bootstrapping." (Velocity Structure Functions)
   - validation: Tests of filament segmentation, spaxel size/binning, and window size on VSF stability (Appendices 9, 10, 11)  
  > "To evaluate this, we conducted a series of spaxel size/binning tests using SITELLE, MEGARA and MUSE data." (Limitations); "To evaluate whether the size and geometry of the analysis window affects VSF measurements, we used the full MUSE velocity map of the central filaments and computed VSFs within circular and rectangular windows of varying sizes." (Limitations)
4. **Analyze optical emission line diagnostics and H\alpha - X-ray surface brightness correlation**: Compute optical line ratios ([O I]/H\alpha, [N II]/H\alpha, [S II]/H\alpha, [S II]6716/6731), WHAN diagrams, and velocity dispersion vs. line ratio correlations. Decompose Chandra X-ray emission into 4 components using pGMCA and compare X-ray and SITELLE H\alpha surface brightness across filament regions.  
  > "As a first step, we examine key emission-line ratios sensitive to ionization mechanisms such as shocks: \[O I\]/H$\alpha$, \[N II\]/H$\alpha$ and \[S II\]/H$\alpha$." (Line Ratios); "To further investigate the nature of the ionizing sources in the SE and FE filaments, we use the Width H$\alpha$-Nitrogen (WHAN) diagram (cite: CidFernandes2010; CidFernandes2011), traditionally used to classify galaxies by dominant ionization mechanisms." (WHAN Diagram); "We use deep archival *Chandra* observations of M87, from which the filamentary X-ray component is isolated using the Poisson Generalized Morphological Component Analysis (pGMCA; (cite: Bobin2020))." (H\alpha – X-Ray Correlation)
   - software: pGMCA
   - parameter: 4 components in pGMCA decomposition; 1 kpc-wide concentric annuli for radial line ratio gradients  
  > "Figure 8 presents these ratio maps along with radial gradients derived from SITELLE data, computed using concentric 1 kpc-wide annuli centered on the galaxy nucleus." (Line Ratios); "We decompose the X-ray emission into four components reflecting a mix of structures: diffuse halo, AGN cavities, and filaments – without a strict one-to-one association." (H\alpha – X-Ray Correlation)
   - assumption: Electron temperature of 10,000 K for [S II] electron density calibration  
  > "In the SE filaments, the mean \[S II\] doublet ratio is $\sim1.37$, corresponding to $n_e \sim 40$ cm$^{-3}$ assuming a electron temperature of 10,000 K and using the calibration from (cite: Proxauf2014)." (Line Ratios)
   - fitting: Weighted linear regression on median line ratio values; Pearson correlation coefficient r for WHAN SNR and velocity dispersion vs. line ratios  
  > "Within each annulus, line ratio distributions are summarized with boxplots (median, interquartile range, and overall spread), and a weighted linear regression on median values to quantify the radial trends." (Line Ratios); "We constructed spaxel-by-spaxel diagrams of velocity dispersion versus \[N II\]/H$\alpha$ and \[S II\]/H$\alpha$ for each pointing and velocity component (where applicable), and quantified the trends using the Pearson correlation coefficient $r$." (Velocity Dispersion vs Line Ratios)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to annulus spacing; tested correlation between W_\alpha and H\alpha SNR  
  > "Tests confirmed that the analysis is insensitive to annulus spacing." (Line Ratios); "To assess measurement impact, we examined $W_\alpha$ versus H$\alpha$ SNR, finding strong positive correlations (Pearson coefficients 0.93 for SE, 0.76 for FE)." (WHAN Diagram)

## Software

- **MEGARA DRP** 0.15: Data reduction of MEGARA observing blocks  
  > "Each observing block was reduced independently using the MEGARA Data Reduction Pipeline `MEGARA DRP` (version 0.15)." (MEGARA)
- **pPXF** : Stellar continuum modeling and subtraction for MEGARA and SITELLE spectra  
  > "The median spectrum was fitted using pPXF algorithm (Penalized Pixel-Fitting; (cite: Cappellari2004))." (Stellar Continuum Modeling and Subtraction); "The resulting spectra were fitted with pPXF using MILES templates, fitting the SN1 and SN3 filter simultaneously." (SITELLE)
- **LMFIT** : Gaussian profile fitting of emission lines in MEGARA spectra  
  > "For each spectrum, the H$\alpha$, \[N II\]$\lambda$6548, 6583 and \[S II\]$\lambda$6716, 6731 lines were fitted simultaneously with Gaussian profiles using the `LMFIT` routine, based on the Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares algorithm (cite: Newville2016)." (Emission Line Fitting)
- **ORBS** 3.0: Data reduction of SITELLE observations  
  > "Data reduction used SITELLE’s dedicated pipelines, `ORBS` and `ORCS` (version 3.0; (cite: Martin2021))." (SITELLE)
- **ORCS** 3.0: Data reduction and emission line map generation for SITELLE data cubes  
  > "Data reduction used SITELLE’s dedicated pipelines, `ORBS` and `ORCS` (version 3.0; (cite: Martin2021))." (SITELLE); "From the calibrated data cubes, with sky and stellar continuum subtracted, `ORCS` was used to generate maps of the continuum, emission line amplitude and flux, radial velocity, and velocity dispersion, along with their associated uncertainties." (SITELLE)
- **correlate2d** : Cross-correlating MEGARA H\alpha filamentary structures with SITELLE for WCS alignment

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA CO(2-1) emission in the SE filament is co-spatial and kinematically aligned with the primary component of the warm ionized gas, with comparable small-scale VSF slopes (~2/3), while the ionized gas exhibits systematically broader velocity dispersions.  
  > "ALMA CO emission is co-spatial and kinematically aligned with the ionized gas, the latter showing broader velocity dispersions." (Abstract); "In the SE filament, CO(2–1) emission detected with ALMA coincides with the ionized gas, and both phases show comparable VSFs on small scales." (Summary and conclusions)
- The warm ionized filaments display complex motions inconsistent with a simple rotating disk model, with outer filaments exhibiting deviations > 100 km s^-1.  
  > "Notably, the SE and FE filaments, as well as the horseshoe-like filament located at $\sim90$$\ensuremath{''}$ southeast from the nucleus, exhibit substantial deviations (> 100 km s$^{-1}$) from model predictions, suggesting additional local processes (e.g., shocks or dynamical instabilities)." (Velocity Field Across the Filament Network); "Therefore, a simple rotating disk model is insufficient to explain the full range of filament motions observed in M87." (Velocity Field Across the Filament Network)
- Localized VSFs across the central and SE filaments show consistent steep slopes (> ~2/3) and flattening at scales of a few hundred parsecs (~0.2-0.3 kpc), possibly pointing to energy injection from Type Ia supernovae or projection/LOS superposition effects.  
  > "Velocity structure functions (VSFs) of the warm and cold gas in the central and SE filaments show consistent steep slopes (> 2/3) and flattening on small scales of a few hundred parsecs, possibly suggesting energy injection from Type Ia supernovae, though interpretation is method-limited." (Abstract); "Most VSFs show steep slopes (> $\sim$2/3) and flattening at small scales of a few hundred parsecs." (Summary and conclusions)
- Emission line diagnostics indicate non-stellar AGN-related ionization dominates across the filaments, with higher excitation near the radio lobes and lower-excitation fossil feedback signatures in the isolated FE filament.  
  > "Ionization diagnostics indicate AGN-related processes (e.g., shocks) dominate, with higher-energy excitation near the radio lobes and (lower-energy) fossil feedback signatures in the FE filament." (Abstract)
- The main filaments follow a strong H\alpha - X-ray surface brightness correlation, whereas the isolated FE filament deviates significantly below the trend.  
  > "Finally, the filaments follow the same strong H$\alpha$ - X-ray surface brightness correlation seen in other clusters, supporting local thermal coupling between phases. However, the FE filament deviates from this trend, possibly due to uplift from past AGN outbursts or limitations in the analysis method." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MEGARA provides higher spectral resolution (R ~ 6000) targeting the SE filaments (overlapping the only known ALMA molecular clump) and the isolated FE filament outside the previous MUSE WFM FOV, while SITELLE maps the entire 11' x 11' filamentary network.  
  > "In this context, we present new integral field spectroscopy of two key regions in M87’s filamentary nebula with MEGARA. The first targets the inner region ($\sim$3 kpc southeast of the nucleus) beyond the inner radio lobes and overleaping the only known molecular gas clump." (Introduction); "Both regions fall outside the previous MUSE wide field mode (WFM) pointing (1$\ensuremath{'}~\times~1\ensuremath{'}$). Complementary SITELLE imaging Fourier transform spectroscopy provides full mapping of the ionized gas across the network." (Introduction)
- Why this method: First-order VSFs quantify velocity variations across spatial scales to probe turbulent driving; WHAN diagrams classify ionization when [O III]5007 and H\beta are outside spectral coverage; pGMCA isolates morphologically distinct X-ray filament emission without prior physical assumptions.  
  > "VSFs are typically expressed as a function of the scalar separation $l = |\mathbf{l}|$, providing a statistical description of velocity variations across spatial scales." (Velocity Structure Functions); "It an alternative to the classical Baldwin, Phillips & Terlevich (BPT) diagram (cite: Baldwin1981) when key emission lines such as \[O III\]$\lambda$5007 and H$\beta$ are unavailable, as in our MEGARA and SITELLE data." (WHAN Diagram); "The pGMCA method can extract a user-defined number of components with no prior physical or instrumental knowledge, by focusing on the morphological diversity of the different emissions." (H\alpha – X-Ray Correlation)
- Alternative: Three-component Gaussian emission line fits were tested for MEGARA spectra but never statistically required.  
  > "A third component was also tested but never required." (Emission Line Fitting)
- Alternative: Standard single mean sky spectrum subtraction in MEGARA DRP was replaced with subtracting the mean of the two nearest sky fiber groups due to strong sky residuals.  
  > "Instead of subtracting a single mean sky spectrum from all fibers, we subtracted the mean of the spectra from the two nearest sky fiber groups for each fiber within the pseudo-slit." (MEGARA)
- Alternative: Classical BPT ionization diagnostics and [O II] electron density diagnostics were not used because H\beta and [O III]5007 are outside SITELLE/MEGARA coverage and the [O II] doublet has low SNR and is unresolved in SITELLE SN1.  
  > "Combined with the unresolved doublet in SN1 data, this prevents the use of \[O II\] for reliable electron density diagnostics. H$\beta$ and \[O III\]$\lambda$5007 are outside the SITELLE filter coverage, further limiting ionization diagnostics." (SITELLE)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): VSF measurements are affected by LOS projection effects and unresolved kinematic substructure, which can create artificial flattenings (as demonstrated when comparing 1-component and 2-component fits in the SE filament).  
  > "Our results in the SE filaments (Section 3.2.1) support this: approximately 23% of spaxels exhibit multiple velocity components, and the $\sim$0.3 kpc flattening vanishes when the VSF is computed using only the first component." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Atmospheric seeing suppresses VSF power and steepens slopes at small scales, making VSFs unreliable below the seeing limit.  
  > "Therefore, we consider the VSFs unreliable below the seeing limit." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Finite window/aperture sizes and reduced pair statistics at large separations can introduce geometric distortions or artificial flattenings in VSFs.  
  > "This suggests the flattening may partly reflect a geometric distortion related to the filament’s cross-section within the aperture, rather than a genuine physical dissipation scale." (Interpretation of Localized VSFs)
- Limitation (authors): Increased noise and lower spectral resolution in SITELLE systematically elevate VSF amplitudes and prevent reliable velocity dispersion analysis.  
  > "Given these limits, velocity-dispersion results are not used in our analysis, though the map is provided for reference in Appendix 8." (SITELLE); "This may partly explain the systematically higher VSF amplitudes seen in SITELLE, likely driven by increased noise and its lower spectral resolution." (Limitations)
- Limitation (authors): Segmentation of MUSE filaments into subregions is visual and subjective, with ambiguity at filament junctions.  
  > "The segmentation is inherently subjective, relying on visual identification of morphological and kinematic continuity in projection." (Across the Central Filaments)
- Limitation (authors): Turbulent heating rate estimates assume perfect small-scale kinematic coupling between cool filaments and the hot ICM plasma, which cannot yet be verified with current X-ray spatial resolution.  
  > "We note that our estimated turbulent heating rate relies on the assumption that the kinematics of cool filaments and hot plasma are perfected coupled on small scales, which cannot be verified with existing X-ray telescopes yet due to their limited spatial resolution." (Turbulent Heating in the FE filament)
- Limitation (authors): Spaxel-level WHAN diagnostics are sensitive to H\alpha SNR and stellar continuum dilution.  
  > "To assess measurement impact, we examined $W_\alpha$ versus H$\alpha$ SNR, finding strong positive correlations (Pearson coefficients 0.93 for SE, 0.76 for FE). This sensitivity highlights limitations of spaxel-level WHAN diagnostics and suggests integrated-region analyses yield more reliable ionization classifications." (WHAN Diagram)
- Limitation (authors): The pGMCA algorithm may struggle to isolate small, low-surface-brightness clumps like the FE filament compared to extended structures.  
  > "Alternatively, the observed discrepancy may reflect limitations in isolating diffuse filamentary X-ray emission using pGMCA: as the algorithm can extract components based on their morphological features, it will more easily retrieve sources that are extended with a characteristic shape than small clumps – particularly in low surface brightness regions." (H\alpha – X-Ray Correlation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The claim of Type Ia supernovae driving turbulence at ~100-300 pc scales is weakened by the disappearance of the VSF flattening in the SE filament once the two LOS velocity components are separated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA CO(2-1) comparison relies entirely on pre-existing masked moment maps from Simionescu et al. (2018) rather than joint spectral fitting of the CO cube.
