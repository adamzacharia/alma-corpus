## Analysis recipe

1. **image the [CII] cube and continuum**: Pipeline-calibrated data re-imaged with CASA tclean (Briggs robust = 2.0); line-free channels give the continuum map; uvcontsub (first-order) removes continuum before the cube; channels averaged to 50 km/s  
  > "All images presented in this work were reconstructed using the `tclean` task down to $3\sigma$ level, with Briggs weighting (robust = 2.0) to maximize the point source sensitivity." (ALMA Observations); "which we subtracted in the $uv$ plane using the task `uvcontsub` with a first-order polynomial function, before making the line cube." (ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub
   - parameter: robust = 2.0; 50 km/s channels; 3 sigma clean depth  
  > "with Briggs weighting (robust = 2.0) to maximize the point source sensitivity" (ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **measure [CII] fluxes, redshifts and line widths in apertures**: Circular apertures (1.5'' diameter for C1, 1.0'' for C2 + Bridge and Tail, 6.0'' for the whole system) on the velocity-integrated map; single Gaussian fits to the spectra for redshift and FWHM  
  > "We used 1$''$.5 and 1$''$.0 diameter apertures to extract the emission properties of C1 and the other components, respectively." (Table 1 notes); "The redshifts were determined from single Gaussian fittings to the \[C II\] spectra" (Table 1 notes)
   - software: CASA, MIRIAD
   - parameter: aperture diameters 1.5'', 1.0'', 6.0''  
  > "The total \[C II\] line flux measured with a $6''.0$ diameter aperture is 6.6 Jy km s$^{-1}$" (From [CII] emission)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian to each spectrum; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "The redshifts were determined from single Gaussian fittings to the \[C II\] spectra" (Table 1 notes)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **derive [CII] luminosity, SFR and molecular gas mass**: L_[CII] from flux via Solomon & Vanden Bout; SFR_[CII] from De Looze 2014 calibration; M_H2 ~ 30 L_[CII] from Zanella 2018  
  > "Following (cite: 2005ARA&A..43..677S), the" (From [CII] emission); "we can estimate the SFR using the (cite: 2014A&A...568A..62D) calibration based on local H II/starburst galaxies" (From [CII] emission)
   - parameter: rest frequency 1900.5369 GHz; SFR calibration uncertainty factor of two  
  > "$\nu_{\rm rest}$ is the rest frequency of 1900.5369 GHz" (From [CII] emission); "with a factor of two calibration uncertainty" (From [CII] emission)
   - assumption: [CII] excited primarily by star formation (SFRs are upper limits if quasar contributes)  
  > "By further assuming that the \[C II\] line is excited primarily by star formation" (From [CII] emission)
   - assumption: Flat LCDM H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "with $H_0$ = 70 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$" (Our target: a pair of quasars at z = 6.05)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Noted that M_H2 and SFR_[CII] are not independent; compared with other z > 6 quasars  
  > "Hence, the \[C II\]-based $M_{\rm H2}$ and SFR$_{\rm [CII]}$ reported here are not necessarily independent." (From [CII] emission)
4. **measure the FIR continuum and derive dust properties**: 2D Gaussian fit with imfit for position and size; modified blackbody (optically thin) gives L_FIR (42.5-122.5 um), L_TIR (8-1000 um), M_dust; SFR_TIR from Murphy 2011  
  > "Our 2D Gaussian fit to the FIR continuum distribution using the CASA task `imfit` found a peak position of" (From FIR continuum emission); "assuming an optically thin modified black-body spectrum and values of dust temperature ($T_{\rm dust}$) and emissivity index $\beta$" (From FIR continuum emission)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: T_dust = 35 K, beta = 1.6, kappa = 0.77 (850 um/lambda)^beta cm2/g  
  > "Continuum Emission ($T_{\rm dust}$ = 35 K, $\beta$ = 1.6" (Table 1); "we assume $T_{\rm dust} = 35$ K, a typical value in local LIRGs (cite: 2012ApJS..203....9U)" (From FIR continuum emission)
   - assumption: T_dust = 35 K (local LIRG value, lower than the canonical 47 K for luminous quasars); beta = 1.6; CMB correction applied; TIR emission entirely from star formation; Kroupa IMF  
  > "However, it seems inappropriate to use such high $T_{\rm dust}$ for the galaxies likely in an earlier evolutionary phase" (From FIR continuum emission); "By assuming that the TIR continuum emission originates solely from star-forming regions" (From FIR continuum emission)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit (imfit); no dust SED fit  
  > "Our 2D Gaussian fit to the FIR continuum distribution using the CASA task `imfit` found a peak position of" (From FIR continuum emission)
   - validation: Sensitivity to T_dust: T_dust = 47 K would raise L_TIR and SFR by ~2.5x; size measurement S/N above the threshold of 10  
  > "if we adopt $T_{\rm dust} = 47$ K, the resultant $L_{\rm TIR}$ and SFR will become $\sim 2.5\times$ higher than the values in Table" (Footnote 2); "This high S/N is well above the threshold of 10 required to make a robust size measurement" (From FIR continuum emission)
5. **map global velocity field and estimate dynamical masses**: Moment-1 (and moment-2) maps with immoments and 3.5 sigma clipping; beam-deconvolved imfit sizes give inclination from axis ratio; thin-disk M_dyn with D = 1.5 a_maj and V_circ = 0.75 FWHM/sin i  
  > "generated with the CASA task `immoments` with $3.5\sigma$ clipping to avoid noisy pixels." (Global gas dynamics); "The disk size is approximated as $D = 1.5 \times a_{\rm maj}$ to account for spatially extended low-level emission, and the circular velocity as $V_{\rm circ} = 0.75 \times {\rm FWHM}_{\rm [CII]}/\sin i$" (Global gas dynamics)
   - software: CASA, immoments, imfit
   - parameter: C1: 0.96'' x 0.68'', i = 45 deg; C2 + Bridge: 0.85'' x 0.35'', i = 66 deg  
  > "$a_{\rm maj} \times a_{\rm min} = (0''.96 \pm 0''.09) \times (0''.68 \pm 0''.07)$ and $i = 45\ensuremath{^\circ}$ for C1" (Global gas dynamics)
   - assumption: [CII] emission arises from thin rotating disks; inclination from deconvolved axis ratio  
  > "Here, the \[C II\] emission of these galaxies is assumed to be mainly originating from thin rotating disks." (Global gas dynamics); "The inclination angles ($i$) of the disks are determined from the axis ratios of the beam-deconvolved Gaussian fits to the emitting regions." (Global gas dynamics)
   - fitting: No kinematic model fit; qualitative tidal-interaction description only  
  > "Note that our attempt here is to explain the global behavior in a qualitative manner, rather than to fit the data with a physical model." (Global gas dynamics)
   - validation: M_dyn compared with M_H2 (comparable)  
  > "we found that their $M_{\rm dyn}$ are comparable to $M_{\rm H2}$" (Global gas dynamics)
6. **test for and quantify a [CII] outflow at C1**: Fit the C1 spectrum (single synthesized beam) with single and double Gaussians (second centroid fixed to first); broad wing treated as outflow; M_out from L_[CII],broad with X_C+, T_ex; outflow rate M_out V_out / R_out; total rate = 3x neutral rate; mass loading factor = total outflow rate / SFR  
  > "we next fit the data with a double Gaussian model (Figure [4](#fig4)c) by fixing the centroid of the second Gaussian to that of the first Gaussian" (Potential indication of AGN feedback); "we obtain a neutral outflow rate of $\dot{M}_{\rm out, neutral} = M_{\rm out}V_{\rm out}/R_{\rm out} = 229 \pm 73~M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: X_C+ = 1.4e-4, T_ex = 200 K, high density limit (n >> n_crit ~ 3e3 cm^-3), R_out = 1.5 kpc, V_out = FWHM/2  
  > "we estimated $M_{\rm out} = (5.6 \pm 1.5) \times 10^8~M_\odot$ for $X_{\rm C^+} = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$ and $T_{\rm ex} = 200$ K" (Potential indication of AGN feedback); "This gives the characteristic size (spatial extent) of $R_{\rm out} = 1.5$ kpc." (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
   - assumption: Wing is due to outflow (symmetric wing disfavors unseen companions); 70% of [CII] from neutral PDRs; constant outflow velocity  
  > "We assumed that 70% of the \[C II\] emission originates from neutral photodissociation regions (cite: 1997ARA&A..35..179H)." (Potential indication of AGN feedback); "The nearly symmetric appearance of the broad wing at C1 disfavors nearby (unseen) companions as its physical origin" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
   - fitting: Gaussian (chi-square) fits of single and double Gaussian to the C1 spectrum; sampler and priors not stated  
  > "We first fit the spectrum with a single Gaussian model" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
   - validation: chi2/d.o.f. 85.5/56 = 1.53 (single) vs 42.7/54 = 0.79 (double) over +/-1500 km/s; double Gaussian statistically preferred  
  > "the single Gaussian fit returns $\chi^2$/d.o.f. = 85.5/56 = 1.53 whereas the double Gaussian fit returns $\chi^2$/d.o.f. = 42.7/54 = 0.79" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)

## Qualitative findings

- A smooth global [CII] velocity gradient runs from C2 through the Bridge and C1 to the Tail, indicating a tidally interacting pair that will merge soon  
  > "There is a clear velocity gradient from C2 to the Tail through the Bridge and C1" (A galaxy merger caught in the act)
- The C2 quasar position is offset from the nearest [CII] local maximum (Bridge), and C2 is blueshifted in [CII] relative to Ly-alpha by about 400 km/s  
  > "However, it is evident that the bright $z$-band emitting region is offset from this Bridge." (A galaxy merger caught in the act)
- Dust-obscured SFR (IR-based) is much lower than [CII]-based SFR, suggesting substantial unobscured star formation or higher T_dust  
  > "We also found that there is a significant discrepancy between SFR$_{\rm [CII]}$ and SFR$_{\rm TIR}$ particularly at C2 + Bridge and the Tail." (From FIR continuum emission)
- The pair is a candidate progenitor of the most luminous quasars  
  > "That is, this pair system may well correspond to a progenitor of the most luminous quasars in the early universe." (Discussion and Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Faint, earlier-phase interacting quasars are expected to be progenitors of luminous quasars; the HSC survey finds such low-luminosity quasars  
  > "Thus, progenitors in the earlier interaction phase, that will evolve to the luminous quasars currently observed, are anticipated to be much fainter." (Introduction)
- Why this method: T_dust = 35 K chosen instead of 47 K because the galaxies are likely in an earlier evolutionary phase; robust = 2.0 chosen to maximize point-source sensitivity  
  > "However, it seems inappropriate to use such high $T_{\rm dust}$ for the galaxies likely in an earlier evolutionary phase" (From FIR continuum emission); "to maximize the point source sensitivity" (ALMA Observations)
- Alternative: Unseen companions as the origin of the broad wing at C1 are disfavored by its nearly symmetric appearance  
  > "The nearly symmetric appearance of the broad wing at C1 disfavors nearby (unseen) companions as its physical origin" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
- Alternative: Canonical T_dust = 47 K for luminous quasars considered; adopting it would make L_TIR and SFR ~2.5x higher  
  > "if we adopt $T_{\rm dust} = 47$ K, the resultant $L_{\rm TIR}$ and SFR will become $\sim 2.5\times$ higher than the values in Table" (Footnote 2)
- Alternative: Violent merger shocks versus bi-conical AGN outflows for the high dispersion regions; authors favor AGN-driven outflow speculation  
  > "we speculate that this is a hint of two-directional (e.g., bi-conical) AGN-driven outflows, rather than violent shocks due to the merger of galaxies" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only statistical errors shown; calibration uncertainty ~10% absolute flux  
  > "In this paper, we show only statistical errors unless mentioned otherwise." (ALMA Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Dust results are sensitive to assumed T_dust and beta  
  > "We emphasize that these results are sensitive to the assumed values of $T_{\rm dust}$ and $\beta$" (From FIR continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Host masses are hard to estimate because of the highly dynamic nature of the system; M_H2 and SFR from [CII] are not independent  
  > "The actual masses of the host galaxies of C1 and C2, and their ratio, are hard to estimate at this moment given the highly dynamic nature of the system." (Global gas dynamics); "Hence, the \[C II\]-based $M_{\rm H2}$ and SFR$_{\rm [CII]}$ reported here are not necessarily independent." (From [CII] emission)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow evidence is tentative and needs higher angular resolution and sensitivity  
  > "further higher angular resolution, higher sensitivity observations are definitely required to confirm the presence of \[C II\] outflows" (Potential indication of AGN feedback)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow inference rests on a single-beam spectrum with a broad wing; no uncertainty on chi-square significance and no test against a bulk-rotation or merger-shock origin beyond qualitative argument
- Caveat (inferred, ours): M_dyn uses crude thin-disk geometry on a clearly disturbed merging system, so inclination and V_circ are poorly defined
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SFR_[CII] and M_H2 from [CII] are calibrated on other populations and are not independent; the ~3-5x offset noted for other quasars is not applied
- Caveat (inferred, ours): T_dust = 35 K is assumed, not fit, so L_TIR, M_dust and SFR_TIR are uncertain by factors of a few
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The outflow mass assumes T_ex = 200 K, high-density limit and a 70% neutral fraction without sensitivity test
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Aperture photometry choices (1.5'' vs 1.0'' diameter) are not tested for contamination between neighbouring components
