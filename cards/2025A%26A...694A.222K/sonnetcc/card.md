## Analysis recipe

1. **Define apertures and measure line profiles and moment-0 maps**: Extract profiles in 5 arcsec circles, measure zero-intensity line widths of C17O/C18O, build 13CO moment-0 maps over C18O velocity range, choose 5 or 7 arcsec circular apertures (non-circular 3-sigma contour for M1-92; rectangular for Hen 3-1475 high-velocity gas)  
  > "To determine the sizes of the apertures for measuring the" (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements); "If the contour marking emission three times above the root mean square" (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements); "Hence, to obtain the spectra of the high-velocity component of Hen 3-1475, we used a rectangular aperture oriented along the axis of the high-velocity emission as described below." (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Aperture diameters 5 arcsec (GLMP 950, Hen 3-1475) and 7 arcsec (GLMP 953, AFGL 5385, HD 187885)  
  > "Following this procedure, we obtain aperture diameters of" (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements)
   - assumption: Circular apertures adequate because sources are nearly circular  
  > "We use circular apertures of similar size when possible for simplicity because the uncertainties introduced by the aperture shape are small for sources that are nearly circular." (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum flux density, size and position**: Integrate continuum within the same apertures; fit 2D Gaussian convolved with beam in the image plane to get beam-deconvolved FWHM  
  > "were obtained by integrating the emission within the same apertures used to extract the line profiles" (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements); "The FWHM sizes of the continuum emission reported below were measured using the Interactive 2D fitting tool in CASA to fit a Gaussian function convolved with the beam to the maps in the image plane." (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: Gaussian fit with CASA interactive 2D fitting tool; sampler/priors not applicable  
  > "The FWHM sizes of the continuum emission reported below were measured using the Interactive 2D fitting tool in CASA to fit a Gaussian function convolved with the beam to the maps in the image plane." (4.1 Methodology for line and continuum measurements)
   - validation: Compared with single-dish continuum and line fluxes to check no flux is lost (e.g. M1-92, Hen 3-1475, HD 187885)  
  > "Hence, no loss of flux is caused by the relatively large extent of the continuum emission." (4.7 M1-92)
3. **Derive 17O/18O ratios from C17O/C18O line fluxes**: Convert flux ratio to abundance ratio accounting for Einstein A, frequency, partition functions and level energies at Tex = 10 K (ratio ~0.95 times the flux ratio)  
  > "causes the derived abundance ratio to be only" (5.1 17O/18O isotopic ratios)
   - parameter: Tex = 10 K, optically thin emission  
  > "For these calculations, we assume optically thin emission and an excitation temperature," (5 Analysis); "of 10 K for all three isotopologues of CO." (5 Analysis)
   - assumption: Same excitation for both molecules; ratio virtually independent of Tex  
  > "Hence, the isotopic ratios we obtain are virtually independent of the assumed excitation temperature as long as the excitation of both molecules share the same value." (5.1 17O/18O isotopic ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with literature AGB and post-AGB 17O/18O samples and with a previous M1-92 value  
  > "Hence, the values we obtain for Hen 3-1475 (1.29) and M1-92 (1.8) are typical for O-rich AGB stars." (6.1.1 Comparison to other sources)
4. **Derive circumstellar gas masses from C18O**: M_gas = M_H/0.7 with M_H from C18O J=2-1 flux, optically thin LTE at Tex=10 K, f(C18O)=8.5e-7 (2.1e-7 for HD 187885)  
  > "Since the abundances of the CO isotopologues at the end of the AGB vary significantly as a function of stellar mass, we selected the isotopologue with the smallest expected change for this calculation," (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances)
   - parameter: f(C18O) = 8.5e-7 relative to H nuclei; hydrogen mass fraction 0.7  
  > "The factor 0.7 corresponds to the approximate fraction of the total mass accounted for by hydrogen at the end of the AGB (cite: Karakas2016)." (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances)
   - assumption: Tex = 10 K; adopted distances; C18O abundance from Karakas & Lugaro 2016 models  
  > "relative to the number of hydrogen nuclei (cite: Karakas2016)" (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainty budget: Tex change 10 to 20 K lowers mass ~20%; distance uncertainty assumed 30%; total factor 1.7  
  > "Increasing the excitation temperature from 10 K to 20 K causes the derived masses to decrease by approximately 20%, while increasing it from 10 K to 40 K causes the derived masses to increase by about 10%." (6.4.1 Uncertainties)
5. **Derive kinematic ages and recent mass-loss rates**: Use 3-sigma C18O contours for size and gas mass; kinematic age from region size, adopted distance and expansion velocity from zero-intensity C18O width; mass-loss rate = mass within the contour / kinematic age  
  > "we determine a recent mass-loss rate for each source." (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances); "These mass-loss rates represent lower limits to the maximum mass-loss rates experienced by each source recently" (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive 13CO abundances consistent with C18O masses**: Analogous expression to the mass equation to find the 13CO abundance relative to H that reproduces the C18O-based gas mass  
  > "that would make the estimated gas masses consistent with those derived based on the" (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared 13CO/C18O ratios in central beam vs 3-sigma regions to test dissociation-radius effects  
  > "Based on these considerations, we conclude that, if present, the effect of different dissociation radii on the line ratios seems to be relatively small" (6.2 13CO and C18O abundances)
7. **Infer initial masses from 17O/18O with stellar evolution models**: Compare measured ratios with MONASH and FRUITY model predictions at solar (and Z=0.0035/0.0028 for HD 187885) metallicity  
  > "The observed isotopic ratios imply remarkably similar initial masses for a given metallicity for the two models." (6.1.2 Derived initial masses)
   - software: MONASH, FRUITY
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Agreement of the two model sets; M1-92 compared with the literature ratio and mass  
  > "The observed isotopic ratios imply remarkably similar initial masses for a given metallicity for the two models." (6.1.2 Derived initial masses)
8. **Check luminosities and distances implied by initial masses**: Take maximum post-AGB luminosity from Miller Bertolami (2016) models for the inferred initial mass and rescale distances from adopted luminosities  
  > "we scaled the distance for each sources based on the luminosities adopted from the literature" (6.1.3 Implications for luminosities and distances)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Compare kinematic ages with post-AGB evolution timescales**: Compare C18O kinematic ages with tau_tr from Miller Bertolami (2016) and Vassiliadis & Wood (1994) for the four sources with spectral types  
  > "it is clear that these four sources had an evolution faster than predicted by models by a factor of a few, at least." (6.1.4 Implications for post-AGB lifetimes)
   - assumption: Kinematic ages are upper limits to post-AGB duration  
  > "represent an upper limit for the duration of the post-AGB evolution of these sources up to now." (6.1.4 Implications for post-AGB lifetimes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Hen 3-1475 shows a point-symmetric high-velocity molecular outflow (first detection in molecular lines above 50 km/s), interleaved with the HST ionised outflow and entrained by it  
  > "To our knowledge, this is the first time these components with projected expansion velocities" (4.5 Hen 3-1475); "Hence, the velocity of the molecular gas is a factor of roughly two lower than that of the ionised gas at a similar distance from the star, indicating that most of the molecular material is entrained by a faster outflow." (6.6 High-velocity outflow of Hen 3-1475)
- HD 187885 shows no lines besides CO isotopologues, possibly due to low initial metallicity  
  > "The lack of lines in the spectrum of HD 187885 might be caused by its low initial metallicity." (4 Observational results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Only six of twenty selected very red post-AGB sources were observed within the project, covering the C17O, C18O and 13CO J=2-1 lines in one tuning  
  > "but only six were successfully observed with ALMA within this project." (2.1 Sample selection)
- Why this method: C18O chosen for gas masses because its abundance changes least during the AGB  
  > "Since the abundances of the CO isotopologues at the end of the AGB vary significantly as a function of stellar mass, we selected the isotopologue with the smallest expected change for this calculation," (5.2 Circumstellar gas masses, recent mass-loss rates, and 13CO abundances)
- Alternative: Dissociation-radius differences between 13CO and C18O as a bias on 13CO abundances considered and judged small  
  > "Based on these considerations, we conclude that, if present, the effect of different dissociation radii on the line ratios seems to be relatively small" (6.2 13CO and C18O abundances)
- Alternative: O-rich nature of Hen 3-1475 and M1-92 could be explained by higher initial metallicity instead of interrupted evolution  
  > "Alternatively, these sources might have an initial metallicity higher than solar" (6.1.2 Derived initial masses)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Derived initial masses are fairly uncertain because of model and initial-composition uncertainties  
  > "we note that the initial masses we derive are fairly uncertain both because of uncertainties in the model calculations and in the initial composition of these sources." (6.1.2 Derived initial masses)
- Limitation (authors): Circumstellar masses uncertain by a factor of 1.7  
  > "we find the circumstellar masses to be uncertain by a factor of 1.7." (6.4.1 Uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample prevents testing O-rich versus C-rich differences  
  > "However, the small number of sources in our sample prevents us from investigating this difference further." (2.1 Sample selection)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed Tex=10 K and optically thin assumption for all lines and sources; 13CO shows tau>~1 by the authors' own estimate
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distances (30% assumed) enter mass as d^2 and drive luminosity consistency checks
- Caveat (inferred, ours): C18O abundance assumed from solar-scaled models; low-metallicity HD 187885 initial isotopic ratios assumed solar
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Aperture choices (5/7 arcsec, 3-sigma contours) bias masses and kinematic ages; expansion velocity from zero-intensity widths
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model-to-initial-mass mapping done by hand from published tables, not reproducible numerically
