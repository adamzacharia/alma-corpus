## Qualitative findings

- The host galaxy of GRB 090423 is not detected in ALMA Band 6 or Spitzer IRAC 3.6 um.  
  > "The host remains undetected to $3\sigma$ limits of $F_
u(222\,{m GHz})\lesssim 33$ $\mu$Jy and $F_
u(3.6\,{m \mu m})\lesssim 81$ nJy." (Abstract)
- The FIR luminosity limit is ~20 times fainter than local ULIRG Arp 220 and comparable to starburst M 82, and represents the deepest FIR limit for any spectroscopically-confirmed galaxy at z >= 4 to date.  
  > "The FIR limit is about 20 times fainter than the luminosity of the local ULIRG Arp 220, and comparable to the local starburst M 82." (Abstract); "and show that the limit on the host of GRB 090423 is the deepest to date compared to any published observations." (Conclusions)
- GRB host galaxies at z >= 4 with measured ISM metallicities from afterglow spectroscopy are attractive targets for ALMA studies of high-redshift obscured star formation.  
  > "Still, looking forward we anticipate that ALMA observations of GRB host galaxies will be highly desirable and productive, especially targeting spectroscopically-confirmed hosts at $z\gtrsim 4$ with detailed ISM metallicity measurements" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GRBs provide precise spectroscopic redshifts and ISM metallicity measurements independent of host brightness, making GRB 090423 at z=8.23 a unique target to probe obscured star formation 630 Myr after the Big Bang.  
  > "In particular, spectroscopy of the optical/near-IR afterglow emission can provide an unambiguous and precise spectroscopic redshift, as well as a measurement of the host galaxy interstellar medium (ISM) properties *independent of the host galaxy brightness*" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Using Ganymede when possible and Ceres to manually calibrate the gain calibrator J0854+2006 resolved epoch-to-epoch variations caused by uncertain asteroid flux models.  
  > "We found some minor variations in the resulting fluxes of J 0854+2006 between the observing blocks, likely due to the use of asteroids, which have uncertain flux models. We therefore used Ganymede when possible and Ceres to determine and manually set the flux for J 0854+2006 in each epoch." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
- Alternative: Direct flux calibration using asteroid models alone was rejected due to variations caused by uncertain asteroid flux models; Ganymede was preferred.  
  > "We found some minor variations in the resulting fluxes of J 0854+2006 between the observing blocks, likely due to the use of asteroids, which have uncertain flux models. We therefore used Ganymede when possible and Ceres to determine and manually set the flux for J 0854+2006 in each epoch." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): If high-redshift galaxies have SEDs like local dwarf I Zw 18 or the GRB 980425 host with weak FIR emission relative to UV, ALMA limits are much less constraining than HST limits, relaxing the SFR upper limit to <= 100 M_sun yr^-1.  
  > "On the other hand, for the I Zw 18 template, which has weak FIR emission, the *HST* limits place a much more stringent constraint than the ALMA data, by about a factor of 600."
