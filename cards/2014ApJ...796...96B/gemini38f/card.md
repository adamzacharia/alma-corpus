## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image the ALMA Band 6 continuum data**: Calibrated gain, bandpass, and absolute flux manually setting J 0854+2006 flux via Ganymede/Ceres, then imaged combined continuum using natural weighting  
  > "We performed data calibration and imaging using the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA)." (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array); "The resulting combined continuum map (natural weighting without primary beam correction) has an rms noise of 11 muJy beam^{-1} for a synthesized beam size (full-width at half maximum) of 1''.0 x 0''.80" (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Natural weighting, beam 1''.0 x 0''.80, center frequency ~222 GHz  
  > "ALMA band 6 (222 GHz) observations" (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array); "The resulting combined continuum map (natural weighting without primary beam correction) has an rms noise of 11 muJy beam^{-1} for a synthesized beam size (full-width at half maximum) of 1''.0 x 0''.80" (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - assumption: 10% absolute flux scale uncertainty  
  > "The overall uncertainty in the absolute flux calibration is about 10%." (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of calibrator flux across observing blocks  
  > "We found some minor variations in the resulting fluxes of J 0854+2006 between the observing blocks, likely due to the use of asteroids, which have uncertain flux models. We therefore used Ganymede when possible and Ceres to determine and manually set the flux for J 0854+2006 in each epoch." (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
2. **Measure continuum flux density limit at GRB afterglow position**: Measured point-source flux density at the known afterglow coordinates and derived 3-sigma upper limit  
  > "No source is detected at the location of GRB 090423 (with a flux density at the source location of -4 +/- 11 muJy), leading an upper limit of F_nu(222 GHz) <= 33 muJy (3sigma; Table 1)." (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Position R.A. = 09h55m33.29s, Decl. = +18°08'57.8'' (J2000), 3-sigma detection threshold  
  > "The position of the afterglow is R.A. = 09h55m33.29s, Decl. = +18°08'57.8'' (J2000) with an uncertainty of about 0''.1 in each coordinate." (Observations); "leading an upper limit of F_nu(222 GHz) <= 33 muJy (3sigma; Table 1)." (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - assumption: Host galaxy is coincident with GRB afterglow position  
  > "No source is detected at the location of GRB 090423 (with a flux density at the source location of -4 +/- 11 muJy)" (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive IR luminosity and obscured SFR upper limits**: Modeled dust emission using modified blackbody SED and template comparison (Rieke et al. 2009; Arp 220, M 82) to compute L_IR(8-1000 um), then converted to SFR_IR  
  > "Using the standard modified blackbody SED of dust emission, with a dust temperature range of T_dust approx 30-50 K and beta approx 1.5-2 (e.g., (cite: sss+14)), the ALMA non-detection corresponds to an upper limit on the integrated IR luminosity of L_IR(8-1000 mu m) <= (2-5) x 10^10 L_odot" (3 Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass); "The limit on the integrated IR luminosity corresponds to an upper bound on the obscured star formation rate (cite: ken98; cwh+10), of SFR_IR <= 3-5 M_odot yr^{-1}." (3 Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
   - parameter: T_dust = 30-50 K, beta = 1.5-2, T_CMB(z=8.23) approx 25 K  
  > "with a dust temperature range of T_dust approx 30-50 K and beta approx 1.5-2 (e.g., (cite: sss+14)), the ALMA non-detection corresponds to an upper limit on the integrated IR luminosity of L_IR(8-1000 mu m) <= (2-5) x 10^10 L_odot; the lower bound on the blackbody temperature is set by the cosmic microwave background temperature T_CMB(z=8.23) approx 25 K" (3 Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
   - assumption: Kennicutt (1998) / Calzetti et al. (2010) SFR_IR calibrations  
  > "The limit on the integrated IR luminosity corresponds to an upper bound on the obscured star formation rate (cite: ken98; cwh+10), of SFR_IR <= 3-5 M_odot yr^{-1}." (3 Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison with Rieke et al. (2009) star-forming galaxy templates  
  > "The IR luminosity upper limit is thus a few times lower than the scale for a LIRG, and agrees well with the comparison to the (cite: raw+09) templates." (3 Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
4. **Derive stellar mass upper limit from Spitzer and HST photometry**: Bruzual & Charlot (2003) population synthesis modeling fitting Spitzer 3.6 um and HST upper limits  
  > "Utilizing the (cite: bc03) stellar population synthesis models with a constant star formation rate, a Salpeter IMF, a metallicity of 0.2 Z_odot, and no dust extinction, we find that for a stellar population age of 10-100 Myr (typical of GRB host galaxies; (cite: sgl09; lb10)) the limit on the host stellar mass is M_* <= (1-5) x 10^7 M_odot." (3 Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)

## Qualitative findings

- The host galaxy of GRB 090423 at z=8.23 remains undetected in 222 GHz ALMA continuum and 3.6 um Spitzer imaging.  
  > "The host remains undetected to 3sigma limits of F_nu(222 GHz) <= 33 muJy and F_nu(3.6 mu m) <= 81 nJy." (abstract)
- The ALMA FIR luminosity limit is about 20 times fainter than Arp 220 and comparable to M 82, placing the most constraining FIR limit on a z > 4 galaxy to date.  
  > "The FIR limit is about 20 times fainter than the luminosity of the local ULIRG Arp 220, and comparable to the local starburst M 82." (abstract); "we compare our millimeter observations to those of field galaxies at z >= 4 (Lyman break galaxies, Lya emitters, and submillimeter galaxies), and find that our limit on the FIR luminosity is the most constraining to date" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GRBs provide precise spectroscopic redshifts and ISM metallicity measurements independent of host brightness, and GRB 090423 at z=8.23 is the most distant spectroscopically confirmed burst.  
  > "In particular, spectroscopy of the optical/near-IR afterglow emission can provide an unambiguous and precise spectroscopic redshift, as well as a measurement of the host galaxy interstellar medium (ISM) properties independent of the host galaxy brightness" (Introduction); "Here, we present deep ALMA observations of the host galaxy of the most distant spectroscopically-confirmed burst to date – GRB 090423 at z=8.23 (cite: sdc+09; tfl+09)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Band 6 continuum probes the rest-frame ~145 um FIR dust peak at z=8.23, reaching sensitivity below LIRG luminosities.  
  > "The observations are aimed at reaching a rest-frame FIR luminosity level below that of a luminous infrared galaxy (LIRG: L_{IR}=10^{11} L_odot)." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Using asteroid models for flux calibration led to variations between blocks, so Ganymede and Ceres were selected to manually set gain calibrator flux.  
  > "We found some minor variations in the resulting fluxes of J 0854+2006 between the observing blocks, likely due to the use of asteroids, which have uncertain flux models. We therefore used Ganymede when possible and Ceres to determine and manually set the flux for J 0854+2006 in each epoch." (2.1 Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): If high-redshift galaxies have SEDs resembling low-metallicity dwarf galaxies like I Zw 18 or GRB 980425 host, FIR limits relax significantly (SFR_IR <= 100 M_sun yr^-1), requiring much deeper observations.  
  > "scaling the SED of the low metallicity dwarf galaxy I Zw 18 relaxes the star formation rate upper limit to <= 100 M_odot yr^{-1}." (Conclusions); "On the other hand, if typical high redshift galaxies have SEDs similar to the local dwarf I Zw 18 or the host galaxy of GRB 980425, then much deeper ALMA observations will be required to detect their FIR emission." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainty in dust temperature and emissivity index at z=8.23 introduces a factor of 2-3 uncertainty in the derived L_IR and SFR_IR upper limits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar mass upper limits strongly depend on unconstrained age and extinction assumptions
