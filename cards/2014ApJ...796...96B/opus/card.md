   - software: CASA
   - parameter: flux calibration uncertainty about 10%  
  > "The overall uncertainty in the absolute flux calibration is about $10\%$." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **image continuum and measure flux limit**: combined continuum map with natural weighting; measure flux at GRB position; 3 sigma upper limit  
  > "No source is detected at the location of GRB 090423 (with a flux density at the source location of $-4\pm 11$ $\mu$Jy), leading an upper limit of $F_\nu(222\,{\rm
  GHz})\lesssim 33$ $\mu$Jy ($3\sigma$" (Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: natural weighting, no primary beam correction  
  > "natural weighting without primary beam correction" (Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Spitzer 3.6 um photometry limit**: GOODS pipeline reduction, mosaic on 0.4'' pixels, split-image noise map, 1.2'' aperture photometry with aperture correction  
  > "We analyzed the data using the Great Observatories Origins Deep Survey (GOODS) pipeline, including corrections for bright star image artifacts (muxbleed and pulldown) with dark and sky background subtraction." (Spitzer Space Telescope); "Photometry in a $1.2"$ radius aperture yields a $3.6$ $\mu$m flux density of $26\pm 27$ nJy, after aperture corrections." (Spitzer Space Telescope)
   - software: GOODS pipeline
   - parameter: 0.4'' x 0.4'' pixel grid  
  > "created a combined mosaic of the individual frames on a $0.4''\times 0.4''$ pixel grid" (Spitzer Space Telescope)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: split-image noise about 2x higher than pixel statistics; no confusion consistent with HST  
  > "The resulting uncertainty is about 2 times higher than that derived from pure pixel statistics in the vicinity of the source." (Spitzer Space Telescope)
4. **constrain IR luminosity via SED templates and modified blackbody**: compare limits with redshifted local galaxy SEDs and Rieke templates; modified blackbody integration over 8-1000 um  
  > "the ALMA non-detection corresponds to an upper limit on the integrated IR luminosity of $L_{\rm IR}(8-1000\, {\rm \mu m})\lesssim (2-5)\times 10^{10}$ L$_\odot$" (Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)

  > "the lower bound on the blackbody temperature is set by the cosmic microwave background temperature $T_{\rm CMB}(z=8.23)\approx
25$ K" (Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)

## Qualitative findings

- Host is undetected in ALMA and Spitzer data  
  > "The host galaxy of GRB 090423 remains undetected in our ALMA and *Spitzer* observations." (Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
- For I Zw 18-like or GRB 980425-host-like SEDs, HST limits are more constraining than ALMA  
  > "for the I Zw 18 template, which has weak FIR emission, the *HST* limits place a much more stringent constraint than the ALMA data, by about a factor of 600." (Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
- Himiko and IOK-1 give more constraining FIR/UV ratios despite shallower FIR limits  
  > "Thus, in terms of the ratio of FIR to UV luminosity, the limits for Himiko and IOK-1 are more constraining than those for the host of GRB 090423." (Comparison to Millimeter Observations of Field Galaxies at $z\gtrsim 4$)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: GRB hosts give spectroscopic redshifts independent of host brightness; ALMA reaches few Msun/yr SFR sensitivity  
  > "Thus, spectroscopically-confirmed GRB host galaxies at high redshift provide an attractive sample for multi-wavelength follow-up observations." (Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Multiple SED templates (Arp 220, M 82, Sd SWIRE, I Zw 18, GRB 980425 host) yield different relative constraining power  
  > "For an Sd galaxy template from the Spitzer Wide-area InfraRed Extragalactic survey (SWIRE) library, the ALMA, *HST*, and *Spitzer* data all place comparable limits on the host SED." (Host Galaxy Properties: IR Luminosity, Obscured Star Formation Rate, and Stellar Mass)
- How they decided it was enough: Depth chosen to reach below LIRG luminosity  
  > "The observations are aimed at reaching a rest-frame FIR luminosity level below that of a luminous infrared galaxy" (Introduction)
- Limitation (authors): Asteroid flux models uncertain causing flux variations between blocks  
  > "We found some minor variations in the resulting fluxes of J 0854+2006 between the observing blocks, likely due to the use of asteroids, which have uncertain flux models." (Atacama Large Millimeter Array)
- Limitation (authors): If high-z galaxies resemble I Zw 18, much deeper ALMA data required  
  > "if typical high redshift galaxies have SEDs similar to the local dwarf I Zw 18 or the host galaxy of GRB 980425, then much deeper ALMA observations will be required to detect their FIR emission" (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): L_IR limit depends strongly on assumed dust temperature and SED shape; CMB heating/contrast effects at z=8.23 treated only via T lower bound
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cosmology and SFR calibration not stated, limiting exact reproduction of luminosities
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-object non-detection limits generalization
