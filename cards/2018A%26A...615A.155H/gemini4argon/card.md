A kinematical model of the nuclear disk involving tilted rings); "The second panel of Fig. 19 provides the absolute value of the DV RA parameter, where we set V RAD = 0." (4.6.2. A kinematical model of the nuclear disk involving tilted rings)

## Qualitative findings

- Instead of a central molecular hole or ring, NGC 4945 hosts a rotating nuclear disk (projected size 10'' x 2'', r ≈ 100 pc) with an unresolved bright central core of size <= 2'' (<= 40 pc).  
  > "Spectral line imaging demonstrates the presence of a rotating nuclear disk of projected size 10 [′′] ×2 [′′] reaching out to a galactocentric radius of r≈100 pc with position angle PA = 45 [◦] ±2 [◦], inclination i = 75 [◦] ±2 [◦] and an unresolved bright central core of size <∼2 [′′] ." (abstract); "Instead of a void we find a central region with strongly enhanced integrated emission." (4.1.1. The nuclear core)
- All observed molecular lines except CH3C2H 5_0->4_0 exhibit absorption against the nuclear 3 mm continuum near systemic velocities (≈500 - 660 km s^-1); only transitions with low critical density (n_crit <= 10^4 cm^-3) lack absorption.  
  > "All our molecular lines, with the notable exception of CH 3 C 2 H, show significant absorption near the systemic velocity (≈571 km s [−][1] ), within a range of ≈500 – 660 km s [−][1] . Apparently, only molecular transitions with low critical H 2 -density (n crit ∼ [<] 10 [4] cm [−][3] ) do not show absorption." (abstract)
- Outside the nuclear disk near systemic velocities, two spiral-like arms (> 15'' or > 285 pc) are connected to the nuclear disk by a deprojected ≈0.6 kpc gaseous bar seen almost ends-on (PA ≈ 90 deg), driving gas inflow from r ≈ 300 pc to r ≈ 100 pc.  
  > "Near the systemic velocity but outside the nuclear disk, both HCN J=1→0 and CS J=2→1 delineate molecular arms of length [>] ∼15 [′′] (∼ [>] 285 pc) on opposite sides of the dynamical center. These are connected by a (deprojected) ≈0.6 kpc sized molecular bridge, likely a dense gaseous bar seen almost ends-on" (abstract); "To summarize, we propose inflow of gas through the bar from galactocentric radii of ≈300 to ≈100pc toward the nuclear disk." (4.7. The bar)
- The orientation (PA and inclination) of NGC 4945 remains remarkably aligned across all scales from the >20 kpc outer disk down to the 200 pc nuclear disk and the 1 pc H2O megamaser disk.  
  > "It is remarkable that on all scales, from the extended disk encompassing >20 kpc to the H 2 O megamaser disk highlighting the central parsec, position angle and inclination appear not greatly changed." (5. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NGC 4945 is a prominent nearby starburst and Seyfert 2 galaxy that lacked interferometric studies of high-density molecular tracers due to its southern location; ALMA Band 3 provides higher sensitivity and ≈2'' resolution to check the SEST 14N/15N ratio and bridge the gap between large-scale structure and the sub-pc maser disk.  
  > "Although this Seyfert 2 galaxy has an infrared luminosity a few times that of the Milky Way, it nevertheless suffers from a lack of interferometric studies of molecular high density tracers, a consequence of its southern location." (1. Introduction); "it nevertheless suffers from a lack of interferometric studies of molecular high density tracers, a consequence of its southern location." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: CH3C2H 5_0->4_0 was selected for kinematic analysis because it is free of absorption and emission outside the nuclear disk; 3D tilted-ring modeling with TiRiFiC was chosen because high inclination and beam smearing along the minor axis render 2D velocity field fits unreliable.  
  > "In view of the line contamination by absorption and, likely even more important, by gas arising from outside the nuclear disk (Sects. 3.2.2, 4.1.3, and 4.7), we thus prefer to choose the CH 3 C 2 H 5 0 →4 0 line." (4.6.1. Observational constraints); "As, however, the disk is observed to be at high inclination and the point spread function’s half power width is large compared to the disk’s diameter along the minor axis, a kinematical analysis on a velocity field might fail: line-of-sight and smearing effects will render a velocity field unreliable." (4.6.2. A kinematical model of the nuclear disk involving tilted rings)
- Alternative: Evaluated excitation, frequency differences, optical depth/line saturation, and chemistry/morphology as explanations for why some lines show absorption and others do not; rejected excitation, frequency, and saturation in favor of critical density (n_crit > 10^4 cm^-3) and small-scale spatial concentration near free-free continuum sources.  
  > "This may be caused by (1) different levels of line excitation, (2) different frequencies leading to changes in continuum flux density" (4.2. Why are some lines absorbed and others not?); "To summarize, notable absorption appears to be only found when critical densities are well in excess of n crit ≈ 10 [4] cm [−][3] ." (4.2. Why are some lines absorbed and others not?)
- Alternative: Tested three radial motion parameterizations in TiRiFiC: (1) radially varying radial motion and vertical gradient, (2) constant radial motion at all radii, and (3) zero midplane radial motion with constant vertical gradient (DVRA); selected (3) as simplest model providing a good fit.  
  > "Tested were the following scenarios, keeping parameters identical for all lines: (1) radially varying radial motion in addition to radially varying vertical gradients of the radial motion, (2) constant radial motion at all radii, and (3) constant radial motion at a given vertical height at all radii and a constant vertical gradient of the radial motion at all radii." (4.6.2. A kinematical model of the nuclear disk involving tilted rings)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The central molecular core (<= 2'' or <= 40 pc) is spatially unresolved by the ≈2'' beam, and subarcsecond resolution is required to resolve small-scale distributions of molecular tracers and continuum.  
  > "This region is not larger than our beam size and may still be unresolved." (4.1.1. The nuclear core); "In any case, subarcsecond resolution is required to reveal the differences in the small scale distributions of the molecular tracers and the mm-wave continuum" (4.2. Why are some lines absorbed and others not?)
- Limitation (authors): HC15N J=1->0 has a low signal-to-noise ratio in 30 minutes of on-source time, leaving a broad range for 14N/15N (≈200 - 500).  
  > "While our observations demonstrate that the ratio is ≫100, it remains to be seen whether it is ≈200 or ≈450. This will await ALMA measurements with higher sensitivity than those presented here with 30 minutes of onsource integration time." (4.4. The nitrogen isotope ratio)
- Limitation (authors): HCO 1_01->0_00, H13CO+ J=1->0, and SiO J=2->1 are severely blended, preventing a detailed spatial and integrated intensity analysis.  
  > "HCO, H [13] CO, and SiO (see Table 1 and Fig. 8) are severely blended and therefore excluded from the list." (Table 4)
- Limitation (authors): The 3D tilted-ring model in TiRiFiC is not guaranteed to be a unique solution, and the surface brightness profile of c-C3H2 2_12->1_01 near the center and parameters of the blended c-C3H2 4_32->4_23 line are unreliable.  
  > "We also cannot guarantee to have found a unique solution." (4.6.2. A kinematical model of the nuclear disk involving tilted rings); "The parameterisation of the c-C 3 H 2 –4 disk is however unreliable. c-C 3 H 2 –2 shows strong absorption, which is why the model surface brightness (Fig. 18, upper panel) is unreliable near the centre." (4.6.2. A kinematical model of the nuclear disk involving tilted rings)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-transition molecular line ratios (all J=1-0 or 2-1 at 3 mm) are used to discuss chemistry and opacity without multi-transition non-LTE excitation modeling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 14N/15N ratio relies on an assumed 12C/13C ratio of 40-50 from single-dish literature and an HC15N line that is affected by continuum absorption near the systemic velocity.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The minor axis of the nuclear disk (~2'') is barely resolved by the ~2.6'' x 1.5'' (or ~2.3'' circular) synthesized beam, making vertical scale height and vertical outflow gradient (DVRA) parameters in TiRiFiC susceptible to beam-smearing degeneracies.
