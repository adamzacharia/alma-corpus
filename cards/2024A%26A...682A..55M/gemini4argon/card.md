## Analysis recipe

1. **Align, self-calibrate, concatenate, and image continuum and 12CO datasets**: Individual phase/amplitude self-calibration in CASA, Gaussian fit to align phase centres to J2000 15h39m27.750s -34d46m17.692s, concatenation of 4 continuum datasets and 2 CO datasets, and Hogbom CLEAN with Briggs robust=0.0 (plus 0''.07 uv taper and Keplerian masking for CO)  
  > "Images were reconstructed using the clean algorithm with Hogbom PSF calculation (Högbom 1974) and Briggs weighting (Briggs 1995)." (4. Calibration and data reduction); "Self-calibration was performed individually on the new Cycle 6 and archival data sets before concatenating the data from each of the observations into a single dataset." (4. Calibration and data reduction); "A uv taper of 0''.07 was applied to the data to produce the images presented here, resulting in a synthesised beam of size of 0''.101 × 0''.079, -41.1◦ ." (4. Calibration and data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: robust = 0.0 (also tested -2.0 and 2), uv taper = 0''.07 for CO, channel width = 768 kHz (1 km/s) for CO, solint = 30s (2019.1.01135), solint = 120s (2017.1.00569), applymode = 'calonly' (2018.1.00271, 2018.1.01458)  
  > "The concatenated continuum image had a synthesised beam of 0''.026 × 0''.018, -75.5◦ using Briggs weighting and robust=0.0." (4. Calibration and data reduction); "Averaging of the spectral windows from 122kHz channels to 768kHz (1 km/s) channels was required in order to extract significant emission from the 12 CO (2-1) line." (4. Calibration and data reduction)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to continuum images to align phase centres  
  > "This was achieved by redefining the phase centre of the measurement set to match that of the centre of a Gaussian fitted to the continuum image from that dataset." (4. Calibration and data reduction)
   - validation: Inspected amplitude vs. uv-distance plots and QA flags, excluding first execution block of 2017.1.00569 due to anomalous extended emission  
  > "The first execution block of project 2017.1.00569 was not included as part of the final concatenated data set because anomalous extended emission was identified in the plots of amplitude against uvdistance." (4. Calibration and data reduction)
2. **Measure image-plane continuum and CO fluxes, disc geometry, radial profiles, R90 sizes, spectral indices, and dust masses**: 2D Gaussian fitting in the image plane for PA and inclination, deprojecting and azimuthally averaging surface brightness for 1D profiles and cumulative 90% flux radii (R90), integrating moment 0 maps above 3σ, combining with Band 7 fluxes for spectral index, and computing optically thin dust mass  
  > "When we integrate over this profile out to the point at which the emission drops to the rms of the image, 90% of the flux from the disc (R 90 ) is contained within 0''.17 (27 au)." (5.1. Continuum emission); "The dust mass can be estimated from the measured continuum flux by assuming optically thin thermal emission and using Equation 1" (5.1. Continuum emission); "Moment maps are created by combining channels containing line emission above 3σ." (5.2. 12CO (2-1) emission)
   - software: CASA, Astropy
   - parameter: 3σ contour threshold for integrated continuum flux and moment map creation  
  > "The integrated flux density measured within a 3σ contour is 28.0 mJy." (5.1. Continuum emission); "Moment maps are created by combining channels containing line emission above 3σ." (5.2. 12CO (2-1) emission)
   - assumption: Optically thin thermal dust emission, distance d = 153 pc (Sz 65) and 154.4 pc (Sz 66), midplane temperature T = 20 K, dust opacity κ_ν = 0.1 (ν/10^12 Hz)^β cm^2 g^-1 with β = 1.0, 10% flux calibration uncertainty  
  > "where d is the Gaia distance to Sz 65 of 153 pc, Bν is the Planck function using frequency of the observation 230.255 GHz and a midplane temperature of 20 K." (5.1. Continuum emission); "We adopted the commonly used prescription κν = 0.1 (ν/1012 Hz)β cm2 g−1 following Beckwith et al. (1990), where β is the opacity power index, which we assumed to be 1.0." (5.1. Continuum emission)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to continuum emission in the image plane  
  > "A Gaussian fit to the emission in the image plane gives a position angle on the sky of 110 ± 0.5 o, a major axis FWHM of 229 ± 3 milliarcsecond and a minor axis FWHM 103 ± 1 milliarcsecond" (5.1. Continuum emission)
   - validation: Compared derived inclination, PA, R90, and dust masses with literature values from Ansdell et al. (2016, 2018), Andrews et al. (2018c), and Hendler et al. (2020)  
  > "Our results are very similar to those found by modelling of the visibilities by Andrews et al. (2018c) using Band 7 observations of Sz 65." (5.1. Continuum emission); "Ansdell et al. (2018) found a larger size of 64 ± 2 au for previous continuum observations with a coarser angular resolution, but our value is consistent with measurements made in analyses in the uv plane by Andrews et al. (2018c) and Hendler et al. (2020)." (5.3.1. Measurements from the image plane)
3. **Non-parametric visibility modelling of 1D radial continuum brightness profiles**: Fit deprojected real visibilities in logarithmic brightness space using frank (log-normal method), testing hyperparameter variations and phase centre offsets  
  > "We used the log-normal fitting method in frank, which fits for the radial profile in logarithmic brightness space." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
   - software: frank
   - parameter: Maximum radius R_max = 2'', number of collocation points N = 300, hyperparameter α = 1.2 (also tested α = 1.05, 1.30), wsmooth varied, phase centre shift grid up to ±5 mas (best shift dRA +1 mas, dDec -4 mas)  
  > "The maximum radius was set to 2''., and the number of collocation points used in the fit, N, was set to 300." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting); "For the remainder of this work, we refer to the alpha = 1.2 case as the ‘frank fit’." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting); "The case with the least residuals is found to be a fit with a shift in space (dRA+1mas, dDec−4mas)." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric 1D disc brightness distribution; power spectrum prior applied to log(I_ν)  
  > "This version applies the power spectrum prior to log(Iν ), and the fitting procedure follows that described in Junklewitz et al. (2016, i.e. RESOLVE)." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting); "The continuum models from the visibility fitting were derived from a 1D radial profile, meaning that they assumed axisymmetry" (5.3.3. Analysis of the molecular gas disc)
   - fitting: Gaussian uv-plane fit for initial geometry followed by non-parametric log-normal visibility fitting and grid search over central position offset  
  > "The frank modelling begins with a Gaussian fit to the emission made in the uv plane in order to determine the geometry of the system." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
   - validation: Subtracted a model of Sz 66 from the visibilities to verify negligible contamination on Sz 65; imaged residual visibilities after subtracting axisymmetric frank models; convolved frank profile with synthesised beam to compare with CLEAN profile  
  > "We verified that our non-parametric fit to the visibility plane successfully isolated only Sz 65 by fitting a model to Sz 66, and subtracted this from the visibilities." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting); "In Figure 7 we show the residual emission that remains after the frank model is subtracted from the clean image of Sz 65." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
4. **Kinematic modelling of 12CO (2-1) rotation velocity maps**: Create peak velocity maps from the 1.0 km/s cube above 3σ using bettermoments, and fit Keplerian rotation models (with an elevated flared emission surface for Sz 65, flat for Sz 66) using eddy MCMC  
  > "We used the tool eddy (Teague 2019) to fit a rotation model to each of Sz 65 and Sz 66 using a model of a gas disc that rotated in a Keplerian manner. We subtracted this model from the map of peak velocities of the cube created with bettermoments (Teague & Foreman-Mackey 2018)" (5.3.3. Analysis of the molecular gas disc)

## Qualitative findings

- Sz 65 exhibits a shallow, low-contrast dust gap at ~20 au and an exterior ring at ~24 au near the outer edge of the dust disc, showing that substructure can exist in compact discs.  
  > "We find evidence of substructure in the dust distribution of Sz 65, namely a shallow gap centred at ≈ 20 au, with an emission ring exterior to it at the outer edge of the disc." (abstract); "The local brightness minimum in the visibility fitting model is at a deprojected separation of 20 au, and the peak brightness of the exterior ring is at 24 au." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
- Visibility fitting of Sz 65 also suggests a tentative smaller feature at ~6 au, which is not seen in the image plane and requires future confirmation.  
  > "The visibility fitting suggests a smaller feature at about 6 au in Sz 65, for which there is no counterpart in either of the profiles extracted from the image plane. We therefore remain cautious about this potential gap." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
- Sz 66 has a very compact dust disc (R90 = 16 au) that is smooth and lacks annular substructures at ~4 au resolution, though frank residual maps show a localised 7σ residual to the south-west.  
  > "R 90 for the Sz 66 dust continuum is found at 0''.11 (~ 16 au). This very compact disc does not show evidence of substructure on scales of 4 au." (5.1. Continuum emission); "The frank model describes the emission well generally, although localised residual emission that reaches 7" (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
- Compact discs like Sz 65 and Sz 66 are not simply radially scaled-down versions of extended, multi-ringed DSHARP discs, but resemble the compact subset of DSHARP discs and CX Tau.  
  > "The observations do not support the hypothesis that these two discs are simply radially smaller size-scaled versions of the bright radially extended DSHARP discs that show multiple substructures." (7. Conclusions)
- No entrained dust or dramatic non-Keplerian gas perturbations are seen between Sz 65 and Sz 66, and tidal truncation is unlikely to be the primary driver of their small gas disc sizes unless the binary orbit is highly eccentric.  
  > "The dust emission (e.g. Figure 1) contains no evidence of entrained dust material between the two discs that might suggest significant binary interaction or a recent fly-by." (5.3.3. Analysis of the molecular gas disc); "Tests of the statistical likelihood of disc truncation due to binary interaction suggest that truncation can only have been a significant influence if the eccentricity of the Sz 65 - Sz 66 orbit is very high." (7. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long-baseline ALMA observations were combined with four archival datasets in more compact configurations to achieve ~4 au resolution for substructure searches while filling in short uv spacings to recover extended emission.  
  > "The continuum observations achieve a synthesised beam size of 0''.026 × 0''.018, equivalent to about 4.0 × 2.8 au, enabling a search for substructure on these spatial scales and a characterisation of the gas and dust disc sizes with high precision." (abstract); "We concatenate these archival data to our new observations in order to ensure a more complete coverage of the uv plane. This was necessary in particular to fill in the shorter-baseline spacings lacking from the extended configuration of the new observations." (3. Observations)
- Why this method: Non-parametric visibility fitting with frank (log-normal method) was used to achieve super-resolution and produce a smoother profile less prone to fitting noisy small-amplitude rings; applymode='calonly' was used on extended configurations to avoid flagging long baselines; a 0''.07 uv taper and 1 km/s spectral averaging were applied to 12CO to boost SNR for a robust detection.  
  > "The log-normal version results in a smoother brightness profile with radius, as the fit is less prone to fitting rings of small amplitude or in noisier baseline ranges." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting); "Averaging of the spectral windows from 122kHz channels to 768kHz (1 km/s) channels was required in order to extract significant emission from the 12 CO (2-1) line." (4. Calibration and data reduction); "The log-normal version results in a smoother brightness profile with radius, as the fit is less prone to fitting rings of small amplitude or in noisier baseline ranges." (5.3.2. Non-parametric visibility fitting)
- Alternative: Optical depth effects as the explanation for the high R_gas / R_dust ratio in Sz 65 (considered less likely because the disc is very well resolved and the ratio of 5.3 is particularly high)  
  > "A high R gas / R dust can also be explained by optical depth effects (Facchini et al. 2017), but we resolved the disc very well and the ratio of 5.3 is particularly high, suggesting that this is less likely to be the case for Sz 65." (5.3.1. Measurements from the image plane)
- Alternative: Amplitude self-calibration on project 2017.1.00569 and inclusion of its first execution block (rejected because amplitude self-cal gave no image quality improvement and the first EB had anomalous extended emission)  
  > "Observations from project 2017.1.00569 were included after phase self-calibration (stopping at solint=120s), but amplitude self-calibration did not offer improvements in image quality." (4.
