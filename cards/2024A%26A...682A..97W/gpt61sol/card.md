## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract compact-source full-Stokes light curves**: After QA2 calibration, self-calibrate to stable extended emission; model it with first-order CLEAN multi-frequency synthesis and fit the time-dependent compact component separately in each sub-band.  
  > "allowing to self-calibrate to the extended source structure and extract the time-dependent point source component, reducing the impact of fluctuating amplitude gains." (Data reduction and conventions); "using a CLEAN multi-frequency-synthesis first-order expansion (cite: Conway1990; Casa2007)." (Data reduction and conventions); "Both variants of the extended emission modeling resulted in very consistent light curves of the compact component, fitted individually in each sub-band." (Data reduction and conventions)
   - parameter: Band 3: four 2 GHz sub-bands centered at 86.3, 88.3, 98.3, 100.3 GHz  
  > "with 4 frequency sub-bands, each 2 GHz wide, centered at 86.3, 88.3, 98.3, and 100.3 GHz." (Data reduction and conventions)
   - parameter: Full Stokes at 4 s cadence; data averaged within sub-bands  
  > "The sub-band depolarization and decorrelation effects are negligible in all cases, hence we work with the data averaged in sub-bands." (Data reduction and conventions); "The data set contains all Stokes parameters, that is, total intensity $\mathcal{I}$, linear polarization $\mathcal{P} = \mathcal{Q} + i\mathcal{U}$, and circular polarization $\mathcal{V}$, observed with a time cadence of 4 s." (Data reduction and conventions)
   - parameter: 227.1 GHz scaled up by 4% for CN absorption  
  > "scaled up by 4% to account for the CN absorption line (cite: Goddi2021)" (Observations and data analysis)
   - assumption: Negligible variation of surrounding arc-minute-scale emission  
  > "assumption of negligible variation of the arc-minute scale radio emission surrounding (cite: Lo1983; Mus2022)" (Data reduction and conventions)
   - fitting: Compact component fitted individually in each sub-band; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "Both variants of the extended emission modeling resulted in very consistent light curves of the compact component, fitted individually in each sub-band." (Data reduction and conventions)
   - validation: Independent per-sub-band CLEAN and first-order multi-frequency synthesis produced very consistent compact-source light curves.  
  > "carrying out an independent CLEAN deconvolution of each sub-band and using a CLEAN multi-frequency-synthesis first-order expansion (cite: Conway1990; Casa2007)." (Data reduction and conventions); "Both variants of the extended emission modeling resulted in very consistent light curves of the compact component, fitted individually in each sub-band." (Data reduction and conventions)
2. **Measure polarization and light-curve statistics**: Compute fractional LP p = sqrt(Q^2 + U^2)/I, fractional CP v = V/I and EVPA chi = 0.5 Arg(Q + iU); summarize means and standard deviations.  
  > "p = \frac{|\mathcal{P}|}{\mathcal{I}} = \frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}^2 + \mathcal{U}^2}}{\mathcal{I}}   \ \ ; \ \ v = \frac{\mathcal{V}}{{\mathcal{I}}}" (Data reduction and conventions); "Furthermore, we define the electric vector position angle (EVPA) as $\chi = 0.5 {\rm Arg}(\mathcal{Q} + i\mathcal{U})$." (Data reduction and conventions); "with mean values and standard deviations reported" (Intensity and polarization)
   - parameter: Main comparison: 2017 April 3 Band 3 and April 6 Band 6  
  > "We focus on comparisons between band 3 observations on 2017 April 3 and band 6 observations on 2017 April 6, given that these light curves are closest in time and of similar total duration." (Intensity and polarization)
   - parameter: Flag points where calibration did not converge; identical procedure for each data set  
  > "in order to remove points for which the calibration procedures did not converge." (Intensity and polarization); "We employed an identical flagging procedure for each data set" (Intensity and polarization)
   - parameter: EVPA statistics calculated using directional statistics  
  > "calculated using directional statistics" (Observations and data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Flagging low-elevation late data did not appreciably change results; historical LP measurements were consistent with 86.3 and 100.3 GHz light curves.  
  > "We verified that flagging these data does not appreciably impact the reported results." (Intensity and polarization); "We observe consistency between these historical measurements and our light curves at 86.3 and 100.3 GHz." (Intensity and polarization)
3. **Measure frequency-dependent depolarization**: Fit spectral indices of fractional LP and absolute fractional CP across ALMA sub-bands.  
  > "where we fitted spectral indices of $p$ and $|v|$" (Depolarization at lower frequencies); "across ALMA sub-bands." (Depolarization at lower frequencies)
   - fitting: Spectral-index fits; sampler, priors, likelihood and weighting not stated

4. **Measure time-dependent RM and derotate EVPAs**: Fit EVPA versus squared wavelength across four Band 3 sub-bands independently at each timestamp; compare with Band 6 RM and compute chi_0 = chi - RM lambda^2.  
  > "we estimated RM as a function of time by fitting a linear model for the EVPA as a function of squared wavelength across 4 sub-bands in band 3, independently for each timestamp." (Faraday rotation); "Subsequently, we compared these measurements with the band 6 results, already discussed in Appendix A of (cite: Wielgus2022_LP)." (Faraday rotation); "\chi_0 = \chi(\lambda) - {\rm RM} \lambda^2" (Faraday rotation)
   - parameter: Mean frequencies 93 and 221 GHz; approximately 6 h observing windows on April 3 and 6  
  > "high time cadence RM measurements in $\sim$6 h duration observing windows are presented for the mean band 3 frequency of 93 GHz (2017 April 3) and mean band 6 frequency of 221 GHz (2017 April 6)." (Faraday rotation)
   - assumption: Linear EVPA-lambda^2 model within each band; wavelength-independent RM expected for small Faraday depth and an external screen  
  > "If the Faraday depth is small and the Faraday screen is external with respect to the emitting region, the RM is independent of the observing wavelength" (Faraday rotation)
   - fitting: Independent linear EVPA-lambda^2 fit at each timestamp; sampler, priors, likelihood and regression weights not stated  
  > "we estimated RM as a function of time by fitting a linear model for the EVPA as a function of squared wavelength across 4 sub-bands in band 3, independently for each timestamp." (Faraday rotation)
   - validation: Good linear fit within each band; compare observed and derotated EVPA variability.  
  > "While the time-dependent model linear in $\lambda^2$ provides a good fit quality across each ALMA band individually, the resulting RM measurements significantly differ between the two bands." (Faraday rotation); "the shape of derotated $\chi_0(t)$ and its measured variability remain overall very similar to those of the observed $\chi(t)$" (Faraday rotation)
5. **Characterize RM variability timescales**: Compute SF(tau) = <(RM(t) - RM(t-tau))^2>; compare Band 3, April 6 Band 6 and joint April 6, 7, 11 Band 6 structure functions.  
  > "{\rm SF}(\tau) = \langle ( {\rm RM}(t) - {\rm RM}(t- \tau) )^2 \rangle" (Quantifying the RM variability); "we compare 2017 Apr 3 and 6 analysis, as well as the joint analysis of band 6 RM calculated for Apr 6, 7, and 11, with a total of $\sim$ 20 h of data." (Quantifying the RM variability)
   - parameter: Joint Band 6 data duration ~20 h  
  > "with a total of $\sim$ 20 h of data." (Quantifying the RM variability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare frequency-dependent structure-function behavior with a numerical GRMHD simulation.  
  > "We observe a similar dependence in the SF of RM in a numerical GRMHD simulation" (Quantifying the RM variability)
6. **Infer electron temperature and emission-radius profiles**: Use VLBI flux densities and descattered elliptical-Gaussian sizes to compute brightness temperatures and emission radii; correct brightness temperature for gravitational redshift; fit temperature and size power laws.  
  > "The VLBI observations measure flux density $S_{\nu}$ and allow to estimate the intrinsic (descattered, see (cite: Johnson2018)) angular size of the source $\theta_{\rm maj} \times \theta_{\rm min}$, modeled as an elliptical Gaussian." (Electrons temperature profile); "T_{\rm B, obs} = 1.22 \times 10^{12} \frac{S_\nu}{\nu^2 \theta_{\rm maj} \theta_{\rm min}}" (Electrons temperature profile); "r_{\rm e}/r_{\rm g} = 0.5(\theta_{\rm maj} \theta_{\rm min})^{0.5}/\theta_{\rm g} - 1" (Electrons temperature profile)
   - parameter: Flux density in Jy, frequency in GHz, angular dimensions in mas; theta_g = 5.1 microarcseconds  
  > "where flux density is given in Jy, frequency in GHz, and angular dimensions in mas." (Electrons temperature profile); "following (cite: Gravity2022) we take $\theta_{\rm g} = 5.1\, \mu$as." (Electrons temperature profile)
   - parameter: Temperature fit uses only three 22-86 GHz points; size-frequency fit uses all four VLBI measurements  
  > "Hence, we fit a power law $T_{\rm e}(r) = T_{0} (r/r_{\rm g})^\gamma$ only to the three 22-86 GHz data points" (Electrons temperature profile); "based on the four VLBI measurements" (Electrons temperature profile)
   - assumption: Thermal electron energy distribution makes brightness temperature a proxy for emitting-electron temperature.  
  > "a proxy for the temperature of the emitting electrons under the assumption of a thermal energy distribution" (Electrons temperature profile)
   - assumption: Subtract 1 from radius estimate for geometric lensing; T_B,int = (1+z) T_B,obs with (1+z) = (1-2M/r_e)^-0.5  
  > "approximately accounts for the geometric lensing around the black hole regardless of the detailed spacetime geometry (cite: Gralla2020; Wielgus2021)." (Electrons temperature profile); "$(1+z) = [-g_{ tt}(r_{\rm e})]^{-0.5} = (1-2M/r_{\rm e})^{-0.5}$, $T_{\rm B, int} = (1+z) T_{\rm B, obs}$" (Electrons temperature profile)
   - fitting: Power-law fits T_e(r) = T_0 (r/r_g)^gamma and r_e versus frequency; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "Hence, we fit a power law $T_{\rm e}(r) = T_{0} (r/r_{\rm g})^\gamma$ only to the three 22-86 GHz data points" (Electrons temperature profile); "we model the intrinsic source size dependence on frequency, finding a scaling of $r_{\rm e} \propto \nu^{-1.2}$" (Electrons temperature profile)
   - validation: Elliptical Gaussian versus published ring model changes brightness-temperature estimates by no more than ~20%.

## Qualitative findings

- Strong low-frequency depolarization: p proportional to nu^3 in Band 3 versus nu in Band 6  
  > "In band 3, we measured a steep depolarization toward lower frequencies $p \propto \nu^3$, inconsistent with the milder depolarization $p \propto \nu$ seen in band 6." (Depolarization at lower frequencies)
- EVPA is substantially more variable in Band 3 than Band 6 on the same timescales  
  > "Furthermore, the EVPA is significantly more variable at 85-101 GHz than at 212-230 GHz on the same timescales" (Intensity and polarization)
- About half the Faraday rotation occurs inside the 93 GHz emission region; internal-screen interpretation favored  
  > "This implies that about half of the Faraday rotation occurs internally with respect to the 93 GHz emission region, that is, at most several $r_{\rm g}$ away from the SMBH’s event horizon." (Faraday rotation)
- Derotation does not substantially reduce EVPA variability; intrinsic emitter variability dominates  
  > "we conclude that the intrinsic variability of the emitter dominates over the variability in the external Faraday screen" (Faraday rotation)
- A reasonable simple RIAF can produce an internal Faraday screen dominated by compact scales  
  > "an internal Faraday screen in , dominated by the contribution from very compact scales, is to be expected for a reasonable set of physical parameters in a RIAF system." (RM in a RIAF model)
- Selected GRMHD model has excessively turbulent and variable magnetic fields compared with observations  
  > "It appears that the magnetic field is far more turbulent and variable in the simulations than in reality" (Comparisons to GRMHD)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Different radio frequencies probe distinct accretion-flow regions; April 3 and 6 light curves are closest in time and have similar duration.  
  > "Therefore, observations at different radio frequencies can be used to probe distinct regions of the accretion flow." (Introduction); "We focus on comparisons between band 3 observations on 2017 April 3 and band 6 observations on 2017 April 6, given that these light curves are closest in time and of similar total duration." (Intensity and polarization)
- Why this method: Stable extended emission permits gain self-calibration; wide Band 3 fractional bandwidth requires frequency-dependent extended-emission modeling.  
  > "allowing to self-calibrate to the extended source structure and extract the time-dependent point source component, reducing the impact of fluctuating amplitude gains." (Data reduction and conventions); "The only difference with respect to the procedure of (cite: Wielgus2022_LC) was motivated by the wide total fractional bandwidth in band 3 observations $\sim$15%, as opposed to $\sim$7% in band 6." (Data reduction and conventions)
- Alternative: Independent CLEAN deconvolution per sub-band tested; first-order CLEAN multi-frequency synthesis adopted after consistent results  
  > "carrying out an independent CLEAN deconvolution of each sub-band and using a CLEAN multi-frequency-synthesis first-order expansion (cite: Conway1990; Casa2007)." (Data reduction and conventions); "Both variants of the extended emission modeling resulted in very consistent light curves of the compact component, fitted individually in each sub-band." (Data reduction and conventions); "The latter approach was adopted for the final data set presented in this paper." (Data reduction and conventions)
- Alternative: Coherent orbital interpretation of polarization loop challenged by opposite rotation direction  
  > "If we attempted to explain this loop in the framework of a coherent orbital motion, we would conclude Keplerian orbit of $\sim$20$r_{\rm g}$ radius." (Intensity and polarization); "However, the direction of the loop’s direction is opposite to the one observed by (cite: Wielgus2022_LP) and (cite: gravity2023), challenging that interpretation." (Intensity and polarization)
- Alternative: Low-frequency depolarization may reflect magnetic-field changes or increased optical depth; uniform-opacity explanation is oversimplified  
  > "possibly separating strong and ordered magnetic fields near the event horizon (cite: Johnson2015) from a weaker and more chaotic component further away." (Depolarization at lower frequencies); "Hence, an increase in the optical depth by $\Delta \tau \lesssim 2$ would suffice to explain the change in fractional polarization." (Depolarization at lower frequencies); "However, the opacity interpretation is only straightforward under the assumption of a uniform optical depth in the emission zone, which is likely an oversimplification." (Depolarization at lower frequencies)
- Alternative: Multiple unresolved Faraday screens versus an internal screen; simple compact VLBI morphology favors the internal interpretation  
  > "implying either a complex unresolved source structure involving multiple Faraday screens, or an internal Faraday screen overlapping with the emission zone at some wavelengths (cite: Burn1966; Brentjens2005)." (Faraday rotation); "VLBI observations reveal a persistent, simple, compact, single-component source morphology across frequencies, hence the internal Faraday screen interpretation is favored." (Faraday rotation)
- Alternative: Unusually low wavelength-independent RM on April 3 remains an alternative because bands were not observed simultaneously  
  > "otherwise one could still attempt to explain our results with an unusually low, but $\lambda$-independent RM on 2017 April 3." (Faraday rotation)
- Alternative: 230 GHz temperature-profile deviation may reflect optically thin nonthermal emission or Doppler-deboosted plunging plasma  
  > "is optically thin and non-thermal, violating assumptions of the $T_{\rm b}$ calculation" (Electrons temperature profile); "and/or that the emission is produced by plasma plunging into the black hole with a relativistic radial velocity component and thus experiencing Doppler deboosting." (Electrons temperature profile)
- Alternative: A jet-sheath emission origin is suggested as a possible alternative to turbulent disk emission in GRMHD  
  > "An interesting alternative is the observed mm radiation originating predominantly in the jet sheath region, which is typically threaded by a more stable, nearly-vertical magnetic field in GRMHD simulations (cite: Moscibrodzka2013)." (Comparisons to GRMHD)
