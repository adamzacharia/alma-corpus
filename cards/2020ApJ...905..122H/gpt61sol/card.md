## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare the line cubes**: For 49 Ceti, subtract continuum with uvcontsub and image/deconvolve with tclean; for HD 21997, use inherited reduced cubes.  
  > "Continuum subtraction was performed for all the data by using the CASA task $\it{uvcontsub}$." (Data Reduction / 49 Ceti); "The CASA task $\it{tclean}$ was employed to Fourier-transform the visibility data and to deconvolve the dirty images at a velocity interval of 2 km s$^{-1}$." (Data Reduction / 49 Ceti); "For HD 21997, we used the ALMA archival data (reduced cube data) reported by (cite: kos13)" (Data Reduction / HD 21997)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub, tclean
   - parameter: 49 Ceti: velocity interval 2 km s^-1  
  > "at a velocity interval of 2 km s$^{-1}$." (Data Reduction / 49 Ceti)
   - parameter: 49 Ceti: Briggs weighting +0.5  
  > "Briggs weighting of +0.5 was applied to all line images for the best compromise between resolution and sensitivity." (Data Reduction / 49 Ceti)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Match spatial resolution**: Smooth the 49 Ceti 12 m data to the ACA beam; adjust all HD 21997 CO beams to 4.0×4.0 arcsec.  
  > "the synthesized beam size for the 12 m array data is adjusted to that for the ACA data ($\sim$6$\ensuremath{''}$) by using the CASA task $\it{imsmooth}$." (Data Reduction / 49 Ceti); "The synthesized beam is adjusted to be 4.0$''$ $\times$ 4.0$''$ with P.A.= 0.0$^{\circ}$ for all the CO lines" (Data Reduction / HD 21997)
   - software: imsmooth
   - parameter: 49 Ceti: ~6 arcsec, corresponding to 340 au  
  > "The angular size of 6$\ensuremath{''}$ corresponds to 340 au." (Data Reduction / 49 Ceti)
   - parameter: HD 21997: 4.0×4.0 arcsec, P.A.=0.0 degrees; 4 arcsec corresponds to 280 au  
  > "The synthesized beam is adjusted to be 4.0$''$ $\times$ 4.0$''$ with P.A.= 0.0$^{\circ}$ for all the CO lines" (Data Reduction / HD 21997); "For this source, the angular size of 4$\ensuremath{''}$ corresponds to 280 au." (Data Reduction / HD 21997)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Make maps and extract spectra**: Make integrated-intensity maps and average spectra over areas with diameters matching the spatial resolution.  
  > "These spectra are prepared by averaging over the 6$\ensuremath{''}$ area in diameter which corresponds to the spatial resolution." (Overall data / 49 Ceti); "These spectra are prepared by averaging over the 4$\ensuremath{''}$ area in diameter which corresponds to the spatial resolution." (Overall data / HD 21997)
   - parameter: 49 Ceti: −6 to 11.5 km s^-1 for 12CO and 13CO maps  
  > "at a 6$\ensuremath{''}$ resolution, where the velocity range for integration is from $-$6 to 11.5 km s$^{-1}$." (Overall data / 49 Ceti)
   - parameter: 49 Ceti C18O(2–1): −4 to 6 km s^-1  
  > "Figure [1](#fig1) (d) shows the integrated intensity map of the C$^{18}$O(2–1) emission at a 6$\ensuremath{''}$ resolution, where the velocity range for integration is from $-$4 to 6 km s$^{-1}$." (Overall data / 49 Ceti)
   - parameter: HD 21997: −4 to 6 km s^-1  
  > "at a 4$\ensuremath{''}$ resolution, where the velocity range for integration is from $-$4 to 6 km s$^{-1}$." (Overall data / HD 21997)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 49 Ceti integration ranges checked against the spectra  
  > "The velocity range for the integrated intensity maps mentioned above is justified by these spectra." (Overall data / 49 Ceti)
4. **Measure line components**: Fit double-Gaussian profiles; for 49 Ceti C18O(2–1), fit only the blue component with a single Gaussian.  
  > "Double-Gaussian fitting is performed on all the spectra except for C$^{18}$O(2–1) to derive the line parameters." (Overall data / 49 Ceti); "For C$^{18}$O(2–1), only the blue shifted component is fitted by the single Gaussian function." (Overall data / 49 Ceti); "Since all the spectra show a double-peak emission, double-Gaussian fitting is performed on the spectra to derive the line parameters." (Overall data / HD 21997)
   - assumption: Ignore small HD 21997 residuals around systemic velocity because they are close to the noise level.  
  > "Since it is close to the noise level, its contribution to the total spectra is not large. Thus, we ignore this feature in the following analyses." (Overall data / HD 21997)
   - fitting: Double Gaussian; single Gaussian for the blue 49 Ceti C18O component  
  > "For C$^{18}$O(2–1), only the blue shifted component is fitted by the single Gaussian function." (Overall data / 49 Ceti); "double-Gaussian fitting is performed on the spectra to derive the line parameters." (Overall data / HD 21997)
   - validation: Inspect residuals; HD 21997 isotopologue spectra retain small residuals near systemic velocity.  
  > "there seem to be small residuals" (Overall data / HD 21997); "around the systemic velocity, which cannot be reproduced in the double-Gaussian fitting." (Overall data / HD 21997)
5. **Fit LTE excitation and opacity**: Use peak-intensity ratios to assess opacity; fit rotational temperature, CO column density and beam filling factor with optical-depth effects.  
  > "the rotational temperature, the column density of $^{12}$CO, and the beam filling factor are derived by using the following equation, where we take the effect of the optical depth into account:" (LTE analysis); "evaluated by using the least-squares analysis on the observed intensities of CO and its isotopic species." (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: Fit blue-shifted and red-shifted components separately  
  > "We treat the data for the blue-shifted and red-shifted components separately." (LTE analysis)
   - assumption: LTE; isotope ratios 13CO/12CO=1/77 and C18O/12CO=1/560  
  > "$T_{\rm{rot}}$ is the rotational temperature which is equivalent with the excitation temperature in the LTE condition" (LTE analysis); "We employ the isotope ratios of 1/77 and 1/560 for $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O, respectively (cite: wil94)." (LTE analysis)
   - fitting: Least-squares fit of T_rot, N and f_d; maximum parameter correlation 0.99 between T_rot and f_d, reflected in quoted errors  
  > "evaluated by using the least-squares analysis on the observed intensities of CO and its isotopic species." (LTE analysis); "The highest correlation among the three parameters in the fit is 0.99" (LTE analysis); "Nevertheless, these two parameters are separately determined in the fit, and the effect of the correlation is reflected in the quoted errors." (LTE analysis)
   - validation: Compare calculated peak intensities and residuals with measurement uncertainties  
  > "The observed peak intensities are well reproduced within the measurement uncertainties for both sources." (LTE analysis)
6. **Check beam filling factors**: Fit 2D Gaussians to previously published high resolution images and compare source-size beam filling factors with the spectral fits.  
  > "by using 2D Gaussian fits. Hence, the beam filling factor is estimated to be 0.04 for both blue-shifted and red-shifted components." (LTE analysis); "by using 2D Gaussian fits on the high resolution channel maps shown in Figure 3 of (cite: kos13), the beam filling factor is estimated to be 0.06." (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: 49 Ceti blue/red source FWHM: 1.9×0.8 and 1.8×0.8 arcsec  
  > "the FWHM (full width half maximum) size of the emitting region of the CO(3–2) lines is estimated to be 1.9$''$ $\times$ 0.8$''$ and 1.8$''$ $\times$ 0.8$''$ for the blue-shifted and red-shifted components, respectively" (LTE analysis)
   - parameter: HD 21997 blue/red source sizes: 1.1×0.9 and 1.0×0.9 arcsec  
  > "the emitting region of the CO emission is derived to be 1.1$''$ $\times$ 0.9$''$ and 1.0$''$ $\times$ 0.9$''$ for the blue-shifted and red-shifted components, respectively" (LTE analysis)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fits  
  > "by using 2D Gaussian fits." (LTE analysis)
   - validation: Source-size estimates approximately agree with fitted filling factors; HD 21997 fitted factors have large errors.  
  > "These values are almost consistent with those determined in the fit" (LTE analysis); "although the observed beam filling factors suffer from a large error." (LTE analysis)
7. **Fit non-LTE excitation with H2 collisions**: Use the authors' LVG code to balance collisional and radiative processes; fix H2 density and fit CO column density, kinetic temperature and filling factor separately for each velocity component.  
  > "In this analysis, the rotational level populations are determined by the balance between the collision and radiation processes, from which the line intensities are calculated." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "Hence, we fix the H$_{2}$ density and determine the remaining three parameters." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "The data for the blue-shifted and red-shifted components are treated separately." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
   - software: authors' non-LTE code
   - parameter: Fixed H2 density range: 3×10^3 to 1×10^7 cm^-3  
  > "The range of the H$_{2}$ density assumed in the fit is from 3$\times$10$^{3}$ to 1$\times$10$^{7}$ cm$^{-3}$." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
   - assumption: LVG model; H2 collision rates from LAMDA  
  > "First, we conduct the non-LTE analysis based on the LVG (large velocity gradient) model (cite: gol74; van07), assuming the collision with H$_{2}$." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "The collisional cross sections are taken from The Leiden Atomic and Molecular Database (LAMDA) (cite: sch05)." (Non-LTE analysis)
   - assumption: Use H2 rates as a proxy for CO/C/C+/O collisions; interpret density as an effective sum of collision partners.  
  > "we cannot distinguish the collision with H$_{2}$ and that of CO/C/C$^{+}$/O in our calculation and just employ the collisional rate coefficients for the H$_{2}$ collision." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "it should approximately be regarded as $n$(H$_{2}$)+$n$(CO)+$n$(C)+$n$(C$^{+}$)+$n$(O)." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
   - assumption: Ignore CO-H collisions  
  > "The rate for the CO-H collision is much lower than that of the CO-H$_{2}$ collision (cite: yan13; wal15a), and thus we have ignored the CO-H collision in this study." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
   - fitting: Least squares; simultaneous four-parameter fitting failed to constrain H2 density, so density is fixed.  
  > "We have initially tried to optimize these four parameters by using the least-squares fit to reproduce the $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O data." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "However, all the four parameters cannot be determined simultaneously in the fit. Specifically, the H$_{2}$ density is not well constrained." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
   - validation: Code tested against published results and RADEX; inspect calculated intensities and residuals.  
  > "We employ the non-LTE code prepared by ourselves in the analysis, which is tested by using the results reported by (cite: gol74) and also those derived from the RADEX code." (Non-LTE analysis); "The fitting is successful, as shown by the residuals." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
8. **Test electron excitation**: Repeat non-LTE LVG analysis with electron collisions, fixing electron density and determining the other three parameters.  
  > "Next, we conduct the non-LTE LVG analysis assuming the collision with an electron, considering that the gas has the secondary origin." (The case of electron collision); "we fix the electron density and determine the remaining three parameters." (The case of electron collision)
   - software: authors' non-LTE code
   - parameter: Electron densities: 30, 1.0×10^2, 1.0×10^3 and 1.0×10^4 cm^-3  
  > "The analysis is conducted for several electron densities from 30 to 10$^{4}$ cm$^{-3}$ for 49 Ceti and HD 21997" (The case of electron collision); "1.0$\times$10$^{2}$" (Table 8); "1.0$\times$10$^{3}$" (Table 8)
   - assumption: Electron collisional rate coefficients calculated using (cite: dic77)  
  > "Here, we employ the collisional rate coefficients calculated by using the method described by (cite: dic77)." (The case of electron collision)
   - assumption: Electrons must exceed 0.01 of CO, C, C+ and O to dominate collisions; maximum electron density around 10^4 cm^-3.  
  > "the electron abundance relative to CO, C, C$^{+}$, and O needs to be higher than 0.01 in order for an electron to become a major collision partner." (The case of electron collision); "the maximum electron density would be around 10$^{4}$ cm$^{-3}$" (The case of electron collision)
   - fitting: Fix electron density because the four parameters cannot be determined simultaneously.  
  > "As in the H$_{2}$ collision case, we cannot determine the four parameters simultaneously in the analysis, and hence, we fix the electron density and determine the remaining three parameters." (The case of electron collision)
   - validation: Compare column densities, temperatures and filling factors with the H2-collision solutions.  
  > "These values resemble those found in the H$_{2}$ collision case, except for the electron density of 30 cm$^{-3}$ where the optical depth of the CO lines is very high due to insufficient excitation." (The case of electron collision); "For HD 21997, the ranges of the column density and the gas kinetic temperature are also similar to those of the H$_{2}$ collision case." (The case of electron collision)
9. **Derive CO densities and abundance ratios**: Convert CO column density to average number density using an assumed line-of-sight length, then derive H2/CO ratios.  
  > "We derive the number density of CO from the column density by assuming the line-of-sight length of the emitting region." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "The number density of CO and H$_{2}$/CO ratio is derived in this way for the other H$_{2}$ density cases" (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
   - assumption: 49 Ceti: almost edge-on; line-of-sight length is the disk radius of 100 au  
  > "Since 49 Ceti has an almost edge-on configuration, the disk radius of 100 au is employed as the line-of-sight length." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)

## Qualitative findings

- 12CO lines are optically thick in both disks.  
  > "These $^{12}$CO lines are indeed optically thick." (LTE analysis); "For HD 21997, the optical depths of the $^{12}$CO(2–1) and $^{12}$CO(3–2) emissions are 66 – 76 and 26 – 36, respectively" (LTE analysis)
- CO level populations are almost thermalized over the assumed gas-density range.  
  > "This means that the level population is almost thermalized in the assumed range of the gas density." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "These similarities seem reasonable because the rotational population is almost in LTE regardless of the collision partner." (The case of electron collision)
- The observed excitation permits little or no H2, but abundant H2 is not excluded.  
  > "the observed line intensities can be reproduced even without H$_{2}$." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "Meanwhile, our result does not rule out the case of abundant H$_{2}$ molecules." (Abstract)
- Low temperatures may indicate inefficient photoelectric heating from a lack of small grains.  
  > "These results would indicate inefficient photoelectric heating due to less abundant small grains." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiple transitions and rare isotopologues constrain temperature and column density beyond 12CO alone.  
  > "For further constraint of the gas kinetic temperature, it is essential to involve the $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O data in the analysis." (Introduction); "It is particularly important to include optically thin lines such as rare isotopologue lines." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Match beams for fair comparison; use non-LTE analysis to determine kinetic temperature; fix collision-partner density because it is unconstrained.  
  > "For a fair comparison among the observations with different spatial resolution, the synthesized beam size for the 12 m array data is adjusted to that for the ACA data." (Table 1); "To derive the gas kinetic temperature, a non-LTE analysis is performed." (LTE analysis); "Specifically, the H$_{2}$ density is not well constrained. Hence, we fix the H$_{2}$ density and determine the remaining three parameters." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Alternative: Simultaneously fitting all four non-LTE parameters was unsuccessful; collision-partner density was fixed instead.  
  > "However, all the four parameters cannot be determined simultaneously in the fit. Specifically, the H$_{2}$ density is not well constrained." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Alternative: H2 densities below 10^3 cm^-3 did not produce convergent fits.  
  > "The least-squares fit does not converge for any of the two sources for the H$_{2}$ density below 10$^{3}$ cm$^{-3}$." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Alternative: Both H2 and electron collision cases were tested.  
  > "Thus, we conduct the non-LTE analysis for the two distinct cases, where the major collision partner is H$_{2}$ or an electron." (Non-LTE analysis)
- Alternative: Primordial and secondary gas origins remain possibilities.  
  > "For the origin of gas, there are mainly two possibilities." (Nature of gas components in debris disks); "Another possibility is that the gas has a secondary origin" (Nature of gas components in debris disks); "Meanwhile, our result does not rule out the case of abundant H$_{2}$ molecules." (Abstract)
- How they decided it was enough: Judge fits by peak-intensity agreement and residuals; use the lowest fixed H2 density yielding successful fitting.  
  > "The observed peak intensities are well reproduced within the measurement uncertainties for both sources." (LTE analysis); "Thus, we set the lowest H$_{2}$ density to be 3$\times$10$^{3}$ cm$^{-3}$, for which the fit is successful." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "The fitting is successful, as shown by the residuals." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Limitation (authors): H2 density is not independently constrained by the fits.  
  > "Specifically, the H$_{2}$ density is not well constrained." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Limitation (authors): High optical depths can violate the simple LVG approximation; more rigorous radiative transfer including dust opacity is deferred.  
  > "the simple approximation by the LVG model (cite: gol74) employed here does not perfectly describe the radiation transfer." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "This situation may not always be fulfilled for very high optical-depth cases." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision); "More rigorous treatments including dust opacity are left for future study." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Limitation (authors): State-to-state collision coefficients for CO, C and C+ are unavailable, preventing explicit treatment of these collisions.  
  > "the state-to-state collisional rate coefficients for the CO, C, and C$^{+}$ are not available, and therefore the contribution of their collisions are not explicitly considered." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Limitation (authors): HD 21997 low-density fits poorly constrain temperature and column density, especially for the blue component.  
  > "particularly for the blue-shifted component, where the gas kinetic temperature and the CO column density are not well constrained for the gas density below 10$^{4}$ cm$^{-3}$." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Limitation (authors): Temperature and filling factor are highly correlated.  
  > "Although the maximum correlation in the fit is 0.99 between ${T_{\rm{kin}}}$ and $f_{d}$, these parameters are well constrained." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Limitation (authors): The faint 49 Ceti C18O line has poorer data quality than the other lines.  
  > "The C$^{18}$O(2–1) line is so faint that the data quality is not as good as the other lines." (Overall data / 49 Ceti)
- Limitation (authors): The available isotopologue resolution does not permit direct radial temperature profiles.  
  > "Since high angular resolution data of the CO isotopologues are not available, we cannot directly derive the radial distribution of the temperature in the disk." (Nature of gas components in debris disks)
- Limitation (authors): Herschel resolution differs substantially, limiting assessment of the C+ contribution.  
  > "Since the spatial and velocity resolutions of the Herschel observation are much different from our observation, it seems difficult to evaluate the contribution of C$^{+}$." (The case of H$_{2}$ collision)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Summary column-density and number-density ranges do not cover all low-electron-density solutions in Tables 8–9; preserve the collision case and density when constructing answer keys.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The narrow summary temperature range omits the HD 21997 red-component central value of 14 K at the lowest electron density.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Derived number densities and abundance ratios depend directly on assumed line-of-sight lengths.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Independent line flux-scale errors and their covariance are unspecified despite reliance on line ratios.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The red 49 Ceti C18O upper limit requires an explicit fitting rule that is absent.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High opacity, strong temperature–filling-factor covariance and weak isotopologue measurements limit parameter identifiability.
