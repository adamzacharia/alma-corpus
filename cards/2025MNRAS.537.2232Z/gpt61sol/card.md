## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble cloud density contrasts and masks**: Construct the density contrast map using Zhou2024-534 and reuse its cloud masks  
  > "We construct a density contrast map based on the calculation in (cite: Zhou2024-534), which shows the density contrast distribution of all clouds in the galaxy." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field); "We divided the surface density map of the galaxy into two distinct regions using the cloud’s mask obtained in (cite: Zhou2024-534), i.e. the cloud itself and all other material outside of it." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
   - assumption: Density contrast represents star formation capability  
  > "$C$ can be used to represent the star formation capability of molecular clouds." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure radial density contrast ratios**: Divide the galaxy into rings and calculate the ratio of clouds above and below C = 1.63  
  > "Based on the distance to the galaxy center ($R_{\rm G}$), we divided the galaxy into different rings." (Density contrast distribution); "we calculate the proportion of clouds with $C >$ 1.63 and $C <$ 1.63 within each ring." (Density contrast distribution)
   - parameter: Median C ∼ 1.63  
  > "For all clouds, the median value of the density contrast $C$ is $\sim$ 1.63." (Density contrast distribution)
   - parameter: 1 kpc rings spanning RG < 1 to 6 < RG < 7 kpc  
  > "|   $R_{\rm G}$ \< 1    |     14      |     39      |   0.358    |     |" (Table 1); "| 6 \< $R_{\rm G}$ \< 7 |     20      |     18      |   1.111    |     |" (Table 1); "|    distance (kpc)     | $C$ \> 1.63 | $C$ \< 1.63 | proportion |     |" (Table 1)
   - assumption: High- and low-density contrast clouds are relatively uniform within each ring  
  > "the distributions of high- and low-density contrast clouds are relatively uniform within each ring, meaning that they are not biased toward any specific part of the ring." (Density contrast distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Assess outer-ring incompleteness; calculate ratios despite incomplete outer rings  
  > "Despite the incompleteness of the outer rings, calculating the ratio of high- to low-density contrast clouds remains feasible." (Density contrast distribution)
3. **Compare clouds with arms, bubbles and magnetic fields**: Overlay literature arms and bubbles on the density contrast map; estimate each cloud's average magnetic field from the literature map  
  > "We overlay the bubbles and spiral arms on the density contrast map." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field); "Based on the total magnetic field strength map (Fig.12 in (cite: Mulcahy2017-600)), we roughly estimate the average magnetic field strength for each cloud." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
   - parameter: Bubble effective radii larger than 55 pc  
  > "Therefore, we only consider the bubbles with the effective radii larger than 55 pc." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
   - assumption: Bubbles triggering high-density contrast clouds should be larger than the clouds  
  > "If high-density contrast clouds are triggered by bubbles, bubbles should be larger than the clouds." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
   - assumption: Literature magnetic field estimate assumes cosmic-ray/magnetic-field energy equipartition  
  > "assuming equipartition between the energy densities of cosmic rays and the magnetic field" (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inspect cloud mixing, bubble association and magnetic-field correlation  
  > "Overall, high- and low-density contrast clouds are mixed together, primarily concentrated within the spiral arms." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field); "Additionally, high-density contrast clouds do not tend to be distributed around bubbles." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field); "there is no correlation between the magnetic field strength and the density contrast." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
4. **Calculate galactic shear**: Calculate Sg = Σcri/Σ, with Σcri = Aσ ln(C′)/(2πG), and A from the rotation curve across each cloud  
  > "S_{g} = \frac{\Sigma_{\rm cri}}{\Sigma}," (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy); "\Sigma_{\rm cri} = \frac{A \sigma \ln(C^{'})}{2\pi G} \, ," (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy); "A =  0.5 \left( \frac{V}{R_{G}} - \frac{dV}{dR_{G}} \right)" (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - parameter: C′ = 100  
  > "where $C^{'}$ is taken to be 100 in (cite: Hunter1998-493)." (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - parameter: V = (2 × 144.8/π) arctan(RG/0.56 kpc) km/s  
  > "V  =  \frac{2V_{\rm max}}{\pi}\arctan(\frac{R_{G}}{R_{\rm t}}) \approx \frac{2 \times 144.8}{\pi}\arctan(\frac{R_{G}}{0.56~{\rm kpc}})~{\rm km/s}," (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - assumption: Spherical cloud for the Oort constant calculation  
  > "For a spherical cloud of radius $R$, with a rotational velocity $V$ at a radius $R_{G}$, following (cite: Dib2012-758), $A$ can be calculated as" (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - assumption: Shear effective for Sg > 1 and ineffective for Sg < 1  
  > "Shear is effective at tearing apart the gas structure if $S_{g}>1$ and ineffective if $S_{g}<1$." (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with the unity threshold and radial density contrast ratios  
  > "only a few structures have $S_{g} >$1, so the effect of shear can be neglected." (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy); "there is a clear trend where $S_{g}$ decreases as $R_{G}$ increases" (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
5. **Calculate rotation-curve tides**: Calculate TG = V²/RG² − ∂(V²/RG)/∂RG  
  > "T_{G} = \frac{V^{2}}{R_{G}^{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial R_{G}}\left(\frac{V^{2}}{R_{G}}\right)  \, ." (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - parameter: Rotation curve from Lang2020-897  
  > "The analytical rotation curve of NGC 628 derived in (cite: Lang2020-897) using CO (2–1) emission is" (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare the radial TG trend with the density contrast ratios  
  > "The galactic shear and tidal strength calculated by the rotation curve consistently decrease as $R_{G}$ increases, inconsistent with the variation of the proportion between high- and low-density contrast clouds with $R_{G}$." (Discussion and conclusion)
6. **Construct the baryonic surface density map**: Convert HI and 3.6 μm intensities to surface densities and add molecular gas, atomic gas and stellar components  
  > "\frac{\Sigma_{\rm HI}}{\rm M_{\odot}~pc^{-2}}  =  2.0\times10^{-2} \left(\frac{I_{\rm HI}}{\rm K~km s^{-1}}\right)." (Baryonic matter); "\frac{\Sigma_{\rm *}}{\rm M_{\odot}~pc^{-2}}  =  350 \left(\frac{\gamma_{\rm 3.4\mu m}}{0.5}\right)
    \left(\frac{I_{\rm 3.6\mu m}}{\rm MJy~sr^{-1}}\right)." (Baryonic matter); "We combined $\Sigma_{\mathrm{CO}}$, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{HI}}$ and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{*}}$ to create a total surface density map to approximate the baryonic matter distribution in the galaxy, which is dominated by $\Sigma_{\mathrm{*}}$." (Baryonic matter)
   - parameter: γ3.4μm = 0.5 M⊙/L⊙  
  > "Here, $\gamma_{\rm 3.4\mu m}$ is the stellar mass-to-light ratio at $3.4$ $\mu$m, we take a value of 0.5 $M_{\odot}/L_{\odot}$ (cite: Leroy2021-257)." (Baryonic matter)
   - assumption: Optically thin 21 cm emission  
  > "assuming optically thin 21 cm emission" (Baryonic matter)
   - assumption: Near-IR imaging traces the old stellar mass distribution  
  > "Near-IR imaging data can be used to trace the old stellar mass distribution." (Baryonic matter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive tidal tensors**: Obtain the potential in Fourier space, inverse-transform it, calculate its Hessian and estimate external tides from tensor eigenvalues  
  > "\Phi_{k,\,\mathrm{2D}}  =  -\frac{2 \pi G \Sigma_{k}}
{\left| {k}\right|(1 + \left|{k H}\right|)}," (Tidal tensor); "The gravitational potential $\Phi_\mathrm{2D}(x,y)$ is the inverse Fourier transform of $\Phi_{k,\,\mathrm{2D}}$." (Tidal tensor); "The tidal tensor $\mathbf{T}$, defined as $T_{ij}  =  \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial i \partial j}$, only has two eigenvalues in the case of 2D, i.e. $\lambda_{i}$ ($i$ = 1,2)." (Tidal tensor)
   - parameter: H = 0 and H = 50 pc  
  > "We calculated the tidal tensors for both $H = 0$ and $H = 50$ pc." (Tidal tensor)
   - parameter: Td,1 = λ1; Td,2 = λ2; Td,3 = (λ2 − λ1)/2  
  > "For three extreme cases, the external tides can be estimated by $\lambda_{1}$, $\lambda_{2}$, and $(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})/2$, and they are called $T_{\rm d,1}$, $T_{\rm d,2}$, and $T_{\rm d,3}$, respectively." (Tidal tensor)
   - parameter: Calculate using both CO-only and total surface density maps  
  > "For the tidal strengths calculated according to the CO surface density map" (Only the molecular gas); "For the tidal strengths calculated based on the total surface density map" (All matter)
   - assumption: Tidal tensor contains self-gravity and external tides  
  > "The tidal tensor of a structure includes both self-gravity and external tides, that is, $\mathbf{T} =  \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{ext}} + \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{int}}$." (Tidal tensor)
   - assumption: Internal tidal energy should be comparable with cloud self-gravity energy in 3D  
  > "In the case of 3D, for a cloud, the energy derived from $\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{int}}$ should be comparable with its self-gravity energy ($E_{\rm g}$)" (Tidal tensor)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare internal tidal energy with self-gravity energy; H = 0 agrees, whereas H = 50 pc gives systematically smaller energy  
  > "$E_{\rm a,int,H = 0}$ is comparable with $E_{\rm g}$." (Tidal tensor); "However, $E_{\rm a,int,H = 50pc}$ is systematically smaller than $E_{\rm g}$." (Tidal tensor)
8. **Calculate neighboring-material tides pixel by pixel**: Separate each cloud from external material using its mask; sum external pixel tides and average across cloud pixels; calculate scalar and vector superpositions  
  > "T \approx \frac{2GM^{'}}{R^{'3}}." (Pixel-by-pixel computation); "The average tidal strength across all pixels within the cloud subsequently provides an estimate of the average tidal strength experienced by the cloud." (Pixel-by-pixel computation); "we also calculate a vector superposition of tides, enabling direct cancellation of tidal interactions." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
   - parameter: External material within 4 cloud effective radii; compare with 5 effective radii  
  > "we first calculated the average tidal strength exerted by the material within a radius of 4 times the effective radius of a cloud, with the center of the cloud as the origin." (Pixel-by-pixel computation); "Then, we extended the calculation to a radius of 5 times the effective radius of the cloud and performed the same calculation." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
   - parameter: Vector tides decomposed along 2D coordinate axes  
  > "By establishing a 2D coordinate system with the calculated pixel/point in a cloud as the origin, we decomposed all external tides along the coordinate axes." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
   - assumption: Scalar superposition represents collective deformation of flexible gas structures  
  > "To quantify the collective tidal strength within a structure induced by external gravity of the neighboring material, we adopt the scalar superposition." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
   - assumption: 2D plane; projected distances can overestimate tidal strength  
  > "Our computation is limited to a 2D plane." (Pixel-by-pixel computation); "the 2D computation results in an underestimation of the distance between them, consequently leading to an overestimation of the tidal strength (the maximum computation, $T_{\rm max}$)." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: The 4- and 5-effective-radius calculations yield similar results for all clouds  
  > "for all clouds in the galaxy, both calculations yield similar results, indicating that the tidal effects of material beyond 4 times the effective radius of a cloud can be neglected." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
9. **Compare tidal predictions with radial cloud contrasts**: Compare CO-only and total-matter tidal calculations, tensor and pixel methods, scale heights, and scalar versus vector radial trends  
  > "Except for $T_{G}$, the other quantities do not vary with the distance to the galaxy center." (Only the molecular gas); "when $R_{\rm G} <$ 4 kpc, $T_{\rm v,tot}$ and $T_{\rm max,tot}$ decrease as $R_{\rm G}$ increases, and then remains relatively constant when $R_{\rm G} >$ 4 kpc." (All matter); "their trends of variation with respect to $R_{\rm G}$ are consistent." (Scale height and scalar versus vector superpositions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare tidal radial trends with the high-/low-density contrast cloud ratio  
  > "This aligns well with the dependence of the proportion of high- and low-density contrast clouds on $R_{\rm G}$" (All matter)

## Qualitative findings

- High-/low-density contrast cloud ratio increases outward and then remains relatively stable  
  > "The proportion of high- and low-density contrast clouds initially increases with the distance to the galactic center ($R_{G}$) and then keeps relatively stable." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Spiral arms, bubbles and magnetic fields do not explain the density contrast variations  
  > "The spiral arms, bubbles and magnetic fields are not responsible for the variations in density contrast observed among molecular clouds." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Shear can be neglected  
  > "only a few structures have $S_{g} >$1, so the effect of shear can be neglected." (Shear and tidal fields of the galaxy)
- Total surface density is dominated by stars  
  > "We combined $\Sigma_{\mathrm{CO}}$, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{HI}}$ and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{*}}$ to create a total surface density map to approximate the baryonic matter distribution in the galaxy, which is dominated by $\Sigma_{\mathrm{*}}$." (Baryonic matter)
- CO-only neighboring-material tidal strengths do not vary with galactocentric radius  
  > "the tidal strengths calculated according to the CO (2-1) surface density map do not vary with $R_{G}$, because the galactic center does not have significantly higher CO surface density." (Discussion and conclusion)
- H = 50 pc lowers tidal strengths without changing the other comparisons  
  > "In the two cases mentioned above, $H = 50$ pc generates lower tidal strengths than $H = 0$, but is otherwise identical to $H = 0$ in all other respects." (Scale height and scalar versus vector superpositions)
- Pixel-by-pixel tides better trace radial variation; tensor analysis supplies the magnitude estimate  
  > "The pixel-by-pixel computation can more precisely reflect the variation in tidal strength with $R_{\rm G}$ than the tidal tensor analysis" (Consequences of the tidal analysis); "Therefore, by combining these two methods, we can achieve both appropriate variation and magnitude estimates of the tidal strength across the galaxy." (Consequences of the tidal analysis)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Include atomic gas and stars because molecular gas alone is inadequate for neighboring-material tides  
  > "Although we focus solely on molecular clouds as sites of star formation in the galaxy, it’s inadequate to only consider molecular gas when accounting for external tidal effects on the clouds." (Baryonic matter); "This is because the stellar mass surface density is significantly higher than the molecular gas surface density, indicating stronger tidal effects." (Consequences of the tidal analysis)
- Why this method: Combine pixel-by-pixel and tensor methods to estimate tidal variation and magnitude  
  > "Therefore, by combining these two methods, we can achieve both appropriate variation and magnitude estimates of the tidal strength across the galaxy." (Consequences of the tidal analysis)
- Alternative: Spiral arms, bubbles and magnetic fields rejected as explanations of the density contrast variation  
  > "Therefore, the spiral arms, bubbles and magnetic fields are not the reasons for the variation in the density contrast of molecular clouds with galactocentric radius." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
- Alternative: CO-only surface density fails to reproduce the radial neighboring-material tidal trend  
  > "Except for $T_{G}$, the other quantities do not vary with the distance to the galaxy center." (Only the molecular gas); "Because the calculations of the other quantities are based on the CO surface density map." (Only the molecular gas)
- Alternative: Compare H = 0 with H = 50 pc  
  > "We calculated the tidal tensors for both $H = 0$ and $H = 50$ pc." (Tidal tensor)
- Alternative: Compare scalar and vector tidal superpositions  
  > "To compare with the scalar superposition, we also calculate a vector superposition of tides, enabling direct cancellation of tidal interactions." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
- How they decided it was enough: Similar results within 4 and 5 effective radii justify neglecting more distant material  
  > "for all clouds in the galaxy, both calculations yield similar results, indicating that the tidal effects of material beyond 4 times the effective radius of a cloud can be neglected." (Pixel-by-pixel computation)
- Limitation (authors): Only rings with radius ≤ 4 kpc are complete  
  > "only rings with a radius of $\leq$ 4 kpc are complete." (Density contrast distribution)
- Limitation (authors): Cloud magnetic field strengths are roughly estimated  
  > "Based on the total magnetic field strength map (Fig.12 in (cite: Mulcahy2017-600)), we roughly estimate the average magnetic field strength for each cloud." (Spiral arm, bubble and magnetic field)
- Limitation (authors): Baryonic surface density is a rough approximation; dark matter is not included in that map  
  > "Strictly speaking, we should include all baryonic matter, and even dark matter." (Baryonic matter); "As a rough approximation, apart from molecular gas surface density ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{CO}}$), we also consider atomic gas surface density ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{HI}}$) and stellar mass surface density ($\Sigma_{\mathrm{*}}$)." (Baryonic matter)
- Limitation (authors): Unknown 3D material distribution prevents correct pixel-by-pixel tidal magnitudes  
  > "cannot provide a correct magnitude estimate of the tidal strength due to the lack of knowledge about the specific 3D spatial distribution of the material in the galaxy." (Consequences of the tidal analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Single-galaxy case study; generality requires larger galaxy samples  
  > "This work is merely a case study, and future work needs to apply similar methods to large galaxy samples containing different types of galaxies to verify the generality of the conclusions presented here." (Discussion and conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Matching radial trends alone does not establish that tides cause suppressed collapse or star formation.
