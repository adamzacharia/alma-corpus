## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image spectral line data**: Standard calibration with quasars and planets, followed by CASA spectral line deconvolution and continuum subtraction.  
  > "Calibration was carried out following standard procedures, using quasars J0538–440, J0607–085, Callisto, and Ganymede. Spectral line imaging was achieved with the CASA package, while data analysis was performed using the GILDAS package." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Beam: 0.65" x 0.47" (PA 35 deg); rms noise: 3–4 mJy beam^-1 per 0.44 km s^-1 channel  
  > "The continuum-subtracted images have a typical clean-beam FWHM of $0.65\ensuremath{''}\times0.47\ensuremath{''}$ (PA = 35$\ensuremath{^\circ}$), and an rms noise level of 3–4 mJy beam$^{-1}$ in 0.44 km s$^{-1}$ channels." (Observations)
   - assumption: Vsys = +1.7 km s^-1  
  > "we adopt here +1.7 km s$^{-1}$ (Lee et al. 2014)." (Results and discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Line identification using JPL molecular database  
  > "The lines were identified using the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) molecular database (Pickett et al. 1998)." (Results and discussion)
2. **Derive rotational temperature and column density for CH3CHO**: LTE optically thin rotational diagram analysis using unblended lines (less than 30% contamination).  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1) indicates temperature of 87$\pm$47 K and a column density of 2$\pm$1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$" (CH_3CHO emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Assumed source size = 0.3" (half the synthesized beam)  
  > "Given that the CH$_3$CHO emission is spatially unresolved, we assume a source size equal to half a beam, namely 0$\ensuremath{''}$3." (CH_3CHO emission)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), optically thin emission  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1)" (CH_3CHO emission)
   - fitting: Linear fit to rotational diagram  
  > "Table 1 lists the emission lines observed towards HH212–MM1 and used for the standard analysis of the rotational diagram (Fig. A.1), which allows us to derive $T_{\rm rot}$ = 87$\pm$47 K and $N_{\rm tot}$ = 2$\pm$1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$." (The CH_3CHO rotational diagram)
   - validation: Synthetic spectra compared to observations, optical depth checked (tau < 0.4)  
  > "see the the rotational diagram in Fig. A.1 and the synthetic spectra in Fig. 2). With these values the opacity is less than 0.4." (CH_3CHO emission)
3. **Model HDO line emission with non-LTE LVG radiative transfer**: Non-LTE Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) modeling in plane-parallel geometry across density, column density, and source size grids.  
  > "we compare the observed emission with that predicted by a non-LTE LVG (Large Velocity Gradient) model using the code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003), a plane parallel geometry, the collisional coefficients for the system HDO-H$_2$ computed by Faure et al. (2012)" (HDO emission)
   - software: Ceccarelli et al. (2003) LVG code
   - parameter: Source sizes 0.3" and 0.06"; densities 10^7 to 10^10 cm^-3; Boltzmann distribution for ortho-to-para H2 ratio  
  > "assuming a Boltzmann distribution for the ortho-to-para H$_2$ ratio" (HDO emission); "the upper panel shows the case of a source size equal to half a beam (0$\ensuremath{''}$3) and the lower panel shows a case with a size that is lower by a factor of five" (HDO emission); "The plots cover densities from 10$^7$ to $10^{10}$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission)
   - assumption: N(HDO)/N(H2) <= 3.2 x 10^-6 (<= 10% of D/H elemental abundance)  
  > "The low end, 10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$, is set by the constraint that the HDO abundance has to be smaller than 10% of the D/H elemental abundance (in agreement with previous observations of HDO/H$_2$O towards hot corinos, assuming an initial H$_2$O abundance of 3 $\times$ 10$^{-5}$; see Taquet et al. 2014, 2015), namely $N$(HDO)/$N$(H$_2$) $\leq 3.2 \times 10^{-6}$" (HDO emission)
   - fitting: Grid parameter search comparing modeled integrated line intensity to measured 4.9 K km s^-1  
  > "Figure 4 shows the temperature versus the HDO column density predicted to generate the observed signal (see Table 1)." (HDO emission)
   - validation: Comparison between optically thin and optically thick regimes against line profile shapes and spatial resolution constraints  
  > "In order to understand the relative importance of each, we study two limit situations: (i) the optically thin case where the kinematics will dominate and (ii) the optically thick case where the profile will be strongly affected by radiative effects." (HDO emission)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of a hot corino in the Orion star-forming region, confirmed by twelve high-excitation CH3CHO lines and one HDO line towards HH212-MM1.  
  > "We report the first detection of a hot corino in Orion." (abstract); "For the first time these high-excitation molecular lines have been detected towards the HH212 inner region revealing the existence of hot gas around the MM1 protostar driving the bipolar SiO jet." (Results and discussion)
- HDO line exhibits an asymmetric low-velocity profile with brighter redshifted emission, inconsistent with an axisymmetric Keplerian disk or infalling envelope, indicating low-velocity outflowing gas such as a disk wind.  
  > "The last and most likely possibility is that the low-velocity HDO emission is dominated by outflowing motions." (Optically thin case); "The asymmetric HDO profile at low velocities indicates that at least some of the deuterated water is associated with slow outflowing gas" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HH212 is a prototypical Class 0 protostar hosting both a bright bipolar jet and a compact rotating disk, making it the ideal laboratory to search for a hot corino and study its link with the disk and outflow.  
  > "The HH212 region is thus, so far, the only protostar where both a bright bipolar jet and a compact rotating disk have been revealed. Given its association with all the ingredients of the Sun-like star formation recipe, HH212 stands out as being the perfect target" (Introduction: The HH212 laboratory)
- Why this method: Optically thin LTE rotational diagrams are suitable for unblended CH3CHO transitions, while non-LTE LVG radiative transfer modeling is needed to interpret the high-excitation HDO line across optical depth and density regimes.  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1) indicates temperature of 87$\pm$47 K" (CH_3CHO emission); "we compare the observed emission with that predicted by a non-LTE LVG (Large Velocity Gradient) model using the code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003)" (HDO emission)
- Alternative: Rotating disk origin for low-velocity HDO asymmetry was rejected because the factor of 2 brightness difference and velocity offset between red and blue peaks would require unprecedented disk non-axisymmetry.  
  > "However, the low-velocity range of the HDO line being due to the protostellar disk is not supported by the large (factor of 2) difference between (i) the blue and red peak brightnesses and (ii) the blue and red peak velocities" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Rotating infalling envelope origin for HDO was ruled out because 1 km s^-1 infall motions arise at 0.3" radii which would be spatially resolved, and infalling gas would produce redshifted absorption rather than the observed blueshifted dip.  
  > "We can also rule out an origin from the rotating infalling envelope; infall motions of 1 km s$^{-1}$ arise from radii 0$\ensuremath{''}$3 (Lee et al. 2014), which would lead to spatially resolved peak emission, unlike the observed emission." (Optically thin case); "because an optically thick infalling envelope would lead to redshifted absorption" (Optically thick case)
- Alternative: Density of 10^6 cm^-3 for HDO was ruled out because N(HDO) would exceed 10% of the elemental D/H abundance to reproduce the observed line intensity.  
  > "We find that it is not possible, then, to reproduce the HDO line intensity and we rule out a density of 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Submillimetre continuum is optically thick at the peak, preventing a direct measurement of H2 column density towards HH212-MM1 from dust continuum.  
  > "Unfortunately, we cannot use the continuum emission to estimate the H$_2$ column density (and hence the CH$_3$CHO abundance) because the submillimetre continuum peak seen in interferometric maps is optically thick (Codella et al. 2007; Lee et al. 2008, 2014)." (CH_3CHO emission)
- Limitation (authors): Emission from CH3CHO and HDO is spatially unresolved at the 0.5"–0.65" resolution, requiring assumed source sizes for column density calculations.  
  > "Figure 1 shows that CH$_3$CHO and HDO are only observed towards the protostellar position and are spatially unresolved." (Results and discussion); "Given that the CH$_3$CHO emission is spatially unresolved, we assume a source size equal to half a beam, namely 0$\ensuremath{''}$3." (CH_3CHO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The physical interpretation depends strongly on whether HDO is assumed to be optically thin or thick, since higher resolution observations are needed to confirm the true emitting size (0.3" vs. 0.06").
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Acetaldehyde abundance calculation relies on H2 column density values borrowed from different protostars (NGC1333-IRAS2A and IRAS4A).
