1. **Spectral line imaging and line identification towards HH212-MM1**: Image continuum-subtracted spectral cubes, extract spectra towards the MM1 continuum peak, and identify molecular transitions using the JPL molecular database.  
  > "Spectral line imaging was achieved with the CASA package, while data analysis was performed using the GILDAS[^1] package." (Observations); "The lines were identified using the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) molecular database (Pickett et al. 1998)." (Results and discussion)
   - software: CASA, GILDAS
   - parameter: Velocity resolution 0.44 km s^-1; clean beam 0.65'' x 0.47'' (PA = 35 deg)  
  > "The continuum-subtracted images have a typical clean-beam FWHM of $0.65\ensuremath{''}\times0.47\ensuremath{''}$ (PA = 35$\ensuremath{^\circ}$), and an rms noise level of 3–4 mJy beam$^{-1}$ in 0.44 km s$^{-1}$ channels." (Observations)
   - assumption: V_sys = +1.7 km s^-1  
  > "The value of +1.3 km s$^{-1}$ adopted in Codella et al. (2014) was affected by a shift in the frequency scale; we adopt here +1.7 km s$^{-1}$ (Lee et al. 2014)." (Footnotes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive CH3CHO rotational temperature, column density, and abundance**: Perform LTE optically thin rotational diagram analysis and synthetic spectrum modeling on unblended CH3CHO lines, then estimate abundance using literature H2 column densities.  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1) indicates temperature of 87$\pm$47 K and a column density of 2$\pm$1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ (see the the rotational diagram in Fig. A.1 and the synthetic spectra in Fig. 2)." (CH$_3$CHO emission); "Taquet et al. (2015) measured N(H$_2$) $\simeq$ 10$^{24}$–10$^{25}$ cm$^{-2}$ towards the NGC1333-IRAS2A and IRAS4A hot corinos on a spatial scale of $\sim$ 470 AU, i.e. a factor $\sim$ 3 higher than is sampled here. If we take these values and 0$\ensuremath{''}$3 as emitting size, then $X_{\rm CH_3CHO}$ $\sim$ 0.2–2 $\times$ 10$^{-9}$" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Selection of 5 CH3CHO lines with < 30% contamination from blended lines  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1)" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin emission  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1) indicates temperature of 87$\pm$47 K and a column density of 2$\pm$1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: Source size equal to half a beam (0.3'', and tested 0.06'')  
  > "Given that the CH$_3$CHO emission is spatially unresolved, we assume a source size equal to half a beam, namely 0$\ensuremath{''}$3." (CH$_3$CHO emission); "In the case of a smaller emitting size, e.g. 0$\ensuremath{''}$06 (as assumed for HDO in Sect. 3), $N_{\rm CH_3CHO}$ increases by a factor of $\sim$ 20." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: N(H2) ~ 10^24 - 10^25 cm^-2 from NGC1333-IRAS2A and IRAS4A  
  > "Taquet et al. (2015) measured N(H$_2$) $\simeq$ 10$^{24}$–10$^{25}$ cm$^{-2}$ towards the NGC1333-IRAS2A and IRAS4A hot corinos on a spatial scale of $\sim$ 470 AU, i.e. a factor $\sim$ 3 higher than is sampled here. If we take these values and 0$\ensuremath{''}$3 as emitting size, then $X_{\rm CH_3CHO}$ $\sim$ 0.2–2 $\times$ 10$^{-9}$" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - fitting: Standard rotational diagram fitting  
  > "Table 1 lists the emission lines observed towards HH212–MM1 and used for the standard analysis of the rotational diagram (Fig. A.1), which allows us to derive $T_{\rm rot}$ = 87$\pm$47 K and $N_{\rm tot}$ = 2$\pm$1 $\times$ 10$^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$." (The CH$_3$CHO rotational diagram)
   - validation: Checked line opacity (< 0.4) and compared synthetic spectra with observed spectra (Fig. 2).  
  > "(see the the rotational diagram in Fig. A.1 and the synthetic spectra in Fig. 2). With these values the opacity is less than 0.4." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
3. **Non-LTE LVG radiative transfer modeling of HDO emission**: Compare observed HDO integrated intensity with predictions from a non-LTE LVG radiative transfer code across grids of density, temperature, and column density for optically thin and optically thick cases.  
  > "To this end, we compare the observed emission with that predicted by a non-LTE LVG (Large Velocity Gradient) model using the code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003), a plane parallel geometry, the collisional coefficients for the system HDO-H$_2$ computed by Faure et al. (2012)[^3], and assuming a Boltzmann distribution for the ortho-to-para H$_2$ ratio[^4]." (HDO emission); "To this end, in the non-LTE LVG predictions in Fig. 4 we add dashed curves where the HDO line opacity is equal to unity, i.e. we assume a moderate thickness to explain the line absorption." (Optically thick case)
   - software: LVG code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003)
   - parameter: H2 density grid from 10^7 to 10^10 cm^-3 (and tested 10^6 cm^-3); source sizes of 0.3'' and 0.06'' (testing 0.04'' to 0.08'')  
  > "Given that the HDO emission is spatially unresolved, the upper panel shows the case of a source size equal to half a beam (0$\ensuremath{''}$3) and the lower panel shows a case with a size that is lower by a factor of five (see Sect. 3.2.2). The plots cover densities from 10$^7$ to $10^{10}$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission); "one needs to assume a larger beam dilution factor requiring a source size smaller than 0$\ensuremath{''}$08 (37 AU) and larger than 0$\ensuremath{''}$04 (18 AU). The lower panel of Fig. 4 shows the intermediate case, with 0$\ensuremath{''}$06 (28 AU)." (Optically thick case)
   - assumption: Plane parallel geometry and Boltzmann distribution for the ortho-to-para H2 ratio  
  > "To this end, we compare the observed emission with that predicted by a non-LTE LVG (Large Velocity Gradient) model using the code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003), a plane parallel geometry, the collisional coefficients for the system HDO-H$_2$ computed by Faure et al. (2012)[^3], and assuming a Boltzmann distribution for the ortho-to-para H$_2$ ratio[^4]." (HDO emission)
   - assumption: N(HDO)/N(H2) <= 3.2 x 10^-6 (HDO abundance < 10% of elemental D/H, assuming initial H2O abundance of 3 x 10^-5)  
  > "The low end, 10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$, is set by the constraint that the HDO abundance has to be smaller than 10% of the D/H elemental abundance (in agreement with previous observations of HDO/H$_2$O towards hot corinos, assuming an initial H$_2$O abundance of 3 $\times$ 10$^{-5}$; see Taquet et al. 2014, 2015), namely $N$(HDO)/$N$(H$_2$) $\leq 3.2 \times 10^{-6}$, where $N$(H$_2$) is consistently derived from the density and size of the source." (HDO emission)
   - assumption: Typical water abundance of ~ 10^-4 to estimate HDO/H2O in optically thick case  
  > "Assuming a typical water abundance of $\sim 10^{-4}$ would imply HDO/H$_2$O $\sim 1.7\times 10^{-2}$–$10^{-3}$, consistent with the values measured in hot corinos (Persson et al. 2014; Ceccarelli et al. 2015)." (Optically thick case)
   - fitting: Grid comparison of predicted T vs N(HDO) curves reproducing observed signal and tau = 1 curves  
  > "Figure 4 shows the temperature versus the HDO column density predicted to generate the observed signal (see Table 1)." (HDO emission); "In order to avoid excessive emission, i.e. to have the two families of curves intersect, and to constrain that $N$(HDO)/$N$(H$_2$) does not exceed the 10% of the D/H elemental abundance, one needs to assume a larger beam dilution factor" (Optically thick case)
   - validation: Compared derived HDO/H2O ratio (1.7 x 10^-2 to 10^-3) with values measured in other hot corinos.  
  > "Assuming a typical water abundance of $\sim 10^{-4}$ would imply HDO/H$_2$O $\sim 1.7\times 10^{-2}$–$10^{-3}$, consistent with the values measured in hot corinos (Persson et al. 2014; Ceccarelli et al. 2015)." (Optically thick case)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of a hot corino in Orion towards the HH212-MM1 protostar.  
  > "We report the first detection of a hot corino in Orion." (abstract); "We thus report the detection of the first hot corino in Orion." (Conclusions)
- CH3CHO and HDO emissions are observed only towards the protostellar position and are spatially unresolved.  
  > "Figure 1 shows that CH$_3$CHO and HDO are only observed towards the protostellar position and are spatially unresolved." (Results and discussion)
- The HDO line profile is asymmetric at low velocities (<= 2 km s^-1 from V_sys) with brighter redshifted emission, indicating a contribution from slow outflowing gas, possibly a disk wind.

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HH212 is the only protostar where both a bright bipolar jet and a compact rotating disk have been revealed, making it ideal for studying the link between hot corino, disk, and outflow with high-resolution (<= 100 AU) ALMA mm observations.  
  > "In addition to the physical and evolutionary connection, the three phenomena have one thing in common: they all need to be studied using mm observations at high spatial resolution (on scales $\leq$ 100 AU)." (Introduction: The HH212 laboratory); "The HH212 region is thus, so far, the only protostar where both a bipolar jet and a compact rotating disk have been revealed. Given its association with all the ingredients of the Sun-like star formation recipe, HH212 stands out as being the perfect target for investigating the link between hot corino, disk, and outflow in the protostellar stage." (Introduction: The HH212 laboratory)
- Why this method: Used unblended CH3CHO lines (< 30% contamination) for LTE rotational diagram analysis; examined both optically thin and optically thick limit cases with a non-LTE LVG model for HDO to separate kinematic and radiative opacity effects.  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO (using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1)" (CH$_3$CHO emission); "In order to understand the relative importance of each, we study two limit situations: (i) the optically thin case where the kinematics will dominate and (ii) the optically thick case where the profile will be strongly affected by radiative effects." (HDO emission)
- Alternative: Origin of optically thin low-velocity HDO asymmetry from a rotating protostellar disk (rejected due to factor of 2 brightness difference and peak velocity asymmetry between blue and red shifted components).  
  > "However, the low-velocity range of the HDO line being due to the protostellar disk is not supported by the large (factor of 2) difference between (i) the blue and red peak brightnesses and (ii) the blue and red peak velocities ($V_{\rm LSR}$–$V_{\rm sys}$ at $\leq$ +1 and $\sim$ –2 km s$^{-1}$)." (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Origin of HDO emission from the rotating infalling envelope (rejected because infall motions of 1 km s^-1 arise from radii 0.3'' which would be spatially resolved, and an optically thick infalling envelope would produce redshifted rather than blueshifted absorption).  
  > "We can also rule out an origin from the rotating infalling envelope; infall motions of 1 km s$^{-1}$ arise from radii 0$\ensuremath{''}$3 (Lee et al. 2014), which would lead to spatially resolved peak emission, unlike the observed emission." (Optically thin case); "If we assume that the HDO line shape is determined by the line opacity rather than pure kinematics, then the line asymmetry is due to blueshifted absorption, revealing again outflowing gas (because an optically thick infalling envelope would lead to redshifted absorption)." (Optically thick case)
- Alternative: Origin of HDO from the inner portion of the fast jet traced by SiO (rejected because the SiO(8-7) profile is much broader, up to +/- 20 km s^-1).  
  > "The SiO(8–7) profile towards MM1 derived from the same ALMA dataset (Podio et al. 2015) is definitely too broad (up to $\pm$ 20 km s$^{-1}$) for HDO to probe the inner portion of the fast jet traced by SiO (Fig. 1)." (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Gas density of 10^6 cm^-3 for the HDO emitting region (rejected because it cannot reproduce the observed HDO line intensity under the D/H abundance constraint).  
  > "In particular, for a size of 0$\ensuremath{''}$3, if the density is 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$, then $N$(HDO) should be $\leq 7 \times 10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$. We find that it is not possible, then, to reproduce the HDO line intensity and we rule out a density of 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): CH3CHO and HDO emissions are spatially unresolved, requiring assumed source sizes (0.3'' or 0.04''-0.08'').  
  > "Figure 1 shows that CH$_3$CHO and HDO are only observed towards the protostellar position and are spatially unresolved." (Results and discussion); "Given that the CH$_3$CHO emission is spatially unresolved, we assume a source size equal to half a beam, namely 0$\ensuremath{''}$3." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
- Limitation (authors): Submillimetre continuum emission towards MM1 is optically thick, preventing a direct estimate of N(H2) from the dust continuum and requiring literature N(H2) values from other hot corinos.  
  > "Unfortunately, we cannot use the continuum emission to estimate the H$_2$ column density (and hence the CH$_3$CHO abundance) because the submillimetre continuum peak seen in interferometric maps is optically thick (Codella et al. 2007; Lee et al. 2008, 2014)." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
- Limitation (authors): Only a single HDO line is detected, leaving an ambiguity between optically thin and optically thick regimes.  
  > "Specifically, we report the detection of twelve high-lying ($E_{\rm u} \geq 150$ K) lines from acetaldehyde (CH$_3$CHO) and one ($E_{\rm u}$ = 335 K) line from deuterated water (HDO)." (Introduction: The HH212 laboratory); "In order to understand the relative importance of each, we study two limit situations: (i) the optically thin case where the kinematics will dominate and (ii) the optically thick case where the profile will be strongly affected by radiative effects." (HDO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CH3CHO rotational diagram uses 5 transitions over a very narrow upper energy range (153 K to 179 K), resulting in a large uncertainty on the rotational temperature (87 +/- 47 K).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The CH3CHO abundance relies on N(H2) values adopted from different protostars (NGC1333-IRAS2A and IRAS4A) measured on a 3x larger spatial scale (~470 AU).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Constraints on the HDO emitting region rely on a single high-excitation transition (Eu = 335 K) and an assumed upper limit on HDO/H2 based on elemental D/H and assumed water abundance.
