## Analysis recipe

1. **identify high-excitation lines towards MM1**: Line identification in the 8 GHz spectrum towards the protostar with the JPL database  
  > "The lines were identified using the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) molecular database (Pickett et al. 1998)." (Results and discussion)
   - software: GILDAS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **CH3CHO LTE rotational diagram**: LTE optically thin rotational diagram using lines with <30% contamination from blends  
  > "using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - parameter: Source size 0.3″ (half a beam)  
  > "Given that the CH$_3$CHO emission is spatially unresolved, we assume a source size equal to half a beam, namely 0$\ensuremath{''}$3." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Synthetic spectra compared with observations; opacity checked < 0.4  
  > "With these values the opacity is less than 0.4." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
3. **derive CH3CHO abundance**: Divide N(CH3CHO) by N(H2) from IRAS2A/IRAS4A hot corinos (continuum optically thick)  
  > "we cannot use the continuum emission to estimate the H$_2$ column density (and hence the CH$_3$CHO abundance) because the submillimetre continuum peak seen in interferometric maps is optically thick" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: N(H2) ≃ 10^24–10^25 cm^-2 (Taquet et al. 2015), size 0.3″  
  > "If we take these values and 0$\ensuremath{''}$3 as emitting size" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with IRAS16293-2422 value (Jaber et al. 2014)  
  > "in agreement with the value recently derived in the IRAS16293-2422 hot corino" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
4. **non-LTE LVG modelling of HDO**: Non-LTE LVG, plane parallel geometry, Faure et al. 2012 collision rates; T vs N(HDO) curves reproducing observed intensity  
  > "we compare the observed emission with that predicted by a non-LTE LVG (Large Velocity Gradient) model using the code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003), a plane parallel geometry" (HDO emission)
   - software: LVG code of Ceccarelli et al. (2003)
   - parameter: Densities 10^7–10^10 cm^-3; source sizes 0.3″ and 0.06″  
  > "The plots cover densities from 10$^7$ to $10^{10}$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission)
   - parameter: N(HDO)/N(H2) ≤ 3.2e-6  
  > "namely $N$(HDO)/$N$(H$_2$) $\leq 3.2 \times 10^{-6}$" (HDO emission)
   - assumption: Boltzmann distribution for H2 ortho-to-para ratio  
  > "assuming a Boltzmann distribution for the ortho-to-para H$_2$ ratio" (HDO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **interpret HDO asymmetry (thin vs thick) and constrain size/density**: Optically thin: kinematic origin (disk, envelope, outflow); optically thick: add τ=1 curves and require intersection with observed-signal curves  
  > "in the non-LTE LVG predictions in Fig. 4 we add dashed curves where the HDO line opacity is equal to unity" (Optically thick case)
   - software: LVG code of Ceccarelli et al. (2003)
   - assumption: Typical water abundance ~10^-4  
  > "Assuming a typical water abundance of $\sim 10^{-4}$" (Optically thick case)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: HDO/H2O compared with hot corino values  
  > "consistent with the values measured in hot corinos (Persson et al. 2014; Ceccarelli et al. 2015)" (Optically thick case)

## Qualitative findings

- CH3CHO and HDO emission is compact, spatially unresolved, only at the protostar position  
  > "Figure 1 shows that CH$_3$CHO and HDO are only observed towards the protostellar position and are spatially unresolved." (Results and discussion)
- HDO profile asymmetric at low velocity, redshifted side brighter  
  > "the redshifted emission is clearly brighter than the blueshifted." (HDO emission)
- Disk and infalling envelope origins of the HDO asymmetry disfavoured; outflowing gas most likely  
  > "The last and most likely possibility is that the low-velocity HDO emission is dominated by outflowing motions." (Optically thin case)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HH212 has both a bright jet and compact rotating disk; ideal to link hot corino, disk and outflow  
  > "HH212 stands out as being the perfect target for investigating the link between hot corino, disk, and outflow in the protostellar stage." (Introduction: The HH212 laboratory)
- Why this method: Two limiting cases (thin vs thick) bracket kinematic vs radiative causes of the HDO profile  
  > "In order to understand the relative importance of each, we study two limit situations" (HDO emission)
- Alternative: Rotating disk origin of HDO asymmetry rejected (would need strong non-axisymmetry)  
  > "This would require a high degree of non-axisymmetry in the molecular disk emission" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Infalling envelope origin rejected  
  > "We can also rule out an origin from the rotating infalling envelope" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Fast SiO jet origin rejected (too broad)  
  > "is definitely too broad (up to $\pm$ 20 km s$^{-1}$) for HDO to probe the inner portion of the fast jet traced by SiO" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Density of 10^6 cm^-3 ruled out  
  > "we rule out a density of 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$" (HDO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Continuum optically thick, so H2 column cannot be measured directly  
  > "Unfortunately, we cannot use the continuum emission to estimate the H$_2$ column density" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
- Limitation (authors): Emission spatially unresolved; source size assumed; smaller size raises N(CH3CHO) by ~20  
  > "In the case of a smaller emitting size, e.g. 0$\ensuremath{''}$06 (as assumed for HDO in Sect. 3), $N_{\rm CH_3CHO}$ increases by a factor of $\sim$ 20." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abundance relies on N(H2) borrowed from other hot corinos measured on ~3x larger scales
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single HDO line cannot discriminate optically thin vs thick; conclusions depend on assumed source sizes
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rotational temperature uncertainty is large (±47 K) from few lines with narrow Eu range
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one beam's worth of resolution; hot corino claim based on unresolved emission
