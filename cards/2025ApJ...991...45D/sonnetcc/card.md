## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA SiO, CO and continuum**: Calibrate with the standard CASA pipeline and image with tclean at robust +2.0; bin line cubes to 2.0 km/s  
  > "All maps were produced using the `TCLEAN` task with a robust weighting factor of $+2.0$." (2.2 ALMA data); "The line cubes were binned to a velocity resolution 2.0 km s$^{-1}$ to improve sensitivity." (2.2 ALMA data)
   - software: CASA 6.5.4, tclean
   - parameter: robust = +2.0  
  > "a robust weighting factor of $+2.0$" (2.2 ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Detect bright emission lines in JWST/MIRI cubes**: peakmoment first stage: extract spectra from a 0.5'' radius aperture on the jet, smooth, find local maxima and keep peaks above 5-7 sigma  
  > "extracted using this tool from a circular aperture of 0.5$''$ radius centered on the collimated jet" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation); "Peaks above a user-defined signal-to-noise threshold (typically 5-7$\sigma$) are recorded as potential emission line candidates." (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation)
   - software: peakmoment, scipy.signal.find_peaks
   - parameter: Channel 4 limited to lambda < 26 um  
  > "For Channel 4, we limited the wavelength range to $\lambda < 26~\mu$m to avoid regions contaminated by blended or noisy lines." (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation)
   - fitting: Linear (degree 1) continuum fit  
  > "A simple linear continuum fit (polynomial of degree 1) is applied here" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Make continuum-subtracted JWST moment-0 maps**: Per line, take a subcube, fit a pixel-wise polynomial to line-free channels, subtract it and compute moment-0 from channels above 3 sigma of the residual  
  > "A pixel-wise polynomial fit of degree 1 is applied across the continuum channels to model and subtract the continuum from original emission" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation); "zeroth-order moment maps (integrated intensity) are computed using the `spectral_cube.SpectralCube` library" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation)
   - software: peakmoment, spectral-cube
   - parameter: Spectral window +/-0.1-0.2 um of line-free channels; exclude +/-0.014-0.02 um around the peak; polynomial order up to 2  
  > "a small user-defined spectral window typically spanning $\pm$ 0.1$-$0.2 $\mu$m of line-free channels" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation); "By default, we exclude $\pm$0.014–0.02 $\mu$m around the peak" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation); "To prevent overfitting, we restrict the maximum polynomial degree to 2 (quadratic)." (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation)
   - fitting: Polynomial continuum fit (default linear)  
  > "By default, we use a first-order (linear) polynomial fit" (3.1 JWST: Tools for Spectral Extraction and Moment Map Generation)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Overlay ALMA maps on JWST maps**: Resample ALMA maps onto the JWST pixel grid with bilinear interpolation, keeping native beams, and overlay SiO contours on JWST moment maps  
  > "we resampled the ALMA maps onto the JWST pixel grid using the `reproject.reproject_interp` function, which applies bilinear interpolation to estimate values at the new pixel locations." (3.3 Combining ALMA Maps with JWST Observations)
   - software: reproject
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors caution that differing resolutions can make compact features look extended or blended  
  > "Due to the differing spatial resolutions, any morphological comparison between ALMA and JWST should be interpreted with caution." (3.3 Combining ALMA Maps with JWST Observations)
5. **Derive H2 excitation temperatures, A_V and column densities**: Rotational (Boltzmann) diagram of MIRI H2 0-0 lines from a 0.5'' aperture on knot B2; A_V from ortho/para line-flux ratios; extinction-corrected single- and two-temperature fits  
  > "we employed the rotational diagram (Boltzmann plot) method using the observed pure rotational transitions in the mid-infrared, as detected by JWST/MIRI." (3.2 Excitation Temperature Calculation from H2 Lines); "We extracted integrated fluxes using an aperture of 0.5$''$ radius centered on a bright" (3.2 Excitation Temperature Calculation from H2 Lines)
   - parameter: Linear fit of ln(Nu/gu) vs Eu gives slope -1/Tex  
  > "A linear fit to this plot gives a slope of $-1/T_{\mathrm{ex}}$." (3.2 Excitation Temperature Calculation from H2 Lines)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin H2 lines  
  > "Assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), the population of the upper levels follows the Boltzmann distribution:" (3.2 Excitation Temperature Calculation from H2 Lines); "Assuming the $H_2$ lines are optically thin, the upper level column density is related to the observed line flux $F_\lambda$ by:" (3.2 Excitation Temperature Calculation from H2 Lines)
   - assumption: Weingartner & Draine mid-IR extinction curve with A_V = 23.3 mag  
  > "extinction curve for the mid-infrared regime to correct extinction to the observed fluxes" (3.2.1 Extinction Correction in H2 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - fitting: Linear least-squares style fit to the diagram (single T) and two-temperature fit for curvature; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "To account for the observed curvature in the excitation diagram, we also performed a two-temperature fit" (3.2.1 Extinction Correction in H2 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - validation: Repeated single- and two-temperature fits with other extinction models (KP5, McClure dense cloud); temperatures vary within uncertainties  
  > "we repeated both the single- and two-temperature fits using the icy dust model KP5" (3.2.1 Extinction Correction in H2 Rotational Diagram Analysis); "The derived temperatures vary within the respective uncertainties; however, the overall two-component structure and temperature trend remain consistent." (3.2.1 Extinction Correction in H2 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
6. **Estimate jet mass-loss rate**: Cylindrical-flow model of the molecular jet with mean N(H2), constant velocity and jet width equal to the MIRI PSF FWHM at 5.5 um  
  > "we model the inner jet as a cylindrical flow of gas primarily composed of molecular hydrogen, with a mean column density $N(\mathrm{H}_2)$" (5.2 Jet Physical condition); "The mass-loss rate through the jet can be approximated by the following expression:" (5.2 Jet Physical condition)
   - parameter: V_j = 125 km/s; W_j ~ 0.29 arcsec  
  > "a constant jet velocity $V_j = 125~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$" (5.2 Jet Physical condition); "we adopt the highest FWHM of the JWST/MIRI PSF at 5.5 $\mu$m, approximately $0.29\ensuremath{''}$, as an estimate for $W_j$." (5.2 Jet Physical condition)
   - assumption: mu_H2 = 2.8; jet unresolved so width set by PSF  
  > "$\mu_{H_2} = 2.8$ is the mean molecular weight per hydrogen molecule" (5.2 Jet Physical condition); "Since the jet is unresolved at the current spatial resolution" (5.2 Jet Physical condition)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with ALMA CO-based mass-loss rates (blue lobe and total)  
  > "which is in agreement with that of estimated from ALMA observation" (5.2 Jet Physical condition)
7. **Compare knot positions and dynamical ages between ALMA and JWST**: Compute knot dynamical ages from jet velocity, inclination and Orion distance; measure offset between JWST H2 peaks and the ALMA SiO Gaussian centroid of knot B2; compare with expected proper motion over 4.37 yr  
  > "the dynamical age of knot B1 is estimated to be $\sim$10 years at a projected separation of $\sim$ 210 AU, while knot B2 is $\sim$30 years old at $\sim$ 630 AU." (5.1 Shock Structure); "the expected displacement over this period is $\sim115$ AU (equivalent to $\sim0.22\ensuremath{''}$ at Orion distance 400 pc and inclination angle 40$\ensuremath{^\circ}$)" (5.1 Shock Structure)
   - assumption: Jet velocity 125 km/s, inclination 40 deg, distance 400 pc  
  > "Assuming a typical proper motion corresponding to a mean jet velocity of $125~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$" (5.1 Shock Structure)
   - fitting: Gaussian centroid of the ALMA SiO knot (details not stated)  
  > "the ALMA SiO Gaussian centroid of knot B2" (5.1 Shock Structure)
   - validation: Offset persists when beam sizes are comparable (S(4) at 8.0242 um); WCS mismatch acknowledged as a possible cause  
  > "the shift in the peak position of the knots persists even when the beam sizes are comparable" (5.1 Shock Structure)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA SiO and CO trace both lobes of the bipolar jet, while JWST/MIRI detects mainly the blueshifted lobe  
  > "JWST/MIRI detects mainly the blueshifted lobe; the redshifted side is likely obscured by strong mid-infrared extinction." (abstract)
- Stratified layers in the outflow and jet, with H2 tracing the forward shock layer and SiO/CO cooler post-shock gas  
  > "The combination of ALMA and JWST reveals stratified layers within the outflow and jet, as well as shock structures" (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HOPS 315 is an evolved protostar with strong accretion and ejection, a compelling target for jet and outflow morphology  
  > "making it a compelling target for investigating outflow and jet morphology" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Maps were not convolved to a common beam to preserve narrow jet knots  
  > "Such convolution would result in a loss of morphological detail, particularly the jet" (3.3 Combining ALMA Maps with JWST Observations)
- Alternative: Extinction-model alternatives (icy dust KP5, McClure dense-cloud) tested; temperatures changed only within uncertainties  
  > "we repeated both the single- and two-temperature fits using the icy dust model KP5" (3.2.1 Extinction Correction in H2 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
- Alternative: Doppler boosting rejected as the cause of the jet asymmetry  
  > "this effect is unlikely to play a significant role in protostellar jets due to their much lower velocities" (5.1.2 Monopolar in infrared but bipolar in submillimeter)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Different JWST and ALMA resolutions limit morphological comparison  
  > "Due to the differing spatial resolutions, any morphological comparison between ALMA and JWST should be interpreted with caution." (3.3 Combining ALMA Maps with JWST Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Possible ALMA/JWST WCS mismatch could contribute to knot offsets  
  > "Another contributing factor could be a slight mismatch in the World Coordinate System (WCS) solutions of the two instruments." (5.1 Shock Structure)
- Limitation (authors): Resolution and time baseline are insufficient to confirm or rule out proper motion  
  > "the current spatial resolution and temporal baseline are insufficient to robustly confirm or rule out proper motion based solely on peak position offsets" (5.1 Shock Structure)
- Limitation (authors): Jet is unresolved, so jet width is set by the PSF  
  > "Since the jet is unresolved at the current spatial resolution" (5.2 Jet Physical condition)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Paper gives two different N(H2) values (1.72e20 in Section 3.2.1, 3.48e20 in Summary), A_V 23.3 vs 23.5 mag, and mass-loss uncertainty 0.1 vs 0.2e-6; the derived mass-loss rate depends on which is right
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Jet width set to PSF FWHM (0.29'') makes the mass-loss rate an upper-limit-like estimate and the quoted uncertainty omits that systematic
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Boltzmann/LTE and optically-thin assumptions are questionable for the long-wavelength lines, which the paper itself says may be optically thick
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Temperature, A_V and column density come from a single 0.5'' aperture at knot B2, with no stated fit weighting or residuals
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Knot offsets are comparable to PSF/beam sizes and the WCS alignment is unchecked; the shock-layer interpretation is not tested against models quantitatively
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Robust +2.0 is the coarsest-resolution weighting, so ALMA beams (~0.4'') are larger than the 0.22'' expected knot displacement; no robust test is shown
