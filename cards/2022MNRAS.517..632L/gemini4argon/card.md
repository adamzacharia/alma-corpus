## Analysis recipe

1. **Create zeroth-moment integrated intensity map and estimate total molecular gas mass**: Smooth-masking moment technique: convolve cube spatially with a Gaussian of beam width, Hanning-smooth in velocity, clip at 0.3 sigma_rms, integrate, and compare to BIMA CO(1-0) flux  
  > "Figure 1 shows the zeroth-moment (integrated-intensity) map of our adopted data cube, created using a smooth-masking moment technique (cite: dame2011)." (ALMA data); "To generate the mask, the data cube was convolved spatially by a Gaussian of width equal to that of the synthesised beam, Hanning-smoothed in velocity and clipped at a fixed threshold ($0.3$ $\sigma_{\rm rms}$)." (ALMA data)
   - parameter: clip threshold = 0.3 sigma_rms  
  > "Hanning-smoothed in velocity and clipped at a fixed threshold ($0.3$ $\sigma_{\rm rms}$)." (ALMA data)
   - assumption: CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) line ratio = 0.78 in temperature units; X_CO = 2 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "derived using our integrated ALMA CO(2-1) intensity and the CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) line ratio of $0.78$, and assuming a standard Galactic CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor $X_{\rm CO}=2\times10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$" (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) line ratio of ~0.78 is similar to average MW ratio of 0.8  
  > "Comparing our CO(2-1) integrated intensity to that in CO(1-0) by (cite: taylor2015), we obtain a CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) line ratio of $\approx0.78$ in temperature units, similar to the average MW ratio of $0.8$ (cite: carilli2013)." (ALMA data)
2. **Identify hierarchy of molecular structures (leaves/clumps, branches, trunks/clouds) and measure their physical properties**: Create 3D mask with CPROPSTOO, decompose hierarchy into leaves, branches, and trunks with ASTRODENDRO, and compute extrapolated and beam-deconvolved properties with modified CPROPSTOO  
  > "We use the dendrogram (i.e. tree analysis) code ASTRODENDRO described by (cite: rosolowsky2008) to identify molecular structures in NGC 404." (Structural decomposition); "A three-dimensional (3D) mask of bright emission is initially created using the code CPROPSTOO (cite: rosolowsky2006; leroy2015)." (Structural decomposition); "Once a hierarchy of structures has been identified by ASTRODENDRO, we use our modified version of CPROPSTOO (cite: liu2021) to calculate the properties of the structures identified." (Structural decomposition)
   - software: CPROPSTOO, ASTRODENDRO
   - parameter: CPROPSTOO mask: peak T_b > 3 sigma_rms (~39 K), expanded to neighbouring pixels with T_b > 1.5 sigma_rms (~20 K), minimum island diameter ~ 3 pc (~100 spaxels)  
  > "All single pixels with brightness temperatures $T_{\rm b}>3$ $\sigma_{\rm rms}$ ($\approx39$ K) are first identified, and the pixel list is then expanded to include all neighbouring pixels with $T_{\rm b}>1.5$ $\sigma_{\rm rms}$ ($\approx20$ K)." (Structural decomposition); "Each discrete region of signal in the mask (i.e. each “island”) is also required to have a minimum projected diameter of $\approx3$ pc ($\approx100$ spaxels in area)" (Structural decomposition)
   - parameter: ASTRODENDRO: min_value = 1.5 sigma_rms (~20 K), min_delta = 1.5 sigma_rms (~20 K), min_area = min_npix = 6  
  > "emission fainter than $min\_value=1.5$ $\sigma_{\rm rms}$ ($\approx20$ K) is not characterised." (Structural decomposition); "identifies local maxima of brightness temperatures at least $min\_delta=1.5$ $\sigma_{\rm rms}$ ($\approx20$ K) above the merger level" (Structural decomposition); "Here we require $min\_area=min\_npix=6$, so that each local maximum spans at least one synthesised beam." (Structural decomposition)
   - assumption: eta = 1.91 for radius R_c; CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) = 0.78; X_CO = 2 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 (including helium); PA = 1 deg and i = 9.3 deg for R_gal; D = 3.06 Mpc  
  > "Measurements of $R_{\rm c}$ assume $\eta=1.91$. Measurements of $M_{\rm c}$ assume a CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) line ratio of $0.78$ in temperature units and a standard Galactic conversion factor $X_{\rm CO}=2\times10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (including the mass contribution from helium)." (Table 1); "Measurements of $R_{\rm gal}$ adopt a fixed position angle ${\rm PA} = 1^\circ$ and inclination angle $i = 9.3\ensuremath{^\circ}$." (Table 1)
   - fitting: Extrapolation to T_edge = 0 K and 2D beam deconvolution in CPROPSTOO; uncertainties via bootstrapping  
  > "extrapolating sizes, linewidths and luminosities to their infinite signal-to-noise ratio ($S/N$) equivalents (i.e. to a brightness temperature at the edge of the structures identified $T_{\rm edge}=0$ K)." (Structural decomposition); "The uncertainties of the measured properties are estimated via a bootstrapping technique." (Structural decomposition)
   - validation: Filter for resolved structures: deconvolved diameter >= synthesised beam in 2D and deconvolved velocity width >= half of one velocity channel  
  > "have deconvolved diameters larger than or equal to the synthesised beam in two dimentions and deconvolved velocity widths at least half of one velocity channel (cite: donovanmeyer2013)." (Structural decomposition)
3. **Fit clump mass distribution functions, size-linewidth relations, mass-size relations, and virial parameters across galactic regions**: Divide structures into central region (R_gal <= 15 pc) and molecular ring (15 < R_gal <= 50 pc); fit broken power-laws to differential mass functions, power-laws to cumulative mass functions, size-linewidth, mass-size, and virial parameter-mass relations; compute virial parameters and self-gravitating gas fractions  
  > "Hereafter we will refer to the central disc/ring and arm-like feature as the central region (galactocentric distances $R_{\rm gal}\le15$ pc), and to the outer incomplete (pseudo-)ring as the molecular ring ($15<R_{\rm gal}\lesssim50$ pc)." (ALMA data); "We therefore fit the normalised differential mass distributions with two separate power-laws, one for the high-mass regime $M_{\rm clump}\gtrsim4000$ M$_\odot$, with a power-law index of $\gamma_{\rm clump}^+$, and the other for the low-mass regime $M_{\rm comp}\lesssim M_{\rm clump}\lesssim4000$ M$_\odot$, with a power-law index of $\gamma_{\rm clump}^-$." (Mass function of clumps); "We also calculate the fraction of emission originating from gravitationally-bound (i.e. $\alpha_{\rm vir,c} \le \alpha_{\rm vir,crit}=2$) structures, and plot this fraction as a function of the structures’ spatial scales in Fig. 8." (Multiple-scale virial analyses)
   - parameter: M_comp = 1.8 x 10^3 M_sun; turnover mass ~ 4000 M_sun; alpha_vir,crit = 2  
  > "The black vertical dashed line in each panel indicates the mass completeness limit $M_{\rm comp}=1.8\times10^3$ M$_\odot$" (Mass function of clumps); "A virial parameter $\alpha_{\rm vir,crit}\approx2$ is often considered the threshold betwen gravitationally-bound and unbound objects (cite: kauffmann2013; kauffmann2017)." (Multiple-scale virial analyses)
   - assumption: Virial mass M_vir,c = 5 sigma_obs,los^2 R_c / G  
  > "$M_{\rm vir,c}\equiv\frac{5\sigma_{\rm obs,los}^2R_{\rm c}}{G}$" (Multiple-scale virial analyses)
   - fitting: Power-law fits and Spearman rank correlation coefficients  
  > "There is no correlation between $\alpha_{\rm vir,c}$ and $M_{\rm c}$ in the central region (left panel of Fig. 7; Spearman rank correlation coefficient $-0.20\pm0.07$), but there is a clear trend in the molecular ring (middle panel of Fig. 7; Spearman rank correlation coefficient $-0.75\pm0.01$)" (Multiple-scale virial analyses)
   - validation: Compared differential mass function slope with cumulative mass function slope; verified negligible rotational contamination by comparing sigma_obs,los with gradient-subtracted velocity dispersion sigma_gs,los  
  > "We note that the power-law index of the differential clump mass distribution function in the high-mass regime $\gamma_{\rm clump}^{+}$ is consistent with the best-fitting power-law indices of the cumulative clump mass distribution function" (Mass function of clumps); "Following the method of (cite: liu2021) (i.e. comparing $\sigma_{\rm obs,los}$ with the gradient-subtracted velocity dispersion measure $\sigma_{\rm gs,los}$), we found no evidence of a significant contribution of galactic rotation at the clump and cloud scales" (Multiple-scale size – linewidth relations)
4. **Construct analytical clump-clump collision model and compare with radially-binned galaxy and clump properties**: Compute radial profiles of Sigma_gas,disc, sigma_gas,disc, Oort's A, Omega, kappa, Q, Jeans and Toomre scales in 2 pc annuli; compare analytical collision predictions (t_coll, lambda_coll, M_c,coll, R_c,coll, energy injection rate, size-linewidth, mass-size) with observed properties and VLA SFR upper limits  
  > "In the molecular ring, however, the collision timescale decreases significantly with increasing galactocentric distance, and it becomes comparable to the shear timescale at" (4.3 First comparison to NGC 404); "The mass surface density $\Sigma_{\rm gas,disc}$ is computed by summing the molecular gas mass within galactocentric annuli of increasing $R_{\rm gal}$ (with a radial bin size of $2$ pc), corrected for inclination (where a fixed position angle $PA=1^\circ$ and inclination angle $i=9.3\ensuremath{^\circ}$ were adopted)." (Collision timescale)
   - software: KinMS
   - parameter: Radial bin size = 2 pc, PA = 1 deg, i = 9.3 deg  
  > "where $\sigma_{\rm gas,disc}(R)$ was obtained again by averaging measurements within galactocentric annuli of increasing $R_{\rm gal}$ (with a radial bin size of $2$ pc and a fixed position angle $PA=1^\circ$ and inclination angle $i=9.3\ensuremath{^\circ}$)." (Vertical support from collision-induced turbulence)
   - assumption: Infinitely thin 2D disc, circular orbits, spherical galaxy mass distribution for tidal radius, completely inelastic equal-mass clump collisions, Kolmogorov turbulence spectrum  
  > "Molecular clumps are assumed to be uniformly distributed over a small (coarse-grained) region of an infinitely thin, two-dimensional disc, and to populate perfectly circular orbits determined by the gravitational potential of the galaxy." (Collision timescale); "Finally, we assume that collisions can only occur between (two) clumps of equal mass" (Collision timescale); "if we assume a Kolmogorov spectrum of turbulence (cite: kolmogorov1941; lighthill1955; kritsuk2007; mivilledeschenes2017)" (Size – linewidth relation regulated by clump-clump collisions)
   - fitting: Analytical model predictions with no free parameters  
  > "Our predicted size – linewidth relation (with no free parameter!) strongly resembles the observed $R_{\rm c}$ – $\sigma_{\rm obs,los}$ trend in the molecular ring" (Size – linewidth relation regulated by clump-clump collisions); "we have therefore predicted an accurate mass – size relation regulated by collision-induced turbulence only, with no free parameter." (5.2.1 Mass – size relation regulated by turbulence)
   - validation: Compared collision energy injection rate with gravitational instability injection rate, supernova feedback injection rate upper limit from VLA 33 GHz continuum, and inward clump migration energy injection rate  
  > "As a sanity check, we now compare the energy injection rate from gravitational instabilities $\dot{\epsilon}_{\rm inject,GI}$ to that from clump-clump collisions $\dot{\epsilon}_{\rm inject,coll}$" (Gravitational instabilities as the onset of clump-clump collisions); "This upper limit to the energy injection rate (per unit mass) from stellar feedback is clearly smaller than the observed energy dissipation rate (per unit mass) of clumps and the energy injection rate (per unit mass) from clump-clump collisions" (Effect of stellar feedback on turbulence)

## Qualitative findings

- NGC 404 has two distinct molecular gas regions: a gravitationally-stable central region (Q = 3-30) and a gravitationally-unstable molecular ring (Q <= 1 at R_gal >= 27 pc).  
  > "We find two distinct regions in NGC 404: a gravitationally-stable central region (Toomre parameter $Q=3-30$) and a gravitationally-unstable molecular ring ($Q\lesssim1$)." (Abstract)
- In the molecular ring, clump-clump collisions driven by gravitational instabilities and galactic shear regulate clump masses, sizes, mass-size relation, and turbulence.  
  > "Our model-predicted clump masses and sizes (and mass – size relation) and turbulence energy injection rates (and size – linewidth relation) match the observations in the molecular ring very well, suggesting clump-clump collisions is the main mechanism regulating clump properties and gas turbulence in that region." (Abstract)
- Clumps in the molecular ring are supported by collision-induced turbulence in rough virial equilibrium, while clouds are dominated by self-gravity.  
  > "Collision-induced turbulence can thus maintain the clumps (but not the clouds) in rough virial equilibria." (Conclusions)
- In the central region, the collision model does not apply, and high velocity dispersions and uniform clump properties are likely explained by inward clump migration from the molecular ring.  
  > "As expected, our collision model does not apply to the central region, where turbulence is likely driven by clump migration." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-spatial resolution (~0.86 x 0.51 pc^2) ALMA CO(2-1) observations resolve sub-parsec to tens-of-parsec molecular structures across the dwarf galaxy disc, with maximum recoverable scale (~10.4'') much larger than the largest single molecular structure.  
  > "In this paper, we perform statistical analyses of the multiple-scale molecular structures of the dwarf lenticular galaxy NGC 404, exploiting high-spatial resolution ($\approx0.86\times0.51$ pc$^2$) Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) $^{12}$CO(2-1) observations." (Introduction); "and a maximum recoverable scale of $\approx10.4\ensuremath{''}$ that is much larger than than the largest single molecular structure identified in this work." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: ASTRODENDRO identifies a full hierarchy of nested structures (leaves, branches, trunks) to probe multiple spatial scales, while CPROPSTOO extrapolates to infinite S/N and deconvolves the beam to correct for finite sensitivity and resolution biases.  
  > "As a dendrogram approach allows us to identify a full hierarchy of molecular structures, we are able to investigate whether the size – linewidth relation (and thus turbulence) is universal from the large-scale ISM to its smallest and densest structures (cite: shetty2012)." (Multiple-scale size – linewidth relations); "The code CPROPSTOO is chosen because it attempts to correct the measured quantities for the finite sensitivity of the observations, extrapolating sizes, linewidths and luminosities to their infinite signal-to-noise ratio ($S/N$) equivalents" (Structural decomposition)
- Alternative: Using gradient-subtracted velocity dispersion (sigma_gs,los) instead of observed velocity dispersion (sigma_obs,los) was rejected as unnecessary because galactic rotation contamination is negligible at clump/cloud scales.  
  > "Following the method of (cite: liu2021) (i.e. comparing $\sigma_{\rm obs,los}$ with the gradient-subtracted velocity dispersion measure $\sigma_{\rm gs,los}$), we found no evidence of a significant contribution of galactic rotation at the clump and cloud scales (i.e. $\sigma_{\rm obs,los}\approx\sigma_{\rm gs,los}$ for most clumps and clouds)." (Multiple-scale size – linewidth relations); "The use of the gradient-subtracted velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\rm gs,los}$ is therefore unnecessary in this work." (Multiple-scale size – linewidth relations)
- Alternative: Cloud-cloud collisions were rejected as unimportant compared to clump-clump collisions because clouds have collision timescales longer than the shear timescale (t_coll,cloud > t_shear).  
  > "Clouds, that by definition contains several clumps, should therefore have $t_{\rm coll,cloud}>t_{\rm shear}$ everywhere." (First comparison to NGC 404); "Cloud-cloud collisions are therefore likely to be much less important than clump-clump collisions in NGC 404." (First comparison to NGC 404)
