## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: Is the 3.3 micron PAH intensity of T Tauri and Herbig disks correlated with the warm (midplane T > 30 K) millimeter dust mass, as expected if PAHs are frozen out on pebbles and only sublimate once pebbles reach warm dust traps?

## Analysis recipe

1. **measure dust disk sizes**: curve-of-growth radius enclosing 90% of the total flux; CASA imfit FWHM for marginally resolved compact disks  
  > "using a curve-of-growth method, encircling 90% of the total flux, following previous works (cite: Tripathi2017)" (Analysis); "for those disks we use the CASA `imfit` task to estimate the FWHM to measure the radius of each dust disk" (Analysis)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: R_size measured at ~1 mm (Band 6 or 7) for most; longer wavelength for HD135344B, T Cha, HD179218  
  > "only for HD135344B, T Cha and HD179218 longer wavelength images were used" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **derive total dust mass at 20 K**: standard optically thin millimeter dust mass equation M = F d^2 / (kappa B(T)) at T = 20 K  
  > "we estimate the total dust mass using the common equation related to the total millimeter flux" (Analysis)
   - parameter: kappa(1000 GHz) = 10 (units as printed: g cm^-2), kappa proportional to nu^beta with beta = 1  
  > "dust opacity $\kappa_{\nu}$ (where $\kappa_{1000GHz}=10$ g cm$^{-2}$ and $\kappa_{\nu}\propto\nu^\beta$ with $\beta$=1)" (Analysis)
   - parameter: 10% uncertainty from ALMA flux calibration  
  > "The uncertainty on this parameter is 10%, following the flux calibration uncertainty of ALMA." (Analysis)
   - assumption: dust temperature 20 K and optically thin emission  
  > "for an assumed temperature of 20 K and optically thin emission (cite: Hildebrand1983)" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **derive midplane temperature profiles**: power-law passive-disk profile (Chiang and Goldreich) for non-transition disks; individually fitted RADMC-3D SED+continuum models for transition disks (or literature profiles)  
  > "The midplane temperature profile of each disk is estimated using a power-law profile assuming a passively heated disk, following (cite: Chiang1997)" (Analysis); "their temperature profiles are estimated individually using a RADMC-3D model fitting the Spectral Energy Distribution" (Analysis)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: flaring angle phi = 0.02  
  > "flaring angle $\phi$=0.02" (Analysis)
   - parameter: RADMC-3D: outer radius 150 au, inner dust disk of 1 au depleted by delta_dust, scale height h(r)=h_c (r/r_c)^psi with psi = 0.15 and r_c = 50 au  
  > "The vertical scale height is described by $h(r)=h_c({r}/{r_c})^{\psi}$ with $\psi=0.15$ and characteristic radius $r_c$=50 au." (SEDs of transition disks)
   - assumption: gas-to-dust ratio 100; small (0.005-1 um) and large (0.005-1000 um) grain populations  
  > "The outer radius is set at 150 au and the surface density is scaled to the total gas disk mass $M_{\rm disk}$ with a gas-to-dust ratio of 100." (SEDs of transition disks); "The dust opacities are computed using a combination of a small grain and large grain population, of 0.005-1 $\mu$m and 0.005-1000 $\mu$m, respectively." (SEDs of transition disks)
   - fitting: free parameters M_disk, h_c, r_cav, delta_dust fitted to radial continuum profiles and SEDs; optimiser, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "The models were fit to both the radial continuum profiles and the SEDs." (SEDs of transition disks)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **compute warm dust mass inside the 30 K radius**: integrate the millimeter flux from 0 to the radius where midplane T drops below 30 K, then convert to mass with the dust mass equation at T = 30 K  
  > "The warm dust mass flux is computed for each disk by integrating the millimeter flux from 0 out to the radius where the midplane temperature drops below 30 K" (Analysis); "for T=30 K, we compute the warm dust mass" (Analysis)
   - parameter: 30 K cutoff (CO snowline values of 18-35 K in the literature; 22-30 K range shown in profiles)  
  > "with the 22-30 K regime and spatial resolution indicated" (Analysis)
   - parameter: 10% uncertainty from ALMA flux calibration; upper limit (total dust mass) used for WX Cha and Sz73  
  > "The uncertainty on the warm dust mass is again 10%, following the flux calibration uncertainty of ALMA." (Analysis); "we use an upper limit to the total dust mass as value" (Analysis)
   - assumption: 30 K is a reasonable single temperature because the dust mass is dominated by the temperature at the outer radius  
  > "the dust mass will be dominated by the temperature at the outer radius due to the shape of the temperature profile and 30 K is a reasonable choice" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: cutoff-temperature sensitivity check: impact of changing the cutoff within 22-30 K judged generally negligible  
  > "Whereas the exact cutoff temperature will impact the warm dust mass, this range shows that the impact is generally negligible." (Analysis)
5. **compute FUV radiation field**: integrate Kurucz stellar spectrum plus 10,000 K blackbody scaled to accretion luminosity over 912-2050 A; chi from L_FUV at 150 au scaled to ISRF; also at R_in and R_size  
  > "The FUV luminosity $L_{FUV}$ is computed by integrating the stellar spectrum between 912-2050 $\AA$." (Analysis); "The FUV radiation field across the disk, named $\chi$, is estimated from $L_{FUV}$ at a distance of 150 au from the star" (Analysis)
   - parameter: ISRF = 1.6e-6 W m^-2  
  > "scaled to the interstellar radiation field ISRF (1.6$\cdot10^{-6}$ W m$^{-2}$ (cite: Habing1968)" (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **test correlations between PAH 3.3 micron intensity and disk properties**: linmix linear fit in log space including upper limits; Pearson correlation coefficients; repeated for detections only and for 6.2, 8.6, 11.3 micron features  
  > "Using the `linmix` package (cite: Kelly2007), we perform a linear fit to the logarithmic data including the upper limits." (Results)
   - software: linmix
   - parameter: 5 sigma PAH limits rescaled to 3 sigma limits for the FUV/total-dust-mass plots  
  > "We have scaled the 5$\sigma$ limits on the PAH intensity to 3$\sigma$ limits for consistency with the linmix methodology in these plots and calculations." (Results)
   - fitting: linmix (Kelly2007) linear fit in log-log space with censored data; sampler, priors and likelihood details not stated  
  > "Using the `linmix` package (cite: Kelly2007), we perform a linear fit to the logarithmic data including the upper limits." (Results)
   - validation: compared coefficients with and without upper limits; removed outliers HD179218 and HD163296  
  > "When considering only the data points of detected PAH 3.3 $\mu$m intensities, the Pearson correlation coefficient is $R=0.71\pm0.21$" (Results)
7. **compare literature PAH spatial extents with CO and continuum profiles**: overlay literature 3.3, 8.6, 11.3 micron PAH extents on 13CO radial profiles and ALMA continuum profiles for 7 targets  
  > "Figure [6](#fig:spatial) shows the spatial extent of the 3.3, 8.6 and 11.3 $\mu$m features from the literature described above." (Spatially resolved PAH emission)
   - assumption: 13CO 3-2 or 2-1 line profile is a proxy of the gas gap inside the dust cavity  
  > "provides a proxy of the gas gap inside the dust cavity" (Spatially resolved PAH emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Disk morphology alone does not set the PAH intensity; spectral type also plays a role, so morphology and spectral type combine  
  > "Therefore, the combination of disk morphology and spectral type likely plays a role." (ALMA data)
- PAH emission peaks inside the cavity for AB Aur, HD100546, HD169142, HD97048, IRS48 and potentially HD100453, rather than at the dust ring itself  
  > "For AB Aur, HD100546, HD169142, HD97048, IRS48 and potentially HD100453 the PAH emission peaks inside the cavity." (Spatially resolved PAH emission)
- HD179218 and HD163296 are outliers in the warm dust mass versus PAH relation, with possible explanations (optically thick emission; icy pebbles halted outside a wide gap)  
  > "The two exceptions in the main correlation with the 3.3 $\mu$m feature in Figure [4](#fig:PAHmain) are HD179218 and HD163296." (Outliers in the warm dust - PAH intensity correlation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: targets beyond 300 pc excluded because their dust disks cannot be constrained without spatially well-resolved ALMA data  
  > "their dust disk cannot be well constrained due to a lack of spatially well-resolved ALMA data" (Sample)
- Why this method: 30 K midplane cutoff chosen as the warm dust boundary because PAH sublimation temperatures are reached only at a few scale heights, so efficient vertical transport of fragmented icy grains is required  
  > "The definition of the cutoff temperature of the warm dust region is thus chosen at 30 K in the midplane." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Cutoff temperatures of 22-30 K tested against the 30 K choice; impact generally negligible  
  > "with the 22-30 K regime and spatial resolution indicated" (Analysis)
- Alternative: Fits to detections only (excluding upper limits) compared against fits including upper limits  
  > "We also compute the Pearson correlation coefficients of the fit of the data of the detected PAH intensities only (excluding the upper limits from the fit)" (Results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Optically thin assumption may not hold, so dust masses are lower limits to pebble mass  
  > "the dust mass values are technically lower limits to the pebble mass" (Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Long-wavelength PAH analysis covers only a small Herbig-only subsample  
  > "This correlation only cover a small part of the sample though, including only Herbig disks." (Origin of PAH emission in protoplanetary disks)
- Limitation (authors): Integrated fluxes cannot show whether PAH emission originates from the dust trap  
  > "From the integrated fluxes studied in this work it remains unclear if the PAH emission indeed originates from the dust trap region." (Spatially resolved PAH emission)
- Limitation (authors): Disk sizes for HD135344B, T Cha and HD179218 measured at longer wavelength and could be larger at 1 mm  
  > "which means that their disk sizes could be somewhat larger at 1 mm" (Analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Correlation with FUV is not unambiguous and does not explain scatter among disks of similar type  
  > "The correlation is not unambiguous and does not explain why disks with similar spectral type or similar disk morphology show such a wide spread in PAH intensity." (Results)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlation is driven by upper limits and a few strong detections; 3.3 micron PAH sample is small (26) and heterogeneous (ISO, VLT, different apertures)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Warm dust mass depends on the assumed temperature law (phi = 0.02 power law) and on the 30 K cutoff; no uncertainty propagated beyond a flat 10%
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar luminosity and spectral type correlate with PAH excitation and with disk mass, so a confounding variable is possible
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heterogeneous ALMA images (different resolutions, wavelengths, published reductions) feed one quantity; no uniform reprocessing
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Pearson r from linmix with censored data is not standard; no significance or bootstrap test of the correlation is given
