## Analysis recipe

1. **Make continuum images**: Select line-free channels in each SPW, combine all SPWs, image baselines >300 m, clean, and apply primary-beam correction  
  > "Continuum images were produced by selecting line-free channels in each SPW and then combining all SPW" (Continuum emission); "In our images we only include data from baselines longer than 300 meters" (Data Reduction and Imaging); "The final images were cleaned to a threshold of 0.3 mJy and then corrected for the primary beam response." (Continuum emission)
   - software: casa, tclean
   - parameter: Uniform and Briggs R=0.5; ~19 and 28 mas  
  > "Continuum images were produced with both uniform weighting and Briggs weighting with a robust parameter $R=0.5$, achieving resolutions of $\sim$19 and 28 mas respectively." (Continuum emission)
   - parameter: Line-free selection on baselines <1000 m; approximately 5 percent of total bandwidth  
  > "Channel selection was accomplished in the *uv* domain using the shorter baselines $<$1000 m where emission is seen and line-free regions can be discerned." (Continuum emission); "Approximately 5 percent of the total bandwidth was assessed to be ‘line-free’" (Continuum emission)
   - assumption: Flat-spectrum source; resulting central frequency about 226 GHz  
  > "have a resulting central frequency of about 226 GHz assuming a flat-spectrum source." (Continuum emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Noise away from bright sources near thermal noise expected from ALMA sensitivity calculator  
  > "near the thermal noise level, as expected from the ALMA sensitivity calculator" (Continuum emission)
2. **Measure continuum structure and brightness**: Use brightness-temperature maps and contours to delineate cores and extended structures; fit Gaussian models to W51e8 and W51north  
  > "in units of brightness temperature ($T_B$), tracing warm dust in the W51e2e, W51e8, and W51north hot-cores, respectively." (Dust continuum sources); "Fitting a Gaussian model to the continuum emission peak in both the uniform and Briggs maps" (W51e8.); "Fitting a Gaussian model in both the uniform and Briggs maps" (W51north.)
   - parameter: W51e2e core contour T_B=130 K; extended contour T_B=15 K  
  > "the yellow contour at $T_B = 130$ K" (W51e2e.); "the red contour at $T_B = 15$ K" (W51e2e.)
   - fitting: Gaussian models; deconvolved FWHM  
  > "Fitting a Gaussian model to the continuum emission peak in both the uniform and Briggs maps provides a consistent deconvolved FWHM size" (W51e8.)
   - validation: Consistent deconvolved sizes in uniform and Briggs maps  
  > "in both the uniform and Briggs maps provides a consistent deconvolved FWHM size" (W51e8.); "in both the uniform and Briggs maps provides a consistent deconvolved FWHM long axis" (W51north.)
3. **Estimate core and filament masses**: Bracket masses using τ=1 beam-area estimates and optically thin M=Sνd²/[κνBν(Td)]; compare recovered flux with Cycle 2  
  > "We first assume that in the highest column density regions (i.e. toward the source centers), the sources are just barely optically thick ($\tau=1$)." (Masses); "M = \frac{S_{\nu} d^2}{\kappa_{\nu} B_{\nu} (T_d)}" (Masses); "We compare the recovered flux in the long-baseline maps to that in the ALMA cycle 2 maps at the same frequency (cite: Ginsburg2017)." (Masses)
   - parameter: Beam area 0.033″×0.024″; τ=1 beam mass 0.36 M_sun  
  > "where $A$ is the beam area (0$\rlap{.}^{\prime\prime}$033 $\times$0$\rlap{.}^{\prime\prime}$024) in cm$^2$, which yields dust masses of each source $M_{Beam}=0.36$ M$_{\odot}$." (Masses)
   - assumption: κ_227GHz=0.0083 cm² g^-1; includes gas-to-dust ratio 100  
  > "We use the same coefficient $\kappa_{227\mathrm{~GHz}} = 0.0083 \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{g}^{-1}$ as in (cite: Ginsburg2017)" (Masses); "the dust opacity coefficient (which includes the gas-to-dust ratio of 100)" (Masses)
   - assumption: Td lower limits from peak continuum brightness or Tgas,min=200 K  
  > "The first estimate is provided by the peak brightness temperature of the mm continuum emission at the source center" (Masses); "Therefore we can assume $T=T_{gas, min} = 200$ K as a second lower limit on $T_d$." (Masses)
   - assumption: Distance 5.4 kpc  
  > "at $D_{\mathrm{W51}}=5.4$ kpc." (Abstract)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare thin/thick estimates and two temperature lower limits; check flux recovery against Cycle 2  
  > "we use these two approaches (assuming thin and thick emission) and these two independent $T_d$ lower limits to obtain mass estimates" (Masses); "We compare the recovered flux in the long-baseline maps to that in the ALMA cycle 2 maps at the same frequency (cite: Ginsburg2017)." (Masses)
4. **Derive luminosity lower limits**: Calculate L=4πr²σ_sbT⁴ from continuum brightness temperatures  
  > "One can use these temperatures to estimate lower limits on the source luminosities." (Protostellar luminosities); "the luminosity can be calculated as $L = 4\pi r^2 \sigma_{sb} T^4$" (Protostellar luminosities)
   - assumption: Blackbody emission from a spherical beam-filling source  
  > "Assuming blackbody emission from a spherical beam-filling source" (Protostellar luminosities)
   - assumption: Peak brightness temperatures are lower limits to millimeter-core surface brightness  
  > "The peak brightness temperatures are a lower limit to the surface brightness of the millimeter core" (Protostellar luminosities)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare with higher luminosity lower limits from coarser Cycle 2 observations  
  > "the coarser-resolution observations carried out with ALMA in cycle 2 recovered more flux" (Protostellar luminosities); "yielding slightly higher luminosities, $L\gtrsim10^4$ L$_{\odot}$" (Protostellar luminosities)
5. **Test for rotation and infall**: Subtract full-SPW median continuum, extract identified lines, and construct moment 0, 1, and 2 maps; inspect unblended dense-gas tracers  
  > "Firstly, the median value over the full SPW was used to estimate and subtract the local continuum (cite: Sanchez-Monge2017)." (Line emission); "Secondly, from the full-SPW cubes we extracted cubes of all the identified spectral lines, using the `Astropy` module `spectral-cube`." (Line emission); "we identified a subset of lines which are unblended and strong enough to be used for kinematic analysis." (Kinematics of dense molecular gas)
   - software: Astropy, spectral-cube

## Qualitative findings

- Dust continuum resolves into multiple streamers converging onto compact cores

- Compact collimated SiO outflows provide indirect evidence for accretion disks

- W51north morphology is best explained by one outflow changing direction after substantial accretion  
  > "We argue this structure is best explained as a single protostellar outflow that has changed direction over a short period of time as a consequence of a substantial accretion event onto its disk." (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Resolve vigorously accreting pre-main-sequence HMYSOs on ~100 au scales to test massive-star accretion models  
  > "observations with spatial resolution of order of 100 au targeting pre-main-sequence HMYSOs that are vigorously accreting are required." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Uniform weighting identifies compact components; Briggs improves filament recovery; natural weighting maximizes line signal-to-noise; outflows provide an indirect accretion-rate proxy  
  > "The former provides the highest angular resolution to identify compact components, while the latter provides comparably better signal-to-noise ratio to recover the filamentary structure around the dusty peaks" (Continuum emission); "we used natural weighting (also excluding baselines shorter than 300 m) in order to provide the highest signal-to-noise ratio" (Line emission); "as a proxy for the infall/accretion rates."
