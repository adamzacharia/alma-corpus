## Analysis recipe

1. **Smooth images to uniform linear resolution within distance intervals and generate integrated intensity maps**: Divide 135 sources into three distance intervals (<3 kpc, 3-6 kpc, >6 kpc), smooth within each interval using CASA to uniform linear resolution, and produce integrated intensity maps for the six molecular transitions.  
  > "To put these massive clumps into the similar physical resolution and discuss the characteristics of the powerlaw tails, the whole sample have been categorized into three distinct distance intervals." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "Within each distance interval, the sample sources have undergone a smoothing process to achieve a uniform linear resolution. The smoothing process was performed using the CASA version." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Linear resolutions: 0.03 kpc (< 3 kpc), 0.07 kpc (3 to 6 kpc), 0.15 kpc (> 6 kpc)  
  > "The linear resolutions attained for the sources in intervals of less than 3 kpc, 3 to 6 kpc, and greater than 6 kpc are approximately 0.03 kpc, 0.07 kpc, and 0.15 kpc, respectively." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Emit regions compared against MRS (~1 arcmin) to verify missing flux is not an issue for compact gravitationally bound structures.  
  > "From these figures, we can see that the emission of molecular lines is mostly concentrated within regions smaller than the MRS (⇠1 [0] ), indicating that the missing flux is not an issue" (2 OBSERVATIONS)
2. **Construct and fit integrated intensity probability distribution functions (I-PDFs)**: Fit the lower-intensity side of the I-PDF peak with a log-normal distribution, subtract the best-fit log-normal profile from the overall I-PDF, and fit the remaining high-intensity component with a linear regression power-law fit.  
  > "The parameter f is determined by fitting the assumed lognormal distribution to the low-intensity region of the I-PDF, while the slopes of the high-density regions are derived from a linear regression power-law fit" (3.1 Integrated intensity probability distribution functions); "Having fitted the lower-intensity side of the peak with a log-normal distribution, we subsequently subtract this best-fit log-normal profile from the overall I-PDF. The remaining secondary component is well characterized by a power-law distribution." (3.1 Integrated intensity probability distribution functions)
   - assumption: I-PDF is composed of a log-normal distribution at low intensity and a power-law tail at high intensity.  
  > "We present the integrated intensity probability distribution function for these molecular tracers, modeled as a combination of a log-normal distribution and a power-law tail." (abstract)
   - fitting: Log-normal fit to low-intensity region and linear regression power-law fit P[log(I)] = s * log(I) + b to the subtracted high-intensity residual.  
  > "The parameter f is determined by fitting the assumed lognormal distribution to the low-intensity region of the I-PDF, while the slopes of the high-density regions are derived from a linear regression power-law fit given by % [ ;>6 ( 퐼 )] = B ⇤ ;>6 ( 퐼 ) + 2 ." (3.1 Integrated intensity probability distribution functions)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Calculate power-law tail molecular line luminosities and fit L_bol - L_mol(p) scaling relations**: Calculate molecular line luminosity for the power-law high-intensity component L_mol(p) from velocity-integrated flux, line frequency, and distance, then fit L_bol vs L_mol(p) scaling relations in log-log space.  
  > "The line luminosity attributed to the power-law high-intensity component is termed L mol (p), to be distingushed from the total line luminosity used in previous works" (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations); "All relations are determined by linear least-squares fits in log–log space." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - fitting: Linear least-squares fits in log-log space  
  > "All relations are determined by linear least-squares fits in log–log space." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate optical depths of HCO+, H13CO+, HCN, and H13CN**: Measure average brightness temperature within a 30 arcsec circular aperture centered on each source and solve the brightness temperature ratio equation between main and 13C isotopologue lines.  
  > "The brightness temperature is determined by taking the average value within a 30 arcsec circular region centered on the source." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations); "By calculating the ratio of the brightness temperatures of HCO [+] to H [13] CO [+] and applying Equations 2 and 3, we derived the optical depths of HCO [+] and H [13] CO [+] ." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - parameter: Circular region aperture = 30 arcsec  
  > "The brightness temperature is determined by taking the average value within a 30 arcsec circular region centered on the source." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and identical excitation temperature for main and isotopologue lines  
  > "Assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and the same excitation temperature for HCO [+] and H [13] CO [+], the optical depth of HCO [+] can be directly determined by comparing the observed brightness temperatures of HCO [+] and H [13] CO [+] (Garden et al. 1991):" (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - assumption: Beam-filling factor = 1 and cosmic background temperature T_bg = 2.73 K  
  > "5 is taken to be 1 since ALMA can well resolve the gas structures (Xu et al. 2023). ) 16 = 2.73 K is the temperature of the cosmic background radiation." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - assumption: Isotope abundance ratio X_12/X_13 = 4.7 * R_gal + 25.05  
  > "We assummed the abundance ratio X 12 /X 13 =[HCO [+] ]/[H [13] CO [+] ] as a function of Galactocentric distance as given by Pineda et al. (2013)," (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirmed optically thick assumption tau > 1 for HCO+, consistent with Xu et al. (2023).  
  > "It is evident that the optically thick assumption, g a, 12 > 1, is always satisfied for HCO [+], consistenting with the findings presented in Xu et al. (2023)." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
5. **Analyze correlations of I-PDF power-law slope s and equivalent density profile exponent alpha with clump properties and star formation efficiency**: Examine power-law slope s against L/M and clump surface density; convert H13CO+ slope s into radial density profile exponents for spheres and filaments; compute Spearman rank correlation coefficients and linear fits with R^2 between L_bol/L_mol(p) and s or alpha.  
  > "We employ the Spearman rank correlation (SRC) test to assess the significance of the correlations in Figure 8." (2016), which is U 2 =1-2/s." (4.2 power-law slope s of H 13 CO +)
   - parameter: Outlier rejection for alpha_c > 2.5 (3 points excluded)  
  > "There are only three points with U 2 values exceeding 2.5, which are distinctly separated from the distribution of the other points. Therefore, these three points are treated as outliers and are excluded from the fitting process." (4.2 power-law slope s of H 13 CO +)
   - assumption: H13CO+ is optically thin so intensity is directly proportional to column density  
  > "Given that our distribution is I-PDF, to convert the power-law tail slope s into the exponent U, we should assume that the tracer is optically thin, which allows the tracer’s intensity to be directly proportional to the column density" (4.2 power-law slope s of H 13 CO +)
   - fitting: Linear regression with coefficient of determination R^2 and Spearman rank correlation (SRC) test  
  > "We employ the Spearman rank correlation (SRC) test to assess the significance of the correlations in Figure 8." (4.1 power-law slope s); "We utilize the coefficient of determination, denoted as < [2] to assess the goodness of fit." (4.1 power-law slope s)

## Qualitative findings

- All six dense gas tracers exhibit a log-normal distribution at low intensities and a power-law tail at high intensities in their I-PDFs.  
  > "All traces demonstrate a log-normal PDF and exhibit power-law tails." (3.1 Integrated intensity probability distribution functions)
- HCN and HCO+ J=1-0 show sublinear L_bol-to-L_mol(p) scaling relations because of high optical depths, whereas H13CN and HC3N show nearly linear relations and trace gravitationally bound dense gas well.  
  > "H [13] CN and HC 3 N, showing highest linearity in the L bol -to-L mol (p) scaling relations, may serve as the most effective tracers for the dense molecular gas mass among the six molecular line tracers." (5 CONCLUSION); "In contrast, HCN and HCO [+] exhibit a significantly sublinear trend in the L bol -to-L mol (p) scaling relations due to the effects of optical thickness, which diminishes their effectiveness as tracers for gravitationally bound gas." (5 CONCLUSION)
- There is no discernible correlation between the I-PDF power-law tail slope s and either the clump luminosity-to-mass ratio (L/M) or clump surface density across three distance intervals.  
  > "Across three distinct distance intervals — those less than 3 kpc, those ranging from 3 to 6 kpc, and those greater than 6 kpc — there is no discernible trend between the slope s of the power-law tails and the L/M ratio or the density of the clumps." (5 CONCLUSION)
- Star formation efficiency traced by L_bol/L_mol(p) is weakly anti-correlated with the I-PDF power-law tail slope s and the equivalent density profile exponent alpha.  
  > "The ratios of L bol -to-L mol ( ? ), serving as indicators of star formation efficiency within massive star-forming clumps, exhibit a weak anti-correlation with the power-law index in the I-PDF." (abstract); "In addition, the star formation efficiency is also weakly anti-correlated with the exponent U of the corresponding equivalent density distribution." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA 12-m + 7-m ATOMS survey observations filter out diffuse emission larger than the 1 arcmin maximum recoverable scale, making them specifically sensitive to dense, compact, gravitationally bound structures.  
  > "The observations are not sensitive to extended structures larger than 1 [0], therefore, diffuse emission in lines is mostly filtered out. In other words, the ALMA observations are more sensitive to dense and compact structures, which are more likely gravitationally bound." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
- Why this method: Using the power-law tail of the I-PDF isolates high-density gravitationally dominated gas rather than total line luminosity, and smoothing within distance intervals puts clumps at uniform linear resolution.  
  > "To put these massive clumps into the similar physical resolution and discuss the characteristics of the powerlaw tails, the whole sample have been categorized into three distinct distance intervals." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "In this work, we focus on the dense gas traced by the power-law tails in the I-PDFs, which is more likely gravitationally bound gas." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
- Alternative: Purely spherical free-fall collapse geometry is considered unlikely to apply to the gas defining the PDF power-law tails because ATOMS clumps exhibit hub-filament systems; singular polytropic cylinder models (filaments) are evaluated as an alternative.  
  > "However, the purely spherical free-fall picture appears unlikely to apply to the gas which defines the power-law distributions within PDFs, as the gas observed in the ATOMS survey exhibits hubfilament systems (Zhou et al. 2022)." (4.2 power-law slope s of H 13 CO +); "For the singular polytropic cylinders, which depict filaments, we use U 5 =1-1/s (Schneider et al. 2016)." (4.2 power-law slope s of H 13 CO +)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): ALMA observations are insensitive to extended structures larger than ~1 arcmin (missing flux), affecting HCN and HCO+ in particular, though the effect on the compact power-law tail component is mitigated.  
  > "The observations are not sensitive to extended structures larger than 1 [0], therefore, diffuse emission in lines is mostly filtered out." (2 OBSERVATIONS); "We note that both HCN and HCO [+] are also susceptible to the effects of missing flux, although the influence on the power-law component may be somewhat mitigated." (3.2 The L bol -L mol scaling relations)
- Limitation (authors): Converting the I-PDF power-law slope into a radial density profile exponent requires assuming the molecular line tracer is optically thin so intensity is directly proportional to column density.  
  > "Given that our distribution is I-PDF, to convert the power-law tail slope s into the exponent U, we should assume that the tracer is optically thin, which allows the tracer’s intensity to be directly proportional to the column density" (4.2 power-law slope s of H 13 CO +)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The formula printed for the log-normal I-PDF in Section 3.1 writes -(I - mu)^2 / (2 sigma^2) in the exponent rather than -(ln I - mu)^2, and Table 1 reports negative values of sigma for several sources, suggesting a discrepancy between the printed equation and the actual parameterization fitted.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Section 4.1, a more negative slope s is steeper and a less negative (larger) slope s is flatter, so the sign of the correlation between L_bol/L_mol(p) and s requires careful attention to whether s or |s| is plotted (Figure 8 plots negative values of s on the x-axis).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): L_bol is derived from single-dish / survey infrared SED fitting on clump scales, whereas L_mol(p) is measured from ALMA interferometric maps solely within the high-intensity power-law tail, introducing a spatial scale mismatch if multiple cores or sub-clumps contribute unequally to L_bol.
