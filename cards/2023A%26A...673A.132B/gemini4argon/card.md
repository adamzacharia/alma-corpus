## Analysis recipe

1. **Compile observed SEDs from UV to NIR for 50 stars in the R136 core**: Convert optical (HST/WFC3) and NIR (VLT/SPHERE) magnitudes to fluxes using SVO filter transmission curves, and bin flux-calibrated HST/STIS UV spectra into 9 synthetic passbands.  
  > "The optical and NIR observed magnitudes are converted to fluxes using filter transmission curves taken from the SVO Filter Profile Service" (Observed SEDs); "For the UV, we bin the observed flux points by taking the flux average over nine different wavelength regions as specified in 5." (Observed SEDs); "With the 4 optical and 3 NIR photometry points for the R136 core stars, the observed SEDs consist of 16 flux points in total." (Observed SEDs)
   - software: multispec
   - parameter: 9 UV synthetic passbands between 1265.00 Å and 1700.00 Å; 16 total flux points per SED  
  > "With the 4 optical and 3 NIR photometry points for the R136 core stars, the observed SEDs consist of 16 flux points in total." (Observed SEDs); "5 lists the wavelength ranges that we used for binning our UV flux measurements." (UV bands)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Summed the STIS spectra of all stars in the cluster core and compared the integrated flux to the large-aperture (2' x 2') HST/GHRS spectrum, finding agreement within 25% overall and within 10% for wavelengths > 1350 Å.  
  > "To check our flux calibration for systematic uncertainties, we evaluate the integrated flux of the cluster core. To this end, we sum the flux-calibrated spectra of all stars in the cluster core." (Sample and data); "For all wavelengths considered in the fitting, the integrated fluxes match within 25%, and for wavelengths > 1350 Å  the match is even within 10% (1)." (Sample and data)
2. **Compute intrinsic model SEDs for the R136 core stars**: Compute non-LTE stellar atmosphere models using Fastwind adopting stellar parameters from Brands et al. (2022), scaling the output SED by stellar surface area to vary stellar radius.  
  > "We compute models of intrinsic SEDs with the model atmosphere code Fastwind (cite: 1997A&A...323..488S; 2005A&A...435..669P; 2012A&A...537A..79R; 2016A&A...590A..88C; 2018A&A...619A..59S)." (Intrinsic SEDs); "For the stellar parameters, we adopt the values of the optical and UV analysis of (cite: 2022arXiv220211080B) for stars in the core of R136." (Intrinsic SEDs)
   - software: Fastwind
   - assumption: Small changes in stellar radius R_* do not significantly change the atmospheric structure, allowing R_* to be varied by scaling the model SED.  
  > "However, for small changes in $R_*$, the structure of the atmosphere will not change significantly and this means that we can change $R_*$ of the model simply by multiplying the output SED with a factor that represents an increase or decrease in the stellar surface area." (Intrinsic SEDs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested mimicking the UV absorption-line forest with a 10% UV flux decrease (finding minor effect on c2 and none on A_5495, R_5495, L), and compared low mass-loss Fastwind models to hydrostatic TLUSTY models in U-B vs B-V and J-H vs H-K planes (differences <= 2% and <= 1%).  
  > "We mimic the magnitude of the effect by representing the UV absorption-line forest by an overall decrease in the UV model flux of 10%. In this case, the best-fitting UV extinction is only slightly lower ($\Delta c_{2} = -0.05$)" (Intrinsic SEDs); "We find that the qualitative behaviour of the two sets of models is similar, and that the absolute differences between the Fastwind and TLUSTY colours are never more than 2%." (Intrinsic SEDs); "Second, we compare the behaviour in the $J-H$ versus $H-K$ plane of the TLUSTY and Fastwind SEDs. Again, we find that the qualitative behaviour of the two sets of models is similar, and furthermore, that the differences are never larger than 1%." (Intrinsic SEDs)
3. **Parameterise the modified NIR-to-UV extinction law and fit individual stellar SEDs**: Construct a cubic spline extinction law in the optical/NIR based on Maíz Apellániz et al. (2014) and a UV parameterisation from Fitzpatrick & Massa (1990) with independent c2 and c4, then fit 5 free parameters per star to the observed SEDs.  
  > "Briefly, we use the optical and NIR law of (cite: 2014AA...564A..63M), which we transform to the functional form of (cite: 1999PASP..111...63F). For the UV, we adopt the parameterisation of and (cite: 1990ApJS...72..163F)." (Extinction law); "We fit five parameters: $R_{5495}$, affecting the shape of the optical and NIR part of the curve; $c_2$ and $c_4$, relating to the UV part of the curve; the extinction in the $V$ band $A_V$; and the stellar angular radius $\theta_R = R_*/d$" (Fitting)
   - software: Python, scipy, numpy, lmfit
   - parameter: Fixed parameters: c1 = 2.030 - 3.007 c2, c3 = 1.463, c5 = 5.9, x0 = 4.558, gamma = 0.945; Free parameters: R_5495, A_V, R_*, c2, c4  
  > "We do not fit the data of the bump as we lack observations here, and instead adopt the values that (cite: 2003ApJ...594..279G) find for LMC SGS 2 (near 30 Doradus), that is, $c_3 = 1.463$, $\gamma = 0.945,$ and $x_0 = 4.558$." (Extinction law); "$c_1$ | 2.030 – 3.007 $c_2$" (Table 3); "(cite: 1990ApJS...72..163F) and do not treat the start of the UV curvature as a free parameter, but adopt a fixed value of $c_5 = 5.9$; we do the same." (Extinction law)
   - assumption: Fixed LMC distance d = 49.59 kpc  
  > "We adopt a fixed LMC distance of $d = 49.59$ kpc for all stars (cite: 2019Natur.567..200P)" (Fitting)
   - assumption: NIR power-law slope alpha_NIR = 1.61 from Maíz Apellániz et al. (2014)  
  > "The NIR part of the law can be approximated by a power law of the form $A_\lambda \propto \lambda^{\alpha_\mathrm{NIR}}$ with $\alpha_\mathrm{NIR} = 1.61$" (Extinction law)
   - fitting: Least-squares Levenberg-Marquardt chi^2 minimisation using lmfit.minimize  
  > "In order to find the best-fitting values of the free parameters $A_V$, $R_*$, and those describing the shape of the curve ($R_{5495}$, $c_2$, and $c_4$), we use the `minimize` function of the Python package `lmfit` (cite: 2014zndo.....11813N). We adopt the least-squares Levenberg-Marquardt method for the minimisation, where we minimise the $\chi^2$ value" (Fitting)
   - validation: Tested refitting SEDs with fixed R_5495 and tested varying the number of UV points, confirming conclusions and spatial trends remain robust.  
  > "We note that the results are robust to changes in the number of UV points that we include in our analysis; a change in the number of points has a negligible effect on the outcome of our fits and would not affect our conclusions." (Observed SEDs); "We note that if we refit the SED while adopting a fixed value of $R_{5495}$(equal for all stars), we still recover the spatial trend for $A_{5495}$, although the gradient is slightly less steep." (Spatial trends in extinction)
4. **Extrapolate extinction to 1657 stars in the outskirts of R136 and analyse spatial trends**: Fit a linear empirical relation between V-I colour and A_5495 for core stars, apply it to 1657 stars with V < 19 in the De Marchi et al. (2011) catalogue, fit spatial trend of A_5495 with RA, and compare with HST, JWST, and ALMA 13CO(2-1) maps.  
  > "we can use this relation to estimate $A_{5495}$ for 1657 sources with $V~<~19$ in the catalogue of (cite: 2011ApJ...739...27D) for which both $V$ and $I$ magnitudes are available." (Extinction towards the outskirts of R136); "Under the assumption that the dust properties of sightlines towards the outskirts of R136 are similar to those of sightlines towards the core, we can use this relation to estimate $A_{5495}$ for 1657 sources with $V~<~19$ in the catalogue of (cite: 2011ApJ...739...27D)" (Extinction towards the outskirts of R136); "10 shows a map of the column density of $^{13}$CO, as well as the positions of the R136 core stars, marked by their extinction." (Extinction due to an extension of the Stapler Nebula)
   - parameter: Magnitude cut V < 19 for outskirts sample (1657 stars)  
  > "estimate $A_{5495}$ for 1657 sources with $V~<~19$ in the catalogue of (cite: 2011ApJ...739...27D) for which both $V$ and $I$ magnitudes are available." (Extinction towards the outskirts of R136)
   - assumption: Dust properties of sightlines towards the outskirts of R136 are similar to those towards the core.  
  > "Under the assumption that the dust properties of sightlines towards the outskirts of R136 are similar to those of sightlines towards the core, we can use this relation to estimate $A_{5495}$ for 1657 sources" (Extinction towards the outskirts of R136)
   - fitting: Linear regression of A_5495 vs V-I and A_5495 vs RA  
  > "A_5495 = [0.38±0.23] + [3.86±0.58](V-I)." (Extinction towards the outskirts of R136); "A_5495 = [1.93±0.04] + [1.74±0.23](RA - 84.68)/0.006" (Spatial trends in extinction)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive revised masses and ages for the WNh stars R136a1, R136a2, and R136a3**: Compare revised stellar luminosities and previously derived T_eff, helium abundance, and surface gravity against single-star evolutionary models using the Bayesian tool Bonnsai.  
  > "For this, we use the Bayesian tool Bonnsai (cite: 2017A&A...598A..60S), in combination with the evolutionary models of (cite: 2011A&A...530A.115B) and (cite: 2015A&A...573A..71K)." (Revised WNh star masses); "For our derivations of stellar masses and ages we use temperature, helium abundance, and surface gravity as derived by (cite: 2022arXiv220211080B), and the revised luminosity from this work." (Revised WNh star masses)
   - software: Bonnsai
   - assumption: Single-star evolution models from Brott et al. (2011) and Köhler et al. (2015)  
  > "in combination with the evolutionary models of (cite: 2011A&A...530A.115B) and (cite: 2015A&A...573A..71K)." (Revised WNh star masses); "Lastly, all these analyses are based on single-star evolution scenarios, but it is important to keep in mind that these very massive stars might be merger products (cite: 2012MNRAS.426.1416B)." (Revised WNh star masses)
   - fitting: Bayesian inference using Bonnsai with Salpeter (1955) IMF prior and Ramírez-Agudelo et al. (2013) rotation distribution prior  
  > "Bonnsai allows the comparison of observed stellar parameters with stellar evolution models in order to infer posterior distributions of model parameters such as initial and current stellar mass." (Revised WNh star masses); "As priors, we adopt the (cite: 1955ApJ...121..161S) initial mass function and the rotation distribution of (cite: 2013A&A...560A..29R)." (Revised WNh star masses)
   - validation: _not stated_

  > "We used `lmfit` version 1.0.3 in combination with Python version 3.8.5." (Fitting)
- **numpy** 1.19.2: Numerical calculations  
  > "Furthermore, we made use of `numpy` version 1.19.2 (cite: harris2020array) and `scipy` version 1.8.1 (cite: 2020SciPy-NMeth)." (Fitting)
- **Bonnsai** : Bayesian inference of initial and current stellar masses and ages of WNh stars

## Qualitative findings

- There is a strong east-to-west spatial gradient in extinction (A_5495 and R_5495) across the 1 pc core of R136, coinciding with cold molecular gas (13CO) from an extension of the Stapler Nebula.  
  > "We detect a strong spatial gradient in the extinction properties across the core of R136, coinciding with a gradient in density of cold gas that is part of an extension of the Stapler Nebula, a molecular cloud lying northeast of the cluster." (abstract)
- High values of R_V towards R136 are accompanied by relatively strong UV extinction with no significant trend between c_2 and R_5495, contrary to Galactic sightlines.  
  > "However, the high values of $R_V$ are accompanied by relatively strong extinction in the ultraviolet, contrary to what is observed for Galactic sightlines." (abstract); "The values show no significant trend as a function of $R_{5495}$, contrary to what is observed for Galactic sightlines (\[eq:fitz_uv_orig\])." (An R_5495-dependent average extinction law for R136)
- For R_V ~ 4.4, about three times (or a factor 2.5 at A_V = 1.0) fewer UV/ionising photons can escape relative to the canonical Galactic extinction law at the same R_V and A_V.  
  > "For $R_{V} \sim 4.4$, about three times fewer ultraviolet photons can escape from the ambient dust environment relative to the canonical Galactic value of $R_{V} \sim 3.1$ at the same $A_{V}$." (abstract); "At $A_V = 1.0$, the extinction towards R136 at UV wavelengths in the range $\lambda \approx 1700 - 1250$ Å is about one magnitude higher compared to the canonical Galactic extinction at the same $R_V$ and $A_V$, implying that the fraction of ionising photons that can escape is a factor 2.5 lower ($e^{\tau} \approx e^{A_V} \approx 0.4$)." (Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 30 Doradus and R136 are proximate and relatively unobscured, allowing resolution of individual massive stars in a starburst-like environment to calibrate dust and stellar properties.  
  > "The proximity of 30 Doradus and its relatively unobscured nature allow us to resolve the stellar population of this starburst-like environment." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Existing LMC and Galactic extinction laws fail to simultaneously fit the high optical R_V and strong UV extinction towards R136, and available data are too sparse to derive a completely new law from scratch, motivating a modification of existing laws using the 'extinction without standards' method.  
  > "When considering the SED from the NIR to the UV, none of the existing extinction laws seem suitable for sightlines towards 30 Doradus." (Extinction law); "The data we have available are insufficient to derive a completely new law: in the NIR and optical, we have only a few data points, and in the near-UV (between the $U$ band and the red end of the STIS/G140M grating at 1710 Å), we lack data altogether." (Extinction law); "We therefore decided to modify an existing law." (Extinction law)
- Alternative: Existing Galactic and LMC extinction laws (e.g. Fitzpatrick 1999, Cardelli et al. 1989, Fitzpatrick et al. 2019, Gordon et al. 2003, Howarth 1983, Pei 1992) were tested and rejected because they cannot simultaneously fit the optical/NIR and UV parts of the R136 SEDs.  
  > "When considering the SED from the NIR to the UV, none of the existing extinction laws seem suitable for sightlines towards 30 Doradus." (Extinction law); "Also, the Galactic laws, many of which allow for a varying $R_V$, are not suitable for 30 Doradus when the analysis is extended to the UV." (Extinction law)
- Alternative: Deriving a completely new extinction law from scratch was rejected due to having too few photometric points in the NIR/optical and no data in the near-UV around the 2175 Å bump.  
  > "The data we have available are insufficient to derive a completely new law: in the NIR and optical, we have only a few data points, and in the near-UV (between the $U$ band and the red end of the STIS/G140M grating at 1710 Å), we lack data altogether." (Extinction law)
- Alternative: Leaving the UV parameter c_1 as a free parameter was tested and rejected because c_1 and c_2 are degenerate given the available data.  
  > "This is because $c_2$ and $c_1$ are (to some extent) degenerate, and we do not have enough data to break this degeneracy. Experiments where we leave $c_1$ free in the fitting process show that, indeed, it is not possible to disentangle these two parameters with our data." (Extinction law)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Pointing inaccuracies in the crowded HST/STIS long-slit observations leave flux calibration uncertainties of order 10% (up to a factor of two in extreme cases), and potential relative calibration divergence at lambda < 1400 Å may cause c_4 to be overestimated.  
  > "As a consequence of pointing inaccuracies, uncertainties in the calibration remain; we estimate these to be on the order of 10% (see below), but in the most extreme cases they could be as large as a factor two." (Sample and data); "If the low fluxes are due to an unknown calibration issue of the STIS spectra, and in reality the shape of the spectra is more like that recorded by GHRS, then in our analysis we have overestimated $c_4$." (The UV curvature (c_4))
- Limitation (authors): The adopted NIR extinction law slope (alpha_NIR = 1.61) is likely imperfect for R136, causing systematic residual patterns in NIR bands and high reduced chi^2 values (dominated by H and K_s bands).  
  > "Upon inspecting the residuals of our fits as a function of $A_V$, we see clear patterns for all NIR bands, indicating that the law is not completely adequate." (Extinction law); "The $H$ and $K_s$ bands dominate the high $\chi^2$ values: relative to the observational uncertainties the residuals in $H$ and $K_s$ are on average six times larger than in the other bands." (An R_5495-dependent average extinction law for R136)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of observations across the 2175 Å bump prevents fitting the Drude profile parameters directly.  
  > "We do not fit the data of the bump as we lack observations here, and instead adopt the values that (cite: 2003ApJ...594..279G) find for LMC SGS 2" (Extinction law)
- Limitation (authors): Inferred initial stellar masses are highly dependent on evolutionary models, chosen observables, and the assumption of single-star evolution rather than stellar mergers.  
  > "We note that initial masses of these stars, both ours and those presented in literature, are highly model dependent (see, e.g. (cite: 2021A&A...647A..13G), (cite: 2022MNRAS.516.4052H), and (cite: 2022arXiv220211080B), their Fig. 17)." (Revised WNh star masses); "Lastly, all these analyses are based on single-star evolution scenarios, but it is important to keep in mind that these very massive stars might be merger products (cite: 2012MNRAS.426.1416B)." (Revised WNh star masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High reduced chi-squared values (average 18.3, up to 164.3) driven by H and K_s band residuals indicate tension between the VLT/SPHERE NIR photometry or its uncertainties and the adopted Maiz Apellaniz et al. (2014) NIR law.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 2175 A bump parameters are fixed to values from LMC SGS 2 (Gordon et al. 2003) without local NUV spectroscopy to verify if the bump strength/width varies across R136.
