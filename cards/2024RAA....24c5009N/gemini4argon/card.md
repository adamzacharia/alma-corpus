## Science

- Fields: evolved_stars
- Question: Re-evaluate the morpho-kinematics of the circumstellar envelope of EP Aquarii—specifically the origin of the high-velocity wings, the formation and spiral nature of the equatorial density enhancement, the bipolar outflows, and the western depression in SiO(5-4) emission—using new analyses of archival ALMA CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4) data.  
  > "The aim of the present article is therefore to shed new light on these issues, by presenting new additional analyses of the emissions of the CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4) lines, in particular in the close neighbourhood of the star." (1 INTRODUCTION); "New analyses of earlier ALMA observations of oxygen-rich AGB star EP Aquarii are presented, which complete a previously published analysis and offer a different interpretation of the morpho-kinematics of the circumstellar envelope (CSE)." (Abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and image high-resolution 2019 (TM0) visibility data separately**: Merge June 5 and June 14, 2019 observations with equal weights, group into velocity channels, and clean using natural weighting with circular masks without continuum subtraction or self-calibration.  
  > "The data of June 5 and 14 observations of the CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4) line emissions have been merged with equal weights and grouped in frequency channels corresponding to Doppler velocity intervals of 0.95 and 0.34 km s − 1, respectively." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION); "Natural weight ing was used for cleaning, producing maps of 2000 × 2000 pixels covering ± 4 arcsec" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION); "We studied in detail images obtained with different mask sizes and retained a radius of 2 arcsec for the SiO(5-4) data and of 1 arcsec for the CO(2-1) data" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - parameter: Channel widths: 0.95 km s^-1 for CO(2-1), 0.34 km s^-1 for SiO(5-4)  
  > "grouped in frequency channels corresponding to Doppler velocity intervals of 0.95 and 0.34 km s − 1, respectively." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - parameter: Image size: 2000 x 2000 pixels covering ±4 arcsec; natural weighting  
  > "Natural weight ing was used for cleaning, producing maps of 2000 × 2000 pixels covering ± 4 arcsec" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - parameter: Cleaning mask radius: 1 arcsec for CO(2-1), 2 arcsec for SiO(5-4)  
  > "retained a radius of 2 arcsec for the SiO(5-4) data and of 1 arcsec for the CO(2-1) data" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - assumption: Systemic star velocity of -33.6 km s^-1  
  > "We refer the Doppler velocity V z to a systemic star velocity of − 33.6 km s − 1 in conformity with earlier work" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared TM0 images with combined data cubes and modelled discs of various sizes to evaluate short-spacing flux loss and distortion beyond ~0.6-0.7 arcsec; tested different mask sizes and self-calibration.  
  > "We studied its effect in much detail, both comparing the produced images with those given by the combined data and by modelling the source as discs of various sizes." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION); "While the decline of detected flux starts well below the nominal MRS, important distortions appear only beyond ∼ 0.6-0.7" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
2. **Characterize the inner and outer Equatorial Density Enhancement (EDE) morpho-kinematics**: Extract Doppler velocity spectra and Vz vs omega position-velocity (PV) maps in 100 mas concentric rings (100-500 mas) for CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4); compute mean and rms of Vz over |Vz| < 2 km s^-1 in 10 deg x 100 mas (omega, R) bins across 1.2 < R < 5.2 arcsec; average central channels (-0.75 < Vz < 0.53 km s^-1) to correct for EDE plane tilt and evaluate spiral vs offset-circle geometry.  
  > "In order to better explore the morpho-kinematics of the nascent EDE, we display in Figure 4 V z vs ω maps of the CO(2-1) emission in 100 mas wide rings centred on the star." (3.1 CO(2-1) emission); "In each ( ω, R ) bin, with a width of 10 ◦ × 100 mas, we calculate the mean and rms values of V z over the central Doppler velocity interval, |V z |< 2 km s − 1 ." (3.1 CO(2-1) emission); "Correcting for this tilt by averaging the brightness over the four central channels, ( − 0.75 <V z < 0.53 km s − 1 ) we obtain the map shown in the left panel of Figure 8." (3.3 Patterns of enhanced CO(2-1) emission)
   - parameter: Inner rings: 100 mas width from R = 100 to 500 mas  
  > "Upper panels: Doppler velocity spectra in successive rings, each 100 mas wide, starting from the circle of radius 100 mas centred on the star." (Fig. 3 caption)
   - parameter: Outer EDE region: 1.2 < R < 5.2 arcsec, |Vz| < 2 km s^-1, bin size 10 deg x 100 mas  
  > "In each ( ω, R ) bin, with a width of 10 ◦ × 100 mas, we calculate the mean and rms values of V z over the central Doppler velocity interval, |V z |< 2 km s − 1 ." (3.1 CO(2-1) emission)
   - assumption: EDE inclination of 10 deg with respect to the plane of the sky and rms flaring angle of ~15 deg  
  > "This suggests a radial expansion velocity at the scale of ∼ 0.3/sin10 ◦ =1.7 km s − 1, consistent with the low value of 1.9 km s − 1 retained in earlier studies (Hoai et al. 2019). The rms deviation of 1 km s − 1 combines the effect of the known rms flaring angle of ∼ 15 ◦, sin15 ◦ × 1.7=0.44 km s − 1 to an intrinsic width of ∼ 0.9 km s − 1 ." (3.1 CO(2-1) emission)
   - fitting: Analytical comparison with pure rotation sine wave (v = 4 km s^-1) in Vz vs omega and geometric comparison of Archimedean spirals (rho = 0.5 + alpha/pi) with offset circles.  
  > "The sine waves shown as reference correspond to pure rotation with a velocity of 4 km s − 1 ." (Fig. 4 caption); "Right: Comparing two spirals of equation ρ = 0 . 5 + α/π (blue and red) with two circles (black) of radius R = 1 . 5 (2) offset by − 0.5 (+0.5) with respect to the origin." (Fig. 8 caption)
   - validation: Compared TM0 and combined data Vz vs omega maps in 100-500 mas rings.  
  > "CO(2-1) emission: TM0 (upper row) and combined (lower row) data. V z vs ω maps in 100 mas wide rings as indicated on top of each panel." (Fig. 4 caption)
3. **Analyze polar outflow arcs and latitudinal velocity dependence in CO(2-1)**: Inspect 51 velocity channels (-8.2 to +8.2 km s^-1) over 1.2 < R < 5.2 arcsec in x-y, omega-R, Vz-R, and Vz-omega projections; divide brightness by power-law radial fits to test for radial-decline bias; compare Vz vs R maps in narrow position angle wedges against parametric latitudinal expansion velocity curves f(alpha) = sin^n(alpha).  
  > "We studied in great detail the data cube (combined data) covering 51 frequency channels (Doppler velocities between − 8.2 and +8.2 km s − 1 ) and projected angular distances from the star between 1.2 and 5.2 arcsec (Figures A1 to A4 of the Appendix)." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission); "The left panel of Figure 11 shows three examples of function f ( α ), all three of the form sin n α, with n = 1 2 , 1 and 2 respectively (in addition to the trivial cases f ( α )=0 and 1)." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
   - parameter: 51 frequency channels (-8.2 < Vz < +8.2 km s^-1), radial range 1.2 < R < 5.2 arcsec  
  > "We studied in great detail the data cube (combined data) covering 51 frequency channels (Doppler velocities between − 8.2 and +8.2 km s − 1 ) and projected angular distances from the star between 1.2 and 5.2 arcsec" (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
   - assumption: Thin radially expanding shell geometry where Vz = V sin(alpha) and R = r cos(alpha)  
  > "As illustrated in Figure 11, a small volume of gas at distance r from the star, at latitude α and having a radial velocity V has coordinates V z = V sin α and R = r cos α ." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
   - fitting: Power-law fit to radial brightness distribution in individual channels; visual comparison of PV flows to f(alpha) = sin^n(alpha) curves for n = 1/2, 1, 2.  
  > "we examined the brightness divided by a power fit to its radial distribution in the relevant frequency channel, found to have an index between − 0.2 and − 0.4." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission); "Comparison with low latitude observations, as shown in the right panels of Figure 11 (see also Figure S3), favours clearly values of n smaller than 1" (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
   - validation: Tested automated pixel-lumping pattern recognition (rejected due to overlap complexity) and verified that dividing by radial power-law fits introduced no significant bias.  
  > "We attempted to do so by using an algorithm lumping together the brighter pixels. But we failed to obtain a satisfactory result, the complexity of the patterns preventing reliable pattern recognition." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission); "In no case was it considered to be a source of significant bias." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
4. **Quantify SiO(5-4) inhomogeneity, the western and northern emission voids, and correlation with CO(2-1)**: Compare observed SiO(5-4) intensity in 0.5 < R < 2.3 arcsec to the smooth normalized product U(R) x S(Vz) in red and blue hemispheres; construct Vz vs omega maps in 100 mas inner rings and Vz vs R maps integrated over 240 deg < omega < 300 deg (western void) and 280 deg < omega < 360 deg (northern void) for both SiO and CO; normalize brightness by power-law fits A*R^b in 0.7 < R < 2.0 arcsec (excluding 240 < omega < 300 deg in blue) and perform linear correlation fits between normalized SiO and CO maps.  
  > "we show in Figure 13 the radial distribution U ( R ), averaged over position angle and integrated over V z, and the Doppler velocity spectrum S ( V z ), of the volume of the data cube defined as 0.5 <R< 2.3 arcsec, 0 <V z < 8 km s − 1 ; we then compare the observed intensity to the product U ( R ) × S ( V z ), properly normalized" (4.2 SiO(5-4) emission: general features); "we take advantage of its confinement to a relatively narrow interval of position angle, 240 ◦ < ω < 300 ◦ to study the distributions of the CO and SiO line emissions in the V z vs R plane, integrated over ω" (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star); "In practice, we consider the ring 0.7 <R< 2.0 arcsec, also excluding the western sextant 240 ◦ <ω< 300 ◦ in the blue" (4.4 SiO(5-4) emission: patterns of enhanced emission)
   - parameter: Smoothness test volume: 0.5 < R < 2.3 arcsec, 0 < |Vz| < 8 km s^-1  
  > "of the volume of the data cube defined as 0.5 <R< 2.3 arcsec, 0 <V z < 8 km s − 1" (4.2 SiO(5-4) emission: general features)
   - parameter: Void position angle sectors: 240 deg < omega < 300 deg (western) and 280 deg < omega < 360 deg (northern)  
  > "240 ◦ < ω < 300 ◦ to study the distributions of the CO and SiO line emissions in the V z vs R plane" (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star); "With the aim of exploring the properties of the northern depression, we repeated the analysis made for the western void in the interval of position angles 280 ◦ < ω < 360 ◦ ." (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star)
   - parameter: Correlation region: 0.7 < R < 2.0 arcsec (excluding 240 deg < omega < 300 deg in blue-shifted hemisphere)  
  > "the ring 0.7 <R< 2.0 arcsec, also excluding the western sextant 240 ◦ <ω< 300 ◦ in the blue" (4.4 SiO(5-4) emission: patterns of enhanced emission)
   - fitting: Power-law radial fit A*R^b and linear pixel-by-pixel correlation fit F_norm(SiO) = F0 + G0 * F_norm(CO) using normalized chi^2.  
  > "A fit of the form F norm (SiO)= F 0 + G 0 ×F norm (CO) gives a normalised χ 2, with the values of √ χ 2 in the examples shown in Figure 18 being 0.14 and 0.21, in the red- and blue-shifted hemispheres, respectively" (4.4 SiO(5-4) emission: patterns of enhanced emission)

  > "Comparing the TM0 and combined data images, and accounting for the different angular resolutions, they clearly reveal the presence in the red-shifted hemisphere of an outflow covering the southern quadrant and of a depression covering the northern quadrant." (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star)
5. **Separate and quantify the two components of the high Doppler velocity wings**: Integrate TM0 SiO(5-4) spectra in 20 mas wide annular rings from mean radius 10 mas to 390 mas; measure the full width at 10% of maximum (FWTM) as a function of R; extrapolate the red-shifted spectrum edge down to continuum level in the combined data to measure V_max as a function of R and omega; construct |Vz| vs R PV maps and intensity maps in 8 < |Vz| < 12 km s^-1 and 12 < |Vz| < 20 km s^-1.  
  > "we use the high resolution TM0 data in the form of Doppler velocity spectra of SiO(5-4) emission, integrated in 20 mas broad annular rings centred on the star with mean radii increasing from 10 mas to 390 mas in steps of 20 mas. On each of these we define values of the full width measured at 10% of maximum, FWTM" (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES); "We also estimate the maximal Doppler velocity V max, reached in the red-shifted hemisphere by the bulk of the expanding CSE, by extrapolating the upper end of the spectrum down to continuum level (essentially zero) using the combined data." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES); "Another illustration is given by the intensity maps displayed in the left pair of panels of Figure 21 for 8 <|V z |< 12 km s − 1 (combined data) and in the right pair of panels of the same figure for 12 <|V z |< 20 km s − 1 (TM0 data)." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)
   - parameter: 20 annular rings of 20 mas width, mean radii from 10 mas to 390 mas; FWTM threshold at 10% of maximum  
  > "integrated in 20 mas broad annular rings centred on the star with mean radii increasing from 10 mas to 390 mas in steps of 20 mas. On each of these we define values of the full width measured at 10% of maximum, FWTM" (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)
   - fitting: Exponential fit to FWTM(R) = 21.3 + 19.0 * exp(-R/0.10) km s^-1; separable linear-sinusoidal fit to V_max(R, omega) = (1.13 - 0.11*R) * [11.1 + 0.8*sin(omega - 71 deg)].  
  > "It is well described by a fit of the form 21.3+19.0 exp( −R /0.10) km s − 1 ." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES); "The dependence of V max on R and ω is well described by a fit of the form V max =(1.13 − 0.11 R ) × [11.1+0.8 sin( ω− 71 ◦ )]." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)
   - validation: Evaluated rms deviation between V_max data and fit (0.36 km s^-1) and noted that the 10% FWTM choice is insensitive to exact threshold value.  
  > "This choice of 10% is a compromise between minimizing systematic uncertainties and staying well above noise and its precise value is of no relevance to the validity of the result." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES); "The rms deviation between data and fit is 0.36 km s − 1 ." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)

## Qualitative findings

- The Equatorial Density Enhancement (EDE) is already formed within 100-200 mas of the star, where its kinematics is dominated by rotation (~few km s^-1) before transitioning to slow radial expansion (< 2 km s^-1) farther out.  
  > "and 200 mas, and its kinematics is dominated by rotation at a few km s − 1 scale." (3.1 CO(2-1) emission); "evidence has been obtained for rotation in the central region evolving to radial expansion at a low rate of less than 2 km s − 1 beyond a" (3.3 Patterns of enhanced CO(2-1) emission)
- When averaging over the four central velocity channels (-0.75 < Vz < 0.53 km s^-1) to correct for the tilt of the EDE plane, the single spiral structure proposed by Homan et al. (2018) is no longer obvious and the features can instead be described as overlapping eccentric arcs.  
  > "Correcting for this tilt by averaging the brightness over the four central channels, ( − 0.75 <V z < 0.53 km s − 1 ) we obtain the map shown in the left panel of Figure 8. The identification of a spiral as proposed by Homan et al. (2018) and shown in the central panel of the figure is no longer obvious." (3.3 Patterns of enhanced CO(2-1) emission)
- The western blue-shifted depression in SiO(5-4) (and a weaker northern red-shifted depression) lies in the wake of a faster-moving shell of ejected gas (R ~ 0.5-1 arcsec, V ~ 5-10 km s^-1) launched a few decades ago, rather than being caused by UV dissociation from a white dwarf companion.  
  > "The void of emission, for both SiO and CO lines, is clearly seen in its wake, suggesting that it simply results from the radial velocity of the shell significantly exceeding that of the wind that followed it." (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star)
- The high-velocity wings consist of two distinct components: the upper velocity end of the global polar outflows (~12 km s^-1) and an effective line broadening confined within R < 200 mas reaching ~20 km s^-1 caused by pulsations and convective cell shocks, refuting the earlier narrow polar jet interpretation of Tuan-Anh et al. (2019).  
  > "This analysis gives therefore evidence for two major contributions to the high velocity wings of the SiO(5-4) Doppler velocity spectrum: one, extending up to some 12 km s − 1, is simply the upper edge of the velocity distribution of the polar outflows; the other, reaching up to 20 km s − 1, is confined to the close neighbourhood of the star" (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Re-reducing the 2019 extended-configuration (TM0) observations separately—which had been down-weighted to 20% in Homan et al. (2020a)—provides ~30 mas resolution needed to probe the inner 200 mas where the EDE, rotation, and high-velocity wings originate, while the combined cubes probe the extended envelope up to 5-10 arcsec.  
  > "(referred to as TM0) used an extended antenna configuration and were combined with the earlier observations of lower angular resolution by Homan et al. (2020a) with a low weight of 20% by fear of possible artefacts" (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
- Why this method: Examining multi-dimensional projections (Vz vs R, Vz vs omega, omega vs R) in narrow rings and wedges avoids smoothing out episodic shell structures and separates tilted-plane velocity effects from true spatial spirals.  
  > "Correcting for this tilt by averaging the brightness over the four central channels, ( − 0.75 <V z < 0.53 km s − 1 ) we obtain the map shown in the left panel of Figure 8." (3.3 Patterns of enhanced CO(2-1) emission); "integrating over the whole range of position angles or over a large radial distance smoothens out the fluctuations resulting from the episodic nature of the mass ejections" (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
- Alternative: White dwarf companion at R ~ 0.4 arcsec west of the star focusing the EDE into a spiral and photodissociating SiO(5-4) via UV radiation (Homan et al. 2018, 2020a)  
  > "The interpretation given by Homan et al. (2020a), giving a major role to a white dwarf companion currently located at ∼ 400 mas west of the star, has been shown to be not unique and, accordingly, to be less reliable than previously thought" (6 SUMMARY AND DISCUSSION)
- Alternative: Narrow polar jets launched at < 25 au and accelerating between 20 and 100 au as the origin of the ±20 km s^-1 wings (Tuan-Anh et al. 2019)  
  > "High Doppler velocity wings, reaching ∼± 20 km s − 1, were interpreted by Tuan-Anh et al. (2019) as two narrow polar structures, referred to as jets, launched from less than 25 au away from the star and building up between ∼ 20 au and ∼ 100 au to maximal velocity." (1 INTRODUCTION); "The interpretation given by Tuan-Anh et al. (2019) of these high velocity wings, using data of lower angular resolution, was mixing both contributions." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)
- Alternative: Automated pixel-lumping algorithm for pattern recognition of arcs in the CO(2-1) channel maps  
  > "We attempted to do so by using an algorithm lumping together the brighter pixels. But we failed to obtain a satisfactory result, the complexity of the patterns preventing reliable pattern recognition." (4.1 CO(2-1) emission)
- Alternative: Self-calibration of the 2019 TM0 visibility data  
  > "We performed self-calibration but, as it did not result in a significant improvement of the quality of the line images, the data used here do not include self-calibration." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
- Alternative: Visibility-level continuum subtraction for the 2019 TM0 data  
  > "We did not subtract the continuum contribution at visibility level, which would have been improper when looking over the stellar disc because of important absorption." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
- How they decided it was enough: Cross-checked TM0 against combined data and simulated discs to ensure TM0 analysis was restricted to R < 0.6-0.7 arcsec where short-spacing distortions are negligible, while admitting that higher sensitivity and molecular lines from vibrationally excited states are still needed to definitively distinguish between models.  
  > "For the purpose of the present work, we checked carefully that whenever TM0 data were used, proper account was taken of this issue." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION); "Only then could one decide with confidence which of the two models is closer to reality. In the present stage, we need additional ALMA observations of high angular resolution and sensitivity to tell them apart." (6 SUMMARY AND DISCUSSION)
- Limitation (authors): SO2(4_{2,2}-3_{1,3}) and 13CO(2-1) spectral windows in the 2019 observations had insufficient sensitivity to provide new information and were omitted.  
  > "However, the latter two sets of data had insufficient sensitivity to contribute significantly new information and their analysis is omitted from the present work." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of short baselines (< 100 m, and a 100-200 m gap on June 14) in the 2019 TM0 data reduces detected flux and introduces anisotropic distortions beyond ~0.6-0.7 arcsec.  
  > "As the angular distance from the star increases, it reduces the detected flux first uniformly, then introducing anisotropic distortions of the emission pattern." (2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION)
- Limitation (authors): Below R ~ 200 mas, available data cannot disentangle the simultaneous contributions of expansion, rotation, EDE formation, dust condensation, and pulsation/convective shock waves.  
  > "Unfortunately, with presently available data, it is difficult to tell apart, in the immediate neighbourhood of the star, say below R∼ 200 mas, the contributions of expansion, of rotation and of phenomena related to the generation of the nascent wind: dust formation and shock waves caused by the interaction between stellar pulsation and convective cell partition (see Section 5)." (3.1 CO(2-1) emission)
- Limitation (authors): The nearly face-on inclination (~10 deg) of the EDE prevents a precise evaluation of its radial expansion velocity.  
  > "Its small inclination prevents a precise evaluation of the morpho-kinematics" (3.3 Patterns of enhanced CO(2-1) emission)
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution is insufficient to resolve the fine spatial/temporal structure associated with the ~110-day stellar pulsation period, and lack of reliable light curve measurements prevents relating inner shock features to stellar phase.  
  > "The angular resolution of the present observations cannot resolve the fine time structure associated with the pulsation period, ∼ 110 days." (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star); "Unfortunately, the lack of reliable light curve measurements prevents commenting on a possible relation with the stellar phase at the time of observations." (5 HIGH DOPPLER VELOCITIES)
- Limitation (authors): The physical mechanism governing the precocious formation of the equatorial disc (and why CO emission has not resumed at R < 200 mas after the western mass ejection while SiO has) remains unexplained, preventing a definitive claim of model uniqueness.  
  > "While appealing, the interpretation of the western void proposed in the present section leaves a number of questions unanswered, in particular concerning the mechanism responsible for the formation of the equatorial disc. The complexity of the observed features prevents claiming its validity and unicity with strong confidence." (4.3 SiO(5-4) emission in the close neighbourhood of the star)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No radiative transfer or hydrodynamical modelling is performed; interpretations of optical depth, excitation temperature effects (beyond a simple Boltzmann factor estimate), and shock wakes are purely kinematic and geometric.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The paper does not specify which software package (e.g., CASA or GILDAS) or deconvolution parameters (stopping threshold, niter) were used to image the 2019 TM0 visibility data.
