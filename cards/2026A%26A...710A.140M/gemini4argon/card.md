   - parameter: 3-sigma clipping  
  > "Mackey (2018), applying a 3σ clipping." (3.1 ALMA continuum images and 12CO emission moment maps)

   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure disk position angles and continuum/12CO fluxes**: Manually measure orientation of major axes for PA. Integrate continuum flux and moment 0 12CO line flux within rectangular apertures tailored to each disk.  
  > "We determined the position angle of each disks by manually measuring the orientation of their major axes (column 2 in Table 2)." (3.1. ALMA continuum images and 12CO emission moment maps); "The fluxes were measured by integrating within rectangular apertures tailored to each disk." (3.2. Millimeter continuum and 12CO (3-2) line fluxes); "The gas flux was extracted from the M0 maps, following the same process as the continuum flux with a 10% calibration error." (3.2. Millimeter continuum and 12CO (3-2) line fluxes)
   - parameter: 10% flux calibration uncertainty combined quadratically with measurement error  
  > "We quadratically combined the measurement error and the calibration error estimated at 10% which correspond to the typical flux calibration errors of ALMA (see ALMA Technical Handbook 1 )." (3.2. Millimeter continuum and 12CO (3-2) line fluxes)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure ALMA continuum and 12CO radial extents**: Extract surface brightness profiles along the major axis integrated over the vertical extent and normalized by peak intensity. For 12CO, visually select the channel with maximum radial extent on blue and red sides in the image cube. Measure diameter at 3-sigma above background noise.  
  > "To measure the radial extent of the disks from their continuum emission we extracted surface brightness profiles along the major axis of each disk. (3.3. ALMA radial extents)
   - parameter: 3-sigma threshold above background (5-sigma minimum between disks on West side of HH 48 NE)  
  > "The major axis size was determined at a level of 3σ above the background." (3.3. ALMA radial extents); "In this case, we estimated the maximal extent as the point of the minimum emission between the two disks, which slightly underestimates the size since this minimum is at the 5σ level." (3.3. ALMA radial extents)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainty estimated by varying the integration window size and quadratically adding 1/10 of the beam size along the major axis; compared cube cuts against Moment 0 maps.  
  > "Therefore, we estimated the associated uncertainty by varying the window over which the profile is estimated and quadratically added a tenth of the beam size along the major axis direction." (3.3. ALMA radial extents); "We note that a few of these values are larger by up to 40% (in the most pathological case) than those estimated from the moment 0 maps due to differences in noise statistics and cloud contamination" (3.3. ALMA radial extents)
4. **Measure ALMA continuum and 12CO vertical extents and disk inclinations**: Extract minor-axis brightness profiles. For continuum, fit a Gaussian to obtain FWHM and quadratically subtract beam FWHM. Estimate inclination from axis ratio using major axis size at 50% peak flux (or ellipse fit to ring for Lup 160708). For 12CO, collapse all channels with detected emission and measure minor-axis extent at 3-sigma without Gaussian fitting.  
  > "The profiles being single peaked because the continuum vertical extent is not well resolved, we estimated the FWHM by fitting Gaussians to the disk profiles except for Lup 160708 where the ring geometry prevents a measurement of the intrinsic vertical extent." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents); "We also quadratically subtract the FWHM of the beam from the measured FWHM to obtain the intrinsic FWHM and propagated the errors." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents); "We have chosen to collapse all channels for which the gas emission was detected to gain SNR. To estimate the gas vertical extent, we followed the same method as for the radial extent of the gas without fitting Gaussians to the disk profiles" (3.4. ALMA vertical extents)
   - parameter: 50% maximum flux for continuum axis ratio; 3-sigma threshold for 12CO vertical extent  
  > "To measure the axis ratio, we use the major axis size at 50% of the maximum flux that can be readily compared to the intrinsic FWHM along the minor axis." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents)
   - fitting: 1D Gaussian fit to continuum minor-axis profile; ellipse fit to continuum ring of Lup 160708  
  > "we estimated the FWHM by fitting Gaussians to the disk profiles except for Lup 160708 where the ring geometry prevents a measurement of the intrinsic vertical extent." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents); "except for Lup 160708 where an ellipse is fitted to the ring." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents)
   - validation: Measurement error set to 1/10 of projected beam size (for continuum) or integration window variation plus 10% of projected beam size (for 12CO).  
  > "The measurements errors correspond to a tenth of the beam size projected in the direction of the cut." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents); "We quadratically added 10% of the beam size in the direction of the cut to that uncertainty." (3.4. ALMA vertical extents)

## Qualitative findings

- Three distinct 12CO moment map morphologies are identified: rectangular elongated shape in edge-on disks in multiple systems, sharp X-shape in single edge-on disks, and fluffy filled-in X-shape with vertical asymmetry in grazing-angle disks.  
  > "We identified three different geometries in the moment maps of Fig. 2: the typical rectangular elongated shape of edge-on disks (HK Tau B, HV Tau C, HH 48 NE, PDS 144 N – intriguingly, all are members of multiple systems), the X-shape associated with edge-on disks (Dutrey et al. 2017; Louvet et al. 2018; Duchêne et al. 2024) with sharp edges (Flying Saucer, Tau 042021, Oph 163131), and finally, the fluffy X-shape for the less inclined disks (Haro 6-5B, Lup 160703 and Lup 160708)." (3.1. ALMA continuum images and 12CO emission moment maps)
- Lup 160708 and Haro 6-5B show ring geometries in continuum and vertical-only emission in PV diagrams.  
  > "two disks only contain information along the vertical direction in their PV diagrams (Haro 6-5B and Lup 160708). This can be the tracer of a ring geometry (Dutrey et al. 2017)," (3.5 Dynamical masses)
- In multiple systems, primary stars host disks with larger continuum and 12CO radial extents, whereas secondary stars host disks with larger 12CO vertical extents.  
  > "We also found that the millimeter dust radial extent is larger in the primary than in secondary component, which is in agreement with Manara et al. (2019), as is also the case for the [12] CO gas radial extent. ## Reasoning

- Why this data: Highly inclined disks allow direct measurement of vertical extents with minimal de-projection, and line-of-sight integration compensates for low optical depth in faint outer regions; 3 disks from Villenave et al. (2020) were excluded due to an envelope, CO outflow, or circumbinary nature.  
  > "Highly inclined disks are the best objects for this study because their vertical extent is more directly accessible, with minimal de-projection required." (1. Introduction); "Moreover, highly inclined disks can provide better constraints on the disk outer radii because of the line-of-sight integration which compensates the low optical depth in the fainter outer regions." (1. Introduction); "We choose to not include 3 disks from the previous survey either because of the presence of an envelope, a CO outflow, or because of the circumbinary nature of the source (IRAS 04302, HH 30, and IRAS 04158 respectively)." (2.1. Target selection)
- Why this method: Dynamical masses from PV diagrams are independent of stellar models and viewing geometry (where edge-on disks block photospheric light); 12CO radial extents were measured on visually selected peak-extent channels in cubes rather than Moment 0 maps because Moment 0 maps suffer from noise statistics and cloud contamination; visual Keplerian curves were used on PV diagrams because modest SNR, cloud contamination, and substructures preclude a robust statistical approach.  
  > "If possible, estimating the dynamical mass thanks to the interaction between the host star and the gas of the disk in rotation is the most reliable method to determine this parameter independently of stellar models or viewing geometry" (1. Introduction); "We note that a few of these values are larger by up to 40% (in the most pathological case) than those estimated from the moment 0 maps due to differences in noise statistics and cloud contamination, confirming the inadequacy of moment maps to estimate the full radial extent of disks." (3.3. ALMA radial extents); "The estimation of stellar masses from PV diagrams is generally ill defined due to the modest SNR per spaxel, cloud contamination, and the presence of possible substructures and asymmetries, which precludes a robust statistical approach." (3.5. Dynamical masses)
- Alternative: Measuring 12CO radial extents from Moment 0 maps instead of channel cubes (rejected because Moment 0 maps underestimate sizes by up to 40%, except for Flying Saucer where the literature diameter of 5.5'' was adopted due to low SNR in the cube).  
  > "We note that a few of these values are larger by up to 40% (in the most pathological case) than those estimated from the moment 0 maps due to differences in noise statistics and cloud contamination, confirming the inadequacy of moment maps to estimate the full radial extent of disks." (3.3. ALMA radial extents); "However, there is an exception for Flying Saucer, for which the value is lower compared to what is apparent in the moment map (Fig. 4) likely due to low SNR. For this disk, in the following part of our study, we adopt the diameter radial extent of 5 [′′] . 5 (Dutrey et al. 2017)." (3.3. ALMA radial extents)
- Alternative: Using HST optical images of HV Tau C to measure its scattered light size (rejected in favor of 2 um VLT/NaCo adaptive optics image due to remnant envelope morphology in optical).  
  > "In the case of HV Tau C, the HST optical images present a rather extended and round morphology indicating the presence of a remnant envelope. To better assess the size of the scattered light disk, we instead used the 2 µm VLT/NaCo adaptive optics image presented in Duchêne et al. (2010)." (2.4. Scattered light archival data (HST and VLT))

  > "Since GAIA DR3 distance measurements are unreliable for edge-on disks, we considered distance of 130 pc for Taurus, 140 pc for Ophiuchus, 140 pc for Upper-Scorpius, 160 pc for Lupus and 190 pc for Chamaeleon (Zucker et al. 2020)." (3.5. Dynamical masses)

- Limitation (authors): Sample suffers from a selection bias toward larger disks because edge-on disks were identified from resolved HST images.  
  > "This is a consequence of a known selection bias as only the larger edge-on disks are well-resolved in the HST images used to identify them in the first place (Angelo et al. 2023)." (5.1.1. Radial extents)

- Limitation (authors): The 1/cos(i) flux correction assumes an infinitely thin emitting layer, which is imperfect near 90 deg inclination or for vertically extended CO emission, and small inclination uncertainties cause large errors in the correction factor.  
  > "We note that this correction assumes an infinitely thin emitting layer, which may not be perfect for the CO emission or for the continuum emission when the inclination approaches 90 [◦] . - Limitation (authors): Measured vertical extents H represent apparent full emitting heights rather than hydrostatic scale heights h.

  > "However, these sizes should be considered as underestimates if the disk radially extends further on one side than the other." (4.3. Optical and NIR scattered light vertical extent)
- Limitation (authors): Dedicated radiative transfer models for highly inclined disks are lacking to disentangle radial drift from optical depth effects and to reconstruct vertical temperature profiles.  
  > "Models of radial drift with radiative transfer dedicated to highly inclined disks are required to disentangle between radial drift and opacity effects based on the R gas / R dust, mm ratio." (5.1.1. Radial extents); "A thorough interpretation of this anti-correlation will require full radiative transfer models tailored to each system, as well as tomographic reconstruction of their vertical temperature profile (e.g., Dutrey et al. 2017; Flores et al. 2021), which is beyond the scope of this study." (5.2.1. Disk properties)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dynamical masses are estimated visually by overplotting 3 Keplerian curves on PV diagrams rather than forward-modeling 3D radiative transfer and kinematics in the visibility or image cube domain; the derived masses are systematically 30-50% higher than literature values.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 3-sigma isophotal sizes (and visual selection of peak-extent channels for 12CO) depend on sensitivity and SNR rather than a flux-fraction metric (e.g., R_68 or R_90) commonly used in the low-inclination literature comparison samples.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a 1/cos(i) geometric correction to edge-on disks (i > 80-88 deg) diverges as i approaches 90 deg and neglects emission from the optically thick vertical outer wall/flared surface.
