## Analysis recipe

1. **Determine geometry and recenter visibilities**: Fit a Gaussian in visibility space; move the phase center to the Gaussian center.  
  > "By using the CASA tool `uvmodelfit`, we fitted the data by a Gaussian shape, and the phase center was corrected to be the center of the Gaussian shape by `fixvis`." (Observations and results)
   - software: CASA, uvmodelfit, fixvis
   - parameter: i = 37.2° ± 0.026°; position angle = 32.4° ± 0.04°.  
  > "The inclination $i=37.2^\circ \pm 0.026^{\circ}$ and position angle $\phi=32.4^\circ \pm 0.04^{\circ}$ are obtained by the Gaussian fit by `uvmodelfit`" (Observations and results)
   - parameter: Adopt the geometry fitted to C43-8.  
  > "We adopted the values of the C43-8 data." (Observations and results)
   - fitting: Gaussian visibility fit with uvmodelfit.  
  > "By using the CASA tool `uvmodelfit`, we fitted the data by a Gaussian shape" (Observations and results)
   - validation: Compare C43-8 and C43-4 geometry fits; test the adopted center using imaginary-image RMS in step 6.  
  > "The fitted inclination and position angle are slightly different in the C43-8 (sparse configuration) and C43-4 (compact configuration) data." (Observations and results); "Hence, the phase center determined by the Gaussian fit is consistent with the center which minimalize the asymmetry." (Asymmetric structure)
2. **Image the continuum**: Combine C43-8 and C43-4 and image both continuum SPWs with tclean/mtmfs.  
  > "We combined the data taken by sparse (C43-8) and compact (C43-4) array configurations to recover the missing flux at larger angular scales." (Observations and results); "the dust continuum image of combined data was synthesized by CASA with the `tclean` task using the `mtmfs` algorithm (cite: rau11) with `nterms`=2." (Observations and results)
   - software: CASA, tclean, mtmfs
   - parameter: Briggs weighting; robust = 0.0; nterms = 2.  
  > "Weighting                  | Briggs" (Table 1); "Robust clean parameter     | 0.0" (Table 1); "with `nterms`=2." (Observations and results)
   - parameter: Synthesized image at 224.9 GHz.  
  > "We obtained a synthesized image at $224.9$ GHz" (Observations and results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Final beam and RMS reported.  
  > "with the beam size of $89.6\times 60.0\mbox{ mas}$ ($17.0 \times 11.4$ au) with PA=$168.8^{\circ}$ and with the $1\sigma$ RMS noise level of $0.029\mbox{ mJy/Beam}$." (Observations and results)
3. **Measure continuum structure and optical-depth diagnostics**: Measure flux above 3σ; inspect spectral-index, brightness-temperature, deprojected axial, and azimuthal profiles.  
  > "The total flux density with $> 3\sigma (0.087\mbox{ mJy})$ emission is measured to be $449.58$ mJy." (Observations and results); "Using the inclination and position angle, we have deprojected the face-on equivalent view, to identify substructures on the disk." (Observations and results); "we show the spectral index map, and panel (d) illustrates the spectral indexes along the major and minor axes." (Observations and results)
   - parameter: Flux threshold: >3σ (0.087 mJy).  
  > "The total flux density with $> 3\sigma (0.087\mbox{ mJy})$ emission is measured to be $449.58$ mJy." (Observations and results)
   - assumption: Passive-disk temperature comparison: grazing angle αg = 0.02 and L* = 11 L⊙.  
  > "The grazing angle $\alpha_g$ is set to be $0.02$ and the stellar luminosity $L_{\ast}$ is $11L_{\odot}$ in the plot." (Observations and results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare brightness and passive-disk midplane temperatures with the spectral-index optical-depth interpretation.  
  > "The brightness temperature is close to the midplane temperature within $0.25$ arcsec from the center, which indicate the optically thick emission." (Observations and results); "The outer region ($R>0.25$ arcsec or $> 50$ au) can be optically thin, which is consistent with the spectral index map mentioned above." (Observations and results)
4. **Fit the axisymmetric intensity profile**: Fit azimuthally averaged real visibilities with a 16-parameter power law, exponential cutoff, two Gaussian bumps, two enhanced/depleted regions, and an inner cavity.  
  > "we adopted a simple power-law intensity profile with exponential cutoff with two Gaussian bumps, two intensity enhanced/depleted regions and an inner cavity" (Model description); "has 16 parameters" (Model description); "We take an average within the bin in radial and azimuthal direction in visibility domain (the overline indicates the average)." (Fitting approach)
   - software: vis_sample, emcee
   - parameter: C43-8 only; fit 233.0 and 216.7 GHz separately.  
  > "Hence, we used only the C43-8 data for the MCMC fitting" (fitting result); "We performed the MCMC fitting for the upper and lower band data separately." (fitting result)
   - parameter: Use ρ < 2000 kλ.  
  > "Since the amplitude of the visibility is comparable with the noise level in $\rho>2000k\lambda$, we used the visibility in the range of $\rho < 2000k\lambda$ in this modeling." (Fitting approach)
   - parameter: Fitting section: bins 10 kλ for ρ > 1200 kλ and 20 kλ for ρ < 1200 kλ; observations section gives the reverse.  
  > "The bin size is $10k\lambda$ for $\rho>1200k\lambda$, and $20k\lambda$ for $\rho<1200k\lambda$." (Fitting approach); "The bin width are $10k\lambda$ for $\rho<1200k\lambda$ while $20k\lambda$ for $\rho>1200k\lambda$" (Observations and results)
   - parameter: Visibility errors: azimuthal standard deviation divided by square root of bin population.  
  > "is calculated by dividing the standard deviation of azimuthal direction in the visibility domain by the square root of the number of data within the bin." (Fitting approach)
   - parameter: 100 walkers; 1000 burn-in steps followed by 1000 steps.  
  > "In the MCMC fitting, we run 1000 steps with 100 walkers after the burnin phase with 1000 steps (2000 steps in total)." (Fitting approach)
   - parameter: Parameter minima and maxima listed in Table 2; Rcav range 0.0–1.9 au in both bands.  
  > "| $R_{\rm cav}$ (au)      | $1.296^{-0.490}_{+0.348}$   | 0.0       | 1.9       | $0.986^{-0.450}_{+0.353}$   | 0.0       | 1.9       |" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric structure; normalize the profile integrated over the entire disk to Ftot.  
  > "In this modeling, we focus on a symmetric structure, following (cite: Zhang_Blake_Bergin2015)." (Fitting approach); "is integrated over the entire disk is normalized to be $F_{\rm tot}$ which is one of the parameters of the model." (Model description)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC with χ² = sum of squared differences between binned observed and model real visibilities, divided by σobs,k²; prior distributions not stated.  
  > "\chi^2 &= \sum_{k}^{N} \left(\frac{{\rm Re}\left(\overline{V}\right)_{\rm obs,k} - {\rm Re}\left(\overline{V}\right)_{\rm model,k}}{\sigma_{\rm obs,k}} \right)^2," (Fitting approach); "We used the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method in the `emcee` package (cite: emcee)." (Fitting approach)
   - validation: Compare observed and model visibilities; reduced χ² = 1.58 (upper) and 1.28 (lower); inspect posterior plots.  
  > "the reduced $\chi^2$ for the model of the upper band data is $1.58$ and that of the lower band data is $1.28$, respectively." (fitting result); "The posterior of the MCMC fitting for the upper hand data is shown" (Posteriors of MCMC fitting)
5. **Validate models and map residuals**: Sample the model with the observed measurement set, image identically, and subtract model and observations in visibility space before residual imaging.  
  > "The model image is first converted to the ALMA measurement set by `vis_simple` with the observed measurement set" (fitting result); "We calculated the residual as the subtraction between the model and the observation in the visibility domain, by using `vis_sample`." (fitting result); "The visibility of the residual is converted by `tclean` task with the imaging parameters listed in Table" (fitting result)
   - software: vis_simple, vis_sample, tclean
   - parameter: Use the observed-image procedure and Table 1 imaging parameters for mock and residual images.  
  > "we made a mock observational image by the same procedure of the imaging of the observed data with the parameters listed in Table" (fitting result)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare major-axis profiles and residual maps; check agreement of residual patterns between upper and lower bands.  
  > "we show the intensity distributions along the major axis in the mock observational image (model) and the observed image." (fitting result); "The pattern of the residual in the upper and lower band data are similar to each other." (fitting result)
6. **Test asymmetry without an intensity model**: Image only imaginary visibilities; compare with model residuals and vary the phase center.  
  > "the difference between the original and $180^{\circ}$ rotated images has only imaginary part, namely, twice the imaginary part of the original image." (Asymmetric structure); "shows the synthesized image produced only from the imaginary part of visibility data." (Asymmetric structure)
   - parameter: Use C43-4 and C43-8, both upper and lower bands.  
  > "We produced the synthesized image using all the data, namely, the C43-4 and C43-8 data in upper and lower bands." (Asymmetric structure)
   - parameter: Shift the phase center by ±2 mas (±0.38 au) horizontally and vertically; compare RMS within 0.6 arcsec (114 au).  
  > "we shift the center in $\pm 2$ mas ($\pm 0.38$ au) in horizontal and vertical directions" (Asymmetric structure); "We calculated the sum of the root mean square value of the intensity at each pixel within the radius of 0.6 arcsec (114 au) from the center" (Asymmetric structure)
   - assumption: The disk is almost axisymmetric; the correct phase center minimizes imaginary-image intensity RMS.  
  > "It is reasonable to assume that the disk structure is almost axisymmetric, despite the disk has some asymmetric structures." (Asymmetric structure); "Under this assumption, the image deprojected from the imaginary part with the ’correct’ phase center has the minimum root mean square of the intensity." (Asymmetric structure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Central and upper-left features agree with model-subtracted images; fiducial Gaussian center has minimum RMS among tested centers.  
  > "which is consistent with the difference btween the observed image and the axisymmetric model" (Asymmetric structure); "The figure with our fiducial phase center has the minimum value of RMS among the listed panels." (Asymmetric structure)
7. **Construct physical radiative-transfer models**: Use RADMC-3D with axisymmetric dust surface density motivated by the visibility fit, Gaussian vertical density, and iterative temperature-dependent scale height.  
  > "We setup a model of an axisymmetric dust surface density distribution which is motivated by the intensity distribution derived in the previous section" (Setup and model description); "In the vertical direction, we adopt a Gaussian shape distribution" (Setup and model description); "After the first run, we calculate the temperature on the midplane at each radius and we preformed simulation again with the scale height given by the midplane temperature." (Setup and model description)
   - software: RADMC-3D, vis_sample
   - parameter: Fix structural parameters to Table 2 C43-8 values; use surface-density slope s = 0.  
  > "are fixed to the values given in Table" (Setup and model description); "For the parameter $s$ that is related to the slope of the dust surface density, we use $s=0$" (Setup and model description)
   - parameter: Radial grid: 0.1–1000 au, 256 logarithmic cells; angular grids: 256 each over 0 < θ < 2π and 0 < φ < π.  
  > "The radial coordinate extends from $0.1$ au to $1000$ au, which is divided into 256 meshes with logarithmic spacing." (Setup and model description); "The azimuthal angle $\theta$ and polar angle $\phi$ are divided into 256 meshes in $0<\theta < 2\pi$ and in $0<\phi<\pi$" (Setup and model description)
   - parameter: 3 × 10^8 photons for thermal Monte Carlo and imaging; 10^7 photons for SED.  
  > "We adopted $3\times 10^{8}$ photons for thermal Monte Carlo radiative transfer and imaging, and for the SED, $10^{7}$ photons are adopted." (Setup and model description)
   - assumption: Star: 1.2 M⊙, 4350 K; test L* = 11 and 22 L⊙.  
  > "The mass and the surface temperature of the central star are set to $1.2M_{\odot}$ and 4350 K, respectively (cite: Luhman2007)." (Setup and model description); "By the SED modeling, the stellar luminosity is estimated by $11L_{\odot}$ (cite: Garufi2020)." (Setup and model description); "Hence, we also consider the case with $L_{\ast}=22L_{\odot}$." (Setup and model description)
   - assumption: Small grains: size distribution ∝s^-3.5, 0.1 μm–0.1 mm.  
  > "one is a small dust component with size distribution $\propto s^{-3.5}$, where $s$ is the size of the grains, and the minimum and maximum sizes are $0.1 \ \mu \mbox{m}$ and $0.1 \ \mbox{mm}$, respectively." (Setup and model description)
   - assumption: Large grains: Gaussian in logarithmic size; peak 1 mm, minimum 0.6 mm, maximum 1.7 mm.  
  > "The other is a large dust component with its size having Gaussian distribution in logarithmic space." (Setup and model description); "The peak of the size distribution $s_{\rm d,large}$ is $1$ mm with the smallest size $0.6$ mm and with the largest size of $1.7$ mm." (Setup and model description)
   - assumption: Large-grain scale height = 0.1 gas scale height; small:large grain mass ratio = 1:9.  
  > "We assume that the scale height of the large grains is 0.1 times the scale height of the gas." (Setup and model description); "The mass ratio between the small and large grains is set to be 1:9." (Setup and model description)
   - assumption: DSHARP dust compositions; average absorption and scattering coefficients over each component's size distribution.

## Qualitative findings

- A faint double-peaked bump is present without an interior gap.  
  > "The dust continuum image shows a smooth disk structure with a faint (low-contrast) dust ring, extending from $\sim 40$ au to $\sim 70$ au, not accompanied by any gap." (Abstract); "the bump (we call the ring without the gap the bump) has two peaks at 40 au and 70 au." (Abstract)
- The continuum image excludes a large cavity, but the small fitted cavity remains unconfirmed.  
  > "The dust continuum image clearly shows no large cavity, and a faint dust bump" (Conclusion); "It indicates that our fitting cannot rule out the solution with no cavity." (Inner cavity and binary)
- Central and outer asymmetric structures appear in both model residuals and imaginary-visibility imaging.  
  > "The residual map between the model and observation indicates asymmetric structures at the center and the outer region of the disk." (Abstract); "These asymmetric structures are also confirmed by model-independent analysis of the imaginary part of the visibility." (Abstract)
- The outer bright and dark millimeter features coincide with bright and faint parts of the SPHERE spiral.  
  > "The location of the positive asymmetric feature in sub-mm observations coincides with the bright NIR spiral while that of the negative asymmetric feature coincides with the location of faint spiral region of the NIR observation." (Asymmetric structure)
- Snowlines and sintering are possible explanations for the double-peaked bump.  
  > "Hence, this bump with double peaks may be formed by the snowline and the sintering effect." (Ring)
- The grain-size interpretation suggests larger grains in the outer disk than near the center.  
  > "This feature implies that the size of the large dust is larger in the outer disk." (Dust size distribution)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combine compact and sparse configurations to recover larger-scale flux; retain only C43-8 for fitting because compact data scatter raises χ² and lacks sufficient resolution alone.  
  > "We combined the data taken by sparse (C43-8) and compact (C43-4) array configurations to recover the missing flux at larger angular scales." (Observations and results); "Because of this difference between the C43-4 and C43-8 data, the reduced $\chi^2$ of the best fit model is much deviated from unity, when the data are combined." (fitting result); "If only C43-4 data is used, the resolution is not enough to identify substructures." (fitting result)
- Why this method: Use two-term mtmfs for differing SPW fluxes; use imaginary visibilities to check asymmetry independently of the fitted disk model.  
  > "the total flux density of the data in $233.0$ GHz ($\sim 500$ mJy) is significantly larger than that in $216.7$ GHz ($\sim 430$ mJy)." (Observations and results); "Hence, the dust continuum image of combined data was synthesized by CASA with the `tclean` task using the `mtmfs` algorithm (cite: rau11) with `nterms`=2." (Observations and results); "This approach of investigating asymmetric structures is totally model-independent." (Asymmetric structure)
- Alternative: Combined-configuration MCMC fitting gives reduced χ² ∼6, mainly from data around 200 kλ; compact-only data cannot resolve the substructures.  
  > "When both the C43-4 and C43-8 data are used, the reduced $\chi^2$ is $\sim 6$" (fitting result); "This large reduced $\chi^2$ is mainly due to points around $\rho = 200k\lambda$." (fitting result); "If only C43-4 data is used, the resolution is not enough to identify substructures." (fitting result)
- Alternative: A pressure bump or planetary-gap dust trap is disfavored by the double peak and absence of an interior gap.  
  > "However, the bump that is formed by the above mechanisms likely has a single peak, while the bump of our model has double peaks." (Ring); "However, we did not detect any gap structure interior to the bump structure." (Ring); "Hence, it might not be the structure induced by the dust trap of the pressure bump and planetary gap." (Ring)
- Alternative: L* = 11 L⊙ needs an envelope contribution to infrared flux; L* = 22 L⊙ reproduces infrared-to-radio fluxes without that contribution.  
  > "In the case with $L_{\ast}=11L_{\odot}$, the flux at far infrared wavelengths is about a factor of two smaller than the observed values." (Intensity and spectral energy distribution); "In this case, we need some contribution from the envelope to account for infrared flux." (Intensity and spectral energy distribution); "the case with $L_{\ast}=22L_{\odot}$ can reproduce the fluxes from the infrared to the radio, by only the emission from the star." - Alternative: Power-law large-grain distributions do not reproduce the within-band central index below 2; a log-normal distribution may be preferred there.  
  > "With the power-law distribution, the spectral index does not become below 2, even if the maximum size of the dust grains is 0.5 mm." (Spectral index); "Hence, the log-normal size distribution of large grains may be preferred for the inner region." (Spectral index)
- Alternative: Vortex dust trapping may struggle to produce such a faint asymmetry.  
  > "Such a faint structure might be difficult to be formed by the dust trap into the vortex." (Asymmetric structure)
- Alternative: Optical thickness could hide spirals; envelope accretion or a jet is also considered as an asymmetry source.  
  > "Alternatively, we cannot see significant spiral pattern because the disk is optically thick."
