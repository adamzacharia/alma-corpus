## Science

- Fields: astrochemistry, star_formation
- Question: What is the relationship between gas-phase CH3OD column density and local line-of-sight temperature across Orion KL at sub-arcsecond resolution, and what does it reveal about grain-surface versus gas-phase D-H exchange chemistry?  
  > "By considering how column densities vary with temperature, we find evidence of chemical processes that can significantly alter the observed column densities." (Abstract); "This allows us to plot gas-phase as a function of the local line-of-site temperature across relatively small scales within the nebula and explore temperature-dependent chemical processes that may affect the observed chemistry." (Introduction)

## Qualitative findings

- CH3OD column density increases with rotational temperature following an underlying power-law, with a superimposed 'shark-tooth' enhancement that rises steeply at ~110 K, peaks near 185 K, and drops sharply above 185 K.  
  > "However, the profile also contains a “shark-tooth” feature where the column density starts to rise more steeply at $\sim$110 K before peaking close to 185 K. At temperatures higher than 185 K, there is a sharp decrease in the column density, and the relationship between column density and rotational temperature returns to the underlying trend." (Column Density)
- The rapid rise in gas-phase CH3OD column density between ~100 and 125 K supports rapid D-H exchange between methanol and heavy water (HDO, D2O) at the hydroxyl site on icy grain mantles followed by thermal desorption.  
  > "The astronomical data are compared with existing theoretical work and support D-H exchange between and heavy water (i.e., HDO and ) at methanol’s hydroxyl site in the icy mantles of dust grains." (Abstract); "These analyses suggest that D-H exchange and rapid desorption increase the gas-phase column density between 100 and 125 K." (Conclusion)
- Enhanced CH3OD column densities are localized to the Hot Core-SW region, and the sharp drop above ~180 K is due to environmental factors (a lack of gas warmer than ~180-200 K in the Hot Core-SW) rather than gas-phase chemical destruction.  
  > "Above $\sim$180 K, the profile in Figure [\[fig:nvt\]](#fig:nvt)a drops sharply. We attribute this to the enhanced deuteration being localized to the Hot Core-SW and that there simply is little gas above 180 K in this region." (Enhanced Deuteration in the Hot Core-SW); "Instead, we propose that the drop in enhanced column density is the result of environment rather than chemistry." (Enhanced Deuteration in the Hot Core-SW)
- CH2DCN exhibits a smooth power-law relationship between column density and rotational temperature without a hump, supporting the conclusion that methyl groups do not undergo D-H exchange.  
  > "In this plot, we see a consistent power-law relationship between temperature and column density, which supports the conclusion by (cite: Osamura2004) that the methyl groups of complex organics would not undergo grain-surface or gas-phase D-H exchange." (Methyl Group Chemistry)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Orion KL is the nearest high-mass star-forming region (~388 pc), allowing sub-arcsecond (~0.7", ~270 au) ALMA observations to resolve small-scale sub-environments; observing all lines in a single LO setup ensures relative flux uncertainties are dominated by thermal noise rather than calibration uncertainty.  
  > "At a distance of $\sim$388 pc,(cite: Kounkel2017) Orion KL is uniquely situated to explore the relationship between relative deuterated methanol abundances and environmental conditions because sub-environments within the nebula can be resolved, even with modest imaging capabilities." (Introduction); "All spectra were obtained in a single local oscillator set-up consisting of 10 spectral windows; as such, the uncertainties for quantities derived from these spectra are dominated by thermal noise and mostly unaffected by calibration uncertainty." (Observations)
- Why this method: Splitting measurement sets to baselines <=500 m matches the imaging of Wilkins (2022) and recovers flux representative of single-dish observations while resolving small-scale variations; assuming the rotational temperature profile of 13CH3OH is valid because the transitions of both isotopologues have similar upper state energies E_u.  
  > "The data introduced here were prepared in the same way as the images presented by (cite: Wilkins2022) In brief, the data cubes were created from measurement sets split to include only baselines of $\le$500 m, resulting in a synthesized beam of $0.\!\!^{\prime\prime}74\times0.\!\!^{\prime\prime}63$." (Observations); "The rotational temperature ($T_{\rm rot}$) profile was assumed to be the same as that previously derived(cite: Wilkins2022) from since the transitions of both isotopologues have similar upper energy states $E_u$." (Deriving Parameters)
- Alternative: Longer heating timescales (t_h >= 10^4 yr) in the thermal desorption model were rejected because they do not follow the increasing column density profile as closely as t_h = 10^3 yr.  
  > "The resulting desorption model from eq [\[eqn:desrate\]](#eqn:desrate) with $t_h=10^3$ yr is shown by the black dashed line in Figure [\[fig:nvt\]](#fig:nvt)a. Longer timescales (i.e., $t_h\ge10^4$ yr) characteristic of massive YSOs do not follow the increasing profile as closely." (Grain-Surface Processes)
- Alternative: Gas-phase D-H exchange via protonation of the hydroxyl group followed by dissociative recombination was rejected as an explanation for the CH3OD drop above 180 K because dissociative recombination destroys methanol (only 3% branching ratio forms methanol) and no corresponding drop is seen in 13CH3OH.  
  > "However, this is unlikely to account for the decrease in gas-phase at warmer temperatures in the Orion KL Hot Core-SW following enrichment on grain surfaces because dissociative recombination reactions tend to destroy gas-phase methanol (and its isotopologues), with methanol production comprising the smallest branching ratio (3%) listed in KIDA(cite: KIDA)." (Enhanced Deuteration in the Hot Core-SW); "A drop in the column density at temperatures above 180 K is not seen in Orion KL (see Figure S5 in the Supporting Information), suggesting that the observed decrease of the \[\]/\[\] ratio (Figure [\[fig:DvsHbyTemp\]](#fig:DvsHbyTemp)b) and of the column density (Figure [\[fig:nvt\]](#fig:nvt)a) above 180 K cannot simply be explained by protonation of the methanol (e.g., by ) followed by dissociative recombination." (Enhanced Deuteration in the Hot Core-SW)
- Alternative: Neutral-radical reaction with the hydroxyl radical (OH) was rejected as an explanation for CH3OD depletion above 180 K because the reaction is too slow at ~200 K.  
  > "Another mechanism considered was the neutral-radical reaction with the hydroxyl radical (OH); however, this reaction is too slow at $\sim$200 K to account for the observed patterns (see Section S4 of the Supporting Information)." (Enhanced Deuteration in the Hot Core-SW)
