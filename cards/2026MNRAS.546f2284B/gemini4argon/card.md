## Qualitative findings

- No significant (>3 sigma) dust continuum emission is detected in any of the individual z > 8 galaxies (excluding MACS0416-Y1) or in any of the 21 stacked maps across flux, dust mass, dust-to-stellar mass ratio, or sub-bins.  
  > "None of the stacks have led to a $> 3 \sigma$ detection, although the $\beta_{\rm UV} > -2$ (i.e., dustier UV-continuum slope) indicate a $2 \sigma$ feature in the flux and dust-to-stellar mass ratio stack." (Dust mass limits at $z > 8$)
- Dust build-up in the z > 8 Universe is inefficient compared to z < 8, requiring a rapid transition in dust formation efficiency or timescale between z > 8 and z < 8, or efficient dust removal via galactic outflows.  
  > "the low estimated dust-to-stellar mass ratios of $\log_{10} M_{\rm dust} / M_{\ast} < -3.5$ to $-3$ support inefficient dust production in the early Universe, and are in line with a rapid transition in dust production efficiencies or timescales between $z > 8$ and $z < 8$." (Conclusions)
- UV-derived optical dust mass estimates exceed the sub-mm dust mass upper limits for all stacks and several individual galaxies, showing that simple optical measures of dust mass are unreliable at z > 8.  
  > "Several individually-observed sources and, more importantly all of the stacks find higher optical dust masses than the sub-mm dust mass limits." (Dust mass limits at $z > 8$); "that the optical measures of dust masses are unreliable, both for individual sources and for the stacks, in line with previous studies of detected $z < 8$ galaxies (cite: Sommovigo2022)." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Limited to galaxies with robust spectroscopic confirmations at z > 8 and deep (> 1 hr) interferometric observations to prevent contamination from low-redshift interlopers in the stacks.  
  > "To avoid the confusing effect of interlopers in this stacking experiment, this study is limited to all objects that are robustly confirmed to lie at $z > 8$." (Sample and Observations)
- Why this method: Image-plane stacking with LINESTACKER allows combining heterogeneous observations from different cycles and facilities (ALMA and NOEMA) with custom physical weightings; flux-weighted dust masses are reported conservatively to avoid domination by single strongly-lensed objects.  
  > "Given the large variation in observations from different observation cycles and necessary weighting schemes, the stacking is performed in the image plane (cite: Lindroos2015). This furthermore facilitates the combination of NOEMA and ALMA data, as well as making the tool readily available for the public." (Stacking analysis); "The flux-based dust mass estimate is about a factor five times more conservative than the dust-stacked mass estimate, but better samples our full galaxy population, avoiding over-weighting of individual lensed systems while still benefiting from the improved sensitivity of stacking (see e.g., Fig 3)." (Choice of stacking method for reporting results)
- Alternative: Smoothing all images to a common beam larger than the largest individual beam before stacking (rejected because it increased per-beam noise by > 3x in high-resolution maps).  
  > "A smoothing to a common beam larger than the largest individual beam was attempted, but resulted in strong ($\sigma_{\rm smoothed} > 3 \times{} \sigma_{\rm unsmoothed}$) increases in the per-beam noise of the sources with the highest-resolution observations." (Stacking analysis)
- Alternative: Including SPT-0615-JD, COS-z-0/COS-z12-1, and unconfirmed z > 8 candidates in the stack (rejected due to extended emission beyond the beam, foreground galaxy confusion, and risk of low-redshift interlopers, respectively).  
  > "Since the optical emission of this source is extended well beyond the ALMA beam, even in heavily tapered ALMA imaging, we do not include this source in our stack" (SPT-0615-JD); "However, a nearby ($\sim 1$ arcsec) dust-emitting galaxy complicates the study of its direct dust emission, even after attempted subtraction of the foreground galaxy. Consequently, we exclude this source from this stacking study." (COS-z-0/COS-z12-1); "Several tentative high-redshift ($z > 8$) sources have enjoyed deep ALMA follow-up, but are suspect of being low-redshift interlopers. For completeness, we list the existing data below, but do not include them in our stacking." (High-redshift unconfirmed targets)
- How they decided it was enough: Restricted sub-sample binning to three physically motivated parameters (redshift, stellar mass, and beta_UV) to avoid false positives from p-hacking.  
  > "In total, we chose criteria to separate the population of galaxies, namely redshift, stellar mass and UV spectral slope ($\beta_{\rm UV}$). Although more binning options are imaginable, these result in a higher chance of false positives through *p-hacking*, and the scientific motivation behind these three bins appears most robust." (Binning by redshift, $\beta$ and stellar mass)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass limits assume a fixed dust temperature (T_dust = 50 K at z = 0) and emissivity index (beta_dust = 2); varying T_dust by +/- 10 K changes dust masses by a factor of ~2, varying beta_dust by +/- 0.5 changes them by ~25%, and internal temperature variations could cause up to 0.25 dex underestimation.  
  > "If the assumed dust temperature is changed by $\pm 10$ K, the predicted dust masses across the entire sample changes roughly by a factor of two, with the colder dust temperature predicting higher dust masses and vice versa. If the assumed dust emissivity index $\beta_{\rm dust}$ is changed by $\pm 0.5$, the predicted dust masses across the entire sample changes by roughly 25 per cent" (Dust mass limits at $z > 8$); "Recent analytical modeling (cite: Sommovigo2025DifferentTdistributions) predicts under-estimation of the dust mass up to 0.25 dex at $T_{z = 0} = 50$ K for realistic internal dust temperature variations $\sigma_{T} / \bar{T} < 0.3$" (Dust mass limits at $z > 8$)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar masses are taken from literature using diverse spectral fitting approaches, introducing systematic uncertainties into the dust-to-stellar mass ratios.  
  > "Here, we note the important caveat that the diversity in spectral fitting approaches can result in systematically different stellar masses across the sample." (Stacking to a single dust-to-stellar mass ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Gravitational lensing is assumed to affect dust and stellar emission equally, ignoring potential differential lensing.  
  > "The effect of gravitational lensing is presumed to affect the stellar and dust components equally, ignoring any effects of differential lensing (cite: serjeant2012), and is thus effectively negated in the per-pixel measure of the dust-to-stellar mass ratio." (Stacking to a single dust-to-stellar mass ratio)
- Limitation (authors): UV-preselection of the sample biases it toward UV-bright galaxies and may miss heavily dust-obscured (optically-dark) systems at z > 8.  
  > "As seen in Figure 1, many of the galaxies in this survey are the UV-brightest objects. This UV-preselection can result in missing the most dust-obscured systems at a given redshift, as exemplified by so-called optically-dark galaxies seen out to $z > 7$ (cite: Fudamoto2021OpticallyDark)." (Dust mass limits at $z > 8$)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stacking unsmoothed maps with beam sizes varying from 0.37 to 1.35 arcsec assumes all sources are strictly point-like; any extended dust component (such as dusty outflows discussed in the text) would be partially resolved out in the smaller-beam observations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Excluding the only sub-mm detected z > 8 galaxy (MACS0416-Y1) from the stacks, while useful to probe the undetected population, biases the sample average downward.
