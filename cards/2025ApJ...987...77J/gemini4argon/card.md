## Science

- Fields: star_formation, milky_way_ism
- Question: Why is the star formation rate linearly proportional to the dense molecular gas mass (the Gao-Solomon relation), and can this proportionality be explained by deterministic relationships between the gravitationally bound gas mass, the mass of the most massive core, the mass of the most massive star, and the star formation rate?  
  > "One of the most profound empirical laws of star formation is the Gao-Solomon relation, a linear correlation between the star formation rate (SFR) and the dense molecular gas mass. It is puzzling how the complicated physics in star-formation results in this surprisingly simple proportionality." (abstract); "In this work, we attempt to explain the Gao-Solomon relation with an alternative picture. Our main hypothesis is that the mass of the most massive core (M_core^max) in a molecular cloud is determined by the mass of the parent gravitational bound gas reservoir (M_gas^bound)." (1. INTRODUCTION)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive molecular hydrogen column density (N_H2) maps for nearby and distant clouds from Herschel data**: Convolve Herschel 5-band images (70, 160, 250, 350, 500 um) to a common 36.9'' resolution, regrid to 4.0'' pixel size, and perform pixelwise single-component modified black-body SED fitting.  
  > "We perform pixelwise single-component modified black-body spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting of the input Herschel images at five bands." (A. COLUMN DENSITY MAP); "convolved to a common angular resolution of the largest telescope beam" (Appendix A. Column density map)
   - parameter: Common angular resolution = 36.9'', pixel size = 4.0''  
  > "convolved to a common angular resolution of the largest telescope beam" (Appendix A. Column density map)
   - assumption: Dust opacity kappa_nu = 1.0(nu/230 GHz)^beta cm^2 g^-1, dust emissivity index beta = 1.8, gas-to-dust mass ratio R = 100, mean molecular weight mu = 2.8, scattering opacity ignored.

## Qualitative findings

- The mass of the most massive core (M_core^max) is significantly and positively correlated with the mass of the parental gravitationally bound gas (M_gas^bound) across three orders of magnitude (10^2 to 10^5 M_sun) with a power-law slope of ~0.5.  
  > "As shown in Figure 2, the mass of the most massive core M_core^max is positively correlated with the mass of the parental gravitationally bound gas M_gas^bound across three magnitudes of M_gas^bound, from 10^2 to 10^5 M_⊙." (3.3. Mass correlation between most massive core and gravitationally bound gas)
- The linear Gao-Solomon relation between SFR and dense gas mass can be explained as the combination of three deterministic relations: SFR vs. m_star^max (slope 2.04), m_star^max vs. M_core^max (30% efficiency), and M_core^max vs. M_gas^bound (slope 0.506), yielding SFR proportional to (M_gas^bound)^1.03.  
  > "we hypothesize that the Gao-Solomon relation is a consequence of combining the following three non-trivial relations" (abstract); "shows a linear regression of all the data points, yielding a slope of 1.03 across all clouds." (4.1. Connection to the star formation relation)
- Core formation in nearby molecular clouds is inconsistent with random (stochastic) sampling of a universal Core Mass Function (CMF), because random sampling produces both a much larger scatter and systematically higher M_core^max values than observed.  
  > "inconsistent with randomly sampling the CMF in two ways: (1) the observed" (4.2. Indication of a non-stochastic sampling of core mass)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combined Herschel HGBS and Hi-GAL data with ALMA-IMF 1.3 mm data to cover both nearby low-mass clouds and distant massive protoclusters across three orders of magnitude in bound gas mass (10^2 to 10^5 M_sun), using ALMA-IMF to resolve dense cores at 2–5.5 kpc.  
  > "and Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observations on a sample of nearby and distant star-forming molecular clouds." (1. Introduction); "to be located at distances between 2 and 5.5 kpc for both resolving core-like structures and covering the entire extent of the parsec-size clouds with ALMA mosaics" (2.2. ALMA-IMF observation dataset)
- Why this method: Smoothed all maps to a common 0.03 pc spatial resolution and used the same SExtractor procedure for consistent core extraction across nearby and distant clouds; used MLE + MCMC on unbinned N-PDFs to avoid binning pitfalls; substituted M_gas^bound for M_gas^dense to avoid HCN excitation ambiguities.  
  > "We emphasize that in this study, all the core-like structures in nearby and distant molecular clouds were extracted using the same procedure and from maps with the same spatial resolution of 0.03 pc for consistency." (3.1. The most massive core); "This approach avoids the need to pre-bin the observed column density distributions, thereby circumventing the potential pitfalls associated with binning (Virkar & Clauset 2012)." (3.2. Parental gravitationally bound gas); "Here, we are pushing forward to use M_gas^bound to substitute the concept of M_gas^dense referred in the Gao-Solomon relation (Gao & Solomon 2004)." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Alternative: Stochastic picture (random sampling of a universal IMF/CMF explained via the central limit theorem) — rejected on sub-galactic/cloud scales because observed M_core^max-M_gas^bound and m_star^max-m_star^cluster relations are too tight and systematically offset compared to random sampling.  
  > "For example, one might consider that each unit of M_gas^dense in the molecular clouds provides an equal chance to randomly sample a universal stellar initial mass function (IMF) through an identical process (hereafter referred to as the stochastic picture)." (1. INTRODUCTION); "In other words, the implementation of randomly sampling the CMF yielded a too large scattering of M_core^max to be consistent with the observed values." (4.2. Indication of a non-stochastic sampling of core mass)
- Alternative: Alternative dust temperature estimation methods for massive cores (Method One: 70 um internal luminosity scaling; Method Three: uniform 100 K for all cores) — tested and rejected in favor of Method Two (100 K, with 300 K for W51-E, W51-IRS2, and G327.29) because Methods One and Three gave unrealistically large M_core^max (> 10^3 M_sun) for a few sources and larger scatter.  
  > "We adopted the method two as the fiducial one, since the other two methods led to unrealistically large M_core^max (e.g., >10^3 M_⊙) for a few sources (Table 3)." (3.1. The most massive core); "Method two is adopted in Figure 2 for its least data scatter." (C. TEMPERATURE ESTIMATION OF CORES WITHIN MASSIVE CLOUDS)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Hydrogen radio recombination lines (H41alpha) used to subtract free-free emission in ALMA-IMF 1.3 mm images may have maser effects in some regions.  
  > "A caveat is that the hydrogen radio recombination lines may have maser effect in some regions" (2.2. ALMA-IMF observation dataset)
- Limitation (authors): Four ALMA-IMF fields (G327.29, G328.25, G351.77, and G353.41) have insufficient (< 5%) short baselines to recover scales larger than 0.1 pc, causing missing flux and potential underestimation of M_core^max.  
  > "As mentioned by Ginsburg et al. (2022), there are four fields, G327.29, G328.25, G351.77, and G353.41 with insufficient (< 5%) short baselines to recover scales larger than 0.1 pc." (3.3. Mass correlation between most massive core and gravitationally bound gas)
- Limitation (authors): The spatial resolution used (0.03 pc) is coarser than the native resolutions of ALMA-IMF and HGBS, so extracted 'cores' (radii 0.04–0.23 pc, median 0.1 pc) are larger than cores defined at <= 0.01 pc in recent high-resolution ALMA studies.  
  > "We note that the spatial resolutions we are using are coarser than the native resolutions in the ALMA-IMF and HGBS data." (3.1. The most massive core)
- Limitation (authors): Why the theoretical galactic-scale SFR-m_star^max relation from Yan et al. (2017) is applicable on the spatial scale of individual molecular clouds is not yet understood and could be a coincidence.

- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core masses for nearby clouds are derived by summing Herschel N_H2 maps (fitted from 70–500 um SEDs), whereas distant cloud core masses are derived from single-band 1.3 mm ALMA continuum flux assuming fixed temperatures (100 K or 300 K); systematic differences between these two mass-derivation methods could artificially steepen or flatten the combined M_core^max-M_gas^bound slope.
