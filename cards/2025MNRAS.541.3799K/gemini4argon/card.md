## Analysis recipe

1. **Construct diagnostic PP and PV maps and visually identify dust lanes and EVFs**: Rotate each CO(2-1) datacube so the positive half of the line of nodes aligns with the negative x-axis, convert angular scales to physical kpc, integrate over velocity and spatial dimensions to create 1 PP and 2 PV maps, visually enclose bar dust lanes with contours in glue, and visually select compact, broad-lined EVFs in the PV maps.  
  > "cube by integrating the cube over velocities and over two spatial dimensions, respectively. Before the integration, the cube is rotated so that the positive half of the line of nodes is along the negative x-axis." (3.1 Diagnostic maps and EVF identification); "Before the integration, the cube is rotated so that the positive half of the line of nodes is along the negative x-axis." (3.1 Diagnostic maps and EVF identification); "We then visually select the EVFs within the extracted dust lanes by looking in the PV maps for features that are abnormally broad-lined compared to the rest of the material on the dust lane, and that have physical sizes in the PP maps smaller or comparable to the width of the dust lane." (3.1 Diagnostic maps and EVF identification)
   - software: glue, ASTROPY
   - parameter: 29 barred galaxies (Class B and C in Stuber et al. 2023) and 13 unbarred test galaxies (Class A and SA)  
  > "in this work we use a sample of 29 galaxies from the PHANGS-ALMA dataset, listed in Table 1, all classified as barred, based on their CO(2–1) morphology, by Stuber et al. (2023) (class B and C there)." (2.1 PHANGS-ALMA CO(2–1) Survey); "To check whether unbarred galaxies can also contain EVFs, we examined all galaxies that are classified as lacking bar-like features in the molecular gas morphology (Class A) in Stuber et al. (2023) which are also classified as unbarred (‘SA’) in photometric studies (de Vaucouleurs et al. 1991; Buta et al. 2015)." (4 RESULTS)
   - assumption: Distances from Anand et al. (2021) and PAs of LON and inclinations from Lang et al. (2020) or Salo et al. (2015)  
  > "In this work, PAs of the LONs for majority of our sample galaxies are taken from Lang et al. (2020) when available, otherwise taken from Salo et al. (2015) (see Table 1). Then we convert angular (arcseconds) into physical (kpc) scales assuming the distances from Anand et al. (2021)" (3.1 Diagnostic maps and EVF identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked a control sample of 13 unbarred galaxies for EVF signatures  
  > "To check whether unbarred galaxies can also contain EVFs, we examined all galaxies that are classified as lacking bar-like features in the molecular gas morphology (Class A) in Stuber et al. (2023) which are also classified as unbarred (‘SA’) in photometric studies (de Vaucouleurs et al. 1991; Buta et al. 2015)." (4 RESULTS)
2. **Estimate EVF physical radii and galactocentric distances**: Visually define a circular region on the dust lane in PP space encompassing the EVF, centre on the brightest point, fit a 2D Gaussian to the PP map to get centre coordinates (x, y) and standard deviations (sigma_x, sigma_y), and compute R_EVF and R_cen.  
  > "To estimate the physical size of the EVFs, we first visually define a circular region on the dust lane, in PP space, that encompasses the entire EVF." (3.2 EVF properties estimation); "We centre the extracted region on the brightest point as an initial guess for the EVF centre. We then fit a 2D Gaussian function to the PP map." (3.2 EVF properties estimation); "We then fit a 2D Gaussian function to the PP map. We thus obtain values for the centre coordinates and standard deviations" (3.2 EVF properties estimation)
   - software: ASTROPY
   - parameter: eta = sqrt(2 ln 2) = 1.18 in R_EVF = eta * sqrt(sigma_x * sigma_y)  
  > "surface brightness as a two-dimensional Gaussian and using the half width at half maximum as the radius" (3.2 EVF properties estimation)

## Qualitative findings

- EVFs are detected in 10 out of 29 barred galaxies (18 features total), while only 1 out of 13 unbarred test galaxies shows a tentative EVF-like feature along a spiral arm.  
  > "After examining the PP and PV diagrams of the 29 galaxies in our sample, we identified EVFs in 10 out of 29 galaxies, which represents approximately 34% of the total." (4 RESULTS); "Of these, only one galaxy –NGC 628– exhibited a tentative EVF-like signature in the PV diagram, along one of its spiral arms." (4 RESULTS)
- Extragalactic EVFs have typical masses of 10^5-10^6 M_sun, radii of ~100 pc, and virial parameters ranging from 76 to 2925, showing they are strongly unbound, out-of-equilibrium structures analogous to Milky Way EVFs.  
  > "Typical masses are in the range 105-106 M⊙, while typical sizes are of the order of 100 pc." (4.1 Physical properties of EVFs); "For all of the EVFs we identified, the virial parameter, αvir, is exceptionally large, ranging from a few hundred to several thousand (see Table 2)." (4.1 Physical properties of EVFs)
- In ~12 of the 18 EVF regions, blob-like CO(2-1) emission resolves into smaller clumps in JWST 7.7 um images, and in 9 of the 18 EVFs faint CO-invisible 7.7 um gas streams extend from the opposite side of the bar to the EVF location.  
  > "In the vicinity of approximately 12 of the EVF regions, while CO(2–1) emission appears as round blob-like features, the emission in 7.7 µm is resolved into smaller clumps." (4 RESULTS); "Upon examining the 7.7 µm band observations of all galaxies with EVFs, we find that out of 18 EVF regions, 9 are associated with faint streams extending from the opposite end of the bar toward the EVF location." (4.2 JWST gas streams)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Resolving features comparable to the largest Milky Way EVFs (~100 pc) requires cloud-scale (~50-150 pc) molecular gas spectroscopy from PHANGS-ALMA, while PHANGS-JWST 7.7 um imaging traces lower column density gas streams that fall below the CO sensitivity limit.  
  > "this program requires observations of molecular clouds in nearby galaxies at resolutions on the order of" (1 Introduction); "Crucially for this work, the 7.7 µm emission has a strong correlation to the CO (2-1) emission away from star forming regions, allowing us to detect CO streams that go undetected in the CO-ALMA data because they fall below the sensitivity limit of the latter." (2.2 PHANGS-JWST Treasury Survey)
- Why this method: Selected galaxies classified as barred by CO(2-1) morphology rather than stellar photometry to ensure the presence of molecular gas bar structures, and used double Gaussian fits for Milky Way EVFs to remove foreground line-of-sight contamination.  
  > "Therefore, to ensure that our selected galaxies exhibit a bar-like feature in molecular gas, in this work we use a sample of 29 galaxies from the PHANGS-ALMA dataset, listed in Table 1, all classified as barred, based on their CO(2–1) morphology, by Stuber et al. (2023) (class B and C there)." (2.1 PHANGS-ALMA CO(2–1) Survey); "Since the single Gaussian fitting technique used for external galaxies is inadequate for cases with overlapping features, we simply adopt a double Gaussian fitting approach to accurately model multiple components and account for foreground contamination." (3.3 Milky Way spectra)
- Alternative: Photometric stellar bar classification (rejected for sample selection because stellar bars can be gas-depleted or unbarred in CO, and vice versa)  
  > "potentially indicating gasdepleted bars where further inflow has ceased, or cases where star-forming clumps mimic elongated features in stellar light but lack coherent gas structures in CO." (2.1 PHANGS-ALMA CO(2–1) Survey)
- Alternative: Stacking observations across different JWST MIRI filters to detect fainter streams (not pursued because multi-filter data were not consistently available for all galaxies)  
  > "Stacking observations across different MIRI filters could help produce deeper images and reveal fainter structures. However, since such data were not consistently available for all galaxies, we did not pursue this approach in our analysis." (4.2 JWST gas streams)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Visual identification and contour enclosure of dust lanes and circular EVF regions in glue is somewhat subjective.  
  > "While this procedure is somewhat subjective, the results are reasonable, as the dust lanes are typically coherent and continuous structures in PP space." (3.1 Diagnostic maps and EVF identification)
- Limitation (authors): 2D Gaussian fitting overestimates the physical radius R_EVF (and thus virial parameter) for unresolved EVFs, so R_EVF values are upper limits.  
  > "We note that this procedure will overestimate the radius of unresolved EVFs, so the radii should be considered upper limits." (3.2 EVF properties estimation); "We caveat that if the EVFs are unresolved in the PHANGS-ALMA CO data (which is possible given their ‘blobby’ appearance), then we will overestimate their virial parameter since REVF will be larger than the true cloud size." (4.1 Physical properties of EVFs)
- Limitation (authors): Parameter uncertainties only reflect covariance matrix fitting errors and do not account for non-Gaussian intensity distributions, overlapping contaminating features, or spatial resolution limits.  
  > "These uncertainties reflect the confidence intervals in the fitting only and they do not take into account that the shape of the intensity distribution within EVF region might be non-Gaussian, that there might be contaminating emission from other features overlapping with the EVF in PP space, or that the radius can be overestimated when the EVF is unresolved." (3.2 EVF properties estimation)
- Limitation (authors): Adopting a constant Galactic alpha_CO ignores galaxy-scale variations and likely overestimates M_CO (underestimating alpha_vir) in high-velocity-dispersion central/bar regions.  
  > "We adopt this procedure for simplicity, but note that it ignores galaxy-scale variations (e.g. den Brok et al. 2025) and might overestimate the mass for high-velocity dispersion features such as the EVF since high velocity dispersion often causes lower αCO values near galactic centres (Teng et al. 2023)." (3.2 EVF properties estimation)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial resolution is insufficient to conclusively determine whether EVFs lie on the leading or trailing edge of thin dust lanes.  
  > "Although it is difficult to assess this conclusively as dust lanes are thin and the spatial resolution is not sufficient to examine this in detail, some galaxies appear consistent with this expectation (e.g. NGC 1512, NGC 2903, NGC 3627), but for some other galaxies the EVFs seem to occur on the leading side of the bar lanes (e.g. NGC 1097 and NGC 4535)." (4.2 JWST gas streams)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): EVF selection is entirely visual and lacks an quantitative, automated detection threshold in PPV space (e.g. a minimum line width or brightness contrast relative to the surrounding dust lane).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Because the spatial resolution of PHANGS-ALMA (50-180 pc) is comparable to the derived EVF radii (70-150 pc), beam smearing of steep velocity gradients across the bar shock could artificially broaden the spectra without requiring distinct colliding streams.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The JWST 7.7 um PAH emission lacks kinematic information, so the physical association and inflow direction of the visually traced streams hitting the EVFs cannot be kinematically confirmed.
