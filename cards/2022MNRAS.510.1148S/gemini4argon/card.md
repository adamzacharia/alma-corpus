## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate visibilities, subtract continuum, and image molecular line cubes**: Calibrate and flag raw datasets with standard ALMA scripts in CASA, subtract continuum in uv-space with first-order polynomials using UVCONTSUB, and image spectral cubes with TCLEAN using natural weighting.  
  > "Calibration and flagging of the raw data sets were performed in the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: mcmullin2007) using the standard ALMA reduction scripts." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Prior to line imaging, we subtracted continuum emission from the obtained visibilities using the UVCONTSUB task of CASA. We fitted first-order polynomials to the line-free channels in the $uv$ space." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Using the continuum-subtracted data, we applied the TCLEAN task to construct spectral cubes of line observations." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, UVCONTSUB, TCLEAN
   - parameter: Natural weighting; channel widths rebinned to 0.9 km s^-1 (C2H, HCO+, HCN), 0.75 km s^-1 (CN), and 1.25 km s^-1 (coarse Band 6)  
  > "Natural weighting was adopted. The channel width was rebinned to 0.9 km s$^{-1}$ for C$_{2}$H, HCO$^+$ and HCN lines and to 0.75 km s$^{-1}$ for CN observations, respectively. In the coarse-resolution Band 6 measurements, a channel width of 1.25 km s$^{-1}$ was used." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: First-order polynomials fitted to line-free channels in uv space for continuum subtraction  
  > "We fitted first-order polynomials to the line-free channels in the $uv$ space." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure integrated line fluxes and 3-sigma upper limits from moment-0 maps**: Construct zeroth-moment maps over line velocity ranges, measure fluxes using fitted elliptical apertures, estimate uncertainties from 16 random background apertures, and derive 3-sigma upper limits for non-detections using HCN or IMSMOOTH-smoothed archival 12CO cubes as templates.  
  > "In the detected cases, zeroth moment maps were constructed by integrating over the velocity ranges of the significant line emission." (Results and analysis); "The integrated line fluxes were derived by using elliptical apertures that were fitted to the observed velocity-integrated emission. To estimate flux uncertainties, we randomly distributed 16 identical elliptical apertures in the background region and computed the standard deviation of the fluxes obtained in them." (Results and analysis); "The CO measurements were taken from the ALMA archive and the template data cubes were smoothed using the IMSMOOTH CASA task to obtain the same synthesised beam as that of the given line." (Results and analysis)
   - software: CASA, IMSMOOTH
   - parameter: 16 random background apertures for uncertainty; 3-sigma upper limits  
  > "To estimate flux uncertainties, we randomly distributed 16 identical elliptical apertures in the background region and computed the standard deviation of the fluxes obtained in them." (Results and analysis); "Finally, using the same elliptical aperture as in the corresponding HCN observations, we computed the 3$\sigma$ upper limits." (Results and analysis)
   - assumption: Expected velocity ranges and spatial extents of non-detected lines match those of HCN (for protoplanetary discs) or 12CO (for debris discs and HD 141569)  
  > "To this end, we determined the velocity range of the expected line by using the HCN measurement of the given object as a template." (Results and analysis); "For the calculation of the upper limits, we used the method described above, with the difference that we utilised the $^{12}$CO measurements of the given sources as a template." (Results and analysis)
   - fitting: Elliptical apertures fitted to the observed velocity-integrated emission  
  > "The integrated line fluxes were derived by using elliptical apertures that were fitted to the observed velocity-integrated emission." (Results and analysis)
   - validation: Selected velocity ranges checked for agreement with those inferred from CO measurements of the same discs  
  > "The selected ranges are in good agreement with those inferred from the CO measurements of the same discs." (Results and analysis)
3. **Calculate molecular photodissociation lifetimes under H2 and C shielding**: Compute photodissociation rates k = k0 * theta and lifetimes tau = 1/k as a function of H2 or neutral atomic carbon column density using literature unattenuated rates and shielding functions.  
  > "The photodissociation rate of a given molecule can be estimated as $k = k_0 \theta$, where $k_0$ is the unattenuated photodissociation rate of the molecule, while $\theta$ is the shielding function (cite: heays2017)." (Photodissociation of molecules); "Figure [3](#fig:pdlifetimes) shows the photodissociation lifetimes ($\tau = 1/k$) of the targeted molecules and CO for the various H$_2$ or neutral atomic carbon column densities." (Photodissociation of molecules)
   - assumption: Only the interstellar UV radiation field is taken into account; dust attenuation is negligible in debris discs  
  > "We took into account only the interstellar UV radiation field in our calculations." (Photodissociation of molecules)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Simulate time-dependent chemical evolution and non-LTE line radiative transfer**: Run the single-point ALCHEMIC chemical kinetics code over a 2D temperature-density grid for 1 Myr across five sets of initial abundances (varying H2/He depletion), then compute non-LTE line brightness temperatures and flux ratios with RADEX.  
  > "To reproduce detections of the CO isotopologues and non-detections of HCO$^+$, CN, HCN, C$_{2}$H, and CS in debris discs, we have utilised the ALCHEMIC chemical model (cite: Semenov_ea_2010) and the RADEX line radiative transfer code (cite: radex), and adopted a temperature-density grid." (Chemical and line flux modelling); "Using the above chemical model, we have calculated the abundance evolution over 1 Myr for constant physical conditions." (Chemical and line flux modelling); "We have calculated the line brightness temperatures with the non-LTE RADEX code." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
   - software: ALCHEMIC, RADEX
   - parameter: Temperature grid: 10 to 300 K; density grid: 10^3 to 10^7 cm^-3; evolution time: 1 Myr; radial UV optical depth tau = 0 to 1; cosmic ray ionisation rate: 1.3e-17 s^-1; H2 depletion factors: 1, 2, 5, 10, 100  
  > "We have modelled chemical evolution over a temperature range between 10 and 300 K and densities between $10^3$ and $10^7$ cm$^{-3}$ (or between $\approx 4 \times 10^{-21}$ and $4 \times 10^{-17}$ g cm$^{-3}$)." (Chemical and line flux modelling); "The ionisation rate was set to $1.3 \times 10^{-17}$ s$^{-1}$, corresponding to a galactic cosmic ray ionisation rate." (Chemical and line flux modelling); "In the other four sets, we multiplied the relative abundances of all species other than hydrogen and helium by a factor of 2, 5, 10, and 100, respectively." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
   - parameter: Emitting gas column length = 15 au; line FWHM = 1 km s^-1  
  > "We have calculated the line brightness temperatures assuming the length of the emitting gas column of 15 au and a line full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 1 km s$^{-1}$." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
   - assumption: Dust-to-gas mass ratio set to 10^-10 for 0.1 um grains; unattenuated UV field equal to vertical ISRF; stellar X-rays and high-energy particles neglected; fixed isotopic ratios 12C/13C = 77 and 16O/18O = 560  
  > "Since primordial (sub-)micron-sized dust has already evolved and became severely depleted, we decided to set the corresponding dust-to-gas mass ratio to a value of $10^{-10}$ for $0.1$ $\mu$m-sized grains, effectively turning off dust-surface reactions." (Chemical and line flux modelling); "Since $^{13}$C and $^{18}$O are not included in the chemical network, we have used fixed isotopic ratios C$^{12}$/C$^{13} = 77$ and O$^{16}$/O$^{18} = 560$ to scale down the resulting molecular abundances (cite: wilson_rood_1994_isotope_ratio)." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 1 Myr evolution timespan verified to reach chemical equilibrium within 1-100 kyr for simple molecules  
  > "This timespan was long enough to reach a chemical equilibrium within 1-100 kyr for the observed, chemically simple molecules (depending on density)." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
5. **Calculate the effect of hydrogen depletion on gas molar mass and disc vertical scale height**: Scale elemental abundances of species heavier than H2 and He by factors of 1 to 10^6, reshuffle into CO, H2O, and H2, and compute gas molar mass and hydrostatic vertical scale height for an isothermal 30 K disc.  
  > "To illustrate this effect, we use the elemental abundances from Table [\[tab:initial_abundances\]](#tab:initial_abundances) and scale abundances of all the species other than molecular hydrogen and helium by uniform factors of $1$ (ISM-like) to $10^6$ (hydrogen is almost fully depleted)." (Effect of hydrogen depletion on disc scale height); "The resulting gas molar masses and the corresponding scale heights for an isothermal, 30 K disc are shown in Fig. [1](#fig:mmw)." (Effect of hydrogen depletion on disc scale height)
   - parameter: Heavy element scaling factor: 1 to 10^6; disc temperature: 30 K  
  > "To illustrate this effect, we use the elemental abundances from Table [\[tab:initial_abundances\]](#tab:initial_abundances) and scale abundances of all the species other than molecular hydrogen and helium by uniform factors of $1$ (ISM-like) to $10^6$ (hydrogen is almost fully depleted)." (Effect of hydrogen depletion on disc scale height); "The resulting gas molar masses and the corresponding scale heights for an isothermal, 30 K disc are shown in Fig. [1](#fig:mmw)." (Effect of hydrogen depletion on disc scale height)
   - assumption: Hydrostatic equilibrium; isothermal 30 K geometrically thin disc; all carbon locked in CO, remaining O and H in water (with and without water formation cases), remaining H in H2  
  > "First, all available carbon is assumed to be locked in CO molecules. Then, the remaining oxygen and available hydrogen are assumed to form water molecules. After that, the remaining hydrogen (if any) is assumed to be bound in H$_2$ molecules. ## Software

- **CASA** : Calibration, flagging, continuum subtraction (UVCONTSUB), spectral line imaging (TCLEAN), and beam smoothing of archival CO template cubes (IMSMOOTH).  
  > "Calibration and flagging of the raw data sets were performed in the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: mcmullin2007) using the standard ALMA reduction scripts." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Prior to line imaging, we subtracted continuum emission from the obtained visibilities using the UVCONTSUB task of CASA." (Observations and Data Reduction); "Using the continuum-subtracted data, we applied the TCLEAN task to construct spectral cubes of line observations." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- **UVCONTSUB** : Subtracting continuum emission from visibilities by fitting first-order polynomials to line-free channels in uv space.  
  > "Prior to line imaging, we subtracted continuum emission from the obtained visibilities using the UVCONTSUB task of CASA. We fitted first-order polynomials to the line-free channels in the $uv$ space." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- **TCLEAN** : Constructing spectral cubes of line observations from continuum-subtracted visibilities.  
  > "Using the continuum-subtracted data, we applied the TCLEAN task to construct spectral cubes of line observations." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- **IMSMOOTH** : Smoothing archival 12CO template data cubes to match the synthesised beam of the targeted line observations.  
  > "The CO measurements were taken from the ALMA archive and the template data cubes were smoothed using the IMSMOOTH CASA task to obtain the same synthesised beam as that of the given line." (Results and analysis)
- **ALCHEMIC** : Single-point time-dependent gas-grain chemical kinetics modelling over a temperature-density grid.  
  > "To reproduce detections of the CO isotopologues and non-detections of HCO$^+$, CN, HCN, C$_{2}$H, and CS in debris discs, we have utilised the ALCHEMIC chemical model (cite: Semenov_ea_2010) and the RADEX line radiative transfer code (cite: radex), and adopted a temperature-density grid." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
- **RADEX** : Non-LTE line radiative transfer calculations of molecular line brightness temperatures.  
  > "We have calculated the line brightness temperatures with the non-LTE RADEX code." (Chemical and line flux modelling)

## Qualitative findings

- CN, HCN, HCO+, and CS lines are detected in all four old Herbig Ae protoplanetary discs (HD 100453, HD 139614, HD 142666, HD 145718), and C2H is detected in HD 139614 and HD 142666.  
  > "By inspecting the obtained data cubes, we found significant CN, HCN, and HCO$^+$ emission towards all four targeted HAe protoplanetary discs (HD 100453, HD 139614, HD 142666, and HD 145718), while the C$_{2}$H line was detected only in HD 139614 and HD 142666 (Figs. [1](#fig:peak), [\[fig:spectra\]](#fig:spectra))." (Results and analysis); "The J=$5-4$ transition of the CS molecule (Band 6, 244.9355565 GHz) was also detected in one of the additional coarse-resolution spectral windows in all four discs." (Results and analysis)
- None of the four CO-rich debris discs nor the transitional/hybrid disc HD 141569 exhibit detectable emission in CN, HCN, HCO+, C2H, or CS.  
  > "None of the four CO-rich debris discs, nor the disc around HD 141569 exhibited detectable line emission in any data cubes." (Results and analysis)
- CN, HCN, C2H, and CS have short photodissociation lifetimes in optically thin debris discs regardless of whether shielding is provided by H2 or neutral C, so their non-detections do not rule out the hybrid scenario.  
  > "Without shielding at these long wavelengths, the latter four molecules have only short photodissociation lifetimes both in hybrid and shielded secondary discs, while in protoplanetary environments they could still be protected from UV by the optically thick dust. The observed low line ratios of these four species to CO in the debris disc sample, therefore, do not necessarily preclude the hybrid scenario (Fig. [2](#fig:ratio))." (Photodissociation of molecules)
- HCO+ is expected to be the second brightest line after CO and its intensity strongly depends on the CO/H2 ratio, falling below the current detection limits everywhere in the modelled parameter space once the H2 depletion factor reaches 5 or higher.  
  > "We find that the HCO$^+$ lines should be the second brightest after the CO lines, and that their intensities strongly depend on the overall CO/H$_2$ ratio of the gas." (Abstract); "However, starting from a higher H$_2$ depletion factor of 5, all the predicted HCO$^+$ J=2-1/$^{13}$CO J=2-1 flux ratios fall below the current detection limit." (Chemical and line flux modelling)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected the four remaining CO-rich debris discs with M_CO > 0.01 M_earth and CO isotopologue data, plus four nearby older (8-10 Myr) Herbig Ae discs and transitional disc HD 141569 as a reference sample; targeted lower rotational transitions in Bands 5 and 6 due to low gas temperatures and low collisional partner densities.  
  > "Therefore, the remaining four CO-rich debris discs, HD 21997, HD 121617, HD 131488, and HD 131835 were selected as targets of this study." (Targets and selected molecules); "For comparison with our debris disc sample , therefore we observed the same molecules in four nearby ($<$160 pc), older, less gas-rich primordial discs around Herbig Ae stars, HD100453, HD139614, HD142666, and HD145718." (Targets and selected molecules); "Considering the typical low gas temperatures (cite: kospal2013; hughes2017) and the possible low density of potential collisional partners in CO-bearing debris discs, which can result in low excitation temperatures, we observed the lower transitions for the selected molecules." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Why this method: Used a single-point chemical model on a temperature-density grid because CO emission in optically thin debris discs forms a relatively narrow ring with tight variations in temperature and UV radiation; turned off dust-surface chemistry by setting dust-to-gas ratio to 10^-10 because sub-micron dust is severely depleted.  
  > "Given that the observed CO emission appears as a relatively narrow ring-like structure in optically thin debris discs, we assume a rather tight variation of temperature and UV radiation strength within the gas emitting area." (Chemical and line flux modelling); "Since primordial (sub-)micron-sized dust has already evolved and became severely depleted, we decided to set the corresponding dust-to-gas mass ratio to a value of $10^{-10}$ for $0.1$ $\mu$m-sized grains, effectively turning off dust-surface reactions." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
- Alternative: Direct measurement of hydrogen depletion via vertical temperature and density structure (hydrostatic scale height) using high-resolution multi-J CO ALMA observations.  
  > "An alternative method to estimate hydrogen depletion of a debris disc using ALMA is based on direct measurements of their vertical temperature and density structure (cite: hughes2017; kral2019)." (Effect of hydrogen depletion on disc scale height)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): For HD 21997, ~50% of the channels covering the HCO+ line were flagged during calibration, requiring the upper limit velocity window to be shifted to a non-flagged part of the spectrum.  
  > "In the case of HD 21997, $\sim$50% of the channels covering the HCO$^+$ line were flagged during the calibration process not to be used for scientific analysis. Therefore, this upper limit estimate was obtained by slightly shifting the velocity range to a non-flagged part of the spectrum." (Results and analysis)
- Limitation (authors): High-energy particle flux and X-ray radiation from the main-sequence stars were neglected in the chemical model.  
  > "High-energetic particle flux and X-ray radiation from the evolved main-sequence star were neglected, as these are much milder compared to the pre-main-sequence phase." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
- Limitation (authors): Current spatial resolution and sensitivity cannot tightly constrain the CO/H2 ratio or the contribution of an extended tenuous disc atmosphere without follow-up higher-resolution CO or deeper HCO+ observations and full thermo-chemical modelling.  
  > "Follow-up higher-resolution CO or more sensitive HCO$^+$ observations combined with feasible thermo-chemical modelling are required to provide tighter constraints on the CO/H$_2$ ratios in these discs." (Chemical and line flux modelling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The chemical and radiative transfer modelling relies on a 0D single-point approximation with a fixed 15 au column length and 1 km/s linewidth under a 1D radial ISRF UV attenuation (tau = 0 to 1), neglecting 2D/3D disc geometry, self-consistent gas thermal balance, and stellar UV spectrum variations across A-type stars.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Archival CO isotopologue fluxes used to compute the line ratios in Figure 2 come from heterogeneous observations with potentially different uv-coverage and spatial filtering.
