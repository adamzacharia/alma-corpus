## Analysis recipe

1. **Re-calibrate archival ALMA visibilities, measure 873 um continuum flux, and estimate visibility noise**: Re-calibrate ALMA science archive visibilities using archive scripts in CASA 4.3.1, image with tclean in CASA 6.1.2.7 using natural weighting and primary beam correction to measure 873 um flux, and run CASA statwt to measure 1-sigma visibility scatter across all baselines.  
  > "In this work, we have used the visibility data provided by the ALMA science archive, and re-calibrated them using COMMON ASTRONOMY SOFTWARE APPLICATIONS version (CASA) 4.3.1 (cite: mcmullin) and the scripts provided by the archive." (DATA); "The CASA task tclean was used to create images in order to measure the flux of the sources at 873 $\mu$m." (DATA); "Using a similar method to (cite: dye_2018) to determine the 1$\sigma$ uncertainties on the visibilities, we used the `CASA` task `statwt` to empirically measure the visibility scatter computed over all baselines." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane)
   - software: CASA, tclean, statwt
   - parameter: natural weighting, primary beam corrected, pixel scale 0.02 arcsec (H-ATLAS) and 0.03 arcsec (HeLMS)  
  > "The images were constructed using a natural weighting scheme and were primary beam corrected." (DATA); "To well sample the minor axis of the primary beam, an image pixel scale of 0.02 arcsec and 0.03 arcsec was used for the H-ATLAS and HeLMS sources respectively." (DATA)
   - assumption: Uncorrelated visibility noise represented by diagonal covariance matrix  
  > "Assuming uncorrelated noise $\boldsymbol n$ in the visibility data, the diagonal matrix $\bf{C}^{-1}$ represents the covariance." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Perform uv-plane strong lens modelling and pixelised source reconstruction**: Semi-linear inversion (SLI) directly on visibilities in the uv-plane using PyAutoLens, PyNUFFT, and PyLops, fitting elliptical power-law mass density profiles (1 for galaxy-scale, 2 for group-scale) plus optional external shear, first with magnification-adapted source pixelisation and then brightness-adapted source pixelisation.  
  > "We used the source inversion method implemented within `PyAutoLens` (cite: Nightingale2021) which is based on the operator approach described within (cite: powell)." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane); "We first reconstructed the background source using a source plane pixelisation adapted to the lens magnification. This magnification-based fit was then used to initialise a new search of parameter space with a source plane that adapted to the brightness of the reconstructed source (cite: Nightingale2015; Nightingale2018)." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane); "We have used the elliptical power-law density profile, which is a generalised form of the singular isothermal ellipsoid that is commonly used to fit strong lens profiles (cite: keeton2001catalog)." (Lens Model)
   - software: PyAutoLens, PyNUFFT, PyLops, DYNESTY
   - parameter: Constant-weight gradient regularisation; 6 to 14 lens mass model parameters plus regularisation and number of source pixels  
  > "We adopted a gradient regularisation scheme, analogous to that which is described in (cite: warren_dye), with a constant weight for the source plane due to it’s simplicty and to not add more computational costs to an already expensive procedure." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane); "The mass model parameters, along with the source plane parameters (regularisation, number of source plane pixels), were fully optimised throughout the lens modelling procedure." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane); "For single lens systems there are six lens model parameters (eight when including external shear) and 12 lens model parameters (14 when including external shear) in the case of group-scale lenses." (Lens Model)
   - assumption: Elliptical power-law surface mass density profile (1 or 2 components) with optional external shear  
  > "When it improves the Bayesian evidence, an external shear component is included to compensate for the influence of line of sight galaxies that may be outside our field of view." (Lens Model); "Where necessary, two elliptical power-law profiles have been used to model the group-scale lenses present in the sample." (Lens Model)
   - fitting: Nested sampling with DYNESTY to maximise Bayesian evidence  
  > "To find the optimal lens model parameters with PYAUTOLENS, we used the nested sampling algorithm DYNESTY (cite: dynesty) to maximise the bayesian evidence as derived within (cite: suyu)." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane)
   - validation: Bayesian evidence comparison for adding external shear, optical/near-IR imaging inspection for number of lens galaxies, and residual image inspection  
  > "We find that in all cases this is sufficient to provide acceptable fits to the data and through the use of optical/near-IR imaging, we see no indication of more than two lenses being required to produce an accurate model." (Lens Model); "Fig [1](#fig:model_plots) shows the model image, residual image and source reconstruction for each of the seven lenses modelled in this work." (Results)
3. **Compute source magnification profiles and total magnification factors**: Rank source plane pixels above a surface brightness threshold by flux density and compute the ratio of lensed image-plane flux (within the image-plane mask) to source-plane flux, repeating 100 times with randomised source plane pixelisation to average.  
  > "Working down a list of source plane pixels, above a surface brightness threshold, ranked by flux density (i.e the product of their reconstructed surface brightness and area), the reconstructed image and average magnification was computed." (Results); "This process was repeated 100 times using a randomised source plane pixelisation for each, to produce an averaged magnification profile." (Results); "The total magnification is computed by calculating the flux in the source plane (for pixels above a surface brightness threshold) and the flux in the image plane due to the lensed image of the source plane pixels." (Results)
   - software: PyAutoLens
   - parameter: 100 realisations of randomised source plane pixelisation; surface brightness threshold; image plane mask  
  > "This process was repeated 100 times using a randomised source plane pixelisation for each, to produce an averaged magnification profile." (Results); "Only the flux lying within the image plane mask contributes to the image plane flux and thus the total magnification." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Comparison of magnification factors with previous literature models where available (e.g. H-ATLAS J083051.0+013225)  
  > "They infer a magnification factor $\mu = 6.9 \pm 0.6$, which is in agreement with the magnification we find with our best fit model of $\mu = 6.7 \pm 0.5$." (Object notes)
4. **Fit demagnified multi-wavelength SEDs and derive intrinsic dust mass, IR luminosity, gas mass, and SFR**: Demagnify observed sub-mm/mm photometry by total magnification factor, fit rest-frame photometry with both a single-temperature optically thick SED and a dual-temperature optically thin SED, integrate the optically thin SED from 3 to 1100 um for L_IR, compute dust mass bounds using 873 um flux, H2 gas mass via scaling relations, and SFR from L_IR.

## Qualitative findings

- Four systems are well described as galaxy-galaxy scale strong lenses (single power-law profile), and three as group-scale lenses (two power-law profiles).  
  > "Four of these systems are galaxy-galaxy scale strong lenses, which are well described by a single power-law mass profile, whilst the remaining three are group-scale lenses, which have been successfully fitted with two power-law mass profiles." (Conclusions)
- Most of the modelled foreground lensing galaxies have power-law mass density slopes close to isothermal (alpha ~ 2), though some show significant deviations.  
  > "The majority of our lens models have mass density slopes close to isothermal, but some systems show significant differences." (Abstract); "We have fitted smooth power-law density profiles (and in some cases two) for each of the lensing systems, and found that most of the lenses are close to isothermal." (Conclusions)
- All seven lensed sub-mm sources have SFR-to-dust-mass ratios consistent with or exceeding the mean ratio for high-redshift SMGs and local ULIRGs.  
  > "We find that these sub-millimetre sources all have ratios of star formation rate to dust mass that is consistent with or in excess of the mean ratio for high-redshift sub-millimetre galaxies and low redshift ultra-luminous infrared galaxies." (Abstract); "Using the midpoint of this range to calculate the SFR to dust mass ratio, we find that all seven of our sources lie above the mean ratio for the SMG/ULIRG population as described in (cite: rowlands)." (Conclusions)
- In the combined sample of 13 strongly lensed SMGs (combining this work with Dye et al. 2018), 9 out of 13 sources are visually classified as having disturbed morphologies.  
  > "9 of the 13 galaxies in our sample are visually classified as being disturbed, with at least two of them having evidence of being mergers (H-ATLAS J142935.3-002836, H-ATLAS J083051.0+01322)." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (~0.12 arcsec) ALMA Band 7 observations combined with strong gravitational lensing magnification probe compact physical scales in the brightest 500 um Herschel-selected SMGs at z > 1.  
  > "High-resolution sub-mm follow-up observations allow for precision lensing analyses, greatly benefiting our characterisation of source properties sensitive to the lens model, such as the intrinsic luminosity, star formation rate and gas and dust masses." (Introduction); "The observation targets for the original ALMA programme were selected from the H-ATLAS and HeLMS surveys for having the brightest 500 $\mu\mathrm{m}$ flux densities and with spectroscopic redshifts \> 1." (DATA)
- Why this method: Modelling visibilities directly in the uv-plane using NUFFT operators avoids bias from spatially correlated image-plane noise caused by incomplete uv-coverage, while constant-weight gradient regularisation keeps computational cost manageable.  
  > "The benefit of working in the image plane is that the task is significantly less computationally expensive, however, due to the incomplete coverage of the uv-plane, spatially correlated noise is introduced which can in principle bias the inferred lens model." (Introduction); "Working directly with the visibility data avoids this problem at the cost of longer modelling times." (Introduction); "We adopted a gradient regularisation scheme, analogous to that which is described in (cite: warren_dye), with a constant weight for the source plane due to it’s simplicty and to not add more computational costs to an already expensive procedure." (The semi-linear inversion method in the uv-plane)
- Alternative: Image-plane lens modelling of CLEANed images (rejected in favour of direct uv-plane visibility modelling to avoid correlated noise bias).  
  > "In this work we have opted to model the visibility data directly rather than to work with CLEANed image plane data." (Conclusions)
- Alternative: Direct Fourier Transform (DFT) for uv-plane inversion (replaced by NUFFT via PyNUFFT due to memory and computational cost).
