8. **Integrate rocket acceleration and photoevaporation**: Use the rocket equation and the Mellema mass-loss model; integrate mass, velocity, and distance through time for B1 and B2.

(Acceleration of globules)

   - parameter: Ionizing photon rate 10^49 photons s^-1; average mass per atom approximately 1.33m_H; case B recombination coefficient approximately 2.6×10^-13 cm^3 s^-1

## Qualitative findings

- Most globules are stable against gravitational collapse.  
  > "This indicates that most of the globules are stable against gravitational collapse" (Jeans’ analysis of the globules)
- Th01A-Th01C forms a T-shape and is kinematically separate from Th01B.  
  > "Thackeray-1, which shows two kinematically separate structures, Th01B and Th01A-Th01C, with the latter forming a T-shape with a north-south pillar-like feature pointing toward HD 101205" (Summary)
- CS is detected in B1 globules but absent from B2 and B3.  
  > "The CS emission is found in all the globules within the block B1, but it is not present in the intermediate velocity block B2 globules, nor in the redshifted block B3 globules." (Other molecular transitions)
- C18O and CH3OH concentrate in dense parts of the main globules.  
  > "C$^{18}$O and CH$_3$OH emission is more concentrated in the densest parts of the main globules (Th01, Th02, and Th03), including Th14 and Th31." (Other molecular transitions)

  > "This indicates a good agreement with a recent fragmentation and detachment from larger globules, and explains why, despite their short lifetimes, small globules and splinters are still observed." (Fragmentation of the globules)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Map cold continuum and molecular emission and cover globules previously imaged by HST.  
  > "Here we present the analysis of Band 3 ALMA data that reveals the cold emission (continuum and molecular) associated with the neutral gas and its kinematic behavior." (Abstract); "a small mosaic of 19 Nyquist-sampled pointings was used to ensure the spatial coverage of those globules mapped in previous Hubble Space Telescope observations" (ALMA Band 3 observations)
- Why this method: Linear gradients suggest acceleration; isolated globules motivate testing rocket acceleration rather than a pillar-based instability scenario.  
  > "In contrast, linear velocity gradients, as observed, can indicate that the globules are being accelerated" (Kinematics of the globules); "but isolated globules following a linear velocity gradient, we discuss a different working hypothesis." (Kinematics of the globules)
- Alternative: Constant-velocity spherical expanding layer is unexpected given the linear velocity gradients.  
  > "This would be unexpected if the globules were located on the surface of a spherical layer that expands at constant velocity." (Kinematics of the globules)
- Alternative: A turbulent origin is difficult to reconcile with the gradients.  
  > "It would also be difficult to reconcile it with an origin of the globules from a large-scale turbulent medium bathed by ionizing radiation" (Kinematics of the globules)
- Alternative: Rayleigh-Taylor instability considered; clear gradients along pillar-like structures are not generally seen.  
  > "An alternative explanation for the velocity gradient is a large-scale Rayleigh-Taylor instability in a denser shell pushed by the expanding HII region" (Kinematics of the globules); "Since we do not see clear velocity gradients along pillar-like structures" (Kinematics of the globules)
- Alternative: B3 could be unrelated projected gas; excluded from further analysis.  
  > "An alternative is that block B3 globules do not relate to the expanding shell and acceleration process from these O stars, and they just serendipitously lie in projection with the other globules." (Kinematics of the globules); "from now on we consider the B3 globules unrelated to Thackeray’s globules and will not include them in further analysis." (Kinematics of the globules)
- Alternative: Th14 velocity gradient may indicate rotation, rocket acceleration, or another instability.  
  > "which may be indicative of rotation, although with the data at hand, we cannot rule out other possible origin, such as rocket acceleration or Rayleigh-Jeans instability, for instance." (Inner structure of Thackeray-1)

  > "$^{13}$CO emission is moderately optically thick ($\tau_{\rm 13CO}\approx 1$) in a few spots of Th01A, Th01B, Th01C, Th02A, and Th14" (Physical characterization of globules); "which may imply density and mass underestimations of factors 1.1-2.3 in these few lines-of-sight." (Physical characterization of globules)
- Limitation (authors): Approximately 10% of optical globules are undetected; some lie at less sensitive mosaic edges.  
  > "About 10% of the globules detected at optical wavelengths are undetected here up to the sensitivity threshold of the ALMA observations." (Globule ID and overall kinematics); "Of these, Th19, Th21, and Th42 lie close to the less sensitive edges of the mosaic" (Globule ID and overall kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Th01B and Th01C cannot be separated spatially in continuum.  
  > "Th01B-Th01C (which cannot be spatially separated in the continuum image, because they overlap in projection along the line of sight)" (Jeans’ analysis of the globules)
- Limitation (authors): Star–globule separation is uncertain; the 13 pc case gives larger acceleration times and initial distances.
