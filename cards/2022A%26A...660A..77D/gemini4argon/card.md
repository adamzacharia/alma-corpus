## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce MUSE observations and assemble the 26-pointing mosaic**: Standard ESO MUSE pipeline reduction, sky subtraction with 'subtract-model', exposure alignment, and pointing alignment to HST B-band mosaic registered to Gaia DR2  
  > "We reduced the MUSE data with the ESO pipeline (cite: weilbacher14; weilbacher20_pipeline), following the standard reduction procedure." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction); "Offsets between different pointings were instead computed by matching each MUSE whitelight image with the B-band HST mosaic obtained from the MAST archive[^3], and registered to the Gaia DR2 (cite: gaia_dr2)." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
   - software: ESO MUSE pipeline, muse_scibasic, muse_create_sky, muse_scipost, muse_exp_align, muse_exp_combine, PAMPELMUSE
   - parameter: subtract-model sky subtraction method; Moffat PSF fit  
  > "For the object frame, we used the ‘subtract-model’ sky subtraction method, that uses sky lines and continuum estimated from the sky frames." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction); "We derived a PSF for each pointing by fitting a Moffat profile as a function of wavelength to bright, isolated point sources with PAMPELMUSE (cite: kamann18), and report the values in Table [\[table:pointings_info\]](#table:pointings_info)." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured median PSF of 0.7'' at 7000 A across the mosaic  
  > "Across the full mosaic we measure a median PSF of $0.7\ensuremath{''}$ at 7000 Å (17 pc at the distance of our target)." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
2. **Derive stellar, ionised gas, and molecular gas kinematics and fit 2D rotation models**: Voronoi binning and PPXF fitting for stellar continuum; single and double Gaussian fitting (with BIC selection) for H-alpha in continuum-subtracted cube; PHANGS-ALMA mom1/mom2 maps and Gaussian fits to CO spectra; 2D double-Plummer rotation curve fitting with MocKinG  
  > "We fitted the stellar continuum with PPXF (cite: cappellari04; cappellari17), using the E-MILES simple stellar populations models (cite: vazdekis16) with an unimodal IMF and Padova 2000 isochrones (cite: girardi00)." (Stellar kinematics); "We Voronoi binned the gas cube to a S/N $\sim$ 20 in H$\alpha$[^5] and fitted the line with a single Gaussian profile." (Ionised gas kinematics); "In order to derive the global kinematic parameters of M83, we fitted the velocity maps of stars and gas with the 2D fitting method described in (cite: epinat08) and based on the Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least-square algorithm. In our work, the method has been upgraded using the MocKinG software[^6] to take into account uncertainties on the line of sight (LoS) velocities, the flux distribution, the linewidth of the profiles and the spatial resolution." (Kinematic fitting)
   - software: PPXF, scipy curve_fit, MocKinG
   - parameter: Stellar Voronoi S/N ~ 250 in 5025-5065 A, fit range 4600-8740 A (excluding 7220-8507 A)  
  > "The bins were targeted to a signal to noise ratio (S/N) $\simeq$ 250 in the continuum range 5025 – 5065 Å, corresponding to a S/N $\simeq$ 40 Å$^{-1}$. We fitted the range 4600 – 8740 Å (in order to include the Ca ii triplet)." (Stellar kinematics); "in particular, we excluded entirely the range 7220 – 8507 Å due to the strong residual sky emission." (Stellar kinematics)
   - parameter: H-alpha Voronoi S/N ~ 20, fit window 22 A (+/- 500 km s^-1)  
  > "We Voronoi binned the gas cube to a S/N $\sim$ 20 in H$\alpha$[^5] and fitted the line with a single Gaussian profile." (Ionised gas kinematics); "We set the initial flux $f_i$ to the integral of the binned spectrum in a window of width 22 Å ($\pm$ 500 km s$^{-1}$) centred on the line." (Ionised gas kinematics)
   - parameter: 2D kinematic fit angular sector of inclusion = 67.5 degrees around major axis with cosine weighting  
  > "We used an angular sector of inclusion of 67.5 degrees (in the galaxy plane) around the major axis[^7], and the LoS velocities were weighted according to their angular distance to the major axis by a cosine function, in order to minimise the contamination due to the predominance of radial motions around the minor axis." (Analysis of the stellar velocity field)
   - assumption: CO(2-1)-to-CO(1-0) line ratio R_21 = 0.65 and alpha_CO = 4.35 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "Using Eq. (1) in (cite: sun20) and assuming a CO(2–1)-to-CO(1–0) line ratio $R_{21} = 0.65$ (cite: denbrok21_alma; leroy21_subm) and a standard Galactic CO-to-H2 conversion factor $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 4.35$ $M_\odot$ pc$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$, these values corresponds to a cold gas surface density of $\simeq 30$ and 170 $M_\odot$ pc$^{-2}$." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least-square algorithm for 2D velocity fields; BIC statistic for single vs double Gaussian selection  
  > "We determined the optimal number of parameters based on the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) statistic (cite: schwarz78), following the approach of (cite: koch21)." (Ionised gas kinematics); "In order to derive the global kinematic parameters of M83, we fitted the velocity maps of stars and gas with the 2D fitting method described in (cite: epinat08) and based on the Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least-square algorithm." (Kinematic fitting)
   - validation: Tested S/N thresholds with kernel filters, residual velocity maps, and alternative kinematic centres from literature  
  > "We assessed the robustness of the fit by masking low-S/N regions in the velocity map using Kernel filters, testing various several S/N thresholds (see also Appendix [9](#section:appendix_kinem_fitting))." (Analysis of the stellar velocity field); "in order to study the robustness of the fit in Appendix [9](#section:appendix_kinem_fitting) we test three additional models against model $(A)$, adopting different centres from literature values." (Analysis of the stellar velocity field)
3. **Estimate dust extinction, separate HII regions from DIG, and perform BPT diagram analysis**: Compute E(B-V) from H-beta/H-alpha ratio using PYNEB and compare with ALMA CO surface density; separate HII regions and DIG using ASTRODENDRO and [SII]/H-alpha cut; analyse BPT emission line ratios against star formation limits and shock models  
  > "We estimated the extinction from the H$\beta$/H$\alpha$ ratio with PYNEB (cite: Luridiana15)." (Extinction traced by the molecular and ionised gas); "We separated the H$\alpha$ emission into H ii regions and DIG using the Python package ASTRODENDRO[^9]." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG); "We studied the physical conditions of the ionised gas in ‘BPT’ emission line diagrams (cite: baldwin81; veilleux87)." (BPT diagram analysis)
   - software: PYNEB, ASTRODENDRO
   - parameter: ASTRODENDRO min leaf size = 4.3 pixels (0.86''), min_delta = 0, SB threshold = 1.23e-15 erg s^-1 cm^-2 arcsec^-2; alternative cut [SII] 6716,31/H-alpha = 0.29  
  > "We set the minimum size of the leaves based on the typical PSF measured at H$\alpha$ (FWHM = 4.3 pixels $\equiv 0.86\ensuremath{''}$) and we fixed the optional `min_delta` parameter (minimum difference in flux between two separate structures) to zero." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG); "We obtained thus $L(H\alpha) = 4.97 \times 10^{35} \mbox{erg s}^{-1}$, corresponding to a SB threshold of 1.23 $\times 10^{-15}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ arcsec$^{-2}$, where we assumed an H ii region diameter of 10 pc (lower limit estimate)." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG); "In their work, (cite: poetrodjojo19) applied a cut \[S ii\] $\lambda$6716,31/H$\alpha$ = 0.29, based on typical ratios observed in H ii regions and DIG in the MW (cite: madsen06). We adopted the same limit, which results in the contours shown in Fig. [6](#fig:halpha_map) (right panel)." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG)
   - assumption: Intrinsic H-alpha/H-beta = 2.863 (case B recombination, T_e = 10^4 K, n_e = 100 cm^-3) and Cardelli et al. (1989) extinction law; T_e ~ 10000 K and n_e = 10^3 cm^-3 for H-alpha luminosity threshold  
  > "We assumed an intrinsic ratio H$\alpha$/H$\beta$ = 2.863 (corresponding to case B recombination with $T_e = 10^4$ K and $n_e$ = 100 cm$^{-3}$, (cite: osterbrock)) and a (cite: cardelli89) extinction law." (Extinction traced by the molecular and ionised gas); "Assuming an electron temperature and density $T_e \sim \num{10 000}$ K and $n_e = 10^3~\mbox{cm}^{-3}$, this gives (cite: draine): $$L(H\alpha) = 1.37 \times 10^{-12}~Q(H^0).$$" (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG)
   - fitting: Single and double component Gaussian fitting of emission lines in Voronoi-binned cubes  
  > "In each diagram the fluxes were obtained by single component Gaussian fitting of the emission lines in the gas cube tessellated to a S/N = 20 in the weakest line of interest (\[O iii\] $\lambda$5007 for the N2- and S2-BPT diagrams, and \[O i\] $\lambda$6300 for the O1 diagram)." (BPT diagram analysis); "We also performed a BPT analysis analogous to Sect. [5.2](#section:BPT) for the two Gaussian components separately." (BPT analysis of the starburst region)
   - validation: Compared H-alpha SB dendrogram method with [SII]/H-alpha ratio cut method and corrected for diffuse background emission via sigma clipping  
  > "Correcting for the diffuse emission coincident with H ii regions results in a negligible increase of $\lesssim 0.1 \%$." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG); "We also estimated the fraction of DIG based on a cut in \[S ii\]/H$\alpha$, as recently done by (cite: kreckel16) and (cite: poetrodjojo19)." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG)

## Software

- **PHANGS-ALMA pipeline** : Processing archival ALMA CO(2-1) data and generating moment maps  
  > "Finally, archival ALMA CO (J = 2–1) data[^2] covering the inner 7.2 kpc ($2.1 \times R_e$) of the galaxy in galactocentric radius have been processed as part of the PHANGS-ALMA survey (cite: leroy21) using the PHANGS-ALMA pipeline (cite: leroy21_pipeline)." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
- **ESO MUSE pipeline** : MUSE data reduction, sky subtraction, alignment, and mosaic combination (`muse_scibasic`, `muse_create_sky`, `muse_scipost`, `muse_exp_align`, `muse_exp_combine`)  
  > "We reduced the MUSE data with the ESO pipeline (cite: weilbacher14; weilbacher20_pipeline), following the standard reduction procedure." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction); "The list of offsets in WCS was then used by `muse_exp_combine` to combine the individual pixel tables into a large mosaic." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
- **PAMPELMUSE** : Fitting Moffat PSF profiles as a function of wavelength to point sources in MUSE pointings  
  > "We derived a PSF for each pointing by fitting a Moffat profile as a function of wavelength to bright, isolated point sources with PAMPELMUSE (cite: kamann18), and report the values in Table [\[table:pointings_info\]](#table:pointings_info)." (Dataset overview and MUSE data reduction)
- **PPXF** : Fitting the stellar continuum and deriving stellar kinematics  
  > "We fitted the stellar continuum with PPXF (cite: cappellari04; cappellari17), using the E-MILES simple stellar populations models (cite: vazdekis16) with an unimodal IMF and Padova 2000 isochrones (cite: girardi00)." (Stellar kinematics)
- **scipy curve_fit** : Gaussian profile fitting of emission lines  
  > "We used the python scipy module `curve_fit`, with an initial guess ($v_i, \sigma_i) = (v_{\rm syst}, 20)$ km/s for the velocity and velocity dispersion, where $v_{\rm syst}$ is the systemic velocity estimated from the stellar kinematics." (Ionised gas kinematics)
- **MocKinG** : 2D kinematic fitting of stellar, H-alpha, and CO velocity fields  
  > "In our work, the method has been upgraded using the MocKinG software[^6] to take into account uncertainties on the line of sight (LoS) velocities, the flux distribution, the linewidth of the profiles and the spatial resolution." (Kinematic fitting)
- **PYNEB** : Estimating dust extinction from the H-beta/H-alpha line ratio  
  > "We estimated the extinction from the H$\beta$/H$\alpha$ ratio with PYNEB (cite: Luridiana15)." (Extinction traced by the molecular and ionised gas)
- **ASTRODENDRO** : Separating H-alpha emission into H II regions and diffuse ionised gas (DIG)  
  > "We separated the H$\alpha$ emission into H ii regions and DIG using the Python package ASTRODENDRO[^9]." (H ii region identification and fraction of DIG)
- **Astropy** : Core Python astronomy utilities  
  > "This research made use of Astropy[^15], a community-developed core Python package for Astronomy (cite: astropy:2013; astropy:2018)." (Acknowledgements)

## Qualitative findings

- Both stars and gas trace regular disk rotation as well as a fast-rotating circumnuclear disk (~30'' or 700 pc in diameter).  
  > "We observe that both stars and gas trace the galactic disk rotation, as well as a fast-rotating nuclear component (30$''$ $\simeq$ 700 pc in diameter), likely connected to secular processes driven by the galactic bar." (abstract)
- East of the nucleus, a redshifted stream (feature C, 50'' or 1250 pc) is seen in both H-alpha (+30 km s^-1) and ALMA CO(2-1) (multiple peaks with one redshifted by +100 km s^-1), surrounded by a 1000 x 1600 pc region with high velocity dispersion and slow-shock ionisation.  
  > "On top of the disk rotation, the gas traces a flow east of the nucleus (50$''$ $\simeq$ 1250 pc in size), that appears redshifted on top of the disk rotation. In the ionised gas, we observe $\Delta v_{\rm H\alpha} \simeq$ +30 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the surroundings, and in the molecular gas we observe multiple peaks of emission, one of which is redshifted by $\Delta v_{\rm CO} \simeq$ +100 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the emission tracing galactic rotation." (Conclusions)
- A double-Gaussian fit to H-alpha reveals a nuclear biconic outflow structure (cones A and B, ~500 pc in size) perpendicular to the bar with velocity separations up to 200 km s^-1 and shock signatures at the far ends.  
  > "A double Gaussian component fit to the H$\alpha$ line also reveals the presence of a nuclear biconic structure whose axis of symmetry is perpendicular to the bar. The two cones (20$''$ $\simeq$ 500 pc in size) appear blue- and redshifted along the line of sight." (abstract)
- Regions with high ionised gas extinction (E(B-V) > 0.5) correspond closely to dense molecular gas (> 100 M_sun pc^-2) traced by ALMA CO(2-1), except in the southern circumnuclear peak due to vertical geometry along the line of sight.  
  > "By comparing the extinction map with the CO intensity emission traced by ALMA (left panels in Fig. [5](#fig:ebv_map)), we observe that indeed regions with high extinction correspond to dense molecular gas, with a surface density larger than $100~M_\ensuremath{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$ (black contours in the top right panel Fig. [5](#fig:ebv_map))." (Extinction traced by the molecular and ionised gas); "The southern CO peak (receding part of the circumnuclear disk) shows very low extinction values. Given the inclination of the galaxy, we interpret this as a perspective effect on the vertical separation of the gas, due to the denser molecular gas residing deeper in the disk: the low extinction traced by the ionised gas would then be estimated based on line ratios from gas that is on top of the CO layer." (Extinction traced by the molecular and ionised gas)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: MUSE wide-field IFS mosaic at ~20 pc resolution bridges 10 pc and kpc scales across diverse galactic environments, while archival ALMA CO(2-1) traces the molecular gas distribution and kinematics.  
  > "Our work complements the very high resolution (10 pc) studies and the large scale (100 pc – kpc) studies, by covering a wide area ($\sim$ 20 arcmin$^2$) at a resolution that still allows the characterisation of individual H ii regions while at the same time sampling a large number of regions ($\sim 4700$, Della Bruna et al., in prep) and a wide range of ISM conditions across the galactic disk." (Introduction); "At the same time, the HST coverage provides us with a detailed catalogue of massive stars and clusters, and the ALMA data trace the distribution of the molecular gas in the galaxy." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Double Gaussian fitting with BIC selection captures unresolved line broadening and double-peaked outflow profiles without overfitting; 2D double-Plummer fitting handles both the disk and steep central component.  
  > "A double component can better capture the emission in the case of extreme line broadening on top of a single emission line peak (caused e.g. by shocks or unresolved flows) and in the case where the line has a double-peaked profile, tracing gas motions on top of the galactic rotation (e.g. resolved flows)." (Ionised gas kinematics); "The Plummer density profile has a finite density core and falls off as $r^{-5}$ at large radii; this very steep fall-off was essential to fit at best the central component." (Analysis of the stellar velocity field)
- Alternative: Past galaxy interaction (e.g., with NGC 5253) as the origin of feature C and central kinematic peculiarities, rejected due to the regular stellar rotation field.  
  > "A final possibility could be a past interaction, possibly with the neighbouring galaxy NGC 5253 (1.8$^\circ$ in projected distance)." (Kinematic features in the starburst region); "However, given the extremely regular stellar rotation field and the general lack of global scale perturbances, we discard this hypothesis." (Kinematic features in the starburst region)
- Alternative: AGN-driven outflow or superposition of bar-driven orbits as the sole explanation for cones A and B, disfavoured compared to a starburst-driven outflow.  
  > "Given the fact that the origin of cones A and B is offset from the galaxy’s centre, the lack of shock-related emission immediately surrounding the central region and the presence of slow shocks, we favour over the presence of an AGN the hypothesis of a starburst-driven outflow cone shocking into the surrounding ISM." (Kinematic features in the starburst region); "Finally, what appear as redshifted and blueshifted cones along our line of sight could simply simply be the result of the superposition of bar-driven orbits, resulting from the non-axisymmetric and time varying potential. However, this effect alone would be difficult to reconcile with the large separation between the two peaks of H$\alpha$ emission (up to 200 km s$^{-1}$) at the centre of the cones." (Kinematic features in the starburst region)

  > "We also note that the artefacts in both maps are arising from minute calibration uncertainties at the scale of a few 10s of km s$^{-1}$ and to undersampling of the MUSE LSF (see Sect. [3.1](#section:kinematics_stars))." (Ionised gas kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Voronoi bins in H II regions are typically smaller than the seeing limit, especially at the blue end of the spectrum.  
  > "We caution that bins located in H ii regions are typically below the seeing limit, especially in the blue end of the spectrum (where PSF sizes range between 0.7 – 0.9 arcsec)." (BPT diagram analysis)
- Limitation (authors): MUSE mosaic only covers the inner optical disk (R <= 1.1 R_e), which may explain the lower global f_DIG compared to wider-field studies.  
  > "This might be due to the fact that the MUSE data only cover the inner portion of the optical disk ($R \leq 1.1~R_e$)." (Origin of the DIG)
