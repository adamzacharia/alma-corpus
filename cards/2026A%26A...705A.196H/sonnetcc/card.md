## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract beam-convolved CLEAN radial profiles**: Deproject and azimuthally average primary-beam-corrected CLEAN images using ellipses matched to the disc orientation  
  > "This was performed by drawing a set of ellipses with the same orientation as the discs and different semi-major axes (cite: Marino2025)." (2.1 Extracting CLEAN profiles); "images by azimuthally averaging the emission (cite: Marino2016)" (2.1 Extracting CLEAN profiles)
   - parameter: Sectors near the minor axis masked where deprojected beam is >=30% of the original beam major axis  
  > "we additionally masked sectors of the disc near their minor axes where the deprojected beam is $\geq30$% the original beam major axis" (2.1 Extracting CLEAN profiles)
   - parameter: Uncertainty is sqrt of average variance divided by number of independent points, with independent points set by arc length over beam major axis  
  > "The uncertainty was estimated as the square root of the average variance divided by the number of independent points being averaged." (2.1 Extracting CLEAN profiles); "The latter was estimated from the length of the arcs being averaged divided by the beam major axis." (2.1 Extracting CLEAN profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Method does not work for edge-on discs where the minor axis is not well resolved  
  > "We note that this method does not work for edge-on discs where the minor axis is not well resolved." (2.1 Extracting CLEAN profiles)
2. **Non-parametric deprojected and deconvolved profile in visibility space (frank)**: 1D Fourier-Bessel series fit to the real visibilities regularised by a Gaussian process, fitted in log surface brightness  
  > "recovers the deprojected and deconvolved radial profile of discs from ALMA visibilities under the assumption of axisymmetry by modelling the real component of the visibilities in 1D with a Fourier-Bessel series regularised by a fast Gaussian process" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - software: frank, arksia
   - parameter: alpha sets the minimum S/N down to which visibilities are fit  
  > "sets the minimum signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) down to which" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - parameter: w_smooth sets smoothing of the reconstructed power spectrum  
  > "$w_\text{smooth}$ determines the amount of smoothing applied to the reconstructed power spectrum." (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - parameter: Number of radial points N and outer radius R_out chosen per source; final per-target values (alpha, w_smooth, N, r_out) tabulated  
  > "were chosen for each source to ensure the full radial extent of disc emission was modelled over a dense grid" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank); "summarises the most appropriate hyperparameters selected to model each disc" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - parameter: Stellar flux subtracted from real visibilities as a constant offset (none subtracted for HD 109573; SED flux for HD 32297)  
  > "were subtracted from the real component of the visibilities as a constant offset in amplitude" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank); "so we conservatively did not subtract any stellar flux before fitting the radial profile" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - parameter: PA, inclination and centring offset fixed to ARKS paper I values  
  > "The position angle, inclination and centering offset of the discs were assumed to be those fitted in (cite: Marino2025) for consistency." (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - assumption: Azimuthal symmetry and low optical depth  
  > "Each of these methods enables radial profile recovery under the assumption of azimuthal symmetry, even if the disc is perfectly edge-on" (2 Extracting radial profiles)
   - assumption: Thin disc (h = 0) for the fits  
  > "Considering that the reduction of oscillatory artefacts and avoidance of negative surface brightness values outweighs the potential inaccuracies of performing the fits under the assumption that the disc is thin" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - fitting: Gaussian-process regularised Fourier-Bessel fit; hyperparameters chosen by inspection rather than by a sampler  
  > "the radial brightness profile should capture structural detail in the disc without introducing obvious oscillatory artefacts (a sign of over-fitting)" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
   - validation: Reconstructed power spectrum must reproduce the observed one without over-fitting undersampled baselines  
  > "the reconstructed power spectrum should reasonably reproduce that from the observations without over-fitting to baselines that are undersampled and carry high uncertainty" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
3. **Non-parametric deprojected and deconvolved profile in image space (rave)**: Fit concentric annuli to the CLEAN image, solving for each annulus brightness  
  > "recovers the radial profile of a disc by fitting models of concentric annuli to its image, solving for the brightness required of each annulus to reproduce the image" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - software: rave
   - parameter: Fit repeated 100 times with Monte Carlo annuli boundary partitions  
  > "the fit is repeated 100 times, each time with a different partitioning of annuli boundaries, which are chosen in a Monte Carlo fashion" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - parameter: Number of annuli N is the only hyperparameter; largest value without significant oscillations  
  > "choosing the maximum value of $N$ that does not generate significant oscillatory artefacts in the fitted profile, beyond which the uncertainties on the radial profile increase" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - parameter: Edge-on rave version for HD 39060, HD 197481 and HD 32297, non-edge-on version for the rest  
  > "Following the approach in (cite: Han2025), we applied the edge-on version to the only three highly inclined discs in the sample that do not have a resolved central cavity" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - parameter: Stellar flux from ARKS paper I if well constrained, otherwise SED flux  
  > "The stellar flux values were assumed to be those fitted in (cite: Marino2025) if they were well-constrained, or the SED-fitted stellar flux otherwise." (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - assumption: Scale height set to the best-fit aspect ratio derived with rave  
  > "profiles presented here were fitted using the best-fit scale height aspect ratio derived with" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - fitting: Uncertainties from scatter among the 100 Monte Carlo annuli partitions  
  > "The degree of scatter between the radial profiles fitted in each iteration was used to estimate the uncertainties on the radial profile" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
   - validation: Fitted both rave versions to all discs and kept the more appropriate one; low sensitivity to CLEAN robust parameter  
  > "Inspection of all discs fitted with both methods confirms that this choice of method produced lower uncertainties and oscillatory artefacts" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave); "is found to be low for reasonable robust parameter values" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
4. **Parametric radial profile fits in visibility space**: Forward model of surface density and temperature to a sky image, converted to visibilities and fitted with MCMC for several functional forms  
  > "to fit vertical and radial surface density profiles, obtaining values for the physical parameters" (2.4.1 Modelling code); "to convert the model image into synthetic visibilities sampled at the same antenna separations and orientations as the data" (2.4.1 Modelling code)
   - software: emcee, Galario, parametric debris disc modelling code (Fehr2023, Zawadzki2025)
   - parameter: Functional forms: double/triple power law, power law + error function, Gaussian, asymmetric Gaussian, double/triple Gaussian, Gaussian + double power law, optionally with Gaussian gaps subtracted  
  > "We fit the radial surface density profile to a variety of functional forms—consisting of combinations of Gaussians, power laws, and error functions" (2.4.2 Radial profiles)
   - parameter: Vertical aspect ratio h_HWHM fixed at 0.015 by default, with exceptions for some discs  
  > "We defaulted to fixing the vertical aspect ratio" (2.4.3 Vertical profiles); "at 0.015, adjusting the value for certain discs based on results from (cite: Zawadzki2025)" (2.4.3 Vertical profiles)
   - parameter: Certain shape parameters fixed to reduce degeneracies  
  > "We fixed certain parameters at the values noted here to minimise degeneracies; exceptions for specific targets are stated explicitly." (Table of functional forms)
   - assumption: Dust in blackbody equilibrium with the star; Gaussian vertical profile; star at the centre of an axisymmetric disc  
  > "assuming that the dust emission is in blackbody equilibrium with the stellar emission" (2.4.1 Modelling code); "We therefore assume that the vertical profile is Gaussian for all targets in this paper." (2.4.3 Vertical profiles); "assuming that the star was located at the centre of the axisymmetric disc" (2.4.2 Radial profiles)
   - fitting: Affine-invariant MCMC (emcee) comparing synthetic and observed visibilities; priors and walker settings not stated  
  > "The code then employs the affine-invariant Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) implementation in" (2.4.1 Modelling code)
   - validation: Profiles from frank, rave and parametric models compared  
  > "are generally in close agreement, confirming the robustness of the modelling for each system" (3.1 Radial profiles)
5. **Select best parametric functional form per disc**: Compare functional forms with AIC and BIC, with visual checks when they disagree  
  > "We compared different parametrisations for each disc with the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian information criterion (BIC)" (2.4.4 Parametric model comparison); "Although AIC and BIC values were the primary basis for designating a functional form as the best fit for a given disc, we took into consideration radial profile visualisations" (2.4.4 Parametric model comparison)
   - parameter: BIC difference >10 is strong evidence; AIC differences converted to Gaussian confidence levels; N data points is sum of real and imaginary visibility components  
  > "BIC differences are considered to provide strong evidence of a better fit when greater than 10 (cite: Kass1995), while AIC differences are converted to a Gaussian probability interval to obtain confidence levels." (2.4.4 Parametric model comparison); "in this case, the sum of the real and imaginary components in the visibility domain" (2.4.4 Parametric model comparison)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Ring and slope parameters taken from the form best suited to the quantity, chosen from AIC and BIC differences  
  > "by 2.355 for discs best fit by multiple Gaussians or by measuring the FWHM of the best functional form from its radial profile" (2.4.4 Parametric model comparison)
6. **Convert surface brightness to dust surface density**: Blackbody equilibrium temperature profile with stellar luminosity and a mass opacity applied to the non-parametric profiles  
  > "assuming a blackbody equilibrium temperature profile, stellar luminosities listed in (cite: Marino2025) and the mass opacity described in (cite: Marino2025)" (3.1 Radial profiles)
   - software: frank, rave
   - assumption: Millimetre dust traces planetesimal distribution  
  > "Assuming that the distribution of millimetre dust reflects the distribution of planetesimals" (Abstract)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Measure ring, edge and gap features from frank surface density profiles**: Peak radius, radii at 25, 50 and 75 percent of peak for each edge, FWHM from 50 percent radii, edge width from 25 to 75 percent radii, gap location from local minimum  
  > "For discs with a single ring, we measured the peak radius and the radii where the inner and outer edges reach 25%, 50%, and 75% of the peak, respectively." (3.2 Quantifying disc features); "We used the 50% radii at the inner and outer edges to calculate the FWHM of the disc, and the difference between the 25% and 75% radii of either edge to define the width of the edge" (3.2 Quantifying disc features); "We used the local minimum within the gap to define its location" (3.2 Quantifying disc features)
   - software: frank
   - assumption: For edges bordering a gap the baseline is the local gap minimum, otherwise 0  
  > "if the edge immediately borders a radial gap, the baseline surface density value was assumed to be the local minimum value of the gap" (3.2 Quantifying disc features)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Population statistics and comparison with REASONS and protoplanetary discs**: Compare fractional width distributions with REASONS and protoplanetary ring samples; correlate width, radius and age  
  > "where the significance was estimated using the ratio between the least-squares slope and its standard error when fitting a linear model in logarithmic space" (4.2 Ring width correlations)
   - assumption: Debris and protoplanetary samples are assumed to be the same population offset only in time (acknowledged caveat)  
  > "this comparison loosely assumes that the protoplanetary and debris discs observed are from the same population and offset only in time" (4.1 Comparison with protoplanetary discs)
   - fitting: Linear model in logarithmic space, least squares  
  > "where the significance was estimated using the ratio between the least-squares slope and its standard error when fitting a linear model in logarithmic space" (4.2 Ring width correlations)
   - validation: Controlled for stellar mass by comparing only A stars; checked that a thin-ring resolution test would resolve 2 to 3 narrow rings  
  > "when comparing only the A stars (for which the ARKS sample is most abundant in) to control for the effect of stellar mass" (4.1 Comparison with protoplanetary discs)
9. **Infer planet masses from gap widths**: Chaotic zone width from overlap of first-order mean-motion resonances converted to planet mass from measured gap width  
  > "it is possible to use the width of each gap to estimate the mass of the corresponding planet clearing the gap" (4.3 Planet constraints from gaps)
   - assumption: Single planet formed in situ on a circular orbit  
  > "all gaps identified in the sample are created by a single planet which formed in situ on a circular orbit" (4.3 Planet constraints from gaps)
   - assumption: Gap clearing by chaotic scattering from overlapping first-order mean-motion resonances  
  > "the mechanism of gap clearing is scattering driven by the chaotic evolution due to the overlap of first-order mean-motion resonances" (4.3 Planet constraints from gaps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Masses not tested against gap depth; compared with direct imaging detection probability maps  
  > "These values are not tested for consistency with the gap depth." (Table 2 notes); "based on the detection probability maps in (cite: Milli2025)" (Table 2 notes)
10. **Constrain planet sculpting from inner edge steepness**: Select discs with power-law inner slope alpha_in >= 15 and compute upper limits on planet mass and semi-major axis with the Pearce et al. sculpting model  
  > "we define discs with $\alpha > 15$ in parametric models with power-law edges as a steep slope based on the cut-off for planet sculpting (cite: Pearce2024)" (3.1 Radial profiles); "with Eq. (17) in (cite: Pearce2024) and with the publicly available planet-truncation model from (cite: Pearce2022)" (4.4 Trends in the steepness of the inner edge)
   - software: SculptingPlanet (Pearce2022 planet-truncation model)
   - assumption: Planet just interior to the inner edge having cleared debris out to the edge  
  > "These constraints rely on the assumption that a planet is located just interior to the inner edge of the disc and has cleared debris out to the inner edge." (4.4 Trends in the steepness of the inner edge)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with literature planet constraints and detection limits (appendix)  
  > "All masses are below the detection limits at both the 99.7% and 50% confidence levels calculated by the companion paper (cite: Milli2025)" (Planet constraints from the inner edge)
11. **Estimate particle eccentricity from outer edge slope**: Approximation e_rms = 0.77 l_out/R_out for a stirred disc with Rayleigh eccentricity and inclination distributions  
  > "We use the approximation derived by (cite: Marino2021) for a model of a stirred disc that assumes a Rayleigh distribution of particle eccentricities and inclinations" (4.5 The outer edge and eccentricity)

## Software

- **frank** : Non-parametric radial profile fitting in visibility space  
  > "code recovers the deprojected and deconvolved radial profile of discs from ALMA visibilities under the assumption of axisymmetry" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
- **arksia** : Pipeline that runs frank for the ARKS sample  
  > "which can be run specifically for the ARKS sample via the pipeline" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
- **rave** : Non-parametric radial profile fitting in image space  
  > "recovers the radial profile of a disc by fitting models of concentric annuli to its image, solving for the brightness required of each annulus to reproduce the image" (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)

  > "The code then employs the affine-invariant Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) implementation in" (2.4.1 Modelling code)

## Qualitative findings

- Half of the sample (multi-ring plus single ring with halos or extra rings) cannot be described by a single ring  
  > "The two categories together account for 50% of the sample, meaning that the radial structure of half the sample cannot be simply described by a single ring." (3.3 The prevalence of substructures)
- No profile is fully described by a single Gaussian  
  > "None of the profiles appear to be fully described by a single Gaussian, which is a common assumption of previous works appropriate for lower-resolution observations." (3.1 Radial profiles)
- Narrow rings with millimetre halos appear in roughly a third of the sample  
  > "which appears in approximately one-third of the ARKS sample" (3.1.3 Group Rx)
- Inner slopes are bimodal: small-radius discs have sharp inner edges, large-radius discs shallow ones  
  > "We find a bimodal distribution of inner slopes: discs that are closer to their host stars" (4.4 Trends in the steepness of the inner edge)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA imaging traces millimetre dust that best follows planetesimals  
  > "This high-resolution ALMA survey is, therefore, well-suited for studying the detailed substructures of planetesimal belts" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Using several independent methods gives informal verification of substructure  
  > "Together, they provide an informal type of verification to more reliably identify and quantify disc substructures." (2 Extracting radial profiles)
- Alternative: Fitted edge-on discs with the non-edge-on rave version and vice versa; kept the more appropriate one  
  > "To confirm the validity of this choice, we fitted the edge-on discs with the non-edge-on version, and vice versa." (2.3 Non-parametric modelling: rave)
- Alternative: Tested sharp cut-offs on inner and outer edges; power law indices did not change  
  > "We found that this did not impact the values obtained for the indices." (4.4 Trends in the steepness of the inner edge)
- How they decided it was enough: frank and rave hyperparameters chosen to avoid oscillatory artefacts; parametric forms added until AIC and BIC no longer justified extra complexity  
  > "the radial brightness profile should capture structural detail in the disc without introducing obvious oscillatory artefacts (a sign of over-fitting)" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
- Limitation (authors): frank does not account for the primary beam, which could bias profiles of large discs  
  > "which could bias the profiles of large discs" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
- Limitation (authors): frank uncertainties are likely underestimated  
  > "which is likely an underestimate of the true uncertainties" (2.2 Non-parametric modelling: frank)
- Limitation (authors): Planet masses from gaps ignore gap depth  
  > "these mass estimates are not tested for compatibility of the depth of the gap and are based solely on its width" (4.3 Planet constraints from gaps)
- Limitation (authors): Classification of discs is phenomenological  
  > "We note that this classification is purely phenomenological and is not designed to reflect their underlying physical origins" (3.1 Radial profiles)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Best form chosen case by case when AIC and BIC disagree, so classification partly subjective
- Caveat (inferred, ours): frank resolution and hyperparameters set by eye per target; no objective selection rule
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Correlations are tentative (2 to 3 sigma) with a small, bright-biased sample and heterogeneous resolution
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Priors, MCMC convergence and per-target fixed parameters are in companion papers, hard to audit here
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Planet mass inferences depend on strong assumptions (single in situ planet, chaotic zone width)
