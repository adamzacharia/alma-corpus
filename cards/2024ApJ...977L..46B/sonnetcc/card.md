## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA cubes and continuum**: Standard CASA reduction; 40 km/s cubes with natural weighting, cleaned to 2 sigma  
  > "The data were reduced using standard procedures with CASA (cite: mcmullin2007)." (2.2 ALMA); "We produced image cubes with 40 km s$^{-1}$ resolution using natural weighting and cleaning to the $2\sigma$ level." (2.2 ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 40 km/s channel width, natural weighting, 2 sigma clean threshold  
  > "We produced image cubes with 40 km s$^{-1}$ resolution using natural weighting and cleaning to the $2\sigma$ level." (2.2 ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum size and flux of the quasar host**: 2D Gaussian fit to the 238 GHz continuum with imfit  
  > "We fit a two-dimensional Gaussian to the 238 GHz continuum source with the CASA task `imfit`." (2.2 ALMA)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Extract the [C II] spectrum and total line flux**: Spectrum extracted in a 1.5'' radius aperture chosen by curve-of-growth analysis  
  > "We used a curve of growth analysis to determine the optimal aperture radius to extract the spectrum." (2.2 ALMA)
   - parameter: aperture radius 1.5''; cumulative flux plateaus there for both optical and [C II] centroids  
  > "thus, all the flux density is recovered with this aperture radius." (2.2 ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Curve of growth plateau independent of whether the optical or [C II] centroid is used  
  > "thus, all the flux density is recovered with this aperture radius." (2.2 ALMA)
4. **Fit the [C II] line profile and test for a second component**: Fit with one and two Gaussians; compare with BIC; make line images across 1.2xFWHM of each Gaussian and look for 3.5 sigma signal in the shifted component  
  > "with one and two Gaussians. Both fits describe the data well, but neither functional form is preferred according to Bayesian Information Criterion." (3.1 [CII] properties); "We did not find any signature above $3.5\sigma$ on the image of the shifted component." (3.1 [CII] properties)
   - parameter: line images average channels across 1.2xFWHM  
  > "We produced line images centered on each Gaussian by averaging the channels containing the line across 1.2$\times$FWHM, which maximizes the S/N for detecting a Gaussian line" (3.1 [CII] properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Shifted component judged consistent with noise; single Gaussian adopted  
  > "Therefore, we use the single Gaussian fit to derive the results presented in Table" (3.1 [CII] properties)
5. **Estimate dynamical mass and black hole to host mass ratio**: Dispersion-dominated estimate M_dyn = (3/2) R sigma^2 / G following Decarli et al. 2018, with R the major semi-axis of the Gaussian fit of the [C II] map and sigma the line velocity dispersion  
  > "we can make a rough estimate of the dynamical mass of the host galaxy, following (cite: decarli2018)." (3.1 [CII] properties); "We assume a dispersion-dominated system as there is no evidence of rotation in the channel maps of Fig." (3.1 [CII] properties)
   - parameter: R = 1.75 +/- 0.25 kpc  
  > "is the major semi-axis of the Gaussian fit of the $\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}$ map ($1.75\pm 0.25\,$kpc)" (3.1 [CII] properties)
   - parameter: sigma = 88 +/- 17 km/s  
  > "is the velocity dispersion from the Gaussian fit of the $\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}$ line ($88\pm17\,$km s$^{-1}$)" (3.1 [CII] properties)
   - assumption: Dispersion-dominated system (no rotation in channel maps); dynamical mass comparable to stellar mass or bulge mass for the main-sequence and local-relation comparisons  
  > "We assume a dispersion-dominated system as there is no evidence of rotation in the channel maps of Fig." (3.1 [CII] properties); "Assuming that the dynamical mass is comparable to stellar or bulge masses" (3.1 [CII] properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive [C II] luminosity and [C II]-based SFR**: log SFR = -6.09 + 0.90 x log L_[CII] with L in solar luminosities  
  > "is calculated using $\log \mathrm{SFR}_{\rm \ensuremath{\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}}}=-6.09+0.90\times \log L_{\rm \ensuremath{\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}}}$, with $L_{\rm \ensuremath{\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}}}$ in solar luminosities (cite: delooze2014)" (Table 1 footnote a)
   - assumption: Flat LCDM, H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Omega_M = 0.30  
  > "we adopt a standard flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $H_0 = 70 \,\mbox{km\,s}^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_M = 0.30$" (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compare NOEMA and ALMA continuum with VLA radio to test synchrotron variability and derive IR luminosity and SFR**: Compare 224/239 GHz NOEMA (2021) and 238 GHz ALMA (2022) flux densities with quasi-simultaneous VLA data; derive L_IR from a modified black body and SFR_IR following Kennicutt 1998  
  > "We, therefore, interpret the differences measured at $\sim$240 GHz to be due to synchrotron variability." (3.2 IR Continuum variability); "The IR luminosities are integrated over $8-1000\,\mu$m assuming a modified black body with dust temperature of $T_d=47\,K$ and emissivity index $\beta=1.6$" (Table 1 footnote c)
   - parameter: T_d = 47 K, beta = 1.6, integration over 8-1000 um  
  > "assuming a modified black body with dust temperature of $T_d=47\,K$ and emissivity index $\beta=1.6$" (Table 1 footnote c)
   - assumption: Mm continuum treated as cold dust to compute IR luminosity, dust mass and SFR_IR  
  > "If we were to assume that the NOEMA/ALMA data trace cold dust from the host galaxies" (3.2 IR Continuum variability)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: VLA flux densities above 3 GHz show a similar decrement; observations 6.5 (2.8) rest-frame days apart  
  > "The VLA flux densities above 3 GHz show an average decrement of $\sim 37\%$." (3.2 IR Continuum variability)
8. **Estimate the synchrotron contribution needed for consistent [C II] and IR SFRs and place the source on the [C II] deficit plane**: Require the [C II]- and IR-derived SFRs to agree and solve for the synchrotron fraction of the mm continuum; compare L_[CII]/L_IR with other z>5.7 quasars  
  > "to make the $\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}$- and IR-derived SFR compatible, the synchrotron contribution needs to be 85% and 80% in the mm observations from 2021 and 2022, respectively" (3.3 [CII] deficit)
   - assumption: Dust-obscured star formation dominates for massive galaxies; host galaxy contribution does not vary on scales of months  
  > "Under the assumption that dust-obscured star formation dominates for massive galaxies (cite: ferrara2022; algera2023)" (3.3 [CII] deficit)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Described as an order-of-magnitude estimate  
  > "We can obtain an order-of-magnitude estimate of the synchrotron contribution to the IR luminosity" (3.3 [CII] deficit)

## Qualitative findings

- [C II] is detected in ALMA but not in NOEMA; the NOEMA flux is consistent with its noise  
  > "The flux of the $\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}$ line is consistent with the noise of the continuum-subtracted NOEMA image, explaining the NOEMA non-detection." (2.2 ALMA)
- No single or dominant rotation signature; channel maps show some evidence of multiple peaks, possibly a merger, but not conclusive  
  > "The emission is highly concentrated in the central channels, although there is some evidence of multiple peaks and different substructures in separate channel images." (3.1 [CII] properties)
- The IR continuum variability is interpreted as synchrotron emission, i.e. direct evidence of synchrotron affecting IR properties  
  > "We interpret J0410–0139’s variability in the IR as due to synchrotron emission" (4 Summary)
- The majority of radio-loud quasars do not have anomalous L_[CII]/L_IR  
  > "The majority of the radio-loud quasars (although the statistics are still limited) do not seem to have particularly anomalous" (3.3 [CII] deficit)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NOEMA and ALMA observations were obtained to characterize the host galaxy of the quasar  
  > "To characterize the host galaxy of J0410–0139, we obtained NOEMA observations targeting" (2.1 NOEMA)
- Why this method: Dispersion-dominated dynamical mass because channel maps show no rotation; spectrum aperture set by curve of growth  
  > "We assume a dispersion-dominated system as there is no evidence of rotation in the channel maps of Fig." (3.1 [CII] properties); "We used a curve of growth analysis to determine the optimal aperture radius to extract the spectrum." (2.2 ALMA)
- Alternative: Two-Gaussian fit of the [C II] line was tested but not preferred by BIC; the second component was consistent with noise in its line image  
  > "Both fits describe the data well, but neither functional form is preferred according to Bayesian Information Criterion." (3.1 [CII] properties)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Current S/N is too low for conclusive kinematic constraints  
  > "Higher S/N data are required to provide conclusive kinematic results." (3.1 [CII] properties)
- Limitation (authors): Radio-loud quasar sample statistics are limited  
  > "a larger sample of radio-loud quasars with $\text{[C\,\textsc{ii]}}$ observations is required to robustly determine any potential differences" (3.3 [CII] deficit)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The synchrotron fraction rests on assuming the [C II] SFR is correct and on one modified black body (T_d = 47 K, beta = 1.6), so the 80-85% value is model-dependent
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The mm variability is based on two epochs from different telescopes (NOEMA vs ALMA) with unstated flux-scale uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA band, line-free channels and clean parameters are not given, so the continuum and line fluxes cannot be reproduced exactly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): [C II] dynamical mass assumes dispersion-dominated kinematics from a S/N~7 detection
