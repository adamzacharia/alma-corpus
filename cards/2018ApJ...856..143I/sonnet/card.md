## Analysis recipe

1. **Continuum-subtract and image line cubes**: uvcontsub on line-free channels, then clean, with spectral binning to ~20 km/s  
  > "After subtracting the continuum emission component using the CASA task “uvcontsub”, we applied the “clean” procedure" (3. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: pixel scale 0.03-0.1 arcsec; <1/3 of beam  
  > "We set the pixel scale smaller than one-third of the beam sizes." (3. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA ANALYSIS)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Continuum fluxes compared between settings agree within ~20% except NGC 1614 and IRAS 15250+3609  
  > "agree within ∼20% with those taken during HCN J=3–2 and HCO [+] J=3–2 observations" (4. RESULTS)
2. **Measure continuum fluxes and sizes**: Peak flux in continuum maps; deconvolved sizes with imfit  
  > "To estimate the intrinsic continuum emission sizes, after deconvolution, of the (U)LIRG’s nuclei, we use the CASA task “imfit”." (4. RESULTS)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - fitting: imfit 2D Gaussian deconvolution  
  > "To estimate the intrinsic continuum emission sizes, after deconvolution, of the (U)LIRG’s nuclei, we use the CASA task “imfit”." (4. RESULTS)
   - validation: Sizes noted to depend on beam; beam sizes given for reference  
  > "the deconvolved sizes are in practice dependent on the achieved synthesized beam sizes" (4. RESULTS)
3. **Measure line fluxes**: Moment 0 maps by summing significant channels; Gaussian fits to continuum-subtracted spectra within the beam at continuum peaks; two Gaussians for double-peaked lines  
  > "we fitted Gaussian curves to the continuum-subtracted spectra, within the beam size, at the individual nuclear positions." (4. RESULTS)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: Gaussian fits within beam recover bulk of flux for compact sources; NGC 1614 uses spatially integrated spectra  
  > "the Gaussian fits of the detected molecular emission lines in the spectra within the beam sizes should recover the bulk of the fluxes." (4. RESULTS)
   - fitting: Gaussian line fits (single or double)  
  > "To estimate the line fluxes, we fitted Gaussian curves to the continuum-subtracted spectra, within the beam size, at the individual nuclear positions." (4. RESULTS)
   - validation: Fluxes in 1 arcsec aperture agree within <30% with beam-sized spectra  
  > "the estimated fluxes in the 1 ′′ aperture spectra agree within <30% to those in the beam sized spectra." (5.3. Relation between Molecular Line and Infrared Luminosity)
4. **Compute line flux ratios (J=4-3/J=3-2, HCN/HCO+, HCN/HNC)**: Ratios of Gaussian-fit fluxes in Jy km/s, with J=3-2 HCN and HCO+ from earlier paper  
  > "Table 11 tabulates the ratios of J=4–3 to J=3–2 flux in (Jy km s [−][1] ) for HCN, HCO [+], and HNC." (4. RESULTS)
   - assumption: ~10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty added to HCN/HNC ratios  
  > "possible ∼10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty is added." (Table 12)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate line opacity attenuation from isotopologue ratios**: Compare main-to-isotopologue flux ratios against 12C/13C abundance ratio of 50-100; derive attenuation ratio between HCN and HCO+/HNC and correct intrinsic ratios  
  > "if the observed flux ratios are smaller than the 12 C-to- 13 C abundance ratio of 50–100 estimated for ULIRGs" (5.2.3. Intrinsic HCN-to-HCO [+] and HCN-to-HNC Flux Ratios)
   - assumption: Isotopologue lines optically thin; correction independent of absolute 12C/13C and alpha  
  > "is independent of the adopted absolute value of the 12 C-to- 13 C abundance ratio (between 50–100) and the α value" (5.2.3. Intrinsic HCN-to-HCO [+] and HCN-to-HNC Flux Ratios)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Constrain gas density and temperature with RADEX**: LVG RADEX grids of J=4-3/J=3-2 ratios vs Tkin and nH2, compared with observed ratios  
  > "using the large velocity gradient (LVG) method in the widely used RADEX code (van der Tak et al. 2007)" (5.2.4. RADEX Calculations and Molecular Gas Physical Properties)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: line widths 400 and 1000 km/s  
  > "line widths of 400 km s [−][1] and 1000 km s [−][1] are used to represent all galaxies (except for Superantennae) and Superantennae, respectively." (5.2.4. RADEX Calculations and Molecular Gas Physical Properties)
   - assumption: Column density of 1e16 cm-2 for HCN, HCO+ and HNC; CMB 3 K included  
  > "We thus assume that the column densities of HCN, HCO [+], and HNC are 1 × 10 [16] cm [−][2]" (5.2.4. RADEX Calculations and Molecular Gas Physical Properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive outflow column density, mass and rate for IRAS 15250+3609**: Absorption equivalent widths converted to column density; outflow mass from redshifted emission sub-peak luminosity; rate = 3 x Mout x V/R (or without 3)  
  > "Adopting the outflowing gas size of R ∼ 80 pc from the nucleus and the relation of M˙ outf = 3 × M outf × V/R (Maiolino et al. 2012; Cicone et al. 2014)" (5.1.1. IRAS 15250+3609)
   - parameter: V = 500 km/s, R ~ 80 pc  
  > "the outflow velocity of V = 500 km s [−][1] is adopted for IRAS 15250+3609." (5.1.1. IRAS 15250+3609)
   - assumption: HCO+-to-H2 abundance ~1e-8; Mdense = 10 x HCN J=1-0 luminosity; lines thermalized and optically thick  
  > "Adopting the HCO [+] -to-H 2 abundance ratio of ∼10 [−][8] (Greve et al. 2009)" (5.1.1. IRAS 15250+3609)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mout from J=4-3 and J=3-2 agree within ~30%  
  > "Both M outf values agree within ∼30%." (5.1.1. IRAS 15250+3609)

## Qualitative findings

- Elevated HCN abundance rather than excitation is primarily responsible for high HCN/HCO+ ratios in AGN-important ULIRGs  
  > "We interpret that elevated HCN abundance is primarily responsible for the high HCN-to-HCO [+] flux ratios in AGN-important ULIRGs." (6. SUMMARY)
- Shock tracers are detected in many ULIRGs, suggesting ubiquitous shock activity  
  > "Shock tracers are detected in many sources, suggesting ubiquitous shock activity in the nearby ULIRG population." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multiple J-transitions are needed to disentangle excitation from abundance  
  > "the excitation effects can be hardly disentangled from abundance effects, given only one J-transition." (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Using molecules with comparable critical density minimizes ambiguity from differing spatial distributions  
  > "using molecules with comparable critical density is recommended, because the differences in their spatial distributions are expected to be much smaller" (1. INTRODUCTION)
- Alternative: Redshifted sub-peaks solely from HCN v2=1f or SO2 emission rejected  
  > "making it difficult to interpret that all four sub-peaks originate solely from SO 2 emission." (5.1.1. IRAS 15250+3609)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only two J-transitions, so RADEX needs an assumed column density  
  > "At least three independent observational data are necessary to meaningfully constrain these three parameters, while we have only two J-transitions." (5.2.4. RADEX Calculations and Molecular Gas Physical Properties)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow N(H2) is a lower limit, and starburst galaxies lack isotopologue data  
  > "we have no isotopologue data for these starburst galaxies." (5.2.3. Intrinsic HCN-to-HCO [+] and HCN-to-HNC Flux Ratios)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small sample (11 sources) and isotopologue data for only three ULIRGs
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gaussian fluxes within one beam for different beam sizes across J-transitions may bias ratios
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No formal uncertainty treatment in RADEX; fixed column density
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 12C/13C and optically thin assumptions for isotopologues
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow rate depends on assumed conversion factors and geometry (R~80 pc)
