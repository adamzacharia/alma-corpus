## Qualitative findings

- Only one star, IRS 7 (17454004-2900228), was detected in the SiO v=2, J=2-1 emission line, confirming that SiO v=2, J=2-1 is much weaker than SiO v=1, J=2-1 in the Galactic center region.  
  > "Only the SiO v = 2, J = 2 - 1 emission line of IRS 7 (17454004-2900228) was detected in this sample." (3.1 Positions, Line intensities, and LSR velocities of the SiO maser stars); "It is confirmed that the SiO v =2 ,J =2 - 1 emission line is also much weaker than the SiO v =1 ,J =2 - 1 emission line in the Galactic center region." (3.1 Positions, Line intensities, and LSR velocities of the SiO maser stars)
- The spatial distribution and proper motions of the SiO maser stars are not correlated with the Mini-Spiral (MS) or the Circum Nuclear Disk (CND).  
  > "Therefore, the proper motions are not correlated with the MS and the CND." (3.2 Proper motions of the SiO maser stars); "Therefore, the proper motions are not correlated with the MS and the CND." (3.2 Proper motions of the SiO maser stars)
- The position angles of the proper motions of SiO maser stars show a bipolar distribution peaking along the Galactic plane, unlike the random distribution of WR and O stars in the NSC.  
  > "The distribution of the position angles are bipolar rather than random. The black arrows indicate the direction of the Galactic plane. The peaks of the distribution coincide with the direction of the Galactic plane." (4.1 Proper motion along the Galactic plane)
- The proper motion speeds across the line of sight dominate over the radial LSR speeds along the line of sight.  
  > "The proper motion amplitude is larger than the LSR velocity amplitude. That is, in the observed SiO maser stars, the motions across the line of sight dominate the motions along the line of sight." (4.2 Disproportionate proper motion speed and LSR speed)
- Many 3D space velocities of the SiO maser stars approach or exceed the upper limit for bound Keplerian orbits around a 4 x 10^6 M_sun black hole, indicating they belong to the Nuclear Stellar Disk (NSD) rather than the Nuclear Star Cluster (NSC).  
  > "Many 3D velocities are near to or larger than the upper limit velocities for Kepler orbits around Sgr A" (abstract); "These results indicate that the SiO maser stars are members of the NSD rather than the NSC." (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA has a sharp synthesized beam, high sensitivity, a large FOV (67.5'' FWHM at 86.2 GHz), and simultaneously detects Sgr A* in continuum so star positions can be directly referenced to Sgr A* while radial velocities are easily obtained from the SiO maser line.  
  > "The radial velocity of the maser star is easily observable, but that of the early-type stars is not easy to infer. ALMA has a very sharp synthesized beam with very high sensitivity." (1 Introduction); "Moreover, the position of the stars can be measured directly by referencing the position of Sgr A" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Velocity integration width was set to 5 km s^-1 to improve sensitivity for detecting fainter stars and increase the sample size for statistical kinematics, and outer FOV limit was extended to primary beam attenuation of 0.2.  
  > "the sensitivity, the velocity integration width of the channel map is set to ∆ _V_ = 5 km s _[−]_ [1]" (2 Data acquisition and reduction); "The outer limit of the FOV was set so that the attenuation by the primary beam pattern of the 12 m antenna is 0.2 because the ALMA has high sensitivity." (2 Data acquisition and reduction)
- Alternative: Additional enclosed mass of M ~ 10^6 M_sun within the region to bind the high-velocity stars to the NSC was considered and rejected because such a large additional mass has not been observed in other observations.  
  > "However, such a mass has not been observed in other observations" (4.3 Large enclosed mass); "However, such a mass has not been observed in other observations (e.g. Reid&Brunthaler 2004, Oyama et al. 2024)." (4.3 Large enclosed mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 5 km s^-1 velocity integration width is too wide to study the maser emission from the stars themselves.  
  > "The integration width is too wide to study the maser emission from the stars itself. However, it is suitable to derive the radial motions of SiO maser stars." (2 Data acquisition and reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Positions of two SiO maser stars (17453828-2900069 and 17453854-2859582) could not be determined in 2021 due to decreased line intensity.  
  > "However, the positions of two SiO maser stars, 17453828-2900069 and 17453854-2859582, could not be determined in 2021 although they were determined in 2017. These failures are caused by the line intensity decrease of the SiO maser emission in 2021." (3.1 Positions, Line intensities, and LSR velocities of the SiO maser stars)
- Limitation (authors): Observed LSR velocities are those of the SiO maser spots in the circumstellar envelopes rather than the systemic velocities of the stars themselves, which can differ by up to ~10 km s^-1.  
  > "Note that the observed LSR velocities are those of the SiO maser spots around the stars, not the LSR velocities of the stars themselves. The difference can be as much as ~ 10 km s^-1." (3.1 Positions, Line intensities, and LSR velocities of the SiO maser stars)
- Limitation (authors): Primary beam attenuation causes detection efficiency for weak stars (S < 10 mJy) to decrease near the outer edge of the channel map.  
  > "On the other hand, the star with S < 10 mJy was not detected near the outer edge of the channel map. Attenuation by the primary beam pattern causes the detection efficiency to decrease somewhat near the outer edge." (3.1 Positions, Line intensities, and LSR velocities of the SiO maser stars)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Selection bias against high radial velocities or weak masers at large radii due to the primary beam cutoff (0.2) and velocity range (-350 to +350 km/s) is not modeled quantitatively.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Statistical claims (bipolar PA distribution, velocity comparisons) rely on visual inspection of histograms and simple means/standard deviations of 35 stars without formal statistical hypothesis tests (e.g., KS test against uniform or NSC distributions).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Potential astrometric shift from changing maser spot distributions within the stellar circumstellar shell between 2017 and 2021 is not quantified.
