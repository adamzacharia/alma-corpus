### Imaging

- task: CASA tclean, two stages within a modified PHANGS-ALMA imaging pipeline  
  > "using a modified version of the PHANGS–ALMA imaging pipeline (cite: Leroy_etal_2021b)" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging); "We first run a shallow, multiscale `tclean` to identify both compact and extended emission across the entire FoV down to" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA visibilities into continuum maps and line cubes**: Observatory-supplied calibration scripts and CASA pipeline; line and continuum subsets extracted, combined across array configurations and imaged with a modified PHANGS-ALMA imaging pipeline (two-stage tclean); primary-beam corrected, beams circularized, cubes converted to brightness temperature  
  > "we extract and image a relevant subset of visibility data for each molecular line and continuum using a modified version of the PHANGS–ALMA imaging pipeline (cite: Leroy_etal_2021b)" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging); "We also convert all line data cubes to brightness temperature units (K) according to the corresponding line frequencies." (2.2 Postprocessing and Data Characteristics)
   - software: CASA, tclean, PHANGS-ALMA imaging pipeline
   - parameter: Line cubes cut to +/-300 km/s around systemic velocity of 778 km/s  
  > "extract the subset of channels within the velocity range of interest ($\pm300\,\mbox{$\rm km~s^{-1}$}$ around the systemic velocity of $778\,\mbox{$\rm km~s^{-1}$}$) for each emission line" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)
   - parameter: Channel widths: 10 km/s for HCN, HCO+, CS; native 0.85 km/s for CO(3-2) (also a 5 km/s version)  
  > "we keep the native $0.85\,\mbox{$\rm km~s^{-1}$}$ channel width for $\mathrm{CO}\,(3\text{--}2)$ as S/N ratio is not a concern there" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)
   - parameter: Clean depth: multiscale to S/N=4, then single-scale to S/N=1 (CO 3-2) or 2 (others)  
  > "down to $\mathrm{S/N}=1$ for the $\mathrm{CO}\,(3\text{--}2)$ line and $\mathrm{S/N}=2$ for all other lines and continua" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)
   - parameter: Briggs robust 1.0; uniform weighting for CO(3-2)  
  > "with a robustness parameter of 1.0, which offers the best trade-off between sensitivity and resolution" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of calibrated data  
  > "The calibrated data show no obvious pathologies upon visual inspection." (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)
2. **Identify compact continuum sources (YMC candidates)**: Select peaks above S/N=5 in the 93 GHz and 350 GHz continuum images, merge counterparts, separate a blended pair by eye  
  > "These sources are selected based on a minimum $\mathrm{S/N}=5$ at peak brightness." (3.1 Identifying ALMA Sources); "which we separate by visual inspection" (3.1 Identifying ALMA Sources)
   - parameter: Peak S/N >= 5 within r < 500 pc  
  > "Considering the number of independent measurements (beams) across the region of interest ($r\,{<}\,500$ pc" (3.1 Identifying ALMA Sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: False positive rate < 0.01 for any selected source  
  > "this detection threshold corresponds to a false positive rate of $<0.01$" (3.1 Identifying ALMA Sources)
3. **Measure continuum fluxes, sizes and line luminosities/widths of each source**: 2D Gaussian fits with imfit inside circular apertures; elliptical apertures from the fits to extract spectra; local background annulus subtracted; line integrated flux and effective width measured using CO(3-2) as velocity prior  
  > "This task fits a 2D Gaussian function to the flux distribution within a user-defined region." (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources); "We use the brightest line in our sample, $\mathrm{CO}\,(3\text{--}2)$, as a prior to determine the velocity range of each source" (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources)
   - software: CASA imfit
   - parameter: Circular aperture of diameter 0.34″ (twice the beam FWHM); imfit dooff=True; beam deconvolution attempted  
  > "First, we define a circular aperture around each identified source with a diameter of $0.34\ensuremath{''}$, which is twice the beam FWHM" (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources); "Note that we set the `imfit` parameter `dooff=True` in order to fit for and subtract a smooth background" (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources)
   - parameter: Isolated-source background annuli: inner/outer radii 2x/3x aperture radii  
  > "For isolated sources, the background annuli are set to have inner/outer radii of $2\times$/$3\times$ the aperture radii for source extraction." (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources)
   - assumption: Line emission is spatially associated with thermal dust, so 350 GHz apertures are used where available  
  > "as we expect molecular line emission to be more spatially associated with thermal dust emission than free-free emission" (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources)
   - fitting: Single 2D Gaussian per source (multi-component for clustered sources #5-#7, #10-#11)  
  > "we manually draw a bigger aperture that encircles all the clustered sources and ask `imfit` to perform a multi-component Gaussian fitting." (Footnote 2)
   - validation: Visual inspection of all imfit results  
  > "We have visually inspected all `imfit` results to ensure reliability." (3.2 Characterizing ALMA Sources)
4. **Cross-match ALMA sources with HST clusters and JWST sources**: HST class 1-2 clusters matched if within the ALMA source major-axis HWHM; JWST 3.35 micron catalog matched with the same criterion (closest if several), plus visual inspection of multi-band JWST images  
  > "we consider an HST cluster to be cospatial with an ALMA source if the distance between their central coordinates is smaller than the major axis half width at half maximum (HWHM) of the ALMA source" (3.3 Cross-matching ALMA–JWST–HST Sources); "we also visually inspect the multi-band JWST images (Figure [4](#fig:jwst-zoom)) near the location of each ALMA source" (3.3 Cross-matching ALMA–JWST–HST Sources)
   - parameter: HST classes 1 and 2 only (compact clusters)  
  > "focus on class 1 and 2 objects (i.e., compact clusters)" (3.3 Cross-matching ALMA–JWST–HST Sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of JWST images, esp. F200W  
  > "if a cross-matched 3.35" (3.3 Cross-matching ALMA–JWST–HST Sources)
5. **Derive gas masses from 350 GHz dust continuum**: Two-component (dust + free-free) decomposition to check dust dominance at 350 GHz; optically thin dust optical depth tau350 = S350/(Omega B_nu(Tdust)); gas mass = tau350/(kappa350 D/G) x Omega d^2  
  > "we assume the 93 GHz and 350 GHz flux densities for each object are a mixture of thermal dust and free-free emission" (4.1 Gas Mass); "After verifying that the 350 GHz continuum is dominated by thermal dust emission, we convert the 350 GHz flux density into a gas mass in two steps." (4.1 Gas Mass)
   - parameter: T_dust = 130 K (~50% uncertainty)  
  > "We use a dust temperature of $T_\mathrm{dust}=130$ K following (cite: Leroy_etal_2018), but there can be a $\sim$50% uncertainty on this value." (4.1 Gas Mass)
   - parameter: kappa350 = 1.9 cm^2/g; D/G = 1/100  
  > "Here $\kappa_{350}=1.9\;\mathrm{cm^2/g}$ is the adopted dust opacity at 350 GHz (cite: Ossenkopf_Henning_1994), and $\mathrm{D/G}=1/100$ the adopted dust-to-gas ratio (cite: Draine_etal_2007)." (4.1 Gas Mass)
   - parameter: Spectral indices alpha = 4.0 (dust), -0.15 (free-free)  
  > "with intrinsic spectral indices of $\alpha=4.0$ (dust) and $\alpha=-0.15$ (free-free)" (4.1 Gas Mass)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust (tau350 ~ 0.001–0.005); synchrotron ignored in dust/free-free split  
  > "our estimated optical depths of $\tau_{350}\approx0.001\text{--}0.005$ for all sources make this approximation appropriate." (4.1 Gas Mass)
   - assumption: Systematic gas mass uncertainty ~0.5 dex  
  > "we expect an overall systematic uncertainty of $\sim$0.5 dex on these gas mass estimates." (4.1 Gas Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Independent HCN(1-0)-based gas masses with alpha_HCN ~ 10 Msun/pc^2 (K km/s)^-1 show correlation and broad agreement (slightly higher)  
  > "Our HCN-based gas masses show clear correlation and broad agreement with the 350 GHz-based estimates" (4.1 Gas Mass)
6. **Derive stellar masses from 93 GHz free-free emission**: Treat 93 GHz flux as free-free, convert to ionizing photon rate Q0 (Emig et al. 2020 formula), then to stellar mass with a Starburst99-based conversion M* = Q0 / 2.2e46 s^-1 Msun  
  > "we choose to use our observed 93 GHz flux densities directly as free-free flux densities, which may introduce a bias of at most $40\%$ per our estimates" (4.2 Stellar Mass); "We further infer a stellar mass from the ionizing photon production rate via" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
   - software: Starburst99
   - parameter: T_e = 6000 K  
  > "Here we use a characteristic electron temperature of $T_\mathrm{e}=6000\,\mathrm{K}$ that is typical of galaxy centers" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
   - parameter: Synchrotron spectral index -0.75 for MERLIN-based bound on synchrotron fraction  
  > "Adopting a characteristic spectral index of $-0.75$ for synchrotron emission" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
   - assumption: 3 Myr old stellar population, Kroupa IMF, solar metallicity; negligible ionizing photon escape  
  > "Equation [\[eq:Q0\]](#eq:Q0) also implicitly assumes that the escape fraction of ionizing photons from the YMCs are negligible" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
   - assumption: Stellar age choice changes masses by 0.3–0.6 dex (zero age: 2x smaller; 5 Myr: 5x larger)  
  > "The main source of systematic uncertainty is the adopted stellar age for Equation [\[eq:Mstar\]](#eq:Mstar), which can introduce 0.3–0.6 dex of variations as discussed above." (4.2 Stellar Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with HST SED-based masses for six cross-matched sources; 93 GHz masses systematically lower by ~0.5 dex  
  > "we find that the 93 GHz-based stellar mass estimates are systematically lower than the HST SED-based mass estimates (median offset ${\sim}0.5$ dex," (4.2 Stellar Mass)
7. **Derive size, total mass, gas fraction, virial mass, escape velocity, free-fall time, gas surface density and Eddington ratio**: Half-light radius from imfit; M_tot = M_gas + M_star; M_vir = 5 sigma^2 R_hl/(f G); v_esc = sqrt(2G(M_tot/3.4)/R_hl); rho_tot = (M_tot/3.4)/(4/3 pi R_hl^3) and t_ff = sqrt(3 pi/(32 G rho)); Sigma_gas = M_gas/(pi R_hl^2/ln2); Eddington ratio from Sigma_gas and f_gas  
  > "Adding together the stellar and gas mass estimates (or upper limits) for each YMC candidate" (4.4 Total Mass & Gas Fraction); "The factor of 3.4 in the numerator accounts for the fraction of mass inside the FWHM of a 3D Gaussian (cite: Leroy_etal_2018)" (4.6 Escape Velocity)
   - parameter: Virial geometric factor f = 5/3 (rho ~ r^-2); sigma from HCN(1-0) effective width  
  > "We adopt a density profile of $\rho \propto r^{-2}$ following (cite: Leroy_etal_2018), which corresponds to $f = 5/3$." (4.5 Virial Mass)
   - parameter: Psi ~ 1e3 Lsun/Msun for ages <~3 Myr (Starburst99, Kroupa IMF)  
  > "where $\Psi\approx10^3\,L_\odot/M_\odot$ is the light-to-mass ratio of a central stellar population with a Kroupa IMF and an age of ${\lesssim}\,3$ Myr" (4.8 Gas Surface Density)
   - assumption: Uniform gas sphere with central star cluster for the Eddington ratio  
  > "The above is derived for a uniform gas sphere with a central star cluster, but other spatial distributions give similar results" (4.8 Gas Surface Density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Caveats on resolution-limited sizes; virial masses likely overestimated; systematic ~0.3–0.5 dex noted  
  > "they also tend to be overestimated because (1) the actual YMC sizes may be smaller than those measured at our data resolution" (4.5 Virial Mass)
8. **Classify YMC candidates and infer an evolutionary timeline**: Classify sources into four types from presence/absence at 350 GHz, 93 GHz, JWST and HST; compare number counts of ALMA YMCs and HST clusters (<10 Myr) in a matched mass range; durations proportional to counts and scaled to a 10 Myr reference window  
  > "we can classify most of them into four categories." (5.1 Nature and Evolutionary Stages); "Under these assumptions, the number count of YMCs in each evolutionary stage should be proportional to the average duration of that stage." (5.2 YMCs versus Evolved Clusters)
   - parameter: HST mass thresholds 10^4.5 and 10^5 Msun  
  > "one at $10^{4.5}\,\mbox{$\rm M_\odot$}$ to directly match the nominal sensitivity limit of the ALMA data, the other at $10^{5}\,\mbox{$\rm M_\odot$}$" (5.2 YMCs versus Evolved Clusters)
   - assumption: (1) SFR of the ring constant over 10 Myr; (2) combined ALMA+HST sample complete over matched mass range up to ~10 Myr; (3) no substantial YMC loss  
  > "Here we assume that (1) the SFR of the starburst ring does not change drastically over a 10 Myr timescale" (5.2 YMCs versus Evolved Clusters)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Agreement with previous observations and simulations; timescales described as preliminary, order-of-magnitude  
  > "Overall, these timescales agree well with previous observations and simulations of various types of star-forming regions giving birth to star clusters" (5.2 YMCs versus Evolved Clusters)
9. **Place YMCs in ring context: SFR share, azimuthal trends, outflow energetics**: Divide total YMC stellar mass by the 93 GHz-bright duration for an instantaneous SFR; examine azimuthal distribution vs ring models (popcorn vs pearls on a string); compare Starburst99 mechanical energy (with 2% retention) to reported outflow energies  
  > "Dividing these two numbers gives us an instantaneous SFR of 0.1–0.2 $\mbox{$\rm M_\odot~yr^{-1}$}$, which is already $\sim$50% of the total SFR of the starburst ring." (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context); "A mere 2% energy retention factor (cite: KimCG_etal_2020; Sirressi_etal_2024) would be enough to power the ionized gas outflow" (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context)
   - software: Starburst99
   - assumption: 4–6 Myr duration of the 93 GHz-bright phase  
  > "the inferred duration of the 93 GHz-bright phase is 4–6 Myr" (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Order-of-magnitude calculations; small-number statistics (~8 YMCs) acknowledged  
  > "Nonetheless, these trends are only based on a small number ($\sim$8) of ALMA YMCs" (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context)

## Qualitative findings

- Of 18 ALMA continuum sources, 14 are interpreted as infant star clusters and 4 (93 GHz-only) as possible supernova remnants  
  > "we identify 14 sources as infant star clusters with high stellar and/or gas masses" (abstract); "One possible explanation is that these 93 GHz-only sources are not forming YMCs, but rather supernovae remnants (SNRs)" (5.1 Nature and Evolutionary Stages)
- No coherent azimuthal evolutionary trend along the ring; YMCs concentrate near the contact points  
  > "we *do not* see a clear, coherent trend when considering all YMCs along the ring." (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context)
- YMCs are too young to power the observed hot and warm outflows now, but their mechanical energy suffices to power them in the future  
  > "The YMCs studied in this work are likely too young to be the main energy source for driving these hot and warm gas outflows." (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Very high resolution (0.1″–0.2″) ALMA data matched to the size of YMCs and to HST/JWST resolution, with short spacings to recover the whole starburst region  
  > "which matches the typical size of YMCs (cite: Ryon_etal_2017)" (2.1 ALMA Data); "We acquired ALMA Band 3 and 7 observations in Cycle 8 (2021.1.00059.S; PI: J. Sun) to highly resolve the central starburst region of NGC 3351" (2.1 ALMA Data)
- Why this method: Stringent S/N=5 detection threshold chosen for sample purity needed for number-count analysis; Briggs robust 1.0 chosen as best sensitivity/resolution trade-off  
  > "We choose this rather stringent criterion to ensure sample purity" (3.1 Identifying ALMA Sources); "with a robustness parameter of 1.0, which offers the best trade-off between sensitivity and resolution" (2.1 Calibration and Imaging)
- Alternative: Stellar-age alternatives for the Q0-to-mass conversion: a zero-age population would give masses 2x smaller; a 5 Myr age would give 5x larger; 3 Myr adopted for consistency with HST ages  
  > "the estimated masses would be 2$\times$ smaller; an age of 5 Myr would instead yield 5$\times$ larger values" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
- Alternative: Inclusion of a synchrotron component in the 93 GHz continuum was considered but not modeled for lack of low-frequency data  
  > "Considering the lack of more sensitive low-frequency data to further constrain the synchrotron spectral index and fraction" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
- Alternative: Popcorn vs pearls-on-a-string (and tidal compression) ring star formation models considered  
  > "There are at least two influential theoretical models of star-forming rings in the literature" (5.3 YMCs in Large-scale Context)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gas and stellar masses have ~0.5 dex systematic uncertainties; synchrotron contribution at 93 GHz could bias free-flux by up to 40%  
  > "the total mass and gas fraction estimates can be affected by the ${\sim}0.5$ dex systematic uncertainties" (4.4 Total Mass & Gas Fraction); "which may introduce a bias of at most $40\%$ per our estimates" (4.2 Stellar Mass)
- Limitation (authors): Source sizes are within a factor of three of the beam and may shrink at higher resolution  
  > "These observations suggest that the estimated source sizes may become smaller as one pushes to smaller beam sizes" (4.3 Size)
- Limitation (authors): HST cluster ages are uncertain especially between 1–10 Myr  
  > "which affect the accuracy of cluster age estimates primarily between $1{-}10$ Myr" (2.2 HST Data)
- Limitation (authors): Timeline rests on assumptions (constant SFR, completeness, no cluster loss) and small-number statistics  
  > "it is possible that some of the assumptions mentioned above are not appropriate for the starburst ring in NGC 3351" (5.2 YMCs versus Evolved Clusters)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Text states S_350 range of 0.6–1.9 mJy but Table 3 lists values up to 3.17 mJy (sources 15, 12)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Systemic velocity is 775 km/s in Table 1 but 778 km/s in the line-extraction text
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 15 of 18 sources are YMC-classified with 4 excluded as possible SNRs without radio follow-up; type counts are small (3-5 each)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar-mass conversion depends on an assumed 3 Myr age and no synchrotron/ionizing-photon escape; offset vs HST masses is ~0.5 dex
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Timeline depends on HST cluster catalog ages (in-prep SED fitting) and unvalidated completeness over matched mass range
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed T_dust = 130 K, kappa and D/G borrowed from NGC 253 studies; no uncertainty propagation beyond quoted systematic dex
