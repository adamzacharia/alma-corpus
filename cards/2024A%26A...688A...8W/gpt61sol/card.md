## Analysis recipe

1. **Characterize the millimeter source and gas**: Inspect ALMAGAL continuum, SiO first moment and integrated 13CO red/blue maps; reanalyze the corresponding 3 mm data set.  
  > "displays the first moment of SiO line emission along with the contours of the dust continuum at a coarser resolution" (The ALMA view of G323); "The contours of the intensity map of the blueshifted and redshifted $^{13}$CO(2–1) emission are shown" (The ALMA view of G323); "Therefore, the corresponding data set from (cite: Zhang;2023), obtained at about the same epoch, was also analyzed using CASA." (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 13CO integration: 7 to 18 km s−1 redshifted; -7 to -15 km s−1 blueshifted, printed as LSR ranges  
  > "The line emission was integrated in the LSR velocity ranges from 7 to 18 km $\rm s^{-1}$ for the redshifted gas and from -7 to -15 km $\rm s^{-1}$ for the blueshifted gas." (The ALMA view of G323)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare gas morphology with Ks nebulosity and inspect SPITZER/IRAC images for outflow signatures.  
  > "the blueshifted gas appears to coincide with the dusty ridge west of the MYSO while the redshifted emission toward the east is co-spatial with the NIR nebulosity." (The ALMA view of G323); "Inspection of the SPITZER/IRAC images does not provide evidence for a bipolar outflow as well." (The ALMA view of G323)
2. **Establish the burst light curve**: Fit VVV(X) image profiles with saturated pixels given zero weight; combine with epoch-averaged (NEO)WISE and optical photometry.  
  > "The affected pixels were given zero weight when fitting the image profile." (VVV(X) survey imaging); "This was performed using the MPFit2DPeak function of the IDL Astronomy Users Library (cite: landsmanIDLAstronomyUser1995), using a tilted Moffat function" (VVV(X) survey imaging); "Given the duration of the event and the particular time sampling of (NEO)WISE, we use the epoch-averaged magnitudes in the following." ((NEO)WISE photometry)
   - software: IDL Astronomy Users Library, MPFit2DPeak
   - parameter: Discard images with seeing worse than 3.5″  
  > "Images with seeing worse than 3.5$''$ were discarded." (VVV(X) survey imaging)
   - parameter: Mean pre-burst Ks magnitude 7.91 mag  
  > "At that date, the polynomial rise approximation started to exceed the error margin of the mean pre-burst $K_{\rm s}$-band magnitude of 7.91 mag." (IR light curves and color change)
   - fitting: Polynomial rise and exponential flux decay; their intersection estimates the peak date.  
  > "The intersection of the polynomial rise approximation and the exponential decay of the NIR flux variability suggests that the peak date of the burst was in late summer 2013" (IR light curves and color change)
   - validation: i and z photometry independently confirms the end of the burst in 2020.  
  > "Both the $i$, and $z$ bands confirm the end of the burst in 2020." (IR light curves and color change)
3. **Detect the light echo and correlate IR with masers**: Optimal image subtraction and division by pre-burst reference images; log-log regression of maser flux against Ks flux.  
  > "For each band, the pre-burst image with the smallest FWHM served as a reference." (The scattered light echo); "ratio images were created by division with the reference image, which cancels these variations (assumed to be constant in time)." (The scattered light echo); "A log-log regression yields a power-law exponent of $1.4\pm0.12$." (IR-maser correlation)
   - software: IDL, ICORE
   - assumption: Spatial extinction variations remain constant in time.  
  > "ratio images were created by division with the reference image, which cancels these variations (assumed to be constant in time)." (The scattered light echo)
   - fitting: Log-log regression  
  > "A log-log regression yields a power-law exponent of $1.4\pm0.12$." (IR-maser correlation)
   - validation: NEOWISE echo features and evolution agree with Ks images.  
  > "The features of the LEs and their evolution seen by NEOWISE correspond to what is found in the $K_{\rm s}$-band." (The scattered light echo)
4. **Measure the FIR afterglow**: Match PACS catalog fluxes with aperture growth curves; interpolate HAWC+ fluxes to PACS wavelengths and average growth-curve ratios.  
  > "Radii that reproduce HPPSC fluxes were derived by varying the aperture size on the PACS images." (FIR imaging); "the HAWC+ 62 and 154 $\mu$m fluxes were interpolated to the PACS wavelengths using a polynomial approximation of the post-burst FIR SED established from all HAWC+ bands." (HAWC+ FIR photometry); "were derived by dividing the values of the respective growth curves and taking their average." (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
   - software: DRP
   - parameter: HAWC+ calibration comparison source exceeds PACS by 15 ± 7%; taken into account in the analysis  
  > "PSF photometry of this object revealed that its HAWC+ flux exceeds the PACS flux by (15$\pm$7)%. Consequently, this was taken into account in the analysis." (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
   - parameter: Relative HAWC+ flux-calibration error 10%  
  > "This yields a relative error of 10%." (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
   - assumption: YSO FIR dust-continuum SED is continuous and featureless.  
  > "since the dust-continuum SED of the YSOs is continuous and featureless in the FIR (cf. (cite: Stecklum:2021))." (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
   - fitting: Polynomial FIR SED approximation; low-order polynomial residuals assess calibration uncertainty.  
  > "The uncertainty of the flux calibration can be assessed from the residuals of a low-order polynomial fit to the observed fluxes" (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
   - validation: 70 and 160 μm ratios agree within errors, supporting the 154 μm correction.  
  > "The ratio values agree within their errors, which a posteriori confirms the 154 $\mu$m flux correction." (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
5. **Fit the pre-burst source structure**: Generate 2500 TORUS equilibrium SEDs; average the ten best models with chi-squared-dependent weights.  
  > "In total, we include 2500 SEDs." (The static pre-burst model); "All parameters of the ten best pre-burst models (blue) are averaged

using weights according to the respective" (The static pre-burst model); "For linear sampled parameters, we use the arithmetic mean, whereas for logarithmically sampled parameters, it is the geometric mean." (The static pre-burst model)
   - software: TORUS, VizieR photometry tool
   - parameter: 11 free parameters; sampled ranges in Table 1  
  > "All parameters were sampled from the ranges given in Table" (The static pre-burst model); "The number of free parameters is as large as 11 and there are degeneracies among the models." (The static pre-burst model)
   - parameter: Dust sublimation temperature 1600 K  
  > "The inner radius of the circumstellar disk is governed by the dust sublimation radius (assuming $T_{\rm sub}{\,}{=}{\,}1\,600{\,}{\rm K}$)." (The static pre-burst model)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric geometry and 2-D cylindrical adaptive mesh  
  > "We assumed an axisymmetric geometry, and the state variables (temperature, density etc.) were stored as a 2-D cylindrical adaptive mesh." (The static pre-burst model)
   - assumption: Protostar, passive disk, curved bipolar cavity and Ulrich-type envelope

## Qualitative findings

- The G323 maser flare arose from an accretion burst.  
  > "The burst origin of the G323 maser flare has been verified." (abstract)
- A one-sided light echo propagated into the interstellar medium.  
  > "A one-sided light echo emerged that was propagating into the interstellar medium." (abstract)
- ALMA does not resolve a possible disk or show a Keplerian rotation signature.  
  > "There is no sign of Keplerian rotation for which the largest velocity differences would be at the center." (The ALMA view of G323); "The coarse resolution of the ALMA 12-m array is not sufficient to resolve a possible disk." (The ALMA view of G323)
- The 13CO morphology challenges a simple outflow interpretation; bulk-gas motion or deflection remain possible.  
  > "Although this challenges the outflow interpretation and possibly argues for bulk-gas motion, a deflection of the blueshifted lobe at the western dust rim cannot be ruled out." (The ALMA view of G323)
- The afterglow depends strongly on burst energetics and the local dust distribution.  
  > "revealed the strong influence of the burst energetics and the local dust distribution on the strength and duration of the afterglow." (abstract)
- G323 likely remains pre-ZAMS, making protostellar bloating feasible.  
  > "This suggests that G323 is still in the pre-ZAMS state at a lower effective temperature, making the bloating scenario seem feasible." (The accreted object)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA supplements the IR characterization; post-burst FIR fluxes constrain burst energy.  
  > "The ALMA continuum and line data are a crucial supplement for the IR observations to complete the characterization of the MYSO." (The ALMA view of G323); "the main objective of the SOFIA/HAWC+ observations is to constrain the burst energy by assessing the strength of a possible FIR afterglow due to the burst." (HAWC+ FIR photometry)
- Why this method: TDRT estimates thermal timescales self-consistently; flux ratios help measure the small FIR excess.  
  > "The use of the time-dependent radiative transfer code TORUS ((cite: Harries2011; Harries2019)) allows us to estimate the thermal timescales self-consistently." (Introduction); "As the excess is quite small, we fit the flux density ratios rather than the absolute values." (Burst energy estimate for the mean model)
- Alternative: Tmin provides a conservative upper energy limit but poorly fits the pre-burst FIR SED and overestimates Ks ratios.  
  > "The Tmin model has too little cold dust and cannot reproduce the FIR fluxes." (The static pre-burst model); "All bursts overestimate the observed $K_{\rm s}$ ratio (red) for the Tmin setting." (Limits on the burst energy)
- Alternative: A dust-sublimation model reduces the maximum Ks ratio by about 1.5 at fixed burst energy and shape.  
  > "The maximum $K_{\rm s}$ ratio is about a factor of 1.5 smaller for the more realistic sublimation model (blue) compared to the mean model (black)." (Reliability of the derived burst energy); "The burst energy (and shape) are the same in both cases." (Reliability of the derived burst energy)
- Alternative: MRI and thermal-instability burst scenarios have incompatible rates and timescales.  
  > "None of the bursts agree with magneto-rotational instability (MRI) or thermal instability (TI) of the disk" (The G323 burst in the context of known MYSO bursts – possible triggering mechanisms)
- Alternative: Sudden extinction changes are not indicated by the light curves.  
  > "However, the $K_{\rm s}$ light curve does not show hints of sudden extinction changes." (Reliability of the derived burst energy); "This is confirmed by the $i$ and $z$-band flux densities, which are the same within the errors after and before the burst." (Reliability of the derived burst energy)
- How they decided it was enough: Check the time step against a shorter interval; recalculate good SED fits with more photon packets; retain ten models because of degeneracies.  
  > "We used a time step of $3.65 {\,} \rm d$, which was verified with an interval shorter by a factor $\sim$7." (TORUS-Code); "Models that provide good fits were recalculated with a sufficient number." (The static pre-burst model); "The number of free parameters is as large as 11 and there are degeneracies among the models." (The static pre-burst model)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA resolution cannot resolve a possible disk; continuum sensitivity cannot constrain larger-scale dust.  
  > "The coarse resolution of the ALMA 12-m array is not sufficient to resolve a possible disk." (The ALMA view of G323); "Unfortunately, the ALMA 1.4 mm map is not sufficiently sensitive to shed light on the dust distribution at larger scales." (The ALMA view of G323)
- Limitation (authors): Saturated (NEO)WISE burst photometry is unsuitable for quantitative conclusions.  
  > "Since the (NEO)WISE fluxes in both bands exceed the limit for which a meaningful bias correction can be applied, the burst light curves are not suitable for drawing quantitative conclusions." (IR light curves and color change)
- Limitation (authors): Only three dust configurations and one burst shape were explored.  
  > "We use only three settings, which are meant to provide limits." (Reliability of the derived burst energy); "In our analysis, we did not consider different burst shapes, but only varied the burst energy." (Reliability of the derived burst energy)
- Limitation (authors): Main models simplify geometry, dust properties and burst-induced changes.  
  > "no protostellar bloating, axis-symmetry, same dust in all regions and no changes during the burst besides heating and cooling" (Prospects for TDRT modeling of MYSO bursts); "It is beyond the scope of this work to use different dust compositions or grain size distributions." (Reliability of the derived burst energy)
- Limitation (authors): Larger grains could lengthen the afterglow and make FIR-based burst energies overestimates.  
  > "If the grains are larger on average, the average cooling efficiency will be lower." (Reliability of the derived burst energy); "In that case, the limits obtained with the HAWC+ fit overestimate the burst energy." (Reliability of the derived burst energy)
- Limitation (authors): No photospheric spectral lines constrain surface gravity; pulsation and bloating remain uncertain.  
  > "Unfortunately, the K-band spectrum" (The accreted object); "does not show photospheric lines, which could be used to derive the surface gravity of the central source." (The accreted object)
- Limitation (authors): TORUS time-dependent modeling excludes line emission and chemical networks.  
  > "line emission is currently not included in the time-dependent version." (Prospects for TDRT modeling of MYSO bursts); "TORUS does not include chemical networks, which are needed to directly obtain chemical burst models." (Prospects for TDRT modeling of MYSO bursts)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 160 μm excess alone is weak relative to its uncertainty; the afterglow interpretation relies on the combined measurements and modeling.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The printed 13CO LSR integration intervals need reconciliation with the adopted systemic velocity of -67.2 km s−1.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abstract gives a lower burst-energy error of 0.8, while the main text, Table 3 and conclusions give 0.7; the answer key uses the latter.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table 1 calls the ratio 100 dust-to-gas, whereas Table 2 calls it gas/dust; this convention must be resolved before implementation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The model tables use different envelope-infall-rate conventions, including a factor 1.33 in the appendix.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The preferred energy uses the Ks fit with FIR-derived bounds rather than a fully specified joint inference.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA observations were taken during the late burst, so they are not an independent pre-burst structural measurement.
