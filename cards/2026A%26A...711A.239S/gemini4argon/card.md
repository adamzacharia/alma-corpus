## Analysis recipe

1. **Data preparation, continuum subtraction, feathering, and S/N masking**: Clean N2H+(1-0) observations with the ALMA-IMF pipeline, subtract continuum using imcontsub in emission-free channels, combine 7m, 12m, and TP data using feather, and construct pixel-by-pixel S/N maps from emission-free RMS noise.  
  > "We use the `imcontsub` task to subtract the continuum emission from the N$_2$H$^+$ line emission. To estimate the continuum emission we consider only emission-free channels, in the range of 12 to 20 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$, where we apply a linear fit using `fitorder` = 0." (ALMA-IMF datacubes); "To recover the cloud emission at all available scales, we combine observations of the 7m and 12m arrays with total power data (TP). For this, we use the `feather` task." (ALMA-IMF datacubes); "To identify reliable spectra, we construct an $\mathrm{S/N}$ map, where the noise is estimated pixel-by-pixel from the RMS in emission-free channels between 12-20 km s$^{-1}$ and 49-61 km s$^{-1}$." (Data preparation)
   - software: CASA, imcontsub, feather, ALMA-IMF imaging pipeline
   - parameter: fitorder = 0 in channels 12 to 20 km s^-1 for imcontsub  
  > "To estimate the continuum emission we consider only emission-free channels, in the range of 12 to 20 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$, where we apply a linear fit using `fitorder` = 0." (ALMA-IMF datacubes)
   - parameter: S/N threshold = 12 for N2H+(1-0); 5 for DCN(3-2), C18O(2-1), SiO(5-4); 7 for H41alpha  
  > "| N$_2$H$^+$(1-0) $^{(*)}$ | 93.173 | 0.23 | 2.59 | 2.10 | 89.3 | 12 | 6.1$\times$10$^{4}$ | 4.47 | This paper |" (Table 1); "We adopt an $\mathrm{S/N}$ threshold of 12, preserving most of the N$_2$H$^+$ structure while excluding noisy or unresolved spectra unsuitable for fitting." (Data preparation)
   - fitting: Linear continuum fit with fitorder = 0  
  > "To estimate the continuum emission we consider only emission-free channels, in the range of 12 to 20 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$, where we apply a linear fit using `fitorder` = 0." (ALMA-IMF datacubes)
   - validation: Evaluated robustness of N2H+ data cube by inspecting negative bowls and noise levels, and tested various signal_cut values.  
  > "Before fitting, we evaluate the robustness of the N$_2$H$^+$ data cube by inspecting negative bowls and noise levels." (Data preparation); "Testing various *signal_cut* values confirms that pixels below an $\mathrm{S/N}$ of 12 yield poor fits or large uncertainties in critical parameters like radial velocities errors." (Choosing input parameters)
2. **Spectral line fitting of N2H+(1-0) hyperfine structure and complementary lines, and velocity component separation**: Fit N2H+(1-0) hyperfine structure with 1 and 2 velocity components using PySpecKit specfit (n2hp_vtau), clean bad fits based on uncertainty thresholds, merge 1- and 2-component models into First (FVC) and Second (SVC) Velocity Components separated at 35.6 km s^-1, and fit single-Gaussian models to DCN, H41alpha, and SiO.  
  > "We use the hyperfine line structure model provided by version V.1.0.1 of `PySpecKit` spectroscopic analysis toolkit (cite: pyspec)." (Analysis of the N2H+ data cube); "Components below 35.6 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$ ($\sim$3,535 pixels) are assigned to the first velocity component (FVC), while those above ($\sim$2,984 pixels) are assigned to the second velocity component (SVC)." (Model cube merging); "For tracers not previously modeled (DCN, H41$\alpha$, and SiO), we apply a single-Gaussian fit using `PySpecKit`." (Complementary tracers line fitting)
   - software: PySpecKit
   - parameter: 1-component guesses: Tex=31.3 K, tau=3.8, Vc=37 km s^-1, sigma=0.78 km s^-1; 2-component guesses: Tex=(15, 20) K, tau=(1, 3), Vc=(33, 37) km s^-1, sigma=(1.4, 0.47) km s^-1; limits: Tex (2.8, 150) K, tau (0.001, 10), Vc (25, 45) km s^-1, sigma (0.23, 5) km s^-1  
  > "| Guesses | 31.3 | 3.8 | 37 | 0.78 | — | — | — | — |" (Table 4); "| Limits | (2.8, 150) | (0.001, 10) | (25, 45) | (0.23, 5) | (2.8, 150) | (0.001, 10) | (25, 45) | (0.23, 5) |" (Table 4)
   - parameter: Cleaning cuts: Vc and sigma errors < 0.3 km s^-1, sigma error != 0, tau/e(tau) > 1, tau <= 100; FVC/SVC velocity boundary = 35.6 km s^-1  
  > "Errors in centroid velocity and velocity dispersion are mostly below 0.3 km s$^{-1}$, with only 5 and 26 pixels exceeding this value, respectively. Since this threshold is conservative relative to the N$_2$H$^+$ velocity resolution, we adopt it as the maximum allowed uncertainty for $\mathrm{V_c}$ and $\sigma$." (Output cleaning); "We therefore apply a mask requiring $\tau/e(\tau) > 1$, where $e(\tau)$ is the uncertainty in $\tau$." (Output cleaning); "We additionally impose an upper limit of $\tau = 100$ for each velocity component, removing 52 pixels above this threshold." (Output cleaning)
   - assumption: Up to two velocity components adequately characterize the N2H+ spectra  
  > "The vast majority of spectra that we inspected are well characterized by either one or two velocity components (see Appendix [8](#sec:ap_linefitting))." (Analysis of the N2H+ data cube)
   - fitting: Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm optimizing the chi^2 function via PySpecKit specfit (n2hp_vtau and single-Gaussian)  
  > "To model the N$_2$H$^+$ data, we used the *specfit* fitting tool based on the built-in *n2hp_vtau* fitter." (Choosing input parameters); "*limits* are lower and upper parameter bounds used across all pixels via the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm optimizing the $\chi^2$ function;" (Choosing input parameters)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to start_pixel by performing 716 fits for R1 and 576 fits for R2 with different initial pixels, and tested different parameter limits.  
  > "We test the $start\_pixel$ parameter while keeping all other inputs fixed, performing 716 fits for R1 and 576 for R2, each using a different initial pixel. We find that the derived parameters and uncertainties are largely independent of the initial pixel." (Output parameters and dependencies)
3. **Moment maps and position-velocity (PV) diagram construction**: Construct moment 0, 1, and 2 maps for FVC and SVC, identify main filaments R1 and R2, rotate N2H+ data cubes by 45 deg with respect to the protocluster center to vertically align filaments, and generate intensity-weighted PV diagrams parallel and perpendicular to the region.  
  > "In Fig. [2](#fig:structures), we display the N$_2$H$^+$ integrated intensity (moment zero map, upper panels), velocity center (moment one map, middle panels), and velocity dispersion (moment two map, bottom panels) maps of the FVC and SVC." (Moment maps); "We use the technique developed in (cite: gonzalez2019) to construct the N$_2$H$^+$ intensity-weighted position velocity diagrams (cite: gutierrez2021; yiyo2024; nico2024). To obtain better defined structures in PV space, we spatially rotate the N$_2$H$^+$ data cubes (45$^{\circ}$ respect to the protocluster center) by vertically aligning the most predominant filaments." (N2H+ PV diagrams)
   - software: FilFinder
   - parameter: Spatial rotation angle = 45 deg; R1 and R2 box dimensions = 0.56 pc length x 0.22 pc width  
  > "To obtain better defined structures in PV space, we spatially rotate the N$_2$H$^+$ data cubes (45$^{\circ}$ respect to the protocluster center) by vertically aligning the most predominant filaments." (N2H+ PV diagrams); "| R1 | 0.56 | 0.22 | 34.4 $\pm$ 0.05 | 0.81 $\pm$ 0.05 | 1.96 $\pm$ 1.4 | 41.2 $\pm$ 7.6 | 1.78 | 0.12 | 0.56 | 6.75 |" (Table 2)
   - parameter: FilFinder parameters: smooth size = 5 pix (0.05 pc), size thresh = 550 pix^2 (0.05 pc^2), adapt thresh = 50 pix (0.49 pc), fill hole size = 20 pix^2 (0.002 pc^2), skel thresh = 80 pix (0.78 pc), branch thresh = 20 pix (0.20 pc)  
  > "| smooth size$^{\mathrm{a}}$ | 5 pix | 0.05 pc |" (Table 5); "| skel thresh$^{\mathrm{e}}$ | 80 pix | 0.78 pc |" (Table 5)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Applied FilFinder automated filament detection algorithm on FVC and SVC moment 0 maps to validate visual filament selection and test extended filament boundaries (R1-EXT, R2-EXT).  
  > "To addres this, and in order to validate the robustness of our filament selection, we use the FilFinder algortihm to independently identify filamentary structures, which we then compare to our original selection." (Alternative filament detection using FilFinder)
4. **N2H+ column density, relative abundance X(N2H+), and total H2 mass estimation**: Calculate N(N2H+) from fitted Tex, tau, and sigma for FVC and SVC after removing biased pixels, reproject N(N2H+) to the 0.83'' pixel scale of the Dell'Ova et al. (2024) N(H2) map, compute pixel-to-pixel ratio X(N2H+) = N(N2H+)/N(H2), find the mode of X(N2H+) via Freedman-Diaconis histogram binning, and convert N(N2H+) to total H2 mass.  
  > "In order to estimate the N$_2$H$^+$ column density, we use the T$_{\mathrm{ex}}$, $\tau$, and $\sigma$ `PySpecKit` output parameters (see Appendix [8](#sec:ap_linefitting)). We apply the column density approximation outlined in (cite: caselli2002a) and (cite: readelli2019), which is described as follows:" (N2H+ column density); "To estimate this ratio, we reproject the N$_2$H$^+$ image to match the pixel scale of the H$_2$ data, which is slightly larger (with a pixel scale of 0.83$^{\ensuremath{''}}$). Subsequently we calculate the relative abundance map by taking the pixel-to-pixel ratio of the two maps:" (Relative abundance); "We obtain a relative abundance of X(N$_2$H$^+$) = (0.93 $\pm$ 0.10) $\times$ 10$^{-10}$ (see Figure [4](#fig:ap_xfactmap), right panel), consistent with previous determinations (cite: caselli2002c; nico2024). This representative value is then applied to derive total H$_2$ mass." (Relative abundance)

## Qualitative findings

- Around 55% of the N2H+ spectra in G012 are well fitted by two velocity components and ~45% by a single velocity component, defining two main velocity structures (FVC and SVC) separated by ~2.5 km s^-1 at 35.6 km s^-1.  
  > "The model fitting results show that G012 is characterized by two main velocity structures separated by $\sim$2.5 $\rm km\,s^{-1}$, which we define as the first velocity component (FVC) and the second velocity component (SVC)." (Analysis of the N2H+ data cube); "Around 55$\%$ of the N$_2$H$^+$ spectra are well fitted by two velocity components, while the remainder are well described by a single velocity component." (Conclusions)
- N2H+ emission and relative abundance decrease at the center of G012 where H41alpha bubbles and C18O emission peak, consistent with N2H+ destruction by heating, free electrons, and CO from the central OB star cluster and H II regions.  
  > "Given the high concentrations of H41$\alpha$ and C$^{18}$O in the center of G012, it is likely that the central H ii regions are heating the gas in the protocluster and destroying the N$_2$H$^+$ molecule through interactions with free electrons (cite: vigren2012) and with the CO molecule (cite: bergin1997; caselli2002b; caselli2012)." (N2H+ destruction)
- DCN(3-2) emission reveals V-shaped velocity structures at the top of R2 (coincident with a hot core and perpendicular SiO outflow) and toward the southern extended part of R1 and the central OB cluster, tracing potential localized gas inflows/accretion.  
  > "DCN emission in this region reveals a clear “V-shape” structure, features seen in other filaments undergoing accretion (cite: liu2019; bonne2020; bonne2023; yiyo2024; nico2024), coincident with the hot core location (see Fig. [8](#fig:dcn_pv), right panel), along with an SiO elongated feature perpendicular to the filament related with an outflow component (cite: armante2024)." (DCN accretion evidence in the R2 filament and towards the central OB star cluster); "We identify filamentary structures traced by DCN exhibiting signatures of potential accretion toward the center of the protocluster, where the OB stellar cluster is located and N$_2$H$^+$ is largely destroyed, suggesting that these structures may represent the remnants of a former hub-filament system." (Conclusions)
- Cores in G012 are kinematically coupled to the dense gas traced by N2H+ and DCN from which they are forming.  
  > "Using N$_2$H$^+$ (1-0) and DCN (3-2) spectral lines, we estimate updated velocities for 48 previously identified cores. In general, the cores appear kinematically coupled to the dense gas traced by N$_2$H$^+$ and DCN from which they are forming." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G012 is an active, massive, evolved protocluster with H II regions, OB star clusters, and a high abundance of cores, making it an optimal testbed for studying high-mass star formation in a feedback-rich environment; N2H+(1-0) has lower depletion onto dust grains and a critical density (2.0-6.1 x 10^4 cm^-3) suited to probe cold dense gas.  
  > "Detections of HC$_3$N, CO outflows, class I methanol masers, H ii regions, OB star clusters, and a high abundance of cores indicate that G012 is an optimal testbed for studying HMSF in an evolved protocluster, particularly the mechanisms regulating the distribution and kinematics of dense gas in a feedback-rich environment (cite: haschick1983; yu2019; xie2023; armante2024)." (Introduction); "This nitrogen-bearing molecule is characterized by lower depletion levels onto dust grains and was first detected in the interstellar medium by (cite: thaddeus1975). For the N$_2$H$^+$(1-0) transition, the critical density ranges from 2.0 $\times$ 10$^4$ cm$^{-3}$ at 100 K to 6.1 $\times$ 10$^4$ cm$^{-3}$ at 10 K (cite: shirley2015), indicating the conditions under which this line becomes an effective tracer." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Combining 7m, 12m, and TP data via feather recovers missing large-scale flux and negative bowls; N2H+ column density is used to derive total H2 mass because the previous H2 dust column density map (Dell'Ova et al. 2024) lacks full spatial coverage over the main filaments; mode of X(N2H+) is used because it is stable under different binning and masking conditions.  
  > "To recover the cloud emission at all available scales, we combine observations of the 7m and 12m arrays with total power data (TP). For this, we use the `feather` task. This combination also allows us to recover the missing flux, visible as negative bowls in the interferometric spectral data produced by the lack of zero-spacing." (ALMA-IMF datacubes); "In G012, previous estimates of the H$_2$ column density map (cite: pierre2024) do not provide the full spatial coverage achieved by the N$_2$H$^+$ map." (Relative abundance); "We determine representative N$_2$H$^+$ relative abundance values using the mode of its distribution, a choice motivated by its stability under different binning and masking conditions (see Appendix [11](#sec:ap_relative))." (Relative abundance)
- Alternative: Single-component vs. two-component hyperfine N2H+ spectral fits: tested both across the cube and retained single-component fits where two-component fits produced poor fits for one component and single-component uncertainties were low.  
  > "To address this, we refit the entire cube using two velocity components and evaluate whether the fits improve both inside and outside the previous selection criteria. For spectra containing a single velocity component, the two-component model often produces poor fits for either the bluest or reddest component. In these cases, we retain the single-component fit when its uncertainties are low and the fit quality is satisfactory." (Output cleaning)
- Alternative: Using FilFinder automated skeletons vs. baseline visual box selections for the main kinematic analysis: rejected using full FilFinder skeletons for the baseline analysis because R2 splits into four branches at its lower end that cannot be reliably distinguished at current resolution.  
  > "In some cases, FilFinder identifies more complex morphologies, particularly in the R2 filament, where most of the elongated structure follows a single main skeleton, but the lower part splits into approximately four possible branches (see right panel in Fig. [13](#fig:filfinder_detection)). Although one could select only the longest path as the main spine of the filament, we consider that it would not be fully reliable to claim that this represents the principal structure." (Variations in physical parameters)
- Alternative: Alternative explanations for the R1 transverse velocity gradient (unresolved fibers/streamers, organized inflows along sub-filaments, or external H II region feedback): evaluated and rejected as primary explanations in favor of coherent large-scale filament rotation due to the smoothness and continuity of the transverse gradient and lack of V-shaped parallel PV structures in the upper R1 region.  
  > "Among the possible explanations, a coherent rotation of the filament provides the most straightforward interpretation of the observed transverse velocity gradient. While localized inflows or feedback related perturbations may contribute to the kinematics in specific regions, particularly in the bottom and extended portion of R1 near to the H ii region, the overall smoothness and continuity of the transverse gradient along the filament are most consistent with a large scale rotational motion." (Potential star-forming scenarios in G012)
- How they decided it was enough: S/N threshold of 12 for N2H+ selected by testing various signal_cut values to exclude noisy/unresolved spectra; velocity uncertainty cut of 0.3 km s^-1 chosen because it is conservative relative to the 0.23 km s^-1 velocity resolution; tau/e(tau) > 2 mask chosen for relative abundance to balance uncertainty against pixel retention (removing ~7% of pixels).  
  > "Testing various *signal_cut* values confirms that pixels below an $\mathrm{S/N}$ of 12 yield poor fits or large uncertainties in critical parameters like radial velocities errors." (Choosing input parameters); "Errors in centroid velocity and velocity dispersion are mostly below 0.3 km s$^{-1}$, with only 5 and 26 pixels exceeding this value, respectively. Since this threshold is conservative relative to the N$_2$H$^+$ velocity resolution, we adopt it as the maximum allowed uncertainty for $\mathrm{V_c}$ and $\sigma$." (Output cleaning); "To minimize the impact of large $\tau$ uncertainties while preserving most of the filamentary structure, we adopt the $\tau/e(\tau) > 2$ mask, which removes only $\sim$7$\%$ of the pixels." (Technical considerations to estimate relative abundance)
- Limitation (authors): Previous H2 column density map from Dell'Ova et al. (2024) has incomplete spatial coverage over the main filaments in G012.  
  > "In G012, previous estimates of the H$_2$ column density map (cite: pierre2024) do not provide the full spatial coverage achieved by the N$_2$H$^+$ map." (Relative abundance)
- Limitation (authors): Filament inclination angle relative to the plane of the sky is unknown, so 3D density, potential, acceleration, and rotational force ratios are 'apparent' POS quantities.  
  > "All quantities are estimated in the POS since we do not have access to inclination information. We refer to these as “apparent” profiles following (cite: gutierrez2021)." (Line-mass profiles and associated 3D model quantities)
- Limitation (authors): SFR and SFE uncertainties only include core mass uncertainties from Motte et al. (2025) and do not include uncertainties in the adopted core lifetimes; furthermore, SFR and SFE estimates are lower limits due to core census incompleteness at the 2.4 kpc distance of G012.  
  > "Errors were estimated solely from the mass uncertainties reported in the (cite: motte2024) catalog, without including possible uncertainties in the adopted timescales." (Star formation rate and efficiency); "Our estimates of SFR and SFE should be regarded as lower limits, since they are based exclusively on the population of pre- and protostellar cores traced by some specific tracers. Unlike nearby star-forming regions, more distant regions such as G012 require more complete core censuses to provide accurate determinations." (Star formation rate and efficiency)
- Limitation (authors): V-shaped PV structures can arise from different kinematic configurations (e.g., rotation, outflows, or feedback perturbations) and do not uniquely trace accretion.  
  > "While such V-shaped structures are often interpreted as signatures of gas inflow, we note that similar PV morphologies can arise from different kinematic configurations (e.g., rotation or outflows), and therefore do not uniquely trace accretion." (DCN accretion evidence in the R2 filament and towards the central OB star cluster)
- Limitation (authors): Current angular resolution (~2.3'' / ~5.5-6 kau) cannot resolve sub-pc substructures such as fibers, forming cores, or accretion streamers, nor confirm whether the four branches at the bottom of R2 are independent substructures.  
  > "Disentangling these other alternative scenarios will require higher resolution observations capable of resolving sub-pc structures such as fibers, forming cores, and accretion streamers, as well as detailed comparisons with simulations of feedback influenced filaments." (Potential star-forming scenarios in G012); "Higher-resolution data will be required to confirm whether these features correspond to dependent or independent substructures near the filament end." (Variations in physical parameters)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a single constant N2H+ relative abundance X(N2H+) = 0.93 x 10^-10 across the entire protocluster to derive H2 filament masses may introduce systematic errors in line-mass profiles, since the paper itself shows X(N2H+) varies spatially due to feedback and heating near H II regions and hot cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Separating overlapping velocity components using a hard velocity boundary at 35.6 km s^-1 can artificially truncate wings or misassign components in regions with large velocity gradients or velocity dispersions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The baseline R1 and R2 boxes (0.56 pc x 0.22 pc) isolate only sub-regions of the filaments; when extended FilFinder masks are used, R1 contains protostellar cores and the SFE ratio between R2 and R1 drops from ~5 to ~1.5.
