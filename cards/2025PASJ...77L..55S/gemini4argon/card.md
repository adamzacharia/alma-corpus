## Qualitative findings

- G0.02 is part of an elliptical structure in the longitude-velocity diagram (the mini-CMZ) corresponding to an inclined 10-pc ring rotating at ~104 km s^-1 with ~100 km s^-1 noncircular motion.  
  > "The entire ellipse is geometrically equivalent to an LV feature that represents a ring of radius R = 9.3 pc, which rotates at A = 104 km s^{-1} and expands at B = 100 km s^{-1} ." (2.3 LV Ellipse fit); "We showed that G0.02 is an edge-on view of a part of a molecular ring of radius _∼_ 10 pc rotating around Sgr A _[∗]_ in _V_ rot _∼_ 100 km s _[−]_ [1] superposed by noncircular motion of _∼_ 100 km s _[−]_ [1] ." (Section 5 Conclusion)
- The sky morphology, arc-shaped LVD, and broad lopsided line profile of G0.02 are reproduced by an eccentric orbit of gas flowing in the gravitational potential of an extended mass distribution, with velocity width driven by orbital shear near the tangential point.  
  > "However, if we cut part of the orbit as in panel G, it reproduces the observed properties such as the curved arc near the tangential direction, the arc-shaped PVD, and the broad line profile that increases toward the sharp end." (3.3.5 Eccentric motion in a spherical potential (U -type) - Panel F, G -); "Because G0.02 is located near the tangential direction of this ellipse, the width of the molecular line increases to several tens km s^{-1} due to shear in orbital velocity." (5 Conclusion)
- A compact bound cloud of radius ~1.6 pc and mass ~10^5 M_sun is tidally disrupted within one orbit in the central 10 pc bulge potential, supporting the view that G0.02 is agalactic arm or density wave feature rather than an isolated cloud.  
  > "The cloud is strongly stretched to make a spiral shape and loses its identity within one orbital rotation. Since such a compact cloud can easily be disrupted, it is generally difficult for any molecular cloud to survive disruption in the central 10 pc." (4.3.1 Tidal disruption of a cloud)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ACES provides high-resolution 12m+7m+TP cubes across the CMZ; CS (J=2-1) was chosen over H13CN (J=1-0) because CS (J=2-1) covers -220 <= v_LSR <= 220 km s^-1 whereas the H13CN internal release cube only covered -200 <= v_LSR <= 200 km s^-1, which was insufficient for G0.02.  
  > "We also have the H^{13}CN (J = 1 - 0) line at 86.3399 GHz, but the velocity coverage (-200 \le v_LSR \le 200 km s^{-1} ) in the current internal release data was not sufficient for the purpose of this paper to explore G0.02. So, we restricted our analysis to the CS (J = 2 - 1 ) line that covers -220 \le v_LSR \le 220 km s^{-1} ." (2.1 Data)
- Why this method: Test-particle orbital simulations in analytical gravitational potentials allow exploration of overall 3D orbital trajectories, PVD morphologies, and velocity shear line profiles across spherical, disc, and bar potentials.  
  > "In order to explain these kinematical properties, we simulate the evolution of a molecular cloud orbiting in the gravitational potential in the central stellar bulge." (3.2 Non-circular motion in the extended-mass potential)
- Alternative: Circularly rotating ring in a spherical potential (I-type PVD): rejected as unrealistic because it requires hydrostatic equilibrium in the radial direction and only produces a straight I-type ridge.  
  > "However, such a circular rotation is realized only if the gas disc is in hydrostatic equilibrium in the radial direction, which may not be realistic here." (3.3.2 Rotating ring in spherical potential (I -type) - Panel B (figure 3) -)
- Alternative: SN-induced local expanding shell: explains the large velocity width and high-velocity straight LV wing, but predicts a symmetric line profile rather than the observed lopsided profile and requires large energy injection (~10^52 erg); not ruled out for the high-velocity wing.  
  > "The result explains the large velocity width, but the line profile is symmetric with respect to the systemic velocity." (3.3.3 SN-induced local expanding cloud - Panel C -); "However, the high-velocity LV wing seems better explained by the SN expansion model but it predicts a more symmetric profile and requires large-energy injection." (4.2 The Line profile)
- Alternative: Expanding ring in a spherical potential (O-type PVD) driven by nuclear activity / focusing MHD wave: reproduces the global O-type LVD of the mini-CMZ and cannot be ruled out.  
  > "This model well reproduces the O -type LVD observed in the mini-CMZ shown in panel A." (3.3.4 Expanding ring in a spherical potential (O -type) - Panel D, E -); "However, the O -type LV feature is better reproduced by the expanding-ring model, and the high-velocity LV wing is explained by the SN-induced local expansion model. So, we cannot rule out these models at this time." (4.1 Summary on the simulated results)
- Alternative: Eccentric flow in a flat disc (q = 1:1:0.63) or triaxial bar potential (q = 1:0.85:0.63): considered and found to produce similar U-type / II-type LVDs and lopsided line profiles that can also explain G0.02.  
  > "The LVD behaviors are similar to that in the spherical potential (panel F), and a part of LVD may explain the LVD of G0.02." (3.3.6 Non-circular flow in disc and bar potentials (U, II -type) - Panel H, I -)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Test-particle simulations neglect hydrodynamic, MHD, and nuclear feedback effects (except in the expanding ring model) and do not perform resonance analysis for closed orbits.  
  > "The integration was performed for a given initial condition without resonance analysis, so that the motion does not necessarily realize closed resonant orbits. In the test particle simulations here, the hydrodynamic and MHD effects are not evaluated, so the results apply only to the overall orbital behavior in the gravitational potential." (3.2 Non-circular motion in the extended-mass potential)
- Limitation (authors): Solving the initial value problem for test particles does not uniquely rule out alternative models (such as expanding ring or local SN expansion).  
  > "The general agreement is that solving the initial value problem does not rule out other models, including those concluded here as unlikely." (3.2 Non-circular motion in the extended-mass potential)
- Limitation (authors): The eccentric orbit simulation reproduces the general properties of G0.02 except for the high-velocity straight LV wing.  
  > "The general property is well reproduced except for the high velocity straight LV wing." (Figure 2 caption)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ellipse fit to the LVD (Eq. 1) is done by eye/manual reading of ridge positions rather than an objective statistical fit with parameter uncertainties.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The test-particle simulations are dimensionless and matched qualitatively to the data without quantitative optimization of physical scales or viewing angles.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Collisional and hydrodynamic shocks at high orbital eccentricity in the inner 10 pc could strongly alter gas trajectories compared to ballistic test particles.
