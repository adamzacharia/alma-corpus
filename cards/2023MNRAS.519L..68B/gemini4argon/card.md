## Science

- Fields: star_formation, astrochemistry, nearby_galaxies
- Question: How do the formation and early evolution of Super Star Clusters (SSCs) in NGC 4945 proceed, and to what extent does positive feedback from the molecular outflow trigger sequential SSC formation versus quenching?  
  > "Then the study of the formation and early evolution of the SSCs in NGC 4945, when compared to the one already carried out toward NGC 253 ((cite: Rico20; Rico21; Rico22)), has the potential to provide further information on the evolution of the starburst and to establish to what extent the sequential star formation (positive feedback) also dominates at later stages or whether the SSC formation has been quenched." (Introduction); "In this work we use ALMA data to study the early evolutionary stage of the proto-SSCs using the HCN and HC$_3$N vibrationally excited emission in NGC 4945." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Select sample of 14 Super Star Clusters (SSCs) across evolutionary stages**: Select 14 SSCs out of 27 in Emig et al. (2020) by peak emission of vibrationally excited HC3N,v71 or HCN*, and by dust continuum peak at ~350 GHz with only CS and ground-state HC3N emissions and high stellar mass (10^5.1 - 10^5.7 M_sun)  
  > "We selected 14 SSCs, out of 27 found by (cite: Emig20), in two ways: i) by the peak emissions of the vibrationally excited HC$_3$N,v$_{71}$ or the HCN$^*$ and, ii) by the dust continuum peak at $\sim$350 GHz with only CS emissions." (Super Star Cluster (SSC) selection); "In the latter case, we searched for the SSCs characterized by high stellar mass (10$^{5.1}$-10$^{5.7}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$) as measured by the radio continuum emission together with high ($>$10$^{5.0}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$) or low ($<$10$^{5.0}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$) gas mass ((cite: Emig20))." (Super Star Cluster (SSC) selection)
   - parameter: Stellar mass range 10^5.1 - 10^5.7 M_sun; gas mass threshold 10^5.0 M_sun  
  > "In the latter case, we searched for the SSCs characterized by high stellar mass (10$^{5.1}$-10$^{5.7}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$) as measured by the radio continuum emission together with high ($>$10$^{5.0}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$) or low ($<$10$^{5.0}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$) gas mass ((cite: Emig20))." (Super Star Cluster (SSC) selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Positions derived from HC3N* and HCN* emissions are compared with dust continuum condensations within the beam  
  > "In most of the cases the positions derived from the HC$_3$N$^*$ and HCN$^*$ emissions are in good agreement with the condensations observed in the dust maps within the beam (see Fig. [1](#positions_regs))." (Super Star Cluster (SSC) selection)
2. **Identify molecular lines and fit LTE line profiles to derive rotational and vibrational temperatures (T_rot and T_vib)**: Simulate line profiles under LTE conditions including opacity effects for a given source size using MADCUBA SLIM; derive T_vib from transitions with the same J across different vibrational states, and T_rot from rotational transitions within the same vibrational state (or from CS and HC3N,v0)  
  > "We used the MADCUBA’s tool SLIM (Spectral Line Identification and Modelling; (cite: Martin19)) to identify the molecular lines and to perform the Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) analysis." (Analysis); "We derive the excitation temperature of the vibrationally excited levels (hereafter, $T_{\rm vib}$) by only considered transitions with the same J but arising from different vibrationally excited states." (Analysis); "To derive the excitation temperature of the rotational levels within the vibrational states (hereafter, $T_{\rm rot}$) we combined all the rotational transitions arising from the same vibrationally states (ground state, $v_{\rm 71}$, $v_{\rm 61}$ and $v_{\rm 72}$)." (Analysis)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM, AUTOFIT
   - parameter: Systemic velocity = 550 km s^-1  
  > "Throughout the paper, we adopted a systemic velocity of 550 km s$^{-1}$, based on CO observations ((cite: Dahlem93))." (Data reduction)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE); T_vib ~ T_dust because HC3N* is mid-IR pumped and mid-IR dust emission is optically thick  
  > "SLIM simulates the line profiles under LTE conditions, including opacity effects for a given source size." (Analysis); "Since HC$_3$N$^*$ transitions are excited through the absorption of mid-IR photons and, at these wavelengths the dust emission is optically thick, the vibrational temperature is expected to closely reflect the dust temperature ($T_{\rm vib}$$\sim$$T_{\rm dust}$)." (Analysis)
   - fitting: Non-linear least-squares fit using the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm in SLIM AUTOFIT  
  > "The AUTOFIT tool in SLIM performs a non-linear least squared fit of the LTE line profiles to the data using Levenberg-Marquartd algorithm." (Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Estimate lower and upper limits to the proto-SSC sizes**: Estimate lower limit to proto-HC3N size from line intensities of v71 rotational transitions assuming optically thick emission; for non-detections of HC3N*, derive upper limit to size from HC3N,v0 line intensity assuming the T_vib upper limit  
  > "We estimated a lower limit to the proto-HC$_3$N size from the line intensities of the rotational transition from the $v_{\rm 71}$ by assuming that their emission is optically thick." (Size of the proto-SSCs); "When HC$_3$N$^*$ was not detected (i.e., proto-HCN and ZAMS-SSCs), we derived an upper limit to the size using the line intensity of the HC$_3$N,v$_0$, assuming the $T_{\rm vib}$ ($\sim$$T_{\rm dust}$) upper limit in Table [2](#Input_data)." (Size of the proto-SSCs)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM
   - assumption: Optically thick emission for v71 transitions; D ~ 3.8 Mpc  
  > "We estimated a lower limit to the proto-HC$_3$N size from the line intensities of the rotational transition from the $v_{\rm 71}$ by assuming that their emission is optically thick." (Size of the proto-SSCs); "NGC 4945, the closest (D$\sim$3.8 Mpc) composite galaxy in which an AGN and SB co-exist" (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive apparent and greenhouse-corrected protostellar luminosities (L_p*), protostellar masses (M_p*), and SSC evolutionary ages (t_age)**: Compute apparent luminosity via Stefan-Boltzmann law L_app = 4 * pi * r^2 * sigma * T_vib^4; apply greenhouse correction factor 4/psi ~ 1/10 to get L_p* = (4/psi) * L_app; estimate ages from L_p*/L_ZAMS using t_age = 10^5 / (1 + L_p*/L_ZAMS) yr  
  > "We estimated the apparent luminosity of the proto-SSCs, $L_{\rm app}$, from the $T_{\rm vib}$ ($\sim$$T_{\rm dust}$; Table [2](#Input_data)) and the lower (upper) sizes (Table [3](#Params_physical)) using the Stefan-Boltzmann law: $L_{\rm app}$= 4$\pi$$r^2$ $\sigma$ $T_{\rm vib}^4$." (Luminosities and masses); "Our apparent luminosity can be corrected of the green-house effect applying the correction factor 4/$\psi$ to estimate the actual luminosity of proto-stars ($L_{\rm p*}$) as $L_{\rm p*}$ = 4/$\psi$ $\times$ $L_{\rm app}$." (Luminosities and masses); "Column (7): Estimated age of SSCs: for $L_{\rm p*}$/$L_{\rm ZAMS}$$\gtrsim$0.05, $t_{\rm age}$=10$^5$/(1 + $L_{\rm p*}$/$L_{\rm ZAMS}$) while for $L_{\rm p*}$/$L_{\rm ZAMS}$$<$0.05, $t_{\rm age}$$\gtrsim$10$^5$ yr (assuming the timescale of an HII region to be $\sim$10$^5$ yr)." (Table 3)
   - parameter: Greenhouse correction factor 4/psi ~ 1/10; HII region timescale ~ 10^5 yr  
  > "Considering a representative value of the $N$($H_{\rm 2}$) of a few 10$^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$, and a typical density profile of the SHC with a power law index lying between 1 and 2, we estimated a correction factor 4/$\psi$$\sim$1/10 (see (cite: Rico20))." (Luminosities and masses); "while for $L_{\rm p*}$/$L_{\rm ZAMS}$$<$0.05, $t_{\rm age}$$\gtrsim$10$^5$ yr (assuming the timescale of an HII region to be $\sim$10$^5$ yr)." (Table 3)
   - assumption: N(H2) of a few 10^24 cm^-2; SHC density profile power-law index between 1 and 2; light-to-mass ratio of 10^3 L_sun M_sun^-1  
  > "Considering a representative value of the $N$($H_{\rm 2}$) of a few 10$^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$, and a typical density profile of the SHC with a power law index lying between 1 and 2, we estimated a correction factor 4/$\psi$$\sim$1/10 (see (cite: Rico20))." (Luminosities and masses); "Assuming the same light-to-mass ratio of 10$^3$ $L_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$ $M_{\ensuremath{\odot}}$$^{-1}$ used by (cite: Leroy18) to the derive the mass in stars in the ZAMS-SSCs, we can also estimate the mass in protostars, $M_{\rm p*}$ of the SSCs." (Luminosities and masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Analyze spatial distribution of SSCs relative to the molecular outflow and correlate T_rot with SSC evolutionary age**: Correlate T_rot(v0) with t_age and compare deprojected galactocentric distances of SSCs with the CO molecular outflow lobes from Bolatto et al. (2021)  
  > "We have also identified a clear linear trend ($\rho$$\sim$0.7) between the rotational temperature derived from the HC$_3$N,v$_0$ line with the evolutionary stage of the SSCs, as illustrated in Fig. [3](#fig_pos_Trot_2)." (The effect of the SSC evolution on the excitation of the parent cloud); "Furthermore, it is remarkable that the proto-HC$_3$N are just bordering the outer edges of the molecular outflow observed in CO by (cite: Bolatto21), as clearly illustrated in Fig. [4](#outflow_map)." (Triggering the SSC formation. Positive feedback)
   - parameter: Inner disk inclination ~ 62 deg  
  > "Adopting an inclination of $\sim$62$^\circ$ for the inner disk ((cite: Chou07)), we derived a deprojected distance of $\sim$65 and $\sim$80 pc, respectively." (Triggering the SSC formation. Positive feedback)
   - assumption: Inner disk inclination of ~62 deg from Chou et al. (2007)  
  > "Adopting an inclination of $\sim$62$^\circ$ for the inner disk ((cite: Chou07)), we derived a deprojected distance of $\sim$65 and $\sim$80 pc, respectively." (Triggering the SSC formation. Positive feedback)
   - fitting: Linear correlation (rho ~ 0.7)

## Qualitative findings

- Vibrationally excited HCN* emission without HC3N* traces the latest stages of the proto-SSC phase just before reaching the ZAMS stage (ages ~10^5 yr).  
  > "The detection of proto-SSC through HCN allows to trace the proto-SSCs phase to much later stages than in NGC 253 as traced only by HC$_3$N$^*$ emission." (HCN* emission. Tracing the latest stages of the proto-SSC phase before reaching the ZAMS-SSC stage.)
- Parental cloud rotational temperature T_rot decreases systematically with SSC age, indicating that heating by ultracompact HII regions in the ZAMS phase is very local.  
  > "The excitation of the parental cloud seems to be related to the SSC evolutionary stage, with high ($\sim$65 K) and low ($\sim$25 K) rotational temperatures for the youngest proto and ZAMS SSCs, respectively. Heating by the HII regions in the SSC ZAMS phase seems to be rather local." (Abstract)
- The youngest proto-SSCs border the outer edges of the CO molecular outflow, indicating triggering by positive feedback in the outflow-shocked regions.  
  > "The youngest proto-SSCs are located at the edges of the molecular outflow, indicating SSC formation by positive feedback in the shocked regions." (Abstract); "Furthermore, it is remarkable that the proto-HC$_3$N are just bordering the outer edges of the molecular outflow observed in CO by (cite: Bolatto21), as clearly illustrated in Fig. [4](#outflow_map)." (Triggering the SSC formation. Positive feedback)
- Proto-SSCs in NGC 4945 are more evolved and have smaller gas reservoirs than those in NGC 253, supporting a sequential SSC formation scenario across different starburst ages.  
  > "The proto-SSCs in NGC 4945 seem to be more evolved than in the starburst galaxy NGC 253. We propose that sequential SSC formation can explain the spatial distribution and different ages of the SSCs in both galaxies." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Vibrationally excited HC3N* and HCN* lines are excited by mid-IR photons and trace the deeply embedded Super Hot Core (proto-SSC) phase that cannot be observed even at mid-IR wavelengths.  
  > "However, the earliest phases of SSCs formation take place deeply embedded in the parental cloud, preventing their observation even at mid-IR." (Introduction); "In this work we use ALMA data to study the early evolutionary stage of the proto-SSCs using the HCN and HC$_3$N vibrationally excited emission in NGC 4945." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Following the LTE analysis and luminosity-ratio age-dating method of Rico-Villas et al. (2020) allows direct comparison of proto-SSC physical properties and ages between NGC 4945 and NGC 253.  
  > "Then the study of the formation and early evolution of the SSCs in NGC 4945, when compared to the one already carried out toward NGC 253 ((cite: Rico20; Rico21; Rico22)), has the potential to provide further information on the evolution of the starburst" (Introduction); "Following the procedure depicted by (cite: Rico20), we first focus on the determination of the physical parameters (e.g., excitation temperature, column density, radial velocity, line width and source size) of the proto-HC$_3$N in the SHC phase." (Analysis)
- Alternative: Bar-driven gas inflow triggering star formation: rejected because inflow operates from 300 pc down to ~100 pc, whereas the selected SSCs lie at <100 pc well inside the region disturbed by the outflow.  
  > "(cite: Henkel18) proposed the presence of an inflow of gas in NGC 4945 through a bar from (deprojected) galactocentric radii of 300 pc to $\sim$100 pc toward the nuclear disk, while the central parts ($\lesssim$100 pc) are disturbed by the outflowing gas. Our SSCs fall well within the outflow region, which makes less feasible that the inflow could induce star-formation." (Triggering the SSC formation. Positive feedback)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): For proto-HCN and ZAMS-SSCs where HC3N* is not detected, only upper limits on T_vib, source size, and L_p* (and lower limits on age) can be derived.  
  > "For the proto-HCN, only traced by the HCN$^*$ lines, we can only derive an upper limit to $T_{\rm vib}$ ($\sim$$T_{\rm dust}$) from the LTE analysis of HC$_3$N J=11-10 (i.e., 100 GHz; see Table [2](#Input_data))." (Analysis); "When HC$_3$N$^*$ was not detected (i.e., proto-HCN and ZAMS-SSCs), we derived an upper limit to the size using the line intensity of the HC$_3$N,v$_0$, assuming the $T_{\rm vib}$ ($\sim$$T_{\rm dust}$) upper limit in Table [2](#Input_data)." (Size of the proto-SSCs)
- Limitation (authors): Proto-HC3N source sizes are lower limits because they are derived assuming optically thick v71 emission, making apparent luminosities lower limits prior to the greenhouse correction.  
  > "We estimated a lower limit to the proto-HC$_3$N size from the line intensities of the rotational transition from the $v_{\rm 71}$ by assuming that their emission is optically thick." (Size of the proto-SSCs); "These apparent luminosities of the proto-SSCs are, in principle, lower limits to the actual luminosity since we have used a lower limit to their sizes." (Luminosities and masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The greenhouse correction factor (4/psi ~ 1/10) is adopted as a single representative value rather than modeled per source, introducing systematic uncertainty in L_p* and t_age.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Angular resolution differs by a factor of ~3 between ~100 GHz (0.14'' x 0.11'') and ~350 GHz (0.42'' x 0.37''), which can affect multi-transition LTE excitation fits if beam dilution and source structure are not matched.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Noticeable arithmetic/typographical inconsistency in Table 3 for proto-HC3N-6: L_p* ~ 0.84 and L_ZAMS = 0.20 would give L_p*/L_ZAMS = 4.2 rather than the printed ~0.42 (likely L_p* is 0.084 x 10^8 L_sun given T_vib=197 K and Size>0.36 pc).
