## Qualitative findings

- When considered as an ensemble, the resolved KS relation for Virgo cluster disk galaxies is consistent with that of field galaxies in HERACLES.  
  > "Figure [2](#fig:global-ks-inc) therefore demonstrates visually the first main conclusion of this paper: when considered as an ensemble, the KS relation for Virgo cluster disk galaxies is consistent with field galaxies." (Ensemble Star Formation Relations in VERTICO)
- There are no significant differences in the ensemble KS parameters between 720 pc (9") and 1.2 kpc (15") resolution.  
  > "These results indicate that there are no significant differences in the KS parameters as a consequence of working at different resolutions, these agree well within the uncertainties of the data." (Ensemble Star Formation Relations in VERTICO)
- HI-deficient galaxies in Virgo exhibit a steeper resolved KS slope, lower molecular gas star formation efficiencies, and sit ~0.60 dex below the star formation main sequence compared to HI-normal cluster galaxies.  
  > "In addition, we find that the HI-deficient galaxies in the Virgo cluster show a steeper resolved KS relation and lower molecular gas efficiencies than HI-normal cluster galaxies." (abstract); "HI-deficient galaxies also have global SFRs that are, on average, 0.60 dex lower than those of galaxies on the SFMS, for a fixed stellar mass." (The KS relation in HI-deficient environments)
- Individual Virgo disk galaxies show large galaxy-to-galaxy variation in KS slopes (0.69 to 1.40, with 67% sub-linear) and molecular gas star formation efficiencies (varying by a factor of ~4), accounting for ~0.20 dex of the 0.42 dex ensemble scatter.  
  > "In individual galaxies, we find that the KS slope ranges between 0.69 and 1.40, and typical star formation efficiencies of molecular gas that can vary from galaxy to galaxy by a factor of $\sim 4$. These galaxy-to-galaxy variations account for $\sim 0.20\,\mathrm{dex}$ in scatter in the ensemble KS relation, which is characterized by a $0.42\,\mathrm{dex}$ scatter." (abstract); "While the slope index $N$ is very close or compatible with unity, we note that the majority of our sample (about 67%) is characterized by sub-linear slopes." (The KS relation for individual galaxies)
- NGC 4501 and NGC 4579 show bimodal KS behavior where their central regions (r < 1.5 kpc) have much longer depletion times than their outer disks.  
  > "There are two additional galaxies, NGC 4501 and NGC 4579, which present a clear bimodal behavior for different galactic environments. Figure [5](#fig:indiv_ks) indicates that their galaxy centers, within approximately 1.5 kpc, are characterized by much larger depletion times than those seen in their overall disks ($r>1.5\,$kpc)." (The KS relation for individual galaxies)
- NGC 4383 and NGC 4654 exhibit strong azimuthal variations in molecular gas depletion times linked to tidal interactions and ram-pressure stripping, respectively.  
  > "We observe clear azimuthal variations of the molecular gas depletion times in two of our galaxies, NGC 4383 and NGC 4654. These can be directly linked to the presence of gravitational tidal interactions and ram-pressure stripping of the gas, respectively, and provide additional evidence that the cluster environment directly affects the resolved physical properties of the molecular gas." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: VERTICO provides homogeneous sub-kpc (720 pc) CO(2-1) mapping across a statistically significant sample of 51 Virgo cluster galaxies actively affected by ram pressure stripping, starvation, and tidal interactions, while excluding i > 80 deg minimizes projection and extinction effects.  
  > "While recent efforts have been made in this direction, VERTICO is the first survey probing the molecular gas content at sub-kpc scales in a complete and statistically significant number of galaxies of the Virgo cluster." (Introduction); "Our selected sub-sample only excludes highly-inclined ($i > 80^\mathrm{o}$) targets from the original dataset, in order to minimize any major projection effects." (VERTICO galaxies)
- Why this method: Least Trimmed Squares (LTS) regression accounts for uncertainties in both variables, outliers, and intrinsic scatter; WISE3 is used with a radial WISE3/WISE4 color correction because WISE3 reaches 9" resolution matching ALMA while WISE4 is a more robust SFR tracer.  
  > "We use WISE3 images to quantify the obscured SFR at an angular resolution of 9” similar to the one reached by ALMA." (Star Formation Rates); "That said, (cite: Leroy2019) show that WISE4 is a much more robust SFR tracer than WISE3, despite the higher sensitivity and resolution of WISE3 imaging. To address this we apply a pixel-by-pixel WISE3-to-WISE4 color correction to the WISE3 images." (Star Formation Rates); "In order to better quantify this relation, we perform a robust linear regression following the method presented in (cite: Cappellari2013), which implements the least trimmed squares (LTS) regression approach of (cite: Rousseeuw1984). This method takes into account the uncertainties in all variables, the presence of outliers in the data, as well as unknown intrinsic scatter in excess of uncertainties." (Ensemble Star Formation Relations in VERTICO)
- Alternative: Using a metallicity-dependent CO-to-H2 conversion factor instead of a constant Galactic alpha_CO = 4.35  
  > "It is well known that the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor depends on metallicity (cite: Wilson1995; Boselli2002; Bolatto2013; Sandstrom2013). The galaxies analyzed in this work are all massive spirals with a similar metallicity range. We thus decided to use a similar conversion factor for all of them." (Molecular gas)
- Alternative: Using WISE4 (22 um) directly instead of color-corrected WISE3 (12 um) for obscured SFR tracing  
  > "The WISE3 band is preferred to the WISE4 band at $22\mu$m for its better angular resolution, although the latter is believed to be a better SFR tracer due to less contamination from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAH) emission (cite: Boselli2004)." (Star Formation Rates)
- Alternative: Using Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) or bisector regressions instead of Least Trimmed Squares (LTS)  
  > "It is worth noting that interpreting the results from simple bisector and OLS regressions is statistically hard, since a linear slope could even result from a scenario where there is no correlation between a predictor and its response (cite: Isobe1990; Shetty2014)." (Fitting methodology and sample)
- Alternative: Weighting each line-of-sight measurement by the inverse number of measurements per galaxy in the ensemble fit  
  > "We address this issue by weighting each line-of-sight measurement by the number of independent measurements that a given galaxy contributes to the overall sample, as also described in (cite: Zabel2020)." (Fitting methodology and sample)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Fitted parameter uncertainties do not include systematic uncertainties from the choice of SFR tracer or variations in the alpha_CO conversion factor (which varies with metallicity, SFR surface density, and in galaxy centers/outflows).  
  > "We note again that these uncertainties on the fitted variables do not reflect important systematic effects such as the choice of SFR tracer or a variable $\alpha_\textrm{CO}$ conversion factor." (The KS relation for individual galaxies); "While we have adopted a constant value in this work, it is well known that the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor varies within and among galaxies." (Fitting methodology and sample)
- Limitation (authors): In the presence of a central AGN (such as in NGC 4501 and NGC 4579), SFRs derived from UV and IR tracers (and the WISE3/WISE4 color correction) are likely inaccurate, and outflowing molecular gas may have a lower alpha_CO.  
  > "If the central molecular gas is outflowing, the standard Galactic conversion factor may not be applicable in this case, since the gas is not likely to be self-gravitating." (The KS relation for individual galaxies); "However, it is challenging to estimate how the presence of a central AGN would affect the calculated SFRs, since it could directly affect both WISE3 and WISE4 filters differently, and therefore the color correction. In the presence of an AGN, the SFR derived using a combination of UV and IR tracers is likely inaccurate." (The KS relation for individual galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): Overlapping interacting galaxies NGC 4567 and NGC 4568 have matching line-of-sight velocities in their overlap region, making it difficult to separate the disk components.  
  > "In this case it is difficult to differentiate the components of each disk, since their line-of-sight velocities match in the overlapping region." (The KS relation for individual galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): NGC 4561 and NGC 4772 are only marginally detected in CO with a small dynamic range in Sigma_mol, so a power-law KS relation cannot be fitted.  
  > "It is important to note that these two targets are not representative of the full sample, since they have been marginally detected in CO in VERTICO (cite: Brown2021)." (The KS relation for individual galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): The HERACLES control sample has a flat FIR luminosity selection, under-representing FIR-faint galaxies and biasing it toward galaxies with large gas masses.  
  > "As a result, the HERACLES sample under-represents FIR-faint galaxies and it is likely biased towards galaxies with large gas mass by design." (Extragalactic CO sample: HERACLES)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-detections / upper limits in CO(2-1) are excluded by the S/N masking prior to fitting, which can censor low-Sigma_mol data and bias the fitted KS slope toward flatter/sub-linear values.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The azimuthal SFE split in Section 5.4 is chosen 'by eye' to maximize visual divergence in the KS diagram without a quantitative statistical significance test across all 37 galaxies.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a constant R_21 = 0.7 and constant alpha_CO = 4.35 across both ram-pressure-stripped outer disks and galaxy centers may systematically skew radial and environmental trends in Sigma_mol.
