  > "To calibrate the observational data, we make use of the standard ALMA data reduction and calibration scripts provided with the raw observations." (ALMA observations of the WHDF LABOCA sources); "In order to ensure our ALMA observations have recovered the full flux (and therefore the full extent) of the WHDF sources, in Fig. 1 we compare the ALMA flux density of these objects with previous LABOCA flux density measurements of (cite: Bielby2012)." (ALMA observations of the WHDF LABOCA sources)
   - software: CASA v4.7.2
   - parameter: no additional tapering or cleaning  
  > "These scripts were ran with the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA v4.7.2; (cite: McMullin2007)) package and we do not perform any additional tapering or cleaning." (ALMA observations of the WHDF LABOCA sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared ALMA flux densities against LABOCA flux densities and tested data smoothing on LAB-04 and other targets to check for missed low surface brightness components.  
  > "In the case of LAB-04, we checked whether any low surface brightness component was missed by trying a range of data smoothing but such tests for LAB-04 and other targets revealed no such components." (ALMA observations of the WHDF LABOCA sources)
2. **Measure sub-mm continuum sizes and flux densities**: Fit 2D Gaussians to the ALMA continuum maps using the CASA imfit routine to measure apparent and beam-deconvolved major and minor axis FWHMs, integrated flux densities, and peak flux densities.  
  > "We measure the apparent and deconvolved sizes of the WHDF SMGs and ATLAS quasars by fitting Gaussians using the imfit routine, which is part of the CASA (v4.7.2) package." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT); "As well as a measurement of the extent of the sources, imfit provides the integrated and peak flux (per beam) for each object which are used to calculate their SFR as detailed in Section 4." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT)
   - software: CASA v4.7.2, imfit
   - assumption: H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_m = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7  
  > "Throughout, we adopt a fiducial cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_\textup{m}=0.3$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.7$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting via CASA imfit  
  > "We measure the apparent and deconvolved sizes of the WHDF SMGs and ATLAS quasars by fitting Gaussians using the imfit routine, which is part of the CASA (v4.7.2) package." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure optical/NIR host galaxy profiles and compare extents to ALMA**: Extract radial profiles from ALMA, HST ACS i-band, and Calar Alto H-band images using IRAF imexamine, fit 1D Gaussian functions using scipy.optimize.curve_fit, deconvolve PSF/beam, and compute optical/NIR to FIR size ratios.  
  > "The measured ALMA, $i$ and $H$-band profiles presented in these figures are all obtained using the iraf (v2.16.1) imexamine routine and we fit these profiles with Gaussian functions using a non-linear least squares fitting technique with the scipy (v0.17.0; (cite: Virtanen2020)) ‘curve_fit’ module." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT)
   - software: IRAF v2.16.1, imexamine, scipy v0.17.0, curve_fit
   - parameter: HST ACS i-band resolution 0.1"; Calar Alto H-band seeing 0.9"  
  > "In Fig. 11 we compare the ALMA profiles of the WHDF sources with the profiles of their counterpart detections from a $0.''1$ resolution HST Advanced Camera for Surveys (ACS) $i$-band imaging." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT); "Fig. 17 shows a comparison of the ALMA profiles of the WHDF sources with their counterpart profiles from the H-band imaging of (cite: Metcalfe2006) with a seeing of $0.''9$, obtained with the Calar Alto Omega Prime camera (cite: Bizenberger1998)." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT)
   - fitting: Non-linear least squares Gaussian fitting with scipy curve_fit  
  > "we fit these profiles with Gaussian functions using a non-linear least squares fitting technique with the scipy (v0.17.0; (cite: Virtanen2020)) ‘curve_fit’ module." (WHDF source sizes and morphologies via CASA IMFIT)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive dust masses, FIR luminosities, SFRs, and SFR surface densities**: Model dust continuum emission as a modified blackbody to compute M_dust and L_FIR/L_IR from observed S_870 or S_1200, convert L_IR to SFR_IR, and divide by effective area or beam area to obtain galaxy-averaged and peak SFR surface densities (including recalculating ALESS SMG values for consistency).  
  > "Following (cite: Decarli2018) and (cite: Beelen2006), we calculate the FIR Luminosity of our SMGs and quasars by modelling the dust continuum emission as a modified black body." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities); "Following (cite: Hodge2019), we report two estimates of the star formation rate surface density, the integral or galaxy-averaged $\sum_{\textup{SFR}}$ and its peak value." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities); "we calculate the FIR Luminosity of our SMGs and quasars by modelling the dust continuum emission as a modified black body" (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities)
   - parameter: R_e = mean of deconvolved major and minor axes FWHM / 2; galaxy-averaged Sigma_SFR = (0.5 * SFR_IR) / (pi * R_e^2); peak Sigma_SFR = SFR_IR(peak) / (pi * a * b / (4 ln 2))  
  > "The galaxy-averaged $\sum_{\textup{SFR}}$ is given by $(0.5\times \textup{SFR}_{\textup{IR}})/(\pi R_e^2)$ where $R_e$ is the half-light radius defined as in Section 3.2 as the mean of its major and minor axes’ FWHM/2." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities); "The peak $\textup{SFR}$ density is calculated using the SFR$_\textup{IR}$ value based on the peak flux density/beam and divided by the beam area defined from its major and minor axes at FWHM, $a, b$, as $\frac{\pi ab}{4\textup{ln}(2)}$." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities)
   - assumption: Opacity law kappa(nu) = 0.077 * (nu / 352 GHz)^beta m^2 kg^-2 with dust emissivity index beta = 1.6  
  > "is the Planck function and $\kappa(\nu)=0.077(\nu/352\textup{GHz})^\beta$m$^2$kg$^{-2}$ is the opacity law, with dust emissivity index, $\beta=1.6$ ((cite: Beelen2006; Decarli2018))." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities)
   - assumption: T_dust = AGN dust temperature from Shanks et al. (2021) when available, otherwise 35 K for WHDF sources, and 47 K for z>6 quasars  
  > "In this work, we set the dust temperature to the AGN dust temperature estimated in when available and otherwise $T_{\textup{dust}}=35$K in the case of our WHDF sources (e.g. (cite: Cooke2018)). We take $T_{\textup{dust}}=47$K for our $z>6$ quasars (see e.g. (cite: Willott2017))." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF and SFR_IR / (M_sun yr^-1) = 1.49e-10 * (L_IR / L_sun)  
  > "Finally, note that for calculating SFR’s we assume a Chabrier IMF." (Introduction); "$\frac{\textup{SFR}_{\textup{IR}}}{\textup{M}_\odot \textup{yr}^{-1}}=1.49\times10^{-10}\frac{L_{\textup{IR}}}{\textup{L}_\odot}$." (SMG and quasar FIR luminosities and SFR densities)
   - assumption: Eddington limit lower bound Sigma_SFR ~ 650 M_sun yr^-1 kpc^-2 assuming gas fraction f_gas = 1 and dust-to-gas ratio f_dg = 1/90  
  > "Here, the gas fraction $f_{\textup{gas}}$ is taken to be unity as the most extreme scenario, while the dust-to-gas ratio $f_{\textup{dg}}$ is assumed to be $1/90$, as proposed for SMGs by (cite: Magnelli2012; Swinbank2014)." (SFR densities and the SFR ‘Eddington limit’)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived SFR surface densities against the Eddington limit and recalculated ALESS values using the same equations.  
  > "We find these recalculated values for the integrated SFR densities are a factor of $2.1\pm0.23$ higher than those given by (cite: Hodge2019) due to their different estimation method." (SFR densities and the SFR ‘Eddington limit’)

## Qualitative findings

- All WHDF SMGs and quasars have compact sub-mm sizes (~1-2 kpc), comparable to z~6 quasars, and are ~3 times smaller than their NIR host galaxies.  
  > "We find the sub-mm sizes of all SMGs to be small ($\approx1-2$kpc) and generally $\sim3$ times smaller than any host detected in the Near-Infra-Red (NIR)." (Abstract); "In all cases, the five SMGs are comparable in sub-mm size to the two known quasars and four $z\approx6$ quasars, also observed with ALMA." (Abstract)
- No evidence of low surface brightness diffuse spiral arms or substructure is detected in any of the WHDF SMGs, unlike the brighter ALESS SMGs of Hodge et al. (2019).  
  > "We detect no evidence of diffuse spiral arms in this complete sample." (Abstract); "There is a difference between our ALMA observations of the eight WHDF SMGs and the six ALESS SMGs observed by (cite: Hodge2019) in that the WHDF sources show no evidence of the low surface brightness spiral arms found in the ALESS sample." (Conclusions)
- There is little distinction between the unidentified WHDF SMGs and known sub-mm loud quasars in FIR luminosity, dust mass, SFR surface density, MIR luminosity, and host-relative size.  
  > "We conclude that in terms of sub-mm size, extent relative to host and SFR density as well as luminosity and Mid-IR (MIR) colour, there is little distinction between the SMGs and sub-mm bright quasars." (Abstract)
- More MIR-luminous SMGs and sub-mm loud quasars in AS2UDS tend to have lower S_100um/S_24um and S_24um/S_8um flux ratios, indicating the presence of hotter dust components in addition to cold dust.  
  > "This result is naturally interpreted if more MIR luminous SMGs and sub-mm-loud QSOs have both hot and cold dust components, compared to less luminous sources that only show a cold dust component." (Do MIR colours and luminosities distinguish QSOs and SMGs?)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The WHDF sample is a complete flux-limited sample in a central 7'x7' area that includes 2 known X-ray quasars as AGN templates, observed at 0.1" resolution with long 1572s exposures to resolve nuclear sizes and detect any diffuse spiral arms; the 4 z>6 quasars provide additional high-luminosity QSO templates at similar kpc physical resolution.  
  > "The WHDF sample is well-suited for comparing AGN and star-forming heat sources since it has the combination of high resolution to measure the size of nuclear features and long 1500s exposures to maximise the chance of detecting low surface brightness features such as spiral arms for each target." (Introduction); "The 4 $z>6$ quasars act as further high luminosity QSO templates with similar kpc scale resolution and similarly long ALMA exposures." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Comparing sub-mm sizes, host-relative extents, and SFR surface densities (including testing against the Eddington limit for star formation) tests whether SMGs differ from known quasars or require an AGN heating mechanism.  
  > "Measuring the star formation rate surface density, $\sum_{\textup{SFR}}$, of SMGs is an additional method of comparing our SMG and quasar sub-samples. $\sum_{\textup{SFR}}$ can also test whether the observed SFR surface density anywhere exceeds the ‘Eddington limit’ of star formation" (Introduction)
- Alternative: Compact FIR sizes compared to optical sizes indicating central starbursts building galactic bulges or bars (e.g., Hodge et al. 2019; Simpson et al. 2015; Gullberg et al. 2019), which the authors argue cannot be ruled out but is equally well explained by a priori predictions of ~1 kpc AGN-heated dust tori.  
  > "These authors attributed the compact size of the sub-mm emitting region of these sources to a central starburst evolving into a galactic bulge or bar. However, an alternative interpretation could be the presence of AGN which are heating the inner regions of these SMGs" (Introduction); "We note that several studies, e.g. (cite: Simpson2015; Elbaz2018; Gullberg2019; Franco2020; Gomez2021; Puglisi2021) argue that the small FIR size compared to the optical size is indicative of galaxies building their bulge. Although this scenario cannot be ruled out, it must be said that an $\approx1$kpc radius for an SMG powered by an AGN was predicted by many authors before the advent of ALMA" (Discussion: SMG and quasar FIR properties compared)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Photometric redshifts for the 5 unidentified WHDF SMGs come from AGN SED fits and have ~12% uncertainties (and for LAB-03 and LAB-10 are estimated from close companions because direct optical/MIR counterparts are undetected).  
  > "The redshifts for LAB-05 and LAB-11 are spectroscopic, the other 5 are from the AGN SED fits of ; these have considerably larger errors, typically $\approx12$%." (Initial FIR detection of WHDF SMGs and quasars); "redshifts estimated based on SED fits using optical/MIR detections of close companions to LAB-03 and LAB-10 since the direct counterpart of these sources are undetected in these bands." (Table 1)
- Limitation (authors): The [C II] emission line was missed in two z>6 quasars (J332-23 and J029-36) due to discovery redshift uncertainties and fell on the edge of the detection window in J025-33.  
  > "All four of our $z\approx6$ quasars were targeted by ALMA for \[C ii\] emission but small uncertainties in the ‘discovery’ redshifts of two of these sources (J332-23 and J029-36) resulted in the \[Cii\] line being missed." (ALMA observations of the $z>6$ ATLAS quasars)
- Limitation (authors): Errors on M_dust, L_FIR, SFR, and Sigma_SFR are underestimated because systematic uncertainties in the dust emissivity index beta and spatial variations in dust temperature are not included.  
  > "We emphasise that the errors on these quantities may be underestimated because we have not included systematic errors such as the variation of the dust emissivity index, $\beta$, which may vary within sources as well as between sources." (SFR densities and the SFR ‘Eddington limit’)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA exposure times and S/N for the WHDF SMGs are ~2x lower than for the Hodge et al. (2019) ALESS sample, which may contribute to the non-detection of low-surface-brightness spiral structure.  
  > "However, some further contribution may arise from lower S/N in the WHDF sub-mm sample compared to ALESS. This latter possibility can be further checked by significantly increasing the ALMA exposure time on the WHDF SMG sample." (Does the presence of low surface-brightness sub-structure depend on SMG FIR luminosity?)
- Limitation (authors): The AS2UDS sample used to test the S_100um/S_24um vs M_4.5 correlation has large incompleteness (48/707 in the full sample, 63% with upper limits in the 1 < z < 1.7 subsample).  
  > "We now perform a test to check if bright MIR luminosities correlate with lower $S_{100\rm \micro m}/S_{24\rm \micro m}$ because of the large incompleteness (48/707) of the sample shown in Fig. 20(a)." (Do MIR colours and luminosities distinguish QSOs and SMGs?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is an internal inconsistency between Eq. 3 (which states rest-frame integration limits of 3-1100 um for L_IR) and Table 6 (which labels the column L_FIR with rest wavelength limits 42.5-122.5 um, while the ratio SFR_IR/L_FIR in Table 6 is ~1.94e-10 instead of the 1.49e-10 in Eq. 4).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison of compact sizes and SFR surface densities between SMGs and quasars demonstrates consistency but cannot uniquely break the degeneracy between compact nuclear starbursts and AGN dust heating without direct spatially resolved temperature or high-ionization tracers.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several WHDF SMGs (LAB-02, LAB-03, LAB-10) lack direct optical/MIR counterparts at the exact ALMA position, making their host size ratios and companion-derived redshifts uncertain.
