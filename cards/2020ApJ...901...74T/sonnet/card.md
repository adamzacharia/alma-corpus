## Analysis recipe

1. **Select mass-limited SFG sample**: 3D-HST catalog; z=1.9-2.6, log M*>11 from FAST SED fits, UVJ selection for SFGs  
  > "we select galaxies in the redshift range of $1.9<z<2.6$." (2.1 Sample selection)
   - software: FAST
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF, Bruzual & Charlot models, Calzetti attenuation, exponentially declining SFHs  
  > "under a (cite: 2003PASP..115..763C) initial mass function and the dust attenuation law of (cite: 2000ApJ...533..682C) to estimate stellar masses." (2.1 Sample selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Image continuum at three resolutions and measure detections/flux densities**: tclean images with different uv selection, robust and taper; S/N from intermediate-res, total flux from low-res  
  > "We detect the 870 $\mu$m continuum emission from 74 of 85 galaxies at S/N$>$5, 62 of which have S/N$>$10, in the intermediate-resolution images." (3.1)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure FIR sizes via visibility fitting**: Fit elliptical/circular Gaussian and exponential disk (n=1) models to visibilities  
  > "we fit elliptical or circular Gaussian models to the observed data by using the `UVMULTIFIT`" (4.1)
   - software: UVMULTIFIT
   - parameter: Only S/N>10 sources (62) used for size discussion  
  > "We use only 62 massive SFGs with S/N$>$10 for discussion of the size measurements at 870 $\mu$m." (4.1)
   - assumption: Disk model: axis ratio and PA fixed to Gaussian values  
  > "We fit the visibility data to exponential disk models by fixing the axis ratio and the position angle to the values derived from Gaussian fitting." (4.1)
   - fitting: Multi-component models for fields with several sources; circular Gaussians for extra components  
  > "When more than one source is detected in the observed field, we use two- or three-component models for the fitting." (Appendix B)
   - validation: Mock sources injected into visibilities of non-detected objects; recovery tested in S/N bins  
  > "The uncertainties in the size measurements are typically $\Delta$FWHM=$\pm$0.2$''$ at S/N=5, $\Delta$FWHM=$\pm$0.1$''$ at S/N=10, and $\Delta$FWHM=$\pm$0.05$''$ at S/N=15." (Appendix B)
4. **Check missing extended flux**: Compare visibility vs image flux; stack model-subtracted visibilities for compact and extended subsamples and image with taper  
  > "we perform a stacking analysis of the model-subtracted visibilities for subsamples of 13 compact objects (excluding U4-34138 and U4-34617) and 47 extended objects, using the `STACKER` tool" (4.2)
   - software: STACKER, CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Simulated Gaussian component of Re=0.5'' recovered ~100% in tapered images  
  > "we find that one can recover almost 100% of the extended emission in the tapered images." (4.2)
5. **Compare FIR size with optical, mass and Halpha sizes; correlations**: Ratios of effective radii (16th/84th percentiles); Spearman rank tests vs galaxy properties  
  > "Then, we compute the Spearman rank correlation coefficient and the $p$-value to test for non-correlation." (4.5)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Derive spatially resolved mass/SFR and project structural evolution**: Deproject Sersic profiles (Abel transform), scale HST to M* and ALMA to SFR, compute M1kpc and Re after adding new stellar mass SFR*tau*beta  
  > "M_{_\star ,\mathrm{new}}=\mathrm{SFR}\times \tau_\mathrm{SF} \times \beta" (5.2)
   - parameter: tau_SF = 0, 300, 600 Myr (and -300 Myr)  
  > "as a function of stellar mass at $\tau_\mathrm{SF}=0$ Myr, 300 Myr and 600 Myr." (5.2)
   - assumption: beta=0.6 mass loss for Chabrier IMF; current SFR and its spatial distribution constant  
  > "We adopt $\beta=0.6$ appropriate for a (cite: 2003PASP..115..763C) IMF." (5.2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Dust emission is more compact than optical emission and half-mass distribution in most massive SFGs, indicating centrally concentrated star formation.  
  > "These results suggest that many massive SFGs intensively form stars in a compact central region, embedded in a more extended disk" (4.4)
- Mass-selected SFGs are more extended in FIR than bright SMGs.  
  > "massive SFGs are distributed over a wide range of the FIR size from $R_\mathrm{e,FIR}=$0.05$''$ to $R_\mathrm{e,FIR}=$0.8$''$ while SMGs are mostly compact" (4.3)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Visibility fitting gives homogeneous size measurements for compact and extended sources, using all information.  
  > "The visibility fitting in the $uv$ plane provides homogeneous size measurements, regardless of the compact or extended nature of the sources analyzed" (4.1)
- Alternative: Image-plane Sersic fitting rejected for primary sizes because extended sources are missed in high-res images.  
  > "In contrast, the extended object is completely missed in the high-resolution image." (4.1)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Half-mass radius may be overestimated due to dust extinction; JWST 3-4 um needed.  
  > "One caveat of our study is that we may overestimate the half-mass radius of massive SFGs due to strong dust extinction in the galaxy centers" (Summary)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is rest-optically selected; optically dark galaxies may be missed.  
  > "it is possible that some unknown number of massive galaxies could be optically dark and missed" (2.1 Sample selection)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Extrapolating constant SFR and fixed spatial distribution over 300-600 Myr is a strong assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): FIR size treated as star-formation size ignoring dust temperature/optical depth gradients
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gaussian vs n=1 profile choice affects sizes; fixed n
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Photometric redshifts for 34 galaxies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistent summary slope (-0.6 vs -0.3)
