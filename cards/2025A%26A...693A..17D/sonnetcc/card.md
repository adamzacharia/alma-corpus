## Analysis recipe

1. **calibrate and image ALMA band 6 continuum and band 3 CO cubes**: Standard ALMA pipeline calibration, then CASA tclean imaging (MFS continuum over four spws; CO cubes of all channels), cleaned to 4x dirty-image RMS, then primary beam correction  
  > "The ALMA data were calibrated with the standard observatory pipeline, and imaged using the Common Astronomy Software Application (cite: McMullin+07)." (ALMA data reduction and imaging); "We imaged the band 6 calibrated visibilities of continuum over the four spectral windows with the multi-frequency synthesis" (ALMA data reduction and imaging)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Pixel size 0.1'' / 0.15'' (continuum) and 0.1'' / 0.05'' (CO); natural weighting for continuum; Briggs robust 1.0 or natural for CO; threshold 4x RMS  
  > "The Briggs weighting with the robust factor of 1.0 was applied for the few galaxies with a high S/N CO detection, otherwise we applied the natural weighting." (ALMA data reduction and imaging); "The clean was repeated down to the threshold of $4\times$ the RMS noise level of the dirty images using the `tclean` routine in CASA." (ALMA data reduction and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **build CO moment-0 maps and spectra and measure I_CO, z_CO, FWHM**: immoments over the optimum channel range (maximising S/N of the 1D spectrum extracted in a custom polygonal aperture), multi-component Gaussian fits to the spectra; upper limits from 4 sigma RMS over ~350 km/s for non-detections  
  > "were obtained by averaging the cleaned cube over the spectral channels where the CO emission is detected, using the `immoments` routine in CASA." (ALMA data reduction and imaging); "upper limits on $I_{\rm CO}$ were derived from the $4\sigma$ RMS noise level of moment-0 maps integrated over $\sim 350~\rm km~s^{-1}$" (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
   - software: CASA, immoments
   - parameter: Custom (polygonal) aperture including all flux above the RMS and typically bigger than the beam; no aperture correction  
  > "the CO emission was extracted within a custom (polygonal) aperture including all the flux above the RMS noise level and typically bigger than the synthesised beam size." (ALMA data reduction and imaging)
   - fitting: Multi-component Gaussian, nonlinear chi2 minimisation (Levenberg-Marquardt); errors from 1000 Monte Carlo realisations of the perturbed spectrum  
  > "we performed multi-component Gaussian fitting using the nonlinear $\chi^2$ minimisation and the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm." (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses); "were estimated using the Monte Carlo approach by perturbing the observed spectrum with 1000 random realisations." (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
   - validation: I_CO from moment-0 maps agrees with I_CO from Gaussian fits  
  > "The derived $I_{\rm CO}$ agree very well with those determined from the Gaussian fitting of the CO line profiles." (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
3. **convert CO flux to L'_CO and molecular gas mass**: L'_CO from Solomon+97 Eq. 3; M_molgas = alpha_CO * L'_CO / r_J1  
  > "We then used Eq. (3) from (cite: Solomon+97) to derive the CO luminosities" (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
   - parameter: r31 = 0.77 +/- 0.14, r41 = 0.61 +/- 0.13 (from Boogaard+20, ASPECS stacking)  
  > "we applied the CO luminosity correction factors $r_{3,1} = 0.77\pm 0.14$ and $r_{4,1} = 0.61\pm 0.13$, respectively, derived by (cite: Boogaard+20)" (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 1 Msun/(K km/s pc2) (nearby starburst value); MW value 4.36 discussed as alternative  
  > "we favour the value derived for nearby starburst galaxies, $\alpha_{\rm CO}^{\rm SB} = 1~M_{\odot}~\rm (K~km~s^{-1}~pc^2)^{-1}$, given the starburst nature of our targets" (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **measure 1.3 mm continuum fluxes and FIR sizes**: Aperture photometry on the primary-beam-corrected maps; elliptical 2D Gaussian fits in the uv plane with GILDAS UV_FIT; Reff,FIR from deconvolved FWHMs; 4 sigma upper limits from dirty-image RMS for non-detections  
  > "The fits were done using the `UV_FIT` routine from the GILDAS software package (cite: Guilloteau+00)" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses); "For the continuum non-detections, we considered $4\sigma$ upper limits estimated from the RMS noise level of the continuum dirty images." (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - software: GILDAS UV_FIT
   - parameter: Free parameters: centre, flux, FWHM major, FWHM minor, position angle; Reff,FIR = half-emission radius from the circularised FWHM  
  > "leaving the centre coordinates, flux, $\rm FWHM_{major}$, $\rm FWHM_{minor}$, and position angle of the Gaussian 2D model as free parameters." (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses); "We computed the effective radius ($R_{\rm eff,FIR}$), defined as the radius enclosing half of the total emission" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - fitting: Fourier transform of an elliptical 2D Gaussian fit in the uv plane (did not converge for J1157+0113)  
  > "The fit in the *uv* plane did not converge for one target J1157+0113" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - validation: Aperture fluxes agree with uv-plane fits  
  > "The derived $S_{\rm 1.3mm}$ agree very well with those determined from the fitting of the continuum emission in the" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
5. **derive L_IR and dust masses from the single 1.3 mm flux**: Scale an optically thin modified black body to S_1.3mm and integrate 8-1000 um for L_IR; M_dust = D_L S / (kappa B_nu(T_dust))  
  > "we scaled the optically thin modified black-body (MBB) function (cite: Casey12) to the measured 1.3 mm (240 GHz) continuum flux of each target, and integrated over the wavelength range between $8~\mu \rm m$ and $1000~\mu \rm m$." (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - parameter: T_dust = 40 K; beta = 1.4; kappa_0 = 40 cm2/g at 100 um  
  > "We assumed $T_{\rm dust} = 40~\rm K$ for the dust associated with the starburst regions of our targets" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses); "we assumed $\beta = 1.4$, $\kappa_0 = 40~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$, and $\lambda_0 = 100$ $\mu$m (cite: BianchiSchneider07)" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission because E(B-V) <= 0.1; no CMB correction applied  
  > "we assumed that the emission models for these galaxies should be optically thin." (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses); "(which we have not applied)" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - fitting: No fit; uncertainties are median absolute deviations over T_dust = 30, 35, 40, 45 K and seven kappa models  
  > "We then derived the uncertainties on $L_{\rm IR}$ and $M_{\rm dust}$ as the median absolute deviations (mad) obtained when averaging over $T_{\rm dust} = 30$ K, 35 K, 40 K, and 45 K and seven different $\kappa$ models" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **measure rest-UV/optical sizes from near-IR imaging**: Single 2D Gaussian fit with GALFIT convolved with a PSF measured from bright field stars, on 10''x10'' background-subtracted cutouts; an extra component for J0146-0220 and J0006+2452  
  > "We used GALFIT (cite: Peng+02) to fit the light distribution of each galaxy with the 2D Gaussian function convolved to the instrumental point spread function (PSF)" (VLT HAWK-I data reduction and analysis); "except for J0146–0220 and J0006+2452 that required an additional component." (VLT HAWK-I data reduction and analysis)
   - software: GALFIT, ESO HAWK-I pipeline
   - fitting: GALFIT 2D Gaussian (sampler/priors not stated)  
  > "Overall, a single 2D Gaussian profile fitted well the observed light profiles for most of our galaxies" (VLT HAWK-I data reduction and analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **derive SFRs, depletion times, gas fractions and star-formation efficiency and compare with literature relations**: SFR_IR = L_IR/1e10 (Kennicutt98, Chabrier IMF); SFR_UV+IR = SFR_UV+SFR_IR; t_depl = M_molgas/SFR_UV+IR; f_molgas = M_molgas/(M_molgas+M_stars); eps_SF = M_stars(young)/(M_molgas+M_stars(young))  
  > "following the calibration of (cite: Kennicutt98) for the (cite: Chabrier03) IMF" (Dust-obscured star formation); "yield very short molecular gas depletion timescales ($t_{\rm depl} = M_{\rm molgas}/\rm SFR_{UV+IR}$)" (Molecular gas mass content and depletion timescale); "following $\epsilon_{\rm SF} = M_{\rm stars}^{\rm young}/(M_{\rm molgas}+M_{\rm stars}^{\rm young})$ (cite: Evans+09; Dessauges+19; Dessauges+23)" (Star-formation efficiency)
   - assumption: M_stars from SFR_UV x 10 Myr (dust-corrected with beta_UV), Chabrier IMF, kappa_UV from BPASS binaries at 0.5 Zsun  
  > "assuming a continuous star formation over 10 Myr, a metallicity of $0.5~Z_{\odot}$, and the (cite: Chabrier03) IMF." (Target sample and their properties)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Conclusions checked against the Milky Way alpha_CO  
  > "but our conclusions do not change when considering the $\sim 4$ times higher $\alpha_{\rm CO}^{\rm MW}$ instead." (Discussion)

- **BPASS** : kappa_UV conversion from L_UV to SFR_UV  
  > "derived using the Binary Population and Spectral Synthesis (BPASS) binary models (cite: Eldridge+17; StanwayEldridge18; Byrne+22)" (Target sample and their properties)

## Qualitative findings

- Bright 1.3 mm continuum in 9 of 12 and CO in 8 of 12 galaxies despite unattenuated blue UV slopes  
  > "Detected in nine galaxies, the ALMA FIR dust continuum emission provides constraints on their IR luminosities, obscured $\rm SFR_{IR}$, and dust masses" (Summary and conclusions)
- No broad CO component (no molecular outflow) in the available CO profiles  
  > "Nevertheless, we do not observe any evidence of outflowing molecular gas in any of the currently available CO line profiles" (Discussion)
- FIR and CO emission co-spatial with the main rest-UV/optical component in seven galaxies, offset in J0146–0220 and J0006+2452  
  > "For seven galaxies, the main rest-UV/optical bright component is co-spatial with the more extended FIR dust continuum emission and the CO emission (when detected)." (Summary and conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA dust continuum and CO provide dust and cold gas masses needed to probe star formation in these galaxies  
  > "are key to inferring their dust and cold molecular gas masses – two physical parameters that are essential to fully probe the star-formation process ongoing in the galaxies" (Introduction)
- Why this method: alpha_CO of nearby starbursts adopted because the targets are starbursts; optically thin dust emission adopted because of low attenuation  
  > "given the starburst nature of our targets" (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses); "we assumed that the emission models for these galaxies should be optically thin." (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
- Alternative: Milky Way alpha_CO = 4.36 considered; conclusions unchanged  
  > "but our conclusions do not change when considering the $\sim 4$ times higher $\alpha_{\rm CO}^{\rm MW}$ instead." (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dust masses and L_IR rest on a single ALMA band, so the FIR SED is poorly constrained  
  > "given the poorly constrained FIR SED with one single band continuum measurement" (From the FIR dust continuum to IR luminosities and dust masses)
- Limitation (authors): Galaxies are too poorly resolved for kinematic analysis  
  > "which do not allow us to perform a detailed kinematic analysis via velocity (moment-1) and dispersion (moment-2) maps given that all our galaxies are not sufficiently resolved." (From the CO emission line to molecular gas masses)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas masses depend linearly on the assumed alpha_CO=1 and the r31/r41 excitation factors taken from other samples; four CO detections are marginal (4-5 sigma, patchy)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): M_stars, SFR_UV and hence t_depl, f_molgas and eps_SF depend on the BPASS 10 Myr assumption and on an absence of an old population that is only loosely constrained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): L_IR and M_dust from a single 1.3 mm point with fixed T_dust = 40 K and no CMB correction; extremely large systematic uncertainty
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Custom polygonal apertures and channel ranges chosen by hand per target, so fluxes are subjective; ALMA beams 0.5-1.9'' are larger than the compact UV components
