## Qualitative findings

- The FLASH selection method successfully identifies strong gravitational lenses down to S500 ~ 20 mJy (such as FLASH-13 at S500 = 23.6 mJy), four to five times fainter than traditional S500 > 100 mJy sub-mm lens selections.  
  > "Particularly the lensing nature of FLASH-13, selected at a mere 𝑆500 = 23.6 mJy, confirms that our method is capable of selecting lenses at four or five times lower apparent flux densities than previous methods (Negrello et al. 2010, 2014, 2017; Vieira et al. 2013)." (4.2 Effectiveness of the FLASH method)
- There is no significant difference in the foreground-background photometric redshift difference distribution between A-, B-, and C-grade sources, and no spectroscopic evidence of foreground-background confusion among the five line-detected sources.  
  > "Although four of these five sources had already been confirmed to be lensing systems through the visual identification methods, none of the five sources with line observations provide indications of confusion between the foreground and background source." (4.1.2 Confusion between fore- and background sources); "The redshift difference distributions do not appear different between the A-, B- and C-grade sources, suggesting there is no large contribution of confusion between fore- and background sources." (Figure 8 caption)
- The foreground redshift distribution of FLASH lenses matches cosmological lensing models, showing no obvious redshift bias in the foreground lens selection.  
  > "The foreground redshift distribution appears similar to the ones predicted from cosmological models (Lapi et al. (2012); Eales (2015), assuming 𝑧𝑠 = 2.5). This suggests there is no obvious redshift bias in the foreground lensing distribution." (Figure 10 caption)
- There is no clear split in reliability R between A-grade and C-grade sources at R > 0.9, likely because positional scatter between Herschel and VIKING positions limits the fidelity of the highest reliability values.  
  > "There does not appear to be a clear split in reliability between the C- and A-grade sources throughout the sample, with several C-grade sources at reliabilities 𝑅 ∼ 0.995." (4.2 Effectiveness of the FLASH method)
- Eighteen of the 40 A-grade sources lack resolved counter-images or multiple imaging, suggesting a substantial (~45%) contribution from weak lensing (mu < 2) or group/cluster magnification bias.  
  > "The contribution of weak lensing from our sub-sample of eighteen sources without multiple imaging (FLASH-3, -12, -14, -20, -28, -30, -34, -37, -46, -47, -52, -54, -60, -61, -65, -68, -69, -71) would nearly double (18/40 = 45 %) the number of strongly-lensed sources when compared to the 𝜇> 2 strong-lensing criterion adopted by Cai et al. (2013)." (4.4 Total number of lenses in the Herschel catalogue)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: While bright lenses (S500 > 100 mJy) are easily identified, the bulk of Herschel lenses lie at faint flux densities (20-40 mJy); resolved ALMA Band 7 continuum observations at 0.15 arcsec resolution directly test the fidelity of VIKING-Herschel lens selection at these faint fluxes and require each target to lie within 10 degrees of a single phase centre for scheduling efficiency.  
  > "While the easiest lenses to identify are at the brightest flux densities (> 100 mJy at 500 micron Negrello et al. 2010, 2014, 2017), both in terms of their apparent brightness and their likelihood to be lensed, the bulk of the Herschel population – and thus also the lenses – reside at the lower flux densities (20 mJy > 𝑆500 > 40 mJy)." (1 INTRODUCTION); "In order to efficiently observe these sources, we require each source to be within ten degrees from a single phase-centre to observe all targets" (2.2 The FLASH sample: Faint lenses found through Associated Selection from Herschel)
- Why this method: Searching for foreground lenses in the VIKING Ks-band detects massive 'red-and-dead' ellipticals that SDSS misses (~50%), while calibrating the likelihood estimator on HerBS lensed galaxies accounts for lensing angular offsets. In ALMA imaging, Briggs robust = 2.0 maximizes sensitivity, and 0.5 arcsec uv-tapering boosts SNR on extended sources for flux extraction.  
  > "Bakx et al. (2020a) have found that the SDSS also misses about half the lenses, so an essential part of our method is that the search for the lens is carried out in the 𝐾𝑆-band in the VIKING survey, where the lens (often a massive ‘red-and-dead’ elliptical) is virtually always bright enough to be detected (Bakx et al. 2020a)." (2.1 Lens identification); "Then, we use CASA for imaging the uv-visibilities using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 2.0 (to maximize the depth of the observations at the expense of slightly increasing the final synthesized beam size)." (3.1 ALMA observations and reduction); "In order to test the effect of resolved observations and to facilitate aperture extraction, we also generate images with a tapering of 0.5 arcseconds." (3.1 ALMA observations and reduction)
- Alternative: Optical SDSS-based lens identification, which misses about half of the foreground lenses at higher redshifts compared to near-infrared VIKING Ks-band selection.  
  > "Bakx et al. (2020a) have found that the SDSS also misses about half the lenses, so an essential part of our method is that the search for the lens is carried out in the 𝐾𝑆-band in the VIKING survey" (2.1 Lens identification)
- Alternative: Simple Gaussian radial probability distribution for counterpart offsets, rejected in favour of a distribution calibrated on HerBS that includes additional offsets from galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-cluster lensing.  
  > "This resulted in the insight that the angular distribution of lensed galaxies is not simply described by a Gaussian distribution, but instead requires a distribution that accounts for an additional offset due to galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-cluster lenses." (2.1 Lens identification)
- How they decided it was enough: Integration times were scaled to be 50% deeper than a Cycle 2 ALMA program of 16 bright Herschel lenses (Amvrosiadis et al. 2018; Dye et al. 2018) where 2-minute integrations at ~0.15 arcsec resolution provided sufficient SNR for full lens reconstruction.  
  > "The observation depth is based on a Cycle 2 ALMA program of 16 bright Herschel sources that showed that even short (2 minute) continuum observations were enough to reveal the lensed structure with enough signal-to-noise and resolution for a full lensing reconstruction (Amvrosiadis et al. 2018; Dye et al. 2018)." (3.1 ALMA observations and reduction); "In this study (2019.1.01784.S; P.I.
