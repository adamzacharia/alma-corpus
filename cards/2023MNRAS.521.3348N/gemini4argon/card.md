# The ALMOND survey: molecular cloud properties and gas density tracers across 25 nearby spiral galaxies with ALMA

`2023MNRAS.521.3348N` | Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 2023 | first author: Neumann, Lukas | arXiv: 2302.03042 | DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stad424

## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation, astrochemistry
- Question: Characterise the impact of cloud-scale structural and dynamical molecular gas properties (surface density, velocity dispersion, virial parameter, internal turbulent pressure) on the kpc-scale dense gas fraction (traced by HCN/CO) and dense gas star formation efficiency (traced by SFR/HCN) across 25 nearby spiral galaxies.  
  > "We explore the correlations of several cloud-scale structural and dynamical gas properties with both the HCN/CO ratio, a proxy for the dense gas fraction ($f_{\text{dense}}$), and the SFR/HCN ratio, a proxy for the dense gas star formation efficiency ($\text{SFE}_{\text{dense}}$), across a sample of 25 galaxies." (Introduction); "Our goal is to characterise the impact of these cloud-scale gas properties on the amount and star-forming ability of the dense gas and its connection with local environment." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Homogenise spatial resolution, sample on hexagonal grids, and create 3D CO-based masks and integrated intensity maps**: Convolve CO(2-1) cubes to cloud-scale resolution (150 pc, and 120 pc / 75 pc for sub-samples) and CO(2-1), HCN(1-0), HCO+(1-0), CS(2-1) cubes and SFR maps to kpc-scale resolution (2.1 kpc, and 1.5 kpc / 1.0 kpc for sub-samples). Resample onto hexagonal grids (2 samples per beam FWHM at cloud scale, 1 sample per beam FWHM at kpc scale). Build 3D PPV masks from CO(2-1) by finding cores with S/N >= 4 in >= 3 adjacent channels and iteratively growing into regions with S/N >= 2 in >= 3 adjacent channels. Collapse masked cubes along velocity axis to get integrated intensities and compute uncertainties from emission-free channel rms.  
  > "At first, we convolve the data cubes to the target resolution using the respective cloud-scale resolution for the $\text{CO(2--1)}$ data and the kpc-scale resolution for the $\text{CO(2--1)}$ and $\text{HCN(1--0)}$($\text{HCO}^+\text{(1--0)}$, $\text{CS(2--1)}$) cubes." (Integrated Intensity Maps); "Then, we put the voxels on hexagonal grids, using one sample per beam (FWHM) for the kpc-scale maps, and two samples per beam (FWHM) for the cloud-scale maps." (Integrated Intensity Maps); "We first identify pixels with high signal-to-noise ratio (S/N; $\text{S/N}\ge 4$) in at least three adjacent velocity channels. In addition we build a low S/N mask requiring at least three adjacent velocity channels with $\text{S/N}\ge 2$." (Integrated Intensity Maps)
   - software: NumPy, SciPy, Astropy
   - parameter: High S/N mask threshold: S/N >= 4 in >= 3 adjacent channels; Low S/N mask threshold: S/N >= 2 in >= 3 adjacent channels  
  > "We first identify pixels with high signal-to-noise ratio (S/N; $\text{S/N}\ge 4$) in at least three adjacent velocity channels. In addition we build a low S/N mask requiring at least three adjacent velocity channels with $\text{S/N}\ge 2$." (Integrated Intensity Maps)
   - parameter: Target resolutions: 150 pc (cloud-scale) and 2.1 kpc (kpc-scale) for full sample; 120 pc / 1.5 kpc and 75 pc / 1.0 kpc for sub-samples  
  > "choosing three cloud-scale physical resolutions (75, 120, 150) associated with the CO(2-1) data and three large-scale physical resolutions (1.0  , 1.5  , 2.1  ) associated with the HCN data" (Variation with Resolution)
   - assumption: CO(2-1) emission regions encompass the regions of dense gas tracer (HCN, HCO+, CS) emission  
  > "As such, CO emission unveils the regions of molecular gas where we also expect to find emission of the dense molecular gas as traced by $\text{HCN(1--0)}$(or $\text{HCO}^+\text{(1--0)}$, $\text{CS(2--1)}$)." (Integrated Intensity Maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare stacked spectra in radial bins with binned integrated intensities  
  > "The two approaches, binning and stacking, yield very similar results within $\sim$ 10 %, on average, and without bias (cite: Gallagher2018b)." (Spectral Stacking)
2. **Compute kpc-scale dense gas ratios (HCN/CO, SFR/HCN, HCO+/CO, SFR/HCO+, CS/CO, SFR/CS) and physical proxies (f_dense, SFE_dense)**: Take ratios of kpc-scale integrated intensities W_HCN / W_CO(2-1) and Sigma_SFR / W_HCN (and analogously for HCO+ and CS). Convert to f_dense and SFE_dense using constant conversion factors.  
  > "$\ensuremath{f_{\text{dense}}}= \dfrac{\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{dense}}}}{\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{mol}}}} = \dfrac{\ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{HCN}}}\ensuremath{W_{\text{HCN}}}}{\ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{CO}}} R_\text{21}^{-1}\ensuremath{W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}}} \approx 2.1\dfrac{\ensuremath{W_{\text{HCN}}}}{\ensuremath{W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}}} \, .$" (Dense Gas Fraction); "$\ensuremath{\text{SFE}_{\text{dense}}}= \dfrac{\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}}}{\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{dense}}}} = \ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{HCN}}}^{-1}\,\dfrac{\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}}}{\ensuremath{W_{\text{HCN}}}} \, .$" (Dense Gas Star Formation Efficiency)
   - software: NumPy
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1, R_21 = 0.64, alpha_HCN = 14 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1  
  > "assuming a constant mass-to-light ratio $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{CO}}}=4.3\, \text{M}_\odot \,\text{pc}^{-2} \,(\text{K}\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1})^{-1}$ (cite: Bolatto2013) and a $\text{CO(2--1)}$-to-$\text{CO(1--0)}$ line ratio of $R_{21}=0.64$" (Dense Gas Fraction); "Similarly, $\Sigma_{\text{dense}}$ is obtained via $W_{\text{HCN}}$ adopting a more uncertain $\alpha_\text{HCN}\approx14\, \text{M}_\odot \,\text{pc}^{-2} \,(\text{K}\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1})^{-1}$" (Dense Gas Fraction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare dense gas star formation relation (Sigma_TIR vs L_HCN) against literature measurements  
  > "Overall, our HCN and SFR measurements are in agreement with previous works confirming the, to zeroth order, linear relation between HCN inferred dense gas mass and IR inferred SFR." (Dense Gas Star Formation Relation)
3. **Derive cloud-scale molecular gas properties (Sigma_mol, sigma_mol, alpha_vir, P_turb) at 150 pc resolution**: Compute pixel-by-pixel Sigma_mol from W_CO(2-1), sigma_mol from the Heyer (2001) effective line width deconvolved for instrumental response, alpha_vir from sigma_mol^2 / Sigma_mol, and P_turb from Sigma_mol * sigma_mol^2.  
  > "$\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{mol}}}= \ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{CO}}}\, R_\text{21}^{-1} \, \ensuremath{W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}}\, .$" (Molecular Gas Surface Density); "$\sigma_\text{measured} = \dfrac{\ensuremath{W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,T_\text{peak}} \, ,$" (Velocity Dispersion); "$\ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{vir}}}= 3.1 \left(\dfrac{\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{mol}}}}{\SI{e2}{\Msun\per\square\parsec}}\right)^{-1} \left(\dfrac{\ensuremath{\sigma_{\text{mol}}}}{\SI{10}{\kilo\metre\per\second}}\right)^2 \left(\dfrac{D_\text{beam}}{\SI{150}{\parsec}}\right)^{-1}$" (Virial Parameter); "$\ensuremath{P_{\text{turb}}}\approx \rho_\text{mol}\ensuremath{\sigma_{\text{mol}}}^2 \approx \dfrac{1}{2R}\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{mol}}}\ensuremath{\sigma_{\text{mol}}}^2 \;.$" (Internal Turbulent Pressure)
   - software: NumPy
   - parameter: D_beam = 150 pc (and 120 pc, 75 pc for sub-samples)  
  > "and $D_\text{beam}$ is the FWHM of the beam, i.e. 150." (Virial Parameter)
   - assumption: Constant alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun pc^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 and R_21 = 0.64; spherical clouds with radius R = D_beam / 2 and density profile rho propto r^-1 (f = 10/9)  
  > "We adopt a constant, Milky Way-like CO-to-$\text{H}_2$ conversion factor of $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{CO}}}=4.3\, \text{M}_\odot \,\text{pc}^{-2} \,(\text{K}\,\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1})^{-1}$ (uncertainty of $\pm 0.1\,\text{dex}$) as suggested by (cite: Bolatto2013) and a constant $\text{CO(2--1)}$-to-$\text{CO(1--0)}$ line ratio of $R_{21}=0.64$" (Molecular Gas Surface Density); "We adopt $f=10/9$ which assumes a density profile of the form $\rho\propto r^{-1}$ (e.g. following (cite: Rosolowski2006)). Given that the cloud-scale resolutions are at the scale of GMCs we take the beam size as the relevant size scale ($R=D_\text{beam}/2$)" (Virial Parameter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Test effect of lowering alpha_CO by a factor of 2 in galaxy centres; test across 75 pc, 120 pc, and 150 pc resolutions  
  > "we also discuss sub-samples, where we have access to higher resolutions, i.e. 75 for five galaxies and 120 for twelve galaxies, respectively. We confirm that the results do not significantly depend on the resolution at which the cloud-scale properties are measured." (Cloud-Scale Molecular Gas Properties); "Therefore, we indicate how lowering $\alpha_{\text{CO}}$ by a factor of two in the central regions of galaxies affects the data points in the correlations studied here." (Conversion Factors)
4. **Compute CO intensity-weighted averages of cloud-scale properties at kpc scale**: Multiply each cloud-scale map X by W_CO(2-1), convolve with a Gaussian kernel Omega to the kpc-scale resolution (2.1 kpc), and divide by the convolved W_CO(2-1) map. Resample to 1 sample per kpc-scale beam FWHM. Estimate uncertainties via 100 Monte Carlo realisations adding Gaussian noise.  
  > "$\langle X \rangle = \dfrac{(X\cdot \ensuremath{W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}})\ast\Omega}{\ensuremath{W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}}\ast\Omega} \; .$" (Weighted Averages); "Then we run the noise-added maps through the weighted averages procedure and repeat this process 100 times. Finally, we take the standard deviation in $\langle\Sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$, $\langle\sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$, $\langle\alpha_{\text{vir}}\rangle$, $\langle P_{\text{turb}}\rangle$ over all realisations as the uncertainty estimate." (Weighted Averages)
   - software: NumPy, SciPy
   - parameter: 100 Monte Carlo iterations for uncertainty estimation; Gaussian convolution kernel to 2.1 kpc  
  > "X is weighted with $W_{\text{CO(2--1)}}$(via multiplication) and convolved to the kpc-scale resolution (here, 2.1  ) indicated by the asterisk using a Gaussian kernel $\Omega$." (Weighted Averages); "Then we run the noise-added maps through the weighted averages procedure and repeat this process 100 times." (Weighted Averages)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare Gaussian convolution weighted averaging with sharp top-hat aperture averaging on NGC 2903  
  > "We compare the two methods for the galaxy NGC 2903 in Figure 2. While both methods lead to very similar results in the centre or along the bar, there are large discrepancies for the adjacent pixels, where the aperture method produces much lower values." (Weighted Averages)
5. **Bin kpc-scale data by <Sigma_mol> and perform Bayesian linear regression and correlation analysis**: Bin each galaxy's data into 20 equally spaced bins in <Sigma_mol> (18 bins for discs-only when separating centres). Compute bin ratios as mean(numerator)/mean(denominator). Define 3-sigma upper limits for HCN/CO and lower limits for SFR/HCN when S/N < 3. Fit log-log linear relations using Bayesian MCMC linear regression in LinMix accounting for heteroscedastic errors on both axes, intrinsic scatter, and censored data.  
  > "We bin each galaxy’s data individually, choosing a fixed number of 20 bins, equally spaced in $\langle\Sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$, over the full data range of each galaxy." (Data Binning); "Considering binned data detected if the signal-to-noise ratio $\ge 3$ and censored (non-detected) if S/N $<3$, we can define upper and lower limits on the binned data." (Data Binning); "We perform the linear regression by making use of the `LinMix` package which is based on the Bayesian approach to linear regression proposed by (cite: Kelly2007)." (Linear Regression and Correlation)
   - software: LinMix, NumPy, SciPy, pandas
   - parameter: 20 bins per galaxy (18 bins for discs only); S/N >= 3 detection threshold; x_off = {2.5, 1.1, 6.5} for {Sigma_mol, sigma_mol, P_turb}; >= 10000 MCMC steps after convergence  
  > "Here, we force the MCMC simulation to take at least 10000 steps after convergence was reached" (Linear Regression and Correlation); "We recenter the distribution in the $x$-axis coordinate to minimise the covariance between the slope and intercept, applying $x_\mathrm{off,x}\equiv \{2.5, 1.1, 6.5\}$ for $x=\{\Sigma, \sigma, P\}$ which is near the middle of the data range." (Linear Regression and Correlation); "but use 18 instead of 20 bins which results in similar bin sizes for the discs data compared to the binning of the full data." (Central Regions vs. Discs)
   - assumption: Covariance between x- and y-axis uncertainties is negligible because HCN/CO uncertainties are dominated by HCN measurement uncertainties  
  > "However, the HCN/CO uncertainties are completely dominated by the $\text{HCN(1--0)}$ measurement uncertainties. Therefore, the uncertainties between the axes show no significant correlation and we neglect the covariance term in the fitting scheme." (Linear Regression and Correlation)
   - fitting: Bayesian linear regression via MCMC Gibbs sampler in LinMix with at least 10000 steps after convergence, modelling true x distribution as a mixture of Gaussians and accounting for censored y data and intrinsic scatter  
  > "In this approach, a likelihood function of the linear regression model is built and MCMC simulations are run using a Gibbs sampler exploring the posterior distribution of the regression parameters. Here, we force the MCMC simulation to take at least 10000 steps after convergence was reached" (Linear Regression and Correlation)
   - validation: Simulate synthetic correlated data with Gaussian noise and intrinsic scatter to quantify log-scale regression bias (~10% flatter slope); compare binned fits with individual line-of-sight fits  
  > "We find that the determined linear regression slope is in fact biased towards lower values by about $\sim$ 10 %. In general, repeating this procedure for the other correlations, we find that the determined slopes are probably $\sim$ 10 % flatter compared to the true correlation, if it were perfectly correlated." (Linear Regression); "Qualitatively, we find the same results for the LOS data as for the binned data, i.e. a positive (negative) correlation between HCN/CO (SFR/HCN) with the cloud-scale molecular gas properties." (Line-of-sight Correlations)

## Qualitative findings

- At 2.1 kpc scale, HCN/CO positively correlates with 150 pc cloud-scale molecular gas surface density, velocity dispersion, and internal turbulent pressure, in agreement with single free-fall time turbulent cloud models.  
  > "We find positive correlations (negative) of HCN/CO (SFR/HCN) with the surface density, the velocity dispersion and the internal turbulent pressure of the molecular gas." (Abstract)
- SFR/HCN negatively correlates with cloud-scale surface density, velocity dispersion, and internal turbulent pressure, contradicting a universal constant dense gas star formation efficiency.  
  > "Lastly, we find systematic variations of the SFR/HCN ratio with cloud-scale molecular gas properties, which are incompatible with a universal star formation efficiency." (Abstract)
- Neither HCN/CO nor SFR/HCN shows a significant correlation with the empirical cloud-scale virial parameter across the full 25-galaxy sample.  
  > "However, including the complete sample of 25 galaxies, our data do not confidently suggest any correlation between HCN/CO and $\ensuremath{\sigma_{\text{mol}}}^2/\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{mol}}}\propto\ensuremath{\alpha_{\text{vir}}}$." (HCN/CO vs. Virial Parameter); "In our data we find no correlation ($\rho=-0.11$ , $p=0.226$) between SFR/HCN and $\langle\ensuremath{\sigma_{\text{mol}}}^2/\ensuremath{\Sigma_{\text{mol}}}\rangle$ tracing $\langle\alpha_{\text{vir}}\rangle$, suggesting that SFR/HCN and $\langle\alpha_{\text{vir}}\rangle$ are uncorrelated." (SFR/HCN vs. Virial Parameter)
- HCO+(1-0) and CS(2-1) exhibit the same positive (with CO) and negative (with SFR) correlations with cloud-scale molecular gas properties as HCN(1-0).  
  > "We find that both HCO$^+$/CO and CS/CO positively correlate with $\langle\Sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$, $\langle\sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$ and $\langle P_{\text{turb}}\rangle$ with Pearson correlation coefficients $\sim 0.8$ and negligible $p$-values $<\num{e-5}$." (HCO+ and CS); "As for SFR/HCN, we find that both SFR/HCO$^+$ and SFR/CS anti-correlate with $\langle\Sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$, $\langle\sigma_{\text{mol}}\rangle$ and $\langle P_{\text{turb}}\rangle$ with $\rho\sim 0.5$ ($p<\num{e-3}$)." (HCO+ and CS)
- Galaxy centres have higher HCN/CO and lower SFR/HCN than discs on average, and barred or AGN-hosting centres have higher HCN/CO than unbarred or non-AGN centres, but centres and discs follow the same scaling relations with cloud-scale properties.  
  > "Nonetheless, centres also have higher $\Sigma_{\text{mol}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{mol}}$, and, hence, are found to follow similar HCN/CO and SFR/HCN relations as are observed in the discs;" (Central Regions vs. Discs); "We also studied the impact of bars and AGN on the central regions of galaxies, finding typically higher HCN/CO and lower SFR/HCN for barred and AGN galaxies compared to their complements (unbarred and without AGN), respectively." (Summary & Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining ACA kpc-scale dense gas mapping (ALMOND) with 100-pc scale CO(2-1) imaging (PHANGS-ALMA) bypasses the prohibitive observing time required to map faint dense gas tracers at cloud scales across full galaxy discs.  
  > "By combining PHANGS-ALMA with spectral mapping of dense gas tracers like $\text{HCN(1--0)}$, we can explore the molecular cloud properties in the extragalactic regime and compare it to the kpc-scale dense gas spectroscopy. This technique bypasses the lack of extragalactic cloud-scale dense gas observations" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Gaussian-convolution intensity-weighted averaging preserves cloud-scale information and down-weights empty regions while matching the kpc-scale beam dilution; binning by <Sigma_mol> and using Bayesian linear regression (LinMix) recovers low-S/N HCN emission and accounts for upper/lower limits and intrinsic scatter.  
  > "The above formalism was introduced by (cite: Leroy2016) and is designed to connect high resolution to low resolution measurements such as conducted in this study, having the advantage of preserving the high resolution information and down-weighting empty regions." (Weighted Averages); "We choose this linear regression method because it accounts for non-detections, determines meaningful fit uncertainties and leads to less biased regression parameter estimates" (Linear Regression and Correlation)
- Alternative: Multi-free-fall turbulent cloud models: rejected in favour of single free-fall models because multi-free-fall models predict SFE_dense increases with Mach number, opposite to the observed negative trend.  
  > "Multi-free-fall models can predict that $\text{SFE}_{\text{dense}}$ increases with Mach number, i.e. the reverse of single free-fall models predictions and the reverse of the trends found here at low $n_0$, $\mathcal{M}$(Figure 2)." (SFR/HCN correlations)
- Alternative: Top-hat aperture intensity-weighted averaging (as in Sun et al. 2020a): rejected in favour of Gaussian kernel convolution because sharp apertures are not symmetrically affected by beam dilution when compared to convolved kpc-scale maps.  
  > "However, comparison with observations performed at or convolved to the averaging scale should only be done using the convolution based method, which is symmetrically affected by beam dilution." (Weighted Averages)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Simplified toy model assumes step-function line emissivity above a fixed effective critical density, constant conversion factors, no radiative transfer, and no power-law tail in the density PDF.  
  > "In this section laying out basic theoretical expectations, we adopt the simpler picture that HCN emission has a step-function dependence on density and emits with a fixed mass-to-light ratio, or conversion factor, above that density threshold." (Does HCN/CO trace dense gas fraction?); "Note that this formalism does not consider radiative transfer modelling and therefore only gives reasonable HCN/CO estimates in terms of comparative analysis." (Line Emissivity)
- Limitation (authors): Constant alpha_CO and alpha_HCN are assumed, which may overestimate Sigma_mol in galaxy centres where alpha_CO is lower.  
  > "Thus, by adopting a constant $\alpha_{\text{CO}}$, we may overestimate $\Sigma_{\text{mol}}$ in the central regions of galaxies and underestimate $\Sigma_{\text{mol}}$ at larger galactocentric radii." (Molecular Gas Surface Density)
- Limitation (authors): Defining galaxy centres as the single 2.1 kpc central pixel is ~3 times larger than typical PHANGS nuclear/bulge sizes (~600 pc) and may underestimate differences between centres and discs; line widths in centres may also be broadened by non-turbulent motions.  
  > "Tracing the velocity dispersion via the line width is appropriate for the discs of galaxies but may lead to biased estimates in the galactic centres (cite: Henshaw2016)." (HCN/CO vs. Velocity Dispersion); "Note that the physical size of the galaxy centres are typically a factor of $\sim 3$ smaller (median size of the centre, i.e. small bulge or nucleus of the PHANGS galaxies is $\sim\SI{600}{\parsec}$, (cite: Querejeta2021b)) compared to the 1  –2   size adopted here." (Central Regions vs. Discs)
- Limitation (authors): Converting symmetric linear uncertainties to logarithmic scale introduces asymmetric errors that bias the LinMix regression slopes to be ~10% flatter.  
  > "In general, repeating this procedure for the other correlations, we find that the determined slopes are probably $\sim$ 10 % flatter compared to the true correlation, if it were perfectly correlated." (Linear Regression)
- Limitation (authors): Significant overlap between barred and AGN galaxies prevents cleanly separating the effects of bars and AGNs in galaxy centres.  
  > "Note that there is a significant overlap between AGN and barred galaxies, so we cannot easily discriminate the impact of bars and AGN." (Impact of Bars and AGN)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Both HCN/CO and <Sigma_mol> share W_CO(2-1) in their definitions (at kpc and cloud scales, respectively), which could induce artificial correlation if CO uncertainties were not negligible compared to HCN uncertainties.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Empirical virial parameter and turbulent pressure assume a fixed cloud radius equal to half the 150 pc beam rather than resolved GMC radii, making them direct algebraic combinations of Sigma_mol and sigma_mol.
