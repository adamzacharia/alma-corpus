## Analysis recipe

1. **Concatenate and image the ALMA 2 mm mosaic**: Concatenate measurement sets of individual scheduling blocks and image collectively as a mosaic with time-bin averaging  
  > "we imaged these data using natural weighting, with a robust value $=2$ to optimize source signal-to-noise ratios, set the cell / pixel size to 0.3 arcseconds (which is well below the synthesized beam size, see below), and averaged the maps over time bins of 10s." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey); "Measurement sets for the individual SBs were concatenated together using the CASA concat command, then reduced collectively as a mosaic." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: natural weighting, robust = 2, cell size = 0.3'', time bin averaging = 10s  
  > "we imaged these data using natural weighting, with a robust value $=2$ to optimize source signal-to-noise ratios, set the cell / pixel size to 0.3 arcseconds (which is well below the synthesized beam size, see below), and averaged the maps over time bins of 10s." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that map pixel SNRs follow a standard normal distribution centered at zero with sigma = 1  
  > "As shown in Figure 2, the SNRs of the map pixels are well represented by a standard normal centered about zero with $\sigma = 1$, above which positive source peaks diverge from the distribution, demonstrating that the SNR $\ge5$ $\sigma$ threshold is an ideal threshold for low source contamination." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
2. **Extract >= 5 sigma 2 mm sources and quantify completeness, false detection rate, and flux boosting**: Identify >= 5 sigma peak pixels using a region grow algorithm; run 100 iterations of beam-convolved Gaussian mock noise maps for false detection rate and 100 iterations of artificial source injection for completeness and flux boosting  
  > "Therefore, we adopt source positions and flux densities corresponding to the point of their peak signal-to-noise by identifying all of the $>$5$\sigma$ pixels using a “region grow” algorithm (cite: Casey2018b; Cooper2022)." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey); "We repeat this process 100 times, and calculate an average of $0.7^{+0.7}_{-0.5}$ of false detections in the Ex-MORA map, with an average SNR of $5.3\pm 0.2$." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey); "Survey completeness is calculated by inserting artificial sources at random positions around the mosaic, then using the same peak finding algorithm to recover them." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
   - parameter: SNR >= 5 sigma threshold; 100 simulation iterations  
  > "We then count the number of serendipitous peaks at or above 5$\sigma$ significance. We repeat this process 100 times" (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
   - assumption: Sources are unresolved point sources because the synthesized beam (~13 x 11 kpc at z=3) is well beyond the physical extent of dust emission  
  > "This beam size translates to physical scales of $\sim 13 \times 11$ kpc at $z=3$, which is well beyond the observed physical extent of dust emission in galaxies at these epochs (cite: hodge16; Gillman2023). Thus, it is unlikely that sources are resolved out of the map, or spatially resolved in general." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: False detection rate estimate (0.7 +0.7/-0.5 at SNR 5.3 +/- 0.2) matches the one likely spurious source (eMORA.31 at 5.3 sigma) lacking multi-wavelength counterparts  
  > "This is in good agreement with estimates in and , as well as our own catalog for which we identify one likely spurious source at the same SNR as the false detection estimates (see next Section)." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
3. **Identify multi-wavelength counterparts, extract NIRCam/multi-wavelength photometry, and filter synchrotron-boosted sources**: Create 50''x50'' cutouts, generate F444W segmentation maps down to SNR-per-pixel of 3 using photutils inside diver, measure PSF-corrected multi-wavelength fluxes, and extrapolate 1.28 GHz and 3 GHz radio slopes to 2 mm to identify synchrotron-boosted sources  
  > "Next, we create a segmentation map using photutils (cite: photutils) from the NIRCam/F444W map, and in some cases include an additional de-blending step to isolate the source from its neighbors. We adopt the arbitrarily shaped segmentation boundary (down to an SNR-per-pixel of 3) of the source as an aperture through which to measure all multi-wavelength photometry." (NIRCam Photometry); "Using data from the VLA-COSMOS and MIGHTEE-COSMOS surveys, we find that $23/36$ sources have detections at both the 1.28 GHz and 3 GHz frequencies. Using these detections, we calculate synchrotron slopes and extend this to the 2 mm wavelength." (Source Selection)
   - software: JWST Calibration Pipeline, SE/SE++, diver, photutils
   - parameter: 50'' x 50'' cutouts; segmentation boundary down to SNR-per-pixel of 3 in F444W  
  > "First, we make $50^{\prime\prime}\times50^{\prime\prime}$ cutouts around the target in the science image and error/weight image from which a flux will be measured." (NIRCam Photometry); "We adopt the arbitrarily shaped segmentation boundary (down to an SNR-per-pixel of 3) of the source as an aperture through which to measure all multi-wavelength photometry." (NIRCam Photometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared diver fluxes to SE/SE++ aperture fluxes and checked consistency between ground-based and JWST data  
  > "The JWST NIRCam (F115W, F150W, F277W, and F444W) fluxes and flux errors measured with diver are generally consistent with the aperture fluxes from SE/SE++ within $1\sigma_{\rm diver}$, but not within $1\sigma_{\rm SE/SE++}\ll\sigma_{\rm diver}$." (NIRCam Photometry)
4. **Compile spectroscopic redshifts and derive photometric redshifts via SED fitting**: Compile archival spectroscopic redshifts, resolve single-CO-line degeneracies using photo-z PDFs, and fit UV-to-near-IR photometry with BAGPIPES (or adopt COSMOS2020 / CIGALE for 6 outlier sources)  
  > "For the majority of the remaining sources, we use photometric redshifts derived via the BAGPIPES SED-fitting code (cite: Carnall2018) using all of the available UV-to-near-IR data (which now includes JWST data in addition to the data in COSMOS2020)." (Source Redshifts); "For this source, we instead run the CIGALE energy-balance SED and photo-$z$ fitting software with a parameterization following (cite: Long2024) and find a solution of $z_\mathrm{phot}\sim5.5$." (Photometric Redshifts Solutions for Unique Sources)
   - software: BAGPIPES, CIGALE
   - parameter: 5 age bins (10, 30, 100, 300, 600 Myr), log U in [-3, -1], Av in [0, 6], M_star in [10^6, 10^13] M_sun, metallicity in [10^-3, 10^0.4]  
  > "We allow the stellar mass to vary between $10^{6-13}$ M$_\odot$, and the stellar metallicity from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{0.4}$, both with log-uniform priors. We adopt a non-parametric star-formation history (SFH) model, with five bins in age (fixed bin edges at 10, 30, 100, 300, and 600 Myr) and a constant star-formation history in each bin." (Source Redshifts); "We include nebular emission, and allow the ionization parameter $\log U$ to vary from –3 to –1. Dust attenuation is included following a (cite: calzetti00) attenuation law with $A_V$ allowed to vary from $0$ to $6$ with a uniform prior." (Source Redshifts)
   - assumption: LambdaCDM cosmology with Planck 2020 parameters (H0 = 67.4 km s^-1 Mpc^-1), Chabrier IMF, Calzetti (2000) dust attenuation law  
  > "Where relevant, we assume a standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with Planck-measured parameters (cite: PlanckCollaboration2020), with H$_0 = 67.4$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, and a Chabrier initial mass function (cite: chabrier)." (Introduction); "Dust attenuation is included following a (cite: calzetti00) attenuation law with $A_V$ allowed to vary from $0$ to $6$ with a uniform prior." (Source Redshifts)
   - fitting: Log-uniform priors on stellar mass and metallicity; non-parametric SFH with Leja et al. (2019) continuity prior; uniform prior on Av  
  > "We allow the stellar mass to vary between $10^{6-13}$ M$_\odot$, and the stellar metallicity from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{0.4}$, both with log-uniform priors." (Source Redshifts); "We adopt the “continuity prior,” to explicitly weigh against significant SFR changes between adjacent bins and avoid overfitting the data (cite: Leja2019)." (Source Redshifts)
   - validation: Compared photo-zs against 18 available spec-zs (sigma_NMAD = 0.04) and COSMOS2020 photo-zs; inspected Herschel SPIRE non-detections and F444W effective radii to reject unphysical BAGPIPES solutions  
  > "We compare the calculated photo-$z$s to spectroscopic redshifts for the 18 available sources in Figure 4 and find good agreement, with a normalized median absolute deviation (cite: Brammer2008) of $\sigma_\mathrm{NMAD} = 0.04$." (Source Redshifts)
5. **Derive 2 mm cumulative number densities, redshift CDF, volume densities, and compare with OIR-dark selection techniques**: Run 10^3 Monte Carlo realizations sampling source flux densities, contamination, completeness, and redshift PDFs to compute cumulative number counts N(>S), redshift CDF, and volume densities in dz = 1 bins (adding cosmic variance in quadrature), and test radio+OIR-dark and JWST OIR-dark selection criteria

## Qualitative findings

- All 36 bona fide Ex-MORA 2 mm sources are detected at minimum in the JWST NIRCam F444W band, while one source (eMORA.31) is undetected at all other wavelengths and confirmed as likely spurious.  
  > "All sources are detected at minimum in the F444W band, with the exception of one source – eMORA.31." (Source Selection)
- Original MORA candidate MORA-12 disappears entirely in the deeper combined Ex-MORA mosaic, confirming it was a spurious noise fluctuation.  
  > "In this new mosaic, MORA-12 disappears entirely, thus confirming its nature as a false object." (Source Selection)
- The Ex-MORA redshift distribution and number counts are consistent with and slightly below the 'dust-poor' early Universe model of Casey et al. (2018a, 2018b), implying a rapidly declining number density of intense dusty starbursts at z > 3.  
  > "While the Ex-MORA survey is successful at identifying primarily $z>3$ IR luminous galaxies, the results are still consistent with – and even markedly below – the ‘dust-poor’ model." (Redshift Distribution of 2 mm Sources)
- Optical/near-IR and radio-based 'OIR-dark' selection techniques only recover 10-40% of the Ex-MORA 2 mm sample, demonstrating strong heterogeneity among high-z dusty galaxies.  
  > "Combined, it seems that these opt/NIR and radio selection techniques capture roughly $10-40$% of all 2 mm-bright sources, supporting the idea that the heavily dust obscured population is quite heterogeneous." (Other High-z Red Selection Techniques)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Observed-frame 2 mm observations leverage a very negative K-correction to efficiently detect z > 3 DSFGs while filtering out the more numerous z < 3 DSFG population; overlapping with the COSMOS-Web JWST field provides deep NIRCam imaging to identify counterparts.  
  > "Ex-MORA is an expansion of the MORA survey designed to identify primarily $z>3$ dusty, star-forming galaxies while simultaneously filtering out the more numerous $z<3$ population by leveraging the very negative $K$-correction at observed-frame 2 mm." (Abstract); "designed to identify primarily $z>3$ dusty, star-forming galaxies while simultaneously filtering out the more numerous $z<3$ population by leveraging the very negative $K$-correction at observed-frame 2 mm" (abstract)
- Why this method: Natural weighting with robust = 2 optimizes point-source SNR; peak SNR extraction is appropriate because the ~1.68'' x 1.44'' beam (~13 x 11 kpc at z=3) leaves high-z dust emission unresolved; diver photometry preserves JWST resolution and resolves inconsistencies between ground-based and JWST aperture fluxes.  
  > "we imaged these data using natural weighting, with a robust value $=2$ to optimize source signal-to-noise ratios" (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey); "Thus, it is unlikely that sources are resolved out of the map, or spatially resolved in general. Therefore, we adopt source positions and flux densities corresponding to the point of their peak signal-to-noise" (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey); "In light of these challenges, we measure fluxes using diver (McKinney et al. in prep.), an empirically-motivated photometry tool built to preserve the resolution of JWST when measuring PSF-matched multi-wavelength photometry." (NIRCam Photometry)
- Alternative: Measuring multi-wavelength aperture fluxes with SE/SE++ was tested and rejected in favor of diver because ground-based and JWST NIRCam fluxes were inconsistent above 1 sigma_ground.  
  > "We tested measuring multi-wavelength aperture fluxes with SE/SE++ (cite: setractor) and found the flux densities to be inconsistent between ground-based (e.g. UltraVISTA) and JWST NIRCam data above 1$\sigma_\mathrm{ground}$." (NIRCam Photometry)
- Alternative: Single-line CO(3-2) redshift solutions (z ~ 2.31 and 2.26) for eMORA.7 and eMORA.12 were rejected in favor of CO(4-3) solutions (z ~ 3.34 and 3.41) based on photo-z PDFs.  
  > "Indeed, the photometric redshifts developed in this work support a $z>3$ solution for both of these sources, and agrees with the CO(4-3) redshift within 1$\sigma$ uncertainty and $\int_{3}^{\infty}$ p(z) $>85\%$. Thus, for these two sources, we adopt the $z\sim3.4$ solutions in this work." (Approaching Degenerate Source Spectroscopic Redshifts)
- Alternative: High photometric redshift (z ~ 7.6) for eMORA.4 was rejected in favor of the single-line CO(4-3) spectroscopic redshift (z = 3.254) because z ~ 7.6 would imply CO(8-7) and multiple detected transitions.  
  > "Thus, we reject the high-$z$ photometrically-derived solution and adopt its redshift as $z=3.254$." (Approaching Degenerate Source Spectroscopic Redshifts)
- Alternative: BAGPIPES photo-z solutions at z ~ 1.5 for eMORA.6, eMORA.20, and eMORA.28 and at z ~ 7 for eMORA.21, eMORA.23, and eMORA.32 were rejected due to Herschel SPIRE non-detections and unphysically large F444W effective radii, respectively.  
  > "All three sources are not detected above 1$\sigma$ significance in the existing Herschel maps (cite: oliver2012herschel), and therefore we reject these solutions." (Photometric Redshifts Solutions for Unique Sources); "BAGPIPES completely failed to fit eMORA.23, despite outputting a redshift estimate and corresponding galaxy properties. For all three sources, we adopt the estimates reported in the COSMOS2020 catalog, which sit at $z>2$." (Photometric Redshifts Solutions for Unique Sources)
- How they decided it was enough: Map pixel SNRs follow a standard normal distribution centered at 0 with sigma = 1 up to 5 sigma, where positive peaks diverge, and 100 mock noise simulations yield only 0.7 (+0.7/-0.5) false detections above 5 sigma.  
  > "As shown in Figure 2, the SNRs of the map pixels are well represented by a standard normal centered about zero with $\sigma = 1$, above which positive source peaks diverge from the distribution, demonstrating that the SNR $\ge5$ $\sigma$ threshold is an ideal threshold for low source contamination." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
- Limitation (authors): Although A-rated, the ALMA project was only partially observed, covering 0.16 deg^2 (577 arcmin^2) instead of the planned 0.2 deg^2.  
  > "Though A-rated, the project was only partially observed, resulting in a large, contiguous mosaic of $\sim$ 577 arcmin$^2$ or 0.16 deg$^2$ centered on $\alpha \approx$ 10:00:15, $\delta \approx$ +02:21:32 (shown in Figure 1)." (The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey)
- Limitation (authors): Half of the sample relies on photometric redshifts, and some spectroscopic redshifts are based on a single CO emission line with potential transition degeneracy.  
  > "A handful of sources have spectroscopic redshifts based on a single CO transition line, meaning their interpretation could be uncertain." (Source Redshifts); "However, when considering sources with photometrically derived redshifts, both of these trends appear to wash out." (Redshift Distribution of 2 mm Sources)
- Limitation (authors): Cosmic variance dominates the volume density uncertainties for 2 mm-luminous sources at z > 2.  
  > "We note that $\sigma_\mathrm{CV}$ dominates the volume density uncertainties for 2 mm-luminous sources at $z>2$." (Volume Density of 2 mm Sources at z>=3)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several citations in the LaTeX source appear blank in the text where Zavala et al. (2021) and Casey et al. (2021) were referenced.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Six out of 18 photometric redshift sources required manual rejection of the primary BAGPIPES fit in favor of COSMOS2020 or CIGALE fits.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Multi-wavelength photometry relies on an unpublished tool (diver; McKinney et al. in prep.), and several spectroscopic redshifts come from unpublished compilations (Khostovan et al. in prep.; Chen et al. in prep.).
