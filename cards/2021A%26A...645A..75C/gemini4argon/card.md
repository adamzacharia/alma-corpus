## Analysis recipe

1. **Perform quantum chemical calculations of CPCA isomers**: Structural optimisation and harmonic/anharmonic vibrational frequency calculations using B3LYP, B97D, MP2, and CCSD with 6-311++G(d,p), cc-pVTZ, and aug-cc-pVTZ basis sets  
  > "In addition to the structural optimisation calculations, we carried out harmonic and anharmonic frequency calculations in order to estimate the energy and the rotational constants of the individual vibrationally excited states" (2. Quantum chemical calculations)
   - software: Gaussian16
   - parameter: MP2/6-311++G(d,p) level of theory for vibrational frequencies and vibration-rotation constants  
  > "The calculations were carried out with the Gaussian16 program package at the MP2/6-311++G(d,p) level of theory." (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared theoretical rotational constants to experimental constants  
  > "We compared the theoretical constants obtained for each of the levels of theory employed to those determined experimentally in this work." (2. Quantum chemical calculations)
2. **Measure and fit laboratory rotational spectra of cis and trans CPCA**: Measure broadband rotational spectra in 31.5–50 GHz and 72–116.5 GHz with GACELA and fit rotational and centrifugal distortion constants using Watson's S-reduced Hamiltonian  
  > "For the trans isomer, we measured a total of 208 a -type R -branch transitions with maximum values of J and K a quantum numbers of 18 and 17, respectively, which were fitted using the SPFIT (Pickett 1991) program" (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis); "The best fit for all these lines was obtained using the S -reduction of Watson’s Hamiltonian in III r representation (Watson 1977). This fit was done using the Kisiel’s ASFIT program (Kisiel & Bialkowska-Jaworska 2019)" (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
   - software: SPFIT, ASFIT
   - parameter: Watson S-reduction in I^r representation (trans) and III^r representation (cis); frequency predictions up to 300 GHz; J_max = 90 for partition function  
  > "with the S -reduction of the Watson’s Hamiltonian in I r representation (Watson 1977)." (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis); "The rotational partition function was calculated considering a maximum value of J = 90" (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
   - assumption: T = 350 K for laboratory gas-phase relative stability; dipole moments from Volltrauer & Schwendeman (1971)  
  > "Assuming a temperature for our experiment of 350K, this implies an energy difference between both isomers of 44.05(75) cm −1" (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
   - fitting: Least-squares Hamiltonian fitting via SPFIT and ASFIT  
  > "σ rms /kHz 9.65" (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
   - validation: RMS residuals of fits (e.g., 9.65 kHz for trans ground state, 37.90 kHz for cis ground state)  
  > "σ rms /kHz 9.65" (Table 3); "σ rms /kHz 37.90" (Table 4)
3. **Search for CPCA and derive column density upper limits in Orion KL and Sgr B2(N)**: Generate LTE synthetic spectra with MADEX using fixed source physical parameters and compare to ALMA SV, IRAM 30 m, and GBT PRIMOS spectra to derive upper limits on column density  
  > "We used the MADEX code (Cernicharo 2012) to exploit the spectroscopic parameters presented in this work and to derive the synthetic spectrum of this species (assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium) according to the physical parameters of the sources" (5. Search for cyclopropanecarboxaldehyde in space); "The column density was the only free parameter for these models. Corrections for beam dilution were applied to each line depending on its frequency." (5. Search for cyclopropanecarboxaldehyde in space)

## Qualitative findings

- Identified 12 and 6 vibrationally excited states for the trans and cis isomers of CPCA, respectively, in addition to the ground states.  
  > "In addition to the ground states, we identified 12 and 6 vibrationally excited states for the trans and cis isomers, respectively, including fundamental modes, multiple excitation quanta, and combination states." (abstract)
- The trans isomer of CPCA is more stable than the cis isomer in the gas phase, with a concentration almost 1.2 times larger at 350 K.  
  > "We find that the gas phase concentration of the trans isomer is almost 1.2 times larger than that of the cis one." (abstract)
- Neither cis nor trans CPCA was detected above the confusion/detection limit in Orion KL or Sgr B2(N).  
  > "We did not find any CPCA above the detection limit of these data and provide upper limits to the column density for the two isomers of CPCA." (6. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: For frequencies above 80 GHz, CPCA a-type transition upper levels exceed 30 K, preventing detection in cold clouds; therefore the search focused on warm high-mass star-forming regions with high CH3CHO abundances (Orion KL and Sgr B2).  
  > "For these frequencies, the energies of the rotational levels of the CPCA a -type transitions are above 30 K, preventing the detection of CPCA in our IRAM 30 m data of cold clouds at 3 mm (Cernicharo et al. 2012)." (5. Search for cyclopropanecarboxaldehyde in space); "Therefore, we focused the search on high-mass star-forming regions which show high abundances of acetaldehyde (CH 3 CHO) such as Orion KL and Sgr B2 (see, e.g. Belloche et al. 2013; Cernicharo et al. 2016)." (5. Search for cyclopropanecarboxaldehyde in space)
- Why this method: Frequency switching with a 50 MHz throw was selected in the laboratory because lines are observed twice and noise improves by sqrt(2); ASFIT was used for cis-CPCA because it is best suited for S-reduction in III^r representation.  
  > "This has previously been confirmed as the most suitable mode, as the lines are observed twice and the noise is improved by a factor √ 2" (3. Experimental setup); "This fit was done using the Kisiel’s ASFIT program (Kisiel & Bialkowska-Jaworska 2019) which is best suited when the S -reduction in III r representation is required." (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
- Alternative: Searching in IRAM 30 m data of cold clouds at 3 mm was rejected because CPCA rotational energy levels above 80 GHz are above 30 K.  
  > "For these frequencies, the energies of the rotational levels of the CPCA a -type transitions are above 30 K, preventing the detection of CPCA in our IRAM 30 m data of cold clouds at 3 mm (Cernicharo et al. 2012)." (5. Search for cyclopropanecarboxaldehyde in space)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Quantum chemical calculations of relative isomer energies fluctuate between methods and are near the level of accuracy of the calculations.  
  > "Secondly, the use of quantum chemical calculations to estimate the relative energies of CPCA isomers should be handled with caution because these relative energies are near to the level of accuracy of the calculations." (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
- Limitation (authors): No c-type rotational transitions of trans-CPCA or transitions from the nu_15 vibrational mode could be measured due to weak intensities.  
  > "In spite of the fact that the trans isomer also presents a c -type dipole moment different from zero, no c -type rotational transitions could be measured in our spectrum." (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis); "We scrutinised the rotational spectrum searching for transitions attributable to ν 15 but all attempts were fruitless." (4. Rotational spectrum and analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Because the CPCA partition function is ~4 times larger than that of CH3CHO at ~100 K, CPCA lines are much weaker, so the derived lower limit on CH3CHO/CPCA (~2-4) is likely far from the true abundance ratio.  
  > "Therefore, it is reasonable to suggest that the derived lower limit ratio is far from the real abundance ratio between these species in the considered regions." (5. Search for cyclopropanecarboxaldehyde in space)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits on column densities in Orion KL and Sgr B2(N) are determined by visual inspection against line confusion rather than a formal statistical threshold.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beam size and extraction aperture for the three Orion KL positions in the ALMA SV data are not fully specified in this paper.
