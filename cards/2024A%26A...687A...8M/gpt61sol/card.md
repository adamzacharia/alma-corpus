## Analysis recipe

1. **Build the laboratory spectroscopic fit**: Assign laboratory rotation-tunneling lines iteratively; fit spectroscopic parameters with SPFIT and calculate spectra with SPCAT.  
  > "Pickett’s programs SPCAT and SPFIT (cite: spfit_1991) were used to calculate and fit the rotational spectra of 3-hydroxypropenal." (Analysis and fitting); "We tested after each round of assignments if one or more spectroscopic parameters would improve the quality of the fit by amounts that warranted keeping the respective parameter in the fit." (Analysis and fitting)
   - software: SPCAT, SPFIT
   - parameter: Laboratory coverage: 150–330 GHz and 400–660 GHz.  
  > "The measurements between 150 and 330 GHz and between 400 and 660 GHz were carried out with the Lille spectrometer (cite: Lille-spectrometer)." (Spectroscopic measurements)
   - parameter: Final present-study list: 11551 transitions at 7551 different frequencies; J ≤ 100, Ka ≤ 59, Kc ≤ 97.  
  > "Our final line list consisted of 11551 transitions from the present study which correspond to 7551 different frequencies, mostly because of unresolved asymmetry splitting." (Observed spectrum and assignments); "The quantum numbers $J$, $K_a$, and $K_c$ reach values of 100, 59, and 97." (Observed spectrum and assignments)
   - assumption: Watson’s S reduced Hamiltonian in the prolate Ir representation on the diagonal; RAS for the off-diagonal interaction.  
  > "We applied here Watson’s S reduced Hamiltonian in the prolate I$^r$ representation on the diagonal" (Analysis and fitting); "The off-diagonal interaction was described employing Pickett’s Reduced Axis System (RAS) (cite: RAS_Pickett_1972)" (Analysis and fitting)
   - fitting: Test additional reasonable parameters after assignment rounds; retain the parameter improving fit quality most; occasionally test removal of poorly constrained parameters.  
  > "If at least one parameter improved the quality of the fit sufficiently, we chose the one that improved the quality the most and searched for additional parameters." (Analysis and fitting); "We tested occasionally if a parameter with relatively large uncertainties can be omitted from the fit without increasing the rms error of the fit by too large amounts." (Analysis and fitting)
   - validation: Inspect residuals and blends; total-fit rms 17.3 kHz and rms error 0.839.  
  > "Transitions with modest residuals around three to four times of the experimental uncertainties were marked for occasional inspection." (Observed spectrum and assignments); "The rms of 17.3 kHz and the rms error of 0.839 for the total fit are obviously dominated by our data." (Observed spectrum and assignments)
2. **Generate improved transition predictions**: Calculate the rotation-tunneling spectrum from the final spectroscopic parameters and deposit it in CDMS.

   - software: SPCAT, CDMS
   - parameter: Accuracy check: calculation at 300 K up to 1.5 THz.  
  > "A calculation at 300 K up to 1.5 THz contains only very few lines with calculated uncertainties exceeding 0.1 MHz" (Discussion of the laboratory spectroscopic results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Use archival observations of additional transitions to strengthen the molecular assignment; select angular resolution close to PILS.  
  > "We searched the ALMA archive for projects possibly covering additional transitions to further strengthen the assignment of 3-hydroxypropenal toward IRAS16293 B." (Search for additional transitions of 3-hydroxypropenal in the ALMA archive); "The angular resolution was limited to the range 0.3–0.7$\ensuremath{''}$ so that it is relatively close to the PILS resolution ($\sim$0.5$\ensuremath{''}$)." (Search for additional transitions of 3-hydroxypropenal in the ALMA archive)
- Why this method: Improve uncertain high-excitation frequencies to distinguish the 125 K and 300 K LTE scenarios.  
  > "Based on the previous catalog of 3-hydroxypropenal lines, it was not possible to distinguish between an excitation temperature of 125 K and 300 K for this species." (Analysis of the ALMA-PILS observations); "Indeed, the lines with high $E_{\rm up}$ that should allow us to constrain the excitation temperature, showed too large frequency uncertainties." (Analysis of the ALMA-PILS observations)
- Alternative: Tex = 300 K, N = 1.8 × 10^15 cm−2 does not reproduce the observations.  
  > "However, the model with $T_{\rm ex}$ = 300 K and $N = 1.8 \times 10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$ does not reproduce the observations." (Analysis of the ALMA-PILS observations)
- Alternative: Lowering the 300 K model column density to 1.0 × 10^15 cm−2 improves agreement but underproduces detected lines.  
  > "A better agreement would be obtained if we lower the column density at $1.0 \times 10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$." (Analysis of the ALMA-PILS observations); "But several detected lines (Fig. 2 of (cite: 3-HP_det_2022)) are in that case under-produced in terms of intensity." (Analysis of the ALMA-PILS observations)
- Alternative: Exclude obviously blended laboratory lines, with limited exceptions for sufficiently symmetric unresolved or accidental blends.  
  > "Obviously blended lines were in most cases not included in the line list except for unresolved asymmetry doublets and occasionally accidental blends of lines assignable to the ground vibrational state of 3-hydroxypropenal" (Observed spectrum and assignments); "as long as the line shape was sufficiently symmetric." (Observed spectrum and assignments)
- How they decided it was enough: Reject the 300 K model using missing predicted lines; accept the molecular detection after seven additional lines agree with the model.  
  > "Indeed, several undetected lines ($\sim$30) are predicted above 3$\sigma$ with this model" (Analysis of the ALMA-PILS observations); "With this new spectroscopic study, the seven additional detected transitions and the good agreement of their intensities with the model, the detection of HOCHCHCHO can now be considered as secure."
