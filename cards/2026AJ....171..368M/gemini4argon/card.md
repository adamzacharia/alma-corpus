## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA Band 4 continuum and spectral line cubes**: Run ALMA calibration pipeline with Perley-Butler 2017 flux scale, flag bad data, split target with MSTRANSFORM, subtract continuum with UVCONTSUB, image continuum and cubes with TCLEAN, apply self-calibration (GAINCAL/APPLYCAL), and correct primary beam with IMPBCOR.  
  > "We used the Common Astronomy Software Application (CASA 5.4.1) with the ALMA data analysis pipeline for calibration and imaging of the data (cite: mc07)." (Observations and data reductions); "We created the continuum emission map of NIRS3 using the TCLEAN task with a HOGBOM deconvolver for line-free channels." (Observations and data reductions); "We applied the task IMPBCOR to correct the primary beam pattern in the continuum images and spectral data cubes." (Observations and data reductions)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: deconvolver = HOGBOM, specmode = cube, Briggs weighting = 0.5  
  > "We created the continuum emission map of NIRS3 using the TCLEAN task with a HOGBOM deconvolver for line-free channels." (Observations and data reductions); "We also generated spectral data cubes using the TCLEAN task with the cube spectral definition mode (SPECMODE) with a Briggs weighting of 0.5." (Observations and data reductions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum flux density, peak intensity, and core size, and model radial intensity profile**: Fit 2D Gaussian to continuum images at 4 frequencies using IMFIT; construct radial profile at 2.32 mm in 0.1'' concentric annuli and fit 5 analytical models (Single Power-law, Single Plummer, Broken Power-law, Double Plummer, Plummer + Gaussian).  
  > "To estimate the physical parameters of the continuum emission images, we fitted a 2-D Gaussian using the IMFIT task over the main core." (Dust continuum emission towards NIRS3); "The radial intensity profile was constructed by azimuthally averaging the 2.32 mm continuum emission image in concentric annuli binned at 0.1$^{\prime\prime}$ centered on the emission peak." (Radial intensity profile); "The models include (A) a single power-law, (B) a single Plummer-like profile (cite: pl11), (C) a broken power-law, (D) a double Plummer profile, and (E) a Plummer profile with an additional Gaussian component (Plummer + Gaussian)." (Radial intensity profile)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Radial bin width = 0.1''  
  > "The fits were performed on the azimuthally averaged radial profile binned at $0.1\ensuremath{''}$ intervals, with the beam profile of $2.30^{\prime\prime} \times 1.28^{\prime\prime}$ ($\sim$ 3791 AU)." (Radial intensity profile)
   - fitting: Chi-squared minimization and model selection via Akaike Information Criterion (AIC)  
  > "To compare the relative performance of the different models quantitatively, we use the Akaike information criterion (AIC) (cite: al74)." (Radial intensity profile)
   - validation: Evaluated reduced chi^2 and Delta AIC across the five models.  
  > "The residuals show minimal systematic deviations across the radial range, with a reduced $\chi^2 = 13.64$, confirming that Model E provides the most robust representation of the observed profile among the five models considered." (Radial intensity profile)
3. **Fit the multi-wavelength SED to derive dust mass, dust temperature, and dust spectral index**: Combine ALMA Band 3, 4, 6, 7 continuum fluxes with AKARI, Herschel, and Spitzer/IRAC upper limits (3.6 to 500 um) and fit radiative transfer model using MCMC.  
  > "We constructed the spectral energy distribution (SED) of NIRS3 using ALMA bands 3, 4, 6, and 7." (Spectral energy distribution (SED)); "We then fitted the SED using the radiative transfer model of (cite: rob17) using the Python-based ASTROPY module (version 5.0) (cite: astro22)." (Spectral energy distribution (SED)); "We employed a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm to fit the radiative model to the observed SED, following the methodology described in (cite: min21)." (Spectral energy distribution (SED))
   - software: ASTROPY, corner.py
   - assumption: Source distance fixed at 1.78 kpc; AKARI, Herschel, and Spitzer/IRAC fluxes treated as upper limits  
  > "Since the angular resolutions of the ALMA observations differ from those of the other instruments, we therefore treated the flux measurements from AKARI, Herschel, and Spitzer/IRAC as upper limits." (Spectral energy distribution (SED)); "For the fitting, the distance to the source was fixed at 1.78 kpc." (Spectral energy distribution (SED))
   - fitting: MCMC algorithm following (cite: min21)  
  > "We employed a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm to fit the radiative model to the observed SED, following the methodology described in (cite: min21)." (Spectral energy distribution (SED))
   - validation: Comparison with literature values from (cite: wan11), (cite: zin15), and (cite: liu20)  
  > "All derived dust parameters are consistent within uncertainties with previously reported values by (cite: wan11), (cite: zin15), and (cite: liu20)." (Spectral energy distribution (SED))
4. **Estimate H2 column density and dust optical depth and model 3D radiative transfer maps**: Compute H2 column density and dust optical depth analytically from peak continuum flux densities at four wavelengths assuming optically thin dust, and simulate 2D maps using RADMC-3D (image, mctherm, tausurf tasks).  
  > "From equation 11, we estimate the column density of molecular at wavelengths of 2.32 mm, 2.29 mm, 2.12 mm, and 2.10 mm, which are shown in Table 1." (Estimation of column density of and dust optical depth); "To understand the distribution of the column density of , dust optical depth, and dust temperature towards NIRS3, we also created modelled images of these physical parameters using the 3-D Monte Carlo radiative transfer code RADMC-3D (cite: dul12)." (Estimation of column density of and dust optical depth)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: n_photons = 3x10^8, r_in = 20 AU, r_out = 4.5x10^3 AU, r_plat = 900 AU, p_env = 1.1  
  > "| Radius of envelope ($r_{\mathrm{out}}$) | 4.5$\times10^{3}$ | AU |" (Appendix); "| Amount of photons employed in the thermal Monte Carlo process ($n_{\mathrm{photons}}$) | 3$\times10^{8}$ | – |" (Appendix); "| Density power-law exponent ($p_{\mathrm{env}}$) | 1.1$^{\color{blue}{\ast}}$ | – |" (Appendix)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust continuum; T_d = 150 K; mean molecular weight mu_H = 1.41; dust-to-gas ratio Z = 0.01; dust opacity kappa_nu = 0.90(nu/230 GHz)^beta cm^2 g^-1 with beta = 1.7  
  > "To estimate the column density of , we used the dust temperature $T_d$ = 150 K (derived from SED), $\mu_H = 1.41$, and $Z = 0.01$ (cite: cox00)." (Estimation of column density of and dust optical depth); "To estimate the mass absorption coefficient ($\kappa_{\nu}$), we adopt the following equation, $\kappa_\nu$ = 0.90($\nu$/230 GHz)$^{\beta}\ \textrm{cm}^{2}\ \textrm{g}^{-1}$, where ${k_{230} = 0.90~\textrm{cm}^{2}\ \textrm{g}^{-1}}$ is the dust emissivity at a gas density of $\rm{10^{6}\ cm^{-3}}$ covered by a thin ice mantle at a frequency of 230 GHz (cite: moto19)." (Estimation of column density of and dust optical depth); "We use $\beta$ $\sim$ 1.7, which was estimated from the SED fitting." (Estimation of column density of and dust optical depth)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Calculated dust optical depth tau_nu = 2.1x10^-2 confirms optically thin assumption.  
  > "The estimated dust optical depth suggests that the dust in NIRS3 is optically thin, considering the current angular resolution of this observation." (Estimation of column density of and dust optical depth)
5. **Extract molecular spectra and perform LTE MCMC spectral modelling to derive column densities, excitation temperatures, and abundances**: Extract spectra from a 2.32'' diameter circular region centered on NIRS3; identify transitions using JPL, CDMS, and LSD databases; fit optically thin LTE models using MCMC in CASSIS; divide column densities by N(H2), N(CH3OH), and N(CH3CN) to obtain fractional abundances.  
  > "We extracted the molecular spectra from the spectral data cubes using a circular region with a diameter of 2.32$^{\prime\prime}$ (average emitting size) centered on the NIRS3 core." (Line emission towards NIRS3); "After spectral extraction, we used CASSIS software for molecular identification and spectral analysis using the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) model with an optically thin assumption with the JPL, CDMS, and LSD molecular databases (cite: vas15; pic98; mu05; mot25)." (Line emission towards NIRS3); "We used the MCMC algorithm in the CASSIS software for fitting LTE-modelled spectra of molecules over the observed spectra." (Line emission towards NIRS3)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Aperture diameter = 2.32''; source size = 2.32''; V_LSR = 5.0 km s^-1; 1000 walkers; 20,000 burn-in steps  
  > "The systemic velocity (V$_{LSR}$) of the molecular spectra of NIRS3 is 5.0 km s$^{-1}$ (cite: liu20)." (Line emission towards NIRS3); "During our MCMC analysis, we employed 1000 walkers, uniformly distributed within the specified parameter ranges, which are shown in Table 2, and ran the chains for a burning sequence of 20,000 steps to ensure convergence." (Line emission towards NIRS3); "In the LTE model, we used a source size of $\sim$2.32$^{\prime\prime}$ (half-maximum diameter of our synthesized beam size)." (Line emission towards NIRS3)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin emission; CMB temperature = 2.7 K  
  > "After spectral extraction, we used CASSIS software for molecular identification and spectral analysis using the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) model with an optically thin assumption with the JPL, CDMS, and LSD molecular databases (cite: vas15; pic98; mu05; mot25)." ## Qualitative findings

- The 2.32 mm radial continuum intensity profile of NIRS3 is best described by a composite Plummer + Gaussian model, indicating a centrally concentrated core with an additional radially localized component.  
  > "The Plummer + Gaussian model (Model E) provides the best fit." (Radial intensity profile); "The residuals show minimal systematic deviations across the radial range, with a reduced $\chi^2 = 13.64$, confirming that Model E provides the most robust representation of the observed profile among the five models considered." (Radial intensity profile)
- All six detected N-bearing molecules have high excitation temperatures (175-220 K) and compact, spatially unresolved emission (2.20''-2.31'') that arises from the warm inner hot core region (T >= 100 K) of NIRS3.  
  > "The high excitation temperatures (175–220 K) indicate that the identified N-bearing molecules arise from the warm inner regions ($T \geq 100$ K) of the source." (Abstract); "It appeared that the emitting diameters of the different N-bearing molecular transitions are smaller than the synthesized beam sizes of the integrated emission maps, implying that the detected transitions of the N-bearing molecules are spatially unresolved." (Spatial distribution of N-bearing molecules)
- NIRS3 displays unusually high relative abundances of the amide-like molecules NH2CN and NH2CHO compared to other hot cores and hot corinos.  
  > "These results demonstrate that, although the cyanide-bearing species in NIRS3 are not as extreme as in Sgr B2 (N), the source is chemically unique in terms of its exceptionally high relative abundances of and ." (Molecular correlation with other sources)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: NIRS3 hosts a chemically rich hot molecular core where O- and S-bearing species were previously studied, but complex N-bearing species had received little emphasis; archival ALMA Band 4 DDT observations provided sufficient spectral coverage to detect multiple N-bearing transitions.  
  > "Previous studies by (cite: zin15) and (cite: liu20) identified rotational emission lines of numerous oxygen (O)- and sulphur (S)-bearing molecules toward NIRS3, but placed little emphasis on complex N-bearing species." (Introduction); "However, the available spectral coverage enables the detection of several molecular transitions, which we exploit here to investigate the chemical properties of the source." (Observations and data reductions)
- Why this method: LTE spectral modelling with CASSIS is justified because the inner gas density of NIRS3 (~3x10^7 cm^-3) is comparable to or exceeds the critical density of dense gas tracers (~10^7 cm^-3), thermalizing energy levels; AIC is used to balance goodness of fit against model complexity for radial profiles.  
  > "The AIC balances the goodness of fit against model complexity and is defined as:" (Radial intensity profile); "The assumption of LTE is valid in the inner region of NIRS3, where the gas density is approximately 3$\times$10$^{7}$ cm$^{-3}$ (cite: sz18), comparable to the critical density of dense gas tracers such as C$^{34}$S($J$ = 7-6) ($\sim10^{7}$ cm$^{-3}$) (see (cite: zin20))." (Line emission towards NIRS3)
- Alternative: Four alternative radial intensity profile models (Single Power-law, Single Plummer, Broken Power-law, Double Plummer) were fitted and rejected in favor of the Plummer + Gaussian model based on higher reduced chi^2 and Delta AIC >= 9.3.  
  > "The single power-law fit (Model A) gives $\alpha = 2.20 \pm 0.06$, but this model provides the poorest fit (reduced $\chi^2 = 20.23$) and is strongly disfavored by the model selection criteria, confirming that a single power-law is insufficient to describe the complex structure." (Radial intensity profile); "Model E is statistically preferred over the other four models. It is favored by $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = 9.3$ relative to the next best model (Double Plummer), while the remaining models show $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} > 10$, indicating little or no empirical support." (Radial intensity profile)
- Alternative: Direct grain-surface reaction CH3 + CN -> CH3CN (Reaction 1) and HC2N + H -> HC3N (Reaction 3) were rejected as primary formation pathways due to ice interaction barriers, competing reactions with H2O ice, and unverified/assumed rate constants.  
  > "Therefore, there is a low probability of the formation of using reaction 1 on the grain surface of the hot cores." (Formation mechanism of); "We also noticed that the rate constants of reaction 3 are assumed. So, we are reluctant to use reaction 3 as the probable formation chain for ." (Formation mechanism of)
