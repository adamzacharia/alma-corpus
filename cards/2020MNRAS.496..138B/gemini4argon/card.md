## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image VLA and ALMA observations and generate moment maps**: Run VLA and ALMA CASA calibration pipelines, image with CLEAN using natural and Briggs (robust = 0.0) weighting, convolve VLA map to ALMA beam, and compute moment 1 and 2 maps with a 4-sigma pixel threshold.  
  > "The observations were reduced by the standard ALMA pipeline system using the casa data analysis package." (2.2 ALMA observations); "the calibrated data were also re-imaged with Briggs weighting (with a default robustness parameter of 0.0), giving a higher resolution of 0 [′′] . 68 × 0 [′′] . 56 with PA = 69.77 [◦]" (2.2 ALMA observations); "To obtain both these maps, we included only the pixels that have a signal to noise (off-source noise in the channel maps) ratio greater than 4." (Figure 7 caption)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.0; SNR > 4 pixel cut for moment maps  
  > "the calibrated data were also re-imaged with Briggs weighting (with a default robustness parameter of 0.0)" (2.2 ALMA observations); "we included only the pixels that have a signal to noise (off-source noise in the channel maps) ratio greater than 4." (Figure 7 caption)
   - assumption: 10% flux calibration accuracy  
  > "The flux density is 21.99 ± 2.21 mJy, assuming a 10% flux calibration accuracy." (Figure 2 caption)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to integrated CO(5-4) line spectrum  
  > "The blue curve is the actual spectrum and the green dashed curve the gaussian fit." (Figure 6 caption)
   - validation: Measured VLA off-source noise level (1.3 uJy/beam) checked against expected thermal noise (1.1-1.3 uJy/beam)  
  > "The overall off-source noise level in the map is 1.3µJy/beam, which is in agreement with the expected noise level of 1.1-1.3µJy/beam with natural weighting." (2.1 VLA observations)
2. **Fit four elliptical Gaussian components to lensed images in the image plane**: Fit four elliptical Gaussians at the image positions in the CO(5-4) and radio maps using a 10-parameter optimization, and test point-source Gaussian fits with AIPS JMFIT.  
  > "In the image plane, we began by fitting four elliptical Gaussians at the positions of the images in the CO(5-4) line map, an approach that makes no assumption about the detail of the lens model" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "The fit was performed using jmfit, part of the Astronomical Image Processing System, aips" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "A preliminary fit of the four lensed images with point-like gaussian components, using aips jmfit, gave obviously unsatisfactory results" (3.2 Radio)
   - software: AIPS, JMFIT
   - parameter: 10 parameters: 4 peak flux densities, FWHMs and axis ratios of A (=D) and B (=C), 2 positional offsets (x, y); major axes fixed tangential to lens centre (100.87, 77.21, 12.99, 151.07 deg)  
  > "The fit used 10 image parameters, namely the peak flux densities of the four components, the FWHMs and the axis ratios of images A and B and two overall positional offsets in the x and y directions" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "the fitted components were constrained to have the major axes tangential to the centre of the lensing galaxy (100.87 [◦], 77.21 [◦], 12.99 [◦] and 151.07 [◦] for A, B, C and D respectively" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - assumption: FWHMs and axis ratios of A and D are equal, and those of B and C are equal; FWHMs constrained to be greater than major axis of dirty beam  
  > "The FWHMs and axis ratios of the merging images (A and D) are assumed equal, as are the FWHMs and axis ratios for images B and C" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "Since the lensed images are spatially resolved, the FWHMs of the images were constrained to be greater than the major axis of the dirty beam." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - fitting: 10-parameter chi-squared minimization  
  > "The 10-parameter optimization yielded a minimum χ red [2] [=] [1][.][5][.]" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - validation: Visual inspection of residual maps and reduced chi-squared (1.5 for CO, 1.8 for radio); point-source fit rejected due to over/under-subtraction in residuals  
  > "the resultant residual shows an over-subtraction of flux in the image positions and under-subtraction in the nearby areas (see Fig. 8, bottom left), demonstrating incompatibility of a point source with our observation." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
3. **Model gravitational lensing in the image plane**: Optimize a Gaussian source profile and flux anomaly parameter f_AD through an SIE lens galaxy mass model against the CO(5-4), submm continuum, and radio images.  
  > "using the Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid (SIE) model developed by Eigenbrod et al. (2006) for the lensing galaxy" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "Allowing the source to have a Gaussian profile, the galaxy position and the source parameters were optimized using the difference between the data and the lensed images convolved with the point spread" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - software: lens modelling code developed by Neal Jackson
   - parameter: SIE lens parameters: critical radius 0.87 arcsec, ellipticity 0.13 at PA -73.1 deg, shear 0.042 at PA 65.4 deg; f_AD between 0 and 1  
  > "An extra parameter, f AD, was introduced, that can vary between 0 and 1 and which multiplies the flux of the component D, while keeping the flux of A the same." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "A standard galaxy model (critical radius 0 [′′] . 87, ellipticity 0.13 at PA −73.1 [◦], shear magnitude 0.042 at PA 65.4 [◦] ) is used for the image plane fitting." (Table 4)
   - assumption: Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid (SIE) lens mass profile and Gaussian source profile  
  > "Allowing the source to have a Gaussian profile, the galaxy position and the source parameters were optimized using the difference between the data and the lensed images convolved with the point spread" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - fitting: Optimization of difference between data and PSF-convolved lensed model images  
  > "the galaxy position and the source parameters were optimized using the difference between the data and the lensed images convolved with the point spread" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - validation: Reduced chi-squared (1.85 for CO, 1.19 for submm continuum, 2.05 for radio) and residual map inspection  
  > "The fit has a χ red [2] [=][ 1][.][85][ and is generally satisfactory apart] from some difference in the structure of image B." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "obtained from the image-plane lens modelling of the submillimetre continuum using the SIE galaxy lens model (Eigenbrod et al. 2006) used in Section 3.1.1 (χ red [2] [=] [1][.][19][).]" (Figure 12 caption)
4. **Fit four elliptical Gaussian components directly to u-v visibilities**: Fit four elliptical Gaussians to the continuum-subtracted CO(5-4) and radio visibility data using uvmultifit with image positions fixed to Inada et al. (2003).  
  > "We first fitted four elliptical gaussians for the integrated CO line using uvmultifit (Mart´ı-Vidal et al. 2014), on the continuum subtracted visibility data of the CO line." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "As for the integrated CO line, we fitted four elliptical Gaussians to the visibility data using uvmultifit, with image positions from Inada et al. (2003) and fixing the component position angles." (3.2 Radio)
   - software: uvmultifit
   - parameter: Image positions and position angles fixed; 8 free parameters for CO (4 fluxes, 2 widths, 2 axis ratios); 6 free parameters for radio (4 fluxes, 1 width, 1 axis ratio)  
  > "We used the positions of the four images from Inada et al. (2003) and kept them fixed, optimising for the flux densities, widths and axes ratios of the images. We kept the axes ratios of A and D (and likewise B and C) equal, as well as their FWHMs." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "The four image flux densities, FWHMs and axis ratios were optimized, keeping the FWHMs and axis ratios of the four images equal, because using any more parameters than these six (four flux densities, one width and one axis ratio) resulted in the program failing to find a fit." (3.2 Radio)
   - assumption: Optical image positions from Inada et al. (2003) apply to CO and radio components  
  > "We used the positions of the four images from Inada et al. (2003) and kept them fixed" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - fitting: Visibility-plane fitting in uvmultifit with visibility weight re-scaling  
  > "The reduced chi squared value before the re-scaling of the visibility weights in the algorithm are 4.68 and 11.24 for the CO line and the radio, respectively." (Table 3)
   - validation: Comparison of u-v A/D flux ratios with image-plane results  
  > "this fitting was found to be 1.28 ± 0.41, once again consistent with the flux ratio found from the image-plane analysis." (3.2 Radio)
5. **Perform lens modelling in the u-v plane and derive intrinsic CO luminosity and dynamical mass**: Fit a Sersic source profile lensed by an SIE galaxy model to the CO(5-4), submm continuum, and radio visibilities using visilens, then compute intrinsic CO luminosity and dynamical mass following Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005).  
  > "We also fitted the u−v data with a lens model using visilens. All the lens galaxy parameters, except the position and mass of the lens galaxy, were fixed assuming the SIE lens model by Eigenbrod et al. (2006)." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "Following Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005), we get a CO line (lensed) luminosity of µL [′] CO [=][ 3][.][16] [×] [10] [10] [K km s] [−][1] [pc] [2] [.]" (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "Solomon & Vanden Bout (2005) use the formula M dyn sin [2] (i) = 233.5R∆V [2] to calculate the dynamical mass, where R is the radius of the molecular disk and ∆V is the FWHM of the CO line." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - software: visilens
   - parameter: Einstein radius = 0.92 arcsec (optimized on CO visibilities, fixed for continuum and radio); Sersic source profile  
  > "For the u − v plane fitting, the lens parameters are the same as for the image plane, except for the Einstein radius (0 [′′] . 92) which was obtained by optimising for it in case of the CO line visibility data and subsequently kept fixed for the submillimeter continuum and radio u − v fits." (Table 4)
   - assumption: Disk radius R is half the FWHM of the integrated CO source (81 mas); flat cosmology with DL = 11400 Mpc, DA = 1790 Mpc  
  > "Using our value of size of the integrated CO line emission source of 81 ± 8 mas (Table 4), we get R = 348 ± 34 pc, approximating R to be equal to half of size of the disk." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
   - fitting: MCMC/parameter distribution optimization in visilens (shown in corner plot Fig. 10)  
  > "Corner plot showing the distribution for different parameters, obtained by fitting a lens model to the CO(5-4) visibility data using visilens, described in Section 3.1.1." (Figure 10 caption)
   - validation: Residual visibility-imaged maps and comparison of source sizes with image-plane fits  
  > "The size of the submillimetre continuum found using u − v plane fitting (0 [′′] . 26±0 [′′] . 07, Table 4) is consistent with the size of 0 [′′] . 21 found using the image plane fitting." (3.1.3 The submillimetre continuum)
6. **Reconstruct velocity-resolved CO(5-4) source structure across frequency channels**: Project each frequency slice of the ALMA CO(5-4) image cube back to the source plane using the fixed SIE lens model, modelling the source in each channel as a single elliptical Gaussian.  
  > "We use the previous SIE lens model (Section 3.1.1) which allows us to fix the galaxy mass parameters, and we represent the source structure in each frequency plane as a single elliptical Gaussian, whose position, flux, width, axis ratio and position angle we allow to vary in order to fit the data." (3.1.2 ALMA observations: velocity-resolved structure in the CO(5-4) line)

## Qualitative findings

- The CO(5-4) emission shows ordered velocity structure consistent with a rotating molecular disk of ~850-2500 pc physical size.  
  > "we could be seeing a structure in ordered motion, the natural explanation of which is the presence of a rotating molecular disk. The structure is of physical dimension approximately 100–300 mas (∼ 850–2500 pc)" (4.1 The radio source and the CO(5-4) line)
- Both the radio continuum and CO(5-4) emission are spatially extended, inconsistent with a point source, and appear co-spatial, originating from a compact star-forming region.  
  > "Our results are consistent with the radio emission being emitted from the same region, but not with a point source of radio emission." (abstract); "Thus the conclusion of radio emission produced in a star-forming region appears natural." (4.1 The radio source and the CO(5-4) line)
- The submillimetre continuum source may be spatially offset from the CO(5-4) and radio emission and is more extended than the CO emitting region, though deeper observations are needed to confirm this.  
  > "Modelling of these observations gives a preliminary indication that the submillimetre continuum source, which traces the illuminated dust, may be spatially displaced from the region emitting the molecular line and radio emission" (4.1 The radio source and the CO(5-4) line)
- The A/D flux ratio in the radio and CO(5-4) line is slightly greater than 1 (much lower than the 12:1 optical anomaly) and is well reproduced by a smooth SIE lens model with an extended source, supporting microlensing as the cause of the optical anomaly.  
  > "We find the flux ratio in the radio, submillimetre continuum and CO lines to be slightly greater than 1 but much less than that in the optical, which can be reproduced with a smooth galaxy mass model and an extended source. Our results, supported by a microlensing simulation, suggest that the most likely explanation for the optical flux anomaly is indeed microlensing." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: SDSS J0924+0219 has an extreme 12:1 optical flux anomaly persisting for 15 years; longer-wavelength radio and submm/CO observations probe source sizes >> 1 uas that are immune to microlensing, while also tracing the star-forming molecular gas and radio continuum.  
  > "Measurements at longer wavelengths, where the continuum source size is ≫ 1µas and not subject to microlensing, are important" (1 INTRODUCTION); "We observed SDSS J0924+0219 in order to investigate the properties of the radio and molecular gas reservoirs in the source, and investigate the causes of the flux anomaly further." (1.1 The quasar SDSS J0924+0219)
- Why this method: Initial image-plane modelling provides easy visual assessment and necessary starting parameters for convergence in u-v plane fitting (uvmultifit and visilens); low SNR and resolution motivated simple parametric Gaussian/Sersic source models rather than pixelized source reconstruction or u-v channel modelling.  
  > "We describe both phases of this process, firstly because the initial starting model is typically necessary to get good convergence in the u − v fitting part of the process, and because it allows easy visual assessment of the fits." (3 ANALYSIS AND RESULTS); "Due to the relatively low signal to noise ratio of the data, we did not attempt to pixelize the source and use semi-linear source reconstruction (Warren & Dye 2003)." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission); "The relatively low resolution, and the complexity of the information about the source that we seek, motivate a simple analysis in which we project each of the frequency-sliced images back to the source plane, without modelling the u − v data explicitly." (3.1.2 ALMA observations: velocity-resolved structure in the CO(5-4) line)
- Alternative: Point-source model for the CO(5-4) and radio emission (rejected because residuals show clear over-subtraction at image positions and under-subtraction in nearby areas)  
  > "the resultant residual shows an over-subtraction of flux in the image positions and under-subtraction in the nearby areas (see Fig. 8, bottom left), demonstrating incompatibility of a point source with our observation." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
- Alternative: Pixelized semi-linear source reconstruction (Warren & Dye 2003) (not attempted due to relatively low signal-to-noise ratio of the data)  
  > "Due to the relatively low signal to noise ratio of the data, we did not attempt to pixelize the source and use semi-linear source reconstruction (Warren & Dye 2003)." (3.1.1 The integrated line emission)
- Alternative: 12:1 A/D flux ratio in the radio map matching the optical anomaly (rejected by constrained image-plane fit showing large residuals)  
  > "It confirms our previous result that the radio data does not demonstrate an A/D flux ratio of 12 and is more consistent with a lower flux ratio." (3.2 Radio)
- Alternative: Circular Gaussian source components per frequency slice instead of elliptical Gaussians (tested and found to yield robust source size and velocity distribution)  
  > "The size of the structure, and the overall distribution of velocities, is robust to different assumptions about the source structure, such as allowing the source components corresponding to each frequency plane to be circular Gaussians."
