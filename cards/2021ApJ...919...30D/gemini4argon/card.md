- Fields: high_z_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: To constrain the dust emissivity index and dust temperature in z ~ 1-3 870μm-selected sub-millimeter galaxies using systematic ALMA 2mm continuum observations combined with multi-wavelength far-infrared data.

## Analysis recipe

1. **Image Band 4 continuum visibilities**: Fourier transform uv visibilities and deconvolve using the clean algorithm with Briggs weighting in CASA  
  > "We generate images from the ALMA visibilities using the clean task in casa." (Data reduction and imaging); "This image is then deconvolved from the point spread function (i.e. the synthesized ‘dirty’ beam) using the clean algorithm (cite: Hogbom1974) with robust (Briggs) weighting of the visibilities; we adopt `robust`$=$0.5." (Data reduction and imaging)
   - software: CASA, clean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5, image size = 166'' x 166'', pixel scale = 0.46''  
  > "we adopt `robust`$=$0.5." (Data reduction and imaging); "Each image is $166\ensuremath{''}\times166\ensuremath{''}$, with a pixel scale of $0.46\ensuremath{''}$." (Data reduction and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified uniform noise and beam properties across all 69 maps with rms below 60 uJy/beam request  
  > "All the maps are good quality with rms below our 60 μJy beam$^{-1}$ request and fairly circular beam (the beam axis ratio varies between 1.05 and 1.11), therefore we use all the maps in a common source extraction step in the next section." (Data reduction and imaging)
2. **Extract 2mm source fluxes, stack non-detections, and apply flux deboosting**: Blind source identification above 4 sigma using boxit and 2D Gaussian fitting with imfit; prior-position extraction for 1.5 <= S/N < 4; median stacking for S/N < 1.5; analytical deboosting for blind detections  
  > "First, we identify sources with fluxes above $4\sigma$ in the maps using the boxit task, which searches the images to find contiguous sets of pixels above the given threshold, and defines a rectangular box containing those pixels. Then, we use the imfit task to fit one or more two-dimensional Gaussian functions to the sources detected in each region defined by boxit." (Source extraction and catalog matching); "For sources below $4\sigma$, since we have prior information based on the Band 7 data, we extract fluxes on their 870μm positions. 17 of those sources are detected with $1.5\le\hbox{S/N}<4$, allowing us to measure their fluxes." (Source extraction and catalog matching); "To further investigate the properties of our 29 undetected sources, we median-stack the 2mm fluxes at the positions of the 870 μm detections." (Undetected sources)
   - software: CASA, boxit, imfit
   - parameter: 4 sigma threshold for blind detection; 1.5 <= S/N < 4 for prior extraction; deboosting factor B = 1 + 0.2 ([S/N]/5)^-2.3  
  > "Therefore, we correct (i.e., deboost) the measured flux densities in both bands based on their signal-to-noise using $S_\nu^\mathrm{deboosted} = S_\nu^\mathrm{measured} / B$, where the boosting factor $B$ is given by equation 5 in (cite: Geach2017): $B=1+0.2\,([\mathrm{S/N}]/5)^{-2.3}$." (Flux deboosting)
   - assumption: Sources are unresolved at ~2.3 arcsec resolution  
  > "Our sources are unresolved at our $\sim2.3$-arcsec resolution." (Source extraction and catalog matching)
   - fitting: Two-dimensional Gaussian function fitting in image plane using imfit  
  > "Then, we use the imfit task to fit one or more two-dimensional Gaussian functions to the sources detected in each region defined by boxit." (Source extraction and catalog matching)
   - validation: Injected random artificial sources into maps with same noise and beam properties to validate deboosting relation; estimated spurious fraction (<10%) and completeness (96%)  
  > "We run simulations of this effect by injecting random sources into maps that have the same noise and synthesized beam properties as our Band 4 and Band 7 observations, and then extracting their fluxes using the same method as for the data" (Flux deboosting); "Following the method described in (cite: Karim2013; Hodge2013), we estimate the fraction of spurious sources in our data with $\hbox{S/N}\ge4$ to be less than 10%, and the corresponding completeness is 96%." (2 mm sources with no counterpart in the ALESS main catalog)
3. **Analyze 870um-to-2mm flux ratios and test for redshift evolution**: Compute S_nu[870um]/S_nu[2mm] ratios, evaluate Spearman rank correlations, and compare redshift slope against Monte Carlo trials from Casey et al. (2018a) Model B  
  > "The median flux density ratio for the 70 SMGs for which we measure a 2 mm flux density is $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}=14\pm5$" (870μm-to-2mm flux density ratios); "Thus, we fit linear relationships, weighted by signal-to-noise in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$, between these quantities both for our simulated Monte Carlo samples from the (cite: Casey2018a) model and the ALESS data, bootstrapping the latter to constrain the uncertainty in the inferred relationship." (Evolution in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$?)
   - parameter: Redshift range 1.2 < z < 5.0 for linear fit in log(1+z) vs flux ratio  
  > "Within the redshift range of the majority of the ALESS data ($1.2<z<5.0$), the redshift evolution of the millimeter color should be linear in $\log(1+z)$ versus $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$." (Evolution in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$?)
   - assumption: Casey et al. (2018a) Model B assumes fixed beta = 1.8 and no redshift evolution in T_dust  
  > "An implicit assumption of this model is that there is no redshift evolution in $T_\mathrm{dust}$  though there is a non-evolving luminosity-dependence of infrared luminosity with some intrinsic scatter. The emissivity spectral index is assumed to be fixed at $\beta=1.8$." (Evolution in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$?)
   - fitting: Linear fit weighted by signal-to-noise with bootstrapping for uncertainties  
  > "Thus, we fit linear relationships, weighted by signal-to-noise in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$, between these quantities both for our simulated Monte Carlo samples from the (cite: Casey2018a) model and the ALESS data, bootstrapping the latter to constrain the uncertainty in the inferred relationship." (Evolution in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$?)
   - validation: Tested validity of Rayleigh-Jeans approximation using simulated dust models across z = 0-10 and T_dust = 15-80 K  
  > "To test this, we generate both optically-thin and general opacity (with $\hbox{$\lambda_\mathrm{thick}$}=100\hbox{\textmu m}$, for reference) dust emission models (Section [4.1](#modeling)) with fixed $\beta=2$ and $T_\mathrm{dust}$ between 15 and 80 K. Then, we place each model at different redshifts ($z=0-10$), and compute the ratio of their flux densities $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$ at each redshift." (Testing the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation)
4. **Fit far-IR to millimeter dust SEDs to derive T_dust, beta, L_dust, and M_dust**: Bayesian SED fitting of rest-frame lambda > 70 um photometry (Herschel/SPIRE 250, 350, 500 um and ALMA Bands 8, 7, 4, 3) using isothermal modified blackbody models (optically-thin and general opacity)  
  > "We use a Bayesian approach to fit the dust SEDs of our sources and recover posterior likelihood distributions for the free dust parameters: dust temperature ($T_\mathrm{dust}$), dust emissivity index ($\beta$), dust luminosity ($L_\mathrm{dust}$), dust mass ($M_\mathrm{dust}$)." (SED fitting method); "Since we assume isothermal dust, for each galaxy we only include observations sampling the SED at wavelengths longer than 70μm in the rest-frame" (SED fitting method)
   - parameter: Priors: T_dust uniform in [15, 80] K, beta uniform in [1.0, 3.0], lambda_thick uniform in [60, 140] um (for general opacity)  
  > "We generate a model library of dust SEDs with dust temperature $T_\mathrm{dust}$ uniformly distributed between 15 and 80 K, and emissivity index $\beta$ uniformly distributed between 1.0 and 3.0. For the general opacity case, we include $\lambda_\mathrm{thick}$ as an additional free parameter and distribute it uniformly between 60μm and 140μm, based on the calculation in the previous section." (SED fitting method)
   - assumption: Isothermal dust grains in thermal equilibrium; kappa_0 = 0.77 cm^2 g^-1 at nu_0 = 353 GHz (850 um); LambdaCDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Lambda = 0.7, Omega_m = 0.3; CMB heating and contrast corrections from da Cunha et al. (2013)  
  > "Throughout the paper, we use a concordance $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $H_0=70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Lambda=0.7$, and $\Omega_m=0.3$ (cite: Planck2018)." (Introduction); "In this paper, we adopt $\kappa_0=0.77\,\mathrm{cm}^2\,\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ at $\nu_0=353$ GHz (i.e., $\lambda_0=850\hbox{\textmu m}$), to be consistent with (cite: daCunha2008; daCunha2015)." (Modeling the dust emission); "For each source at redshift $z$, we place the model dust SEDs in the observed-frame and apply the appropriate cosmic microwave background (CMB) corrections as described in (cite: daCunha2013b)." (SED fitting method)
   - fitting: Grid-based chi^2 goodness-of-fit evaluation over model library to obtain marginalized posterior likelihood distributions (median and 16th-84th percentile confidence ranges)  
  > "We compare these model fluxes with the observed fluxes (and upper limits, when available) by evaluating the $\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit of each model in the library. Then we obtain marginalized likelihood distributions for all the free parameters." (SED fitting method)
   - validation: Tested parameter recovery accuracy on 5,000 mock dust SEDs with 15% flux perturbations, and tested sensitivity to missing bands and opacity assumptions  
  > "We generate a library of $5,000$ models with dust temperatures uniformly distributed between 15 and 80 K, $\beta$ between 1 and 3, dust luminosity $\log(\hbox{$L_\mathrm{dust}$}/\hbox{$L_\odot$})$ between 11.3 and 13.5 (a luminosity range similar to that of our SMGs; (cite: daCunha2015))." (Accuracy of our fitting method); "We then fit our mock observations in the same way as we fit the actual observations in Section [4.1](#modeling)." (Accuracy of our fitting method)

## Qualitative findings

- The detection rate of SMGs at 2mm is practically independent of redshift, and preferentially selects the brightest 870um sources with highest dust masses, luminosities, SFRs, and stellar masses.  
  > "At the depth of our observations, the detection rate of our SMGs at 2mm is practically independent of redshift, and brighter SMGs (with higher 870μm fluxes and stellar masses) are the most likely to be detected." (Summary & Conclusions)
- The Rayleigh-Jeans approximation is invalid for 870um and 2mm observations (and even 1.3mm and 3mm observations) at z ~ 1-3, leading to a systematic underestimation of the dust emissivity index beta by at least 0.5.  
  > "At the typical redshift of our SMG sample ($z\simeq2.7$), the RJ approximation over-predicts the flux ratios by at least 0.15 dex, which translates to an underestimation of the emissivity index by at least $\Delta\beta=0.5$ (but the difference can be much larger for the cooler dust temperatures)." (Testing the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation)
- Assuming general dust opacity (lambda_thick = 60-140 um) instead of optically-thin dust yields dust temperatures ~10 K warmer and dust masses ~0.5 dex (factor of 3) lower, while beta and L_dust remain robust.  
  > "The parameters are well-correlated, however, we find strong systematic offsets in the derived dust temperatures: the general opacity scenario produces $T_\mathrm{dust}$ typically $\sim$10 K warmer than the optically-thin case (see also (cite: Simpson2017)). This leads to a strong offset in the inferred dust masses, which are typically 0.5 dex (i.e., a factor of 3) lower in the general opacity scenario than in the optically-thin case" (Comparison with the general opacity model results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Long-wavelength 2mm observations are needed to sample the low-frequency tail of the dust emission and break the degeneracy between dust temperature and emissivity index beta in the well-studied, unbiased ALESS 870um sample.  
  > "However, $\beta$ has only been directly measured in small samples of SMGs using observations at the long enough wavelengths needed to break the intrinsic degeneracy between $\beta$ and the cold dust temperature" (Introduction); "To provide constraints on the dust emissivity index and temperature, we use ALMA to perform a systematic study of the 2mm emission of a complete sample of 870μm-selected SMGs." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Compact C34-1 configuration (~2.3'' resolution) separates multiple SMGs in a field without resolving out individual sources (<0.5''); simple isothermal modified blackbody models are the maximum complexity supported by the number of photometric bands; Bayesian fitting avoids spurious T_dust-beta correlations produced by chi^2 minimization on noisy data.  
  > "This antenna configuration was sufficient to achieve our desired resolution of 2.3 arcsec, which allows us to separate the different sources in fields where there are multiple ALESS SMGs, while not resolving out each individual source (based on their typical sizes of $\lesssim0.5$ arcsec; (cite: Simpson2015; Hodge2016))." (Observations); "Moreover, given the relatively low number of points sampling the dust emission of our sources, such simple isothermal models are the most complexity that can be afforded." (Modeling the dust emission); "Bayesian methods such as ours are shown to produce more robust results (see also, e.g., (cite: Kelly2012)) because they treat uncertainties rigorously and self-consistently, and as a result they do not produce spurious correlations between the parameters due to measurement uncertainties." (Accuracy of our fitting method)
- Alternative: Multi-temperature dust SED models with varying emissivity indexes (rejected because the number of free parameters would exceed the available observational constraints)  
  > "Modelling the emission with multiple temperature components with varying emissivity indexes would result in a much larger number of free parameters than observational constraints, and the multiple dust parameters would be very difficult to constrain." (Modeling the dust emission)
- Alternative: Rayleigh-Jeans approximation to derive beta directly from the 870um-to-2mm flux ratio (rejected because the bands do not sample the RJ regime at z ~ 1-3, systematically underestimating beta)  
  > "We conclude that the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation cannot be used to constrain the dust emissivity indexes of our galaxies using our ALMA observations at 870μm and 2mm." (Testing the Rayleigh-Jeans approximation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The well-sampled subset of 27 SMGs is biased towards brighter, lower-redshift, and warmer-dust sources due to requiring a Herschel/SPIRE 250um detection.  
  > "By combining our ALMA measurements with existing *Herschel*/SPIRE observations, we construct a (biased) subset of 27 galaxies for which the cool dust emission is sufficiently well sampled to obtain precise constraints on their dust properties using simple isothermal models." (Abstract); "When the second criterion is applied (i.e., detections in Bands 4 and 7, plus at 250μm), we tend to select models at lower redshifts, we lose a large fraction of low $T_\mathrm{dust}$ models, and the $\beta$ distribution becomes flatter." (Selection effects)
- Limitation (authors): Current observations cannot distinguish between optically-thin and optically-thick (general opacity) dust models, introducing a factor of ~3 systematic uncertainty in dust masses, plus another factor of ~3 from the normalization of dust emissivity kappa_0.  
  > "We find that in our general opacity scenario we would obtain dust masses that are typically three times lower. Our current observations are not sufficient to distinguish between optically-thin and optically-thick dust." (Summary & Conclusions); "Uncertainties in the normalization of the dust emissivity per unit mass can lead to further $\sim$ factor of 3 systematic uncertainties in the dust masses (e.g., (cite: Galliano2018; Bianchi2019; Inoue2020))." (Summary & Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Sample size and individual source S/N are too small to robustly characterize the redshift evolution of the 870um-to-2mm flux ratio (requires samples ~5x larger).  
  > "A robust characterization of the evolution of this ratio, and its implications on measured dust temperatures and emissivity indexes will require samples $\sim5\times$ larger than currently exist." (Evolution in $S_\nu\mathrm{[870\hbox{\textmu m}]}/S_\nu\mathrm{[2mm]}$?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Isothermal dust assumption neglects temperature mixing along the line of sight, which can broaden the SED and artificially lower the inferred beta.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Herschel/SPIRE 250, 350, and 500 um photometry has much coarser angular resolution than ALMA, potentially introducing deblending systematics in fields with multiple SMGs.
