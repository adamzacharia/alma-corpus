## Science

- Fields: debris_disks, protoplanetary_disks, exoplanets, stellar_physics
- Question: Investigate the presence of circumplanetary or debris disks around two young, nearby, wide-orbit substellar companions, G 196-3 B and VHS J125601.92-125723.9 b.  
  > "We aim at investigating the presence of debris disks around two of the closest (~20 pc), young substellar companions, namely G 196-3 B and VHS J125601.92-125723.9 b (VHS J1256-1257 b), whose masses straddle the borderline between planets and brown dwarfs." (abstract); "The aim of our current study is to investigate the possibility of circumplanetary or debris disk presence in two substellar companions that reunite unique conditions of youth, proximity, and companion–host separation: VHS J125601.92-125723.9 b (from now on VHS J1256-1257 b) and G 196-3 B." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Update distance, luminosity, and mass of VHS J1256-1257 b**: Integrate observed flux to compute bolometric luminosity using Gaia EDR3 distance, and compare with BT-Settl evolutionary models to derive companion mass  
  > "With this new determination and following the approach explained in (cite: Gauza2015), the mass of VHS J1256-1257 b turns out to be 20.5^+6.0_-4.5 M_Jup, using the BT-Settl evolutionary models" (Target selection); "and the luminosity of log($L$/${\rm L_{\odot}}$) = -4.57$\pm$0.2 which we computed from the observed flux integration following the procedure described in (cite: Zakhozhay2017)." (Target selection)
   - parameter: d = 21.15+-0.21 pc, age = 150-300 Myr  
  > "The *Gaia* Early Data Release 3 (EDR3) (cite: GaiaCollaboration2016; Lindegren2021) provides a distance of 21.15$\pm$0.21 pc for the primary binary component." (Target selection); "VHS J1256-1257 is a multiple system formed by at least three low-mass objects, which is exceptional for its proximity, 22.2 pc, and young age, 150–300 Myr" (Target selection)
   - assumption: Caffau et al. (2011) solar abundances  
  > "using the BT-Settl evolutionary models (cite: 1998A&A...337..403B; 2003A&A...402..701B; Chabrier2000) with the (cite: 2011SoPh..268..255C) solar abundances" (Target selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Calibrate and image NOEMA and ALMA millimeter continuum data and measure upper limits**: Calibrate NOEMA data with GILDAS-CLIC and clean with Högbom method; calibrate ALMA data with Pipeline 42254 and image with CASA tclean in mfs mode; measure 1-sigma rms noise and adopt 3-sigma flux upper limits  
  > "Data calibration was performed with the GILDAS-CLIC software (sep-2019 version). Continuum was obtained by averaging line-free channels over the 7.744 MHz width (USB) centered at 230.0 GHz." (NOEMA); "The calibrated data were then imaged with the tclean task of the same CASA package in mfs mode, that is, combining all spectral channels into one image." (ALMA); "We built the spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of both targets by adopting the flux upper limits with a 3-sigma confidence for all NOEMA, ALMA and VLA data." (Dusty disk mass upper limits)
   - software: GILDAS-CLIC, CASA, tclean
   - parameter: NOEMA: robust = 3, 1700 iterations; ALMA: pixel size = 0.16'', natural weighting  
  > "Images were cleaned using robust weighting with the Högbom’s method (cite: hogbom2003) and a robust parameter of 3. To obtain the convergence of the flux 1700 iterations were used." (NOEMA); "VHS J1256-1257 data were imaged as a single field with a pixel size of 0.16'' and natural weighting in order to optimize the point-source sensitivity." (ALMA)
   - assumption: 3-sigma detection threshold for point-source upper limits  
  > "corresponding to minimum detected signals of 0.153 mJy and 0.108 mJy (adopting a 3-sigma detection limit)." (NOEMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tapering NOEMA baselines above 50 m projected length to verify non-detection  
  > "Nevertheless, even by tapering baselines above 50 m projected length, no detections were found." (NOEMA)
3. **Estimate disk outer radii and characteristic dust temperatures**: Compute disk outer radius as 1/3 of the Hill sphere radius, and evaluate dust temperature from companion luminosity and primary irradiation  
  > "The temperature of the dust was evaluated using the relation of (cite: vanderPlas2016): $T_{\rm d} = a\: (L_{\ast}/L_{\odot})^{b}$" (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "As in (cite: Shabram_2013) and (cite: Rab2019), for our targets, we assumed the disk outer radius (R_d) as that given by 1/3 of the Hill sphere radius" (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "For this "close" companion, we computed the characteristic dust temperature assuming T^*4_d = T_d^4 + T_ir^4" (Dusty disk mass upper limits)
   - parameter: For R_d = 10-35 AU: [a,b] = [41, 0.22]; for R_d < 10 AU: [a,b] = [57, 0.23]; for R_d = 40-70 AU: [a,b] = [25, 0.17]; for R_d > 90 AU: [a,b] = [22, 0.16]  
  > "for R_d<10 AU we used [a,b] = [57,0.23], for R_d = 10-35 AU: [a,b] = [41,0.22], for R_d = 40-70 AU: [a,b] = [25,0.17], for R_d > 90 AU: [a,b] = [22,0.16]." (Footnote 9)
   - assumption: Disk outer radius is 1/3 of the Hill radius; companions at separations > 220 au are treated as isolated for heating; for separations <= 220 au, irradiation from primary is added assuming distance to dust equals companion separation and no intervening material  
  > "We made the general assumption that disk-host companions at separations larger than 220 au from their primaries are far enough and the disk heating from the companions is dominating over the hitting induced by the brighter primaries" (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "roughly assuming that the distance to the dust equals to the separation of the companion and that there is no disk material in between them." (Dusty disk mass upper limits)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Calculate dust opacities and derive dusty disk mass upper limits**: Compute wavelength-dependent dust opacities with OpacityTool (and Beckwith et al. 1990 extrapolation for VLA) and calculate optically-thin dust mass upper limits for target companions and literature sample  
  > "To compute the mass upper limits on any hypothetical dusty disk surrounding VHS J1256-1257 b and G 196-3 B, we used the following equation (cite: Beckwith1990; Ricci2017), which is valid for optically-thin, gas-depleted disks" (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "The wavelength-dependent opacity $\kappa_{\nu}$ was calculated for the NOEMA and ALMA data using the code `O`pacityTool available through the Project Diana" (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "Then, we computed dust mass upper limits for all objects in Table 2 following the procedure described in Section 4." (Comparison to other substellar companions)
   - software: OpacityTool
   - parameter: kappa_nu = 4.9 cm^2/g at 340 GHz (0.88 mm), 2.3 cm^2/g at 230 GHz (1.3 mm), 0.33 cm^2/g at 33 GHz (0.9 cm), 0.10 cm^2/g at 10 GHz (3 cm), 0.06 cm^2/g at 6 GHz (5 cm), 0.014 cm^2/g at 1.4 GHz (21.4 cm)  
  > "$\kappa_{\nu} = 4.9$ cm$^2$/g at $\nu$ = 340 GHz ($\lambda$ = 0.88 mm)" (footnote 8); "$\kappa_{\nu} = 0.10$ cm$^2$/g at $\nu$ = 10 GHz ($\lambda$ = 3 cm)" (footnote 8)
   - assumption: Optically-thin, gas-depleted disks; grain size distribution from 0.1 to 100 um with power-law -3.5, porosity 0.25, carbonaceous mass/volume fraction 0.13/0.15  
  > "which is valid for optically-thin, gas-depleted disks (i.e., we assumed that very little gas is left by the ages of our targets)" (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "except for the dust particles size distribution that we used for the range from 0.1 to 100 mu m." (Dusty disk mass upper limits); "dust particles size distribution from 0.005 mu m to 3000 mu m with the powerlaw of -3.5, the porosity (so the volume fraction of vacuum) of 0.25, mass and volume fraction of carbonaceous material of 0.13 and 0.15, respectively." (Footnote 7)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Recompute dust masses under uniform 20 K assumption for population comparison**: Recompute dust mass upper limits and detections for substellar companions and stellar debris disks assuming constant T_dust = 20 K and kappa_nu = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1 to compare with protoplanetary disks from Testi et al. (2022)  
  > "For a proper comparison with the data of (cite: Testi2022), we redid our computations of the disk dust mass upper limits for the substellar companions following the same adoption" (Comparison to free-floating sources); "For consistency, we computed the dust masses and dust mass upper limits for all debris disks ((cite: Sepulveda2019; Marino2020; Sullivan2022), Matrà 2022, in preparation) following the approach of (cite: Testi2022)." (Comparison to free-floating sources)
   - parameter: T_dust = 20 K, kappa_nu = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "For the computation of the disk dust masses around central objects with masses from substellar to several times the solar mass, (cite: Testi2022) adopted $\kappa_{\nu}$ = 2.3 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ and a constant $T_{dust}$ = 20 K." (Comparison to free-floating sources)
   - assumption: Constant dust temperature of 20 K across all objects; stellar mass-luminosity relation M_* = L_*^0.25 for M_* > 0.43 M_sun and M_* = (L_*/0.23)^(1/2.3) for < 0.43 M_sun  
  > "we used a simple mass—luminosity relation $M_{\ast} = L_{\ast}^{0.25}$ (where both parameters are in solar units) applicable to the masses \> 0.43 M$_{\odot}$ (cite: Duric2003)" (Comparison to free-floating sources); "for it we recomputed the mass, using the following relation: $M_{\ast} = (L_{\ast}/0.23)^{1/2.3}$, following the prescription by (cite: Duric2003)." (footnote 12)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Neither G 196-3 B nor VHS J1256-1257 b is detected in millimeter or centimeter continuum emission.  
  > "Neither G 196-3 B nor VHS J1256-1257 b were detected in the NOEMA, ALMA and VLA data." (abstract)
- Sub-mm and mm detections of disks in the substellar regime only occur for very young (< 10 Myr) sources (OTS44, SR 12 c, PDS 70 c), while no disks are detected around older brown dwarfs despite deeper mass sensitivity.  
  > "The only sub-mm and mm detections in the substellar regime (OTS44, SR 12 c and PDS 70 c) in Figure 4 correspond to sources with an age of less than 10 Myr, while there is no single disk detection around brown dwarfs with older ages despite the observations are more sensitive (due to the closer distances)." (Comparison to free-floating sources)
- Stellar debris disks form a sequence in dust mass versus host mass that is parallel to, and lower than, protoplanetary disks for central masses between ~0.5 and ~2 M_sun.  
  > "Interestingly, the sequence described by the debris disks appears to be parallel to that of the protoplanetary disks for the central object mass interval between $\sim$0.5 and $\sim$2 M$_\odot$." (Comparison to free-floating sources)
- Extrapolating the stellar debris disk sequence into the substellar domain indicates that sub-mm and mm observations must be at least 100 times deeper to detect substellar debris disks.  
  > "the extrapolation of the debris disk stellar sequence into the substellar domain using the same decreasing slope observed for protoplanetary disks indicates that sub-mm and mm observations must be at least 100 times deeper than current observations in order to detect the presence of substellar debris disks" (Comparison to free-floating sources)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G 196-3 B and VHS J1256-1257 b are two of the closest (~20 pc), young (50-300 Myr) substellar companions at wide separations with strong infrared excesses, making them prime candidates for second-generation debris disks.  
  > "Both are companions at wide orbits (>=100 au) of M-type dwarfs and their ages (50–100 Myr and 150–300 Myr, respectively) are thought to be adequate for the detection of second generation disks." (abstract); "The aim of our current study is to investigate the possibility of circumplanetary or debris disk presence in two substellar companions that reunite unique conditions of youth, proximity, and companion–host separation: VHS J125601.92-125723.9 b (from now on VHS J1256-1257 b) and G 196-3 B." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Natural weighting in tclean was chosen to optimize point-source sensitivity; a constant T_dust = 20 K and kappa_nu = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1 was also used to enable direct comparison with the protoplanetary disk sample of Testi et al. (2022).  
  > "VHS J1256-1257 data were imaged as a single field with a pixel size of 0.16'' and natural weighting in order to optimize the point-source sensitivity." (ALMA); "For a proper comparison with the data of (cite: Testi2022), we redid our computations of the disk dust mass upper limits for the substellar companions following the same adoption" (Comparison to free-floating sources)
- Alternative: Tapering NOEMA baselines above 50 m projected length was tested to search for extended emission, still yielding no detection.  
  > "Nevertheless, even by tapering baselines above 50 m projected length, no detections were found." (NOEMA)
- Alternative: Excluded LkCa15 b, HD 100546 b, and V1400 Cen from the comparison sample due to uncertain/unconstrained companion masses and questioned existence.  
  > "Excluded from Tables 1 and 2 are LkCa15 b and HD 100546 b presented in (cite: Ricci2017) and (cite: Pineda2019), because their substellar mass and luminosity parameters are very uncertain." (Comparison to other substellar companions); "Also excluded is the V1400 Cen system (cite: Mamajek2012; Kenworthy2015; Kenworthy2020) because of the unconstrained mass of the companion (see also the Introduction section)." (Comparison to other substellar companions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Dust opacity kappa_nu has large uncertainties due to unknown dust physical properties and internal disk processes.  
  > "$\kappa_{\nu}$ suffers from a large uncertainty since it depends not only on the dust physical properties (e.g. (cite: Semenov2003; 2016A&A...586A.103W)) but on the physical processes happening inside the disk as well (cite: Isella2014; Wu2022)." (Dusty disk mass upper limits)
- Limitation (authors): Significant differences (up to a factor of 15 for VHS J1256-1257 b, or an order of magnitude compared to RADMC-3D models) arise from different dust temperature assumptions.  
  > "The main reason is the radiative transfer model (RADMC-3D) used by (cite: Perez2019) that gives mostly lower dust temperatures by ~30 K for disk sizes in the interval 1–10 au" (Comparison to other substellar companions); "There are significant differences between our computations of disk dust mass upper limits and those obtained by adopting a constant dust temperature. As an example, our VHS J1256-1257 b’s estimate is 15 times larger than the one from the disk constant temperature." (Comparison to free-floating sources)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): At T_d = 4.5 K and 5.3 K, the radiative equilibrium temperatures calculated from the van der Plas et al. (2016) scaling are very close to the CMB temperature (2.73 K), where standard Planck-function optically-thin mass estimates without CMB background correction can be sensitive to temperature floors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The van der Plas et al. (2016) empirical temperature-luminosity parameterization was calibrated on younger protoplanetary disks around low-mass stars/brown dwarfs and may not accurately describe gas-depleted debris disks around 50-300 Myr companions.
