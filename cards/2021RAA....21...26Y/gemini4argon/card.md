## Analysis recipe

1. **Select cores and measure core radii**: Identify the peak of N2H+ integrated intensity as the core center, fit a 1D Gaussian function to the radial profile of emission intensity, and define the core radius R as 2 sigma of the Gaussian fit.

(Measurement of the core Radius); "We find that the intensity of the emission line as a function of the distance to the core center may be fit by a Gaussian function (see the red lines in Fig. [6](#fig:r_omc3_mms7) and [7](#fig:r_omc3_m))." (Measurement of the core Radius); "In the text, we take the core radius, $R$, to be $2 \sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the best fit Gaussian function." (Measurement of the core Radius)
   - parameter: R = 2 sigma (also tested 1 sigma and 3 sigma)  
  > "In the text, we take the core radius, $R$, to be $2 \sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the best fit Gaussian function." (Measurement of the core Radius); "To examine whether the choice of the core radius affects our conclusion, here we calculate the Collapsing Indexes with $R$ set to 1$\sigma$ and 3$\sigma$." (Measurement of the core Radius)
   - assumption: Cores are spherically symmetric objects  
  > "We treat the selected cores as spherically symmetric objects and identify the position where the molecular line emission peaks as the center." (Measurement of the core Radius)
   - fitting: Gaussian function fit to radial intensity profile  
  > "We find that the intensity of the emission line as a function of the distance to the core center may be fit by a Gaussian function (see the red lines in Fig. [6](#fig:r_omc3_mms7) and [7](#fig:r_omc3_m))." (Measurement of the core Radius)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity of Collapsing Index to R = 1 sigma, 2 sigma, and 3 sigma; also calculated ellipsoid correction factors for gravitational potential energy.  
  > "The resulting CI values are listed in Table [2](#tab:diffci), which shows that, for both OMC3-mms7 and OMC3-m, the different choices for the core radius change the CI only slightly."    - assumption: Uniform kinetic temperature across each core; optically thin line emission  
  > "The kinetic temperature, derived from JVLA NH$_3$ observations, varies only slightly within the cores (cite: li2013), and we adopt the average kinetic temperature of 15.7 K and 13.4 K for OMC3-mms7 and OMC3-m, respectively." 3. **Calculate the Collapsing Index (CI)**: Compute the average non-thermal velocity dispersion squared in the inner region (b <= R/2) and outer region (b > R/2), then evaluate CI = (sigma_in^2 - sigma_out^2) / sigma_out^2 and compare against the critical turbulent threshold of 0.3.

(Collapsing Index); "We adopt 0.3, the expected CI if only turbulence contributes to $\sigma_{m NT}$, as the critical value to determine whether a prestellar core is collapsing." (Collapsing Index)
   - parameter: Inner/outer boundary at impact parameter b = R/2; critical threshold CI = 0.3  
  > "in the inner region with $b\le R/2$, while $\sigma_{m out}=\left \langle \sigma_{m NT-outer}^2 ight angle$ is the average over the outer region with $b> R/2$."    - assumption: Kolmogorov scaling law for statistically homogeneous and isotropic turbulence in non-collapsing cores; Singular Isothermal Sphere (SIS) free-fall velocity profile for collapsing cores  
  > "To estimate the turbulent velocity dispersion, we make use of the Kolmogorov scaling law (cite: kolmogorov1941) for inertial-range scales," (Theoretical expectation); "To estimate the contribution of the collapse, we adopt the singular isothermal sphere (SIS) model of (cite: shu1977), for the collapse velocity," (Theoretical expectation)

  > "As a supportive clue of its dynamic status, the asymmetric profiles of the molecular lines $^{13}$CO (J=1-0) and C$^{18}$O (J=1-0) in the dense core region show signatures of possibly infall for OMC3-mms7." (Collapsing Index)

## Qualitative findings

- Gravitational infall causes a rapid increase in optically thin line width toward the core center, whereas homogeneous turbulent line broadening decreases slightly only at the core edge and remains nearly flat toward the center.  
  > "In particular, gravitational infall motion would give rise to a rapid increase of line width toward the core center, whereas the contribution from turbulence remains almost invariant with decreasing radius." (Introduction)
- OMC3-mms7 has initiated gravitational collapse, whereas OMC3-m is turbulence-dominated and in hydrostatic balance.  
  > "For OMC3-mms7, we find that CI is much larger than 0.3, suggesting that gravitational collapse has already initiated in this core." (Collapsing Index); "On the other hand, the CI of OMC3-m is close to 0.3, indicating that its non-thermal line width is dominated by turbulence, and the core has not started to collapse." (Collapsing Index)
- Three isolated low-mass starless cores (L183, L1498, L1517B) all have CI significantly smaller than 0.3, indicating they are in hydrostatic balance and not undergoing global free-fall collapse.  
  > "The CI for all these isolated starless cores is found to be significantly smaller than 0.3, suggesting that they are not collapsing (cf. Fig. [3](#fig:2panel_core))." (Collapsing Index)
- Non-thermal turbulent velocity dispersions in all five studied cores (both OMC and isolated low-mass cores) are subsonic.  
  > "The velocity dispersions in all the cores are subsonic, which validates our theoretical treatment based on the scaling behavior of incompressible or weakly compressible turbulence." (Collapsing Index)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA combined with Nobeyama provides high spatial resolution (~3" or 0.006 pc, smaller than the ~0.01 pc thermal Jeans scale) and high sensitivity (~12 mJy/beam, >2x deeper than Hacar et al. 2018) in N2H+ (J=1-0), an optically thin and chemically stable tracer that probes deep into dense cores.  
  > "A meaningful measurement of CI requires spatially and spectrally resolved images of optically thin and chemically stable dense gas tracers." (Collapsing Index: A New Method to Identify Star-forming Cores Based on ALMA Images); "The spatial resolution is $\sim$3", corresponding to 0.006 pc, smaller than the thermal Jeans scale $\sim 0.01$ pc; the rms noise is $\sim$12 mJy/beam, and the velocity resolution is 0.11 km/s." (Selected Orion Cores); "For the cores selected for this study, our data are more than 2 times deeper compared to (cite: hacar2018) of 25 mJy/beam, thus provide better dynamic range." - Why this method: Canonical column density radial profile methods suffer from projection effects and resolution limits, whereas turbulent motions and gravitational infall have distinct spatial line-width behaviors along the radius of a core.  
  > "However, due to the projection effect and the resolution limit, the observed column density profile can hardly distinguish different theoretical models unambiguously" (Introduction); "In particular, gravitational infall motion would give rise to a rapid increase of line width toward the core center, whereas the contribution from turbulence remains almost invariant with decreasing radius." (Introduction)
- Alternative: Including core rotation and bipolar outflows in the line-width model was rejected because adding at least three more parameters (scaling, radial distribution of rotation velocity, inclination angle) would smear the distinct signatures of gravity and turbulence.  
  > "If rotation is considered, at least three more parameters, i.e. the scaling and radial distribution of the rotation velocity, and the inclination angle of the rotation axis have to be added in the model." (Collapsing Index); "The increase of complication will smear the different features of gravity and turbulence found in this paper." (Collapsing Index)
- Alternative: Using density-weighted integration of the infall velocity dispersion (Equation B.7) was tested and rejected in favor of the unweighted analytical approximation (Equation 4) because both give qualitatively very similar radial profiles.  
  > "As seen in Fig. [5](#fig:density_variation), the behaviors of the blue and black lines with and without density weighting are qualitatively very similar, suggesting that, as a first order approximation, Equation [\[equ:vginter\]](#equ:vginter) in the text well captures the key features" (Derivations of equations in Section 2 and the effects of the core density profile)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The speculation that collapse-induced turbulent velocity scales as r^-1/2 is based on dimensional analysis and needs to be tested by future numerical simulations.  
  > "Our speculation is subject to tests by future numerical simulations of turbulence in a collapsing core." (Theoretical expectation)
- Limitation (authors): The CI method does not detect very initial stage, small-amplitude contracting motions or envelope expansion with core collapse (EECC) motions.  
  > "Our CI method, as shown in Fig. [3](#fig:2panel_core), does not identify these possible initial stage, small amplitude core contracting motion or EECC motion as core collapse." (Collapsing Index)
- Limitation (authors): The method is currently restricted to approximately spherical cores with negligible rotation and minimal influence from bipolar outflows.  
  > "We have restricted our study to a selection of prestellar cores with spherical morphology that are not significantly affected by rotation or bipolar outflows." (Conclusion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The analytical model assumes uniform weighting along the line of sight in the main text; while Appendix A shows density weighting (rho^2) gives a qualitatively similar curve, the exact value of the critical CI threshold and fitted v_g(R) could shift quantitatively under realistic density and abundance profiles.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): N2H+ (J=1-0) has hyperfine structure; the paper mentions using SCOUSE (a multi-Gaussian fitter) rather than an explicit hyperfine-structure fitting routine, without specifying how the 7 hyperfine components or optical depth were handled when extracting the intrinsic line width.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): OMC3-mms7 is associated with a known bipolar outflow ~10" north of the core center; although argued to be on the periphery, outflow-driven shocks or unresolved velocity gradients could still contaminate the central line width.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Uncertainties on the fitted parameters u_t(R) and v_g(R) and on the measured CI values are not reported.
