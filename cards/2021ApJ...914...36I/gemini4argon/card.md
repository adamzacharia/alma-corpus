## Reasoning

- Why this data: J1243+0100 is the only known low-luminosity (M_1450 > -25 mag) quasar at z > 7, close to the knee of the QLF, allowing a less-biased study of early SMBH-galaxy co-evolution and feedback.  
  > "Therefore, sensitive observations of lower luminosity objects, even at $z>7$, are necessary to gain a less-biased picture of early SMBH/galaxy evolution." (Introduction); "Our target of this work, HSC J124353.93$+$010038.5 (hereafter J1243$+$0100), is the only low-luminosity quasar known at $z > 7$, discovered by (cite: 2019ApJ...872L...2M)." (Our target: J1243+0100)
- Why this method: Fitting visibilities in the uv plane avoids deconvolution uncertainties when separating point-source and extended emission; extracting the spectrum within the 2 sigma contour of the moment-0 map yields lower noise than a circular aperture for resolved complex sources.  
  > "Doing this fit in the $uv$ plane avoids uncertainties in the deconvolution process (cite: 2015ApJ...810..133I; 2017ApJ...835..286I; 2019ApJ...887..107F; 2020ApJ...900....1F)." (Continuum properties); "This method gives lower noise than measurement within a circular aperture, particularly when the source is resolved and complex (cite: 2020A&A...643A...2B)." (Line spectrum)
- Alternative: Single Gaussian fit to the [C II] line spectrum, rejected due to poor fit (chi^2/d.o.f = 50.1/27) compared to a double Gaussian fit (chi^2/d.o.f = 16.5/25).  
  > "Given this broad component, the spectrum is poorly fit with a single Gaussian (Figure 4a, Table 2) with a returned $\chi^2$/d.o.f = 50.1/27 (estimated over $\pm 1000$ km s$^{-1}$). A double Gaussian fit (Figure 4b, Table 2), both centered on the same frequency, gives much better results: $\chi^2$/d.o.f. = 16.5/25." (Line spectrum)
- Alternative: Close companion galaxies or merger-induced shocks as the origin of the broad [C II] wing, rejected due to lack of detected companions within 3'', symmetric line profile, compact wing size (<0''.52), and rotation-dominated host kinematics.  
  > "It could be due to (A) close companion/merger galaxies or (B) fast \[C II\] outflows (cite: 2021arXiv210101199I). However, we do not consider cenario (A) to be likely for a number of reasons" (Nature of the broad wing component)
- Alternative: Starburst-driven outflow as the origin of the broad [C II] wing, rejected because the mass loading factor (>= 5-9) and velocity extent (~900 km s^-1) exceed typical starburst-driven values.  
  > "It is also rare for starburst-driven neutral outflows to reach velocities greater than $\sim 500$–600 km s$^{-1}$ even for ULIRG-class objects (cite: 2005ApJ...621..227M; 2005ApJS..160..115R; 2018MNRAS.473.1909G; 2020A&A...633A..90G), whereas the observed \[C II\] profile of J1243$+$0100 extends to $\sim 900$ km s$^{-1}$. Thus we conclude that the fast \[C II\] outflow of J1243$+$0100 is *quasar-driven*." (Nature of the broad wing component)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Single-band continuum observations cannot constrain T_dust and beta; varying T_dust between 35 and 60 K changes L_FIR from 1.7 to 6.1 x 10^12 L_sun.  
  > "For example, varying $T_{\rm dust}$ over the range $35-60$ K (cite: 2013ApJ...772..103L; 2010MNRAS.409...75H; 2020MNRAS.494.3828D) with $\beta = 1.6$ results in $L_{\rm FIR} = (1.7-6.1) \times 10^{12}~L_\odot$. Continuum measurements over a range of wavelengths are needed to constrain $T_{\rm dust}$." (Continuum properties)
- Limitation (authors): The spatial extent of the [C II] broad wing in the uv plane is only 1.7 sigma from zero, requiring use of a 3 sigma upper limit.  
  > "We inferred a spatial extent of ${\rm FWHM} = 0\ensuremath{''}.29 \pm 0\ensuremath{''}.17$ or $1.5 \pm 0.9$ kpc. This extent is only $1.7\sigma$ from zero, so Table 4 also lists the $3\sigma$ limit of the extent (2.7 kpc)." (Decomposed [C II] spatial extent)
- Limitation (authors): Quoted error on M_dyn does not include uncertainties in disk inclination angle or geometry of the line-emitting region.  
  > "The quoted error does not include the uncertainties of the inclination angle or the geometry of the line-emitting region." (Dynamical mass)
- Limitation (authors): The bulge mass estimate from rotation curve modeling is tentative due to limited spatial resolution (~0''.6-0''.7 beam vs 0''.1 rings).  
  > "The core gas dynamics are governed by rotation, with a rotation curve suggestive of a compact bulge ($\sim 3.3 \times 10^{10}~M_\odot$), although it is not yet spatially resolved." (Abstract); "However, our resolution is limited, thus this estimate of $M_{\rm bulge}$ is *tentative*." (Gas dynamical modeling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3DBarolo tilted-ring model fits 6 rings separated by 0''.1 across a radius of 0''.6, which heavily oversamples the 0''.70 x 0''.56 beam; the inner rotation curve and inferred compact bulge mass may be strongly affected by beam smearing and fixed inclination/center assumptions.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Both M_dyn and M_bulge rely on small inclination angles (20.5 deg and 25 deg), where sin(i) corrections amplify any axis-ratio measurement errors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The double Gaussian spectral fit forces the broad wing to share the exact systemic velocity center of the narrow core, which could bias the wing flux and width if the outflow is asymmetric.
