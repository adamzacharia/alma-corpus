## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure 1.1 mm continuum source properties and spectral index of the extended jet**: Fit a 2D Gaussian to the compact 1.1 mm continuum source to derive flux, position, and size; measure flux of the extended jet component and compare with literature 3.6 cm flux to compute spectral index.  
  > "Based on a 2D Gaussian fit, we derive the flux, position and size of the compact continuum source." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "Reipurth et al. (2004) measured a 3.6 cm flux of 0.10±0.01 mJy towards GV Tau N. We estimate a flux of 4.6±0.5 mJy for the extended component, which would imply a 3.6 cm to 1.1 mm spectral index, α 1 [3] . [.] 1 [6] mm [cm] [∼] [+][1.1.]" (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
   - parameter: Position of continuum source adopted as offset (0",0") at alpha(J2000)=04h29m23.731s, delta(J2000)=24d33m01.11s  
  > "It should be noted that the coordinates of the compact continuum source are α(J2000) = 04 [h] 29 [m] 23 [s] .731, δ(J2000) = 24 [◦] 33 [′] 01 [′′] .11." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "The position of the continuum source is adopted as offset (0 [′′],0 [′′] ) through this paper." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
   - assumption: d = 180±17 pc  
  > "GV Tau (Haro 6-10) is a T Tauri binary system embedded in the L1524 molecular cloud (d=180±17 pc) (Gaia Collaboration 2018)." (1 INTRODUCTION)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit  
  > "Based on a 2D Gaussian fit, we derive the flux, position and size of the compact continuum source." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
   - validation: Comparison of derived flux and position with previous PdBI measurements by Fuente et al. (2012) and Gaia DR2 proper motions  
  > "The estimated flux is ∼48 mJy which is in good agreement with the previous measurement by Fuente et al. (2012)." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "Taking into account the lower angular resolution of Fuente et al. (2012), beam∼0.5 [′′], we consider that the observed displacement can be due to the proper motions of GV Tau N." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
2. **Map HCN 3→2 morphology and kinematics and extract PdBI/ALMA spectra**: Generate zero, first, and second moment maps (using channels >3sigma for moment 0), channel maps, and position-velocity diagrams along outflow axes (Jet 1 at PA=222 deg, Jet 2 at PA=195 deg) and perpendicular disc axes (D1, D2); overlay Keplerian velocity curves on D1 p-v diagram.  
  > "We have produced the zero-moment map using only the channels with emission >3σ." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "In Fig. 4 we present the first and second moment maps of the HCN 3→2 emisison as observed with ALMA." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS); "In order to explore the kinematics of the circumbinary disc, we performed the p-v diagrams along the directions perpendicular to Jet 1 and Jet 2, named D 1 and D 2, respectively (Fig. 5)." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
   - parameter: Threshold >3sigma (sigma = 2.4 mJy/beam) for zero-moment map  
  > "We have produced the zero-moment map using only the channels with emission >3σ." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
   - parameter: Jet 1 PA = 222 deg, Jet 2 PA = 195 deg, systemic velocity v_lsr = 6.3 km s^-1  
  > "red-shifted from the systemic velocity of v lsr = 6.3 km s [−][1]" (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "the red lobe is close to the direction defined by the giant Herbig Haro flow, PA=222 [◦] (Devine et al. 1999) (hereafter, Jet 1), and the blue lobe is placed at PA=195 [◦], the direction of the Herbig Haro jet detected by Movsessian & Magakian (1999) (hereafter, Jet 2)." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
   - assumption: Stellar mass M_* = 0.8 M_sun and trial inclinations i = 20 deg, 40 deg, and 65 deg for Keplerian curves  
  > "For comparison we compare the observations with the Keplerian curves for a star with M ∗ =0.8 M ⊙ and inclination angles i=20 [◦], 40 [◦], and 65 [◦] ." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
   - fitting: Visual comparison of p-v diagram along D1 with Keplerian rotation curves  
  > "We find reasonable agreement between the observations in the inner R<20 au region and the Keplerian curves assuming i=40 [◦] curve." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
   - validation: Comparison of ALMA integrated HCN 3→2 flux with PdBI flux from Fuente et al. (2012) to evaluate interferometric filtering (~40% recovered)  
  > "In order to check for possible filtering in the HCN 3→2 line map, we have compared ALMA fluxes with those measured by Fuente et al. (2012) using the PdBI." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "Therefore, the high spatial resolution ALMA observations recover ∼ 40% of the flux detected in PdBI observations." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
3. **Decompose and fit Herschel/HIFI far-IR line profiles**: Fit Gaussian components to Herschel/HIFI spectra of H2O, 13CO 10→9, HCN 7→6, and CN 5→4, decomposing water lines into a wide (FWHP ~ 8 km s^-1) and a narrow (FWHP ~ 2.7 km s^-1) component.  
  > "the profile of the water lines can be fitted using two components: i) a wide Gaussian with" (4 FAR INFRARED LINES); "Indeed, the profile of the water lines can be fitted using two components: i) a wide Gaussian with FWHP∼ 8 km s [−][1], and (ii) a narrower component with FWHP∼2.7 km s [−][1], similar to [13] CO." (4 FAR INFRARED LINES)
   - software: HIPE, GILDAS/CLASS
   - parameter: HIFI beam sizes: 40" (556.94 GHz, 566.95 GHz), 35" (620.30 GHz), 20" (987.93 GHz), 18.5" (1101.35 GHz, 1113.34 GHz)  
  > "o-H 2 O 1 10 →1 01 556.94 40 0.93 ( 0.02) 7.52 ( 0.09) 7.92 ( 0.23) 0.11" (Table A1); "p-H 2 O 1 11 →0 00 1113.34 18.5 2.14 ( 0.05) 7.58 ( 0.08) 7.42 (0.20) 0.27" (Table A1); "HCN 7→6 620.30 35 0.14 ( 0.03) 7.02 ( 0.37) 3.71 ( 1.14) 0.04" (Table A1)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fitting  
  > "The Gaussian fits to these components are shown in Table A1." (4 FAR INFRARED LINES)
   - validation: Comparison of wide p-H2O 2_02→1_11 line wings with the spatially averaged ALMA HCN 3→2 spectrum inside R<0.3"  
  > "In order to explore the origin of this component, we compare its profile with the mean HCN 3→2 spectrum in the region R<0.3” (see Fig. 3)." (4 FAR INFRARED LINES)
4. **Derive molecular column densities and abundance ratios across spatial scales**: Analyze line intensity ratios and fit line profiles under Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) for FIR (H2O, 13CO, HCN, CN) and mm interferometric (PdBI 13CO 3→2 and R<0.18" averaged ALMA HCN 3→2) transitions; compare with NIR absorption values.  
  > "In the optically thin limit, and assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), we estimate R LTE (H 2 O@987 GHz/1113 GHz)=0.5 at 200 K, and increases with temperature until ∼0.63 for T k >500 K." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY); "We can fit the PdBI [13] CO 3→2 line profile, assuming LTE, T rot =250−500 K and N( [13] CO)=(2.6−5.0)×10 [17] cm [−][2] ." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY); "The R<0.18” averaged profile of the HCN 3→2 emission can be fitted with T rot =250−500 K and N(HCN)=(2.03.5)×10 [15] cm [−][2]" (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
   - parameter: Averaging radius R < 0.18" for ALMA HCN 3→2 to match PdBI 13CO 3→2 beam (~0.3")  
  > "For this comparison we use the averaged HCN 3→2 spectrum in R<0.18”, since the beam of [13] CO 3→2 observations is ∼ 0.3”." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
   - assumption: Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)  
  > "In the optically thin limit, and assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE), we estimate R LTE (H 2 O@987 GHz/1113 GHz)=0.5 at 200 K" (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
   - assumption: T_rot ~ 500 K and source size 0.5" for wide FIR H2O component; T_rot = 250 K and source size 18.5" for narrow FIR 13CO, HCN, and CN lines  
  > "assuming T rot ∼ 500 K as derived from HCN absorption observations, optically thick emission, and a source size of 0.5” (see Fig. 1), we predict T mb ∼ 0.23 K for the pH 2 O lines after diluting to the Herschel beam" (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY); "In these cases, the lines show narrow profile and therefore, we assume a lower rotation temperature T rot =250 K, similar to those measured from NIR absorption lines of [13] CO (Gibb et al. 2007, 2008)." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY); "In order to make a fair comparison between molecules, we assumed a source size of 18.5 ” ." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
   - assumption: T_rot = 250-500 K for mm interferometric 13CO 3→2 and HCN 3→2 emission  
  > "We can fit the PdBI [13] CO 3→2 line profile, assuming LTE, T rot =250−500 K and N( [13] CO)=(2.6−5.0)×10 [17] cm [−][2] ." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
   - fitting: LTE line ratio and profile modeling  
  > "We can fit the PdBI [13] CO 3→2 line profile, assuming LTE, T rot =250−500 K and N( [13] CO)=(2.6−5.0)×10 [17] cm [−][2] ." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
   - validation: Consistency check predicting T_mb ~ 0.23 K for optically thick p-H2O at 500 K diluted in Herschel beam, matching observed T_mb (0.25-0.27 K)  
  > "assuming T rot ∼ 500 K as derived from HCN absorption observations, optically thick emission, and a source size of 0.5” (see Fig. 1), we predict T mb ∼ 0.23 K for the pH 2 O lines after diluting to the Herschel beam, which is fully consistent with Herschel observations." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of H2O (o-H2O 1_10→1_01, p-H2O 1_11→0_00, and p-H2O 2_02→1_11) towards GV Tau N using Herschel/HIFI.  
  > "These observations constitute the first detection of H 2 O towards GV Tau N." (ABSTRACT); "We present the first detection of water towards GV Tau (see Fig. 2). In particular, three water lines, p-H 2 O 1 11 →0 00, o-H 2 O 1 10 →1 01 and p-H 2 O 2 02 →1 11 have been detected." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)
- ALMA resolves a compact 1.1 mm dusty disc (R~8 au), an elongated continuum jet, an asymmetric HCN 3→2 molecular ring/disc eroded along the jet direction, and one or two misaligned molecular outflows.  
  > "Moreover, ALMA high spatial resolution (∼ 7 au) images of the continuum at 1.1 mm and the HCN 3→2 line resolve different gas components towards GV Tau N, a gaseous disc with R∼25 au, an ionized jet, and one (or two) molecular outflows." (ABSTRACT); "In the azimuthal direction, the minimum of emission is located in the outflow direction, suggesting that the jet has eroded the circumstellar material, sweeping out the molecular gas." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
- The morphology and kinematics can be explained if GV Tau N is itself a binary whose primary has a highly inclined individual disc (i~80 deg) relative to a less inclined circumbinary gaseous disc (i~40 deg), with Jet 1 lying near the equatorial plane of the circumbinary disc.  
  > "All observations can be explained if GV Tau N is binary, and the primary component has a highly inclined individual disc relative to the circumbinary disc." (ABSTRACT); "The most energetic outflow, Jet 1, should be in (or close to) the equatorial plane of the circumbinary disc in order to explain the cavity cleaned of gas observed in the HCN maps." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High spatial resolution (~7 au) ALMA interferometric images of 1.1 mm continuum and HCN 3→2 are needed to resolve the circumstellar disc and outflow kinematics that were unresolved at ~50 au in previous PdBI observations, while Herschel/HIFI provides high spectral resolution far-IR transitions of H2O, 13CO, HCN, and CN to probe water and gas excitation.  
  > "Fuente et al. (2012) reported interferometric images of the HCN 3→2 and HCO [+] 3→2 lines at an angular resolution of ∼50 au. Only the HCN 3→2 emission was detected towards GV Tau N, and was interpreted as coming from the unresolved planet forming region." (1 INTRODUCTION); "Moreover, ALMA high spatial resolution (∼ 7 au) images of the continuum at 1.1 mm and the HCN 3→2 line resolve different gas components towards GV Tau N" (ABSTRACT)
- Why this method: Because Herschel/HIFI angular resolution cannot spatially resolve GV Tau N and GV Tau S or the disc from the envelope, high spectral resolution kinematic decomposition and comparison with spatially resolved ALMA HCN 3→2 line profiles are used to determine the origin of the FIR lines.  
  > "Unfortunately, the Herschel angular resolution does not allow to resolve the two components of the binary, GV Tau N and GV Tau S, and we need to use the kinematic information to discern the origin of the observed lines." (4 FAR INFRARED LINES)
- Alternative: Single precessing bipolar outflow vs. two different bipolar outflows to explain the misaligned red (PA=222 deg) and blue (PA=195 deg) high-velocity lobes.  
  > "This misalignment can be interpreted as the consequence of a precessing bipolar outflow. Alternatively, these lobes might belong to two different bipolar outflows." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
- Alternative: High-velocity gas along Jet 2 being a second bipolar outflow vs. being gas associated with the walls of the cavity excavated by Jet 1.  
  > "Given the angular resolution of the observations, it might also be associated to the walls of the cavity excavated by Jet 1." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
- Alternative: Time variability of radio emission or dust thermal contamination as alternative explanations for the steep 3.6 cm to 1.1 mm spectral index (alpha ~ +1.1) of the extended jet.  
  > "We should also consider the possibility that radio emission has varied on time scales of tens of years." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES); "It would be also possible that we have overestimated the flux coming from the jet because a fraction of the extended emission is coming from dust thermal emission." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
- Alternative: Self-absorption by a lower-density envelope at the systemic velocity vs. jet-disc interaction to explain the double-peaked HCN 3→2 profile.  
  > "Also, self-absorption by the lower density envelope at the systemic velocity would produce the two-peak feature." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneous 3.6 cm and 1.1 mm observations and potential dust continuum contribution make the jet spectral index uncertain.  
  > "Simultaneous measurements of the flux at several wavelengths would be desirable to further constrain the cm-mm spectral index." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
- Limitation (authors): ALMA high spatial resolution HCN 3→2 observations filter out ~60% of the extended flux compared to PdBI.  
  > "Therefore, the high spatial resolution ALMA observations recover ∼ 40% of the flux detected in PdBI observations." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
- Limitation (authors): Low signal-to-noise ratio of the PdBI 13CO 3→2 observations prevents producing a good quality spatial image.  
  > "The signal-to-noise ratio of our [13] CO 3→2 observations does not allow to obtain a good quality image." (3 (SUB-)MILLIMETER INTERFEROMETRIC IMAGES)
- Limitation (authors): Herschel/HIFI angular resolution (18.5"-40") cannot spatially resolve GV Tau N from GV Tau S or measure the emitting source size directly, introducing large uncertainty in column densities.  
  > "Unfortunately, the Herschel angular resolution does not allow to resolve the two components of the binary, GV Tau N and GV Tau S, and we need to use the kinematic information to discern the origin of the observed lines." (4 FAR INFRARED LINES); "Again, the unknown source size introduces a large uncertainty in our calculations." (6 MOLECULAR CHEMISTRY)

(5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS); "We recall that there is a degeneracy between the stellar mass and the inclination angle." (5 KINEMATICS OF THE MOLECULAR GAS)
