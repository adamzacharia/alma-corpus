## Science

- Fields: protoplanetary_disks, astrochemistry
- Question: Investigate the emission of H2CO (excitation temperature, column density, and line luminosities) in 20 Class II protoplanetary disks from the Lupus and Upper Sco regions and evaluate how H2CO emission connects to disk and stellar properties.  
  > "In this study, we investigate the emission of H$_2$CO and its link to disk properties, using a sample of 20 Class II disks in the Lupus and Upper Sco star-forming regions spanning over 1-6 Myr." (Abstract); "This paper presents a detailed study of the H$_2$CO emission in 20 Class II disks around 0.3-0.8 M$_\odot$ stars and tests how H$_2$CO emission changes with disk parameters such as gas mass, gas radius, dust mass, dust radius, stellar luminosity, and stellar mass." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Generate moment-zero maps using Keplerian masking**: Apply Keplerian masks to channel maps to create moment-zero maps; for Upper Sco, inclination and PA are from 1.3 mm continuum, v_sys from eddy, and mask adjustments evaluated by eye on channel maps (using 12CO J=2-1 for non-detections).  
  > "We first generate moment-zero maps for the H$_2$CO lines of 20 disks by applying Keplerian masks to channel maps." (Analysis); "The best-fitting masks are decided by eye evaluation of masks in individual channel maps. For sources without $3\sigma$ H$_2$CO line detections, we generate masks based on $^{12}$CO $J$=2-1 line emission." (Analysis)
   - software: eddy
   - parameter: PA, incl, M_star, Dist, v_sys, and CO Mask Radius per disk as listed in Table 3; velocity ranges in Table [tab:flux]  
  > "Table 3 shows the Keplerian mask parameters for all Lupus and Upper Sco sources (except for Upper Sco 6, which does not have H$_2$CO data at the time of this paper)." (Additional Tables)
   - assumption: Keplerian rotation  
  > "We first generate moment-zero maps for the H$_2$CO lines of 20 disks by applying Keplerian masks to channel maps." (Analysis)
   - fitting: By-eye evaluation of masks in individual channel maps; v_sys found using eddy  
  > "The best-fitting masks are decided by eye evaluation of masks in individual channel maps." (Analysis); "The v$_{\text{sys}}$ values for Upper Sco were found using the `eddy` Python package (cite: eddy)." (Additional Tables)
   - validation: Upper Sco line fluxes checked for consistency against those obtained with Keplerian mask parameters from AGE-PRO IV  
  > "Our line fluxes for the Upper Sco disks are consistent with those obtained using the Keplerian mask parameters from (cite: AGE-PRO_IV_UpperSco), but we use a slightly larger Keplerian mask to be more conservative for the fainter Upper Sco disks." (Analysis)
2. **Measure integrated line fluxes, upper limits, and R90 radii via curve-of-growth**: Project elliptical apertures onto moment-zero maps using continuum inclination and PA; integrate total flux within R100 (first maximum of integrated flux curve) and determine R90 (radius enclosing 90% of total cumulative flux).  
  > "After generating the moment-zero maps, we use the curve-of-growth method to estimate the line fluxes and uncertainties of H$_2$CO line emission (cite: AGEPRO_I_overview; Deng_Lupus)." (Analysis); "The 100% flux radius, denoted as R$_{100}$, is the radius corresponding to the first maxima in the integrated flux, and the total flux is measured within this radius." (Analysis); "The R$_{90}$ values were calculated by finding the radius at which the enclosed flux reached 90$\%$ of the total cumulative flux." (Additional Tables)
   - parameter: R100 determined independently for Band 6 and Band 7; 3-sigma detection threshold requiring both 3-sigma line flux and visible channel map emission  
  > "Because the Band 6 and Band 7 data have different angular resolutions ($\sim$0.35$''$ vs. $\sim$0.70$''$), R$_{100}$ is determined independently for each H$_2$CO transition, and fluxes are integrated within the corresponding aperture." (Analysis); "A significant detection requires both a 3$\sigma$ line flux measurement and visible emission in the channel maps." (H$_2$CO detections)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compute deprojected radial intensity profiles**: Deproject image coordinates to disk-plane coordinates and compute azimuthally averaged radial profiles and standard-deviation uncertainties within each radial bin using GoFish radial_profile().  
  > "We use the `GoFish` package (cite: GoFish) to compute the radial profiles for the H$_2$CO line emission using the `radial`$\_$`profile()` function." (Analysis); "The uncertainty is calculated by taking the standard deviation within each radial bin." (Analysis)
   - software: GoFish
   - parameter: bin width dr = 4 x pixel size (pixel size 0.025" for Band 6, 0.04" for Band 7); r_min = 0; r_max = 5 x R_max (CO Mask Radius in Table 3)  
  > "We use a bin width `dr` that is four times the pixel size ($0.025\ensuremath{''}$ for Band 6 and $0.04\ensuremath{''}$ for Band 7 ((cite: AGEPRO_I_overview)), and we set the minimum radius to zero and the maximum radius to five times the $R_{\text{max}}$ value given in Table 3 under the “CO Mask Radius" column." (Analysis)
   - assumption: Disk inclination and position angle from continuum emission (Table 3)  
  > "We use the position and inclination angles given in (cite: ageproX) (see Table 3) to convert image coordinates to disk-plane coordinates." (Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive excitation temperature, total column density, and optical depth via rotational diagram**: Iteratively solve rotational diagram equations with optical depth correction C_tau using Taylor expansion of tau_ul and MCMC linear fitting; for single-line detections, fix T_ex at 10 K and 40 K to compute column density range.  
  > "This process is repeated until the difference in optical depth between iterations is less than 0.001$\%$ or until 5000 iterations has passed." (Rotational diagram method); "For the four disks with only one H$_2$CO line detected, we constrain the column density with two fixed excitation temperatures at 10 and 40 K." (Rotational diagram method)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Solid angle Omega from R90 radius; convergence threshold < 0.001% or max 5000 iterations; 4 MCMC walkers, 5000 steps, burn-in 100 steps, thinning every 15th step; 5000 Monte Carlo draws for tau_ul error  
  > "The solid angle is found by converting the R$_{90}$ radius, at which 90$\%$ of the flux is enclosed, to radians." (Rotational diagram method); "This process is repeated until the difference in optical depth between iterations is less than 0.001$\%$ or until 5000 iterations has passed." (Rotational diagram method); "We then use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling with `emcee.EnsembleSampler` to explore the probability distribution of the parameters with 4 ensemble walkers and an MCMC chain with 5000 steps. From the resulting MCMC chains, flat samples for m and b are created by discarding the first 100 steps and thinned by taking every 15th step." (Rotational diagram method)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), nuclear spin-weights of 3:1 for ortho:para H2CO in CDMS partition function  
  > "We use the total H$_2$CO partition function that assumes nuclear spin-weights of 3 and 1 for the ortho-H$_2$CO and para-H$_2$CO, respectively." (Rotational diagram method); "We also assume local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions, which may not be the case." (Rotational diagram method)
   - fitting: Linear fit for slope m and intercept b sampled with emcee.EnsembleSampler (4 walkers, 5000 steps, 100 burn-in, thinned by 15)  
  > "At each iteration, we calculate the slope m and the y-intercept b from a linear fit. We then use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling with `emcee.EnsembleSampler` to explore the probability distribution of the parameters with 4 ensemble walkers and an MCMC chain with 5000 steps." (Rotational diagram method)
   - validation: Optical depth converged before 5000 iterations for all disks  
  > "The optical depth converges before 5000 iterations for all disks." (Rotational diagram method)
5. **Evaluate correlations between H2CO properties and disk/stellar parameters**: Use Bayesian linear regression (linmix) to test correlations involving H2CO line luminosities (accounting for upper limits) and Monte-Carlo Spearman-rho tests (scipy.stats.spearmanr) for T_ex, N_tot, and R90 using detections only.  
  > "For correlations between H$_2$CO line luminosity and disk properties and correlations between H$_2$CO line luminosity and CO line luminosity, we use the Python package `linmix` to account for non-detections (cite: Kelly2007)." (Correlation Analysis); "For the correlations between disk properties and excitation temperature and total column density, as well as those with the $R_{90}$ values, we only use disks with clear detections. A Monte-Carlo Spearman-$\rho$ test with 10,000 iterations is performed to calculate correlations using the `scipy.stats.spearmanr` Python module." (Correlation Analysis)
   - software: linmix, scipy.stats.spearmanr
   - parameter: linmix: K = 2 Gaussians, 5,000 to 100,000 MCMC steps, positive correlation if r > 0.3 (very strong if r > 0.7); Spearman-rho: 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations, alternative='greater', significant if p-value < 0.05  
  > "`Linmix` models the independent variable distribution as a weighted sum of K separate Gaussian distributions, and we set K=2. It produces posterior distributions for the slope, y-intercept, and intrinsic scatter by running between 5,000 and 100,000 MCMC steps." (Correlation Analysis); "Two parameters are considered to be positively correlated if the correlation coefficient r is greater than 0.3, and very strongly positively correlated if r is greater than 0.7." (Correlation Analysis); "Two parameters are considered to be positively correlated if the p-value is less than 0.05." (Correlation Analysis)
   - assumption: For single-line detections, the average of column densities at 10 K and 40 K is used in correlation tests; input parameters in Spearman test follow normal distributions around observed values.  
  > "For disks with only one H$_2$CO detection, the two column densities corresponding to excitation temperatures of 10 and 40 K are averaged when calculating the correlations." (Correlation Analysis); "For each iteration, the input parameters are randomly sampled from normal distributions centered around the observed values with a standard deviation of the observed error." (Correlation Analysis)
   - fitting: Hierarchical Bayesian linear regression in linmix; non-parametric Spearman rank-order correlation in scipy  
  > "`Linmix` uses a hierarchical Bayesian model to fit a straight line to data, accounting for upper limits of non-detections." (Correlation Analysis); "The Spearman rank-order correlation coefficients (denoted as $\rho$) and p-values are calculated for each iteration, and the final $\rho$ and p-values are the median values of the resulting distributions." (Correlation Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- H2CO emission is detected in 9 of 20 Class II disks in Lupus and Upper Sco, with Lupus 2, Upper Sco 1, and Upper Sco 8 showing central depressions in p-3-2 radial profiles.  
  > "Within this sample we achieve a detection rate of H$_2$CO of 45% (9/20), and set robust upper limits for the non-detections." (Abstract); "Notably, Lupus 2, Upper Sco 1, and Upper Sco 8 show a central depression." (H$_2$CO detections)
- H2CO line luminosities are strongly correlated with dust disk radius, gas disk radius, dust disk mass, gas disk mass, stellar mass, stellar luminosity, and CO isotopologue line fluxes, but not with gas-phase CO abundance [CO/H].  
  > "We find that the line luminosities of both H$_2$CO transitions are correlated with gas disk radius, dust disk radius, dust disk mass, gas mass, stellar luminosity, and stellar mass." (Correlation Analysis); "We do not find correlations between H$_2$CO line luminosities and the CO abundance (\[CO/H\])." (Correlation Analysis); "We find that H$_2$CO line luminosity is strongly correlated with the CO, $^{13}$CO, and C$^{18}$O line luminosities." (Correlation Analysis)
- All seven disks in the sample with resolved dust substructures have significant H2CO detections, supporting efficient H2CO formation via CO ice hydrogenation in dust traps.  
  > "Overall, all seven disks in the sample with definite substructures show significant H$_2$CO detections." (Substructures and H$_2$CO Detections); "The parallel between substructures and H$_2$CO detections supports the existence of dust traps in the disks with substructures that can accelerate H$_2$CO formation on grain surfaces." (Substructures and H$_2$CO Detections)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The AGE-PRO sample provides a homogeneous set of Class II disks with a narrow stellar mass range (0.3-0.8 M_sun) across two differently aged regions (Lupus and Upper Sco), extending down to smaller and lower-mass disks than previous surveys and enabling tests with gas mass for the first time.  
  > "In this paper, we use a more homogeneous sample with disks from the AGE-PRO sample (cite: AGEPRO_I_overview) that have a narrow range of stellar masses but a wide span of ages. The AGE-PRO sample also allows us to test dependencies with gas mass for the first time." (Introduction); "As shown in Figure 4, the AGE-PRO sources add a population of smaller and lower-mass disks that were underrepresented in previous samples (cite: Pegues20)." (Possible causes of the correlation between H$_2$CO luminosities and disk properties)
- Why this method: Keplerian masking and curve-of-growth maximize SNR for integrated fluxes; R90 is used instead of R100 for solid angle in rotational diagrams because R90 is more robust at low SNR; linmix is used to incorporate non-detection upper limits into correlation fits.  
  > "We use R$_{90}$ rather than R$_{100}$ because H$_2$CO is not uniformly distributed and does not have high signal-to-noise, so R$_{90}$ is usually more robust than R$_{100}$." (Rotational diagram method); "For correlations between H$_2$CO line luminosity and disk properties and correlations between H$_2$CO line luminosity and CO line luminosity, we use the Python package `linmix` to account for non-detections (cite: Kelly2007)." (Correlation Analysis)
- Alternative: Using R100 instead of R90 for the emitting solid angle in rotational diagrams was rejected because H2CO is non-uniform and has low SNR, making R90 more robust.  
  > "We use R$_{90}$ rather than R$_{100}$ because H$_2$CO is not uniformly distributed and does not have high signal-to-noise, so R$_{90}$ is usually more robust than R$_{100}$." (Rotational diagram method)
- Alternative: Explanation that H2CO luminosity correlates with disk size solely due to larger emitting area was argued against because H2CO emitting radii (R90) are only marginally correlated with gas and dust disk sizes.  
  > "A natural first guess might be that larger disks provide a more extended emitting area, thereby increasing the integrated line luminosity. However, we find that the H$_2$CO emitting radii are only marginally correlated with the gas and dust disk sizes, which argues against the idea that emitting area alone is the dominant driver." (Possible causes of the correlation between H$_2$CO luminosities and disk properties)

- Limitation (authors): Having only two H2CO transitions leads to higher and likely underestimated uncertainties on T_ex and N_tot, and it is unclear how representative two points are of the total level population.  
  > "Since we only have two H$_2$CO lines, we expect higher uncertainties on our excitation temperatures than other studies that have detections toward more H$_2$CO lines." (Rotational diagram method); "With only two points in our rotational diagram, it is unclear how representative these points are of the total population distribution." (Rotational diagram method)
- Limitation (authors): Absolute ALMA flux calibration uncertainty (5-10%) is not included in the flux error propagation, and LTE is assumed which may not hold.  
  > "Additionally, in this work we only include the noise level in the channel maps when determining the flux uncertainty. The absolute flux calibration uncertainty of our ALMA observations is around 5-10%, which adds additional uncertainty to the flux and consequently to $T_{\text{ex}}$ and $N_u^{\text{thin}}$. We also assume local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions, which may not be the case." (Rotational diagram method)
- Limitation (authors): Band 7 p-4-3 observations have a large beam (~0.7-0.8"), leaving radial profiles poorly resolved, and 7/10 Lupus disks lacked spatial resolution to detect dust substructures.  
  > "For the p-4-3 line, the beam size is large ($\sim$ 0.8$''$) and therefore the radial profiles are not well resolved." (H$_2$CO detections); "We note that 7/10 Lupus disks did not have enough resolution to detect substructures, so there could be additional substructures that are undetected in the Lupus region (cite: ageproX)." (Substructures and H$_2$CO Detections)
- Limitation (authors): Small sample size of disks with two-line detections (5 in AGE-PRO, 4 in literature) limits the ability to detect correlations with T_ex and N_tot or determine evolution with disk age.  
  > "This small sample size makes it challenging to find correlations with disk parameters for the total column density and excitation temperature." (Correlation Analysis); "With only five disks with significant H$_2$CO detections in both lines, we cannot come to a clear conclusion on how H$_2$CO evolves with disk age." (Potential evolution of H$_2$CO?)
- Limitation (authors): Missing H2CO data for Upper Sco 6 (both lines) and Upper Sco 9 and 10 (p-4-3 line).  
  > "Only H$_2$CO line p-3-2 is detected in Upper Sco 9 and Upper Sco 10, but no H$_2$CO p-4-3 observations were taken for these two disks." (H$_2$CO detections); "Table 3 shows the Keplerian mask parameters for all Lupus and Upper Sco sources (except for Upper Sco 6, which does not have H$_2$CO data at the time of this paper)." (Additional Tables)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fitting a 2-parameter linear model (slope and intercept) to exactly 2 data points (p-3-2 and p-4-3) has zero degrees of freedom; without including the 5-10% absolute flux calibration uncertainty between Band 6 and Band 7, T_ex uncertainties (especially +/-0.9 K for Upper Sco 1) are severely underestimated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Band 6 (~0.35") and Band 7 (~0.70") images were not matched in uv-coverage or convolved to a common beam before extracting fluxes and R90 values for the rotational diagram.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using a 3rd-order Taylor expansion around tau=0 for optical depth Correction when derived tau values reach 1.78 to 3.66 (Lupus 2 and Upper Sco 1) introduces approximation errors in the optically thick regime.
