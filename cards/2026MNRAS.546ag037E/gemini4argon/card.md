## Science

- Fields: stellar_physics, agn_and_quasars
- Question: Whether the newly-discovered millimetre fundamental plane of black hole accretion holds for accreting stellar-mass black holes (X-ray binaries), and whether ADAF or compact jet models can explain the correlation for these sources.  
  > "Our main aim is to establish whether the newly-discovered mmFP holds also for accreting stellar-mass black holes, and if it does whether ADAF models can also explain the correlation of this type of sources, as they seem to do for AGN of all accretion rates." (Introduction); "Here we use mm and X-ray data of $5$ X-ray binaries (XRBs) to demonstrate that these stellar-mass black holes also lie on the mm fundamental plane, as they do at radio wavelengths." (Abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image new ACA and archival ALMA continuum observations**: Reduce data using the CASA pipeline following Davis et al. (2022) and Ruffa et al. (2024), and image the four continuum spectral windows together using CASA TCLEAN in multi-frequency synthesis mode.  
  > "The data were reduced using the COMMON ASTRONOMY SOFTWARE APPLICATIONS (CASA) pipeline (cite: McMullin07), adopting a version appropriate for each dataset and a standard calibration strategy." (New ACA observations); "The continuum images were produced by combining the four continuum spectral windows using the CASA task TCLEAN in multi-frequency synthesis mode." (New ACA observations); "The mm luminosity of Cygnus X-1, GRS 1915+105 and GX 339-4 was thus also estimated from archival ALMA continuum observations at $230$ GHz for Cygnus X-1 (Proposal ID: 2016.1.00496.S) and GX 339-4 (Proposal ID: 2019.1.01324.T) and at $104$ GHz for GRS 1915+105 (Proposal ID: 2017.1.00051.S), following the same procedure described in Section 2 of (cite: Ruffa24)." (Archival Observations)
   - software: CASA, TCLEAN
   - parameter: 230 GHz (ACA, Cygnus X-1 archival, GX 339-4 archival); 104 GHz (GRS 1915+105 archival)  
  > "The mm luminosity of Cygnus X-1, GRS 1915+105 and GX 339-4 was thus also estimated from archival ALMA continuum observations at $230$ GHz for Cygnus X-1 (Proposal ID: 2016.1.00496.S) and GX 339-4 (Proposal ID: 2019.1.01324.T) and at $104$ GHz for GRS 1915+105 (Proposal ID: 2017.1.00051.S)" (Archival Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure mm continuum fluxes, scale non-230 GHz fluxes, and derive 230 GHz luminosities**: Measure continuum fluxes and upper limits as in Ruffa et al. (2024), scale archival observations taken at 98, 104, and 350 GHz to 230 GHz assuming a flat spectral index, and compute luminosities using literature distances.  
  > "The measured mm fluxes and associated luminosities (estimated as described in (cite: Ruffa24)) of all our targets, including upper limits for non-detections, are listed in Table" (New ACA observations); "Archival mm fluxes at frequencies other than $230$ GHz were scaled to that band assuming a flat spectral index ($\alpha=0$)." (Archival Observations)
   - assumption: Flat mm spectral index alpha = 0 for scaling 98, 104, and 350 GHz fluxes to 230 GHz  
  > "Archival mm fluxes at frequencies other than $230$ GHz were scaled to that band assuming a flat spectral index ($\alpha=0$)." (Archival Observations)
   - assumption: Target distances from Gultekin et al. (2019) and Tremou et al. (2020)  
  > "The distance and black hole mass of Cygnus X-1, GRS 1915+105, XTE J1118+480 and A0620-00 were taken from (cite: Gultekin2019), those of GX 339-4 from (cite: Tremou20)." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to spectral index in the range -0.15 to 0.15, finding flux variations of at most ~6-14%.  
  > "For GRS 1915+105 where the archival mm observation was taken at 104GHz if we assume a spectral index range of -0.15 to 0.15 which has been observed in XRBs (cite: Fender2000) the scaled flux would range from decreasing by 11.1% to increasing by 12.6%." (Archival Observations); "Similarly, for XTE J1118+480 where the archival mm observation was taken at 350GHz assuming the same spectral index range the scaled flux would range from increasing by 6.5% and decreasing 6.1%." (Archival Observations); "Finally, for A0620-00 where the archival mm observation was taken at 98GHz assuming the same spectral index range the scaled flux could range from increasing by 13.7% to decreasing by 12.0%." (Archival Observations)
3. **Compile and band-scale intrinsic 2-10 keV X-ray luminosities and assess variability**: Retrieve X-ray luminosities from Swift (0.3-10 keV), Chandra (3-9 keV), and literature, scale them to the 2-10 keV band using assumed spectral/photon indices, and calculate fractional variability F_var for A0620-00.  
  > "We note that the Swift data were taken in the $0.3$ – $10$ keV X-ray band, so we scaled the derived luminosities to the $2$ – $10$ keV energy range assuming a spectral index of $-0.8$ (the mean spectral index reported by (cite: ReevesTurner00))." (Archival Observations); "This observation was taken at $3--9$ keV range so as with the Swift data we scale it to the $2--10$ keV range using the $\Gamma$ reported in (cite: Dincer2017) for A0620-00 of 2.07." (Archival Observations); "To quantify this we use the data from Table 1 of (cite: Dincer2017) and compute the fractional variability, $F_{\rm var}$." (Archival Observations)
   - assumption: X-ray spectral index of -0.8 for scaling Swift 0.3-10 keV data to 2-10 keV  
  > "We note that the Swift data were taken in the $0.3$ – $10$ keV X-ray band, so we scaled the derived luminosities to the $2$ – $10$ keV energy range assuming a spectral index of $-0.8$ (the mean spectral index reported by (cite: ReevesTurner00))." (Archival Observations)
   - assumption: Photon index Gamma = 2.07 for scaling Chandra 3-9 keV data of A0620-00 to 2-10 keV  
  > "This observation was taken at $3--9$ keV range so as with the Swift data we scale it to the $2--10$ keV range using the $\Gamma$ reported in (cite: Dincer2017) for A0620-00 of 2.07." (Archival Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit the M_BH - L_nu,mm relation and the mm Fundamental Plane (mmFP)**: Fit the combined SMBH + XRB sample (including new ACA data for Cygnus X-1 and archival 230 GHz data for GX 339-4) using LTS_LINEFIT and LTS_PLANEFIT, and evaluate Spearman rank correlation coefficients.  
  > "Following (cite: Ruffa24), we fitted the $M_{\rm BH}$ – $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ and mmFP data, including the XRBs, using the LTS_LINEFIT and LTS_PLANEFIT routines of (cite: Cappellari2013), respectively." (Results); "When performing these fits we only included the new ACA data for Cygnus X-1 and the archival data for GX 339-4 (due to its substantial flux variability in the ACA observations)." (Results); "We also performed Spearman rank analyses to quantify the statistical significance of the correlations, and show the resulting correlation coefficients $\rho$ and $p$-values in the top-left corner of each panel of Figure 1 which are similar or better then the correlation coefficients in (cite: Ruffa24)." (Results)
   - software: LTS_LINEFIT, LTS_PLANEFIT
   - fitting: Linear regression in log space using LTS_LINEFIT and LTS_PLANEFIT routines of Cappellari et al. (2013)  
  > "Following (cite: Ruffa24), we fitted the $M_{\rm BH}$ – $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ and mmFP data, including the XRBs, using the LTS_LINEFIT and LTS_PLANEFIT routines of (cite: Cappellari2013), respectively." (Results)
   - validation: Spearman rank correlation analysis (rho and p-values) and comparison of best-fitting coefficients and scatters with SMBH-only fits of Ruffa et al. (2024).  
  > "In both cases, the best-fitting coefficients agree well with those derived when fitting only the SMBHs (cite: Ruffa24), and both relations have similar or improved scatters compared to the SMBH-only fits." ## Qualitative findings

- Stellar-mass black holes (XRBs) follow the same M_BH - L_nu,mm relation and millimetre Fundamental Plane as SMBHs, extending the correlations by ~5 orders of magnitude.  
  > "As clearly illustrated by Figure 1, both SMBHs and XRBs follow the same $M_{\rm BH}$ – $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ relation and lie on the same mmFP." (Results); "As illustrated in Figure 1 and discussed above the four XRBs with ACA data (apart from GX 339-4) and the five XRBs with archival data (apart from A0620-00) analysed in this work are in good agreement with the $M_{\rm BH}$ – $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ correlation and the mmFP, extending both by $\approx5$ orders of magnitude." (Physical Drivers)
- GX 339-4 lies well within the mmFP scatter in 2017 archival data, but deviates significantly in 2024 ACA data after a large increase in mm luminosity.  
  > "Firstly, GX 339-4 is significantly outside of the observed scatter of both correlations when its $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ is inferred using our newly-acquired ACA data, whereas it is well within the scatter when $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ is estimated using archival data" (Discussion and conclusions)
- A0620-00 lies outside the scatter in archival 98 GHz observations due to its very low quiescent mm luminosity, while its ACA upper limit is consistent with the relations.  
  > "Secondly, the archival data of A0620-00 lies significantly out side of the scatter of both relations due to its very low mm luminosity." (Discussion and conclusions); "Its corresponding ACA upper limit, however, lies in line with both relations." (Discussion and conclusions)
- Both ADAF and compact jet models encompass the XRB observations and reproduce the general existence of the correlation across BH masses, but overpredict XRB black hole masses by ~2 orders of magnitude (except BHJET at an unrealistic 2.5 deg inclination).  
  > "all the sources lie within the area covered by the ADAF model solutions (illustrated by the black grid), although these overpredict the BH masses of the persistent transients (listed in Table" (ADAF model); "Furthermore, we note that the model solutions at $i=90^{\circ}$ (green grid in Figure 4) overpredict $M_{\rm BH}$ of the persistent transients by about the same order of magnitude as the ADAF solutions (see Section 4.1)." (Compact jet model)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ACA at 230 GHz was chosen to match the 230 GHz observations of the primary WISDOM sample defining the mmFP, and because XRBs do not have extended emission so ACA angular resolution is sufficient; contemporaneous Swift X-ray data were chosen to minimise temporal variability.  
  > "We observed the four targets at $230$ GHz ($\approx1$ mm) to match the previous continuum observations of the primary sample that defined the mmFP (cite: Ruffa24)." (New ACA observations); "The ACA was chosen as XRBs do not have extended emission, so the angular resolution (4$''$.448–6$''$.646) of the observations is irrelevant." (New ACA observations); "When possible we used the Swift catalogue, as for the majority of our sample sources these were the observations most contemporaneous to the ACA ones, thus minimising the impact of temporal flux variability." (Archival Observations)
- Why this method: Adopted the same reduction, fitting (LTS_LINEFIT/LTS_PLANEFIT), and SED modelling (Pesce et al. 2021 ADAF and Lucchini et al. 2022 BHJET) procedures as Ruffa et al. (2024) for direct comparison with the SMBH mmFP; restricted fits to 230 GHz detections to avoid spectral-index scaling bias.  
  > "Following (cite: Ruffa24), we fitted the $M_{\rm BH}$ – $L_{\rm \nu,mm}$ and mmFP data, including the XRBs, using the LTS_LINEFIT and LTS_PLANEFIT routines of (cite: Cappellari2013), respectively." (Results); "We do this so we are not fitting non-detections in the ACA observations or sources without 230GHz archival observations where our choice of spectral index used to scale the available flux may bias our results." (Results); "To test this hypothesis, we adopt the same approach as (cite: Ruffa24) and compare the observed mm and X-ray luminosities of the three ACA-observed persistent transients and the archival observations of GX 339-4 to those extracted from mock spectral energy distributions (SEDs)." (Physical Drivers)
- Alternative: Dusty torus models: rejected for XRBs as there is no evidence for a dusty torus in XRBs and torus models underpredict high-luminosity AGN mm emission by at least 2 orders of magnitude.  
  > "As there is currently no evidence for the presence of a dusty torus in XRBs, we do not consider here the associated emission mechanisms (see (cite: Ruffa24) for the application of such models to SMBHs)." (Physical Drivers)
- Alternative: Fitting archival non-230 GHz observations (such as XTE J1118+480) or ACA non-detections in the linear/plane regressions: excluded to avoid biasing the fits with spectral index assumptions or upper limits.  
  > "We do this so we are not fitting non-detections in the ACA observations or sources without 230GHz archival observations where our choice of spectral index used to scale the available flux may bias our results." (Results); "We do not include the archival data for XTE J1118+480 as it did not have archival 230GHz observations and we wanted to ensure our conclusions were not biased by our choice of spectral index used to scale the available flux." (Results)
- Alternative: Using the 2010 Swift observation of A0620-00 or the marginal 230 GHz ALMA detection of A0620-00: rejected in favour of the 2013 Chandra observation and 98 GHz ALMA detection.  
  > "In the case of A0620-00 we took the mm flux from (cite: Gallo2019) which was observed at $98$ GHz (Proposal ID: 2016.1.00773.S). This paper also reported a measurement at $230$ GHz but it was a marginal detection." (Archival Observations); "There was no recent Swift data for A0620-00 which the only observation being taken in 2010 which has a low count. We instead use the Chandra observation from (cite: Dincer2017) and use it for both our archival and ACA data points." (Archival Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Scaling archival mm fluxes from 98, 104, and 350 GHz to 230 GHz assuming a flat spectral index (alpha = 0) introduces uncertainties of ~6% to 14%.  
  > "Archival mm fluxes at frequencies other than $230$ GHz were scaled to that band assuming a flat spectral index ($\alpha=0$). This unavoidably introduces uncertainties in our comparisons." (Archival Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneous mm and X-ray observations (especially for A0620-00 where Chandra data is from 2013 and F_var = 0.83, and GRS 1915+105 with a 9-month gap) mean temporal variability can significantly impact source positions and increase scatter.  
  > "This data was taken in 2013 meaning that A0620-00 is a source where variability can have an significant impact." (Archival Observations); "We also note that the lack of simultaneous X-ray observations for some sources may further increase the observed scatter." (Discussion and conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Potential contribution from the donor star wind to the mm emission of Cygnus X-1 is not modeled.  
  > "For Cygnus X-1 in the soft state and potentially in the hard state there might be some contribution to the mm emission from the donor star wind. This contribution should be investigated further." (Physical Drivers)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainties in the free parameters describing plasma physics in both ADAF and compact jet models (kept fixed to default values calibrated only for SMBHs) prevent strong conclusions about the physical mechanism.  
  > "We speculate that this is caused by the large uncertainties of the parameters describing the plasma physics in both models, most of which have so far been calibrated only for SMBHs. This clearly prevents us from drawing strong conclusions on this issue at this stage." (Discussion and conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The linear and plane fits only actually add two XRB data points (Cygnus X-1 ACA and GX 339-4 archival) to the 48 SMBHs from Ruffa et al. (2024), so the leverage of stellar-mass black holes is determined by just 2 points.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cygnus X-1 during the new ACA observation was classified as being in the soft state, yet it was included in the mmFP fit, whereas the abstract suggests the plane only applies to XRBs in the hard state.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Conversely, GX 339-4 was reported to be in the hard state during both the 2017 archival and 2024 ACA observations, which contradicts the abstract's claim that its deviation from the plane in 2024 occurred after a state change (a contradiction explicitly noted in Section 5).
