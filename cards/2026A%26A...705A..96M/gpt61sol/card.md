## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare clean continuum visibilities**: Select pipeline continuum channels; inspect line-rich sources; test continuum residuals using ft and uvsub; average in frequency.  
  > "To create the continuum visibilities we use the continuum channel ranges provided for the pipeline over all the available spectral windows." (Observations); "We then subtracted the model from the data using the CASA task uvsub." (Observations); "We then averaged in frequency resulting in channel widths of $\sim$10 MHz and $\sim$30 MHz for the band 3 and band 6 data, respectively." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, ft, uvsub
   - parameter: Combined datasets: final channel widths 30 or 60 MHz; bandwidth-smearing intensity loss limited to ~1%  
  > "We averaged in frequency up to the maximum that would not result in a reduction of the intensity larger than $\sim$1% for sources away from the phase center" (Self-calibration and imaging); "The final channel widths was 30 MHz or 60 MHz, depending on where additional sources appeared in the field of view." (Self-calibration and imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual images checked for noise only; L1551 dirty cubes checked for remaining lines  
  > "Finally, we imaged these residuals checking that only noise was left." (Observations); "For the line rich source L1551 IRS 5 (cite: 2020BianchiL1551), we also created dirty cubes of the visibilities after line flagging and found no obvious remaining lines." (Observations)
2. **Self-calibrate and image both bands**: Self-calibrate extended observations, combine with self-calibrated FAUST compact data, then repeat phase and amplitude self-calibration.  
  > "We performed self-calibration separately for the observations in the extended configuration, which were then combined with the self-calibrated compact configuration observations and self-calibrated again together." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean, gaincal
   - parameter: Extended phase: seven iterations, inf with combine=scan down to int; typically shortest successful solint=30s  
  > "We started with phase-only self-calibration, performing seven iterations with decreasing solint intervals from ’inf’ (with combine$=$scan) down to ’int’." (Self-calibration and imaging); "In most cases this interval was 30s," (Self-calibration and imaging)
   - parameter: Extended amplitude trials: inf and 600s; accepted solutions used inf  
  > "We performed two steps with solint values of $inf$ and 600s." (Self-calibration and imaging); "For all of them, the solution with solint$=$inf was used" (Self-calibration and imaging)
   - parameter: Combined phase: six steps, initial inf model from extended data, then 60s to 9s  
  > "We performed six successive steps of phase-only self-calibration." (Self-calibration and imaging); "For this first round, we used the final image after self-calibration of the extended configuration as the model with ’inf’ as the solint interval." (Self-calibration and imaging); "The resultant model was then used for the next steps reducing the solint intervals from 60s to 9s, always keeping the last solution that resulted in improved S/N and image fidelity." (Self-calibration and imaging)
   - parameter: Combined amplitude: solint=inf; combined-SPW solnorm=True then False, then per-SPW solnorm=False  
  > "In all of them we use solint$=inf$." (Self-calibration and imaging); "In the first and second one we combine all spectral windows and use solnorm$=$True and False, respectively." (Self-calibration and imaging); "For the third and final we keep solnorm$=$False and attempt a solution per spectral window." (Self-calibration and imaging)
   - fitting: Phase-only followed by amplitude+phase gain solutions  
  > "The self-calibration for the FAUST observations consisted of rounds of solint intervals with ’int’ for phase-only solutions, followed by amplitude+phase solutions computed per-scan." (Self-calibration and imaging)
   - validation: Keep solutions improving S/N and image fidelity; reject amplitude solutions creating artifacts or changing flux scale  
  > "We kept the amp+phase selfcal solution only if the S/N remained the same or improved, there were no artifacts or changes to the flux scale (with no other apparent change in the image)." (Self-calibration and imaging)
3. **Measure disk geometry**: 2D Gaussian fits for centers, position angles and inclinations; generally use 3 mm, but 1.3 mm for IRS7A and Elias 29.  
  > "The values are obtained from a 2D Gaussian fit to the 3 mm images using the CASA task imfit." (Continuum maps); "The reported values come from the fit to the 3 mm emission for all sources except RCrA IRS 7 A and Elias 29." (Disk center, position angle and inclinations); "For the latter the values correspond to the 1.3 mm fit as the 3 mm shows evidence of significant free-free contamination." (Disk center, position angle and inclinations)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - assumption: Circular disk geometry  
  > "Inclination is derived assuming a circular geometry for the disk." (Disk center, position angle and inclinations)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian  
  > "The values are obtained from a 2D Gaussian fit to the 3 mm images using the CASA task imfit." (Continuum maps)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Construct aligned spectral-index maps**: Match uv ranges and synthesized beams; align images by cross-correlation; calculate α=ln(Iν1/Iν2)/ln(ν1/ν2).  
  > "Thus we performed additional imaging limiting the baselines so as to cover the same uv-wavelength range in both wavelengths." (Imaging for spectral index); "\alpha = \frac{\ln(I_{\nu_1}/I_{\nu_2})}{\ln(\nu_1/\nu_2)}," (Spectral Index maps); "To correct for this, we calculated the shift between the images by looking at their cross-correlation" (Spectral Index maps)
   - software: tclean, scipy, fftconvolve
   - parameter: ν1=225 GHz, ν2=100 GHz; ν1=223 GHz for IRAS 16293 A/B  
  > "GHz and 100 GHz, respectively." (Spectral Index maps); "correspond to 225" (Spectral Index maps); "223 GHz in the case of IRAS 16293 A/B" (Spectral Index maps)
   - parameter: Map-pixel distribution summarized using Gaussian KDE  
  > "computed using a Gaussian kernel density estimator (KDE) aggregating all pixels in the spectral index maps for all disk and CBD structures." (Spectral Index maps)
   - fitting: Cross-correlation shift maximizing image similarity  
  > "This finds the shift that maximizes the similarity between the images" (Spectral Index maps)
   - validation: Inspect before/after gradients; results not significantly affected by alignment  
  > "The most visually obvious gradients (IRS7B, IRS7A and CXO 24) are removed after the alignment." (Alignment between 1.3 mm and 3 mm); "We note that none of the results in this work are significantly affected by this small correction." (Alignment between 1.3 mm and 3 mm)
5. **Extract radial brightness profiles**: Deproject aligned matched-beam images; azimuthally average; convert intensity to brightness temperature with full Planck function.  
  > "To build the intensity profile we first deprojected the disks using the center coordinates and inclinations from the 2D Gaussian fit to the 3 mm emission" (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles); "We then calculated the azimuthally averaged profiles from the deprojected images." (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles); "the intensity is given as brightness temperature $T_b$ calculated using the full Planck function." (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles)
   - parameter: Extended subset: geometric mean deconvolved FWHM at least 3 beam sizes in at least one wavelength  
  > "This subsample corresponds to those for which the geometric mean of the deconvolved FWHM axes from the 2D gaussian fit is at least 3 times the size of the synthesized beam in at least one of the wavelengths." (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles)
   - parameter: Inclined disks: <10–20 degrees around major axis; L1551 IRS5 N excludes northern contamination  
  > "For the more inclined disks (RCrA SMM1C, VLA 1623 W, VLA 1623 B) we use only a limited range of azimuthal angles ($<$ 10-20 degrees) around the disk major axis." (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles); "Similarly, for the circumstellar disk L1551 IRS5 N, we also excluded some azimuthal angles to the north, to avoid contamination from emission connected to the circumbinary disk" (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Bin uncertainty σstd/√N, with N the number of beams  
  > "The uncertainty in each bin is calculated as $\sigma_{std}/\sqrt{N}$, where $\sigma_{std}$ and $N$ are the standard deviation, and number of beams in each bin, respectively." (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles)
6. **Fit disk intensity models**: Extended disks: beam-convolved modified self-similar profiles I(r)∝(r/rc)^γ1 exp[−(r/rc)^γ2]. Compact disks: Gaussian model profiles from imfit.  
  > "I(r) \propto  \left(\frac{r}{r_c} \right)^{\gamma_1}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{r}{r_c}\right)^{\gamma_2}\right]" (Fits to the intensity profiles); "Each model was convolved with the synthesized beam, and the values for each of the parameters were obtained using the Python module `emcee` (cite: 2013Foreman-Mackeyemcee)." (Fits to the intensity profiles); "For the remaining (more compact disks), we use the results of the 2D Gaussian fit to compute model radial profiles." (Fits to the intensity profiles)
   - software: emcee, imfit
   - parameter: Free parameters: rc, γ1, γ2, Ftot  
  > "Together with $r_c$, $\gamma_1$, and $\gamma_2$, the fourth free parameter is taken as the total flux of the model, $F_{\rm tot}$." (Fits to the intensity profiles)
   - parameter: Compact Gaussian: γ1=0, γ2=2; rc=major-axis FWHM/(2√ln2)  
  > "with $\gamma_1=0$ and $\gamma_2=2$, resulting in a Gaussian profile." (Fits to the intensity profiles); "We compute $r_c$ using the value for the deconvolved FWHM (major axis) as FWHM/$(2\sqrt{\ln2})$." (Fits to the intensity profiles)
   - assumption: Face-on compact-disk flux is Gaussian total flux divided by cos(i), appropriate for optically thick emission  
  > "The final parameter $F_{\rm tot}$, is computed as the total flux from the 2D Gaussian fit divided by $\cos(i)$, which corresponds to the face-on flux," (Fits to the intensity profiles); "the $\cos(i)$ correction applies for optically thick emission (cite: 2021TazzariMulti)" (Fits to the intensity profiles)
   - fitting: emcee; setup deferred to (cite: 2024MaureiraSM1); posterior medians used for subsequent models  
  > "We refer the reader to (cite: 2024MaureiraSM1) for further details of the emcee setup." (Fits to the intensity profiles); "For the fit obtained from emcee, we use the median of the posterior solutions as the model." (Optical depths at 1.3 and 3 mm)
   - validation: Model fits and residuals displayed for both bands  
  > "show the fit to the intensity profiles and residuals for the observations at 1.3 mm and 3 mm, respectively." (Fit to intensity profiles of extended disks)
7. **Compare sizes, spectral indices and luminosities**: Derive R68 and R95 from fitted profiles; compare wavelengths, mean α, inclination and face-on disk flux.  
  > "the radius containing 68% and 95% of the total flux, respectively." (Disk sizes at 1.3 and 3 mm); "shows the mean spectral index value for the individual disks in our sample as a function of R$_{68}$ at 3 mm." (Spectral index versus disk size); "The relation is shown as a function of the flux of the disk as seen face-on, and all fluxes are rescaled at a common distance of 150 pc." (Disk size-luminosity relations)
   - parameter: Exclude unresolved spectral-index disks IRS7A, Elias 29 and IRAS 16293 A1/A2 from inclination-trend comparison  
  > "we exclude disks for which the spectral index map for the disk is not well resolved" (Spectral index versus disk size); "(RCrA IRS7A, Elias 29 and IRAS 16293 A1/A2)." (Spectral index versus disk size)
   - assumption: Optically thick face-on inclination correction  
  > "The inclination correction for the face-on flux for optically thick emission" (Disk size-luminosity relations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare both R68 and R95 and literature Class II relations  
  > "shows the comparison of the disk sizes at 1.3 and 3 mm using R$_{68}$, as well as R$_{95}$." (Disk sizes at 1.3 and 3 mm); "compares the observed Class 0/I disks with the relations derived for Class II disks in (cite: 2021TazzariMulti)." (Disk size-luminosity relations)
8. **Compare brightness temperatures with irradiation**: Compare fitted 1.3 mm brightness temperatures with passive midplane temperatures Tmid=(φL*/(8πr²σSB))^0.25.  
  > "T^{\mathrm{irr}}_{mid}=\left(\frac{\varphi L_{*}}{8\pi r^2\sigma_{SB}}\right)^{0.25}" (Comparison of brightness and dust temperature); "The above expression is an approximation valid for passively heated disks (cite: 1997ChiangSpectral)." (Comparison of brightness and dust temperature)
   - parameter: L*=0.1–29 L☉; φ=0.01–0.3  
  > "we consider the limiting values 0.1 and 29 L$_{\odot}$ for the protostar luminosity $L_{*}$, and 0.01 and 0.3 for the flaring angle" (Comparison of brightness and dust temperature)
   - assumption: Passive irradiation temperature approximation  
  > "The above expression is an approximation valid for passively heated disks (cite: 1997ChiangSpectral)." (Comparison of brightness and dust temperature)

## Qualitative findings

- Most individual disks are optically thick at both wavelengths.  
  > "We find that the disks in our sample are mostly optically thick at 1.3 and 3 mm." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Some disks require temperatures above irradiation-only predictions.  
  > "The results suggest that the temperatures in these disks are higher than what is expected from irradiation alone, and additional heating such as viscous heating might need to be considered." (Optically thick emission and comparison with Class II disks)
- IRS 63 is the only clear annular substructure; it is also detected at 3 mm here.  
  > "The only clear substructure in our sample is the ring in the Class I disk IRS 63" (Dust substructure and molecular line detection); "and for the first time here also at 3 mm," (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles)
- High optical depths prevent direct grain-size inference across individual disks.  
  > "The high optical depth in the observed disks prevents us from directly using the resolved spectral index to infer the dust emissivity index $\beta$, a proxy for the maximum grain sizes, throughout the disk." (Disk sizes at 1.3 and 3 mm and grain growth)
- CBD grain-growth differences between Class 0 and Class I are tentative.  
  > "The results for the CBDs suggests a possible trend of higher $\beta$ values for the Class 0 sources." (Dust emissivity and grain sizes in the Class 0/I CBDs); "the Class I circumbinary disk L1551 IRS 5 could host larger mm-sized grains, based on the estimated lower $\beta$ values." (Dust emissivity and grain sizes in the Class 0/I CBDs)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution two-band continuum permits spatially resolved spectral-index measurements of optically thick emission; configurations chosen for FAUST combination.  
  > "The high resolution enabled spatially resolved spectral index maps to probe the extent of the optically thick emission." (Introduction); "The configuration and frequency setup was chosen to be combined with the observations from FAUST (PI: S. Yamamoto, ID: 2018.1.01205.L)." (Observations)
- Why this method: Match spatial scales for spectral indices; use generalized profiles because Gaussians leave extended-disk residuals.  
  > "To obtain spectral index maps it is important to create images at the different bands that recover as closely as possible the same spatial scales." (Imaging for spectral index); "a 2D Gaussian fit can result in obvious residuals that indicate that this type of intensity profile is not a good model." (Fits to the intensity profiles)
- Alternative: Free-free emission cannot explain low α over large disk radii.  
  > "This implies that free-free contamination, expected only near the central region, cannot fully account for the low observed values." (Spectral Index maps)
- Alternative: Scattering and temperature gradients can both produce α below 2.  
  > "can be due to either positive gradients of temperature for increasing depth into the disk" (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles); "or due to self-scattered emission due to the presence of large grains" (Radial intensity and spectral index profiles)
- Alternative: Variable flaring and Lbol reduce but do not remove all irradiation-model discrepancies.  
  > "In both cases the discrepancy decreases but still persist for at least several sources." (Other predictions for the size-luminosity relation)
- Alternative: A disk edge may reflect a surface-density drop or an opacity transition associated with grain sizes.  
  > "the observed sharp outer disk edge in the dust emission profile is not tracing a drop of the dust surface density, but instead a sudden change in the dust opacity," (Disk sizes at 1.3 and 3 mm and grain growth)
- Alternative: Colder CBD temperature prescription falls below observed brightness temperatures; envelope prescriptions yield lower optical depths.  
  > "using other parametrization more typical of envelopes (e.g., (cite: 2019GalametzLowdust)) results in higher temperatures and thus even lower optical depths." (Optical depth and masses in the CBDs); "results in temperatures in some regions that go below the observed brightness temperatures." (Optical depth and masses in the CBDs)
- How they decided it was enough: Retain shortest self-calibration interval improving S/N and reducing artifacts; accept amplitude solutions only without adverse flux or image changes.  
  > "Then, for each source and band, we kept the solution with the shortest solint interval that would still result in improved S/N and visible reduction of artifacts." (Self-calibration and imaging); "We kept the amp+phase selfcal solution only if the S/N remained the same or improved, there were no artifacts or changes to the flux scale (with no other apparent change in the image)."
