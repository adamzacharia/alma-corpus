## Analysis recipe

1. **Select galaxy sample and assess stellar mass completeness**: Select H-band star-forming galaxies using UVJ criteria across four CANDELS fields, correct HST-ALMA astrometric offsets, and perform Monte Carlo simulations of the M* - L_1.6um relation to determine 90% completeness limits in 9 redshift bins.  
  > "The SFGs are UVJ selected, following the definition from Muzzin et al. (2013)." (2.1. Sample); "We corrected them using the offsets provided by Franco et al. (2020)." (2.2. Observations); "We derive the completeness from Monte Carlo simulations by generating, from a uniform redshift distribution in the redshift bin and a given M⋆, the corresponding L1.6 µm−obs taking into account the dispersion, and comparing it to the corresponding H-band detection limit for each catalogue." (3. Stacking)
   - software: EAZY
   - parameter: 9 redshift bins from z = 0.1 to 5.0; 6 stellar mass bins from log10(M*/Msun) = 8.5 to 12.0  
  > "The two axes represent the boundaries of the bins in redshift and M⋆." (Table 2)
   - parameter: Completeness threshold = 90%  
  > "We assume that catalogues are complete when the completeness is above 90% (S15)." (3. Stacking)
   - assumption: Salpeter (1955) IMF and cosmology (Omega_M, Omega_Lambda, h) = [0.30, 0.70, 0.70]; factor 1.7 conversion from Chabrier (2003) IMF  
  > "For this work, we adopted a Salpeter (1955) initial mass function (IMF) and the cosmological parameters (ΩM, ΩΛ, h) = [0.30, 0.70, 0.70]. We used a factor of 1.7 to convert M⋆ and SFR from a Chabrier (2003) to a Salpeter (1955) IMF whenever necessary" (1. Introduction)
   - assumption: Observed 1.6 um luminosity relates to stellar mass as M* = C * L_1.6um^alpha  
  > "In summary, we assumed, for each redshift bin, that the observed luminosity at 1.6 µm (Lobs 1.6 µm) can be related to M⋆ simply by M⋆ = C × Lα 1.6 µm−obs with a scatter." (3. Stacking)
   - fitting: Photometric data fitted up to IRAC 4.5 um with EAZY assuming delayed exponentially declining SFH and Bruzual & Charlot (2003) models  
  > "Photometric data were fitted up to IRAC 4.5 µm, with EAZY (Brammer et al. 2008), by assuming a delayed exponentially declining star formation history with the Bruzual & Charlot (2003) stellar population synthesis model." (2.1. Sample)
   - validation: Checked completeness at 3 < z < 5 against deep JWST-NIRCam CEERS survey in EGS, finding 89.7% of log10(M*/Msun) > 10 galaxies are detected in HST H-band  
  > "We found that 89.7% of the galaxies with log 10 ( _M_ ⋆ / _M_ ⊙ ) - 10 (209 galaxies detected, 233 overall) detected in the JWST bands are also detected in the HST" (Sect. 3)
2. **Perform image-plane mean stacking and photometry in Spitzer, Herschel, and ALMA bands**: Mean stack postage stamps of detected and undetected galaxies in bins of M* and z, rotating Herschel stamps by 90 deg successively. Measure fluxes via aperture photometry (Spitzer 24 um and ALMA 1.13 mm) or PSF + background fitting (Herschel PACS and SPIRE), and apply clustering and filtering corrections.  
  > "We choose to use mean stacking for our work, rather than median stacking." (3. Stacking); "It consists in fitting only a PSF and a local background term: S (x, y) = ϕ × PS F(x, y) + ε" (3.2. Herschel); "The resulting flux density in each stacked image was obtained via standard aperture measurements or PSF fitting method" (Sect. 3)
   - parameter: Spitzer 24 um aperture radius = 4 pixels (4.8 arcsec), containing ~60% of point-source flux  
  > "The aperture is chosen to be 4 pixels (i.e. 4′′. 8) in radius, which contains about ∼60% of the total flux for a point source." (3.1. Spitzer)
   - parameter: Herschel PSF fitting radius = 0.9 * FWHM; SPIRE Gaussian PSF FWHM = 18.15, 25.15, 36.3 arcsec at 250, 350, 500 um  
  > "The fit is performed on a fixed aperture of radius of 0.9×FWHM, as this was found to minimise the clustering contamination to ϕ (S15)." (3.2. Herschel); "For the PACS maps, we used the truncated PSF derived from Vesta, while for Herschel-SPIRE the PSF is assumed to be Gaussian with a FWHM of 18′′. 15, 25′′. 15 and 36′′. 3 respectively at 250, 350 and 500 µm" (3.2. Herschel)
   - parameter: ALMA 1.13 mm aperture radius = 1 arcsec, S_AP^PSF / S_tot^PSF = 0.85  
  > "we applied an aperture photometry within a radius of 1 [′′], containing 85% of the total flux density" (Sect. 3.3)
   - assumption: Clustering bias is negligible in Spitzer 24 um and ALMA 1.13 mm stacks due to small PSF FWHM  
  > "As the 24 µm MIPS-Spitzer map (∼5′′. 8 PSF FWHM) has a better resolution than Herschel-PACS (∼7′′ and ∼ 11′′. 3 PSF FWHM at 100 µm and 160 µm, respectively), we do not expect the clustering bias (see Sect. 3.2) to be a dominant effect on the stacked stamp." (3.1. Spitzer); "the clustering bias is negligible on the stacked stamp." (Sect. 3.3)
   - assumption: Herschel clustering bias correction factors from S15 (0%, 3%, 8%, 13%, 25% at 100, 160, 250, 350, 500 um) and PACS high-pass filter corrections (17% undetected, 10% detected)  
  > "100 0%+7% −7% 160 3%+9% −8% 250 8%+12% −8% 350 13%+12% −10% 500 25%+19% −18%" (Table 4); "It was shown in Popesso et al. (2012) that a correction factor of 17%, and 10% should be taken into account when stacking undetected faint, and detected, sources in the PACS maps (100 µm and 160 µm), respectively." (3.2. Herschel)
   - fitting: Linear PSF + constant background fit in Herschel bands; uncertainties from 100 random edge apertures (Monte Carlo) and 100 bootstrap realisations with replacement  
  > "while σMC is the standard deviation of the signal in 100 circles, of the same radius r, randomly positioned on the edges of the stacked stamp." (3. Stacking); "In this study, we compute a 100 runs. The error is then deduced from the standard deviation of the computed flux densities for this bin." (3. Stacking)
   - validation: Tested uv-plane vs image-plane stacking on a few ALMA bins, finding only a 10% flux difference  
  > "Furthermore, on few test bins we only observed a 10% difference between fluxes calculated from stacks in the uv and image planes." (3.3. ALMA)
3. **Fit stacked FIR-to-mm SEDs to derive L_IR, T_dust, and M_dust**: Fit stacked fluxes (with S/N >= 3) and 5 sigma upper limits using the Schreiber et al. (2018) template library; integrate best-fit SED over 8-1000 um rest-frame for L_IR, extract mass-weighted T_dust, and associate M_dust by re-fitting the S18 library with Draine & Li (2007) models.  
  > "To fit the SED, we only considered fluxes with S/N ≥ 3. Stacked flux measurements with S/N < 3 are replaced by conservative 5σ upper limits." (4.1. SED fitting procedure); "From these SED fits, we measured the corresponding infrared luminosity (LIR) by integrating the best-fit SED in the range 8–1000 µm rest frame." (4.1. SED fitting procedure); "In practice, we have re-fitted the model library of S18 with a Draine & Li (2007) model to associate the Mdust." (4.1. SED fitting procedure)
   - parameter: Detection threshold S/N >= 3; non-detections and z > 4 MIPS 24 um fluxes set to 5 sigma upper limits  
  > "To fit the SED, we only considered fluxes with _S_ / _N_ ≥ 3. Stacked flux measurements with _S_ / _N_ < 3 are replaced by conservative 5σ upper limits." (Sect. 4.1)
   - parameter: Effective greybody emissivity beta = 1.5; T_dust^M restricted to S18 evolution +- 10 K when only 1 or 2 points are detected  
  > "The luminosity weighted TL dust, for the library from S18, is calculated from a grey body of effective emissivity β = 1.5 (S18)." (4.1. SED fitting procedure); "In the cases where we only had one or two points to perform our fit (i.e. the SED peak was not well defined in this case), we chose to restrict the TM dust during the fit to the TM dust evolution from S18 ±10 K." (4.1. SED fitting procedure)
   - assumption: No AGN contribution to stacked SED; amorphous silicate and graphite grain dust masses (Draine & Li 2007 scale, factor of ~2 higher than Galliano et al. 2011)  
  > "We have assumed a form of SED that we would expect for the main sequence galaxies, and no active galactic nuclei (AGN) contribution to the stacked SED." (4.1. SED fitting procedure); "However, the Mdust deduced from Galliano et al. (2011) is about a factor of 2 lower than those deduced from Draine & Li (2007), without affecting the Mgas as the factor cancels out when converting Mdust to Mgas." (4.1. SED fitting procedure)
   - fitting: Template fitting with S18 library; L_IR uncertainties derived by randomly varying stacked photometry within uncertainties  
  > "The error on LIR was obtained by varying their stacked photometry randomly within their uncertainties." (4.1. SED fitting procedure)
   - validation: Compared S18 vs Draine & Li (2007) fits (differences -7% for L_IR, 1% for T_dust, 4% for M_dust); performed mock stacking simulations (bias -1% for L_IR, -6% for T_dust, 7% for M_dust); checked AGN contribution with Fritz et al. (2006) models (<10% L_IR) and CMB heating following da Cunha et al. (2013)  
  > "Overall we verified that fitting our stacked flux with the S18 or Draine & Li (2007) libraries has little to no impact on our results, as the differences on some key properties such as LIR, Tdust and Mdust (once corrected for the emissivity chosen in S18) are quite small: −7%+7% −4% for LIR, 1%+12% −6% for Tdust and 4%+33% −17% for Mdust." (4.1. SED fitting procedure); "As a result, we find no clear evidence of significant averaging bias and thus decided not to apply any correction." (Sect. 4.3); "We checked for any AGN contribution by fitting our SED with a combination of Draine & Li (2007) dust model, and Fritz et al. (2006) AGN model." (Sect. 4.4)
4. **Derive total star formation rates and fit the star-forming main sequence (MS) and dust temperature evolution**: Combine average UV SFR (from rest-frame 1500 A L_UV) and IR SFR (from stacked L_IR) in each bin, correct for log-normal mean-to-median offset, and fit the MS evolution (Eq. 12 and Eq. 13) and linear T_dust(z) relation (Eq. 7).  
  > "In Fig. 3, we display the mean Tdust over each redshift bin, weighted by the number of galaxies in each bin of M⋆, as a function of redshift." (5.1. Dust temperature); "To obtain the total SFR associated with a galaxy, or in our case the stack of several galaxies, it is necessary to combine the SFRs deduced from both the UV and the IR as: S FRTot = S FRUV + S FRIR." (5.2. The main sequence of star-forming galaxies); "The S FR − M⋆ correlation was then fitted using the formula introduced in S15 as it is able to capture the bending of the main sequence at the high-mass end (see Eq. (12))" (5.2. The main sequence of star-forming galaxies)
   - parameter: Gaussian MS scatter sigma_dist = 0.3 dex for mean-to-median correction; gamma = 1.1 fixed in Eq. 13  
  > "To correct for this, we assume that the dispersion of this Gaussian distribution in logarithmic space is σdist = 0.3 dex (S15)." (5.2. The main sequence of star-forming galaxies); "Following recommendations from Daddi et al. (2022), we set γ = 1.1, which should help to reduce the errors and should not affect the result much." (5.2. The main sequence of star-forming galaxies)
   - assumption: Kennicutt (1998a) L_IR-to-SFR_IR conversion (L_IR = 5.8e9 * SFR_IR) and Daddi et al. (2004) L_UV-to-SFR_UV conversion (SFR_UV = 2.17e-10 * L_UV)  
  > "LIR [L⊙] = 5.8 × 109 × S FRIR [M⊙ yr−1]. (6)" (4.3. Simulations to correct for averaging biases); "The S FRUV, uncorrected for dust attenuation, is computed from LUV (rest-frame 1500 Å UV luminosity) following Daddi et al. (2004): S FRUV [M⊙ yr−1] = 2.17 × 10−10 LUV [L⊙]. (11)" (5.2. The main sequence of star-forming galaxies)
   - fitting: Parametric fits of T_dust(z) (Eq. 7) and SFR_MS(M*, z) (Eq. 12 and Eq. 13) including upper limits

## Qualitative findings

- Adding ALMA 1.13 mm data to Herschel allows the stacking analysis to reach lower stellar masses and higher redshifts (3.1 <= z <= 5.0) and better constrains the SED peak.  
  > "We show that the addition of ALMA to Herschel allows us to reach lower M⋆ and higher redshifts." (abstract); "The addition of ALMA to this study results in an improvement of the SED at high redshift (i.e, at 3.1 ≤ z ≤ 5.0)." (4.2. Adding ALMA to Herschel)
- The mean mass-weighted dust temperature of main sequence galaxies evolves linearly with redshift up to z ~ 5 with no apparent correlation with stellar mass.  
  > "The mean Tdust of MS galaxies evolves linearly with redshift, with no apparent correlation with M⋆." (abstract)
- The SFR-M* main sequence has a slope close to unity at low masses with a bending at the high-mass end that becomes more prominent at z < 2.  
  > "We confirm that the SFR-M⋆ star formation main sequence (MS) follows a linear evolution with a slope close to unity with a bending at the high-mass end at z < 2." (abstract)
- Massive galaxies (M* >= 10^10 Msun) account for more than 70% of the total cosmic SFR density across all redshifts up to z ~ 5, while lower-mass galaxies (M* <= 10^10 Msun) have a roughly constant contribution.  
  > "We show that, up to z ∼ 5, massive galaxies (i.e. M⋆ ≥ 1010 M⊙ ) account for most of the total SFR density (ρSFR ), while the contribution of lower-mass galaxies (i.e. M⋆ ≤ 1010 M⊙ ) is rather constant." (abstract); "We again observe that massive galaxies (i.e. log10 (M⋆/M⊙) > 10) dominate the total ρSFR at all redshifts (i.e. they account for more than 70%)." (6. Cosmic star formation history)
- In the TNG100 cosmological simulation, massive galaxies do not form enough stars around cosmic noon (z ~ 2) and have lower gas fractions than observed, possibly due to overly efficient AGN feedback.  
  > "We find that TNG100 exhibits a noticeable difference in the evolution of the CSFH, that is, the marked evolution of massive galaxies found in the observations appears to be smoothed in the simulation, possibly due to feedback that is too efficient." (abstract); "We conclude that the mechanisms used to regulate star formation (in particular feedback from AGNs) are possibly too strong in this simulation, namely too efficient at producing a drop in the gas content in massive galaxies." (9. Summary and conclusions)
- High-redshift galaxies (z = 3-5) exhibit a stronger star formation efficiency (shorter depletion time) at fixed gas surface density compared to low-redshift galaxies.  
  > "Finally, we find hints that the star formation efficiency of distant galaxies (z = 3–5) is stronger (shorter depletion time) as compared to low-redshift galaxies." (abstract); "Overall, even though the evolution of the SFE with redshift is not well constrained, this study suggests that galaxies at high-redshift form stars more efficiently at a fixed gas surface density." (8.3. The SFE and Kennicutt-Schmidt relation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining deep Herschel surveys in four fields with the blind 1.13 mm GOODS-ALMA survey accounts for dust attenuation and reaches lower stellar masses and higher redshifts (up to z ~ 5) while constraining the FIR SED peak.  
  > "Attenuation by dust is accounted for thanks to the combination of deep surveys by Herschel and the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter array (ALMA)." (abstract); "However, the addition of ALMA measurements to Herschel using stacking has not been carried out yet. The reason is that this requires access to an extensive study programme on ALMA. This has only recently been possible thanks to blind surveys such as the GOODS-ALMA survey (Franco et al. 2018, 2020; Gómez-Guijarro et al. 2022a)." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Stellar-mass selection and mean stacking avoid uncertain extrapolations of the IR luminosity function (which is polluted by starbursts), avoid non-linear median stacking biases, and allow recovery of total flux for faint galaxy populations; the S18 SED library has few free parameters suited for sparse FIR sampling; the MZR gas mass method accounts for metallicity variations with stellar mass.  
  > "In order to avoid uncertain extrapolations of the infrared luminosity function, which is often polluted by the contribution of starbursts, we base our analysis on stellar mass." (abstract); "the mean stack gives us access to the total flux in the stack, which we currently miss to infer" (Sect. 3); "We favoured the present method because it takes into account the variation of metallicity and the associated change in dust-to-gas ratio as a function of stellar mass." (5.3. Gas mass)
- Alternative: Median stacking (rejected in favour of mean stacking because median is non-linear and introduces systematic biases at low S/N or for skewed flux distributions)  
  > "We choose to use mean stacking for our work, rather than median stacking." (Sect. 3)
- Alternative: Stacking undetected sources on residual maps separately from detected sources (rejected in favour of stacking all sources directly on the original maps for consistency and to avoid biases)  
  > "Following S15, we have therefore chosen to treat detected and undetected sources in the same way for consistency (i.e. directly from the image and not from the residual)." (3. Stacking)
- Alternative: Stacking ALMA data in the uv plane (rejected in favour of image-plane stacking to save computing time after tests showed only a 10% difference)  
  > "We decided not to stack directly in the uv plane but in the image plane in order to save computing time as this should not impact the results of this study." (3.3. ALMA)
- Alternative: Fitting SEDs directly with Draine & Li (2007) / Draine et al. (2014) models (rejected as primary fit in favour of Schreiber et al. 2018 templates because Draine & Li has too many free parameters for sparse high-z/low-mass FIR data)  
  > "Because we do not have many points on the FIR at low M⋆ and high redshifts, we used the library from Schreiber et al. (2018, hereafter S18), which is well suited for our study given its small number of free parameters. Choosing a model with more parameters, such as Draine & Li (2007) and Draine et al. (2014), would imply fixing parameters on some bins." (4.1. SED fitting procedure)
- Alternative: Alternative gas mass derivations: Leroy et al. (2011) delta_GDR, Mannucci et al. (2010) FMR metallicity, Galliano et al. (2021) amorphous carbon dust model, and using only Scoville et al. (2016) RJ continuum method (tested and compared; MZR + Magdis et al. 2012 + Draine & Li 2007 scale adopted as baseline)  
  > "We note that by using the δGDR relation of Leroy et al. (2011), instead of Magdis et al. (2012), would not change our conclusions" (5.3. Gas mass); "We produced a version of our analysis using the same method for consistency check, it is presented in Appendix B." (5.3. Gas mass)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): H-band selection may become a less robust proxy for stellar mass at z > 3 and misses H-dropout galaxies (which are added a posteriori from Wang et al. 2019).  
  > "H-dropout galaxies are by definition not included in these samples, as they are not detected in H-band. However, we used the sample from Wang et al. (2019) to add and discuss their impact in any necessary analyses." (2.1. Sample); "This hypothesis may however become less robust at high redshifts" (Sect. 3)
- Limitation (authors): Optical-to-mm centroid offsets and high angular resolution cause the ALMA stacked images to be extended on scales larger than the ALMA PSF.  
  > "Due to the high angular resolution of the ALMA data, and to possible small offsets between the optical centroids (on which our stack positions are based) and millimetre centroids, the stacked images are extended on scales larger than that of the ALMA PSF." (3.3. ALMA)
- Limitation (authors): Very massive galaxies (M* > 2-3 x 10^11 Msun) at z > 3 cannot be probed because they are extremely rare.  
  > "Another possibility is that the bending still occurs at high redshifts, but only for very high-mass galaxies (M⋆ > 2 − 3 × 1011 M⊙), which we could not probe with this study because they are extremely rare." (5.2. The main sequence of star-forming galaxies)
- Limitation (authors): Total gas masses derived from dust at z < 0.4 cannot be used for H2 because the atomic gas (HI) content is not probed.  
  > "Therefore, we decided not to consider our Mgas for z < 0.4 (i.e. our first redshift bin), as we could not probe the HI content of the galaxy at these redshifts." (5.3. Gas mass)
- Limitation (authors): Galaxy morphology and effective radii Re are not directly measured in this study, preventing direct fitting of the size term in the gas scaling relation and requiring literature Re(z, M*) relations for the Kennicutt-Schmidt analysis.  
  > "And we do not explore the morphology of galaxies in this study, which makes it impossible to probe the term Eµ, which compares the effective radius of galaxies (Re) to the mean effective radius of the star forming population Re 0." (5.3. Gas mass); "We do not probe Re in this study, thus, we chose to use Re(z, M⋆), for galaxies with M⋆ ≥ 1010, from Wang et al. (2022)." (8.3. The SFE and Kennicutt-Schmidt relation)
- Limitation (authors): The low stellar mass and high redshift regime of the cosmic gas mass density relies on uncertain extrapolations and is sensitive to the functional form chosen to fit M_gas.  
  > "The latter part is a potential weakness of all studies including ours, since the low stellar mass – high redshift of the cosmic gas mass density range relies on rather uncertain extrapolations." (7. Cosmic evolution of the gas mass density)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA 1.13 mm coverage is only available in a 69 arcmin^2 sub-region of GOODS-South (8,659 galaxies out of 128,952 total across 1,077 arcmin^2), so the ALMA stacked photometry probes ~1/15th the volume and sample size of the Herschel stacks and is more susceptible to cosmic variance.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In the two lowest-mass high-z bins (10.0 <= log10(M*) <= 10.5 at 3.1 <= z <= 3.9 and 3.9 <= z <= 5.0), only the single ALMA 1.13 mm point is detected, requiring T_dust to be restricted to the S18 evolution +- 10 K for L_IR and switching from the MZR dust-to-gas method to the Scoville et al. (2016) single-band calibration for M_gas.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Kennicutt-Schmidt and SFE evolution conclusions at z > 3 depend on assumed average effective radii Re(z, M*) taken from Wang et al. (2022) rather than size measurements of the stacked galaxies themselves.
