- Question: Whether an infalling-rotating envelope with a centrifugal barrier, and associated chemical change, can be identified around IRAS 16293-2422 Source A using ALMA archival lines of OCS, CH3OH, HCOOCH3 and H2CS

## Analysis recipe

1. **Image continuum and lines**: CLEAN imaging, continuum from line-free channels subtracted in uv; moment 0/1 maps  
  > "The continuum image was prepared by averaging line-free channels" (2 Data)
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5  
  > "robustness parameter of 0.5" (ALMA Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure source sizes and position angle**: 2D Gaussian fits to moment 0 maps; PA from red/blue extreme emission peaks and continuum  
  > "The position angle of the disk/envelope system is thus determined to be 65$^\circ$$\pm$10$^\circ$" (4.1 OCS)
   - assumption: Systemic velocity 3.8 km/s  
  > "we employ 3.8 km s$^{-1}$ as the systemic velocity of Source A" (4.1 OCS)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian  
  > "The 2D Gaussian-fitted size of the OCS line intensity distribution deconvolved by the beam is" (Line intensity Distribution)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit infalling-rotating envelope model to OCS PV diagrams**: Ballistic model PV diagrams convolved with beam and 1 km/s line width, compared by eye and residual rms along envelope and outflow directions  
  > "Judging from the goodness of the simultaneous fit for the two directions (Figures 6 and 7) by eye" (5.1 OCS)
   - parameter: r_CB 40–60 AU, M=0.75 Msun, outer radius 180 AU  
  > "The outer radius of the OCS distribution is derived to be 180 AU." (5.1 OCS)
   - assumption: Density r^-1.5, constant abundance, optically thin, inclination 60 deg  
  > "we assume a density profile proportional to $r^{-1.5}$ and a constant abundance of molecules for simplicity" (5.1 OCS)
   - fitting: Grid comparison, no chi-square  
  > "Systematic errors caused by these assumptions would overwhelm the statistical noise, which makes the chi-square analysis difficult" (5.1 OCS)
   - validation: Residual rms table; density profile r^0 and r^-2.5 tested; Keplerian-only model fails along outflow direction  
  > "we also conducted the simulations by using density profiles of $r^0$ and $r^{-2.5}$" (5.1 OCS)
4. **Model CH3OH, HCOOCH3 and H2CS**: Same M, r_CB, i with smaller outer radii (80, 55, 150 AU); Keplerian disk model for H2CS with outer radius 50 AU  
  > "the outer radii of the infalling-rotating envelope model for CH$_3$OH and HCOOCH$_3$ are set to be 80 AU and 55 AU, respectively." (5.2 CH3OH and HCOOCH3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual comparison of PV diagrams  
  > "Judging from the goodness of the simultaneous fit for the two directions (Figures [6](#fig:PV_varParams_envelope) and [7](#fig:PV_varParams_outflow)) by eye" (Infalling-Rotating Envelope Model)
5. **Derive gas kinetic temperature from H2CS line ratios**: Integrated intensity ratios in 0.5'' circles at 1.0'', 0.5'', 0.0'' offsets compared with RADEX  
  > "The intensity ratios are compared with those calculated by using RADEX code (van der Tak et al. 2007)" (6.2 Origin of the Chemical Change)
   - software: RADEX
   - parameter: n(H2) 1e7–1e9 cm-3; N(H2CS) 1e13–1e15 cm-2  
  > "from $10^7$ to $10^9$ cm$^{-3}$ and from $10^{13}$ to $10^{15}$ cm$^{-2}$" (6.2 Origin of the Chemical Change)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Temperatures barely depend on density and column density  
  > "The gas kinetic temperatures barely depend on the H$_2$ density and the column density of H$_2$CS." (6.2 Origin of the Chemical Change)
6. **HCOOCH3/CH3OH column density ratios**: LTE column densities per component over limited velocity ranges  
  > "we employed the LTE approximation" (6.3 Abundance of HCOOCH3 Relative to CH3OH)
   - assumption: Tex 100 K (envelope), 130 K (CB), 100 K (disk); CH3OH optically thin  
  > "assumed to be 100 K, 130 K, and 100 K, respectively" (6.3 Abundance of HCOOCH3 Relative to CH3OH)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- OCS traces the infalling-rotating envelope; CH3OH and HCOOCH3 are compact near the centrifugal barrier; H2CS spans envelope to Keplerian disk  
  > "OCS resides in the envelope, while CH$_3$OH and HCOOCH$_3$ have more compact distributions." (6 Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Simple model chosen to concentrate on velocity structure  
  > "we employ a simple model concentrating on the velocity structure of the envelope" (5.1 OCS)
- Alternative: Pure Keplerian motion rejected for OCS since the outflow-direction velocity gradient is not reproduced  
  > "the velocity gradient along the outflow direction cannot be reproduced" (5.1 OCS)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Residual rms has no statistical meaning; mass strongly degenerate with inclination  
  > "the protostellar mass is highly correlated with the inclination angle of the envelope" (5.1 OCS)
- Limitation (authors): Resolution insufficient to distinguish accretion shock from protostellar heating  
  > "discrimination of the two possibilities is left for future studies." (6.2 Origin of the Chemical Change)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model fit by eye with no uncertainty estimation
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Column density ratios depend on assumed excitation temperature and optically thin CH3OH
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inclination fixed; mass degenerate
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ratio components integrate limited velocity ranges
