## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, concatenate, image ACA visibilities, and feather with IRAM 30 m data**: Apply standard ALMA calibration, concatenate visibilities from 36 science goals across projects, image with tclean using multi-scale deconvolver, natural weighting, and auto-multithresh down to ~1 sigma, then combine 12CO(J=2-1) ACA data with IRAM 30 m data using feather.  
  > "Prior to the imaging process, we concatenated all visibilities obtained in each science goal with a total number of 36." (Observations and Data Reduction); "We used the `tclean` task with `multi-scale` deconvolver (cite: kepley2020) to recover extended emission as much as possible. In `tclean` task, we applied the natural weighting and used the `auto-multithresh` procedure to identify automatically regions containing emission in the dirty and residual images." (Observations and Data Reduction); "To compensate for such diffuse components, we combined the ACA 7 m-array $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) data with the IRAM 30 m data using the `feather` task." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, feather
   - parameter: natural weighting  
  > "In `tclean` task, we applied the natural weighting and used the `auto-multithresh` procedure to identify automatically regions containing emission in the dirty and residual images." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - parameter: threshold ~ 1 sigma noise level  
  > "We continued the deconvolution process until the intensity of the residual image attains the $\sim$1 $\sigma$ noise level." (Observations and Data Reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared global 12CO(J=2-1) luminosity between ACA, IRAM 30 m, and ACA+IRAM data over the observed region; checked C18O upper limit against convolved archival 12 m data (2017.1.00461.S) in NGC 604.  
  > "To evaluate the missing flux of the ACA observations, we measured the global $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) luminosities over the M33 disk obtained by the ACA 7 m antennas and by the IRAM 30 m telescope (cite: druard2014)." (Observations and Data Reduction); "To check the validity of the upper limit, we retrieved the ALMA archival data (project code 2017.1.00461.S) and examined the C$^{18}$O($J=2-1$) emission in a GMC associated with NGC 604." (CO Maps)
2. **Construct velocity-integrated intensity (zeroth moment) maps for 12CO and 13CO**: Convolve H I data from 20'' to 40'', regrid to match CO data, determine representative H I velocity V_rep in each pixel, and integrate 12CO(J=2-1) and 13CO(J=2-1) emission from V_rep - 30 km/s to V_rep + 30 km/s.  
  > "Firstly, we convolved the H i data whose original angular resolution is 20$\ensuremath{''}$ to 40$\ensuremath{''}$ in order to reduce the effect of the anomalous H i velocity components. Then, we regridded them to match our CO data and determined the representative H i velocity $V_{\rm rep}$ in each pixel." (CO Maps); "Finally, we calculated the zeroth moment in $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) emission from $V_{\rm rep}$ $-$ 30 km s$^{-1}$ to $V_{\rm rep}$ $+$ 30 km s$^{-1}$." (CO Maps); "We also calculated the $^{13}$CO($J=2-1$) zeroth moment from $V_{\rm rep}$ $-$ 30 km s$^{-1}$ to $V_{\rm rep}$ $+$ 30 km s$^{-1}$." (CO Maps)
   - parameter: H I convolved resolution = 40''  
  > "Firstly, we convolved the H i data whose original angular resolution is 20$\ensuremath{''}$ to 40$\ensuremath{''}$ in order to reduce the effect of the anomalous H i velocity components." (CO Maps)
   - parameter: Integration velocity window = V_rep +/- 30 km/s  
  > "To correctly measure the CO intensity in M33, we needed the velocity range of $V_{\rm rep}$ $\pm$ 30 km s$^{-1}$ for the calculation of the $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) zeroth moment." (CO Maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested V_rep +/- 20 km/s window and found that some molecular clouds dropped ~30% of CO flux, justifying +/- 30 km/s.  
  > "In fact, we found that some molecular clouds dropped $\sim$30% of CO flux if we apply the velocity range of $V_{\rm rep}$ $\pm$ 20 km s$^{-1}$." (CO Maps)
3. **Decompose molecular clouds and measure basic cloud properties**: Convolve ACA+IRAM 12CO(J=2-1) cube to 7.5'' circular beam, mask emission using cprops_mask, identify local maxima and assign emission via watershed algorithm in PYCPROPS, deconvolve beam and channel width to obtain intrinsic radius R and velocity dispersion sigma_v, and filter out clouds with undefined radius or at the edge of ACA FOV.  
  > "Firstly, we convolved the ACA+IRAM $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) cube data to 7.5$''$ in order to identify molecular clouds by a circular beam. Then, we made masked cube data using `cprops_mask` provided by (cite: rosolowsky2021)." (Cloud Decomposition); "To do this, we use a watershed algorithm, which associates all the cube pixels in the emission-masked data with a local maximum." (Cloud Decomposition); "Thus, we consider 848 molecular clouds after excluding small clouds with undefined radius and clouds at the edge of ACA FOV from the originally identified clouds by `PYCPROPS`." (Cloud Decomposition)
   - software: PYCPROPS, cprops_mask
   - parameter: Convolved circular beam = 7.5'' (30 pc)  
  > "Firstly, we convolved the ACA+IRAM $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) cube data to 7.5$''$ in order to identify molecular clouds by a circular beam." (Cloud Decomposition)
   - parameter: High significance mask > 4 sigma in 3 continuous velocity channels; low significance mask > 3 sigma in 3 continuous velocity channels over 7.5'' beam  
  > "The criteria of the emission mask are that high significance emission is required to be more than 4 $\sigma$ in continuous three velocity channels and low significance emission which is adjacent to high significance emission is required to be more than 3 $\sigma$ in continuous three velocity channels at least over the size of the ACA 7.5$''$ beam." (Cloud Decomposition)
   - parameter: Local maxima merge threshold: T_max - T_merge >= 2 sigma; minimum spatial separation = 7.5''; minimum spectral separation = 7 km/s  
  > "The neighbor is rejected if $T_{\rm max} - T_{\rm merge}$ is less than 2 $\sigma$, which means that such a local maximum is likely a noise fluctuation." (Cloud Decomposition); "We adopted the beam size (7.5$''$) as a minimum spatial separation and also adopted 7 km s$^{-1}$ as a minimum spectral separation," (Cloud Decomposition)
   - parameter: Radius conversion coefficient = 1.91; channel width Delta V_chan = 0.7 km/s  
  > "The coefficient 1.91 converts the rms size to the effective spherical radius of the cloud (cite: solomon1987; rosolowsky2006)." (Cloud Decomposition); "where $\sigma_{v, {\rm chan}}$ is the velocity resolution element, which is related to the velocity channel width ($\Delta V_{\rm chan}$ = 0.7 km s$^{-1}$) as $\sigma_{v, {\rm chan}} = \Delta V_{\rm chan}/(2 \sqrt{2\,{\rm ln}\,2})$." (Cloud Decomposition)
   - assumption: Distance D = 840 kpc (1'' = 4 pc)  
  > "Here, $\sigma_{\rm beam}$ is calculated as $7.5\ensuremath{''} \times 4$ = 30, where the factor of 4 is the spatial size in parsec of 1$\ensuremath{''}$ at the distance of M33 (840 kpc)." (Cloud Decomposition)
   - fitting: Bootstrapping with 10,000 trials per cloud, using median absolute deviation scaled by square root of oversampling rate for uncertainties.  
  > "We repeated the resampling and remeasuring 10,000 times for each cloud, and evaluated the uncertainties. The final uncertainty in each property is the median absolute deviation of the bootstrapped values scaled up by the square root of the over-sampling rate, which corresponds to the number of pixels per beam size." (Catalog Description)
   - validation: Tuned low significance mask threshold from default 2 sigma to 3 sigma to reject fake emission at cloud edges; compared extrapolated to observed values; separated low-S/N (<10) and high-S/N (>=10) clouds in subsequent analyses.  
  > "In particular, the low significance mask does not reject fake emission (i.e., noise) at the cloud edge. Thus, we carefully tuned the rms thresholds, and finally we adopted 3 $\sigma$ for low significance masks." (Cloud Decomposition); "Since our catalog includes 147 molecular clouds whose S/N ratio is lower than 10, we examine the properties of such low-S/N clouds in the following analyses." (Catalog Description)
4. **Calculate CO luminosities, luminosity-based masses, and virial masses**: Compute L_CO = S D^2, luminosity mass M_CO from L_CO using constant X_CO and R_21, and virial mass M_Vir = 1040 R sigma_v^2 and virial parameter alpha = M_Vir / M_CO.  
  > "From the basic properties of molecular clouds, we calculated additional properties such as the CO luminosity $L_{\rm CO} = SD^2$ where $D = 840$ kpc and cloud masses." (Luminosities and Masses); "We also calculated the virial mass as $M_{\rm Vir} = 1040\,R\,\sigma_v^2$ for a spherical and virialized cloud with a density profile of $\rho \propto r^{-1}$ (cite: bolatto2013)." (Luminosities and Masses)
   - assumption: Distance D = 840 kpc  
  > "From the basic properties of molecular clouds, we calculated additional properties such as the CO luminosity $L_{\rm CO} = SD^2$ where $D = 840$ kpc and cloud masses." (Luminosities and Masses)
   - assumption: Constant X_CO = 4.0 x 10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 (includes helium contribution via factor 4.35)  
  > "We adopted a constant $X_{\rm CO}$ of $4.0 \times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: gratier2017) over the M33 disk." (Luminosities and Masses)
   - assumption: Constant 12CO(J=2-1)/12CO(J=1-0) intensity ratio R_21 = 0.60 across M33  
  > "Thus, we adopted a constant $R_{2-1/1-0}$ of 0.60 across the M33 disk in this study." (Luminosities and Masses)
   - assumption: Spherical and virialized cloud with density profile rho proportional to r^-1  
  > "We also calculated the virial mass as $M_{\rm Vir} = 1040\,R\,\sigma_v^2$ for a spherical and virialized cloud with a density profile of $\rho \propto r^{-1}$ (cite: bolatto2013)." (Luminosities and Masses)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared average R_21 = 0.60 from existing single-dish M33 data with MW and nearby galaxy studies; verified total molecular gas mass from ACA+IRAM (2.9 x 10^8 M_sun) against IRAM 30 m (3.0 x 10^8 M_sun).

## Qualitative findings

- High-mass clouds (>= 10^5 M_sun) tend to associate with 8 um-bright sources in the spiral arm region, whereas low-mass clouds (< 10^5 M_sun) tend to be apart from 8 um-bright sources and exist in the inter-arm region.  
  > "We found that high-mass clouds ($\geq 10^5\,M_{\odot}$) tend to associate with the $8\,\mu$m-bright sources in the spiral arm region, while low-mass clouds ($< 10^5\,M_{\odot}$) tend to be apart from such $8\,\mu$m-bright sources and to exist in the inter-arm region." (Abstract)
- Most molecular clouds in M33 show smaller velocity dispersions than the Galactic R - sigma_v relation at a given radius, likely due to lower surface densities.  
  > "We found that most of the molecular clouds in M33 show smaller velocity dispersions than the Galactic $R-\sigma_v$ relation at a given radius. This is presumably due to low-surface density molecular clouds, which may be maintained even by the small turbulence." (Summary)
- Small IRAM GMCs are identified as single molecular clouds in ACA+IRAM data, while large IRAM GMCs (> 3 x 10^5 M_sun) are resolved into multiple ACA+IRAM clouds.  
  > "We found that a small IRAM GMC is identified as a single molecular cloud even in ACA+IRAM CO data, while a large IRAM GMC (typically its $M_{\rm CO}$ is larger than $3 \times 10^5 M_{\odot}$) can be resolved into multiple ACA+IRAM clouds." (Summary)
- The velocity dispersion of a large IRAM GMC is mainly dominated by the line-of-sight velocity difference between small internal clouds rather than internal cloud velocity broadening when v_diff > 2 km/s.  
  > "This suggests that the velocity dispersion of a large cloud is mainly dominated by the line-of-sight velocity difference between small clouds inside the GMC in the case of $v_{\rm diff} >$ 2 km s$^{-1}$, while the velocity dispersion of individual internal clouds determines the overall velocity dispersion of the GMC if $v_{\rm diff}$ is less than 2 km s$^{-1}$." (Origin of Velocity Dispersion in IRAM GMCs)
- High-mass clouds in M33 are almost virialized (alpha <= 3), indicating that high-mass star formation likely onsets within them via gravitational instability.  
  > "Based on the comparison between $M_{\rm CO}$ and $M_{\rm Vir}$ for ACA+IRAM clouds, we found that high-mass clouds in M33 are almost virialized. This indicates that the high-mass star formation likely onsets within such high-mass clouds by the gravitational instability." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: M33 is close (840 kpc) with moderate inclination (55 deg), allowing ACA 7 m-array stand-alone observations combined with IRAM 30 m data to map the entire molecular disk at 30 pc resolution with high sensitivity to recover both diffuse gas and low-mass (< 10^5 M_sun) clouds missed by external galaxy surveys like PHANGS-ALMA.  
  > "Thus, we need further molecular-cloud surveys covering such less massive clouds in nearby galaxies as a complementary study to PHANGS-ALMA survey." (Introduction); "The main purposes of the new ACA observations are to obtain the spatial distribution in CO($J=2-1$) emission with the higher sensitivity and higher angular resolution compared to earlier studies in M33 and to identify low-mass ($< 10^5\,M_{\odot}$) clouds as well as high-mass ($\geq 10^5\,M_{\odot}$) clouds." (Introduction); "We combined the ACA 7 m-array $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) data with the IRAM 30 m data to compensate for emission from diffuse molecular-gas components." (Abstract)
- Why this method: Used multi-scale clean to recover extended emission, H I velocity windows to minimize noise in moment 0 maps without losing broad CO wing flux, PYCPROPS with tuned 3 sigma low-significance masking to objectively decompose complex hierarchical structures while rejecting edge noise, and HIIphot to verify 8 um association after removing diffuse emission.  
  > "We used the `tclean` task with `multi-scale` deconvolver (cite: kepley2020) to recover extended emission as much as possible." (Observations and Data Reduction); "To minimize the effect of the noise, we determined the velocity channel in which the CO emission is expected to appear using the atomic (H i) data (cite: koch2018) as follows." (CO Maps); "To identify individual emission structures in an objective way and to investigate the properties of molecular clouds, we employed `PYCPROPS` (cite: rosolowsky2021), a Python implementation of the algorithm to catalog molecular clouds, `CPROPS` (cite: rosolowsky2006)." (Cloud Decomposition)
- Alternative: Rejected integrating CO zeroth moment over V_rep +/- 20 km/s in favor of V_rep +/- 30 km/s because some molecular clouds dropped ~30% of CO flux.  
  > "In fact, we found that some molecular clouds dropped $\sim$30% of CO flux if we apply the velocity range of $V_{\rm rep}$ $\pm$ 20 km s$^{-1}$. To correctly measure the CO intensity in M33, we needed the velocity range of $V_{\rm rep}$ $\pm$ 30 km s$^{-1}$ for the calculation of the $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) zeroth moment." (CO Maps)
- Alternative: Rejected default cprops_mask low significance threshold of 2 sigma in favor of 3 sigma because 2 sigma did not reject fake emission (noise) at cloud edges.  
  > "Although the default settings of `cprops_mask` are 4 $\sigma$ and 2 $\sigma$ for high and low significance masks, respectively, we found that the emission masks with these default settings are not suitable for the ACA+IRAM $^{12}$CO($J=2-1$) cube data. In particular, the low significance mask does not reject fake emission (i.e., noise) at the cloud edge." (Cloud Decomposition)
- Alternative: Rejected previously reported R_21 = 0.8 (Druard et al. 2014) in favor of constant R_21 = 0.60 based on re-evaluating M33 single-dish data and recent nearby galaxy studies.  
  > "We found that the average $R_{21}$ in M33 is 0.60, but this value is lower than the previously-reported $R_{21}$ of 0.8 (cite: druard2014)." (Luminosities and Masses)
- Alternative: Rejected the hypothesis that smaller velocity dispersions in M33 are due to a lack of active star formation feedback (like the Planck Cold Cloud), because >70% of M33 clouds are associated with star-forming regions.  
  > "Considering that the $R-\sigma_v$ relation obtained in M33 is similar to that in the PCC, it is suggested that many molecular clouds in M33 are not associated with active star formation like the PCC. However, this contradicts the fact that more than 70% of clouds in M33 are associated with star-forming regions (cite: gratier2012; konishi2023)." (Size–Line-width Relation)
- How they decided it was enough: Continued tclean deconvolution until residual intensity reached ~1 sigma; used 10,000 bootstrap iterations per cloud in PYCPROPS to evaluate uncertainties.  
  > "We continued the deconvolution process until the intensity of the residual image attains the $\sim$1 $\sigma$ noise level." (Observations and Data Reduction); "We repeated the resampling and remeasuring 10,000 times for each cloud, and evaluated the uncertainties." (Catalog Description)
- Limitation (authors): Beam smearing at 30 pc resolution makes compact C18O(J=2-1) emission undetectable in the ACA map.  
  > "Thus, we consider that the beam smearing effect makes C$^{18}$O($J=2-1$) emission undetectable in the ACA 30 pc-resolution map." (CO Maps)
- Limitation (authors): Small clouds with minor axis dispersion smaller than the beam (sigma_min < sigma_beam) have undefined deconvolved radii and are excluded, as are clouds at the edge of the ACA FOV where primary beam correction causes larger uncertainties.  
  > "Note that if $\sigma_{\rm min}$ is smaller than $\sigma_{\rm beam}$, the resultant $R$ is not properly defined. We do not consider such small clouds further in this paper." (Cloud Decomposition); "We do not consider molecular clouds lying at the edge of the ACA field-of-view (FOV) further because the primary beam correction causes larger uncertainties in the obtained properties of molecular clouds." (Cloud Decomposition)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming a constant R_21 = 0.60 across the M33 disk introduces an error of about 30% in M_CO because R_21 varies spatially from 0.4 to 0.8.  
  > "Note that, as pointed out by (cite: yajima2021), $R_{21}$ varies within an individual galaxy; in fact, $R_{21}$ in M33 varies from position to position, typically ranging from 0.4 to 0.8. Therefore we consider that the assumption of a constant $R_{2-1/1-0}$ over the M33 disk yields an error of about 30%." (Luminosities and Masses)
- Limitation (authors): The catalog includes 147 clouds with peak S/N < 10, whereas CPROPS requires S/N >= 10 for stable recovery of cloud properties.  
  > "As reported in (cite: rosolowsky2006), the `CPROPS` algorithm requires a minimum S/N ratio of 10 for stable recovery of cloud properties. Since our catalog includes 147 molecular clouds whose S/N ratio is lower than 10, we examine the properties of such low-S/N clouds in the following analyses." (Catalog Description)
- Limitation (authors): Distributions of cloud radius and velocity dispersion depend on the spatial and velocity resolutions of the input data cube due to the hierarchical structure of the ISM.  
  > "Note that, as pointed out by (cite: hughes2013), such distributions of the cloud radius and the velocity dispersion depend on both the spatial and the velocity resolutions of the input data cube because ISM in galaxies generally has a hierarchical structure from parsec to kiloparsec scales." (Basic Properties of Cataloged Molecular Clouds)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A constant X_CO conversion factor is assumed across the entire M33 disk despite known metallicity gradients and local environmental variations.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only the 12CO(J=2-1) cube was feathered with single-dish IRAM 30 m data; the 13CO(J=2-1) cube is stand-alone ACA data and may suffer from missing flux on extended scales, potentially biasing R_13/12 downward.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Completeness limit estimation relies on a simple linear scaling (7x lowest cloud mass) inherited from an older BIMA survey (Engargiola et al. 2003) rather than synthetic cloud injection tests on the ACA+IRAM cube.
