- Question: What is the constraint on the gravitational slip parameter eta_PPN at galactic scales at z = 0.299 when combining strong gravitational lensing and galactic dynamics for the SDP.81 lens system?

## Analysis recipe

1. **Decompose lens galaxy surface brightness profile into Multi-Gaussian Expansion (MGE) components**: Fit PSF from unsaturated field stars with IRAF/DAOPHOT, subtract lensed arcs from HST image and interpolate across excluded regions, then fit a sum of 9 concentric 2D elliptical Gaussians using MgeFit and FIND_GALAXY.  
  > "The point spread function (PSF) was obtained fitting a collection of unsaturated stars in the field near the galaxy using the `IRAF` task `DAOPHOT` (cite: DAOPHOT), resulting in a PSF with full width at half maximum (FWHM) approximately equal to $0.108\ensuremath{''}$" (HST data); "We interpolate the subtracted image across the regions where the arcs were excluded (see panel (c) of Figure 1), obtaining a lens image without the source emission. Then a new galaxy light profile is fitted using the interpolated image" (HST data); "The MGE fit results in 9 Gaussian components described in Table" (Combined modelling)
   - software: IRAF, DAOPHOT, PyRAF, MgeFit, FIND_GALAXY
   - parameter: Pixel scale = 0.09'', PSF FWHM = 0.108'', PA = 11.9 deg, N = 9 Gaussian components, R_eff = 1.14''  
  > "For our purposes, we used the deepest image, F160W, which has a pixel scale of $0.09\ensuremath{''}$." (HST data); "To fulfil this requirement, we rotate the kinematical and the ALMA data by the position angle of $11.9\ensuremath{^\circ}$, determined by the `FIND_GALAXY` algorithm (cite: Cappellari2002)." (Combined modelling); "Since we do not have enough data to constrain the dark matter halo on larger scales, we fix $r_s$ as 10 times the effective radius ($R_{\text{eff}} =  1.14\ensuremath{''}$) of the stellar component" (Combined modelling)
   - assumption: Oblate axisymmetric geometry; stellar mass follows light modulated by a Gaussian M/L profile  
  > "Following (cite: Cappellari2002), and assuming an oblate axisymmetric model, the intrinsic luminosity density can be written, in cylindrical coordinates, as" (Stellar mass profile); "We assume that the stellar mass component follows the observed luminosity distribution of the lens galaxy, modulated by a Gaussian" (Combined modelling)
   - fitting: 2D elliptical Gaussian decomposition via MgeFit  
  > "whilst the light and dark matter mass parametrizations were performed using the `MgeFit` package (cite: Cappellari2002)." (Lens modelling)
   - validation: Visual inspection of HST interpolated image, model, and residuals in Figure 1  
  > "The HST interpolated image, the final model and the residuals showing the deflected source can be seen in Figure 1, with the ALMA emission overlaid in panel (c)." (HST data)
2. **Extract spatially-resolved stellar kinematics (V_rms map) from VLT/MUSE data**: Remove telluric lines with ZAP, align astrometry to HST by 0.18'' with ASTROALIGN, exclude spaxels contaminated by strong lensed source emission, select spaxels with SNR > 2.0 in 5000-7000 A rest-frame, Voronoi-bin with VorBin to SNR >= 10, fit V and sigma_V with pPXF using Indo-US stellar templates, and compute V_rms = sqrt(V^2 + sigma_V^2).  
  > "We use the Zurich Atmosphere Purge code (cite: ZAP) to remove telluric lines which may have been left in the previous reduction step." (MUSE data); "we sum nearby spectra using the adaptive spatial two-dimensional binning scheme from `VorBin` package (cite: Vorbin_2003) to increase the SNR in each resulting Voronoi bin at a minimum of 10." (MUSE data); "After fitting all the Voronoi bins, we construct the $V_\text{rms} = \sqrt{V^2 + \sigma_V^2}$ map, which is the root-mean-square velocity for each Voronoi bin." (MUSE data)
   - software: ZAP, ASTROALIGN, VorBin, pPXF, MPDAF
   - parameter: MUSE pixel scale = 0.2'', PSF FWHM = 0.421'', astrometric shift = 0.18'', SNR > 2.0 threshold in 5000-7000 A rest-frame, Voronoi bin target SNR >= 10, additive polynomial of order 4, Indo-US template FWHM = 1.35 A  
  > "From the collapsed image, a PSF (FWHM$=0.421\ensuremath{''}$, which corresponds to $\sim 1930$ pc at the redshift of the lens galaxy) was built following the same procedure used for the HST image. To align the MUSE data with the HST data, we use the HST image as a reference and perform an astrometric shift equal to $0.18\ensuremath{''}$ in the MUSE data using the `ASTROALIGN`" (MUSE data); "We fit the two first moments of the Gauss-Hermite polynomials $[V, \sigma_V]$, including an additive polynomial of order 4 for each Voronoi bin." (MUSE data)
   - assumption: Central bin velocity represents galaxy systemic velocity and is subtracted from all bins  
  > "The central velocity, i.e., the velocity of the central bin, was subtracted from each of the Voronoi bin velocities to obtain the real velocity with respect to the centre of the galaxy." (MUSE data)
   - fitting: Penalized pixel-fitting (pPXF) of first two Gauss-Hermite moments [V, sigma_V] with 200 bootstrap resamplings of residuals (with bias = 0) to estimate 1-sigma uncertainties.  
  > "Then we fit this new spectrum with `pPXF` (setting the bias flag equal to zero) and save the outputs $[V, \sigma_V]$. We repeat this procedure generating 200 new spectra and measuring $V$ and $\sigma_V$." (MUSE data)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to stellar template choice by re-deriving kinematics with MILES and X-Shooter (XSL DR2) stellar libraries.  
  > "To estimate the level of uncertainty introduced by these issues, we evaluated how the inferred kinematics changes by the choice of a particular stellar template, re-deriving the kinematic map using the Medium resolution INT Library of Empirical Spectra (cite: Miles_models) and the X-Shooter Spectral Library (cite: XSL)." (Kinematics)
3. **Prepare ALMA Band 7 continuum image and error map for image-plane lens modelling**: Bin the ALMA Band 7 continuum image from 0.005'' to 0.01'' pixels, rotate by PA = 11.9 deg, and build an error map from the background rms measured in CASA plus 10% of each pixel value added in quadrature.  
  > "We use the image of continuum band 7, which includes the $\sim 250\mu$m rest-frame emission, for the lens modelling. The image was also binned from a pixel scale of $0.005\ensuremath{''}$ to a pixel scale of $0.01\ensuremath{''}$, to increase the modelling efficiency and reduce the covariance between the pixels (cite: Dye2015)." (ALMA data); "Moreover, the error map was estimated by the rms background value, measured using `CASA` in a region where no emission is seen, with the addition of $10$% of the value of each pixel summed in quadrature to account for possible calibration errors." (ALMA data); "To fulfil this requirement, we rotate the kinematical and the ALMA data by the position angle of $11.9\ensuremath{^\circ}$, determined by the `FIND_GALAXY` algorithm (cite: Cappellari2002)." (Combined modelling)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Binned pixel scale = 0.01'', synthesised beam = 0.0308'' x 0.0235'' at PA = 15 deg, rotation PA = 11.9 deg, calibration error = 10%  
  > "During the modelling, we assumed the synthesised beam sizes described in the ALMA data: $0.0308\ensuremath{''}  \times 0.0235\ensuremath{''}$ ($\sim 141\text{\,pc} \times 108\text{\,pc}$ at the lens redshift), position angle (PA) of $15^{\ensuremath{^\circ}}$, counterclockwise from North." (ALMA data)
   - assumption: Image-plane pixel covariance errors are small due to dense uv-plane coverage; lens galaxy light contamination is negligible in Band 7.  
  > "Due to the wavelength observed by ALMA, the lens light is not visible, and only the deflected source is captured, making this dataset very suitable for lens modelling since the contamination due to the lens galaxy light is negligible." (ALMA data); "However, due to the high coverage of the $uv-$plane in these ALMA data, the error associated with the image plane is significantly reduced." (Lens modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Perform 5-phase joint Bayesian modelling of ALMA gravitational lensing and MUSE stellar dynamics**: Simultaneously fit ALMA Band 7 lensing (via PyAutoLens with custom MGE deflection angles + elliptical NFW + external shear, scaled by (1 + eta_PPN)/2) and MUSE V_rms kinematics (via JAM with MGE stellar + MGE-parametrized NFW potential) across a 5-phase pipeline (Ph1: Sersic source; Ph2: Voronoi pixelization hyperparameters; Ph3: lens+JAM refinement I; Ph4: brightness-based Voronoi K-means hyperparameters; Ph5: lens+JAM refinement II with 300-seed bootstrap likelihood cap), then compute Einstein radius and dark matter fraction.  
  > "Unfortunately, `PyAutoLens` does not provide the MGE parametrization as an alternative to light or mass profile. For this reason, we implement the MGE deflection angle in the context of the `PyAutoLens` framework, whilst the light and dark matter mass parametrizations were performed using the `MgeFit` package (cite: Cappellari2002)." (Lens modelling); "To solve the axisymmetric Jeans equations and compute the projected second velocity moment, we use the Jeans Anisotropic Modelling (cite: Cappellari2008; Cappellari2020), in its Python implementation." (Kinematic modelling); "Since the lensing and the kinematical data are independent, we can consider the individual likelihoods of each model separately, such that we can produce a single likelihood for the combined modelling by multiplying the respective likelihoods:" (Combined modelling)
   - software: PyAutoLens, JAM, MgeFit, dynesty, MGETOOLS
   - parameter: 10 free parameters in Ph5 (Upsilon_0, delta, upsilon_0, kappa_s, q_DM, i, beta_z, shear_mag, shear_phi, eta_PPN); fixed NFW scale radius r_s = 10 * R_eff = 11.4''; 300 K-means seed evaluations for Ph5 lens likelihood cap  
  > "Using the MP$_3$ and MP$_4$ results, we compute 300 likelihood evaluations, each of them using a different K-means seed." (Pipeline prescription); "Since we do not have enough data to constrain the dark matter halo on larger scales, we fix $r_s$ as 10 times the effective radius ($R_{\text{eff}} =  1.14\ensuremath{''}$) of the stellar component (cite: Kravtsov2013; Sonnenfeld2015)." (Combined modelling); "To summarise, our fiducial model during the Ph5 has ten free parameters: three related to the stellar mass component ($\Upsilon_0, \delta, \upsilon_0$); two related to the dark matter halo component ($\kappa_s, q_{\text{\tiny{DM}}}$); one inclination ($i$); one parameter describing the anisotropy ($\beta_z$); two describing the effects of a possible external shear in the lens model ($\text{shear}_{\text{mag}}, \text{shear}_{\phi}$); and the gravitational slip parameter ($\eta_{\text{PPN}}$)." (Combined modelling)
   - assumption: Planck 2015 LCDM cosmology (H_0 = 67.7 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_Lambda = 0.6911, Omega_m = 0.3089); dark matter halo centre and orientation aligned with stellar component; constant orbital anisotropy beta_z; no SMBH in fiducial model.  
  > "Throughout the paper, unless otherwise stated, the cosmological parameters assumed are: $H_0 = 67.7$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.6911$, $\Omega_{m} = 0.3089$ (cite: Planck_2015)." (Introduction); "We also assume that the dark matter centre, the luminous mass centre, and the lens light centre coincide." (Combined modelling); "Additionally, we do not include the contribution of a supermassive black hole (SMBH), since the spatial resolution of the IFU data would not allow imposing strong constrains on this parameter." (Combined modelling)
   - fitting: Static nested sampling with dynesty using multiple ellipsoids bounding, random walk proposal (N_walks = 15), 180 live points (100 in Ph2), dlogz = 10 in Ph1-Ph2 and dlogz = 0.8 in Ph3-Ph5; joint likelihood L_Model = L_Lens * L_Dyn; Ph1 prior on eta_PPN is N[1.00, 0.09].  
  > "For all pipeline phases, we use the static version of `dynesty`. We employ a multiple ellipsoids bounding distribution (cite: Mukherjee_2006; Feroz_2019), which can better handle with multi-modal distributions, and a random walk proposal sampling (cite: Metropolis; Hastings1970) with $N_{\text{walks}} = 15$." (Bayesian inference); "Since in the first two phases, we are only interested in estimating a marginal region of the parameter space where the model has physical solutions, we define the termination criteria (cite: Skilling2006) as `dlogz`$= 10$. On the other hand, in the last three phases, the termination criteria are `dlogz`$= 0.8$. Moreover, as the multi-dimensional parameter space has many dimensions, we use a total of 180 live points in each phase, except for Ph2, which runs with 100 live points." (Bayesian inference)
   - validation: Verified that MGE parametrization of elliptical NFW profile introduces only ~0.04% error in the deflection angle.  
  > "We conclude that the error related to this parametrization in the deflection angle is of the order of $0.04\%$." (Combined modelling)
5. **Quantify systematic uncertainties from mass profile, cosmology, and stellar kinematics**: Re-run the 5-phase pipeline for 3 alternative mass models (r_s = 5 R_eff, free r_s, and Wong et al. 2015 composite model with spherical NFW + constant M/L + SMBH) and 5 alternative H_0 values (67.28, 68.20, 74.2, 72.2, 76.2 km s^-1 Mpc^-1), combine with the kinematic template dispersion difference (XSL vs MILES), and add systematic uncertainties in quadrature.  
  > "First, we fix the dark matter radius to be 5 times the effective radius, for which we infer a $\eta_{\text{PPN}} = 1.12^{+0.03}_{-0.02}$ , and a fraction of dark matter within the Einstein ring consistent with $f_{\text{DM}} \sim 35\%$. Next, although our data do not cover the possible extended halo of dark matter, we consider the dark matter scale radius as a free parameter." (Alternative Mass Profiles); "For the models assuming $H_0$ determined by time-delay, we infer $\eta_{\text{PPN}} = (1.11^{+0.04}_{-0.04} \,; 1.13^{+0.02}_{-0.03} \,; 1.14^{+0.04}_{-0.04})$, for $H_0 = (74.2 \,; 72.2 \,; 76.2)$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, respectively." (Cosmology); "We now can combine in quadrature all the systematic uncertainties to estimate our final inference on the slip parameter: $0.017$ due to the effects related to the mass model adopted, $0.043$ related to the uncertainty on the cosmology, and $0.19$ uncertainty from the kinematic fit." (Final inference on \eta_{\text{PPN}})
   - software: PyAutoLens, JAM, dynesty, pPXF
   - parameter: Alternative mass models: r_s = 5 R_eff, r_s free, Wong2015-like; alternative H_0 = 67.28, 68.20, 72.2, 74.2, 76.2 km s^-1 Mpc^-1; stellar libraries: MILES and XSL DR2  
  > "Therefore, we opt to run the fiducial model again, however with different values of the Hubble constant, defined by the lower ($H_0 = 67.28$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$) and upper ($H_0 = 68.20$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$) values of the $1\sigma$ confidence level." (Cosmology); "re-deriving the kinematic map using the Medium resolution INT Library of Empirical Spectra (cite: Miles_models) and the X-Shooter Spectral Library (cite: XSL)." (Kinematics)
   - assumption: Systematic uncertainties from mass model, cosmology, and kinematics are independent and can be summed in quadrature.  
  > "We now can combine in quadrature all the systematic uncertainties to estimate our final inference on the slip parameter: $0.017$ due to the effects related to the mass model adopted, $0.043$ related to the uncertainty on the cosmology, and $0.19$ uncertainty from the kinematic fit." (Final inference on \eta_{\text{PPN}})
   - fitting: Standard deviation across alternative mass models (0.017) and across 6 H_0 runs (0.043), plus propagation of mean 3.4% velocity dispersion template offset into 13.6% (0.19) uncertainty on eta_PPN.  
  > "Taking the standard deviation between the different values of $\eta_{\text{PPN}}$ for these alternative mass models, we can estimate an additional systematic uncertainty of the order of $0.017$ on the slip parameter." (Alternative Mass Profiles); "Considering the standard deviation of the values of $\eta_{\text{PPN}}$ inferred for this six values of $H_0$, we infer an additional uncertainty of the order of $0.043$ on the slip parameter, related to the uncertainty on the Hubble constant." ## Qualitative findings

- No evidence of rotation is seen in the MUSE stellar velocity map of the SDP.81 lens galaxy.  
  > "The central velocity, i.e., the velocity of the central bin, was subtracted from each of the Voronoi bin velocities to obtain the real velocity with respect to the centre of the galaxy. We do not see any evidence of rotation." (MUSE data)
- The SDP.81 lens galaxy is baryon-dominated in its inner regions within the Einstein radius (f_DM ~ 35%).  
  > "We have found that the dark matter fraction, $f_{\text{DM}}$, is of the order of $\sim 35\%$, showing that the galaxy is baryonic dominated in the inner regions." (Main Results)
- Systematic uncertainties related to the stellar kinematics and low spectral SNR at the galaxy outskirts dominate the error budget on eta_PPN.  
  > "The uncertainty introduced by our lack of knowledge about the kinematic properties is of the order of $0.19$, and ally with the low SNR at the outskirts of the lens galaxy is the main source of systematics in our analysis." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 7 provides tens-of-parsec resolution of the lensed submm source without contamination from the foreground lens galaxy light, while HST F160W traces the lens stellar mass distribution and VLT/MUSE provides spatially-resolved stellar kinematics.  
  > "To impose constraints on $\eta_{\text{PPN}}$, combining different datasets that provide complementary information about the same massive object is necessary. The dataset used in this work is composed of high-resolution photometric data from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST), IFS data from the Multi-Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE), and interferometric data from ALMA." (Data); "Due to the wavelength observed by ALMA, the lens light is not visible, and only the deflected source is captured, making this dataset very suitable for lens modelling since the contamination due to the lens galaxy light is negligible." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: Image-plane lens modelling allows masking background sky and fitting thousands of pixels instead of millions of visibilities for computational efficiency; MGE parametrization provides an analytical deprojection and simple Gaussian quadrature for both the lensing deflection angle and axisymmetric JAM kinematics; the 5-phase pipeline with a parametric Phase 1 and Phase 5 likelihood cap avoids unphysical under/over-magnified SLI solutions and Voronoi discretization spikes.  
  > "Furthermore, the intrinsic stellar luminosity density (i.e. the deprojected quantity) has an analytical form for this parametrization. At the same time, the gravitational potential can be evaluated using a single and simple Gaussian quadrature." (Stellar mass profile); "In this work, we opt to perform the modelling in the image plane instead of the visibility plane of ALMA data since it allows us to mask and fit only the regions where the emission of the lensed source is detected. Furthermore, this choice makes the modelling more computationally efficient (we model thousands of pixels instead of millions of visibilities)" (Lens modelling); "To avoid the under/over-magnified solutions, we initialize the modelling considering a parametric source profile, for which these unphysical solutions do not exist." (Pipeline prescription)
- Alternative: Using the lens-subtracted HST image for lens modelling was rejected because better ALMA interferometric data were available.  
  > "After modelling the lens galaxy light profile and subtracting it from the original image, the deflected source is revealed. However, the lens-subtracted image is not used for modelling since better interferometric data are available." (HST data)
- Alternative: Assigning an independent free M/L parameter to each MGE Gaussian component was rejected in favour of a 3-parameter Gaussian-modulated M/L profile to avoid introducing too many free parameters.  
  > "Instead of using a different $M/L$ for each MGE component, which can introduce a lot of free parameters in our model, we opt to use a $M/L$ modulated by a Gaussian function" (Stellar mass profile)
- Alternative: Visibility-plane lens modelling of the ALMA data was rejected in favour of image-plane modelling for computational efficiency and spatial masking.  
  > "In this work, we opt to perform the modelling in the image plane instead of the visibility plane of ALMA data since it allows us to mask and fit only the regions where the emission of the lensed source is detected." (Lens modelling)
- Alternative: Deriving angular diameter distances model-independently from Type Ia supernovae (Pan-STARRS1) was rejected because SN Ia samples only reach z < 2.3, below the source redshift z_s = 3.042.  
  > "However, it is necessary to have supernovae data covering the redshift range of both lens and source galaxies, which is currently limited by $z < 2.3$, considering the Pan-STARRS1 Medium Deep Survey sample (cite: Scolnic_2018), which is far below the redshift of our source galaxy ($z_s = 3.042$)." (Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: Used dynesty stopping criteria dlogz = 10 in Ph1-Ph2 to locate the physical parameter space and dlogz = 0.8 in Ph3-Ph5 with 180 live points (100 in Ph2) and 300 K-means seed evaluations for the Ph5 likelihood cap.  
  > "Using the MP$_3$ and MP$_4$ results, we compute 300 likelihood evaluations, each of them using a different K-means seed." (Pipeline prescription); "Since in the first two phases, we are only interested in estimating a marginal region of the parameter space where the model has physical solutions, we define the termination criteria (cite: Skilling2006) as `dlogz`$= 10$. On the other hand, in the last three phases, the termination criteria are `dlogz`$= 0.8$." (Bayesian inference)
- Limitation (authors): Cleaning visibilities into an image correlates pixels by the synthesized beam, which can bias image-plane modelling if pixel covariance is not accounted for.  
  > "As a consequence of the procedure of obtaining an image from the visibilities, the image pixels become correlated by the beam, which could bias the image-plane modelling if the uncertainties do not take into account the covariance between the pixels." (Lens modelling)
- Limitation (authors): MUSE spectroscopic data have low SNR in the galaxy outskirts, preventing strong constraints on the orbital anisotropy beta_z, the dark matter halo scale radius r_s, or a central SMBH.  
  > "Since we do not have enough data to constrain the dark matter halo on larger scales, we fix $r_s$ as 10 times the effective radius ($R_{\text{eff}} =  1.14\ensuremath{''}$) of the stellar component (cite: Kravtsov2013; Sonnenfeld2015)." (Combined modelling); "Additionally, we do not include the contribution of a supermassive black hole (SMBH), since the spatial resolution of the IFU data would not allow imposing strong constrains on this parameter." (Combined modelling); "Nevertheless, as evidenced by the anisotropy posterior distribution, this parameter is not well constrained by the kinematical data." (Main Results)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming a LCDM cosmology to compute angular diameter distances introduces a circularity when testing General Relativity.  
  > "Although, assuming a cosmology derived under the $\Lambda$CDM can raise discussions about the recursion of testing GR using predictions of itself." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Priors in Phases 3 and 5 are iteratively narrowed around the previous phase median (MP_1 +- 20% or 1 sigma in Ph3; MP_3 +- 10% or 1 sigma in Ph5), and a informative Gaussian prior N(1.00, 0.09) is placed on eta_PPN in Phase 1, which can artificially shrink statistical posteriors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Image-plane lens modelling of cleaned ALMA maps neglects off-diagonal pixel-to-pixel noise covariance introduced by the synthesized beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Text in Section 4.5 says the MGE fit was performed on the HST/F160W image, whereas the Table 1 caption labels the MGE components as coming from the HST/F110W image.
