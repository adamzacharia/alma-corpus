## Analysis recipe

1. **Quantum chemical computations of n- and i-butanal conformers**: Geometry optimization and harmonic vibrational frequency calculations at B2PLYP-D3BJ/aug-cc-pVTZ and CCSD/cc-pVTZ levels, followed by single-point CCSD(T)/CBS energy calculations.  
  > "The geometries were optimized at the density functional theory level, employing the double-hybrid functional B2PLYP (cite: 2006Grimme) combined with Grimme’s D3BJ dispersion (cite: 2011Grimme), in conjunction with the correlation consistent basis set of Dunning aug-cc-pVTZ" (Computational section); "Additional optimizations were also carried out using the ab initio coupled-cluster with single and double excitations (CCSD) method (cite: Raghavachari89). In this case the Dunning’s cc-pVTZ (correlation-consistent polarized valence triple-zeta) basis set was employed." (Computational section); "To obtain more accurate stability order, single point calculations were carried out on the CCSD/cc-pVTZ optimized geometries. Energies are calculated at the CCSD(T) (coupled-cluster with single and double excitations including triple excitations through a perturbative treatment) level extrapolated to the complete basis set limit, denoted as CCSD(T)/CBS." (Computational section)
   - software: Gaussian, CFOUR
   - parameter: n=3 (aug-cc-pVTZ) and n=4 (aug-cc-pVQZ) for CBS extrapolation  
  > "It is computed from the n$^{-3}$ extrapolation equation (cite: Helgaker97) applied to the CCSD(T) energies calculated with the triple- (n=3 aug-cc-pVTZ) and quadruple-zeta (n=4, aug-cc-pVQZ) basis sets." (Computational section)
   - assumption: Harmonic approximation for vibrational frequencies  
  > "On the optimized geometries, harmonic vibrational frequencies were calculated at both levels of theory." (Computational section)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirmed stationary points on the potential energy surface and compared theoretical rotational and centrifugal distortion constants with experimental values.  
  > "This allows the zero-point vibrational energy (ZPE) correction to be estimated, the nature of the stationary points located on the potential energy surface to be confirmed, and the vibrational contribution to the partition function to be computed." (Computational section); "Also, from a comparison between the experimental and theoretically predicted parameters, we can infer that the rotational constants as well as the quartic centrifugal distortion constants are in very good agreement with the CCSD predicted ones." (Millimeter-wave spectra of i-PrCHO)
2. **Laboratory measurement and spectroscopic fitting of millimeter-wave rotational spectra of n- and i-butanal**: Measure room-temperature absorption spectra from 75 to 325 GHz, assign transitions, fit rotational and centrifugal distortion constants using Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian in the I^r-representation, and compute rotational and vibrational partition functions.  
  > "The room-temperature rotational spectra were recorded separately from 75 to 325 GHz using the millimeter-wave absorption spectrometer at the University of Valladolid (for a detailed description of the instrument, see (cite: Daly14; Alonso16))." (Measurement of the rotational spectra); "Pickett’s SPFIT/SPCAT program package (Pickett 1991) was used to perform the corresponding fits using a Watson’s A-reduced Hamiltonian in the I$^{r}$-representation (cite: Watson77)." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO); "We used SPCAT (Pickett 1991) to compute Q$_r$´s values from first principles at the typical temperatures as applied in the JPL database (cite: Pickett98)." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO)
   - software: AABS, XIAM, SPFIT/SPCAT
   - parameter: Gas pressure ~20 ubar, modulation frequency 10.2 kHz, modulation depth 20-35 kHz, 2f demodulation with 30 ms time constant  
  > "In both experiments, we filled our double-pass free-space glass cell with an optimum gas pressure of about 20 $\mu$bar (no external heating was needed for any of the molecules), which was maintained during the whole course of the experiment." (Measurement of the rotational spectra); "Also, the output of the synthesizer was frequency modulated at a frequency of 10.2 kHz, with modulation depths varying between 20 to 35 kHz." (Measurement of the rotational spectra); "After the radiation passed through the cell, the signal was detected using solid-state zero-bias detectors and was sent to a phase-sensitive lock-in amplifier with 2f demodulation (time constant of 30 ms)." (Measurement of the rotational spectra)
   - parameter: J up to 150 for Q_r calculation of gauche i-PrCHO; vibrational modes below 1000 cm^-1 for Q_v  
  > "We estimated the vibrational part, Q$_v$, using a harmonic approximation and Eq. 3.60 of (cite: Gordy70), where only the vibrational modes below 1000 cm $^{-1}$ were taken into consideration." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO); "We computed the values of Q$_r$ from first principles up to J=150 at different temperatures, using the Pickett’s program (cite: Pickett98)." (Millimeter-wave spectra of i-PrCHO)
   - assumption: Experimental uncertainty of unblended symmetric lines is about 30 kHz; harmonic approximation for vibrational partition function  
  > "The experimental uncertainty of the unblended symmetric lines is estimated to be about 30 kHz." (Measurement of the rotational spectra); "We estimated the vibrational part, Q$_v$, using a harmonic approximation and Eq. 3.60 of (cite: Gordy70), where only the vibrational modes below 1000 cm $^{-1}$ were taken into consideration." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO)
   - fitting: Iterative fittings and predictions using Watson's A-reduced Hamiltonian in I^r-representation combining millimeter-wave lines with previous microwave data.  
  > "All in all, we measured more than 2800 rotational transitions for the cis-gauche n-PrCHO along with 1375 transitions for the cis-trans conformer, including in the data set the microwave lines previously observed by Hotopp et al. (2012). Pickett’s SPFIT/SPCAT program package (Pickett 1991) was used to perform the corresponding fits using a Watson’s A-reduced Hamiltonian in the I$^{r}$-representation (cite: Watson77)." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO)
   - validation: Unit-less weighted deviation of the fit close to 1.0 (0.79 to 1.02) and rms deviation < 30 kHz; XIAM predictions confirmed absence of internal rotation splittings.  
  > "No splittings due to internal rotation motion could be observed for either of the conformers, which is in perfect agreement with the computed barrier heights." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO); "Hence, the present experimental data enabled the determination of accurate rotational constants and the full set of quartic and most of the sextic centrifugal distortion constants, showing a remarkable rms deviation of less than 25 kHz for gauche i-PrCHO." (Millimeter-wave spectra of i-PrCHO)

  > "To analyze the spectrum of Sgr B2N1S), we produced synthetic spectra under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) using the software Weeds (cite: Maret11)." (Non-detection of butanal); "Assuming that the more complex aldehydes propanal and butanal trace the same region as acetaldehyde, we produced LTE synthetic spectra for these species adopting the same parameters as acetaldehyde with only the column density left as a free parameter." (Non-detection of butanal)

   - parameter: V_sys = 62 km s^-1, emitting size = 2.0'', T_rot = 250 K and 170 K, linewidth = 5.0 km s^-1, V_off = 0.0 km s^-1  
  > "Each species was modeled with a set of five parameters: size of the emitting region ($\theta_{\rm s}$), column density ($N$), temperature ($T_{\rm rot}$), linewidth ($\Delta V$), and velocity offset ($V_{\rm off}$) with respect to the assumed systemic velocity of the source, $V_{\rm sys}=62$ km s$^{-1}$." (Non-detection of butanal)
   - assumption: LTE assumption justified by high densities; torsional temperature equal to rotational temperature; propanal and butanal trace the same region and physical parameters as acetaldehyde.  
  > "The high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (cite: Bonfand19) justify this assumption." (Non-detection of butanal); "Assuming that the more complex aldehydes propanal and butanal trace the same region as acetaldehyde, we produced LTE synthetic spectra for these species adopting the same parameters as acetaldehyde with only the column density left as a free parameter." (Non-detection of butanal)
   - fitting: Population diagram fit across three torsional states of CH3CHO; LTE synthetic spectrum modeling with column density as free parameter.  
  > "Only a fit taking into account the three torsional states allowed us to derive a temperature, which is found to be high, as reported in Table 5." (Non-detection of butanal)

## Qualitative findings

- Neither n-butanal nor i-butanal is detected toward Sgr B2(N1S) or G+0.693-0.027; propanal is also not detected toward Sgr B2(N1S).  
  > "We report non-detections of n- and i-butanal toward both sources, Sgr B2(N1S) and G+0.693-0.027." (abstract); "While propanal is not detected toward Sgr B2(N1S) either, with an abundance at least 5–11 lower than that of acetaldehyde, propanal is found to be 7 times less abundant than acetaldehyde in G+0.693-0.027." (abstract)
- The family of interstellar aldehydes in the Galactic center region exhibits a drop of approximately one order of magnitude in abundance at each incremental increase in molecular complexity.  
  > "Our astronomical results indicate that the family of interstellar aldehydes in the Galactic center region is characterized by a drop of one order of magnitude in abundance at each incrementation in the level of molecular complexity." (abstract); "Our results show that with each incremental increase in the size of the alkyl group in the aldehyde molecule, there is a drop of approximately one order of magnitude in abundance." (Conclusions)
- Grain-surface chemistry is sufficient to reproduce observed aldehyde ratios in G+0.693-0.027, whereas gas-phase production may play a dominant role in Sgr B2(N1S).  
  > "Grain-surface chemistry appears sufficient to reproduce aldehyde ratios in G+0.693-0.027; gas-phase production may play a more active role in Sgr B2(N1S)." (abstract); "This assumes that gas-phase production of aldehydes is dominant in Sgr B2(N1S), while in G+0.693 aldehyde production on dust grains is sufficient to reproduce observed molecular ratios." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Accurate millimeter-wave laboratory frequencies are mandatory for detecting complex molecules in high-sensitivity spectral surveys such as the ALMA ReMoCA survey of Sgr B2(N) and the IRAM 30m / Yebes 40m survey of G+0.693-0.027; Sgr B2(N1S) was chosen for its lower continuum opacity compared to the main hot core peak.  
  > "Accurate spectroscopic data at high frequencies are usually mandatory for the eventual astronomical detection of many interstellar molecules in several spectral line surveys, such as the EMoCA and ReMoCA surveys conducted with Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) toward Sgr B2(N) (cite: Belloche2017; Belloche2019) and the IRAM 30m survey of the G+0.693-0.027 giant molecular cloud (cite: 2020Rivilla)." (Measurement of the rotational spectra); "This position is offset by about 1$\ensuremath{''}$ to the south of the main hot core Sgr B2(N1) and was chosen by (cite: Belloche19) because of its lower continuum opacity compared to the peak of the hot core." (Observations)
- Why this method: LTE modeling is justified in Sgr B2(N) by the high densities of the hot-core regions, and lower excitation temperatures (170 K in Sgr B2(N1S) and 12 K without vibrational partition function in G+0.693) were adopted to match expected physical and chemical desorption conditions.  
  > "The high densities of the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) (cite: Bonfand19) justify this assumption." (Observations); "Afterward, given that our chemical model suggests that propanal and butanal may trace lower temperatures than acetaldehyde (see Sect. 7), we also computed column density upper limits assuming a temperature of 170 K instead of 250 K." (Non-detection of butanal); "Since the excitation temperatures of the molecules in G+0.693 are low ($T_{\rm ex}$=5$-$20 K), we did not use the vibrational contribution of the partition function (Q$_v$) in this case." (Non-detection of butanal)
- Alternative: Tested both 250 K and 170 K rotational temperatures for propanal and butanal upper limits toward Sgr B2(N1S) because grain-surface desorption in the chemical model peaks at ~170 K rather than 250 K.  
  > "Afterward, given that our chemical model suggests that propanal and butanal may trace lower temperatures than acetaldehyde (see Sect. 7), we also computed column density upper limits assuming a temperature of 170 K instead of 250 K." (Non-detection of butanal); "which is predicated on a uniquely grain-surface origin for propanal, an excitation temperature of 170 K was also tested in the spectral analysis of Sgr B2(N1S)" (Discussion: Interstellar chemistry of acetaldehyde, propanal, and butanal)
- Alternative: Searched for internal rotation splittings using XIAM and Loomis-Wood plots, and for c-type transitions of trans i-butanal, but neither was observed.  
  > "No splittings due to internal rotation motion could be observed for either of the conformers, which is in perfect agreement with the computed barrier heights." (Millimeter-wave spectra of n-PrCHO); "Also, c-type lines were predicted but not observed." (Millimeter-wave spectra of i-PrCHO)

- Limitation (authors): Rotational temperature of acetaldehyde within the torsional ground state alone is not constrained toward Sgr B2(N1S) due to the small energy range covered and residual contamination from unidentified species.  
  > "Due to the small energy range covered by the transitions of the torsional ground state reported in this diagram and the residual contamination from unidentified species, the rotational temperature within the torsional ground state is not constrained." (Non-detection of butanal)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties on column density upper limits toward Sgr B2(N1S) could be as high as a factor of two due to baseline uncertainties and spectral confusion/blending.  
  > "Estimating the uncertainties on these upper limits is not straightforward because of the uncertainties affecting the level of the baseline and because of the spectral confusion that leads to blends with identified or unidentified species. These uncertainties could be as high as a factor of two." (Non-detection of butanal)
- Limitation (authors): Butanal and gas-phase propanal formation pathways are not explicitly included in the astrochemical kinetics network, and the chemical models do not distinguish conformers.  
  > "The larger aldehydes were not included in that network, however." (Discussion: Interstellar chemistry of acetaldehyde, propanal, and butanal); "(we note that the chemical models do not explicitly distinguish conformers)." (Discussion: Interstellar chemistry of acetaldehyde, propanal, and butanal); "Unlike the treatment for acetaldehyde, the chemical model includes only grain-surface mechanisms for the production of propanal" (Discussion: Interstellar chemistry of acetaldehyde, propanal, and butanal)

(Discussion: Interstellar chemistry of acetaldehyde, propanal, and butanal)
