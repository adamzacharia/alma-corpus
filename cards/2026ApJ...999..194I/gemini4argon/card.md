## Qualitative findings

- Sh 2-283-1a SMM1 is a hot molecular core in the outer Galaxy characterized by compact (<0.03 pc), warm (>100 K), and dense (~4 x 10^6 cm^-3) gas with rich molecular line emission including COMs.  
  > "Toward Sh 2-283-1a SMM1, a variety of molecular species (carbon-, oxygen-, nitrogen-, sulfur-, and silicon-bearing species), including COMs, were detected at the emission peak of 0.87 mm continuum. The rotational diagram and continuum analyses revealed the presence of a warm ($T >$100 K), dense ($n \sim$ 4 $\times$ 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$), and compact ($<$0.03 pc) region associated with the protostar ($\sim$6.7 $\times$ 10$^3$ L$_\odot$)." (Summary)
- Molecular abundances normalized by CH3OH in Sh 2-283-1a SMM1 are similar to those in WB89-789 SMM1, showing that chemically rich hot cores exist in different regions of the outer Galaxy.  
  > "A comparison of the molecular abundances relative to CH$_3$OH between the outer Galactic hot cores reveals a similarity, demonstrating that such chemically rich hot cores exist in different regions of the outer Galaxy." (Summary)
- Environmental factors such as dust temperature during the ice-forming stage and cosmic ray intensity govern hot core chemistry rather than simple metallicity scaling.  
  > "The Magellanic hot cores show different trends despite having metallicities similar to those of the outer Galaxy, indicating that the chemical complexity of hot cores is governed by environmental conditions (e.g., cosmic ray intensity and dust temperature) rather than simple metallicity scaling." (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sh 2-283-1a SMM1 was discovered in a Cycle 9 ALMA protostar survey of five outer Galactic star-forming regions as an outflow-driving source exhibiting rich high-excitation molecular emission lines, allowing the sample of outer Galactic hot cores to be expanded beyond a single known object.  
  > "Therefore, increasing the sample size of hot cores in the outer Galaxy is crucial for such comparative studies." (Introduction); "Moreover, one of these outflow-driving sources, Sh 2-283-1a SMM1, showed a chemically rich nature, and a variety of molecular transition lines including high-excitation lines ($E_u >$100 K) of CH$_3$OH and SO$_2$ were detected." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Rotational diagram and LTE analyses are justified because the core gas density (~4 x 10^6 cm^-3) is high and previous work at comparable spatial resolution (~5000 au) showed LTE column densities agree with non-LTE results within a factor of two.  
  > "(cite: Shimonishi2021) demonstrated that column densities derived from LTE analysis are generally consistent with those from non-LTE analysis within a factor of two for the source with spatial resolution ($\sim$5000 au) and gas density comparable to those in this work. Thus, we consider the LTE assumption to be reasonable for this source." (Column densities and gas temperatures)
- Alternative: Using N(CH3OH) directly from the CH3OH rotational diagram was rejected for abundance comparisons because N(CH3OH)/N(13CH3OH) ~ 10 indicates optical depth effects; N(CH3OH) scaled from 13CH3OH was used instead.  
  > "To avoid this underestimation, we estimated $N$(CH$_3$OH) from the $^{13}$CH$_3$OH column density, assuming a $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio of 100 based on the isotope ratio gradients reported by (cite: Milam2005) and (cite: Yan2023) at $D_{\mathrm{GC}}$ = 15.7 kpc." (Column densities and gas temperatures)
- Alternative: Non-ice dust opacity (kappa_nu = 3.54 cm^2 g^-1 at 870 um) was calculated as an alternative for N(H2), yielding 7.4 x 10^22 cm^-2, while thin ice mantle opacity (1.89 cm^2 g^-1) was adopted as representative.  
  > "Assuming non-ice dust, the derived $N_{\rm{H_2}}$ is 7.4 $\times$ 10$^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$ with $\kappa_{\nu}$ = 3.54 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at 870 $\mu$m and $T_\mathrm{d}$ = 150 K. In this paper, we adopt $N_{\rm{H_2}}$ = 1.4$^{+ 1.3}_{-0.3}$ $\times$ 10$^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$ as the representative value of H$_2$ column density." (Column density of molecular hydrogen)

- Limitation (authors): Derived line intensities of species with broad wing components (e.g., SO and HCO+) may be slightly overestimated due to outflow contamination.  
  > "Note that the derived intensities of the lines associated with broad wing components (e.g., SO and HCO$^+$) may be slightly overestimated due to the contamination from the broad components." (Spectra)
