## Analysis recipe

1. **Determine optical/NIR stellar positions and binary separations**: Extract primary star coordinates and parallaxes from Gaia DR2 (verifying reliability via RUWE), and fit stellar profiles (or saturated PSF wings) on pipeline-reduced VLT/NACO 2 um adaptive optics images to measure binary separations and position angles.  
  > "While Gaia is used to obtain precise stellar positions of the primaries, NACO data are essential for determining the offset between the primary and secondary star, and the position angle of the system" (Optical and near-infrared archival data); "We determined the central positions (in detector coordinates) of KK Oph A and KK Oph B on the pipeline-reduced NACO images using stellar profile fitting." (KK Oph system); "Based on the profile fitting to the saturated PSF wings we obtain a separation of 1.46$''$$\pm$0.02$''$." (HD 144668 system)
   - software: not stated
   - parameter: NACO image resolution 75 mas, position precision 0.2 pixels (2.7 mas) for KK Oph  
  > "For the KK Oph system these images have a typical image quality of 75 mas spatial resolution." (KK Oph system); "The precision of the position measurement is 0.2 pixels, which is 2.7 mas at the given pixel scale." (KK Oph system)
   - assumption: Distances of 221 pc for KK Oph and 161.1 pc for HD 144668  
  > "We obtain a separation of 1.6088$''$$\pm$0.0075$''$, i.e., 356$\pm$2 au projected separation at the adopted distance of 221 pc." (KK Oph system); "Assuming the distance of 161.1 au the projected separation is 235$\pm$3 au." (HD 144668 system)
   - fitting: Stellar profile fitting and saturated PSF wing fitting on NACO images  
  > "We determined the central positions (in detector coordinates) of KK Oph A and KK Oph B on the pipeline-reduced NACO images using stellar profile fitting." (KK Oph system); "Based on the profile fitting to the saturated PSF wings we obtain a separation of 1.46$''$$\pm$0.02$''$." (HD 144668 system)
   - validation: Verified Gaia DR2 astrometric reliability by computing the renormalised unit weight error (RUWE); compared ALMA mm peak positions and separations against Gaia and NACO positions.  
  > "HD 144668 A has a reliable astrometric solution in Gaia Data Release 2 (Lindegren et al. 2018), as we verified by computing the renormalised unit weight error (Gaia technical note Gaia-C3-TN-LU-LL-124-01)." (HD 144668 system); "From the Gaia and NACO data we obtain a binary separation measurement of 1.6088$''$$\pm$0.0075$''$. This measurement is consistent with the 1.62$''$ separation between the millimetre peaks." (Geometry of the two binary systems)
2. **Fit 2D Gaussians to continuum sources and subtract point-source models to identify outer disc asymmetries**: Fit 2D Gaussians to isolated primary and secondary continuum emission in Band 6 and Band 7 images using CASA imfit, place point sources of the fitted flux and position in blank images, convolve with the synthesized beam, and subtract from the ALMA images.  
  > "We first use CASA task *imfit* to find the best-fit 2D Gaussian distribution in the region which isolates the emission of a disc from the emission of the disc of its companion." (Resolved asymmetries in the outer regions of the discs); "We then proceed by placing point sources of appropriate flux at the best-fit locations of the Gaussians (also listed in Table [\[tab1\]](#tab1)) in blank images and convolve these images with the appropriate synthesized beam sizes. This is then subtracted from the ALMA images." (Resolved asymmetries in the outer regions of the discs)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: Synthesized beams: 0.53'' x 0.52'' (HD 144668 B6), 0.31'' x 0.28'' (HD 144668 B7), 0.58'' x 0.51'' (KK Oph B6)  
  > "| HD 144668 | 16:08:34.274 | $-$39.06.18.68 | 1.2 | 26.25 | 25.61$\pm$0.18 | 0.532 x 0.525 | 0.53 x 0.52 |" (Table 1); "| HD 144668$^{*}$ | 16:08:34.275 | $-$39:06:18.68 | 0.87 | 56.0$\pm$1.1 | 54.02$\pm$0.53 | 0.32 x 0.28 | 0.31 x 0.28 |" (Table 1); "| KK Oph | 17:10:08.124 | $-$27.15.19.12 | 1.2 | 28.90 | 27.03$\pm$0.16 | 0.606 x 0.598 | 0.58 x 0.51 |" (Table 1)
   - assumption: Unresolved inner disc emission can be modelled as a point source convolved with the synthesized beam  
  > "To establish the significance of the emission in these extended regions we subtract the emission expected from a point source, convolved with the synthesized beam." (Resolved asymmetries in the outer regions of the discs)

## Qualitative findings

- Both circum-primary and circum-secondary discs in KK Oph and HD 144668 are detected in mm continuum, with primary discs having about three times the dust mass of secondary discs, and no firm detection of a common circumbinary envelope.  
  > "In both systems the circum-primary and the circum-secondary discs are detected in the millimetre continuum emission, but not in $^{13}$CO nor C$^{18}$O lines." (Abstract); "The primaries have about three times more dust in their discs than the secondaries." (Abstract); "There is no detected emission that would firmly indicate a common envelope or a circum-binary structure in either of the systems." (ALMA observations)
- Point-source subtraction reveals 4-sigma extended asymmetries in the outer regions of both primary discs pointing away from the companion direction, consistent with dynamical perturbation in fly-by scenarios.  
  > "Subtraction of beam-convolved point source emission for the KK Oph system also reveals excess emission up to 4 $\sigma$ level in the North-East direction of the primary (see Figure [1](#fig1))." (Resolved asymmetries in the outer regions of the discs); "In our Band 6 data for HD 144668 this procedure reveals a significant extent of emission stretching from the North to the West of the primary star reaching 4$\sigma$ levels (see Fig. [1](#fig1))." (Resolved asymmetries in the outer regions of the discs); "In both cases the extended emission is located away from the projected direction of the binary companion, which is consistent with hydrodynamical models of dynamical perturbation of protoplanetary discs in fly-by scenarios" (Asymmetry of the primary disc)
- Neither 13CO nor C18O J=2-1 emission is detected in any of the discs, implying gas masses below 0.1 M_Jup and indicating that gas giant planet formation can no longer occur in these systems.  
  > "Upper limits on the gas masses imply less than 0.1 M$_{\textrm{jup}}$ in any of these discs, meaning that giant planets can no longer form in them." (Abstract); "Our gas mass limits are all below 0.1 M$_{jup}$ **(Tab. [1](#tab3),** showing that giant planet formation is no longer possible in the two observed binary systems." (Maximum gas mass from 13CO upper limits)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Millimetre observations spatially resolve the intermediate-separation binaries (~1.5''-1.6'') to measure individual dust and gas disc masses and probe large-scale structures, while Gaia and NACO archival data provide precise stellar astrometry and binary separations.  
  > "A crucial insight into planet formation from discs around intermediate-separation binaries (a few hundred au) can be provided by observations in the millimetre wavelength regime, which can yield information on the disc masses and reveal large scale structures." (Introduction); "While Gaia is used to obtain precise stellar positions of the primaries, NACO data are essential for determining the offset between the primary and secondary star, and the position angle of the system" (Optical and near-infrared archival data)
- Why this method: Interactive clean with masks was used due to the complex brightness distribution; differential spectral index calculation was used to cancel the 10-15% absolute flux calibration uncertainty between bands; Monte Carlo orbital sampling was used because the 3D binary orbital parameters are unknown.  
  > "Due to the complex structure of the brightness distribution, image reconstruction was performed interactively, using the *clean* task in CASA Software Package." (ALMA observations); "In this situation, a way to remove the calibration uncertainty is to use the ratio of flux of the primary to the flux of the secondary in each image to draw comparisons between the two discs, rather than measuring their individual $\alpha$ and $\beta$ values." (Differential measurement of \beta between the circum-primary and circum-secondary discs); "Since the orbital parameters in the above equations are unknown we follow (cite: Torres1999SubstellarApproach) and (cite: Harris2012ASystems) and sample them using a Monte Carlo approach." (Tidal truncation of the primary disc)
- Alternative: Re-imaging HD 144668 with tclean instead of clean (tested, resulted only in a 10 mas smaller beam).  
  > "As a test, we re-imaged HD 144668 using tclean. A marginally smaller synthesized beam (by 10 mas) was the only difference." (ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Phase self-calibration on HD 144668 with a 60 s solution interval (tested, yielded no improvement in S/N of ~165).  
  > "The two 1.875 GHz spectral windows had enough S/N for phase self-calibration with a 60 s solution interval, i.e half the total time on target. However, there was no improvement in S/N ($\approx$165 both before and after)." (ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Foreground cloud absorption causing the 13CO non-detections (rejected because line-of-sight extinction E(B-V) implies a maximum 13CO J=2-1 optical depth of tau = 0.35 for HD 144668 and an order of magnitude lower for KK Oph).  
  > "The optical depth we obtain for the $^{13}$CO $J=$2–1 line for this column, and assuming local $H_2$ density of 10$^2$cm$^{-2}$ and a temperature of 20 K, is $\tau=$0.35 for HD 144668 and an order of magnitude lower $\tau$ ." (Upper limits on gas mass); "Considering that we used the maximum E(B-V), this is the maximum optical depth expected so we can safely assume that no line of sight absorption is affecting the emission from either HD 144668 or KK Oph." (Upper limits on gas mass)
- Alternative: Optically thick 0.87 mm emission (tau = 1, 37% escaping) for the HD 144668 primary disc when computing differential beta (explored as an alternative, which increases beta^A - beta^B to 3.7).  
  > "If we assume the disc around the primary is optically thick at 0.87 mm - for example if only 37$\%$ of the emission escapes the disc (i.e., $\tau_{0.87mm}=$1), we adjust the Eq. [\[eq3\]](#eq3) such that" (Differential measurement of \beta between the circum-primary and circum-secondary discs); "then $\beta^A-\beta^B$ difference becomes as large as 3.7, allowing for even more evolved dust grains in the disc around the secondary star in comparison to the disc around the primary." (Differential measurement of \beta between the circum-primary and circum-secondary discs)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The discs are only marginally resolved, preventing a direct measurement of disc inclination from the mm images.  
  > "Even though the disc structure is only marginally resolved, we find indications of large-scale asymmetries in the outer regions of the primary discs, most likely due to perturbation by the companion." (Abstract); "Our mm-imaging does not allow us to derive the disc inclination." (Geometry of the two binary systems)
- Limitation (authors): A low-density common envelope or circumbinary structure bridging the stars below the 2-sigma detection threshold cannot be ruled out without higher-sensitivity observations.  
  > "In fact, low-level emission at <2$\sigma$ connects the primaries with the secondaries but higher sensitivity observations would be needed to ascertain whether this is due to beam smearing or an underlying low-density common structure." (ALMA observations)
- Limitation (authors): The distance to KK Oph has large uncertainties due to problematic stellar photometry and an unreliable Gaia DR2 astrometric warning label, and Gaia astrometry for both secondary stars is unreliable.  
  > "Hereby we adopt the distance of 221 pc, with a note that this star has problematic stellar photometry and hence this distance estimate carries large uncertainties." (KK Oph system); "Regarding the companion, HD 144668B, the renormalised unit weight error tells us that its Gaia astrometric solution is not reliable." (HD 144668 system)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass estimates suffer from large opacity uncertainties (up to 50% or factors of 3-4.5 depending on maximum grain size) and do not probe solids larger than ~1 cm.  
  > "We report the corresponding minimum dust mass estimates in Tab. 3, with a note that these values may be up to 50$\%$ higher due to uncertainties in the opacity." (Dust mass); "For the plausible upper limits of 1mm and 1cm we find opacity variations of a factor 3 and 4.5 for the primary and secondary respectively." (Differential measurement of \beta between the circum-primary and circum-secondary discs)
- Limitation (authors): The negative opacity spectral index for HD 144668B (beta = -0.29) is incompatible with the Rayleigh-Jeans assumption, and CO freeze-out beyond the snowline could cause the gas mass upper limits to be underestimated by a factor of 3-4.  
  > "For the the secondary (HD 144668B) with our assumption of the disc being the same temperature (20 K) we find $\alpha=1.71$, $\beta=-0.29$ and $p=2.8$, which is intriguing because a) it is very different to the spectral indices of the primary and b) because it would be incompatible with being in the Rayleigh-Jeans regime." (Comparing millimetre spectral indices of the HD 144668 circum-primary and circum-secondary discs); "The assumption of standard CO abundance and isotopic ratio may be underestimating the derived masses by a factor of at most 3-4, in case CO may largely be frozen out." (Upper limits on gas mass)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inconsistency in the stated dust opacity value used to calculate Table 2 dust masses: Section 4.2.1 states kappa_nu = 1.15 cm^2/g is adopted, whereas Section 4.2.3 states an opacity of 2 cm^2/g was chosen for the calculation in Section 4.2.1 (and wavelength is listed as 1.3 mm in text vs. 1.2 mm in Table 1 and Section 4.2.1).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Typo in the ALMA project code in the Acknowledgements, Footnote 2, and Data Availability sections (printed as 2013.0.01600.S instead of 2015.1.01600.S as in Section 3.1).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The Band 6 (0.53'' x 0.52'') and Band 7 (0.31'' x 0.28'') continuum fluxes for HD 144668 were compared directly in the image plane without matching uv-coverage or convolving to a common beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Carbon depletion / volatile C/H reduction (common in Lupus discs as shown by Miotello et al. 2016) beyond simple freeze-out is not accounted for in the 13CO gas mass upper limits.
