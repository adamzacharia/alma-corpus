## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: How long do young star clusters remain associated with their natal giant molecular clouds (GMCs), and how does the hierarchical spatial structure of star clusters and GMCs evolve over time across different galactic environments?  
  > "Here we study the connection between young star clusters and cold gas across a range of extragalactic environments by combining the high resolution (1$''$) PHANGS–ALMA catalogue of GMCs with the star cluster catalogues from PHANGS–HST." (Abstract); "This powerful analysis afforded by the high resolution data allows us to study how the stars and gas are organized in multi-scale structures, how those structures evolve in time, and measure some of the timescales of star formation—how long do star clusters stay associated with their natal gas clouds?" ## Analysis recipe

1. **Filter catalogues to HST and ALMA footprint overlap and perform nearest-neighbour separation analysis**: Identify the overlap region between HST and ALMA footprints, filter star clusters (Classes 1, 2, 3) and GMCs to this region, compute 1st, 2nd, and 3rd nearest-neighbour separations between star clusters and GMC centres (ignoring GMC sizes), split into age bins (<=10 Myr and >10 Myr), and calculate median separations and S/N via 1000 bootstrap samples.  
  > "In order to accurately match the star clusters with their nearest neighbour and/or natal GMC, we first find the region of overlap between the HST and ALMA footprints and only consider clusters and clouds which lie within this overlap region." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs); "Then, as a first step in correlating the star clusters and GMCs, we perform a nearest neighbour analysis to find the separations between star clusters and the nearest GMC centres." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs); "where $\sigma_{m old}$ and $\sigma_{m young}$ represent the standard deviations in the median values for 1000 bootstrapped samples for the older and younger age bins, respectively." (4.1 Nearest Neighbour Analysis)
   - parameter: Age bin threshold: 10 Myr (also tested 5 Myr)  
  > "We then split the clusters into two age bins—$10$ Myr and younger and older than $10$ Myr." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs); "No significant differences are found if the age threshold is lowered to 5 Myr." (Footnote 4)
   - parameter: 1000 bootstrapped samples for median separation standard deviation  
  > "where $\sigma_{m old}$ and $\sigma_{m young}$ represent the standard deviations in the median values for 1000 bootstrapped samples for the older and younger age bins, respectively." (4.1 Nearest Neighbour Analysis)
   - assumption: GMC sizes are ignored in the nearest neighbour point-to-point separation analysis  
  > "In this analysis, the sizes of the GMCs are not taken into account." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked second and third nearest neighbour separations and compared median separations to theoretical estimates from Kruijssen et al. (2019) and Chevance et al. (2020).  
  > "We also test the second and third nearest neighbour separations (not shown) and find the same trend." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs); "Finally, we include in certain sub-panels of Figure 6 theoretical estimates for the expected nearest neighbour separations $r_n$." (4.1 Nearest Neighbour Analysis)
2. **Associate star clusters with GMCs using GMC radii and derive cloud dispersal timescales**: Classify star clusters by line-of-sight overlap with the closest GMC into radial bins (< 1 R_GMC, 1-2 R_GMC, 2-3 R_GMC, or > 3 R_GMC unassociated) while applying a 10 km/s drift velocity cutoff (v * t(age) < 3 R_GMC). Compute median cluster ages and bootstrap uncertainties for each radial bin across individual galaxies, the ensemble, and PHANGS environmental masks (centre, bar, interarm, spiral arm, disk) as well as spiral arm pitch angle bins.  
  > "Consistent with (cite: grasha18) and (cite: grasha19), in this analysis the radius of a GMC is considered which allows for a cluster to be classified as either: within the radius of the closest GMC; within 1 to 2 radii of the closest GMC; within 2 to 3 radii of the closest GMC; or beyond which we consider to be “unassociated.”" (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs); "We calculate the uncertainty on the medians via bootstrapping the cluster ages included in the PHANGS–HST catalog." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs); "We have incorporated a $10$ km s$^{-1}$ velocity cutoff in our analysis to account for clusters that may have drifted during their lifetime into a chance line-of-sight alignment with a non-natal GMC; we require all cluster-GMC associations to satisfy $v 	imes t({m age}) < 3 R_{m GMC}$." (Footnote 3)
   - parameter: Radial association bins: <1 R_GMC, 1-2 R_GMC, 2-3 R_GMC, >3 R_GMC (unassociated)  
  > "Consistent with (cite: grasha18) and (cite: grasha19), in this analysis the radius of a GMC is considered which allows for a cluster to be classified as either: within the radius of the closest GMC; within 1 to 2 radii of the closest GMC; within 2 to 3 radii of the closest GMC; or beyond which we consider to be “unassociated.”" (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs)
   - parameter: Velocity cutoff: v = 10 km s^-1 such that v * t(age) < 3 R_GMC  
  > "We have incorporated a $10$ km s$^{-1}$ velocity cutoff in our analysis to account for clusters that may have drifted during their lifetime into a chance line-of-sight alignment with a non-natal GMC; we require all cluster-GMC associations to satisfy $v 	imes t({m age}) < 3 R_{m GMC}$." (Footnote 3)
   - parameter: Spiral arm pitch angle split thresholds: 20 degrees and 30 degrees  
  > "Spiral arms are split by pitch angle at 20 degrees." (4.3.1 Dependence on Spiral Arm Structure); "A division at 30 degrees was also checked and showed no dependence on the pitch angle dividing threshold." (4.3.1 Dependence on Spiral Arm Structure)
   - assumption: A star cluster can only be associated with a single GMC; if aligned with multiple GMCs, the most massive GMC is chosen  
  > "We only allow a star cluster to be associated with a single GMC; individual GMCs can still have multiple star clusters associated them. In cases where a star cluster is aligned with multiple GMCs, we opt for the most massive GMC to be the associated one." (3.1 Associating Star Clusters with GMCs)
   - assumption: Assumed galactic disk scale height of 100 pc for oblate spheroidal GMC radius modeling in PHANGS-ALMA  
  > "Therefore, the clouds are modeled to be oblate spheroidal if the spherical radius exceeds the assumed scale height ($100$ pc for the PHANGS–ALMA sample)." (Data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived dispersal timescale (~4-6 Myr) with independent measurements from M51 (Grasha et al. 2019) and the uncertainty principle for star formation (Chevance et al. 2020; Kim et al. 2021).  
  > "This result is consistent with the measurement from (cite: grasha19) in M51 using the LEGUS star clusters and CO(1–0) maps from PAWS." (4.2 Star Clusters Associated with GMCs); "accounting for uncertainties and intrinsic variation between galaxies, our inferred ensemble-wide timescale for clusters is consistent with the sum of the embedded and unembedded timescales found by (cite: chevance20), (cite: kim21), and (cite: kim22)." (4.2 Star Clusters Associated with GMCs)
3. **Measure angular two-point autocorrelation functions and fit power-laws**: Generate random catalogues within the HST-ALMA overlap footprint with similar sample size as real catalogues. Compute 2D angular two-point autocorrelation functions using the Landy & Szalay (1993) estimator via astroML's bootstrap_two_point_angular for all clusters, clusters <= 10 Myr, clusters > 10 Myr, and GMCs. Drop the largest-scale angular bins to avoid edge effects, convert angular scale to parsecs, and fit power-laws using Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least squares minimization.  
  > "In order to minimize errors in the generation of the random catalog, we only place the random objects within the region of the HST and ALMA footprint overlap and ensure the random catalogue has a similar sample size as the real catalogs." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions); "To measure the autocorrelation functions, we use the ASTROML function `bootstrap_two_point_angular` on both the star cluster and GMC catalogs." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions); "We then fit power-laws to the autocorrelation functions using a Levenberg–Marquardt non-linear least squares minimization because in a fully hierarchical (or fractal) distribution a smooth power-law decline is expected and indicates a scale-free distribution (cite: calzetti89)." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
   - software: astroML
   - parameter: Number of angular bins: tested 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25; 15 or 20 bins optimal  
  > "We test the effects of angular bin sizes by running the analysis with 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 angular bins and find the same trends for each galaxy; see Figure 4 for NGC 1566 for a typical example." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions); "Utilizing 15 or 20 bins appears to provide the optimal spatial resolution for our galaxy sample." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
   - parameter: Reference spatial scale for amplitude evaluation: 300 pc  
  > "Table 4. Two-Point Autocorrelation Functions: Best-Fitting Power Law Slopes and Amplitudes at 300 pc" (Table 4)
   - assumption: Positions are not deprojected for disk inclination because moderate to low inclinations (<= 60 deg) have minimal impact  
  > "Our sample of galaxies has moderate to low disk inclinations ($\lesssim60$$^\circ$), and thus correcting for disk inclination has minimal impact and does not change our conclusions." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
   - fitting: Power-law fit using Levenberg–Marquardt non-linear least squares minimization  
  > "We then fit power-laws to the autocorrelation functions using a Levenberg–Marquardt non-linear least squares minimization because in a fully hierarchical (or fractal) distribution a smooth power-law decline is expected and indicates a scale-free distribution (cite: calzetti89)." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to number of angular bins (5, 10, 15, 20, 25) and age cut (5 Myr vs 10 Myr).  
  > "We test the effects of angular bin sizes by running the analysis with 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 angular bins and find the same trends for each galaxy; see Figure 4 for NGC 1566 for a typical example." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions); "No significant differences are found if the age threshold is lowered to 5 Myr." (Footnote 4)
4. **Measure angular cross-correlation functions between star clusters and GMCs**: Generate random catalogues for both star clusters and GMCs within the overlap region and compute the two-point angular cross-correlation function using the Landy & Szalay (1993) cross-correlation estimator for all clusters, clusters <= 10 Myr, and clusters > 10 Myr with GMCs, dropping the largest angular bins.  
  > "We generate random catalogs for both the cluster and the cloud populations, and measure the total sample sizes within each catalog:" (3.3 Angular cross-correlation Functions); "Similar to the autocorrelation analysis, we estimate the cross-correlation functions of all the star clusters with the GMCs, clusters ${\leq}10$ Myr with the GMCs, and clusters ${>}10$ Myr with the GMCs. The largest angular bins are dropped in order to minimize edge effects." (3.3 Angular cross-correlation Functions)
   - parameter: Number of angular bins: 10 bins chosen (tested 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25)  
  > "We also test the cross-correlation estimations using $5, 10, 15, 20, 	ext{and\ } 25$ angular bins." (3.3 Angular cross-correlation Functions); "In contrast to the 20 bins used for the autocorrelation functions, 10 bins was chosen here because there is less data (i.e., fewer star cluster-GMC pairs compared to cluster-cluster pairs)." (3.3 Angular cross-correlation Functions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 angular bins and verified that general trends hold regardless of bin count.  
  > "We also test the cross-correlation estimations using $5, 10, 15, 20, 	ext{and\ } 25$ angular bins. As with the autocorrelation functions, we find the same general trends for each galaxy regardless of the number of angular bins." (3.3 Angular cross-correlation Functions)

## Qualitative findings

- Young star clusters (<=10 Myr) lie closer to their nearest neighbour GMCs than older star clusters (>10 Myr) in 10 out of 11 galaxies (all except the flocculent galaxy NGC 4571).  
  > "In all galaxies except NGC 4571, the young clusters are found to be closer to their nearest neighbour GMCs than the older populations." (4.1 Nearest Neighbour Analysis)
- Star clusters within 1 GMC radius have a median age of ~1 Myr, and by 2 to 3 GMC radii the median age reaches 4-6 Myr across all galaxies and galactic environments, indicating clusters disassociate from their natal GMCs after ~4-6 Myr.  
  > "For clusters between 2 and 3 GMC radii, the median ages are no greater than 6 Myr across all galaxies (the range is $4-6$ Myr, with uncertainties for a given galaxy on the order of 0.5 Myr" (4.2 Star Clusters Associated with GMCs); "Across all five environments, the median cluster age is 5 Myr for clusters between 2 and 3 GMC radii." (4.3 Dependence on Galactic Environment)
- There is no dependence of star cluster age or star cluster-GMC separation on spiral arm pitch angle.  
  > "Given these results, we find no dependence of star cluster age and star cluster–GMC separation on the spiral arm pitch angle." (4.3.1 Dependence on Spiral Arm Structure)
- Young star clusters (<=10 Myr) exhibit steeper autocorrelation power-law slopes and higher clustering amplitudes on small scales than older (>10 Myr) clusters in all galaxies except NGC 4571, showing that the inherited fractal structure dissolves over time.  
  > "Young (${\leq}10$ Myr) star clusters are more strongly autocorrelated on kpc and smaller spatial scales than the $>$$10$ Myr stellar populations, indicating that the hierarchical structure dissolves over time." (Abstract); "Except for the flocculent galaxy NGC 4571, each galaxy shows a steeper power-law slope for the younger clusters than for the older clusters." (4.4 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
- GMCs are much less spatially autocorrelated than young star clusters and appear closer to randomly/uniformly distributed across spatial scales, with a downturn at scales below twice the typical GMC radius.  
  > "However, in most cases, the distribution of the GMCs is found to be much less correlated; the GMCs seem to be closer to randomly distributed." (4.4 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions); "The value of $2 R_{\mathrm{GMC}}$ is presented in each panel of Figure 10, where we would expect to start noticing the impact of the cloud identification on the autocorrelation function (and thus explains the downturn in the GMC autocorrelation function at small scales in most galaxies)." (4.4 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining ~1'' (~100 pc) PHANGS-ALMA CO(2-1) GMC catalogues with ~0.05'' PHANGS-HST star cluster catalogues across 11 nearby galaxies with completed catalogues enables resolving individual GMCs and compact star clusters across diverse galactic environments.  
  > "Combining these ${\sim}1\ensuremath{''}$ CO maps with high-resolution (${\sim}0.05\ensuremath{''}$) Hubble Space Telescope (HST) observations of young star clusters like from LEGUS (cite: adamo17) and PHANGS–HST (cite: lee22) provides an unprecedented look at the connection between individual clouds and the products of star formation." (Introduction); "As the development of the PHANGS–HST cluster catalogue and PHANGS–ALMA GMC catalogue pipelines are still on-going, we focus on 11 PHANGS–HST galaxies which currently have completed cluster and GMC catalogs." (Data)
- Why this method: Nearest-neighbour and radial overlap analyses directly connect clusters to natal clouds to yield an independent measurement of the cloud dispersal timescale, while two-point autocorrelation and cross-correlation functions (with power-law fits) quantify the scale-free fractal clustering and its dissolution over time.  
  > "By connecting the star clusters with their natal gas clouds, we are able to provide an independent measurement of the cloud dispersal timescale which can be compared to the results from (cite: chevance20; chevance20c)." (Introduction); "We then fit power-laws to the autocorrelation functions using a Levenberg–Marquardt non-linear least squares minimization because in a fully hierarchical (or fractal) distribution a smooth power-law decline is expected and indicates a scale-free distribution (cite: calzetti89)." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
- Alternative: Deprojecting galaxy disk inclinations for the 2D autocorrelation functions was considered unnecessary and omitted because the sample has moderate to low inclinations (<=60 deg) where deprojection has minimal impact.  
  > "Our sample of galaxies has moderate to low disk inclinations ($\lesssim60$$^\circ$), and thus correcting for disk inclination has minimal impact and does not change our conclusions." (3.2 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
- Alternative: More detailed environmental mask subdivisions (e.g., separating bars and bar ends) were rejected in favour of simplified masks (centre, bar, interarm, spiral arms, disk).  
  > "The masks do provide further differentiation of environments for example, bars and bar ends, but we opt for the more simplified masks." (4.3 Dependence on Galactic Environment)
- Alternative: Applying the spiral arm pitch angle analysis to star clusters outside of spiral arms was rejected because spiral arm pitch angles vary significantly within individual galaxies.  
  > "We limit this analysis to only clusters found within spiral arms as the pitch angle of spiral arms within individual galaxies can range significantly." (4.3.1 Dependence on Spiral Arm Structure)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Native resolution GMC catalogues have heterogeneous completeness and sensitivity limits across the galaxy sample.  
  > "For this study, we use the ‘native resolution’ GMC catalogs which have been constructed with the best available resolution and noise for each galaxy. This leads to heterogeneous limits across the sample (cite: rosolowsky21)." (Data)
- Limitation (authors): Class 3 compact associations may contain multiple stellar populations of different ages, causing large uncertainties in their age measurements.  
  > "The Class 3 compact associations, which are typically young, prove to be difficult to model given their potential for containing multiple ages within a single association, which causes large uncertainties in their age measurements." (Data)
- Limitation (authors): The 10 km/s velocity cutoff (v * t < 3 R_GMC) is a coarse approximation that influences the median age of unassociated clusters, and GMC radii and peak spacings are strongly tied to the CO data spatial resolution and crowding.  
  > "However, we must acknowledge that this trend is influenced by our implementation of a velocity cutoff." (4.2 Star Clusters Associated with GMCs); "We acknowledge, though, that this a coarse approximation and is limited by the fact that GMC radii are highly related to the resolution of the CO data, and the spatial resolution of the CO data affects the minimum peak spacing." (Footnote 3)
- Limitation (authors): SED fitting of PHANGS-HST clusters assumes solar metallicity for all sources, underestimating the ages of old globular clusters in galactic bulges (returning ~few 100 Myr instead of ~10 Gyr).  
  > "(cite: turner21) note that there are likely globular clusters in the centre of NGC 3351 but the SED fitting performed on the PHANGS–HST clusters does not recover ages as old as expected (${\sim}10$ Gyr) for these globular clusters, due to the limiting assumption of solar metallicity for all sources." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): UV-optical HST observations are insensitive to star clusters less massive than ~10^3 - 10^4 M_sun and to heavily embedded clusters.  
  > "or (2) the ultraviolet-optical HST data are insensitive to clusters less massive than ${\sim}10^3 {-} 10^4~M_\odot$ (see §2) or to heavily buried stellar clusters (a follow-up project with the James Webb Space Telescope has been approved for 19 PHANGS targets, to uncover and characterize such embedded systems)." (4.4 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
- Limitation (authors): CPROPS blends overlapping GMCs with similar velocities into single clouds, artificially suppressing the GMC autocorrelation function at spatial separations below 2 R_GMC.  
  > "This peak in the correlation most likely results from CPROPS combining overlapping GMCs into a single GMC (if they have similar velocities), which prevents us from finding a significant number of GMCs with separations less than twice the typical GMC radius." (4.4 Angular Two-Point Correlation Functions)
- Limitation (authors): Cross-correlation functions suffer from poor pair statistics in some galaxies, leading to empty separation bins ('gaps').  
  > "In some of the cross-correlation functions, there is a “gap” resembling a piece-wise function. This is because there are no star cluster–GMC pairs found with separations that fall in that bin." (4.5 Cross-correlation Functions); "However, the cross-correlation functions are difficult to interpret given poor statistics in a few of the galaxies." (Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The velocity cutoff condition v * t(age) < 3 R_GMC directly couples the maximum allowed age of an 'associated' cluster to R_GMC; for a typical R_GMC = 60 pc and v = 10 km/s, 3 R_GMC / v is ~18 Myr, which artificially truncates the upper tail of the age distribution of associated clusters and biases the median ages.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using native-resolution GMC catalogues means physical resolution varies by a factor of ~2 across the galaxy sample (distances from 9.8 to 19.6 Mpc), directly affecting CPROPS cloud radii and therefore the physical scale of the 1, 2, and 3 R_GMC association bins.
