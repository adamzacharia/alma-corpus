## Analysis recipe

1. **Select the target sample of Upper Scorpius disks**: Filter the 37 Upper Scorpius disks from (cite: Carpenter2025) and (cite: Zallio_2026) based on Gaia parallax precision, well-constrained rotational profiles, multiplicity, constraint on CO emitting height, consistency of dynamical mass, and availability of VLT/X-Shooter stellar properties.  
  > "The targets were selected based on the availability of accurate dynamical masses, reliable stellar parameters, and precise *Gaia* parallaxes ($\varpi/\sigma(\varpi)>10$)." (Sample); "We excluded from the sample four disks with poorly constrained rotational profiles, and two multiple systems." (Sample); "The final sample is composed of 20 sources from the sample above that have well-determined stellar properties, which were derived from broad-band flux-calibrated medium-resolution spectra (Empey et al., in prep.) derived from VLT/X-Shooter and analyzed with `FRAPPE`" (Sample)
   - software: FRAPPE
   - parameter: Gaia parallax precision varpi/sigma(varpi) > 10; final sample of 20 sources  
  > "The targets were selected based on the availability of accurate dynamical masses, reliable stellar parameters, and precise *Gaia* parallaxes ($\varpi/\sigma(\varpi)>10$)." (Sample); "The final sample is composed of 20 sources from the sample above that have well-determined stellar properties" (Sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Estimate and assign realistic uncertainties to dynamical stellar masses**: Generate a grid of mcfost radiative transfer models of 12CO J=3-2, transform them into synthetic ALMA visibilities using csalt, fit them with csalt parametric prescription A and emcee, and assign the mean statistical uncertainty per stellar mass and disk size bin (Table 1) to the observed dynamical masses from (cite: Zallio_2026).  
  > "After creating `mcfost` ((cite: Pinte_2006; Pinte_2009)) radiative transfer models of $^{12}$CO $J=3-2$, we use built-in routines of `csalt` to transform them into radio-interferometric observed data, using the same total integration time ($\sim180$ s per disk)" (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size); "We then perform fits using `csalt` with the parametric prescription A (see (cite: Zallio_2026); this is a complete disk prescription, which also considers for the emitting height of the optically thick $^{12}$CO layer) on the synthetic observation we created." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size); "We evaluate the mean statistical uncertainty for each stellar bin and for each disk size (reported in the third column of Table 1) of the uncertainties returned from the `csalt` fits." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
   - software: mcfost, csalt, emcee
   - parameter: M_star = 1, 0.5, 0.05 M_sun; inclination = 8, 30, 55 deg; R_c = 5, 20, 50 au; integration time ~180 s per disk  
  > "Our grid of models tests the accuracy of mass inferences for stars with masses of $1$, $0.5$, and $0.05$ $M_{\odot}$ to explore stellar mass values down to the sub-stellar range, with disks inclined at 8°, 30°, and 55°." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size); "We perform these simulations using a gas disk mass of $10^{-2}\>M_\star$, a dust disk mass of $10^{-4}\>M_\star$, and three different cut-off radii ($R_{\rm{c}}=5, 20, 50$ au)" (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
   - assumption: Purely Keplerian rotation (including vertical height of optically thick 12CO layer); gas disk mass = 10^-2 M_star; dust disk mass = 10^-4 M_star  
  > "One limitation of our analysis lies in the assumption of purely Keplerian rotation (including the dependence on the disk’s vertical height) within protoplanetary disks." (Results); "We perform these simulations using a gas disk mass of $10^{-2}\>M_\star$, a dust disk mass of $10^{-4}\>M_\star$, and three different cut-off radii ($R_{\rm{c}}=5, 20, 50$ au)" (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
   - fitting: Parametric visibility fitting with csalt (prescription A) and posterior sampling with emcee  
  > "In brief, the analysis involved fitting a parametric model to ALMA visibilities using the `csalt` software (Andrews et al., in prep.), and sampling the posterior distribution with `emcee` ((cite: Foreman_2013))." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses); "We then perform fits using `csalt` with the parametric prescription A (see (cite: Zallio_2026); this is a complete disk prescription, which also considers for the emitting height of the optically thick $^{12}$CO layer) on the synthetic observation we created." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
   - validation: Compare fitted stellar masses from synthetic observations against true input stellar masses in the simulation grid; fits agree with true mass except at inc = 55 deg (~10% discrepancy in the worst case).  
  > "Most of our fits return results in agreement with the true stellar mass, except for the sources with inc $=55$°, as shown in Fig. 3, where the discrepancy is $\sim10\%$ in the worst case." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
3. **Derive evolutionary model-based stellar masses and ages from the HR diagram**: Fit observed effective temperatures and stellar luminosities on the HR diagram across ten PMS evolutionary track grids using Bayesian inference in ysoisochrone, both without a mass prior and with a Gaussian stellar mass prior based on the dynamical mass.  
  > "In addition to these dynamical masses, we derive stellar masses from the HR diagram ($M_{\star,\text{HRD}}$) using the effective temperatures $(T_{\text{eff}})$ and stellar luminosities $(L_\star)$ presented in Empey et al. (in prep.) for different PMS evolutionary models." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses); "To extract stellar masses and ages (together with their uncertainties), we used the `python` package `ysoisochrone` developed by (cite: Deng_2025), based on the `IDL` code developed by (cite: Pascucci_2016), which uses a Bayesian inference approach." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses); "When including a gaussian mass prior based on the dynamical mass and its associated uncertainty, the age-mass degeneracy reduces greatly, as shown in Fig. 5 (right)." (The impact of stellar mass priors on the age of single objects)
   - software: ysoisochrone, seaborn
   - parameter: 10 evolutionary track grids: Baraffe+15, Feiden+16 (magnetic and non-magnetic), PARSEC v2.0, Siess+2000, and SPOTS (f = 0%, 17%, 34%, 51%, 85%)  
  > "In particular, we used the evolutionary models presented in (cite: Baraffe2015), the magnetic and non-magnetic tracks presented in (cite: Feiden2016), the PARSEC v2.0 tracks presented in (cite: Nguyen_2022), the tracks from (cite: Siess_2000), and the stellar SPOTS tracks" (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses)
   - assumption: Feiden (2016) magnetic tracks for ages <= 1 Myr were corrected by interpolating data and removing numerical artifact outliers  
  > "are incorrect for ages $\leq1$ Myr, containing numerical artifacts, which we corrected by interpolating the data and removing the outliers." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model-based stellar masses, footnote 3)
   - fitting: Bayesian inference using ysoisochrone (with flat/uninformed mass prior and with Gaussian dynamical mass prior)  
  > "To extract stellar masses and ages (together with their uncertainties), we used the `python` package `ysoisochrone` developed by (cite: Deng_2025), based on the `IDL` code developed by (cite: Pascucci_2016), which uses a Bayesian inference approach." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses); "When including a gaussian mass prior based on the dynamical mass and its associated uncertainty, the age-mass degeneracy reduces greatly, as shown in Fig. 5 (right)." (The impact of stellar mass priors on the age of single objects)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Compare dynamical masses and HR diagram masses across evolutionary models**: Compute the percentage of sources agreeing within 1-sigma (quadratic sum of uncertainties), the median mass ratio <R> = <M_star,dyn / M_star,HRD> for sources in 0.1 < M_star < 1.3 M_sun, and Spearman rank correlation coefficients between relative mass discrepancy and dynamical mass.  
  > "In addition to the fraction of sources in agreement, we quantify the typical offset between dynamically-derived stellar masses and theoretical track-inferred stellar masses by computing the median mass ratio: $\langle R \rangle = \langle M_{\star,\text{dyn}} / M_{\star,\text{HRD}} \rangle$" (Comparison of M_star,dyn and M_star,HRD); "To ensure a fair comparison among the models, we report the percentage of agreement and the median mass ratio $\langle R \rangle$ only for sources with a defined mass value in all evolutionary models (i.e., within the mass range $0.1 < M_\star < 1.3 \> M_\odot$)." (Comparison of M_star,dyn and M_star,HRD); "To assess whether the mass discrepancies exhibit a stellar-mass dependence, we computed Spearman rank correlation coefficients between $(M_{\star,\rm{HRD}} - M_{\star,\rm{dyn}})/M_{\star,\rm{dyn}}$ and $M_{\star,\rm{dyn}}$." (Results)
   - parameter: Mass range 0.1 < M_star < 1.3 M_sun; agreement threshold +/- 1 sigma  
  > "To ensure a fair comparison among the models, we report the percentage of agreement and the median mass ratio $\langle R \rangle$ only for sources with a defined mass value in all evolutionary models (i.e., within the mass range $0.1 < M_\star < 1.3 \> M_\odot$)." (Comparison of M_star,dyn and M_star,HRD); "Figs. 1 and 2 show that the best match with dynamical stellar masses is provided by the SPOTS pre-main sequence tracks with $f=17\%$ cold spot coverage ($100\%$ agreement within $\pm1\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the quadratic sum of the model and dynamical mass uncertainties)" (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Evaluated fraction of sources in agreement and <R> including upper and lower limits, confirming results remain unchanged apart from minor variations.  
  > "We also evaluated the fraction of sources with consistent mass estimates and $\langle R \rangle$ including upper and lower limits, and found that, apart from minor variations, the results remain unchanged." (Comparison of M_star,dyn and M_star,HRD)

## Software

- **FRAPPE** : analyzing VLT/X-Shooter spectra to derive stellar properties (in Empey et al., in prep.)  
  > "The final sample is composed of 20 sources from the sample above that have well-determined stellar properties, which were derived from broad-band flux-calibrated medium-resolution spectra (Empey et al., in prep.) derived from VLT/X-Shooter and analyzed with `FRAPPE`" (Sample)
- **csalt** : fitting parametric disk models to ALMA 12CO visibilities and transforming mcfost radiative transfer models into synthetic radio-interferometric visibilities  
  > "In brief, the analysis involved fitting a parametric model to ALMA visibilities using the `csalt` software (Andrews et al., in prep.), and sampling the posterior distribution with `emcee` ((cite: Foreman_2013))." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses); "After creating `mcfost` ((cite: Pinte_2006; Pinte_2009)) radiative transfer models of $^{12}$CO $J=3-2$, we use built-in routines of `csalt` to transform them into radio-interferometric observed data, using the same total integration time ($\sim180$ s per disk)" (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
- **emcee** : sampling the posterior distribution in visibility modeling  
  > "In brief, the analysis involved fitting a parametric model to ALMA visibilities using the `csalt` software (Andrews et al., in prep.), and sampling the posterior distribution with `emcee` ((cite: Foreman_2013))." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses)
- **ysoisochrone** v1.3.3: Bayesian inference to extract stellar masses and ages from HR diagrams using PMS evolutionary tracks  
  > "To extract stellar masses and ages (together with their uncertainties), we used the `python` package `ysoisochrone` developed by (cite: Deng_2025), based on the `IDL` code developed by (cite: Pascucci_2016), which uses a Bayesian inference approach." (Derivation of dynamical and evolutionary model–based stellar masses); "https://github.com/DingshanDeng/ysoisochrone; the specific version (v1.3.3) used in this work is archived on Zenodo at https://zenodo.org/records/18420752" (Footnote 4)
- **mcfost** : creating radiative transfer models of 12CO J=3-2 for the numerical simulation grid  
  > "After creating `mcfost` ((cite: Pinte_2006; Pinte_2009)) radiative transfer models of $^{12}$CO $J=3-2$, we use built-in routines of `csalt` to transform them into radio-interferometric observed data, using the same total integration time ($\sim180$ s per disk)" (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
- **seaborn** : Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) visualization of posterior age distributions  
  > "The visualization was produced using the `seaborn` package ((cite: Waskom2021)), which implements Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to provide a smooth representation of the underlying probability density." (Age distributions for different evolutionary tracks)

## Qualitative findings

- SPOTS evolutionary tracks with f=17% cold spot coverage best reproduce dynamical stellar masses in the 0.1-1.3 M_sun range.  
  > "The SPOTS evolutionary tracks of (cite: Somers_2020) with $f=17\%$ best reproduce ($100\%$ agreement within $\pm1\sigma$ uncertainties) the dynamical stellar masses in the stellar range $0.1-1.3$ $M_{\odot}$." (Conclusions)
- Most evolutionary models show no statistically significant stellar-mass dependence in their mass discrepancies, except PARSEC v2.0 and SPOTS models with f >= 51% which show a negative correlation.  
  > "Most models show no statistically significant correlation ($p>0.05$), but PARSEC v2.0 and SPOTS models with $f\geq51\%$ display a negative correlation (Spearman coefficient $\rho\leq-0.77$, with $p<10^{-3}$), implying a significant mass-dependent bias." (Results)
- Incorporating dynamical stellar mass priors into HR diagram fitting greatly reduces age-mass degeneracy and makes median inferred ages across different evolutionary tracks much more consistent.  
  > "When including a gaussian mass prior based on the dynamical mass and its associated uncertainty, the age-mass degeneracy reduces greatly, as shown in Fig. 5 (right)." (The impact of stellar mass priors on the age of single objects); "When using stellar dynamical masses as mass priors for HR diagram fitting, the median inferred ages across different tracks become consistent, and the scatter decreases by $\gtrsim77\%$, while individual ages can vary by up to $\sim25\%$." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets in Upper Scorpius were selected because they have accurate ALMA dynamical masses from 12CO rotation, precise Gaia parallaxes (varpi/sigma > 10), and broad-band flux-calibrated VLT/X-Shooter spectra that allow reliable modeling of veiling, extinction, and spectral type.  
  > "The targets were selected based on the availability of accurate dynamical masses, reliable stellar parameters, and precise *Gaia* parallaxes ($\varpi/\sigma(\varpi)>10$)." (Sample); "These spectra are well-suited for young stars because they allow a better modeling of veiling, extinction, and spectral type for the targets (e.g., (cite: Herczeg_2014; Manara_2013; Manara_2020; Manara_2023))." (Sample)
- Why this method: Because statistical uncertainties on dynamical masses from parametric visibility fitting in Zallio et al. (2026) are unrealistically small, a grid of mcfost + csalt simulations was used to evaluate and assign conservative mean statistical uncertainties.  
  > "Since the stellar mass statistical uncertainties reported by (cite: Zallio_2026) are systematically underestimated due to limitations of fitting visibilities with parametric models, we decide to test the accuracy of the `csalt` dynamical mass measurements by creating and fitting a grid of numerical simulations." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size); "This choice is motivated by the fact that the mean statistical uncertainties are found to be greater than the systematic uncertainty, and we therefore opt for a conservative approach." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
- Alternative: Including upper and lower mass limits when computing the fraction of consistent sources and median mass ratio <R> (tested and found to leave results unchanged apart from minor variations).  
  > "We also evaluated the fraction of sources with consistent mass estimates and $\langle R \rangle$ including upper and lower limits, and found that, apart from minor variations, the results remain unchanged." (Comparison of M_star,dyn and M_star,HRD)
- Alternative: Modeling deviations from Keplerian motion due to pressure gradients and disk self-gravity (not considered because angular and spectral resolution are insufficient to detect them).  
  > "However, due to the limited angular ($0.1-0.3^{\prime\prime}$) and spectral ($\sim480$ ms$^{-1}$) resolution of the data presented in (cite: Carpenter2025), we emphasize that such deviations remain undetectable, and were therefore not considered in the analysis." (Results)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Assumption of purely Keplerian rotation neglects pressure gradients and disk self-gravity, which can alter inferred central masses by 5-10%.  
  > "One limitation of our analysis lies in the assumption of purely Keplerian rotation (including the dependence on the disk’s vertical height) within protoplanetary disks." (Results); "For instance, (cite: Andrews_2024) and (cite: Longarini_2025) demonstrated that deviations from Keplerian motion induced by pressure support can alter the inferred mass of the central object by as much as $5-10\%$." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Limited angular (0.1-0.3 arcsec) and spectral (~480 m s^-1) resolution of the ALMA observations.  
  > "However, due to the limited angular ($0.1-0.3^{\prime\prime}$) and spectral ($\sim480$ ms$^{-1}$) resolution of the data presented in (cite: Carpenter2025), we emphasize that such deviations remain undetectable, and were therefore not considered in the analysis." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): Comparison is restricted to the age of Upper Scorpius (4-14 Myr), and whether agreement holds at younger ages remains to be tested.  
  > "Since evolutionary models have a strong time-dependent aspect, our comparison demonstrates the consistency of disk-based stellar masses with HR diagram predictions at the age of Upper Scorpius ($4-14$ Myr, (cite: Ratzenbock_2023a; Ratzenbock_2023b))." (Conclusions); "Whether this agreement holds at different ages remains to be tested, and will be explored, for example, with the DEC/O Large Program for the younger ages." (Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): In the simulation grid, csalt fits at disk inclination of 55 deg show a discrepancy of ~10% in the worst case.  
  > "Most of our fits return results in agreement with the true stellar mass, except for the sources with inc $=55$°, as shown in Fig. 3, where the discrepancy is $\sim10\%$ in the worst case." (Uncertainties on the dynamical mass measurements due to inclination, stellar mass, and disk size)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The stellar parameters (Teff, L*) come from an unpublished work in preparation (Empey et al., in prep.) and are not tabulated in the main text, making independent verification of the HRD fitting dependent on the external repository.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 100% agreement for SPOTS f=17% depends directly on the inflated dynamical mass uncertainties assigned via the coarse 3x3 simulation grid in Table 1.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Cold spot coverage is assumed constant across the entire 0.1-1.3 M_sun mass range, whereas spot properties may vary with stellar mass or magnetic activity.
