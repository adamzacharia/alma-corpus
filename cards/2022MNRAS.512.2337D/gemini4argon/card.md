## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure ALMA millimeter/submillimeter flux densities and uncertainties**: Retrieve final calibrated images from the ALMA archive, perform Gaussian fits and RMS image noise measurements in identical regions using CASA VIEWER(), and sum RMS and flux calibration uncertainties in quadrature.  
  > "For our analysis we used the final calibrated images retrieved from the archive. Photometric measurements of RMS imaged noise and Gaussian fits were performed within CASA (Common AStronomy Software Applications; version 5.4; (cite: McMullin2007)) utilizing the VIEWER() function tools." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "The same regions were selected in all the data sets, with only reliable converged fits reported." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "Flux density error bars were estimated summing in quadrature the rms measured from final calibrated images and the calibration ALMA uncertainty for data collected in band 3 and band 7 (which correspond to 5% and 10% of the flux, respectively )." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Calibration uncertainty = 5% (Band 3), 10% (Band 7); upper limits at 3 sigma  
  > "the calibration ALMA uncertainty for data collected in band 3 and band 7 (which correspond to 5% and 10% of the flux, respectively )." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "Upper limits are given at $3\sigma$ level" (Table 4)
   - fitting: Gaussian fits in CASA VIEWER()  
  > "Photometric measurements of RMS imaged noise and Gaussian fits were performed within CASA (Common AStronomy Software Applications; version 5.4; (cite: McMullin2007)) utilizing the VIEWER() function tools." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations)
   - validation: Only reliable converged fits reported  
  > "The same regions were selected in all the data sets, with only reliable converged fits reported." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations)
2. **Reduce and image ATCA radio observations and extract flux densities**: Process public ATCA datasets in Miriad via splitting, manual or sumthreshold flagging (pgflag), calibration, clean & restore imaging, and phase decorrelation correction (calred).  
  > "We processed the public dataset through the data reduction package Miriad (cite: Sault1995) using standard procedures for the 5.5, 9.0, 9.5, 16.7 and 21.2 GHz datasets, which included splitting, manual flagging, calibration and clean & restore imaging." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "The 2.1 GHz datasets were split out and flagged via the sumthreshold algorithm (cite: Offringa2010) implemented in the Miriad task pgflag using the user guide suggested settings." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "Phase decorrelation, more evident in the 16.7-21.2 GHz datasets was accounted for and fixed by comparing triple product flux outputs from the Miriad task calred with restored image peak flux density values in phase calibrator scans." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations)
   - software: Miriad, pgflag, calred
   - parameter: robust = 0 (6C configuration until 2019 Sep 13, all antennas); robust = 2 (H168 configuration past 2019 Sep 13, antenna-6 excluded); multiplicative systematic error = 3% (2.1-9.5 GHz), 5% (16.7-21.2 GHz)  
  > "The ATCA observations until 2019 Sep 13th were taken 6C extended array configuration and thus imaged with robustness parameter robust=0 and all antennas were included." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "Observations past 2019 Sep 13th were performed in H168 compact hybrid array configuration; they were imaged with robustness parameter robust=2 and antenna-6 visibilities were excluded." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations); "The multiplicative term was estimated at 3% at 2.1, 5.5, 9.0 and 9.5 GHz, and 5% at 16.7 and 21.2 GHz." (ALMA and ATCA radio observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Reduce Swift BAT, XRT, and UVOT data and MASTER optical polarimetry**: Process BAT light curves and spectra with HEASOFT and XSPEC, retrieve and convert XRT light curves, perform host-subtracted aperture photometry on UVOT images with HEADAS/UVOTSOURCE, and perform aperture photometry and Stokes Q calculation on MASTER polarized images.  
  > "BAT data were processed using HEASOFT package (v6.25)." (Swift/BAT); "The analysis pipeline used software HEADAS 6.24 and UVOT calibration 20170922." (Swift/UVOT); "In this interval, the time-averaged 3 $\sigma$ upper limit is $Q$ $\lesssim$6%, where $Q=\left(F_{0}-F_{90}\right)/\left(F_0 + F_{90}\right)$ is the Stokes parameter, $F_{0}$ and $F_{90}$ are the fluxes measured by the two perpendicular filters." (Polarization)
   - software: HEASOFT, BATECONVERT, BATMASKWTEVT, BATTBLOCKS, XSPEC, HEADAS, UVOTSOURCE
   - parameter: UVOT extraction radius = 3'' aperture corrected to 5''; MASTER aperture size = 3.9''  
  > "The source counts were extracted using a region of 3$''$ radius. In order to be consistent with the UVOT calibration, these count rates were then corrected to 5$''$ using the curve of growth contained in the calibration files." (Swift/UVOT); "After astrometric binding of each image, we performed standard aperture photometry using an aperture size of 3.9$\ensuremath{''}$." (MASTER polarization measurements)
   - assumption: Galactic N_H = 5.6 x 10^20 cm^-2; afterglow and supernova negligible after 58 days in UVOT  
  > "in addition to the Galactic value $N_H=5.6\times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (cite: Willingale2013)." (Swift/XRT); "At these late times we can assume that the emission from the afterglow and supernova is negligible compared to the host after this time (both components have AB magnitude $>$23 at this stage)." (Swift/UVOT)
   - fitting: Power-law and Band function fits in XSPEC for BAT/XRT spectra; Bayesian blocks for BAT duration  
  > "We used BATTBLOCKS to run the Bayesian Block algorithm over the 64 ms, background-subtracted, 15–350 keV light curve" (Swift/BAT); "Spectral analysis was carried out using XSPECV12.10.1 (cite: Arnaud1996)" (Swift/BAT)
   - validation: Chi-squared per degree of freedom reported for spectral fits  
  > "Its spectrum, integrated from $T_0$-52 s to $T_0$-47 s, is well fit by a simple power law with a photon index of $1.89 \pm 0.22$ ($\chi^{2}/\mathrm{d.o.f.} = 1.04$)" (Swift/BAT)
4. **Model multi-wavelength light curves and broadband SEDs with Forward Shock and Reverse Shock components**: Fit X-ray, UVOIR, and radio light curves with power laws and smoothly broken power laws; model broadband SEDs at 4 epochs (1200 s, 6800 s, 21600 s, 1 d) using two smoothly broken power-law components (FS and RS); test standard FS model using afterglowpy.  
  > "we derive that the emission rises with a slope of $-3.2 \pm 0.5$, reaches its peak at around 1400 s and then decays with index $1.65 \pm 0.18$." (A forward shock plus reverse shock scenario); "To better disentangle the different emission components we studied the afterglow SED at four different epochs: 1200 s (slightly before the peak), 6800 s (during the steep decay), 21600 s (around the time of the H.E.S.S. detection), and 1 d after the BAT trigger. Each component was described with a smoothly broken power-law (SBPL), and its evolution was determined according to the standard fireball model." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED)); "We used afterglowpy (cite: Ryan2020) to describe the multi-wavelength data with the predictions of a standard FS propagating in a constant density medium." (Standard Forward Shock model)
   - software: afterglowpy
   - parameter: SED epochs: 1200 s, 6800 s, 21600 s, 1 d; FS spectral indices fixed to beta_low = -1/3 and beta_up = 0.75 (p ~ 2.50)  
  > "To better disentangle the different emission components we studied the afterglow SED at four different epochs: 1200 s (slightly before the peak), 6800 s (during the steep decay), 21600 s (around the time of the H.E.S.S. detection), and 1 d after the BAT trigger." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED)); "Assuming an electron spectral energy index $p$ $\sim$ 2.50, as derived from the radio light curves, we set $\beta^{FS}_{low}$=-1/3 and $\beta^{FS}_{up}$=0.75 for the FS spectral index below and above the peak energy, respectively." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED))
   - assumption: Constant density external medium; standard fireball closure relations for FS  
  > "The first spectral component tracks the evolution of a forward shock (FS) expanding in a constant density medium, as observed at the low frequency radio data." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED))
   - fitting: Smoothly broken power-law fits to light curves and SEDs  
  > "Each component was described with a smoothly broken power-law (SBPL), and its evolution was determined according to the standard fireball model." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED))
   - validation: Comparison with standard FS predictions in afterglowpy showing underestimation of early X-ray/optical and high-frequency radio emission  
  > "While the late X-rays and low-frequency radio data can be reasonably well described by this FS model, the early time X-rays/optical data and the high-frequency radio emission are significantly underestimated by the model." (Standard Forward Shock model)
5. **Derive burst energetics, circumburst density, and jet opening angle**: Calculate isotropic-equivalent kinetic energy from X-ray luminosity at 14 d, infer circumburst density using log(E_K/n) from literature, and derive lower limits on jet opening angle and collimation-corrected energy from the non-detection of a jet break up to 100 d.  
  > "We use its X-ray luminosity at 14 d, $L_X$ $\approx 1.7 \times 10^{43}$ erg s$^{-1}$, to estimate the total isotropic-equivalent blastwave energy (cite: Granot2002):" (Burst Energetics and Environment); "and the ratio log $(E_K/n) \approx$ 54 from (cite: Salafia2021), we infer a circumburst density $n\approx$0.01 - 0.1 cm$^{-3}$, typical of the interstellar medium." (Burst Energetics and Environment); "Finally, we can use our observations to set a lower limit to the jet-break time $t_j\gtrsim100$ d. For the energies and density estimated above, this corresponds to a jet opening angle (cite: Rhoads99; Sari99):" (Burst Energetics and Environment)
   - parameter: t_j >= 100 d, nu_X ~ 5 keV at 14 d  
  > "where we use $z$=0.00785, $\nu_X$ $\sim$5 keV, and assume $p$=2.5." (Burst Energetics and Environment); "Finally, we can use our observations to set a lower limit to the jet-break time $t_j\gtrsim100$ d." (Burst Energetics and Environment)
   - assumption: p = 2.5, fraction of emitting electrons xi = 1, epsilon_e = 0.1, epsilon_B = 10^-4, standard LambdaCDM cosmology  
  > "We adopted a standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology (cite: 2018arXiv180706209P)." (Introduction); "where we use $z$=0.00785, $\nu_X$ $\sim$5 keV, and assume $p$=2.5. We further assume that the fraction of emitting electrons is $\xi$=1." (Burst Energetics and Environment)
   - fitting: Analytical scaling relations from Granot & Sari (2002), Rhoads (1999), and Sari et al. (1999)  
  > "We use its X-ray luminosity at 14 d, $L_X$ $\approx 1.7 \times 10^{43}$ erg s$^{-1}$, to estimate the total isotropic-equivalent blastwave energy (cite: Granot2002):" (Burst Energetics and Environment); "For the energies and density estimated above, this corresponds to a jet opening angle (cite: Rhoads99; Sari99):" (Burst Energetics and Environment)
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Sensitive upper limits on optical linear polarization disfavor off-axis jet models as the explanation for the delayed X-ray and optical afterglow peak.  
  > "We report sensitive limits on the linear polarization of the optical emission, disfavouring models of off-axis jets to explain the delayed afterglow peak." (Abstract)
- The multi-wavelength afterglow requires at least two emission components: a bright reverse shock dominating early optical/X-ray and high-frequency radio emission, and a forward shock dominating late-time and low-frequency radio emission.  
  > "The study of the multi-wavelength light curves and broadband spectra supports a model with at least two emission components: a bright reverse shock emission, visible at early times in the optical and X-rays and, later, in the radio band; and a forward shock component dominating at later times and lower radio frequencies." (Abstract)
- Standard fireball closure relations for a reverse shock expanding in a constant density medium do not provide an acceptable fit without modifications such as low initial Lorentz factor, high RS-to-FS magnetic field ratio, continued energy injection, or shell interaction.  
  > "By imposing the standard closure relations for a RS expanding in a constant density medium, no acceptable fit could be found." (Spectral Energy Distribution (SED)); "Although the forward/reverse shock model naturally describes the salient features of the afterglow, its basic version, as described by the standard fireball model and closure relations (cite: vanderHorst2014), does not provide a good fit to the dataset." (Summary and Conclusions)
- GRB 190829A shares prompt and afterglow energetics with cosmological long GRBs rather than sub-energetic nearby bursts.  
  > "A combined study of the prompt and afterglow properties shows many similarities with cosmological long GRBs, suggesting that GRB 190829A is an example of classical GRBs in the nearby universe." (Abstract)
