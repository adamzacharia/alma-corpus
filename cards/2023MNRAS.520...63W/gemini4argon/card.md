## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract multi-wavelength ancillary metrics (MUSE H-alpha, ALMA CO, HST dust) at cluster positions**: Perform aperture photometry on MUSE images with a radius matching the MUSE resolution (no continuum or sky subtraction); extract ALMA CO(2-1) intensity at the nearest pixel to the cluster sky position; evaluate dust lane masks at cluster positions.  
  > "The H$\alpha$ flux measurements are determined from the MUSE images using aperture photometry with a radius that matches the MUSE resolution. No continuum or annular sky subtraction is performed." (MUSE and ALMA Observations); "Given that the ALMA resolution element is much larger than the circular aperture used for HST photometry, we simply extract the CO intensity on the ALMA map at the pixel nearest to the cluster sky position." (MUSE and ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: MUSE aperture radius = ~0.8"  
  > "While this provides a uniform dataset, it has the disadvantages of lower resolution (approximately 0.8$\ensuremath{''}$ for MUSE, compared to $0.1\ensuremath{''}$ for HST)." (MUSE and ALMA Observations); "The H$\alpha$ flux measurements are determined from the MUSE images using aperture photometry with a radius that matches the MUSE resolution." (MUSE and ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: ALMA beam size = ~1.2"  
  > "For a typical PHANGS-HST galaxy at 16 Mpc median distance, the 1.2 arcsec ALMA beam size corresponds to approximately 90 pc." (MUSE and ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared MUSE H-alpha and ALMA CO against high-resolution HST F657N H-alpha imaging and visual inspection of B-V-I color snapshots.  
  > "In Figure 14 we compare the MUSE HA6562_FLUX values from Figure 13 with HST F657N (H$_{\alpha}$) measurements made from 2 pixel aperture photometry." (H\alpha measurements in NGC 3351: MUSE vs. HST)
2. **Visual inspection to establish a training/validation set of bad pipeline ages**: Visually inspect HST B-V-I color images, H-alpha images, and ALMA CO(2-1) maps, plus cluster environment and isolation, to estimate cluster ages and flag clusters where the Version 1.0 pipeline SED age differs by a factor of 10 or more.  
  > "This search was performed by co-author M. Floyd, who visually inspected color images (to check for the presence of dust that would be consistent with large $E(B-V)$ values)), and inspected H$\alpha$ and ALMA CO (2-1) images" (Visual Examination to Identify Bad Ages); "A designation of "bad age" requires a difference of a factor of ten or more between the visually estimated age and the version 1.0 SED age estimate, as described in (cite: turner21)." (Visual Examination to Identify Bad Ages)
   - software: Hubble Legacy Archive custom software
   - parameter: Bad age threshold = factor of 10 or more difference  
  > "A designation of "bad age" requires a difference of a factor of ten or more between the visually estimated age and the version 1.0 SED age estimate" (4.1 Visual Examination to Identify Bad Ages)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared flagged clusters in NGC 1433 with independent visual examination by Hannon et al. (2022), matching 7 of 8 sources.  
  > "A similar examination was made in NGC 1433 by (cite: hannon22), and eight clusters with underestimated ages were identified. Seven out of the same eight clusters are also identified as having bad ages in the current work" (Visual Examination to Identify Bad Ages)
3. **Construct hybrid SED age-dating solutions for star clusters**: Select candidate old globular clusters using color criteria (OGC-box or VI-limit) and refit their SEDs in CIGALE with 1/50th solar metallicity and E(B-V) < 0.1 mag; for NGC 1365 also select V-I > 1.5 mag clusters and refit with solar metallicity and 1.5 < E(B-V) < 2.5 mag; revert young/old interlopers identified via MUSE H-alpha flux threshold back to the baseline solar metallicity solution.  
  > "1). Identify clusters which are likely to be old globular clusters due to their position in the $U-B$ vs. $V-I$ color-color diagram (i.e., the OGC-box solution, where $-0.7 < U-B < 1.2$ and $0.95 < V-I < 1.7$), or their $V - I$ value (i.e., the VI-limit solution, where $V-I > 0.95$)." (Procedures); "2). Fix ages for the identified clusters using a low-metallicity (1/50th solar) model and low maximum allowed $E(B-V) < 0.1$ mag, appropriate for most old globular clusters." (Procedures); "2). Identify the high reddening objects (i.e., V-I $>$ 1.5), which are nearly all young, dusty clusters, and use a solar metallicity model, *but require higher values of $E(B-V)$* (1.5 $<$ $E(B-V)$ $2.5$ mag)." (Procedures)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: OGC-box (NGC 1433, 3351, 628): -0.7 < U-B < 1.2 and 0.95 < V-I < 1.7; VI-limit: V-I > 0.95  
  > "(i.e., the OGC-box solution, where $-0.7 < U-B < 1.2$ and $0.95 < V-I < 1.7$), or their $V - I$ value (i.e., the VI-limit solution, where $V-I > 0.95$)." (Procedures)
   - parameter: NGC 1365 smaller OGC-box: -0.6 < U-B < 0.5 and 0.95 < V-I < 1.5; high-red region: V-I > 1.5  
  > "1). Use a smaller OGC-box to identify old globular cluster candidates (i.e., the orange box in the bottom right panel of Figure 7; $-0.6 < U-B < 0.5$ and $0.95 < V-I < 1.5$)." (Procedures); "2). Identify the high reddening objects (i.e., V-I $>$ 1.5), which are nearly all young, dusty clusters" (Procedures)
   - parameter: Interloper H-alpha threshold = 3.0 x 10^-16 erg/s/cm^2/pixel; CO threshold = 2 K km s^-1  
  > "3). Identify potential young interlopers (i.e., young clusters with enough dust to give them colors similar to old globular clusters) using either H$\alpha$ flux (i.e., with H$\alpha > 3.0 \times 10^{-16}$ erg/s/cm$^2$/pixel, where pixel refers to MUSE pixels)" (Procedures); "Here, *clusters above a CO intensity value of 2 K km s$^{-1}$ are predicted to be young*." (NGC 1433)
   - assumption: Bruzual & Charlot (2003) SSP models, Fitzpatrick (1999) reddening law with R_V = 3.1, solar metallicity Z = 0.02 for young clusters, and 1/50th solar metallicity Z = 0.0004 for old globular clusters.  
  > "For the original baseline solution, we adopted a fixed solar metallicity ($Z=0.02$) model, and fit for the best combination of age and reddening" (HST Observations); "For the hybrid solutions that will be discussed later in this paper, we also performed fits with 1/50th solar metallicity ($Z=0.0004$), that are used for likely old globular clusters. The (cite: fitzpatrick99) reddening law with R$_{V}$ = 3.1 is used." (HST Observations)
   - fitting: Minimum chi-squared SED fitting in CIGALE across NUV, U, B, V, I bands over log Age = 6.0-10.2 yr.  
  > "The age-dating results presented throughout this paper are based on SED fits between cluster photometry in the NUV, U, B, V, and I bands and the Bruzual & Charlot (2003) stellar population models, through the CIGALE fitting software (cite: Boquien19)." (HST Observations); "The top plot in Figure 4 shows that the strongest likelihood peak is the youngest one, at log Age = 6.8 (i.e., 7 Myr), and that is what is reported, incorrectly, as the minimum $\chi^{2}$ result by CIGALE." (Problem 2: Allowing reddening to be a free parameter can result in large errors)
   - validation: Checked remaining clusters above the diagonal line in log Age vs. E(B-V), spot-checked clusters below the diagonal line, and validated NGC 1365 young/old classifications using JWST 21 micron brightness.  
  > "The recent JWST observations of NGC 1365 ((cite: whitmore22), (cite: lee22b)) provide an additional check on our age estimates. One result from this work is the finding that 21 micron flux is an excellent predictor of whether a cluster is old (faint or missing at 21 micron) or young (bright at 21 micron)." (Solution for NGC 1365 - improvement by \approx13 % in the overall success fraction)
4. **Fit cluster age distributions in NGC 628**: Bin Class 1+2 clusters in NGC 628 by age above the completeness limit for low-mass (log M/M_sun = 4.2-4.5) and high-mass (log M/M_sun > 4.5) regimes, and fit a power law dN/d_tau proportional to tau^gamma.  
  > "When fit to a power law $dN/d\tau \propto \tau^{\gamma}$, where $\tau$ = Age and $\gamma$ is the power law index, the original distribution (with incorrect ages for old globular clusters) has a best value of $\gamma=-0.18\pm0.02$" (Age Distribution in NGC 628); "Interestingly, the age distribution for lower-mass log $(M/M_{\odot})=4.2-4.5$ clusters is essentially unaffected by the age corrections (with best fits of $-0.59$ in all 3 cases)" (Age Distribution in NGC 628)
   - parameter: Mass bins: log(M/M_sun) > 4.5 and log(M/M_sun) = 4.2-4.5; age range out to log Age = 8.4 (standard) and 9.2 (extended)  
  > "The resulting change in the age distribution is particularly noticeable for clusters more massive than log $(M/M_{\odot})>4.5$." (Age Distribution in NGC 628); "Here, we are able to plot the age distribution out to log Age = 9.2 (above the completeness limit), as opposed to only out to log Age = 8.4 as used in (cite: adamo17)" (Age Distribution in NGC 628)
   - fitting: Power-law fit dN/d_tau proportional to tau^gamma  
  > "When fit to a power law $dN/d\tau \propto \tau^{\gamma}$, where $\tau$ = Age and $\gamma$ is the power law index" (Age Distribution in NGC 628)
   - validation: Tested sensitivity to removing the youngest age bin and compared directly against LEGUS results from Adamo et al. (2017).  
  > "If we remove the youngest age bin from the fit, due to concerns about potential inclusion of unbound associations (cite: bastian12b), the fits are only weakly affected" (Age Distribution in NGC 628)

## Qualitative findings

- Old globular clusters fit with a fixed solar metallicity model are systematically misassigned younger ages (mostly around 100 Myr or 5 Myr) and moderate-to-high reddening, populating a region above a diagonal line in the log Age vs. E(B-V) diagram.  
  > "A large fraction of the incorrectly age-dated globular clusters are systematically given ages around 100 Myr, polluting the younger populations as well." (Abstract); "all of the bad ages identified manually, and completely independently, are above a diagonal line that runs from (log Age, $E(B-V)$) = (6, 1.0) to (9, 0.1)." (Mapping Bad Ages to the log Age - E(B-V) and Color-Color Diagrams)
- H-alpha emission from MUSE is more effective at identifying young reddened interlopers in the old globular cluster color region than ALMA CO(2-1) intensity or HST dust maps.  
  > "*Overall, we find that H$\alpha$ emission from MUSE, even at $\approx$factor of 10 lower resolution than the HST images, is better able to identify reddened, young clusters which are interlopers in the OGC-box or the VI-limit region, than CO intensity or relative dust strength*." (Comparison between H\alpha and Dust)
- NGC 1365 hosts a dense population of ~60 intermediate-age (~300 Myr) clusters around its central region that form a reddening plume in the U-B vs. V-I diagram, resulting from bar dynamics and gas-star decoupling into overshoot regions.  
  > "Finally, we note the discovery of a dense population of $\approx300$ Myr clusters around the central region of NGC 1365. and discuss how this results naturally from the dynamics in a barred galaxy." (Abstract); "There are approximately 60 of these $\approx$300 Myr clusters (log Age around 8.7 in the upper right panel in Figure 11)." (The 300 Myr ‘plume’ in NGC 1365)

## Claim tuples

- H: Adopting a low-metallicity (1/50th solar) SED fit with E(B-V) < 0.1 mag for color-selected old globular cluster candidates (and higher E(B-V) limits for very red clusters in dusty galaxies) significantly reduces catastrophic age-dating errors. | E: Constructed hybrid SED solutions using OGC-box and VI-limit color cuts, refitting candidates in CIGALE with Z=0.0004 and E(B-V) < 0.1 mag (plus 1.5 < E(B-V) < 2.5 mag for V-I > 1.5 mag in NGC 1365), and evaluated accuracy via visual inspection and JWST 21 micron brightness. | R: Reduced the fraction of catastrophic age errors from 13-21% to 3-8%, improving overall age-dating success fractions by 9% to 18% (reaching 92-97%). | F: Post-facto hybrid color and metallicity rules successfully correct the ages of the vast majority of old globular clusters and heavily reddened young clusters. | **supported**
- H: H-alpha emission is a better discriminator of reddened young cluster interlopers among color-selected globular cluster candidates than ALMA CO(2-1) intensity or HST-derived dust maps. | E: Compared MUSE H-alpha flux, ALMA CO(2-1) intensity, and HST dust lane values at the positions of OGC-box clusters in all four galaxies against visual classifications and high-resolution HST H-alpha images. | R: Using CO instead of H-alpha results in 2% to 4% more incorrect ages across the four galaxies, and dust measurements show similar false-interloper rates to CO. | F: H-alpha emission is directly tied to massive young stars in recently formed clusters and identifies interlopers more reliably than CO or dust, which have a weaker spatial association with young clusters. | **supported**
- H: Correcting the systematically underestimated ages of old globular clusters significantly alters the observed cluster age distribution in NGC 628. | E: Fitted the power-law slope gamma of the cluster age distribution dN/d_tau in NGC 628 before and after applying the hybrid OGC-box and VI-limit age corrections. | R: For massive clusters (log M/M_sun > 4.5), the slope steepens from gamma = -0.18 ± 0.02 in the original pipeline catalog to gamma ≈ -0.7 (and gamma = -0.78 ± 0.07 over the extended age baseline). | F: Contamination of intermediate-age bins (~100 Myr) by misdated old globular clusters artificially flattens the high-mass cluster age distribution; correcting them yields steep decline rates consistent across mass bins. | **supported**

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Four PHANGS-HST galaxies were chosen to span a wide range of star formation rates and dust content (from low-SFR, relatively dust-free NGC 1433 to high-SFR, dusty NGC 1365), with uniform MUSE H-alpha and ALMA CO(2-1) coverage.  
  > "Four galaxies from the PHANGS-HST survey (cite: lee22) have been chosen for this exploratory study. These include galaxies that span the range from low SFR and low dust content, to high SFR and dust content." (Pilot Galaxy Sample)
- Why this method: Because old globular clusters have subsolar metallicity, fitting them with a solar-metallicity model forces SED fitters up the reddening vector to young/intermediate ages; isolating them via optical color cuts and applying a 1/50th solar metallicity model with low E(B-V) breaks this degeneracy.  
  > "In order to provide accurate estimates for clusters of all ages, we need to include fits to both high and low metallicity models in our age-dating procedure." (Problem 1: Star clusters do not all have the same metallicity); "The strong concentration of bad age points just above the solar model is the primary reason we are able to fix the age-metallicity problem for old globular clusters by adopting the 1/50th solar metallicity solution for clusters in this region of color-color space." (Mapping Bad Ages to the log Age - E(B-V) and Color-Color Diagrams)
- Alternative: Using the Calzetti attenuation curve instead of the Fitzpatrick (1999) reddening law (tested and found to change log age by ~±0.1 for only a handful of clusters).  
  > "For example, the estimated ages for only a handful of clusters change by $\sim \pm0.1$ in log age when the Calzetti attenuation curve is adopted instead." (HST Observations)
- Alternative: Adopting galaxy-specific or radially varying metallicities (rejected for this pilot study because the metallicity grid is coarse and radial gradients are weak compared to the factor-of-50 difference between young clusters and old globular clusters).  
  > "In principle, we could attempt to use different metallicities for different galaxies, e.g., using the values in (cite: santoro22). However, the grid of available metallicity models is fairly coarse, and nearly all of the galaxies in our sample would use the solar metallicity model in any case." (Problem 1: Star clusters do not all have the same metallicity)
- Alternative: Using ALMA CO(2-1) intensity or HST dust maps instead of MUSE H-alpha to identify interlopers (tested and rejected as primary criteria because they yield 2-4% more incorrect ages).  
  > "We find that young ‘Interlopers’ can be identified from their H$\alpha$ flux. CO (2-1) intensity or the presence of dust can also be used, but our tests show that they do not work as well." (Abstract)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Low angular resolution of MUSE H-alpha (~0.8") and ALMA CO(2-1) (~1.2") compared to HST (~0.1") causes contamination from nearby regions in ~20% of H-alpha interloper identifications.  
  > "The lower resolution, for both the MUSE H$\alpha$ and the CO observations ($\approx1.2\ensuremath{''}$), is one of the primary limitations of our pilot study." (MUSE and ALMA Observations); "This "resolution problem" is one of the primary failure modes of our current approach, and happens in approximately 20 % of the cases where the H$\alpha$ flux indicates the presence of a young interloper." (Solutions for NGC 3351 - improvement by \approx9 % in the overall success fraction)
- Limitation (authors): Post-facto box/cutoff rules in color-color space create discontinuities at the boundaries of the selection regions.  
  > "Similarly, in the current approach there are discontinuities at the edges of regions identified in color-color space that can be eliminated." (Future Work)
- Limitation (authors): HST machine-learning dust estimates suffer from a saturation problem in the densest dust lanes of NGC 3351 and NGC 1365.  
  > "We also note that in both NGC 3351 and NGC 1365 there is a tendency for the objects with the highest values of H$\alpha$ flux to have low dust values (i.e., they turn back toward the upper left in Figure 15). We believe this is due to a saturation problem since these objects are usually in the densest dust lanes." (Comparison between H\alpha and Dust)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Visual 'ground truth' age estimates used to define the success fraction are subjective and rely partly on the same imaging/gas tracers used in the hybrid rules.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The color and reddening cuts are tuned empirically on the four pilot galaxies (with custom rules required for NGC 1365) and may not generalize without adjustment to other galaxies.
