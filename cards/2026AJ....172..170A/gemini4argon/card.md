## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST/NIRSpec IFU cubes, subtract continuum, construct moment-0 maps, and align with ALMA maps**: Process NIRSpec IFU data with JWST Calibration Pipeline v1.17.1 and JEDIce post-reduction steps; fit and remove local linear baseline per transition and integrate residual over fixed-width frequency window for moment-0 maps; align IFU maps with ALMA Band 6 continuum and 12CO J=2-1 using target astrometry and continuum cross-correlation.  
  > "The data were processed with the JWST Calibration Pipeline v1.17.1 (cite: Bushouse_1_17_1_jwst_pipeline) and following the post-reduction calibration steps that are discussed in detail in (cite: Bergner_JEDIce_program_oveview_2026)." (Observation Results); "For each transition of interest, we fit and removed a local linear baseline and integrated the residual over a fixed-width frequency window to construct moment-0 maps." (Observation Results); "The IFU maps were aligned with the ALMA Band 6 continuum and $^{12}$CO $J=2-1$ emission (230.54 GHz) using the target astrometry and continuum cross-correlation." (Observation Results)
   - software: JWST Calibration Pipeline v1.17.1
   - parameter: R ~ 2700; 0.1'' spatial sampling; 3'' x 3'' FOV  
  > "The NIRSpec IFU provides a $3\ensuremath{''}\times3\ensuremath{''}$ field of view with spatial sampling of $0.1\ensuremath{''}$, corresponding to $\sim15$ au at the distance of $\rho$ Oph (cite: Ortiz_leon_gould_belt_distances_oph_2017; Ortiz_Leon_GaiaDR2_oph_serpens_aquila_2018), and spectral resolving power $R\sim2700$ in the wavelength range of interest." (Observation Results)
   - assumption: Distance of 147 pc  
  > "This is comparable to the JWST NIRSpec IFU angular resolution of $\sim0.1^{\ensuremath{''}}$, corresponding to $\sim15$ au at 147 pc." (Observation Results)
   - fitting: Local linear baseline fit to line-free channels  
  > "For each transition of interest, we fit and removed a local linear baseline and integrated the residual over a fixed-width frequency window to construct moment-0 maps." (Observation Results)
   - validation: Constructed continuum and line-to-continuum ratio maps over the same spectral window to verify that outer-disk O(2) emission is locally emitted rather than scattered light  
  > "As a check, we also construct continuum maps by integrating the baseline fits over the same spectral window as the line emission and then produce line-to-continuum ratio maps (Figures 5 and 6 in the Appendix 6)." (Morphology of H$_2$ Emission)
2. **Extract outer-disk H2 spectrum and measure line surface brightnesses and ratios**: Extract spectrum in bright outer-disk aperture where 1-0 O(2) coincides with CO contours; frequency-integrate continuum-subtracted spectra in each pixel within the aperture and average over all pixels.  
  > "For the spectral analysis, we extracted a spectrum in the bright outer-disk region, where the 1–0 O(2) emission is strong and coincides with the CO gas contours (see 2.2)." (Observation Results); "Surface brightnesses are computed by frequency-integrating the continuum-subtracted spectra in each pixel within the extraction aperture and then averaging over all pixels." (Spectral Characteristics)
   - parameter: 7% absolute flux calibration uncertainty added in quadrature to propagated local RMS noise  
  > "Uncertainties are obtained by propagating the local RMS noise through the spectral integration, with an additional 7% absolute flux calibration uncertainty added in quadrature." (Spectral Characteristics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Model UV-only excitation and collisional de-excitation with Meudon PDR code**: Run 1D plane-parallel isochoric slab models with isotropic external UV irradiation using the Meudon PDR code across a grid of hydrogen densities and UV field strengths, and compare emergent line ratios and surface brightnesses to observations.  
  > "To do this, we ran isochoric (constant-density) models using the Meudon PDR code, where the gas is treated as a 1D plane-parallel slab with isotropic external UV irradiation incident on the slab surfaces (cite: LePetit_MeudonPDR_2006)." (Illustrative UV-only models); "We extend beyond the diffuse PDR regime to densities characteristic of outer-disk gas, spanning hydrogen densities $n_{\rm H}=10^{2}$–$10^{9}$ cm$^{-3}$ and normalized UV field strengths $\chi_{\rm UV}=100$ and $1000$" (Illustrative UV-only models)
   - software: Meudon PDR code
   - parameter: n_H = 10^2-10^9 cm^-3; chi_UV = 100 and 1000 (Draine units)  
  > "We extend beyond the diffuse PDR regime to densities characteristic of outer-disk gas, spanning hydrogen densities $n_{\rm H}=10^{2}$–$10^{9}$ cm$^{-3}$ and normalized UV field strengths $\chi_{\rm UV}=100$ and $1000$" (Illustrative UV-only models)
   - assumption: 1D plane-parallel constant-density slab; self-consistent thermal balance  
  > "The temperature is computed self-consistently through thermal balance and varies strongly with depth into the slab as the UV field becomes attenuated by dust and H$_2$ self-shielding" (Illustrative UV-only models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate cosmic-ray ionization rate and analytical collisional de-excitation effects**: Assume CRs contribute half of observed 1-0 O(2) intensity to dilute UV overprediction of v=2-1 and 3-2 O(2) lines; apply Bialy et al. 2025 radiative-limit formula to estimate CR ionization rate, and solve analytical two-level steady-state rate equations for v=1, J=0 and J=2 to evaluate collisional redistribution.  
  > "In this case, the CR excitation contributes approximately half of the observed 1-0 O(2) surface brightness, $I_{\rm CR} \approx I_{\rm obs}/2$." (The case for a cosmic-ray contribution); "Nevertheless, using the formalism of (cite: Bialy2025_B68_CRH2), which applies in the purely radiative limit (i.e., without collisional redistribution), the corresponding order-of-magnitude CR ionization rate estimate is then" (The case for a cosmic-ray contribution); "We consider steady-state populations in the two lowest para-H$_2$ rotational sublevels of the first vibrationally excited state, ($v=1$, $J=0$) and ($v=1$, $J=2$)." (Impact of Collisional De-excitation on CR-excited H$_2$)
   - parameter: I_CR ~ 2.5e-5 erg s^-1 cm^-2 sr^-1; E_ul = 7.6e-13 erg; alpha_ul ~ 1.0; b_(v=1,J=0) = 1.57; gN(H2) ~ 6.5e21 cm^-2  
  > "where we adopt $I_{\rm CR}\approx2.5\times10^{-5}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ sr$^{-1}$, $\lambda = 2.627 \mu$m for the O(2) transition ($E_{ul}=7.6\times10^{-13}$ erg), a branching ratio $\alpha_{ul}=A_{ul}/A_{\rm tot}\simeq1.0$, and a CR excitation probability $b_{(v=1,J=0)}=1.57$" (The case for a cosmic-ray contribution)
   - parameter: A = 8.5e-7 s^-1; q1 = 2.2e-13 cm^3 s^-1 at T = 100 K; q2 = 8.7e-15 cm^3 s^-1  
  > "At $T=100$ K, we adopt $q_1 = 2.2\times10^{-13}$ cm$^{3}$ s$^{-1}$ from (cite: Flower_Roueff_H2collisions_98), and impose detailed balance, $q_2(T) = 5  q_1  \exp(-484 \text{ K}/T)$, giving $q_2\simeq8.7\times10^{-15}$ cm$^{3}$ s$^{-1}$." (Impact of Collisional De-excitation on CR-excited H$_2$)
   - assumption: CRs contribute ~50% of 1-0 O(2) surface brightness; effective emitting column gN(H2) may be up to an order of magnitude larger due to dust settling; collisional redistribution enhances 1-0 O(2) by up to a factor of ~2  
  > "In such cases, the effective emitting column may exceed the fiducial value adopted here, perhaps by as much as an order of magnitude or even more (the exact factor is highly uncertain)" (The case for a cosmic-ray contribution); "Thus, collisional redistribution can increase the 1–0 O(2) emissivity by at most a factor of $\sim2$." (Impact of Collisional De-excitation on CR-excited H$_2$)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Collider density inferred from analytical CR calculation (~3e7 cm^-3) compared with density regime where UV PDR models reproduce observed 1-0 O(4)/O(2) and S(0)/O(2) ratios  
  > "Notably, the collider density inferred from this illustrative CR-based estimate is comparable to the densities at which the UV PDR models reproduce the observed 1–0 O(4)/1–0 O(2) and 1–0 S(0)/1–0 O(2) ratios (Figure 3)" (Impact of Collisional De-excitation on CR-excited H$_2$)

## Qualitative findings

- Ro-vibrational H2 emission in the outer disk of Oph 163131 is vibrationally hot (detected up to v=3) but rotationally cold (dominated by J_u=0 O(2) lines with strongly suppressed higher-J and ortho lines).  
  > "We present JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations of the edge-on disk Oph 163131, which reveal a unusual ro-vibrational H$_2$ spectrum dominated by the 1–0 O(2) line (2.627 $\mu$m), with suppressed higher-$J$ emission despite excitation to $v=2$ and $3$." (Abstract)
- The extended O(2) H2 emission broadly follows the ALMA 12CO J=2-1 outer gas disk beyond ~200 au, increasing above and below a dark midplane lane that encloses the compact ALMA Band 6 millimeter dust continuum lane.  
  > "The extended O(2) emission broadly follows the CO($J=$2–1) gas disk shown as cyan contours in Figure 1." (Morphology of H$_2$ Emission); "The H$_2$ emission is significantly weaker in the midplane region within a radius of $\sim$200 au, forming a dark lane most noticeable in the O(2) H$_2$ maps (top row of Figure 1)." (Morphology of H$_2$ Emission)
- Neither UV pumping alone nor cosmic-ray excitation alone can quantitatively reproduce all observed H2 line ratios; a stratified combination of moderate external UV irradiation, deep cosmic-ray excitation, and high-density collisional de-excitation is required.  
  > "Compared to UV, the CRs have the advantage of readily populating the $v$=1 levels without overheating the gas and thus alleviating the problem of overpredicting the para-to-ortho line ratios, and penetrating deeper into the disk, as required by the O(2) emission morphology." (Conclusion); "However, they underpredict the 3-2 and 2-1 O(2) line intensity relative to 1-0 O(2) line (opposite to the UV case), and thus are unlikely to provide an explanation of the observed spectra *alone*." (Conclusion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Oph 163131 is an edge-on disk with extreme dust settling (scale height <= 0.5 au at 100 au) and an extended, cold (30-40 K) CO gas disk, allowing JWST/NIRSpec IFU to spatially disentangle local outer-disk H2 gas emission from near-IR scattered starlight.  
  > "In edge-on protoplanetary disks, the optically thick disk midplane obscures most or all direct stellar light from the observer, providing a unique opportunity to disentangle the spatial origin of disk emission." (Introduction); "This system, therefore, provides a well-controlled disk environment for assessing how irradiation, ionization, and collisional redistribution shape near-infrared H$_2$ excitation." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Extracting the spectrum beyond ~200 au where line-to-continuum ratio is highest avoids contamination from inner-disk emission scattered by dust, while 1D high-density PDR modeling and analytical rate equations isolate the effects of UV, CRs, and collisional redistribution.  
  > "These maps further confirm that the O(2) emission is spatially extended and that the line-to-continuum ratio becomes largest outside the scattered-light dominated part of the disk, supporting a more direct association between the detected H$_2$ emission and locally emitting outer-disk gas." (Morphology of H$_2$ Emission)
- Alternative: Pure thermal excitation (e.g., shocks or warm gas collisions): rejected for the outer disk because outer-disk gas temperatures from CO are 30-40 K, which cannot thermally populate v >= 1 vibrational levels.  
  > "This requirement is impossible to satisfy through thermal excitation alone (i.e. collisional excitation by molecules at the kinetic temperature of the gas) at the low temperatures inferred for the outer disk ($\sim$30–40 K at radii beyond 200 au; (cite: Flores_unusualanatomy_oph163131_2021; Villenave_highly_setled_Oph163131_2022))." (Non-thermal excitation of ro-vibrational H$_2$ in a cold, dense gas)
- Alternative: UV-only excitation in a dense PDR: rejected as a complete explanation because it overpredicts the ortho 1-0 O(3)/O(2) ratio by a factor of ~4.5 to 10 and underpredicts absolute 1-0 O(2) brightness at chi_UV = 100.  
  > "UV-only, dense PDR models can reproduce some aspects of the observed spectrum, particularly the domination of the O(2) lines and the ordering of these lines in brightness, but do not reproduce the line ratios quantitatively, particularly when the observed lines from the $v=1$ level (such as O(3)) are taken into account." (Conclusion)
- Alternative: Cosmic-ray excitation alone: rejected because CR secondary electrons weakly populate v >= 2 levels and thus underpredict the observed v=2-1 and 3-2 O(2) intensities relative to 1-0 O(2), and in very cold gas underpredict 1-0 O(3).  
  > "However, they underpredict the 3-2 and 2-1 O(2) line intensity relative to 1-0 O(2) line (opposite to the UV case), and thus are unlikely to provide an explanation of the observed spectra *alone*." (Conclusion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Brightest Q-branch H2 transitions (2.40-2.43 um) fall within the NIRSpec high-resolution detector gap.  
  > "In the high-resolution grating settings, however, the brightest Q-branch transitions (2.40–2.43 $\mu$m) unfortunately fall within the detector gap." (Observation Results)
- Limitation (authors): In the scattered-light dominated conical regions, continuum emission is >= 10x stronger than line emission, so scattered line emission from closer to the star cannot be excluded there.  
  > "These show that although H$_2$ lines are detected within the scattered-light regions, the continuum emission there is relatively strong ($\gtrsim 10\times$ that of the line), making it more difficult to exclude the possibility that some fraction of the observed line emission is scattered into the aperture from regions closer to the star." (Morphology of H$_2$ Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Meudon PDR models assume a 1D plane-parallel constant-density slab with perpendicular sightline, rather than a 2D/3D stratified edge-on disk geometry with dust settling and grain growth.  
  > "In particular, the nearly edge-on viewing geometry may increase the effective path length through the shallow UV-pumped outer disk layers relative to the perpendicular sightline assumed in the plane-parallel slab models" (Illustrative UV-only models)
- Limitation (authors): Inferred cosmic-ray ionization rate is an order-of-magnitude illustration subject to large uncertainties in the effective emitting H2 column (due to dust settling), foreground extinction, and missing low-temperature collisional rate coefficients.  
  > "The corresponding CR ionization rate inferred here should be treated as an order-of-magnitude illustration rather than a precise inference." (The case for a cosmic-ray contribution); "This estimate should be regarded as illustrative rather than a formal density measurement; at lower temperatures $q_1$ decreases, shifting the inferred density upward, but accurate low-temperature rate coefficients are not yet available." (Impact of Collisional De-excitation on CR-excited H$_2$)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): UV and CR excitation are not modeled self-consistently within a single unified 2D/3D thermo-chemical disk model; instead, 1D constant-density UV-only PDR slabs and an analytical CR estimate are combined heuristically.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): X-ray ionization from the central protostar produces secondary electrons similar to cosmic rays and is not quantitatively modeled or ruled out as a contributor to the v=1 H2 excitation.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Adopting the B68 effective emitting column gN(H2) as a baseline for an edge-on settled protoplanetary disk introduces at least an order-of-magnitude systematic uncertainty in the inferred CR ionization rate.
