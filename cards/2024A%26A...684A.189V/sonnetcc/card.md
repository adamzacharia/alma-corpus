- Fields: star_formation, astrochemistry

## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify molecular lines in the continuum-window spectra**: Spectra extracted from the two continuum spw bands over a 0.82'' x 0.77'' region and lines identified in CASSIS against JPL, CDMS and VAMDC  
  > "which extends within 0.82$^{\prime\prime}$ $\times$ 0.77$^{\prime\prime}$ centred at $\alpha$ (2000) = 17h11m23.107s" (Observations); "The line identification was performed using the CASSIS" (Line identification)
   - software: CASSIS, JPL database, CDMS database, VAMDC
   - parameter: Extraction region 0.82'' x 0.77'' centred at 17h11m23.107s, -27d24m33.005s  
  > "which extends within 0.82$^{\prime\prime}$ $\times$ 0.77$^{\prime\prime}$ centred at $\alpha$ (2000) = 17h11m23.107s" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Candidate detections checked against other transitions with similar Einstein coefficient, upper level energy and statistical weight (formamide candidate not confirmed)  
  > "To confirm the detection, we checked for additional transitions with similar spectroscopic parameters (Einstein coefficient, upper energy level, and statistical weight)." (Line identification)
2. **Make moment 0 and moment 1 maps and spectra of isolated lines**: Moment 0 maps of CH3OCH3 and CH3OCHO lines, moment 1 map of the unblended CH3OCH3 258.549 GHz line, spectra from a ~0.4'' beam, all with GILDAS  
  > "The maps presented in the following were obtained using the IRAM/GILDAS" (Spatial distribution); "we used the unblended 258.549 GHz transition of dimethyl ether to compute the velocity field (moment 1) around" (Spatial distribution)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: GILDAS version dec22a  
  > "package (version dec22a)" (Spatial distribution)
   - parameter: Spectra extracted from a ~0.4'' beam including both 11A and 11B  
  > "The spectra from both transitions were extracted from a $\sim$ 0.4$^{\prime\prime}$ beam size, corresponding to the beam for both transitions" (Spatial distribution)
   - assumption: Emission assumed compact when locating the three Gaussian components  
  > "The profile is a simple three-fit Gaussian, and we used it to try to determine the exact location, assuming that the emission is compact." (Spatial distribution)
   - fitting: Three-Gaussian profile for the 258.549 GHz line (fit details not given)  
  > "The profile is a simple three-fit Gaussian" (Spatial distribution)
   - validation: Velocity field compared with the methanol velocity field of Vastel2022  
  > "The same velocity field has been obtained for the methanol transition (cite: Vastel2022)." (Spatial distribution)
3. **Construct position-velocity diagram of CH3OCH3**: PV cut parallel to the velocity gradient seen in the moment 1 map of the 258.549 GHz line  
  > "produced from a cut oriented parallel to the velocity gradient observed in the moment 1 map of the dimethyl ether emission at 258.549 GHz" (Spatial distribution)
   - parameter: Cut PA = 140 deg east of north; cut width 0.5''  
  > "whose PA is 140$^{\circ}$ east of north" (Spatial distribution); "The cut width is 0.5$^{\prime\prime}$, comparable to the angular resolution of the FAUST data" (Spatial distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Three velocity structures compared with those identified in methanol  
  > "This figure clearly shows the three velocity structures that were identified with methanol (cite: Vastel2022)" (Spatial distribution)
4. **Locate emission peaks per velocity channel with 2D Gaussian fits**: 2D Gaussian fits per channel of the CH3OCH3 258.549 GHz line in velocity ranges [-5, 1], [1, 6] and [6, 13] km/s to obtain intensity-peak positions; also red/blue high-velocity channels with S/N > 5  
  > "we performed 2D Gaussian fits on the CH$_3$OCH$_3$ transition at 258.549 GHz (the strongest unblended transition with a high S/N) in each channel" (Spatial distribution); "in the high-velocity channels where the S/N is higher than five" (Spatial distribution)
   - software: GILDAS
   - parameter: Only peaks brighter than 5 times the Gaussian fit error and S/N > 10 retained, giving position accuracy better than 0.02''  
  > "we only considered intensity peaks brighter than five times its Gaussian fit error and a S/N higher than ten, corresponding to a position accuracy better than 0.02$^{\prime\prime}$" (Spatial distribution)
   - parameter: Position accuracy = theta/(2 x S/N), theta = synthesised beam  
  > "where $\theta$ is the synthesised beam size, and S/N is the signal-to-noise ratio of the peak with respect to the map noise (cite: Condon1997; Condon1998)" (Spatial distribution)
   - assumption: Emission in each velocity channel is compact and centrally peaked  
  > "Since the iCOMs emission in each velocity channel is compact and centrally peaked" (Spatial distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Centroid distribution compared with the methanol analysis (similar except for the ~3 km/s component) and with CO high-velocity gas  
  > "This 2D analysis is similar to our analysis of methanol, except for the middle component at $\sim$ 3 km s$^{-1}$" (Spatial distribution)
5. **LTE radiative transfer fit of CH3OCHO and CH3OCH3 to get abundance ratios**: LTE fit in CASSIS of all CH3OCH3 and CH3OCHO transitions simultaneously (both continuum windows plus setup 2 spw 47 and 43), using methanol non-LTE parameters as input, with blended lines included  
  > "obtained by simultaneously fitting all lines of CH$_3$OCH$_3$ and CH$_3$OCHO" (Radiative transfer modelling); "We then used the parameters (column density, source size, and kinetic temperatures) found from the non-LTE modelling of the methanol transitions (cite: Vastel2022) to compute the column densities of CH$_3$OCH$_3$ and CH$_3$OCHO using the CASSIS software." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Source sizes fixed first, then allowed to vary; V_LSR and line widths from Gaussian fits; Tex set to methanol kinetic temperatures of the three components  
  > "We fixed the source sizes and let the best-fit model converge around the $V_{LSR}$ line width for the Gaussian fits and the kinetic temperatures of methanol for the excitation temperatures of the three components." (Radiative transfer modelling); "We then let the source size vary." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: Fitted V_LSR -1.9, 3.0, 9.2 km/s and FWHM 2, 5, 4 km/s for the three components  
  > "The corresponding $V_{LSR}$ for the three components are -1.9, 3.0, and 9.2 km s$^{-1}$, and the FWHM values are 2, 5, and 4 km s$^{-1}$ respectively." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: Spectra extracted from the same ~0.4'' region as the 257.911 GHz CH3OCH3 line  
  > "we extracted the spectra from the same region as for the CH$_3$OCH$_3$ transition at 257.911 GHz, corresponding to $\sim$ 0.4$^{\prime\prime}$" (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium  
  > "We first performed a local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) analysis to estimate the CH$_3$OCH$_3$/CH$_3$OH and CH$_3$OCHO/CH$_3$OH abundance ratios." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - assumption: Excitation temperatures equal to methanol kinetic temperatures  
  > "and the kinetic temperatures of methanol for the excitation temperatures of the three components" (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - assumption: CH3OH column density from non-LTE modelling of its 13C isotopologue  
  > "The methanol column density was computed from non-LTE modelling using its $^{13}$C isotopologue (cite: Vastel2022)." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - fitting: CASSIS best-fit LTE model; fit statistic, priors and the definition of the quoted [min, max] ranges not stated  
  > "lists the results from the best fit obtained using all transitions in both continuum windows" (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - validation: Best-fit LTE model overplotted on observed CH3OCH3 and CH3OCHO spectra  
  > "show the best-fit LTE model (in red) obtained by simultaneously fitting all lines of CH$_3$OCH$_3$ and CH$_3$OCHO" (Radiative transfer modelling)
6. **Derive t-HCOOH, H2CCO and CH3O13CHO column densities and acetaldehyde upper limit**: Same LTE model (beam size, Tex, V_LSR, FWHM, filling factor) from CH3OCHO and CH3OCH3 applied to t-HCOOH and H2CCO; 12C/13C = 68 for the methyl formate isotopologue  
  > "Taking the three components with the same beam size, excitation temperature, and $V_{LSR}$ as derived from the methyl formate and dimethyl ether into account, we obtained the column densities quoted in Table" (Radiative transfer modelling); "The column densities can be constrained from the LTE modelling, again using the excitation temperature, filling factor, and FWHM as for CH$_3$OCH$_3$ and CH$_3$OCHO." (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: 12C/13C = 68  
  > "we used a $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C ratio of 68 (cite: Milam2005)" (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - assumption: Same source size, excitation temperature and V_LSR as the CH3OCHO/CH3OCH3 fit  
  > "With the same source size and excitation temperatures as for CH$_3$OCHO and CH$_3$OCH$_3$" (Radiative transfer modelling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Blended 246.106 and 247.514 GHz lines better reproduced once t-HCOOH is added; modelled 13C lines compatible with noise but detection not firm  
  > "The 246.106 and 247.514 GHz lines are now better reproduced by the LTE model." (Radiative transfer modelling); "we cannot conclude on a firm detection of the methyl formate isotopologue" (Radiative transfer modelling)
7. **Upper limits on N(C2H5CN) and N(C2H3CN)**: LTE column-density formula with an upper limit on integrated intensity W_u = sigma x deltaV x sqrt(DeltaV/deltaV) applied to the strongest expected line of each species  
  > "For the derivation, we assumed an LTE condition and used the following equation:" (Appendix: Upper limits); "The upper limit of the integrated intensity (in K km s$^{-1}$) was calculated from the following equation:" (Appendix: Upper limits)
   - software: CDMS database
   - parameter: Excitation temperature 80 K for all three components; source sizes 0.15, 0.13, 0.12''; DeltaV 2, 5, 4 km/s  
  > "Temperature (K) | 80 | 80 | 80" (Table 3); "For the calculations, we adopted the parameters from Table" (Appendix: Upper limits)
   - parameter: Partition function fit Q(T) = a x T^b to CDMS values: C2H5CN (a, b) = (7.2158, 1.4995); C2H3CN (a, b) = (0.95923, 2.0865)  
  > "For C$_2$H$_5$CN, ($a$, $b$)=(7.2158, 1.4995). For C$_2$H$_3$CN, ($a$, $b$)=(0.95923, 2.0865)." (Appendix: Upper limits)
   - assumption: LTE; T_BB = 2.73 K  
  > "For the derivation, we assumed an LTE condition and used the following equation:" (Appendix: Upper limits)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Observed spectra of three lines per species inspected; features present are offset from the three velocity components

## Qualitative findings

- iCOM and precursor emission is compact and encompasses both protostars, which are only 0.2'' (~28 au) apart  
  > "The emission from the detected iCOMs and their precursors is compact and encompasses both protostars" (abstract)
- PV and 2D analysis of CH3OCH3 show three spatial and velocity regions, two near 11B and one near 11A  
  > "A Position-Velocity and 2D analysis are performed on the strongest and uncontaminated CH$_3$OCH$_3$ transition and show three different spatial and velocity regions" (abstract)
- CH3OCH3 velocity field shows a northwest-southeast gradient centred between 11A and 11B at the systemic velocity  
  > "shows a clear northwest-southeast gradient centred in between 11A and 11B at the systemic velocity of the source" (Spatial distribution)
- Ketene emission is more extended than the other iCOMs and may trace warm gas streaming from the circumbinary disk  
  > "Therefore, ketene could be tracing a more extended region, likely the warm gas streaming in from the circumbinary disk, rather than the shocked regions at the end of the streamer." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 12m array alone used because the iCOM emission is compact  
  > "We used the 12m array (TM 1 and TM 2) due to the compact emission of the iCOMs transitions." (Observations)
- Why this method: Three-Gaussian fit and centroid analysis adopted assuming compact emission; centroid fitting gives astrometry better than the beam at high S/N  
  > "In other words, a centroid fitting to the compact molecular emission yields astrometry with a precision better than that of the spatial resolution in the case of a high S/N." (Spatial distribution)
- Alternative: Thermal desorption in a classical hot corino judged unlikely because emission does not peak on the protostars  
  > "The dust in between 11A and 11B could be warm enough for the methanol to sublimate, but the iCOM emission does not peak on the hot cores themselves, which seems to rule this hypothesis out." (Discussion)
- Alternative: External irradiation ruled out by the embedded location and high gas temperature  
  > "Therefore, although external irradiation could produce shifted emission of molecular species from a protostellar core like this (cite: Lindberg2012; Lindberg2015)" (Discussion)
- Alternative: Formamide candidate at 260.190 GHz not confirmed because companion transitions are not detected; ketene assigned instead  
  > "but it has not been detected at a 4$\sigma$ level (0.07 K)" (Line identification)
- Alternative: Dust opacity could affect the apparent spatial distribution of iCOMs  
  > "It is worth mentioning that the dust opacity could be a major actor in the observed apparent spatial distribution of iCOMs" (Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Angular resolution is insufficient to locate the iCOM emission within the binary  
  > "Considering the angular resolution of the present observations, we cannot determine the exact location of the iCOMs emission within the binary system." (Spatial distribution)
- Limitation (authors): CH3O13CHO not firmly detected, with an unexplained velocity shift  
  > "Considering the noise and the spectral resolution of our observations for this transition, we cannot conclude on a firm detection of the methyl formate isotopologue." (Radiative transfer modelling)
- Limitation (authors): rms too high to explain the CH3CHO non-detection  
  > "Our $rms$ is too high to be able to explain the non-detection of this species." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beam (~0.4'') is larger than the 0.2'' binary separation, so shock-versus-hot-corino conclusions rest on centroid offsets of ~20 au and on comparison with the methanol analysis
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Conclusions say H2CCO and t-HCOOH ratios are much lower than in other sources, while the Discussion says t-HCOOH is lower than L1157-B1 by ~3 and comparable to IRAS 16293 B
- Caveat (inferred, ours): LTE column-density ranges have no stated confidence definition and Tex is borrowed from methanol kinetic temperatures; the dust optical depth is not corrected for
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Several CH3OCH3, CH3OCHO and t-HCOOH lines are blended, so the fit depends on a joint model
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Ketene is detected in a single transition so its column density and extended-emission interpretation are weakly constrained
