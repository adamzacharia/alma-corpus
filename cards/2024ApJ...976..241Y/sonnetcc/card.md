## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ATOMS 3-mm and QUARKS 1.3-mm data**: ATOMS calibrated in CASA 5.6 and ACA+12-m jointly imaged with tclean (natural weighting, pblimit 0.2); QUARKS ACA and 12-m calibrated independently in CASA 6.5, then jointly imaged with tclean  
  > "The ACA and 12-m array data were jointly imaged and cleaned using natural weighting for pblimit = 0.2, in the CASA tclean task" (2 ALMA observations); "The ACA and 12-m data were independently calibrated in CASA 6.5, and then jointly imaged and cleaned using the CASA tclean task for both continuum images and line cubes." (2 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: ATOMS: natural weighting, pblimit = 0.2  
  > "using natural weighting for pblimit = 0.2, in the CASA tclean task" (2 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract the IRS 17 filament from the 3-mm continuum**: getsf extraction on the 3-mm map giving a single filament skeleton, footprint, centre, length, width, integrated flux  
  > "we obtained a single filament skeleton and its footprint" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum); "These include the center positions, length, width, and integrated flux." (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: Widths are half-power diameters taken directly from getsf profiles, mean of the median widths from both sides  
  > "refer to the mean values of the median widths obtained from both side by the" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum); "without any fitting, such as Gaussian or Plummer fitting" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive filament mass, line mass and density from 3-mm flux**: Optically thin dust mass M = S_int D^2 R_gd / (kappa B_nu(T)); line mass M/L; mean density assuming uniform cylinder  
  > "we can compute the mass of the IRS 17 filament using the expression" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum); "The line mass ($m_{\rm F}$) was computed as $m_{\rm F} = M_{\rm F}/L_{\rm F}\sim95.8$ $M_{\odot}$ $\rm pc^{-1}$." (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - parameter: R_gd = 100  
  > "the ratio of gas-to-dust, $R_{\rm gd}$, is assumed to be 100" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - parameter: kappa = 0.18 cm2/g at 3 mm  
  > "the dust opacity, $\kappa_\nu$, is taken as $0.18 \rm\,cm^{2}~g^{-1}$ at 3 mm" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - parameter: T_dust = 25 K  
  > "$T_{\rm dust}= \rm 25\,K$ is adopted for the IRS 17 filament" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - assumption: 3-mm emission optically thin and thermal dust  
  > "Assuming that 3-mm continuum emission is optically thin and arises primarily from thermal dust radiation" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - assumption: Uniform density distribution for the filament  
  > "assuming a uniform density distribution for the filament" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Line mass compared with Herschel values for Musca, Taurus B211/3 and Orion ISF  
  > "This line mass is around 2–3 times higher than those inferred from" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
4. **Identify fibers and condensations in the 1.3-mm continuum**: getsf applied to the 1.3-mm map for fibers and condensations; SExtractor added to recover missed condensations  
  > "algorithm was applied to the 1.3-mm dust continuum map" (3.2 Fibers and condensations in QUARKS 1.3-mm dust continuum); "To recover the missing condensations, another source extraction method, SExtractor (cite: 1996A&AS..117..393B; 2016zndo....804967B) was utilized" (3.2 Fibers and condensations in QUARKS 1.3-mm dust continuum)
   - software: getsf, SExtractor
   - parameter: Fiber criteria: peak flux >= 3 rms; length >= 3 x beam; aspect ratio >= 3  
  > "ii) the length of a fiber is at least 3 times the synthesized beam size at 1.3-mm, and iii) the aspect ratio is at least 3." (3.2 Fibers and condensations in QUARKS 1.3-mm dust continuum); "the peak flux of a fiber is detected at least at 3 $rms$ significance level" (3.2 Fibers and condensations in QUARKS 1.3-mm dust continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of the getsf output showed missed fibers/condensations, motivating SExtractor  
  > "However, upon a careful visual inspection, several dense fibers and condensations appear not be recognized by the" (3.2 Fibers and condensations in QUARKS 1.3-mm dust continuum)
5. **Measure gas kinematics of filament and fibers in H13CO+ (1-0)**: Moment maps (0, 1, 2) of the ATOMS H13CO+ (1-0) cube; velocity centroid and dispersion tabulated per filament/fiber  
  > "The velocity centroid and the velocity dispersion of both filament and the fibers are tabulated in Table" (3.2 Fibers and condensations in QUARKS 1.3-mm dust continuum); "we present the velocity dispersion (Moment 2) map of H$^{13}$CO$^{+}$ (1–0)" (3.3 Filament and fibers seen in molecular gas emission)
   - assumption: Thermal sound speed 0.3 km/s at 25 K used as reference for transonic motion  
  > "compared to the typical thermal sound speed of 0.3" (3.3 Filament and fibers seen in molecular gas emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with DCN velocity map from QUARKS and with N2H+ fiber dispersions of Hacar et al. 2018 in Orion ISF  
  > "these velocity components can also be observed in the velocity map of the DCN line" (3.3 Filament and fibers seen in molecular gas emission)
6. **Derive masses, line masses and densities of fibers and condensations**: Same optically thin dust mass equation at 1.3 mm; fiber line mass and mean density; condensation density for uniform sphere with n_c = 3M/(4 pi R^3 mu m_H)  
  > "we quantified the mass of fibers ($M_{\rm f}$) and condensations ($M_{\rm c}$) from the integrated flux at 1.3-mm" (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations); "assuming a uniform spherical density distribution, their number density can be inferred as" (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations)
   - parameter: kappa_1.3mm = 0.899 cm2/g; T = 25 K; mu = 2.8  
  > "given the corresponding dust opacity $\kappa_{\rm 1.3mm} = 0.899\,\mathrm{cm^2\,g^{-1}}$ (cite: 1994A&A...291..943O) and the temperature of 25 K" (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations); "the mean molecular weight $\mu=2.8$" (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations)
   - assumption: Masses may be underestimated (25 K too high for non-YSO condensations; flux filtering) or overestimated for protostellar condensations (up to 50 K)  
  > "Additionally, we cannot rule out over estimation of mass for condensations harboring protostars where temperatures could be as high as 50 K." (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Condensation masses compared with TUKH122 and NGC 2071 IR values  
  > "These results for condensations are comparable to values derived in other intermediate-mass star-forming regions" (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations)
7. **Classify fertile fibers and measure condensation spacing**: Fibers with at least three condensations are fertile; minimum-spanning tree gives separations between condensations within fertile fibers  
  > "In the IRS 17 filament, four fibers (namely, f 1 and f 5–7) are classified as fertile, while the rest are considered sterile." (3.2.2 Classification of fertile fibers); "The MST approach (e.g. (cite: 2019A&A...629A.135D)) determines the shortest distances that can possibly connect each of the objects in a given field." (3.2.2 Classification of fertile fibers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
8. **Assess gravitational stability of filament and fibers (critical line mass)**: Compare observed line mass with thermal and turbulent critical line mass m_crit = 2 sigma_eff^2 / G for isothermal infinite cylinders  
  > "Filaments and fibers with an observed line mass greater than $m_{\rm crit}$ (supercritical) are expected to collapse under their own gravity" (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
   - parameter: Thermal m_crit ~41.5 Msun/pc at T = 25 K; turbulent m_crit from H13CO+ velocity dispersion  
  > "This yields $m_{\rm crit}\sim$ 41.5 $M_{\odot}$ $\rm pc^{-1}$." (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
   - assumption: Isothermal infinite cylinders; gas temperature equal to dust temperature 25 K; magnetic fields ignored  
  > "Here, we assume it to be the same as the average dust temperature of $T=$ 25 K." (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers); "our analysis above ignores the magnetic fields, as such information is not available" (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
   - assumption: At fiber scales sigma_tot ~ c_s via the sigma-L relation, so turbulent term negligible  
  > "Thus, for the length scale of the IRS 17 fibers, the role of turbulence would be negligible." (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked 10 K versus 25 K against the observed presence or absence of condensations in f2 and f4  
  > "adopting a temperature of 10 K would render fibers f 2 and f 4 supercritical, which contradicts their observed nature" (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
9. **Assess virial state of condensations**: Virial parameter alpha_vir = 5 sigma_eff^2 R_c / (G M_c), with thermal sound speed only; alpha_vir <= 2 means collapse  
  > "The subvirial cores with $\alpha_{\rm vir}\leq2$ will collapse to form stars" (3.5 Stability of condensations); "Hence, we consider only the thermal motion and replace $\sigma_{\rm eff}$ by the sound speed of gas, $c_{\rm s}$." (3.5 Stability of condensations)
   - parameter: c_s = 0.3 km/s at 25 K for all condensations  
  > "Assuming the same $c_{\rm s}$ value (i.e., $0.3\,\rm km\,s^{-1}$) for all condensations in the IRS 17 filament at a temperature of 25 K" (3.5 Stability of condensations)
   - assumption: Turbulence negligible at condensation scales; temperature range 10-50 K considered for non-YSO and YSO-hosting condensations  
  > "The former case will make the virial parameters about 6 times lower than the current values" (3.5 Stability of condensations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Association of condensations with Class I/0 YSOs and SiO/CO outflows as independent support  
  > "The association of these YSOs and outflows lends strong support to our understanding that these condensations are potential seeds of star formation." (3.5 Stability of condensations)
10. **Compare fertile-fiber condensation spacing with cylinder fragmentation theory**: Take mean condensation density in each fiber as central density, compute isothermal scale height H = c_s (4 pi G rho_c)^-1/2 and predicted lambda_max = 22 H, compare with MST separations  
  > "within each fiber as its central density" (3.6 Fragmentation of fibers); "the separation of dense condensations within fibers can be predicted from $\lambda_{\rm max}=22H$" (3.6 Fragmentation of fibers)
   - parameter: T = 25 K; median fiber radius ~700 au so R >> H  
  > "yielding $H\sim60\,\rm au$ at a temperature of 25 K" (3.6 Fragmentation of fibers)
   - assumption: Fibers are isothermal, hydrostatic gas cylinders  
  > "Hence, assuming the fibers as isothermal and hydrostatic gas cylinders" (3.6 Fragmentation of fibers)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Predicted lambda_max compared with observed separation of 900-2300 au  
  > "This agrees with the observed typical separation between dense condensations within fertile fibers" (3.6 Fragmentation of fibers)

## Qualitative findings

- The IRS 17 filament has a complex multi-component velocity field, but individual fibers are velocity coherent and fall into three bundles  
  > "Furthermore, the detected fibers are seen to be in three bundles with similar kinematics" (3.3 Filament and fibers seen in molecular gas emission)
- The 3-mm cores in the filament are not periodically spaced  
  > "these dense cores within the filament do not exhibit a periodic chain-like distribution" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: QUARKS 1.3-mm complements ATOMS 3-mm with higher angular resolution; H13CO+ traces relatively dense gas  
  > "complementing the ATOMS survey with higher angular resolution" (2 ALMA observations); "allows us to trace relatively dense gas compared to CO and its isotopologue lines" (3.3 Filament and fibers seen in molecular gas emission)
- Why this method: getsf chosen for handling uneven noise, separating blended sources/filaments and extracting extended emission  
  > "due to its effectiveness in handling uneven noise background, separating blended sources/filaments, and extracting extended emission features" (3.1 IRS 17 filament seen in ATOMS 3-mm continuum)
- Alternative: A dust temperature of 10 K rejected for the stability analysis because it would make fibers f2 and f4 supercritical despite having no condensations  
  > "adopting a temperature of 10 K would render fibers f 2 and f 4 supercritical, which contradicts their observed nature since no associated condensations are present" (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Derived masses may be biased by the assumed 25 K temperature and by missing flux  
  > "the high-resolution interferometric observations could have filtered out diffuse, low-density emission" (3.2.1 Derived parameters of fibers and condensations)
- Limitation (authors): No matching-resolution dense-gas line data for turbulence in condensations  
  > "However, we lack molecular line data" (3.5 Stability of condensations)
- Limitation (authors): Magnetic fields ignored  
  > "our analysis above ignores the magnetic fields, as such information is not available" (3.4 Stability of the IRS 17 filament and fibers)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single assumed dust temperature (25 K) for filament, fibers and condensations drives all masses, line masses, densities and virial parameters, and no uncertainty on T is propagated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Condensation count differs between Sect. 3.2 (30) and abstract/summary (29)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Statistical significance of periodic spacing from only four fertile fibers with a few condensations each is not tested against random or non-periodic spacing
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Velocity dispersion from H13CO+ at 1.8'' x 1.4'' is mismatched in resolution to 0.34'' fibers and condensations, so turbulent support at fiber scale is inferred from a scaling relation rather than measured
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fiber identification from continuum only with extraction parameters unstated, so reproducibility of the fiber and condensation lists is uncertain
