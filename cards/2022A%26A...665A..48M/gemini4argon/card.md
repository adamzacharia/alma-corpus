## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA CO(1-0) spectral cube and continuum, and generate moment maps**: Reimage calibrated ALMA visibilities using tclean to produce spectral datacubes at 30 km/s and 80 km/s resolution, and compute moment 0, 1, and 2 maps at reference frequency 99.759 GHz.  
  > "The calibrated ALMA data were reimaged using the latest version of CASA at the time of writing (CASA 6.3), using the `tclean` task to produce a spectral datacube for the CO(1-0) at 30 km s$^{-1}$ resolution" (ALMA CO(1-0) analysis); "The reference frequency chosen to make the moment maps was 99.759 GHz, corresponding to the CO(1-0) line being redshifted to $z=0.1555$." (ALMA CO(1-0) analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: natural weighting; spectral resolution 30 km/s (and 80 km/s for channel maps); reference frequency 99.759 GHz  
  > "For all imaging, we use natural weighting of the visibilities." (ALMA CO(1-0) analysis); "The reference frequency chosen to make the moment maps was 99.759 GHz, corresponding to the CO(1-0) line being redshifted to $z=0.1555$." (ALMA CO(1-0) analysis)
   - assumption: z = 0.1555; no correction for beam smearing in moment maps  
  > "We note that the moment maps are not corrected for beam smearing effects, as they are largely being used to gauge the phenomenological behaviour of the cold gas." (ALMA CO(1-0) analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract regional CO(1-0) spectra, fit velocity profiles, and derive molecular gas masses**: Extract CO(1-0) spectra from four regions of interest (total detection, central BCG region, south-western spur, western spur), fit Gaussian profiles to the spectra, and convert CO luminosities to molecular gas masses.  
  > "we chose several regions of interest for more detailed spectral analysis: the total CO(1-0) detection, the central region containing the BCG, the south-western spur also traced by H$\alpha$, and the western spur" (Velocity structure in the BCG); "Following, for example, (cite: Narayanan2012) or (cite: Bolatto2013), we assume a standard CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor $\alpha_{CO} = M_{\rm mol} / L_{\rm CO} = 3.6~\rm M_{\odot}/L_{\odot}$, finding molecular gas masses of $M_{\rm mol} \approx [3.6, 1.4, 0.63, 0.16]\times 10^9~\rm M_{\odot}$" (Velocity structure in the BCG)
   - parameter: Four regions of interest: total, central BCG, south-western spur, western spur  
  > "we chose several regions of interest for more detailed spectral analysis: the total CO(1-0) detection, the central region containing the BCG, the south-western spur also traced by H$\alpha$, and the western spur" (Velocity structure in the BCG)
   - assumption: CO-to-H2 conversion factor alpha_CO = M_mol / L_CO = 3.6 M_sun/L_sun  
  > "we assume a standard CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor $\alpha_{CO} = M_{\rm mol} / L_{\rm CO} = 3.6~\rm M_{\odot}/L_{\odot}$" (Velocity structure in the BCG)
   - fitting: Two Gaussian profiles fitted to the bimodal CO(1-0) spectrum  
  > "we discount this possibility as the emission is well-described by the fitting of two Gaussian profiles, with a reduced $\chi^2=1.05$." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
   - validation: Reduced chi^2 = 1.05 for the two-Gaussian fit, discounting foreground CO absorption  
  > "we discount this possibility as the emission is well-described by the fitting of two Gaussian profiles, with a reduced $\chi^2=1.05$." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
3. **Analyze Chandra X-ray surface brightness, thermodynamic profiles, AGN upper limits, and cavity toy model**: Fit 2D elliptical profiles and 2D beta-model to 0.5-7.0 keV flux map, perform unsharp masking, extract spectra in contour and radial bins to fit MEKAL/MKCFLOW models in XSPEC and JACO, compute AGN point source upper limits with celldetect/aprates, and simulate 3D beta-model with empty spheres along the line of sight.  
  > "We fitted a more general elliptical model to the 2$\times$2 binned image described previously, using Sherpa (cite: 2001SPIE.4477...76F)." (Chandra X-ray analysis); "We fit the projected temperature and metallicity of the annuli and the resultant temperature, deprojected electron density profile, and entropy parameter profile using the joint analysis of cluster observations (JACO) code (cite: 2007ApJ...664..162M)" (Chandra X-ray analysis); "Using the CIAO *aprates* task we calculated a 3$\sigma$ upper limit on the counts expected on an AGN point source." (Limits on the X-ray luminosity of the AGN)
   - software: CIAO, IRAF, Sherpa, contbin, XSPEC, JACO
   - parameter: N_H = 7.4 x 10^20 cm^-2; min 2500 net counts per radial annulus; Gaussian smoothing 1 pixel (0.984'') and 10 pixels (9.84'') for unsharp mask  
  > "X-ray spectra were extracted using *specextract* in the energy range 0.3-11.0 keV over circular annuli centred at the X-ray peak with at least 2500 net counts." (Chandra X-ray analysis); "For all spectral fits, the Galactic foreground extinction is fixed at N$_H$ = 7.4$\times 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Chandra X-ray analysis)
   - assumption: Projected temperature approximately equal to deprojected temperature; cooling time threshold 7.7 x 10^9 yr; fully ionised plasma n = 2.3 n_H  
  > "In this procedure we have made the approximation that the projected temperature is approximately equal to the deprojected temperature." (Chandra X-ray analysis); "We assume a fully ionised plasma such that the total number density n = $2.3n_H$." (Radio bubble limits)
   - fitting: Cash statistic in Sherpa and XSPEC; Bayesian background-marginalised posterior probability distribution in aprates  
  > "We used the Cash statistic (cite: 1979ApJ...228..939C) in the fitting, and allowed all the parameters to vary (including the centre and orientation angle)." (Chandra X-ray analysis); "We fit the unbinned spectral data with the Cash statistics (cite: 1979ApJ...228..939C) implementation in XSPEC (modified c-stat)." (Chandra X-ray analysis)
   - validation: Comparison of bolometric luminosity with XMM-Newton value and R_500 with literature  
  > "This luminosity is reasonably consistent with the bolometric luminosity calculated from *XMM-Newton* ($L_\textsc{x} = 21.06 \pm 0.07 \times 10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$ within $R_{500}$ = 1155.3 $\pm$ 4 kpc; (cite: 2009AA...498..361P))" (Radio bubble limits)
4. **Measure H-alpha luminosity, optical emission-line kinematics, and BPT line ratios from SOAR data**: Measure continuum-subtracted H-alpha+[NII] luminosity inside an 8'' aperture, extract 1D spectra in 3-pixel (0.45'') bins along the Goodman slit, fit Gaussian profiles and deblend H-alpha+[NII] using IRAF splot, and cross-correlate stellar continuum with an SDSS template using fxcor.  
  > "We calculated a total H$\alpha$+\[NII\] luminosity of $189\pm6\times10^{40}$ erg s$^{-1}$ for the cluster inside a circular aperture with a radius of 8$\ensuremath{''}$ (21.5 kpc)" (SOAR H\alpha imaging and spectral observations); "We use a Gaussian profile to fit each of the lines and the *splot* bootstrap resampling (100 realisations) method to compute errors on the Gaussian profile fits." (SOAR analysis); "For the H$\alpha$ + \[NII\] complex, we deblend the features by simultaneously fitting the H$\alpha$ line with the two \[NII\] lines using *deblend* in *splot*." (Goodman line ratios)
   - software: IRAF
   - parameter: 8'' radius aperture for H-alpha photometry; 17 regions of 3 pixels (0.45'') each for spectroscopy; instrumental velocity width 294 km/s  
  > "Based on the width of single unsaturated FeAr lines in the calibration spectra, the instrumental line velocity width of the SOAR/Goodman setup used was 294 km s$^{-1}$, consistent with expectations." (SOAR analysis); "We extracted 17 regions of 3 pixels each over the extended H$\alpha$ + \[NII\] region using the 2D calibrated spectral image discussed earlier." (Goodman line ratios)
   - assumption: E(B-V) = 0.1491, A_V/E(B-V) = 3.1; [NII] 6584 / [NII] 6548 ratio fixed to 3  
  > "To correct the image from foreground Galactic extinction we used an E(B-V) = 0.1491 and assumed A$_V$/E(B-V) = 3.1 (cite: 1998ApJ...500..525S)." (SOAR H\alpha imaging and spectral observations); "we used the line measurements for the \[NII\] $\lambda$ 6548 line and multiplied it by a factor of three (the constant \[NII\] line ratio fixed by atomic physics) to determine the \[NII\] $\lambda$ 6584 value" (Goodman line ratios)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fitting with bootstrap resampling (100 realisations) in splot; Fourier cross-correlation in fxcor with 1000 random sections  
  > "We use a Gaussian profile to fit each of the lines and the *splot* bootstrap resampling (100 realisations) method to compute errors on the Gaussian profile fits." (SOAR analysis); "The correlation result was based on an emission-line-free range between 6100-7100 Å and the error is based on results from 1000 randomly selected sections 200 Å wide." (SOAR analysis)
   - validation: Verified night sky line centres within 1 Angstrom of wavelength solution  
  > "We verified the centres of the night sky lines in the object frames ($\lambda\lambda$5577, 5889, 6300, 6363, 6863, 6923, 7276, 7316, and 7340 Å) were within 1 Å of our wavelength solution." (SOAR analysis)
5. **Measure low-frequency radio continuum fluxes, spectral index, and residual structures**: Measure integrated flux densities from VLA 1.4 GHz, GMRT 610 MHz, and VLITE 338 MHz observations, combine with archival survey fluxes to determine the spectral index, and subtract a 2D Gaussian model from the GMRT image to inspect residuals.  
  > "The dominant central 610 MHz radio source (Figure [5](#fig:radio), middle) can be accurately modelled by a single 2D Gaussian of dimensions $6.8\ensuremath{''} \times4.6\ensuremath{''}$ and PA $8.2\ensuremath{^\circ}$" (Low-frequency radio analysis); "Taken together with the archival low-frequency radio observations, we find a median spectral index $\alpha=-1.02$ (where $S \propto \nu^\alpha$ (see Figure [6](#fig:radio_spec)), indicative of ageing in the electron population." (Low-frequency radio analysis); "Subtracting the fit 2D Gaussian from the GMRT 610 MHz image provides a clear view of the fainter substructures directly surrounding the dominant central source." (Low-frequency radio analysis)
   - software: CASA, EVLA Pipeline, SPAM
   - parameter: 2D Gaussian model dimensions 6.8'' x 4.6'', PA 8.2 deg at 610 MHz; 92 GHz ALMA point excluded from spectral index fit  
  > "The dominant central 610 MHz radio source (Figure [5](#fig:radio), middle) can be accurately modelled by a single 2D Gaussian of dimensions $6.8\ensuremath{''} \times4.6\ensuremath{''}$ and PA $8.2\ensuremath{^\circ}$" (Low-frequency radio analysis); "We note that we have excluded the 92 GHz continuum data point provided by ALMA, which at 4.05 mJy $\pm 0.01$ mJy lies above the power law extrapolation." (Low-frequency radio analysis)
   - assumption: Power law spectrum S proportional to nu^alpha  
  > "we find a median spectral index $\alpha=-1.02$ (where $S \propto \nu^\alpha$ (see Figure [6](#fig:radio_spec)), indicative of ageing in the electron population." (Low-frequency radio analysis)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to 610 MHz central source  
  > "The dominant central 610 MHz radio source (Figure [5](#fig:radio), middle) can be accurately modelled by a single 2D Gaussian of dimensions $6.8\ensuremath{''} \times4.6\ensuremath{''}$ and PA $8.2\ensuremath{^\circ}$" (Low-frequency radio analysis)
   - validation: Comparison of positive residuals against mirrored negative sidelobe pairs and ALMA CO(1-0) emission  
  > "In the residual image in Figure [5](#fig:radio) (right panel), it is straightforward to identify two nearby pairs of negative sidelobes." ## Qualitative findings

- Chandra imaging does not reveal bilateral X-ray cavities in the ICM despite the cluster hosting a powerful radio source and having a strong cool core.  
  > "the *Chandra* imaging observations surprisingly do not reveal the bi-lateral X-ray cavities one might expect to see in the intracluster medium (ICM) of an extreme cool core hosting a powerful radio source" (abstract)
- ALMA CO(1-0) observations reveal a large molecular gas reservoir co-spatial with the extended H-alpha emission, with a bimodal velocity distribution in the core and a south-western spur.  
  > "Fig. [13](#fig:Halpha_and_CO) (upper panel) shows the molecular gas traced by CO emission to be co-spatial with the warmer gas traced by H$\alpha$ emission" (Velocity structure in the BCG); "The second row of Fig. [14](#fig:alma_co_regions) reveal there is a significant bimodality to the molecular gas velocity distribution for the CO(1-0) emission superposed on the BCG, while the brightest singular offset feature corresponds to the south-west extension tracing the H$\alpha$ spur (third row)." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
- The radio, mm, and optical AGN peaks are co-located but offset by 3.5'' (9.4 kpc) to the east of the X-ray peak due to sloshing.  
  > "Notably, the radio, millimetre-wave, and optical data all locate the AGN 3.5$''$ (9.4 kpc projected separation) to the east of the X-ray peak (see Figure [5](#fig:radio) & [7](#fig:radio_overlays))." (Low-frequency radio analysis)
- The cluster core has a strongly peaked metallicity profile and low central entropy, indicating the cool core remains intact and sloshing near the core is likely subsonic.  
  > "We argue that the sloshing near the core is likely subsonic, as expected, given the co-location of the H$\alpha$, CO(1-0), radio continuum, and stellar emission peaks and their proximity to the X-ray peak. Further, the X-ray emission from the core is strongly concentrated, as is the distribution of metals, indicating the cool core remains largely intact." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 3 observations were performed to probe the location and dynamics of cold molecular gas via CO(1-0) and compare with optical H-alpha, radio, and X-ray structures.  
  > "We use millimetre-wave observations performed by ALMA on December 24, 2018, to probe the location and dynamics of the molecular gas traced by the lowest transition of the dominant form of carbon monoxide, CO(1-0)" (ALMA Band 3 observations); "For comparison with the optical data, we turn to the view ALMA provides of the cold molecular gas, as traced by CO(1-0)." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- Alternative: Foreground CO(1-0) absorption causing the central dip in the CO spectrum was considered and discounted because a two-Gaussian emission fit yields reduced chi^2 = 1.05 and known CO absorption dips are only a few percent against continuum.  
  > "While the possibility exists that the observed dip could be due to CO(1-0) absorption from foreground molecular gas, we discount this possibility as the emission is well-described by the fitting of two Gaussian profiles, with a reduced $\chi^2=1.05$." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
- Alternative: A rotating disk or bilateral bubbles pushing gas to explain the optical velocity gradient were rejected because the velocity structure is one-sided and lacks a two-peaked profile.  
  > "The optical velocity structure does not appear to be due to a rotating disk, since it is one-sided and the disk would have to be off-centre from the peak of the emission, even though the emission peaks are aligned." (Velocity structure in the BCG); "Additionally, the optical emission is inconsistent with a pair of bubbles pushing the emission-line gas in front of each one because that would produce a profile with two peaks if the gas is otherwise optically thin." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
- Alternative: Suppression of existing X-ray cavities by strong sloshing was rejected because timescales are >= 0.5 Gyr, no radio mini-halo is observed, and the low-entropy, metal-peaked cool core remains intact.  
  > "The lack of a radio mini-halo, the compact distribution of metals in the core, and the strong, luminous, low-entropy cool core all indicate the sloshing near the core is too weak to significantly disrupt any putative bubbles" (Summary and conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Moment maps of CO(1-0) are not corrected for beam smearing effects.  
  > "We note that the moment maps are not corrected for beam smearing effects, as they are largely being used to gauge the phenomenological behaviour of the cold gas." (ALMA CO(1-0) analysis)
- Limitation (authors): Bright central AGN causes dynamic range limitations and residual sidelobes in both VLA and GMRT radio images.  
  > "Due to dynamic range limitations ($\sim 2500$ in this image), residual sidelobes from the strong central radio source in RXC J2014.8-2430 are a dominant contribution to the central background noise, raising the local noise by a factor of two." (GMRT radio observations); "However, the bright central AGN in both of the radio images leads to dynamic range issues, so that much of the structure left after modelling the sources could be dominated by residuals due to imperfect phase calibration." (Radio bubble limits)
- Limitation (authors): Cannot distinguish whether the optical velocity gradient indicates gas infall or outflow, and only radial velocities are measured rather than true 3D sloshing speeds.  
  > "However, we cannot distinguish whether the velocity gradient is indicative of gas infall or outflow." (Velocity structure in the BCG); "Since we only measure radial velocities of the line, the true 3D sloshing speed could be somewhat higher." (Velocity structure in the BCG)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA imaging details (synthesized beam, continuum subtraction, masking, and exact extraction apertures for the four regions) are omitted from the text.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Molecular gas masses rely on a single assumed standard Galactic CO-to-H2 conversion factor without uncertainty propagation.
