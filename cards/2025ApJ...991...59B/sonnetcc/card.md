## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify 1.2 mm continuum sources**: Sources identified from 10 sigma contours of the 1.2 mm continuum image; two fainter sources (E, F) added because they coincide with molecular line peaks  
  > "Individual sources are identified based on the $10\sigma$ contours, where $\sigma$ is the continuum image rms of $1.01 \times 10^{-4}$ Jy beam$^{-1}$." (3.1 1.2 mm Continuum Emission and Source Identification); "we also identify two fainter continuum sources that are associated with the molecular line emission peaks" (3.1 1.2 mm Continuum Emission and Source Identification)
   - parameter: Threshold 10 sigma with sigma = 1.01e-4 Jy/beam  
  > "Individual sources are identified based on the $10\sigma$ contours" (3.1 1.2 mm Continuum Emission and Source Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectra and identify spectral lines**: Mean spectrum inside the half-maximum 1.2 mm contour of each source; initial line identification with CASA imview and Splatalogue; detection criteria in channels above sigma  
  > "For each source, the spectra were extracted from the data cubes as the mean of the emission enclosed by the half-maximum 1.2 mm continuum emission contour." (3.2 Spectral Analysis); "We performed the initial spectral line identification using the CASA task" (3.2.1 Line Identification); "which uses the NRAO spectral line database Splatalogue." (3.2.1 Line Identification)
   - software: CASA imview, Splatalogue, CDMS, JPL catalog
   - parameter: Detection: three consecutive channels at least 3 sigma; tentative: four channels above 2 sigma with at least one above 3 sigma; sigma per source and spectral window by sigma clipping  
  > "To consider a line detected, three frequency channels in a row must be at least three times the noise level" (3.2.1 Line Identification); "using a sigma clipping analysis" (3.2.1 Line Identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Make integrated-intensity and velocity maps**: Moment 0 maps integrated over the channels with emission (multi-transition maps for CH3OH, SO2, CH3CN, 33SO); moment 1 maps with a 4 sigma pixel mask  
  > "The maps were created by integrating over a single transition using channels where emission was present" (3.2.2 Spatial Distribution of the Molecular Line Emission); "use a pixel mask where any pixel with intensity below $4\sigma$ in the corresponding integrated intensity map is excluded from the velocity map" (Appendix C Molecular Gas Kinematics)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Rotational diagram estimates of Trot and N**: Linear fit of ln(Nu/gu) versus Eu for lines fit with a single Gaussian; used for CH3OH (sources A, C, D, E) and SO2 (source A) to seed XCLASS  
  > "Multiple methanol transitions are available for sources A, C, D, E; only source A has a sufficient number of the SO$_2$ transitions suitable for the analysis." (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis); "In this form, we can fit a linear function to our data points and get the rotational temperature and column density from the slope and y-intercept, respectively." (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - parameter: Only lines fit by a single Gaussian (no blends or multi-peaked lines); Smu^2, Eu and partition function from CDMS, partition function interpolated to the fitted temperature  
  > "We select only lines that can be fit with a single Gaussian function to get the integrated line intensities" (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis); "we interpolate the partition function values for the given temperatures in CDMS" (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - parameter: Three SO2 transitions (Eu < 100 K and log(S mu^2) > 1) excluded to avoid opacity effects  
  > "Three SO$_2$ transitions are excluded from the fit to avoid possible opacity effects." (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - assumption: LTE and optically thin lines  
  > "The rotational diagram analysis assumes that the gas is in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and that the lines are optically thin" (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - fitting: Linear fit to rotational diagram; fit method details not stated  
  > "we can fit a linear function to our data points" (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Full XCLASS spectral modelling with Monte Carlo errors**: 1D radiative transfer (LTE or non-LTE with RADEX extensions) fitting of core/foreground components; stepwise fitting (multi-transition molecules first, then all molecules with fixed Trot); upper limits for undetected species; uncertainties by Monte Carlo noise resampling  
  > "XCLASS models a given spectrum by solving the radiative transfer equation for a one-dimensional object in either local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) or non-LTE based on user input." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling); "First, we fit only molecules that have multiple transitions detected, allowing the temperature to be determined." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - software: XCLASS, RADEX
   - parameter: Free parameters per component: source size, Trot, column density, line width, velocity offset; single-transition species use Trot fixed to the CH3OH (or SO2 for S-bearing) temperature  
  > "We instead fix their rotational temperatures to the CH$_3$OH temperature from the initial fit or, for SO-bearing species, the SO$_2$ temperature if it was included in the initial run." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - parameter: Hydrogen recombination lines in source A: electron temperature fixed to 15000 K  
  > "For the hydrogen recombination lines, we fixed the electron temperature to 15000 K" (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - parameter: Non-LTE (sources C, D, E): H2 collision partner, A/E CH3OH ratio fixed at one, collision partner density free  
  > "For the non-LTE case, the collision partner was H$_2$, the ratio of A and E CH$_3$OH was fixed at one, and the collision partner density was fit as a free parameter." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - parameter: Upper limits: Trot, line width and velocity offset set to CH3OH values, column density free  
  > "To determine column density upper limits for a given molecule with XCLASS, we set its temperature, line width, and velocity offset to that of CH$_3$OH and their column density is allowed to vary." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - parameter: Monte Carlo noise resampling with 300 iterations; error = standard deviation of best-fit results  
  > "We perform 300 iterations to obtain a sufficiently large number of parameter sets with the best fit parameters having a well-sampled Gaussian distribution." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling); "The final parameter errors are taken to be the standard deviation of the best fit results from these 300 runs." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - assumption: Beam-filling source for single-component fits; for two components one is beam filling and the other sub-beam (source size and column density are degenerate)  
  > "Due to a degeneracy between source size and column density, we assume that the source is beam filling when a single component is present." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling); "For two components, one is beam filling and the other is smaller than the beam." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - assumption: Initial single core component in LTE; second component or non-LTE only if the fit is unsatisfactory  
  > "We assume that they can be well represented by a single core component in LTE." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - fitting: Least-squares-type optimiser, priors and likelihood not stated; XCLASS fit started from rotational diagram values  
  > "Using the rotational temperatures and column densities from the rotational diagram analysis as initial estimates, we perform a full spectral modeling analysis" (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
   - validation: Alternative model setups tested (two components, non-LTE) and parameter errors from Monte Carlo resampling  
  > "We estimate the uncertainties of the parameters obtained in the XCLASS analysis using Monte Carlo noise resampling." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling); "We tested a two CH$_3$OH component run with XCLASS with a core in LTE and a foreground in non-LTE." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
6. **Derive H2 column density, gas mass, size and density**: Kauffmann et al. (2008) dust-continuum formulae with 870 um intensities (sources A, C, D) or 1.2 mm intensities (source E); mean intensity within the 50% contour of the 1.2 mm peak; deconvolved effective sizes; n(H2) = N(H2)/deconvolved size  
  > "For sources A, C, and D we use the archival 870 $\mu$m continuum image to determine $I_{\rm \nu}^{\rm beam}$." (3.3 H2 Column Density); "We instead use the 1.2 mm continuum emission to estimate $I_{\rm \nu}^{\rm beam}$ for this source." ## Qualitative findings

- N 160A–mm A is a bona fide hot core, the eighth in the LMC  
  > "This marks the eighth detection of a hot core in the LMC." (3.3 H2 Column Density)
- Source A is the most chemically rich source, with CH3CN detected only toward it  
  > "Another COM, methyl cyanide (CH$_3$CN) is associated with the brightest source, N 160A–mm A, the most chemically rich source in the field." (abstract)
- Sources C, D, E have cold CH3OH (Trot < 30 K)  
  > "Our results indicate that sources C, D, and E are associated with cold methanol" (3.2.3 Rotational Diagram Analysis)
- Source A has the highest abundances of all detected species except CS; N-bearing species less abundant than S-bearing  
  > "Newly identified hot core N 160A–mm A has the highest abundance of CH$_3$OH and all the other detected species except CS" (4.2 Molecular Abundances in N 160A–mm); "the abundances of N-bearing species are lower than those of S-bearing species" (4.2 Molecular Abundances in N 160A–mm)
- SiO and S-bearing species point to shocks toward source A; broadest lines there  
  > "The detection of SiO toward source A points to the presence of shocks." (3.2.5 Kinematics)
- Galactic hot cores show higher COM abundances than metallicity-scaled LMC and SMC hot cores  
  > "with the Galactic cores having higher abundances than the LMC and SMC" (4.3 Hot Core Abundances)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Project aimed at identifying hot cores in LMC fields with YSOs, masers and SO emission to enlarge the small known sample  
  > "One of the goals of the project is to identify hot cores, expanding a very small sample of known hot cores in the LMC." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: 870 um continuum used for N(H2) because dust thermal emission dominates there; CH3OH Trot used for all species in source A for consistency with other sources  
  > "At this wavelength, it is expected that the total continuum emission is from dust thermal emission, with little to no contribution from the free-free emission." (3.3 H2 Column Density); "We choose the CH$_3$OH temperature over the CH$_3$CN temperature for source A for the sake of consistency over the sample" (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
- Alternative: Two SO2 components and non-LTE SO2 tested for source A; best was two LTE core components (one sub-beam 0.4 arcsec, one beam filling)  
  > "so we tested two components as well as non-LTE SO$_2$." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
- Alternative: Two-component CH3OH (LTE core plus non-LTE foreground) tested for source C, no significant improvement  
  > "The addition of a second component did not significantly improve the results indicating that a single core component model provides a good fit to the observed spectra." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
- Alternative: CH3CN temperature not used for the common temperature because its detections are tentative  
  > "because the CH$_3$CN detections are tentative and therefore the temperature determination is less reliable" (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
- How they decided it was enough: 300 Monte Carlo iterations judged sufficient for well-sampled Gaussian parameter distributions  
  > "We perform 300 iterations to obtain a sufficiently large number of parameter sets with the best fit parameters having a well-sampled Gaussian distribution." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Kinematics not analysed in detail  
  > "A detailed analysis of the kinematics in N 160A–mm is out of scope of this paper." (3.2.5 Kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): CH3CN temperature difference may come from tentatively detected lines or sub-thermal excitation  
  > "however, some of the difference may be caused by the CH$_3$CN temperature determination being based on tentatively detected lines." (3.2.4 Full Spectral Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Sources E and F at the field edge with higher noise; B and F could not be analysed  
  > "Sources E and F are located toward the edge of the field of view and are therefore in an area with higher noise." (3.2.2 Spatial Distribution of the Molecular Line Emission)
- Limitation (authors): Origin of cold CH3OH emission uncertain  
  > "The origin of the cold CH$_3$OH emission is uncertain." (5 Summary and Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The abstract defines hot cores as >=1e6 cm^-3, but n(H2) for source A is 6.3e5 cm^-3; the classification leans on temperature and COMs
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust temperature for N(H2) is set to the CH3OH rotational temperature, so N(H2) and every abundance depend on that gas-dust coupling assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single-component beam-filling assumption and a source size/column density degeneracy affect the column densities
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CH3CN (the second COM) is only tentatively detected and gives a ~84 K temperature
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abundances use the mean intensity within the 50% contour but spectra and N(H2) come from different wavelengths and smoothed beams
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Metallicity scaling of LMC/SMC abundances is a simple linear factor; Galactic comparison uses single-dish data at possibly different physical scales
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Environmental explanation (LMC-SMC interaction) rests on a single source and is speculative
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Key XCLASS extensions are private, limiting independent reproduction
