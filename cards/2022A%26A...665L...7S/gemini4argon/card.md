## Analysis recipe

1. **Search for galaxy overdensities at z > 3**: Map the distance to the fifth nearest neighbor (Σ_5th) for i- and Ks-detected sources at z_phot > 3 in the COSMOS2020 catalog using GALCLUSTER  
  > "Using GALCLUSTER, which is publicly available software designed to automatically search for galaxy overdensities, we mapped the distance of the fifth neighborhood $\Sigma_{5th}$ for $i$- and $K_s$-detected sources (${\rm S/N}_{i}>5$ & $K_s<25$ AB mag) at $z_{\rm phot}>3$ in the COSMOS2020 catalog (cite: Weaver2022COSMOS2020)." (Selection)
   - software: GALCLUSTER
   - parameter: S/N_i > 5, K_s < 25 AB mag, z_phot > 3, 5th nearest neighbor  
  > "we mapped the distance of the fifth neighborhood $\Sigma_{5th}$ for $i$- and $K_s$-detected sources (${\rm S/N}_{i}>5$ & $K_s<25$ AB mag) at $z_{\rm phot}>3$ in the COSMOS2020 catalog (cite: Weaver2022COSMOS2020)." (Selection)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure Optical/NIR photometry, photometric redshifts, and stellar masses of candidate members**: Adopt COSMOS2020/Farmer photometry and LEPHARE SED fitting; repeat THE FARMER photometry adding HPC1001.a and h as priors to deblend in Ks and IRAC images  
  > "Therefore, we chose to adopt the COSMOS2020/Farmer - LEPHARE combination as the benchmark for our analysis, in which the average photo$-z$ uncertainty of this sample is 0.2." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts); "In order to properly deblend HPC1001, we repeated the FARMER photometry this time by adding HPC1001.a and h in the prior catalog (at their respective ALMA position for a and $K_s$ position for h), and by simultaneously fitting all sources in the $K_s$ and IRAC images." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
   - software: THE FARMER, LEPHARE, EAZY
   - assumption: Chabrier initial mass function and H0=73, ΩM=0.27, Λ0=0.73 cosmology  
  > "We adopt cosmology $H_0=73$, $\Omega_M=0.27$, and $\Lambda_0=0.73$ as well as a Chabrier initial mass function (cite: Chabrier2003)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Simultaneous profile modeling for photometry; galaxy and quasar templates using Bayesian and χ² SED fitting  
  > "Apart from a galaxy template, we also performed a fit assuming a quasar template to examine the systematics of the galaxy template solutions" (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts); "As a sanity check, we also compared the redshift probability distribution functions (PDFs) as derived based on Bayesian and $\chi^2$ methods and were found to be consistent (Fig. 2)." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
   - validation: Cross-matched COSMOS spec-z catalog to Farmer LEPHARE photo-z at 3.5 < z < 4 (finding negligible median bias 1.3e-3); compared four independent photo-z methods and Bayesian vs χ² PDFs  
  > "Furthermore, we calculated the systematic redshift bias at $3.5<z<4$ by cross-matching the COSMOS spec-$z$ catalog (M. Salavato et al. in prep.) to the Farmer LEPHARE photo-$z$, and found it to be negligible, that is to say median $(z_{\rm phot}-z_{\rm spec})/(1+z_{\rm spec})=1.3\times10^{-3}$." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts); "As a sanity check, we also compared the redshift probability distribution functions (PDFs) as derived based on Bayesian and $\chi^2$ methods and were found to be consistent (Fig. 2)." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
3. **Measure ALMA 1.2 mm continuum and integrated FIR-to-radio photometry**: Measure ALMA 1.2 mm continuum using aperture photometry for slightly resolved HPC1001.a and peak fluxes for point-like HPC1001.b, d, and e; measure integrated FIR-to-radio photometry via super-deblending at the fixed position of HPC1001.a  
  > "Among the four ALMA sources, only HPC1001.a is slightly resolved, while the rest are point-like. We thus adopted aperture photometry for HPC1001.a and peak fluxes for HPC1001.b, d, and e." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED); "We measured its integrated far-IR (FIR) to radio photometry by performing the super-deblending technique as described in (cite: Jin2018cosmos) at the fixed position of HPC1001.a." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
   - parameter: Fixed prior position of HPC1001.a for super-deblending  
  > "We measured its integrated far-IR (FIR) to radio photometry by performing the super-deblending technique as described in (cite: Jin2018cosmos) at the fixed position of HPC1001.a." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit FIR-to-radio SED and derive individual and total SFRs, dust masses, and gas fractions**: Fit integrated FIR-to-radio photometry with STARDUST using a GN20 starburst template at z=3.7, then split total SFR_IR and M_dust among the four ALMA sources proportional to their ALMA 1.2 mm flux; compute gas mass from dust mass  
  > "In order to constrain the IR star formation rate (SFR) and dust mass ($M_{\rm dust}$) of the group, we fit the integrated FIR to radio photometry using the STARDUST code (cite: Kokorev2021)." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED); "we derived SFRs and dust masses for the four ALMA sources by splitting the total values based on the ALMA 1.2 mm flux of each source" (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED); "Assuming $M_{\rm gas}=100\times M_{\rm dust}$ and accounting for a 20% uncertainty due to unknown metallicity (cite: Jin2019alma; Jin2022), HPC1001.b would have an inferred gas fraction of $M_{\rm gas}/M_{*}=0.2\pm0.1$ and a gas depletion time of 200 Myr." (Star formation activity & dust fraction)
   - software: STARDUST
   - parameter: Redshift fixed at z = 3.7 for integrated SED fit  
  > "The integrated SED (Fig. 3-right) was well fitted by a starburst template (GN20, (cite: Magdis2012SED)) at $z=3.7$ with dust-obscured ${\rm SFR_{IR}}=743~M_\odot~yr^{-1}$." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
   - assumption: All ALMA-detected sources share the same SED shape as the integrated SED (GN20 template)  
  > "Assuming that all ALMA detected sources share the same SED shape as the integrated SED, we derived SFRs and dust masses for the four ALMA sources" (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
   - assumption: Optically thick dust emission in HPC1001.a could overestimate dust mass by a factor of two (included in uncertainty)  
  > "Given the ALMA brightness and NIR faintness, HPC1001.a could be a starburst with optically thick dust emission, in which case the inferred dust mass is likely to be overestimated by a factor of two (cite: Cortzen2020GN20; Jin2022)." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio M_gas = 100 * M_dust with 20% metallicity uncertainty  
  > "Assuming $M_{\rm gas}=100\times M_{\rm dust}$ and accounting for a 20% uncertainty due to unknown metallicity (cite: Jin2019alma; Jin2022), HPC1001.b would have an inferred gas fraction of $M_{\rm gas}/M_{*}=0.2\pm0.1$ and a gas depletion time of 200 Myr." (Star formation activity & dust fraction)
   - fitting: Template fitting with STARDUST using GN20 starburst template  
  > "In order to constrain the IR star formation rate (SFR) and dust mass ($M_{\rm dust}$) of the group, we fit the integrated FIR to radio photometry using the STARDUST code (cite: Kokorev2021)." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED); "The integrated SED (Fig. 3-right) was well fitted by a starburst template (GN20, (cite: Magdis2012SED)) at $z=3.7$ with dust-obscured ${\rm SFR_{IR}}=743~M_\odot~yr^{-1}$." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Estimate dark matter halo mass and expected baryonic accretion rate**: Estimate halo mass using six independent methods (most massive galaxy M_halo-M_* relation, total stellar mass scaling relations from van der Burg+14 and Shuntov+22, X-ray 0.5-2 keV upper limit in 32'' aperture, galaxy number overdensity with bias factor, and extreme value statistics in 2 deg^2), then apply Daddi+2022 scaling relations for BAR and SFR  
  > "We estimated the dark matter halo mass $M_{\rm DM}$ of HPC1001 with six methods" (Dark matter halo mass); "Based on these estimates, we adopted an average halo mass of log$(M_{\rm DM}/M_\odot)=13$ with an uncertainty of 0.4 dex that is representative at these faint levels" (Dark matter halo mass); "Applying the scaling relations of (cite: Daddi2022Lya), for a dark matter halo with log$(M_{\rm DM}/M_\odot)=13$ at $z=3.7$, the expected cold baryonic accretion rate (BAR) is 2780 M$_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ which in turn corresponds to a SFR of 800 M$_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$." (Star formation activity & dust fraction)
   - parameter: Completeness limit log(M_*/M_sun) > 9.7 at z=3.7; extrapolation down to 10^7 M_sun; 32'' radius aperture for X-ray; R_vir < 20'', velocity dispersion σ_V = 400 km s^-1, galaxy bias 10-20  
  > "we estimated the halo mass based on the stellar masses above the completeness limit of the COSMOS survey, that is $\log(M_*/M_{\odot})>9.7$ at $z=3.7$ (cite: Weaver2022COSMOS2020)." (Dark matter halo mass); "Using a $32''$ radius aperture, we placed a two sigma upper limit $S_{\rm 0.5-2 keV} < 4.2\times 10^{-16}~{\rm ergs~s^{-1}~cm^{-2}}$" (Dark matter halo mass); "assuming a group velocity dispersion $\sigma_V=400$ km s$^{-1}$, we found that the galaxy number density of HPC1001 (in putative $R_{\rm vir}<20''$) is more overdense than the average by a factor of 180 at $z\sim3.7$ in COSMOS2020." (Dark matter halo mass)
   - assumption: Field galaxy stellar mass function at 3 < z < 4 (Muzzin+2013); Planck cosmology mean density 7.77e-26 kg m^-3; collapsed structure for virial radius  
  > "We then extrapolated a total stellar mass of $M_{\rm *,total}=2.6\times10^{11}~M_\odot$ down to $10^7M_{\odot}$, assuming the stellar mass function of field galaxies at $3<z<4$ (cite: Muzzin2013)." (Dark matter halo mass); "Applying a mean baryon and dark matter density of $7.77\times10^{-26}~$kg m$^{-3}$ in comoving volume from Planck cosmology and a galaxy bias factor of 10–20 at $z=3.7$ (cite: Tinker2010haloBias), we obtained a halo mass of log$(M_{\rm DM}/M_\odot)=13-13.4$" (Dark matter halo mass); "Assuming the structure is collapsed, the virial radius corresponding to this halo mass would be 140 kpc (cite: Goerdt2010core)" (Dark matter halo mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-checked halo mass across six independent methods  
  > "We estimated the dark matter halo mass $M_{\rm DM}$ of HPC1001 with six methods" (Dark matter halo mass)

## Software

- **GALCLUSTER** : Searching for galaxy overdensities by mapping fifth-nearest-neighbor distance in COSMOS2020  
  > "Using GALCLUSTER, which is publicly available software designed to automatically search for galaxy overdensities, we mapped the distance of the fifth neighborhood $\Sigma_{5th}$ for $i$- and $K_s$-detected sources (${\rm S/N}_{i}>5$ & $K_s<25$ AB mag) at $z_{\rm phot}>3$ in the COSMOS2020 catalog (cite: Weaver2022COSMOS2020)." (Selection)
- **THE FARMER** : Simultaneous profile-fitting photometry in COSMOS2020 and custom deblended Ks and IRAC photometry including HPC1001.a and h  
  > "COSMOS2020 uses two different methods for obtaining photometry: the aperture photometry (Classic) and THE FARMER" (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts); "In order to properly deblend HPC1001, we repeated the FARMER photometry this time by adding HPC1001.a and h in the prior catalog (at their respective ALMA position for a and $K_s$ position for h), and by simultaneously fitting all sources in the $K_s$ and IRAC images." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
- **LEPHARE** : Optical/NIR SED fitting to derive photometric redshifts and stellar masses  
  > "and two algorithms, LEPHARE and EAZY (cite: Ilbert2006LePhare; Brammer2008EAZY), for the spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting of each of the two versions of photometry." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts); "Therefore, we chose to adopt the COSMOS2020/Farmer - LEPHARE combination as the benchmark for our analysis" (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
- **EAZY** : Optical/NIR SED fitting for photometric redshift comparison in COSMOS2020  
  > "and two algorithms, LEPHARE and EAZY (cite: Ilbert2006LePhare; Brammer2008EAZY), for the spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting of each of the two versions of photometry." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
- **STARDUST** : Fitting the integrated FIR-to-radio SED to constrain IR star formation rate and dust mass  
  > "In order to constrain the IR star formation rate (SFR) and dust mass ($M_{\rm dust}$) of the group, we fit the integrated FIR to radio photometry using the STARDUST code (cite: Kokorev2021)." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Chosen COSMOS2020/Farmer + LEPHARE over Classic aperture photometry because Farmer simultaneously models galaxy profiles and is robust against severe blending in crowded regions like HPC1001, and has negligible redshift bias against spec-z.  
  > "We note though that the COSMOS2020 Classic catalog (and the COSMOS2015 catalog) is using aperture photometry of $2''$ ($3''$), which is severely affected by blending in crowded regions such as HPC1001. On the other hand, Farmer photometry is extracted by simultaneous modeling of the profiles of all galaxies within the group, making it more robust against blending." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
- Alternative: Quasar template SED fitting was tested to check systematics of galaxy template solutions; galaxy templates provided the best fit for seven of eight sources.  
  > "Apart from a galaxy template, we also performed a fit assuming a quasar template to examine the systematics of the galaxy template solutions (but not to serve as a bona fide AGN classification). A galaxy template provides the best fit for seven out of eight sources, ensuring the fidelity of the derived stellar masses." (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): No spectroscopic lines were detected in the ALMA cubes; spectroscopic follow-up (e.g., [CII], [CI], Lyα) is required to confirm group membership and virialization.  
  > "While no lines were identified in the data cubes, continuum emission at 1.2 mm was detected from four sources at 5–23$\sigma$ significance levels." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED); "In order to fully characterize the nature of HPC1001 and explore the quiescence of HPC1001.b, [CII]158$\mu$m and [CI]609,369$\mu$m observations are crucial to infer spectroscopic redshifts, confirm group members, and robustly measure gas masses and SFRs." (A candidate for a galaxy in quenching)
- Limitation (authors): The SED, photo-z, and stellar mass of HPC1001.h are highly uncertain and potentially have underestimated uncertainties due to non-detection below Ks and faintness in IRAC.  
  > "The SED of HPC1001.h is highly uncertain due to its nondetection at wavelengths shorter than $K_s$ and its faintness in IRAC" (Optical+NIR SEDs and photometric redshifts)
- Limitation (authors): HPC1001.a could have optically thick dust emission, which would cause its inferred dust mass to be overestimated by a factor of two.  
  > "Given the ALMA brightness and NIR faintness, HPC1001.a could be a starburst with optically thick dust emission, in which case the inferred dust mass is likely to be overestimated by a factor of two (cite: Cortzen2020GN20; Jin2022)." (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
- Limitation (authors): Individual ALMA-detected galaxy SFRs and dust masses rely on the assumption that all four sources share the same SED shape as the integrated group SED.  
  > "Assuming that all ALMA detected sources share the same SED shape as the integrated SED, we derived SFRs and dust masses for the four ALMA sources" (FIR, (sub)millimeter, and radio SED)
- Limitation (authors): HPC1001 is not covered by the JWST COSMOS-Web survey.  
  > "Unfortunately, HPC1001 is not covered by the JWST COSMOS-Web survey." (A candidate for a galaxy in quenching)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): All group member identifications rely solely on photometric redshifts; projection effects along the line of sight over Δz ~ 0.4 (~300 cMpc) cannot be ruled out without spectroscopy.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Splitting the integrated Herschel/SCUBA2/MeerKAT dust mass and SFR_IR strictly in proportion to 1.2 mm flux assumes identical dust temperatures across galaxies in very different evolutionary states (starburst HPC1001.a vs quenching candidate HPC1001.b).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data reduction, beam size, and continuum sensitivity are completely omitted from the text.
