1. **Measure 1.2 mm continuum peak position, intensity, and deconvolved size**: 2D Gaussian fit to the 1.2 mm dust continuum image  
  > "The peak position and peak intensity of the 1.2 mm continuum are derived via a 2D Gaussian fit to the image to be ($\alpha_{2000}$, $\delta_{2000}$) = (15$^{m h}$43$^{m m}$02$^{m s}$.24, -34$^\circ$09$'$06.$''$93) and 8.69$\pm$0.32 mJy beam$^{-1}$, respectively, and the synthesized beam size is 0.18$''$$''$ (P.A. -84.5$\ensuremath{^\circ}$)." (Continuum)
   - assumption: Gaussian spatial distribution for the central continuum peak  
  > "The deconvolved size of the central peak is (0.13$''$$\pm$0.02$''$$) 	imes ($0.11$''$$\pm$0.02$''$) (P.A. 124$^\circ$$\pm$65$^\circ$) assuming a Gaussian distribution." (Continuum)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit  
  > "The peak position and peak intensity of the 1.2 mm continuum are derived via a 2D Gaussian fit to the image to be ($\alpha_{2000}$, $\delta_{2000}$) = (15$^{m h}$43$^{m m}$02$^{m s}$.24, -34$^\circ$09$'$06.$''$93) and 8.69$\pm$0.32 mJy beam$^{-1}$, respectively" (Continuum)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract spectra, identify CH3OH lines, and map high-excitation CH3OH emission**: Extract spectra toward the 0.2'' (~30 au) area around the continuum peak and toward the continuum peak position; identify CH3OH lines above 3 sigma; create stacked integrated-intensity map of blueshifted (-0.2 to 5.0 km s^-1) and redshifted (5.8 to 10.6 km s^-1) components for the three CH3OH lines with E_u > 400 K; fit Gaussian profiles to the 9 CH3OH lines toward the continuum peak to measure peak intensities and line widths  
  > "Figures 2(a) and (c) show the spectrum observed toward the 0.$''$2 ($\sim$30 au) area around the continuum peak at both frequency setups. We identify 9 CH$_3$OH lines above the 3 $\sigma$ noise level, where $\sigma$ means the root-mean-square (rms) noise of one channel, as listed in Table 2." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH); "Figure 1(b) depicts the stacked integrated-intensity map of the blueshifted (-0.2 km s$^{-1}$ to 5.0 km s$^{-1}$) and redshifted (5.8 km s$^{-1}$ to 10.6 km s$^{-1}$) components for the three high-excitation lines whose upper state energies are higher than 400 K." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH); "We obtain the peak intensities and the widths of the nine CH$_3$OH lines for the blueshifted component toward the continuum peak by a Gaussian fit, as listed in Table 2 (columns 8 and 10)." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
   - parameter: Blueshifted velocity range: -0.2 km s^-1 to 5.0 km s^-1; Redshifted velocity range: 5.8 km s^-1 to 10.6 km s^-1  
  > "Figure 1(b) depicts the stacked integrated-intensity map of the blueshifted (-0.2 km s$^{-1}$ to 5.0 km s$^{-1}$) and redshifted (5.8 km s$^{-1}$ to 10.6 km s$^{-1}$) components for the three high-excitation lines whose upper state energies are higher than 400 K." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH)
   - assumption: Systemic velocity of 5.5 km s^-1  
  > "We here employ the systemic velocity of this protostellar source of 5.5 km s$^{-1}$, which is previously reported on the basis of the Keplerian rotation motion of the disk component by (cite: Okoda et al.(2018))." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit to each of the 9 CH3OH line profiles toward the continuum peak  
  > "We obtain the peak intensities and the widths of the nine CH$_3$OH lines for the blueshifted component toward the continuum peak by a Gaussian fit, as listed in Table 2 (columns 8 and 10)." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Derive rotation temperature, column density, and beam filling factor of CH3OH**: Simultaneous non-linear least-squares fit of source rotation temperature (T), column density (N), and beam filling factor (f) to the observed peak intensities and line widths of the 9 CH3OH lines using radiative transfer equations (1) and (2) under LTE  
  > "To find the best fit values of the source temperature ($T$), the column density ($N$), and the beam filling factor ($f$) using the nine CH$_3$OH line intensities, based on the above equations (1) and (2), a non-linear least-squares fit on the observed peak intensities considering the line widths is conducted (cite: Imai et al.(2022)), where the three parameters are optimized simultaneously." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
   - parameter: T_cmb = 2.7 K; simulated spectrum uses systemic velocity 2.9 km s^-1 and average line width 3.4 km s^-1  
  > "the systemic velocity for the calculated spectra is set to 2.9 km s$^{-1}$ in Figures [2](#sp)(b) and (d). We use the average line width of 3.4 km s$^{-1}$ in the above spectrum simulation." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH); "The observed spectrum are blueshifted, and hence, the systemic velocity for the calculated spectra is set to 2.9 km s$^{-1}$ in Figures 2(b) and (d). We use the average line width of 3.4 km s$^{-1}$ in the above spectrum simulation." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) with a single rotation temperature  
  > "Under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), the observed intensity ($T_{m obs}$) and the line optical depth ($	au_{m line}$) are given as:" (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH); "Hence, we assume here the LTE condition with a single rotation temperature, and report the parameters derived with all the observed CH$_3$OH lines as the best effort." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
   - fitting: Non-linear least-squares optimization of T, N, and f with chi^2 surface error estimation  
  > "To find the best fit values of the source temperature ($T$), the column density ($N$), and the beam filling factor ($f$) using the nine CH$_3$OH line intensities, based on the above equations (1) and (2), a non-linear least-squares fit on the observed peak intensities considering the line widths is conducted (cite: Imai et al.(2022)), where the three parameters are optimized simultaneously." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH); "Figures 3(a), (c), and (d) show the $\chi^2$ plots for the $N-T$, $N-f$, and $T-f$ planes, respectively. The minimum $\chi^2$ value for each plane corresponds to the best fit parameter values determined by the least squares fit, where the gray line represents the uncertainties (1$\sigma$)." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
   - validation: Checked residuals (comparable to intensity errors), simulated the full spectrum to compare with observations (reproduces within ~3 sigma), and tested fitting only the 5 lines with E_u < 350 K to check for infrared pumping effects  
  > "The residuals (observed intensity minus calculated intensity for the best fit parameters) are comparable to the intensity errors, and hence, the fit is successful." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH); "As the high excitation lines of $E_uk_{m B}^{-1}$ $>$ 400 K are included in the analysis, their excitations would be affected by infrared pumping. This effect may lead to the high rotation temperature in the analysis. To assess this possibility, we tried to conduct a similar analysis by using the 5 lines of $E_uk_{m B}^{-1}$ $<$ 350 K." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH); "The calculated spectrum reasonably reproduces the observations within difference of up to around 3$\sigma$ noise level in the intensity." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
4. **Detect CH2DOH and CH3OCHO lines and derive their column densities and abundance ratios relative to CH3OH**: Measure integrated intensities of CH2DOH (at 234.471 GHz) and blended CH3OCHO features (at 234.5 GHz and 247.1 GHz), evaluate column densities using LTE equations (1) and (2), and compute abundance ratios relative to CH3OH  
  > "We here discuss detections of the CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO lines in the observed spectrum (Figure 2) based on the rms noise of the integrated intensity." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO); "The column density of CH$_2$DOH is evaluated by using equations (1) and (2)." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO); "We estimate the column density of CH$_3$OCHO by using equations (1) and (2), to be (1.1-3.2)$	imes$10$^{17}$cm$^{-2}$ and the CH$_3$OCHO/CH$_3$OH ratio to be 0.02-0.15." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO)
   - parameter: Line width fixed to average CH3OH line width of 3.4 km s^-1  
  > "Since only one line is available for CH$_2$DOH, the rotation temperature and the beam filling factor are assumed to be the same as those determined for CH$_3$OH, and the line width is employed for the average value of the CH$_3$OH lines (3.4 km s$^{-1}$)." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO)
   - assumption: LTE, and rotation temperature and beam filling factor are identical to those derived for CH3OH (T = 119 +20/-26 K, f = 0.018 +0.005/-0.003)  
  > "Since only one line is available for CH$_2$DOH, the rotation temperature and the beam filling factor are assumed to be the same as those determined for CH$_3$OH, and the line width is employed for the average value of the CH$_3$OH lines (3.4 km s$^{-1}$)." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO); "Here, the rotation temperature and the beam filling factor are assumed to be the same as those derived for CH$_3$OH." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO)

  > "Then, the column density of CH$_2$DOH is derived to be (1.8-4.3)$	imes$10$^{17}$ cm$^{-2}$, where the errors of the rotation temperature and beam filling factor derived from the CH$_3$OH lines are considered in the error estimation." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO)

## Qualitative findings

- IRAS 15398-3359 harbors a compact hot corino within the 50 au area around the protostar, making it a hybrid chemical source with warm carbon-chain chemistry on large scales (100-1000 au) and hot corino chemistry on small scales (~10 au).  
  > "These results, in combination with previous reports, indicate that IRAS 15398$-$3359 is a source with hybrid properties showing both hot corino chemistry rich in complex organic molecules on small scales $\sim$10 au) and warm carbon-chain chemistry (WCCC) rich in carbon-chain species on large scales ($\sim$100-1000 au)." (Abstract)
- The high-excitation CH3OH emission is predominantly blueshifted and slightly extended toward the northwest along the disk midplane (P.A. 130 deg), which may trace a thin surface layer of the disk structure heated by accretion shocks and/or protostellar irradiation.  
  > "The distribution of the blueshifted component is concentrated within the 50 au area around the continuum peak position, with a slight extension along the disk midplane (P.A 130$^\circ$) toward the northwest." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH); "One possibility which we speculate is that the CH$_3$OH emission could arise from the thin surface layer of the disk structure, as indicated by the yellow part in the schematic illustration of Figure 1(d)." (Small Beam Filling Factor vs. Slight Extension toward the Northwest)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution (0.2''-0.3'', ~30 au) and high-sensitivity ALMA Band 6 observations from the FAUST Large Program are needed to probe the ~10-50 au region around the protostar without contamination from the 100-1000 au scale outflow and envelope.  
  > "Hot corinos have been identified even in some sources which were hitherto classified as a WCCC source abundant in carbon-chain molecules, owing to high-resolution and high-sensitivity observations." (Introduction); "Since their CH$_3$OH spectrum is averaged over the 2$''$ ($\sim$300 au) area around the continuum peak, it traces a larger structure on a few 100 au scale. Rather, part of the outflow feature is likely contaminating the emission." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH)
- Why this method: Simultaneous non-linear least-squares LTE fitting of 9 CH3OH lines spanning E_u = 61-537 K allows separate determination of rotation temperature, column density, and beam filling factor because the lines have moderate optical depths (not optically thin) and high H2 density (>10^7-10^8 cm^-3) near the protostar justifies LTE.  
  > "The LTE condition is generally justified near the protostar ($\sim$10 au scale) where the H$_2$ density is 10$^7-$10$^8$ cm$^{-3}$ or higher (cite: Jorgensen et al.(2013); Sakai et al.(2014a))." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH); "Note that the column density and the beam filling factor can be determined separately because the lines are not optically thin (Table 2). Approximately, the relative strength of the lines provides a measure of the excitation temperature and the column density, while the actual intensities provide a measure of the beam filling factor." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
- Alternative: Fitting only the 5 CH3OH lines with E_u < 350 K to avoid potential infrared pumping of the E_u > 400 K lines; yielded similar best-fit parameters (107 K, 2.4x10^18 cm^-2, f=0.021) but rejected due to heavy parameter degeneracy and poor constraints.  
  > "To assess this possibility, we tried to conduct a similar analysis by using the 5 lines of $E_uk_{m B}^{-1}$ $<$ 350 K. The best fit values of the rotation temperature, the column density, and the beam filling factors obtained from the 5 lines are 107 K, 2.4$	imes$10$^{18}$ cm$^{-2}$, and 0.021, respectively, which are not very different from those derived from all the lines. However, the parameters are not well constrained due to heavy correlation among the parameters, according to the error estimation using the $\chi^2$ values." (Physical Parameters of CH$_3$OH)
- Alternative: Alternative explanations for the weak redshifted CH3OH emission: foreground gas absorption at systemic velocity, an inverse P-Cygni profile from infalling gas onto the disk, dust shielding of the redshifted outflow launching point by the disk midplane, or an asymmetric gas distribution.  
  > "The absorption at the systemic velocity (5.5 km s$^{-1}$) might be caused by the absorption due to the foreground gas, but an inverse P-cygni profile could more reasonably explain the asymmetric spectral profiles of CH$_3$OH lines (Figure 1(c))." (Small Beam Filling Factor vs. Slight Extension toward the Northwest); "If the CH$_3$OH emission comes from the launching point of the primary outflow, its redshifted part could be shielded by dust of the disk midplane. It should also be noted that the weak redshifted emission could also originate from asymmetric distribution of the CH$_3$OH emitting gas." (Small Beam Filling Factor vs. Slight Extension toward the Northwest)
- Alternative: Photodesorption of CH3OH on the disk surface: rejected in favor of thermal desorption / accretion shock heating because laboratory experiments show CH3OH photodesorption is inefficient.  
  > "Note that photodesorption of CH$_3$OH is inefficient, as shown by the laboratory experiments (cite: Bertin et al.(2016); Cruz-Diaz et al.(2016)). Thus, thermal desorption in the surface layer should be considered here." (Small Beam Filling Factor vs. Slight Extension toward the Northwest)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The CH3OH emitting region is much smaller than the synthesized beam size and is spatially unresolved.  
  > "The beam filling factor is derived to be 0.018$^{+0.005}_{-0.003}$, indicating that the emitting region of CH$_3$OH is much smaller than the synthesized beam size and is not resolved." (Abstract)
- Limitation (authors): The redshifted component is not clearly detected in the CH3OH lines, preventing a detailed kinematic analysis or confirmation of the disk surface origin without higher angular resolution observations.  
  > "Unfortunately, we do not see the redshifted component clearly in the CH$_3$OH lines to discuss the kinematics in detail." (High Excitation Lines of CH$_3$OH); "In any case, the present observation cannot allow us to discuss further detailed kinematics of the CH$_3$OH emitting region." (Small Beam Filling Factor vs. Slight Extension toward the Northwest)
- Limitation (authors): The lower-excitation CH2DOH line at 247.6257463 GHz (E_u = 29 K) predicted by the LTE model is not detected at the 3 sigma level, possibly due to foreground absorption or proximity to the correlator band edge.  
  > "However, it is not seen with the 3$\sigma$ confidence level in IRAS 15398$-$3359, although it is expected to be deteted in the calculation (Figure 2 (d)). We do not fully account for this non-detection, but here note two possibilities. Since this line is quite low-excitation, it would be seriously affected by the absorption due to the extended foreground gas. Alternatively, the line is very close to the band edge of the correlator, which would provide a less accurate calibration." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO)
- Limitation (authors): CH2DOH and CH3OCHO column densities rely on assuming the same rotation temperature and beam filling factor as CH3OH because only one line of CH2DOH and two blended features of CH3OCHO are detected.  
  > "Since only one line is available for CH$_2$DOH, the rotation temperature and the beam filling factor are assumed to be the same as those determined for CH$_3$OH, and the line width is employed for the average value of the CH$_3$OH lines (3.4 km s$^{-1}$)." (CH$_2$DOH and CH$_3$OCHO)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Setup 1 and Setup 2 have different synthesized beam sizes (~0.34'' x 0.29'' vs ~0.20'' x 0.18'', differing by a factor of ~2.7 in beam area), yet a single beam filling factor (f = 0.018) appears to be fit simultaneously across all 9 lines from both setups without explicit mention of convolving the cubes to a common resolution.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Noticeable discrepancy in Section 2 text vs Table 1 regarding array configurations: the text says C43-5 and C43-2 were combined, whereas Table 1 lists C43-5 and C43-2 for Setup 1, and C43-4 and C43-1 for Setup 2.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust optical depth at 1.2 mm toward the continuum peak is not subtracted or accounted for in the radiative transfer equation (Equation 1 only uses B_nu(T_cmb) as the background), although continuum brightness temperature is ~4.39 K.
