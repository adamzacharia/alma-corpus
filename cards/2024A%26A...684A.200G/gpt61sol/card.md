## Analysis recipe

1. **Fit the changing continuum asymmetry**: Fit squared visibilities and closure phases with star, halo and azimuthally modulated disk; global fits vary one parameter or parameter set across epochs.  
  > "We used chromatic geometric models that consist of a point-like central star, a scattered light component (called halo), and a circumstellar environment to fit the continuum interferometric quantities." (Choice of the model); "The global fit is obtained by forcing all the model parameters to be nonvariable over time, except for the parameter —or set of parameters— describing the feature to be tested for variability." (Global fit modeling)
   - software: scipy.optimize.minimize, Aspro
   - parameter: First-order azimuthal modulation, m = 1.  
  > "The order of the azimuthal modulation is taken in our case to be $m$ = 1." (Choice of the model)
   - assumption: Unresolved star; fully resolved halo with zero visibility.  
  > "The star is assumed to be unresolved and the halo to be fully resolved" (Choice of the model); "with a visibility of zero." (Choice of the model)
   - assumption: Stellar black body at T_eff = 10500 K.  
  > "the spectral index of the star assumed to be a black body at $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$=10500 K." (Choice of the model)
   - fitting: Sequential least squares programming with scipy.optimize.minimize, then MCMC (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013).  
  > "The model fitting consists in an initial minimization procedure with `scipy.optimize.minimize` using a sequential least squares programming method to obtain an initial guess of the free parameters" (Choice of the model); "followed by a procedure based on a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC; (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013)) numerical approach, which is robust against trapping in local minima." (Choice of the model)
   - validation: Aspro synthetic-data tests of configuration dependence; azimuthal-modulation chi-square maps show one global minimum.  
  > "We then fitted the different synthetic data sets with the same parametric model and then compared the results obtained for each parameter." (Fit dependence on the *(u,v)* plane coverage); "with one single global minimum identified in the $c_1$,$s_1$ diagrams." (Disk azimuthal asymmetry)
2. **Estimate the asymmetry's orbital period**: Fit a sine function for uniform circular motion to the time-variable position angle.  
  > "The gray line is a sine function corresponding to a uniform circular motion fitted to the data to derive a period estimate." (Orbital period of the crescent-like feature)
   - parameter: Exclude small-configuration epochs P2, P5, P6, G6 and G10.  
  > "The small-configuration epochs P2, P5, P6, G6, and G10 are not taken into account as the small baselines provide marginally weaker constraints on the azimuthal modulation because of the low level of closure phase signal detected." (Orbital period of the crescent-like feature)
   - assumption: Same asymmetric feature monitored over 11 years in H and K; circular orbit.  
  > "Assuming this is the same asymmetric feature that is monitored with the VLTI over the 11 year period in both the H and K bands, we attempt to investigate the orbital properties of the emission feature." (Orbital period of the crescent-like feature); "we have implemented here the simplest case of a circular orbit" (Orbital period of the crescent-like feature)
   - fitting: Sine fit; best period 12.6 ± 0.1 months.  
  > "The best sine fit gives a period of 12.6$\pm$0.1 months." (Orbital period of the crescent-like feature)
   - validation: Compare fits with and without epochs near 60 months; investigate a shorter-period local minimum.  
  > "If these epochs were found to correspond to outliers and were removed, the fit would give a $\chi^2_r$ value of 38$\pm$17, while the estimated period would remain the same" (Orbital period of the crescent-like feature); "our sine fit also shows a local minimum for a similar period of around $\sim$8.5 months, although with a poorer $\chi^2_r$ value of $\sim$350." ## Qualitative findings

- Continuum variability is dominated by a changing asymmetric brightness distribution.  
  > "The system, which is spatially resolved by both instruments at all epochs, shows temporal variability dominated by changes in the asymmetric spatial distribution of brightness rather than in the flux ratio between the central star and its circumstellar environment." (Summary)
- A time-variable azimuthal modulation best describes the continuum data.  
  > "This analysis indicates that, among the tested models, the one with a time-variable disk azimuthal modulation (model #6) best describes our data" (Temporal variability)
- Hydrodynamic effects are favored for the crescent-like asymmetry.  
  > "The origin of the azimuthal asymmetry is preferentially connected to hydrodynamic effects, either induced by disk instabilities generating a vortex or by an undetected low-mass (substellar or planetary-mass) companion embedded in the low-density region of the disk and launching a wound spiral." (Summary)
- The compact Brγ region is offset and appears between the star and dusty feature.  
  > "The location of the compact Br$\gamma$-line-emitting region is offset with respect to the central star and appears to be consistently located inside the dust cavity and between the dusty feature and the star." (Summary)
- ALMA does not determine the adopted outer-disk dust mass; the literature value is retained.  
  > "Because of the uncertain estimate of the disk dust mass, we choose to adopt the value reported by the latter authors for the outer disk component." (Disk structure)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA 1.3 mm photometry provides a rough mass comparison for the adopted outer component.  
  > "This value can be compared to the rough mass estimate obtained from the archival ALMA photometry point at 1.3 mm (ID: 2015.1.01600.S/PI Panic), which was not included in the work of these latter authors." (Disk structure)
- Why this method: Global fitting limits spurious variability caused by different interferometric configurations.  
  > "This may lead to the detection of spurious variability in the model parameters" (Global fit modeling); "Therefore, instead of fitting each single epoch with our ten-parameter model and searching for individual variability trends, we implement a so-called global fit of the data." (Global fit modeling)
- Alternative: A fully nonvariable system gives the poorest GRAVITY continuum fit.  
  > "The nonvariable system (#1) gives the poorest fit." (Temporal variability)
- Alternative: Moving the one-component disk inner radius to ∼0.6 au overestimates the NIR flux.  
  > "Simply moving the inner radius to $\sim$0.6 au in the one-component model described above is not possible because this leads to further overestimation of the NIR flux in the SED." (Disk structure)
- Alternative: A PAH-based inner component fits the SED but supplies only ∼5% of K-band and 10% of H-band flux.  
  > "However, in the case of Model Q, the flux fractional contribution of the LDC is estimated to be only $\sim$ 5% in the K-band and 10% in the H-band." (Location and temperature of the dust)
