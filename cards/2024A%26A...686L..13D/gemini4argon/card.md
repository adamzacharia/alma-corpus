## Qualitative findings

- Emission in the inner 300 au of IRAS 4A2 arises from three distinct spatial-velocity zones: a compact (<50 au) ice sublimation zone at ±1.5 km s^-1 traced by CH3OH, an intermediate zone (50-150 au) at 2-6 km s^-1 traced by both SiO and CH3OH with an inverted velocity gradient, and an extended (>150 au) outflow zone above 7 km s^-1 traced by SiO.  
  > "The emission arises from three zones: i) a very compact and unresolved region (<50 au) dominated by the ice sublimation zone, at ±1.5 km s−1 with respect to v sys, traced by methanol;" (abstract); "ii) an intermediate region (between 50 au and 150 au) traced by both SiO and CH 3 OH, between 2 and 6 km s−1 with respect to v sys, with an inverted velocity gradient (with respect to the large scale emission), whose origin is not clear;" (abstract); "iii) an extended region (> 150 au) traced by SiO, above 7 km s−1 with respect to v sys, and dominated by the outflow." (abstract)
- CH3OH peaks slightly closer to the protostar than SiO (by ~0.1'') in both red- and blue-shifted ranges.  
  > "methanol peaks closer to the source than SiO in both blue and red ranges" (3. The kinematical structure of the IRAS 4A inner regions)
- CH3OH and SiO in the intermediate region (inner ~100-150 au) probe the base of a wide-angle disk wind crossing the plane of the sky, making this the first disk wind candidate detected in SiO and the second in CH3OH.  
  > "We propose that CH 3 OH and SiO in the inner 100 au probe the base of a wide-angle disk wind." (abstract); "This is the first detection of a disk wind candidate in SiO, and the second ever in CH 3 OH." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous studies did not resolve the inner 200 au (<0''.7) of IRAS 4A; ALMA FAUST provides 50 au spatial resolution and high spectral resolution in selected chemical tracers (SiO and CH3OH) to disentangle disk, envelope, and outflow emission.  
  > "However, none of the previous studies resolve the inner 200 au (below 0′′. 7). The FAUST data provide the superb spatial resolution of 50 au, that allows us to explore for the first time these inner regions using selected chemical tracers." (1. Introduction); "The high spatial and spectral resolution observation of FAUST have been crucial to study the interplay between the disk, the envelope and the outflowing gas." (5. Conclusions)
- Why this method: Combining kinematics with the distinct chemical formation pathways of CH3OH (ice mantle sublimation or mild shock sputtering) and SiO (strong shock core sputtering or mantle sputtering) allows retrieval of the physical mechanisms at play on 50 au scales.  
  > "The unique combination of kinematics and chemistry allowed us to disentangle the emission at very small scales (50 au) using the chemical formation pathway of each species to retrieve the physical mechanisms at play." (5. Conclusions)
- Alternative: Rotating disk / inner envelope (rejected as sole explanation because the velocity gradient PA differs by ~30 deg from perpendicular to the large-scale jet, and SiO requires fast shocks >10 km s^-1 rather than pure thermal sublimation).  
  > "However, i) the PA (∼−30◦ ) of the velocity gradient differs of ∼ 30◦ from the perpendicular direction of the jet/outflow direction at large scale (above 300 au), as expected for disk emission." (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient); "In summary, the observed methanol emission could be the result of the ice mantle sublimation in the inner rotating envelope/disk, while a fast shock (>10 km s−1 ) needs to occur to sputter the dust grains so to explain the observed SiO abundance." (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient)
- Alternative: Slow shocks (<5-10 km s^-1) at the infalling envelope-disk interface (considered unlikely because it is unclear if slow shocks can sputter enough SiO, and emission would follow disk kinematics for which there is no strong evidence).  
  > "Alternatively, the two species could be released by grain mantle sputtering at the infalling envelope-disk interface, where slow shocks (< 5-10 km s−1 ) occur (e.g., Sakai et al. 2014). However, it is unclear if this slow shocks can sputter enough SiO, and if so, the emission would follow the disk kinematics, for which we do not have strong evidence (see above)." (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient)
- Alternative: Jet/outflow on the plane of the sky or precessing across the plane of the sky (rejected because a jet in the plane of the sky would show blue/red emission in both lobes along the whole jet length, a precessing jet crossing the plane of the sky would show periodic alternating velocity patterns at larger scales, and 3D precession modeling by Chuang et al. 2021 excluded this).  
  > "If it is close to the plane of the sky one expects to observe blue- and red-shifted emission in both lobes and along the whole jet length, not only in the inner 300 au (see e.g., Cabrit & Bertout 1990; Podio et al. 2021)." (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient); "However, if this was the case we would expect to see an alternation of blue- and red- shifted emission with a periodic pattern and also at larger scales. Moreover, this scenario has been excluded by Chuang et al. (2021) based on a 3D precession model." (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient)
- Alternative: Interaction of the jet with the disk wind creating an unresolved shocked shell, or disk wind rotation (considered as an alternative sub-scenario where SiO is an indirect tracer, requiring inclination >80 deg and <10 au resolution to test).  
  > "It is important to notice that the emission of SiO can also be the result of the interaction of the jet with the disk wind giving rise to an unresolved shocked shell (see e.g., Lee et al. 2022b), or a disk wind rotation (e.g., Lee et al. 2022b). In this way, SiO can be considered as an indirect tracer." (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient)

- Limitation (authors): The 50 au (~0''.15) spatial resolution is insufficient to resolve kinematical structures in the PV diagrams or to assess whether a rotating/infalling disk or envelope signature or shocked shell is present, requiring observations down to 10 au.  
  > "The inversion of velocity is clearly shown, but the PV diagrams are limited in spatial/angular resolution to reveal any other kinematical structure." (3. The kinematical structure of the IRAS 4A inner regions); "ii) unfortunately, the present spatial resolution ( ∼ 50 au) is not enough to assess whether the signature of a rotating/infalling envelope is present in CH 3 OH and/or SiO (see Figure D.2 in appendix);" (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient); "Finally, we emphasise that observations down to 10 au will be essential to reveal the presence and the possible contribution of the unresolved rotating disk." (5. Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Mm dust emission is optically thick in the central region of IRAS 4A2 and can partially absorb the mm iCOMs emission.  
  > "thick in the central region (Maury et al. 2019; Galametz et al. 2019; Li et al. 2017; Ko et al. 2020; GuerraAlvarado et al. 2023) to completely obscure (for 4A1) or partially absorb (for 4A2) the mm iCOMs emission" (1. Introduction (The target))
- Limitation (authors): High-velocity SiO emission extends beyond the spectral window bandwidth.  
  > "Please note that the SiO emission is actually going beyond the spectral window bandwidth, therefore beyond the upper velocity range value reported in the figure." (Appendix A: Overview of FAUST SiO at large scale)
- Limitation (authors): The individual bolometric luminosities of IRAS 4A1 and 4A2 are not measured separately due to lack of angular resolution in the infrared, and the jet inclination of IRAS 4A2 is highly uncertain (45-88 deg).  
  > "The inclination of the IRAS 4A2 jet is yet highly uncertain" (4. On the origin of the inverse velocity gradient); "The bolometric luminosity (L bol ) of IRAS 4A1 and IRAS 4A2 is not measured singularly due to the lack of angular resolution observation at infrared wavelengths." (Appendix C: SiO and CH3OH: sputtering or sublimation?)
- Limitation (authors): The reactivity of frozen Si and SiO with water ice and the exact binding energy distribution of SiO on interstellar ices are uncertain.  
  > "However, SiO and Si could be highly reactive with the water molecules in the ice preventing the sublimation of pure SiO and Si into the gas phase." (Appendix C: SiO and CH3OH: sputtering or sublimation?)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Column densities and abundance ratios are derived from single transitions of CH3OH and SiO assuming LTE, optically thin emission, and a single fixed excitation temperature of 100 K; optical depth effects (especially for CH3OH) or non-LTE excitation could alter the CH3OH/SiO ratio.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ~0.1'' spatial offset between CH3OH and SiO emission peaks is smaller than the synthesized beam (~0.14''-0.18'') and relies on peak pixel identification rather than 2D Gaussian or visibility-plane fitting.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No quantitative kinematic model of a wide-angle disk wind is fitted to the data or PV diagrams; the disk wind interpretation is qualitative.
