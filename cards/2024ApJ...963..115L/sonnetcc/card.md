## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies
- Question: Is the low molecular-gas star formation efficiency of green valley galaxies (relative to main sequence galaxies with similar CO-traced molecular gas fractions) due to a lack of dense gas (f_dense) or to a low star formation efficiency of the dense gas (SFE_dense)?  
  > "We investigate whether the deficit of molecular gas star formation efficiency ($\rm SFE_{\rm mol}$) that leads to the low specific star formation rate in these green valley galaxies is due to a lack of dense gas" (abstract); "The low SFE$_{\rm mol}$ of green valley galaxies can be attributed to either lower $\rm SFE_{\rm dense}$ or lower $f_{\rm dense}$ than that in main sequence galaxies." ## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate the Band 3 data**: Standard ALMA pipeline in CASA  
  > "The data were processed by the standard pipeline in the Common Astronomy Software Applications package (cite: mcm07)." (2 Sample and Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Systematic flux uncertainty typically 5%-10% in Band 3 quoted from the ALMA Proposer's Guide  
  > "The systematic flux uncertainty associated with the calibration is typically 5%-10% in Band 3 (see ALMA Proposer’s Guide)" (2 Sample and Observations)
2. **Image HCN and HCO+ cubes**: tclean with Briggs weighting, user-specified image center, 0.5 arcsec pixels and a 2.5 arcsec restoring beam to match MaNGA; lines imaged separately; channels binned to 30-50 km/s  
  > "The task tclean was employed for deconvolution with a robust = 0.5 weighting (Briggs)." (2 Sample and Observations); "We adopted a user-specified image center, pixel size (0.5$\ensuremath{''}$), and restoring beamsize (2.5$\ensuremath{''}$) to match the image grid and the spatial resolution of the MaNGA images." (2 Sample and Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "The task tclean was employed for deconvolution with a robust = 0.5 weighting (Briggs)." (2 Sample and Observations)
   - parameter: channel binning 30 to 50 km/s  
  > "the spectral channels are binned to 30 to 50 km s$^{-1}$, varying from galaxy to galaxy" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Restoring beam is similar to the native tclean beam (2.7 x 2.4 arcsec)  
  > "The restoring beamsize is similar to that of the native beamsize reported by the tclean (2.7$\ensuremath{''}$ $\times$ 2.4$\ensuremath{''}$)." (2 Sample and Observations)
3. **Build integrated intensity maps and total line intensities**: immoments on the cubes, integrating a hand-set velocity range without clipping; total intensities from summing spaxels within 1.5 Re  
  > "The integrated intensity maps of HCN and $\rm HCO^{+}$ were constructed using the task IMMOMENTS in CASA." (2 Sample and Observations); "We compute the total intensities of CO, HCN, and $\rm HCO^{+}$ by summing up all the spaxel values within a 1.5 effective radius" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - software: CASA, immoments
   - parameter: velocity range set by hand to match the line profile, no clipping  
  > "integrating emission from a velocity range set by hand to match the observed line profile without any clipping in the signal" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - parameter: 1.5 Re aperture from the Pipe3D catalog  
  > "within a 1.5 effective radius (1.5$R_{e}$) provided in the Pipe3D catalog" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Global HCN/CO and HCO+/CO flux ratios compared with previous works  
  > "broadly consistent with the global values found in previous works (cite: use15; jim19)" (2 Sample and Observations)
4. **Convert line luminosities to gas masses**: Constant alpha_CO for CO and constant alpha_HCN and alpha_HCO+ for the dense gas  
  > "is computed from the CO luminosity by adopting a constant conversion factor ($\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}$) of 4.35 $\rm M_{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: bol13)" (2 Sample and Observations); "$\alpha_{\rm HCN}$ = 10 $\rm M_{\odot}$(K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$)$^{-1}$" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.35 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1, includes helium and heavier elements  
  > "which includes contributions from molecular hydrogen, helium, and heavier elements" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - assumption: alpha_HCN = alpha_HCO+ = 10 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1, fixed because dense-gas conversion factors are unexplored at kpc scales  
  > "we have chosen to utilize fixed conversion factors" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - assumption: Fixed factor fair because metallicity within 0.07 dex of solar  
  > "The difference is within 0.07 dex of solar metallicity, making a fixed conversion factor for all 5 galaxies a fair assumption." (2 Sample and Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive spaxel SFR and stellar mass surface densities from MaNGA**: Pipe3D value-added products; Balmer-decrement extinction correction, Halpha-to-SFR (Kennicutt 1998, Salpeter IMF), per-spaxel normalisation by inclination-corrected physical area  
  > "We apply the correction of dust extinction to emission line measurements using the Balmer decrement computed on a spaxel basis" (2 Sample and Observations); "we normalize the stellar mass and SFR derived for each spaxel to the physical area of one spaxel corrected for inclination taken from NSA" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - software: Pipe3D
   - assumption: intrinsic H-alpha/H-beta = 2.86, Milky Way extinction curve with Rv = 3.1  
  > "assuming an intrinsic H$\alpha$/H$\beta$= 2.86 and a Milky Way extinction curve with Rv = 3.1 (cite: car89)" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - assumption: Salpeter IMF  
  > "We use a Salpeter initial mass function (IMF)." (1 Introduction)
   - assumption: H-alpha in star-forming spaxels is purely from star formation; SFR in retired spaxels is an upper limit  
  > "the SFR estimated using the H$\alpha$ to SFR conversion serves only as an upper limit for retired spaxels" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Apply S/N cuts and classify spaxels**: S/N cuts on emission lines, BPT classification, and Halpha EW cuts to define star-forming, retired and other spaxels  
  > "and S/N \> 2.5 for CO, HCN, and $\rm HCO^{+}$ lines" (2 Sample and Observations); "We then identify star-forming spaxels to be those satisfying both BPT-classified star-forming criteria and the H$\alpha$ equivalent width (EW) $>$ 6 Å cut (cite: cid11)." (2 Sample and Observations)
   - parameter: S/N > 3 for H-alpha and H-beta, > 2 for [OIII] and [NII], > 2.5 for CO, HCN, HCO+  
  > "We limit our analyses to spaxels having signal-to-noise (S/N) \> 3 for strong emission lines, i.e., H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$ lines, S/N \> 2 for weak lines" (2 Sample and Observations)
   - parameter: retired: H-alpha EW < 3 A and S/N > 3 in H-alpha  
  > "The retired spaxels are selected to be those having H$\alpha$ EW $<$ 3 Å and S/N $>$ 3 in H$\alpha$ (cite: ell21b)." (2 Sample and Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Trends stated to be stable against the lower S/N limits  
  > "The trends presented in this work are found to be stable against the lower limits of the S/N cut." (2 Sample and Observations)
7. **Resolved relations: dense-gas SK relation, SFE_mol vs f_dense and SFE_dense, sSFR dependences**: Spaxel-by-spaxel Pearson correlations for all spaxel types, ODR fit of the star-forming spaxels of 7815-12705 as reference line, comparison with EMPIRE; sSFR decomposed into SFE x f_dense x f_mol  
  > "The Pearson correlation coefficients ($r$) are computed using all types of spaxels and are displayed in each panel." (3.1 Dense gas Schmidt-Kennicutt Relation); "we fit the star-forming spaxels of the main sequence galaxy 7815-12705 with the orthogonal distance regression (ODR) fitting method for the three gas tracers separately" (3.1 Dense gas Schmidt-Kennicutt Relation)
   - fitting: ODR fit to star-forming spaxels of 7815-12705 for each gas tracer; priors/likelihood not applicable  
  > "we fit the star-forming spaxels of the main sequence galaxy 7815-12705 with the orthogonal distance regression (ODR) fitting method for the three gas tracers separately" (3.1 Dense gas Schmidt-Kennicutt Relation)
   - validation: Shuffling tests and S/N-limited tests (steps 8)  
  > "To test this effect, we generate synthesized datasets by shuffling $\Sigma_{\rm mol}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm dense}$" (3.3 Is the molecular gas SFE dependent on the dense gas SFE?)
8. **Test for artificial correlations from shared denominators/numerators**: Generate synthetic datasets by shuffling Sigma_mol and Sigma_dense (and Sigma_mol and Sigma_SFR), 500 trials each; repeat on the central half of the Sigma_SFR range  
  > "The procedure is then repeated 500 times." (3.3 Is the molecular gas SFE dependent on the dense gas SFE?); "by only selecting data points in the central half of the $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ range, specifically -3 \< Log$_{10}$$\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ \< -1.5" (3.3 Is the molecular gas SFE dependent on the dense gas SFE?)
   - parameter: 500 trials; Sigma_SFR range -3 < log10 Sigma_SFR < -1.5 for the restricted test  
  > "The procedure is then repeated 500 times." (3.3 Is the molecular gas SFE dependent on the dense gas SFE?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Median Pearson r of shuffled data compared with real data  
  > "the correlation between $\rm SFE_{\rm mol}$ and $\rm SFE_{\rm dense}$ is stronger when using the real physical dataset than in the synthesized data" (3.3 Is the molecular gas SFE dependent on the dense gas SFE?)
9. **Check S/N effects and compare global properties with literature samples**: Repeat SK correlations with an upper S/N(CO) limit of 10; compare global SFR vs L_CO/L_HCN/L_HCO+ and L_HCN/L_CO with literature samples  
  > "we repeat the correlation analyses for the star-forming spaxels in main sequence galaxies but impose an upper limit of S/N(CO) = 10 this time" (4.1 Scatter in Schmidt-Kennicutt relations); "we plot the global SFR versus the ratio of dense molecular gas to total molecular gas traced by $L_{\rm{HCN}}$/$L_{\rm{CO}}$ (left panel)" (4.2 Comparison with previous studies in the literature)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Non-star-forming (retired plus other) spaxels deviate from the dense-gas SK relation of star-forming spaxels toward lower Sigma_SFR, for both HCN and HCO+  
  > "the non-star-forming spaxels, which dominate the regions of GV galaxies, deviate significantly from the dense gas SK relation formed by the star-forming spaxels toward lower values of $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ at a given dense gas surface density" (5 Summary)
- Star-forming and non-star-forming spaxels share a similar range of f_dense despite different SFE_mol  
  > "the two types of spaxels (star-forming vs. non-star-forming) share a similar range of $f_{\rm dense}$" (3.2 Is the molecular gas SFE dependent on the dense gas fraction?)
- 4 of 5 galaxies have HCN/HCO+ > 1 in most spaxels; 8081-12703 (GV) is centred near 1  
  > "One can see that 4 out of 5 galaxies have HCN/$\rm HCO^{+}$ $>$ 1 in most spaxels." (4.1 Scatter in Schmidt-Kennicutt relations)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HCN and HCO+ are fainter than CO by more than an order of magnitude, so the sample starts from CO-bright MS and GV galaxies to obtain detections in reasonable ALMA time; the resolution is matched to MaNGA and CO  
  > "we start with the CO-bright sample for both GV and MS targets, allowing us to obtain a possible HCN and $\rm HCO^{+}$ detection within a reasonable amount of ALMA integration time" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Spatially resolved observations are needed because dense gas is more concentrated than CO or stars, so a global HCN/CO ratio does not capture gas-phase variations  
  > "Spatially resolved observations are therefore critical for assessing star formation tracers, CO, and HCN (and $\rm HCO^{+}$) on well-matched scales." (1 Introduction)
- Alternative: Variable (e.g. metallicity-dependent) conversion factors were not adopted; fixed alpha_CO, alpha_HCN and alpha_HCO+ used for all 5 galaxies and for comparison with literature  
  > "for the purposes of this study and for making a comparison with other works in the literature, we have chosen to utilize fixed conversion factors" (2 Sample and Observations)
- Alternative: Different SFR tracers (EMPIRE total infrared vs H-alpha here) judged not responsible for the dense gas SK offset, because the CO-based SK relation agrees  
  > "However, the difference in the star formation rate tracers does not seem to be responsible for the systematic difference seen in the dense gas SK relation between our samples since there is a good agreement in the CO-based SK relation." (3.1 Dense gas Schmidt-Kennicutt Relation)
- How they decided it was enough: S/N thresholds chosen by balancing uncertainty and number of spaxels  
  > "The choice of the S/N threshold is made by balancing the uncertainty and the number of spaxels." (2 Sample and Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is biased toward CO-bright galaxies not deficient in molecular gas  
  > "The sample is thus biased toward galaxies that are not deficient in molecular gas." (1 Introduction)
- Limitation (authors): Fixed dense-gas conversion factors may not hold; dense-gas conversion factors poorly known  
  > "we cannot be certain that the fixed conversion factors hold for the galaxies in this study or even for EMPIRE" (3.1 Dense gas Schmidt-Kennicutt Relation)
- Limitation (authors): Narrow dynamic range in dense gas surface density and few data points  
  > "the dynamical range in the dense gas surface density in our sample is rather narrow and the number of data points is small" (4.1 Scatter in Schmidt-Kennicutt relations)
- Limitation (authors): Coarse resolution and moderate S/N prevent examining the link between SFE and velocity dispersion  
  > "Owing to the coarse spatial resolution and moderate S/N of the dense gas observations used in this work, it is not feasible to directly examine the link between SFE and velocity dispersion." (4.1 Scatter in Schmidt-Kennicutt relations)
- Limitation (authors): H-alpha SFR in retired spaxels is only an upper limit; correlation analyses affected by shared numerator/denominator  
  > "the SFR estimated using the H$\alpha$ to SFR conversion serves only as an upper limit for retired spaxels" (2 Sample and Observations)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only 5 galaxies (3 GV, 2 MS) with a few hundred spaxels at a 2.5 arcsec beam; spaxels are oversampled (0.5 arcsec pixels) so they are not independent and Pearson r uncertainties are not given
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed alpha_HCN = alpha_HCO+ = 10 can systematically shift SFE_dense; GV vs MS differences could partly reflect different excitation or conversion factors (HCN/HCO+ ratio differs in 8081-12703)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): H-alpha SFR in retired spaxels is contaminated by evolved stars, so low SFE_dense in these regions is an upper limit and the 'retired' vs 'star-forming' split is partly circular
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainties on integrated intensities or luminosities in Table 2; sensitivity and flux-scale errors are not propagated into the correlation analysis
