## Analysis recipe

1. **Phase-centrevisibilities, remove secondary sources, and image ALMA Band 7 continuum**: Run standard ALMA pipeline in CASA 5.4.0, phase-centre the calibrated measurement set on the 2D Gaussian centroid using fixvis, model and subtract secondary sources in three fields (cid_451, cid_1205, cid_1143) with a 2D Gaussian and uvsub, and image with tclean using natural weighting (Briggs robust = 2).  
  > "to phase-centre the calibrated measurement set on the central position of the source, determined by fitting a 2D Gaussian profile on the reduced image provided by the ALMA datareduction pipeline." (3.1 Data reduction and imaging); "We modelled the secondary source with a 2D Gaussian and then we removed it from the uv -data using the CASA task uvsub ." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging); "We generated the maps using the Briggs weighting scheme with robust parameter = 2 (corresponding to natural weighting)." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging)
   - software: CASA, fixvis, tclean, uvsub
   - parameter: cleaning threshold = 2xRMS, pixel size = 1/5 beam size, Briggs robust = 2 (and tested robust = 0.5)  
  > "We set the cleaning threshold of tclean to 2×RMS and the pixel size to be one fifth of the beam size." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit on pipeline image for phase centre and on secondary sources for uvsub  
  > "to phase-centre the calibrated measurement set on the central position of the source, determined by fitting a 2D Gaussian profile on the reduced image provided by the ALMA datareduction pipeline." (3.1 Data reduction and imaging)
   - validation: Tested simultaneous two-source uv-plane fitting and robust = 0.5 imaging; found consistent sizes and morphologies.  
  > "We also tested that fitting the two sources simultaneously in the uv -plane gives consistent results." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging); "We also created maps using the Briggs weighting scheme with robust = 0.5 (higher resolution, beam size 0.16-0.20”) and we found no appreciable difference in the morphology and size of the FIR emission that would affect our conclusions." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging)
2. **Measure FIR continuum sizes, morphologies, and flux densities**: Extract visibility amplitudes from phase-centred MS, bin in 50 klambda uv-distance intervals, and fit symmetric point source, 2D Gaussian, 2D Gaussian + point source, and exponential (Sersic n=1) models in Python using MCMC (PyStan), selecting the best model via BIC. Also fit 2D Gaussian in uv-plane with CASA uvmodelfit (for axial ratio and PA) and in image plane with CASA viewer.  
  > "We extracted the visibility amplitudes from the phase-centred calibrated measurements sets. Then, we binned the data in uv -distance intervals of 50 kλ." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "We tested the following models: point source, 2D Gaussian, 2D Gaussian plus a point source, and exponential profile (equivalent to a Sérsic profile with index n = 1)." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "To assess the preferred model to fit our data, we use the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC; Schwarz 1978):" (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities)
   - software: CASA, uvmodelfit, CASA viewer, Python, Stan, PyStan
   - parameter: uv-distance bin width = 50 klambda; uvmodelfit iterations = 20; S/N >= 8 threshold for reliable sizes  
  > "We ran uvmodelfit on the phase-centred calibrated measurement sets assuming a 2D Gaussian model and using 20 iterations, which are enough to reach convergence." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "Then, we binned the data in uv -distance intervals of 50 kλ." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities)
   - assumption: Cosmology: Omega_lambda = 0.7, Omega_M = 0.3, H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1; symmetrical models (axial ratio = 1) for collapsed visibility fits  
  > "Throughout this work, we assume wavelength in vacuum and a cosmological model with Ω λ = 0.7, Ω M = 0.3, and H 0 = 70 km s −1 Mpc −1 ." (1. Introduction); "For simplicity, we only considered symmetrical models, that is an axial ratio of one." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities)
   - fitting: MCMC via PyStan with Gaussian likelihood, uniform priors (Table A.1), median of marginal posterior as best fit, 16th-84th percentiles as uncertainties, and BIC for model selection.  
  > "Specifically, we employed PyStan 4, the python interface to Stan . We assumed a Gaussian likelihood and use uniform priors, allowing the free parameters to vary in a large parameter range (the prior ranges are tabulated in Table A.1 in the appendix)." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "We derived the best fit parameters by taking the median values from the marginal posterior distributions. The uncertainties are given as the 16th and 84th percentiles of the posterior distributions." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities)
   - validation: Compared collapsed uv-visibility fits against CASA uvmodelfit, image-plane 2D Gaussian fits in CASA viewer, 0.5" uv-tapered maps for low-S/N sources, and archival low-resolution ALMA 260 um fluxes.  
  > "We verify that we obtain consistent results by performing the analysis also in the image plane, using the 2D Gaussian fitting routine available in the CASA viewer ." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "Thus, we also check the flux densities derived from the ALMA images created by applying tapering (with tapering size = 0.5") using CASA ." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "To assess how much flux we are potentially missing in our ALMA data, we compare these flux measurements with other 260 µm flux measurements from lower resolution data from the ALMA Archive (compiled by Circosta et al. 2018) and from Scholtz et al. (2020)." (3.3.2. Flux densities)
3. **Fit UV-to-FIR SEDs and quantify AGN and synchrotron contributions to 260 um flux**: Fit UV-to-FIR photometry including ALMA Band 7 fluxes using CIGALE to derive M_star, SFR, and AGN torus contribution at rest-frame 260 um; cross-check with F_870um/F_24um diagnostic, a modified black-body (MBB) torus model, and radio-extrapolated power-law synchrotron emission.  
  > "We use the same SED fitting approach used by Circosta et al. (2018) for the full SUPER sample, with the only difference that we include the ALMA band 7 photometry." (3.4. SED fits including the ALMA flux densities); "As an alternative approach, we model the torus emission using a modified black-body (MBB) following the methodology described by Lani et al. (2017)." (5.1. Origin of the FIR ALMA Band 7 emission); "To estimate the contribution from synchrotron emission to the ALMA Band 7 flux (rest-frame ∼260 µm), we extrapolate from the radio fluxes using a power-law model (Dicken et al. 2008)." (5.1. Origin of the FIR ALMA Band 7 emission)
   - software: CIGALE, AGNfitter

## Qualitative findings

- Rest-frame 260 um continuum emission in the high-S/N SUPER AGN is dominated by dust heated by star formation, with <= 4% contribution from AGN-heated dust and <= 1% from synchrotron emission.  
  > "From spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting, we find that the main contribution to the 260 µm flux density is dust heated by star formation, with ≤ 4% contribution from AGN-heated dust and ≤ 1% from synchrotron emission." (abstract)
- Mean FIR size in z ~ 2 X-ray AGN is slightly smaller than in non-AGN when combining SUPER with literature samples.  
  > "However, combining our sample with the literature samples, we find that the mean FIR size in X-ray AGN (R e = 1.16 ± 0.11 kpc) is slightly smaller than in non-AGN (R e = 1.69 ± 0.13 kpc)." (abstract)
- Halpha emission is dominated by AGN ionisation in all but one target (cid_346) based on BPT diagnostics, preventing its use as an unobscured star formation tracer.  
  > "In summary, the BPT analysis suggests that Hα emission is dominated by AGN ionisation in most of our sample." (5.3.1. Origin of the Hα emission); "Our BPT analysis confirms that for all but one target (cid_346) the Hα emission is AGN dominated. Thus, we cannot use Hα as a star formation tracer in our sample" (5.5. Implications of our results)
- FIR continuum and ionised gas (Halpha and [O III]) exhibit different morphologies and spatial offsets in 4/6 high-S/N targets, and in two targets (cid_346, cid_1143) the ionised gas/outflow is oriented roughly perpendicular to the FIR major axis.  
  > "Specifically, in most of our targets (4/6) there is a significant offset between the central position of the ionised gas emission and the FIR emission (0.4 − 1.9 kpc)." (6. Summary and conclusions); "In at least two objects (cid_346 and cid_1143), the outflow is almost perpendicular to the direction of the major axis of the FIR emission, which can be an indication that the outflow propagates following the path of least resistance." (6. Summary and conclusions)
- No clear evidence of instantaneous suppression or disturbance of obscured star formation by [O III] ionised outflows is observed on ~2 kpc scales.  
  > "Under the assumption that the FIR emission is a reliable tracer of obscured star formation, we find that the obscured star formation activity in these AGN host galaxies is not clearly affected by the ionised outflows." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 7 continuum observations at 0.15-0.30" (~2 kpc) resolution were chosen to trace rest-frame ~260 um dust-obscured star formation at matched spatial resolution to the SINFONI AO IFS maps of Halpha and [O III] outflows, for SUPER targets with prior FIR photometry and [O III] detections.  
  > "To select the sub-sample to be observed with ALMA, we considered all the SUPER objects with at least one photometric detection, based on the photometry we had in hand (i.e. a signal-to-noise S/N - 3 in the catalogue from Circosta et al. 2018) in the FIR" (2. Sample); "We use FIR continuum observations from ALMA with the goal of tracing the dust obscured star formation at the same resolution (∼2 kpc) as the [O iii] and Hα emission line maps extracted from our SINFONI IFS data." (3. ALMA observations and analyses)
- Why this method: Fitting sizes directly in the uv-visibility plane avoids dependence on imaging choices (weighting, cleaning threshold), while natural weighting (robust = 2) in tclean was selected to closely match the ~0.3" PSF of the SINFONI IFS maps.  
  > "The beam sizes obtained using natural weighting are in the range 0.17-0.25” and closely match the resolution of the SINFONI maps." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging); "Measuring the sizes directly from the uv -visibilities has the advantage that it is not dependent on the choices made to create the images (e.g. weighting scheme, cleaning threshold, ...)." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities)
- Alternative: Briggs weighting with robust = 0.5 (higher resolution, 0.16-0.20" beam) was tested for ALMA imaging, but natural weighting (robust = 2) was used because no appreciable difference in morphology or size was found and robust = 2 better matched the SINFONI PSF.  
  > "We also created maps using the Briggs weighting scheme with robust = 0.5 (higher resolution, beam size 0.16-0.20”) and we found no appreciable difference in the morphology and size of the FIR emission that would affect our conclusions." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging)
- Alternative: Simultaneous two-source uv-plane fitting was tested as an alternative to subtracting the secondary source with uvsub in cid_451, cid_1205, and cid_1143, yielding consistent results.  
  > "We also tested that fitting the two sources simultaneously in the uv -plane gives consistent results." (3.1. Data reduction and imaging)
- Alternative: Mapping Halpha star formation from residuals after subtracting AGN-dominated line components (as in Carniani et al. 2016) was considered, but rejected because no significant Halpha residual emission was present.  
  > "After subtracting the emission line fitting model from our data, we do not find any significant residual at the position of Hα, both in the maps and in the integrated spectra (see residual panels in Figure 3). Thus, we cannot use this method to map the Hα emission from star formation." (4.4. Hα and [OIII] maps)
- Alternative: Akaike Information Criterion (AIC and AICc) was considered alongside BIC for model selection and noted to give the same qualitative results.  
  > "5 We note that using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) or the Akaike Information Criterion corrected for sample size (Akaike 1973, 1974) will give the same qualitative results." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities)
- How they decided it was enough: Adopted a peak S/N >= 8 - 10 threshold in ALMA maps for reliable size measurements (excluding cid_1057 and cid_451 from resolved analyses), BIC to select the preferred visibility profile and Type 2 spectral line components, and 20 iterations for uvmodelfit convergence.  
  > "To assess the preferred model to fit our data, we use the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC; Schwarz 1978):" (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "At least a S/N≳ 10 on the flux is required to obtain reliable size measurements (Simpson et al. 2015)." (3.2. Modelling the ALMA continuum data: sizes and flux densities); "( ⋆ ) If the peak S / N < 8 in the ALMA maps, we do not consider the size measurements to be reliable." (Table 2)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA sample is biased towards heavily X-ray obscured (log N_H > 22), optical Type 2 AGN, and the upper end of the SFR distribution of the SUPER parent sample because of the FIR pre-selection.  
  > "In summary, although the ALMA sample presented in this paper has bolometric luminosities which are representative of the parent sample, we are biased towards heavily obscured (high N H ) and Type 2 targets." (2. Sample); "Given the presence of many SFR upper limits in the parent sample, our targets are likely to represent the upper end of the SFR distribution of the SUPER sample." (3.4. SED fits including the ALMA flux densities)
- Limitation (authors): Two of the eight targets (cid_1057 and cid_451) have low ALMA peak S/N (3.6 and 5.9), which is insufficient for reliable size measurements and spatially resolved analysis.  
  > "The other two targets were detected with S/N of 3.6 and 5.9, which are insufficient for spatially resolved analysis." (abstract)
- Limitation (authors): High-resolution ALMA observations (~0.2") may resolve out diffuse, extended FIR emission.  
  > "Our high-resolution ALMA images are potentially missing some of the more diffuse emission, and therefore the measured flux densities cannot be considered as ‘total’ flux densities." (3.3.2. Flux densities)
- Limitation (authors): Using monochromatic 260 um continuum to map obscured SFR assumes negligible spatial variation in dust temperature and emissivity index beta, and lacks radiative transfer modelling of host-scale diffuse dust heating by the AGN.  
  > "Detailed radiative transfer modelling would be required to test this effect on our sample, but this is beyond the scope of this work." (5.1. Origin of the FIR ALMA Band 7 emission); "In this study, we use the monochromatic FIR emission at ∼ 260 µm to trace obscured star formation. This relies on the assumption that spatial variations of the shape of the FIR SED (due to changes in the dust properties, as for example the dust temperature and emissivity index β) are negligible." (5.5. Implications of our results)
- Limitation (authors): SINFONI observations lack the S/N and spatial resolution for spatially resolved BPT diagnostics or Balmer decrement extinction corrections, and AO observations may miss extended line flux.  
  > "We note that we did not correct the line fluxes for obscuration. In most of the cases we are not able to calculate the Balmer decrement, because the Hβ line is not detected." (4.3. Spectral line fitting); "However, due to the limited S/N of our observations, we cannot perform a BPT analysis in a spatially resolved manner." (4.4. Hα and [OIII] maps)
- Limitation (authors): The FIR vs non-AGN size comparison is limited by small sample size and heterogeneity across literature surveys.  
  > "The significance of the difference in dust size between AGN and non-AGN presented in this paper is still limited by the sample size and by the lack of homogeneity in the samples (although we have tried to minimise these as much as possible)." (5.2. FIR size comparison with other samples from the literature)
- Limitation (authors): Star formation suppression occurring on spatial scales smaller than the ~2 kpc resolution or on different timescales cannot be ruled out.  
  > "However, we cannot rule out that star formation suppression is happening on smaller spatial scales than the ones we probe with our observations (< 2 kpc) or on different timescales." (abstract)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Symmetrical 1D collapsed uv-visibility models were used for the primary size measurements despite three of the six high-S/N sources showing significant elongation (axial ratios 1.3 to 4.6 in uvmodelfit).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparing Gaussian-derived sizes for some literature samples with exponential-derived sizes in Tadaki et al. (2020) mixes profile definitions that systematically differ by ~38%, though the authors checked single-model subsets.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SINFONI astrometric uncertainties (0.03-0.14") are comparable to the measured FIR-to-ionised-gas centroid offsets (0.02-0.23") for several targets.
