## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure OH 119 um absorption velocities and equivalent widths**: Fit the OH spectra and derive non-parametric v50, v84, vmax (98% of absorption) and equivalent widths over blueshifted velocity ranges, for both SPT and low-z samples  
  > "we use the fits to the spectra to measure the velocities above which 50 and 84% of the absorption takes place" (2.2 OH Outflow Training Sample); "that we take to be the velocity above which 98% of the absorption takes place" (2.2 OH Outflow Training Sample)
   - parameter: Low-z PACS spectra broadened by FWHM~300 km/s instrumental resolution accounted for  
  > "including the broadening from the FWHM$\approx$300" (2.2 OH Outflow Training Sample)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Quantities are non-parametric and depend little on the fitting method  
  > "We note that all these quantities are non-parametric and therefore depend little on the exact methods used to fit the PACS spectra." (2.2 OH Outflow Training Sample)
2. **Adopt outflow geometry and energetics definitions**: Time-averaged thin shell, n ~ r^-2, constant outflow rate; Mdot = 4 pi R^2 mu m_H N_H v/R; pdot = Mdot v; Edot = 0.5 Mdot v^2  
  > "in which a mass-conserving outflow with constant outflow rate expands following a density profile $n \propto r^{-2}$" (2.1 Assumed Outflow Geometry); "where the expression for kinetic power assumes negligible contribution from turbulent (i.e. non-bulk) sources." (2.1 Assumed Outflow Geometry)
   - parameter: v_out = v84 (velocity above which 84% of absorption occurs)  
  > "We take the characteristic velocity to be $v_{84}$, the velocity above which 84% of the absorption occurs." (2.1 Assumed Outflow Geometry)
   - parameter: R_out = r_dust, effective radius of the rest-frame ~100 um dust emission (lens reconstruction for SPT, PACS for low-z)  
  > "the effective radius of the dust emission at rest-frame" (2.1 Assumed Outflow Geometry)
   - assumption: Mean mass per hydrogen atom 1.4 (includes helium)  
  > "$\overline{\mu} = 1.4$ the mean mass per hydrogen atom" (2.1 Assumed Outflow Geometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with published low-z momentum and energy; may overestimate momentum by ~30% and energy by ~70%, no systematic difference in Mdot  
  > "we may be overestimating the outflow momentum and energy by $\sim$30 and 70%, respectively, while we find no systematic difference in the mass outflow rates" (4.4 Outflow Momentum and Energetics)
3. **Optically thin outflow rate and empirical correction versus vmax**: Compute minimum OH column density under optically thin absorption, integrate tau*v over v < -200 km/s, then fit a log-linear correction of Mdot_thin to published rates as a function of vmax  
  > "Under the assumption that the absorption is optically thin, the minimum column density of OH molecules" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model); "Here we calculate the integrated optical depth over velocities more blueshifted than" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
   - parameter: T_ex = 100 K  
  > "We assume an excitation temperature $T_\mathrm{ex} = 100$ K as found in literature OH studies" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
   - parameter: [OH/H] = 2.5e-6  
  > "We adopt an OH abundance $[\mathrm{OH}/\mathrm{H}]=2.5 \times 10^{-6}$, as commonly assumed in the literature" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
   - parameter: f_cov = 0.3 (rescales Mdot_thin linearly, absorbed in intercept b)  
  > "we adopt a covering fraction $\ensuremath{f_{\mathrm{cov}}}= 0.3$, the average value determined for the low-redshift reference sample" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
   - assumption: Optically thin absorption gives a hard lower bound on the outflow rate  
  > "this calculation does at least provide a hard lower bound on the true outflow rate" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
   - fitting: Log-linear fit with Monte Carlo resampling of uncertainties; intrinsic dispersion ~0.15 dex  
  > "determining uncertainties on the fit using the same Monte Carlo resampling method we use for all outflow rate techniques." (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model); "The analysis indicates an intrinsic dispersion of $\sim$0.15 dex around the best-fit relation" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
   - validation: Correlation of the thin-to-published ratio with outflow velocity checked with v84 and v50 as well, consistent within uncertainties  
  > "we obtain results consistent within the uncertainties from $v_{84}$ and $v_{50}$ as well" (3.1 Simple Optically-Thin Model)
4. **Simple empirical linear estimates (S18-like and HC20-like)**: Fit published low-z Mdot linearly against EW(v<-100), and against EW(v<-200)*sqrt(L_IR/1e12 Lsun), then apply to SPT sources  
  > "here we simply fit a linear relationship between the published literature" (3.2 Simple Empirical Estimates); "which is more readily available for all literature reference sources" (3.2 Simple Empirical Estimates)
   - parameter: Intercept free (not forced through zero) in both fits  
  > "We do not force this fit to have a zero intercept." (3.2 Simple Empirical Estimates); "we do not fit a line forcing the y-intercept to be zero as done in" (3.2 Simple Empirical Estimates)
   - parameter: Constant intrinsic scatter of 150 Msun/yr assumed  
  > "we assume a constant 150" (3.2 Simple Empirical Estimates)
   - fitting: Linear fits with Monte Carlo resampling (sampler and priors not stated)  
  > "we estimate uncertainties on the derived outflow rates through a Monte Carlo procedure, repeatedly resampling the measurements within the uncertainties" (3 Outflow Rate Estimates)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Multivariate partial least squares estimate**: PLS regression from OH velocity metrics, EWs, L_IR and f_AGN to published Mdot of the reference sample, applied to SPT sources  
  > "(PLS) technique (cite: wold66)" (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate); "the combination of parameters that includes $v_{50}$, $v_{84}$, $v_\mathrm{max}$, $\mathrm{EW}_{v < -100}$, $\mathrm{EW}_{v < -200}$, $\mathrm{EW}_{\mathrm{total}}$, $L_{\rm{IR}}$, and $f_\mathrm{AGN}$" (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate)
   - parameter: Four or five PLS components minimized the mean squared error for the reference sample  
  > "a maximum of four or five PLS components minimized the mean squared error in the predicted outflow rates of the reference sample" (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: No detectable intrinsic scatter between predicted and published rates; scatter upper limit ~100 Msun/yr; consistent results across many observable combinations  
  > "there is no longer any detectable intrinsic scatter between the predicted and published outflow rates" (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate); "We experimented extensively with various numbers and combinations of observables and found consistent results for the predicted outflow rates of the SPT sources in almost all cases." (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate)
6. **Combine methods into joint outflow rate**: Equal combination of the Monte Carlo trials of the four methods gives Mdot_joint, adopted as best estimate  
  > "We also show a joint distribution of the outflow rates created by equally combining the Monte Carlo trials from each method." (3.4 Summary and Method Comparison)
   - assumption: Joint distribution is not a true joint probability since methods are not independent  
  > "While this should not be considered a true joint probability distribution of the outflow rates" (3.4 Summary and Method Comparison)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Four methods agree at about factor-of-two level; uncertainties probably underestimated by systematics  
  > "suggest that we are able to estimate the outflow rates for our sources at about the factor-of-two level." (3.4 Summary and Method Comparison)
7. **Derive masses, mass loading, depletion times, momentum and energy and compare with literature and theory limits**: Compute Mout, pdot, Edot and eta=Mdot/SFR; compare with L_AGN, L_SF momentum and energy coupling limits and with low-z samples; fit power law of Mdot vs SFR to combined samples  
  > "Meanwhile, a continuous starburst can generate a maximum of" (4.4 Outflow Momentum and Energetics); "Fitting a power-law to the combined samples, we find a best-fit relationship" (4.2 Molecular Outflow Rate Scaling Relations)
   - parameter: SFR = 1.49e-10 L_IR  
  > "SFR$= 1.49 \times 10^{-10}" (1 Introduction)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 0.8 Msun (K km/s pc^2)^-1 for SPT sources  
  > "For the SPT sample, we assume $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\rm{CO}}}= 0.8$" (4.3 Outflow Masses and Depletion Times)
   - assumption: Sensitivity tested by doubling f_AGN limits and raising CO-based outflow rates by 0.5 dex  
  > "We also detail changes to our interpretation that would result from doubling our present upper limits on" (4.2 Molecular Outflow Rate Scaling Relations)
   - fitting: Power law with intrinsic scatter fit to combined samples (method details not stated)  
  > "an additional intrinsic scatter on the outflow rates of $\approx$0.25 dex" (4.2 Molecular Outflow Rate Scaling Relations)
   - validation: Conclusions checked against factor-of-two change in f_AGN, CO outflow rate offset and lower characteristic velocity  
  > "our conclusions are also not changed in the event that our present limits on" (4.4 Outflow Momentum and Energetics)
8. **Estimate escape fractions of outflowing gas**: Assume outflow mass versus velocity proportional to equivalent width versus velocity (excluding systemic absorption) and compare with galaxy escape velocity from a truncated isothermal sphere  
  > "assuming the outflowing mass as a function of velocity is directly proportional to the equivalent width as a function of velocity" (4.5 Fate of the Outflowing Gas); "To estimate the galaxy escape velocity for each source, we assume a spherical isothermal mass distribution truncated at a maximum radius" (4.5 Fate of the Outflowing Gas)
   - parameter: r_max/r = 10; r = r_dust (circularized effective dust size from lens reconstruction)  
  > "we take $r$ to be the circularized effective size of the dust emission from our lensing reconstructions" (4.5 Fate of the Outflowing Gas)
   - assumption: Galaxy mass from molecular gas mass assuming a typical DSFG gas fraction; outflowing gas at distance equal to dust size; no further acceleration or deceleration  
  > "assuming a typical gas fraction for DSFGs at these redshifts (cite: aravena16)" (4.5 Fate of the Outflowing Gas); "we also assume that the outflowing gas is no longer being accelerated" (4.5 Fate of the Outflowing Gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Escape velocities agree with simple estimates from CO or [CII] line widths and point-like mass profiles; typical uncertainty ~40%  
  > "we estimate typical uncertainties on the galaxy escape velocities of $\approx$40%" (4.5 Fate of the Outflowing Gas)
9. **Compare escaping outflow mass and metals with CGM budgets**: Sum outflow mass above the escape velocity (below halo escape velocity), assume solar metallicity for metal mass, compare with cool CGM gas and metal mass of z~2-3 quasar hosts  
  > "under the simplifying assumption that the molecular outflows have approximately solar metallicity" (4.6 Implications for Circumgalactic Medium Enrichment)
   - assumption: All escaping material enters the CGM; the quasar CGM range is an approximate upper bound for the DSFGs  
  > "We assume all of this material enters the CGM" (4.6 Implications for Circumgalactic Medium Enrichment)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- No SPT source shows evidence of AGN in the mid-IR or other data; deeply buried AGN cannot be ruled out  
  > "No source shows evidence of an AGN in the mid-IR (or in any other data, e.g." (3 Outflow Rate Estimates); "we cannot rule out that deeply buried AGN are present" (5 Conclusions)
- Tentative correlation of outflow velocity with L_IR within the z>4 sample, absent in combined low-z sample  
  > "The same is not true for the combined low-redshift galaxies with OH data." (5 Conclusions)
- Mass loading factors are sub-unity and uncorrelated with other quantities  
  > "The mass loading factors do not clearly correlate with any other quantity including SFR or" (5 Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Redshift of each target placed the OH 119 um lines in an ALMA atmospheric window; targets are intrinsically luminous lensed SPT DSFGs  
  > "our primary selection criterion was merely that the redshift of each target place the OH 119" (5 Conclusions)
- Why this method: PLS suits few reference objects with many correlated observables and handles extrapolation better than e.g. random forests  
  > "PLS is well-suited to cases such as ours where the number of objects in the reference sample is relatively few but the number of measured quantities for each sample object is large" (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate)
- Alternative: Four CO-based sources excluded from the empirical outflow rate estimation out of caution  
  > "but we exclude them from our later empirical outflow rate estimation out of an abundance of caution" (2.2 OH Outflow Training Sample)
- Alternative: Other simple geometries give higher outflow energetics; the thin-shell geometry is adopted as conservative  
  > "this assumed geometry leads to more conservative outflow energetics than other simple geometries" (2.1 Assumed Outflow Geometry)
- How they decided it was enough: Number of PLS components chosen by minimizing mean squared error in the reference sample  
  > "a maximum of four or five PLS components minimized the mean squared error in the predicted outflow rates of the reference sample" (3.3 Multivariate Empirical Estimate)
- Limitation (authors): Broad applicability of the empirical outflow-rate formulae is questionable  
  > "we caution that the broad applicability of these formulae is questionable." (3 Outflow Rate Estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties likely underestimated due to systematics in assumptions  
  > "We emphasize that for both samples these uncertainties are likely underestimated due to systematics in many of the assumptions" (3.4 Summary and Method Comparison)
- Limitation (authors): Upper limits on outflow properties cannot be set for the 3 sources without unambiguous outflows  
  > "It is essentially not possible to set upper limits on the outflow properties for the remaining 3 sources" (3 Outflow Rate Estimates)
- Limitation (authors): Sample is not cleanly selected or complete  
  > "it is by no means a cleanly selected or complete sample" (5 Conclusions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow rates rest on low-z OH training sample biased toward fast outflows; transferring to z>4 DSFGs is untested
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single characteristic velocity v84 and R_out = r_dust drive the energetics; momentum and energy admitted to be overestimated
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Small training sample (~13 OH sources) for PLS with many predictors; cross-validation not reported
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Joint distribution combines non-independent methods with equal weights
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data processing and OH spectral fitting are entirely deferred to Paper I, so this paper alone is not self-contained
