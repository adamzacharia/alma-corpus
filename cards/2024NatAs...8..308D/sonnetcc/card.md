## Science

- Fields: evolved_stars, astrochemistry
- Question: Can anisotropic molecular emission (SiN, SiC, NS), photodissociation signatures and CO density structures around the AGB star W Aql constrain the orbit of its F9 main-sequence companion?  
  > "For the first time, we have used a combined chemical and dynamical analysis to reveal a highly eccentric and long-period orbit for W Aquilae, a binary system containing an AGB star and a main sequence companion." (abstract)
- Target **W Aql (S-type AGB star with F9 companion)**: coordinates _not stated_; z/distance 395 pc

## Analysis recipe

1. **combine configurations and make spectral cubes**: Combine the three ALMA configurations in CASA and image one cube per transition with baseline weighting tuned for resolution and surface brightness sensitivity  
  > "We combined the three data sets to maximise the sensitivity of images" (3.4.1 Data reduction); "weighting the contributions of the baselines to optimise the resolution and surface brightness sensitivity" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Velocity resolution 1.1-1.3 km/s; 2 or more channels averaged in some cases  
  > "depending on frequency, and in some cases we averaged 2 or more channels to increase sensitivity" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - parameter: Typical rms <~ 2 mJy  
  > "The typical rms noise is $\lessapprox2$ mJy." (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - assumption: All velocities in the LSR frame  
  > "All velocities are adjusted to the LSR frame." (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Interferometric noise artefacts at >=5 sigma judged negligible; relative astrometry ~0.002 arcsec (extended) and ~0.005 arcsec (combined)  
  > "The chances of interferometric noise causing artefacts $\geq 5 \sigma$ in these images is negligible." (3.4.1 Data reduction)
2. **make moment-zero maps and position-velocity diagrams**: Moment zero maps from channels above ~3 sigma; PV diagrams from a 3 arcsec wide slit through the AGB star at 33 deg east of north, chosen by scanning slit angles  
  > "Moment zero (integrated intensity) maps were made by summing all the channels with emission above $\sim3\sigma_{\mathrm{rms}}$." (3.4.1 Data reduction); "The final slit position of $33^\circ$ east of north was chosen on the basis of the clarity and intensity of the associated PV diagram." (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
   - parameter: Slit width 3 arcsec (0.3 arcsec gave lower SNR)  
  > "We produced a series of position-velocity diagrams of SiN using a wide slit (total width $3^{\prime\prime}$) to encompass all the emission seen in the zeroth moment map" (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
   - parameter: Slit angles scanned in 5 deg steps, then 1 deg steps around the best angles  
  > "We tested all possible slit angles passing through the position of the AGB star in increments of $5^\circ$ and then $1^\circ$ around the angles producing the most distinct PV diagrams." (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
   - parameter: Continuum peak taken as AGB star position (0,0)  
  > "The peak of the continuum emission was assumed to be the position of the AGB star." (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - fitting: Parabola fitted to PV points >= 3 sigma, weighted by flux; partial ellipse (half-width 14 km/s for SiN, 13 km/s for SiC) overplotted to follow extreme velocities  
  > "We fit a parabola to the points in the PV diagram with intensities $\geq 3 \sigma$ above the noise, weighted by the flux at those points." (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
   - validation: Independently chosen slit angle passes through the present position of the F9 star  
  > "Even though the slit angle was determined independently, we find that it passes through the present position of the F9 star" (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
3. **infer orbital inclination from SiN structure**: Arc in the PV diagram and no measurable offset between summed blue and red SiN channels imply an edge-on geometry; uncertainty set by beam size  
  > "Owing to the noisy edges of the contours, we could not conclusively determine whether there is an offset between them along the axis connecting the present positions of the AGB and F9 stars." (3.4.3 SiN and SiC); "Consequently, we take the orbital inclination of the system to be $i=90\pm7^\circ$, where the uncertainty is derived from the beam size." (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **fit line profiles for central velocities**: Fit a soft parabola profile to the spectra of each line (except 13CN and NS) to obtain central velocities  
  > "we fit soft parabola profiles" (3.4.1 Data reduction); "The soft parabola profile was chosen over a Gaussian profile because the majority of the lines studied here exhibit double-peaked emission" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - fitting: F0, v_cent, v_inf and gamma free  
  > "The parameters $F_0$, $\upsilon_\mathrm{cent}$, $\upsilon_\infty$, and $\gamma$ are left as free parameters in the fit." (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - validation: Central velocities generally agree with the stellar LSR velocity of -23 km/s  
  > "The central velocities of the lines were generally in agreement with the previously measured stellar LSR velocity" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
5. **measure the AGB continuum diameter**: Fit a uniform disc to the calibrated visibilities of all configurations combined with line channels excluded  
  > "We took the calibrated data for all ALMA configurations combined, excluding channels with line emission, and fit a uniform disc (UD) to the visibilities" (3.4.2 AGB angular diameter)
   - parameter: Adopted conservative uncertainty of 3 mas  
  > "we adopt a conservative uncertainty of 3 mas" (3.4.2 AGB angular diameter)
   - assumption: A uniform disc is a better representation of the mm stellar brightness than a Gaussian  
  > "At mm wavelengths, a UD is expected to be a better representation of stellar brightness distribution than a Gaussian distribution." (3.4.2 AGB angular diameter)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SNR > 100 implies sub-mas formal precision; result compared with optical diameter  
  > "The SNR is $>100$, suggesting sub-mas precision, based on the nominal uncertainty of beam size divided by SNR" (3.4.2 AGB angular diameter)
6. **check resolved-out flux against APEX**: Compare ALMA spectra with APEX single-dish spectra of the same lines  
  > "we compared spectra extracted from the ALMA data with previous observations of the same lines taken with the APEX single antenna" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - assumption: Missing CO flux is smooth large-scale emission that does not affect the small structures analysed  
  > "it is only smooth large-scale flux that is resolved out" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SiN fully recovered; CO 66% lost; H12CN about 28% lost; SiO, SiS, CS 10-20% lost  
  > "For CO we found 66% of the flux was resolved out, whereas all the flux was recovered by ALMA for SiN." (3.4.1 Data reduction)
7. **extract spectra around the F9 star**: Extract spectra in 100 mas radius apertures centred on the F9 star, the AGB star and the mirror position on the opposite side, and compare profiles  
  > "We extracted spectra in circular apertures of radii 100 mas (corresponding to a projected radius of 40 au) centred on the F9 star to check for anomalous emission." (3.4.8 Molecular emission around F9 star); "We also checked the shape of the line profiles extracted for an equivalent 100 mas aperture on the opposite side of the AGB star from the F9 star" (3.4.8 Molecular emission around F9 star)
   - assumption: In an expanding envelope, velocity maps to line-of-sight position  
  > "in an expanding circumstellar envelope, the velocity axis has a correspondence to the line of sight spatial axis" (3.4.8 Molecular emission around F9 star)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Opposite-side profile resembles the AGB-centred profile, unlike the F9-centred one  
  > "found that those line profiles were more similar to the AGB-centred line profiles than those centred on the F9 star" (3.4.8 Molecular emission around F9 star)
8. **measure stellar separation in HST and SPHERE images**: Fit star positions in the HST and SPHERE images with lmfit and compare the two epochs  
  > "We measured the positions of the AGB and F9 stars using the python package" (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging); "We find the separation between the two stars is $475\pm 1.0$ mas in the HST epoch, and $491\pm 1.8$ mas in the SPHERE epoch." (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging)
   - software: lmfit
   - assumption: Distance 395 pc for converting to au  
  > "The 2019 SPHERE position corresponds to a projected separation of 194 au, at our adopted distance of 395 pc." (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: SPHERE uncertainty includes orientation, distortion, plate-scale stability and statistical position error  
  > "For SPHERE, the astrometric uncertainty includes the orientation with respect to north, the distortion, the plate scale stability and the statistical position uncertainty" (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging)
9. **model the SiN abundance in the arc**: 1D accelerated lambda iteration radiative transfer compared with SiN spectra from eight 0.25 arcsec apertures (A to H) along the 33 deg east of north axis  
  > "We used the accelerated lambda iteration method" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling); "we extracted the SiN spectra from round apertures with radii of $0.25^{\prime\prime}$, evenly spaced with centres separated by $0.3^{\prime\prime}$ starting from the continuum peak" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - software: ALI, CALPGM
   - parameter: SiN levels up to N=20 and 59 radiative transitions, fine structure included and hyperfine neglected  
  > "We include SiN energy levels up to $N=20$ in the ground vibrational state and the 59 radiative transitions connecting those levels." (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling); "and take fine structure into account but neglect the closely spaced hyperfine structure, which is not resolved in our observations" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: Collision rates: CN-He scaled by 1.37  
  > "scaled by 1.37 to account for the different reduced mass of the SiN-$\mathrm{H}_2$ system" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: Velocity law v0 = 3 km/s, R_in = 2e14 cm, v_inf = 16.5 km/s, beta = 2; mass-loss rate 3e-6 Msun/yr  
  > "with $\upsilon_0 = 3~\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}$ the velocity at the dust condensation radius, $R_\mathrm{in} = 2\times 10^{14}$ cm, $\upsilon_\infty = 16.5~\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}$ the terminal expansion velocity and $\beta = 2$" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - parameter: H2 overdensity: factor of five between 8e15 and 1.5e16 cm  
  > "The overdensity relates to an increase in the $\mathrm{H}_2$ number density by a factor of five between the radii of $8\times 10^{15}$ cm and $1.5\times 10^{16}$ cm" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - assumption: SiN emission is optically thin so a spherical model can represent the arc; arc approximated by a wedge of a spherical shell; distance 395 pc  
  > "Our approach is viable because the SiN emission is expected to be optically thin (and indeed we find a peak tangential optical depth of $\tau \lesssim 0.2$ in the model)" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - fitting: Trial of abundance profile shapes (constant, step, Gaussian shell) with free inner and outer radii, compared with the aperture spectra; no sampler, priors or likelihood stated  
  > "These included a constant abundance, step functions of different constant SiN abundances, and Gaussian shells of various widths and positions." (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling); "We also left the inner and outer radii of the SiN emitting region as free parameters." (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
   - validation: Best-fit profile spans the H2 overdensity; model line profiles plotted against the spectra; peak abundance agrees with chemical models for a carbon-rich star  
  > "the extended peak of the SiN abundance spans the region of the $\mathrm{H}_2$ overdensity" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
10. **identify circular structures in the CO emission**: Plot radial intensity against anticlockwise angle (angle-radius plots) of the central three CO channels; centred circles appear as horizontal lines and off-centre circles as sinusoids  
  > "we plotted the radial intensity against anticlockwise angle to help find such structures" (3.4.6 CO); "Circular structures centred on the AGB star would appear as horizontal lines in such a plot, whereas off-centre circular structures appear as sinusoids." (3.4.6 CO)
   - parameter: Black circle radius 1.35 arcsec, centre 0.1 arcsec north of the AGB star  
  > "The radius of this circle is $1.35^{\prime\prime}$ and its centre is $0.1^{\prime\prime}$ to the north of the AGB star." (3.4.6 CO)
   - parameter: White circle radius 10.75 arcsec, offset north  
  > "another circle with radius $10.75^{\prime\prime}$ which falls close to the edge of the ALMA field of view" (3.4.6 CO)
   - fitting: Circle radii and widths read off the angle-radius plot; uncertainties 10.75 +/- 0.75 and 1.35 +/- 0.10 arcsec  
  > "There we found the uncertainties in the radii of the circles to be $10.75\pm0.75^{\prime\prime}$ for the white circle and $1.35\pm0.10^{\prime\prime}$ for the black circle." (3.8 Orbital parameters from ALMA observations)
   - validation: Black circle coincides with the outer edge of the SiN emission and matches earlier lower-resolution ALMA CO arcs  
  > "The fact that the outer edge of the SiN emission overlaps with the inner circular structure" (Results)
11. **simulate an eccentric binary wind and compare with CO**: Run a 3D SPH Phantom simulation of an eccentric AGB + F9 system, then post-process with MCFOST to produce CO channel maps and angle-radius plots comparable to the ALMA data  
  > "we performed high-resolution 3D smoothed particle hydrodynamic" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations); "we processed the Phantom model with the radiative transfer code MCFOST" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
   - software: Phantom, MCFOST
   - parameter: About 7e6 SPH gas particles launched at 13 km/s; gravity-only sink particles for the two stars  
  > "The AGB star and companion are represented by gravity-only sink particles, and the wind consists of $\sim 7\times 10^{6}$ SPH gas particles that are gradually launched from boundary shells around the AGB star" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
   - parameter: Ideal-gas polytropic index 1.2; no pulsation or rotation  
  > "Cooling within the wind is regulated by the equation of state for an ideal gas with polytropic index $\gamma =1.2$" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
   - parameter: Main model e = 0.92, a = 125 au, evolved ~5000 yr; second model e = 0.93, r_p = 10 au with F9 accretion radius 1 au (0.05 au in main model)  
  > "with eccentricity $e = 0.92$ and semi-major axis $a = 125$ au" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations); "we set a large accretion radius for the F9 star (1 au compared with 0.05 au in our main model)" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)

  > "using the effective stellar temperatures of both the AGB (2300 K) and F9 (6000 K) stars and silicate dust from" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations); "The computation was sped up by only considering the lowest 6 CO levels" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
   - assumption: Constant mass-loss rate and free wind; radiation, pulsation, realistic cooling and anisotropic mass loss neglected  
  > "neglect the impact of additional effects such as radiation, radiation pressure, pulsations, realistic cooling, and variable or anisotropic mass loss" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)

## Software

- **CASA** : Combine the three configurations and make spectral image cubes  
  > "using the Common Astronomy Software Applications for Radio Astronomy" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
- **lmfit** : Fit stellar positions in HST and SPHERE images  
  > "We measured the positions of the AGB and F9 stars using the python package" (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging)
- **ALI (accelerated lambda iteration code)** : 1D radiative transfer to derive the SiN abundance distribution  
  > "We used the accelerated lambda iteration method" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
- **CALPGM** : Compute SiN energy levels and Einstein A coefficients  
  > "The energy levels and Einstein A coefficients were calculated using" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling); "and take fine structure into account but neglect the closely spaced hyperfine structure, which is not resolved in our observations" (3.5 Radiative transfer modelling)
- **Phantom** : 3D smoothed particle hydrodynamic simulation of the eccentric binary wind  
  > "These simulations were performed with the SPH code Phantom" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
- **MCFOST** : Radiative transfer post-processing of the Phantom model to CO channel maps  
  > "we processed the Phantom model with the radiative transfer code MCFOST" (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
- **CDMS** : Reference list of transition frequencies, energies and line strengths  
  > "The Cologne Database for Molecular Spectroscopy, CDMS" (Table 1 notes)

## Qualitative findings

- First detections of SiC and NS towards an S-type AGB star; SiN, SiC and NS emission is asymmetric  
  > "These are the first detections of SiC and NS towards an S-type AGB star and their emission is also asymmetric" (Results)
- SiO, SiS, CS and HCN show truncated emission toward the F9 star, interpreted as photodissociation by the companion  
  > "we see evidence of the F9 star driving additional photodissociation in the zeroth moment maps of SiO, SiS, CS and HCN" (Results)
- Eccentric-orbit SPH models produce near-spherical offset shells like the CO circles, and reproduce the blue/red channel asymmetry  
  > "We also determined that the different emission distributions seen in blue (elongated) and red (circular) channels of our ALMA observations are reproduced in the hydrodynamic model" (Results)
- No periastron distance in the solutions is smaller than the Roche limit, so no direct interaction between the stars is expected  
  > "none of our orbital solutions (Table [3](#tab:solutions)) have periastron separations smaller than the Roche limit" (Discussion)
- HC3N is found on the same side as CN, where HCN is depleted  
  > "from which we can infer that the presence of CN preferentially drives the formation of HC$_3$N to the south and west of the AGB star" (3.6.3 HCN, CN and HC3N)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-resolution ALMA data resolve the inner envelope structure and molecular distributions that earlier lower-resolution data could not  
  > "This enabled us to observe small structures at high angular resolutions" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
- Why this method: Direct imaging cannot constrain a ~1000 yr orbit, so tracers in the envelope are used instead  
  > "Hence, we require other markers in the circumstellar environment of the AGB star to constrain the orbital parameters of the W Aql system." (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging)
- Alternative: Gaia-derived distances were not adopted; 395 pc kept  
  > "In this work, we continue to use a distance of 395 pc because this value falls within the uncertainties of both Gaia-derived values" (3.1 Distance)
- Alternative: 1993 HST WFPC image rejected as unusable  
  > "the degraded angular resolution makes it unusable for our study" (3.3 Spatially resolved imaging)
- Alternative: Narrow 0.3 arcsec PV slit rejected for lower SNR  
  > "Using a narrower slit (such as $0.3^{\prime\prime}$) resulted in a lower SNR in the PV diagram, making an analysis more troublesome." (3.4.3 SiN and SiC)
- Alternative: Gaussian line profile rejected in favour of the soft parabola  
  > "The soft parabola profile was chosen over a Gaussian profile because the majority of the lines studied here exhibit double-peaked emission" (3.4.1 Data reduction)
- Alternative: Red and pink CO circles ruled out as periastron structures  
  > "Therefore, the red and pink circles cannot have formed during periastron." (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): SPH model is simplified and used for qualitative comparison, not a fit  
  > "Hence we aim for a qualitative understanding rather than a direct fit to the data." (3.7 Hydrodynamic simulations)
- Limitation (authors): NS detection is tentative  
  > "The detection of NS is tentative" (3.6.2 NS)
- Limitation (authors): Only a third of the CO flux recovered  
  > "Although only one third of the flux was recovered by ALMA" (3.4.6 CO)
- Limitation (authors): Chemical models assume the companion stays close to the AGB star  
  > "An underlying assumption in these models is that the companion star is always close to the AGB star" (3.6.1 SiN and SiC)
- Limitation (authors): CN distribution needs 12CN coverage and more sensitive data  
  > "would be required to fully understand the distribution of CN around W Aql." (3.6.3 HCN, CN and HC3N)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The orbit is a family of solutions from a grid with no formal likelihood; the period depends on a hand-read circle radius and an assumed wind velocity law
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Distance (395 pc) is adopted and scales the projected separation and period
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SiN abundance is a 1D model of a wedge, fitted without stated goodness-of-fit or uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Chemical model predictions come from an earlier paper with the companion always close, which does not match W Aql at present
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Imaging, weighting and CASA settings are not given, so per-line maps cannot be regenerated exactly
