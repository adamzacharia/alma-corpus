## Analysis recipe

1. **Identify C18O condensations and derive their LTE and virial properties**: Integrate C18O_all over -2 to +14 km/s, run clumpfind with lowest detection level of 5 sigma (3 Jy beam^-1 km s^-1), and calculate LTE column density, H2 mass, virial mass, and virial parameter.  
  > "Figures 1(a) and (b) show the C$^{18}$O intensity distribution integrated over $-2$ to $+14$ km s$^{-1}$, which covers the velocity ranges of the ring cloud, finger cloud, and bridge feature." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution); "The condensations were identified from the C$^{18}$O$_{\rm all}$ integrated intensity map in Figure 1(a) using the “clumpfind” algorithm (cite: clumpfind_wil1994), where the lowest detection level was set to 5 $\sigma$ ($=$ 3 Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$)." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution); "We derived the virial mass, $M_{\rm vir}$ = 210$r_{\rm C^{18}O}$(2$\sqrt{\rm 2ln2}\sigma{_v}$)$^2$, assuming a uniform density inside the cores (cite: Mac1988) without effects of the magnetic field and external pressure." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution)
   - software: clumpfind
   - parameter: velocity range = -2 to +14 km s^-1; lowest detection level = 5 sigma (3 Jy beam^-1 km s^-1)  
  > "Figures 1(a) and (b) show the C$^{18}$O intensity distribution integrated over $-2$ to $+14$ km s$^{-1}$" (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution); "where the lowest detection level was set to 5 $\sigma$ ($=$ 3 Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$)." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution)
   - assumption: LTE; T_ex = 30-50 K from 13CO_all brightness temperature; [16O]/[18O] = 550; [H2]/[12CO] = 10^4; uniform density sphere without magnetic field or external pressure  
  > "The H$_2$ column density $N_{\rm H_2}$ and H$_2$ mass $M_{\rm H_2}$ of the condensations were estimated assuming Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution); "These condensations show brightness temperatures $T_{\rm b}$ of 30–50 K in the $^{13}$CO$_{\rm all}$ data, and these values were used for the excitation temperature $T_{\rm ex}$ in the calculations. A [$^{16}$O]/[$^{18}$O] ratio of 550 and a [H$_2$]/[$^{12}$CO] ratio of $10^4$ was adopted (e.g., (cite: fre1982; leu1984; wil1994))." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

  > "We performed the aperture photometry toward both sources at the central 12$''$ diameter in the 233-GHz map." (Discovery of two compact mm-sources: Source A and Source B); "The $r_{\rm dust}$ values of Source A and Source B were measured by fitting the horizontal and vertical profiles across the peaks with a Gaussian function, with values of $\sim$460 and $\sim$200 AU, respectively (Figure 3)." (233-GHz data); "The aperture radii of the photometry for measuring $S_{\rm int}$ were set to $6''.5$ and $0''.5$ for the 341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ image and the 233-GHz image, respectively." (Summary)
   - parameter: Aperture radii: 6''.5 (341-GHz_ACA, background annulus 6''.5 to 9''.8), 0''.5 (233-GHz), and 12'' diameter (233-GHz vs ACA flux comparison)  
  > "We performed the aperture photometry toward both sources at the central 12$''$ diameter in the 233-GHz map." (Discovery of two compact mm-sources: Source A and Source B); "The aperture radii of the photometry for measuring $S_{\rm int}$ were set to $6''.5$ and $0''.5$ for the 341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ image and the 233-GHz image, respectively. In the 341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ image, the local background level of each source was estimated around the source using a circular annulus with inner and outer radii of $6''.5$ and $1.5\times6''.5 = 9''.8$, respectively." (Summary)
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: Gaussian function fitted to horizontal and vertical profiles across the continuum peaks  
  > "The $r_{\rm dust}$ values of Source A and Source B were measured by fitting the horizontal and vertical profiles across the peaks with a Gaussian function, with values of $\sim$460 and $\sim$200 AU, respectively (Figure 3)." (233-GHz data)
   - validation: Compared 233-GHz_ACA with H30alpha_ACA to identify free-free contamination and chose 341-GHz_ACA for cold dust core properties; compared 12-m 233-GHz flux to 233-GHz_ACA flux to quantify missing flux.  
  > "This means that the ionized gas (i.e., free-free) emission contaminates the 233-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ image (see Section 3.2). We thus used the 341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ map (Figure 2 (b,d)), whose distribution reasonably agrees with that in C$^{18}$O (Figure 1 (a)) tracing cold/dense materials, to estimate the core properties of Source A and B." (341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data); "These figures account for approximately 24% and 13% of the total fluxes measured with the 233-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ map for the same areas." (Discovery of two compact mm-sources: Source A and Source B)
3. **Estimate H2 column densities, masses, and number densities of Source A and Source B from dust continuum**: Calculate N_H2 and M_H2 from peak and integrated continuum fluxes at 341 GHz (ACA) and 233 GHz (12-m) using optically thin dust emission equations, and derive n_H2 assuming a spherical geometry.  
  > "The $N_{\rm H_2}$ and $M_{\rm H_2}$ of the sources were calculated from the peak and total fluxes of the sources ($S_{\rm peak}$ and $S_{\rm int}$) at the optically-thin limit as follows;" (341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data); "The $N_{\rm H_2}$ and $M_{\rm H_2}$ of the sources were calculated from the peak and total fluxes of the sources ($S_{\rm peak}$ and $S_{\rm int}$) at the optically-thin limit as follows;" (Physical properties of Source A and Source B, 341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data)
   - parameter: kappa_341GHz = 1.8 cm^2 g^-1; kappa_233GHz = 0.899 cm^2 g^-1; R_g/d = 100  
  > "Here, an $R_{\rm g/d}$ of 100 was assumed. The dust opacity $\kappa_{\nu}$ at 341-GHz was assumed to be 1.8 cm$^{-2}$ g$^{-1}$ from a thin ice mantle model of (cite: oss1994) for a density of $10^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data); "with a $\kappa_\nu$ of 0.899 cm$^{-2}$ g$^{-1}$, and an $R_{\rm g/d}$ of 100." (233-GHz data)
   - assumption: Optically thin dust emission; D = 1.7 kpc; T_dust = 20-40 K for 341-GHz_ACA; T_dust = 100 K for 233-GHz; spherical geometry for n_H2  
  > "Considering the proximity of the exciting stars to Source A and Source B, which may lead to higher temperatures, $T_{\rm dust}$ values of 20–40 K were assumed in this study." (341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data); "The number densities of the condensations $n_{\rm H_2}$ were calculated as an order of $10^7$ cm$^{-3}$ from the $M_{\rm H_2}$ assuming a sphere." (341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data); "we adopted $T_{\rm d}$ = 100 K based on the recent molecular line measurements toward the G353.273+0.641 high-mass protostellar disk (cite: mot2019)." (233-GHz data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Analyze small-scale C18O kinematics toward Source A**: Create integrated intensity map over -2 to +4 km/s, first moment map, and position-velocity (p-v) diagram of high-resolution C18O data around Source A, and compare with Keplerian rotation and infall models.  
  > "Figure 4(a) shows the intensity distribution of the C$^{18}$O data integrated over a velocity range of $-2$–$+4$ km s$^{-1}$, in which the C$^{18}$O emission was detected in the presented region." (Velocity distribution of the C$^{18}$O emission in Source A); "Figure 4(b) shows a first moment map of the C$^{18}$O data. A clear velocity gradient along the southwest-northeast direction is present, which can also be observed in the position-velocity (p-v) diagram in Figure 4(c)." (Velocity distribution of the C$^{18}$O emission in Source A)
   - parameter: velocity integration range = -2 to +4 km s^-1  
  > "Figure 4(a) shows the intensity distribution of the C$^{18}$O data integrated over a velocity range of $-2$–$+4$ km s$^{-1}$, in which the C$^{18}$O emission was detected in the presented region." (Velocity distribution of the C$^{18}$O emission in Source A)
   - assumption: Tested Keplerian rotation or infall motion (v proportional to r^-1)  
  > "Infall motion of gas, whose velocity is proportional to $r^{-1}$ (e.g., (cite: oha2014)), is an alternative idea to explain the observed velocity gradient." (Velocity distribution of the C$^{18}$O emission in Source A)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked alignment between the C18O velocity gradient axis and the 12CO outflow lobe axis.  
  > "However, the directions of the outflow lobes which will be presented in the next subsection are not perpendicular to the direction of the velocity gradient (see arrows in Figure 4(b))." (Velocity distribution of the C$^{18}$O emission in Source A)
5. **Characterize high-velocity 12CO molecular outflows from Source A and Source B**: Extract 12CO and C18O spectra to determine v_sys and wing velocities, map outflow lobes and velocity distributions |v_mom1 - v_sys|, and compute dynamical timescales, outflow masses, mass outflow rates, and mechanical force.  
  > "Figure 6(a) shows the spatial distributions of the outflow lobes in Source A from the high-resolution $^{12}$CO data, whereas Figure 6(b) presents velocity distributions of the outflow lobes, defined as $|v_{\rm mom1} - v_{\rm sys}|$, where $v_{\rm mom1}$ and $v_{\rm sys}$ are the first moment and systemic velocity of the source, respectively." (High-velocity molecular outflows for Source A and Source B); "The dynamical timescale of the outflow $t_{\rm dyn}$ was calculated as $t_{\rm dyn} = l_{\rm max}/v_{\rm max}$. The H$_2$ mass of the outflow lobe was calculated using a CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor of $2\times10^{20}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ (cite: bol2013). The mass flow rate $\dot{M_{\rm lobe}}$ was measured as $M_{\rm flow}/t_{\rm dyn}$." (Summary)
   - parameter: v_sys = 2 km s^-1; X_CO = 2 x 10^20 (K km s^-1)^-1 cm^-2  
  > "The $v_{\rm sys}$ value was determined from the C$^{18}$O$_{\rm ACA}$ spectra in Figure 5 as 2 km s$^{-1}$." (High-velocity molecular outflows for Source A and Source B); "Therefore, in this study we adopt a CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor of $2\times10^{20}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ (cite: bol2013), as a conservative way to estimate the lower-limit of $M_{\rm lobe}$." (High-velocity molecular outflows for Source A and Source B)
   - assumption: Inclination angle i = 30-70 deg (0 deg for plane of sky); X_CO conversion factor provides a lower limit to M_lobe  
  > "If we assume the inclination angle of 30$^{\circ}$–70$^{\circ}$, the mass outflow rates $\dot{M_{\rm lobe}}$ of the two lobes were derived as (0.4–1.6) $\times$10$^{-4}$ and (1.3–6.3)$\,\times$10$^{-4}$ $M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$, respectively." (High-velocity molecular outflows for Source A and Source B)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Cross-checked blueshifted outflow tip of Source A with 2.12 um H2 shock emission from VLT/NACO.  
  > "The triangle in Figure 6(a) depicts the position of the H$_2$ emission at 2.12 $\mu$m reported by (cite: der2009) using VLT/NACO, which is located near the tip of the blueshifted lobe, suggesting the presence of shocked molecular gas caused by the outflow." (High-velocity molecular outflows for Source A and Source B)
6. **Estimate infrared upper-limit luminosities and compare with theoretical accretion luminosities**: Measure standard deviation of flux density in 4'' area around Source A and Source B in Spitzer/IRAC 3.6, 4.5, 5.6, and 8.0 um bands to integrate L_3.6-8.0 upper limits, and compare with L_acc calculated from M_* and M_dot_acc.  
  > "Here, the Spitzer/IRAC 3.6, 4.5, 5.6, and 8.0 $\mu$m archival images (cite: wol2006) were used to estimate the upper-limit of the luminosity at 3.6–8.0 $\mu$m ($L_{3.6-8.0}$) for Source A and Source B, by integrating the upper-limits of the flux in the four bands." (Infrared-quiet sources); "The upper-limit of the flux in each band was measured as the standard deviation of the flux density distribution around a 4$''$ area of the source, and the derived upper-limits range $\sim$100–400 MJy str$^{-1}$ for the four bands." (Infrared-quiet sources)

  > "The upper-limit of the flux in each band was measured as the standard deviation of the flux density distribution around a 4$''$ area of the source" (Infrared-quiet sources); "Assuming that $\dot{M_{\rm acc}}$ is equal to the total $\dot{M_{\rm lobe}}$ of the redshifted and blueshifted lobes, the expected $L_{\rm acc}$ for $M_{*}$ of 0.1 and 1 $M_\odot$ is calculated as 140 and 770 $L_\odot$ for Source A and 310 and 1700 $L_\odot$ for Source B, respectively." ## Qualitative findings

- Twenty-one massive, gravitationally bound C18O condensations are embedded within 0.1-0.2 pc wide filamentary structures extending northwest-southeast in the center of RCW 38, and all except #13 (harboring Source A) lack compact 233-GHz continuum sources and are likely starless precursors of intermediate- or high-mass stars.  
  > "The virial parameter, $\alpha_{\rm vir}$ = $M_{\rm vir}$/$M_{\rm H_2}$, is the order of unity, suggesting that these condensations are gravitationally bounded." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution); "The observed core properties, especially mass and size, in RCW 38 are consistent with those in infrared dark clouds on the Galactic plane (e.g., (cite: oha2016)), indicating that the identified condensations are likely precursors of high-mass or at least intermediate-mass stars." (Formation of massive condensations in RCW 38)
- The eastern rim of C18O condensation #13 follows the western rim of IRS 1, while IRS 1 coincides with H30alpha emission, indicating IRS 1 arises from strong irradiation of the outer envelope of condensation #13 by IRS 2.  
  > "indicating that the eastern rim of the condensation #13 follows the western rim of the bright ridge of IRS 1, and IRS 1 is spatially coincident with the H30$\alpha_{\rm ACA}$ distribution. This result suggests that the bright infrared emission of IRS 2 arises from the strong irradiation of the outer envelope of the condensation by the adjacent IRS 2." (Large-scale C$^{18}$O distribution)
- Both Source A and Source B show single continuum peaks at 270 AU resolution with no signs of fragmentation and no H30alpha or infrared excess, indicating deeply embedded massive protostars prior to ionizing their surroundings.  
  > "This nature is consistent with the non-detection of H30$\alpha$ emission from the continuum peaks with the 12-m array, meaning that the young sources do not start to ionize their surroundings." (Discovery of two compact mm-sources: Source A and Source B); "Note that the two sources exhibit a single peak at the 270 AU resolution, indicating no signs of fragmentation." (Source A and Source B; nurseries of massive protostars?)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: RCW 38 is the youngest super star cluster in the Milky Way (0.1-0.5 Myr) at 1.7 kpc with abundant remaining molecular gas in its central ~1 pc, and ALMA Cycle 3 Band 6 and 7 observations provide the ~270 AU resolution needed to resolve fragmentation, collapse, and outflows in massive YSOs.  
  > "Despite strong dissipative and destructive effects caused by feedback from the formed massive stars, plenty of molecular gas still remains within the central $\sim$1 pc of RCW 38, offering a unique opportunity to observe the natal gas of the super star cluster during formation (cite: fuk2016)." (Introduction); "The minimum angular resolution of the dataset is $0''.17\times0''.15$, which corresponds to 289$\times$255 AU at 1.7 kpc." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used 341-GHz_ACA rather than 233-GHz_ACA for core mass estimates because 233-GHz_ACA is contaminated by free-free emission; adopted a CO-to-H2 conversion factor for outflow masses because low S/N in 12-m line data prevents disentangling opacity and excitation effects.  
  > "This means that the ionized gas (i.e., free-free) emission contaminates the 233-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ image (see Section 3.2). We thus used the 341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ map (Figure 2 (b,d)), whose distribution reasonably agrees with that in C$^{18}$O (Figure 1 (a)) tracing cold/dense materials, to estimate the core properties of Source A and B." (341-GHz$_{\rm ACA}$ data); "It is not simple to measure the H$_2$ masses of the outflow lobe $M_{\rm lobe}$, because this value strongly depends on the line opacity, excitation temperature, inclination, etc; moreover it is difficult to distinguish the effects of these parameters for the relatively low-S/N ratio of the line data obtained with the 12-m array." (High-velocity molecular outflows for Source A and Source B)
- Alternative: Resolved-out emission as the reason for the lack of a C18O condensation toward Source B (rejected because the ACA map also shows no C18O counterpart).  
  > "As Source B is observed in the ACA map (Figure 1(c)), which covers extended emission, the possibility of resolved-out can be dismissed to interpret the absence of C$^{18}$O condensation." (Discovery of two compact mm-sources: Source A and Source B)
- Alternative: Infall motion of gas (v proportional to r^-1) considered as an alternative to Keplerian rotation to explain the C18O velocity gradient in Source A.  
  > "Infall motion of gas, whose velocity is proportional to $r^{-1}$ (e.g., (cite: oha2014)), is an alternative idea to explain the observed velocity gradient." (Velocity distribution of the C$^{18}$O emission in Source A)
- Alternative: Collect-and-collapse compression by expanding HII region from IRS 2 (rejected because the distance ~0.3 pc is too small to compress gas by the required 1-2 orders of magnitude).  
  > "In the case of RCW 38, however, the distance between the massive condensations and exciting source (IRS 2) is too small for the accumulated gas to induce fragmentation." (Formation of massive condensations in RCW 38)
- Alternative: Thermal Jeans instability and competitive accretion considered as alternatives to cloud-cloud collision for forming the massive condensations and driving high accretion rates.  
  > "Although the thermal jeans instability with a volume density of 10$^4$–10$^5$ cm$^{-3}$ may also describe the core separation, the observed density and mass are one or two orders of magnitude higher than those in the fragmentation mode." (Formation of massive condensations in RCW 38); "The competitive accretion (e.g., (cite: bon2001)) may alternatively realize the mass accretion rate of $\sim$10$^{-4}$ $M_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$ (cite: wan2010), and thus we cannot fully exclude the competitive accretion scenario for high-mass star formation in the RCW 38 region." (Formation of massive condensations in RCW 38)
