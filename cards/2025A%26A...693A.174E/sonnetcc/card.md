## Analysis recipe

1. **Image CO(2-1) cube from ALMA**: Calibrate each configuration in CASA, concat TE and TC, subtract continuum in uv plane, tclean with Briggs robust 2, primary beam correct  
  > "The data were calibrated, concatenated, and cleaned with the CASA software (version 5.6.1-8; (cite: McMullin07))." (2.2 ALMA and VLA data)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub, concat
   - parameter: robust = 2, 15 km/s channels, zeroth-order uvcontsub in line-free channels  
  > "a robust parameter set to 2 to achieve the best sensitivity, equivalent to a natural weighting" (2.2 ALMA and VLA data); "specifying a zeroth-order polynomial in the channels free from emission lines" (2.2 ALMA and VLA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Reduce JWST MIRI MRS cubes and extract nuclear and extended spectra**: JWST pipeline, extra hot/cold pixel mask, spectrum-by-spectrum background subtraction, residual fringing correction, nuclear point-source extraction and 1.8'' radius extended extraction  
  > "The data were reduced using the JWST Science Calibration Pipeline (version 1.11.4), with the context 1130 for the calibration References Data System" (2.1 JWST/MIRI observations); "The extended spectrum was extracted in an aperture of 1.8$''$ radius centered in the nucleus (diameter of 3.6$''$ $\simeq$ 634 pc) and including the nuclear aperture." (3.1 Nuclear and extended mid-infrared spectra)
   - software: JWST Science Calibration Pipeline, rfc1d-utils
   - parameter: Nuclear aperture radius 1.5 x PSF FWHM per channel (0.42'' in Ch1-s to 1.252 in Ch4-l)  
  > "These radii range from 0.42$''$ on Ch1-s to 1.252 on Ch4-l." (3.1 Nuclear and extended mid-infrared spectra)
   - fitting: Channel scaling factors from linear fits, less than 1.2% of the flux  
  > "These scaling factors were determined by conducting linear fits, and they represent less than 1.2% of the flux measured at any given wavelength" (3.1 Nuclear and extended mid-infrared spectra)
   - validation: Compared with Spitzer/IRS and VISIR spectra  
  > "The continuum shape of the IRS and MIRI extended spectra are almost identical, as expected, since they were extracted in the same aperture." (3.1 Nuclear and extended mid-infrared spectra)
3. **Make H2 flux, velocity and dispersion maps**: Spaxel-by-spaxel single Gaussian plus local continuum fits with ALUCINE after boxcar smoothing  
  > "This tool creates flux, velocity, and velocity dispersion ($\sigma$) maps by fitting the emission lines using single or multiple Gaussian components and a local continuum on a spaxel-by-spaxel basis." (3.2.1 Warm versus cold molecular gas)
   - software: ALUCINE, QFITSVIEW
   - parameter: Single Gaussian component; boxcar average of one pixel along X and Y  
  > "We used a single Gaussian component to fit the emission line profiles." (3.2.1 Warm versus cold molecular gas); "We selected a boxcar average of one pixel along both the X and Y axes." (3.2.1 Warm versus cold molecular gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure H2 line profiles in six regions**: Extract spectra in six 0.5'' radius regions, fit single Gaussian with local continuum, correct sigma for instrumental width  
  > "We modeled each line profile with a single Gaussian and subtracted a local continuum, defined by interpolating between two regions adjacent to the emission line." (3.2.2 H2 molecular gas in specific regions)
   - parameter: Six regions of 0.5'' radius (~90 pc) at high-sigma locations  
  > "extracted in six regions of 0.5$''$ radius ($\sim$90 pc)" (3.2.2 H2 molecular gas in specific regions)
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per line  
  > "We modeled each line profile with a single Gaussian" (3.2.2 H2 molecular gas in specific regions)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Fit rotating-disc models to CO(2-1), H2 S(1) and S(3)**: 3D tilted-ring fits with 3DBarolo; residuals vs data used to find non-circular motions  
  > "allows the user to model rotation in the observed emission line datacubes by fitting a 3D tilted ring." (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: SEARCH in 3DFIT with a mask threshold of 3 sigma_rms  
  > "We used the SEARCH parameter in the 3DFIT task to find spatial and spectral coherent emissions, applying a mask with a threshold of 3$\sigma_{\rm rms}$." (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - parameter: Centre fixed to continuum peak at 200 GHz for CO(2-1) and to atomic-line peaks ([NeIII] for S(1), [ArIII] for S(3)) for H2  
  > "We fixed the central position to the peak of the continuum at 200  GHz for the CO(2$-$1) emission" (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - parameter: Free parameters: rotation velocity, velocity dispersion, PA and inclination  
  > "We allowed the code to fit the observed rotation velocity and velocity dispersion while also varying the PA and the inclination of the disc." (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - assumption: S(4) and S(5) not modelled because of the higher number of spaxels with no signal  
  > "we decided not to use them due to the higher number of spaxels with no signal" (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Residual maps and model PVDs compared with data  
  > "We also included the PVDs generated from the model datacube created by" (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
6. **Build position-velocity diagrams of S(1), S(3) and CO**: PVDs in 0.6'' pseudo-slits along kinematic major/minor axes and chosen PAs, overlaid with model PVDs  
  > "We extracted them using a pseudo-slit width of 0.6$''$ ($\sim$100 pc)." (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - software: 3DBarolo
   - parameter: Slit width 0.6''  
  > "We extracted them using a pseudo-slit width of 0.6$''$ ($\sim$100 pc)." (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Derive H2 temperatures, column densities and masses**: Rotational diagrams from extinction-corrected line fluxes in the six regions and a total 3.6'' aperture, fit with a power-law temperature distribution and a two-temperature model  
  > "a two-temperatures model, which represents a warm and a hot component" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
   - parameter: Power-law model limited to 3500 K and 200 K  
  > "We assumed upper and lower temperatures of 3500 K and 200 K." (Footnote to 3.4)
   - parameter: Warm T < 500 K, hot T > 500 K  
  > "We derived the warm ($T < 500\,$K) and hot ($T > 500\,$K) molecular gas masses of the different regions from these excitation diagrams" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
   - assumption: LTE; extinction A_V = 1 mag with Cardelli89 law  
  > "Therefore, we can assume local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions (cite: Roussel07)." (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas); "corrected for extinction by adopting the value of A$_V$=1 mag from (cite: Prieto14) and the (cite: Cardelli89) reddening law" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: chi2/d.o.f. < 1.9 for the fits  
  > "Generally, the models fit the data reasonably well ($\rm{\chi^2/d.o.f. < 1.9}$)." (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
8. **Estimate cold molecular gas mass from CO(2-1)**: CO(2-1) luminosity in a 3.6'' aperture via Solomon05 Eq. 3, converted with Milky-Way alpha_CO  
  > "We used equation (3) of (cite: Solomon05) to compute the CO(2$-$1) line luminosity" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
   - parameter: Aperture 3.6'' diameter  
  > "using the CO(2$-$1) flux integrated in the same 3.6$''$ diameter aperture" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
   - assumption: CO thermalized and optically thick (R21 = 1); Milky-Way alpha_CO  
  > "we assumed that the CO emission is thermalized and optically thick" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas); "and adopted the Milky-Way CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor (cite: Bolatto13)" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with ULIRG alpha_CO (0.8) and NGC 3256 alpha_CO values  
  > "we assume the typical value of ULIRGs of 0.8 (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^2$)$^{-1}$ (cite: Narayanan12)" (Footnote to 3.4)
9. **Compute SFR from PAH and test star-formation-driven outflow at R5**: SFR from 11.3 um PAH luminosity (Shipley16 Eq. 12); outflow mass rate from R5 H2 mass, outflow velocity and radius  
  > "Eq. 12 in (cite: Shipley16), as in (cite: Ramos Almeida23), we measured a star formation rate (SFR)" (4 Discussion)
   - parameter: Outflow velocity ~200 km/s, radius ~0.5'' (88 pc)  
  > "of $\sim$200 km s$^{-1}$, and a radius of $\sim$0.5$''$ (88 pc, which is the R5 radius)" (4 Discussion)
   - assumption: L(11.3 um) multiplied by two to match PAHFIT-derived SFRs; R5 mass treated as upper limit to the outflow mass  
  > "was multiplied by a factor of two to make the SFR comparable with PAHFIT-derived measurements" (Footnote to Section 4); "so we consider the latter as an upper limit to the outflow mass" (4 Discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO(2-1) and H2 show a nuclear trailing spiral connected to a ring, consistent with gas response to a nuclear bar  
  > "These features are also detected in CO(2$-$1) and H$_2$" (abstract); "A nuclear trailing spiral connected with a ring is indicative of gas that is losing angular momentum and feeding the SMBH in the presence of a bar" (4 Discussion)
- Velocity dispersion increases from S(1) to S(5) in all regions  
  > "range between 40 and 170 $\rm{km~s^{-1}}$, increasing from S(1) to S(5) in all regions" (3.2.2 H2 molecular gas in specific regions)
- High-dispersion S(3) knot lies in a region devoid of CO(2-1)  
  > "This area of higher velocity dispersion in S(3) coincides with an area devoid of CO(2$-$1) emission" (4 Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST integral field spectroscopy combined with ALMA to study the molecular gas kinematics at high spatial resolution  
  > "The integral field capabilities of the JWST (cite: Gardner23), combined with ALMA, now offer the opportunity to study the molecular phase of the ISM in galaxies" (Introduction)
- Why this method: S(1) and S(3) chosen for 3DBarolo because S(4) and S(5) have more empty spaxels  
  > "Although S(4) and S(5) would generally be preferable for tracing hotter and more excited molecular gas, we decided not to use them due to the higher number of spaxels with no signal" (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
- Alternative: S(4) and S(5) not used for 3DBarolo modelling due to spaxels without signal  
  > "we decided not to use them due to the higher number of spaxels with no signal" (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
- Alternative: Jet-driven outflow considered for the R5 knot but not supported by any spatial correspondence with the jet  
  > "A jet-driven outflow could be another possible scenario for explaining the kinematics, but we do not see any spatial correspondence with the jet" (4 Discussion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): CO-to-H2 conversion factor is the main uncertainty on the cold gas mass  
  > "The major source of uncertainty for this mass gas value is the assumed conversion factor, which could be up to a factor of 3$-$4 lower" (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
- Limitation (authors): High nuclear velocity dispersions in MIRI data may be partly beam smearing  
  > "In the case of the MIRI data, the high nuclear velocity dispersions might be partly due to beam smearing" (3.2.3 Modeling the molecular gas kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): R5 temperature from single-component fit because S(5) is lacking, giving high uncertainty  
  > "This high temperature comes from the fit using only one temperature component, as S(5) measurement is lacking in this region, resulting in high uncertainty for this measurement." (3.4 Properties of the warm molecular gas)
- Limitation (authors): Gravity torques due to the nuclear bar not computed  
  > "The computation of the gravity torques due to the nuclear bar is beyond the scope of this paper." (4 Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 3DBarolo fits use very different inclinations for CO (75 deg) and H2 (33-37 deg), so the kinematic comparison depends on the disc geometry and FOV
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Two-temperature fits to five lines per region have few degrees of freedom and no stated fitting algorithm or error treatment
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Outflow mass, rate and energy for R5 are upper limits from one low-mass knot, with a rough velocity and radius
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA beam (0.65'') is coarser than MIRI Ch1-Ch2 resolution, so beam smearing and resolution differences affect the CO vs H2 comparison
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The statement that the jet does not affect the gas rests on a visual comparison with a 1999 VLA map
