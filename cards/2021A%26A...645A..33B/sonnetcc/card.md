## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, continuum-subtract and image cubes and continuum maps**: ALMA pipeline calibration; uv-plane continuum subtraction with first-order polynomial; tclean cubes (hogbom, natural weighting, 40 km/s channels, 3 x rms threshold); same procedure for continuum maps  
  > "ALMA data were calibrated using the CASA software (cite: McMullin07) in the pipeline mode." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis); "The same deconvolution procedure was adopted to produce continuum maps." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean, GILDAS CLIC, MAPPING
   - parameter: 40 km/s channels, natural weighting (except J1639+2824)  
  > "a natural weighting of the visibilities and a common spectral channel width of 40 km s" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - parameter: J1639+2824 taper beyond 900 klambda  
  > "we applied a tapering of the visibilities beyond 900 k$\lambda$" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - parameter: Continuum-subtraction fit to |v|>500 km/s, first-order polynomial  
  > "We performed a fit in the uv plane to all channels with velocity $|v|>500$ km s$^{-1}$ by adopting a first order polynomial continuum model." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure continuum and line flux densities and sizes**: Image-plane 2D Gaussian fit with imfit and uv-plane fit with MAPPING uvfit; point or Gaussian source models; QSO fitted and subtracted before nearby emitters  
  > "fitted the data in the image plane with a two-dimensional Gaussian profile by using CASA task" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis); "performed a fit of the visibilities in the uv plane with MAPPING task uvfit" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis); "In the case of multiple sources, we first fitted and subtracted emission from the QSO host-galaxy, then fitted emission from nearby emitters." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - software: CASA imfit, MAPPING uvfit
   - parameter: uv channels averaged spectrally and temporally to 30 s  
  > "channels associated with the continuum emission, were spectrally and temporally averaged (to 30 s)" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - parameter: Gaussian preferred if size differs from zero at >3 sigma and detection significance increases  
  > "A Gaussian model was preferred when resulting into an increased significance of the detection and a size different from null size at $>3\sigma$" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - assumption: Upper limits computed as 3 x rms for a point source  
  > "upper limit computed for a point source as $3\times\sigma_{\rm cont}$" (Table 2 notes)
   - fitting: Point-source or Gaussian source models fitted to visibilities; image-plane 2D Gaussian  
  > "We then fitted point source or Gaussian source models to the visibilities." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - validation: Subtracted models give uniform residual noise maps; image and uv fits consistent  
  > "We checked that subtracting the models resulted into uniform residual noise maps." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis); "We verified that (i) and (ii) give consistent sizes and flux density values." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
3. **Extract 1-D spectra and fit line profiles**: Spectra extracted from continuum-subtracted cubes over a region around the line peak (SNR>2) and fitted with a single Gaussian to get FWHM, centroid, z_cold  
  > "One-dimensional spectra were extracted from the continuum-subtracted datacubes from a region centred on the peak of CO or" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis); "This provided us with FWHM and centroid of the emission line profiles" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - software: scipy.optimize.curve_fit
   - parameter: region includes emission at SNR>2  
  > "including emission detected at $\rm SNR>2$" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - fitting: Gaussian profile fit with scipy.optimize.curve_fit, all parameters free  
  > "we fitted them with a Gaussian profile by using the python package scipy.optimize.curve_fit leaving all parameters free to vary" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
   - validation: Two-Gaussian fits give consistent line significance and parameters  
  > "We verified that by fitting the spectra with a two-Gaussian model, the line significance and parameters were consistent within the uncertainties with the single Gaussian fit." (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
4. **Derive line luminosities and CO excitation**: L'CO and L[CII] from Solomon05 Eq. 3 and Eq. 1; CO(1-0) luminosity inferred from mid-J lines using ladder ratios  
  > "luminosity according to Eq. (3) and Eq. (1) in (cite: Solomon05), respectively" (3.2 CO and [CII] emission); "We thus adopt $L^\prime_{\rm CO(5-4)}/L^\prime_{\rm CO(1-0)}\simeq0.69$ and $L^\prime_{\rm CO(4-3)}/L^\prime_{\rm CO(1-0)}\simeq0.87$ (cite: Carilli&Walter13)" (3.3 Investigating the CO spectral line energy distribution)
   - parameter: CO(1-0) upper limit = 3 x rms of velocity-integrated JVLA map over 685 km/s for J0801+5210, assuming CO(5-4) FWHM  
  > "we have integrated the JVLA datacubes around $z_{\rm cold}$ over 685 km s$^{-1}$, assuming the same FWHM of CO(5$-$4)" (3.2 CO and [CII] emission)

## Qualitative findings

- Six of eight QSOs show CO or [CII] line emitters at about the same redshift; two are also detected in continuum  
  > "Specifically, six out of eight WISSH QSOs analysed here show line emitters within the ALMA or NOEMA field of view" (3.2 CO and [CII] emission)
- CO(5-4)/CO(1-0) ratio of J0801+5210 higher than in APM08279+5255, indicating a steep CO SLED  
  > "is even higher than that expected in APM08279$+$5255, that is the QSO with the steepest CO SLED observed so far" (6.3 CO SLED)
- No cold-gas outflow wings detected; sensitivity too limited for meaningful upper limits  
  > "We found no detection of high-velocity cold gas." (6.5 Gas fraction and feedback from QSO winds)
- J1433+0227 [CII] is compatible with a rotating disk with deviations from pure rotation  
  > "is compatible with a rotating disk, although deviations from pure rotation are visible along both major and minor kinematic axes" (5 CO and [CII] kinematics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Targets from different projects, collected from PI programs and all public ALMA archive data as of December 2019; represent WISSH L_Bol and z range  
  > "The sub-sample of WISSH QSOs analysed here derives from different projects, originally designed with different purposes." (2.1 Sample description); "Globally, the selection of our targets does not introduce strong biases" (2.1 Sample description)
- Why this method: Starburst-like alpha_CO adopted since most targets are in merging systems  
  > "given the evidence that $\sim80\%$ of WISSH targets are in merging systems, we adopt $\alpha_{\rm CO}\sim0.8$ to infer the molecular gas mass" (6.2 Star formation efficiency and gas depletion)
- Alternative: alpha_CO ~3.5 (Accurso17 function for non-interacting galaxies) not adopted; starburst alpha_CO ~0.8 used instead  
  > "suggests a larger $\alpha_{\rm CO}\sim3.5$ for non-interacting galaxies with" (6.2 Star formation efficiency and gas depletion)
- Alternative: Two-Gaussian line fits tested and found consistent with the single-Gaussian fit  
  > "We verified that by fitting the spectra with a two-Gaussian model" (2.2 Data reduction and analysis)
- Alternative: Dust-based vs [CII]-based gas mass for J1433+0227 and J1015+0020 compared  
  > "An alternative approach to estimate $M_{\rm gas}$ consists in using the luminosity of the" (6.2 Star formation efficiency and gas depletion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Limited angular resolution hampers continuum morphology study  
  > "The limited angular resolution of most observations presented in this work hampers a detailed study of the continuum emission morphology in these QSOs." (3.1 Continuum emission)
- Limitation (authors): Large uncertainties on M* and f_gas from inclination and alpha_CO  
  > "However, we caution that large uncertainties affect these measurements" (6.5 Gas fraction and feedback from QSO winds)
- Limitation (authors): Moment-map interpretation for marginally resolved J1549+1245 and J1639+2824 is uncertain  
  > "the interpretation of moment maps is not straightforward and can be affected by large uncertainties" (5 CO and [CII] kinematics)
- Limitation (authors): Sensitivity too limited to constrain cold-gas outflow luminosity  
  > "prevents us from putting meaningful upper limits on the luminosity of cold gas outflows in our targets" (6.5 Gas fraction and feedback from QSO winds)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mixed-quality, heterogeneous archival ALMA/NOEMA/JVLA data; Mdyn from marginally resolved lines with weak inclination constraints
- Caveat (inferred, ours): alpha_CO = 0.8 and AGN contribution of 50% to L_IR drive the SFE, depletion time and f_gas conclusions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO(1-0) luminosity inferred from mid-J via assumed ladder for most targets
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Stellar mass from Mdyn-Mgas-MBH with only four targets; f_gas offset sensitive to alpha_CO and to dark matter assumption
- Caveat (inferred, ours): SED fitting details inherited from Duras17; no uncertainties on T_dust assumed 60 K for non-Herschel sources
