## Analysis recipe

1. **Register and combine calibrated KCWI cubes**: Fit a 2D Gaussian to line-free quasar continuum, assign Gaia coordinates and co-add reduced cubes.  
  > "The WCS coordinates in the header were corrected by 2D fitting a gaussian to the quasar continuum emission, far from any emission lines, which was then assigned the coordinates from Gaia (cite: GAIARef)." (Data Reduction); "The reduced data cubes were then co-added using CWITools (cite: CWIToolsRef)" (Data Reduction)
   - software: CWITools
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to quasar continuum away from emission lines.  
  > "The WCS coordinates in the header were corrected by 2D fitting a gaussian to the quasar continuum emission, far from any emission lines, which was then assigned the coordinates from Gaia (cite: GAIARef)." (Data Reduction)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Subtract the quasar and measure nebular emission**: Fit and subtract a continuum-template PSF spectrum; construct surface-brightness, velocity and dispersion moment maps.  
  > "The quasar PSF is reconstructed by assuming a continuum template and fitting this to the observed spectrum far from the wings of broad line emissions or any narrow lines." (Data Reduction); "This is normalized against the peak flux and then subtracted for all wavelength channels." (Data Reduction); "The co-added cubes are used to construct the moment maps using CWITools." (Data Reduction)
   - software: CWITools
   - parameter: Moment 0 surface brightness; Moment 1 and 2 from equations 18–21 in CWIToolsRef.  
  > "the first and second moment calculations for the Moment 1 and 2 maps, which derive from equations 18–21 in (cite: CWIToolsRef)." (Data Reduction)
   - assumption: Systemic redshifts from OSIRIS measurements.  
  > "The systemic redshift used to calculate the moment maps is used from OSIRIS measurements in (cite: QUART_OSIRIS)" (Appendix: All KCWI Maps — Ly$\alpha$ maps)
   - fitting: Continuum-template fit away from broad-line wings and narrow lines; normalize against peak flux.  
  > "The quasar PSF is reconstructed by assuming a continuum template and fitting this to the observed spectrum far from the wings of broad line emissions or any narrow lines." (Data Reduction); "This is normalized against the peak flux and then subtracted for all wavelength channels." (Data Reduction)
   - validation: Inspect broad-line-region residuals; no significant residuals beyond 2 pixels, approximately 0.6 arcsec.  
  > "Good PSF subtraction is confirmed by observing residuals in the vicinity of broad line regions." (Data Reduction); "The PSF subtraction leaves no significant residuals beyond 2 pixels from the quasar ($\sim 0.6^{ \prime \prime}$)." (Data Reduction)
3. **Compare radial profiles and luminosities**: Compare Lyα radial profiles, total luminosity, redshift and projected area with literature samples.  
  > "shows the radial surface brightness profiles around the QUART sample in comparison to other Ly$\alpha$  nebulae from the literature." (Radial profiles); "shows the dependence of total Ly$\alpha$  luminosity on the observed on-sky area in square arcseconds." (Luminosity)
   - parameter: Exclude inner 8 kpc; report sample 25th and 75th percentiles to approximately 80 kpc.  
  > "Emission from the inner 8 kpc is not considered for the entire sample to avoid any issues due to residuals from PSF subtraction." (Radial profiles); "we only indicate the percentile values indicating 25th and 75th percentile of the sample radial profiles out to $\sim$ 80 kpc, since half our sample have maximum extent up to this distance." (Radial profiles)
   - parameter: Sensitivity-matched luminosity–area comparison at 2 × 10^-18 erg s^-1 cm^-2.  
  > "This comparison gets stronger when we compare for reduced sensitivity of 2 $\times 10^{-18}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Luminosity)
   - assumption: ΩM = 0.308; ΩΛ = 0.692; H0 = 67.8 km s^-1 Mpc^-1.  
  > "We assume $\Omega_M = 0.308$, $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.692$ and $H_0 = 67.8 \, \mathrm{km \, s}^{-1}\, \mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ for the cosmological parameters (cite: Planck2016)." (Introduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeat luminosity–area comparison at reduced sensitivity.  
  > "This comparison gets stronger when we compare for reduced sensitivity of 2 $\times 10^{-18}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Luminosity)
4. **Measure nebular asymmetry and orientation**: Calculate flux-weighted second moments, Stokes Q and U, minor-to-major axis ratio and major-axis angle.  
  > "We calculate the second order moment of the observed flux (cite: Stoughton2002)." (Asymmetry); "The ratio between the minor and major axes of the nebula gives the asymmetry $\alpha$" (Asymmetry)
   - parameter: Flux-weighted centroids of 2σ isophotes.  
  > "Where $x_{Neb}$ and $y_{Neb}$ are flux weighted centroids for the $2\sigma$ isophotes of the nebulae and $r$ is the distance of a given pixel from the centroid." (Asymmetry)
   - parameter: For 4C 09.17 and 4C 57.29 He II/C IV, use the vector joining emission centroid and quasar.  
  > "We make an exception in the calculation of the angle of major axis for 4C 09.17 and the He II  and C IV  nebulae around 4C 57.29 due to the highly asymmetric nature of the emission." (Asymmetry); "In this case, we compute the centroid of emission for Ly$\alpha$, He II, and C IV  emission, and construct a major axis by joining the centroid with the location of the quasar." (Asymmetry)
   - parameter: Adopt approximately 5° angle uncertainty from pixel extent at approximately 30 kpc.  
  > "we consider the angular extent subtended by a single pixel at a distance of $\sim$ 30 kpc to be the uncertainty in our calculation, which is $\sim 5^{\circ}$." (Asymmetry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Inject noise realizations; compare morphology at lower sensitivity.  
  > "Injecting the moment map with noise realizations to get a spread of angular values gives a very small spread in the angle" (Asymmetry); "we need to compare the asymmetry in other literature sources at a lower sensitivity of 2 $\times 10^{-18}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ ." (Asymmetry)
5. **Compare nebular and radio axes**: Use the quasar-to-radio-flux-centroid vector as the radio axis and compare it with nebular major axes.  
  > "We further plot the the major axis of the radio jet emission (black) by plotting the vector from the quasar to the flux weighted centroid of radio emission." (Major axes of radio and CGM emission)
   - parameter: Alignment criterion: angular difference less than approximately 45°.  
  > "We consider the CGM emission to be aligned with the ionization cone if the angular difference between the major axis of the CGM and the radio jet are less than a half-opening angle of $\sim$ 45$^{\circ}$" (Major axes of radio and CGM emission)
   - assumption: Radio jets trace the quasar ionization cone.  
  > "It is therefore useful to consider the radio jets as tracers of the quasar ionization cone" (Major axes of radio and CGM emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare Lyα angles with Heckman1991a; account for assumed 5° angle uncertainty.  
  > "We find a reasonable agreement between the two sets of values." (Major axes of radio and CGM emission); "Given the uncertainty in the major axis calculation which we have assumed to be $5^{\circ}$" (Major axes of radio and CGM emission)
6. **Test ionization mechanisms**: Construct He II/Lyα ratio maps; compare with recombination expectations and radio-jet positions to assess photoionization, scattering and shocks.  
  > "We investigate quasar photoionization by constructing He II/Ly$\alpha$  emission ratio maps" (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae); "There are three potential sources we consider, i.e. photoionization by the quasar, shock excitation due to interaction between the radio jet and the CGM, and resonant scattering of photons." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
   - parameter: R0(T) approximately 0.23 for Case A and 0.3 for Case B at approximately 2 × 10^4 K.  
  > "where $R_0(T) \sim 0.23$ for Case A reionization conditions and $R_0(T) \sim 0.3$ for Case B reionization conditions, at a Temperature of $\sim 2 \times 10^4 K$." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
   - assumption: Uniform gas temperature.  
  > "Assuming that the gas is at a uniform temperature" (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
   - assumption: Lyα-probed hydrogen is mostly ionized; observed ratio is hydrogen-density-squared weighted.  
  > "it is reasonable to assume that the phase of hydrogen probed by Ly$\alpha$  radiation consists of mostly ionized hydrogen." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae); "This implies the observed ratio is weighted by the square of the density of Hydrogen in the CGM." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Check exceptional high-ratio pixels against the 2σ detection limit and inspect dispersion at radio jets.  
  > "The only exceptions are a few pixels south of 3C 9 and a small clump NE and SW of 4C 09.17, both of which are close to the 2$\sigma$ detection limit and likely result of noisy detections." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae); "Firstly, we note that the kinematics of all three emission lines do not show high dispersion at the location of the radio jets." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
7. **Interpret spectra at radio jets**: Integrate KCWI Lyα and He II spectra within radio 5σ contours and compare their components.  
  > "We extract Ly$\alpha$  and He II  spectra at the location of the radio jets by taking the 5$\sigma$ contours from radio maps and extracting nebular emission from the KCWI data from those regions." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
   - parameter: Exclude spaxels less than 3 pixels from the quasar.  
  > "Data from spaxels that are less than 3 pixels away from the quasar are excluded to remove any residual emission from the AGN." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
   - assumption: Brighter Doppler-boosted jet lobe is in front of the quasar.  
  > "As FR-II jets typically have one lobe appear brighter than the other due to Doppler boosting of the synchrotron radiation, it establishes whether that side of the jet is in front (if brighter) or behind (if fainter) the quasar." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
   - assumption: He II more directly indicates CGM gas velocities.  
  > "The presence of He II  emission from the same region helps resolve this degeneracy to some extent, as the presence of He II  likely indicates the true gas velocities in the CGM." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Use corresponding He II components to distinguish multiple kinematic components from foreground absorption.  
  > "multiple Ly$\alpha$  peaks with corresponding He II  peaks are likely to be two independent components" (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets); "the presence of a single He II  component in between two Ly$\alpha$  components likely indicates the absorption scenario." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
8. **Compare CGM and host luminosities**: Compare integrated CGM luminosities with literature quasar bolometric and PSF-subtracted host Hα and [O III] luminosities.  
  > "measurements of the quasars bolometric luminosity $Q_{bol}$, and the total host galaxy H$\alpha$  and \[O III\]  emissions (after subtracting emissions from the PSF) as calculated in (cite: QUART1) and (cite: QUART2)." (Comparison with OSIRIS observations of quasar host galaxies)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- QUART sources are more luminous in Lyα at a given projected area, including after matching sensitivity.  
  > "The QUART sources do indicate a higher luminosity compared to the (cite: AB2019) sample for the same area." (Luminosity); "This comparison gets stronger when we compare for reduced sensitivity of 2 $\times 10^{-18}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$" (Luminosity)
- Sensitivity changes can either increase or decrease measured nebular asymmetry.  
  > "We note that the observed asymmetry does not systematically increase or decrease with improvement in sensitivity, as both behaviors are observed in our sample" (Asymmetry)
- He II and C IV emission is consistent with quasar photoionization and subsequent recombination.  
  > "Given the alignment of radio axes with respect to the He II  nebulae, a photoionization and subsequent recombination scenario is consistent with the observations, for regions with He II  emission." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
- The bent jet in 4C 11.45 coincides with the He II/C IV boundary, suggesting a change in CGM conditions.  
  > "For 4C 11.45, we find that the bent radio jet location coincides with the edge of He II  and C IV  nebulae, indicating a transition from the inner CGM influenced by galactic processes to an outer CGM influenced by the larger environment." (Summary)
- CGM Lyα luminosity shows no trends with quasar bolometric or host Hα/[O III] luminosities.  
  > "We find that for our sample, the Ly$\alpha$  luminosity shows no trends with the quasar bolometric luminosity or the host galaxy’s H$\alpha$  and \[O III\]  emissions." (Comparison with OSIRIS observations of quasar host galaxies)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Compact resolved radio jets fit inside the KCWI field, enabling spatial comparison with CGM emission.  
  > "The jets are fully contained within the KCWI field of view, making it possible to correlate the jet emission with observed nebular emission in the CGM." (Discussion)
- Why this method: He II helps separate gas velocities from Lyα scattering; a 45° alignment tolerance allows radio jets to be imperfect ionization-cone proxies.  
  > "The presence of He II  emission from the same region helps resolve this degeneracy to some extent, as the presence of He II  likely indicates the true gas velocities in the CGM." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets); "This larger opening angle allows for a misalignment between the radio jet and ionization cone, consistent with previous work (cite: Wilson1996:IonizationCones)." (Major axes of radio and CGM emission)
- Alternative: Enhanced inner Lyα could reflect selection bias or increased scattering of quasar photons.  
  > "While this could be due to a selection bias, it can also originate due to enhanced emission in the inner CGM due to an increased contribution from scattering of Ly$\alpha$  photons from the quasar." (Radial profiles)
- Alternative: Multiple Lyα peaks can represent separate gas components or one component with foreground absorption.  
  > "An additional problem in identifying components occurs due to the degeneracy in distinguishing two separate kinematic components or one component with a foreground absorber." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
- Alternative: A wider approximately 60° half-opening angle would align all He II and C IV axes.  
  > "We also note that a choice of a wider opening angle of $\sim 60^{\circ}$ would align all He II  and C IV  axes with the radio jet" (Summary)
- Alternative: The candidate shock signature in 3C 9 could instead be a geometric enhancement.  
  > "While we cannot rule out this being the effect of enhanced emission due to geometric effects, this is the only indication of shock induced Ly$\alpha$  emission we see in our sample." (Sources of Ionization for the nebulae)
- How they decided it was enough: Check PSF residuals and use a conservative common angle uncertainty after noise-realization tests.  
  > "The PSF subtraction leaves no significant residuals beyond 2 pixels from the quasar ($\sim 0.6^{ \prime \prime}$)." (Data Reduction); "we choose a smaller extent to keep our uncertainty estimate uniform and conservative across all sources." (Asymmetry)
- Limitation (authors): KCWI field coverage can truncate large-radius Lyα profiles.  
  > "the higher brightness of $z\sim$ 3 quasars beyond the inner 60 kpc can be due to the KCWI FoV not covering the full extent of emission." (Radial profiles)
- Limitation (authors): Seven sources are insufficient to establish redshift evolution robustly.  
  > "While we observe an increase in luminosity from $z = 2.5$ to $z = 2$, this is statistically not a large enough sample to robustly identify evolution with redshift." (Redshift evolution)
- Limitation (authors): A sky line prevents reliable He II moment-map extraction for 4C 04.81.  
  > "While we detect He II  the flux is mixed with the sky line so moment maps for He II  could not be extracted at the same level of confidence and have been omitted here." (4C 04.81)
- Limitation (authors): Inflow in 4C 05.84 is tentative because Lyα traces the last scattering surface.  
  > "However, due to the resonant scattering of Ly$\alpha$  photons, the kinematics only trace the surface of last scattering, so the indication of inflowing material is tentative and needs further investigation." (Ly$\alpha$ emission at the location of radio jets)
- Limitation (authors): Comparisons with Wang23 are affected by different absorption corrections and PSF masking.  
  > "The maps used for asymmetry and radio jet axis comparison with nebular major axes are corrected for intrinsic absorption by foreground absorbers." (How does the CGM around blue quasars with resolved radio jets compare with CGM around other types of quasars?); "We have masked the PSF region to remove any issues due to PSF residuals, so the two samples are not strictly comparable." (How does the CGM around blue quasars with resolved radio jets compare with CGM around other types of quasars?)
