## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, flag lines and average channels**: Pipeline calibration with ALMA scripts, then line flagging and channel averaging to 8 channels of 200 MHz per spw  
  > "We identified and flagged the spectral lines that were found in both data sets." (Observations); "We also averaged channels for the low and high-resolution data; the final self-calibrated datasets contain spectral windows of 8 channels of 200 MHz each." (Observations)
   - software: CASA 5.6.1
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Self-calibrate the short-baseline data**: Phase-only (6 iterations, solint down to 18 s) then 2 rounds of amp+phase self-cal, imaged with MTMFS robust 0.5 at each step  
  > "We applied 6 iterations of phase-only self-calibration decreasing the time solution interval down to 18s." (Observations); "The MTMFS deconvolver (cite: 2011A&A...532A..71R) was used with scales of 0, 1, 3, and 5 times the beam size." (Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs robust 0.5  
  > "We also used a Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Self-cal continued until S/N stopped improving; S/N improved by factor ~17.4  
  > "until no improvement in the signal-to-noise ratio was found." (Observations); "By the end of the whole self-calibration process, we achieved an increase in the S/N by a factor of $\sim$17.4." (Observations)
3. **Align and combine short- and long-baseline data**: Gaussian fit to the high-resolution image, then FIXVIS and FIXPLANETS shifts applied to low-resolution data; self-cal of combined data attempted but gave no improvement  
  > "This was done by applying a Gaussian fit using CASA in the high-resolution image and using the tasks FIXVIS and FIXPLANETS to apply shifts to the low-resolution data." (Observations); "due to the low S/N at the long baselines, no improvement was found." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, fixvis, fixplanets
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Reweight and make final 0.45 mm images**: STATWT to recompute weights; TCLEAN with MTMFS nterms=1, scales 0,1,3,5 beams, pixel = beam/10; two images: robust 0 with baselines <12 klambda, and robust -0.5  
  > "With our final data set at hand, we recalculated the weights using the task STATWT in CASA." (Observations); "The first one, made by setting the parameter robust to 0 and using baselines shorter than 12 $k\lambda$ in length," (Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean, statwt
   - parameter: Image 1: beam ~23 mas, rms 270 uJy/beam; Image 2 (robust -0.5): beam ~12 mas, rms 380 uJy/beam  
  > "This image has an rms noise of 270 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ and a beam size of $\sim$23 mas." (Observations); "A second image was done by setting the parameter robust to -0.5, which results in a beam size of $\sim$12 mas and an rms noise of 380 $\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Extract radial brightness-temperature profiles**: Convolve to round beams, deproject with known inclination and PA, average azimuthally within +/-0.2 rad of the major axis  
  > "we could avoid this loss in resolution by simply averaging emission azimuthally within $\pm$0.2 rad of the major axis of the disk." (Observations); "Radial profiles of the brightness temperature obtained from the intensity profiles of both images are shown in Figure [2](#Fig2)." (Observations)
   - parameter: inclination 46.72 deg, PA 138.02 deg  
  > "we used the known inclination and position angles of the disk, 46.72$^{\circ}$ and 138.02$^{\circ}$, respectively (cite: 2015ApJ...808L...3A)" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Fit multi-wavelength SED at each radius for T_dust, Sigma_dust, a_max**: 1D slab model with scattering fitted independently at each radius to profiles at 0.45, 0.87, 1.3, 2.1 and 7.0 mm; new 0.45 mm image robust 1.0 convolved to 50 mas, profiles in wedges along the major axis with ~0.01 arcsec radial bins; run for compact and 90% porous grains  
  > "Then, we convolved it with a 50 mas Gaussian beam and extracted radial profiles in wedges along the major axis within radial beams of approximately 0.01 arcseconds." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties); "Subsequently, each point in the radial profiles was modeled independently." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: Flux calibration uncertainty 10% (0.45, 0.9, 1.3, 7 mm) and 5% (2.1 mm)  
  > "We assume a flux calibration uncertainty of 10% at 0.45 mm, 0.9 mm, 1.3 mm and 7 mm, and 5% at 2.1 mm." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - parameter: Porous grains with 90% porosity  
  > "we also perform an analysis by considering spherical porous dust particles with a porosity of 90%." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - assumption: Particle size distribution n(a) ~ a^-p with p = 3.5  
  > "We set the value of the exponent $p$ to 3.5, which is a common value used in the Interstellar Medium (ISM) (cite: 1977ApJ...217..425M)." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - assumption: DSHARP dust composition, effective medium approximation  
  > "The dust opacity values were obtained assuming the DSHARP dust composition and following the effective medium approximation (cite: 2018ApJ...869L..45B)." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - assumption: Dust settled in the midplane (not valid within ~32 au)  
  > "While this assumption holds well for the outer HL Tau disk, it is not met within the initial $\sim$ 32 au." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - fitting: MCMC with emcee at each radius; uniform priors on Sigma_dust and a_max; wide temperature prior from passive-irradiation profile (L_star = 6 +/- 5 Lsun, flaring angle 0.005-0.3); likelihood not stated  
  > "We assume uniform priors in dust surface density and maximum grain size." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties); "and a range in $\phi$ (i.e., flaring angle) between 0.005 and 0.3." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
   - validation: Model radial intensity profiles shown against data in Appendix A from 500 random SEDs; models fail to reproduce data at 13 au gap  
  > "we show the model radial intensity profiles at each wavelength and their uncertainties, obtained from the median, 16th, and 86th percentiles of 500 random SEDs computed from the MCMC chains." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties); "Our models find a sudden, and likely unphysical, increase in particle size at the 13 au gap." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
7. **RADMC-3D toy models of the NE-SW asymmetry in the inner disk**: Radiative transfer at 0.45 mm of a generic disk inside 32 au with a gap at 8-17 au, grid of dust mass (1, 3, 5 MJ within 32 au) and H/R (0.01 to 0.1); images convolved to the observed beam; normalised azimuthal profiles from a 0.12-0.15 arcsec ring compared to data  
  > "We use RADMC-3D (cite: 2012ascl.soft02015D) to perform radiative transfer models at 0.45 mm while varying parameters such as the disk dust mass and scale height." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk); "A gap between 8 au and 17 au was included in the model to capture the effects of the asymmetry at the outer edge of the gap." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk)
   - software: RADMC-3D, optool
   - parameter: DSHARP opacities with a_min 0.050 um, a_max 3 mm; R_in = 1 au; flaring index 0.14; Sigma proportional to r^-1  
  > "using a$_{min}$ as 0.050 $\mu$m and a$_{max}$ as 3mm." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk); "We assumed that all the grains are at the same height and we set a small inner radius $R_{in}$=1 au." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk)
   - assumption: Stellar mass 1.7 Msun, L = 6 Lsun, Teff = 4395 K, inclination 46.72 deg, distance 147 pc  
  > "the inclination angle for HL Tau was set to 46.72$^{\circ}$ and the distance to 147 pc." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk)
   - fitting: No fitting; qualitative comparison of azimuthal profiles  
  > "we do not try to reproduce the exact flux of our observations, since that would require a much more detailed modeling varying more disk parameters." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk)
   - validation: Compared model azimuthal profiles with observed profile; mass-scale-height degeneracy inferred, then broken using the 0.86 MJ dust mass within 32 au from step 6  
  > "In other words, the higher the dust mass, the less dust scale height one needs to reproduce an asymmetry." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk)
8. **Estimate free-free contamination at the disk centre**: Extrapolate VLA-based jet free-free flux (23-43 GHz) from an assumed optically-thin turnover at 100 GHz with spectral index -0.1 to 650 GHz  
  > "A conservative assumption is that emission from the jet becomes totally optically thin at 100 GHz." (4.3 Substructure in the inner disk)
   - assumption: Free-free turns optically thin at 100 GHz; spectral index -0.1 above  
  > "A conservative assumption is that emission from the jet becomes totally optically thin at 100 GHz." (4.3 Substructure in the inner disk)

## Qualitative findings

- Emission at 0.45 mm is mostly optically thick and gives a tight constraint on the dust temperature profile  
  > "Our new 0.45 mm data traces mainly optically thick emission, providing a tight constraint to the dust temperature profile." (abstract)
- Substructures at 0.45 mm have lower contrast than at longer wavelengths and the 13 au gap is very deep  
  > "substructures are detected with a lower contrast between dark and bright rings." (3 Results)
- Snowline positions alone do not explain most rings  
  > "We therefore conclude that mechanisms other than snowlines (e.g., MHD effects or planet-disk interactions) must be responsible for most, if not all, the ring substructures in HL Tau." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
- Dust masses are lower and temperatures differ relative to the earlier compact-grain analysis, mainly from adding Band 9  
  > "The primary difference between their analysis and ours is the inclusion of Band 9 in our sample." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Optically thick short-wavelength emission constrains dust temperature and albedo and probes gaps; Band 9 gives the highest resolution  
  > "For instance, optically thick emission impose better constraints on the temperature and the albedo of the dust particles, improving the results from multi-wavelength modeling of disks." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Porous grains included because recent studies suggest HL Tau contains porous dust  
  > "This is motivated by very recent results which strongly suggest that the HL Tau disk contains porous dust particles" (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
- Alternative: Free-free contamination as the cause of the inner brightness-temperature rise was rejected  
  > "First, we discard that this increase is due to free-free contamination." (4.3 Substructure in the inner disk)
- Alternative: Different weightings (natural, uniform, several robust values) were explored before choosing two images  
  > "We explored different weightings: natural, uniform, and Briggs with different robust values." (Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration stopped when S/N no longer improved  
  > "until no improvement in the signal-to-noise ratio was found." (Observations); "Since not enough solutions were found with any method to improve the quality of the combined data set, the self-calibration process was stopped." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): 1D slab model assumes midplane-settled dust and is not valid within ~32 au  
  > "While this assumption holds well for the outer HL Tau disk, it is not met within the initial $\sim$ 32 au." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
- Limitation (authors): Fit at the 13 au gap gives an unphysical particle-size jump due to limited resolution at long wavelengths  
  > "Our models find a sudden, and likely unphysical, increase in particle size at the 13 au gap." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
- Limitation (authors): Radius points are not independent, so ring/gap contrast of model parameters is smoothed  
  > "Consequently, the contrast of the model parameters between rings and gaps will be slightly smoothed in our results." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
- Limitation (authors): Dust mass depends strongly on assumed composition and should be treated with caution  
  > "Therefore, the composition of the grains plays a crucial role, and the total dust mass derived in the subsequent section should be approached with caution." (4.1 Modeling of the dust properties)
- Limitation (authors): RADMC-3D models are toy models not matching the absolute flux  
  > "we do not try to reproduce the exact flux of our observations, since that would require a much more detailed modeling varying more disk parameters." (4.2 Azimuthally asymmetric emission in the inner disk)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Each radius is fitted independently with a 1D slab model, even though the paper says the midplane-settled assumption fails inside 32 au, so inner-disk masses and grain sizes are uncertain
- Caveat (inferred, ours): H/R >= 0.08 rests on a qualitative toy model with only 3 mass and 5 scale-height values, with the dust mass taken from the same fit that is admitted to be invalid there
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No self-calibration improvement on the combined data, so phase errors on long baselines could affect the 12 mas image and the brightness-temperature rise within 2.5 au
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Brightness temperature at 12 mas beam and the absolute Band 9 flux scale (assumed 10% uncertainty) are not independently validated
