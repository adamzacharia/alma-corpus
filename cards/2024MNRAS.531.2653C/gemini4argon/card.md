## Qualitative findings

- Two redshifted high-velocity jets with bullet-like structures are identified: J1 oriented N-S from 4A1, and J2 oriented NE-SW (PA 29°) from 4A2.  
  > "In the redshifted HV1 regime, a north-south jet (J1) stemming from source 4A1 is evident. The jet shows bullet-like structures and reaches almost ∼5′′ (∼1500 au) from the protostar." (3.1.1 High Velocity (HV1 and HV2)); "We have successfully identified two distinct jets in the redshifted emission, originating from 4A1 and 4A2. The jet from 4A1 is oriented in a N-S direction, while that from 4A2, observed for the first time, exhibits a NE-SW orientation with a PA of 29◦ ." (5 CONCLUSIONS)
- Four distinct outflows are identified in the IRAS 4A system (two driven by 4A1 and two driven by 4A2), and none of the outflow cavities is bent.  
  > "Furthermore, we identify four distinct outflows in the region for the first time, with each protostar, 4A1 and 4A2, contributing to two of them." (ABSTRACT); "Each protostar has two outflow systems, with 4A1 showcasing three outflow cavities and 4A2 presenting four. Contrary to prior studies, we argue that none of the outflows is bent." (5 CONCLUSIONS)
- The feature at 20–25'' north of 4A2 previously interpreted as a sharp bend or directional variability is instead the remnant of the western wall of a second, low-velocity outflow cavity (C4-red).  
  > "However, we suggest that it is rather linked with the LV component of the 4A2 outflow (C4-red; see Sect. 3.3), being the remnant of its western cavity wall." (3.1.2 Intermediate Velocity (IV)); "Our study challenges previous interpretations of the directional variability observed in the outflow of IRAS 4A2, suggesting instead that it represents the remnants of the western wall of the lowvelocity outflow cavity." (5 CONCLUSIONS)
- The misalignment of the cavities from each protostar and the lower velocities of the more extended cavities indicate episodic ejection events with jet precession in both 4A1 and 4A2.  
  > "We propose that the misalignment of the cavities is due to a jet precession in each protostar, a notion supported by the observation that the more extended cavities of the same source exhibit lower velocities, indicating they may stem from older ejection events." (ABSTRACT)
- A velocity gradient of 3–7 km s−1 between the western and eastern walls of the C2-red cavity indicates possible cavity rotation.  
  > "Notably, we observe a velocity gradient in the red-shifted cavity walls, with the west wall exhibiting a velocity of approximately 3–7 km s−1 red-shifted compared to the east wall." (4.1 The different ejection events in the IRAS 4A system); "This intriguing observation hints at a potential rotation of the cavity, adding a layer of complexity to the dynamics of the 4A2 outflow system." (4.1 The different ejection events in the IRAS 4A system)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Three shock/outflow tracers (SiO, H2CO, HDCO) observed at high spatial (~150 au) and spectral resolution in the ALMA FAUST Large Program provide complementary insights into jets, bow-shocks, and cavities of different ages/velocities.  
  > "We employ these three tracers because a posteriori and after a fast check of the maps for this source we found that they offer complementary insights into the observed dynamics, with H 2 CO and HDCO playing a crucial role in identifying the cavities and enriching our understanding of the system’s dynamics." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Primary beam attenuation correction was omitted to preserve morphological features extending toward or beyond the edge of the field of view.  
  > "By not applying correction for primary beam attenuation, we ensure that features extending beyond the field of view, are not inadvertently excluded in our analysis." (2 OBSERVATIONS)
- Alternative: Bent northern outflow cavities from 4A1 and/or 4A2 (rejected based on the detection of Bow1 at 10'' closing the N-S 4A1 cavity and Bow2 closing C2-red)  
  > "We contend that neither of the cavities is bent. In fact, the non-detection of the bow-shock Bow1 in the previous studies has led to uncertainties regarding the extent and orientation of the cavity associated with 4A1, where it was thought that it is extended up to ∼17′′ and bent to the east." (4.2 Comparison with previous studies)
- Alternative: Deflection / directional variability of the 4A2 jet by an external dense core at ~20'' (rejected in favour of C4-w being the western wall of an older low-velocity outflow cavity C4-red)  
  > "Initially, this structure was attributed to a deflection of the 4A2 outflow caused by a dense core (Choi 2005). However, we suggest that it is rather linked with the LV component of the 4A2 outflow (C4-red; see Sect. 3.3), being the remnant of its western cavity wall." (3.1.2 Intermediate Velocity (IV))
- Alternative: Proper motion of the protostars over time causing cavity misalignment (rejected after checking historical coordinates and finding no significant proper motion)  
  > "To verify that the misalignment is attributed to a precession rather than the potential shifting position of protostars over time, we examined the presence of proper motions of this source. We found no significant proper motion" (4.2 Comparison with previous studies)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Narrow spectral window of SiO limits detection at velocities beyond 35 km s−1.  
  > "The narrow spectral window limits detection beyond 35 km s−1, however, the emission is expected to be relatively low beyond this velocity threshold." (3.1 SiO emission)
- Limitation (authors): Extended emission of C4-red and the distant SiO blob at ~35'' from 4A2 are partially filtered out or lie outside the field of view.  
  > "The cavity is not entirely detected, likely due to filtering." (3.3.3 Low Velocity (LV)); "It is worth noting that the SiO blob at ∼35′′ from 4A2 observed by Choi (2005); De Simone et al. (2022) is ∼6′′ out of our FoV and was not detected in our observations due to filtering." (4.1 The different ejection events in the IRAS 4A system)
- Limitation (authors): Spatial resolution (~150 au) is insufficient to resolve potential close binary companions (~12 au) for 4A1 and 4A2 to determine the physical cause of jet precession.  
  > "With our observations, it is challenging to determine the origin of the precession of 4A1 and 4A2’ jets, as the identification of a potential binary companion for each protostar would require an angular resolution of about 12 au." (4.2 Comparison with previous studies)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Identification of outflow cavities and their PAs/extensions relies on visual morphological matching (hand-drawn ellipses) rather than quantitative kinematic fitting.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 7-m ACA observations are mentioned in Section 2, but only the 12-m arrays are stated to be combined for the final cubes, which may exacerbate spatial filtering of large-scale cavity walls.
