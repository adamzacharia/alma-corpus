## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA continuum and spectral lines**: Pipeline calibration followed by CASA imaging using natural, Briggs (R=0.5), and uniform weighting; continuum subtraction via polynomial fit to visibilities  
  > "Visibilities were calibrated with default calibration scripts delivered by the observatory and data were processed, including imaging, with the Common Astronomy Software Applications (cite: casa)." (ALMA observations); "Continuum images include only line-free channels and cubes with line emission had continuum removed by a polynomial fit to calibrated visibilities." (ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Briggs R=0.5; natural weighting; effective frequency 224.75 GHz  
  > "We first present results for the continuum emission imaged at an effective frequency of 224.75 GHz or 1.334 mm. For the analysis, images were produced at different weighting schemes. An example map with Briggs weighting and the robust parameter of R=0.5" (ALMA results: continuum)
   - assumption: Self-calibration could not improve quality, so original calibrated visibilities were used  
  > "An attempt to improve data quality by a self-calibration procedure was unsatisfactory and visibilities with the original calibration were used." (ALMA observations)
   - fitting: Polynomial fit to line-free visibility channels  
  > "cubes with line emission had continuum removed by a polynomial fit to calibrated visibilities." (ALMA observations)
   - validation: Verification of continuum sensitivity (9.8 muJy/beam) and comparison with previous SMA and VLBI positions  
  > "The continuum sensitivity of the ALMA data is 9.8 muJy per beam at a beam full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 28x25 mas and at natural weighting of visibilities." (ALMA observations); "The location is consistent with a VLBI position of V838 Mon’s masers (cite: Ortiz-Leon)." (ALMA results: continuum)
2. **Fit continuum components in the submm image**: 2D Gaussian fitting to compact continuum sources to determine positions, deconvolved sizes, position angles, and fluxes  
  > "A Gaussian fit to this compact source (at Briggs’ R=0.5) gives a size (corrected for the beam-size) of (17.6+-0.8)x(7.6+-1.4) mas with the major axis at a position angle (PA) of 132 deg +- 4 deg." (ALMA results: continuum); "A Gaussian fit yields a beam-deconvolved size of (24.0+-3.5)x(18.5+-3.5) mas at a PA of 166 deg +- 45 deg (i.e. is nearly circular) and a flux of 510+-48 muJy" (ALMA results: continuum)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 2D Gaussian deconvolution on Briggs R=0.5 map  
  > "A Gaussian fit to this compact source (at Briggs’ R=0.5) gives a size (corrected for the beam-size) of (17.6+-0.8)x(7.6+-1.4) mas" (ALMA results: continuum)
   - assumption: Point sources convolved with Gaussian beam; distance of 5.9 kpc  
  > "projected distance to V838 Mon of 225 au (at the 5.9 kpc distance)" (ALMA results: continuum)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting to Component M and Component B  
  > "A Gaussian fit to this compact source (at Briggs’ R=0.5) gives a size (corrected for the beam-size) of (17.6+-0.8)x(7.6+-1.4) mas with the major axis at a position angle (PA) of 132 deg +- 4 deg." (ALMA results: continuum)
   - validation: Inspection of residual map after subtracting the two Gaussian components  
  > "(see the residual map in Fig. 1)." (ALMA results: continuum)
3. **Extract molecular line moments and evaluate physical gas conditions**: Generation of zeroth, first, and second moment maps for detected molecular transitions; LTE radiative transfer calculation revised with updated beam filling factors to derive column densities and molecular mass  
  > "The maps of velocity-integrated emission (zeroth moment) are shown in Fig. 3." (ALMA results: spectral lines); "Since our new ALMA observations provide the actual sizes of the emission regions, we revised the multi-line models by implementing updated beam filling factors." (ALMA results: spectral lines); "These values are slightly higher than in (cite: KamiSubmm), but they yield an overall mass of H2 of 0.1 M_odot that is consistent with the old data." (ALMA results: spectral lines)
   - parameter: CO 2-1 brightness temperatures of 30-170 K, beam filling factor = 1  
  > "Brightness temperatures (per synthesized beam) of CO 2-1 are of 30-170 K. Assuming a beam filling factor of one everywhere and given the moderate optical depth of the emission, this range is a rough measure of excitation temperatures" (ALMA results: spectral lines)
   - assumption: LTE excitation conditions; CO as representative proxy of H2 distribution  
  > "We assume that CO is the most representative probe of the entire molecular remnant, that is, it can be used as a proxy of the H2 distribution." (ALMA results: spectral lines)
   - fitting: LTE excitation analysis matching multi-line profiles  
  > "there, an LTE excitation analysis was based on SMA observations of multiple lines observed in a much wider spectral range than covered here with ALMA. Since our new ALMA observations provide the actual sizes of the emission regions, we revised the multi-line models" (ALMA results: spectral lines)
   - validation: Consistency with total H2 mass previously estimated from SMA data  
  > "yield an overall mass of H2 of 0.1 M_odot that is consistent with the old data." (ALMA results: spectral lines)
4. **Reconstruct 3D dust distribution and model broad-band SED**: Radiative transfer modeling with RADMC-3D assuming radiative equilibrium dust temperatures heated by two stars (V838 Mon and B3 V companion), ray-tracing to compute images and SED from optical to mm wavelengths  
  > "We attempted a reconstruction of dust distribution in the remnant of V838 Mon using RADMC-3D (cite: radmc3d). For a given configuration of a dust density distribution and location of radiation sources, dust temperature is calculated using the radiative equilibrium assumption." (Dust)
   - software: RADMC-3D
   - parameter: B3 V companion: T_eff=18 kK, R=4.5 R_odot, L=1.9e3 L_odot; V838 Mon: T_eff=3300 K, R=464 R_odot, L=2.3e4 L_odot; dust: Mg-rich silicates (70% Mg, 30% Fe pyroxene, a=0.1 um, rho_d=3 g cm^-3)  
  > "The B3 V companion was represented by a black body of a temperature of 18 kK, a radius of 3.1 10^11 cm (4.5 R_odot), and a luminosity of 1.9 10^3 L_odot." (Dust); "The optimal values we arrived at are 3.25 10^13 cm (464 R_odot) for the radius and 2.3 10^4 L_odot for the luminosity." (Dust); "We arbitrarily implemented an opacity curve of Mg-rich silicates (amorphous pyroxene with 70% Mg-rich and 30% Fe-rich) compiled from data in (cite: silicate1) and (cite: silicate2) and for a single grain size of 0.1 mu m. Grain density was set to 3 g cm^-3." (Dust)
   - assumption: Only two stellar heat sources in sky plane separated by 230 au; components: inclined disk (M), spherical mergeburst shell, shell around B star, and two Gaussian clouds  
  > "The only heat sources in the system are the two stars." (Dust); "The simulated M component is a disk-like or torus-like structure immediately surrounding the stellar remnant of V838 Mon and has a dust mass of 9.4 10^-4 M_odot." (Dust)
   - fitting: Exploratory grid search running thousands of RADMC-3D simulations to match the SED and spatial submm features  
  > "Even in the simplest considered configuration, the modeled system has numerous free parameters. They cannot be uniquely constrained by our observations and therefore the model we attempted to match to the observations was rather exploratory. After running thousands of simulations, we arrived at a model described below" (Dust)
   - validation: Simultaneous visual and quantitative match to the SED (U-band to mm) and ALMA continuum image morphology  
  > "A comparison between observed and simulated images and SEDs are shown in Figs. 9 and 10." (Dust)
5. **Reconstruct 3D spatio-kinematic structure of SiO emission**: 3D morpho-kinematic modeling in the optically thin limit with Shape software, implementing two shell-like structures with linear velocity gradients and a cone-shaped void  
  > "To investigate the possible structure of the outflow in molecular tracers other than CO, we used the Shape software (cite: shape1), which gives more flexibility in constructing complex spatial models than RADMC-3D. We focused on reproducing the observations in optically thin limit with very simplified radiative transfer." (Molecular gas)
   - software: Shape
   - parameter: Linear velocity fields v = k*r with k=1.7 for inner shell and k=1.9 for outer shell; cone-shaped void in NW toward B star  
  > "The latter were implemented as simple relations with radial velocity increasing linearly with distance (v=kr). The velocity gradient is steeper for the outer structure (k=1.9) than for the inner one (k=1.7)." (Molecular gas); "The larger structure is more spherically symmetric, but it required introducing a cone-shaped void in the north-western part." (Molecular gas)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission and simplified radiative transfer  
  > "We focused on reproducing the observations in optically thin limit with very simplified radiative transfer." (Molecular gas)
   - fitting: Qualitative adjustment of 3D geometry and velocity field to match channel maps and position-velocity diagrams  
  > "Our simple model satisfactorily reproduces structures in channel maps, shown in Appendix 11, and position-velocity diagrams but no attempt to reproduce absolute intensities was undertaken in this case." (Molecular gas)
   - validation: Comparison with SiO channel maps and position-velocity cuts  
  > "Our simple model satisfactorily reproduces structures in channel maps, shown in Appendix 11, and position-velocity diagrams" (Molecular gas)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA submm continuum directly resolves the B-type companion at a projected separation of 225.4 au from V838 Mon.  
  > "For the first time, we identify the position of the B-type companion relative to the outbursting component of V838 Mon." (abstract); "Its projected distance to V838 Mon of 225 au (at the 5.9 kpc distance) is close to the separation a ~ 250 au calculated from the timing of the interaction of the B-type star with the merger ejecta" (ALMA results: continuum)
- The central merger remnant is associated with an elongated submm structure (17.6 x 7.6 mas) consistent with an inclined dusty disk or torus.  
  > "The merger product is also associated with an elongated structure, 17.6 x 7.6 mas (104 x 45 au), seen in continuum emission, and which we interpret as a disk seen at a moderate inclination." (abstract)
- The molecular gas envelope is clumpy, non-spherical, and shows evidence of chemical differentiation and shock interaction near the B companion, with SO2/H2S and AlOH enhanced and SiO depleted.  
  > "Some species appear to have enhanced abundances in the interaction region, including SO2/H2S and AlOH, others are depleted (SiO, Sect. 6.2)." (Interaction with the B star); "The B-type star not only deflects the merger ejecta but also changes its chemical composition with an involvement of circumstellar shocks." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 6 in its most extended configuration provides ~25 mas resolution, necessary to resolve the 250 au binary separation and the circumstellar dust/gas distribution at 5.9 kpc.  
  > "With submillimeter observations at 20 mas resolution, for the first time we spatially resolve both gas and dust in the circumstellar environment." (Introduction)
- Why this method: 3D radiative transfer with RADMC-3D and morpho-kinematic modeling with Shape allow disentangling geometric projection and radiative effects from complex multi-wavelength observations.  
  > "We attempted a reconstruction of dust distribution in the remnant of V838 Mon using RADMC-3D (cite: radmc3d). For a given configuration of a dust density distribution and location of radiation sources, dust temperature is calculated using the radiative equilibrium assumption." (Dust); "To investigate the possible structure of the outflow in molecular tracers other than CO, we used the Shape software (cite: shape1), which gives more flexibility in constructing complex spatial models than RADMC-3D." (Molecular gas)
- Alternative: Spherical dusty cloud for Component M was rejected because it produced significantly higher visual extinction toward the central star than observed.  
  > "We also attempted to reproduce the M component by a spherical dusty cloud, such as one expected from a spherically expanding supergiant wind. However, such a configuration results in a higher extinction towards the central star than observed. We favor models with a flattened structure, such as a disk or a torus." (Dust)
- Alternative: Self-calibration was attempted on the ALMA visibility data but rejected as it did not improve data quality satisfactorily.  
  > "An attempt to improve data quality by a self-calibration procedure was unsatisfactory and visibilities with the original calibration were used." (ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Significant ongoing Bondi-Hoyle accretion onto the B star was considered but found energetically negligible (accretion rate ~10^-10 M_sun/yr, luminosity ~0.006 L_sun) and accretion is largely prevented by radiation pressure stopping ejecta.  
  > "Although we invoke the BHL scenario here, we do not claim that there is significant ongoing accretion onto the B star... it implies a very low accretion luminosity of ~0.006 L_odot, insignificant for the system energetics." (Wind accretion (deflection)); "Therefore, it is stopped by the radiation pressure about 1000 stellar radii above the stellar photosphere, well before the dust grains can evaporate. No matter is accreted on the companion." (Shocks induced by the wind of the B star)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only a single rotational transition was observed for each molecule, preventing measurement of temperature gradients across the remnant.  
  > "Although ALMA spatially resolved the source, we are unable to investigate temperature gradients through line ratios because for each species ALMA covered only a single transition." (ALMA results: spectral lines)
- Limitation (authors): Degeneracy and lack of uniqueness in RADMC-3D and Shape 3D reconstructions due to numerous free parameters.  
  > "Even in the simplest considered configuration, the modeled system has numerous free parameters. They cannot be uniquely constrained by our observations and therefore the model we attempted to match to the observations was rather exploratory." (Dust); "Although we were able to construct models that very satisfactorily reproduce maps and spectral profiles of CO observed by ALMA, the solutions we find are by no means unique or particularly revealing." (Molecular gas)
- Limitation (authors): The fast atomic ejecta (500 km/s) reaches scales of ~600 mas and is invisible to ALMA submm observations, leaving the atomic mass unconstrained.  
  > "The mass of the atomic component remains unconstrained." (abstract); "Our observations therefore show us only the inner (~half) part of the gaseous remnant. Observations with an instrument capable of resolving atomic or dust-scattered emission at scales of 600 mas would be necessary to probe the high-velocity ejecta." (Unseen atomic gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 3D RADMC-3D dust model has significant parameter degeneracies, meaning geometry and dust mass numbers should be treated as illustrative rather than uniquely determined.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The SiO void and shock chemistry interpretation rests partly on qualitative morphological comparison, as non-LTE chemistry modeling was not performed.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Identification of Component M as an inclined disk is suggestive but remains marginally resolved with ALMA, awaiting confirmation by IR interferometry.
