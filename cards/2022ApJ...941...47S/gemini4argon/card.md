## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, star_formation
- Question: What are the properties, distribution, and heating/excitation mechanisms of the molecular, atomic, and ionized gas and dust in the ring of M 104, and how do its [CII] and CO emissions compare to early-type and star-forming galaxies?  
  > "In order to further delve into the mysteries still surrounding M 104, we compare different gas and dust tracers from a wide–array of archival datasets." (Introduction); "By comparing the observed [CII] emission to indicators of unattenuated star formation like H$\alpha$ and FUV, indicators of dust heating like polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission, and indicators of molecular gas like CO, we can better determine what physical processes lead to an increase or decrease in the amount of [CII] emission we observe from a galaxy." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce and recalibrate Herschel/PACS [CII] unchopped spectroscopic data and VLT/MUSE optical cubes**: Modified HIPE 15 unchopped pipeline with Python scripts to remove band-switch, long-term, and cosmic-ray transients and calibrate to the telescope background; reprocess 10 MUSE observations with MUSE pipeline 2.8.5 and shift velocity frame to LSRK.  
  > "The data reduction has been done using the *unchopped pipeline with transient corrections* available in HIPE 15 (cite: Fadda2016) with some important modifications which are described in detail in the appendix 5." (Herschel/PACS spectra); "We used the MUSE pipeline version 2.8.5 and the pre–processed calibration data from the archive (cite: Weilbacher2020)." (MUSE spectroscopy); "To directly compare the cubes we changed the spectral reference system to LSRK by adding 0.9 km/s to the MUSE data" (MUSE spectroscopy)
   - software: HIPE, MUSE pipeline, Python
   - parameter: PACS cube spatial pixel = 3 arcsec; MUSE cube spatial pixel = 0.4 arcsec; MUSE velocity shifts = -28.5 km/s (barycentric) and +0.9 km/s (LSRK)  
  > "The data are finally reprojected into a spectral cube with a spatial pixel of 3 arcseconds." (Transient correction of PACS data); "The final cube was reprojected to a 0.4 arcsec pixel grid" (MUSE spectroscopy)
   - assumption: Telescope background is stable and dominates total flux over weak target flux  
  > "Moreover, since the flux of the observed target is very weak and the background of the telescope dominates total flux, the total flux does not differ too much from the off-target positions for the majority of the observation." (Transient correction of PACS data)
   - fitting: Combination of exponential functions for cosmic-ray transients via specTransCorr module  
  > "Such transients can be fitted with a combination of exponential functions as explained in (cite: Fadda2016)." (Transient correction of PACS data)
   - validation: Reduced off-target MUSE sky spectra to verify wavelength calibration shifts (< 0.01 Angstrom near Halpha/Hbeta); noted telescope-background PACS calibration was previously verified against SOFIA/FIFI-LS in NGC 7331.  
  > "We verified that the corrections were of the same order of those of the M 104 observations (around 0.2 Å) and that the main lines close to the H$\beta$ and H$\alpha$ lines had a shift of less than 0.01 Å." (MUSE spectroscopy); "Also, as shown in (cite: Sutter2022), the recalibration of unchopped data using the telescope background as absolute reference agrees very well with measurements done with SOFIA/FIFI-LS in NGC 7331 (cite: Fadda2019)." (Transient correction of PACS data)
2. **Produce pixel-by-pixel line intensity, velocity, and velocity dispersion maps**: Fit CO, [CII], and Halpha lines across the ALMA, PACS, and MUSE spectral cubes using sospex, masking pixels with SNR < 5.  
  > "lines in the ALMA, PACS, and MUSE spectral cubes to produce maps of the line intensity, velocity, and velocity dispersion using the interactive software" (CO, [CII], and Halpha maps); "The pixels where the signal-to-noise ratio of the line fit was lower than 5 are left black." (CO, [CII], and H$\alpha$ maps)
   - software: sospex
   - parameter: SNR threshold = 5  
  > "The pixels where the signal-to-noise ratio of the line fit was lower than 5 are left black." (CO, [CII], and H$\alpha$ maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure galaxy inclination angle**: Mask the central bulge and fit an ellipse to the infrared (IRAC Ch4, MIPS 24, PACS 70, 100, 160) and ALMA CO intensity images using direct linear least squares.  
  > "We found that the ring is perfectly fit by an ellipse once the central bulge is masked using the direct linear least squares fitting method as described by (cite: Halir1998)." (Galaxy inclination angle); "Since the images which are less disturbed by the nuclear emission are the PACS 160$\mu$m and the ALMA CO intensity image, we accept the value fitted in the case of the CO: 84.3$^o\pm0.2^o$." (Galaxy inclination angle)
   - assumption: The ring of M 104 is intrinsically circular  
  > "it is possible to fit an ellipse and compute a more accurate value of the inclination, based on the hypothesis of a circular ring." (Galaxy inclination angle)
   - fitting: Direct linear least squares ellipse fitting (cite: Halir1998)  
  > "using the direct linear least squares fitting method as described by (cite: Halir1998)." (Galaxy inclination angle)
   - validation: Varied flux thresholds for pixel selection to estimate fit stability (all bands agree within 0.7 deg) and compared with literature values.  
  > "The errors reported have been computed by using different flux thresholds to select the pixels used in the fitting of the ellipses and can be considered an estimate of the stability of the fit. The values are within a small range (0.7$^o$)." (Galaxy inclination angle)
4. **Extract aperture spectra, fit emission/absorption lines, and analyze gas and stellar kinematics**: Extract spectra in 59 apertures along the ring and interior (10.7'' circular diameter for PACS/MUSE; 14.3'' x 7.7'' elliptical beam shape for ALMA), fit CO and [CII] with sospex and optical spectra with showspectra, and fit linear v_los vs projected distance.  
  > "Such apertures have the diameter of the [CII] beam (10.7 arcsec)." (Aperture spectra); "In the case of ALMA we used apertures with the same center but with the elliptical shape of the ALMA beam." (Aperture spectra); "while for the MUSE spectra we used the software" (Aperture spectra)
   - software: sospex, showspectra
   - parameter: Circular aperture diameter = 10.7 arcsec; ALMA aperture = 14.3'' x 7.7'' ellipse  
  > "Such apertures have the diameter of the [CII] beam (10.7 arcsec)." (Aperture spectra); "In the case of ALMA we used apertures with the same center but with the elliptical shape of the ALMA beam." (Aperture spectra)
   - assumption: Circular ring rotating with constant angular velocity Omega; distance = 31.1 Mpc (70 pc/arcsec)  
  > "In fact, assuming a circular ring rotating with the angular velocity $\Omega$, the component of the velocity along the line of sight is: $$\begin{equation} v_{los} = r_{0} \Omega \cos\phi + v_{sys} = r_{p} \Omega + v_{sys}, \end{equation}$$" (Kinematics of stars and gas)
   - fitting: Gaussian profiles for ALMA CO; Voigt profiles for PACS [CII]; simultaneous absorption + emission components for Balmer lines; SDSS template cross-correlation and weighted emission-line mean for MUSE velocities; linear fit of v_los vs r_p.  
  > "We fit the [CII] line using a Voigt function which accurately describes the profile of the line. In the case of the ALMA data we opted for fitting Gaussian functions" (Kinematics of stars and gas); "In the case of the MUSE data, we measured the redshifts from the optical spectra of the selected apertures using two methods: cross-correlation with SDSS templates (mainly based on absorption features) and measurement of emission lines" (Kinematics of stars and gas); "The Balmer lines (H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$) were always fit using a component in absorption and one in emission." (LIER and HII regions)
   - validation: Compared systemic velocity and angular velocity across four independent tracers (CO, [CII], optical emission, optical cross-correlation) and with Lavaux & Hudson (2011).  
  > "The data of the [CII], CO, and optical spectra give compatible values for slope and systemic velocity inside the errors." (Kinematics of stars and gas)
5. **Classify regions into HII/Comp and LIER using optical emission line diagnostics**: Construct BPT and weak-line diagnostic diagrams ([NII]/Halpha vs [OIII]/Halpha and EW(Halpha) vs [NII]/Halpha) from MUSE aperture line fluxes to classify regions.  
  > "To determine the classification for all the defined apertures, we used two alternative diagnostic diagrams developed for weak line galaxies as described in (cite: CidFernandes2010) which are shown in the two bottom panels of Fig. 7." (LIER and HII regions); "In the following figures, apertures in the HII/Comp and LIER regions as determined used the bottom panel of Figure 7 are marked with orange and green symbols, respectively." (LIER and HII regions)
   - software: showspectra
   - parameter: 3-sigma lower limits used when Hbeta is barely visible  
  > "In the cases where this line is barely visible, we show 3-$\sigma$ lower limits." (LIER and HII regions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared classifications across four different optical diagnostic diagrams in Figure 7.  
  > "Regardless of the diagnostic used, the majority of the spectra lie in the LIER region." (LIER and HII regions)
6. **Smooth multi-wavelength photometry and fit SEDs to derive FIR luminosity, attenuated UV, SFR, and PAH fluxes**: Smooth FUV to PACS 160 um images to 10.7'' resolution, extract fluxes in the 10.7'' circular apertures, fit SEDs with CIGALE, integrate models for FIR (8-1000 um) and attenuated UV (91.2-206.6 nm), and subtract non-PAH continuum from IRAC4 and WISE3 to get 7.7 and 11.3 um PAH fluxes.  
  > "Spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting was performed using the Code for Investigating Galaxy Emission, or CIGALE (cite: Boquien2019)." (SED Modeling); "All of the available photometry described in Section 2.4 was first smoothed to a common resolution of 10.7$''$, the native resolution of the PACS spectroscopy at 158 $\mu$m." (SED Modeling); "To remove contributions from stars, large dust grains, and AGN emission from these bands, the modelled flux from each of these components is summed, convolved with the transmission function for the specified band, and then subtracted from the observed flux." ([CII] and PAH emission)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: Common resolution = 10.7''; FIR integration range = 8-1000 um; UV_att integration range = 91.2-206.6 nm; f_AGN = 0 for ring regions, f_AGN = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 for interior regions; Z = Z_odot  
  > "The modeled attenuated light is then integrated from $\lambda = 91.2-206.6$nm to determine the total amount of attenuated UV light." ([CII] and UV attenuation); "The FIR fluxes were computed by integrating the modelled SEDs from $\lambda=8-1000 \mu$m." ([CII] and total infrared luminosity); "Regions interior to the ring were modeled adding a component of AGN emission, while the regions along the ring were modeled assuming no AGN component." (SED Modeling)
   - assumption: Energy balance between UV absorption and IR emission; Bruzual & Charlot (2003) SSP with Chabrier (2003) IMF; Calzetti (2000) attenuation; Draine et al. (2014) dust models; Fritz et al. (2006) AGN model; 10% uncertainty on CIGALE-derived quantities.  
  > "For both region sets, we use the (cite: BruzualCharlot2003) stellar population model with a (cite: Chabrier2003) IMF and the (cite: Draine2014) dust models. We set the metallicity at $Z = Z_{\odot}$." (SED Modeling); "For measurements determined using the CIGALE SED models (PAH 7.7$\mu$m and 11.3$\mu$m, FIR, and UV$_{\rm{ att}}$) we assume 10% errors due to the uncertainties introduced during the modelling process." (Measurements)
   - fitting: Grid of models in CIGALE (parameters in Table 5)  
  > "Table notes: Multiple values for a parameter define a grid of models. Values of unlisted parameters are left at the CIGALE defaults." (Table 5)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Estimate total and resolved molecular hydrogen (H2) masses and compare with SFR (Kennicutt-Schmidt relation)**: Integrate total and aperture CO(1-0) emission, apply a factor of 2 optical depth correction and Eq. 3 of Bolatto et al. (2013) to obtain M(H2), and divide M(H2) and CIGALE SFR by deprojected aperture area.  
  > "We can estimate the total mass of molecular hydrogen in the ring by integrating the total CO emission. We assume a factor 2 correction to account for the likelihood that the $^{12}$CO$_{1\rightarrow 0}$ line will become optically thick in dense star–forming regions (cite: Hughes2017). By applying the conversion from Eq. 3 from (cite: Bolatto2013), we find out that the total molecular hydrogen mass of the ring of M 104 is $0.9\times10^9$ M$_\odot$." (Measuring the $H_2$ Mass); "Both the H$_2$ gas mass and the SFR are converted to surface densities by dividing by the deprojected area of the aperture used in the measurement." ([CII] and CO emission)
   - parameter: Optical depth correction factor = 2; inclination = 84.3 deg; distance = 31.1 Mpc  
  > "We assume a factor 2 correction to account for the likelihood that the $^{12}$CO$_{1\rightarrow 0}$ line will become optically thick in dense star–forming regions (cite: Hughes2017)." (Measuring the $H_2$ Mass)
   - assumption: CO(1-0) optical depth correction of 2 (cite: Hughes2017) and CO-to-H2 conversion from Eq. 3 of (cite: Bolatto2013)  
  > "We assume a factor 2 correction to account for the likelihood that the $^{12}$CO$_{1\rightarrow 0}$ line will become optically thick in dense star–forming regions (cite: Hughes2017). By applying the conversion from Eq. 3 from (cite: Bolatto2013), we find out that the total molecular hydrogen mass of the ring of M 104 is $0.9\times10^9$ M$_\odot$." (Measuring the $H_2$ Mass)

   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- Molecular hydrogen (traced by CO) and dust (traced by far-IR) are uniformly distributed along the ring of M 104, whereas ionized gas (Halpha and [CII]) is distributed in discrete knots.  
  > "In particular, molecular hydrogen measured by CO emission and dust measured by far-infrared light are uniformly distributed along the ring. The ionized gas revealed by H$\alpha$ and [CII] emission is distributed in knots along the ring." (Abstract)
- Stars and gas (ionized, atomic, and molecular) along the ring share the same regular rotational kinematics within measurement errors.  
  > "The kinematics of stars and gas along the ring is extremely regular. The ionized gas traced by the H$\alpha$ emission as well as the gas traced by CO and [CII] have the same rotational velocity inside the measurement errors." (Summary and Conclusions)
- The majority of regions in M 104 are classified as LIERs, and the galaxy exhibits low [CII]/FIR ratios similar to early-type galaxies rather than star-forming galaxies.  
  > "Regardless of the diagnostic used, the majority of the spectra lie in the LIER region." (LIER and HII regions); "The [CII]/FIR ratio of M104 is much lower than that of typical star-forming galaxies and is instead much more similar to that of early-type galaxies." (Abstract)
- There is no correlation between H2 surface density and star formation rate surface density along the ring of M 104, indicating inefficient star formation and that [CII] mainly traces ionized and neutral atomic gas rather than molecular gas.  
  > "Figure 14 clearly shows that there is no correlation between $\Sigma_{H_2}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm{SFR}}$ in the regions within M 104." ([CII] and CO emission); "It is therefore clear that the molecular hydrogen of the ring is not converted efficiently into stars and that most of the [CII] emission is probably not related to molecular gas, but rather to ionized and neutral atomic gas." (Summary and Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Compiling multi-wavelength archival datasets (ALMA CO, Herschel [CII] and FIR, MUSE optical spectroscopy, UV-to-IR photometry) of the nearby, nearly edge-on galaxy M 104 allows isolating different ISM phases along the dusty ring and comparing with unresolved high-redshift and early-type galaxies.  
  > "As individual emission lines and features trace different component of the interstellar medium (ISM), by compiling multi–wavelength observations of M 104 we can build a more complete picture of what is happening inside this galaxy" (Introduction); "Viewing M 104 nearly edge–on also gives us an unique perspective to study the distribution of different ISM phases, especially as we can observe the inner and outer edges of the dusty ring." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used telescope background for PACS flux calibration because unchopped calibration blocks suffer from detector transients; used weak-line optical diagnostics (Cid Fernandes et al. 2010) because Hbeta is heavily absorbed; excluded SPIRE 250 um from SED fitting to preserve the 10.7'' spatial resolution of PACS [CII].  
  > "In our analysis, we use the telescope background to calibrate the data. As shown in the appendix, this introduces a correction of 50% to 60% with respect to the calibration obtained using the calibration blocks." (Herschel/PACS spectra); "The typical diagnostic diagrams which make use of the H$\beta$ emission line are not possible for many of the spectra." (LIER and HII regions); "With this in mind, we choose not to include the 250 $\mu$m data and retain the highest possible spatial resolution, even if by using the 250 $\mu$m data we could have obtained more accurate estimates of the total far-IR flux." (SED Modeling)
- Alternative: Using standard Herschel archive PACS cubes calibrated with calibration blocks (rejected due to a 20-25% transient gradient and ~55% flux overestimation)  
  > "This generates a large gradient in the final data which is clearly visible in the spectral cubes stored in the Herschel archive." (Herschel/PACS spectra); "As visible in the ratio of signal over telescope background, the signal calibrated with the calibration blocks (the technique used in the archival product) grossly overestimates the flux (by approximately 55%, in this case)." (Transient correction of PACS data)
- Alternative: Using standard ESO archive combined MUSE cubes (rejected because incorrect offsets during coaddition produced fake double sources)  
  > "However, some of the observations were coadded with incorrect offsets producing fake double sources. We decided to reprocess the 10 observations covering the region observed in [CII] and CO" (MUSE spectroscopy)
- Alternative: Including Herschel/SPIRE 250 um photometry in CIGALE SED fits (rejected because the ~18'' PSF is much larger than the 10.7'' PACS [CII] resolution)  
  > "Additional Far-IR photometery is available from the SPIRE instrument on *Herschel* was not included in the SED analysis due to the larger beam of even the shortest–wavelength SPIRE band." (SED Modeling)
- Alternative: Including PDR models on the [CII]/FIR vs CO/FIR plot (rejected because CO emission in M 104 does not trace ongoing star formation or PDR conditions)  
  > "For this reason, we choose to not include PDR models on this plot, as it seems the CO emission may not be tracing this phase of the ISM." ([CII] and CO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Excluding SPIRE 250 um photometry to preserve spatial resolution reduces the accuracy of total far-IR flux estimates.  
  > "With this in mind, we choose not to include the 250 $\mu$m data and retain the highest possible spatial resolution, even if by using the 250 $\mu$m data we could have obtained more accurate estimates of the total far-IR flux." (SED Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Strong stellar absorption in the optical spectra prevents measuring Hbeta and [OIII] in emission in many apertures.  
  > "In contrast, due to attenuation, the [OIII] and H$\beta$ lines are not always measurable in emission." (LIER and HII regions)
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA beam (14.3'' x 7.7'') intercepts multiple molecular clouds with slightly different velocities along the inclined ring, artificially broadening the CO line profile except at the major axis extremes.  
  > "As we consider parts of the ring in other angles, the beam intercepts several molecular clouds with slightly different velocity. These components are very close in velocity and difficult to resolve. For this reason, the profile of the line is approximately Gaussian and its velocity dispersion increases dramatically along the rest of the ring." (CO, [CII], and H$\alpha$ maps)
- Limitation (authors): Herschel/PACS velocity resolution is insufficient to resolve multiple velocity components of [CII] along the ring.  
  > "In the case of the *Herschel* observations, the velocity resolution of the [CII] line is not sufficient to distinguish different components along the ring." (CO, [CII], and H$\alpha$ maps)
- Limitation (authors): ETG comparison sample could not be included in the PAH analysis due to sparse mid-to-far-IR photometric coverage for global SED decomposition.  
  > "The ETG sample is not included in these measurements due to uncertainties in our PAH estimation when considering global measurements." ([CII] and PAH emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Aperture mismatch: ALMA CO fluxes were extracted in 14.3'' x 7.7'' elliptical apertures matching the ALMA beam, whereas [CII], MUSE, and SED fluxes were extracted in 10.7'' circular apertures without convolving the cubes to a single common beam.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only ALMA 7 m array (ACA) data were used without 12 m or Total Power observations, so extended emission on scales larger than the ACA maximum recoverable scale may be filtered out, and clumpy structure below 14.3'' x 7.7'' is unresolved.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a factor of 2 optical depth correction designed for dense star-forming regions (Hughes et al. 2017) alongside a standard CO-to-H2 conversion factor may overestimate H2 mass in a quiescent, LIER-dominated ring.
