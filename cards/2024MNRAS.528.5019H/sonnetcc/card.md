## Science

- Fields: high_z_galaxies, cosmology
- Question: Combine all archival ALMA Band 6 data on the HUDF into the deepest 1.23 mm map, catalogue the dusty star-forming galaxies and their JWST/HST counterparts, and measure how much of the cosmic infrared background at 1.23 mm is resolved by detections plus stacking  
  > "with the goal of finding new DSFGs, identifying their counterparts, assessing how much of the background light has been resolved and providing an image that can be used by others for stacking analyses." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **retrieve and calibrate archival uv data**: Query the ALMA archive for Band 6 data within 1.5 arcmin of the HUDF centre, download raw data, calibrate with the provided scriptForPI, split science targets, time-average and channel-average  
  > "calibrated it using the provided SCRIPTFORPI and the CASA (cite: Mcmullin2007) version used by the observatory at the time of the observations" (2.1 Obtaining archival ALMA data); "We split the science targets from the calibration targets, then time-averaged the data by 30 s and averaged the frequency channels by a factor of 4 in order to reduce the volume of data." (2.1 Obtaining archival ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, CANFAR
   - parameter: time averaging 30 s; frequency channel averaging factor 4  
  > "We split the science targets from the calibration targets, then time-averaged the data by 30 s and averaged the frequency channels by a factor of 4 in order to reduce the volume of data." (2.1 Obtaining archival ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **make combined uv-plane continuum images**: Image all averaged uv data jointly with tclean in MFS mode with natural weighting and a 250 klambda taper (also untapered and uniform-noise variants), for a deep central map and a wider shallower map  
  > "All of the time-averaged and frequency-averaged $uv$ data were imaged using the standard CASA task TCLEAN." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane); "We also chose natural weighting (each $uv$ point is weighted by its instrumental noise), but with a 250 k$\lambda$ $uv$ taper." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
   - software: CASA tclean
   - parameter: pixel 0.2 arcsec (deep tapered), primary beam cutoff 0.2; wide map pixel 0.12 arcsec, cutoff 0.4  
  > "We set the cutoff of the map to 0.2 times the primary beam, which is where the footprint of our map roughly matches the footprint of the ASPECS map." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane); "We set the pixel size to 0.06 arcsec for the map with no $uv$ tapering, and 0.12 arcsec for the map with 250 k$\lambda$ $uv$ tapering" (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
   - parameter: clean threshold 20 uJy/beam (deep), 100 uJy/beam (wide)  
  > "Lastly, we cleaned the image down to 20 $\umu$Jy beam$^{-1}$, similar to the cleaning level chosen by ASPECS." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
   - assumption: No correction for spectral variation across about 30 per cent bandwidth  
  > "The frequencies that are combined to produce our map range from about 211 to 275 GHz, or roughly 30 per cent, thus we do not attempt to correct for potential artefacts." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Maps with no taper and with uniform noise (large survey programmes only) used to check flux densities; no systematic differences found  
  > "were used to check the reliability of our flux densities, and we found no systematic differences between the measurements." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
3. **build noise and S/N maps**: Mask known galaxies from published catalogues, compute rms in cutouts around each pixel of the masked map, smooth the result  
  > "For each galaxy we masked a region 3 times larger than the beamsize, and then multiplied this by the signal map. We next made cutouts of 5 times the beamsize around each pixel and calculated the rms." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
   - parameter: mask 3 x beamsize; rms cutout 5 x beamsize; Gaussian smoothing with kernel equal to cutout size  
  > "The resulting noise map was then smoothed with a Gaussian kernel with the same size as the cutout regions to remove artefacts." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Gaussian fit to the pixel distribution gives mean -0.08 and width 4.60 uJy/beam versus expected minimum noise 4.58 uJy/beam  
  > "We find a mean value of $-0.08\,\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$ (very close to zero, as expected) and a width of $4.60\,\mu$Jy beam$^{-1}$." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
4. **detect sources and set the threshold**: Run peak finding on the S/N map and its negative; choose the threshold by purity = 1 - N_neg/N_pos  
  > "We ran the simple peak-finding algorithm FIND_PEAKS, available in the PHOTUTILS PYTHON module, on both the S/N map contained within the region of interest, and on the negative of the same S/N map." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - software: photutils find_peaks, Python
   - parameter: purity 0.7 corresponds to peak S/N = 3.6; 45 sources above threshold  
  > "we choose a purity of 0.7, which corresponds to a peak ${\rm S/N}\,{=}\,3.6$. There are 45 sources above this threshold." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - assumption: Source extraction relies on Gaussian noise; negative peaks used as the false-positive proxy  
  > "Our source extraction relies on the Gaussianity of the noise" (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with published surveys; negative peaks (14 beyond 3.6) versus expected false positives (about 3); six sources lack counterparts  
  > "The measured number of negative peaks more significant than 3.6 is 14, which is larger than the expectation from a Gaussian distribution" (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources)
5. **measure source flux densities**: Fit a 2D Gaussian with position and amplitude fixed at the peak pixel, derive number of beams, integrated flux S1230 = S_peak x N_b if N_b > 1 else peak pixel  
  > "for each source we fit a 2-dimensional Gaussian profile, fixing the position to the peak pixel and the amplitude to the value of the peak pixel, but allowing the size, ellipticity and position angle to vary." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources); "We also flag fits where the minor/major axis ratio is less than 0.5 as bad fits, and use peak pixel values for these sources." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - parameter: wide map: peak pixel used above 1000 uJy and a 2 arcsec aperture for ALMA-HUDF-69  
  > "We therefore set a threshold of 1000 $\mu$Jy above which we do not fit a 2D Gaussian, and instead just take the peak pixel value." (7 Sources in the wider region)
   - assumption: No deboosting corrections applied  
  > "While we have not performed any deboosting corrections for the individual flux densities measured in our catalogue" (4.1 Resolved source contribution to the cosmic infrared background)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit, uncertainties from the noise map with fit uncertainties propagated  
  > "Uncertainties are taken from the noise map, and uncertainties from the fits are propagated to the integrated flux densities." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - validation: Compared with ASAGAO, ASPECS, GOODS-ALMA and Dunlop et al. flux densities after frequency correction, generally good agreement  
  > "We find generally good agreement with all of the previously-published flux densities after applying the above corrections." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
6. **compare with published catalogues**: Match published sources within 1 arcsec; scale Dunlop et al. and GOODS-ALMA flux densities to 243 GHz with a modified blackbody  
  > "in this comparison we simply match published sources to our new catalogue using a search radius of 1 arcsec." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - parameter: beta = 1.8, Td = 25 K, galaxy redshift or z = 1.5 if unknown; GOODS-ALMA correction factor 0.8  
  > "so we correct their flux densities to the mean frequency of our map (243 GHz) assuming a modified blackbody SED with spectral index $\beta\,{=}\,1.8$, a dust temperature of 25 K" (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - assumption: modified blackbody SED beta = 1.8, Td = 25 K  
  > "so we correct their flux densities to the mean frequency of our map (243 GHz) assuming a modified blackbody SED with spectral index $\beta\,{=}\,1.8$, a dust temperature of 25 K" (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 13 new DSFGs relative to published catalogues  
  > "It appears that most of the detections in our combined map coincide with published sources, but there are 13 new DSFGs." (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
7. **match ALMA sources to JADES and CANDELS counterparts**: Search JADES within 1.9 arcsec/(S/N) (minimum 0.3 arcsec); CANDELS within a uniform 0.6 arcsec after applying a 0.25 arcsec southward offset; checked by eye  
  > "we thus search the JADES catalogue out to a distance of 1.9 arcsec/(S/N), where S/N here is the peak S/N of each source found above our detection threshold." (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources); "Here, we simply apply a uniform search radius of 0.6 arcsec" (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources)
   - parameter: minimum JADES search radius 0.3 arcsec  
  > "we apply a minimal search radius of 0.3 arcsec." (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources)
   - assumption: 2.5 x radial positional uncertainty gives 95 per cent correct matches  
  > "one must go out to a distance of 2.5$\delta r$ in order to find a correct match with 95 per cent certainty" (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 39 of 45 sources have JWST counterparts; six have none and may include false positives  
  > "This leaves six ALMA-detected sources with no optical or near-infrared counterpart" (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources)
8. **derive photometric redshifts and stellar masses (JADES)**: Dual-image SExtractor photometry with TPHOT for lower-resolution bands, then LePhare SED fitting; spectroscopic redshifts fixed where known  
  > "To calculate photometric redshifts and hence stellar masses, we performed SED fitting using LEPHARE (cite: ArnoutsIlbert2011), with a Bruzual-Charlot (cite: BruzualCharlot2003) template set, incorporating a Chabrier (cite: Chabrier2003) initial mass function." (2.4.1 JADES photometric redshifts and stellar masses)
   - software: SExtractor, TPHOT, LePhare
   - parameter: two metallicities (0.2 Zsun, Zsun); exponentially declining star-formation histories with tau 0.1 to 15 Gyr  
  > "We used two metallicities, 0.2 Z$_{\odot}$ and Z$_{\odot}$, and included exponentially declining star-formation histories with $\tau$ ranging between 0.1 and 15 Gyr." (2.4.1 JADES photometric redshifts and stellar masses)
   - assumption: Bruzual-Charlot templates, Chabrier IMF, Calzetti attenuation law  
  > "The template set includes a Calzetti (cite: Calzetti2000) dust-attenuation law" (2.4.1 JADES photometric redshifts and stellar masses)
   - fitting: LePhare template fitting  
  > "we performed SED fitting using LEPHARE (cite: ArnoutsIlbert2011)" (2.4.1 JADES photometric redshifts and stellar masses)
   - validation: Median photometric redshift uncertainty Delta z/(1+z) about 0.06 (0.03 for the ALMA galaxies)  
  > "The median uncertainty in our photometric redshifts for all of our HUDF galaxies is $\Delta z / (1\,{+}\,z)\,{\simeq}\,0.06$." (2.4.1 JADES photometric redshifts and stellar masses)
9. **stack JADES galaxies in the ALMA map**: Mask ALMA-detected sources, subtract the weighted mean, convolve hits maps of each stellar-mass and redshift bin with the ALMA beam, and solve a weighted linear regression for each bin amplitude  
  > "We therefore fit for the amplitude in each bin independently, which reduces to solving a simple weighted linear regression between the ALMA map and maps of ‘hits’ in the JADES catalogue for each bin." (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)
   - software: Python
   - parameter: four stellar mass bins, 7.4 to 11.4 in log10(M*/Msun), width 1 dex  
  > "we define a grid of four stellar mass bins, logarithmically spaced between $\log \left( M_{\ast}/\mathrm{M}_{\odot} \right) \,{=}\,7.4$ and $\log \left( M_{\ast}/\mathrm{M}_{\odot} \right) \,{=}\,11.4$" (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)
   - parameter: four redshift bins between 0 and 8, width 2  
  > "We also define a grid of four redshift bins between 0 and 8, with a width of 2." (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)
   - parameter: source mask of 3 x FWHM diameter  
  > "with a source mask set to be $3\times{\rm FWHM}$ in diameter." (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)
   - assumption: Redshift bins are wide enough that clustering correlations between bins are negligible, so bins are fit independently  
  > "In our case, redshift bins have a width of 2, so these correlations are expected to be negligible." (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)
   - fitting: Weighted linear least squares with inverse-variance weights; 1 sigma errors from 1000 random-position catalogues per bin (Gaussian)  
  > "To estimate the uncertainties in $\hat{S}_{\alpha}$, for each redshift and stellar mass bin we generate 1000 catalogues with the same number of sources in each bin but with random positions" (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)
   - validation: Null stack on 21 JADES stars is consistent with noise; stacking on the dirty map changes flux densities by under 1 per cent  
  > "we also stacked our ALMA map at the positions of 21 sources from the JADES catalogue flagged as stars that happen to fall within our ALMA map" (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions); "we also tried stacking directly on the dirty map, but we found that the resulting flux densities changed by ${<}1$ per cent." (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)

  > "In total, we directly or statistically detect a total CIB intensity of $(8.7\,{\pm}\,0.5)\,{\rm Jy}\,{\rm deg}^{-2}$ in our map" (4.1 Resolved source contribution to the cosmic infrared background)
   - parameter: detected area 1.15e-3 deg^2; stack area 1.11e-3 deg^2  
  > "where the area used in our stack is $1.11\,{\times}\,10^{-3}\,{\rm deg}^2$, slightly less than the full map due to our source mask" (4.1 Resolved source contribution to the cosmic infrared background)
   - assumption: No additional bright (above 0.85 mJy) sources in the map, so the GOODS-ALMA count is added as a constant  
  > "noting that there are no galaxies with $S_{1230}\,{>}\,0.85$ mJy present in our map" (4.1 Resolved source contribution to the cosmic infrared background)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stacking on the full unmasked map gives 8.7 +/- 0.8 Jy/deg^2, consistent with detect-then-stack  
  > "we obtain a CIB estimate of $(8.7\,{\pm}\,0.8)\,{\rm Jy}\,{\rm deg}^{-2}$, consistent with our approach of detecting bright objects then masking them to add the stacking result" (4.1 Resolved source contribution to the cosmic infrared background)
11. **estimate the absolute CIB at the effective map frequency**: Compute the map transmission function from inverse-variance weights of uniform-coverage programmes, derive nu_eff with the FIRAS modified blackbody, then derive the CIB amplitude from the FIRAS fit or a power-law interpolation of Planck CIB at 217 and 353 GHz  
  > "we can calculate the mean transmission function of all of the relevant observations used to produce our final map, weighted by each observation’s inverse variance." (4.2 The absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - parameter: beta = 0.65, Td = 18.5 K fixed; nu_eff = 241.4 GHz; 10,000 Gaussian draws  
  > "To propagate uncertainties, we draw 10,000 Gaussian random numbers from the measured amplitudes and calculate the corresponding CIB value at 241.4 GHz." (4.2 The absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - assumption: Each observation's transmission is flat across its two sidebands; CIB spectral shape fixed  
  > "Lacking the data needed to do the fit ourselves, we simply fix $\beta$ and $T_{\rm d}$ (thus fixing the shape of the CIB spectrum), but take into account the uncertainties in the amplitude throughout our calculations." (4.2 The absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo propagation of amplitude uncertainties; exact power-law solution through two Planck points  
  > "there is an exact solution because there are two measurements and two free parameters" (4.2 The absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - validation: _not stated_
12. **test consistency of the measured CIB with the absolute CIB using SIDES fluctuations**: Take random HUDF-sized patches from a SIDES 1 deg^2 tile to get fractional fluctuations, then compare the measured CIB with Monte Carlo draws of the absolute CIB and fluctuation amplitude  
  > "We next take 100 random patches from the 1 deg$^2$ tile, each with the area of our image of the HUDF (here 4.2 arcmin$^2$)." (4.3 Consistency between resolved sources and the absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - software: SIDES
   - parameter: patches of 4.2 arcmin^2; fluctuation delta I / I = 17 per cent; 10,000 Gaussian draws  
  > "The standard deviation of the SIDES simulation patches is 1.9 Jy deg$^{-2}$, thus fluctuations are $\delta I/I\,{=}\,17\,$per cent." (4.3 Consistency between resolved sources and the absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - assumption: No fluctuations from the S1230 > 0.85 mJy population (treated as a constant) and no contribution from galaxies below the stacking mass limit  
  > "this assumes that there are no additional fluctuations from the population of $S_{1230}\,{>}\,0.85$ mJy galaxies" (4.3 Consistency between resolved sources and the absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo probability that measured CIB is at or below the absolute value given measurement error and fluctuations  
  > "our statistic is the fraction of the area of the resulting posterior distribution that is less than zero" (4.3 Consistency between resolved sources and the absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
   - validation: SIDES stacking range holds 97 +/- 1 per cent of the CIB, and the JADES galaxy count (1856) matches SIDES expectation (1890 +/- 150)  
  > "We find that the average ratio is 97 per cent, with a standard deviation of 1 per cent." (4.3 Consistency between resolved sources and the absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
13. **test uv-plane against image-plane combination**: Convolve the archive MFS images to a common beam, reproject to a common grid, build noise maps, and combine with inverse-variance weights and edge apodization; compare detections with the uv-combined map  
  > "The final step is to combine them, weighting each pixel by its inverse variance." (9.1 Data combination in the image plane)
   - software: photutils create_matching_kernel, reproject reproject_interp
   - parameter: common beam from the largest beam; 0.2 arcsec grid spanning 5 arcmin; Gaussian apodization with sigma equal to beamsize out to 3 x beamsize  
  > "The apodizing function chosen was a Gaussian with a standard deviation of the beamsize, and the apodization was applied out to 3 times the beamsize from the edge of each map." (9.1 Data combination in the image plane)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: uv combination gives 36 peaks above S/N 4 versus 29 in the image-plane map; mean peak S/N higher by about 1.2  
  > "The mean peak S/N of all matching sources is higher by about 1.2." (9.2 Comparison between uv-plane combination and image-plane combination)

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA-selected sources are mostly high stellar mass galaxies at z of about 1 to 3, and 1.23 mm is sensitive primarily to z > 1  
  > "We find that most of our ALMA-selected sources are high-stellar-mass galaxies between $z\,{=}\,1$ and 3" (3.4 Stellar mass-redshift distribution)
- ALMA-detected high-mass galaxies have redder NIRCam colours than similarly massive ALMA-undetected galaxies  
  > "mm-bright ALMA sources tend to have redder NIRCam colours than the galaxies with similarly high stellar masses that we have *not* detected with ALMA." (3.4 Stellar mass-redshift distribution)
- Galaxies below about 10^8 Msun are too faint to be detected even by stacking; the CIB appears to have converged over the probed stellar mass range  
  > "This is consistent with the CIB having essentially converged over the stellar mass range that we have probed." (4.3 Consistency between resolved sources and the absolute value of the cosmic infrared background)
- Stacking implies about 60 undetected galaxies near 10^10 Msun at about 15 uJy and more than 300 near 10^9 Msun at a few uJy  
  > "there are about 60 galaxies with stellar masses around $10^{10}\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$ lying just below our $3.6\,\sigma$ ALMA threshold, which have flux densities around 15 $\umu$Jy" (3.5 Stacking on near-IR-selected positions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Band 6 holds the most extensive ALMA observations of the HUDF, so it gives the deepest archival map  
  > "This band currently contains the most extensive ALMA observations of the HUDF, and so this is where we expect to produce the deepest archival map." (2.1 Obtaining archival ALMA data)
- Why this method: uv-plane combination is optimal; a 250 klambda taper gave the most detected sources without resolving out much flux  
  > "The optimal way to combine interferometric images is to add the observations in the $uv$ plane, then image the entire data set." (9.2 Comparison between uv-plane combination and image-plane combination); "We note that we found similar source flux densities across the different tapers, meaning that at 250 k$\lambda$ we are not resolving out much flux." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
- Alternative: Tapers of 150 klambda and 50 klambda rejected because the larger beams gave fewer detected sources  
  > "Lastly, we tested tapers of 150 k$\lambda$ and 50 k$\lambda$, but we found that the trade-off in sensitivity for the larger synthesized beams led to fewer detected sources." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
- Alternative: Untapered map resolves many sources, making photometry harder  
  > "On the other hand, the map with no $uv$ taper resolves a larger number of sources, making photometry more challenging." (2.3 Data combination in the uv plane)
- Alternative: Image-plane combination tested and found worse; ASPECS pilot data excluded from it because degrading to its large beam raised the rms  
  > "our final combined image therefore does not contain data from the ASPECS pilot, although this amounts to only a small loss of data" (9.1 Data combination in the image plane)
- How they decided it was enough: Detection threshold set at purity 0.7 (S/N 3.6) as a compromise, similar to but slightly more conservative than previous catalogues  
  > "Our choice of threshold is therefore similar to (although slightly more conservative than) what was used in previous studies" (3.1 Cataloguing 1-mm sources)
- Limitation (authors): No flux boosting (deboosting) correction applied to the catalogue flux densities  
  > "While we have not performed any deboosting corrections for the individual flux densities measured in our catalogue" (4.1 Resolved source contribution to the cosmic infrared background)
- Limitation (authors): Absolute stellar masses are uncertain; only relative masses used for binning  
  > "The absolute uncertainties in stellar masses are expected to be large, but the relative uncertainties should be small, so ignore them." (2.4.1 JADES photometric redshifts and stellar masses)
- Limitation (authors): Total background estimate remains uncertain and the HUDF variance is about 20 per cent  
  > "There is still a large uncertainty in the total background estimate" (6 Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Some of the six sources without counterparts may be false positives; negative peaks (14) exceed the Gaussian expectation  
  > "so we cannot rule out the possibility that some of them are false positives." (3.3 Matching mm-selected sources to near-IR-selected sources)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Calibration relied on heterogeneous observatory scripts and CASA versions from different cycles; no flux-scale cross-check between programmes is reported beyond comparison with published catalogues
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Detection threshold from negative peaks gives purity 0.7 but 14 negative peaks above 3.6 versus about 3 expected from Gaussian statistics suggests more false positives than assumed
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CIB comparison depends on an unpublished JADES catalogue (McLeod et al. in prep.) and on the SIDES model for fluctuations; text is inconsistent on 100 versus 1000 SIDES patches and on 22 versus 23 Jy/deg^2 for the Planck value
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No deboosting, and stacking with fixed catalogue positions assumes ALMA flux is unaffected by clustering of undetected galaxies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rest of CIB discussion depends on a mild 2 sigma negative fluctuation; HUDF selection not random
