## Science

- Fields: astrochemistry, star_formation
- Question: Characterize the abundances and emission morphology of nine O-bearing and six N-bearing complex organic molecules toward the hot molecular core G31.41+0.31, compare them with other sources and chemical models, and search for chemical differentiation.  
  > "The aim of this work is to characterize the abundances in G31 and to compare them with the values estimated in other sources. Moreover, we search for a possible chemical segregation between O-bearing and N-bearing species in G31" (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract and baseline-subtract the central spectrum**: Extract spectra from an area equal to the beam size centered on the continuum peak across all basebands, align them, and subtract continuum baseline using STATCONT.  
  > "For all the observed basebands, the spectra have been extracted from an area equal to the size of the beam and centered toward the peak of the continuum." (Observations); "The baseline has been analyzed by (cite: colziguapos) with the software STATCONT (cite: alvaro2018), to statistically determine the value of the continuum level and then subtract it from the spectrum." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, STATCONT
   - parameter: Aperture size = 1.2'' x 1.2'' (beam size)  
  > "the spectra have been extracted from an area equal to the size of the beam and centered toward the peak of the continuum." (Observations)
   - parameter: Continuum level uncertainty = ~11%  
  > "Both from the alignment of the different spectra and from the statistical analysis performed with STATCONT on the final spectrum, the uncertainty on the determination of the continuum level is $\sim11\%$." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Consistency between spectral alignment and STATCONT statistical analysis (~11% continuum uncertainty)  
  > "Both from the alignment of the different spectra and from the statistical analysis performed with STATCONT on the final spectrum, the uncertainty on the determination of the continuum level is $\sim11\%$." (Observations)
2. **Fit molecular transitions under LTE to derive physical parameters and abundances**: Select unblended transitions based on preliminary line identification and fit synthetic LTE spectra using MADCUBA-SLIM AUTOFIT, applying vibrational partition function corrections and isotopic ratios for optically thick species.  
  > "The baseline-subtracted spectrum has been analyzed with the SLIM (Spectral Line Identification and Modeling) tool within the MADCUBA package (cite: martin2019)." (Spectral fitting); "we assumed a single temperature component that fills the beam and used the AUTOFIT tool of MADCUBA-SLIM, which finds the best agreement, minimizing the $\chi^{2}$, between the observed spectra and the predicted LTE model, taking into account also the optical depth." (Spectral fitting)
   - software: MADCUBA, SLIM, AUTOFIT
   - parameter: Reference velocity V_0 = 96.5 km s^-1  
  > "instead of the absolute value of V$_{\rm LSR}$, we give the difference V–V$_0$ using as reference velocity V$_0=96.5\,$km s$^{-1}$ (cite: beltran2018)." (Spectral fitting)
   - parameter: Partition function correction factors: 1.13 for C2H5OH (at 119 K), 1.062 for C2H5CN (at 83 K), 1.24 for C2H513CN (at 126 K)  
  > "$c$) corrected for the partition function factor at 119 K of 1.13;" (Analysis); "$e$) corrected for the partition function factor at 83 K of 1.062; $f$) corrected for the partition function factor at 126 K of 1.24;" (Analysis)
   - parameter: Fixed FWHM for selected species: 7.0 km s^-1 (CH318OH, CH3OCH3, CH3COCH3), 7.5 km s^-1 (CH3CHO), 7.2 km s^-1 (aGg' and gGg'-(CH2OH)2), 9.7 km s^-1 (C2H3CN), 6.0 km s^-1 (C2H513CN); fixed T_ex = 120 K for aGg'-(CH2OH)2  
  > "$a$): quantity kept fixed during the fitting;" (Analysis); "Therefore, to help the fitting algorithm to converge, we have fixed the excitation temperature to the value found for gGg’-(CH$_2$OH)$_2$ and the FWHM to 7.2 $\mathrm{km\,,s^{-1}}$." (Ethylene glycol: gGg’-(CH$_2$OH)$_2$ and aGg’-(CH$_2$OH)$_2$)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), single temperature component filling the 1.2'' beam  
  > "we assumed a single temperature component that fills the beam" (Spectral fitting); "During the fit we have assumed local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), justified by the high density of G31 (rough estimate of $n\sim10^8\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ , (cite: mininni2020))." (Spectral fitting)
   - assumption: N_H2 = (1.0 +/- 0.2) x 10^25 cm^-2, 12C/13C = 37 +/- 12, 16O/18O = 333 +/- 143, 11% error floor on N and T_ex  
  > "The abundances have been calculated using $N_{\rm{H_2}}=(1.0\pm0.2)\times10^{25}\,\rm{cm^{-2}}$ (cite: mininni2020); to calculate the corrected values of column densities and abundances of a species from the analysis of its $^{13}$C or $^{18}$O isotopologue, we have used the ratios $^{12}$C/$^{13}$C = $37\pm12$ ( from (cite: yan2019)) and $^{16}$O/$^{18}$O = $333\pm143$ (from (cite: wilsonrood))" (Analysis); "For all the species we have assumed an error of 11% on the values of $N$ and $T_{\rm{ex}}$, to reflect the uncertainty on the level of continuum." (Spectral fitting)
   - fitting: Chi-squared minimization via MADCUBA-SLIM AUTOFIT  
  > "used the AUTOFIT tool of MADCUBA-SLIM, which finds the best agreement, minimizing the $\chi^{2}$, between the observed spectra and the predicted LTE model, taking into account also the optical depth." (Spectral fitting)
   - validation: Sensitivity tests fixing T_ex (e.g., fixing T_ex = 120 K for CH3CHO and 100 K for CH313CN) and cross-checking isotopologues and vibrationally excited states  
  > "To quantify how the column density would change if the temperature were higher, we have performed a fit using the same transitions and fixing the value of $T_{\rm{ex}}$ to 120 K." (Acetaldehyde: CH$_3$CHO); "Fixing the value of $T_{\rm{ex}}$ to 100 K the column density from the fitting procedure would be $8.1\times10^{15}\,\rm{cm^{-2}}$, consistent inside the errors with the estimate leaving $T_{\rm{ex}}$ as a free parameter." (Methyl cyanide: CH$_3$CN, $^{13}$CH$_3$CN, and CH$_3^{13}$CN)
3. **Create normalized mean emission maps and fit 2D Gaussians to determine peak positions and sizes**: Subtract continuum from cubes with STATCONT, compute moment-0 maps for up to 8 unblended lines per species, normalize each map to its peak, average them with CASA immath, and fit a 2D Gaussian and compute residuals with CASA imfit.  
  > "To create the maps of each line, we first removed the continuum emission from the cubes using the software STATCONT (cite: alvaro2018), and then created the integrated intensity (moment 0) map for each line." (Analysis of the maps); "To obtain a mean map of each species, we normalized each map to its peak intensity and then averaged all the maps of the same species together, using the task *immath* of CASA." (Analysis of the maps); "The mean maps obtained with this procedure are shown in Fig. 1, and have been fitted with a 2D Gaussian using the task *imfit* inside CASA to obtain the position of the peak of emission and the size of the emission." (Analysis of the maps)
   - software: STATCONT, CASA
   - parameter: Up to 8 unblended lines per species covering low and high E_U  
  > "To create the emission maps of the species analyzed in this work we selected for each of them eight of the most unblended lines, or the maximum number possible for those species for which we detect less than eight (mostly) unblended transitions to fit." (Analysis of the maps)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit using CASA task imfit  
  > "have been fitted with a 2D Gaussian using the task *imfit* inside CASA to obtain the position of the peak of emission and the size of the emission." (Analysis of the maps)
   - validation: Inspection of 2D Gaussian residual maps (residuals in core center <= 3% of peak) and comparison with moment-0 maps of individual low-energy transitions in Appendix D  
  > "The task *imfit* produces also the maps of the residuals (i.e. the difference between the maps in input and the best 2D models computed by the task itself)" (Analysis of the maps); "This is due to the fact that the emission is nearly Gaussian, with the residual values contributing at maximum for a factor of 3% with respect to the peak emission." (Positions of molecular species emission peaks)
4. **Compare COM abundances with 27 astronomical sources and gas-grain chemical models**: Compare abundances relative to methanol with 27 literature sources and compare absolute abundances (w.r.t. H2) with the slow, medium, and fast warm-up chemical models of Garrod et al. (2022).  
  > "In Fig. 5 we plot the abundances w.r.t. methanol of the O-bearing, N-bearing, and O- and N-bearing species detected in G31, together with the abundances of the same COMs found in other twenty-seven sources in literature." (Comparison with other sources); "In Fig. 6 we plot the ratio between the peak abundances reached in the three final models (*slow* in the upper panel, *medium* in the middle panel, and *fast* in the lower panel) and the abundance derived in G31 for the COMs presented in this work." (Comparison with chemical models)
   - parameter: 30% assumed uncertainty on chemical model abundances  
  > "We assumed an error of 30% on the values derived from the chemical models." (Comparison with chemical models)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked beam-dilution effects by comparing 1.2'' column densities against 0.2'' high-resolution ALMA data from Beltrán et al. (2018) and Colzi et al. (2021)  
  > "(cite: colziguapos) presented the emission maps of O- and N-bearing species at a higher resolution of 0.2$''$ and confirmed that for these species the emission fills the beam of 1.2$''$ of the data analyzed in this paper. Moreover, the estimate of the column density from the high-resolution data is consistent within a factor 2 with the estimate obtained with our resolution." (Comparison with other sources)

(Spectral fitting); "used the AUTOFIT tool of MADCUBA-SLIM, which finds the best agreement, minimizing the $\chi^{2}$, between the observed spectra and the predicted LTE model" (Spectral fitting)

## Qualitative findings

- The emission of all analyzed COMs is centered toward the continuum peak of the G31 HMC, with CH3CHO showing a South-West contour shift and extended diffuse residual emission.  
  > "From the mean maps of the different species shown in Fig. 1, we can see that the emission of all the COMs analyzed is centered toward the continuum peak of the HMC" (Emission morphology); "with the exception of CH$_3$CHO that shows very diffuse emission in all the envelope around the HMC, with spots over the 10 *rms* level, and a prominent more compact spot to the southern edge of the G31 main core." (Emission morphology)
- There is no clear systematic difference in excitation temperature or FWHM between O-bearing, N-bearing, and O- and N-bearing species in G31.  
  > "From Fig. 4, $T_{\rm{ex}}$ does not show a clear difference between O-bearing species, N-bearing species, and O- and N-bearing species." (Spectral analysis); "The FWHM values vary between 6 to 10 km s$^{-1}$, not showing any particular trend among molecular species." (Spectral analysis)
- Both peak velocity differences (V-V_0) and emission peak positions indicate possible chemical differentiation of CH3CHO and CH2OHCHO with respect to the other O-bearing species.  
  > "Considering together the hints from spectral analysis and from map analysis, there are multiple indications of a possible chemical differentiation for the two O-bearing species CH$_3$CHO and CH$_2$OHCHO, with respect to the other O-bearing species." (Conclusions)
- There is no unique template for COM abundances in hot molecular cores; G31 matches AFGL4176 well, while Galactic Center HMCs have significantly higher COM abundances relative to methanol.  
  > "Overall, it seems that there is not a unique template for the abundances of COMs in HMCs." (Comparison with other sources); "The only source with a remarkable similarity in chemical composition with G31, over the large number of molecules presented in this discussion, is AFGL4176." (Comparison with other sources)
- The COM abundances in G31 agree within a factor of 10 with the Garrod et al. (2022) gas-grain chemical models, best matching an intermediate warm-up timescale between the slow (5x10^4 yr) and medium (2x10^5 yr) models, except for CH3NCO which is underproduced by at least a factor of 500.  
  > "This might indicate that the best match with G31 could be with an intermediate timescale between the *slow* and the *medium* values." (Comparison with chemical models); "The only species that is not well reproduced by any of the models is CH$_3$NCO, which is at least a factor $500$ more abundant in G31." (Comparison with chemical models)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: G31 is an extremely chemically rich HMC outside the Galactic Center with four embedded compact sources, and the unbiased 32 GHz ALMA Band 3 survey (GUAPOS) covers many transitions of O- and N-bearing COMs free from extreme GC environmental effects.  
  > "Thus, G31 is an ideal candidate to investigate the complex chemistry in high-mass star-forming regions outside the Galactic Center (GC), because the extreme conditions in the GC, in terms of interstellar radiation field and cosmic-rays flux can have an impact on the chemistry" (Introduction); "In fact, (cite: beltran2021) highlighted the presence of four sources (labeled as A, B, C, and D) when observing this region at high angular resolution, which makes G31 an ideal laboratory to test whether different sources embedded in the same core show different chemistry." (Conclusions)
- Why this method: Simultaneous LTE fitting of multiple unblended transitions in MADCUBA-SLIM constrains V_LSR better than the velocity resolution, and normalizing moment-0 maps to their peak before averaging prevents the brightest lines from biasing the emission size and peak position.  
  > "The simultaneous fit of multiple transitions for each molecular species allows the determination of V$_{\rm LSR}$ with an error smaller than the resolution in velocity of the spectrum ($\sim1.5\,\rm{km/s}$)." (Spectral fitting); "Normalizing the different maps before averaging allows us to give the same weight to every map, and to obtain a mean map in which the FWHM of the emitting region is not biased by the brightest lines." (Analysis of the maps)
- Alternative: Fitting the ground state transitions of CH3OH (v=0) and CH3CN (v=0) with a single temperature component was rejected because the lines are optically thick (with CH3OH showing inverse P-Cygni profiles) and G31 has a temperature gradient; isotopologues and vibrationally/torsionally excited states were used instead.  
  > "We have not been able to constrain the fit of CH$_3$OH v=0 with a single temperature component. This could be connected both to the presence of a temperature gradient in the source, studied with high angular resolution data by (cite: beltran2018), and to the high abundance of this molecular species that leads to optically thick transitions." (Methanol: CH$_3$OH, $^{13}$CH$_3$OH, and CH$_3^{18}$OH); "For CH$_3$CN v$=0$, it has not been possible to have a good convergence of the fit because the majority of the transitions (K=0, 1, and 2) have high optical depths." (Methyl cyanide: CH$_3$CN, $^{13}$CH$_3$CN, and CH$_3^{13}$CN)
- Alternative: Deriving CH3OH column density from 13CH3OH or CH3OH v_t=1 was rejected in favor of CH318OH because CH318OH is optically thinner, and deriving CH3CN column density from CH313CN was rejected in favor of 13CH3CN because 13CH3CN is optically thinner.  
  > "we can see that CH$_3^{18}$OH is optically thinner than $^{13}$CH$_3$OH, therefore in the discussion we will adopt the value derived from CH$_3^{18}$OH for the column density of CH$_3$OH" (Methanol: CH$_3$OH, $^{13}$CH$_3$OH, and CH$_3^{18}$OH); "we can see that $^{13}$CH$_3$CN is optically thinner than CH$_3^{13}$CN, therefore in the discussion we will adopt the value derived from $^{13}$CH$_3$CN for the column density of CH$_3$CN." (Methyl cyanide: CH$_3$CN, $^{13}$CH$_3$CN, and CH$_3^{13}$CN)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The 1.2'' angular resolution is insufficient to resolve the four embedded compact cores (A, B, C, D) or conclusively establish spatial segregation between molecular species whose peak separations are smaller than the beam.  
  > "The distances between the center of emission of the different species are smaller than the beam (1.2$''$), therefore the spatial resolution of the observations is not sufficient to have conclusive results about the possible presence of spatial segregation for some classes of molecular species, or for selected species." (Positions of molecular species emission peaks)
- Limitation (authors): A single-temperature LTE model cannot fully capture the temperature gradient in G31, causing a small number of transitions (e.g. in CH3COCH3 and CH3OH v=0) to be poorly reproduced.  
  > "A small number of transitions of CH$_3$COCH$_3$ are overestimated by the best-fit model. A better agreement over the whole spectra would require a more accurate description of the temperature gradient of the source G31" (Acetone: CH$_3$COCH$_3$)
- Limitation (authors): For species whose unblended transitions only span low upper-state energies (E_U < 100 K, such as CH3CHO, aGg'-(CH2OH)2, 13CH3CN, CH313CN, C2H3CN, C2H5CN), the fitted excitation temperatures may be underestimated.  
  > "some of the molecular species presented in this work do not have unblended transitions with $E_{\rm{U}}>100\,\rm{K}$, leading to possibly underestimated excitation temperatures." (Spectral analysis)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixing FWHM (and T_ex for aGg'-(CH2OH)2) by visual inspection for several species reduces the formal uncertainties on column density and velocity offset V-V_0.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Velocity differences of ~0.5-1.0 km s^-1 are smaller than the spectral resolution (~1.3-1.7 km s^-1) and could be influenced by optical depth, line blending, or different spatial filtering/weighting across the rotating toroid and outflows rather than purely chemical differentiation among the four sub-cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single H2 column density and a single beam-filling factor of unity are assumed for all molecules despite measured emission sizes varying from ~1.4'' to ~2.0'' and excitation temperatures spanning 54 K to 285 K.
