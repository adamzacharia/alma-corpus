## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure central clump properties from ATLASGAL 870 um maps**: Extract integrated 870 um flux within a uniform circular aperture of radius 0.25 pc centered on the enhanced 870 um emission, and compute clump mass and mass surface density using dust temperatures from literature.  
  > "From a careful scrutiny of the 870 $\mu$m maps of the entire sample, an optimum aperture with radius 0.25 pc is found suitable to encompass most of the 870 $\mu$m emission (see Fig. [\[fig:overview_hfs_app\]](#fig:overview_hfs_app)). Hence, we consider this as the radius of the clump, $R_{\rm clump}$." (Clumps in the HFSs); "With the above parameters, the clump mass ($M_{\rm clump}$) was computed following Eqs. B1–B2 of (cite: Liu21)." (Clumps in the HFSs); "The mass surface density ($\Sigma_{\rm clump}$) of the clumps was derived from $\Sigma_{\rm clump} = M_{\rm clump}/(\pi R_{\rm clump}^2)$." (Clumps in the HFSs)
   - parameter: R_clump = 0.25 pc  
  > "an optimum aperture with radius 0.25 pc is found suitable to encompass most of the 870 $\mu$m emission (see Fig. [\[fig:overview_hfs_app\]](#fig:overview_hfs_app)). Hence, we consider this as the radius of the clump, $R_{\rm clump}$." (Clumps in the HFSs)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust mass ratio R_gd = 100, dust opacity k_870um = 1.78 cm^2 g^-1  
  > "In the computation, we assumed the gas-to-dust mass ratio to be $R_{\rm gd}=100$, and the dust opacity per gram of dust to be $k_{\rm 870 \mu m}=1.78$ cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$, which corresponds to the opacity of dust grains with thin ice mantles at gas densities of $10^{6}$ $\ensuremath{\mbox{~cm}}^{-3}$ (cite: Oss94)." (Clumps in the HFSs)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Uncertainty estimated at ~50% from combined uncertainties in dust opacity (~30%), gas-to-dust ratio (~20%), dust temperature (~20%), and kinematic distance (~10%).  
  > "According to (cite: San17; San19), the uncertainty of both $M_{\rm clump}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm clump}$ could be about $50\%$, which accounts for the combined uncertainties from $k_{\rm 870 \mu m}$ ($\sim 30\%$), $R_{gd}$ ($\sim 20\%$), $T_{\rm d}$ ($\sim 20\%$), and the kinematic distance ($\sim10\%$)." (Clumps in the HFSs)
2. **Subtract free-free emission from 3 mm continuum for cores with compact HII regions**: Estimate free-free continuum intensity from integrated H40alpha recombination line intensity and subtract it from the 3 mm continuum image for the five cores associated with compact HII regions.  
  > "To estimate the contribution of free-free emission, we used the H40$\alpha$ hydrogen recombination line observations under the assumption of local thermodynamical equilibrium and optically thin emission." (Derived parameters of cores); "The above estimated free-free emission intensity was subsequently subtracted from the 3 mm continuum image to yield the dust continuum emission image." (Derived parameters of cores)
   - parameter: nu = 99.0 GHz (H40alpha rest frequency)  
  > "$\nu = 99.0$ GHz is the rest frequency of H40$\alpha$ observed in the ATOMS data;" (Derived parameters of cores)
   - assumption: Local thermodynamical equilibrium, optically thin emission, electron temperature T_e = 6000 K, helium-to-hydrogen abundance N_He / N_H = 0.08  
  > "To estimate the contribution of free-free emission, we used the H40$\alpha$ hydrogen recombination line observations under the assumption of local thermodynamical equilibrium and optically thin emission." (Derived parameters of cores); "and we assume the electron temperature of $T_e = 6000$ K as well as a relative abundance of helium to hydrogen of $N_\mathrm{He}/N_\mathrm{H} = 0.08$." (Derived parameters of cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Assume an upper limit of T_e = 6000 K to avoid over-subtraction of free-free continuum for small-scale extracted cores.  
  > "We assume an upper limit of $T_e = 6000$ K to avoid over subtraction of free-free continuum for the small scale extracted cores (cite: Liu15)." (Derived parameters of cores)
3. **Extract compact cores in the 9 IR-bright HFSs and adopt IR-dark cores from literature**: Run the Dendrogram algorithm on the ATOMS 3 mm combined 12m+7m continuum data to identify leaf structures as cores, and adopt the 224 cores in the 8 IR-dark HFSs from Sanhueza et al. (2019).  
  > "A total of 224 compact cores in the 8 IR-dark HFSs have already been identified from ASHES 1.3 mm continuum by (cite: San19)." (Core identification); "Examining the performance of both schemes (especially for the low-mass cores in the IR-dark HFSs) and to maintain uniformity, we use *Dendrogram* alone to extract cores in the 9 IR-bright HFSs from ATOMS 3 mm, combined 12m+7m continuum data." (Core identification)
   - software: astrodendro
   - parameter: intensity threshold = 2.5 sigma, step = 1.0 sigma, minimum pixels = 1 synthesized beam, integrated flux cut >= 4 sigma  
  > "Following (cite: San19), an intensity threshold of 2.5 $\sigma$, a step of 1.0 $\sigma$ (the rms noise of the continuum data, see Sect. [2.2](#sec:almadata)), and a minimum number of pixels equal to those contained in each synthesized beam (half of the beam considered in (cite: San19)) were used as input parameters." (Core identification); "Finally, cores with integrated flux densities less than 4 $\sigma$ were excluded to avoid spurious identification, where 4 $\sigma$ was determined from the corresponding negative level (i.e., $-4$ $\sigma$) within which the interferometric sidelobe effects cannot be ruled out." (Core identification)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: 4 sigma flux threshold determined from negative (-4 sigma) sidelobe level.  
  > "Finally, cores with integrated flux densities less than 4 $\sigma$ were excluded to avoid spurious identification, where 4 $\sigma$ was determined from the corresponding negative level (i.e., $-4$ $\sigma$) within which the interferometric sidelobe effects cannot be ruled out." (Core identification)
4. **Classify star-forming nature of cores and derive core masses and surface densities**: Cross-match cores with outflow, hot core, compact HII region, and 8/24 um point source associations, assign temperatures based on classification, and compute M_core and Sigma_core.  
  > "In total, 112 out of 310 cores are protostellar ones associated with one or more star-forming signatures (see above). The remaining 198 cores without any of these signatures are treated here as prestellar candidates." (Star-forming nature of cores); "The mass ($M_{\rm core}$) and mass surface density ($\Sigma_{\rm core}$) parameters of the cores were calculated following the same approach outlined in Sect. [3.1](#sec:clumps), where the dust opacities per gram of dust were adjusted to be 0.9 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ for 1 mm and 0.2 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ for 3 mm according to (cite: Oss94)." (Derived parameters of cores)
   - assumption: T_dust = natal clump temperature for prestellar candidates, 50 K for outflow-associated cores, 100 K for hot core or compact HII region cores; dust opacity = 0.9 cm^2 g^-1 (1 mm) and 0.2 cm^2 g^-1 (3 mm); R_gd = 100.  
  > "Cores without detectable star-forming signatures are roughly assigned the temperature of their natal clumps. For cores with associated star-forming signatures, the temperature was assumed to be 50 K for those associated with outflows (cite: San19), and 100 K for those associated either with hot cores or compact HII regions (cite: Liu21)." (Derived parameters of cores); "where the dust opacities per gram of dust were adjusted to be 0.9 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ for 1 mm and 0.2 cm$^{2}$ g$^{-1}$ for 3 mm according to (cite: Oss94)." (Derived parameters of cores)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared core masses derived from ATOMS 12m data alone (MRS ~18'') with 12m+7m combined data (MRS ~60''), finding a factor of ~1.3 difference on average.  
  > "In Fig. [3](#fig:core_mass_msig2), we compare the masses estimated from the ATOMS 12m continuum data alone with a MRS of $\sim 18$ $''$ (similar to the MRS of the ASHES data) with that derived from the 12m+7m combined data having a MRS of $\sim 60$$''$. From this comparison, we estimate a nominal factor of $\sim$1.3 on average higher mass estimates for the cores extracted in the IR-bright HFSs using ATOMS 12m+7m data." (Derived parameters of cores)
5. **Identify, filter, classify, and estimate luminosities of candidate YSOs**: Search Spitzer/MIPSGAL 24 um catalogue and perform manual aperture photometry for 10 uncatalogued bright sources, filter foreground/background stars using Gaia EDR3 parallaxes, cross-match with the SPICY IRAC YSO catalogue, and estimate L_bol from 24 um fluxes.  
  > "From the 24 $\mu$m point source catalogue of the Galactic plane from Spitzer/MIPSGAL (cite: Gut15), we searched for point sources associated with the HFS sample studied here, and obtained their 24 $\mu$m photometric fluxes." (YSOs in the HFSs); "To alleviate this, we correlate the identified sources with Gaia EDR3 data within a radius of $2$$''$. The parallax distances of the Gaia-matched sources were subsequently calculated." (YSOs in the HFSs); "Furthermore, we classify the identified YSOs cross-matching (within $2$$''$ radius) using the SPICY catalogue that compiles $\sim$ 120,000 Spitzer/IRAC candidate YSOs for the Inner Galactic Midplane (cite: Kuh21)." (YSOs in the HFSs)
   - parameter: Search area 1.5'-4.8'; Gaia EDR3 matching radius = 2''; parallax distance filter > 10% difference from HFS distance; SPICY matching radius = 2''  
  > "Here, we have used different search areas depending on the spatial extent of the HFSs in the 8 $\mu$m image (i.e., image size in Fig. [\[fig:overview_hfs\]](#fig:overview_hfs), 1.5$'$–4.8$'$)." ## Qualitative findings

- Central massive clumps and their embedded most massive cores show increasing mass and mass surface density from the IR-dark to the IR-bright evolutionary stage.  
  > "The central massive clumps and their associated most massive cores are observed to follow a trend of increasing mass ($M$) and mass surface density ($\Sigma$) with evolution from IR-dark to IR-bright stage." (Abstract)
- Massive dense cores transition from a wider hierarchical sub-clustering distribution in IR-dark HFSs (peaking at ~0.16 pc) to a centrally peaked clustering distribution in IR-bright HFSs (peaking at ~0.04 pc).  
  > "For IR-dark HFSs, the cores peak within $\sim 0.16$ pc of the centre displaying a hierarchical sub-clustering mode. This transforms to a centrally-peaked clustering mode in IR-bright HFSs where the cores peak within $\sim 0.04$ pc of the centre." (Summary and conclusions)
- YSOs exhibit a luminosity/mass-segregated cluster distribution with high-luminosity YSOs preferentially located in the central hub-clump region and low-luminosity YSOs distributed over several parsecs.  
  > "From the spatial distributions of YSOs in the HFSs, we find the picture of a mass-segregated cluster of YSOs in which high-luminosity YSOs typical of high-mass protostars are preferentially located in the central hub-clump region, and surrounded by a population of low-luminosity YSOs typical of low-mass protostars in the entire HFS cloud extending to several parsecs." (Summary and conclusions)
- Outflows in HFSs do not preferentially align parallel or perpendicular to hub-composing filaments, instead escaping toward low-density inter-filamentary cavities and having limited disruptive effect on ongoing star formation.  
  > "Keeping the above caveats in mind, our results show that the observed outflows (1) do not preferentially align parallel or perpendicular to the filaments and (2) tend to be oriented toward the voids of the hub-composing filaments." (Effects of outflow feedback on star formation)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 8 IR-dark HFSs from ASHES and 9 IR-bright HFSs from ATOMS with clear HFS morphology in 8 um emission to probe two distinct evolutionary stages at matched linear FoVs (~1.0-1.2 pc) and spatial resolutions (~0.02-0.03 pc).  
  > "The sample was selected particularly to contain two different infrared (IR) characteristics (i.e., 8 IR-dark and 9 IR-bright objects) since these two IR types can represent two different evolutionary stages (see Sect. [2.1](#sec:sample))." (Introduction); "Given their typical distances (see Sect. [2.1](#sec:sample)), the IR-dark and bright HFSs selected from the respective ASHES, and ATOMS surveys have very close linear-scale FoVs (i.e., $\sim 1.0$ pc and $\sim 1.2$ pc, respectively)." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: Used a uniform 0.25 pc circular aperture for clumps to avoid low-contrast issues with automated algorithms in IR-dark clouds, and used Dendrogram alone on combined 12m+7m data for cores to maintain uniformity across both IR-dark and IR-bright samples.  
  > "To avoid inaccurate clump identification for these low-contrast cases and to maintain uniformity, we define a circular aperture to enclose the enhanced 870 $\mu$m emission in the central area of both IR-dark and bright HFSs analysed here." (Clumps in the HFSs); "Examining the performance of both schemes (especially for the low-mass cores in the IR-dark HFSs) and to maintain uniformity, we use *Dendrogram* alone to extract cores in the 9 IR-bright HFSs from ATOMS 3 mm, combined 12m+7m continuum data." (Core identification)
- Alternative: Using Dendrogram or CASA-imfit to identify central clumps on 870 um maps; rejected because low intensity contrast in IR-dark HFSs yields large aspect ratios (>3) encompassing multiple entities.  
  > "Generally, such density structures can be identified using several widely-used algorithms, such as *Dendrogram* and *CASA-imfit*. However, the use of these algorithms is limited by the intensity contrast of the clumps with respect to their natal clouds especially for the IR-dark HFSs studied here where the contrast is low (see Fig. [1](#fig:overview_hfs))." (Clumps in the HFSs)
- Alternative: Two-step core identification using Dendrogram followed by CASA-imfit as in Liu et al. (2021); rejected in favor of Dendrogram alone to maintain uniformity with Sanhueza et al. (2019).  
  > "While the former study used the *Dendrogram* algorithm, the later used a two-step process in which the initial identification was carried out using *Dendrogram* then followed by CASA–*imfit* to estimate the parameters. Examining the performance of both schemes (especially for the low-mass cores in the IR-dark HFSs) and to maintain uniformity, we use *Dendrogram* alone to extract cores in the 9 IR-bright HFSs from ATOMS 3 mm, combined 12m+7m continuum data." (Core identification)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Different angular resolutions, maximum recoverable scales (19'' vs 60''), and mass sensitivities between ASHES and ATOMS introduce observational biases into core mass and surface density distributions.  
  > "Note that the different angular resolutions and maximum recoverable scales may lead to some observation biases to the properties of cores (e.g., mass), which will be discussed in Sect. [3.2.3](#sec:core_params_derived)." (ALMA data); "Instead, it could be in part a result of the observation bias caused by the different maximum recoverable scales (MRS) in the ASHES ($\sim 20$$''$) and ATOMS ($\sim60$$''$) combined 12m+7m data." (Derived parameters of cores)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of total power observations in the ASHES and ATOMS surveys may limit the recovered spatial extent of molecular outflows.  
  > "However, this result may be an observational consequence due to the lack of total power in the analysis made in the ASHES and ATOMS surveys." (Effects of outflow feedback on star formation)
- Limitation (authors): Outflow orientation analysis is subject to incomplete outflow identification due to overlapping outflows, 2D projection effects, and small sample size (17 HFSs).  
  > "Firstly, the outflow identifications in the two studies used here are probably incomplete due to the presence of multiple overlapping outflows which tend to occur in high-mass star formation regions. Secondly, projection effects would also influence the observed orientations. Last but not the least, one needs to study more number of HFSs-outflow systems to enable a more robust statistical investigation." (Effects of outflow feedback on star formation)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Comparison between IR-dark and IR-bright cores is heterogeneous: ASHES uses 1.3 mm mosaic data (MRS 19'', beam 1.2'', mass sensitivity 0.04 M_sun) whereas ATOMS uses 3 mm single-pointing data (MRS 60'', beam 2.0'', mass sensitivity 1.2 M_sun).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core dust temperatures are assigned discrete assumed values (clump T_dust, 50 K, or 100 K) rather than measured per core, which directly drives the factor of ~24 core mass increase claim.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The IR-dark HFSs are systematically ~1.4 times more distant on average (3.9 kpc vs 2.7 kpc) than the IR-bright HFSs.
