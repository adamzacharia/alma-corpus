## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate 12m, 7m, and TP data and generate the combined TP data cube**: Run observatory CASA pipeline on 12m and 7m EBs; calibrate TP spectra in CASA, grid onto 5.62'' pixels with a 6-pixel prolate spheroidal function, solve for relative flux scales across 120 EBs via design matrix inversion, subtract linear baselines from cubes, combine RA and DEC scans with the Emerson & Graeve (1988) method, and convert to Jy/beam.  
  > "We generated separate data cubes for the 120 EBs, calculated the flux ratios and errors of all of their pairings, and solved for relative flux scales by inverting the design matrix." (Total Power Array); "Spectral baselines were subtracted with a straight line fit in these cubes." (Total Power Array); "The RA and DEC data cubes were combined with the (cite: Emerson:1988hz) method." (Total Power Array)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: TP pixel size = 5.62'', prolate spheroidal function size = 6 pixels, effective FWHM = 56.6''  
  > "The calibrated spectra were re-sampled on a grid with pixel size of $5.62\ensuremath{''}$ using the prolate spheroidal function with a size of 6 pixels (cite: Schwab:1980aa; Schwab:1984)." (Total Power Array); "The effective full-width half maximum (FWHM) beam size after this regridding – hence smoothing – is $56.6\ensuremath{''}$." (Total Power Array)
   - parameter: Conversion factor C_Jy/K = 40.7 Jy/K (eta_mb = 0.856)  
  > "The antenna temperature $T_{a}^*$ was converted to the Jy/beam unit by multiplying the coefficient, $C_{Jy/K} = 40.7$ Jy/K, measured by the observatory." (Total Power Array)
   - fitting: Straight-line spectral baseline fit; design matrix inversion for relative EB flux scales  
  > "Spectral baselines were subtracted with a straight line fit in these cubes." (Total Power Array)
   - validation: Checked scatter of 120 EB scaling coefficients (1.3%) and reduction of scan noise power by a factor of ~2 in the combined RA+DEC cube  
  > "The derived 120 scaling coefficients, whose median was set to 1, have a small scatter of only 1.3%." (Total Power Array); "The combination of the RA and DEC scans reduce the noise power by a factor of about 2." (Total Power Array)
2. **Convert TP cube to visibilities and jointly deconvolve 12m+7m+TP data cubes**: Deconvolve the TP cube with a Gaussian+prolate spheroidal beam and convert to visibilities using TP2VIS, scale 7m and TP visibility weights by 5 and 20, jointly image the 435-pointing mosaic in MIRIAD (INVERT and MOSSDI2) using two-step Steer CLEAN, apply Jorsater & van Moorsel (1995) residual scaling, and convert to Rayleigh-Jeans brightness temperature.  
  > "The TP data cube was converted to the form of interferometric data (visibilities) using the Total Power to Visibilities package (cite: Koda:2019aa)." (Total Power Cube to Visibilities); "The 12m, 7m, and TP array data were jointly-deconvolved with the Steer CLEAN algorithm (the INVERT and MOSSDI2 tasks) in the Multichannel Image Reconstruction, Image Analysis, and Display (MIRIAD) software package" (Imaging); "In effect, the residual map from step (2), hence the 1.5% of the total flux, was scaled (multiplied) by a factor based on the ratio of beam areas (0.96, Table 4) and added to the clean component map." (Imaging)
   - software: TP2VIS, MIRIAD
   - parameter: 7m weight scale = 5, TP weight scale = 20, Briggs robust = -0.2, cell size = 0.25''  
  > "Thus, the weights of the 7m and TP visibilities are scaled up by factors of 5 and 20, respectively." (Total Power Cube to Visibilities); "We measured the sensitivity and beam size at various robust values and adopted ${\rm robust}=-0.2$ as the optimal value for this study." (Imaging); "The cell size is set to $0.25\ensuremath{''}$." (Imaging)
   - parameter: Channel widths = 5, 2, and 1 km/s; conversion T_b [K] = 25.4 S_nu [Jy]  
  > "We made three data cubes with three channel widths: 5, 2, and 1$\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$." (Imaging); "In our observations we have $\lambda=2.60\rm\, mm$, $b_{\rm maj}=2.12\ensuremath{''}$, and $b_{\rm min}=1.71\ensuremath{''}$, and hence, $T_{\rm b}=25.4S_{\nu}$." (Conversion from Jy/beam to K)
   - assumption: Single average T_sys per EB  
  > "For simplicity, we adopted a single average $T_{\rm sys}$ value for each EB, as it does not vary much within an EB in ALMA Band 3 observations." (Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared total integrated flux of the 12m+7m+TP cube (1.68e4 Jy km/s) against the TP cube (1.67e4 Jy km/s, 0.6% error) and checked PSF radial profile of an isolated compact source for sidelobes down to ~1/1000  
  > "The total integrated flux in the cube is $S_{\nu} dv =1.68\times 10^4 \, {\rm Jy \cdot km \, s^{-1}}$, which is consistent with that of the TP data cube with a 0.6% error (Section 3.2)." (Imaging); "The zoom-in (bottom plot) shows that there are no large-scale, systematic sidelobes left in the CLEANed image (panel b) down to $\sim 1/1000$." (Maps)
3. **Generate 3D signal masks and 2D moment maps**: Create two 3D mask cubes (at native resolution and smoothed to 5'') from regions >2 sigma containing at least one >4 sigma peak, expand spatially by one PSF major-axis diameter, combine masks, and compute integrated intensity (mom0), peak temperature (unmasked), velocity field (mom1), and velocity dispersion (mom2, using the 1 km/s cube).  
  > "The first mask is defined with the regions above $>2\sigma$ including at least one $>4\sigma$ peak in 3-D." (Maps); "The second mask is made in the same way, but with the data cube smoothed to a $5\ensuremath{''}$ resolution to recover diffuse, extended emission around more significant emission." (Maps); "We use the cube with the $5\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ channel width for generating these maps except for the $\sigma_v$ map, where we use the $1\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ cube so that dispersions narrower than $5\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ can be measured." (Maps)
   - parameter: Mask thresholds: >2 sigma envelope with >=1 peak >4 sigma, expanded by 1 PSF major-axis diameter; smoothed cube resolution = 5''  
  > "The first mask is defined with the regions above $>2\sigma$ including at least one $>4\sigma$ peak in 3-D. The regions are expanded spatially outward from their edge by one PSF major-axis diameter to include the envelopes of the emission." (Maps)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit global disk kinematic and geometric parameters**: Fit a rotating disk model to the data cube in 10'' radial bins using 3D-Barolo in three sequential steps: (1) fit V_sys over r = 20-280'' with center fixed to the K-band symmetry center; (2) fit PA and inclination over r = 100-250''; (3) fit V_rot over r >= 100''.  
  > "We apply the 3D-Barolo (BBarolo) tool (cite: Di-Teodoro:2015aa) to the data cube and derive the kinematic and geometric parameters of the rotating disk" (Kinematic Parameters); "First, we fix the center position to the symmetry center of $K$-band isophotes (cite: Thatte:2000aa; Diaz:2006aa) and obtain $V_{\rm sys} = 511 \pm 3\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ for the radial range of $r = 20$–$280\ensuremath{''}$." (Kinematic Parameters); "Second, we fix ($\alpha_{\rm J2000}$, $\delta_{\rm J2000}$) and $V_{\rm sys}$, and derive PA $= 225 \pm 1 \ensuremath{^\circ}$ and $i = 26 \pm 2 \ensuremath{^\circ}$ for the radial range of $r = 100$–$250\ensuremath{''}$ where the rotation curve is almost flat." (Kinematic Parameters)
   - software: BBarolo
   - parameter: Radial bin = 10''; radial ranges: r = 20-280'' for V_sys, r = 100-250'' for PA and i, r >= 100'' for V_rot  
  > "Since we need only the global disk parameters, we adopt a $10\ensuremath{''}$ radial bin to speed up the fitting." (Kinematic Parameters); "The average value in $r\gtrsim 100\ensuremath{''}$ is $V_{\rm rot} = 174 \pm 10\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$, or $\Delta V=348\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ in the full width." (Kinematic Parameters)
   - assumption: Center fixed to K-band isophote symmetry center (13h37m0.57s, -29d51m56.9s); bar pattern speed Omega_b = 57.4 km/s/kpc from Hirota et al. (2014)  
  > "First, we fix the center position to the symmetry center of $K$-band isophotes (cite: Thatte:2000aa; Diaz:2006aa)" (Kinematic Parameters); "We adopt the pattern speed of the bar $\Omega_{\rm b}=57.4\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}\, {\rm kpc}^{-1}$ from (cite: Hirota:2014wd)" (Kinematic Parameters)
   - fitting: Three-step 3D tilted-ring fitting in 3D-Barolo  
  > "For stability in the fitting, we derive the parameters in three steps." (Kinematic Parameters)
   - validation: Compared derived parameters with previous CO study by Crosthwaite et al. (2002)  
  > "These parameters are consistent with the ones derived in the previous CO study (cite: Crosthwaite:2002yu)." (Kinematic Parameters)
5. **Derive molecular gas masses, surface densities, radial profiles, and regional PDFs**: Convert CO fluxes and integrated intensities to H2 and total molecular gas masses and surface densities, compute azimuthally averaged radial profiles in 10'' bins corrected for inclination and PA, and construct PDFs of sigma_v, T_b, I_CO, and T_peak in four radial zones plus masses of filamentary structures.  
  > "This flux is translated to the total H$_2$ gas mass of $M_{\rm H_2}=2.6\times 10^9 ~{\rm M_{\odot}}$ and total molecular gas mass of $M_{\rm gas}=3.6\times 10^{9}~{\rm M_{\odot}}$" (Molecular Gas Mass and Surface Density); "The azimuthal average is calculated in each $10\ensuremath{''}$ ($218\, {\rm pc}$) bin, including the corrections for the inclination and position angle of the disk." (Molecular Gas Mass and Surface Density); "Figure 12 shows the probability distribution functions (PDFs) of (b) velocity dispersion $\sigma_v$, (c) brightness temperature in the cube $T_{\rm b}$, (d) integrated intensity $I_{\rm CO}$ (=$\int T_{\rm b}dv$), and (e) peak brightness temperature $T_{\rm peak}$ (=$\max T_{\rm b}$), in these regions." (Molecular Gas Distribution)
   - parameter: Radial zones: central (r < 20''), bar (20-80''), inner disk (80-160''), outer disk (160-300''); radial profile bin = 10'' (218 pc)  
  > "In the following subsections, we will discuss the distribution and properties of molecular gas, by separating the disk into four regions according to their radii in the disk plane ($i=26\ensuremath{^\circ}$): the central region ($r<20\ensuremath{''}$), the bar region (20-80$\ensuremath{''}$), the inner disk (80-160$\ensuremath{''}$), and the outer disk (160-300$\ensuremath{''}$)." (Molecular Gas Distribution)
   - assumption: Constant CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO = 2.0e20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1, helium/heavy element factor = 1.36, distance D = 4.5 Mpc, inclination i = 26 deg, thermalized optically-thick CO(1-0) emission with T_CMB = 2.725 K  
  > "For this paper, we temporarily adopt the consensus value of $X_{\rm CO}=2.0\times 10^{20} \,\rm cm^{-2}\cdot [K\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}]^{-1}$ (cite: Bolatto:2013ys) for simplicity" (Molecular Gas Mass and Surface Density); "$M_{\rm gas}=1.36M_{\rm H_2}$." (Molecular Gas Mass and Surface Density); "Under an assumption of thermalized, optically-thick CO(1-0) emission, $T_{\rm b}$, $T_{\rm peak}$, and $I_{\rm CO}$ are related to the kinetic temperature $T_{\rm kin}$" (Molecular Clouds in the MW Disk as Reference)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared derived total H2 mass with previous single-dish measurements by Crosthwaite et al. (2002) and Lundgren et al. (2004)  
  > "The $M_{\rm H_2}$ estimated here is consistent with the previous measurements: practically the same as $2.5\times 10^9 ~{\rm M_{\odot}}$ by (cite: Crosthwaite:2002yu) and 26% lower than $3.4\times 10^9 ~{\rm M_{\odot}}$ by (cite: Lundgren:2004aa)." (Molecular Gas Mass and Surface Density)
6. **Estimate dynamical and gas assembly timescales for filamentary structures**: Calculate disk and bar pattern rotation timescales and compare them to the 1D converging-flow assembly timescale t_form = (l_f / Delta_v) (Sigma_f / Sigma_i - 1) for interarm and spiral-arm filamentary structures.  
  > "We consider a case that a long and narrow structure is formed by a converging gas flow compressing a sheet of ambient gas in one direction (like forming a wrinkle on a sheet)." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures); "$t_{\rm form} = \frac{l_{\rm i}-l_{\rm f}}{\Delta  v}=\frac{l_{\rm f}}{\Delta v} \left( \frac{\Sigma_{\rm f}}{\Sigma_{\rm i}}-1 \right) \approx \frac{l_{\rm f}}{\Delta  v} \frac{\Sigma_{\rm f}}{\Sigma_{\rm i}}$." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures)
   - parameter: Filament width l_f = 100 pc, surface density contrast Sigma_f / Sigma_i = 10, interarm convergence velocity Delta_v = 10 km/s, spiral arm Delta_v >= 30 km/s  
  > "In order to build up the mass of a molecular structure of width, $l_{\rm f}\sim 100\, {\rm pc}$, and contrast, $\Sigma_{\rm f}/\Sigma_{\rm i} \sim 10$" (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures); "Here, we simply adopt $\Delta v=10\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ as a fiducial value in the interarm regions, surmised from a measurement in M51, a galaxy with more prominent spiral arms." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures); "The formation timescale is shorter in the spiral arms: $t_{\rm form}\lesssim 30\,{\rm Myr}$ for $\Delta v \gtrsim 30\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures)
   - assumption: 1D coherent converging flow at constant velocity width Delta_v conserving mass  
  > "We adopt a simplistic assumption of a coherent converging flow at a constant velocity width of $\Delta v$ (i.e., *all* gas over an area of width $l_{\rm i}$ is moving in the same directions toward a narrow structure)." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Software

- **CASA** 5.1 (also discusses TCLEAN in version 6.4): Calibration of 12m, 7m, and TP array data  
  > "We used the Common Astronomy Software Application (cite: McMullin:2007aa; Bean:2022aa) version 5.1 for calibration (this section)" (Data Reduction); "MIRIAD works better than CASA primarily because CASA’s TCLEAN task, as of version 6.4, does not account for spatial variations of PSF across the mosaic" (MIRIAD Over CASA for Mosaic Imaging)

  > "The 12m, 7m, and TP array data were jointly-deconvolved with the Steer CLEAN algorithm (the INVERT and MOSSDI2 tasks) in the Multichannel Image Reconstruction, Image Analysis, and Display (MIRIAD) software package (cite: Sault:1995kl; Sault:1996uq)." (Imaging)
- **TP2VIS** : Converting the Total Power data cube into interferometric visibilities  
  > "The TP data cube was converted to the form of interferometric data (visibilities) using the Total Power to Visibilities package (cite: Koda:2019aa)." ## Qualitative findings

- Brightness temperature, velocity dispersion, and integrated intensity (surface density) of molecular gas decrease radially outward from the galactic center to the outer disk, and are higher in spiral arms than in interarm regions.  
  > "The full disk coverage shows that the characteristics of molecular gas change radially from the center to outer disk, with the locally measured brightness temperature, velocity dispersion, and integrated intensity (surface density) decreasing outward." (Abstract); "They are higher in the spiral arms and lower in the interarm regions." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Molecular spiral arms transition from coherent, narrow ridges in the inner disk to broader, less spatially coherent, flocculent structures in the outer disk, consistent with a transition from density-wave to swing-amplification behavior.  
  > "However, while the arms are still present in the outer disk, they appear less spatially coherent, and even flocculent." (Abstract); "The overall distribution of the molecular structures appears to be consistent with the gradual radial transition from the spiral density-wave picture in the inner disk to the swing amplification picture in the outer disk." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Massive (~10^7 M_sun), kpc-long filamentary molecular structures are abundant in interarm regions and generally show little or no H-alpha star formation.  
  > "Massive filamentary gas concentrations are abundant even in the interarm regions." (Abstract); "These interarm structures host little or no star formation traced by H$\alpha$." (Abstract)
- Extended, low-brightness CO emission surrounds the bar and spiral arms and likely arises from an ensemble of small, spatially unresolved molecular clouds rather than diffuse gas outside clouds.  
  > "The new map also shows extended CO emission, which likely represents an ensemble of unresolved molecular clouds." (Abstract); "As discussed in Section 9.1, it is unlikely that the majority of this CO emission comes from diffuse molecular gas outside molecular clouds. Instead, it is likely from small, unresolved clouds." (Extended CO Emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Fundamental CO(1-0) observations across the whole disk at 40 pc resolution and 10^4 M_sun (3 sigma) sensitivity are required to avoid the warm/dense excitation bias of CO(2-1), resolve typical Milky Way-sized clouds (D ~ 40 pc), and detect the lower-mass (<10^5 M_sun) cloud population.  
  > "A cloud-scale resolution of $40\, {\rm pc}$, the typical diameter of Galactic molecular clouds (cite: Scoville:1987vo), is necessary for mapping the full cloud population and resolving cloud properties" (Introduction); "We therefore need CO(1-0) observations to take a full census of molecular gas in galactic disks." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used identical 435-pointing positions for 12m and 7m arrays and full-disk OTF TP scans to enable consistency checks and minimize scan errors; converted TP to visibilities with TP2VIS and jointly deconvolved in MIRIAD because CASA's TCLEAN and SDINTIMAGING do not account for spatially varying PSFs across a large mosaic.  
  > "In this way, each pointing position could be imaged with the 12m and 7m array data separately and together, which is useful for consistency checks." (12m and 7m Arrays); "MIRIAD works better than CASA primarily because CASA’s TCLEAN task, as of version 6.4, does not account for spatial variations of PSF across the mosaic, even though the *uv* coverage varies among the mosaic pointing positions." (MIRIAD Over CASA for Mosaic Imaging)
- Alternative: CASA TCLEAN and SDINTIMAGING for mosaic imaging (rejected because they do not account for spatial PSF variations across large mosaics and caused runaway divergences at mosaic edges)  
  > "Because of this, we often ran into runaway divergences in TCLEAN, most likely, but not always, at the edges of the mosaic which is covered either by the 12m or 7m array primary beam, but not both." (MIRIAD Over CASA for Mosaic Imaging); "Even the new task SDINTIMAGING (cite: Rau:2019aa) does not account for the spatially-variant PSF." (MIRIAD Over CASA for Mosaic Imaging)
- Alternative: High-order polynomial baseline subtraction on individual TP spectra in the CASA pipeline (rejected in favor of straight-line baseline fits in integrated data cubes to avoid artifacts and improve computational efficiency)  
  > "The data reduction pipeline uses a high-order polynomial fit to individual spectra, which sometimes causes artifacts." (Total Power Array); "It can be applied to individual spectra as done in the pipeline, or equivalently, to data cubes after their integration, as we did in this study. The latter is computationally much more efficient." (Total Power Array)
- Alternative: In-situ formation or stellar-feedback-driven assembly of massive interarm filamentary structures (rejected because in-situ converging flows take ~100 Myr over ~1 kpc scales and feedback models only explain less massive structures)  
  > "Together with the required condition for a coherent converging flow ($\gtrsim 10\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$) over a large scale ($\gtrsim 1\, {\rm kpc}$), the required long timescale suggests that the formation of the filamentary structures in interarm regions is unlikely." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures); "However, the feedback-driven models so far explained only less massive structures (cite: Smith:2020aa; Tress:2021aa; Kim:2022ab)." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The adopted constant CO-to-H2 conversion factor X_CO has at least a factor of two uncertainty and may vary with galactic radius (especially in the central region).  
  > "For this paper, we temporarily adopt the consensus value of $X_{\rm CO}=2.0\times 10^{20} \,\rm cm^{-2}\cdot [K\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}]^{-1}$ (cite: Bolatto:2013ys) for simplicity, with caveats that this value has at least a factor of two uncertainty (cite: Bolatto:2013ys), may vary with galactic radius (cite: Lada:2020aa)" (Molecular Gas Mass and Surface Density); "[Note that for simplicity, we assume a constant $X_{\rm CO}$ across the whole disk, while it is suggested that the $X_{\rm CO}$ in the central region could be lower than the adopted value (cite: Sodroski:1995aa; Arimoto:1996aa; Oka:1998vn; Strong:2004aa).]" (The Central Region: r<20'' (~ 0.44 kpc))
- Limitation (authors): Line-of-sight overlap of multiple clouds may affect integrated intensity and velocity dispersion measurements in the central and bar regions.  
  > "The integrated intensity $I_{\rm CO}$ map (Figure 5) could portray multiple overlapping clouds along any given line of sight, particularly in the central region." (Molecular Clouds and Their Spatial Variations); "The $\sigma_{v}$ is measured at a cloud-scale resolution (40 pc) and likely traces the dispersion within individual molecular clouds, except in the central and bar regions where the line-of-sight overlap of multiple clouds may be an issue." - Limitation (authors): The dynamical evolution scenario is based only on basic timescale estimates and morphologies, using a fiducial convergence velocity surmised from M51 rather than derived from M83's velocity field.  
  > "Here, we simply adopt $\Delta v=10\, {\rm km \, s^{-1}}$ as a fiducial value in the interarm regions, surmised from a measurement in M51, a galaxy with more prominent spiral arms." (Gas Assembly and Molecular Structures); "The discussions are based only on a basic estimation of their formation timescale and on their morphologies. It is not surprising that some exceptions exist." (Summary and Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): An observatory-announced amplitude calibration error in the 12m and 7m array observations was neglected (estimated impact at most ~0.7% at the galactic center).  
  > "We neglect it because its impact is small, and is, at most, only about 0.7% at the galactic center in the velocity channels where the emission is the strongest." (Footnotes)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Relative weight scaling factors (5 for 7m, 20 for TP) were chosen by visual inspection of the uv weight density plot rather than an objective optimization criterion.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Beam filling factor f=1 is assumed to convert brightness temperature to kinetic temperature in prominent structures, which only provides a lower limit on T_kin if substructures are clumpy below 40 pc.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Masses of individual filamentary structures in Section 8.6 are reported without specifying the exact spatial/velocity polygon boundaries used to integrate them.
