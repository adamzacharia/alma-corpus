## Analysis recipe

1. **Image and combine multi-array ALMA CO data and smooth to a common resolution**: Apply multiscale CLEAN with natural weighting in tclean, combine 7m+TP for CO(3-2) and 12m+7m for CO(2-1) using feather, apply primary beam correction, and smooth all CO cubes to a 3.5'' x 3.5'' beam  
  > "We applied the multiscale CLEAN algorithm with the natural weighting scheme (cite: 2008ISTSP...2..793C). Here we used the `tclean` task to make the images." (ALMA CO); "We then combined the cleaned 7 m data and the calibrated TP array data for CO($J$ = 3–2) and the cleaned 12 and 7 m array data for CO($J$ = 2–1) using the `feather` task." (ALMA CO); "All the CO datasets were smoothed to match the beam size of $3.5\ensuremath{''} \times 3.5\ensuremath{''}$." (Table 1)
   - software: CASA, tclean, feather
   - parameter: Velocity resolution 0.4 km s^-1; PB threshold 0.2 for 12CO(2-1) and 0.5 for 12CO(3-2) and 13CO(2-1); smoothed beam 3.5'' x 3.5''  
  > "The primary beam correction was applied with a threshold of 0.2 for $^{12}$CO($J$ = 2–1) and 0.5 for $^{12}$CO($J$ = 3–2) and $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1)." (ALMA CO); "All the CO datasets were smoothed to match the beam size of $3.5\ensuremath{''} \times 3.5\ensuremath{''}$." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Measure cloud line parameters, sizes, virial masses, and CO intensity ratios**: Identify eight clouds detected in 13CO(2-1) at >= 3 sigma, fit single Gaussian profiles to CO spectra, measure cloud sizes from the half-peak integrated intensity contour area, compute virial masses, and map the 12CO(2-1)/12CO(1-0) ratio  
  > "Eight of them, K, L, N, O–R, and T, are detected in $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1) with $3\sigma$ or higher significance." (Distributions of CO and X-Rays); "Cols. (4–8): Physical properties of CO emission derived from a single Gaussian fitting." (Table 1); "Col. (9): $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1) derived cloud size defined as ($A$/$\pi$)$^{0.5}$ $\times 2$, where $A$ is the total cloud surface area surrounded by the half intensity of peak integrated intensity contour." (Table 1)
   - parameter: Velocity range V_LSR = 285.5-287.8 km s^-1 for peak intensity maps  
  > "Figures 1a–1d show the peak intensity maps of $^{12}$CO($J$ = 1–0), $^{12}$CO($J$ = 2–1), $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1), and $^{12}$CO($J$ = 3–2) at $V_\mathrm{LSR} = 285.5$–287.8 km s$^{-1}$" (Distributions of CO and X-Rays)
   - assumption: Virial equilibrium for cloud mass estimation  
  > "Col. (10): Mass of the cloud derived using the virial theorem and $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1) properties, $M_\mathrm{vir}$." (Table 1)
   - fitting: Least-squares single Gaussian fitting to CO spectra  
  > "Cols. (4–8): Physical properties of CO emission derived from a single Gaussian fitting." (Table 1); "Here, $dv$ is the half-maximum half-width (HMHW) linewidth of the CO line profile derived by least-squares fitting with a Gaussian kernel, and $dr$ is the cloud radius." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - validation: Confirmed that 12CO(3-2)/12CO(1-0) intensity ratio distribution is similar to R_2-1/1-0  
  > "We also confirmed that the intensity ratio distribution of $^{12}$CO($J$ = 3–2)/$^{12}$CO($J$ = 1–0) is similar to that of $R_\mathrm{2-1/1-0}$." (Intensity Ratio of 12CO(J = 2–1)/12CO(J = 1–0))
3. **Derive kinetic temperature and H2 number density via non-LTE LVG analysis**: Perform non-LTE radiative transfer calculations using the Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) approximation on 12CO(2-1), 12CO(3-2), and 13CO(2-1) line intensities for each of the eight clouds  
  > "Using $^{12}$CO($J$ = 2–1, 3–2) and $^{13}$CO($J$ = 2–1) line emission data taken with the Atacama Large Millimeter/Submillimeter Array, we derived the H$_2$ number density and kinetic temperature of eight $^{13}$CO-detected clouds using the large velocity gradient approximation" (Abstract); "In order to estimate the kinetic temperature $T_\mathrm{kin}$ and H$_2$ number density $n$(H$_2$) of molecular clouds in N49, we performed a non-LTE analysis using the large velocity gradient (LVG) approximation (cite: 1974ApJ...189..441G; 1974ApJ...187L..67S)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis); "The $^{12}$CO($J$ =1–0) data were not included in the LVG analysis because the lowest-$J$ transition may be subject to self-absorption owing to a large optical depth (cite: 2010PASJ...62...51M)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - parameter: Individual velocity gradient dv/dr calculated for each cloud from HMHW linewidth dv and cloud radius dr  
  > "Here, $dv$ is the half-maximum half-width (HMHW) linewidth of the CO line profile derived by least-squares fitting with a Gaussian kernel, and $dr$ is the cloud radius." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis); "Since the eight molecular clouds have different cloud sizes and linewidths, we calculated individual $dv/dr$ for each cloud." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - assumption: Isothermal spherical cloud with uniform photon escape probability and velocity gradient dv/dr  
  > "This non-LTE code calculates the radiative transfer of multiple transitions of atomic and/or molecular lines, assuming an isothermal spherical cloud with uniform photon escape probability and velocity gradient of $dv/dr$." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - assumption: Abundance ratios [12CO/H2] = 1.6 x 10^-5 and [12CO/13CO] = 50  
  > "We also used the abundance ratio of \[$^{12}$CO/H$_2$\]$~=~1.6 \times 10^{-5}$ and the abundance ratio of isotopes \[$^{12}$CO/$^{13}$CO\] = 50 (cite: 1987ApJ...315..621B; 2010PASJ...62...51M; 2011AJ....141...73M; 2014ApJ...796..123F)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit X-ray shell geometry and T_kin vs n(H2) correlation**: Reprocess archival Chandra data to create a 0.5-7.0 keV flux image, fit radial X-ray profile with a 3D spherical Gaussian shell model, and fit the T_kin vs n(H2) relation for clouds N, O, P, Q, R using MPFITEXY  
  > "We then made an exposure-corrected, energy-ﬁltered image using the task `ﬂuximage` in the energy band of 0.5–7.0 keV as a broad-band X-ray image." (Chandra X-Rays); "To calculate the shell diameter and thickness of the X-ray shell in N49, we performed a least-squares fitting using a three-dimensional spherical shell model with a Gaussian function $F(r)$ (cite: 2017ApJ...843...61S; 2021ApJ...923...15S; 2022ApJ...938...94A):" (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis); "Clouds N, O, P, Q, and R show a negative correlation which was fitted by the least-squares method using the `MPFITEXY` routine (cite: 2010MNRAS.409.1330W)" (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - software: CIAO, CALDB, chandra_repro, fluximage, MPFITEXY
   - parameter: SNR geometric center at (05h25m59.57s, -66d04m56.4s); X-ray energy band 0.5-7.0 keV  
  > "We then made an exposure-corrected, energy-ﬁltered image using the task `ﬂuximage` in the energy band of 0.5–7.0 keV as a broad-band X-ray image." (Chandra X-Rays); "Figure 3 shows the radial distributions of X-ray intensity and $T_\mathrm{kin}$ of the eight molecular clouds from the geometric center of the SNR at ($\alpha_\mathrm{J2000}$, $\delta_\mathrm{J2000}) = (05^\mathrm{h}25^\mathrm{m}59.57s$, $-66\ensuremath{^\circ}04\ensuremath{'}56.4s$)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - assumption: Three-dimensional spherical shell geometry with Gaussian radial profile  
  > "To calculate the shell diameter and thickness of the X-ray shell in N49, we performed a least-squares fitting using a three-dimensional spherical shell model with a Gaussian function $F(r)$ (cite: 2017ApJ...843...61S; 2021ApJ...923...15S; 2022ApJ...938...94A):" (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - fitting: Least-squares fitting for shell profile and MPFITEXY least-squares fitting for T_kin vs n(H2)  
  > "To calculate the shell diameter and thickness of the X-ray shell in N49, we performed a least-squares fitting using a three-dimensional spherical shell model with a Gaussian function $F(r)$ (cite: 2017ApJ...843...61S; 2021ApJ...923...15S; 2022ApJ...938...94A):" (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis); "Clouds N, O, P, Q, and R show a negative correlation which was fitted by the least-squares method using the `MPFITEXY` routine (cite: 2010MNRAS.409.1330W)" (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Model cosmic-ray diffusion and time evolution of gas temperature in a PDR**: Calculate cosmic-ray diffusion length and run 1D plane-parallel PDR models over two stages (10^7 yr pre-SN at standard cosmic-ray ionization rate, then 10^5 yr post-SN with enhanced ionization rates) to track T_kin at the cloud center (A_V = 5 mag)  
  > "Adopting $t_\mathrm{age} = 1.5 \times 10^{11}$ s (cite: 2012ApJ...748..117P), $E = 100$ MeV, and $B = 10~\mu$G, we obtain $l_\mathrm{diff} \sim$6 pc" (Discussion); "In the first step, the model simulates the temporal evolution of the gas temperature and chemical abundances for 10$^7$ yr, assuming the standard $\zeta$ value of $1\times10^{-17}$ s$^{-1}$." (APPENDIX Time evolution of the kinetic temperature); "In the second step, the model follows the evolution for $1\times10^5$ yrs with an enhanced $\zeta$ value in a range between $3\times10^{-17}$ s$^{-1}$ and $1\times10^{-14}$ s$^{-1}$." (APPENDIX Time evolution of the kinetic temperature)
   - parameter: t_age = 1.5 x 10^11 s, E = 100 MeV, B = 10 uG for diffusion; G_0 = 110, n_(HI+H2) = 1 x 10^3 cm^-3, N_H/A_V = 3.5 x 10^21 cm^-2, A_V = 5 mag, zeta = 3 x 10^-17 to 1 x 10^-14 s^-1 for PDR model  
  > "Adopting $t_\mathrm{age} = 1.5 \times 10^{11}$ s (cite: 2012ApJ...748..117P), $E = 100$ MeV, and $B = 10~\mu$G, we obtain $l_\mathrm{diff} \sim$6 pc" (Discussion); "As input parameters for the model, we employ the UV intensity $G_0$ = $110$ (cite: 2012ApJ...744..160S) and the hydrogen (Hi+H$_2$) gas density $n_{\mathrm{HI+H_2}}$ = $1\times10^3$ cm$^{-3}$." (APPENDIX Time evolution of the kinetic temperature); "The ratio of the hydrogen column density to $A_V$ is assumed to be $3.5\times10^{21}$ cm$^{-2}$ (cite: 2006A&A...447..991C). The gas temperature evolution at the cloud center ($A_V = 5$ mag.) after the explosion is shown in Figure 5." (APPENDIX Time evolution of the kinetic temperature)

## Qualitative findings

- The molecular clouds associated with SNR N49 fall into two groups: three shock-front clouds (K, L, T) with high kinetic temperatures (~42-46 K) and lower densities (~480-720 cm^-3), and five more distant clouds (N, O, P, Q, R) with moderate temperatures (~19-21 K) and higher densities (~780-1320 cm^-3).  
  > "The physical properties of the clouds are divided into two categories: three of them near the shock front show the highest temperatures of $\sim$50 K with densities of $\sim$500–700 cm$^{-3}$, while other clouds slightly distant from the SNR have moderate temperatures of $\sim$20 K with densities of $\sim$800–1300 cm$^{-3}$." (Abstract)
- Clouds K, L, and T are heated by supernova shocks, representing the first compelling evidence of shock-heated molecular clouds in an extragalactic SNR, consistent with thermal conduction producing X-ray recombining plasma.  
  > "The former clouds were heated by supernova shocks, but the latter were dominantly affected by the cosmic-ray heating. These findings are consistent with the efficient production of X-ray recombining plasma in N49 due to thermal conduction between the cold clouds and hot plasma." (Abstract); "This is the first compelling evidence of shock-heated molecular clouds in an extragalactic SNR." (Summary)
- Clouds N, O, P, Q, and R lie along a constant-pressure relation (negative correlation between T_kin and n(H2)) and were not evaporated by shocks, whereas clouds K, L, and T are shifted to higher pressure and lower density, indicating partial shock evaporation.  
  > "The negative correlation between $T_\mathrm{kin}$ and $n$(H$_2$) indicates that clouds N, O, P, Q, and R have constant pressure and did not experience shock evaporation within the short SNR age of $\sim$4800 yr." (Summary); "On the other hand, clouds K, L, and T, which are on the inside or on the edge of the shell, are significantly shifted from this pressure-constant line to a higher-pressure region and have slightly reduced densities, suggesting that the shock-engulfed clouds were partially evaporated through the shock-cloud interaction." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: N49 in the LMC is associated with molecular clouds and has a known distance and very little line-of-sight contamination; multi-transition CO and 13CO ALMA observations resolve sub-parsec clouds and enable non-LTE temperature and density determinations.  
  > "On the other hand, there have been no such studies on extragalactic SNRs especially in the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC), despite the advantages of very little contamination along the line of sight (cite: 2010A&A...520A..24S) and its known distance (cite: 2013Natur.495...76P)." (Introduction); "LHA 120-N49 (hereafter N49) in the LMC is one of the best extragalactic SNRs to study shock-heating and compression of a gaseous medium because it is believed to be associated with molecular clouds (cite: 1997ApJ...480..607B; 2010AJ....139...68V; 2015ApJ...808...77U)." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Non-LTE Large Velocity Gradient (LVG) analysis of multiple CO transitions and 13CO(2-1) constrains both kinetic temperature and H2 density, while excluding 12CO(1-0) avoids self-absorption bias at large optical depth.  
  > "In order to estimate the kinetic temperature $T_\mathrm{kin}$ and H$_2$ number density $n$(H$_2$) of molecular clouds in N49, we performed a non-LTE analysis using the large velocity gradient (LVG) approximation (cite: 1974ApJ...189..441G; 1974ApJ...187L..67S)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis); "The $^{12}$CO($J$ =1–0) data were not included in the LVG analysis because the lowest-$J$ transition may be subject to self-absorption owing to a large optical depth (cite: 2010PASJ...62...51M)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
- Alternative: Heating by local OB stars or young stellar objects was rejected because no such extra heating sources are associated with the molecular clouds near N49.  
  > "It is noteworthy that no extra heating sources such as OB stars and/or young stellar objects are associated with the molecular clouds in the vicinity of the SNR." (Intensity Ratio of 12CO(J = 2–1)/12CO(J = 1–0))
- Alternative: Cosmic-ray heating alone as the cause of the ~40-50 K temperatures in clouds K, L, and T was rejected because the ~6 pc cosmic-ray diffusion length means cosmic rays heat all clouds equally.  
  > "In other words, cosmic-ray heating is not sufficient to increase the kinetic temperature of K, L, and T significantly above those of the unshocked clouds (O, Q, R, etc)." (Discussion)
- Alternative: Including 12CO(J=1-0) in the LVG analysis was rejected due to potential self-absorption from high optical depth.  
  > "The $^{12}$CO($J$ =1–0) data were not included in the LVG analysis because the lowest-$J$ transition may be subject to self-absorption owing to a large optical depth (cite: 2010PASJ...62...51M)." (Large Velocity Gradient Analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Decomposing the density and velocity distribution of the shocked clouds K, L, and T requires even higher-angular-resolution observations.  
  > "Quantifying this scenario requires a decomposition of the density and velocity distribution of clouds K, L and T which requires even higher-resolution data (cite: 2021MNRAS.500.1721M; 2022MNRAS.509.2180S)." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Projection effects affect the apparent radial distances of clouds O, Q, and R relative to cloud K.  
  > "Although clouds O, Q, and R have smaller radial distances than cloud K, this is likely due to projection effects." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Confirming the thermal conduction origin of the X-ray recombining plasma requires higher angular and spectral resolution X-ray spectroscopy (e.g., Athena, XRISM).  
  > "Additional spatially resolved X-ray spectroscopy with high-angular/spectral resolution is needed to confirm this scenario. The Athena mission has the potential to accomplish these (cite: 2013arXiv1306.2307N)." (Discussion)
- Limitation (authors): Directly estimating the cosmic-ray ionization rate in N49 requires follow-up observations of lines such as HCO+, DCO+, and/or [CI].  
  > "Further follow-up observations using multiple emission lines such as HCO$^{+}$, DCO$^{+}$ (cite: 2014A&A...568A..50V), and/or \[Ci\] (cite: 2023arXiv230615983Y) are needed to estimate the cosmic-ray ionization rate in the vicinity of the LMC SNR N49." (Discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The LVG analysis relies on fixed assumed abundance ratios ([12CO/H2] = 1.6e-5 and [12CO/13CO] = 50) for all clouds, whereas selective photodissociation or shock chemistry could alter the [12CO/13CO] ratio in the shock-front clouds K, L, and T (which have weak 13CO detections and large T_kin uncertainties, e.g., 46 +57/-18 K for cloud T).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 12CO(3-2) was observed only with the 7 m + TP arrays (3.48'' x 2.76'' beam), whereas 12CO(2-1) and 13CO(2-1) were observed with 12 m + 7 m arrays without Total Power data, potentially introducing differential spatial filtering of extended emission despite smoothing to 3.5''.
