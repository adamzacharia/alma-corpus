## Analysis recipe

1. **Build the DALI model grid**: Run DALI thermochemical models for T Tauri and Herbig templates, varying one parameter at a time from the fiducial model  
  > "Only one parameter is varied at a time in each model, starting from the fiducial values." (3.1 Parameters that affect vertical CO surface); "Key stellar and disk parameters such as stellar luminosity and temperature, total disk mass, carbon abundance and critical radius are varied to determine their effect on the CO emitting surface." (abstract)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: Fiducial parameters in Table 1; sampled values in Table 2 (bold values fiducial)  
  > "Initial parameters in fiducial DALI model" (Table 1); "Variation of DALI model parameters. Bold numbers indicate the fiducial model values." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Initial radial structure follows the self-similar viscously evolving disk solution  
  > "The initial setup of the radial structure for the gas and dust in DALI follows the self-similar solution to a viscously evolving disk" (2.1 Initial DALI setup)
   - assumption: T Tauri accretion UV: accreted-mass potential energy released with 100% efficiency as 10^4 K blackbody; no accretion for Herbig model  
  > "It was assumed that the gravitational potential energy of the accreted mass is released with 100% efficiency as blackbody emission with $T = 10^{4}$ K" (2.1 Initial DALI setup); "No accretion is considered in the Herbig system, based on observational evidence" (2.1 Initial DALI setup)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Solve vertical hydrostatic equilibrium iteratively**: DALI hydrostatic solver iterates density and temperature structure starting from the Gaussian initial model  
  > "DALI has a hydrostatic equilibrium solver implemented, which self-consistently iterates over consecutive models, starting from the initial parametrization, to compute the disk structure." (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: 6th iteration of the solver used for all models  
  > "all presented models in this work will consider the output from the 6th iteration of the self-consistent hydrostatic equilibrium solver" (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
   - assumption: Self-gravity neglected  
  > "Self-gravity may become relevant for the most massive disks in our parameter space, producing lower emitting layers than our prescription." (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Convergence checked by minimal change in temperature and vertical location of the CO layer between iterations  
  > "which in this case is checked by verifying that the variation of the temperature and vertical location of the CO layer is minimal" (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
3. **Extract the 12CO 2-1 tau=1 surface and fit the characteristic z/r**: Take the tau=1 surface of 12CO 2-1 from the model, fit an exponentially tapered power law, and take the best-fit constant z/r inside 80% of the taper radius  
  > "which is directly computed from the models as the location where the integrated column density of material results in an optical depth ($\tau$) of 1 for $^{12}$CO $2-1$" (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium); "Each vertical profile is fitted by an exponentially tapered power-law and characterized by the $z/r$ value that represents the structure inwards of 80% of the tapering radius." (abstract)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: 30 walkers, 500 burn-in steps, 800 steps for confidence intervals  
  > "We use 30 walkers, with 500 steps as burn-in and 800 steps to evaluate confidence intervals." (3 Results)
   - parameter: Uniform initialisation of z0, phi, r_taper, psi within z0 in [0,100], phi in [0,10], r_taper/au in [50,500], psi in [0,10]  
  > "We initialize the MCMC assuming a uniform distribution of the four parameters within the following intervals" (3 Results)
   - assumption: Only the rising portion of the surface is characterised; the outer drop is treated as a resolution artifact  
  > "In some model outputs this drop appears as a sudden, almost vertical, feature in the outer radii which is likely a resolution artifact that does not impact our results." (3 Results)
   - fitting: emcee MCMC sampler, 30 walkers, uniform initial distributions; likelihood not stated  
  > "using a Monte Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) sampler as implemented by emcee" (3 Results); "We use 30 walkers, with 500 steps as burn-in and 800 steps to evaluate confidence intervals." (3 Results)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure sensitivity of z/r to each parameter**: Compute characteristic z/r for each varied model and compare as percentage variation from the fiducial model  
  > "For every case the characteristic $z/r$ is computed as described at the beginning of Section 3." (3.1 Parameters that affect vertical CO surface); "presents the percentual variation from the fiducial model for disk masses of 10$^{-3}$ M$_{\odot}$ and 10$^{-2}$ M$_{\odot}$" (3.1 Parameters that affect vertical CO surface)
   - software: DALI, emcee
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive the z/r-Md relation per stellar type and carbon depletion**: Cubic spline through the model grid in disk mass, separately for T Tauri and Herbig models and for each volatile carbon abundance; used to extrapolate to nearby masses  
  > "The parameter space between our models is covered using a cubic spline which we use to extrapolate our results beyond our mass grid, to nearby values." (3.1 Parameters that affect vertical CO surface); "We refer to this result as the $z/r$-$M_d$ relationship, noting that volatile carbon depletion will shift it and must be accounted for." (3.1 Parameters that affect vertical CO surface)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Measure characteristic z/r of the observed disks**: Surfaces from channel-map emission maxima (previous works, ALFAHOR or DISKSURF); z/r computed for the mean profile and for its lower and upper limits, which sets the uncertainty  
  > "As done for the DALI models, the vertical profiles of the observational sample are processed to obtain their characteristic $z/r$." (3.2 Model comparison to observations); "$z/r$ is calculated for the mean value and also for the lower and upper limits of the profiles, which indicate the dispersion within radial bins and will give the uncertainty on the characteristic $z/r$" (3.2 Model comparison to observations)
   - software: ALFAHOR, DISKSURF
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Re-analysis of six DISKSURF disks with ALFAHOR gave comparable results with lower scatter  
  > "are re-analysed using ALFAHOR, yielding comparable results but with a lower scatter" (3.2 Model comparison to observations)
7. **Infer disk mass and carbon depletion by comparing with literature masses**: Compare the z/r-Md relation at ISM carbon, depletion 10 and depletion 100 with literature masses; adopt dust-based mass (gas-to-dust 100) as the anchor unless a dynamical mass exists, and pick the preferred depletion that matches  
  > "For disks that show a large scatter in their literature values we focus on the total disk mass extracted from the dust mass assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100 as our main indicator of the expected value, unless a dynamical mass estimate is available in which case we prefer that value." (3.3 Accounting for volatile carbon depletion)
   - parameter: Carbon depletion grid values of 10 and 100 (and ISM); depletion 100 masses above ~1 Msun are not reported  
  > "assuming carbon depletion of 100 produces unrealistic disk mass values of" (3.3 Accounting for volatile carbon depletion)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio of 100 for dust-derived masses, treated as lower limits of total mass  
  > "We favor the second scenario as dust-derived masses are expected to be lower limits of the total disk mass" (3.2 Model comparison to observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Preferred depletions for HD 163296 and MWC 480 agree with spatially resolved values from MAPS  
  > "For MWC 480 the preferred carbon depletion from our results ranges between 10-100, also in agreement with the spatially resolved values of (cite: MAPS_Zhang)." (3.3 Accounting for volatile carbon depletion)

## Qualitative findings

- T Tauri models show larger z/r than Herbig models at the same mass and carbon depletion  
  > "Across all masses, for the same degree of carbon depletion, the T Tauri systems display a larger $z/r$ than the disks around Herbig stars" (3.1 Parameters that affect vertical CO surface)
- With ISM carbon, measured z/r is below that expected from dust masses for most disks; exceptions DM Tau, Elias 2-27, HD 97048, HD 34282  
  > "The only exceptions are DM Tau, Elias 2-27, HD 97048 and HD 34282." (3.2 Model comparison to observations)
- T Tauri disks appear more carbon depleted than Herbig disks  
  > "T Tauri disks seem to be more carbon depleted than Herbig systems." (5 Conclusions)
- Different transitions of the same CO isotopologue give negligible z/r variation; 13CO and C18O layers lie closer to the midplane  
  > "within the same isotopologue, different transitions create a negligible variation in the recovered $z/r$, as the emission comes from similar regions" (4.3 Caveats and future perspectives); "the vertical layer of $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O will be more settled towards the midplane" (4.3 Caveats and future perspectives)
- Ignoring hydrostatic equilibrium underestimates optically thick CO flux and overestimates inferred disk masses  
  > "not accounting for hydrostatic equilibrium can underestimate the CO flux, therefore overestimating the calculated disk masses" (4.1 Low gas-to-dust or high carbon depletion?)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Mid-inclination disks with published 12CO 2-1 cubes and extracted surfaces, with disk mass estimates from several methods  
  > "This sample has been constructed from mid-inclination disks with available $^{12}$CO $2-1$ data cubes from which the surfaces have been extracted and published in previous works" (3.2 Model comparison to observations)
- Why this method: Hydrostatic equilibrium solved self-consistently because the Gaussian initial setup is not realistic; 12CO 2-1 chosen as the bright, common surface tracer  
  > "The initial setup presented in the previous section is a useful approximation, but is not realistic" (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium); "We select this specific transition as it is easily observed by ALMA in protoplanetary disks and the most common tracer of the disk vertical extent" (Introduction)
- Alternative: Low gas-to-dust ratio instead of carbon depletion; disfavoured because dust masses are likely lower limits and dynamical masses indicate gas-to-dust of at least 100  
  > "We favor the second scenario as dust-derived masses are expected to be lower limits of the total disk mass" (3.2 Model comparison to observations); "However we favor the high carbon depletion over low gas-to-dust" (4.1 Low gas-to-dust or high carbon depletion?)
- Alternative: Static Gaussian vertical structure rejected in favour of the hydrostatic solution  
  > "We use this approach for all of our analysis." (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
- How they decided it was enough: Convergence of the hydrostatic solver reached after a couple of iterations; sixth iteration adopted  
  > "Convergence is reached rapidly after a couple of iterations, therefore all presented models in this work will consider the output from the 6th iteration" (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
- Limitation (authors): Highly structured or transition disks are not well represented by the smooth full-disk models  
  > "Highly structured or transition disks, which is the case for several disks in our sample, are likely not accurately represented by our models." (4.3 Caveats and future perspectives)
- Limitation (authors): Depletion value is an order-of-magnitude approximation and possibly a lower limit  
  > "The volatile carbon depletion value should be taken with caution, as it is a single order of magnitude approximation" (3.3 Accounting for volatile carbon depletion)
- Limitation (authors): Models use a single disk-integrated carbon depletion and a standard radial density profile  
  > "a standard radial density profile and a single disk-integrated volatile carbon depletion value" (4.3 Caveats and future perspectives)
- Limitation (authors): Self-gravity omitted, which can lower emitting layers for the most massive disks  
  > "Self-gravity may become relevant for the most massive disks in our parameter space, producing lower emitting layers than our prescription." (2.2 Hydrostatic equilibrium)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Mass and carbon depletion are degenerate; the depletion is only fixed by assuming a dust-based gas-to-dust ratio of 100 as truth, so depletion is partly circular
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust-based masses are uncertain by large factors (opacity, temperature, scattering), which feed directly into the inferred depletion
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only two template stars; stellar-mass dependence within each class is not explored beyond luminosity and temperature
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Observed z/r values come from heterogeneous surface extractions (ALFAHOR vs DISKSURF), resolutions and data sets, with uncertainties from radial-bin scatter only
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Model z/r is defined from the tau=1 surface, while the observed one is from channel-map emission maxima; the systematic offset between them is not quantified in this paper
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No uncertainty propagation for the literature mass and depletion choices; 'preferred depletion' is judged by eye at 0, 10 or 100
