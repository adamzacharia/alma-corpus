## Analysis recipe

1. **Derive ALMA continuum flux density and upper limit on CO(1-0) line flux and cold molecular gas mass**: Measure nuclear continuum at 115.3 GHz rest-frame; compute 3-sigma upper limit on CO(1-0) integrated line flux from channel RMS using Sage et al. (2007) and Young et al. (2011) prescription; convert to L'_CO via Solomon et al. (1997) and to M_H2,CO via alpha_CO, then scale by area ratio  
  > "Continuum emission from the nucleus is well detected, with a flux density at 115.3 GHz in the rest-frame of S 115 GHz = 1.6 ± 0.01 mJy bm [−][1] . CO(1–0) line emission is not detected." (2.2. ALMA interferometry); "We follow the prescription of Sage et al. (2007) and Young et al. (2011) by setting σ [2] line [= ∆] [v] [2] [σ] [2] [ N] [l] [ (1][.] [+] [N] [l] [/] [N] [b] [)" (2.2. ALMA interferometry); "To estimate a molecular gas mass from the luminosity, we adopt the standard conversion factor appropriate for, e.g., the Milky Way, α CO = 4.3 M ⊙ / [K km s [−][1] pc [2] ]" (2.2. ALMA interferometry)
   - parameter: FWHM = 100 km s^-1, sampling = 43.26 km s^-1, N_b = 118, RMS = 0.2 mJy bm^-1  
  > "Assuming a line width of FWHM=100 km s [−][1], a sampling of 43.26 km s [−][1] as in our data, and RMS = 0.2 mJy bm [−][1], we find σ line = 30.7 mJy km s [−][1] bm [−][1] ." (2.2. ALMA interferometry); "and N b = 118 is the number of spectral channels from which σ was estimated." (2.2. ALMA interferometry)
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.3 M_sun / (K km s^-1 pc^2), and CO emission extends over the same area as warm molecular gas seen with NIRSpec (factor 5.3 larger than beam)  
  > "we adopt the standard conversion factor appropriate for, e.g., the Milky Way, α CO = 4.3 M ⊙ / [K km s [−][1] pc [2] ] (e.g., Bolatto et al. 2013), and find a 3σ upper limit of 1.5 × 10 [8] M ⊙ bm [−][1] ." 2. **Model JWST emission lines and stellar CO band head absorption**: Spaxel-by-spaxel MCMC fitting of 1 or 2 Gaussians plus linear continuum for emission lines (selected by BIC), and convolved HD 9138 K4III stellar template plus 2nd order polynomial for CO band heads between 2.485 and 2.62 um  
  > "We reproduced the emission line features using a Gaussian line profile coupled with a linear continuum in frequency." (3.1. Emission Lines); "We allow up to 2 Gaussians and select the number used by the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)." (3.1. Emission Lines); "To model the CO band head absorption features, we used a template provided by Winge et al. (2009) instead of a Gaussian line profile." (3.2. CO band head absorption)
   - software: emcee, astropy, spectres
   - parameter: 5000 steps and 200 walkers (two runs) for emission lines; 2000 steps and 100 walkers for CO band heads  
  > "We use 5000 steps and 200 walkers. We then perform a second run starting from the best fit parameters by likelihood of the first run with a normal distribution with a 0.1% standard deviation for the initial positions." (3.1. Emission Lines); "We use 2000 steps and 100 walkers when performing the fitting." (3.2. CO band head absorption)
   - assumption: Normal distribution of stellar velocities; instrumental line spread function accounted for  
  > "The templates are of single stars; to adjust for a stellar population we assume the distribution of velocities is normal." (3.2.    - fitting: MCMC with emcee using maximum likelihood plus uniform parameter priors and a Gaussian prior on redshift separation (sigma_z = 0.001)  
  > "The sampling was performed with the python package emcee (Goodman & Weare 2010)" (3. Emission line and stellar continuum fitting); "We define the prior probability function for all parameters to be uniform between the boundaries and − inf otherwise." (3.1. Emission Lines); "where < z > is the mean z for all N Gaussians in the model and the standard deviation of z separation probability σ z = 0.001." (3.1. Emission Lines)
   - validation: Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) for 1 vs 2 Gaussian components  
  > "We allow up to 2 Gaussians and select the number used by the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)." (3.1. Emission Lines)

## Qualitative findings

- The previous IRAM PdBI 5-sigma detection of CO(1-0) line emission by Nesvadba et al. (2010) is spurious, confirming that the majority of molecular gas in 3C 326 N is warm.  
  > "This is about a factor 3 less than the M H 2 = 2 × 10 [9] M ⊙ of warm molecular gas previously found with Spitzer (Ogle et al. 2007), and suggests that the majority of the molecular gas in 3C 326 N is indeed warm." (2.2. ALMA interferometry); "Given the new, deeper observations available now from ALMA with a much larger bandwidth, we consider this previous detection spurious." (2.2. ALMA interferometry)
- A kpc-sized cavity in the Northern part of the molecular disk with hourglass-shaped velocity field traces an expanding, jet-inflated bubble within a rotating gas disk.  
  > "At 10× higher spatial resolution with NIRSpec, we identify a kpc-sized cavity in the Northern hemisphere of the disk, delineated by three bright knots of line emission at the far end." (7. Summary); "We interpret this feature as the signature of an expanding, jet-inflated bubble within a rotating gas disk." (7. Summary)
- Shocks driven by the radio jet dominate the heating of H2 and most warm ionised gas down to 190 pc scales, and turbulent line broadening is sufficient to suppress star formation.  
  > "We show that most of the gas, with the exception of [NeIII]λ15.56, is predominantly heated by shocks driven by the radio jets into the gas, both for the ro-vibrational and rotational H 2 lines, and that the accompanying line broadening is sufficient to suppress star formation in the molecular gas." (abstract); "Line emission of H 2 and most warm ionised lines is heated by shocks. This has already been shown previously from source-integrated data (Nesvadba et al. 2010), however, we can now also rule out the presence of smaller areas dominated by heating from young stars, down to scales of 190 pc." (7. Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST NIRSpec and MIRI MRS provide orders of magnitude higher sensitivity and spatial resolution (0.11''-0.4'') to resolve gas kinematics down to GMC scales (~190 pc), while archival ALMA Band 3 data test the previous IRAM CO(1-0) detection with deeper sensitivity and larger bandwidth.  
  > "Nearly 15 years after the end of the cold Spitzer mission, the James Webb Space Telescope is now again enabling new mid-infrared observations of AGN, at orders of magnitude higher sensitivity, spectral and spatial resolution" (1. Introduction); "Given the new, deeper observations available now from ALMA with a much larger bandwidth, we consider this previous detection spurious." (2.2. ALMA interferometry)
- Why this method: Two-component Gaussian MCMC fitting with BIC selection captures complex dual-component line profiles across the disk, and stellar CO band head fitting isolates systemic redshift without hydrodynamic wind contamination.  
  > "we find that fits with two Gaussians are preferred in almost all regions of the galaxy over single-Gaussian fits." (4.4.1 Ro-vibrational line emission of H2); "Stellar absorption lines are the most robust tracers of the systemic redshift, because the stellar component dominates the mass budget within the central few kpc of early-type galaxies (3 × 10 [11] M ⊙ in 3C 326 N compared to 2 × 10 [9] M ⊙ in molecular gas Nesvadba et al. 2010), and because it is not affected by hydrodynamic processes like AGN or starburst-driven winds" (4.3. Continuum morphology and stellar absorption bands)

  > "Nonetheless, the overall complexity of the gas kinematics and morphology make it difficult to clearly separate a kinematically perturbed from the quiescently rotating part of the disk, and to derive, e.g., a robust estimate of the gas energetics in a simple, unique, and analytic way."   > "Brγ is covered, but not detected, probably because the interstellar line emission is filling in the stellar Brγ absorption line. At R ∼ 2700, the spectral resolving power of NIRSpec does not allow us to robustly isolate these two components." (4.2. Emission line and CO band head detections)
