## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract ALMA 1.2 mm continuum fluxes and identify sources**: Identify sources with SNR >= 4 on the 2'' uv-tapered mosaic and extract peak and integrated fluxes on a line-free / emission-line subtracted 1.2 mm image using CASA imfit.  
  > "13 sources with a signal-to-noise (SNR) $\geq$4 were identified including one at RA=06:00:09.143 and DEC=$-$20:08:26.579 with an elongated half light radius of 1$''$ 3." (Observation and Data Analysis); "By extracting the flux on a line-free image at 1.2mm using the CASA *imfit* task, the peak flux is $\rm{S^{peak}_{1.2mm}}$=366$\pm$62 $\rm{\mu Jy.beam^{-1}}$ and the integrated flux is $\rm{S^{int}_{1.2mm}}$=484$\pm$135 $\mu$Jy on an emission line subtracted image." (Observation and Data Analysis); "In the ALMA data, z6.3b also shows a faint dust detection with a S/N$\sim$3.5 ($\rm{f_{peak}=226\pm63 \mu Jy.beam^{-1}}$ as measured with imfit)." (Lens models and multiple images)
   - software: CASA, imfit
   - parameter: SNR >= 4 detection threshold  
  > "13 sources with a signal-to-noise (SNR) $\geq$4 were identified including one at RA=06:00:09.143 and DEC=$-$20:08:26.579 with an elongated half light radius of 1$''$ 3." (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fitting with CASA imfit  
  > "By extracting the flux on a line-free image at 1.2mm using the CASA *imfit* task, the peak flux is $\rm{S^{peak}_{1.2mm}}$=366$\pm$62 $\rm{\mu Jy.beam^{-1}}$ and the integrated flux is $\rm{S^{int}_{1.2mm}}$=484$\pm$135 $\mu$Jy on an emission line subtracted image."    - validation: _not stated_
2. **Extract multi-wavelength optical/NIR/IRAC photometry and deblend overlapping sources**: Search within 2'' radius on HST RELICS images, build catalogues with SExtractor in dual-image mode on PSF-matched images, and deblend overlapping sources (foreground, arc, z6.3a, z6.3b) using Sersic profile modelling with GALFIT on WFC3-NIR, ACS, and IRAC images.  
  > "Photometric catalogues were built using version 2.19.5 of *SExtractor* (cite: SExtractor) in dual image mode on psf matched images." (Observation and Data Analysis); "Noting the overlapping foreground object, in order to extract the photometry of MACS0600-arc we model each source with a Sersic profile using GALFIT (cite: GALFIT) on the WFC3-NIR and IRAC images" (Observation and Data Analysis); "On the IRAC data, where the separation is not clearly resolved, we used the flux ratio observed at 1.6$\mu$m to separate the contribution of each source." (Table 3 caption)
   - software: SExtractor, GALFIT
   - parameter: DETECT_THRESH = 2 sigma; ANALYSIS_THRESH = 2 sigma; DETECT_MINAREA = 5 px  
  > "The extraction parameters were defined to maximise the detection of faint objects (DETECT_THRESH = 2$\sigma$ ; ANALYSIS_THRESH = 2$\sigma$ ; DETECT_MINAREA= 5 px) on a detection picture composed by the sum of all WFC3 images." (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - parameter: Error bars measured in 0.4'' radius circle on residual image; upper limits in 0.4'' (HST), 0.8'' (HAWK-I), 1.2'' (IRAC) radius apertures  
  > "Error bars are measured on the residual image in a 0$''$ 4 radius circle." (Observation and Data Analysis); "Upper photometric limits on the ACS and WFC3 data were estimated in a 0.4$''$ radius aperture, on HAWK-I data in a 0.8$''$ radius aperture (taking into account the seeing of the data), and in a 1.2$''$ radius aperture for IRAC images." (Table 3 caption)
   - assumption: Sersic profile for GALFIT modelling  
  > "Noting the overlapping foreground object, in order to extract the photometry of MACS0600-arc we model each source with a Sersic profile using GALFIT (cite: GALFIT) on the WFC3-NIR and IRAC images" (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - fitting: GALFIT Sersic profile fitting  
  > "Noting the overlapping foreground object, in order to extract the photometry of MACS0600-arc we model each source with a Sersic profile using GALFIT (cite: GALFIT) on the WFC3-NIR and IRAC images" (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - validation: Tested GALFIT photometry against standard SExtractor MAG_AUTO photometry on ACS F606W and F814W images where only the foreground source is detected (m_F606W^GALFIT = 26.55 +/- 0.35 vs m_F606W^SExtractor = 26.72 +/- 0.08).  
  > "We tested our method on the ACS data where only the foreground source is detected and compared the GALFIT photometry with that derived using the standard MAG_AUTO photometry in single image mode with SExtractor (cite: SExtractor)." (Observation and Data Analysis); "In F606W, our extracted photometry ($\rm{m_{F606W}^{GALFIT}=26.55\pm0.35}$) is in excellent agreement with the *SExtractor* photometry ($\rm{m_{F606W}^{SExtractor}=26.72\pm0.08}$)." (Observation and Data Analysis)
3. **Estimate photometric redshifts**: Template-based SED fitting using HYPERZ for the foreground source and multiple images.  
  > "We determine the photometric redshift of the foreground source from a template based SED-fitting method with HYPERZ (cite: Hyperz)." (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - software: HYPERZ
   - parameter: 0 < z < 8 (or z in [0.0:3.0] for foreground); 0.0 < A_v < 3.0 mag  
  > "We then searched for a solution between $0<z<8$, with an extinction range $\rm{0.0 < A_v < 3.0}$ mag assuming a Calzetti reddening law (cite: Calzetti2000)." (Observation and Data Analysis); "The colour of the foreground source is similar to the colour of cluster members, we therefore allow a redshift range of $z \in$ \[0.0:3.0\]." (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF, solar metallicity templates from Bruzual & Charlot (2003), empirical SEDs from Coleman et al. (1980), starburst templates from Kinney et al. (1996), and Calzetti (2000) reddening law  
  > "We used the standard list of templates including evolutionary SEDs with Chabrier IMF (cite: Chabrier2003) and solar metallicity from (cite: Bruzual2003), empirical SEDs built by (cite: Coleman1980) and two starburst galaxies from (cite: Kinney1996)." (Observation and Data Analysis); "We then searched for a solution between $0<z<8$, with an extinction range $\rm{0.0 < A_v < 3.0}$ mag assuming a Calzetti reddening law (cite: Calzetti2000)." (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - fitting: Template-based SED fitting  
  > "We determine the photometric redshift of the foreground source from a template based SED-fitting method with HYPERZ (cite: Hyperz)." (Observation and Data Analysis)
   - validation: Consistent with spectroscopic redshift  
  > "Allowing a larger redshift range, the MACS0600-arc data provides a solution at $z_{photo}$=$6.68^{+0.24}_{-0.67}$ with an extinction $\rm{A_v}$=0.5mag, broadly consistent with the spectroscopic value (Figure 2)." (Observation and Data Analysis)
4. **Construct cluster lens models, identify multiple images, and compute magnifications and UV-FIR offset**: Build three independent cluster mass models using GLAFIC, LENSTOOL, and Light-Traces-Mass (LTM) constrained by 9 other secure multiply-imaged systems (5 with MUSE redshifts) to predict multiple image positions and magnification factors, and measure UV-to-FIR spatial offset.  
  > "The three models used here are produced using GLAFIC (cite: GLAFIC), LENSTOOL (cite: Lenstool), and Light-Traces-Mass (LTM; (cite: Zitrin2015))." (Lens models and multiple images); "Four modellers (MO, JR, GC, AZ) voted on the choice of multiply-imaged systems which, besides the $z\sim6$ system, included nine other systems considered as secure." (Lens models and multiple images); "We also take benefit from the detection of the dust continuum in two of the multiple images (z6.3 and the arc) to estimate the physical offset between the FIR continuum and the UV continuum." (Lens models and multiple images)
   - software: GLAFIC, LENSTOOL, LTM
   - parameter: Three to four dark matter halos for parametric models  
  > "The parametric modeling of MACS0600 typically included three to four dark matter halos and, whereas for the LTM models, the masses of several bright cluster members were modeled independently." (Lens models and multiple images)
   - assumption: Pseudo isothermal elliptical mass distributions and empirical scaling relations for parametric models; dark matter follows smoothed luminosity-weighted galaxy distribution for LTM  
  > "The first two models can be regarded as parametric, in the sense that both the galaxies and their dark matter halos are described using independent analytic forms (e.g., combinations of pseudo isothermal elliptical mass distributions) and adopt empirical scaling relations for cluster members." (Lens models and multiple images); "The LTM method is similar, but here the dark matter is assumed to follow the light distribution and thus is modeled as a smoothed version of the luminosity-weighted galaxy distribution." (Lens models and multiple images)
   - fitting: Minimization using multiple image constraints; source position adjusted in LENSTOOL/GLAFIC to match [CII] 158 um line flux ratio; arc delensed in LTM  
  > "All models are minimized using available multiple image constraints to find the best-fit solution and its associated errors." (Lens models and multiple images); "The two parametric models (LENSTOOL and GLAFIC) extracted values by first planting a compact source in the expected position, and adjusting its exact position with respect to the critical curves to match the observed \[CII\] 158 $\mu$m line flux ratio." (Lens models and multiple images)
   - validation: Checked consistency of WFC3 colors (F125W-F160W = 0.32 +/- 0.15), F160W surface brightness (21.9 +/- 0.3), 1.2 mm continuum flux ratio, and [C II] 158 um detections across multiple images.  
  > "Colors measured on WFC3 images are similar with averaged values of F125W-F160W = 0.32$\pm$0.15." (Lens models and multiple images); "All images have consistent surface brightness in the WFC3 images (e.g. the averaged value on F160W is 21.9$\pm$0.3) reinforcing the multiple images system hypothesis." (Lens models and multiple images); "The 1.2mm flux ratio between 1.3b and the arc is 0.62$^{+0.33}_{-0.24}$ in excellent agreement with ratios observed in the near infrared and with the predicted lens-model magnifications (see below)." (Lens models and multiple images)
5. **Confirm spectroscopic redshift and measure Ly-alpha upper limit with Gemini/GMOS**: Reduce Gemini-North/GMOS spectra using Gemini IRAF package, extract spectrum in 1.5 x seeing diameter aperture, bin by factor of 30 in spectral direction, fit with stacked spectrum of 81 LBGs via chi^2 minimization, and inspect 2D spectra for Ly-alpha emission.  
  > "We reduced the data using the Gemini IRAF package, as recommended by the instrument team." (Spectroscopic follow-up); "We extracted the spectrum in a 1.5$\times$seeing diameter aperture and a break is clearly identified between 850nm and 900nm. To improve the signal, we binned the spectra in the spectral direction with a binning factor of 30 (see Figure 1)." (Spectroscopic follow-up); "We determined the spectroscopic redshift by fitting our observed spectra with a stacked spectra coming from 81 LBGs (cite: Jones2012)." (Spectroscopic follow-up)
   - software: Gemini IRAF package
   - parameter: 1.5 x seeing diameter extraction aperture; spectral binning factor of 30; redshift search range z = 0 to z = 8  
  > "We extracted the spectrum in a 1.5$\times$seeing diameter aperture and a break is clearly identified between 850nm and 900nm. To improve the signal, we binned the spectra in the spectral direction with a binning factor of 30 (see Figure 1)." (Spectroscopic follow-up); "We searched for the best-fit using a $\chi^2$ minimization technique over a redshift range between $z=$0 and $z=8$." (Spectroscopic follow-up)
   - assumption: Magnification factor mu = 31 for intrinsic EW upper limit  
  > "no emission line is detected in any of the 3 images allowing us to place a firm upper limit on the Ly-$\alpha$ luminosity in this $z\sim$6 system at L(Ly-$\alpha$, 2$\sigma$) $<$ 2.4$\times$10$^{42}$ erg/s, corresponding to a rest-frame EW of $<$113Å /$\mu$ and EW<3.6Å assuming a magnification factor of $\mu$=31." (Spectroscopic follow-up)
   - fitting: chi^2 minimization against stacked LBG template over 0 <= z <= 8  
  > "We searched for the best-fit using a $\chi^2$ minimization technique over a redshift range between $z=$0 and $z=8$." (Spectroscopic follow-up)
   - validation: Ruled out z ~ 1.1 Balmer break interloper by non-detection of [OII]3727,3729 doublet; consistent with photometric redshift and ALMA [C II]158 um line.  
  > "The best fit is found at $z_{spec}$=6.19$^{+0.06}_{-0.16}$, consistent with both the photometric redshift and the \[CII\]158$\mu$m emission line detected in the ALMA data." (Spectroscopic follow-up); "One can also argue that if the break seen in the Gemini data is the Balmer Break at $z\sim$1.1 instead of the Lyman Break at $z\sim$6, we would have detected the \[OII\]3727,3729 doublet, which makes the high-redshift identification for this system robust." (Spectroscopic follow-up)
6. **Derive intrinsic physical properties, galaxy size, stellar age, and SN dust production rate**: Fit SEDs with MAGPHYS for dust-detected images (arc and z6.3b) and BAGPIPES for remaining images, measure UV slope beta, compute intrinsic size on z6.4 following Oesch et al. (2010), estimate stellar age from Balmer Break amplitude (F160W vs 4.5 um), and compute dust mass produced per core-collapse supernova.

## Qualitative findings

- MACS0600-z6 is a quintuply-imaged lensed galaxy at z = 6.07 behind MACSJ0600.1-2008, with 1.2 mm dust continuum detected in the arc (~4 sigma) and image z6.3b (~3.5 sigma).  
  > "We report the discovery of an intrinsically faint, quintuply-imaged, dusty galaxy MACS0600-z6 at a redshift $z=$6.07 viewed through the cluster MACSJ0600.1-2008 ($z$=0.46)." (Abstract); "In the ALMA data, z6.3b also shows a faint dust detection with a S/N$\sim$3.5 ($\rm{f_{peak}=226\pm63 \mu Jy.beam^{-1}}$ as measured with imfit)." (Lens models and multiple images)
- The dust-to-stellar mass ratio and inferred dust production per supernova in MACS0600-z6 appear larger than in equivalent lower-redshift and local sources, though adopting a higher dust temperature would reduce the inferred dust mass.  
  > "Strikingly, the dust production rate in this relatively young galaxy appears to be larger than that observed for equivalent, lower redshift sources." (Abstract); "For a temperature of 85K, the estimated dust mass would decrease to $M_{dust}$=6.3$^{+1.6}_{3.6}$$\times$10$^6$M$_{\odot}$, and its dust-to-stellar mass ratio will be comparable to previous findings at high-redshift." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- The IRX-beta relation for MACS0600-z6 and most z ~ 6 galaxies follows the Calzetti attenuation law.  
  > "Assuming a dust temperature of 35K to estimate the IR luminosity of MACS0600-z6 and the other $z\sim$6 galaxies, Figure 1 shows that most $z\sim$6 galaxies follow the Calzetti law." (Physical Properties)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Strong gravitational lensing by the foreground cluster MACSJ0600.1-2008 observed in the ALMA Lensing Cluster Survey provides the high magnification (mu >= 30) needed to probe dust and physical properties in an intrinsically faint, sub-luminous galaxy at z >= 6.  
  > "We use a detailed mass model of the cluster and infer a magnification $\mu\gtrsim$30 for the most magnified image of this galaxy, which provides an unprecedented opportunity to probe the physical properties of a sub-luminous galaxy at the end of cosmic reionisation." (Abstract); "With current facilities, the only way to characterise the physical properties of this sub-luminous population is to use the magnification afforded by gravitational lensing (for a review see (cite: Kneib2011))." (Introduction)
- Why this method: A 2'' uv-taper was applied to ALMA data to maximise detection of faint sources; GALFIT Sersic modelling was used to deblend overlapping foreground and lensed sources and account for PSF differences; three independent lens models were used to assess magnification and positional uncertainties.  
  > "A 2$''$ *uv*-taper was used to maximise the detection of faint sources." (Observation and Data Analysis); "Noting the overlapping foreground object, in order to extract the photometry of MACS0600-arc we model each source with a Sersic profile using GALFIT (cite: GALFIT) on the WFC3-NIR and IRAC images, which accounts for PSF difference between HST, VLT and Spitzer." (Observation and Data Analysis); "To determine the lensing configuration and hence the intrinsic properties of this source and the associated uncertainties, we have utilised several different lens models from within the ALCS team." (Lens models and multiple images)
- Alternative: Low/intermediate-redshift interloper (e.g., z ~ 1.1 Balmer break instead of z ~ 6 Lyman break) rejected because the [OII]3727,3729 doublet would have been detected in the Gemini/GMOS spectrum.  
  > "One can also argue that if the break seen in the Gemini data is the Balmer Break at $z\sim$1.1 instead of the Lyman Break at $z\sim$6, we would have detected the \[OII\]3727,3729 doublet, which makes the high-redshift identification for this system robust." (Spectroscopic follow-up)
- Alternative: Image z6.3a excluded from the genuine quintuply-imaged system and mean physical property averages because it is not quintuply imaged and does not contribute significantly to the flux compared to z6.3b.  
  > "It is unclear whether image z6.3a is truly part of the lensed source despite not being quintuply imaged as well, and – while we acknowledge the possibility that z6.3a may be related – we consider only z6.3b a genuine multiple image of this system." (Lens models and multiple images); "Excluding image MACS0600-z6.3a, which does not seem to contribute significantly to the flux compared to MACS0600-z6.3b, the mean stellar mass is 2.9$^{+11.5}_{-2.3}\times$10$^9$M$_{\odot}$" (Physical Properties)
- Alternative: Higher dust temperature (T_dust = 85 K) explored as an alternative to T_dust = 30 K, showing that it would reduce the dust mass and make the dust-to-stellar mass ratio comparable to previous high-z findings.  
  > "In view of this, we explore whether a higher dust temperature would change our conclusions. For a temperature of 85K, the estimated dust mass would decrease to $M_{dust}$=6.3$^{+1.6}_{3.6}$$\times$10$^6$M$_{\odot}$, and its dust-to-stellar mass ratio will be comparable to previous findings at high-redshift." (Discussion and Conclusion)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Absence of WFC3 coverage at the position predicted by GLAFIC and LTM models makes the clear identification of the fifth image (z6.5) challenging, with ~12'' scatter among models.  
  > "While all models reassuringly agree on the positions of the five multiple images, there is larger scatter ($\sim$12$''$) in the predicted location of the fifth image, z6.5." (Lens models and multiple images); "The absence of WFC3 data at the expected position makes difficult the clear identification of the image." (Table 3 caption)
- Limitation (authors): Arc magnification estimates are likely lower limits; alternative methods next to the critical curves yield mu ~ 150-200 with larger uncertainty.  
  > "We note that the magnifications for the arc are likely lower limits. If the magnification for the arc is instead estimated by directly averaging the magnification next to the critical curves, or alternatively by comparing the area of the arc to its delensed size, values around $\mu\sim150-200$ are obtained, although such estimates are typically more uncertain." (Lens models and multiple images)
- Limitation (authors): Supernova dust yield estimate is highly uncertain due to unknown SN production rates in early metal-poor galaxies and dust destruction/ejection processes.  
  > "Although this estimate is highly uncertain given the uncertain production rate of SNe in early metal poor galaxies and possible dust destruction and ejection processes in low mass galaxies at high redshift, it is perhaps somewhat larger than that derived locally" (Physical Properties)
- Limitation (authors): Single sub-mm continuum wavelength (1.2 mm) leaves the dust temperature unconstrained below the Herschel 85 K upper limit; deeper multi-band ALMA observations (e.g., Band 5) are needed.  
  > "Further ALMA observations at longer wavelength (e.g. in band 5) are needed to constrain the dust temperature and to refine our dust mass estimates." (Discussion and Conclusion)
