## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract normalized absorption spectra and measure integrated optical depths and column densities**: Extract spectral line profiles from the continuum-peak pixels of standard pipeline datacubes, normalize by the continuum flux in the respective spectral window continuum map, integrate optical depth over the HCO+ line span (or 3 km/s for non-detections), and apply linear conversion factors to derive column densities.  
  > "the new ALMA absorption line profiles were taken from the continuum-peak pixels of the spectral line datacubes from the standard pipeline product for the various species, and they were normalized by the continuum flux in the appropriate continuum map" (2.1. Absorption line spectra); "the 3σ values measured over the span of the HCO [+] line, when given. In the five cases when HCO [+] was not observed or not detected, they are given over an arbitrary 3 km s [−][1] interval." (Section 2.1)
   - parameter: N(C2H) = 2.71 x 10^13 cm^-2 Y_CCH (with observed F=2-1 and F=1-0 components having 5/8 of total line strength)  
  > "The observed components included 5/8 of the total integrated line strength of the N=1-0 J=3/2-1/2 transition. We define the total as Υ CCH . N(C 2 H) = 2.71 × 10 [13] cm [−][2] Υ CCH" (Section 2.1)
   - parameter: N(HCN) = 1.89 x 10^12 cm^-2 Y_HCN  
  > "N(HCN) = 1.89 × 10 [12] cm [−][2] Υ HCN where Υ HCN is the integrated optical depth summed over all three hyperfine components." (Section 2.1)
   - parameter: N(HCO+) = 1.10 x 10^12 cm^-2 Y_HCO+ and N(H2) = N(HCO+)/3 x 10^-9  
  > "N(H 2 ) = N(HCO [+] )/3 × 10 [−][9] and N(HCO [+] ) = 1.10 × 10 [12] cm [−][2] Υ HCO+" (Section 2.3)
   - assumption: Permanent dipole moment of mu = 3.89 Debye for HCO+ and no collisional excitation above CMB  
  > "which corresponds to a permanent dipole moment of mu = 3.89 Debye for HCO+." (2.3. Atomic and molecular hydrogen column densities); "Deriving N(HCO+) in the limit of no collisional excitation is a good approximation." (6. Summary)
   - assumption: LTE hyperfine line strengths in 4:2:2:1 ratio for HCO  
  > "HCO is a widely distributed species whose lowest rotational transition is spread over four hyperfine components with LTE line strengths in a 4:2:2:1 ratio." (3.3. HCO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Confirmed in Section 5.1 that HCO+ excitation temperature is ~2.76 K, validating the limit of no collisional excitation.  
  > "Deriving N(HCO [+] ) in the limit of no collisional excitation is a good approximation." (Section 6)
2. **Form pure emission spectra of HCO+, 12CO, and 13CO from IRAM 30m observations**: Average four HCO+ emission spectra displaced by 1.2 HPBW (30'') in the four cardinal directions from the continuum source to avoid absorption contamination; average five positions (on-source and four displaced by 1 HPBW = 22'') for 12CO and 13CO on the main-beam antenna temperature scale.  
  > "The HCO+ profiles we show are averages of spectra displaced by 1.2 HPBW = 30'' from the continuum source in each of the four cardinal directions, as discussed in Section F." (2.2. Emission line spectra); "The spectra toward and displaced 1 HPBW = 22'' from the continuum differ little so we averaged all five positions to form the 12CO and 13CO spectra shown in Figure 6." (Appendix F: Forming emission spectra in the presence of a continuum background.)
   - parameter: Channel spacing 48.8 kHz (0.164 km s^-1) and spectral resolution 97.7 kHz (0.33 km s^-1) at 89.1885 GHz  
  > "The IRAM 30m emission profiles have a channel spacing and approximate spectral resolution of 48.8 and 97.7 kHz, or 0.164 and 0.33 km s^-1 at the HCO+ rest frequency." (2.2. Emission line spectra)
   - assumption: Emission displaced by 22''-30'' is representative of the gas along the continuum sight line  
  > "Although the averaged CO and HCO+ spectra discussed in Section 5 were derived over slightly regions they (as earlier) seem to present a coherent picture of the HCO+ excitation." (Appendix F: Forming emission spectra in the presence of a continuum background.)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared CO spectra toward and displaced by 22'' from the continuum source and found they differ little.  
  > "The spectra toward and displaced 1 HPBW = 22'' from the continuum differ little so we averaged all five positions to form the 12CO and 13CO spectra shown in Figure 6." (Appendix F: Forming emission spectra in the presence of a continuum background.)
3. **Analyze Galactic-scale distributions and power-law abundance relations across the sample**: Perform power-law linear regressions in log-log space of E_B-V, N(HI), N(H2), and W_CO against sin(|b|), and of molecular column densities against E_B-V, N(HCO+), and N(C2H).  
  > "The gas and dust column densities are plotted against sin(|b|), which is proportional to the nominal path length through a plane-parallel stratified Galactic disk. The numerical values derived from these plots are given in Table 1." (3.1. Galactic-scale properties); "a and b are fits to log (value of the y-species) = a+b×log (value of the x-species), and r is the correlation coefficient." (Table 1 footnote)
   - parameter: N(HI) = 1.823 x 10^18 cm^-2 W_HI  
  > "in the optically thin limit as N(HI) = 1.823 × 10 [18] cm [−][2] W HI" (Section 2.3)
   - assumption: Optically thin 21cm HI emission  
  > "in the optically thin limit as N(HI) = 1.823 × 10 [18] cm [−][2] W HI" (Section 2.3)
   - fitting: Linear regression of log(y) = a + b log(x)  
  > "a and b are fits to log (value of the y-species) = a+b×log (value of the x-species), and r is the correlation coefficient." (Table 2 footnote)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Decompose HCO+, HCN, and C2H absorption profiles into Gaussian kinematic components**: Simultaneously fit all kinematic and hyperfine components by varying central optical depth, central velocity, and FWHM to minimize deviations from the observed line/continuum ratio exp(-tau), using HCO+ decomposition as an initial template for HCN and C2H, and match components across species.  
  > "For each species, the decomposition simultaneously fit all kinematic components and hyperfine components by varying the central optical depth, central velocity, and profile FWHM while minimizing deviations from the observed line/continuum ratio exp(-tau)." (4. Component decomposition); "We used the results for HCO+ as an initial template for HCN and C2H in decompositions that considered all observed kinematic and hyperfine components." (4. Component decomposition)
   - parameter: Velocity matching criterion: separation in central velocity < 1/3 of the FWHM of the narrower component  
  > "The matched components are those for which the separation in central velocity is smaller than one-third of the FWHM of the narrower of the possible pair of components." (4. Component decomposition)
   - assumption: Hyperfine components of HCN and C2H have optical depth ratios proportional to their intrinsic LTE line strengths  
  > "The hyperfine components of HCN and C2H were fit assuming optical depth ratios proportional to their intrinsic line strengths." (4. Component decomposition)
   - fitting: Least-squares minimization of deviations from exp(-tau) over central optical depth, velocity, and FWHM  
  > "varying the central optical depth, central velocity, and profile FWHM while minimizing deviations from the observed line/continuum ratio exp(-tau)." (4. Component decomposition)
   - validation: Purged ambiguous weak broad components; verified matched components account for 88%-98% of total column density.  
  > "the component list was therefore ruthlessly purged to achieve a minimum set of matched lines. As noted below, the matched sets account for the vast majority of the aggregate optical depth in every species" (4. Component decomposition)
5. **Compare HCO+ and CO in absorption and emission to derive excitation temperature, gas density, and X_CO**: Interpolate ALMA absorption and IRAM 30m emission profiles onto common spectral grids for channel-by-channel comparison, and compare integrated profile quantities against weak-excitation radiative transfer models.  
  > "In one format, ALMA and IRAM 30m profiles were interpolated onto common spectral grids for a channel-to-channel comparison of the new results between species and tracers." (5. HCO+ and CO in absorption and emission); "The right panel of Figure 7 plots the integrated HCO+ emission brightness and optical depth for the new and old results together with iso-density lines derived from the weak excitation limit" (5.1. HCO+ compared in absorption and emission)
   - assumption: Weak excitation limit (including electron collisions in diffuse gas with ionization fraction > 10^-4)  
  > "When the collisional excitation of a species is sufficiently far below thermalization, all energy deposited in the rotation ladder by collisions escapes the medium, regardless of the optical depth." (5.1. HCO+ compared in absorption and emission); "The rate coefficient is 30-100 times larger under diffuse gas conditions where the ionization fraction exceeds 10^-4" (5.1. HCO+ compared in absorption and emission)
   - fitting: Regression line fit of HCO+ emission brightness vs optical depth  
  > "For most sources, the excitation temperature inferred from the regression line passing through 0.015 K emission brightness at tau = 1 is 2.758 K." (5.1. HCO+ compared in absorption and emission)
   - validation: Compared derived densities n(H) = 50-200 cm^-3 with independent C2 excitation studies by Neufeld et al. (2024).  
  > "Almost all sources fall in the range n(H) = 50 - 200 cm^-3, with n(H) = 500 cm^-3 toward J0502. Similar densities in diffuse molecular gas were inferred by Neufeld et al. (2024) from a study of excitation in C2." (5.1. HCO+ compared in absorption and emission)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA's data-gathering capabilities allowed simultaneous observation of C2H, HCN, CS, and HCO absorption alongside HCO+ and CO during surveys of the dark neutral medium in Chamaeleon and the Galactic anticenter.  
  > "In the course of these observations along 13 sight lines in Chamaeleon and 33 sight lines in the Galactic anticenter, we also used the prodigious data-gathering abilities of ALMA to observe absorption from other species" (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: HCO+ emission was observed at positions displaced by 30'' from the background continuum source because emitted and absorbed fluxes of HCO+ are comparable at single-dish telescopes, preventing pure emission measurements directly on-source.  
  > "This was necessary because the emitted and absorbed fluxes are comparable, and no pure emission spectrum was obtained toward the continuum at the 30m telescope." (2.2. Emission line spectra)
- How they decided it was enough: Stopped observing CS in the Galactic anticenter after failing to detect CS along all 13 sight lines in Chamaeleon within the integration times that sufficed for HCO+ and C2H.  
  > "Far fewer (25) sight lines were observed in total in CS after our failure to detect CS absorption along the 13 sight lines in Chamaeleon within the integration times that sufficed to detect HCO+ and C2H." (1. Introduction); "No absorption from CS was reliably detected in our observations of Chamaeleon. We therefore did not observe CS in the Galactic anticenter." (2.1. Absorption line spectra)
- Limitation (authors): All directions in the Galactic anticenter were observed for the same integration time, resulting in strongly varying rms line-to-continuum sensitivities depending on background source flux.  
  > "All directions in the Galactic anticenter were observed for the same time, which resulted in strongly varying rms line/continuum sensitivities." (2.1. Absorption line spectra)
- Limitation (authors): The sample is an inhomogeneous amalgamation of datasets taken over 30 years with different selection criteria and is biased toward higher reddening and molecular gas content than the Galactic average.  
  > "The sample is an amalgamation of datasets that were taken over the course of nearly 30 years for various purposes, at instruments that overlap only partially in their sky coverage." (3. The sample); "The sample values of E B−V lie above the Galactic average at all latitudes" (Section 3.1)
- Limitation (authors): Integration times were too short to provide useful upper limits on CS chemistry at E_B-V <= 0.5 mag.  
  > "The integration times needed to study the other molecules were in general too brief to provide much useful information on CS beyond that noted in the Introduction." (Section 3.4)
- Limitation (authors): Weak broad absorption components introduce ambiguities when matching Gaussian components across species.  
  > "Ambiguities are introduced in the case of weak broader components, and the component list was therefore ruthlessly purged to achieve a minimum set of matched lines." (4. Component decomposition)
- Limitation (authors): Averaged CO and HCO+ emission spectra were derived over slightly different spatial regions offset from the pencil-beam absorption sight line.  
  > "Although the averaged CO and HCO+ spectra discussed in Section 5 were derived over slightly regions they (as earlier) seem to present a coherent picture of the HCO+ excitation." (Appendix F: Forming emission spectra in the presence of a continuum background.)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Spatial beam mismatch between pencil-beam interferometric absorption against compact extragalactic sources and single-dish IRAM 30m emission averaged at 22''-30'' offsets.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Constant N(HCO+)/N(H2) = 3 x 10^-9 conversion factor is assumed across all diffuse sight lines to infer N(H2).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Sample inhomogeneity and variable continuum flux densities mean detection statistics mix intrinsic astrochemical thresholds with instrumental sensitivity limits.
