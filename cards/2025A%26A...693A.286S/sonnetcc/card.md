- Question: Do disks around intermediate mass T Tauri (IMTT) stars, the precursors of Herbig stars, differ from Herbig disks in dust mass, gas mass, gas-to-dust ratio and radii, and what does this imply for planet formation around intermediate mass stars

## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and self-calibrate the 12-m data**: Phase self-calibration on continuum, rounds repeated until peak S/N stops improving, then applied to line spectral windows; single amplitude round only for SU Aur; no self-cal for ACA  
  > "The 12-m array data were phase self-calibrated based on the continuum data for multiple rounds up until the peak signal-to-noise did not improve compared to the previous round." (Data selection and reduction); "The resulting calibration table was applied to the line spectral windows by using the `applycal` task." (Data selection and reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: 1 to 3 rounds in most cases, solution interval starting at inf and decreasing by factors of two; S/N gain factor 1.7 on average; applied for 12 targets  
  > "In most cases one to three rounds were done" (Data selection and reduction); "then decreasing by factors of two, which increases the signal-to-noise ratio by a factor of 1.7 on average" (Data selection and reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Stop criterion is peak S/N no longer improving between rounds  
  > "up until the peak signal-to-noise did not improve compared to the previous round" (Data selection and reduction)
2. **Image continuum and CO lines**: Continuum imaged with multifrequency synthesis; lines imaged after uvcontsub with multiscale clean; velocity resolution varies per dataset; archive products used for mosaics  
  > "For the continuum data, the imaging was done using multifrequency synthesis." (Data selection and reduction); "Different velocity resolutions were used depending on the dataset." (Data selection and reduction)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: multiscale scales 0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15 times the beam in pixels (~5 pixels)  
  > "For the imaging we used `multiscale` using 0 (point source), 1, 2, 5, 10 and 15 times the size of the beam in pixels ($\sim5$ pixels) as the size of the scales." (Data selection and reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Measure disk integrated fluxes and 90% radii**: Increasing aperture size to find the maximum disk integrated flux, which also returns a disk size; Keplerian masks for line emission  
  > "To obtain the disk integrated fluxes, we use the same method as described in and which uses an increasing aperture size to ascertain what the maximum amount of disk integrated flux is." (Data selection and reduction); "This method also returns a size of the disk." (Data selection and reduction)
   - parameter: Keplerian mask velocities determined by eye so all emission is enclosed; a mask was applied even when inclination and PA were not available  
  > "The velocities of the Keplerian masks were determined by fitting by eye, such that all emission was enclosed." (Table of disk parameters (notes))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Derive dust masses from continuum flux**: Hildebrand (1983) relation, optically thin emission: M_dust = F_nu d^2 / (kappa_nu B_nu(T_dust))  
  > "using the relationship of (cite: Hildebrand1983) to directly relate the continuum emission to the dust mass, assuming optically thin emission" (Data selection and reduction)
   - parameter: kappa_nu = 10 cm^2/g at 1000 GHz scaled as nu^beta with beta = 1  
  > "such that it equals 10 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ at a frequency of 1000 GHz" (Data selection and reduction); "The power-law index $\beta$ is assumed to be equal to 1." (Data selection and reduction)
   - assumption: Optically thin emission  
  > "assuming optically thin emission" (Data selection and reduction)
   - assumption: T_dust scaled with stellar luminosity following Andrews et al. 2013 (25 K x (L/Lsun)^(1/4) in the paper's equation)  
  > "The dust temperature is given by the relationship of (cite: Andrews2013), which scales the dust temperature by the stellar luminosity in solar luminosities via" (Data selection and reduction)
   - assumption: Stellar luminosities from Valegard2021 used for the dust mass  
  > "The dust masses were recalculated using the stellar luminosities of (cite: Valegard2021)." (Table of disk fluxes and radii (notes))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note dust masses may be underestimated by up to a factor of ~7 if emission is not optically thin or opacity is wrong  
  > "Recent works have indeed shown that dust masses can be underestimated by up to a factor of $\sim7$ due to these effects" (Gas masses and radii)
5. **Build dust mass and radius distributions and compare with Herbig, Lupus, Upper Sco**: Cumulative distributions with lifelines (handles upper limits), log-normal fit by bootstrapping 10^5 samples, two-sample KS test with scipy  
  > "We can obtain cumulative distributions from the dust masses using the `lifelines` package (cite: DavidsonPilon2021)" (Dust masses and radii); "Using a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test using `scipy` (cite: 2020SciPy-NMeth), we can assess whether the distributions are sampled from the same underlying distribution." (Dust masses and radii)
   - software: lifelines, scipy
   - parameter: 10^5 bootstrap samples  
  > "using a bootstrapping method with $10^5$ samples" (Dust masses and radii)
   - assumption: Log-normal underlying distribution; five NOEMA Herbig disks added to the Herbig comparison sample  
  > "by fitting a log-normal distribution to the cumulative distributions" (Dust masses and radii)
   - fitting: Log-normal fit to cumulative distributions with bootstrap; priors/likelihood not stated  
  > "fitting a log-normal distribution to the cumulative distributions using a bootstrapping method with $10^5$ samples" (Dust masses and radii)
   - validation: KS test p-values for dust masses and radii; 1 sigma confidence band on the cumulative distribution  
  > "We obtain a $p$-value of 0.962" (Dust masses and radii)
6. **Test whether the Herbig DALI grid applies to IMTT disks**: Compare disk integrated line luminosities of the Herbig grid with two smaller IMTT grids, with and without accretion UV (blackbody at 10^4 K), raytraced at 100 pc  
  > "To test this, we compare the grid of models from to two smaller grids of models with the only difference being that an effective stellar temperature of 5500 K and luminosity of 10 $L_\odot$ are used for the IMTT disks" (Model setup); "The accretion was added to the stellar spectrum with a blackbody at 10$^4$ K." (Model setup)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: Teff 5500 K, L = 10 Lsun, accretion luminosity 1.5 Lsun, models run to chemical equilibrium at 1 Myr  
  > "The accretion luminosity is set as 1.5 $L_\odot$" (Model setup); "The models were run until chemical equilibrium was reached at 1 Myr." (Model setup)
   - assumption: CO isotopologue chemistry network of Miotello2016 (isotope-selective photodissociation, fractionation, self-shielding, freeze-out); viscous accreting disk parametric density with Gaussian vertical distribution  
  > "The models use a parametric description for the density structure, motivated by a viscous accreting disk" (Model setup)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Including accretion UV moves the IMTT luminosity distribution back to that of the Herbig grid, so the Herbig models are adopted  
  > "This shows that the Herbig disk models can be used for the IMTT models" (Model setup)
7. **Derive gas masses, gas-to-dust ratios and gas radii**: Compare observed CO isotopologue disk integrated luminosities to DALI models, select models by disk and stellar parameters, and read off the disk gas mass; combine with dust mass for gas-to-dust; fit gas vs dust radius relation  
  > "we obtain the disk mass after selecting models based on the disk and stellar parameters in Table" (Gas masses and radii); "Fitting a relationship through the scatter of the resolved disks we find a ratio of 2.8±0.2 between the gas and dust radius for the IMTT disks." (Gas masses and radii)
   - software: DALI
   - parameter: Upper limits for IMTT disks mostly reach the maximum disk mass in the model grid  
  > "Hence, most have upper limits of the maximum disk mass in the model grid." (Gas masses and radii)
   - assumption: ISM gas-to-dust ratio of 100 used as reference; planet-formation section assumes gas-to-dust 100  
  > "all disks show gas-to-dust ratios of at least the ISM value of 100 or higher" (Gas masses and radii)
   - fitting: Linear gas-vs-dust radius relation fit; method not detailed  
  > "Fitting a relationship through the scatter of the resolved disks" (Gas masses and radii)
   - validation: Radius ratio repeated after removing four disks with difficult radii (GW Ori, HT Lup, SR 21, SU Aur); compared with Herbig disks and Trapman2019 drift limit  
  > "Removing these four disks results in a gas to dust radius ratio of 2.6±0.3" (Gas masses and radii)
8. **Compare dust masses of group I and group II IMTT disks**: Split IMTTs by Meeus group from Valegard2021, build cumulative distributions and log-normal fits as in step 5  
  > "As (cite: Valegard2021) has classified the IMTTs into group I and group II based on the (cite: Meeus2001) classification" (Evolution of Herbig disks)
   - software: lifelines
   - fitting: Log-normal fit to cumulative distributions  
  > "Figure [11](#fig:cdf_dust_groups) presents the two cumulative mass distributions in the left panel, with the fitted log-normal probability distributions in the right panel." (Evolution of Herbig disks)
   - validation: Group I vs II compared with Herbig result; morphology check (cavities in group I)  
  > "For the resolved disks, the group I disks indeed show cavities" (Evolution of Herbig disks)
9. **Compare disk dust and gas masses to exoplanet masses**: Build exoplanet solid and envelope mass distribution from NASA Exoplanet Archive around stars with M >= 1.5 Msun, split by heavy-element mass relation, sum planets per star, normalise to occurrence rate 10.7%  
  > "To obtain a distribution for the intermediate mass stars, we only include the planets around stars with a minimum mass of 1.5 $M_\odot$." (Planet formation around intermediate mass stars); "The number of stars hosting at least one giant planet is 10.7% (cite: Wolthoff2022), hence we again normalize the distribution so that it adds up to 10.7%." (Planet formation around intermediate mass stars)
   - parameter: Both planet mass and minimum mass M sin i used; no detection method restriction  
  > "we set no detection method and use both the planet mass $M_p$ and minimum planet mass $M_p\sin(i)$ in our distribution" (Planet formation around intermediate mass stars)
   - assumption: Heavy-element mass of the planet equals the dust mass needed to form it (Thorngren2016 relation); gas-to-dust ratio of 100 when comparing with disk gas  
  > "so we use the relationship from (cite: Thorngren2016) to obtain a heavy-element mass of the planet, and we assume that this is the dust mass necessary to form the planet" (Planet formation around intermediate mass stars)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Mulders2021 efficiency argument  
  > "This is in line with what (cite: Mulders2021) finds." (Planet formation around intermediate mass stars)

## Qualitative findings

- Resolved IMTT disks all show single or multiple rings; most disks unresolved at the available resolution  
  > "The majority of disks are unresolved, but all disks which are resolved exhibit single or multiple rings." (Continuum images)
- Streamers and envelopes seen in CO for GW Ori, T Tau and SU Aur complicate radius and flux measurement  
  > "This extended emission does pose difficulties in obtaining a measure of the radii and integrated fluxes." (Gas images)
- Group I IMTT disks show cavities, group II are full disks  
  > "For the resolved disks, the group I disks indeed show cavities" (Evolution of Herbig disks)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: A significant fraction of the Valegard2021 IMTT sample has ALMA millimeter observations, so archival data were compiled  
  > "A significant fraction of the IMTTs compiled by (cite: Valegard2021) has ALMA millimeter observations." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Same dust-mass and DALI gas-mass methods as the Herbig studies so the populations can be compared directly; IMTT grid test justified reuse of Herbig models  
  > "This shows that the Herbig disk models can be used for the IMTT models" (Model setup); "But, as both the IMTTs and the Herbigs are biased in the same way, comparisons can still be made." (Data selection and reduction)
- Alternative: Two IMTT-specific DALI grids tested (with and without accretion UV) instead of directly reusing the Herbig grid; Herbig grid retained  
  > "One grid has accretion UV added and one grid does not." (Model setup)
- Alternative: T Tau CO data excluded because of complex structures  
  > "In this work, for T Tau, we only use the continuum data as the CO observations show very complex structures making it difficult to obtain a good measure of the disk mass and size." (Data selection and reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: Self-calibration rounds continued until peak S/N no longer improved  
  > "up until the peak signal-to-noise did not improve compared to the previous round" (Data selection and reduction)
- Limitation (authors): Upper limits on IMTT CO fluxes do not constrain gas masses; most limits reach the grid maximum  
  > "the upper limits obtained for the IMTT disks are not" (Gas masses and radii)
- Limitation (authors): Resolved-structure fraction lower than Herbig, attributed to lower spatial resolution; lack of deep observations  
  > "which is likely due to the lower spatial resolution of the data available in our work" (Continuum images); "showing a clear lack of deep observations towards IMTT disks" (Continuum images)
- Limitation (authors): Sample biased towards high accretors and brightest disks  
  > "the IMTT disks are likely biased towards the high accretors and brightest disks" (Introduction)
- Limitation (authors): Dust masses may be underestimated (optical depth, opacity), seen as high gas-to-dust ratios  
  > "This could indicate that either the assumption of optically thin emission does not hold, or that the assumed dust opacity is incorrect." (Gas masses and radii)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Dust masses use a single luminosity-scaled temperature and fixed kappa and beta; IMTTs and Herbigs may differ in temperature and grain properties, which could mask real differences
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Heterogeneous archival resolution and sensitivity: many disks unresolved, so R90 and flux completeness vary across the sample
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas masses rely on a Herbig DALI grid tested only on a small subset of IMTT stellar parameters, and upper limits are unconstraining, so the gas-mass comparison is weak
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Many CO fluxes are contaminated by foreground cloud, streamers or absorption; no systematic treatment is described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-rejection of the KS test with small samples is weak evidence for identical distributions
