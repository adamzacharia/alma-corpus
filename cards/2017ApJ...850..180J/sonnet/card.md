## Analysis recipe

1. **Build velocity fields**: Convert cubes to velocity; single-Gaussian fit per pixel with AIPS XGAUS, rejecting poor fits  
  > "The best velocity fields were found using the single Gaussian fitter in the AIPS task XGAUS." (3.1 Velocity Fields)
   - software: AIPS XGAUS, CASA
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Define ring widths**: Ring width between beam minor-axis FWHM/3 and /2; flat warp-less disk  
  > "an upper limit of the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the minor axis of the synthesized beam divided by three and a lower limit of the FWHM of the minor axis of the synthesized beam divided by two" (3.2 Ring Widths)
   - assumption: constant inclination and position angle, circular rotation  
  > "we will assume a constant inclination and position angle across each disk" (3.3 Rotation Fitting)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit tilted rings**: ROTCUR with two sets of initial estimates (RC1 by eye, GAUS from Gaussian fits) and all ring-number combinations (96 runs); fix centre, then vsys, then phi and i, finally fit only vc  
  > "By varying both parameter estimates and ring number, we thus consider the results of $16\times6=96$ full runs." (3.3 Rotation Fitting)
   - software: GIPSY ROTCUR
   - parameter: success: vc>3 dvc, d i<50 deg, d phi<50 deg  
  > "Success is defined as: $v_{\rm c}>3\times\delta v_{\rm c}$, $\delta i<50^{\circ}$, and $\delta \phi<50^{\circ}$" (3.3 Rotation Fitting)
   - fitting: Least-squares ROTCUR; vc uncertainty is max of ROTCUR error, channel width, and vc|sin i - sin(i±di)|  
  > "The uncertainty in rotational velocity for each ring is estimated as the maximum of the uncertainty by ROTCUR for that ring, the velocity width of a channel" (3.3 Rotation Fitting)
   - validation: Model velocity field from VELFI subtracted from data; normalized residual  
  > "The residual velocity values for each pixel are added in quadrature and normalized by the number of pixels" (3.3 Rotation Fitting)
4. **Derive dynamical masses**: M_dyn = v^2 r / G at largest ring and per ring; dark matter by subtracting dust, stellar and H2 mass  
  > "Using the radius and velocity of the largest ring of our models results in the values shown in Table 3." (4.1 Dynamical Mass)
   - assumption: circular orbits; Planck16 cosmology  
  > "An estimate of each source's dynamical mass could be found by assuming circular orbits" (4.1 Dynamical Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared to literature masses; systematic ~1 dex from mock tests  
  > "we estimate a systematic uncertainty of $\sim1$ dex" (4.1 Dynamical Mass)
5. **Constrain velocity dispersion**: GALMOD cubes for AzTEC/C159 and J1319 with fixed ROTCUR parameters, flat density, thin disk; SIMOBSERVE; compare integrated spectra channel by channel  
  > "By comparing the spectra on a channel-by-channel basis with the spectrum of our original data cube, we calculated the goodness of fit." (5.1 Implementation)
   - software: GIPSY GALMOD, CASA simobserve
   - parameter: sigma_v 5 to 145 km/s; scale height 0.2 x max radius  
  > "these were assumed to be thin ($0.2\times$maximum radius; (cite: lang17)) and flat ($\rho(r)=A$)" (5.1 Implementation)
   - fitting: Discrete grid, minimum chi-square  
  > "We note that a full Bayesian approach (*e.g.*, MultiNest; (cite: fero08)) is preferable to our discrete exploration of the parameter space" (GALMOD Modeling, Results)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Test method on mock M51 cubes**: Four mock cubes (low/medium resolution, 3 h/30 h) run through the same pipeline  
  > "Observations of each were simulated using both 3 hours on source (H3) and 30 hours on source (H30)." (Model Creation)
   - software: CASA simobserve, GIPSY ROTCUR
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with M51 literature rotation curve and inclination  
  > "We have scaled the rotation curve of (cite: oika14) (their Figure 1) and plotted it over our mock rotation curves" (Appendix, Discussion)

## Qualitative findings

- Low-luminosity galaxies show linear rotation curves likely due to beam smearing; luminous ones show tentative flattening  
  > "the mainly linear rotation curves are likely non-physical and actually caused by beam smearing effects" (7 Conclusion)
