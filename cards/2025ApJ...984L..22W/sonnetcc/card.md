## Analysis recipe

1. **Take fiducial exoALMA 12CO and 13CO cubes**: Use the fiducial exoALMA 12CO and 13CO J=3-2 images at 0.15 arcsec and 100 m/s, continuum-subtracted  
  > "we make use of the fiducial exoALMA images (cite: Teague_exoALMA) of the $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO $J=3-2$ emission lines (0$\hspace{1pt}.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$15; 100$\,$m$\,$s$^{-1}$, continuum-subtracted)" (Observations)
   - parameter: Emission below 5 sigma masked in the 12CO peak-intensity maps  
  > "Emission below 5$\sigma$ is masked." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Model Keplerian rotation and extract velocity residuals**: Model the emission channels with DISCMINER to get a smooth Keplerian model, extract centroid velocity, peak intensity and line width from data and model, then compute residuals  
  > "For both lines, the molecular emission channels were modeled with the DISCMINER package (cite: Izquierdo2021), which returns a smooth Keplerian model of the line intensity profile." (Observations)
   - software: DISCMINER
   - parameter: Line profiles of data and model fitted with a Gaussian, except a double-peaked bell profile for HD 34282  
  > "Here, this is done by fitting both the data and model line profiles with a Gaussian function, except for the HD 34282 disk for which a double-peaked bell profile is adopted." (Observations)
   - parameter: For HD 34282 the back-side contribution is subtracted when computing residuals  
  > "For the HD 34282 disk, the contribution from the back side has been subtracted in the computation of the residuals" (Velocity residuals)
   - assumption: The other three disks are close to face on and need no lower-surface removal  
  > "The other three disks are close to face on and do not require a removal of the lower surface." (Velocity residuals)
   - fitting: Gaussian line-profile fits to data and model  
  > "fitting both the data and model line profiles with a Gaussian function" (Observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Build and explore an analytical vortex model**: Vortex flow as streamlines of constant velocity around a centre, Gaussian radial velocity profile, elliptical streamlines in the vortex frame, projected along the line of sight  
  > "setting up the vortex flow as streamlines of constant velocity around a central position (r$_0$, $\phi_0$) in polar disk coordinates, with the rotation velocity of the vortex varying as a Gaussian in the radial direction" (Model description)
   - software: eddy
   - parameter: Maximum velocity v_max at radius r_v  
  > "The velocity in the vortex increases to a maximum velocity $v_{\mathrm{max}}$ at the radius $r_{\mathrm{v}}$." (Model description)
   - parameter: Aspect ratio chi (azimuthal major axis over radial minor axis) and Gaussian width omega_V  
  > "The vortex is further described by an aspect ratio $\chi$, the ratio of the major axis in the azimuthal direction to the minor axis in the radial direction." (Model description); "The width of the Gaussian profile is given by $\omega_{\mathrm{V}}$." (Model description)
   - assumption: Elliptical streamlines in the vortex frame, analogous to a shearing box  
  > "the streamlines are elliptical in the vortex frame in Cartesian coordinates, analogous to a shearing-box" (Model description)
   - assumption: Illustrative projection with i = 16 deg and PA = 243 deg (as HD 135344B)  
  > "using an inclination of $16\,\degree$ and position angle of $243\,\degree$ for the projection (same as for HD 135344B)" (Model description)
   - fitting: Implemented in eddy to fit a rotation map in disk regions with the main rotation pattern fixed; none of the runs converged  
  > "which allows to fit a Keplerian profile to a rotation map" (Parameter exploration); "However, none of these runs converged and resulted in very degenerate distributions." (Parameter exploration)
   - validation: Fits failed; conclusion that a simple model cannot constrain the vortex properties  
  > "we conclude that a simple model is not sufficient to constrain the properties of the vortex" (Parameter exploration)
4. **Run 2D hydrodynamical vortex simulations**: 2D PLUTO simulations of a vortex triggered at an MRI dead-zone inner edge and of a vortex at the edge of a gap opened by a 1 Jupiter-mass planet  
  > "2D numerical simulations with the grid-based code PLUTO (cite: Mignone2007), version 4.4" (Methods II: Simulations)
   - software: PLUTO
   - parameter: LINEAR reconstruction, 2nd order Runge-Kutta, HLLC solver, viscosity via Super-Time-Stepping, CFL 0.4  
  > "We use a PLUTO numerical setup with LINEAR reconstruction method, 2nd order Runge-Kutta time-stepping, the Harten-Lax-Van Leer Riemann (HLLC) solver" (Methods II: Simulations); "We set the Courant number (CFL) to 0.4." (Methods II: Simulations)
   - parameter: Domain 0.3-3.0 code units radially and 2 pi in azimuth; 1024 x 2048 cells (r, phi), uniform spacing  
  > "The simulation domain covers 0.3-3.0 code units of length in the radial direction, and $2 \pi$ rad in azimuth." (Disk Setup); "The simulations were run with a grid of $1024$ and $2048$ cells in $r$ and $\phi$, respectively" (Disk Setup)
   - parameter: alpha_DZ = 1e-5, alpha_0 = 1e-3, r_DZ = 1.2 r_0, Delta_DZ = H(r_DZ)  
  > "with $\alpha_{\mathrm{DZ}}=10^{-5}$ and $\alpha_{0}=10^{-3}$ being the $\alpha$-viscosity parameters in the MRI-dead and MRI-active regions, respectively." (Dead Zone Edge); "We set $\Delta_{\mathrm{DZ}}=H(r_{\mathrm{DZ}})$, corresponding to the standard run from (cite: Miranda2017) (see their Table 1)." (Dead Zone Edge)
   - parameter: Planet case: 1 M_J planet at r_p = r_0, smoothing length 60% of local scale height, mass taper over 30 orbits, constant alpha = 1e-5  
  > "In a second setup, we include a $1\,M_{\mathrm{J}}$ planet" (Planet); "a potential smoothing length set to $60\%$ of the local disk pressure scale height" (Planet); "we include a tapering such that the planet mass smoothly reaches its final value in $30$ planetary orbits" (Planet)
   - parameter: Scaling radius 70 au; snapshots at 400 orbits (dead zone) and 580 planetary orbits  
  > "We have used a scaling radius of 70 au to convert from code units to physical units" (Planet); "The simulation snapshots depicted correspond to 400 orbits measured at 1 code unit of length (dead zone) and 580 planetary orbits, respectively." (Planet)
   - assumption: 2D gas-only disk  
  > "We consider a two-dimensional disk in polar coordinates, including the evolution of the gas only." (Disk Setup)
   - assumption: Locally isothermal equation of state, H/r = 0.05 at r_0, sound speed proportional to r^-0.25, flared disk H proportional to r^1.25  
  > "Our simulations follow a locally isothermal equation of state $P=c_{\mathrm{s}}^2 \Sigma$" (Disk Setup); "The value of $c_{\mathrm{s},0}$ is set so that $H(r_0)/r_0=0.05$" (Disk Setup); "Our disk model results in a flared disk, with $H\propto r^{1.25}$." (Disk Setup)
   - assumption: No disk self-gravity  
  > "The effects of disk self-gravity are not taken into account in our models." (Disk Setup)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Make mock 12CO velocity residuals of the dead-zone vortex**: Post-process the dead-zone simulation with RADMC-3D into synthetic 12CO channel maps of a flat disk with each source's geometry, vortex placed at the dust asymmetry, convolved with a 0.15 arcsec beam, and compute line-of-sight velocity maps  
  > "we post-processed the dead zone simulation with RADMC-3D (cite: Dullemond2012) and computed mock images of a flat disk model." (Results); "we convolved the synthetic data cubes with a 0$\hspace{1pt}.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$15 Gaussian beam, and computed the line-of-sight velocity maps using BETTERMOMENTS (cite: bettermoments)" (Results)
   - software: RADMC-3D, bettermoments
   - parameter: Sub-Keplerian background subtracted from the azimuthal velocity input to give the expected residuals  
  > "we subtracted the sub-Keplerian background from the azimuthal velocity used as an input in the radiative transfer model" (Results)
   - parameter: Synthetic 12CO channel maps by ray-tracing at the spectral resolution of the data  
  > "We then created synthetic $^{12}$CO channel maps via ray-tracing with the same spectral resolution as the data." (Results)
   - parameter: Source geometry used and vortex matched radially and azimuthally to the dust asymmetries  
  > "we took the geometry of the sources into account and then matched the radial and azimuthal location of the vortex with the dust asymmetries seen in the data" (Results)
   - assumption: Flat disk; vortex magnitude may be underestimated  
  > "Moreover, we assume a flat disk and the magnitude of the vortex may be significantly underestimated." (Unconvolved model images)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Compare observed residuals with the vortex mock**: Visual comparison of 12CO and 13CO velocity residuals with the dead-zone vortex mock residuals, with dust continuum contours marking the crescents  
  > "Comparing the data and model, it becomes apparent that most disks do not show a clear vortex kinematic signature around the dust crescents as predicted by the simulation." (Results); "The continuum emission is overlaid on the data to highlight the location of the dust crescents." (Results)
   - parameter: Continuum contours placed equally between 3 sigma and the continuum peak  
  > "The continuum emission (top row) is overlaid as contours, placed equally between 3$\sigma$ and the continuum peak" (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compare gas residual spirals with NIR scattered-light spirals**: Polar-deprojected CO residuals (centroid velocity, peak intensity, line width) shown beside polar-deprojected scattered light for HD 135344B and MWC 758, with NIR spirals overlaid as contours  
  > "we present the polar-deprojected CO residuals for the centroid velocity, peak intensity, and line width alongside the polar-deprojected scattered light emission" (Spiral features)
   - parameter: Deprojection uses the inclination and position angle of Table 1  
  > "The deprojection is performed by using the inclination and position angle" (Spiral features)
   - assumption: Simple deprojection is sufficient because the disks are face-on  
  > "This is sufficient due to the face-on nature of the disk." (Spiral features)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- None of the four disks shows a distinctive vortex kinematic signature around the dust crescents  
  > "we find that none of the sources show a distinctive vortex signature around the dust crescents in the kinematics." (abstract)
- HD 135344B shows a feature similar to the simulation predictions and is the most promising target for follow-up  
  > "HD 135344B exhibits a prominent feature similar to the predictions from the simulations, thus making this the most promising target for sensitive follow-up studies" (abstract)
- Including a planet adds its own Doppler flip and much stronger spirals that mask the vortex pattern  
  > "If a planet is included in the disk, it creates its own Doppler-flip signature and much stronger spiral features, which overlay on the pattern associated with the vortex, thus making it much harder to distinguish." (Results)
- Inner velocity-residual spirals align somewhat with NIR spirals in HD 135344B and MWC 758  
  > "For both disks, the inner spirals seen in the velocity residuals appear to align somewhat with the NIR spirals." (Spiral features)
- Dust asymmetries are about the size of the beam, so vortex signatures may be unresolved  
  > "it becomes clear that they are very similar, thus the vortex signatures may not be resolved and therefore do not clearly show up in the kinematics." (Results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: The four disks are among the six most asymmetric exoALMA disks in continuum and show evidence of azimuthal dust traps  
  > "They are among the six most asymmetric disks in continuum (cite: Curone_exoALMA) in the exoALMA sample and all show evidence of azimuthal dust traps in their millimetre (mm) continuum" (Observations)
- Why this method: A dead-zone edge is used to trigger the vortex at a chosen location, so the dead-zone and planet cases can be compared  
  > "we set up the simulation in this way to trigger a vortex at a particular location" (Methods II: Simulations); "creating a vortex in the same location for both the dead zone and embedded planet simulation makes it easier to compare the two cases" (Methods II: Simulations)
- Alternative: CS J=7-6 images excluded because of much lower SNR at the same resolution  
  > "The CS $J = 7-6$ images are excluded from this work due to their much lower SNR at the same resolution." (Observations)
- Alternative: Fitting the analytical vortex model with eddy was tried and abandoned because the fits did not converge  
  > "However, none of these runs converged and resulted in very degenerate distributions." (Parameter exploration)
- How they decided it was enough: No comprehensive parameter study was intended; the simulations aim to capture some of the complexity seen in the data  
  > "we do not aim for a comprehensive parameter study but intend to expand beyond the relatively simple analytical model" (Methods II: Simulations)
- Limitation (authors): Predictions rely on 2D models; vertical motions are not captured  
  > "The vortex predictions presented in this work rely on 2D models." (Summary & Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Results may be limited by spatial resolution relative to the dust asymmetries  
  > "our results may also limited be by the spatial resolution in comparison to the size of the dust asymmetries" (Summary & Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Low inclination of three of four sources makes azimuthal motions hard to detect  
  > "We are further limited by the low inclination of three of the four sources, making it hard to detect azimuthal motions projected into the line-of-sight." (Parameter exploration)
- Limitation (authors): Mock images assume a flat disk, so the vortex magnitude may be underestimated  
  > "Moreover, we assume a flat disk and the magnitude of the vortex may be significantly underestimated." (Unconvolved model images)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The comparison between data and simulation is visual, with no quantitative metric or significance test
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Only one vortex model (dead-zone edge) was convolved and compared; vortex properties were not varied to fit each source
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inferring a vortex from residuals depends on the DISCMINER Keplerian subtraction in companion papers, so residual artefacts could mimic or hide a signal
- Caveat (inferred, ours): 12CO and 13CO trace upper layers, which the 2D midplane-like model does not represent
