## Analysis recipe

1. **Locate dusty sources**: Select Doppler-shifted Brγ peaks, subtract continuum and measure source positions relative to Sgr A*.  
  > "For the construction of line maps, we select the Doppler-shifted Br$\gamma$ peak and subtract the underlying continuum." (Line/Channel maps); "we extract the related position of the investigated source and, hence, the distance to the SMBH." (Line/Channel maps)
   - assumption: Sgr A* position inferred from the S2 orbit.  
  > "From this orbit, we estimate the position of the SMBH, which is used as a reference source." (Keplerian orbit and MCMC simulation); "The orbital elements for S2 are taken from (cite: Do2019S2)" (Keplerian orbit and MCMC simulation)
   - fitting: Gaussian position fit for D9; standard deviation used for its positional uncertainty.  
  > "To determine the positions of D9, we use a Gaussian fit which simultaneously provides a fitting error." (Identifying dusty objects); "we use the standard deviation to estimate a related uncertainty." (Identifying dusty objects)
   - validation: Brγ line identification cross-checks photometric source positions.  
  > "Since we use the Br$\gamma$ line to cross-check the individual positions of the objects" (Photometry)
2. **Fit source orbits**: Least-square Keplerian fits relative to Sgr A*, with MCMC uncertainty estimation.  
  > "For the orbital analysis of the dusty sources, we use a Keplerian fit to derive positions employing a least-square fit with respect to the location of Sgr A\*." (Keplerian orbit and MCMC simulation); "From the results of the MCMC sampler, we extract the uncertainties to reflect the variety of possible solutions." (MCMC simulations)
   - software: emcee
   - assumption: Dusty sources gravitationally bound to Sgr A* on Keplerian orbits.  
  > "all dusty sources are gravitationally bound to Sgr A\* which translates into a Keplerian orbit to describe their trajectory." (Data and Tools)
   - fitting: emcee; Keplerian fit results used as priors.  
  > "For the MCMC simulations, we utilize the emcee PYTHON package described in (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013)." (MCMC simulations); "As a prior, we use the Keplerian fit results" (MCMC simulations)
   - validation: Plot fitted solutions against measured astrometric points.  
  > "With this information, we plot all solutions to visualize the validity of the fit compared to the measured data points." (Keplerian fit solution)
3. **Test orbital organization**: Calculate orbital PAs with arctan2; inspect PA, inclination and longitude-of-ascending-node distributions against the S-star arrangement.  
  > "The authors use the arctan2 function to estimate the PA because it ensures non-repetitive angles compared to the arctan function" (Position angle of the dusty sources); "Intriguingly, we find a similar non-randomized arrangement for the LOAN as we do for the PA and the inclination." (Position angle of the dusty sources)
   - parameter: Inclination bin size: half the sample number; same bin size for LOAN.  
  > "Using a bin size of half the sample number, we find the corresponding arrangement of the inclination" (Position angle of the dusty sources); "With the same bin size as in Fig. [6](#fig:inc), we show the LOAN distribution" (Position angle of the dusty sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare the inclination distribution with S-stars in the red disk.  
  > "we find a similar distribution as the S-stars in the red disk of the S-cluster (cite: Ali2020)." (Position angle of the dusty sources)
4. **Measure photometry and colors**: Calibrate source intensities against reference stars, average magnitudes, convert to flux densities and construct H-K/K-L colors.  
  > "For the final flux density values, we use average magnitude values of the individual dusty sources" (Photometric analysis); "we list the mean magnitudes and the H-K/K-L colors of the related dusty source." (Magnitude and flux)
   - parameter: SINFONI H+K continuum data, 2006–2019; NACO L-band analysis restricted to 2006–2018.  
  > "For the photometry, we focus on the H+K continuum data observed with SINFONI between 2006 and 2019." (Photometry); "we limit the analysis of the NACO L-band to the epochs 2006-2018." (Photometry)
   - parameter: Flux-density calibrators: S2 and IRS2L.  
  > "are calculated using the calibration sources S2 and IRS2L" (Magnitude and flux)
   - assumption: IRS16NW is non-variable.  
  > "IRS16NW (Fig. [1](#fig:finding_chart)) is a non-variable He star (cite: Krabbe1995; Blum1996; Ott1999)." (Photometric analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare both calibrators and reference-star magnitudes with literature; use inter-calibrator variations for flux-density uncertainties.  
  > "It is reassuring that we find matching flux density values using both calibrators, demonstrating the robustness of the analysis." (Magnitude and flux); "Minor variations between both calibrators are used to construct the indicated uncertainties of the related flux density values." (Magnitude and flux); "With the mean magnitudes, we find consistent values with the listed literature references." (Photometry)
5. **Infer stellar properties**: Fit the measured continuum flux densities with a HYPERION analytical Class I YSO model.  
  > "To fit the flux-density distribution, we utilized the radiative transfer code `HYPERION` and implemented a Young Stellar Object Class I model." (abstract); "we conduct a comprehensive exploration to identify the best-fit parameters that represent the flux density values" (Spectral Energy Distribution)
   - software: HYPERION
   - parameter: Select a best-fit intrinsic inclination for each SED.  
  > "For every single SED, we select the best-fit inclination of the system." (Spectral Energy Distribution)
   - assumption: Class I YSO interpretation.  
  > "implemented a Young Stellar Object Class I model." (abstract)
   - fitting: Comprehensive exploration of best-fit parameters; sampler, likelihood and search bounds not stated.  
  > "we conduct a comprehensive exploration to identify the best-fit parameters that represent the flux density values" (Spectral Energy Distribution)
   - validation: Compare modeled SEDs with measured flux densities; D9 is not satisfactorily represented.  
  > "We note that all the investigated sources are satisfyingly represented by a Class I YSO model except for D9." (Spectral Energy Distribution)
6. **Test coreless-cloud survival**: Calculate dust-evaporation timescales and compare with the observed persistence and photometric behavior of G2/DSO.  
  > "For a quantitative estimate of the usual lifetimes of grains, we follow the work of (cite: Draine1979) and (cite: Drozdov2021)." (Electron temperature and gas temperature); "Considering the data baseline covering 15 years, we find no indications that the nature of G2/DSO is consistent with a coreless gas cloud." (Temperature response of coreless clouds)
   - parameter: n_p≈n_e≈10×10^4 cm^-3; a_0=0.1 μm; T_Gas=10000 K.  
  > "We set $\rm n_p\,\approx\,n_e\,\approx\,10 \times 10^4\,cm^{-3}$, $a_0\,=\,0.1\,\mu m$, and $\rm T_{Gas}\,=\,10000\,K$ (cite: Drozdov2021; Czerny2023)" (Electron temperature and gas temperature)
   - parameter: Cloud radius: half the SINFONI PSF, 60/2 mas.  
  > "For the above equation, we use a cloud radius comparable to half of the SINFONI PSF, i.e., 60/2 mas." (Electron temperature and gas temperature)
   - assumption: Compare particle-collision evaporation with radiation transport between hot gas and cold dust.  
  > "While the cloud evaporates due to particle collision using the approach from (cite: Drozdov2021)" (Electron temperature and gas temperature); "the object gets destroyed because of radiation transport between the hot (gas) and cold (dust) medium with the Ansatz suggested by (cite: Draine2011b)." (Electron temperature and gas temperature)

## Qualitative findings

- Dusty-source orbital PAs place the sample in the S-star red disk.  
  > "The PA of all sources shows that they are arranged in the red disk that was identified by (cite: Ali2020)" (Conclusion)
- Class I YSO SED models satisfactorily represent the investigated sources except D9.  
  > "We note that all the investigated sources are satisfyingly represented by a Class I YSO model except for D9." (Spectral Energy Distribution)
- Intrinsic system inclination does not correlate with orbital inclination.  
  > "While we do not find a correlation between the orbit and system inclination" (Origin of the dusty population)
- X8 has a blueshifted Brγ double-line feature consistent with bipolar morphology.  
  > "we show the blueshifted Br$\gamma$ line that exhibits the mentioned double-line feature which underlines the bipolar morphology of X8." (X8)
- Lense-Thirring timescales motivate an additional dynamical explanation for the disk arrangement.  
  > "Since the Lense-Thirring timescale is typically shorter than the lifetime of the dusty sources, an additional dynamical explanation is required to address the disk-like configuration" (Conclusion)
