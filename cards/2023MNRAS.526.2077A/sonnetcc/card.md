## Analysis recipe

1. **Self-calibrate and align the continuum data**: Self-calibration with the SNOW package, using uvmem models in place of tclean models; Band 6 aligned to Band 7 with VisAlign without flux scaling  
  > "Self-calibration of the continuum data-sets was performed with the `SNOW` package (cite: casassus_22_selfcal)" (2.1 ALMA observations); "the Band 6 data-set was aligned to the Band 7 data-set using the `VisAlign` package (cite: casassus_22_selfcal), but without scaling in flux" (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - software: CASA, SNOW, uvmem, VisAlign
   - parameter: Briggs weights with robustness 1.0  
  > "for Briggs weights with robustness parameter of 1.0" (2.1 ALMA observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Peak signal to noise compared with standard tclean selfcal: ~49 (B6) and ~90 (B7) vs ~60 and ~100 with SNOW  
  > "For comparison, the standard `tclean` selfcal resulted in PSNR $\sim$ 49 in Band 6 and PSNR $\sim$ 90 in Band 7" (2.1 ALMA observations)
2. **Reduce SPHERE/IRDIS polarised intensity image**: irdap reduction to Q_phi(H); subtraction of unresolved central polarisation using an annulus with no disc signal  
  > "To remove this component we selected an annular region devoid of disc signal centered on the star with a radius of 40 to 90 pixels." (2.2 SPHERE observations)
   - software: irdap
   - parameter: irdap version 1.3.3  
  > "pipeline irdap (cite: VanHolstein2020A&A...633A..64V) version 1.3.3" (2.2 SPHERE observations)
   - parameter: Annulus radius 40 to 90 pixels for the unresolved polarisation  
  > "To remove this component we selected an annular region devoid of disc signal centered on the star with a radius of 40 to 90 pixels." (2.2 SPHERE observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Same unresolved-polarisation parameters applied to the 2016 epoch for comparison  
  > "Note that we subtracted the unresolved polarisation from both data-sets using irdap and considering the parameters described above." (2.2 SPHERE observations)
3. **Fit ring geometry and extract azimuthal profiles**: MPolarMaps optimizes PA, inclination and stellar offsets by minimizing dispersion in the radial profile, then records ring intensity profile I(theta); for Q_phi(H) an ellipse was fitted first because the ring is not continuous  
  > "by minimizing the dispersion in the radial profile of the continuum and under the assumption of axial symmetry" (3.1 Azimuthal profiles); "we fitted a continuous ellipse to the emission and used the result to run the `MPolarMaps` optimization" (3.1 Azimuthal profiles)
   - software: MPolarMaps
   - assumption: Axial symmetry of the ring; cavity centred on Gaia coordinates for the profiles  
  > "Given this, we can safely center the cavity to *Gaia* coordinates" (3.1 Azimuthal profiles)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Offset between star and cavity centre shown to be within ALMA and Gaia pointing errors  
  > "so the shifted cavity coordinates for both bands are within the errors" (2.1 ALMA observations)
4. **Locate radio and IR decrements and measure their shifts**: Positions of decrements taken as the minima of the ring intensity profile I(theta); the angular shift eta_shift between radio and IR dips is measured on the plane of the disc  
  > "These positions, indicated by thin lines, correspond to the minima in $I^{\circ}$($\theta$)." (3.1 Azimuthal profiles)
   - software: MPolarMaps
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive inner disc orientation from the IR shadows**: Idealized geometrical model relating shadow PA, subtended angle and stellar offset to inner disc inclination, PA and outer disc scale height  
  > "propose an algorithm that considers the location of the observed shadows as the intersection of the inner disc midplane with a perfectly circular outer disc" (3.3.1 Idealized geometrical model)
   - parameter: Observed Q_phi(H): PA = 161.7 deg, angular separation omega = 170.9 deg, stellar offset b = 0.14  
  > "Observed and calculated values for the orientation of the inner disc in DoAr44 using the idealized geometrical argument." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Perfectly circular outer disc; shadow minimum may not lie in the inner disc midplane (acknowledged caveat)  
  > "the exact locations of the center of the shadows, this is, the decrement minimum, might not necessarily be in the midplane of the inner disc" (3.3.1 Idealized geometrical model)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with GRAVITY-based inner disc orientations and with Paper I RT predictions  
  > "$\xi_{1}$ = 27 $\pm$ 9 deg, which is consistent with our result" (3.3.1 Idealized geometrical model)
6. **Compare SPHERE epochs with RT predictions**: Qualitative comparison of 2016 and 2019 Q_phi(H) profiles with RT predicted profiles for xi ~ 20 and ~ 30 deg from Paper I  
  > "we find that our 2019 $Q_{\phi}(H)$ observations are better reproduced by a misalignment of $\xi$ $\sim$ 20 deg" (3.3.2 Radiative transfer predictions)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Compute spectral index map between 230 and 350 GHz**: 230 GHz image smoothed to the 350 GHz beam, then alpha = log(I230/I350)/log(230/350) with propagated error map  
  > "In order to analyse this, we compute the spectral index map between 230 GHz and 350 GHz" (3.4 Spectral index map); "We smoothed the 230 GHz image to obtain the same synthesized (elongated) beam as that of a Briggs 2.0 weighting scheme for 350 GHz" (3.4 Spectral index map)
   - parameter: Intensity cut sigma_alpha < 0.7 (>0.27 mJy in Band 6, >0.67 mJy in Band 7)  
  > "which translates into intensities greater than 0.27 mJy in Band 6 (14 $\sigma$) and 0.67 mJy in Band 7 (26 $\sigma$)" (3.4 Spectral index map)
   - assumption: Dust opacity index beta = 1.7 for interpreting cavity spectral index  
  > "considering a dust opacity index $\beta$ = 1.7 (cite: draine2006)" (3.4 Spectral index map)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Systematic calibration uncertainty gives +/-0.13 in absolute alpha, not included in the error map  
  > "translates into a $\pm$ 0.13 uncertainty in the absolute value of $\alpha$" (3.4 Spectral index map)
8. **Estimate gas surface density from thermal lag**: Interpolate the T(phi) profile grid of the thermal-lag model for the measured eta_shift to obtain Sigma_g for the northern and southern decrements, assuming a retrograde disc  
  > "Figure [15](#fig:disc_mass) shows the resulting $T(\phi)$ profiles for different values of $\eta_{\mathrm{shift}}$ and $\Sigma_g$." (3.5 Disc gas mass estimates)
   - parameter: Maximum grain size a_max = 1 cm  
  > "We computed the $T(\phi)$ profiles considering a maximum grain size of $a_{\mathrm{max}}$ = 1 cm" (3.5 Disc gas mass estimates)
   - parameter: Gas-to-dust ratio f_gd = 100 and filling factor f = 1  
  > "We also use the established gas-to-dust mass gas ratio for the diffuse ISM, $f_{\mathrm{gd}}$ = 100, and a filling factor for compact grains $f$ = 1." (3.5 Disc gas mass estimates)
   - assumption: Outer disc has a retrograde direction of rotation so that the radio-IR shift is a thermal lag  
  > "provided that the outer disc has a retrograde direction of rotation" (3.5 Disc gas mass estimates)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Lower southern Sigma_g said to be consistent with the smoother, broader southern decrement  
  > "which is consistent with the southern decrement being smoother and broader" (3.5 Disc gas mass estimates)

## Qualitative findings

- The ALMA 230 and 350 GHz images resolve the ring and show intensity dips, confirming the radio decrements predicted from Paper I; the dips are much more prominent in the IR than in the mm  
  > "We found intensity dips in the resolved ALMA imaging of the radio continuum, confirming the predictions from paper I." (Conclusions); "which shows that the decrements are much more prominent in the IR data" (3.2 Radio/IR intensity decrements)
- The southern Q_phi(H) dip changed morphology between the 2016 and 2019 epochs, interpreted as a change in inner disc orientation (xi ~ 30 to ~ 20 deg)  
  > "the variability in the morphology and the location of the dips is most likely related to an illumination effect due to a shift in the orientation of the tilted inner disc" (3.3 Warped inner disc)
- Spectral index ~2 along the ring and at the decrements suggests optically thick emission, so the dips probably are temperature decrements  
  > "The intensity dips may correspond to temperature decrements, since the spectral index map between 230 GHz and 350 GHz shows values $\alpha \sim$ 2 in the location of the shadows" (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Multi-epoch ALMA Band 6/7 and SPHERE data to follow the shadow variability and test the earlier radio dip proposed from deconvolved coarse data  
  > "using multi-epoch ALMA Band 6 and Band 7 data and comparing follow-up SPHERE polarised intensity observations to the previous published epoch" (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: Geometrical model chosen to link shadow positions to inner disc orientation; RT predictions used for a qualitative check  
  > "We analyse this interpretation by using two approaches: an idealized geometrical model for the location of the shadows and a qualitative analysis with radiative transfer (RT) predictions." (3.3 Warped inner disc)
- Alternative: A Gaia pointing error was rejected as the cause of the offset between cavity centre and stellar position  
  > "Given this, we can safely discard that this shift is due to a *Gaia* pointing error" (2.1 ALMA observations)
- Alternative: Azimuthal shift from projection effects of emission height alone was disfavoured because it would shift the northern and southern decrements in opposite directions  
  > "if the azimuthal shift was only related to projection effects due to the emission heights, we would see the northern and southern decrements shifted opposite relative to rotation" (3.3.1 Idealized geometrical model)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Thermal lag analysis would benefit from simultaneous multi-frequency imaging  
  > "However, a more detailed analysis with simultaneous multi-frequency imaging could provide a better grip on such thermal lag." (3.2 Radio/IR intensity decrements)
- Limitation (authors): Southern radio decrement is poorly defined compared with the northern one  
  > "We also note that the radio decrements in the south of the disc cannot be defined with high precision compared to the northern decrements" (3.2 Radio/IR intensity decrements)
- Limitation (authors): Spectral index interpretation ambiguous; more multi-wavelength data and modelling required  
  > "However, further multi-wavelength data and modelling is required to resolve these ambiguities in DoAr 44." (3.4 Spectral index map)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 21.4 deg relative inclination rests on one idealized geometrical model with an imperfect circular-ring and cavity-centre assumption; no independent fit of the SPHERE image with RT is done
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Epoch-to-epoch SPHERE differences could be affected by observing conditions and the unresolved-polarisation subtraction, which the paper says moves the dip locations
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The thermal-lag Sigma_g depends on assumed a_max, f_gd, f and a retrograde rotation, none of which are tested, and the uncertainty derivation is not explained
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Radio dip contrasts are small (e.g. 98 percent, 83 percent of peak) and the paper gives only 1/10 beam-based errors on positions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Band 6 PA given as 59.9 in the text but 59.2 in Table 1
