## Analysis recipe

1. **Make Stokes I, Q, U images**: tclean with Briggs weighting after phase-only self-calibration  
  > "All images were generated with Briggs weighting." (Imaging)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive debiased PI, PF and polarization angles**: PI=sqrt(Q^2+U^2-sigma^2); PF=PI/I; PA=0.5 arctan(U/Q), above 3 sigma_PI  
  > "We therefore debiased the PI image as ${\rm PI}=\sqrt{Q^2+U^2-\sigma_{\rm PI}^2}$" (Imaging)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Compare polarization across wavelengths**: Images made with same uv range, 0.87 mm smoothed to match beam; compute Delta PA and PF ratios  
  > "the images of the polarization vector differences (Supplementary Figure 1) were generated using the same uv distance." (Imaging of the differences)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit B_r:B_phi:B_z (single and dual layer)**: Chi-square fit of angular offset between observed B (polarization rotated 90 deg) and toroidal field vs position angle at 200+-50 au in the south  
  > "We find that $|B_r |:|B_\phi |:|B_z | = 0.26:1:0.23$ best fits the observations from the chi-squared fit as shown in Figure 3." (Single Layer Model)
   - assumption: inclination 27 deg, PA 161 deg; same p_max in both layers for dual model  
  > "assuming the disk inclination and position angle of 27$^\circ$ and 161$^\circ$, respectively" (Results)
   - fitting: chi-square minimization  
  > "We find that $|B_r |:|B_\phi |:|B_z |  = 0.26:1:0.23$ best fits the observations from the chi-squared fit as shown in Figure 3." (Single Layer Model)
   - validation: Compared with alternate ratio parameters; reduced chi2 ~9 (single) vs ~16 (dual)  
  > "The reduced chi-square value for the best-fit model is approximately 16, which is larger than that of the single layer model." (Dual Layer Model)
5. **Estimate eta_A, B strength, Am, beta, alpha, power-law index**: Balance of B_phi generation by vertical shear and ambipolar dissipation; eta_A=B^2/(4 pi C gamma rho^1.5); flux argument B_r/B_z=1/(p+2)  
  > "By assuming the balance between the generation of $B_\phi$ by vertical shear motion and the dissipation of $B_\phi$ by ambipolar diffusion, $B_\phi$ can be estimated" (Methods)
   - assumption: M*=2.4 Msun, T=30 K, Sigma_g=0.2 g cm-2, zeta_CR=1e-17 s-1, HCO+ dominant ion, H_dust~H_gas, r~200 au, H~20 au  
  > "By applying the central star of $2.4$ $M_\odot$, temperature of $30$ K, and $\frac{B_z}{B_\phi}$ of 0.23" (Methods)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with MRI boundary, simulations and Zeeman upper limit  
  > "the magnetic field strength of $0.3$ mG at $\sim 200$ au from the central star is consistent with a prediction from numerical simulations" (Magnetic Field Strength and Activity)

## Qualitative findings

- Southern polarization is consistent across wavelengths and attributed to magnetically aligned grains in a toroidal field  
  > "we conclude that at least the smaller dust grains in the southern region are magnetically aligned" (Results)

## Claim tuples

- H: Southern polarization arises from magnetic grain alignment rather than self-scattering | E: Compared polarization vectors and fractions at four wavelengths | R: Delta PA ~0 and fraction ratios ~1 in south; ~90 deg flips in north | F: South traces magnetically aligned grains | **supported**
- H: 3D field components can be derived from offset from toroidal | E: Chi-square fit of single and dual layer models | R: Br:Bphi:Bz = 0.26:1:0.23 (single) | F: Toroidal dominant; single layer preferred | **partially**
- H: Field strength and activity can be inferred | E: Analytic ambipolar diffusion balance | R: B~0.3 mG, Am~0.4, beta~200 | F: MRI may operate near the boundary | **partially**
