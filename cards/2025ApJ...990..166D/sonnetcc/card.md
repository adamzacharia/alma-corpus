## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA CO(2–1) cubes and continuum**: Joint imaging of all ALMA data per object in CASA with natural weighting; four native channels averaged  
  > "The data were jointly imaged for each object in `CASA` (cite: mcmullin_casa_2007) using natural image weighting to create CO(2–1) spectral cubes and continuum images." (2.2 ALMA Observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: four native channels averaged, ≈34 km/s resolution  
  > "The final spectral cubes for both objects average four native channels, with spectral resolution of $\approx$34 $\rm{km\,s}^{-1}$" (2.2 ALMA Observations)
   - parameter: no continuum subtraction (no significant continuum)  
  > "we did not perform continuum subtraction in the $uv$ or image plane." (2.2 ALMA Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Build moment maps and split central galaxy from tidal tails**: Moment maps from >=4 sigma peaks dilated to the 2 sigma contour in each channel; central component separated from tails by cutting at the minimum between features  
  > "we construct moment maps by identifying $\geq$4$\sigma$ peaks in the CO cube and dilating outward to the $2\sigma$ contour in each spectral channel." (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1)); "the central component is separated from the northern and southern tidal tails by cutting perpendicular to the minimum level of the region between each feature" (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
   - parameter: 4 sigma peak seed, dilation to 2 sigma per channel  
  > "identifying $\geq$4$\sigma$ peaks in the CO cube and dilating outward to the $2\sigma$ contour in each spectral channel" (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Procedure chosen to avoid noise from signal-free channels  
  > "This approach avoids overwhelming the tidal features with noise from signal-free channels and ensures accurate flux measurements." (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
3. **Fit CO spectra, measure sizes and derive molecular gas masses**: Single-Gaussian fits to the CO spectra of each region; IMFIT on integrated intensity maps for deconvolved sizes; L'CO converted to M_H2  
  > "The CO spectra of the central galaxy and extended features for each object were analyzed by fitting the spectra with single Gaussian components." (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1)); "The spatial extent of each CO component was determined using the `CASA IMFIT` task on the integrated intensity maps to find their respective deconvolved sizes." (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
   - software: CASA IMFIT
   - parameter: Gaussian line widths used as proxies for gas velocity dispersions  
  > "We used these fits to extract CO line widths, used as proxies for gas velocity dispersions." (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
   - assumption: alpha_CO = 4.0 Msun/(K km/s pc^2) and r21 = 1.0 (thermalized emission)  
  > "Applying a standard CO-H2 conversion factor of $\alpha_{\rm CO} = 4.0$ $\rm M_{\odot}$/ (K km s$^{-1}$ pc$^{2}$), appropriate for non-starbursting, high-metallicity galaxies (cite: bolatto_co–h2_2013), and assuming thermalized emission with $r_{21}=1.0$" (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
   - fitting: Single Gaussian per component; sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "fitting the spectra with single Gaussian components" (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
   - validation: Direct channel summation gives line fluxes consistent with Gaussian fits  
  > "The direct summation of the emission across channels with significant flux yields total line fluxes consistent with the values derived from the Gaussian fits." (3.1 Molecular Gas Features in ALMA CO(2–1))
4. **Cross-check alpha_CO with 2 mm dust continuum**: Dust-based molecular gas mass from the 2 mm continuum compared with the CO-based mass; 3 sigma upper limits for the tails  
  > "We use 2 mm dust continuum measurements as an independent method to cross-check our $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\rm{CO}}}$ assumptions, offering a test for consistency." (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust); "For the tidal tails in both PSBs we do not detect dust continuum emission at 2 mm and thus report $3\sigma$ upper limits." (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust)
   - assumption: standard dust emissivity assumptions; mass-weighted dust temperature of 25 K  
  > "using standard dust emissivity assumptions and a mass-weighted dust temperature of 25 K (cite: dunne_census_2003; scoville_ism_2016; privon_interpretation_2018; liang_dust_2019)" (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Upper/lower sideband flux ratio consistent with Rayleigh-Jeans dust emission; dust-based alpha_CO ≈ 3.9 agrees with fiducial 4.0  
  > "The flux ratio between the upper (149 GHz) and lower (137 GHz) sidebands is $1.3\pm0.4$, consistent with the expectation for dust emission on the Rayleigh-Jeans tail" (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust); "This implies a CO-H$_2$ conversion factor of $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\rm{CO}}}\approx 3.9$ which is in agreement with our fiducial assumptions" (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust)
5. **Reduce HST data and derive Halpha maps and SFRs**: Grizli reduction of F110W and G102 data; continuum model subtracted from 2D grism spectra to make Halpha maps; Halpha fluxes measured in apertures matching CO regions; SFRs from the Kennicutt relation  
  > "The WFC3 direct imaging and G102 grism exposures for both galaxies were reduced using the ‘Grism redshift & line’ analysis software (cite: brammer_grizli_2019)" (2.3 HST Observations); "The apertures used for the H$\alpha$ flux measurements were chosen to match the regions where CO emission was detected" (2.3 HST Observations); "We then estimate the star formation rates (SFRs) using the relation from (cite: kennicutt_star_1998), corrected to a Chabrier IMF (cite: muzzin_well-sampled_2010)." (2.3 HST Observations)
   - software: Grizli
   - parameter: drizzled to 0.06''/pixel; Halpha maps 80 x 80 pixels  
  > "which resamples the images onto a finer grid with a final pixel scale of $0.06\ensuremath{''}$ / pixel." (2.3 HST Observations)
   - parameter: [NII] contamination scaling ~1.3 (J1448+1010) and ~0.6 (J2258+2313)  
  > "for each galaxy ($\sim$1.3 for SDSS J1448+1010 and $\sim$0.6 for SDSS J2258+2313) as measured using Keck/NIRES in (cite: 2025ApJ...981...60Z)" (2.3 HST Observations)
   - assumption: direct image representative of source morphology  
  > "assuming that the direct image is representative of the morphology of the source." (2.3 HST Observations)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF  
  > "Throughout this paper we assume a (cite: chabrier_galactic_2003) initial mass function" (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: Grizli continuum and emission-line model fit to 2D spectra  
  > "The pipeline then fits continuum and emission line models for the 2D spectra" (2.3 HST Observations)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Image VLA 6 GHz continuum and fit Gaussians**: A+B configuration visibilities combined, VLA pipeline plus manual flagging; Briggs imaging; CASA IMFIT 2D Gaussians for integrated and peak flux; Condon (1997) analytic limit for the point-like J2258+2313 size  
  > "imaging was performed with Briggs weighting (cite: briggs_high_1995) with a robust parameter of zero." (2.4 VLA Observations); "we use the `CASA IMFIT` task to fit 2D Gaussian components to the images." (3.3 Radio Morphology via VLA Continuum); "we use the analytic approximation for point sources from (cite: condon_errors_1997) for SDSS J2258+2313." (3.3 Radio Morphology via VLA Continuum)
   - software: CASA IMFIT, standard VLA pipeline
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0  
  > "with a robust parameter of zero" (2.4 VLA Observations)
   - parameter: w-projection used for J2258+2313 only  
  > "The imaging process for SDSS J2258+2313 additionally required the use of the w-projection algorithm (cite: cornwell_noncoplanar_2008)" (2.4 VLA Observations)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit with IMFIT; priors not applicable  
  > "we use the `CASA IMFIT` task to fit 2D Gaussian components to the images." (3.3 Radio Morphology via VLA Continuum)
   - validation: _not stated_
7. **Test whether the radio emission could be past star formation**: Compare observed 6 GHz flux density with predictions from an exponentially fading radio luminosity based on each galaxy's SFH, and compare the radio-derived SFR with the SED SFR  
  > "we consider a simplified model where the radio luminosity fades exponentially following the suppression of star formation." (3.5 Evidence of AGN); "Using a standard conversion between radio luminosity and SFR (cite: murphy_calibrating_2011)" (3.5 Evidence of AGN)
   - parameter: tau = 50, 100, 150 Myr; exponential decline from the SFR at the onset of quenching  
  > "We therefore calculate predicted radio flux densities for three fiducial values of $\tau$ (50, 100, and 150 Myr) and consider an assumed exponential decline computed from the SFR at the onset of quenching." (3.5 Evidence of AGN)
   - assumption: Exponential radio decay is a simplified prescription, not a precise model of radio fading  
  > "Thus, Eq. [\[eq:1\]](#eq:1) provides a simplified prescription of the evolution of radio luminosity as opposed to a precise modeling of the physical onset of radio fading." (3.5 Evidence of AGN)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Authors note the result is not definitive because the radio-tracing timescale is uncertain  
  > "we note that the results are not intended to be definitive, as the actual timescale over which radio synchrotron emission traces star formation remains uncertain." (3.5 Evidence of AGN)
8. **Compare SFR and M_H2 (depletion times)**: Plot SFR vs M_H2 for central galaxies and tail regions against PHIBSS/PHIBSS2 and COLDGASS samples with depletion-time lines; SED-derived SFRs for the centrals, Halpha SFRs for tails  
  > "To assess how efficiently gas is converted into stars across the regions in these PSBs" (4.3 Is Star Formation Ongoing in the Tails?); "The SFRs derived from SED-fitting (cite: bezanson_now_2022), rather than H$\alpha$ emission, are used for the central galaxies due to concerns that the H$\alpha$ emission may be contaminated by AGN activity" (4.3 Is Star Formation Ongoing in the Tails?)
   - parameter: non-detections plotted as 3 sigma upper limits  
  > "are plotted as upper limits ($3\sigma$) in Figure [8](#fig:ksplot)." (4.3 Is Star Formation Ongoing in the Tails?)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
9. **Compare gas turbulence and virial state with literature**: Size vs velocity dispersion and sigma/R vs gas surface density compared with a virial relation and literature GMC/galaxy samples  
  > "we examine the size-velocity dispersion relation for the central galaxies and their associated tidal tail clumps" (5.2.1 Turbulence in the Molecular Gas); "we expect $\Sigma_{\rm gas} \sim 370\sigma^2 / R$ where $\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ is in units of $\ensuremath{\rm{M}_\odot}\rm pc^{-2}$, $\sigma$ in $\rm{km\,s}^{-1}$, and $R$ in pc." (5.2.1 Turbulence in the Molecular Gas)
   - parameter: structures within a factor of three of the virial relation considered bound  
  > "Structures within a factor of three (shaded band) are typically considered gravitationally bound and consistent with virial equilibrium" (5.2.1 Turbulence in the Molecular Gas)
   - assumption: Velocity dispersions from single-Gaussian CO(2–1) fits, not corrected for ordered motions  
  > "We do not correct these dispersions for ordered motions and therefore interpret them as proxies for the intrinsic gas dispersion." (5.2.1 Turbulence in the Molecular Gas)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
10. **Place the radio luminosities in context of radio-AGN samples**: 6 GHz flux densities converted to 1.4 GHz luminosities with a power-law spectral index and compared with literature radio-AGN samples versus stellar mass and f_burst  
  > "we convert the 6 GHz flux densities of the two PSBs to their corresponding 1.4 GHz luminosities using a spectral index of $\alpha = -0.7$" (5.2.2 The Role of Radio-Mode Feedback from AGN)
   - parameter: spectral index alpha = -0.7  
  > "using a spectral index of $\alpha = -0.7$" (5.2.2 The Role of Radio-Mode Feedback from AGN)
   - assumption: typical synchrotron power law S_nu ∝ nu^alpha  
  > "assuming the typical power-law relation for synchrotron emission $S_\nu \propto \nu^\alpha$" (5.2.2 The Role of Radio-Mode Feedback from AGN)

## Qualitative findings

- Roughly half of the molecular gas in each system lies in extended tidal tails, far more than in low-redshift mergers  
  > "In both galaxies, approximately half of the molecular gas is located in the tidal features, significantly higher than in the low-redshift mergers." (4.2 Did Tidal Gas Removal Have a Role in Quenching?)
- SDSS J1448+1010 shows Halpha in its northern tail while SDSS J2258+2313 shows none outside the centre  
  > "SDSS J2258+2313 does not show H$\alpha$ emission outside the central region, highlighting a difference in the spatial distribution of potential star-forming activity between the two systems." (3.2 HST Grism Spectroscopic Characteristics)
- J1448+1010 radio emission is AGN-dominated; J2258+2313 radio emission is consistent with past star formation  
  > "In contrast, the radio emission from SDSS J2258+2313 aligns with predictions for past star formation" (3.5 Evidence of AGN)
- Central galaxies lie above the virial relation, indicating non-virialized, turbulent gas  
  > "The central galaxies are above the relation, exceeding what is expected for virialized structures." (5.2.1 Turbulence in the Molecular Gas)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Deep low-resolution ALMA data to detect faint extended CO, and higher-resolution data to measure the spatial extent of J2258+2313  
  > "to better understand the distribution of CO emission, particularly the low-surface-brightness extragalactic emission." (2.2 ALMA Observations); "We acquired higher-resolution ${\sim}0.5\ensuremath{''}$ observations to measure the spatial extent of the CO(2–1) emission in this object" (2.2 ALMA Observations)
- Why this method: Natural weighting chosen to maximize sensitivity to low-surface-brightness emission  
  > "Natural weighting allows us to maximize sensitivity especially for low-surface-brightness emission at the cost of slightly lower spatial resolution." (2.2 ALMA Observations)
- Alternative: AGN-driven outflows, virial shock heating and environmental effects judged unlikely primary quenching drivers compared with tidal removal  
  > "While multiple processes could contribute a secondary role to the quenching of star formation in these PSBs, the tidal removal of cold gas during a major merger is most likely to be the driving mechanism of quenching in these PSBs." (4.2 Did Tidal Gas Removal Have a Role in Quenching?)
- Alternative: Different alpha_CO for tails (ULIRG-like 1.0) considered as a sensitivity test  
  > "If we adopt a Milky Way-like $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\rm{CO}}}= 4.0$ for the central galaxies but a ULIRG-like $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\rm{CO}}}= 1.0$ in the tails" (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust)
- Alternative: Shallow F098M direct images not combined with F110W because of limited depth  
  > "so we do not combine these with the deeper F110W images in our analysis." (2.3 HST Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): alpha_CO may differ between tails and centres, with no evidence for or against  
  > "Although we presently have no evidence for (or against) this scenario, it is possible that $\ensuremath{\alpha_{\rm{CO}}}$ differs between the tidal tails and the central galaxies." (3.4 Cross-Checking Molecular Gas Mass: CO vs. Dust)
- Limitation (authors): Halpha-based SFR for J1448+1010 centre is likely a significant overestimate because of AGN  
  > "The SFR measurement is likely to be a significant overestimate for SDSS J1448+1010 due to the presence of an AGN." (Table 2 notes)
- Limitation (authors): Only two objects studied, so the parameter space of tidal gas removal is not sampled  
  > "However with only two objects studied thus far, we have yet to sample the full parameter space of tidal gas removal." (6 Conclusions)
- Limitation (authors): Velocity dispersions not corrected for ordered motions  
  > "We do not correct these dispersions for ordered motions and therefore interpret them as proxies for the intrinsic gas dispersion." (5.2.1 Turbulence in the Molecular Gas)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas masses depend on a single alpha_CO and r21 = 1.0; tails and centres may differ, and tail masses at 4 sigma-seeded 2 sigma masks may be sensitive to the masking choice
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Velocity-dispersion comparisons with GMC samples mix very different physical scales and are uncorrected for beam smearing and ordered motion
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The radio-fading test uses three fiducial tau values and an assumed exponential decline, so it cannot discriminate AGN from star formation firmly for J2258+2313
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Halpha aperture SFRs depend on a [NII] correction taken from another paper and on grism contamination subtraction
