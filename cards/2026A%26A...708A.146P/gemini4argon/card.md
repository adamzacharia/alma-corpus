## Analysis recipe

1. **Align multi-wavelength astrometry (HST and MUSE to Gaia DR3 and ALMA)**: Cross-match bright stars in HST broad-band images with Gaia EDR3/DR3 catalogue (using 3 bright objects when possible to solve for field rotation), then align MUSE cubes to the corrected HST images using bright stars and update FITS headers.  
  > "To solve for accurate and reliable astrometry, we use the positions of several stars detected both in HST images and present in the Gaia catalogue." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets); "Whenever possible, we use 3 bright objects to solve for potential rotation of the HST fields." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets); "In order to provide an uniform dataset for various science projects across the collaboration, we choose to edit the HST images and MUSE cubes header files to correct to the Gaia DR3 astrometry." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets)
   - software: ESO pipeline exp_align
   - parameter: 3 bright stars per field when possible  
  > "Whenever possible, we use 3 bright objects to solve for potential rotation of the HST fields." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked alignment of targets between corrected HST and ALMA data; mean HST offset found to be 0.235 arcsec and MUSE offsets < 0.8 arcsec.  
  > "The resulting offsets are sub-arcsec with a mean value of 0.235 arcsec. After correction, we find the alignment of our targets between the HST and ALMA data to be excellent." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets); "The systemic offsets are in most cases smaller than the beam size (∼ 0.8 arcsec)." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets)
2. **Extract calibrated visibilities and perform manual self-calibration and continuum subtraction**: Extract pipeline-calibrated measurement sets from the observatory, interactively clean continuum image of the quasar field to build a point-source model, perform phase-only self-calibration (30s to 4m intervals) for quasars >= 4 mJy and amplitude self-calibration for quasars > 20 mJy, and run uvcontsub on Q2131-1207.  
  > "Specifically, we extract the calibrated measurement sets (ms) produced with the Common Astronomy Software Applications (CASA, version 6.5.6) by the observatory." (5.3. Data reduction); "During the first round of interactive cleaning, we clean the quasar based on the point source assumption." (5.3. Data reduction); "During self-calibration, the calibration tables are generated with the task gaincal and apply to the pipeline-calibrated measurements using applycal." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, gaincal, applycal, uvcontsub
   - parameter: quasar flux >= 4 mJy for phase self-cal; solint = 30 s to 4 min  
  > "In six of the observed fields, the background quasars are bright enough at mm frequencies (≥ 4 mJy) to perform self-calibration." (5.3. Data reduction); "Then, we derive a phase-only calibration with various time intervals ranging from 30 seconds to 4 minutes, depending on the brightness of the quasar." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - parameter: quasar flux > 20 mJy and 5 sigma threshold for amplitude self-cal  
  > "We also apply amplitude self-calibration if the quasar is brighter than 20 mJy and accepts the signals down to 5σ when constructing the clean models." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - assumption: Point source assumption for the background quasar in the first round of interactive cleaning  
  > "During the first round of interactive cleaning, we clean the quasar based on the point source assumption." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared RMS sensitivity before and after self-calibration (improved by factors of 1.1 to 7.6) and against automated observatory self-calibration products (improved by ~1.2).  
  > "After self-calibration, the RMS sensitivity is improved by factors ranging from 1.1 to 7.6, compared to the ones without selfcalibration." (5.3. Data reduction); "Besides, our manual self-calibration also improves the sensitivity by a factor of about 1.2 compared to the automated observatory-based self-calibration pipeline products." (5.3. Data reduction)
3. **Image continuum and spectral line datacubes**: Image visibilities into continuum images and 3D datacubes using CASA tclean with two weighting schemes (natural and Briggs robust=0.5) and two velocity resolutions (15 km/s and 4-channel binned 60 km/s), using gridder='mosaic' for multi-pointing fields.  
  > "We then image the (self-)calibrated visibility into continuum images and 3D images (datacubes) with the CASA task tclean." (5.3. Data reduction); "As a result, four different types of images and datacubes are produced, for each of the two weighting schemes and each two of the spectral resolution choices." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: weighting = natural and Briggs (robust = 0.5)  
  > "The "natural" weighting is used to maximize the detection sensitivity, and the "Briggs" weighting (robust=0.5) is used to achieve an optimal balance between sensitivity and spatial resolution." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - parameter: spectral resolution = 15 km/s and 60 km/s (4-channel binning)  
  > "We further apply a spectral binning of four channels to improve the signal-to-noise ratio, resulting in a spectral resolution of 60 km/s." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - parameter: gridder = 'mosaic' for 4 multi-pointing fields  
  > "The images of the mosaic fields are created by setting the gridder to "mosaic"." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured final RMS noise levels across fields (173 to 492 uJy/beam) and synthesized beams (0.57 to 1.02 arcsec).  
  > "The final RMS noise level of the cubes ranges from 180 to 500 mJy/beam and is typically < 250 mJy/beam. The resulting angular resolution varies from 0.57 to 1.02 arcsec." (5.3. Data reduction)
4. **Search for CO emission lines and extract primary-beam-corrected spectra**: Search for emission lines on non-primary-beam-corrected datacubes (especially natural weighting 60 km/s cubes), apply primary beam correction with impbcor, and extract spectra in CASA using varied aperture sizes and position angles to optimize SNR.  
  > "The search for emission lines was performed on the datacubes without primary beam correction." (5.3. Data reduction); "After we located the sources, their spectra were extracted from the primary beam-corrected datacubes generated by impbcor, which properly accounts for the increased noise level near the edge of the fields." (5.3. Data reduction); "The spectra were extracted within CASA using different aperture sizes and position angles to optimize the final SNR (Bollo et al. 2026)." (5.3. Data reduction)
   - software: CASA, impbcor
   - assumption: Cosmology H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, Omega_M = 0.3, Omega_Lambda = 0.7; Milky Way SLED and alpha_CO = 4.6 M_sun (K km/s pc^2)^-1 for survey design mass sensitivity  
  > "Throughout this paper, we use H_0 = 70 kms^-1 Mpc^-1, Ω_M = 0.3 and Ω_Λ = 0.7." (1. Introduction); "the z=0.5 luminosity distance of dL=2129 Mpc using a (0.3,0.7,0.7) cosmology." (5.1. Survey design)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Optimized SNR over different aperture sizes and position angles; full sample detections reported in companion paper Bollo et al. (2026).  
  > "The spectra were extracted within CASA using different aperture sizes and position angles to optimize the final SNR (Bollo et al. 2026)." (5.3. Data reduction)

  > "We then image the (self-)calibrated visibility into continuum images and 3D images (datacubes) with the CASA task tclean." (5.3. Data reduction)
- **gaincal** : Generating phase and amplitude self-calibration tables  
  > "During self-calibration, the calibration tables are generated with the task gaincal and apply to the pipeline-calibrated measurements using applycal." (5.3. Data reduction)
- **applycal** : Applying self-calibration tables to pipeline-calibrated measurement sets

## Qualitative findings

- Manual phase and amplitude self-calibration on bright background quasars (>= 4 mJy) in the ALMA field-of-view significantly improves RMS sensitivity (by factors of 1.1 to 7.6) and outperforms automated pipeline self-calibration by ~1.2.  
  > "After self-calibration, the RMS sensitivity is improved by factors ranging from 1.1 to 7.6, compared to the ones without selfcalibration." (5.3. Data reduction); "Besides, our manual self-calibration also improves the sensitivity by a factor of about 1.2 compared to the automated observatory-based self-calibration pipeline products." (5.3. Data reduction)
- Aligning HST broad-band images and VLT/MUSE cubes to Gaia DR3 stars removes sub-arcsecond offsets (mean 0.235 arcsec for HST, <0.8 arcsec for MUSE) and yields excellent astrometric alignment with ALMA.  
  > "The resulting offsets are sub-arcsec with a mean value of 0.235 arcsec. After correction, we find the alignment of our targets between the HST and ALMA data to be excellent." (3.5. Astrometry across multi-wavelength datasets)
- The Cycle 10 ALMA Large Programme increases the number of galaxies with multi-phase circumgalactic medium constraints by more than a factor of 2, providing a representative sample of 39 z~0.5 HI-selected galaxies down to log M_H2 > 9 M_sun.  
  > "The project increases the number of galaxies with observational constraints of their multi-phase circumgalactic medium by more than a factor 2, thus producing a representative sample of H i-rich galaxies." ## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA Band 4 and Band 6 cover redshifted CO(2-1), CO(3-2), and CO(4-3) for z~0.5 HI-selected galaxies; configurations C-2, C-3, and C-4 give 0.57-1.02 arcsec resolution to match VLT/MUSE and enable kinematic forward-modelling while LAS of 7 arcsec recovers all emission (< 5 arcsec); including the quasar in the field-of-view enables self-calibration.  
  > "The strategy of including the quasar in the field-of-view improves the sensitivity of the observations by enabling self-calibration hence maximising the detection sensitivity." (5.1. Survey design); "We set angular resolution ranging 0.57-1.02 arcsec to optimise the forward-modelling of the velocity field and so as to enable comparison with the ionised gas probed in emission with MUSE and the neutral circumgalactic gas probed in absorption with VLT and Keck spectroscopy (Rahmani et al. 2018a)." (5.1. Survey design); "Based on early ALMA observations, the sizes of targets were known to be significantly less than 5 arcsec so that there were no issues with missing emission which is more extended than the chosen largest recoverable scale (LAS) of 7 arcsec." (5.1. Survey design)
- Why this method: Producing four datacube types (natural vs Briggs robust=0.5 weighting, 15 km/s vs 60 km/s channel width) optimizes both blind/targeted line searches (natural, 60 km/s) and resolved kinematic modelling of strong emitters (Briggs, 15 km/s); searching on non-PB-corrected cubes provides uniform apparent noise.  
  > "The natural weighting with the 60 km/s products is better suited for searches for line emissions, while the Briggs weighting with different velocity resolutions is better suited for detailed kinematic analysis of the targets with strong line emissions." (5.3. Data reduction); "The search for emission lines was performed on the datacubes without primary beam correction. This provides a homogeneous apparent noise level across the field-of-view." (5.3. Data reduction)
- Alternative: Automated observatory-based self-calibration pipeline products were compared against manual self-calibration and found to be less sensitive by a factor of ~1.2.  
  > "Besides, our manual self-calibration also improves the sensitivity by a factor of about 1.2 compared to the automated observatory-based self-calibration pipeline products." (5.3. Data reduction)
- How they decided it was enough: Exposure times (2.4 to 7.9 hr) were set to reach a 5-sigma molecular gas mass limit of log M_H2 > 9 M_sun at z=0.5 assuming a Milky Way SLED and alpha_CO = 4.6.  
  > "the z=0.5 luminosity distance of dL=2129 Mpc using a (0.3,0.7,0.7) cosmology. The resulting exposure times ranged from 2.4 to 7.9 hrs" (5.1. Survey design)
- Limitation (authors): Because of ALMA's restricted primary beam (44 arcsec in Band 4, 27 arcsec in Band 6) compared to MUSE, not all 79 MUSE-detected associated galaxies could be targeted with ALMA (60 targeted in total across all ALMA runs, 39 in the Large Programme).  
  > "Because of the restricted primary beam however, not all the known associated galaxies detected in MUSE are targeted with ALMA." (2. The MUSE-ALMA Haloes primary target selection)
- Limitation (authors): One quasar field (Q1110+0048) lacks high-resolution optical quasar spectroscopy because the UVES PI-run was not completed.  
  > "All the quasar spectra are observed at high-spectral resolution but one (Q1110+0048 which contains one absorber) due to the lack of completion of the UVES PI-run." (2. The MUSE-ALMA Haloes primary target selection)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Unit inconsistency in the text for sensitivity: Section 5.1 writes '500 umJy/beam', Figure 7 and Section 5.3 write '180 to 500 mJy/beam', whereas Table 2 gives '173 to 492 uJy/beam' and fails to specify whether the RMS is measured in the continuum or per 15/60 km/s channel.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The paper is a survey design and data release overview; the actual CO flux measurements, molecular gas masses, and kinematic fits are deferred to companion papers (Bollo et al. 2026, Barfety et al. 2026, O'Beirne et al. 2026).
