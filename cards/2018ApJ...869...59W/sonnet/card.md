## Analysis recipe

1. **Image ALMA HCO+ and SMA data**: CASA imaging of archive-calibrated ALMA data; MIR calibration and MIRIAD imaging of SMA data  
  > "The visibility data were calibrated using the MIR IDL package (https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/rtdc/SMAdata/process/mir/) and imaged using the MIRIAD package (cite: sau95)." (2.2 SMA Observations at 225 GHz)
   - software: CASA, MIR IDL, MIRIAD
   - parameter: ALMA uv-taper 200 klambda, robust 0.5  
  > "The image cube was made with a uv-taper of 200 k$\lambda$ (0$\ensuremath{''}$11) and briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5." (2.1 HCO$^+$ Observations with ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Fit continuum visibilities with two Gaussians**: uvfit with two elliptical Gaussian components; envelope dust mass from optically thin formula  
  > "The visibility data were fitted with two elliptical Gaussian components using the MIRIAD task *uvfit*." (3.1 1.3 mm Continuum Emission)
   - software: MIRIAD
   - assumption: kappa=0.899 cm2/g, Tdust=15 K, d=140 pc  
  > "In the envelope, $\kappa_\nu$ is assumed to be 0.899 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: oss94) and $T_{\rm dust}$ is assumed to be 15 K" (3.1 1.3 mm Continuum Emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared compact flux to Kwon et al. 2011 and total to single-dish flux of Beckwith et al. 1990  
  > "the total flux (i.e. compact + extended components) of $\sim$870 mJy is consistent with the flux of $\sim$880 mJy measured with the single-dish telescope" (3.1 1.3 mm Continuum Emission)
3. **Estimate missing flux in lines**: Convolve primary-beam-corrected SMA/ALMA maps to IRAM 30 m beam and compare spectra  
  > "The spectra extracted from the convolved images were compared with the IRAM 30 m spectra." (3.3 Velocity Structure of HL Tau)
   - software: CLASS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Fit geometrically thin Keplerian disk to HCO+ channel maps**: Minimum chi2 fit of Keplerian model to high-velocity HCO+ channel maps with free Mstar, PA, inclination  
  > "we performed minimum $\chi^2$ fitting of the geometrically-thin Keplerian disk model to the ALMA velocity channel maps of the HCO$^{+}$ (1–0) line at the high-velocity of $|\Delta V| > 3$ km s$^{-1}$" (4.1.2 Keplerian Disk Modeling)
   - parameter: Fit velocity ranges V_LSR = 2.5-4.0 and 10.0-11.5 km/s; sigma = 0.4 km/s; vsys = 7 km/s  
  > "The velocity ranges of the fitting are restricted to $V_{LSR}$ = 2.5 - 4.0 km $s^{-1}$ and 10.0 - 11.5 km s$^{-1}$" (4.1.2 Keplerian Disk Modeling)
   - assumption: Dynamical center fixed at 2.9 mm continuum peak; moment 0 of model equal to observed  
  > "The dynamical center of the model disk is fixed to be the peak position of the 2.9 mm continuum emission, and $v_{\rm sys}$ is set to be 7 km s$^{-1}$." (4.1.2 Keplerian Disk Modeling)
   - fitting: chi2 minimization over Mstar, theta, i  
  > "The fitting parameters are thus $M_{\star}$, $\theta$, and $i$" (4.1.2 Keplerian Disk Modeling)
   - validation: Compared with continuum PA/inclination and residual channel maps  
  > "The best-fit $\theta$ and $i$ are consistent with those estimated from the 2.9 mm continuum emission." (4.1.2 Keplerian Disk Modeling)
5. **Radiative transfer disk+envelope model fit to 13CO and C18O**: SPARX LTE radiative transfer; model cubes sampled on observed uv, CLEANed, and chi2 compared to moment 0 maps and major-axis intensity profiles  
  > "The image cubes generated with the radiative transfer calculations of the molecular lines are sampled on the observed $uv$ grids, and the model visibilities are continuum-subtracted, Fourier-transformed, and CLEANed" (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
   - software: SPARX
   - parameter: 0.0001 < Md < 0.35 Msun, 200 K < T1au < 650 K; gamma=1 (disk), 0.5 (envelope); rc=100 au; Mstar 1.4 Msun  
  > "We computed models with 0.0001 $M_{\odot}$$< M_d <$0.35 $M_{\odot}$ and 200 K $< T_{1{\rm au}} <$ 650 K for the $\chi^2$ fitting" (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
   - assumption: LTE, no spatial gas-dust segregation, thermal coupling; 13CO/C18O abundances 3.5e-6 and 4.8e-7  
  > "we assume that there is no spatial segregation between the gas and dust components, and that gas and dust are thermally well coupled" (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
   - fitting: Grid chi2 with progressively narrowed ranges and steps; disk mass limits from bounding all data points  
  > "the ranges and steps of the parameters are progressively narrowed down to approach the best fit" (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
   - validation: Tested gamma, abundance ratio 10 vs 7.3, and C18O depletion in disk; adjusted dust opacity to match continuum  
  > "An abundance ratio of 10 between $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O (cite: bri05; smi15) is also tested" (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
6. **Gas-to-dust ratios and infall rate**: Combine SPARX gas masses with dust masses; envelope with single-dish correction; infall rate 2 pi r Sigma vff  
  > "we have also estimated gas and dust masses of the envelope with the single-dish measurements." (5.1 Gas to Dust Ratio in HL Tau)
   - assumption: Envelope T=15 K, C18O abundance 4.8e-7, LTE optically thin  
  > "with the LTE condition, the optically-thin approximation, and a C$^{18}$O abundance of $4.8\times10^{-7}$" (5.1 Gas to Dust Ratio in HL Tau)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- High-velocity gas traces a Keplerian disk and low-velocity gas an infalling, flattened envelope.  
  > "The high-velocity component traces the Keplerian rotating disk, and the low-velocity component traces the infalling envelope." (abstract)
- SO emission is compact, offset toward the redshifted outflow, and has no clear velocity gradient.  
  > "the SO emission likely has the contamination from the outflow" (3.2 Molecular line maps)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Large FOV and short baselines of SMA and ALMA HCO+ data allow envelope characterization  
  > "enable us to derive the physical conditions of the extended protostellar envelope" (Introduction)
- Why this method: HCO+ chosen for Keplerian fit due to higher critical density and low contamination  
  > "suggesting less contamination from the ambient materials" (4.1.2 Keplerian Disk Modeling)
- Alternative: gamma of 1.0 and 1.5 in envelope tested; 0.5 reproduces best  
  > "$\gamma$ of 1.0 and 1.5 in the envelope have also been tested, but the models with $\gamma$ of 0.5 best reproduce the intensity profile of the envelope." (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Simple power-law density profile cannot fully reproduce intensity profiles; only limits on disk mass  
  > "We found that the observed intensity profiles cannot be fully reproduced with our models having a simple power-law density profile." (4.2 SPARX Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Envelope gas mass from SMA underestimated due to >80% missing flux  
  > "the gas-to-dust mass ratio in the envelope is underestimated with the SMA observations because the molecular-line emission in the envelope has a higher missing flux" (5.1 Gas to Dust Ratio in HL Tau)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Disk dust mass is taken from the literature and is optically thick-sensitive, so the gas-to-dust ratio inherits that uncertainty
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas mass limits depend on assumed CO abundance, LTE and equal gas-dust temperature
- Caveat (inferred, ours): High reduced chi2 (~9-11) indicates poor model fits
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA data are used only for HCO+ kinematics, not the gas mass
