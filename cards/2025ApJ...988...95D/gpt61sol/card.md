(Excitation temperatures, column densities, and abundances relative to H2)

  > "The column density of molecular hydrogen ($\mathit{N}_{{\rm H}_{2}}$) was estimated under the assumption of optically thin emission from dust" (Excitation temperatures, column densities, and abundances relative to H2)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: 12-m data suit compact hot-core COM emission and reduced susceptibility to missing flux.  
  > "In this work, we utilized data from the 12-m array to identify COM lines within the HCs as their compact sizes in high mass star-forming regions and reduced susceptibility to missing flux make this approach suitable." (Data reduction)
- Why this method: Use CH3OH normalization to assess uncertain dust-based H2 columns and abundance-correlation systematics.  
  > "Given the challenges in accurately determining the column density of H$_{2}$ from dust continuum, we also discussed the abundance of the detected species from different sources relative to CH$_{3}$OH" (Comparison with other sources); "To mitigate this concern, we have presented an additional correlation using abundances normalized by CH$_{3}$OH" (Comparison with other sources)
- Alternative: Smaller line-emitting source sizes were considered for opacity estimates; continuum sizes retained for consistency.  
  > "We evaluated how source size assumptions affect opacity estimates" (Results — Isotopic ratio); "Therefore, we have retained the use of the continuum deconvolved size for consistency across all species, but note that the actual ratios may be slightly higher than reported." (Results — Isotopic ratio)
- Alternative: Compare both H2 and CH3OH abundance normalizations; CH3OH normalization weakens correlations.  
  > "To mitigate this concern, we have presented an additional correlation using abundances normalized by CH$_{3}$OH" (Comparison with other sources); "In this analysis, the correlations are significantly weaker, indicating that previously observed correlations might be driven by individual outliers or systematic uncertainties." (Comparison with other sources)
- Alternative: Fast and Medium warm-up models compared with Slow; Slow gives the closest overall match.  
  > "The observed abundances in MM1 show partial agreement with chemical model predictions for three different warm-up timescales (Fast, Medium, and Slow)." (Conclusions); "The Slow warm-up model provides the closest match overall, but 5 species still deviate by more than an order of magnitude." (Conclusions)

  > "These parameters were adjusted iteratively to ensure that the simulated spectra closely matched the observed lines, allowing for precise characterization of the molecular inventory." (Identification of molecular lines)
