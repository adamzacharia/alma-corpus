## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image Band 3 and Band 7 data**: Standard ALMA pipeline calibration, then tclean for continuum and for line cubes (Briggs 1.0 for Band 3 line; natural weighting for Band 3 continuum and Band 7)  
  > "We search for both continuum and line emission in these data using the CASA routine *tclean*." (2.2 Band 3 Observations); "Inspecting each cube leads us to select the Briggs 1.0 cube as our final science image" (2.2.2 Line)
   - software: CASA, tclean, uvcontsub
   - parameter: Cubes made with Briggs weights 0.0-2.0 (0.5 step); Briggs 1.0 chosen; 6 pixels across restoring beam major axis  
  > "We produce cubes using an array of different Briggs weights ranging from 0.0 to 2.0 with an 0.5 step" (2.2.2 Line); "use cell sizes sufficient to place 6 pixels across the restoring beam major axis for each clean attempt" (2.2.2 Line)
   - parameter: Band 3 cube: per-channel rms 0.052 mJy/beam, channel width 53.6 km/s; image centered with 5.2 at the phase center  
  > "This final image cube has a per-channel rms of $0.052$ mJy / beam" (2.2.2 Line); "we consistently use the map with 5.2 at the phase center" (2.2.2 Line)
   - parameter: Band 7: uvcontsub with 1st-order polynomial over 277.763-277.279 GHz and 277.201-275.919 GHz  
  > "We fit a 1st-order polynomial over the range 277.763 GHz - 277.279 GHz and 277.201 GHz - 275.919 GHz" (2.3.2 Line)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: JvM correction found unnecessary because clean beam approximates the dirty beam; maximum recoverable scale 4.11" exceeds the ~1.5" emission scale so no significant flux resolved out  
  > "We determined that the JvM correction is not required for this source, as in these data the clean beam is a very good approximation to the shape and volume of the dirty beam on scales containing real emission." (2.2 Band 3 Observations)
2. **Extract spectra and fit CO(4-3) and [N II] line fluxes, widths and redshifts**: Spectral extraction in the image plane within a custom aperture, Gaussian fit to each lensed image's spectrum  
  > "We perform our spectral extraction in the image (i.e. unreconstructed) plane." (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements); "We perform a Gaussian fit to the extracted spectrum of each lensed image within a custom aperture" (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements)
   - software: astropy
   - parameter: Custom aperture chosen to include low-surface-brightness ~2 sigma emission near the source edge  
  > "used to optimally include low-surface-brightness, $\sim 2\sigma$ emission near the edge of the source" (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements)
   - assumption: Line profiles are single Gaussians  
  > "The CO emission is well modeled by a Gaussian in both lensed images" (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements)
   - fitting: Gaussian fit with astropy; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "using the (cite: astropy1) software package" (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements)
   - validation: Reduced chi-squared 0.638 (5.2) and 0.522 (5.1) for CO; 1.41 for [N II]; binning by 2 does not change line intensity  
  > "with a reduced chi-squared of 0.638 for the 5.2 component and 0.522 for the 5.1 component (Figure 2)" (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements); "Binning the spectrum by a factor of 2 did not have a significant impact on the derived line intensity." (3.1 CO(4-3) and [N II]205um Measurements)
3. **Derive intrinsic dust mass upper limit from Band 3 continuum**: Prescription of Section 3.4 of Solimano et al. 2021 applied to the 3 sigma continuum limit at rest 600 um, corrected for lensing magnification  
  > "we use the prescription given in Section 3.4 of (cite: Solimano2021)" (3.2 Total Dust Mass); "we derive an intrinsic $3\sigma$ dust mass upper limit" (3.2 Total Dust Mass)
   - parameter: T_dust = 25 K, beta = 1.8, kappa = 0.9 cm^2/g at rest 600 um  
  > "setting our dust temperature to $T_{dust} = 25$K and using a standard dust emissivity index of $\beta$=1.8" (3.2 Total Dust Mass); "we use a dust mass absorption coefficient of 0.9 cm$^2$ g$^{-1}$ (cite: Draine2001)" (3.2 Total Dust Mass)
   - assumption: Cold-dust temperature 25 K dominates the dust mass; alternative luminosity-weighted T_dust = 40 K tested  
  > "This $T_{dust} = 25$K is the expected temperature of the cold dust component that dominates the dust mass" (3.2 Total Dust Mass); "If we assume a luminosity-weighted dust temperature based on the SED fits of (cite: Pope2023) ($T_{dust} = 40$K)" (3.2 Total Dust Mass)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with the MAGPHYS SED dust mass of Pope et al. 2023 and found consistent  
  > "This dust mass is consistent with expectations from the MAGPHYS SED" (3.2 Total Dust Mass)
4. **Source-plane reconstruction and 2D Sersic fit**: Lenstool reconstruction channel by channel with the non-cored Limousin et al. 2016 HFF model, then 2D Sersic profile fit  
  > "We apply this reconstruction on a channel-by-channel basis." (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction); "we perform a 2D Sersic profile fit (cite: astropy1)" (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction)
   - software: Lenstool, astropy
   - parameter: Reconstruction at spectroscopic CO(4-3) redshift z=4.27; channel-averaged reconstructed beam approximated by placing the image-plane beam at the spectral and spatial center  
  > "We also produce an approximate channel-averaged reconstructed beam by placing the image-plane synthesized beam in the spectral and spatial center of the image." (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction)
   - assumption: Magnification roughly constant across the image, so the channel-averaged beam approximates the source-plane beam  
  > "As the magnification across any individual image of Az9 is relatively constant, this produces a reasonable approximation to the true source-plane beam." (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction)
   - fitting: 2D Sersic fit with astropy; sampler/priors not stated  
  > "we perform a 2D Sersic profile fit (cite: astropy1)" (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction)
   - validation: Relative CO fluxes of 5.1 and 5.2 consistent with lens-model prediction; at most ~10% of 5.2 flux could be lost beyond the caustic  
  > "We find that the relative CO fluxes of the 5.1 and 5.2 images are consistent with each other given the (cite: Limousin2016) model prediction." (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction); "at most $\sim10\%$ of the flux from the 5.2 could be lost beyond the caustic" (3.4 Source Plane Reconstruction)
5. **Fit tilted-ring kinematics of CO(4-3) in the source plane**: 3D-Barolo SEARCH to build the emission mask, then 3DFIT; a first run with centers, PA and inclination free, a second with those fixed at their means  
  > "We begin by using 3D-Barolo's SEARCH module to isolate the regions in each channel containing real emission." (3.6 Kinematic Analysis); "We then perform a second run with these parameters fixed at their means from the previous run, allowing only the rotational velocity and velocity dispersion to vary, in order to determine our final fitted values." (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
   - software: 3D-Barolo
   - parameter: SEARCH: primary SNR cut 4.5 sigma, secondary 3.5 sigma, at least 30 pixels, significant flux in at least 2 consecutive channels  
  > "We use a primary SNR cut of $4.5\sigma$, a secondary SNR cut of $3.5\sigma$, and we require regions to contain at least 30 pixels" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis); "contain significant flux in at least 2 consecutive channels to be included in the final mask" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
   - parameter: Ring geometry: maximum radius 0.8 x CO FWHM major axis; minimum ring radius = beam minor axis / 2.5; minimum model radius 0  
  > "a minimum ring radius of the beam minor axis divided by 2.5" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis); "We use a minimum model radius of 0" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
   - parameter: Initial guesses Vmax = 150 km/s, Vsigma = 30 km/s; inclination and PA initial guesses from [C II] modeling  
  > "we use initial guesses of 150 km/s and 30 km/s based on initial inspection of the moment 1 and moment 2 maps" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
   - assumption: Rotating gaseous disk made of concentric rings, convolved with the beam  
  > "building a model made up from multiple concentric rings, which it combines to produce a model of a rotating gaseous disk" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
   - fitting: 3D-Barolo forward-modeling tilted-ring fit; optimizer/likelihood not stated  
  > "Using a forward-modeling approach, 3D-Barolo is able to robustly recover disc galaxy kinematic properties even at very low spatial resolution" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
   - validation: Results insensitive to initial guesses of Vrot and Vsigma; fitted inclination/PA (47.6, 188.9 deg) compared to [C II] (46.6, 189.2 deg); no multiply-peaked velocity profile  
  > "These derived quantities are not sensitive within uncertainty to initial guesses of $V_{rot}$ and $V_{\sigma}$." (4.4 Kinematic Properties); "We find an inclination of 47°.6 and a position angle of 188°.9 compared to an inclination of 46°.6 and a position angle of 189°.2" (3.6 Kinematic Analysis)
6. **Dynamical mass and dynamical upper limit on alpha_CO**: Disk dynamical mass equation of Wang et al. 2013 using twice the Sersic half-light radius as diameter and the mean Vrot  
  > "we use an equation first developed by (cite: Wang2013) and commonly used throughout the literature (cite: Capak2015; Cassata2020): $M_{dyn, disk}[M_{\odot}] = 1.16 \times 10^{5} V_{cir}^2 D$" (4.4.1 Dynamical Mass)
   - parameter: R_1/2 = 2.74 kpc (D = 2 R_1/2), Vrot = 148 +/- 32 km/s  
  > "Using twice our half-light radius $R_{1/2}=2.74$ kpc as the diameter and our radially-averaged rotational velocity $V_{rot}$=148 $\pm$ 32 km/s" (4.4.1 Dynamical Mass)
   - assumption: Disk geometry; maximum alpha_CO assumes the dynamical mass contains only stars and molecular gas (no neutral gas or dark matter) and r41 = 0.63  
  > "assuming it contains *no neutral gas or dark matter*, only stars and molecular gas, and using $r_{41} = 0.63$" (4.4.2 Dynamical Constraints on alpha_CO)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Discussed turbulent pressure support and beam smearing as systematics, judged not significant because 3D-Barolo convolves with the beam  
  > "3D-Barolo accounts for beam smearing through its convolution step (cite: Teodoro2015) and fully 3-D approach." (4.4.1 Dynamical Mass)
7. **Molecular gas mass, gas fraction and depletion time with Monte Carlo systematics**: 5000-run Monte Carlo drawing L'CO(4-3), r41 and alpha_CO (and stellar mass) to get M_H2  
  > "We use a 5000-run Monte Carlo approach to explore how possible combinations of reasonable estimates for $r_{41}$ and $\alpha_{CO}$ impact the inferred molecular gas mass of Az9." (3.8 Molecular Gas Mass Uncertainties)
   - parameter: r41 = 0.63 +/- 0.44 (normal, negative values excluded); alpha_CO uniform 0.8-6.1  
  > "In this exercise, we use the $r_{41} = 0.63 \pm 0.44$ found by (cite: Castillo2023), excluding any negative values." (3.8 Molecular Gas Mass Uncertainties); "We draw from a uniform $\alpha_{CO}$ distribution $\alpha_{CO} = 0.8 - 6.1$" (3.8 Molecular Gas Mass Uncertainties)
   - assumption: Lower alpha_CO limit is the QSO/SMG value of Carilli & Walter 2013; upper limit set by the kinematic (dynamical mass) argument; no additional CO lines to constrain excitation  
  > "with the lower limit corresponding to the 0.8 value for SMGs and QSOs (cite: Carilli2013) and the upper limit motivated by our kinematic work in Section 4.4.1" (3.8 Molecular Gas Mass Uncertainties)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo sampling (not a fit); L'CO drawn from a normal distribution based on observed value, error and lensing correction

## Qualitative findings

- Az9 hosts PDR conditions similar to local normal star-forming galaxies (VALES) and broadly comparable to the much more massive AzTEC-1  
  > "Az9 appears to host PDR conditions consistent with the local normal star-forming galaxies studied in the VALES program" (4.3 Gas Conditions in PDRs)
- A metallicity-calibrated alpha_CO (34) would give an unphysical gas mass above the dynamical mass, so local alpha_CO(Z) calibrations may not apply to high-z low-mass galaxies  
  > "The result that locally calibrated fitting functions for $\alpha_{CO}$ may not be appropriate in high-redshift galaxies is not new" (4.5 Metallicity Constraints)
- Az9 is a rotationally dominated system consistent with the [C II] kinematics  
  > "This result is consistent with the $V/\sigma$ found for the \[C II\] $(5.3 \pm 3.6)$ in the same system (cite: Pope2023)." (4.4 Kinematic Properties)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Low-mass, highly obscured high-z galaxies are not well studied at millimeter wavelengths; strong lensing makes Az9 accessible  
  > "However, due to observational challenges, they are not well studied at millimeter wavelengths." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Briggs 1.0 chosen because it gives a fine beam without missing significant line flux relative to natural weighting  
  > "as this weight produces a relatively fine resolving beam that does not miss significant observed line flux when compared to natural weighting" (2.2.2 Line)
- Alternative: Briggs weights 0.0 to 2.0 explored; Briggs 1.0 selected  
  > "We produce cubes using an array of different Briggs weights ranging from 0.0 to 2.0 with an 0.5 step in order to explore how various weighting schemes affect our results." (2.2.2 Line)
- Alternative: Re-imaging with image 5.1 at the phase center did not help, so all values come from the map centered on 5.2  
  > "Re-imaging with the 5.1 image at the phase center did not substantially improve its detection" (2.2.2 Line)
- Alternative: Alternative T_dust = 40 K for the dust mass upper limit  
  > "we derive a dust mass upper limit of log$_{10}[M_{dust} (M_{\odot})] < 7.52$, also consistent with the mass estimate from those SED fits." (3.2 Total Dust Mass)
- Alternative: Alternative optical-thickness correction (dividing optically thin tracers by 2) not applied  
  > "an equivalent correction (not applied in this work) is sometimes performed by keeping the luminosity of optically thick tracers the same and instead dividing the luminosity of optically thin tracers by a factor of 2" (3.5 PDR Modeling)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Gas mass limited by poorly constrained r41 and alpha_CO at high redshift and low stellar mass  
  > "The strength of this conclusion is limited by the systematic uncertainties on $\alpha_{CO}$ and $r_{41}$, which are poorly unconstrained at high redshift and low stellar mass." (6 Summary)
- Limitation (authors): Single-PDR model is a simplified picture of galaxy-wide ISM  
  > "We note that a single PDR, as modeled here, is not a realistic description of the ISM of an entire galaxy." (3.5 PDR Modeling)
- Limitation (authors): No additional CO lines to test excitation, so r41-related systematics cannot be addressed  
  > "However, without observations of additional CO lines, we cannot definitively address this issue." (3.8 Molecular Gas Mass Uncertainties)
- Limitation (authors): Band 7 observation reached only a quarter of the requested time, leaving a low-SNR [N II] detection and an imprecise metallicity  
  > "The total time on source was 26 minutes" (2.3 Band 7 Observations); "The high uncertainty here is driven by the low signal-to-noise of our observation of the" (4.5 Metallicity Constraints)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Low-resolution CO data (~2 beams across the source) make the tilted-ring disk classification and V/sigma model-dependent; merger misclassification is acknowledged only briefly
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Gas mass depends on a wide, partly ad hoc alpha_CO prior (0.8-6.1) whose upper limit is itself derived from the dynamical mass, and on a single CO line with an r41 taken from massive galaxies
- Caveat (inferred, ours): [N II]205um is only ~2.4 sigma, yet it sets the [C II] PDR fraction and the metallicity
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Vrot is quoted as 148 +/- 32 in the text and 148 +/- 33 in Table 2 and the Summary
- Caveat (inferred, ours): PDR modeling is a single-slab interpretation of a galaxy-integrated measurement with inputs partly from another paper
