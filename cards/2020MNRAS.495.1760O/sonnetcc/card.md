## Science

- Fields: nearby_galaxies, high_z_galaxies, cosmology
- Question: Are there empirical relations among radio continuum, infrared and CO luminosities that can be used to estimate molecular gas mass and its cosmic evolution up to z = 1.5  
  > "we look for empirical relations amongst the radio luminosity, the infrared luminosity, and the CO-based molecular gas mass." (abstract); "we explore the evolution of the molecular gas mass function and the cosmological molecular gas mass density in six redshift bins up to $z = 1.5$." (abstract)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Assemble the multi-survey galaxy sample**: Combine seven literature surveys, match radio data from FIRST/NVSS, require S/N > 5 and remove galaxies with CO apertures smaller than optical size  
  > "we remove from the sample all galaxies with CO apertures smaller than the Petrosian aperture" (2.2 Final sample); "(2) radio detections at S/N $>5$ resulting in a radio luminosity range" (2.2 Final sample)
   - software: SIMBAD
   - parameter: FIRST match radius 4 arcsec for xCOLD GASS; NVSS match radius 20 arcsec for the other samples  
  > "using a matching radius of 20$\ensuremath{''}$" (2.1 Radio data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Mismatches checked against SIMBAD (optical minor axis and nearby dominant galaxies)  
  > "To discard possible mismatches, we use the SIMBAD" (2.1 Radio data)
2. **Measure radio flux densities and 1.4 GHz luminosities**: Smooth JVLA images to a common resolution, measure integrated flux with a Gaussian fit, fit spectral index, k-correct to 1.4 GHz and compute L_1.4GHz = 4 pi D_L^2 S/(1+z)  
  > "which allows us to use the same Gaussian profile in each image." (2.1 Radio data); "we $k$-correct the flux density, $S_{\rm 1.4GHz}$, using the measured radio spectral index, including error propagation." (2.1 Radio data)
   - software: CASA
   - assumption: alpha_radio = -0.7 +/- 0.3 for galaxies with a single radio measurement  
  > "assuming the typical radio spectral index of $-0.7\pm0.3$ ((cite: ibar09)) expected for star-forming galaxies" (2.1 Radio data)
   - assumption: Lambda-CDM with H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, OmegaM = 0.3, OmegaL = 0.7  
  > "Throughout the text, we assume a $\Lambda$-CDM cosmology with $H_0=70$  km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm M} = 0.3$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.7$." (1 Introduction)
   - fitting: Spectral index fitted to all available JVLA measurements per galaxy (method not stated)  
  > "These spectral indices and errors were obtained from the best fit based on all the JVLA measurements available" (2.1 Radio data)
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Screen for AGN contamination**: Match to the Veron-Cetty and Veron catalogue and apply WISE colour-colour criteria  
  > "we matched the entire sample with the 13$\rm^{th}$ Edition of the Catalog of Quasar and AGNs (cite: veron10)" (2.1 Radio data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Excess 1.4 GHz emission looked for in Fig. 1; none found, so no correction applied  
  > "confirming that making corrections for any excess in the RC emission is unnecessary." (2.1 Radio data)
4. **Fit pairwise L_1.4GHz, L_IR and L'_CO relations**: Linear fits log(y) = m log(x) + b to the 278-galaxy sample  
  > "$\log(y) = m \times \log(x) + b$" (Table 3)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with Magnelli and Villanueva relations; the new RC-CO relation closely matches the fit to the data  
  > "closely matches the best fit of our sampled data." (3.1 The evolution of the RC–CO relation)
5. **Derive RC-CO relation as a function of redshift**: Combine the Magnelli RC-IR relation with the Villanueva IR-CO relation to obtain L'_CO(L_1.4GHz, z)  
  > "we obtain an expression for the RC–CO relation as a function of redshift" (3.1 The evolution of the RC–CO relation)
   - assumption: Validity constrained up to z = 1.5 by the redshift range of the input relations  
  > "our results are constrained up to $z=1.5$ based mainly on the redshift constraints" (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
   - fitting: Errors propagated assuming a Gaussian probability distribution  
  > "Errors were propagated assuming a Gaussian probability distribution." (3.1 The evolution of the RC–CO relation)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Convert radio luminosity functions to molecular gas mass functions**: Apply the RC-CO relation to literature RCLFs to obtain CO luminosity functions, then M_mol = alpha_CO L'_CO  
  > "we use the conversion factor between $L^{'}_{\rm CO}$ and the molecular gas mass" (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
   - parameter: Six redshift bins centred at z = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.1 and 1.5  
  > "six redshift bins centred at $<z> = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.1$ and $1.5$" (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
   - assumption: Constant alpha_CO = 3.6 Msun/(K km/s pc^2), helium not included  
  > "Although this value for $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ does not take into account the helium contribution" (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with semi-analytic MGMF models of Popping12 and Lagos18  
  > "We compare our results with those obtained from the semi-analytic MGMF models developed by (cite: popping12) and (cite: lagos18)." (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
7. **Integrate the MGMF to get the cosmic molecular gas mass density**: Integrate MGMF over mass; also a fixed integration range using the continuous Smolcic09 RCLF model  
  > "After fixing the integration mass limit in the range $9 < \log(M_{\rm mol}/{\rm M}_\odot)< 11$" (3.3 Cosmic molecular gas mass content)
   - parameter: 9 < log(Mmol/Msun) < 11 for fixed-limit density  
  > "After fixing the integration mass limit in the range $9 < \log(M_{\rm mol}/{\rm M}_\odot)< 11$" (3.3 Cosmic molecular gas mass content)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Results fall inside errors of the ASPECS direct measurements of Decarli16  
  > "Our results fall inside the errors of the direct measurements obtained by (cite: decarli16)" (3.3 Cosmic molecular gas mass content)
8. **Fit the CO-RC-IR plane**: Fit a plane to L'_CO versus L_IR and L_1.4GHz with MPFIT  
  > "we obtained a numerical expression using the MPFIT algorithm" (3.4 The empirical CO–RC–IR plane)
   - software: MPFIT
   - fitting: MPFIT least-squares; priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "we obtained a numerical expression using the MPFIT algorithm" (3.4 The empirical CO–RC–IR plane)
   - validation: Scatter between plane-predicted and measured L'_CO is 0.27 dex, lower than pairwise relations  
  > "shows a scatter of 0.27 dex" (5.2 Using the CO–RC–IR plane to predict CO emission in galaxies)

## Qualitative findings

- The RC, IR and CO relations are robust enough to estimate CO emission where no CO measurement exists  
  > "These correlations are sufficiently robust to be used for samples where no CO measurements exist." (abstract)
- Estimates agree with semi-analytic models and the ASPECS ALMA large programme  
  > "The agreement of our estimates with semi-analytical models, as well as with the results from the ALMA large programme, ASPECS" (6 Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Large wide-area survey samples give more statistically robust results than earlier small samples  
  > "We base our work on large samples of galaxies built from wide-area surveys, resulting in more statistically robust work than was previously possible." (1 Introduction)
- Why this method: _not stated_
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): MGMF over-estimates relative to semi-analytic models at z > 1.5  
  > "our results for the MGMF show a considerable over-estimate in comparison with the semi-analytic models for RCLF at redshifts $z > 1.5$." (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
- Limitation (authors): Constant alpha_CO ignores variation with galaxy properties such as metallicity  
  > "with the assumption of a constant $\alpha_{\rm CO}$, we ignore any possible variation of the conversion factor as a function of galaxy properties" (3.2 The RC–CO correlation used to estimate the molecular gas mass function)
- Limitation (authors): Main uncertainty in the density comes from the measured RC luminosity functions  
  > "The main source of uncertainty in $\rho_{(M_{\rm mol})}$ comes from the uncertainty in the measured RC luminosity functions." (3.3 Cosmic molecular gas mass content)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The RC-CO relation is built by chaining two literature relations; the sample fit is only a consistency check
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Pairwise fit method and its treatment of upper limits and selection effects are not described
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A sample of nearby galaxies (median z = 0.017) is extrapolated to z = 1.5 without direct validation of redshift evolution
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Constant alpha_CO may bias the density at high z where starbursts are more common
