## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate raw ALMA visibility data and concatenate multi-epoch observations**: Import raw ASDM files to MSs, flag pointing scans/autocorrelations/shadowed antennas and manual edge/initial channels, apply Tsys and WVR tables, solve bandpass and phase/amplitude gains, bootstrap absolute flux scale using power-law interpolation of ALMA calibrator catalog, self-calibrate Circinus on the brightest line, and concatenate multi-day MSs.  
  > "We start by importing the raw ALMA Science Data Model files and converting them to CASA Measurement Sets (MSs) using the `importasdm` task." (Data reduction procedure); "Following this gain calibration, we bootstrap the absolute flux density calibration from the flux density calibrator to the gain calibrator using the `fluxscale` task." (Data reduction procedure); "After initial gain calibration, we further self-calibrate the phases to the strongest spectral line, averaging over the three channels ($\sim$0.6 km s$^{-1}$) centered at 561.6 km s$^{-1}$ and using a solution interval of 60 seconds." (Circinus)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Bandpass pre-gaincal averaging over ~5% of each spectral window per integration time  
  > "Prior to determining the bandpass solution, we first track the phase variations of the bandpass calibrator on every integration time by averaging over a small number of frequency channels ($\sim$5% of each spectral window) using the `gaincal` task." (Data reduction procedure)
   - parameter: Circinus phase self-calibration: 3 channels (~0.6 km s^-1) centered at 561.6 km s^-1, solint = 60 seconds  
  > "After initial gain calibration, we further self-calibrate the phases to the strongest spectral line, averaging over the three channels ($\sim$0.6 km s$^{-1}$) centered at 561.6 km s$^{-1}$ and using a solution interval of 60 seconds." (Circinus)
   - assumption: Power-law spectral energy distribution S_nu = S_{nu,0} (nu / nu_0)^alpha for flux density calibrators  
  > "For each flux density calibrator, we assume a power-law spectral energy distribution to extrapolate from the ALMA calibrator source catalog measurements to the $\sim$183 GHz observing frequencies relevant for the observations in this paper." (Data reduction procedure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Empirically estimate flux density calibration reliability using historical ALMA monitoring epochs with a third intermediate frequency nu_mid, finding 5% to 10% precision.  
  > "We also use the historical ALMA monitoring observations to empirically estimate the reliability of the flux density calibration for each target." (Additional calibration details); "We find a typical flux density precision between 5% and 10%." (Additional calibration details)
2. **Image spectral line cubes and continuum maps**: Deconvolve Stokes I visibilities using Högbom CLEAN in CASA tclean for each channel to produce image cubes, and combine line-free channels/windows for continuum images.  
  > "We carry out all imaging for this paper using the Högbom CLEAN method (cite: Hogbom_1974) as implemented in CASA’s `tclean` task, combining the calibrated XX and YY visibilities to form Stokes I images." (Imaging procedure); "We carry out imaging separately for every channel in each spectral window to produce image cubes, and we also carry out continuum imaging by combining multiple channels to form a single image." (Imaging procedure)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: natural weighting, standard gridding, 300x300 pixels, pixel size ~1/8 nominal beam FWHM, loop gain = 0.1, stopping criterion peak residual ~5x expected RMS, no CLEAN boxes  
  > "Each image is reconstructed on a $300 \times 300$ pixel field of view, with pixel sizes chosen to be equal to ${\sim}1/8$ of the nominal beam FWHM." (Imaging procedure); "We use a loop gain of 0.1, and as our stopping criterion we require that the peak residual be $\sim$5 times the expected image RMS level." (Imaging procedure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Identify 183 GHz spectral line detections and measure peak flux densities and sensitivities**: Extract spectra from the central region of each image cube, apply 10-channel boxcar smoothing in frequency, compute RMS across the full spectral window, and identify peaks reaching >= 5 sigma across multiple channels.  
  > "We manually inspect spectra extracted from the central region of each of the image cubes for signs of spectral line emission, both before and after carrying out boxcar smoothing in frequency by 10 channels (corresponding to a $\sim$2 km s$^{-1}$ post-averaging spectral resolution)." (Spectral line identification); "After initially estimating the spectral sensitivity by computing the RMS across the full spectral window[^6], we identify any peaks that reach the ${\gtrsim}5\sigma$ level. If the source shows comparably elevated emission across multiple channels, then we consider it to be a detection." (Spectral line identification)
   - parameter: boxcar smoothing = 10 channels (~2 km s^-1 resolution); detection threshold >= 5 sigma across multiple channels; continuum nondetection threshold < 3 sigma  
  > "After initially estimating the spectral sensitivity by computing the RMS across the full spectral window[^6], we identify any peaks that reach the ${\gtrsim}5\sigma$ level. If the source shows comparably elevated emission across multiple channels, then we consider it to be a detection." (Spectral line identification); "Spectral line detections are determined by the criteria described in [2.3](#sec:Identification); continuum nondetections are only reported if the emission level at the expected location of the AGN does not rise above the $3\sigma$ level." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Compute 183 GHz isotropic maser luminosities and compare with 22 GHz spectra**: Integrate the 183 GHz flux density over velocity and compute isotropic luminosity L_iso using adopted source distances, and compare qualitatively and quantitatively with literature 22 GHz spectra.  
  > "Instead, we characterize the strength of a maser system using the “isotropic luminosity,” $L_{\text{iso}}$, which corresponds to the luminosity that an isotropic emitter would need to have to produced the observed flux level." (Results and discussion); "L_{\text{iso}} = \left( 1.9 \times 10^{-4} \text{ L}_{\odot} \right) \left( \frac{D}{1 \text{ Mpc}} \right)^2 \left( \frac{\int S_{v} dv}{1 \text{ mJy km\,s\ensuremath{^{-1}}}} \right) ." (Results and discussion)
   - parameter: Rest frequency nu_0 = 183.310087 GHz; H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1; peculiar velocity uncertainty = 300 km s^-1  
  > "Throughout this paper, all velocities are quoted using the optical convention in the heliocentric reference frame unless otherwise specified, and we adopt a Hubble constant value of $H_0 = 70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$." (Introduction); "$^*$When computing the uncertainty in isotropic luminosity, we incorporate an assumed 300 km s$^{-1}$ peculiar-velocity-induced uncertainty in the recession velocity for galaxies whose distances are estimated using the Hubble law." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Isotropic emission for L_iso; H_0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 and 300 km s^-1 peculiar velocity uncertainty for Hubble law distances  
  > "Throughout this paper, all velocities are quoted using the optical convention in the heliocentric reference frame unless otherwise specified, and we adopt a Hubble constant value of $H_0 = 70$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$." (Introduction); "$^*$When computing the uncertainty in isotropic luminosity, we incorporate an assumed 300 km s$^{-1}$ peculiar-velocity-induced uncertainty in the recession velocity for galaxies whose distances are estimated using the Hubble law." (Table 2)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Map 183 GHz maser spot positions in Circinus via visibility modeling**: Fit a point source model directly to the self-calibrated, time-averaged Stokes I complex visibilities in each spectral channel individually using nested sampling.  
  > "To map the 183 GHz maser system in Circinus, we fit a point source model directly to the visibilities in each spectral channel individually." (Circinus); "We fit directly to the Stokes I complex visibilities in each spectral channel, after self-calibration and averaging in time across the entire observing track." (Maser position modeling)
   - software: dynesty
   - parameter: Plot threshold: geometric mean positional uncertainty sqrt(sigma_x * sigma_y) < 20 mas; reference maser spot at v = 561.6 km s^-1  
  > "We plot only the maser features that have geometric mean fitted positional uncertainties less than 20 mas; i.e., we require that $\sqrt{\sigma_x \sigma_y} < 20$ mas, given an uncertainty in the $x$-position of $\sigma_x$ and an uncertainty in the $y$-position of $\sigma_y$." (Circinus); "Because we have self-calibrated using the brightest spectral line at 561.6 km s$^{-1}$(see [5.16](#app:Circinus)), all positions are referenced to the location of the associated maser spot." (Maser position modeling)
   - assumption: Each maser spot in a spectral channel is a single point source; noise sigma is constant across visibilities within a single channel; brightest 183 GHz spot at 561.6 km s^-1 corresponds to the 22 GHz reference spot at 566.3 km s^-1  
  > "We assume that each maser spot is a point source, for which we fit the position $(x,y)$ and flux density $S$ in every spectral channel." (Maser position modeling); "where $\hat{V}_i$ represents an observed complex visibility and $\sigma$ is the uncertainty (which we assume to be the same for all data points within a single channel)." (Maser position modeling); "We note that (cite: Greenhill_2003) referenced their VLBI map to a maser spot with a velocity[^7] of 566.3 km s$^{-1}$, while we have referenced our map to a maser spot with a velocity of 561.6 km s$^{-1}$; in both cases, the reference maser is the brightest one in the spectrum." (Circinus)
   - fitting: Nested sampling via dynesty; complex Gaussian likelihood; uniform priors: p_S ~ U(0, 10) Jy, p_x, p_y ~ U(-0.5, 0.5) arcsec, p_sigma ~ U(0, 5) Jy  
  > "Within a single spectral channel, our likelihood function for each data point $i$ is a complex Gaussian (cite: TMS)," (Maser position modeling); "We specify uniform priors of $p_S \sim \mathcal{U}(0,10)$ (in units of Jy) for $S$, $p_x \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.5,0.5)$ (in units of arcseconds) for $x$ and $y$, and $p_{\sigma} \sim \mathcal{U}(0,5)$ (in units of Jy) for $\sigma$." (Maser position modeling); "We use the nested sampling package `dynesty` (cite: Speagle_2020) to carry out the fitting separately for each spectral channel." (Maser position modeling)
   - validation: Compare spatial distribution and velocity gradient against 22 GHz VLBI maps of Circinus.  
  > "Our map shows that the 183 GHz maser system in Circinus is spatially resolved by these ALMA observations, covering a region that appears to be well-matched in size and shape to the ${\sim}90\text{\,mas} \times 50$ mas distribution of 22 GHz masers." (Circinus)
6. **Fit and map serendipitous molecular lines (HNC and HC3N) in Circinus and NGC 1068**: Image continuum spectral window cubes with CASA tclean and fit a 1D Gaussian plus constant continuum background to the spectrum at every pixel location using scipy curve_fit.  
  > "We use CASA’s `tclean` task to make image cubes from the continuum spectral windows for both galaxies, and we use the `curve_fit` function from the `scipy` Python library to to fit a Gaussian function to each pixel in the image cube." (Other molecular species in Circinus and NGC 1068)
   - software: CASA, scipy
   - parameter: 4 free parameters per pixel: constant background S_0, peak brightness A, center velocity v_0, standard deviation sigma  
  > "Here, $S_0$ is a constant-valued background continuum brightness level, $A$ is the peak brightness level of the Gaussian, $v_0$ is the center velocity of the Gaussian, and $\sigma$ is its standard deviation." (Other molecular species in Circinus and NGC 1068)
   - assumption: Single Gaussian line profile per pixel; pixel uncertainty equal to continuum RMS level  
  > "We use a least squares optimization scheme to carry out the fits, taking the uncertainty in each pixel’s value to be given by the continuum RMS level (see [2](#tab:Detections))." (Other molecular species in Circinus and NGC 1068)
   - fitting: Least squares optimization via scipy curve_fit; parameter uncertainties from diagonal elements of the inverse Hessian matrix  
  > "We use a least squares optimization scheme to carry out the fits, taking the uncertainty in each pixel’s value to be given by the continuum RMS level (see [2](#tab:Detections)). The corresponding uncertainty estimates for each modeled parameter are then given by the diagonal elements of the inverse Hessian matrix." (Other molecular species in Circinus and NGC 1068)
   - validation: Compare rotation sense and spatial distribution against previous HI and molecular line maps.  
  > "This sense and orientation of rotation matches that seen in large-scale neutral hydrogen maps of Circinus (cite: Jones_1999), and it also matches that seen in the 183 GHz maser system presented in this paper (see [3.13](#sec:Circinus))." (Circinus); "The emission is strongest in an eastern “knot” within the circimnuclear disk, as seen in previous observations (cite: Viti_2014; Garcia-Burillo_2019; Qiu_2020)." (NGC 1068)

## Qualitative findings

- 183 GHz maser emission is detected in 13 out of 20 targeted AGN known to host 22 GHz megamasers, increasing the number of AGN known to host (sub)millimeter megamasers by a factor of 5.  
  > "Out of 20 sources observed, we detect significant 183 GHz maser emission from 13; this survey thus increases the number of AGN known to host (sub)millimeter megamasers by a factor of 5." (Abstract)
- 183 GHz emission is systematically fainter in flux density than 22 GHz emission from the same targets (roughly an order of magnitude lower), but isotropic luminosities at 183 GHz and 22 GHz are comparable.  
  > "We find that the 183 GHz emission is systematically fainter than the 22 GHz emission from the same targets, with typical flux densities being roughly an order of magnitude lower at 183 GHz than at 22 GHz." (Abstract); "However, the isotropic luminosities of the detected 183 GHz sources are comparable to their 22 GHz values." (Abstract)
- In 9 of the 13 detections, only a single line complex near the systemic velocity is detected; NGC 1194 and CGCG 074-064 show one additional high-velocity feature, while ESO 269-G012 and Circinus show rich multi-component spectra.  
  > "For the majority (9 out of 13) of our 183 GHz maser detections, the spectrum exhibits only a single, largely featureless line complex located near the systemic velocity of the galaxy." (Summary and conclusions); "Of the remaining 4 sources, two (NGC 1194 and CGCG 074-064) have a single additional spectral feature detected at velocities that are offset by several hundred km s$^{-1}$ from the systemic velocity of the galaxy, while the other two (Circinus and ESO 269-G012) exhibit considerably more complex spectral structure." (Summary and conclusions)
- In Circinus, visibility modeling spatially resolves the 183 GHz maser distribution (~90 mas x 50 mas), showing the same northeast-blueshifted to southwest-redshifted orientation as the 22 GHz VLBI map and consistent with tracing the disk at larger orbital radii.  
  > "Our map shows that the 183 GHz maser system in Circinus is spatially resolved by these ALMA observations, covering a region that appears to be well-matched in size and shape to the ${\sim}90\text{\,mas} \times 50$ mas distribution of 22 GHz masers." (Circinus); "The 183 GHz masers are consistent with being located in the disk identified at 22 GHz, but at larger orbital radii than are occupied by the 22 GHz disk masers." (Circinus)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected 20 AGN already known to host 22 GHz disk megamasers (from Pesce et al. 2015) or strong AGN-associated 22 GHz megamasers to test for 183 GHz maser emission using ALMA Band 5.  
  > "We used ALMA’s Band 5 to observe 20 AGN known to host 22 GHz water megamaser systems and spanning a range of redshifts out to $z \approx 0.05$; the targets are listed in [1](#tab:Observations)." (Data reduction and imaging); "Each of the targeted galaxies was selected either because it was identified as a disk maser by (cite: Pesce_2015), and/or – as in the case of NGC 1068, NGC 1386, Circinus, and NGC 5643 – because it is a strong 22 GHz megamaser source associated with an AGN." (Data reduction and imaging)
- Why this method: Manual calibration was performed on all datasets to include observations that did not pass QA2 and to apply self-calibration uniformly; natural weighting was chosen to maximize point source sensitivity; direct visibility fitting was used for Circinus to achieve super-resolution astrometry via high SNR.  
  > "To maintain uniformity, we thus carry out our own data reduction on all datasets." (Data reduction and imaging); "We use the default “standard” $(u,v)$-gridding setting in `tclean`, and we specify natural visibility weighting to maximize the point source sensitivity of our images." (Imaging procedure); "With a $\sim$0.6 arcsecond beam and signal-to-noise ratios exceeding 200 for some lines, we expect to achieve relative positional uncertainties of several mas for a number of the maser features in the 183 GHz spectrum." (Circinus)
- Alternative: Using standard ALMA QA2 pipeline products directly (rejected to include non-QA2 datasets and perform self-calibration uniformly)  
  > "Though most of the observations taken as part of these ALMA projects have corresponding quality assurance 2 (QA2) data processing, we also include a handful of observations that did not achieve sufficient sensitivity to pass the QA2 level." (Data reduction and imaging); "To maintain uniformity, we thus carry out our own data reduction on all datasets." (Data reduction and imaging)
- Alternative: Line-by-line comparison between 183 GHz and 22 GHz spectra (rejected in favor of qualitative comparison of gross structures due to intrinsic maser variability on ~week timescales and large temporal separation between epochs)  
  > "We note that because these megamaser sources are known to be intrinsically variable on timescales of $\sim$weeks at 22 GHz (cite: Lo_2005), the typically large temporal separation between the observations of the 183 GHz masers presented in this paper and their 22 GHz counterparts from the literature renders a line-by-line comparison of the two spectra potentially fraught." (Results and discussion); "Instead, we opt here for a somewhat more qualitative comparison, aiming to determine whether the two sets of spectra exhibit comparable gross structures." (Results and discussion)
- Alternative: Using Hubble law distances for nearby galaxies NGC 5643 and Circinus (rejected in favor of SN Ia and Tully-Fisher distances because Hubble law distances are very uncertain at low redshift)  
  > "Because NGC 5643 is nearby enough that the Hubble law distance is expected to be very uncertain, we instead adopt a Type Ia Supernova distance of $12.4 \pm 0.5$ Mpc to this system from (cite: Burns_2020)." (NGC 5643); "Because Circinus is nearby enough that the Hubble law distance is very uncertain, we instead adopt the Tully-Fisher distance measurement of $4.2 \pm 0.8$ Mpc to this system from (cite: Karachentsev_2013)." (Circinus)
- Alternative: Using the targeted flux calibrator J1550+0527 for NGC 5765b (rejected because it was < 0.5 Jy; used gain calibrator J1504+1029 instead)  
  > "The targeted flux density calibrator for NGC 5765b, J1550+0527, was quite weak (flux density less than $\sim$0.5 Jy) at the time of the observation. We thus use the gain calibrator, J1504+1029, to also calibrate bandpass and flux density for NGC 5765b." (NGC 5765b)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Atmospheric 183 GHz water absorption creates a steep transmission gradient across the spectral windows for low-redshift targets (e.g., Circinus, NGC 1386), which is not compensated when computing the average RMS noise level.  
  > "We note that for our lowest-redshift targets (e.g., Circinus, NGC 1386), the steep transmission gradient caused by the atmospheric 183 GHz line causes the the RMS noise level to change substantially across the spectral windows. We do not attempt to compensate for this effect when computing the RMS, so our estimate is an average value across the entire spectral range." (Spectral line identification)
- Limitation (authors): Lack of absolute astrometric referencing between the 183 GHz ALMA map and the 22 GHz VLBI map of Circinus requires assuming that the brightest 183 GHz spot (561.6 km s^-1) and 22 GHz spot (566.3 km s^-1) coincide spatially.  
  > "It is plausible that both of these lines originate from the same parcel of masing gas (with the $\sim$5 km s$^{-1}$ difference in velocity then attributable to dynamical evolution during the $\sim$20 years separating the two observations), but the lack of absolute astrometric referencing between the two observations means that we cannot be sure; the relative locations of the 183 GHz maser system and the overplotted 22 GHz disk midline in [3](#fig:Circinus) should thus be understood to be an assumption." (Circinus)
- Limitation (authors): Large positional uncertainties in the C-4 configuration ALMA observations of Circinus prevent unambiguously distinguishing between a disk and outflow origin for the 183 GHz masers.  
  > "However, the current large uncertainties in our maser position measurements (relative to the size of the system) ultimately make it difficult to unambiguously distinguish between a disk and outflow origin for the 183 GHz masers in Circinus." (Circinus)
- Limitation (authors): Non-simultaneity between the 183 GHz ALMA observations and literature 22 GHz spectra, combined with maser time variability, prevents strict line-by-line comparisons.  
  > "We note that because these megamaser sources are known to be intrinsically variable on timescales of $\sim$weeks at 22 GHz (cite: Lo_2005), the typically large temporal separation between the observations of the 183 GHz masers presented in this paper and their 22 GHz counterparts from the literature renders a line-by-line comparison of the two spectra potentially fraught." (Results and discussion)
