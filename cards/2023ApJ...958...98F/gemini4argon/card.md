## Analysis recipe

1. **Measure basic dust continuum properties and dust disk size**: Fit a 2D Gaussian profile in the image plane using CASA imfit to obtain center coordinates, position angle, and inclination; integrate flux density above 5 sigma; compute azimuthally averaged intensity profiles using GoFish to derive radii enclosing 68%, 90%, and 95% of total flux.  
  > "We fit a 2D Gaussian profile using the *imfit* function in CASA to measure the basic dust disk properties (cite: Ohashi2023)." (Millimeter continuum); "Because the Gaussian fit does not properly account for the extended dust continuum structure, we integrate the flux above 5$\sigma$ to derive a total flux density of $317\pm 0.5$ mJy." (Millimeter continuum); "We used GoFish (cite: GoFish) to compute an azimuthally averaged flux (assuming inclination of $i=47^\circ$ and position angles of $\rm P.A.=149^\circ$), and defined the radii enclosing the 68$\%$, 90$\%$ and 95$\%$ of total flux" (Millimeter continuum)
   - software: CASA, GoFish
   - parameter: Flux integration threshold = 5 sigma; i = 47 deg; P.A. = 149 deg  
  > "we integrate the flux above 5$\sigma$ to derive a total flux density of $317\pm 0.5$ mJy." (Millimeter continuum); "We used GoFish (cite: GoFish) to compute an azimuthally averaged flux (assuming inclination of $i=47^\circ$ and position angles of $\rm P.A.=149^\circ$)" (Millimeter continuum)
   - assumption: Thin axisymmetric disk geometry transforming deconvolved aspect ratio 0.685 to inclination 46.7 deg  
  > "and a deconvolved disk aspect ratio of $0.685$ that we transform to disk inclination of $i_{disk} = 46.7^\circ$." (Millimeter continuum)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit via CASA imfit  
  > "We fit a 2D Gaussian profile using the *imfit* function in CASA to measure the basic dust disk properties (cite: Ohashi2023)." (Millimeter continuum)
   - validation: Visual confirmation of inner and outer rings in azimuthally averaged intensity profiles across different robust parameters, compared against Segura-Cox et al. (2020)  
  > "On the right panel of Figure [\[fig:continuum_image\]](#fig:continuum_image), we show azimuthally averaged intensity profiles of the continuum emission for different robust parameters. We visually confirm the presence of an inner ring at $\sim 0.16\ensuremath{''}$, and an outer ring at $0.34\ensuremath{''}$." (Millimeter continuum)
2. **Calculate dust disk mass**: Apply the Hildebrand (1983) optically thin thermal dust emission formula at 225 GHz using two dust temperature prescriptions.  
  > "We calculate the mass of the dust disk following equation ([\[eq:eq_dust_mass\]](#eq:eq_dust_mass)) proposed by (cite: Hildebrand1983), which assumes that the emission is optically thin and comes only from a thermal component." (Millimeter continuum); "We use two different dust temperatures to measure the mass dust of the disk: 1) the standard $\rm T_{dust} =$ 20 K value often used in Class II sources (cite: Pascucci16)" (Millimeter continuum); "2) the temperature prescription proposed by (cite: Tobin2020) of $\mathrm{T_{dust}= 43 \, (L_{bol})^{1/4}}$, which for Oph IRS63 yields a temperature $\rm T_{dust} =$46 K" (Millimeter continuum)
   - parameter: nu = 225 GHz; S_nu = 317 mJy; D = 132 pc; T_dust = 20 K and 46 K  
  > "In this equation, $S_{\nu}$ is the flux density calculated by integrating the emission above $5\sigma$, D is the distance to the source of 132 pc, $B_{\nu}(T_{d})$ is the Planck function at the observed frequency (225 GHz)" (Millimeter continuum)
   - assumption: Optically thin thermal emission; dust opacity kappa_nu = 2.3 cm^2 g^-1 of dust; D = 132 pc; L_bol = 1.3 L_sun  
  > "which assumes that the emission is optically thin and comes only from a thermal component." (Millimeter continuum); "and $\kappa_{\nu}$ is the absorption coefficient adopted as $\kappa_{\nu}$ = 2.3 cm$^{2}$ per gram of dust (cite: Beckwith1990)." (Millimeter continuum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Noted as lower limits because 1.3 mm continuum in the disk is optically thick  
  > "These values should be regarded as lower limits for the total disk mass of solids since the 1.3 mm emission in the disk is optically thick as discussed in (cite: Segura-Cox2020)." (Millimeter continuum)
3. **Generate moment maps and characterize molecular emission morphology**: Create moment 0 (integrated intensity), moment 1 (velocity), and moment 8 (peak intensity) maps of 12CO, 13CO, C18O, SO, and H2CO; fit a 2D Gaussian to the SO ring central depression to measure its offset from the continuum peak.  
  > "The images correspond to integrated intensity maps (moment 0) of three CO isotopologues: $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO, C$^{18}$O, in addition to the SO and H$_2$CO lines." (Molecular tracers); "Figure [\[fig:C18O_map\]](#fig:C18O_map) shows the moment 8 (maximum value in the spectrum) and the moment 1 maps for the C$^{18}$O line." (Envelope); "The center of the SO ring, or more precisely, the center of the emission drop, is offset by approximately $0.11\ensuremath{''}$ relative to the dust continuum emission, as measured through a 2D Gaussian fitting (see Figure [2](#fig:SO_spirals_annotation))." (Spirals)
   - software: CASA, CARTA
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit to SO ring emission drop  
  > "The center of the SO ring, or more precisely, the center of the emission drop, is offset by approximately $0.11\ensuremath{''}$ relative to the dust continuum emission, as measured through a 2D Gaussian fitting (see Figure [2](#fig:SO_spirals_annotation))." (Spirals)
   - validation: Positional offset compared against ALMA nominal positional accuracy of 0.023''  
  > "Although small, this difference is significant given that we can measure the positions of the SO emission with an accuracy of $0.023\ensuremath{''}$" (Spirals)
4. **Kinematic analysis of the disk and envelope via Position-Velocity (PV) diagrams**: Extract PV cuts along the disk major axis (5-pixel wide or ~0.5'') for 13CO, C18O, and SO; fit broken power-law (13CO, SO) or single power-law (C18O) rotation profiles using SLAM in both ridge and edge modes with MCMC to determine inner power-law index, break radius R_b, outer power-law index, systemic velocity, and dynamical stellar mass M_star.  
  > "To determine if the compact ($<2\ensuremath{''}$) disk-like structure is a rotating Keplerian disk and whether the large ($\sim10\ensuremath{''}$) structure is an infalling envelope, we perform a kinematic analysis using the Spectral Line Analysis/Modeling (SLAM) code (cite: SLAM)." (Kinematic analysis of the disk and envelope); "First, we obtained PV cuts along the major axis of the disk for the $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O isotopologues (5-pixel wide or $\sim 0.5\ensuremath{''}$) and show them in Figure [1](#fig:CO_PV_digrams_SLAM)." (CO emission); "We fit the full functional form of equation ([\[eq:slam\]](#eq:slam)), which consist of two power laws, to the $^{13}$CO emission." (CO emission)
   - software: SLAM
   - parameter: PV cut width = 5 pixels (~0.5''); P.A. = 149 deg; emission threshold > 5 sigma; filtered-out velocity channels excluded  
  > "First, we obtained PV cuts along the major axis of the disk for the $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O isotopologues (5-pixel wide or $\sim 0.5\ensuremath{''}$)" (CO emission); "In both cases, we assumed a distance of $d$=132 pc, a disk inclination of $i=47\degree$, and excluded the filtered-out velocity channels from the fit." (CO emission)
   - assumption: d = 132 pc; i = 47 deg; Keplerian rotation forced to derive M_star  
  > "In both cases, we assumed a distance of $d$=132 pc, a disk inclination of $i=47\degree$, and excluded the filtered-out velocity channels from the fit." (CO emission); "The stellar mass obtained from SLAM relates to this inner power law index, and by forcing Keplerian rotation, we derive a mass of $M_\star = 0.5 \pm 0.2$ $M_\odot$ (fifth column in Table [1](#table:slam_parameters))." (CO emission)
   - fitting: MCMC fitting in SLAM using both ridge (emission peak) and edge (outermost contour) modes; final parameters obtained by averaging/median of ridge and edge results  
  > "SLAM selects data points from the input PV in two ways; the ridge mode traces the peak of the emission at different channels, while the edge mode traces the outermost emission contour. SLAM performs the fitting process in an MCMC fashion" (Kinematic analysis of the disk and envelope); "The indicated uncertainties are the 14th and 68th statistical uncertainties from the MCMC analysis from SLAM. The adopted disk and stellar properties are obtained by averaging the results from the edge and ridge methods." (Table 1)
   - validation: Compared derived dynamical stellar mass with Brinch & Jørgensen (2013) after rescaling for distance and inclination; compared kinematic break radius with moment 1 velocity gradient transition  
  > "Our derived dynamical mass, is approximately consistent with the one derived by (cite: Brinch2013) of $\rm M_\star =0.4 \, M_\odot$ after re-scaling by our chosen source distance ($d$) and disk inclination angle" (CO emission); "The results from SLAM are consistent with what is seen in the SO moment 1 map in Figure [\[fig:Disk-size-comparison\]](#fig:Disk-size-comparison)" (SO emission)
5. **Model C18O and SO spiral/arc structures as infalling rotating streamers**: Apply the Mendoza et al. (2009) analytical ballistic model of a finite-sized rotating cloud collapsing onto a central point mass; manually adjust initial polar angle theta, azimuthal angle phi, and cloud radius R_cloud to visually match projected 2D trajectories and PV diagram velocity profiles for 3 C18O streamers and 3 SO spirals.  
  > "Thus, we would like to test whether their morphology can be reproduced by a collapsing rotating cloud that accretes to a central gravitational object. For this, we follow the analytical solutions proposed by (cite: Mendoza2009)." (Infalling streamer model); "We manually adjusted the $\theta$ and $\phi$ angles to match the position and velocities of the three arc-like features." (Infalling streamer model); "For SO, we set a smaller disk radius of $R_{disk} = 110$ au (following the results from SLAM on SO), and to better match the tightness of the spirals, we had to define a much smaller initial position of the streaming particles of only 275 au to 1320 au to match different spirals." (Infalling streamer model)
   - parameter: M_star = 0.5 M_sun; i = 47 deg; P.A. = 149 deg; near side = 59 deg E of N; For C18O: R_disk = 265 au, R_cloud = 5300 au, h_0 = 1250 au km s^-1, (theta, phi) = (57, 188), (85, 270), (108, 300) deg; For SO: R_disk = 110 au, R_cloud = 550, 275, 1320 au, (theta, phi) = (124, 260), (55, 155), (67, 75) deg  
  > "For simplicity, we assume that the rotational axis of the cloud and the disk are aligned ($i=47\degree$, P.A.= $149\degree$, the near side is $59\degree$ east of north)." (Infalling streamer model); "To model C$^{18}$O, we defined the inner edge of the cloud to be of size $R_{cloud} \sim 5300$ au (20 times the disk’s radius), fixed the mass of the protostar to $M_{\star} =0.5 \, M_\odot$ protostar, and set the initial angular specific momentum of the particles to $h_0 = \sqrt{R_{disk} \, GM_\star} \sim 1250$ $\rm au \, km \, s ^{-1}$" (Infalling streamer model); "C$^{18}$O - East | 57 | 188 | 5300" (Table 2)
   - assumption: Negligible pressure gradient (supersonic flow); rigid-body rotation at spherical cloud boundary; negligible self-gravity (central star dominates); aligned cloud and disk rotational axes  
  > "The assumptions involved in this model are: 1) The pressure gradient of the flow is negligible, 2) the particles at the boundary of the spherical cloud rotate as a rigid body, 3) self-gravity is negligible, and thus the massive central source dominates the motion." (Infalling streamer model)
   - fitting: Manual visual adjustment of theta, phi, and R_cloud  
  > "We manually adjusted the $\theta$ and $\phi$ angles to match the position and velocities of the three arc-like features." (Infalling streamer model); "We acknowledge that since we have not performed a mathematical fitting procedure, the values shown in Table [2](#table:streamer_c18o_parameters) might not be the best or even unique solutions to match the observations." (Infalling streamer model)
   - validation: Visual comparison of model trajectories over moment maps and velocity curves over PV diagrams

## Qualitative findings

- ALMA observations of Oph IRS63 reveal a shell-like bipolar outflow in 12CO (with wide and narrow blue-shifted components), an extended (>1300 au) rotating and infalling envelope in 13CO, large-scale arc/streamer structures in C18O, and three small-scale spiral arms connecting to a ring-like structure at ~0.5'' in SO.  
  > "Our ALMA observations of Oph IRS63 show a myriad of protostellar features, such as a shell-like bipolar outflow (in $^{12}$CO), an extended rotating envelope structure (in $^{13}$CO), a streamer connecting the envelope to the disk (in C$^{18}$O), and several small-scale spiral structures seen towards the edge of the dust continuum (in SO)." (Abstract); "The blue-shifted side of the outflow exhibits both a wide- and narrow-angle component, each displaying a complex shell-like structure." (Summary)
- The SO ring and spirals likely trace heated gas at the landing impact zone (slow shocks) where infalling streamers from the inner envelope meet the outer edge of the disk.  
  > "In either case, the temperature increase needed to enhance SO might have originated at the landing zone of the infalling material, i.e., the SO ring could trace an impact zone of the infalling envelope on the disk, as predicted, for example, in the slow shock models of (cite: Aota2015)." (The SO ring and spirals)
- The northern C18O streamer closely follows the outer edge of the red-shifted 12CO outflow cavity, suggesting the outflow compressed ambient envelope gas that is now infalling along the cavity rim.  
  > "The northern part of the red-shifted outflow appears to be spatially close to the C$^{18}$O streamer. We speculate that when the outflow opened the cavity observed in $^{12}$CO, it pushed the envelope creating a localized higher density region that we observe as a C$^{18}$O streamer." (Summary)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Oph IRS63 is the most evolved Class I source in the eDisk sample and the youngest source with known multiple dust rings/gaps (Segura-Cox et al. 2020); because the planned eDisk long-baseline observations were not executed, short-baseline ALMA DDT line data were combined with archival high-resolution continuum data to study the gas kinematics, streamers, and disk structure.  
  > "The ring and gap structures detected by (cite: Segura-Cox2020) make Oph IRS63 a prime target for the study of early planet formation in embedded disks." (Introduction); "We requested observations for Oph IRS63 as part of the eDisk Large program (project code: 2019.1.00261.L, PI N. Ohashi), however, our long-baseline data was not observed. Thus, we only present short-baseline molecular observations for Oph IRS63 obtained through ALMA DDT observations (2019.A.00034.S: PI: J. Tobin), and complement them with long-baseline continuum-only observations obtained from the ALMA archive (project code: 2015.1.01512.S, PI: D. Segura-Cox)." (Observations and Data Reduction)
- Why this method: SLAM PV power-law fitting distinguishes Keplerian rotation (v prop r^-0.5) from infall (v prop r^-1) and is necessary because embedded envelope emission precludes standard Class II flux-enclosed gas radius measurements; C18O is used for envelope mass because it is optically thin; near-IR Br-gamma is used for stellar accretion because high extinction prevents optical tracers.  
  > "It is worth mentioning that a Keplerian rotating structure has a rotational profile that falls off as the square root of the radius (i.e., $V \propto r^{-0.5}$), while an infalling envelope has a steeper drop in the rotational velocity with a profile following $V \propto r^{-1}$ (cite: Momose1998; Yen2013)." (Kinematic analysis of the disk and envelope); "To estimate this, we use the C$^{18}$O (2-1) emission as it is optically thin, and thus we can calculate the amount of mass traced by it." (Mass infalling rate); "Due to the embedded nature of Oph IRS63, optical tracers cannot be used to measure the mass accretion rate. Thus, we use the Br$\gamma$ line at 2.166 $\ensuremath{\mu}m$ obtained from the SpeX and iSHELL near-infrared spectra (see Appendix [3](#appendix:infrared))." ((proto)Stellar mass accretion rate)
- Alternative: Outflow origin for the SO emission was ruled out based on its kinematics (velocities within ±1.3 km/s of V_sys) and spatial distribution.  
  > "Due to the kinematics and spatial distribution of SO shown, we can rule out an outflow origin for the case of Oph IRS63." (Spirals)
- Alternative: Continuum over-subtraction artifact versus a true physical depletion ring to explain the central SO emission drop; both remain possible, though the 0.11'' offset from the dust peak favors a real ring.  
  > "Since we cannot distinguish between these two possibilities, we will refer to this feature as a ring. However, we caution the reader that more observations are needed to confirm that this is not a purely continuum-subtraction artifact." (The SO ring and spirals); "An additional support to the ring being a real structure comes from the off-centering between the continuum emission and SO emission depression." (The SO ring and spirals)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cloud contamination and interferometric filtering affect molecular channels near the systemic velocity and cause missing flux, making the C18O envelope mass a lower limit.  
  > "In some molecular tracers, our observations suffer from cloud contamination at channel maps close to systemic velocity. This feature is most likely caused by gas being filtered out by the ALMA interferometer." (Molecular tracers); "Additionally, our mass estimate is likely a lower limit value due to the flux missed by the interferometer." (Mass infalling rate)
- Limitation (authors): Continuum over-subtraction causes negative absorption artifacts at the disk center in optically thick lines (all species except SO), and cannot be completely ruled out as the cause of the central depression in SO.  
  > "Additionally, we see a strong negative absorption at the disk position for all the molecular species (except in SO). This artifact is caused by continuum over-subtraction, resulting from optically thick molecular lines at the line center" (Molecular tracers); "However, we caution the reader that more observations are needed to confirm that this is not a purely continuum-subtraction artifact." (The SO ring and spirals)
- Limitation (authors): The 1.3 mm dust continuum is optically thick in the disk, so the derived dust masses are lower limits; Toomre Q also depends on the assumed gas-to-dust ratio of 100.  
  > "These values should be regarded as lower limits for the total disk mass of solids since the 1.3 mm emission in the disk is optically thick as discussed in (cite: Segura-Cox2020)." (Millimeter continuum); "It is worth mentioning that the main uncertainties in deriving these values are that the gas-to-dust ratio could be different from 100 and the dust mass is likely a lower-limit." (Envelope infall vs. stellar accretion rate)
- Limitation (authors): The streamer parameters in the Mendoza et al. (2009) model were adjusted manually without mathematical optimization and are not unique solutions.  
  > "We acknowledge that since we have not performed a mathematical fitting procedure, the values shown in Table [2](#table:streamer_c18o_parameters) might not be the best or even unique solutions to match the observations." (Infalling streamer model)
- Limitation (authors): The envelope-to-disk mass infall rate is only an order-of-magnitude estimate, subject to missing interferometric flux and the assumption of free-fall velocity.  
  > "As mentioned in previous works that calculate such mass infall rates (cite: Yen2019; Ohashi1997; Takakuwa2013), this quantity is just an order of magnitude estimate, it could be higher if we consider that part of the large-scale emission can be filtered out by the interferometer, or it could be lower if the infalling velocity is slower than free-fall." (Mass infalling rate)
- Limitation (authors): The gas-to-dust disk size ratio uses a kinematic break radius for gas rather than a flux-enclosed radius due to envelope contamination, making it not directly comparable to Class II disk studies.  
  > "This gas-to-dust size ratio might not be directly comparable to studies in Class II disks because, in such cases, both the dust and gas disks’ radii are measured based on a fixed amount of flux enclosed in a certain radius (e.g., 90$\%$ or 95$\%$)." (Gas vs. dust disk size)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The edge and ridge methods in SLAM yield substantially different stellar masses (0.73 vs 0.22 M_sun for 13CO; 0.66 vs 0.33 M_sun for C18O) and outer power-law slopes (1.6 vs 0.9 for 13CO), so the adopted mean values carry systematic method-dependent uncertainty beyond formal MCMC errors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming the cloud and disk rotation axes are strictly aligned in the streamer modeling may be an oversimplification, especially given that the red-shifted outflow axis appears tilted relative to the disk axis and the three SO spirals require very different initial cloud radii (275, 550, and 1320 au).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The C18O mass infall rate integrates over the entire extended C18O envelope outside 265 au rather than isolating individual coherent streamer filaments, which may mix general envelope gas with active streamer channels.
