## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image ALMA continuum Stokes I/Q/U and N2D+ line cubes**: CASA calibration, two rounds of phase-only self-calibration on continuum, tclean imaging of Stokes I, Q, U and line cubes with C-1+C-3+C-4 combined  
  > "We performed two rounds of phase-only self-calibrations for the dust continuum." (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations); "We imaged the molecular line cubes and Stokes $I$, $Q$, and $U$ maps of dust continuum using the CASA task *TCLEAN*" (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Briggs robust = 0.5  
  > "with a Briggs weighting parameter of robust = 0.5" (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations)
   - parameter: pixel size 0.1''  
  > "We adopted a pixel size of $0.1 \ensuremath{''}$ for the imaging." (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Derive polarized intensity, position angle and field orientation from ALMA Q/U**: Debiased PI from Q, U and sigma_QU; theta_p = 0.5 arctan(U/Q) with propagated uncertainty; field orientation obtained by rotating polarization angles by 90 deg  
  > "$PI = \sqrt{Q^2 + U^2 - \sigma_{QU}^2}$ (cite: 2006PASP..118.1340V)" (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations); "The polarization position angle $\theta_{\mathrm{p}}$ is estimated with $\theta_{\mathrm{p}} = 0.5 \arctan(U/Q)$." (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations); "we rotate the dust polarization position angle by 90$\ensuremath{^\circ}$ to infer the magnetic field orientation" (3.1 Dust polarization and magnetic fields)
   - parameter: sigma_PI ~ sqrt(2) sigma_QU, with sigma_QU the 1-sigma rms on the background region of the Q or U maps  
  > "where $\sigma_{QU}$ is the 1$\sigma$ rms noise on the background region of the $Q$ or $U$ maps" (2.1 ALMA dust polarization and molecular line observations)
   - assumption: observed linear polarization is due to dust grain alignment  
  > "Assuming that the observed linear polarization of the dust continuum is due to dust grain alignment" (3.1 Dust polarization and magnetic fields)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: three-configuration images compared with the earlier two-configuration images, which show similar field morphology  
  > "exhibit magnetic field morphologies similar to the two configurations-combined (C-1 and C-3) ALMA images" (3.1 Dust polarization and magnetic fields)
3. **Compare field orientations across Planck, JCMT and ALMA scales**: Angular differences between orientations in each pixel; clump-scale averages from Q/U averaging within 1 pc (JCMT), cloud scale 10 pc (Planck), core scale 0.1 pc (ALMA)  
  > "we average the Planck polarization data (i.e., $Q$ and $U$) toward the cloud within a circle of 10 pc and the JCMT polarization detection toward each clump within a circle of 1 pc" (3.1.1 Comparing multi-scale magnetic fields); "we adopt the core positions in (cite: 2009ApJ...696..268Z) and average the polarization detections of each core within a radius of 0.1 pc" (3.1.1 Comparing multi-scale magnetic fields)
   - parameter: JCMT detection area S/N(PI) > 2  
  > "in every pixel of the JCMT detection area (S/N($PI$)$>$2)" (3.1.1 Comparing multi-scale magnetic fields)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Estimate core, clump and environment masses and column densities; fit mass-radius and density-radius relations**: Core dust mass from ALMA continuum flux with a modified blackbody; clump and cloud masses from the COMB map; environment from Planck tau353 and FCRAO 13CO; minimum chi-squared power-law fits  
  > "we estimate the dust mass with" (3.2.1 Core); "With a simple minimum chi-squared power-law fit, we obtain $M\propto r^{1.59\pm0.14}$." (3.2.4 Scaling relations)
   - parameter: beta ~1.5, T = 15 K, gas-to-dust 100, mu_H2 = 2.8  
  > "We adopt a dust emissivity index ($\beta$) of $\sim$1.5 (cite: 2007A&A...466.1065B; 2007ApJ...654L..87C) and a gas temperature of 15 K (cite: 2018RNAAS...2...52W)." (3.2.1 Core); "where $\mu_{\mathrm{H_2}} = 2.8$ is the mean molecular weight per hydrogen molecule" (3.2.1 Core)
   - parameter: ALMA area with S/N(I) > 5 used for total mass and effective radius; spherical average density n = 0.75 N / r  
  > "we only report the total mass and effective radius ($\sqrt{A/\pi}$) for the ALMA area with S/N($I$)$>$5" (3.2.4 Scaling relations); "the average number density is estimated from the average column density with $n = 0.75 N / r$" (3.2.4 Scaling relations)
   - assumption: gas-to-dust ratio 100; kappa = (nu/1 THz)^beta; spherical structures for density  
  > "Adopting a gas-to-dust ratio of $\Lambda = 100$ (cite: 1972ApJ...172..491S)" (3.2.1 Core); "Assuming the studied structures are spherical" (3.2.4 Scaling relations)
   - fitting: minimum chi-squared power-law fit, assuming a factor-of-2 uncertainty on mass and on density  
  > "Here we assume an uncertainty of a factor of 2 for the mass during the fitting." (3.2.4 Scaling relations); "Here we assume an uncertainty of a factor of 2 for the density during the fitting." (3.2.4 Scaling relations)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Measure velocity centroids, linewidths and velocity dispersions at each scale (incl. ALMA N2D+)**: Intensity-weighted velocity centroid maps; single-component Gaussian fits to average spectra to get FWHM and sigma_v; sigma_c from centroid dispersion; ALMA window within ~3 km/s of each clump Vlsr  
  > "we fit a single-component Gaussian profile for each line to obtain the Full Width at Half Maximum" (3.3.2 Multi-scale statistics); "For ALMA observations toward MM4 and MM9, we only consider velocities within $\sim$3 km s$^{-1}$ with respect to the $V_{\mathrm{lsr}}$ of each clump" (3.3 Molecular line and velocity fields)
   - parameter: FWHM = sqrt(8 ln 2) sigma_v  
  > "The relation between the FWHM and velocity dispersion is $FWHM=\sqrt{8\ln{2}} \sigma_v$." (3.3.2 Multi-scale statistics)
   - assumption: thermal contribution not subtracted; lines optically thin  
  > "so we regard the total linewidth as the non-thermal linewidth and do not subtract the thermal contribution." (3.3.2 Multi-scale statistics); "All lines are likely to be optically thin as the main-beam temperature of the line peaks is smaller than the gas/dust temperature" (3.3 Molecular line and velocity fields)
   - fitting: single-component Gaussian per line, no separation of multiple velocity components  
  > "We do not try to identify or separate the multiple velocity components" (Appendix Molecular line)
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **Estimate magnetic field strength at environment, clump and core scales**: DCF for Planck environment; ADF fits for JCMT clumps; CY16 (velocity centroid dispersion) and Liu21/Ost01 correction variants; core B recomputed with ALMA N2D+ velocity information  
  > "we use the angular dispersion function (ADF) method (cite: 2009ApJ...696..567H; 2009ApJ...706.1504H; 2016ApJ...820...38H), a modified DCF method" (3.4.2 Clump); "Here we recalculate the magnetic field strength with the CY16 and Liu21 corrections and with the velocity information from the ALMA N$_2$D$^+$ line data." (3.4.3 Core)
   - parameter: ADF lag binning interval l_bin = l_beam/2  
  > "We adopt a binning interval of $l_{\mathrm{bin}} = l_{\mathrm{beam}}/2$ for the distance lag." (3.4.2 Clump)
   - parameter: Environment: sigma_theta = 3.9 deg within r = 15 pc; sigma_c = 0.54 km/s  
  > "The angular dispersion of the Planck magnetic field is $\sigma_\theta = 3.9\ensuremath{^\circ}$ within the considered region." (3.4.1 Environmental gas)

## Qualitative findings

- Clump-scale average fields are bimodal relative to the cloud-scale Planck field (5 clumps within 30 deg, 5 clumps misaligned by 60-90 deg)  
  > "The bimodal distribution implies that the clump-scale magnetic field is organized with respect to the uniform cloud-scale Planck field." (3.1.1 Comparing multi-scale magnetic fields)
- In MM4 the core-scale field is preferentially aligned with the clump-scale field, and the clump field is perpendicular to the chain of cores  
  > "The clump-scale magnetic field is perpendicular to the chain of cores in MM4" (3.1.1 Comparing multi-scale magnetic fields)
- Relative orientation of field and column density gradient goes from perpendicular to parallel to random as column density increases, indicating a trans-to-sub-Alfvenic environment  
  > "As column densities increase, the magnetic field and column density gradient transit from preferentially more perpendicular to more parallel, then transit back to a random alignment." (Summary)
- Measured velocity statistics are discontinuous between instruments and lines, so no universal sigma_v-r relation is claimed  
  > "we find the velocity statistics are not continuous at different scales revealed by different instruments and lines, nor universal in different regions" (3.3.2 Multi-scale statistics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Follow-up of an earlier ALMA study, adding JCMT and Planck polarization to cover larger scales  
  > "As a follow-up work of (cite: 2020ApJ...895..142L), in this paper, we utilize the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope (JCMT) dust polarization observations and the Planck dust polarization data" (Introduction)
- Why this method: Angular dispersion alone cannot give M_A without corrections, so VGT is used as an alternative; ADF used where ordered-field structure is non-linear  
  > "Using the angular dispersion of polarization position angles to derive the Alfvénic Mach number $\mathcal{M}_{A}$ requires corrections for the LOS signal integration and other effects." (3.5.1 Alfvenic Mach number)
- Alternative: LOS-integration correction of the ADF method (Houde et al. 2009) not adopted because the autocorrelation function width could not be derived  
  > "So we refrain from adopting the correction for this effect suggested by (cite: 2009ApJ...706.1504H)." (3.4.2 Clump)
- Alternative: Alfven Mach number not derived from polarization angular dispersions because correction factors are unclear in individual regions  
  > "Thus, we refrain from deriving $\mathcal{M}_{A}$ from polarization angular dispersions in G28.34." (4.2 Magnetic fields or turbulence?)
- Alternative: Coupling-term energy equipartition not adopted in the DCF analysis; turbulent energy equipartition kept  
  > "we keep our assumption of turbulent energy equipartition" (3.4.2 Clump)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Turbulence anisotropy correction could not be applied at the cloud scale  
  > "Thus we are unable to correct this effect in this work." (3.4.1 Environmental gas)
- Limitation (authors): Only one core (MM4-core4) has enough ALMA polarization detections for a field strength estimate; MM9 polarization is marginal  
  > "only one core (MM4-core4) has sufficient polarization detections to derive the magnetic field strength" (3.4.3 Core); "the marginal polarization detection in MM9 does not support any statistics" (3.1.1 Comparing multi-scale magnetic fields)
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainty of lambda from modified DCF methods is hard to assess because of many assumptions  
  > "Although it is hard to assess the uncertainty of the $\lambda$ values estimated from the modified DCF methods due to the many assumptions adopted" (4.1.3 Gravity vs Magnetic fields)
- Limitation (authors): Large-scale turbulent support cannot be determined because of uncertain turbulent velocity dispersion  
  > "Due to the uncertainties in the turbulent velocity dispersion, we are unable to determine whether these large-scale structures are turbulence-supported or not." (4.1.2 Gravity vs Turbulence)
- Limitation (authors): JCMT POL-2 filters out large-scale total intensity, which may bias polarization fraction and lambda_KTH  
  > "This comparison suggests that the JCMT POL-2 data filters out a significant amount of the total intensity" (2.3 Planck 353 GHz dust polarization data)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): ALMA enters only at the core scale (MM4-core4 B strength, MM4 and MM9 masses and N2D+ kinematics); the cloud-to-clump results rest on Planck, JCMT and FCRAO data
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A single core provides the core-scale magnetic criticality and virial result
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed T = 15 K, beta = 1.5 and one density power law are applied across 0.05-15 pc, and the environment mass and density are extrapolated rather than measured
- Caveat (inferred, ours): DCF correction factors (CY16, Liu21, Ost01) change B by factors of ~1.5-3, so lambda ranges across and sometimes straddles 1
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Velocity dispersions from single-Gaussian fits are affected by LOS depth, off-position filtering and interferometric filtering, and enter alpha_turb as bounds only
- Caveat (inferred, ours): MM4-core4 B, radius and density are inherited from earlier work and are not re-derived here
