## Analysis recipe

1. **fit laboratory rotational spectra**: Fit of ground-state and three excited vibrational state spectra with a semi-rigid rotor Hamiltonian plus 14N nuclear quadrupole coupling, FTMW and mm/submm data combined  
  > "In total 48 hyperfine components were measured and analyzed using the effective Hamiltonian $H=H_{\text{R}}+H_{\text{Q}}$" (3.1 Ground-state rotational spectrum)
   - software: AABS
   - parameter: Weights from experimental uncertainties of 5 kHz (FTMW) and 30 kHz (millimeter wave)  
  > "Transitions measured in the FTMW spectra and those in the millimeter wave spectra were adequately weighted assuming the experimental uncertainties of 5 and 30 kHz, respectively." (3.1 Ground-state rotational spectrum)
   - assumption: Watson semi-rigid rotor Hamiltonian in A-reduction and I^r representation  
  > "is the standard Watson semi-rigid rotor Hamiltonian in $A$-reduction and I$^{\text{r}}$ representation (cite: Watson1977)" (3.1 Ground-state rotational spectrum)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **compute partition functions and vibrational correction**: Rotational partition function by direct summation over energy levels with SPCAT; vibrational part from Gordy and Cook Eq. 3.60 using 18 normal-mode frequencies; F_vib = Qvib x Qrot / Qvib-rot  
  > "We used the SPCAT program (cite: Pickett1991) to undertake this summation numerically" (3.2 Rotational spectra in excited vibrational states); "$F_{\rm vib}$ was computed as $Q_{\text{vib}}\times Q_{\text{rot}}/Q_{\text{vib-rot}}$ using the values listed in Table [1](#T2)." (4.2 Nondetection of propiolamide)
   - software: SPCAT
   - parameter: Maximum J of 200; vibrational energies 170, 210 and 300 cm-1 for v_in=1, v_out=1, v_inv=1  
  > "the vibrational energies of 170, 210, and 300 cm$^{-1}$ for $v_{\text{in}}=1$, $v_{\text{out}}=1$, and $v_{\text{inv}}=1$, respectively" (3.2 Rotational spectra in excited vibrational states)
   - assumption: Vibrational energies only roughly estimated from relative line intensities  
  > "The values of the vibrational energies were roughly estimated by comparison of spectral intensities of the same rotational transitions" (3.2 Rotational spectra in excited vibrational states)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **model Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2) spectra with LTE synthetic spectra**: Weeds LTE modeling of each species separately, then summed; propiolamide modeled with acetamide parameters, only column density free  
  > "The spectra of Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2) were modeled with the software Weeds (cite: Maret11) under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE)." (4.1 Observations); "We only kept the column density of propiolamide as a free parameter." (4.2 Nondetection of propiolamide)
   - software: Weeds
   - parameter: Five parameters per species: size theta_s, column density N, T_rot, linewidth, velocity offset; Vsys = 62 km/s (N1S), 74 km/s (N2)  
  > "and velocity offset ($V_{\rm off}$) with respect to the assumed systemic velocity of the source, $V_{\rm sys}=62$ km s$^{-1}$ for Sgr B2(N1S) and $V_{\rm sys}= 74$ km s$^{-1}$ for Sgr B2(N2)." (4.1 Observations)
   - parameter: Sgr B2(N1S): theta_s = 2.0'', T_rot = 160 K, dV = 5.0 km/s, Voff = 0.0, F_vib = 1.18  
  > "HCCC(O)NH$_2$ | n | 0 | 2.0 | 160 | $<$ 8.5 (15) | 1.18 | 5.0 | $0.0$ | $>$ 48 |" (Table (Sgr B2(N1S) column densities))
   - parameter: Sgr B2(N2): theta_s = 0.9'', T_rot = 180 K, dV = 5.0 km/s, Voff = 0.0, F_vib = 1.30  
  > "HCCC(O)NH$_2$ | n | 0 | 0.9 | 180 | $<$ 1.0 (16) | 1.30 | 5.0 | $0.0$ | $>$ 13 |" (Table (Sgr B2(N2) column densities))
   - assumption: LTE, justified by the high densities of the hot-core regions  
  > "This assumption is appropriate because the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) have high densities (cite: Bonfand19)." (4.1 Observations)
   - fitting: Only the column density of propiolamide is free; other parameters fixed to acetamide values. Sampler, priors and likelihood not stated  
  > "We only kept the column density of propiolamide as a free parameter." (4.2 Nondetection of propiolamide)
   - validation: Synthetic spectrum compared visually with ReMoCA spectra; no emission found  
  > "We found no evidence for emission of propiolamide toward either source." (4.2 Nondetection of propiolamide)
4. **derive column density upper limits and ratio to acetamide**: Upper limits on propiolamide column density from the nondetection, compared with the acetamide column density and with the HC3N/CH3CN ratio  
  > "implies that propiolamide is at least $\sim$50 and $\sim$13 times less abundant than acetamide toward Sgr B2(N1S) and Sgr B2(N2), respectively." (5.1 Comparison to other molecules in Sgr B2(N))
   - software: Weeds
   - assumption: Same T_rot, size, linewidth and velocity as acetamide  
  > "We used the parameters derived for acetamide, CH$_3$C(O)NH$_2$, by (cite: Belloche17; Belloche19) to compute LTE synthetic spectra of propiolamide" (4.2 Nondetection of propiolamide)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_

  > "We found no evidence for emission of propiolamide toward either source." (4.2 Nondetection of propiolamide)

  > "the observed upper limit to the ratio of propiolamide to acetamide seems consistent with the ratios of related species as determined from past simulations." (abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Sgr B2(N) high-mass star-forming region chosen as search target; ReMoCA used as latest 3 mm imaging survey; N1S chosen for its lower continuum opacity  
  > "We chose as a target for this interstellar search the high-mass star-forming region Sagittarius (Sgr) B2(N), which is located close to the Galactic center." (1 Introduction); "was chosen by (cite: Belloche19) for the lower opacity of its continuum emission, which allows for a deeper look into the molecular content of this hot core." (4.1 Observations)
- Why this method: Reliable interstellar searches need laboratory-measured or well-predicted rotational transitions; LTE justified by high density  
  > "Since reliable interstellar searches for propiolamide should be based on rotational transitions measured directly in the laboratory or those predicted from a data set that covers a broad spectral range" (1 Introduction); "This assumption is appropriate because the regions where hot-core emission is detected in Sgr B2(N) have high densities (cite: Bonfand19)." (4.1 Observations)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Vibrational energies used in the partition function were only roughly estimated  
  > "The values of the vibrational energies were roughly estimated by comparison of spectral intensities of the same rotational transitions" (3.2 Rotational spectra in excited vibrational states)
- Limitation (authors): Propiolamide and its chemistry are not in the chemical models, so formation mechanisms cannot be quantified  
  > "As this molecule and its surrounding chemistry are not included in our chemical models (other than in the most trivial fashion), the implications of such a mechanism cannot be quantified." (5.2 Formation mechanisms for propiolamide)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Upper limits rest on acetamide LTE parameters (size, T_rot, linewidth) assumed identical for propiolamide, with no exploration of other temperatures
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The detection threshold and the lines used for the limit are not specified
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No error estimates on the ALMA-derived limits beyond the stated bounds
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Chemical-model comparison relies on unpublished Garrod et al. (in prep.) models
