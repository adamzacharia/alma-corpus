## Analysis recipe

1. **Create moment-zero integrated intensity maps for WISDOM galaxies and extract central cutouts for PHANGS and simulated galaxies**: For WISDOM, apply the smooth-mask technique to primary beam-corrected cubes (smooth spatially with Gaussian FWHM = 1.5x beam and spectrally with 4-channel boxcar, threshold at >3x RMS of smoothed cube, apply mask to unsmoothed cube). For PHANGS, extract the innermost 3x3 kpc^2 from 'strict' moment-zero maps. For simulations, take face-on projected gas mass surface density maps and apply a 10 M_sun pc^-2 cut.  
  > "Each mask was generated by taking a copy of the cleaned, primary beam-corrected cube and smoothing it, first spatially using a Gaussian of full-width half-maximum (FWHM) equal to 1.5 times that of the synthesised beam, and then spectrally using a boxcar with a width of 4 channels." (Moment maps); "As we concentrate on the structure of the molecular ISM in galaxy centres we extract the innermost 3$\times$3 kpc$^2$ of each PHANGS map (the typical area covered by the WISDOM observations)." (PHANGS galaxies); "so we create mock moment zero maps by imposing a gas mass surface density cut of 10 M$_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$, where the gas is expected to become primarily molecular (cite: 2009ApJ...693..216K)." (Simulations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: spatial smoothing Gaussian FWHM = 1.5 x synthesised beam; spectral boxcar width = 4 channels; amplitude threshold > 3 x RMS noise; central region = 3 x 3 kpc^2; simulation threshold = 10 M_sun pc^-2  
  > "We then select pixels with an amplitude in the smoothed cube greater than 3 times the root-mean-squared (RMS) noise in that cube." (Moment maps)
   - assumption: Gas in simulations becomes primarily molecular above a surface density of 10 M_sun pc^-2.  
  > "so we create mock moment zero maps by imposing a gas mass surface density cut of 10 M$_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$, where the gas is expected to become primarily molecular (cite: 2009ApJ...693..216K)." (Simulations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Verified that exact moment-map threshold and regenerating PHANGS maps with WISDOM technique do not affect results, and exact simulation surface density cut does not change conclusions.  
  > "The exact threshold used to create the moment maps does not have a strong effect on our results, unless it is set unreasonably low" (Moment maps); "Regenerating the PHANGS moment-zero maps by applying the WISDOM masked-moment technique would not affect our results." (PHANGS galaxies)
2. **Estimate total H2 gas masses and central 1 kpc mean molecular gas surface densities**: Convert integrated CO intensities in moment-zero maps to H2 mass within the ALMA field of view and mean surface density within an elliptical aperture of radius 1 kpc (accounting for inclination and setting non-detected pixels to zero).  
  > "Mean molecular gas surface densities within the central one kiloparsec (in radius) of each galaxy were estimated from our moment zero map, using the $\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}$ and line ratio assumptions described above, and taking the inclination of the source into account." (Molecular gas masses); "This molecular gas surface density is calculated within an elliptical aperture 1 kpc in radius." (Central molecular gas mass surface density)
   - parameter: aperture radius = 1 kpc; fixed uncertainty = 10%  
  > "We here assume a fixed 10% uncertainty in our total H$_2$ masses and surface densities to reflect the uncertainty in the distance estimates we have available." (Molecular gas masses)
   - assumption: Milky Way-like CO-to-H2 conversion factor of 2x10^20 cm^-2 (K km s^-1)^-1 (alpha_CO ~ 4.36 M_sun (K km s^-1)^-1 pc^-2), line ratios T_b,CO(2-1)/T_b,CO(1-0) = 0.7 and T_b,CO(3-2)/T_b,CO(1-0) = 0.3, and zero surface density in non-detected regions.  
  > "We assume a typical Milky Way-like CO-to-H$_{2}$ conversion factor of 2$\times$10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ (cite: Dickman:1986jz) (equivalent to $\alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}\approx4.36\,$M$_{\odot}$ (K km s$^{-1}$)$^{-1}$ pc$^{-2}$) and that the $J_{\rm upper}=2$ or $3$ CO lines have a line ratio ${T_{\mathrm{b, CO(2-1)}}/T_{\mathrm{b, CO(1-0)}}}=0.7$ and ${T_{\mathrm{b, CO(3-2)}}/T_{\mathrm{b, CO(1-0)}}}=0.3$ (cite: 2021arXiv210911583L)." (Molecular gas masses); "We assume that the surface density of the gas in non-detected regions is zero, and include this in our averages." (Central molecular gas mass surface density)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Tested calculating mean surface density using only detected material, which increases calculated surface densities by less than a factor of two on average.  
  > "Calculating the mean surface density of only the detected material instead makes very little difference (increasing the calculated surface densities by less than a factor of two on average)." (Central molecular gas mass surface density)
3. **Convolve moment-zero maps to a uniform physical resolution and compute non-parametric morphology statistics (Asymmetry, Smoothness, Gini)**: Convolve moment-zero maps covering the inner 3x3 kpc^2 to a fixed physical resolution of 120 pc, mask using elliptical apertures containing 90% of the CO flux, and compute Asymmetry (180 deg rotation subtraction), Smoothness (boxcar smoothing subtraction with width sigma = 500 pc), and Gini coefficient.  
  > "In each case these measurements are performed on the moment-zero map, which covers the inner $3\times3$ kpc$^{2}$ of each system (see Table 3). The moment maps are further masked using elliptical apertures (defined using the position angle and inclination of each system) that contain 90% of the CO flux." (Non-parametric gas morphology measurements); "The boxcar filter width $\sigma$ is set to 500 pc here, and we explore the impact of this choice in Section 4.1." (Smoothness); "To avoid this issue, in this paper we present measurements of the above statistics made on moment zero maps convolved to a fixed physical resolution of 120 pc." (The impact of resolution)
   - software: NUMPY, SCIPY, ASTROPY
   - parameter: convolved resolution = 120 pc; elliptical aperture flux threshold = 90% (+/- 5% for uncertainties); Smoothness boxcar filter width sigma = 500 pc; Asymmetry rotation angle = 180 deg  
  > "We calculate uncertainties that correspond to the change in each statistic when altering this flux threshold by $\pm5$%, and list these in Table 3." (Non-parametric gas morphology measurements)
   - assumption: Background correction terms for Asymmetry and Smoothness are negligible because statistics are applied to masked moment-zero maps.  
  > "We note that a background Asymmetry term is typically included when calculating this parameter in optical images, but this is neglected here as the correction is minimal" (Asymmetry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared measurements at native resolution vs 120 pc, tested uv-plane tapering vs image-plane convolution on several WISDOM galaxies (<10% difference), tested removing 12 galaxies with native resolution 130-220 pc, tested varying flux threshold between 75-95%, and tested removing the faintest 30% of sightlines.  
  > "Tests on several of the WISDOM objects suggests that the non-parametric morphology measurements obtained from tapered data differ very little (by $<10$%) from those obtained on convolved datasets" (The impact of resolution); "To test this we repeated the analysis described above after removing the faintest 30% of sightlines for each object. This did not change any of our conclusions." (Faint sightlines)
4. **Correlate ISM morphology statistics with galaxy physical properties, bars, AGN, Toomre Q ratio, and stellar volume density**: Compute Spearman rank correlation coefficients between non-parametric morphology indices (A, S, Gini) and galaxy parameters (M_*, sigma_*, mu_*, Sigma_H2,1kpc, Sigma_H2,1kpc/mu_*, M_H2/M_*, SFR, sSFR, SFE, rho_*, rho_*,q), perform KS tests for barred vs unbarred and AGN vs non-AGN galaxies, and calculate Q_*/Q_g.  
  > "The Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients (listed in Table 1) show that these correlations are all significant, with the stellar mass and effective stellar mass surface density correlations being the strongest." (Stellar mass and the galaxy potential); "Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests doe not reveal any evidence of a difference in the Asymmetry and Smoothness values of barred and non-barred galaxies" (Bars); "Assuming $\sigma_g$ is $\approx$8 km s$^{-1}$(e.g. (cite: 2013AJ....146..150C)), in Figure 8 we show that $\approx$75% of our sample galaxies have $\frac{Q_*}{Q_g}<1$" (Understand the correlations with $\mu_*$)
   - software: SCIPY
   - parameter: significance threshold p < 0.05; gas velocity dispersion sigma_g = 8 km s^-1; axis ratios q = 1 (spherical) or q = 0.8 (ETGs) and 0.27 (spirals)  
  > "In the right panel of Figure 9 we assume that our ETGs are as spherical as the roundest slow-rotators in (cite: 2017MNRAS.472..966F), while all spirals are as flat as the flattest fast-rotating galaxies from the same work ($q=$0.8 and 0.27, respectively)." (Understand the correlations with $\mu_*$)
   - assumption: Constant gas velocity dispersion sigma_g ~ 8 km s^-1 across galaxies; for WISDOM sources, mean gas surface density within R_e is estimated using total H2 mass and R_e.  
  > "given that all the ETGs in the WISDOM sample have centrally concentrated gas that is all contained within the effective radius we can estimate the mean surface density using the total masses and radii presented in Table" (Understand the correlations with mu_*)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Repeated correlation analysis after removing galaxies with inclinations i > 60 deg and i > 45 deg, and tested spiral-only subsamples.  
  > "We tested how much this could affect our results both by checking for residual correlations with inclination (see Section 5.1), and by repeating our entire analysis with all systems at $i>60^{\circ}$ removed." (Galaxy inclination); "Even imposing an extreme inclination cut by considering galaxies with $i<45^{\circ}$ does not change our key results" (Galaxy inclination)
5. **Determine optimal predictors of ISM morphology using multivariate linear regression and feature selection**: Use univariate and multivariate ordinary least-squares linear regression and Sequential Feature Selection with cross-validation in scikit-learn on 12 observed parameters to find the optimal subset predicting A, S, and Gini.  
  > "We begin by using the linear regressor in SCIKIT-LEARN (cite: scikit-learn) to fit linear relations between our non-parametric morphology measures and observations quantities." (Optimal predictors); "We then attempt to extend this by using the multivariate linear regressor (again from SCIKIT-LEARN) which finds the $n$-dimensional hyperplane that best fits our observed data using an ordinary least-squares regression." (Optimal predictors); "In order to determine which parameters provide the most diagnostic power we make use of ‘Sequential Feature Selection’ (cite: FERRI1994403) within SCIKIT-LEARN." (Optimal predictors)
   - software: SCIKIT-LEARN
   - parameter: 12 candidate features: stellar mass, effective radius, inclination, SFR, sSFR, SFE, Sigma_H2,1kpc, M_H2, mu_*, Sigma_H2,1kpc/mu_*, beam size in pc, beam size in arcsec  
  > "We include the following observed parameters, which we believe could potentially drive the observed correlations (either physically, or via observational effects): stellar mass, effective radius, inclination, SFR, sSFR, SFE, ${\Sigma_{\rm H_2,1kpc}}$, M$_{H2}$, $\mu_*$, ${\Sigma_{\rm H_2,1kpc}}$/$\mu_*$, and the beam size in parsecs and arcseconds." (Optimal predictors)
   - assumption: Log-linear relations between non-parametric morphology indicators and physical parameters.  
  > "We then attempt to extend this by using the multivariate linear regressor (again from SCIKIT-LEARN) which finds the $n$-dimensional hyperplane that best fits our observed data using an ordinary least-squares regression." (Optimal predictors)
   - fitting: Ordinary least-squares linear regression and cross-validated Sequential Feature Selection  
  > "This algorithm attempts to find the optimum combination of features to include in a feature subset using cross-validation." (Optimal predictors)
   - validation: Quantified root-mean-squared (RMS) scatter as features were added sequentially (RMS = 0.29 for 1 feature, 0.26 for 2, 0.25 for 3, vs 0.24 for all 12).  
  > "We find that $\mu_*$ is the single feature that can predict the Asymmetry parameter most strongly, with an RMS scatter of 0.29. If allowed to include a second parameter the algorithm chooses ${\Sigma_{\rm H_2,1kpc}}$ (resulting in predictions with an RMS scatter of 0.26). The third important parameter is the SFE, resulting in predictions with an RMS scatter of 0.25." (Optimal predictors)

## Qualitative findings

- Early-type galaxies have smooth, regular, symmetric central molecular gas morphologies, whereas spiral galaxy centres have much more clumpy and asymmetric molecular ISM distributions at the same physical scale (120 pc).  
  > "We show that early-type galaxies tend to have smooth, regular molecular gas morphologies, while the ISM in spiral galaxy bulges is much more asymmetric and clumpy when observed at the same spatial scales." (Abstract); "It seems that early type systems have a smooth, symmetric ISM distribution (low Asymmetry, Smoothness and Gini), while the ISM in spiral galaxy centres is clumpy and asymmetric (high Asymmetry, Smoothness and Gini)." (Non-parametric morphologies)
- Effective stellar mass surface density (or volume density) is the strongest predictor of central molecular gas morphology, indicating that the depth and shape of the stellar gravitational potential dominate over gas self-gravity.  
  > "We find the effective stellar mass surface (or volume) density to be the strongest predictor of the morphology of the molecular gas, while star formation and bars maybe be important secondary drivers." (Abstract); "We find that gas self-gravity is not the dominant process shaping the morphology of the molecular gas in galaxy centres." (Abstract)
- Optically classified AGN in the PHANGS spiral sample do not show statistically significant differences in central molecular gas morphology compared to non-AGN galaxies.  
  > "No significant differences in the non-parametric gas morphology coefficients were found between galaxies with and without AGN." (AGN)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: WISDOM provides high angular resolution (~0.1 arcsec, ~30 pc) ALMA maps of a diverse sample of ETGs and spirals, while PHANGS provides a large, homogeneously selected sample of 60 nearby mostly spiral galaxies with similar surface brightness sensitivity (~0.17 K vs 0.2 K) that was not selected for optical dust-lane regularity.  
  > "This issue should be ameliorated somewhat by the inclusion of the larger PHANGS sample which used different selection criteria (see below), and will be discussed further in Section 4.3.1." (Sample selection); "The PHANGS galaxies were observed with ALMA to a typical surface brightness sensitivity of 0.17 K (or $\approx$11 M$_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$ with 10 km s$^{-1}$ channels and assumptions as per Section 2.1.4), very similar to the sensitivity reached by the WISDOM observations, simplifying comparisons between the two." (PHANGS galaxies)
- Why this method: Non-parametric morphology indices (Asymmetry, Smoothness, Gini) Convolved to a fixed 120 pc physical resolution allow quantitative, resolution-matched comparisons between observations and idealised hydrodynamic simulations.  
  > "To quantitatively investigate the morphology of the ISM in the WISDOM galaxies (and compare to the simulated galaxies), we make use of modified versions of the Asymmetry statistic, Smoothness statistic and Gini coefficient" (Non-parametric gas morphology measurements); "To avoid this issue, in this paper we present measurements of the above statistics made on moment zero maps convolved to a fixed physical resolution of 120 pc." (The impact of resolution)
- Alternative: Concentration and M20 statistics were considered alongside Asymmetry, Smoothness, and Gini, but rejected because they showed no strong correlations with any probed physical variables.  
  > "The concentration and M20 statistics (which both measure the central concentration of the gas; (cite: 2000AJ....119.2645B; Conselice2003)) typically used alongside Asymmetry, Smoothness and Gini do not show strong correlations with any of the physical variables probed here, and hence we do not consider them further." (Non-parametric gas morphology measurements)
- Alternative: Tapering visibilities in the uv-plane and re-imaging to reach 120 pc resolution was considered, but image-plane convolution was used instead because calibrated visibilities were not available for all sources and tests showed <10% difference.  
  > "As we do not have access to the calibrated interferometric data for all our sources, however, we here simply convolved each map to a fixed physical resolution of 120 pc. Tests on several of the WISDOM objects suggests that the non-parametric morphology measurements obtained from tapered data differ very little (by $<10$%) from those obtained on convolved datasets" (The impact of resolution)
- Alternative: Three physical scenarios for setting central ISM morphology were considered; scenario (i) gravitational potential alone and scenario (iii) star formation feedback alone were disfavoured in favour of scenario (ii) both potential well depth and star-formation-driven turbulence.  
  > "Below we outline three broad scenarios which could be at play in shaping the morphology of the ISM in our galaxy centres." (What sets the morphology of the ISM?); "Overall our results seem to favour scenario ii) discussed above- the morphology of the gas in galaxy centres seems to be set not by self-gravity, but by both the depth of the potential well, and turbulence driven by star formation." (Optimal predictors)
- How they decided it was enough: In Sequential Feature Selection, adding parameters beyond the top three (mu_*, Sigma_H2,1kpc, SFE) did not substantially improve the RMS scatter of the prediction (0.25 for 3 features vs 0.24 for all 12).  
  > "The third important parameter is the SFE, resulting in predictions with an RMS scatter of 0.25. Adding additional parameters beyond this point does not substantially improve the prediction." (Optimal predictors)
- Limitation (authors): WISDOM targets were selected to have regular central dust lanes in HST imaging, which may bias their molecular gas morphologies, and WISDOM dominates the ETG sample.  
  > "In addition, where possible, targets were selected to have regular dust lanes in their centres (based on imaging from the Hubble Space Telescope; HST). This may bias the morphology of the molecular media in these targets, assuming the dust and molecular gas are co-spatial." (Sample selection); "WISDOM dominates the ETG sample used here, thus the impact of selection effects may be larger in this population." (Sample selection)
- Limitation (authors): Stellar velocity dispersion measurements were compiled from disparate literature sources (IFU, long-slit, fibre) with different apertures, introducing significant scatter.  
  > "Given their disparate origins (e.g. from IFU, long-slit and fibre spectroscopic measurements within different apertures), significant scatter should be expected when correlating these with other galaxy properties, but we include them here for completeness." (Ancillary data)
- Limitation (authors): Molecular gas masses and surface densities rely on a fixed Milky Way CO-to-H2 conversion factor and fixed CO line ratios, and neglect atomic gas.  
  > "Further uncertainties due to the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor and assumed line-ratio may also be present, and we discuss these additional factors further in later sections." (Molecular gas masses); "We note that the mass surface densities measured here are for molecular gas only, and rely intimately on our assumptions for the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor." (Central molecular gas mass surface density)
- Limitation (authors): Simulated galaxies have lower effective stellar surface densities and significantly lower central 1 kpc gas surface densities than the observed galaxies due to simulation setup, and do not directly track molecular chemistry.  
  > "Formally the simulations do not track molecular gas, so we create mock moment zero maps by imposing a gas mass surface density cut of 10 M$_{\odot}$ pc$^{-2}$" (Simulations); "By construction the simulated galaxies have somewhat lower stellar surface densities within their effective radii" (Simulations)
- Limitation (authors): Uniform 3D rotation curves, resolved stellar mass maps, direct homogeneous gas turbulence measurements, intrinsic 3D galaxy axis ratios (q), and gas inflow rates are not available for the sample.  
  > "In principle, one would wish to correlate measures of ISM morphology directly with the properties of each galaxy’s circular velocity curve. However, suitable rotation curves for these galaxies are not available uniformly, and obtaining them is beyond the scope of this work." (Stellar mass and the galaxy potential); "Direct, homogeneous measurements of the molecular gas turbulence of our sample galaxies unfortunately are not available, and obtaining them is beyond the scope of this work." (Star formation); "Unfortunately we do not know the 3D geometry of our galaxies, and thus $q$, and cannot estimate $\rho_*$ with certainty." (Understand the correlations with $\mu_*$)
- Limitation (authors): Twelve galaxies have native spatial resolutions of 130-220 pc, coarser than the 120 pc target resolution.  
  > "Twelve of the galaxies observed in this work have native spatial resolutions of 130-220 pc, significantly larger than our 120 pc limit." (The impact of resolution)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Applying a constant Milky Way alpha_CO and fixed CO(2-1)/CO(1-0) and CO(3-2)/CO(1-0) line ratios across both quiescent ETGs and starburst/barred spiral nuclei could introduce systematic biases in Sigma_H2,1kpc and SFE that correlate with mu_*.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using global SFR and global or FOV-limited H2 masses to compute SFE, rather than spatially resolved central SFE within the inner 3x3 kpc^2, mixes disc and nuclear regimes in PHANGS spirals.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Strong internal collinearity between mu_*, Sigma_H2,1kpc, and SFE (since SFE = SFR/M_H2) means ordinary least-squares regression coefficients can be unstable and reflect algebraic coupling rather than independent physical drivers.
