## Science

- Fields: debris_disks
- Question: What is the occurrence rate and what are the properties of debris discs around F stars in the 23 Myr-old beta Pic moving group compared to other young moving groups and older field stars, and what explains the rapid decay in fractional luminosity?  
  > "Only 20% of old field stars have detectable debris discs, leaving open the question of what disc, if any, is present around the remaining 80%." (Abstract); "This paper considers the population of F stars in the 23 Myr-old BPMG where we find that 9/12 targets possess discs." (Abstract); "This is higher than the $20\%$ seen for Gyr-old stars (cite: su-et-al-2006; marshall-et-al-2016; sibthorpe-et-al-2018) leading to the question why we get such a high frequency." (Introduction)

## Analysis recipe

1. **Assess IR excess and fit modified blackbody SEDs**: Fit PHOENIX stellar photosphere models to photometry to determine L and Teff, subtract stellar emission to identify >=3sigma IR excesses (22-100 um), and fit a modified blackbody model to the excess emission.  
  > "We fit PHOENIX stellar photosphere models (cite: brott-hauschildt-2005) for each target using the stellar luminosity and the stellar temperature as model parameters." (Modelling procedure); "After subtracting the stellar emission the disc is fitted with a modified blackbody model (cite: backman-paresce-1993) for which the thermal emission of the dust is described as" (Modelling procedure)
   - parameter: T_BB, R_BB, f_d, lambda_0, beta  
  > "The parameter $\lambda_0$ represents the characteristic wavelength while $\beta$ is the opacity index. From this model we derive the dust temperature, $T_\text{BB}$, and the resulting blackbody radius of the disc, $R_\text{BB}$, as well as the fractional luminosity, $f_\text{d}$ (see Tab. [\[tab:SEDresults\]](#tab:SEDresults))." (Modelling procedure)
   - assumption: Dust acts like blackbodies in equilibrium with stellar radiation for R_BB; parameter values follow a normal distribution for confidence levels  
  > "Here, $R_\text{BB}$ is the distance from the star that the temperature implies if the dust acted like blackbodies in equilibrium with the stellar radiation." (Modelling procedure); "The resulting distribution in parameter space represents an estimate for the probability distribution of the parameters and thus allows us to calculate the confidence levels for the parameters assuming that the values follow a normal distribution (cite: pawellek-2017)." (Modelling procedure)
   - fitting: Minimum chi^2 grid/random sampling around best fit to estimate parameter probability distribution  
  > "We start at the position of the minimum $\chi^2$ in parameter space, i.e. from the best fitting $f_\text{d}$ and $R_\text{BB}$. A set of new parameter values is randomly generated from which we calculate the SED." (Modelling procedure)
   - validation: Checked WISE and MIPS images for binary confusion (e.g., HD 14082A, HD 199143, HD 19994, HD 56986)  
  > "After checking the WISE and MIPS data we found the photometry to be confused in all bands since those instruments were not able to differentiate between the two stellar components." (Notes for particular targets)
2. **Calibrate, concatenate, average, and image archival ALMA data**: Calibrate each dataset with original observatory CASA/pipeline version, concatenate multi-epoch visibility datasets in CASA v5.4.0, average in time and frequency, image continuum with tclean (multiscale, MFS), visually inspect cubes for CO gas, and integrate within 5" circular aperture for non-detection upper limits.  
  > "Later processing was carried out homogeneously in CASA v5.4.0. If available, for each target, we concatenated multiple datasets at similar frequencies to obtain a final combined visibility dataset. We also averaged in time (to 30s integrations) and frequency (to 2 GHz-wide channels) to reduce the size of each dataset and speed up imaging and modelling." (ALMA reduction procedure); "We conservatively derive 3$\sigma$ upper limits of $<5.8$ and $<3.5$ mJy for the flux density of the two belts, respectively, by spatially integrating emission within a 5$\ensuremath{''}$ radius circular region centered on the expected stellar location." (ALMA reduction procedure); "We visually checked the data cubes of the four ALMA resolved targets for CO gas emission, but did not detect any." (Gas emission)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: 30s time averaging, 2 GHz channel width, 5" radius aperture for upper limits  
  > "We also averaged in time (to 30s integrations) and frequency (to 2 GHz-wide channels) to reduce the size of each dataset and speed up imaging and modelling." (ALMA reduction procedure); "We conservatively derive 3$\sigma$ upper limits of $<5.8$ and $<3.5$ mJy for the flux density of the two belts, respectively, by spatially integrating emission within a 5$\ensuremath{''}$ radius circular region centered on the expected stellar location." (ALMA reduction procedure)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Measured RMS noise levels in image regions free of source emission  
  > "The weighting choices, RMS noise levels (measured in image regions free of source emission), and synthesised beam sizes achieved are listed in Table [\[tab:obslog\]](#tab:obslog)." (ALMA reduction procedure)
3. **Model ALMA visibilities to derive belt radii and geometries**: Compute model images of a 3D axisymmetric radial and vertical Gaussian dust density distribution using RADMC-3D, Fourier transform and sample at observed u-v points using GALARIO, and fit to observed visibilities with MCMC using EMCEE v3.  
  > "For the visibility modelling, we follow the method described e.g. in (cite: matra-et-al-2019b), using RADMC-3D[^2] to calculate model images from a given density distribution, which we here assume to be a radial and vertical Gaussian described by" (ALMA reduction procedure); "After producing a model image, we Fourier Transform it and sample the model visibility function at the same *u-v* locations as the data using the GALARIO software package (cite: tazzari-et-al-2018)." (ALMA reduction procedure); "This process is then used to fit the model to the data through a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) implemented using the EMCEE v3 software package (cite: foreman-mackey-et-al-2013; foreman-mackey-et-al-2019)." (ALMA reduction procedure)
   - software: RADMC-3D, GALARIO, EMCEE
   - parameter: F_nu_star, F_nu_belt, R (r_c), Delta R (FWHM of Gaussian), h=H/R (fixed to 0.03 when unconstrained), i, PA, Delta RA, Delta Dec, weight rescaling factor  
  > "The vertical aspect ratio $h=H/R$ is radially constant and fixed to 0.03 for belts that are too face-on or too low S/N for it to be meaningfully constrained." (ALMA reduction procedure); "In addition to the model parameters entering the equation describing the density distribution, we fit for RA and Dec offsets of the belt’s geometric center from the phase center of the observations, and for a weight rescaling factor to account for the inaccurate data weights (and hence uncertainties) typically delivered by ALMA (cite: marino-et-al-2018; matra-et-al-2019b)." (ALMA reduction procedure)
   - assumption: Radial and vertical Gaussian density distribution; radially constant vertical aspect ratio h=H/R; blackbody grains with temperature scaling as r^-0.5  
  > "For the visibility modelling, we follow the method described e.g. in (cite: matra-et-al-2019b), using RADMC-3D[^2] to calculate model images from a given density distribution, which we here assume to be a radial and vertical Gaussian described by" (ALMA reduction procedure); "When calculating model images, we also assume the grains to act as blackbodies and therefore to have a temperature scaling as $r^{-0.5}$." (ALMA reduction procedure)
   - fitting: MCMC using EMCEE v3 with Goodman & Weare (2010) affine-invariant sampler, likelihood proportional to exp(-chi^2), uniform priors on all model parameters, reporting 50th (+34/-34) percentiles  
  > "This samples the posterior probability function of the n-dimensional parameter space of our model using the affine-invariant sampler of (cite: goodman-weare-2010). We use a likelihood function $\propto e^{-\chi^2}$ and uniform priors on all model parameters." (ALMA reduction procedure); "Tab. [\[tab:resolveddisc\]](#tab:resolveddisc) shows best-fit parameters and uncertainties derived for each of the resolved belts, taken as the 50$^{+34}_{-34}$th percentiles of the marginalised posterior probability distribution of each parameter." (ALMA reduction procedure)
   - validation: Subtracted best-fit visibility model from data and imaged residuals without CLEAN deconvolution, plus inspected deprojected visibility profiles  
  > "Fig. [2](#fig:ALMA_Models) shows full-resolution model images and residuals obtained by subtracting the best fit visibility model from the data, and imaging without CLEAN deconvolution." (ALMA reduction procedure)
4. **Fit Herschel/PACS images and compare resolved radii to blackbody radii**: Convolve an axisymmetric optically thin power-law ring model with the rotated/scaled PSF of calibration star HD 164058, fit to Herschel/PACS images using emcee, and fit the power-law relation R_disc/R_BB = A*(L/L_sun)^B to the combined BPMG and Matrà et al. (2018) ALMA samples.  
  > "The PSF of the star is derived from observations of the *Herschel* calibration star HD 164058 and then rotated to the appropriate orientation and scaled to the stellar flux derived from the SED." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS); "The model disc is convolved with the PSF and then compared to the *Herschel*/PACS image by calculating the goodness of fit, $\chi^2$. We estimate the parameters using the emcee package." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS); "Including the ALMA-sample of (cite: matra-et-al-2018) and fitting a trend of the form in eq. [\[eq:trueradius\]](#eq:trueradius) for how the ratio depends on stellar luminosity we infer a slight decrease of the ratio with stellar luminosity finding parameter values of $A = 2.92\pm0.50$ and $B=-0.13\pm0.07$." (Ratio of spatially resolved disc radius to blackbody radius)
   - software: EMCEE
   - parameter: total disc flux density, R_in, R_out, R_FIR = 0.5*(R_in + R_out), inclination, PA, 2D pointing offset; A and B for radius-ratio trend  
  > "The free parameters of the model are the total disc flux density, the inner and outer edges of the disc, $R_\text{in}$ and $R_\text{out}$, inclination, and position angle. We also include a 2D offset to account for non-perfect *Herschel* pointing." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS); "To get disc radii to be compared with those inferred from ALMA images that assumed a Gaussian radial profile, we derive a radius from *Herschel*/PACS, $R_\text{FIR} = 0.5\times (R_\text{in} + R_\text{out})$." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS)
   - assumption: Axisymmetric, optically thin disc with surface brightness proportional to r^-1.5 and blackbody dust temperature proportional to r^-0.5  
  > "Then we generate an axisymmetric, optically thin disc model and assume that the surface brightness is proportional to $r^{-1.5}$ with $r$ being the distance to the star. The dust temperature at a distance $r$ is assumed to follow $r^{-0.5}$ as for blackbody grains." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS)
   - fitting: chi^2 minimization and parameter estimation with emcee  
  > "The model disc is convolved with the PSF and then compared to the *Herschel*/PACS image by calculating the goodness of fit, $\chi^2$. We estimate the parameters using the emcee package." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS)
   - validation: Compared Herschel inclinations, position angles, and radii directly against ALMA results  
  > "In all cases the inclination and position angle are in agreement with ALMA observations listed in Tab. [\[tab:resolveddisc\]](#tab:resolveddisc)." (FIR observations with Herschel/PACS)
5. **Model SEDs of spatially resolved discs with a grain size distribution**: Use the SONATA code with Mie theory and fixed resolved radial Gaussian parameters to fit the SED at wavelengths >5 um for minimum grain size, size distribution index, and dust mass; test for a warm inner component using BIC and sublimation criteria.  
  > "We use the SONATA code (cite: pawellek-et-al-2014; pawellek-krivov-2015) and apply the same PHOENIX-GAIA stellar photospheric models (cite: brott-hauschildt-2005) to determine the host star contribution in a similar approach as for the modified blackbody fits (MBB, see §[3](#sec:IRExcess))." (Modelling approach); "Therefore, we are left with three free parameters to fit: the minimum grain size, $s_\text{min}$, the size distribution index, $q_\text{SED}$, and the amount of dust, $M_\text{dust}$, for particles between $s_\text{min}$ and $s_\text{max}$ assuming a bulk density $\varrho$." (Modelling approach); "While the former studies assumed a better two-component fit when $\chi^2_\text{one}/\chi^2_\text{two}>3$ was fulfilled we use the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) instead, which is" (Modelling approach)
   - software: SONATA
   - parameter: s_min, q_SED (fixed to 3.5 for HD 160305), M_dust, s_max = 10 cm (fixed), R_disc and Delta R_disc (fixed to resolved values), warm belt Delta R_disc/R_disc = 10% (fixed)  
  > "The grain sizes lie between a minimum and a maximum value, $s_\text{min}$ and $s_\text{max}$ where we fix the maximum grain size to 10 cm." (Modelling approach); "We assume this radius to be the location of the warm dust belt and fix the belt width to $\Delta R_\text{disc}/R_\text{disc}=10\%$." (Modelling approach); "Therefore, we fix the size distribution index, $q_\text{SED}$, to 3.5 (cite: dohnanyi-1969) to reduce the number of free parameters." (Modelling approach)
   - assumption: Compact spherical grains of pure astronomical silicate (Draine 2003) with bulk density 3.3 g/cm^3; Mie theory; sublimation temperature of 1300 K; warm and cold rings share the same grain size distribution  
  > "It assumes compact spherical grains and uses Mie theory to derive the absorption efficiencies (cite: bohren-huffman-1983). The dust composition is assumed to be pure astronomical silicate (cite: draine-2003) with a bulk density of 3.3 g/cm$^{3}$." (Modelling approach); "Finally, we fit both disc components assuming that the warm and cold dust ring possess the same size distribution of dust grains." (Modelling approach)
   - fitting: chi^2 SED fitting with model selection between 1- and 2-component models via Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)  
  > "We use the classification given in (cite: kass-raftery-1995) to infer whether a one- or a two-component model is more likely." (Modelling approach)
   - validation: Checked three warm-component criteria (WISE22/MIPS24 excess >=3sigma over single ring, BIC improvement, R > R_sub at 1300 K)  
  > "We follow the three criteria given in (cite: ballering-et-al-2013) and (cite: pawellek-et-al-2014) to check for the presence of a warm component for the five discs." (Modelling approach)
6. **Compare populations, model evolutionary detection rates, and derive minimum planetesimal sizes**: Verify Teff stability across age with MESA; compare detection rates across BPMG, 45 Myr, 150 Myr, and DEBRIS samples using Fisher's exact test; compute completeness grids in f_d vs R_BB space; simulate 100,000 evolved 92-star samples with f_d(t) = f_d(t_0)*(t/t_0)^alpha; and calculate minimum planetesimal sizes s_c and disc masses from analytical collisional timescales.  
  > "We use the “Modules for Experiments in Stellar Astrophysics” (cite: paxton-et-al-2011; paxton-et-al-2013; paxton-et-al-2015; choi-et-al-2016) to check the evolution of stellar temperature over time." (Stellar population at different ages); "Using the DEBRIS sample as reference and Fisher’s exact test (cite: fisher-1956) we tested the hypothesis that the incidence rates for the BPMG, the 45 Myr group and the 150 Myr group are similar to the DEBRIS sample." (Comparing the samples); "We generate 100,000 artificial samples of 92 targets similar to the size of the DEBRIS sample." (Modelling detection rates)
   - software: MESA
   - parameter: Stellar masses 1.15 to 1.58 M_sun, [Fe/H]=0.0; Fisher test alpha = 0.05; grid R_BB = 0.01-1000 au, f_d = 10^-7 to 10^-2; evolution t_0 = 23 Myr (or 2 Myr), alpha = -1, -2; collisional e = i = 0.1, v_imp ~ 0.4 km/s  
  > "We use the lowest ($1.15M_\text{sun}$) and highest ($1.58M_\text{sun}$) stellar masses in our sample of F stars to analyse its parameter space and assume a stellar metallicity of $[\text{Fe}/\text{H}]=0.0$." (Stellar population at different ages); "Using blackbody radii between 0.01 and 1000 au and fractional luminosities between $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-2}$ we generated a grid of fiducial discs assuming a pure blackbody model." (Fractional luminosity versus radius); "In the following section we fix both eccentricity and inclination to a value of 0.1." (Collision model)
   - assumption: Constant disc radius over time during fractional luminosity decay; collisional timescale equals system age (23 Myr for BPMG, 1000 Myr for HD 10647 and HD 109085); basalt and sand material laws  
  > "To describe the evolution process we use the collisional evolution model (§ [10](#sec:collisions)) and assume that the disc radius stays constant while the fractional luminosity decreases over time." (Same population scenario); "We calculate the minimum sizes of the planetesimals feeding the collisional cascade using eq. ([\[eq:timescale\]](#eq:timescale)) assuming that the collisional timescale, $t_\text{c}$, is similar to the age of the system." (Minimum sizes of planetesimals feeding the cascade)
   - fitting: Monte Carlo simulation of 100,000 samples of 92 targets with Poisson 95% confidence level  
  > "The dashed red line in Fig. [10](#fig:Evolution) shows that in this case the DEBRIS sample should have a detection rate of $41\pm10\,\%$ following Poisson statistics with a 95% confidence level." (Expected detection rates)
   - validation: Tested the Monte Carlo detection rate model on the BPMG sample itself (100,000 samples of 12 targets), reproducing the ~75% peak  
  > "To test the model we considered what it would predict for the BPMG, i.e. for a set of 100,000 samples each containing 12 targets randomly taken from the actual BPMG sample with the same age as the BPMG." (Modelling detection rates)

## Qualitative findings

- Nine out of twelve F stars in the BPMG (75%, or 9/11 = 82% of those with FIR data) possess infrared excess indicative of a debris disc.  
  > "We identified nine out of twelve stars ($75\%$) that show infrared excess and therefore suggest the presence of a debris disc. Since we cannot draw strong conclusions on HD 173167 (see § [3.3](#sec:targetnotes)) we might even say that nine out of eleven systems ($\sim82\%$) possess debris discs." (Stars with IR excess)
- The debris disc around HD 164249 is spatially resolved for the first time with ALMA at 1.35 mm, revealing a belt radius of 63 au, width of 24 au, and inclination <49 deg.  
  > "The disc around HD 164249 was observed with ALMA at 1.35 mm and is spatially resolved for the first time increasing the number of resolved debris discs reported in the literature to 153 according to the database for resolved discs[^3]." (A newly resolved disc around HD 164249); "It shows a face-on orientation with an inclination below 49$^\circ$. The planetesimal belt is found at 63 au with a disc width of 24 au using a Gaussian disc model." (A newly resolved disc around HD 164249)
- No evidence for a gap is found in the ALMA visibility modelling of HD 15115 when using a 3D Gaussian radial and vertical density model.  
  > "Our results do not show evidence for a gap in the disc, which may be because of the different parameterisations of the two models; (cite: macgregor-et-al-2019) assumes a 2D disc model using a power law for the radial surface density distribution and an infinitesimally small vertical scale height, whereas our disc model assumes Gaussian radial and vertical density distributions (the latter was found to be marginally resolved in our analysis)." (Previously resolved discs)
- No CO gas emission is detected in visual inspection of the ALMA data cubes of the four resolved BPMG discs (including CO J=3-2 for HD 181327).  
  > "We visually checked the data cubes of the four ALMA resolved targets for CO gas emission, but did not detect any." (Gas emission)
- Three of the four resolved BPMG discs have minimum grain sizes close to the blow-out limit (s_min/s_blow ~ 1), suggesting high dynamical excitation.  
  > "For three targets in our sample our modelling finds that $s_\text{min}$ is close to $s_\text{blow}$ leading to a $s_\text{min}/s_\text{blow}$ ratio of $\sim1$." (Fitting results); "The low $s_\text{min}/s_\text{blow}$ ratio for F stars in the BPMG, which is reported for the first time, could indicate high levels of dynamical excitation similar to that found for discs around A-type stars (cite: pawellek-krivov-2015)." (Fitting results)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Young nearby moving groups (<100 Myr) like BPMG have discs at their brightest and are coeval; F-type stars offer the best compromise between high stellar luminosity (maximizing disc detection) and a reasonably large sample size; sub-mm ALMA observations trace mm-sized grains that stay close to the parent planetesimal belt.  
  > "The best compromise between a high stellar luminosity and a reasonably large number of targets within the same moving group is given by F-type stars." (Introduction); "Hence, the disc size inferred in sub-mm observations is the best tracer of the location of a system’s planetesimal belt, which might differ from the radial extent seen in near-infrared scattered light." (Disc imaging)
- Why this method: Re-analysing archival ALMA visibilities with a uniform 3D Gaussian model (RADMC-3D + GALARIO + EMCEE) and Herschel images with a uniform PSF-convolved model ensures homogeneous radius measurements across the sample; completeness maps in f_d vs R_BB space remove distance and sensitivity biases when comparing samples.  
  > "Three of the analysed datasets have already been presented in literature work (cite: macgregor-et-al-2019; perez-et-al-2019; kral-et-al-2020), but are re-analysed here to maintain consistency across the sample." (ALMA reduction procedure); "Plotting detection rate versus age can be misleading, since different surveys reach different sensitivities to discs, for example due to the different distance of the stars in their samples." (Fractional luminosity versus radius)
- Alternative: 2D power-law radial surface density model with a gap for HD 15115 (MacGregor et al. 2019): not needed when using a 3D Gaussian radial and vertical density model.  
  > "Our results do not show evidence for a gap in the disc, which may be because of the different parameterisations of the two models; (cite: macgregor-et-al-2019) assumes a 2D disc model using a power law for the radial surface density distribution and an infinitesimally small vertical scale height, whereas our disc model assumes Gaussian radial and vertical density distributions (the latter was found to be marginally resolved in our analysis)." (Previously resolved discs)
- Alternative: Delayed stirring (t_0 ~ 2 Myr, waiting 21 Myr to ignite collisional cascade): rejected as unrealistic because protoplanetary discs dissipate in a few Myr and discs at different radii have different evolution timescales.  
  > "While delayed stirring is expected in some models (cite: kenyon-bromley-2008), this explanation would require all discs to wait 21 Myr before being ignited, whereas all discs are located at different radii and so should have different evolution timescales." (Delayed stirring)
- Alternative: Gas drag or evolving small-grain size distribution causing rapid luminosity drop: rejected because no significant gas is present and SED models show no evidence for changing s_min or q.  
  > "However, there is no evidence for a change in small dust properties in the SED models’ $s_\text{min}$ or $q$, and there is not significant gas in these systems." (Fast depletion)
- Alternative: Planetesimal depletion via stellar flare heating or YORP radiation forces: rejected for the bulk sample because most BPMG discs lie at ~80 au where these effects are negligible.  
  > "However, the majority of the discs in the BPMG lie too far out ($\sim80$ au) for this to be important." (Fast depletion); "However, the relatively slow evolution of Solar system asteroids (cite: durech-et-al-2018) suggests that this radiation effect might be negligible, since it should be weaker for planetesimals at several tens of au." (Fast depletion)
- Alternative: Two-population scenario (swept-up primordial dust ring + faint outer Kuiper-belt analogue): considered unlikely on its own because outer DEBRIS-like belts are not detected and the inner belts still require rapid non-collisional depletion.  
  > "However, while the two-population scenario is not impossible, we would still need to invoke a rapid depletion as proposed in the first scenario (§ [7.1](#sec:samepopulation))." (Conclusions)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Small number of targets in the BPMG (12 stars) and small number of spatially resolved discs limit the statistical significance of radius distribution comparisons.  
  > "However, this difference is not statistically significant due to the small number of targets." (Abstract)
- Limitation (authors): HD 173167 and several stars in the 45 Myr and 150 Myr moving groups lack far-infrared photometry beyond 22 um (or only have IRAS upper limits), preventing firm conclusions on cold disc presence for those stars.  
  > "The star HD 173167 only possesses photometric data up to 22$\mu$m so that we cannot draw any conclusions about a possible far-infrared excess." (Notes for particular targets); "For several targets only data up to mid-infrared wavelengths (WISE22) are available or upper limits from IRAS at 25, 60 and 100$\mu$m, but no *Spitzer*/MIPS or other far-infrared data." - Limitation (authors): High ALMA upper limits for HD 14082A (<5.8 mJy) and HD 29391 (<3.5 mJy) due to the 14" phase-center offset (46% primary beam response) for HD 14082A and the small 0.2" beam for HD 29391.  
  > "The high values for the upper limits are caused by the relatively small beam used for the observation of HD 29391 and the fact that HD 14082A is significantly offset from the phase center, increasing the already high RMS of that observation." (ALMA reduction procedure)
- Limitation (authors): Visual inspection of CO J=3-2 in HD 181327 yielded a non-detection, and a full gas analysis optimising detection across excitation conditions was not performed.
