## Qualitative findings

- The HI disc is sharply truncated within the inner star-forming disc, displays a central depression, and shows no diffuse low-column-density gas, distortions, asymmetries, or tails.  
  > "This implies that the H I disc is sharply truncated and does not extend beyond the inner, star-forming disc of NGC 1436." (3 MORPHOLOGY AND KINEMATICS OF THE COLD GAS DISC); "we can confidently rule out any recent disturbance to the gas disc, while we see the presence of a H I depression in the center of the galaxy." (3 MORPHOLOGY AND KINEMATICS OF THE COLD GAS DISC)
- CO follows the HI ring, has no central detection, and co-rotates consistently with the HI in a settled disc.  
  > "Both H I and CO are distributed within a settled ring where they are co-rotating, with no hints of any recent tidal or hydrodynamical disturbances." (3 MORPHOLOGY AND KINEMATICS OF THE COLD GAS DISC); "The rotational velocity of H I and CO are consistent with one another and follow the typical rotation of a settled disc." (3 MORPHOLOGY AND KINEMATICS OF THE COLD GAS DISC)
- Both the inner and outer discs experienced normal cosmic inside-out evolution until ~5 Gyr ago, a burst of star formation peaking 3-4 Gyr ago, followed by rapid quenching in the outer disc and slow starvation-like decline in the inner disc.  
  > "In summary, our SFH analysis indicates that both the inner and outer disc populations went through three main star-formation phases: (i) a rather typical build up activity and a steady decline in star-formation activity until some 5 Gyr ago, (ii) a burst of star formation peaking between 3 and 4 Gyr ago, and (iii) a final drop in star-formation until present times, gradually reducing it by two orders of magnitude in the inner disc and completely quenching star formation in the outer disc." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous ATCA HI data lacked the resolution and sensitivity to detect recent environmental interactions or recover the full single-dish HI flux; MeerKAT provides at least 10x better resolution and sensitivity.  
  > "In this paper we present H I data of NGC 1436 from the MeerKAT Fornax Survey (Serra et al. 2023), whose resolution and sensitivity are at least a factor of 10 better than those of the previous ATCA H I data." (1 INTRODUCTION)
- Why this method: Exploiting the axisymmetry of the galaxy via elliptical annular sectors allows estimating SFH uncertainties from sector-to-sector variance rather than enforcing regularization; a multiplicative polynomial replaces two-component dust reddening due to relative flux calibration limits.  
  > "However, rather than aiming at a regularized SFH solution (Cappellari 2017) we decide to exploit the axisymmetry of the system to capture the uncertainties of the SFH-reconstruction, as done earlier using MUSE data by Martinsson et al. (2018)." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY); "However, the quality of the relative flux calibration of the MUSE data is insufficient to grant such an approach, and we resort to using a multiplicative polynomial correction instead." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY)
- Alternative: Warped disc model with flat rotation curve in TiRiFiC (rejected in favour of a flat disc model with single PA and inclination)  
  > "In the latter, we forced TiRiFiC to fit a flat rotation curve, but allowed the disc to warp letting the inclination vary. Between these two approaches, we obtained better results modelling NGC 1436 with a flat disc." (3 MORPHOLOGY AND KINEMATICS OF THE COLD GAS DISC)
- Alternative: Regularized SFH solution in pPXF (rejected in favour of averaging across axisymmetric annular sectors)  
  > "However, rather than aiming at a regularized SFH solution (Cappellari 2017) we decide to exploit the axisymmetry of the system to capture the uncertainties of the SFH-reconstruction" (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY)
- Alternative: Two-component dust reddening correction in GandALF and fitting Hbeta in the outer disc (rejected due to relative flux calibration limitations and risk of spurious Hbeta detections)  
  > "However, the quality of the relative flux calibration of the MUSE data is insufficient to grant such an approach, and we resort to using a multiplicative polynomial correction instead." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY); "For these reasons we decide to omit the Hβ line from our GandALF fits for the outer quiescent disc, where Hα emission from diffuse ionised gas is only barely detected." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Relative flux calibration of MUSE data is insufficient for two-component physical dust reddening correction, requiring an arbitrary-order multiplicative polynomial and omission of NaD and outer-disc Hbeta.  
  > "However, the quality of the relative flux calibration of the MUSE data is insufficient to grant such an approach, and we resort to using a multiplicative polynomial correction instead." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY)
- Limitation (authors): MUSE wavelength coverage does not extend down to blue age-sensitive features like the D4000 break and Hdelta, biasing the recovery of young (< 1 Gyr) stellar populations.  
  > "The presence of such young stars is at odds with the notable absence of any star-formation activity and it is certainly a bias in the template mix adjustment process, which in turn may stem from intrinsic limitations in our ability to constrain the presence of young stellar populations using the MUSE spectra, as indeed these do not extend down to important features (e.g. the D4000 break and Hδ)." (4 STAR FORMATION HISTORY)
- Limitation (authors): The relative role of gravitational (tidal/merger) versus hydrodynamical (ram-pressure) processes in driving the past transformation cannot be uniquely disentangled.  
  > "The relative role that gravitational and hydrodynamical processes might have played to drive such a morphological transformation is not clear." (5 DISCUSSION)
