## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare matched JWST maps**: Convolve to 0.90''; apply 3σ cuts and by-eye diffraction-spike masks  
  > "The JWST data are finally all convolved to a common Gaussian resolution of 0.90$''$" (JWST MIRI & NIRCam); "A 3$\sigma$ signal-to-noise cut is performed on each MIRI filter used in this work." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam); "By-eye masking is done to exclude regions where these spikes contaminate the maps." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam)
   - software: jwst, WebbPSF
   - parameter: CRDS jwst_1201; JWST pipeline v1.13.4; PHANGS-JWST data 1.1.0  
  > "JWST data are reduced using the most up-to-date calibration reference data system (CRDS) context (jwst_1201), and JWST calibration pipeline version v1.13.4, and the PHANGS-JWST data version 1.1.0." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam)
   - parameter: Pixel scale 0.11''  
  > "The pixel scale is left at the original pixel scale of 0.11$''$" (JWST MIRI & NIRCam)
   - parameter: 3σ: F770W 0.09; F1130W 0.13; F2100W 0.29 MJy/sr  
  > "our 3$\sigma$ limits are 0.09 MJy/sr in F770W, 0.13 MJy/sr in F1130W, and 0.29 MJy/sr in F2100W." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam)
   - parameter: Discard pixels with convolved spike-mask value >0.9  
  > "removing all pixels where the convolved mask has a value greater than 0.9." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Noise limits agree with Williams2024 and convolved pipeline error maps  
  > "These values are in good agreement with the characterization of noise levels presented in (cite: Williams2024) and match well with the expected values from convolving the pipeline-generated error maps." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam)
2. **Subtract starlight and construct R_PAH**: Scale F200W to subtract F770W starlight; R_PAH = (F770W_ss + F1130W)/F2100W  
  > "we scale the NIRCam F200W maps to predict the amount of starlight in the F770W band" (Starlight Subtraction); "R_{\rm{PAH}} = \frac{\rm{F770W_{ss}} + \rm{F1130W}}{\rm{F2100W}}" (Defining R_PAH)
   - software: CIGALE
   - parameter: F770W_starlight/F200W = 0.13±0.04; stellar ages 0.5–13 Gyr  
  > "We use a ratio of F770W$_{\rm{starlight}}$/F200W = 0.13$\pm$0.04, determined from a range of reasonable stellar emission models with ages spanning 0.5 Gyr–13 Gyr" (Starlight Subtraction)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF; Calzetti attenuation; Draine2014 dust models  
  > "the (cite: Chabrier2003) initial mass function." (Starlight Subtraction); "These starlight models are also assumed to experience attenuation following (cite: Calzetti2000)." (Starlight Subtraction); "Dust emission is set using the (cite: Draine2014) models, with the available range of $q_{\rm PAH}$." (Starlight Subtraction)
   - assumption: F770W and F1130W dominated by PAHs; F2100W provides non-PAH continuum leverage  
  > "the F770W and F1130W bands are dominated by PAH emission where PAHs are present" (Defining R_PAH); "the F2100W band includes a contribution from non-PAH dust continuum sufficient to give leverage on the PAH-to-continuum ratio" (Defining R_PAH)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- R_PAH traces PAH fraction under most typical ISM conditions, with weaker sensitivity at high q_PAH  
  > "we argue that $R_{\rm PAH}$ is a good indicator of PAH fraction in most typical ISM conditions observable at $\geq 10$pc scales." (Summary); "at high values of $q_{\rm PAH}$$>5$% (super-Milky Way $q_{\rm PAH}$ values), the slope between them becomes flatter" (R_PAH as a Tracer of q_PAH)
- Low R_PAH in H II regions suggests rapid PAH destruction  
  > "The good correspondence between the low $R_{\rm PAH}$ values and the nebular regions, which span a wide range of ages and luminosities, suggest that PAH destruction occurs quickly compared to the H II region lifetime." (Summary)
- Diffuse PAH fractions appear fairly uniform and similar to the Milky Way  
  > "These comparisons suggest that we are seeing $q_{\rm PAH}$ values in the diffuse neutral gas of our galaxies that is very similar to what is found in the Milky Way." (Behavior of the PAH Fraction Outside H II Regions); "the consistency of $R_{\rm PAH}$ outside of nebular regions suggests that the diffuse ISM of these galaxies has a fairly uniform $q_{\rm PAH}$." (Behavior of the PAH Fraction Outside H II Regions)
- sSFR best predicts diffuse R_PAH changes; radiation-field hardness is a possible explanation  
  > "we find that sSFR remains the best predictor of changes in $\langle R_{\rm PAH,Diffuse}\rangle$." (1 kpc Scales); "This could indicate that in galaxies with the highest sSFR, a harder radiation field is irradiating the PAHs in the diffuse ISM" (Summary)
- Morphological environment shows broadly similar diffuse R_PAH distributions  
  > "all five environments show similar distributions of $R_{\rm PAH, Diffuse}$, suggesting that galactic environment does not drive changes to $R_{\rm PAH, Diffuse}$." (R_PAH, Diffuse and Galaxy Environment)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST provides resolved PAH and dust-continuum imaging; ancillary maps characterize gas and star formation  
  > "The MIRI F770W and F1130W filters capture the emission from two of the strongest PAH features, the 7.7 $\mu$m and 11.3 $\mu$m features." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam); "The F2100W filter is dominated by dust continuum emission." (JWST MIRI & NIRCam); "we use ancillary data products to determine the gas and stellar properties of the galaxies in our sample." (Ancillary Data)
- Why this method: MIR ratios provide high spatial resolution without costly spectroscopy; coarse gas estimates suffice  
  > "providing a high spatial resolution estimation of the PAH fraction without costly spectroscopy." (Defining R_PAH); "For this work we require only a coarse estimate of the molecular gas surface density" (Ancillary Data)
- Alternative: F300M starlight correction considered; F200W preferred for signal-to-noise and smaller stellar-model variation  
  > "We use the F200W band to correct for starlight instead of the F300M due to the higher signal-to-noise of the F200W measurements and the narrower range of F770W/F200W ratios produced by different stellar emission models" (Starlight Subtraction)
- Alternative: Single PAH-band ratios are viable alternatives but more sensitive to PAH size, charge, and heating  
  > "using only one PAH tracing band can provide a viable alternative tracer of PAH fraction, although both alternatives have the potential to confuse changes in the PAH fraction with changes in the size, ionization state, and heating" (Defining R_PAH)
- Alternative: Dust-equilibrium heating alone considered unlikely to explain nebular R_PAH decreases  
  > "Based on the modeled values of ${\overline{U}}$ found within the LMC at similar spatial resolution, it is unlikely that $U$ reaches the necessary values for this to happen within our sample." (Results from Previous 10 pc Far-IR SED modeling)
- Alternative: Flux-weighted binning tested; generally yields lower R_PAH than equal pixel weighting  
  > "We can see that in most cases the flux-weighted average values are lower than the pixel-weighted values." (Flux weighting vs pixel weighting)
- How they decided it was enough: FIR and dust-model agreement validate the proxy; 1 kpc regions contain both gas classes and permit sSFR measurements  
  > "We also compare observed values of $R_{\rm PAH}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ at low resolution for galaxies with both JWST and FIR observations and find good correlation." (Summary); "this size is large enough to contain both significant numbers of pixels identified as nebular and diffuse, while also allowing us to obtain a meaningful measurement of sSFR" (1 kpc Scales)
- Limitation (authors): Nebular regions mix H II emission, PDRs, and diffuse gas  
  > "our nebular regions always include contributions from both the H II region, surrounding PDR, and depending on the H II region size and galaxy distance and inclination, a substantial contribution from the surrounding “diffuse” gas." (The Destruction of PAHs in H II Regions)
- Limitation (authors): Residual saturation contamination may increase scatter in NGC 1365  
  > "the chosen cuts may not be adequately removing pixels impacted by the saturation in the nucleus, leading to erroneous high or low values of F2100W" (Comparison to Infrared SED Modeling Results)
- Limitation (authors): R_PAH becomes less sensitive to q_PAH at high PAH fractions  
  > "$R_{\rm PAH}$ is less sensitive to changes in $q_{\rm PAH}$ at $q_{\rm{PAH}} \geq 4.5$%" (Expectation from Dust Models)
- Limitation (authors): Interpretation depends on grain models and the radiation-field distribution  
  > "In models that make different assumptions about the radiation field distribution (e.g. a single radiation field intensity), the mapping between $R_{\rm PAH}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ can differ significantly." (Results from Previous 10 pc Far-IR SED modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Limited metallicity coverage prevents a firm threshold measurement  
  > "as only 5 galaxies in our sample have Z lower than this threshold it is difficult to ascertain the veracity of this trend." (Summary)
- Limitation (authors): Coagulation and UV attenuation cannot be distinguished with these data  
  > "Determining whether the main cause of this decreasing trend is coagulation or UV attenuation requires further spectral information not available with this dataset." (Behavior of the PAH Fraction Outside H II Regions)
- Limitation (authors): R_PAH-to-q_PAH mapping is uncertain at very low PAH fractions  
  > "at very low $q_{\rm PAH}$, the $q_{\rm PAH}$-$R_{\rm PAH}$ mapping may be more uncertain." (The Destruction of PAHs in H II Regions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ~1'' CO maps and 11''–15'' HI maps sample different spatial scales, potentially biasing pixel-based molecular fractions and gas-normalized Hα.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A fixed CO conversion and line ratio can alter the apparent high-molecular-fraction trend.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Oversampled, convolved pixels are correlated; effective independent sample sizes and correlation uncertainties are not specified.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The main text alternates between averages and medians, and reports distinct scatter values for different nebular/diffuse summaries.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The sSFR correlation supports a radiation-hardness interpretation indirectly; hardness is not measured for this analysis.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A positive PAH proxy correlation with moderate scatter does not uniquely determine absolute q_PAH.
