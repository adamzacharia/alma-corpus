## Analysis recipe

1. **Reduce JWST/MIRI coronagraphic images and extract photometry and astrometry of detected sources**: Retrieve Level 1 data from MAST, process with JWST pipeline v1.14.0, subtract background, register to coronagraph center, subtract stellar diffraction using linear combination and PCA of 9 SGD reference star frames, simulate PSF with WebbPSF and diffraction code, and extract flux/position via PSF fitting and aperture photometry.  
  > "Level 1 data are retrieved from MAST (Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes), processed with v1.14.0 of the pipeline together with CRDS files 1241." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis); "We tested various algorithms and retained a linear combination (which uses the downhill simplex minimization) of the 9 SGD reference star images, as well as the Principal Component Analysis (PCA), as the two algorithms providing the best detection of CC#1." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis); "Based on these CC#1 PSF models, we extracted the flux and the photometry of the object by minimizing the residuals between the data and the models in a 1.5 λ/D area with 3 free parameters (positions, and flux)" (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
   - software: JWST pipeline v1.14.0, WebbPSF, diffraction code [38,39]
   - parameter: Annular optimization region 0.5'' to 3'' (linear combination) and 0.5'' to 5'' with 8 PCA components; 1.5 λ/D photometric extraction area; roll 1 only used for photometry.  
  > "We obtained the best compromise by selecting the annular region between 0.5’’ and 3’’, while the 3 sources were masked with a 1 λ/D patch" (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis); "We proceeded similarly for the PCA, decreasing to 8 components to build the final image to be subtracted to the data in an annular region from 0.5’’ to 5’’ (point sources not masked)." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis); "In the roll 2 image, the PSF is more asymmetrical as the planet is closer to the quadrant edge in comparison to roll 1, so we decided to consider only roll 1 data for the photometric analysis." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
   - assumption: Flat field correction is skipped as inappropriate for the MIRI coronagraph.  
  > "We skipped the Flat Field correction which is not appropriate for the MIRI coronagraph [21]." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
   - fitting: Downhill simplex algorithm or Nelder-Mead algorithm with 3 free parameters (positions and flux) in a 1.5 λ/D area.  
  > "we extracted the flux and the photometry of the object by minimizing the residuals between the data and the models in a 1.5 λ/D area with 3 free parameters (positions, and flux) and using either a downhill simplex algorithm or the NelderMead algorithm [40]." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
   - validation: Compared two independent PSF simulation codes (yielding consistent transmissions of 0.66 and 0.62 in roll 1), checked alternate annular region (2-3''), and averaged across calibrated/uncalibrated files and PSF/aperture photometry.  
  > "The two approaches give similar transmissions: 0.66 and 0.62 in roll 1 and 0.31 and 0.28 in roll 2." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis); "We averaged the values of the different methods (calibrated files and contrast, aperture and PSF model for the photometric extraction) while the error bar is built from the extreme values ((max-min)/2) for being conservative." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
2. **Re-image archival ALMA Band 7 data and measure upper limit at CC#1 position**: Retrieve pipeline-calibrated visibility datasets from the ALMA archive, image single epochs and combined visibilities in CASA using tclean (mosaic mode for combined) with Briggs 0.5 and natural weighting, and measure primary-beam-corrected 3-sigma upper limit at CC#1 location.  
  > "We retrieved the pipeline-calibrated visibility datasets from the ALMA archive, and re-imaged each of the datasets with the CASA software using the tclean task, with Briggs 0.5 weighing as a compromise between resolution and sensitivity." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "We repeated the same imaging process also on the combined visibility datasets, but using the tclean mosaic mode to account for the different phase centers of the different observations" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "In this natural weighting image, the 3-sigma upper limit on the flux density of a point source at the same location as found for CC#1 in the MIRI image, when corrected for the primary beam response at that location, is 76 μJy." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - software: CASA, tclean
   - parameter: Hogbom deconvolution down to a residual threshold of 0.1 mJy (~3x RMS); Briggs 0.5 weighting and natural weighting.  
  > "We perform standard Hogbom deconvolution through tclean down to a residual threshold of 0.1 mJy, which corresponds to roughly 3 times the RMS noise level of each observation." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "To maximize point source sensitivity at the expense of angular resolution, we also use the same method to create a separate image with natural weighting, which has an RMS of 21 μJy/beam for a beam size of 0.29”x0.24”." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - assumption: Evaluated upper limit assuming CC#1 is an unresolved point source that had not moved between 2016 and 2024.  
  > "Supplementary Information Data Fig. 1 shows the expected location of source CC#1, which is undetected in the ALMA data, if it had not moved compared to the MIRI location over the 8 years between observations." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
3. **Fit astrometry and morphology of the bright ALMA East source and compare with MIRI East source**: Fit a point-source model in the visibility domain to each of the 3 ALMA epochs using MCMC (emcee) to obtain astrometry, fit a 2D Gaussian to the combined image to measure integrated flux and size, and translate the ALMA position to the 2024 MIRI epoch using Gaia DR3 stellar proper motion.  
  > "we first measure its RA and Dec in each of the 3 epochs of observations by fitting a point source model with a flux density, RA offset and Dec offset from phase center to the visibility data. We did this using MCMC through the emcee package" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "To investigate this further, we fit a 2D Gaussian to the combined image, finding best fit FWHMs of 0.35”x0.26” with a PA of 101 degrees, and a spatially integrated flux of 4.0+/-0.4 mJy." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "We can therefore translate the ALMA galaxy position (fixed in time, from Supplementary Information Table 1) into an offset from the (Gaia DR3-predicted) position of the TWA 7 star at the MIRI epoch" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: ALMA astrometric systematic error of 5% of the beam size (9 mas for combined observation) added in quadrature to statistical MCMC uncertainties.  
  > "We then once again quadratically add the expected ALMA astrometric systematic (5% of the beam of the combined observation, i.e. 9 mas) to the statistical uncertainty, leading to the final combined ALMA coordinate for the East source." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - assumption: The East source is extragalactic and stationary between the 2016 ALMA and 2024 MIRI epochs.  
  > "Assuming the source to be extragalactic, we expect its position not to change between the ALMA and MIRI epochs." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - fitting: MCMC via emcee with uniform, uninformative priors on flux density, RA offset, and Dec offset in visibility space; 2D Gaussian fit in image plane.  
  > "We did this using MCMC through the emcee package, with uniform, uninformative priors on each of the fitted parameters." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "To investigate this further, we fit a 2D Gaussian to the combined image" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
   - validation: Checked consistency of RA and Dec across the three individual ALMA epochs and inspected residual visibilities/images after model subtraction.  
  > "The resulting RA and Dec (Supplementary Information Table 1) of the East source is consistent across the three ALMA epochs" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "As shown by imaging the residual visibilities, this is because the source is extended, with extended emission clearly present in each residual image after point source subtraction." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
4. **Derive VLT/SPHERE near-IR upper limits at the position of CC#1**: Reanalyse archival VLT/SPHERE IRDIS H2-H3 high-contrast imaging data from the SHINE survey using the PACO algorithm to compute 5-sigma contrast curves and flux upper limits.  
  > "As a next step, a more advanced algorithm, PAtch COvariance (PACO [52,53,54]) was used to reanalyse the SHINE data." (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits); "The 5-sigma upper limits for at the position of TWA 7b, 6.4e-19 W/m2/um and 9.2e-19 W/m2/um for respectively H2 and H3 used in the present paper, were directly deduced from the contrasts computed using the approach described in [54]." (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits)
   - software: PACO
   - parameter: H2 (1.593 ± 0.055 μm) and H3 (1.667 ± 0.056 μm) filters; 5-sigma confidence level.  
  > "The H2-H3 filter pairs (λ H2 = 1.593 ± 0.055 μm; λ H3 = 1.667 ± 0.056 µm) were used for IRDIS" (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits); "The 5-sigma upper limits for at the position of TWA 7b, 6.4e-19 W/m2/um and 9.2e-19 W/m2/um for respectively H2 and H3 used in the present paper" (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits)
   - assumption: Local patch noise statistics modeled through mixtures of scaled multivariate Gaussians.  
  > "PACO underlying models capture the noise statistics at the local scale of small patches through mixtures of scaled multivariate Gaussians." (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Contrasts improved by more than one to two magnitudes compared to classical algorithms [48].  
  > "As a result, the contrasts in the IRDIS H2-H3 data were improved by more than one to two magnitudes, depending on the angular separation, compared to classical algorithms." (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits)
5. **Estimate background galaxy contamination probability and rule out solar system object**: Check proper motion across 2-hour exposures against solar system ephemerides; combine JWST 11 μm source counts, GOODS 8 μm infrared luminosity functions at z=0.1-1, and 14 SWIRE starburst/AGN templates scaled to 22 μJy at 11 μm subject to ALMA 870 μm and SPHERE 1.6 μm upper limits.  
  > "No trail was observed during the 2 hour-long exposures, and no apparent motion was observed between the two images recorded during the sequences taken 2 hours apart, indicating thus that CC#1 has a proper motion less than ~0.05”/hr." (Main); "Focusing on the starbursts and AGN, we considered 14 templates. They were computed for a dozen redshifts, and calibrated all to have a flux of 22 μJy at (11μm)." (Methods, 2. Estimation of the probability for an intermediate-redshift galaxy); "We used the infrared luminosity function of galaxies at redshifts between z=0.1 and z=1, in the GOODS fields, by [47], to estimate the abundance of these star-forming galaxies, as a function of their 8 μm luminosities." (Methods, 2. Estimation of the probability for an intermediate-redshift galaxy)
   - parameter: 11 μm flux = 22 μJy; 870 μm upper limit = 96 μJy (or 76 μJy); 1.6 μm upper limit = 0.6 μJy (point source) to 60 μJy (extended 0.6 arcsec); redshift range z = 0.1 to 1; radius = 1.5''.  
  > "The source has a flux of 22 μJy at 11 μm, and is not detected with ALMA at 870 μm, but with a tapered resolution of 2 arcsec. ## Software

- **CASA** : Re-imaging archival ALMA visibility datasets  
  > "We retrieved the pipeline-calibrated visibility datasets from the ALMA archive, and re-imaged each of the datasets with the CASA software using the tclean task" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- **tclean** : Deconvolving and imaging single-epoch and combined mosaic ALMA visibility datasets with Briggs 0.5 and natural weighting  
  > "re-imaged each of the datasets with the CASA software using the tclean task, with Briggs 0.5 weighing as a compromise between resolution and sensitivity." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "We repeated the same imaging process also on the combined visibility datasets, but using the tclean mosaic mode to account for the different phase centers of the different observations" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- **emcee** : MCMC fitting of a point-source model to the ALMA visibility data for the East source across 3 epochs  
  > "we first measure its RA and Dec in each of the 3 epochs of observations by fitting a point source model with a flux density, RA offset and Dec offset from phase center to the visibility data. We did this using MCMC through the emcee package" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- **JWST pipeline** v1.14.0 (with CRDS files 1241): Processing Level 1 JWST/MIRI data into calibrated files  
  > "Level 1 data are retrieved from MAST (Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes), processed with v1.14.0 of the pipeline together with CRDS files 1241." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
- **WebbPSF** : Simulating the JWST/MIRI coronagraphic PSF and transmission  
  > "We used both the diffraction code developed in [38,39] and WebbPSF [41] to simulate the MIRI PSF, taking into account the coronagraph" (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
- **HADES** : Atmospheric and thermal evolution forward modeling of the planet candidate  
  > "Using the HADES model [28] it is possible to find fits for the JWST photometric data point while accounting for the 5-sigma upper limits provided by the high-contrast VLT/SPHERE images at 1.59 (H2) and 1.67 (H3) micrometers (Figure 2)." (Main)
- **swift_rmvs3** : Symplectic N-body dynamical simulations of the planet-disk interaction  
  > "The disk evolution was computed using the symplectic N-body code swift_rmvs3[32]." (Figure 3 caption)
- **PACO** : Reanalysing VLT/SPHERE IRDIS H2-H3 high-contrast imaging data to compute detection limits  
  > "As a next step, a more advanced algorithm, PAtch COvariance (PACO [52,53,54]) was used to reanalyse the SHINE data." (Supplementary Informations, 2. VLT/SPHERE upper limits)

## Qualitative findings

- An unambiguous point source (CC#1 / TWA 7 b) is detected at ~1.5'' North-West of TWA 7 (~51 au projected, ~52 au de-projected) in JWST/MIRI F1140C images, lying directly on the narrow R2 debris ring within an azimuthal underdensity.  
  > "We unambiguously detected a source 1.5” from the star, that is best interpreted as a cold, sub-Jupiter mass planet. Its estimated mass (~ 0.3 M J ) and position (~ 52 au, de-projected) can thoroughly account for the main disk structures." (Main); "The observed source is located right on the R2 narrow ring, and, moreover, within a region identified as underdense compared to the rest of the ring by [17] (Figure 3a)." (Main)
- The spatially resolved MIRI East source (~6.7'' East of TWA 7) is firmly associated with the bright, extended background galaxy detected in archival 2016 ALMA Band 7 observations once TWA 7's proper motion is accounted for.  
  > "We find the offsets to be entirely consistent with one another within the uncertainties, and therefore firmly associate the MIRI East source to the background galaxy detected by ALMA." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- A super-solar metallicity is required by the HADES atmospheric model fits for TWA 7 b.  
  > "A metallicity range above solar is required. Additional data will be necessary to further constrain this parameter." (Main)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: JWST/MIRI provides unprecedented thermal-IR sensitivity to sub-Jovian planets around the young, nearly pole-on three-ring debris disk of TWA 7; archival ALMA Band 7 data are reanalysed to test for a sub-mm counterpart to CC#1 and verify the nature of the East source.  
  > "We have used the unprecedented sensitivity of JWST/MIRI [9,10] in the thermal IR to search for such planets in the disk of the ~6.4 Myr old star TWA 7. With its pole-on orientation, this three-ring debris disk is indeed ideally suited for such a detection." (Main); "We reanalysed archival ALMA data on TWA 7 from project 2015.1.01095 to search for any counterpart for CC#1 and to establish the nature of the East source detected in the MIRI images." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- Why this method: Used tclean mosaic mode to combine ALMA observations with different phase centers; used natural weighting to maximize point-source sensitivity at CC#1's location and Briggs 0.5 as a compromise between resolution and sensitivity; used only JWST roll 1 for photometry because CC#1 is closer to the 4QPM transition edge in roll 2.  
  > "We repeated the same imaging process also on the combined visibility datasets, but using the tclean mosaic mode to account for the different phase centers of the different observations" (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "To maximize point source sensitivity at the expense of angular resolution, we also use the same method to create a separate image with natural weighting, which has an RMS of 21 μJy/beam for a beam size of 0.29”x0.24”." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "In the roll 2 image, the PSF is more asymmetrical as the planet is closer to the quadrant edge in comparison to roll 1, so we decided to consider only roll 1 data for the photometric analysis." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
- Alternative: Solar system object (including distant dwarf planets or hypothetical Planet Nine) considered and rejected based on lack of proper motion (< 0.05''/hr) and ephemeris constraints.  
  > "The first origin one can consider is a solar system object." (Main); "Based on the constraints from the planetary ephemeris [26] and the predictions of the orbit of Planet Nine [27], a solar system origin for CC#1 can be excluded definitely." (Main)
- Alternative: Intermediate-redshift starburst or AGN background galaxy considered and found to have a low probability (~0.34% within 1.5'').  
  > "The second possible origin is a background galaxy." (Main); "Using such galaxy templates at various redshifts and published galaxy counts in JWST fields of view, we estimated that the probability of finding one such galaxy in a 1.5” radius region centered on TWA 7 is ~0.34% (see details in Methods)." (Main)
- Alternative: Target Acquisition (TA) images for JWST contrast calibration were tested and rejected due to broad TA filter bandwidth (~8-18 μm), spectral type mismatch, and disk emission beyond 15 μm.  
  > "The method using TA shows some net discrepancies, likely because the TA filter is very broad (~8-18μm) while the targets have different spectral types (M3 for TWA7 and for K0/K5 for the commissioning targets). Beside, the disk emission of TWA 7 should also start to kick off beyond 15 μm. As a result, we did not use the TA method in the following." (Methods, 1.2 Data analysis)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): No second-epoch data are available yet to test whether CC#1 shares common proper motion with TWA 7.  
  > "No data is available to test whether this third source shares a common proper motion with the star." (Main)
- Limitation (authors): The extended TWA 7 debris disk is interferometrically filtered out in the high-resolution ALMA images, and its SNR in tapered data is too low to constrain the stellar position directly from disk emission.  
  > "Note that, as explained in [20], this image does not show the disk because it is too extended and faint; it is then interferometrically ‘filtered out’ by these ALMA configuration observations." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis); "The SNR on the disk is consequently too low to produce sufficiently accurate constraints on the location of the TWA star for comparison with the MIRI data." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): A single 2D Gaussian model leaves significant residuals when subtracted from the ALMA East galaxy image, indicating a more complex core-halo radial surface brightness profile.  
  > "While this model is better at capturing the resolved emission, significant residuals are still present when subtracting this best fit from the image. This suggests a more complex radial surface brightness distribution for this galaxy, with a strongly peaked core and a faint halo of emission." (Supplementary Informations, 1. ALMA data analysis)
- Limitation (authors): N-body dynamical simulations model massless planetesimals rather than dust grains subject to radiation pressure, and planet mass estimates depend on assuming the planet is coeval with the star.  
  > "It however depends on the age of the planet, assumed here to be coeval with its parent star. A younger planet would lead to a smaller mass." (Main); "While Figure 3a pictures the grains and Figure 3b the planetesimals, and while some differences may be expected between both distributions due to the radiation pressure acting on the grains" (Main)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Without a second epoch of JWST astrometry to confirm common proper motion, the planetary nature of CC#1 relies on a probabilistic argument against a background galaxy and physical plausibility.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The ALMA non-detection of CC#1 is evaluated at its 2024 MIRI sky position (appropriate for testing a stationary background galaxy), whereas a bound planet on a ~550 yr orbit would have moved slightly between 2016 and 2024.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Note a typo in the Supplementary Information text where the ALMA project code is printed as 2015.1.01095 instead of 2015.1.01015.S (as given in Data Availability and Acknowledgements).
