  > "The 7 m and two configuration 12 m data were first processed via the standard ALMA reduction pipeline separately (pipeline version 42866M for the 7m observations, 42866 for the C-4 observations, and 2020.1.0.40 for the C-6 observations) and then combined following standard procedures." (ALMA data); "For the spectroscopic data cubes, we first used the CASA task uvcontsub with the polynomial order of unity to subtract the continuum emission in the $uv$ domain and then cleaned the channels with a width of 1 $\hbox{${\rm km\,s}^{-1}$}$ in the range of 70-140 $\hbox{${\rm km\,s}^{-1}$}$." (ALMA data)
   - software: CASA, ALMA reduction pipeline, uvcontsub
   - parameter: robust = 0.5  
  > "For all the data products, we used Briggs cleaning and set the robustness parameter robust to the default value of 0.5" (ALMA data)
   - parameter: multiscale scales = [0, 6, 10, 20] pixels  
  > "We used the multiscale deconvolver (cite: 2008ISTSP...2..793C) with scales of zero (delta-function) and 6, 10, and 20 times the pixel size" (ALMA data)
   - parameter: uvcontsub fitorder = 1  
  > "we first used the CASA task uvcontsub with the polynomial order of unity to subtract the continuum emission in the $uv$ domain" (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Also restored 1.3 mm continuum image with a 0.3" circular beam to verify elongated morphology of MM1.

2. **Measure MM1 disk/toroid geometry, scale height, kinematics, and central protostellar mass**: Measure deconvolved major and minor axes of MM1 from 1.3 mm continuum, compute disk scale height H using the jet inclination, and derive central star mass from H, disk radius, and CH3OH line FWHM; examine velocity gradients in CH3OH and CH3CN moment-1 and position-velocity diagrams.  
  > "Assuming the jet is perpendicular to the disk, then $H = (b-a \times$ $\sin$(8$\hbox{$^{\circ}$}$))/$\cos(8$$^{\circ}$$)$ $\approx$ 0$\hbox{$\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}$33 (2060 au), which is about half of the major diameter of the disk/toroid." (1.3 mm continuum); "where the FWHM linewidth is set to 6 $\hbox{${\rm km\,s}^{-1}$}$ which is the value found for the 217.3 GHz $\nu_{t}$=1 6(1,5)–7(2,6) line of the typical disk tracer CH$_{3}$OH from the ALMA data. The radius of the disk, $R_{\rm disk} = a/2$, is 0$\hbox{$\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}$325 (see above). Then the derived mass of the central star $M_{\star}$ is about 15 M$_{\odot}$" (1.3 mm continuum)
   - parameter: a = 0.65" (4088 au), b = 0.41" (2608 au), R_disk = 0.325"  
  > "where the deconvolved major and minor diameters $a$ and $b$ are 0$\hbox{$\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}$65 (4088 au) and 0$\hbox{$\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}$41 (2608 au), respectively." (1.3 mm continuum)
   - parameter: FWHM(CH3OH) = 6 km s^-1  
  > "where the FWHM linewidth is set to 6 $\hbox{${\rm km\,s}^{-1}$}$ which is the value found for the 217.3 GHz $\nu_{t}$=1 6(1,5)–7(2,6) line of the typical disk tracer CH$_{3}$OH from the ALMA data." (1.3 mm continuum)

3. **Map outflow/jet morphology, fit jet precession, and test for jet rotation in ALMA line cubes**: Construct velocity-integrated maps of 13CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4), fit a 2D projected sinusoidal precession model to the SiO(5-4) jet emission, and inspect the SiO(5-4) moment-1 map within a 2.0"-wide belt along the jet axis for transverse velocity gradients.  
  > "Fig. 3 presents the velocity-integrated maps of two typical jet/outflow tracers, the $^{13}$CO (2-1) (left panel) and the SiO (5-4) lines (right panel)." (13CO (2-1) and SiO (5-4) maps); "In order to model it, we used a simple sinusoidal equation (cite: 2009ApJ...698..184W), adapted from (cite: 1996AJ....112.2086E) to fit the SiO(5-4) emission," (Jet precession); "Fig. 5 shows the intensity-weighted velocity (moment-1) SiO (5-4) map of the jet in G26.50+0.28. Only the 2$\hbox{$\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}$0-wide belt along the jet symmetry axis is shown to highlight the kinematics in the collimated jet." (Jet kinematics)
   - parameter: Jet belt width = 2.0" for SiO(5-4) moment-1 map  
  > "Only the 2$\hbox{$\,.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$}$0-wide belt along the jet symmetry axis is shown to highlight the kinematics in the collimated jet." (Jet kinematics)
   - assumption: Inclination i fixed to 8.0 deg; distance = 6.3 kpc; jet velocity ~ 30 km s^-1 (or ~ 200 km s^-1) to estimate precession period  
  > "The inclination $i$ is fixed to 8$\hbox{$^{\circ}$}$ according to the calculations using water masers (see Section 4)." (Table 2); "Assuming a jet velocity of $\sim$ 30 $\hbox{${\rm km\,s}^{-1}$}$ (see Section 4.3), the precession period should be at the order of 7000 years." (Jet precession)
   - fitting: Sinusoidal trajectory fit to SiO(5-4) emission with i fixed to 8 deg, solving for psi, alpha, lambda, and phi_0.  
  > "The fitting results are summarized in Table 2 and illustrated in Fig. 3 by the solid black curves." (Jet precession)
   - validation: Compared precession direction and phase against VLBA water maser cluster positions and 3D velocities.  
  > "However, the fitted precession motion is consistent with the distribution and 3D motions of the water masers (see Section 4)." (Jet precession)
4. **Calibrate 15-epoch VLBA water maser data, extract maser spot positions, and fit 3D proper motions and ring geometry**: Calibrate and image 15 epochs of VLBA 22.235 GHz data using AIPS/ParselTongue with inverse phase-referencing on a reference maser spot; fit 2D Gaussians with AIPS task SAD; fit a 3D ellipse to the MC-1 ring to derive inclination; fit linear constant-velocity proper motions to 216 spots persisting >= 5 epochs.  
  > "The data were calibrated and imaged using the Astronomical Image Processing System (AIPS) scripts written in ParselTongue (cite: 2006ASPC..351..497K)." (VLBA data); "We fit two-dimensional Gaussian brightness distributions to each identified maser spot to obtain its position using the AIPS task SAD" (VLBA data); "We select 216 spots persisting for at least 5 epochs to calculate their proper motions. A constant velocity (linear) model is used in the fitting, since no clear evidence of acceleration is found." (Proper motions)
   - software: AIPS, ParselTongue, SAD
   - parameter: Reference spot at V_LSR = 104.11 km s^-1 (channel 1000), (R.A., DEC)_J2000 = (18:38:40.1707, -05:35:42.7749)  
  > "where the fringe fit in all the epochs was performed with a strong and point-like water maser spot identified at V$_{\rm LSR}$ = 104.11 $\hbox{${\rm km\,s}^{-1}$}$ (channel 1000) and (R.A., DEC)$_{\rm J2000}$ = (18:38:40.1707, -05:35:42.7749)." (VLBA data)

## Qualitative findings

- A collimated jet is discovered in the middle of the wide-angle (~140 deg) outflow cavity in G26.50+0.28, oriented perpendicular to the elongated MM1 disk/toroid and its CH3OH/CH3CN velocity gradient.  
  > "These observations reveal, for the first time, a collimated jet located in the middle of the outflow cavity. The jet is found to be perpendicular to an elongated disk/toroid and its velocity gradient." (abstract); "The jet is embraced by cavity shells with an opening angle of about 140$\hbox{$^{\circ}$}$." (Discussion and summary)
- The enhanced north-south outflow shells are asymmetric and likely result from interactions between wide-angle winds and the ambient north-south filamentary structure seen in Herschel 250 um data.  
  > "The wide-angle winds are interacting with the large-scale filamentary structure, enhancing the $^{13}$CO (2-1) and SiO(5-4) emission in the north-south shells." (13CO (2-1) and SiO (5-4) maps)
- No clear evidence of jet rotation (velocity gradient perpendicular to the jet axis) is found in either the ALMA SiO(5-4) moment-1 map or the VLBA water maser velocities.  
  > "However, no clear evidence of jet rotation is found in the ALMA and VLBA observations." (abstract); "Besides the knots, no clear velocity trend, i.e. a roughly constant velocity perpendicular to the jet axis is seen in Fig. 5." (Jet kinematics)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Combining sub-arcsecond (~0.2") multi-configuration ALMA thermal line/continuum imaging with milli-arcsecond (~0.002") 15-epoch VLBA water maser 3D kinematics connects large-scale outflow morphology with the jet base to remove ambiguity in maser and outflow interpretation.  
  > "To achieve a comprehensive understanding of the jet properties, it is necessary to complement VLBI observations with high angular resolution imaging of thermally exited gas (cite: 2022A&A...659A..81B)." (Introduction); "In this paper, we focus on the study of the jet/outflow system in G26.50+0.28 by using three-configuration Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) data and fifteen epoch Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA) water maser data, with resolutions of $\approx$ 0.2$\hbox{$^{\prime\prime}$}$ (1260 au) and $\approx$0.002$\hbox{$^{\prime\prime}$}$ (13 au), respectively, to achieve a comprehensive understanding of the jet properties in G26.50+0.28." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Used individual water maser spots rather than flux-weighted maser features for proper motions because feature positions have larger standard deviations due to rapid maser intensity variability; used inverse phase-referencing on an isolated maser spot at the systemic velocity to measure internal motions directly.  
  > "However, we find that the standard deviation of the amplitudes of the proper motions derived from maser features in the two maser clusters (see Section 4.3) is larger than that derived from maser spots." (VLBA data); "In brief, for this unique case, we have a co-moving local “observer” to monitor the internal velocity of the maser spots, significantly eliminating the systemic motions, e.g., Galactic rotation as well as its non-circular (peculiar) velocity components." (The reference spot)
- Alternative: Using water maser features instead of individual maser spots for proper motion analysis (found to yield consistent directions but larger velocity amplitude dispersion due to flux-weighting of variable spots).  
  > "However, we find that the standard deviation of the amplitudes of the proper motions derived from maser features in the two maser clusters (see Section 4.3) is larger than that derived from maser spots." (VLBA data)
- Alternative: Subtracting the mean or isotropic-expansion model velocity of persistent maser spots to define the reference frame (rejected because ~80% of spots lie in the blue lobe, which biases the mean motion).  
  > "It is not applicable in G26.50+0.28 since the maser spots are mainly located in the blue lobe of the outflow, which likely biases the mean motion." (The reference spot)
- Alternative: Interpretation of the outflow as a single northeast-southwest wide-angle bipolar outflow at P.A. ~ 45 deg as proposed by Qiu et al. (2012) (superseded by the collimated jet + wide-angle wind interacting with a north-south filament).  
  > "Complemented by the water maser distribution and its kinematics (see Section 4), we derive a different interpretation, i.e. a collimated jet and wide-angle wind scenario." (13CO (2-1) and SiO (5-4) maps)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): The ALMA angular resolution (~0.2"-0.3") is not high enough to fully resolve the thin inner disk in MM1.  
  > "However, the resolution of the ALMA data is not high enough to fully resolve it." (1.3 mm continuum)
- Limitation (authors): The possibility that an additional jet is hiding in the strong north-south shells cannot be excluded.  
  > "We should note that the possibility that an additional jet is hiding in the relatively strong north-south shells cannot be excluded." (13CO (2-1) and SiO (5-4) maps)
- Limitation (authors): The amplitude of the jet wiggles is small, so the sinusoidal precession fit is not tightly constrained and fits the blueshifted lobe relatively poorly.  
  > "However, we should note that the amplitude of the jet wiggles is small. Therefore the fitting could not be well confined, which may be the reason for the relative poor fitting of the blueshifted lobe." (Jet precession)
- Limitation (authors): The assumption that water maser flux is directly proportional to wind particle intensity is coarse because maser excitation depends on geometry, path length, and shock/gas properties.  
  > "We should note that this assumption is relatively coarse, since the excitation and intensity of masers depends on many factors like the geometry, path lenghts, and the properties of shocks and the local gas (cite: 1992ARA&A..30...75E)." (The distribution of the water maser spots)
- Limitation (authors): Water maser proper motions and 3D velocities against opening angle have large dispersions.  
  > "It seems to present a constant distribution of the 3D jet velocity ($V_{\rm 3D}$) against the opening angle with respect to the symmetry axis of the jet albeit with large dispersion." (Proper motions)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 4.2 text, the fitted major and minor axes of MC-1 are printed as 27.6" and 4.0" (arcseconds), whereas Fig. 7 and the VLBA scale show MC-1 is ~30 mas across (a unit typo in the text: arcsec instead of mas).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The conclusion favoring the X-wind model rests on a flat V_3D vs. opening angle relation over a narrow opening angle range (<= 8 deg) with ~40-50% velocity dispersion (15.7 and 13.4 km s^-1 on ~30-35 km s^-1), where a Keplerian disk-wind gradient could easily be masked by scatter.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The precession fit predicts both maser clusters MC-1 and MC-2 should lie north of the mean jet axis, which contradicts their observed positions unless the driving protostar is offset from the MM1 continuum peak.
