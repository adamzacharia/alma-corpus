## Analysis recipe

1. **Generate moment maps and apply S/N masking**: Pixel-by-pixel Gaussian fitting on the datacube plus a constant for continuum residual to derive zeroth, first, and second moment maps, then mask pixels below S/N thresholds.  
  > "Initially, we performed a pixel-by-pixel Gaussian fitting on the datacube to generate the moment maps: flux (zeroth moment), velocity (first moment), and velocity dispersion (square root of the second moment)." (Moment maps); "We also accounted for an additional constant to match any potential residual of continuum emission." (Moment maps)
   - parameter: 5 sigma_flux threshold for [C II]; 3 sigma_flux threshold for [O III]; vmin and vmax at 16th and 84th percentiles of best-fitting Gaussian  
  > "We then excluded all the pixels in the kinematic maps with \[C$\scriptstyle\rm~II$\] integrated flux emission lower than 5$\sigma^{\rm flux}$." (Moment maps); "For \[O$\scriptstyle\rm~III$\] observations, we adopted a lower threshold of 3$\sigma^{\rm flux}$ because the observed flux density of the oxygen line is usually fainter than that of the \[C$\scriptstyle\rm~II$\] line (cite: Carilli:2013; Inoue:2016; Carniani:2017b; Marrone:2018; Walter:2018; Hashimoto:2019; Harikane:2020; Witstok:2022)." (Moment maps)
   - fitting: Pixel-by-pixel Gaussian + constant fitting  
  > "Initially, we performed a pixel-by-pixel Gaussian fitting on the datacube to generate the moment maps: flux (zeroth moment), velocity (first moment), and velocity dispersion (square root of the second moment)." (Moment maps)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Construct 3D mock datacube kinematic models**: Generate 3D mock datacubes using KINMS with 5x10^5 line-emitting particles for an exponential disk (and optional Sérsic bulge for M* > 10^10 M_sun), convolved with the ALMA PSF.  
  > "We used the Python routine KINMS (cite: Davis:2013) to create the datacube models for the fitting process." (3D datacube model); "KINMS initializes $5\times10^5$ line-emitting particles given by the user." (3D datacube model); "Each spectral slice of the datacube is convoluted with a point spread function (PSF) as large as the ALMA beam of the data sets analyzed in this work." (3D datacube model)
   - software: KINMS
   - parameter: 5x10^5 particles; R0 = 5 kpc for disk mass M_d; Sérsic index n = 4 for bulge  
  > "As this works focuses on galaxies at $z>4$, which have a typical disk scale length of $<2$ kpc (cite: Shibuya:2015), we defined the mass of the disk, $M_d$, as the mass computed within a radius $R_0 = 5$ kpc, which corresponds to  2-3 disk scale lengths: $M_d = M_d(R_0) = M_d(r = 5 {\rm kpc})$." (Kinematic models); "In our analysis, we opted for a de Vaucouleurs profile by assuming $n=4$ for the bulge model (cite: deVaucouleurs:1956)." (Kinematic models)
   - assumption: Thin exponential disk with constant mass-to-light ratio; negligible dark matter contribution within two scale radii; for disk+bulge model, bulge effective radius r_e = 0.5 r_d and bulge mass M_b = M_* from literature.  
  > "We therefore neglected the contribution of dark matter to the circular motion and focused on the disk and bulge components, which are dominant within two scale radii from the galaxy center (cite: Sofue:2013)." (Kinematic models); "For the galaxies that were fit with both bulge and disk components, we did not add additional free parameters, but we assumed that the bulge effective radius is equal to half of the exponential disk scale radius and that the bulge mass is as high as the stellar mass, which has been reported in the literature for our galaxies (see Table \[\[tab:properties\]\](#tab:properties))." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting)
   - fitting: For massive galaxies (M* > 10^10 M_sun), both exponential disk and disk+bulge models are tested and the one minimizing chi^2 is selected.  
  > "In this work, we therefore fit the kinematics maps of massive galaxies with two models: one model that includes both components, and the other model with the exponential disk alone." (Kinematic models); "Then we select the best-fitting model that better reproduces the moment maps by minimizing the $\chi^2$." (Kinematic models)
   - validation: Tested against CASA simobserve mock cubes in Appendices B and C for pixel correlation, beam smearing, and S/N effects.  
  > "To verify the impact of the beam smearing on the inferred kinematic parameters, we simulated a set of ALMA observations, performed a kinematic analysis based on method I, and evaluated the discrepancy between the best-fits results and input parameters." (Impact of beam smearing on the disk size estimate)
3. **Method I: 2D kinematic fitting of resolved galaxies**: Three-step MCMC fitting of moment maps using EMCEE: (1) fit flux map for center (x0, y0) and scale radius (r_d) and fix them; (2) fit velocity map for v_sys, inc, PA, and M_rot; (3) fit all three moment maps simultaneously using step 2 results as priors, sampling uncorrelated pixels separated by more than the beam semi-major axis across 100 trials.  
  > "We initially fit the flux map and determined the center and size of the galaxy." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting); "In the second step, we fit the velocity map and inferred the best-fitting results for the $v_{\rm sys}$, $inc$, $PA,$ and $M_{\rm rot}$ , which were used as priors for the last steps, in which all moment maps were fit simultaneously." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting); "Therefore, we opted to estimate the likelihood by using only independent pixels in the moment maps that were randomly extracted from the maps at a distance greater than the semi-major axis of the beam." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting)
   - software: EMCEE, KINMS
   - parameter: 100 random pixel-extraction trials; 50% burn-in phase; median and 16th/84th percentiles for parameters and errors  
  > "This procedure was performed 100 times for each target to ensure that the convergence of the free parameters did not depend on the pixel selection." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting); "We then combined the walkers for every trial (after a 50% burn-in phase) to derive the best parameters (median values) and error estimates (16th and 84th percentile) of the free parameter distribution for each target." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting)
   - assumption: Pixels separated by a distance greater than the semi-major axis of the beam are uncorrelated.  
  > "In each map, we randomly selected pixels that were separated from each other by a distance greater than the semi-major axis of the beam (see an example of the randomly selected pixel for the target COS-30 in figure [13](#fig:galaxyexample))." (Method I uncertainties and pixel correlation)
   - fitting: Bayesian MCMC with EMCEE maximizing likelihood L = exp(-0.5 chi^2) on independent pixels  
  > "The parameter space was explored using a Bayesian approach based on the Monte Carlo Markov chains (MCMC) method, which allowed us to infer the posterior probability distribution of the free parameters. In particular, we used the package EMCEE (cite: Foreman-Mackey:2013)." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting); "Our fitting process, similar to others used in the literature (cite: Neeleman:2020; Pensabene:2020; Neeleman:2021), determined the best-fitting parameters by maximizing the likelihood function $\mathcal{L} = {\rm exp}(-0.5\chi^2),$ and $\chi^2$ is defined as" (Method I uncertainties and pixel correlation)
   - validation: Compared results on ALESS 073.1, DLA0817g, HZ4, and COS-29 against literature 3DBAROLO and 2D fits, and tested on CASA simobserve mock datasets.  
  > "To exclude any possible bias due to the different fitting algorithms, we also analyzed four high-redshift galaxies that were studied by (cite: Lelli:2021), (cite: Neeleman:2020) (cite: Jones:2021), (cite: Herrera-Camus:2022), and (cite: Posses:2022), and we compare their results with ours." (Results)
4. **Method II: 1D kinematic fitting of spatially integrated spectra for unresolved galaxies**: Fit the flux map to get scale radius r_d and initial inclination constraint; fix M_rot = M_* + M_gas (using alpha_[CII] = 30 M_sun/L_sun); fit spatially integrated spectrum with KINMS model spectrum using MCMC, then divide derived sigma_gas by empirical correction factor of 1.6.  
  > "We initially fit the flux map (step 1 of method I) and inferred both the scale length radius and a first constraint on the disk inclination." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting); "After the scale radius and mass of the galaxy were estimated, the only free parameters of our datacube model are the disk inclination $inc$ and the intrinsic velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\rm gas}$." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting); "Hence, we applied a correction factor of 1.6 to the results inferred from method II." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting)
   - software: EMCEE, KINMS
   - parameter: alpha_[CII] = 30 M_sun/L_sun; correction factor = 1.6 on sigma_gas; flat prior on sigma_gas in [0, 300] km s^-1; flat prior on inc within +/- 3 sigma of flux-map fit  
  > "We fit the data spectrum by using an MCMC procedure and adopting a flat prior distribution for the velocity dispersion in the range 0 km s$^{-1}$ and 300 km s$^{-1}$." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting); "Since the inclination plays a major role in the global profile shape and the subsequent determination of velocity dispersion of the galaxy, we left as parameter the inclination assuming flat priors between $\pm 3 \sigma$ of the best-fitting inclination estimated from the flux map." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting)
   - assumption: M_rot = M_* + M_gas; alpha_[CII] = 30 M_sun/L_sun with 0.2 dex uncertainty.  
  > "with $\alpha_{\rm [CII]}={\rm  30 M_\odot / L_\odot}$. The uncertainty on the conversion factor between \[C$\scriptstyle\rm~II$\] luminosity and gas mass is about 0.2 dex." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting)
   - fitting: MCMC fit of S^model(a, b, sigma_gas, inc) = b + a F^datacube(sigma_gas, inc) to integrated spectrum  
  > "In conclusion, the spectrum of each galaxy was fit with a spectrum $S^{\rm model}$ defined as $$\begin{equation}
  S^{\rm model}(a,b,\sigma_{\rm gas}, inc) = b + a F^{\rm datacube}(\sigma_{\rm gas}, inc)
.
\end{equation}$$" (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting)
   - validation: Tested Method II on Method I galaxies, finding a median overestimation factor of 1.6 in sigma_gas, and tested free vs fixed radius/inclination on simulated ALMA datacubes (Appendix D).  
  > "To understand the correctness of the value of velocity dispersion extrapolated with method II, we exploited the galaxies analyzed with method I, and we also analyzed them with method II." (Method II tests and correction factor); "Analyzing the difference between the two different values, we found that the velocity dispersion estimated with method II is overestimated in the median by a factor of 1.6." (Method II tests and correction factor)

## Qualitative findings

- Gas kinematics of z > 5 main-sequence galaxies are consistent with rotating but turbulent disks, with velocity dispersions 4-5 times higher than local galaxies and z~5 dust-obscured galaxies.  
  > "The gas kinematics of the high-$z$ galaxy population observed with ALMA is consistent within the errors with rotating but turbulent disks." (abstract); "We indeed infer a velocity dispersion that is systematically higher by 4-5 times than the local galaxy population and the $z\sim5$ dust-obscured galaxies reported in the literature." (abstract)
- Velocity dispersions measured from [C II] and [O III] are similar in three out of four galaxies observed in both lines.  
  > "The velocity dispersion computed with both the gas tracer \[C$\scriptstyle\rm~II$\], which traces the multiphase medium, and \[O$\scriptstyle\rm~III$\], which only traces the ionized gas, does not show a significant difference for three out of four galaxies, while a galaxy shows a velocity dispersion traced with ionized gas greater than a factor of 2, as it does for lower-redshift galaxies (cite: Girard:2021)." (Conclusions)
- Gravitational energy released by mass transport within the disk is the main driver of gas velocity dispersion in z > 5 main-sequence galaxies, whereas star formation feedback alone is insufficient.  
  > "The comparison with the theoretical predictions suggests that the main driver of the velocity dispersion in high-redshift galaxies is the gravitational energy that is released by the transport of mass within the disk." (abstract); "This high-velocity dispersion cannot be sustained by similar models in which star formation feedback (i.e., supernova explosions) is the only source of the velocity dispersion." (Conclusions)
- V_max / sigma_gas at z > 4 depends on stellar mass and dust content rather than gas fraction alone.  
  > "The ratio $V_{\rm max}/\sigma_{\rm gas}$ does not show a clear dependence on the gas fraction, as was assumed by (cite: Wisnioski:2015). However, we also found a dependence on the stellar mass and the dust mass." (Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Selected z > 4 main-sequence star-forming galaxies (excluding SMGs, quasars, and clear mergers) with [C II] or [O III] observations at resolution < 1.5'' and S/N > 7 from the public ALMA archive to represent the bulk of the early galaxy population.  
  > "We aimed to explore the kinematic properties of high-redshift star-forming galaxies and therefore excluded known submillimeter galaxies (SMGs) and quasars, which do not represent the bulk of the galaxy population (cite: Weiss:2013)." (Sample selection and observations); "Finally, we removed observations in which the far-infrared lines are detected with a signal-to-noise ratio[^3] (S/N)$<7$, which is not sufficient to perform a kinematic analysis on the data." (Sample selection and observations)
- Why this method: Convolving 3D KINMS models with the ALMA beam directly accounts for beam smearing, random sampling of pixels separated by more than the beam semi-major axis avoids noise correlation in 2D fits, and 1D integrated spectrum fitting extracts velocity dispersion when low resolution/S/N prevents a clear velocity gradient.  
  > "In this way, the fitting process already takes into account the effect of beam smearing without the need for further corrections." (3D datacube model); "Therefore, we opted to estimate the likelihood by using only independent pixels in the moment maps that were randomly extracted from the maps at a distance greater than the semi-major axis of the beam." (Method I: 2D kinematic fitting); "To overcome this problem and gain some information regarding the kinematics of these galaxies, we determined the kinematic properties from the spatially integrated spectrum." (Method II: 1D kinematic fitting)
- Alternative: Dark matter halo contribution to the rotation curve was neglected because observations only probe inner regions (within two scale radii) where baryons dominate.  
  > "We therefore neglected the contribution of dark matter to the circular motion and focused on the disk and bulge components, which are dominant within two scale radii from the galaxy center (cite: Sofue:2013)." (Kinematic models)
- Alternative: Fitting all moment map pixels as independent was rejected because spatial correlation within a beam underestimates parameter uncertainties.  
  > "This means that the assumption that individual pixels in the moment maps are independent can lead to an underestimation of the free parameter uncertainties." (Method I uncertainties and pixel correlation)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Only rotating disk models were considered; the angular resolution of the ALMA data is insufficient to distinguish rotating disks from spatially unresolved mergers separated by less than the half-beam size (< 2 kpc).  
  > "A caveat of the present work is that we only considered rotating disk models. An alternative scenario would be that the observed velocity gradients are a consequence of two spatially unresolved sources at different systematic velocities that are located at a distance smaller than the half-beam size (i.e., $<$ 2 kpc)." (Kinematic models)
- Limitation (authors): V_max / sigma_gas is underestimated by the fitting method when the beam size exceeds three times the galaxy size (FWHM_beam / 2r_d = 3.5).  
  > "We find that $V_{\rm max}/\sigma_{\rm gas}$ is underestimated if the beam size is more than three times the galaxy size." (Beam-smearing effect)
- Limitation (authors): Poor angular resolution and low S/N prevent constraining inclination, dynamical mass, and velocity dispersion for some targets, yielding only upper or lower limits.  
  > "Due to the poor angular resolution of some observations, we were not able to constrain the disk inclination, dynamical mass, and velocity dispersion of all galaxies. In these cases, we inferred upper limits to the kinematics parameters." (Results)
- Limitation (authors): High-redshift observations do not probe the low-SFR regime needed to distinguish between feedback+transport and transport-only turbulence models.  
  > "Unfortunately, our high-redshift observations are not able to explore the region at low SFR, so that we are not able to distinguish between the feedback+transport and the transport-only models." (Drivers of the velocity dispersion)
- Limitation (authors): Assuming a fixed dust temperature for some galaxies may have overestimated their dust masses.  
  > "For some galaxies, the dust temperature was assumed to be a fixed value. We therefore note that the correlation between the dust temperature and the dust mass may have overestimated some of the dust mass estimates." (Sample properties)
