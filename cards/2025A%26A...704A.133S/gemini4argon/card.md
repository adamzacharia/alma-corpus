## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, self-calibrate, continuum-subtract, and image continuum and molecular line cubes**: Standard ALMA pipeline calibration followed by phase-only and phase-and-amplitude self-calibration on continuum, uvcontsub continuum subtraction, and imaging in CASA 6.2.1 using the eDisk reduction script  
  > "Subsequent reduction and imaging were performed using the Common Astronomy Software Application (cite: Mcmullin_2007) version 6.2.1, following a script developed for the eDisk Large Program." (Observations); "The spectral line cubes were then generated by subtracting the continuum emission from the visibility data using the CASA task *uvcontsub*." (Observations)
   - software: CASA, uvcontsub
   - parameter: robust = 2.0 for continuum contours and spectral line images (robust = 0.5 for OphIRS63)  
  > "We adopted the robust value of 2.0 for both the continuum contours and the spectral line images used in this work to increase the signal-to-noise ratio, particularly of the weaker spectral line emissions." (Observations); "For OphIRS63, which only has short baseline observations, we adopt the robust value of 0.5." (Observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Generate moment 8 and moment 9 maps and determine molecular detections across the sample**: Create peak intensity (moment 8) and peak velocity (moment 9) maps for each targeted molecule using pixels >= 3 sigma, requiring spatial structures to extend beyond one synthesized beam for a clear detection  
  > "In order to characterize the different morphologies and the kinematics observed toward the eDisk sources, we create peak intensity (moment 8) and peak velocity (moment 9) maps for each of the targeted molecules." (Results); "These maps are made only using pixels where emissions are detected at a level of $\geq3\sigma$. Here, a “clear” detection in the molecular emission is defined to be a spatial structure in the moment maps that extends beyond the area of a synthesized beam." (Results)
   - parameter: Pixel threshold >= 3 sigma; spatial extent > 1 synthesized beam area  
  > "These maps are made only using pixels where emissions are detected at a level of $\geq3\sigma$. Here, a “clear” detection in the molecular emission is defined to be a spatial structure in the moment maps that extends beyond the area of a synthesized beam." (Results)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Requiring spatial structure > 1 synthesized beam area to avoid false positives from isolated noise fluctuations exceeding 3 sigma  
  > "This ensures that any marginal emissions or isolated peaks that can arise from noise fluctuations and exceed the 3$\sigma$ threshold are not identified as false positives." (Results)
3. **Classify morphological components (outflows, high-velocity jets, cavity walls, inner envelope/disk, streamers) across sources and lines**: Visual and kinematic inspection of large-scale (15 arcsec) and small-scale (5 arcsec) moment 8 and moment 9 maps, separating low-velocity outflows (< 20 km s^-1) and high-velocity jets (> 20 km s^-1 relative to v_sys)  
  > "For the purpose of this study, we rely exclusively on the results seen in the moment 8 and moment 9 plots to identify and define the different components." (Results); "Compared to low-velocity outflows with $|v_{sys} - v_{chan}| < 20$ km s$^{-1}$, these jets are much more collimated, have narrower opening angles, and originate much closer to the protostar." (Outflowing material)
   - parameter: |v_sys - v_chan| = 20 km s^-1 velocity threshold between low-velocity outflows and high-velocity jets  
  > "Outflows generally consist of low-velocity components, with gases moving at velocities up to 20 km s$^{-1}$ relative to the $v_{sys}$ of the source." (Outflowing material); "Additionally, we also detect high-velocity molecular jets with $|v_{sys} - v_{chan}| > 20$ km s$^{-1}$, in emissions of $^{12}$CO and SiO toward four sources." (Conclusions)
   - fitting: _not stated_

## Qualitative findings

- CO isotopologues, SO, and H2CO are detected throughout the sample, whereas SiO, DCN, and CH3OH are predominantly detected toward Class 0 sources  
  > "Certain molecules such as CO isotopologues, SO, and H$_{2}$CO are found throughout the sample, while molecules such as SiO, DCN, and CH$_{3}$OH are predominantly detected toward the Class 0 sources." (Results)
- 12CO primarily traces molecular outflows and outflow cavity walls, while SiO and high-velocity 12CO trace collimated jets  
  > "Emissions in $^{12}$CO are seen toward all sources and primarily trace outflowing materials. A few sources also show high-velocity jets in SiO emission and high-velocity channel maps of $^{12}$CO." (abstract)
- 13CO and C18O trace the rotating inner envelope and disk regions, while large-scale 13CO, c-C3H2, and H2CO delineate UV-irradiated and shock-heated outflow cavity walls  
  > "The $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O emissions are well-known tracers of high-density regions and trace the inner envelope and disk regions with clear signs of rotation seen at continuum scales." (abstract); "The walls of the outflow cavities have high densities and are delineated by large-scale $^{13}$CO emissions. Exposure to UV radiation and outflow shocks leads to increased temperatures in the cavity walls, leading to enhanced emissions from $c-$C$_3$H$_2$ and H$_{2}$CO in these regions." (Conclusions)
- DCN and CH3OH show compact emission peaking at the protostar, with CH3OH contained within the DCN emitting region and tracing hot corino regions (T > 100 K) in BHR71 IRS1/2, IRAS16544, and likely IRAS04166  
  > "Both DCN and CH$_{3}$OH, when detected, show compact emissions from the inner envelope and disk regions that peak at the position of the protostar. The CH$_{3}$OH emissions are contained within the region of DCN emissions, which suggests that CH$_{3}$OH traces the hot core regions." (abstract); "The presence of CH$_{3}$OH in the inner envelope and disk regions of BHR71 IRS1/2, IRAS16544, and likely IRAS04166 indicates that these sources are “hot corinos”, where the temperatures exceed 100 K in the innermost ($\leq 100$ au) dense regions around protostars (cite: Ceccarelli_2004; Ceccarelli_2007)." (Inner envelope and disk)
- Accretion streamers are present in both OphIRS63 and L1489, the only two eDisk sources showing clear continuum substructures  
  > "Notably, streamers are present in both OphIRS63 and L1489, the only two sources in our sample that also show clear signs of substructures, suggesting a possible link between the presence of streamers and the development of these substructures." (Accretion streamers)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Uniform, well-characterized sample of 19 Class 0/I sources observed consistently at ~0.1 arcsec (~15 au) resolution with short and long baselines (MRS ~3 arcsec), improving on heterogeneous and lower-resolution (~0.5-3 arcsec) prior surveys  
  > "First, unlike the sources in (cite: Tychoniec_2021), which were drawn from various studies, the eDisk program utilizes a uniform and well-characterized sample of sources, all observed in the same manner." (Introduction); "Second, the eDisk observations achieve higher spatial resolutions compared to those in the previous study ($\sim$0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$1 than $\sim$0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$5 – 3$''$). This enables us to investigate the physical and chemical processes in the innermost regions of protostellar disks." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Adopted Briggs robust = 2.0 to increase S/N of weaker spectral line emissions (robust = 0.5 for OphIRS63 to keep beam size within a factor of 2-3), and used moment 8 and moment 9 maps with a >= 3 sigma threshold and > 1 beam area extent to avoid false positives  
  > "We adopted the robust value of 2.0 for both the continuum contours and the spectral line images used in this work to increase the signal-to-noise ratio, particularly of the weaker spectral line emissions. For OphIRS63, which only has short baseline observations, we adopt the robust value of 0.5." (Observations); "These maps are made only using pixels where emissions are detected at a level of $\geq3\sigma$. Here, a “clear” detection in the molecular emission is defined to be a spatial structure in the moment maps that extends beyond the area of a synthesized beam." (Results)
- Alternative: Deriving kinetic temperatures from H2CO (3_{0,3}-2_{0,2}) to (3_{2,2}-2_{2,1}) line ratios was rejected because RADEX calculations show the 3_{0,3}-2_{0,2} line is optically thick on disk scales  
  > "A simple non-local thermal equilibrium (non-LTE) calculation using RADEX (cite: Tak_2007) demonstrates that the 3$_{0,3}$–2$_{0,2}$ transition becomes optically thick under these conditions, even for a conservative H$_{2}$CO abundance of 10$^{-9}$, which is why this method is not applicable on these scales." (Morphology of H$_{2}$CO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): OphIRS63 only has short-baseline observations due to scheduling constraints  
  > "Both short and long baseline data were obtained for all sources in our sample, with the exception of OphIRS63, for which only short baseline data could be obtained due to scheduling constraints." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Archival data for TMC1A and B335 only cover 12CO, 13CO, and C18O alongside the continuum, lacking the other molecular species  
  > "Alongside the continuum emission, the archival data for TMC1A and B335 included molecular emissions from $^{12}$CO, $^{13}$CO, and C$^{18}$O." (Observations)
- Limitation (authors): Optically thick and cold foreground clouds and bright optically thick continuum at small radii cause absorption features and continuum oversubtraction in C18O, 12CO, 13CO, and DCN  
  > "This feature likely results from the optically thick and cold foreground clouds absorbing the warmer continuum emissions at low velocities, causing the continuum oversubtraction. This absorption also occurs toward the emissions of $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO but due to their relatively high brightness, is less apparent and important in those molecules." (C$^{18}$O)
- Limitation (authors): Complex surrounding environment in the Coronet region contaminates and conceals emissions toward IRS5N and IRS7B, and line opacity plus spatial filtering affect low-velocity channels near v_sys  
  > "Both IRS7B and IRS5N are part of the complex Coronet region, which harbors multiple highly energetic molecular hydrogen emission-line objects (MHOs) and dozens of Herbig–Haro (HH) objects that likely interact and conceal any emission from these sources (cite: Wang_2004). Furthermore, any low-velocity emissions close to the $v_{sys}$ of these sources are difficult to distinguish due to line opacity effects and spatial filtering." ($^{12}$CO)
- Limitation (authors): Small maximum recoverable scale (~3 arcsec) means eDisk observations are not optimized for detecting large-scale accretion streamers and may miss streamers originating at larger scales  
  > "However, it is important to note that the eDisk observations were not optimized for detecting large-scale streamers." (Accretion streamers); "In comparison, the eDisk survey probed considerably smaller spatial scales, with spatial resolutions of $\sim$0$.\!\!^{\prime\prime}$1 and maximum recoverable scales of only $\sim$3$''$." (Accretion streamers)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Component classifications (outflows, cavity walls, disks, streamers) rely purely on visual inspection of moment 8 and moment 9 maps rather than quantitative kinematic modeling or radiative transfer fitting
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Non-detections of DCN, CH3OH, and SO in Class I sources may be driven by lower envelope masses and sensitivity limits rather than intrinsic chemical evolution
