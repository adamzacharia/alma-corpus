## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and post-process the 12m spectral line cubes**: Restore QA3-reprocessed measurement sets with scriptForPI.py, split spectral windows and combine 12m configurations, build continuum startmodel from cleanest continuum (.tt0 and .tt1), deconvolve line cubes with multi-scale tclean using a 10% pbmask, convolve models to a common beam with radio-beam, apply JvM correction to residuals, and subtract continuum in the image plane with STATCONT  
  > "The custom ALMA-IMF data pipeline developed to produce the calibrated and imaged continuum data (as described in (cite: Ginsburg2022)) was subsequently adapted to process the full spectral line windows." (ALMA-IMF data pipeline); "The JvM correction rescales the residuals to the same units (Jy per clean beam) by multiplying them by the $\epsilon$ factor before the final image restoration." (JvM correction); "To make the cubes more readily usable, we convolve the model in every channel to a common Gaussian beam per spectral window using the `radio-beam` tool." (JvM correction)
   - software: CASA, tclean, astroquery, radio-beam, STATCONT
   - parameter: pbmask = 10% of primary beam response  
  > "For these reasons, we opted to use the primary beam mask (pbmask) as our `tclean` mask, setting its limit to 10% of the primary beam response." (Cleaning optimisation and masking)
   - parameter: Cleaning threshold = 5 sigma (10 sigma for G351.77)  
  > "We found that using a relatively shallow threshold of 5$\sigma$ resulted in the most stable outcomes, that is, mitigating the effects of divergence and ‘stippling’ over the full dataset." (Cleaning threshold); "In the end, for this source, we ran `tclean` only down to 10$\sigma$ level for all spectral windows." (Cleaning threshold)
   - parameter: Multi-scale scales = geometric series from 0 increasing by ~x2 minor beam/cell size up to ~5.5''  
  > "We defined the scales used in multi-scale `tclean` using a geometric series starting at 0 and increasing by $\sim \times 2$ the ratio of the minor beam axis to the cell size until a scale of $\sim$5.5$''$ is reached." (Cleaning optimisation and masking)
   - assumption: Dirty beam volume is integrated up to its first null to compute JvM epsilon factor  
  > "The dirty beam is only considered up to its first null since this is the part approximated to a Gaussian during deconvolution and because the volume of a dirty beam without zero spacing integrated over its full domain is formally zero." (JvM correction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Internal quality assessments of tclean products testing masking methods, deconvolvers, and thresholds to prevent stippling and divergence  
  > "We performed several iterations of `tclean` using the Hogbom and multi-scale deconvolvers and tested methods to mask the line emission: 1) an internally developed auto-masking, 2) the CASA auto multi-threshold masking, and 3) a primary beam mask." (Cleaning optimisation and masking)
2. **Estimate cube noise levels homogeneously across all fields and spectral windows**: Calculate MAD (scaled by 1.4826) on the 25% of channels with the lowest intensity within manually defined continuum noise regions on JvM-corrected, non-continuum-subtracted, non-primary-beam-corrected cubes, then scale by 1/epsilon to convert to mJy per dirty beam  
  > "To estimate noise levels, we use the median-absolute-deviation (MAD) estimator and take a threshold cut in intensity so that the noise is estimated using only the 25% of the channels with the lowest intensity across the full spectral window, and using the manually defined regions which were created for the continuum estimates of the noise in (cite: Ginsburg2022)." (Noise estimation); "The most conservative approach to obtaining the noise, which we adopt here, is to estimate the noise from emission and line-free regions in the JvM cubes (in units of Jy per clean beam) and scale by 1/$\epsilon$ factor (see 3.5), thus, taking the higher noise estimates in units of Jy per dirty beam." (JvM correction)
   - software: astropy.stats.median_abs_deviation
   - parameter: 25% lowest intensity channels; MAD scale factor = 1.4826  
  > "To estimate noise levels, we use the median-absolute-deviation (MAD) estimator and take a threshold cut in intensity so that the noise is estimated using only the 25% of the channels with the lowest intensity across the full spectral window, and using the manually defined regions which were created for the continuum estimates of the noise in (cite: Ginsburg2022)." (Noise estimation); "Implemented in `astropy.stats.median_abs_deviation` and we scale this by 1.4826 such that the reported value is equivalent to the standard deviation if the underlying data are normally distributed." (Footnotes)
   - assumption: Noise estimated from a smaller clean beam should be scaled up by the dirty-to-clean beam volume ratio (1/epsilon)  
  > "In other words, we assume the noise estimated from a smaller clean beam should be larger by the ratio of the dirty and clean beam areas." (JvM correction)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared achieved noise against theoretical proposal noise scaled to the same spectral resolution, beam size, and central frequency  
  > "we scaled the requested noise to the same spectral resolution, beam size, and central frequency as those obtained in the observed data cubes for all spectral windows." (Noise estimation)
3. **Create global DCN (3-2) moment maps**: Shift B6 spw1 STATCONT continuum-subtracted, non-primary-beam-corrected cubes to DCN (3-2) rest frame, extract a 50 km s^-1 subcube around protocluster V_LSR, and compute moment 0, 1, and 2 maps over manually determined velocity ranges using a 4 sigma threshold  
  > "We then extract a 50 km s$^{-1}$ wide subset of channels around the $V_{\rm LSR}$ reported in (cite: Motte2022) and create the first three moment maps towards the 15 protoclusters, using the non-primary beam corrected cubes." (Global scale DCN (3-2) morphology); "The DCN (3-2) emission is integrated over a manually determined velocity range for each protocluster, using a 4$\sigma$ threshold to identify channels where DCN (3-2) emission is present over an area larger than the beam size (the velocity ranges used for each protocluster are provided in the figure captions)." (Global scale DCN (3-2) morphology); "Furthermore, for the first and second-moment maps, we take an additional threshold cut per channel and include only pixels that contain emission $>$4$\sigma$, preventing noisy pixels from being added into them." (Global scale DCN (3-2) morphology)
   - software: spectral-cube
   - parameter: Subcube width = 50 km s^-1; pixel and channel threshold = 4 sigma (dirty beam noise); rest frequency = 217.23854 GHz  
  > "DCN (3-2) has a rest frequency of 217.23854 GHz (cite: muller2001; muller2005) and is located in B6, spectral window 1, with a spectral resolution of 0.34 km s$^{-1}$." (Global scale DCN (3-2) morphology); "Furthermore, for the first and second-moment maps, we take an additional threshold cut per channel and include only pixels that contain emission $>$4$\sigma$, preventing noisy pixels from being added into them." (Global scale DCN (3-2) morphology)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Extract core-averaged background-subtracted DCN (3-2) spectra and fit hyperfine structure**: Extract average DCN (3-2) spectrum for each of the 595 continuum cores from an elliptical aperture of 2x the continuum FWHM, subtract background spectrum from an elliptical annulus (2x to 3x continuum FWHM, excluding overlapping neighbour cores), fit single-component DCN hyperfine structure model in PySpecKit, and classify detections into Single- or Complex-type  
  > "We use an elliptical aperture with a major (minor) axis length twice that of the continuum source major (minor) FWHM to extract the spectrum, where the continuum core sizes are taken from (cite: louvet22)." (DCN (3-2) line extraction and fitting); "We extract the background emission from an elliptical annulus with an inner major (minor) axis size equal to the size of the spectral aperture (i.e. twice the continuum source major (minor) FWHM) and outer radius size 1.5x larger (i.e. three times the continuum source major (minor) FWHM)." (DCN (3-2) line extraction and fitting); "The resulting core-average, background-subtracted spectrum for each core is then fitted using a single component hyperfine structure model (HFS) adapted for the DCN (3-2) transition in `PySpecKit` (cite: Ginsburg2011)." (DCN (3-2) line extraction and fitting)
   - software: spectral-cube, PySpecKit
   - parameter: Core aperture = 2x continuum FWHM; background annulus = 2x to 3x continuum FWHM; detection criteria: S/N > 4 sigma (from MAD in 30 line-free channels) and velocity dispersion > 0.2 km s^-1; 7 hyperfine components from CDMS  
  > "A core is determined to have a DCN (3-2) detection if a velocity dispersion of $>$0.2 km s$^{-1}$ and a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $>$4$\sigma$ are found in the core averaged, background-subtracted spectrum." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores); "The noise in the spectrum is estimated from the MAD in 30 continuous channels ($\sim$10 km s$^{-1}$) from either the lower or upper part of the spectrum that were identified by eye to be in a line free part of the spectrum." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores)
   - assumption: DCN (3-2) is an optically thin dense gas tracer with critical density ~10^7 cm^-3; FWHM linewidth = sigma_obs * sqrt(8 ln 2)  
  > "We utilise the molecular transition DCN (3-2) as a proxy for the gas associated with the core as it is typically an optically thin tracer with a critical density of $\sim$10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$." (Analysis); "The linewidths are taken as $\sigma_{obs}\times\sqrt{8ln2}$, where $\sigma_{obs}$ is the observed velocity dispersion from single component HFS fits to the core-averaged, background-subtracted spectra." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores)
   - fitting: Single-component hyperfine structure (HFS) model fit in PySpecKit; single-component Gaussian fit in PySpecKit for comparison lines (DCO+, OC33S, C18O, OCS, 13CS, N2D+) in G338.93  
  > "The resulting core-average, background-subtracted spectrum for each core is then fitted using a single component hyperfine structure model (HFS) adapted for the DCN (3-2) transition in `PySpecKit` (cite: Ginsburg2011)." (DCN (3-2) line extraction and fitting); "They are fitted and classified as Single- or Complex-type spectra in the same way as for DCN (3-2) (described in 4.3), but using only a single component Gaussian fit in pyspeckit." (Comparison with other molecular lines)
   - validation: Visual inspection of all fitted spectra to separate Single- and Complex-type spectra; repeated extraction and fitting without background subtraction to test for background-subtraction bias  
  > "This is done manually by visually inspecting all fitted DCN (3-2) spectra." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores); "To assess if the background subtraction could bias the results, we also perform an extraction on the non-background subtracted DCN (3-2) spectra towards each core in 11." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores)
5. **Analyze core-to-core velocity dispersion, spatial spread, and non-thermal velocity dispersion across evolutionary stages**: Compute core velocity offsets, core-to-core velocity dispersions, radial spatial offsets, and non-thermal velocity dispersions for the 266 Single-type cores; apply Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests between evolutionary stages and compute Pearson's correlation coefficient between non-thermal velocity dispersion and deconvolved core size  
  > "In 14, we plot the core-to-core velocity dispersion vs the spatial spread (following, e.g. (cite: Stutz2016)) for each core grouped by their host region’s evolutionary stage." (Core-to-core velocity distribution); "In 16, we show the non-thermal velocity dispersion-size relation for the 266 cores in our sample from all 15 protoclusters, where the non-thermal velocity dispersion ($\sigma_{NT}$) is estimated assuming: $$\begin{equation} \label{eq:thermaldisp} \sigma_{NT} = \sqrt{\sigma^2 - \frac{kT_{k}}{\mu m_H}}, \nonumber \end{equation}$$" (DCN (3-2) linewidths); "We fit Pearson’s correlation coefficient ($\rho$), which measures the linear correlation between two variables, and find a value of 0.18, indicating no correlation exists between the dispersion and core sizes in these data." (DCN (3-2) linewidths)
   - parameter: T_k = 18 K to 300 K (from continuum dust temperature); smoothed continuum beam = 2700 au  
  > "and has a range from 18 K to be 300 K for all cores" (Core kinematics and distribution with evolutionary classification); "We use here the smoothed core catalogue, where the continuum maps are smoothed to the same angular resolution of 2700 au." (Footnotes)
   - assumption: Gas kinetic temperature T_k for DCN (3-2) equals the core dust temperature from Louvet et al.; core size is the geometric mean of major and minor continuum FWHMs deconvolved by the smoothed beam (unless deconvolved size < 0.5x non-deconvolved size)  
  > "The core sizes are the geometric mean of the major and minor continuum core FWHMs from (cite: louvet22) deconvolved by the smoothed beam." (DCN (3-2) linewidths); "Unless the deconvolved size is less than half of the non-deconvolved size, then the non-deconvolved source size is used." (Footnotes)
   - fitting: Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests and Pearson's linear correlation coefficient  
  > "Furthermore, a KS test on the DCN (3-2) linewidths of those cores situated in the evolved regions, when compared with the DCN (3-2) linewidths of cores from intermediate $+$ young regions, gives a $P_{values}$<0.001, suggesting that they are not drawn from the same population." (DCN (3-2) linewidths)
   - validation: Tested randomizing the cluster centre definition using ten random cores per protocluster and confirmed spatial spread trends on the full continuum core sample  
  > "However, if we take ten random cores in each protocluster as the central position, the evolved protoclusters always have larger standard deviations in their spatial spread, which is significant with a KS-test." (Core-to-core velocity distribution)

## Qualitative findings

- Global DCN (3-2) emission traces more widespread, elongated filamentary structures in evolved protoclusters compared to compact hub-type clumps in young protoclusters.  
  > "In addition, we provide an overview of the DCN (3-2) emission across the ALMA-IMF protoclusters, where the global DCN (3-2) emission displays a diversity of morphology and kinematics across the sample, tracing more widespread, filamentary structures in the evolved regions, compared to young protoclusters where the DCN emission is found in more compact hub-type structures." (Summary)
- DCN (3-2) recovers the highest percentage of continuum cores with Single-type spectra in G338.93 (43%) compared to other dense gas tracers (5-31%), making it the best single proxy for core dense gas in the ALMA-IMF bandwidth.  
  > "DCN (3-2) exhibits Single-type spectra towards the highest percentile ($\sim$43%) of continuum cores in G338.93, versus 5-31% for the other species." (Comparison with other molecular lines); "Thus, using only a single tracer, DCN (3-2) likely still provides the best proxy of the dense gas associated with the continuum cores in the ALMA-IMF bandwidth coverage." (Comparison with other molecular lines)
- Evolved protoclusters have a significantly higher fraction of DCN-detected cores and a wider spatial distribution of cores than young and intermediate protoclusters.  
  > "The evolved protoclusters have a higher percentage of DCN-detected cores, suggesting that the DCN (3-2) transition is likely a more complete tracer of the core population in evolved protoclusters." (Summary); "Furthermore, the cores with DCN (3-2) detections in evolved regions tend to be more spatially extended over the region than in the younger protoclusters." (Summary)
- There is no significant difference in core-to-core velocity dispersion across evolutionary stages and no correlation between non-thermal velocity dispersion and core size.  
  > "We find no obvious difference between the average core-to-core velocities and only a tentative difference in linewidths with the protocluster evolutionary stage." (Summary); "We fit Pearson’s correlation coefficient ($\rho$), which measures the linear correlation between two variables, and find a value of 0.18, indicating no correlation exists between the dispersion and core sizes in these data." (DCN (3-2) linewidths)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: ALMA-IMF homogeneously surveys 15 massive Galactic protoclusters at ~2000 au resolution across ~6.7 GHz of bandwidth; DCN (3-2) was selected because it is an optically thin tracer of dense gas (~10^7 cm^-3) that coincides well with thermal dust cores and recovers the highest fraction of Single-type core spectra.  
  > "ALMA-IMF was designed to observe homogeneously 15 of the most extreme and massive Galactic clouds within a distance range of 2-5.5 kpc." (Observations); "We utilise the molecular transition DCN (3-2) as a proxy for the gas associated with the core as it is typically an optically thin tracer with a critical density of $\sim$10$^7$ cm$^{-3}$." (Analysis)
- Why this method: Multi-scale tclean with a 10% primary beam mask and 5 sigma threshold avoided divergence and stippling; continuum startmodel avoided uv-plane subtraction divergence; JvM correction and radio-beam common-beam convolution ensured consistent flux scales across shallowly cleaned cubes; elliptical annulus background subtraction reduced foreground/background cloud contamination.  
  > "Multi-scale deconvolution with a primary beam mask produced the most stable and optimal results over the full set of spectral windows." (Cleaning optimisation and masking); "As discussed previously, these cubes are not deeply cleaned due to divergence and stippling issues over the full bandwidth, which can result in significant real emission remaining in the residuals. Thus, the JvM correction provides a more accurate estimation of the flux in our data." (JvM correction); "As with the continuum cores, where the background is filtered to estimate the core properties more accurately, we chose to perform background subtraction on the DCN (3-2) emission to limit contamination from background and foreground DCN (3-2) within the dense regions of the protocluster." (DCN (3-2) line extraction and fitting)
- Alternative: Simple uv-plane continuum subtraction prior to tclean (rejected due to complexity of data, varying dynamic range, and tclean divergence)  
  > "Given the large extent of the mosaic coverage, the complexity of the data, and the varying dynamic range across an individual field, we chose not to perform a simple continuum subtraction in the uv-plane before running `tclean`." (Continuum start-model)
- Alternative: Hogbom deconvolver, internally developed auto-masking, and CASA auto multi-threshold masking (rejected because Hogbom recovered less extended emission, auto-masking failed to cover full extended emission, and auto multi-threshold had convergence problems with multi-scale)  
  > "We performed several iterations of `tclean` using the Hogbom and multi-scale deconvolvers and tested methods to mask the line emission: 1) an internally developed auto-masking, 2) the CASA auto multi-threshold masking, and 3) a primary beam mask." (Cleaning optimisation and masking); "Furthermore, auto multi-threshold had additional convergence problems when working with multi-scale deconvolution." (Cleaning optimisation and masking)
- Alternative: Deep cleaning thresholds of <= 3 sigma or 4 sigma (rejected due to stippling artifacts and divergence across multiple spectral windows)  
  > "In addition to the stippling, we found that cleaning to levels of 4$\sigma$ or deeper also resulted in divergence across multiple spectral windows, compared with setting the noise threshold to $\geq$5$\sigma$." (Cleaning threshold)
- Alternative: Non-background subtracted core spectra (tested in Appendix E.2; resulted in broader average linewidths of ~2.1 km s^-1 vs ~1.6 km s^-1 due to background contamination)  
  > "The average linewidth of the fits, however, is smaller ($\sim$1.6 km s$^{-1}$) for the background-subtracted compared with ($\sim$2.1 km s$^{-1}$) for the non-background subtracted, which is due to the removal of the background contribution." (Comparison of the background and non-background subtracted DCN spectra)
- How they decided it was enough: Cleaning stopped at 5 sigma (10 sigma for G351.77) because cleaning to <=4 sigma caused divergence and stippling; core detections required S/N > 4 sigma and velocity dispersion > 0.2 km s^-1, with visual inspection filtering out Complex-type spectra.  
  > "In addition to the stippling, we found that cleaning to levels of 4$\sigma$ or deeper also resulted in divergence across multiple spectral windows, compared with setting the noise threshold to $\geq$5$\sigma$." (Cleaning threshold); "A core is determined to have a DCN (3-2) detection if a velocity dispersion of $>$0.2 km s$^{-1}$ and a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) $>$4$\sigma$ are found in the core averaged, background-subtracted spectrum." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores)
- Limitation (authors): DR1 includes only 12m-array data without 7m or TP short spacings, causing negative bowls and absorption features; Band 3 spw0 (N2H+) is excluded from DR1 due to extended emission artifacts.  
  > "For spectral window 0 in B3, which includes the N$_2$H$^+$ line, the 12 m only data still contain artefacts due to the extended emission and missing short spacings, we thus exclude this window from this DR1 release." (Data products); "We note that in a few regions (e.g. G327.29 and G351.77), the moment 0 maps display negative bowls, resulting from the missing short spacings in this 12m-array only data." (Global scale DCN (3-2) morphology)
- Limitation (authors): Divergent channels around the V_LSR of 12CO (2-1) and C18O (2-1) were masked and left uncleaned in the final cubes, and G351.77 was cleaned only to 10 sigma.  
  > "In these cases, we chose to mask the channels that showed divergence and re-run the `tclean`, leaving those channels uncleaned in the final cubes (the masked channel ranges can be obtained in the `imaging_parameters.py` file)." (Cleaning threshold); "In the end, for this source, we ran `tclean` only down to 10$\sigma$ level for all spectral windows." (Cleaning threshold)
- Limitation (authors): Overestimation of the continuum startmodel at bright hot cores with line forests causes localised negative residuals, and STATCONT continuum subtraction can be imperfect towards hot cores or bright outflows.  
  > "The `STATCONT` procedure employed simultaneously over the full spectral window can satisfactorily remove the continuum for most of the bandwidth and fields, however, towards, the positions of hot core candidates or bright outflows, the continuum subtraction could be improved." (Released line cubes and post-processed image products); "Towards several regions that contain bright hot cores, there can be a slight overestimation of the continuum startmodel at the positions of the hot cores, resulting in localised negative features in some of the residuals." (Known data issues)
- Limitation (authors): 91 detected cores with Complex-type DCN spectra (typically higher mass ~17 M_sun) and 238 non-detected cores (including lowest-mass cores < 1 M_sun) are excluded from the single-component kinematic analysis.  
  > "While the $V_{\rm LSR}$ from Complex-type spectral fits are provided for reference, the linewidths are not given, and these cores are excluded from the following analysis." (DCN (3-2) detection of cores); "This may, however, be biased by the fact that we do not have estimates for all of the cores." (Summary)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Excluding the 91 Complex-type cores (which have the highest average mass of ~17 M_sun) from the linewidth and velocity dispersion analysis introduces a strong selection bias against massive/optically thick cores.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core-to-core velocity dispersions change by up to a factor of 2 depending on whether sub-clusters or line-of-sight cloud components (e.g. in W51-IRS2, W51-E, G338.93) are separated.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Using 12m-only data without short spacings (7m+TP) and subtracting a local annulus background can artificially narrow linewidths and create spurious absorption features.
