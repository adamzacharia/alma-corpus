## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate and image continuum and line cubes**: CASA calibration and imaging; ACA plus 12 m combined; natural weighting at 3 mm, briggs robust 0.5 at 1.3 mm with phase-only self-calibration; highest-resolution 1.3 mm from archive product  
  > "The ACA data and 12 m-array data were combined and imaged using the same version of CASA with natural weighting" (ALMA 3 mm observations); "using the *briggs* weighting. The *robust* parameter is adopted as 0.5." (ALMA 1.3 mm observations)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Pixel size 0.4 arcsec (Band 3), 0.05 arcsec (Band 6)  
  > "The pixel size is 0.4$^{\prime\prime}$" (ALMA 3 mm observations); "with a pixel size of 0.05$^{\prime\prime}$ using the *briggs* weighting." (ALMA 1.3 mm observations)
   - parameter: Three rounds of phase-only self-calibration, shortest solution interval 12 s (Band 6)  
  > "We conducted three rounds of phase-only self-calibration with a shortest solution interval of 12 s." (ALMA 1.3 mm observations)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify and characterise spiral arms in 3 mm continuum and molecular peak-intensity maps**: Model arms as logarithmic spirals R = R0 exp(a theta), generate model images, convolve with the beam, and pick the best match by Pearson correlation with the observed map  
  > "we model the spiral arms as logarithmic spirals, described by $R(\theta)=R_0e^{a\theta}$" (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology); "By calculating the Pearson correlation coefficient between the model images and the 3 mm continuum map, we identified the spiral model that best matches the original continuum map" (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology)
   - software: polarTransform, Python
   - parameter: Starting radius R0 of each arm determined manually from a polar plot made with polarTransform  
  > "The starting radius $R_0$ of each spiral arm is manually determined from the projected polar plot." (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology); "The polar plots were made via Python package *polarTransform*." (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology)
   - parameter: Model images made with the Bresenham line algorithm and convolved with a 2D Gaussian of beam FWHM  
  > "convolved them with a 2-dimensional Gaussian kernel with the same full width at half maximum (FWHM) as the synthesized beam" (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology)
   - assumption: Pitch angle given by arctan|a|; the same a for S2 in H13CO+ assumes continuum and H13CO+ map the same gas  
  > "the pitch angle is given by $\arctan{|a|}$" (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology); "assuming that both map the same gas" (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology)
   - fitting: Best a chosen by maximum Pearson correlation between model and map  
  > "we identified the spiral model that best matches the original continuum map" (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology)
   - validation: Best-fit a robust against the choice of beam FWHM  
  > "The best fit of $a$ is robust against the choice of the FWHM." (Spiral-arm-like structures: Morphology)
3. **Test velocity coherence along arms and bar with PV diagrams**: PV cuts along the identified curves and bar axis; velocity gradients read from PV and moment 1 maps  
  > "The PV cuts are placed along the curves we identified in the previous section." (Spiral-arm-like structures: Gas Kinematics)
   - assumption: Velocity gradients are interpreted as infall toward the central object  
  > "The velocity gradients seen in the PV diagrams may indicate that the gas is infalling to the center of the clump driven by the gravitational force of the central object." (Spiral-arm-like structures: Gas Kinematics)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Model arm kinematics with an infalling-rotating particle trajectory**: Analytic streamline of a particle in a rotating cloud falling onto a central point mass, parameters adjusted by hand to match peak-intensity and velocity maps of S2 and S5  
  > "using an analytic solutions *(cite: 2009MNRAS.393..579M, 2020NatAs...4.1158P)* of a particle moving within a rotating cloud toward a central mass" (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory); "We manually adjust the model and streamline parameters to find the best match parameter set." (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory)
   - parameter: S2: initial distance 52,000 au, position angle 21 deg, polar angle 70 deg  
  > "using the initial distance of 52,000 au, the position angle of 21$^{\circ}$, and the polar angle of 70$^{\circ}$ for S2" (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory)
   - parameter: Angular velocity 4e-14 rad/s, initial radial velocity 0.6 km/s  
  > "The angular velocity is assumed as $4\times10^{-14}~{\rm rad~s^{-1}}$ and the initial radial velocity is 0.6 $\rm km~s^{-1}$." (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory)
   - parameter: Central mass 50-80 Msun  
  > "assuming a central mass of 50-80$\,M_{\odot}$ in S2 and S5" (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory)
   - assumption: Rotating-plane inclination between 0 and 20 deg (0 deg = face-on); 10 deg plane used for the polar angle  
  > "we assume the inclination angle to be between 0$^{\circ}$ to 20$^{\circ}$ (where 0$^{\circ}$ represents a face-on view)" (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory)
   - fitting: Manual adjustment of parameters, no formal fit  
  > "We manually adjust the model and streamline parameters to find the best match parameter set." (Infalling-rotating particle trajectory)
   - validation: Model reproduces kinematics along S2 and S5 but not the V-shaped profiles of S1 and S2  
  > "While this idealized kinematic model with a central mass of 50-80 $M_{\odot}$ successfully reproduces the gas kinematics along S2 and S5" (Spiral-arm discussion)
5. **Fit the pseudodisk geometry and central mass with an infalling-rotating envelope model**: Model CH3OH 4_2-3_1 E1 vt=0 emission as a flattened infalling and rotating envelope with FERIA; chi2 grid search over four free parameters  
  > "Here we model the pseudodisk as a flattened infalling and rotating envelope (IRE) with the publicly available code FERIA" (IRE modeling); "The central mass $M_{*,e}$, the centrifugal radius $r_{\rm CB}$, the outer radius of the IRE $r_{\rm out}$ and the inclination $i$ are treated as free parameters." (IRE modeling)
   - software: FERIA
   - parameter: Position angle fixed to the velocity gradient in the centroid velocity map  
  > "The position angle of the IRE model is fixed to the velocity gradient observed in the centroid velocity map." (IRE modeling)
   - parameter: Fine-grid ranges: M 20-70 Msun, i 0-20 deg, r_CB 0-200 au, r_out 800-1200 au  
  > "20–70" (Table S1 (parameter range)); "800–1200" (Table S1 (parameter range))
   - assumption: Model has only a central point mass; 45 Msun best fit considered too massive for an early protostar  
  > "The model only considers a central point mass, and the best-fit 45 $M_{\odot}$ is too massive for a protostar at a very early stage." (IRE modeling)
   - fitting: Coarse then fine chi2 grid search (not MCMC)  
  > "we employed a $\chi^2$ grid search to explore the parameter space in this study." (IRE modeling); "we began with a coarse $\chi^2$ grid search to constrain the parameter ranges, followed by a fine grid search." (IRE modeling)
   - validation: Chi2 shows a ridge-like degeneracy between central mass and inclination; model reproduces curvature in centroid velocity maps; CH3CN lines show similar geometry  
  > "Notably, the $\chi^2$ distribution in the $i$ – $M_{*,e}$ space reveals a ridge-like pattern, where central mass and inclination are correlated." (IRE modeling); "Similar geometry and kinematics of the pseudodisk are also well revealed by other dense gas tracers such as CH$_3$CN lines" (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
6. **Measure size and inclination of the compact disk from long-baseline continuum**: 2-dimensional Gaussian fit to the compact source in the 1.3 mm long-baseline image  
  > "On the basis of 2-dimensional Gaussian fitting to the disk, the size and inclination of the disk are $\sim$110 au and $\sim75^{\circ}$, respectively." (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fit (fitting tool not stated)  
  > "On the basis of 2-dimensional Gaussian fitting to the disk" (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
   - validation: Inclination compared with the pseudodisk and with the 12CO bipolar outflow  
  > "Additionally, the presence of $^{12}$CO bipolar outflow suggests the disk is not face-on." (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
7. **Fit Keplerian rotation to the 12 GHz methanol maser disk**: Project maser spots onto a PV cut and fit a Keplerian rotation curve with scaled enclosed mass M sin^2 i and system velocity free  
  > "we modeled the spatial-kinematics of the maser using a Keplerian rotation model" (Dynamics of the maser disk); "and the system velocity as free parameters" (Dynamics of the maser disk)
   - software: SciPy
   - parameter: Southeast and northwest blueshifted/redshifted clusters selected by hand; northeast cluster excluded as outflow-related  
  > "We manually selected two blueshifted and redshifted maser clusters in the southeast and northwest" (Dynamics of the maser disk)
   - parameter: PA of PV cut fixed to steepest velocity gradient in ALMA data; disk center at the long-baseline 1.3 mm continuum peak  
  > "The position angle of the position-velocity cut was fixed as the direction of the steepest velocity gradient in the ALMA observation." (Dynamics of the maser disk); "The disk center was assumed to be the peak of the long-baseline 1.3 mm continuum." (Dynamics of the maser disk)
   - assumption: Stellar mass 8-16 Msun from spectral type B3-B0 gives disk inclination 20-30 deg  
  > "Assuming this stellar mass range, the inclination of the maser disk would be $20^{\circ}$ to $30^{\circ}$." (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
   - fitting: Least-squares fit in SciPy  
  > "The scaled enclosed mass was estimated using the least-square fitting, implemented in SciPy" (Dynamics of the maser disk)
   - validation: Remaining masers follow Keplerian rotation; using the pseudodisk inclination would give an unphysical >180 Msun mass  
  > "the remaining 12 GHz Class II methanol masers follow Keplerian rotation very well" (Spirals-bar-disk discussion); "the mass of HMYSO $M_{*,d}$ would be over 180 $M_{\odot}$" (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
8. **Derive gas mass and mass inflow rate along the spiral arms**: Optically thin column density of H13CO+ and CCH, converted to mass with an abundance, then inflow rate = velocity gradient x mass / tan(inclination)  
  > "assuming optically thin conditions with *(cite: 2015PASP..127..266M)*" (Mass assembly rate at different scales); "we further calculate the inflow rate $\dot{M}_{||}$ via *(cite: 2013ApJ...766..115K)*" (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
   - parameter: Excitation temperature 20 K  
  > "the excitation temperature which is adopted as 20 K" (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
   - parameter: H13CO+ abundance 1.28e-10, CCH abundance 3.72e-8  
  > "We assume the abundances of H$^{13}$CO$^+$ and CCH to be 1.28$\times10^{-10}$ *(cite: 2013ApJ...777..157H)*" (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
   - parameter: Spectroscopic constants from CDMS  
  > "are taken from CDMS Database *(cite: 2001A&A...370L..49M)*" (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
   - assumption: Inclination 45 deg for all arms except S2 (30 deg) and S5 (60 deg)  
  > "We assume the inclination angle to all be 45$^{\circ}$ except for S2 and S5, which are 30$^{\circ}$ and 60$^{\circ}$, respectively" (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
   - assumption: Moment 1 velocity gradient is due to inflow  
  > "Assuming that the velocity gradient $\nabla{V}_{||}$, which is the velocity profile in the Moment 1 maps, is due to the inflow" (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with rates in similar high-mass regions from the literature; inclination sensitivity discussed (10 or 80 deg changes rate by factor 5.7)

## Qualitative findings

- Three dusty spiral arms (~20,000 au) feed a ~7,500 au bar, which channels gas to a ~2,000 au pseudodisk, then a Keplerian disk and inner disk driving a bipolar outflow  
  > "three $\sim$20,000 au spiral arms feed a $\sim$7,500 au central bar, which channels gas to a $\sim$2,000 au pseudodisk." (abstract)
- The compact disk is misaligned with the nearly face-on pseudodisk, and maser velocities are reversed relative to the pseudodisk velocity field  
  > "The combined evidence indicates that the disk and the pseudodisk are notably misaligned." (Spirals-bar-disk discussion); "The most remarkable feature is that the velocity distribution of the masers is opposite to the velocity field of the pseudodisk" (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
- Mass inflow rate stays ~1e-4 Msun/yr from spirals to bar but drops two orders of magnitude to the disk accretion rate  
  > "which is two orders of magnitude smaller than the mass inflow rate along the spiral arms and the streamers." (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: _not stated_
- Why this method: Chi2 grid search rather than MCMC because model simplicity dominates the uncertainty  
  > "instead of conducting a precise fitting method like MCMC, we employed a $\chi^2$ grid search to explore the parameter space in this study." (IRE modeling)
- Alternative: MCMC fitting of the IRE model was not used because model uncertainty could exceed observational noise  
  > "Note that the uncertainties arising from the simplicity of the model could dominate over the observational noise." (IRE modeling)
- Alternative: North-east maser cluster excluded from the Keplerian fit as likely outflow- or tentative-eastern-arm-related  
  > "or it is associated with the tentative eastern spiral arm" (Dynamics of the maser disk)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Accretion rate is a lower limit (optically thin 12CO assumed, missing short spacings) and not corrected for outflow inclination  
  > "Since we assume $^{12}$CO emission is optically thin, and due to the potential missing flux caused by the lack of short spacing of the interferometer, this accretion rate would be the lower limit of the exact accretion rate." (Spirals-bar-disk discussion); "As we cannot derive the inclination of the outflow, the accretion rate has not been corrected for the projection with a factor of $\cos{i}/\sin^2{i}$." (Mass assembly rate at different scales)
- Limitation (authors): Idealised particle-trajectory model fails to capture the whole system's dynamics  
  > "While this idealized kinematic model with a central mass of 50-80 $M_{\odot}$ successfully reproduces the gas kinematics along S2 and S5, it can not capture the entire system" (Spiral-arm discussion)
- Limitation (authors): IRE model is insensitive to the central mass  
  > "The model is insensitive to central mass but tightly constrains the inclination to $<15^\circ$." (IRE modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Theory for a counter-rotating massive disk is unclear  
  > "it is still theoretically unclear whether this scenario could happen in massive disks" (Spirals-bar-disk discussion)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Table S2 in the supplement and the Table in Methods give different outflow values (e.g. mass-loss rate 5.7e-7 and 3.6e-7 versus 4.85e-5 and 2.31e-5 Msun/yr); the text total of 9.3e-7 matches the supplement only
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Arm identification, R0, streamline parameters and maser cluster selection are manual, so results are not objectively reproducible
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Single source; the claimed analogy to barred spiral galaxies is morphological only and the arms are traced in different tracers with different resolutions
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Inflow rates depend strongly on assumed inclination (45 deg), Tex (20 K), abundances and the velocity-gradient interpretation, with no formal uncertainties
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Misalignment inference rests on a nearly face-on pseudodisk with a degenerate mass-inclination fit and on masers assumed to trace a Keplerian disk
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Missing flux from the interferometer is acknowledged only for the CO outflow but may also affect mass estimates from 3 mm continuum and lines
