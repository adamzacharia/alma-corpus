## Analysis recipe

1. **Calibrate, image, and extract radio and millimeter flux densities**: Calibrate and image VLA, MeerKAT, ATCA, ALMA, and SMA datasets using standard pipelines/tasks, and extract point-source flux densities  
  > "We obtained all flux densities and uncertainties using the imtool fitsrc command within the python-based pwkit package (cite: Williams2017)." (Radio Observations); "For the ALMA observations, we used the standard NRAO pipeline (version: 2021.2.0.128) in CASA (version: 6.2.1.7) to calibrate and image the data." (Millimeter Observations)
   - software: CASA, tclean, pwkit, MIRIAD, SARAO Science Data Processor (SDP), SMA COMPASS pipeline
   - assumption: Point source fit for VLA flux density extraction  
  > "We assumed a point source fit, as preferred by the data." (Radio Observations)
   - fitting: Point source fitting via pwkit imtool fitsrc for VLA data  
  > "We obtained all flux densities and uncertainties using the imtool fitsrc command within the python-based pwkit package (cite: Williams2017)." (Radio Observations)
   - validation: MeerKAT source fluxes confirmed against NVSS overlapping sources  
  > "We confirmed via the secondary SDP products that the source fluxes in the MeerKAT images were consistent with $\sim90\%$ of the sources overlapping with the NRAO VLA Sky Survey (cite: Condon1998)." (Radio Observations)
2. **Extract and fit Chandra X-ray spectrum**: Reprocess Chandra ACIS-S exposures, detect source with wavdetect, extract spectra with specextract, and simultaneously fit an absorbed power-law model in Xspec  
  > "We reprocessed the observations using the repro task within CIAO 4.15.2 with standard ACIS data filtering and using the latest calibration database (CALDB, v4.10.7)." (X-ray Observations); "we fit all the spectra simultaneously with an absorbed simple power-law model ($\texttt{tbabs*ztbabs*pow}$ within $\texttt{Xspec}$)." (X-ray Observations)
   - software: CIAO, CALDB, Xspec
   - parameter: 1.5'' source region radius and 30'' background region radius  
  > "We extracted a spectrum of the source with $\texttt{specextract}$ using a 1.5$''$ radius source region and a source-free background region of 30$''$ radius in all four observations." (X-ray Observations)
   - assumption: Galactic column density $N_{H,\rm MW}=2.67\times 10^{20}\,\rm{cm^{-2}}$ and intrinsic absorption $N_{H, \rm int} = 0\,\rm{cm^{-2}}$  
  > "Similar to (cite: p1), we find no evidence for additional intrinsic absorption and thus assume $N_{H, \rm int} = 0\,\rm{cm^{-2}}$." (X-ray Observations)
   - fitting: Simultaneous spectral fit with tbabs*ztbabs*pow in Xspec  
  > "we fit all the spectra simultaneously with an absorbed simple power-law model ($\texttt{tbabs*ztbabs*pow}$ within $\texttt{Xspec}$)." (X-ray Observations)
   - validation: Checked for variability across the four observations  
  > "Since there is no statistically significant evidence of variability across the four observations, we fit all the spectra simultaneously" (X-ray Observations)
3. **Fit multi-frequency radio/mm Spectral Energy Distributions (SEDs)**: Fit each epoch's SED with a weighted Granot & Sari (2002) synchrotron model accounting for both $\nu_a \ll \nu_m$ and $\nu_m \ll \nu_a$ regimes  
  > "we fit the spectral energy distributions (SEDs) with a weighted model that accounts for two possible origins for the peak frequency — $\nu_m$ (i.e., relativistic outflow) or $\nu_a$ (i.e., non-relativistic outflow) — using the models developed by (cite: Granot2002)" (Spectral Energy Distributions)
   - software: emcee
   - parameter: $s_1= 1.06$, $s_2= 1.76 + 0.05p$, $s_4 = 3.63p - 1.60$, weights $w_1 = (\nu_m/\nu_a)^2$ and $w_2 = (\nu_a/\nu_m)^2$  
  > "where $s_1= 1.06$ and $s_2= 1.76 + 0.05p$ are smoothing parameters that describe the shape of the spectrum across each break." (Spectral Energy Distributions); "where $w_1 = (\nu_m/\nu_a)^2$ and $w_2 = (\nu_a/\nu_m)^2$." (Spectral Energy Distributions)
   - fitting: MCMC sampling using emcee with 100 chains, 2000 steps, 1000 burn-in steps, Gaussian likelihood, uniform prior $p=1.5-3.5$, and fractional systematic error parameter $\sigma$  
  > "We determine the best fit parameters of the model — $F_\nu(\nu_m)$, $\nu_a$, $\nu_m$ and $p$ — using the Python Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) module emcee (cite: Foreman-Mackey2013), assuming a Gaussian likelihood" (Spectral Energy Distributions); "For $p$ we use a uniform prior of $p=1.5-3.5$." (Spectral Energy Distributions); "The posterior distributions are sampled using 100 MCMC chains, which were run for 2,000 steps, discarding the first 1,000 steps" (Spectral Energy Distributions)
   - validation: Examined sampler distribution for convergence  
  > "discarding the first 1,000 steps to ensure the samples have sufficiently converged by examining the sampler distribution." (Spectral Energy Distributions)
4. **Derive physical outflow parameters for spherical geometry via equipartition analysis**: Minimum energy equipartition analysis using SED peak parameters and cooling break constraint from combined radio+mm+Chandra SED  
  > "Using the inferred values of $\nu_p$, $F_{\nu,p}$ and $p$, we derive the physical properties of the outflow and ambient medium using a minimum energy analysis assuming a spherical geometry, following the method outlined in (cite: p1)" (Spherical Outflow Equipartition Analysis)
   - parameter: Fixed $\nu_p=2.9$ GHz (with 0.6 GHz RMS propagated into errors), $p=2.16$, $\epsilon_B = 10^{-3}$, launch time $t_0=622$ days  
  > "Given the steady value of $\nu_p$ (with variations dominated by epoch-to-epoch scatter), we fix $\nu_p=2.9$ GHz to focus on the long-term trends in the physical parameters" (Spherical Outflow Equipartition Analysis); "Values in this table are calculated using an outflow launch time of $t_0=622$ days, $\nu_p$= 2.9 GHz, and $\epsilon_b = 0.001$." (Table 2)
   - assumption: Spherical outflow geometry and $\epsilon_B \approx 10^{-3}$  
  > "With the value of $\nu_c$ determined, we find that $\epsilon_{B}\approx 10^{-3}$." (Spherical Outflow Equipartition Analysis)
   - fitting: Linear fit to radius evolution to infer outflow launch time $t_0$, and broadband SED fit with cooling break  
  > "fitting such an evolution with the launch time (i.e., time at which $R=0$) as a free parameter, we find $t_0\approx 620$ d" (Spherical Outflow)
   - validation: Checked cooling break $\nu_c\approx 250$ GHz against VLA+SMA observations at 2160 days  
  > "Fitting this model to the data we find $\nu_c\approx 250$ GHz at $\approx 1950$ days. This is consistent with our VLA+SMA observations at 2160 days, which do not exhibit a cooling break between the radio and mm bands." (Spherical Outflow Equipartition Analysis)
5. **Model off-axis relativistic jet scenarios (generalized equipartition and structured jet forward modeling)**: Apply generalized off-axis equipartition analysis of Matsumoto & Piran (2023) using $\nu_m$ constraint, and forward model afterglow emission from a steep angular structured jet  
  > "Below we consider two off-axis jet model approaches, using the analytic formalism of (cite: Matsumoto2023), which is an extension of the equipartition analysis in the spherical case from §4.2, and the numerical approach of (cite: Gill-Granot-18)" (Off-Axis Relativistic Jet)
   - parameter: Top-hat jet: $f_A=f_V=(\Gamma\theta_j)^2$, $\theta_j=0.1$, $\epsilon_e=0.1$, $\epsilon_B=10^{-3}$, $\eta=1$; Structured jet: $k=0$, $q\approx 12$, $a\approx b\approx 12$  
  > "We assume a narrow jet geometry with $f_{A}=f_{V}=(\Gamma\theta_{j})^2$ and $\theta_{j}=0.1\,(\approx5.7^{\circ})$ (see Appendix B in (cite: Matsumoto2023)), and take into account the deviation from equipartition with $\epsilon_{e}=0.1$ and $\epsilon_{B}=10^{-3}$." (Generalized Equipartition Analysis); "The key finding is that the model requires a large off-axis viewing angle, with $q\approx 12$, in good agreement with the simple top-hat calculation in §5.2.1. This, coupled with the steep gradient in the angular structure, with $a\approx b\approx 12$" (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling)
   - assumption: Thin-shell geometry, locally spherical dynamics without lateral spreading, constant density external medium ($k=0$), and ad-hoc $\epsilon_e\propto t^4$ at early times for the structured jet  
  > "Lateral spreading of the flow, which becomes important as the initially relativistic jet is sufficiently decelerated (cite: Rhoads-99; Sari+99; Granot-Piran-12), is not included in the model" (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling); "Our model includes an ad-hoc temporal evolution of $\epsilon_e\propto t^4$ in the first few hundred days of evolution" (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared predicted $\nu_m$ across viewing angles $\theta_{\rm obs}=70-90\,\rm deg$ against observed SEDs  
  > "We find that only viewing angles of $\theta_{\rm obs} \gtrsim 80^{\circ}$ have predicted values of $\nu_m$ in agreement with the observed value" (Generalized Equipartition Analysis)

## Qualitative findings

- Radio and millimeter light curves of AT2018hyz continue to rise across all observed frequencies from 972 to 2160 days, reaching a luminosity of ~10^40 erg s^-1.  
  > "We find that the light curves continue to rise at all frequencies during this time period, following a power law of about $F_\nu\propto t^3$ (compared to $F_\nu\propto t^{5.7}$ at $972-1400$ days), and reaching a peak luminosity of $L\approx 10^{40}$ erg s$^{-1}$" (Abstract)
- The SED peak frequency remains roughly constant (~2.9 GHz) over 1030 days while the peak flux density increases by an order of magnitude.  
  > "The multi-frequency data indicate that the peak frequency does not significantly evolve over the 1030-day span of our observations, while the peak flux density increases by an order of magnitude." (Abstract)
- Both a delayed (~620 days) mildly relativistic spherical outflow and a prompt, highly off-axis (80-90 deg) relativistic jet can explain the current observations.  
  > "The observed behavior is consistent with two possible scenarios: (i) a delayed spherical outflow launched about 620 days post-disruption with a velocity of $\approx 0.3c$ and an energy of $\sim10^{50}$ erg, and (ii) a highly off-axis ($\approx 80-90^\circ$) relativistic jet" (Abstract)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Millimeter (ALMA, SMA) and X-ray (Chandra) observations combined with multi-frequency radio data constrain the synchrotron cooling break (nu_c) and magnetic field equipartition fraction (epsilon_B), while continuous monitoring tracks the light curve and SED peak evolution.  
  > "Since $\nu_c$ has a strong dependence on the magnetic field strength, measuring its location directly from the data can help to determine the value of $\epsilon_B$ and whether the outflow deviates from equipartition." (Spherical Outflow Equipartition Analysis)
- Why this method: Comparing the spectral peak nu_p and characteristic frequency nu_m breaks the degeneracy between Lorentz factor Gamma and viewing angle theta_obs in generalized off-axis equipartition analysis, while forward modeling tests a structured jet self-consistently.  
  > "Therefore, a direct measurement of $\nu_{\rm m}$ gives an independent constraint on $\Gamma$ and breaks the degeneracy between $\Gamma$ and $\theta_{\rm obs}$ in the equipartition analysis." (Generalized Equipartition Analysis); "We next test the off-axis jet scenario with forward modeling of the emission, taking into account the jet hydrodynamics and the effect on the temporal and spectral evolution self-consistently." (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling)
- Alternative: SED model without a synchrotron cooling break is rejected because it over-predicts the Chandra X-ray measurement  
  > "We find that as in (cite: p1), a model that does not include a cooling break over-predicts the Chandra measurement." (Spherical Outflow Equipartition Analysis)
- Alternative: Off-axis jet viewing angles of <=70 deg are rejected because they predict nu_m >= 10 GHz, in conflict with the observed SEDs  
  > "We find that only viewing angles of $\theta_{\rm obs} \gtrsim 80^{\circ}$ have predicted values of $\nu_m$ in agreement with the observed value; smaller off-axis angles of even $\approx 70^\circ$ predict $nu_m\gtrsim 10$ GHz, in conflict with the observed SEDs." (Generalized Equipartition Analysis)
- Alternative: Fixed epsilon_e in the structured jet forward model is rejected because it yields high-energy spectra that are too soft above the spectral peak  
  > "A fixed $\epsilon_e$ yields much softer high-energy model spectra above the spectral peak (at $\nu\approx2$\,GHz) that disagree with observations." (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling)
- Alternative: Delayed radio emission caused by the outflow encountering a sudden high-density circumnuclear medium is disfavored because the inferred density is not unusually high  
  > "Crucially, we do not infer an unusually high density, which might be expected if the radio emission was delayed due to rapid shift from low to high density." (Circumnuclear Density)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Cannot currently distinguish between the delayed spherical outflow and prompt highly off-axis jet scenarios until the light curve peak is observed or VLBI imaging is obtained  
  > "the true origin of the outflow will not be clear until we see the end of the rise in the radio light curve." (Introduction); "Continued radio observations to capture the light curve peak, as well as VLBI observations, could distinguish between these scenarios." (Abstract)
- Limitation (authors): Lateral spreading of the jet is not included in the structured jet forward model  
  > "Lateral spreading of the flow, which becomes important as the initially relativistic jet is sufficiently decelerated (cite: Rhoads-99; Sari+99; Granot-Piran-12), is not included in the model (cite: Kumar-Granot-03; vanEerten-MacFadyen-12; Duffell-Laskar-18)." (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling)
- Limitation (authors): The structured jet forward model requires an ad-hoc temporal evolution of epsilon_e proportional to t^4 at early times  
  > "Our model includes an ad-hoc temporal evolution of $\epsilon_e\propto t^4$ in the first few hundred days of evolution" (Off-Axis Jet with Steep Angular Structure – Forward modeling)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): There is a slight discrepancy in the stated epoch of the second ALMA observation between the text (1441 days) and Table 3 (2022 Sept 24, listed as 1421 days, whereas 2022 Sept 24 is 1441 days after 2018 Oct 14).
- Caveat (inferred, ours): In Table 3, the 2022 July 12 ALMA row lists both 97.5 GHz and 240 GHz measurements, whereas the text in Section 2.2 states 'the first epoch was in band 3 only'.
