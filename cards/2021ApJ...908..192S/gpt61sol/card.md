## Analysis recipe

1. **Create continuum images**: Inspect archive images/cubes, search detected sources for lines, and interactively image continuum at four beam levels.  
  > "Before the formal reduction work, we first checked the combined continuum image and spectral cube of each target, delivered by the ALMA archive." (ALMA); "We performed continuum imaging at four different levels of synthesized beam size: Briggs weighting (ROBUST=0.5), natural weighting (ROBUST=2), 1$^{\prime\prime}$ *uv*-tapering and 2$^{\prime\prime}$ *uv*-tapering." (ALMA); "Interactive cleaning was performed during each imaging process." (ALMA)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Inspect spectra for obvious sources above 4 sigma  
  > "If any obvious source was detected above a 4$\sigma$ significance, we would examine the spectral cubes, searching for possible spectral line features" (ALMA)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Detect sources and measure ALMA fluxes**: SExtractor Kron-like aperture photometry in uncorrected tapered maps; derive aperture errors and then primary-beam-correct fluxes and errors.  
  > "We ran SEXTRACTOR for source detection and automatic photometry with Kron-like elliptical apertures (PHOT_AUTOPARAMS values of 1.8 and 2.5) in the primary-beam-uncorrected maps where the primary beam response is greater than 0.2." (ALMA detection and photometry); "We then corrected the flux densities and their errors for the gain of primary beam." (ALMA detection and photometry)
   - software: SEXTRACTOR
   - parameter: 1 arcsec-tapered maps; S/N > 4.0; HLS0840 additionally selected in the 2 arcsec-tapered map  
  > "We therefore detected 28 sources at S/N$>$4.0 based on the 1$^{\prime\prime}$-tapered images." (ALMA detection and photometry); "We also included another S/N$>$4.0 detection in 2$^{\prime\prime}$-tapered images, namely HLS0840." (ALMA detection and photometry)
   - parameter: Aperture errors from aperture-to-beam area ratio and continuum RMS  
  > "We estimated the photometric errors ($\sigma_\mathrm{aper}$) based on aperture-to-beam size ratio ($\Omega_\mathrm{aper}/\Omega_\mathrm{beam}$) and continuum RMS ($\sigma_\mathrm{RMS}$)" (ALMA detection and photometry)
   - assumption: Gaussian-blurred Gaussian white noise for aperture-error and detection simulations  
  > "which is derived from a simulation of applying random apertures (enclosed area as $\Omega_\mathrm{aper}$) on Gaussian-blurred (kernel area as $\Omega_\mathrm{beam}$) Gaussian white noise maps." (ALMA detection and photometry)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Random source-free apertures validate errors; mock noise maps estimate false detections; injected point sources estimate completeness.  
  > "We also evaluated the photometric uncertainty by directly applying random apertures on source-free regions in ALMA primary-beam-uncorrected maps." (ALMA detection and photometry); "We then run SEXTRACTOR on the mock noise maps with the same settings used for the scientific maps, and treated all the extracted sources as false detections." (False detection rate and Completeness of ALMA Detections); "We generate 100 sources with a given flux density on a Gaussian white noise map, comparable to the combined area of 20 single-pointing FoVs." (False detection rate and Completeness of ALMA Detections)
3. **Measure ancillary photometry**: PACS aperture photometry; GALFIT SPIRE PSF/Gaussian fitting using ALMA positions; HST aperture photometry.  
  > "*Herschel*/PACS 100 and 160 m flux densities were measured with an aperture radius of 5$^{\prime\prime}$, and the uncertainty was inferred from the variance of sky background." (Herschel); "PSF photometry was conducted using GALFIT (cite: galfit)." (Herschel); "Aperture photometry of SMGs in *HST* images is conducted with SEXTRACTOR." (Other ancillary data)
   - software: GALFIT, SEXTRACTOR
   - parameter: SPIRE point sources except 2D Gaussians for HLS0840/HLS0546; ALMA positional priors; fixed relative positions for multiples  
  > "We assumed point-source models for the majority of ALMA-detected SMGs, except for two extended sources ($R_\mathrm{e}>1.6\ensuremath{''}$), HLS0840 and HLS0546, which were fit with a 2D Gaussian model." (Herschel); "We used ALMA source coordinates as prior source positions in the SPIRE maps." (Herschel); "If multiple sources exist in a field, the relative positions of their models are fixed." (Herschel)
   - fitting: Float SPIRE position for single sources; add nearby PACS/WISE components where necessary  
  > "For single-source cases, we floated the source position in the SPIRE images due to the coarse spatial sampling." (Herschel); "we also included other PACS (A2813, A3088) or *WISE*-detected (RXCJ2155) sources to optimize the fitting through multi-component decomposition." (Herschel)
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Measure IRAC light profiles and deblend fluxes**: GALFIT Sérsic/PSF modeling; aperture and triple-Sérsic treatment for HLS1314; ALMA-template MCMC neighborhood subtraction for five complex blended sources.  
  > "We use GALFIT to model the source brightness and morphology of 24 cluster-lensed SMGs." (Spitzer/IRAC); "Since three clumps were seen in HLS1314 in the 1$^{\prime\prime}$-tapered ALMA map, we also modeled its morphology with triple Sérsic profiles at the positions of the ALMA clumps." (Spitzer/IRAC); "To perform reliable IRAC photometry on these five sources, we adopted their ALMA continuum images as their morphological models in the IRAC bands." (Spitzer/IRAC)
   - software: GALFIT, emcee
   - parameter: Sérsic n = 0.2–4.0; otherwise fixed to 0.2, 0.5, 1.0 or 1.5 according to goodness of fit  
  > "We adopted a Sérsic model for the majority of SMGs, and a range of Sérsic index ($n$) between 0.2 and 4.0 was allowed." (Spitzer/IRAC); "If the best-fit $n$ is beyond this range, we fixed it at 0.2, 0.5 (as Gaussian), 1.0 (as exponential) or 1.5, depending on the goodness of fit." (Spitzer/IRAC)
   - parameter: Native Briggs continuum clipped at 3 sigma and convolved with IRAC PSFs  
  > "We clipped their Briggs-weighted, native Band-6 continua at $3\sigma$ and then convolved them with the corresponding PSFs as the source models." (Spitzer/IRAC)
   - assumption: ALMA morphology represents the five complex sources in IRAC; nearby optical sources are 2D Gaussians  
  > "we adopted their ALMA continuum images as their morphological models in the IRAC bands." (Spitzer/IRAC); "Based on the $i$-band optical images from the Pan-STARRS DR1 and DES DR1 (cite: ps1dr1; desdr1), we set up 2D Gaussian models for their nearby sources" (Spitzer/IRAC)
   - fitting: MCMC fits ALMA-source brightnesses, optical-source radii/brightnesses and sky background  
  > "We then used an MCMC routine (cite: emcee) to fit in the IRAC images the brightnesses of the ALMA sources, the effective radii and brightnesses of the optical sources, and the sky background." (Spitzer/IRAC)
   - validation: Gaussian versus Sérsic comparison; summed HLS1314 clumps recover total IRAC magnitudes  
  > "Gaussian fits do not show significant deviations in magnitude or source size from Sérsic fits" (Spitzer/IRAC); "When the photometric measurements of the three clumps were combined, the total magnitudes derived in the IRAC images were recovered." (Spitzer/IRAC)
5. **Fit dust-continuum sizes**: Fit Gaussian brightness models directly to ALMA visibilities; single-source UVMODELFIT or multiple-source UVMULTIFIT.  
  > "modeling their visibility data using UVMODELFIT (for single-source case) and UVMULTIFIT (for multiple-source case; (cite: uvmultifit)), both under CASA environment." (Quantitative morphological modeling); "We assumed Gaussian models for the surface brightness profile of all sources" (Quantitative morphological modeling)
   - software: CASA, UVMODELFIT, UVMULTIFIT, SEXTRACTOR
   - parameter: Initial positions, flux densities and shapes from SExtractor; 26 morphological components  
  > "We used ALMA source positions, flux densities and morphological parameters, obtained through SEXTRACTOR photometry, as the initial guess of source models in the *uv*-plane." (Quantitative morphological modeling); "As already described, we continue to decompose HLS1314 as three sources, so 26 sources are studied in this subsection." (Quantitative morphological modeling)
   - parameter: HLS0612 single source; HLS1314 three sources  
  > "UVMULTIFIT favored a single-source model. Therefore, we discarded the three-source fitting results." (Quantitative morphological modeling); "the goodness of a three-source fit with HLS1314 was better than that of a single-source one, so we continued to decompose it into HLS1314-A, B and C" (Quantitative morphological modeling)
   - assumption: Gaussian-profile radius bias smaller than median measurement uncertainty  
  > "Since the $R_\mathrm{e}$ modeled by a Gaussian profile is smaller than exponential one by only $\sim0.045$ dex in (cite: hodge16), less than the median uncertainty of $R_\mathrm{e}$ in this work ($\sim0.063$ dex)" (Quantitative morphological modeling)
   - fitting: Gaussian visibility fitting; compare alternative profiles and component counts by goodness of fit  
  > "There is no clear difference between the $\chi^2$ of the best-fit circular Gaussian and exponential models for HLS0546 and HLS0553-C, and the Gaussian profile fits HLS0612 better." (Quantitative morphological modeling)
   - validation: 10–15 simulated visibility sets per low-S/N/extended source; recovered median sizes within 1 sigma; no conspicuous flux boosting  
  > "we simulated the visibility data for these sources 10–15 times per each with CASA, and the RMS noise of mock data was controlled to match with that of our observations." (ALMA detection and photometry); "The median $R_\mathrm{e}$ measured from the 10–15 sets of simulated visibility data for each source is consistent with that of the input model at the $1\sigma$ confidence level" (Quantitative morphological modeling); "Based on our simulations, no conspicuous flux boosting effect was identified." (ALMA detection and photometry)
6. **Fit SEDs and photometric redshifts**: High-z MAGPHYS; photo-z extension when spectroscopic redshift is unknown; generally six bands from IRAC to ALMA plus ancillary photometry.  
  > "We perform SED modeling with the high-$z$ extension of MAGPHYS (cite: magphys; dacunha15)." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "We also use the photo-$z$ extension of MAGPHYS (cite: battisti19) to estimate the photometric redshift ($z_\mathrm{phot}$) and physical properties simultaneously when the spectroscopic redshift ($z_\mathrm{spec}$) is unknown." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "For the majority of our sample, their SEDs are modeled using six-band photometry from 3.6 m to 1.3 mm." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - software: MAGPHYS
   - parameter: No lensing correction to input photometry  
  > "We adopt our observed photometric measurements to feed the SED fitting routine without applying any lensing magnification correction." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - parameter: HLS1623: IRAC and ALMA only for MAGPHYS  
  > "we only use its IRAC and ALMA flux densities for MAGPHYS SED fitting." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - assumption: Chabrier IMF; delayed exponential SFH; two-component dust absorption; UV/IR energy balance  
  > "MAGPHYS assumes a Chabrier IMF (cite: chabrier03), a continuous delayed exponential star-formation history (SFH), a two-component dust absorption law (cite: cf00) and energy balance between dust absorption in the UV and re-emission in the infrared." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - assumption: No differential magnification  
  > "We also assume that there is no differential magnification effect" (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - assumption: Flat LambdaCDM; h=0.7; Omega_m=0.3  
  > "Throughout this paper, we assume a flat $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with $h=0.7$ and $\Omega_m = 0.3$." (Introduction)
   - fitting: Photometric redshift is the median likelihood-distribution value; uncertainty from that distribution  
  > "Here the $z_\mathrm{phot}$ is the median of likelihood distribution of redshift for each source." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "We assess the uncertainty of photometric redshift estimate based on the likelihood distribution obtained with MAGPHYS." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - validation: Independent FIR redshifts from Rieke LIRG templates agree within approximately 1 sigma  
  > "We also evaluate the far-IR $z_\mathrm{phot}$ by matching with the LIRG templates in (cite: rieke09), and the derived redshifts are consistent with those by MAGPHYS within $\sim 1\sigma$ confidence interval." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
7. **Fit dust temperatures**: Modified-blackbody fits to FIR continuum over rest-frame 50; propagate photometric-redshift uncertainty  
  > "We also study the dust temperature with far-IR data over a rest-frame wavelength of 50 m, following (cite: greve12)." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "We fit the dust continua of all SMGs with modified black-body (MBB)." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "We also incorporate the uncertainty of redshift in that of the dust temperature if the $z_\mathrm{spec}$ is unknown." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - parameter: HLS1623: SPIRE-only temperature fit  
  > "Here, we use its SPIRE-only SED to assess the dust temperature" (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - assumption: kappa = 0.040 × (nu/250 GHz)^beta m² kg−1; rest-frame frequency; beta=1.8  
  > "The dust absorption coefficient is assumed to be $\kappa = 0.040\times (\nu/250\,\mathrm{GHz})^\beta$ in the unit of m^2^   kg^−1^, where $\nu$ is the frequency in GHz in the rest-frame." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "We assume a fixed dust emissivity of $\beta=1.8$" (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - fitting: Modified-blackbody fitting  
  > "We fit the dust continua of all SMGs with modified black-body (MBB)." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
   - validation: MBB dust masses agree with MAGPHYS with 0.08 dex dispersion  
  > "The dust masses derived from this MBB fitting are consistent with those from MAGPHYS, listed in Table [1](#tab:04_prop), with a $1\sigma$ dispersion of 0.08 dex." (SED fitting and photometric redshift)
8. **Fit FIR color relations**: Compare f250/f500 with f350/f1.3mm and fit color versus redshift  
  > "We first compare the FIR colors, namely $f_{250}/f_{500}$ and $f_{350}/f_{1.3\si{mm}}$, of all galaxies detected in our survey" (Multi-wavelength color and redshift); "We then compare $f_{350}/f_{1.3\si{mm}}$ versus source redshifts" (Multi-wavelength color and redshift)
   - software: emcee
   - fitting: MCMC power-law color–color relation and exponential color–redshift relation  
  > "An MCMC fitting through EMCEE suggests the power-law relation" (Multi-wavelength color and redshift); "Similar MCMC routine implies an underlying relation as $\log(f_{350}/f_{1.3\si{mm}}) = -(0.376  \pm 0.044) \times z + (2.102 \pm 0.095)$" (Multi-wavelength color and redshift)
   - validation: Compare to the unlensed Ikarashi sample after a 0.53 flux conversion  
  > "This is consistent with the unlensed SMG sample presented in (cite: ikarashi15), for which we apply a conversion from 1.1 mm flux densities to 1.3 mm by a factor of 0.53." (Multi-wavelength color and redshift)
9. **Derive mass ratios and gas timescales**: Compare dust-to-stellar mass ratio with sSFR; K-S comparison to AS2UDS; derive gas fractions and depletion times.  
  > "Figure [7](#fig:05_dsr) plots the dust-to-stellar mass ratio versus sSFR in all 29 SMGs, color-coded with dust temperature." (Dust to stellar mass ratio); "We perform a Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K-S) test of $\log(M_\mathrm{dust}/M_\ast)$ between our sample and AS2UDS SMGs in (cite: dudzevic19)" (Dust to stellar mass ratio); "Assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100, we calculate the gas depletion time scale ($\tau_\mathrm{dep} = M_\mathrm{gas}/\mathrm{SFR}$) for our sample" (Dust to stellar mass ratio)
   - assumption: Gas-to-dust ratio 100 for depletion times; Scoville rest-frame 850 luminosity conversion for low-dust-ratio gas fractions  
  > "Assuming a gas-to-dust ratio of 100" (Dust to stellar mass ratio); "Applying a (cite: scoville16) conversion from luminosity at a rest frame of 850 m ($L_{\nu,850}$) to the molecular gas mass ($M_\mathrm{gas}$)" (Dust to stellar mass ratio)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compare depletion times to AS2UDS recomputed with a similar method  
  > "This is consistent with the median value of AS2UDS sample ($\sim 320$ Myr) recomputed with similar method (cite: dudzevic19)." (Dust to stellar mass ratio)
10. **Construct size ratios and surface densities**: Use ALMA half-light radii for dust, geometric-mean IRAC radii for stars, and luminosity/mass divided by 2 pi Re² for surface densities.  
  > "We adopt the half-light radii measured at 1.3 mm ALMA *uv*-plane ($R_\mathrm{e,ALMA}$) as the effective radius of dust continuum (noted as $R_\mathrm{e,dust}$) and thus the star-forming region." (Dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio); "With the IRAC data, we define the effective radius as the geometric mean of the effective radii measured at 3.6/4.5 m" (Dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio); "We hereby define the IR surface luminosity as $\Sigma_\mathrm{IR} = L_\mathrm{IR}/(2\pi R_\mathrm{e,ALMA}^2)$" (IR surface luminosity and dust temperature)
   - parameter: Sigma_star = M_star/(2 pi Re,star²)  
  > "namely $\Sigma_\mathrm{star} = M_\ast/(2\pi R_\mathrm{e,star}^2)$" (Stellar surface density)
   - assumption: IRAC radii represent stellar mass; ALMA radii trace star formation; radial dust temperature/opacity gradients ignored  
  > "Therefore, we directly adopt the $R_\mathrm{e,IRAC}$ as a representation of the effective radius of stellar mass" (Dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio); "We note that this ignores any radial gradients of dust temperature or opacity that could alter the measured size of dust continuum." ## Qualitative findings

- Most dust continua are more compact than stellar continua; HLS0546 and HLS0840 are extended low-surface-brightness exceptions.  
  > "Except for three sources (HLS1623, HLS0546 and HLS0840), 23 out of the 26 sources have more compact ALMA dust continua relative to their stellar components." (Dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio); "Two sources (HLS0840 and HLS0546) are discovered as spatially extended main-sequence SMGs with low surface brightness" (Summary)
- Dust-to-stellar size ratio decreases with increasing IR surface luminosity.  
  > "We find that with the increase of $\Sigma_\mathrm{IR}$, the dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio decreases slowly." (Dust-to-stellar size ratio versus IR surface luminosity)
- Main-sequence SMGs include both central-starburst and galaxy-wide-star-formation morphologies.  
  > "some of them are dominated by central starburst while others are dominated by galaxy-wide star formation" (MS offset versus dust-to-stellar size ratio)
- Observed structural distributions are consistent with compaction and potentially subsequent inside-out quenching.  
  > "our observations are consistent with the theoretical evolutionary track of galaxies in a gas compaction and potentially subsequent “inside-out” quenching process" (Comparison with theoretical evolutionary tracks)
- Centroid-offset data do not rule out co-centered stellar and dust emission.  
  > "we cannot rule out the co-centered distributing scenario between the ongoing star formation and the evolved stellar population" (Spatial offset between ALMA and IRAC counterparts)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Longer wavelengths reduce stellar-extinction bias; cluster lensing brightens and stretches sources while often avoiding foreground blending.  
  > "In order to avoid the heavy dust obscuration of stellar continuum in SMGs, it is also better to sample the rest-frame near-infrared (NIR) bands rather than the optical ones" (Introduction); "these targets are sufficiently bright at multiple wavelengths, and their angular sizes are also stretched significantly" (Introduction); "cluster-lensed ones are often free from blending with foreground galaxies, ensuring simplicity of the morphological modeling on their stellar component." (Introduction)
- Why this method: Tapering improves extended-source detectability; emphasize lensing-independent quantities because detailed lens models are incomplete.  
  > "Since *uv*-tapering can increase the detectability of extended structures, though at the expense of resolution, we performed the source extraction with the 1$^{\prime\prime}$-tapered image products." (ALMA detection and photometry); "Since accurate lens models are not yet available for many of the observed SMGs, we focus our discussion on quantities that are independent of lensing effects, such as surface brightness and size ratios." (Summary)
- Alternative: Gaussian IRAC fits checked against Sérsic fits; no significant magnitude/size deviations.  
  > "We also applied a 2D Gaussian model for all the SMGs." (Spitzer/IRAC); "Gaussian fits do not show significant deviations in magnitude or source size from Sérsic fits" (Spitzer/IRAC)
- Alternative: PSF models replace divergent Gaussian/Sérsic fits for HLS0043-B and HLS0307-B.  
  > "HLS0043-B and HLS0307-B were fit with PSF models, since their faintness ($\sim$ 21 mag) and heavy blending with bright foreground sources ($\sim$ 17 mag) led to a divergence in the Gaussian/Sérsic modeling." (Spitzer/IRAC)
- Alternative: Exponential ALMA profiles compared with Gaussian profiles.  
  > "There is no clear difference between the $\chi^2$ of the best-fit circular Gaussian and exponential models for HLS0546 and HLS0553-C, and the Gaussian profile fits HLS0612 better." (Quantitative morphological modeling)
- Alternative: HLS0612 three-source fit discarded; HLS1314 three-source fit retained.  
  > "UVMULTIFIT favored a single-source model. Therefore, we discarded the three-source fitting results." (Quantitative morphological modeling); "the goodness of a three-source fit with HLS1314 was better than that of a single-source one" (Quantitative morphological modeling)
- Alternative: Shared-variable mock relation gives slope −0.46, inconsistent with the fitted relation at >5 sigma.  
  > "the resultant slope is $-0.46$." (Dust-to-stellar size ratio versus IR surface luminosity); "our fittings suggest that such a slope can be ruled out at a confidence of $>5\sigma$" (Dust-to-stellar size ratio versus IR surface luminosity)
- Alternative: Simple two-component model fails to reproduce compact main-sequence SMGs in the main-sequence plots.  
  > "it fails to reproduce the main-sequence SMGs dominated by central starburst." (MS offset versus dust-to-stellar size ratio)
- Alternative: CODEX 52909 excluded from further analysis because candidate sources are only approximately 3 sigma.  
  > "To avoid any confusion of $\sim3\sigma$ fake sources in this mosaic ALMA FoV, we did not analyze this field any further." (ALMA detection and photometry)
- How they decided it was enough: S/N=4 selected to keep expected false detections ≲1; simulated fluxes and sizes check low-S/N and extended-source reliability.  
  > "we applied an S/N cut of our detections at S/N=4. This ensures the total false detection number to be $\lesssim1$ in all of our ALMA maps." (False detection rate and Completeness of ALMA Detections); "The median $R_\mathrm{e}$ measured from the 10–15 sets of simulated visibility data for each source is consistent with that of the input model at the $1\sigma$ confidence level" (Quantitative morphological modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Incomplete lens models prevent accurate intrinsic masses and luminosities.  
  > "This is due to a lack of detailed lens models for several of the clusters in this study." (SED fitting and photometric redshift); "Without appropriate lensing correction, we cannot accurately determine the intrinsic stellar mass of our lensed SMG sample." (Star formation in lensed SMGs)
- Limitation (authors): HLS0840 radius exceeds its maximum recoverable angular scale and has a large uncertainty.  
  > "All of our measured effective radii at 1.3 mm are smaller than their corresponding maximum recoverable angular scales for given ALMA antenna configurations, except for HLS0840" (Quantitative morphological modeling); "As a result, the Re measurement of HLS0840 has a large error bar (1.7$''$)." (Quantitative morphological modeling)
- Limitation (authors): Missing optical/NIR constraints can overestimate extinction and stellar mass.  
  > "Due to the lack of deep rest-frame UV and optical data, it is not clear whether the determination of such high $A_V$ values is reliable." (Multi-wavelength color and redshift); "the lack of NIR data may lead to an overestimate of dust extinction and thus stellar mass through an energy-balance approach in the SED fitting" (The connection between SMGs and cQs)
- Limitation (authors): Fully obscured compact stellar components may be absent from IRAC light profiles.  
  > "it is possible that a compact and obscured stellar component remains undetected at the ALMA/IRAC continuum centroid" (Dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio)
- Limitation (authors): Radial dust temperature/optical-depth gradients can bias dust radii and underestimate Sigma_IR.  
  > "We note that this ignores any radial gradients of dust temperature or opacity that could alter the measured size of dust continuum." (Dust-to-stellar continuum size ratio); "the $\Sigma_\mathrm{IR}$ might be underestimated." (IR surface luminosity and dust temperature)
- Limitation (authors): Only two independent sources probe Sigma_IR > 10^12 L kpc−2.  
  > "only two independent sources in our sample (HLS0553 and HLS2332) are at $\Sigma_\mathrm{IR}> 10^{12}$ L  kpc^−2^." (IR surface luminosity and dust temperature)
- Limitation (authors): The simulation comparison is tentative because observed SMGs have higher stellar masses.  
  > "This result is tentative because the SMG sample is biased to a higher stellar mass" (Comparison with theoretical evolutionary tracks)
- Limitation (authors): True SFHs require additional J/H photometry and spectroscopy.  
  > "the true SFH can only be determined accurately with more observational data." (Further test of the evolutionary sequence)
- Limitation (authors): Merger incidence is a lower limit and depends on FIR-bright companions with comparable magnification.  
  > "we do not have a uniform survey of companions due to the cluster lensing effect, and therefore our measurement should be a lower limit." (Trigger mechanism of SMGs); "the need for companions to be FIR-bright and to lie close enough in the source plane to suffer comparable amplification, which may weaken these conclusions." (Trigger mechanism of SMGs)
- Limitation (authors): Extended-source completeness may be below 50% at 1.4 mJy.  
  > "our completeness can be lower than 50% at 1.4 mJy for these extended sources." (False detection rate and Completeness of ALMA Detections)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The 26 fitted components include multiple images and HLS1314 clumps; independence and repeated-source weighting need explicit treatment in correlation errors.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Both axes of the main structural correlation contain the fitted dust radius; reproducing the stated mock-data test is essential.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): The largest dust source is also the one exceeding its recoverable angular scale.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): A cross-sectional comparison with stacked evolutionary tracks does not establish individual galaxies' histories.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): No differential magnification is assumed although size ratios and stellar/dust tracers differ spatially.
