## Analysis recipe

1. **Convolve cubes, generate moment-0 maps, and partition into hexagonal regions**: Convolve all ALMA image cubes to a common 0.8'' beam, integrate over velocity windows to make moment-0 maps, and partition into 56 pc hexagonal regions keeping detections with S/N >= 3 and regions with >5 detected transitions.  
  > "We first convolved all image cubes to 0.$^{\ensuremath{''}}8$, which is the largest beam size among the datasets." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps); "We used the HExagonal Region Averager (HERA) code (cite: erica_behrens_2024_13839853) to partition the integrated intensity maps into adjoining hexagonal regions, each with a characteristic size of 56 pc, equivalent to the $\sim0.8\ensuremath{''}$ beam size." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps); "We therefore analyze only hexagonal regions with more than five detected transitions (see Section 3.4)." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps)
   - software: CASA, HERA
   - parameter: Common beam = 0.8''; Hexagon size = 56 pc; S/N threshold >= 3; > 5 detected transitions per hexagon (198 regions total)  
  > "The code retains only those regions that satisfy a specified signal-to-noise ratio threshold, in our case S/N $\geq$ 3." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps); "We identify 198 regions where more than five molecular transitions are detected above the three-sigma level, ensuring that the number of observational constraints exceeds the number of free parameters in the Bayesian inference." (Posterior Results and Model Preference)
   - parameter: Velocity integration windows: |v - v_sys| <= 230 km s^-1 for most transitions; v - v_sys in [-510, 230] km s^-1 for C2H (1-0)  
  > "For most transitions, we integrated fluxes within $|v-v_{sys}| \leq 230\ \mathrm{km\ s}^{-1}$ to encompass these motions (cite: Garcia2014)." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps); "Since these components appear blended at our velocity resolution, we employed a broader integration window of $v-v_{sys} \in [-510, 230]\ \mathrm{km\ s}^{-1}$ to include the complete C$_{2}$H (1−0) transition (cite: Garcia2017)." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps)
   - assumption: Distance = 14 Mpc (1'' ~ 70 pc); v_sys(HEL) ~= 1130 km/s  
  > "Located at a distance of 14 Mpc ((cite: Bland1997); 1$''$ $\sim$ 70 pc), its proximity enables sufficient spatial resolution to distinguish between star formation and AGN-driven processes." (Introduction); "The rest frequencies were determined using the systemic velocity from (cite: Garcia2019), with $v_{sys}(HEL) \simeq$ 1130 km/s." (ALMA data)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Moment-0 noise calculated from line-free channel noise: sigma_mom0 = sigma_chan * sqrt(N_chan) * Delta V  
  > "The noise on the moment 0 map, $\sigma_{\mathrm{mom}0}$ (in Jy beam$^{-1}$ km s$^{-1}$), is calculated using the noise in one line-free channel, $\sigma_{\mathrm{chan}}$ (in Jy beam$^{-1}$), the number of channels included for each pixel in the moment 0 map, $N_{\mathrm{chan}}$, and the velocity resolution, $\Delta V$ (in km s$^{-1}$):$\sigma_{\mathrm{mom}0} = \sigma_{\mathrm{chan}} \sqrt{N_{\mathrm{chan}}}\Delta V$." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps)
2. **Generate chemical model grid and train neural network emulator**: Run two-stage UCLCHEM models (both quiescent and heating scenarios) across a 4D parameter grid, filter out abundances < 10^-15, normalize to (-1, 1), and train a 4-hidden-layer TensorFlow neural network to predict fractional molecular abundances.  
  > "In this work, we employ two types of chemical models: a quiescent cloud model and a heating model." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow); "We constructed a neural network using the TensorFlow package to emulate the chemical modeling calculations." (Neural Network Architecture); "To keep only physically meaningful results in our training set, we set a lower limit of $10^{-15}$ for fractional abundances and removed any outputs below this value." (Training Data Set)
   - software: UCLCHEM, TensorFlow
   - parameter: 4 inputs: T_K in [30, 300] K (26 log points), n_H2 in [10^3, 10^7] cm^-3 (23 log points), zeta in [1, 10^3] zeta_0 (31 log points), N_H2 in [10^22, 10^25] cm^-2 (26 log points); 481,988 unique combinations  
  > "This sampling scheme yielded 481,988 unique parameter combinations for our chemical modeling." (Training Data Set); "| $T_{\mathrm{K}}$$^{a}$ | Temperature (K) | 30-300 | Log | 26 |" (Training Data Set); "| $n_{\mathrm{H}_2}$ | Volume Density (cm$^{-3}$) | 10$^3$–10$^7$ | Log | 23 |" (Training Data Set)
   - parameter: Architecture: 4 hidden layers with 2048 nodes each, ReLU activation, MSE loss, early stopping after 20 epochs without validation loss improvement; 80%/10%/10% train/val/test split  
  > "The architecture includes four hidden layers with 2048 nodes each." (Neural Network Architecture); "To prevent overfitting, we implemented an early stopping criterion that halts training when the validation loss has not improved over 20 consecutive epochs." (Neural Network Architecture); "We divided the `UCLCHEM` output into three subsets: a training set containing 80% of the data ($\sim$360,000 points), a validation set with 10% ($\sim$40,000 points), and a test set with the remaining 10% ($\sim$40,000 points)." (Training Data Set)
   - assumption: Stage 1 freefall collapse from n = 100 cm^-3, T = 10 K, zeta_0 = 1.3e-17 s^-1 over 6 Myr; Stage 2 evolution for 1 Myr at fixed visual extinction A_v = 10 mag  
  > "In stage 1, a freefall collapse model simulates the conversion of atomic/ionic gas to molecular form under fixed physical conditions: initial density of 100 cm$^{-3}$, temperature of 10 K, with the standard cosmic ray ionization rate for molecular hydrogen of $\zeta_{0}$ = 1.3 x 10$^{-17} s^{-1}$(cite: indriolo2015)." (Training Data Set); "This stage tracks chemical evolution over 6 Myr, with the final molecular abundances serving as initial conditions for stage 2." (Training Data Set); "In this stage, we adopt a visual extinction of $A_v$ = 10 mag for all models." (Training Data Set)
   - fitting: Supervised neural network training minimizing mean-squared-error (MSE) loss with early stopping  
  > "During training, we use a mean-squared-error loss function to quantify the difference between the neural network predictions and the abundances from the training data." (Neural Network Architecture)
   - validation: Evaluated on 10% held-out test set (~40,000 points); reproduces UCLCHEM HNC predictions with mean relative error of 0.35%  
  > "The network reproduces the `UCLCHEM` predictions with a mean relative error of 0.35% and accurately recovers abundance variations spanning approximately eight orders of magnitude." (Traning Results)
3. **Perform hierarchical Bayesian inference coupled with non-LTE radiative transfer**: Couple neural network chemical abundances with SpectralRadex non-LTE radiative transfer to predict line ratios, and sample the hierarchical Bayesian posterior across 198 hexagonal regions using Nautilus for both heating and quiescent models.  
  > "We perform hierarchical Bayesian inference that links a non-LTE radiative transfer module (`SpectralRadex`) with chemical modelling." (abstract); "Bayesian inference is carried out with `Nautilus` (cite: nautilus), which combines neural-network accelerated transport, importance sampling, and ultrafast likelihood approximations." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow); "In contrast to previous intensity-based implementations, we use line ratios as observables." (Hierarchical Bayesian model)
   - software: SpectralRadex, Nautilus
   - parameter: Observables: m = n - 1 chained log line ratios y_i^obs = ln(r_i) where r_k = I_k / I_{k+1}; Linewidths = 150, 100, and 50 km s^-1 for CND, stellar bar, and starburst ring  
  > "We adopted linewidths of 150, 100, and 50 km s$^{-1}$ for the CND, stellar bar, and starburst ring, respectively (cite: Scourfield2020)." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow); "To avoid over-weighting specific transitions, we construct $m = n - 1$ ratios in a chain form, where $n$ is the number of transitions detected above 3$\sigma$ in a given region." (Hierarchical Bayesian model)
   - parameter: Hyperparameter priors: mu_n in (3.0, 6.00), mu_T in (1.55, 2.30), mu_N in (22.5, 24.0), mu_zeta in (0.0, 2.0), sigma_error in (0.02, 0.5)  
  > "| $\mu_{n}$ | (3.0, 6.00) |" (Hierarchical Bayesian model); "| $\sigma_{\rm error}$ | (0.02, 0.5) |" (Hierarchical Bayesian model)
   - assumption: All transitions optically thin at best-fit parameters; C2H (N=1-0) modeled as a single transition; ensemble of region parameters follows a multivariate Student-t distribution with soft correlation bounds  
  > "The ensemble of $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ is modeled with a multivariate Student-$t$ distribution with location parameter $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$, which describes the intrinsic dispersion and correlations of the physical parameters across all regions." (Hierarchical Bayesian model); "In the radiative transfer calculation, we model C$_2$H using the $N = 1$–0 transition as a whole, which does not fully account for the relative excitation of individual hyperfine components." (Posterior predictive check); "Our analysis confirms that all transitions remain optically thin at the best-fit parameters in all regions, particularly for $C_{2}H$, which consists of hyperfine structures." (Comparison with previous work)
   - fitting: Hierarchical Bayesian nested/importance sampling with Nautilus using multivariate normal likelihood in log-ratio space and additive normal non-statistical error epsilon_i ~ N(0, sigma_error)  
  > "We therefore adopt a multivariate normal likelihood: $\mathbf{y}_i^{\rm obs} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y}_i^{\rm mod},\, \mathbf{C}_i)$, where $\mathbf{y}_i^{\rm mod}$ is the model prediction and $\mathbf{C}_i$ is the covariance matrix derived from measurement uncertainties and ratio propagation." (Hierarchical Bayesian model); "To account for residual non-statistical effects such as calibration systematics and modelling imperfections, we introduce an additive term in log-ratio space: $\mathbf{y}_i^{\rm obs} = \mathbf{y}_i^{\rm mod} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i$, where $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_i$ follows a normal distribution with zero mean and global scale parameter $\sigma_{\rm error}$." (Hierarchical Bayesian model)
   - validation: Bayesian evidence (log Z) comparison between heating and quiescent models, and posterior predictive checks (PPCs) comparing 16th-84th percentile predicted line ratios against observations  
  > "The Bayesian evidence (log $\mathcal{Z}$) provides an objective criterion for model selection, as it measures the marginal likelihood averaged over the full parameter space." (Posterior Results and Model Preference); "To assess the goodness of fit of the Bayesian inference, we perform posterior predictive checks (PPCs) for all the sub-regions." (Posterior predictive check)
4. **Extract and fit CO(1-0) and 13CO(1-0) line profiles**: Extract CO(1-0) and 13CO(1-0) spectra from selected regions in the CND, arcs, and inner edges of the starburst ring and fit single-component Gaussian profiles to test for non-Gaussian outflow wings and multiple kinematic components.  
  > "Within the CND, we present CO line profiles from the E-knot and W-knot (middle-bottom subplots in Fig. 7). Single-component Gaussian fits clearly fail to reproduce the observed profiles." (CO Line Profiles); "We present the spectra extracted from the N-SB-Bar-2 region, where both the CO(1–0) and $^{13}$CO(1–0) profiles display clear signatures of complex kinematics, as shown in Fig. 8." (CO Line Profiles)
   - parameter: Single-component Gaussian FWHM values in SSC1, SSC2, SSC3 and N-SB-Bar regions  
  > "The SSC spectra are well described by single, nearly symmetric Gaussian components with FWHM values of $55.74$, $51.94$, and $46.59$ km s$^{-1}$, while the N-SB-Bar spectra, although not systematically broader in FWHM (one is comparable at $55.85$ km s$^{-1}$ and the other two are narrower at $37.88$ and $40.00$ km s$^{-1}$), are markedly more *non-Gaussian*" (CO Line Profiles)
   - fitting: Single-component Gaussian line profile fitting  
  > "Single-component Gaussian fits clearly fail to reproduce the observed profiles." (CO Line Profiles)
   - validation: Comparison of CO(1-0) features against optically thinner 13CO(1-0) spectra to test for CO self-absorption vs intrinsic kinematic components  
  > "The appearance of a secondary peak at $-120$ km s$^{-1}$ in both transitions confirms this feature is intrinsic to the gas rather than an artifact of CO self absorption, while the extended blue wing in the CO emission suggests the presence of outflowing molecular gas across this region." ## Software

- **CASA** : Calibration and imaging of archival ALMA data  
  > "All data were calibrated and imaged using CASA (cite: CASA)." (ALMA data)
- **HERA (HExagonal Region Averager)** : Partitioning integrated intensity maps into 56 pc hexagonal regions with S/N >= 3  
  > "We used the HExagonal Region Averager (HERA) code (cite: erica_behrens_2024_13839853) to partition the integrated intensity maps into adjoining hexagonal regions, each with a characteristic size of 56 pc, equivalent to the $\sim0.8\ensuremath{''}$ beam size." (Region selection and Moment-0 Maps)
- **UCLCHEM** : Time-dependent gas-grain chemical modeling to generate the training dataset for the neural network emulator  
  > "The chemical abundances are computed with `UCLCHEM` (cite: holdship2017), a gas grain chemical modeling code that uses user-defined chemical networks and physical modules to simulate a wide range of conditions." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow); "The final abundances from stage 2 constitute our neural network training dataset." (Training Data Set)
- **SpectralRadex** : Non-LTE radiative transfer calculations within the Bayesian inference pipeline  
  > "We perform hierarchical Bayesian inference that links a non-LTE radiative transfer module (`SpectralRadex`) with chemical modelling." (abstract); "We performed radiative transfer calculations using `SpectralRadex` (cite: Holdship2021)." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow)
- **Nautilus** : Neural-network accelerated Bayesian posterior sampling and evidence calculation  
  > "Sampling is done with `Nautilus`." (abstract); "Bayesian inference is carried out with `Nautilus` (cite: nautilus), which combines neural-network accelerated transport, importance sampling, and ultrafast likelihood approximations." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow)
- **TensorFlow** : Constructing and training the neural network emulator for UCLCHEM chemical abundances  
  > "We constructed a neural network using the TensorFlow package to emulate the chemical modeling calculations." (Neural Network Architecture)

## Qualitative findings

- Regions within the CND along the AGN wind direction, arc regions along the jet, and regions near SSCs and the bar-ring interface favor the heating chemical model over the quiescent cloud model.  
  > "Regions within the CND along the AGN wind direction show the strongest preference for the heating model, consistent with previous studies that identified outflow-driven disturbances in these areas (cite: Garcia2014; Garcia2019)." (Posterior Results and Model Preference); "In the northern starburst ring, regions near the SSCs, compact young stellar clusters associated with intense star formation, and at the bar–ring interface favor the heating model, consistent with strong cloud compression." (Posterior Results and Model Preference)
- Enhanced gas densities (>10^4 cm^-3) and elevated temperatures (>120 K in the SW inner edge) along the inner edge of the starburst ring and in the second arc region (~10^5 cm^-3, ~60 K) suggest compression from the AGN-driven outflow.  
  > "The second arc regions (Green triangle in Fig. 5), situated between the starburst ring and the primary arc structures, display significantly enhanced gas densities ($\sim$10$^{5}$ cm$^{-3}$) and lower gas temperatures ($\sim$60 K)." (Gas Density Enhancement); "Additionally, we identify density enhancements exceeding 10$^{4}$ cm$^{-3}$ accompanied by elevated temperatures ($>$120 K) at the inner edge of the southwestern starburst ring." (Gas Density Enhancement)
- CO(1-0) and 13CO(1-0) line profiles at the inner edge of the northern starburst ring exhibit extended blue wings and a secondary peak at -120 km s^-1, confirming intrinsic complex kinematics and disturbed gas along the outflow path.  
  > "The appearance of a secondary peak at $-120$ km s$^{-1}$ in both transitions confirms this feature is intrinsic to the gas rather than an artifact of CO self absorption, while the extended blue wing in the CO emission suggests the presence of outflowing molecular gas across this region." (CO Line Profiles)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Low-J transitions of common tracers (HCN, HNC, HCO+, CS, CN, C2H) are well detected across both the CND and the entire starburst ring with comparable resolution and strong S/N, avoiding numerous 3-sigma upper limits.  
  > "Since our science goal requires maps that cover both the full starburst ring and the CND with comparable resolution and sensitivity, we focus on low-$J$ transitions of common tracers well detected across both regions, including HCN, HNC, HCO$^{+}$, CS, CN, and C$_{2}$H." (ALMA data); "Including them would introduce numerous $3\sigma$ upper limits with little benefit for our Bayesian analysis." (ALMA data)
- Why this method: Coupling non-LTE radiative transfer with time-dependent chemistry breaks density-temperature degeneracies, a neural network emulator overcomes the prohibitive computational cost of UCLCHEM, and hierarchical Bayesian modeling with chained line ratios suppresses spurious correlations and accounts for calibration/modeling systematics.  
  > "To overcome the degeneracies inherent to isolated radiative transfer or chemical modeling, previous studies have demonstrated that coupling non-LTE radiative transfer calculations with time-dependent chemistry provides significantly tighter constraints on gas density and temperature (cite: Viti2017; Harada2019)." (Introduction); "To overcome this limitation, we implemented a neural network that can efficiently emulate the chemical evolution calculations, following the methods of (cite: mijolla2019), replacing `UCLCHEM` in our analysis pipeline (see Fig. 1)." (Bayesian and Neural Network Workflow); "The HB approach enables a self-consistent treatment of measurement uncertainties and additional non-statistical scatter, while constraining the population distribution of physical parameters across all regions." (Hierarchical Bayesian model)
- Alternative: Including additional archival molecular transitions that are only bright in limited areas (rejected because they would introduce numerous 3-sigma upper limits with little benefit).  
  > "Although the archive includes many additional transitions, most are only bright in limited areas and are weak or undetected elsewhere. Including them would introduce numerous $3\sigma$ upper limits with little benefit for our Bayesian analysis." (ALMA data)
- Alternative: Extending the UCLCHEM training grid with buffer points outside the target parameter ranges (rejected because current emulator accuracy is sufficient and gas in NGC 1068 rarely reaches boundary values).  
  > "Although (cite: Berhens2024) introduced additional data points outside the target parameter ranges to mitigate this behaviour, we do not extend the parameter space with buffer points because the current model performance is sufficient for our analysis and previous studies of NGC 1068 show that the molecular gas rarely reaches these boundary values (cite: viti2014; Scourfield2020; huang2022; Josh2022)." (Traning Results)
- Alternative: Using shock chemical models for jet-ISM interaction regions (not performed because shock-based emulators are still in development).  
  > "A shock model would likely better describe these conditions, but since shock-based emulators are still in development, we focus here on the heating–quiescent comparison." (Posterior Results and Model Preference)
- How they decided it was enough: Restricted analysis to hexagons with S/N >= 3 and >5 detected transitions so the number of observational constraints exceeds the 4 free model parameters; halted neural network training via 20-epoch early stopping and did not add boundary buffer points because mean relative error (0.35% for HNC) was sufficient.  
  > "To prevent overfitting, we implemented an early stopping criterion that halts training when the validation loss has not improved over 20 consecutive epochs." (Neural Network Architecture); "Although (cite: Berhens2024) introduced additional data points outside the target parameter ranges to mitigate this behaviour, we do not extend the parameter space with buffer points because the current model performance is sufficient for our analysis" (Traning Results); "We identify 198 regions where more than five molecular transitions are detected above the three-sigma level, ensuring that the number of observational constraints exceeds the number of free parameters in the Bayesian inference." (Posterior Results and Model Preference)
- Limitation (authors): Neural network emulator produces outliers when input volume density or column density lies close to the parameter space boundaries.  
  > "A small number of outliers deviate from the true abundances by several orders of magnitude, and these cases occur mainly when the input volume density ($n_{\mathrm{H_2}}$) or column density ($N_{\mathrm{H_2}}$) lies close to the boundaries of the parameter space (cite: Berhens2024)." (Traning Results)
- Limitation (authors): Reliance on low-J transitions and 0.8'' (~56 pc) spatial resolution blends multiple gas phases within the beam and is less sensitive to high-density clumps than high-J transitions.  
  > "More importantly, our analysis relies on low-J transitions, which are less sensitive to dense gas than the high-J transitions used in earlier work, consistent with (cite: Josh2022), who showed that low-J HCN is not an effective tracer of high-density material." (Comparisons with Previous Studies); "The differences we find likely arise because our $\sim50$ pc resolution beam contains multiple gas components." (Comparisons with Previous Studies)
- Limitation (authors): Due to limited molecular detections in the innermost CND, physical parameters cannot be derived for the AGN region itself.  
  > "Due to the limited number of available molecular constraints in the innermost CND, likely related to the strong ionized outflow (cite: Garcia2014; huang2022; vollmer2022), we do not derive physical parameters for the AGN region." (Comparisons with Previous Studies)
- Limitation (authors): Density and temperature diagnostics alone cannot definitively distinguish between jet/outflow-driven ram pressure compression and shock compression from cloud-cloud collisions at the bar-ring interface.  
  > "We note that while the combined density and temperature measurements provide valuable insights into the physical conditions across the starburst ring, these diagnostics alone cannot definitively distinguish between the various compression mechanisms at play." (Gas Density Enhancement)
- Limitation (authors): C2H (N=1-0) hyperfine components are blended and modeled as a single transition in SpectralRadex, leading to larger scatter and poorer reproduction of C2H-related ratios in posterior predictive checks.  
  > "The C$_2$H related ratio is noticeably less well reproduced compared to the other ratios." (Posterior predictive check); "In the radiative transfer calculation, we model C$_2$H using the $N = 1$–0 transition as a whole, which does not fully account for the relative excitation of individual hyperfine components." (Posterior predictive check)
- Limitation (authors): Having mostly single low-J transitions per species (except HNC and CN) leaves residual degeneracy between density, temperature, and column density.  
  > "We also use fewer species than (cite: Holdship2022b), which limits the constraining power and leaves some degeneracy between density, temperature, and column density." (Posterior predictive check)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Fixed regional linewidths (150, 100, 50 km/s) are assumed in SpectralRadex rather than using spatially resolved measured linewidths from each hexagon.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Assuming a constant beam filling factor of unity across 56 pc hexagons may bias column density and volume density estimates if dense gas is clumpy on sub-beam scales.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): CO(1-0) and 13CO(1-0) data provenance (project code, beam size, RMS, MRS) is omitted from Section 2 and Table 1.
