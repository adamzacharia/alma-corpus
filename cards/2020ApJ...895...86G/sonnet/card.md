## Analysis recipe

1. **Prepare line and continuum data and make continuum maps**: uvcontsub first-order baseline fit; split spectral windows into continuum and line cubes; 2D Gaussian fit of continuum images  
  > "We have divided each spectral window into two data cubes: continuum and line emission for the analysis." (3 Observations, data analysis and line identification)
   - software: CASA
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian for peak flux, integrated flux and deconvolved size  
  > "We obtained the peak flux, integrated flux, and the deconvolved beam size by using two-dimensional Gaussian fitting of the continuum images." (4.1 Continuum images)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Identify lines**: Check blending, VLSR, Eup and Einstein coefficient with CASSIS and CDMS/JPL; confirm with LTE modeling  
  > "we checked line blending, V$_{LSR}$ velocity, upper state energy (E$_{up}$), and Einstein coefficient." (3 Observations, data analysis and line identification)
   - software: CASSIS
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: LTE modeling used to confirm or reject identifications  
  > "we used LTE modeling to confirm or reject the identification." (3 Observations, data analysis and line identification)
3. **Rotation diagram for Trot and column density**: Optically thin LTE rotation diagram with single Gaussian line fits; hyperfine intensity split by S mu^2; strongest hyperfine used; only non-blended CH3NCO lines  
  > "we have split the observed intensity flux according to their S$\mu^{2}$ values. Then we have used the most probable (maximum intensity) hyperfine transitions in rotational diagram analysis." (4.2 Rotation diagram analysis)
   - assumption: optically thin lines in LTE  
  > "Assuming the observed transitions of these species are optically thin and are in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE)" (4.2 Rotation diagram analysis)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
4. **Hydrogen column density and dust optical depth from continuum**: N_H2 = (S/Omega)/(2 kappa B(Td) Z mu_H m_H) at four frequencies; averaged  
  > "By taking the average of these four continuum values, we obtained column density of $\sim 1.35 \times10^{25}$ cm$^{-2}$." (4.3 Hydrogen Column density estimation)
   - assumption: Z=0.01, mu_H=1.41, Td=200 K, kappa ~0.20 cm2/g (Ossenkopf & Henning extrapolation)  
  > "Here, we used $Z=0.01$, $\mu_H=1.41$ (cite: cox00), and dust temperature 200 K." (4.3 Hydrogen Column density estimation)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with alternative kappa from Motogi et al. (beta=1.6), giving ~2 times lower column density  
  > "Observed average hydrogen column density is of $\sim$2 times lower (see Table [3](#table:hcolden)) if we consider the mass absorption coefficients values as estimated following (cite: moto19)." (4.3 Hydrogen Column density estimation)
5. **LTE MCMC line-profile fitting**: CASSIS python interface; chi2 minimization with MCMC over column density, Tex, FWHM, VLSR; source size from Gaussian fit of moment maps  
  > "We have used the python scripting interface available in CASSIS for our model calculation to find out the best-fitted physical parameters" (4.5 LTE fitting using MCMC)
   - software: CASSIS
   - parameter: Tex ranges 100-600 K; FWHM 3-9 km/s; VLSR 66.5-67.9 km/s  
  > "| 154.636867 | 154.03118 - 154.96636 | 4-8 | 7.97 | 7.5$\times$10$^{16}$ | 3.92$\times$10$^{-1}$ | 100-200 | 101.70 | 1.21 | 66.5-67.5 | 66.54 |" (Table 7)
   - assumption: LTE  
  > "We have assumed that the source is under the LTE condition." (4.5 LTE fitting using MCMC)
   - fitting: chi2 with calibration error term; reduced chi2  
  > "we have used the $\chi^2$ minimization process by considering the N number of spectra." (4.5 LTE fitting using MCMC)
   - validation: Compared with rotation-diagram temperatures  
  > "which are consistent with the high rotational temperatures of these molecules obtained by the rotational diagram analysis" (4.5 LTE fitting using MCMC)
6. **Chemical modeling and quantum chemistry**: Three-phase gas-grain model, Models A and B; Gaussian 09 MP2/aug-cc-pVDZ binding energies and reaction enthalpies; rate constants varied to match observed abundances  
  > "we have used similar method and basis set (MP2/aug-cc-pVDZ) as mentioned in (cite: sil17) and (cite: das18)." (6.2 Binding energies and reaction pathways)
   - software: Gaussian 09
   - parameter: Model B best: rho_max=1e7 cm-3, Tmax=200 K, Tice=20 K, tcoll=(2-3)e5 yr, tw=5e4 yr, tpw=1e5 yr  
  > "can be able to explain the observation of these three species simultaneously when we have considered $\rho_{max}=10^7$ cm$^{-3}$, $T_{max}=200$ K and $T_{ice}=20$ K." (6.3.2 Results obtained with Model B)
   - assumption: cosmic ray rate 1.3e-17 s-1; diffusion energy 0.5 times adsorption energy  
  > "A cosmic ray rate of $1.3 \times 10^{-17}$ s$^{-1}$ is considered in all our models." (6 Chemical Modeling)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Compared with observed abundances; no-Haupa test with alpha=0  
  > "we have checked with $\alpha=0$ for the gas phase reactions 16-19." (6.3.3)
