## Analysis recipe

1. **Smooth continuum maps to matched physical resolution**: Convolve the 1.3 mm (Band 6) and 3 mm (Band 3) cleanest continuum maps of all 15 fields with a Gaussian kernel to achieve a homogeneous spatial resolution of 2700 au.  
  > "To extract a spatially homogeneous core sample, we smoothed the 1.3 and 3 mm maps to the lowest native resolution of ≃2700 au. We convolved each map with a Gaussian kernel" (3.3. Source extraction at matched physical resolution)
   - parameter: Target spatial resolution = 2700 au; Gaussian kernel width θ_conv = sqrt((2700 au / D)^2 - θ_native^2)  
  > "To extract a spatially homogeneous core sample, we smoothed the 1.3 and 3 mm maps to the lowest native resolution of ≃2700 au." (3.3. Source extraction at matched physical resolution)
   - assumption: Distances D to each protocluster from Paper I (2000 to 5500 pc)  
  > "(1) Phase center, evolutionary stage and distance to the region taken from Tables 1 and 4 of Paper I." (Table 1)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that the mean extracted source size after smoothing is uniform across all 15 fields (L = 3840 ± 270 au, Fig. 1 right panel).  
  > "The mean size is similar for each field with" (3.3 Source extraction at matched physical resolution)
2. **Extract compact continuum sources and compare algorithms**: Two-step extraction on both native and 2700 au smoothed maps using getsf and GExt2D: detect source candidates on 1.3 mm maps without primary beam correction, then measure fluxes and sizes on 1.3 mm and 3 mm maps after primary beam correction.  
  > "To extract sources in the continuum emission maps, we used two extraction tools: _getsf_ (v210414, Men’shchikov 2021b) and _GExt2D_ (v210603, Bontemps et al., in prep.)." (3.1. Extraction tools); "Firstly, we detected source candidates in the continuum images at 1.3 mm (Band 6) without primary beam correction." (3.2. Source extraction at native angular resolutions); "Secondly, we measured the sources’ fluxes and sizes in the continuum maps at 1.3 mm and 3 mm (Band 3) after primary beam correction." (3.2. Source extraction at native angular resolutions)
   - software: getsf, GExt2D
   - parameter: _not stated_
   - assumption: _not stated_
   - fitting: Automated Gaussian fitting after background subtraction in GExt2D; multi-scale decomposition in getsf; linear regression on complementary cumulative source flux functions (SFFs) above the 12 mJy median.  
  > "The third step corresponds to automated Gaussian fitting at each locations of compact sources after subtracting the derived background." (3.1. Extraction tools); "We fit the cumulative SFF with a power law from the median value of the samples, that is 12 mJy, up to the flux at which the number of cores is below ten" (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods)
   - validation: Compared getsf (677 sources) and GExt2D (1020 sources): ~80% of getsf sources are found by GExt2D, both yield identical high-flux SFF slopes (ζ ≃ 1.85), and ~95% of smoothed getsf sources match native-resolution getsf sources.  
  > "We see a good agreement between these extractions as ∼80 % of the sources found by _getsf_ are also found by _GExt2D_ (Table 2)." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods); "The fitted highflux slopes are similar for both catalogues, with ζ [′] ≃ 0.85. These exponents, adjusted onto the complementary cumulative SFFs, correspond to power-laws with exponents ζ = ζ [′] + 1 ≃ 1.85." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods); "Indeed, ≃95% of the sources extracted in the smoothed maps were also extracted at the native angular resolutions with the _getsf_ algorithm." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods)
3. **Filter out free-free contaminated sources using 1.3 mm and 3 mm spectral indices**: Rescale 3 mm integrated fluxes for 1.3 mm vs 3 mm source size differences, compute spectral index γ (Eq. 2), and reject sources with γ < 2. For sources undetected by getsf at 3 mm but lying on pixel-by-pixel spectral index map regions with γ < 2 (3 mm background > 3σ), measure aperture upper-limit 3 mm flux (Eq. 3) and reject those with γ < 1.  
  > "We remove 68 sources with γ < 2, which are presumably contaminated by freefree emission." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources); "For the sources undetected at 3 mm we estimate their flux, _S_ 3 mm [upper] [, using] the integrated flux in the aperture corresponding to the footprint of the source detected at 1.3 mm." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources); "and we rejected the 16 sources with spectral index below 1, those for which free-free emissions could substantially contribute to the emission." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources)
   - parameter: Rejection thresholds: γ < 2 for sources detected at 3 mm (68 rejected); γ < 1 for 3 mm aperture upper limits on free-free background (16 rejected).  
  > "We remove 68 sources with γ < 2, which are presumably contaminated by freefree emission." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources); "and we rejected the 16 sources with spectral index below 1, those for which free-free emissions could substantially contribute to the emission." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources)
   - assumption: Linear 3 mm flux rescaling with source axes works for optically thick emission or optically thin isothermal envelope with density profile ρ(r) ∝ r^-2.  
  > "This rescaling works both for an optically thick emission and for an optically thin emission of an isothermal protostellar envelope with a density profile ρ( _r_ ) ∝ _r_ [−][2], where _r_ is the radius of the envelope." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Visual inspection of sources overlaid on pixel-by-pixel spectral index maps where 3 mm intensity exceeds 3σ.  
  > "The maps display the spectral index value only where the 3 mm intensity exceeds 3 σ." (Fig. 4 caption)
4. **Assign dust temperatures to each core**: Assign per-core dust temperatures from 2.5" resolution PPMAP Bayesian SED fitting maps (combining free-free decontaminated ALMA Band 6, SOFIA/HAWC+, Herschel PACS/SPIRE, and APEX SABOCA/LABOCA), overriding with 100±50 K for 49 methyl formate hot core candidates and 300±100 K for 3 extreme hot cores.  
  > "Following the pilot study by Motte et al. (2018), we used the Bayesian procedure _PPMAP_ (Marsh et al. 2015; Dell’Ova et al. 2024) to build the temperature maps of the ALMA-IMF protoclusters." (4.2.2. Core temperatures); "Following the method discussed in Bonfand et al. (2024), we set 100±50 K to the 49 sources whose position corresponds to the peaks of extended methyl formate emission; these sources are classified as hot core candidates." (4.2.2. Core temperatures); "Additionally, we set 300±100 K to sources n [◦] 1, n [◦] 2, and n [◦] 1 in W51-IRS2, W51-E and G327.29 respectively" (4.2.2. Core temperatures)
   - software: PPMAP
   - parameter: PPMAP angular resolution = 2.5"; temperature uncertainty = 25% (or 5 K for T < 20 K); hot core T = 100±50 K or 300±100 K.  
  > "Finally, we obtain a unique temperature for each ′′ core at an angular resolution of 2.5 ." (4.2.2. Core temperatures); "The error associated with _PPMAP_ derivation is ≈5 K; to account for potential systematic contributions, we adopt a 25% error on the _PPMAP_ temperature estimates [5] ." (4.2.2. Core temperatures)
   - assumption: Methyl formate (CH3OCHO) sublimates from dust grains at ~100 K and traces internal heating in hot cores.  
  > "Methyl formate forms at the surface of dust grains in lukewarm (30-40 K) regions, and is then released in the gas phase when the temperature reaches ∼100 K (e.g. Garrod et al. 2009)." (4.2.2. Core temperatures)
   - fitting: Bayesian procedure PPMAP (detailed in Dell'Ova et al. 2024)  
  > "Following the pilot study by Motte et al. (2018), we used the Bayesian procedure _PPMAP_ (Marsh et al. 2015; Dell’Ova et al. 2024) to build the temperature maps of the ALMA-IMF protoclusters." (4.2.2. Core temperatures)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **Derive core masses and evaluate gravitational boundedness**: Convert 1.3 mm integrated and peak fluxes to core mass using Eq. 4 (with first-order optical depth correction), and evaluate gravitational boundedness using the critical Bonnor-Ebert mass ratio M_BE / M_core < 2.  
  > "We converted the measured integrated flux at 1.3 mm, _S_ 1 [int] .3 mm [,] into a mass using the formula presented in Paper III that includes a first order correction for the optical thickness" (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness); "We consider that the sources are gravitationally bound if _M_ BE / _M_ source < 2 (see e.g. Louvet et al. 2021). As listed in Table 3, this filter excludes no sources." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
   - parameter: Boundedness threshold: M_BE / M_source < 2; M_BE = 2.4 * R * σ_th^2 / G; R = (a * b^2)^(1/3) * D.  
  > "where _M_ core is the core mass and _M_ BE is the mass of the critical Bonnor-Ebert sphere (Bonnor 1956) whose size matches that of the core: _M_ BE = 2.4 × _R_ × σ [2] th [/] _[G]_ [.]" (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness); "We consider that the sources are gravitationally bound if _M_ BE / _M_ source < 2 (see e.g. Louvet et al. 2021)." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
   - assumption: Dust + gas opacity per unit mass κ_1.3mm = 0.1 * (ν / 1000 GHz)^β cm^2 g^-1 with β = 1.5 (Ossenkopf & Henning 1994); isothermal adiabatic index γ = 1; mean molecular weight per free particle µ = 2.4.  
  > "κ 1.3 mm = 0.1 (ν/1000 GHz) [β] cm [2] g [−][1] is the dust opacity per unit mass (dust + gas), with an opacity index β = 1.5 typical of cold and dense environment (Ossenkopf & Henning 1994)" (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness); "where γ = 1 is the adiabatic index (isothermal), _k_ _B_ is the Boltzmann constant, µ = 2.4 is the mean molecular weight per free particle, and _m_ p is the mass of a proton." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Checked that assuming a 3D oblate core shape (V ∝ a^2 * b) instead of prolate (V ∝ a * b^2) has no effect on core boundedness.  
  > "We stress that choosing 3D oblate core shape ( _V_ ∝ _a_ 2 × _b_ ) has no effect on the boundedness of the cores." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
6. **Determine mass completeness limits per field via synthetic core injection**: Inject synthetic 2D circular Gaussian cores (FWHM = 3840 au) onto the source-subtracted background maps in 10 mass bins across 4 draws (~3800 synthetic cores per field), re-extract with getsf, and find the 90% recovery mass limit.  
  > "we injected synthetic cores on top of their background emission (i.e. the emission subtracted from each source). The synthetic cores are injected in the form of 2D circular Gaussian with a FWHM corresponding to 3840 au" (5.1. Completeness tests); "We then extract the synthetic cores with the _getsf_ method (see Sect. 3.1). To obtain a good statistics in each bin of mass, we repeated this procedure four times per field." (5.1. Completeness tests); "We define the completeness as the mass limit above which more than 90 % of the synthetic cores get extracted." (Appendix A)
   - software: getsf
   - parameter: Gaussian FWHM = 3840 au; minimum separation > 2.5"; border exclusion = 5"; 10 flux bins (equivalent to 0.5-5 M_sun at 20 K, or 1-10 M_sun in W51-E); 4 draws per field (~3800 cores total); completeness threshold = 90%.  
  > "We inject these synthetic sources randomly, provided the centre of the cores are separated by more than 2.5 [′′] . We forbid the injection of sources within 5 [′′] of the border of the maps" (5.1. Completeness tests); "The flux of the synthetic sources ranges from equivalent masses of 0.5 to 5 _M_ ⊙ in all fields for gas at 20 K, except in W51-E where we adopted a mass range of 1 to 10 _M_ ⊙ ." (5.1. Completeness tests); "Cumulating the four draws we obtain ≃3800 cores in total in each field (or ≃380 per bin) and we probe ≃80 % of the background area." (5.1. Completeness tests)
   - assumption: Synthetic core fluxes converted to mass using the mean temperature of the cores extracted in the corresponding region.  
  > "From these fluxes, we compute the mass of the synthetic cores when adopting the mean temperature of the cores extracted in the corresponding region." (5.1. Completeness tests)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: Discarded W51-E (completeness limit 3.86 M_sun) and adopted the highest remaining field completeness limit (1.64 M_sun) as the global completeness limit.  
  > "We exclude W51-E from the analysis of the global CMF (see Sect. 6.1) and we restrict the core samples to cores exceeding 1.64 _M_ ⊙ to fit the high-mass tail of the CMF." (5.1. Completeness tests)
7. **Fit the high-mass slope of the global CMF and quantify uncertainties and Salpeter tension**: Fit a power-law PDF p(x) ∝ x^-α to the 330 bound cores above x_min = 1.64 M_sun (excluding W51-E) using Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance in the powerlaw Python package, propagate uncertainties via 10^3 Monte Carlo realizations, and test tension with Salpeter (α = 2.35) via 10^5 synthetic draws.  
  > "We follow the procedure presented by Clauset et al. (2009), and use its implementation in the python package `powerlaw` presented by Alstott et al. (2014)." (5.2.1. Method); "We show the resulting cumulative CMFs generated from 10 [3] trials in Fig. 6. Each draw is fitted via MLE (see Sect. 5.2.1), fixing _x_ min to 1.64 _M_ ⊙ ." (5.2.2. Uncertainty of the fit); "we selected 330 sources from a perfect mass distribution with an exponent of 2.35 and fitted the slope of its high-mass tail through the MLE. We repeated the operation 10 [5] times." (5.2.3. Comparison with the Salpeter-slope)
   - software: powerlaw
   - parameter: x_min = 1.64 M_sun; n = 330 cores; 10^3 Monte Carlo trials with β ~ N(1.5, 0.2), T ~ N(T_core, σ_T), and Gaussian flux errors; 10^5 Salpeter synthetic trials.  
  > "We, therefore, select _x_ min = 1.64 _M_ ⊙, which corresponds to a core sample of 330 elements (excluding W51-E)." (5.2.1. Method); "In our Monte Carlo simulation, the opacity index β can take random normal values with a mean value of 1.5" (5.2.2 Uncertainty of the fit)
   - assumption: High-mass tail of the CMF follows a power-law distribution p(x) = C x^-α above x_min.  
  > "If we assume that the CMF can be represented by a power-law _p_ ( _x_ ) = _Cx_ [−][α], where _C_ is a constant, there must be some lower mass value, _x_ min, from which the power-law fit is accurate" (5.2.1. Method)
   - fitting: Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) with Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) distance minimization (Clauset et al. 2009) coupled with 10^3 Monte Carlo error propagation.  
  > "The originality of the method by Clauset et al. (2009) is to propose a method to determine the best parameters _x_ min and α using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) distance." (5.2.1. Method); "We obtain a mean exponent value for the slope of the CMF of 1.97±0.06, where the uncertainty is the quadratic sum of the statistical uncertainty from Eq. 7 (σ ≃ 0.06) and from the uncertainties on the temperature, opacity index, and flux measurement uncertainties (σ ≃ 0.02)." (5.2.2. Uncertainty of the fit)
   - validation: Verified that the first KS distance minimum occurs at x_min = 1.40 M_sun (α = 2.07 ± 0.06), compatible with x_min = 1.64 M_sun (α = 2.11 ± 0.06 before MC), and that sample size (330 cores) far exceeds the ~40-50 threshold for stable MLE index values.  
  > "We note that _x_ min = 1.40 _M_ ⊙ corresponds to α = 2.07 ± 0.06, compatible with the α associated with _x_ min = 1.64 _M_ ⊙ ." (5.2.1. Method); "Clauset et al. (2009) report stable index values when the sample exceeds ≈50 elements above the completeness limits." (5.2.1. Method)

## Qualitative findings

- At native angular resolution, extracted mean source sizes correlate linearly with physical resolution; smoothing to a homogeneous 2700 au resolution yields a uniform mean core size of 3840 ± 270 au across all 15 protoclusters.  
  > "The left panel of Fig. 1 shows a linear dependency between the mean source size and the linear resolution. To obtain a homogeneous sample of sources the data must be smoothed to a matched spatial resolution." (3.2. Source extraction at native angular resolutions); "The mean size is similar for each field with _L_ = 3840 ± 270 au." (3.3. Source extraction at matched physical resolution)
- The high-flux slope of the source flux function (ζ ≃ 1.85) is independent of whether getsf or GExt2D is used for source extraction.  
  > "This analysis proves that the overall statistics of source flux is independent of the two extraction algorithms we employed." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods)
- The fraction of sources rejected due to free-free emission contamination increases with protocluster evolutionary stage: ~0.5% in young regions, ~10% in intermediate regions, and ~25% in evolved regions.  
  > "The degree of rejection depends on the evolutionary stages of the regions: ≃ 0.5% in the young regions, ≃ 10% in the intermediate regions, and ≃ 25% in the evolved regions." (4.2.1. Removing contaminated sources)
- All 593 thermal dust cores across the 15 protoclusters satisfy the thermal Bonnor-Ebert gravitational boundedness criterion (M_BE / M_core < 2).  
  > "We consider that the sources are gravitationally bound if _M_ BE / _M_ source < 2 (see e.g. Louvet et al. 2021). As listed in Table 3, this filter excludes no sources." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
- The global high-mass CMF slope in the 15 high-mass protoclusters (α = 1.97 ± 0.06) is top-heavy and significantly flatter than the Salpeter IMF slope (α = 2.35), implying either that these protoclusters will form top-heavy stellar IMFs or that the CMF must evolve toward a Salpeter slope at later stages.  
  > "We confirm that the CMF in a representative sample of high-mass Galactic protoclusters is shallower than the Salpeter slope. We suggest that these massive protoclusters will give birth to top-heavy stellar clusters, or, in order to reconcile with the Universal IMF, the CMF needs to evolve and become Salpeterlike at latter stages of the cluster formation." (7. Conclusions)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: Previous CMF studies in high-mass protoclusters used narrow single-pointing fields or disparate physical resolutions and sensitivities; ALMA-IMF observed 15 representative Milky Way high-mass protoclusters (>500 M_sun in inner parts, distances 2-6 kpc) homogeneously at ~2 kau resolution and <60 mK sensitivity at 1.3 mm.  
  > "However, the comparison of core samples between these studies — which are captured at different evolutionary stages — becomes complex or impossible when the observations are carried out at disparate physical resolutions and/or sensitivities. Thus, it became imperative to obtain a sample of high-mass protoclusters representative of the Milky Way observed homogeneously." (1. Introduction)
- Why this method: Used cleanest continuum maps to avoid molecular line contamination; smoothed maps to 2700 au to eliminate resolution bias on core sizes and masses; used two extraction tools (getsf and GExt2D) to test algorithmic bias and selected getsf as more conservative and consistent across resolutions; used Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) instead of binned histograms or cumulative linear regression to avoid binning bias and correlated errors.  
  > "The cleanest maps were constructed flagging all channels contaminated by molecular emission (see Fig. 3 in Paper II). Since we wish to assemble the most reliable sample of cores, we use the cleanest continuum maps" (2. Observations); "In the following, we choose to focus on the extractions performed by _getsf_ since it is more conservative than _GExt2D_, which is confirmed by the large fraction of _getsf_ sources confirmed with _GExt2D_ ." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods); "The representation of a distribution in the form of histograms, as in Fig. 3, further fitted through linear regression may give inaccurate results (Clauset et al. 2009)." (5.2.1. Method)
- Alternative: bsens continuum maps (constructed using the full continuum bandwidth) were rejected in favor of cleanest continuum maps because bsens may include significant molecular line contamination toward hot cores.  
  > "The bsens maps were constructed using the full range of the continuum spectral windows — which may include significant molecular emission, especially toward hot cores. The cleanest maps were constructed flagging all channels contaminated by molecular emission (see Fig. 3 in Paper II). Since we wish to assemble the most reliable sample of cores, we use the cleanest continuum maps" (2. Observations)
- Alternative: GExt2D source catalog was used for comparison but not selected for the main CMF analysis because getsf is more conservative and shows better consistency (95% vs 75%) between native and smoothed resolutions.  
  > "In the following, we choose to focus on the extractions performed by _getsf_ since it is more conservative than _GExt2D_, which is confirmed by the large fraction of _getsf_ sources confirmed with _GExt2D_ ." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods); "Indeed, ≃95% of the sources extracted in the smoothed maps were also extracted at the native angular resolutions with the _getsf_ algorithm. This fraction drops to ≃75% in the case of the _GExt2D_ extractions." (4.1. Getsf versus GExt2D extraction methods)
- Alternative: 3D oblate core geometry (V ∝ a^2 * b) was tested as an alternative to prolate geometry (V ∝ a * b^2) and found to have no effect on core boundedness.  
  > "We stress that choosing 3D oblate core shape ( _V_ ∝ _a_ 2 × _b_ ) has no effect on the boundedness of the cores." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
- Alternative: W51-E was excluded from the global CMF fit because its 90% mass completeness limit (3.86 M_sun) is much higher than the other 14 fields (≤ 1.64 M_sun).  
  > "These numbers exclude W51-E, for which we find completeness as high as 3.9 _M_ ⊙ . We exclude W51-E from the analysis of the global CMF (see Sect. 6.1)" (5.1. Completeness tests)
- Alternative: Linear regression on binned histograms or cumulative CMFs was rejected in favor of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) because histograms suffer from non-Gaussian log-scale noise and arbitrary bin widths, while cumulative distributions have non-independent data points.  
  > "The representation of a distribution in the form of histograms, as in Fig. 3, further fitted through linear regression may give inaccurate results (Clauset et al. 2009)." (5.2.1. Method); "The representation in the form of a cumulative CMF, however, prevents the estimation of the uncertainty as the data are not independent. To circumvent these issues, we choose to fit the high-mass end of the CMF using maximum likelihood estimates (MLE)." (5.2.1. Method)
- Alternative: Exponential and log-normal functional forms were also fitted to the 10^3 Monte Carlo CMF realizations in Appendix E alongside the power-law fit.  
  > "In this Appendix we display in Fig. a:incertitudes the functional parameters retrieved when adjusting a power-law (in blue), an exponential (in green), or a log-normal (in orange) with the MLE technique (see Sect. 5.2.1." (Appendix E: Monte Carlo simulations for fitting the slope of the CMF)
- How they decided it was enough: Injected ~3800 synthetic cores across 4 draws per field to cover ~80% of the background area for completeness; verified that the 330-core sample above 1.64 M_sun far exceeds the ~40-50 core threshold required for stable MLE power-law index convergence and ran 10^5 Monte Carlo draws to test statistical power against the Salpeter slope.  
  > "Cumulating the four draws we obtain ≃3800 cores in total in each field (or ≃380 per bin) and we probe ≃80 % of the background area." (5.1. Completeness tests); "We put forward that 330 cores above the completeness limit convey a statistical conclusive sample. Clauset et al. (2009) report stable index values when the sample exceeds ≈50 elements above the completeness limits." (5.2.1. Method)
- Limitation (authors): Gravitational boundedness is only a first-order thermal Bonnor-Ebert check because turbulence, external pressure, and magnetic field support are not currently available for each core.  
  > "We note that this is a first-order check of the boundedness of cores. A more accurate determination would require computing the equilibrium of each source taking into account its turbulence, external pressure, and magnetic field support in addition to the thermal support. Unfortunately, we lack all these pieces of information at the moment." (4.2.3. Core mass and boundedness)
- Limitation (authors): 84 sources potentially contaminated by free-free emission were rejected rather than flux-corrected and retained (though re-introducing massive free-free-corrected cores would not alter the conclusion).  
  > "Free-free emission: in Sect. 4.2, we rejected all sources that are arguably contaminated by free-free emission from our core sample. We ended up rejecting 84 sources (see Table 3)." (6.3. Limitations and perspectives)
- Limitation (authors): Cores are probed at 2700 au spatial resolution, leaving potential sub-fragmentation and multiplicity on scales below 1000 au (down to ~200 au disk scales) unresolved.  
  > "Sub-fragmentation: we probed the cores at a spatial resolution of 2700 au. Recent observations in the high-mass protostellar core G335 showed that a binary system, and perhaps even a triple system, could take place below 1000 au (Olguin et al. 2021, 2022)." (6.3. Limitations and perspectives); "At present date, it is thus uncertain how will be the multiplicity in the ALMAIMF fields." (6.3. Limitations and perspectives)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Core temperatures from PPMAP have a 2.5 arcsec angular resolution (6000-13750 au across 2-5.5 kpc), which is coarser than the 2700 au core extraction resolution, while hot core temperatures are assigned discrete values (100±50 K or 300±100 K) rather than individually fitted values.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Notice that Monte Carlo propagation of temperature, opacity index, and flux uncertainties shifts the MLE power-law exponent systematically from α = 2.11 ± 0.06 to α = 1.97 ± 0.06, which is larger than the quoted 0.02 systematic scatter around the MC mean.
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Section 6.3 states that re-introducing massive young stellar objects with free-free emission 'would steepen our CMF even further', whereas adding high-mass cores actually flattens (decreases α of) the high-mass tail of the CMF.
