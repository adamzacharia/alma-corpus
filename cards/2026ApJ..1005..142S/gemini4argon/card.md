## Analysis recipe

1. **Extract spectra and continuum fluxes and measure line parameters**: Convolve all images to a common 0.54'' circular beam, extract spectra and continuum flux from a 0.50'' diameter circular aperture centered on the HC1 continuum peak, identify lines using CDMS and JPL databases, and fit Gaussian profiles (or directly integrate) to measure line parameters  
  > "The spectra and continuum flux of HC1 are extracted from a circular region with a diameter of 0.50$''$ (560 au at the distance of RX1713), centered at its continuum and molecular emission peak mentioned above." (Spectra); "Upon spectral extraction, all images are convolved to have a common circular beam size of 0.54$''$." (Spectra); "We have measured the peak brightness temperature ($T_{b}$), the line FWHMs (full widths at half maximums, $\Delta$$V$), the integrated intensities ($\int T_{b} dV$), and the LSR velocity ($V_{LSR}$) for each line by fitting a Gaussian profile." (Measured line parameters)
   - software: CASA
   - parameter: Aperture diameter = 0.50'' (560 au); convolved circular beam = 0.54''  
  > "The spectra and continuum flux of HC1 are extracted from a circular region with a diameter of 0.50$''$ (560 au at the distance of RX1713), centered at its continuum and molecular emission peak mentioned above." (Spectra); "Upon spectral extraction, all images are convolved to have a common circular beam size of 0.54$''$." (Spectra)
   - parameter: Detection threshold = 3 sigma at V_sys = -8.0 km s^-1 (tentative = 2.5 to 3 sigma); upper limit assumed FWHM = 4 km s^-1 at 2 sigma  
  > "The detection criteria adopted here are a 3$\sigma$ significance level and velocity coincidence with the systemic velocity ($V_{\rm sys}$) of HC1 ($-8.0$ km s$^{-1}$), corresponding to the typical $V_{\rm sys}$ of the CH$_3$OH lines detected in HC1." (Spectra); "Upper limits are estimated assuming $\Delta$$V$ = 4 km s$^{-1}$." (Measured line parameters)
   - assumption: Distance = 1.12 +/- 0.01 kpc  
  > "It is located at the distance of 1.12 $\pm$ 0.01 kpc, as precisely estimated by a dust extinction study of nearby GAIA sources (cite: Lei21)." (Target)
   - fitting: 1D Gaussian profile fitting to spectral lines; direct integration over emission frequency range where Gaussian does not fit well  
  > "We have measured the peak brightness temperature ($T_{b}$), the line FWHMs (full widths at half maximums, $\Delta$$V$), the integrated intensities ($\int T_{b} dV$), and the LSR velocity ($V_{LSR}$) for each line by fitting a Gaussian profile." (Measured line parameters); "For spectral lines for which a Gaussian does not fit well, their integrated intensities are calculated by directly integrating the spectrum over the frequency region of emission." (Measured line parameters)
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **Construct integrated intensity maps and measure deconvolved source sizes**: Integrate spectral cubes over velocity ranges of detected emission (stacking multiple lines for some species) and fit 2D Gaussian profiles to estimate deconvolved source sizes  
  > "The images are constructed by integrating the spectral data over the velocity ranges where the emission is detected." (Distribution of molecular line emission); "We also estimated the deconvolved source sizes as $\sqrt{\mathrm{FWHM_{maj}} \times \mathrm{FWHM_{min}} - \Theta_{\mathrm{beam}}^2 }$, where $\mathrm{FWHM_{maj}}$ and $\mathrm{FWHM_{min}}$ are the major and minor FWHM sizes derived from two-dimensional Gaussian fits" (Images)
   - parameter: Upper limit set when deconvolved size is smaller than half the beam size  
  > "Note that only upper limits are estimated for emission whose deconvolved source sizes are smaller than half of the beam size." (Images)
   - fitting: 2D Gaussian fits to emission images  
  > "where $\mathrm{FWHM_{maj}}$ and $\mathrm{FWHM_{min}}$ are the major and minor FWHM sizes derived from two-dimensional Gaussian fits, and $\Theta_{\mathrm{beam}}$ is the geometric mean of the beam major and minor axes (see Table 3)." (Images)

## Qualitative findings

- First detection of hot molecular cores (HC1 and HC2) in a supernova-feedback region, associated with Class I intermediate-mass protostars inside the X-ray shell of RX J1713.7-3946.  
  > "We report the first detection of hot molecular cores in a supernova remnant using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array." (Abstract); "The detected hot cores (HC1 and HC2) are located inside the X-ray shell of the young supernova remnant RX J1713.7$-$3946, and both sources are associated with Class I intermediate-mass protostars." (Abstract)
- Column density ratios of COMs (HCOOCH3/CH3OH, CH3OCH3/CH3OH, CH3CHO/CH3OH), deuterated methanol (CH2DOH/CH3OH), and S- and N-bearing molecules (OCS/CH3OH, C2H5CN/CH3CN) in HC1 are indistinguishable from hot cores/corinos in typical star-forming environments.  
  > "Despite being located within a supernova-feedback region, the column density ratios of complex organic molecules (HCOOCH$_3$/CH$_3$OH, CH$_3$OCH$_3$/CH$_3$OH, and CH$_3$CHO/CH$_3$OH), a deuterated molecule (CH$_2$DOH/CH$_3$OH), and sulfur- and nitrogen-bearing species (OCS/CH$_3$OH and C$_2$H$_5$CN/CH$_3$CN) in HC1 are indistinguishable from those observed in hot cores/corinos in more typical star-forming environments." (Abstract)
- Isotope ratios (12C/13C and 34S/33S) in HC1 are consistent with the local ISM, indicating no significant modification by the supernova event.  
  > "These values are consistent with those in the local ISM (cite: Yan23), suggesting that the isotope ratios in HC1 have not been significantly modified by the supernova event." (Molecular column densities and gas temperatures)
- Survival of molecular complexity in HC1 is explained by short exposure time (< 1000 yr) near the outer edge of the supernova shell and/or magnetic field shielding that keeps the effective cosmic-ray ionization rate below 10^-15 s^-1.  
  > "The elapsed time since the onset of exposure to high-energy particles and photons may be too short for the chemical composition of the hot core to be significantly altered, and/or the hot-core region may be shielded by magnetic fields amplified by supernova feedback, which could suppress the penetration of enhanced cosmic rays." (Abstract); "Our calculations show that, for an ionization rate below 10$^{-15}$ s$^{-1}$, the gas-phase molecular abundances do not change significantly over a timescale of $\sim$1000 years." (Discussion)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: High-spatial-resolution ALMA observations toward Peak D and Peak C in RX J1713.7-3946 were chosen because both fields exhibit CO bipolar outflow signatures indicating ongoing star formation inside a young SNR shell.  
  > "Chemical analyses of compact, dense, and high-temperature regions associated with protostars are essential for understanding the impact of supernova feedback on the chemical evolution of COMs in star- and planet-forming regions." (Introduction); "The Peak D field includes a source that exhibits signatures of a bipolar molecular outflow traced by CO gas in the ALMA archival data (ID: 2017.1.01406.S)." (Target); "The presence of protostellar outflows is a good indicator of ongoing star formation." (Target)
- Why this method: Normalized COM abundances to CH3OH because it is a parental species of larger COMs; used rare isotopologues for CH3OH, SO, CS, HCO+, and HCN to avoid optical depth effects; adopted bare grain dust opacity due to ice sublimation at >100 K.  
  > "The normalization by CH$_3$OH is adopted because this molecule is considered to be a parental species of larger COMs (cite: Gar06)." (Chemical properties); "For molecular species whose main isotopologues are likely to be optically thick (CH$_3$OH, SO, and CS), as well as for species whose main isotopologues are not detected (HCO$^+$ and HCN), column densities are estimated using their rare isotopologues." (Molecular column densities and gas temperatures); "Taking into account the sublimation of ice mantles within the hot-core region, we adopt the dust opacity for bare dust grains estimated by (cite: Oss94)." (Derivation of the H$_2$ column density from the dust continuum)
- Alternative: Thin ice-coated dust grain opacity was considered as an alternative to bare dust grain opacity, which would increase the derived N(H2) by approximately a factor of two.  
  > "If instead the opacity of thin ice-coated grains is adopted, the derived $N_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ would be approximately a factor of two larger." (Derivation of the H$_2$ column density from the dust continuum)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Uncertainties and upper limits in Table 3 do not include systematic errors from spectroscopic constants.  
  > "Table notes: Uncertainties and upper limits are at the 2$\sigma$ level and do not include systematic errors due to adopted spectroscopic constants." (Table 3)
- Limitation (authors): Important uncertainty in N(H2) arises from the choice of dust opacity (bare vs. ice-coated grains gives a factor of two difference).  
  > "It should be noted that an important source of uncertainty arises from the dust opacity." (Derivation of the H$_2$ column density from the dust continuum)
- Limitation (authors): Herschel far-infrared data used for bolometric luminosity have coarse angular resolution (~6'' and 11'' at 70 and 160 um) compared to ALMA.  
  > "For the Herschel far-infrared data, which are crucial for determining the bolometric luminosity, the angular resolution is coarse compared to the present ALMA observations ($\sim$6$''$ and 11$''$ at 70 and 160 $\mu$m)." (Spectral energy distributions)
