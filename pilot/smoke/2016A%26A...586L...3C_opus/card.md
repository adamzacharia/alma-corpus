## Analysis recipe

1. **identify lines in MM1 spectrum**: Extract spectrum towards MM1 across the 8 GHz bandwidth and identify lines with the JPL database  
  > "The lines were identified using the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) molecular database (Pickett et al. 1998)." (Results and discussion)
   - software: GILDAS, JPL molecular database
   - assumption: Vsys = +1.7 km/s  
  > "we adopt here +1.7 km s$^{-1}$ (Lee et al. 2014)." (Results and discussion)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
2. **CH3CHO LTE rotation diagram**: LTE optically thin rotational diagram analysis of CH3CHO lines with <30% blending contamination, plus synthetic spectra  
  > "using only lines with less than 30% contamination from blended lines, in terms of integrated emission, see Table 1" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - software: not stated
   - parameter: source size 0.3" (half a beam)  
  > "Given that the CH$_3$CHO emission is spatially unresolved, we assume a source size equal to half a beam, namely 0$\ensuremath{''}$3." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: LTE, optically thin  
  > "An LTE (Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium) optically thin analysis of CH$_3$CHO" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: opacity checked to be < 0.4  
  > "With these values the opacity is less than 0.4." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
3. **estimate CH3CHO abundance**: Divide N(CH3CHO) by literature N(H2) from other hot corinos, since continuum is optically thick  
  > "we cannot use the continuum emission to estimate the H$_2$ column density (and hence the CH$_3$CHO abundance) because the submillimetre continuum peak seen in interferometric maps is optically thick" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - assumption: N(H2) ~ 1e24–1e25 cm^-2 from Taquet et al. (2015), emitting size 0.3"  
  > "If we take these values and 0$\ensuremath{''}$3 as emitting size" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: compared with IRAS16293-2422 value (Jaber et al. 2014)  
  > "in agreement with the value recently derived in the IRAS16293-2422 hot corino" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
4. **non-LTE LVG modelling of HDO**: Non-LTE LVG plane-parallel model predicting T vs N(HDO) reproducing observed HDO intensity for densities 1e7–1e10 cm^-3 and two source sizes  
  > "we compare the observed emission with that predicted by a non-LTE LVG (Large Velocity Gradient) model using the code by Ceccarelli et al. (2003), a plane parallel geometry" (HDO emission); "The plots cover densities from 10$^7$ to $10^{10}$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission)
   - software: LVG code (Ceccarelli et al. 2003), BASECOL
   - parameter: source sizes 0.3" and five times smaller (0.06")  
  > "the upper panel shows the case of a source size equal to half a beam (0$\ensuremath{''}$3) and the lower panel shows a case with a size that is lower by a factor of five" (HDO emission)
   - assumption: Boltzmann ortho-to-para H2 ratio; HDO-H2 rates from Faure et al. (2012)  
  > "assuming a Boltzmann distribution for the ortho-to-para H$_2$ ratio" (HDO emission)
   - assumption: N(HDO)/N(H2) <= 3.2e-6 (10% of D/H elemental abundance, initial H2O 3e-5)  
  > "namely $N$(HDO)/$N$(H$_2$) $\leq 3.2 \times 10^{-6}$" (HDO emission)
   - fitting: grid comparison of model curves with observed integrated intensity  
  > "Figure 4 shows the temperature versus the HDO column density predicted to generate the observed signal (see Table 1)." (HDO emission)
   - validation: _not stated_
5. **interpret HDO asymmetry, optically thin case**: Qualitative comparison of HDO vs CH3CHO profiles against disk, envelope and outflow scenarios  
  > "Figure 3 compares the HDO profile derived towards MM1 with that of CH$_3$CHO(18$_{0,18}$–17$_{0,17}$)A" (HDO emission)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: _not stated_
6. **constrain conditions in optically thick case**: Overlay tau = 1 curves on LVG predictions and find size range where they intersect observed-signal curves  
  > "in the non-LTE LVG predictions in Fig. 4 we add dashed curves where the HDO line opacity is equal to unity" (Optically thick case)
   - software: LVG code (Ceccarelli et al. 2003)
   - assumption: typical water abundance ~1e-4 for HDO/H2O  
  > "Assuming a typical water abundance of $\sim 10^{-4}$" (Optically thick case)
   - fitting: _not stated_
   - validation: HDO/H2O compared with values measured in hot corinos  
  > "consistent with the values measured in hot corinos (Persson et al. 2014; Ceccarelli et al. 2015)" (Optically thick case)

## Qualitative findings

- CH3CHO and HDO are seen only towards the protostar and are spatially unresolved  
  > "Figure 1 shows that CH$_3$CHO and HDO are only observed towards the protostellar position and are spatially unresolved." (Results and discussion)
- HDO profile asymmetric at low velocity with brighter redshifted emission  
  > "the redshifted emission is clearly brighter than the blueshifted." (HDO emission)
- Disk and infalling envelope origins of the HDO asymmetry disfavoured; outflow most likely  
  > "The last and most likely possibility is that the low-velocity HDO emission is dominated by outflowing motions." (Optically thin case)

## Reasoning

- Why this data: HH212 has both a bright bipolar jet and a compact rotating disk, ideal for linking hot corino, disk and outflow  
  > "HH212 stands out as being the perfect target for investigating the link between hot corino, disk, and outflow in the protostellar stage." (Introduction: The HH212 laboratory)
- Why this method: Two limiting opacity cases studied to separate kinematic and radiative effects on HDO profile  
  > "we study two limit situations: (i) the optically thin case where the kinematics will dominate and (ii) the optically thick case" (HDO emission)
- Alternative: Rotating disk origin of HDO low-velocity emission rejected due to large red/blue asymmetry  
  > "However, the low-velocity range of the HDO line being due to the protostellar disk is not supported by the large (factor of 2) difference" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Infalling envelope origin ruled out  
  > "We can also rule out an origin from the rotating infalling envelope" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Fast SiO jet origin rejected (SiO too broad)  
  > "is definitely too broad (up to $\pm$ 20 km s$^{-1}$) for HDO to probe the inner portion of the fast jet traced by SiO" (Optically thin case)
- Alternative: Density of 1e6 cm^-3 ruled out  
  > "We find that it is not possible, then, to reproduce the HDO line intensity and we rule out a density of 10$^6$ cm$^{-3}$." (HDO emission)
- How they decided it was enough: _not stated_
- Limitation (authors): Continuum optically thick, so H2 column (and abundance) cannot be measured directly  
  > "Unfortunately, we cannot use the continuum emission to estimate the H$_2$ column density" (CH$_3$CHO emission)
- Limitation (authors): Emission unresolved; derived column depends on assumed size (factor ~20 for 0.06")  
  > "In the case of a smaller emitting size, e.g. 0$\ensuremath{''}$06 (as assumed for HDO in Sect. 3), $N_{\rm CH_3CHO}$ increases by a factor of $\sim$ 20." (CH$_3$CHO emission)
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Rotation temperature has ~55% uncertainty from only five lines
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Abundance relies on N(H2) from other sources at ~3x larger scales
- Caveat (inferred, ours): HDO conclusions rest on a single transition; opacity regime undetermined
- Caveat (inferred, ours): Source sizes assumed (half-beam) rather than measured
